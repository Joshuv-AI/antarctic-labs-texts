Philosophy of contraherent cosheaves
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. Contraherent cosheaves are module objects over algebraic varieties defined by gluing using the colocalization functors. Contraherent cosheaves are designed to be used for globalizing contramodules and contraderived categories for the purposes of Koszul duality and semi-infinite algebraic geometry. One major technical problem associated with contraherent cosheaves is that the colocalization functors, unlike the localizations, are not exact. The reason is that, given a commutative ring homomorphism R ⟶ S R\longrightarrow S arising in connection with a typical covering in algebraic geometry, the ring S S is usually a flat, but not a projective R R -module. We argue that the relevant difference between projective and flat modules, from the standpoint of homological algebra, is not that big, as manifested by the flat/projective and cotorsion periodicity theorems. The difference becomes even smaller if one observes that the ring S S is often a very flat R R -module.

0.2.

One can observe that comodules are defined using the tensor product
functors, while coderived categories (in one of the approaches) are
defined using the infinite coproduct functors.
Both the tensor products and the infinite coproducts commute with
localizations.
Furthermore, the localization of a torsion module is a torsion module.
This is one way to explain why the quasi-coherent sheaves, which
are glued from modules using the localization functors, are suitable
for use with the comodules, torsion modules, and coderived categories.

Dual-analogously, contramodules over coalgebras are defined using
the covariant Hom functors, while contraderived categories (in one of
the approaches) are defined using the infinite product functors.
Both the covariant Hom functors and the infinite products commute with
the colocalizations.
Furthermore, the colocalization of a derived complete module (also
known as a contramodule) over a commutative ring with a fixed finitely
generated ideal is again a derived complete module.
This suggests that the contraherent cosheaves, which are glued from
modules using the colocalization functors, should be suitable for use
with the contramodules, derived complete modules, and contraderived
categories.

0.5.

Let

be a commutative ring homomorphism interpreted
geometrically as corresponding to a covering or a part of a covering.
In the simple context of the Zariski topology on schemes, one
can suppose that the related morphism of affine schemes

\Spec S\longrightarrow\Spec R

To make things even simpler and more explicit, let us assume
that
\Spec S
is a
principal
affine open subscheme in

\Spec R

S=R[r^{-1}]
, where

is an element.

Then the
localization functor

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
takes
an
R
-module

S
-module

The

The quasi-coherent sheaves are glued from their affine module pieces
using the localization functors.
The contraherent cosheaves are glued from their affine module pieces
using the colocalization functors.

One immediately observes that the localization functor

R[r^{-1}]\otimes_{R}{-}
is
exact
, while the colocalization functor

\Hom_{R}(R[r^{-1}],{-})
is
not exact
.
Indeed, the
R
-module

is flat, but usually

The main technical problems of the theory of contraherent cosheaves
arise from this fact.
The first consequence is that the category of quasi-coherent sheaves

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
on a scheme

is
abelian
, but the category of
contraherent cosheaves

is only

(in Quillen’s

The unpleasant technical complication of the
nonlocality of

[
61

also arises from here.

0.6.

In fact, as far as flat modules go, the flat

The theory of

The branch of contraherent cosheaf theory based on very flatness is

The main result in this direction is the

It claims that the commutative ring homomorphisms

In fact, if

So there are lots of commutative ring homomorphisms with this

0.7.

Another branch of the contraherent cosheaf theory, not relying on
very flatness but instead based on the belief that arbitrary flat
modules can be dealt with successfully, is called the
locally
cotorsion contraherent cosheaves
.

The main results supporting the thesis that flat modules are not
that much more complicated than projective ones are called
the
periodicity theorems
in homological algebra.
The two most important ones for the contraherent cosheaf purposes
are the
flat/projective periodicity theorem

[
8
,
51
]

and the
cotorsion periodicity theorem

[
2
]
.
A series of the present author’s recent papers on
the topic
[
3
,
94
,
77
,
78
,
80
,
81
,
82
,
83
,
84
]
was intended
to achieve a better understanding of the subject, with the applications
to contraherent cosheaves in mind.

So far, the point seems to be that the flat modules are the direct
limits of (finitely generated) projective ones.
The known results on the periodicity theorems suggest that
the passage to the direct limit closure is a relatively harmless
operation from the homological algebra standpoint, tending not
to complicate things too much.

In particular, even though the projective dimensions of flat modules
can well be infinite in general, the passage from projective modules
to flat ones as the direct limits behaves in many ways similarly
to a passage to an ambient exact category of objects of finite
resolution dimension with respect to the original one.
This is one of the main points which the exposition in this paper
is intended to emphasize.

The contraherent cosheaves are important devices for globalizing

The required preliminary material for the definition of a contraherent

Finally, in Section

1.
Why Contraherent Cosheaves? Comodules and Contramodules

1.1.
Basics

Let
R
be a commutative ring.
A
coalgebra

\mathcal{C}
over
R
is an
R
-module endowed with
two maps of
comultiplication

\mu\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{R}\mathcal{C}
and

counit

\epsilon\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow R
, which must be
R
-module
maps satisfying the usual coassociativity and counitality axioms.
A
left
\mathcal{C}
-comodule

\mathcal{M}
is an
R
-module endowed with
a
left coaction
map
\nu\colon\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{R}\mathcal{M}
, which must
be an
R
-module map satisfying the usual coassociativity and
counitality axioms.

A
left
\mathcal{C}
-contramodule

\mathfrak{P}
is an
R
-module endowed
a
left contraaction
map
\pi\colon\Hom_{R}(\mathcal{C},\mathfrak{P})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
,
which must be an
R
-module map satisfying the contraassociativity
and contraunitality axioms.
We refer to
[
76
, Section 8]
or
[
62
, Sections 1.1–1.2
and 2.5]
for the details.

The category of left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}}
is abelian with
an exact forgetful functor
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}

if and
only if

\mathcal{C}
is a flat
R
-module.
The category of left
\mathcal{C}
-contramodules
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}
is abelian with
an exact forgetful functor
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}

if and
only if

\mathcal{C}
is a projective
R
-module
[
62
, Proposition 2.12]
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a finitely generated projective
R
-module, then
the categories of left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules and left
\mathcal{C}
-contramodules
are equivalent to each other and to the category of left modules
over the
R
-algebra
\Hom_{R}(\mathcal{C},R)
.
This is a trivial, uninteresting case from the coalgebra theory
perspective.

Both comodules and contramodules play a crucial role in the contexts
of nonhomogeneous Koszul duality
[
58
,
71
,
76
]
and
semi-infinite homological algebra
[
57
,
62
]
.

1.2.
Discussion

How does one globalize the definitions from
Section
1.1
to a scheme
X

replacing a commutative ring
R
?

Let us start with a coalgebra before passing to comodules and
contramodules.
One can say that a quasi-coherent coalgebra
\mathcal{C}
over a scheme
X
is
a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module endowed with two maps of

comultiplication

\mu\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{C}
and counit

\epsilon\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}
, which must of course be

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module maps.
The coassociativity and counitality axioms can be written down
in this context in a quite obvious way.

Similarly, a quasi-coherent left comodule
\mathcal{M}
over
a quasi-coherent coalgebra
\mathcal{C}
over
X
is a quasi-coherent

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module endowed with a
left coaction
map

\nu\colon\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
, which must be an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module map
satisfying the obvious coassociativity and counitality equations.
Then the category of quasi-coherent left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}}

is abelian with an exact forgetful functor
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}

if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is a flat quasi-coherent sheaf.

But what is a
\mathcal{C}
-contramodule
?
We would argue that an attempt to define the notion of
a “quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
with a
\mathcal{C}
-contramodule structure”
runs into difficulties (unless we are in the trivial special case
when
\mathcal{C}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module of finite rank).

What does it mean that a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{P}
on
X
is
a
\mathcal{C}
-contramodule?
Following the definition in
Section
1.1
, one has to
consider some kind of “Hom object” from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{P}
.

The internal
\qHom
sheaf from a coherent sheaf to a quasi-coherent
one (say, on a Noetherian scheme) is quasi-coherent, but it is not
interesting for us to assume
\mathcal{C}
to be a coherent sheaf.
The internal
\qHom
sheaf from one quasi-coherent sheaf to another
one is not even quasi-coherent.

One can apply the coherator functor
[
108
, Section B.12]
to
the internal Hom sheaf
\qHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})
, and obtain
the quasi-coherent internal Hom sheaf
\qHom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})
.
What happens if one defines a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{C}
-contramodule
\mathcal{P}

as a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
endowed with a contraaction map

\pi\colon\qHom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
?
Notice that this is
not
the same thing as having a map
of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
\qHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
,
as the coherator functor is adjoint to the inclusion of the category
of quasi-coherent sheaves into the category of sheaves of

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
on the right
and not not on the left side.

Still, it appears that one can spell out the contraassociativity and
contraunitality axioms in this context without running into
any problems.
Indeed, such definition of a contramodule over a coalgebra makes sense
in any closed monoidal category; and the category of quasi-coherent
sheaves
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
is a closed monoidal category with respect to
the tensor product
\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}
and
\qHom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}
operations.

But the quasi-coherent internal Hom operation is not local with
respect to restrictions to open subschemes.
Suppose given a quasi-coherent contramodule
\mathcal{P}
over
\mathcal{C}
in
the sense of the definition above.
We would argue that there is
no
way to restrict
\mathcal{P}
to
an open subscheme
U\subset X
, at least, staying on the level of
abelian or exact (rather than derived) categories.

Indeed, let
j\colon U\longrightarrow X
denote the open immersion morphism.
Given a contraaction map
\pi\colon\qHom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
, one
can apply the inverse image functor
j^{*}
and obtain the induced
map
j^{*}\pi\colon j^{*}\qHom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow j^{*}\mathcal{P}
.
How does one pass from that to a map
\qHom_{U{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(j^{*}\mathcal{C},j^{*}\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow j^{*}\mathcal{P}
?
The natural map between the quasi-coherent sheaves

j^{*}\qHom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})
and
\qHom_{U{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(j^{*}\mathcal{C},j^{*}\mathcal{P})
goes in
the other direction:
j^{*}\qHom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\qHom_{U{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(j^{*}\mathcal{C},j^{*}\mathcal{P})
.

Notice that, in any event, the category of quasi-coherent
contramodules over
\mathcal{C}
(as defined above) is abelian with an exact
forgetful functor to
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}

if and only if
the functor

\Hom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},{-})\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
is exact.
The latter condition is seldom satisfied.
For example, if
\mathcal{C}\simeq\coprod_{\xi\in\Xi}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is isomorphic, as
an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, to a coproduct of an infinite number of copies of
the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, then the functor
\Hom_{X{\operatorname{\mathrm{-qc}}}}(\mathcal{C},{-})

is isomorphic to the functor of infinite product of copies of
a quasi-coherent sheaf
\prod_{\xi\in\Xi}({-})\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
.
The latter functor is rarely exact.
So one is not likely to obtain an abelian category of quasi-coherent
contramodules.

All in all, someone may want to try to pick up this definition of
a “quasi-coherent contramodule over a quasi-coherent coalgebra”
and build a theory of it.
But from our perspective it appears to be less promising than
the notion of a
contraherent cosheaf
on
X
with
a
\mathcal{C}
-contramodule structure.
The latter can be defined using the notion of the functor

\Cohom
from a quasi-coherent sheaf to a contraherent cosheaf;
see
[
61
, Section 2.4]
and Section
5.5
below.
Notice that some conditions must be imposed either on
a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
, or on a contraherent cosheaf
\mathfrak{P}
,
or on both, to make the construction of the contraherent cosheaf

\Cohom_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{P})
well-defined.

2.
Why Contraherent Cosheaves? Torsion and Derived Complete
Modules

2.1.
Basics

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
I\subset R
be an ideal.
Then an
R
-module
M
is said to be
I
-torsion
if
for each elements
m\in M
and
s\in I
there exists an integer

n\geq 1
such that
s^{n}m=0
.
When the ideal
I
is finitely generated, this condition can be
rephrased by saying that for every element
m\in M
there exists
an integer
n\geq 1
such that
I^{n}m=0
.

The full subcategory of
I
-torsion
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}

is closed under direct sums, submodules, quotients, and extensions
(in other words, it is a hereditary torsion class) in the ambient
abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
In particular, the category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}
is abelian and
the inclusion functor
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is exact.
The inclusion functor has a right adjoint functor
\Gamma_{I}\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}
assigning to every
R
-module
M

its maximal
I
-torsion submodule
\Gamma_{I}(M)\subset M
.

Assume that the ideal
I\subset R
is finitely generated.
Then it makes sense to consider the
I
-adic completion functor

P\longmapsto\Lambda_{I}(P)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}P/I^{n}P
on the category of

R
-modules (cf.
[
113
, Theorem 3.6]
).
An
R
-module
P
is said to be
I
-adically separated

if the natural map
\lambda_{I,P}\colon P\longrightarrow\Lambda_{I}(P)
is
injective; and
P
is said to be
I
-adically complete
if
the map
\lambda_{I,P}
is surjective.

The full subcategory of
I
-adically separated and complete

R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-secmp}}}}\allowbreak\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is usually

not
abelian
[
65
, Example 2.7(1)]
, and generally not
well-behaved from the homological algebra perspective.
This is related to the fact that the functor
\Lambda_{I}
, constructed
as a composition of a right exact and a left exact functor, is not
even exact in the middle
[
113
, Example 3.20]
.
Well behaved (abelian) replacements of the category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-secmp}}}}

are certain slightly larger full subcategories in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
, i. e.,
intermediate categories between
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-secmp}}}}
and
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.

Two reasonably well-behaved replacements of the category of

I
-adically separated and complete
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-secmp}}}}

are known.
The more popular one is called the category of
derived

I
-adically complete
R
-modules in the sequential sense
in
the language of
[
115
]
and the category of
I
-contramodule

R
-modules
in the language of
[
63
,
65
,
87
,
72
]
.
The other one is called the category of
derived
I
-adically
complete
R
-modules in the idealistic sense
in the language
of
[
115
]
and the category of
quotseparated

I
-contramodule
R
-modules
in the language of
[
87
,
72
]
.

An
R
-module
P
is said to be
an
I
-contramodule

[
63
, Section 2]
,

[
65
, Sections 2, 5, 7, and 9]
,
[

(or

[

,
[

for every element

2.2.
Discussion

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a Noetherian formal scheme
[
34
, Section II.9]
.
How does one globalize the definitions of the categories

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}
and
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctra}}}}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctra}}}}^{\mathsf{qs}}
from
Section
2.1
to a formal scheme

\mathfrak{X}
replacing a Noetherian commutative ring
R
with an ideal
I
?

To simplify the discussion, let us assume that
\mathfrak{X}
is covered by
two affine Noetherian formal schemes
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{U}_{2}

(with an affine intersection
\mathfrak{V}=\mathfrak{U}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{U}_{2}
).
As a further simplification, assume that
\mathfrak{V}
is a principal open
affine both in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
and in
\mathfrak{U}_{2}
.

This means that we are given two Noetherian commutative rings

R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, two ideals
I_{\alpha}\subset R_{\alpha}
and two elements

r_{\alpha}\in R_{\alpha}
,
\alpha=1
,
2
.
We are also given an isomorphism of topological commutative rings

(1)

\varprojlim\nolimits_{n\geq 1}(R_{1}[r_{1}^{-1}]/I_{1}^{n}[r_{1}^{-1}])\simeq\varprojlim\nolimits_{n\geq 1}(R_{2}[r_{2}^{-1}]/I_{2}^{n}[r_{2}^{-1}])

with the adic topologies.
The affine formal schemes
\mathfrak{U}_{\alpha}
are the formal spectra of
the topological rings
\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}R_{\alpha}/I_{\alpha}^{n}
with
their adic topologies,
\mathfrak{U}_{\alpha}=\Spf\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}R_{\alpha}/I_{\alpha}^{n}
,
and the intersection
\mathfrak{V}=\mathfrak{U}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{U}_{2}
is the formal spectrum of
the topological ring (
1
).
The whole formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is the union
\mathfrak{U}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{U}_{2}
.

What should one mean by a torsion module over
\mathfrak{X}
, and what should
one mean by a contramodule over
\mathfrak{X}
?

Let us start with the torsion modules.
Given an
I_{\alpha}
-torsion
R_{\alpha}
-module
M_{\alpha}
,
the localization
M_{\alpha}[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]=R_{\alpha}[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]\otimes_{R_{\alpha}}M_{\alpha}
is
an
I_{\alpha}[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]
-torsion

R_{\alpha}[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]
-module.
So it seems to be quite natural to define a
quasi-coherent
torsion sheaf
\mathcal{M}
over
\mathfrak{X}
as a pair
\mathcal{M}=(M_{1},M_{2})
of

I_{\alpha}
-torsion
R_{\alpha}
-modules
M_{\alpha}
together with
an isomorphism

M_{1}[r_{1}^{-1}]\simeq M_{2}[r_{2}^{-1}]

of discrete/torsion modules over the topological
ring (
1
).
This definition of a quasi-coherent torsion sheaf generalizes to
many ind-schemes
[
7
, Section 7.11.4]
,
[
74
, Chapter 2]
;
the whole theory developed in the book
[
74
]
is based on
the ind-scheme version of this definition.

Passing to the contramodules, we start with the observation that
the functor
R_{\alpha}[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]\otimes_{R_{\alpha}}{-}\,\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow R[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
does
not
take

I_{\alpha}
-contramodules to
I_{\alpha}[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]
-contramodules.

Example 2.1
.

Let
k
be a field and
R=k[x,y]
be the ring of polynomials in two
variables over
k
.
Put
I=(x)\subset R
and
r=y\in R
.
Then
P=\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}R/x^{n}R
is an
I
-contramodule
R
-module
(in fact, it is the
free
I
-contramodule
R
-module with one
generator
).
Still, the
R
-module
P[y^{-1}]
is
not
an
I
-contramodule

R
-module, and the
R[y^{-1}]
-module
P[y^{-1}]
is
not

an
I[y^{-1}]
-contramodule
R[y^{-1}]
-module.

The point is that
P[y^{-1}]
is
x
-adically separated, but it is

not

x
-adically complete.
In fact, one has

(2)

P[y^{-1}]=\bigl(\varprojlim\nolimits_{n\geq 1}k[x,y]/x^{n}k[x,y]\bigr)[y^{-1}]\subsetneq\varprojlim\nolimits_{n\geq 1}k[x,y,y^{-1}]/x^{n}k[x,y,y^{-1}].

For example, the expression
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}/y^{n}
is
element of the right-hand side, but not of the left-hand side
of (
2
).
The right-hand side of (
2
)
is the
x
-adic completion of the left-hand side.

Since all contramodules are adically complete (though they need not
be adically separated)
[
65
, Theorem 5.6]
, it follows that

P[y^{-1}]

cannot
be either an
I
-contramodule
R
-module
or an
I[y^{-1}]
-contramodule
R[y^{-1}]
-module.
(See
[
114
]
for a weaker result which is also sufficient for
the purposes of this example.)

What should be meant by a
quasi-coherent sheaf of contramodules
over
\mathfrak{X}
?
Suppose given an
I_{1}
-contramodule
R_{1}
-module
P_{1}
and

I_{2}
-contramodule
R_{2}
-module
P_{2}
.
So one can say that
P_{1}
is a contramodule over
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
and

P_{2}
is a contramodule over
\mathfrak{U}_{2}
.
What could it mean that the localizations of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
agree
over
\mathfrak{V}=\mathfrak{U}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{U}_{2}
?

Asking for an isomorphism
P_{1}[r_{1}^{-1}]\simeq P_{2}[r_{2}^{-1}]
does
not seem to make sense.
Isomorphic as objects
of what category
?
Example
2.1
clearly shows that

P_{\alpha}[r_{\alpha}^{-1}]
need not be even modules over
the ring (
1
).

One could ask for an isomorphism

\Delta_{I_{1}[r_{1}^{-1}]}\bigl(P_{1}[r_{1}^{-1}]\bigr)\simeq\Delta_{I_{2}[r_{2}^{-1}]}\bigl(P_{2}[r_{2}^{-1}]\bigr)

of contramodules over the topological
ring (
1
).
This condition is, at least, well-defined; but is it well-behaved?

If one wants to prove that the resulting category of “quasi-coherent
sheaves of contramodules on
\mathfrak{X}
” is abelian, it would be convenient
to know that the gluing functors are exact.
The functor
\Delta_{I}
is certainly
not
exact in most cases
(but only right exact, being a left adjoint).
Is the functor

(3)

P\longmapsto\Delta_{I[r^{-1}]}\bigl(P[r^{-1}]\bigr)\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctra}}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muR[r^{-1}]{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I[r^{-1}]{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctra}}}}

exact for every Noetherian commutative ring
R
with an ideal

I\subset R
and an element
r\in R
?
It would be interesting to find out.

What can one say about
the functor (
3
) ?
It is the functor of contraextension of scalars

f^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}

[
69
, Section 2.9]

with respect to the natural morphism of topological rings

f\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, where
\mathfrak{R}=\Delta_{I}(R)=\Lambda_{I}(R)
and

\mathfrak{S}=\Lambda_{I[r^{-1}]}(R[r^{-1}])
.
But this does not yet seem to help much.

If the functor (
3
) is
not

exact, then it is not even clear how to define a reasonable exact
category structure on the category of “quasi-coherent sheaves
of contramodules over
\mathfrak{X}
”.
Perhaps one should better restrict the gluing construction to
the full subcategories of
I_{\alpha}
-contramodule
R_{\alpha}
-modules
adjusted to (
3
).

It is well-known and easy to show that the functor
\Lambda_{I}

takes short exact sequences of flat modules to short exact
sequences
[
56
, Lemma 3.5]
.
Consequently, the natural morphism
\mathbb{L}_{0}\Lambda_{I}(F)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{I}(F)
is an isomorphism for all flat
R
-modules
F

[
56
, Proposition 3.6]
; so the functor

\Delta_{I}=\mathbb{L}_{0}\Lambda_{I}
also preserves exactness of short
exact sequences of flat modules.

This allows one to define an exact category of
quasi-coherent
sheaves of flat contramodules on
\mathfrak{X}
by restricting the gluing
construction above to
R_{\alpha}
-flat
I_{\alpha}
-contramodule

R_{\alpha}
-modules.
This definition generalizes naturally to
ind-schemes
[
7
, Section 7.11.3]
; the resulting category is
called the category of
flat pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves

in
[
74
, Section 3]
.
But flat modules are only a tiny subcategory in the category of
all modules.

From our perspective, a more promising approach is to glue

I_{\alpha}
-contramodule
R_{\alpha}
-modules using
the
colocalization
rather than the localization functors.
One starts with the following helpful observation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring,
I\subset R
be a (finitely generated)
ideal, and
r\in R
be an element.
Then, for any
I
-contramodule
R
-module
P
, the colocalization

\Hom_{R}(R[r^{-1}],P)
is an
I[r^{-1}]
-contramodule

R[r^{-1}]
-module.

Proof.

An
R[r^{-1}]
-module is an
I[r^{-1}]
-contramodule if and only if
its underlying
R
-module is
an
I
-contramodule
[
65
, Theorem 5.1]
; so it suffices to
show that
\Hom_{R}(R[r^{-1}],P)
is an
I
-contramodule
R
-module.
This is a particular case of
[
65
, Lemma 6.1(b)]
.
Simply put, for any
R
-module
M
, the
R
-module
\Hom_{R}(M,P)

can be obtained from
P
using the operations of kernel and
infinite product; and the full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctra}}}}
is
closed under kernels and infinite products in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
∎

So one can define a
contraherent cosheaf of
contramodules over
\mathfrak{X}
as a pair
\mathfrak{P}=(P_{1},P_{2})
of

I_{\alpha}
-contramodule
R_{\alpha}
-modules
P_{\alpha}
together
with an isomorphism

\Hom_{R_{1}}(R_{1}[r_{1}^{-1}],P_{1})\simeq\Hom_{R_{2}}(R_{2}[r_{2}^{-1}],P_{2})

of contramodules over
the ring (
1
) with
its defining ideal of the adic topology
(cf. the last paragraph of
Section
2.1
).
To make this definition well-behaved, one needs to assume adjustedness
to the colocalization, i. e., impose the
contraadjustedness

condition
[
61
, Section 1.1]
,
[
65
, Section 2]

\Ext^{1}_{R_{1}}(R_{1}[r_{1}^{-1}],P_{1})=0=\Ext^{1}_{R_{2}}(R_{2}[r_{2}^{-1}],P_{2}).

The question can and should be asked as to why there are enough

I_{\alpha}
-contramodule
R_{\alpha}
-modules satisfying
the contraadjustedness condition.
Similar questions were addressed in
[
61
, Sections C.2–C.3
and D.4–D.5]
and
[
85
, Section 7]
;
see also
[
87
, Section 5]
.
As the output of the definition above, one obtains
the
exact category of contraherent cosheaves of contramodules
over
\mathfrak{X}
.

The reader should be
warned
that the definition of
a “contraherent cosheaf of contramodules on
\mathfrak{X}
” as spelled out
above
depends
on the choice of an affine open covering

\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{U}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{U}_{2}
.
Actually, it depends on it for two reasons.

Firstly, the (very weak) contraadjustedness condition as formulated
above depends on the covering.
This can be resolved by making the contraadjustedness condition more
restrictive, or more specifically, assuming contraadjustedness with
respect to all elements
r\in R_{\alpha}
rather than only
r=r_{\alpha}
.

Secondly, and more unpleasantly, the contraherence property is
not local
[
61
, Example 3.2.1]
.
See the discussion of
locally contraherent cosheaves

in
[
61
, Sections 3.1–3.2]
and
Remark
5.4
below.
So one needs to deal with the whole family of
exact categories
of locally contraherent cosheaves of contramodules on
\mathfrak{X}
with
respect to varying open coverings, establishing the fact that these
exact categories “do not differ too much from each
other”
[
61
, Lemma 4.6.1(b)]
and relying on it.

3.
Why Contraherent Cosheaves? Coderived and Contraderived
Categories

3.1.
Basics

Let
R
be a ring.
For the purposes of the discussion in
the next Section
3.2
,
the ring
R
can be assumed to be commutative for simplicity,
though this assumption is not needed in the present section.

There are two approaches to defining the
coderived
and
the
contraderived category
of
R
-modules in the current
literature.
The coderived and contraderived categories
in the sense of
Positselski

[
57
,
58
,
16
,
60
,
61
,
64
,
67
,
90
,
71
,
75
]

have more elementary flavor, while the coderived and contraderived
categories
in the sense of
Becker

[
38
,
42
,
36
,
51
,
6
,
107
,
92
,
93
,
95
]
have more
set-theoretic flavour.

It is still an open problem whether the two approaches
ever

do not agree with each other within their common domain of
applicability
[
67
, Examples 2.5(3) and 2.6(3)]
.
We refer to
[
92
, Remark 9.2]
and
[
76
, Section 7]
for
a historical and philosophical discussion.

Derived categories of the second kind
is a generic name for
the coderived and contraderived categories.

Let
\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
denote the homotopy category of unbounded
complexes of
R
-modules.
Any short exact sequence of complexes of
R
-modules can be viewed
as a bicomplex with three rows, so a total complex (totalization)
can be attached to it.

A complex of
R
-modules is said to be
coacyclic in the sense
of Positselski
if it belongs to the minimal full triangulated
subcategory of
\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
containing the totalizations of short
exact sequences of
R
-modules and
closed under infinite direct
sums
.
The full triangulated subcategory of Positselski-coacyclic complexes
is denoted by
\Ac^{\mathsf{pco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})\subset\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
, and the related
triangulated Verdier quotient category

\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{pco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})=\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})/\Ac^{\mathsf{pco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})

is called the
coderived category of
R
-modules in the sense
of Positselski
.

A complex of
R
-modules is said to be
contraacyclic in
the sense of Positselski
if it belongs to the minimal full triangulated
subcategory of
\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
containing the totalizations of short
exact sequences of
R
-modules and
closed under infinite direct
products
.
The full triangulated subcategory of Positselski-contraacyclic
complexes is denoted by
\Ac^{\mathsf{pctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})\subset\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
, and
the related triangulated Verdier quotient category

\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{pctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})=\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})/\Ac^{\mathsf{pctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})

is called the
contraderived category of
R
-modules in the sense
of Positselski
.

The
coderived category of
R
-modules in the sense of Becker

can be simply defined as the homotopy category of unbounded complexes
of injective
R
-modules

(4)

\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})=\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{inj}}).

A more sophisticated approach is to define the
coacyclic
complexes of
R
-modules in the sense of Becker
as such complexes

A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\in\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
that
\Hom_{\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})}(A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})\allowbreak=0
for all complexes of injective
R
-modules

J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\in\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{inj}})
.
The full triangulated subcategory of Becker-coacyclic complexes
is denoted by
\Ac^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})\subset\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
.
Then the coderived category in the sense of Becker can be defined as
the related Verdier quotient category

(5)

\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})=\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})/\Ac^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}).

It is a known theorem that
the definitions (
4
)
and (
5
) agree.
In other words, the composition of functors

\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{inj}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})/\Ac^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})

is a triangulated equivalence
[
6
, Proposition 1.3.6(2)]
,

[
52
, Theorem 2.13]
,
[
43
, Corollary 5.13]
,

[
92
, Corollary 9.5]
,
[
93
, Corollary 7.9]
.

The
contraderived category of
R
-modules in the sense of
Becker
can be simply defined as the homotopy category of unbounded
complexes of projective
R
-modules

(6)

\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})=\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}).

A more sophisticated approach is to define the
contraacyclic
complexes of
R
-modules in the sense of Becker
as such complexes

B^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\in\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
that
\Hom_{\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})}(P^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},B^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})=0

for all complexes of projective
R
-modules

P^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\in\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}})
.
The full triangulated subcategory of Becker-contraacyclic complexes
is denoted by
\Ac^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})\subset\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
.
Then the contraderived category in the sense of Becker can be defined
as the related Verdier quotient category

(7)

\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})=\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})/\Ac^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}).

It is a known theorem that
the definitions (
6
)
and (

) agree.
In other words, the composition of functors

\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})/\Ac^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})

is a triangulated equivalence

,

[

,
[

,

[

.

It is clear from
[

or

(see also

) that
any coacyclic complex in the sense of Positselski is also

Similarly,
[

The Positselski and Becker approaches are known to agree under

.

All (Becker or Positselski) coacyclic complexes of modules are

All (Becker or Positselski) contraacyclic complexes of modules are

So both the coderived and the contraderived category are larger

Remark 3.1

If the coproduct functors are not exact in an abelian category

Hence the functors of cohomology of complexes would not be defined

Dually, if the product functors are not exact in an abelian category

runs into the problem that some contraacyclic

To the present author, this appears as undesirable behavior.

4.
Category-Theoretic and Module-Theoretic Preliminaries

The notion of a
cotorsion pair
(also known as
a
cotorsion theory
) plays a key role in the theory of
contraherent cosheaves.
In this section, we spell out the abstract definition in
category-theoretic context, as well as two main concrete examples:
the
flat cotorsion pair
in the category of modules over
an associative ring, and the
very flat cotorsion pair
in
the category of modules over a commutative ring.

4.1.
Cotorsion pairs in exact categories

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category (in the sense of Quillen); see
the survey
[
11
]
for the background.
A discussion of the Yoneda Ext groups in exact categories can be
found in the paper
[
59
, Section A.7]
.

Let
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{C}\subset\mathsf{E}
be two classes of objects.
The notation
\mathsf{F}^{\perp_{1}}\subset\mathsf{E}
stands for the class of
all objects
X\in\mathsf{E}
such that
\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{E}}(F,X)=0
for all
F\in\mathsf{F}
.
Dually,
{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathsf{C}\subset\mathsf{E}
is the class of all objects

Y\in\mathsf{E}
such that
\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{E}}(Y,C)=0
for all
C\in\mathsf{C}
.

Similarly,
\mathsf{F}^{\perp_{\geq 1}}\subset\mathsf{E}
is the class of all objects

X\in\mathsf{E}
such that
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{E}}(F,X)=0
for all
F\in\mathsf{F}
and
n\geq 1
.
Dually,
{}^{\perp_{\geq 1}}\mathsf{C}\subset\mathsf{E}
is the class of all objects

Y\in\mathsf{E}
such that
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{E}}(Y,C)=0
for all
C\in\mathsf{C}
and
n\geq 1
.

A pair of classes of objects
\mathsf{F}
,
\mathsf{C}\subset\mathsf{E}
is called
a
cotorsion pair
if
\mathsf{C}=\mathsf{F}^{\perp_{1}}
and

\mathsf{F}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathsf{C}
.
Informally, one thinks of a cotorsion pair as a pair of classes of
“somewhat projective objects” and “somewhat injective objects”
that fit together or complement each other in a suitable sense
(see the introduction to the paper
[
73
]
for a discussion).

For example the pairs of classes (all objects, injective objects)
and (projective objects, all objects) are two trivial examples of
cotorsion pairs in an arbitrary exact category.
The following definition tells what it means that “there are
enough projective/injective objects” in the context of
a cotorsion pair.

A cotorsion pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})
in
\mathsf{E}
is said to be
complete

if for every object
E\in\mathsf{E}
there exist admissible short exact
sequences

(8)

\displaystyle 0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{\prime}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muF\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muE\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu0

(9)

\displaystyle 0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muE\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muF^{\prime}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu0

in
\mathsf{E}
with
F
,
F^{\prime}\in\mathsf{F}
and
C
,
C^{\prime}\in\mathsf{C}
.
The sequence (
8
) is known as
a
special precover sequence
, and
the sequence (
9
) is called
a
special preenvelope sequence
.
Collectively, the sequences (
8
–
9
) are referred to as

approximation sequences
.

When there are enough injective and projective objects in
an exact category
\mathsf{E}
(e. g.,
\mathsf{E}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
with the abelian
exact structure), the condition on a cotorsion pair in
\mathsf{E}
to be
complete is still nontrivial.
But, at least, for any cotorsion pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})
in
\mathsf{E}
, one can
say that all the projective objects of
\mathsf{E}
belong to
\mathsf{F}
, and
all the injective objects of
\mathsf{E}
belong to
\mathsf{C}
.
So every object of
\mathsf{E}
is an admissible quotient object of
an object from
\mathsf{F}
and an admissible subobject of an object
from
\mathsf{C}
.
These weak forms of the completeness condition on a cotorsion pair
are not guaranteed in an abelian/exact category
\mathsf{E}
in general;
so they need to be assumed in the next definition.

A class of objects
\mathsf{F}
in an exact category
\mathsf{E}
is said to be

generating
if for every object
E\in\mathsf{E}
there exists an object

F\in\mathsf{F}
together with an admissible epimorphism

F\twoheadrightarrow E
in
\mathsf{E}
.
Dually, a class of objects
\mathsf{C}
in an exact category
\mathsf{E}
is said
to be
cogenerating
if for every object
E\in\mathsf{E}
there exists
an object
C\in\mathsf{C}
together with an admissible monomorphism

E\rightarrowtail C
in
\mathsf{E}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})
be a cotorsion pair in an exact category
\mathsf{E}
.
Assume that the class
\mathsf{F}
is generating and the class
\mathsf{C}
is
cogenerating in
\mathsf{E}
.
Then the following four conditions are equivalent:

(1)

the class
\mathsf{F}
is closed under kernels of admissible
epimorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
;

(2)

the class
\mathsf{C}
is closed under cokernels of admissible
monomorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
;

(3)

\Ext^{2}_{\mathsf{E}}(F,C)=0
for all
F\in\mathsf{F}
and
C\in\mathsf{C}
;

(4)

\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{E}}(F,C)=0
for all
F\in\mathsf{F}
,
C\in\mathsf{C}
, and

n\geq 1
.

Proof.

This result, going back to
[
23
, Theorem 1.2.10]
,
can be found in various formulations in
[
26
, Lemma 5.24]
,

[
98
, Lemma 4.25]
,
[
105
, Lemma 6.17]
,
and
[
92
, Lemma 1.4]
.
For a generalization, see
[
94
, Lemma 6.1]
.
∎

A cotorsion pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})
satisfying the equivalent conditions of
Lemma
4.1
is called
hereditary
.

For any class of objects
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{E}
, the pair of classes

(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})
with
\mathsf{C}=\mathsf{S}^{\perp_{1}}
and
\mathsf{F}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathsf{C}
is
a cotorsion pair in
\mathsf{E}
.
The cotorsion pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})=({}^{\perp_{1}}(\mathsf{S}^{\perp_{1}}),\>\mathsf{S}^{\perp_{1}})
is said to be
generated by
the class of
objects
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{E}
.

4.2.
Eklof–Trlifaj theorem

In this section, for the sake of simplicity of exposition we restrict
ourselves to the classical case of the module category
\mathsf{E}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}

with the abelian exact structure.
In fact, the main results hold in much greater generality.

Let
R
be an associative ring.
Let
F
be an
R
-module and
\alpha
be an ordinal.
An
\alpha
-indexed filtration
on
F
is a family of submodules

(F_{\beta}\subset F)_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}
satisfying the following
conditions:

•

F_{0}=0
and
F_{\alpha}=F
;

•

for all ordinals
0\leq\gamma\leq\beta\leq\alpha
, one has

F_{\gamma}\subset F_{\beta}
;

•

for all limit ordinals
0<\beta\leq\alpha
, one has

F_{\beta}=\bigcup_{\gamma<\beta}F_{\gamma}
.

An
R
-module
F
endowed with an
\alpha
-indexed filtration

(F_{\beta}\subset F)_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}
is said to be

filtered by
the quotient modules
F_{\beta+1}/F_{\beta}
,

0\leq\beta<\alpha
.

Given a class of objects
\mathsf{S}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
, an
R
-module
F

is said to be
filtered by
\mathsf{S}
if there exists
an ordinal
\alpha
and an
\alpha
-indexed filtration

(F_{\beta}\subset F)_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}
on
F
such that, for
every
0\leq\beta<\alpha
, the
R
-module
F_{\beta+1}/F_{\beta}

is isomorphic to an
R
-module from
\mathsf{S}
.
In another language, the
R
-module
F
is said to be
a
transfinitely iterated extension
(
in the sense of
the direct limit
) of
R
-modules from
\mathsf{S}
.

The class of all
R
-modules filtered by modules from
\mathsf{S}

is denoted by
\Fil(\mathsf{S})\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
A class of
R
-modules
\mathsf{F}
is said to be
deconstructible

if there exists a
set
(rather than a proper class) of

R
-modules
\mathsf{S}
such that
\mathsf{F}=\Fil(\mathsf{S})
.

The following observation is known as the
Eklof lemma
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any class of objects
\mathsf{C}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
, the class

{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathsf{C}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is closed under transfinitely iterated
extensions (in the sense of the direct limit).
So one has
\Fil({}^{\perp_{1}}\mathsf{C})={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathsf{C}
.

Proof.

The classical references are
[
18
, Lemma 1]

or
[
26
, Lemma 6.2]
.
The result is actually much more general and, properly understood,
holds in any abelian category
[
85
, Lemma 4.5]
,

[
92
, Proposition 1.3]
, and even in any exact
category
[
94
, Lemma 6.5]
.
∎

Given a class of objects
\mathsf{F}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
, let us denote by

\mathsf{F}^{\oplus}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
the class of all direct summands of
objects from
\mathsf{F}
.
The following important result is called the
Eklof–Trlifaj
theorem

[
18
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathsf{S}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
be a
set
(rather than a proper class)
of modules. Then

(a)
the cotorsion pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})=({}^{\perp_{1}}(\mathsf{S}^{\perp_{1}}),\>\mathsf{S}^{\perp_{1}})
generated by
\mathsf{S}

in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is complete;

(b)
one has
\mathsf{F}=\Fil(\{{}_{R}R\}\cup\mathsf{S})^{\oplus}
, where

{}_{R}R
is the free
R
-module with one generator.

Proof.

The classical references are
[
18
, Theorems 2 and 10]

or
[
26
, Theorem 6.11 and Corollary 6.14]
.
The result is actually more general and, properly stated, holds in
any
efficient exact category

[
98
, Corollary 2.15]
,

[
105
, Theorem 5.16]
, and also in any locally presentable
abelian category
[
85
, Corollary 3.6 and Theorem 4.8]
,

[
92
, Theorems 3.3 and 3.4]
.
See
[
85
, Section 1.3]
or
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
for a discussion.
∎

4.3.
Two cotorsion pairs in module categories

Let
R
be an associative ring.
A left
R
-module
C
is said to be

The following theorem is essentially one of the formulations of

Proof.

The nontrivial part is the complete cotorsion pair assertion;

Then

Main Definition and Main Technical Problem

5.1.
Quasi-coherent sheaves

Let
X
be a scheme.
The definition of a contraherent cosheaf on
X
in
the preprint
[
61
]
is modeled after the following description
of quasi-coherent sheaves on
X

[
21
, Section 2]
.

A quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{M}
on
X
is the same thing as the following
set of data:

•

to every affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, an
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module

\mathcal{M}(U)
is assigned;

•

to every pair of embedded affine open subschemes
V\subset U\subset X
, a homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules
\mathcal{M}(U)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(V)

is assigned

satisfying the following conditions:

•

for every pair of embedded affine open subschemes
V\subset U\subset X
, the homomorphism
\mathcal{M}(U)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(V)
induces an isomorphism
of
\mathcal{O}(V)
-modules

\mathcal{O}(V)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(U)}\mathcal{M}(U)\simeq\mathcal{M}(V);

•

for every triple of embedded affine open subschemes
W\subset V\subset U\subset X
, the triangular diagram of homomorphisms of

\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules

\mathcal{M}(U)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{M}(V)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{M}(W)

is commutative.

The definition of a contraherent cosheaf on
X
is dual to
this definition of a quasi-coherent sheaf.
But there are delicate details and caveats.

5.2.
Terminological remark

From today’s perspective, some terminological choices in the long
preprint
[
61
]
appear to be not optimal.
Apparently, the significance of the colocalization functors

\Hom_{R}(S,{-})
and the related terminology in the context of
the contraherent cosheaf theory, emphasized in the abstract and
the introduction to the present paper, was not properly realized at
the time when the book manuscript
[
61
]
was written.

What appears today as the proper terminological solutions were
suggested in the recent preprint
[
77
]
.
Perhaps it would be better to use the terms “colocally
contraadjusted” and “colocally cotorsion” for what are called
“locally contraadjusted” and “locally cotorsion contraherent
cosheaves” both in
[
61
]
and in the present paper.

The terminology of “colocally projective” and “colocally flat
contraherent cosheaves” used in
[
61
, Sections 4.2–4.3]

is particularly problematic.
The word “colocal” in these terms was not used in the sense
of a reference to the colocalization functors.
Rather, it meant something like “opposite or complementary to local”.
The term “antilocal” was suggested for this purpose in
[
77
]
.

So, later in this paper
(in Section
8.5
), we will use the term

antilocal contraherent cosheaves
for what were called
“colocally projective contraherent cosheaves” in
[
61
]
.
In the same vein, what were called “colocally flat contraherent
cosheaves” in
[
61
]
should be called
antilocally flat
.

5.3.
Locally contraadjusted contraherent cosheaves

Let
X
be a scheme.
A
contraherent cosheaf

\mathfrak{P}
on
X

[
61
, Section 2.2]

is the following set of data:

•

to every affine open subscheme
U\subset X
,
an
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathfrak{P}[U]
is assigned;

•

to every pair of embedded affine open subschemes
V\subset U\subset X
, a homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules
\mathfrak{P}[V]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}[U]

is assigned

satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

for every pair of embedded affine open subschemes
V\subset U\subset X
, the homomorphism
\mathfrak{P}[V]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}[U]
induces
an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}(V)
-modules

\mathfrak{P}[V]\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{O}(U)}(\mathcal{O}(V),\mathfrak{P}[U]);

(ii)

for every pair of embedded affine open subschemes
V\subset U\subset X
, one has

\Ext_{\mathcal{O}(U)}^{1}(\mathcal{O}(V),\mathfrak{P}[U])=0;

(iii)

for every triple of embedded affine open subschemes
W\subset V\subset U\subset X
, the triangular diagram of homomorphisms of

\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules

\mathfrak{P}[W]\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathfrak{P}[V]\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathfrak{P}[U]

is commutative.

Remark 5.1
.

No higher Ext vanishing condition (
\Ext^{n}=0
for
n\geq 2
) is
mentioned in (ii), because these conditions hold automatically.
In fact, for any open immersion of affine schemes
V\longrightarrow U
,
the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
has projective dimension at most
1
.
This assertion can be explained in several ways.

On the one hand, the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
is very flat by
Example
4.6
.
All very flat modules over a commutative ring
R
have projective
dimension at most
1
by Theorem
4.5
(d).

On the other hand, any countably presented flat module over
an associative ring
R
has projective dimension at most
1

[
26
, Corollary 2.23]
.
The assertion that the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
is countably
presented can be also explained in various ways.

One approach is to observe that the
R
-module
R[s^{-1}]
is
flat and countably presented for any element
s
in a commutative
ring
R
.
Then use the Čech coresolution as in
[
61
, proof of
Lemma 1.2.4]
and the
flat coherence

lemma
[
80
, Lemma 4.1]
,
[
81
, Corollary 10.12]
,

[
82
, Corollary 4.7]
.

Alternatively, countable presentability of the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module

\mathcal{O}(V)
follows immediately from finite presentability of
the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(V)
.
For the latter claim, we refer to
[
30
, Definition I.6.2.1,
Proposition I.6.2.6(i), and Proposition I.6.2.9]
.

Remark 5.2
.

Condition (ii) can be equivalently restated by saying that
the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathfrak{P}[U]
has to be contraadjusted
(in the sense of the definition in
Section
4.3
).
Indeed, put
R=\mathcal{O}(U)
; then, for every element
s\in R
,
the scheme
V=\Spec R[s^{-1}]
is an affine open subscheme in
U
,
and consequently in
X
.
So (ii) implies the contraadjustedness of
\mathfrak{P}[U]
.
Conversely, for any affine open subscheme
V\subset U
,
the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
is very flat by
Example

.
Hence
\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{O}(U)}(\mathcal{O}(V),C)=0
for every contraadjusted

\mathcal{O}(U)
-module

.

For this reason, the contraherent cosheaves on
X
in the sense
of the definition above are called
locally contraadjusted
.
Condition (ii) is called the
contraadjustedness condition
,
while condition (i) is called the
contraherence condition
.

Remark 5.3
.

It needs to be explained what the word “cosheaf” means in
the definition of a contraherent cosheaf given above.
No “cosheaf axiom” appears in that definition; there are only
the contraherence and contraadjustedness axioms.

The same question can (and should) be asked about the definition
or description of quasi-coherent sheaves from
Section
5.1
(going back
to
[
21
, Section 2]
), which contains no sheaf axiom, but only
a quasi-coherence axiom.

The answer to this question (for contraherent cosheaves) is
explained in
[
61
, Sections 2.1–2.2]
.
Essentially, the point is that it suffices to define a (co)sheaf
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules on any topology base
\mathbf{B}
of a ringed space

(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
, and it suffices to check the (co)sheaf axiom for coverings
of open subsets belonging to
\mathbf{B}
by other open subsets belonging
to
\mathbf{B}

[
30
, Section 0.3.2]
,
[
61
, Theorem 2.1.2]
.
In the situation at hand, one takes
\mathbf{B}
to be the topology
base consisting of all affine open subschemes of a scheme
X
.

Furthermore, restricted to coverings of affine open subschemes by
afine open subschemes, the sheaf/cosheaf axiom follows from
the quasi-coherence/contraherence axiom.
In the case of the cosheaves, one also needs to use
the contraadjustedness axiom
[
61
, Theorem 2.2.1]
.
So any contraherent cosheaf on a scheme
X
in the sense of
the definition above can be uniquely extended a cosheaf of

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules defined on all open subsets of
X
and satisfying
the cosheaf axiom for all coverings of open subsets by other
open subsets.

Remark 5.4
.

So one can start with a cosheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
\mathfrak{P}
on
a scheme
X
, and consider the contraherence and contraadjustedness
axioms (i–ii) as conditions imposed on a cosheaf
\mathfrak{P}
.
This is the approach taken in the exposition in
[
61
]
.
Then the question about a local or nonlocal nature of these axioms
arises.

In this context, it turns out that the notion of quasi-coherent
sheaf is indeed local, but the notion of a contraherent cosheaf
is
not
.
Specifically, the contraadjustedness axiom (ii) is still local, but

not
the contraherence axiom (i).
This is explained in
[
61
, Sections 3.1–3.2]
; see
specifically
[
61
, Example 3.2.1]
.

In other words, given an open covering
X=U\cup V
of a scheme

by two open subschemes
U
and
V
, any two quasi-coherent sheaves
on
U
and
V
that agree on the intersection

can be glued
together into a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
.
But two contraherent cosheaves on
U
and
V
that agree on
U\cap V

can be only glued into a
locally contraherent cosheaf
on
X
,
generally speaking.

Locally contraherent cosheaves on a scheme

are still cosheaves
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, which can be viewed as well-defined on and
satisfying the cosheaf axiom for all the open subsets of
X
.
The category of
\mathbf{W}
-locally contraherent cosheaves

depends on a chosen open covering
\mathbf{W}
of a scheme
X
.
It can be defined in the spirit of the definition of a contraherent
cosheaves by three conditions (i–iii) above; the only difference is
that one considers the base
\mathbf{B}
of all affine open subschemes

U
in
X

subordinate to
the open covering
\mathbf{W}
.
This means that
U
ranges over all the affine open subschemes of

such that

for some
W\in\mathbf{W}
.

The category of all locally contraherent cosheaves

has a natural exact category structure.
The full subcategories
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}
and
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--lcth}}}_{\mathbf{W}}
are closed under
extensions and kernels of admissible epimorphisms in

; so
they are also exact categories
[
61
, Section 3.2]
.
For a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme

, the categories
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}

and
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--lcth}}}_{\mathbf{W}}
are resolving subcategories in
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--lcth}}}
.
The resolution dimensions of all objects of

are bounded by a constant

5.5.
The functor
\Cohom

The functor
\Cohom_{X}
from a quasi-coherent sheaf to a contraherent
cosheaf on a scheme

is dual-analogous to the functor of
tensor product of quasi-coherent sheaves,
\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\times X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
, in the same way as the Hom functor of
modules over a ring is dual-analogous to the tensor product functor
of modules.
The notation
\Cohom
comes from the interpretation of
quasi-coherent sheaves as comodules over a coring
\mathcal{C}
over
a commutative ring
A
and quasi-coherent sheaves as (approximately)
contramodules over the same coring.
See Section
0.3
;
cf. the definition of the Cohom functor for comodules and
contramodules in
[
62
, Sections 2.5-2.6]

or
[
57
, Sections 0.2.4 and 3.2]
.

Let
X
be a scheme,
\mathcal{M}
be a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
, and

\mathfrak{P}
be a contraherent cosheaf on
X
.
Then the contraherent cosheaf
\Cohom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{P})
is defined by
the rule

\Cohom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{P})[U]=\Hom_{\mathcal{O}(U)}(\mathcal{M}(U),\mathfrak{P}[U])

for all affine open subschemes
U\subset X
.
One can easily check that the contraherence condition (i) from
Section

holds for

\Cohom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{P})
for any quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{M}
and
contraherent cosheaf
\mathfrak{P}
on
X

[
61
, Section 2.4]
.
The problem is that the contraadjustedness condition (ii) need
not hold in general.

Example 5.5
.

Here is an example of a commutative ring
R
, an
R
-module
M
,
and a cotorsion
R
-module
P
such that the
R
-module

\Hom_{R}(M,P)
is not even contraadjusted.
The argument is based on the following observations.
Let
f\colon A\longrightarrow B
be a homomorphism of commutative rings and
C

be a
B
-module.
Then

(a)

if
C
is a contraadjusted
B
-module, then
C
is also
a contraadjusted
A
-module
[
61
, Lemma 1.2.2(a)]
;

(b)

if
C
is a cotorsion
B
-module, then
C
is also
a cotorsion
A
-module
[
61
, Lemma 1.3.4(a)]
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}[x]
be the ring of polynomials in one variable
x
with
integer coefficients.
Pick a prime number
p
, and consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-module of
p
-adic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Then
\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule.
Consider the quotient
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}
, and pick
an embedding
i\colon\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow J
of the
\mathbb{Z}
-module

\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}
into an injective
\mathbb{Z}
-module (i. e., a divisible
abelian group)

.
Denote by
g\colon\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow J
the composition
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}\overset{i}{\rightarrowtail}J
.
Put
P=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus J
, and let the generator
x\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
act
in

by the operator

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
g&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\colon\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus J\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus J

This defines a
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module structure on

We claim that the
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module

is cotorsion.
Indeed, there is a short exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-modules

acts by zero
in

and in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
In other words, if

is the ring homomorphism
for which

\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module structures on

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are obtained from their

-module structures by
the restriction of scalars via

Now

is an injective, hence cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}
-module; and

\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},\>\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is
a cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}
-module by
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
,

[

, Lemma 1.3.2(b) or 1.3.3(b)]
,

[

, or
[

, Theorem 9.5
or Example 12.1]
.
According to assertion (b) above, it follows that

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are
cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-modules; hence so is their extension

.

Put
M=\mathbb{Z}
, and let the generator

This makes

\mathbb{Z}[x]

One easily computes

.
Now the restriction of scalars with respect to the ring homomorphism

\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{Z}\oplus J
.
The
\mathbb{Z}

According to assertion (a) above, it follows that the

The contraherent cosheaf
\Cohom_{X}({-},{-})

5.6.
Discussion

The category of quasi-coherent sheaves
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
on any scheme
X
is
abelian.
An inspection of the description of quasi-coherent sheaves in
Section
5.1
leads one to the conclusion
that this fortunate state of affairs holds due to the fact that,
for any affine scheme
U
and affine open subscheme
V\subset U
,
the tensor product functor
\mathcal{O}(V)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(U)}{-}\,\colon\mathcal{O}(U){\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(V){\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is exact.
In other words, it is important that the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)

is flat.

In the definition of a contraherent cosheaf in
Section
5.3
, the localization
functor
\mathcal{O}(V)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(U)}{-}
is replaced by the colocalization
functor
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}(U)}(\mathcal{O}(V),{-})\colon\mathcal{O}(U){\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\allowbreak\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(V){\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
The
main technical problem
of the theory of contraherent
cosheaves is that the functor
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}(U)}(\mathcal{O}(V),{-})
is not exact
as a functor between the abelian categories of modules.
In other words, the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
is usually
not

projective.

As a consequence, one needs to impose the contraadjustedness
condition (ii) in the definition of a contraherent cosheaf
(see Remark
5.2
).
This condition has no analogue in the definition of a quasi-coherent
sheaf.
Imposing this condition makes the category of contraherent cosheaves

not abelian, but only exact
(in Quillen’s sense).
Furthermore, there are several versions of the condition, giving
rise to several versions of the contraherent cosheaf category.

In particular, let
U
be an affine scheme with the ring of
functions
R=\mathcal{O}(U)
.
Then the category of locally contraadjusted contraherent cosheaves
on
U
(as defined in Section
5.3
)
is equivalent to the category of contraadjusted
R
-modules, while
the categories of locally cotorsion and locally injective contraherent
cosheaves (as defined in Section
5.4
) are
equivalent to the categories of cotorsion
R
-modules and injective

R
-modules, respectively:

(10)

\displaystyle U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}

\displaystyle\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cta}},

(11)

\displaystyle U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}

\displaystyle\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}},

(12)

\displaystyle U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lin}}

\displaystyle\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{inj}}.

These are exact, but not abelian categories.

One may want to try dispensing with the contraadjustedness
condition altogether by simply dropping it from the definition of
a contraherent cosheaf, while keeping conditions (i) and (iii) only.
The present author expects that the resulting additive category
will
not
be abelian (on a nonaffine scheme), and generally not
well-behaved from the homological algebra point of view.
But it might be worth trying.

Various specific technical problems of the theory of contraherent
cosheaves usually arise as consequences of the main technical problem.
One class of such specific technical problems is that various
functors are only partially defined.

A functor taking values in an abelian category may fail to be exact,
but it is usually everywhere defined.
For example, if
F\colon\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{A})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{B})
is a triangulated
functor between the derived categories of
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
, then
one can compose
F
with the inclusion functor
\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{A})

and the degree-zero cohomology functor
H^{0}\colon\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
,
producing a (nonexact) functor
\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
.

In contrast, complexes in an exact category
\mathsf{E}
have
no

cohomology objects in
\mathsf{E}
, generally speaking.
Whenever a functor of abelian categories is not exact, a similar
functor of exact categories tends to be only partially defined.
We have seen an example of this phenomenon in
Section
5.5
, for the functor
\Cohom_{X}
.

Another unpleasant specific technical problem of the theory of
contraherent cosheaves, also arising from nonexactness of
the colocalization functors, is the nonlocality of contraherence;
see Remark
5.4
.

5.7.
Two branches of the theory

So, technical problems of the theory of contraherent cosheaves over
schemes arise from the fact that, for an affine open subscheme
V

in an affine scheme
U
, the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
is usually only
flat, but not projective.
What can be done to mitigate the problems?

There are two approaches, hence two branches of the theory of
contraherent cosheaves.
One point of view is that, as far as flat modules go,
the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
is not that bad.
It belongs to a class of flat modules over commutative rings with
particularly simple homological behavior, viz., the
very flat
modules
; see Section
4.3

and specifically Example
4.6
.
Taking this point of view leads one to consider
the
locally contraadjusted contraherent cosheaves
,
see Section
5.3
.

The other point of view is that flat modules generally are
“not that bad”, or not that much different from projective modules.
Adopting this approach means restricting oneself to
the
locally cotorsion contraherent cosheaves
, as defined
in Section
5.4
.

Each one of the two approaches comes with its own set of
technical problems.
At the time of writing of the long preprint
[
61
]
,
I thought that locally cotorsion contraherent cosheaves were more
suited for Noetherian schemes of finite Krull dimension, while on
non-Noetherian schemes one had to consider locally contraadjusted
contraherent cosheaves.
Ten years have passed, some work has been done, and now it seems
that both the locally contraadjusted and locally cotorsion
contraherent cosheaves behave reasonably well on all schemes;
certainly better than it was originally expected.
The aim of the next
Sections
6
–
7
is to
explain why this is the case.

6.
Very Flat Morphisms are Ubiquitous

The discussion of the
\Cohom
functor in Section
5.5

(see item (1)) illustrates the point that, when dealing with locally
contraadjusted contraherent cosheaves, one often needs one’s modules
and sheaves to be very flat (and not just flat).
An even more compelling manifestation of this phenomenon in
the context of inverse images of contraherent cosheaves will be
discussed in Section
6.3
below.

In this section we discuss the problem that very flat modules,
morphisms, and sheaves might be too few, and the solution in the form
of a theorem telling that they are actually quite common and numerous.

6.1.
Change of scalars and tensor product

Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a homomorphism of commutative rings.
Then, for any flat
R
-module
F
, the
S
-module
S\otimes_{R}F
is flat.
Similarly, for any very flat
R
-module
F
, the
S
-module

S\otimes_{R}F
is very flat
[
61
, Lemma 1.2.2(b)]
.

On the other hand, if
G
is a flat
S
-module and
f
makes
S

a flat
R
-module, then
G
is also a flat
R
-module.
The analogue of this result for very flat modules is more complicated.

A ring homomorphism
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
is called
very flat
if,
for every element
s\in S
, the
R
-module
S[s^{-1}]
is
very flat
[
87
, Section 9]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The following two conditions on a commutative ring homomorphism

f\colon R\longrightarrow S
are equivalent:

(1)

every very flat
S
-module is also very flat as an
R
-module;

(2)

the ring homomorphism
f
is very flat.

Proof.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2) For any element
s\in S
, the
S
-module

S[s^{-1}]
is very flat by the definition.
Assuming (1), it follows that
S[s^{-1}]
has to be a very flat

R
-module.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1) This is
[
61
, Lemma 1.2.3(b)]
.
∎

The condition that the ring
S
itself is a very flat
R
-module
is
not
sufficient for a ring homomorphism
f\colon R\longrightarrow S

to be very flat.
For a counterexample, see
[
87
, Example 9.7]
.

Here is another manifestation of more complicated behavior of
very flat modules as compared to flat ones.
Given two flat modules
F
and
G
over a commutative ring
R
,
the tensor product
F\otimes_{R}G
is also a flat
R
-module.
Similarly, if
F
and
G
are very flat
R
-modules, then

F\otimes_{R}G
is a very flat
R
-module
[
61
, Lemma 1.2.1(a)]
.

However, let
R
and
S
be two commutative rings.
Let
F
be an
R
-
S
-bimodule and
G
be an
S
-module.
In this context, if
F
is a flat
R
-module and
G
is
a flat
S
-module, then
F\otimes_{S}G
is a flat
R
-module.
The similar assertion holds for flat (bi)modules over arbitrary
associative rings.
But the similar assertion for very flat modules is
not
true.

Example 6.2
.

Consider the commutative
R
-algebra
S
from
[
87
, Example 9.7]

mentioned above.
So
S
is a free
R
-module, but there is an element
s\in S

such that
S[s^{-1}]
is
not
a very flat
R
-module.
Consider the
R
-
S
-bimodule
F=S
and the
S
-module

.
Then the
R
-module

is very flat, and the
S
-module

is
very flat.
But the
R
-module
F\otimes_{S}G=S[s^{-1}]
is not very flat.

6.2.
Very flat quasi-coherent sheaves and their direct images

Let
X
be a scheme.
A quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is said to be
very flat

if, for every affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module

\mathcal{F}(U)
is very flat.
It suffices to check this condition for affine open subschemes

U\subset X
belonging to any given affine open covering of
the scheme
X

[
61
, Lemma 1.2.6(a) and Section 1.7]
.
So the very flatness property of quasi-coherent sheaves is local
(cf.
[
77
, Example 2.5]
).

Let
f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
be a morphism of schemes.
Then, for any flat quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
the inverse image
f^{*}\mathcal{F}
on
Y
of the quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}

on
X
is also flat.
Similarly, for any very flat quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
the quasi-coherent sheaf
f^{*}\mathcal{F}
on
Y
is also very flat.
This follows from the stability of the classes of flat and
very flat modules under the extension of scalars with respect to
homomorphisms of commutative rings, as per the discussion in
Section
6.1
.

Now let
f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
be an affine morphism of schemes
(i. e., for every affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, the open
subscheme
f^{-1}(U)\subset Y
is affine).
The morphism
f
is said to be
flat
if, for every
pair of affine open subschemes
U\subset X
and
V\subset Y

such that
f(V)\subset U
, the
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
is flat.
This is equivalent to the
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(f^{-1}(U))

being flat for all affine open subschemes
U\subset X
.
If this is the case then, for every flat quasi-coherent sheaf

\mathcal{G}
on
Y
, the direct image
f_{*}\mathcal{G}
on
X
of the quasi-coherent
sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on
Y
is also flat.

A morphism of schemes
Y\longrightarrow X
is said to be
very flat

if, for every pair of affine open subschemes
U\subset X
and

V\subset Y
such that
f(V)\subset U
, the
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-module

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
is very flat
[
61
, Section 1.7]
.
For an affine morphism
f
, very flatness of the
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-modules

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(f^{-1}(U))
is
not
sufficient for the morphism
f
to be
very flat.
For example, a morphism of affine schemes
f\colon\Spec S\longrightarrow\Spec R

is very flat if and only if the related morphism of rings
R\longrightarrow S

is very flat in the sense of
Section
6.1
, i. e., the
R
-modules

S[s^{-1}]
are very flat for all
s\in S
.

Very flatness of the
R
-module
S
would imply very flatness of
the
R^{\prime}
-modules
S^{\prime}=R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}S
for all commutative ring
homomorphisms
R\longrightarrow R^{\prime}
, including in particular such ring
homomorphisms corresponding to open immersions of affine schemes

U\longrightarrow\Spec R
.
So the
\mathcal{O}_{\Spec R}(U)
-modules
\mathcal{O}_{\Spec S}(f^{-1}(U))
would be
very flat in this case.
But this is
not
sufficient for very flatness of the ring
homomorphism
R\longrightarrow S
or the scheme morphism

f\colon\Spec S\longrightarrow\Spec R
, as explained in
Section
6.1
.

Let
f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
be a very flat affine morphism of schemes.
Then, for every very flat quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on
Y
,
the quasi-coherent sheaf
f_{*}\mathcal{G}
on
X
is also very flat.
This follows from the stability of the class of very flat modules
under the restriction of scalars with respect to very flat morphisms
of commutative rings, as per Section
6.1
.

6.3.
Inverse images of contraherent cosheaves

Let
f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
be a morphism of schemes.
Given a contraherent cosheaf
\mathfrak{P}
on
X
, one would like to construct
a contraherent cosheaf
f^{!}\mathfrak{P}
on
Y
(in the notation of
[
61
]
).
There are two problems arising in this connection.

A minor issue is the nonlocality of contraherence (as per
Remark
5.4
).
Contraherent cosheaves on the scheme
X
are defined in terms of
affine open subschemes
U\subset X
, and similarly contraherent
cosheaves on
Y
are defined in terms of affine open subschemes

V\subset Y
.
A morphism of schemes
f
is called
coaffine
if, for every
affine open subscheme
V\subset Y
, there exists an affine open
subscheme
U\subset X
such that
f(V)\subset U
.
This is
not
always the case.
Cf. the “Jouanolou trick” and its generalization by
Thomason
[
112
, Proposition 4.3 or 4.4]
.

The major issue is that the inverse image
f^{!}\mathfrak{P}
can be only
defined in the following cases (cf. the discussion of partially
defined functors between exact categories in
Section
5.6
):

(1)

if
\mathfrak{P}
is a locally contraadjusted (locally) contraherent
cosheaf and
f
is a very flat morphism of schemes, then

f^{!}\mathfrak{P}
is a locally contraadjusted locally contraherent cosheaf;

(2)

if
\mathfrak{P}
is a locally cotorsion (locally) contraherent
cosheaf and
f
is a flat morphism of schemes, then

f^{!}\mathfrak{P}
is a locally cotorsion locally contraherent cosheaf;

(3)

if
\mathfrak{J}
is a locally injective (locally) contraherent
cosheaf and
f
is an arbitrary morphism of schemes, then
f^{!}\mathfrak{J}

is locally injective locally contraherent cosheaf.

To illustrate the nature of the major issue, let us consider
the case of a morphism of affine schemes
f\colon\Spec S\longrightarrow\Spec R
.
Then the contraherent cosheaves on
\Spec R
are the same things
as
R
-modules with suitable adjustedness properties, as in
formulas (
10
–
12
)
from Section
5.6
.

The inverse image functor
f^{!}
assigns to an
R
-module
P

the
S
-module
\Hom_{R}(S,P)
.
If
P
is a contraadjusted
R
-module and
R\longrightarrow S
is a very
flat homomorphism, then
\Hom_{R}(S,P)
is a contraadjusted

S
-module
[
61
, Lemma 1.2.3(a)]
.
If
P
is a cotorsion
R
-module and
S
is a flat
R
-module,
then
\Hom_{R}(S,P)
is a cotorsion

S
-module
[
61
, Lemma 1.3.5(a)]
.
If
J
is an injective
R
-module, then
\Hom_{R}(S,J)
is
an injective
S
-module
[
61
, Lemma 1.3.4(b)]
.

More generally, for any very flat coaffine morphism of schemes

f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
, there is a well-defined inverse image functor

f^{!}\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}\longrightarrow Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}
.
For any flat coaffine morphism of schemes
f
, there is
a well-defined functor
f^{!}\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}\longrightarrow Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}
.
For any coaffine morphism of schemes
f
, there is
a well-define functor
f^{!}\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lin}}\longrightarrow Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lin}}

[
61
, Section 2.3]
.

For a noncoaffine morphism of schemes
f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
, the inverse
image functors
f^{!}
in the respective cases (1–3) take contraherent
cosheaves on
X
to
\mathbf{W}
-locally contraherent cosheaves on
Y
,
where
\mathbf{W}
is the open covering of
Y
by the preimages

f^{-1}(U)\subset Y
of the affine open subschemes
U\subset X

[
61
, Section 3.3]
.

6.4.
The problem

The discussion in
Sections
1.2
and
and
3.2
illustrates the point
that the functor
\Cohom_{X}
from quasi-coherent sheaves to contraherent
cosheaves is important.
According to item (1) in Section
5.5
,
for a locally contraadjusted contraherent cosheaf
\mathfrak{P}
on
X
,
the contraherent cosheaf
\Cohom_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{P})
is well-defined whenever

\mathcal{F}
is a very flat quasi-coherent sheaf.
Thus, for the theory of locally contraadjusted contraherent cosheaves
to be useful enough, one needs a plentiful supply of very flat
quasi-coherent sheaves.

What does it mean “to have a plentiful supply” in this context?
The result of
[
61
, Lemma 4.1.1]
tells that, on
a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme
X
, every quasi-coherent
sheaf is a quotient sheaf of a very flat one.
This is what one usually means by “having enough flat objects”
(of a particular kind).
Is that enough for our purposes, or what else is needed?

It would be helpful to know that quasi-coherent sheaves arising
under various constructions are very flat, at, least, in the contexts
where the ones arising under comparable constructions are flat.
In particular, the preservation of very flatness of quasi-coherent
sheaves by direct and inverse images is discussed in
Section
6.2
.
For the direct image functor
f_{*}\colon Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
with
respect to an affine morphism of schemes
f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
to preserve
very flatness, the morphism
f
should be very flat.
Thus, we are interested in having an abundant supply of very flat
morphisms of schemes.

The discussion of inverse images of contraherent cosheaves in
Section
6.3
provides a more
compelling argument supporting the same point.
According to item (1), for an inverse image functor
f^{!}\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}\longrightarrow Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--lcth}}}_{\mathbf{W}}
to be well-defined (for a suitable open covering

\mathbf{W}
of the scheme
Y
), the morphism
f
should be very flat.
Surely the inverse image functors are of utmost importance.
Thus, for the theory of locally contraadjusted (locally) contraherent
cosheaves to be substantial enough, one needs an abundant supply
of very flat morphisms of schemes.

6.5.
The solution

In the context of more geometric chapters of algebraic geometry,
flat morphisms of finite type between Noetherian schemes describe
deformations of algebraic varieties.
Are all such morphisms very flat?
This was conjectured in the February 2014 version of the long
prepring
[
61
]
; see
[
61
, Conjecture 1.7.2]
.
The assertion became known as the
Very Flat Conjecture
.

The Very Flat Conjecture was proved in the paper
[
87
]
.
The main result of that paper,
[
87
, Main Theorem 1.1]
,
claims the following.
Let
R\longrightarrow S
be a morphism of commutative rings making
S

a finitely presented
R
-algebra.
Assume that
S
is a flat
R
-module.
Then
S
is a very flat
R
-module.

More generally, let
R\longrightarrow S
be a morphism of commutative rings
making
S
a finitely presented
R
-algebra; and let
F
be
a finitely presented
S
-module.
Assume that
F
is a flat
R
-module.
Then
F
is a very flat
R
-module
[
87
, Main Theorem 1.2]
.

The following corollary is easily deduced
[
87
, Corollary 9.1]
.
Let
R
be a commutative ring, and let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a morphism
of commutative rings making
S
a finitely presented, flat
R
-algebra.
Then
f
is a very flat morphism of commutative rings (in the
sense of Section
6.1
).

So very flat morphisms of schemes are, at least, as common as flat
morphisms of finite presentation, which is to say, quite common.
To give another example, for any (possibly infinite-dimensional)
schemes
X
and
Y
over a field
k
, the natural projection

X\times_{k}Y\longrightarrow X
is a very flat morphism of schemes.
This surprisingly nontrivial assertion (cf. an early geometric proof
of a particular case in
[
61
, Corollary 1.7.15]
) is known to be
true in view of
[
87
, Corollary 9.8]
.

Alongside with the class of very flat
R
-modules, there is
the class of
finitely very flat

R
-modules

[
87
, Section 1.3]
, which is a subclass of very
flat modules having some technical advantages and disadvantages.
For any commutative ring
R
, a finitely presented commutative

R
-algebra
S
, and a finitely presented
S
-module
F
that is
flat over
R
, the
R
-module
F
is actually finitely very
flat
[
87
, Main Theorem 1.5]
.

The assertions of
[
87
, Noetherian Main Lemma 1.4]
and

[
87
, Finitely Very Flat Main Lemma 1.6]
, as well as their
generalizations in
[
87
, Theorems 1.11 and 1.12]
, provide
potentially useful criteria for (finite) very flatness of flat
modules over commutative rings and flat quasi-coherent sheaves.
In particular
[
87
, Theorem 1.11]
tells that the property
of very flatness of flat quasi-coherent sheaves satisfies descent
with respect to surjective morphisms of finite type between
Noetherian schemes.

6.6.
Discussion

What are the advantages of very flat modules/quasi-coherent sheaves
as compared to the flat ones?
A closely related question is: What are the advantages of locally
contraadjusted contraherent cosheaves as compared to the locally
cotorsion ones?

One answer to this question (cf. the discussion in
Section
7
below) is that the class of all
very flat modules over commutative rings
R
combines four properties:

(i)

all
R
-modules in this class are flat and have uniformly bounded
finite projective dimension (in fact, projective dimension
\leq 1
);

(ii)

this class of
R
-modules is preserved by transfinitely iterated
extensions (in the sense of Section
4.2
),
direct summands, kernels of surjective morphisms, extensions of scalars,
and tensor products over
R
;

(iii)

all
R
-modules are quotient modules of modules from this class;

(iv)

for every affine open subscheme
V
in an affine scheme
U
,
the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
\mathcal{O}(V)
belong to this class.

Property (i) is Theorem
4.5
(d)
and
[
61
, beginning of Section 1.1]
, property (ii) was discussed
in Theorem
4.5
(a) and
Section
6.1
, property (iii) holds because
all projective
R
-modules are very flat, and property (iv) is
Example
4.6
.
From this point of view, the results of
Section
6.5
(the Very Flat Conjecture)
come as a bonus.

The class of flat modules satisfies (ii), (iii), and (iv), but it
need not satisfy (i).
Only over Noetherian commutative rings of finite Krull
dimension
[
97
, Corollaire II.3.2.7]
and rings of
cardinality
\leq\aleph_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

[
31
, Théorème 7.10]
(cf.
[
97
, Corollaire II.3.3.2]
)
flat modules have finite projective dimensions.
Are there any other alternatives?

For example, the class of all flat
R
-modules of projective
dimension
\leq n
has the properties (i), (iii), and (iv),
but is unlikely to be preserved by tensor products.
Still, one can brute force all the four properties by considering
the class of all direct summands of
R
-modules filtered by
countably presented flat
R
-modules (in the sense of
Section
4.2
).
Such modules have projective dimensions at most
1

by
[
26
, Corollary 2.23]
, and their class is closed under
tensor products.
Furthermore, all finitely presented
R
-algebras are obviously
countably presented as
R
-modules.

A perhaps less ugly alternative to the class of very flat modules is
the class of
quite flat
modules over commutative rings, which
was introduced in
[
88
, Section 8]
and studied in
the paper
[
35
]
.
All the four properties (i–iv) are satisfied for this class,
which is intermediate between the class of very flat modules and
the class of direct summands of modules filtered by countably presented
flat modules.
For Noetherian commutative rings
R
,
[
35
, Theorem 2.4]
tells
that all (direct summands of modules filtered by) countably generated
flat
R
-modules are quite flat.

6.7.
Conclusion

The class of very flat modules over commutative rings is
the minimal class closed under transfinitely iterated extensions,
direct summands, and containing the
R
-modules
R[r^{-1}]
for
all commutative rings
R
and elements
r\in R
.
These are weak versions of properties (ii) and (iv) from
Section
6.6
.
It is also the minimal
very local
class of modules in
the sense of
[
77
, Section 2]
that is the left-hand class of
a cotorsion pair in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
for every commutative ring
R
.

Having a large category of contraherent cosheaves is a mixed blessing.
The larger is the right-hand class of a cotorsion pair, the smaller
is the left-hand class.
The right-hand class of all contraadjusted modules is very large, and
its corresponding left-hand class of all very flat modules is
the smallest one as compared to possible alternatives.
But one needs very flat modules, morphisms, and sheaves in order to
perform constructions with the locally contraadjusted contraherent
cosheaves.

Still, very flat homomorphisms of commutative rings are surprisingly
ubiquitous, as (the proof of) the Very Flat Conjecture tells.
That is what makes the theory of locally contraadjusted contraherent
cosheaves viable.

7.
Flat Modules are Not That Much Different From Projectives

Let
U
be an affine scheme with the ring of functions
R=\mathcal{O}(U)
.
One would like to think of the exact category of contraherent
cosheaves on
U
as being “essentially” equivalent to
the abelian category of
R
-modules, but this not literally true.
Actually, three versions of the exact category of contraherent
cosheaves on
U
are described by
formulas (
10
–
12
)
from Section
5.6
.

What about the derived categories?
Do the exact categories
U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}
,
U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
, and
U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lin}}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{inj}}
have the same
derived categories as the ambient abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
?
Furthermore, what about the contraderived categories?
The aim of this section is to discuss these questions by putting
them in a wider context.
Briefly, the answers are positive for the contraadjusted and
cotorsion modules, and negative for the injective ones.

7.1.
Exact categories of finite homological dimension

An exact category
\mathsf{E}

\leq d

\Ext_{\mathsf{E}}^{d+1}(A,B)=0

The

Let us revisit these definitions in the context of exact categories.

We denote by

\mathsf{E}

The homotopy categories of bounded below, bounded above, and
two-sided bounded complexes are denoted by
\Hot^{+}(\mathsf{E})
,

\Hot^{-}(\mathsf{E})

A discussion of the acyclic complexes and the derived categories

\mathsf{D}^{\star}(\mathsf{E})

\mathsf{E}
(where

,

[

]

[

.
We refer to
[

for our idiosyncratic terminology
of
exact
vs.

complexes in exact categories.
This distinction is mostly not relevant to our tasks in this paper,
as the exact categories we are really interested in are
idempotent-complete.

A complex in an exact category

is said to be

[

,

[

,

if it belongs to the minimal thick

The full subcategory of absolutely acyclic complexes is denoted

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category. Then

(a)
every absolutely acyclic complex in
\mathsf{E}

(b)

has finite homological dimension, then every
acyclic complex in
\mathsf{E}

Proof.

In part (a), one proves that the class of all exact complexes in
an exact category is closed under extensions.
Part (b) is
[
57
, Remark 2.1]
.
∎

An exact category
\mathsf{A}
is said to have

if
the infinite coproduct functors are everywhere defined in
\mathsf{A}
and
preserve admissible short exact sequences.
Dually, an exact category
\mathsf{B}
is said to have

\mathsf{B}
and preserve admissible

A complex in an exact category
\mathsf{A}

Dually, a complex in an exact category
\mathsf{B}

.

Lemma 7.2
.

(a)
In an exact category with exact coproducts, every

In an exact category of finite homological dimension,

Proposition 7.5
.

(a)
Let
\mathsf{A}
be an exact category of finite homological
dimension with enough injective objects.
Then the clases of acyclic, absolutely acyclic, and Becker-coacyclic
complexes in
\mathsf{A}
coincide.
The composition of triangulated functors

\Hot(\mathsf{A}^{\mathsf{inj}})\simeq\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{abs}}(\mathsf{A})=\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bco}}(\mathsf{A})=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{A}).

(b)
Let
\mathsf{B}
be an exact category of finite homological
dimension with enough projective objects.
Then the classes of acyclic, absolutely acyclic, and
Becker-contraacyclic complexes in
\mathsf{B}
coincide.
The composition of triangulated functors
\Hot(\mathsf{B}_{\mathsf{proj}})\longrightarrow\Hot(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{abs}}(\mathsf{B})=\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(\mathsf{B})=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{B})
is
a triangulated equivalence

\Hot(\mathsf{B}_{\mathsf{proj}})\simeq\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{abs}}(\mathsf{B})=\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(\mathsf{B})=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{B}).

One has

and
\Ac^{\mathsf{abs}}(\mathsf{B})=\Ac(\mathsf{B})
by

.
Furthermore,
\Ac^{\mathsf{abs}}(\mathsf{A})\subset\Ac^{\mathsf{bco}}(\mathsf{A})
and

\Ac^{\mathsf{abs}}(\mathsf{B})\subset\Ac^{\mathsf{bctr}}(\mathsf{B})
by

The argument with the totalization of a finite acyclic complex of
complexes similar to
[

, proof of Theorem 3.6]

or

proves that the compositions

Conclusions: as a general rule, all absolutely acyclic, coacyclic,
or contraacyclic complexes tend to be acyclic.
However,
we do not know
how to prove that all Becker
co/contraacyclic complexes in an exact category are acyclic without
making some restrictive assumptions, or what kind of restrictive
assumptions would be optimal for the task.
This is not a problem for abelian categories.

On the other hand, in any exact category of finite homological
dimension, all acyclic complexes are absolutely acyclic.
Hence they are also coacyclic and contraacyclic both in the senses
of Positselski and Becker.

7.2.
Exact subcategories of finite (co)resolution dimension

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category.
A full subcategory
\mathsf{F}\subset\mathsf{E}
is said to be
resolving
if

•

the full subcategory
\mathsf{F}
is closed under extensions and kernels
of admissible epimorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
;

•

the full subcategory
\mathsf{F}
is generating in
\mathsf{E}
(in the sense
of Section
4.1
).

Dually, a full subcategory
\mathsf{C}\subset\mathsf{E}
is said to be

coresolving
if

•

the full subcategory
\mathsf{C}
is closed under extensions and
cokernels of admissible monomorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
;

•

the full subcategory
\mathsf{C}
is cogenerating in
\mathsf{E}
(in the sense
of Section
4.1
).

For example, the left-hand classes of hereditary cotorsion pairs are
resolving, and the right-hand classes are coresolving.
Furthermore, any (co)resolving subcategory is closed under extensions;
so it inherits an exact category structure from the ambient exact
category.

The classical concepts of projective, injective, and flat dimensions
generalize naturally to (co)resolving subcategories.
Let us assume the exact category
\mathsf{E}
to be, at least, weakly
idempotent-complete (in the sense of
[
11
, Section 7]
).

Given a resolving subcategory
\mathsf{F}
in an exact category
\mathsf{E}
, one
says that an object
E\in\mathsf{E}
has
resolution dimension
\leq d

with respect to
\mathsf{F}
if there exists an exact sequence

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muF_{d}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muF_{d-1}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muF_{0}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muE\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu0

in
\mathsf{E}
with
F_{i}\in\mathsf{F}
for all
0\leq i\leq d
.
The
coresolution dimension
with respect to a coresolving
subcategory is defined dually.

The (co)resolution dimension of an object with respect to
a (co)resolving subcategory does not depend on the choice of
a (co)resolution, in the same sense as the projective/injective/flat
dimension of a module does not depend on the choice of a (co)resolution.
This result can be found in
[
106
, Proposition 2.3]

or
[
61
, Corollary A.5.2]
; see also
[

, Proposition 6.2]
.

, Theorem 7.11]
.
∎

There is not much we can say concerning the comparison of
the co/contraderived categories in the sense of Becker for an exact
category and its (co)resolving subcategory.
All we can offer is the following lemma.

Lemma 7.9
.

(a)
Let
\mathsf{A}
be an exact category and
\mathsf{C}\subset\mathsf{A}

be a coresolving subcategory closed under direct summands.
Then the classes of injective objects in
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{C}

coincide,
\mathsf{C}^{\mathsf{inj}}=\mathsf{A}^{\mathsf{inj}}
.
If there are enough injective objects in
\mathsf{A}
, then there are also
enough injective objects in
\mathsf{C}
.

(b)
Let
\mathsf{B}
be an exact category and
\mathsf{F}\subset\mathsf{B}

be a resolving subcategory closed under direct summands.
Then the classes of projective objects in
\mathsf{B}
and
\mathsf{F}

coincide,
\mathsf{F}_{\mathsf{proj}}=\mathsf{B}_{\mathsf{proj}}
.
If there are enough projective objects in
\mathsf{B}
, then there are also
enough projective objects in
\mathsf{F}
. ∎

The two propositions and lemma above exhaust our list of results
about the derived/coderived/contraderived categories for

arbitrary
(co)resolving subcategories.
The assertions below assume
finite (co)resolution dimension
.

.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category,
\mathsf{F}\subset\mathsf{E}

Example 7.11
.

With the finite (co)resolution dimension conditions dropped,

It suffices to consider the abelian category

.

\mathsf{E}
be an exact category,
\mathsf{F}\subset\mathsf{E}

Conclusions: as a general rule, the passage to a (co)resolving
subcategory with uniformly bounded (co)resolution dimension tends to
not change the derived, coderived, and contraderived categories.
This has been established for the conventional derived categories
and the co/contraderived categories in the sense of Positselski.
For the co/contraderived categories in the sense of Becker,
the formulations and proofs of such assertions remain to be worked out.
When the condition of finite (co)resolution dimension is dropped,
the situation becomes more varied.

7.3.
Derived categories of flat and projective modules

Let
R
be an associative ring.
Consider the exact category of flat
R
-modules
\mathsf{E}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}

(with the exact structure inherited from the abelian exact structure
of the ambient abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
).
Then the full subcategory of projective
R
-modules
\mathsf{F}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
is a resolving subcategory in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
.

The resolution dimensions of objects of
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
with respect to

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}
(i. e., the projective dimensions of flat
R
-modules)
are finite when
R
is a Noetherian commutative ring of finite
Krull dimension
[
97
, Corollaire II.3.2.7]
, when the ring
R

has cardinality
\leq\aleph_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

[
31
, Théorème 7.10]
(cf.
[
97
, Corollaire II.3.3.2]
),
and for some noncommutative rings with dualizing complexes
[
13
, first
assertion of Proposition 1.5]
,
[
64
, Proposition 4.3]
.
But, generally speaking, the projective dimensions of flat modules
can be infinite.
Nevertheless, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 7.13
.

For any ring
R
, the inclusion of exact categories

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
induces an equivalence of their
unbounded derived categories,

\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}})=\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}})\simeq\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}).

Proof.

This is
[
51
, Proposition 8.1 and
Theorem 8.6(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)]
.
Notice that, by the definition, the acyclic complexes in the exact
category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
are precisely all the acyclic complexes of
flat
R
-modules
with flat
R
-modules of cocycles
(rather
than arbitrary acyclic complexes of flat
R
-modules).
∎

Thus, even though the assumption of
Proposition
7.10

does
not
hold for the resolving subcategory
\mathsf{F}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}

in the exact category
\mathsf{E}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
, the conclusion of
Proposition
7.10
(b) still
holds true in this case for the derived category symbols
\star=-

(by Proposition
7.7
(b))
and, much more nontrivially,
\star=\varnothing
(by
Theorem
7.13
).
In this sense, one can say that the resolving subcategory

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}
in the exact category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
behaves “as if
the resolution dimensions were finite”.

For a generalization of
Theorem
7.13

to graded-flat and graded-projective (curved) DG-modules over
a (curved) DG-ring, see
[
95
, Theorem 4.12]
.

7.4.
Becker-contraderived category of flat modules

The following theorem is largely a restatement of
Theorem
7.13
.
Notice that, by Lemma
7.9
(b),
the projective objects of the exact category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
are
precisely all the projective
R
-modules,
(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})_{\mathsf{proj}}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}
.
There are enough projective objects in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
.

Theorem 7.14
.

For any ring
R
, the class of all Becker-contraacyclic complexes
in the exact category
\mathsf{B}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
coincides with the class of
all acyclic complexes in this exact category.

\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}})\longrightarrow\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})=\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})
is
a triangulated equivalence

\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}})\simeq\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})=\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}).

Proof.

The first assertion is
precisely
[
51
, Theorem 8.6(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)]
.
The second assertion is
[
51
, Proposition 8.1]
.
∎

Thus, even though the assumptions of
Proposition
7.5
(b)
do
not
hold for the exact category
\mathsf{B}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
,
the conclusion of this proposition still partly holds true for
this exact category.
In this sense, one can say that the exact category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}

behaves “as if its homological dimension were finite”
(see also Section
7.6
below).

7.5.
Derived categories of cotorsion and all modules

For any ring
R
, the full subcategory of cotorsion
R
-modules

\mathsf{C}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
is a coresolving subcategory in the abelian
category of
R
-modules
\mathsf{E}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
By the
cotorsion dimension
of an
R
-module one means its
coresolution dimension with respect to the coresolving subcategory

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category,
A
,
B\in\mathsf{E}
be two objects,
and
d\geq-1
be an integer.
One says that the object
A
has
projective dimension
\leq d

in
\mathsf{E}
if
\Ext_{\mathsf{E}}^{d+1}(A,E)=0
for all objects
E\in\mathsf{E}
.
Dually, one says that the object
B
has
injective
dimension
\leq d
in
\mathsf{E}
if
\Ext_{\mathsf{E}}^{d+1}(E,B)=0
for all
objects
E\in\mathsf{E}
.

We start with a straightforward lemma and corollary.

Lemma 7.15
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category,
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})
be a hereditary
cotorsion pair in
\mathsf{E}
, and
d\geq 0
be an integer.
Then all objects of
\mathsf{F}
have projective dimensions
\leq d

in
\mathsf{E}
if and only if all objects of
\mathsf{E}
have coresolution
dimensions
\leq d
with respect to
\mathsf{C}
. ∎

Corollary 7.16
.

For any ring
R
and integer
d\geq 0
, the following two conditions
are equivalent:

(1)

the projective dimensions of all flat
R
-modules do not
exceed
d
;

(2)

the cotorsion dimensions of all
R
-modules do not exceed
d
.
∎

So the cotorsion dimensions of modules can be infinite, generally
speaking.
Nevertheless, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 7.17
.

For any ring
R
, the inclusion of exact categories
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
induces an equivalence of their unbounded derived
categories,

\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}})\simeq\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}).

Proof.

This is essentially
[
2
, first assertion of Theorem 5.1(2)]
.
See
[
94
, Proposition 9.6]
for additional details.
∎

Thus, even though the assumption of
Proposition
7.10

does
not
hold for the coresolving subcategory
\mathsf{C}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}

in the exact category
\mathsf{E}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
, the conclusion of
Proposition
7.10
(a) still
holds true in this case for the derived category symbols
\star=+

(by Proposition
7.7
(a))
and, much more nontrivially,
\star=\varnothing
(by
Theorem
7.17
).
In this sense, one can say that the coresolving subcategory

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
in the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
behaves “as if
the coresolution dimensions were finite”.

For a version of Theorem
7.17

for (curved) DG-modules over a (curved) DG-ring,
see
[
95
, Theorem 3.7]
.

7.6.
Becker-coderived category of flat modules

An
R
-module is said to be
flat cotorsion
if it is
simultaneously flat and cotorsion.
The full subcategory of flat cotorsion
R
-modules is denoted by

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{cot}}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}\cap R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.

The flat cotorsion
R
-modules are precisely all the injective
objects of the exact category of flat
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
,
and there are enough such injective objects.
The flat cotorsion
R
-modules are also precisely all the projective
objects of the exact category of cotorsion
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
,
and there are enough such projective objects.
These assertions follow from the fact that the flat cotorsion pair
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is complete (by Theorem
4.4
);
see
[
78
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Theorem 7.18
.

For any ring
R
, the class of all Becker-coacyclic complexes in
the exact category
\mathsf{A}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
coincides with the class of
all acyclic complexes in this exact category.
The composition of triangulated functors
\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{cot}})\longrightarrow\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})=\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})

is a triangulated equivalence

\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{cot}})\simeq\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})=\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}).

Proof.

The inclusion
\Ac(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})\subset\Ac^{\mathsf{bco}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})

means that, for any acyclic complex of flat
R
-modules
F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

with flat modules of cocycles, and for any complex of flat
cotorsion
R
-modules
Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
, the complex of abelian groups

\Hom_{R}(F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
is acyclic.
More generally, this assertion holds for any complex of (not
necessarily flat) cotorsion
R
-modules
Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
.
This is the result of
[
2
, Theorem 5.3]
.

It remains to show that for every complex of flat
R
-modules

G^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
there exists a complex of flat cotorsion
R
-modules

Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
together with a closed morphism of complexes of
R
-modules

G^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
whose cone is an acyclic complex of flat

R
-modules with flat
R
-modules of cocycles.
Indeed, by
[
25
, Definition 3.3 and Corollary 4.10]
, for
any complex of
R
-modules
G^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
, there exists a short exact
sequence of complexes of
R
-modules
0\longrightarrow G^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow 0
, where
Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is a complex of
cotorsion
R
-modules and
F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is an acyclic complex of flat

R
-modules with flat
R
-modules of cocycles.
Now if
G^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is a complex of flat
R
-modules, then
Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

is a complex of flat cotorsion
R
-modules.
Since
F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\in\Ac(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})
and the totalization of the short
exact sequence
0\longrightarrow G^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow 0

belongs to
\Ac^{\mathsf{abs}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})\subset\Ac(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})
(see
Lemma
7.1
(a)), the cone of the morphism

G^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
also belongs to the triangulated subcategory

\Ac(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})\subset\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})
.
∎

Thus, even though the assumptions of
Proposition
7.5
(a)
do
not
hold for the exact category
\mathsf{B}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
,
the conclusion of this proposition still partly holds true for
this exact category.
In this sense, one can say once again that the exact category

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
behaves “as if its homological dimension were finite”
(cf. Section
7.4
).

For a version of Theorem
7.18
for
(curved) DG-modules over a (curved) DG-ring,
see
[
95
, Theorem 4.13]
.

7.7.
Becker-contraderived categories of cotorsion and
all modules

As mentioned in the beginning of
Section
7.6
, the flat cotorsion

R
-modules are the projective objects of the exact category of
cotorsion
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
.
For any exact category
\mathsf{E}
, we denote by
\Com(\mathsf{E})
the category
of all cochain complexes in
\mathsf{E}
.

Theorem 7.19
.

For any ring
R
, the class of all Becker-contraacyclic complexes
in the exact category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
coincides with the class
of all complexes of cotorsion
R
-modules that are
Becker-contraacyclic as complexes in the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
,

\Ac^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}})=\Com(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}})\cap\Ac^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}).

The composition of triangulated functors
\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{cot}})\longrightarrow\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}})
is
a triangulated equivalence

\Hot(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{cot}})\simeq\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{bctr}}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}).

Proof.

It follows from Theorems
7.14

and
7.18
that, for every complex of
projective
R
-modules
P^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
there exists a complex of flat
cotorsion
R
-modules
Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
together with a morphism of complexes
of
R
-modules
P^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
whose cone is acyclic in
the exact category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
.
Conversely, for every complex of flat cotorsion
R
-modules
Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

there exists a complex of projective
R
-modules
P^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
together
with a morphism of complexes of
R
-modules
P^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

with the same property.
Furthermore, by
[
2
, Theorem 5.3]
, the complex

\Hom_{R}(F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
is acyclic for all complexes

F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\in\Ac(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})
and
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\in\Com(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}})
.
Hence the complex
\Hom_{R}(P^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
is acyclic if and only if
the complex
\Hom_{R}(Q^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
is.
This proves the first assertion of the theorem.

To prove the second assertion, notice that the class of all complexes
of flat modules
\Com(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})
is deconstructible in the abelian
category of all complexes of modules
\Com(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})

by
[
104
, Proposition 4.3]
.
There are enough projective objects in the Grothendieck abelian
category
\Com(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
, and they belong to
\Com(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}})
.
By a suitable version of the Eklof–Trlifaj theorem (cf. Theorem
4.3
), there exists a complete cotorsion pair

(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{C})
in
\Com(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
with

7.8.
Periodicity theorems

The most nontrivial aspects of the results of
Sections
7.3
–
7.7
are based on the class of
phenomena called the
periodicity theorems
, or more specifically
the
flat and projective periodicity
and the
cotorsion
periodicity
.

Let
L
be an
R
-module.
An
R
-module
M
is said to be
L
-periodic
if there is
a short exact sequence of
R
-modules
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
.
Periodicity theorems are results claiming that if a module
L

belongs to a certain class, a module
M
belongs to a certain class,
and the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
belongs
to a certain class of exact sequences, then the module
M
actually
has to belong to a certain (more narrow) class of modules.

The
flat and projective periodicity theorem
claims that if
L

is a projective
R
-module and
M
is a flat
R
-module, then
M

is actually a projective
R
-module
[
8
, Theorem 2.5]
.
The
cotorsion periodicity theorem
claims that if
L
is
a cotorsion
R
-module, then
M
is also a cotorsion

R
-module
[
2
, Theorem 1.2(2) or Proposition 4.8(2)]
.

Periodicity theorems are closely related to results about modules of
cocycles in acyclic complexes.
A general discussion of this connection can be found
in
[
14
, proof of Proposition 7.6]
,

[
22
, Propositions 1 and 2]
, and
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
;
see
[
78
, Proposition 1.1]
and
[
94
, Proposition 8.4]

for generalizations.
In particular, in any acyclic complex of projective modules with
flat modules of cocycles, the modules of cocycles are actually
projective
[
51
, Remark 2.15]
, while in any acyclic complex
of cotorsion modules, the modules of cocycles are also
cotorsion
[
2
, Theorem 5.1(2)]
.

A general discussion of known periodicity theorems with a long list
of such theorems and connections between them can be found in
the introduction to the paper
[
3
]
.
In particular, beyond the flat/projective periodicity theorem and
cotorsion periodicity theorem, there is also
the fp-injective/injective periodicity theorem
[
107
, Theorem 5.4
and Corollary 5.5]
and the fp-projective periodicity
theorem
[
99
, Example 4.3]
,
[
3
, Theorems 0.7–0.8
or 4.1–4.2]
.
Subsequent generalizations can be found in
the preprint
[
78
, Theorems 0, A, and B]
.

7.9.
Flat things as direct limits

The classical
Govorov–Lazard theorem
tells that flat

R
-modules are precisely the direct limits of projective
R
-modules,
or equivalently, of finitely generated free
R
-modules
[
27
,
44
]
,

[
26
, Corollary 2.22]
.
This result can be generalized very widely if one agrees to replace
“finitely generated free/projective modules” by “countably presented
flat modules”.
As stated in the abstract to the paper
[
83
]
, a general
principle seems to be that “anything flat is a direct limit of
countably presented flats”.

In particular, all at most countable diagrams of flat modules, such
as, e. g., complexes of flat modules, are direct limits of
diagrams/complexes of countably presented flat
modules
[
81
, Corollaries 10.3–10.4]
.
All acyclic complexes of flat modules with flat modules of
cocycles are direct limits of such complexes of countably presented
flat modules
[
81
, Corollary 10.14]
.
All graded-flat (curved) DG-modules are direct limits of countably
presented graded-flat (curved) DG-modules
[
95
, Proposition 4.7]
.

Over a countably coherent ring (e. g., over a coherent ring or
a countably Noetherian ring), all acyclic complexes of flat modules
(with arbitrary modules of cocycles) are direct limits of such
complexes of countably presented flat modules
[
83
, Theorem 4.2]
.
Furthermore, for any given integer
m\geq 0
, all modules of flat
dimension
m
over such a ring are direct limits of countably presented
modules of flat dimension at most
m

[
83
, Corollary 5.2]
.

All flat quasi-coherent sheaves on countably quasi-compact,
countably quasi-separated schemes (e. g., on the usual quasi-compact
quasi-separated schemes) are direct limits of locally countably
presented flat quasi-coherent sheaves
[
94
, Theorems 2.4 and 3.5]
.
All complexes of flat quasi-coherent sheaves on such schemes are
direct limits of complexes of locally countably presented flat
quasi-coherent sheaves
[
94
, Theorem 4.1]
.
All acyclic complexes of flat quasi-coherent sheaves with flat
sheaves of cocycles on such schemes are direct limits of complexes
of locally countably presented flat quasi-coherent sheaves from
the same class
[
94
, Theorem 4.2]
.
The proofs of these assertions are based on category-theoretic
results of
[
12
, Proposition 3.1]
,

[
96
, Pseudopullback Theorem 2.2]
, and the paper
[
81
]

(going back to
[
111
, Theorem 3.8, Corollary 3.9, and
Remark 3.11(II)]
).

All homotopy flat complexes of flat modules are direct limits of
bounded complexes of finitely generated projective
modules
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
.
It follows that all homotopy flat complexes of flat quasi-coherent
sheaves on countably quasi-compact, semi-separated schemes (e. g.,
on the usual quasi-compact semi-separated schemes) are direct limits
of homotopy flat complexes of locally countably presented flat
quasi-coherent sheaves
[
94
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Furthermore, all flat quasi-coherent sheaves over certain stacks
are direct limits of locally countably presented
ones
[
80
, Theorem 3.1]
, and so are flat pro-quasi-coherent
pro-sheaves over certain ind-schemes
[
80
, Theorem 10.1]
.
All acyclic complex of flat quasi-coherent sheaves with flat sheaves
of cocycles over such stacks are direct limits of complexes of
locally countably presented flat quasi-coherent sheaves with flat
sheaves of cocycles, and the same applies to such
ind-schemes
[
80
, Corollaries 4.5 and 11.4]
.

Given a commutative ring
R
, all (say, coassociative and counital;
or coassociative, cocommutative, and counital) coalgebras over
R

with flat underlying
R
-modules are direct limits of coalgebras
from the same class with flat and countably presented underlying

R
-modules
[
84
, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2]
.

The importance of countably presented flat modules lies in
the fact that they have projective dimensions at most
1

[
26
, Corollary 2.23]
,
[
83
, Corollary 2.4]
.
The proof of
quasi-coherent cotorsion periodicity

in
[
94
, Theorem 9.2]
is based on this fact.
Similarly one obtains cotorsion periodicity theorems for
quasi-coherent sheaves over stacks and pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves
over ind-schemes
[
80
, Theorems 5.4 and 12.3]
, as well as
certain versions of the “derived
=
coderived category” theorem
for flat quasi-coherent sheaves and flat pro-quasi-coherent
pro-sheaves
[
80
, Theorems 6.5 and 13.2]
.

Flat contramodules over topological rings
is another name for
flat pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves over ind-affine ind-schemes
(under suitable assumptions; see
[
74
, Example 3.8(2)]
).
The exposition in
[
80
]
is written in the language of comodules
and contramodules.
In fact, the comparison between the quasi-coherent sheaves over
stacks and the comodules over corings is relatively unproblematic;
but (nonflat) pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves form a badly behaved
full subcategory in the abelian category of
contramodules
[
74
, Examples 3.1 and 3.8(1)]
.
So the cotorsion periodicity theorem for ind-affine
ind-schemes
[
80
, Theorem 12.3]
should be stated in the language
of contramodules rather than pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves
(and it is done this way in
[
80
]
).
We do
not
know what, if anything, should a quasi-coherent
cotorsion periodicity theorem for non-ind-affine ind-schemes say.

7.10.
Periodicity theorems II

The aim of this section is to discuss the nature of periodicity
theorems as it appears in our present understanding.

It appears that both the flat/projective and cotorsion periodicity
theorems stem from the fact that all flat modules are direct limits
of (finitely generated) projective ones.
Similarly, both the fp-projective and fp-injective/injective
periodicity theorems stem from the fact that all modules are direct
limits of finitely presented ones.

Concerning the cotorsion and fp-injective/injective periodicities,
this connection is expressed in the form of a general category-theoretic
theorem in
[
94
, Theorem 8.1 and Corollary 8.2]
.
A common generalization of the cotorsion and fp-injective/injective
periodicity theorems based on these results from
[
94
]
is
worked out in
[
78
, Theorem 0(b) in Section 0.1 and Theorem B in
Section 0.3]
.
The quasi-coherent cotorsion periodicity on quasi-compact
semi-separated schemes
[
94
, Theorem 9.2]
, as well as
generalizations to stacks and ind-schemes
[
80
, Theorems 5.4
and 12.3]
, are corollaries of
[
94
, Corollary 8.2]
(cf. the last paragraph of Section
7.9
).

Concerning the flat/projective and fp-projective periodicities,
a common generalization of both, expressing their interpretation as
properties of the direct limit closure, can be found
in
[
78
, Theorem 0(a) in Section 0.1 and Theorem A in
Section 0.2]
.

A general concept of the direct limit completion of an exact category
is worked out in the preprint
[
82
]
.
Some related periodicity theorems can be found in
[
82
, Theorems 7.1,
7.6, and 8.3]
.

7.17.
Conclusion

For the purposes of the derived, coderived, and contraderived
categories, finite homological dimension is “harmless”.
This statement actually refers two two assertions: firstly,
the passage to an exact subcategory of finite (co)resolution
dimension does not change the derived/coderived/contraderived
category; and secondly, for an exact category of finite homological
dimension, the derived and coderived/contraderived category agree
whenever they are well-defined.

The discussion in this section illustrates the thesis that
the passage to the direct limit closure of an exact (sub)category is
almost as “harmless” as finite homological dimension
(see Section
7.10
).
Thus, even though projective dimensions of flat modules may be
infinite, flat modules are “not that much different” from
projective ones.

The typical covers in algebraic geometry are related to commutative
ring homomorphisms
R\longrightarrow S
making
S
a flat, but not a projective

R
-module.
The main technical problem of the theory of contraherent cosheaves
consists in this fact, and various specific technical problems tend
to arise from this main one.
The discussion in this section suggests that such problems are
surmountable.

8.1.
Preliminary discussion

The interaction of local and global phenomena is a fundamental
aspect of geometry.
Many properties of modules over commutative rings are
local

covering
U=\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^{d}U_{\alpha}
of an affine scheme

For example, the projectivity
[

[

The property of a module over commutative ring to be cotorsion is

This fact stands in the way of a direct attempt to reduce

As pointed out in

colocal

Contraherent cosheaves are defined by gluing modules over commutative

Hence the idea to use contraherent cosheaves in order to obtain
a local proof of the quasi-coherent cotorsion periodicity.

The exposition in this section is based on the discussion of
local, colocal, and antilocal properties in the paper
[
77
]

as well as on certain constructions with contraherent cosheaves

[

The class of cotorsion modules over commutative rings is

[

8.6.
Underived naïve co-contra correspondence

A
co-contra correspondence
is an equivalence between
the coderived category of a comodule-like category and
the contraderived category of a contramodule-like category.
A
naïve co-contra correspondence
is an equivalence
between the conventional derived category of a comodule-like
category and the conventional derived category of a contramodule-like
category.
We refer to the introductions to the papers
[
63
,
67
]
for
a detailed discussion of the philosophy of co-contra correspondence.

In the context of quasi-coherent sheaves and contraherent cosheaves
over a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme
X
,
the
underived naïve co-contra correspondence
is
an equivalence of exact categories
[
61
, Lemma 4.6.7]

(15)

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}\simeq X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}.

Furthermore, it is important for us that the category
equivalence (
15
) restricts to an equivalence
between the full subcategories
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}\subset X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}

and
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}_{\mathsf{al}}\subset X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}
, providing an equivalence
of exact categories
[
61
, proof of Corollary 4.6.8(a)]

(16)

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}\simeq X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}_{\mathsf{al}}.

The equivalence of categories (
15
) is given
by the respective restrictions of the partially adjoint functors

\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},{-})\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muX{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}

and

\mathcal{O}_{X}\odot_{X}{-}\,\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muX{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}

defined in
[
61
, Sections 2.5 and 2.6]
.
It is instructive to look into the constructions of the co-contra
correspondence functors for corings over associative
rings
[
57
, Sections 0.2.6 and 5.1]
,
[
62
, Section 3.4]
,
which served as an inspiration for the more complicated constructions
for sheaves and cosheaves over schemes.

8.7.
Details of the constructions

Let us briefly sketch the constructions of the functors
\fHom_{X}

and
\odot_{X}
here.

The functor of contraherent
\fHom
from a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{M}

to a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{P}
on
X
is defined by the rule

\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})[U]=\Hom_{X}(j_{U}{}_{*}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})

for all affine open subschemes
U\subset X
.
Here
\Hom_{X}({-},{-})=\Hom_{X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}}({-},{-})
is the notation for
the groups of morphisms in the category of quasi-coherent sheaves

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
, while
j_{U}\colon U\longrightarrow X
is the identity open immersion
morphism.
Given two embedded affine open subschemes
V\subset U\subset X
,
there is a natural morphism
j_{U}{}_{*}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow j_{V}{}_{*}j_{V}^{*}\mathcal{M}
of quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
, inducing
a homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})[V]\longrightarrow\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})[U]
.

Similarly to the construction of the contraherent cosheaf

\Cohom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{P})
in Section
5.5
, the contraherence
condition (i) from Section
5.3

holds for
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})
for any pair of quasi-coherent sheaves

\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{P}
on
X
.
The contraadjustedness condition (ii) for
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})
holds under
certain assumptions on
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{P}
, as per
[
61
, Section 2.5]
.
As a result, the contraherent cosheaf
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})
is well-defined
in the following cases:

(1)

if
\mathcal{F}
is a very flat quasi-coherent sheaf and
\mathcal{P}
is
a contraadjusted quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
, then

\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
is a locally contraadjusted contraherent cosheaf;

(2)

if
\mathcal{F}
is a flat quasi-coherent sheaf and
\mathcal{P}
is
a cotorsion quasi-coherent sheaf, then
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
is a locally
cotorsion contraherent cosheaf;

(3)

if
\mathcal{M}
is an arbitrary quasi-coherent sheaf and
\mathcal{J}
is
an injective quasi-coherent sheaf, then
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{J})

is a locally cotorsion contraherent cosheaf;

(4)

if
\mathcal{F}
is a flat quasi-coherent sheaf and
\mathcal{J}
is
an injective quasi-coherent sheaf, then
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{J})

is a locally injective contraherent cosheaf.

So
\fHom_{X}\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{op}}\times X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\dasharrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}
is
a partially defined functor of two arguments
(cf. the discussion of partially defined functors taking values in
exact categories in Section
5.6
).

For the purposes of the naïve co-contra correspondence, we
are interested in cases (1–2) with the structure sheaf
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Then one has

(17)

\displaystyle\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},{-})\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}

\displaystyle\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muX{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}

(18)

\displaystyle\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},{-})\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}

\displaystyle\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muX{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}.

Both these functors are exact by construction (because
the quasi-coherent sheaves
j_{U}{}_{*}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
are very flat on
X
).

For any quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{M}
and contraherent cosheaf
\mathfrak{P}

on
X
, the
contratensor product

\mathcal{M}\odot_{X}\mathfrak{P}
is constructed
as the (nondirected) colimit of the following diagram of quasi-coherent
sheaves on
X
, indexed by the poset of all affine open subschemes

U\subset X
ordered by inclusion.
To every affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, the quasi-coherent sheaf

j_{U}{}_{*}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}\mathfrak{P}[U]
is assigned.
Here the tensor product sign
\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}
means the tensor product
of an object
j_{U}{}_{*}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{M}\in X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
endowed with an action of
the ring
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
and a usual
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-module
\mathfrak{P}[U]
.
For any pair of embedded affine open subschemes
V\subset U\subset X
,
there is a natural isomorphism
j_{V}{}_{*}j_{V}^{*}\mathcal{M}\simeq j_{U}{}_{*}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)
of quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
,
which allows to construct the morphisms in
the diagram
[
61
, Section 2.6]
.

The adjunction isomorphism
[
61
, formula (20) in Section 2.6]

\Hom_{X}(\mathcal{M}\odot_{X}\mathfrak{P},\>\mathcal{J})\simeq\Hom^{X}(\mathfrak{P},\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{J}))

holds for all quasi-coherent sheaves
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{J}
and all
contraherent cosheaves
\mathfrak{P}
on
X
for which the contraherent
cosheaf
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{J})
is well-defined (as per items (1–4) above).
Here
\Hom^{X}({-},{-})=\Hom_{X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}}({-},{-})
is the notation for
the groups of morphisms in the category of contraherent cosheaves

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}
.
Taking
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and making
\mathcal{J}
range over the injective
quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
, one concludes from item (4) that
the restriction of the functor
\mathcal{O}_{X}\odot_{X}{-}
to the full subcategory
of antilocal contraherent cosheaves is an exact functor

(19)

\mathcal{O}_{X}\odot_{X}{-}\,\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muX{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}

[
61
, proof of Lemma 4.6.7]
.

8.8.
Details of the arguments: antilocality

In order to finish the proofs of the exact category
equivalences (
15
)
and (
16
), it still needs to be
explained that the essential image of
the functor (
17
)
is contained in the full subcategory
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}\subset X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}
,
the essential image of the functor (
19
)
is contained in the full subcategory
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}\subset X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
,
and the functor (
19
) takes the full
subcategory
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}_{\mathsf{al}}\subset X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}
into
the full subcategory
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}\subset X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
.
It also needs to be explained why the resulting pair of adjoint
functors between the categories
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}
and
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}

is a category equivalence.

The most important property of the classes of objects involved
on which these proofs are based in the
antilocality
, and
the most important property of the functors involved is
the
compatibility with the direct images
.

First of all, for any affine morphism of quasi-compact semi-separated
schemes
f\colon Y\longrightarrow X
, there is the exact functor of direct image of
quasi-coherent sheaves
f_{*}\colon Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
taking
Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}

into
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}
and
Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
into
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}

[
61
, Section 2.5]
.
Dual-analogously, there is the exact functor of direct image of
contraherent cosheaves
f_{!}\colon Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}

[
61
, Sections 2.3 and 3.3]
taking
Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}
into

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}^{\mathsf{lct}}
and
Y{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}
into
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}

[
61
, Section 4.2]
.

The direct image functors
f_{*}
and
f_{*}
form a commutative square
diagram with the functors
\fHom_{Y}(\mathcal{O}_{Y},{-})
and
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},{-})

[
61
, formula (44) in Section 3.8]
, as well as with
the functors
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\odot_{Y}{-}
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}\odot_{X}{-}

[
61
, formula (46) in Section 3.8]
.

The antilocality property of classes of quasi-coherent sheaves and
contraherent cosheaves applies in the following setting.
Let
X=\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^{d}U_{\alpha}
be a finite affine open
covering of a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme
X
.
Then the contraadjusted quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
are precisely
the direct summands of finitely iterated extensions of the direct images
of contraadjusted quasi-coherent sheaves with respect to the open
immersions
U_{\alpha}\longrightarrow X

[
61
, Corollary 4.1.4(c)]
.
Similarly, the cotorsion quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
are precisely
the direct summands of finitely iterated extensions of the direct
images of cotorsion quasi-coherent sheaves from the open subschemes

U_{\alpha}\subset X

[
61
, Corollary 4.1.11(c)]
.

Furthermore, the antilocal (locally contraadjusted) contraherent
cosheaves on
X
are precisely the direct summands of finitely iterated
extensions of the direct images of contraherent cosheaves
from
U_{\alpha}

[
61
, Corollary 4.2.5(c)]
.
Similarly, the antilocal locally cotorsion contraherent cosheaves on

X
are precisely the direct summands of finitely iterated extensions
of the direct images of locally cotorsion contraherent cosheaves
from
U_{\alpha}

[
61
, Corollary 4.2.6(c)]
.

The proofs of the exact category
equivalences (
15
)
and (
16
) are based on these
observations.
See
[
61
, Lemma 4.6.7 and proof of Corollary 4.6.8(a)]

for further details.

8.9.
Proof of quasi-coherent cotorsion periodicity

Now we can present our proof of the quasi-coherent cotorsion
periodicity theorem.

Proof of
Theorem
8.3
.

We are given an acyclic complex of cotorsion quasi-coherent sheaves

\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
on a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme
X
.
Obviously, any cotorsion quasi-coherent sheaf is contraadjusted.
By Proposition
8.4
,
the sheaves of cocycles of the complex
\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
are, at least,
contraadjusted.

Now the equivalence of exact
categories (
15
) implies that the complex
of antilocal contraherent cosheaves
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
is
acyclic in the exact category
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}
.
Furthermore, by formula (
16
),
the antilocal contraherent cosheaf
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{C}^{n})
is locally
cotorsion for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For every affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, we have a complex
of
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})[U]
.
By the definition of the exact structure on
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}
, which is
inherited by the resolving subcategory
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}\subset X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}
,
the complex of
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})[U]
is acyclic.

By the cotorsion periodicity theorem for modules over
rings
[
2
, Theorem 5.1(2)]
, any acyclic complex
of cotorsion
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules has cotorsion
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules
of cocycles; so the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules of cocycles of the complex

\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})[U]
are cotorsion.
Now it is clear that the complex
\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})[U]
is
acyclic in the exact category of cotorsion
\mathcal{O}(U)
-modules

\mathcal{O}(U){\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
.

As this holds for every affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, we
can conclude that the complex of contraherent cosheaves

\fHom_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
is acyclic not only in the exact category
of antilocal locally contraadjusted contraherent cosheaves

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}
, but also in the exact category of antilocal locally
cotorsion contraherent cosheaves
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--ctrh}}}_{\mathsf{al}}^{\mathsf{lct}}
.
Hence the formula (
16
) tells
that the complex
\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is acyclic in the exact category of
cotorsion quasi-coherent sheaves
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
.
The latter assertion means that the quasi-coherent sheaves of
cocycles of the complex
\mathcal{C}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
are cotorsion, as desired.
∎

8.10.
Conclusion

One can say, and indeed we argued in
Sections
1
–
3
, that contraherent cosheaves
are an important device for globalizing contramodules and
contraderived categories.
Then one needs contraadjusted and/or cotorsion modules over
commutative rings in order to define the contraherent cosheaves
and work with them.

Alternatively, one may be interested in cotorsion modules for
some other, unrelated reasons.
Then one may run into the problem that the class of cotorsion
modules is not preserved by the localization functors in commutative
algebra, and the class of cotorsion quasi-coherent sheaves on
schemes is not local.

If one still wants to develop a local point of view on cotorsion
modules, one needs to use the colocalization functors instead of
the localizations.
In this context, the contraherent cosheaves are indispensable.

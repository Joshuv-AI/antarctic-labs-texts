Higher genus nonorientable maximal surfaces in the Lorentz-Minkowski 3-space
Shoichi Fujimori, Shin Kaneda

Abstract
We study nonorientable maximal surfaces in Lorentz-Minkowski 3-space. We prove some existence results for surfaces of this kind with high genus and one end.

1
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{L}^{3}
be the three dimensional Lorentz-Minkowski space with the metric
\langle{~},{~}\rangle:=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}-dx_{3}^{2}
. An immersion
f:M\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
of a 2-manifold
M
into
\mathbb{L}^{3}
is called
spacelike
if the induced metric
ds^{2}=f^{*}\langle{~},{~}\rangle
is positive definite. A spacelike immersion
f:M\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
is said to be
maximal
if the mean curvature vanishes identically.

Definition 1.1

(Maxfaces
[
UY
]
, see also
[
FKKRSUYY
]
)
.

Let
M
be a Riemann surface and
f:M\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
a
C^{\infty}
-map.

f
is called a maxface if there exists an open dense subset
W
of
M
so that the restriction
f|_{W}:W\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
is a conformal maximal immersion and the rank of
df
at
p
is positive for all
p\in M
.
A point
p\in M
is said to be singular if rank
(df)<2
at
p
.

Theorem 1.2

(Weierstrass-type representation
[
Ko
,
UY
]
)
.

Let
(g,\eta)
be a pair consisting of a meromorphic function
g
and a holomorphic differential
\eta
on a Riemann surface
M
such that

(1.1)

(1+|g|^{2})^{2}\eta\bar{\eta}

gives a Riemannian metric on
M
.
Set

(1.2)

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(1+g^{2})\eta\\
i(1-g^{2})\eta\\
2g\eta\end{pmatrix}.

Suppose that

(1.3)

\operatorname{Re}\oint_{\gamma}\Phi={\bf{0}}

hols for any
\gamma\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}).
Then

(1.4)

f=\operatorname{Re}\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\Phi:M\to\mathbb{L}^{3},\qquad(z_{0}\in M)

is a maxface.
The singular set of f corresponds to
\{p\in M\>|\>|g(p)|=1\}
, and the first and second fundamental forms of
f
are given by

(1-|g|^{2})^{2}\eta\bar{\eta}\quad and\quad\eta dg+\bar{\eta}d\bar{g}

respectively. We call
(M,g,\eta)

(
or
(g,\eta)
)
the Weierstrass data of
f:M\to\mathbb{L}^{3}

(
We define the Weierstrass data of a nonorientable maxface later
)
.

Remark 1.3
.

The condition (
1.3
) is called the period problem, which guaranties the well-defindness of
f
.
It is equivalent to

(1.5)

\oint_{\gamma}g^{2}\eta+\overline{\oint_{\gamma}\eta}=0

and

(1.6)

\operatorname{Re}\oint_{\gamma}g\eta=0

for any
\gamma\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

Definition 1.4

(
[
UY
, Definition 1.3]
)
.

A maxface
f:M\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
is said to be
complete
if there exists a compact set
C
and a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor
T
on
M
such that
T
vanishes on
M\backslash C
and

is a complete Riemannian metric.

Proposition 1.5

(
[
UY
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
be a complete maxface with the Weierstrass data
(M,g,\eta)
.
Then the Riemann surface
M
is biholomorphic to a compact Riemann surface
\overline{M}
minus a finite number of points
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
.
Moreover,

.

We say that a complete maxface

is of genus

\overline{M}
is a compact Riemann surface of genus
k
.

Let
M^{\prime}
be a nonorientable surface with conformal coordinates.
We denote
\pi:M\to M^{\prime}
the orientable conformal double cover of
M^{\prime}
.

Definition 1.6

(
[
FuL
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

A conformal map
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
is said to be a
nonorientable maxface

is a maxface. In addition,
f^{\prime}
is said to be complete if

We say that a complete nonorientable maxface

is of genus

if the double cover

is biholomorphic to

Let

be a nonorientable maxface, and let

denote the antiholomorphic order two deck transformation associated with the orientable double cover

. Since

, we have

(1.7)

Conversely, if

satisfying that

the Weierstrass data of

.

has at most two real roots. Straightforward computations give that

\displaystyle Q_{1}(0)=-(k-1)^{2}<0,

\displaystyle Q_{1}(r_{0}^{-})=\frac{4(k^{2}+k+1)(-1-2k+\sqrt{k(k+2)(2k+1)})}{(k+1)(k+2)^{2}}>0,

\displaystyle Q_{1}\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)=-\frac{1+2k}{(k+1)^{2}}<0,

where

(2.13)

r_{0}^{-}:=\frac{k(k+2)-\sqrt{k(k+2)(2k+1)}}{(k+1)(k+2)}.

Hence the intermediate value theorem yields the existence of the exactly two real numbers
s_{0}\in(0,r_{0}^{-})
and
s_{1}\in(r_{0}^{-},k/(k+1))
such that
Q_{1}(s_{0})=Q_{1}(s_{1})=0
.
Moreover,
Q_{1}|_{(0,s_{0})}<0
,
Q_{1}|_{(s_{0},s_{1})}>0
, and
Q_{1}|_{(s_{1},\infty)}<0
hold.
As
A_{0}(r_{0})>0
, the sign of
p^{\prime}(r_{0})
coincides with that of
Q_{1}(r_{0})
.
Recall that
0\leq r_{0}^{-}<r_{0}
by (
2.10
) and (
2.13
).
Since
p^{\prime}(r_{0})>0
while
r_{0}\in(r_{0}^{-},s_{1})
,
p
has at most one root lying in
(r_{0}^{-},s_{1})
.
Similarly, since
p^{\prime}(r_{0})<0
while
r_{0}\in(s_{1},\infty)
,
p
has at most one root lying in
(s_{1},\infty)
.

It only remains to prove
r_{0}\neq s_{1}
or
Q_{2}(s_{1})<0
holds.
In the case of
k=2
,

\displaystyle Q_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=-9r^{4}+30r^{3}-42r^{2}+18r-1,

\displaystyle Q_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=-9r^{4}+42r^{2}-36r+3.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle s_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{A}+\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{-A+\frac{62}{\sqrt{A}}-9}\approx 0.599176,\quad Q_{2}(s_{1})\approx-4.65184<0,

where
A=2\sqrt[3]{3\sqrt{197}+46}+2\sqrt[3]{46-3\sqrt{197}}-3
.

In the case of
k\geq 3
, the discriminant
D(Q_{2})
of
Q_{2}
satisfies

D(Q_{2})=-256(k+1)^{6}\left(432k^{4}-1448k^{3}+1011k^{2}-162k+19\right).\\

A straightforward computation gives that

\displaystyle 432k^{4}-1448k^{3}+1011k^{2}-162k+19

\displaystyle\quad=432(k-3)^{4}+3736(k-3)^{3}+11307(k-3)^{2}+13464(k-3)+4528.

Therefore
D(Q_{2})<0
and hence
Q_{2}
has two real roots and two complex conjugate roots.
On the other hand, since

\displaystyle Q_{2}(0)

\displaystyle=3(k-1)^{2}>0,

\displaystyle Q_{2}\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=4-4k-\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}}<0,

\displaystyle Q_{2}\left(r_{0}^{-}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-4(k^{5}+2k^{4}-3k^{3}-7k^{2}-8k-3)}{(k+1)^{2}(k+2)^{2}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\dfrac{4(k^{3}-2k^{2}-3k-2)\sqrt{k(k+2)(2k+1)}}{(k+1)^{2}(k+2)^{2}}<0,

Q_{2}
cannot have a real root in (
r_{0}^{-},k/(k+1)
), and hence
Q_{2}(s_{1})<0
.

This proves that
p
has exactly two real roots
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0,1\}
.
∎

3
Symmetry

We consider the symmetry of the surfaces constructed in Theorem
2.2
.
Let
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
be the nonorientable complete maxface constructed in Theorem
2.2
with the maxface
f=f^{\prime}\circ\pi:M_{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
.
An isometry
S:M_{k}\to M_{k}
is said to be a symmetry of
f
if there exists a Lorentzian isometry
\tilde{S}:\mathbb{L}^{3}\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
such that
f\circ S=\tilde{S}\circ f
.
We denote by Sym(
M_{k}
) the symmetry group of
M_{k}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The surface
f:M_{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
intersects the
(x_{1},x_{2})
-plane in
(k+1)
straight lines which meet at equal angles at the origin,
and the symmetry group
Sym
(M_{k})
of the surface
f:M_{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
is equal to the dihedral group
D(k+1)
generated by
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
in
(\ref{eq:k1k2})
.

Proof.

We will determine Sym
(M_{k})
.
The elements of the dihedral group
D(k+1)
generated by
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
in (
2.4
) can be described as follows:

•

K_{1}^{j}
is rotation by
-2\pi j/(k+1)
about the
x_{3}
-axis,
j=1,\dots,k+1
,

•

K_{1}^{j}K_{2}
is the reflectional symmetry with respect to the straight line
x_{2}=-\tan((k-1)j\pi/(k+1))x_{1},x_{3}=0,j=1,\dots,k+1
,

where
K_{1},K_{2}
are given in (
2.6
).
Thus
D(k+1)\subset{\rm{Sym}}(M_{k})
.

Next, we observe the asymptotic behavior of the ends.
At
(z,w)=(0,0)
,
w
is a local coordinate for the Riemann surface
\overline{M_{k}}
, and then

z=z(w)=w^{k+1}\left(-\frac{1}{r}+\mathcal{O}(w^{k+1})\right).

We have

(3.1)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{w}+\mathcal{O}(w^{k}),

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{i\alpha_{1}}{w^{k+1}}+\mathcal{O}(1)\right)dw,

\displaystyle g\eta

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{i\alpha_{2}}{w^{k+2}}+\mathcal{O}(w^{k})\right)dw,

\displaystyle g^{2}\eta

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{i\alpha_{3}}{w^{k+3}}+\frac{\alpha_{4}}{w^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(w^{k-1})\right)dw,

where
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha_{4}\in\mathbb{C}
are constants.
Note that
g\eta
is exact (see (
2.2
)).
This implies that the end of
f
is asymptotic to the end of the surface determined by the Weierstrass data

(g_{0},\eta_{0})=\left(\frac{1}{z},\frac{i}{z^{k+1}}dz\right)\text{\quad on \quad$\mathbb{D}_{\varepsilon}^{*}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\>|\>0<|z|<\varepsilon\}$}.

Moreover, let
f_{0}:\mathbb{D}_{\varepsilon}^{*}\to\mathbb{L}^{3}
be the end of maxface with the Weierstrass data
(g_{0},\eta_{0})
.
Then

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-\sin(k\theta)}{\rho^{k}k}-\frac{\sin(k+2)\theta}{\rho^{k+2}(k+2)},\frac{\cos(k\theta)}{\rho^{k}k}-\frac{\cos(k+2)\theta}{\rho^{k+2}(k+2)},\frac{-2\sin(k+1)\theta}{\rho^{k+1}(k+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

where
z=\rho e^{i\theta}
.
Since

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2}=\frac{1}{(k+2)^{2}\rho^{2(k+2)}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2k+2}}\left(\frac{-2\cos 2\theta}{k(k+2)}+\frac{4\sin^{2}(k+1)\theta}{(k+1)^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\rho^{2})\right),

we have

\mathcal{D}=f_{0}^{-1}(\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{L}^{3}\>|\>x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2}\geq R\})\cup\{0\}\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}_{\varepsilon}}

is a conformal disk if
R
is large enough.
Since Sym(
M_{k}
) leaves
\mathcal{D}
invariant and fixes
0
, the group
\{S|_{\mathcal{D}}\>|\>S\in{\rm{Sym}}(M_{k})\}
is included in
D(k+1)
.
Therefore
{\rm{Sym}}(M_{k})=D(k+1)
.
∎

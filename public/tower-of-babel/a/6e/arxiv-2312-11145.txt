1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper we fix
d\geqslant 2
.
Let
(W_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on a stochastic basis
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},{\mathbf{P}})
.
Consider the following stochastic differential equation (SDE) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
driven by Brownian motion
W
:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ t\geqslant 0,

(1.1)

where the drift
b
is a time-dependent distribution.
Since
b
is a distribution, the drift term does not make a meaning in the classical sense as we cannot assign a value to a distribution at the point
X_{t}
.
To formulate a solution, one natural approach is to employ mollifying approximation.
Let
\phi_{n}(x)=n^{d}\phi(nx)
be a family of modifiers, where
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a smooth probability density function with compact support. Define
the smooth approximation of
b
by convolution as:

\displaystyle b_{n}(t,x):=b(t,\cdot)*\phi_{n}(x).

(1.2)

We begin by introducing the concept of weak solutions for the above SDE with a distributional drift.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the space of all probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Definition 1.1

(Weak solutions)
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}:=(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},{\mathbf{P}})
be a stochastic basis and
(X,W)
be a pair of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued continuous
{\mathscr{F}}_{t}
-adapted processes on
\mathfrak{F}
.
We call
(\mathfrak{F},X,W)
a weak solution of SDE (
1.1
) with initial distribution
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

if
W
is an
{\mathscr{F}}_{t}
-Brownian motion,
{\mathbf{P}}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\mu
, and for all
t\in[0,T]
,

X_{t}=X_{0}+A^{b}_{t}+\sqrt{2}W_{t},\ \ a.s.,

where
A^{b}_{t}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{t}_{0}b_{n}(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
exists in the
L^{2}
-sense.

It is noted that the definition of a weak solution in Definition
1.1
depends on the choice of mollifiers
\phi_{n}
.
The goal of this paper is to find conditions on
b
as weak as possible so that SDE (
1.1
) can be solved in the sense of Definition
1.1
and to construct the associated Markov process.

To present our results, we commence by conducting a basic scale analysis, which enables us to distinguish between subcritical, critical, and supercritical drifts.
Let
{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
(resp.
\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
) be the (resp. homogenous) Bessel potential space, where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
(see
[
5
]
or Subsection 2.1 below for precise definitions).
Suppose that for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
q,p\in[1,\infty]
,

b\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}),

and SDE (
1.1
) admits a solution denoted by
X
.
For
\lambda>0
, define

X^{\lambda}_{t}:=\lambda^{-1}X_{\lambda^{2}t},\ \ W^{\lambda}_{t}:=\lambda^{-1}W_{\lambda^{2}t},\ \ b_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda b(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x).

Formally, one sees that

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{\lambda}_{t}=b_{\lambda}(t,X^{\lambda}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{\lambda}_{t}.

Moreover, by the change of variable we have

\|b_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p})}=\lambda^{1+\alpha-\frac{d}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}\|b\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p})}.

As

, we categorize SDE (
1.1
) into the following three cases:

\mbox{{\bf Subcritical}: $\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1+\alpha$};\ \mbox{{\bf Critical}: $\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1+\alpha$};\ \mbox{{\bf Supercritical}: $\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}>1+\alpha$}.

To solve the SDE (
1.1
), a crucial step involves establishing a more precise regularity estimate for the associated Kolmogorov equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u+f.

(1.3)

Let’s perform a straightforward analysis of the differentiability index
\alpha
when

with
\alpha<0

According to the Schauder theory of the heat equation,

\mathbf{C}^{2+\alpha}

meaningful, we need to stipulate that

\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}

1.1.
Main results

In this subsection, we present our main results in two cases: the subcritical case and the supercritical case. Our first result establishes the well-posedness of SDE (
1.1
) in the subcritical case when
b
is divergence-free.

Theorem 1.2

(Subcritical case)
.

Let
\alpha_{b}\in(-1,-\frac{1}{2}]
and
p_{b},q_{b}\in[2,\infty]
satisfy
\frac{d}{p_{b}}+\frac{2}{q_{b}}<1+\alpha_{b}
.
Suppose that
b\in\cap_{T>0}{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}
is divergence-free. Then for any
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, there is a weak solution to SDE (
1.1
)
starting from initial distribution
\mu
in the sense of Definition
1.1
, which is also unique
in the class that the following Krylov estimate holds:
for any
(\alpha,p,q)\in[\alpha_{b},0]\times[2,\infty]^{2}
with

\displaystyle\tfrac{q_{b}}{2}\leqslant q\leqslant q_{b},\ \ p\leqslant p_{b},\ \ \alpha-\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{2}{q}\geqslant\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}},

(1.5)

for any
T>0
, there is a
\theta>0
such that for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}\cap{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}C^{\infty}_{b}
and
0\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}f(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{C}(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1+\theta}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}},

(1.6)

where the constant
C
only depends on
\theta,m,T,d,\alpha,p,q,\alpha_{b},p_{b},q_{b}
and
\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}}
.
Moreover, for each
t>0
and
X_{0}=x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the law of
X_{t}
admits a density
\rho_{t}(x,x^{\prime})
called the heat kernel of
\Delta+b\cdot\nabla
,
which enjoys the following two sides Gaussian estimate:
for fixed
T>0
, all
x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
t\in(0,T]
,

C_{0}t^{-d/2}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}/t}\leqslant\rho_{t}(x,x^{\prime})\leqslant C_{1}t^{-d/2}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{1}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}/t},

where
C_{0},C_{1},c_{0},c_{1}>0
only depend on the parameters
T,d,\alpha_{b},p_{b},q_{b}
and
\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the subcritical case, our result improves upon
[
20
, Theorem 2.10]
since our initial distribution is not required to have an
L^{2}
-density; it can be a Dirac measure.
Moreover, we are working in the whole space, not in the torus.
Our proof is based on the Schauder estimate of heat equation in Besov spaces
and Zvonkin’s transformation.
Moreover, by (
1.6
), one easily sees that the solution does not depend on the choice of the mollifiers.

To present our main result in the supercritical case, we introduce the following class of distributions for later use:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}:=\left\{b\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\|b\|_{\mathcal{B}}:=\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|b\cdot\nabla\varphi\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}}/\|\varphi\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}<\infty\right\},

(1.7)

where
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
stands for the class of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued Schwartz distributions over

.

Examples:

(i) Suppose that
b=\mathord{{\rm div}}a
, where
a:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is an antisymmetric matrix-valued bounded measurable function. Then

.
In fact, for any

and

\displaystyle\langle b\cdot\nabla\varphi,h\rangle=\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}a\cdot\nabla\varphi,h\rangle=-\langle a,\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla h\rangle\leqslant\|a\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2}\|\nabla h\|_{2},

where we have used that
\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi=0
.

(ii)
{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{\infty,2}\subset{\mathcal{B}}
. We shall prove it after introducing Besov spaces (see Lemma
2.7

In the following we fix a time level

Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{T}:=C([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the Banach space of all continuous functions which is endowed with the natural filtration

(Supercritical case)
.

Let
\alpha_{b}\in[-1,0]

Remark 1.5
.

In the supercritical case, we are unable to demonstrate the uniqueness of weak solutions apriorily. However, the assertion
(A)
affirms that we can identify a subsequence such that, for any initial value, the corresponding approximation solution converges weakly to a solution.
In the case where
b=b_{1}+b_{2}
, with
b_{1}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathcal{B}}
and
b_{2}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}L^{2}
,
it is unique in the approximation sense since it is not necessary to choose a subsequence. Here, an open question is to show the Markov property in
(B)
for all time
s\in[0,T)
. This is closely related to the regularity of the associated Kolmogorov equations.

Remark 1.6
.

One of the motivations for exploring the supercritical case arises from the observation of the super-diffusion phenomenon, as discussed in
[
8
,
9
]
.
In this context, the drift term is defined as the Leray projection of the 2-dimensional white noise, specifically represented as:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big(b(f)b(g)\big)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\hat{f}(\xi)\left({\mathbb{I}}_{2\times 2}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\hat{g}(-\xi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi,

where

is the Fourier transform of Schwartz function

Given the absence of well-posedness results for the related stochastic differential equations as presented in
[

, modifications were introduced to the drift term by truncating its Fourier transform, i.e.

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big(b_{\varepsilon}(f)b_{\varepsilon}(g)\big)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{|\xi|<1/\varepsilon\}}\hat{f}(\xi)\left({\mathbb{I}}_{2\times 2}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\hat{g}(-\xi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi,

where

is taken
1
in
[
9
]
. Note that
estimate (

) and Lemma

below allows us to establish the tightness of solutions to the SDE with the modified drift term

, where
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
.

To establish conclusions

As an application, we consider the following singular interaction particle system in

-density

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})
as

b({\bf x}):=\left(\sum_{j\not=1}\gamma_{j}K(x_{1}-x_{j}),\cdots,\sum_{j\not=N}\gamma_{j}K(x_{N}-x_{j})\right).

It can be verified that
b\in{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{Nd};\mathbb{R}^{Nd})
is divergence-free. Consequently, by Theorem
1.4
above,
the SDE (
1.9
) has a solution
\mathbf{X}^{N}_{t}
with a density
\rho^{N}_{t}
that satisfies the Liouville equation:

\partial_{t}\rho^{N}_{t}=\Delta\rho^{N}_{t}-\sum_{i\not=j}\gamma_{j}K(x_{i}-x_{j})\partial_{x_{i}}\rho^{N}_{t}.

In
[
23
, Proposition 1]
, the entropy solution of the above Liouville equation is directly constructed. However, the existence of a solution to the SDE (
1.9
) appears to be an open question.
In the context of the two-dimensional vortex model, where

K(x)=\frac{(x_{2},-x_{1})}{|x|^{2}}=\Big(-\partial_{x_{1}}\arctan\big(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\big),\partial_{x_{2}}\arctan\big(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\big)\Big)\in{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

known as the Biot-Savart law, and when
\gamma_{j}
has the same sign, Takanobu
[
40
]
established,
through a purely probabilistic argument, the existence and uniqueness of a solution that avoids collisions starting from a
point
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})
with
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
(see also
[
40
]
for some extensions). For general
\gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
Osada
[
31
]
showed the same result based on heat kernel estimates for generators in a generalized divergence form, obtained in
[
32
]
, and on potential theoretical results.
In our construction, two particles of the solution are allowed to meet, and as a result, the singular point can be touched.

1.2.
Comparison with related works

In this subsection we make a detailed comparison with the related well-known results.

Subcritical case:
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},0]
.

Our result, Theorem
4.8
below, not only encompasses but also extends the work presented in
[
14
,
48
]
for the range
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},0]
. In particular, when
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
,
our conditions for the coefficient
b
are not compared to the conditions stated in
[
48
]
, where
b\in\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{p}
with
p>2d
is time-independent. Instead, our result allows
b
to
belong to
{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\mathbf{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{p,1}
with
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<\frac{1}{2}
, which encompasses cases with time integrability index
q\in(4,\infty]
.

Energy method comparison for
\alpha\in(-1,-\frac{1}{2}]
.
In contrast to the energy method employed in
[
20
]
, our approach differs significantly. We will outline these differences within the context of the following three aspects.

•

Dimensional dependence:

Our supercritical condition, which requires
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{p}
with
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p}<1
, is dimension-dependent. This is in contrast to the assumption made in
[
20
,
19
]
,
where they require
b\in\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{2+}
,
and this assumption is independent of dimension.

•

Initial conditions:

In Lemma
5.3
below, our method achieves the tightness without any assumption on the initial data. Furthermore, for the existence of a weak solution and the uniqueness of the generalized martingale solution, we only require that the law of the initial data has an
L^{2}
-density with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

•

Methodology:
Our methodologies differ fundamentally from each other.
In
[
20
]
, the focus is on establishing the existence of a unique energy solution on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. The key aspect of the energy method is to find a stationary distribution
\mu
and show the following Itô trick:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{m}\right]\lesssim_{C}T^{m/2}\|(-{\mathscr{L}}_{s})^{-1/2}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{p},

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{s}:=\frac{1}{2}({\mathscr{L}}+{\mathscr{L}}^{*})
is the symmetric part of the generator
{\mathscr{L}}:=\Delta+b\cdot\nabla
, which is exactly the Laplacian
\Delta
when
\mathord{{\rm div}}b=0
.
On the other hand, in order to consider the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the authors in
[
19
]
introduce an additional term
-\frac{1}{n}X_{t}
to construct the stationary distribution, followed by taking the limit as
n\to\infty
.
This approach makes it challenging to address the time-dependent case.
In our work, we make use of a classical Krylov-type estimate, where the condition
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p}<1
is sharp due to scaling. Reducing the assumption to
p>2
is difficult within our framework. Importantly, our method does not necessitate the existence of a stationary distribution, which can be challenging in unbounded spaces. Additionally, our approach allows us to handle time-dependent drifts efficiently.
Furthermore, our method could be used to deal with the multiplicative noise as well as the degenerate kinetic SDEs, which seems not possible by the energy method developed from
[
20
]
.

Rough path method.
Recently, numerous studies have been dedicated to investigating singular SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions. Due to the absence of Markov and martingale properties for fractional Brownian motions, traditional stochastic analysis arguments are no longer applicable. In
[
6
,
18
]
, path-by-path well-posedness is established by using the Young integral in rough path theory and the stochastic sewing lemma. It’s important to note that this method only works with subcritical drifts.

2.
Prelimiaries

In this section, we recall the definition and some properties of Besov and Bessel potential spaces. Additionally, we establish a Schauder estimate for the heat equation in Besov spaces, which appears to be new. This estimate will be used to solve the Kolmogorov equation in the subcritical case.

Let
{\mathscr{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
be the Schwartz space of all rapidly decreasing functions on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
, and
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})

the dual space of
{\mathscr{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
called Schwartz generalized function (or tempered distribution) space. Given
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
,
the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
and inverse Fourier transform
\check{f}
are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}\xi\cdot x}f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x,\quad\xi\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},

\displaystyle\check{f}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\xi\cdot x}f(\xi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi,\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}.

For
q\in[1,\infty]
and a normed space
{\mathbb{B}}
, we shall simply denote

{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbb{B}}:=L^{q}([0,T];{\mathbb{B}}),\ \ {\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}:={\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}L^{q},

where
(L^{p},\|\cdot\|_{p})
is the usual
L^{p}
-space in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Moreover, we use
C_{b}=C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the Banach space of all bounded continuous functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
and
C^{\infty}_{b}=C^{\infty}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the space of all smooth functions with bounded derivatives of all orders.

2.1.
Besov and Bessel potential spaces

To introduce the Besov space, we first introduce the dyadic partition of unity. Let
\phi_{-1}
be a symmetric
nonnegative
C^{\infty}-
function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\phi_{-1}(\xi)=1\ \mathrm{for}\ \xi\in B_{1/2}\ \mathrm{and}\ \phi_{-1}(\xi)=0\ \mathrm{for}\ \xi\notin B_{2/3}.

For
j\geqslant 0
, we define

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(\xi):=\phi_{-1}(2^{-(j+1)}\xi)-\phi_{-1}(2^{-j}\xi).

(2.1)

By definition, one sees that for
j\geqslant 0
,
\phi_{j}(\xi)=\phi_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)
and

\mathrm{supp}\,\phi_{j}\subset B_{2^{j+2}/3}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}},\quad\sum^{n}_{j=-1}\phi_{j}(\xi)=\phi_{-1}(2^{-(n+1)}\xi)\to 1,\quad n\to\infty.

Definition 2.1
.

For given
j\geqslant-1
, the block operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
is defined on
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
by

{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f(x):=(\phi_{j}\hat{f})\check{\,\,}(x)=\check{\phi}_{j}*f(x),

with the convention
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\equiv 0
for
j\leqslant-2
.
In particular, for
j\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f(x)=2^{jd}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\check{\phi}_{0}(2^{j}y)f(x-y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

(2.2)

For
j\geqslant-1
, by definition it is easy to see that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{j}={\mathcal{R}}_{j}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j},\ \mbox{ where }\ \widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}:={\mathcal{R}}_{j-1}+{\mathcal{R}}_{j}+{\mathcal{R}}_{j+1},

(2.3)

and
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
is symmetric in the sense that

\langle g,{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f\rangle=\langle f,{\mathcal{R}}_{j}g\rangle,\ \ f,g\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stands for the dual pair between
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Now we recall the definition of Besov spaces (see
[
1
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The Besov space
{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,q}
is defined by

{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,q}:=\left\{f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}:=\left(\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{sjq}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f\|_{p}^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty\right\}.

Remark 2.3
.

For
s\in(0,1)
, an equivalent characterization of
{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,q}
is given by (see
[
1
, p74, Theorem 2.36]
or
[
22
, Theorem 2.7]
)

\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}\asymp\left(\int_{|h|\leqslant 1}\left(\frac{\|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)\|_{p}}{|h|^{s}}\right)^{q}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}h}{|h|^{d}}\right)^{1/q}+\|f\|_{p}.

In particular, for any
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, there is a constant
C=C(s,d,p)>0
such that

\|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)\|_{p}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,\infty}}(|h|^{s}\wedge 1),

and for any
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s_{0}}_{p,\infty}}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s_{0}+s}_{p,\infty}}(|h|^{s}\wedge 1).

(2.4)

For
(\alpha,p)\in({\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}})\times[1,\infty]
, let
{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
be the Bessel potential space defined by:

{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}:=\big\{f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}:=\|({\mathbb{I}}-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f\|_{p}<\infty\big\},

where
({\mathbb{I}}-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f
is defined through Fourier’s transform

({\mathbb{I}}-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f:=\big((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\alpha/2}\hat{f}\big)^{\check{}}.

For
\alpha=0,1,2,\cdots
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we define

{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}:=\big\{f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}:=\|f\|_{p}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}f\|_{p}<\infty\big\},

and for
\alpha=-1,-2,\cdots
and
p\in[1,\infty]
,

{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}:=({\mathbf{H}}^{-\alpha}_{p/(p-1)})^{\prime}.

Note that we do not use the space
{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{1}
for
\alpha<0
.
Moreover, for integer
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, an equivalent norm in
{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
is given by (cf.
[
37
, p135, Theorem 3]
)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\asymp\|(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f\|_{p}.

(2.5)

Below we recall some well-known facts about Besov spaces and Bessel potential spaces, where (
2.6
) and (
2.11
) below are easily derived by definition.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i)

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s^{\prime}>s
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow L^{p}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p,\infty},\ \ {\mathbf{B}}^{s^{\prime}}_{p,\infty}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,q}.

(2.6)

(ii)

For any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, we have the following embedding (see
[
5
, Theorem 6.4.4]
)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,2}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p},\ \ p\in[2,\infty),\\
&{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,2},\ \ p\in(1,2],\end{aligned}\right.

(2.7)

and for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in(1,\infty]
(see
[
5
, Theorem 6.2.4]
),

{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,1}\subset{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}\subset{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}.

(iii)

For
1\leqslant p_{1}\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
,
q\in[1,\infty]
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}-\tfrac{d}{p_{1}}+\tfrac{d}{p}
, it holds that (see
[
5
, Theorem 6.5.1]
)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1},q}},

(2.8)

and for
1<p_{1}\leqslant p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1}}}.

(2.9)

(iv)

Let
\alpha\in[-1,0)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. For any
p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
satisfying

p_{1}\geqslant p,\ p_{2}\geqslant\tfrac{p_{1}}{p_{1}-1},\ \ \tfrac{1}{p}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{p_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{p_{2}}<\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{\alpha}{d},

there is a constant
C>0
such that
for all
f\in{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p_{1}}
and
g\in{\mathbf{H}}^{-\alpha}_{p_{2}}
(see
[
48
, Lemma 2.1]
),

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p_{1}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-\alpha}_{p_{2}}},

(2.10)

and

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{\infty}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{\infty}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{\infty}}.

(2.11)

Next we recall the well-known Bony decomposition and related paraproduct estimates.
Let
S_{k}
be the cut-off low frequency operator defined by

\displaystyle S_{k}f:=\sum_{j=-1}^{k-1}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f\to f,\ \ k\to\infty.

(2.12)

For
f,g\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define

f\prec g:=\sum_{k\geqslant-1}S_{k-1}f{\mathcal{R}}_{k}g,\ \ f\circ g:=\sum_{|i-j|\leqslant 1}{\mathcal{R}}_{i}f{\mathcal{R}}_{j}g.

The Bony decomposition of
fg
is formally given by (cf.
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle fg=f\prec g+f\circ g+g\prec f=:f\preceq g+f\succ g.

(2.13)

We recall the following paraproduct estimates (cf.
[
1
, Theorem 2.82 and Theorem 2.85]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p,p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{1},q_{2}\in[1,\infty]
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
and
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(i)

If
\beta<0
, then there is a constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta,d,p,q,p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2})>0
such that

\|f\prec g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p_{2},q_{2}}}.

Moreover, for
\beta=0
, we have

\|f\prec g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{p_{1}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p_{2},q}}.

(ii)

If
\alpha+\beta>0
, then there is a constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta,d,p,q,p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2})>0
such that

\|f\circ g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p_{2},q_{2}}}.

Moreover, when
\alpha+\beta=0
and
q=1
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\circ g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\circ g\|_{p}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{2}}}.

(2.14)

Proof.

We only prove (
2.14
). By definition and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|f\circ g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\circ g\|_{p}\leqslant\sum_{|i-j|\leqslant 1}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{i}f{\mathcal{R}}_{j}g\|_{p}\leqslant\sum_{|i-j|\leqslant 1}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{i}f\|_{p_{1}}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}g\|_{p_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{i}2^{-q_{1}\alpha i}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{i}f\|^{q_{1}}_{p_{1}}\right)^{1/q_{1}}\left(\sum_{j}2^{q_{2}\alpha j}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{i}f\|^{q_{2}}_{p_{2}}\right)^{1/q_{2}}=\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{2}}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we discuss a decomposition of
b\cdot\nabla u
, that arises in the study of PDE (
1.3
),
where
b\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a distribution-valued vector field and

.
By Bony’s decomposition, we can write

\displaystyle b\cdot\nabla u=\{\mathord{{\rm div}}(b\preceq u)+b\succ\nabla u\}-\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u=:b\odot\nabla u-\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u.

(2.15)

In particular, if
\mathord{{\rm div}}b=0
, then

b\cdot\nabla u=b\odot\nabla u.

We have the following regularity estimate.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
\alpha\in(-1,0]
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, there is a
C=C(d,\alpha,p,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|b\odot\nabla u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim_{C}\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1+d/p}_{p,1}}

(2.16)

and

\displaystyle\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p/2,\infty}}\lesssim_{C}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-\alpha-2}_{p,q/(q-1)}}\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha}_{p,q}}.

(2.17)

Proof.

Since
\alpha\in(-1,0]
, by Lemma
2.5
we have

\|\mathord{{\rm div}}(b\preceq u)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim\|b\preceq u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1+\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}}

and

\|b\succ\nabla u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

Hence, by embedding (
2.8
),

\|b\odot\nabla u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim_{C}\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1+d/p}_{p,1}}.

For the second estimate (
2.17
), it follows by Lemma
2.5
.
∎

We also have the following easy result.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
b,\mathord{{\rm div}}b\in{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{\infty,2}
, then
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
, where
{\mathcal{B}}
is defined in (
1.7
).

Proof.

By definition (
2.15
), (
2.7
) and Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\|b\odot\nabla u\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathord{{\rm div}}(b\preceq u)\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}}+\|b\succ\nabla u\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}}\lesssim\|b\preceq u\|_{2}+\|b\succ\nabla u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{2,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\prec u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{2,1}}+\|b\circ u\|_{2}+\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{\infty,2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{\infty,2}}\big(\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1}_{2,2}}+\|u\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}\big),

and

\displaystyle\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}}\lesssim\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\prec u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{2,2}}+\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\circ u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{2,2}}\lesssim\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}_{\infty,2}}\|u\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{1}_{2,2}}.

Combining the above two estimates and by the definition of

and (

2.2.
Schauder’s estimate for heat equation in Besov spaces

In this subsection we study the regularity estimates for heat equation in Besov spaces.
Let
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the Gaussian heat semigroup given by

\displaystyle P_{t}f=p_{t}*f\quad\text{where}\quad p_{t}(x)=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

For
\lambda\geqslant 0
and a time-dependent distribution
f_{t}(\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we define

\displaystyle u^{\lambda}_{t}(x):={\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{t}(f)(x):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}P_{t-s}f_{s}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,\ \ t>0.

(2.18)

In particular, in the distributional sense,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{\lambda}=(\Delta-\lambda)u^{\lambda}+f.

(2.19)

We now show the following Schauder estimate, which shall play a basic role in the subcritical case.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
1\leqslant q\leqslant q^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
and
1\leqslant p\leqslant p^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
. Define

\alpha^{\prime}:=\tfrac{2}{q}-\tfrac{2}{q^{\prime}}+\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{d}{p^{\prime}}.

For any
T>0
, there is a constant
C=C(T,\alpha,d,p,q,p^{\prime},q^{\prime})>0
such that for all
\lambda\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},q}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}},

(2.20)

and for
\theta\in(0,2+\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{2}{q})
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}-\theta}_{p^{\prime},1}}\lesssim_{C,\theta}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}.

(2.21)

Proof.

Let
r\in[1,\infty]
be defined by
1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}
and
T>0
.
For any
j\geqslant-1
, by Young’s inequality and Bernstein’s inequality (see
[
1
, Lemma 2.1, p52]
), one sees that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{t}(f)\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(p_{t-s}*f_{s})\|_{p^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{KJ2}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{t-s}*\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{t-s}\|_{r}\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(d-\frac{d}{r})j}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{t-s}\|_{1}\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Let
r^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
be defined by
1+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}
.
By Young’s inequality for time variable, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{t}(f)\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\lesssim 2^{(d-\frac{d}{r})j}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-r^{\prime}\lambda s}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{s}\|_{1}^{r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

It is well-known that for any
\theta\geqslant 0
, there is a constant
C=C(\theta,d)>0
such that (see
[
21
]
)

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{s}\|_{1}\lesssim_{C}1\wedge(2^{2j}s)^{-\theta},\ \ j\geqslant-1,\ s\in(0,T].

If
r^{\prime}<\infty
, then by the change of variable, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-r^{\prime}\lambda s}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{s}\|_{1}^{r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-r^{\prime}\lambda s}[1\wedge(2^{2j}s)^{-2}]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(2^{-2j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-r^{\prime}2^{-2j}\lambda s}[1\wedge s^{-2}]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(2^{-2j}(1\wedge(r^{\prime}2^{-2j}\lambda)^{-1})\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}.

If
r^{\prime}=\infty
, then we directly have

\sup_{j\geqslant-1}\sup_{s\geqslant 0}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{s}\|_{1}\lesssim 1.

Thus combining the above calculations, we obtain that for any
j\geqslant-1
and
\lambda\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{\cdot}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}L^{p^{\prime}}}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{t}(f)\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim 2^{(d-\frac{d}{r}-\frac{2}{r^{\prime}})j}(1\wedge(2^{-2j}\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}})\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad=2^{(\alpha^{\prime}-2)j}(1\wedge(2^{-2j}\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}})\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\alpha^{\prime}=d-\frac{d}{r}-\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}=\tfrac{2}{q}-\tfrac{2}{q^{\prime}}+\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{d}{p^{\prime}}
.

Now, for any
\gamma\in[1,q]
and
\theta\geqslant 0
, by the definition of Besov norm and Minkowskii’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}-\theta}_{p^{\prime},\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{\gamma(2+\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}-\theta)j}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{\cdot}(f)\|^{\gamma}_{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\right\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{\gamma(2+\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}-\theta)j}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{\cdot}(f)\|^{\gamma}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}L^{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{\gamma(\alpha-\theta)j}(1\wedge(2^{-2j}\lambda)^{-\frac{\gamma}{r^{\prime}}})\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{-\gamma\theta j}(1\wedge(2^{-2j}\lambda)^{-\frac{\gamma}{r^{\prime}}})\left(\int_{0}^{T}2^{q\alpha j}\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{j}f_{s}\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.

In particular, for
\theta=0
, if we take
\gamma=q
, then we get (
2.20
).
For
\theta>0
, if we take
\gamma=1
, then

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}-\theta}_{p^{\prime},1}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{-\theta j}(1\wedge(2^{-2j}\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}})\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}.

Note that for
\theta\in(0,\frac{2}{r^{\prime}})=(0,2+\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{2}{q})
,

\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{-\theta j}(1\wedge(2^{-2j}\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}})\lesssim_{C}1\wedge\lambda^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}\lesssim_{C}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

It is noted that (
2.21
) does not hold for
\theta=0
. Indeed, by (
2.7
) and Fourier’s transform,

\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2}_{2,2}}\asymp\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{2}_{2}}\asymp\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{0}_{2}}\asymp\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{2,2}},

where
\asymp
means that both sides are comparable up to a constant.
Let
1\leqslant q\leqslant q^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
and
1\leqslant p\leqslant p^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
and
\theta\in(0,2-\frac{2}{q})
.
By (
2.20
), (
2.21
) and
{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}
, we immediately have

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha-(\frac{2}{q}-\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{p^{\prime}})}_{p^{\prime},q}}+(1+\lambda)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha-(\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{p^{\prime}})-\theta}_{p^{\prime},1}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}.

(2.22)

3.
A study of PDEs with distribution drifts

In this section, with
\lambda\geqslant 0
, our objective is to investigate the solvability of the nonhomogeneous partial differential equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-\lambda u+b\cdot\nabla u+f,\quad u_{0}\equiv 0,

(3.1)

where
b
and
f
are distributions.
We will establish the well-posedness of PDE (
3.1
) in two cases: the subcritical case and the supercritical case, utilizing different methods.
In the subcritical case, we employ the Duhamel formula, while in the supercritical case, we utilize the maximal principle and the energy method.
Throughout this section we fix a terminal time
T>0
.

3.1.
Subcritical case:
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1+\alpha

In this section, we show the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to PDE (
3.1
) by using Lemma
2.6

under the following subcritical conditions:

(H
sub
)

Let
(\alpha_{b},p_{b},q_{b})\in(-1,-\frac{1}{2}]\times[2,\infty]^{2}
with
\frac{d}{p_{b}}+\frac{2}{q_{b}}<1+\alpha_{b}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\kappa^{b}_{1}:=\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa^{b}_{2}:=\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-2-\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}/(q_{b}-1)}}<\infty.

Remark 3.1
.

Since
\frac{d}{p_{b}}+\frac{2}{q_{b}}<1+\alpha_{b}
and
\alpha_{b}\in(-1,-\frac{1}{2}]
, it is easy to see that for any
q\geqslant\tfrac{q_{b}}{2}
,

\displaystyle 2+\alpha_{b}+\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}<2-\tfrac{4}{q_{b}}\leqslant 2-\tfrac{2}{q}.

(3.2)

Remark 3.2
.

Suppose that
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1/2}_{p_{b},1}
with
\frac{d}{p_{b}}+\frac{2}{q_{b}}<\frac{1}{2}
. Then
(H
sub
)
holds for
\alpha_{b}=-\frac{1}{2}
. Indeed, by (
2.6
)
we have

\kappa^{b}_{1}=\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1/2}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1/2}_{p_{b},1}},\ \ \kappa^{b}_{2}=\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-3/2}_{p_{b},q_{b}/(q_{b}-1)}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1/2}_{p_{b},1}}.

If
b
is time-independent, then it was covered by the condition
b\in{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{p_{b}}
with
p_{b}>2d
in
[
48
]
.

For convenience of notations, we introduce the following parameter set for later use:

\displaystyle\Theta:=(T,d,\alpha_{b},p_{b},q_{b},\kappa^{b}_{1},\kappa^{b}_{2}).

(3.3)

Now we show the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
(H
sub
)
holds. Let
(\alpha,p,q)\in[-1,0]\times[1,\infty]^{2}
satisfy

\tfrac{q_{b}}{2}\leqslant q\leqslant q_{b},\ \ p\leqslant p_{b},\ \ \alpha-\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{2}{q}\geqslant\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}.

For any
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}
,
there is a
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\Theta)>0
such that for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
, there is a unique
u^{\lambda}

with the regularity that for any
\theta\in(0,2+\alpha_{b}+\frac{d}{p_{b}}-\frac{2}{q_{b}}]
,

\displaystyle\|u^{\lambda}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}+(1+\lambda)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\|u^{\lambda}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta}_{p_{b},1}}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}},

(3.4)

where
C=C(\Theta,\alpha,p,q)>0
, which solves the following integral equation:

u^{\lambda}_{t}={\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}_{t}(b\odot\nabla u^{\lambda}-\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u^{\lambda}+f),

where
{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}
is defined by (
2.18
).

Proof.

We only show the a priori estimate (
3.4
). The existence follows by standard Picard’s iteration.
For simplicity of notations, we drop the superscript
\lambda
over
u^{\lambda}
.

By Schauder’s estimate (
2.22
) and (
2.16
), we have for
\theta\in(0,2-\frac{2}{q_{b}})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(b\odot\nabla u)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}+(1+\lambda)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(b\odot\nabla u)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta}_{p_{b},1}}\\
&\qquad\lesssim_{C}\|b\odot\nabla u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}\lesssim_{C}\kappa^{b}_{1}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{1+d/p_{b}}_{p_{b},1}},\end{split}

(3.5)

where
C=C(\Theta,\theta)>0
.
On the other hand, by (
2.21
), we have for any
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,2-\frac{2}{q_{b}})
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2-d/p_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta^{\prime}}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim_{C}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}/2}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p_{b}/2,\infty}},

(3.6)

and for any
\theta^{\prime\prime}\in(0,2-\frac{4}{q_{b}})
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2-d/p_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta^{\prime\prime}}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim_{C}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{2}}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}/2}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p_{b}/2,\infty}}.

(3.7)

Since
\alpha_{b}\in(-1,-\frac{1}{2}]
and
\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}+\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}<1+\alpha_{b}
, we have

\displaystyle 0<\vartheta_{0}:=-\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}<2-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}.

So, for fixed
\theta\in(0,2+\alpha_{b}+\frac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}]
and
\vartheta\in(0,\vartheta_{0})
, it holds that

\vartheta+\theta<2-\tfrac{4}{q_{b}}.

If we take
\theta^{\prime}=\vartheta
in (
3.6
) and
\theta^{\prime\prime}=\vartheta+\theta
in (
3.7
), then by (
2.17
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},1}}+(1+\lambda)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta}_{p_{b},1}}\\
&\qquad\lesssim_{C}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}/2}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{0}_{p_{b}/2,\infty}}\lesssim_{C}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\kappa^{b}_{2}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}},\end{split}

(3.8)

where
C=C(\Theta,\theta,\vartheta)>0
.
Moreover, by
\alpha-\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{2}{q}\geqslant\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}
and (
2.22
), for
\theta\in(0,2-\frac{2}{q})
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}+(1+\lambda)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\|{\mathscr{I}}^{\lambda}(f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}.

(3.9)

Combining (
3.5
), (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and by (
3.2
), we obtain that for any
\theta\in(0,2+\alpha_{b}+\frac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}]

and
\vartheta\in(0,\vartheta_{0})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}+(1+\lambda)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta}_{p_{b},1}}\\
&\qquad\leqslant C_{1}\kappa^{b}_{1}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{1+d/p_{b}}_{p_{b},1}}+C_{2}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\kappa^{b}_{2}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}+C_{3}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}},\end{split}

(3.10)

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(\Theta,\theta)>0
,
C_{2}=C_{2}(\Theta,\theta,\vartheta)>0
and
C_{3}=C_{3}(\Theta,\theta,\alpha,p,q)>0
.

Since
\frac{2}{q_{b}}+\frac{d}{p_{b}}<1+\alpha_{b}
, one can choose
\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}(d,\alpha_{b},p_{b},q_{b})
being close to zero so that

\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{1+d/p_{b}}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim_{C}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta_{0}}_{p_{b},1}}.

Thus by (
3.10
) with
\theta=\vartheta=\theta_{0}
, one can take
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\Theta)>0
large enough so that for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
,

\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}+(1+\lambda)^{\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta_{0}}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}.

The estimate (
3.4
) is then obtained by substituting this into (
3.10
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

By (
2.19
), it is easy to see that the unique solution in the above theorem also satisfies that for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\partial_{t}\langle u^{\lambda},\varphi\rangle=\langle u^{\lambda},\Delta\varphi\rangle-\lambda\langle u^{\lambda},\varphi\rangle+\langle b\odot\nabla u^{\lambda}-\mathord{{\rm div}}b\preceq u^{\lambda},\varphi\rangle+\langle f,\varphi\rangle.

In other words,
u^{\lambda}
is a weak solution of PDE (
3.1
).

3.2.
Supercritical case:
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<2+\alpha

We introduce the following supercritical index set

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}_{d}:=\Big\{(\alpha,p,q)\in[-1,0]\times[2,\infty]^{2},\ \tfrac{d}{p}+\tfrac{2}{q}<2+\alpha\Big\},

(3.11)

and also the following energy space:

{\mathscr{V}}_{T}:=\left\{u:\|u\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}<\infty\right\}.

We have the following simple lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
(\alpha,p,q)\in{\mathscr{I}}_{d}
and
T>0
, there is a constant
C=C(\alpha,p,q,d,T)>0
such that for any
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
and
u,g\in{\mathscr{V}}_{T}
,

\|\langle fu,g\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\|u\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\|g\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}.

Proof.

Let
(\alpha,p,q)\in{\mathscr{I}}_{d}
. Consider the case
p\in[2,\infty)
. Let
r\in(1,2]
be defined by
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=1
.
Let us choose
r_{0}\in(1,r]
so that
-\alpha-\frac{d}{r}\leqslant 1-\frac{d}{r_{0}}
. By Sobolev’s embedding (
2.9
), we have

|\langle fu,g\rangle|\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\|ug\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-\alpha}_{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\|ug\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{r_{0}}}.

Let
\frac{1}{r_{0}}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}
. By (
2.5
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\|ug\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{r_{0}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{r_{1}}\|g\|_{2}+\|u\|_{r_{1}}\|\nabla g\|_{2}+\|g\|_{r_{1}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}.

Hence, for
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}=1
,

\|\langle fu,g\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\Big(\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}L^{r_{1}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}+\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}L^{r_{1}}}\Big).

Since
p,q\in[2,\infty)
satisfies
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<2+\alpha
, one has
r_{1},q_{1}\in[2,\infty)
and
\frac{d}{r_{1}}+\frac{2}{q_{1}}>\frac{d}{2}
. By
[
49
, Lemma 2.1]
, we conclude the desired estimate.

Next for
p=\infty
, by definition we have

|\langle fu,g\rangle|\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{\infty}}\|ug\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-\alpha}_{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{\infty}}\|ug\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{1}},

and by Hölder’s inequality,

\|ug\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}+\|u\|_{2}\|\nabla g\|_{2}+\|g\|_{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}.

Hence,

\|\langle fu,g\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{\infty}}\Big(\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}+\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\Big).

The proof is complete.
∎

Next we make the following assumption on
b
.

(H
sup
)

Let
(\alpha_{b},p_{b},q_{b})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{d}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\kappa^{b}_{1}:=\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa^{b}_{2}:=\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}}<\infty.

Recall the parameter set (
3.3
). Now we can show the following existence and uniqueness result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Under
(H
sup
)
, for any
(\alpha,p,q)\in{\mathscr{I}}_{d}
and
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
,
there is a weak solution
u
to PDE (
3.1
) in the sense that
for any smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle u,\varphi\rangle=\langle u,\Delta\varphi-b\cdot\nabla\varphi+\mathord{{\rm div}}b\,\varphi\rangle+\langle f,\varphi\rangle,

(3.12)

and for some
C=C(\Theta,\alpha,p,q)>0
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}+\|u\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}.

(3.13)

If in addition that
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}L^{2}
, then the uniqueness holds in the class (
3.13
).

Proof.

(Existence)
Let
b_{n}:=b*\phi_{n}
and
f_{n}:=f*\phi_{n}
be the mollifying approximation. Clearly,

\kappa^{b_{n}}_{1}\leqslant\kappa^{b}_{1},\ \ \kappa^{b_{n}}_{2}\leqslant\kappa^{b}_{2}.

Since
b_{n},f_{n}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}C^{\infty}_{b}
,
it is well-known that there is a classical solution
u_{n}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}C^{\infty}_{b}
to the following PDE

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n}=\Delta u_{n}+b_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}+f_{n}=\Delta u_{n}+\mathord{{\rm div}}(b_{n}u_{n})-(\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{n})u_{n}+f_{n},\quad u_{n}(0)=0.

By
[
49
, Theorem 2.2]
, there is a constant
C=C(\Theta,\alpha,p,q)>0
such that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}+\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\lesssim_{C}\|f_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}.

(3.14)

By (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\Delta u_{n}+\mathord{{\rm div}}(b_{n}u_{n})-(\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{n})u_{n}+f_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}}+\|b_{n}u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-2}_{2}}+\|(\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{n})u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2}}+\|f_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2}}\leqslant C,

where
C
is independent of
n
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}(t)-u_{n}(s)\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2}}\leqslant\|\partial_{t}u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2}}|\sqrt{|t-s|}\leqslant C\sqrt{|t-s|}.

By Aubin-Lions’ lemma, there is a function
u\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}\cap{\mathscr{V}}_{T}
and subsequence
\{n_{k}\}
such that
for each
t
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle u_{n_{k}}(t),\varphi\rangle=\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle,\ \ u_{n_{k}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\to}}u\mbox{ weakly in ${\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}$},

(3.15)

and for each
R>0
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\|u_{n_{k}}(s)-u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=0.

Thus, for each
t\in[0,T]
,

\|u(t)\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c},\|\varphi\|_{1}\leqslant 1}\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle=\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c},\|\varphi\|_{1}\leqslant 1}\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle u_{n_{k}}(t),\varphi\rangle\leqslant\sup_{n}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{\infty}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}.

Furthermore, for any
\varphi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we also have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle u_{n_{k}}(t),\varphi\rangle=\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle.

(3.16)

By taking weak limits, it is easy to see that
u
is a weak solution of PDE (
3.1
). Indeed,
since
\frac{d}{p_{b}}+\frac{2}{q_{b}}<2+\alpha
, without loss of generality we may assume
q_{b},p_{b}<\infty
. Otherwise, we may choose
q_{b}^{\prime},p_{b}^{\prime}<\infty
so that

\frac{d}{p^{\prime}_{b}}+\frac{2}{q^{\prime}_{b}}<2+\alpha
.
Noting that for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\langle b_{n_{k}}u_{n_{k}},\nabla\varphi\rangle-\langle bu,\nabla\varphi\rangle=\langle(b_{n_{k}}-b)u_{n_{k}},\nabla\varphi\rangle+\langle u_{n_{k}}-u,b\cdot\nabla\varphi\rangle,

by Lemma
3.5
and
p_{b},q_{b}\in[2,\infty)
, we have

\|\langle(b_{n_{k}}-b)u_{n_{k}},\nabla\varphi\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\lesssim_{C}\|(b_{n_{k}}-b)\chi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}}\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\to}}0,

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}
and
\chi=1
on the support of
\varphi
,
and due to
b\cdot\nabla\varphi\in{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}
, by (
3.15
),

\|\langle u_{n_{k}}-u,b\cdot\nabla\varphi\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\to}}0.

Similarly, we have

\|\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{n_{k}}u_{n_{k}}-\mathord{{\rm div}}bu,\varphi\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\to}}0.

(Uniqueness)
Let
u
be any weak solution of PDE (
3.1
) with
f=0
and satisfy

\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}+\|u\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}<\infty.

Define
u_{n}:=(u*\phi_{n})\chi_{n}
, where
\chi_{n}=n^{-d}\chi(x/n)
and
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a cuttoff function with
\chi(x)=1
on
B_{1}
.
By the chain rule and the definition we have

\partial_{t}\langle u,u_{n}\rangle/2=\langle\partial_{t}u,u_{n}\rangle=\langle u,\Delta u_{n}\rangle-\langle u,b\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle+\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}bu,u_{n}\rangle.

In particular,

\frac{\langle u(t),u_{n}(t)\rangle}{2}=-\int^{t}_{0}\langle\nabla u,\nabla u_{n}\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s-\int^{t}_{0}\Big[\langle u,b\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle-\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}bu,u_{n}\rangle\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Suppose that
b=b_{1}+b_{2}
, where
b_{1}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathcal{B}}
and
b_{2}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}L^{2}
.
Noting that

\displaystyle|\langle u,b_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle|\leqslant\|u\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}\|b_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{n}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}}\leqslant\|u\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}\|b_{1}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}}\leqslant C\|b_{1}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\|u\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}},

(3.17)

and

|\langle u,b_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle|\leqslant\|u\|_{\infty}\|b_{2}\|_{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{\infty}\|b_{2}\|_{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2},

by the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\frac{\|u(t)\|^{2}_{2}}{2}\leqslant-\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big|\langle u,b\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle-\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}bu,u_{n}\rangle\Big|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

By the definition of
{\mathcal{B}}
, it is easy to see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{n}\mbox{ exists in ${\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{2}$},\ \ \lim_{n\to\infty}b_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{n}=b_{2}\cdot\nabla u\mbox{ in $L^{1}$}.

Hence, by
\mathord{{\rm div}}b\in L^{\infty}
and commutating the order of limits (guaranteeing by the above two limits),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle u,b\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\langle u_{m},b\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}b\,u_{m},u_{n}\rangle-\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\langle b\cdot\nabla u_{m},u_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}b\,u,u\rangle-\lim_{m\to\infty}\langle b\cdot\nabla u_{m},u\rangle,

which implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle u,b\cdot\nabla u_{n}\rangle=\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}b\,u,u\rangle/2.

Thus,

\frac{\|u(t)\|^{2}_{2}}{2}\leqslant-\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}|\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}b\,u,u\rangle|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{2}^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Now by Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain
u(t)=0
. The uniqueness is proven.
∎

4.
Subcritical case: Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we establish the weak well-posedness of SDE (
1.1
) under the subcritical assumption
(H
sub
)
.
To achieve this, we employ the classical Zvonkin transformation, ensuring both the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution.

Throughout this section we fix
T>0
. To introduce a general notion of Krylov’s estimate, we start by fixing a Banach space
{\mathbb{B}}
that consists of distributions
f_{t}:[0,T]\to{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
so that

{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}C_{b}\cap{\mathbb{B}}
is dense in
{\mathbb{B}}
. Moreover, we make the following assumption:

\bullet

For any bounded measurable function
g:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with that the function
x\to g(t,x)
is uniformly Lipschitz with respect to
t\in[0,T]
, the product
gf
is well-defined for all
f\in{\mathbb{B}}
,
and there is a constant
C_{g}>0
such that for all
f\in{\mathbb{B}}
,

\displaystyle\|gf\|_{{\mathbb{B}}}\leqslant C_{g}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{B}}}.

(4.1)

It’s worth noting that the space
{\mathbb{B}}:={\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}
with
\alpha\in[-1,0]
and
p,q\in[1,\infty)
satisfies the above assumption.
Indeed, (
4.1
) follows by Lemma
2.5
. Moreover, let
\phi_{n}(x)=n^{d}\phi(nx)
, where
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a smooth probability density function. For
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}
, if we define

f_{n}(t,x)=f(t,\cdot)*\phi_{n}(x),

then by definition,
f_{n}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}C^{\infty}_{b}
, and by the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leqslant\int^{T}_{0}\sum_{j\geqslant-1}2^{\alpha jq}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\phi_{n}*{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f(t)-{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f(t)\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t=0.

It must be noticed that for
q=\infty
or
p=\infty
, the above property does not hold since
C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is not dense in
L^{\infty}
.
However, for
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}
, by (
2.4
), it holds that for any
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
,

\|f_{n}-f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\infty,\infty}}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\infty,\infty}}n^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}.

This is also the only reason that we require
p,q<\infty
below.

Now we introduce the following notion used below.

Definition 4.1

(Krylov’s estimate)
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued stochastic process on a probability space
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbf{P}})
.
For
\theta\in(0,1)
, one says that
(X,{\mathbb{B}})
satisfies Krylov’s estimate with parameter
\theta
if for any
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}C_{b}\cap{\mathbb{B}}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and any stopping times
\tau_{0}\leqslant\tau_{1}\leqslant T
with
\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}\leqslant\delta
,

\left\|\int^{\tau_{1}}_{\tau_{0}}f(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\delta^{\frac{1+\theta}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathbb{B}},

where the constant
C=C(m,{\mathbb{B}},d,T)>0
.

Remark 4.2
.

Here we require
\theta>0
, that is crucial for Young’s integral in (
4.5
) below.

The following proposition is a direct conclusion of Krylov’s estimate.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
(X,{\mathbb{B}})
satisfies Krylov’s estimate with parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
.
For any
f\in{\mathbb{B}}
, let
f_{n}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}C_{b}\cap{\mathbb{B}}
be such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{{\mathbb{B}}}=0.

Then the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{\cdot}_{0}f_{n}(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
exists in
L^{p}(\Omega,C([0,T]))
for any
p\geqslant 1
and the limit is denoted by

A^{f}_{t}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{t}_{0}f_{n}(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
, which does not depend on the choice of approximation sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Furthermore, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|A^{f}_{t}\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}+\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,T]}\frac{\|A^{f}_{t}-A^{f}_{s}\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}}{|t-s|^{(1+\theta)/2}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{\mathbb{B}}.

(4.2)

We now show a substitution formula for Young’s integrals that will be used to show the uniqueness by Zvonkin’s transformation.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
(X,{\mathbb{B}})
satisfies Krylov’s estimate with parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
and for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|X_{0}\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}+\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{C}|t-s|^{1/2},\quad\forall s,t\in[0,T].

(4.3)

Let
g:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded function satisfying

\displaystyle|g(t,x)-g(s,y)|\lesssim_{C}\sqrt{|t-s|}+|x-y|.

(4.4)

Then for any space-time function
f\in{\mathbb{B}}
, the integral
\int^{t}_{0}g(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}A^{f}_{s}
is well-defined as Young’s integral
and

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}g(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}A^{f}_{s}=A^{f\cdot g}_{t}\ \ a.s.,

(4.5)

where
A^{f}
is defined in Proposition
4.3
.

Proof.

For a function
h:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we write

\displaystyle[h]_{\alpha}:=\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,T]}\frac{|h(t)-h(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}.

By (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion, for any
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1+\theta}{2})
,
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in{\mathbb{B}}
,

\displaystyle\big\|[A^{f}_{\cdot}]_{\alpha}\big\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{\mathbb{B}},\ \ \big\|[X_{\cdot}]_{\beta}\big\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}<\infty.

(4.6)

Below we fix
\alpha>\frac{1}{2},\beta<\frac{1}{2}
with
\alpha+\beta>1
and a sample point
\omega
such that

\displaystyle[A^{f}_{\cdot}(\omega)]_{\alpha}<\infty,\ \ [X_{\cdot}(\omega)]_{\beta}<\infty.

(4.7)

For any
0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T
, we define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s,t}(\omega):=g(s,X_{s}(\omega))(A^{f}_{t}(\omega)-A^{f}_{s}(\omega)).

For simplicity, we drop the dependence of
\omega
.
We note that for any
0\leqslant s<u<t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\delta\Gamma_{s,u,t}:=\Gamma_{s,t}-\Gamma_{s,u}-\Gamma_{u,t}=(g(s,X_{s})-g(u,X_{u}))(A^{f}_{t}-A^{f}_{u}).

By (
4.4
) and (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle|\delta\Gamma_{s,u,t}|\lesssim|u-s|^{\beta}|t-u|^{\alpha}[X_{\cdot}]_{\beta}[A^{f}_{\cdot}]_{\alpha}.

Since
\alpha+\beta>1
, by the sewing lemma (cf.
[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
), we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}g(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}A^{f}_{r}:=\lim_{|\pi|\to 0}\sum_{r,s\in\pi}\Gamma_{r,s}\quad\text{exists},

where
\pi
is any partition of
[0,t]
, and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}(g(r,X_{r})-g(s,X_{s})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}A^{f}_{r}\right|=|\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{s}-\Gamma_{s,t}|\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\beta}[X_{\cdot}]_{\beta}[A^{f}_{\cdot}]_{\alpha}.

In particular, taking
s=0
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}A^{f}_{r}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|g\|_{\infty}|A^{f}_{t}|+C[X_{\cdot}]_{\beta}[A^{f}_{\cdot}]_{\alpha}.

(4.8)

Now, let
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}C_{b}\cap{\mathbb{B}}
be such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{{\mathbb{B}}}=0
. By definition, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}A^{f_{n}}_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}(gf_{n})(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r=A^{gf_{n}}_{t}\quad a.s.

(4.9)

By (
4.8
), (
4.6
) and (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}(A^{f_{n}}_{r}-A^{f}_{r})\right|\right)+{\mathbf{E}}|A^{gf_{n}}_{t}-A^{gf}_{t}|

\displaystyle\quad={\mathbf{E}}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}A^{f_{n}-f}_{r}\right|\right)+{\mathbf{E}}|A^{g(f_{n}-f)}_{t}|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|f-f_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{B}}}+\|g(f-f_{n})\|_{{\mathbb{B}}}\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Thus, there is a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}(A^{f_{n_{k}}}_{r}-A^{f}_{r})\right|+|A^{gf_{n_{k}}}_{t}-A^{gf}_{t}|=0\quad a.s.

By taking
n=n_{k}
and letting
k\to\infty
in (
4.9
), we get (
4.5
).
∎

Below we always suppose that
(H
sub
)
holds with
q_{b},p_{b}<\infty
.
Let

\theta_{0}:=1+\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}},\ \ b_{n}(t,x):=b(t,\cdot)*\phi_{n}(x).

By the property of convolution, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa^{b_{n}}_{1}=\|b_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}&\leqslant\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}}=\kappa^{b}_{1},\\
\kappa^{b_{n}}_{2}=\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-2-\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}/(q_{b}-1)}}&\leqslant\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{-2-\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}/(q_{b}-1)}}=\kappa^{b}_{2}.\end{split}

(4.10)

We consider the following approximation SDE:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{n}_{t}=b_{n}(t,X^{n}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ X^{n}_{0}\sim\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

(4.11)

For simplicity of notations, we write

{\mathscr{I}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}:=\Big\{(\alpha,p,q)\in[-1,0]\times[1,\infty)^{2}:\tfrac{q_{b}}{2}\leqslant q\leqslant q_{b},\ \ p\leqslant p_{b},\ \ \alpha-\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{2}{q}\geqslant\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}\Big\}.

Now we can show the following crucial Krylov estimate.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
(\alpha,p,q)\in{\mathscr{I}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}
,
(X^{n},{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q})
satisfies Krylov’s estimate with parameter
\theta_{0}
uniformly in
n
.

Proof.

Given
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, let
u_{n}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}C^{\infty}_{b}
be the unique smooth solution to the following backward PDE

\partial_{t}u_{n}+\Delta u_{n}-\lambda u_{n}+b_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}+f=0,\ \ u_{n}(T)=0.

For any
(\alpha,p,q)\in{\mathscr{I}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b},q_{b}}
, by (
3.4
) and (
4.10
), there is a
\lambda_{0}\geqslant 1
so that for all
\theta\in(0,2+\alpha_{b}+\frac{d}{p_{b}}-\frac{2}{q_{b}}]
and
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}.

(4.12)

By Itô’s formula, we have

u_{n}(t,X^{n}_{t})=u_{n}(0,x)+\int^{t}_{0}(\lambda u_{n}-f)(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}\int^{t}_{0}\nabla u_{n}(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}.

In particular, for any stopping time
\tau_{0}\leqslant\tau_{1}\leqslant T
with
\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}\leqslant\delta
,

\left|\int^{\tau_{1}}_{\tau_{0}}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|\leqslant 2\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}+\lambda\delta\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{2}\left|\int^{\tau_{1}}_{\tau_{0}}\nabla u_{n}(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}\right|.

By BDG’s inequality, we have for any
m\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{\tau_{1}}_{\tau_{0}}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\lambda\delta)\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}+m\left\|\left(\int^{\tau_{1}}_{\tau_{0}}|\nabla u_{n}(s,X^{n}_{s})|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\lambda\delta)\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}+m\delta^{1/2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{\infty}.

Here and below, the implicit constant is independent of
m,n,\delta,\lambda
.
Since

by (

) and embedding (

), we have

\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{d/p_{b}}_{p_{b},1}}=\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-(1+\theta_{0})}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{1+\theta_{0}}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}},

and

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{1+d/p_{b}}_{p_{b},1}}=\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\alpha_{b}-2/q_{b}-\theta_{0}}_{p_{b},1}}\lesssim(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{\tau_{1}}_{\tau_{0}}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[(1+\lambda\delta)(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{1+\theta_{0}}{2}}+m\sqrt{\delta}(1+\lambda)^{-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}}\right]\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}.

In particular, if we take

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{\tau_{1}}_{\tau_{0}}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\lesssim m\delta^{\frac{1+\theta_{0}}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}.

(4.13)

By a standard approximation, the above estimate still holds for any

. The proof is complete.

5.
Supercritical case: Proof of Theorem
1.4

Throughout this section we still fix
T>0
and always assume that supercritical assumption
(H
sup
)
holds.
In Subsection
5.1
, we focus on establishing the existence of weak solutions. The crucial step in this regard is demonstrating the tightness of approximation solutions. Notably, in the supercritical case, due to the drift being a distribution, it’s not possible to establish a Krylov estimate similar to that in Lemma

for any moments. Instead, we can only establish a Krylov estimate for the second-order moment, as demonstrated in Lemma

In Subsection

5.1.
Existence of weak solutions

We still consider the approximation SDE

{\mathbf{E}}|X^{n}_{\tau}(x_{0})-x_{0}|\leqslant\ell(\delta)/3+2d\delta/\ell(\delta)+\ell(\delta)/3\leqslant\ell(\delta).

The proof is complete since
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int^{(t+\delta)\wedge T}_{t}|g(s)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=0
for
g\in L^{1}([0,T])
.
∎

By the above lemma and Prokhorov’s theorem,
({\mathbb{P}}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is relatively compact in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})
.
Next, we want to show that any accumulation point of the sequence
({\mathbb{P}}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is indeed a weak solution of SDE (
1.1
).
However, to take the limit, we need a different type of Krylov’s estimate, which, in turn, requires that the initial distribution has an
L^{2}
-distribution.

For approximation SDE (
5.1
), it is well-known that for any
t\in(0,T]
,
X^{n}_{t}
admits a smooth density
\rho_{n}(t,x)
that satisfies the Fokker-Planck equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{n}=\Delta\rho_{n}-\mathord{{\rm div}}(b_{n}\rho_{n}).

(5.5)

We need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}
and let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\chi=1
on
B_{1}
and
\chi=0
on
B^{c}_{2}
. We have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\leqslant(\kappa^{b}_{2}+1)\mathrm{e}^{\kappa^{b}_{2}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{2},

(5.6)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{n}\|\rho_{n}(1-\chi(\cdot/R))\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}=0.

(5.7)

Proof.

First of all, since
b_{n}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}C^{\infty}_{b}
, by the maximum principle, we have

\rho_{n}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. By multiplying both sides of (
5.5
) by
\varphi
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{n}\varphi=\Delta(\rho_{n}\varphi)-\mathord{{\rm div}}(b_{n}\rho_{n}\varphi)+g^{\varphi}_{n},

(5.8)

where

g^{\varphi}_{n}:=b_{n}\rho_{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi-2\nabla\rho_{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi-\rho_{n}\Delta\varphi.

Multiplying both sides of (
5.8
) by
\rho_{n}\varphi
and integrating on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|\rho_{n}\varphi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla(\rho_{n}\varphi)\|_{2}^{2}=\langle b_{n}\rho_{n}\varphi_{n},\nabla(\rho_{n}\varphi)\rangle+\langle g^{\varphi}_{n},\rho_{n}\varphi\rangle.

Noting that

\langle b_{n}\rho_{n}\varphi_{n},\nabla(\rho_{n}\varphi)\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{n},(\rho_{n}\varphi)^{2}\rangle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{n}\|_{\infty}\|\rho_{n}\varphi\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{\infty}\|\rho_{n}\varphi\|^{2}_{2}

and

\langle g^{\varphi}_{n},\rho_{n}\varphi\rangle=\langle b_{n},\varphi\rho^{2}_{n}\nabla\varphi\rangle+\langle\rho^{2}_{n},|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\rangle,

we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{n}(t)\varphi\|^{2}_{2}+2\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla(\rho_{n}\varphi)\|^{2}_{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\rho_{0}\varphi\|^{2}_{2}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{\infty}\|\rho_{n}\varphi\|^{2}_{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}\Big(\langle b_{n},\varphi\rho^{2}_{n}\nabla\varphi\rangle+\langle\rho^{2}_{n},|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\rangle\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\|\rho_{n}\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\leqslant(\kappa^{b}_{2}+1)\mathrm{e}^{\kappa^{b}_{2}}\left(\|\rho_{0}\varphi\|_{2}+\|\langle b_{n},\varphi\rho^{2}_{n}\nabla\varphi\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|\langle\rho^{2}_{n},|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\right).

(5.9)

Let
\chi_{R}(x):=\chi(x/R).

Replacing
\varphi
in (
5.9
) by
\chi_{R}
and letting
R\to\infty
, by the dominated convergence theorem, it is easy to derive

\|\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\leqslant(\kappa^{b}_{2}+1)\mathrm{e}^{\kappa^{b}_{2}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{2}.

On the other hand, replacing
\varphi
in (
5.9
) by
\bar{\chi}_{R}:=1-\chi_{R}
, we get

\|\rho_{n}\bar{\chi}_{R}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\leqslant(\kappa^{b}_{2}+1)\mathrm{e}^{\kappa^{b}_{2}}\left(\|\rho_{0}\bar{\chi}_{R}\|_{2}+\|\langle b_{n},\bar{\chi}_{R}\rho^{2}_{n}\nabla\bar{\chi}_{R}\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|\langle\rho^{2}_{n},|\nabla\bar{\chi}_{R}|^{2}\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\right).

By Lemma
3.5
and the definition of
{\mathscr{V}}_{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\langle b_{n},\bar{\chi}_{R}\rho^{2}_{n}\nabla\bar{\chi}_{R}\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{q_{b}}}\|\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\|\bar{\chi}_{R}\nabla\bar{\chi}_{R}\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{q_{b}}}\|\rho_{n}\|^{2}_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}/R\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\|^{2}_{2}/R

and

\|\langle\rho^{2}_{n},|\nabla\bar{\chi}_{R}|^{2}\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\lesssim\|\rho_{n}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}/R\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\|^{2}_{2}/R.

Hence,

\sup_{n}\|\rho_{n}\bar{\chi}_{R}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\bar{\chi}_{R}\|_{2}+\|\rho_{0}\|^{2}_{2}/R,

which converges to zero as
R\to\infty
. The proof is complete.
∎

We have the following Krylov estimate.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}
. Under
(H
sup
)
, for any
(\alpha,p,q)\in{\mathscr{I}}_{d}
,
there is a constant
C=C(\Theta,\alpha,p,q)>0
such that for all
t\in(0,T]
and
f\in C_{c}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int^{t}_{0}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{2}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{2}.

(5.10)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Fix
t\in[0,T]
. Let
u
solve the following backward PDE

\partial_{s}u_{n}+\Delta u_{n}+b_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}+f=0,\quad u_{n}(t)=0.

By (
5.3
) with
\tau_{0}=s<t
and
\tau_{1}=t
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f(r,X_{r}^{n}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathscr{F}}_{s}\right)=u_{n}(s,X^{n}_{s}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X_{s}^{n})\left(\int_{s}^{t}f(r,X_{r}^{n}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]

\displaystyle=2{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X_{s}^{n}){\mathbf{E}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f(r,X_{r}^{n}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathscr{F}}_{s}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]

\displaystyle=2{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X_{s}^{n})u_{n}(s,X_{s}^{n}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]

\displaystyle=2\int^{t}_{0}\langle f(s)u_{n}(s),\rho_{n}(s)\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant 2\|\langle fu_{n},\rho_{n}\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{t}}.

(5.11)

By Lemma
3.5
, we get

{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\|\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{t}}\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{t}}.

The desired estimate follows by (
5.6
) and (
5.2
).
∎

Remark 5.6
.

One can not show the following stronger estimate

{\mathbf{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{2}\right)\lesssim_{C}\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{2}

since we are using the estimate (
5.6
) of the density and
f
is a distribution.

Now we can show the following existence result of weak solutions.

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose that
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}
. Under
(H
sup
)
, there is a weak solution to SDE (
1.1
)
such that for all
t\in(0,T]
and
f\in C_{c}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int^{t}_{0}f(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{2}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{2},

(5.12)

where
C=C(\Theta,\alpha,p,q)>0
.
Moreover, for each
t\in(0,T]
,
X_{t}
admits a density
\rho_{t}\in L^{2}
with
\|\rho\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\leqslant(\kappa^{b}_{2}+1)\mathrm{e}^{\kappa^{b}_{2}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{2}
and in the distributional sense

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta\rho-\mathord{{\rm div}}(b\rho).

(5.13)

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
and Prokhorov’s criterion, there is a subsequence
n_{k}
and a probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})
so that
{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}
weakly converges to
{\mathbb{P}}
as
k\to\infty
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
weakly converges to
{\mathbb{P}}
as
n\to\infty
.
By Skorokhod’s representation theorem, if necessary, changing the probability space,
we may assume that there is a continuous process
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that

\displaystyle X_{\cdot}^{n}\to X_{\cdot}\quad\text{a.s. in ${\mathbb{C}}_{T}$.}

Estimate (
5.12
) follows by taking limits for (
5.10
).
To show that
X
is a weak solution of SDE (
1.1
),
it suffices to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}b_{n}(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r-\int_{0}^{t}b_{n}(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right|^{2}=0.

Noticing that for fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}b_{m}(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r-\int_{0}^{t}b_{m}(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right|^{2}=0,

once we have shown

\displaystyle\lim_{m,m^{\prime}\to\infty}\sup_{n}{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}})(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right|^{2}=0,

(5.14)

then it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}b_{n}(r,X_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r=:A^{b}_{t}\mbox{ in $L^{2}$}.

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\chi=1
on
B_{1}
and
\chi=0
on
B^{c}_{2}
. For
R>0
, define cutoff function

\chi_{R}(x):=\chi(x/R).

Since
\frac{d}{p_{b}}+\frac{2}{q_{b}}<2+\alpha
, one can choose finite
\bar{q}_{b},\bar{p}_{b}\in[2,\infty)
with
\bar{q}_{b}\leqslant q_{b},\bar{p}_{b}\leqslant p_{b}
and such that
\frac{d}{\bar{p}_{b}}+\frac{2}{\bar{q}_{b}}<2+\alpha
.
Then for fixed
R>0
, by Lemma
5.5
, we have

\lim_{m,m^{\prime}\to\infty}\sup_{n}{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}((b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}})\chi_{R})(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right|^{2}\lesssim\lim_{m,m^{\prime}\to\infty}\|(b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}})\chi_{R}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\bar{q}_{b}}_{t}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{\bar{p}_{b}}}=0.

Thus for proving (
5.14
), it remains to show that for
\bar{\chi}_{R}(x):=1-\chi_{R}(x)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{m,m^{\prime}}\sup_{n}{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}((b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}})\bar{\chi}_{R})(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right|^{2}=0.

(5.15)

Let
u^{m,m^{\prime}}_{n,R}=u
solve the following backward PDE:

\partial_{s}u+\Delta u+b_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}+(b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}})\bar{\chi}_{R}=0,\quad u(t)=0.

By (
5.11
) and Lemma
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}((b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}})\bar{\chi}_{R})(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right|^{2}\leqslant 2\|\langle(b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}})\bar{\chi}_{R}u^{m,m^{\prime}}_{n,R},\rho_{n}\rangle\|_{L^{1}_{t}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|b_{m}-b_{m^{\prime}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{t}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}}\|\bar{\chi}_{R}\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{t}}\|u^{m,m^{\prime}}_{n,R}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{t}}\lesssim\|b\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{t}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}}\|\bar{\chi}_{R}\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{t}},

which implies (
5.15
) by (
5.7
).
Finally, for (
5.13
), as in the proof of existence in Theorem
3.6
, it follows by (
5.5
) and (
5.6
).
∎

5.2.
Generalized martingale problems

Let
(\alpha,p,q)\in{\mathscr{I}}_{d}
(see (
3.11
) for a definition). For any
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
, by Theorem
3.6
, there exists a weak solution to the following backward PDE

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u=f,\quad u(T)\equiv 0

(5.16)

with

\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}\cap{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}}.

Note that the weak solution may be not unique if we do not assume
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}
. However, if we consider the following regularized PDE

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n}+\Delta u_{n}+b_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}=f,\quad u_{n}(T)\equiv 0.

(5.17)

Then for each
n
, there is a unique solution
u_{n}
denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{f}^{n}
so that

\|{\mathcal{S}}_{f}^{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}\cap{\mathscr{V}}_{T}}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}},

where
C
is independent of
n
.
In other words,
f\mapsto{\mathcal{S}}_{f}^{n}
is a bounded linear operator from
{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
to
{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}\cap{\mathscr{V}}_{T}
. By Banach-Steinhaus theorem, there is a subsequence
n_{k}
and a bounded linear operator
{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}\to{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}\cap{\mathscr{V}}_{T}
so that
for each
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha}_{p}
and
t\in[0,T]
,
\varphi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(see the proof of Theorem
3.6
),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle{\mathcal{S}}_{f}^{n_{k}}(t),\varphi\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t),\varphi\rangle,

(5.18)

and for each
R>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\|{\mathcal{S}}_{f}^{n_{k}}(s)-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=0.

(5.19)

In the following we fix such an operator
{\mathcal{S}}
and construct a unique generalized martingale solution associated with
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Of course, its definition depends on the choice of subsequence
n_{k}
as well as the mollifiers
\phi_{n_{k}}
.
We emphasize that when
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{f}
is nothing, but the unique solution of (
5.16
), which is independent of the choice of
n_{k}
and the mollifiers.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We call a probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})
a generalized martingale solution of SDE (
1.1
) starting from
\mu
and associated with the operator
{\mathcal{S}}
,
if
{\mathbb{P}}\circ({w}_{0})^{-1}=\mu
and for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle M_{t}^{f}:={\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(0,{w}_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}f(r,{w}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,\ \ w_{\cdot}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{T},

is an almost surely martingale under
{\mathbb{P}}
with respect to the natural filtration
{\mathscr{B}}_{s}
in the sense that there is a Lebesgue null set
{\mathscr{N}}\subset(0,T)
such that for all
0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T
with
s,t\notin{\mathscr{N}}
,

{\mathbb{E}}(M^{f}_{t}|{\mathscr{B}}_{s})=M^{f}_{s}.

Remark 5.9
.

Since, for each
t\in[0,T]
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}
, in order to make sense of
M_{t}^{f}
, we have assumed that the marginal distribution
{\mathbb{P}}\circ w^{-1}_{t}
has a density.
However, it’s essential to note that although
t\mapsto M^{f}_{t}
is defined pointwise, we lack information about the path properties of
t\mapsto M^{f}_{t}
.
For instance, we have no knowledge of its cádlág property since we only have
{\mathcal{S}}_{f}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}
.

Now we show the following main result.

Theorem 5.10
.

Assume
(H
sup
)
holds. If the initial distribution
\mu
has an
L^{2}
-density w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure, then there is a unique
generalized martingale solution to SDE (
1.1
) associated with the operator
{\mathcal{S}}
in the sense of Definition
5.8
.

Proof.

(Existence)

Let
{\mathbb{P}}
be any accumulation point of
({\mathbb{P}}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. We want to show that
{\mathbb{P}}
is a martingale solution in the sense of Definition
5.8
.
Up to taking a subsequence, without loss of generality we assume that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\to{\mathbb{P}}\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$ in ${\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})$ weakly.}

For
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, let
u_{n}={\mathcal{S}}^{n}_{f}
solve the following backward PDE:

\partial_{t}u_{n}+\Delta u_{n}+b_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}=f,\ \ u_{n}(T)=0.

By Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle u_{n}(t,X^{n}_{t})=u_{n}(0,X^{n}_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}\int^{t}_{0}\nabla u_{n}(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}.

(5.20)

Define

\displaystyle M^{n}_{t}:=u_{n}(t,{w}_{t})-u_{n}(0,{w}_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}f(r,{w}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

Since
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}={\mathbf{P}}\circ(X^{n}_{\cdot})^{-1}
, by (
5.20
) one sees that
M^{n}_{t}
is a martingale under
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
with respect to
{\mathscr{B}}_{s}
.
More precisely, for any
s<t
and bounded continuous
{\mathscr{B}}_{s}
-measurable functional
G_{s}
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}((M^{n}_{t}-M^{n}_{s})G_{s})=0,

equivalently,

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\Big(((u_{n}(t,{w}_{t})-u_{n}(s,{w}_{s}))G_{s}\Big)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\left(G_{s}\int_{s}^{t}f(r,{w}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right).

Clearly, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\left(G_{s}\int_{s}^{t}f(r,{w}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(G_{s}\int_{s}^{t}f(r,{w}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right).

(5.21)

By (
5.19
), there is a Lebesgue null set
{\mathscr{N}}\subset[0,T]
depending on
f
such that for all
t\notin{\mathscr{N}}
and
R>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|{\mathcal{S}}^{n_{k}}_{f}(t)-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}=0.

(5.22)

We want to show that for
s,t\notin{\mathscr{N}}
with
s<t
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\Big((u_{n}(t,{w}_{t})-u_{n}(s,{w}_{s}))G_{s}\Big)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(({\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(s,{w}_{s}))G_{s}\Big).

(5.23)

For fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, since
x\mapsto u_{m}(t,x)
is bounded continuous, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\Big((u_{m}(t,{w}_{t})G_{s}\Big)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(u_{m}(t,{w}_{t})G_{s}\Big).

On the other hand, noting that for
R>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\big|(u_{m}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})){\mathbbm{1}}_{B_{R}}({w}_{t})\big|

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}\big|u_{m}(t,x)-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,x)\big|\rho_{n}(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u_{m}(t,\cdot)-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\|\rho_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})},

by (
5.22
) and (
5.9
) we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{n}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\big|(u_{m}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})){\mathbbm{1}}_{B_{R}}({w}_{t})\big|=0

and

\lim_{m\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\big|(u_{m}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})){\mathbbm{1}}_{B_{R}}({w}_{t})\big|=0.

Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\big|(u_{m}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})){\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{c}_{R}}({w}_{t})\big|

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\big|u_{m}(t,x)-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,x)\big|\rho_{n}(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\leqslant\|u_{m}(t)\|_{\infty}\mu^{n}_{t}(B^{c}_{R}),

where
\mu^{n}_{t}={\mathbb{P}}^{n}\circ w^{-1}_{t}
.
By the tightness of
(\mu^{n}_{t})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, we have

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{m,n}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\big|(u_{m}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})){\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{c}_{R}}({w}_{t})\big|\leqslant C\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{n}\mu^{n}_{t}(B^{c}_{R})=0.

Thus we get (
5.23
). Hence, for all
s,t\notin{\mathscr{N}}
with
s<t
and any bounded continuous
{\mathscr{B}}_{s}
-measurable

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(({\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t,{w}_{t})-{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(s,{w}_{s}))G_{s}\Big)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(G_{s}\int_{s}^{t}f(r,{w}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right),

which gives

{\mathbb{E}}(M^{f}_{t}|{\mathscr{B}}_{s})=M^{f}_{s}.

In particular, by noticing that
u_{n}(T)={\mathcal{S}}_{f}(T)=0
, (
5.23
) holds for

and
s\notin{\mathscr{N}}
. Now, we show (
5.23
) holds for

and

as well.
We only need to show that for any bounded continuous function

,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}u_{n}(0,{w}_{0})g({w}_{0})={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(0,{w}_{0})g({w}_{0}).

We note that
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\circ({w}_{0})^{-1}={\mathbb{P}}\circ({w}_{0})^{-1}=\mu_{0}
and by (
5.18

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle u_{n}(0),\rho_{0}g\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(0),\rho_{0}g\rangle.

Therefore, we have
{\mathbb{E}}(M^{f}_{t}|{\mathscr{F}}_{0})=0
.
The existence of a generalized martingale solution is proven.

(Uniqueness)
Let
{\mathbb{P}}_{1},{\mathbb{P}}_{2}
be two generalized martingale solutions with initial distribution
\mu_{0}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\rho_{0}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x
in the sense of Definition
5.8
.
By the definition, for any
f\in C_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, there is a Lebesgue full measure set
{\mathscr{A}}_{f}\subset[0,T]
containing
0
and
T
such that for any
s,t\in{\mathscr{A}}_{f}
and bounded
{\mathscr{B}}_{s}
-measurable
G_{s}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}((M^{f}_{t}-M^{f}_{s})G_{s})=0,\ \ i=1,2.

(5.24)

Since
C_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is separable, we can choose
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}_{f}
independent of
f
.

Firstly, taking
(s,t)=(0,T)
,
G\equiv 1
in (
5.24
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}f(t,{w}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)=-\langle{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(0),\rho_{0}\rangle={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}f(t,{w}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right).

which implies that
{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{2}
have the same one dimensional time marginal distribution. In fact, taking
f(t,x)=h(t)g(x)
in the above equality,
where

and

for Lebesgue almost all

and any

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}g({w}_{t})={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}g({w}_{t}).

Since

is continuous, we further have for any

and

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}g({w}_{t})={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}g({w}_{t})\Rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\circ{w}_{t}^{-1}={\mathbb{P}}_{2}\circ{w}_{t}^{-1}.

Below we show that
{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
and

have the same finite dimensional time marginal distribution by induction.
We assume that for some

,
{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
and

have the same

-dimensional time marginal distribution, that is, for any

{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\circ({w}_{t_{1}},\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})^{-1}={\mathbb{P}}_{2}\circ({w}_{t_{1}},\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})^{-1}.

Let

and

and

By (

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}\left(\int_{t_{n}}^{T}f(s,{w}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\,g({w}_{t_{1}}\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})\right)=-{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}\Big({\mathcal{S}}_{f}(t_{n},{w}_{t_{n}})g({w}_{t_{1}}\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})\Big),\quad i=1,2,

(5.25)

which implies by the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}\left(\int_{t_{n}}^{T}f(s,{w}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\,g({w}_{t_{1}}\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})\right)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}\left(\int_{t_{n}}^{T}f(s,{w}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\,g({w}_{t_{1}}\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})\right).

Therefore, for any

and Lebesgue almost all

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}\left(g_{1}({w}_{t_{n+1}})g({w}_{t_{1}}\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})\right)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}\left(g_{1}({w}_{t_{n+1}})g({w}_{t_{1}}\cdots,{w}_{t_{n}})\right).

By the continuity of

, we get for all

with

,

{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\circ({w}_{t_{1}},...,{w}_{t_{n}},{w}_{t_{n+1}})^{-1}={\mathbb{P}}_{2}\circ({w}_{t_{1}},...,{w}_{t_{n}},{w}_{t_{n+1}})^{-1}.

Namely,
{\mathbb{P}}_{1}={\mathbb{P}}_{2}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Now we can give

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
n_{k}
be any subsequence and
n_{k}^{\prime}
be the subsubsequence of
n_{k}
used in the definition of
{\mathcal{S}}_{f}
.
By Theorems
5.10
and
5.7
, any accumulation point of
{\mathbb{P}}_{n^{\prime}_{k}}
is a generalized martingale solution, and meanwhile, a weak solution.
By the uniqueness of generalized martingale solutions, for any initial value
\mu
, without further selecting a subsequence,
{\mathbb{P}}_{n^{\prime}_{k}}
weakly converges to the weak solution of SDE (
1.1
).
If
b=b_{1}+b_{2}
, where
b_{1}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathcal{B}}
and
b_{2}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}L^{2}
, then PDE (
5.16
) has a unique weak solution. Thus, no necessary to select a subsequence,
{\mathcal{S}}_{f}
is well-defined, and
as above, the whole sequence
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
weakly converges to the weak solution of SDE (
1.1
).

Finally, we show the Markov property by showing it for the generalized martingale solution. Since
C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is separable, by Definition
5.8
,
we can find a Lebesgue zero set
{\mathscr{N}}\subset(0,T)
such that for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}\Big[M^{f}_{T}-M^{f}_{s}\big|{\mathscr{B}}_{s}\Big]=0={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}\Big[M^{f}_{T}-M^{f}_{s}\big|{\mathscr{B}}_{s}\big|w_{s}\Big]={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}\Big[M^{f}_{T}-M^{f}_{s}\big|w_{s}\Big],

which in turn implies by

that

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}[{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(s,w_{s})|{\mathscr{B}}_{s}]+{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}\left[\int_{s}^{T}f(r,w_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathscr{B}}_{s}\right]={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}[{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(s,w_{s})|w_{s}]+{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}\left[\int_{s}^{T}f(r,w_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|w_{s}\right].

Hence, for any

and

In particular, for fixed

and

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}\left[\int_{(t-1/n)\vee s}^{t}f(w_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathscr{B}}_{s}\right]={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}\left[\int_{(t-1/n)\vee s}^{t}f(w_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|w_{s}\right],\ \ n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Multiplying both sides by

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}[f(w_{t})|{\mathscr{B}}_{s}]={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}}[f(w_{t})|\omega_{s}].

The proof is complete.
∎

6.
Applications to diffusion in random environment

In this section, we introduce certain Gaussian noise
b
so that our results can be applied to them. In particular, we construct the diffusion process in random environment using this noise.

6.1.
Vector-valued Gaussian fields

In this section we introduce the notion of vector-valued Gaussian field.
Fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi):=\{\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\}_{i,j=1}^{m}
be a matrix-valued signed Radon measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We assume that

(A)

For each Borel measurable set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
(\mu_{ij}(A))_{i,j=1}^{m}
is a symmetric semi-positive definite Hermit matrix, that is, for any complex numbers
(a_{i})^{m}_{i=1}
,

\displaystyle\mu_{ij}(A)=\mu_{ji}(A),\ \ \sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i}\bar{a}_{j}\mu_{ij}(A)\geqslant 0.

(6.1)

Moreover,
\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)=\mu(-{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)
and for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{i,j=1,\cdots,m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+|\xi|)^{\ell}|\mu_{ij}|_{\rm var}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)<\infty,

(6.2)

where
|\cdot|_{\rm var}
stands for the total variation measure of a signed measure.

For two
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
-valued Schwartz functions
f,g\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, we define

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mu;k}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2k})\widehat{f_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{g_{j}}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi).

Since
\xi\to\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)
is symmetric, one sees that
\langle f,g\rangle_{\mu;k}=\langle g,f\rangle_{\mu;k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is an inner product on
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
,
and by (
6.1
), for any
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mu;k}:=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2k})\widehat{f_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f_{j}}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right)^{1/2}\geqslant 0.

Hence,
\|\cdot\|_{\mu;k}
is a seminorm on linear
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
. Clearly,

\|\cdot\|_{\mu}:=\|\cdot\|_{\mu;0}\leqslant\|\cdot\|_{\mu;k}\leqslant\|\cdot\|_{\mu;k+1}.

It must be noticed that
\|f\|_{\mu}=0
does not imply
f=0
.
Let
E_{0}
be the null space of
\|\cdot\|_{\mu}
, i.e.,

E_{0}:=\{f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m}):\|f\|_{\mu}=0\}.

We define
{\mathbb{H}}_{k}
being the completion of the quotient space
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})/E_{0}
with respect to the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mu;k}
.
Thus we obtain a Hilbert space and

{\mathbb{H}}_{k+1}\subset{\mathbb{H}}_{k}\subset{\mathbb{H}}_{0}.

Remark 6.1
.

Assume
\ell\geqslant 0
in (
6.2
), then the Dirac measure
\delta^{\otimes m}_{0}
belongs to
{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
. Indeed, for
f_{n}(x)=n^{d}f(nx)
, where
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
satisfies

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{i}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=1
for each
i=1,\cdots,m
, we have

\|f_{n}-f_{n^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{\mu}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\widehat{f_{i}}(\xi/n)-\widehat{f_{i}}(\xi/n^{\prime}))\overline{(\widehat{f_{j}}(\xi/n)-\widehat{f_{j}}(\xi/n^{\prime}))}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi).

Since
\ell\geqslant 0
in (
6.2
) and
\widehat{f_{i}}(0)=1
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f_{n^{\prime}}\|_{\mu}=0.

In general, the element in
{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
may be not a Schwartz distribution.

For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
, define

\tau_{x}f(\cdot):=f(\cdot-x)\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\ \ \nabla^{k}f(\cdot):=\partial^{k}_{x}f(\cdot)\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\otimes({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{\otimes k}).

Noting that

\widehat{\tau_{x}f}(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}\hat{f}(-\xi),\ \ \widehat{\nabla^{k}f}(\xi)={\rm i}^{k}\xi^{\otimes k}\hat{f}(\xi),

we clearly have

\|\tau_{x}f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}},\ \ \|\nabla^{k}f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{k}}.

In particular, we can extend
\tau_{x}:{\mathbb{H}}_{0}\to{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
and
\nabla^{k}:{\mathbb{H}}_{k}\to{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
being bounded linear operators.

We have the following simple lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
f\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
, the function
x\mapsto\tau_{x}f\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
is continuous, and
for any
f\in{\mathbb{H}}_{k}
,
x\mapsto\tau_{x}f\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
is
k
-order Fréchet differentiable and

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}^{k}\tau_{x}f=\tau_{x}\nabla^{k}f\ \ \text{in ${\mathbb{H}}_{0}$}.

(6.3)

Proof.

By the boundedness
\|\tau_{x}f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}}
, we may assume
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
. Thus, by definition,

\|\tau_{x}f-\tau_{y}f\|^{2}_{\mu}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}\xi\cdot x}-\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}\xi\cdot y}|^{2}\widehat{f}_{i}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}_{j}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi),

which implies by the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{x\to y}\|\tau_{x}f-\tau_{y}f\|^{2}_{\mu}=0.

For (
6.3
), it suffices to show it for
k=1
. For
h,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
h\not=0
, by definition we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\tau_{x+h}f-\tau_{x}f}{h}-\tau_{x}\nabla f\right\|^{2}_{\mu}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}\xi\cdot h}-1}{h}+{\rm i}\xi\right|^{2}\widehat{f}_{i}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}_{j}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi).

Noting that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sup_{h}\left|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}\xi\cdot h}-1}{h}+{\rm i}\xi\right|^{2}|\widehat{f}_{i}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}_{j}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\leqslant\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}\widehat{f}_{i}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}_{j}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)<\infty,

by the dominant convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\|\frac{\tau_{x+h}f-\tau_{x}f}{h}-\tau_{x}\nabla f\right\|_{\mu}=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we introduce the following notion of vector-valued Gaussian random field (see
[
39
]
).

Definition 6.3
.

Let
U
be the real valued Gaussian random field on
{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
, that is,
U
is a continuous linear operator from
{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
to
L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbf{P}})
,
and for each
f\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
,

U(f)
is a real-valued Gaussian random variable with mean zero and variance
\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}}
. In particular,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big(U(f)U(g)\big)=\langle f,g\rangle_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}}.

(6.4)

We shall call
U
an
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
-valued Gaussian noise over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with matrix-valued
spectral measure
\mu
.

We call
U
an
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
-valued Gaussian noise. The reason is that
for given real valued Schwartz function
h\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}})
and
i=1,\cdots,m
, if we define

\displaystyle f_{i}:=(0,\cdots,0,h,0,\cdots,0)\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\ \ U_{i}(h):=U(f_{i}),

(6.5)

then it is easy to see that

U=(U_{1},\cdots,U_{m}).

Moreover, for any
f,g\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}})
, we have

{\mathbf{E}}(U_{i}(f)U_{j}(g))=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\overline{\hat{g}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi),

which gives the covariance between different components.
In particular, for
f=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
, we shall write

U(f)=\langle U,f\rangle=U_{1}(f_{1})+\cdots+U_{m}(f_{m}).

On the other hand, in the above definition, the Gaussian field
U
is defined as a bounded linear operator from
{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
to
L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbf{P}})
. A natural question arises: can we view
U
as an
m
-dimensional random distribution of the spatial variable
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
? We shall provide an affirmative answer and show its Besov regularity in the variable
x
below.

To achieve this, we first introduce the convolution of
U
with
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
. For a given
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
, we define

(\varphi*U)(x):=U_{\varphi}(x):=U(\tau_{x}\varphi)\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbf{P}}).

Theorem 6.4
.

For any
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
, the function
x\mapsto U_{\varphi}(x)
is continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbf{P}})
,
and for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{k}
,
x\mapsto U_{\varphi}(x)
is
k
-order Fréchet differentiable, and for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}_{x}U_{\varphi}(x)=\langle U,\tau_{x}\nabla^{k}\varphi\rangle,\ \ {\mathbf{P}}-a.s.

(6.6)

Moreover, for any
p\geqslant 2
, there is a constant
C=C(d,p)>0
such that for all
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|\nabla^{k}U_{\varphi}(x)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|\varphi\|_{\mu;k}.

(6.7)

Proof.

Note that

{\mathbf{E}}|U_{\varphi}(x)-U_{\varphi}(y)|^{2}={\mathbf{E}}|U(\tau_{x}\varphi-\tau_{y}\varphi)|^{2}=\|\tau_{x}\varphi-\tau_{y}\varphi\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}}^{2}.

The continuity of
x\mapsto U_{\varphi}(x)
now follows by Lemma
6.2
.
Similarly, by (
6.3
), we have (
6.6
).
Moreover, by the the hypercontractivity of Gaussian random variables, we have for any
p\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}U_{\varphi}(x)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|U_{\nabla^{k}\varphi}(x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widehat{\nabla^{k}\varphi_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\nabla^{k}\varphi_{j}}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\xi|^{2k}\widehat{\varphi_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right)^{1/2}\leqslant\|\varphi\|_{\mu;k}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now for
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}})
, we define

U_{\varphi}(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{\varphi}(x)f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
,
U_{\varphi}
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued Gaussian noise with spectral measure

\mu_{\varphi}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\hat{\varphi}_{i}(\xi)\hat{\varphi}_{j}(-\xi)\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi).

Proof.

For any
f,g\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}})
, by definition and Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big(U_{\varphi}(f)U_{\varphi}(g)\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathbf{E}}\big(U_{\varphi}(x)U_{\varphi}(y)\big)f(x)g(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widehat{\tau_{x}\varphi_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\tau_{y}\varphi_{j}}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right]f(x)g(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}(\xi\cdot x-\xi\cdot y)}\widehat{\varphi_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)}\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right]f(x)g(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hat{f}(-\xi)\hat{g}(\xi)\widehat{\varphi_{i}}(\xi)\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(-\xi)\mu_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)=\langle f,g\rangle_{\mu_{\varphi}}.

The result now follows.
∎

Below, for a Banach space
{\mathbb{B}}
, let
{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{B}}):={\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{B}})
be the
{\mathbb{B}}
-valued Besov space,
and for any
\rho_{\kappa}(x)=(1+|x|)^{-\kappa}
, where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let
{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};{\mathbb{B}})
be the weighted Besov space, which is defined by (see
[
22
]
)

\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};{\mathbb{B}})}:=\|f\rho_{k}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{B}})}.

We have the following regularity result.

Theorem 6.6
.

Under
(A)
, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\kappa>d/p
and
\beta<\ell/2
, it holds that

\displaystyle U\in{\mathbf{B}}^{\ell/2}_{\infty}(L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m}))\subset{\mathbf{B}}^{\ell/2}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m}))\subset L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})).

(6.8)

Proof.

First of all, recalling the definition of
(\phi_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
in (
2.1
),
one sees that for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle|\phi_{j}(\xi)|\lesssim_{C}1\wedge\big(2^{\gamma j}(1+|\xi|)^{-\gamma}\big),\ \ j\geqslant-1,\ \ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(6.9)

Indeed, for
j=-1
, since supp
\phi_{-1}\subset{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
, it is obvious. For
j\geqslant 0
, noting that

K_{j}:=\text{supp}\phi_{j}\subset\{\xi:2^{j-1}\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant{2^{j+1}}\},

we have for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle|\phi_{j}(\xi)|\leqslant{\mathbf{1}}_{K_{j}}(\xi)\lesssim 2^{j\gamma}/(1+|\xi|)^{\gamma}.

For each
i=1,\cdots,m
, under (
6.2
), by definition (
6.5
) and the hypercontractivity of Gaussian random variables, we have for any
p\in[1,\infty)
and
j=0,1,\cdots,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}U_{i}(x)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}U_{i}(x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|U_{i}(\tau_{x}\check{\phi}_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\phi_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\mu_{ii}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\ell j/2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+|\xi|)^{\ell}\mu_{ii}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{-\ell j/2},

(6.10)

and for
\kappa>d/p
,

\|\rho_{\kappa}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}U\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};L^{p}(\Omega))}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{-p\kappa}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}U(x)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\lesssim 2^{-p\ell j/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{-p\kappa}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

Hence, by
[
22
, Theorem 2.7]
,

U\in{\mathbf{B}}^{\ell/2}_{\infty}(L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m}))\subset{\mathbf{B}}^{\ell/2}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})).

Finally, for
\beta<\ell/2
, by Fubini’s theorem, we have

\Big\|\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\beta j}\|\rho_{\kappa}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}U\|_{p}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\beta j}\|\rho_{\kappa}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}U\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\Omega)}\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{(\beta-\ell/2)j}\|U\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\ell/2}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m}))}.

The proof is completed .
∎

6.2.
Examples: Divergence-free Gaussian field

In this section we present concrete examples to illustrate our results.
The following example is standard.

Example 6.7
.

Let
m=1
. For
\gamma\in[0,d)
, let

\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi):=|\xi|^{-\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi.

Obviously, (
6.2
) holds.
In this case, for
\gamma>0
, it is well known that
for some
c_{d,\gamma}>0
(see
[
37
, p117, Lemma 2]
),

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(x)=c_{d,\gamma}|x|^{\gamma-d}.

In particular, for any
f,g\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big(U(f)U(g)\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(-\xi)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)=c_{d,\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\gamma}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

For
\gamma=0
, we have

\hat{\mu}(x)=\delta_{0}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)

and

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big(U(f)U(g)\big)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)g(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

Namely,
X
is a space white noise on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
As in (
6.10
) and by the change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}U(x)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\phi_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)\right)^{1/2}=2^{j(d-\gamma)/2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\phi_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi}{|\xi|^{\gamma}}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\kappa>d/p
and
\beta<(\gamma-d)/2
,

\displaystyle U\in{\mathbf{B}}^{(\gamma-d)/2}_{\infty}(L^{p}(\Omega))\subset{\mathbf{B}}^{(\gamma-d)/2}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};L^{p}(\Omega))\subset L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa})).

(6.11)

In particular, bigger
\gamma
means that Gaussian field
U
has better regularity.

Example 6.8

(Divergence free vector-valued Gaussian field)
.

For given
\gamma\in[0,d)
, define

\displaystyle\mu^{(\gamma)}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi):=|\xi|^{-\gamma}\Big({\mathbb{I}}_{d\times d}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi.

(6.12)

It is easy to see that (
6.2
) holds for
\ell<\gamma-d
, and for
a:=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

a^{T}\Big({\mathbb{I}}_{d\times d}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\bar{a}=|a|^{2}-\sum_{i,j}(a_{i}\xi_{i}\bar{a}_{j}\xi_{j})/|\xi|^{2}=|a|^{2}-\Big|\sum_{i}(a_{i}\xi_{i})\Big|^{2}/|\xi|^{2}\geqslant 0.

Thus
(A)
holds.
Let
b^{(\gamma)}
be the
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued Gaussian random field with matrix-valued spectral measure
\mu^{(\gamma)}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)
.
Then by (
6.8
), for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\kappa>d/p
and
\beta<\frac{\gamma-d}{2}
,

b^{(\gamma)}\in L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})).

In particular, there is a
{\mathbf{P}}
-null set
N\subset\Omega
so that for all
\omega\not\in N
,

b^{(\gamma)}(\omega,\cdot)\in{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p}(\rho_{\kappa};{\mathbb{R}}^{m}),

and

\mathord{{\rm div}}b^{(\gamma)}(\omega,\cdot)=0.

In fact, for any
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}|\langle\mathord{{\rm div}}b^{(\gamma)},f\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{E}}|\langle b^{(\gamma)},\nabla f\rangle|^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widehat{\nabla f}(\xi)\Big({\mathbb{I}}_{d\times d}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\big[\overline{\widehat{\nabla f}(\xi)}\big]^{*}|\xi|^{-\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\xi\Big({\mathbb{I}}_{d\times d}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\xi^{*}|\xi|^{-\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi=0,

(6.13)

where
*
stands for the transpose of a row vector.
In particular, one can apply Theorem
1.2
to solve the following SDE with
\gamma\in(d-2,d)
:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b^{(\gamma)}(\omega,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

where
X_{t}
can be thought of the diffusion in random environment (see
[
13
]
).

Remark 6.9
.

Consider
d=3
. Let
{\bf U}^{(\gamma)}:=(U_{1},U_{2},U_{3})
be the 3-dimensional Gaussian field with spectral measure matrix
\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)=|\xi|^{-\gamma}{\mathbb{I}}_{3\times 3}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi
,
where
\gamma\in(1,3)
.
Let
b^{(\gamma)}
be the
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
-valued Gaussian field in the above example.
It is easy to see that (see
[
13
]
)

\displaystyle b^{(\gamma)}=\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf U}^{(\gamma)},\quad\nabla\times:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\partial_{3}&\partial_{2}\\
\partial_{3}&0&-\partial_{1}\\
-\partial_{2}&\partial_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Remark 6.10
.

Consider
d=2
. Let
U
be the two-dimensional spatial white noise with spectral measure
\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi)={\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi
(see Example
6.7
).
Let
{\mathscr{S}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be the class of Schwartz function
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with
0\notin{\rm supp}(\hat{f})
.
Let
b^{(0)}
be the
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
-valued Gaussian field in Example
6.8
. Then
it is easy to see that for any
f,g\in{\mathscr{S}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big((\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}U)(f)(\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}})U(g)\big)={\mathbf{E}}(b^{(0)}(f)b^{(0)}(g)),

where
\nabla^{\perp}:=(\partial_{x_{2}},-\partial_{x_{1}})
.
In other words,

\displaystyle b^{(0)}=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}U\mbox{ on ${\mathscr{S}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})$}.

However,
{\mathscr{S}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is not a deterministic class since for
f\in{\mathscr{S}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=0
.
So we can not claim
b^{(0)}=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}U
as distributions.

Example 6.11
.

6.3.
Stochastic heat equations

Let
\gamma\in(-\infty,d)
and
\eta^{(\gamma)}
be an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued time-white Gaussian noise with matrix-valued spectral measure
\mu^{(\gamma)}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t
,
where
\mu^{(\gamma)}
is given by (
6.12
).
More precisely,
\eta^{(\gamma)}
is a Gaussian field over
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with covariance given by that
for any
f,g\in L^{2}([0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\big(\eta^{(\gamma)}(f)\eta^{(\gamma)}(g)\big)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\hat{f}_{i}(t,\xi)\hat{g}_{j}(t,-\xi)\mu^{(\gamma)}_{ij}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.

Now, we consider the following
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued stochastic heat equation (abbreviated as SHE) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{(\gamma)}=\Delta u^{(\gamma)}+\eta^{(\gamma)},\quad u^{(\gamma)}_{0}=0.

(6.15)

Let

p_{t}(x)=(4\pi t)^{-1}\exp\{-|x|^{2}/(4t)\}.

By Duhamael’s formula, the solution is determined by the following formula:

\displaystyle u^{(\gamma)}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\eta^{(\gamma)}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s):=\eta^{(\gamma)}({\mathbf{1}}_{(0,t)}\tau_{x}p_{t-\cdot}).

Noting that for any
j\geqslant 0
and
i=1,\cdots,m
,

{\mathcal{R}}_{j}u^{(\gamma)}_{i}(t,x)=\eta^{(\gamma)}_{i}({\mathbf{1}}_{(0,t)}\tau_{x}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{t-\cdot}),

as in Theorem
6.6
, by (
2.1
) and the change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}u^{(\gamma)}_{i}(t,x)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}u^{(\gamma)}_{i}(t,x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\tau_{x}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}p_{t-s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu^{(\gamma)}_{ii}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\phi_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{-\gamma}(1-|\xi_{i}|^{2}/|\xi|^{2}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{j(d-\gamma)}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\phi_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2^{2j+1}s|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{-\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(d-\gamma-2)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\phi_{0}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{-2-\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi,

and also,

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{0}u^{(\gamma)}_{-1}(t,x)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\phi_{-1}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{-\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi<\infty.

Hence, for
\gamma<d-2
,

u^{(\gamma)}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{B}}^{1+\frac{\gamma-d}{2}}_{\infty}(L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))).

Moreover, as (
6.13
), we also have

\mathord{{\rm div}}u^{(\gamma)}=0.

In particular, we can use Theorem
1.2
to
time-dependent
vector field
b^{(\gamma)}(t,x)=u^{(\gamma)}(t,x)
with
\gamma\in(d-4,d-2)
.

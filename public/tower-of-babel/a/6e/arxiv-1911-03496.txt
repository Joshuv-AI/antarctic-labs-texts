Isomorphism between the 𝑹 \boldsymbol{R} -Matrix and Drinfeld Presentations of Quantum Affine Algebra: Types 𝑩 \boldsymbol{B} and 𝑫 \boldsymbol{D}
Naihuan JING †, Ming LIU ‡§ and Alexander MOLEV §

Abstract
Following the approach of Ding and Frenkel [ Comm. Math. Phys. 156 (1993), 277–300] for type A A , we showed in our previous work [ J. Math. Phys. 61 (2020), 031701, 41 pages] that the Gauss decomposition of the generator matrix in the R R -matrix presentation of the quantum affine algebra yields the Drinfeld generators in all classical types. Complete details for type C C were given therein, while the present paper deals with types B B and D D . The arguments for all classical types are quite similar so we mostly concentrate on necessary additional details specific to the underlying orthogonal Lie algebras.

Introduction

The quantum affine algebras
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
associated with simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
admit at least three different presentations. The original definition of Drinfeld
[
9
]
and Jimbo
[
17
]
was followed by the
new realization
of Drinfeld
[
10
]
which is also known as the
Drinfeld presentation
, while the
R
-
matrix presentation
was introduced by Reshetikhin and Semenov-Tian-Shansky
[
23
]
and further developed by Frenkel and Reshetikhin
[
12
]
. A detailed construction of an isomorphism between the first two presentations was given by Beck
[
1
]
.

An isomorphism between the Drinfeld and
R
-matrix presentations of the algebras
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})

in type
A
was constructed by Ding and Frenkel
[
8
]
. In our previous work
[
20
]
we were able to extend this construction to the remaining classical types and gave detailed arguments in type
C
.
The present article is concerned with types
B
and
D
, where we use the same approach as in
[
20
]
and mostly concentrate on necessary changes specific to the orthogonal Lie algebras
\mathfrak{o}_{N}
and their root systems. In particular, this applies to low rank relations with the underlying Lie algebras
\mathfrak{o}_{3}
and
\mathfrak{o}_{4}
, and to formulas for the universal
R
-matrices.

As with the corresponding isomorphisms between the
R
-matrix and Drinfeld presentations
of the Yangians (see respective details in
[
4
,
16
]
and
[
19
]
),
their counterparts in the quantum affine algebra case allow one to
connect two sides of the representation theory in an explicit way:
the parameterization of finite-dimensional irreducible representations via their
Drinfeld polynomials can be translated from one presentation to another;
see
[
5
, Chapter 12]
,
[
15
]
and
[
24
]
.
As another consequence of the
isomorphism theorems, one can derive the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt
theorem for the
R
-matrix presentation of the
quantum affine algebra from the corresponding result
of Beck
[
2
]
for
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
. We will give a more detailed account
of these applications in our forthcoming project.

To work with the quantum affine algebras in types
B
and
D
, we apply the
Gauss decomposition
of the generator matrices in the
R
-matrix presentation in the same way as in types
A
and
C
; see
[
8
]
and
[
20
]
. We show that the generators arising from the Gauss decomposition satisfy the required relations of the Drinfeld presentation. To demonstrate that the resulting homomorphism is injective we follow the argument of Frenkel and Mukhin
[
11
]
and rely on the formula for the universal

R
-matrix due to Khoroshkin and Tolstoy
[
21
]
and Damiani
[
6
]
.

Similar to the type
C
case, we
will introduce the
extended quantum affine algebra
in types
B
and
D

defined by an
R
-matrix presentation. We prove
an embedding theorem which will allow us to regard
the extended algebra of rank
n-1
as a subalgebra of the
corresponding algebra of rank
n
. We also
produce a Drinfeld-type presentation for the extended quantum affine algebra
and give explicit formulas for generators
of its center. It appears to be very likely that these formulas can be included
in a general scheme as developed by Wendlandt
[
25
]
in the Yangian context.

To state our isomorphism theorem, let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{N}
be the orthogonal Lie algebra, where odd and even values
N=2n+1
and
N=2n
respectively correspond to the simple Lie algebras of types
B_{n}
and
D_{n}
. Choose their simple roots in the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,n-1,

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{n}&\text{if}\quad\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{2n+1},\\
\epsilon_{n-1}+\epsilon_{n}&\text{if}\quad\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{2n},\end{cases}

where

\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n}
is an orthonormal basis of a Euclidian space with the inner product

(\cdot\,,\cdot)
. The Cartan matrix
[A_{ij}]
is defined by

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}.

(1.1)

For a variable
q
we set
q_{i}=q^{r_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, where
r_{i}=(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})/2
.
We will use the standard notation

\displaystyle[k]_{q}=\frac{q^{k}-q^{-k}}{q-q^{-1}}

(1.2)

for a nonnegative integer
k
, and

\displaystyle[k]_{q}!=\prod_{s=1}^{k}[s]_{q},\qquad{k\brack r}_{q}=\frac{[k]_{q}!}{[r]_{q}!\,[k-r]_{q}!}.

We will take
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt\big(q^{1/2}\big)
as the base field to define most of our quantum algebras.
In type
B_{n}
we will need its extension obtained by adjoining the square root of
[2]_{q_{n}}=q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2}
.

The
quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
in its Drinfeld presentation

is the associative algebra
with generators

x_{i,m}^{\pm}
,
a_{i,l}
,
k_{i}^{\pm}
and
q^{\pm c/2}
for

and

m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt
with
l\neq 0
, subject to the following defining relations:
the elements
q^{\pm c/2}
are central,

\displaystyle k_{i}k_{i}^{-1}=k_{i}^{-1}k_{i}=1,\qquad q^{c/2}q^{-c/2}=q^{-c/2}q^{c/2}=1,

\displaystyle k_{i}k_{j}=k_{j}k_{i},\qquad k_{i}\,a_{j,k}=a_{j,k}\,k_{i},\qquad k_{i}\,x_{j,m}^{\pm}\,k_{i}^{-1}=q_{i}^{\pm A_{ij}}x_{j,m}^{\pm},

\displaystyle[a_{i,m},a_{j,l}]=\delta_{m,-l}\,\frac{[mA_{ij}]_{q_{i}}}{m}\,\frac{q^{mc}-q^{-mc}}{q_{j}-q_{j}^{-1}},

\displaystyle[a_{i,m},x_{j,l}^{\pm}]=\pm\frac{[mA_{ij}]_{q_{i}}}{m}\,q^{\mp|m|c/2}\,x^{\pm}_{j,m+l},

\displaystyle x^{\pm}_{i,m+1}x^{\pm}_{j,l}-q_{i}^{\pm A_{ij}}x^{\pm}_{j,l}x^{\pm}_{i,m+1}=q_{i}^{\pm A_{ij}}x^{\pm}_{i,m}x^{\pm}_{j,l+1}-x^{\pm}_{j,l+1}x^{\pm}_{i,m},

\displaystyle[x_{i,m}^{+},x_{j,l}^{-}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{q^{(m-l)\,c/2}\,\psi_{i,m+l}-q^{-(m-l)\,c/2}\,\varphi_{i,m+l}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\sum_{l=0}^{r}(-1)^{l}{{r}\brack{l}}_{q_{i}}x^{\pm}_{i,s_{\pi(1)}}\cdots x^{\pm}_{i,s_{\pi(l)}}x^{\pm}_{j,m}x^{\pm}_{i,s_{\pi(l+1)}}\cdots x^{\pm}_{i,s_{\pi(r)}}=0,\qquad i\neq j,

where in the last relation we set
r=1-A_{ij}
. The elements

\psi_{i,m}
and
\varphi_{i,-m}
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
are defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(u):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\psi_{i,m}u^{-m}=k_{i}\,{\rm exp}\left(\big(q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}\big)\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}a_{i,s}u^{-s}\right),

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(u):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{i,-m}u^{m}=k_{i}^{-1}{\rm exp}\left({-}\big(q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}\big)\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}a_{i,-s}u^{s}\right),

whereas
\psi_{i,m}=\varphi_{i,-m}=0
for
m<0
.

To introduce the
R
-matrix presentation of the quantum affine algebra we will use the
endomorphism algebra
{\rm{End}\,}\big(\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\big)\cong{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}
.
For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{2n+1}
consider the following elements of the endomorphism algebra
(extended over
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt\big(q^{1/2}\big)
):

\displaystyle P=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ji},\qquad Q=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}q^{\bar{\imath}-\bar{\jmath}}\,e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes e_{ij}

and

\displaystyle R=q\,\sum_{i=1,i\neq i^{\prime}}^{N}e_{ii}\otimes e_{ii}+e_{n+1,n+1}\otimes e_{n+1,n+1}+\sum_{i\neq j,j^{\prime}}e_{ii}\otimes e_{jj}+q^{-1}\sum_{i\neq i^{\prime}}e_{ii}\otimes e_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{R =}{}+\big(q-q^{-1}\big)\sum_{i<j}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ji}-\big(q-q^{-1}\big)\sum_{i>j}q^{\bar{\imath}-\bar{\jmath}}\,e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes e_{ij},

where
e_{ij}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}
are the matrix units, and we used the notation
i^{\prime}=N+1-i
and

\displaystyle\big(\,\overline{1},\overline{2},\dots,\overline{N}\,\big)=\left(n-\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2},0,-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{3}{2},\dots,-n+\frac{1}{2}\right).

In the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{2n}
we define the elements
P
,
Q
and
R
by the same formulas
by taking
N=2n
, except that the second term
e_{n+1,n+1}\otimes e_{n+1,n+1}
in
the expression for
R
should be omitted, while the barred symbols are now given by

\displaystyle\big(\,\overline{1},\overline{2},\dots,\overline{N}\,\big)=(n-1,\dots,1,0,0,-1,\dots,-n+1).

In both cases, following
[
12
]
consider the formal power series

\displaystyle f(u)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}\hskip 1.0ptu^{k},

whose coefficients
f_{k}
are rational functions in
q
uniquely determined by the relation

\displaystyle f(u)\hskip 1.0ptf(u\xi)=\frac{1}{\big(1-u\hskip 1.0ptq^{-2}\big)\big(1-u\hskip 1.0ptq^{2}\big)(1-u\hskip 1.0pt\xi)\big(1-u\hskip 1.0pt\xi^{-1}\big)},

(1.3)

where
\xi=q^{2-N}
. Equivalently,
f(u)
is given by the infinite product formula

\displaystyle f(u)=\prod_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(1-u\,\xi^{2r}\big)\big(1-u\,q^{-2}\,\xi^{2r+1}\big)\big(1-u\,q^{2}\,\xi^{2r+1}\big)\big(1-u\,\xi^{2r+2}\big)}{\big(1-u\,\xi^{2r-1}\big)\big(1-u\,\xi^{2r+1}\big)\big(1-u\,q^{2}\xi^{2r}\big)\big(1-u\,q^{-2}\xi^{2r}\big)}.

(1.4)

In accordance with
[
18
]
, the
R

R(u)
given by

\displaystyle R(u)=f(u)\big(q^{-1}(u-1)(u-\xi)R-\big(q^{-2}-1\big)(u-\xi)P+\big(q^{-2}-1\big)(u-1)\,\xi\,Q\big)

(1.5)

is a solution of the
Yang–Baxter equation

\displaystyle R_{12}(u)\,R_{13}(u\hskip 1.0ptv)\,R_{23}(v)=R_{23}(v)\,R_{13}(u\hskip 1.0ptv)\,R_{12}(u).

The associative algebra
U^{R}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})

is generated by an invertible central element
q^{c/2}
and
elements
{l}^{\pm}_{ij}[\mp m]
with
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant N
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}

subject to the following defining relations. We have

\displaystyle{l}_{ij}^{+}[0]={l}^{-}_{ji}[0]=0\quad\text{for}\quad i>j\qquad\text{and}\qquad{l}^{+}_{ii}[0]\,{l}_{ii}^{-}[0]={l}_{ii}^{-}[0]\,{l}^{+}_{ii}[0]=1,

while the remaining relations will be written in terms of the formal power series

\displaystyle{l}^{\pm}_{ij}(u)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{l}^{\pm}_{ij}[\mp m]\,u^{\pm m},

(1.6)

which we combine into the respective matrices

\displaystyle{L}^{\pm}(u)=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}{l}_{ij}^{\pm}(u)\otimes e_{ij}\in U^{R}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})\big[\big[u,u^{-1}\big]\big]\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}.

Consider the tensor product algebra
{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes U^{R}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})

and introduce the series with coefficients in this algebra by

\displaystyle{L}^{\pm}_{1}(u)=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}{l}_{ij}^{\pm}(u)\otimes e_{ij}\otimes 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad{L}^{\pm}_{2}(u)=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}{l}_{ij}^{\pm}(u)\otimes 1\otimes e_{ij}.

(1.7)

The defining relations then take the form

\displaystyle{R}(u/v)L^{\pm}_{1}(u)L^{\pm}_{2}(v)=L^{\pm}_{2}(v)L^{\pm}_{1}(u){R}(u/v),

(1.8)

\displaystyle{R}(uq^{c}/v)L^{+}_{1}(u)L^{-}_{2}(v)=L^{-}_{2}(v)L^{+}_{1}(u){R}(uq^{-c}/v),

(1.9)

together with the relations

\displaystyle{L}^{\pm}(u)D{L}^{\pm}(u\,\xi)^{{\rm t}}D^{-1}=1,

(1.10)

where
{\rm t}
denotes the matrix

e_{ij}^{{\rm t}}=e_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}}
and
D
is the diagonal matrix

\displaystyle D={\rm diag}\,\big[q^{\overline{1}},\dots,q^{\overline{N}}\hskip 1.0pt\big].

(1.11)

Now apply the
Gauss decomposition
to the matrices
{L}^{+}(u)
and
{L}^{-}(u)
.
There exist unique matrices of the form

\displaystyle F^{\pm}(u)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\dots&0\,\\
f^{\pm}_{21}(u)&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
f^{\pm}_{N\,1}(u)&f^{\pm}_{N\,2}(u)&\dots&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad E^{\pm}(u)=\begin{bmatrix}\,1&e^{\pm}_{12}(u)&\dots&e^{\pm}_{1\,N}(u)\,\\
\,0&1&\dots&e^{\pm}_{2\,N}(u)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

and
H^{\pm}(u)={\rm diag}\,\big[h^{\pm}_{1}(u),\dots,h^{\pm}_{N}(u)\big]
,
such that

\displaystyle L^{\pm}(u)=F^{\pm}(u)H^{\pm}(u)E^{\pm}(u).

(1.12)

Set

\displaystyle X^{+}_{i}(u)=e^{+}_{i,i+1}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{c/2}\big)-e_{i,i+1}^{-}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{-c/2}\big),\qquad X^{-}_{i}(u)=f^{+}_{i+1,i}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{-c/2}\big)-f^{-}_{i+1,i}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{c/2}\big),

for

, and

\displaystyle X^{+}_{n}(u)=\begin{cases}e^{+}_{n,n+1}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{c/2}\big)-e_{n,n+1}^{-}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{-c/2}\big)&\text{for type $B_{n}$},\\
e^{+}_{n-1,n+1}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{c/2}\big)-e_{n-1,n+1}^{-}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{-c/2}\big)&\text{for type $D_{n}$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle X^{-}_{n}(u)=\begin{cases}f^{+}_{n+1,n}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{-c/2}\big)-f^{-}_{n+1,n}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{c/2}\big)&\text{for type $B_{n}$},\\
f^{+}_{n+1,n-1}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{-c/2}\big)-f^{-}_{n+1,n-1}\big(u\hskip 1.0ptq^{c/2}\big)&\text{for type $D_{n}$}.\end{cases}

Combine the generators
x^{\pm}_{i,m}
of the algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
into the series

\displaystyle x^{\pm}_{i}(u)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt}x^{\pm}_{i,m}\,u^{-m}.

Main Theorem
.

The maps
q^{c/2}\mapsto q^{c/2}
,

\displaystyle x^{\pm}_{i}(u)\mapsto\big(q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}\big)^{-1}X^{\pm}_{i}\big(uq^{i}\big),

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(u)\mapsto h^{-}_{i+1}\big(uq^{i}\big)\,h^{-}_{i}\big(uq^{i}\big)^{-1},

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(u)\mapsto h^{+}_{i+1}\big(uq^{i}\big)\,h^{+}_{i}\big(uq^{i}\big)^{-1},

for
i=1,\dots,n-1
, and

\displaystyle x^{\pm}_{n}(u)\mapsto\begin{cases}\big(q_{n}-q_{n}^{-1}\big)^{-1}[2]_{q_{n}}^{-1/2}X^{\pm}_{n}\big(uq^{n}\big)&\text{for type $B_{n}$},\\
\big(q_{n}-q_{n}^{-1}\big)^{-1}X^{\pm}_{n}\big(uq^{n-1}\big)&\text{for type $D_{n}$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(u)\mapsto\begin{cases}h^{-}_{n+1}\big(uq^{n}\big)\,h^{-}_{n}\big(uq^{n}\big)^{-1}&\text{for type $B_{n}$},\\
h^{-}_{n+1}\big(uq^{n-1}\big)\,h^{-}_{n-1}\big(uq^{n-1}\big)^{-1}&\text{for type $D_{n}$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(u)\mapsto\begin{cases}h^{+}_{n+1}\big(uq^{n}\big)\,h^{+}_{n}\big(uq^{n}\big)^{-1}&\text{for type $B_{n}$},\\
h^{+}_{n+1}\big(uq^{n-1}\big)\,h^{+}_{n-1}\big(uq^{n-1}\big)^{-1}&\text{for type $D_{n}$},\end{cases}

define an isomorphism
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})\to U^{R}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
.

To prove the Main Theorem we embed
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
into an extended
quantum affine algebra
U^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
which is defined by
a Drinfeld-type presentation. The next step is to use the Gauss decomposition to
construct a homomorphism from
the extended quantum affine algebra to the
algebra
U(R)
which is defined by the same
presentation as the algebra
U^{R}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
, except that the relation
(
1.10
) is omitted. The expressions on the left hand side of (
1.10
),
considered in the algebra
U(R)
, turn out to be scalar matrices,

\displaystyle{L}^{\pm}(u)D{L}^{\pm}(u\xi)^{{\rm t}}D^{-1}={z}^{\pm}(u)\,1,

for certain formal series
{z}^{\pm}(u)
. Moreover, all coefficients of these series
are central in
U(R)
. We will give explicit formulas for
{z}^{\pm}(u)
,
regarded as series with coefficients in the algebra
U^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
,
in terms of its Drinfeld generators.
The quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
can therefore be considered
as the quotient of
U^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
by the relations
{z}^{\pm}(u)=1
.

As a final step, we construct the inverse map
U(R)\to U^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})

by using the universal
R
-matrix for the quantum affine algebra and producing
the associated
L
-operators corresponding to the vector representation of the
algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})
.

Quantum affine algebras

Recall the original definition of the quantum affine algebra

U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
as introduced by Drinfeld
[
9
]
and Jimbo
[
17
]
.
We suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
of rank
n

and
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the corresponding (untwisted) affine Kac–Moody algebra with the affine
Cartan matrix
[A_{ij}]_{i,j=0}^{n}
. We let
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}

denote the simple roots and use the notation of
[
5
, Sections 9.1 and 12.2]

so that
q_{i}=q^{r_{i}}
for
r_{i}=(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})/2
.

2.1
Drinfeld–Jimbo definition and new realization

The
quantum affine algebra

U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
is a unital associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}\big(q^{1/2}\big)
with generators
E_{\alpha_{i}}
,
F_{\alpha_{i}}
and

k_{i}^{\pm 1}
with
i=0,1,\dots,n
, subject to the defining relations:

\displaystyle k_{i}k_{i}^{-1}=k_{i}^{-1}k_{i}=1,\qquad k_{i}k_{j}=k_{i}k_{j},

\displaystyle k_{i}E_{\alpha_{j}}k_{i}^{-1}=q^{A_{ij}}_{i}E_{\alpha_{j}},\qquad k_{i}F_{\alpha_{j}}k_{i}^{-1}=q^{-A_{ij}}_{i}F_{\alpha_{j}},

\displaystyle[E_{\alpha_{i}},F_{\alpha_{j}}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{k_{i}-k_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{1-A_{ij}}(-1)^{r}{{1-A_{ij}}\brack{r}}_{q_{i}}(E_{\alpha_{i}})^{r}E_{\alpha_{j}}(E_{\alpha_{i}})^{1-A_{ij}-r}=0,\qquad\text{if}\quad i\neq j,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{1-A_{ij}}(-1)^{r}{{1-A_{ij}}\brack{r}}_{q_{i}}(F_{\alpha_{i}})^{r}F_{\alpha_{j}}(F_{\alpha_{i}})^{1-A_{ij}-r}=0,\qquad\text{if}\quad i\neq j.

By using the braid group action, the set of generators of the algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})

can be extended to the set of affine root vectors of the form
E_{\alpha+k\delta}
,
F_{\alpha+k\delta}
,

E_{(k\delta,i)}
and
F_{(k\delta,i)}
, where
\alpha
runs over the positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\delta
is the basic imaginary root; see
[
1
,
3
]
for details.
Moreover, we can introduce
k_{\alpha}=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n}k_{i}^{m_{i}}
for every
\alpha=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}m_{i}\alpha_{i}
,
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Especially, we denote
q^{C}=k_{\delta}
.
The root vectors are used in the explicit isomorphism between the
Drinfeld–Jimbo presentation of the algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})

and the “new realization” of Drinfeld which goes back to
[
10
]
, while detailed arguments
were given by Beck
[
1
]
; see also
[
3
, Lemma 1.5]
.
In particular, for the Drinfeld presentation of the algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{N})

given in the Introduction, we find that the isomorphism between these presentations is given by

\displaystyle q^{c/2}\mapsto q^{C/2},\qquad x^{+}_{ik}\mapsto o(i)^{k}E_{\alpha_{i}+k\delta},\qquad

\displaystyle x^{-}_{i,-k}\mapsto o(i)^{k}F_{\alpha_{i}+k\delta},\qquad

\displaystyle k\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle x^{+}_{i,-k}\mapsto-o(i)^{k}F_{-\alpha_{i}+k\delta}\,k_{i}^{-1}q^{kC},\qquad

\displaystyle x^{-}_{i,k}\mapsto-o(i)^{k}q^{-kC}\,k_{i}\,E_{-\alpha_{i}+k\delta},\qquad

\displaystyle k>0,

\displaystyle a_{i,k}\mapsto o(i)^{k}q^{-kC/2}E_{(k\delta,i)},\qquad

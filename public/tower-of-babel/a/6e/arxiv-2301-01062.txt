On the cohomology of Torelli groups. II
Oscar Randal-Williams

Abstract
. We describe the ring structure of the rational cohomology of the Torelli groups of the manifolds # g ​ S n × S n \#^{g}S^{n}\times S^{n} in a stable range, for 2 ​ n ≥ 6 2n\geq 6 . Some of our results are also valid for 2 ​ n = 2 2n=2 , where they are closely related to unpublished results of Kawazumi and Morita.

Introduction

This paper can be considered as a (somewhat extensive) addendum to our earlier work with Kupers
[
KRW20b
]
. We shall be concerned with the manifold
W_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#^{g}S^{n}\times S^{n}
generalising to higher dimensions the orientable surface of genus
g
, its topological group
\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(W_{g})
of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms, and various subgroups of it. The first kind of subgroups are
\mathrm{Diff}(W_{g},D^{2n})\leq\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(W_{g},*)\leq\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(W_{g})
, the diffeomorphisms which fix a disc and a point respectively. The second kind are their
Torelli subgroups

\mathrm{Tor}(W_{g},D^{2n}),\quad\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g},*),\quad\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g}),

consisting of those diffeomorphisms which in addition act trivially on
H_{n}(W_{g};\mathbb{Z})
. The intersection form on this middle cohomology group is nondegenerate and
(-1)^{n}
-symmetric, giving a homomorphism

\alpha_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(W_{g})\longrightarrow G_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})&\text{ if $n$ is odd,}\\
\mathrm{O}_{g,g}(\mathbb{Z})&\text{ if $n$ is even.}\end{cases}

This map is not always surjective, but its image is a certain finite-index subgroup
G^{\prime}_{g}\leq G_{g}
, even when restricted to
\mathrm{Diff}(W_{g},D^{2n})
, so there is an outer
G^{\prime}_{g}
-action on each of the Torelli subgroups. This makes the rational cohomology of each of the Torelli groups into
G^{\prime}_{g}
-representations.

In
[
KRW20b
]
, for
2n\geq 6
we determined
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}(W_{g},D^{2n});\mathbb{Q})
as a ring and as a
G^{\prime}_{g}
-representation in a range of degrees tending to infinity with
g
. Using the Serre spectral sequence associated to various simple fibrations relating the different Torelli groups we were able to also determine
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g},*);\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g});\mathbb{Q})
as
G^{\prime}_{g}
-representations. This kind of argument was not able to determine the ring structure, however, as multiplicative information gets lost when passing to the associated graded of the Serre filtration. Here we shall determine
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g},*);\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g});\mathbb{Q})
as
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras too: this is achieved in Theorem
4.1
. The statement given there is more powerful, but just as in
[
KRW20b
, Section 5]
one can extract from it the following presentation for
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g});\mathbb{Q})
, which is easier to parse. (A presentation for
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g},*);\mathbb{Q})
can be extracted in a similar way.)

Let us write
H(g)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{n}(W_{g};\mathbb{Q})
, on which
G^{\prime}_{g}
operates in the evident way. Let
\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H(g)\otimes H(g)\to\mathbb{Q}
denote the intersection form, and
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2g}
be a basis of
H(g)
with dual basis
\{a_{i}^{\#}\}_{i=1}^{2g}
in the sense that
\lambda(a_{i}^{\#},a_{j})=\delta_{ij}
, so that the form dual to the pairing
\lambda
is
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{2g}a_{i}\otimes a_{i}^{\#}
. In Section
2.2
we will construct certain “modified twisted Miller–Morita–Mumford classes”, which when restricted to the Torelli group yield
G^{\prime}_{g}
-equivariant maps

\bar{\kappa}_{c}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H(g)^{\otimes r}\longrightarrow H^{n(r-2)+|c|}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g});\mathbb{Q})

for each
c\in\mathbb{Q}[e,p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n-1}]=H^{*}(B\mathrm{SO}(2n);\mathbb{Q})
and each
s\geq 0
. When
r=0
we write
\bar{\kappa}_{c}=\bar{\kappa}_{c}(1)
; these agree with the usual Miller–Morita–Mumford classes
\kappa_{c}
. Finally, we write
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\chi(W_{g})=2+(-1)^{n}2g
and will always suppose that this is not zero (i.e. that
(n,g)\neq(\text{odd},1)
).

Theorem A
.

If
2n\geq 6
then, in a range of degrees tending to infinity with
g
,
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g});\mathbb{Q})
is generated as a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra by the classes
\bar{\kappa}_{c}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})
for
c
a monomial in
e,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1}
, and
r\geq 0
, such that
n(r-2)+|c|>0
. A complete set of relations in this range is given by

(i)

\bar{\kappa}_{c}(v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma(r)})=\mathrm{sign}(\sigma)^{n}\cdot\bar{\kappa}_{c}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})
,

(ii)

\bar{\kappa}_{e}(v_{1})=0
,

(iii)

\begin{aligned} \sum_{i}\bar{\kappa}_{x}(v\otimes a_{i})\cdot\bar{\kappa}_{y}(a_{i}^{\#}\otimes w)&=\bar{\kappa}_{x\cdot y}(v\otimes w)+\tfrac{1}{\chi^{2}}\bar{\kappa}_{e^{2}}\cdot\bar{\kappa}_{x}(v)\cdot\bar{\kappa}_{y}(w)\\
&\quad\quad-\tfrac{1}{\chi}\bigl(\bar{\kappa}_{e\cdot x}(v)\cdot\bar{\kappa}_{y}(w)+\bar{\kappa}_{x}(v)\cdot\bar{\kappa}_{e\cdot y}(w)\bigr),\end{aligned}

(iv)

\begin{aligned} \sum_{i}\bar{\kappa}_{x}(v\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i}^{\#})=\tfrac{\chi-2}{\chi}\bar{\kappa}_{e\cdot x}(v)+\tfrac{1}{\chi^{2}}\bar{\kappa}_{e^{2}}\cdot\bar{\kappa}_{x}(v)\end{aligned}
,

(v)

\bar{\kappa}_{\mathcal{L}_{i}}=0
, where
\mathcal{L}_{i}
denotes the
i
th Hirzebruch
\mathcal{L}
-class,

for
v\in H(g)^{\otimes r}
and
w\in H(g)^{\otimes s}
.

As in
[
KRW20b
, Section 5.4]
this is not the smallest possible presentation: it can be simplified by manipulating graphs; we leave the details to the interested reader.

For
2n=4
or
2n=2
there is still a map from the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra given by this presentation to
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g});\mathbb{Q})
. If
2n=2
then (in a stable range) this map is an isomorphism onto the maximal algebraic subrepresentation in degrees
\leq N
, assuming that
H^{*}(B\mathrm{Tor}^{+}(W_{g});\mathbb{Q})
is finite-dimensional in degrees

for all large enough

. This is known to hold for

by work of Johnson
[
Joh85
]
.

Characteristic classes

2.1.
Recollection on twisted Miller–Morita–Mumford classes.

If
\pi^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
is an oriented smooth
W_{g}^{2n}
-bundle equipped with a section
s\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{H}
denotes the local coefficient system
x\mapsto H_{n}((\pi^{\prime})^{-1}(x);\mathbb{Q})
on
X^{\prime}
, then it is explained in
[
KRW20b
, Section 3.2]
that there is a unique class
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{s}\in H^{n}(E^{\prime};\mathcal{H})
characterised by

(i)

for each
x\in X^{\prime}
the element
\varepsilon|_{(\pi^{\prime})^{-1}(x)}\in H^{n}((\pi^{\prime})^{-1}(x);\mathbb{Q})\otimes H_{n}((\pi^{\prime})^{-1}(x);\mathbb{Q})
is coevaluation, and

(ii)

s^{*}\varepsilon=0
.

The proof is as follows. The Serre spectral sequence yields an exact sequence

0\to H^{n}(X^{\prime};\mathcal{H})\overset{(\pi^{\prime})^{*}}{\to}H^{n}(E^{\prime};\mathcal{H})\to H^{0}(X^{\prime};\mathcal{H}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{H})\overset{d_{n+1}}{\to}H^{n+1}(X^{\prime};\mathcal{H})\overset{(\pi^{\prime})^{*}}{\to}H^{n+1}(E^{\prime};\mathcal{H})

and the section
s
shows that the right-hand map
(\pi^{\prime})^{*}
is injective, so that the map
d_{n+1}
is zero, and splits the left-hand map
(\pi^{\prime})^{*}
. The class
\text{coev}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime};\mathcal{H}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{H})
then gives rise to a unique
\varepsilon
satisfying the given properties.

We then defined the twisted Miller–Morita–Mumford class

(2.1)

\kappa_{\varepsilon^{a}c}=\kappa_{\varepsilon^{a}c}(\pi^{\prime},s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi^{\prime}_{!}(\varepsilon^{a}\cdot c(T_{\pi^{\prime}}E^{\prime}))\in H^{(a-2)n+|c|}(X^{\prime};\mathcal{H}^{\otimes a}).

2.2.
Modified twisted Miller–Morita–Mumford classes

If
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to X
is an oriented smooth
W_{g}^{2n}
-bundle but is not equipped with a section then, as long as
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\chi(W_{g})=2+(-1)^{n}2g\neq 0
(i.e.
(n,g)\neq(\text{odd},1)
, cf. Remark
2.1
), the cohomological role of the section can instead be played by the
transfer map

\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi_{!}(e\cdot-)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{*}(E;\mathcal{H})\longrightarrow H^{*}(X;\mathcal{H}),

where
e\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=e(T_{\pi}E)\in H^{2n}(E;\mathbb{Q})
denotes the Euler class of the vertical tangent bundle. The projection formula

\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi_{!}(e\cdot\pi^{*}(x))=\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi_{!}(e)\cdot x=\tfrac{\chi}{\chi}x=x

shows that this map splits
\pi^{*}
. Thus in this situation there is a unique class
\bar{\varepsilon}\in H^{n}(E;\mathcal{H})
characterised by

(i)

for each
x\in X
the element
\bar{\varepsilon}|_{\pi^{-1}(x)}\in H^{n}(\pi^{-1}(x);\mathbb{Q})\otimes H_{n}(\pi^{-1}(x);\mathbb{Q})
is coevaluation, and

(ii)

\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi_{!}(e\cdot\bar{\varepsilon})=0
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
(n,g)=(\text{odd},1)
then there is no class
\bar{\varepsilon}\in H^{n}(E;\mathcal{H})
satisfying (i) and natural under pullback. To see this it suffices to give one example of a smooth oriented
W_{1}
-bundle for which
\bar{\varepsilon}
does not exist. Consider the Borel construction for the evident action of
S^{1}\times S^{1}
on
W_{1}=S^{n}\times S^{n}
given by considering
S^{n}
as the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{(n+1)/2}
. This gives a smoth oriented
W_{1}
-bundle over
B(S^{1}\times S^{1})
with total space
E\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{(n-1)/2}\times\mathbb{CP}^{(n-1)/2}
. Thus
H^{n}(E;\mathcal{H})=0
as
n
is odd but
E
has a cell structure with only even-dimensional cells.

By analogy with (
2.1
) we may then define the modified twisted Miller–Morita–Mumford class

(2.2)

\kappa_{\bar{\varepsilon}^{a}c}=\kappa_{\bar{\varepsilon}^{a}c}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{!}(\bar{\varepsilon}^{a}\cdot c(T_{\pi}E))\in H^{(a-2)n+|c|}(X;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes a}).

If
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to X
does have a section
s\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to E
then the class
\varepsilon\in H^{n}(E;\mathcal{H})
is also defined, and we may compare it with
\bar{\varepsilon}
as follows:

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to X
has a section then
\bar{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon-\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi^{*}\kappa_{\varepsilon e}
.

Proof.

The classes
\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}\in H^{n}(E;\mathcal{H})
are both defined, and agree when restricted to the fibres of the map
\pi
, so by considering the Serre spectral sequence for
\pi
we must have
\bar{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon=\pi^{*}(x)
for some class
x\in H^{n}(X;\mathcal{H})
. Applying
\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi_{!}(e\cdot-)
we see that
x=\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi_{!}(e\cdot(\bar{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon))=0-\tfrac{1}{\chi}\pi_{!}(e\cdot\varepsilon)=-\tfrac{1}{\chi}\kappa_{\varepsilon e}
. (Here we have used, as we often will, the fact that
e
has even degree to commute it past
\varepsilon
.)
∎

Remark 3.2

In all cases one can apply the (modified) contraction formula to pass from a graph to a sum of graphs with strictly fewer edges, and so by iterating to a sum of graphs with no edges. These are disjoint unions of labelled corollas, and so correspond to partitions of

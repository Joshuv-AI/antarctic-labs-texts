A Norton-Hoff model with an elastic and inelastic constitutive relationships dependent on temperature
Sebastian Owczarek

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to prove the existence of weak solution for a quasi-static evolution of thermo-visco-elastic model with Norton-Hoff law of plasticity. The dependence on temperature occurs both in the elastic constitutive equations (generalised Hooke’s law) and in describing the evolution of visco-elastic strain. These thermal effects have not been previously considered. The approximations of the considered models did not allow in literature such a general models. The main idea of the article is the revocation to R. Temam articles on the plasticity from eighties of the previous century and to write down the equations related to the plastic deformations in the same way. For the obtained equations we propose approximations in a flow rule. Thanks to this manner of writing the equations, we show the existence of a weak solution. To characterize the weak limits in nonlinearities occurring in the system at different levels of approximation, the following tools are used: Young measures, Boccardo’s and Gallouët’s approach, truncation methods for heat equation used by D. Blanchard and Minty-Browder trick.



1
Introduction

1.1
Description of the problem

In this article, we take into account the model describing inelastic strains in solids, which are subject to the effects of temperature. The mathematical model describing such dynamics consists of the classical continuum mechanical models (see for example
[
1
,
46
]
) and a nonlinear heat equation derived from the first law of thermodynamics. From mathematical point of view a nonzero thermal expansion of material makes the system very complicated and the mathematical analysis of equations describing such process is one of the challenging problems in applied mathematics. One of the main reasons is that the nonlinearities occurring in the system are only integrable functions and the standard energy methods do not work. By analyzing the literature (which we will cover in detail later) we can conclude that the original model is not satisfactorily researched from mathematical point of view. In the considered models, simplifications are made which allow to obtain results concerning the existence of very weak solutions. Here, we study a system that in the presented generality was not considered. For such a system, we use our proposed approximations, thanks to which we show the existence of classic weak solutions. The approach presented in this article is completely new and has not been used in in the framework under consideration.

Let us assume the body occupies the bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and fix a positive real number
{\mathfrak{T}}>0
. The dynamics of inelastic strains in solids can be described in two ways. The standard unknowns in this theory are the displacement field
u:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and the stress tensor
\sigma:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\cal S}^{3}
, where
{\cal S}^{3}
denotes the set of symmetric
3\times 3
-matrices. A very popular description is described in Alber’s book
[
1
]
, which was very often used in recent years, see for example
[
2
,
17
,
23
,
29
,
27
,
43
]
. In this approach the inelastic part of the infinitesimal strain tensor
\varepsilon(u)=\mathrm{sym}(\nabla_{x}u)
is described by an additional internal variable
\varepsilon^{p}
i.e.

\varepsilon(u)=(\varepsilon(u)-\varepsilon^{p})+\varepsilon^{p}

and the first part on the right-hand side describes the pure elastic deformations (
\mathrm{sym}(\nabla_{x}u)
denotes the symmetric part of the gradient of the displacement). Then, the elastic constitutive equation takes the form of a generalized Hooke’s law:

\sigma=\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon(u)-\varepsilon^{p})\,,

where the operator
\mathbb{C}:{\cal S}^{3}\rightarrow{\cal S}^{3}
is a classical
4^{\mathrm{th}}
order
elasticity tensor. The inelastic constitutive equation is given in the form of evolution equation for
\varepsilon^{p}

\varepsilon^{p}_{t}=\,G(\sigma,\varepsilon^{p})\,,

(1.1)

where
G
is a given constitutive function (in general
G
may be a multifunction) and
\varepsilon_{t}^{p}
denotes the time derivative of the tensor
\varepsilon_{t}^{p}
. In the literature we can find various examples of the function
G
(for instance
[
1
,
18
,
39
,
20
]
and many others). Observe that selection of the vector fields
G
lead to different models.

Here, we are going to use a different description that was used by Roger Temam in the book
[
46
]
and also in the work
[
47
,
9
,
10
,
33
]
. This approach does not introduce an additional unknown
\varepsilon^{p}
. It is based only on standard unknowns
u
and
\sigma
. Assuming small deformations and taking into account a special density of external forces acting on the material (a dumping term), the slow motion of the body is governed by the classical balance of momentum

\mathrm{div}(\sigma)=-F-\mathrm{div}\,{\mathbb{C}}(\varepsilon(u_{t}))\,,

(1.2)

where the acceleration term is omitted since we consider a quasi-static problem. The function
F:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
in (
1.2
) describes the applied body forces. The general equations (
1.2
) must be completed by constitutive relations. The body is subject to thermal expansion, therefore the Cauchy stress tensor consists of two stresses

\sigma=T-f(\theta)\,{1\!\!\!\>1}\,,

(1.3)

where
T:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\cal S}^{3}
is an elastic one, the second one is the thermal stress and
\theta:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is the temperature of the material. The function
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a nonlinear given constitutive function depending on considered material. In this paper we deal with the isotropic materials. This means that the 4th order tensor of elastic constants
{\mathbb{C}}
is defined by the Lamé constants
\lambda
and
\mu
i.e.

{\mathbb{C}}_{ijkl}=\mu(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})+\lambda\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\,,

(1.4)

where
\delta
denotes the Kronecker symbol. Then, the elastic constitutive equation takes the form of a generalized Hooke’s law:

T=2\mu\varepsilon(u)+\lambda\mathrm{tr}(\varepsilon(u)){1\!\!\!\>1}\,.

(1.5)

The assumptions
\mu>0
and
3\lambda+2\mu>0
means that the deformation of elastic energy is positive definite and they imply that the inverse operator of
{\mathbb{C}}
exists. Hence, inverting the relation (
1.5
) we obtain

\varepsilon(u)={\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T\,,

(1.6)

where
{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}:{\cal S}^{3}\rightarrow{\cal S}^{3}
is a positive definite operator which leads to

\varepsilon(u)=\frac{1}{2\mu}T-\frac{\lambda}{2\mu(2\mu+3\lambda)}\mathrm{tr}(T){1\!\!\!\>1}\,.

(1.7)

In this paper we give a viscous-elastic model. The viscous properties are associated with inelastic deformation. Then the inelastic constitutive relation is in the form

{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}+G(T,\theta)=\varepsilon(u_{t})\,,

(1.8)

where
G
is a given constitutive function, which in our model depends on
T
and
\theta
. We are going to deal with a generalization of the Norton-Hoff type constitutive function which is known and very popular in the literature, see
[
47
,
27
,
23
,
29
,
9
]
. More precisely, we replace equation (
1.8
) by

{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}+\big\{|\dev(T)|-\beta(\theta)\big\}_{+}^{r}\,\frac{\dev(T)}{|\dev(T)|}=\varepsilon(u_{t})\,,

(1.9)

where
\dev(T)=T-\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{tr}\,(T)\cdot{1\!\!\!\>1}
denotes the deviator of a
3\times 3
-tensor. The symbol
\{\xi\}_{+}
denotes the nonnegative part of the real number
\xi
. The function
\beta:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is given and depends on the material under consideration, while
r>1
is a fixed number. The equations (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.9
) are completed by the heat equation on the temperature function
\theta
. It is a consequence of the first principle of thermodynamics, hence the heat transfer is governed by the equation

\theta_{t}-\Delta\theta+f(\theta)\mathrm{div}\,u_{t}=\big\{|\dev(T)|-\beta(\theta)\big\}_{+}^{r}|\dev(T)|\,.

(1.10)

In conclusion, the problem that we study in this article is: for a fix positive real number
{\mathfrak{T}}>0
, find the displacement field
u:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the stress tensor
T:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\cal S}^{3}
and the temperature of the material
\theta:\Omega\times[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
solution of the following system of equations

\begin{split}\mathrm{div}(T-f(\theta){1\!\!\!\>1})&=-F-\mathrm{div}\,{\mathbb{C}}(\varepsilon(u_{t}))\,,\\[4.30554pt]
{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}+\big\{|\dev(T)|-\beta(\theta)\big\}_{+}^{r}\,\frac{\dev(T)}{|\dev(T)|}&=\varepsilon(u_{t})\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\theta_{t}-\Delta\theta+f(\theta)\mathrm{div}\,u_{t}&=\big\{|\dev(T)|-\beta(\theta)\big\}_{+}^{r}|\dev(T)|\,,\end{split}

(1.11)

where the tensor
{\mathbb{C}}
is defined in (
1.4
). The equations in (
1.11
) are studied for
x\in\Omega
and time
t\in[0,{\mathfrak{T}}]
.

The system (
1.11
) is considered with the nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition for the displacement and with the nonhomogeneous Neumann boundary condition for the temperature

\begin{split}u(x,t)&=g_{D}(x,t)\quad\,\textrm{ for}\quad x\in\partial\Omega\quad\textrm{and}\quad t\geq 0\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial\,\theta}{\partial\,n}(x,t)&=g_{\theta}(x,t)\quad\,\,\,\textrm{ for}\quad x\in\partial\Omega\quad\textrm{and}\quad t\geq 0\,.\end{split}

(1.12)

Finally, we adjoin to the system (
1.11
) the following initial conditions

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad T(x,0)=T_{0}(x),\quad\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x)\,.

(1.13)

There are many different possible ways to deal with problems in which the thermal effects are included. However, none of them guarantee solvability of the problem in the general case. One can linearize the system of equations i.e.
f(\theta)=c_{\theta}(\theta-\theta_{0})\,,
while the heat equation takes the form

\theta_{t}-\Delta\theta+c_{\theta}(\theta-\theta_{0})\,\mathrm{div}u_{t}=r\,.

The nonlinear term is usually approximated in the linear theory by a linear term

c_{0}\dyw u_{t}
with the physical argument that the temperature in the
deformation process remains close to the reference temperature, see e.g.
[
41
,
27
,
6
,
31
]
. The first mathematical analysis of thermoelasticity including the nonlinear heat equation was done in
[
12
]
and later in
[
13
]
. The authors consider the visco-elastic
Kelvin-Voigt
model
, i.e. they assume that the constitutive relation is of the form

\sigma=A\sym(\nabla u)+B\sym(\nabla u_{t})-f(\theta)\,{1\!\!\!\>1}\,,

where
A,B
are symmetric and positive definite linear operators acting on
symmetric matrices and
f
is a constitutive function describing the
thermal part of the stress tensor. The additional term
B\sym(\nabla u_{t})
in the constitutive relation allows to control the very difficult term
f(\theta)\dyw u_{t}
in the
heat equation. Under the fundamental assumption of a
sublinear growth of the function
f
the authors have proven the existence of
renormalized
solutions
of the considered system of equations. The other approach is to add additional dumping term into right-hand side of momentum equation, see
[
22
,
23
,
40
]
. This term also helps to control the difficult term in the heat equation. Moreover, in these articles the coupling with thermal effects occurs only in an elastic constitutive relationship (the constitutive function
G
does not depend on
\theta
).

Additionally, it is worth to emphasize the works
[
29
,
28
,
34
]
and
[
41
,
27
]
, where the authors deal with thermo-visco-elasticity systems. However, in these works the thermal expansion does not appear, which means that the Cauchy stress tensor does not depend on temperature function. Coupling between temperature and displacement occur only in the inelastic constitutive equation. An important issue is the fact that in the systems considered by Gwiazda at al. the total energy is conserved, contrary to the systems analysed in
[
31
,
24
,
7
,
6
]
in which the lack of the total energy is observed. This is caused by the linearisation. The temperature occurring in nonlinear dissipation term of heat equation is only linearised (without any linearision of the Cauchy stress tensor).

Another point of view on thermo-mechanical problems is presented by S. Bartels and T. Roubíček (see for example
[
8
]
and
[
43
]
) where the authors use, the so called, enthalpy transformation and consider energetic solutions. In both of this papers the authors study Kelvin-Voigt viscous material, but in the article
[
8
]
they consider a plasticity model with hardening in quasistatic case, while in
[
43
]
the perfect plasticity in dynamical case is considered.

We emphasize that in the present article we consider a model in which coupling with thermal effects occurs both: in the generalized Hooke’s law (equation (
1.3
)) and in the inelastic constitutive equation (
1.9
). The total energy of the system (
1.11
) is also conserved.

1.2
Main assumptions and main result

The dissipation term
f(\theta)\dyw u_{t}
on the right-hand side of the heat equation
\eqref{Main}_{3}
is expected in the space
L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,{\mathfrak{T}}))
. It is known that, in general, for integrable data a weak solution might not exist. From articles
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
25
]
we are able to deduce that a solution of heat equation with
L^{1}
-data is expected as a function from
L^{p}((0,{\mathfrak{T}})\times\Omega)
for
p<\frac{N+2}{N}
, where
N
denotes the dimension of a space in which the heat equation is considered. For this reason, in this paper it is assumed that the function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous, satisfies the following growth condition

|f(r)|\leq a+B|r|^{\alpha}\qquad\mathrm{for\,all}\qquad r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\,,\,\,a,\,B\geq 0\,\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\,\alpha\in\Big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{6}\Big)

(1.14)

and there exists constant
\tilde{B}>0
such that

|f(r)|\leq\tilde{B}(1+|r|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\mathrm{for\,all}\qquad r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{-}\,.

(1.15)

The above growth assumption on the function
f
were first used in the articles
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
and then also in
[
23
]
,
[
40
]
.

Additionally let assume that the function
\beta:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies the following conditions:

(C1)

\beta\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}})
,

(C2)

there exists
d>0
such that
\beta(r)\in[0,d]
for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(C3)

there exists
\tilde{d}>0
such that
|\beta^{\prime}(r)|\in[0,\tilde{d}]
for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Recall that the Prandtl-Reuss inelastic flow rule with the von Mises yield function is in the form
[
37
,
26
]

\varepsilon(u_{t})-{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}\in\partial I_{K}(T)\,,

(1.16)

where the set of admissible elastic stresses
K
is defined in the following form

K=\{T\in{\cal S}^{3}:|\dev(T)|\leq k\}\,,

with
k>0
a given material constant (the yield limit). The function
I_{K}
is the indicator function of the set
K
and the function
\partial I_{K}
denotes the subgradient of the convex, proper, lower semicontinuous function
I_{K}
in the sense of convex analysis (for more details we refer to
[
5
]
). As it know the flow rule (
1.16
) can be obtained as a limit of visco-elastic (Norton-Hoff) flow rule (see for example
[
47
]
)

\varepsilon(u_{t})-{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}=\big\{|\dev(T)|-k)\big\}_{+}^{r}\,\frac{\dev(T)}{|\dev(T)|}

(1.17)

with
r\geq 1
. A natural extension of (
1.16
), by including heat effects, is the following flow rule

\varepsilon(u_{t})-{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}\in\partial I_{K(\theta)}(T)\,,

(1.18)

where the set
K(\theta)
is in the form
K(\theta)=\{T\in{\cal S}^{3}:|\dev(T)|\leq k-\theta\}
. The natural area that is used in practice is when
0\leq\theta\leq k
(more information can be found for example in
[
24
]
). Then the approximation of equation (
1.18
) takes the form

\varepsilon(u_{t})-{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}=\big\{|\dev(T)|-(k-\theta))\big\}_{+}^{r}\,\frac{\dev(T)}{|\dev(T)|}\,.

(1.19)

Assuming in the equation
\eqref{Main}_{2}
that
\beta(\theta)=k-\theta
and
0\leq\theta\leq k
we obtain that the assumptions (C1), (C2) and (C3) are fulfils naturally. Therefore, the system considered in this article can be treated as an approximation of the Prandtl-Reuss model in which thermal effects were taken into account.

We will assume that the given data
F
,
g_{D}
,
g_{\theta}
,
u_{0}
,
T_{0}
,
\theta_{0}
have the regularities

\begin{split}F\in L^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\,,&\quad g_{D}\in W^{1,\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};W^{\alpha,\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(\partial\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\,,\\[4.30554pt]
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\,,&\quad(T_{0},\dev(T_{0}))\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3})\times L^{2r}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3}_{\mathrm{dev}})\,,\\[4.30554pt]
g_{\theta}\in L^{2}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{2}(\partial\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}))\,,&\quad\theta_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}})\,,\end{split}

(1.20)

where
\alpha
is coming from assumption (
1.14
) and we can observe that
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\in(2,6)
. Such an atypical assumptions on the data
F
and
g_{D}
are related to the nonhomogeneous boundary condition for the Dirichlet boundary condition for the displacement vector. Systems with nonhomogeneous boundary condition for the displacement, where the temperature dependence occurs in the two constitutive equations, not been investigated.

Let us introduce a definition of a solution for the system (
1.11
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
1<q<\frac{5}{4}
. We say that a vector
(u,T,\theta)
is a solution of the system (
1.11
) with the boundary and initial conditions (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) if:

1.
it has the following regularities:

\begin{split}u\in H^{1}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};H^{1}_{g_{D}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\,,&\quad T\in L^{\infty}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{2}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3}))\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\dev(T)\in L^{r+1}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{r+1}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3}_{\mathrm{dev}}))\,,&\quad T_{t}\in L^{\frac{r+1}{r}}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{\frac{r+1}{r}}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3}))\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\theta\in L^{q}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};W^{1,q}(\Omega))&\cap C([0,{\mathfrak{T}}];L^{1}(\Omega))\,,\\[4.30554pt]
f(\theta)\in L^{2}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{2}(\Omega))\,,&\quad\theta_{t}\in L^{1}\big(0,{\mathfrak{T}};\big(W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\big)^{\ast}\big)\,,\end{split}

(1.21)

where
H^{1}_{g_{D}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):\,u=g_{D}\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\}
and the space
\big(W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\big)^{\ast}
is the space of all linear bounded functionals on
W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)

(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1)
.

2.
the equations (
1.11
) are satisfied in the following form

\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\Omega}\big(T-f(\theta){1\!\!\!\>1}\big)\varepsilon(\psi)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon(u_{t})\,\varepsilon(\psi)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\Omega}F\,\psi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t

(1.22)

for every function
\psi\in C^{\infty}([0,{\mathfrak{T}}];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{{\mathfrak{T}}}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}\,\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t+&\int_{0}^{{\mathfrak{T}}}\int_{\Omega}\big\{|\dev(T)|-\beta(\theta)\big\}^{r}_{+}\frac{\dev(T)}{|\dev(T)|}\,\dev(\varphi)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t\\[4.30554pt]
&=\int_{0}^{{\mathfrak{T}}}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon(u_{t})\,\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t\end{split}

(1.23)

for all
\varphi\in L^{r+1}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{r+1}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3}))
and

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\Omega}\theta_{t}\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\theta\,\nabla\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\Omega}f\big(\theta)\mathrm{div}(u_{t})\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 8.61108pt=\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\Omega}\big\{|\dev(T)|-\beta(\theta)\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T)|\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{\theta}\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\end{split}

(1.24)

for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}([0,{\mathfrak{T}}];C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}))
.

3.
for almost all
x\in\Omega
the initial conditions

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad T(x,0)=T_{0}(x),\quad\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x)\,.

(1.25)

are met.

Remark 1.2
.

Taking in the equation (
1.22
) the test function from the space
C^{\infty}([0,{\mathfrak{T}}];C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
, we deduce that the weak divergence
\rm{div\,}(T-f(\theta){1\!\!\!\>1}+{\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon(u_{t}))
fulfills

\rm{div\,}(T-f(\theta){1\!\!\!\>1}+{\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon(u_{t}))=-F\in L^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))

and in that sense the equation
\eqref{Main}_{1}
holds for almost all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,{\mathfrak{T}})
. Additionally, the equation (
1.23
) holds for all functions in the linear space

, therefore

{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T_{t}(x,t)+\big\{|\dev(T(x,t))|-\beta(\theta(x,t))\big\}_{+}^{r}\,\frac{\dev(T(x,t))}{|\dev(T(x,t))|}=\varepsilon(u_{t}(x,t))

for almost all

1.24
) holds for almost everyone

i.e.

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\theta_{t}\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\ +\int_{\Omega}\nabla\theta\,\nabla\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}f\big(\theta)\mathrm{div}(u_{t})\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 8.61108pt=\int_{\Omega}\big\{|\dev(T)|-\beta(\theta)\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T)|\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}g_{\theta}\,\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}S(x)\,.\end{split}

Theorem 1.3
.

\mathrm{(Main\,result)}

Let assume that the growth assumptions (
1.14
), (
1.15
) and the assumptions (C1), (C2), (C3) are satisfied. Additionally assume that the given data
F
,
g_{D}
,
g_{\theta}
,
u_{0}
,
T_{0}
,
\theta_{0}
have the regularities specified in (
1.20
). Then there exists a solution of the system (
1.11
) with the boundary and initial conditions (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is our proposed approximation of inelastic constitutive relationship
\eqref{Main}_{3}
. Simultaneously we make a truncation in the thermal part of the stress tensor and in nonlinear terms occurring in the heat equation
\eqref{Main}_{2}
. The applied approximation allows for obtaining the
L^{2}
-strong solutions (Definition
2.1
) in any step of approximation. Comparing our results with other from the literature, it can be concluded that in many cases the approximations used did not allow to consider the models in the presented generality. For example, in articles
[
22
,
23
]
and
[
40
]
the authors use the Yosida approximation to show the existence in the used approximation. The Yosida approximation did not allow to consider the model in which the flow rule depends on the temperature. Additionally, solutions were obtained only in the renormalised sense. In works
[
29
,
28
,
34
]
and
[
27
]
, Gwiazda et al. proposed a very complicated two-level Galerkin approximation for the visco-elastic strain tensor. With the help of this approximation, they were able to consider a flow rules which depends on the temperature. However, the dependence on temperature in an elastic constitutive relationship occurs only in elementary cases such as homogeneous thermal expansion
[
27
]
. In these articles, for the models in question, very weak solutions were obtained, where the visco-elastic strain tensor can be recovered from the equation on its evolution, only. We also want to mention two articles
[
32
]
and
[
35
]
in which the dependence on temperature occurs in both: the elastic and inelastic constitutive equations. However, ignoring the differences in the constitutive relations under consideration, it is assumed that the nonlinear function

2.3
Young measures tools

In this section, for the convenience of the reader, we present all the tools concerning Young measures which we need to characterize the weak limit
\omega^{k}
. For more details and proofs, we refer to
[
38
, Corollaries 3.2-3.4]
and
[
3
, Theorem 2.9]
, see also
[
36
]
.
Let us consider a measurable set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and by
C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
we will denote the closure of continuous functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with a compact support.
C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
equipped with a norm
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}|f(\lambda)|
is a Banach space. From the Riesz representation theorem the dual of
C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
can be identified with the space
\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
of bounded Radon measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. The duality pair between
C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and
\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})

is defined by

\langle\nu,f\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f(\lambda)\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu(\lambda)\,.

(2.44)

We will start with the Fundamental theorem on Young measures, see Theorem 3.1 of
[
38
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a measurable set of finite measure and let
z_{j}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a sequence of measurable functions.
Then there exist a subsequence (still denote by
z_{j}
) and weak
∗
measurable map
\nu_{x}:E\rightarrow\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
such that the following holds

(i)

\nu_{x}\geq 0
,
\|\nu_{x}\|_{\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{x}\leq 1
for a.e.
x\in E
.

(ii)

For all
f\in C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})

f(z_{j})\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\bar{f}\quad\textrm{in}\quad L^{\infty}(E)\,,

where

\bar{f}(x)=\langle\nu_{x},f\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f(\lambda)\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{x}(\lambda)\,.

(iii)

Let
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be compact. If
\mathrm{dist}\,(z_{j},K)\rightarrow 0
in measure, then
\mathrm{supp}\,\nu_{x}\subset K
.

(iv)

Furthermore
\|\nu_{x}\|_{\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}=1
for a.a.
x\in E
if and only if the sequence does not go to infinity, i.e. if

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\,\sup\big|\{|z_{j}|\geq M\}\big|=0\,.

(v)

Assume that
\|\nu_{x}\|_{\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}=1
for a.a.
x\in E
,
A\subset E
is measurable,
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and the set
\{f(z_{j})\}
is relatively weakly compact in
L^{1}(A)
. Then

f(z_{j})\rightharpoonup\bar{f}\quad\textrm{in}\quad L^{1}(A)\,,\quad\bar{f}(x)=\langle\nu_{x},f\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f(\lambda)\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{x}(\lambda)\,.

Theorem
2.11
refers to the existence of Young measures. Let us now formulate a few properties related to these measures.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let us assume that a sequence
z_{j}
of measurable functions from
E
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
generates the Young measure
\nu:E\rightarrow\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
. Then
z_{j}\rightarrow z
in measure if and only if
\nu_{x}=\delta_{z(x)}
a.e.

Lemma 2.13
.

Suppose that the sequence of maps
z_{j}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
generates Young measure
\nu_{x}:E\rightarrow\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
. Let
f:E\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a Carathéodory function (i.e. measurable in the first argument and continuous in the second one). Let us also assume that the negative part
\{f(x,z_{j}(x))\}_{-}
is weakly relatively compact in
L^{1}(E)
. Then

\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{E}f(x,z_{j}(x))\,{\mathrm{d}}x\geq\int_{E}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f(x,\lambda)\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{x}(\lambda)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,.

(2.45)

Additionally, if the sequence of functions
x\mapsto|f|(x,z_{j}(x))
is weakly relatively compact in
L^{1}(E)
, then

f(\cdot,z_{j}(\cdot))\rightharpoonup\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f(\cdot,\lambda)\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{x}(\lambda)\quad\mathrm{in}\quad L^{1}(E)\,.

And the last property is follow

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
u_{j}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
v_{j}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be measurable and suppose that
u_{j}\rightarrow u
a.e. while
v_{j}
generates the Young measure
\nu
. Then the sequence of pairs
(u_{j},v_{j}):E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}
generates the Young measure
x\rightarrow\delta_{u(x)}\otimes\nu_{x}

f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta_{m}^{k})\big)v\rightarrow f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)v\quad\mathrm{in}\quad L^{2}(0,{\mathfrak{T}};L^{2}(\Omega))\,.

(2.65)

The information (
2.65
) allows us to use standard tools in the Galerkin method and obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&\theta^{k}_{t}\,v\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\theta^{k}\nabla v\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)\mathrm{div}(\tilde{u}_{t}+u^{k}_{t})\,v\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\[4.30554pt]
=&\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\big(\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T^{k})|\big)\,v\,{\mathrm{d}}x\end{split}

(2.66)

for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and almost all
t\in(0,{\mathfrak{T}})
. Additionally we got

\int_{\Omega}\big(T^{k}-f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big){1\!\!\!\>1}\big)\varepsilon(w)\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}(\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t}))\,\varepsilon(w)\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0

(2.67)

for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and almost all
t\in(0,{\mathfrak{T}})
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T^{k}_{t}\,\tau\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}\,\frac{\dev(T^{k})}{|\dev(T^{k})|}\,\tau\,{\mathrm{d}}x

(2.68)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}|\dev(T^{k})|^{2r-1}\,\frac{\dev(T^{k})}{|\dev(T^{k})|}\,\tau\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\big(\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t})+\varepsilon(\tilde{u}_{t})\big)\tau\,{\mathrm{d}}x

for all
\tau\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3})\times L^{2r}(\Omega;{\cal S}^{3}_{\mathrm{dev}})
and almost all
t\in(0,{\mathfrak{T}})
. Formula (
2.68
) completes the proof of the existence of solutions for any approximation step
k>0
.

Proof of the Main Result

3.1
Estimates independent on truncation

In this chapter we are going to pass to the limit with

and obtain solutions in the sense of Definition
1.1
.
At the beginning we prove some a priori estimates for the sequence of approximate solutions
\{(T^{k},u^{k},\theta^{k})\}_{k>0}

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the given data satisfy all requirements of (
1.20
).
Then there exists a positive constant
C({\mathfrak{T}})
(not depending on

\displaystyle\|T^{k}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{k}\|\dev(T^{k}(t))\|^{2r}_{L^{2r}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon(u_{t}^{k})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau+\int_{\Omega}|\theta^{k}(t)|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\,\,C({\mathfrak{T}})\,,

where

.

Proof.

The proof of the above inequality is based on the proof of Theorem 4.1 from
[
23
]
. In this article, we consider nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition for the displacement vector, which was not taken into account in
[
23
]
. As a consequence we get an additional term that needs to be bounded independently of
k>0
. Theorem
3.1
is an essential part of the proof of main result, so we decided to present it in the complete form. Fix
M>0
. Testing the equation (
2.67
) by
w=Mu_{t}^{k}
, the equation (
2.68
) by
\tau=MT^{k}
and the equation (
2.66
) by
v={\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})
we obtain

\begin{split}M\int_{\Omega}\big(T^{k}&-f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big){1\!\!\!\>1}\big)\varepsilon(u_{t}^{k})\,{\mathrm{d}}x+M\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}(\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t}))\,\varepsilon(u_{t}^{k})\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0\,,\\[4.30554pt]
M\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T^{k}_{t}T^{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}x&+M\int_{\Omega}\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T^{k})|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\[4.30554pt]
&+\frac{M}{k}\int_{\Omega}|\dev(T^{k})|^{2r}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=M\int_{\Omega}\big(\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t})+\varepsilon(\tilde{u}_{t})\big)T^{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,,\\[4.30554pt]

(3.1)

Integrating (
3.1
) with respect to time and adding all the equations in (
3.1
) we arrive to the following identity

\begin{split}&M\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T^{k}(t)\,T^{k}(t){\mathrm{d}}x+M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t})\,\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]

&\hskip 8.61108pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)\mathrm{div}\,u^{k}_{t}\,\big(M-{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 17.22217pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}\,{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 25.83325pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\big(\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T^{k})|\big)\,{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\,,\end{split}

(3.2)

where the function
\varphi_{M}(\cdot)
is defined by the formula (
2.5
).
Notice that the first initial integral of (
3.2
) is independent on
k>0
. On the other hand, the linear increase of the function
\varphi_{M}
at infinity allows us to estimate the second initial integral as follows

\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{M}({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta_{0}))\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq C\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\,

(3.3)

for every
k>0
, where the constant
C>0
is independent of
k
. Moreover,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}&{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\big(\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T^{k})|\big)\,{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T^{k})|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\,.\end{split}

(3.4)

Using the Hölder, the Young inequalities and regularity of the given data we conclude that

\begin{split}&M\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T^{k}(t)\,T^{k}(t){\mathrm{d}}x+M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t})\,\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]

&\leq\quad\tilde{C}({\mathfrak{T}})+M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|T^{k}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\varepsilon(u^{k}_{t})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 8.61108pt+D\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)\,\big(M-{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\big)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 17.22217pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}\,{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\,,\end{split}

(3.5)

where
\nu>0
is any positive constant and the constants
\tilde{C}({\mathfrak{T}})
and
D
do not depend on
k>0
. Let us start by estimating the last integral in (

) that arises from nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}>2
). Recalling (
1.14
) we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}&\int_{\Omega}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}\,{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\leq M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big|f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})\big)\big||\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big(a+B|{\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k})|^{\alpha}\big)|\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big(a+B|\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k}|^{\alpha}\big)|\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq M\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\big(a+B|\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k}|^{\alpha}\big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}|^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\Big)^{1-\alpha}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq M\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}2^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}(a^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k}|)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}|^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\Big)^{1-\alpha}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq M\Big(\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}2^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}(a^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|\tilde{\theta}+\theta^{k}|)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau+(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{div}\,\tilde{u}_{t}|^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\Big)\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq\quad\hat{C}({\mathfrak{T}})+M2^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\theta^{k}|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\,,\end{split}

(3.6)

where the constants
\hat{C}({\mathfrak{T}})
does not depend on
k>0
. Until the proof is completed, we have one more integral to estimate on the right-hand side of (

[
23
]
, but for the convenience of the reader we have decided to present it here. Let us define two sets

and

Q_{2}=\{(x,\tau)\in\Omega\times(0,t):\,-M<\theta^{k}(x,\tau)<M\}
. For almost every
(x,\tau)\in\Omega\times(0,t)
the following inequality is satisfied

\begin{split}\Big(f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)\Big)^{2}\big(M-{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\big)^{2}&\leq 4M^{2}f^{2}\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)\chi_{Q_{1}}\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 8.61108pt+4M^{2}f^{2}\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)\chi_{Q_{2}}\,,\end{split}

(3.7)

where
\chi_{Q_{1}}
and
\chi_{Q_{2}}
are the characteristic functions of the sets
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, respectively. The inequality (

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Big(f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)\Big)^{2}\big(M-{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k})\big)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau&\leq 4M^{2}\int_{Q_{1}}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]
&+4M^{2}\int_{Q_{2}}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\,.\end{split}

(3.8)

The regularity of

(3.9)

Again the regularity of
\tilde{\theta}
and the growth conditions on

\begin{split}4M^{2}\int_{Q_{1}^{\prime\prime}}f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t&\leq 4CM^{2}\int_{Q_{1}^{\prime\prime}}(1+|\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta}|)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbb{C}}^{-1}T^{k}_{t}\,T^{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}\,|\dev(T^{k})|\,{\mathrm{d}}x

(3.11)

Remark 3.4
.

The growth condition (

\int\limits_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int\limits_{\Omega}|f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\leq A+\tilde{M}\int\limits_{0}^{\mathfrak{T}}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\theta^{k}|^{2\alpha}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\,,

\Big\{f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big)\mathrm{div}\,u^{k}_{t}+{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\Big(\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T^{k})|\Big)\Big\}_{\lambda>0}

.

Remark 3.5

Let us examine the following integral

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}&\int_{\Omega}\Big|\big\{|\dev(T^{k})|-\beta(\theta^{k}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}\,\frac{\dev(T^{k})}{|\dev(T^{k})|}\Big|^{\frac{r+1}{r}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\\[4.30554pt]

G^{i}=f\big({\mathcal{T}}_{i}(\theta^{i}+\tilde{\theta})\big)\mathrm{div}\,u^{i}_{t}+{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\Big(\big\{|\dev(T^{i})|-\beta(\theta^{i}+\tilde{\theta})\big\}^{r}_{+}|\dev(T^{i})|\Big)

(3.44)

for
i=k,\,l
. Observe that the function
{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k}-\theta^{l})\rightarrow 0
for almost all
(x,\tau)\in\Omega\times(0,t)
(as
k,\,l\rightarrow\infty
). Additionally,
{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k}-\theta^{l})
is uniformly bounded. The Egorov Theorem entails that for every
\nu>0
, there exists a measurable subset
A
of
\Omega\times(0,t)
such that
|A|<\nu
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k}-\theta^{l})
converges to
0
uniformly on
\Omega\times(0,t)\setminus A
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&\varphi_{M}(\theta^{k}-\theta^{l})(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Big(G^{k}-G^{l}\Big){\mathcal{T}}_{M}(\theta^{k}-\theta^{l})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{M}(\theta^{k}-\theta^{l})(0)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\[4.30554pt]

\end{split}

(3.45)

From Lemma 4.5, we know that the sequence
G^{k}
converges weakly in

) tends to zero. Notice that

\varphi_{M}(r)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}|r|^{2}&\textrm{if}\quad|r|\leq M\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{1}{2}M|r|&\textrm{if}\quad|r|>M\\

and the sequence

The asymptotic expansion of a Mathieu-exponential series
R. B. Paris

Abstract
We consider the asymptotic expansion of the functional series S μ ± ​ ( a , λ ) = ∑ n = 0 ∞ ( ± 1 ) n ​ e − λ ​ n ( n 2 + a 2 ) μ S_{\mu}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{n}e^{-\lambda n}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}} for λ > 0 \lambda>0 and μ ≥ 0 \mu\geq 0 as | a | → ∞ |a|\to\infty in the sector | arg ⁡ a | < π / 2 |\arg\,a|<\pi/2 . The approach employed consists of expressing S μ ± ​ ( a , λ ) S_{\mu}^{\pm}(a;\lambda) as a contour integral combined with suitable deformation of the integration path. Numerical examples are provided to illustrate the accuracy of the various expansions obtained. Mathematics subject classification (2010): 30E15, 33E20, 33C15, 34E05, 41A60 Keywords: Mathieu series, asymptotic expansions, exponential asymptotics, modified Bessel function



1. Introduction

The functional series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}

(1.1)

is known as a Mathieu series
[
3
]
, which originally arose (in the case
\mu=2
) in problems dealing with the elasticity of solid bodies.
The asymptotic expansion for large
a
of more general functional series of this type has been discussed in
[
7
]
and
[
12
]
.
More recently, Gerhold and Tomovski
[
1
]
extended the asymptotic study of (
1.1
) by introducing the factor
z^{n}
, where
|z|\leq 1
. From this result they were able to deduce, in particular, the large-
a
expansions of the trigonometric Mathieu series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n\sin nx}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}},\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n\cos nx}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}.

Subsequently, the above trigonometric series were generalised in the form
[
9
]

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\gamma}{\cal C}_{\nu}(bn/a)}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\qquad(b>0),

(1.2)

where
{\cal C}_{\nu}
denotes the oscillatory Bessel functions
J_{\nu}(x)
and
Y_{\nu}(x)
with argument proportional to
n/a
, and their large-
a
asymptotics determined.
In addition, this last study also considered the inclusion of the modified Bessel function
K_{\nu}(x)
of similar argument,
which contains the decaying exponential as a special case when
\nu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
, since

K_{1/2}(x)=(\pi/2x)^{1/2}e^{-x}.

In
[
10
]
, the case where the additional term is a Gaussian exponential, namely the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\gamma}e^{-\lambda n^{2}/a^{2}}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\qquad(\lambda>0),

has been considered. For even integer values of the parameter
\gamma
it is found that the large-
a
asymptotic expansion consists of an algebraic expansion with a finite number of terms together with a sequence of increasingly subdominant exponentially small contributions. This situation is analogous to the well-known Poisson-Jacobi transformation (corresponding to
\mu=\gamma=0
) given by
[
11
, p. 124]

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda n^{2}/a^{2}}=\frac{a}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}-\frac{1}{2}+a\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}a^{2}/\lambda}.

The asymptotic expansion we consider here is the alternating Mathieu series coupled with a decaying exponential (depending linearly on the summation index
n
) of the form

S_{\mu}^{\pm}(a;\lambda):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{n}e^{-\lambda n}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\qquad(\mu\geq 0,\ \lambda>0)

(1.3)

for
|a|\to\infty
in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
. In
[
9
, §5]
, the related series with the exponential factor
e^{-bn/a}
, resulting from (
1.2
) with
{\cal C}_{\nu}(nb/a)=K_{\nu}(bn/a)
when
\nu=\gamma=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
, was shown to possess the asymptotic expansion

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{n}e^{-bn/a}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}-a^{2\mu-1}\biggl(\frac{\pi}{2b}\biggr)^{\!\!1/2}\biggl\{\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}J_{\mu}(a;b/a)\\
0\end{array}\!\!\!\biggr\}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2b}{\pi a}}\,\{R^{\pm}(a;\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})+R^{\pm}(a;-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\}

as
|a|\to\infty
in
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
, where the quantity
J_{\mu}(a;b/a)
is given in (
3.1
) and

R^{\pm}(a;w):=\frac{a^{-w-2\mu}b^{w}}{2^{w+1}}\Gamma(-w)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(\mu)_{k}}{k!a^{2k}}Z^{\pm}(-\omega_{k})F_{k}(w,b).

Here
Z^{+}(x):=\zeta(x)
,
Z^{-}(x):=(1-2^{1-x})\zeta(x)
,
\omega_{k}:=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+w+2k
, with
\zeta(s)
being the Riemann zeta function, and
F_{k}(w,b)
are polynomials expressed as a terminating hypergeometric series defined by

F_{k}(w,b):={}_{1}F_{2}(-k;\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+w,1-\mu-k;-b^{2}/4).

However, if we set
b=\lambda a
, where
\lambda>0
is finite, to obtain a series equivalent to that in (
1.3
), it is seen that the polynomials
F_{k}(w,b)=F_{k}(w,\lambda a)
with the consequence that the formal series
R(a;\pm\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})
lose
their asymptotic character.

Rather than adopting a Mellin transform approach used in
[
9
]
, we express
S_{\mu}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)
as a contour integral combined with suitable integration path deformation. Such an approach has been employed by Olver in his well-known book
[
5
, p. 303]
, who showed that in the particular case
\lambda=0

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}=\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}-\mu}\sqrt{\pi}}{a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}((2k+1)\pi a)}{((2k+1)\pi a)^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}}

(1.4)

for
\mu>0
.
For large values of
a
in
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
, the sum of Bessel functions decays exponentially fast.
Series of the form (
1.4
) have arisen in aerodynamic interference calculations; see
[
4
]
.
We shall find that when
\lambda>0
there is an analogous sum of
K
-Bessel functions (with complex argument) together with an additional term possessing an algebraic asymptotic expansion when
a
is large.

We note at this point the special evaluations for non-negative integer
\mu
given by

S_{0}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)=\frac{e^{\lambda}}{e^{\lambda}\mp 1}

(1.5)

and

\displaystyle S_{1}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{{\cal F}_{1}}{2a^{2}},\qquad S_{2}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)=\frac{1}{4a^{4}}({\cal F}_{1}+{\cal F}_{2}),

\displaystyle S_{3}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{16a^{6}}(3{\cal F}_{1}+3{\cal F}_{2}+2{\cal F}_{3}),

\displaystyle S_{4}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{32a^{8}}(5{\cal F}_{1}+5{\cal F}_{2}+4{\cal F}_{3}+2{\cal F}_{4}),

\displaystyle S_{5}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{256a^{10}}(35{\cal F}_{1}+35{\cal F}_{2}+30{\cal F}_{3}+20{\cal F}_{4}+8{\cal F}_{5}),\dots\,,

(1.6)

where

{\cal F}_{n}=F_{n}(a)+F_{n}(-a),\quad F_{n}(a)={}_{n+1}F_{n}\biggl(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}1,ia,\ldots,ia\\
1+ia,\ldots,1+ia\end{array};\,\pm e^{-\lambda}\biggr)

and
{}_{n+1}F_{n}
is the generalised hypergeometric function.

2. Derivation of the expansion for
S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)

We first consider the sum
S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)
.
Following
[
5
, p. 303]
, we have

S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)=\frac{1}{2i}\int_{\infty}^{(0+)}\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{\sin\pi t}\,\frac{dt}{(a^{2}+t^{2})^{\mu}},\qquad(|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi),

(2.1)

where the integration path encloses only the poles of the integrand situated at
t=0,1,2,\ldots\
. Let us first consider the case
a>0
. The integrand then has branch points at
t=\pm ia
with cuts along the imaginary axis
emanating from these points and passing to infinity. Since
\lambda>0
, the loop contour may be deformed to coincide with the imaginary axis between
[-i(a-\rho),i(a-\rho)]
(with an indentation of radius
\rho
around the origin on the left-hand side) together with the portions of the imaginary axis situated above
ia
and below
-ia

on the right-hand side of the cuts; see Fig. 1. Provided
\mu<1
, the contribution from the indentations of radius
\rho
round the branch points vanishes as
\rho\to 0
.

Figure 1:
The deformed integration path in the
t
-plane. The heavy lines emanating from the points
t=\pm ia
denote branch cuts. The arrow denotes the direction of integration.

2.1 The contribution between the branch points

We first deal with the integral between the branch points which we denote by
I_{1}
. We have

I_{1}=-\frac{a^{1-2\mu}}{2i}\int_{-1+\rho}^{1-\rho}\frac{e^{-\lambda iat}}{\sinh\pi at}\,\frac{dt}{(1-t^{2})^{\mu}},

where the integration path is indented to the left at the origin, so that

I_{1}=\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda),\qquad H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda):=a^{1-2\mu}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin\lambda at}{\sinh\pi at}\,\frac{dt}{(1-t^{2})^{\mu}}

(2.2)

when
\rho\to 0
. Now

\frac{\sin\lambda x}{\sinh\pi x}=\frac{\lambda}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}A_{k}x^{2k}\qquad(|x|<\infty),

where

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,\quad A_{1}=\frac{1}{6}(\lambda^{2}+\pi^{2}),\quad A_{2}=\frac{1}{360}(3\lambda^{4}+10\lambda^{2}\pi^{2}+7\pi^{4}),

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{15120}(3\lambda^{6}+21\lambda^{4}\pi^{2}+49\lambda^{2}\pi^{4}-31\pi^{6}),

\displaystyle A_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1814400}(5\lambda^{8}+60\lambda^{6}\pi^{2}+294\lambda^{4}\pi^{4}+620\lambda^{2}\pi^{6}+381\pi^{8}),\ldots\ .

Then we find

H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)=\frac{\lambda a^{1-2\mu}}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}A_{k}a^{2k}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2k}}{(1-t^{2})^{\mu}}\,dt

=\frac{\lambda a^{1-2\mu}}{2\pi}\Gamma(1-\mu)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}A_{k}\Gamma(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}{\Gamma(k+\frac{3}{2}-\mu)}\,a^{2k}\qquad(\mu<1).

(2.3)

This form of expansion is suitable for computation when
a
is not large.

To deal with the situation when
a\to\infty
, we expand the factor
(1-t^{2})^{-\mu}
appearing in (
2.2
) by the binomial theorem to obtain

H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)=a^{1-2\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}t^{2k}\frac{\sin\lambda at}{\sinh\pi at}\,dt.

Since
\sinh\pi at
decays exponentially for
t
bounded away from the origin, we can replace the upper limit 1 by
\infty
, thereby introducing an error of O(
e^{-\pi a})
. From the result
[
2
, (3.524.1)]
, we have

B_{k}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{2k}\,\frac{\sin\lambda\tau}{\sinh\pi\tau}\,d\tau=\frac{i(2k)!}{(2\pi)^{2k+1}}\biggl\{\zeta\biggl(2k+1,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)-\zeta\biggl(2k+1,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr\}

where
\zeta(a,s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+a)^{-s}
is the Hurwitz zeta function. Using the fact that
\zeta(2k+1,z)=-\psi^{(2k)}(z)/(2k)!
, where
\psi(z)
is the logarithmic derivative of the gamma function, and
[
6
, (5.15.6)]

\psi^{(2k)}(1-z)-\psi^{(2k)}(z)=\pi\biggl(\frac{d}{dz}\biggr)^{\!\!2k}\!\!\cot\pi z,

(2.4)

we find that

B_{k}=\frac{i}{(2\pi)^{2k+1}}\biggl\{\psi^{(2k)}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)-\psi^{(2k)}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr\}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{2k+1}}\biggl(\frac{d}{dx}\biggr)^{\!\!2k}\tanh x\biggr|_{x=\lambda/2}.

The first few coefficients
B_{k}
are given by:

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tanh x,\quad B_{1}=\frac{\sinh x}{4\cosh^{3}x},\quad B_{2}=\frac{\sinh x}{4\cosh^{5}x}(2-\sinh^{2}x),

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh x}{8\cosh^{7}x}\,(17-26\sinh^{2}x+2\sinh^{4}x),

\displaystyle B_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh x}{4\cosh^{9}x}\,(62-192\sinh^{2}x+60\sinh^{4}x-\sinh^{6}x),\ldots\ ,

where
x=\lambda/2
.
Hence we obtain the expansion

H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)\sim a^{-2\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}B_{k}}{k!\,a^{2k}}

(2.5)

as
a\to+\infty
.

2.2 The contribution beyond the branch points

Consider the contribution to
S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)
resulting from the part of the integration path situated in the interval
[ia,i\infty)
. We have

I_{2}=-\frac{1}{2i}\int_{ia}^{\infty i}\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{\sin\pi t}\,\frac{dt}{(a^{2}+t^{2})^{\mu}}=-\frac{a^{1-2\mu}e^{-\pi i\mu}}{2i}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda iat}}{\sinh\pi at}\,\frac{dt}{(t^{2}-1)^{\mu}}.

Now on
t\in[1,\infty)

\mbox{cosech}\,\pi at=2e^{-\pi at}(1-e^{-2\pi at})^{-1}=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-(2k+1)\pi at},

so that substitution in
I_{2}
followed by term-by-term integration yields

I_{2}=ie^{-\pi i\mu}a^{1-2\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-X_{k}t}}{(t^{2}-1)^{\mu}}\,dt,\qquad X_{k}:=(2k+1)\pi a+\lambda ia

(2.6)

=\frac{i\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-\pi i\mu}}{\Gamma(\mu)\sin\pi\mu}\,(2a^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}(X_{k})}{(X_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}},

where the integral has been evaluated in terms of a modified Bessel function by
[
5
, p. 254]
when
\mu<1
.

The contribution from the path
[-ia,-i\infty)
is the conjugate of the above expression. Hence we find

I_{2}+{\overline{I}}_{2}=T_{\mu}(a;\lambda):=\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}-\mu}\sqrt{\pi}}{a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(\pi\mu-\theta_{k})}{\sin\pi\mu}\biggl|\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}(X_{k})}{(X_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}}\biggr|,

(2.7)

where

\theta_{k}:=\arg\biggl(X_{k}^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}(X_{k})\biggr).

Collecting together the results in (
2.2
) and (
2.7
), we finally obtain the expansion

S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)=\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)+\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}-\mu}\sqrt{\pi}}{a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(\pi\mu-\theta_{k})}{\sin\pi\mu}\biggl|\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}(X_{k})}{(X_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}}\biggr|,

(2.8)

where
X_{k}
is defined in (
2.6
).
When
\lambda=0
, we have
H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)\equiv 0
,
\theta_{k}\equiv 0
and (
2.8
) reduces to Olver’s expansion stated in (
1.4
). However, the result (
2.8
) has been established provided
\mu<1
. It does not appear possible to extend the validity of (
2.8
) to
\mu\geq 1
by analytic continuation, as was the case in Olver’s treatment when
\lambda=0
. When
a
is large,
S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)
is seen to consist of an algebraic expansion given in (
2.5
) together with an exponentially small contribution from the infinite sum of modified Bessel functions.

When
a
is allowed to take on complex values in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
, the branch points
\pm ia
move off the imaginary axis to the points
ae^{(\phi\pm\frac{1}{2}\pi)i}
, where
\phi=\arg\,a
, as indicated in
[
5
, p. 303]
. The analysis in this case follows that given above and we conclude that the expansion (
2.8
) holds for
|a|\to\infty
in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
.

3. Derivation of the expansion for
S_{\mu}^{+}(a;\lambda)

Since the residue of
\cot\pi t
at any integer
n
is
1/\pi
, we have

S_{\mu}^{+}(a;\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda n}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}=\frac{1}{2i}\int_{\infty}^{(0+)}\frac{\cot\pi t\,e^{-\lambda t}}{(t^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\,dt

where the path encloses only the poles at
t=0,1,2,\ldots\,
. The integration path can be deformed as illustrated in Fig. 1; the upper and lower parts of this path are denoted by
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, respectively.
We have (see
[
5
, p. 305]
)

\frac{\cot\pi t}{2i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\!\!\!\!-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\displaystyle{\frac{e^{\pi it}}{2i\sin\pi t}}&(\mbox{on}\ \ C_{1})\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\displaystyle{\frac{e^{-\pi it}}{2i\sin\pi t}}&(\mbox{on}\ \ C_{2}),\end{array}\right.

so that

S_{\mu}^{+}(a;\lambda)=J_{\mu}(a;\lambda)-\frac{1}{2i}\int_{C_{1}}\frac{e^{-\lambda t+\pi it}}{\sin\pi t\,(t^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\,dt+\frac{1}{2i}\int_{C_{2}}\frac{e^{-\lambda t-\pi it}}{\sin\pi t\,(t^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\,dt.

The integral
J_{\mu}(a;\lambda)
is given by

J_{\mu}(a;\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{(t^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\,dt=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}a^{1-2\mu}\Gamma(1-\mu)}{2(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\lambda a)^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}}\,{\bf K}_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}(\lambda a),

(3.1)

where
{\bf K}_{\nu}(x):={\bf H}_{\nu}(x)-Y_{\nu}(x)
is the Struve function defined in
[
6
, (11.5.2)]
.

The contribution between the branch points
\pm ia
is

\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+H^{+}_{\mu}(a;\lambda),

where

H^{+}_{\mu}(a;\lambda)=a^{1-2\mu}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\pi au}\,\frac{\sin\lambda au}{\sinh\pi au}\,\frac{du}{(1-u^{2})^{\mu}}.

This integral may be evaluated as in Section 2 by expanding
\sin\lambda au/\sinh\pi au
. However the resulting integrals are expressible in terms of two
{}_{2}F_{2}
hypergeometric functions and so will not be presented here. Our main interest is the estimation of
H^{+}_{\mu}(a;\lambda)
for large
a
. Proceeding as in Section 2 we find that

H^{+}_{\mu}(a;\lambda)\sim a^{1-2\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi au}\,\frac{\sin\lambda au}{\sinh\pi au}\,u^{2k}\,du

=a^{1-2\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!(\pi a)^{2k+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-w}\,\frac{\sin\lambda w/\pi}{\sinh w}\,w^{2k}dw.

For
k\geq 1
, we have

\frac{1}{2i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w(1\pm i\lambda/\pi)}}{\sinh w}\,w^{2k}dw=\frac{-i(2k)!}{2^{2k+1}}\,\zeta\biggl(2k+1,1\pm\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)

upon use of the result
[
2
, (3.552.1)]

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta x}}{\sinh x}\,dx=2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)\,\zeta(2k+1,\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\beta)

valid for
\Re(\alpha)>1
,
\Re(\beta)>-1
. Then, we define the coefficients
{\hat{B}}_{k}
by

{\hat{B}}_{k}:=\frac{i(-1)^{k-1}(2k)!}{(2\pi)^{2k+1}}\biggl\{\zeta\biggl(2k+1,1-\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)-\zeta\biggl(2k+1,1+\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr\}

=\frac{i(-1)^{k-1}}{(2\pi)^{2k+1}}\biggl\{\psi^{(2k)}\biggl(1+\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)-\psi^{(2k)}\biggl(-\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\biggr)-\frac{i(-1)^{k}(2k)!}{(\lambda/2\pi)^{2k+1}}\biggr\},

where we have employed the result
\psi^{(2k)}(1+z)=\psi^{(2k)}(z)+(2k)!/z^{2k+1}

[
6
, (15.5.5)]
.
Application of (
2.4
) then yields

{\hat{B}}_{k}=2^{-2k-1}\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{d}{dx}\biggr)^{\!\!2k}\coth x-\frac{(2k)!}{x^{2k+1}}\biggr\}_{x=\lambda/2}.

The first few coefficients are given by:

{\hat{B}}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\biggl(\coth x-\frac{1}{x}\biggr),\quad{\hat{B}}_{1}=\frac{1}{4}\biggl(\frac{\cosh x}{\sinh^{3}x}-\frac{1}{x^{3}}\biggr),\quad{\hat{B}}_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\biggl(\frac{\cosh x}{\sinh^{5}x}\,(2+\cosh^{2}x)-\frac{3}{x^{5}}\biggr),

{\hat{B}}_{3}=\frac{1}{8}\biggl(\frac{\cosh x}{\sinh^{7}x}\,(17+26\cosh^{2}x+2\cosh^{4}x)-\frac{45}{x^{7}}\biggr),

{\hat{B}}_{4}=\frac{1}{4}\biggl(\frac{\cosh x}{\sinh^{9}x}\,(62+192\cosh^{2}x+60\cosh^{4}x+\cosh^{6}x)-\frac{315}{x^{9}}\biggr),\ldots\ ,

where
x=\lambda/2
.
Then we have the expansion

H^{+}_{\mu}(a;\lambda)\sim a^{-2\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(\mu)_{k}{\hat{B}}_{k}}{k!a^{2k}}

(3.2)

as
a\to\infty
.

Finally, the contribution from
[ia,\infty i)
is

I_{2}=-e^{-\pi i\mu}\frac{a^{1-2\mu}}{2i}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(\pi+i\lambda)at}}{\sinh\pi at}\,\frac{dt}{(t^{2}-1)^{\mu}}=ie^{-\pi i\mu}a^{1-2\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-{\hat{X}}_{k}t}}{(t^{2}-1)^{\mu}}\,dt

=\frac{i\sqrt{\pi}e^{-\pi i\mu}}{(2a^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\mu)\sin\pi\mu}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}({\hat{X}}_{k})}{{\hat{X}}_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}},

where

{\hat{X}}_{k}:=(2k+2)\pi a+\lambda ia.

(3.3)

The contribution from the path
[-ia,-i\infty)
is the conjugate of the above expression. Hence we find

I_{2}+{\overline{I}}_{2}={\hat{T}}_{\mu}(a;\lambda):=\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}-\mu}\sqrt{\pi}}{a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(\pi\mu-{\hat{\theta}}_{k})}{\sin\pi\mu}\biggl|\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}({\hat{X}}_{k})}{({\hat{X}}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}}\biggr|,

(3.4)

where

{\hat{\theta}}_{k}:=\arg\biggl({\hat{X}}_{k}^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}({\hat{X}}_{k})\biggr).

Collecting together the results in (
3.1
) – (
3.4
), we finally obtain the expansion

S_{\mu}^{+}(a;\lambda)=\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+J_{\mu}(a;\lambda)+H_{\mu}^{+}(a;\lambda)+\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}-\mu}\sqrt{\pi}}{a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(\pi\mu-{\hat{\theta}}_{k})}{\sin\pi\mu}\biggl|\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}({\hat{X}}_{k})}{({\hat{X}}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}}\biggr|

(3.5)

valid when
\mu<1
, where
{\hat{X}}_{k}
is defined in (
3.3
). The exponentially small sum of Bessel functions is seen to be
O(a^{1-2\mu}e^{-2\pi a})
for large
a
, which is smaller than the equivalent sum arising in
S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)
of
O(a^{1-2\mu}e^{-\pi a})
.

When
a
is complex satisfying
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
, the analysis follows the same procedure and we conclude that the expansion (
3.5
) holds for
|a|\to\infty
in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
.

4. Concluding remarks

We have obtained a representation of the Mathieu-exponential sums
S_{\mu}^{\pm}(a;\lambda)
in (
1.1
) in the form of an infinite sum of modified Bessel functions of complex argument together with a contribution that results from integration between the branch points
\pm ia
. For large
a
in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
, this last contribution possesses an algebraic-type asymptotic expansion. When
\lambda=0
, our result in the case of
S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)
reduces to the expression given by Olver stated in (
1.4
).

A problem with the representation (
2.8
) is that it has been derived only when
0\leq\mu<1
. It is not obvious how this result can be analytically continued into
\mu\geq 1
, as is the case in (
1.4
). One approach might be to exploit the fact that

S_{\mu+1}(a;\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}e^{-\lambda n}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu+1}}=-\frac{1}{2\mu a}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)\qquad(0<\mu<1).

Differentiation of the right-hand side of (
2.8
) using the series expansion (
2.3
) for
H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)
and the properties of the
K
-Bessel function shows that its value is given by (
2.8
) with
\mu
replaced by
\mu+1
. This enables us to extend the representation to
1<\mu<2
, and by continuation to higher ranges of
\mu
.
The representations when
\mu=1,2,\ldots,5
are displayed in (
1.6
). Thus, the algebraic part of the expansion is given by

S_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)\sim\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+H_{\mu}^{-}(a;\lambda)\sim\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+\frac{1}{a^{2\mu}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}B_{k}}{k!a^{2k}}\qquad(\mu\geq 0)

as
|a|\to\infty
in
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
. When
\mu=0
, we note that the right-hand side of the above expression reduces to
e^{\lambda}/(1+e^{\lambda})
as stated in (
1.5
).
Similar considerations can be brought to bear on the sum
S_{\mu}^{+}(a;\lambda)
.

Finally, we observe that when
\lambda=0
, the quantity
J_{\mu}(a;\lambda)
defined in (
3.1
) reduces to

J_{\mu}(a;0)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\mu-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}{2a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}.

Since
H_{\mu}^{+}(a;0)\equiv 0
, we consequently find that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda n}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}=\frac{1}{2a^{2\mu}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\mu-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}{2a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}+\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}-\mu}\sqrt{\pi}}{a^{2\mu-1}\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}((2k+2)\pi a)}{((2k+2)\pi a)^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}}

for
\mu>0
and
|\arg\,a|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
, which complements Olver’s result in (
1.4
).

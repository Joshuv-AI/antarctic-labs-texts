Introduction

1.1
Free vector lattice over a set.

Following the approach of de Pagter and Wickstead (cf.
[
7
, Definition 3.1]
),
a
free vector lattice over
a non-empty set
A
is a pair
(F,i)
,
where
F
is a vector lattice and
i:A\to F
is a map such that,
for any vector lattice
E
and for any map
q:A\to E
, there exists
a unique vector lattice homomorphism
T:F\to E
satisfying

q=T\circ i
. If
(F,i)
is a free
vector lattice over
A
, then
F
is generated by
i(A)
as a vector lattice.
The existence of a free vector lattice
FVL(A)
over
A
is the long
established fact going back to Birkhoff
[
4
]
. A concrete representation
of
FVL(A)
as a vector lattice of real-valued functions was
given by Weinberg
[
9
]
and Baker
[
3
]

(cf. also
[
5
]
). Notice that
FVL(A)
is always Archimedean.
Recently, a
free Banach lattice
FBL(A)
over

A
was introduced and investigated by de Pagter and Wickstead
[
7
]

(see also
[
8
]
for a simple alternative approach to a free
lattice norm on
FVL(A)
). In the present note, we define
a
free uniformly complete vector lattice over

A
and give some of its representations. For further unexplained
notations and terminology we refer to
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
]
.

1.2
ru
-Completion of a vector lattice.

A net
x_{\alpha}
in a vector lattice
U
is relatively uniformly convergent
(
ru
-convergent
)
to
x\in U
if there exists
u\in U_{+}
(
a regulator of the convergence
)
such that, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\alpha_{\varepsilon}

with
|x_{\alpha}-x|\leqslant\varepsilon u

for all
\alpha\geqslant\alpha_{\varepsilon}
(see, e.g.
[
6
]
). As the
ru
-convergence is sequential,
we can restrict ourselves to considering
ru
-convergent sequences.
In particular, a vector lattice
U
is
uniformly complete

iff every
ru
-Cauchy sequence in
U
is
ru
-convergent.
Uniformly complete vector lattices will be abbreviated as UCVLs.

Recall that an Archimedean UCVL
F
is called
an
ru
-completion of
E
if there is a Riesz embedding

i:E\to F
such that, for each other Archimedean UCVL
G
and each Riesz
homomorphism
T:E\to G
, there exists a unique Riesz homomorphism

S:F\to G
satisfying
T=S\circ i
. Clearly,
if an
ru
-completion of a vector lattice exists, it must be unique
up to a Riesz isomorphism.
It is long known that if
E
is Archimedean, then the intersection of all uniformly complete
sublattices containing
E
of the Dedekind completion
E^{\delta}
of
E
is the

ru
-completion of
E
(see, for example
[
2
]
). Even though,
it is rather unpractical to deal with such an abstract description of the
ru
-completion.

1.3
A concrete construction of an
ru
-completion of a vector lattices.

Suppose
E
is a sublattice of
some UCVL
U
. Let
F
be the intersection of all uniformly complete
sublattices of
U
containing
E
. Clearly,
F
is a UCVL.
Define a transfinite sequence

(F_{\beta})_{\beta\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}

of sublattices of
U
by:

F_{1}:=E
;

F_{\beta+1}:=\{x\in U:x_{n}\ruc x(x_{1}),\ \text{\rm for a sequence}\ x_{n}\ \text{\rm in}\ F_{\beta}\}
;

F_{\beta}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}};\ \gamma<\beta}F_{\gamma}

for a limit ordinal
\beta
.

Then
F_{\beta_{1}}\subseteq F_{\beta_{2}}
if
\beta_{1}\leqslant\beta_{2}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
E
be a sublattice of a UCVL
U
.
Then
F=\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}
.

Proof.

Take an
ru
-Cauchy sequence
x_{n}\in\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}

with the regulator
x_{1}
. Since
U
is a UCVL, there exists
x\in U
such that

x_{n}\ruc x(x_{1})
. For each
x_{n}
,
there exists
\beta_{n}
with
x_{n}\in F_{\beta_{n}}
and, if we take
an ordinal
\beta
such that
\beta\geqslant\beta_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
then
x\in F_{\beta+1}\subseteq\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}
.
Therefore,
\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}
is a UCVL containing

E
which is contained in
U
. Thus,

F\subseteq\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}
.

Pick an
ru
-complete sublattice
V
of
U
containing
E
.
Clearly,
F_{1}=E\subseteq V
. Assume

F_{\beta}\subseteq V
for all
\beta<\gamma
.
If
\gamma
is a limit ordinal then

F_{\gamma}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\gamma}F_{\beta}\subseteq V

by the assumption.
If
\gamma=\beta+1
then, for each
x\in F_{\gamma}
,
there exists a sequence
x_{n}
in
F_{\beta}\subseteq V

with
x_{n}\ruc x(x_{1})
. Since
U
is a UCVL,

x\in V
. Since
x\in F_{\gamma}
was taken arbitrary,
F_{\gamma}\subseteq V
.
By the transfinite induction,
F_{\gamma}\subseteq V

for all
\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}
. Since an
ru
-complete
sublattice
V
of
U
containing
E
is abitrary then

\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}\subseteq F
.

In view of
F\subseteq\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}
,
we conclude
F=\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{O}rd}}F_{\gamma}
.
∎

Proposition 1
.

Let
E
be a sublattice of some Archimedean UCVL
U
.
Then
\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{\rm Ord}}}F_{\gamma}

is the
ru
-completion of
E
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
,
F=\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\text{O}rd}}F_{\gamma}

is a UCVL. In order to show that
F
is an
ru
-completion of
E
,
let
G
be an Archimedean UCVL and
T:E\to G
be a Riesz
homomorphism. It is enough to show that
T
has
a unique extension to a Riesz
homomorphism
S:F\to G
. First we prove that such an extension
S

if exists is unique. Indeed assume
S_{1}:F\to G
is another such an extension.
Clearly,
S
agrees with
S_{1}
on
F_{1}=E
.
Assume
S
agrees with
S_{1}
on
F_{\beta}
for all
\beta<\gamma
.
Let
x\in F_{\gamma}
.
If
\gamma
is a limit ordinal then

x\in F_{\gamma}=\bigcup_{\beta<\gamma}F_{\beta}
.
Then
x\in F_{\beta}
for some
\beta<\gamma
, and hence

Sx=S_{1}x
by the assumption.
If
\gamma=\beta+1
then there exists a sequence

x_{n}
in
F_{\beta}
with
x_{n}\ruc x(x_{1})
. In other words, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists
n_{k}
with
|x_{n}-x|\leqslant\frac{1}{k}x_{1}
for all
n\geqslant n_{k}
.
Then

|Sx_{n}-Sx|+|S_{1}x_{n}-S_{1}x|\leqslant\frac{1}{k}(Sx_{1}+S_{1}x_{1})\ \ \ \ \ (\forall n\geqslant n_{k}),

and hence
Sx_{n}\ruc Sx(Sx_{1}+S_{1}x_{1})
and

S_{1}x_{n}\ruc S_{1}x(Sx_{1}+S_{1}x_{1})
in
G
.
Since
G
is Archimedean, we conclude
Sx=S_{1}x
,
as desired.

In order to show the existence of the required extension
S:F\to G
,
we use Lemma
1
and apply the transfinite induction by
\gamma
.
So, assume that extensions
S_{\alpha}:F_{\alpha}\to G
exist already
for all
\alpha<\gamma
. By the same arguments as above,
they are unique on each of
F_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\gamma
,
and hence
S_{\alpha}
agree with
S_{\beta}
on
F_{\min{(\alpha,\beta)}}
.
Let
x\in F_{\gamma}
.

(A) If
\gamma
is a limit ordinal then
x\in F_{\alpha}

for some
\alpha<\gamma
, and we set
S_{\gamma}x:=S_{\alpha}x
.

(B) If
\gamma=\beta+1
, there exists a sequence

x_{n}
in
F_{\beta}
with
x_{n}\ruc x(x_{1})
. Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists
n_{k}
with
|x_{n}-x|\leqslant\frac{1}{k}x_{1}
for all
n\geqslant n_{k}
, and hence

|x_{n}-x_{m}|\leqslant|x_{n}-x|+|x_{m}-x|\leqslant\frac{2}{k}x_{1}\ \ \ \ (\forall n,m\geqslant n_{k}).

Then
|S_{\beta}x_{n}-S_{\beta}x_{m}|\leqslant\frac{2}{k}Sx_{1}
for all
n,m\geqslant n_{k}
.
Therefore, the sequence
(S_{\beta}x_{n})_{n}
is
ru
-Cauchy in
G
.
Since
G
is an Archimedean UCVL, there exists a unique

y\in G
satisfying
S_{\beta}x_{n}\ruc y
in
G
. We set
S_{\gamma}x:=y
.
It is easy to see that this
y
does not depend on the choice of a sequence

x_{n}
in
F_{\beta}
such that
x_{n}\ruc x(x_{1})
. Therefore,
S_{\gamma}x

is well defined.

Finally, let
x\in F
then
x\in F_{\gamma}
for some
\gamma
and
we set
Sx:=S_{\gamma}x
. Since the linear and the lattice operations
are
ru
-continuous, the constructed extension
S:F\to G
is a Riesz embedding.
∎

2
Free
ru
-complete vector lattice over a set

2.1
Free
ru
-complete vector lattice over A.

Although the next definition does not, as such,
appear in the literature, we think it belongs to folklore.

Definition 1
.

If
A
is a non-empty set, then a free UCVL over
A
is
a pair
(F,i)
, where
F
is a UCVL and
i:A\to F
is a map with the property that,
for any UCVL
E
and for any map
q:A\to E
, there exists a unique Riesz
homomorphism
T:F\to E
such that
q=T\circ i
.

We denote a free UCVL over
A
by
FUCVL(A)
. It is an initial object in
the category, whose objects are pairs
(E,q)
with a UCVL
E
and
q:A\to E
,
and whose morphisms
T:(E_{1},q_{1})\to(E_{2},q_{2})
are Riesz homomorphisms
from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
satisfying
q_{2}=T\circ q_{1}
.
Thus
FUCVL(A)
is defined similarly to
FVL(A)
in a proper subcategory.
Routine arguments show that if
FUCVL(A)
exists
it is unique up to a Riesz isomorphism and the
map
i:A\to FUCVL(A)
above is injective.
By
[
3
, Thm.2.4]
,
FVL(A)
is a vector
sublattice of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
generated by the evaluation functionals
\delta_{a}
on
\mathbb{R}^{A}
,

\delta_{a}(\xi)=\xi(a)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
be a non-empty set, and assume
FVL(A)
to be
a vector sublattice of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
. The intersection
F

of all
ru
-complete sublattices of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
containing

FVL(A)
together with the
embedding
a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}\delta_{a}
is a
FUCVL(A)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
is
ru
-complete then
F

is a
ru
-completion of
FVL(A)
by Proposition
1
.

Let
E
be an Archimedean UCVL with a map
q:A\to E
.
We need to show the existence of a Riesz homomorphism

T:F\to E
satisfying
q=T\circ i
. Using
ru
-completeness
of both
F
and
E
we shall do this by transfinite induction.
In view of Lemma
1
, we represent
F
as the union

\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in{\text{O}rd}}F_{\alpha}
of a non-decreasing family
of sublattices of the UCVL
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
as follows:

1)

F_{1}=FVL(A)
;

2)

If
\alpha=\beta+1
, then
F_{\alpha}
is a sublattice
of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
consisting of all
ru
-limits of sequences of
F_{\beta}

with regulators of
F_{\beta}
;

3)

If
\alpha
is a limit ordinal, then
F_{\alpha}

is a sublattice of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
consisting of all
ru
-limits
of sequences of
\bigcup\limits_{\beta<\alpha}F_{\beta}

with regulators of
\bigcup\limits_{\beta<\alpha}F_{\beta}

The existence and uniqueness of
T_{1}:F_{1}\to E
is given by definition of
FVL(A)
.
Let
\alpha
be an ordinal such that
T_{\beta}
is already constructed for all
\beta<\alpha
, and let
x\in F_{\alpha}
.
Note that these
T_{\beta}
are Riesz homomorphisms defined on
F_{\beta}
, and if

\beta_{1}\leqslant\beta_{2}
then
T_{\beta_{1}}
is the restriction of
T_{\beta_{2}}

to
F_{\beta_{1}}
.

If
\alpha=\beta+1
, for some sequence
x_{n}\in F_{\beta_{n}}
,
\beta_{n}<\alpha
,
we have

|x_{n}-x|\leqslant\frac{1}{2n}x_{1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
|x_{m}-x_{k}|\leqslant|x_{m}-x|+|x_{k}-x|\leqslant\frac{1}{n}x_{1}
for all
m,k\geqslant n
and hence

|T_{\beta_{m}}x_{m}-T_{\beta_{k}}x_{k}|\leqslant\frac{1}{n}T_{\beta}x_{1}
for all
m,k\geqslant n
.
Thus, the sequence
T_{\beta_{n}}x_{n}
is
ru
-Cauchy in
E
with the regulator
T_{\beta}x_{1}
.
Since
E
is
ru
-complete,
T_{\beta_{n}}x_{n}\ruc y\in E
with the regulator

T_{\beta}x_{1}
. Define
T_{\alpha}x:=y
.
We skip elementary arguments showing that
T_{\alpha}:F_{\alpha}\to E
is
a well defined Riesz homomorphism.

If
\alpha
is a limit ordinal, then
x_{\beta_{n}}\to x
with a regulator

u\in F_{\beta_{0}}
for
\beta_{n}<\alpha
, where
n=0,1,2\ldots
.
Without lost of generality, we may assume
|x_{\beta_{n}}-x|<\frac{1}{2n}u
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
|x_{\beta_{m}}-x_{\beta_{k}}|\leqslant|x_{\beta_{n}}-x|+|x_{\beta_{k}}-x|\leqslant\frac{1}{n}u

for all
m,k\geqslant n
, and hence
T_{\beta_{n}}x_{n}
is
ru
-Cauchy in
E
with
regulator
T_{\beta_{0}}u
and since
E
is
ru
-complete,

T_{\beta_{n}}x_{n}\ruc y
for some
y\in E
.
Now, we set
T_{\alpha}x:=y
.

If
x\in F
, then due to
F=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\text{\rm Ord}}F_{\alpha}
,
it holds
x\in F_{\alpha}
for some ordinal
\alpha
. We set
Tx:=T_{\alpha}x
.
It should be clear that
T:F\to E
is well defined.
∎

2.2
Restrictions and projections.

Following the tradition, for
B\subseteq A
, we identify

\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{B}}
with a sublattice of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}

by assigning
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{B}}
with
\hat{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}

such that
\hat{\xi}(f)=\xi(f|_{B})
(see
[
7
]
for more details).
By
[
7
, Proposition 3.5(2)]
, there exists a unique Riesz
homomorphism projection
P_{B}
of
FVL(A)
onto
FVL(B)
satisfying

P_{B}(\delta_{a})=\begin{cases}\delta_{a}&\text{if}\ \ a\in B\\
0&\text{if}\ \ a\in A\setminus B\,.\end{cases}

In particular,
FVL(A)=\bigcup\limits_{B\in{\cal P}_{fin}(A)}FVL(B)
,
where we denote by
{\cal P}_{fin}(A)
the set of all finite subsets
of
A

[
7
, Prop.3.7]
.

Denote by
H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
(by
H(\Delta_{A})
) the space of all positively
homogeneous real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{A}
(on
\Delta_{A}:=[-1,1]^{A}
)
which are continuous in the product topology of
\mathbb{R}^{A}
(of
\Delta_{A}
).
Clearly,
H(\Delta_{A})
is a closed in
\|.\|_{\infty}
-norm vector sublattice
of the Banach lattice of continuous functions
C(\Delta_{A})
on the
compact Hausdorff space
\Delta_{A}
, and hence
H(\Delta_{A})
is itself a Banach lattice.
It is well known that
FVL(A)
may be identified with a sublattice of
H(\mathbb{R}^{A})

and hence with a sublattice of
H(\Delta_{A})
in view of
[
7
, Lemma 5.1]
.
By
[
7
, Corollary 5.7]
,
FBL(A)
is embedded into
H(\Delta_{A})
as
a vector sublattice
J(FBL(A))
and even as an order ideal, as mentioned in
[
7
]
.
Furthermore,
J(FBL(A))=H(\Delta_{A})
iff
A
is finite and, in this case,
FBL(A)
is isomorphic to
H(\Delta_{A})
under the supremum norm
[
7
, Theorem 8.2]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
B
be a non-empty finite set. Then
FUCVL(B)
is Riesz isomorphic to
FBL(B)
,
H(\Delta_{B})
, and to
H(\mathbb{R}^{B})
.

Proof.

As
FVL(B)
and
FBL(B)
possess (the same) strong order unit (cf.
[
7
, Proposition 5.3 and Theorem 8.2]
) and
FBL(B)
is Riesz isomorphic
to the completion of
FVL(B)
under its strong unit norm,
FBL(B)
is the
ru
-closure of
FVL(B)
,
and hence
FUCVL(B)
is Riesz isomorphic to
FBL(B)
.
Thus,
FBL(B)
is the
ru
-completion of
FVL(B)
as
FBL(B)
is
ru
-compete.
The fact that
FBL(B)
and
H(\Delta_{B})
are Riesz isomorphic is the result of

[
7
, Proposition 5.3]
. As
B
is finite, the restriction map
R:H(\mathbb{R}^{B})\to H(\Delta_{B})
is
surjective and hence is a Riesz isomorphism (e.g. by
[
7
, Lemma 5.1]
).
∎

For the convenience, we include the proof of the following certainly well known proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
A
be a non-empty set, and let
x\in FBL(A)
.
Then there exists a sequence
x_{n}
in
FVL(A)

which
ru
-converges to
x
with a regulator
u\in FBL(A)
.

Proof.

Since
x\in FBL(A)
, then there exists a sequence
x_{n}
in
FVL(A)

satisfying
\|x_{n}-x\|_{F}\leqslant\frac{1}{n2^{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\|.\|_{F}
is
the extension to
FBL(A)
of a free lattice norm on
FVL(A)
.
Let
u=\|.\|_{F}\text{-}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|x_{n}-x|\in FBL(A)
.
Then
|x_{n}-x|\leqslant\frac{1}{n}u
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

and hence
x_{n}\ruc x
with the regulator
u
.
∎

Since any Banach lattice is UCL, it follows from Proposition
2

that
FBL(A)
contains an
ru
-completion of
FVL(A)
.
Below, we show that
FUCVL(A)
is a proper sublattice of
FBL(A)
unless
A
is finite.

Proposition 3
.

If a sequence
g_{n}\in H(\mathbb{R}^{A})

ru
-converges with a regulator

u\in H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
to some
g\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
then
g\in H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
.

Proof.

Without lost of generality, we may assume that
|g_{n}-g|\leqslant\frac{1}{n}u
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Clearly,
g
is positively homogeneous. It remains to show continuity of
g
.
Let
\eta_{\alpha}\to\eta
in the product topology of
\mathbb{R}^{A}
.
Then, for some
M
, there is
\alpha_{0}
with
u(\eta_{\alpha})\leqslant M

for all
\alpha\geqslant\alpha_{0}
. Hence

|g(\eta_{\alpha})-g(\eta)|\leqslant|g(\eta_{\alpha})-g_{n}(\eta_{\alpha})|+|g_{n}(\eta_{\alpha})-g_{n}(\eta)|+|g_{n}(\eta)-g(\eta)|\leqslant

|g_{n}(\eta_{\alpha})-g_{n}(\eta)|+\frac{2M}{n}\quad\quad(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad(\forall\alpha\geqslant\alpha_{0}).

Since
g_{n}
is continuous in the product topology of
\mathbb{R}^{A}
,
there exists
\alpha_{n}
with
|g_{n}(\eta_{\alpha})-g_{n}(\eta)|\leqslant\frac{M}{n}

for
\alpha\geqslant\alpha_{n}
.
It follows that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
|g(\eta_{\alpha})-g(\eta)|\leqslant\frac{3M}{n}
,
if
\alpha\geqslant\alpha_{0},\alpha_{n}
. Thus
g
is continuous and hence
g\in H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
is a
ru
-closed sublattice of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}
with regulators from
H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
.

Considering
FVL(A)
as a sublattice of
H(\Delta_{A})
, we obtain immediately the following result.

Corollary 2
.

If a sequence
g_{n}\in FVL(A)

ru
-converges with a regulator
u\in R(H(\mathbb{R}^{A}))
to some

g\in H(\Delta_{A})
then
g\in R(H(\mathbb{R}^{A}))
, where
R:H(\mathbb{R}^{A})\to H(\Delta_{A})

is the restriction map.

It is worth to compare Corollary
2
with
[
7
, Example 5.2]
.

2.3
Applications.

The following theorem is a refinement of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A
be a non-empty set. The intersection
F
of all
ru
-closed vector
sublattices of
H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
containing
FVL(A)
is a
FUCVL(A)
.

Proof.

By Corollary
1
,
H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
is a sublattice of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{A}}

closed under
ru
-convergence with regulators of
H(\mathbb{R}^{A})
.
By Theorem
1
,

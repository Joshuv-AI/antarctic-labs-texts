A Stable Jacobi polynomials based least squares regression estimator associated with
an ANOVA decomposition model.

Abstract




a
University of Carthage, National School of Engineering of Bizerte, Menzel Abderrahman 7035, Tunisia.

University of Carthage, Faculty of Sciences of Bizerte, Jarzouna 7021, Tunisia.

Abstract
—
In this work, we construct a stable and fairly fast estimator for solving non-parametric multidimensional regression problems. The proposed estimator is based on the use of multivariate Jacobi polynomials that generate a basis for a reduced size
of
d-
variate finite dimensional polynomial space. An ANOVA decomposition trick has been used for building this later polynomial space. Also, by using some results from the theory of positive definite random matrices, we show that the proposed estimator is stable under the condition that the i.i.d. random sampling points for the different covariates of the regression problem, follow a
d-
dimensional Beta distribution. Also, we provide the reader with an estimate for the
L^{2}-
risk error of the estimator. Moreover, a more precise estimate of the
quality of the approximation
is provided under the condition that the regression function belongs to some weighted Sobolev space. Finally,
the various theoretical results of this work
are supported by numerical simulations.

Keywords:
Non-parametric Regression, Jacobi polynomials, generalized polynomials chaos, ANOVA decomposition, least squares, stable regression estimator, risk error.

1
Introduction

In this work, we combine the popular technique of generalized polynomials chaos (gPC)

[
39
,
43
]
and a special family of
d-
variate Jacobi polynomials
in order
to solve a
d-
dimensional non-parametric regression problem. This last problem is one of the important as well as an active research topic from the machine learning area, see for example
[
39
,
40
]
. Note that a machine learning algorithm can be briefly described as an algorithm for the approximation of an unknown function
f
that maps in general a random vectors
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to an observed real valued variable
Y.
An estimator or an approximation
\widehat{f}
of
f
is constructed by the use a training data set
\{(X_{i},Y_{i}),\,1\leq i\leq n\}.
Note that unlike a parametric learning algorithm,
where
\widehat{f}
is given in terms of a set of fixed size of parameters, a non-parametric learning algorithm does not require any assumption about the function
f
or its estimator
\widehat{f}.
Usually, for a non-parametric (NP) model, the function
f
lies in an infinite dimensional functional space. Consequently, the NP models have the advantage to better fit a wide range of the true functions
f
. We should mention that the multidimensional NP regression problem is frequently encountered in a wide range of scientific fields. An NP learning algorithm for solving this problem aims to provide a convenient estimate
\widehat{f}
for the true regression function
f
associated with the regression problem,

Y_{i}=f(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,n\,.

(1)

Here, the
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are assumed to be i.i.d. random vectors and the

(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
are centered i.i.d. random variables with a finite variance

\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{1}^{2}]
and independent from the
X_{i}.
The goal of a learning algorithm is to minimize the empirical risk
\mathcal{R}_{emp}(f)
over a given class of functional space
\mathcal{H}.
That is to solve the minimization problem

\widehat{f}=\arg\min_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{R}_{emp}(f)=\arg\min_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L(f(X_{i}),Y_{i}),

(2)

where,
L(\cdot,\cdot)
is a non-negative loss function. Among the frequently used loss functions from the literature, we cite the Tikhonov regularized loss
[
35
]
and the weighted
\ell_{2}
loss function
[
33
]
, given respectively by

L_{\lambda}(F(X_{i}),Y_{i})=\big(f(X_{i})-Y_{i}\big)^{2}+\lambda\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad L_{\omega}(f(X_{i}),Y_{i})=\omega_{i}\big(f(X_{i})-Y_{i}\big)^{2},

for some convenient regularization parameter
\lambda>0
and finite weight sequence
(\omega_{i})_{i}.
In general, the least squares scheme is used to solve the minimization problem (
2
).

It is well known that despite the superiority of the NP model in terms of quality of approximation of the true functional
f
, it suffers from the curse of dimensionality for large or even moderate values of
d,
the number of covariates. The computational load by an NP algorithm grows fast with the dimension
d
and its convergence rate or its associated risk error rate slows drastically. For instance, it has been shown in
[
37
]
, see also
[
5
,
15
]
that if
f
is of class
C^{p}
with
p-
th derivative being Hölder continuous, then the optimal convergence rate of any NP least-squares estimator
\widehat{f}_{n},
solution of the minimization problem (
2
) is given by

{\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\|f-\widehat{f}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\Big]=O\Big(n^{-2p/(2p+d)}\Big).}
In order to reduce the computational load required by an algorithm for solving more general models with random inputs or models from uncertainty quantification (UQ) area, a popular technique of polynomial chaos expansion (PCE) is successfully used in the literature. This technique aims to approximate the output variable
Y
by using a projection over a reduced size of orthogonal polynomial basis. The PCE scheme has been first introduced by N. Wiener in his pioneer work
[
42
]
, for the Hermite polynomials and Gaussian random variables.
Recently, there is a growing interest in the study and the use for UQ applications of a generalized version of PCE, called generalized polynomial chaos (gPC). The gPC was first introduced by
[
43
]
and it aims to extend the PCE to various discrete and continuous probability distributions associated with the set of weight functions for the family of orthogonal polynomials of the Askey–scheme. For more details on PCE and gPC schemes and their associated UQ related applications, the reader is refereed to
[
16
,
17
,
21
,
22
,
26
,
40
,
44
]
. Nonetheless, the gPC based learning algorithm still has the limitation to be slow for moderate large values of the dimension
d
. To overcome this problem, various solutions have been considered in the literature. Among the popular adopted solutions, we cite the use of sparsity and optimal sampling techniques, see for example
[
7
,
9
,
14
,
18
,
22
,
24
,
29
,
34
]
, dimension reduction through a sensitivity analysis techniques,
[
2
,
8
]
, as well as the use of partial functional ANOVA decomposition technique, see for example
[
20
,
25
,
30
,
32
,
36
,
38
]
.
More precisely, a partial functional ANOVA decomposition consists
in the approximation of a real valued function
f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})
by a sum of functions with reduced number of variables of the form
f_{i}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})
where
1\leq m\leq d
and the
i_{j}\in[[1,d]].
Note that
for
the full ANOVA decomposition, that is
m=d,
the uniqueness of the ANOVA decomposition of a function
f\in L^{1}(J^{d}),\,J=[0,1]
has been shown in
[
36
]
. Also, among the popular reduced size multivariate polynomials spaces used by a gPC scheme, we cite the total degree space of degree
N,
given by

\mathcal{P}_{N}^{TD}=\mbox{Span}\{\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{i}}=x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{i_{d}},\quad\|\boldsymbol{i}\|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|i_{j}|\leq N\}

and the hyperbolic cross space of degree
N,
given by

\mathcal{P}_{q,N}^{HC}=\mbox{Span}\{\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{i}}=x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{i_{d}},\quad\|\boldsymbol{i}\|_{q}=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|i_{j}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq N\},

0<q<1.
For more details on these polynomials spaces, the reader is refereed to
[
7
]
.

In this work, we introduce a new
d-
variate polynomial space constructed from uni-variate Jacobi polynomials associated with parameters
\beta=\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and orthonormal over
I^{d}=[-1,1]^{d}.
More precisely, for two positive integers
N\geq 1
and

1\leq m\leq\min(d,N),
we let the ANOVA type Jacobi polynomials space

\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}=Span\left\{\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i})\ ;\ \boldsymbol{u}\subset[[1,d]]\ ;\ \ |\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m;\ \boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},\ \ ||\boldsymbol{k}||_{1}\leq N\right\}\,.

(3)

Here, the
\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}
are the orthonormal uni-variate Jacobi polynomials , associated with a parameter
\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{2}.
The dimension of

\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}
is given by
{\displaystyle M_{N,m,d}=\dim\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}.}
For the extreme case
m=\min(d,N),
the space
\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}
is reduced to the total degree space polynomials of degree
N,
given by
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{TD}.
One of the main results of this work is to prove the stability of our proposed least-squares estimator

\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
which is the solution of the minimization problem (
2
) with the functional space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}
and a loss function
L
given by the Euclidean distance of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Note that there is a growing interest in the study of the stability issue of estimators of functions with random inputs, see for example
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
27
,
28
]
. In the present work, we show that under the condition that the
X_{i}
follow a
d-
variate
\mbox{Beta}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)
distribution with support
I^{d},
the positive definite
n\times n
random matrix involved in the construction of our proposed least-squares based estimator
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
is with high probability well conditioned in the
2-
norm. Moreover, we give an estimate for the
L^{2}-
risk error of a truncated version of the
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
which we denote by
\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}.
More precisely, if
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
denotes the weighted
L^{2}-
norm associated with the weight
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\alpha}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-x_{i}^{2})^{\alpha},

then we give an estimate of the
L^{2}-
risk error
\mathbb{E}\Big[\|f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|^{2}_{\alpha}\Big].
This later is given in terms of the classical bias-variance decomposition. The variance term decays at a rate of
O\big(\frac{M_{N,m,d}}{n}\big).
Here,
M_{N,m,d}
is the dimension of our proposed polynomial space
\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}.
The bias term f the
L^{2}-
risk involves the quantity
\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\|_{\alpha},
where
\Pi_{N,m}
denotes the orthogonal projection over
\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}.
An estimate of this last quantity is given under the hypothesis that the true regression function lies in a weighted Sobolev space with given Sobolev smoothness property.

This work is organized as follows. In section 2, we give some mathematical preliminaries on Matrix Chernoff eigenvalues bounds, the Gershgorin circle theorem for bounding the spectrum of a square matrix, as well as some properties of the Jacobi polynomials. In particular, we give some bounds of these polynomials that will be used for proving different results of this work. In section 3, we describe the new adopted and reduced size ANOVA type multivariate Jacobi polynomial space
\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}.
Moreover, we provide the reader with an estimate of the dimension of this later. Section 4 of this work is devoted to the proof of the stability of the proposed least-squares and gPC based NP regression estimator
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}.
In section 5, we give an estimate for the weighted
L^{2}-
risk error of a truncation version of the estimator
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}.
Moreover, in section 6, we give an estimate for the bias term of the previous weighted
L^{2}-
risk error, when the true regression function belongs to some weighted Sobolev space. Finally, in section 7, we give some numerical simulations that illustrate the different results of this work.

For

The family

\left\{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)}\right\}\cup\left\{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\ \boldsymbol{u}\subset\mathcal{D},\ \ \boldsymbol{k}\in F_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\}

is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(I^{d},\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

Since

\mbox{Span}\left\{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)}\right\}\cup\left\{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\ \boldsymbol{u}\subset\mathcal{D},\ \ \boldsymbol{k}\in F_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\}=\mbox{Span}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{d}P^{(\alpha)}_{k_{i}},\,k_{i}\geq 0\right\}

and since this later is dense in
L^{2}(I^{d},\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha}),
then it suffices to
establish the orthornormality of the vectors

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)}
and
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}
, we consider the following three cases.

1.

Computation of
<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}
.
We first assume that
|\boldsymbol{u}|\geq 1
, then we have

\displaystyle<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{i=1}^{d}w_{\alpha}(x_{i})dx_{1}\ldots dx_{d}

(15)

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\left(\widetilde{P}_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{2d-2|\boldsymbol{u}|}\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i})\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}w_{\alpha}(x_{i})dx_{1}\ldots dx_{d}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i})\right)^{2}w_{\alpha}(x_{i})dx_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{1\leq j\leq d;j\notin\boldsymbol{u}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\widetilde{P}_{0}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})\right)^{2}w_{\alpha}(x_{j})dx_{j}\right)=1\,.

In a similar manner, we have
<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}=1
.

2.

Computation of
<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,l}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}
in the case where
|\boldsymbol{u}|\geq 1
and
\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{l}

As
\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{l}
then
\exists\ i_{0}\in\boldsymbol{u}
such that
\boldsymbol{k}_{i_{0}}\neq\boldsymbol{l}_{i_{0}}

\displaystyle<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,l}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,l}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}

(16)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{\alpha}}\right)^{d-|\boldsymbol{u}|}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{P}_{k_{i_{0}}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i_{0}})\widetilde{P}_{l_{i_{0}}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i_{0}})w_{\alpha}(x_{i_{0}})dx_{i_{0}}\right)\prod_{j\neq i_{0};j\in\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{P}_{k_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})\widetilde{P}_{l_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})w_{\alpha}(x_{j})dx_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=0

3.

Computation of
<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v,l}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}
. Let us consider the case where
\boldsymbol{u}\neq\boldsymbol{v}
,
|\boldsymbol{u}|\geq 1
and
|\boldsymbol{v}|\geq 1

As
\boldsymbol{u}\neq\boldsymbol{v}
then : (1)
\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{l}
and
(2)
\exists\ i_{0}\in\boldsymbol{u}\backslash\boldsymbol{v}
(or
\boldsymbol{v}\backslash v
). Without loss of generality, we will suppose that

.

\displaystyle<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v,l}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}

(17)

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v,l}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\left(\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i})\right)\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{d-|\boldsymbol{u}|}{2}}\left(\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{v}}\widetilde{P}_{l_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{d-|\boldsymbol{v}|}{2}}\right)\prod_{s=1}^{d}w_{\alpha}(x_{s})dx_{s}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{P}_{k_{i_{0}}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i_{0}})\frac{w_{\alpha}(x_{i_{0}})}{\sqrt{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}}dx_{i_{0}}\right)\int_{[-1,1]^{d-1}}\frac{\left(\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}\backslash i_{0};j\in\boldsymbol{v}}\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i})\widetilde{P}_{l_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})\right)}{\sqrt{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}^{2d-|\boldsymbol{u}|-|\boldsymbol{v}|-1}}\prod_{s\in[[1,d]]\backslash i_{0}}w_{\alpha}(x_{s})dx_{s}=0

Similarly to (

), we have
in the case where
|\boldsymbol{v}|=0

<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}=0
.

∎

Next, we consider the following reduced size polynomial space,
defined for
m\in[[1,N]]
on which we will construct our estimator.

\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}=Span\left\{\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\widetilde{P_{k_{i}}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i})\ ;\ \boldsymbol{u}\subset\mathcal{D}\ ;\ \ |\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m;\ \boldsymbol{k}\in F_{\boldsymbol{u}},\ \ ||\boldsymbol{k}||_{1}\leq N\right\}\,.

(18)

We will call this space ANOVA type polynomial space.
The dimension of the space
\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}
as well as an estimate of its upper bound are given by the following proposition.

Proposition 2
.

For any positive integers
1\leq m\leq\min(d,N),
we have

M_{N,m,d}=\dim\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}.

(19)

Moreover, we have

M_{N,m,d}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\pi}{e}\Big(1+\frac{m}{\min(d,N)-m+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{2\big(\max(d,N)-m+\frac{1}{2}\big)}\cdot\left(\frac{\max(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}}{m+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2m}&\mbox{ if }m\leq\frac{1}{2}\min(d,N),\\
&\\
2^{\min(d,N)}\Big(1+\frac{m}{\max(d,N)-m}\Big)^{\max(d,N)-m}\Big(\frac{\max(d,N)}{m}\Big)^{m}&\mbox{ if }\frac{1}{2}\min(d,N)\leq m<\min(d,N)\end{array}\right.

(20)

and, if
m=\min(d,N)
,

M_{N,m,d}={N+d\choose m}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{(2d+2N+1)e}}\Big(1+\frac{N}{d+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{d+\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1+\frac{d}{N+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{N+\frac{1}{2}}

(21)

Proof.

We first check (
19
).
For this purpose, we first consider the two special cases
m=2
and
m=3,
. Then, we check the previous identity for any
m\leq\min(d,N).
For
m=2,
the space
\mathcal{P}_{N,2,d}
can be rewritten as

\mathcal{P}_{N,2,d}=H_{1}^{N}\oplus H_{2}^{N}\,,

with

H_{1}^{N}=Span\{\widetilde{P}_{l}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i}),\ 0\leq l\leq N,\ \ 1\leq i\leq d\}\,

and

H_{2}^{N}=Span\{\widetilde{P}_{k_{1}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{i})\widetilde{P}_{k_{2}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j}),\ (k_{1},k_{2})\in[[1,N-1]]^{2},\ 2\leq k_{1}+k_{2}\leq N,\ \ 1\leq i<j\leq d\}\,.

It is not difficult to check that the dimensions of
H_{1}^{N}

and
H_{2}^{N}
are given by

\dim H_{1}^{N}=1+Nd=\sum_{k=0}^{1}{d\choose k}{N\choose k},\qquad\dim H_{2}^{N}=\frac{d(d-1)N(N-1)}{4}={d\choose 2}{N\choose 2}.

Hence,

\dim\mathcal{P}_{N,2,d}=M_{N,2,d}=\sum_{k=0}^{2}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}.

In a similar manner; for
m=3,
we have
\mathcal{P}_{N,3,d}=\mathcal{P}_{N,2,d}\oplus H_{3}^{N}\,,
with

H_{3}^{N}=Span\Big\{\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j_{i}}),\ (k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\in[[1,N-2]]^{3},\ 3\leq k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}\leq N,\ \ 1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\leq d\Big\}\,.

Note that there exist
{d\choose 3}
different
3-
tuples

(x_{j_{1}},x_{j_{2}},x_{j_{3}})
in
H_{3}^{N}.
Moreover, for each
such a tuple, there correspond
{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}k(k-1)={N\choose 3}}
different polynomials products

{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}},\,3\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}k_{i}\leq N.}
Consequently, we have

{\displaystyle\dim H_{3}^{N}={d\choose 3}{N\choose 3}}
and

\dim\mathcal{P}_{N,3,d}=\dim\mathcal{P}_{N,2,d}+\dim H_{3}^{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{3}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}.

Continuing in this manner, one gets the recurrence formula

\dim\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}=\dim\mathcal{P}_{N,m-1,d}+\dim H_{m}^{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}+{d\choose m}{N\choose m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}.

Next, to prove (
20
), we proceed as follows.
By using (
8
), one gets for any integers
n\geq k\geq 0

\displaystyle{n\choose k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(n-k+1)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2e^{2}}}\frac{\left(\frac{n+\frac{1}{2}}{e}\right)^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}{\left(\frac{k+\frac{1}{2}}{e}\right)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{n-k+\frac{1}{2}}{e}\right)^{n-k+\frac{1}{2}}}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2e}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1+\frac{k}{n-k+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{n-k+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{n+\frac{1}{2}}{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{k}\,.

Since for
1\leq m\leq\frac{1}{2}\min(d,N),
the finite sequences
\Big\{{d\choose i}\Big\}_{0\leq i\leq m},\Big\{{N\choose i}\Big\}_{0\leq i\leq m}
are increasing, then by using (
22
), one gets

\displaystyle M_{N,m,d}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(m+1){d\choose m}{N\choose m}=(m+1)\,\frac{\Gamma(d+1)\cdot\Gamma(N+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma\left(d-m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma\left(N-m+\frac{1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{e}\Big(1+\frac{m}{\min(d,N)-m+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{2\big(\max(d,N)-m+\frac{1}{2}\big)}\cdot\left(\frac{\max(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}}{m+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2m}\,.

In a similar manner, for
\frac{1}{2}\min(d,N)\leq m<\min(d,N),
we have

\displaystyle M_{N,m,d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\min(d,N)}{\min(d,N)\choose k}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\max(d,N)\choose k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\min(d,N)}\frac{\max(d,N)^{\max(d,N)}}{m^{m}((\max(d,N)-m)^{\max(d,N)-m}}.

This last inequality is a consequence of the inequality,

\sum_{k=0}^{m}{n\choose k}\leq\frac{n^{n}}{m^{m}(n-m)^{n-m}},\qquad\forall\,\,0\leq m<n.

The previous inequality is a direct consequence of the following upper bound for the binomial coefficient, see [
[
13
]
, p.353]

{n\choose k}\leq{\displaystyle 2^{nH\big(k/nbig)}},

where for
0<p<1,

H(p)
is the binary entropy function, given by

{\displaystyle H(p)=-p\log_{2}(p)-(1-p)\log_{2}(1-p).}

For the last case where
m=\min(d,N)
, using
Chu-Vandermonde’s identity, see for example
[
31
]
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{m}{d\choose k}{N\choose k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(d,N)}{\min(d,N)\choose k}{\max(d,N)\choose k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(d,N)}{\min(d,N)\choose\min(d,N)-k}{\max(d,N)\choose k}={N+d\choose m}.

Using

, we get

{N+d\choose m}=\frac{\Gamma(N+d+1)}{\Gamma(d+1)\Gamma(N+1)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2e}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N+d+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{N+d+\frac{1}{2}}{d+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{d+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{N+d+\frac{1}{2}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{N+\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(23)

Remark 1
.

For the particular case
m=d
, the space

\mathcal{P}_{N,d,d}
is the usual total degree polynomial of order

From Proposition

4
Stability of the NP estimator

In this section, we first describe our proposed Least squares NP regression estimator based on the use of the orthonormal
d-
variate polynomials
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}.
Then, we prove the stability of the proposed estimator under the condition that the random sampling covariates follow a
d-
dimensional Beta distribution.

Recall that the regression system at hand is given by

Y_{i}=f(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,n\,.

(24)

Here,
f
is the regression function to be approximated, the
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the covariates which we assume to be i.i.d. random vectors
and the

(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
are centered i.i.d. random variables with a finite variance

\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{1}^{2}]
. Also we assume that the
X_{i}
are independent from the
\varepsilon_{j}.
In the sequel, we assume that for a real
\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{2},
the set
\{X_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,n\}
is a random sampling set with the
X_{i}
following a
d-
variates
\mbox{Beta}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)-
distribution.
For two positive integers
m\leq d
and
N
,
we build an estimator
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
of the regression function
f
which is obtained using the approximation of
f
by its projection over
\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}
. For
\boldsymbol{u}\subset\mathcal{D}
with
|u|\geq 1
and
\boldsymbol{k}\in F_{\boldsymbol{u}}
, let
\Phi_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{(\alpha)}:=\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u,k}}^{(\alpha)}
.
Let
K_{N,0}:=\left\{(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}
and
for
p\in[[1,m]]
, let
K_{N,p}
be the subsets defined as follows

K_{N,p}:=\left\{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}\in F_{\boldsymbol{u}}:|\boldsymbol{u}|=p\ ;\ ||k_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{1}\leq N\right\}\,\qquad K_{N,m,d}:=\bigcup_{p=0}^{m}K_{N,p}.

Let
g:K_{N,m,d}\mapsto[[1,M_{N,m,d}]]
be a correspondence (order) defined on the indexes
(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u})
of our basis :
g(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u})\in[[1,M_{N,m,d}]]
. Then, we can introduce the notation

\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}:=\Phi_{g^{-1}(j)}^{(\alpha)},\ \mbox{with}\ i\in[[1,M_{N,m,d}]]\,.

Using these definitions, our NP regression estimator is given by

\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\sum_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}\in K_{N,m,d}}\widehat{C}_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(f)\Phi_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{N,m,d}}\widehat{C}_{j}(f)\Psi_{j}(x),\quad x\in I^{d}.

(25)

Assuming that
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
satisfies
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(X_{i})=Y_{i}

and multiplying the previous equation by
\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}
, we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{M_{N,m,d}}\widehat{C}_{j}(f)\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Psi_{j}(X_{i})=\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}Y_{i}.

(26)

This system can be rewritten as a system of linear equations where the unknown is the expansion coefficients vector
\widehat{C}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\left(\widehat{C}_{j}\right)_{1\leq j\leq M_{N,m,d}}^{T}
:

F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}.\widehat{C}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}Y.

Consider the positive definite random matrix (to be checked later on),

G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\left(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{T}\cdot F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}.

(27)

Then, we have

\widehat{C}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{T}.\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}Y,

(28)

with

F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(i,j)=\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}.\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{i}).

(29)

Next, we show that our proposed NP regression estimator is stable in the sense that the random matrix
G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
is well conditioned with respect to the
2-
norm. As the random matrix

G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
is positive definite, its
condition number denoted by
\kappa_{2}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)
is given by

\kappa_{2}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)=\frac{\lambda_{\max}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)}{\lambda_{\min}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)}.

(30)

Recall that the i.i.d. random samples

(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
follow the
d-
variate Beta distribution on
I^{d}

with the density function
\boldsymbol{g}_{\alpha}
given by

\boldsymbol{g}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{d}}\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha}(x){\mathds{1}}_{I^{d}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{x}){\mathds{1}}_{I^{d}}(\boldsymbol{x})\,.

(31)

Before stating the theorem about the condition number upper bound (Theorem
1
), we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4
.

For
\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a positive integer

D(N,\alpha):=\begin{cases}\pi^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}e^{(\alpha+\frac{3}{4})}\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \alpha>-\frac{1}{2}\\
2\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \alpha=-\frac{1}{2}\,.\end{cases}

(32)

Then, we have

\left\|\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)}\right\|_{\infty}=\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\,.

(33)

Moreover, for
1\leq|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m
and
||\boldsymbol{k}_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{1}\leq N
, we have

\left\|\Phi_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{(\alpha)}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot[D(N,\alpha)]^{m}\,.

(34)

Proof.

Equality (
33
)
is immediate since
\Phi_{0}^{(\alpha)}=\left[\widetilde{P}_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right]^{d}=\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}
. Also from the definition of
\Phi_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{(\alpha)}
, we have

\|\Phi_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\widetilde{P}_{0}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}^{d-|\boldsymbol{u}|}\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\|\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}=\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d-|\boldsymbol{u}|}{2}}\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\|\widetilde{P}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}\,.

Next, for the case where
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
and by using
Proposition
1

and Lemma
2
, one gets :

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d-|\boldsymbol{u}|}{2}}\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\left[\frac{\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}N^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}e^{\alpha+\frac{3}{4}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right]

(35)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}|}{2}}\left[\frac{\pi^{\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}|}{4}}N^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})|\boldsymbol{u}|}e^{(\alpha+\frac{3}{4})|\boldsymbol{u}|}}{\sqrt{2}^{|\boldsymbol{u}|}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\left[\left(\pi^{3}\right)^{\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}|}{4}}N^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})|\boldsymbol{u}|}e^{(\alpha+\frac{3}{4})|\boldsymbol{u}|}\right]\,.

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\left[\pi^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}e^{(\alpha+\frac{3}{4})}\right]^{m}\,.

Finally, for the case
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
and by
using again Proposition
1

and Lemma
2
, one gets

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{k_{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\left(h_{0}^{(-\frac{1}{2})}\right)^{\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}|}{2}}\prod_{i\in\boldsymbol{u}}\left[\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right]

(36)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\pi^{\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}|}{2}}\left[\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right]^{|\boldsymbol{u}|}\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}2^{m}\,.

∎

In the following theorem, we show that with high probability, the

norm condition number of the positive definite random matrix

is bounded by a convenient constant depending on a parameter

The results of this theorem can be considered as a generalization of a similar result given in
[

]
for tensor product multivariate Jacobi polynomials basis.

Theorem 1
.

Under the previous notation and hypotheses on
N,m,\alpha,
let
0<\delta<1
and let
G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
be the random matrix given by (

). Then, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\kappa_{2}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)\leq\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\right)\geq 1-2M_{N,m,d}.\exp{\left(-\delta^{2}\frac{n}{3D^{2m}(N,\alpha)M_{N,m,d}}\right)}.

(37)

Here, the quantity
D(N,\alpha)
is as defined by Lemma
4
and
M_{N,m,d}
is as given by Proposition 2.

Proof.

From (
29
), we have
F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}F(p,i)=\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}}}{n^{1/2}}.\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})
and
G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\left(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{T}\cdot F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\,,

G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(i,j)=\sum_{p=1}^{n}\frac{\left[2^{2\alpha+1}\mbox{B}(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\right]^{d}}{n}.\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})=\sum_{p=1}^{n}\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}.\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\,.

Consequently, one gets

\mathbb{E}[G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(i,j)]=\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\right]\,.

Now to compute
\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\right]
, we let
i=g(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u})
and
j=g(\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{v}),
then by using Lemma
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Phi_{\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\right]

(38)

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\Phi_{\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\frac{\prod_{s=1}^{d}w_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})d\boldsymbol{x}_{s}}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}<\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}^{(\alpha)},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}\delta_{(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}),(\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{v})}=\frac{1}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}\delta_{i,j}\,.

Hence, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\right]=\frac{1}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}\delta_{i,j}
. Therefore
\mathbb{E}\big[G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\big]

is the identity matrix of dimension
M_{N,m,d}
.
Also, note that

G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\sum_{p=1}^{n}H_{p}\,,

where
H_{p}(i,j)=\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}.\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})
.
We show that, for all
p\in[[1,n]]
, all eigenvalues of
H_{p}
are non-negative. Moreover, we give an estimate for
an upper bound for the eigenvalues of the
H_{p}
.
The matrix
H_{p}
can be written as
H_{p}=A^{T}_{p}.A_{p}
where

A_{p}:=\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\sqrt{n}}.\left(\Psi_{1}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p}),\ldots,\Psi_{M_{N,m,d}}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\right)
. The relation
H_{p}=A^{T}_{p}.A_{p}

implies that the eigenvalues of all the matrices
H_{p}
are non-negative. Applying Gershgorin Theorem to
H_{p}
, one gets

\lambda_{\max}(H_{p})\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq M_{n,m,d}}\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}\left(\big|\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\big|^{2}+\sum_{j\neq i}\big|\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{p})\big|\right).

Therefore

{\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}(H_{p})\leq\frac{D(N,\alpha)^{2m}}{n}\cdot M_{N,m,d}}
for all
l\in[[1,n]]
.
Next, by applying the matrix Chernoff Theorem to the matrix
G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
as a sum of positive semi definite random matrices
G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
and satisfying
0\preceq H_{p}\preceq L\cdot I_{M_{N,m,d}},

L:=\frac{\left(D(N,\alpha)\right)^{2m}\cdot M_{N,m,d}}{n}
and as
\mu_{{\min}}=\lambda_{{\min}}\mathbb{E}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)=1
,

\mu_{{\max}}=\lambda_{{\max}}\mathbb{E}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)=1
, we get :

\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{{\min}}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)\geq 1-\delta\right)\geq 1-M_{N,m,d}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}n}{2[D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}\cdot M_{N,m,d}}\right)

(39)

and

\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{{\max}}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)\leq 1+\delta\right)\geq 1-M_{N,m,d}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}n}{3[D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}\cdot M_{N,m,d}}\right)

(40)

for
\delta\in(0,1)
. Finally, consider the events

A_{1}:=\left(\kappa_{2}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)\leq\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\right)
,
A_{2}=\left(\lambda_{min}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)\geq 1-\delta\right)
and

A_{3}:=\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)\leq 1+\delta\right)
.
Using the fact that
\mathbb{P}(A_{1}^{c})\leq\mathbb{P}(A_{2}^{c})+\mathbb{P}(A_{3}^{c})
, we get

\mathbb{P}(A_{1})\geq 1-2M_{n,m,d}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}n}{3[D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}.M_{N,m,d}}\right)\,.

∎

) and (

), one concludes that a convenient choice for the value of the parameter

2-
norm condition number of the random matrix

5
L^{2}-
Risk error of the NP regression estimator

In this section, we give an estimate for the
L^{2}-
risk error of our proposed NP regression estimator. For this purpose and it is done in
[
12
]
, we assume that there exists a constant
K_{f}
such that

|f(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq K_{f},\ \forall\ \boldsymbol{x}\ \in\ I^{d}\,.

(41)

We let
\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
denote the truncated version of the estimator
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
, given by

\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\mbox{Sign}\left(\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)\min\left\{K_{f},|\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})|\right\}\,.

(42)

Theorem 2
.

Let
f
be a function satisfying the hypothesis (
41
) and let

\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
be the truncated version of the estimator
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
, given by (
42
). For

\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\delta<1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\|f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}\right]\leq 4K_{f}^{2}\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}M_{N,m,d}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}n}{2[D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}\cdot M_{N,m,d}}\right)\\
+\frac{M_{N,m,d}}{n(1-\delta)^{2}}\bigg([D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}\bigg)+\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}\,,

(43)

where the quantity
D(N,\alpha)
is as defined by Lemma
4
.

Proof.

As it is done in
[
12
]
, We write
\Omega
as
\Omega_{+}\cup\Omega_{-},
where

\Omega_{-}:=\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}):\lambda_{{\min}}(G)<1-\delta\}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \Omega_{+}:=\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}):\lambda_{{\min}}(G)\geq 1-\delta\}\,.

Then

\mathbb{E}\left[||f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}||_{\alpha}^{2}\right]=\int_{\Omega_{-}}||f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}||_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}+\int_{\Omega_{+}}||f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}||_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}\,,

where
d\rho_{n}
is the probability measure on
(I^{d})^{n}
given by the tensor product

d\rho_{n}(\boldsymbol{u_{1}},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u_{n}})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}dg_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{u_{k}})\,,

with
g_{\alpha}
given in (
31
).
To get an upper bound for
\int_{\Omega_{-}}\|f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}
, we proceed as follows.
From (
39
), we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{{\min}}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)<1-\delta\right)\leq M_{N,m,d}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}n}{2[D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}\cdot M_{N,m,d}}\right)\,.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{-}}\|f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle\leq 4K_{f}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{-}}d\rho_{n}=4K_{f}^{2}\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{{\min}}\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)<1-\delta\right)

(44)

\displaystyle\leq 4K_{f}^{2}\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}M_{N,m,d}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}n}{2[D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}\cdot M_{N,m,d}}\right)\,.

For an estimate of an Upper bound of
\int_{\Omega_{+}}||f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}||_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}
, we use
the fact that

|f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}|
is upper bounded by
|f-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}|
and the fact that
(f-\Pi_{N,m}f)
and
(\Pi_{N,m}f-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)})

are orthogonal. Hence, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{+}}\|f-\widehat{F}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{+}}\|f-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}

(45)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{+}}\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\|\Pi_{N,m}f-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}||_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}\,.

On the other hand, let
C_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}
the vector containing the coefficients of
\Pi_{N,m}f
on the basis

\{\Psi_{1}^{(\alpha)},\ldots,\Psi_{M_{N,m,d}}^{(\alpha)}\}
,i.e.,

\Pi_{N,m}f(X_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{N,m,d}}C_{j}\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)}(X_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. This leads to

C_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\left(G_{n,N,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{T}.\sqrt{\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}}\left(\Pi_{N,m}(f)(X_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}\,.

The identity (
28
) used for the computation of
\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}

can be rewritten as:

\widehat{C}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{T}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}}\left(f(X_{i})+\epsilon_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}.

Combining the previous two equations, we get

\widehat{C}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}-C_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}=\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{-1}(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)})^{T}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}}\cdot\left(g(X_{i})+\epsilon_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}\,.

where
g(X_{i}):=\left(f-\Pi_{N,m}(f)\right)(X_{i})
.
From Parseval’s equality, we have on
\Omega_{+}

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{N,m}(f)-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=||\widehat{C}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}-C_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}||_{l_{2}}^{2}

(46)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n}\left\|\left(G_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\left\|\left(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{T}\cdot\left(g(X_{i})+\epsilon_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}\right\|_{l_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}}{n(1-\delta)^{2}}\left\|(F_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)})^{T}\cdot\left(g(X_{i})+\epsilon_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}\right\|_{l_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{2d}}{n^{2}(1-\delta)^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{N,m,d}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\left(\Psi_{j}(X_{k})(g(X_{k})+\epsilon_{k})\right)\cdot\left(\Psi_{j}(X_{l})(g(X_{l})+\epsilon_{l})\right)\,.

Considering the expectations of both sides of the previous inequality and taking into account that

<\Psi_{j}^{(\alpha)},g>_{\alpha}=0
and that
the
\varepsilon_{k}
are independent from the
X_{k}
with
\mathbb{E}(\varepsilon_{k})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(\epsilon_{k}^{2})=\sigma^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Pi_{N,m}(f)-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{2d}}{n^{2}(1-\delta)^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{N,m,d}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(E\left[\Psi_{j}^{2}(X_{k})g^{2}(X_{k})\right]+\sigma^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi_{j}^{2}(X_{k})\right]\right)

(47)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{2d}}{n^{2}(1-\delta)^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{N,m,d}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi_{j}^{2}(X_{k})g^{2}(X_{k})\right]+\frac{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}M_{N,m,d}\cdot\sigma^{2}}{n(1-\delta)^{2}}.

Using Lemma
4
, we have :

\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi_{j}^{2}(X_{k})g^{2}(X_{k})\right]\leq\|\Psi_{j}||_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[g^{2}(X_{k})\right]\leq\left(D(N,\alpha)\right)^{2m}\cdot\frac{\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{2d}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{+}}\|\Pi_{N,m}(f)-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Pi_{N,m}(f)-\widehat{f}_{N,n,m}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}\right]

(48)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{N,m,d}}{n(1-\delta)^{2}}\bigg([D(N,\alpha)]^{2m}\cdot\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{d}\bigg)\,.

∎

6
Quality of the estimation in a weighted Sobolev space

In this paragraph, we give a precise rate of convergence of our estimator in the case where the
d-
variate regression functions belongs to a weighted Sobolev space. More precisely, we give an estimate for the term
\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}
in the
L^{2}-
risk error given by Theorem 2. For this purpose, we recall some definitions and results mainly borrowed from
[
30
]
.
Let

\mathbb{T}
be a unit torus and let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).
The Fourier series expansion of
f
is given by

f(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f)e^{2i\pi\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}},\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad(\mbox{or }\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(49)

We have the following partition of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
:

\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{D}}F_{\boldsymbol{u}}
.
This gives the analysis of variance (ANOVA) decomposition of
f
:

f(x)=f_{\emptyset}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}f_{i}(x_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{d}f_{i,j}(x_{i},x_{j})+\ldots+f_{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{D}}f_{\boldsymbol{u}}(x_{\boldsymbol{u}})\,,

where the functions
f_{\boldsymbol{u}}
are called ANOVA terms, see
[
30
]
.
Let
\boldsymbol{U}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D})
. The truncated ANOVA decomposition over
\boldsymbol{U}
is defined as:

T_{\boldsymbol{U}}(f):=\sum_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{U}}f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\,.

In particular, for
1\leq s\leq d
, we define

T_{s}(f):=\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq s}f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\,.

Definition 1
.

Let
s>0
. Let
w^{(s)}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mapsto[1,\infty)
be the weight function defined by

w^{(s)}(\boldsymbol{p}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+|\boldsymbol{p}_{j}|\right)^{s}\,\,\ ,\ \forall\ \boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

We associate to this weight function the Sobolev space

H^{s}(I^{d}):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(I^{d});\,\|f\|_{H^{s}(I^{d})}:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(w^{(s)}(\boldsymbol{p}))^{2}\cdot|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f)|^{2}<+\infty\right\}\,,

(50)

and the weighted Wiener algebra

\mathcal{A}^{s}(I^{d}):=\left\{f\in L^{1}(I^{d});\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}(I^{d})}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w^{(s)}(\boldsymbol{p})\cdot|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f)|<+\infty\right\}\,.

The following lemma provides us with an estimate for the decay rate of the expansion series expansion coefficients of the
f_{\boldsymbol{u}}
when
f\in H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})
and with respect to the basis functions
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}.

Lemma 5
.

Let
0<\xi<\frac{1}{2}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\boldsymbol{k}\in F_{\boldsymbol{u}}
such that
||\boldsymbol{k}||_{1}\geq N+1
with

\frac{\frac{N+1}{|\boldsymbol{u}|}}{\ln\left(\frac{N+1}{|\boldsymbol{u}|}\right)}\geq-\ln\left(\xi\right)\left(1+s+\frac{d}{2}\right)\,.

For any

f\in H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})

|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|=|<f_{\boldsymbol{u}},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}|\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}^{-s-\frac{d}{2}}\left(||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{2}+||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}\right)\,.

(51)

Proof.

We have:

f_{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)e^{2i\pi\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}},\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in I^{d}.

(52)

For
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|\boldsymbol{v}|}
, we have :

C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})=<f_{\boldsymbol{u}},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})<e^{2i\pi\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})>_{\alpha}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{p}}\,,

where
d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{p}}=<e^{2i\pi\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})>_{\alpha}
.
Note that if
\boldsymbol{v}\neq\boldsymbol{u}
, then

C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)=<f_{\boldsymbol{u}},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}>_{\alpha}=0\,.

Consequently, we need only to estimate

C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)
.
In the special case
|\boldsymbol{u}|=0
, it is easy to show that
|d_{p}|:=|<e^{2i\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{x}},\Phi_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{x})>_{\alpha}|\leq 1
. Next, for
|\boldsymbol{u}|>0
, we have

\displaystyle\big|d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|<e^{2i\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{x}},\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}}\right)^{d-|\boldsymbol{u}|}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{u}}\widetilde{P}_{k_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})>_{\alpha}\Big|

(53)

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\prod_{l=1}^{d}e^{2i\pi p_{l}x_{l}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}}\right)^{d-|\boldsymbol{u}|}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{u}}\widetilde{P}_{k_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})\boldsymbol{w}(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}\Big|

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{l\in\mathcal{D}\backslash\boldsymbol{u}}\Bigg|\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{e^{2i\pi p_{l}x_{l}}}{\sqrt{h_{0}^{(\alpha)}}}w_{\alpha}(x_{l})dx_{l}\Bigg|\right]\times\left[\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{u}}\Bigg|\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2i\pi p_{j}x_{j}}\widetilde{P}_{k_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})w_{\alpha}(x_{j})dx_{j}\Bigg|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{u}}\Bigg|\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2i\pi p_{j}y}\widetilde{P}_{k_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(y)w_{\alpha}(y)dy\Bigg|=\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{u}}\Bigg|d_{p_{j},k_{j}}\Bigg|\,,

where, for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

d_{l,r}:=\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2i\pi ly}\widetilde{P}_{r}^{(\alpha)}(y)w_{\alpha}(y)dy
. Using (
6
), we get

d_{l,r}=i^{r}\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{2r+2\alpha+1}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(r+2\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(r+1)}}\frac{J_{r+\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi l)}{(2\pi l)^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}\,,

where
J_{a}
is the Bessel function of the first kind and order
a>-1
.
Note that since
d_{l,r}=(-1)^{r}d_{-l,r}
then
|d_{l,r}|=|d_{-l,r}|

which allow us to consider only the case where
l\geq 0
. Now, we apply the inequality (
7
) to the Bessel function
J_{a}

of the previous equation. We obtain :

|d_{l,r}|\leq\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{2r+2\alpha+1}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(r+2\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(r+1)}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(r+\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}\frac{(\pi|l|)^{r}}{2^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}\,.

Using (
8
), we get

\displaystyle|d_{l,r}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{r}(e\pi|l|)^{r}\frac{\pi^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{4}}}{2^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}\sqrt{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}\frac{(r+2\alpha+\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{r}{2}+\alpha+\frac{1}{4}}}{(r+\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}\cdot(r+\alpha+1)^{r+\alpha+1}}

(54)

\displaystyle\leq K(\alpha)\sqrt{r}\left(\frac{e\pi|l|}{r+\alpha}\right)^{r}\,,

with
K(\alpha):=\frac{\pi^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{\alpha+\frac{1}{4}}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}(\alpha+1)^{\alpha}
.
We also have, from Cauchy-Schwartz inequality that

|d_{p_{j},k_{j}}|\leq 1
for all
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
and
p_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Injecting this in (
53
), we get

|d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}|\leq|d_{p_{j_{0}},k_{j_{0}}}|\,

where
j_{0}\in[[1,d]]
is such that
||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}=|k_{j_{0}}|
.
Let
0<\xi<1
such that

||\boldsymbol{p}||_{\infty}\leq\xi\frac{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}{e\pi}
. This implies that

|d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}|\lesssim_{\alpha}\sqrt{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\xi^{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\ ,\ \mbox{if}\ ||\boldsymbol{p}||_{\infty}\leq\xi\frac{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}{e\pi}\,,

where the notation
\lesssim_{\gamma}
means in general that the inequality is true up to constant depending only on a variable
\gamma
. Let us now re-write the expression of
C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})
in the following manner :

C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})=\underbrace{\sum_{e||\pi||_{\infty}\leq\xi||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}}_{S_{1}}+\underbrace{\sum_{e||\pi||_{\infty}>\xi||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}}_{S_{2}}.

To get an upper bound for
|S_{1}|,
we let

\mathcal{A}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:||\boldsymbol{p}||_{\infty}\leq\xi\frac{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}{e\pi}\}
. This set contains at most

\left(2\left[\frac{\xi}{e\pi}||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}\right]+1\right)^{d}
elements where
[x]
denotes the integer part of
x
. Hence, by using Bessel’s and Cauchy-Schwartz inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle|S_{1}|=\Bigg|\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}}a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}}|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}}|d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(55)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}}||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}\xi^{2||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\sqrt{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\xi^{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\left(2\left[\frac{\xi}{e\pi}||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}\right]+1\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\xi^{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{2}\,.

Next, we get an upper bound for
|S_{2}|
. For thus purpose, we suppose that
f\in H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})
. It follows, using Lemma3.9 of
[
30
]
,
that
f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\in H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})

\forall\,|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq d
.
Applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to
|S_{2}|^{2}
followed by
Bessel’s inequality, we obtain

|S_{2}|^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|^{2}\right).\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}|d_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p}}|^{2}\right)\\
\leq\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|^{2}\right)\|\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{k}}^{(\alpha)}\boldsymbol{w}^{(\alpha)}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|^{2}\right).||\Phi_{\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{k}}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\alpha}^{2}\cdot\|\boldsymbol{w}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|^{2}\right)\,.

(56)

Note that for
\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}
, we have
\left[\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+|p_{j}|\right)^{s+\frac{d}{2}}\right]^{2}\geq||\boldsymbol{p}||_{\infty}^{2s+d}\geq\left[\xi\frac{||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}{(e\pi)}\right]^{2s+d}
.
Consequently, for
f_{u}\in H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})
, we have :

|S_{2}|^{2}\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{e\pi}{\xi||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\right)^{2s+d}\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|p_{j}|^{2}\right)^{s+\frac{d}{2}}|a_{\boldsymbol{p}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{e\pi}{\xi||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\right)^{2s+d}||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}^{2}\,.

Hence,

|S_{2}|\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{-s-\frac{d}{2}}\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}\,.

(57)

Combining (
55
) and (
57
), we get

\displaystyle|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\xi^{\|\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}}\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}+\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{-s-\frac{d}{2}}\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}

(58)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\xi^{\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}}+\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{-s-\frac{d}{2}}\right)\left(\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}+\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}\right)

Finally, if
\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{1}\geq(N+1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
\frac{N+1}{|\boldsymbol{u}|.(\ln(N+1)-\ln(|\boldsymbol{u}|))}\geq\frac{1}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{\xi}\right)}\left(1+s+\frac{d}{2}\right),
then

\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\xi^{\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{-s-\frac{d}{2}}
. Consequently

|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})|\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}^{-s-\frac{d}{2}}\left(\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}+\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}\right)\,.

∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
f\in H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})
and let
1\leq m\leq\min\{N,d\}
with
N>e.m-1
and
such that

\frac{\frac{N+1}{m}}{\ln\left(\frac{N+1}{m}\right)}\geq-\ln\left(\xi\right)\left(1+s+\frac{d}{2}\right)\,,

where
0<\xi<\frac{1}{2}
. Suppose that
s+\frac{d}{2}>m+1,
then

\|f-\Pi_{N,m}(f)\|_{\alpha}\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}\frac{1}{2^{(s+\frac{d}{2})(m+1)}}||f||_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}+\frac{m\|f\|}{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}.\left(2+\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{d}\left(\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}\,.

(59)

and

\|f-\Pi_{N,m}(f)\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}\frac{1}{2^{(s+\frac{d}{2})(m+1)}}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}(I^{d})}+\frac{\|f\|D^{m}(N,\alpha)m}{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}\left(2+\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{d}\left(\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}\,.

(60)

Here,
\|f\|=||f||_{2}+||f||_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}}
and

D(N,\alpha)
is as given by Lemma
4
.

Proof.

The orthogonal projection of
f
on
\mathcal{P}_{N,m,d}
,
\Pi_{N,m}f
, verifies

\left\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\right\|_{\alpha}=\Big\|\big[f-T_{m}(f)\big]+\big[T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N,m}(T_{m}(f))\big]+\big[\Pi_{N,m}(T_{m}(f))-\Pi_{N,m}(f)\big]\Big\|_{\alpha}

Note that for
N\geq m
,
\Pi_{N,m}(T_{m}(f))=\Pi_{N,m}(f)
and consequently, we have

\left\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\right\|_{\alpha}\leq\left\|f-T_{m}(f)\right\|_{\alpha}+\left\|T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N.m}(T_{m}(f))\right\|_{\alpha}.

From
[
30
]
, we have for
f\in H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})

\|f-T_{m}f\|_{L^{2}(I^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{2^{(s+\frac{d}{2})(m+1)}}\|f\|_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}(I^{d})}\,.

To get an upper bound for
\left\|T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N,m}(T_{m}(f))\right\|_{\alpha}
, we proceed as follows.
The function
\Pi_{N,m}f
writes as

\Pi_{N,m}f=\sum_{|\boldsymbol{v}|\leq m;||\boldsymbol{k}||_{1}\leq N}C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}(f)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}(x)\,.

hence,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N,m}(T_{m}(f))\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m}f_{\boldsymbol{u}}-\Pi_{N,m}\Big(\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m}f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\Big)\Big\|_{\alpha}

(61)

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m}\left[f_{\boldsymbol{u}}-\Pi_{N,m}(f_{\boldsymbol{u}})\right]\right\|_{\alpha}=\left\|\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m;|\boldsymbol{v}|\leq m;\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{1}\geq N+1}C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{v}}^{(\alpha)}\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m;\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{1}\geq N+1}C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}^{(\alpha)}\right\|_{\alpha}\,.

Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\left\|T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N,m}(T_{m}(f))\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m;\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{1}\geq N+1}|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)|\cdot\left\|\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}^{(\alpha)}\right\|_{\alpha}=\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m;\|\boldsymbol{k}_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1}\geq N+1}|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)|

(62)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|=1}^{m}\sum_{\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{1}\geq N+1}|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|=1}^{m}\sum_{\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{N+1}{|\boldsymbol{u}|}}|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)|\,.

Let
1\leq|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq m
and
\boldsymbol{k}\in F_{\boldsymbol{u}}.
Then
for
1\leq j\leq m
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
, the number of elements
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{k})
such that

|\boldsymbol{u}|=j
and

||\boldsymbol{k}||_{\infty}=l

is
{d\choose j}j(l+1)^{j-1}\,.

Let
\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|:=||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{2}+||f_{\boldsymbol{u}}||_{H^{s+\frac{d}{2}}}
. Using the result of Lemma3.9 in
[
30
]
and adapting its proof for the
||.||_{H^{s}}-
norm, we conclude that for all
\boldsymbol{u}\subset\mathcal{D}
with
|\boldsymbol{u}|\geq 1

\|f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\|\leq\|f\|.

Moreover, by using the result of Lemma
5
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N,m}(T_{m}(f))\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}\|f\|\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m}{d\choose j}\,j\,\sum_{l\geq\frac{N+1}{j}}(l+1)^{j-1}\frac{1}{l^{s+\frac{d}{2}}}

(63)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m}{d\choose j}\,j\,\left(1+\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{j-1}\sum_{l\geq\frac{N+1}{j}}l^{j-1-s-\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m\|f\|}{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}.\left(2+\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{d}\left(\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}\,.

In a similar manner, we have

\left\|f-\Pi_{N,m}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f-T_{m}(f)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N.m}(T_{m}(f))\right\|_{\infty}\,.

In
[
30
]
, it has been shown that

\left\|f-T_{m}(f)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2^{(s+\frac{d}{2})(m+1)}}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}(I^{d})}.

On the other hand and by using Lemma
4
and Lemma
5
, one gets

\displaystyle\left\|T_{m}(f)-\Pi_{N.m}(T_{m}(f))\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|=1}^{m}\sum_{\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{N+1}{|\boldsymbol{u}|}}|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)|.\left\|\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}^{(\alpha)}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|=1}^{m}\sum_{\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{N+1}{|\boldsymbol{u}|}}|C_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{u}}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)|.\cdot\frac{D^{m}(N,\alpha)}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}

\displaystyle\frac{D^{m}(N,\alpha)}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\cdot\|f\|\sum_{|\boldsymbol{u}|=1}^{m}\sum_{\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{N+1}{|\boldsymbol{u}|}}||\boldsymbol{k}||^{-s-\frac{d}{2}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D^{m}(N,\alpha)}{\left(h_{0}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\|f\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}{d\choose j}\,j\,\sum_{l\geq\frac{N+1}{j}}l^{j-1-s-\frac{d}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}

\displaystyle\|f\|D^{m}(N,\alpha)\sum_{j=1}^{m}{d\choose j}\,j\,\left(1+\frac{j}{N+1}\right)^{j-1}\sum_{l\geq\frac{N+1}{j}}l^{m-1-s-\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,d,s}

\displaystyle\frac{m\|f\|D^{m}(N,\alpha)}{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}\left(2+\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{d}\left(\frac{m}{N+1}\right)^{s+\frac{d}{2}-m}\,.

∎

Remark 3
.

From (
59
), one concludes that under the conditions that
{\displaystyle\gamma_{m,N}=\frac{m}{N+1}<1}

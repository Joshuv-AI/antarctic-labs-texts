Frobenius Categories over a Triangular Matrix Ring and Comma Categories
Dancheng Lu, Panpan Xie

Abstract
. We introduce the dual notions of ℰ ⁡ ( 𝒳 , M , 𝒴 ) \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{X},M,\mathcal{Y}) and ℳ ⁡ ( 𝒳 , M , 𝒴 ) \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X},M,\mathcal{Y}) , and investigate when they have enough injective objects or projective objects, when they are resolving or co-resolving, and when they are Frobenius categories. In the case that they are Frobenius categories, we establish a recollement of their stable categories. Finally some applications to comma categories are given.

In this section we recall some notions and results which we will use later.

We refer to

. Let
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}
carries a canonical exact structure. An object

is

\mathcal{C}
induces a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P,X_{1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P,X_{2})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P,X_{3})\rightarrow 0

\mathcal{C}

if every

induces a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow K\rightarrow P\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
P
is projective.
Injective
objects of
\mathcal{C}
and that
\mathcal{C}

has enough injective objects
are defined dually. We write
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{C})
and
\mathcal{P(C)}
for the classes of injective objects and projective objects of
\mathcal{C}
respectively. An exact category is a
Frobenius category
if it has enough projective objects and injective objects, and if its projective and injective objects coincide, i.e.,
\mathcal{I(C)}=\mathcal{P(C)}
.

The notion of a Frobenius category is very important because its stable category is a triangulated category and many triangulated categories including derived category and homotopy category of an abelian category are

\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius category. By definition, the stable category

Here,

stands for the set of morphisms from

If

is closed under extensions, then so is

, since

Next, we aim to classify all projective objects of

when it is an exact category. We begin with a lemma, which deals with this question under the hypothesis as weak as possible.
To this end we introduce condition (*).

(*)

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M,X)=0
for any

. For short, we will denote them by
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{X})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M,\mathcal{X})=0
respectively.
Lemma
3.2
can be simplified a lot under these hypothesises.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M,\mathcal{X})=0
and that
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{X},M,\mathcal{Y})
is closed under extensions. If either
\mathcal{Y}
has enough injective objects or
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{X})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, then for any
(P,Q)_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{X},M,\mathcal{Y})
,
(P,Q)_{\varphi}
is a projective object of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{X},M,\mathcal{Y})
if and only if

P
is a projective object of
\mathcal{X}
and
Q
is
\mathcal{Y}
-projective.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M,\mathcal{X})=0
, the functor
\mathbf{p}
is exact, and particularly, condition (*) holds. Thus, if
(P,Q)_{\varphi}
is a projective object of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{X},M,\mathcal{Y})
, then

P
is a projective object of
\mathcal{X}
and
Q
is
\mathcal{Y}
-projective by Lemma

. Conversely, we assume that
P
is a projective object of
\mathcal{X}
and
Q
is
\mathcal{Y}
-projective. If
\mathcal{Y}
has enough injective objects, then
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{B}(Q,Y)=0
for

by the Long Exact Sequence Theorem, and so condition (iii) in Lemma
3.2
is satisfied (by noting that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{B}(Q,\operatorname{Ker}(\phi))=0
); If
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{X})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, then the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)\rightarrow Y\xrightarrow{\phi}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,X)\rightarrow 0
in condition (iii) lies in
\mathcal{Y}
and thus condition (iii) is also satisfied. Consequently,
(P,Q)_{\varphi}
is a projective object of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{X},M,\mathcal{Y})
by Lemma
3.2
.

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose

\mathrm{(1)}

\mathrm{(2)}

\mathcal{Y}
are Frobenius category and

\mathrm{(3)}

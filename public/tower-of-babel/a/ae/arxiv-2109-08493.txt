Geometry of lines on a cubic fourfold
Frank Gounelas, Alexis Kouvidakis

Abstract
. For a general cubic fourfold X ⊂ ℙ 5 X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5} with Fano scheme of lines F F , we prove a number of properties of the universal family of lines I → F I\to F and various subloci. We first describe the moduli and ramification theory of the genus four fibration p : I → X p:I\to X and explore its relation to a birational model of F F in I I . The main part of the paper is devoted to describing the locus V ⊂ F V\subset F of triple lines, i.e., the fixed locus of the Voisin map ϕ : F ⇢ F \phi:F\dashrightarrow F , in particular proving it is an irreducible projective singular surface of class 21 ​ c 2 ​ ( 𝒰 F ) 21\mathrm{c}_{2}(\mathcal{U}_{F}) and detailing its intersection with the locus S S of second type lines. A consequence of the analysis of the singularities of V V is a geometric proof of the fact that if X X is very general, then the number of singular (necessarily 1-nodal) rational curves in F F of primitive class is 3780.

Introduction

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}_{\mathbf{C}}
be a smooth cubic fourfold and
F\subset\operatorname{G}(2,6)
its Fano scheme of lines. The geometry of
these varieties has received a lot of attention since the beginning of the 20th century and in particular since the
foundational papers
[
CG72
,
AK77
]
. Consider the universal family
I=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}_{F})
of lines, for
\mathcal{U}_{F}
the universal
subbundle of the Grassmannian
\operatorname{G}(2,6)
restricted to
F
, with the two projections

\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{X}
\textstyle{F.}

It turns out that the morphism
p
is a fibration of
(2,3)
-complete intersections in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
associated to the
geometry of
X
, and all but finitely many fibres are genus 4 curves. As such, each fibre
C_{x}=p^{-1}(x)\subset I
,
which parametrises lines in
X
through
x
, comes equipped with two
g^{1}_{3}
linear systems by restricting the rulings
of the corresponding quadric. The aim of this paper is to study the fibrations
p,q
and the geometry and intersection
theory of various geometric loci in each variety.

Recall that there are two types of points
[\ell]\in F
, i.e., lines
\ell\subset X
, depending on the decomposition of
the normal bundle
N_{\ell/X}
. The generic line is called of
first type
, whereas there is a surface
S\subset F
parametrising those of
second type
(see Section
2
for formal definitions).

Our first goal is to further expand in Section
3
on the geometry of the
curves
C_{x}
, which we achieve by studying properties of the fibration
p
on
I
but also the induced restricted
map over
q^{-1}(S)\subset I
. To name one example, we obtain in Corollary
3.9
that the fibration
p
is a
fibration in general genus 4 curves.

Moving now to geometric constructions on
F
, Voisin
[
Voi04
]
defined a map

\phi:F\dashrightarrow F

taking a point
[\ell]
corresponding to a line
\ell\subset X
to the
residual line

[\ell^{\prime}]
,
in the sense that, if
\ell
is general, there is a unique
\Pi_{\ell}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
so that
X\cap\Pi_{\ell}=2\ell+\ell^{\prime}
. This
map has been studied in detail in
[
Ame11
]
and if
X
does not contain any planes (the generic case, which we are
in), is resolved by a single blowup of the locus
S
. This gives impetus to the construction of the following map

\displaystyle\psi:

\displaystyle F\dashrightarrow X

\displaystyle[\ell]\mapsto\ell\cap\ell^{\prime}.

The locus of indeterminacy of
\psi
also includes the locus
V\subset F
of
triple lines
, i.e., lines for
which there is a
\Pi_{\ell}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
so that
X\cap\Pi_{\ell}=3\ell
. To the best of our knowledge, this locus has so far
eluded study. Note that it can be viewed as the closure of the fixed locus

\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)\subset F

of the Voisin map
\phi
. We will prove that it consists of both first and second type lines. Using results of Section

3
, we may interpret a general point
[\ell]\in V
as a triple ramification point of one of the

g^{1}_{3}
’s on
C_{x}
for

.

The main part of this paper is devoted, in Section
4
, to the study of various universal families
related to
V,S
and
F
. We conclude the following, where
\mathcal{U}_{F}
is the restriction of the universal bundle from the
Grassmannian and

the restriction of the Plücker polarisation.

Theorem A
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a general cubic, then the locus
V\subset F
of triple lines is a projective irreducible
general type singular surface, whereas the locus
C=S\cap V
is an irreducible nodal curve. Their classes are

\displaystyle[V]

\displaystyle=21{\mathrm{c}}_{2}(\mathcal{U}_{F})\in\operatorname{CH}^{2}(F),

\displaystyle[C]

\displaystyle=\frac{35}{2}H_{F}^{3}\in\operatorname{H}_{2}(F,\mathbf{Z}).

In particular, the class of

is a multiple of the generator (cf.
[
Voi10
]
) of the known extremal ray of the
effective cone
\operatorname{Pseff}^{2}(F)
. Much of Section
4
is devoted to studying the strict
transform
\widetilde{V}
of

in
\operatorname{Bl}_{S}(F)
, which we prove is the vanishing locus of a section of a tautological bundle on the

. As a corollary to the analysis of the

V
, we obtain the following, which to the best of our knowledge was not previously known (despite
similar results, e.g.,
[
NO21

).

Theorem B
.

If

is a very general cubic, then the number of

(1)

nodal rational curves in
F
of primitive class
\beta\in\operatorname{H}_{2}(F,\mathbf{Z})
,

(2)

singularities of
V

(3)

nodes of

are all equal to 3780.

As a further application of the analysis above, in Section
5
we study the loci

R,R^{\prime},N\subset I

which are the closure of the locus of ramification, residual to all ramification and triple ramification points
respectively, of the two
g^{1}_{3}

. The projection

is generically finite of degree

\phi
. On the other hand, we compute that the map
\psi
from above has degree 24, and
in Corollary

we prove that it is resolved by

S
and then the strict transform of

.

Finally, putting everything together, we prove the following in Theorem
5.3
and compute in Proposition

5.7
the classes of the above loci in the Chow group of

in terms of the tautological line bundle

and the pullback

of the class of the Plücker polarisation (or the relative canonical

\omega_{p}
of the fibration
p:I\to X
).

Theorem C
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a general cubic fourfold, then the locus of ramification points

is irreducible and smooth outside

and has
normalisation the blowup of
F
first at
S
and then the strict transform of
V
. The classes of
R,R^{\prime}
in
\operatorname{CH}^{1}(I)

and
N

\operatorname{CH}^{2}(I)
are given as follows

\displaystyle[R]

\displaystyle=4q^{*}H_{F}+l=4\mathrm{c}_{1}(\omega_{p})-3l,

\displaystyle[R^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=4q^{*}H_{F}+16l=4\mathrm{c}_{1}(\omega_{p})+12l,

\displaystyle[N]

\displaystyle=21(l^{2}-lq^{*}H_{F})=21(2l^{2}-l\mathrm{c}_{1}(\omega_{p})).

In the appendix, we reprove the first two of the above class computations using the genus four fibration

Background and Notation

As the notation surrounding cubic fourfolds is substantial, we devote this section to fixing that used in the

For a vector bundle

Throughout,

and

\textstyle{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The subvariety

3.
The Curve of Lines Through a Point

In this section we collect and extend some facts about the
(2,3)
-intersection
C_{x}
parametrising lines through a
point
x\in X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
of a smooth cubic fourfold. As mentioned in the introduction, if
X
is general then
C_{x}
is
always a curve, whereas for arbitrary
X
there can be only finitely many points where it is a surface. The following is
well known to experts but we include the argument so as to fix notation and extract a bit further out of its proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a smooth cubic fourfold and
x\in X
such that
C_{x}=p^{-1}(x)
is 1-dimensional.

(1)

If
\ell
is a line of first type through
x
, then
C_{x}
is smooth at
[\ell]
.

(2)

If
\ell
is a line of second type through
x
, then
C_{x}
is singular at the point
[\ell]
.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be a line of first type in
X
. We may assume that it is given by the equations

\mathbf{V}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})
. Then the equation of
X
may take the form
[
CG72
, 6.9]
.

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F=&\ x_{4}x_{0}^{2}+x_{5}x_{0}x_{1}+x_{3}x_{1}^{2}+x_{0}Q_{0}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})+\\
&x_{1}Q_{1}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})+P(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})=0.\end{split}

Since
x\in\ell
we may assume that
x=[1,a,0,0,0,0]\in\ell
(the case
x=[0,1,0,0,0,0]
is treated similarly). To
find
C_{x}
, we write the equation as

\displaystyle x_{4}x_{0}^{2}+x_{5}x_{0}(x_{1}-ax_{0})+ax_{5}x_{0}^{2}+x_{3}(x_{1}-ax_{0})^{2}+2ax_{0}x_{3}(x_{1}-ax_{0})

\displaystyle+a^{2}x_{0}^{2}x_{3}+x_{0}Q_{0}+(x_{1}-ax_{0})Q_{1}+ax_{0}Q_{1}+P=0.

Putting
x_{0}=1
and
x^{\prime}_{1}=x_{1}-a
we get

[a^{2}x_{3}+x_{4}+ax_{5}]+[x_{5}x^{\prime}_{1}+2ax_{3}x^{\prime}_{1}+Q_{0}+aQ_{1}]+[x_{3}{x^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}+x^{\prime}_{1}Q_{1}+P]=0.

Note in this case that
\Pi_{\ell}=\mathbf{V}(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})
is the tangent 2-plane to X along
\ell
.
The lines through
x=(0,0,0,0,0)\in{\mathbb{A}}^{5}
(in the
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}
coordinates) are determined by
their slopes
[x^{\prime}_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}:x_{4}:x_{5}]\in\mathbb{P}^{4}
, with the slope of the line
\ell
being
[1:0:0:0:0]
. They are
parametrised by the following curve which is given by the system

\displaystyle a^{2}x_{3}+x_{4}+ax_{5}=0,

\displaystyle T_{2}=x_{5}x^{\prime}_{1}+2ax_{3}x^{\prime}_{1}+Q_{0}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})+aQ_{1}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})=0,

\displaystyle T_{3}=x_{3}{x^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}+x^{\prime}_{1}Q_{1}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})+P(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})=0.

Substituting
x_{4}
from the 1st equation,
T_{2}=0,T_{3}=0
become equations in
the variables
x^{\prime}_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5}
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. The point in
C_{x}
which corresponds to the line
\ell
is then
[1:0:0:0]
(in
the
x^{\prime}_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5}
-coordinates). At this point the gradients of the surfaces
T_{2},T_{3}
are
\langle 0,0,2a,1\rangle
and

\langle 0,0,1,0\rangle
respectively. Hence the intersection is transversal and the curve
C_{x}
is smooth at the
point
[\ell]
with the tangent line given by
x_{3}=x_{5}=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Note then that the plane spanned by the line
\ell
and the above tangent line is the tangent plane
\Pi_{\ell}
to
\ell
in
X
.

Let now
\ell
be a line of second type, given again by
\mathbf{V}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})
. Then the equation of
X
may take the form

[
CG72
, 6.10]

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F=&\ x_{4}x_{0}^{2}+x_{5}x_{1}^{2}+x_{0}Q_{0}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})+x_{1}Q_{1}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\\
&+P(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})=0.\end{split}

A similar calculation shows that with centre the point
x=[1:a:0:0:0:0]\in\ell
(the case
x=[0:1:0:0:0:0]
is
treated similarly) the equation of
X
becomes (by putting
x_{0}=1
and
x_{1}-a=x^{\prime}_{1}
)

[x_{4}+a^{2}x_{5}]+[2ax_{5}x^{\prime}_{1}+Q_{0}+aQ_{1}]+[x_{5}{x^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}+x^{\prime}_{1}Q_{1}+P]=0.

Note in this case that
H_{\ell}=\mathbf{V}(x_{4},x_{5})
is the tangent 3-space to
X
along
\ell
. Then
C_{x}
is the
intersection of two surfaces in a 3-dimensional projective space in variables
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5}
with gradients at

\langle 0,0,0,2a\rangle
and
\langle 0,0,0,1\rangle
respectively at the point
[1:0:0:0]
corresponding to
\ell
. Hence the intersection is not transversal at the point
[\ell]
,
C_{x}

is singular there with the tangent cone given by the plane
x_{5}=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Note then that the space
spanned by the line
\ell
and the above tangent cone is the tangent space
H_{\ell}
to
\ell
in
X
.
∎

To summarise the geometry, for
x\in\ell\subset X
, the tangent space to the line
\ell\subset X
is spanned by
\ell

and the tangent cone to
C_{x}
at the point
[\ell]
. This space has dimension 2 (i.e.,
[\ell]
is a line of first type)
if and only if
C_{x}
is smooth at
[\ell]
and has dimension 3 (i.e.,
[\ell]
is a line of second type) if and only if

C_{x}
has a singularity at
[\ell]
(with tangent cone of dimension two). When we consider
C_{x}
as a curve in
F

(i.e., as
q(p^{-1}(x))
), the singular points correspond to the intersection points with the surface
S
. When

x\notin W
this intersection is empty and the curve
C_{x}
is smooth.

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
CG72
, Definition 6.6]
, lines of first or second type are defined as those for which the image of the dual mapping
is a smooth plane conic or a two-to-one covering of the projective line respectively.

In fact, when the above line
\ell
is of second type,
\nabla F([s:t:0:0:0:0])=\langle 0,0,0,0,s^{2},t^{2}\rangle
and the
fibres of the two-to-one covering are formed by the points
[s:\pm t:0:0:0:0]
, with ramification points the two points
at infinity
x_{0}=[1:0:0:0:0:0],\ x_{1}=[0:1:0:0:0:0]
. With
x=[1:a:0:0:0:0]\in\ell
,
a\neq 0
and
Y_{x}=T_{x}X\cap X
,
one sees that
Y_{x}
contains
\ell
and has two singular points on
\ell
, namely
x
and
x^{\prime}=[1:-a:0:0:0:0]
, the
conjugate of
x
under the dual mapping. For
a=0
,
Y_{x_{0}}
has a non-ordinary singularity at
x_{0}
and the same for

Y_{x_{1}}
. Finally note that for a point
x\in\ell
, with
[\ell]\in S
, the singularity of
C_{x}
corresponding to
\ell
has the same
type as the section
Y_{x}=Y_{x^{\prime}}
has at the conjugate point
x^{\prime}
(see
[
W05
, Section 2, pg.6, case (i)]
,

[
CML09
, Section 3.1]
). Hence if
x^{\prime}
is not in the Hessian this singularity is nodal. If there are several second
type lines containing
x
, each one induces a singularity as above.

On the other hand, if
[\ell]\in F\setminus S
and
x\in\ell

then
Y_{x}
does not have a singularity on
\ell
other than
x
. In particular, if
x\notin W
then the only
singularity of
Y_{x}
is at
x
.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have the following two correspondences

(1)

between pairs
(Y,x)
where
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a cubic threefold with a singular ordinary double point

x
(resp.
A_{2}
singularity at the point
x
), and smooth non-hyperelliptic curves of genus 4 with
canonical image which lies on a smooth quadric (resp. quadric cone).

(2)

between pairs
(Y,x)
where
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a cubic threefold with two
ordinary double points so that
x
is one of them, and smooth non-hyperelliptic genus 3 curves with two marked
points and with nodal canonical image lying on a smooth quadric.

In both cases the canonical image of the corresponding curve parametrises the lines in
Y
through
x
.

Remark 3.4
.

The cases of (1) above are exactly the smooth
(2,3)
complete intersections in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Note that in the case of a
quadric cone, as the curve is smooth, it necessarily does not pass through the vertex of the cone. In the second, the
line joining the two singularities of
Y
is necessarily contained in
Y
by Bezout.

Proof.

The first has appeared many times in the literature, see, e.g.,
[
CG72
, p.306-307]
and
[
CML09
, §3.1]
. The
second is a minor modification of this so we briefly sketch the construction. For

(C^{\prime},p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{3,2}^{\mathrm{n}hyp}
, the linear system
|K_{C^{\prime}}+p_{1}+p_{2}|
gives a morphism
C^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{3}

with image a sextic curve
C
with one node
p
. Just as in the classical construction of the canonical embedding of
a non-hyperelliptic genus 4 curves,
\operatorname{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},I_{C}(2))=\mathbf{C}
so there is a unique quadric
Q
containing
C
,
which we may assume is smooth by genericity. Also, the linear system
|I_{C}(3)|
induces a birational map
h:\mathbb{P}^{3}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{4}
with image a singular cubic threefold
Y
. This can also be realised by blowing up
\mathbb{P}^{3}
at
the curve
C
to obtain a variety
\widetilde{Y}
with one node (
C
and
\widetilde{Y}
have the same singularity count and
type) lying above the node of
C
, and blowing down the strict transform of the quadric
Q
to obtain
Y
. The
threefold
Y
will be singular both at the image
x
of the quadric but also the image of the singular point of

\widetilde{Y}
. Projecting from
x
gives the inverse map to
h
.
∎

Note that from Lemma
2.2
, if
X
is general, the locus
W\subset X
spanned by lines of second type is an
irreducible divisor.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a general cubic fourfold and
W\subset X
the locus spanned by lines of second type. For any

x\in X
the curve
C_{x}\subset F
parametrising lines passing through
x
is reduced and connected. Moreover, the following
hold.

(1)

For
x\in X\setminus W
the curve
C_{x}
is irreducible and smooth of genus 4 and if
x
is general then it is
general in moduli.

(2)

For
x\in W
general,
C_{x}
is irreducible and has only one node and its normalisation is a
general curve of genus 3 with the two preimages of the node being general points.

(3)

For any
x\in W
the total Tjurina number of
C_{x}
is
\leqslant 6
, and if reducible,
C_{x}
is the union of two
irreducible, at worst nodal, plane cubics meeting at 3 distinct points. Hence, there can be at most 4 lines of
second type through
x
.

Proof.

From
[
LSV17
, Lemma 2.7]
we know that
C_{x}
is reduced for all
x\in X
, and that it is connected follows from

[
BVdV79
]
. For any
x
not in
W
, from Lemma
3.1
it follows that
C_{x}
is also smooth and
hence irreducible, proving the first part of (1). We now prove the remainder of (1) and (2).

For (1), let
C
be a curve of genus 4 which is general in moduli (resp., for (2) a general arithmetic genus 4 curve
with one node). From Lemma
3.3
we obtain a cubic threefold
Y
with one (resp. two) nodes, in such a
way that
C
parametrises lines contained in
Y
passing through the marked node
p_{0}
. Write the equation
determining
Y
as
F(x_{0},\ldots,x_{4})=0
with singularity at the marked node
p_{0}=[1:0:\ldots:0]
(in such a way
that the other node is not on the
x_{0}=0
hyperplane), and consider the cubic equation

G(x_{0},\ldots,x_{5})=F(x_{0},...,x_{4})+x_{0}^{2}x_{5}.

It is easy to check that
X_{0}=\mathbf{V}(G)
defines a smooth cubic fourfold with
Y
as the tangent hyperplane section at the
point
[1:0:\ldots:0]
, so that we have
p_{0}\in X_{0}
and that
C=C_{p_{0}}
is the curve of lines through
p_{0}
.
Note that in the second case,
Y
will be a hyperplane section of
X_{0}
with two nodes, and the line between these
nodes is of second type (cf., Remark
3.2
for the converse statement that the tangent
hyperplane at a general point of a general line of second type has two singularities).

Let now

\mathcal{X}\to|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{5}}(3)|_{\mathrm{sm}}

the universal family of smooth cubic fourfolds,

p:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{X}
the projection from the universal family of lines on all of them, and

q:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{F}
the morphism to the universal Fano variety of lines (we discuss this and some of the
universal families mentioned below in the beginning of the following section). From semicontinuity of fibre
dimension, the generic fibre of the morphism
p:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{X}
is a smooth curve of genus
4
. In fact,

[
CS09
, Lemma 2.1, Corollary 2.2]
prove that for any smooth cubic fourfold
X
, there are only finitely
many points in
X
over which the fibre of
p:I\to X
is not 1-dimensional, so this locus is codimension
4
in

\mathcal{X}
. The universal locus
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{F}
of second type lines admits a projection to the
Grassmannian
\operatorname{G}(2,6)
with isomorphic, irreducible fibres (see
[
Huy23
, Proposition 2.2.13]
and its
proof). Hence
\mathcal{S}
is irreducible and of codimension 2 in
\mathcal{F}
so that the total space

q^{-1}(\mathcal{S})
of second type lines in
\mathcal{I}
is irreducible and likewise its image

\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{X}
, the locus of points on lines of second type, which is a divisor in
{\mathcal{X}}

since
q^{-1}(\mathcal{S})\to\mathcal{W}
is generically finite from Lemma
2.2
. We conclude that the
non-empty open subset of
\mathcal{W}
parametrising points where the fibres of
p
are curves is irreducible.

Since a general
X
degenerates to
X_{0}
, so will the corresponding family of curves
C_{x}
degenerate to
C
,
proving (1), since being general is an open condition. Similarly, the curve
C_{x}
for a
general point
x\in W\subset X
must be general and irreducible with one node, since being nodal is
an open condition in a flat family of curves and
\mathcal{W}
is irreducible. This completes (2).

We now prove (3). As mentioned in Section
2
, the class of
C_{x}
in cohomology is
2\beta
, and
since for the very general
X
the class
\beta
generates the algebraic part of cohomology, it must be the case
that for general
X
the class
\beta
is irreducible, so that every curve representing it must be irreducible,
implying
C_{x}
has at most two irreducible components. From
[
NO21
, Lemma 3.8]
it follows that each such
irreducible component has arithmetic genus
1
and at worst nodes, implying that the two components must meet at
three distinct points as the total arithmetic genus is 4. The statement about singularities follows from the
correspondence between singularities of the blow up
\widetilde{Y}_{x}
at
x
of the tangent hyperplane section

Y_{x}=T_{x}X\cap X
and those of
C_{x}
of
[
CML09
, 3.1-3.3]
and from the fact that for a general
X
, every
hyperplane has isolated singularities of Tjurina number at most
6
from
[
LSV17
, Corollary 3.7]
. As the
arithmetic genus of
C_{x}
is four and from Lemma
3.1
every singularity corresponds to a second type
line through
x
, the above discussion implies that there can be at most 4 lines of second type through
x
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In particular, from Lemma
3.1
the curve
C_{x}
can have at most four singular points for
x
an
arbitrary point of a general cubic fourfold. It would be desirable to have a complete list of possible singularities
which occur for

and a description of the corresponding loci in

.

Corollary 3.7
.

For
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
a general cubic fourfold, the morphism
p:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}_{S})\to W
is birational and the degree of
W

in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
is 225.

Proof.

That through a general point

there passes precisely one second type line follows from the fact that each
second type line through
x
forces an extra singularity of
C_{x}
by Lemma
3.1
, so we conclude from
Proposition
3.5
, as the generic
x\in W
has
C_{x}
irreducible with one node.
That the degree of
W
is 225 was already computed in Lemma
2.6
, under the assumption that
the degree of the above map is one.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

More generally, let
n\geqslant 3
and let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be a general cubic
n
-fold,
F
its Fano scheme of lines
and
F_{2}\subset F
the locus of lines of second type, which is smooth of dimension
n-2=\frac{1}{2}\dim F
from

[
Huy23

R,R^{\prime}\subset I
of Section
5
using Hurwitz spaces, and
in particular obtain again some of the results of Proposition
5.7
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\lambda
be the determinant of the Hodge bundle of the curve
fibration
p:I\to X
. Then

•

[R]=4\omega_{p}+8p^{*}\lambda-p^{*}[W]
,

•

[R^{\prime}]=4\omega_{p}+68p^{*}\lambda-8p^{*}[W]
.

Proof.

We denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}
the part of the space of admissible covers of genus
4
and degree
3
,
with
b=12
labelled branched points, consisting of the union of the locus of smooth admissible covers and the
boundary divisors
E_{0}
and
E_{3}
(see
[
vdGK12
]
for the notation) where a point of
E_{0}
corresponds to a map
X\to P
,
with
X=(C_{1}\cup R_{1})\cup C_{2}
and
P=\mathbb{P}^{1}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with
C_{2}
a smooth genus
3
curve and all
other curves rational, as in
[
vdGK12
, Proposition 3.1, ii]
for
k=2
. A point of
E_{3}
corresponds to a map
X\to P
, with
X=S\cup C
, where
C
is a smooth genus
4
curve and all other curves rational, as in
[
vdGK12
, Proposition
4.1, ii]
for
k=2
. We have the diagram of universal curves

\scriptstyle{\pi_{b}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{t}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4,1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,b}}
\textstyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}.}

The map
h
is generically finite of degree
N_{0}=2\,b!
which is the number of
g^{1}_{3}
’s times the possible
permutations of the branch points. The map
t
is equipped with
b
disjoint sections
\tau_{i}:\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}\to\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}}
corresponding to the
b
labelled
ramification points sitting over the labelled branched points. These commute with the natural sections
s_{i}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,b}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,b+1}
, i.e.,
f\tau_{i}=s_{i}j
. Let
T_{i}
be the image of

\tau_{i}
. Note that each ramification point of a 3-to-1 map
F:X\to P
admits all the labels - in other words,
if
i\in\{1,...,b\}
and
p
is a ramification point of
F
then there exists an
h\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}
so that the fibre over
h
represents the map
F
and
\tau_{i}(h)=p
. Therefore the
images of the
T_{i}
’s under the map
\phi
coincide and are equal to the union of all ramification points of all the

g^{1}_{3}
’s.

The map
h
is simply branched at
E_{0}
and
E_{3}
. The map
\phi
factors via the fibre product
\mathcal{X}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4,1}\times_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}
. Let

\textstyle{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\scriptstyle{t}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\pi}}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{h}}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4,1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}}

the induced diagram, with
\phi=\widetilde{h}\rho
. We have
\omega_{\widetilde{\pi}}=\widetilde{h}^{*}\omega_{\pi}
. The map

h
is ramified at the points
x\in E_{0}
and therefore the fibre of
\widetilde{\pi}
over
x
has an
A_{1}
singularity
corresponding to the node over the fibre of
\pi
. The map
\rho
resolves the singularity and the corresponding
exceptional divisor
\mathcal{C}_{1}
(the union of the
C_{1}
from above) consists of
(-2)
-curves. In addition, on
the fibres over
E_{0}
the map
\rho
contracts an exceptional divisor
\mathcal{R}_{1}
which consists of

(-1)
-curves - blow-up along a smooth locus (the union of the tails
R_{1}
as above). The map
h
is also ramified
at the points
x\in E_{3}
but since the fibre over
x
maps to a smooth curve, the fibre of
\widetilde{\pi}
over
x
is
a smooth curve. The map
\rho
is the blow-up at the triple ramification point locus of the fibres of
\widetilde{\pi}
.
The exceptional divisor
\mathcal{S}
consists of
(-1)
-curves (the curves
S
as above). Since the restriction of

\omega_{t}
on
(-2)
-curves is trivial as
C_{1}
gets contracted via
\phi
, we get that
\omega_{t}=\phi^{*}\omega_{\pi}+B
with
B=\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{S}
.

Let
\alpha
be a divisor class on
\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{4,3}}
. We then have

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\phi_{*}\alpha)

\displaystyle=\pi_{*}\phi_{*}(\phi^{*}\omega_{\pi}\,\alpha)

\displaystyle=h_{*}t_{*}(\phi^{*}\omega_{\pi}\,\alpha)

\displaystyle=h_{*}t_{*}(\omega_{t}\,\alpha-B\alpha)

\displaystyle=h_{*}t_{*}(\omega_{t}\,\alpha)-h_{*}t_{*}(B\alpha).

We have
\omega_{t}=f^{*}\omega_{\pi_{b}}+R_{f}
, with
R_{f}
the ramification divisor of the map
f
, that is

R_{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{b}T_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle h_{*}t_{*}(\omega_{t}\,\alpha)

\displaystyle=h_{*}t_{*}(f^{*}\omega_{\pi_{b}}\,\alpha)+h_{*}t_{*}(R_{i}\,\alpha)

\displaystyle=h_{*}t_{*}(f^{*}\omega_{\pi_{b}}\,\alpha)+\sum_{i=1}^{b}h_{*}t_{*}(\tau_{*}\tau_{i}^{*}\alpha)

\displaystyle=h_{*}t_{*}(f^{*}\omega_{\pi_{b}}\,\alpha)+\sum_{i=1}^{b}h_{*}(\tau_{i}^{*}\alpha).

Suppose now that
\alpha=R_{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{b}T_{i}
. Note that the degree of
R
over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
is 2b so
we have

R={2\over N_{0}}\phi_{*}R_{f}.

By
[
vdGK11
, Lemma 4.2]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{b}\tau_{i}^{*}R_{f}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{b}\tau_{i}^{*}T_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{b}t_{*}\tau_{i*}\tau_{i}^{*}T_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{b}t_{*}T_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=t_{*}(R_{f}^{2})=-{1\over 2}j^{*}\psi

and
t_{*}(f^{*}\omega_{\pi_{b}}\,R_{f})=j^{*}\psi
. Therefore
h_{*}t_{*}(\omega_{t}\,R_{f})={1\over 2}h_{*}j^{*}\psi
. Also,

t_{*}(BR_{f})=2E_{3}
. This is because
\mathcal{S}
has two ramification points and
\mathcal{R}_{1}
has none. Therefore

\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\,\phi_{*}R_{f})={1\over 2}h_{*}j^{*}\psi-2h_{*}E_{3}.

Following formulas in
[
vdGK12
]
we have:

\displaystyle h_{*}j^{*}\psi

\displaystyle=40N_{0}(9\lambda-\delta_{0})

\displaystyle 2h_{*}E_{3}

\displaystyle=N_{0}(132\lambda-15\delta_{0})

and so,

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\,R)

\displaystyle={2\over N_{0}}\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\,\phi_{*}R_{f})=40(9\lambda-\delta_{0})-2(132\lambda-15\delta_{0})

\displaystyle=96\lambda-10\delta_{0}.

Writing
R=a\omega_{\pi}+b\pi^{*}\lambda+c\pi^{*}\delta_{0}
, we get
a=4
(because the fibre
has 24 points and
24=4\cdot 6
, with
6=2g-2
). This implies

\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\,R)=4(12\lambda-\delta_{0})+6b\lambda+6c\delta_{0}=(48+6b)\lambda+(-4+6c)\delta_{0}.

Hence
48+6b=96
which implies
b=8
and
-4+6c=-10
giving
c=-1
.

Suppose now that
\alpha=A
is the sum of the residual sections to the
T_{i}
’s. Then
R^{\prime}={2\over N_{0}}\phi_{*}A
. We have

h_{*}t_{*}(\omega_{t}\,A)=h_{*}t_{*}(f^{*}\omega_{\pi_{b}}\,A)=h_{*}t_{*}(f^{*}\omega_{\pi_{b}}A)=j^{*}\psi
and
B=2E_{0}+2E_{3}
. This
is because both
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}
contain two points which are residual to the ramification points.
Then

\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\,\phi_{*}A)=h_{*}j^{*}\psi-2h_{*}E_{0}-2h_{*}E_{3}.

We have
h_{*}E_{0}={N_{0}\over 2}\delta_{0}
. Therefore
\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\,R_{1})={2\over N_{0}}\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi}\,\phi_{*}A)=80(9\lambda-\delta_{0})-2(132\lambda-15\delta_{0})-2\delta_{0}=456\lambda-52\delta_{0}
. Writing now
R^{\prime}=4\omega_{\pi}+b\pi^{*}\lambda+c\pi^{*}\delta_{0}
we have as above that
48+6b=456
which implies
b=68

and
-4+6c=-52
giving
c=-8
.
∎

Abstract
. We study the recovery of functions in various norms, including L p L_{p} with 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , based on function evaluations. We obtain worst case error bounds for general classes of functions in terms of the best L 2 L_{2} -approximation from a given nested sequence of subspaces and the Christoffel function of these subspaces. In the case p = ∞ p=\infty , our results imply that linear sampling algorithms are optimal up to a constant factor for many reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.

1.
Introduction and summary of results

The aim of the present paper is to identify properties of function classes
F
that allow for the existence of good linear sampling recovery
algorithms for
approximation in a given seminorm.

Our results combine ideas from
[
9
]
and
[
51
]
,
but we consider also approximation apart from
L_{2}
and
L_{\infty}
.
In fact, we provide a method to transfer results for
L_{2}
-approximation to
rather general seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{G}
. Namely, we consider the following setting.

Assumption B.
Assume that:

(B.1)

F
is a separable metric space of complex-valued functions on a set
D
such that function evaluation
f\mapsto f(x)
is, for each
x\in D
, continuous on
F
.

(B.2)

\mu
is a measure on
D
and
F
is continuously embedded into
L_{2}(\mu)
.

(B.3)

G
is a seminormed space which contains
F
, and
G\cap L_{2}
is complete w.r.t. the natural seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{*}:=\|\cdot\|_{G}+\|\cdot\|_{2}
. If two functions from
G
are equal
\mu
-almost everywhere, then their seminorm in
G
is the same.

(B.4)

(V_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of subspaces of
G\cap L_{2}
of dimension
n
.

All the assumptions are satisfied, for instance, if

•

D
is a compact topological space,

•

\mu
is a Borel probability measure with support
D
,

•

F
is a compact subset of the space
C(D)
of continuous functions (equipped with the metric induced by the maximum norm),

•

and
G
is either
C(D)
or the semi-normed space
L_{p}(\mu)
of
p
-integrable functions on
D
, where
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

But in principle, also infinite measures
\mu
and other seminorms like the energy norm are allowed, and it is not needed that
F\subset C(D)
to obtain results for
L_{p}
or even uniform approximation. See Section
4.3
for an example that goes beyond the aforementioned special case.

The
n
-th linear sampling number of the class
F
in
G
is defined by

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}(F,G)\,:=\,\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{D}\\
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}\in G\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{f\in F}\,\Big\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})\,\varphi_{i}\Big\|_{G}.

This is the
minimal worst case error
that
can be achieved with
a linear sampling recovery algorithm
based on at most
n
function values
if the error is measured in
G
.
In the following, whenever
F
is a normed space, we abuse the definition by taking the supremum over the unit ball of
F
in place of
F
.

Recently, there has been much progress on the approximation problem in the case
G=L_{2}
. We will comment on the history below. Although the main goal of this paper is to study the sampling numbers in norms different from
L_{2}
, our first contribution is a slight improvement of the best known bound in the case
G=L_{2}
.
The main assumption here is that there exists a sequence of subspaces

V_{n}

that are “good” for
L_{2}
-approximation. We compare the linear sampling numbers with the error of best
L_{2}
-approximation within the spaces
V_{n}
, i.e., with

(1)

\alpha_{n}\,:=\,\alpha(V_{n},F)\,:=\,\sup_{f\in F}\|f-P_{V_{n}}f\|_{2}\;=\;\sup_{f\in F}\,\inf_{g\in V_{n}}\|f-g\|_{2},

where
P_{V_{n}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
V_{n}
in
L_{2}(\mu)
.
The latter is the error of best
L_{2}(\mu)
-approximation in the space
V_{n}
.

Based on the ideas from
[
9
]
, we obtain that there are optimal sampling algorithms
whenever the numbers
\alpha_{n}
decay fast enough.

Proposition 1
.

There is an absolute constant
c>0
such that the following holds.
Let
D
,
\mu
and
F
satisfy
Assumption B
with
G=L_{2}
.
Moreover, let
(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be
as in (
1
).
Then, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there
are
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in D
and
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}\in V_{m}
, where
n\leq cm
,
such that the algorithm
A_{m}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})\varphi_{i}
satisfies

(2)

\sup_{f\in F}\,\|f-A_{m}f\|_{2}\;\leq\;\frac{70}{\sqrt{m}}\sum_{k>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\sqrt{k}}.

Note that the corresponding algorithm is indeed a suitable weighted least squares method.
Moreover, by
choosing the sequence of subspaces
V_{n}
accordingly,
we can replace the
\alpha_{k}

on the right hand side of (
2
) by
the Kolmogorov widths of
F
in
L_{2}
,
see Corollary
11
.

For approximation in general norms,
the idea is to lift the previous results from
L_{2}
-approximation to
G
-approximation, using
the quantity

(3)

\Lambda_{n}\;:=\;\Lambda(V_{n},G)\;:=\;\sup\limits_{f\in V_{n},\,f\neq 0}\frac{\|f\|_{G}}{\|f\|_{2}}.

Depending on the choice of
V_{n}
and
G
, the
\Lambda(V_{n},G)
correspond to the inverse of the Christoffel function, or the best constant in Bernstein or Nikol’skii inequalities.
Its appearance is no surprise as
it is
an important quantity in approximation theory,
especially for
G=L_{\infty}
and
V_{n}
being a space of polynomials, see
[
13
,
18
,
43
]
.
It has already turned out to be instrumental in
proving results in the uniform norm, see e.g.
[
36
,
51
]
.

The general procedure of lifting results from
L_{2}
-approximation to
G
-approximation will be explained in Section
2.1
.
The outcome is the following result for linear sampling recovery
in general norms.

Theorem 2
.

Let
Assumption B
hold and
let
(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(\Lambda_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be defined as in (
1
) and (
3
).
Moreover, let
c
and
A_{m}

from Proposition
1

be the universal constant
and the linear algorithm

A_{m}\colon F\to V_{m}
, that uses at most
cm
function evaluations,
respectively.

Then, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have

\sup_{f\in F}\,\|f-A_{m}f\|_{G}\,\leq\sum_{k>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{2\alpha_{k}\Lambda_{4k}}{k}+\frac{70\Lambda_{m}}{\sqrt{m}}\sum_{k>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\sqrt{k}}.

This leads to the following corollary on the order of convergence of the sampling numbers, which we formulate for the sake of simplicity only in a special case.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure supported on a compact topological space
D
,

F
be a compact subset of
C(D)
,

1\leq p\leq\infty
, and

(V_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

be a nested sequence of subspaces of

C(D)

of dimension
n
.
Moreover, let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
with

\alpha>\max\{\beta,1/2\}
.
If

\Lambda(V_{n},L_{p})\;\lesssim\;n^{\beta}(\log n)^{\delta}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{f\in F}\,\inf_{g\in V_{n}}\|f-g\|_{2}\,\lesssim\,n^{-\alpha}(\log n)^{\gamma}

then

g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}(F,L_{p})\;\lesssim\;n^{-\alpha+\beta}(\log n)^{\gamma+\delta}.

In Section
4
we discuss examples that show that
this bound is often sharp up to constants.

Remark 4

(The case
G=L_{\infty}
)
.

In this paper,
L_{\infty}
denotes the space of bounded functions equipped with the strong sup-norm. Our general
Assumption B
would also admit to choose
G
as the the space of essentially bounded functions with the weak sup-norm, but we are more interested in point-wise estimates. We choose the (somewhat unusual) notation
L_{\infty}
for the space of bounded functions for convenience, in order to have a unified notation for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
If
V_{n}
is an
n
-dimensional subspace of
L_{\infty}
, the numbers
\Lambda(V_{n},L_{\infty})
can be expressed in terms of an arbitrary
L_{2}
-orthonormal basis

\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}

of
V_{n}
.
We have

\Lambda(V_{n},L_{\infty})\,=\,\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|b_{k}\right|^{2}\Big\|_{\infty}^{1/2}.

If the measure
\mu
is finite, then

\Lambda(V_{n},L_{\infty})\,\geq\,\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu(D)}\int_{D}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|b_{k}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu\bigg)^{1/2}\,=\,\sqrt{\frac{n}{\mu(D)}}.

That is, the numbers
\Lambda(V_{n},L_{\infty})
grow at least like
\sqrt{n}
.
This optimal behavior of
\Lambda_{n}
appears, for instance, for spaces spanned by
trigonometric monomials,
the Haar or the Walsh system, certain wavelets, the Chebychev polynomials,
and the spherical harmonics,
if
\mu
is their corresponding orthogonality measure.
In other cases,
\Lambda(V_{n},L_{\infty})
might be larger.
We will discuss some examples in Section
4
.

Remark 5

(The case
G=L_{p}
)
.

For
2\leq p<\infty
,
and in the setting of Remark
4
, an easy computation shows

\Lambda(V_{n},L_{p})\,\leq\,\Lambda(V_{n},L_{\infty})^{1-2/p}\,=\,\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|b_{k}\right|^{2}\Big\|_{\infty}^{1/2-1/p}.

There are
numerous results in the literature
on uniform and
L_{p}
-approximation, often for specific classes
F

and explicit algorithms, see Section
4
.
The advantage of Theorem
2

is its generality and simplicity.
However, this result is non-constructive.
The underlying (least squares) algorithm is based on a random construction and subsampling
based on the solution of the Kadison-Singer problem,
see Remark
7
.
Since the used algorithm is specified
by the knowledge of
F
and
(V_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
only,
Theorem
2
shows the existence of
an algorithm that is
universal
in
p
.

1.1.
Uniform approximation in Hilbert spaces

Our results can be further improved if we consider unit balls of
Hilbert spaces. This also leads to some interesting results on
the power of linear algorithms based on function values
compared to
arbitrary algorithms

for uniform approximation, i.e.,
G=L_{\infty}
.
Let us recall that in this case, i.e.,
if the error is measured with
\|\cdot\|_{G}=\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, we approximate
with respect to the (strong) sup-norm.

For a comparison of the power of different algorithms or information,
one usually takes the Gelfand widths of the class
F
as the ultimate benchmark. The
Gelfand
n
-width
of
F
in
G
is defined by

\displaystyle c_{n}(F,G)\,:=\,\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to G\\
N\colon F\to\mathbb{C}^{n}\;\text{ linear}\end{subarray}}\;\sup_{f\in F}\,\big\|f-\psi\circ N(f)\big\|_{G}

and measures the worst case error of the optimal (possibly non-linear) algorithm using
n
pieces of arbitrary linear information.
Unfortunately,
Theorem
2
does not allow for a direct comparison of
g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}(F,G)
and
c_{n}(F,G)
.
This is clearly a desirable goal
since it corresponds to the question
whether,
for a fixed “budget” (
n
) and under the given
assumptions on the “problem instances” (
f\in F
),
other algorithms may perform better
than linear algorithms based on function values.

By techniques very similar to the ones used for proving Theorem
2
, we obtain the following
improvement in the case of

reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
which satisfy an additional boundedness assumption,
see Section
3.1
for details. In this case, we obtain
that linear sampling algorithms are optimal among all
(possibly non-linear) algorithms;
a result that has been observed independently in
[
14
, Thm. 2.2]
.

Theorem 6
.

There are
absolute constants

c,C\in\mathbb{N}

such that the following holds.
Let
\mu
be a measure
on a set
D

and

H\subset L_{2}
be a
reproducing kernel Hilbert space
with kernel

K(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,b_{k}(x)\,\overline{b_{k}(y)},\qquad x,y\in D,

where
(\sigma_{k})\in\ell_{2}
is a non-increasing sequence and
\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

is an orthonormal system in

L_{2}

such that there is a constant
B>0
with

\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|b_{k}\right|^{2}\Big\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,B

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Under these conditions we have
H\subset L_{\infty}
and

g_{cn}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H,L_{\infty})^{2}\,\leq\,B\cdot C\cdot\sum\limits_{k>n}\sigma_{k}^{2}.

In particular, if
\mu
is a finite measure, then

g_{cn}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H,L_{\infty})\,\leq\,\sqrt{B\cdot C\cdot\mu(D)}\cdot c_{n}(H,L_{\infty}).

This result
represents a special case of a more general result for reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
where we do not need the uniform boundedness of the basis, see our Theorem
15
and
[
14
, Thm. 2.1]
.
These results further improve on the previous results of
[
35
]
in the Hilbert space setting,
see
[
51
, Sect. 5]
for a discussion.

Another general bound in this context is due to Novak, see
[
45
, Prop. 1.2.5]
or
[
47
, Thm. 29.7]
,
which states that

(4)

g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}(F,L_{\infty})\,\leq\,(1+n)\,c_{n}(F,L_{\infty})

whenever
F
is a Banach space.
In fact, (
4
) holds with the (smaller) Kolmogorov widths in
L_{\infty}
on the right hand side, see
[
45
,
47
]
for details.
The factor
(1+n)
cannot be improved:
For each
n
, there is even a Hilbert space where (
4
) is an equality,
see
[
47
, Example 29.8]
.

In the forthcoming paper
[
27
]
it is shown that the factor
(1+n)
can be improved to
\sqrt{n}
when allowing for a constant oversampling factor, i.e.,
c\cdot n
sampling points
instead of
n
.

Let us emphasize again that the result in Theorem
6
applies to several important
cases of orthonormal systems, which are listed in Remark
4
.
Moreover,
we do not require any
decay of the Gelfand width
c_{n}
.
In this sense, the comparison statement is even stronger than the recently obtained
result for
L_{2}
-approximation which
we want to address shortly,
because the techniques of this paper also depend on these results.

For this, let us also define the

linear
n
-width
(or
n
-th approximation number
) as

(5)

a_{n}(F,G)\,:=\,\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\colon F\to G\\
{\rm rank}(T)\,\leq\,n\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{f\in F}\,\big\|f-Tf\big\|_{G}.

This is the minimal worst case error over
F

that can be achieved with arbitrary linear measurements
by using only linear algorithms.

We clearly have the relations

g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}\geq a_{n}\geq c_{n}

whenever function evaluation
is a continuous linear functional on
F
(for every
x\in D
).
Moreover,
it is a classical result that

a_{n}(F,L_{\infty})=c_{n}(F,L_{\infty})

if
F
is symmetric and convex,
but they are different in many other situations,
see
[
46
, Section 4.2.2]
.

In the case of

L_{2}
-approximation in Hilbert spaces,
a sharp bound on the sampling numbers was recently obtained in
[
9
]
.
This result shows that
g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}
has the same asymptotic decay as
a_{n}

whenever the sequence
(a_{n})
decays fast enough.
In fact, if
H\hookrightarrow L_{2}(\mu)
is
a separable reproducing kernel Hilbert space,
then we have

g_{cn}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H,L_{2})\,\leq\,\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq n}a_{k}(H,L_{2})^{2}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with a universal constant
c\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a much more general family of classes
F

(like compact subsets of the space of bounded functions),
the similar relation

(6)

g_{cn}^{\mathrm{lin}}(F,L_{2})\,\leq\,\bigg(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq n}a_{k}(F,L_{2})^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

was shown for all
p<2
with a constant
c\in\mathbb{N}
determined by
p
.
See also
[
30
,
31
,
36
,
42
]
for forerunners,

[
20
,
21
,
32
]
for corresponding lower bounds,
and
[
58
]
for
an approach via Kolmogorov numbers in
L_{\infty}
,
which allows for treating the case where the sequence
(a_{k})_{k}
is not square-summable, see
[
59
]
.
Proposition
1
and Corollary
11
are a slight improvement of (
6
).
In particular, these results show that

g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}(F,L_{2})\;\asymp\;a_{n}(F,L_{2})

if
a_{n}(F,L_{2})
decays with a polynomial rate greater than
1/2
, see
[
9
, Corollary 4]
.
This shows that (linear) algorithms based on function values
are asymptotically as powerful as arbitrary linear algorithms
in this case.
However, note that there are many cases where the above holds, but the minimal error
c_{n}(F,L_{2})
of non-linear algorithms decays much faster.
For example, for the class
W^{s}_{1}=W^{s}_{1}([0,1])
,
s\geq 2
,
i.e.,
the unit ball of the
univariate Solobev space
on the interval equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}}:=\|f\|_{p}+\|f^{(s)}\|_{p}
,
we have

a_{n}(W^{s}_{1},L_{2})\asymp\sqrt{n}\,c_{n}(W^{s}_{1},L_{2})\asymp n^{-s+1/2}
,
see e.g.
[
50
, Theorem VII.1.1]
and the references therein.

In contrast, Theorem
6
above shows that
the optimality of linear algorithms based on function values
is true for uniform approximation
without assumption on the main rate
\alpha
,
or restriction to linear algorithms,
at least for certain Hilbert spaces.

The remaining paper is structured as follows.
Section
2
is for our most general results.
In Section
3
,
we
then prove our results for reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.
It does not rely on the results from Section
2
.
In Section
4
we then consider several applications of our theorems. These are
L_{p}
-approximation in

•

several classes of periodic functions on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(Section 4.1),

•

Sobolev spaces on manifolds (Section 4.2), as well as

•

approximation in
L_{\infty}
- and
H^{1}
-norm for a weigthed Sobolev space without uniformly bounded basis (Section 4.3).

We think that our bounds for approximation in norms different from
L_{p}
are worth to be
compared to the existing literature for further specific
examples.
They might be optimal in several situations
where the good approximation spaces
(V_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
F
in
L_{2}
are also good approximation spaces in
G
.
We leave that for future research.

Remark 7

(The algorithm)
.

The complexity bounds given in Theorem
2
are based
on the very same algorithm used in
[
9
]
for
L_{2}
-approximation, which is non-constructive.
This is a
weighted least squares method
,
with specific weights that were introduced in
[
30
]
to prove that

n\log n
random sampling points (with a specific density) are
“good” for recovery in
L_{2}
,
see also
[
31
,
41
,
61
]
,
or
[
53
]
for a survey that considers
other information than function evaluation, too.

A first subsampling approach to obtain
n
optimal points was
considered in
[
42
]
.
This uses a (finite-dimensional)
frame subsampling

that is based on
the non-constructive solution to the Kadison-Singer problem.
In
[
9
]
, this was extended to an infinte-dimensional version,
leading to an optimal bound.
It is open if this can be done constructively,
see e.g.
[
2
]
for first results.

Remark 8

(
s
-numbers)
.

The numbers

a_{n}
and
c_{n}

are well-studied objects in approximation theory and
the geometry of Banach spaces, and are therefore well-known
for many classes
F
.
In fact, they fall under the general category of
s
-numbers

which allows the use of some fundamental techniques.
We refer to
[
8
,
49
]
for a general treatment of this,
and to
[
11
,
23
,
57
]
for more approximation theoretic
background.

In contrast, the numbers
g_{n}^{\mathrm{lin}}

do not (yet)
fit into such a general framework, and their dependence on
n
is
often still unknown.
It is therefore of interest, and one of the
major concerns of

information-based complexity
,
to determine the relations of all the above widths depending on
properties of the classes
F
,
see e.g.
[
46
,
47
,
60
]
.

Remark 9

(Tractability)
.

Theorem
6
is particularly useful if we
consider approximation for multivariate functions,
e.g. on
D=[0,1]^{d}
, and are interested
in the dependence of the complexity on the dimension
d
,
that is, in the tractability of the recovery problem.

For example, for uniform approximation of periodic functions,
the theorem implies that the curse of dimensionality holds for approximation
based on function values (sometimes called
standard information
)
if and only if it holds for linear information.
Details can be found in
[
14
]
.
The corresponding statement for
L_{2}
-recovery is not true,
see, e.g.,
[
21
]
,
but similar results can be obtained if
c_{n}(H,L_{2})
decays exponentially
with
n
, see
[
28
]
.

1.2.
Notation

For a measure
\mu
on a set
D
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
we denote by
L_{p}=L_{p}(\mu)

the space of complex-valued functions that are

p
-integrable w.r.t.
\mu
,
and by
L_{\infty}
the space of bounded functions on
D
.
The norms in the respective spaces are given by

\|f\|_{p}\ :=\left(\int_{D}|f|^{p}\,{\rm d}\mu\right)^{1/p}

and
\|f\|_{\infty}\,:=\,\sup_{x\in D}\;|f(x)|
.
Moreover,
if
D
is a topological space, then
C(D)
is the space of all continuous functions on
D
.

If
H
is a Hilbert space we denote with
\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{H}
the inner product of
f,g\in H
. By
\log
we denote the logarithm with base 2 and
a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
.
For two sequences

(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(b_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}
we write

a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
if there exists a constant
c>0
,
such that
a_{n}\leq cb_{n}
for all but finitely many
n
.
We write
a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
if
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
and
b_{n}\lesssim a_{n}
.
If the constant
c
depends, e.g, only on the two parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
,
then we indicate it by
\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\asymp_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Without indication it may depend on all involved parameters, except for
n
.

2.
Results for general function classes

We consider the approximation problem on general function classes with respect to general seminorms.
For this,
let
F
,
G
and
(V_{n})
that satisfy
Assumption B
be fixed, and
recall
the shorthand notations

\Lambda_{n}\;:=\;\Lambda(V_{n},G)\;:=\;\sup\limits_{f\in V_{n},\,f\neq 0}\frac{\|f\|_{G}}{\|f\|_{2}}

and

\alpha_{n}\,:=\,\alpha(V_{n},F)\,:=\,\sup_{f\in F}\|f-P_{V_{n}}f\|_{2}\;=\;\sup_{f\in F}\,\inf_{g\in V_{n}}\|f-g\|_{2},

where
P_{V_{n}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
V_{n}
in
L_{2}(\mu)
.
The latter is the error of best
L_{2}(\mu)
-approximation in the space
V_{n}
.
We also choose an orthonormal system
\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

such that
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}

form a basis of
V_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.1.
From
L_{2}
-approximation to approximation in
G

We first show how to lift
L_{2}
-error bounds
to error bounds in
G
using the spectral function
\Lambda_{n}

and later apply this to algorithms with good
L_{2}
-error bounds.

Lemma 10
.

Let
Assumption B
hold and let

(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(\Lambda_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be defined as in (
1
) and (
3
), respectively.
For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, any mapping
A\colon F\to V_{m}
and all
f\in F
, we have

\|f-Af\|_{G}\,\leq\,2\sum_{k>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\alpha_{k}\,\Lambda_{4k}}{k}\,+\,\Lambda_{m}\cdot\|f-Af\|_{2}.

Proof.

Note that there is nothing to prove if the right hand side of the inequality is infinite
so we may assume that it is finite.
We first observe

\displaystyle\|f-Af\|_{G}

\displaystyle\leq\,\|f-P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}+\|P_{V_{m}}f-Af\|_{G}

\displaystyle\leq\,\|f-P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}+\Lambda_{m}\cdot\|P_{V_{m}}f-Af\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\|f-P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}+\Lambda_{m}\cdot\|f-Af\|_{2}.

In order to estimate
\|f-P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}
,
we first
show that
P_{V_{m}}f
converges in
G\cap L_{2}
.
We denote
I_{\ell}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}\colon 2^{\ell}<k\leq 2^{\ell+1}\}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
m\in I_{s}
and
n\in I_{t}
with
s\leq t
. Then

\displaystyle\|P_{V_{n}}

\displaystyle f-P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}\,\leq\,\sum_{\ell=s}^{t}\bigg\|\sum\limits_{k\in I_{\ell}\colon m<k\leq n}\left\langle f,b_{k}\right\rangle_{2}b_{k}\bigg\|_{G}

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum_{\ell=s}^{t}\bigg\|\sum\limits_{k\in I_{\ell}}\left\langle f,b_{k}\right\rangle_{2}b_{k}\bigg\|_{2}\cdot\Lambda_{2^{\ell+1}}\,\leq\,\sum_{\ell\geq s}\alpha_{2^{\ell}}\Lambda_{2^{\ell+1}}.

Further we use the fact that the sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

is non-increasing, and therefore

\alpha_{2^{\ell}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\ell-1}}\sum\limits_{j\in I_{\ell-1}}\alpha_{k},

and hence, since the sequence
(\Lambda_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

is non-decreasing,

\begin{split}\|P_{V_{n}}f-&P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}\,\leq\,2\sum_{\ell\geq s}\sum\limits_{j\in I_{\ell-1}}\frac{\alpha_{j}\Lambda_{2^{\ell+1}}}{2^{\ell}}\\
&\leq 2\sum_{\ell\geq s}\sum\limits_{j\in I_{\ell-1}}\frac{\alpha_{j}\Lambda_{4j}}{j}\,\leq\,2\sum_{j>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\alpha_{j}\Lambda_{4j}}{j},\end{split}

which is convergent due to the assumption.
This means that
(P_{V_{m}}f)
is a Cauchy sequence in
G
.
Since we also know that
P_{V_{m}}f
converges to
f
in
L_{2}
,
we obtain that
(P_{V_{m}}f)
is a Cauchy sequence in
G\cap L_{2}
and converges to some
g\in G\cap L_{2}
by Assumption (B.3). In particular,
f=g
almost everywhere.
Thus, using again Assumption (B.3), we obtain

\|f-P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}\,=\,\|g-P_{V_{m}}f\|_{G}\,\leq\,2\sum_{j>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\alpha_{j}\Lambda_{4j}}{j},

which yields the statement.
∎

Now, we could proceed by inserting into Lemma

the known error bound for

-approximation
from
[

, Thm. 3]
.
Instead, we provide a slight improvement of
[

.

-error bound

Proof.

3.
Improvements in the Hilbert space setting

Since our results are slightly stronger in this case,
we now consider
uniform approximation if

F
is the unit ball of a Hilbert space.
For this, we (clearly) need that function evaluation
is a well-defined linear functional for all functions from
F
.
Hilbert spaces with this property are called
reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS).

3.1.
Preliminaries

Let

H
be a RKHS of complex-valued functions defined on
a set
D
with a
bounded kernel
K\colon D\times D\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of the form

(7)

K(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,b_{k}(x)\overline{b_{k}(y)},

with pointwise convergence on
D\times D
, where
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\ldots\geq 0

and
\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is orthonormal in
L_{2}(\mu)
w.r.t. the measure
\mu
.

For the embedding operator
\Id\colon H\rightarrow L_{2}(\mu)
,
the sequence
(\sigma_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of its singular numbers.
Further we put
e_{k}(x):=\sigma_{k}b_{k}(x)
,
k=1,2,\dots
.
The crucial property for functions
f
from the RKHS
H
is the identity

f(x)=\left\langle f,K(\cdot,x)\right\rangle_{H}

for all
x\in D
. It ensures that point evaluations are continuous functionals on
H
.
In the framework of this paper, the boundedness of the kernel
K
is assumed, i.e.,

(8)

\|K\|_{\infty}:=\sup\limits_{x\in D}\sqrt{K(x,x)}<\infty.

The condition (
8
) implies that
H
is continuously embedded into
L_{\infty}
, i.e.,

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|K\|_{\infty}\cdot\|f\|_{H}\,.

Taking into account
[
55
, Lemma 2.6]
,
we have that the set
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

is an orthonormal basis in
H
.
Hence, for every
f\in H
, it holds

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\langle f,e_{k}\right\rangle_{H}e_{k},

where
\left\langle f,e_{k}\right\rangle_{H}
,
k=1,2,\dots
, are the Fourier coefficients of
f
with respect to the system
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. Let us further denote

P_{m}f:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left\langle f,e_{k}\right\rangle_{H}e_{k}

the projection onto the space
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\}
,
which we denote by
V_{m}
.
Due to Parseval’s identity, the norm of the space
H
takes the following form

\|f\|^{2}_{H}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\left\langle f,e_{k}\right\rangle_{H}|^{2}.

We also define

K_{m}(x,y):=\sum_{k>m}\sigma_{k}^{2}b_{k}(x)\overline{b_{k}(y)},

i.e., the reproducing kernel of the orthogonal complement of
V_{m}
in
H
.
In this section, we only consider uniform appproximation and the corresponding Christoffel function of the subspaces
V_{m}
, i.e.,

(9)

\Lambda_{m}\,=\,\Lambda(V_{m},L_{\infty})\,=\,\sup\limits_{f\in V_{m},\,f\neq 0}\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{2}}.

3.2.
The main results

Using the Christoffel function (
9
),
we can lift
L_{2}
-error bounds
for
V_{m}
-valued algorithms
to
L_{\infty}
-error bounds.

Lemma 14
.

For any mapping
A\colon H\to V_{m}
and all
f\in H
, we have

(10)

\|f-Af\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,\|K_{m}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|f-P_{m}f\|_{H}\,+\,\Lambda_{m}\cdot\|f-Af\|_{2},

where

\|K_{m}\|_{\infty}^{2}\,\leq\,2\sum\limits_{k>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\Lambda_{4k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}}{k}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality,

(11)

\|f-Af\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,\|f-P_{m}f\|_{\infty}+\|P_{m}f-Af\|_{\infty}.

To estimate the first term in (
11
), we
use that
(\Id-P_{m})^{2}=\Id-P_{m}
and that the operator
\Id-P_{m}
is self-adjoint,
and get, for all
x\in D
,

\begin{split}\left|(f-P_{m}f)(x)\right|^{2}&=\left|\left\langle(\Id-P_{m})f,K(\cdot,x)\right\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}\\
&=\left|\left\langle(\Id-P_{m})^{2}f,K(\cdot,x)\right\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}\\
&=\left|\left\langle(\Id-P_{m})f,(\Id-P_{m})K(\cdot,x)\right\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}\\
&=\left|\left\langle f-P_{m}f,K_{m}(\cdot,x)\right\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\|f-P_{m}f\|_{H}^{2}\cdot K_{m}(x,x).\end{split}

We turn to the second term in (
11
).
Since
A
maps to
V_{m}
, we have
Af=P_{m}(Af)
, and hence

\displaystyle\|P_{m}f-

\displaystyle Af\|_{\infty}=\|P_{m}(f-Af)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{m}\cdot\|P_{m}(f-Af)\|_{2}\leq\Lambda_{m}\cdot\|f-Af\|_{2}.

This gives (
10
). It remains to bound
K_{m}(x,x)
.
Using that the sequence
(\sigma_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is non-increasing, i.e.,
for all
2^{\ell}\leq k<2^{\ell+1}
it holds

\sigma_{k}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\ell-1}}\sum\limits_{2^{\ell-1}<j\leq 2^{\ell}}\sigma_{j}^{2},

we obtain by a straight-forward calculation

K_{m}(x,x)\leq\sum_{\ell\geq\lfloor\log m\rfloor}\sum_{2^{\ell}\leq k<2^{\ell+1}}\sigma_{k}^{2}\left|b_{k}(x)\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{\ell\geq\lfloor\log m\rfloor}\frac{\Lambda_{2^{\ell+1}}^{2}}{2^{\ell-1}}\sum_{2^{\ell-1}<j\leq 2^{\ell}}\sigma_{j}^{2}\,.

Hence, in view of the relations
2^{\ell+1}\leq 4j
and
1/2^{\ell-1}\leq 2/j
, that are true for all
2^{\ell-1}<j\leq 2^{\ell}
, we get

K_{m}(x,x)\,\leq\sum_{\ell\geq\lfloor\log m\rfloor}\sum_{2^{\ell-1}<j\leq 2^{\ell}}\frac{2\Lambda_{4j}^{2}}{j}\sigma_{j}^{2}\,\leq\,2\sum\limits_{k>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\Lambda_{4k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}}{k}.

∎

According to
[
9
, Thm. 23]
,
there is a linear algorithm

A_{m}\colon H\to V_{m},\quad A_{m}(f)=\varphi(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n}))

using
n\leq cm
samples,
satisfying the
L_{2}
-error bound

(12)

\displaystyle\|f-A_{m}f\|_{2}^{2}\,

\displaystyle\leq\,433\,\max\bigg\{\sigma_{m+1}^{2},\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k>m}\sigma_{k}^{2}\bigg\}\cdot\|f-P_{m}f\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{866}{m}\sum_{k>\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\sigma_{k}^{2}\cdot\|f-P_{m}f\|_{H}^{2}

for all
f\in H
, where
c>0
is a universal constant.
If we plug this algorithm into Lemma
14
,
we immediately get the following result.
Recall that this result has recently also been observed in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 15
.

There is an
absolute constant

c>0
,
such that the following holds.
Let
(D,\Sigma,\mu)
be a measure space
and

H\subset L_{2}(\mu)
be a
RKHS with bounded kernel
K\colon D\times D\to\mathbb{C}
of the form (
7
) and
\Lambda_{m}
be defined as in (
9
),
where
V_{m}=\operatorname{span}\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}
.
Then it holds for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
that

g^{\mathrm{lin}}_{cm}(H,L_{\infty})\,\leq\,\sqrt{\frac{866\,\Lambda_{m}^{2}}{m}\sum_{k>\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\sigma_{k}^{2}}\,+\,\sqrt{2\sum_{k>\lfloor m/4\rfloor}\frac{\Lambda_{4k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}}{k}}\,.

This estimate simplifies a lot
in the case that
\Lambda_{m}^{2}
grows linearly with
m
,
as we stated in Theorem
6
in the introduction.
In this case, our upper bounds are sharp up to constants
due to a matching lower bound on the Gelfand width in
L_{\infty}
.
In fact, it has been shown
in
[
48
, Thm. 3]

that

(13)

\sum_{k>m}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\leq\,\mu(D)\cdot c_{m}(H,L_{\infty})^{2},

see also

[
7
, Lemma 3.3]
,
[
33
, Proposition 2.7]
, and
[
35
, Thm. 4]
.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

We apply Theorem
15
with
4n
instead of
m
(and thus

c=4C
) for the upper bound, and (
13
) for the lower bound.
∎

Remark 16
.

The assumption on the basis in Theorem
6
is clearly fulfilled

4.
Examples

We now apply our results to a wide range of Hilbert as well as non-Hilbert classes of functions.

4.1.
Spaces of periodic functions

Let
\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi]
be a torus where the endpoints of the interval are identified. By
\mathbb{T}^{d}
we denote a
d
-dimensional torus and equip it with the normalized Lebesgue measure
(2\pi)^{-d}{\rm d}\mathbf{x}
. For functions

f\in L_{1}:=L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
we define
the Fourier coefficients

f_{\mathbf{k}}=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}{\rm d}\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
w.r.t. the trigonometric system

\left\{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\colon\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}

which forms a
1
-bounded orthonormal basis of
L_{2}
,
i.e., for

f\in L_{2}

we have

f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}

in the sense of convergence in
L_{2}
.

4.1.1.
Hilbert spaces on the
d
-torus

Let us consider the space
H^{w}
of integrable w.r.t.
{\rm d}\mu(\mathbf{x})=(2\pi)^{-d}{\rm d}\mathbf{x}
functions
f
on the torus
D=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, such that

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{w}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(w(\mathbf{k}))^{2}|f_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}<\infty,

where
w(\mathbf{k})>0
,
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, are certain weights which satisfy the condition

(14)

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(w(\mathbf{k}))^{-2}<\infty\,

and
f_{\mathbf{k}}
are the Fourier coefficients w.r.t. the trigonometric system

\left\{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\colon\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}=:\{b_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
It was shown in
[
7
, Thm. 3.1]
that the condition (
14
) on the weights

w(\mathbf{k})
is necessary and sufficient for the existence of a compact embedding
H^{w}\hookrightarrow L_{\infty}
.
The space
H^{w}
is a RKHS with kernel

K_{w}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})}}{\left(w(\mathbf{k})\right)^{2}}\,,

which is bounded under the condition (
14
).
We get the following.

Corollary 17
.

There are absolute constants

C,c>0
,
such that for the introduced above RKHSs

it holds for all

that

g_{cm}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\leq C\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k>m}\sigma_{k}^{2}}\,\leq\,C\cdot c_{m}(H^{w},L_{\infty}),

where
\{\sigma_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is the non-increasing rearrangement of the sequence
\{(w(\mathbf{k}))^{-1}\colon\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
.

Proof of Corollary
17
.

The singular numbers
\{\sigma_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
of the operator

\Id\colon H^{w}\to L_{2}
coincide with

(w(\mathbf{k}))^{-1}
, which are rearranged in the non-increasing order.
We apply Theorem
6
with

\{b_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}=\{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\colon\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
,
where

\|b_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq 1

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

The result of Corollary
17
improves the respective estimate from
[
51
, Thm. 6.2]
where the authors have an additional

-factor on the right hand side.
We also mention here the earlier results
[

, Theorem 4.11]
,

[

]
,
[

]
.

In particular, if

Proof of Theorem

It is known
[

,

]

\sigma_{m}=c_{m}(H^{s}_{\mathrm{mix}},L_{2})
it holds

\sigma_{m}\lesssim_{s,d}m^{-s}(\log m)^{s(d-1)}\,
.
Hence, Theorem
18
follows
immediately from
Corollary

\beta=(1/2-1/q)_{+}
and
\delta=(d-1)(1/q-1/2)_{-}
, we get that
there are linear sampling algorithms
A_{m}\colon\mathbf{E}^{r}_{d}\to Q_{\ell}^{\prime}
,

r>1/2
, with
m\asymp\dim Q_{\ell}^{\prime}\asymp 2^{\ell}\ell^{d-1}
many points,
satisfying

\sup_{f\in\mathbf{E}^{r}_{d}}\|f-A_{m}f\|_{q}\lesssim m^{-(r-\frac{1}{2}-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})_{+})}(\log m)^{(d-1)(r+(1/q-1/2)_{-})},

where
1\leq q<\infty
.

The corresponding lower bound for the
L_{2}
-approximation follows from the embedding
\mathbf{H}^{r}_{1}\subset\mathbf{E}^{r}_{d}
and the estimate for the Kolmogorov widths of the classes
\mathbf{H}^{r}_{1}
,
r>1
, in
L_{2}
, see
[
11
, Thm. 4.3.12]
.

If
q=\infty
, we put
\beta=1/2
,
\delta=0
and hence get

\sup_{f\in\mathbf{E}^{r}_{d}}\|f-A_{m}f\|_{\infty}\lesssim m^{-(r-1)}(\log m)^{(d-1)r}.

∎

Remark 26
.

In the case of
L_{q}
-approximation with

2<q<\infty
,
one can derive (better)
bounds for
f\in\mathbf{E}^{r}_{d}
from the embedding
\mathbf{E}^{r}_{d}\subset\mathbf{H}^{r-1/2}_{2}
.
The corresponding estimate
m^{-(r-1+1/q)}(\log m)^{(d-1)(r-1+2/q)}
for Smolyak-type algorithms was obtained in
[
6
, Thm. 6.6]

(putting there
r_{1}=r-1/2
,
p=2
,
\theta=\infty
,
\mu=d
,
m\asymp 2^{n}n^{d-1}
).
For
q=2
, the bounds coincide and are asymptotically sharp.
Note, that
[
24
, Thm. 5.1 (Remark 6.4)]
(putting
\alpha=r
,
p=\infty
,

\sigma_{\infty,q^{\prime}}=1-1/q
and
m\asymp 2^{n}n^{d-1}
) also yields the upper bound
m^{-(r-1+1/q)}(\log m)^{(d-1)(r+1/q)}
for
Smolyak-type recovery in
L_{q}
,
2<q<\infty
, which is worse in the logarithmic rate even than those obtained in our Corollary
25
.

4.2.
Sobolev spaces on manifolds

Let
D
be a compact homogeneous Riemannian manifold
equipped with the normalized volume measure
\mu
.
Typical examples are the torus and the sphere.
We let
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<\dots
be the eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator and
E_{0},E_{1},\dots
be the corresponding eigenspaces. The eigenspaces are known to be finite-dimensional and orthogonal in
L_{2}(\mu)
. We take orthonormal bases
(b_{\ell,k})_{k\leq\dim(E_{\ell})}
of the spaces
E_{\ell}
. By the addition theorem, see
[
15
, Thm. 3.2]
,
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\dim(E_{\ell})}|b_{\ell,k}(x)|^{2}\,=\,\dim(E_{\ell}),\quad x\in D.

We define the generalized Sobolev spaces
H^{w}=H^{w}(D)
via the reproduing kernel

K(x,y)\,=\,\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}w_{\ell}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\dim(E_{\ell})}b_{\ell,k}(x)\overline{b_{\ell,k}(y)},

where we assume that the sequence
(w_{\ell})
is non-increasing and satisfies

(18)

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}w_{\ell}^{2}\dim(E_{\ell})\,<\,\infty.

This condition is needed for the kernel to be well-defined
and ensures the boundedness of the kernel at the same time.
The classical Sobolev spaces
H^{s}=H^{s}(D)
of smoothness
s
are obtained
if we put
w_{\ell}^{2}=\lambda_{\ell}^{-s}

and then the relation (
18
) is satisfied if and only if
s>\dim(D)/2
, see again
[
15
]
and the references therein.

To apply our results, we observe that

\Lambda_{m}^{2}=m\quad\text{and}\quad K_{m}(x,x)\,=\,\sum_{\ell>L}^{\infty}w_{\ell}^{2}\,\dim(E_{\ell})

for all
m
of the form

m:=\sum_{\ell\leq L}\dim(E_{\ell}),\quad L\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

By Lemma
14
and the
L_{2}
-error bound (
12
) from
[
9
, Thm. 23]
we obtain universal constants
C,c
such that,
for
m
as above,

(19)

g_{cm}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\,\leq\,C\max\bigg\{\sqrt{m}\,w_{L+1},\,\sqrt{\sum_{\ell>L}^{\infty}w_{\ell}^{2}\,\dim(E_{\ell})}\bigg\},

or more generally, for
2\leq p\leq\infty
,

(20)

g_{cm}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H^{w},L_{p})\,\leq\,Cm^{-1/p}\max\bigg\{\sqrt{m}\,w_{L+1},\,\sqrt{\sum_{\ell>L}^{\infty}w_{\ell}^{2}\,\dim(E_{\ell})}\bigg\}.

On the other hand, the approximation numbers (as defined in (
5
)) satisfy
a_{m}(H,L_{2})=w_{L+1}
and (
13
) gives

a_{m}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\,=\,c_{m}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\,=\,\sqrt{\sum_{\ell>L}^{\infty}w_{\ell}^{2}\,\dim(E_{\ell})}.

such that (
19
) alternatively reads

g_{cm}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\,\leq\,C\max\big\{\sqrt{m}\cdot a_{m}(H^{w},L_{2}),\,a_{m}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\big\}.

Note that both terms in the maximum are dominated by
a_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(H^{w},L_{\infty})
.
In this sense,
standard information (function values) is as powerful as linear information for the problem of uniform approximation for all generalized Sobolev spaces on compact homogeneous Riemannian manifolds.

We also note that
the dimension of
E_{\ell}
increases at most polynomially (see Weyl’s formula) such that the relation (
20
) in fact holds for all
m\in\mathbb{N}

and a modified constant
c
,
now depending on the dimension of the manifold
D
.
It can also be deduced from Weyl’s formula that

a_{m}(H^{s},L_{\infty})\,\asymp_{D,s}\,m^{-s/\dim(D)+1/2},

and

a_{m}(H^{s},L_{2})\,\asymp_{D,s}\,m^{-s/\dim(D)},

such that we obtain the following.

Corollary 27
.

There are universal constants
c,C>0
such that for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, any compact homogeneous Riemannian manifold
D
of dimension
d
, all Sobolev spaces
H^{w}=H^{w}(D)
as above, and all
2\leq p\leq\infty
,
we have

g_{cm}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H^{w},L_{p})\,\leq\,Cm^{-1/p}\max\big\{\sqrt{m}\cdot a_{m}(H^{w},L_{2}),\,a_{m}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\big\},

whenever
m
equals the number of eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator smaller than some
T>0
(counted with multiplicity).
In particular, for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s>d/2
,

g_{m}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H^{s},L_{p})\;\lesssim_{d,s}\;m^{-s/d+(1/2-1/p)_{+}}.

Remark 28
.

The asymptotic relation

g_{m}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H^{s},L_{p})\;\asymp_{d,s}\;m^{-s/d+(1/2-1/p)_{+}}

is known.
This is a classical result for Sobolev spaces on the cube (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Chapter 6]
)
and can be transferred to smooth manifolds, e.g., as in
[
29
]
.
We refer to
[
22
, Section 3.3]
and
[
12
,
18
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
for more direct approaches
to function recovery on manifolds.
Our contributions are the alternative proof,
the dimension-independent constants
and a simple almost-optimal algorithm, see below.

If we insert the algorithm from
[
30
, Thm. 1]
instead of
[
9
, Thm. 23]
into our Lemma
14
,
we obtain the following slightly worse
but more practical error bound.
Namely, for all
m=\dim(W_{L})
, where
W_{L}
denotes the span of the eigenspaces
E_{0},\dots,E_{L}
, the least-squares algorithm

(21)

A_{n}(f)\,=\,\underset{g\in W_{L}}{\rm argmin}\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(x_{i})-g(x_{i})|^{2},

using
n=cm\log(m)
i.i.d. samples
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in D
distributed according to the volume measure
\mu
,
satisfies with high probability that

\|f-A_{n}f\|_{p}\,\leq\,Cm^{-1/p}\max\big\{\sqrt{m}\cdot a_{m}(H^{w},L_{2}),\,a_{m}(H^{w},L_{\infty})\big\}

for all
f\in H^{w}
and
2\leq p\leq\infty
.
Here,
C,c>0
are universal constants again,
independent of the dimension.

In particular, we see that i.i.d. random samples are optimal up to a logarithmic oversampling factor for uniform approximation on such manifolds. This observation was already made in
[
29
]
. We improve upon the corresponding observation in
[
29
]
in the sense of the dimension-independent constants, the more general sequence
w
, and a possibly simpler algorithm
A_{n}
.

Recall that the logarithmic oversampling factor can be removed by using only a subset of the random points in (
21
), but the corresponding subsampling approach from
[
9
]
is non-constructive.
We note that there is also a constructive way to (not remove but) reduce the logarithmic oversampling factor using the subsampling approach from
[
2
]
.

Finally, let us mention that our results can also be applied for non-homogeneous Riemannian manifolds as the asymptotic bound
\Lambda_{m}\lesssim\sqrt{m}
remains true in this setting, see
[
39
, Lemma 5.2]
.

4.3.
A non-uniformly bounded basis system

Let us define a second order operator
A
on
[-1,1]
by

Af(x)=-((1-x^{2})f^{\prime})^{\prime}
.
Together with the uniform measure
\rm dx
on
[-1,1]
, it
characterizes for
s>1
weighted Sobolev spaces

H(K_{s}):=\{f\in L_{2}\colon\ A^{s/2}f\in L_{2}\}\,.

These spaces are RKHS with the kernel

K_{s}(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{{L}_{k}(x){L}_{k}(y)}{1+(k(k+1))^{s}}\,,

where
{L}_{k}\colon[-1,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, are

L_{2}
-normalized Legendre polynomials.
We refer to
[
3
]
for the details and the first
L_{2}
-approximation result for Gauss points.

In the case of
L_{\infty}
-approximation, the Christoffel function satisfies

\Lambda_{m}^{2}=\Lambda(\Pi_{m},L_{\infty})^{2}=\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|L_{k}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{2k+1}{2}=\frac{m^{2}}{2}\,,

where
\Pi_{m}:=\operatorname{span}\{L_{k}\colon\ 0\leq k\leq m-1\}
.

We get the following result.

Theorem 29
.

For all

s>1
, it holds that

(22)

g_{m}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H(K_{s}),L_{\infty})\,\lesssim_{s}\,m^{-s+1}.

The estimate (
22
) is better by a logarithmic factor than those obtained in Section 6.3 of
[
51
]
.

We now consider the sampling numbers of functions from
H(K_{s})
in the space
H^{1}
, where
H^{1}
denotes the space of all functions with weak first derivative being in
L_{2}
and corresponding (semi-)norm. Due to notational similarity to the Besov-Nikol’skii spaces (defined above) there might be cause for confusion.

Theorem 30
.

For all
s>2
, it holds that

g_{m}^{\mathrm{lin}}(H(K_{s}),H^{1})\,\lesssim_{s}\,m^{-s+2}.

Proof.

It was shown by E. Schmidt in 1944
[
52
, Formula (13)]
, that for the Christoffel function of the set
V_{m}=\Pi_{m}
of univariate polynomials of degree
m
it holds

\Lambda(\Pi_{m},H^{1})\asymp m^{2}.

Hence, taking into account that in this setting

\alpha(\Pi_{m},H(K_{s}))=(1+(m(m+1))^{s})^{-1/2}
,
we get the result via Corollary
13
.
∎

Note, that there are many papers devoted to estimation of the recovery error in the
H^{1}
-norm (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
16
,
17
]
and the references therein). This problem is connected to the numerical recovery of the solutions to a PDE (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
).

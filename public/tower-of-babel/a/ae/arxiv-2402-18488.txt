Introduction

In the 1960s, H. Jacquet and R. Langlands

The local results have been generalized to

(1)

for any character

(2)

L^{2}(G)\cong l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)

as
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma,\sigma)
modules (see
[
11
, Proposition 4.1]
).
Let
H
be a square-integrable representation of
G
.
There exists a left
G
-invariant map
p_{H}\colon L^{2}(G)\to H
.
As
p_{H}
commutes with the left action of
\Gamma
, we known
p\in\mathcal{R}(\Gamma,\overline{\sigma})\otimes B(l^{2}(\mathbb{N}))
and it gives the von Neumann dimension
\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\Gamma,\sigma)}H=(\tau\otimes\tr)(p)
.
It is related to the formal degree
d(\pi)
of
H
as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a unimodular group and
\Gamma
be a lattice in
G
.
Suppose
\pi
is a ordinary (or
\sigma
-projective) square-integrable representation of
G
.
We have

\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\Gamma,\sigma)}\pi=\mu(\Gamma\backslash G)\cdot d(\pi)
.

This result was first proved in
[
3
, §3]
for semi-simple simply-connected Lie groups without compact factors (see also
[
13
, §3.5]
).
It is then extended to projective representations in
[
28
]
.
See also
[
5
]
and
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
for more details.

We discuss the ICC condition of
S
-arithmetic groups, which is needed in the following sections.
This is an extension of
[
13
, Lemma 3.3.1]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
G
is a semi-simple algebraic group over a totally real field
F
such that
G(\mathbb{R})
is connected, center-free, and without a compact factor, then
\Gamma=G(\mathcal{O}_{S})
is an ICC group if
V_{\infty}\subset S
.

Proof
:
Take
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that its conjugacy class
C_{\Gamma}(\gamma)=\{g\gamma g^{-1}|g\in\Gamma\}
is finite.
Then the Zariski closure
\overline{C_{\Gamma}(\gamma)}=C_{\Gamma}(\gamma)
.
By Borel density theorem,
[
31
, Theorem 3.2.6]
,
G(\mathcal{O}_{F})
is Zariski dense in
G(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus
\Gamma
is also Zariski dense.
Consider the map
G(\mathbb{R})\to\overline{C_{\Gamma}(\gamma)}
given by
g\mapsto g\gamma g^{-1}
.
We have
|G(\mathbb{R})/Z_{G(\mathbb{R})}(\gamma)|=|\overline{C_{\Gamma}(\gamma)}|
is finite.
Thus
Z_{G(\mathbb{R})}(\gamma)
is a finite-index subgroup of
G(\mathbb{R})
.
Then
Z_{G(\mathbb{R})}(\gamma)=G(\mathbb{R})
and
\gamma\in Z(G(\mathbb{R}))
, which shows
\gamma=e
, the identity.

Remark 3.4
.

If
H^{2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{T})
is trivial, e.g.,
H^{2}(\PSL(2,\mathbb{Z});\mathbb{T})=1
, all these
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma,\sigma)
are isomorphic to the untwisted group von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
.
For a
S
-arithmetic groups, its second cohomology groups are not trivial in general (see
[
1
]
).
Their twisted group von Neumann algebras may be not isomorphic to the ordinary one.

4
Dimensions over the principal
S
-arithmetic groups

Let
F
be a totally real number field with degree
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=n
.
For each non-archimedean place
v
, we denote the integral ring by
\mathcal{O}_{v}
and the maximal ideal by
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
.
Let
\varpi_{v}
be a uniformizer.
Let
q_{v}
the cardinality of the residue field
\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q_{v}}
.
For the finite set of ramified places
S
such that
V_{\infty}\subset S
, we let
\mathcal{O}_{S}
be the ring of
S
-integers, i.e.,
\mathcal{O}_{S}=\{x\in F^{*}||x|_{v}\leq 1,v\notin S\}
.

We will consider the correspondence over the
S
-arithmetic groups of
\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}),\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})
as well as the ones of
\PD^{\times}(\mathcal{O}_{S}),D^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
.
Note the latter two are finite groups.
We first discuss
\SL(2)
with a family of local Haar measures
\{\nu_{p}\}
given in
[
25
, §3.5-3.6]
:

•

For
p\in S\cap V_{\infty}
,
F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
and the Haar measure is normalized in the sense
\nu_{p}(\SO(2))=1
.

•

For
p\in S\cap V_{f}
, let
I_{v}
be a Iwahori subgroup of
\SL(2,F_{v})
, e.g, the inverse image of
\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}|a\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{v}}^{*},b\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{v}}\}
under the map
\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{v})\to\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{q_{v}})
.
The Haar measure is normalized by
\nu_{v}(I_{v})=1
.
As
|\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{v})/I_{v}|=q_{v}+1
,
\nu_{v}(\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{v}))=q_{v}+1
.

Let
\nu_{S}=\prod_{v\in S}\nu_{v}
be the product measure on
\SL(2,\mathbb{A}_{S})=\prod_{v\in S}\SL(2,F_{v})
.
Thus, by
[
24
, Theorem 3.7]
, we have

\nu_{S}(\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\SL(2,\mathbb{A}_{S}))=d_{F}^{3/2}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\cdot\zeta_{F}(2)\cdot\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(q_{v}+1),

(6)

where
d_{F}
is the discriminant of
F
and
\zeta_{F}
is the Dedekind zeta function of
F
.
The functional equation of
\zeta_{F}
(see
[
22
, Theorem VII.5.9]
) gives
\zeta_{F}(2)=\frac{(2\pi)^{2n}}{2^{n}}d_{F}^{-3/2}|\zeta_{F}(-1)|
.
Thus we obtain

\nu_{S}(\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\SL(2,\mathbb{A}_{S}))=\frac{|\zeta_{F}(-1)|}{2^{n}}\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(q_{v}+1),

(7)

where
\zeta_{F}(-1)
is proved to be rational
[
9
, Theorem 8.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be a unital commutative ring.
We have

\PGL(2,R)/\PSL(2,R)\cong R^{*}/(R^{*})^{2}
.

Proof
:
Let us consider the determinant
\det\colon\GL(2,R)\to R^{*}
.
It induces a homomorphism
\eta\colon\PGL(2,R)\to R^{*}/(R^{*})^{2}
given by

\eta([g])=[\det(g)]\in R^{*}/(R^{*})^{2}
,

where
[g]
is the image of
g\in\GL(2,R)
in
\PGL(2,R)
.
If
[g_{1}]=[g_{2}]
, there exists some
a\in R^{*}
such that
g_{1}=ag_{2}
and
\det(g_{1})=a^{2}\det(g_{2})
.
Hence
\eta
is a well-defined map.
As
\ker\eta=\PSL(2,R)
,
we have
\PGL(2,R)/\PSL(2,R)\cong R_{v}^{*}/(R_{v}^{*})^{2}
.

For a prime number
p
, we let
\delta_{F}(p,S)=\sum_{v\in S,v|p}e_{v}f_{v}
, where
e_{v},f_{v}
are the ramification index and inertia degree of a place
v
over
p
.

Lemma 4.2
.

With respect to the Haar measures above, we have

\mu_{S}(\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\PGL(2,\mathbb{A}_{S}))=\frac{2^{\delta_{F}(2,S)+1}\cdot|\zeta_{F}(-1)|}{2^{2n}}\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(q_{v}+1)

Proof
:
We first identify
\frac{\mu_{S}(\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\PGL(2,\mathbb{A}_{S}))}{\nu_{S}(\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\SL(2,\mathbb{A}_{S}))}
with

\prod_{v\in S}\frac{|\PGL(2,F_{v})/\PSL(2,F_{v})|}{|\SL(2,F_{v})/\PSL(2,F_{v})|}\cdot\left(\frac{|\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})/\PSL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}|)}{|\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})/\PSL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})|}\right)^{-1}
.

Observe
|F_{v}^{*}/(F_{v}^{*})^{2}|=2,4,2^{e_{v}f_{v}}
if
v
lies over
\mathbb{R}
, odd prime or
2
(see
[
6
, Proposition 4.3.20]
).
We apply Lemma
4.1
to get
|\PGL(2,F_{v})/\PSL(2,F_{v})|
and the Dirichlet’s Theorem for
S
-units
[
23
, §5.4]
to get
|\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})/\PSL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}|)
.
The ratio above is
2^{\delta_{F}(2,S)+1-n}
.
Then it follows Equation
6
.

Now we consider the formal degrees of the local Steinberg representations

\St_{p}
of
\GL(2,F_{v})
and
\St_{S}=\prod_{v\in S}\St_{v}
of
\GL(2,\mathbb{A}_{S})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

With respect to the Haar measures above, we have

d(\St_{S})=2^{n}\cdot 2^{-\delta_{F}(2,S)}\cdot\prod_{v\in S_{f}}\frac{q_{v}-1}{2(q_{v}+1)}
.

Proof
:
The formal degrees of
\St_{v}
are listed as follows.

1.

For
v\in V_{\infty}
,
the Haar measure is normalized as
\mu_{\infty}(\SL(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\SL(2,\mathbb{R}))=\frac{1}{24}
, we have
\mu_{\infty}(\PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\PSL(2,\mathbb{R}))=\frac{1}{24}
.
As shown in

[
13
, Example 3.3.4]
,
\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}))}\mathcal{D}_{2}^{+}=\frac{1}{12}
(see the discussion below Lemma
2.4
for
\mathcal{D}_{2}^{\pm}
).
Thus, by Proposition
3.2
,
d(\mathcal{D}_{2}^{+})=d(\mathcal{D}_{2}^{-})=2
as discrete series of
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
.

Note
\St_{\infty}=\mathcal{D}_{2}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{2}^{-}
(up to a central character).
Hence
\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}))}\St_{\infty}=\frac{1}{6}
.
Observe that

\mu_{\infty}(\PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\PGL(2,\mathbb{R}))=2\mu_{\infty}(\PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\PSL(2,\mathbb{R}))=\frac{1}{12}
.

Thus, by Proposition
3.2
again, we know
d(\St_{\infty})=2
.

2.

For
p\in S_{f}
, as
\mu_{v}(\SL(\mathcal{O}_{v}))=q_{v}+1
, we have
\mu_{v}(\PSL(\mathcal{O}_{v}))=\frac{q_{v}+1}{2}
and
\mu_{v}(\PGL(\mathcal{O}_{v}))=q_{v}+1
or
2^{e_{v}f_{v}}(q_{v}+1)
when
2\mid q_{v}
or
2\nmid q_{v}
.
By
[
27
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
7
, §2.2]
(where the local Haar measure
\nu_{p}
is normalized in the sense of that the volume
\PGL(\mathcal{O}_{v})
is always
1
),
we have
d(\St_{v})=\frac{q_{v}-1}{2(q_{v}+1)}
or
2^{-e_{v}f_{v}}\frac{q_{v}-1}{2(q_{v}+1)}
, which depends on
2\mid q_{v}
or
2\nmid q_{v}
.

Hence we have

d(\St_{S})=\prod_{v\in S}d(\St_{v})=2^{n}\cdot 2^{-\delta_{F}(2,S)}\cdot\prod_{v\in S_{f}}\frac{q_{v}-1}{2(q_{v}+1)}
.

We let
\sigma_{\pi}
denote the
2
-cocycle of the projective representation which comes from an ordinary irreducible representation
\pi
.

Proposition 4.4
.

\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}),\sigma_{\St_{S}})}\St_{S}=\frac{2|\zeta_{F}(-1)|}{2^{|S|}}\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(q_{v}-1)

Proof
:
It is straightforward by Proposition
3.2
, Lemma
4.2
, and
4.3
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Given a global Jacquet-Langlands correspondence
\JL\colon\pi^{\prime}=\otimes_{v\in V}\pi^{\prime}_{v}

\mapsto

\pi=\otimes_{v\in V}\pi_{v}
, we have

\frac{\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}),\sigma_{\pi_{S}})}\pi_{S}}{\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PD^{\times}(\mathcal{O}_{S}),\sigma_{\pi^{\prime}_{S}})}\pi^{\prime}_{S}}=\frac{2|\zeta_{F}(-1)||\PD^{\times}(\mathcal{O}_{S})|}{2^{|S|}}\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(q_{v}-1)

Proof
:
By Proposition
3.1
,
\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PD^{\times}(\mathcal{O}_{S}),\sigma_{\pi^{\prime}_{S}})}\pi^{\prime}_{S}=\frac{\dim\pi^{\prime}_{S}}{|\PD^{\times}(\mathcal{O}_{S})|}
.
It suffice to prove
\frac{\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}),\sigma_{\pi_{S}})}\pi_{S}}{\dim\pi^{\prime}_{S}}=\frac{2|\zeta_{F}(-1)|}{2^{|S|}}\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(q_{v}-1)
.
By Lemma
2.4
, it is equivalent to
\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}),\sigma_{\St_{S}})}\St_{S}=\frac{2|\zeta_{F}(-1)|}{2^{|S|}}\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(q_{v}-1)
, which is obtained in Proposition
4.4
.

Let
\zeta_{D}
be the zeta function of
D
(see
[
29
, §3.2]
), which has a simple pole at
z=1
and is known to have a factorization:

\zeta_{D}(s)=\zeta_{F}(2s)\zeta_{F}(2s-1)\prod_{v\in S_{f}}(1-q_{v}^{1-2s})
.

Observe
|\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})/\PSL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})|=2
and
|\PGL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})/\PSL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})|=2^{|S|}
by Lemma
4.1
together with Dirichlet’s Theorem for
S
-units
[
23
, §5.4]
.
We may take the discrete subgroups
\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}))
and
D^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
in
D^{1}(\mathbb{A}_{1})=\prod_{v\in S}D^{1}(F_{v})
and obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume
\pi^{\prime}=\otimes_{v\in V}\pi^{\prime}_{v}\mapsto\pi=\otimes_{v\in V}\pi_{v}
as above, we have

\frac{\dim_{\mathcal{L}(\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S}))}\pi_{S}}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\pi^{\prime}_{S}}=\left\lvert\frac{\zeta_{D}(0)}{\zeta_{F}(0)}\right\rvert.

Please note
\SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})
is not center-free and thus not ICC while
\PSL(2,\mathcal{O}_{S})
is an ICC group by Proposition
3.3
.

Remark 4.7
.

\PD^{\times}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
is contained in a cyclic, dihedral or exceptional group
A_{4},S_{4},A_{5}
(see
[
30

A necessary condition that
\PD^{\times}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
is a cyclic group of order of

or a dihedral group of order

.
Hence there exists a constant

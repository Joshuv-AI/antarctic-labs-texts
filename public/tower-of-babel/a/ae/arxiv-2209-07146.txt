Enhancements in cross-temporal forecast reconciliation, with an application to solar irradiance forecasts
Tommaso Di Fonzo, Daniele Girolimetto

Abstract
In recent works by Yang et al. 2017a , Yang et al. 2017b , and Yagli et al. 2019 , geographical, temporal, and sequential deterministic reconciliation of hierarchical photovoltaic (PV) power generation have been considered for a simulated PV dataset in California. In the first two cases, the reconciliations are carried out in spatial and temporal domains separately. To further improve forecasting accuracy, in the third case these two reconciliation approaches are sequentially applied. During the replication of the forecasting experiment, some issues emerged about non-negativity and coherence (in space and/or in time) of the sequentially reconciled forecasts. Furthermore, while the accuracy improvement of the considered approaches over the benchmark persistence forecasts is clearly visible at any data granularity, we argue that an even better performance may be obtained by a thorough exploitation of cross-temporal hierarchies. To this end, in this paper the cross-temporal point forecast reconciliation approach is applied to generate non-negative, fully coherent (both in space and time) forecasts. In particular, (i) some useful relationships between two-step, iterative and simultaneous cross-temporal reconciliation procedures are for the first time established, (ii) non-negativity issues of the final reconciled forecasts are discussed and correctly dealt with in a simple and effective way, and (iii) the most recent cross-temporal reconciliation approaches proposed in literature are adopted. The normalised Root Mean Square Error is used to measure forecasting accuracy, and a statistical multiple comparison procedure is performed to rank the approaches. Besides assuring full coherence, and non-negativity of the reconciled forecasts, the results show that for the considered dataset, cross-temporal forecast reconciliation significantly improves on the sequential procedures proposed by Yagli et al. 2019 , at any cross-sectional level of the hierarchy and for any temporal granularity.

In this framework, by extending the seminal idea by
Hyndman et al. 2011

The purpose is to improve the accuracy of the initial forecasts by combining forecasts at different aggregation levels in space and time, and by incorporating in the final forecasts the information given by cross-sectional and temporal constraints.

\textbf{y}^{[1]}=\text{vec}\left({\textbf{Y}^{[1]}}^{\prime}\right)
,
and
R
is the temporal summing matrix (
Di Fonzo and Girolimetto 2021
).

2.3
Cross-temporal bottom-up reconciliation

Bottom-up is an old and classic approach in the forecast reconciliation literature (
Dunn et al. 1976
,
Dangerfield and Morris 1992
).
This approach simply consists in obtaining the upper-level series’ forecasts by summing-up the base forecasts of the bottom level series in the hierarchy.
While its representation is rather straightforward when only a single dimension (either space or time) is involved, it is instructive considering how ‘bottom-up’ works with cross-temporal hierarchies.
The cross-temporal bottom-up (ct
(bu)
) reconciliation of
k^{*}+m
hierarchical time series’ base forecasts at different time granularities, may be represented as:

\text{vec}\left(\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{ct}(bu)}^{\prime}\right)=\textbf{F}\text{vec}\left(\widehat{\textbf{B}}^{[1]\prime}\right)\quad\leftrightarrow\quad\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{\text{ct}(bu)}=\textbf{F}\widehat{\textbf{b}}^{[1]},

(2)

where
\textbf{F}=\textbf{S}\otimes\textbf{R}
is the cross-temporal summing matrix, with
\otimes
denoting the Kronecker product,
and

\widehat{\textbf{b}}^{[1]}=\text{vec}\left(\widehat{\textbf{B}}^{[1]\prime}\right)
is the vector containing the base forecasts of the high-frequency bottom time series. In
A
it is shown that the cross-temporal bottom-up reconciliation can be tought of as a two-step sequential reconciliation approach, where either cross-sectional reconciliation of the high-frequency bottom time series base forecasts is followed by temporal reconciliation, or
vice-versa
.
This observation opens the way to ‘partly bottom-up’ cross-temporal reconciliation approaches, where forecasts of the
n
time series for different time granularities, and aggregation coherent only along a single dimension, are subsequently cross-temporally reconciled
via
simple bottom-up according to the other dimension.
We call these cross-temporal forecast reconciliation approaches either ct
(rec_{te},bu_{cs}
), or ct
(rec_{cs},bu_{te}
),
where ‘
rec_{te}
’ and ‘
rec_{cs}
’ denote a generic forecast reconciliation approach in time and in space, respectively.

2.4
Regression-based cross-temporal reconciliation

Let us consider the multivariate regression model

\widehat{\textbf{Y}}=\textbf{Y}+\textbf{E},

where the involved matrices have each dimension
[n\times(k^{\ast}+m)]
and contain, respectively, the base (
\widehat{\textbf{Y}}
) and the target forecasts (
Y
), and the coherency errors (
E
) for the
n
component variables of the hierarchical time series of interest.
Consider now the vectorized version of the model, that is

\mathrm{vec}\left(\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{\prime}\right)=\mathrm{vec}\left(\textbf{Y}^{\prime}\right)+\mathrm{vec}\left(\textbf{E}^{\prime}\right)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\widehat{\textbf{y}}=\textbf{y}+\boldsymbol{\eta},

(3)

where
\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mathrm{vec}\left(\textbf{E}^{\prime}\right)

is the cross-temporal reconciliation error with zero mean and p.d. covariance matrix
\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}
.
Assuming
\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}
known,
the optimal combination reconciled forecasts
\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{\text{oct}}=\mathrm{vec}\left(\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{oct}}^{\prime}\right)
are found by linearly constrained minimization of the generalized least squares (GLS) objective function

\left(\textbf{y}-\widehat{\textbf{y}}\right)^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}^{-1}\left(\textbf{y}-\widehat{\textbf{y}}\right)\qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\;\textbf{H}^{\prime}\textbf{y}=\textbf{0},

(4)

where

\textbf{H}^{\prime}\textbf{y}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\textbf{U}^{\ast}\\
\textbf{I}_{n}\otimes\textbf{Z}\end{array}\right]\textbf{y}=\textbf{0}_{[n(k^{\ast}+m)\times 1]}

is a full row-rank cross-temporal zero-constraint matrix, with
\textbf{U}^{\ast}=\left[\textbf{0}_{(n_{a}m\times nk^{\ast})}\;\textbf{I}_{m}\otimes\textbf{U}^{\prime}\right]\textbf{P}
, and
P
is the
[n(k^{\ast}+m)\times n(k^{\ast}+m)]
commutation matrix, such that
\textbf{P}\mathrm{vec}(\textbf{Y})=\textbf{y}
(
Di Fonzo and Girolimetto 2021
).

The cross-temporally reconciled forecasts according to the projection approach solution (

van Erven and Cugliari 2015

Wickramasuriya et al. 2019

Panagiotelis et al. 2021

) are given by

\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{\text{oct}}=\left[\textbf{I}_{n(k^{\ast}+m)}-\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}\textbf{H}\left(\textbf{H}^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}\textbf{H}\right)^{-1}\textbf{H}^{\prime}\right]\widehat{\textbf{y}}=\textbf{M}_{ct}\widehat{\textbf{y}}.

(5)

where

\mathcal{I}_{T}

\textbf{b}^{[1]}=\mathrm{vec}\left(\textbf{B}^{[1]\prime}\right)

2.5
Heuristic and iterative cross-temporal reconciliation

Kourentzes and Athanasopoulos 2019
proposed an ensemble forecasting procedure (denoted KA), that exploits the simple averaging of different forecasts. It consists in the following steps (for further details, see
Di Fonzo and Girolimetto 2021
):

KA-Step 1

compute the temporally reconciled forecasts for each variable
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and arrange them in the
[n\times(k+m)]
matrix
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{te}}
;

KA-Step 2

starting from
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{te}}
, compute the time-by-time cross-sectional reconciled forecasts for all the temporal aggregation levels (
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{cs}}
), and collect all the
(n\times n)
projection matrices used to reconcile forecasts of
k
-level temporally aggregated time series,
\textbf{M}_{cs}^{[k]}
,
k\in\mathcal{K}
;

KA-Step 3

transform the step 1 forecasts once more, by computing time-by-time cross-sectional reconciled forecasts for all temporal aggregation levels using the
(n\times n)
matrix
\overline{\textbf{M}}
, given by the average of the matrices
\textbf{M}_{\text{cs}}^{[k]}
:

\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{KA}}=\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\textbf{M}_{\text{cs}}^{[k]}\right)\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{te}}=\overline{\textbf{M}}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{te}}.

Di Fonzo and Girolimetto 2021
presented an iterative approach, that produces cross-temporally reconciled forecasts by alternating forecast reconciliation along one dimension (cross-sectional or temporal), based on the first two steps of the KA approach. The iteration
j\geq 1
can be described as follows:

Step 1

compute the temporally reconciled forecasts (
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{te}}^{(j)}
) for each variable
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
of
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{cs}}^{(j-1)}
;

Step 2

compute the time-by-time cross-sectional reconciled forecasts (
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}^{(j)}_{\text{cs}}
) for all the temporal aggregation levels of
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{te}}^{(j)}
.

At
j=0
, the starting values are given by
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{\text{cs}}^{(0)}=\widehat{\textbf{Y}}
, and the iterates end when the entries of matrix
\textbf{D}_{\text{te}}=\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}^{(j)\prime}_{\text{cs}}
, containing all the temporal discrepancies, are small enough according to a suitable convergence criterion
9
9

9

Denoting
d_{\text{te}}
a scalar measure of the gross temporal discrepancies contained in the matrix
\textbf{D}_{\text{te}}

d_{\text{te}}<\delta
, where
\delta
is a positive tolerance value.
Di Fonzo and Girolimetto 2021
propose to use

d_{\text{te}}=\left\|\textbf{D}_{\text{te}}\right\|_{1}
, where
||\textbf{X}||_{1}=\sum_{i,l}|x_{i,l}|
. When the number of series and the time granularities increase, this criterion may be too demanding, resulting in more iterations than necessary to converge. An alternative choice, avalable in
FoReco
(
Girolimetto and Di Fonzo 2022
), and used in this paper, is setting

, where
||\textbf{X}||_{\infty}=\max|x_{i,l}|
.
.
The order of the dimensions to be reconciled in the two steps within each iteration is arbitrary (on this point, see
Di Fonzo and Girolimetto 2021
): in the description above,

reconciliation is iteratively performed (ite

3
Cross-temporal coherency of sequential approaches and some remarkable equivalences

In order to exploit both cross-sectional and temporal hierarchies,
Yagli et al. 2019
consider two sequential reconciliation approaches: Spatial-then-Temporal-Reconciliation (STR), and Temporal-then-Spatial-Reconciliation (TSR).
In the former case, cross-sectional reconciliation of the base forecasts is performed first for any temporal granularity, followed by the temporal reconciliation of the individual series’ forecasts. In the latter case, the order of application of the two reconciliation approaches is reversed, temporal reconciliation being performed first, followed by cross-sectional reconciliation.
In general, one would expect that the forecasts obtained this way are different (i.e.,

\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{TSR}\neq\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{TSR}
),
and either cross-sectionally (TSR) or temporally (STR), but not cross-temporally, reconciled:

\textbf{U}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{TSR}=\textbf{0}_{[n_{a}\times(k^{*}+m)]},\quad\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{TSR}\neq\textbf{0}_{(k^{*}\times n)},\qquad\textbf{U}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{STR}\neq\textbf{0}_{[n_{a}\times(k^{*}+m)]},\quad\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{STR}=\textbf{0}_{(k^{*}\times n)}.

1

5.1
Non negative forecast reconciliation: sntz

Each approach, when used in its ‘free’ version, i.e., without considering non-negative constraints in the linearly constrained quadratic program (
4
), is not guaranteed to always produce non-negative reconciled forecasts (see section
4.1
). This fact may be an issue for the analyst, since in many practical situations negative forecasts could have no meaning, thus undermining the quality of the results found and the conclusions thereof.
In what follows, we consider a simple heuristic strategy to avoid negative reconciled forecasts, without using any sophisticated, and time consuming, numerical optimization procedure
15
15

15

Recent contributions on this topic in the hierarchical forecasting field are
Wickramasuriya et al. 2020
and
Girolimetto and Di Fonzo 2022
.
.
More precisely, possible negative values of the free reconciled high-frequency bottom time series forecasts are set to zero. Denote
\widetilde{\textbf{B}}^{[1]}_{0}
the matrix containing the non-negative reconciled forecasts produced by a ‘free’ approach, and the ‘zeroed’ ones. The complete vector of non-negative cross-temporal reconciled forecasts is computed as the cross-temporal bottom-up aggregation of
\widetilde{\textbf{b}}^{[1]}_{0}=\text{vec}\left[\left(\widetilde{\textbf{B}}^{[1]}_{0}\right)^{\prime}\right]
, that is, according to expression (
2
):

\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{0}=\textbf{F}\widetilde{\textbf{b}}^{[1]}_{0}.

We call set-negative-to-zero (sntz) this simple, and quick device to obtain non negative reconciled forecasts. While it certainly increases the forecasting accuracy of the high-frequency bottom time series forecasts wrt the ‘free’ counterparts, this does not hold true in general for the upper level series forecasts. In addition, even if the originally reconciled forecasts are obtained according to an unbiased approach, the sntz-reconciled forecasts are no more unbiased, like the non-negative forecasts obtained through numerical optimization procedures (
Wickramasuriya et al. 2020
). However, in practical situations the differences between the results produced by the sntz heuristic, and those obtained through a numerical optimization procedure could be negligible. For example, for the dataset and the forecasting experiment in hand, we have found that the forecasting performance of the non-negative reconciliation approaches through the non-linear optimization procedure
osqp
(
Stellato et al. 2020
) implemented in
FoReco
(
Girolimetto and Di Fonzo 2022
), is basically the same as the one obtained with the sntz heuristic.

6
Conclusion

Renewable energy is providing increasingly more energy to the grid all over the world. But grid operators must carefully manage the balance between the generation and consumption of energy to make the best use of abundant renewable energy.
For an ISO, this provides greater grid stability, higher revenue and better use of what sun is available at any one time.
Better short-term solar energy forecasts mean lower-emissions, cheaper energy and a more stable electricity grid.
Solar forecasting is thus a key tool to achieve these results.
In this paper, cross-temporal point forecast reconciliation has been applied to generate non-negative, fully coherent (both in space and time) forecasts of PV generated power. Both methodological and practical issues have been tackled, in order to develop effective and easy-to-handle cross-temporal forecasting approaches.
Besides assuring both spatial and temporal coherence, and non-negativity of the reconciled forecasts, the results show that for the considered dataset, cross-temporal forecast reconciliation significantly improves on
the sequential procedures proposed by
Yagli et al. 2019
, at any cross-sectional level of the hierarchy and for any temporal aggregation order.
However, these findings should be considered neither conclusive, nor valid in general. Other forecasting experiments, through simulations and using other datasets, as well as in solar forecasting and in other application fields, should be performed to empirically robustify the results shown so far.
In addition, other research queries could raise in the field of cross-temporal PV forecast reconciliation, like
using reduced temporal hierarchies, as well as forecasting through appropriate Machine Learning end-to-end approaches (
Stratigakos et al. 2022
), and probabilistic instead of deterministic (point) forecasting (
Panamtash and Zhou 2018
,
Jeon et al. 2019
,
Panagiotelis et al. 2022
,
Yang 2020a
,
Yang 2020b
,
Ben Taieb et al. 2021
).
All these topics are left for future research.

Cross-sectional bottom-up reconciliation of
k^{*}+m
hierarchical time series’ base forecasts

The cross-sectional bottom-up (
cs(bu)
) reconciliation of hierarchical time series’ base forecasts at different temporal granularities (temporal aggregation levels)
k\in\mathcal{K}
, may be represented as follows:

\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{\tau,cs(bu)}^{[k]}=\textbf{S}\widehat{\textbf{b}}_{\tau}^{[k]},\quad\tau=1,\ldots,\frac{m}{k},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}\quad\rightarrow\quad\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs(bu)}^{[k]}=\textbf{S}\widehat{\textbf{B}}^{[k]},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

In compact notation we have:

\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs(bu)}=\textbf{S}\widehat{\textbf{B}}\quad\rightarrow\quad\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs(bu)}^{\prime}=\widehat{\textbf{B}}^{\prime}\textbf{S}^{\prime}\quad\rightarrow\quad\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{cs(bu)}=\left(\textbf{S}\otimes\textbf{I}_{k^{*}+m}\right)\widehat{\textbf{b}},

where
\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{cs(bu)}=\text{vec}\left(\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs(bu)}^{\prime}\right)
, and
\widehat{\textbf{b}}=\text{vec}\left(\widehat{\textbf{B}}^{\prime}\right)
.

The
cs(bu)
reconciled forecasts are obviously cross-sectionally coherent, but in general they are not temporally coherent:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\textbf{U}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs(bu)}=\textbf{0}_{[n_{a}\times(k^{*}+m)]}&\leftrightarrow&\left(\textbf{U}^{\prime}\otimes\textbf{I}_{k^{*}+m}\right)\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{cs(bu)}=\textbf{0}_{[n_{a}(k^{*}+m)\times 1]}\\
\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs(bu)}^{\prime}\neq\textbf{0}_{[k^{*}\times n]}&\leftrightarrow&\left(\textbf{I}_{n}\otimes\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\right)\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{cs(bu)}\neq\textbf{0}_{[k^{*}n\times 1]}\end{array}.

Temporal bottom-up reconciliation of
n
individual time series’ base forecasts

The temporal bottom-up (
te(bu)
) reconciliation of
n
individual time series’ base forecasts at different temporal granularities (temporal aggregation levels), may be represented as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}\widetilde{\textbf{a}}_{i,te(bu)}=\textbf{R}\widehat{\textbf{a}}_{i}^{[1]},\quad i=1,\ldots,n_{a}\\
\widetilde{\textbf{b}}_{j,te(bu)}=\textbf{R}\widehat{\textbf{b}}_{j}^{[1]},\quad j=1,\ldots,n_{b}\\
\end{array}\quad\rightarrow\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\widetilde{\textbf{A}}_{te(bu)}=\textbf{R}\widehat{\textbf{A}}^{[1]\prime}\\
\widetilde{\textbf{B}}_{te(bu)}=\textbf{R}\widehat{\textbf{B}}^{[1]\prime}\\
\end{array}

and, finally,

\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{te(bu)}^{\prime}=\textbf{R}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{[1]\prime}\quad\rightarrow\quad\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{te(bu)}=\left(\textbf{I}_{n}\otimes\textbf{R}\right)\widehat{\textbf{y}}^{[1]}.

The
te(bu)
reconciled forecasts are obviously temporally coherent, but in general they are not cross-sectionally coherent:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{te(bu)}^{\prime}=\textbf{0}_{[k^{*}\times n]}&\leftrightarrow&\left(\textbf{I}_{n}\otimes\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\right)\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{te(bu)}=\textbf{0}_{[k^{*}n\times 1]}\\
\textbf{U}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{te(bu)}\neq\textbf{0}_{[n_{a}\times(k^{*}+m)]}&\leftrightarrow&\left(\textbf{U}^{\prime}\otimes\textbf{I}_{k^{*}+m}\right)\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{te(bu)}\neq\textbf{0}_{[n_{a}(k^{*}+m)\times 1]}\end{array}.

n

(8)

where
\widehat{\textbf{b}}^{[1]}=\text{vec}\left(\widehat{\textbf{B}}^{[1]\prime}\right)

Let us consider now the coherency checks for the

•

cross-sectional coherency

\left(\textbf{U}^{\prime}\otimes\textbf{I}_{k^{*}+m}\right)\left(\textbf{S}\otimes\textbf{R}\right)\widehat{\textbf{b}}^{[1]}=\left(\textbf{U}^{\prime}\textbf{S}\otimes\textbf{R}\right)\widehat{\textbf{b}}^{[1]}=\textbf{0}_{[n_{a}(k^{*}+m)\times 1]},

\textbf{U}^{\prime}\textbf{S}=\left[\textbf{I}_{n_{a}}\;-\textbf{C}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\textbf{C}\\

Appendix B
Proof of the theorem
1

1.

Let
\textbf{U}^{\prime}_{n_{a}\times n}
and
\textbf{Z}^{\prime}_{m\times(k^{\ast}+m)}
be the cross-sectional and temporal, respectively, zero constraints kernel matrix. Let

\textbf{M}_{cs}^{[k]}=\textbf{M}_{cs}=\left[\textbf{I}_{n}-\textbf{W}\textbf{U}\left(\textbf{U}^{\prime}\textbf{W}\textbf{U}\right)^{-1}\textbf{U}^{\prime}\right],\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

and

\textbf{M}_{te,i}=\textbf{M}_{te}=\left[\textbf{I}_{k^{\ast}+m}-\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}\textbf{Z}\left(\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}\textbf{Z}\right)^{-1}\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\right],\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

be the cross-sectional and temporal projection matrices, respectively, such that:

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}^{[k]}&=&\textbf{M}_{cs}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{[k]},&k\in\mathcal{K},\\
\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{te,i}&=&\textbf{M}_{te}\widehat{\textbf{y}}_{i},&i=1,\ldots,n,\end{array}

where

\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{[k]}
and
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}^{[k]}
are the
\left(n\times\frac{m}{k}\right)
matrices with, respectively, the base and the cross-sectional reconciled forecasts of the
n
series at time granularity
k
, and

\widehat{\textbf{y}}_{i}
and
\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{te,i}
are the
\left[(k^{*}+m)\times 1\right]
vectors with, respectively, the base and the temporal reconciled forecasts at all time granularities for the
i
-th series.
In compact matrix form we get:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}&=&\left[\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}^{[m]}\;\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}^{[k_{p-1}]}\ldots\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}^{[k_{2}]}\;\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}^{[1]}\right]=\textbf{M}_{cs}\left[\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{[m]}\;\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{[k_{p-1}]}\ldots\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{[k_{2}]}\;\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{[1]}\right]=\textbf{M}_{cs}\widehat{\textbf{Y}},\\
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{te}&=&\left\{\textbf{M}_{te}\left[\widehat{\textbf{y}}_{1}\ldots\widehat{\textbf{y}}_{i}\ldots\widehat{\textbf{y}}_{n}\right]\right\}^{\prime}=\left\{\textbf{M}_{te}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{\prime}\right\}^{\prime}.\end{array}

Then,
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cst}
is obtained by first operating a cross-sectional reconciliation (step 1,
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}
), and then reconciling via temporal hierarchies the forecasts obtained in the previous step (step 2,
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cst}
). In matrix terms:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\text{step 1 }&\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}&=\textbf{M}_{cs}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\\
\text{step 2 }&\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cst}&=\left\{\textbf{M}_{te}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}^{\prime}\right\}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cs}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}=\textbf{M}_{cs}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}\end{array}

where
\widehat{\textbf{Y}}
is the (
n\times(k^{\ast}+m)
) base forecasts matrix.
Viceversa,
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}
is obtained by first operating a temporal reconciliation (step 1,
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{te}
), and finally a cross-sectional reconciliation is applied (step 2):

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\text{step 1 }&\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{te}&=\left\{\textbf{M}_{te}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{\prime}\right\}^{\prime}=\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}\\
\text{step 2 }&\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}&=\textbf{M}_{cs}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{te}=\textbf{M}_{cs}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cst}.\end{array}

The forecasts
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}
(and
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cst}
) (a single two-step iteration) are cross-temporally reconciled because:

\displaystyle\textbf{U}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}

\displaystyle=\textbf{U}^{\prime}\textbf{M}_{cs}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}=\left[\textbf{U}^{\prime}-\textbf{U}^{\prime}\textbf{W}\textbf{U}\left(\textbf{U}^{\prime}\textbf{W}\textbf{U}\right)^{-1}\textbf{U}^{\prime}\right]\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}=\left[\textbf{U}^{\prime}-\textbf{U}^{\prime}\right]\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}=\textbf{0}_{n_{a}\times(k^{\ast}+m)}

and

\displaystyle\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\textbf{M}_{te}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{\prime}\textbf{M}_{cs}^{\prime}=\left[\textbf{Z}^{\prime}-\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}\textbf{Z}\left(\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}\textbf{Z}\right)^{-1}\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\right]\textbf{M}_{cs}^{\prime}=\left[\textbf{Z}^{\prime}-\textbf{Z}^{\prime}\right]\widehat{\textbf{Y}}^{\prime}\textbf{M}_{cs}^{\prime}=\textbf{0}_{m\times n}.

2.

Using matrix vectorization and structural representation for the cross-temporal reconciliation problem we have:

\begin{aligned} \widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{oct}=\textbf{F}\left(\textbf{F}^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}^{-1}\textbf{F}\right)^{-1}\textbf{F}^{\prime}\widehat{\textbf{y}}\end{aligned},

where
\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{oct}=\mathrm{vec}\left(\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{oct}^{\prime}\right)
,
\widehat{\textbf{y}}=\mathrm{vec}\left(\widehat{\textbf{Y}}_{oct}^{\prime}\right)
, and
\textbf{F}=\textbf{S}\otimes\textbf{R}
is the [
n(k^{\ast}+m)\times mn_{b}
] cross-temporal summing matrix. Let
\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}=\textbf{W}\otimes\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}
be the cross-temporal error covariance matrix, by exploiting the properties of the Kronecker product (
Henderson and Searle 1981
;
Harville 2008
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{oct}

\displaystyle=\textbf{F}\left(\textbf{F}^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}^{-1}\textbf{F}\right)^{-1}\textbf{F}^{\prime}\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{ct}^{-1}\widehat{\textbf{y}}

temporal then cross-sectional
iterative procedure and the vectorization
\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{tcs}=\mathrm{vec}\left(\widehat{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{tcs}

Finally,
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}=\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{oct}
and, following from point 1,
\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}=\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cst}=\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{oct}
.

The heuristic KA with constant cross-sectional and temporal error covariance matrices

In the
Kourentzes and Athanasopoulos 2019
approach (section
2.5
), the temporally reconciled predictions at step 1,
\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}
, are cross-sectionally reconciled via premultiplication by the matrix
\overline{\textbf{M}}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\textbf{M}^{[k]}_{cs}
.
As
\textbf{W}^{[k]}=\textbf{W}
,
k\in\mathcal{K}
,

\textbf{M}^{[k]}_{cs}=\textbf{M}_{cs}
, and
\overline{\textbf{M}}=\textbf{M}_{cs}
.
Then,

\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{KA}=\overline{\textbf{M}}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}=\textbf{M}_{cs}\widehat{\textbf{Y}}\textbf{M}_{te}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{cst}=\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{tcs}.

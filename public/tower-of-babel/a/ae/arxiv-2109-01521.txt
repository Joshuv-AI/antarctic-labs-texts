Stabilization of the damped plate equation under general boundary conditions
Jérôme Le Rousseau, Emmanuel Wend-Benedo Zongo

Abstract
. We consider a damped plate equation on an open bounded subset of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , or a smooth manifold, with boundary, along with general boundary operators fulfilling the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition. The damping term acts on a region without imposing a geometrical condition. We derive a resolvent estimate for the generator of the damped plate semigroup that yields a logarithmic decay of the energy of the solution to the plate equation. The resolvent estimate is a consequence of a Carleman inequality obtained for the bi-Laplace operator involving a spectral parameter under the considered boundary conditions. The derivation goes first though microlocal estimates, then local estimates, and finally a global estimate. Keywords: Carleman estimates; stabilization; Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition; resolvent estimate.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded connected open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or a smooth bounded connected
d
-dimensional manifold, with smooth
boundary
\partial\Omega
, where we consider a damped plate equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{t}y+\Delta^{2}y+\alpha(x)\partial_{t}y=0&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
B_{1}y_{|\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega}=B_{2}y_{|\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega}=0,\\
y_{|t=0}=y^{0},\ \ \partial_{t}y_{|t=0}=y^{1},\end{cases}

where
\alpha\geq 0
and
where
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
denote two boundary differential operators.
The damping property is provided by
+\alpha(x)\partial_{t}
thus
referred as the
damping term
.
As introduced below
\Delta^{2}
is the bi-Laplace operator, that is,
the square of the Laplace operator. Here, it is associated with a
smooth metric
g
to be introduced below; it is thus rather the
bi-Laplace-Beltrami operator. This equation appears in models for the description of mechanical vibrations of
thin domains. The two boundary operators are of
k_{j}
,
j=1,2
respectively, yet at most of order
3
in the direction
normal to the boundary. They
are chosen such that the two following properties are fulfilled:

(1)

the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro boundary condition holds (this condition is fully
described in what follows);

(2)

along with the homogeneous boundary conditions given above the
bi-Laplace operator is self-adjoint and nonnegative. This guarantees the
conservation of the energy of the solution in the case of a
damping free equation, that is, if
\alpha=0
.

We are concerned with the decay of the energy of the solution in the
case
\alpha
is not identically zero. We shall prove that the damping
term yields a stabilization property: the energy decays to zero as
time
t\to\infty
and we shall prove that the decay rate is at least logarithmic.

1.1.
On the stabilization of waves and plates

If no geometrical condition is imposed on the damping region, here as
given by the
support of the function
\alpha
one cannot expect a exponential decay
rate as observed in the case this region fulfills the celebrated
Rauch-Taylor condition, often coined GCC for geometrical control
condition
[
40
,
6
]
. The GCC expresses that all rays of
geometrical optics reach the damping region
\{\alpha>0\}
in a finite time.
Here, with no such condition, a logarithmic decay rate is quite
natural if one has in mind the equivalent result obtained for the wave
equation in the works of G. Lebeau
[
36
]
and G. Lebeau and
L. Robbiano
[
38
]
. We also refer to Chapter 6 in

[
33
]
and Chapters 10 and 11 in
[
34
]
where the
result of
[
36
,
38
]
are reviewed and generalized in
particular on the framework of general boundary conditions as those we
consider here. See also the work of P. Cornilleau and L. Robbiano for
a quite exotic boundary condition, namely the Zaremba condition.

Among the existing results available in the literature for plate type
equations, many of them concern the “hinged” boundary conditions,
that is,
u_{|\partial\Omega}=0
and
\Delta u_{|\partial\Omega}=0
.
We first mention these result. An important result obtained in

[
25
]
on the controllability of the plate equation on a
rectangle domain with an arbitrarily small control domain. The method
relies on the generalization of Ingham type inequalities in

[
27
]
. An exponential stabilization result, in the same
geometry, can be found in
[
39
]
, using similar techniques. In

[
39
]
the localized damping term involves the time derivative

\partial_{t}y
as in (
1.1
). Interior nonlinear
feedbacks can be used for exponential stabilization

[
43
]
. There, feedbacks are localized in a neighborhood of
part of the boundary that fulfills multiplier-type conditions. A
general analysis of nonlinear damping that includes the plate equation
is provided in
[
2
]
under multiplier-type conditions. For
“hinged” boundary conditions also, with a boundary damping term, we
cite
[
4
]
where, on a square domain, a necessary and
sufficient condition is provided for exponential stabilization.

Note that under “hinged” boundary conditions the bi-Laplace operator
is precisely the square of the Dirichlet-Laplace operator. This makes
its mathematical analysis much easier, in particular where using
spectral properties, and this explains why this type of boundary
conditions appears very frequently in the mathematical literature.

A more challenging type of boundary condition is the so-called
“clamped” boundary conditions,
that is,
u_{|\partial\Omega}=0
and
\partial_{\nu}u_{|\partial\Omega}=0
,
for which few results are available. We
cite
[
1
]
, where a general analysis of nonlinearly damped
systems that includes the plate equation
under multiplier-type conditions is provided. In
[
3
]
, the
analysis of discretized general nonlinearly damped system is also
carried out, with the plate equation as an application. In
[
44
]
, a nonlinear
damping involving the p-
Laplacian
is used also under multiplier-type
conditions. In
[
15
]
, an
exponential decay is obtained in the case of “clamped” boundary
conditions, yet with a damping term of the Kelvin-Voigt type, that is
of the form
\partial_{t}\Delta y
, that acts over the whole domain.
In the case of the “clamped” boundary conditions, the logarithmic-type stabilization
result we obtain here was proven in
[
35
]
. The present article
thus stands as a generalization of the stabilization result of

[
35
]
if considering a whole class of boundary condition instead
of specializing to a certain type. The present work contains in
particular also the case of “hinged” boundary conditions.

1.2.
Method

Following the works of
[
36
,
38
,
35
]
we obtain a
logarithmic decay rate for the energy of the solution to (
1.1
)
by means of a resolvent estimate
for the generator of the semigroup associated with the damped plate
equation (
1.1
). This estimate follows from a Carleman inequality
derived for the operator
P_{\sigma}=\Delta^{2}-\sigma^{4}
where
\sigma
is a
spectral parameter for the generator of the semigroup.

Our first goal is thus the derivation of the Carleman inequality for
the operator
P_{\sigma}
near the boundary under the boundary conditions
given by
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

Then, from the Carleman estimate one deduces an observation inequality
for the operator
P_{\sigma}
in the case of the prescribed boundary
conditions. The resolvent estimate then follows from this observation inequality.

1.3.
On Carleman estimates

A Carleman estimate is a weighted
a priori
inequality for the solutions of a partial partial differential equation, where the weight is of exponential type.
For instance, for a partial differential operator
P
away from the boundary, it takes the form

\displaystyle{\|e^{\tau\varphi}u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C{\|e^{\tau\varphi}Pu\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
for
\varphi
well
chosen and some
\tau_{0}
chosen sufficiently large. The exponential weight
function involves a parameter
\tau
that can be taken as large as
desired, making Carleman inequalities very powerful estimates.
Additional terms on the left-hand side of the inequality can be
obtained, including higher-order derivatives of the function
u
,
depending of course of the order of the operator
P
itself.
For a second-order elliptic operator such as the Laplace operator one
has

\displaystyle\tau^{3/2}{\|e^{\tau\varphi}u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\tau^{1/2}{\|e^{\tau\varphi}Du\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\tau^{-1/2}\sum_{|\beta|=2}{\|e^{\tau\varphi}D^{\beta}u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C{\|e^{\tau\varphi}\Delta u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

under the so-called sub-ellipticity condition; see Chapter 3 in

[
33
]
. Note that the power of the large parameter
\tau

adds to
3/2
with the order of the derivative in each term on the
left-hand side. In fact, in the calculus used to derive such estimates
one power of
\tau
is equivalent to a derivative of order one. Thus
with this
3/2
compared with the order two of the operator one says
that one looses a half-derivative in the estimate.

This type of estimate was used for the first time by T. Carleman

[
13
]
to achieve uniqueness properties for the Cauchy
problem of an elliptic operator. Later, A.-P. Calderón and
L. Hörmander further developed Carleman’s method

[
12
,
20
]
. To this day, the method based on Carleman estimates
remains essential to prove unique continuation properties;
see for instance
[
45
]
for an overview. On such questions,
more recent advances have been concerned with differential operators
with singular potentials, starting with the contribution of D. Jerison
and C. Kenig
[
26
]
. There, Carleman estimates rely on

L^{p}
-norms rather than
L^{2}
-norms as in the estimates
above. The proof of such
L^{p}
Carleman estimates is very
delicate. The reader is also referred to
[
42
,
29
,
30
,
16
,
31
]
.
In more recent years, the field of applications of Carleman estimates
has gone beyond the original domain; they are also used in the study
of:

•

Inverse problems, where Carleman estimates are used to obtain
stability estimates for the unknown sought quantity (for instance
coefficient, source term) with respect to norms on measurements
performed on the solution of the PDE, see for instance

[
11
,
24
,
32
,
23
]
; Carleman estimates are also
fundamental in the construction of complex geometrical optic solutions
that lead to the resolution of inverse problems such as the Calderón
problem with partial data
[
28
,
17
]
.

•

Control theory for PDEs; Carleman estimates yield the null controllability of linear parabolic equations

[
37
]
and the null controllability of classes of semi-linear
parabolic equations
[
19
,
5
,
18
]
.
They can also be used to prove unique continuation properties, that in
turn are crucial for the treatment of low frequencies for exact
controllability results for hyperbolic equations as in
[
6
]
.

For a function supported near a point at the boundary, in normal geodesic coordinates where
\Omega
is locally given by
\{x_{d}>0\}
(see Section
1.4
below) the estimate
can take the form

\displaystyle\sum_{|\beta|\leq 2}\tau^{3/2-|\beta|}{\|e^{\tau\varphi}D^{\beta}u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{|\beta|\leq 1}\tau^{3/2-|\beta|}{|e^{\tau\varphi}D^{\beta}u_{|x_{d}=0^{+}}|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C{\|e^{\tau\varphi}\Delta u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This is the type of estimate we seek here for the operator
P_{\sigma}
,
with some uniformity with respect to
\sigma
.

1.4.
Geometrical setting

On
\Omega
we consider a Riemannian metric
g_{x}=(g_{ij}(x))
, with
associated cometric
(g^{ij}(x))=(g_{x})^{-1}
.
It stands as a bilinear form that act on vector fields,

\displaystyle g_{x}(u_{x},v_{x})=g_{ij}(x)u_{x}^{i}v_{x}^{j},\quad u_{x}=u_{x}^{i}\partial_{x_{i}},\ v_{x}=v_{x}^{i}\partial_{x_{i}}.

For
x\in\partial\Omega
we denote by
\nu_{x}
the unit outward
pointing
normal vector
at
x
, unitary in the sense of the
metric
g
, that is

\displaystyle g_{x}(\nu_{x},\nu_{x})=1\ \ \text{and}\ \ g_{x}(\nu_{x},u_{x})=1\quad\forall u_{x}\in T_{x}\partial\Omega.

We denote by
\partial_{\nu}
the associated derivative at the boundary,
that is,
\partial_{\nu}f(x)=\nu_{x}(f)
.
We also denote by
n_{x}
the unit outward
pointing
conormal vector
at
x
, that is,

n_{x}=\nu_{x}^{\flat}
, that is,
(n_{x})_{i}=g_{ij}\nu_{x}^{j}
.

Near a boundary point we shall often use normal geodesic coordinates
where
\Omega
is locally given by
\{x_{d}>0\}
and the
metric
g
takes the form

\displaystyle g=dx^{d}\otimes dx^{d}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq d-1}g_{ij}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j}.

Then, the vector field
\nu_{x}
is locally given by
(0,\dots,0,-1)
. The same for the one form
n_{x}
.

Normal geodesic coordinates allow us to locally formulate
boundary problems in a half-space geometry. We write

\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ x_{d}>0\}\qquad\text{where}\ x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\ \text{with}\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},x_{d}\in\mathbb{R}.

We shall naturally denote its closure by
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
, that is,

\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};x_{d}\geq 0\}
.

The Laplace-Beltrami operator
is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g}f)(x)=(\det g_{x})^{-1/2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\partial_{x_{i}}\big((\det g_{x})^{1/2}g^{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}f\big)(x).

in local coordinates. Its principal part is given by

\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}g^{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}
and
its principal symbol by
\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}g^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
.

The bi-Laplace operator is
P=\Delta^{2}_{g}
. In the main text of the
article we shall write
\Delta
,
\Delta^{2}
in place of
\Delta_{g}
,
\Delta_{g}^{2}
.

1.5.
Main results

1.5.1.
Carleman estimate

We state the main Carleman estimate for the operator
P_{\sigma}
in
normal geodesic coordinates as presented in Section
1.4
. A point
x^{0}\in\partial\Omega
is
considered and a weight function
\varphi
is assumed to be defined
locally and
such that

(1)

\partial_{d}\varphi\geq C>0
locally.

(2)

(\Delta\pm\sigma^{2},\varphi)
satisfies the sub-ellipticity
condition of Definition
6.1
locally. This is a necessary and
sufficient condition for a Carleman estimate to hold for a
second-order operator
\Delta\pm\sigma^{2}
, regardless of
boundary conditions
[
33
, Chapters 3 and 4]
.

(3)

(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2},\varphi)
satisfies the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition of Definition
4.1
at
\varrho^{\prime}=(x^{0},\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)

for all
(\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
such that
\tau\geq\kappa_{0}\sigma
, for some
\kappa_{0}>0
. This means that the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds after the conjugation of the operator
P_{\sigma}

and the boundary operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
by the weight function

\exp(\tau\varphi)
.

Theorem 1.1

(Carleman estimate)
.

Let
\kappa_{0}^{\prime}>\kappa_{0}>0
. Let
x^{0}\in\partial\Omega
.
Let
\varphi
be such that the properties above hold locally.
Then, there exists
W^{0}
a neighborhood of
x^{0}
,
C>0
,
\tau_{0}>0
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\tau^{-1/2}{\|e^{\tau\varphi}u\|}_{4,\tau}+{|\trace(e^{\tau\varphi}u)|}_{3,1/2,\tau}\leq C\big({\|e^{\tau\varphi}P_{\sigma}u\|}_{+}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}{|e^{\tau\varphi}B_{j}v_{|x_{d}=0^{+}}|}_{7/2-k_{j},\tau}\big),

for
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
,

\kappa_{0}\sigma\leq\tau\leq\kappa_{0}^{\prime}\sigma
, and
u\in\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(W^{0}_{+})
.

The volume norm is given by

\displaystyle{\|e^{\tau\varphi}u\|}_{4,\tau}=\sum_{|\beta|\leq 4}\tau^{4-|\beta|}{\|e^{\tau\varphi}D^{\beta}u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The trace norm is given by

\displaystyle{|\trace(e^{\tau\varphi}u)|}_{3,1/2,\tau}=\sum_{0\leq n\leq 3}{|\partial_{\nu}^{n}(e^{\tau\varphi}u)_{|x_{d}=0^{+}}|}_{7/2-n,\tau},

where the norm
{|.|}_{7/2-n,\tau}
is the
L^{2}
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}

after applying the Fourier multiplier
(\tau^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{7/4-n/2}
. These norms are well described in Section
2.3
.

Observe that the Carleman estimate of Theorem
1.1
exhibits a loss of a half-derivative. A
more precise statement is given in Theorem
7.3
in Section
7.2
.

1.5.2.
Stabilization result

Let
(\mathsf{P}_{0},D(\mathsf{P}_{0}))
be the unbounded operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
given by the domain

(1.4)

\displaystyle D(\mathsf{P}_{0})=\big\{u\in H^{4}(\Omega);\ B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=0\big\},

given by
\mathsf{P}_{0}u=\Delta^{2}u
for
u\in D(\mathsf{P}_{0})
. As written above
the two boundary differential operators are such that
(\mathsf{P}_{0},D(\mathsf{P}_{0}))

is self-adjoint and nonnegative.

Let
y(t)
be a strong solution of the plate equation (
1.1
). A precise definition of strong
solutions is given in Section
9.3
. One has
y^{0}\in D(\mathsf{P}_{0})
and

y^{1}\in D(\mathsf{P}_{0}^{1/2})
. Its energy is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(y)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\big({\|\partial_{t}y(t)\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(\mathsf{P}_{0}y(t),y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).

Theorem 1.2

(logarithmic stabilization for the damped plate equation)
.

There exists
C>0
such that for any such strong solution to the
damped plate equation (
1.1
) one
has

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(y)(t)\leq\frac{C}{\big(\log(2+t)\big)^{4}}\big({\|\mathsf{P}_{0}y^{0}+\alpha y^{1}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+{\|\mathsf{P}_{0}^{1/2}y^{1}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big).

A more precise and more general statement is given in Theorem
10.3
in Section
10.2
.

1.6.
Some open questions

1.6.1.
Boundary damping

Here, we have considered a damping that acts in the interior of the
domain
\Omega
. The study of boundary damping, as in
[
38
]
for
the wave equation, is also of relevance.
Yet we foresee that it requires to specify more the used boundary
operators. This was not our goal here as we wished to treat general
boundary operators here.

1.6.2.
Spectral inequality

If the boundary operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are well chosen, the
bi-Laplace operator
\Delta^{2}
can be selfadjoint on
L^{2}(\Omega)
;
see Section
9.1
. Associated with
the operator is then a Hilbert basis
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of

L^{2}(\Omega)
. In the case of “clamped” boundary condition the
following spectral inequality was proven in
[
35
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(Spectral inequality for the “clamped” bi-Laplace
operator)
.

Let
\O
be an open subset of
\Omega
.
There exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{C\mu^{1/4}}{\|u\|}_{L^{2}(\O)},\qquad\mu>0,\quad u\in\Span\{\phi_{j};\ \mu_{j}\leq\mu\}.

The proof of this theorem is based on a Carleman inequality for the
fourth-order elliptic operator
D_{s}^{4}+\Delta^{2}
, that is, after the
addition of a variable
s
. Extending this strategy to the type of
boundary conditions treated here was not successful because it is not
guaranteed that having the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
\Delta^{2}
,
B_{1}
, and

B_{2}
implies that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
D_{s}^{4}+\Delta^{2}
,
B_{1}
, and

B_{2}
. Yet, the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition is at the heart of the proof of our
Carleman estimate. Proving a spectral estimate as in the above
statement for the general boundary conditions considered here is
an open question.

1.6.3.
Quantification of the unique continuation property

For a second-order operator like the Laplace operator
\Delta
and a
boundary operator
B
of order
k
(yet of order at most one in the
normal direction) such that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro holds one can derive the
following inequality that locally quantifies the unique continuation property
up to the boundary; see
[
33
, Lemma 9.2]
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
x^{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
V
be a neighborhood of
x^{0}
where the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds. There exist
W
an open neighborhood of
x^{0}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, and
C>0
, such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{H^{1}(W)}\leq C{\|u\|}_{H^{1}(V)}^{1-\delta}\Big({\|\Delta u\|}_{L^{2}(V)}+{|Bu|}_{H^{1-k}(V\cap\partial\Omega)}+{\|u\|}_{H^{1}(V_{\varepsilon})}\Big)^{\delta},

for
u\in H^{2}(V)
, with

V_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in V;\ \dist(x,\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon\}
.

This inequality can be obtained from a Carleman estimate as in
Theorem
1.1
for the Laplace
operator, yet with the large parameter
\tau
allowed to be chosen as
large as desired. This is exploited in an optimization procedure on
the parameter
\tau
in
[\tau_{0},+\infty[
for some
\tau_{0}
. Note
however that in the statement of Theorem
1.1
one has
\sigma\lesssim\tau\lesssim\sigma
. Thus, the
optimization procedure cannot be carried out in
[\tau_{0},+\infty[
. While having a
result quantifying the unique continuation under Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro-type conditions
for the bi-Laplace operator
similar to that of Proposition
1.4
can be
expected, the Carleman estimate we obtain in the present article cannot be used, at
least directly, for a proof as carried out in
[
33
]
or in
former sources such as
[
37
]
.

1.7.
Outline

This article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we recall some basic aspects of
pseudo-differential operators with a large parameters
\tau>0
and
some positivity inequality of Gårding type in particular for
quadratic forms in a half-space or at the boundary. Associated with
the large parameters are Sobolev like norms, also in a half-space or
at the boundary.

In Section
3
, the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro boundary condition are
properly defined for an elliptic operator, we give examples focusing
on the Laplace and bi-Laplace operator and we give a formulation in
local normal geodesic coordinates that we shall mostly use throughout
the article. For the bi-Laplace operator we provide a series of
examples of boundary operators for which the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro boundary conditions
holds and moreover the resulting operator is symmetric. We also show
that the algebraic conditions that characterize the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition are
robust under perturbation. This last aspect is key in the
understanding of how the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition get preserved under conjugation
and the introduction of a spectral parameter. This is done in
Section
4
, where an
analysis of the configuration of the roots of the conjugated
bi-Laplace operator is performed. In Section
4.5
the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for the conjugated
operator is exploited to obtain a symbol positivity for a quadratic
form to prepare for the derivation of a Carleman estimate.

In Section
5
we derive a estimation of
the boundary traces. This is precisely where the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition is
used. The result is first obtained microlocally and we then apply a
patching procedure.

To obtain the Carleman estimate for the bi-Laplace operator with
spectral parameter
\Delta^{2}-\sigma^{4}
in Section
6
we first
derive microlocal estimates for the operators
\Delta\pm\sigma^{2}
.
Imposing
\sigma
to be non-zero, in the sense that
\sigma\gtrsim\tau
, the previous estimates exhibits losses in different microlocal
regions. Thus concatenating the two estimates one derives an estimate
for
\Delta^{2}-\sigma^{4}
where losses do not accumulates. A local Carleman
estimate with only a loss of a half-derivative is obtained. This is
done in Section
7
. With the traces estimation obtained in Section
5
one obtains the local Carleman estimate of
Theorem
1.1
.

For the application to stabilization we have in mind, in
Section
8
we use a global weight
function and derive a global version of the Carleman estimate for

\Delta^{2}-\sigma^{4}
on the whole
\Omega
.
This leads to an observability inequality.

In Section
9
we
recall aspects of strong and weak solutions to the damped plate
equation, in particular through a semigroup formulation.
With the observability inequality obtained in Section
8
we derive in Section
10
a
resolvent estimate for the generator of the plate semigroup that in
turn implies the stabilization result of Theorem
1.2
.

1.8.
Some notation

The canonical inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{m}

(z,z^{\prime})_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}z_{k}\bar{z^{\prime}}_{k},
for
z=(z_{0},\cdots,z_{m-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{m},

z^{\prime}=(z^{\prime}_{0},\cdots,z^{\prime}_{m-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}.
The associated norm will be
denoted
|z|^{2}_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}|z_{k}|^{2}.

We shall use the notations
a\lesssim b
for
a\leq Cb
and

a\gtrsim b
for
a\geq Cb
, with a constant
C>0
that may change
from one line to another. We also write
a\asymp b
to denote

a\lesssim b\lesssim a.

For an open set
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we set
U_{+}=U\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and

(1.6)

\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(U_{+})=\{u=v_{|\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};v\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }\supp(v)\subset U\}.

We set
\overline{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=\{u_{|\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};\ u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
with

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the usual Schwartz space in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{and}\ \forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x^{\alpha}D_{x}^{\beta}u(x)|<\infty.

We recall that the Poisson bracket of two smooth functions is given by

\{f,g\}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{\xi_{j}}f\partial_{x_{j}}g-\partial_{x_{j}}f\partial_{\xi_{j}}g\right).

2.
Pseudo-differential calculus: notation, definitions, and some
properties

In a half-space geometry motivated by the normal geodesic coordinates
introduced in Section
1.4

we shall use
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and we shall
consider the operators
D_{d}=-i\partial_{d}
and
D^{\prime}=-i\partial^{\prime}
, with

\partial^{\prime}=(\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{d-1}})
.

2.1.
Pseudo-differential operators with a large a parameter on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

In this subsection we recall some notions on semi-classical
pseudo-differential operators with large parameter
\tau\geq 1.
We
denote by
S_{\tau}^{m}
the space of smooth functions
a(x,\xi,\tau)

defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
with
\tau\geq 1
as a large parameter,
that satisfies the following : for all multi-indices

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists

C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such
that

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a(x,\xi,\tau)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\tau}^{m-|\beta|},\ \ \text{where}\ \ \lambda_{\tau}^{2}=\tau^{2}+|\xi|^{2},

for all

(x,\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,\infty).
For
a\in S_{\tau}^{m},

one defines the associated pseudo-differential operator of order
m
, denoted by

A=\mathrm{Op}(a)
,

\displaystyle Au(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi,\tau)\hat{u}(\xi)d\xi,\qquad u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

One says that
a
is the symbol of
A
. We denote
\Psi_{\tau}^{m}
the set
of pseudo-differential operators of order
m.
We shall denote by

\mathscr{D}_{\tau}^{m}
the space of semi-classical differential operators, i.e, the
case when the symbol
a(x,\xi,\tau)
is a polynomial function of order

m
in
(\xi,\tau).

2.2.
Tangential pseudo-differential operators with a large a parameter

Here, we consider pseudo-differential operators that only act in the
tangential direction
x^{\prime}
with
x_{d}
behaving as a parameter. We shall
denote by
S_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m}
, the set of smooth functions
b(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau)

defined for
\tau\geq 1
as a large parameter and satisfying the
following: for all multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists

C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such
that

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi^{\prime}}b(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m-|\beta|},\ \ \text{where}\ \ \lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{2}=\tau^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},

for all

(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[1,\infty).
For
b\in S_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m}
,
we define the associated tangential pseudo-differential operator

B=\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(b)
of order
m
by

\displaystyle Bu(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}e^{ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi^{\prime}}b(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau)\hat{u}(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})d{\xi^{\prime}},\qquad u\in\overline{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}).

We define
\Psi_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m}
as the set of tangential
pseudo-differential operators of order
m,
and

\mathscr{D}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m}
the set of tangential differential
operators of order
m.
We also set

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m}=\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(\lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m}).

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.
If we consider
a
of the form

\displaystyle a(x,\xi,\tau)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}a_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau)\xi_{d}^{j},\quad a_{j}\in S_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m+m^{\prime}-j},

we define
\mathrm{Op}(a)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(a_{j})D^{j}_{x_{d}}.
We
write
a\in S_{\tau}^{m,m^{\prime}}
and
\mathrm{Op}(a)\in\Psi_{\tau}^{m,m^{\prime}}.

2.3.
Function norms

For functions norms we use the notation
\|.\|
for functions
defined in the interior of the domain and
|.|
for functions defined on the boundary.
In that spirit, we shall use the notation

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{+}={\|u\|}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})},\quad(u,\tilde{u})_{+}=(u,\tilde{u})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})},

for functions defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and

\displaystyle{|w|}_{\partial}={\|w\|}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})},\quad(w,\tilde{w})_{\partial}=(w,\tilde{w})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})},

for functions defined on
\{x_{d}=0\}
, such as traces.

We introduce the following norms, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{m,m^{\prime},\tau}\asymp\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}{\|\Lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m+m^{\prime}-j}D_{x_{d}}^{j}u\|}_{+},

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{m,\tau}={\|u\|}_{m,0,\tau}\asymp\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}{\|\Lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m-j}D_{x_{d}}^{j}u\|}_{+},

for
u\in\overline{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
. One has

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{m,\tau}\asymp\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m}\tau^{m-|\alpha|}{\|D^{\alpha}u\|}_{+},

and in the case
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{m,m^{\prime},\tau}\asymp\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{d}\leq m\\
|\alpha|\leq m+m^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\tau^{m+m^{\prime}-|\alpha|}{\|D^{\alpha}u\|}_{+},

with
\alpha=(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

The following argument will be used on numerous occasions: for
m\in\mathbb{N},

m^{\prime},\ell\in\mathbb{R},
with
\ell>0,

\displaystyle{\|u\|}_{m,m^{\prime},\tau}\ll{\|u\|}_{m,m^{\prime}+\ell,\tau},

if
\tau
is chosen
sufficiently large.

At the boundary
\{x_{d}=0\}
we define the following norms, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle{|\trace(u)|}_{m,m^{\prime},\tau}^{2}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}{|\Lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m+m^{\prime}-j}D^{j}_{x_{d}}u_{|x_{d}=0^{+}}|}_{\partial}^{2},\qquad u\in\overline{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}).

2.4.
Differential quadratic forms

2.4.1.
Differential quadratic forms in a half-space

Definition 2.1

(interior differential quadratic form)
.

Let
u\in\overline{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
. We say that

(2.1)

Q(u)=\sum_{s=1}^{N}(A^{s}u,B^{s}u)_{+},\qquad A^{s}=\mathrm{Op}(a^{s}),\ B^{s}=\mathrm{Op}(b^{s}),

is an interior differential quadratic form of type
(m,r)
with smooth
coefficients, if for each
s=1,\dots N
, we have
a^{s}(\varrho)\in S_{\tau}^{m,r^{\prime}}
and
b^{s}(\varrho)\in S_{\tau}^{m,r^{\prime\prime}}
, with
r^{\prime}+r^{\prime\prime}=2r
,
\varrho=(x,\xi,\tau)
.

The principal symbol of the quadratic form
Q
is defined as the
class of

(2.2)

q(\varrho)=\sum_{s=1}^{N}a^{s}(\varrho)\overline{b^{s}}(\varrho)

in
S_{\tau}^{2m,2r}/S_{\tau}^{2m,2r-1}
.

A result we shall use is the following microlocal Gårding inequality.

Proposition 2.2

(microlocal Gårding inequality)
.

Let
K
be a compact set of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
and let

\mathscr{U}
be an conic open set of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
contained in
K\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Let also
\chi\in S_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{0}
be homogeneous of degree
0
, be such
that

\supp(\chi)\subset\mathscr{U}
. Let
Q
be an interior differential quadratic
form of type
(m,r)
with homogeneous
principal symbol
q\in S_{\tau}^{2m,2r}
satisfying, for some
C_{0}>0

and
\tau_{0}>0
,

\Re q(\varrho)\geq C_{0}\lambda_{\tau}^{2m}\lambda_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{2r},\quad\text{for}\ \tau\geq\tau_{0},\ \ \varrho=(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d}),\ \varrho^{\prime}=(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau)\in\mathscr{U},\ \xi_{d}\in\mathbb{R}.

For
0<C_{1}<C_{0}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
\tau_{\ast}
,
C>0
,
and
C_{N}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Re Q(\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(\chi)u)

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}{\|\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(\chi)u\|}_{m,r,\tau}^{2}-C{|\trace(\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(\chi)u)|}_{m-1,r+1/2,\tau}^{2}-C_{N}{\|u\|}_{m,-N,\tau}^{2},

for

and

.

We refer to
[
8

and
[

A local version of the result is the following one that follows from Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition 2.3

(Gårding inequality)
.

Let
U_{0}
be a bounded open subset
of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
and let

be an interior differential quadratic

(m,r)
with homogeneous
principal symbol

satisfying, for some

and

For

there exist
\tau_{\ast}

\displaystyle\Re Q(u)

2.5.
Symbols and operators with an additional large parameter

In this article, we shall use operators with a symbol that depends
on an additional large parameter
\sigma
, say
a(x,\xi,\tau,\sigma)
. They
will satisfy estimate of the form

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a(x,\xi,\tau,\sigma)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{(m-|\beta|)/2}.

We observe that if
\tau\gtrsim\sigma
one has

\displaystyle\lambda_{\tau}^{2}\leq\tau^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\lesssim\lambda_{\tau}^{2}.

Thus, as far as pseudo-differential calculus is concerned it is as if

a\in S_{\tau}^{m}
and this property will be
exploited in what follows.

Similarly if
a=a(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
fulfills a tangential-type estimate of the form

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi^{\prime}}a(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{(m-|\beta|)/2},

if one has
\tau\gtrsim\sigma
one will be able to apply techniques
adapted to symbols in
S_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{m}
and associated operators, like for instance the results on differential quadratic forms listed in
Section
2.4
.

3.
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro boundary conditions for an elliptic operator

Let
P
be an elliptic differential operator of order
2k
on

\Omega
,
(k\geq 1)
, with principal symbol
p(x,\omega)
for

(x,\omega)\in T^{*}\Omega
. One defines the following polynomial in

z,

\tilde{p}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=p(x,\omega^{\prime}-zn_{x}),

for

x\in\partial\Omega,

\omega^{\prime}\in T^{*}_{x}\partial\Omega,

z\in\mathbb{R}
and
where
n_{x}
denotes the outward unit pointing conormal vector at
x

(see Section
1.4
). Here
x
and
\omega^{\prime}

are considered to act as parameters. We denote by
\rho_{j}(x,\omega^{\prime}),

1\leq j\leq 2k
the complex roots of
\tilde{p}.
One sets

\tilde{p}^{+}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=\prod\limits_{\Im\rho_{j}(x,\omega^{\prime})\geq 0}(z-\rho_{j}(x,\omega^{\prime})).

Given boundary operators
B_{1},\cdots,B_{k}
in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega,
with principal symbols
b_{j}(x,\omega)
,
j=1,\cdots,k,
one also sets
\tilde{b}_{j}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=b_{j}(x,\omega^{\prime}-zn_{x}).

Definition 3.1

(Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro boundary condition)
.

Let
(x,\omega^{\prime})\in T^{*}\partial\Omega
with
\omega^{\prime}\neq 0.
One says that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},\cdots,B_{k})
at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
if for any polynomial function
f(z)
with complex coefficients, there exists
c_{1},\cdots,c_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and a polynomial function
g(z)
with complex coefficients such that, for all
z\in\mathbb{C},

f(z)=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq k}c_{j}\tilde{b_{j}}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)+g(z)\tilde{p}^{+}(x,\omega^{\prime},z).

We say that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},\cdots,B_{k})
at
x\in\partial\Omega
if it holds at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
for all
\omega^{\prime}\in T^{*}_{x}\partial\Omega
with
\omega^{\prime}\neq 0.

3.1.
Some examples

For instance the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds in the following cases

•

P=-\Delta
on
\Omega,
with the Dirichlet boundary condition,
Bu_{|\partial\Omega}=u_{|\partial\Omega}.

•

P=\Delta^{2}
on
\Omega,
along with the so-called clamped
boundary conditions, i.e,

B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=u_{|\partial\Omega}
and

B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=\partial_{\nu}u_{|\partial\Omega},
where

\nu
is the normal outward pointing unit vector to

\partial\Omega
; see Section
1.4
.

•

P=\Delta^{2}
on
\Omega,
along with the so-called hinged boundary conditions, i.e,
B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=u_{|\partial\Omega}
and
B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=\Delta u_{|\partial\Omega}.

3.2.
Case of the bi-Laplace operator

With
P=\Delta^{2}
on
\Omega
, along with the general boundary operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
of orders
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
respectively, we give a matrix criterion of the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition. The general boundary operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are then given by

B_{\ell}(x,D)=\sum\limits_{0\leq j\leq\min(3,k_{\ell})}B_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}-j}(x,D^{\prime})(i\partial_{\nu})^{j},\qquad\ell=1,2,

with
B_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}-j}(x,D^{\prime})
differential operators acting in the
tangential variables.
We denote by
b_{1}(x,\omega)
and
b_{2}(x,\omega)
the principal symbols of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
respectively. For
(x,\omega^{\prime})\in T^{*}\partial\Omega,
we set

\tilde{b}_{\ell}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=\sum\limits_{0\leq j\leq\min(3,k_{\ell})}b_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}-j}(x,\omega^{\prime})z^{j},\qquad\ell=1,2.

We recall that the principal symbol of
P
is given by

p(x,\omega)=|\omega|^{4}_{g}.
One thus has

\tilde{p}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=p(x,\omega^{\prime}-zn_{x})=\big(z^{2}+|\omega^{\prime}|_{g}^{2}\big)^{2}.

Therefore

\tilde{p}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=(z-i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g})^{2}(z+i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g})^{2}
. According
to the above definition we set

\tilde{p}^{+}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=(z-i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g})^{2}.
Thus, the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
with
\omega^{\prime}\neq 0
if
and only if for any function
f(z)
the complex number
i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g}

is a root of the polynomial function
z\mapsto f(z)-c_{1}\tilde{b}_{1}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)-c_{2}\tilde{b}_{2}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)
and its
derivative for some
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. This leads to the
following determinant condition.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P=\Delta^{2}
on
\Omega
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be two boundary
operators. If
x\in\partial\Omega
,
\omega^{\prime}\in T_{x}^{*}\partial\Omega
, with

\omega^{\prime}\neq 0
, the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
if and only if

(3.1)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{1}&\tilde{b}_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{z}\tilde{b}_{1}&\partial_{z}\tilde{b}_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\omega^{\prime},z=i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g})\neq 0.

Remark 3.3
.

With the determinant condition and homogeneity, we note that if the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
it also holds
in a conic neighborhood of
(x,\omega^{\prime})
by continuity. If it holds at
x\in\Omega,
it also holds in a neighborhood of
x
.

3.3.
Formulation in normal geodesic coordinates

Near a boundary point
x\in\partial\Omega
, we shall use normal
geodesic coordinates. These coordinates are recalled at the beginning
of Section
2
. Then the principal symbols of
\Delta
and

\Delta^{2}
are given by
\xi_{d}^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime})
and

(\xi_{d}^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}))^{2}
respectively, where
r(x,\xi^{\prime})
is the
principal symbol of a tangential differential elliptic operator

R(x,D^{\prime})
of order 2, with

r(x,\xi^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq d-1}g^{ij}(x)\xi^{\prime}_{i}\xi^{\prime}_{j}\ \ \text{and}\ \ r(x,\xi^{\prime})\geq C|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}.

Here
g^{ij}
is the inverse of the metric
g_{ij}.
Below, we shall
often write
|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}=r(x,\xi^{\prime})
and we shall also write

|\xi|^{2}_{x}=\xi_{d}^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}),
for
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}).

If
b_{1}(x,\xi)
and
b_{2}(x,\xi)
are the principal symbols of the
boundary operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in the normal geodesic
coordinates then the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
with
P=\Delta^{2}
at
(x,\xi^{\prime})
reads

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x})\neq 0,

if
|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}\neq 0
according to Lemma
3.2
. If the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at some
x^{0}
,
because of homogeneity, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

(3.2)

\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right|(x^{0},\xi^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x})\geq C_{0}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1},\qquad\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

3.4.
Stability of the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition under perturbation

To prepare for the study of how the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition behaves under
conjugation with Carleman exponential weight and the addition of a
spectral parameter, we introduce some perturbations in the formulation
of the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
as written in (
3.2
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
V^{0}
be a compact set of
\partial\Omega
be such that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
at every point
x
of
V^{0}
. There exist
C_{1}>0

and
\varepsilon>0
such that

(3.3)

\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right|(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)\geq C_{1}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1},

for
x\in V^{0}
,
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
, and
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
, if
|\zeta^{\prime}|+|\delta|\leq\varepsilon|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}
.
Moreover one has

(3.4)

\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)\\[4.0pt]
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\tilde{\delta})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\tilde{\delta})\end{pmatrix}\right|\geq C_{1}|\delta-\tilde{\delta}|\,|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1},

for
x\in V^{0}
,
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
, and
\delta,\tilde{\delta}\in\mathbb{C}
,
if
|\zeta^{\prime}|+|\delta|+|\tilde{\delta}|\leq\varepsilon|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}
.

Proof.

From (
3.2
), since
V^{0}
is compact
having the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holding at every point
x
of
V^{0}
means
there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

(3.5)

\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right|(x,\xi^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x})\geq C_{0}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1},\qquad x\in V^{0},\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

The first part is a consequence of the mean value theorem,
homogeneity and (
3.5
) with say
C_{1}=C_{0}/2
.

For the second part it is sufficient to assume that

\delta\neq\tilde{\delta}
since the result is obvious otherwise.
For
j=1,2
one writes the Taylor formula

\displaystyle b_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\tilde{\delta})

\displaystyle=b_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)+(\tilde{\delta}-\delta)\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)

\displaystyle\quad+(\tilde{\delta}-\delta)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\partial_{\xi_{d}}^{2}b_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta_{s})\,ds,

with
\delta_{s}=(1-s)\delta+s\tilde{\delta}
,
yielding

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\delta}-\delta}

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)\\[4.0pt]
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\tilde{\delta})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\tilde{\delta})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad+(\tilde{\delta}-\delta)\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}^{2}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta_{s})&\partial_{\xi_{d}}^{2}b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta_{s})\end{pmatrix}ds.

With homogeneity, if
|\zeta^{\prime}|+|\delta|+|\tilde{\delta}|\lesssim|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}
one
finds

\displaystyle\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta)\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}^{2}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta_{s})&\partial_{\xi_{d}}^{2}b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime},i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}+\delta_{s})\end{pmatrix}\right|\lesssim|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-2},

Thus with
|\delta-\tilde{\delta}|\leq\varepsilon|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}
, for

\varepsilon>0
chosen sufficiently small, using the first part of the lemma one obtains
the second result.
∎

3.5.
Examples of boundary operators yielding symmetry

We give some examples of pairs of boundary operators
B_{1},B_{2}
that
fulfill (1) the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition and (2) yield symmetry for the bi-Lalace operator
P=\Delta^{2}
, that is,

\displaystyle(Pu,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,Pv)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for
u,v\in H^{4}(\Omega)
such that
B_{j}u_{|\partial\Omega}=B_{j}v_{|\partial\Omega}=0
,
j=1,2
.

We first recall that following Green formula

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\Delta u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,\Delta v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

which applied twice gives

(Pu,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,Pv)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+T(u,v)

with

\displaystyle T(u,v)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{n}\Delta u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(\Delta u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},\Delta v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}\Delta v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Using normal geodesic coordinates in a neighborhood of the whole boundary

\partial\Omega
allows one to write
\Delta=\partial_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{\prime}

where
\Delta^{\prime}
is the resulting Laplace operator on the boundary,
that is, associated with the trace of the metric on

\partial\Omega
. Since
\Delta^{\prime}
is selfadjoint on
\partial\Omega

this allows one to write formula (
3.5
)
in the alternative forms

\displaystyle T(u,v)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{n}^{3}u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-((\partial_{n}^{2}+2\Delta^{\prime})u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},(\partial_{n}^{2}+2\Delta^{\prime})v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}^{3}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

or

\displaystyle T(u,v)

\displaystyle=((\partial_{n}^{3}+2\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{n})u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(\partial_{n}^{2}u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}^{2}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},(\partial_{n}^{3}+2\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{n})v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

We start our list of examples with the most basics ones.

Example 3.5

(Hinged boundary conditions)
.

This type of conditions refers to
B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=u_{|\partial\Omega}
and
B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=\Delta u_{|\partial\Omega}
. With (
3.5
) one finds

T(u,v)=0
in the case of homogeneous conditions, hence symmetry.

Note that the hinged boundary conditions are equivalent to having

B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=u_{|\partial\Omega}
and
B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=\partial_{n}^{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}
.
With the notation of Section
3
this gives

\tilde{b}_{1}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=1
and
\tilde{b}_{2}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=(-iz)^{2}=-z^{2}
. It follows that

\det\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{1}&\tilde{b}_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{z}\tilde{b}_{1}&\partial_{z}\tilde{b}_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\omega^{\prime},z=i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g})=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&|\omega^{\prime}|^{2}_{g}\\[2.0pt]
0&-2i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g}\end{pmatrix}=-2i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g}\neq 0,

if
\omega^{\prime}\neq 0
and thus the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds by Lemma
3.2
.

With the hinged boundary conditions observe that the bi-Laplace operator is precisely
the square of the Dirichlet-Laplace operator. This makes its analysis
quite simple and this explains why this type of boundary condition is
often chosen in the mathematical literature. Observe that symmetry is
then obvious without invoking the above formulae.

Example 3.6

(Clamped boundary conditions)
.

This type of conditions refers to
B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=u_{|\partial\Omega}
and
B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega}
. With (
3.5
) one finds

T(u,v)=0
in the case of homogeneous conditions, hence symmetry.
With the notation of Section
3
this gives

\tilde{b}_{1}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=1
and
\tilde{b}_{2}(x,\omega^{\prime},z)=-iz
. It follows that

\det\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{1}&\tilde{b}_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{z}\tilde{b}_{1}&\partial_{z}\tilde{b}_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\omega^{\prime},z=i|\omega^{\prime}|_{g})=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&|\omega^{\prime}|_{g}\\[2.0pt]
0&-i\end{pmatrix}=-i\neq 0.

Thus the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds by Lemma
3.2
.

Note that with the clamped boundary conditions the bi-Laplace operator
cannot be seen as the square
of the Laplace operator with some well chosen boundary condition as
opposed to the case of the hinged boundary conditions displayed
above.

4.
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for the conjugated
bi-Laplacian with spectral parameter

Set
P_{\sigma}=\Delta^{2}-\sigma^{4}
with
\sigma\in[0,+\infty)
and denote by
P_{\sigma,\varphi}=e^{\tau\varphi}P_{\sigma}e^{-\tau\varphi}
the conjugate operator of
P_{\sigma}
with
\tau\geq 0
a large parameter and
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
. We shall refer to
\varphi
as the weight function. The principal symbol of
P_{\sigma}
in normal geodesic coordinates is given by

p_{\sigma}(x,\xi)=(\xi_{d}^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}))^{2}-\sigma^{4}.

Observe that
e^{\tau\varphi}D_{j}e^{-\tau\varphi}=D_{j}+i\tau\partial_{j}\varphi\in\mathscr{D}_{\tau}^{1}.
So, after conjugation, the principal symbol becomes

\displaystyle p_{\sigma,\varphi}(x,\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle=p_{\sigma}(x,\xi+i\tau d_{x}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\left((\xi_{d}+i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)\right)^{2}-\sigma^{4}

\displaystyle=\left((\xi_{d}+i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)-\sigma^{2}\right)\left((\xi_{d}+i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+\sigma^{2}\right)

We write

p_{\sigma,\varphi}(x,\xi,\tau)=q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{1}(x,\xi,\tau)q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{2}(x,\xi,\tau)

with

\displaystyle q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{j}(x,\xi,\tau)=(\xi_{d}+i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2},\ \ j=1,2.

We consider two boundary operators

B_{1}
and
B_{2}
of order
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
with
b_{j}(x,\xi)
for
principal symbol,
j=1,2
. The associated conjugated operators

\displaystyle B_{j,\varphi}=e^{\tau\varphi}B_{j}e^{-\tau\varphi},

have respective principal symbols

\displaystyle b_{j,\varphi}(x,\xi,\tau)=b_{j}(x,\xi+i\tau d\varphi),\quad j=1,2.

We assume that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P_{0},B_{1},B_{2})
as in
Definition
3.1
for any point
(x,\omega^{\prime})\in T^{*}_{x}\partial\Omega.
We wish to know if the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition can hold
for
(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2},\varphi),
as given by the following
definition (in local coordinates for simplicity).

Definition 4.1
.

Let

(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
with

(\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\neq 0
. One says that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for

(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2},\varphi)
at
(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
if for
any polynomial function
f(\xi_{d})
with complex coefficients there
exist
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and a polynomial function

\ell(\xi_{d})
with complex coefficients such that, for all

\xi_{d}\in\mathbb{C}

f(\xi_{d})=c_{1}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)+c_{2}b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)+\ell(\xi_{d})p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau),

with

p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)=\prod\limits_{\Im\rho_{j}(\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\geq 0}(\xi_{d}-\rho_{j}(\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)),

where
\rho_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma),

j=1,\cdots,4,
denote the complex
roots of
p_{\sigma,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)
viewed as a polynomial in

\xi_{d}
.

In what follows, we shall assume that
\partial_{d}\varphi>0.

Locally, one has
\partial_{d}\varphi\geq C_{1}>0
, for some
C_{1}>0
.

4.1.
Discussion on the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition
according to the position of the roots

With the assumption that
\partial_{d}\varphi>0,
for any point

(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
at most two roots lie in the upper complex closed half-plane (this is explained below). We then enumerate the following cases.

•

Case 1 : No root lying in the upper complex closed
half-plane, then
p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)=1
and the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition of Definition
4.1
holds trivially at
(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
.

•

Case 2 : One root lying in the upper complex closed
half-plane. Let us denote by
\rho^{+}
that root, then

p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)=\xi_{d}-\rho^{+}.
With
Definition
4.1
, for any choice of
f
, the polynomial
function
\xi_{d}\mapsto f(\xi_{d})-c_{1}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)-c_{2}b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)

admits
\rho^{+}
as a root for
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
well
chosen. Hence, the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at

(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
if and only
if

\displaystyle b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+},\tau)\neq 0\ \ \text{or}\ \ b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+},\tau)\neq 0.

Note that it then suffices to have

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1,\varphi}&b_{2,\varphi}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1,\varphi}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2,\varphi}\end{pmatrix}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+},\tau)\neq 0.

•

Case 3 : Two different roots lying in the upper complex
closed half-plane. Let denote by
\rho^{+}_{1}
and
\rho^{+}_{2}
these
roots. One has

p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)=(\xi_{d}-\rho^{+}_{1})\xi_{d}-\rho^{+}_{2}).

The Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at
(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
if and only
if the complex numbers
\rho^{+}_{1}
and
\rho^{+}_{2}
are the roots of
the polynomial function

\xi_{d}\mapsto f(\xi_{d})-c_{1}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)-c_{2}b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau),

for
c_{1},c_{2}
well chosen. This reads

\begin{cases}f(\rho^{+}_{1})=c_{1}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{1},\tau)+c_{2}b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{1},\tau),\\
f(\rho^{+}_{2})=c_{1}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{2},\tau)+c_{2}b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{2},\tau).\end{cases}

Being able to solve this system in
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
for any
f
is equivalent to having

(4.1)

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{1},\tau)&b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{1},\tau)\\[4.0pt]
b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{2},\tau)&b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+}_{2},\tau)\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

•

Case 4 : A double root lying in the upper complex closed
half-plane. Denote by
\rho^{+}
this root; one has

p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)=(\xi_{d}-\rho^{+})^{2}.
The
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at at
(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
if and only if for any choice of
f
, the
complex number
\rho^{+}
is a double root of the polynomial function

\xi_{d}\mapsto f(\xi_{d})-c_{1}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi,\tau)-c_{2}b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi,\tau)

for some
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}.
Thus having the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition is equivalent of having the following
determinant condition,

(4.2)

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+},\tau)&b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+},\tau)\\[4.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+},\tau)&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho^{+},\tau)\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

Observe that case 4 can only occur if
\sigma=0
(then one has

(\xi^{\prime},\tau)\neq(0,0)
). If
\sigma>0
then only cases 1, 2, and 3
are possible. This is precisely stated in Lemma
4.7
. This will be an important point in what follows.

We now state the following important proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
x^{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Assume that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for

(P_{0},B_{1},B_{2})
at
x^{0}
and thus in a compact neighborhood
V^{0}

of
x^{0}
(by Remark
3.3
). Assume also that

\partial_{d}\varphi\geq C_{1}>0
in
V^{0}
. There exist
\mu_{0}>0
and

\mu_{1}>0
such that if

(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\in V^{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
with

(\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\neq(0,0,0)
,

\displaystyle|d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi(x)|\leq\mu_{0}\partial_{d}\varphi(x)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sigma\leq\mu_{1}\tau\partial_{d}\varphi(x),

then the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2},\varphi)
at

(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma).

The proof of Proposition
4.2
is given below. We first need to
analyze the configuration of the roots of the symbol

p_{\sigma,\varphi}
starting with each factor
q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{j}
,
j=1,2
.

4.2.
Root configuration for each factor

We consider either factors
\xi_{d}\mapsto q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{j}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)
. We recall that

\displaystyle q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{j}(x,\xi,\tau)=(\xi_{d}+i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2},\ \ j=1,2.

First, we consider the case
r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{-}
, that is, equal to
-\beta^{2}
with
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, the roots of
\xi_{d}\mapsto q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{j}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)
are given by

\displaystyle-i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi+\beta\quad\text{and}\quad-i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi-\beta.

Both lie in the lower complex open half-plane.

Second, we consider the case
r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{-}
. There exists a unique
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
such
that
\Re\alpha_{j}>0
and

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=r(x,\xi^{\prime})-\tau^{2}r(x,d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}+i2\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2},

where
\tilde{r}(x,.,.)
denotes the symmetric bilinear form associated with the quadratic form
r(x,.)
.
Then, the two roots of
\xi_{d}\mapsto q_{\sigma,\varphi}^{j}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)
are given by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\pi_{j,1}=-i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi-i\alpha_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{j,2}=-i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi+i\alpha_{j}.

One has
\Im\pi_{j,1}<0
since
\partial_{d}\varphi\geq C_{1}>0
.
With
\Im\pi_{j,2}=-\tau\partial_{d}\varphi+\Re\alpha_{j}
one sees that
the sign of
\Im\pi_{j,2}
may change.
The following lemma gives an algebraic characterization of the sign of
\Im\pi_{j,2}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
\partial_{d}\varphi>0
. Having
\Im\pi_{j,2}<0
is
equivalent to having

(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}r(x,\xi^{\prime})+\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}<\tau^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+(-1)^{j+1}\sigma^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}.

Proof.

From
4.4
one has
\Im\pi_{j,2}<0
if and only if

\Re\alpha_{j}<\tau\partial_{d}\varphi=|\tau\partial_{d}\varphi|
, that is, if
and only if

\displaystyle 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\Re\alpha_{j}^{2}-4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}+(\Im\alpha_{j}^{2})^{2}<0,

by Lemma
4.4
below. With (
4.2
) this gives the result.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
m=z^{2}.
For
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such
that
x_{0}\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|\Re z|\lesseqqgtr|x_{0}|\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad 4x_{0}^{2}\Re m-4x_{0}^{4}+(\Im m)^{2}\lesseqqgtr 0.

Proof.

Let
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}.
On the one hand we have

z^{2}=x^{2}-y^{2}+2ixy=m
and
\Re m=x^{2}-y^{2},

\Im m=2xy.
On the other
hand we have

\displaystyle 4x_{0}^{2}\Re m-4x_{0}^{4}+(\Im m)^{2}

\displaystyle=4x_{0}^{2}(x^{2}-y^{2})-4x_{0}^{4}+4x^{2}y^{2}

\displaystyle=4(x_{0}^{2}+y^{2})(x^{2}-x_{0}^{2}),

thus with the same sign as
(x^{2}-x_{0}^{2})
.
Since
|\Re z|\lesseqqgtr|x_{0}|\ \Leftrightarrow\ x^{2}-x_{0}^{2}\lesseqqgtr 0
the conclusion follows.
∎

With the following two lemmata we now connect the sign of
\Im\pi_{j,2}
with the low frequency
regime
|\xi^{\prime}|\lesssim\tau
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume there exists
K_{0}>0
such that
|d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|\leq K_{0}|\partial_{d}\varphi|
. Then, there exists
C_{K_{0}}>0
such that
\Im\pi_{j,2}<0

if
C_{K_{0}}|\xi^{\prime}|+\sigma\leq\tau\partial_{d}\varphi
,
j=0,1
.

Proof.

With Lemma
4.3
having
\Im\pi_{j,2}<0
reads

(4.5)

(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}r(x,\xi^{\prime})+\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}<\tau^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+(-1)^{j+1}\sigma^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}.

On the one hand, since
|d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|\leq K_{0}|\partial_{d}\varphi|
one has

(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}r(x,\xi^{\prime})+\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}\leq K(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},

for some
K>0
that depends on
K_{0}
, using that
|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}\eqsim|\xi^{\prime}|
.
On the other hand one has

\displaystyle\tau^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+(-1)^{j+1}\sigma^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\geq\tau^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-\sigma^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}.

Thus (
4.5
) holds if one has

\displaystyle\tau^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-\sigma^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\geq K(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},

that is,
\tau^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\geq K|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\sigma^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
W
be a bounded open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x^{0}\in W
. Assume that

\partial_{d}\varphi>0
in
\overline{W}
and let
\kappa_{0}>0
. Then, there exists
C>0
such
that

\displaystyle|\xi^{\prime}|\leq C\tau\quad\text{if}\ \Im\pi_{j,2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)<0\ \text{and}\ \kappa_{0}\sigma\leq\tau,\quad x\in W.

Proof.

With Lemma
4.3
having
\Im\pi_{j,2}<0
reads

(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}r(x,\xi^{\prime})+\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}<\tau^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+(-1)^{j+1}\sigma^{2}(\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}.

In particular,
this implies

r(x,\xi^{\prime})<\tau^{2}|d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+(-1)^{j+1}\sigma^{2}\leq(\sup_{W}|d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+1/\kappa_{0}^{2})\tau^{2}.

The result follows since
|\xi^{\prime}|\asymp r(x,\xi^{\prime})
.
∎

As mentioned in Section
4.1
, we
have the following result.

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume that
\sigma>0
. Then,

\pi_{1,2}\neq\pi_{2,2}
. Moreover, the roots
\pi_{1,2}
and
\pi_{2,2}
cannot be both real.

Proof.

With the forms of the roots given in (

then

.

Assume now that
\pi_{1,2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\pi_{2,2}\in\mathbb{R}
, that is,

\Im\pi_{1,2}=\Im\pi_{2,2}=0
. This reads
\Re\alpha_{j}=\tau\partial_{d}\varphi
, giving

, for

and

. With Lemma

one

\displaystyle 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\Re\alpha_{j}^{2}-4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}+(\Im\alpha_{j}^{2})^{2}=0,\qquad j=1,2.

∎

4.4.
Local stability of the algebraic conditions associated
with the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition

In Section
3
we saw that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for

(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2})
in Definition
3.1
exhibits some stability
property. This was used in the statement of Proposition
4.2
that states how the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for

(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2})
can imply the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition of
Definition
4.1
for

(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2},\varphi)
, that is, the version of this condition
for the conjugated operators.

A natural question would then be: does the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for the
conjugated operators enjoy the same stability property? The answer is
yes. Yet, this is not needed in what follows. In fact, below one
exploits the algebraic conditions listed in Section
4.1
once the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition is know to hold at a point
\varrho^{0\prime}=(x^{0},\xi^{0\prime},\tau^{0},\sigma^{0})
in tangential
phase space. One thus rather needs to know that these algebraic
conditions are stable. Here also the answer is positive and is the
subject of the present section.

As in Definition
4.1
for
\varrho^{\prime}=(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
one denotes by

\rho_{j}(\varrho^{\prime})

the roots of
p_{\sigma,\varphi}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)
viewed as a polynomial in

\xi_{d}
.

Let
\varrho^{0\prime}=(x^{0},\xi^{0\prime},\tau^{0},\sigma^{0})\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
.
One sets

\displaystyle J^{+}=\big\{j\in\{1,2,3,4\};\ \Im\rho_{j}(\varrho^{0\prime})\geq 0\},\qquad J^{-}=\big\{j\in\{1,2,3,4\};\ \Im\rho_{j}(\varrho^{0\prime})<0\}

and, for
\varrho^{\prime}=(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
,

\displaystyle\kappa^{+}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{\prime})=\prod_{j\in J^{+}}\big(\xi_{d}-\rho_{j}(\varrho^{\prime})\big),\qquad\kappa^{-}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{\prime})=\prod_{j\in J^{-}}\big(\xi_{d}-\rho_{j}(\varrho^{\prime})\big).

Naturally, one has
\kappa^{+}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{0\prime},\xi_{d})=p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x^{0},\xi^{0\prime},\xi_{d},\tau^{0})
and
\kappa^{-}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{0\prime},\xi_{d})=p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{-}(x^{0},\xi^{0\prime},\xi_{d},\tau^{0})
.
Moreover, there exists a conic neighborhood
\mathscr{U}_{0}
of

\varrho^{0\prime}
where both
\kappa^{+}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{\prime})
and

\kappa^{-}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{\prime})
are smooth with respect to
\varrho^{\prime}
.
One has

\displaystyle p_{\sigma,\varphi}=p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{-}=\kappa^{+}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}\kappa^{-}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}.

Note however that for
\varrho^{\prime}=(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\in\mathscr{U}_{0}
it may very well happen that

p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)\neq\kappa^{+}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})

and
p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{-}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)\neq\kappa^{-}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})
.

The following proposition can be found in
[
8
, proposition 1.8]
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition hold at
\varrho^{0\prime}=(x^{0},\xi^{0\prime},\tau^{0},\sigma^{0})\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
for

(P_{\sigma},B_{1},B_{2},\varphi)
. Then,

(1)

The polynomial

\xi_{d}\mapsto p_{\sigma,\varphi}^{+}(x^{0},\xi^{0\prime},\xi_{d},\tau^{0})
is of degree less than or equal to
two.

(2)

There exists a conic neighborhood
\mathscr{U}
of
\varrho^{0\prime}
such that

\{b^{1}_{\varphi}(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d}),b^{2}_{\varphi}(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})\}
is complete
modulo
\kappa^{+}_{\varrho^{0\prime}}(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})
at every point
\varrho^{\prime}=(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\in\mathscr{U}
,
namely for
any polynomial function
f(\xi_{d})
with complex coefficients there
exist

one finds that
\mathsf{P}_{0}
is selfadjoint, using
that a symmetric maximal monotone operator is selfadjoint (see for
instance Proposition 7.6 in
[
9
]
).

The resolvent of
\mathsf{P}_{0}+\id
being compact on
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathsf{P}_{0}
has a sequence of
eigenvalues with finite multiplicities. With the assumed nonnegativity
(
9.4
) they take the form of a sequence

\displaystyle 0\leq\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{k}\leq\cdots

that grows to
+\infty
. Associated with this sequence is

(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
a Hilbert basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Any
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
reads
u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}u_{j}\phi_{j}
, with

u_{j}=(u,\phi_{j})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
We define the Sobolev-like scale

(9.5)

\displaystyle H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega);(\mu_{j}^{k/4}u_{j})_{j}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})\}\quad\text{for}\ k\geq 0.

One has
D(\mathsf{P}_{0})=H^{4}_{B}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}(\Omega)=H^{0}_{B}(\Omega)
. Each
H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)
,
k\geq 0
, is equipped with
the inner product and norm

\displaystyle(u,v)_{H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+\mu_{j})^{k/2}u_{j}\overline{v_{j}}.\qquad{\|u\|}_{H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+\mu_{j})^{k/2}|u_{j}|^{2},

yielding a Hilbert space structure.
The space
H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{k^{\prime}}_{B}(\Omega)
if
0\leq k^{\prime}\leq k
and the injection is compact.
Note that one uses
(1+\mu_{j})^{k/2}
in place of
\mu_{j}^{k/2}
since

\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
may not be trivial. Note that if
k=0
one recovers the
standard
L^{2}
-inner product and norm.

Using
L^{2}(\Omega)
as a pivot space, for
k>0
we also define the space

H^{-k}_{B}(\Omega)
as the dual space of
H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)
. One
finds that any
u\in H^{-k}_{B}(\Omega)
takes the form of the
following limit of
L^{2}
-functions

\displaystyle u=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}u_{j}\phi_{j},

for some
(u_{j})_{j}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
\big((1+\mu_{j})^{-k/4}u_{j}\big)_{j}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})
, with the limit occurring in

\big(H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)\big)^{\prime}
with the natural dual strong topology. Moreover, one has
u_{j}=\langle u,\overline{\phi_{j}}\rangle_{H^{-k}_{B},H^{k}_{B}}
.
If
u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}u_{j}\phi_{j}\in H^{-k}_{B}(\Omega)
and
v=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}v_{j}\phi_{j}\in H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)
one finds

\displaystyle\langle u,\overline{v}\rangle_{H^{-k}_{B},H^{k}_{B}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}u_{j}\overline{v_{j}}.

One can then extend (or restrict) the action of
\mathsf{P}_{0}
on any space

H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
. One has
\mathsf{P}_{0}:H^{k}_{B}(\Omega)\to H^{k-4}_{B}(\Omega)
continuously with

(9.6)

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{0}u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mu_{j}u_{j}\phi_{j},\quad\text{with convergence in}\ H^{k-4}_{B}(\Omega)\ \text{for}\ u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}u_{j}\phi_{j}\in H^{k}_{B}(\Omega).

In particular, for
u\in H^{4}_{B}(\Omega)=D(\mathsf{P}_{0})
and
v\in H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)

one has

(9.7)

\displaystyle(\mathsf{P}_{0}u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}u,\bar{v}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mu_{j}u_{j}\overline{v_{j}}

and if
u,v\in H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)
one has

(9.8)

\displaystyle(u,v)_{H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)}=(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}u,\bar{v}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+\mu_{j})u_{j}\overline{v_{j}}.

Note that

(9.9)

\displaystyle\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}u,\bar{v}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}=(\mathsf{P}_{0}^{1/2}u,\mathsf{P}_{0}^{1/2}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

with the operator
\mathsf{P}_{0}^{1/2}
easily defined by means of the Hilbert
basis
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. In fact,
H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)
is the domain
of
\mathsf{P}_{0}^{1/2}
viewed as un unbounded operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We make the following observations.

(1)

If
\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})=\{0\}
then

\displaystyle(u,v)\mapsto\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}u,\bar{u}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}},

is also an inner-product on
H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)
, that yields an
equivalent norm.

(2)

If
0
is an eigenvalue, that is,

\dim\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})=n\geq 1
then
(\phi_{0},\dots,\phi_{n-1})
is a
orthonormal basis of
\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
for the
L^{2}
-inner product.
From a classical unique continuation
property, since
\alpha(x)>0
for
x
in an open subset of
\Omega
one sees that

(9.10)

\displaystyle(u,v)\mapsto(\alpha u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

is also an inner product on
the finite dimensional space
\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
.
We introduce a second basis
(\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{n-1})
of
\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
orthonormal with respect to this second
inner product.

In what follows, we treat the more difficult case where
\dim\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})=n\geq 1
. The case
\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})=\{0\}
is left to the
reader.

9.2.
The plate semigroup generator

Set
{\mathcal{H}}=H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)\oplus L^{2}(\Omega)
with natural inner
product and norm

(9.11)

\displaystyle\big((u^{0},u^{1}),(v^{0},v^{1})\big)_{{\mathcal{H}}}=(u^{0},v^{0})_{H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)}+(u^{1},v^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(9.12)

\displaystyle{\big\|(u^{0},u^{1})\big\|}_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}={\|u^{0}\|}_{H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)}^{2}+{\|u^{1}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Define the unbounded operator

(9.13)

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
\mathsf{P}_{0}&\alpha(x)\end{pmatrix},

on
{\mathcal{H}}

with domain given by
D(A)=D(\mathsf{P}_{0})\oplus H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)
.
This domain is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
and
A
is a closed operator. One has

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\ker(A)=\big\{\phantom{}^{t}(u^{0},0);\ u^{0}\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})\big\}.

The important result of this section is the following proposition.

Proposition 9.1
.

The operator
(A,D(A))
generates a bounded semigroup
S(t)=e^{-tA}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
.

The understanding of this generator property relies on the
introduction of a reduced function space associated with

\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
, following for instance the analysis of

[
38
]
. It will be also important in the derivation of a precise
resolvent estimate in Section
10.1
. If

\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})=\{0\}
, that is,
\mu_{0}>0
, this procedure is not
necessary. For
v\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
,
v\neq 0
, we introduce the
linear form

(9.14)

\displaystyle F_{v}:{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle(u^{0},u^{1})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\alpha v,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-1}\big((\alpha u^{0},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(u^{1},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big),

We set

(9.15)

\displaystyle\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}=\bigcap_{{v\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})}\atop{v\neq 0}}\ker(F_{v})=\bigcap_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\ker(F_{\varphi_{j}}),

with the basis
(\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{n-1})
of
\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
introduced above.
If
(v,0)\in\ker(A)
, with
0\neq v\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
,
note that
F_{v}(v,0)=1
.
We set
\Theta_{j}=\phantom{}^{t}(\varphi_{j},0)
,
j=0,\dots,n-1
and

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{N}}V=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F_{\varphi_{j}}(V)\Theta_{j},\qquad\text{for}\ V\in{\mathcal{H}},

and
\Pi_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\id_{{\mathcal{H}}}-\Pi_{\mathcal{N}}
. We obtain that

\Pi_{\mathcal{N}}
and
\Pi_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}
are
continuous
projectors associated with
the direct sum

(9.16)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}=\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}\oplus\mathcal{N}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \dot{{\mathcal{H}}}=\ker(\Pi_{\mathcal{N}}).

Note that

\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}
are
not orthogonal
in
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Yet, it is important to note the following result.

Lemma 9.2
.

We have
\range(A)\subset\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

Proof.

Let
U=\phantom{}^{t}(u^{0},u^{1})=AV
with
V=\phantom{}^{t}(v^{0},v^{1})\in D(A)
. One has
u^{0}=-v^{1}\in H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)
and
u^{1}=\mathsf{P}_{0}v^{0}+\alpha v_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. If
0\neq\varphi\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
one writes

\displaystyle(\alpha\varphi,\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}F_{\varphi}(U)

\displaystyle=(-\alpha v^{1},\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\mathsf{P}_{0}v^{0}+\alpha v^{1},\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{P}_{0}v^{0},\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(v^{0},\mathsf{P}_{0}\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

using that
v^{0},\varphi\in D(\mathsf{P}_{0})
, that
(\mathsf{P}_{0},D(\mathsf{P}_{0}))
is
selfadjoint, and that
\varphi\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
. The conclusion
follows from the definition of
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
in (
9.15
).
∎

The space
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
inherits the natural inner product and norm of

{\mathcal{H}}
given in (
9.11
).
Yet one finds that the inner product

(9.17)

\displaystyle((u^{0},u^{1}),(v^{0},v^{1}))_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}=\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0},\overline{v^{0}}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}+(u^{1},v^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and associated norm

(9.18)

\displaystyle{\|(u^{0},u^{1})\|}_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}=\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0},\overline{u^{0}}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}+{\|u^{1}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

yields an equivalent norm on
\dot{\mathcal{H}}
by a Poincaré-like argument.

We introduce the unbounded operator

\dot{A}
on
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
given by the domain

D(\dot{A})=D(A)\cap\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}

and such that
\dot{A}V=AV
for
V\in D(\dot{A})
. We then have

A=\dot{A}\circ\Pi_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}
.
Observe that
D(\dot{A})=\Pi_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}\big(D(A)\big)
since

\mathcal{N}=\ker(A)\subset D(A)
. Thus,
one has

(9.19)

\displaystyle D(A)=D(\dot{A})\oplus{\mathcal{N}}.

As for the decomposition of
{\mathcal{H}}
given in (
9.16
)
note that
D(\dot{A})
and
{\mathcal{N}}
are not orthogonal.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}

be such that
\Re z<0
. We have

{\|(z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A})U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}\geq|\Re z|\,{\|U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}},\quad U\in D(\dot{A}).

The proof of this lemma is quite classical. It is given in
Appendix
A.2
.

With the previous lemma, with the Hille-Yosida theorem one
proves the following result.

Lemma 9.4
.

The operator
(\dot{A},D(\dot{A}))
generates a semigroup of
contraction

\dot{S}(t)=e^{-t\dot{A}}
on
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

If we set

(9.20)

\displaystyle S(t)=\dot{S}(t)\circ\Pi_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}+\Pi_{\mathcal{N}},

we find that
S(t)
is a semigroup on
{\mathcal{H}}
generated by
(A,D(A))
,
thus proving Proposition
9.1
.
If
Y^{0}\in D(A)
, the solution of the semigroup equation

\frac{d}{dt}Y(t)+AY(t)=0
reads

(9.21)

\displaystyle Y(t)=S(t)Y^{0}=\dot{S}(t)\circ\Pi_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}Y^{0}+\Pi_{\mathcal{N}}Y^{0}.

We set
\dot{Y}(t)=\Pi_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}Y(t)=\dot{S}(t)\circ\Pi_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}Y^{0}
.

The adjoint of
\dot{A}
has domain
D(\dot{A}^{*})=D(A)
and is given
by

\displaystyle\dot{A}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-\mathsf{P}_{0}&\alpha(x)\end{pmatrix}.

Similarly to Lemma
9.3
one has
the following result with a similar proof.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}

be such that
\Re z<0
. We have

{\|(z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A}^{*})U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}\geq|\Re z|\,{\|U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}},\quad U\in D(\dot{A}^{*})=D(\dot{A}).

9.3.
Strong and weak solutions to the damped plate equation

For
y(t)
a solution to the damped plate equation (
9.1
) one has
Y(t)=\phantom{}^{t}(y(t),\partial_{t}y(t))

formally solution to
\frac{d}{dt}Y(t)+AY(t)=0
and conversely.

The semigroup
S(t)
generated by
A
as given by
Proposition
9.1
allows one to go beyond
this formal observation and one obtains the
following well-posedness result for strong solutions of the damped
plate equation.

Proposition 9.6

(strong solutions of the damped plate equation)
.

For
(y^{0},y^{1})\in H^{4}_{B}(\Omega)\times H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)
there exists a unique

\displaystyle y\in\mathscr{C}^{0}\big([0,+\infty);H^{4}_{B}(\Omega)\big)\cap\mathscr{C}^{1}\big([0,+\infty);H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)\big)\cap\mathscr{C}^{2}\big([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)\big)

such that

(9.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}y+Py+\alpha\partial_{t}y=0\quad\text{in}\ L^{\infty}([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)),\qquad y_{|t=0}=y^{0},\ \partial_{t}y_{|t=0}=y^{1}.

Moreover, there exists
C>0
such that

(9.23)

\displaystyle{\|y(t)\|}_{H^{4}_{B}(\Omega)}+{\|\partial_{t}y(t)\|}_{H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)}\leq C\big({\|y^{0}\|}_{H^{4}_{B}(\Omega)}+{\|y^{1}\|}_{H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)}\big),\qquad t\geq 0.

With
Y(t)
as above, for such a solution
y(t)
one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Y(t)+AY(t)=0,\qquad Y(0)=Y^{0}=\phantom{}^{t}(y^{0},y^{1}),

that is,

\displaystyle Y(t)=S(t)Y^{0}\in\mathscr{C}^{0}\big([0,+\infty);D(A)\big)\cap\mathscr{C}^{1}\big([0,+\infty);H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)\oplus L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

A weak solution to the damped plate equation is simply associated with
an initial data
(y^{0},y^{1})\in H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
and
given by the first coordinate of
Y(t)=S(t)Y^{0}
. Then one has

\displaystyle Y(t)\in\mathscr{C}^{0}\big([0,+\infty);{\mathcal{H}}\big)\cap\mathscr{C}^{1}\big([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)\oplus H^{-2}_{B}(\Omega)\big).

or equivalently

\displaystyle y\in\mathscr{C}^{0}\big([0,+\infty);H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)\big)\cap\mathscr{C}^{1}\big([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap\mathscr{C}^{2}\big([0,+\infty);H^{-2}_{B}(\Omega)\big).

For a strong solution, the natural energy is given by

(9.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(y)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\big({\|\partial_{t}y(t)\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(\mathsf{P}_{0}y(t),y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).

Observe that if
y^{0}\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
then
y(t)=y^{0}
is solution
to (
9.1
) with
y^{1}=0
. This is
consistent with the form of the semigroup
S(t)
given in (
9.20
). Such a solution is independent of the evolution
variable
t
, and thus, despite damping, there is no decay. However,
note that such a solution is ‘invisible’ for the energy defined in
(
9.24
). In fact, for a strong solution to (
9.1
) as given by Proposition
9.6
one has

(9.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(y)(t)=\frac{1}{2}{\|\dot{Y}(t)\|}_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{2},

with
\dot{Y}(t)
as defined below
(
9.21
) and
{\|.\|}_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}
defined in
(
9.18
). For a strong solution, we write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(y)(t)

\displaystyle=\Re(\partial_{t}y(t),\partial_{t}^{2}y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}\partial_{t}y(t),\overline{y(t)}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathsf{P}_{0}y(t),\partial_{t}y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\Re(\partial_{t}y(t),(\partial_{t}^{2}+\mathsf{P}_{0})y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-\Re(\partial_{t}y(t),\alpha\partial_{t}y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 0

since
\alpha\geq 0
. Thus, the energy of a strong solution is nonincreasing.
To understand the decay of the energy one has to focus on the
properties of the semigroup
\dot{S}(t)
and its generator
(\dot{A},D(\dot{A}))
on
\dot{\mathcal{H}}
. This is done in Section
10.1
.

For a weak solution
y(t)\in\mathscr{C}^{0}\big([0,+\infty);H^{2}_{B}(\Omega)\big)\cap\mathscr{C}^{1}\big([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)\big)
the energy is
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(y)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\big({\|\partial_{t}y(t)\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}y(t),\overline{y(t)}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}\big)

that coincides with (
9.24
) for a strong solution.
The stabilization result we are interested in only concerns strong
solutions (see Section
10.2
). Thus, we shall
not mention weak solutions in what follows.

10.
Resolvent estimates and applications to
stabilization

Here we use the observability inequality of Theorem
8.2

to obtain a resolvent estimate for the plate
semigroup generator that allows one to deduce a stabilization result
for the damped plate equation. This a sequence of argument comes from
the seminal works of Lebeau
[
36
]
and
Lebeau-Robbiano
[
38
]
.

10.1.
Resolvent estimate

We prove a resolvent estimate for the unbounded operator
(\dot{A},D(\dot{A}))
that
acts on
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
. First, we establish that
\{\Re z\leq 0\}
lies in the
resolvent set of
\dot{A}
.

Proposition 10.1
.

The spectrum of
(\dot{A},D(\dot{A}))
is contained in
\{z\in\mathbb{C};\Re(z)>0\}
.

The proof of this proposition is rather classical based on a unique
continuation argument and a Fredholm index argument for a compact
perturbation. It is given in Appendix
A.3
.

Theorem 10.2
.

Let
\mathscr{O}
be an open subset of
\Omega
such that
\alpha\geq\delta>0
on
\mathscr{O}
. Then, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
the unbounded
operator
i\sigma\id-\dot{A}
is invertible on
\dot{\mathcal{H}}
and
for there exist
C>0
such that

(10.1)

\displaystyle{\|(i\sigma\id-\dot{A})^{-1}\|}_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{{\mathcal{H}}})}\leq Ce^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}},\qquad\sigma\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By Proposition
10.1

i\sigma\id-\dot{A}
is indeed invertible. Observe that it then
suffices to prove the resolvent estimate (
10.1
) for
|\sigma|\geq\sigma_{0}
for some
\sigma_{0}>0
.

Let
U=\phantom{}^{t}(u^{0},u^{1})\in D(\dot{A})
and
F=\phantom{}^{t}(f^{0},f^{1})\in\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
be such that

(i\sigma\id-\dot{A})U=F
. This reads

\displaystyle f^{0}=i\sigma u^{0}+u^{1},\qquad f^{1}=-\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0}+(i\sigma-\alpha)u^{1}.

which gives

(\mathsf{P}_{0}-\sigma^{2}-i\sigma\alpha)u^{0}=f

with
f=(i\sigma-\alpha)f^{0}-f^{1}
. Computing the
L^{2}
-inner product
with
u^{0}
one finds

\displaystyle((\mathsf{P}_{0}-\sigma^{2})u^{0},u^{0})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-i\sigma(\alpha u^{0},u^{0})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(f,u^{0})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

As
\alpha\geq 0
, computing the imaginary part one obtains

\displaystyle\sigma{\|\alpha^{1/2}u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=-\Im(f,u^{0})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
\alpha\geq\delta>0
in
\mathscr{O}
by assumption and since we consider

|\sigma|\geq\sigma_{0}
one has

\displaystyle\delta\sigma^{0}{\|u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O})}^{2}\leq{\|f\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}{\|u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Applying Theorem
8.2
(with Remark
8.3
) one has

\displaystyle{\|u^{0}\|}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}\lesssim e^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}}\big({\|f\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+{\|u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O})}\big),

replacing
|\sigma|
by
|\sigma|^{2}
therein.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle{\|u^{0}\|}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}\lesssim e^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}}\big({\|f\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+{\|f\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}{\|u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\big),

for
|\sigma|\geq\sigma_{0}
.
With Young inequality we write, for
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle e^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}}{\|f\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}{\|u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1}e^{2C|\sigma|^{1/2}}{\|f\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon{\|u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus, with
\varepsilon
chosen sufficiently small one obtains

\displaystyle{\|u^{0}\|}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}\lesssim e^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}}{\|f\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
u^{1}=f^{0}-i\sigma u^{0}
and
f=(i\sigma-\alpha)f^{0}-f^{1}
we finally obtain that

\displaystyle{\|u^{0}\|}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}+{\|u^{1}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}}\big({\|f^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+{\|f^{1}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}}{\|F\|}_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}.

Since
u^{0}\in H^{4}(\Omega)
one has

\displaystyle\big|(\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0},u^{0})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big|\leq{\|u^{0}\|}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}{\|u^{0}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq{\|u^{0}\|}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}

and thus one finally obtains

\displaystyle{\|U\|}_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}=(\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0},u^{0})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+{\|u^{1}\|}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{C|\sigma|^{1/2}}{\|F\|}_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{2},

which concludes the proof of the resolvent estimate (
10.1
).
∎

10.2.
Stabilization result

As an application of the resolvent estimate of
Theorem
10.2
, we give a logarithmic stabilization result of the damped
plate equation (
1.1
).

For the plate generator
(A,D(A))
its iterated domains are
inductively given by

\displaystyle D(A^{n+1})=\{U\in D(A^{n});AU\in D(A^{n})\}.

With Proposition
9.6
, for
Y^{0}=\phantom{}^{t}(y^{0},y^{1})\in D(A^{n})
then the first
component of
Y(t)=S(t)Y^{0}
is precisely the solution to (
9.1
).
One has
Y(t)=\dot{Y}(t)+\Pi_{\mathcal{N}}Y^{0}
with
\dot{Y}(t)=\dot{S}(t)\Pi_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}Y^{0}
with the semigroup
\dot{S}(t)

defined in Section

. Moreover,
by (
9.25
) the energy of
y(t)
is
given by the square of the
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
-norm of
\dot{Y}(t)
.

With the resolvent estimate of Theorem
10.2
, with the result of
Theorem 1.5 in
[
7
]
one obtains the following bound for the

y(t)
:

(10.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(y)(t)

\displaystyle={\|\dot{Y}(t)\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\big(\log(2+t)\big)^{4n}}{\|A^{n}Y^{0}\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}^{2}.

We have thus obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10.3

(logarithmic stabilization for the damped plate equation)
.

Assume that conditions (i) to (iv) of Section
9
hold. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 1
. Then, there exists
C>0
such
that for any
Y^{0}=\phantom{}^{t}(y^{0},y^{1})\in D(A^{n})
the associated
solution

of the damped plate equation (

) has the logarithmic energy decay given by (

Note that for

A.1.
A perfect elliptic estimate

Here we consider
a(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})
polynomial in the
\xi_{d}
variable and
such that its root have negative imaginary parts microlocally.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\kappa_{0}>0
.
Let
a(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})\in S_{\tau}^{k,0}
, with
\varrho^{\prime}=(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
and with

k\geq 1
,
that is,
a(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})=\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}(\varrho^{\prime})\xi_{d}^{k-j}
,
and where the coefficients
a_{j}
are homogeneous in

(\xi^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)
. Moreover, assume that
a_{0}(\varrho^{\prime})=1
.
Set
A=\mathrm{Op}(a)
.

Let
\mathscr{U}
be a conic open subset of
W\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
where
\tau\geq\kappa_{0}\sigma
and such
that all the roots of
a(\varrho^{\prime},\xi_{d})
have a negative imaginary part for
\varrho^{\prime}\in\mathscr{U}
.

Let
\chi(\varrho^{\prime})\in S_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$},\tau}^{0}
be homogeneous of degree zero and such
that
\supp(\chi)\subset\mathscr{U}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exist
C>0
,
C_{N}>0
, and
\tau_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle{\|\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(\chi)v\|}_{k,\tau}+{|\trace(\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(\chi)v)|}_{k-1,1/2,\tau}\leq C{\|A\mathrm{Op}_{\mbox{\tiny${\mathsf{T}}$}}(\chi)v\|}_{+}+{\|v\|}_{k,-N,\tau},

for
w\in\overline{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
\tau\geq\max(\tau_{0},\kappa_{0}\sigma)
.

We refer to
[
8
]
for a proof (see Lemma 4.1 therein and its
proof that adapts to the presence of the parameter
\sigma
with

in a straightforward manner).

A.2.
Basic resolvent estimation

Here we provide a proof of Lemma
9.3

Let
U=\phantom{}^{t}(u^{0},u^{1})\in D(\dot{A})
. With (
9.17
) We write

\displaystyle((z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A})U,U)_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{pmatrix}zu^{0}+u^{1}\\
zu^{1}-\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0}-\alpha u^{1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}u^{0}\\
u^{1}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}

\displaystyle=z{\|U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}^{2}+\langle\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{1},\overline{u^{0}}\rangle_{H^{-2}_{B},H^{2}_{B}}-(\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0},u^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\alpha u^{1},u^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=z{\|U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}^{2}+2i\Im(u^{1},\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\alpha u^{1},u^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Computing the real part one obtains

(A.1)

\displaystyle-\Re((z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A})U,U)_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}=-\Re(z){\|U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}^{2}+(\alpha u^{1},u^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

As
\alpha\geq 0
and
\Re z<0
, this gives

\displaystyle|\Re((z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A})U,U)_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}|\geq|\Re(z)|\,{\|U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}^{2},

which yields the conclusion of Lemma
9.3
. ∎

A.3.
Basic estimation for the resolvent set

Here we provide a proof of Proposition
10.1
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
. We consider the two cases.

Case 1:
\Re z<0
.

By Lemma
9.3

z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A}
is injective. Moreover, as its adjoint

\overline{z}\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A}^{*}
is injective and satisfies
{\|(\overline{z}\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A}^{*})U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}\gtrsim{\|U\|}_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}
for
U\in D(\dot{A})
by Lemma
9.5
the map

z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A}
is surjective (see for instance
[
9
, Theorem 2.20]
). The estimation of
Lemma
9.3
then gives the
continuity of the operator
(z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A})^{-1}
on
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

Case 2:
\Re z=0
.

We start by proving the injectivity of
z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A}
.
Let thus
U=\phantom{}^{t}(u^{0},u^{1})\in D(\dot{A})
be such that
zU-\dot{A}U=0
.
This gives

(A.2)

\displaystyle zu^{0}+u^{1}=0,\quad-\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0}+(z-\alpha)u^{1}=0.

First, if
z=0
one has
u^{1}=0
, and then
\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0}=0
. Thus,

u^{0}\in\ker(\mathsf{P}_{0})
given
U\in\mathcal{N}=\ker(A)
. From
the definition of
\dot{\mathcal{H}}
this gives
U=0
.

Second,
if now
z\neq 0
, using (
A.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\Re((z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A})U,U)_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}=-(\alpha u^{1},u^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

As
\alpha\geq 0
, this implies that
u^{0}
vanishes a.e. on
\supp(\alpha)
.
Observe that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{0}u^{0}=zu^{1}=-z^{2}u^{0}.

The function
u^{0}
is thus an eigenfunction for
\mathsf{P}_{0}
that
vanishes on an open set.
With the unique continuation property we obtain that

u^{0}
vanishes in
\Omega
and
u^{1}
as well.

If we now prove that
z\id_{\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}}-\dot{A}
is
surjective, the result then follows from the closed graph
theorem as
\dot{A}
is a closed operator. We write

z\id_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}-\dot{A}=T+\id_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}
with

T=(z-1)\id_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}-\dot{A}
. By the first part of the
proof,
T
is invertible with a bounded inverse. The operator

T
is unbounded on
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
. We denote by
\tilde{T}
the
restriction of
T
to
D(\dot{A})
equipped with the graph-norm
associated with
\dot{A}
. The operator
\tilde{T}
is bounded. It is also
invertible. It is thus a bounded
Fredholm operator of index
\ind\tilde{T}=0
. Similarly, we denote
by
\iota
the injection of
D(\dot{A})
into
\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}
and

\tilde{A}
the restriction of
\dot{A}
on
D(\dot{A})

viewed as a bounded operator. We have

z\iota-\tilde{A}=\tilde{T}+\iota
. Since
\iota

is a compact operator, we obtain that
z\iota-\tilde{A}
is
also a bounded Fredholm operator of index
0
.
Hence,
z\iota-\tilde{A}
is surjective since

z\id_{{\mathcal{H}}}-\dot{A}
is injective as proven above. Consequently,

z\id_{\mathcal{H}}-\dot{A}
is surjective.
This concludes the proof of Proposition
10.1
.
∎

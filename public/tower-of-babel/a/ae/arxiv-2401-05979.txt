Second Wilson number from third-order perturbation theory for the symmetric single-impurity Kondo model at low temperatures
Kevin Bauerbach, Florian Gebhard 1

Abstract
We determine the impurity-induced free energy and the impurity-induced zero-field susceptibility of the symmetric single-impurity Kondo model from weak-coupling perturbation theory up to third order in the Kondo coupling at low temperatures and small magnetic fields. We reproduce the analytical structure of the zero-field magnetic susceptibility as obtained from Wilson’s renormalization group method. This permits us to obtain analytically the first two Wilson numbers.



I
Overview

I.1
Introduction

The Kondo model represents one of the fundamental models in theoretical many-particle physics. It is the basis of a theoretical description
phenomena due to magnetic impurities in metals, e.g., the increase of the electrical resistance at low temperatures, typically below the so-called Kondo temperature
T_{\rm K}

de Haas et al. 1934
.
The underlying fundamental interaction is the antiferromagnetic
s
-
d
spin-exchange interaction
between a localized electron and the conduction electron spin polarization at the magnetic impurity
Zener 1951
.
At low temperatures,
T\ll T_{\rm K}
, the conduction-electron cloud completely screens the spin-1/2 impurity, i.e., the conduction-electron Kondo cloud
and the impurity spin form the so-called Kondo singlet state. Recently, the Kondo cloud was observed experimentally for the first time in a quantum dot
Borzenets et al. 2020
.

After the establishment of the model Hamiltonian by Kasuya in 1956
Kasuya 1956
and the first theoretical explanation of the electrical resistance minimum by Kondo in 1964
Kondo 1964
,
many important contributions to the solution of the Kondo problem were published in the 1970s and 1980s. These include, in particular,
Wilson’s renormalization group (RG)
Wilson 1975
treatment, the numerical renormalization group (NRG)
Wilson 1975
;
Krishna-murthy et al. 1980a
;
Krishna-murthy et al. 1980b
,
and also the Bethe Ansatz
Tsvelick and Wiegmann 1983a
;
Andrei et al. 1983a
treatments. A detailed historical overview of the physics of the Kondo model in the 20th century can be found in Hewson’s book
Hewson 1993
.
A more recent study
Barcza et al. 2019
treats the ground-state properties of the symmetric single-impurity Kondo model in detail, and compares
various analytical methods, e.g., the Gutzwiller and Yosida variational wave functions, and numerical approaches such as the density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) and
the NRG methods. Also in the year 2020, Bauerbach et al.
Bauerbach et al. 2020
introduced a simplification of the Kondo model, the so-called Ising-Kondo model,
in which the spin-exchange term of the full Kondo Hamiltonian is neglected. Thereby, the complexity of the Kondo model is reduced to that of potential scattering of spinless fermions,
which can be solved analytically using the equation of motion method.

The Kondo singlet is discernible in the magnetic susceptibility at zero field. The contribution of a free spin-1/2 to the zero-field magnetic susceptibility
is given by Curie’s law,

\frac{\chi_{0}^{\rm ii,free}(T)}{(g_{e}\mu_{\rm B})^{2}}=\frac{1}{4T}\;,

(1)

where
g_{e}\approx 2
is the electronic
g
-factor, and
\mu_{\rm B}
is the Bohr magneton, and the superscript ‘ii’ denotes the
impurity-induced contribution to the magnetic susceptibility.
Apparently,
\chi_{0}^{\rm free}(T)
diverges for
T\to 0
because the spin can freely orient itself in the magnetic field.
In the spin-1/2 Kondo model, the impurity spin is locked into the Kondo singlet at zero temperature with a small but finite excitation energy of the order
of
T_{\rm K}
. It is one of the central achievements of Wilson’s renormalization group method
to show that the impurity contribution to the zero-field magnetic susceptibility is finite, and given by
Wilson 1975

\frac{\chi_{0}^{\rm ii}(T=0)}{(g_{e}\mu_{\rm B})^{2}}=\frac{c_{0}}{\sqrt{\pi e}}\frac{D}{\tilde{D}(j)}\frac{\exp(1/j)}{\sqrt{j}}\exp\left[-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{r}j^{r}\right]\;,

(2)

where
j>0
is the antiferromagnetic Kondo coupling parameter,
c_{0}
and
\alpha_{r}
are undetermined constants,
and
\tilde{D}(j)/D
is the effective bandwidth in units of the bare bandwidth
D
.
For small couplings,
j\ll 1
, the numerical renormalization group can be used to calculate the unknown parameters with high accuracy, see, e.g., Ref.
Barcza et al. 2019

for a recent study. The constant

w=\frac{4c_{0}}{\sqrt{\pi e}}=\frac{e^{\gamma_{\rm E}+1/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\approx 0.410705\;,

(3)

where
\gamma_{\rm E}\approx 0.577216
is Euler’s number, is often called the “Wilson number”
Hewson 1993
.

More generally, the bandwidth renormalization factor has a Taylor series expansion,

\tilde{D}(j)=D\Bigl(c_{0}-c_{1}\frac{j}{2}+\mathcal{O}[j^{2}]\Bigr)\;,

(4)

where we denote
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
as the first and second Wilson numbers, respectively.
In this work, we determine the first two Wilson numbers analytically.

I.2
Summary of results

Wilson’s renormalization group gives an analytic expression for the impurity-induced zero-field magnetic susceptibility
at low temperatures
T
in terms of a series expansion in the Kondo coupling, see p. 830 in Ref.
Wilson 1975
, (
k\equiv k_{\rm B}\equiv 1
)

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{\rm ii,W}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}\biggl[1-j+j^{2}\ln\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}(j)}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle\qquad-j^{3}\left(\ln^{2}\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}(j)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}(j)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{O}[j^{4}]\biggr]\;.

In this work, we calculate the free energy of the symmetric single-impurity Kondo model up to third
order in
j
for small temperatures
T
and even smaller magnetic fields,
B\ll T\ll D\equiv 1
.
The expansion up to second order in
B
permits us to reproduce the analytic structure given in Eq. (
5
).
This is one of the major achievements of this work.

Starting from this comparison we
derive the effective bandwidth
\tilde{D}(j)
up to and including first order in
j
with the result

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3/4+\gamma_{\rm E}}}{4\pi}\approx 0.300049\;,

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3/4+\gamma_{\rm E}}}{32\pi}\left(-13+\pi^{2}-8\ln 2\right)\approx-0.325387

(6)

for the first two Wilson numbers.
While
c_{0}
is known from the literature, see, e.g., Refs.
Andrei et al. 1983a
;
Barcza et al. 2020
,
the second Wilson number
c_{1}
is a new result.

II
Anisotropic single-impurity Kondo model

In this section we define the model Hamiltonian of the
{\rm XXZ}
anisotropic single-impurity Kondo model and introduce the physical quantities of interest in this study.

II.1
Model Hamiltonian

The Hamiltonian of the anisotropic single-impurity Kondo model
reads
Hewson 1993
;
Schrieffer and Wolff 1966
;
Sólyom 2009

\hat{H}_{\rm K}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}_{\rm sd}

(7)

with

\hat{H}_{0}=\hat{T}+\hat{H}_{\rm m}\;,

(8)

where
\hat{T}
is the kinetic energy of the host electrons,

\hat{V}_{\rm sd}
is their interaction with the impurity spin at the lattice origin, and

\hat{H}_{\rm m}
describes the electrons’ interaction with an external magnetic field.

II.1.1
Host electrons

The kinetic energy of the host electrons is given by

\hat{T}=\sum_{\sigma}\hat{T}_{\sigma}\;,\;\hat{T}_{\sigma}=\sum_{i,j}t_{i,j}\hat{c}_{i,\sigma}^{+}\hat{c}_{j,\sigma}^{\vphantom{+}}\;.

(9)

Here,
\hat{c}_{i,\sigma}^{+}
(
\hat{c}_{i,\sigma}^{\vphantom{+}}
) creates
(annihilates) an electron with spin
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
on lattice site
i
,
where
t_{i,j}=t_{j,i}^{*}
are the matrix elements for the tunneling
of an electron from site
j
to site
i
on a lattice with
L
sites (
L
is even).
Assuming translational invariance,
t_{i,j}=t(i-j)
, the
kinetic energy is diagonal in momentum space
Barcza et al. 2020
,

\hat{T}_{\sigma}=\sum_{k}\epsilon(k)\hat{a}_{k,\sigma}^{+}\hat{a}_{k,\sigma}^{\vphantom{+}}\;,

(10)

where

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{r}e^{{\rm i}kr}\hat{c}^{+}_{r,\sigma}\;,

\displaystyle\hat{c}^{+}_{r,\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k}e^{-{\rm i}kr}\hat{a}^{+}_{k\sigma}

(11)

with
k
from the first Brillouin zone.
The corresponding density of states of the host electrons is given by

\rho_{0}(\omega)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k}\delta(\omega-\epsilon(k))\;.

(12)

We assume a bipartite lattice so that
there exists half a reciprocal lattice vector
Q
for which

\epsilon(Q-k)=-\epsilon(k)
for all
k
from the first Brillouin zone.
As a consequence, the density of states of the host electrons is symmetric,

\rho_{0}(-\omega)=\rho_{0}(\omega)
.

In the following, we set half the bandwidth
W
as our energy unit, i.e.,
W=2
,
so that
\rho_{0}(|\omega|>1)=0
.
To be definite, we choose to work with a constant density of states,

\rho_{0}(|\omega|\leq 1)=\frac{1}{2}\;.

(13)

We shall consider
the case where the paramagnetic host electron system is half-filled, i.e.,
the electron numbers fulfill

N_{\uparrow}=N_{\downarrow}=L/2
and
N=N_{\uparrow}+N_{\downarrow}=L
;
the thermodynamic limit,
N,L\to\infty
with
n=N/L=1

fixed, is implicit. In the supplemental material we outline a strategy to generalize the results
to a general density of states that is finite at the Fermi energy.

II.1.2
Kondo interaction

In the (anisotropic) Kondo model, the host electrons interact locally
with the impurity spin at the origin,

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\rm sd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\perp}+\hat{V}_{z}\;,

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\perp}\left(\hat{s}^{x}_{0}\hat{S}^{x}+\hat{s}^{y}_{0}\hat{S}^{y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{\perp}}{2}\left(\hat{s}_{0}^{+}\hat{S}^{-}+\hat{s}_{0}^{-}\hat{S}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{\perp}}{2L}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\left(\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{d}^{+}_{\Downarrow}\hat{d}^{\phantom{i}}_{\Uparrow}+\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}\hat{d}^{+}_{\Uparrow}\hat{d}^{\phantom{i}}_{\Downarrow}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\rm z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{z}\hat{s}^{z}_{0}\hat{S}^{z}

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{z}}{4L}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\left(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}\right)\left(\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}-\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}\right),

where
\hat{V_{\perp}}
describes the spin-flip component of the Hamiltonian and
\hat{V}_{z}
is the
z
component. The operators
\hat{d}^{+}_{s}
(
\hat{d}^{\vphantom{+}}_{s}
) create (annihilate)
an impurity electron with spin
s=\;\Uparrow,\Downarrow
.
In Eq. (
14
) it is implicitly understood that
the impurity is either filled with an electron with
spin
\Uparrow
or with spin
\Downarrow
. The individual operators are defined as

\hat{S}^{+}=\hat{d}^{+}_{\Uparrow}\hat{d}^{\phantom{i}}_{\Downarrow}\;,\quad\hat{S}^{-}=\hat{d}^{+}_{\Downarrow}\hat{d}^{\phantom{i}}_{\Uparrow}\;,\quad\hat{n}^{d}_{s}=\hat{d}^{+}_{s}\hat{d}^{\phantom{i}}_{s}\;,

(15)

and

\hat{s}_{0}^{+}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}\;,\quad\hat{s}^{-}_{0}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}\;.

(16)

Note that in the isotropic single-impurity Kondo model we have
J_{\perp}=J_{z}\equiv J_{\rm K}
. Furthermore, for
J_{\perp}=0
,
J_{z}\neq 0
, we obtain the Ising-Kondo model
Bauerbach et al. 2020
.

II.1.3
External magnetic field

To investigate the magnetic properties of the Kondo model, we couple the electrons to a global external magnetic field

\hat{H}_{\rm m}=-B\left(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}\right)-B\sum_{i}\left(\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}-\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\right)

(17)

with the local density operators

\hat{n}^{d}_{s}
and

\hat{n}_{i,\sigma}=\hat{c}^{+}_{i,\sigma}\hat{c}^{\vphantom{+}}_{i,\sigma}
.
Here, the magnetic energy reads

B=g_{e}\mu_{\rm B}\mathcal{H}/2>0\;,

(18)

where
{\cal H}
is the external field,

g_{e}\approx 2
is the electrons’ gyromagnetic factor, and
\mu_{\rm B}

is Bohr’s magneton.

II.1.4
Particle-hole transformation

In the definition of the particle-hole transformation we include a spin-flip operation,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau}_{S}:\;

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{k,\sigma}^{\vphantom{+}}\mapsto\hat{a}_{Q-k,\bar{\sigma}}^{+}\;,\hat{d}_{s}^{\vphantom{+}}\mapsto\hat{d}_{\bar{s}}^{+}\;,

(19)

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{k,\sigma}^{+}\mapsto\hat{a}_{Q-k,\bar{\sigma}}^{\vphantom{+}}\;,\hat{d}_{s}^{+}\mapsto\hat{d}_{\bar{s}}^{\vphantom{+}}\;,

where
\bar{\uparrow}=\downarrow
(
\bar{\Uparrow}=\Downarrow
)
and
\bar{\downarrow}=\uparrow
(
\bar{\Downarrow}=\Uparrow
)
denotes the flipped spin. The transformation
\widetilde{\tau}_{S}
implies
\hat{c}_{0,\sigma}^{+}\mapsto\hat{c}_{0,\bar{\sigma}}^{\vphantom{+}}
.

It is not difficult to show that the chemical potential
\mu(T)=0
guarantees a half-filled host-electron system for all temperatures
T
, see also Ref.
Barcza et al. 2019
.
To see this, we consider the average particle number

\langle\hat{N}\rangle(\mu)=\frac{1}{Z(\mu)}{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta(\hat{H}-\mu\hat{N})}\hat{N}\right]

(20)

with the grand canonical partition function

Z(\mu)={\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta(\hat{H}-\mu\hat{N})}\right]\;.

(21)

The particle-hole transformation
\widetilde{\tau}_{S}
, Eq. (
19
), leaves the Hamiltonian invariant,
\tilde{\tau}_{S}:\;\hat{H}_{\rm K}\mapsto\hat{H}_{\rm K}
, but it affects the particle number operator,
\tilde{\tau}_{S}:\;\hat{N}\mapsto 2L-\hat{N}
. Thus,

Z(\mu)={\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta(\hat{H}-\mu(2L-\hat{N}))}\right]=e^{2L\beta\mu}Z(-\mu)

(22)

and

\displaystyle\langle\hat{N}\rangle(\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2L\beta\mu}\frac{1}{Z(-\mu)}{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta[\hat{H}-\mu(2L-\hat{N})]}(2L-\hat{N})\right]

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2L-\frac{1}{Z(-\mu)}{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta(\hat{H}+\mu\hat{N})}\hat{N}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2L-\langle\hat{N}\rangle(-\mu)\;.

This implies that
\langle\hat{N}\rangle(\mu=0)=L
for all
T
.

II.2
Impurity-induced zero-field susceptibility

In our study, we are interested in the impurity contribution to the zero-field magnetic susceptibility, which can be determined from the impurity contribution to the free energy.

II.2.1
Impurity-induced free energy

The free energy of the anisotropic single-impurity Kondo model is defined by (
\beta=1/T
,
k_{\rm B}\equiv 1
)

\mathcal{F}^{\rm aSIKM}(B,T,J_{\perp},J_{z})=-T\ln Z=-T\ln{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{\rm K}}\right]\;,

(24)

where
Z
is the partition function. In general, the free energy consists of a contribution of the host electrons and of an impurity-induced part,

\mathcal{F}^{\rm aSIKM}(B,T,J_{\perp},J_{z})=\mathcal{F}^{\rm h}(B,T)+\mathcal{F}^{\rm ii}(B,T,J_{\perp},J_{z})\;,

(25)

where

\mathcal{F}^{\rm h}(B,T)=-T\ln{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\right]\;.

(26)

Note that

\displaystyle Z_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\right]

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{F}\left[e^{-\beta\left(\hat{T}-B\left(\hat{N}_{\uparrow}-\hat{N}_{\downarrow}\right)\right)}\right]2\cosh(B/T)\;.

Here,
{\rm Tr}_{F}
means that we perform the trace only over the bath states.

We focus on the impurity-induced contribution to the free energy, whereby we drop the index ‘
{\rm ii}
’ in the following to simplify the notation,

\mathcal{F}^{\rm ii}(B,T,J_{\perp},J_{z})\equiv\mathcal{F}(B,T,J_{\perp},J_{z})\;.

(28)

II.2.2
Impurity-induced zero-field susceptibility

The impurity-induced zero-field susceptibility is defined as the second derivative of the impurity-induced free energy
Barcza et al. 2020

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{0}^{\rm ii}(B,T,J_{\perp},J_{z})}{(g_{e}\mu_{\rm B})^{2}}=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial B^{2}}\mathcal{F}(B,T,J_{\perp},J_{z})\;.

(29)

For the zero-field susceptibility, we need to know the free energy up to and including second order in
B
.

III
Perturbation theory at finite temperatures

In this section, we derive the impurity-induced free energy of the anisotropic single-impurity Kondo model
from weak-coupling perturbation theory up to and including third order in the Kondo coupling
at low temperatures and weak magnetic fields,
B\ll T\ll 1
.

III.1
Formal expansion

We define

\hat{A}(\tau)=e^{\tau\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{A}e^{-\tau\hat{H}_{0}}\;.

(30)

For our purposes, where
\hat{A}
does not change the total magnetization, we have

\hat{A}(\tau)=e^{\tau\hat{T}}\hat{A}e^{-\tau\hat{T}}

(31)

because
[\hat{A},\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}+\hat{N}_{\uparrow}-\hat{N}_{\downarrow}]_{-}=0
. Moreover, we define

\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{0}}{\rm Tr}\left(e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{A}\right)\;.

(32)

At small couplings we find the partition function

Z=Z_{0}(1-V_{1}+V_{2}-V_{3})

(33)

so that the impurity-induced contribution to the free energy can be written as a power series in the coupling,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(0)}+\mathcal{F}^{(1)}+\mathcal{F}^{(2)}+\mathcal{F}^{(3)}

(34)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T\ln(2\cosh(\beta B))\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle TV_{1}\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-TV_{2}+\frac{T}{2}V_{1}^{2}\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle TV_{3}-TV_{1}V_{2}+\frac{T}{3}V_{1}^{3}\;,

(35)

up to and including the third order in the coupling constant. The individual terms read

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\tau\,\langle\hat{V}(\tau)\rangle\;,

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\tau_{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{2}}{\rm d}\tau_{1}\,\langle\hat{V}(\tau_{2})\hat{V}(\tau_{1})\rangle\;,

\displaystyle V_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\tau_{3}\,\int_{0}^{\tau_{3}}{\rm d}\tau_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\tau_{2}}{\rm d}\tau_{1}\,\langle\hat{V}(\tau_{3})\hat{V}(\tau_{2})\hat{V}(\tau_{1})\rangle\;.

In the following, we separately discuss the individual orders of the perturbation theory.

III.2
Leading order term

From Eq. (
III.1
) we find the leading order term

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T\ln(2\cosh(B/T))

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T\ln 2-\frac{B^{2}}{2T}+\mathcal{O}[B^{3}]

for
B\ll T\ll 1
.

III.3
First order term

The integrand in the first order term from Eq. (
III.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{0}}{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}e^{\tau\hat{T}}\hat{V}e^{-\tau\hat{T}}\right]

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{0}}{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}\right]=\langle\hat{V}\rangle\;,

where we used the fact that the trace is cyclic and that
[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{T}]=0
. Moreover,
{\rm Tr}
is the trace over states with definite spin orientation into the
z
-direction.
This implies that we need an even number of spin-flip operators within the trace, see Eq. (
15
), to obtain a finite result.
For example, for an odd number of spin-flip operators, we see for an expectation value of impurity operators

\langle\Uparrow|\hat{S}^{+}|\Uparrow\rangle=\langle\Downarrow|\hat{S}^{-}|\Downarrow\rangle=0\;.

(39)

Hence,

\langle\hat{V}(\tau)\rangle=\langle\hat{V}\rangle=\langle\hat{V}_{z}\rangle\;.

(40)

With

\hat{M}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\left(\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}-\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k,\downarrow}\right)\;,

(41)

we find

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\tau\,\langle\hat{V}(\tau)\rangle

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\langle\hat{V}_{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{z}}{4T}\frac{1}{Z_{0}}{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\left(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}\right)\hat{M}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{z}}{4T}\frac{2\sinh(\beta B)}{2\cosh(\beta B)}\langle\hat{M}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{z}}{4T}\tanh(\beta B)M_{0}(B,T)\;,

where we defined

\displaystyle M_{0}(B,T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{M}\rangle=\frac{1}{L}\left(N_{\uparrow}(B,T)-N_{\downarrow}(B,T)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon-B)}}-\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon+B)}}\right)

because

\displaystyle n_{k,\uparrow}\equiv\langle\hat{n}_{k,\uparrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon(k)-B)}}\;,

\displaystyle n_{k,\downarrow}\equiv\langle\hat{n}_{k,\downarrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon(k)+B)}}

(44)

hold for the non-interacting host electrons.

At low temperatures and small fields,
B\ll T\ll 1
, we have

\displaystyle\tanh(B/T)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{B}{T}\;,

\displaystyle M_{0}(B,T)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2B}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\frac{\rho_{0}(\epsilon)}{\left[2\cosh\left(\epsilon/(2T)\right)\right]^{2}}

(45)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2B}{T}\rho_{0}(0)T=2B\rho_{0}(0)\;.

We define
j_{z}=J_{z}\rho_{0}(0)
and use Eq. (
III.1
) to find the first order contribution

V_{1}\approx\frac{B^{2}}{2T^{2}}j_{z}\;,\quad\mathcal{F}^{(1)}=TV_{1}\approx\frac{B^{2}}{2T}j_{z}\;.

(46)

III.4
Second-order term

According to Eq. (
III.1
), the second-order term reads

\mathcal{F}^{(2)}=-TV_{2}+\frac{T}{2}V_{1}^{2}\;,

(47)

with
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
from Eq. (
III.1
). With our results of the first order, see Eq. (
46
), we find

\frac{T}{2}V_{1}^{2}=\frac{T}{2}\frac{B^{4}}{4T^{4}}j_{z}^{2}=\frac{B^{4}}{8T^{3}}j_{z}^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}[B^{4}]\;,

(48)

so we can ignore this contribution at small fields.
We are left with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-TV_{2}+\mathcal{O}[B^{3}].

(49)

With Eq. (
III.1
), we have

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\left\langle\hat{V}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}(\lambda_{1})\right\rangle

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}e^{\lambda_{2}\hat{T}}\hat{V}e^{(-\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1})\hat{T}}\hat{V}e^{-\lambda_{1}\hat{T}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}e^{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\hat{T}}\hat{V}e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\hat{T}}\right]\;,

where made use of the fact that the trace is cyclic. The substitution
\lambda=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
then leads to

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda\,\left\langle\hat{V}\hat{V}(-\lambda)\right\rangle\int_{\lambda}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda\,(\beta-\lambda)\left\langle\hat{V}\hat{V}(-\lambda)\right\rangle\;.

Now we use the fact that the trace runs over definite spin states, see section
III.3
,

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda\,(\beta-\lambda)\left\langle\hat{V}_{z}\hat{V}_{z}(-\lambda)\right\rangle

(52)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda\,(\beta-\lambda)\left\langle\hat{V}_{\perp}\hat{V}_{\perp}(-\lambda)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{2}^{z}+V_{2}^{\perp}\;.

The calculations of
V_{2}^{z}
and
V_{2}^{\perp}
are rather lengthy, and thus moved to the Appendix. In Appendix
A
we show that

\displaystyle V_{2}^{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{j_{z}^{2}}{8T}\left[-2\ln 2+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}T^{2}+B^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{T}\right]\;,

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{j_{\perp}^{2}}{4T}\biggl[-2\ln 2+\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{3}

\displaystyle\qquad+B^{2}\left(1-\frac{2}{T}\left(-1-\gamma_{\rm E}+\ln(2\pi T)\right)\right)\biggr]\;.

Note that
V_{2}^{z}
is the second-order Ising-Kondo contribution to the single-impurity Kondo model, see Ref.
Bauerbach et al. 2020
.
In Sect. SM I of the Supplemental Material, we explicitly calculate the exact result of the free energy of the Ising-Kondo model
for a constant density of states, see Eq. (
13
).
In this way, we can compare the results from perturbation theory of Appendix
A
with those from the Taylor series of the exact result.
Evidently, the two results agree.

Hence, with Eq. (
49
) the second order of the impurity-induced free energy reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\left(-2\ln 2+\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{3}\right)j_{z}^{2}+\frac{B^{2}}{8}j_{z}^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{8T}j_{z}^{2}

(54)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(-2\ln 2+\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{3}\right)j_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{B^{2}}{4}j_{\perp}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{B^{2}}{2T}j_{\perp}^{2}\left(1+\gamma_{\rm E}-\ln(2\pi T)\right)

with
j_{\perp}=J_{\perp}\rho_{0}(0)
. This result agrees with our previous calculation
Barcza et al. 2020
.

III.5
Third-order term

According to Eq. (
III.1
), the third order is given by

\mathcal{F}^{(3)}=TV_{3}-TV_{1}V_{2}+\frac{T}{3}V_{1}^{3}\;,

(55)

where
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
were determined in eqs. (
46
), (
52
) and (
III.4
).
Since
V_{1}\propto B^{2}/T^{2}
, we see that
(T/3)V_{1}^{3}\sim B^{6}/T^{5}
which is negligible for
B\ll T\ll 1
. In this limit, the remaining contribution is

\mathcal{F}^{(3)}=TV_{3}-TV_{1}V_{2}(B=0)\;,

(56)

where
TV_{1}V_{2}(B=0)
is known, and we may focus on
V_{3}
.
In the second term we only need
V_{2}(B=0)
because
V_{1}
is already second order in
B
, see Eq. (
46
). From Eq. (
III.1
) we find

\displaystyle V_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{3}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{3}}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

(57)

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}(\lambda_{3})\hat{V}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}(\lambda_{1})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{3}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{3}}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\tau_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\bigg[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\left(\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{3})+\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{3})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{2})+\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{2})\right)\left(\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{1})+\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{1})\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{3}^{z}+V_{3}^{a}+V_{3}^{b}+V_{3}^{c}

with the four contributions

\displaystyle V_{3}^{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{3}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{3}}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

(58)

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{3})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{1})\right]\;,

\displaystyle V_{3}^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{3}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{3}}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

(59)

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{3})\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{1})\right]\;,

\displaystyle V_{3}^{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{3}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{3}}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

(60)

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{3})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{1})\right]\;,

\displaystyle V_{3}^{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{3}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{3}}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\frac{1}{Z_{0}}

(61)

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{3})\hat{V}_{\perp}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{1})\right]\;.

Again, we used the fact that the trace runs over definite spin states. Therefore, we need an even number of spin-flip operations within the trace.

The Ising-Kondo contribution
V_{z}^{(3)}
is known from the exact result, see Sect. SM I of the Supplemental Material, (
V_{1}^{z}\equiv V_{1}
)

V_{3}^{z}-V_{1}V_{2}^{z}+\frac{V_{1}^{3}}{3}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{96}\frac{B^{2}}{T^{2}}j_{z}^{3}

(62)

and thus

V_{3}^{z}=B^{2}j_{z}^{3}\left(\frac{\ln 2}{8T^{3}}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{96T^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{48T}\right)+\mathcal{O}[B^{3}]

(63)

with
j_{z}=J_{z}\rho_{0}(0)
.

The calculation of the terms
V_{3}^{i}
in Eq. (
57
),
i=a,b,c
, are very lengthy,
so we defer their analysis to Appendix
B
, and simply state the result of the third-order contribution to the free energy,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j_{\perp}^{2}j_{z}\biggl[-\frac{12\ln(2)^{2}+\pi^{2}}{16}+\frac{\pi^{2}T}{4}

(64)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{4}(-1+\ln 2)+\mathcal{O}[B^{0}T^{3}]\biggr]

\displaystyle+B^{2}j_{\perp}^{2}j_{z}\left(\frac{3}{4}\left(-2+\ln 2\right)+\mathcal{O}[T]\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{B^{2}}{T}j_{z}^{3}\frac{\pi^{2}}{96}+\mathcal{O}[j_{z}^{3}B^{2}T]

\displaystyle+\frac{B^{2}}{2T}j_{\perp}^{2}j_{z}\biggl[1+\gamma_{\rm E}+\gamma_{\rm E}^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}+\frac{\ln 2}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\ln^{2}(2)+2\ln 2\ln\pi+\ln^{2}(\pi)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\ln(2\pi T)(1+2\gamma_{\rm E})+2\ln(2\pi)\ln T

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\ln^{2}(T)\biggr]+\mathcal{O}[B^{3}]\>.

IV
Zero-field susceptibility

In this section, we determine the zero-field susceptibility and compare our result with the series expansion of the zero-field susceptibility determined by Wilson using his renormalization group
Wilson 1975
. From now on we consider the isotropic single-impurity Kondo model,

J_{z}\equiv J_{\perp}\equiv J_{\rm K}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad j_{z}=j_{\perp}=j\;,

(65)

where
j=J_{\rm K}\rho_{0}(0)
.

IV.1
Calculation

Using the definition of the zero-field susceptibility (
29
) and eqs. (
34
), (
37
), (
46
), (
54
)
and (
64
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{0}^{\rm ii}}{(g_{e}\mu_{\rm B})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(0)}+\chi_{0}^{(1)}+\chi_{0}^{(2)}+\chi_{0}^{(3)}+\mathcal{O}[j^{4}]

(66)

with [
j_{\perp}=j_{z}=j=J_{\rm K}\rho_{0}(0)
]

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}+\mathcal{O}[T^{0},j^{0}]\;,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4T}j+\mathcal{O}[T^{0},j^{1}]\;,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}j^{2}\frac{1}{4}\left(-3-4\gamma_{\rm E}+4\ln(2\pi T)\right)+\mathcal{O}[T^{0},j^{2}]\;,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}j^{2}\left(-\frac{3}{4}-\gamma_{\rm E}+\ln(2\pi T)\right)+\mathcal{O}[T^{0},j^{2}]\;,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}j^{3}\biggl[-1-\gamma_{\rm E}-\gamma_{\rm E}^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}-\frac{\ln 2}{2}-\ln^{2}(2)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad-2\ln 2\ln\pi-\ln^{2}(\pi)+\ln(2\pi T)(1+2\gamma_{\rm E})

\displaystyle\quad\qquad-2\ln(2\pi)\ln T-\ln^{2}(T)\biggr]+\mathcal{O}[T^{0},j^{3}]\;.

IV.2
Zero-field susceptibility from Wilson’s renormalization group

Wilson used his renormalization group to derive the zero-field susceptibility
in terms of a series expansion, see p. 830 in Ref.
Wilson 1975
, (
k=k_{\rm B}=1
)

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{\rm ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}\biggl[1+2J_{\rm W}\rho_{0}(0)+4(J_{\rm W}\rho_{0}(0))^{2}\ln\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(J_{\rm W}\rho_{0}(0))^{3}\left(8\ln^{2}\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}}\right)+4\ln\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}}\right)\right)\biggr]

with Wilson’s coupling constant
J_{\rm W}
and

\tilde{D}\equiv\tilde{D}(J_{\rm W}\rho_{0}(0))=D(c_{0}+c_{1}J_{\rm W}\rho_{0}(0)+\mathcal{O}[j^{2}])\;,

(69)

where
D
is the band width;
D\equiv 2
in our work.
Our goal is to determine analytical expressions for the first and second Wilson numbers
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
.

IV.3
Comparison

Now we are in the position to compare the individual orders in the coupling
j
between our zero-field susceptibility
\chi_{0}^{\rm ii}
from Eq. (
IV.1
)
with those of Wilson’s
\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{\rm ii}
from Eq. (
IV.2
). The leading orders, describing a free spin, naturally agree. From first order we identify

2J_{\rm W}\rho_{0}(0)\equiv-j=-J_{\rm K}\rho_{0}(0)\;.

(70)

Therefore, we can rewrite Eq. (
IV.2
) in terms of our coupling parameter
j
in the form

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{\rm ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}\biggl[1-j+j^{2}\ln\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}}\right)

(71)

\displaystyle\qquad-j^{3}\left(\ln^{2}\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{T}{\tilde{D}}\right)\right)\biggr]\;.

Furthermore, using Eq. (
69
), we find that Wilson’s constant term to second order can be written as

\displaystyle-j^{2}\ln\tilde{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-j^{2}\ln\left(2c_{0}-jc_{1}+\mathcal{O}[j^{2}]\right)

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-j^{2}\left[\ln(2c_{0})+\ln\left(1-\frac{jc_{1}}{2c_{0}}+\mathcal{O}[j^{2}]\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-j^{2}\left[\ln(2c_{0})-j\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{0}}+\mathcal{O}[j^{2}]\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-j^{2}\ln(2c_{0})+j^{3}\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{0}}+\mathcal{O}[j^{4}]\;,

where we used that
j\ll 1
in the next to last step. Thus, we have

-\ln\tilde{D}=-\ln(2c_{0})+j\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{0}}+\mathcal{O}[j^{2}]\;.

(73)

Using this expression, we obtain for Eq. (
71
) up to and including the third order in
j

\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{\rm ii}=\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(0)}+\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(1)}+\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(2)}+\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(3)}+\mathcal{O}[j^{4}]

(74)

with

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}\;,

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4T}j\;,

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}j^{2}\left(\ln T-\ln(2c_{0})\right)\;,

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4T}j^{3}\bigg(-\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{0}}+\ln^{2}(T)-2\ln T\ln(2c_{0})

(75)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\ln^{2}(2c_{0})+\frac{\ln T}{2}-\frac{\ln(2c_{0})}{2}\bigg)\,.

The leading and first-order terms are seen to agree explicitly, as discussed before. From the comparison of eqs. (
IV.1
) and (
74
)
to second order we can determine the first Wilson number
c_{0}
,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}j^{2}\frac{1}{4}\left(-3-4\gamma_{\rm E}+4\ln(2\pi T)\right)

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}j^{2}\left(\ln T-\ln(2c_{0})\right)

(76)

which leads to

\displaystyle\ln(2c_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}+\gamma_{\rm E}-\ln(2\pi)\approx-0.510661\;,

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3/4+\gamma_{\rm E}}}{4\pi}\approx 0.300049\;.

(77)

This result was obtained earlier, e.g., in Refs.
Andrei et al. 1983a
;
Barcza et al. 2020
.
Frequently, the number
w=4c_{0}/(\sqrt{\pi e})\approx 0.410705
is denoted ‘the’ Wilson number, see Eq. (
3
).

Finally, from third order,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4T}j^{3}\bigg(-1-\gamma_{\rm E}-\gamma_{\rm E}^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}-\frac{\ln 2}{2}-\ln^{2}(2)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-2\ln 2\ln\pi-\ln^{2}(\pi)+\ln(2\pi T)[1+2\gamma_{\rm E}]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-2\ln(2\pi)\ln T-\ln^{2}(T)\bigg)\;,

\displaystyle\chi_{0,{\rm W}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4T}j^{3}\bigg(-\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{0}}+\ln^{2}(T)-2\ln T\ln(2c_{0})

(78)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\ln^{2}(2c_{0})+\frac{\ln T}{2}-\frac{\ln(2c_{0})}{2}\bigg)\;,

we obtain the second Wilson number

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-13+\pi^{2}}{16}-\frac{\ln 2}{2}\approx-0.542223\;,

(79)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3/4+\gamma_{\rm E}}}{32\pi}\left(-13+\pi^{2}-8\ln 2\right)\approx-0.325387\;.

V
Final remarks

As seen from the analytical structure of the perturbation expansion in Eq. (
IV.2
),
the theory cannot be applied at very low temperatures because of the logarithmic divergences.
Eq. (
2
) shows that the
zero-field susceptibility at zero temperature to first order in
j

also requires the coefficient
\alpha_{1}
that is not within reach using
our perturbation theory approach. It was determined numerically by Wilson
Wilson 1975
, and also in Ref.
Barcza et al. 2019
.
Unfortunately, we do not have a recipe to determine
\alpha_{1}
analytically. Our attempts to use perturbation theory
at zero temperature and finite magnetic field failed because we were not able to sum the contributions
proportional to
j\ln(B)
.

We also tried to make contact with the Bethe Ansatz solution at finite temperatures.
The leading-order corrections were extracted from the Takahashi equations
Andrei et al. 1983b
;
Tsvelick and Wiegmann 1983b
,
see Refs.
Hewson 1993
;
Barcza et al. 2020
for a short review. Bortz and Klümper
Bortz and Klümper 2004
succeeded to derive
the free energy at finite fields from two coupled integral equations that can be treated numerically, and confirmed the earlier results to second order
in the Kondo coupling. Unfortunately, we did not succeed to derive a systematic series expansion of the Klümper equations
for weak coupling so that we cannot compare Bethe Ansatz results with those from our third-order perturbation theory.
Such a comparison would permit to identify the parameters in the Bethe Ansatz treatment with those of the lattice model.

Appendix A
Derivation of the second-order contributions

In this section we derive the two contributions to second order of the impurity-induced free energy from Sect.
III.4
, namely,
the Ising-Kondo contribution and the spin-flip contribution.

A.1
Second-order Ising-Kondo contribution

To derive the second-order Ising-Kondo contribution, we start from Eq. (
52
)

V_{2}^{z}=\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\left\langle\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{1})\right\rangle\;,

(A.1)

see also eqs. (
II.1.2
), (
30
), and (
32
). Recall that
the free Hamiltonian for the Kondo model in a magnetic field is defined by (
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
)

\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{k,\sigma}\left(\epsilon(k)-\sigma B\right)\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\sigma}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k,\sigma}-B\left(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}\right)\;.

(A.2)

In momentum space, the Ising-Kondo coupling reads

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{z}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{z}}{4}\left(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}\right)

(A.3)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}e^{(\epsilon(k)-\epsilon(k^{\prime}))\lambda}\left(\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}-\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}\right)\;,

where
\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}
does not change the impurity-spin
s=\Uparrow,\Downarrow
,
just as
\hat{c}^{+}_{0,\uparrow}\hat{c}^{\phantom{+}}_{0,\uparrow}-\hat{c}^{+}_{0,\downarrow}\hat{c}^{\phantom{+}}_{0,\downarrow}
does not change the
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
spins in the host-electron bath.

We use

\left(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}\right)\left(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}\right)=\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}+\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}-2\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}=1\;,

(A.4)

since
\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}+\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}=1
and
\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow}=0
. This identity is a direct consequence of the single-impurity model. We find

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{J_{z}}{4L}\right)^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime},p,p^{\prime}}\left\langle\hat{M}_{k,k^{\prime}}\hat{M}_{p,p^{\prime}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\times\,e^{(\epsilon(k)-\epsilon(k^{\prime}))\lambda_{2}}e^{(\epsilon(p)-\epsilon(p^{\prime}))\lambda_{1}}\;,

where

\hat{M}_{k,k^{\prime}}=\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}-\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}\;.

(A.6)

With

n_{k,\uparrow}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon(k)-B)}}\;,\qquad n_{k,\downarrow}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon(k)+B)}}

(A.7)

we further obtain [
\hat{V}_{1}\hat{V}_{2}\equiv\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{1})
]

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}_{1}\hat{V}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{J_{z}}{4L}\right)^{2}\sum_{k,p}\left(n_{k,\uparrow}-n_{k,\downarrow}\right)\left(n_{p,\uparrow}-n_{p,\downarrow}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{J_{z}}{4L}\right)^{2}\sum_{k,p}e^{(\epsilon(k)-\epsilon(p))(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(n_{k,\uparrow}(1-n_{p,\uparrow})+n_{k,\downarrow}(1-n_{p,\downarrow})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{J_{z}}{4}\right)^{2}\left(M_{0}(B)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{J_{z}}{4}\right)^{2}\Bigl(f(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1},B)f(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1},-B)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+f(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1},-B)f(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1},B)\Bigr)

with
M_{0}
from Eq. (
III.3
) and

f(\lambda,B)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k}n_{k,\uparrow}e^{\epsilon(k)\lambda}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{\epsilon\lambda}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon-B)}}\;.

(A.9)

Note that

\displaystyle 1-n_{p,\uparrow}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon(p)-B)}-1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon(p)-B)}}=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon(p)-B)}}

and therefore [
\rho_{0}(-\epsilon)=\rho_{0}(\epsilon)
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\sum_{p}e^{-\epsilon(p)\lambda}(1-n_{p,\uparrow})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{-\beta\lambda}}{1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon-B)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{\epsilon\lambda}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon+B)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\lambda,-B)\;,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k}e^{\epsilon(k)\lambda}n_{k,\downarrow}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{\epsilon\lambda}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon-B)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\lambda,-B)\;,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\sum_{p}e^{-\epsilon(p)\lambda}(1-n_{p,\downarrow})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\lambda,B)\;.

(A.11)

In the next step, we use the substitution
\lambda=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
, so that for a general function
h(x)
we can reduces the double integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,h(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda\,h(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda\,h(\lambda)\int_{\lambda}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda\,(\beta-\lambda)h(\lambda)\;,

into a single integral.
For a more detailed derivation, see Sect. SM-VI B 1 in the Supplemental Material. In Eq. (
A.1
) this results in

\displaystyle V_{2}^{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda_{2}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}}{\rm d}\lambda_{1}\,\left\langle\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{2})\hat{V}_{z}(\lambda_{1})\right\rangle

(A.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{J_{z}}{4}M_{0}(B)\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{J_{z}}{4}\right)^{2}\ell(B,T)\;\;

with

\ell(B,T)=\int_{0}^{\beta}{\rm d}\lambda\,(\beta-\lambda)f(\lambda,B)f(\lambda,-B)\;.

(A.14)

Apparently, the problem is reduced to the calculation of the integral
\ell(B,T)
.
The derivation of this integral is lengthy and can be found in the Supplemental Material, Sect. SM V A.
At
B\ll T\ll 1
the result is

\displaystyle\ell(B,T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\left(-2\ln 2+\frac{T^{2}\pi^{2}}{3}+B^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}B^{2}T^{2}\right)

(A.15)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}[B^{3}]\;.

Finally, with eqs. (
A.13
) and (
III.3
), i.e.,
M_{0}(B\ll 1)\approx 2B\rho_{0}(0)
, we obtain [
j_{z}=J_{z}\rho_{0}(0)
]

\displaystyle V_{2}^{z}=-\frac{1}{T}\left(-2\ln 2+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}T^{2}+B^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{T}\right)\left(\frac{j_{z}^{2}}{8}\right)\;,

in agreement with the analytical solution in Ref.
Bauerbach et al. 2020
, see also Eq. (S342) of the Supplemental Material.

A.2
Second-order spin-flip contribution

Now we calculate the spin-flip contribution to second order. Using Eq. (
II.1.2
), (
30
), and (

), we
evaluate

V_{2}^{\perp}(\lambda)=\frac{J_{\perp}^{2}}{4}\Big\langle\hat{s}_{0}^{+}\hat{S}^{-}\hat{s}_{0}^{-}(-\lambda)\hat{S}^{+}+\hat{s}_{0}^{-}\hat{S}^{+}\hat{s}_{0}^{+}(-\lambda)\hat{S}^{-}\Big\rangle\;,

(A.17)

because
\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\lambda)=\hat{\mathbf{S}}
for the local spin. Then, [
\beta=1/T
]

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{\perp}^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\left\langle\Downarrow|e^{\beta B(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow})}|\Downarrow\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{s}^{+}_{0}\hat{s}_{0}^{-}(-\lambda)\right\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\Uparrow|e^{\beta B(\hat{n}^{d}_{\Uparrow}-\hat{n}^{d}_{\Downarrow})}|\Uparrow\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{s}^{-}_{0}\hat{s}_{0}^{+}(-\lambda)\right\rangle_{F}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{J_{\perp}}{2L}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\bigg(e^{-\beta B}\left\langle\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}e^{-\lambda\hat{T}}\hat{a}^{+}_{p,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{p^{\prime},\uparrow}e^{\lambda\hat{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+e^{\beta B}\left\langle\hat{a}^{+}_{k,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}e^{-\lambda\hat{T}}\hat{a}^{+}_{p,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\phantom{+}}_{p^{\prime},\downarrow}e^{\lambda\hat{T}}\right\rangle\bigg)\;.

The application of Wick’s theorem then leads to

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{J_{\perp}}{2L}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\bigg(e^{-\beta B}e^{\lambda(\epsilon(k)-\epsilon(k^{\prime}))}n_{k,\uparrow}\left(1-n_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+e^{\beta B}e^{\lambda(\epsilon(k)-\epsilon(k^{\prime}))}n_{k,\downarrow}(1-n_{k^{\prime},\uparrow})\bigg).

We introduce the function
\tilde{f}(\lambda,B)
, see also Eq. (
A.9
),

\tilde{f}(\lambda,B)=\frac{f(\lambda,B)}{\rho_{0}(0)}=\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon\frac{\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)e^{\lambda\epsilon}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon-B)}}

(A.20)

with
\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)=\rho_{0}(\epsilon)/\rho_{0}(0)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k}e^{\lambda\epsilon(k)}n_{k,\uparrow}=\rho_{0}(0)\tilde{f}(\lambda,B)\;.

(A.21)

Furthermore, with
\rho_{0}(\epsilon)=\rho_{0}(-\epsilon)
we find

\displaystyle p(\lambda,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k}e^{-\lambda\epsilon(k^{\prime})}(1-n_{k^{\prime},\downarrow})

(A.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)e^{-\lambda\epsilon}\left(1-\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon+B)}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{-\lambda\epsilon}}{1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon+B)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\rho_{0}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{\lambda\epsilon}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon-B)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(0)\tilde{f}(\lambda,B)\;.

Thus, [
j_{\perp}=J_{\perp}\rho_{0}(0)
]

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{j_{\perp}}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(e^{-\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\lambda,B)+e^{\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\lambda,-B)\right)\;.

We note that

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\beta-\lambda,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{(\beta-\lambda)\epsilon}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon-B)}}

(A.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon\,\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)\frac{e^{\lambda\epsilon}e^{-\beta\epsilon}e^{\beta B}e^{-\beta B}}{1+e^{-\beta}(\epsilon+B)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\beta B}\tilde{f}(\lambda,-B)\;,

where we used
\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)=\tilde{\rho}(-\epsilon)
. Then,

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta/2}{\rm d}\lambda\,(\beta-\lambda)\frac{j_{\perp}^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(e^{-\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\lambda,B)+e^{\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\lambda,-B)\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\beta/2}{\rm d}\mu\,\mu\frac{j_{\perp}^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}\Bigl(e^{-\beta B}e^{2\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\mu,-B)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+e^{\beta B}e^{-2\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\mu,B)\Bigr)\;,

where we substituted
\mu=\beta-\lambda
. Thus,

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{0}^{\beta/2}{\rm d}\lambda\,\left(\frac{j_{\perp}}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

(A.26)

\displaystyle\times\left(e^{-\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\lambda,B)+e^{\beta B}\tilde{f}^{2}(\lambda,-B)\right)\,.

(A.27)

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}=V_{2,\alpha}^{\perp}+V_{2,\beta}^{\perp}

(A.28)

with

\displaystyle V_{2,\alpha}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\left(\frac{j_{\perp}}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

(A.29)

\displaystyle\times\biggl(e^{-\beta B}\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon_{1}\,\frac{\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon_{1})}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon_{1}-B)}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\times\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon_{2}\,\frac{\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon_{2})}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon_{2}-B)}}\left(\frac{e^{\beta/2(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})}}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left[B\to(-B)\right]\bigg)\;.

The second term in
V_{2,\alpha}^{\perp}
, where we simply have to change the sign of
B
, is the negative of the first term. This can be seen by performing the substitution
\epsilon_{1}\to-\epsilon_{2}
and
\epsilon_{2}\to-\epsilon_{1}
in the first term, so that the term vanishes altogether,

(A.30)

Thus,

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{2,\beta}^{\perp}

(A.31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\beta)\left(\frac{j_{\perp}}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon_{1}\,\frac{\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon_{1})}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon_{1}-B)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\epsilon_{2}\,\frac{\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon_{2})}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon_{2}-B)}}\frac{e^{-\beta B}}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left[B\to(-B)\right]\bigg)\;.

We define

l(B,T)=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}\epsilon_{1}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon_{1}-B)}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}\epsilon_{2}}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon_{2}-B)}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}

(A.32)

for a constant density of states,
\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)=1

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\left(\frac{j_{\perp}}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2\cosh(\beta B)}

(A.33)

\displaystyle\times\left(e^{-\beta B}l(B,T)+e^{\beta B}l(-B,T)\right)\;.

The evaluation of

(A.34)

Finally, we arrive at

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\left(\frac{j_{\perp}}{2}\right)^{2}\biggl[-2\ln 2+\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{3}

(A.35)

\displaystyle+B^{2}\left[1-\frac{2}{T}\left(-1-\gamma_{\rm E}+\ln(2\pi T)\right)\right)\biggr]\;.

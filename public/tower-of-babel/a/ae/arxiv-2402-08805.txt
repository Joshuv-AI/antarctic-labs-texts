Deriving the Gibbons-Maldacena-Nunez no-go theorem from the Raychaudhuri equation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Mir Mehedi Faruk

Abstract
In this article, we point out that to solve the null Raychaudhuri equation for higher dimensional spacetime with accelerating FRW solution in external directions and static compact internal directions, it is necessary to violate the Strong Energy condition in higher dimensions. This constraint is well-known in obtaining accelerating cosmological solutions in string compactification, first described by Gibbons-Maldacena-Nunez. In deriving this constraint, we do not make any assumptions regarding the matter content.

R_{MN}t^{M}t^{N}\geq 0,\quad t^{2}<0.

(2)

Considering the FRW metric in the physical time co-ordinates,

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}.

(3)

As we know,

Accelerating solutions are identified with

\displaystyle\ddot{a}/a=\dot{H}+H^{2}>0.

(4)

\displaystyle\iff\gamma\geq 0,

II
The null Raychaudhuri equation

In order to establish a singularity theorem, it is necessary to have an effective means of anticipating the appearance of focal points along geodesics. Raychaudhuri’s equation offers such a methodology
3
3

3

Interested readers please look into

Raychaudhuri’s equation reveals that the occurrence of focal points is quite common, as gravity has a propensity to focus nearby geodesics.
The Raychaudhuri equation for null geodesic congruences is the following,

\displaystyle\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda}=-\frac{1}{D-2}\theta^{2}-\sigma^{2}-R_{MN}l^{M}l^{N}

(7)

Here,

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g_{D}}}\partial_{M}(\sqrt{-g_{D}}l^{M})

(8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{MN}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{M}l_{N}+\nabla_{N}l_{M})-\frac{1}{D-2}\hat{h}_{MN}\theta

(9)

And,
\hat{h}_{MN}
is the transverse metric, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{MN}l^{M}=0

(10)

These are of course transverse to null rays.

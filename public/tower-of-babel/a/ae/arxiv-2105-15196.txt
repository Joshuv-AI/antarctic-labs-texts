A novel second-order nonstandard finite difference method for solving one-dimensional autonomous dynamical systems Journal: Elsevier
Manh Tuan Hoang

Abstract
In this work, a novel second-order nonstandard finite difference (NSFD) method that preserves simultaneously the positivity and local asymptotic stability of one-dimensional autonomous dynamical systems is introduced and analyzed. This method is based on novel non-local approximations for right-hand side functions of differential equations in combination with nonstandard denominator functions. The obtained results not only resolve the contradiction between the dynamic consistency and high-order accuracy of NSFD methods but also improve and extend some well-known results that have been published recently in [Applied Mathematics Letters 112(2021) 106775], [AIP Conference Proceedings 2302(2020) 110003] and [Applied Mathematics Letters 50(2015) 78-82]. Furthermore, as a simple but important application, we apply the constructed NSFD method for solving the logistic, sine, cubic, and Monod equations; consequently, the NSFD schemes constructed in the earlier work [Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics 110(1999) 181-185] are improved significantly. Finally, we report some numerical experiments to support and illustrate the theoretical assertions as well as advantages of the constructed NSFD method.

Introduction

The concept of nonstandard finite difference (NSFD) schemes was first introduced by Mickens in 1980 to overcome drawbacks of standard finite difference ones
[
2
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
,

. Nowadays, NSFD schemes have been widely used as one of the powerful and efficient approaches for solving differential equation models arising in both theory and applications. The main advantage of NSFD schemes over standard ones is that they can preserve important qualitative properties of solutions of the corresponding differential equations regardless of chosen step sizes. However, most of NSFD schemes are only of first order accuracy. For this reason, there have been many attempts to formulate higher-order NSFD schemes for ordinary differential equations (ODEs)
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

In very recent works
[
6
,
11
]
, second-order NSFD methods preserving the local asymptotic stability (LAS) of one-dimensional autonomous ODEs have been introduced. These results partially resolved the contradiction between the dynamic consistency and high-order accuracy of NSFD schemes; however, the constructed methods did not preserve the positivity of solutions of differential equations.

Motivated and inspired by this, in this work we construct a novel NSFD method that not only preserves simultaneously the positivity and LAS of one-dimensional autonomous dynamical systems but also is accurate of order
2
. The method is based on novel non-local approximations for right-hand side functions of differential equations in combination with nonstandard denominator functions. The obtained results not only resolve the contradiction between the dynamic consistency and high-order accuracy of NSFD schemes but also improve and extend the well-known results constructed in
[
6
,
11
,
23
]
. Moreover, it should be emphasized that elementary stable NSFD schemes introduced in some previous works
[
3
,
8
,
9
]
are only of first order accuracy and not positive.

It is worthy noting that our constructed NSFD method is completely different from NSFD schemes formulated in
[
23
]
and
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
. More clearly, we use non-local approximations based on novel representations of right-hand side functions and especially, denominator functions depend not only on the step size but also on numerical approximations, meanwhile, the NSFD schemes introduced in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
and
[
23
]
used denominator functions only depend on the step size. Moreover, the NSFD scheme formulated in
[
23
]
used a non-local approximation based on the sign of the right-hand side functions at each iterative step; however, our method does not require the determination of the sign of the right-hand functions. In fact, the positive and elementary stable NSFD method in
[
23
]
can be considered as a particular case of our method, but it is only of first-order accuracy. This will be confirmed by a numerical example performed in Subsection
4.1
.

Importantly, the present idea used to construct the NSFD method can be developed for formulating dynamically consistent NSFD methods having high-order accuracy for general multi-dimensional autonomous and non-autonomous dynamical systems, which are governed by ODEs, fractional-order ODEs and partial differential equations. Furthermore, the constructed method is fully explicit; hence, it is easy to be implemented and can be combined with extrapolation techniques to improve the accuracy.

As a simple but important application, we apply the constructed NSFD method for solving the logistic, sine, cubic, and Monod equations, which were considered in

2
Definitions and preliminaries

Consider general autonomous ODEs of the form

\dfrac{dy}{dt}=f(y),\quad t\geq 0,\quad y(0)=y_{0},

(1)

where
y:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Besides, we consider a general finite difference method for Eq. (
1
)

D_{h}(y_{n})=F_{h}(f;y_{n}),

(2)

where
D_{h}(y_{n})\approx\dfrac{dy}{dt}|_{t=t_{n}}
,
F_{h}(f;y_{n})\approx f(y(t_{n}))
,
y_{n}\approx y(t_{n})
,
t_{n}=nh
and
h>0
is the step size.

Definition 1

(Positivity-preserving numerical schemes
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
23
]
)
.

The finite difference method (
2
) is called positive, if, for any value of the step size
h
, and

y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}|\,y\geq 0\}
its solution remains positive, i.e.,
y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 2

(Elementary stable numerical schemes
[
2
,
8
]
)
.

The finite difference method (
2
) is said to be elementary stable if, for any value of
the step-size
h
, its only fixed points
y^{*}
are the same as the equilibria of the ODE (
1
) and the local stability properties of each
y^{*}
are the same for both the differential equation and the difference method.

Definition 3

(Concept of NSFD schemes
[
2
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
)
.

The one-step finite difference scheme (
2
) for solving Eq. (
1
) is called a nonstandard
finite difference (NSFD) method if at least one of the following conditions is satisfied:

1.

D_{h}(y_{n})=\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\varphi(h)}
, where
\varphi(h)=h+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
is a non-negative function;

2.

F_{h}(f;y_{n})=g(y_{k},y_{k+1},h)
, where
g(y_{k},y_{k+1},h)
is a non-local approximation of the right-hand side of Eq. (
1
).

We recall that Eq. (
1
) is said to be
positive
if
y(t)\geq 0
holds for all
t\geq 0
whenever
y_{0}\geq 0
. Thanks to
[
10
, Lemma 1]
, we obtain the following result on the positivity of solutions of Eq. (
1
).

Lemma 1
.

The set
\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a positively invariant set of Eq. (
1
) (i.e,
y(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
whenever
y_{0}\geq 0
) if and only if
f(0)\geq 0
.

3
Construction of the nonstandard finite difference method

3.1
Auxiliary results

We first introduce some notations

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}^{+}=\big\{f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\big|\,f(y)\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad y\geq 0\big\},\\
&\mathcal{F}^{-}=\big\{f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\big|\,f(y)\leq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad y\geq 0\big\},\\
&\mathcal{B}^{L}=\big\{f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\big|\,f(y)\geq L\quad\text{for all}\quad y\geq 0\big\}\quad\text{for each}\quad M\geq 0.\end{split}

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{B}^{L}
is not empty for any
L\geq 0
, for example,
f(y)=L
,
f(y)=y^{2}+L
and
f(y)=Le^{y}
belong to
\mathcal{B}^{L}
. Moreover, the set
\mathcal{B}^{L}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
(
k\geq 1
) is also not empty.

Lemma 2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying:

(i)

f(y)\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
,
k\geq 1
.

(ii)

f
has a finite number of zeros, i.e, the equation
f(y)=0
has only a finite number of solutions.

Then
f
can be represented in the form

f(y)=f^{+}(y)+f^{-}(y),\quad f^{+},f^{-}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad f^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{+},\quad f^{-}\in\mathcal{F}^{-}.

Proof.

Assume that
0\leq y_{1}\leq y_{2}<\ldots\leq y_{m}
with
m<\infty
are zeros of
f
. We set

l=\min_{y\in[0,y_{m}]}f(y),\quad L=\max_{y\in[0,y_{m}]}f(y),\quad M=max\{|l|,\,\,|L|\}.

Since
f
has only a finite number of zeros,
f(y)
does not change sign when
y>y_{m}
. We consider two following cases for the sign of
f(y)
when
y>y_{m}
.

Case 1:
The case when
f(y)>0
for all
y>y_{m}
. In this case, we take

f^{+}(y)=f(y)+g(y),\qquad f^{-}(y)=-g(y),\quad\text{for some}\quad g\in\mathcal{B}^{M}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

Obviously,
f^{-}(y)\leq 0
for all
y\geq 0
. On the other hand, since
g\in B^{M}
,
g(y)\geq M
for all
y\geq 0
. Consequently, for
y\in[0,y_{m}]

f^{+}(y)=f(y)+g(y)\geq l+M\geq l+|l|\geq 0.

Hence,
f^{+}(y)\geq 0
for all
y\geq 0
.

Case 2:
The case when
f(y)<0
for all
y>y_{m}
. In this case, we choose

f^{-}(y)=f(y)-g(y),\qquad f^{+}(y)=g(y),\quad\text{for some}\quad g\in\mathcal{B}^{M}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

Obviously,
f^{+}(y)\geq 0
for all
y\geq 0
. Furthermore

f^{-}(y)=f(y)-f(y)\leq L-g(y)\leq L-M\leq L-|L|=0\quad\text{for all}\quad y\in[0,y_{m}].

Hence,
f^{-}(y)\geq 0
for all
y\geq 0
.

The proof is completed by combining Cases 1 and 2.
∎

Remark 1
.

Based on the proof of Lemma
2
, we can write a procedure to determine representations of the function
f
as follows:

Step 1. Determine all zeros the function
f
, i.e., solve the equation
f(y)=0
(using numerical methods such as Newton’s method if needed). Since
f
has only only a finite number of zeros, this step is always done. Suppose that
0\leq y_{1}\leq y_{2}\leq\ldots\leq y_{m}
with
m<\infty
are all the zeros of
f
.

Step 2. Compute (by using numerical methods if needed)

l=\min_{y\in[0,y_{m}]}f(y),\quad L=\max_{y\in[0,y_{m}]}f(y),\quad M=max\{|l|,\,\,|L|\}.

Step 3. Determine the sign (positive or negative) of the function
f
on
(y_{m},\infty)
. This step is always done because
f
has only only a finite number of zeros.

Step 4. If
f(y)>0
for all
y>y_{m}
, we take

f^{+}(y)=f(y)+g(y),\qquad f^{-}(y)=-g(y),\quad\text{for some}\quad g\in\mathcal{B}^{M}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

If
f(y)<0
for all
y>y_{m}
, we choose

f^{-}(y)=f(y)-g(y),\qquad f^{+}(y)=g(y),\quad\text{for some}\quad g\in\mathcal{B}^{M}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

Note that the set
\mathcal{B}^{M}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is always nonempty for each
M>0
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying:

(i)

f(y)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
.

(ii)

f
has a finite number of zeros.

(iii)

f(0)\geq 0
.

Then
f
can be represented in the form

f(y)=f^{+}(y)+yf^{-}(y),\quad f^{+},f^{-}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad f^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{+},\quad f^{-}\in\mathcal{F}^{-}.

(3)

Proof.

The proof includes two steps as follows.

Step 1.
Consider the case when
f(0)=0
. We define

g(y):=\begin{cases}&\dfrac{f(y)}{y},\qquad y>0,\\
&f^{\prime}(0),\qquad y=0.\end{cases}

It is clear that
f(y)=yg(y)
and
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
.
From Lemma
2
, the function
g
admits a representation

g(y)=g^{+}(y)+g^{-}(y),\quad g^{+},g^{-}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad g^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{+},\quad g^{-}\in\mathcal{F}^{-}.

Consequently,

f(y)=f^{+}(y)+yf^{-}(y),\quad f^{+}(y):=yg^{+}(y),\quad f^{-}(y)=g^{-}(y).

Step 2:
If
f(0)>0
, we set
g(y)=f(y)-f(0)
. Then,
g(0)=0
. From Step 1 we have that
g
has a representation

g(y)=g^{+}(y)+yg^{-}(y),\quad g^{+},g^{-}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad g^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{+},\quad g^{-}\in\mathcal{F}^{-}.

Hence,

f(y)=g(y)+f(0)=\big[f(0)+g^{+}(y)\big]+yg^{-}(y),

where
f(0)+g^{+}(y)\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
g^{-}(y)\in\mathcal{F}^{-}
. The proof is completed by combining Step 1 and Step 2.
∎

Remark 2
.

Similarly to Remark
1
, the proof of Theorem
1
also provides a way to determine representations of the function
f
. In many cases, it is easy to find representations in the form (
3
) for a given function
f
, for instance

\begin{split}&y-y^{2}=y+y(-y)=(y+y^{2})+2y(-y)=(y+ye^{y})+y(-y-e^{y}),\\
&e^{y}-1=(e^{y}+y-1)+y(-1)=(e^{y}+y^{2}-1)+y(-y),\\
&\sin(y)=\big[\sin(y)+y\big]+y(-1)=\big[\sin(y)+ye^{y}\big]+y(-e^{y}).\end{split}

Hence, the representations in the form (
3
) of
f
may not be unique. This leads to an optimal problem for the representation of the function
f
.

Remark 3
.

In
[
5
]
, a representation theorem for real-valued functions was constructed (see
[
5
, Theorem 10]
); however, this theorem did not affirm the differentiability of negative and positive parts
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
. It only proved that
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
are continuous. Actually, as will be seen in Lemma
4
, the differentiability of
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
is very essential in the construction of the elementary stable NSFD method.

3.2
Main results

Throughout this paper, we always assume that:

(A1)

The right-hand side function
f
of Eq. (
1
) satisfies
f(0)\geq 0
. This condition implies that
y(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
whenever
y_{0}\geq 0
.

(A2)

The equation (
1
) has a finite number of equilibria and each of which is hyperbolic, i.e.,
f^{\prime}(y_{*})\neq 0
for any equilibrium point
y_{*}
of (
1
). This condition means that the LAS of equilibria of (
1
) can be investigated by the Lyapunov indirect method (see
[
1
,
22
]
).

Our main objective is to construction an NSFD method preserving simultaneously the positivity and LAS of Eq. (
1
). First, we utilize Theorem
1
to transform the right-hand side function
f
of Eq. (
1
) to the form:

f(y)=f^{+}(y)+yf^{-}(y),\quad f^{+},f^{-}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad f^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{+},\quad f^{-}\in\mathcal{F}^{-}.

(4)

Then we propose the following NSFD scheme

\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\varphi(h,y_{n})}=f^{+}(y_{n})+\alpha y_{n}f^{-}(y_{n})+\beta y_{n+1}f^{-}(y_{n}),

(5)

where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies

\ \varphi(h,y)=h+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\quad\mbox{as}\quad h\to 0,

(6)

and
\alpha,\beta
are real numbers satisfying

\alpha+\beta=1,\quad\alpha\leq 0,\quad\beta\geq 0.

(7)

For convenience, the variables in the function
\varphi(h,y)
will be omitted in some places; Furthermore, throughout the rest of this section, we always assume that the conditions (
6
) and (
7
) are satisfied.

Remark 4
.

(i)

The denominator function
\varphi
of the NSFD method (
5
) depends not only on
h
but also on
y_{n}
to guarantee that it is accurate of order
2
(see Lemma
5
). Furthermore, it is determined by the correct asymptotic behaviour modelling.

(ii)

Differently from most of NSFD schemes introduced in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
, the NSFD scheme (
5
) uses a non-local approximation with the appearance of the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
as weights. These parameters not only ensure the positivity (see Lemma
3
) but also make the method more flexible. As will be seen in Subsection
4.1
, the NSFD method (
5
) can become an exact scheme with suitable parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Lemma 3

(Positivity of the NSFD method)
.

Let
y_{0}\geq 0
be any initial data for Eq. (
1
) and
\{y_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be approximations generated by the NSFD method (
5
). Then we have
y_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
. In other words, the NSFD method (
5
) preserves the positivity of Eq. (
1
) for all finite step sizes
h>0
.

Proof.

The theorem is proved by mathematical induction. We first transform the scheme (
5
) to the explicit form

y_{n+1}=\dfrac{y_{n}+\varphi f^{+}(y_{n})+\varphi\alpha y_{n}f^{-}(y_{n})}{1-\varphi\beta f^{-}(y_{n})}

(8)

Assume that
y_{n}\geq 0
. From

f^{+}(y_{n})\geq 0,\quad f^{-}(y_{n})\leq 0,\quad\alpha\leq 0,\quad\beta\geq 0,\quad\varphi\geq 0,

we deduce that
y_{n+1}\geq 0
. The proof is complete.
∎

To determine the Jacobian of the scheme (
5
) at its equilibria conveniently, we rewrite the scheme (
8
) in the form

y_{n+1}=y_{n}+\dfrac{\varphi f(y_{n})}{1-\varphi\beta f^{-}(y_{n})}.

(9)

Assume that the set of equilibria of Eq. (
1
) is
\Omega=\{y_{1}^{*},y_{2}^{*},\ldots,y_{m}^{*}\}
. We denote by
\Omega^{s}
is the set of locally asymptotically stable equilibria of Eq. (
1
), that is

\Omega^{s}=\{y^{*}\in\Omega|f^{\prime}(y^{*})<0\}.

Lemma 4

(Stability of the NSFD method)
.

Assume that the function
\varphi(h,y)
satisfies

\varphi(h,y^{*})<\dfrac{2}{2\beta f^{-}(y^{*})-f^{\prime}(y^{*})},\qquad\forall y^{*}\in\Omega^{s}.

(10)

Then the NSFD method (
5
) is elementary stable.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that equilibria of (
1
) and (
9
) are identical. Let
y^{*}
be any equilibrium point of (
1
). The Jacobian of (
9
) at
y^{*}
is given by

J(y^{*})=1+\dfrac{\varphi(h,y^{*})f^{\prime}(y^{*})}{1-\varphi(h,y^{*})\beta f^{-}(y^{*})}

We consider two following cases:

(i) If
f^{\prime}(y^{*})>0
, then
y^{*}
is an unstable equilibrium point of (
1
). We need to prove that
y^{*}
is also an unstable equilibrium point of (
9
). Indeed, since

J(y^{*})=1+\dfrac{\varphi(h,y^{*})f^{\prime}(y^{*})}{1-\varphi(h,y^{*})\beta f^{-}(y^{*})}>1,

y^{*}
is an unstable equilibrium point of (
9
).

(ii) If
f^{\prime}(y^{*})<0
, then
y^{*}
is a locally asymptotically stable equilirium point of (
1
). We will prove that
y^{*}
is a locally asymptotically stable equilirium point of (
9
). Indeed, we have

J(y^{*})=1+\dfrac{\varphi(h,y^{*})f^{\prime}(y^{*})}{1-\varphi(h,y^{*})\beta f^{-}(y^{*})}<1.

On the other hand,
J(y^{*})>-1
if and only if

2>\varphi(h,y^{*})\big[2\beta f^{-}(y^{*})-f^{\prime}(y^{*})\big].

This inequality is satisfied if (
10
) holds. Hence, if (
10
) then
|J(y^{*})|<1
, or equivalently,
y^{*}
is a stable equilibrium point of (
9
).

The proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 5

(The second-order NSFD method)
.

Let
\varphi(h,y)
be a function satisfying

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y)=f^{\prime}(y)-2\beta f^{-}(y),\quad\forall y\geq 0.

(11)

Then the NSFD method (
5
) is accurate of order
2
.

Proof.

Using the Taylor expansion for
y(t)
we obtain

y(t_{n+1})=y(t_{n}+h)=y(t_{n})+hy^{\prime}(t_{n})+\dfrac{h^{2}}{2}y^{\prime\prime}(t_{n})+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}).

(12)

Let us denote

G(h,y)=y+\dfrac{\varphi(h,y)f(y)}{1-\varphi(h,y)\beta f^{-}(y)}.

It is easy to verify that

G(0,y)=y,\quad\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial h}(0,y)=f(y),\quad\dfrac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y)+2\beta f^{-}(y).

Consequently,

\begin{split}y(t_{n})+\dfrac{\varphi f(y(t_{n}))}{1-\varphi\beta f^{-}(y(t_{n}))}&=G(0,y(t_{n}))+h\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial h}(0,y(t_{n}))+\dfrac{h^{2}}{2}\dfrac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y(t_{n}))+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\\
&=y(t_{n})+hf(y(t_{n}))+\dfrac{h^{2}}{2}\bigg[\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y(t_{n}))+2\beta f^{-}(y(t_{n}))\bigg]+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}).\end{split}

(13)

From (
12
) and (
13
) we deduce that if (
11
) holds then

y(t_{n+1})-\bigg[y(t_{n})+\dfrac{\varphi f(y(t_{n}))}{1-\varphi\beta f^{-}(y(t_{n}))}\bigg]=\mathcal{O}(h^{3}),

which means that the NSFD scheme is accurate of order 2.
∎

From Lemmas
3
,
4
and
5
, we obtain

Theorem 2
.

The NSFD method (
5
) is positive, elementary stable and accurate of order
2
if

\begin{split}(H1)\qquad&\varphi(h,y)=h+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\quad\mbox{as}\quad h\to 0,\quad\varphi(h,y)\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad(h,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
(H2)\qquad&\varphi(h,y^{*})<\dfrac{2}{2\beta f^{-}(y^{*})-f^{\prime}(y^{*})},\qquad\forall y^{*}\in\Omega^{s},\\
(H3)\qquad&\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y)=f^{\prime}(y)-2\beta f^{-}(y),\quad\forall y\geq 0.\\
(H4)\qquad&\alpha+\beta=1,\quad\alpha\leq 0,\quad\beta\geq 0.\end{split}

(14)

Next, we introduce a function
\varphi(h)
satisfying the conditions
(H1)-(H3)
. Let us denote

\lambda(y):=-f^{\prime}(y)+2\beta f^{-}(y),

\varphi(h,y)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1-e^{-h\lambda(y)}}{\lambda(y)},\quad&\lambda(y)\neq 0,\\
h,\qquad&\lambda(y)=0.\end{cases}

(15)

Proposition 1
.

The function
\varphi(h,y)
given by (
15
) satisfies the conditions
(H1)-(H3)
in Theorem
2
.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that
\varphi(h,y)
satisfies
(H1)
and
(H3)
. Since
\lambda(y^{*})>0
for all
y^{*}\in\Omega^{s}
, we have

\varphi(h,y^{*})<\dfrac{1}{\lambda(y^{*})}=\dfrac{1}{-f^{\prime}(y^{*})+2\beta f^{-}(y^{*})}<\dfrac{2}{-f^{\prime}(y^{*})+2\beta f^{-}(y^{*})},

which implies that
(H2)
is also satisfied. The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 5
.

(i)

Lemma 2 in
[
6
]
provides an important result for selecting the denominator functions satisfying the conditions

4.2
The cubic differential equation

Consider the cubic differential equation

\dfrac{dy}{dt}=y(1-y^{2}),\quad y(0)\geq 0.

(18)

In
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the cubic equation (
18
) has two equilibrium point
y^{*}=0
(unstable) and
y^{*}=1
(stable).

Now, Theorem
2
will be applied to obtain a positive and elementary stable NSFD scheme of second order for Eq. (
18
). For this purpose, we choose

f^{+}(y)=y,\quad f^{-}(y)=y^{2},\quad\beta=\dfrac{3}{2},\quad\alpha=-\dfrac{1}{2}.

Then we obtain

\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\varphi}=y_{n}+\dfrac{1}{2}(y_{n})^{3}-\dfrac{3}{2}(y_{n})^{2}y_{n+1},

or equivalently,

y_{n+1}=\dfrac{(2+\varphi)y_{n}+\varphi(y_{n})^{3}}{2+3\varphi(y_{n})^{2}}.

(19)

By Theorem
2
, the scheme (
19
) is positive, elementary stable and accurate of order
2
if

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y)=f^{\prime}(y)-2\beta f^{-}(y)=1.

For this condition, the function
1-e^{-h}
is most suitable; consequently, we obtain

y_{n+1}=\dfrac{(2+\varphi)y_{n}+\varphi(y_{n})^{3}}{2+3\varphi(y_{n})^{2}},\quad\varphi(h)=1-e^{-h}.

(20)

In
[
19
]
, Mickens proposed the following NSFD scheme that incorporates the maximum symmetry in modeling the nonlinear term:

y_{n+1}=\Bigg[\dfrac{(2+\phi)+\phi(y_{n})^{2}}{(2-\phi)+3\phi(y_{n})^{2}}\Bigg]y_{n},\quad\phi=\dfrac{1-e^{-2h}}{2}.

(21)

Clearly, the scheme (
20
) is different from (
21
). However, it is easy to show that the NSFD scheme (
21
) is a particular case of the NSFD method (
5
). Indeed, for the NSFD scheme (
5
) we choose

f^{+}(y)=\dfrac{1}{2}y+(y)^{3},\quad f^{-}(y)=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{3}{2}y^{2},\quad\alpha=0,\quad\beta=1.

Note that in this form
f^{-}\notin\mathcal{F}^{-}
. Then the NSFD method (
5
) for Eq. (
18
) becomes

\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\varphi(h)}=\dfrac{1}{2}y_{n}+\dfrac{1}{2}(y_{n})^{3}+\dfrac{1}{2}y_{n+1}-\dfrac{3}{2}(y_{n})^{2}y_{n+1},

or equivalently,

y_{n+1}=\Bigg[\dfrac{(2+\varphi)+\varphi(y_{n})^{2}}{(2-\varphi)+3\varphi(y_{n})^{2}}\Bigg]y_{n}.

(22)

It is clear that the scheme (
22
) will coincident with (
21
) if
\varphi=({1-e^{-2h})}/{2}
. Since
f^{-}(y)=(1/2)-(3/2)y^{2}\notin\mathcal{F}^{-}
, the scheme (
22
) is not positive for any function
\varphi(h)>0
. A simple condition for the scheme to be positive is
\varphi(h)<1/2
for all
h>0
. Fortunately, the function
\varphi=({1-e^{-2h})}/{2}
satisfies this condition; therefore, the scheme (
21
) is positive. Importantly, the function
\varphi=({1-e^{-2h})}/{2}
satisfies Theorem
2
; consequently, the scheme (
21
) is also of second order accuracy.

can be generalized as follows.

\dfrac{dy}{dt}=ay-by^{m},\quad a,b>0,\quad m\geq 2,\quad y(0)\geq 0

\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\varphi(h)}=ay_{n}-b\bigg(1-\dfrac{m}{2}\bigg)(y_{n})^{m}-b\dfrac{m}{2}{(y_{n})}^{m-1}y_{n+1},\quad\varphi(h)=\dfrac{1-e^{-ah}}{a}.

4.3
The modified Monod equation

In
[
19
]
, Mickens considered the modified Monod equation

\dfrac{dy}{dt}=\dfrac{(\lambda-1)y-(\lambda+1)y^{2}}{1+y},\quad\lambda>1,

(25)

and proposed the following NSFD scheme

\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\phi}=\Bigg[\dfrac{(\lambda-1)-(1+\lambda)y_{n+1}}{1+y_{n}}\Bigg]y_{n},\quad\phi=\dfrac{1-e^{-Rh}}{R},\quad R=\lambda-1.

(26)

Clearly, the scheme (
26
) is a particular case of the NSFD method (
6
) with

f^{+}(y)=\dfrac{(\lambda-1)y}{1+y},\,\,f^{-}(y)=\dfrac{(\lambda+1)y}{1+y},\,\,\beta=1,\,\,\varphi(h,y)=\dfrac{1-e^{-Rh}}{R},\,\,R=\lambda-1.

Although the scheme (
26
) is positive and elementary stable, it is only of first order accuracy. However, thanks to Theorem
2
it is easy to determine conditions for this scheme to be accurate of order
2
. More clearly, the scheme (
26
) is of second-order accuracy if

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y)=-\dfrac{(\lambda+3)+4(\lambda+1)y+3(\lambda+1)y^{2}}{(1+y)^{2}}.

A denominator function satisfying this above condition is

\varphi(h,y)=\dfrac{1-e^{-R(y)h}}{R(y)},\quad R(y)=\dfrac{(\lambda+1)+4(\lambda+1)y+3(\lambda+1)y^{2}}{(1+y)^{2}}.

This function ensures that the scheme (
26
) is of second order accuracy.

4.4
The sine equation

In
[
19
]
, Mickens considered the since equation

\dfrac{dy}{dt}=\sin(\pi y)

(27)

and formulated the following NSFD scheme

\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\phi}=sin(\pi y_{n}),\quad\phi=\dfrac{1-e^{-\pi h}}{\pi}.

(28)

It is easy to verify that the scheme (
28
) is of first order accuracy. We now use Theorem
2
to obtain a second-order NSFD scheme for the sine equation. For this purpose, we rewrite Eq. (
27
) in the form

\dfrac{dy}{dt}=\sin(\pi y)=f^{+}(y)+yf^{-}(y)=\big[sin(\pi y)+\pi y\big]+y(-\pi).

Note that
y+sin(y)\geq 0
for all
y\geq 0
. Applying the NSFD method (
5
) with
\beta=1
we obtain

y_{n+1}=\dfrac{y_{n}+\varphi\big[sin(\pi y_{n})+\pi y_{n}\big]}{1+\varphi\pi}.

(29)

The scheme is elementary stable and positive, and it is accurate of order
2
if

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial h^{2}}(0,y)=\pi\cos(\pi y)-2\pi.

Hence, the function

\varphi(h,y)=\dfrac{1-e^{-\lambda(y)h}}{\lambda(y)},\quad\lambda(y)=\pi\cos(\pi y)-2\pi

guarantees that the NSFD scheme (
29
) is positive, elementary stable and accurate of order
2
.

4.5
An extension of the NSFD method for systems of ODEs

In this subsection, we generalize the NSFD method (
5
) for systems of ODEs. For this purpose, we first consider the following general class of two-dimensional differential equations including several models in population dynamics

\begin{split}&\dfrac{dx}{dt}=x\big(f_{+}(x,y)-f_{-}(x,y)\big):=f(x,y),\quad x(0)=x_{0}\geq 0,\\
&\dfrac{dy}{dt}=y\big(g_{+}(x,y)-g_{-}(x,y)\big):=g(x,y),\quad y(0)=y_{0}\geq 0,\end{split}

(30)

where
f_{+},f_{-}
and
g_{+},g_{-}
are positive for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
. In
[
5
]
, Cresson and Pierret constructed a nonstandard finite difference method preserving dynamical properties including positivity and stability of the model (
30
). It was proved that the constructed NSFD method is convergent and of order one (see
[
5
, Theorem 5.2]
).

We now utilize the approach is this work to construct a second-order NSFD method for the model (
30
). For this reason, we propose the following NSFD method

\begin{split}&\dfrac{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}{\varphi_{1}(x_{n},y_{n},h)}=x_{n}f_{+}(x_{n},y_{n})-\alpha_{1}x_{n}f_{-}(x_{n},y_{n})-\beta_{1}x_{n+1}f_{-}(x_{n},y_{n}),\\
&\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{\varphi_{2}(x_{n},y_{n},h)}=y_{n}g_{+}(x_{n},y_{n})-\alpha_{2}y_{n}g_{-}(x_{n},y_{n})-\beta_{2}y_{n+1}g_{-}(x_{n},y_{n}),\end{split}

(31)

where
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are denominator functions determined by the correct asymptotic behaviour modelling and

\begin{split}&\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}=1,\quad\alpha_{1}\leq 0,\quad\beta_{1}\geq 0,\\
&\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}=1,\quad\alpha_{2}\leq 0,\quad\beta_{2}\geq 0.\end{split}

(32)

Note that when
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
do not depend on
x_{n}
and
y_{n}
, we will obtain the NSFD method constructed in
[
5
]
.

The method (
31
) can be rewritten in the explicit form

\begin{split}&x_{n+1}=\dfrac{x_{n}+\varphi_{1}x_{n}f_{+}(x_{n},y_{n})-\varphi_{1}\alpha_{1}x_{n}f_{-}(x_{n},y_{n})}{1+\varphi_{1}\beta_{1}f_{-}(x_{n},y_{n})},\\
&y_{n+1}=\dfrac{y_{n}+\varphi_{2}y_{n}g_{+}(x_{n},y_{n})-\varphi_{2}\alpha_{2}y_{n}g_{-}(x_{n},y_{n})}{1+\varphi_{2}\beta_{2}g_{-}(x_{n},y_{n})}.\end{split}

(33)

Therefore, the NSFD method (
33
) is positive for all
h>0
if the condition (
32
) holds. Assume that (
32
) is satisfied. Similarly to Theorem
5
, we obtain:

Theorem 3
.

The NSFD method (
33
) is positive and accurate of order
2
if

\begin{split}&\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{1}}{\partial h^{2}}(x,y,0)=\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,y)f(x,y)+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y)g(x,y)-2\beta_{1}f_{-}(x,y),\\
&\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{2}}{\partial h^{2}}(x,y,0)=\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial x}(x,y)f(x,y)+\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial y}(x,y)g(x,y)-2\beta_{2}g_{-}(x,y).\end{split}

(34)

Using the approach in
[
5
]
, thanks to the Lyapunov indirect method
[
1
,
22
]
, we can determine a stability thresholds
h^{*}=(h_{1}^{*},h_{2}^{*})
for the NSFD method (
33
), i.e., the method (
33
) preserves the local asymptotic stability of the model (
30
) if

0<\varphi_{1}<h_{1}^{*},\quad 0<\varphi_{2}<h_{2}^{*}.

(35)

Hence, we only need to choose denominator functions satisfying the conditions (
34
) and (
35
). Such denominator functions can be determined based on Proposition
1
.

Generally, we now consider the following general class of dynamical systems

\dfrac{dy_{i}}{dt}=f_{i}(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}),\quad y_{i}(0)=y_{i}^{0}\geq 0,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n,

(36)

where the right-hand function
f
satisfies appropriate conditions, which guarantee that solutions of (
36
) exist, are unique and positive for
t>0
.

Suppose that the functions
f_{i}
(
i=1,2,\ldots,n
) admit a representation of the form

f_{i}(y)=f_{i}^{+}(y)+y_{i}f_{i}^{-}(y),

(37)

where
f^{+},f^{-}
are of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
class,
f^{+}
is positive and
f^{-}
is negative for all
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Then, we propose the following NSFD method

\dfrac{y_{i}^{n+1}-y_{i}^{n}}{\varphi_{i}(y^{n},h)}=f_{i}^{+}(y^{n})+\alpha_{i}y^{n}f_{i}^{-}(y^{n})+\beta_{i}y_{i}^{n+1}f^{-}(y^{n}),

(38)

with

\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}=1,\quad\alpha_{i}\leq 0,\quad\beta_{i}\geq 0.

Using the approach used in Section
3
, we can determine conditions for the function
\varphi_{i}
such that the NSFD scheme (
38
) is positive, elementary stable and of second-order accuracy.

In general, it is not an easy task to verify whether the function
f
admits a representation of the form (
37
). However, in some cases, it is easy to find a such representation of the function
f
, for example, for the classical Lotka-Volterra system
[
1
]
, we can write

\begin{split}&\dfrac{dx}{dt}=ax-bxy=ax+bx(-y),\quad a,b>0,\\
&\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-cy+exy=exy+y(-c),\quad c,e>0\end{split}

(39)

or for the classical SIRS epidemic model
[
1

, we have

\begin{split}&\dfrac{dS}{dt}=-\dfrac{\beta}{N}SI+\mu R=\mu R+S\bigg(-\dfrac{\beta}{N}I\bigg),\quad\beta,\mu>0,\\
&\dfrac{dI}{dt}=\dfrac{\beta}{N}SI-\gamma I=\dfrac{\beta}{N}SI+I(-\gamma),\quad\gamma>0,\\
&\dfrac{dR}{dt}=\gamma I-\mu R=\gamma I+R(-\mu).\end{split}

(40)

5
Conclusions and remarks

In this work, we have constructed the NSFD method that is not only positive and elementary stable but also accurate of order
2
. The theoretical assertions are supported by a set of numerical simulations.
The obtained results not only resolve the contradiction between the dynamic consistency and high-order accuracy of NSFD schemes but also improve the well-known results constructed in
[
6
,
11
,
19
,
23
]
. Furthermore, the constructed method is easy to be implemented and can be used to solve a larger class of differential equations arising in both theory and applications.

Our future research directions include the construction of high-order NSFD methods that are not only elementary stable and positive but also preserve other important qualitative features of solutions of differential equations, such as global asymptotic stability and periodicity. Also, the construction of dynamically consistent NSFD methods having high-order accuracy for general multi-dimensional autonomous and non-autonomous dynamical systems governed by ODEs and fractional-order ODEs will be studied.

Modified celestial amplitude in Einstein gravity
Shamik Banerjee, Sudip Ghosh, Pranjal Pandey, Arnab Priya Saha

Abstract
In this paper we evaluate the modified celestial amplitude for gravitons and gluons, as defined in arXiv:1801.10171[hep-th]. We find that the modified (tree) amplitude is finite for gravitons in Einstein gravity. The modified amplitude behaves like correlation function of operators inserted at various points of null-infinity in the Minkowski space-time. Therefore, unlike the standard celestial amplitudes, these are three dimensional objects. We also show that this amplitude admits conformal soft factorization recently studied in the literature.

Introduction

In flat space holography the natural observables are the
S
-matrix elements. They are the analogues of the correlation functions of the boundary CFT in the AdS/CFT correspondence. In a holographic description it is expected that the global symmetries of the dual theory should match the asymptotic symmetries of the bulk theory of quantum gravity. When the bulk is four dimensional asymptotically flat space-time, the asymptotic symmetry group is the extended BMS group
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962zza
;
Strominger:2013jfa
;
Barnich:2009se
;
Kapec:2014opa
;
Campiglia:2015kxa

SL(2,\mathbb{C})
which acts on the celestial sphere as the group of
global
conformal transformations. Therefore, from a holographic perspective, it is desirable to have a (complete) set of observables which transform naturally under the (Lorentz) conformal group. In order to achieve this
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz

;
Pasterski:2017kqt

(u,z,\bar{z})\xrightarrow{L.T}\bigg(\frac{u}{|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

Now,
the modified Mellin amplitude
\mathcal{A}\big(\{u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}\}\big)
is essentially the
S

(u,z,\bar{z})

\ket{u,z,\bar z,\lambda,\sigma, in/out}=N\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\omega^{i\lambda}e^{i\omega u}\ket{p(\omega,z,\bar z),\sigma,in/out}

(19)

The main difference between the Mellin transforms
\tilde{S}
and
\mathcal{A}

\tilde{S}

\mathcal{A}

\tilde{S}
and
\mathcal{A}

Now, as we have already discussed, the Mellin amplitude
\tilde{S}
describes the scattering process when the asymptotic particles are described by the conformal primary wave functions
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
,

\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(x^{\mu}|z,\bar{z})=\frac{(\mp i)^{\Delta}\Gamma(\Delta)}{(-q(z,\bar{z})\cdot x\mp i\delta)^{\Delta}}\ ,\quad\Delta=1+i\lambda,\quad\delta\rightarrow 0+

(20)

where
q^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})=(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z})
is a unit null-vector. The superscript
\pm
refers to the Mellin transformation of positive and negative energy plane waves, respectively. Now, the wave function (
20
) is distinguished by the fact that it is singular along the null-hyperplane,
x\cdot q(z,\bar{z})=0
, which passes through the origin of the Minkowski space. In other words, one can say that the particle described by (
20
) is localised on the null-hyperplane,
x\cdot q(z,\bar{z})=0
. Now, since the Klein-Gordon equation is Poincare invariant, it is natural to consider other solutions in which the particle is localised on different null-hyperplanes in Minkowski space. The complete set of such solutions can be parametrized as
Banerjee:2018gce
,

\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(x^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})=\frac{(\mp i)^{\Delta}\Gamma(\Delta)}{(-q(z,\bar{z})\cdot x+u\mp i\delta)^{\Delta}},\quad-\infty<u<\infty,\quad\delta\rightarrow 0+

(21)

The particle described by the wave function (
21
) is now localized along the null-hyperplane,
x\cdot q(z,\bar{z})-u=0
and by varying
(u,z,\bar{z})
we can generate all the null-hyperplanes in the Minkowski space. From the Poincare transformation property of the wave function (
21
) one can conclude that the three parameters
(u,z,\bar{z})
have the interpretation of Bondi coordinates at null-infinity
Banerjee:2018gce
. The modified Mellin amplitude
\mathcal{A}
describes the scattering process when the asymptotic particles are described by wave functions (
21
). To be more precise, for the
\tilde{S}
amplitude the wave functions are
\big\{\Phi_{\Delta_{i}}^{\pm}(x^{\mu}|u,z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\big\}
. Since the retarded time
u
is the
same for all the particles
, we can set
u=0
by time translation invariance. On the other hand, the
\mathcal{A}
amplitude is computed with the external particle wave functions
\big\{\Phi_{\Delta_{i}}^{\pm}(x^{\mu}|u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\big\}
and so
different particles are now localized at different retarded times
. The
separation in retarded time is the main difference between the
\mathcal{A}
and
\tilde{S}
amplitudes
.

2
Regularization

The modified Mellin amplitude
\mathcal{A}
has the form,

\mathcal{A}\big(\{u_{p},z_{p},\bar{z}_{p},\lambda_{p},\sigma_{p}\}\big)=\prod_{p=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{p}\ \omega_{p}^{i\lambda_{p}}e^{-i\varepsilon_{p}\omega_{p}u_{p}}S\big(\{\omega_{p},z_{p},\bar{z}_{p},\sigma_{p}\}\big)

(22)

For large values of energy the exponential factors
e^{\pm i\omega u}
oscillate rapidly. These can be regulated by adding a small imaginary part to
u
. To be more precise, we make the following shift,

u_{p}\rightarrow u_{p}-i\varepsilon_{p}\delta,\qquad\delta\rightarrow 0+

(23)

The origin of the convergence factor can also be traced back to the conformal primary wave-functions (
21
),

\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(x^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})=\frac{(\mp i)^{\Delta}\Gamma(\Delta)}{(-q(z,\bar{z})\cdot x+u\mp i\delta)^{\Delta}}

(24)

We can see that effectively
u
has a small imaginary part
\propto\delta
, whose sign depends on the sign of the energy.

Therefore the amplitude
\mathcal{A}
should be understood as,

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{A}\big(\{u_{p},z_{p},\bar{z}_{p},\lambda_{p},\sigma_{p}\}\big)=\prod_{p=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{p}\ \omega_{p}^{i\lambda_{p}}e^{-i\varepsilon_{p}\omega_{p}(u_{p}-i\varepsilon_{p}\delta)}S\big(\{\omega_{p},z_{p},\bar{z}_{p},\sigma_{p}\}\big)\\
=\prod_{p=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{p}\ \omega_{p}^{i\lambda_{p}}e^{-i\varepsilon_{p}\omega_{p}u_{p}}e^{-\delta s}S\big(\{\omega_{p},z_{p},\bar{z}_{p},\sigma_{p}\}\big),\quad\delta\rightarrow 0+\end{gathered}

(25)

where

s=\sum_{p}\omega_{p}>0

(26)

This provides the necessary damping for the integrals to be well defined and plays a crucial role in the rest of the paper. In most of the formulas below, we will not explicitly write down the convergence factor but it should always be kept in mind.

3.1
Four particle graviton tree amplitude in Einstein gravity

It is known that the tree-level Mellin amplitude
\tilde{S}
for gravitons is divergent in Einstein gravity
Stieberger:2018edy
. An interesting resolution suggested by
Stieberger:2018edy
is that instead of Einstein gravity one should consider graviton scattering in string theory and this indeed makes the amplitude
\tilde{S}
finite. However, it seems that there is no limit in which one can recover the classical Einstein gravity from the
\tilde{S}
amplitude computed in string theory. In this paper we show that the modified Mellin amplitude
\mathcal{A}
is in fact finite for tree level graviton scattering amplitude in Einstein gravity. It turns out that separating the asymptotic particles in retarded time naturally regularizes the
\mathcal{A}
amplitudes in Einstein gravity. Let us now describe the results.

With gravitons
1,2
of negative helicities and
3,4

where

We define the kinematic variables

Using the definition of spinor helicity variables

\textbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\sigma_{i}^{\ast})=\begin{cases}1,&\sigma_{i}^{\ast}~\epsilon~[0,1]\\

Now using

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{A}(1^{--}2^{--}3^{++}4^{++})(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})\\

\delta(|z-\bar{z}|)
constraint.

4.1

-point graviton amplitudes

Consider a
n
-point graviton scattering amplitude
S_{n}\left(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\sigma_{i}\right)
. Now if we take the limit where the momentum of the
n
-th particle goes to zero, then according to Weinberg’s soft graviton theorem

the

-point amplitude factorizes into a

point amplitude as follows

\begin{split}S_{n}\left(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\sigma_{i}\right)=\mathcal{F}^{(0)}S_{n-1}\left(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\sigma_{i}\right)+\ldots\end{split}

(44)

where
\mathcal{F}^{(0)}
is the universal leading Weinberg soft factor. The ellipses in equation
44
denote subleading contributions in the soft momentum. However, in this section we will only concern ourselves with the leading soft factor.

Now if the soft graviton has positive helicity, the soft factor can be expressed in terms of spinor helicity variables as
Cachazo:2014fwa

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{(0)}_{+}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{[ni]}{\langle ni\rangle}\frac{\langle xi\rangle\langle yi\rangle}{\langle xn\rangle\langle yn\rangle}\end{split}

(45)

Here we have introduced a subscript
(+)
in the soft factor to explicitly denote the case that the soft graviton here has positive helicity. Now using the parametrisation of null momenta in equation
2
this becomes

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{(0)}_{+}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{i}\omega_{i}}{\varepsilon_{n}\omega_{n}}\frac{\bar{z}_{ni}z_{xi}z_{yi}}{z_{ni}z_{xn}z_{yn}}\end{split}

(46)

Similarly in the case where the soft graviton has negative helicity, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{(0)}_{-}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\langle ni\rangle}{[ni]}\frac{[xi][yi]}{[xn][yn]}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{i}\omega_{i}}{\varepsilon_{n}\omega_{n}}\frac{z_{ni}}{\bar{z}_{ni}}\frac{\bar{z}_{xi}\bar{z}_{yi}}{\bar{z}_{xn}\bar{z}_{yn}}\end{split}

(47)

Let us now consider the conformal soft limit of the modified Mellin transform of the
n
-point graviton amplitude. Following
Fan:2019emx
;
Nandan:2019jas
;
Pate:2019mfs
, we define this as,

\begin{split}&\lim_{\lambda_{n}\to 0}i\lambda_{n}\hskip 1.13791pt\mathcal{A}_{n}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i})\end{split}

(48)

Recently in
Puhm:2019zbl
the conformal soft limit of graviton amplitudes has been studied. It is important to note that the main difference between our analysis and the analysis in
Puhm:2019zbl
is that we are using the prescription (
6
) of
Banerjee:2018gce
;
Banerjee:2018fgd
for defining the Mellin transform of scattering amplitudes which is a modification of the definition proposed in
Pasterski:2017ylz
and subsequently used in

. Now, using our definition for the Mellin transform in equation (
6
) we get

\begin{split}&\lim_{\lambda_{n}\to 0}i\lambda_{n}\hskip 1.13791pt\mathcal{A}_{n}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i})\\
&=\int\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}d\omega_{k}\hskip 1.42271pt\omega_{k}^{i\lambda_{k}}\hskip 2.84544pte^{-i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{k}u_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{n}\left(\lim_{\lambda_{n}\to 0}i\lambda_{n}\hskip 1.13791pt\omega_{n}^{i\lambda_{n}-1}\right)\hskip 1.13791pt\omega_{n}\hskip 1.13791pte^{-i\varepsilon_{n}\omega_{n}u_{n}}S_{n}\left(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\sigma_{i}\right)\\

(49)

where in the last line above we have used the identity

\begin{split}\delta(\sigma_{1}^{*})=\delta\left(-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{4}}{D}\frac{\bar{z}_{34}z_{24}}{\bar{z}_{13}z_{12}}\right)=\left|\frac{z_{12}\bar{z}_{13}}{\bar{z}_{34}}D\right|\delta(z_{24})\end{split}

(72)

Further on the support of
\delta\left(z_{24}\right)
, we have

\begin{split}f(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})&\rightarrow\frac{z_{12}^{3}\bar{z}_{34}^{4}}{z_{13}z_{14}\bar{z}_{12}\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{14}}\left|\frac{1}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{24}}\right|\delta\left(z_{34}\right)\\
&=\frac{\bar{z}_{34}^{4}}{\bar{z}_{12}\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{14}}\left|\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{24}}\right|\mathrm{sgn}(z_{12})\delta(z_{34})\end{split}

(73)

Now due to the delta functions
\delta\left(z_{24}\right)
and
\delta\left(z_{34}\right)
, the remaining simplex variables
\sigma^{*}_{2},\sigma^{*}_{3}
and
\sigma^{*}_{4}
become

\begin{split}&\sigma_{2}^{*}\rightarrow\rho^{*}_{2}=\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{4}\frac{\bar{z}_{43}}{D_{3}},\quad\sigma_{3}^{*}\rightarrow\rho^{*}_{3}=-\varepsilon_{3}\varepsilon_{4}\frac{\bar{z}_{42}}{D_{3}},\quad\sigma_{4}^{*}\rightarrow\rho^{*}_{4}=\frac{\bar{z}_{32}}{D_{3}}\\
\end{split}

(74)

where

\begin{split}D\rightarrow\frac{D_{3}}{\bar{z}_{23}},\quad D_{3}=(1-\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{3})\bar{z}_{42}+(\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{2}-1)\bar{z}_{43}\end{split}

Combining the above results we then obtain

\begin{split}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{4}\sigma_{i}^{\ast 1+i\lambda_{i}}\right)f(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\delta(\sigma_{1}^{*})&\rightarrow\left(\prod_{i=2}^{4}\rho_{i}^{\ast i\lambda_{i}}\right)\frac{z_{12}}{\bar{z}_{12}}\frac{\bar{z}_{43}^{4}}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{14}D_{3}^{2}}\hskip 1.42271pt\mathrm{sgn}(\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\bar{z}_{34}\bar{z}_{42}\bar{z}_{32}D_{3})\delta(z_{24})\delta(z_{34})\\
&=\left(\prod_{i=2}^{4}\rho_{i}^{\ast i\lambda_{i}}\right)\frac{z_{12}}{\bar{z}_{12}}\frac{\bar{z}_{43}^{4}}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{14}D_{3}^{2}}\hskip 1.42271pt\delta(z_{24})\delta(z_{34})\end{split}

Note that the last line in the above expression follows from the fact that due to the
3
-particle indicator function
\prod_{i=2}^{4}\textbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\sigma_{i}^{\ast})
, we get
\mathrm{sgn}(\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\bar{z}_{34}\bar{z}_{42}\bar{z}_{32}D_{3})=\mathrm{sgn}\left(\rho^{*}_{2}\rho^{*}_{3}\rho^{*}_{4}\right)=1
.

Now equation (
69
) takes the form

\begin{split}&\lim_{\lambda_{1}\to 0}i\lambda_{1}\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{A}(1^{--},2^{--},3^{++},4^{++})(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})\\
&=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left[\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\Gamma(2+i\Lambda^{\prime})}{\left(i\sum\varepsilon_{i}\rho_{i}^{\ast}u_{i}+\delta\right)^{2+i\Lambda^{\prime}}}\right]\left(\prod_{i=2}^{4}\rho_{i}^{\ast i\lambda_{i}}\right)\frac{z_{12}}{\bar{z}_{12}}\frac{\bar{z}_{43}^{4}}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{14}D_{3}^{2}}\hskip 1.42271pt\delta(z_{24})\delta(z_{34})\prod_{i=2}^{4}\textbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\rho_{i}^{\ast})\\
&=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\hskip 1.13791pt\varepsilon_{3}\varepsilon_{4}\left[\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1+i\Lambda^{\prime}}{\left(i\sum\varepsilon_{i}\rho_{i}^{\ast}u_{i}+\delta\right)}\right]\rho^{*}_{2}\left(\frac{z_{12}}{\bar{z}_{12}}\frac{\bar{z}_{32}\bar{z}_{42}}{\bar{z}_{31}\bar{z}_{41}}\right)\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{A}(2^{--},3^{++},4^{++})(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})\end{split}

(77)

where
\mathcal{A}(2^{--},3^{++},4^{++})(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})
is the modified Mellin transform of the
3
-point
\overline{\mathrm{MHV}}
graviton amplitude
S(2^{--},3^{++},4^{++})(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
.This was evaluated in section (

) and is given by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{A}(2^{--},3^{++},4^{++})(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})\\
&=\kappa\left[\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\Gamma(1+i\Lambda^{\prime})}{\left(i\sum\varepsilon_{i}\rho_{i}^{\ast}u_{i}+\delta\right)^{1+i\Lambda^{\prime}}}\right]\left(\prod_{i=2}^{4}\rho_{i}^{\ast i\lambda_{i}}\right)\left(\frac{\rho^{*}_{4}\rho^{*}_{3}}{\rho^{*}_{2}}\frac{\bar{z}_{43}^{3}}{\bar{z}_{32}\bar{z}_{24}}\right)^{2}\frac{\delta(z_{24})\delta(z_{34})}{2\rho^{*}_{4}\rho^{*}_{3}\rho^{*}_{2}D_{3}^{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{4}\textbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\sigma_{i}^{\ast})\end{split}

(78)

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}}\mathcal{A}(2^{--}3^{++}4^{++})=-\left[\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1+i\Lambda^{\prime}}{\left(i\sum\varepsilon_{i}\rho_{i}^{\ast}u_{i}+\delta\right)}\right]i\epsilon_{2}\rho^{*}_{2}\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{A}(2^{--},3^{++},4^{++})\\
\end{split}

(79)

Using this in equation (

\begin{split}&\lim_{\lambda_{1}\to 0}i\lambda_{1}\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{A}(1^{--},2^{--},3^{++},4^{++})(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})\\
&=\frac{\kappa}{2}\left(-i\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\varepsilon_{4}\frac{z_{12}}{\bar{z}_{12}}\frac{\bar{z}_{32}\bar{z}_{42}}{\bar{z}_{31}\bar{z}_{41}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}}\left(\mathcal{A}(2^{--}3^{++}4^{++})(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})\right)\\

Evidently the result in (

4.5
Conformal soft limit in gluon four point amplitude

Conformal soft limit of celestial amplitudes in gauge theory has been studied in
Pate:2019mfs
. In this section we show how to take soft limit in modified Mellin transformed gauge amplitude. The 4-point modified Mellin MHV amplitude in Yang-Mills theory in
\left(-,+,+,+\right)
spacetime signature obtained in Eq.(
3.2
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left(1^{-}2^{-}3^{+}4^{+}\right))(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\Gamma\left(i\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\lambda_{i}\right)}{\left(i\sum\varepsilon_{i}\sigma_{i}^{\ast}u_{i}+\delta\right)^{i\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\lambda_{i}}}\right]\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{4}\sigma_{i}^{\ast i\lambda_{i}}\right)\frac{\sigma_{1}^{\ast}\sigma_{2}^{\ast}}{\sigma_{3}^{\ast}\sigma_{4}^{\ast}}\frac{z_{12}^{3}}{z_{23}z_{34}z_{41}}\frac{\delta(|z-\bar{z}|)}{z_{13}\bar{z}_{13}z_{24}\bar{z}_{24}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\textbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\sigma_{i}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\Gamma\left(i\Lambda\right)}{\left(i\varepsilon_{4}\bigl\{-\frac{z_{24}\bar{z}_{34}}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{13}}u_{1}+\frac{z_{34}\bar{z}_{14}}{z_{23}\bar{z}_{12}}u_{2}-\frac{z_{24}\bar{z}_{14}}{z_{23}\bar{z}_{12}}u_{3}+u_{4}\bigr\}+\delta\right)^{i\Lambda}}\right]\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\frac{z_{23}\bar{z}_{34}}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{14}}\right)^{1+i\lambda_{1}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\frac{z_{13}\bar{z}_{34}}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{42}}\right)^{1+i\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{4}}\frac{\bar{z}_{13}z_{23}}{\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}}\right)^{-1+i\lambda_{4}-i\Lambda}\delta\left(|z_{12}z_{34}\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{24}-z_{13}z_{24}\bar{z}_{12}\bar{z}_{34}|\right)\frac{z_{12}^{3}}{z_{23}z_{34}z_{41}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\textbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\sigma_{i}^{\ast}).

Here
\Lambda=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\lambda_{i}
. The indicator functions signify that
\sigma_{i}^{\ast}
should be positive otherwise the amplitude vanishes. For two-to-two scattering process,
ij\rightleftarrows kl
with
\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}=-\varepsilon_{k}=-\varepsilon_{l}
the indicator functions imply

\displaystyle 12\rightleftarrows 34

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle z_{1}>z_{3}>z_{2}>z_{4}\quad\text{or}\quad z_{1}<z_{3}<z_{2}<z_{4},

\displaystyle 13\rightleftarrows 24

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle z_{1}>z_{2}>z_{3}>z_{4}\quad\text{or}\quad z_{1}<z_{2}<z_{3}<z_{4},

\displaystyle 14\rightleftarrows 23

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle z_{1}>z_{3}>z_{4}>z_{2}\quad\text{or}\quad z_{1}<z_{3}<z_{4}<z_{2}.

(82)

Similar orderings exist for
\bar{z}_{ij}
. It can be checked that above conditions are consistent with replacing the product of indicator functions by step functions as

\prod_{i=1}^{4}\textbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\sigma_{i}^{\ast})=\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\frac{z_{23}\bar{z}_{34}}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{14}}\right)\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\frac{z_{13}\bar{z}_{34}}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{42}}\right)\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{4}}\frac{\bar{z}_{13}z_{23}}{\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}}\right).

(83)

Including the factor
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{4}}\frac{\bar{z}_{13}z_{23}}{\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}}\right)^{-i\Lambda}
denominator of Eq.(
4.5
) becomes

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\left[i\varepsilon_{3}\left(\frac{z_{23}\bar{z}_{34}}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{14}}u_{1}+\frac{z_{13}\bar{z_{34}}}{z_{12}\bar{z}_{24}}u_{2}+\frac{\bar{z}_{13}}{\bar{z}_{12}}u_{3}+\frac{\bar{z}_{13}z_{23}}{\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}}u_{4}\right)+\delta\right]^{i\Lambda}.

(84)

To take soft limit we multiply both sides of Eq.(
4.5
) by
\lim_{\lambda_{4}\rightarrow 0}i\lambda_{4}
. Following Eq.(
50
) we get

\lim_{\lambda_{4}\rightarrow 0}i\lambda_{4}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{4}}\frac{\bar{z}_{13}z_{23}}{\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}}\right)^{-1+i\lambda_{4}}=2\delta\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{4}}\frac{\bar{z}_{13}z_{23}}{\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}}\right)=\text{sgn}\left(\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}z_{23}\right)2\frac{\bar{z}_{14}z_{42}}{z_{23}}\delta\left(\bar{z}_{13}\right).

(85)

Using the above relation delta function appearing in Eq.(
4.5
) becomes

\delta\left(|z_{12}z_{34}\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{24}-z_{13}z_{24}\bar{z}_{12}\bar{z}_{34}|\right)=\frac{\text{sgn}\left(z_{13}z_{24}\bar{z}_{34}\right)}{z_{13}z_{24}\bar{z}_{34}}\delta\left(\bar{z}_{12}\right).

(86)

These two constraints also imply
\bar{z}_{12}=\bar{z}_{13}=\bar{z}_{23}=0
. Then in the soft limit Eq.(
84
) takes the form

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\left[i\varepsilon_{3}z_{12}^{-1}\left(z_{23}u_{1}+z_{13}u_{2}+z_{12}u_{3}\right)+\delta\right]^{i\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}}.

(87)

We note that in the soft limit argument inside the last
\Theta
function in Eq.(
83
) vanishes which gives
\Theta\left(0\right)=\frac{1}{2}
. Then combining the delta functions and other
z
-dependent factors we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda_{4}\rightarrow 0}i\lambda_{4}\mathcal{A}\left(1^{-}2^{-}3^{+}4^{+}\right)(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\lambda_{i})

(88)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{sgn}\left(z_{23}z_{31}\right)\frac{z_{31}}{z_{34}z_{41}}\delta\left(\bar{z}_{12}\right)\delta\left(\bar{z}_{13}\right)\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{1}^{i\lambda_{1}}\varepsilon_{2}^{i\lambda_{2}}\varepsilon_{3}^{i\lambda_{3}}z_{12}^{1+i\lambda_{3}}z_{23}^{-1+i\lambda_{1}}z_{31}^{-1+i\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Gamma\left(i\left(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\right)\right)}{\left[i\left(u_{1}z_{23}+u_{2}z_{31}+u_{3}z_{12}\right)+\delta\right]^{i\left(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\right)}}\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\frac{z_{23}}{z_{12}}\right)\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\frac{z_{31}}{z_{12}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{z_{31}}{z_{34}z_{41}}\mathcal{A}\left(1^{-}2^{-}3^{+}\right).

Here the 3-point gluon amplitude is expressed in spacetime with split signature
\left(-,+,-,+\right)
. In this case 3-point gluon amplitude can be worked out in analogous way to the graviton 3-point amplitude.

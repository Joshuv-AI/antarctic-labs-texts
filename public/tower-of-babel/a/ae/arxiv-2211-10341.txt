The Kudla-Millson form via the Mathai-Quillen formalism
Romain Branchereau

Abstract
In [ 6 ] , Kudla and Millson constructed a q q -form φ K ​ M \varphi_{KM} on an orthogonal symmetric space using Howe’s differential operators. It is a crucial ingredient in their theory of theta lifting. This form can be seen as a Thom form of a real oriented vector bundle. In [ 9 ] Mathai and Quillen constructed a canonical Thom form and we show how to recover the Kudla-Millson form via their construction. A similar result was obtained by [ 3 ] for signature ( 2 , q ) (2,q) in case the symmetric space is hermitian and we extend it to an arbitrary signature.

Introduction

Let
(V,Q)
be a quadratic space over
\mathbb{Q}

is the connected component of the identity and acts transitively on

. Hence we can identify
\mathbb{D}^{+}
with
G(\mathbb{R})^{+}/K
, where

is a compact subgroup of

and is isomorphic to

. Moreover let

be a lattice in

and

For every vector

of codimension

q

\Gamma_{v}
denote the stabilizer of

in
\Gamma
. We can view
\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}
as a rank

\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{v}^{+}

\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{v}^{+}

\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}

, Kudla and Millson constructed a closed

-invariant differential form

\displaystyle\varphi_{KM}\in\left[\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{+})\otimes\mathscr{S}(V(\mathbb{R}))\right]^{G(\mathbb{R})^{+}},

(1.1)

where

acts on the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(V(\mathbb{R}))
from the left by
(gf)(v)\coloneqq f(g^{-1}v)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}}\varphi_{KM}(v)\wedge\omega=2^{-\frac{q}{2}}e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}_{v}}\omega.

(1.2)

Another way to state it is to say that in cohomology we have

\displaystyle[\varphi_{KM}(v)]=2^{-\frac{q}{2}}e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}\operatorname{PD}(\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}_{v})\in H^{q}\left(\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}\right),

where

Kudla-Millson theta lift.

In order to motivate the interest in the Kudla-Millson form, let us briefly recall how it is used to construct a theta correspondence between certain cohomology classes and modular forms. Let
\omega
be the Weil representation of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
in
\mathscr{S}(V(\mathbb{R}))
. We extend it to a representation in
\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{+})\otimes\mathscr{S}(V(\mathbb{R}))
by acting in the second factor of the tensor product. Building on the work of
[
10
]
, Kudla and Millson
[
7
,
8
]
used their differential form to construct the theta series

\displaystyle\Theta_{KM}(\tau)\coloneqq y^{-\frac{p+q}{4}}\sum_{v\in L}\Bigl(\omega(g_{\tau})\varphi_{KM}\Bigr)(v)\in\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{+}),

(1.4)

where
\tau=x+iy
is in
\mathbb{H}
and
g_{\tau}
is the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{y}&x\sqrt{y}^{-1}\\
0&\sqrt{y}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
that sends
i
to
\tau
by Möbius transformation. This form is
\Gamma
-invariant, closed and holomorphic in cohomology. Kudla and Millson showed that if we integrate this closed form on a
compact

q
-cycle
C
in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+})
, then

\displaystyle\int_{C}\Theta_{KM}(\tau)=c_{0}(C)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl<C,C_{2n}\bigr>e^{2i\pi n\tau}

(1.5)

is a modular form of weight
\frac{p+q}{2}
, where

\displaystyle C_{n}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\Gamma\backslash L\\
Q(v,v)=n\end{subarray}}C_{v}

(1.6)

and the
special cycles

C_{v}
are the images of the composition

\displaystyle\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{v}^{+}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}\longrightarrow\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}.

(1.7)

Thus, the Kudla-Millson theta series realizes a lift between the (co)-homology of
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}
and the space of weight
\frac{p+q}{2}
modular forms.

The result.

Let
E
be a
G(\mathbb{R})^{+}
-equivariant vector bundle of rank
q
over
\mathbb{D}^{+}
and
E_{0}
the image of the zero section. By the equivariance we also have a vector bundle
\Gamma_{v}\backslash E
over
\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}
. The
Thom class
of the vector bundle is a characteristic class
\operatorname{Th}(\Gamma_{v}\backslash E)
in
H^{q}(\Gamma_{v}\backslash E,\Gamma_{v}\backslash(E-E_{0}))
defined by the Thom isomorphism; see Subsection
3.6
. A
Thom form
is a form representing the Thom class. It can be shown that the Thom class is also the Poincaré dual class to
\Gamma_{v}\backslash E_{0}
. Let
s_{v}\colon\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{v}\backslash E
be a section whose zero locus is
\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{v}^{+}
, then

\displaystyle s_{v}^{\ast}\operatorname{Th}(\Gamma_{v}\backslash E)\in H^{q}\left(\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+},\Gamma_{v}\backslash(\mathbb{D}^{+}-\mathbb{D}_{v}^{+})\right).

(1.8)

Viewing it as a class in
H^{q}(\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+})
it is the Poincaré dual class of
\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{v}^{+}
. Since the Poincaré dual class is unique, property (
1.3
) implies that

\displaystyle[\varphi_{KM}(v)]=2^{-\frac{q}{2}}e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}s_{v}^{\ast}\operatorname{Th}(\Gamma_{v}\backslash E)\in H^{q}\left(\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}\right),

(1.9)

on the level of cohomology.

For arbitrary oriented real metric vector bundles, Mathai and Quillen used the Chern-Weil theory to construct in
[
9
]
a canonical Thom forms on
E
. We denote by
U_{MQ}
the canonical Thom form in
\Omega^{q}(E)
of Mathai and Quillen. Since
U_{MQ}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, it is also a Thom form for the bundle
\Gamma_{v}\backslash E
for every vector
v
. The main result is the following.

Theorem
.

(Theorem
4.5
) We have
\varphi_{KM}(v)=2^{-\frac{q}{2}}e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}s_{v}^{\ast}U_{MQ}
in
\Omega^{q}(\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+})

For signature
(2,q)
, the spaces are hermitian and the result was obtained by a similar method in
[
3
]
using the work of Bismut-Gillet-Soulé.

The Kudla-Millson form

\displaystyle\left[\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{+})\otimes\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{p+q})\right]^{G(\mathbb{R})^{+}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\left[\sideset{}{{}^{q}}{\bigwedge}\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{p+q})\right]^{K}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\varphi_{e}

in

3.3

Covariant derivatives

A
covariant derivative
on the vector bundle

is a differential operator

\displaystyle\nabla_{P}\colon\Omega^{0}(P/K,P\times_{K}z_{0})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}(P/K,P\times_{K}z_{0})

(3.20)

such that for every smooth function

we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{P}(fs)=df\otimes s+f\nabla_{P}(s).

(3.21)

The inner product on

\displaystyle\Omega^{i}(P/K,P\times_{K}z_{0})\times\Omega^{j}(P/K,P\times_{K}z_{0})

\displaystyle(\omega_{1}\otimes s_{1},\omega_{2}\otimes s_{2})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\langle\omega_{1}\otimes s_{1},\omega_{2}\otimes s_{2}\rangle=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle,

(3.22)

\displaystyle d\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{P}s_{1},s_{2}\rangle+\langle s_{1},\nabla_{P}s_{2}\rangle

(3.23)

and

in

by abuse of notation. Note that for the representation (
3.2
) this is simply the map

\displaystyle\rho\colon\mathfrak{k}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(z_{0})

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\longmapsto{\left.\kern-1.2ptX\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{z_{0}}}

(3.25)

since

splits as

\displaystyle\rho(\theta_{P})\in\Omega^{1}(P,\mathfrak{so}(z_{0})).

(3.26)

In particular, if

is a section of

then we can identify it with a
K
-invariant smooth map

in

is a

Lemma 3.2

The form

is basic, hence gives a

-valued form on

See
[
1
, p. 24]

\displaystyle\langle\rho(\theta_{P})f_{s_{1}},f_{s_{2}}\rangle+\langle f_{s_{1}},\rho(\theta_{P})f_{s_{2}}\rangle=0.

Hence if we denote by
\nabla_{P}
is the covariant derivative defined by

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{P}s_{1},s_{2}\rangle+\langle s_{1},\nabla_{P}s_{2}\rangle=\langle df_{s_{1}},f_{s_{2}}\rangle+\langle f_{s_{1}},df_{s_{2}}\rangle=d\langle f_{s_{1}},f_{s_{2}}\rangle=d\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle.

∎

\det\langle v_{i},w_{j}\rangle_{i,j}&\textrm{if}&k=l.\end{array}\right.

(3.50)

If

where

and extended by linearity, where the symbol

\displaystyle\exp(2x\eta-\eta^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\left(2x\eta-\eta^{2}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{k\choose l}(2x\eta)^{k-l}\left(-\eta^{2}\right)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{k\choose l}(2x)^{k-l}(-1)^{l}\eta^{l+k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{n}(x)\eta^{n},

(4.31)

where

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq l\leq k\leq n\\
k+l=n\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!(k-l)!}(2x)^{k-l}.

(4.32)

The conditions on
k
and
l
imply that
n
is less than or equal to
2k
. First suppose that
n
is even. Then we have that
k
is between
\frac{n}{2}
and
n
, so that the sum above can be written

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\frac{n}{2}}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!(2k-n)!}(2x)^{2k-n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}-m}}{(\frac{n}{2}-m)!(2m)!}(2x)^{2m}=\frac{1}{n!}H_{n}(x),

(4.33)

where in the second step we let
m
be
k-\frac{n}{2}
. If
n
is odd then
k
is between
\frac{n+1}{2}
and
n
, so that the sum can be written

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\frac{n+1}{2}}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!(2k-n)!}(2x)^{2k-n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}-m}}{(\frac{n-1}{2}-m)!(2m+1)!}(2x)^{2m+1}=\frac{1}{n!}H_{n}(x).

(4.34)

∎

Applying the lemma to (
4.28
) we get

\displaystyle\int^{B}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{p}\exp\left(2\sqrt{\pi}x_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int^{B}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{p}\exp\left(2\sqrt{2\pi}x_{\alpha}\frac{\eta_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2}}-\left(\frac{\eta_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int^{B}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{p}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{2^{-n/2}}{n!}H_{n}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{\alpha}\right)\eta_{\alpha}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{p}}\frac{2^{-\frac{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}}{2}}}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!}H_{n_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{1}\right)\cdots H_{n_{p}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{p}\right)\int^{B}\eta_{1}^{n_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{p}^{n_{p}}.

(4.35)

If
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}
is different from
q
, then the Berezinian of
\eta_{1}^{n_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{p}^{n_{p}}
vanishes and we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{p}}\frac{2^{-\frac{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}}{2}}}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!}H_{n_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{1}\right)\cdots H_{n_{p}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{p}\right)\int^{B}\eta_{1}^{n_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{p}^{n_{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{q}{2}}\sum_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{p}=q}\frac{H_{n_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{1}\right)\cdots H_{n_{p}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{p}\right)}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!}\int^{B}\eta_{1}^{n_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{p}^{n_{p}}.

(4.36)

Note that

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mu=p+1}^{p+q}\omega_{\alpha\mu}\otimes e_{\mu}\right)^{n_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n_{\alpha}}}(\omega_{\alpha\mu_{1}}\otimes e_{\mu_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\omega_{\alpha\mu_{n_{\alpha}}}\otimes e_{\mu_{n_{\alpha}}})

\displaystyle=n_{\alpha}!\sum_{\mu_{1}<\dots<\mu_{n_{\alpha}}}(\omega_{\alpha\mu_{1}}\otimes e_{\mu_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\omega_{\alpha\mu_{n_{\alpha}}}\otimes e_{\mu_{n_{\alpha}}}),

(4.37)

where the sums are over all
\mu_{i}
’s between
p+1
and
p+q
. If
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}
is equal to
q
we have

\displaystyle\int^{B}\eta_{1}^{n_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{p}^{n_{p}}

\displaystyle=\int^{B}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{p}\left(\sum_{\mu=p+1}^{p+q}\omega_{\alpha\mu}\otimes e_{\mu}\right)^{n_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int^{B}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{p}n_{\alpha}!\sum_{\mu_{1}<\dots<\mu_{n_{\alpha}}}(\omega_{\alpha\mu_{1}}\otimes e_{\mu_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\omega_{\alpha\mu_{n_{\alpha}}}\otimes e_{\mu_{n_{\alpha}}})

\displaystyle=n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!\sum\int^{B}(\omega_{\alpha(p+1)}\otimes e_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\omega_{\alpha(p+q)}\otimes e_{q})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{q(q+1)}{2}}n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!\sum\omega_{\alpha_{1}(p+1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\alpha_{q}(p+q)},

(4.38)

where the sums in the last two lines go over all tuples
\underline{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
with
\alpha
between
1
and
p
, and the value
\alpha
appears exactly
n_{\alpha}
-times in
\underline{\alpha}
. Hence

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi^{0}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{e}}(v)=\begin{multlined}2^{-q}\pi^{-\frac{q}{2}}\sum\omega_{\alpha_{1}(p+1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\alpha_{q}(p+q)}\otimes H_{n_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{1}\right)\\
\cdots H_{n_{p}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{p}\right)\exp\left(2\pi{\left.\kern-1.2ptQ\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{z_{0}}}(v,v)\right).\end{multlined}

After multiplying by
\exp\left(-\pi Q(v,v)\right)
we get

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{e}}(v)=\begin{multlined}2^{-q}\pi^{-\frac{q}{2}}\sum\omega_{\alpha_{1}(p+1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\alpha_{q}(p+q)}\otimes H_{n_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{1}\right)\\
\cdots H_{n_{p}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{p}\right)\exp\left(-\pi Q_{z_{0}}^{+}(v,v)\right).\end{multlined}

The form is now rapidly decreasing in
v
, since the Siegel majorant is positive definite. We have

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{e}}\in\left[\sideset{}{{}^{q}}{\bigwedge}\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{p+q})\right]^{K}.

(4.43)

Theorem 4.5
.

We have
2^{-\frac{q}{2}}\varphi(v)=\varphi_{KM}(v)
.

Proof.

It is a straightforward computation to show that

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-n_{\alpha}/2}H_{n_{\alpha}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{\alpha}\right)\exp(-\pi x_{\alpha}^{2})

\displaystyle=\left(x_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{\alpha}}}\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\exp(-\pi x_{\alpha}^{2}).

(4.44)

Hence applying this we find that the Kudla-Millson form, defined by the Howe operators in (
2.25
), is

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi_{KM}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{e}}(v)

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}2^{-q}(2\pi)^{-\frac{q}{2}}\sum\omega_{\alpha_{1}(p+1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\alpha_{q}(p+q)}\otimes H_{n_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{1}\right)\\
\cdots H_{n_{p}}\left(\sqrt{2\pi}x_{p}\right)\exp\left(-\pi{\left.\kern-1.2ptQ\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{z_{0}}}(v,v)\right)\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{q}{2}}e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi^{0}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{e}}(v).

(4.47)

∎

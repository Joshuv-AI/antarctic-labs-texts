Conservation and Breakdown of Modulus of Continuity in the Propagation of Loglog Vortex for 2D Euler equation
Woohyu Jeon

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the persistency of loglog singular structure for 2D Euler equation under the perturbation of continuous function. First, we prove that when loglog vortex is perturbed by the function with modulus of continuity μ ⁡ ( r ) = ( log ⁡ 1 r ) − α ​ ( 0 < α < 1 ) \mu(r)=\left(\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-\alpha}~~(0<\alpha<1) , the loglog vortex is propagated while the perturbation term maintaining same modulus of continuity. We then show that this result is sharp: There exists a smooth function such that when the loglog vortex is perturbed by the smooth function, the norm of perturbation term corresponding to modulus of continuity μ ⁡ ( r ) = ( log ⁡ 1 r ) − α \mu(r)=\left(\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-\alpha} instantly blows up for all α > 1 \alpha>1 .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Persistence of singular structure

Singularity, by which we mean a quantity is infinite, is one of the most important and interesting object in mathematical physics. For example, a fundamental question in fluid dynamics is whether a singularity could be formed from smooth initial data in finite time, e.g., for 3D Navier-Stokes equation and 3D Euler equation. Besides the singularity formation issues, one can alternatively start from singularity and investigate whether the singular structure persists. We illustrate the persistency of singular structure for 2D Euler equation in the vorticity form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &w_{t}+u\cdot\nabla w=0,\\
&w(\cdot,0)=w_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where the vorticity

and velocity field

are related through Biot-Savart law

(1.2)

for
(a,b)^{\perp}\coloneqq(-b,a)
. The system (

However, in many physical phenomena, vorticity is sharply concentrated in a small region so that it would be modeled through unbounded or even measure-valued function, which could be regarded as a singularity. 1.2.
Main Result

We first define a function space induced from a modulus of continuity.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mu:[0,c]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a modulus of continuity (MOC), i.e.,
\mu
is a non-decreasing continuous function such that
\mu(0)=0
. Then, the space
C^{\mu}
is defined by

C^{\mu}\coloneqq\Big\{f\in L^{\infty}\,\Big|\,\left\|f\right\|_{C^{\mu}}\coloneqq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0<\left|x-y\right|<c}\frac{\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|}{\mu(\left|x-y\right|)}<\infty\Big\}.

For example, for the modulus of continuities
\mu_{1}(r)=r\log\frac{1}{r}
and
\mu_{2}(r)=r^{\alpha}
, the space
C^{\mu_{1}}
and
C^{\mu_{2}}
are the collection of log-Lipschitz function and Hölder continuous function respectively. In this paper, we fix the following three modulus of continuities:

\varphi_{\alpha}(r)=\left(\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-\alpha},\qquad\psi_{\alpha}(r)=r\left(\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{1-\alpha},\qquad\phi_{\beta}(r)=\left(\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-\beta}

(1.8)

for
\alpha\in(0,1),\beta\in(1,\infty)
.

We are now ready to state the first main result which corresponds to persistence of singular structure in the propagation of loglog vortex
w_{s}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Conservation of modulus of continuity in the propagation of loglog vortex)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be given and
g_{0}\in C^{\varphi_{\alpha}}
be a compactly supported function. Then, the unique solution
w(x,t)
of 2D Euler equation starting from initial vorticity

w_{0}(x)=w_{s}(x)+g_{0}(x)

could be written as the form of

w(x,t)=w_{s}(x-\phi^{*}(t))+g(x,t)

for
g\in L^{\infty}_{\textnormal{loc}}([0,\infty),C^{\varphi_{\alpha}})
. Here,
\phi^{*}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the particle trajectory of the origin.

Theorem
1.4
gives positive answer to Question
1.2
for
X=C^{\varphi_{\alpha}}
. While constant vorticity condition is necessary in the neighborhood of point vortex to prove the propagation of point vortex up to bounded correction, Theorem
1.4
does not require any specific condition in the neighborhood of loglog vortex. Also, our result shows that loglog vortex is not strong enough to break continuity of perturbation term for wide class of continuous initial vorticity, beyond the boundedness of perturbation term which was shown in
[
4
]
.

Theorem
1.4
leads to the natural question of the existence of other function space
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which also gives positive answer to Question
1.2
. However, the next main result shows that Theorem
1.4
is sharp: The answer to Question
1.2
is
No
for
X=C^{\phi_{\beta}}
for any
\beta\in(1,\infty)
. More precisely:

Theorem 1.5

(Breakdown of modulus of continuity in the propagation of loglog vortex)
.

There exists a compactly supported smooth function
g_{0}
such that for any
\beta\in(1,\infty)
and
T>0
, the unique solution
w(x,t)
starting from the initial vorticity

w_{0}(x)=w_{s}(x)+g_{0}(x),

which, by Theorem
1.4
, could be written in the form of

w(x,t)=w_{s}(x-\phi^{*}(t))+g(x,t)

for
g\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty})
and trajectory of the origin
\phi^{*}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|g(\cdot,t)\right\|_{C^{\phi_{\beta}}}=\infty.

(1.9)

Note that the initial perturbation
g_{0}
chosen in Theorem
1.5
is compactly supported smooth function. Furthermore, as can be seen in its precise definition (
3.1
),
g_{0}
is identically zero in a neighborhood of the origin in which the local

-norm blows up instantaneously in the sense of (

It is also noteworthy that one does not prevent blow-up even though the norm is estimated in lower regularity space whose regularity deteriorates continuously in time, i.e.,
H^{s(t)}
or
C^{0,\alpha(t)}
(See, e.g.,
[
2
]
for this kind of loss of regularity for logarithmically singular surface quasi-geostophic equation).

\displaystyle\frac{\left|g(\Phi(x,t),t)-g(\Phi(y,t),t)\right|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{\left|\Phi(x,t)-\Phi(y,t)\right|}\right)^{-\alpha}}\leq\frac{\left|g_{0}(x)-g_{0}(y)\right|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\right)^{-\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\left(\log\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\right)^{-\alpha}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{\left|\Phi(x,t)-\Phi(y,t)\right|}\right)^{-\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\underbrace{\frac{\left|v(\Phi(x,s),s)\cdot\nabla w_{s}(\Phi(x,s),s)-v(\Phi(y,s),s)\cdot\nabla w_{s}(\Phi(y,s),s)\right|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{\left|\Phi(x,s)-\Phi(y,s)\right|}\right)^{-\alpha}}}_{\underset{\text{Lem }\ref{forcing term}}{\leq}C\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|v(\cdot,s)\right\|_{C^{\psi_{\alpha}}}}\cdot\frac{\left(\log\frac{1}{\left|\Phi(x,s)-\Phi(y,s)\right|}\right)^{-\alpha}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{\left|\Phi(x,t)-\Phi(y,t)\right|}\right)^{-\alpha}}\,ds

(2.16)

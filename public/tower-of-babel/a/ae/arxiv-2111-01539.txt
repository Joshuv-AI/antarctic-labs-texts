Abstract
. We improve the homology stability range for the 3rd integral homology of symplectic groups over commutative local rings with infinite residue field. As an application, we show that for local commutative rings containing an infinite field of characteristic not 2 2 the symbol map from Milnor-Witt K K -theory to higher Grothendieck-Witt groups is an isomorphism in degrees ≤ 3 \leq 3 .

A

over a ring
R

R
-module of finite rank.
A map of spaces is a map of
R
-modules between spaces.
A submodule

A map of spaces

An element

A

\langle x,x\rangle=0

Note that symplectic forms satisfy
\langle x,y\rangle=-\langle y,x\rangle

A symplectic space

has even rank then the kernel is required to be

has odd rank, then the kernel is required to have rank

From now on, let

be a commutative local ring.
A basis

is the

is split with kernel of rank

A

Remark 2.1
.

Let

be a basis of a symplectic space

Proof.

(1)

7.
Localising homology groups

The goal in this section is to show that if
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R)
is a surjection (injection, isomorphism) in homology then so is
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R)
; see Proposition
7.1
.

Recall that

\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R)
of matrices

(7.1)

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&c&{{}^{t}\!u}\psi M\\
0&1&0\\
0&u&M\end{smallmatrix}\right)

where
\psi=\psi_{2n}
,
M\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R)
,
u\in R^{2n}
,
c\in R
.
We let the group
R^{*}
of units of
R
act from the left on
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R)
by conjugation with the matrix
T_{b}\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R)
for
b\in R^{*}
where

T_{b}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}b&0&0\\
0&b^{-1}&0\\
0&0&1_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

and
1_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix.
Note that
T_{b}\cdot A\cdot T_{b}^{-1}=A
for
A\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R)
and
T_{b}\cdot A\cdot T_{b}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)
for
A\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)
since

T_{b}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&c&{{}^{t}\!u}\psi M\\
0&1&0\\
0&u&M\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot T_{b}^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b^{2}c&b{{}^{t}\!u}\psi M\\
0&1&0\\
0&bu&M\end{smallmatrix}\right).

By functoriality, this defines an
R^{*}
-action (hence a left
\mathbb{Z}[R^{*}]
-module structure) on the homology groups
H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{q}(R))
for
q=2n,2n+1,2n+2
.
The action is trivial for
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
and
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}
, the latter because
T_{b}\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R)
, but that action is non-trivial for
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}
, in general.
Let
m
be an integer such that
m>2t
.
We choose units

a_{1},...,a_{m}\in R^{*}
such that for every non-empty subset
I\subset\{1,...,m\}
the partial sum
a_{I}=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}
is a unit in
R
.
This is possible since
R
has infinite residue field.
Let
s_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[R^{*}]
be the element

s_{m}=-\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,...,m\}}(-1)^{|I|}\langle a_{I}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}[R^{*}]

first considered in
[
Sch17a
, §2]

where
\langle u\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}[R^{*}]
denotes the element of the group ring corresponding to
u\in R^{*}
.
Since
R^{*}
acts trivially on
H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R))
and
H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R))
, multiplication by
s_{m}
on those groups is the identity map
[
Sch17a
, p. 7]
in view of the equality

1=-\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,...,m\}}(-1)^{|I|}.

In particular, we have

H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R))=s_{m}^{-1}H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R)),\hskip 12.91663ptH_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R))=s_{m}^{-1}H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R)).

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
m,n,t\geq 0
be integers with
m>2t
, and let
R
be a local ring with infinite residue field.
Then localisation of the maps
H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R))\to H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R))\to H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R))
at
s_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[R^{*}]
induces a commutative diagram of abelian groups

\textstyle{H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{s_{m}^{-1}H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{s_{m}^{-1}H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{s_{m}^{-1}H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R))}

in which the outer two vertical arrows and the lower left horizontal arrow are isomorphisms.
In particular, if the map
H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R))\to H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R))
is a surjection (injection, isomorphism) then so is
H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R))\to H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}(R))
.

Proof.

We have already seen that the two outer vertical maps are isomorphisms.
Also, a localisation of a surjection (injection, isomorphism) is a surjection (injection, isomorphism).
So, all we have to prove is that the lower left horizontal map is an isomorphism.
The inclusion of groups
\varepsilon:\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)

has a retraction

(7.2)

\rho:\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R):\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&c&{{}^{t}\!u}\psi M\\
0&1&0\\
0&u&M\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto M.

This defines an exact sequence of groups

1\to G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R)\to 1.

Since the map
\rho
is
R^{*}
-equivariant, the group
R^{*}
acts on the exact sequence and hence on the associated Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence

E^{2}_{p,q}=H_{p}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R),H_{q}(G))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)).

We localise that spectral sequence at
s_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[R^{*}]
to obtain the spectral sequence

(7.3)

s_{m}^{-1}E^{2}_{p,q}=H_{p}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R),s_{m}^{-1}H_{q}(G))\Rightarrow s_{m}^{-1}H_{p+q}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}(R)).

Here we used that
s_{m}^{-1}H_{p}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R),H_{q}(G))=H_{p}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R),s_{m}^{-1}H_{q}(G))

since
R^{*}
acts trivially on
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(R)
.
There is a central extension of groups

1\to(R,+)\to G\to(R^{2n},+)\to 1

where the second map sends

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&c&{{}^{t}\!u}\psi\\
0&1&0\\
0&u&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in G

to
u\in R^{2n}
.
This central extension is
R^{*}
-equivariant where
b\in R^{*}
acts on
(R,+)
via multiplication by
b^{2}
and on
(R^{2n},+)
via multiplication by
b
.
We have
s_{m}^{-1}H_{0}(G)=\mathbb{Z}
since
R^{*}
acts trivially on
H_{0}(G)
.
By Proposition
[
Sch21
, D.4]
, we have
s_{m}^{-1}H_{q}(G)=0
for
m>2q>0
.
In particular, the localised Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence (
7.3
) degenerates at
E^{2}
for
m>2t\geq 2q
to yield the isomorphism

\rho:s_{m}^{-1}H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+1}R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{t}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}R)

for
t<m/2
. Since
\rho
is a retract of
\varepsilon
, we are done.
∎

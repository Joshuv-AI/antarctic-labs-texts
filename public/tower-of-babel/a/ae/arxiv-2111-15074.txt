Periodicity of Grover walks on bipartite regular graphs with at most five distinct eigenvalues
Sho Kubota

Abstract
We determine connected bipartite regular graphs with four distinct adjacency eigenvalues that induce periodic Grover walks, and show that it is only C 6 C_{6} . We also show that there are only three kinds of the second largest eigenvalues of bipartite regular periodic graphs with five distinct eigenvalues. Using walk-regularity, we enumerate feasible spectra for such graphs. Keywords: Grover walk, quantum walk, periodicity, walk-regular MSC 2020 subject classifications: 05C50; 81Q99

In this paper,

As we will see in the beginning of Section

There are two main theorems.

.

Let

is periodic if and only if

.

2
Preliminaries

See
[
10
]
for basic terminologies related to graphs.
Throughout this paper,
we assume that all graphs are finite, simple, and connected whether we mention or not.
Let
M
be a square matrix.
We call the multiset of eigenvalues of
M
the
spectrum
or the
M
-spectrum
,
and denote it by
\Spec(M)
.
For example, when the
M
-spectrum is
\{4,0,0,0,-2,-2\}
,
we describe the multiplicities by superscript, as in
\{[4]^{1},[0]^{3},[-2]^{2}\}
.
The matrices
I_{n}
and
J_{n}
denote the identity matrix and the all-ones matrix of size
n
, respectively.
The subscripts can be omitted if the sizes of these matrices are clear in context.

2.1
Graphs and their spectra

The
adjacency matrix

A=A(\Gamma)\in\mathbb{C}^{V\times V}
of a graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
is defined by

A_{x,y}=\begin{cases}1\qquad&\text{if $\{x,y\}\in E$,}\\
0\qquad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We call eigenvalues of
A(\Gamma)

adjacency eigenvalues
.

Proposition 2.1

(Proposition 3.3.1 in
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
k
-regular graph with the adjacency eigenvalues

k=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
,
where
n
is the number of vertices.
Then we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}=nk
.

A graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
is said to be
bipartite

if its vertex set can be partitioned into two parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
such that
for any edge one end is in
V_{1}
and the other end is in
V_{2}
.
The two parts
V_{1},V_{2}
are called
partite sets
of
V
.
It is well-known that the
A
-spectrum of a bipartite graph is symmetric about the origin and vice versa.
See Theorem 8.8.2 in
[
10
]
for details.
In this paper,
we are mainly concerned with (connected) bipartite regular graphs
with four or five distinct adjacency eigenvalues.
The
A
-spectrum of such a graph is usually denoted by
\{[k]^{1},[\theta]^{a},[0]^{b},[-\theta]^{a},[-k]^{1}\}
,
but we will simply write it as
\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
.
The following is a theorem due to Hoffman,
often used in studies of graphs with few distinct adjacency eigenvalues.

Proposition 2.2

(Theorem 1 and its proof in
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
k
-regular graph with
n
vertices,
and let the distinct adjacency eigenvalues be
k>\lambda_{2}>\dots>\lambda_{s}
.
Then we have

q(A(\Gamma))=\frac{q(k)}{n}J_{n},

where
q(x)=\prod_{i=2}^{s}(x-\lambda_{i})
.

2.2
Grover walks

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. Define
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)=\{(x,y),(y,x)\mid\{x,y\}\in E(\Gamma)\}
.
The origin
x
and terminus
y
of
a=(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}
are denoted by
o(a),t(a)
, respectively.
We write the inverse arc of
a
as
a^{-1}
.

We introduce several matrices on Grover walks for a graph
\Gamma
.
The
boundary matrix

d=d(\Gamma)\in\mathbb{C}^{V\times\mathcal{A}}
is defined by

d_{x,a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\deg x}}\delta_{x,t(a)}
,
where
\delta_{a,b}
is the Kronecker delta.
The
shift matrix

S=S(\Gamma)\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}}

is defined by
S_{a,b}=\delta_{a,b^{-1}}
.
Define the
time evolution matrix

U=U(\Gamma)\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}}

by
U=S(2d^{*}d-I)
.
Quantum walks defined by
U
is called
Grover walks
.
The
discriminant

T=T(\Gamma)\in\mathbb{C}^{V\times V}
is defined by
T=dSd^{*}
.
Henceforth we will consider not only the spectrum of the adjacency matrix,
but also the ones of the discriminant and the time evolution matrix.
Define
\Spec_{A}(\Gamma)=\Spec(A(\Gamma))
for a graph
\Gamma
.

\Spec_{T}(\Gamma)
and
\Spec_{U}(\Gamma)
are defined in the same way.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
k
-regular graph,
and let
A
and
T
be the adjacency matrix and the discriminant of
\Gamma
, respectively.
Then we have
T=\frac{1}{k}A
.
Therefore, the absolute values of eigenvalues of
T
does not exceed
1
.

We omit a proof.
See Section 3 in
[
24
]
for more general claim and its proof.
Relationship between
U
-spectra and
T
-spectra has been studied
not only in the Grover walks but also in more general models
[
13
,
20
,
22
]
.
We cite the result in
[
13
]
,
but the statement is slightly modified to fit our setting.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a connected graph.
Then we have

\Spec_{U}(\Gamma)=\{e^{\pm i\arccos\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Spec_{T}(\Gamma)\}\cup\{1\}^{M_{1}}\cup\{-1\}^{M_{-1}},

where
M_{1}=|E|-|V|+1
and
M_{-1}=|E|-|V|+\dim\Ker(T+I)
.

By Lemma
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
,
the
U
-spectrum is obtained from the
A
-spectrum via the discriminant when a graph is regular.

Let
U
be the time evolution matrix of a graph
\Gamma
.
We say that
\Gamma
is
periodic
if there exists a positive integer
\tau
such that
U^{\tau}=I
.
When a graph
\Gamma
is periodic,
the positive integer
\min\{\tau\in\mathbb{N}\mid U^{\tau}=I\}
is called the
period
.
As we see immediately, periodicity is determined by
U
-spectrum.

Lemma 2.5

(Lemma 5.3 in
[
27
]
)
.

Let
U
be the time evolution matrix of a graph
\Gamma
.
Then, we have

\{\tau\in\mathbb{N}\mid U^{\tau}=I\}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{N}\mid\lambda^{\tau}=1\text{ \emph{for any} $\lambda\in\Spec_{U}(\Gamma)$}\}.

In particular,
\Gamma
is periodic if and only if
there exists a positive integer
\tau
such that
\lambda^{\tau}=1

for any eigenvalue
\lambda
of
U
.

3
Bipartite regular graphs with at most five distinct adjacency eigenvalues

In this section,
we derive a general fact on bipartite regular periodic graphs with four or five distinct adjacency eigenvalues.
Note that the bipartite regular graph with two distinct adjacency eigenvalues
is the complete graph
K_{2}
, which is known to be periodic
[
14
]
.
Bipartite regular graphs with three distinct adjacency eigenvalues
have the
A
-spectra of the form
\{[k]^{1},[0]^{n-2},[-k]^{1}\}
.
It is well-known that connected regular graphs with three distinct adjacency eigenvalues are strongly regular.
Recovering the parameters from the eigenvalues,
we see that their complements are disconnected.
Disconnected strongly regular graphs are disjoint unions of the complete graphs with the same size
(See Lemma 10.1.1 in
[
10
]
).
This implies that the graphs are the complete bipartite graphs
K_{k,k}
,
which are periodic shown in
[
14
]
.
In the end, we are concerned with bipartite regular graphs with four or five distinct adjacency eigenvalues.

Let
\Gamma
be a bipartite
k
-regular graph with
n
vertices and the
A
-spectrum
\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
,
where
a\geq 1
and
b\geq 0
.
Proposition
2.1
derives
2k^{2}+2a\theta^{2}=nk
.
We may assume that
\theta>0
without loss of generality,
and we have

(3.1)

\theta=\sqrt{\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{2a}}.

On the other hand,
let the partite set of the vertex set be
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
,
then we obtain

(3.2)

|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|.

Indeed, since
\Gamma
is bipartite,
we can display as

A(\Gamma)=\begin{bmatrix}O&N\\
N^{\top}&O\end{bmatrix}

for some
p\times q
matrix
N
.
The graph
\Gamma
is
k
-regular,
so
N{\bm{1}}_{q}=k{\bm{1}}_{p}
and
N^{\top}{\bm{1}}_{p}=k{\bm{1}}_{q}
,
where
{\bm{1}}_{r}
denotes the all-ones vector of size
r
.
We have
kp={\bm{1}}_{p}^{\top}(N{\bm{1}}_{q})=({\bm{1}}_{p}^{\top}N){\bm{1}}_{q}=kq
, i.e.,
p=q
.
This implies
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|
.

A complex number
\alpha
is said to be an
algebraic integer

if there exists a monic polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
p(\alpha)=0
.
Let
\Omega
denote the set of algebraic integers.
Note that since the characteristic polynomial of the adjacency matrix
is a monic polynomial with integer coefficients,
the adjacency eigenvalues of a graph
\Gamma
are algebraic integers,
i.e.,

(3.3)

\Spec_{A}(\Gamma)\subset\Omega.

It is well-known that
\Omega
is a subring of
\mathbb{C}
, and
\Omega\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}
.
See
[
17
,
28
]
for algebraic integers.
In addition, we will use integral bases of quadratic fields.
A positive integer
m>1
is said to be
square-free

if it is not divisible by
p^{2}
for any prime number
p
.

Proposition 3.1

(Proposition 2.34 in
[
17
]
)
.

Let
m>1
be a square-free integer.
Then

\Omega\cap\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})=\begin{cases}\{p+q\sqrt{m}\mid p,q\in\mathbb{Z}\}\quad&\text{if $m\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}$},\\
\{p+\frac{1+\sqrt{m}}{2}q\mid p,q\in\mathbb{Z}\}\quad&\text{if $m\equiv 1\pmod{4}$}.\end{cases}

Note that
m
is not square-free when
m\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a periodic graph.
If
\lambda\in\Spec_{T}(\Gamma)
, then
2\lambda\in\Omega
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, there exists a positive integer
\tau

such that
\Lambda^{\tau}=1
for any
\Lambda\in\Spec_{U}(\Gamma)
.
The eigenvalue
\Lambda
is a root of the monic polynomial
x^{\tau}-1\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
so it is an algebraic integer.
Let
\lambda\in\Spec_{T}(\Gamma)
.
Theorem
2.4
derives
e^{\pm i\arccos\lambda}\in\Spec_{U}(\Gamma)
.
Since
\Omega
is a ring,
we have
2\lambda=e^{i\arccos\lambda}+e^{-i\arccos\lambda}\in\Omega
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a bipartite
k
-regular graph with the
A
-spectrum

\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
,
where
a\geq 1
and
b\geq 0
.
Then
\Gamma
is periodic if and only if

k
is even and
\theta\in\{\frac{k}{2},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}k,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}k\}
.

Proof.

First, we show the sufficient condition for
\theta=\frac{k}{2}
.
The other cases are shown in the same way.
By Lemma
2.3
, we have
\Spec_{T}(\Gamma)=\{[\pm 1]^{1},[\pm\frac{1}{2}]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
.
Theorem
2.4
implies

\Spec_{U}(\Gamma)\subset\{\pm 1,e^{\pm\frac{\pi}{3}i},e^{\pm\frac{\pi}{2}i}\}
,
where the multiplicities are ignored.
Thus, there exists a positive integer
\tau
such that

\lambda^{\tau}=1
for any
\lambda\in\Spec_{U}(\Gamma)
,
and hence
\Gamma
is periodic by Lemma
2.5
.

We show the necessary condition.
We may assume that
\theta>0
without loss of generality.
We have
\frac{\theta}{k}\in\Spec_{T}(\Gamma)
since
\Gamma
is
k
-regular.
Lemma
3.2
derives
\frac{2\theta}{k}\in\Omega
.

Consider the case
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}
.
We have
\frac{2\theta}{k}\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}
.
Lemma
2.3
implies
0<\frac{\theta}{k}<1
.
Since
\frac{2\theta}{k}
is an integer, we have
\frac{2\theta}{k}=1
, i.e.,
\theta=\frac{k}{2}
.
From (
3.3
), we have

\theta\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
k
is even.

Next, we consider the case
\theta\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.
By (
3.1
), there exist a square-free integer
m>1
and
r\in\mathbb{Q}

such that
\theta=r\sqrt{m}
.
Thus, we have

(3.4)

\frac{2\theta}{k}=\frac{2r}{k}\sqrt{m}\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m}).

We claim that
m\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
Suppose
m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
By Proposition
3.1
, there exists
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\frac{2r}{k}\sqrt{m}=p+\frac{1+\sqrt{m}}{2}q.

Since
1,\sqrt{m}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
,
we have

(3.5)

p+\frac{q}{2}=0

and

(3.6)

\frac{2r}{k}=\frac{q}{2}.

Since
0<\frac{2\theta}{k}<2
,
Equalities (
3.4
) and (
3.6
) imply

0<q\sqrt{m}<4
.
Since
m\geq 5
, we have
0<q<\frac{4}{\sqrt{m}}<2
,
and hence
q=1
. Equality (
3.5
) shows that
p=-\frac{1}{2}
, which contradicts to
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Now, we have
m\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}
.
By Proposition
3.1
, there exists
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\frac{2r}{k}\sqrt{m}=p+q\sqrt{m}
.
Since
1,\sqrt{m}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
,
we have
p=0
and

(3.7)

q=\frac{2r}{k}.

Since
0<\frac{2\theta}{k}<2
,
Equalities (
3.4
) and (
3.7
) imply
0<q\sqrt{m}<2
.
Since
m\geq 2
, we have
0<q<\frac{2}{\sqrt{m}}<2
.
This implies
q=1
, and hence
(q,m)=(1,2),(1,3)
.
Therefore, we have
\frac{2\theta}{k}=\frac{2r}{k}\sqrt{m}=q\sqrt{m}\in\{\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3}\}
,
i.e.,
\theta\in\{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}k,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}k\}
.
The eigenvalue
\theta
is also in
\Omega\cap\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
,
so
k
must be even from Proposition
3.1
.
∎

4
Four distinct adjacency eigenvalues

In this section,
we consider a bipartite regular graph
\Gamma
with four distinct adjacency eigenvalues.
As mentioned in Proposition 15.1.3 in
[
4
]
,
it is known that such a graph is the incidence graph of a symmetric 2-design.
Therefore there are a very large number of connected bipartite regular graphs with four distinct adjacency eigenvalues, of which we will show that only
C_{6}
is periodic.
Let the
A
-spectrum of
\Gamma
be
\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{a}\}
, where
a\geq 1
.
Let
n
be the number of vertices of
\Gamma
.
Then we have
a=\frac{n}{2}-1
.
From the previous section,
if
\Gamma
is periodic, then
\theta\in\{\frac{k}{2},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}k,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}k\}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a bipartite
k
-regular graph with the
A
-spectrum

\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\}
,
where
n
is the number of vertices of
\Gamma
.
Then
\Gamma
is periodic if and only if
\Gamma
is isomorphic to the cycle graph
C_{6}
.

Proof.

It is well-known that the cycle graph
C_{6}
has the
A
-spectrum
\{[\pm 2]^{1},[\pm 1]^{2}\}
,
which is periodic.
We show that the assumed periodic graph is determined to
C_{6}
.
Since
\Gamma
is bipartite,
its adjacency matrix is displayed as

A=A(\Gamma)=\begin{bmatrix}O&N\\
N^{\top}&O\end{bmatrix}

for some matrix
N
.
This matrix
N
is square matrix of size
\frac{n}{2}
from (
3.2
).
We have

\displaystyle\{[k^{2}]^{2},[\theta^{2}]^{n-2}\}

\displaystyle=\Spec(A^{2})

\displaystyle=\Spec(NN^{\top})\cup\Spec(N^{\top}N)

\displaystyle=\Spec(NN^{\top})\cup\Spec(NN^{\top}).

This implies
\Spec(NN^{\top})=\{[k^{2}]^{1},[\theta^{2}]^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\}
,
that is,

(4.1)

\Spec(NN^{\top}-\theta^{2}I)=\{[k^{2}-\theta^{2}]^{1},[0]^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\}.

We take an eigenvector
\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}{\bm{1}}_{\frac{n}{2}}
of norm
1

associated to the eigenvalue
k^{2}-\theta^{2}
,
and eigenvectors
x_{1},\dots,x_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
associated to the eigenvalue
0

such that
x_{i}^{\top}x_{j}=\delta_{i,j}
.
The matrix
P=[\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}{\bm{1}}_{\frac{n}{2}},x_{1},\dots,x_{\frac{n}{2}-1}]

is an orthogonal matrix and diagonalizes
NN^{\top}-\theta^{2}I
.
Thus we have

NN^{\top}-\theta^{2}I=P\diag(k^{2}-\theta^{2},0,\dots,0)P^{\top}=\frac{2(k^{2}-\theta^{2})}{n}J_{\frac{n}{2}},

that is,

(4.2)

NN^{\top}=\theta^{2}I+\frac{2(k^{2}-\theta^{2})}{n}J_{\frac{n}{2}}

Since
\Gamma
is
k
-regular and
N
is
\{0,1\}
-matrix, we have

(NN^{\top})_{x,x}=\sum_{z\in V(\Gamma)}N_{x,z}N_{x,z}=\sum_{z\in V(\Gamma)}N_{x,z}=k

for any vertex
x\in V(\Gamma)
.
The
(x,x)
entries of both sides of Equality (
4.2
) are

k=\theta^{2}+\frac{2(k^{2}-\theta^{2})}{n}
.
In particular, we have

(4.3)

k>\theta^{2}.

Suppose
\theta\in\{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}k,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}k\}
.
Since
\theta\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}k
,
Inequality (
4.3
) derives
k<2
.
However,
k
is even by Theorem
3.3
.
This is impossible.
Thus
\theta=\frac{k}{2}
.
By (
4.3
) again, we have
k<4
, and hence
k=2
because
k
is even.
The
A
-spectrum of
\Gamma
is determined to be
\{[\pm 2]^{1},[\pm 1]^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\}
.
Equality (
3.1
) implies
n=6
.
Since
\Gamma
is connected,
the graph is determined to be the cycle graph
C_{6}
.
∎

5
Five distinct adjacency eigenvalues

In contrast to bipartite regular graphs with four distinct adjacency eigenvalues,
we can construct a large number of periodic graphs with five distinct adjacency eigenvalues.
On the other hand, of the feasible spectra,
there are many graphs whose existence is unknown.
Using tools of spectral graph theory,
we discuss feasible periodic graphs with five distinct adjacency eigenvalues.

5.1
Construction

Let
\Gamma
be a graph.
We write
\Gamma\otimes J_{m}
as the graph defined by

A(\Gamma\otimes J_{m})=A(\Gamma)\otimes J_{m}
.
If
m=1
, we simply regard
\Gamma\otimes J_{1}
as
\Gamma
.
If a graph
\Gamma
is
k
-regular and has the
A
-spectrum

\{[k]^{1},[\lambda_{1}]^{f_{1}},\dots,[\lambda_{s}]^{f_{s}}\}
,
then the graph
\Gamma\otimes J_{m}
is
km
-regular, and

\displaystyle\Spec_{A}(\Gamma\otimes J_{m})

\displaystyle=\{[km]^{1},[\lambda_{1}m]^{f_{1}},\dots,[\lambda_{s}m]^{f_{s}},[0]^{nm-n}\},

\displaystyle\Spec_{T}(\Gamma\otimes J_{m})

\displaystyle=\left\{[1]^{1},\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}}{k}\right]^{f_{1}},\dots,\left[\frac{\lambda_{s}}{k}\right]^{f_{s}},[0]^{nm-n}\right\},

where
n
is the number of vertices of
\Gamma
.
In particular, if
A(\Gamma)
has the eigenvalue 0,
then

and
T(\Gamma\otimes J_{m})
have the same spectrum except for the multiplicities.
From this observation,
it follows that

and

are also periodic graphs.
Note that the

ignoring its multiplicities is

\{\pm 1,\pm i,e^{\pm\frac{\pi}{3}i},e^{\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}i}\}
from Theorem

.
Thus, the period is
12
.
Similarly, the period of

is
8
.

Another construction method is to use graph products.
Let
\Gamma
and
\Delta
be graphs with

and

The

is the graph defined by

A(\Gamma\mathrel{\square}\Delta)=A(\Gamma)\otimes I_{m}+I_{n}\otimes A(\Delta)
.
The

A(\Gamma\otimes\Delta)=A(\Gamma)\otimes A(\Delta)
.
In particular, the graph
\Gamma\otimes K_{2}
is called the

of

.
The

is

.
These graph products can also be used to construct periodic graphs.
For example, the line graph of the

has the

5.2
Feasible spectra

From Theorem
3.3
,
if a bipartite
k
-regular graph
\Gamma
with five distinct adjacency eigenvalues is periodic,
the second largest eigenvalue can only take one of the three values, and

must be even.
However, there are still infinite possibilities for the number of vertices and multiplicities of eigenvalues.
We need to narrow down candidates for
A
-spectra of graphs.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a bipartite
k
-regular graph with the
A
-spectrum

\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
,
where
a,b\geq 1
.
If we display the adjacency matrix
A
of
\Gamma
as

A=\begin{bmatrix}O&N\\
N^{\top}&O\end{bmatrix},

then we have
NN^{\top}N=\theta^{2}N+\frac{2k}{n}(k^{2}-\theta^{2})J_{\frac{n}{2}}
,
where
n
is the number of vertices.

Proof.

The two vectors

\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{1}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\\
{\bm{1}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\beta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{1}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\\
-{\bm{1}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\end{bmatrix}

are eigenvectors of
A
associated to
k,-k
with norm
1
, respectively.
Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n-2}
be eigenvectors of
A
associated to
\lambda\not\in\{k,-k\}

such that
x_{i}^{\top}x_{j}=\delta_{i,j}
.
Define
M=(A^{2}-\theta^{2}I_{n})A
.
Then
x_{1},\dots,x_{n-2}
are eigenvectors of
M
associated to
0
.
Consider
Q=[\alpha,\beta,x_{1},\dots,x_{n-2}]
.
The matrix
Q
is an orthogonal matrix and diagonalizes
M
, so we have

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=Q\diag\left((k^{2}-\theta^{2})k,-(k^{2}-\theta^{2})k,0\dots,0\right)Q^{\top}

\displaystyle=(k^{2}-\theta^{2})k(\alpha\alpha^{\top}-\beta\beta^{\top})

\displaystyle=\frac{2k(k^{2}-\theta^{2})}{n}\begin{bmatrix}O&J_{\frac{n}{2}}\\
J_{\frac{n}{2}}&O\end{bmatrix}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\left(A^{2}-\theta^{2}I_{n}\right)A

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{bmatrix}O&N\\
N^{\top}&O\end{bmatrix}^{2}-\theta^{2}I_{n}\right)\begin{bmatrix}O&N\\
N^{\top}&O\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}O&NN^{\top}N-\theta^{2}N\\
N^{\top}NN^{\top}-\theta^{2}N^{\top}&O\end{bmatrix}.

Comparing (1,2)-block, we obtain the statement.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

With the above notation,
we have
n\leq 2k(k^{2}-\theta^{2})
.

Proof.

Pick
x\in V(\Gamma)
.
Let
\Gamma_{j}(x)
be the set of vertices at distance
j
from
x
.
Display as
\Gamma_{2}(x)=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{t}\}
.
Suppose that
\Gamma_{1}(x)=\Gamma_{1}(y_{i})
for any
y_{i}\in\Gamma_{2}(x)
.
Since
\Gamma
is connected,

\Gamma
is isomorphic to the complete bipartite graph
K_{k,k}
.
However, this contradicts the assumption of the
A
-spectrum of
\Gamma
.
Thus, there exists
y_{i}\in\Gamma_{2}(x)
such that
\Gamma_{1}(y_{i})\neq\Gamma_{1}(x)
.
Since
\Gamma
is
k
-regular, neither
\Gamma_{1}(y_{i})
nor
\Gamma_{1}(x)
is included in the other.
Thus, we have
\Gamma_{1}(y_{i})\setminus\Gamma_{1}(x)\neq\emptyset
.
Pick
z\in\Gamma_{1}(y_{i})\setminus\Gamma_{1}(x)
.
The pair of vertices
x,z
satisfies
N_{x,z}=0
and
(NN^{\top}N)_{x,z}\geq 1
.
By Lemma
5.1
,

1\leq(NN^{\top}N)_{x,z}=\left(\theta^{2}N+\frac{2k}{n}(k^{2}-\theta^{2})J_{\frac{n}{2}}\right)_{x,z}=\frac{2k}{n}(k^{2}-\theta^{2}).

We have the statement.
∎

A graph
\Gamma
with the adjacency matrix
A
is said to be
walk-regular

if
(A^{r})_{x,x}
is independent of the choice of
x
for each positive integer
r
.
Walk-regular graphs are regular since
\deg x=(A^{2})_{x,x}
is a constant.
See
[
9
]
for more information on walk-regular graphs.
Walk-regularity provides conditions for the existence of graphs.
Let the constant
(A^{r})_{x,x}
be
c_{r}
for a positive integer
r
,
and let the adjacency eigenvalues be
k=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
We have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{r}=\tr(A^{r})=\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}(A^{r})_{x,x}=nc_{r},

where
n
is the number of vertices.
The constant
c_{r}
is the number of closed walks, so

c_{r}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{r}

is a non-negative integer.
This condition restricts feasible spectra of walk-regular graphs.
Van Dam pointed out that regular graphs with four adjacency eigenvalues are walk-regular
[
5
]
.
Koledin and Stanić showed that
regular bipartite graphs with three distinct non-negative adjacency eigenvalues are walk-regular
[
19
]
.
Although a proof is similar, the graphs we consider are also walk-regular.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a bipartite
k
-regular graph with the
A
-spectrum

\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
,
where
a,b\geq 1
.
Then
\Gamma
is walk-regular.

Proof.

Let
n
be the number of vertices of
\Gamma
,
and let
A
be the adjacency matrix.
By Proposition
2.2
, we have
q(A)=\frac{q(k)}{n}J_{n}
,
where
q(x)=x(x+k)(x^{2}-\theta^{2})
.
In particular,

A^{4}
can be expressed as a linear combination of
A^{3},A^{2},A,I_{n}
, and
J_{n}

with rational coefficients.
Thus for any positive integer
r
,
there exist
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4},\alpha_{5}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

A^{r}=\alpha_{1}A^{3}+\alpha_{2}A^{2}+\alpha_{3}A+\alpha_{4}I_{n}+\alpha_{5}J_{n}
.
On the other hand,
(A^{3})_{x,x}=A_{x,x}=0
for any
x\in V(\Gamma)
since
\Gamma
is bipartite.
We have
(A^{r})_{x,x}=k\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5}
,
and hence
\Gamma
is walk-regular.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a bipartite
k
-regular graph with
n
vertices.
Suppose that the
A
-spectrum is
\Spec_{A}(\Gamma)=\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\theta]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
,
where
a,b\geq 1
.
Then we have the following.

(i)

a=\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{2\theta^{2}}
and
b=n-2-\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{\theta^{2}}
.
In particular,

\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{2\theta^{2}}
and
n-2-\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{\theta^{2}}
are positive integers;

(ii)

\frac{2(k^{2}+\theta^{2})}{k}\leq n\leq 2k(k^{2}-\theta^{2})
; and

(iii)

For any positive integer
r
,
we have
\frac{1}{n}(2\cdot k^{2r}+(nk-2k^{2})\theta^{2r-2})\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

(i)
Since the sum of the multiplicities is the number of vertices, we have

(5.1)

2a+b+2=n.

Proposition
2.1
implies

(5.2)

2k^{2}+2a\theta^{2}=nk,

which leads to
a=\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{2\theta^{2}}
.
Equalities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) imply
b=n-2-\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{\theta^{2}}
.
Since the multiplicities of eigenvalues are positive integers,

\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{2\theta^{2}}
and
n-2-\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{\theta^{2}}
are also positive integers.

(ii)
Since
a\geq 1
,
we have
\frac{nk-2k^{2}}{2\theta^{2}}\geq 1
, i.e.,
n\geq\frac{2(k^{2}+\theta^{2})}{k}
.
On the other hand,
Lemma
5.2
derives
n\leq 2k(k^{2}-\theta^{2})
.
Thus, we have
\frac{2(k^{2}+\theta^{2})}{k}\leq n\leq 2k(k^{2}-\theta^{2})
.

(iii)
By Lemma
5.3
, the graph
\Gamma
is walk-regular.
Thus, the constant
c_{2r}
is a non-negative integer for any positive integer
r
.
We have
\frac{1}{n}(2\cdot k^{2r}+(nk-2k^{2})\theta^{2r-2})\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

The above proposition narrows down the candidates of graphs.
Indeed, the condition (ii) makes possibility of

finite when

The conditions (i) and (iii) further restrict possibilities for

and

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a walk-regular graph with the adjacency eigenvalues

k=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
Denote by
q
the number of quadrangles in
\Gamma
and
by
q_{x}
the number of quadrangles containing a vertex
x\in V(\Gamma)
.
Then we have

(i)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{4}=n(2k^{2}-k)+8q

(ii)

The number
q_{x}
is a constant independent of the choice of a vertex,
and we have
q_{x}=\frac{4q}{n}

Proof.

Let the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma
be
A
.
First, we observe the

entry of

for a vertex
x

It represents the number of closed walks of length
4
from
x
to
x
.
See Figure

There are four possibilities for a closed walk of length
4

Case (a) shows a closed walk
(x,y_{1},x,y_{1},x)
where only one vertex
y_{1}
appears besides
x
.
There are
k
ways to get
y_{1}
, so there are
k
walks of this type.
Case (b) shows a closed walk
(x,y_{1},x,y_{2},x)
where two vertices
y_{1},y_{2}
are adjacent to
x
.
The total number getting
y_{1},y_{2}
is
k(k-1)
.
Thus, there are
k(k-1)
walks of this type.
Case (c) shows a closed walk
(x,y_{1},y_{2},y_{1},x)

where
y_{1}
is adjacent to
x
but
y_{2}
is not adjacent to
x
.
There are
k
ways to take a vertex
y_{1}
,
and for each of them there are
k-1
ways to take
y_{2}
.
Thus, there are
k(k-1)
walks of this type.
Case (d) shows a closed walk that forms a quadrangle.
For each quadrangle, there are two closed walks
(x,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},x)
and
(x,y_{3},y_{2},y_{1},x)
.
Thus, we get
2q_{x}
walks of this type.
By the four cases, we have

(5.3)

(A^{4})_{x,x}=2k^{2}-k+2q_{x}.

Let the set of the quadrangles in
\Gamma
be
\mathcal{Q}
.
Count
|\{(x,Q)\in V(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{Q}\mid x\in Q\}|
in two ways, and we obtain

(5.4)

\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}q_{x}=4q.

Indeed,

|\{(x,Q)\in V(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{Q}\mid x\in Q\}|=\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}|\{Q\in\mathcal{Q}\mid x\in Q\}|=\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}q_{x},

and we have

|\{(x,Q)\in V(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{Q}\mid x\in Q\}|=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|\{x\in V(\Gamma)\mid x\in Q\}|=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}4=4q.

Thus,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{4}=\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}(A^{4})_{x,x}=\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}(2k^{2}-k+2q_{x})=n(2k^{2}-k)+8q.

Equality (
5.3
) and walk-regularity derive
c_{4}=(A^{4})_{x,x}=2k^{2}-k+2q_{x}
,
so
q_{x}
is also a constant.
Thus, Equality (
5.4
) implies
4q=\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}q_{x}=nq_{x}
,
that is,
q_{x}=\frac{4q}{n}
.
∎

6
Discussion and questions

In this paper,
we completely determined bipartite regular graphs with four distinct adjacency eigenvalues
that induce periodic Grover walks, and show that it is only
C_{6}
.
In addition, we showed that there are only three kinds of the second largest eigenvalues
of bipartite regular periodic graphs with five distinct eigenvalues.
Focusing on certain conditions obtained from walk-regularity and the number of quadrangles,
we have narrowed down periodic graphs.
However, there are still many in the tables whose existence is unknown.
Table
2
is directly related to the existence and classification of integral graphs,
which is a problem in spectral graph theory.
Indeed, the case
k=4
in Table
2
was completed by the results of Stevanović
[
33
]
.
Similar problems for the case
k\geq 6
remain to be solved:

Question 6.1
.

Fix an even number
k\geq 6
.
Classify the integral graphs whose
A
-spectra are the form
\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\frac{k}{2}]^{a},[0]^{b}\}
.

None of the graphs in Table
4
have been found so far.
The existence of such graphs should also be investigated:

Question 6.2
.

\{[\pm k]^{1},[\pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}k]^{a},[0]^{b}\}

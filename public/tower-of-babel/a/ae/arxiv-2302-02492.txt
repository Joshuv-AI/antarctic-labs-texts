Abstract
. Exceptional groups of type E 6 E_{6} contain dual pairs where one member is Spin ⁡ ( 8 ) \mathrm{Spin}(8) , and the other is T ⋊ ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ T\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} , where T T is a two-dimensional torus and the non-trivial element in ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} acts on T T by the inverse involution. Introduction

Let

The group

By functoriality of Galois cohomology we have a natural map

In this paper we shall exclusively be interested in the case when the type of

2.2.
Real groups
T_{E,K}

Assume

. Every real Freudenthal-Jordan algebra

of dimension 9 is isomorphic to
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
or
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
,
the set of fixed points of an involution of the second kind on
M_{3}(\mathbb{C})

Recall the quadratic etalé algebra

which arises from the map of Galois cohomology

H^{1}(F,{\rm PGL}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{1}(F,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=\{\text{etal\'{e} quadratic $F$-algebras}\}.

Over
\mathbb{R}
, there are two quadratic etalé algebras:
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{C}
. If

.
If

.
The group

[

]

Our next task is to describe the possible embeddings. In the real case there are 2 cubic etalé algebras:

E=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
E=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
. If

is isomorphic to

M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
then both

embed into
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
and these embeddings are unique up to conjugation. So assume that
J
is isomorphic to

H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, the set of fixed points of an involution of the second kind on
M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
.
The involution of the second kind on
M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
on arises from a non-degenerate hermitian-symmetric form

on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Let

h=\epsilon_{1}z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}+\epsilon_{2}z_{2}\bar{z}_{2}+\epsilon_{3}z_{3}\bar{z}_{3}
, where
\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1
.
We have 8 choices for signs, but we get 4 different involutions since

and
-h
give the same involution. In this way we get 4 Jordan algebras

together with
an embedding of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into
J
as diagonal matrices.
One of this Jordan algebras is definite, i.e.

is definite, while three other are indefinite and isomorphic to each other.
However the three embeddings are not isomorphic, and we get 4 classes of embeddings in all.
These represent all isomorphism classes of embeddings of
E=\mathbb{R}^{3}
into such Jordan algebras. Finally,
E=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
does not embed into the
definite Jordan algebra and there is a unique embedding of this

into an indefinite Jordan algebra. For each embedding it is easy to compute

T_{E,K}^{\circ}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
such that the conjugation action of the non-trivial element in

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on

is the inverse involution.
The possible cases of the two-dimensional torus
T^{\circ}_{E,K}
are tabulated in the following table, where
\mathbb{T}
the group of complex numbers of norm one.

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c}&E=\mathbb{R}^{3}&E=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}\\
\hline\cr K=\mathbb{R}^{2}&(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{3}/\Delta(\mathbb{R}^{\times})&(\mathbb{R}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times})/\Delta(\mathbb{R}^{\times})\\
\hline\cr K=\mathbb{C}&(\mathbb{T})^{3}/\Delta(\mathbb{T})&(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times})/\Delta(\mathbb{T})\end{array}

We introduce a refined notation for characters of these tori. A character
\chi
of

is a triple of
characters
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})
of
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
such that
\chi_{1}\cdot\chi_{2}\cdot\chi_{3}=1
. A character of
\mathbb{T}
is represented by an integer. Thus

\chi
of
(\mathbb{T})^{3}/\Delta(\mathbb{T})
is represented by a triple of integers

such that

. In the remaining two cases

\chi_{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\chi_{\mathbb{C}}=1

Main statement

3.1.
Representations of
T_{E,K}

Let
\chi
be a character of
T^{\circ}_{E,K}
. If
\chi\neq\chi^{-1}
, let
\rho(\chi)\cong\rho(\chi^{-1})
be the unique irreducible representation of

T_{E,K}
such that the restriction to

T^{\circ}_{E,K}
is
\chi\oplus\chi^{-1}
. If
\chi=\chi^{-1}
, then
\chi
extends to a character of
T_{E,K}
, in two ways, denoted by
\rho(\chi)^{\pm}
.
These two representations
are indistinguishable unless
\chi=1
. Then one extension is the trivial representation, denoted by
\rho(1)
, and the other the sign representation
\epsilon
.
Note that non-trivial quadratic characters
\chi
appear only in the split case.

3.2.
Some tempered representations of
M_{E}

To every unitary character
\chi
of
T_{E,K}
we shall attach
a packet
P(E,K,\chi)=P(E,K,\chi^{-1})
of tempered representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)^{\det}
,
obtained by restricting an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
.
We need additional notation. Let
F
be a local field, and
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
a pair of characters of
F^{\times}
.
Let
\mu_{1}\times\mu_{2}
be the unique infinite-dimensional sub-quotient of the principal series representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)

obtained by normalized parabolic induction from the pair of characters.
Let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{\times}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
be the sign character. It is the unique non-trivial quadratic character of
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.
Let
\nu:\mathbb{R}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\times}
be
\nu(x)=x
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.
Let

. Observe that
\nu^{n}\times\omega
, when restricted to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, is a sum of two (limits of)
discrete series representations with the lowest
{\rm{SO}}_{2}
-types
\pm(|n|+1)
.

Case
E=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
K=\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})
be a unitary character of
(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{3}/\Delta(\mathbb{R}^{\times})
.
The packet

consists of representations appearing in the restriction to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\det}
of

(\chi_{1}\times 1)\otimes(\chi_{2}\times 1)\otimes(\chi_{3}\times 1).

This representation is irreducible when restricted to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
unless
\chi_{i}=\omega
for at least one

. The group

{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\det}
is large enough so that the restriction is still irreducible if precisely one

is
\omega
. In view of the relation

\chi_{1}\cdot\chi_{2}\cdot\chi_{3}=1
, at most two

can be

\chi

and

.

Case

and

Let
\chi=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})

The packet

consists of representations appearing in the restriction to

(\nu^{n_{1}}\times\omega)\otimes(\nu^{n_{2}}\times\omega)\otimes(\nu^{n_{3}}\times\omega).

The restriction to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

consists of 4 representations.

Case
E=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
and

.

The restriction from
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})^{\det}

Let
\chi=(\chi_{\mathbb{R}},\chi_{\mathbb{C}})
be a unitary character of
(\mathbb{R}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times})/\Delta(\mathbb{R}^{\times})
.
The packet
P(E,K,\chi)
consists of the restriction to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})^{\det}
of

(\chi_{\mathbb{R}}\times 1)\otimes(\chi_{\mathbb{C}}\times 1).

Case
E=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
and
K=\mathbb{C}
.

We are again restricting from
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})^{\det}

Let
\chi=(m,\chi_{\mathbb{C}})
be a unitary character of

The packet

consists of the restriction to

3.3.
Main result

Let
V
be the Harish-Chandra module of the minimal representation of
H_{J}
. Consider the dual pair

G_{E}\times T_{E,K}
corresponding to an embedding
E\rightarrow J
. Let
K_{E}
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G_{E}
.
Let
\rho
be an irreducible representation of
T_{E,K}
.
Then there exists a
(\mathfrak{g}_{E},K_{E})
-module
\Theta(\rho)
, such that

\Theta(\rho)\otimes\rho\cong V/\cap_{\varphi\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(V,\rho)}\ker(\varphi)

where
\varphi
are homomorphisms in the sense of Harish-Chandra modules.
Observe that a priori
\Theta(\rho)
could be trivial or infinite length otherwise.
The following is the main result of this paper, that will be proved in the next two section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G_{E}\times T_{E,K}
be the dual pair arising from an embedding
E\rightarrow J
.
Let
\chi
be a unitary character of
T_{E,K}^{\circ}
.

•

If

is not one of the 4 embeddings
\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow H_{3}(\mathbb{C})

\Theta(\rho(\chi))\cong L(\pi(\chi),1)
, unless
\chi
is quadratic and non-trivial, and then

\Theta(\rho^{\pm}(\chi))\cong L(\pi^{\pm}(\chi),1)
.

•

If
E\rightarrow J
is one of the 4 embeddings
\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, then

\Theta(\rho(\chi))\cong L(\pi,1)
, where
\pi\in P(E,K,\chi)
. As we run through all 4 embeddings
\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
,

\pi
runs through the 4 representations in
P(E,K,\chi)
.

The representation
\Theta(\epsilon)
is always irreducible, and can be described as it sits in certain degenerate principal series representations, along with

\Theta(\rho(1))
.
Let
I_{E}(s)
denote the (normalized) degenerate principal series for
G_{E}
where we induce
|\det|^{s}
from
P_{E}
. Let
I^{\omega}_{E}(s)
be the quadratic twist
of this series, i.e. we induce

. (Recall that

is the sign character of

.)
The following result is due to Avner Segal
[

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Theta_{E\rightarrow J}(\rho)
denote the theta lift of

•

For every

0\rightarrow\oplus\Theta_{E\rightarrow H_{3}(\mathbb{C})}(\epsilon)\rightarrow I_{E}(1/2)\rightarrow\Theta_{E\rightarrow M_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\rho(1))\rightarrow 0.

•

For every
E
, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Theta_{E\rightarrow M_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\epsilon)\rightarrow I^{\omega}_{E}(1/2)\rightarrow\oplus\Theta_{E\rightarrow H_{3}(\mathbb{C})}(\rho(1))\rightarrow 0.

Here

is any Jordan algebra arising from an involution of the second kind on

. The sum, in both sequences, is over the isomorphism

6.2.
Split case

Assume that

.
In this case
T^{\circ}_{E,K}\cong\mathbb{T}^{3}/\Delta\mathbb{T}
, and a character
\chi
is a given triple of integers
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})

such that
\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}=0
. This triple is the infinitesimal character of
\chi
.
Thus the lift
\Theta(\chi)
has the infinitesimal character

\beta+\frac{\nu_{1}}{2}\alpha_{1}+\frac{\nu_{2}}{2}\alpha_{2}+\frac{\nu_{3}}{2}\alpha_{3}.

The group
G=G_{E}
is split.
Let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Recall that
K\cong{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}^{4}/\mu_{2}
.
We have 4 embeddings
\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, which give rise to
4 non-conjugated embeddings of
G
into
H_{J}
. On the level of maximal compact groups the 4 different embeddings are easily distinguished. We have
4 embeddings

each obtained by picking one
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
-factor of

and mapping it to the unique
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
factor of

. The other three
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
are
mapped into
{\mathrm{SU}}_{6}
, uniquely up to conjugation in
{\mathrm{SU}}_{6}
. So we fix one embedding of

into
K_{J}
and assume, without loss of generality, that the first
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
factor
of
K
maps into the
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
-factor of
K_{J}
. We note that
V
is
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
-admissible, for this

. Hence the restriction of

to
G
is also admissible, and this
dual pair correspondence was determined in
[
Lo97
]
.
Each
\Theta(\rho)
is an irreducible quaternionic representation, which can be described as a derived functor module.
Here we give a reinterpretation of that result in terms of Langlands parameters.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume

6.3.
Quasi-split case

Assume that
E=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
,
In this case
T^{\circ}_{E,K}\cong(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times})/\Delta(\mathbb{T})
and a character

\chi
is a pair
(m,\chi_{\mathbb{C}})
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that the restriction of
\chi_{\mathbb{C}}=1
on
\mathbb{T}
is
given by
z\mapsto z^{-m}
. In this case the differential of
\chi_{\mathbb{C}}
is a pair of complex numbers

(\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
such that
\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}=-m
. Then
(m,\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
,
is the infinitesimal character of
\chi
and the lift
\Theta(\chi)
has the infinitesimal character

\beta+\frac{m}{2}\alpha_{1}+\frac{\nu_{2}}{2}\alpha_{2}+\frac{\nu_{3}}{2}\alpha_{3}.

The character
\chi
is unitary if and only if
\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}
is purely imaginary.

In this case
G=G_{E}
is quasi-split.
Let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Recall that
K\cong{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times_{\mu_{2}}{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)
. The embedding of
K
into
K_{J}
is given by a sequence

{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\subseteq{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\subseteq{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SU}}(6)

where the first inclusion is identity on
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)
and the diagonal embedding of
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
into two
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
. The second embedding is identity on the first
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}

and the unique embedding of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\subseteq{\mathrm{SU}}_{6}
. The maximal compact subgroup of
T^{\circ}_{E,K}
commutes with
K
, so its Lie algebra is
spanned by

h=\frac{1}{3}(1,1,1,1,-2,-2),

thought of as a diagonal matrix of
\mathfrak{sl}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
, the Lie algebra of
{\mathrm{SU}}_{6}/\mu_{3}
. This choice of the generator of the Lie algebra fixes an isomorphism
of the maximal compact subgroup of
T^{\circ}_{E,K}
with
{\mathrm{U}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathrm{SU}}_{6}/\mu_{3}
. The maximal compact subgroup of
T_{E,K}
is generated by

U(1)
and
\delta
.

We now describe the representations of
G
appearing in the correspondence. To that end,
let
P=MAN
be the maximal parabolic subgroup of
G
such that the connected component of
M^{\circ}
of
M
is
isomorphic to
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R})
. The maximal compact subgroup of
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R})
is isomorphic to

({\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2})\times_{\mu_{2}}{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
, and we fix such an isomorphism.
Assume
m\geq 0
. Let
\sigma_{m}
be the lowest weight
\mathfrak{su}_{2,2}
-module with the minimal type

V_{0}\otimes V_{0+m}[m+2]
, and the lowest
{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
-weight
m+2
as the notation indicates.
We have the following:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\chi
be a unitary character of
T^{\circ}_{E,K}
such that the restriction to
{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
is
\chi_{m}
.
Let
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\circ}}^{G}(\sigma_{m}\otimes e^{\lambda})
be the unitarizable principal series representation obtained by inducing
\sigma_{m}\otimes e^{\lambda}
where
connected component
P^{\circ}
of
P
, and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

•

If
\chi\neq 1
, then
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{m}\otimes e^{\lambda})\cong L(\pi(\chi),1)
, for
\lambda
depending on
\chi
.

•

If
\chi=1
, then
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{0})\cong L(\rho(1),1)\oplus\Sigma
where
\Sigma
is irreducible with the minimal
K
-type
V_{(0,0)}\otimes V_{4}
.

Proposition
6.2
gives us a control of the
K
-types of
L(\pi(\chi),1)
, since we can compute the
K
-types of principal series representations:

Proposition 6.3
.

If
V_{(x,y)}\otimes V_{z}
is a
K
type in
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\circ}}^{G}(\sigma_{m}\otimes e^{\lambda})
then
z\equiv x-y\equiv m\pmod{2}
and

z>x-y\geq m
. If that is the case, the multiplicity of the type is equal to the number of integers
t
such that

x+y-m\geq 2t\geq x-y-m\text{ and }z\geq 2t+2+m.

Proof.

The
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
types of
\sigma_{m}
are

V_{0}\otimes V_{0+m}[m+2]+V_{1}\otimes V_{1+m}[m+4]+\ldots

Counting multiplicities of these types in
V_{(x,y)}\otimes V_{z}
gives the multiplicity of

V_{(x,y)}\otimes V_{z}
in
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\circ}}^{G}(\sigma_{m}\otimes e^{\lambda})
.

We now count
t
such that
V_{t}\otimes V_{t+m}[2t+2+m]
is contained in

V_{(x,y)}\otimes V_{z}
. Clearly
2t+2+m\leq z
is a necessary condition, along with
z\equiv m\pmod{2}
.
Next, restrict
V_{(x,y)}
to
{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
. Then
V_{t}\otimes V_{t+m}
is contained in
V_{(x,y)}
if and only if the inequalities of Lemma
5.2

are satisfied. But these are exactly the same as claimed.
∎

We now need the branching rule for the restriction of

V_{n\omega_{3}}
from
{\mathrm{SU}}_{6}/\mu_{3}
to
({\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2})\times{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
where, recall,
{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
is the maximal compact subgroup of
T^{\circ}_{E,K}
.
Let
V_{n\omega_{3}}[m]
be the subspace such that
{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
acts as the character
\chi_{m}
.
The following is a consequence of the Gelfand-Zetlin branching, applied twice to obtain a branching from
{\mathrm{SU}}_{6}
to
{\mathrm{SU}}_{4}\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
,
combined with the restriction from
{\mathrm{SU}}_{4}
to
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)
, see
[
HTW
]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume
m\geq 0
.
The restriction of
V_{n\omega_{3}}[m]
to
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
is

V_{n\omega_{3}}[m]=\sum_{t\geq 0}(\sum V_{(x,y)})\otimes V_{n-m-2t}

where the inner sum is over all
V_{(x,y)}
such that
x+y\equiv m\pmod{2}
and

2n-2t-2m\geq x+y-m\geq 2t\geq x-y-m\geq 0.

Let
\chi
be a character of
T_{E,J}^{\circ}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and let
\chi_{m}
be the restriction of
\chi
to
{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
. Let
V[m]
be the summand of the minimal
representation on which
{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
acts by
\chi_{m}
. Let
\Theta(\chi)
be the full lift of
\chi
to
G
.
Note that
\Theta(\chi)
is a quotient of
V[m]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Assume
m\geq 0
. The multiplicity of
V_{(x,y)}\otimes V_{z}
in
V_{n\omega_{3}}[m]\otimes V_{n+2}
is 0 unless
z>x-y\geq m
and
z\equiv x-y\equiv m\pmod{2}
. In that case
the multiplicity is increasing as
n
increases and stabilizes at the number of
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

x+y-m\geq 2t\geq x-y-m\geq 0\text{ and }z\geq m+2t+2.

Proof.

By the previous lemma the multiplicity is equal to the number of
t
such that

2n-2t-2m\geq x+y-m\geq 2t\geq x-y-m\geq 0

and
V_{z}
is a summand in the tensor product

V_{n+2}\otimes V_{n-m-2t}=V_{2n-m-2t+2}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{m+2t+2}.

Thus
t
has to satisfy the additional inequalities

2n-m-2t+2\geq z\geq m+2t+2.

The proposition follows by combining the two sets of inequalities and taking
n
large enough.
∎

In view of Proposition
6.5
, we can apply Lemma
5.15
to the
U(\mathfrak{a})
-module

X=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K}(V_{(x,y)}\otimes V_{z},V_{n\omega_{3}}[m]\otimes V_{n+2})

giving an upper bound on the types of
\Theta(\chi)
. That bound is the same as
the multiplicities of types in
V_{(x,y)}\otimes V_{z}
in
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\circ}}^{G}(\sigma_{m}\otimes e^{\lambda})
,
given by Proposition
6.3
, thus we have the following:

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\chi
be a character
T_{E,K}^{\circ}
whose restriction to the maximal compact subgroup is
\chi_{m}
. Then the
K
-types and
their multiplicities in
\Theta(\chi)
are less than or equal to those of the degenerate principal series
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\circ}}^{G}(\sigma_{m}\otimes e^{\lambda})
. The equality holds for types of multiplicity one.

Theorem 6.7
.

Assume
E=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
and
J=H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, so
T^{\circ}_{E,K}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Let
\chi
be a unitary character of
T^{\circ}_{E,K}
.

(1)

If
\chi\neq 1
then
\Theta(\rho(\chi))\cong L(\pi(\chi),1)
.

(2)

\Theta(\rho(1))\cong L(\pi(1),1)
and
\Theta(\epsilon)\cong\Sigma
the unique irreducible with the minimal
K
-type

V_{(0,0)}\otimes V_{4}
and the infinitesimal character
(1,1,0,0)
.

Proof.

If
\chi\neq 1
then
\Theta(\chi)\cong\Theta(\chi^{-1})\cong\Theta(\rho(\chi))
. Since
\Theta(\rho(\chi))
contains
L(\pi(\chi),1)
,
by the remark following Theorem
3.2
,
(1) follows at once from Corollary
6.6
. For (2) we need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.8
.

The type
V_{(0,0)}\otimes V_{2n}
,
n>0
, appears in
\Theta(\rho(1))
for
n
odd, and
in
\Theta(\epsilon)
for
n
even, with multiplicity one.

Proof.

The
K
types trivial on
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)
, i.e.
x=y=0
, appear in
V[m]
only for
m=0
. Hence, we can restrict
V
, not just
V[0]
, to compute the types of

\Theta(\rho(1))
and
\Theta(\epsilon)
. So we take the
n
-type
V_{n+2}\otimes V_{n\omega_{3}}
and restrict it to
K={\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}
.
We shall use that the restriction of
V_{n\omega_{3}}
to
{\mathrm{Sp}}(3)
is the sum of
V_{(n,m,m)}
and that

\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
acts on
V_{(n,m,m)}
by
(-1)^{n-m}
. By the branching from
{\mathrm{Sp}}(3)
to
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\times{\mathrm{Sp}}(1)
, the type trivial
on

appears only for

and with

on the
{\mathrm{Sp}}(1)

Note that
\delta
acts on it as

. Using the Clebsch-Gordan formula, we see that the

appears the first time in the

-type of the minimal representation, and
\delta
acts on it by

. Whether the type appears in
\Theta(\rho(1))
or

depends precisely on this sign, see the proof of Corollary
5.10
. The lemma is proved.
∎

The above Lemma implies that
\Theta(\epsilon)\neq 0
and contains the type
V_{(0,0)}\otimes V_{4}
. We also know that

\Theta(\rho(1))\supseteq L(\pi(1),1)
and we have a bound on all types of the sum

\Theta(1)=\Theta(\rho(1))\oplus\Theta(\epsilon)
. Hence the minimal type of
\Theta(\epsilon)
is
V_{(0,0)}\otimes V_{4}
and therefore contains
\Sigma
.
Both lifts are irreducible since there is no room for anything else.
∎

atlas
simple roots are given by the columns of the Cartan matrix for Lie type

. In these
coordinates, the weight
\gamma=(1,1,0,0)

(0,1,0,0)

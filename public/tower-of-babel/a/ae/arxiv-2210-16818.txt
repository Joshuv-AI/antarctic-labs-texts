Optimal control problems governed by time-fractional diffusion equations with control constraint
B.T.Kien, B.N.Muoi, C.F.Wen, J.C.Yao

Abstract




Abstract.
A class of optimal control problems governed by linear fractional diffusion equation with control constraint is considered. We first establish some results on the existence of strong solution to the state equation and the existence of optimal solutions for the optimal control problem. Then we derive first-and second-order optimality conditions for locally optimal solutions to the problem.

Key words.
Optimal control, Fractional diffusion equation, Solution existence, First-and second-order optimality
conditions.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be an open and bounded set in
\mathbb{R^{N}}
with
N=2,3
and boundary
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
,
Q=[0,T]\times\Omega
and
\Sigma=[0,T]\times\partial\Omega
, where
T>0
. Let
D:=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. We consider the problem of finding a function
u\in L^{\infty}(Q)
and the corresponding state
y\in L^{p}([0,T],D)
with
1<p<\infty
, which solve

\displaystyle\psi(y,u)=\int_{Q}L(t,x,y(t,x),u(t,x))dtdx\to\min

(1)

s.t.

\displaystyle\frac{d^{\alpha}y}{dt^{\alpha}}=\Delta y+u\quad{\rm in}\quad Q

(2)

\displaystyle y=0\quad\text{on}\quad\Sigma

(3)

\displaystyle y(0)=y_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega

(4)

\displaystyle a\leq u(t,x)\leq b\quad\text{a.e.}\quad(t,x)\in Q,

(5)

where
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}
is a fractional derivative operator in the sense of Caputo, which is given in Section 2,
L:Q\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Caratheodory function, and
y_{0}\in D
.

The system (
2
)-(
4
) is a time-fractional diffusion equation. Note that several physical systems are not truly modelled with differential equations of integer-order. So the fractional-order differential equations have been introduced in order to describe accurately these systems. A motivation for studying fractional diffusion equations comes from the fact that they describe efficiently anomalous diffusion on fractals such as physical objects of fractional dimension, like some amorphous semiconductors or strongly porous materials (see
[
2
]
,
[
22
]
and references therein).

In contrast with optimal control problems governed by differential equations with integer order (see
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
32
]
), the class of optimal control problems governed by fractional differential equations have not been studied systematically yet. Recently, there have been some mathematicians studying optimal control problems governed by fractional differential equations. For the papers which have close connections to the present work, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
. Among them, O.P. Agrawal
[
1
]
, considered optimal control problems governed by fractional differential equations (FDEs) without constraints. She gave first-order necessary conditions and some numerical examples for the problem. Kien et al.
[
17
]
studied problems governed by FDEs with control constraint. They gave first-and second-order optimality conditions for locally optimal solutions to the problem. G.M. Mophou et al.
[
12
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
considered strongly convex optimal control problems governed by a time-fractional diffusion equations with Riemann–Liouville derivative and the cost functions of the type

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|y(u)-y_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)},\gamma>0

for cases: control constraints, no constraint and state constraints, respectively. Then they established results on the existence of optimal solutions and first-order optimality conditions. Note that, this class of problems are convex so the second-order optimality conditions are fulfilled automatically. Recently Bahaa
[
4
]
has extended the results of
[
24
]
by considering the problem with control constraint. However, all of them mainly investigated first-order optimality conditions for the problem.

In this paper we consider problem (
1
)-(
5
) governed by time-fractional diffusion equation with Caputo derivative and with control constraint (
5
), where the cost function may not be convex. We shall prove the existence of solutions to the state equation and the existence of optimal solutions to (
1
)-(
5
). We then derive first- and second-order optimality conditions. We show that when
p=2
and
1>\alpha>1/2
, a theory of no-gap second-order optimality conditions is obtained. Optimality conditions are important for numerical methods of computing optimal solutions. Based on second-order sufficient conditions, we can give error estimate for approximate solutions. Nevertheless, to our best knowledge, so far there have been no result on the second-order optimality conditions for this class of problems.

The study of optimal control problems governed by fractional diffusion equations are quite complicated. One of the difficulties is that the regularity of the solution to the state equation is poor. It is known that, if the control function is Hölder continuous, then the state function is also Höder continuous. Unfortunately, in optimal control problems, the control variables usually belong to
L^{\infty}
-spaces. So the solutions to the state equation only belong to
L^{p}
-spaces. Therefore, the cost function is difficult to be differentiable with respect to the state variable. Another difficulty for the problem is about compactness of bounded sequences in function spaces with Banach space values. To overcome these difficulties, we shall use a result from
[
15
]
in order to study regularity of solutions to the state equation. We also use a recent result on the compact embedding in
[
19
]
which is a version of the so-call Aubin’s Lemma for evolution equations to show that if a sequence is weakly convergent then it is strongly convergent. Besides, techniques of optimal control from
[
7
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
, nonlinear and variational analysis are also used to deal with the problem.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present some auxiliary results on the fractional derivatives for functions with values in Banach space. Section 3 is devoted to results on the existence and regularity of solutions to the state equation and the existence of optimal solutions to the problem. Section 4 is destined for establishing first-and second-order optimality conditions.

Some auxiliary results on fractional derivatives

Let
X
be a Banach space. We denote by
L^{p}([0,T],X)
the space of X-valued functions
u:[0,T]\to X
such that

\|u\|_{p}:=(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{X}^{p}dt)^{1/p}<+\infty
, where

. Also we denote by
C([0,T],X)

\|y\|_{1,p}=\|y\|_{0}+\|\dot{y}\|_{p},

where
\|y\|_{0}=\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|y(t)\|_{X}

in
C([0,T],X)
.

the left Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator of
order

\theta
. The operator

is called
the right Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator of
order

\theta
. For the case
\theta=0
, we set
I_{0+}^{0}=I_{T-}^{0}=I
, the identity operator. Here the integrals are taken in Bochner’s sense. By the same argument as in the proof of
[
11

, we can show that

\displaystyle(I_{0+}^{\theta_{1}}(I_{0+}^{\theta_{2}}\phi)(t)=(I_{0+}^{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}\phi)(t)\quad\forall\phi\in L^{1}([0,T],X).

(6)

Let us denote by
I_{0+}^{\theta}(L^{p}([0,T],X))
the space of summable functions
f:[0,T]\to X
such that
f=I_{0+}^{\theta}\phi
for some
\phi\in L^{p}([0,T],X)
. The space

Proposition 2.1

f\in I_{0+}^{\theta}(L^{p}([0,T],X))
if and only if
I_{0+}^{1-\theta}f\in W^{1,p}([0,T],X)
.

Definition 2.1

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\phi\in C([0,T],X)
. Then each of the expressions

(D_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi)(t):=\frac{d}{dt}I_{0}^{1-\alpha}\phi(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\phi(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}d\tau

(D_{T-}^{\alpha}\phi)(t):=-\frac{d}{dt}I_{T-}^{1-\alpha}\phi=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\phi(\tau)}{(\tau-t)^{\alpha}}d\tau

is called a left Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative and a right Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative, respectively. The operator
D_{0+}^{\alpha}
and
D_{T-}^{\alpha}
is called the left Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative operator and the right Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative operator, respectively.

A sufficient condition for the existence of
D_{0+}^{\alpha}(\phi)
and
D_{T-}^{\alpha}(\phi)
is to require
\phi\in W^{1,1}([0,T],X)
(see
[

).

Proposition 2.2

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then the following assertions are fulfilled.

(i)
If
f\in L^{1}([0,T],X)
, then

\displaystyle D_{0+}^{\alpha}I_{0+}^{\alpha}f=f\quad{\rm a.e.}

(7)

(ii)
If
f\in I_{0+}^{\alpha}(L^{1})
, then

\displaystyle I_{0+}^{\alpha}D_{0+}^{\alpha}f=f\quad{\rm a.e.}

(8)

Proof
.
(i)
. Using (
6
) and the definition of
D_{0+}^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle D_{0+}^{\alpha}I_{0+}^{\alpha}f=D^{1}I_{0+}^{1-\alpha}I_{0+}^{\alpha}f=D^{1}I_{0+}^{1}f=f.

(ii)
. Let
f=I_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi
for some
\phi\in L^{1}([0,T],X)
. Then from (

\displaystyle I_{0+}^{\alpha}D_{0+}^{\alpha}f=I_{0+}^{\alpha}D_{0+}^{\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{0+}\phi=I_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi=f.

\Box

Definition 2.2

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\phi\in L^{p}([0,T],X)
with
p\geq 1
. The operator
\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}
is defined by

\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}\phi(t):=D_{0+}^{\alpha}(\phi-\phi(0))=D_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi-\frac{\phi(0)}{(t-0)^{\alpha}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}

is called the left Caputo differential operator of order
\alpha
, and the operator
\frac{\hat{d}^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}
defined by

\frac{\hat{d}^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}\phi(t):=D_{T-}^{\alpha}(\phi-\phi(T))=D_{T-}^{\alpha}\phi-\frac{\phi(T)}{(T-t)^{\alpha}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}

is called the right Caputo differential operator of order
\alpha
provided all terms exist.

From the definition, we see that if
\phi(0)=0
, then
\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}\phi=D_{0+}^{\alpha}(\phi)
. Also, if
\phi(T)=0
, then
\frac{\hat{d}^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}\phi=D_{T-}^{\alpha}(\phi)
.

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that
X=H
is a Hilbert space,
f\in I_{T-}^{\alpha}(L^{q}([0,T],H))
and
g\in I_{0+}^{\alpha}(L^{p}([0,T],H)
with
p,q\geq 1
and
1/p+1/q\leq 1+\alpha
. Then one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(f,D_{0+}^{\alpha}g)_{H}dt=\int_{0}^{T}(g,D_{T-}^{\alpha}f)_{H}dt

(9)

Particularly, when
f(T)=0
and
g(0)=0
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(f,\frac{d^{\alpha}g}{dt^{\alpha}})_{H}dt=\int_{0}^{T}(g,\frac{\hat{d}^{\alpha}f}{dt^{\alpha}})_{H}dt.

(10)

Here
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
denotes the scalar product in
H
.

Proof
. We first claim that if
\phi_{1}\in L^{p}([0,T],H)
and
\phi_{2}\in L^{q}([0,T],H)
with
1/p+1/q\leq 1+\alpha
, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{2}(t),I_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi_{1}(t))_{H}dt=\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{1}(t),I_{T-}^{\alpha}\phi_{2}(t))_{H}dt.

(11)

In fact, from the assumption we see that the integrals in both sides are defined. By Fubini’s Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{2}(t),I_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi_{1}(t))_{H}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{2}(t),\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}\phi_{1}(s)ds)_{H}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}(\phi_{2}(t),\frac{1}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}\phi_{1}(s)_{H}dsdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{s}^{T}(\phi_{2}(t),\frac{1}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}\phi_{1}(s))_{H}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{1}(s),I_{T-}^{\alpha}\phi_{2}(s))_{H}ds.

The claim is justified. We now prove (
9
). By the assumption, we have

f=I_{T-}^{\alpha}\phi_{2}
and
g=I_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi_{1}
for some

\phi_{2}\in L^{q}([0,T],H)
and
\phi_{1}\in L^{p}([0,T],H)
. Then
\phi_{2}=D_{T-}^{\alpha}(f)
. Using (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(f,D_{0+}^{\alpha}g)_{H}dt=\int_{0}^{T}(I_{T-}^{\alpha}\phi_{2}(t),\phi_{1}(t))_{H}dt=\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{2}(t),I_{0+}^{\alpha}\phi_{1})_{H}dt=\int_{0}^{T}(D_{T-}^{\alpha}f(t),g(t))_{H}dt.

We obtain formula (
9
).

\Box

Proposition 2.3

Suppose that

and
\alpha\in(0,1)

I_{0+}^{\alpha}f\in C^{\alpha}([0,T],X)

\displaystyle\|I_{0+}^{\alpha}f\|_{C([0,T],X)}\leq\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}[\frac{f(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{f(\tau)}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}]d\tau+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{f(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau.

Hence

\displaystyle\|(I_{0+}^{\alpha}f)(t)-(I_{0+}^{\alpha}f)(t_{0})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\big|\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}\big|d\tau+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}[\frac{1}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}]d\tau+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau

. Then we write

\displaystyle(I_{0+}^{\alpha}f)(t_{0})-(I_{0+}^{\alpha}f)(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(\tau)}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\frac{f(\tau)}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}[\frac{f(\tau)}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{f(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}]d\tau+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\frac{f(\tau)}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|(I_{0+}^{\alpha}f)(t_{0})-(I_{0+}^{\alpha}f)(t)\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{0}^{t}\big|\frac{1}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}\big|d\tau+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\frac{1}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{0}^{t}\big[\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}\big]d\tau+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\frac{1}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\frac{1}{\alpha}[t^{\alpha}-t_{0}^{\alpha}+(t_{0}-t)^{\alpha}]+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],X)}\frac{1}{\alpha}(t_{0}-t)^{\alpha}

3
Solution existence

In this section, we investigate the existence of solutions to the state equation (
2
)-(
4
) and the existence of optimal solution to problem (1)-(5).

In the sequel, we will assume that
J=[0,T]
,
D=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
V=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H=L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then we have

D\hookrightarrow V\hookrightarrow H.

Besides, since
n=2,3
, the embedded theorem implies that
D\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
is compact. Given functions
f
and
g
on
J
, the convolution of
f
and
g
is denoted by
f\ast g
which is defined by

(f\ast g)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(t-s)g(s)ds.

We say
y
is a
strong solution
to (

)-(

) if
y\in C(J,H)
and
\Delta y+u\in L^{p}(J,H)
with

such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{d^{\alpha}y}{dt^{\alpha}}=\Delta y+u\quad{\rm a.e.}\quad t\in J,\\
y(0)=y_{0}.\end{cases}

(16)

Here
y_{0}\in D
and
u\in L^{p}(J,H)
are given.

Let us assume that
y\in C(J,H)
is a solution to (
16
). Then
\Delta y+u\in L^{p}(J,H)
with
p\geq 1
. By Proposition
2.4
,

satisfies the integral equation

\displaystyle y(t)=y_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Delta y(s)}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}ds+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u(s)}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}ds

(17)

or equivalently

\displaystyle y(t)=y_{0}+k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y+u),

(18)

where

\displaystyle k_{1-\alpha}(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)t^{1-\alpha}}.

(19)

Each solution of (
18
) is called a

to (
2
)-(
4
). It is clear that any strong solution is a mild solution. We now establish formula representing solution of (

).

It is know that
\Delta:D\subset H\to H
is a sectorial operator. Namely, the resolvent set
\rho(\Delta)
contains the sector

S_{\theta}=\{\lambda\in C:\lambda\neq 0,|{\rm arg}\lambda|<\theta\},\pi/2<\theta<\pi,

and

\|R(\lambda,\Delta)\|_{L(L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{M}{\lambda}\quad\forall\lambda\in S_{\theta}.

Hence
\Delta
is a generator of a uniformly bounded analytic semigroup
\{S(t),t\geq 0\}
(see Theorem 2.4.1 and Proposition 2.4.2 in
[
21
]
,
[
20
, Theorem 3.1.2]
and
[
26
, Theorem 2.7, p. 211]
). Besides, the following formula for the resolvent operator is valid:

\displaystyle R(\lambda,A)=(\lambda I-A)^{-1}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}S(t)dt\quad\forall t>0.

(20)

In other words,
R(\lambda,\Delta)
is the Laplace transform of
S(t)
.

Let us denote by
z
the Laplace transform of

\displaystyle e(t,x)\begin{cases}\geq 0\quad&{\rm a.e.}\quad(t,x)\in Q_{a},\\
\leq 0\quad&{\rm a.e.}\quad(t,x)\in Q_{b},\\
0\quad&{\rm a.e.}\quad(t,x)\in Q_{ab}.\end{cases}

Proof
. It is clear that
(\overline{y},\overline{u})
is a locally optimal solution of (
1
)-(
5
) if and only if
\overline{u}
is a locally optimality solution of problem (
41
). By definition, there exists an open ball

so that

f(u)\geq f(\overline{u})\quad\forall u\in B(\overline{u},\epsilon)\cap K_{\infty}.

Fix any
v\in K_{\infty}
. Then for
\lambda>0
small enough, we have

v_{\lambda}=\overline{u}+\lambda(v-\overline{u})\in B(\overline{u},\epsilon)\cap K_{\infty}
. Hence

f(\overline{u}+\lambda(v-\overline{u}))\geq f(\overline{u}).

This implies that

\frac{f(\overline{u}+\lambda(v-\overline{u}))-f(\overline{u})}{\lambda}\geq 0.

By letting
\lambda\to 0+
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle f^{\prime}(\overline{u}),v-\overline{u}\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall v\in K_{\infty}.

(53)

By Proposition
3.2
, equation (
52
) has a unique solution
\varphi\in C(J,H)\cap L^{q}(J,D)
. Hence
(i)
is valid. From Proposition
4.3
and (
53
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(L_{u}[t,x]-\varphi(t,x))(v(t,x)-\overline{u}(t,x))dtdx\geq 0\quad\forall v\in K_{\infty}.

(54)

By putting
L_{u}[t,x]-\varphi(t,x)=e(t,x)
, we see that
e\in L^{q}(J,L^{\infty}(\Omega))
which satisfies
(ii)
and (
54
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{Q}e(t,x)(v(t,x)-\overline{u}(t,x))dtdx\geq 0\quad\forall v\in K_{\infty}.

(55)

Using variational arguments as in the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
, we can show that
e
satisfies
(iii)
. The theorem is proved.

To deal with second-order optimality conditions, we need the so-called critical cone as follows.

Definition 4.1

A couple
(y,v)\in L^{p}(J,D)\times L^{p}(Q)
is said to be a critical direction to problem (
1
)-(
5
) at
(\overline{y},\overline{u})
if the following conditions are fulfilled:

(c_{1})

\int_{Q}(L_{y}[t,x]y(t,x)+L_{u}[t,x]v(t,x))dtdx\leq 0
;

(c_{2})

y=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y+v)
;

(c_{3})

v(t,x)\geq 0
for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{a}
and
v(t,x)\leq 0
for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{b}
.

The set of critical directions at
(\overline{y},\overline{u})
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{p}[(\overline{y},\overline{u})]
which is a closed convex cone containing
(0,0)
.

We now take a couple
(y,v)\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{y},\overline{u})
such that
v\in L^{\infty}(Q)
. Then
(c_{2})
is equivalent to saying that
y=z_{v}=G^{\prime}(\overline{u})v
. Moreover, if

is a locally optimal solution, then

Indeed, it is clear that
e(t,x)v(t,x)\geq 0
for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q
. Since
y=z_{v}
we have from Proposition
4.3
and (
50
) that

\displaystyle 0\geq\int_{Q}(L_{y}[t,x]y(t,x)+L_{u}[t,x]v(t,x))dtdx=\int_{Q}(L_{u}[t,x]-\varphi(t,x))v(t,x)dtdx=\int_{Q}e(t,x)v(t,x)dt\geq 0.

(56)

Let
\mathcal{L}(y,u,\varphi,e)
be the Lagrange function associated with the problem, which is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(y,u,\varphi,e)=\int_{Q}[L(t,x,y,u)+y(\frac{\hat{d}^{\alpha}\varphi}{dt^{\alpha}}-\Delta\varphi)-\varphi u-eu]dtdx.

(57)

Then conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
of Theorem
4.1
is equivalent to the condition

\displaystyle\nabla_{(y,u)}\mathcal{L}(\overline{y},\overline{u},\varphi,e)=0.

. We divide the proof into two steps.

{\it Step1}
. Proving (
59

(y,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{p}[(\overline{x},\overline{u})]

(y,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{p}[(\overline{x},\overline{u})]

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{f(\overline{u}+\lambda v_{\rho})-f(\overline{u})}{\lambda}=\lambda f^{\prime}(\overline{u})v_{\rho}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{u}+\theta\lambda v_{\rho})[v_{\rho},v_{\rho}],

It follows that

\displaystyle 0\leq f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{u}+\theta\lambda v_{\rho})[v_{\rho},v_{\rho}].

By letting

, we obtain
0\leq f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{u})[v_{\rho},v_{\rho}].
This and Proposition
4.3
imply

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{Q}[L_{yy}[t,x]y_{\rho}^{2}+2L_{yu}[t,x]y_{\rho}v_{\rho}+L_{uu}[t,x]v_{\rho}^{2}]dtdx

(60)

Now we have

\displaystyle y=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y+v)

\displaystyle y_{\rho}=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y_{n}+v_{\rho})

\displaystyle y-y_{\rho}=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta(y-y_{\rho})+(v-v_{\rho})).

(A4)

\displaystyle\big|\int_{Q}[L_{yy}[t,x]y_{\rho}^{2}-\int_{Q}L_{yy}[t,x]y^{2}dtdx\big|=\big|\int_{Q}L_{yy}[t,x](y_{\rho}-y)(y_{\rho}+y)dtdx\big|

\int_{Q}[2L_{yu}[t,x]y_{\rho}v_{\rho}+L_{uu}[t,x]v_{\rho}^{2}]dtdx\to\int_{Q}[2L_{yu}[t,x]yv+L_{uu}[t,x]v^{2}]dtdx.

. Proving (

) for any
(y,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{p}[(\overline{x},\overline{u})]

(y,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{p}[(\overline{x},\overline{u})]

in

|v_{n}(t,x)|=|P_{[-n,n]}(v(t,x))-P_{[-n,n]}(0)|\leq|v(t,x)|.

in
L^{p}(Q)
and in
L^{2}(Q)
. Let us put

(y_{n},v_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{p}[(\overline{x},\overline{u})]

, we note that if
\overline{u}(t,x)=a

. Hence

\overline{u}(t,x)=b

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(L_{y}[t,x]y_{n}(t,x)+L_{u}[t,x]v_{n}(t,x))dtdx=\int_{Q}e(t,x)v_{n}(t,x)dtdx

\displaystyle y=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y+v)

\displaystyle y_{n}=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y_{n}+v_{n}).

This implies that

For the remaining part of this section, we give second-order sufficient optimal conditions for locally optimal solution to (

)-(

Let

be a nonempty and closed subset in a Banach space

and
\overline{w}\in K

\displaystyle T^{\flat}(K;\overline{w})

\displaystyle:=\left\{h\in X\;|\;\forall t_{n}\to 0^{+},\exists h_{n}\to h,\overline{w}+t_{n}h_{n}\in K\ \ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

\displaystyle T(K,\overline{w}):

\displaystyle=\left\{h\in X\;|\;\exists t_{n}\to 0^{+},\exists h_{n}\to h,\overline{w}+t_{n}h_{n}\in K\ \ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

are called
the adjacent tangent cone
and
the contingent cone
to
K
at
\overline{w}
, respectively. It is well-known that when

is convex, then

T^{\flat}(K;\overline{w})=T(K;\overline{w})=\overline{\rm cone}(K-\overline{w}),

where

{\rm cone}(K-\overline{w}):=\{\lambda(h-\overline{w})\;|\;h\in\Omega,\lambda>0\}.

Let
K_{p}
be defined by (
37
). Then from
[
16
, Lemma 2.4]
, we have

\displaystyle T^{\flat}(K_{p},\overline{u})=\big\{v\in L^{p}:v(t,x)\ \text{satisfies}\ (c_{3})\big\}.

Hence condition
(c_{3})
in
\mathcal{C}_{p}[(\overline{y},\overline{u})]
is equivalent to saying that
v\in T^{\flat}(K_{p},\overline{u})
.

Below, we set
Y=L^{p}(J,D)
and
U=L^{\infty}(Q)
. Given
\epsilon>0
, the symbols
B(\overline{y},\epsilon)_{Y}
is a ball in
Y
with center at
\overline{y}\in Y
and radius
\epsilon>0
.

We now have

Theorem 4.3

Suppose
(A4)
,
p\geq 2
,
1>\alpha>1/2
and
(\varphi,e)
satisfies the conclusion of Theorem 4.1. Furthermore,
suppose that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\displaystyle L_{uu}(t,x,y,u)\geq\Lambda\quad\forall(t,x,y,u)\in Q\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

(63)

(ii)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{(y,u)}\mathcal{L}(\overline{y},\overline{u},\varphi,e)[(z,v),(z,v)]>0\quad\forall(z,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{2}[(\overline{y},\overline{u})]\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

(64)

Then there exist positive constants
\varrho
and
\epsilon

\displaystyle\psi(y,u)\geq\psi(\overline{y},\overline{u})+\varrho\|u-\overline{u}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\forall(y,u)\in B_{Y}(\overline{y},\epsilon)\times B_{U}(\overline{u},\epsilon).

. On the contrary, we suppose the conclusion were

\{(y_{n},u_{n})\}\subset\Phi
and

such that
y_{n}\to\overline{y}
in
Y
,

in

(65)

If
u_{n}=\overline{u}
, then we can show that
y_{n}=\overline{y}
. It follows that
\psi(\overline{y},\overline{u})<\psi(\overline{y},\overline{u})+0

u_{n}\neq\overline{u}
for all
n\geq 1

Define
t_{n}=\|u_{n}-\overline{u}\|_{2}

\displaystyle y_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=y_{0}+k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y_{n}+u_{n}),

\displaystyle\overline{y}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta\overline{y}+\overline{u}).

\displaystyle z_{n}=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta z_{n}+v_{n}),\quad z_{n}(0)=0.

3.2

\displaystyle\|z_{n}\|_{L^{2}(J,D)}+\|\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}z_{n}\|_{L^{2}(J,H)}\leq C\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(J,H)}=C\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}.

\displaystyle z=k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta z+v).

The remaining part of the proof will be divided into two steps.

Step 1
. Showing that

\displaystyle\psi_{y}(\overline{y},\overline{u})z+\psi_{u}(\overline{y},\overline{u})v=\int_{Q}(L_{y}[t,x]z(t,x)+L_{u}[t,x]v(t,x))dtdx\leq 0.

(69)

Hence condition

\mathcal{C}_{2}[(\overline{y},\overline{u})]

(c_{2})
is fulfilled. For
(c_{3})

u_{n}-\overline{u}\in K_{\infty}-\overline{u}
. This implies that

where
T^{\flat}(K_{\infty};\overline{u})

at

in

Hence condition
(c_{3})
of
\mathcal{C}_{2}[(\overline{y},\overline{u})]
is fulfilled.

Step 2.
Showing that
(z,v)=0
.

Note that
\langle e,u_{n}-\overline{u}\rangle\geq 0
and

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta y_{n}+u_{n})

\displaystyle\overline{y}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+k_{1-\alpha}\ast(\Delta\overline{y}+\overline{u}).

This implies that

Hence

\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}(y_{n}-\overline{y})-\Delta(y_{n}-\overline{y})-(u_{n}-\overline{u})=0.

This and Lemma

yield

\displaystyle\int_{Q}[(y_{n}-\overline{y})D_{*T-}^{\alpha}\varphi-\varphi(\Delta(y_{n}-\overline{y})-\varphi(u_{n}-\overline{u})]dtdx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\varphi(t)[\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}(y_{n}-\overline{y})-\Delta(y_{n}-\overline{y})-(u_{n}-\overline{u})]dtdx=0.

Combining these with (
66
) and definition of Lagrange function (
57
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(y_{n},u_{n},\varphi,e)-\mathcal{L}(\overline{y},\overline{u},\varphi,e)

\displaystyle=\psi(y_{n},u_{n})-\psi(\overline{y},\overline{u})-\langle e,u_{n}-\overline{u}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\psi(y_{n},u_{n})-\psi(\overline{y},\overline{u})\leq o(t_{n}^{2}).

From this, (
58
) and a second-order Taylor expansion, we get

\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{2}\nabla^{2}_{(y,u)}\mathcal{L}(\overline{y},\overline{u},\varphi,e)[(z_{n},v_{n}),(z_{n},v_{n})]+o(t_{n}^{2})\leq o(t_{n}^{2}),

or, equivalently,

\nabla^{2}_{(y,u)}\mathcal{L}(\overline{y},\overline{u},\varphi,e)[(z_{n},v_{n}),(z_{n},v_{n})]\leq\frac{o(t_{n}^{2})}{t_{n}^{2}}.

(70)

This means

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(L_{yy}[t,x]z_{n}^{2}+2L_{yu}[t,x]z_{n}v_{n}+L_{uu}[t,x]v_{n}^{2})dtdx\leq\frac{o(t_{n}^{2})}{t_{n}^{2}}.

(71)

Let us claim that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Q}(L_{yy}[t,x]z_{n}^{2}+2L_{yu}[t,x]z_{n}v_{n}+L_{uu}[t,x]v_{n}^{2})dtdx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{Q}(L_{yy}[t,x]z^{2}+2L_{yu}[t,x]zv+L_{uu}[t,x]v^{2})dtdx.

Indeed, since
L_{uu}[t,x]\geq\Lambda>0
, the functional

u\mapsto\int_{Q}L_{uu}[t,x]u^{2}dtdx
is convex and so it is sequentially lower semicontinous. Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Q}L_{uu}[t,x]v_{n}^{2}dtdx\geq\int_{Q}L_{uu}[t,x]v^{2}dtdx.

Since
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{2}(J,V)
,
\|z_{n}+z\|_{L^{2}(J,V)}\leq M
for some constant
M>0
. Using assumption
L_{yy}[\cdot,\cdot]\in L^{\infty}(Q)
in
(i)
, we have

\displaystyle|\int_{Q}L_{yy}[t,x](z_{n}^{2}-z^{2})dtdx|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|L_{yy}[t,x]||z_{n}(t,x)-z(t,x)||z_{n}(t,x)+z(t,x)|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|L_{yy}[t,\cdot]\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|z_{n}(t,\cdot)-z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z_{n}(t,\cdot)+z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{yy}[\cdot,\cdot]\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\|\|z_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)-z(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|z_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)+z(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{yy}[\cdot,\cdot]\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\|\|z_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)-z(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(Q)}M.

This implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Q}L_{yy}[t,x]z_{n}^{2}(t,x)dtdx=\int_{Q}L_{yy}[t,x]z^{2}(t,x)dtdx.

Similarly, we can show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Q}L_{yu}[t,x]z_{n}(t,x)v_{n}(t,x)dtdx=\int_{Q}L_{yu}[t,x]z(t,x)v(t,x)dtdx.

Hence the claim is justified.
By letting
n\to\infty
, we obtain from (
71
) that

\nabla^{2}_{(y,u)}\mathcal{L}(\overline{y},\overline{u},\varphi,e)[(z,v),(z,v)]\leq 0.

Combining this with (
64
), we conclude that
(z,v)=(0,0)
.

Finally, from (
63
), (
71
) and
\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(L_{yy}[t,x]z_{n}^{2}+2L_{yu}[t,x]z_{n}v_{n})dtdx+\Lambda

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q}(L_{yy}[t,x]z_{n}^{2}+2L_{yu}[t,x]z_{n}v_{n}+L_{uu}[t,x]v_{n}^{2})dtdx\leq\frac{o(t_{n}^{2})}{t_{n}^{2}}.

By letting
n\to\infty
and using
(z,v)=(0,0)
, we obtain
\Lambda\leq 0
which is absurd. Therefore, the theorem is proved.

\Box

Remark 4.1

When
0<\alpha\leq 1/2
, the embebded theorem is not applicable and the proof of Theorem
4.2
is collapsed. Therefore, establishing second-order sufficient conditions for this case is an open problem.

When
1/2<\alpha<1
and
p=2
, Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
give no-gap second-order conditions on the common critical cone
\mathcal{C}_{2}[(\overline{y},\overline{u})]
.

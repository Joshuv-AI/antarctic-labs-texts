The weak convergence order of two Euler-type discretization schemes for the log-Heston model
Annalena Mickel, Andreas Neuenkirch

Abstract
We study the weak convergence order of two Euler-type discretizations of the log-Heston Model where we use symmetrization and absorption, respectively, to prevent the discretization of the underlying CIR process from becoming negative. If the Feller index ν \nu of the CIR process satisfies ν > 1 \nu>1 , we establish weak convergence order one, while for ν ≤ 1 \nu\leq 1 , we obtain weak convergence order ν − ϵ \nu-\epsilon for ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 arbitrarily small. We illustrate our theoretical findings by several numerical examples. Key words: Heston model, CIR process, discretization schemes for SDEs, Kolmogorov PDE, Euler scheme, non-standard assumptions 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 60H35; 65C05; 65C30; 91G60

Introduction and Main Result

The Heston model
[
24
]
is given by the stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\displaystyle\begin{split}dS_{t}&=\mu S_{t}dt+\sqrt{V_{t}}S_{t}\Big(\rho dW_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{t}\Big),\qquad&S_{0}=s_{0},\\
dV_{t}&=\kappa(\theta-V_{t})dt+\sigma\sqrt{V_{t}}dW_{t},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,&V_{0}=v_{0},\end{split}

(1)

with
s_{0},v_{0},\kappa,\theta,\sigma>0
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\rho\in[-1,1]
,
T>0
and independent Brownian motions
W=(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
,
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
which are defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
where the filtration satisfies the usual conditions. Throughout this article the initial values
s_{0}
,
v_{0}
are assumed to be deterministic.
Here,
S
models the price of an asset and
V
its volatility which is given by the so called Cox–Ingersoll–Ross process (CIR).

The Heston model extends the celebrated Black-Scholes model by using a stochastic volatility instead of a constant one. Its numerical analysis is not only of theoretical relevance. With the recent rise of volatility trading in financial markets, stochastic volatility models as the Heston model are becoming more and more important and numerical calculations are essential for the practical use of these models.

To price options with maturity at time
T
and discounted payoff function
g:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, one is interested in the value of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[g(S_{T})\right],

which can be approximated through a (multi-level) Monte Carlo simulation.
Usually, the log-Heston model instead of the Heston model is considered in numerical practice. With
X_{t}=\log(S_{t})
this yields the SDE

\displaystyle\begin{split}dX_{t}&=\Big(\mu-\frac{1}{2}V_{t}\Big)dt+\sqrt{V_{t}}\Big(\rho dW_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{t}\Big),\\
dV_{t}&=\kappa(\theta-V_{t})dt+\sigma\sqrt{V_{t}}dW_{t},\end{split}

(2)

and the exponential is then incorporated in the payoff, i.e.
g
is replaced by

h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
h(x)=g(\exp(x))
.

Note that the state space of the log-Heston SDE (
2
) is
\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
and the square-root coefficients are not globally Lipschitz continuous. Additionally, even if
g
satisfies a linear growth condition,
g(S_{T})
might not belong to
L^{p}
if
p>1
. See e.g.
[
7
]
for this so-called moment explosion. Thus, the (log-)Heston SDE does not satisfy the standard assumptions for the numerical analysis of SDEs.

Due to its popularity and its difficulty various methods for the simulation of the (log-)Heston model have been proposed and studied:

(i)

exact simulations schemes which are based on the transition density of the CIR process and the characteristic function of the log-asset price process, see e.g.
[
14
,
37
,
20
,
31
,
21
,
30
]
,

(ii)

semi-exact discretization schemes which simulate the CIR process exactly, but discretize the log-asset price, see e.g.
[
6
,
39
,
32
]
,

(iii)

full discretization schemes which discretize both components of the SDE, see e.g.
[
25
,
26
,
6
,
35
,
34
,
36
,
5
]
,

(iv)

and miscellaneous methods as e.g. tree methods in
[
1
,
16
]
or the moment matching methods in
[
6
]
.

Euler-type discretization schemes are frequently used for the log-Heston model and seem to be efficient in many cases, but a weak error analysis, i.e. an analysis of the bias of these discretization schemes, is not available yet. Since the discretization of the CIR process might become negative, these schemes require fixes as absorption and symmetrization or a modification of the square-root coefficient for negative values which make these schemes applicable but their analysis even more challenging. For a survey of such fixes and modifications see e.g.
[
36
]
. Moreover, for the importance of the weak error in (multi-level) Monte Carlo simulations see
[
17
]
and
[
19
]
.

Here, we will use two of these schemes for the discretization of the CIR component. The Symmetrized Euler (SE) scheme given by

\displaystyle v^{sym}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\biggl|v^{sym}_{k}+\kappa\left(\theta-v^{sym}_{k}\right)(t_{k+1}-t_{k})+\sigma\sqrt{v^{sym}_{k}}\left(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}\right)\biggr|,

(3)

where
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{k}<...<t_{N}=T
and
v^{sym}_{0}=v_{0}
, has been proposed and studied by Bossy and Diop in
[
12
]
. They state the weak convergence order one (for sufficiently regular test functions) under the assumption
\frac{\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}}>1
for this scheme.
Instead of reflecting negative values, the Euler scheme with absorption or Absorbed Euler (AE) scheme sets all occurring negative values of the CIR discretization to zero, i.e.

v^{abs}_{k+1}=\left(v^{abs}_{k}+\kappa\left(\theta-v^{abs}_{k}\right)(t_{k+1}-t_{k})+\sigma\sqrt{v^{abs}_{k}}\left(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}\right)\right)^{+}

(4)

and
v^{abs}_{0}=v_{0}
.
Its exact origin is unknown and also no weak convergence rates are known for it.
Using SE or AE, we then discretize the log-price process with the standard Euler method:

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\Big(\mu-\frac{1}{2}v_{k}\Big)(t_{k+1}-t_{k})+\sqrt{v_{k}}\Big(\rho\left(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}\right)+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\left(B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}\right)\Big).

(5)

We will work with an equidistant discretization

t_{k}=k\Delta t,\quad k=0,\ldots,N,

with
\Delta t=T/N
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, we will use the standard notations for the spaces of differentiable functions. In particular, the subscript
pol
denotes polynomial growth. See also Subsection
3.1
.
Finally, we set

\nu=\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}},

i.e.
\nu
is the Feller index of the CIR process
V=(V_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.

Our analysis leads us to the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

(i) Let
f\in C^{6}_{pol}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))
and
\nu>1
.
Then both schemes satisfy

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\frac{\left|\mathbb{E}\left[f(x_{N},v_{N})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[f(X_{T},V_{T})\right]\right|}{\left|T/N\right|}<\infty.

(ii) Let
f\in C^{6}_{pol}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))
and
\nu\leq 1
. Then both schemes satisfy

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\frac{\left|\mathbb{E}\left[f(x_{N},v_{N})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[f(X_{T},V_{T})\right]\right|}{\left|T/N\right|^{\alpha}}=0

for all
\alpha\in(0,\nu)
.

Thus, for
\nu>1
we have weak convergence order one and for
\nu\leq 1
we have weak convergence order
\nu-\epsilon
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
. In view of these convergence rates, exact and semi-exact simulation schemes are relevant alternatives for small Feller indices
\nu
.

.

Our analysis builds on the regularity of the Heston PDE established in
[
13
]
. Moreover, we use the local time-approach for the analysis of the discretization of the CIR process from
[
12
]
and we modify and improve the analysis of the probability that the discretization of the CIR process becomes negative from
[
12
,
15
]
. In particular, we are able to remove the restriction

for the Feller index in comparison to
[
12
,
15
]
.

As already mentioned,
[

]
studies the weak error of the symmetrized Euler scheme for the CIR process. In
[

]
the strong error of a fully truncated Euler scheme for the CIR process is analyzed, while
[
13
]
studies a hybrid-tree scheme for option pricing in stochastic volatility models with jumps.

Remark 1.3
.

The (positivity preserving) weak approximation of the CIR process has been also studied by Alfonsi in
[
2
,
3
]
. In particular, weak first and second order schemes have been derived in these references. The weak approximation of the log-Heston model by a combined Euler scheme for

. In these works weak convergence order one is obtained for smooth test functions

Another approach for the weak approximation of the CIR process and the Heston model, which combines the cubature on Wiener space approach and the classical Runge-Kutta method for ordinary differential equations, has been introduced in
[

]

]

.

A decay of the weak convergence rate for

The Heston model was introduced in the 1990s and is now a classical stochastic volatility model; our analysis addresses a problem that has been open for a long time. Meanwhile, the Heston model has been extended in several ways, e.g., by adding jumps

In this section, we will test numerically whether the weak convergence rates of Theorem

are attained even under milder assumptions on the test function

.
We will consider a call, a put and a digital option. These payoffs are at most Lipschitz continuous which is typical in financial applications. This lack of smoothness is in contrast to the usual assumptions on

Remark 2.1
.

Our analysis does not carry over to modified Euler schemes for the CIR process that take negative values, as e.g. the full-truncation Euler
[
15
]
.
In these schemes the approximation of the CIR component is not bounded from below which prohibits our application of the Kolmogorov PDE and Itō’s lemma. See
[
36
]
for a survey on modified Euler schemes.

Remark 2.2
.

Bally and Talay analyze in
[
8
]
the weak error of the Euler scheme for SDEs with
C_{b}^{\infty}
-coefficients, i.e. coefficients which are infinitely differentiable and whose derivatives of any order are bounded, that satisfy an additional non-degeneracy condition of Hörmander type (UH). They establish weak order one for the Euler scheme for test functions
f
that are only measurable and bounded. However, the log-Heston model does not satisfy the above assumptions and an adaptation of the approach of
[
8
]
to the log-Heston model leads to the restrictive assumption
\nu>\frac{9}{2}
in
[
4
]
.

The cubature on Wiener space methodology, see
[
29
]
, is another approach to deal with test functions
f
of low smoothness. This approach transfers the quadrature of the SDE into the quadrature of a related collection of ordinary differential equations. It allows to handle Lipschitz continuous test functions for SDEs with
C_{b}^{\infty}
-coefficients, which satisfy the (UHG) condition, see
[
28
]
. The latter is a relaxation of the (UH) condition.

3
Auxiliary Results

In this section, we will collect and establish several auxiliary results for the weak error analysis.

3.1
Kolmogorov PDE

To obtain the Kolmogorov backward PDE for the Heston Model, we look at the following integral equations:

\displaystyle V^{s,v}_{t}

\displaystyle=v+\int_{s}^{t}\kappa(\theta-V^{s,v}_{r})dr+\sigma\int_{s}^{t}\sqrt{V^{s,v}_{r}}dW_{r},

\displaystyle{X}^{s,x,v}_{t}

\displaystyle=x+\mu(t-s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}V^{s,v}_{r}dr+\rho\int_{s}^{t}\sqrt{V^{s,v}_{r}}dW_{r}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\int_{s}^{t}\sqrt{V^{s,v}_{r}}dB_{r}.

Without loss of generality, we will set
\mu=0
throughout the remainder of this manuscript. We now define

\displaystyle u(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f\left({X}^{t,x,v}_{T},V^{t,v}_{T}\right)\right],\,t\in[0,T],\,x\in\mathbb{R},\,v\geq 0,

and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The corresponding Kolmogorov backward PDE is then given by

\displaystyle u_{t}(t,x,v)=

\displaystyle\text{ }\frac{v}{2}u_{x}(t,x,v)-\kappa(\theta-v)u_{v}(t,x,v)

(6)

\displaystyle-\frac{v}{2}\left(u_{xx}(t,x,v)+2\rho\sigma u_{xv}(t,x,v)+\sigma^{2}u_{vv}(t,x,v)\right)\text{, }\quad t\in(0,T),x\in\mathbb{R},v>0,

\displaystyle u(T,x,v)=

\displaystyle\text{ }f(x,v)\text{, }\quad x\in\mathbb{R},v\geq 0.

In our error analysis we will follow the now classical approach of
[
38
]
which exploits the regularity of (
6
). For the latter we will rely on the recent work of Briani et al.
[
13
]
. To state their regularity results, we will need the following notation:

For a multi-index
l=(l_{1},...,l_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define
s(l)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}
and for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
\partial^{l}_{y}=\partial^{l_{1}}_{y_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\partial^{l_{d}}_{y_{d}}
. Moreover, we denote by
|y|
the standard Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain or a closure of a domain and
q\in\mathbb{N}
.
C^{q}\left(\mathcal{D}\right)
is the set of all real-valued functions on
\mathcal{D}
which are
q
-times continuously differentiable.
C^{q}_{pol}\left(\mathcal{D}\right)
is the set of functions
g\in C^{q}\left(\mathcal{D}\right)
such that there exist
c,a>0
for which

\displaystyle|\partial^{l}_{y}g(y)|\leq c(1+|y|^{a}),\qquad y\in\mathcal{D},\,s(l)\leq q.

We define
C^{q}_{pol,T}\left(\mathcal{D}\right)
as the set of functions
v\in C^{\lfloor q/2\rfloor,q}_{pol}\left([0,T)\times\mathcal{D}\right)
such that there exist
c,a>0
for which

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t<T}|\partial^{k}_{t}\partial^{l}_{y}v(t,y)|\leq c(1+|y|^{a}),\qquad y\in\mathcal{D},\,2k+s(l)\leq q.

Proposition 3.1

(Briani, Caramellino, Terenzi; Proposition 5.3 and Remark 5.4)
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that
f\in C^{2q}_{pol}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))
. Then, the solution
u
of PDE (
6
) satisfies
u\in C^{q}_{pol,T}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))
.

Constants whose values depend only on

,
x_{0}
,

,
\kappa
,
\theta
,
\sigma
and

.

3.2
Properties of the discretization schemes

We write both volatility schemes as Itō processes for
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
:

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{sym}_{t}

\displaystyle=\biggl|v_{n(t)}^{sym}+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\kappa\left(\theta-v_{n(t)}^{sym}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\sqrt{v_{n(t)}^{sym}}dW_{s}\biggr|,

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{abs}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(v_{n(t)}^{abs}+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\kappa\left(\theta-v_{n(t)}^{abs}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\sqrt{v_{n(t)}^{abs}}dW_{s}\right)^{+}.

Here we have set
n(t):=\max\{k\in\{0,...,N\}:t_{k}\leq t\}
and
\eta(t):=t_{n(t)}
.
The log-price discretization is the same for both schemes:

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{t}^{\star}

\displaystyle=x_{n(t)}^{\star}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}v_{n(t)}^{\star}ds+\rho\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\sqrt{v_{n(t)}^{\star}}dW_{s}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\sqrt{v_{n(t)}^{\star}}dB_{s},

where
\star\in\{sym,abs\}
.
We define

\displaystyle Z_{t}^{\star}:=v_{n(t)}^{\star}+\kappa\big(\theta-v_{n(t)}^{\star}\big)(t-\eta(t))+\sigma\sqrt{v_{n(t)}^{\star}}\left(W_{t}-W_{\eta(t)}\right)

(7)

and use the Tanaka-Meyer formulae for
\hat{v}^{sym}_{t}=\bigl|Z_{t}^{sym}\bigr|
and for
\hat{v}^{abs}_{t}=\left(Z_{t}^{abs}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{sym}_{t}=v_{n(t)}^{sym}

\displaystyle+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\operatorname{sign}\left(Z_{s}^{sym}\right)\kappa\left(\theta-v_{n(s)}^{sym}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\operatorname{sign}\left(Z_{s}^{sym}\right)\sqrt{v_{n(s)}^{sym}}dW_{s}

(8)

\displaystyle+\left(L_{t}^{0}(Z^{sym})-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z^{sym})\right)

and

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{abs}_{t}=\text{ }v_{n(t)}^{abs}

\displaystyle+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z_{s}^{abs}>0\}}\kappa\left(\theta-v_{n(s)}^{abs}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z_{s}^{abs}>0\}}\sqrt{v_{n(s)}^{abs}}dW_{s}

(9)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{t}^{0}(Z^{abs})-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z^{abs})\right).

Here
L^{0}(Z^{\star})=(L_{t}^{0}(Z^{\star}))_{t\in[0,T]}
is the local time of
Z^{\star}
in zero.
For almost all
\omega\in\Omega

is continuous and non-decreasing with

. See e.g. Theorem 7.1 in Chapter III of
[
27
]
.

Moreover, note that

{P}(Z_{t}^{sym}=0|v_{n(t)}^{sym}=v)={P}(Z_{t}^{abs}=0|v_{n(t)}^{abs}=v)=0,\,\,\qquad v\geq 0,\,t\in[0,T]\setminus\{t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}\},

and so
P(Z_{t}^{\star}=0)=0
for all
t\in[0,T]\setminus\{t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}\}
and
\star\in\{sym,abs\}
.

From
[
12
]
we know that the moments of the symmetrized Euler scheme are bounded. This also holds for the Euler scheme with absorption.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any

Lemma 3.4

Let

\displaystyle P(Z_{t}^{\star}\leq 0)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{{v}_{n(t)}^{\star}(1-\kappa\Delta t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\Delta t}\right)\right],\qquad t\in[0,T]\setminus\{t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}\},

Proof.

Let

Since

we need to consider only
P(Z_{t}^{\star}<0)

\star

\displaystyle P(Z_{t}<0|{v}_{n(t)}=v)\leq P\left(W_{t}-W_{\eta(t)}<\frac{-v(1-\kappa(t-\eta(t)))-\kappa\theta(t-\eta(t))}{\sigma\sqrt{v}}\right)

for

\displaystyle P(Z_{t}<0|{v}_{n(t)}=0)=0.

\displaystyle P\left(G<\beta\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)

for

\displaystyle P(Z_{t}\leq 0)

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\Delta t<\frac{1}{\kappa}
and set

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}=\frac{1-\kappa\Delta t}{2}.

(i) Consider the sequence
(c_{j})_{0\leq j\leq N}
with

\displaystyle c_{0}=\alpha_{N},\qquad c_{1}=\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N},\qquad c_{j+1}=c^{2}_{j}+\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N},\quad j=1,\ldots,N-1.

Then, we have

\displaystyle c_{j}\leq 1-\alpha_{N}-\frac{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}{1+\varepsilon(j-1)},\qquad j=1,\ldots,N,

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
.

(ii) Define the sequence
(a_{j})_{0\leq j\leq N}
by

\displaystyle a_{j}=\frac{2(\alpha_{N}-c_{j})}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t},\qquad j=0,\ldots,N.

Then, we have
a_{j}\geq 0
for
j=0,\ldots,N
.
Moreover, let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
and

\displaystyle c=\exp\left(\kappa\left(\nu T+\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{\sigma^{2}\nu}{v_{0}e}\right\}\right)^{\nu}.

(10)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\kappa\theta\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j+1}\Delta t\right){\exp\left(-v_{0}a_{k+1}\right)}\leq c\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}

for all
k=1,\ldots,N
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
\Delta t\in(0,\frac{1}{\kappa})
, we know that

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}=\frac{1-\kappa\Delta t}{2}<\frac{1}{2},\qquad\alpha_{N}>\frac{1-\kappa\frac{1}{\kappa}}{2}=0

and therefore
\alpha_{N}\in(0,1/2)
.
By construction, we then have
c_{j}\geq 0
,
j=0,\ldots,N
.
Now let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
. We show that

\displaystyle c_{j}\leq 1-\alpha_{N}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j-1+\varepsilon^{-1}},\qquad j=1,\ldots,N,

by induction. For
j=1
, we have

\displaystyle c_{1}=\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N}=1-\alpha_{N}-\left(1-\alpha_{N}\right)^{2}\leq 1-\alpha_{N}-\frac{1}{4}\leq 1-\alpha_{N}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{-1}},

since
1/4\geq(1-\varepsilon)\varepsilon
.

Suppose that the statement holds for a fixed
j\in\{1,...,N\}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle c_{j+1}=\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N}+c^{2}_{j}\leq

\displaystyle\,\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N}+\left(1-\alpha_{N}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j-1+\varepsilon^{-1}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N}+1-2\alpha_{N}+\alpha_{N}^{2}-2(1-\alpha_{N})\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j-1+\varepsilon^{-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,+\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{(j-1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,1-\alpha_{N}-2(1-\alpha_{N})\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j-1+\varepsilon^{-1}}+\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{(j-1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2}}.

Now for

c_{j+1}\leq 1-\alpha_{N}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j+\varepsilon^{-1}}

to be true, it is sufficient that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j-1+\varepsilon^{-1}}-\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{(j-1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2}}\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j+\varepsilon^{-1}}

(11)

since
2(1-\alpha_{N})\in(1,2)
. Equation (
11
) can be verified by a simple computation.

(ii) Since
c_{j+1}=c^{2}_{j}+\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N}

and
c_{0}=\alpha_{N}
,
c_{1}=\alpha_{N}-\alpha_{N}^{2}\leq\alpha_{N}
, we can establish by induction that
c_{j}\leq\alpha_{N}
. Since

a_{j}=\frac{2(\alpha_{N}-c_{j})}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}

we therefore have
a_{j}\geq 0
for
j=0,\ldots,N
.
It follows that

\displaystyle-\kappa\theta\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j+1}\Delta t=\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(c_{j+1}-\alpha_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(1-2\alpha_{N}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j+\varepsilon^{-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{k}\left(1-2\alpha_{N}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{j+\varepsilon^{-1}}\right)dj

\displaystyle=\frac{2\kappa\theta(1-\varepsilon)}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})-\ln(k+\varepsilon^{-1})\right)+\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}}\kappa\Delta tk

\displaystyle\leq\nu(1-\varepsilon)\ln\left(\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon k}\right)+\kappa\nu T.

Using the definition of
a_{k+1}
and
\alpha_{N}
, as well as the estimate for
c_{k+1}
from (i) we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\left(-v_{0}a_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}\left(c_{k+1}-\alpha_{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}\left(1-2\alpha_{N}-\frac{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}{1+\varepsilon k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{2v_{0}\kappa}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{2v_{0}\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{2v_{0}\kappa}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{v_{0}\varepsilon}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon k}\right),

since we have
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\kappa\theta\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j+1}\Delta t\right)\exp\left(-v_{0}a_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\exp\left(\kappa\left(\nu T+\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)\exp\left(-\frac{v_{0}\varepsilon}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon k}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon k}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\exp\left(\kappa\left(\nu T+\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)\exp\left(-\frac{v_{0}\varepsilon}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon k}\right)\left(\frac{v_{0}\varepsilon}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon k}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}{v_{0}\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}.

The inequality

x^{\alpha}\exp(-x)\leq\alpha^{\alpha}\exp(-\alpha),\qquad\alpha>0,\,x>0,

and using again that
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
now yield

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\kappa\theta\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j+1}\Delta t\right)\exp\left(-v_{0}a_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\kappa\left(\nu T+\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\frac{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}{e}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}{v_{0}\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\kappa\left(\nu T+\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}\nu}{v_{0}e}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\kappa\left(\nu T+\frac{2v_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{\sigma^{2}\nu}{v_{0}e}\right\}\right)^{\nu}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu(1-\varepsilon)},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.6

(13)

for

Proof.

Lemma
3.4
and (
12

The first step of this proof is to describe a sequence
(a_{j})_{0\leq j\leq N}
whose first element is equal to
\frac{(1-\kappa\Delta t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\Delta t}
and which has some suitable properties to bound the term on the left side of (
12
). Suppose that
\Delta t<\frac{1}{\kappa}
. Define the sequence
(a_{j})_{0\leq j\leq N}
as in the previous Lemma, i.e.

\displaystyle a_{j}=\frac{2(\alpha_{N}-c_{j})}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}

with

\displaystyle c_{0}=\alpha_{N},\qquad c_{1}=\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N},\qquad c_{j+1}=c^{2}_{j}+\alpha_{N}-\alpha^{2}_{N},\quad j=1,\ldots,N-1,

and
\alpha_{N}=\frac{1-\kappa\Delta t}{2}
. In particular, we have
a_{0}=0,

a_{1}=\frac{2\alpha^{2}_{N}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}=\frac{(1-\kappa\Delta t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\Delta t}

\displaystyle a_{j+1}=\frac{2(\alpha_{N}-c_{j+1})}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}=\frac{2(\alpha^{2}_{N}-c^{2}_{j})}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}=\frac{4\alpha_{N}(\alpha_{N}-c_{j})-2(\alpha_{N}-c_{j})^{2}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta t}=2\alpha_{N}a_{j}-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}_{j}\sigma^{2}\Delta t.

Next, we take a look at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-{v}_{k}^{\star}a_{i}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-{v}_{k}^{\star}a_{i}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}\right]\right]

and bound the conditional expectation using that
a_{i}\geq 0
,
|v|\geq v
and
v^{+}\geq v
, respectively. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-{v}_{k}^{\star}a_{i}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-a_{i}\left(\kappa\theta\Delta t+v_{{k-1}}^{\star}(1-\kappa\Delta t)+\sigma\sqrt{{v}_{{k-1}}^{\star}}\left(W_{t_{k}}-W_{t_{k-1}}\right)\right)\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left(-a_{i}\left(\kappa\theta\Delta t+{v}_{{k-1}}^{\star}(1-\kappa\Delta t)\right)\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-a_{i}\sigma\sqrt{{v}_{{k-1}}^{\star}}\left(W_{t_{k}}-W_{t_{k-1}}\right)\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left(-a_{i}\left(\kappa\theta\Delta t+{v}_{{k-1}}^{\star}(1-\kappa\Delta t)\right)\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}a^{2}_{i}\sigma^{2}{v}_{{k-1}}^{\star}\Delta t\right).

Since

\displaystyle a_{i+1}=a_{i}(1-\kappa\Delta t)-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}_{i}\sigma^{2}\Delta t,

it follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-{v}_{k}^{\star}a_{i}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(-a_{i}\kappa\theta\Delta t\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-{v}_{{k-1}}^{\star}a_{i+1}\right)\right].

Plugging in
a_{1}
and applying this upper bound
k
times, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{{v}_{k}^{\star}(1-\kappa\Delta t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\Delta t}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-{v}_{k}^{\star}a_{1}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(-\kappa\theta\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Delta t\right)\exp\left(-v_{0}a_{k+1}\right).

The assertion now follows from the second part of the previous Lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
p\geq 1
there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\hat{v}_{t}^{\star}-\hat{v}_{\eta(t)}^{\star}\big|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

holds
for
\star\in\{sym,abs\}
.

Proof.

The proof for the Symmetrized Euler can be found in
[
11
]
. We prove the statement for the Absorbed Euler by a similar approach and drop the
abs
-label to simplify the notation. We have

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{v}_{t}-\hat{v}_{\eta(t)}\right|^{p}\right]=\sup\limits_{k=0,...,N-1}\sup\limits_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{v}_{t}-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right|^{p}\right].

Since

\displaystyle\left|\left(v+z\right)^{+}-v\right|\leq|z|

for
v>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we have for
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
that

\displaystyle\left|\hat{v}_{t}-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\hat{v}_{t_{k}}+\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right)(t-t_{k})+\sigma\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t_{k}}}\left(W_{t}-W_{t_{k}}\right)\right)^{+}-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right)(t-t_{k})+\sigma\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t_{k}}}\left(W_{t}-W_{t_{k}}\right)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\Delta t\right)^{p}\left|\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right)\right|^{p}+2^{p-1}\sigma^{p}\hat{v}_{t_{k}}^{p/2}\left|W_{t}-W_{t_{k}}\right|^{p}.

Using Lemma
3.2
we obtain for all
k=0,\ldots,N-1
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{v}_{t_{k}}^{p/2}\left|W_{t}-W_{t_{k}}\right|^{p}\right]\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{v}_{t_{k}}^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|W_{t}-W_{t_{k}}\right|^{2p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{k=0,...,N-1}\sup\limits_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{v}_{t}-\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\right|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\beta>0
,
\delta>0
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
,
\Delta t\leq\frac{1}{2\kappa}
and
\star\in\{sym,abs\}
. Then, there exist constants
C_{\delta}>0
and

such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}^{0}\left(Z^{\star}\right)-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}\left(Z^{\star}\right)\right]\leq C_{\delta}\Delta t\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\delta}},\qquad t\in[0,T],

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L_{t}^{0}\left(Z^{\star}\right)-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}\left(Z^{\star}\right)\right|^{1+\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\leq C_{\beta,\delta}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\delta)(1+\beta)^{2}}}\qquad t\in[0,T].

Proof.

(i) To simplify the notation, we drop the
\star
-label.
By the occupation time formula, see e.g. Theorem 7.1 in Chapter III of
[
27
]
, we have for any
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
and for any non-negative Borel-measurable function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that
P
-a.s

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(z)\left(L_{t}^{z}\left(Z\right)-L^{z}_{t_{k}}\left(Z\right)\right)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\phi(Z_{s})d\langle Z\rangle_{s}=\sigma^{2}\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\phi(Z_{s})\hat{v}_{t_{k}}ds.

Here
L^{z}(Z)
is the local time of
Z
in
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since

P^{Z_{s}|\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}=v}=\mathcal{N}\left(v+\kappa(\theta-v)(s-\eta(s)),\sigma^{2}v(s-\eta(s))\right)

we have
for any
v>0
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(z)\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}^{z}\left(Z\right)-L^{z}_{t_{k}}\left(Z\right)|\hat{v}_{t_{k}}=v\right]dz=\sigma^{2}\int_{t_{k}}^{t}v\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(Z_{s})|\hat{v}_{t_{k}}=v\right]ds

\displaystyle\text{ }=\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(z)\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{v}}{\sqrt{2\pi(s-t_{k})}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z-v-\kappa(\theta-v)(s-t_{k})\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}v(s-t_{k})}\right)dsdz.

Since the above equation holds for any non-negative Borel-measurable function
\phi
, we must have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}^{z}\left(Z\right)-L^{z}_{t_{k}}\left(Z\right)|\hat{v}_{t_{k}}=v\right]

\displaystyle=\sigma\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{v}}{\sqrt{2\pi(s-t_{k})}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z-v-\kappa(\theta-v)(s-t_{k})\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}v(s-t_{k})}\right)ds

for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
Setting
z=0
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}^{0}\left(Z\right)-L_{t_{k}}^{0}\left(Z\right)|\hat{v}_{t_{k}}=v\right]

\displaystyle=\sigma\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{v}}{\sqrt{2\pi(s-t_{k})}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(v+\kappa(\theta-v)\left(s-t_{k}\right)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}v\left(s-t_{k}\right)}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{v}}{\sqrt{2\pi\left(s-t_{k}\right)}}\exp\left(-\frac{v\left(1-\kappa\left(s-t_{k}\right)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)}\right)ds.

Since for
\delta>0
there exist a
c_{\delta}>0
such that
\sqrt{y}\exp(-y)\leq c_{\delta}\exp\left(-\frac{y}{1+\delta}\right)
for all
y\geq 0
, we have

\frac{\sqrt{v}(1-\kappa\left(s-t_{k}\right))}{\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)}}\exp\left(-\frac{{v}\left(1-\kappa\left(s-t_{k}\right)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)}\right)\leq c_{\delta}\exp\left(-\frac{{v}\left(1-\kappa\left(s-t_{k}\right)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)(1+\delta)}\right).

Moreover, since
1-\kappa\left(s-t_{k}\right)\in[1/2,1]

we obtain

\frac{\sqrt{v}}{\sqrt{s-t_{k}}}\exp\left(-\frac{{v}\left(1-\kappa\left(s-t_{k}\right)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)}\right)\leq\sqrt{8}\sigma c_{\delta}\exp\left(-\frac{{v}\left(1-\kappa\left(s-t_{k}\right)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)(1+\delta)}\right).

It follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}^{0}\left(Z\right)-L_{t_{k}}^{0}\left(Z\right)|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t_{k}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\left(s-t_{k}\right)}}\exp\left(-\frac{\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\left(1-\kappa(s-t_{k})\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{\delta}\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\left(1-\kappa(s-t_{k})\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}(s-t_{k})(1+\delta)}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{\delta}\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\left(1-\kappa\Delta t\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\Delta t(1+\delta)}\right)ds.

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\hat{v}_{t_{k}}\left(1-\kappa\Delta t\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\Delta t(1+\delta)}\right)\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\Delta t\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\delta}}.

(ii) For the second statement note first that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L^{0}_{t}(Z)-L^{0}_{\eta(t)}(Z)\right|^{1+\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(L^{0}_{t}(Z)-L^{0}_{\eta(t)}(Z)\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\left(L^{0}_{t}(Z)-L^{0}_{\eta(t)}(Z)\right)^{\beta+1-\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}

by Hölder’s inequality.
Now, consider first

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L_{t}^{0}\left(Z\right)-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}\left(Z\right)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\,+\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\operatorname{sign}\left(Z_{s}\right)\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}}dW_{s}\right|^{p}\right]\right)

since

We can conclude from Lemma

(14)

This regularization is not required for the symmetrized Euler scheme, but to present both proofs in a concise way, we use it for both schemes.

For

\displaystyle u^{\gamma}(T,x,v)=

(15)

, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(L_{t}^{0}(Z)-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z)\right)k^{\gamma}(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{v}_{t})\right]dt\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left|L_{t}^{0}(Z)-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z)\right|^{1+\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\left(\mathbb{E}|k^{\gamma}(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{v}_{t})|^{\frac{1+\beta}{\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}dt.

As before, we can show that there exists a constant

C_{f,\beta}>0
, such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|k^{\gamma}(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{v}_{t})|^{\frac{1+\beta}{\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}\leq C_{f,\beta}.

Since

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L_{t}^{0}(Z)-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z)\right|^{1+\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\leq C_{\beta,\delta}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\delta)(1+\beta)^{2}}},

again by Proposition
3.9
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(L_{t}^{0}(Z)-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z)\right)k^{\gamma}(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{v}_{t})\right]dt\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{f,\beta,\delta}\Delta t\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\delta)(1+\beta)^{2}}}.

(22)

Summarizing (
20
), (
21
) and (
4.1.3
) we have shown that

\displaystyle|e_{n}^{(3,\gamma)}|\leq C_{f,\beta,\delta}\Delta t\left(\Delta t+\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\delta)(1+\beta)^{2}}}\right),

(23)

uniformly in
\gamma\in(0,1]
.

4.1.4
The fourth term

Finally, consider

e^{(4,\gamma)}_{n}=\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{R}(u^{\gamma})(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{v}_{t})\right]dt.

Since

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{R}(u^{\gamma})(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{v}_{t})\right]\leq C_{f}

due to Equation (
15
) and the Lemmata
3.2
,
3.3
,
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}|e^{(4,\gamma)}_{n}|\leq C_{f}\Delta t,

(24)

uniformly in
\gamma\in(0,1]
.

4.1.5
The conclusion

Recall that
\Delta t=T/N
.
Adding the estimates for term one up to term four we have derived that

\displaystyle|e_{n}^{\gamma}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{f,\beta,\delta}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\delta)(1+\beta)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{f,\beta}\Delta t\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\beta}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{f,\beta,\delta}\Delta t\left(\Delta t+\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\delta)(1+\beta)^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{f}\gamma\Delta t.

For any given
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
we now can find
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
,
\beta>0
and
\delta>0
such that

\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}+\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\delta)(1+\beta)^{2}}\geq 1+\nu(1-\epsilon)

and

1+\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\beta}\geq 1+\nu(1-\epsilon).

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle|e_{n}^{\gamma}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{f,\epsilon}\Delta t\left(\Delta t+(\Delta t)^{\nu(1-\epsilon)}\right)+C_{f}\gamma\Delta t

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|e_{n}^{\gamma}|\leq C_{f}\gamma+C_{f,\epsilon}\left(\Delta t+(\Delta t)^{\nu(1-\epsilon)}\right).

Since

|\mathbb{E}\left[f(x_{N},v_{N})-f(X_{T},V_{T})\right]|\leq\limsup_{\gamma\searrow 0}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|e_{n}^{\gamma}|

we have that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\left[f(x_{N},v_{N})-f(X_{T},V_{T})\right]|\leq C_{f,\epsilon}\left(\Delta t+(\Delta t)^{\nu(1-\epsilon)}\right),

which concludes the proof.

4.2
The absorbed Euler scheme

All tools and auxiliary results have been derived simultaneously for the symmetrized and the absorbed Euler scheme. The only difference is that we have

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{abs}_{t}=\text{ }v_{n(t)}^{abs}

\displaystyle+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z_{s}^{abs}>0\}}\kappa\left(\theta-v_{n(s)}^{abs}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z_{s}^{abs}>0\}}\sqrt{v_{n(s)}^{abs}}dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{t}^{0}(Z^{abs})-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z^{abs})\right)

instead of

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{sym}_{t}=v_{n(t)}^{sym}

\displaystyle+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\operatorname{sign}\left(Z_{s}^{sym}\right)\kappa\left(\theta-v_{n(s)}^{sym}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\operatorname{sign}\left(Z_{s}^{sym}\right)\sqrt{v_{n(s)}^{sym}}dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\left(L_{t}^{0}(Z^{sym})-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(Z^{sym})\right).

Besides this difference, we can carry out the proof completely analogous.

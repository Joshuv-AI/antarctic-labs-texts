Abstract.
We study some properties of a triad of circles associated with a triangle.
Each circle is inside the triangle, tangent to two sides of the triangle, and
externally tangent to the circle on the third side as diameter.
In particular, we find a nice relation involving the radii of the inner and outer Apollonius circles of the three circles in the triad.

Keywords.
Paasche point, Apollonius circle, barycentric coordinates, Mathematica.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020).
51-02, 51M04.

3.
Metric Relationships involving
\rho_{a}
,
\rho_{b}
,
\rho_{c}

In addition to Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
2.5
, the following
symmetric relationship involving
\rho_{a}
,
\rho_{b}
, and
\rho_{c}
holds.

Theorem 3.1
.

For the triad of circles associated with
\triangle ABC
, we have

\rho_{a}\rho_{b}+\rho_{b}\rho_{c}+\rho_{c}\rho_{a}-2r(\rho_{a}+\rho_{b}+\rho_{c})+2r^{2}=0.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.1
, we have
\rho_{a}=r(1-\tan\frac{A}{2})
,

\rho_{b}=r(1-\tan\frac{B}{2})
, and
\rho_{c}=r(1-\tan\frac{C}{2})
.
Substituting these values into the expression

\rho_{a}\rho_{b}+\rho_{b}\rho_{c}+\rho_{c}\rho_{a}-2r(\rho_{a}+\rho_{b}+\rho_{c})+2r^{2}

and simplifying (using
Mathematica
), shows that the expression is equal to

-r^{2}\cos\left(\frac{A+B+C}{2}\right)\sec\frac{A}{2}\sec\frac{B}{2}\sec\frac{C}{2}.

Since
A+B+C=\pi
, this expression is equal to 0.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For the triad of circles associated with
\triangle ABC
, we have

p(r-\rho_{a})(r-\rho_{b})(r-\rho_{c})=r^{4}.

Proof.

This result follows from Theorem
2.1
and the trigonometric identity

\tan\frac{A}{2}\tan\frac{B}{2}\tan\frac{C}{2}=\frac{r}{p}

which comes from
[
2
, p. 358]
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For the triad of circles associated with
\triangle ABC
, we have

(2)

\rho_{a}=\frac{\Delta-(p-b)(p-c)}{p}.

Similar formulas hold for
\rho_{b}
and
\rho_{c}
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.1
and the well-known identities

\tan\frac{A}{2}=\frac{r}{p-a}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad r=\frac{\Delta}{p},

we get

(3)

\displaystyle\rho_{a}

\displaystyle=r\left(1-\frac{r}{p-a}\right)=r-\frac{r^{2}}{p-a}=\frac{\Delta}{p}-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{p^{2}(p-a)}

(4)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta}{p}-\frac{p(p-a)(p-b)(p-c)}{p^{2}(p-a)}=\frac{\Delta-(p-b)(p-c)}{p}.

This complete the proof.
∎

\gamma_{a}
,
\gamma_{b}
,
\gamma_{c}

Theorem 4.1
.

The barycentric coordinates of the center of
\gamma_{a}
are

D=aS+2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c):bS-2b(p-b)(p-c):cS-2c(p-b)(p-c).

Proof.

Let
y
be the distance between
D
and the sideline
BC
. Summing the areas of triangles
DBC
,
DCA
and
DAB
we obtain

(5)

ay+b\rho_{a}+c\rho_{a}=2\Delta.

Plugging (
2
) into (
5
) we get

\displaystyle ay+(b+c)\cdot\frac{\Delta-(p-b)(p-c)}{p}=S

\displaystyle ay+(b+c)\cdot\frac{S-2(p-b)(p-c)}{2p}=S

\displaystyle 2pay+(b+c)S-2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c)=(a+b+c)S

\displaystyle 2pay=2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c)+aS

(6)

\displaystyle ay=\frac{aS+2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c)}{2p}

By using (
2
) and (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\Delta DBC:\Delta DCA:DAB=ay:b\rho_{a}:c\rho_{a}

\displaystyle=aS+2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c):bS-2b(p-b)(p-c):cS-2c(p-b)(p-c)

which are the desired barycentric coordinates. ∎

A concurrence at the Paasche point.

In
[

the following result is stated.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
ABC
is an acute triangle. Let
\gamma_{a}
be the circle touching
CA
and
AB
from inside of
ABC
and also externally tangent to the semicircle of diameter
BC
,
in point
T_{a}
. Define
T_{b}
and
T_{c}
cyclically (Figure
12
). Then
ABC
is perspective to
T_{a}T_{b}T_{c}
, and the perspector is
X_{1123}
, the Paasche point of
\triangle ABC
.

Figure 12
.

Proof.

We use homogeneous barycentric coordinates with respect to the triangle
ABC
. Let
M_{a}
be the midpoint of
BC
.
The point
T_{a}
divides the segment joining the centers of the circles
\gamma_{a}
and
\omega_{a}
in the ratio
\rho_{a}:\frac{a}{2}
.
Using theorem
4.1
we have that the sum of coordinates of
D
is

\displaystyle aS+2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c)+bS-2b(p-b)(p-c)+cS-2c(p-b)(p-c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+b+c)S+2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c)-2(b+c)(p-b)(p-c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+b+c)S=2pS.

Therefore, by writing the coordinates of
M_{a}
in the form
M_{a}=0:pS:pS
, we get

T_{a}=\frac{a}{2}\cdot D+\rho_{a}\cdot M_{a}.

It follows that the first coordinate of
T_{a}=x_{a}:y_{a}:z_{a}
is given by

\displaystyle x_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{2}\left(aS+2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c)\right)+\rho_{a}\cdot 0

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{2}\left(aS+2(p-b)(p-c)(b+c)\right).

In a similar way we find that

\displaystyle y_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{2}\left(bS-2b(p-b)(p-c)\right)+\rho_{a}pS

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(S-2(p-b)(p-c)\right)(ab+S)

and

\displaystyle z_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{2}\left(cS-2c(p-b)(p-c)\right)+\rho_{a}pS

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(S-2(p-b)(p-c)\right)(ac+S).

Hence

\displaystyle T_{a}=

\displaystyle a^{2}S+2a(p-b)(p-c)(b+c):

\displaystyle\left(S-2(p-b)(p-c)\right)(ab+S):

\displaystyle\left(S-2(p-b)(p-c)\right)(ac+S).

The equation of line
AT_{a}
is
z_{a}y+y_{a}z=0
, i.e.

AT_{a}:\quad(ac+S)y-(ab+S)z=0.

The cyclic substitution
a\to b
,
b\to c
,
c\to a
gives

\displaystyle BT_{b}:\quad(bc+S)x-(ab+S)z=0,

\displaystyle CT_{c}:\quad(bc+S)x-(ac+S)y=0.

A direct verification shows that
AT_{a}
,
BT_{b}
, and
CT_{c}
concur at the Paasche point

X_{1123}=(ab+S)(ac+S):(ab+S)(bc+S):(ac+S)(bc+S).\qed

Lemma 6.1

If

\displaystyle v^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{b(a-b+c)\left(-2a^{2}+ab+4ac+b^{2}+bc-2c^{2}\right)}{(a+b+c)^{2}},

\displaystyle w^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c(a+b-c)\left(-2a^{2}+4ab+ac-2b^{2}+bc+c^{2}\right)}{(a+b+c)^{2}}.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.3
, we have

\rho_{a}=\frac{\Delta-(p-b)(p-c)}{p},

and similarly

\rho_{b}=\frac{\Delta-(p-a)(p-c)}{p},\qquad\rho_{c}=\frac{\Delta-(p-a)(p-b)}{p}.

Figure 13.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
\rho_{b}<\rho_{c}
. Let
E^{\prime}
be the foot of the perpendicular from
E
to
FF_{a}
.
Applying the Pythagorean Theorem to triangle

(see figure

), taking into account that

and

, we obtain

\displaystyle u^{2}

\displaystyle=EF^{2}=E_{a}F_{a}^{2}+(FF_{a}-EE_{a})^{2}=(2r)^{2}+(\rho_{c}-\rho_{b})^{2}

\displaystyle=4r^{2}+\left(\frac{(p-a)(p-c)-(p-a)(p-b)}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\cdot\frac{\Delta^{2}}{p^{2}}+\frac{(p-a)^{2}(b-c)^{2}}{p^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4p(p-a)(p-b)(p-c)+(p-a)^{2}(b-c)^{2}}{p^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a(b+c-a)\left(a^{2}+ab+ac-2b^{2}+4bc-2c^{2}\right)}{(a+b+c)^{2}}.

The formulas relating to

and

Lemma 6.2
.

If
\theta
,
\varphi
,
\psi
are three positive real numbers such that
\theta+\varphi+\psi=360^{\circ}
, then we have

\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\varphi+\cos^{2}\psi-2\cos\theta\cos\varphi\cos\psi=1.

Proof.

Using the addition formulas we have

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\varphi+\cos^{2}\psi-2\cos\theta\cos\varphi\cos\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\varphi+\cos^{2}(360^{\circ}-\theta-\varphi)-2\cos\theta\cos\varphi\cos(360^{\circ}-\theta-\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\varphi+\cos^{2}(\theta+\varphi)-2\cos\theta\cos\varphi\cos(\theta+\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\varphi+\left(\cos\theta\cos\varphi-\sin\theta\sin\varphi\right)^{2}-2\cos\theta\cos\varphi\left(\cos\theta\cos\varphi-\sin\theta\sin\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\varphi+\cos^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\varphi+\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\varphi-2\cos^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\varphi+\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\varphi-\cos^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta(1-\cos^{2}\varphi)+\cos^{2}\varphi+\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi(\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta)+\cos^{2}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi+\cos^{2}\varphi=1.\qed

Theorem 7.1
.

For the triad of circles associated with
\triangle ABC
,
let
x=\rho_{a}
,
y=\rho_{b}
, and
z=\rho_{c}
.
Let
u,v,w
be the radii of the greatest circles inscribed in the circular segments shown in Figure
20
.
Then

xw+yu+zv=xv+zu+yw.

Figure 20
.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.1
, we have

x=r\left(1-\tan\frac{A}{2}\right),\quad y=r\left(1-\tan\frac{B}{2}\right),\quad z=r\left(1-\tan\frac{C}{2}\right).

Figure 21.

On the other hand, we have (see Figure
21
)

u=\frac{1}{2}MN=\frac{1}{2}BM\cdot\tan\frac{A}{2}=\frac{a}{4}\tan\frac{A}{2}

and similarly

v=\frac{b}{4}\tan\frac{B}{2},\quad w=\frac{c}{4}\tan\frac{C}{2}.

Observe that

\tan\frac{A}{2}=\frac{r}{s-a}=\frac{\Delta}{s(s-a)},\quad\tan\frac{B}{2}=\frac{\Delta}{s(s-b)},\quad\tan\frac{C}{2}=\frac{\Delta}{s(s-c)}

so, using the Heron formula
\Delta^{2}=s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)
, we get

\displaystyle u(y-z)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{4}\tan\frac{A}{2}\left(r\left(1-\tan\frac{B}{2}\right)-r\left(1-\tan\frac{C}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{ar}{4}\tan\frac{A}{2}\left(\tan\frac{C}{2}-\tan\frac{B}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{ar}{4}\left(\tan\frac{A}{2}\tan\frac{C}{2}-\tan\frac{A}{2}\tan\frac{B}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{ar}{4}\left(\frac{\Delta}{s(s-a)}\frac{\Delta}{s(s-c)}-\frac{\Delta}{s(s-a)}\frac{\Delta}{s(s-b)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{ar}{4}\left(\frac{s-b}{s}-\frac{s-c}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{ac-ab}{s}.

Similarly, we have

v(z-x)=\frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{ba-bc}{s},\qquad w(x-y)=\frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{cb-ca}{s}.

Therefore

\displaystyle xw+yu+zv-(v+zu+yw)=

\displaystyle u(y-z)+v(z-x)+w(x-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{ac-ab}{s}+\frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{ba-bc}{s}+\frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{cb-ca}{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{ac-ab+ba-bc+cb-ca}{s}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

S n S_{n} -extensions with prescribed norms
Sebastian Monnet

Abstract
. Given a number field k k , a finitely generated subgroup 𝒜 ⊆ k × \mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{\times} , and an integer n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we study the distribution of degree n n field extensions of k k with Galois closure group S n S_{n} , such that the elements of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} are norms. For n ≤ 5 n\leq 5 , and conjecturally for n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 , we show that the density of such extensions is the product of so-called “local masses” at the places of k k . When n n is an odd prime, we give formulae for these local masses, allowing us to express the aforementioned density as an explicit Euler product. For n = 4 n=4 , we determine almost all of these masses explicitly and give an efficient algorithm for computing the rest, again giving us the tools to evaluate the density as an Euler product.



1.
Introduction

Fix a number field
k
and let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{\times}
be a finitely generated subgroup. In
[
FLN22
]
, Frei, Loughran, and Newton count abelian extensions
K/k
such that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}K^{\times}
. The natural follow-up is: what about non-abelian extensions? Counting non-abelian number fields in general is the subject of Malle’s Conjecture (
[
Mal04
, Conjecture 1.1]
), arguably the biggest open problem in arithmetic statistics, and we do not address it here. Instead, we tackle one of the best-understood classes of non-abelian number field,
S_{n}
-
n
-ics.

An
S_{n}
-
n
-ic extension of
k
is a degree
n
field extension
K/k
, whose normal closure
\widetilde{K}
has
\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)\cong S_{n}
. Given a real number
X
, write
N_{k,n}(X)
for the number of
S_{n}
-
n
-ics
K/k
with
\Nm(\disc(K/k))\leq X
. The “Malle–Bhargava heuristics” give conjectural asymptotics for
N_{k,n}(X)
, which have been proved for
n\leq 5
by Bhargava and collaborators (see
[
Bha07
]
for the original heuristics and
[
BSW15
]
for the proof when
n\leq 5
). The cases
n\geq 6
are wide open and very famous.

Given a real number
X
, write
N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})
for the number of
S_{n}
-
n
-ic extensions
K/k
such that
\Nm(\disc(K/k))\leq X
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}K^{\times}
. We are primarily interested in the leading coefficient,

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})}{X},

of the asymptotic expansion of
N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})
, as well as the proportion

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})}{N_{k,n}(X)}

of
S_{n}
-
n
-ics with
\mathcal{A}
inside their norm groups.

Since
N_{k,n}(X)
is only known for
n\leq 5
, barring major breakthroughs in the field, we can only hope to solve our problem completely for
n=3,4,5
. It is common for papers to focus only on one of these three cases, but we have opted to address all three. In fact, contingent on the Malle–Bhargava heuristics, we extend our results to conjectures for
n\geq 6
.

1.1.
Qualitative results

In Section
2
, we explore our problem in as much generality as possible, namely for all
n\geq 3
. As already discussed, our results are conjectural for
n\geq 6
, since they assume the Malle–Bhargava heuristics. Specialising to the known cases, we isolate the following headline result:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\in\{3,4,5\}
, we have

0<\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})}{N_{k,n}(X)}\leq 1,

with equality if and only if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{\times n}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
n\in\{3,4,5\}
. For every finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{\times}
, there are infinitely many
S_{n}
-
n
-ic extensions
K/k
with
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}K^{\times}
.

For arbitrary
n\geq 3
, the more general (conjectural) analogue of Theorem
1.1
is contained in Theorems
2.17
and
2.19
.

Let us unpack the content of Theorem
1.1
. It tells us that, for
n=3,4,5
, any finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{\times}
is contained in the norm group of a positive proportion of
S_{n}
-
n
-ic extensions. Moreover, it tells us that
\mathcal{A}
can only be contained in 100% of such norm groups if it is contained in
every
such norm group.

Remark 1.3
.

In the special case where
\mathcal{A}
is generated by one element, Corollary
1.2
can be obtained by classical methods, using Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem. See
[
FLN22
, Example 1.13]
for details. For general subgroups
\mathcal{A}
, ours is the first proof we are aware of.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we expand the limit
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})}{N_{k,n}(X)}
as an Euler product over all primes of the number field
k
. The factors of this Euler product are expressed in terms of a notion called
mass
, which we now define.

Throughout this paper, we use the term
p
-adic field
to mean a finite-degree extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, for some rational prime
p
. We use the term
local field
to refer to a field that is either
p
-adic or isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. For a
p
-adic field
F
, write
\mathbb{F}_{F}
for its residue field and
q_{F}
for the size
\#\mathbb{F}_{F}
. For a local field
F
, write
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}
for the set of isomorphism classes of degree
n
étale algebras over
F
. For a subset
S\subseteq\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}
, the
mass

m(S)
of
S
is defined as follows:

•

If
F
is
p
-adic, then

m(S)=\frac{q_{F}-1}{q_{F}}\cdot\sum_{L\in S}\frac{\Nm(\disc(L/F))^{-1}}{\#\Aut(L/F)}.

•

If
F
is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, then we define

m(S)=\sum_{L\in S}\frac{1}{\#\Aut(L/F)}.

Given a subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq F^{\times}
, write
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}^{\mathcal{A}}
for the set of étale algebras
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}
with
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{L/F}L^{\times}
. For each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
, we may view
\mathcal{A}
as a subgroup of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
, which allows us to write
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
for the mass

m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}=m\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big).

Write
\Pi_{k}
for the set of primes of
k
, both finite and infinite.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
n\in\{3,4,5\}
, we have

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{s=1}\big(\zeta_{k}(s)\big)\cdot\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Pi_{k}}m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}},

and the same result holds conjecturally for
n\geq 6
, subject to the Malle–Bhargava heuristics.

1.2.
Computing the masses
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}

Theorem
1.4
tells us that we can state the density
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}
as an explicit Euler product if we can evaluate the masses
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
. As such, we dedicate much of this paper to a careful study of these masses. The masses for infinite primes are the easiest to state:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be an infinite prime of
k
and let
f:k\to\mathbb{C}
be the corresponding embedding. The following two statements are true:

•

If
f
is a real embedding, then

m\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\begin{cases}\sum_{s=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{s!(n-2s)!2^{s}}\quad\text{if $f(\alpha)>0$ for all $\alpha\in\mathcal{A}$},\\
\sum_{s=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}\frac{1}{s!(n-2s)!2^{s}}\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

•

If
f
is a complex embedding, then

m\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\frac{1}{n!}.

Recall that we are most interested in the cases
n=3,4,5
, since these are the values of
n
for which the Malle–Bhargava heuristics have been proved. We address these cases and more, studying
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
when
n=4
and whenever
n
is an odd prime.

For
p
-adic fields
F
, the definition of mass has a normalising factor
\frac{q_{F}-1}{q_{F}}
. To avoid this cumbersome notation, we define the
pre-mass
of a set
S\subseteq\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}
to be

\widetilde{m}(S)=\sum_{L\in S}\frac{\Nm(\disc(L/F))^{-1}}{\#\Aut(L/F)}.

Since it is trivial to compute the mass from the pre-mass, all remaining results in the current subsection will be about pre-mass.

Suppose that
F
is a
p
-adic field. Let
L/F
be an étale algebra, and write
L=L_{1}\times\ldots\times L_{r}
, where each
L_{i}
is a field extension of
F
with ramification index
e_{i}
and inertia degree
f_{i}
. The
splitting symbol

(L,F)
of
L
over
F
is the symbol
(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}})
, which is defined up to permutation of indices (see Definition
2.1
and the subsequent discussion for a more rigorous definition). The
degree
of the splitting symbol
(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}})
is defined to be the quantity
\sum_{i}e_{i}f_{i}
. Given a degree
n
splitting symbol
\sigma
, write
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\sigma/F}^{\mathcal{A}}
for the set of
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}^{\mathcal{A}}
with
(L,F)=\sigma
. When
\sigma
is a splitting symbol of the form
(f^{e})
, and for a finite group
G
, write
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\sigma/F}^{G/F,\mathcal{A}}
for the set of field extensions
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\sigma/F}^{\mathcal{A}}
such that the Galois closure
\widetilde{L}
of
L
over
F
has
\Gal(\widetilde{L}/F)\cong G
.

Our next result, Theorem
1.7
, gives
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
for all
p
-adic fields
F
, whenever
n
is a rational prime. Consequently, for such
n
, it allows us to write down all the masses
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
. In particular, this covers the cases
n=3
and
n=5
, two of the three we are especially interested in. For a
p
-adic field
F
, a finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq F^{\times}
, and a positive integer
n
, define

\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{n}=\mathcal{A}F^{\times n}/F^{\times n}.

For each nonnegative integer
t
, write

\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{n}_{t}=\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{n}\cap\Big(U_{F}^{(t)}F^{\times n}/F^{\times n}\Big),

where
U_{F}^{(t)}
is the
t^{\mathrm{th}}
term of the unit filtration, given by
U_{F}^{(t)}=1+\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{t}
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field, let
n
be a positive integer, and let
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
be a subgroup of
F^{\times}/F^{\times n}
. A
stratified generating set
for
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
is a collection
(A_{i})_{i\geq 0}
, indexed by nonnegative integers, where each
A_{i}
is a subset of
F^{\times}
, such that the following three conditions hold:

(1)

For each
i
and each
\alpha\in A_{i}
, we have
v_{F}(\alpha)=i
.

(2)

For
\alpha,\beta\in\bigcup_{i}A_{i}
, if
\alpha\neq\beta
then
[\alpha]\neq[\beta]
in
F^{\times}/F^{\times n}
.

(3)

The image of
\bigcup_{i}A_{i}
under the natural map
F^{\times}\to F^{\times}/F^{\times n}
is a minimal generating set for the group
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
p
be a rational prime, let
F
be a
p
-adic field with residue field of size
q
, and let
\ell
be a rational prime. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq F^{\times}
be a finitely generated subgroup. Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a stratified generating set for
\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}
. The following four statements are true:

(1)

We have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\ell/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(\ell)/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{\ell})/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\big(1-\ell^{-1}\big)+\sum_{d=1}^{\ell-2}\frac{\Part(d,\ell-d)}{q^{d}},

where
\Part(d,m)
is the partition function defined in Section
2
.

(2)

We have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(\ell)/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\ell}\quad\text{if $A_{1}=\varnothing$},\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3)

Suppose that
p\neq\ell
. Then we have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{\ell})/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{q^{\ell-1}}\quad\text{if $q\not\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}$ or $\mathcal{A}\subseteq F^{\times\ell}$},\\
\frac{1}{\ell q^{\ell-1}}\quad\text{if $q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}$ and $A_{0}=\varnothing$ and $\#A_{1}=1$},\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4)

Suppose that
p=\ell
. Then we have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\frac{1}{q^{p-1}}-\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F}\big)+\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F,\mathcal{A}}\big).

The two missing ingredients,
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F}\big)
and
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F,\mathcal{A}}\big)
, are as follows. We have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F}\big)=\frac{q-1}{p-1}\cdot q^{-2}\cdot\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{e_{F}\geq p-1}\cdot A\Big(\Big\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\Big\rceil\Big)+\mathbbm{1}_{(p-1)\nmid e_{F}}\cdot B\Big(\Big\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\Big\rceil\Big)\Big)+\mathbbm{1}_{\mu_{p}\subseteq F}\cdot q^{-(p-1)(e_{F}+1)},

where
A
and
B
are the explicit functions defined in Appendix
B
, and

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F,\mathcal{A}}\big)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mu_{p}\subseteq F}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}_{\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}}=0}\cdot\frac{q^{-(p-1)(e_{F}+1)}}{\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}}+\frac{1}{(p-1)\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}}\cdot q^{-2}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq c\leq\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\rceil\\
c\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{q\cdot\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}_{c}-\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}_{c-1}}{q^{(p-2)c+\lfloor\frac{c-2}{p}\rfloor}}.

Since the sizes
\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}_{c}
can all be different, the series for
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F,\mathcal{A}}\big)
does not have a closed form. In the special case where
\mathcal{A}
is generated by one element, we can write down such a closed form, which is stated in Theorem
3.36
. Along the way, we find the exact number of extensions
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F}
with each possible discriminant, which we state in Theorem
3.24
.

We now turn our attention to the final case of particular interest:
n=4
. When
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
k
not lying over
2
, the pre-masses
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{4/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
take a relatively simple, explicit form, stated in Theorem
4.1
.

When
\mathfrak{p}\mid 2
, the situation is more difficult; since
4
is not prime, wildly ramified quartic extensions behave significantly worse than wildly ramified prime-degree extensions, so our methods from the prime case do not generalise. However, since there are only finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}\mid 2
, it suffices to have a practicable method for computing
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{4/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
for each such
\mathfrak{p}
. In principle, this can be done by brute-force, since there are finitely many isomorphism classes of quartic étale algebras over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and we can find all of them e.g. using the methods of
[
PR01
]
. Unfortunately,
[
Kra66
, Theorem 2]
tells us that
\#\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{4/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is on the order of
2^{3[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]}
, which becomes very large very quickly, so the brute-force approach is unfeasible for all but very low-degree extensions
k_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. One of our main results is a significant improvement over this brute-force approach, whose time complexity we will state shortly, in Theorem
1.8
; before the statement, we introduce some notation.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field for some rational prime
p
. In most computer algebra packages,
p
-adic fields are defined up to a certain
precision
, which refers to the length of
p
-adic series representations stored. We will be working with field extensions
E/F
with
[E:F]\leq 4
, and we need enough precision to construct the quotient
E^{\times}/E^{\times 4}
of units modulo fourth powers. It follows that we need precision on the order of
e_{F}
. Suppose that we are working with precision
m
. In any sensible implementation, the computation time for a field operation in
F
will be polynomial in
[F:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
and
m
. Since for our applications we have
m=O(e_{F})
, a field operation in
F
can be performed with time complexity
O(t([F:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]))
, for some polynomial function
t
. Moreover, since we are bounding the degree by a constant, any field operation in any extension
E/F
with
[E:F]\leq 4
can also be performed with time complexity
O(t([F:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]))
. Given a function
f
in unspecified variables, we write

O_{F}(f)=O(f\cdot t([F:\mathbb{Q}_{p}])).

Thus, the notation
O_{F}
should be thought of as Big O notation, where we are suppressing the polynomial
t([F:\mathbb{Q}_{2}])
to account for field operations in
F
, and quadratic and quartic extensions thereof.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
be a subset whose image in
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times 4}
generates the group
\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{4}
. By the discussion above, the brute-force computation of
\widetilde{m}(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{4/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}})
has time complexity of at least
O_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\#\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})\cdot 2^{3[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]})
. We present the following improvements:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
k
lying over
2
, and choose a set
\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})
as above. There are two algorithms for computing
\widetilde{m}(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{4/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}})
, whose time complexities respectively are as follows:

(1)

O_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\big(e_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cdot\#\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})\cdot 2^{2[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]}\cdot[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]^{3}\big).

(2)

O_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\big(e_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cdot 2^{\#\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})}\cdot 2^{[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]}\cdot[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]^{3}\big).

Remark 1.9
.

The first time complexity in Theorem
1.8
is unconditionally a big improvement over the brute-force algorithm. The second time complexity is a significantly bigger improvement, conditional on the number of generators of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{4}
being kept reasonably small. Thus, the algorithms are both useful, and the choice between them will depend on the application.

We now discuss the ingredients of the algorithms in Theorem
1.8
. For
\mathfrak{p}\mid 2
, Theorem
4.1
gives an explicit formula for
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\sigma/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
, whenever
\sigma\not\in\{(1^{2}1^{2}),(2^{2}),(1^{4})\}
. Thus, we need only address these three cases. For
\sigma=(1^{2}1^{2})
and
\sigma=(2^{2})
, we state the required pre-masses in Theorems
4.7
and
4.8
, respectively.

The last hurdle is to compute
\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{4})/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
for primes
\mathfrak{p}\mid 2
. This final case is significantly harder, because there is so much wild ramification at play. We now give a brief overview of the results, which are stated in full technicality in Theorem
4.9
. We partition by Galois closure group
G
, considering the sets
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{4})/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{G/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}
separately for each
G
. For
G\in\{A_{4},S_{4}\}
, we can state the pre-mass explicitly. For other
G
, we will instead consider the quantities

\#\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{4})/k_{\mathfrak{p}},m}^{G/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}=\#\Big\{L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{4})/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{G/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}:v_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(d_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}})=m\Big\}

for each possible
m
, where
d_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is the discriminant ideal of the extension
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
. From these quantities, it is trivial to compute the pre-mass by summing over
m
. We give explicit expressions for
\#\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{4})/k_{\mathfrak{p}},m}^{G/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}
in the cases
G\in\{D_{4},V_{4}\}
.

So far, every quantity we have stated is reasonably explicit, and we have not yet bifurcated into the two algorithms promised in Theorem
1.8
. It is only the final piece, the pre-mass of wildly ramified
C_{4}
-extensions, that requires genuine algorithms to compute. Given a quadratic extension
E/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, write
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{2/E}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
for the set of quadratic field extensions
L/E
such that
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a
C_{4}
-extension. We say that a quadratic extension
E/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
C_{4}
-extendable
if
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{2/E}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\neq\varnothing
. Given a
C_{4}
-extendable extension
E/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the set
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{2/E}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
can be parametrised by the quotient group
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times 2}
. Under this parametrisation, the set
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{2/E}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}
, which consists of the extensions
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{2/E}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
with
\mathcal{A}
in their norm group, corresponds to a certain subset
N_{E}^{\mathcal{A}}
of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times 2}
. In particular, we can easily express the quantities
\#\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{2})/E,m_{2}}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}
in terms of the sizes of the subsets

N_{E,c}^{\mathcal{A}}=N_{E}^{\mathcal{A}}\cap\big(U_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{(c)}k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times 2}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times 2}\big),

for
-1\leq c\leq 2e_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. When
[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]
is small, the set
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times 2}
is small, so we can compute the sets
N_{E,c}^{\mathcal{A}}
by brute-force, iterating over the
2^{2+[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]}
elements of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times 2}
. This brute-force method yields the first time complexity in Theorem
1.8
. On the other hand, if
[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]
is large, we can use a more sophisticated algorithm whose running time is dominated by
2^{\#\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})}
. This more sophisticated method gives the second time complexity in Theorem
1.8
. Once the sizes
\#N_{E,c}^{\mathcal{A}}
have been computed, we can quickly write down the pre-mass

\widetilde{m}\big(\big\{L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{4})/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}:\text{$E$ embeds into $L$}\big\}\big).

Iterating over the
2^{2+[k_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]}
quadratic extensions

of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we can compute the pre-mass of
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{4})/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{C_{4}/k_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{A}}
, and hence of
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{4/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}

Proof of Theorem

Suppose that

\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\{\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}:r+2s=n\text{ and $r,s\geq 0$}\}.

is predictable and epimorphic, respectively. Write
\mathrm{Split}_{n}^{\mathrm{pred}}
and
\mathrm{Split}_{n}^{\mathrm{epi}}
for the sets of predictable and epimorphic degree
n
splitting symbols, respectively.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field and let
\sigma
be a predictable splitting symbol. Write
\sigma=(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}})
and let
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\sigma/F}
. Let
g=\gcd(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
. Then we have

N_{L/F}L^{\times}=\{x\in F^{\times}:g\mid v_{F}(x)\}.

In particular, if
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}^{\mathrm{epi}}
, then

N_{L/F}L^{\times}=F^{\times}.

Proof.

Write
L=L_{1}\times\ldots\times L_{r}
, where each
L_{i}/F
is a field extension with ramification index
e_{i}
and inertia degree
f_{i}
. For each
i
, let
L_{i}^{\mathrm{ur}}
be the maximal unramified subextension of
L_{i}/F
. By considering the towers
L_{i}/L_{i}^{\mathrm{ur}}/F
, it is easy to see that we have

\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times e_{i}}\subseteq N_{L_{i}/F}L_{i}^{\times}

for each
i
. Moreover, for each
i
, there is some
x_{i}\in N_{L_{i}/F}L_{i}^{\times}
with
v_{F}(x_{i})=f_{i}
. Since the
e_{i}
are mutually coprime, there exist integers
r_{i}
such that
\sum_{i}r_{i}e_{i}=1
, so for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
, we have

\alpha=\prod_{i}(\alpha^{e_{i}})^{r_{i}}\in\prod_{i}N_{L_{i}/F}L_{i}^{\times}=N_{L/F}L^{\times}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\subseteq N_{L/F}L^{\times}
. On the other hand, there are integers
s_{i}
such that
\sum_{i}s_{i}f_{i}=g
. Setting

x=\prod_{i}x_{i}^{s_{i}}\in N_{L/F}L^{\times},

we obtain
v_{F}(x)=g
, so

\{x\in F^{\times}:g\mid v_{F}(x)\}\subseteq N_{L/F}L^{\times}.

Conversely, we have

N_{L_{i}/F}L_{i}^{\times}\subseteq N_{L_{i}^{\mathrm{ur}}/F}L_{i}^{\mathrm{ur},\times}=\{x\in F^{\times}:f_{i}\mid v_{F}(x)\}\subseteq\{x\in F^{\times}:g\mid v_{F}(x)\}

for each
i
, so we are done.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field and let
\mathcal{A}=\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\rangle
be a finitely generated subgroup of
F^{\times}
. Let
n
be a positive integer and suppose that
n\mid v_{F}(\alpha_{i})
for each
i
. For every finite prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
, we have

\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathrm{pred}}\subseteq\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}.

Proof.

This is immediate from Lemma
2.8
.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
n
be an integer with
n\geq 3
, let
p
be a rational prime with
p>n
, let
F
be a
p
-adic field, and let
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}\setminus\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}^{\mathrm{pred}}
. Then
v_{F}(d_{L/F})\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
(L,F)=(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}})
. Since
p>n
, we have
p\nmid e_{i}
for each
i
, so by Lemma
2.6
we have

v_{F}(d_{L/F})=\sum_{i}f_{i}(e_{i}-1).

Since
(L,F)
is not predictable, the
e_{i}
are not mutually coprime, so there is some
d\geq 2
with
d\mid e_{i}
for all

, and therefore
e_{i}\geq 2
for each
i
. If any of the integers
e_{i}
is at least
3
, then we are done. On the other hand, if
e_{i}=2
for every
i
, then

v_{F}(d_{L/F})=\sum_{i}f_{i}=\frac{n}{2}>1,

so we are done.
∎

Definition 2.11

(Partitions)
.

Let
d
be a nonnegative integer and let
m
be a positive integer. The symmetric group
S_{m}
acts by permutation of coordinates on the set
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
of nonnegative integers. We define a
partition of
d
into
m
parts
to be an equivalence class

[(a_{i})]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}/S_{m},

such that
\sum_{i}a_{i}=d
. This is often referred to as a partition into “at most”
m
parts, but for our purposes it is more convenient to allow parts to be zero. Write
\Part(d,m)
for the number of partitions of
d
into
m
parts. Given a partition

\pi=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})

of
d
into
m
parts, write
\sum\pi
for the sum
\sum_{i}a_{i}
.

Let
\sigma=(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}})
be a degree
n
splitting symbol with

\sum_{i}f_{i}(e_{i}-1)=d.

Then we obtain a partition

\pi(\sigma):=(e_{1}-1,\ldots,e_{1}-1,e_{2}-1,\ldots,e_{2}-1,\ldots,e_{r}-1,\ldots,e_{r}-1),

of
d
into
n-d
parts, where each term
e_{i}-1
appears
f_{i}
times. For a splitting symbol
\sigma=(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}})
, define

d_{\sigma}:=\sum_{i}f_{i}(e_{i}-1)

and

\#\Aut(\sigma)=\Big(\prod_{i}f_{i}\Big)\cdot\#\{\sigma\in S_{n}:(e_{\sigma(i)},f_{\sigma(i)})=(e_{i},f_{i})\text{ for all $i$}\}.

Note that
d_{\sigma}
can be read off from
\pi(\sigma)
. Recall that, given a
p
-adic field
F
, we write
q
as shorthand for the size
q_{F}
of the residue field of
F
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field for some rational prime
p
. Let
n
and
d
be integers with
0\leq d\leq n-1
, and let

be a partition of
d
into

\widetilde{m}\big(\{L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}:\pi(L,F)=\pi_{0}\}\big)=\frac{1}{q^{d}}.

Proof.

In the case
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, this is
[
Bha07
, Proposition 2.2]
. The appendix of
[
Bha07
]
explains how to extend the proof to arbitrary
F
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

Let

be a

-adic field. Let

Lemma 2.15
.

For
n\geq 3
, we have

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Pi_{k}}\frac{\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)}{\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\big)}>0.

Proof.

Since the factor

\frac{\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)}{\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\big)}

is strictly positive for each
\mathfrak{p}
, without loss of generality we may assume that
\mathfrak{p}
is a finite prime with
\chara(\mathcal{O}_{k}/\mathfrak{p})>n
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{i})=0
for all
i
. Then all degree
n
étale algebras

are tamely ramified, so Lemma
2.6
tells us that

v_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(d_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}})=d_{(L,k_{\mathfrak{p}})},

for each
L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. For
0\leq d\leq n-1
, let

a_{d}=\Part(d,n-d).

Corollary
2.9
, Lemma
2.10
, and Corollary
2.13
tell us that

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)\geq\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathrm{pred}}\big)\geq a_{0}+a_{1}q^{-1}

and

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\big)=a_{0}+a_{1}q^{-1}+\ldots+a_{n-1}q^{-(n-1)}.

It is easy to write down a positive real number
a
, independent of
\mathfrak{p}
, such that

1-aq^{-2}\leq\frac{\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)}{\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\big)}\leq 1,

and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
F
be a local field and let
n
be any positive integer. Then

\bigcap_{L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/F}}N_{L/F}L^{\times}=F^{\times n}.

Proof.

If
F
is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, then the result is easy to see. Therefore, we will assume that
F
is
p
-adic. By the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups, we may write

F^{\times}/F^{\times n}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}(\mathbb{Z}/d_{i}\mathbb{Z})e_{i},

for basis elements
e_{i}\in F^{\times}/F^{\times n}
and integers
d_{i}\geq 2
with
d_{i}\mid n
. For each
i
, let

A_{i}=\bigoplus_{j\neq i}(\mathbb{Z}/d_{j}\mathbb{Z})e_{j},

and let
E_{i}/F
be the degree
d_{i}
abelian field extension with

\big(N_{E_{i}/F}E_{i}^{\times}\big)/F^{\times n}=A_{i}.

For each
i
, let
L_{i}/E_{i}
be any field extension of degree
\frac{n}{d_{i}}
, so that

\big(N_{L_{i}/F}L_{i}^{\times}\big)/F^{\times n}\subseteq A_{i}.

The result follows since
\bigcap_{i}A_{i}=0
.
∎

Theorem 2.17
.

Let

, let

be a number field, and let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{\times}
be a finitely generated subgroup. Assuming Conjecture
2.3
is true for

with equality if and only if

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\ker\Big(k^{\times}\to\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Pi_{k}}k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times n}\Big).

Proof.

Since we are assuming that Conjecture
2.3
holds for
n
, Lemma
2.15
tells us that

0<\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,n}(X;\mathcal{A})}{N_{k,n}(X)}\leq 1,

with equality if and only if
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathcal{A}}=\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{n/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Pi_{k}
. The result then follows from Lemma
2.16
.
∎

The main purpose of this section is to prove Theorem

-adic field with residue field of size

Lemma 3.1
.

We have

\mathrm{Split}_{\ell}=\{(1^{\ell}),(\ell)\}\sqcup\mathrm{Split}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{epi}}.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{Split}_{\ell}\setminus\{(1^{\ell}),(\ell)\}

\ell=\sum_{i}f_{i}e_{i},

Corollary 3.2
.

We have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\ell/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{\ell})/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(\ell)/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)+\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\ell/F}^{\mathrm{epi}}\big).

Proof.

This follows easily from Lemmas
2.8
and

.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\ell/F}^{\mathrm{epi}}\big)=\Big(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\Big)+\sum_{d=1}^{\ell-2}\frac{\Part(d,\ell-d)}{q^{d}}.

Proof.

Since
(1^{\ell})
is the only degree

\sigma
with

, we have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\ell/F}^{\mathrm{epi}}\big)=\sum_{d=0}^{\ell-2}\widetilde{m}\big(\{L\in\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{\ell/F}^{\mathrm{epi}}:d_{(L,F)}=d\}\big).

, it follows from Lemma

that

.
∎

Recall from Section

that for each positive integer

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a stratified generating set for

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(\ell)/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\ell}\quad\text{if $A_{1}=\varnothing$},\\

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the unramified degree

∎

Because of Corollary

and

3.1.

For each positive integer

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

be a positive integer coprime to

and define

, define

L_{j}=F\Big(\sqrt[e]{\zeta_{q-1}^{j}\pi_{F}}\Big).

The extensions

are all nonisomorphic, and

\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{e})/F}=\big\{L_{j}:j=0,1,\ldots,g-1\big\}.

This is essentially

, where we replace the polynomial

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
p\neq\ell
. The following two statements are true:

(1)

If
\ell\mid q-1
, then
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{\ell})/F}=\{L_{0},\ldots,L_{\ell-1}\}
, where
L_{j}=F\Big(\sqrt[\ell]{\zeta_{q-1}^{j}\pi_{F}}\Big)
. Moreover, for each

we have

and

N_{L_{j}/F}L_{j}^{\times}=\big\{u^{\ell}((-1)^{\ell+1}\zeta_{q-1}^{j}\pi_{F})^{m}:u\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times},m\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}.

(2)

If
\ell\nmid q-1
, then
\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{\ell})/F}=\{L\}
, where
L=F(\sqrt[\ell]{\pi_{F}})
. Moreover, we have
\Aut(L/F)=1
and
N_{L/F}L^{\times}=F^{\times}
.

Proof.

In both cases, the classification of extensions comes from Lemma
3.5
. To prove the statements about norms and automorphisms, we consider the two cases separately.

(1)

Suppose that
\ell\mid q-1
, so that
\zeta_{\ell}\in F^{\times}
. Since the elements
\zeta_{\ell}^{j}\pi_{F}
are all uniformisers of
F
, and
\pi_{F}
is an arbitrary uniformiser, we only need to prove the result for

. The element
\sqrt[\ell]{\pi_{F}}
has minimal polynomial

over

, which splits in

, so

is Galois, hence cyclic. By class field theory, we have

Moreover, it is clear that

By
[

, Part II, Proposition 3.7]
, we have

[\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times\ell}]=\#\mu_{\ell}(F)=\ell.

It follows that

\big[F^{\times}:\big\{u^{\ell}((-1)^{\ell+1}\pi_{F})^{m}:u\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times},m\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}\big]=\ell,

and the result follows.

(2)

Suppose that

. Since

and

, the result
[
NS13
, Page 140, Proposition 5.7]
tells us that

. It follows that the polynomial

is not Galois, hence it has only one automorphism. The maximal abelian subextension of

is

, so it has trivial norm group.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

We have

\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}\big)-\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F}\big)+\widetilde{m}\big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F,\mathcal{A}}\big).

Then the maximal abelian subextension of

is

\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{\mathcal{A}}=\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F,\mathcal{A}}\cup\Big(\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}\setminus\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{p})/F}^{C_{p}/F}\Big),

Let

be an abelian extension of
p
-adic fields, and let

for the conductor of the extension

For a character

Lemma 3.14

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
0\leq i\leq\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
, and let
m\in U_{F}^{(i)}
.

(1)

If
p\nmid i
, then
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}F^{\times p}
if and only if
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}
.

(2)

If
p\mid i
, then
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}F^{\times p}
if and only if there is some
x\in\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{\frac{i}{p}}
such that
(1+x)^{p}\equiv m\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{i+1}}
.

(3)

If
p\mid i
and
i<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
, then we always have
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}F^{\times p}
, and in particular

m\equiv\Big(1+\pi_{F}^{\frac{i}{p}}y\Big)^{p}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{i+1}},

where
[y]\in\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}

is the unique element with
[y]^{p}=\Big[\frac{m-1}{\pi_{F}^{i}}\Big],
which exists by Lemma
3.17
.

(4)

If
i=\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
, then
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}F^{\times p}
if and only if
\Big[\frac{m-1}{{\pi_{F}^{e_{F}/(p-1)}p}}\Big]\in\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}
is in the image of the map

\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F},\quad y\mapsto y+\frac{\pi_{F}^{e_{F}}}{p}y^{p},

and in that case
m\equiv\Big(1+\pi_{F}^{\frac{i}{p}}y\Big)^{p}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{i+1}}
, for each
y
in the preimage.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.17
, the case
i=0
is trivial, so we assume that
i\geq 1
. For the first two statements, the “if” directions are trivial, so we focus on the “only if”.

Suppose that
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}F^{\times p}\setminus U_{F}^{(i+1)}
, so
v_{F}(m-1)=i
. We will show that this implies
p\mid i
and there is some
x\in\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{\frac{i}{p}}
with
(1+x)^{p}\equiv m\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{i+1}}
, proving the first two statements.

Since
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}F^{\times p}
, there is some
c\in F^{\times}
such that
v_{F}(c)=0
and
m\equiv c^{p}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{i+1}}
. Write
c=1+x
for
x\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
, so that

m-1\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}x^{j}+x^{p}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{i+1}}.

Since
v_{F}(m-1)>0
, we have
v_{F}(x)>0
, so

(1)

\begin{cases}v_{F}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}x^{j}\Big)=e_{F}+v_{F}(x),\\
v_{F}(x^{p})=pv_{F}(x).\end{cases}

Suppose for contradiction that
v_{F}(x)>\frac{e_{F}}{p-1}
. Then
v_{F}(x)+e_{F}<pv_{F}(x)
, so

i=v_{F}(m-1)=v_{F}(x)+e_{F}>\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1},

which is impossible since by assumption
i\leq\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
.

Therefore,
v_{F}(x)\leq\frac{e_{F}}{p-1}
. We will consider the cases
v_{F}(x)=\frac{e_{F}}{p-1}
and
v_{F}(x)<\frac{e_{F}}{p-1}
separately. Suppose first that
v_{F}(x)=\frac{e_{F}}{p-1}
. Then
pv_{F}(x)=e_{F}+v_{F}(x)=\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
, so Equation (
1
) tells us that

i=v_{F}(m-1)\geq\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1},

and therefore
i=\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
and
v_{F}(x)\geq\frac{i}{p}
, as required.

Suppose instead that
v_{F}(x)<\frac{e_{F}}{p-1}
. Then
pv_{F}(x)<v_{F}(x)+e_{F}
, so Equation (
1
) tells us that

i=v_{F}(m-1)=pv_{F}(x),

and therefore
p\mid i
and
v_{F}(x)\geq\frac{i}{p}
. Thus we have proved Statements
(1)
and
(2)
.

Suppose that
p\mid i
and
i<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
. By Lemma
3.17
, there is a
y\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
with
y^{p}\equiv\frac{m-1}{\pi_{F}^{i}}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}}
. Let
x=\pi_{F}^{\frac{i}{p}}y
. Then we have

(1+x)^{p}\equiv 1+x^{p}\equiv m\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{i+1}},

so Statement
(3)
follows from Statement
(2)
.

Suppose that
i=\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
. Statement
(2)
tells us that
m\in U_{F}^{(i+1)}F^{\times p}
if and only if there is some
x\in\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{\frac{e_{F}}{p-1}}
with

m-1\equiv px+x^{p}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}+1}},

and Statement
(4)
follows easily.
∎

Lemma 3.20
.

Suppose that
(p-1)\mid e_{F}
. Define
\varphi
to be the map

\varphi:\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F},\quad y\mapsto y+\frac{\pi_{F}^{e_{F}}}{p}y^{p}.

q\quad\text{if $\mu_{p}\not\subseteq F$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The map is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear, and its kernel consists of the roots of the polynomial

X\Big(X^{p-1}+\frac{\pi_{F}^{e_{F}}}{p}\Big)\in\mathbb{F}_{F}[X].

Since
\mathbb{F}_{F}
contains all
(p-1)^{\mathrm{st}}
roots of unity, the polynomial has either
1
or
p
roots. By Hensel’s lemma, any root of
X^{p-1}+\frac{\pi_{F}^{e_{F}}}{p}
in
\mathbb{F}_{F}
lifts to a root in
F
, which exists if and only if
-p\in F^{\times(p-1)}
. The result
[

states that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p-1]{-p})=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
, and the result follows.

\chi:F^{\times}/F^{\times p}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}

\chi\big(\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}\big)=\chi\big(U_{F}^{(c)}F^{\times p}/F^{\times p}\big)=0.

In other words, we need to compute the size of the annihilator

\Big(\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}+\Big(U_{F}^{(c)}F^{\times p}/F^{\times p}\Big)\Big)^{\bot}.

It is easy to see that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Big(\big(\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}+(U_{F}^{(c)}F^{\times p}/F^{\times p})\big)^{\bot}\Big)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Big(F^{\times}/U_{F}^{(c)}F^{\times p}\Big)-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\big(\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}/\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}_{c}\big),

so

\#\Big(\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}+(U_{F}^{(c)}F^{\times p}/F^{\times p})\Big)^{\bot}=\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}_{c}}{\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{p}}\cdot\#\Big(F^{\times}/U_{F}^{(c)}F^{\times p}\Big).

The result then follows by Lemma
3.12
.
∎

Theorem 3.36
.

is the sum of the following seven quantities:

(1)

(2)

(3)

\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}}\cdot\frac{q-p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\cdot q^{-2-(p-2)(c_{\alpha}+1)-\lfloor\frac{c_{\alpha}}{p}\rfloor}.

(5)

\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}-1}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{(p-1)\nmid e_{F}}\cdot\frac{q-1}{pq^{2}(p-1)}\cdot B\Big(\Big\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\Big\rceil\Big).

(6)

-\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}-1}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}\not\equiv-1,0\pmod{p}}\cdot\frac{q-1}{pq^{2}(p-1)}\cdot B(c_{\alpha}+1).

(7)

\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mu_{p}\subseteq F}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\cdot q^{-(p-1)(e_{F}+1)}.

Proof.

If
p\nmid v_{F}(\alpha)
, then the result follows from Corollary
3.28
, so we will assume that
p\mid v_{F}(\alpha)
. Lemma
3.34
tells us that
c_{\alpha}\geq 1
. By Lemma
3.35
, the pre-mass is the sum of the following four quantities:

(1)

\frac{q-1}{p-1}\cdot q^{-2}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq c\leq c_{\alpha}\\
c\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}\end{subarray}}q^{-(p-2)c-\lfloor\frac{c-2}{p}\rfloor}.

(2)

\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}}\cdot\frac{q-p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\cdot q^{-2-(p-2)(c_{\alpha}+1)-\lfloor\frac{c_{\alpha}}{p}\rfloor}.

(3)

\frac{q-1}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\cdot q^{-2}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{\alpha}+2\leq c<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}+1\\
c\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}\end{subarray}}q^{-(p-2)c-\lfloor\frac{c-2}{p}\rfloor}.

(4)

\mathbbm{1}_{(p-1)\mid e_{F}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mu_{p}\subseteq F}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\cdot q^{-(p-1)(e_{F}+1)}.

By Lemma
3.25
, the first three quantities rearrange to:

(1)

\frac{q-1}{p-1}\cdot q^{-2}\cdot(\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}\geq p}\cdot A(c_{\alpha})+\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}\not\equiv 0,1\pmod{p}}B(c_{\alpha})).

(2)

\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}}\cdot\frac{q-p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\cdot q^{-2-(p-2)(c_{\alpha}+1)-\lfloor\frac{c_{\alpha}}{p}\rfloor}.

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}<\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}-1}\cdot\frac{q-1}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\cdot q^{-2}\cdot\Big(

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{e_{F}\geq p-1}\cdot A\Big(\Big\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\Big\rceil\Big)-\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}\geq p-1}A(c_{\alpha}+1)

\displaystyle+\mathbbm{1}_{\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\rceil\not\equiv 0,1\pmod{p}}B\Big(\Big\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\Big\rceil\Big)-\mathbbm{1}_{c_{\alpha}\not\equiv-1,0\pmod{p}}B(c_{\alpha}+1)\Big).

We saw in the proof of Corollary

\big\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\big\rceil\equiv 0,1\pmod{p}
if and only if

\mathbbm{1}_{\lceil\frac{pe_{F}}{p-1}\rceil\not\equiv 0,1\pmod{p}}=\mathbbm{1}_{(p-1)\nmid e_{F}}.

The result
[

\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p-1]{-p})=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})

\mathbbm{1}_{(p-1)\mid e_{F}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mu_{p}\subseteq F}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mu_{p}\subseteq F},

and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.29

m_{1}+2m_{2}=m\end{subarray}}\Big(\#\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{2})/F,m_{1}}^{\mathcal{A}}\Big)\Big(\#\textnormal{\'{E}t}_{(1^{2})/F,m_{2}}^{\mathcal{A}}\Big).

\Sigma
be the set of pairs

(1)

\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}

(2)

(2)

(\overline{\chi}_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2})

\#\Sigma=\Big(\#\Big(\frac{V}{M+N}\Big)\Big)^{2}\cdot\Big(\#\Big(\frac{M+N}{N}\Big)-1\Big)\Big(\#\Big(\frac{M+N}{N}\Big)-2\Big).

We evaluate the sizes of the relevant vector spaces.

(1)

Corollary
3.22
tells us that

\#V=2q^{\frac{m}{6}}.

(2)

By the second and third isomorphism theorems for groups, we have

N=\frac{\mathcal{A}U_{F}^{(m/3)}F^{\times 2}}{U_{F}^{(m/3)}F^{\times 2}}\cong\frac{\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{m/3}},

so

\#N=\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{m/3}}.

(3)

We have

M+N=\frac{\mathcal{A}U_{F}^{(m/3-1)}F^{\times 2}}{U_{F}^{(m/3)}F^{\times 2}},

so

\#(M+N)=\Big[\mathcal{A}U_{F}^{(m/3-1)}F^{\times 2}:U_{F}^{(m/3-1)}F^{\times 2}\Big]\cdot\#W_{m/3-1}.

By the second and third isomorphism theorems for groups, we have

\frac{\mathcal{A}U_{F}^{(m/3-1)}F^{\times 2}}{U_{F}^{(m/3-1)}F^{\times 2}}\cong\frac{\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{m/3-1}},

so Corollary

tells us that

\#(M+N)=q\cdot\frac{\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{m/3-1}^{2}}.

It follows that

\#\Sigma=\frac{4q^{\frac{m}{3}-2}}{(\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2})^{2}}\cdot\Big(q\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{m/3}-\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{m/3-1}\Big)\Big(q\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{m/3}-2\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{m/3-1}\Big).

∎

Definition 4.39

Lemma 4.49
.

Let
F
be a
2
-adic field, let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq F^{\times}

be a nonnegative integer. Given choices for

\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})

The first algorithm is by brute-force. For each

and each
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})

4.22
and
4.46

We now describe the second algorithm. Using Lemma
4.45
, fix a basis for
F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
. Also using Lemma
4.45
, for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})

with time complexity
O_{F}(\#\mathcal{G}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}_{2}])
. Moreover, using Lemma
4.45
, we can quickly express these generating sets in terms of our fixed basis for
F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
, and by Lemma
4.40
we can reduce all of these generating sets to bases with time complexity

O_{F}(\#\mathcal{G}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]^{3}).

Define the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector subspace

By Lemmas
4.22
,
4.45
, and
4.46
, we can compute the intersection
\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})\cap N_{\omega}
with time complexity
O_{F}(\#\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]^{3})
.
Taking successive intersections, Lemma
4.48
tells us that we can compute a basis of

with time complexity

O_{F}(\#\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]^{3}).

Write
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}=\mathcal{G}_{4}(\mathcal{A})\setminus N_{\omega}

Let

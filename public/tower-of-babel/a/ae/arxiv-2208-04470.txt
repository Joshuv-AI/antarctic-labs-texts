Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
Robert Conte

Abstract
We establish a one-to-one correspondence between, on one hand the four types of transcendental meromorphic solutions of the autonomous Schwarzian differential equations which are elliptic, on the other hand the four binomial equations of Briot and Bouquet possessing an elliptic solution.



When looking for first order ordinary differential equations (ODEs)
whose general solution is singlevalued,
Briot and Bouquet
[
1
,
7
]
enumerated and integrated all
first order binomial equations having such a property,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\hbox{d}u}{\hbox{d}z}\right)^{k}=R(u),

(1)

with
k
a positive integer and
R
a rational function.

Modulo
homographic transformations (which leave (
1
) form-invariant),
only four of them are elliptic,
and their general solution is a homographic transform of, respectively,

\wp
,
\wp^{\prime}
,
\wp^{2}
,
\wp^{3}
,
where
\wp
denotes the elliptic function of Weierstrass.

From another point of view,
namely the growth at infinity of meromorphic solutions of ODEs,
another class of ODEs also form-invariant under homographic transformations
has been thoroughly studied,
this class is made of the autonomous Schwarzian differential equations

\displaystyle\left\{u;z\right\}^{p}=R(u),\left\{u;z\right\}=\frac{u^{\prime\prime\prime}}{u^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{u^{\prime\prime}}{u^{\prime}}\right)^{2},

(2)

with
p
a positive integer and
R
a rational function.

Ishizaki
[
8
]
enumerated all ODEs (
2
)
possessing particular transcendental meromorphic solutions.

Modulo
homographies, there are only six of them,
whose canonical form with an equal number
(counting multiplicity) of poles and zeroes in
R
is,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\left\{u;z\right\}=c\frac{(u-\sigma_{1})(u-\sigma_{2})(u-\sigma_{3})(u-\sigma_{4})}{(u-\tau_{1})(u-\tau_{2})(u-\tau_{3})(u-\tau_{4})}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}}\\
\displaystyle{\left\{u;z\right\}^{3}=c\frac{(u-\sigma_{1})^{3}(u-\sigma_{2})^{3}}{(u-\tau_{1})^{2}(u-\tau_{2})^{2}(u-\tau_{3})^{2}}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}}\\
\displaystyle{\left\{u;z\right\}^{2}=c\frac{(u-\sigma_{1})^{2}(u-\sigma_{2})^{2}}{(u-\tau_{1})^{2}(u-\tau_{2})(u-\tau_{3})}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}}\\
\displaystyle{\left\{u;z\right\}^{3}=c\frac{(u-\sigma_{1})^{3}(u-\sigma_{2})^{3}}{(u-\tau_{1})^{3}(u-\tau_{2})^{2}(u-\tau_{3})}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}}\\
\displaystyle{\left\{u;z\right\}=c\frac{(u-\sigma_{1})(u-\sigma_{2})}{(u-\tau_{1})(u-\tau_{2})}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}}\\
\displaystyle{\left\{u;z\right\}=c,}\end{array}\right.

where
c
,
\tau_{j}
,
\sigma_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,4
are complex constants,
the
\tau_{j}
’s being distinct and
the
\sigma_{j}
’s depending on the
\tau_{j}
’s.

Then two of us
[
10
]
found explicitly
all the transcendental meromorphic solutions of Eqs. (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
),
except those solutions of Eq. (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
)
5
that have no Picard exceptional values.
One of the key points in the derivation of these solutions is to show that each of them
obeys a first order differential equation of the form (
1
)
by applying Nevanlinna theory
[
9
]
on the value distribution of meromorphic functions
and the Laurent expansions around poles.
Nevertheless, for Eq. (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
)
5
, the argument in
[
10
]

only works when
u
possesses at least one Picard exceptional value.
Then transcendental meromorphic solutions of Eqs. (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
) can be obtained
by solving the corresponding first order differential equations.

We remark that all transcendental meromorphic solutions of Eqs. (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
) given in
[
10
]
belong to class
W
,
which consists of elliptic functions and their degeneracies,
i.e. functions
R(z)
or
R(e^{az}),a\in\mathbb{C}
, where
R
is a rational function.
As shown by Eremenko
[
6
]
, functions in class
W

constitute all transcendental meromorphic solutions of a class of
autonomous algebraic differential equations
[
3
,
6
,
12
]
.
It turns out that Ermenko’s method can be further generalized to a wider class
of differential equations
[
4
,
5
]
.

For the last equation of (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
), all the transcendental meromorphic solutions
are the three-parameter homographic transforms of
e^{az}

(or equivalently
\coth(az/2)
),
c=-a^{2}/2
.

For the first four equations in (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
),
all the transcendental meromorphic solutions are a one-parameter elliptic function.
Since the solutions
u(z)
of Theorems 5 and 6 in Ref.
[
10
]

obey the respective ODEs

\displaystyle\hbox{(Thm 5) }\left(\frac{\hbox{d}u}{\hbox{d}z}\right)^{3}=L^{3}u^{2}(u-1)^{2}(u+1)^{2},

(10)

\displaystyle\hbox{(Thm 6) }\left(\frac{\hbox{d}u}{\hbox{d}z}\right)^{4}=L^{4}u^{2}(u-1)^{3}(u+1)^{3},

(11)

they admit the general solutions

\displaystyle\hbox{(Thm 5) }u(z)=-\frac{2L^{3}}{27\wp^{\prime}(z-z_{0})}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}

(12)

\displaystyle\hbox{(Thm 6) }u(z)=1+\frac{L^{4}}{32(\wp^{2}(z-z_{0})-L^{4}/64)}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}

(13)

with
z_{0}
arbitrary.

Therefore all the transcendental meromorphic solutions of the first four equations in the list (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
)
are the most general homographic transforms of, respectively,

\wp(z-z_{0})
,
\wp^{\prime}(z-z_{0})
,
\wp^{2}(z-z_{0})
,
\wp^{3}(z-z_{0})
.
This establishes a one-to-one correspondence with the above recalled result of Briot and Bouquet.

Choosing the identity for the above mentioned homographic transformations,
one deduces a second canonical form of equations (
Equivalence of two canonical lists of elliptic functions
),

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left\{u;z\right\}&=\displaystyle{-\frac{3}{8}\frac{(4u^{2}+g_{2})^{2}+32g_{3}u}{4u^{3}-g_{2}u-g_{3}}}&u=\wp(z,g_{2},g_{3})\\

\left\{u;z\right\}&=\displaystyle{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}}&u=\displaystyle{\frac{c_{1}e^{az}+c_{2}}{c_{3}e^{az}+c_{4}}}\cdot\end{array}\right.

Remark
.
The first five equations (
14
) have no other singlevalued solutions
than the above mentioned one-parameter meromorphic functions.
Indeed,
each of them admits at least one Laurent series
whose Fuchs indices
[
2
]
are
j=-1
and two irrational numbers,
respectively

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u&=z^{-2}+\dots&\hbox{indices }(j+1)(j^{2}-j-3)=0,\\
u&=-2z^{-3}+\dots&\hbox{indices }(j+1)(j^{2}-j-8)=0,\\
u&=z^{-4}+\dots&\hbox{indices }(j+1)(j^{2}-j-15)=0,\\
u&=z^{-6}+\dots&\hbox{indices }(j+1)(j^{2}-j-35)=0,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{u-ia}}&=\displaystyle{\frac{2i}{a^{3}}}z^{-2}+\dots&\hbox{indices }(j+1)(j^{2}-j-3)=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

(15)

thus lowering by two the maximal number (three) of arbitrary constants
in any singlevalued solution.

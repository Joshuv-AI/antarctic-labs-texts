Generalizations of Burch Ideals and Ideal-Periodicity
Tejas Rao

Abstract




Consider a minimal free resolution of a module
M
over a local Noetherian ring
R
. Over such rings, resolutions are often infinite, for example by the The Auslander-Buchsbaum formula when
\operatorname{depth}(R)=0

[
1
]
. The question of periodicity in infinite resolutions is the subject of intensive research for example in the works of Eisenbud, Peeva, and Gasharov, and the central survey of Avramov
[
6
,
5
,
7
]
.

The weaker question of whether the ideals of minors of maps in these resolutions are periodic is more recent. Dao, Kobayashi, and Takahashi, introduced an invariant of depth
0
rings called the Burch Index, among other things proving that certain conditions allowed for direct summands to be present in a step in a resolution
[
4
]
. Applying and these techniques, Eisenbud and Dao showed that the
1\times 1
minors of modules over a depth
0
local ring
R
of embedding dimension
\geq 2
are periodic provided that the Burch index is at least
2

[
3
]
. More specifically, they showed that in this case the syzygies
\operatorname{syz}_{n}^{R}(M)
in the resolution have
k
as a direct summand for all sufficiently large
n
, and simultaneously that the ideals of
1\times 1
minors are asymptotically all
\mathfrak{m}

[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
. Brown, Dao, and Sridhar further researched this
ideal-periodicity
, proving
2
-periodicity over complete intersections and Golod rings
[
2
]
.

The case of periodicity for Burch index
0
and
1
local depth
0
rings is still open, and will be a major part of this paper. We will introduce certain generalizations of Burch Indices, which allow one to prove periodicity in classes of Burch Index
1
and
0
rings.
In addition, these generalizations often make sense in positive depth Noetherian local rings, and periodicity is proven in some such rings as well. Extensive calculations are utilized, entirely in Macaulay2
[
8
]
.

Throughout this paper we consider regular local rings
(S,\mathfrak{n},k)
with
S=k[[x_{1},...,x_{n}]]
, and corresponding reductions
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
with
R=S/I
. We will write minimal
R
-resolutions
\epsilon:(F_{\bullet},A_{\bullet})\rightarrow M
and consider the
R
-ideals generated by
1\times 1
minors of the matrices:
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{1})
. The primary object of study is the
N
-Burch Ideal

\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I):=\mathfrak{n}I:(I:N)

and corresponding
N
-Burch Index

\operatorname{Burch}_{N}(I):=\operatorname{length}_{S}(N/(\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I)\cap N))

We will consider iterated Burch Indices

\operatorname{Burch}^{j}(I):=\operatorname{Burch}_{\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I)}(I)

where
\operatorname{BI}^{0}(I):=\mathfrak{m}
. Let
n
be the first index such that
\operatorname{Burch}^{n}(I)=0
and consider the generalized Burch Index, given as

\operatorname{gb}(I)=\begin{cases}\max\{\operatorname{Burch}^{j}(I)\}_{j<n}&\text{if $n\neq 1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

If there is no such
n
, we take the supremum of all
\operatorname{Burch}^{j}(I)
. Also let Burch depth be

\operatorname{bd}(I)=\sup\{j|\operatorname{Burch}^{i}(I)=1\text{ for }i\leq j\}

With this terminology, the primary theorem of Eisenbud and Dao’s paper on Burch Rings, Theorem
4.1
, states that any resolution
\epsilon:(F_{\bullet},A_{\bullet})\rightarrow M
over a ring
R=S/I
for which
\operatorname{gb}(I)\geq 2
and
\operatorname{bd}(I)=0
, satisfies
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{m})=\mathfrak{m}
for
m>>0

[
3
]
. The first main result of this paper extends this to arbitrary Burch Depth:

Theorem 1
.

If
\operatorname{gb}(I)\geq 2
, then all minimal resolutions
\epsilon:(F_{\bullet},A_{\bullet})\rightarrow M
of modules over
R
satisfy
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{m})+\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{m+1})=N
for some fixed ideal
N
and
m>>0
. In particular,
N=\operatorname{BI}^{j}(I)
for some
0\leq j\leq\operatorname{bd}(I)
.

To prove this we will utilize two key propositions, Lemma
2
and Lemma
4
. This theorem will be proven in Section

. Parsing what

implies, we must find ideals

and

. Here is such an example:

Example 1
.

Let

. Notice that the rings

, and
\operatorname{Burch}^{n}(I)=m
(one can in fact take any

, and when

). In particular, one can compute

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}^{1}(I)

\displaystyle=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{m-1},y^{2})

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}^{2}(I)

\displaystyle=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{m-1},y^{3})

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}^{n-1}(I)

\displaystyle=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{m-1},y^{n})

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}^{n}(I)

\displaystyle=I+(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{m-1})^{2}

\displaystyle(I:n)

\displaystyle=(x_{1}y,x_{2}y,...,x_{m-1}y,y^{n})

One may also note that

. Thus,

. In particular,

and
\operatorname{bd}(I)=n
.

Our second main result is related to a notion of
untwisting
. In particular, we develop under certain conditions a column-wise Burch approach, where we need only positive of
\operatorname{Burch}_{\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(c)}(I)
for
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(c)
the ideal generated by entries of some column in a matrix in a resolution, in Lemma

. Intuitively, the ideals

to, under certain conditions, break apart these columns into smaller ideals

. This culminates in the second main result of this paper:

Theorem 2
.

Fix
S=k[[x_{1},...,x_{n}]]
and an ideal
I
. Assume for each

, there exists some

and there exists some

\alpha x_{j}
and

are minimal generators of

. Then for any minimal

-resolution
\epsilon:(F_{\bullet},A_{\bullet})\rightarrow M
, if for each

there exists an index
m
such that
(0)\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(c_{m})\subset(x_{j})
,

\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{a})=\mathfrak{m}

for
a>>0
.

This theorem applies to some Burch Index
0
rings, as well as positive depth local rings:

Example 2
.

Let
S=k[[x,y,z,w]]
and
I=(xz,yz,zw,xw)
. The conditions of the above theorem are satisfied, but as

. In particular,
\operatorname{BI}(I)=0
. We have (after modding out by

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}_{(x)}(I)

\displaystyle=(xy,x^{2})

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}_{(y)}(I)

\displaystyle=(w^{2},yw,y^{2},xy,x^{2})

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}_{(z)}(I)

\displaystyle=(z^{2})

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}_{(w)}(I)

We choose columns contained in each Burch ideal to test the above theorem. In particular, consider the resolution

N
-Burch Ideals

In this section we will flesh out some of the details of the introduction, and prove some initial results. Throughout this paper, we let
(S,\mathfrak{n},k)
be a regular local ring, and for an ideal
I\subset S
write
R=S/I
as a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
. The main thrust of the paper of Eisenbud and Dao is to consider ideals of the form

\displaystyle\operatorname{BI}(I)

\displaystyle=I\mathfrak{n}:(I:\mathfrak{n})

called the
Burch Ideal

[
3
]
. Eisenbud and Dao restrict to the case where
\operatorname{depth}(S/I)=0
and
I\neq 0
so that

\mathfrak{n}^{2}\subset\operatorname{BI}(I)\subset\mathfrak{n}

This allows us to define the Burch index as

\operatorname{Burch}(I)=\dim_{k}(\mathfrak{n}/BI_{S}(I))

If we instead start with an arbitrary depth
0
local ring
R
, we can write
\hat{R}=S/I
as some minimal regular presentation of the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\hat{R}
, and compute
\operatorname{Burch}(R)=\operatorname{Burch}(I)
. Similarly, we write
\operatorname{BI}(R)=\operatorname{BI}(I)\hat{R}\cap R
. Theorem
2.3
of Eisenbud and Dao states that
\operatorname{Burch}(R),\operatorname{BI}(R)
are well-defined, independent of choice of presentation
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
.

The main result of
[
3
]
is the following

Theorem 3

(Eisenbud and Dao (Theorem 4.1))
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring of depth
0
and embedding dimension
\geq 2
. For every non-free
R
-module
M
:

(1) If
\operatorname{Burch}(R)\geq 2
then
k
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{syz}_{i}^{R}(M)

for some
i\leq 5
and for all
i\geq 7

(2) If
\operatorname{Burch}(R)\geq 1
and
k
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{syz}_{s}^{R}(BI(R))
for some
s\geq 1
,

then
k
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{syz}_{i}^{R}(M)
for some
i\leq s+4
and for all
i\geq s+6
.

Throughout this paper, we let
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{j})
be the
R
-ideal generated by the
1\times 1
minors of the
j
-th matrix

in some minimal free resolution. When the embedding dimension of

is at least
2
, we have that
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{j})=\mathfrak{m}
for all

, when the respective conditions of the above theorem are met, thus tying these results directly to ideal-periodicity.

The Theorem above indicates that the resolution of interest is that of the Burch Ideal
\operatorname{BI}(R)
. In particular, if

is direct summand of
\operatorname{syz}_{s}^{R}(\operatorname{BI}(R))
, then we get a similar result to the Burch Index

and greater cases. However, Eisenbud and Dao show this is not always the case

]

Example 3

(Eisenbud and Dao (Ex 4.5))
.

Let
S=k[[a,b]]
,

. One can check

has Burch index
1
. Let

. Then
\operatorname{syz}_{1}^{R}(M)=M^{\oplus 2}
, indicating no syzygy has

Thus
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{j}))=(a,b^{2})
for all

the matrices in some minimal free resolution of

. Thus

Proposition 1
.

There exist rings

with
\operatorname{Burch}(R)=1
such that
\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{1})\neq\mathfrak{m}
for some module

and any

.

We begin weakening the restriction on the original Burch Ideal definitioin. However, we still restrict to the case where
I\neq 0
for non-triviality, and remark that periodicity of
1\times 1
minors is well understood in the regular local ring case. We initially care about cases where
\operatorname{depth}(S/I)=0
. The reason is twofold. First, from the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, if the projective dimension of

is finite, then

\operatorname{pd}(M)+\dim R=\operatorname{depth}(M)

Thus if the projectve dimension of

is finite,

is free. Second, this condition, along with

, ensures that
(I:\mathfrak{n})
is a proper ideal of
R
, as it is well known a local Noetherian ring

is depth

for some nonzero

. In particular this allows us to form the bounds

\mathfrak{n}^{2}\subset\operatorname{BI}(I):=\mathfrak{n}I:(I:\mathfrak{n})\subset\mathfrak{n}

However, we will also consider positive depth rings

in this paper, in which case
\operatorname{BI}(I)=R
since
(I:\mathfrak{n})=I
. When definitions and theorems differ for positive depth rings, we will make a disclaimer.

Let
I,N\subset S
be ideals. We introduce

\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I):=\mathfrak{n}I:(I:N)

Note that unlike in the normal Burch ideal case,
\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I)
is not necessarily contained in

(I:N)\supset(I:J)
for all

, and this containment need not be strict. Thus let

\mathfrak{n}J^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I)\subset J^{\prime}

Example 4
.

Let
S=k[[x,y]]
,

, and

. Then
(I:(y))=(x,y)=(I:\mathfrak{n})
. In particular, the a priori bounds we have on

\mathfrak{n}^{2}\subset\operatorname{BI}_{(y)}(I)\subset\mathfrak{n}

. Of course, here
\operatorname{BI}_{(y)}(I)=\mathfrak{n}^{2}\not\subset(y)
.

Further, when
\operatorname{depth}(R)=0
, we let

\operatorname{Burch}_{N}(I)=\operatorname{length}_{S}(N/(\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I)\cap N))

\operatorname{BI}^{0}(I)=\mathfrak{n}

\operatorname{BI}^{j}(I)=\mathfrak{n}I:(I:\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I))=\operatorname{BI}_{\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I)}(I),\text{ }j>0

\operatorname{Burch}^{j}(I)=\operatorname{length}_{S}(\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I)/\operatorname{BI}^{j}(I)),\text{ }j>0

Proposition 2
.

\operatorname{Burch}^{j}(I)
is well-defined.

Proof.

We must check that
\operatorname{BI}^{j}(I)\subset\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I)
. We use induction. When

, this is true as
\operatorname{BI}^{0}(I)=\mathfrak{m}
and
\operatorname{BI}^{1}(I)\subset\mathfrak{m}
by the conditions on

\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I)\subset\operatorname{BI}^{j-2}(I)

\operatorname{BI}^{j}(I)=nI:(I:\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I))\subset nI:(I:\operatorname{BI}^{j-2}(I))=\operatorname{BI}^{j-1}(I)

since

whenever

.
∎

In the positive depth case, we keep the above definitions the same, except
\operatorname{Burch}^{1}(I):=\operatorname{length}_{S}(\mathfrak{n}/(\operatorname{BI}(I)\cap\mathfrak{n}))=0
. This then yields
\operatorname{Burch}^{j}(I)=0
, further noting that
\operatorname{BI}^{j}(I)=R
for

. As positive depth rings seem to quite ’un-Burch’ rings, one may expect that periodicity is impossible to prove with Burch techniques. We show in Section

Remark 1
.

If
\operatorname{Burch}(I)\neq 0
, then
\operatorname{depth}(R)=0

Burch Duality and Burch Closure

Definition 1
.

Consider an
m\times n
matrix
A
. Throughout this paper let
[x]_{p}
be the
m\times 1
vector with
x
as the
p
-th entry, and
0
elsewhere.

Remark 2
.

We often denote the reduction of
S
-ideals
N
simply as
N
. Similarly we drop the reduction notation and interchangeably consider
x\in S
an element of both
S
and
R
.

Definition 2

(Realization Set)
.

The Realization Set of an ideal
N
,
\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)
, is the set of elements
x^{*}\in(I:N)
such that
x^{*}N\not\subset\mathfrak{n}I
. The Realized Set of an ideal
N
is the difference

\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)=N-(\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I)\cap N)

of sets.

We also identify all elements in the realization and realized sets, respectively, that differ by multiplication by a nonzero element of
k
.
Note that
\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)
is nonempty iff
\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I)\cap N\subsetneq N
and also iff
\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)
is nonempty, both by definition. Thus the following remark:

Remark 3
.

\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)\neq\emptyset\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)\neq\emptyset\Leftrightarrow\operatorname{Burch}_{N}(I)>0

We say
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)

realizes

x\in\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)
if
x^{*}x
is a minimal generator of
I
.

Example 5
.

Let
S=k[[x,y,z]]
,
I=(x^{2}y,xy^{2}z,z^{3})
, and
N=(x^{2},y,z^{2})
. Then
\operatorname{BI}_{N}(I)=(x,z^{2},yz,y^{2})
,

\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)=(x^{2},y,z^{2})-(x,z^{2},yz,y^{2})\cap(x^{2},y,z^{2})=(y)-(yz,y^{2})

Since
(I:N)=(x^{3},xyz,x^{2}y)
and here

are precisely the elements of
(I:N)
that
realize

y
,

\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)=\{xyz\}

Lemma 1

(Burch Duality)
.

\operatorname{Burch}_{N}(I)>0\Rightarrow\operatorname{Burch}_{(I:N)}(I)>0

Further, choose
x\in\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)\neq\emptyset
. Then
x\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}((I:N))
. In particular, if
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)
realizes
x\in\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)
, then
x\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}((I:N))
realizes
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{r}_{I}((I:N))
.

Proof.

Assume
\operatorname{Burch}_{N}(I)>0
. Let
x\in\mathfrak{r}_{I}(N)
. Then
x(I:N)\subset I
and yet
x(I:N)\not\subset\mathfrak{n}I
. Thus
xx^{*}
is a minimal generator of
I
for some
x^{*}\in(I:N)
. In particular,
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)
.

Now consider
\operatorname{BI}_{(I:N)}(I)=\mathfrak{n}I:(I:(I:N))
. Since
x(I:N)\in I
,
x\in(I:(I:N))
. But then since
xx^{*}
is a minimal generator of
I
,
x\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}((I:N))
. Because
x^{*}(I:(I:N))\not\subset\mathfrak{n}I
this also shows
x^{*}\not\in\operatorname{BI}_{(I:N)}(I)
, and since
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)\subset(I:N)
,
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{r}_{I}((I:N))
.
∎

Remark 4
.

The Realized Set of

is the set of elements

such that

is a minimal generator of
I
, for some
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{R}_{I}(N)\subset(I:N)

\operatorname{BI}_{I}(N)

\operatorname{BI}_{I}(N)(I:N)\subset\mathfrak{n}I
.

We use this duality to prove a certain general periodicity. To better understand the conditions of this lemma, consider Corollaries

\displaystyle x^{*}R\subset\operatorname{\mathscr{I}}_{1}(A_{m+(2a-1)})

3

We are now equipped to prove the result on generalized Burch Index. First we need a helper lemma.

Lemma 3

(Minimal Generators of Tor)
.

Consider

of
R

Proof.

Because

is a free
R

and thus

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}x_{m}e_{m}\otimes f_{m}\mod\operatorname{im}(A_{j+1}\otimes Y)

(Positive Depth)

One straightforward way to get

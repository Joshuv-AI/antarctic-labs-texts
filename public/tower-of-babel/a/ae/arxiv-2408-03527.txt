Deformed Intersections of Half-spaces


Abstract
This paper is devoted to the classification problems concerning extended deformations of convex polyhedra and real hyperplane arrangements in the following senses: combinatorial equivalence of face posets, normal equivalence on normal fans of convex polyhedra, and sign equivalence on half-spaces. The extended deformations of convex polyhedra arise from parallel translations of given half-spaces and hyperplanes, whose normal vectors give rise to the so-called “derived arrangement” proposed by Rota as well as Crapo in different forms. We show that two extended deformations of convex polyhedra are normally (combinatorially, as a consequence) equivalent if they are parameterized by the same open face of the derived arrangement. Note that these extended deformations are based on parallel translations of the given hyperplanes. It allows us to study three deformations of real hyperplane arrangements: parallel translations, conings, and elementary lifts, whose configuration spaces are parameterized by open faces of the derived arrangement. Consequently, it gives a characterization of the normal, combinatorial, and sign equivalences of those three deformations via the derived arrangement. Additionally, the relationships among these three equivalence relations are discussed, and several new descriptions of real derived arrangements associated with faces and sign vectors of real hyperplane arrangements are provided. Keywords: Convex polyhedron, hyperplane arrangement, deformation, parallel translation, coning, elementary lift, derived arrangement. MSC classes: 52C35, 52B05, 52C40.

Introduction

Convex polyhedra are very interesting geometric objects with rich combinatorial structures. Their study is closely linked to various combinatorial objects, such as hyperplane arrangements and (affine) oriented matroids. Recently, research on deformations of polytopes is greatly flourishing, with new and exciting ideas continuously emerging. A deformation of a polytope
P
is also a polytope obtained from
P
by translating the facets of
P
without passing a vertex. As the most prominent example, the generalized permutohedra are deformations of a regular permutohedron, initially introduced by Edmonds in 1970 under the name of polymatroids
[
15
]
and rediscovered by Postnikov in 2009
[
33
]
. Topics related to deformations of polytopes have attracted a lot of research attentions, such as Aguiar-Ardila
[
1
]
, Ardila-Sanchez
[
3
]
, Castillo-Liu
[
11
]
, Lee-Liu
[
23
]
, Padrol-Pilaud-Poullot
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
, etc. Their primary focus is on studying the descriptions, combinatorial structures and algebraic structures of deformations and deformation cones for various interesting polytopes including permutohedra, nestohedra, graphical zonotopes, among others. However, our primary interest lies in investigating the classification problem concerning normal and combinatorial equivalences on extended deformations of convex polyhedra that arise from parallel translations of given half-spaces and hyperplanes. Notably, these extended deformations correspond to the faces of parallel translations of a given linear arrangement. This correspondence enables us to explore a similar topic related to normal, combinatorial and sign equivalence classes in three types of deformations of real hyperplane arrangements: parallel translation, coning and elementary lift. Since sign vectors of real hyperplane arrangements naturally induce (affine) oriented matroids in
[
9
]
, these associated questions are also studied in (affine) oriented matroids.

The deformations of a polytope precisely form a polyhedral cone under dilation and Minkowski sum, called the deformation cone
[
33
]
. Postnikov, Reiner and Williams
[
34
]
offered five equivalent ways to characterize deformations of a simple polytope, which naturally lead to different descriptions for the deformation cone including vertex deformation cone, edge length deformation cone, facet deformation cone, among others. Most recently, Castillo and Liu
[
11
]
not only extended their methods to general polytopes, but also introduced two additional equivalent definitions for the deformations of polytopes. Their definition in
[
11
, Definition 2.2]
, based on the idea of “moving facets without passing vertices”, provided a crucial technique for parameterizing the deformation cone of a polytope using the height deformation cone.

Inspired by Castillo-Liu’s work, we define and examine extended deformations of convex polyhedra. To this end, we always work with
m
fixed non-zero vectors
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2},\ldots,\bm{u}_{m}
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
U
be a fixed
m\times n
real matrix with row vectors
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2},\ldots,\bm{u}_{m}
in turn. For any subset
I\subseteq[m]
, denote by
U_{I}
the submatrix of
U
consisting of rows indexed by
I
, and by
\bm{a}_{I}
the subvector of
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
according to the same convention. We define
(\bm{a},I,J,K)
as a tetrad, where
I,J,K
is always a partition of the set
[m]
and
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Associated with each such tetrad
(\bm{a},I,J,K)
, we consider the (possibly empty) convex polyhedron
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

P(\bm{a},I,J,K):=\big\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid U_{I}\bm{x}=\bm{a}_{I},\;U_{J}\bm{x}\leq\bm{a}_{J},\;U_{K}\bm{x}\geq\bm{a}_{K}\big\}.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is nonempty. A nonempty convex polyhedron
P
is an
extended deformation
of
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
if
P
can be written as
P=P(\bm{b},I,J,K)
for some
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In this sense, the vector
\bm{b}
is called an
extended deforming vector
for
P
.

Clearly each extended deformation of a convex polyhedron is parameterized by the corresponding extended deforming vector, which is not necessarily unique as illustrated in
Example 3.16
. However, our definition provides a optimal and particularly precise framework for characterizing the normal, combinatorial and sign equivalence classes in the three deformations of real hyperplane arrangements as discussed in
Section 4
.

In addition, the deformations of a polytope can equivalently be described by normal fans. Namely, a polytope
Q
is a deformation of a polytope
P
if the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(Q)
is a coarsening of the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(P)
in
[
11
, Proposition 2.6]
. To study the deformation cone of a Coxeter permutohedron, the authors in
[
5
]
extended the concept of deformation from polytopes to convex polyhedra, and also introduced ‘extended deformation’ of convex polyhedra. Specifically, a convex polyhedron
Q
is an ‘extended deformation’ of a convex polyhedron
P
if the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(Q)
coarsens a convex subfan of
\mathcal{N}(P)
in
[
5
, Definition 2.3]
. As stated in
Example 3.16
, the two mentioned definitions of the extended deformation of convex polyhedra are not completely equivalent. According to our definition, if
Q
is an extended deformation of a convex polyhedron
P
, then the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(Q)
either coarsens a convex subfan of the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(P)
or a convex subfan of
\mathcal{N}(Q)
is refined by
\mathcal{N}(P)
.

Two convex polyhedra
P
and
Q
are said to be
normally equivalent
(
combinatorially equivalent
, resp.) if they have the same normal fan (their face posets are isomorphic, resp.). Another terms that are sometimes used instead of ‘normally equivalent’ are ‘analogous’
[
4
]
that is a very important technique for proving the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality or ‘strongly isomorphic’ and ‘strongly combinatorially equivalent’
[
27
,
28
]
. From this perspective, the interior of the deformation cone of a polytope
P
precisely corresponds to a special class of normally equivalent polytopes consisting of those deformations with the same normal fan as
P
, referred to as the type cone
[
27
]
. In general,
[
35
, Theorem 2.4]
implied that deforming vectors in each open face of the height deformation cone exactly determine a class of normally equivalent polytopes, where these polytopes share the same normal fan. This phenomenon triggers the following result, showing that the convex polyhedra parameterized by the same open face of the derived arrangement
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
are normally and combinatorially equivalent. The derived arrangement
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is defined in (
2.5
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F
be a face of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
and
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
. Then for any partition
I,J,K
of
[m]
,

P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
and
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)
are normally and combinatorially equivalent.

Note that a face in a real hyperplane arrangement is a convex polyhedron. So the term ‘normally equivalent’ is naturally extended to real hyperplane arrangements.
Roughly speaking, two hyperplane arrangements
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are
normally equivalent
if there is an order-preserving bijection between their faces such that each corresponding face pair shares the same normal fan. We say that two real hyperplane arrangements
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are
sign equivalent
(
combinatorially equivalent
,
semi-lattice equivalent
, resp.) if their faces have the same signs (their face posets are isomorphic, their semi-lattices are isomorphic, resp.). Detailed definitions can be found in
Definition 2.2
. The result in (
4.6
) quickly tells us that the normal and sign equivalences are stronger than the combinatorial and semi-lattice equivalences for real hyperplane arrangements.

Our other main goal is to address classification problems in the three deformations of real hyperplane arrangements: parallel translation, coning and elementary lift. We always regard
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
as the linear arrangement

\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}:=\big\{H_{\bm{u}_{i}}:\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{x}\rangle=0\mid i=1,2,\ldots,m\big\}

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that may be a multi-arrangement. For any
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we say that

•

a
parallel translation

\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined as

\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}:=\big\{H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}:\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{x}\rangle=a_{i}\mid i=1,2,\ldots,m\big\};

•

a
coning

c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
is a linear arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
given by

c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}:=\big\{cH_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}:\bm{\langle}\bm{u}_{i},\bm{x}\rangle+a_{i}\cdot x_{n+1}=0\mid i=1,2,\ldots,m\big\}\textstyle\bigsqcup\limits\{K_{0}:x_{n+1}=0\};

•

an
elementary lift

\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is a linear arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
defined to be

\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}:=\big\{H_{(\bm{u}_{i},a_{i})}:\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{x}\rangle+a_{i}\cdot x_{n+1}=0\mid i=1,2,\ldots,m\big\}.

Recently, Chen, Fu and Wang in
[
19
,
12
]
provided a full description of the semi-lattice equivalence classes in the three deformations by the derived arrangement
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
. Here we uniformly characterize the normal or combinatorial classes in the corresponding deformations using the same geometric structure
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
F
be a face of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
and
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
. Then

(i)

the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
are normally and combinatorially equivalent;

(ii)

the conings
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
are combinatorially equivalent;

(iii)

the elementary lifts
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{b}}
are combinatorially equivalent.

Furthermore, we also show that every open face of the derived arrangement
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
corresponds precisely to a unique sign equivalence class of the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
, conings
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and elementary lifts
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}
belong to the same open face of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
;

(ii)

the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
are sign equivalent;

(iii)

the conings
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
are sign equivalent;

(iv)

the elementary lifts
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{b}}
are sign equivalent.

Notice that the derived arrangement
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
decomposes the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
into finitely many open faces, that is,

\mathbb{R}^{m}=\bigsqcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})}\mbox{\rm relint}(F),

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
is the face poset of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
and
\sqcup
denotes disjoint union. Then
Theorem 1.3
and
Theorem 1.4
show that each of the configuration spaces of parallel translation
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
, coning
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and elementary lift
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}
parameterized by
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, is partitioned into at most
|\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})|
equivalence classes based on any one of the combinatorial, sign or normal equivalences.

Additionally, an (affine) oriented matroid, as described in
[
9
]
, uses the covector axiomatization to model the combinatorial structure of sign vectors of real hyperplane arrangements. This allows us to address analogous questions in (affine) oriented matroids in
Section 5
.

The derived arrangement is a key tool in our study, originating from Crapo’s introduction of the ‘geometry of circuits’ to characterize the matroid
M(m,n,\mathcal{C})
of circuits of the configuration
\mathcal{C}
of
m
generic points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Subsequently, Manin-Schechtman
[
26
]
defined the same object as higher braid arrangements, known as ‘discriminantal arrangement’. Most recently, the authors in
[
12
]
introduced derived arrangements as a geometric model of derived matroids, and further explained that the derived arrangement can be viewed as a generalization of the discriminant arrangement. The concept of derived matroid was first proposed by Rota
[
37
]
, and explicitly defined for binary matroids by Longyear
[
25
]
and for general representable matroids by Oxley-Wang
[
29
]
. In
Section 6
, we also achieve several alternative characterizations for derived arrangements associated with faces and sign vectors of real arrangements.

Organization.

Section 2
sets necessary foundations on convex polyhedra and hyperplane arrangements. In
Section 3
, we study extended deformations of convex polyhedra, and prove

.
Section 4
is devoted to showing

and

Section 5

Preliminaries

In this section, we review necessary notations and definitions on convex polyhedra and real hyperplane arrangements.

2.1. Notations.

Here we shall fix our notations. For any non-zero vector
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and real number

, notations
H_{\bm{u}},H_{\bm{u},a},H_{\bm{u},a}^{-}
,
H_{\bm{u},a}^{+}
,
\mathring{H}_{\bm{u},a}^{-}
and
\mathring{H}_{\bm{u},a}^{+}
refer to a linear hyperplane

H_{\bm{u},a}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\bm{u},\bm{x}\rangle=a\},

the closed half-spaces

and the open half-spaces

\mathring{H}_{\bm{u},a}^{-}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\bm{u},\bm{x}\rangle<a\},\quad\mathring{H}_{\bm{u},a}^{+}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\bm{u},\bm{x}\rangle>a\}

in turn, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote by
\mbox{\rm supp}(\bm{x})
the support of
\bm{x}
given by

\mbox{\rm supp}(\bm{x}):=\big\{i\in[m]:x_{i}\neq 0\big\},

where
[m]:=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. The relative interior of a subset
A
of a topological space is denoted by
\mbox{\rm relint}(A)
. For convenience, we always assume
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
and
\bm{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
for any
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

2.2. Convex polyhedra.

Terminology on convex polyhedra mainly refers to books
[
16
,
39
]
. A
convex polyhedron

P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces. Particularly, if these closed half-spaces pass all through the origin
\bm{o}
, then the convex polyhedron
P
is called a
polyhedral cone
. A bounded convex polyhedron is called a
polytope
. A hyperplane
H
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
supporting hyperplane
of
P
if
P\cap H\neq\emptyset
and
P
is contained in one of the closed half-spaces
H^{-}
and
H^{+}
. In this case, we call the set
P\cap H
a
face
and
\mbox{\rm relint}(P\cap H)
an
open face
of
P
respectively. By convention,
\emptyset
and
P
are
improper faces
of
P
. Faces of convex polyhedra form an important poset. A
face poset

\mathcal{F}(P)
of
P
is a set of all faces, partially ordered by inclusion. A member
F
in
\mathcal{F}(P)
is called a
k
-face
if

\dim(F):=\dim\big(\mbox{\rm aff}(P)\big)=k,

where
\mbox{\rm aff}(P)
denotes the minimum affine subspace containing
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
(\dim(P)-1)
-faces of
P
are called
facets
. By convention,
\dim(\emptyset)=-1
.

A
support function

h_{P}(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
of
P
is defined as

h_{P}(\bm{u}):=\sup\big\{\langle\bm{u},\bm{x}\rangle:\bm{x}\in P\big\},\quad\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By convention,
H_{\bm{0}}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
F(P,\bm{u}):=H_{\bm{u},h_{P}(\bm{u})}\cap P
. If
h_{P}(\bm{u})=\infty
, both sets
H_{\bm{u},h_{P}(\bm{u})}
and
F(P,\bm{u})
are empty. Otherwise, the hyperplane
H_{\bm{u},h_{P}(\bm{u})}
is a supporting hyperplane of
P
. In this sense,
F(P,\bm{u})
is exactly the corresponding face of
P
.

Let
P
be a convex polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
normal cone

N_{P}(\bm{x})
of
P
at
\bm{x}
is defined by

N_{P}(\bm{x}):=\big\{\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\bm{x}\in H_{\bm{u},h_{P}(\bm{u})}\big\}.

When
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
are two relative interior points of a face
F\in\mathcal{F}(P)
, clearly
N_{P}(\bm{x})=N_{P}(\bm{y})
. For this reason, the
normal cone

N_{P}(F)
of
P
at a nonempty face
F
is defined as

N_{P}(F):=N_{P}(\bm{x})

for some
\bm{x}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
. The
normal fan

\mathcal{N}(P)
of
P
is the collection of its normal cones, i.e.,

\mathcal{N}(P):=\big\{N_{P}(F)\mid F\mbox{ is a nonempty face of }P\big\}.

The
support
of
\mathcal{N}(P)
is the union of normal cones in
\mathcal{N}(P)
, denoted by
|\mathcal{N}(P)|
.

We now introduce the normal and combinatorial equivalence relations on convex polyhedra, which will be discussed later.

Definition 2.1
.

Two convex polyhedra
P
and
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are said to be:

•

combinatorially equivalent
if their face posets are isomorphic, that is
\mathcal{F}(P)\cong\mathcal{F}(Q)
;

•

normally equivalent
if they have the same normal fan, that is
\mathcal{N}(P)=\mathcal{N}(Q)
.

2.3. Real hyperplane arrangements.

Terminology on hyperplane arrangements mainly refers to the literature
[
40
]
. A
real hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in an
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When the intersection of all hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
is nonempty,
\mathcal{A}
is called a
central arrangement
and the intersection is referred to as a
center
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}
is
linear
if the center contains the origin
\bm{o}
. For any
X\in L(\mathcal{A})
, the
localization
of
\mathcal{A}
at
X
is the subarrangement

\mathcal{A}_{X}:=\{H\in\mathcal{A}\mid X\subseteq H\},

and the
restriction

\mathcal{A}/X
of
\mathcal{A}
on
X
is a hyperplane arrangement in
X
defined by

\mathcal{A}/X:=\{H\cap X\neq\emptyset\mid H\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{X}\}.

There are two important posets related to real hyperplane arrangements: intersection poset (semi-lattice) and face poset. The
intersection poset
or
semi-lattice

L(\mathcal{A})
consists of all nonempty intersections of some hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
, ordered by the reverse inclusion, and including
\mathbb{R}^{n}:=\bigcap_{H\in\emptyset}H
as the minimum element. In general,
L(\mathcal{A})
is a semi-lattice, and it is a lattice if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is central. The
complement
of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

M(\mathcal{A}):=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}}H.

It consists of finitely many connected components, called
regions
of
\mathcal{A}
. More generally, for any
k
-dimensional element
X
in
L(\mathcal{A})
, each region of
\mathcal{A}/X
is a
k
-dimensional open face
of
\mathcal{A}
, and its closure is referred to as a
k
-dimensional face
of
\mathcal{A}
. The
face poset

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
is the set of all faces, partially ordered by inclusion. It serves as a bridge between real hyperplane arrangements and (affine) oriented matroids, see
Section 5
for details.

A hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
decomposes the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into a finite number of relatively open topological cells (open faces). Each open face is completely determined by its position information relative to every hyperplane
H_{i}
: it either lies on the hyperplane, lies on its positive side, or lies on its negative side. This leads us to associate a sign vector with every (open) face. For any point
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a
sign vector
of
\bm{x}
induced by
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x}):=\big(\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})_{1},\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})_{2},\ldots,\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})_{m}\big),

where the
i
-th component is determined as follows:

\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})_{i}:=\begin{cases}+,&\mbox{if }\quad\bm{x}\in\mathring{H}_{i}^{+},\\
0,&\mbox{if }\quad\bm{x}\in H_{i},\\
-,&\mbox{if }\quad\bm{x}\in\mathring{H}_{i}^{-},\end{cases}

for
1\leq i\leq m
. This sign vector encodes the position of
\bm{x}
with respect to each hyperplane. Thus all points lying in an open face of
\mathcal{A}
have the same sign vector. For this reason, we define a
sign vector
of a face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
to be

\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F):=\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})

(2.1)

for some
\bm{x}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
. Go one step further, we define a
sign
of
\mathcal{A}
as

\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}):=\big\{\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})\mid\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\big\}.

Alternatively,
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})
can be represented as the following form

\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})=\big\{\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\mid F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\big\}.

(2.2)

We now define four types of equivalence relations on real hyperplane arrangements, which will be studied in
Section 4
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
be a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\{H_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,H_{m}^{\prime}\}
be a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are said to be:

•

semi-lattice equivalent
if their semi-lattices are isomorphic, that is
L(\mathcal{A})\cong L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
;

•

combinatorially equivalent
if their face posets are isomorphic, that is
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
;

•

sign equivalent
if they have the same sign, that is
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})=\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
.

When
n=n^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are said to be:

•

normally equivalent
if there is an order-preserving bijection
\Psi
from
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
to
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
such that
F
and
\Psi(F)
are normally equivalent for any face
F
of
\mathcal{A}
. In this sense, the map
\Psi
is called a
normal map
from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

2.4. Derived arrangement.

The derived arrangement is a key ingredient to obtain our results. Associated with the linear arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
, let

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}):=\big\{C\subseteq[m]\mid\{\bm{u}_{i}\mid i\in C\}{\;\rm is\;a\;minimum\;linearly\;dependent\;set}\big\}.

(2.3)

Each member of
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
is said to be a
circuit
of
[m]
with respect to
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
. It is clear that for every
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
, there is a unique vector
\bm{c}^{C}=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
(up to a nonzero constant multiple) such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\bm{u}_{i}=\bm{0}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad c_{i}\neq 0\Longleftrightarrow i\in C,

(2.4)

called a
circuit vector
of
C
. The
derived arrangement

\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is a linear arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined as

\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}:=\big\{H_{\bm{c}^{C}}:\langle\bm{c}^{C},\bm{x}\rangle=0\mid C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})\big\}.

(2.5)

3
Extended deformations of convex polyhedra

In this section, we show that all extended deformations of a convex polyhedron
P
retain the same boundedness as
P
in
Corollary 3.8
, and also prove
Theorem 1.2
.

3.1
Boundedness

We start by introducing Farkas’ lemma which serves as a crucial tool for determining both the solvability of inequality systems and the boundedness of convex polyhedra.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
22
]
, Farkas’ lemma)
.

Let
A
be a
m\times n
real matrix and
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then exactly one of the following statements is true

1.

there exists a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{a}
,

2.

there exists a row vector
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}
satisfying
\bm{y}A=\bm{0}
and
\langle\bm{y},\bm{a}\rangle<0
.

The following general Farkas’ lemma for equality and (strict) inequality constraints can be derived from
Lemma 3.1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A_{i}
be a
m_{i}\times n
real matrix and
\bm{a}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,5
. Then exactly one of the following statements is true

1.

there exists a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

A_{1}\bm{x}=\bm{a}_{1},\quad A_{2}\bm{x}\leq\bm{a}_{2},\quad A_{3}\bm{x}<\bm{a}_{3},\quad A_{4}\bm{x}\geq\bm{a}_{4}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad A_{5}\bm{x}>\bm{a}_{5},

2.

there exist row vectors
\bm{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,5
satisfying that

\bm{y}_{i}\geq\bm{0}\;(i=1,2),\quad\bm{y}_{j}\leq\bm{0}\;(j=4,5),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{5}\bm{y}_{i}A_{i}=\bm{0},

and

\sum_{i=1}^{5}\langle\bm{y}_{i},\bm{a}_{i}\rangle<0\quad\mbox{\rm~or~}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{5}\langle\bm{y}_{i},\bm{a}_{i}\rangle=0\mbox{\rm~and~}\bm{y}_{3}\neq\bm{0}\mbox{\rm~or~}\bm{y}_{5}\neq\bm{0}.

Proof.

Clearly the statement 1 is equivalent to that there exist
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

t\geq 1,\quad A_{1}\bm{x}=t\bm{a}_{1},\quad A_{2}\bm{x}\leq t\bm{a}_{2},\quad A_{3}\bm{x}\leq t\bm{a}_{3}-\bm{1},\quad-A_{4}\bm{x}\leq-t\bm{a}_{4}\;\mbox{\rm~and~}\;-A_{5}\bm{x}\leq-t\bm{a}_{5}-\bm{1}.

Namely, there exist
(\bm{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying

\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}&-1\\
A_{1}&-\bm{a}_{1}\\
-A_{1}&\bm{a}_{1}\\
A_{2}&-\bm{a}_{2}\\
A_{3}&-\bm{a}_{3}\\
-A_{4}&\bm{a}_{4}\\
-A_{5}&\bm{a}_{5}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{x}\\
t\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}-1\\
\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}\\
-\bm{1}\\
\bm{0}\\
-\bm{1}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.1)

For convenience, we denote the matrix on the left by
\tilde{A}
and the vector on the right by
\tilde{\bm{a}}
. From
Lemma 3.1
, the system in (
3.1
) is infeasible if and only if there exists a row vector

\tilde{\bm{y}}=(y_{0},\bm{y}_{1}^{+},\bm{y}_{1}^{-},\bm{y}_{2},\bm{y}_{3},\bm{y}_{4}^{-},\bm{y}_{5}^{-})\in\mathbb{R}^{1+m_{1}+m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}+m_{4}+m_{5}}

satisfying

\tilde{\bm{y}}\geq\bm{0},\quad\tilde{\bm{y}}\tilde{A}=\bm{0}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad\langle\tilde{\bm{y}},\tilde{\bm{a}}\rangle<0.

(3.2)

Let
\bm{y}_{1}=\bm{y}_{1}^{+}-\bm{y}_{1}^{-}
,
\bm{y}_{4}=-\bm{y}_{4}^{-}
and
\bm{y}_{5}=-\bm{y}_{5}^{-}
. Then (
3.2
) becomes

y_{0}\geq 0,\quad\bm{y}_{i}\geq\bm{0}\;(i=2,3),\quad\bm{y}_{j}\leq\bm{0}\;(j=4,5),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{5}\bm{y}_{i}A_{i}=\bm{0}

and

\sum_{i=1}^{5}\langle\bm{y}_{i},\bm{a}_{i}\rangle=-y_{0},\quad-\langle\bm{y}_{3},\bm{1}\rangle+\langle\bm{y}_{5},\bm{1}\rangle<y_{0}.

If
y_{0}>0
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{5}\langle\bm{y}_{i},\bm{a}_{i}\rangle<0
and
-\langle\bm{y}_{3},\bm{1}\rangle+\langle\bm{y}_{5},\bm{1}\rangle\leq 0<y_{0}
always holds.
If
y_{0}=0
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{5}\langle\bm{y}_{i},\bm{a}_{i}\rangle=0
and
-\langle\bm{y}_{3},\bm{1}\rangle+\langle\bm{y}_{5},\bm{1}\rangle<0
means
\bm{y}_{3}\neq\bm{0}
or
\bm{y}_{5}\neq\bm{0}
. Then we obtain that the system in (
3.1
) is infeasible if and only if the statement 2 holds. So we complete this proof.
∎

Recall from (
1.1
) that the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
are partitioned into the adjoint union of open faces of the derived arrangement
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
. This demonstrates that for each member
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, there is a unique face
F
of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
satisfying
{\bm{x}}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
, denoted by
F_{\bm{x}}
. Indeed,
F_{\bm{x}}
is the inclusion-minimum face of
\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
containing
{\bm{x}}
. The following two lemmas are crucial for establishing the close connections among faces of extended deformations of convex polyhedra.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
and
F_{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
. Then
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
for any
\bm{b}\in F_{\bm{a}}
.

Proof.

We give a proof by contradiction. Suppose
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)=\emptyset
. This is equivalent to the insolvability of the following inequality systems

U_{I}\bm{x}=\bm{b}_{I},\quad U_{J}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}_{J}\;\mbox{\rm~and~}\;U_{K}\bm{x}\geq\bm{b}_{K}.

(3.3)

Applying
Proposition 3.2
to (
3.3
), it is further equivalent to that there exists a row vector
\bm{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

satisfying

\bm{y}_{J}\geq\bm{0},\quad\bm{y}_{K}\leq\bm{0},\quad\bm{y}U=\bm{0}\;\mbox{\rm~and~}\;\langle\bm{y},\bm{b}\rangle<0.

(3.4)

From the definition of the circuit vector in (
2.4
), we have that each circuit vector
\bm{c}^{C}
satisfies
\bm{c}^{C}U=\bm{0}
for
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
. Note that
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
is the set of inclusion-minimum nonempty supports of members in the solution space of the equation
\bm{x}U=\bm{0}
. Hence, there exists a circuit
C_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
such that
C_{0}\subseteq\mbox{\rm supp}(\bm{y})
and its circuit vector
\bm{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
satisfying
\bm{c}U=\bm{0}
. Suppose
\bm{c}_{J}\geq\bm{0}
and
\bm{c}_{K}\leq\bm{0}
and
\langle\bm{c},\bm{b}\rangle<0
, then we arrive at
\langle\bm{c},\bm{a}\rangle<0
via
\bm{b}\in F_{\bm{a}}
. By
Proposition 3.2
, we obtain
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)=\emptyset
, a contradiction. Therefore, we can assume that
\bm{c}
coincides with one of the following both cases:

•

\bm{c}_{J}
contains negative entries, or
\bm{c}_{K}
contains positive entries and
\langle\bm{c},\bm{b}\rangle\leq 0
;

•

\bm{c}_{J}=\bm{0},\bm{c}_{K}=\bm{0}
and
\langle\bm{c},\bm{b}\rangle=0
.

For the former case, let
\lambda=\mbox{\rm min}\big\{-\frac{y_{j}}{c_{j}},-\frac{y_{k}}{c_{k}}\mid c_{j}<0,j\in J\mbox{\rm~and~}c_{k}>0,k\in K\big\}
and
\bm{y}^{\prime}=\bm{y}+\lambda\bm{c}
. For the latter case, let
\lambda=-\frac{y_{i}}{c_{i}}
for some
i\in C_{0}
and
\bm{y}^{\prime}=\bm{y}+\lambda\bm{c}
. Then, in either case,
\bm{y}^{\prime}
always satisfies

\bm{y}^{\prime}_{J}\geq\bm{0},\quad\bm{y}^{\prime}_{K}\leq\bm{0},\quad\bm{y}^{\prime}U=0,\quad\langle\bm{y}^{\prime},\bm{b}\rangle<0\;\mbox{\rm~and~}\;\mbox{\rm supp}(\bm{y}^{\prime})\subsetneqq\mbox{\rm supp}(\bm{y}).

If
\bm{y}^{\prime}
is not a circuit vector of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
, then as with
\bm{y}
, we can repeat the same step to yield a row vector
\bm{y}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with the same properties as (
3.4
) and
\mbox{\rm supp}(\bm{y}^{\prime\prime})\subsetneqq\mbox{\rm supp}(\bm{y}^{\prime})
. After finitely many steps, we eventually obtain a circuit vector
\bm{y}^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying (
3.4
). This implies
\langle\bm{y}^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}},\bm{a}\rangle<0
from
\bm{b}\in F_{\bm{a}}
. Using
Proposition 3.2
again, we arrive at
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)=\emptyset
, which contradicts the assumption
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
. So we get
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Associated with a tetrad
(\bm{a},I,J,K)
, we give a (possibly empty) relatively open convex polyhedron
\mathring{P}(\bm{a},I,J,K)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to be

\mathring{P}(\bm{a},I,J,K):=\big\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid U_{I}\bm{x}=\bm{a}_{I},\;U_{J}\bm{x}<\bm{a}_{J},\;U_{K}\bm{x}>\bm{a}_{K}\big\}.

Using the same method as in the proof of
Lemma 3.3
, we also achieve the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathring{P}(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
and
F_{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
. Then
\mathring{P}(\bm{b},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
for any
\bm{b}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F_{\bm{a}})
.

We now proceed to describe the boundedness of convex polyhedra.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
. Then
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is unbounded if and only if there exist
\bm{u}\in\mbox{\rm relint}\big(P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\big)
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
such that
\{\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}\subseteq P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
.

Proof.

For sufficiency, since
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
contains a ray
\{\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}
,
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is unbounded. Next, we verify the necessity by negation. Suppose
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\cap\{\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}\neq\{\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}
for any
\bm{u}\in\mbox{\rm relint}\big(P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\big)
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
. Since
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is a closed convex set, there is
r_{\bm{u},\bm{v}}\geq 0
such that

P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\cap\{\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}=[\bm{u},\bm{u}+r_{\bm{u},\bm{v}}\bm{v}],

where
[\bm{u},\bm{u}+r_{\bm{u},\bm{v}}\bm{v}]
denotes the segment from
\bm{u}
to
\bm{u}+r_{\bm{u},\bm{v}}\bm{v}
. Given
\bm{u}_{0}\in\mbox{\rm relint}\big(P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\big)
, we have

\displaystyle P(\bm{a},I,J,K)

\displaystyle=P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\bigcap\bigcup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\{\bm{u}_{0}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}

\displaystyle=P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\bigcap\bigcup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}[\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{0}+r_{\bm{u}_{0}}\bm{v}]

\displaystyle\subseteq B^{n}(\bm{u}_{0},r_{\bm{u}_{0}}),

where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid||\bm{x}||=1\}
is the unit sphere, and
B^{n}(\bm{u}_{0},r_{\bm{u}_{0}})
is the ball with centre
\bm{u}_{0}
and radius
r_{\bm{u}_{0}}
given by

r_{\bm{u}_{0}}=\sup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\big\{r_{\bm{u}_{0},\bm{v}}\mid P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\cap\{\bm{u}_{0}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}=[\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{0}+r_{\bm{u}_{0},\bm{v}}\bm{v}]\big\}.

This means that
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is bounded, a contradiction. So
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is unbounded.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
. Then there exist
\bm{u}\in\mbox{\rm relint}\big(P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\big)
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
such that
\{\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}\subseteq P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
if and only if there is
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
such that
\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{v}\rangle=0
,
\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{v}\rangle\leq 0
and
\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}\rangle\geq 0
for each
i\in I
,
j\in J
and
k\in K
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
I=\big\{i\in[m]\mid P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\big\}
. For necessity, we first assert that
\bm{u}+r\bm{v}
belongs to
\mathring{H}^{-}_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}
(
\mathring{H}^{+}_{\bm{u}_{k},a_{k}}
, resp.) for any
r\geq 0
and
j\in J
(
k\in K
, resp.). Arguing by negation, suppose there are
r_{0}>0
and
j_{0}\in J
satisfying
\bm{u}+r_{0}\bm{v}\in H_{\bm{u}_{j_{0}},a_{j_{0}}}
, i.e.,
\langle\bm{u}_{j_{0}},\bm{u}+r_{0}\bm{v}\rangle=a_{j_{0}}
.
Noting
\langle\bm{u}_{j_{0}},\bm{u}\rangle<a_{j_{0}}
and
r_{0}>0
, then

\langle\bm{u}_{j_{0}},\bm{v}\rangle=\frac{a_{j_{0}}-\langle\bm{u}_{j_{0}},\bm{u}\rangle}{r_{0}}>0.

Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
\langle\bm{u}_{j_{0}},\bm{u}+(r_{0}+\varepsilon)\bm{v}\rangle>a_{j_{0}}
. Namely,
\bm{u}+(r_{0}+\varepsilon)\bm{v}\notin P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
for all
\varepsilon>0
, which contradicts
\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\in P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
for
r\geq 0
. So,
\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\in\mathring{H}^{-}_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}
for
r\geq 0
and
j\in J
. Using the same argument as the former case, we can show
\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\in\mathring{H}^{+}_{\bm{u}_{k},a_{k}}
for
r\geq 0
and
k\in K
as well.
Therefore, the assertion holds. In other words, the assertion says that

\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\rangle<a_{j}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\rangle>a_{k}

for
r\geq 0
,
j\in J
and
k\in K
. This implies
\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{v}\rangle\leq 0
and
\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}\rangle\geq 0
for
j\in J
and
k\in K
. In addition, for
i\in I
and
r\geq 0
, we have
\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}\rangle=a_{i}
and
\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\rangle=a_{i}
. So
\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{v}\rangle=0
for any
i\in I
.

For sufficiency, taking
\bm{u}\in\mbox{\rm relint}\big(P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\big)
, clearly
\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}\rangle=a_{i}
,
\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{u}\rangle\leq a_{j}
and
\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}\rangle\geq a_{k}
for each
i\in I
,
j\in J
and
k\in K
. Together with conditions
\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{v}\rangle=0
,
\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{v}\rangle\leq 0
and
\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}\rangle\geq 0
for
i\in I
,
j\in J
and
k\in K
, we have

\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\rangle=a_{i},\quad\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\rangle\leq a_{j}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\rangle\geq a_{k}

for any
i\in I
,
j\in J
,
k\in K
and
r\geq 0
. Namely, the ray
\{\bm{u}+r\bm{v}\mid r\geq 0\}
is contained in
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

The next theorem states that the convex polyhedra
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
and
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)
are either both bounded or unbounded whenever
\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}
come from the same face of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
and
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in F
. For any partition
I,J,K
of
[m]
,
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is a polytope
(
an unbounded convex polyhedron, resp.
)
if and only if
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)
is a polytope
(
an unbounded convex polyhedron, resp.
)
.

Proof.

From
Lemma 3.3
, we have that
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
. Suppose
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
. Summarizing
Lemma 3.5
and
Lemma 3.6
, we acquire that
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
is bounded (unbounded, resp.) if and only if
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)
is bounded (unbounded, resp.). This completes the proof.
∎

For each partition
I,J,K
of
[m]
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)
the collection of all vectors
\bm{a}
satisfying
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
, i.e.,

\mathcal{C}(I,J,K):=\big\{\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset\big\}.

Fillastre and Izmestiev in
[
18
, Theorem 1.30]
showed that the solution set
\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)
is always a polyhedral cone in the parameter space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. This leads us to consider the following result.

Corollary 3.8
.

For any partition
I,J,K
of
[m]
, the boundedness of the convex polyhedra
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
keeps consistent for all
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)
.

Proof.

When
\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)=\emptyset
, the case is trivial. Suppose
\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)
is nonempty. From
Lemma 3.3
, we know that if
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)
, then
P(\bm{y},I,J,K)
is nonempty for any
\bm{y}\in F_{\bm{x}}
with
F_{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
. Namely,
F_{\bm{x}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)
for each
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}(I,J,K)
. Combining
[
18

, this implies that

To prove

Lemma 3.9

(

The faces of a convex polyhedron

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose

. If
F
is a facet of

, then
F=H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\cap P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
for some

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
I=\emptyset
and
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
is a full-dimensional convex polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\bm{x}
be a relative interior of the facet
F
. Note the basic fact that
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
is the disjoint union of the relative interiors of its faces. Then
\bm{x}
is on the boundary of
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
. Since the boundary of
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
is equal to
\bigcup_{i\in[m]}\big(H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)\big)

. Assume

containing

This means that
H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
is a facet of
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
. So
F=H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
.
∎

Suppose
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
. For each
\bm{x}\in P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
, the
active index set
is given by

I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(\bm{x}):=\big\{i\in[m]\mid\bm{x}\in H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\big\}.

If
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}

obviously. For this reason, we define the

of each face

as

I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F):=I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(\bm{x}),\quad\forall\;\bm{x}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F),

and denote by

We call
\big(I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F),J_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F),K_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F)\big)
an

of the face
F
induced by
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
. The following lemma shows that the active triple index completely characterizes a nonempty face
F
of
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
, and also determines the dimension of

.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\neq\emptyset
and
F
be a nonempty face of
P(\bm{a},I,J,K)
. Then we have

F=P\big(\bm{a},I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F),J_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F),K_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F)\big)

and

\dim(F)=n-\mbox{\rm rank}\big\{\bm{u}_{i}\mid i\in I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(F)\big\}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
I=\emptyset
and
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
is a full-dimensional convex polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When
F=P
, the case is trivial. Next we consider that
F
is a proper face. Since
F\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
for each

and
F\subseteq P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
, we have

F\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)}H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\bigcap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K).

Then
H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)\neq\emptyset
for

. So
H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
is a proper face of
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
for each
i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)
. Let

I_{0}=\big\{i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)\mid\dim\big(H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)\big)=n-1\big\}.

According to
Lemma 3.10
, we know that the set of all facets of
P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)
containing

is exactly the set
\big\{H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)\mid i\in I_{0}\big\}
. This is a subset of
\big\{H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)\mid i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)\big\}
. Recall from the property (ii) in

that we have

\bigcap_{i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)}H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\bigcap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I_{0}}\big(H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\cap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)\big)=F.

So
F=\bigcap_{i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)}H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\bigcap P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)

can be written in the form

F=P\big(\bm{a},I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F),J_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F),K_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)\big).

Since
\big(\bigcap_{j\in J_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)}\mathring{H}_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}^{-}\big)\bigcap\big(\bigcap_{i\in K_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)}\mathring{H}_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}^{+}\big)
is an
n
-dimensional open convex polyhedron, we arrive at
\dim(F)=\dim\big(\bigcap_{i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)}H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\big)
. Immediately, we have

\dim(F)=n-\mbox{\rm rank}\big\{\bm{u}_{i}\mid i\in I_{P(\bm{a},\emptyset,J,K)}(F)\big\}

via the elementary linear algebra. This completes the proof.
∎

When vectors
\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}
are from the same open face of the derived arrangement

, we can establish a one-to-one correspondence between the faces of

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
and
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
. Then the map
\Phi:\mathcal{F}\big(P(\bm{a},I,J,K)\big)\to\mathcal{F}\big(P(\bm{b},I,J,K)\big)

and

and

are empty sets, there is nothing to prove. Suppose

is well-defined, injective and surjective in turn. Clearly we have
\Phi(G)=\bigcap_{i\in I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(G)}\big(H_{\bm{u}_{i},b_{i}}\cap P(\bm{b},I,J,K)\big)

for each

is a face of
P(\bm{b},I,J,K)

To verify the injectivity of

I_{P(\bm{b},I,J,K)}\big(\Phi(G)\big)=I_{P(\bm{a},I,J,K)}(G).

Noting

It is worth remarking that the polyhedral cone can be expressed as the

\mbox{\rm pos}\{\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,\bm{u}_{m}\}:=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}\geq 0\Big\}.

By convention, define

. The following is a formula to calculate the normal cones of convex polyhedra, which can be founded in
[

, 6.46 Theorem]

]

Suppose

of

In 2000, Kim and Luc

Lemma 3.14

(
[
21
]

Let

The next proposition tells us that the normal equivalence is a stronger relation than the combinatorial equivalence for convex polyhedra.

Proposition 3.15
.

If two convex polyhedra
P
and
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are normally equivalent, then they are combinatorially equivalent.

Proof.

According to the definition of the normally equivalent polyhedra in
Definition 2.1
, there is a natural bijection
\Phi:\mathcal{F}(P)\to\mathcal{F}(Q)
satisfying
\Phi(\emptyset)=\emptyset

and
N_{P}(F)=N_{Q}\big(\Phi(F)\big)

F
of

. Given faces

, it follows from

that
\Phi(F)\subseteq\Phi(G)
. Hence,
P
and

∎

and

.

4
Equivalence relations and classifications on real hyperplane arrangements

In the section, we are concerned with two subjects. One of them is to establish explicit relationships among four types of equivalence relations on real hyperplane arrangements: semi-lattice, combinatorial, normal and sign equivalences. The other one aims to describe the equivalence classes of the latter three relations for three types of deformations of the linear arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
: parallel translation
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
, coning
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
, and elementary lift
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}
.

4.1
Equivalence relations on real hyperplane arrangements

Let us first introduce the coning operator of hyperplane arrangements. It allows for comparing affine and linear arrangements, and also serves as a bridge between the signs of affine arrangements and their conings. Associated with an affine hyperplane
H:u_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}x_{n}=a
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define a linear hyperplane
cH
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
to be
cH:u_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}x_{n}+ax_{n+1}=0
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an affine arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
coning

c\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{A}
is a linear arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
consisting of all
cH
with
H\in\mathcal{A}
and an additional hyperplane
K_{0}:x_{n+1}=0
. In other words, the coning of
\mathcal{A}
is the linear arrangement
c\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
consisting of linear spans of affine hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
and a new linear arrangement
K_{0}
that is parallel to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times 1
.

[
41
, Theorem 3.2]
showed that a poset
P
is a geometric semi-lattice if and only if there is a geometric lattice
L
with an atom
a
such that
P=L-\{x\in L\mid a\leq x\}
, where both
L
and
a
are uniquely determined by
P
. It is clear that
K_{0}
is exactly an atom of
L(c\mathcal{A})
such that

L(\mathcal{A})\cong L(c\mathcal{A})-\{X\in L(c\mathcal{A}):X\subseteq K_{0}\}.

Therefore,
[
41
, Theorem 3.2]
directly deduces the following relation

L(\mathcal{A})\cong L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\Longleftrightarrow L(c\mathcal{A})\cong L(c\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

(4.1)

for two real affine arrangements
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. It is also not difficult to check that

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{F}(c\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}(c\mathcal{A}^{\prime}).

(4.2)

When
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are real linear arrangements,
[
2
, Corollary 1.69]
stated that if
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, then
L(\mathcal{A})\cong L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
. Together with (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we can immediately extend the result to real affine arrangements. But, the converse may be not true.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}

(
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, resp.
)
be a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(
\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
, resp.
)
. If
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are combinatorially equivalent, then they are semi-lattice equivalent.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
be a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each face
F
of
\mathcal{A}
, we define an
active index set

I_{\mathcal{A}}(F)
as

I_{\mathcal{A}}(F):=\big\{i\in[m]\mid F\subseteq H_{i}\big\},

and denote by

J_{\mathcal{A}}(F):=\big\{j\in[m]\mid F\subseteq H^{-}_{j},F\nsubseteq H_{j}\big\}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad K_{\mathcal{A}}(F):=\big\{k\in[m]\mid F\subseteq H^{+}_{k},F\nsubseteq H_{k}\big\}.

The triple
\big(I_{\mathcal{A}}(F),J_{\mathcal{A}}(F),K_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\big)
is referred to as an
active triple index
of the face
F
induced by
\mathcal{A}
. In this notation, there is a canonical way to write the face
F
in the following form

F=P\big(\bm{a},I_{\mathcal{A}}(F),J_{\mathcal{A}}(F),K_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\big).

(4.3)

Actually, the sign vector
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)
of a face
F
of
\mathcal{A}
, as defined in (
2.1
), is also characterized by its active triple index. More precisely,
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)
can be written in the following form
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)=\big(\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)_{1},\ldots,\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)_{m}\big)
, where
each
i
-th component is

\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)_{i}=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if }\quad i\in I_{\mathcal{A}}(F),\\
-,&\mbox{if }\quad i\in J_{\mathcal{A}}(F),\\
+,&\mbox{if }\quad i\in K_{\mathcal{A}}(F).\end{cases}

(4.4)

In addition, the order relation of
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
has an easy combinatorial description in terms of the sign vectors of the faces. Namely,
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}
in
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
if and only if the sign vector of
F_{1}
is obtained from that of
F_{2}
by replacing some
+
or
-
. Let
\leq
be the partial order on the set
\{+,-,0\}
defined as
0<+,-
with
+
and
-
incomparable. This further induces the
sign order

\leq
on
\{\pm,0\}^{m}
, that is, for any members
\bm{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}),\bm{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in\{\pm,0\}^{m}
,
\bm{s}\leq\bm{w}
if and only if
s_{i}\leq w_{i}
for each
i\in[m]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
be a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}
in
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
if and only if
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\leq\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\leq\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2})
. This means

I_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\supseteq I_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2}),\quad J_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\subseteq J_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2})\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad K_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\subseteq K_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2}).

(4.5)

It follows from (
4.3
) that

F_{1}=P\big(\bm{a},I_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1}),J_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1}),K_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\big)\subseteq P\big(\bm{a},I_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2}),J_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2}),K_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2})\big)=F_{2}.

Conversely, suppose
F_{1},F_{2}
are the faces of
\mathcal{A}
and
F_{1}
is a nonempty face of
F_{2}
. Then the active triple indices of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
satisfy the same relations as in (
4.5
). Following (
4.4
), we have
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\leq\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2})
, which completes the proof.
∎

With
Proposition 4.1
and
Lemma 4.2
, we further achieve the next theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}

(
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\{H_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,H_{m}^{\prime}\}
, resp.
)
be a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(
\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
, resp.
)
. If
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are sign equivalent, then they are combinatorially and semi-lattice equivalent.

Proof.

Recall from (
2.2
) that we have

\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})=\big\{\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\mid F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\big\}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\big\{\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})\mid F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\big\}.

Since
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})=\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, the above equations automatically give rise to a bijection
\Phi:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
such that

\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\big(\Phi(F)\big)=\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F),\quad\forall\,F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}).

So, to prove
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, it can be reduced to showing that
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}
in
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
if and only if
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{1})\leq\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(F_{2})
. It follows from
Lemma 4.2
that this holds. Hence, we arrive at
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
. Combining
Proposition 4.1
, we conclude
L(\mathcal{A})\cong L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
.
∎

Immediately, applying
Theorem 4.3
to (i), (iii) and (iv) in
Theorem 1.4
, we deduce that (ii) and (iii) hold in
Theorem 1.3
. Additionally, we quickly derive the following result from
Proposition 4.1
and
Definition 2.2
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be hyperplane arrangements in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are normally equivalent, then they are combinatorially and semi-lattice equivalent.

We conclude this section by summarizing the following relations:

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\mbox{ are normally or sign equivalent}\Rightarrow\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\Rightarrow L(\mathcal{A})\cong L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}).

(4.6)

Based on the study in

and

Theorem 1.3
that for each face
F
of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
, the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and

\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
.

Proof of (i) in
Theorem 1.3
.

By
Corollary 4.4
, it reduces to showing that
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
are normally equivalent. Note that the expressing form of faces in (
4.3
) induces a map

\Psi:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}),\quad G\mapsto\Psi(G)=P\big(\bm{b},I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G),J_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G),K_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G)\big).

(4.7)

Since
G=P\big(\bm{a},I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G),J_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G),K_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G)\big)
, we have
\Phi(G)\neq\emptyset

. This implies that
\Phi(G)
is a face of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
, that is,
\Psi
is well-defined. It follows from
I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}\big(\Psi(G)\big)=I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G)
in
Lemma 3.12
that we arrive at

\big(I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(G)),J_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(G)),K_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(G))\big)=\big(I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G),J_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G),K_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(G)\big).

(4.8)

Then
G
and
\Psi(G)
are normally equivalent via
Theorem 1.2
.

Next we show that
\Psi
is bijective. According to (
4.3
), we deduce that two faces
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
are identical if and only if
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
share the same active triple index. Then, when

4.8
), we get
\Psi(F_{1})
and
\Psi(F_{2})
have distinct active triple indices as well. This implies
\Psi(F_{1})\neq\Psi(F_{2})
. Namely,
\Psi
is injective. Similarly, define a map

\Psi^{-1}:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}),\quad G\mapsto\Psi^{-1}(G)=P\big(\bm{a},I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(G),J_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(G),K_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(G)\big).

As the roles of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
can be interchanged, we can also ascertain that
\Psi^{-1}
is an injection. Furthermore, the equation (
4.8
) guarantees that
\Psi\big(\Psi^{-1}(G)\big)=G
for any face
G
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
. Hence,
\Psi
is a surjection. Up to now we have proved that
\Psi
is a bijection.

It remains to prove that
\Psi
is an order-preserving map. Suppose
G_{0}
is a proper face of
G
. Then
G_{0}
is also a face of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
.
From (
4.8
),
G_{0}
and
\Psi(G_{0})
(

and
\Psi(G)
, resp.) have the same active triple index. It follows from

that

is a face of

, i.e.,
\Psi
is order-preserving. We complete the proof.
∎

To verify that the results (i) and (ii) in
Theorem 1.4
are equivalent mutually, we build the connection between the circuit vectors and sign vectors of parallel translations of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
. Given a real number
x
, without causing confusion, we also use the notation
\mbox{\rm sign}(x)
to denote the sign of
x
, that is,
\mbox{\rm sign}(x)
equals
+
if
x>0
,
-
if
x<0
,
0
else. Associated with a circuit vector
\bm{c}^{C}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
of
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
, we denote by

S_{\bm{c}^{C}}:=\big\{\bm{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\{\pm,0\}^{m}\mid s_{i}=\mbox{\rm sign}(c_{i}),\forall\,i\in C\big\}.

For convenience, let

C^{+}:=\{i\in C\mid c_{i}>0\}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad C^{-}:=\{i\in C\mid c_{i}<0\}.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\bm{c}^{C}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
be a circuit vector of
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
,
\bm{a}\in\mathring{H}_{\bm{c}^{C}}^{-}
and
\bm{b}\in H_{\bm{c}^{C}}^{+}
. Then

\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\cap S_{\bm{c}^{C}}\neq\emptyset\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})\cap S_{\bm{c}^{C}}=\emptyset.

Proof.

Suppose
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{|C|}
satisfies
\bm{y}U_{C}=0
. Since
\{\bm{u}_{i}\mid i\in C\}
is the minimum linearly dependent set via (
2.3
),
\bm{y}=\lambda\bm{c}^{C}_{C}
for some non-zero real number
\lambda
. Recall from (
2.4
) that
C=C^{+}\sqcup C^{-}
. It follows from

Proposition 3.2
that
\mathring{P}(\bm{a}_{C},\emptyset,C^{-},C^{+})\neq\emptyset
. This implies
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\cap S_{\bm{c}^{C}}\neq\emptyset
. Likewise, we can obtain
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})\cap S_{\bm{c}^{C}}=\emptyset
.
∎

We now proceed to show that the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
have the same sign if and only if the vectors
\bm{a},\bm{b}
come from the same open face of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
in
Theorem 1.4
.

Proof of (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) in
Theorem 1.4
.

To obtain (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii), let the map
\Psi:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})
be defined as in (
4.7
). Since
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
for
F\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
,
\Psi
is a bijection by the proof of (i) in
Theorem 1.3
. Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.8
), we have
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})=\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})
via (
2.2
).

Prove (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) by negation. Suppose
\bm{a}\in\mathring{H}_{\bm{c}^{C}}^{-}
and
\bm{b}\in H_{\bm{c}^{C}}^{+}
for some
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
and
H_{\bm{c}^{C}}\in\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
. It follows from
Lemma 4.5
that
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\neq\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})
, which contradicts the assumption
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})=\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})
. We finish the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.10
further illustrates that the normal and sign equivalences of parallel translations of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
are consistent whenever
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is not a multi-arrangement. The following results are necessary to establish this.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\bm{c}^{C}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
be a circuit vector of
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
and
\bm{a}\in\mathring{H}_{\bm{c}^{C}}^{-}
. Then

P(\bm{a}_{C},\emptyset,C^{-},C^{+})
is a closed region of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}_{C}}:=\{H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\mid j\in C\}
and
\dim\big(P(\bm{a}_{C},\emptyset,C^{-},C^{+})\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}\big)=n-1
for each
i\in C
.

Proof.

From the proof of
Lemma 4.5
, we have
\mathring{P}(\bm{a}_{C},\emptyset,C^{-},C^{+})\neq\emptyset
. This means that
P(\bm{a}_{C},\emptyset,C^{-},C^{+})
is a closed region of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}_{C}}
. For simplicity, let
R:=P(\bm{a}_{C},\emptyset,C^{-},C^{+})
. Given a member
i\in C
, suppose
R\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}^{-}
. We prove
\dim(R\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}})=n-1
by negation. Suppose
\dim(R\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}})\leq n-2
. If
\dim(R\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}})=-1
, i.e.,
R\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}=\emptyset
, then
R\subseteq\mathring{H}_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}^{-}
. Equivalently, the following inequality systems

\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{x}\rangle<a_{j}\mbox{\rm~for~}j\in C^{+},\quad\langle\bm{u}_{j},\bm{x}\rangle>a_{j}\mbox{\rm~for~}j\in C^{-}\setminus\{i\}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad\langle\bm{u}_{i},\bm{x}\rangle<a_{i},

have a solution. As with the proof of
Lemma 4.5
, this is impossible. So
\dim(R\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}})\geq 0
. Let
G=R\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
and
\dim(G)=k\leq n-2
. From
Lemma 3.10
and the property (ii) in
Lemma 3.9
, we have
G=\bigcap_{j\in I_{G}}(H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\cap R)
and
i\notin I_{G}
, where

I_{G}=\big\{j\in C\mid G\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\cap R\mbox{\rm~and~}\dim(H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\cap R)=n-1\big\}.

Since
\big\{\bm{u}_{j}\mid j\in C\setminus\{i\}\big\}
is a linearly independent set, then

2\leq|I_{G}|=n-k\leq|C|-1\leq n.

If
n-k\leq|C|-2
, then
I_{G}\sqcup\{i\}
contains a circuit
C_{0}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
since
G\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
. Then
C_{0}
is a nonempty proper subset of
C
, a contradiction. If
n-k=|C|-1
, then
\bigcap_{j\in C}H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}=G\neq\emptyset
. This means
\bm{a}\in H_{\bm{c}^{C}}
contradicting
\bm{a}\in\mathring{H}_{\bm{c}^{C}}^{-}
. So we obtain
\dim(R\cap H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}})=n-1
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is not a multi-arrangement. Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
be normally equivalent with a normal map
\Psi
. Then each map
\Psi_{i}
defined as

\Psi_{i}:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},b_{i}}),\quad F\mapsto\Psi_{i}(F)=\Psi(F),

is a normal map.

Proof.

Recall from
Lemma 3.13
that the normal cones of all closed regions of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},b_{i}}
are exactly the same vector space
\mbox{\rm span}(\bm{u}_{i})
. Together with that
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is not a multi-arrangement, then the map
\Psi
bijectively sends the closed regions of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
to the closed regions of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},b_{i}}
. Since every face of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
is also a face of some closed region of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
, the order-preserving property of
\Psi
guarantees that
\Psi
sends the faces of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}}
to the faces of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},b_{i}}
. Namely,
\Psi_{i}
is well-defined. According to the definition of
\Psi_{i}
and the property of
\Psi
, we can easily check that
\Psi_{i}
is a normal map.
∎

It is worth noting that given a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a face
F
of
\mathcal{A}
can be also represented as

F=P\big(\bm{a}_{\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},F)},I_{\mathcal{A}}(F),\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(F),\bar{K}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\big),

(4.9)

where
\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(F):=\big\{j\in J_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\mid F\cap H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\neq\emptyset\big\}
,
\bar{K}_{\mathcal{A}}(F):=\big\{k\in K_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\mid F\cap H_{\bm{u}_{k},a_{k}}\neq\emptyset\big\}
, and

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},F)=I_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\sqcup\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\sqcup\bar{K}_{\mathcal{A}}(F).

The triple
\big(I_{\mathcal{A}}(F),\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(F),\bar{K}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)\big)
is referred to as a
valid active triple index
of
F
induced by
\mathcal{A}
. Following
Lemma 3.13
, it is a obvious fact that the normal cone of every nonempty face
F_{0}
of
F
is completely determined by the valid active triple index. Thus, the following result is observed.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is not a multi-arrangement. Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
are normally equivalent if and only if there is an order-preserving bijection
\Psi:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})
such that
F,\Psi(F)
have the same valid active triple index for any
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})
.

Proof.

The sufficiency is trivial. For necessity, let
\Psi
be a normal map from
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
to
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
. According to
Definition 2.2
, it remains to show that
F
and
\Psi(F)
have the same valid active triple index for any
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})
. First
i\in I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)
implies
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},a_{i}})
. From
Lemma 4.7
,
\Psi(F)\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}/H_{\bm{u}_{i},b_{i}})
, i.e.,
i\in I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F))
. Hence,
I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)\subseteq I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F))
. By symmetry, we have
I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)\supseteq I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F))
. So

I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)=I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F)).

For any
j\in\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)
,
F(F,\bm{u}_{j})=H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}\cap F
is a proper face of
F
and
F\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{j},a_{j}}^{-}
. Since the map
\Psi
is order-preserving and
F,\Psi(F)
are normally equivalent, we have

\Psi\big(F(F,\bm{u}_{j})\big)=F\big(\Psi(F),\bm{u}_{j}\big)=H_{\bm{u}_{j},h_{\Psi(F)}(\bm{u}_{j})}\cap\Psi(F)

is a proper face of
\Psi(F)
and
\Psi(F)\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{j},h_{\Psi(F)}(\bm{u}_{j})}^{-}
. Applying
Lemma 4.7
again, we get
H_{\bm{u}_{j},h_{\Psi(F)}(\bm{u}_{j})}\cap\Psi(F)\subseteq H_{\bm{u}_{j},b_{j}}\cap\Psi(F)
. This means
H_{\bm{u}_{j},b_{j}}=H_{\bm{u}_{j},h_{\Psi(F)}(\bm{u}_{j})}
, i.e.,
j\in\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F))
. So
\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)\subseteq\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F))
. By symmetry, we can arrive at
\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)\supseteq\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F))
. Therefore,

\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)=\bar{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F)).

Likewise, we can also acquire
\bar{K}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}}(F)=\bar{K}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}}(\Psi(F))
. We complete the proof.
∎

Given a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
be the collection of the valid active triple indices of all faces of
\mathcal{A}
. Immediately, (
4.9
) establishes a natural one-to-one correspondence between the face poset
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
by mapping each face of
\mathcal{A}
to its corresponding valid active triple index. This leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is not a multi-arrangement. Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the parallel translations
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}

5
Oriented matroids

In this section, we revisit the normal and sign equivalence relations on real hyperplane arrangements from the perspective of (affine) oriented matroids. Oriented matroids, introduced by Bland-Las Vergnas in 1978
[
10
]
, can be thought of as a combinatorial abstraction of real hyperplane arrangements, of polytopes and others. As we are concerned with real hyperplane arrangements, we only consider covectors, and do not discuss other perspectives on oriented matroids. A more general setting and background on oriented matroids can be found in the book
[
9
]
by Björner et al..

Let us start with some necessary terminology. A
sign vector
is a function
X:E\to\{+,0,-\}
, that is, an assignment of signs to every element of
E
. The power set
\{+,0,-\}^{E}
is the collection of all possible sign vectors and
X_{e}
stands for
X(e)
for all
e\in E
. The
support
of
X
is
\underline{X}:=\{e\in E\mid X_{e}\neq 0\}
and its
zero set

z(X):=E\setminus\underline{X}
. The
opposite

-X
of a sign vector
X
is defined as
(-X)_{e}=-(X_{e})
. The
composition

X\circ Y
of two sign vectors
X
and
Y
is defined to be

X\circ Y:=\begin{cases}X_{e},&\mbox{ if }X_{e}\neq 0,\\
Y_{e},&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The
separation set
of two sign vectors
X
and
Y
is

S(X,Y):=\{e\in E\mid X_{e}\neq-Y_{e}\neq 0\}.

With these terminologies, we are now ready to define oriented matroids.

Definition 5.1

(Covector Axioms)
.

An
oriented matroid

\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{L})
is an ordered pair of a finite set
E
and a collection
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\{\pm,0\}^{E}
of sign vectors satisfying:

•

the zero vector
\bm{0}\in\mathcal{L}
,

•

X\in\mathcal{L}
implies
-X\in\mathcal{L}
,

•

X,Y\in\mathcal{L}
implies
X\circ Y\in\mathcal{L}
,

•

if
X,Y\in\mathcal{L}
and
e\in S(X,Y)
, then there exists
Z\in\mathcal{L}
such that
Z_{e}=0
and
Z_{f}=(X\circ Y)_{f}=(Y\circ X)_{f}
for all
f\notin S(X,Y)
.

In this sense, the members of
\mathcal{L}
are called
covectors
of
\mathcal{M}
.

Next, we describe how the motivating model for the covector axiomatization of oriented matroids arises from central real arrangements. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{\bm{u}_{e}}\mid e\in E\}
be a central arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By a quick review of Subsection 2.3, for each point
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the central arrangement
\mathcal{A}
induces a unique sign vector
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})\in\{\pm,0\}^{E}
based on the position information. Clearly
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})=\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{y})
if and only if
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
come from the same open face
\mathcal{A}
. Thus the set consisting of the sign vectors
\mbox{\rm sign}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x})
for all points
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, has only finitely many members, which agrees with
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})
via (
2.2
). Notably,
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})
exactly obeys the covector axiomatization of oriented matroids. Hence, the order pair
\big(E,\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})\big)
automatically forms an oriented matroid, denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
.

In general, affine oriented matroids can be thought of an abstraction of affine real arrangements. Suppose
\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}=\{H_{\bm{u}_{e},a_{e}}\mid e\in E\}
is an affine arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall from
Subsection 4.3
that
\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}
leads to a linear arrangement

c\mathcal{A}=\{cH_{\bm{u}_{e},a_{e}}\mid e\in E\}\sqcup\{H_{\bm{u}_{g}}=K_{0}:x_{n+1}=0\}

in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by coning. Observe from the bijection
\epsilon
in (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and (
4.14
) that

\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})=\big\{\bm{s}_{E}\mid\bm{s}\in\mbox{\rm sign}(c\mathcal{A})\mbox{\rm~and~}\bm{s}_{g}=+\big\}.

Based on the important observation, affine oriented matroids can be naturally obtained from oriented matroids. From
Definition 5.2
, the order pair
\big(E,\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A})\big)
gives an affine oriented matroids on
E
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
as well.

Definition 5.2

(Affine Oriented Matroid)
.

Let
\big(E\sqcup\{g\},\mathcal{L}\big)
be an oriented matroid,
g
be a distinguished element that is not a loop. The order pair
(E,\mathcal{W}\mbox{\rm)}
is an
affine oriented matroid
on
E
, where

\mathcal{W}:=\big\{X_{E}:=(X_{e})_{e\in E}\mid X\in\mathcal{L}\mbox{\rm~and~}X_{g}=+\big\}.

In an unpublished manuscript 1992, Karlander
[
20
]
has given an axiomatization of affine oriented matroids, which provides an intrinsic characterization for such affine sign vector system
\mathcal{W}
, not depending on an ambient oriented matroid. Recently, Baum and Zhu
[
7
]
further reassessed Karlander’s axiomatization of affine oriented matroids.

Two (affine) oriented matroids
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if they have the same covectors, denoted by
\mathcal{M}\sim\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. Immediately, the connection between face posets of real hyperplane arrangements and covectors of (affine) oriented matroids reveals the following fact.

Fact 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{e}\mid e\in E\}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\{H^{\prime}_{e}\mid e\in E\}
be hyperplane arrangements in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are sign equivalent if and only if the corresponding (affine) oriented matroids
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
are equivalent.

For convenience, suppose
E=[m]
and
g=m+1
later. With
Theorem 4.11
and
Fact 5.3
, we have the following result.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
are normally equivalent if and only if
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})
are equivalent up to relabeling.

Furthermore, the following is a direct consequence of
Corollary 4.10
and
Fact 5.3
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
is not a multi-arrangement. Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}}
are normally equivalent if and only if
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})
are equivalent.

Applying
Fact 5.3
to
Theorem 1.4
, we immediately obtain the next corollary.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}
belong to the same open face of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})
and

are equivalent;

(iii)

\mathcal{M}(c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})

Let

\Omega_{p}:=\big\{\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}):\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\big\},\quad\Omega_{c}:=\big\{\mathcal{M}(c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}):\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\big\},\quad\Omega_{e}:=\big\{\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}):\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\big\}.

6
Derived arrangement

The derived arrangement is closely related to combinatorics, algebra, topology, and other fields. For more detailed information, please refer to Athanasiadis
[
6
]
, Bayer-Brandt
[
8
]
, Cohen-Falk-Randell
[
13
]
, Falk
[
17
]
, Libgober-Settepanella
[
24
]
, Sawada-Settepanella-Yamagata
[
38
]
, Yamagata
[
42
]
, Ziegler
[
43
]
, and others. In this section, we provide several new characterizations for real derived arrangements associated with the faces and sign vectors.

Let us first define two operators related to real hyperplane arrangements: face operator and sign operator. Associated with the linear arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
, we define a
sign operator
on each subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(S):=\bigcup_{\bm{a}\in S}\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}).

(6.1)

Conversely, for any vector
\bm{s}\in\{\pm,0\}^{m}
, it yields a subset

\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\bm{s}):=\big\{\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\bm{s}\in\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\big\}

(6.2)

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We further define a
face operator
on each subset
S\subseteq\{\pm,0\}^{m}
to be

\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(S):=\bigcap_{\bm{s}\in S}\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\bm{s}).

First note the following fact from
Lemma 4.5
.

Fact 6.1
.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}
be distinct faces of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
, and
\bm{a}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F_{1}),\bm{b}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F_{2})
. Then

\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\nsubseteq\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{b}})\nsubseteq\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}).

To obtain our results, the next lemma is necessary.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
. Then

\mbox{\rm relint}(F)=\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big).

Proof.

For any
\bm{s}\in\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm relint}(F)\big)
, clearly
\mbox{\rm relint}(F)\subseteq\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\bm{s})
, which means

\mbox{\rm relint}(F)\subseteq\bigcap_{\bm{s}\in\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm relint}(F)\big)}\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\bm{s})=\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big).

We now prove the opposite side. According to (i) and (ii) in
Theorem 1.4
, we deduce

\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm relint}(F)\big)=\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}),\quad\forall\,\bm{x}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F).

Following this, for a fixed
\bm{a}\in\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
, we have

\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big)=\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\big).

This further implies
\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})\subseteq\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}})
for any
\bm{x}\in\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big)
. Together with
Fact 6.1
, we conclude

\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}})=\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}),\quad\forall\,\bm{x}\in\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big).

Once again, we derive from (i) and (ii) in
Theorem 1.4
that each
\bm{x}\in\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big)
belongs to
\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
. Consequently, we have
\mbox{\rm relint}(F)=\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big)
.
∎

With
Lemma 6.2
, we give an alternative characterization of the open faces of real derived arrangements.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
S
be a nonempty subset in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(S)\big)=S
if and only if
S
is a open face of
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
.

Proof.

Lemma 6.2
indicates that the sufficiency holds. For necessity, given a point
\bm{a}\in S
, we assert
S=\mbox{\rm relint}(F_{\bm{a}})
with
F_{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{F}(\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}})
. From (i) and (ii) in
Theorem 1.4
, we have

\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm relint}(F_{\bm{a}})\big)=\mbox{\rm sign}(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}).

Applying
Lemma 6.2
again, we derive
S=\mbox{\rm relint}(F_{\bm{a}})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Furthermore,
Theorem 6.3
directly leads to the following result.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. If
\mbox{\rm Face}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}\big(\mbox{\rm Sign}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}}(\mbox{\rm relint}(F))\big)=\mbox{\rm relint}(F)
for any face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{A}
coincides with the derived arrangement
\delta\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{o}}
.

Notably, by uniformly substituting
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
with either
c\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}}
or
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{a}}
in (
6.1
) and (
6.2
),
Theorem 1.4
ensures that
Theorem 6.3
and
Corollary 6.4
hold in both cases.

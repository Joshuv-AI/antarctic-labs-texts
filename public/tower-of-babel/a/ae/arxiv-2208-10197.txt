Affine perturbation series of the deflection angle of a ray near the photon sphere of a Reissner-Nordström black hole
Naoki Tsukamoto 1

Abstract
We investigate the affine perturbation series of the deflection angle of a ray near the photon sphere of an extreme Reissner-Nordström black hole. We compare the 0 0 th and 1 1 st orders of the affine perturbation series with the deflection angle in a strong deflection limit. We conclude that the 0 0 th order of the affine perturbation series is more accurate than the deflection angle in the strong deflection limit and that we should improve a strong-deflection-limit analysis by using the 0 0 th order of the affine perturbation series.

II
Deflection angle in a Reissner-Nordström spacetime

A line element in a Reissner-Nordström spacetime is given by

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-A(r)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{A(r)}+r^{2}\left(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}\right),

(5)

where
A(r)
is given by

A(r)\equiv 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}.

(6)

It has an event horizon at
r=M+\sqrt{M^{2}-Q^{2}}
for
\left|Q\right|\leq M
.
There are time translational and axial Killing vectors
t^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\partial_{t}
and
\phi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\partial_{\phi}

because of the stationarity and axisymmetry of the spacetime, respectively.
We can assume
\theta=\pi/2
and
Q\geq 0
without loss of generality.

From
k^{\mu}k_{\mu}=0
, where
k^{\mu}\equiv\dot{x}^{\mu}
is the wave-number vector of a ray and
the dot denotes a differentiation with respect to an affine parameter on the orbit of the ray,
the orbit is expressed by

-A(r)\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{\dot{r}^{2}}{A(r)}+r^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}=0.

(7)

At the closest distance
r=r_{0}
, it gives

A_{0}\dot{t}^{2}_{0}=r_{0}^{2}\dot{\phi}_{0}^{2}.

(8)

Here and hereinafter, quantities with the subscript
0
denote the quantities at the closest distance.
From the conserved energy of the ray defined by

E\equiv-g_{\mu\nu}t^{\mu}k^{\nu}=A(r)\dot{t},

(9)

the conserved angular momentum of the ray defined by

L\equiv g_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\mu}k^{\nu}=r^{2}\dot{\phi},

(10)

and Eq. (
8
),
the impact parameter of the ray is obtained as

b(r_{0})\equiv\frac{L}{E}=\frac{r_{0}^{2}\dot{\phi}_{0}}{A_{0}\dot{t}_{0}}=\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-2Mr_{0}+Q^{2}}}.

(11)

Here, we have assumed
L\geq 0
without loss of generality.
The equation of the motion of the ray (
7
) can rewritten as

\dot{r}^{2}+V(r)=0,

(12)

where
V(r)
is the effective potential of the ray defined by

V(r)\equiv E^{2}\left(-1+\frac{A(r)}{r^{2}}b^{2}\right).

(13)

The motion of the ray is permitted in a region satisfying
V(r)\leq 0
.
Let
r_{\mathrm{m}}
be the largest positive solution of
V^{\prime}(r)=0
for
Q<3M/(2\sqrt{2})
.
We obtain
r_{\mathrm{m}}
as

r_{\mathrm{m}}\equiv\frac{3M+\sqrt{9M^{2}-8Q^{2}}}{2}.

(14)

When the ray has a critical impact parameter
b=b_{\mathrm{m}}
(or
r_{0}=r_{\mathrm{m}}
), where

b_{\mathrm{m}}\equiv b(r_{\mathrm{m}})=\frac{r_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}}{\sqrt{r_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}-2Mr_{\mathrm{m}}+Q^{2}}},

(15)

the ray forms an unstable circular light sphere, called a photon sphere, at
r=r_{\mathrm{m}}
and
its effective potential holds
V_{\mathrm{m}}=V_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}=0
and
V_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime\prime}<0
for
Q<3M/(2\sqrt{2})
.
Here and hereinafter, quantities with the subscript m denote the quantities at
r=r_{\mathrm{m}}
or
r_{0}=r_{\mathrm{m}}
.
The ray with
b<b_{\mathrm{m}}
(or
r_{0}<r_{\mathrm{m}}
) goes inside of the photon sphere while
the ray with
b>b_{\mathrm{m}}
(or
r_{0}>r_{\mathrm{m}}
) is scattered on the outside of the photon sphere.
We assume
b>b_{\mathrm{m}}
(or
r_{0}>r_{\mathrm{m}}
) and
Q<3M/(2\sqrt{2})
.

From Eq. (
7
),
the deflection angle of the ray is obtained as

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int^{\infty}_{r_{0}}\frac{bdr}{\sqrt{r^{4}-b^{2}r^{2}+2Mb^{2}r-b^{2}Q^{2}}}-\pi

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int^{h_{0}}_{0}\frac{dh}{\sqrt{G(h)}}-\pi,

where
h
,
h_{0}
, and
G(h)
are defined by
h\equiv M/r
,
h_{0}\equiv M/r_{0}
, and

\displaystyle G(h)\equiv-q^{2}h^{4}+2h^{3}-h^{2}+h_{b}^{2},

(17)

respectively, and where
q
and
h_{b}
are defined by

q\equiv Q/M
and
h_{b}\equiv M/b
.
By using the relation

h_{b}^{2}=q^{2}h_{0}^{4}-2h_{0}^{3}+h_{0}^{2},

(18)

we can rewrite
G(h)
as

\displaystyle G(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h-h_{0})\left[-q^{2}h^{3}+(-q^{2}h_{0}+2)h^{2}\right.

(19)

\displaystyle\left.+(-q^{2}h_{0}^{2}+2h_{0}-1)h+(-q^{2}h_{0}^{2}+2h_{0}-1)h_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-q^{2}(h-h_{0})(h-h_{-1})(h-h_{1})(h-h_{2}),

where
h_{-1}
,
h_{1}
, and
h_{2}
are three of four real solutions of

and

holds.
The deflection angle can be expressed as

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q}\int^{h_{0}}_{h_{-1}}\frac{dh}{\sqrt{-(h-h_{0})(h-h_{-1})(h-h_{1})(h-h_{2})}}

(20)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{q}\int^{0}_{h_{-1}}\frac{dh}{\sqrt{-(h-h_{0})(h-h_{-1})(h-h_{1})(h-h_{2})}}-\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{q\sqrt{(h_{2}-h_{0})(h_{1}-h_{-1})}}\left[K(k)-F\left(\Psi,k\right)\right]-\pi,

where

is the incomplete elliptic integral of the first kind defined by

(21)

is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind defined by

,

(22)

and
\Psi

(23)

We define variables

and

by

(24)

and

(25)

respectively, and where

In the strong deflection limit

,

,

.

\alpha

0

IV
Conclusion

The deflection angle in the
0
th order of the affine perturbation series
\alpha_{\mathrm{0th}}

is more accurate than the deflection angle
\alpha_{\mathrm{SDL}}
in the strong deflection limit
because
\alpha_{\mathrm{SDL}}
has a hidden error.
The hidden error given by
\alpha_{\mathrm{0th}}-\alpha_{\mathrm{SDL}}
is comparable with
1
st order terms when the deflection angle is
\pi
.
This implies that we should improve a strong-deflection-limit analysis for a gravitational lensing by using
\alpha_{\mathrm{0th}}
.
The improvement would not be difficult since we obtain
\alpha_{\mathrm{0th}}
from
\alpha_{\mathrm{SDL}}
by using relation (
30
).

The effective Dirac algebra by gauge field interaction in relativistic electrodynamics
B.T.T.Wong

Abstract
Conventional relativistic electrodynamics is set on flat Minkowski spacetime, where all computable quantities are calculated from the flat metric η μ ​ ν \eta_{\mu\nu} . We can redefine the metric of spacetime from the Dirac algebra. In this paper, we study how an electrodynamic interaction can alter the normal gamma matrix to an effective one and result in a shift in the metric perturbatively. The curvature properties inferred from the curved metric are also investigated. We also study how the spin operator is changed under the interaction that contribute to an effective spin operator and how the spin of an electron will be slightly deviated from 1 / 2 1/2 . Then we perform canonical quantization of the effective Dirac algebra. Finally we apply our results to the relativistic hydrogen case and demonstrate how such system curves the spacetime metric.

Effective Dirac Algebra

We would revisit the Dirac equation and investigate it in a new perspective. For our purpose we will keep the constants
\hbar
and
c

We rewrite the free Dirac equation in the form of

\psi(x)=\frac{i\hbar}{mc}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi(x)=\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi(x)\,,

(8)

where
\lambda=\frac{h}{mc}
is the Compton wavelength. For the Dirac equation with electromagnetic interaction, we rewrite equation (
8

\psi(x)=\frac{i\hbar}{mc}\frac{1}{1+\frac{he}{mc}\gamma^{\rho}A_{\rho}(x)}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi(x)=\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\,\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\gamma^{\rho}A_{\rho}(x)}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi(x)\,.

(9)

Or in tensor notation we have

\psi_{c}=\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\gamma^{\rho}A_{\rho}}\right)_{ca}\gamma^{\mu}_{ab}\partial_{\mu}\psi_{b}\,.

Therefore, with interaction, we have an extra term contributed by the photon gauge field
A_{\mu}

\not{A}=\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}

9

f[\not{A}(x)]=\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\not{A}(x)}\,.

(11)

The functional can be expanded perturbatively,

\displaystyle f[\not{A}(x)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\not{A}}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}}

(12)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{4}e^{4}}{16\pi^{2}}A^{4}+O(e^{6})\bigg)\big(1-\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\not{A}\big)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\not{A}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{3}e^{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\not{A}A^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{4}e^{4}}{16\pi^{2}}A^{4}+O(e^{5})\,.

\not{A}
, where for odd-power series it is
\gamma^{\rho}
dependent, while of even-power series it is independent of
\gamma^{\rho}

f_{\rm{even}}[A_{\rho}]=\sum_{k=0,2,4,\cdots}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\bigg)^{k}A^{k}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}}\,,

(14)

and we have

f[\not{A}]=f_{\rm{even}}[A_{\rho}]-f_{\rm{odd}}[\gamma^{\rho},A_{\rho}]\,.

(15)

Using
c=1
in natural unit, we can define the effective mass
\mathcal{M}(x)

9

\mathcal{M}(x)=m\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}(x)\bigg)\,.

Alternatively, in equation (
9

\Gamma^{\mu}_{L}=f[\not{A}]\gamma^{\mu}\,.

(17)

The subscript
L
indicates that

is on the left-hand side. Yet we can also define the effective gamma matrix by having
f[\not{A}]
on the right hand side, but we need to introduce a commutator for compensating such change

(18)

Then we remain to evaluate the commutator. Using the identity
\{\gamma^{\mu},\not{A}\}=2A^{\mu}

f[\not{A}]\gamma^{\mu}=\Big(\gamma^{\mu}-\frac{\lambda e}{\pi}A^{\mu}\Big)f_{\rm{even}}+\gamma^{\mu}f_{\rm{odd}}\,.

It follows that

[\gamma^{\mu},f[\not{A}]]=\frac{\lambda e}{\pi}A^{\mu}f_{\rm{even}}-2\gamma^{\mu}f_{\rm{odd}}=\frac{\lambda e}{\pi}(A^{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}\not{A})f_{\rm{even}}\,.

(20)

Using equation (
13

[\gamma^{\mu},f[\not{A}]]=\frac{\lambda e}{\pi}\bigg(\frac{A^{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}\not{A}}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}}\bigg)\,.

Therefore the Dirac equation with electromagnetic interaction can be written as order of

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\Gamma^{\mu}_{R}\partial_{\mu}\psi-\frac{i\lambda^{2}e}{2\pi^{2}}\bigg(\frac{A^{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}\not{A}}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}}\bigg)\partial_{\mu}\psi

[\gamma^{\mu},f[\not{A}]\,]=\gamma^{\mu}f[\not{A}]-f[\not{A}]\gamma^{\mu}\implies\gamma^{\mu}f[\not{A}]\,[\gamma^{\mu},f[\not{A}]\,]+f[\not{A}]\gamma^{\mu}

(25)

and substituting equation (
25

\displaystyle\{\Gamma^{\mu}_{L},\Gamma^{\nu}_{L}\}

(26)

\displaystyle=f[\not{A}]\,\bigg\{[\gamma^{\mu},f[\not{A}]\,]+f[\not{A}]\gamma^{\mu}\bigg\}\gamma^{\nu}+f[\not{A}]\,\bigg\{[\gamma^{\nu},f[\not{A}]\,]+f[\not{A}]\gamma^{\nu}\bigg\}\gamma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=f[\not{A}]\,[\gamma^{\mu},f[\not{A}]\,]\gamma^{\nu}+f[\not{A}]\,[\gamma^{\nu},f[\not{A}]\,]\gamma^{\mu}+f^{2}[\not{A}](\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})

To further simply terms of

Then the last two terms in the last line of equation

\displaystyle=\Big(\gamma^{\mu}(2A^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\not{A})+\gamma^{\nu}(2A^{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}\not{A})\Big)

Substituting this result back into equation(

\{\Gamma^{\mu}_{L},\Gamma^{\nu}_{L}\}=2f^{2}[\not{A}]\eta^{\mu\nu}+\frac{\lambda e}{\pi}f[\not{A}]\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}}\bigg)(2\eta^{\mu\nu}\not{A}-\gamma^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}A^{\mu})\,.

Therefore we have the full form of curved (inverse) metric matrix as follow,

g^{\mu\nu}\boldsymbol{1}=f^{2}[\not{A}]\eta^{\mu\nu}+\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}f[\not{A}]\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}}\bigg)(2\eta^{\mu\nu}\not{A}-\gamma^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}A^{\mu})\,.

Since

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\not{A}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}-\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\not{A}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\not{A}^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}+O(e^{3})

2f^{2}[\not{A}]=2\eta^{\mu\nu}+\bigg(\frac{2\lambda e}{\pi}\not{A}+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}A^{2}+O(e^{3})\bigg)\eta^{\mu\nu}\,.

(39)

3
Curvature and geometric properties from effective Dirac algebra

In this section, we would like to calculate how the geometry changes due to metric change arised from the effective Dirac algebra. Since the metric is perturbated, we can use the linearized perturbation theory of general relativity.

The Christoffel connection is given by
[
21
]

\mathrm{\Gamma}^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\rho\lambda}(\partial_{\mu}h_{\nu\lambda}+\partial_{\nu}h_{\lambda\mu}-\partial_{\lambda}h_{\mu\nu})\,.

(45)

Upon explicit computation,

\mathrm{\Gamma}^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}[(\delta^{\rho}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}+\delta^{\rho}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\rho})A^{2}+2A_{\nu}F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,\rho}+2A_{\mu}F_{\nu}^{\,\,\,\rho}+2A^{\rho}C_{\mu\nu}]\,,

(46)

where
C_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is a symmetric tensor, and
F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,\rho}=\eta^{\rho\lambda}F_{\mu\lambda}

the anti-symmetric field strength tensor. Therefore, the geodesic equation is,

(47)

The Riemannian tensor is given by

21
]

(48)

After some lines of algebra, the Riemannian tensor is explicitly computed to be

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}A_{\beta}(\eta_{\mu\sigma}\partial_{\rho}-\eta_{\mu\rho}\partial_{\sigma})A^{\beta}+\partial_{\mu}A_{\beta}(\eta_{\nu\rho}\partial_{\sigma}-\eta_{\nu\sigma}\partial_{\rho})A^{\beta}

\displaystyle+A_{\alpha}(\eta_{\mu\sigma}\partial_{\rho}-\eta_{\mu\rho}\partial_{\sigma})\partial_{\nu}A^{\alpha}+A_{\alpha}(\eta_{\nu\rho}\partial_{\sigma}-\eta_{\nu\sigma}\partial_{\rho})\partial_{\mu}A^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Big((A_{\sigma}\partial_{\rho}-A_{\rho}\partial_{\sigma})F_{\nu\mu}+(A_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-A_{\nu}\partial_{\mu})F_{\rho\sigma}+F_{\mu\nu}F_{\sigma\rho}

The second term of the Ricci tensor in equation (

) is

Lastly we have the forth term in equation (

The Ricci scalar is given by

(54)

Finally the Einstein tensor is defined by

G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}R=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}\,.

(55)

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\Big(2(\Box\eta_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu})A^{2}+\partial_{\nu}A^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}A_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}A^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}A_{\nu}+A^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu})

(56)

\displaystyle-A_{\nu}\Box A_{\mu}-A_{\mu}\Box A_{\nu}-2\partial^{\alpha}A_{\mu}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}A^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}A^{\alpha}\Big)\,,

which is a non-linear equation.

4
Effective
\gamma^{5}
, effective spin operator and effective spin of the electron

In this section, we would like to study how the spin of an electron changes under the effective Dirac algebra. First, the
\gamma^{5}
is a parity operator defined by
\gamma^{5}=i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}
, which is the only operator that commutes with all other
\gamma^{\mu}
matrices
[
17
]
. The spin operator is constructed by the
\gamma^{5}
matrix, in the Dirac representation
[
17
]
,

S^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}

(57)

for
i=1,2,3
, where
S^{1},S^{2}\text{and}S^{3}
correspond to the
S_{x},S_{y},S_{z}
spin components respectively. It can be easily found that

S^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(S^{i})^{2}=S_{x}^{2}+S_{y}^{2}+S_{z}^{2}=\frac{3}{4}\boldsymbol{1}\,.

(58)

And as
S^{2}=s(s+1)\boldsymbol{1}
, this implies
s=\frac{1}{2}
. Thus the spin operator describes the spin-half electron.

We will see how the interaction with gauge field effectively changes the spin of the electron. The effective
\gamma^{5}
operator becomes

\Gamma_{L}^{5}=i\Gamma_{L}^{0}\Gamma_{L}^{1}\Gamma_{L}^{2}\Gamma_{L}^{3}=if[\not{A}]\gamma^{0}f[\not{A}]\gamma^{1}f[\not{A}]\gamma^{2}f[\not{A}]\gamma^{3}\,.

(59)

Up to second order, after some algebras one finds,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L}^{5}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{3}\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}\not{A}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}+O(e^{3})\bigg)\gamma^{k}

(60)

\displaystyle=i\bigg(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\Big(-\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}+2A^{2}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}-\not{A}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\Big)+O(e^{3})\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\Big(-\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}+2A^{2}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}-\not{A}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}\Big)+O(e^{3})\bigg)\,.

Then it follows that,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L}^{5}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{5}+i\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg(4A^{2}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}-\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}-\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}

(61)

\displaystyle-\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\not{A}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}-\not{A}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\bigg)+O(e^{3})\,.

With some manipulation, finally we can write
\Gamma_{L}^{5}
as some correction of the original
\gamma^{5}
matrix with some extra correction terms

\Gamma_{L}^{5}=\bigg(1+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}A^{2}\bigg)\gamma^{5}+F(A^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu})+O(e^{3})\,,

(62)

where

F(A^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu})=-\frac{i\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\bigg(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}A^{(3)}+A^{(0)}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}+2\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}A^{(2)}A^{(3)}-\big(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}A^{(2)}+\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}A^{(0)}\big)\gamma^{3}\not{A}\bigg)\,.

(63)

We can see that only if
A^{\mu}
vanishes we get back
\gamma^{5}
.

Next we compute the effective spin operator. We have

\Sigma^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{5}_{L}\Gamma_{L}^{0}\Gamma^{i}_{L}\,.

(64)

and we want to express the effective spin operator
\Sigma^{i}
in terms of the original
S^{i}
with some extra corrections. We can use the result in equation(
61
),

\displaystyle\Sigma^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\gamma^{5}+i\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg(4A^{2}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}-\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}-\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}

(65)

\displaystyle-\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\not{A}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}-\not{A}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\bigg)\Bigg)\times\bigg(\gamma^{0}-\frac{\lambda e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\not{A}\gamma^{0}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}\gamma^{0}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\gamma^{i}-\frac{\lambda e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\not{A}\gamma^{i}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}\gamma^{i}\bigg)

\displaystyle=S^{i}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\Bigg(A^{3}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\not{A}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}-\frac{i}{4}\not{A}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}A^{2}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}-\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{5}\not{A}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{i}\Bigg)

Employing the identity of
\{\gamma^{\mu},\not{A}\}=2A^{\mu}
and the fact that
\not{A}\not{A}=A^{2}
to the fifth term and the sixth term in the last line of equation(
64
), we get respectively,

-\frac{i}{4}\not{A}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}=-\frac{i}{2}A^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}+\frac{1}{4}A^{2}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}

(66)

and

-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{i}=-\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}A^{i}+\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}\not{A}\,.

(67)

After some simplification, we finally obtain the desired formula,

\Sigma^{i}=\bigg(1+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}A^{2}\bigg)S^{i}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\{S^{i},\not{A}\}+F(A^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu},\gamma^{5})\,,

(68)

where

\displaystyle F(\gamma^{\mu},A^{\nu},\gamma^{5})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\bigg(\gamma^{5}A^{0}\gamma^{i}+\frac{i}{2}A^{i}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}

(69)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\not{A}\gamma^{2}\not{A}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}-\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{5}\not{A}\gamma^{0}\not{A}\gamma^{i}\bigg)

Thus we can see that how the origin spin operator is corrected by the gauge field, coupling and the anti-commutator
\{S^{i},\not{A}\}
. We also clearly see that when the gauge field is turned off, we get back the original spin operator
S^{i}
.

To see how the spin is changed, first for convenience, in equation
68
we define,

G^{i}(S^{i},\not{A},A^{\mu},\gamma^{5})=\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\{S^{i},\not{A}\}+F(A^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu},\gamma^{5})\,.

(70)

Therefore, let’s rewrite equation (
68
) as

\Sigma^{i}=\bigg(1+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}A^{2}\bigg)S^{i}+G^{i}(S^{i},\not{A},A^{\mu},\gamma^{5})\,.

(71)

The effective spin operator square
\Sigma^{2}
is, therefore,

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\Sigma^{i})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\bigg(1+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}A^{2}\bigg)S^{i}+G^{i}(S^{i},\not{A},A^{\mu},\gamma^{5})\right)^{2}

(72)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\bigg(1+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}A^{2}\bigg)^{2}(S^{i})^{2}+\bigg(1+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}A^{2}\bigg)\{S^{i},G\}+G^{2}\right)\,.

Next for our approximation, we would like to keep the second order terms only, therefore we have

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(1+\frac{3\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{\pi^{2}}A^{2}\bigg)\sum_{i=1}^{3}(S^{i})^{2}+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\{S^{i},G\}\bigg)+O(e^{4})

(73)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\boldsymbol{1}+\frac{9\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}\boldsymbol{1}+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\{S^{i},G\}\bigg)+O(e^{4})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{3}{4}\boldsymbol{1}+\Delta\,,

where
\Delta
is the additional matrix extra terms that

\Delta=\frac{9\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}A^{2}\boldsymbol{1}+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\{S^{i},G\}\bigg)+O(e^{4})\,.

(74)

Now we have

\Sigma^{2}=s^{\prime}(s^{\prime}+1)\boldsymbol{1}\,,

(75)

where
s^{\prime}=s+\delta
and
s=\frac{1}{2}
is the original spin of the electron,
\delta
is the small deviation matrix. By expanding,

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=(s+\delta)(s+\delta+1)\boldsymbol{1}

(76)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+\delta+1\bigg)\boldsymbol{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\boldsymbol{1}+2\delta+\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{3}{4}\boldsymbol{1}+2\delta\,,

where
\delta^{2}
is very small and can be dropped. Comparing like-terms in equation
73
, we identify the small spin deviation as, in matrix form,

\delta=\frac{\Delta}{2}\,.

(77)

When the gauge field interaction is turned off, i.e.
A^{\mu}=0
, we will also have
G=0
. Then we can see that this gives us back the original spin value of the electron.

5
Quantization of the effective Dirac algebra

So far we have investigated the classical theory for effective Dirac algebra by gauge field interaction. Next we will carry out quantization of the classical theory of effective Dirac algebra . First of all, we know that the quantization of the Dirac field is to impose the equal time anti-commutation relation (ETCR) on the Dirac spinors, for which the spinor field is turned into field operator,

\displaystyle\{\hat{\psi}_{a}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}),\hat{\psi}_{b}^{\dagger}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})\}=\delta^{3}(\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}-\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})\delta_{ab}\,,

(78)

\displaystyle\{\hat{\psi}_{a}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}),\hat{\psi}_{b}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})\}=\{\hat{\psi}_{a}^{\dagger}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}),\hat{\psi}_{b}^{\dagger}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})\}=0\,.

This will be the same when we promote to the effective case. We also know that the quantization of the gauge field reads,

\displaystyle[\hat{A}_{\mu}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}),\hat{\Pi}_{\nu}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})]=-i\eta_{\mu\nu}\delta^{3}(\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}-\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})\,,

(79)

\displaystyle[\hat{A}_{\mu}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}),\hat{A}_{\nu}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})]=[\hat{\Pi}_{\mu}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}),\hat{\Pi}_{\nu}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})]=0\,,

where
\hat{\Pi}^{\nu}=-\hat{\dot{A}}^{\nu}
is the conjugated momentum. This will also be same when we promote to the effective case. However, as in the effective case the curved metric tensor is defined by the gauge fields, see in equations
29
and
31
, then the quantization amounts to quantizing the effective Dirac algebra, so is to quantize the metric. This marks the difference from the non-effective case, where in flat space time, the normal Dirac alegbra and the Minkowski metric are not quantized. In other words, the quantized effective Dirac algebra reads,
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{L},\hat{\Gamma}^{\nu}_{L}\}=2\hat{g}^{\mu\nu}\boldsymbol{1}
, which is

\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{L},\hat{\Gamma}^{\nu}_{L}\}=2f^{2}[\hat{\not{A}}]\eta^{\mu\nu}+\frac{\lambda e}{\pi}f[\hat{\not{A}}]\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\hat{A}^{2}}\bigg)(2\eta^{\mu\nu}\hat{\not{A}}-\gamma^{\mu}\hat{A}^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\hat{A}^{\mu})\,.

(80)

And the quantized metric reads,

\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}\,\bigg\{f^{2}[\hat{\not{A}}]\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\lambda e}{2\pi}f[\hat{\not{A}}]\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\hat{A}^{2}}\bigg)(2\eta_{\mu\nu}\hat{\not{A}}-\gamma_{\mu}\hat{A}_{\nu}-\gamma_{\nu}\hat{A}_{\mu})\bigg\}\,.

(81)

Up to second order, by equations(
39
) and (
43
), the quantized versions are

\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{L},\hat{\Gamma}^{\nu}_{L}\}=\bigg(2+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\hat{A}^{2}\bigg)\eta^{\mu\nu}\boldsymbol{1}-\bigg(\frac{\lambda e}{\pi}-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\hat{\not{A}}\bigg)(\gamma^{\mu}\hat{A}^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\hat{A}^{\mu})+O(e^{3})\,,

(82)

and

\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}=\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\hat{A}^{2}\bigg)\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\hat{A}_{\mu}\hat{A}_{\nu}\,.

(83)

It is noticed that explicitly the quantized gauge field is given by

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{p}}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathrm{\boldsymbol{p}}}}}\sum_{r=0}^{3}\Big(a^{r}_{\mathrm{\boldsymbol{p}}}\epsilon^{r}_{\mu}(\mathrm{\boldsymbol{p}})e^{-ip\cdot x}+a^{r\dagger}_{\mathrm{\boldsymbol{p}}}\epsilon^{r}_{\mu}(\mathrm{\boldsymbol{p}})e^{ip\cdot x}\Big)\,,

(84)

in which we have to some over all the polarizations of the photon. Now we want to find out the commutation relation of the metric in terms of the quantized gauge fields for the perturbative case. We would like to compute
[\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{x}}),\hat{\dot{g}}_{\rho\sigma}(t,\mathrm{\boldsymbol{y}})]

is the conjugated momentum of the metric. Differentiating equation (

) with respect to time, we obtain

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\alpha\beta}(\hat{\dot{A}}_{\alpha}\hat{A}_{\beta}+\hat{A}_{\alpha}\hat{\dot{A}}_{\beta})+\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}(\hat{\dot{A}}_{\mu}\hat{A}_{\nu}+\hat{A}_{\mu}\hat{\dot{A}}_{\nu})

[A+B,C+D]=[A,C]+[A,D]+[B,C]+[B,D]\,.

The first term gives

[AB,CD]=A[B,C]D+[A,C]BD+CA[B,D]+C[A,D]B.

(89)

Thus the commutators in equation (

\displaystyle\Big[\hat{A}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{A}_{\beta}(x),\hat{\Pi}_{\lambda}(y)\hat{A}_{\delta}(y)\Big]

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\alpha\beta}\hat{A}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{A}_{\beta}(x),-\frac{\lambda^{2}e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}(\hat{\Pi}_{\rho}(y)\hat{A}_{\sigma}(y)+\hat{A}_{\rho}(y)\hat{\Pi}_{\sigma}(y))\bigg]

Discussion on future work for the non-abelian case

In this discussion section, we would like to give a brief reivew on the difficulty in applying the above results in section 2 to the non-abelian case. We will see that in fact, such generalization is highly non-trivial and we cannot apply the derivation in section 2 due to the non-abelian nature, and we will see why. This demands future work. For the non-abelian case, the Dirac equation with interaction is

i\hbar\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Psi-g\hbar A_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}\Psi-mc\Psi=0\,,

(116)

where
A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}
and
t^{a}
is the generator of the Lie group, for example SU(3) for coloured gluons. The full Dirac spinor
\Psi
is the tensor product of the colour state to the normal Dirac spinor,

\Psi(x)=|c\rangle\otimes\psi(x)\,,

(117)

where
|c\rangle
is the
3\times 1
column colour vector that transforms according to the SU(3) group. The
\Psi
is then a
12\times 1
column spinor. If we explicitly investigate the term

A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\gamma^{\mu}\,,

(118)

one can see that in general
t^{a}
and
\gamma^{\mu}
are of different dimensions. For example in the SU(3) case
t^{a}
are
3\times 3
Gellmann matrices, while
\gamma^{\mu}
are
4\times 4
matrices. Since by noting that the full spinor
\Psi
is factorized into colour state and normal Dirac spinor, therefore the term
A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\gamma^{\mu}
means to be
A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\otimes\gamma^{\mu}
. Therefore the Dirac equation for non-abelian interaction means to be

\bigg(i\hbar(\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}-g\hbar A_{\mu}\otimes\gamma^{\mu}-mc\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}\bigg)(|c\rangle\otimes\psi)=0\,,

(119)

where
\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix associated to the SU(3) generator matrices, while
\boldsymbol{1}
is the
4\times 4
identity matrix associated to the gamma matrices. Then we can write

\Psi(x)=\frac{i\hbar}{mc}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}+\frac{\hbar g}{mc}A_{\rho}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}}\right)(\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\Psi(x)\,.

(120)

It is also beneficial to express the equations in tensor notations. Notice that
A_{\mu ij}=A^{a}t^{a}_{ij}
. Also define

\Psi_{jq}=(\,|c\rangle\otimes\psi)_{jq}=c_{j}\psi_{q}\,.

(121)

Therefore the tensor components of interaction term acting on the full spinor reads

\big[(A_{\mu}\otimes\gamma^{\mu})\Psi\big]_{ip}=A_{\mu ij}\gamma^{\mu}_{pq}\Psi_{jq}\,.

(122)

Then it follows that in tensor notation, equations (
119
) is

(i\hbar\tilde{\delta}_{ij}\gamma^{\mu}_{pq}\partial_{\mu}-g\hbar A_{\mu ij}\gamma^{\mu}_{pq}-mc\tilde{\delta}_{ij}\delta_{pq})\Psi_{jq}(x)=0\,.

(123)

And that for equation (
120
) is

\Psi_{ip}(x)=\frac{i\lambda}{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}+\frac{\lambda g}{2\pi}A_{\rho}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}}\right)_{ijpq}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\gamma^{\mu})_{jaqb}\partial_{\mu}\Psi_{ab}(x)\,.

(124)

So far so good. However, now consider the functional,

f[A_{\rho}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}]=\frac{1}{\tilde{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}+\frac{\lambda g}{2\pi}A_{\rho}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}}\,.

(125)

We cannot do the expansion analysis as we have in equation (
12
) This is because
\not{A}\not{A}=A^{2}
only applies for the abelian case. For non-abelian case, this is not true. We can check the following,

\displaystyle(A_{\rho}\otimes\gamma^{\rho})(A_{\sigma}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma})

\displaystyle=(A_{\rho}^{a}t^{a}\otimes\gamma^{\rho})(A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{b}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma})

(126)

\displaystyle=A_{\rho}^{a}t^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{b}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{a}t^{b}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{a}t^{b}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}+A_{\sigma}^{b}A_{\rho}^{a}t^{b}t^{a}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho})\,.

But since
A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{a}t^{b}\neq A_{\sigma}^{b}A_{\rho}^{a}t^{b}t^{a}
, i.e.
A_{\rho}A_{\sigma}\neq A_{\sigma}A_{\rho}
due to the fact that the generators do not commute (as
[t^{a},t^{b}]=if^{abc}t^{c}
), so (
126
) cannot be further factorized. Therefore we conclude that
(A_{\rho}\otimes\gamma^{\rho})(A_{\sigma}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma})\neq A^{2}
. Instead, there is an extra-term correction by the structural constant, we see that from the last line of equation (
126
),

\displaystyle(A_{\rho}\otimes\gamma^{\rho})(A_{\sigma}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{a}t^{b}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}+A_{\sigma}^{b}A_{\rho}^{a}(t^{a}t^{b}-if^{abc}t^{c})\otimes\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho})

(127)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{a}t^{b}\otimes\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}+A_{\sigma}^{b}A_{\rho}^{a}t^{a}t^{b}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho}-if^{abc}t^{c}A_{\sigma}^{b}A_{\rho}^{a}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{a}t^{b}\otimes(\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}+\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho})-if^{abc}t^{c}A_{\sigma}^{b}A_{\rho}^{a}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{a}t^{b}\otimes(2\eta^{\rho\sigma}\boldsymbol{1})-if^{abc}t^{c}A_{\sigma}^{b}A_{\rho}^{a}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho})

\displaystyle=A^{2}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}-\frac{i}{2}f^{abc}A_{\rho}^{a}A_{\sigma}^{b}t^{c}\otimes\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\rho}\,.

Therefore when we carry out series expansion on the functional for the non-abelian case in equation (
125
), the terms will be much more complicated as it involves terms with the structural constant
f^{abc}
and so on, and this demands rigorous work and a better formalism to be developed in the future. Hence, the generalization of effective Dirac algebra from the abelian case to the non-abelian case is for more complicated than it seems to be.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we have derived classically an explicit formula for the curved metric that is defined by the effective gamma matrix due to gauge field interaction. This shows how the metric can be connected with gauge fields. We also worked out the perturbation metric
h_{\mu\nu}
by series expansion from the effective gamma matrix and study the geometry contributed by the effective Dirac algebra. Then we demonstrated how the parity operator and spin operator are corrected under the effective gamma matrix in the presence of gauge field interaction, and how the spin of an electron is deviated by a small correction. Then we conduct the canonical quantization of the effective Dirac algebra and hence work out the equal time commutation relation of the metric tensor. Our work shows how to quantize a metric in terms of quantizing gauge fields. Finally, we apply our results to the case of relativistic hydrogen atom and show how such a system results in curving the spacetime metric. This shows how a relativistic, electromagnetic system contribute to changing the geometry of spacetime. At the end we lay some foundations for the future work on the generalization of the effective Dirac algebra to the non-abelian case.

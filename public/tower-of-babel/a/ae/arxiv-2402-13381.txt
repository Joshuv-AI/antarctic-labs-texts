Tree semi-separable matrices: a simultaneous generalization of sequentially and hierarchically semi-separable representations
Nithin Govindarajan, Shivkumar Chandrasekaran

Abstract
We present a unification and generalization of sequentially and hierarchically semi-separable (SSS and HSS) matrices called tree semi-separable (TSS) matrices. Our main result is to show that any dense matrix can be expressed in a TSS format. Here, the dimensions of the generators are specified by the ranks of the Hankel blocks of the matrix. TSS matrices satisfy a graph-induced rank structure (GIRS) property. It is shown that TSS matrices generalize the algebraic properties of SSS and HSS matrices under addition, products, and inversion. Subsequently, TSS matrices admit linear time matrix-vector multiply, matrix-matrix multiply, matrix-matrix addition, inversion, and solvers.

Introduction

Matrices in applied problems of interest often exhibit a structure of low rank in their off-diagonal blocks. These structures have, for instance, been observed in the discretization of integral equations, Schur complements of discretizations of PDEs, Cauchy-like matrices, evaluations of potentials, and companion matrices, amongst others. Many frameworks have been proposed to efficiently represent these low-rank structures so that efficient linear algebra operations could be performed with such matrices. This includes the fast multiple method (FMM)
[
greengard1987fast
]
, semi-separable and quasi-separable matrices
[
vandebril2008matrix
,
bella2008computations
,
eidelman1999new
]
, sequentially semi-separable (SSS) matrices
[
chandrasekaran2002fast
,
chandrasekaran2005some
]
, hierarchically semi-separable (HSS) matrices
[
chandrasekaran2006fast
,
xia2010fast
]
,
\mathcal{H}
- and
\mathcal{H}^{2}
-matrices
[
borm2003introduction
,
hackbusch2002data
,
hackbusch2015hierarchical
]
, and hierarchically off-diagonal low-rank (HODLR) matrices
[
ambikasaran2013mathcal
,
aminfar2016fast
]
. These frameworks are all related and have their benefits, pitfalls, and special use cases. An exact analysis of the matter is however beyond the scope of this paper.

This paper is concerned with the structure that arises from the inverse of a (block-)sparse matrix whose adjacency graph is a tree. This will generally be a dense matrix but with a special low-rank structure. In particular, if we look at sums and products of such matrices, they retain these structures, even though they no longer are the inverse of a sparse matrix. None of the aforementioned frameworks or representations can capture these low-rank structures efficiently. In this paper, we present a new class of representations for rank-structured matrices that actually can capture these structures exactly. We call these representations
tree semi-separable
(TSS) matrices.

Interestingly, TSS matrices simultaneously unify and generalize SSS and HSS matrices. A main technical contribution in our paper is a construction algorithm that obtains a minimal TSS representation for any dense matrix. This algorithm is, in effect, a unification and generalization of the algorithms for doing the same for SSS and HSS matrices. As an important byproduct, we prove the universality theorem (
Theorem

4
). Given an arbitrary matrix, this theorem guarantees the existence of a TSS representation with the dimensions of its generators specified by the ranks of certain sub-blocks of the matrix, i.e., the so-called Hankel blocks. This result will allow us to generalize certain algebraic properties of TSS matrices which are known to already hold for the SSS and HSS cases. We show that TSS matrices satisfy a graph-induced rank structure (GIRS) property (see
[
chandrasekaran2019graph
]
) and describe the properties of TSS under addition, products, and inversion. Finally, we describe the algorithms for matrix-vector multiply and solution of linear systems.

It is well-known that SSS and HSS matrices are used extensively in practice. TSS matrices are a more flexible drop-in replacement for HSS and SSS matrices. We anticipate that this flexibility can lead to even further improvements in many practical applications. This is however the subject of future work.

2.3
Algebra with SSS and HSS matrices

SSS and HSS matrices share common algebraic properties. Any dense matrix can be converted into an SSS or HSS matrix. The dimensions of the generators, and thus the efficiency of the representation, are specified by the ranks of the off-diagonal (i.e., so-called Hankel) blocks. Sums and products of SSS (respectively HSS) matrices are again SSS (respectively HSS) but with a doubling in the size of the generators. The inverse of an SSS (respectively HSS) is an SSS (respectively HSS) matrix of the same generator dimensions. SSS (respectively HSS) representations of matrices that have small Hankel block ranks can be multiplied with vectors in linear time. The same holds for the solve operation. For the latter, one common approach to both representations is a lifting technique that solves the linear system as a larger block-sparse system
[
chandrasekaran2007fast
]
. All these commonalities do not come from nowhere, since SSS and HSS belong to the same family of a more general class of matrices.

4.1
Graph-induced rank structure of TSS matrices

Inverses of tridiagonal matrices are examples of dense matrices whose off-diagonal blocks possess low-rank qualities. For graph-partitioned matrices
(\mathrm{T},\mathbb{G})

graph-induced rank structure
(GIRS). To describe GIRS, we first introduce the concept of a Hankel block. Let
\mathbb{A}\subset\mathbb{V}
and let
\bar{\mathbb{A}}=\mathbb{V}\setminus\mathbb{A}

\mathrm{T}

\mathrm{\Pi}_{1}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{\Pi}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{T}\{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}\}&\mathrm{T}\{\mathbb{A},\bar{\mathbb{A}}\}\\
\mathrm{T}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}&\mathrm{T}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\bar{\mathbb{A}}\}\end{bmatrix}.

We call

if

Definition 2

The pair

such that

\rank\mathrm{T}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}\leq c\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{A}),\qquad\forall\mathbb{A}\subset\mathbb{V}.

to

in
\mathbb{G}

4.2
The universality theorem

While
Proposition

3
shows that TSS matrices satisfy the GIRS-property, a question arises whether the converse also holds: does every tree-graph-partitioned matrix that satisfies the GIRS-property admit a TSS representation whose rank-profile is bounded by the GIRS constant? This question is intimately tied to whether TSS are universal representations, i.e., if a TSS representation can be found for any graph-partitioned matrix for which the graph is acyclic and connected.

For SSS and HSS matrices both these questions can be answered in the affirmative. Interestingly, it turns out that the same holds for the more general class of TSS matrices.
Given a graph-partitioned matrix
(\mathrm{T},\mathbb{G})
with
G
acyclic and connected, the rank-profile of the corresponding TSS representation can be derived from the ranks of Hankel blocks whose edge count is unity. For a tree, these so-called
unit Hankel blocks
are quite straightforward to enlist as every edge
(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}
can be uniquely paired with one such unit Hankel blocks. Indeed, once a root node
r\in\mathbb{V}
for the graph has been picked, it must hold that either
j=\mathcal{P}(i)
or
i=\mathcal{P}(j)
for the corresponding tree
\mathbb{G}(r)
. Let

\mathbb{H}_{(i,j)}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}(i)&j=\mathcal{P}(i)\\
\mathbb{V}\,\setminus\mathcal{D}(j)&i=\mathcal{P}(j)\end{cases}

and let
\mathrm{H}_{(i,j)}
denote the Hankel block corresponding with subset
\mathbb{H}_{(i,j)}
, i.e.,
\mathrm{H}_{(i,j)}=\mathrm{T}\{\mathbb{H}_{(i,j)},\bar{\mathbb{H}}\}_{(i,j)}
with
\bar{\mathbb{H}}_{(i,j)}=\mathbb{V}\setminus\mathbb{H}_{(i,j)}
. We have the following result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(\mathrm{T},\mathbb{G})
be a graph-partitioned matrix with
\mathbb{G}
being acyclic and connected. Then
\mathrm{T}\in\mathbb{F}^{M\times N}
admits an TSS representation with rank-profile

\rho_{e}=\rank\mathrm{H}_{e},\qquad e\in\mathbb{E}.

Furthermore, such a TSS representation is minimal in the sense that any other TSS representation for
\mathrm{T}
with rank-profile
\{\rho^{\prime}_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
must necessarily satisfy
\rho^{\prime}_{e}\geq\rho_{e}
for all
e\in\mathbb{E}
.

The proof of
Theorem

4
is postponed to
where we shall introduce an explicit algorithm to convert a dense matrix into a TSS representation.

Theorem

4
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 5
.

Let
(\mathrm{T},\mathbb{G})
be a graph-partitioned matrix with
\mathbb{G}
being acyclic and connected. Then
\mathrm{T}\in\mathbb{F}^{M\times N}
satisfies the GIRS-property for constant
c>0
if, and only if,
\mathrm{T}\in\mathbb{F}^{M\times N}
admits a TSS representation with rank-profile
\rho_{e}\leq c
for all
e\in\mathbb{E}
.

4.3
Sums, products, and inverses of TSS matrices

The algebraic properties of SSS and HSS matrices under addition, multiplication, and inversion generalize to TSS matrices. The following three propositions may be established from
Theorem

4
.

Proposition 6
(TSS addition).

Let
\mathrm{T}_{1},\mathrm{T}_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{M\times N}
, with
M=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}m_{i}
and
N=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}n_{i}
, be TSS matrices of rank-profiles
\{\rho_{1,e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
and

\{\rho_{2,e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
associated with the acyclic connected graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
. Then,
\mathrm{T}_{3}=\mathrm{T}_{1}+\mathrm{T}_{2}
is a TSS matrix of rank profile
\{\rho_{1,e}+\rho_{2,e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
.

Proof.

Since
\rank\mathrm{T}_{3}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}\leq\rank\mathrm{T}_{1}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}+\rank\mathrm{T}_{2}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}
for any Hankel block induced by
\mathbb{A}
, the result directly follows from
Theorem

4
.

Proposition 7
(TSS product).

Let
\mathrm{T}_{1}\in\mathbb{F}^{M\times N}
and
\mathrm{T}_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{N\times P}
, with
M=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}m_{i}
,
N=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}n_{i}
, and
P=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}p_{i}
, be TSS matrices of rank-profiles
\{\rho_{1,e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
and

\{\rho_{2,e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
associated with the acyclic connected graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
. Then,
\mathrm{T}_{3}=\mathrm{T}_{1}\mathrm{T}_{2}
is a TSS matrix of rank profile
\{\rho_{1,e}+\rho_{2,e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\rank\mathrm{T}_{3}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rank\mathrm{T}_{1}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}\mathrm{T}_{2}\{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}\}+\mathrm{T}_{1}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\bar{\mathbb{A}}\}\mathrm{T}_{2}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rank\left(\mathrm{T}_{1}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}+\rank\mathrm{T}_{2}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}\right)

for any Hankel block induced by
\mathbb{A}
, the result directly follows from
Theorem

4
.

Proposition 8
(TSS inverse).

Let
\mathrm{T}\in\mathbb{F}^{N\times N}
, with
N=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}n_{i}
, be a non-singular TSS matrix of rank-profile
\{\rho_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
associated with the acyclic connected graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
. Then,
\mathrm{T}^{-1}
is also a TSS matrix of rank profile
\{\rho_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{E}}
.

Proof.

Since
\rank\mathrm{T}^{-1}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}=\rank\mathrm{T}\{\bar{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{A}\}
for any Hankel block induced by
\mathbb{A}
, the result follows from
Theorem

4
.

5.2
The general construction algorithm

The approach taken for the example of the previous section generalizes for a generic TSS representation. This procedure to convert a dense matrix into TSS form consists of two phases: an upsweep and a downsweep phase. In the upsweep phase, one starts at the leaves of the tree and works up towards the root. In this process, all the
\mathrm{B}
’s,
\mathrm{C}
’s, and
\mathrm{U}
’s are computed. In the downsweep phase that follows, one starts at the root of the tree and then works down towards the leaves. In this second leg, the
\mathrm{P}
’s,
\mathrm{Q}
’s,
\mathrm{W}
’s, and
\mathrm{V}
’s are computed.

Algorithm 1
(TSS construction algorithm).

Let
\mathbb{G}(r)
be a tree with root node
r\in\mathbb{V}
and let
\mathrm{T}\in\mathbb{F}^{M\times N}
be the associated graph-partitioned matrix with
M=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}m_{i}
and
N=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{V}}n_{i}
. A set of generators for the TSS representation of
T
is obtained by following the steps outlined below.

1.

Diagonal stage.
Set
\mathrm{D}^{i}=\mathrm{T}\{i,i\}
for
i\in\mathbb{V}
.

2.

Upsweep stage.
For
l=L,L-1,\ldots,1
do the following:

(a)

For every

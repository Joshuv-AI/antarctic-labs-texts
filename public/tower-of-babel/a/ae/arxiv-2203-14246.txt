Markov partitions for the geodesic flow on compact Riemann surfaces of constant negative curvature
Huynh M. Hien

Abstract
It is well-known that hyperbolic flows admit Markov partitions of arbitrarily small size. However, the constructions of Markov partitions for general hyperbolic flows are very abstract and not easy to understand. To establish a more detailed understanding of Markov partitions, in this paper we consider the geodesic flow on Riemann surfaces of constant negative curvature. We provide a rigorous construction of Markov partitions for this hyperbolic flow with explicit forms of rectangles and local cross sections. The local product structure is also calculated in detail.



Keywords:
Markov partitions; Symbolic dynamics; Geodesic flows; Constant negative curvatures

Introduction

Symbolic dynamics has had a great history development and is a very useful method to
study general dynamical systems. Instead of working on general dynamical systems, one can consider respective symbolic systems via symbolic dynamics.
The symbolic dynamics of a dynamical system is constructed from Markov partitions, which have been attracting a lot of mathematicians. In 1967, a Markov partition for hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on 2-torus was constructed by Adler and Weiss in
[
1
]
.
Then Sinai
[
24
,
25
]
used successive approximations to construct a Markov partition for arbitrary
C
-diffeomorphisms.
Bowen
[
4
]

used Sinai’s method to give a construction of Markov partitions for Smale’s Axiom A diffeomorphisms with the help of Smale’s Spectral Decomposition Theorem in
[
26
]
. In the case
of
C
-flows on three-dimensional manifolds, a construction of Markov partitions
was given by Ratner
[
21
]
. The author also introduced a Markov partition for transitive Anosov flows (so-called
C
-flows) on
n
-dimensional manifolds
[
22
]
. In 1973, Bowen modified and generalized the construction in
[
4
]
to have a Markov partition for

C^{1}
-hyperbolic flows in
[
6
]
, which has become a classic reference.
Pollicott
[
14
]
then constructed symbolic dynamics for Smale flows, which is a class of continuous flows on metric spaces provided a local product structure.
The result generalizes Bowen’s construction of symbolic dynamics for
C^{1}
-hyperbolic flows in
[
6
]
.
The problem is that all the constructions of Markov partitions mentioned above are very abstract and not easy to understand.

Pollicott and Sharp have found symbolic dynamics very useful in counting closed orbits for hyperbolic flows
[
19
]
and
presenting asymptotic estimates
for pairs of closed geodesics whose length differences lie in a prescribed
family of shrinking intervals
[
18
]
; see also
[
16
,
17
]
for other applications. Under supervision of Knieper, Bieder in his PhD thesis
[
3
]
used symbolic dynamics to construct partner orbits for hyperbolic flows.
The purpose of construction of symbolic dynamics is to prove that a hyperbolic flow is semi-conjugated to a hyperbolic symbolic flow,
in which the symbolic dynamics must be constructed from a Markov partition. A Markov partition is family of rectangles satisfying the Markov property.
Then one can associate to a hyperbolic flow a mixing subshift of finite type

\sigma:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda

and a Hölder continuous function

r:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that, with at
most a finite number of exceptions, the prime periodic orbit

\{x,\sigma x,...,\sigma^{k-1}x\}
corresponds to the prime periodic orbit

\gamma
whose word length and length are given by

|\gamma|=k
and
l_{\gamma}=r^{k}(x)=r(x)+r(\sigma x)+\cdots+r(\sigma^{k-1}x)
, respectively;
where
\Lambda=\{x=(x_{n})_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}:A(x_{n},x_{n+1})=1,\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
,

A
is the corresponding adjacency matrix with entries 0 or 1 of the Markov partition
and
x_{n}
are the symbols of rectangles in the Markov partition.
Thus instead of working on the original hyperbolic flow, this viewpoint offers several advantages.

The main tool for the construction of the hyperbolic symbolic flow is the existence of a Markov partition for a basic set. However, for general hyperbolic flows,
Markov partitions are not explicit and their constructions are not easy to understand as mentioned above. For instance, there are several results in
[
6
]
which need to be carefully verified.
To establish a more detailed understanding of Markov partitions, in this paper we consider a concrete hyperbolic dynamical system, namely the geodesic flow on compact Riemann surfaces of constant negative curvature. We introduce explicit forms of rectangles as well as local cross sections. This leads to a more explicit and intuitive Markov partition for the system. Coordinalization of Poincaré sections helps us calculate the local product structure in detail and prove the existence
of a pre-Markov partition.
Especially, we even could somewhat simplify
[
6
]
, in that we do not need several of the lemmas in this work. In addition, all important results in
[
6
]
in relevant to Markov partitions are rigorously verified.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we give an introduction to the theory of the geodesic flow on compact factors of the hyperbolic plane with auxiliary results that will be used in this paper. Section
3
studies local product structure of the flow with specific calculations. Section
4
presents explicit forms of local cross sections and rectangles. Expansivity of the flow is studied in Section
5
. The final section provides a rigorous construction of Markov partitions for the flow.

2
The geodesic flow on compact factors of the hyperbolic plane

We consider the geodesic flow on compact Riemann surfaces of constant negative curvature.
It is well-known that any compact orientable surface
with a metric of constant negative curvature is isometric to a factor
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}
,
where
\mathbb{H}^{2}=\{z=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,y>0\}
is the hyperbolic plane endowed
with the hyperbolic metric
ds^{2}=\frac{dx^{2}+dy^{2}}{y^{2}}
and

\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of the projective Lie group
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})={\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\{\pm E_{2}\}
;
here
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is the group of all real
2\times 2
matrices with unity determinant,
and
E_{2}
denotes the unit matrix. The group
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
acts transitively on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
by
Möbius transformations

z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
.
If the action is free (of fixed points), then the factor
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}
has a Riemann surface structure.
Such a surface is a closed Riemann surface of genus at least
2

and has the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
as the universal covering.
The geodesic flow
(\varphi_{t}^{\mathcal{X}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on the unit tangent bundle
\mathcal{X}=T^{1}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2})

goes along the unit speed geodesics on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

On the other hand, the unit tangent bundle
T^{1}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2})

is isometric to the quotient space

\Gamma\backslash{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})=\{\Gamma g,g\in{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\}
,
which is the system of right co-sets of
\Gamma
in
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, by an isometry

\Xi
.
Then the geodesic flow
(\varphi_{t}^{\mathcal{X}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
can be equivalently described as the natural
‘quotient flow’

\varphi^{X}_{t}(\Gamma g)=\Gamma ga_{t}

(2.1)

on
X=\Gamma\backslash{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
associated to the flow
\phi_{t}(g)=ga_{t}
on
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})

by the conjugate relation

\varphi_{t}^{\mathcal{X}}=\Xi^{-1}\circ\varphi_{t}^{X}\circ\Xi\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Here
a_{t}\in{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
denotes the equivalence class obtained from the matrix
A_{t}=\Big({\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{t/2}&0\\
0&e^{-t/2}\end{array}}\Big)\in{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

There are some more advantages to work on
X=\Gamma\backslash{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})

rather than on
\mathcal{X}=T^{1}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2})
. One can calculate explicitly the stable and unstable manifolds
at a point
x
to be

W^{s}_{X}(x)=\{\theta^{X}_{t}(x),t\in\mathbb{R}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad W^{u}_{X}(x)=\{\eta^{X}_{t}(x),t\in\mathbb{R}\},

(2.2)

where
(\theta^{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
(\eta^{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

are the
stable horocycle flow
and
unstable horocycle flow

defined by
\theta^{X}_{t}(\Gamma g)=\Gamma gb_{t}
and
\eta^{X}_{t}(\Gamma g)=\Gamma gc_{t}
;
here
b_{t},c_{t}\in{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
denote
the equivalence classes obtained from

B_{t}=\big({\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{cc}1&t\\
0&1\end{array}}\big),\ C_{t}=\big({\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
t&1\end{array}}\big)\in{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
The flow
(\varphi^{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

is hyperbolic, that is,
for every
x\in X
there exists an orthogonal and
(\varphi_{t}^{X})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-stable splitting of the tangent space

T_{x}X

T_{x}X=E^{0}(x)\oplus E^{s}(x)\oplus E^{u}(x)

such that the differential of the flow
(\varphi_{t}^{X})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is uniformly expanding on
E^{u}(x)
, uniformly contracting on
E^{s}(x)
and isometric on
E^{0}(x)={\rm span}\big\{\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{t}^{X}(x)|_{t=0}\big\}
. One can choose

\displaystyle E^{s}(x)={\rm span}\Big\{\frac{d}{dt}\,\theta^{X}_{t}(x)\Big|_{t=0}\Big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad E^{u}(x)={\rm span}\Big\{\frac{d}{dt}\,\eta^{X}_{t}(x)\Big|_{t=0}\Big\}.

General references for this section are
[
2
,
8
,
12
]
,
and these works may be consulted for the proofs to all results which are stated above.
In what follows, we will drop the superscript
X
from
(\varphi^{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}},(\theta^{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}},(\eta^{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
to simplify notation.

2.1
Distance on
\Gamma\backslash{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})

Lemma 2.1
.

There is a natural Riemannian metric on
\mathsf{G}={\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})

such that the induced metric function
d_{\mathsf{G}}
is left-invariant under
\mathsf{G}
and

d_{\mathsf{G}}(a_{t},e)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|t|,\quad d_{\mathsf{G}}(b_{t},e)\leq|t|,\quad d_{\mathsf{G}}(c_{t},e)\leq|t|\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
e=\pi(E_{2})
is the unity of
\mathsf{G}
.

See
[
8
, Subsection 9.3]
for more details.

We define a metric function
d_{X}
on
X=\Gamma\backslash{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
by

d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})=\inf_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma}d_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma_{1}g_{1},\gamma_{2}g_{2})=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}d_{\mathsf{G}}(g_{1},\gamma g_{2}),

(2.3)

where
x_{1}=\Gamma g_{1}
,
x_{2}=\Gamma g_{2}
.
In fact, if
X
is compact, one can prove that the infimum is a minimum:

d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma}d_{\mathsf{G}}(g_{1},\gamma g_{2}).

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
x\in X
and
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

d_{X}(\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{s}(x))\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|t-s|.

Proof.

Suppose
x=\Gamma g
for some
g\in{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, then

\displaystyle d_{X}(\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{s}(x))

\displaystyle=d_{X}(\Gamma ga_{t},\Gamma ga_{s})\leq d_{\mathsf{G}}(ga_{t},ga_{s})

\displaystyle=d_{\mathsf{G}}(a_{t},a_{s})=d_{\mathsf{G}}(a_{t-s},e)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|t-s|.

∎

It is well-known that the Riemann surface
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}
is compact if and only if
the quotient space
X=\Gamma\backslash{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is compact. It is possible to derive
a uniform lower bound on
d_{\mathsf{G}}(g,\gamma g)

for
g\in{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If the space
X=\Gamma\backslash{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is compact, then there exists
0<\sigma_{*}<1
such that

d_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma g,g)>\sigma_{*}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}.

The number
\sigma_{*}
is called an injectivity radius. See
[
23
, Lemma 1, p. 237]
for a similar result on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

2.2
Poincaré sections

Definition 2.4

(Poincaré section)
.

Let
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
. The
(closed) Poincaré section
of radius
\varepsilon
at
x
is defined by

P_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{(\theta_{s}\circ\eta_{u})(x):|u|\leq\varepsilon,|s|\leq\varepsilon\}=\{\Gamma gc_{u}b_{s}:|u|\leq\varepsilon,|s|\leq\varepsilon\},

where
g\in\mathsf{G}
is such that
x=\Gamma g
; see Figure
2
(a) for an illustration.

Another version of Poincaré section is

\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{(\eta_{u}\circ\theta_{s})(x):|u|\leq\varepsilon,|s|\leq\varepsilon\}=\{\Gamma gb_{s}c_{u}:|s|\leq\varepsilon,|u|\leq\varepsilon\}.

Note that both sets do not depend on the choice of
g\in\mathsf{G}
such that
x=\Gamma g
. Similarly to open Poincaré sections in
[
9
]
one can coordinalize Poincaré sections in the case that the radius is small enough.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be compact,
\varepsilon\in\,(0,\sigma_{*}/4)
, and
x=\Gamma g
for
g\in\mathsf{G}
.

(a) For every
y\in P_{\varepsilon}(x)
there exists a unique couple
(u,s)\in\,[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\,\times\,[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
such that

y=\Gamma gc_{u}b_{s}
.
Then we write
y=(u,s)_{x}
.

(b) For every
y\in\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}(x)
there exists a unique couple
(s,u)\in\,[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\,\times\,[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
such that
y=\Gamma gb_{s}c_{u}
.
Then we write
y=(s,u)^{\prime}_{x}
.

See
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
for a proof.

3
Local product structure

In this section we construct local product structure for the system.
We use explicit forms of (local) stable and unstable manifolds
to calculate local product structure in detail.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
. The local stable and local unstable manifold at
x
are given by

W_{\varepsilon}^{s}(x)=\{\theta_{s}(x):|s|<\varepsilon\}=\{\Gamma gb_{s}:|s|<\varepsilon\}

and

W_{\varepsilon}^{u}(x)=\{\eta_{u}(x):|u|<\varepsilon\}=\{\Gamma gc_{u}:|u|<\varepsilon\}.

Note that both sets are independent of the choice of
g\in{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
x=\Gamma g
.
We also need the notion of local weak-stable and local weak-unstable manifold:

W^{ws}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{\Gamma ga_{t}b_{s}:|t|<\varepsilon,|s|<\varepsilon\},

W^{wu}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{\Gamma ga_{t}c_{u}:|t|<\varepsilon,|u|<\varepsilon\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\sigma_{*}/5)
. There exists a
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
with the following property.
If
x,y\in X
satisfy
d_{X}(x,y)<\delta
, then the intersection

W^{ws}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)

consists of a unique point, and furthermore the intersection

W^{wu}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(y)

consists of a unique point.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion only.
In order to show that such a
z\in W^{ws}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)

does exist, note that according to Lemma
2.7
there is
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
so that the following holds.
If
u\in\mathsf{G}
and
d_{\mathsf{G}}(u,e)<\delta
, then there is

A=\bigg(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\bigg)\in{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})

(3.5)

such that
u=\pi(A)
and
|a-1|+|b|+|c|+|d-1|<\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\}
.
Fix
x,y\in X
with
d_{X}(x,y)<\delta
. Let
g\in\mathsf{G}
and
h\in\mathsf{G}
satisfy
x=\Gamma g

and
y=\Gamma h
as well as
d_{X}(x,y)=d_{\mathsf{G}}(g,h)
. Then

d_{\mathsf{G}}(g^{-1}h,e)=d_{\mathsf{G}}(g,h)=d_{X}(x,y)<\delta,

and hence there is
A\in{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
as in (
3.5
)
such that
g^{-1}h=[A]
and
|a-1|+|b|+|c|+|d-1|<\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\}
;
then in particular
d\in[1/2,3/2]
holds. We can write
g^{-1}h=a_{t}b_{s}c_{u}
for

t=-2\ln d,\quad s=bd,\quad u=\frac{c}{d}.

Then
hc_{-u}=ga_{t}b_{s}
and also
|t|=2|\ln d|\leq 4|d-1|<\varepsilon
due to
|\ln(1+z)|\leq 2|z|

for
|z|\leq 1/2
. Furthermore,
|s|=|b||d|\leq 2|b|<\varepsilon/2

and
|u|=\frac{|c|}{|d|}\leq 2|c|<\varepsilon/2
. Therefore if we put

z=\Gamma ga_{t}b_{s}=\Gamma hc_{-u}\in X
,
then
z\in W^{ws}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)
.
It remains to prove that the intersection point is unique.
To establish this assertion, suppose that also

z^{\prime}\in W^{ws}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)
.
Then
z^{\prime}=\Gamma ga_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}}=\Gamma hc_{-u^{\prime}}

for some
|t^{\prime}|,|s^{\prime}|,|u^{\prime}|<\varepsilon
. Hence
\Gamma hc_{u}a_{t}b_{s}=\Gamma g=\Gamma hc_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}}
, which means that

hc_{u}a_{t}b_{s}=\gamma hc_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}}
for an appropriate element

\gamma\in\Gamma
. This yields

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma hc_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}},hc_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{G}}(hc_{u}a_{t}b_{s},hc_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}})=d_{\mathsf{G}}(c_{u}a_{t}b_{s},c_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{G}}(c_{u}a_{t}b_{s},e)+d_{\mathsf{G}}(c_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}},e)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|u|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|t|+|s|+|u^{\prime}|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|t^{\prime}|+|s^{\prime}|<5\varepsilon<\sigma_{*}.

From the property of
\sigma_{*}
we deduce that
\gamma=e

and therefore
c_{u}a_{t}b_{s}=c_{u^{\prime}}a_{t^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}}
.
Multiplying out the matrices
we obtain
u=u^{\prime}
,
t=t^{\prime}
, and
s=s^{\prime}
, and accordingly

z=\Gamma gb_{-s}=\Gamma gb_{-s^{\prime}}=z^{\prime}
. ∎

Corollary 3.3

(Local product structure)
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\sigma_{*}/5)
. There exists a positive number
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
with the following property.
If
x,y\in X
and
d_{X}(x,y)\leq\delta
, then
there is a unique
v=v(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}
,
|v|\leq\varepsilon
such that

W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{v}(x))\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)\neq\varnothing.

More precisely, the intersection is a single point, denoted by
\langle x,y\rangle
.
Furthermore, the map
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is continuous on

\{(x,y)\in X\times X:d_{X}(x,y)<\delta\}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\sigma_{*}/5)
and let
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
be as in Lemma
3.2
.
Let
x,y\in X
be such that
d_{X}(x,y)<\delta
.
Then
W^{ws}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)\neq\varnothing
,
i.e., if
x=\Gamma g
and
y=\Gamma h
, then there are
s,v,u\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)

such that
\Gamma ga_{v}b_{s}=\Gamma hc_{u}
. Since
\varphi_{v}(x)=\Gamma ga_{v}

we have

W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{v}(x))=\{\Gamma ga_{v}b_{s^{\prime}}:|s^{\prime}|<\varepsilon\},

and hence
\Gamma ga_{t}b_{s}=\Gamma hc_{u}\in W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{v}(x))\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)
. If also
W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{v^{\prime}}(x))\cap W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(y)\neq\varnothing
for some
|v^{\prime}|<\varepsilon
, then
\Gamma ga_{v^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}}=\Gamma hc_{u^{\prime}}

for appropriate
|s^{\prime}|,|u^{\prime}|<\varepsilon
. From Lemma
3.2
we obtain

v=v^{\prime}
,
s=s^{\prime}
, and
u=u^{\prime}
, so that
v
is unique. That the intersection
is a single point also follows from Lemma
3.2
; see Figure
1
for an illustration.
The last assertion is obvious.
∎

\varepsilon\in(0,\sigma_{*}/5)
and let
\delta_{1}=\delta(\varepsilon)

Corollary

.

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x,y,z,w\in X
be such that
{\rm diam\,}\{x,y,z,w\}<\delta_{2}
. Then

(a)
\langle\langle x,y\rangle,z\rangle=\langle x,\langle y,z\rangle\rangle=\langle x,z\rangle
;

(b)
\langle\langle x,y\rangle,\langle z,w\rangle\rangle=\langle x,w\rangle
.

Proof.

For
x,y,z,w\in X
, we first check that all the notations make sense if
{\rm diam\,}\{x,y,z,w\}<\delta_{2}
.
Obviously
\langle a,b\rangle
makes sense for all
a,b\in\{x,y,z,w\}
.
Write
y=\Gamma g
and
w=\Gamma h
for
g,h\in{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
By the proof of Lemma
3.2
,

\langle x,y\rangle=\Gamma gc_{u_{1}}
for some
|u_{1}|<\delta_{1}/3

and
\langle z,w\rangle=\Gamma hc_{u_{2}}
for some
|u_{2}|<\delta_{1}/3
.
Then

\displaystyle d_{X}(\langle x,y\rangle,z)

\displaystyle=d_{X}(\Gamma gc_{u_{1}},z)\leq d_{X}(\Gamma gc_{u_{1}},\Gamma g)+d_{X}(y,z)

\displaystyle\leq|u_{1}|+d_{X}(y,z)<\delta_{1}/3+\delta_{1}/3<\delta_{1}

so

\langle\langle x,y\rangle,z\rangle
makes sense.
Similarly,
\langle x,\langle y,z\rangle\rangle
also makes sense.
Next,

\displaystyle d_{X}(\langle x,y\rangle,\langle z,w\rangle)

\displaystyle=d_{X}(\Gamma gc_{u_{1}},\Gamma hc_{u_{2}})\leq d_{X}(\Gamma gc_{u_{1}},\Gamma g)+d_{X}(y,w)+d_{X}(\Gamma h,\Gamma hc_{u_{2}})

\displaystyle<|u_{1}|+\delta_{1}/3+|u_{2}|<\delta_{1}

and hence

\langle\langle x,y\rangle,\langle z,w\rangle\rangle
also makes sense.

(a) Note that if
x^{\prime}\in W^{ws}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(x)
, then
W^{ws}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(x^{\prime})\subset W^{ws}_{\delta_{1}}(x)

and if
z^{\prime}\in W^{u}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(z)
, then
W^{u}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(z^{\prime})\subset W^{u}_{\frac{2\delta_{1}}{3}}(z)
.
By Lemma
3.2
,
\langle x,y\rangle\in W^{ws}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(x)
and

\langle\langle x,y\rangle,z\rangle\in W^{ws}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(\langle x,y\rangle)\cap W^{u}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(z)\in W^{ws}_{\delta_{1}}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\delta_{1}}(z)=\langle x,z\rangle.

Similarly,

\langle y,z\rangle\in W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(z)
implies

\langle x,\langle y,z\rangle\rangle\in W^{ws}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(\langle y,z\rangle)\in W^{ws}_{\frac{\delta_{1}}{3}}(x)\cap W^{u}_{\frac{2\delta_{1}}{3}}(z)=\langle x,z\rangle.

(b) Applying (a), we have
\langle\langle x,y\rangle,\langle z,w\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle x,y\rangle,w\rangle=\langle x,w\rangle
.
∎

This section deals with rectangles included in

that leads to more explicit Markov partition afterwards.

4.1
Local cross sections

Definition 4.1

(Local cross section)
.

A set

S\subset X
is called a
cross section of time

\varepsilon>0
for the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
if

(a)

S
is closed;

(b)

S\cap\varphi_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}(x)=\{x\}
for all
x\in S
.

See Figure
2
(a) for an illustration.

Figure 2:
(a) Local cross section, (b) Poincaré section

We consider an example of local cross sections.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0,\alpha>0
be such that

4\varepsilon+2\alpha<\sigma_{*}
and let
z=\Gamma g\in X
. The closed Poincaré sections

\displaystyle P_{\varepsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\{\Gamma gc_{u}b_{s},\ |u|\leq\varepsilon,|s|\leq\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\{\Gamma gb_{s}c_{u},\ |s|\leq\varepsilon,|u|\leq\varepsilon\}

are local cross sections of time
\alpha
and with diameters at most
4\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Obviously,
P_{\varepsilon}(z)
is closed.
Note that

{\mathcal{Q}}:=\varphi_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}(P_{\varepsilon}(z))=\{\Gamma gc_{u}b_{s}a_{t},|u|\leq\varepsilon,|s|\leq\varepsilon,|t|\leq\alpha\}.

In order to verify Assumption (b), we check that every point
x\in{\mathcal{Q}}
has a unique triple
(u,s,t)\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^{2}\times[-\alpha,\alpha]
such that

x=\Gamma gc_{u}b_{s}a_{t}
.
To show its uniqueness, suppose that
z=\Gamma g_{1}=\Gamma g_{2}
and
x=\Gamma g_{1}b_{s_{1}}c_{u_{1}}a_{t_{1}}=\Gamma g_{2}b_{s_{2}}c_{u_{2}}a_{t_{2}}
for

g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathsf{G}
and
(u_{i},s_{i},t_{i})\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^{2}\times[-\alpha,\alpha]
. Then there are
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
such that

\gamma g_{1}=g_{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma^{\prime}g_{1}c_{u_{1}}b_{s_{1}}a_{t_{1}}=g_{2}c_{u_{2}}b_{s_{2}}a_{t_{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}g_{1}c_{u_{1}}b_{s_{1}},g_{1}c_{u_{1}}b_{s_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma^{-1}g_{2}c_{u_{2}}b_{s_{2}}a_{t_{2}-t_{1}},g_{1}c_{1}b_{s_{1}})=d_{\mathsf{G}}(g_{1}c_{u_{2}}b_{s_{2}}a_{t_{2}-t_{1}},g_{1}c_{u_{1}}b_{s_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{G}}(c_{u_{2}-u_{1}}b_{s_{2}}a_{t_{2}-t_{1}},b_{s_{1}})\leq d_{\mathsf{G}}(c_{u_{2}-u_{1}}b_{s_{2}}a_{t_{2}-t_{1}},e)+d_{\mathsf{G}}(b_{s_{1}},e)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|u_{2}-u_{1}|+|s_{2}|+|t_{2}-t_{1}|+|s_{1}|<4\varepsilon+2\alpha<\sigma_{*}.

From the property of
\sigma_{*}
, this implies that
\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}=e
, so that
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.
Then
g_{2}c_{u_{2}}b_{s_{2}}a_{t_{2}}=\gamma g_{1}c_{u_{1}}b_{s_{1}}a_{t_{1}}=g_{2}c_{u_{1}}b_{s_{1}}a_{t_{1}}

yields
c_{u_{1}}b_{s_{1}}a_{t_{1}}=c_{u_{2}}b_{s_{2}}a_{t_{2}}
,
and consequently
u_{2}=u_{1},s_{2}=s_{1},t_{2}=t_{1}
by considering matrices.
This leads to
\varphi_{[-\alpha,\alpha]}(x)\cap P_{\varepsilon}(z)=\{x\}
and hence
P_{\varepsilon}(z)
is a local cross section of time
\varepsilon
.
For the last assertion, if
x=\Gamma gc_{u_{x}}b_{s_{x}},y=\Gamma gc_{u_{y}}b_{s_{y}}\in P_{\varepsilon}(z)
, then

d_{X}(x,y)\leq d_{\mathsf{G}}(c_{u_{x}}b_{s_{x}},c_{u_{y}}b_{s_{y}})\leq|u_{x}|+|s_{x}|+|u_{y}|+|s_{y}|\leq 4\varepsilon

shows
{\rm diam\,}P_{\varepsilon}(z)\leq 4\varepsilon
. The same argument can be applied for
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}(z)
.
∎

By the same manner as in the previous proof, it follows the next result.

Let

for

If

\langle x,y\rangle=\Gamma gc_{u_{x}}b_{s_{x}+s}a_{v}=\Gamma hc_{u_{y}}b_{s_{y}}c_{u},\mbox{\ \ }\langle x,y\rangle_{D}=\Gamma gc_{u_{x}}b_{s_{w}}=\Gamma gc_{u_{y}}b_{s_{y}}c_{u}a_{-v}\in D,

\displaystyle v=-2\ln(1+(u_{y}-u_{x})s_{y}),\quad s_{w}=\frac{s_{y}}{1+(u_{y}-u_{x})s_{y}}.

B_{-s_{x}}C_{u_{y}-u_{x}}B_{s_{y}}=\begin{pmatrix}1-(u_{y}-x_{x})s_{y}&s_{x}-s_{x}-s_{x}s_{y}(u_{y}-u_{x})\\
u_{y}-u_{x}&1+(u_{y}-u_{x})s_{y}\end{pmatrix}

and

Definition 4.7

(Rectangle)
.

Let

is called a

if

(
R_{1}
)

R
is closed in
D
;

(
R_{2}
)

\langle x,y\rangle_{D}\in R
for all
x,y\in R
.

See Figure
3
for an illustration. In the case that
R
is a rectangle, for
x,y\in R
we can write
\langle x,y\rangle_{R}

for
\langle x,y\rangle_{D}
since it does not depend on
D
.

Remark 4.8
.

(a)
If
R_{1}\subset D_{1}
and
R_{2}\subset D_{2}
are rectangles
and
R_{1}\cap R_{2}\neq\varnothing
, then

R_{1}\cap R_{2}
is a rectangle.

(b) If

is a rectangle, then

for appropriately small

and
y=\varphi_{\tau}(y^{\prime})
for

.
Write
x^{\prime}=\Gamma g
and
y^{\prime}=\Gamma h
. Assume that

\langle x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\rangle=\Gamma ga_{v}b_{s}=\Gamma hc_{u}

\langle x,y\rangle=\Gamma ga_{\tau}a_{v^{\prime}}b_{s^{\prime}}=\Gamma ha_{\tau}c_{u^{\prime}}

with

and

.

\langle x,y\rangle=\Gamma ga_{v}b_{s}a_{\tau}=\varphi_{\tau}(\langle x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\rangle)

\varphi_{\lambda}(\langle x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\rangle)=\langle x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\rangle_{R}\in\mathbb{R}

\varphi_{\lambda}(\langle x,y\rangle)=\varphi_{\tau}(\varphi_{\lambda}(\langle x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\rangle))\in\varphi_{\tau}(R)

implies that
\varphi_{\lambda}(\langle x,y\rangle)=\langle x,y\rangle_{\varphi_{\tau}(R)}\in\varphi_{\tau}(R)
.

Proposition 4.9

(Rectangle)
.

Let

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=

We only prove for

.
Take
x=\Gamma gc_{u_{x}}b_{s_{x}},\ y=\Gamma gc_{u_{y}}b_{s_{y}}\in S

on

5
Expansivity

In this section we study a nice property of hyperbolic dynamical systems, named expansivity.
Roughly speaking,
for more variation of expansivities, the reader can
if two orbits of the flow are close enough for the whole time then they must be identical.

Definition 5.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
(M,d)
be a compact metric space.
A continuous flow
\phi_{t}:M\longrightarrow M
is called
expansive
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
with the following property.
If
s:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function with
s(0)=0
and

d(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{s(t)}(y))<\delta\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

then
y=\phi_{\tau}(x)
for some
\tau\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
.

The next result was initially introduced in
[
6
]
to prove the expansivity of general hyperbolic flows.
Expansivity of the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
was reproved in
[
10
]
by a new approach, using the injectivity radius.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
6
]
)

For each
\varepsilon>0

If

and

(5.14)

(5.15)

d_{X}(\varphi_{t}(w),\varphi_{t}(x))<2\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in[-L,L],

Proof.

6
Construction of Markov partitions

In this section we give a rigorous construction of Markov partitions. We will use the forms of rectangles and local cross sections in Section
4
to construct a so-called pre-Markov partition, and then we
follow Bowen’s work in
[
6
]
to construct a Markov partition of arbitrarily small size step by step, in that we even could somewhat simplify
[
6
]
. The special forms of rectangles leads to a more explicit and intuitive Markov partition.

First, we introduce the notion of ‘proper family’.

Definition 6.1

(Proper family)
.

Let
\alpha>0
be given and let
{\mathscr{T}}=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\}
be a family of closed sets in
X
. We call
\mathscr{T}
a
proper family of size

\alpha
if

(i)
X=\varphi_{[-\alpha,0]}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}T_{i})
;

there is a family of differential local cross sections
{\mathscr{D}}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{n}\}
such that

(ii)
{\rm diam\,}D_{i}<\alpha
;

(iii)
T_{i}\subset{\rm int\,}D_{i}
;

(iv) for
i\neq j
, at least one of the sets

D_{i}\cap\varphi_{[0,\alpha]}(D_{j})
and
D_{j}\cap\varphi_{[0,\alpha]}(D_{i})

is empty.

In particular, it follows from (iv) that if
i\neq j
, then
D_{i}\cap D_{j}=\varnothing
.

Definition 6.2

(Poincaré map)
.

Let
{\mathscr{T}}=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\}
be a proper family. For any
x\in{\mathcal{T}}=T_{1}\cup\dots\cup T_{n}
, denote by

t(x)
the first return time, which is the smallest
t>0
such that
\varphi_{t}(x)\in{\mathcal{T}}
.
The map
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{T}}:{\mathcal{T}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}
defined by

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{T}}(x)=\varphi_{t(x)}(x)

is called the
Poincaré map
with respect to the family
{\mathscr{T}}
.

The first return time is also strictly bounded from below by a positive number as follows.

Proposition 6.3
.

The Poincaré map
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{T}}:T_{1}\cup\dots\cup T_{n}\to T_{1}\cup\dots\cup T_{n}
is a bijection.

Proof.

Take
x,y\in{\mathcal{T}}=T_{1}\cup\dots\cup T_{n}
such that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{T}}(x)={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{T}}(y)
or equivalently
\varphi_{t(x)}(x)=\varphi_{t(y)}(y)
. In order to obtain
x=y
, we must show
t(x)=t(y)
.
Suppose, in a contrary, that
t(x)\neq t(y)
. If
t(x)>t(y)
then
0<t(x)-t(y)<t(x)

and
y=\varphi_{t(x)-t(y)}(x)\in{\mathcal{T}}
, which contradicts the definition of
t(x)
.
The same occurs for
t(x)<t(y)
. Therefore
t(x)=t(y)
and we deduce that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{T}}
is injective.
Since
({\varphi_{t}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is time reversal invariant,
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{T}}
is surjective, which completes the proof.
∎

Note that the first return time map
t
and the Poincaré map

x_{N+1},y_{N+1}
are not in the same member of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{j_{N+1}}
. Then there is some
i\in I_{j_{N+1}}

for which
x_{N+1}
and
y_{N+1}
are in different
{E_{j_{N+1}i}^{a}}
’s; see Figure
9
.
Taking
z^{\prime}\in{\rm int\,}E_{j_{N+1}i}
, due to (
6.49
),

\langle y_{N+1},z^{\prime}\rangle_{C_{j_{1}}}=\langle x_{N+1},z^{\prime}\rangle_{C_{j,N+1}}
by Lemma
4.18
. Since
x_{N+1}
and
y_{N+1}

are in different
E_{j_{N+1}}^{a}
’s, we may assume that

\hat{x}:=\langle z^{\prime},x_{N+1}\rangle_{C_{j_{N+1}}}\in E_{j_{N+1}i}

and
\hat{y}:=\langle z^{\prime},y_{N+1}\rangle_{C_{j_{N+1}}}\notin E_{j_{N+1}i}
; see (
6.42
)-(
6.45
).
Then
\bar{x}={\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(\hat{x})\in C_{i}
and
\bar{y}={\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(\hat{y})\notin C_{i}
.
Let
y_{N+1}^{\prime}={\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(y_{N+1})
and
x_{N+1}^{\prime}={\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(x_{N+1})
.
Using Lemma
6.12
(a), there is
s\in\{1,\dots,n\}
so that

\varphi_{-L}(\bar{x})\in\varphi_{[-\alpha/2,\alpha/2]}(C_{s})

and

{\rm pr}_{D_{i}}\varphi_{L}W^{s}({\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(\bar{x}),C_{s})\subset W^{s}({\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(\bar{x}),C_{i}).

(6.50)

Furthermore, due to
N>\frac{L}{2\alpha}
, there is
k^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,N\}
such that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}^{N-k^{\prime}}(x)={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}^{-k^{\prime}}(x_{N+1})={\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(x_{N+1}^{\prime})\in C_{s}
.
Also
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}^{N-k^{\prime}}(y)={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}^{-k^{\prime}}(y_{N})={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}^{-k^{\prime}}(y_{N})\in C_{s}

since
y\overset{{}_{N}}{\sim}x
.
Owing to

\hat{y}=\langle z^{\prime},y_{N+1}\rangle_{D_{j_{N+1}}}=\langle\langle z^{\prime},x_{N+1}\rangle_{D_{j_{N+1}}},y_{N+1}\rangle_{D_{j_{N+1}}}=\langle\hat{x},y_{N+1}\rangle_{D_{j_{N+1}}},

we get

\bar{y}={\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(\hat{y})=\langle{\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(\hat{x}),{\rm pr}_{D_{i}}(y_{N+1})\rangle_{D_{i}}=\langle\bar{x},y^{\prime}_{N+1}\rangle_{D_{i}}

by Lemma
5.3
. Also, apply Lemma
5.3
again to obtain

\displaystyle{\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(\bar{y})=\

\displaystyle\langle{\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(\bar{x}),{\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(y^{\prime}_{N+1})\rangle_{D_{s}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\langle{\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(\bar{x}),{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}^{-k^{\prime}}(y_{N})\rangle_{C_{s}}\in W^{s}({\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(\bar{x}),C_{s}).

It follows from (
6.50
) that

\bar{y}={\rm pr}_{D_{i}}\varphi_{L}({\rm pr}_{D_{s}}\varphi_{-L}(\bar{y}))\in W^{s}(\bar{x},C_{i})\subset C_{i},

which contradicts
\bar{y}\notin C_{i}
and hence
x_{N+1},y_{N+1}
are in the same member of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{j_{N+1}}
.

Next, we verify that
y_{N+1}={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}(y_{N})
. Suppose on the contrary that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}(y_{N})=\bar{y}_{N+1}\in C_{k}
for some
k\neq j_{N+1}
.
Then
y_{N}\in E_{j_{N}k}
implies that
x_{N}\in E_{j_{N}k}
since
x_{N},y_{N}
belong to the same member of

{\mathfrak{C}}_{j_{N}}
. There is
\tau\in(t(x_{N}),2\alpha]
such that

\varphi_{\tau}(x_{N})\in C_{k}
. This implies

\varphi_{\tau}(x_{N})=\varphi_{\tau-t(x_{N})}(x_{N+1})\in C_{k}\cap\varphi_{[0,2\alpha]}(C_{j_{N+1}})\subset D_{k}\cap\varphi_{[0,2\alpha]}(D_{j_{N+1}}).

(6.51)

On the other hand,
y_{N+1}=\varphi_{s}(y_{N})\in D_{j_{N+1}}
for some
s\in(0,2\alpha]

and
\bar{y}_{N+1}=\varphi_{t(y_{N})}(y_{N})\in C_{k}
with
0<t(y_{N})<s
.
Then

y_{N+1}=\varphi_{s}(y_{N})=\varphi_{s-t(y_{N})}(\bar{y}_{N+1})\in D_{j_{N+1}}\cap\varphi_{[0,2\alpha]}(D_{k}),

(6.52)

which is impossible due to (
6.51
) and Condition (e) in Theorem
6.9
. Therefore,
y_{N+1}={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}(y_{N})
and so
y_{1}\overset{N}{\sim}x_{1}
.
To summarize, we have proved that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathscr{C}}(y)\in G_{q}
as well as
y_{1}\in W^{s}(x_{1},G_{q})
. This completes
the proof of
(M_{s})
. The proof of
(M_{u})
is analogous.

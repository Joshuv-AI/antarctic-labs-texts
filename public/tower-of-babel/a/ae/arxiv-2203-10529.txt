On the rigorous mathematical derivation for the viscous primitive equations with density stratification
Xueke Pu, Wenli Zhou

Abstract
In this paper, we rigorously derive the governed equations describing the motion of stable stratified fluid, from the mathematical point of view. Specially, we prove that the scaled Boussinesq equations strongly converge to the viscous primitive equations with density stratification as the aspect ration parameter goes to zero, and the rate of convergence is of the same order as the aspect ratio parameter. Moreover, in order to obtain this convergence result, we also establish the global well-posedness of strong solutions to the viscous primitive equations with density stratification.



AMS Subject Classification: 35Q35, 35Q86, 86A05, 86A10

Key Words: Boussinesq equations; Primitive equations; Density stratification; Hydrostatic approximation; Strong convergence

Introduction

The primitive equations are considered as the fundamental model in geophysical flows (see, e.g.,
[
41
,
33
,
37
,
32
,
40
]
). For large-scale ocean dynamics, an important feature is that the vertical scale of ocean is much smaller than the horizontal scale, which means that we have to use the hydrostatic approximation to simulate the motion of ocean in the vertical direction. Owing to this fact and the high accuracy of hydrostatic approximation, the primitive equations of ocean dynamics can be formally derived from the Boussinesq equations (see
[
26
,
10
]
).

The small aspect ratio limit from the Navier-Stokes equations to the primitive equations was studied first by Azérad-Guillén
[
1
]
in a weak sense, then by Li-Titi
[
28
]
in a strong sense with error estimates, and finally by Furukawa
et al.

[
14
]
in a strong sense but under relaxing the regularity on the initial condition. Subsequently, the strong convergence of solutions of the scaled Navier-Stokes equations to the corresponding ones of the primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity was obtained by Li-Titi-Yuan
[
30
]
. Furthermore, the rigorous justification of the hydrostatic approximation from the scaled Boussinesq equations to the primitive equations with full viscosity and diffusivity was obtained by Pu-Zhou
[
34
]
.

From a physical point of view, fluid flow is strongly influenced by effect of stratification (see, e.g.,
[
33
,
32
,
40
]
). An important observation for effect of stratification is that the density of a fluid changes with depth. In some mathematical studies, considering the hydrodynamic equations with density stratification term can often obtain better results (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
). These two facts show that density stratification term is of great significance both physically and mathematically. Therefore, the aim of this paper is to derive rigorously the governed equations describing the motion of stable stratified fluid, i.e., the viscous primitive equations with density stratification, from the mathematical point of view.

Let
\varOmega_{\tau}=M\times(-\tau,\tau)
be a
\tau
-dependent domian, where
M
is smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here,
\tau=H/L
is called the aspect ratio, measuring the ratio of the vertical and horizontal scales of the motion, which is usually very small. Say, for large-scale ocean circulation, the ratio
\tau\sim 10^{-3}\ll 1
.

Denote by
\nabla_{h}=(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})
the horizontal gradient operator. Then the horizontal Laplacian operator
\Delta_{h}
is given by

\Delta_{h}=\nabla_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}=\partial_{xx}+\partial_{yy}.

Let us consider the anisotropic Boussinesq equations defined on
\varOmega_{\tau}

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi+\frac{g\varrho}{\rho_{b}}\vec{k}=\mu_{h}\Delta_{h}u+\mu_{z}\partial_{zz}u,\\
\partial_{t}\varrho+u\cdot\nabla\varrho=\kappa_{h}\Delta_{h}\varrho+\kappa_{z}\partial_{zz}\varrho,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the three dimensional velocity field
u=(v,w)=(v_{1},v_{2},w)
, the pressure
\pi
and density
\varrho
are the unknowns.
g
is the gravitational acceleration and
\rho_{b}
is the reference constant density.
\vec{k}=(0,0,1)
is unit vector pointing to the
z
-direction.
\mu_{h}
and
\mu_{z}
represent the horizontal and vertical viscosity coefficients respectively, while
\kappa_{h}
and
\kappa_{z}
represent the horizontal and vertical heat conduction coefficients respectively.

For simplicity, the reference constant density
\rho_{b}
is set to be
\rho_{b}=1
. In fact, the anisotropic Boussinesq equations (
1.1
) have an elementary exact solution
(u,\pi,\varrho)=(0,\bar{p}(z),\bar{\varrho}(z))
satisfying the hydrostatic approximation

\frac{d\bar{p}(z)}{dz}+g\bar{\varrho}(z)=0.

Assume that

\displaystyle p(x,y,z,t)=\pi(x,y,z,t)-\bar{p}(z),

\displaystyle\rho(x,y,z,t)=\varrho(x,y,z,t)-\bar{\varrho}(z).

Then the anisotropic Boussinesq equations (
1.1
) become

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+(v\cdot\nabla_{h})v+w\partial_{z}v+\nabla_{h}p=\mu_{h}\Delta_{h}v+\mu_{z}\partial_{zz}v,\\
\partial_{t}w+v\cdot\nabla_{h}w+w\partial_{z}w+\partial_{z}p+g\rho=\mu_{h}\Delta_{h}w+\mu_{z}\partial_{zz}w,\\
\partial_{t}\rho+v\cdot\nabla_{h}\rho+w\partial_{z}\rho+\left(\frac{d\bar{\varrho}(z)}{dz}\right)w=\kappa_{h}\Delta_{h}\rho+\kappa_{z}\partial_{zz}\rho+\kappa_{z}\frac{d^{2}\bar{\varrho}(z)}{dz^{2}},\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v+\partial_{z}w=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Let
N=\left(-g\frac{d\bar{\varrho}(z)}{dz}\right)^{1/2}
. If
N>0
, then
N
is called the buoyancy or Brunt-Väisälä frequency. When
\frac{d\bar{\varrho}(z)}{dz}<0
, the density decreases with height and lighter fluid is above heavier fluid, which is called stable stratification.

Firstly, we transform the anisotropic Boussinesq equations (
1.2
), defined on the
\tau
-dependent domain
\varOmega_{\tau}
, to the scaled Boussinesq equations defined on a fixed domain. To this end, we introduce the following new unknowns with subscript
\tau

\displaystyle u_{\tau}=(v_{\tau},w_{\tau}),~v_{\tau}(x,y,z,t)=v(x,y,\tau z,t),

\displaystyle w_{\tau}(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{\tau}w(x,y,\tau z,t),~p_{\tau}(x,y,z,t)=p(x,y,\tau z,t),

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}(x,y,z,t)=(g\tau)\rho(x,y,\tau z,t),~\bar{p}_{\tau}(z)=\bar{p}(\tau z),~\bar{\varrho}_{\tau}(z)=(g\tau^{2})\bar{\varrho}(\tau z),

for any
(x,y,z)\in\varOmega=:M\times(-1,1)
and for any
t\in(0,\infty)
. Then the last two scalings allow us to write the pressure and density non-dimensionally as

\bar{p}_{\tau}(z)+p_{\tau}(x,y,z,t)=\bar{p}(\tau z)+p(x,y,\tau z,t)=\pi(x,y,\tau z,t)

and

\bar{\varrho}_{\tau}(z)+\tau\rho_{\tau}(x,y,z,t)=(g\tau^{2})(\bar{\varrho}(\tau z)+\rho(x,y,\tau z,t))=(g\tau^{2})\varrho(x,y,\tau z,t),

respectively.

Suppose that
\mu_{h}=\kappa_{h}=1
and
\mu_{z}=\kappa_{z}=\tau^{2}
. Under these scalings, the anisotropic Boussinesq equations (
1.2
) defined on
\varOmega_{\tau}
can be written as the following scaled Boussinesq equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{\tau}+(v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{\tau}+w_{\tau}\partial_{z}v_{\tau}+\nabla_{h}p_{\tau}=\Delta_{h}v_{\tau}+\partial_{zz}v_{\tau},\\
\tau(\partial_{t}w_{\tau}+v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{h}w_{\tau}+w_{\tau}\partial_{z}w_{\tau})+\frac{1}{\tau}(\partial_{z}p_{\tau}+\rho_{\tau})=\tau\Delta_{h}w_{\tau}+\tau\partial_{zz}w_{\tau},\\
\partial_{t}\rho_{\tau}+v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{h}\rho_{\tau}+w_{\tau}\partial_{z}\rho_{\tau}+\frac{1}{\tau}w_{\tau}\frac{d\bar{\varrho}_{\tau}}{dz}=\Delta_{h}\rho_{\tau}+\partial_{zz}\rho_{\tau}+\frac{1}{\tau}\frac{d^{2}\bar{\varrho}_{\tau}}{dz^{2}},\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{\tau}+\partial_{z}w_{\tau}=0,\end{cases}

(1.3)

defined on the fixed domain
\varOmega
.

When the fluid is steadily stratified, we can assume for simplicity that
\bar{\varrho}(z)=1-(1/g)N^{2}z
for some positive constant
N^{2}
, where
N
represents the strength of the stable stratification. This assumption leads to
\bar{\varrho}_{\tau}(z)=(g\tau^{2})\bar{\varrho}(\tau z)=g\tau^{2}-\tau^{3}N^{2}z
, and hence the third equation of the scaled Boussinesq equations (
1.3
) becomes

\partial_{t}\rho_{\tau}+v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{h}\rho_{\tau}+w_{\tau}\partial_{z}\rho_{\tau}-\tau^{2}N^{2}w_{\tau}=\Delta_{h}\rho_{\tau}+\partial_{zz}\rho_{\tau}.

Set
\tau^{2}\cdot N^{2}=1
, i.e.,
N\sim 1/\tau
, which means that the stratification effect is very strong. In such a case, the scaled Boussinesq equations (
1.3
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{\tau}-\Delta v_{\tau}+(v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{\tau}+w_{\tau}\partial_{z}v_{\tau}+\nabla_{h}p_{\tau}=0,\\
\tau^{2}\left(\partial_{t}w_{\tau}-\Delta w_{\tau}+v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{h}w_{\tau}+w_{\tau}\partial_{z}w_{\tau}\right)+\partial_{z}p_{\tau}+\rho_{\tau}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\rho_{\tau}-\Delta\rho_{\tau}+v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{h}\rho_{\tau}+w_{\tau}\partial_{z}\rho_{\tau}-w_{\tau}=0,\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{\tau}+\partial_{z}w_{\tau}=0.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Next, we supply the scaled Boussinesq equations (
1.4
) with the following boundary and initial conditions

\displaystyle v_{\tau},w_{\tau},p_{\tau}~\textnormal{and}~\rho_{\tau}~\textnormal{are periodic in}~x,y,z,

(1.5)

\displaystyle(v_{\tau},w_{\tau},\rho_{\tau})|_{t=0}=(v_{0},w_{0},\rho_{0}),

(1.6)

where
(v_{0},w_{0},\rho_{0})
is given. Moreover, we also equip the system (
1.4
) with the following symmetry condition

v_{\tau},w_{\tau},p_{\tau}~\textnormal{and}~\rho_{\tau}~\textnormal{are even,~odd,~even and odd with respect to}~z,~\textnormal{respectively}.

(1.7)

Noting that the above symmetry condition is preserved by the scaled Boussinesq equations (
1.4
), i.e., it holds provided that the initial data satisfies this symmetry condition. Due to this fact, throughout this paper, we always suppose that the initial data satisfies

v_{0},w_{0},\textnormal{and}~\rho_{0}~\textnormal{are periodic in}~x,y,z,~\textnormal{and are even,~odd,~and odd in}~z,~\textnormal{respectively}.

(1.8)

In this paper, we will not distinguish in notation between spaces of scalar and vector-valued functions. Namely, we will use the same notation to denote both a space itself and its finite product spaces. For convenience, we denote by notation
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{p}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{p,M}
the
L^{p}(\varOmega)
norm and
L^{p}(M)
norm, respectively. Moreover, since the scaled Boussinesq equations (
1.4
) satisfy the symmetry condition (
1.7
), it follows from the divergence-free condition that

is uniquely determined as

w_{0}(x,y,z)=-\int^{z}_{0}\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{0}(x,y,\xi)d\xi,

(1.9)

for any
(x,y)\in M
and
z\in(-1,1)
. Hence only the initial condition of
(v_{\tau},\rho_{\tau})
is given throughout the paper.

For the proof of the existence of weak solutions to the scaled Boussinesq equations (
1.4
), we refer to the work of Lions-Temam-Wang
[

. Specifically, for any initial data

, with

, we can prove that there exists a global weak solution

of the scaled Boussinesq equations (

), subject to boundary and initial conditions (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and symmetry condition (

). Moreover, by the similar argument as Lions-Temam-Wang

for initial data

, with

) are defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Given
(u_{0},\rho_{0})=(v_{0},w_{0},\rho_{0})\in L^{2}(\varOmega)
, with
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
. We say that a space periodic function

is a weak solution of the system (

), subject to boundary and initial conditions (

)-(
1.6

(ii)

satisfies the following integral equality

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}\bigg\{(-v_{\tau}\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}-\tau^{2}w_{\tau}\partial_{t}\varphi_{3}-\rho_{\tau}\partial_{t}\psi+\rho_{\tau}\varphi_{3}-w_{\tau}\psi)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\left[\nabla v_{\tau}:\nabla\varphi_{h}+\tau^{2}\nabla w_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{3}+\nabla\rho_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\psi\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\left[(u_{\tau}\cdot\nabla)v_{\tau}\cdot\varphi_{h}+\tau^{2}(u_{\tau}\cdot\nabla w_{\tau})\varphi_{3}+(u_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\tau})\psi\right]\bigg\}dxdydzdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\varOmega}\left(v_{0}\cdot\varphi_{h}(0)+\tau^{2}w_{0}\varphi_{3}(0)+\rho_{0}\psi(0)\right)dxdydz,

for any spatially periodic function
(\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi_{h},\varphi_{3},\psi)
, with
\varphi_{h}=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
, such that

Remark 1.1
.

Similar to the theory of three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations, e.g., see Temam

(1.10)

for a.e.

Main results

Now we are to state the main results of this paper. In order to obtain the following strong convergence results, i.e., Theorem
2.2
and
2.3
, we firstly establish the global well-posedness of strong solutions to the viscous primitive equations with density stratification (

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that a periodic function pair
(v_{0},\rho_{0})\in H^{1}(\varOmega)
, with

\int^{1}_{-1}\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{0}(x,y,z)dz=0,~\int_{\varOmega}v_{0}(x,y,z)dxdydz=0,~\textnormal{and}~\int_{\varOmega}\rho_{0}(x,y,z)dxdydz=0.

Then, for any
T>0
, there exists a unique strong solution
(v,\rho)
depending continuously on the initial data to the system (
1.11
) on the time interval
[0,T]
, subject to boundary and initial conditions (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and symmetry condition (
1.7
), such that
(v,\rho)\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\varOmega))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{2}(\varOmega))
and
(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}\rho)\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\varOmega))
.

The existence of weak solutions to the scaled Boussinesq equations (

.

Given a periodic function pair
(v_{0},\rho_{0})\in H^{1}(\varOmega)

\int^{1}_{-1}\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{0}(x,y,z)dz=0,~\int_{\varOmega}v_{0}(x,y,z)dxdydz=0,~\textnormal{and}~\int_{\varOmega}\rho_{0}(x,y,z)dxdydz=0.

(v_{\tau},w_{\tau},\rho_{\tau})

3.2

Multiplying the equation (

) and (

note that we have used the following facts that

\displaystyle\int_{\varOmega}\left[\left(v\cdot\nabla_{h}\right)v-\left(\int^{z}_{0}\nabla_{h}\cdot v(x,y,\xi,t)d\xi\right)\partial_{z}v\right]\cdot|v|^{2}vdxdydz=0,

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\int_{M}\left(\int^{1}_{-1}|v|dz\right)\left(\int^{1}_{-1}|\rho|^{2}|\nabla_{h}\rho|dz\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\int_{M}\left(\int^{1}_{-1}|v|dz\right)\left(\int^{1}_{-1}|\rho|^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}\left(\int^{1}_{-1}|\rho|^{2}|\nabla_{h}\rho|^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}dxdy

Due to the Young inequality, we have

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{4}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert^{2}_{4}+\frac{3}{8}\int_{\varOmega}|\rho|^{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dxdydz

(3.5)

A similar argument as integral term

\displaystyle D_{2}:

\displaystyle=\int_{\varOmega}\left(-\int^{z}_{0}\rho(x,y,\xi,t)d\xi\right)(\nabla_{h}\cdot|v|^{2}v)dxdydz

\displaystyle=-\int_{\varOmega}\nabla_{h}p_{\gamma}(x,y,t)\cdot|v|^{2}vdxdydz

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{h}p_{\gamma}\right\rVert_{2,M}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\cdot(v\otimes v)\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\rho\right\rVert_{2}\right)

Substituting (

\displaystyle D_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{4}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{4}\left\lVert|v|\nabla v\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\right)\left(\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert|v|\nabla v\right\rVert_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{4}\left\lVert|v|\nabla v\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{4}\left\lVert|v|\nabla v\right\rVert^{3/2}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{4}_{4}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert^{4}_{4}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{1}{4}\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|v|\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}

In order to estimate the second integral term

Here
C
is a positive constant that does not depend on
\tau
. It deduces from the above estimate that

\displaystyle(v_{\tau},\tau w_{\tau},\rho_{\tau})\rightarrow(v,0,\rho),~in~L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}(\varOmega)\right),

\displaystyle(\nabla v_{\tau},\tau\nabla w_{\tau},\nabla\rho_{\tau},w_{\tau})\rightarrow(\nabla v,0,\nabla\rho,w),~in~L^{2}\left([0,T];L^{2}(\varOmega)\right).

Obviously, the rate of convergence is of the order

. The theorem is thus proved.

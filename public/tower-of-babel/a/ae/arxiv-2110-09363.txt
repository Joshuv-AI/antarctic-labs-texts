Master equations governing the coupling between spin-currents and gravity
A. de Camargo, R. F. Sobreiro, V. J. Vasquez Otoya

Abstract
In this work we consider the Einstein-Hilbert action in the first order order formalism coupled to Dirac spinors. From the little group symmetry, we derive the corresponding Bargmann-Wigner current which is conserved but not gauge invariant. Therefore, we construct a gauge invariant version of the Bargmann-Wigner current which is not conserved but potentially observable. Because it is not conserved we split this current into fermionic and gravitational sectors and derive their broken continuity equations for each sector. These equations compose the master equations governing the interaction of spin-currents with gravity. Furthermore, we derive the corresponding master equations in the weak field approximation.

Rigorously, all inertial frames are related through Poincaré transformations. Nevertheless, since the Poincaré group is not semi-simple, we construct only a gauge theory for the Lorentz group. The translational sector, however, is not discarded because it can be interpreted as a vector representation of the Lorentz group.

\Lambda(x)\in SO(1,3)

M\longmapsto T(M)\sim\mathbb{R}^{(1,3)}

\eta_{ab}

\eta^{ab}

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{ab}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\;,

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{ab}e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\nu}_{b}\;.

(2.2)

These relations are easily obtained from the invariance of the line element

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\mu}e^{\mu}_{b}

\displaystyle\delta^{a}_{b}\;,

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\mu}e^{\nu}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\;.

(2.3)

Thence, the vierbein field encodes all metric properties of

A second mathematical object can be defined, the spin-connection
\omega^{a}_{\mu b}
, characterizing the parallelism properties of

\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=e_{a}^{\mu}\left(\partial_{\alpha}e_{\beta}^{a}+\omega_{\alpha b}^{a}e^{b}_{\beta}\right)\;.

(2.4)

This relation is also known as the compatibility condition, ensuring the vanishing of non-metricity. In the context of a gauge theory for the local Lorentz group the spin-connection emerges naturally as the gauge field of the theory. Hence, the double indices in the tangent space is associated with the fact that the spin-connection is an algebra-valued field in the adjoint representation of the Lorentz group. As a consequence,
\omega_{\mu}^{ab}=\omega_{\mu}^{ba}
.

In the gauge approach we are constructing, the vierbein and the spin-connection are regarded as the fundamental gravitational fields. The infinitesimal local Lorentz transformations of these fields are defined by

\displaystyle\delta\omega^{ab}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\alpha^{ab}\;,

\displaystyle\delta e^{a}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}e^{b}_{\mu}\;,

\displaystyle\delta e^{\mu}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{a}^{\phantom{a}b}e^{\mu}_{b}\;,

2.2

2.4

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\omega^{ab}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\omega^{ab}_{\mu}+\omega^{a}_{\phantom{a}c\mu}\omega^{cb}_{\nu}-\omega^{a}_{\phantom{a}c\nu}\omega^{cb}_{\mu}\;,

\displaystyle T^{a}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\mu}e_{\nu}^{a}-D_{\nu}e_{\mu}^{a}\;,

(2.6)

which transform covariantly under gauge transformations.

At this point we introduce fermions in the theory. Precisely, Dirac spinors representing, for instance, electrons. The Clifford algebra can be defined in tangent space,
\{\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}\}=2\eta^{ab}

\displaystyle\delta\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\alpha^{ab}\sigma_{ab}\psi\;,

\displaystyle\delta\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\bar{\psi}\alpha^{ab}\sigma_{ab}\;,

(2.7)

\sigma_{ab}=\dfrac{i}{2}[\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}]

With all ingredients defined, we can now define the theory governing the dynamics of all fields. Gravity coupled with Dirac spinors is then described by the Einstein-Hilbert-Dirac action,

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2k}\int d^{4}x\;\mathrm{e}R^{ab}_{\mu\nu}e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\nu}_{b}+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\;\mathrm{e}\bar{\psi}\left(i\gamma^{a}e^{\mu}_{a}D_{\mu}-m\right)\psi\;,

The field equations can be easily computed from (

\displaystyle R^{a}_{\mu}

\displaystyle R^{ab}_{\mu\nu}e^{\nu}_{b}\;,

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{a}_{\mu}e^{\mu}_{a}\;,

(2.10)

being respectively the Ricci tensor and the curvature scalar. For the spin-connection we get,

D_{\nu}\left[\mathrm{e}\left(e_{a}^{\mu}e_{b}^{\nu}-e_{b}^{\mu}e_{a}^{\nu}\right)\right]=k\mathrm{e}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{c}e^{\mu}_{c}\sigma_{ab}\psi\;.

(2.11)

For the
\bar{\psi}

\left(i\gamma^{a}e_{a}^{\mu}D_{\mu}-m\right)\psi=0\;.

(2.12)

From (

\bar{\psi}\left(i\tilde{D}_{\mu}\gamma^{a}e_{a}^{\mu}+m\right)=0\;,

Symmetries and currents

In this section we summarize the main currents relevant to this work, namely, gauge current, chiral currents, and the Bargmann-Wigner current. The most relevant symmetry of a gauge theory is, as expected, the gauge symmetry whose transformations are given in (

Gauge principle and spin current master equations

Following
[
1
,
5
,
9
]
the idea is to construct, by induction, a gauge invariant version of the Bargmann-Wigner current (
3.12
). The result should be an observable of the model, as established by the gauge principle
[
19
,
8
]
, but will not be conserved any more. In the case of electrodynamics coupled with fermions
[

,
5
,
9
]
, this was done by imposing the minimal coupling rule
\partial\longrightarrow D
. In the present case we need extra careful because we are dealing with a non-Abelian gauge theory with non-vanishing background. Henceforth, our
ansatz
is to perform the following substitutions in (
3.12
)

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\psi

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}\psi\;,

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}\omega^{ab}_{\gamma}-\partial_{\gamma}\omega^{ab}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle R^{ab}_{\beta\gamma}\;.

(4.1)

The first one is the minimal coupling rule employed in
[
5
,
9
]
. The second one is intuitively the natural choice. The reason is that in the Abelian example of electrodynamics (or in any Abelian gauge theory), the quantity
\partial_{\beta}\omega^{ab}_{\gamma}-\partial_{\gamma}\omega^{ab}_{\beta}
is indeed the field strength. Hence, the gauge invariant current we are looking for is given by

\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\mathrm{e}}{4k}e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\alpha}_{b}\epsilon_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\nu\beta\gamma}R^{ab}_{\beta\gamma}-\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2}e^{\mu}_{c}e^{\nu}_{a}e^{\alpha}_{b}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{c}\gamma^{5}\sigma^{ab}D_{\alpha}\psi\;.

Since this current is not conserved anymore, it can be composed by two distinguished gauge invariant parts, the bosonic and the fermionic one, respectively written as

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2k}e^{\mu}_{a}\tilde{R}^{a\nu}\;,

\displaystyle F^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2}e^{\mu}_{c}e^{\nu}_{a}e^{\alpha}_{b}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{c}\gamma^{5}\sigma^{ab}D_{\alpha}\psi\;,

(4.3)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{ab}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}^{\phantom{\mu\nu}\alpha\beta}R^{ab}_{\alpha\beta}\;,

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{a\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{b}^{\alpha}\tilde{R}^{ab\;\mu}_{\alpha}\;.

(4.4)

We remark that, for

, characterizing a fixed Minkowski spacetime, the fermionic current in (

) reduces, in form, to the one in

5
Weak field approximation

The weak field approximation is taken from the usual linearization of the vierbein:

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\eta^{a}_{\mu}+h^{a}_{\mu}\;,

\displaystyle e_{a}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\eta_{a}^{\mu}-h_{a}^{\mu}\;,

(5.1)

with
\{\eta^{a}_{\mu},\eta_{a}^{\mu}\}
being the Minkowski vierbeins defining the spacetime background to be the flat Minkowski manifold. The fields
\{h^{a}_{\mu},h_{a}^{\mu}\}

\mathrm{e}\approx 1+\mathrm{h}

\mathrm{h}=\eta^{\mu}_{a}h^{a}_{\mu}

\omega=\underline{\omega}(\eta+h)+\bar{\omega}\approx\frac{\partial\underline{\omega}}{\partial h^{a}_{\mu}}\bigg|_{h=0}h^{a}_{\mu}+\bar{\omega}=z_{a}^{\mu}h^{a}_{\mu}+\bar{\omega}\;,

being the metric independent part of the spin-connection and
\underline{\omega}(\eta)=0

\bar{\omega}
is taken as a small fluctuation. From (

6
Conclusions

In this work we derived the master equations governing the interaction between gravitational fields and gauge invariant spin-currents as broken continuity equations given by (
4.5
) and (
4.9
). The corresponding equations in the weak field approximation are given by equations (
5.8
) and (
5.13
). Our results are valid for weakly interacting charged fermions, in the electromagnetic sense. It is clear from our results that curvature plays an explicit influence on the non-conservation of the spin-currents. Torsion, however, only influences the fermionic sector. Moreover, since the derivative of the vierbein does not appear in the starting action, it does not contribute to the bosonic spin-current. Thence, the contribution for the bosonic spin-current comes exclusively from the spin-connection. This is a particularity of the Einstein-Hilbert action in the first order formalism. Another interesting point is that the gauge current couples to curvature in order to spoil the conservation of the bosonic gauge invariant spin-current. On the fermionic gauge invariant spin-current, it is the chiral current-curvature coupling that contributes to the non conservation of the current. This coupling is similar to the electromagnetic case
[
5
,
9
]
Furthermore, torsion couples to the current
\Theta
defined in (
4.10
). In fact, the current
\Theta
, up to a normalization factor, appear in the right hand side of the non conservation equation of the second chiral current, see (
3.5
).

One more comment can be made, the inclusion of a cosmological constant term
\sim\Lambda\mathrm{e}
will not affect any of our results because it does not contain any field derivatives (so it will not contribute to the Bargmann-Wigner current). Moreover, a cosmological constant term only affects the field equation of the vierbein, which is not used in the obtainment of the master equations.

Our results remain at formal level in this work. Nevertheless, equations (
4.5
) and (
4.9
) (and their linear versions (
5.8
) and (
5.13
)) compose the essence for applications in order to find new physical effects on spin-currents under the influence of gravitational fields. An important observation is that these equations are valid for dynamical gravity systems as well as for non-dynamical gravitational fields (fixed background fields). These applications are left for future investigations. Obvious applications concern gravitational waves and black holes. But effects on Earth gravity, although probably weak, are not ruled out.

Completely decomposable rank-metric codes
Paolo Santonastaso

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate completely decomposable rank-metric codes, i.e. rank-metric codes that are the direct sum of 1-dimensional maximum rank distance codes. We study the weight distribution of such codes, characterizing codewords with certain rank weights. Additionally, we obtain classification results for codes with the largest number of minimum weight codewords within the class of completely decomposable codes.

94B05; 94B65; 94B27

rank-metric code; decomposable code; weight distribution

Introduction

Rank-metric codes have become increasingly popular in the recent years due to their several applications and because of their own interest. These codes have indeed been studied in connection with several algebraic and combinatorial objects, such as semifields, linear sets, tensorial algebras, and skew algebras, see e.g.
[
31
]
. Such codes can be seen as subspaces of matrices over a field, namely
\mathbb{K},
or as subspaces of vectors of length
n
over a degree
m
extension of
\mathbb{K}
.
In this paper, we investigate special classes of codes that are the (external) direct sum of two or more codes, i.e.,
decomposable codes
. The first application of the direct sum of rank-metric codes is discussed in
[
11
]
, dealing with the decoding problem beyond the error-correcting capability of the code. Rank-metric codes have been widely used in network coding, see e.g.
[
5
]
and in the schemes proposed in
[
33
,
32
]
, the authors employ direct sum of maximum rank distance (MRD) codes.

Decomposable codes have also been explored in the literature in other metrics. For instance, in the Hamming metric, classes of efficiently decodable decomposable codes are studied in
[
34
,
13
]
. In
[
23
]
, some properties on the structure of these codes are examined in the context of algebraic geometric codes.

In this paper, we focus on
completely decomposable rank-metric codes
, i.e., rank-metric codes
\C
that are, up to equivalence, the (external) direct sum of
1
-dimensional MRD codes
\C_{1},\ldots,\C_{k}
. We characterize completely decomposable rank-metric codes as those codes that admit a basis
{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}}
such that the sum of the weights of the
\mathbf{c}_{i}
’s equals the length of the code; see
Theorem

3.2
. This property is also expressed in terms of the supports of the codewords of such a basis. Moreover, we show that completely decomposable codes are not minimal rank-metric codes and we can detect the set of minimal codewords, see
Proposition

3.8
. We also relate the study of the weight distribution of a completely decomposable rank-metric code
\C
to some of its shortened and punctured codes. In particular, we characterize codewords with certain rank weights, see
Lemma

4.4
and
Theorem

4.5
. Furthermore, we obtain classification results for codes with the maximum number of minimum weight codewords within the class of completely decomposable codes, see
Theorem

4.9
and
Theorem

4.13
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we first recall some properties of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subpaces of a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space under the action of a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
induced by a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Then, in Subsection
2.2
, we study multiplicative properties of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Finally, in Subsection
2.3
, we recall the results on rank-metric codes and their geometry. In the following,
q
is a prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the finite field with
q
elements.

2.1
Dual of a subspace

We recall the notion of the dual of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of the
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space
V
.
Let
\sigma\colon V\times V\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
be a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form defined on
V
and consider

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sigma^{\prime}:&V\times V&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
&(x,y)&\longmapsto&\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\sigma(x,y)),\end{array}

where
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(x)=x+x^{q}+\ldots+x^{q^{m-1}}
denotes the trace function.
In this way,
\sigma^{\prime}
turns out to be a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form on
V
seen as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space of dimension
km
. Consider
\perp
and
\perp^{\prime}
as the orthogonal complement maps defined by
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
, respectively. For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
V
, the
dual subspace of
U
with respect to
\sigma^{\prime}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
in
V
. If
\perp_{1}^{\prime}
and
\perp_{2}^{\prime}
are orthogonal complement maps defined by the nondegenerate reflexive bilinear forms
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
, respectively, then the dual subspaces
U^{\perp_{1}^{\prime}}
and
U^{\perp_{2}^{\prime}}
of
U
are
\mathrm{GL}(k,q^{m})
-equivalent, cf.
[
27
, Proposition 2.5]
.

Moreover, for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
V
, we have
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}})=km-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)
and for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
, it holds that
W^{\perp^{\prime}}=W^{\perp}
. As a consequence, the following relation holds.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
27
, Property 2.6]
)
.

Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
and
W
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
.
Then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap W^{\perp})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W)+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W).

In the next, we will always consider
V={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and
\sigma
as the standard inner product

\sigma\colon((u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}),(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}))\in(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k})^{2}\mapsto u_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+u_{k}v_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}},

and hence

\sigma^{\prime}\colon((u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}),(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}))\in(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k})^{2}\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(u_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+u_{k}v_{k})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

Note that, when
k=1
,
\sigma^{\prime}=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\cdot)
is a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and in what follows, if
W
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, we define

W^{\perp^{*}}=\{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\colon\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(ab)=0,\mbox{ for every }a\in W\},

the ortogonal complement of
W
with respect to
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\cdot)
.

If
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, the form

(u,v)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}(uv)

is a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
, as well. So, in the following, when it is necessary, we use the symbol
\perp^{*}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}
to denote the orthogonal complement defined by
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
.

When
W
is generated by a subsequence of a geometric progression of a generator
\lambda
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
,
W^{\perp^{*}}
is as follows.

Proposition 2.2

(see
[
26
, Proposition 2.8]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
be such
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\lambda)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Let
W=\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, with
t\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
. Then
W^{\perp^{*}}=\delta^{-1}\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{m-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, where
\delta=f^{\prime}(\lambda)
and
f(x)
is the minimal polynomial of
\lambda
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Recall that if
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, then the
degree
of
\lambda
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the degree of the minimal
polynomial of
\lambda
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, or equivalently, it is the smallest integer
e
such that
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
.
We extend the above lemma in the case that
\lambda
has degree less than
m
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
m=se
, with
s,e>1
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
having degree
e
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and let
t\leq e
be a positive integer. Then

(\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})^{\perp^{*}}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}})\oplus c\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{e-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},

for some
c\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}\setminus\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}))
.

Proof.

Note that

{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}^{\perp^{*}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}})=:Z,

(1)

and
Z
is and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
having dimension
s-1
. By
Proposition

2.2
, we have that

(\langle 1,\ldots,\lambda^{t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})^{\perp^{*}_{{q^{e}/q}}}=d\langle 1,\ldots,\lambda^{e-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},

(2)

for some
d\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}^{*}
, where here the orthogonal complement
\perp^{*}_{q^{e}/q}
is defined with respect to
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{e}/q}(\cdot)
. Let
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
such that
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}(c)=d
. Note that
Z\cap c\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{e-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}=\{0\}
. Indeed, assume that there exist
z\in Z
and
u\in\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{e-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, such that
z=cu
. Therefore, since
Z
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace, we get that
c\in Z
and so, by (
1
), we have

0\neq d=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}(c)=0,

a contradiction.
Now, we prove that, with this choice of
c
, we have

(\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})^{\perp^{*}}=Z\oplus c\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{e-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}.

For
z\in Z,u\in\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{e-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and
w\in\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, we get

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}((z+cu)w)&=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(zw)+\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(cuw)\\
&=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{e}/q}\left(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}(zw)\right)+\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{e}/q}\left(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}(cuw)\right)\\
&=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{e}/q}\left(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}}(zw)\right)+\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{e}/q}\left((du)w\right)\\
&=0+0,\end{array}

where the last equality follows by (
1
) and (
2
), proving the assertion.
∎

Finally, we also show how the duality
\perp^{\prime}
acts on the direct product of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, that will be useful in the next.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and define
U=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Then

U^{\perp^{\prime}}=U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}}.

Proof.

For
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\in U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}}
, and for every
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in U
,

\begin{array}[]{rl}\sigma^{\prime}((a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}),(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}))&=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+a_{k}b_{k})\\
&=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{1}b_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{k}b_{k})\\
&=0,\end{array}

implying that

U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}}\subseteq U^{\perp^{\prime}}.

(3)

Now, if
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
, for every
i
, we have that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}
and hence,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}})=km-(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k})
. On the other hand,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}})=m-n_{i}
and so
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\cdots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}})=km-(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k})
. Therefore

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\cdots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}})

implying the equality in (
3
).
∎

2.2
Product of subspaces

The study of the properties of the product of subspaces in a finite field extension is related to the linear analogue of some problems arising from additive combinatorics, such as Kneser’s Theorem
[
15
]
and Vosper’s theorems
[
16
,
35
]
. In recent years, such investigations have been related to different contexts, such as in designing probabilistic decoding algorithms of LRPC codes
[
12
,
10
]
, proposed in ROLLO
[
22
]
for the NIST post-quantum cryptography competition, and in the classifications of geometric objects
[
24
,
26
]
. In the following, we recall some useful results and provide a characterization for pairs
(U_{1},U_{2})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces such that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of their product is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.

For a subset
S
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, we denote by
\langle S\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace generated by
S
and for two
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, we define

U_{1}U_{2}:=\langle\{a_{1}a_{2}\colon a_{1}\in U_{1},a_{2}\in U_{2}\}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}.

Note, that if
U_{1}=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and
U_{2}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, we have that

U_{1}U_{2}=a_{1}U_{2}+\ldots+a_{t}U_{2},

(4)

where the sum on the right-hand side is intended to be the sum of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector spaces. Moreover, via the duality
\perp^{*}
defined in the above section, we also have

(U_{1}U_{2})^{\perp^{*}}=(a_{1}U_{2}+\ldots+a_{t}U_{2})^{\perp^{*}}=a_{1}^{-1}U_{2}^{\perp^{*}}\cap\ldots\cap a_{t}^{-1}U_{2}^{\perp^{*}}.

(5)

For prime extensions, the linear analog of the Cauchy-Davenport inequality reads as follows.

Theorem 2.5

(see
[
14
, Corollary 2.5]
and
[
4
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Assume that
m
is a prime. Let
U_{1},U_{2}
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}),\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})\geq 1
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})\leq m-1
. Then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})\geq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})-1.

(6)

The following classification of pairs of subspaces
(U_{1},U_{2})
attaining the equality in (
6
), also known as
critical pair
, holds.

Theorem 2.6

(see
[
3
, Theorem 3]
and
[
3
, Lemma 5]
)
.

Assume that
m
is a prime. Let
U_{1},U_{2}
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
such that
2\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}),\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})
.

1.

If
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})\leq m-2
and

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})-1,

(7)

then
U_{1}=c_{1}\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1})-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and
U_{2}=c_{2}\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, for some
c_{1},c_{2},\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
.

2.

If
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})\leq m-1
,
U_{1}=c_{1}\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1})-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, for some
c_{1},\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})-1,

(8)

then
U_{2}=c_{2}\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, for some
c_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
.

In the next lemma, we characterize
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
satisfying (
8
), and for which
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})=m-1
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
U_{1},U_{2}
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, with
1\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}),\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})<m
. Assume that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2})=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1})
. If

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})=m-1,

then
U_{2}=cU_{1}^{\perp^{*}}
, for some
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}U_{2})=m-1
, there exists
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, such that
U_{1}U_{2}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(bx))
, see e.g.
[
18
]
. This means that for any
w\in U_{2}
, we have that

\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(buw)=0,

for every
u\in U_{1}
. Hence,
bw\in U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}
. This implies that
bU_{2}\subseteq U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}
. Finally, by a dimensional argument, we get the assertion.
∎

2.3
Rank-metric codes

The
(rank) weight

{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{v})
of a vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-dimension of the vector space generated over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by its entries, i.e,
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{v})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})
. The rank distance between two vectors
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is defined as
d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})={\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}).

An
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

(rank-metric) code

\C
is a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
endowed with the rank distance, where the parameter
d
is its
minimum distance
, and it is defined as
d=\min\{d(\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2})\colon\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\in\C,\mathbf{c}_{1}\neq\mathbf{c}_{2}\}.
We also write that
\C
is an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code if the parameter
d
is not known/relevant.
A
generator matrix
for
\C
is a matrix
G\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
, such that
\C=\{\mathbf{x}G\colon\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\}
.

Moreover, we denote by
A_{i}(\C)
, or simply by
A_{i}
, the number of codewords in
\C
of weight
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
and
(A_{0},\ldots,A_{n})
is called the
weight distribution
of
\C
.

A Singleton-like bound for a rank-metric code holds. For an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code, it holds

mk\leq\max\{m,n\}(\min\{n,m\}-d+1),

(9)

see
[
9
]
. An
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is said to be
maximum rank distance code (MRD)
if its parameters attains the equality in (
9
).

We also recall the notion of support of a vector.
Let
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m})
be an ordered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. For any vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
define the matrix
\Gamma(\mathbf{v})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times m}
, where

v_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\mathbf{v})_{ij}\gamma_{j},\qquad\mbox{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

that is
\Gamma(\mathbf{v})
is the matrix expansion of the vector
\mathbf{v}
with respect to the basis
\Gamma
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. The
(rank) support
of
\mathbf{v}
is defined as the column span of
\Gamma(\mathbf{v})
, i.e.

\supp(\mathbf{v})=\mathrm{colsp}(\Gamma(\mathbf{v}))\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}.
The support of a vector does not depend on the choice of
\Gamma
and we can talk about the support of a vector without mentioning
\Gamma
, see e.g.
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
and clearly,
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{v})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\supp(\mathbf{v}))
. Finally, the support of a code
\C
is the sum of the supports of the codewords defining a basis for
\C
, i.e.

\supp(\C):=\supp(\mathbf{c}_{1})+\ldots+\supp(\mathbf{c}_{k}),

(10)

where
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}\}
is a basis of
\C
and the sum on the right-hand side is intended to be of vector spaces, see e.g.
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
. We also refer to
[
2
,
21
]
, for more details on the rank support. We will say that two codes
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if and only if there exist a matrix
A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,q)
such that

\C^{\prime}=\C\cdot A=\{\mathbf{c}A:\mathbf{c}\in\C\}
. We say that an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code is
nondegenerate
if
\supp(\C)={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, or equivalently if the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-column span of any generator matrix of
\C
has dimension
n
.

2.4
The geometry of rank-metric codes

The geometric counterpart of rank-metric codes are the systems.

Definition 2.8
.

An
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
(or simply
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
)
system

U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
n
, such that

\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and

d=n-\max\left\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap H)\mid H\textnormal{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}$-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Two
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems

Rank-metric codes and systems are related in the following way. Let
\C
be an

-subspace

\C

U
.

For a vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}

\mathbf{x}^{\perp}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}y_{i}=0\}.

The following holds.

Theorem 2.9

(see
[
30

)
.

Let

(11)

As a consequence, for an

Moreover, it can be proved that two codes are equivalent if and only if their associated systems are equivalent.
The above argument allows to estabilish a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of

3
Direct sum of 1-dimensional MRD codes

In this section, we investigate
completely decomposable codes
, i.e. codes that are, up to equivalence, the (external) direct sum of
1
-dimensional MRD codes.

Let
\C_{1},\ldots,\C_{t}
be codes such that
\C_{i}
is an
[n_{i},k_{i}]_{q^{m}/q}
code. We define the
(external) direct sum

\C_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\C_{t}
of
\C_{1},\ldots,\C_{t}
as the
[n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t},k_{1}+\ldots+k_{t}]_{q^{m}/q}
code defined as

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\C_{i}=\C_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\C_{t}:=\{(\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{t})\colon\mathbf{c}_{i}\in\C_{i}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{t}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}}.

(14)

We investigate codes as in (
14
), where the
\C_{i}
’s are 1-dimensional MRD codes, or equivalently, 1-dimensional nondegenerate codes.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\C
be an
[n,1]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then
\C
is an MRD code if and only if
\C
is nondegenerate, or in other words
\C=\langle\mathbf{c}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, with
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})=n
.

Proof.

Assume that
\C
is an MRD code. Then
m=\max\{m,n\}(\min\{m,n\}-d+1)
, implying that

d=\min\{m,n\}-\frac{m}{\max\{m,n\}}+1.

The minimum distance
d
is a positive integer and so
\max\{m,n\}=m
, from which
n\leq m
and
d=n
. Thus,
\C=\langle\mathbf{c}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, for some
\mathbf{c}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
of weight
n
, implying that
\C
is a nondegenerate code.
Conversely, if
\C
is nondegenerate then necessarily
n\leq m
and
d=n
. Therefore, the equality in (
9
) holds.
∎

In the following, for
\ell
matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{\ell}
, where
A_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{t_{i}\times n_{i}}
, the direct sum
A_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus A_{\ell}
is the block matrix

A_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus A_{\ell}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&A_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\ddots&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&A_{\ell}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{(t_{1}+\ldots+t_{\ell})\times(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{\ell})}.

In the next result, we characterize codes that are, up to equivalence, the direct sum of 1-dimensional MRD codes as those that admit a basis
{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}}
such that the sum of the weights of the
\mathbf{c}_{i}
’s equals the length of the code. Additionally, equivalent conditions in terms of support of the
\mathbf{c}_{i}
’s and of generator matrix for such codes are provided.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
m>n_{1}\geq\ldots\geq n_{k}\geq 1
be positive integers. Then the following are equivalent:

1)

\C
is equivalent to a code
\C^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C_{i}
, where
\C_{i}\mbox{ is an MRD }[n_{i},1]_{q^{m}/q}\mbox{ code, for every }i
;

2)

\C
admits a basis
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}\}
such that
n=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}
and

\supp(\C)=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{k}\supp(\mathbf{c}_{i}),

(15)

with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\supp(\mathbf{c}_{i}))=n_{i}
, for every
i
;

3)

\C
admits a basis
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}\}
such that

n=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}n_{i},\mbox{ with }n_{i}={\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c}_{i})
, for every
i
;

4)

a generator matrix of a code equivalent to
\C
is of the form

G=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mathbf{u}_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\ddots&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\mathbf{u}_{k}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{u}_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k},

where
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{1\times n_{i}}
and
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u}_{i})=n_{i}
, for each
i
.

Proof.

\underline{1)\Rightarrow 2)}
.
Assume that
\C
is equivalent to a code
\C^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C_{i}
, where
\C_{i}
is an MRD
[n_{i},1]_{q^{m}/q}
code, for every
i
. So,
\C_{i}=\langle\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\prime}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, with
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\prime})=n_{i}
, for any
i
. Clearly
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}
. Now, since
\C
is equivalent to
\C^{\prime}
, there exists a matrix
A\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}(n,q)
such that
\C=\C^{\prime}\cdot A
. Then the codewords

\mathbf{c}_{1}^{\prime\prime}=(\mathbf{c}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0})A,\mathbf{c}_{2}^{\prime\prime}=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{c}_{2}^{\prime},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0})A,\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}^{\prime\prime}=(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{c}_{k}^{\prime})A\in\C^{\prime}\cdot A=\C,

form a basis of
\C
. Note that

\supp(\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\prime\prime})=\supp((\mathbf{0},\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0}))A=(\underbrace{(0,\ldots,0)}_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}\times\underbrace{(0,\ldots,0)}_{n_{i+1}+\cdots+n_{k}})A,

see e.g.
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
, from which we get the assertion.

\underline{2)\Rightarrow 3)}
.
The assertion immediately follows by the fact that
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\supp(\mathbf{c}))
, for any codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\C
.

\underline{3)\Rightarrow 4)}
.
Assume that there exists a basis
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}\}
of
\C
as in the hypotheses. Let
G^{\prime}
be a generator matrix for
\C
having as rows the codewords
\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{c}_{1}=\mathbf{e}_{1}G^{\prime},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}=\mathbf{e}_{k}G^{\prime},

where the
\mathbf{e}_{i}
’s are the vectors of the standard basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Let
U
be a system associated with
\C
defined as the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-column span of
G^{\prime}
.
By using (
11
), we have that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\perp})=n-{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c}_{i})\mbox{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\perp})=kn-n.

Let consider the dual subspace
U^{\perp^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of
U
. Then
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}})=km-n
and by (
13
), we get

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle\mathbf{e}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})=km-n.

Hence,

U^{\perp^{\prime}}=(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle\mathbf{e}_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})\oplus\ldots\oplus(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle\mathbf{e}_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}),

i.e.
U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle\mathbf{e}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\{(0,\ldots,0,w_{i},0,\ldots,0)\colon w_{i}\in W_{i}\}
, where
W_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
having dimension
m-n_{i}
. This implies that

U^{\perp^{\prime}}=W_{1}\times W_{2}\times\ldots\times W_{k}.

Now, by
Proposition

2.4
, we have that

U=(U^{\perp^{\prime}})^{\perp^{\prime}}=(W_{1}\times\ldots\times W_{k})^{\perp^{\prime}}=U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\ldots\times U_{k},

with
U_{i}=W_{i}^{\perp^{*}}
, where
W_{i}^{\perp^{*}}=\{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\colon\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(ab)=0,\mbox{ for any }b\in W_{i}\}
. Also,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W_{i})=n_{i}
. The assertion follows by the definition of system associated to
\C
and Remark
2.10
.

\underline{4)\Rightarrow 1)}
.
Clearly, the code having
G
as a generator matrix is of the form
\bigoplus_{i}^{k}\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
. Hence,
\C
is equivalent to
\bigoplus_{i}^{k}\C_{i}
, where
\C_{i}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
and so the assertion follows.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

An
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code

is said to be

such that
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u}_{i})=n_{i}
, for each
i
, will be said to be in

.

By Theorem
3.2
, we immediately obtain the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

A completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
is nondegenerate.

.

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

[

.

Remark 3.6
.

Theorem

3.2
shows that codes that are, up to equivalence, direct sum of

-dimensional MRD codes can be also characterized as those nondegenerate codes admitting a basis
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}\}

\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{k}

In the next, we prove that the type of a completely decomposable code is uniquely determined.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. If
\C
is equivalent to a completely decomposable code
\C^{\prime}
of type
(n^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,n^{\prime}_{k})
, then
n_{i}=n^{\prime}_{i}
, for any
i
.

Proof.

Assume that
\C
is equivalent to a completely decomposable code
\C^{\prime}
of type
(n^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,n^{\prime}_{k})
and suppose, by contradiction that

n_{1}=n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{\ell}=n_{\ell}^{\prime}
and
n_{\ell+1}\neq n_{\ell+1}
, for some
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
n_{\ell+1}>n^{\prime}_{\ell+1}
.
By
Remark

3.5
, we know that, up to equivalence, there exist a system of the form
U=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k}
and a system of the form
U^{\prime}=U_{1}^{\prime}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\prime}
associated with
\C
and with
\C^{\prime}
, respectively, where
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}_{i})=n^{\prime}_{i}
. Since
\C
is equivalent to
\C^{\prime}
, then the associated systems are equivalent. Therefore, there exists a matrix
B\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}(k,q^{m})
, such that

U^{\prime}=U\cdot B=\{\mathbf{x}B\colon\mathbf{x}\in U\},

see Remark
2.10
.
Let consider
W=\langle\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{\ell+1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},
where the
\mathbf{e}_{i}
’s are the vectors of the standard basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, and let
W^{\prime}=W\cdot B=\{\mathbf{u}B\colon\mathbf{u}\in W\}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{rl}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime})&=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((U\cdot B)\cap(W\cdot B))\\
&=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((U\cap W)\cdot B)\\
&=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+1}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{\ell+1}\\
&=n_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+n^{\prime}_{\ell}+n_{\ell+1}.\end{array}

(16)

We complete a basis of
W^{\prime}
to a basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
by adding some vectors
\mathbf{e}_{i_{\ell+2}},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}
of the standard basis
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{k}\}
.
Therefore

\begin{array}[]{rl}n&=n^{\prime}_{1}+\ldots+n^{\prime}_{k}\\
&=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime})\\
&=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}\cap(W^{\prime}\oplus\langle\mathbf{e}_{i_{\ell+2}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\ldots\oplus\langle\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})))\\

Completely decomposable codes are examples of rank-metric codes that are not minimal but for which the set of minimal codewords can be described. The notion of minimal code in the rank metric was introduced and studied in
[
2
]
,
where an its geometric characterization was provided.

Let
\C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. A nonzero codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\C
is a
minimal codeword
if for every nonzero
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\C
,
\supp(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\subseteq\supp(\mathbf{c})
implies that
\mathbf{c}=\alpha\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
, for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. We say that
\C
is

minimal
if all its nonzero codewords are minimal.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, with
k\geq 2
and
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
be an its generator matrix in a weight complementary form. Then
\C
is not a minimal code and the set of minimal codewords of
\C
is

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\left\{(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\alpha\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0})\colon\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{c}
be a nonzero codeword of
\C
. Then
\mathbf{c}
is of the form
\mathbf{c}=(\alpha_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})
, for some
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. It is easy to see that if there exist two different indices
i\neq j
such that
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\neq 0
, then the codeword
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\alpha_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0})
is such that
\supp(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\subseteq\supp(\mathbf{c})
. However,
\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
are not
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-proportional and so
\mathbf{c}
is not minimal. Therefore, if
\mathbf{c}
is a minimal codeword of
\C
, then
\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\alpha_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0})
for some
i
and
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. Now, we prove that if

\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\alpha_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0})
then it is minimal. Indeed,
note that

\supp(\mathbf{c})=\supp((\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\alpha_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0}))\subseteq\{0\}^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}\times\{0\}^{n_{i+1}+\cdots+n_{k}}.

By using that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\supp(\mathbf{c}))={\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})=n_{i}
, we get that

\supp(\mathbf{c})=\{0\}^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}\times\{0\}^{n_{i+1}+\cdots+n_{k}}.

Let
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=(\beta_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})
be a nonzero codeword of
\C
such that
\supp(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\subseteq\supp(\mathbf{c})
. Observe that, for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we have that
\supp(\mathbf{c}_{j})\subseteq\supp(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})
, where
\mathbf{c}_{j}=(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\beta_{j}\mathbf{u}_{j},\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0})
. Therefore, using that
\supp(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\subseteq\supp(\mathbf{c})
, we get
\beta_{j}=0
, for any
j\neq i
, implying that
\mathbf{c}=\alpha\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
, for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. This proves the assertion.
∎

Finally, we investigate the dual operation on completely decomposable codes. Recall that, the
dual code
of a code
\C
is

\C^{\perp}=\left\{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}v_{i}=0,\mbox{ for every }(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\C\right\}.

If
\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C_{i}
is a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
then its dual is
\C^{\perp}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C_{i}^{\perp_{i}}
, where
\C_{i}^{\perp_{i}}
is the dual of
\C_{i}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
, for every
i
. In general, it is not the direct sum of
1
-dimensional MRD codes. This means the property of being completely decomposable is not preserved under duality. On the other hand, we prove that such property is preserved under the operation of the geometric dual, recently introduced in
[
6
]
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
and let
U
be a system associated with
\C
. Assume that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})<m
, for any
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.
Then a
geometric dual
of
\C
(with respect to
\perp^{\prime}
) is defined as
\C^{\prime}
, where
\C^{\prime}
is any code associated with the system
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
.

Remark 3.10
.

In order to give the definition of the geometric dual of a rank-metric code, we have assumed that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})<m
for any
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. This condition is equivalent to the one used originally in
[
6
]
, which relies on the notion of generalized rank weights.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Let
U
be a system associated with
\C
. Then
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})<m
, for any
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and a geometric dual
\C^{\prime}
of
\C
is a completely decomposable
[km-n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(m-n_{k},\ldots,m-n_{1})
.

Proof.

By
Remark

3.5
, we know that, up to equivalence, there exists a system associated with
\C
of the form

U=U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\ldots\times U_{k}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}.

First, we prove that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})<m
, for any
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Clearly, the assertion is true, if
m>n
. So, assume that
m\leq n
and suppose by contradiction that there exists a nonzero
\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=m
. Then, there exists some
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, such that

\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\colon i=1,\ldots,k\mbox{ and }i\neq j\}\cup\{\mathbf{u}\}

is a basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Therefore,

n=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)\geq\sum_{i\neq j}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle\mathbf{e}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=n-n_{j}+m>n,

a contradiction. So,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})<m
, for any
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.
Now, by definition, a system associated with
\C^{\prime}
is

U^{\perp^{\prime}}=(U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k})^{\perp^{\prime}}=U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}},

with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=m-n_{i}
, where the last equality follows from
Proposition

2.4
.
Then the assertion follows by
Theorem

3.2
.
∎

4
On the weight distribution of completely decomposable codes

In general, determining the weight distribution of a code from its structure is a challenging task, even providing some information about it is difficult. For rank-metric codes, the weight distribution of optimal codes is uniquely determined, see
[
9
]
. Except for such codes, very few is known about the weight distribution for other classes of codes. For some classes of
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank metric codes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, particularly when
k\in\{2,3\}
, the weight distributions have been determined relying on their specific geometric structures, see, e.g.,
[
24
,
29
,
17
,
28
,
19
]
.

In this section, we show that for completely decomposable codes, we can describe some properties on the weight distribution and codewords having certain weights. Also, we provide the number of minimum weight codewords of a completely decomposable code relying on properties of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
associated with the code. As a consequence, we determine bounds on the number of minimum weight codes and we also provide constructions attaining such bounds. Finally, we characterize completely decomposable codes having the maximum number of minimum weight codewords.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable codes of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. By definition, up to equivalence, a generator matrix of
\C
is of the form
\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{u}_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k},
where
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{1\times n_{i}}
and
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u}_{i})=n_{i}
, for each
i
. In other words,

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C_{i},

(17)

with
\C_{i}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, for every
i
. Since the metric properties of a code are invariant under equivalence, we often consider
\C
as in (
17
).

We start with the following lower bound on the weight of a codeword
\mathbf{c}
of a completely decomposable code, depending on how we write
\mathbf{c}
as a linear combination of the fixed basis of
\C
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
and
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
be an its generator matrix in a weight complementary form. Then any nonzero codeword
\mathbf{c}=(\alpha_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})
has weight

{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})\geq\max\{n_{j}:\alpha_{j}\neq 0\}.

(18)

In particular,
\C
is an
[n,k,n_{k}]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

Proof.

The assertion immediately follows by the fact that if a codeword
\mathbf{c}=(\alpha_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})\in\C
is such that
\alpha_{j}\neq 0
, for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, then

{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})\geq{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u}_{j})=n_{j}.

∎

Proposition
4.1
and the Singleton-like bound imply that for any completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, it holds

mk\leq\max\{m,n\}(\min\{m,n\}-n_{k}+1).

(19)

Clearly if
n_{1}=\cdots=n_{k}=1
, with
k>1
, or in other words
n=k
, then
\C={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and it is an MRD code. Now, we show that completely decomposable codes are not MRD codes for
n>k
.

Proposition 4.2
.

A completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, with
n>k>1
, is not an MRD code.

Proof.

First assume that
m\geq n
. Then, (
19
) reads as
k=n-n_{k}+1=(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k-1}+1)
and so
n_{1}=\ldots=n_{k}=1
and
n=k
, a contradiction to our assumptions. Now, assume that
n>m
. We known that, up to equivalence, there exists a system associated with
\C
of the form
U=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k}
, where
U_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
having dimension
n_{i}
. If
\C
is an MRD codes, then by
[
36
, Section 5]
, see also
[
19
, Theorem 4.9]
, we get that
n=mk/(h+1)
, for some positive integer
h
such that
h+1\mid mk
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W)\leq h
, for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
having dimension
h
. Therefore,
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
implies that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\leq 1
, for any
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle\mathbf{e}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=n_{i}
, where the
\mathbf{e}_{i}
’s are standard basis vectors, we have that
n_{1}=\ldots=n_{k}=1
. So,
n=k
and we still get a contradiction.
∎

In the next, we show some properties on the weight distribution of completely decomposable codes.
For this aim, the operations of shortening and puncturing on rank-metric codes are very useful. We refer to
[
7
,
20
]
for their properties.

We will use special type of shortening and puncturing based on the structure of completely decomposable codes.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
and
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
be an its generator matrix in a weight complementary form. Let
t\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. We call

\Sigma(\C,t):=\{(\alpha_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})\in\C\mid\alpha_{i}=0,\mbox{ for any }i=1,\ldots,t-1\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}

and

\Pi(\C,t):=\{(\alpha_{t}\mathbf{u}_{t},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})\mid\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{t}+\cdots+n_{k}}

the
t
-shortened code
and
t
-punctured code
of
\C
, respectively.

Note that, for any
t\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
\Sigma(\C,t)
is an
[n,k-t+1,n_{k}]_{q^{m}/q}
code and
\Pi(\C,t)
is a completely decomposable
[n_{t}+\cdots+n_{k},k-t+1,n_{k}]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{t},\ldots,n_{k})
.

By Proposition
4.1
, if a codeword
\mathbf{c}\notin\Sigma(\C,t+1)
, we have that
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})\geq n_{t}
. In the next result, we describe the codewords contained in
\Sigma(\C,t)\setminus\Sigma(\C,t+1)
having weight
n_{t}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Let
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
be an its generator matrix in a weight complementary form and let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
, for every
i
.
For any integer
t\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
, the set of codewords of
\Sigma(\C,t)\setminus\Sigma(\C,t+1)
of weight
n_{t}
is

\mathcal{W}_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\beta(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{u}_{t},\xi_{t+1}\mathbf{u}_{t+1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})\colon&\xi_{h}\in\left(U_{t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)^{\perp^{*}},\\
&\mbox{for }h\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}\mbox{ and }\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}\end{array}\right\}.

Proof.

Assume that
U_{t}^{\perp^{*}}=\langle a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m-n_{t}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and, by (
4
), we note that

U_{t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-n_{t}}a^{\prime}_{i}U_{h}.

Moreover, there exists a system associated with the code of the form
U=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k}
and its dual is
U^{\perp^{\prime}}=U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}}
, by
Proposition

2.4
.
First we prove that the codewords of
\C
contained in
\mathcal{W}_{t}
have weight equal to
n_{t}
. Let
\mathbf{c}=\beta(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{u}_{t},\xi_{t+1}\mathbf{u}_{t+1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})\in\mathcal{W}_{t}
. Since
\xi_{h}\in\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-n_{t}}a^{\prime}_{i}U_{h}\right)^{\perp^{*}}=a_{1}^{\prime-1}U_{h}^{\perp^{*}}\cap\ldots\cap a_{m-n_{t}}^{\prime-1}U_{h}^{\perp^{*}}
, where the equality follows by (
5
), we get

\xi_{h}=w_{h,1}a_{1}^{\prime-1}=\ldots=w_{h,m-n_{t}}a_{m-n_{t}}^{\prime-1},

(20)

for some
w_{h,1},\ldots,w_{h,m-n_{1}}\in U_{h}^{\perp^{*}}
, for any
h\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}
. Let

\mathbf{z}_{j}=(0,\ldots,0,a_{j}^{\prime},w_{t+1,j},\ldots,w_{k,j})\in U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}},

for
j\in\{1,\ldots,m-n_{t}\}
.
Since
a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m-n_{t}}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent, we get that
\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{m-n_{t}}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent, as well, and by using (
20
), we have
\langle\mathbf{z}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=\ldots=\langle\mathbf{z}_{m-n_{t}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
. It follows that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}})\cap\langle\mathbf{z}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq m-n_{t}.

Now,
\mathbf{c}=\frac{\beta}{a_{1}^{\prime}}\mathbf{z}_{1}G

and hence,
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})={\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{z}_{1}G)\leq n_{t}
, by (
13
). Taking into account (
18
), we get that
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})=n_{t}
.
Conversely, let
\mathbf{c}=(\alpha_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})\in\Sigma(\C,t)\setminus\Sigma(\C,t+1)
of weight
n_{t}
. Then
\alpha_{i}=0
, if
i<t
,
\alpha_{t}\neq 0
and
\mathbf{c}=\alpha_{t}(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{u}_{t},\beta_{t+1}\mathbf{u}_{t+1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})
, with
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}/\alpha_{t}
. So,
\mathbf{c}=\alpha_{t}\mathbf{z}G
, with
\mathbf{z}=(0,\ldots,0,1,\beta_{t+1},\ldots,\beta_{k})
. Since
n_{t}={\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{c})={\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{z}G)
, then, by using (
13
), we get
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((U_{t}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}})\cap\langle\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=m-n_{t}
. Therefore, there exist
m-n_{t}

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent vectors such that

\langle(0,\ldots,0,w_{t,1},\ldots,w_{k,1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,w_{t,m-n_{t}},\ldots,w_{k,m-n_{t}})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}=(U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}})\cap\langle\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}.

In particular,
w_{t,1},\ldots,w_{t,m-n_{t}}\neq 0
and
\beta_{i}=w_{i,h}/w_{t,h}
, for any
h\in\{t+1,\ldots,m-n_{t}\}
and any
i\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}
.
Note that, in this way,
w_{t,1},\ldots,w_{t,m-n_{t}}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent.
Then
\langle w_{t,1},\ldots,w_{t,m-n_{t}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}=U_{t}^{\perp^{\prime}}
and hence there exists a matrix
B=(b_{ij})\in\mathrm{GL}(m-n_{t},q)
such that

B\left(\begin{matrix}w_{t,1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{t,m-n_{t}}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1}^{\prime}\\
\vdots\\
a_{m-n_{t}}^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right).

For any
i\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}
, define
\overline{w}_{i,1},\ldots,\overline{w}_{i,m-n_{t}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
as

\left(\begin{matrix}\overline{w}_{i,1}\\
\vdots\\
\overline{w}_{i,m-n_{t}}\end{matrix}\right)=B\left(\begin{matrix}w_{i,1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{i,m-n_{t}}\end{matrix}\right).

Hence,
\overline{w}_{i,1},\ldots,\overline{w}_{i,m-n_{t}}\in U_{i}^{\perp^{\prime}}
, for any
i\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}
and for
h\in\{1,\ldots,m-n_{t}\}

\sum_{l=1}^{m-n_{t}}b_{hl}(0,\ldots,0,w_{t,l},\ldots,w_{k,l})=(0,\ldots,0,a_{h}^{\prime},\overline{w}_{2,h},\ldots,\overline{w}_{k,h})\in U_{1}^{\perp^{\prime}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{\prime}}.

This implies that

(0,\ldots,0,a_{h}^{\prime},\overline{w}_{2,h},\ldots,\overline{w}_{k,h})\in(U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}})\cap\langle\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},

for any
h\in\{1,\ldots,m-n_{t}\}
. Let
\beta_{h}=\overline{w}_{h,i}/a_{i}^{\prime}
. So
\beta_{h}\in a_{i}^{\prime-1}U_{h}^{\perp^{*}}
, for every
i\in\{t+1,\ldots,m-n_{t}\}
providing that
\beta_{h}\in a_{1}^{\prime-1}U_{h}^{\perp^{*}}\cap\ldots\cap a_{m-n_{t}}^{\prime-1}U_{h}^{\perp^{*}}=\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-n_{t}}a^{\prime}_{i}U_{h}\right)^{\perp^{*}}=\left(U_{1}^{\perp^{\prime}}U_{h}\right)^{\perp^{*}}
, where the last two equalities follow by (
5
) and (
4
), respectively. Therefore,
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{W}_{t}
, that proves our assertion.
∎

Now, we are able to describe all the minimum weight codewords. For a subset
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
and a positive integer
i
, we denote by
A_{i}(S)
the number of elements in
S
having weight
i
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
having as generator matrix
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
in a weight complementary form and let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
, for every
i
.
Assume that

n_{k}=\ldots=n_{k-{\ell}}\neq n_{k-{\ell}-1}
, for some
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
, with
n_{0}:=0
.
The number
A_{n_{k}}(\C)
of codewords of weight
n_{k}
, is

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=\sum_{t=k-\ell}^{k}A_{n_{k}}\left(\Sigma(\C,t)\setminus\Sigma(\C,t+1)\right)=\begin{cases}q^{m}-1&\mbox{ if }\ell=0,\\
(q^{m}-1)\left(\sum\limits_{i=k-\ell}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{h=i+1}^{k}q^{j_{i,h}}+1\right)&\mbox{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

where
j_{i,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)
, for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
.

Proof.

A codeword
\mathbf{c}
of
\C
is of the form
\mathbf{c}=(\alpha_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\alpha\mathbf{u}_{k})
, for some
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. If
\mathbf{c}
has minimum weight
n_{k}
, by (
18
), we need to have
\mathbf{c}\in\Sigma(\C,k-\ell)
. This implies that the codewords of
\C
having weight
n_{k}
are of the form

(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\alpha_{k-\ell}\mathbf{u}_{k-\ell},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}),

for some
\alpha_{k-\ell},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Clearly, if
\ell=0
, then
A_{n_{k}}=q^{m}-1
. So, assume that
\ell\geq 1
. Denote by
\mathcal{W}_{t}
the subset of
\Sigma(\C,t)\setminus\Sigma(\C,t+1)
in which all the codewords having weight
n_{k}
. The sets
\mathcal{W}_{k-\ell},\ldots,\mathcal{W}_{k}
determine a partition of the codewords of
\C
having weight
n_{k}
, and since
\lvert\mathcal{W}_{t}\rvert=A_{n_{k}}(\Sigma(\C,t)\setminus\Sigma(\C,t+1))
, we get

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=\sum_{t=k-\ell}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{W}_{t}\rvert=\sum_{t=k-\ell}^{k}A_{n_{k}}(\Sigma(\C,t)\setminus\Sigma(\C,t+1)).

So in order to determine the quantity
A_{n_{k}}(\C)
, it is enough to compute
\lvert\mathcal{W}_{t}\rvert
, for any
t\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k\}
. Clearly,
\lvert\mathcal{W}_{k}\rvert=q^{m}-1
. By Lemma
4.4
, we get that

\mathcal{W}_{t}=\left\{\beta(\mathbf{0},\ldots,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{u}_{t},\xi_{t+1}\mathbf{u}_{t+1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})\colon\xi_{h}\in\left(U_{t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)^{\perp^{*}},\mbox{ for }h\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}\right\},

for every
t\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
.
As a consequence,

\lvert\mathcal{W}_{t}\rvert=(q^{m}-1)q^{j_{t,t+1}+\ldots+j_{t,k}},

where
j_{t,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)
, for every
t\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}
. Finally, we have

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=\sum_{t=k-\ell}^{k-1}\lvert\mathcal{W}_{t}\rvert+q^{m}-1=(q^{m}-1)\left(\sum_{t=k-\ell}^{k-1}q^{j_{t,t+1}+\ldots+j_{t,k}}+1\right),

where
j_{t,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)
, for
t\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{t+1,\ldots,k\}
.
∎

As a consequence of
Theorem

4.5
, we derive upper and lower bound on the number of minimum weight codewords.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Assume that

n_{k}=\ldots=n_{k-{\ell}}\neq n_{k-{\ell}-1}
, for some
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
, with
n_{0}:=0
.
Then

(q^{m}-1)(\ell+1)\leq A_{n_{k}}(\C)\leq(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{(\ell+1)(m-n_{k})}-1}{q^{m-n_{k}}-1}.

Proof.

Up to equivalence, we can assume that
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
is a generator matrix in a complementary weight form for
\C
and let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
, for every
i
. By using
Theorem

4.5
, we know that

A_{n_{k}}=\begin{cases}q^{m}-1&\mbox{ if }\ell=0,\\
(q^{m}-1)\left(\sum\limits_{i=k-\ell}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{h=i+1}^{k}q^{j_{i,h}}+1\right)&\mbox{ otherwise }\end{cases}

where
j_{i,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)
, for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
.
Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h})\geq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{h})\geq n_{k}
, we have
0\leq j_{i,h}\leq m-n_{k}
and hence we get the upper bound on
A_{n_{k}}
when
j_{i,h}=m-n_{k}
and the lower bound when
j_{i,h}=0
, for any
i,h
.
∎

4.1
Tightness of the bounds on minimum weight codewords

In this subsection, we study the tightness of the bounds on the number of minimum weight codewords for a completely decomposable code provided in
Corollary

4.6
.

First, we note that the lower bound of Corollary
4.6
is sharp. Indeed, assume that
m=2e
and let
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
. Consider,
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
, where
k\leq q-1
, such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{e}/q}(\mu_{i})\neq\mathrm{N}_{q^{e}/q}(\mu_{j})
and
\mathrm{N}_{q^{e}/q}(\mu_{i}\mu_{j}\xi^{q^{e}+1})\neq 1
, for every
i\neq j
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\lambda)
.
The codes admitting a generator matrix of the form

G=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mathbf{u}_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\ddots&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\mathbf{u}_{k}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times ke},

where
\mathbf{u}_{i}=(1+\xi\mu_{i},\lambda+\xi\mu_{i}\lambda^{q},\ldots,\lambda^{e-1}+\xi\mu_{i}(\lambda^{e-1})^{q}),
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
are completely decomposable
[ke,k,e]_{q^{m}/q}
codes of type
(e,\ldots,e)
having
(q^{m}-1)k
codewords of minimum weight
e
. See
[
37
, Section 6.2]
for more details on the weight distribution of such codes.

The upper bound of
Corollary

4.6
is also sharp as proved in the following result.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
m=re
, with
r>1
and
e\geq 1
. Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}(\xi)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Define
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{(r-1)e}
, such that the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
forms an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
\langle 1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{r-2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
. Let
\C_{i}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
. Then the code

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C_{i},

is a completely decomposable
[k(r-1)e,k,(r-1)e]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
((r-1)e,\ldots,(r-1)e)
having
(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{ke}-1}{q^{e}-1}
codewords of minimum weight
(r-1)e
and all the other nonzero codewords have weight
m
.

Proof.

Let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
and note that
U_{i}=\langle 1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{r-2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
, for each
i
. By
Theorem

4.5
, we know that

A_{e}(\C)=(q^{m}-1)\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{h=i+1}^{k}q^{j_{i,h}}+1\right)

(21)

where
j_{i,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
. So, in this case,

U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}=(\langle 1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{r-2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}})^{\perp^{*}}=\zeta{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}},

(22)

for some
\zeta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Therefore,

j_{i,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)=m-(r-1)e=e.

By substituting such values in (
21
), we get the number of minimum weight codewords. Finally, we show that all nonzero codewords have weight
m
or
m-e=(r-1)e
, proving the assertion. Note that a system associated with
\C
is
U=U_{1}\times\cdots\times U_{k}
and let
G
be a generator matrix of
\C
whose columns form and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis for
U
. By using (
13
), we get that a codeword
\mathbf{x}G
of
\C
has weight
i
if and only if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=m-i
. Moreover,
Proposition

2.4
, together with (
22
), imply that

U^{\perp^{\prime}}=U_{1}^{\perp^{*}}\times\ldots\times U_{k}^{\perp^{*}}=\zeta{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}\times\ldots\times\zeta{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}.

Therefore,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\in\{0,e\}
, implying
i\in\{m-e,m\}
.
∎

In the next, we characterize completely decomposable codes for which the number of minimum weight codewords attains the upper bound in
Corollary

4.6
.
To this aim, we need the following lemma, whose proof is straightforward.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
S
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and let
a_{2},\ldots,a_{g}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Assume that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(a_{2},\ldots,a_{g})
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. If
S=Sa_{2}=\ldots=Sa_{g}
, then
S
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.

The next result characterizes completely decomposable codes having the maximum number of minimum weight codewords.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Let

n_{k}=\ldots=n_{k-{\ell}}\neq n_{k-{\ell}-1}
, for some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
, with
n_{0}:=0
. If

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{(\ell+1)(m-n_{k})}-1}{q^{m-n_{k}}-1},

then
m=re
, for some
r>1
and
e\geq 1
,
n_{k}=(r-1)e
, and up to equivalence,

\Pi(\C,k-\ell)=\underbrace{\C^{\prime}\oplus\ldots\oplus\C^{\prime}}_{\ell+1},

where
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, where the entries of
\mathbf{u}
forms an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. In particular, if
\ell=k-1
, then up to equivalence, we have

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C^{\prime},

with
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, where the entries of
\mathbf{u}
form an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.

Proof.

Up to equivalence, there exists a generator matrix
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
of
\C
in a weight complementary form.
Let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
. The upper bound of
Corollary

4.6
is attained if and only if

m-n_{k}=j_{i,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right),

or in other words

n_{k}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right),

(23)

for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
.
First, let consider
i=k-1
and
h=k
and the pair
(U_{k-1},U_{k})
.
Let
a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{m-n_{k}}^{\prime}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
U_{k-1}^{\perp^{*}}
. Since,
U_{k-1}^{\perp^{*}}U_{k}=a_{1}^{\prime}U_{k}+\cdots+a_{m-n_{k}}^{\prime}U_{k}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{k})=n_{k}
, by (
23
), we get that

a_{1}^{\prime}U_{k}=\cdots=a_{m-n_{k}}^{\prime}U_{k}.

So, let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{m-n_{k}}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and so
m=er
, for some positive integer
r
. Clearly,
e\geq m-n_{k}
. By Lemma
4.8
, we get that
U_{k}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace and as a consequence
e\mid n_{k}
and
n_{k}=(r-1)e
. As a consequence,
U_{k-1}^{\perp^{*}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace of dimension
1
. Hence, since
m=re
,
U_{k-1}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace of dimension
r-1
. So
U_{k-1}=c_{k-1}\langle 1,\ldots,\xi^{r-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
and
U_{k}=c_{k}\langle 1,\ldots,\xi^{r-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
, for some
c_{k-1},c_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}(\xi)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, see e.g.
[
24
, Proposition 2.5]
. Arguing as before on the pairs
(U_{i},U_{h})
, for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
, we get that

U_{i}=c_{i}\langle 1,\ldots,\xi^{r-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}},

for some
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k\}
.

So, a system associated with
\C
is

U=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k-\ell-1}\times c_{k-\ell}H\times\ldots c_{k-1}H\times c_{k}H,

where
H=\langle 1,\ldots,\xi^{r-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
that is equivalent to

U^{\prime}=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k-\ell-1}\times H\times\ldots\times H.

This means that, up to equivalence,

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-\ell-1}\C_{i}\oplus\underbrace{\C^{\prime}\oplus\ldots\oplus\C^{\prime}}_{\ell+1},

where
\C_{i}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, for some
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
, with
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u}_{i})=n_{i}
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-\ell-1\}
and
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, where the entries of
\mathbf{u}
forms an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
\langle 1,\ldots,\xi^{r-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
. Therefore,
\Pi(\C,k-\ell)
is as in the assertion.
∎

In the next result, we prove that a completely decomposable code with all blocks of the same length and the maximum number of minimum weight codewords is equivalent to the code constructed in
Proposition

4.7
. In particular, its weight distribution is uniquely determined.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Assume that

n_{1}=\ldots=n_{k}
and

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{(\ell+1)(m-n_{k})}-1}{q^{m-n_{k}}-1}.

Then
m=re
, for some
r>1
and
e\geq 1
,
n_{k}=(r-1)e
, and up to equivalence,
we have

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C^{\prime},

with
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, where the entries of
\mathbf{u}
form an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
\langle 1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{r-2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
, for some
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}(\xi)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

4.9
, we have that
m=re
, for some
r>1
and
e\geq 1
,
n_{k}=(r-1)e
, and up to equivalence,

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C^{\prime},

with
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, where the entries of
\mathbf{u}
form an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Let
U^{\prime}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}
. Note that
U^{\prime}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and
U=U^{\prime}\times\ldots\times U^{\prime}
is a system associated with
\C
. So,
U^{\prime}=c\langle 1,\ldots,\xi^{r-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}
, for some
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}(\xi)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, see e.g.
[
24
, Proposition 2.5]
. Then the system

\langle 1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{r-2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}\times\ldots\times\langle 1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{r-2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}

is equivalent to
U
, implying the assertion.
∎

As proved in Theorem
4.9
, it is possible to construct codes whose number of minimum weight codewords attains the upper bound in
Corollary

4.6
, only when
m=re
and
n_{k}=(r-1)e
and so they have a common divisor
e
. Such a bound is not tight when the degree extension
m
is a prime.

Corollary 4.11
.

Assume that
m
is a prime. Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Assume that

n_{k}=\ldots=n_{k-{\ell}}\neq n_{k-{\ell}-1}
, for some
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
, with
n_{0}:=0
. Then

A_{n_{k}}(\C)\leq(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{\ell+1}-1}{q-1}.

(24)

Proof.

Up to equivalence, assume that
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
is a generator matrix in a weight complementary form for
\C
and let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
, for every
i
. By using
Theorem

4.5
, we know that

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=\begin{cases}q^{m}-1&\mbox{ if }\ell=0,\\
(q^{m}-1)\left(\sum\limits_{i=k-\ell}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{h=i+1}^{k}q^{j_{i,h}}+1\right)&\mbox{ otherwise }\end{cases}

where
j_{i,h}=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)
, for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
. By hypotheses, we have that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}})=m-n_{k}
, for any
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
, implying that, by Theorem
2.5
,

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h}\right)\geq m-n_{k}+n_{k}-1=m-1,

and so
j_{i,h}\leq 1
, for every
i
and
h
. Then, the assertion follows.
∎

By the above result, when
m
is prime, the quantity
(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{\ell+1}-1}{q-1}
is roughly
q^{m+\ell}
in (
24
) provides an upper bound on
A_{e}(\C)
, significantly reducing the previous upper bound in
Corollary

4.6
defined by the quantity
(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{(\ell+1)(m-n_{k})}-1}{q^{m-n_{k}}-1}
that is roughly
q^{m+\ell(m-n_{k})}
.

Remark 4.12
.

Note that by the above theorem, we also deduce that the equality holds in (
24
), i.e.

(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{\ell+1}-1}{q-1}=A_{n_{k}}(\C)

if and only if
j_{i,h}=1
, or equivalently
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{h})=m-1
, for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
and
h\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
.

For a prime degree extension, we characterize completely decomposable code
\C
such that
A_{n_{k}}(\C)
reaches the maximum in (
24
).

Theorem 4.13
.

Assume that
m
is prime. Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Let

n_{k}=\ldots=n_{k-{\ell}}\neq n_{k-{\ell}-1}
, for some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
, with
n_{0}:=0
. Then

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{\ell+1}-1}{q-1},

if and only if, up to equivalence, we have

\Pi(\C,k-\ell)=\underbrace{\C^{\prime}\oplus\ldots\oplus\C^{\prime}}_{\ell+1},

with
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, for some
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n_{k}})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{k}}
, where
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u})=n_{k}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{*}}U)=m-1
, with
U=\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{n_{k}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
.

Proof.

Up to equivalence, we can assume that
\C
has a generator matrix
G=\mathbf{u}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbf{u}_{k}
in a weight complementary form. Let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
, for every
i
. By Remark
4.12
, we have that
A_{n_{k}}(\C)=(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{\ell+1}-1}{q-1}
if and only if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}}U_{k})=m-1,

for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k-1\}
. Moreover, by hypotheses,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\perp^{*}})=m-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{k})
holds, Therefore, by using Lemma
2.7
, we have that
U_{i}=d_{i}U_{k}
, for certain
d_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. So, a system associated with
\C
is

U=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k-\ell-1}\times d_{k-\ell}U_{k}\times\ldots d_{k-1}U_{k}\times U_{k},

that is equivalent to

U^{\prime}=U_{1}\times\ldots\times U_{k-\ell-1}\times U_{k}\times\ldots\times U_{k}.

This implies that, up to equivalence,

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-\ell-1}\C_{i}\oplus\underbrace{\C^{\prime}\oplus\ldots\oplus\C^{\prime}}_{\ell+1},

where
\C_{i}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-\ell-1\}
and
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{k}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{k}^{\perp^{*}}U_{k})=m-1
, from which we get the assertion.

∎

In particular, if the
n_{i}
’s are equal, every completely decomposable code, having the maximum possible number of minimum weight codewords, is uniquely determined by one element
\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
.

Corollary 4.14
.

Assume that
m
is prime. Let
\C
be a completely decomposable
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
with
n_{1}=\ldots=n_{k}
. Then

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1},

if and only if, up to equivalence, we have

\C=\bigoplus_{i_{1}}^{k}\C^{\prime},

where
\C^{\prime}=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, for some
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n_{k}})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{k}}
, with
{\mathrm{w}}(\mathbf{u})=n_{k}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{*}}U)=m-1
, with
U=\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{n_{k}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
.

Proof.

It immediately follows by
Theorem

4.13
, for the case
\ell=k-1
.
∎

We introduce a family of completely decomposable codes having as number of minimum weight codewords as in
Theorem

4.13
.
For an element
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
having degree
e
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and a positive integer
t\leq e
, define
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda,e,t}=(1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{t-1})
and
\C_{\lambda,e,t}
the nondegenerate
[t,1]_{q^{m}/q}
code generated by
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda,e,t}
. Moreover, we denote the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace generated by the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda,e,t}
by
U_{\lambda,e,t}
, i.e.

U_{\lambda,e,t}=\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}.

When the degree of
\lambda
equals to
m
, or in other words,
\lambda
is a generator of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we simply write
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda,t},\C_{\lambda,t}
and

instead of
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda,m,t},\C_{\lambda,m,t}
and
U_{\lambda,m,t}
, respectively.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let

n_{k}=\ldots=n_{k-{\ell}}\neq n_{k-{\ell}-1}
, for some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
, with
n_{0}:=0
. For
e\geq n_{k-\ell}
and
e\mid m
, the code

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-\ell-1}\C_{i}\oplus\C_{\lambda,e,n_{k-\ell}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\C_{\lambda,e,n_{k}},

where
\C_{i}
is an MRD
[n_{i},1]_{q^{m}/q}
code, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-\ell-1\}
, is a completely decomposable code of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
with

A_{n_{k}}(\C)=(q^{m}-1)\frac{q^{\ell+1}-1}{q-1}.

In particular, the value
A_{n_{k}}(\C)
does not depend on the choice of
\lambda
.

Proof.

If
\ell=0
, the assertion is trivial. So assume that
\ell>0
. Let
t=n_{k-\ell}=\cdots=n_{k}
. Note that
\C_{\lambda,e,t}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{\lambda,e,t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
and the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the entries of
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda,e,t}
is
U_{\lambda,e,t}
. Therefore, by using
Theorem

4.13
, we get the assertion if we prove that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{\lambda,e,t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{\lambda,e,t})=m-1.

Assume first that
e=m
. By using Proposition
2.2
, for every
i\in\{k-\ell,\ldots,k\}
, we get that

U_{\lambda,t}^{\perp^{*}}=c\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{m-t-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},

for some nonzero
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Therefore,

U_{\lambda,t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{\lambda,t}=c\langle 1,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda^{m-2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}

and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{\lambda,t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{\lambda,t})=m-1
.

Assume now that
m=es
, for some
e,s>1
. By using Proposition
2.3
, we get that

U_{\lambda,e,t}^{\perp^{*}}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}})\oplus cU_{\lambda,e,e-t},

for some
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Therefore, since
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}})
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, we get

U_{\lambda,e,t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{\lambda,e,t}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q^{e}})\oplus cU_{\lambda,e,e-1},

implying that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{\lambda,e,t}^{\perp^{*}}U_{\lambda,e,t})=e(s-1)+e-1=es-1=m-1
, form which we get the assertion.
∎

Note that, when
m
is prime, the quantity
A_{n_{k}}(\C)
reaches the maximum in (
24
).

Remark 4.16
.

Under certain assumptions on the
n_{i}
’s, the codes

\C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\C_{\lambda,n_{i}}

reach the equality on the upper bound for maximum weight codewords of
[
29
, Theorem 5.8]
.

By
Proposition

4.15
, we also get that the number of codewords having minimum weight
n_{k}
does not depend on the choice of
\lambda
. However, different choices of
\lambda
can produce codes with different weight distribution.

Example 4.17
.

Let
q=2
,
m=6
and
e\mid m
. Define
\mathbf{n}=(2,2,2)
. As proved in
Proposition

4.15
, the number of codewords of the code

\C_{\lambda}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\C_{\lambda,e,2}
,
having minimum weight 2 does not depend on
\lambda
and

A_{2}(\C_{\lambda})=(2^{6}-1)\frac{2^{3}-1}{2-1}=441,

for any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}
, where
e
is such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{e}}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(\lambda)
. However,
by using MAGMA, it is possible to show that there exist
\lambda_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(\lambda_{1})={\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}
and
\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{3}}
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(\lambda_{2})={\mathbb{F}}_{2^{3}}
for which the weight distribution of
\C_{\lambda_{1}}
is

(A_{0},A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4},A_{5},A_{6})=(1,0,441,2646,35280,127008,96768).

and the weight distribution of
\C_{\lambda_{2}}
is

(A^{\prime}_{0},A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A^{\prime}_{4},A^{\prime}_{5},A^{\prime}_{6})=(1,0,441,4158,24696,148176,84672).

Therefore, the completely decomposable
[6,3,2]_{2^{6}/2}
codes
\C_{\lambda_{1}}
and
\C_{\lambda_{2}}
of type
(2,2,2)
have a different weight distribution.

Finally, in Corollary
4.10
, we have showed that a completely decomposable code with all blocks of the same length and the maximum number of minimum weight codewords as in
Corollary

4.6
is equivalent to the code constructed in
Proposition

4.7
and hence its weight distribution is uniquely determined. In the next example, we prove that for the case in which
m
is prime, there exist completely decomposable codes having the maximum number of minimum weight codewords as in
Corollary

4.11
, but having different weight distributions.

Example 4.18
.

Let
q=2
and
m=7
. Define
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})=(3,3,3)
and consider the
[9,3,3]_{2^{7}/2}
code
\C_{\lambda}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\C_{\lambda,n_{i}}
, for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{7}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. By
Proposition

4.15
, we know that the number of codewords of
\C_{\lambda}
having minimum weight 3 does not depend on
\lambda
and

A_{3}(\C_{\lambda})=(2^{7}-1)\frac{2^{3}-1}{2-1}=127\cdot 7=889.

However, by using MAGMA, we get that there exists
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{7}}
such that the weight distribution of
\C_{\lambda}
is

(A_{0},A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4},A_{5},A_{6},A_{7},A_{8},A_{9})=(1,0,0,127\cdot 7,127\cdot 42,127\cdot 336,127\cdot 2688,127\cdot 13440,0,0).

and the weight distribution of the code
\C^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{7}}}
, where
\mathbf{u}=(1,\lambda,\lambda^{3})

(A^{\prime}_{0},A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A^{\prime}_{4},A^{\prime}_{5},A^{\prime}_{6},A^{\prime}_{7},A^{\prime}_{8},A^{\prime}_{9})=(1,0,0,127\cdot 7,0,127\cdot 294,127\cdot 3108,127\cdot 13104,0,0).

[9,3,3]_{2^{7}/2}
codes
\C_{\lambda}
and
\C^{\prime}
of type
(3,3,3)

m
is prime and they have the same number of codewords with the minimum weight 3.

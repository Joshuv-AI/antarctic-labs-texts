Stabilization for Euler-Bernoulli beam equation with a local degenerated Kelvin-Voigt damping
Fathi Hassine

Abstract
. We consider the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation with a local Kelvin-Voigt dissipation type in the interval ( − 1 , 1 ) (-1,1) . The coefficient damping is only effective in ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) and is degenerating near the 0 0 point with a speed at least equal to x α x^{\alpha} where α ∈ ( 0 , 5 ) \alpha\in(0,5) . We prove that the semigroup corresponding to the system is polynomially stable and the decay rate depends on the degeneracy speed α \alpha .

2.
Problem statement and main result

The system that we are concerned with is the following Euler–Bernoulli beam equation clamped at two boundaries with
internal Kelvin–Voigt damping

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\ddot{u}(x,t)+u^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x,t)+\left(b(x)\dot{u}^{\prime\prime}(x,t)\right)^{\prime\prime}=0&(x,t)\in(-1,1)\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(-1,t)=u(1,t)=0&t\in(0,+\infty),\\
u^{\prime}(-1,t)=u^{\prime}(1,t)=0&t\in(0,+\infty),\\
\displaystyle u(x,0)=u^{0}(x),\;\dot{u}(x,0)=u^{1}(x)&x\in(-1,1),\end{array}\right.

where
b
is the coefficient damping which assumed to be a non-negative function such that

b(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }x\in(-1,0)\\
a(x)&\text{if }x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

The natural energy of system (
2.1
) is given by

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(|\dot{u}(x,t)|^{2}+|u^{\prime\prime}(x,t)|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}x

and it is dissipated according to the following law

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E(t)=-\int_{-1}^{1}b(x)\,|\dot{u}^{\prime\prime}(x,t)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,\;\forall\,t>0.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=H_{0}^{2}(-1,1)\times L^{2}(-1,1)
be the Hilbert space endowed with the inner product define for
(u,v),\,(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{H}
by

\left\langle(u,v),(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{-1}^{1}u^{\prime\prime}(x)\,.\,\overline{\tilde{u}}^{\prime\prime}(x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{-1}^{1}v(x)\,.\,\overline{\tilde{v}}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

By setting
U(t)=(u(t),v(t))
and
U^{0}=(u^{0},u^{1})
we can rewrite system (
2.1
) as a first order differential equation as follows

(2.2)

\dot{U}(t)=\mathcal{A}U(t),\qquad U(0)=U^{0}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}),

where

\mathcal{A}(u,v)=(v,-u^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}-(bv^{\prime\prime})^{\prime\prime}),

with

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}:\;(v,u^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}+(bv^{\prime\prime})^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

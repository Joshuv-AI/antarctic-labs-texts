Material-based analysis of spin-orbital Mott insulators
Ryuta Iwazaki, Hiroshi Shinaoka, and Shintaro Hoshino

Abstract
We present a framework for analyzing Mott insulators using a material-based tight-binding model. We start with a realistic multiorbital Hubbard model and derive an effective model for the localized electrons through the second-order perturbation theory with respect to intersite hopping. This effective model, known as the Kugel-Khomskii model, is described by SU( N N ) generators, where N N is the number of localized states. We solve this model by the mean-field theory that takes local correlations into account and reveal spin-orbital ordered states. To include spatial correlations, we apply the classical Monte Carlo based on the path-integral approach with SU( N N ) coherent states, and also derive the equation of motion for spin-orbital degrees of freedom. Our approach is applicable to any Mott insulator with reasonable computational cost. The 5 ​ d 5d -pyrochlore oxide is used here as demonstration.



Introduction
.—
Multiorbital systems with strongly correlated electrons have been attracting attention due to their diverse physical phenomena, such as electronic ordering and multiferroic behavior.
It is crucial to uncover their material-specific physical properties in order to make a serious comparison with experimental results.
In materials with weakly correlated electrons, density functional theory (DFT)-based calculations have been successful in describing their electronic properties.
On the other hand, in the strongly correlated regime, it is useful to construct a tight-binding model using localized Wannier functions and subsequently employ a multiorbital Hubbard model with local Coulomb repulsive interactions as a fundamental model.
Unfortunately, it is extremely difficult to perform the calculations in a realistic setting due to the immense numerical cost.
A theoretical framework that is applicable to realistic strongly correlated electron systems is highly desired, which will enable material prediction through, for example, high-throughput screening
Jain et al. 2016
.

In the present work, we focus on the Mott insulators where the electrons are localized with strong local Coulomb interaction.
Even in this case, the spin-orbital degrees of freedom generate a number of interesting phenomena such as magnetic orderings, multiferroic behaviors and spin liquids

Realistic Kugel-Khomski model
.—
The realistic effective model for the localized electrons are constructed based on the multiorbital Hubbard model derived from the first principles calculation.
Let us begin with the Hamiltonian
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}+\mathscr{H}_{t}
, where

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{t}=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sum_{ab}t^{ab}_{ij}c_{ia}^{\dagger}c_{jb}+\mathrm{H.c.}

(1)

describes the intersite hopping term.
The operator
c_{ia}
annihilates the electron at the atom site
i
with the spin(
\sigma
)-orbital(
\gamma
) index
a=(\gamma,\sigma)
.
The symbol
\langle ij\rangle
indicates the summation with respect to the pairs of atomic sites, and includes the terms other than the nearest neighbor sites.
The local part
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is further divided into three components as
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathscr{H}_{U}+\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{SOC}}+\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{CEF}}
, which are the Coulomb interaction, the spin-orbit coupling and the local crystalline electric field, respectively.
The Coulomb interaction is written as

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{U}=\sum_{i\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}U_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}}c_{i\gamma_{1}\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{i\gamma_{2}\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{i\gamma_{4}\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i\gamma_{3}\sigma},

(2)

which is parameterized by the Slater-Condon parameters as typically used in LDA+
U
or LDA+DMFT framework

.
Specifically for the three orbital case as in
t_{2g}
orbital, the standard Slater-Kanamori form is employed:

U_{\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma}=U/2
,
U_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}=U^{\prime}/2
,
U_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}=U_{\gamma\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}}=J/2
for
\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}
(
U^{\prime}=U-2J
) and the other terms are zero.

Os derived from electronic-structure calculation
sup
.
Since the band structure [see Fig.
2
(a)] is similar to the other materials with different filling such as

;

, we use the data of the

We analyze the multiorbital Hubbard model in the strong coupling limit (

), where the electrons are localized.
First of all, we derive the eigenenergies and eigenfunctions in the atomic model with only
\mathscr{H}_{\rm loc}
, which is necessary for specifying the model Hilbert space at low energies,
i.e.
, the number
N
of the localized states.

shows the single-site eigenenergy diagram of
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for each number

When we focus on the odd number of the filling

In this paper, we choose

for a demonstration of our scheme, which allows us to choose the size of the model space as
N=2
,
4
,
6
based on Fig.

.
We call them SU(2), SU(4) and SU(6) models, respectively.
The SU(
N
) model contains
N^{2}-1
operators for each atom.
The procedure for the simplest
N=2
case is summarized in Supplementary Matreial (SM)
sup
.
Although the dimension of the model Hilbert space may be dependent on the lattice site, we here take the same
N
for all the sites.

Once the model space is specified, we treat the intersite Hamiltonian
\mathscr{H}_{t}
as a perturbation, to obtain the effective Hamiltonian which gives a correct eigenenergies within the restricted Hilbert space
Ôkubo 1954
;
Bloch 1958
;
des Cloizeaux 1960
;
Durand 1983
;
Kuramoto 2020
.
While there are several choices of the form of the effective Hamiltonian, the Hermitian Hamiltonian (des Cloizeaux type) is easier to be handled
des Cloizeaux 1960
;
Durand 1983
.
We focus on the two atoms which are connected by the hopping matrix
\mathscr{H}_{t}
,
and expand this two-site Hamiltonian up to second order of
\mathscr{H}_{t}

sup
.
Thereby we obtain the matrix element of the effective Hamiltonian whose size is
N^{2}\times N^{2}
.
We can rewrite the obtained effective Hamiltonian by complete local operators
\mathscr{O}_{i}
at the site
i
.
We employ the numerical calculation with matrix multiplications for this procedure
Iwazaki and Hoshino 2021
.
Collecting all the combinations of the two-site Hamiltonians, we obtain the following realistic Kugel-Khomskii model:

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\mathscr{O}]=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sum_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}I_{ij}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}\mathscr{O}_{j}^{\xi^{\prime}}-\sum_{i}\sum_{\xi}H_{i}^{\xi}\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi},

(3)

where both the zeroth- and second-order contributions are involved in this effective Hamiltonian.
We have defined the local operators
\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}=\sum_{\alpha\beta}O_{\alpha\beta}^{\xi}|\alpha\rangle_{i}\,{}_{i}\langle\beta|
(
\alpha=1,\cdots,N
,
\xi=0,\cdots,N^{2}-1
), where
|\alpha\rangle_{i}
is a state vector in the model Hilbert space at site
i
.
We use the matrices
O^{\xi}_{\alpha\beta}
with completeness and orthonormality (
e.g.
for single orbital model, we take the SU(2) generators, which are the Pauli matrices)
sup
.
We emphasize that this Hamiltonian is derived from the first-principles calculation data, where the tunable parameters are only the local Coulomb interaction parameters
U
and
J
.
In the actual calculation, the data of
I_{ij}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
is outputted with the data structure similar to the original input of
t_{ij}^{ab}
.

Since it is in general difficult to interpret the physical meaning of the local operators
\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}
, it is desirable to transform them into physical quantities defined in terms of the original electronic system.
Let us consider the local physical quantity
\mathscr{A}_{i}
.
This can be spin or orbital operator if we choose the form of
\mathscr{A}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ab}A_{ab}c_{ia}^{\dagger}c_{ib}
where the matrix
A
is composed of a direct product of the matrices in spin and orbital spaces.
By using the projection operator onto the model Hilbert space,
\mathscr{P}=\prod_{i}\sum_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle_{i}\,{}_{i}\langle\alpha|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}\mathscr{A}_{i}\mathscr{P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi}\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}\sum_{\alpha\beta}{}_{i}\langle\alpha|\mathscr{A}_{i}|\beta\rangle_{i}O^{\xi}_{\beta\alpha}.

(4)

We can get the matrix element
{}_{i}\langle\alpha|\mathscr{A}_{i}|\beta\rangle_{i}
by analyzing
\mathscr{H}_{\rm loc}
.
Thus, once the expectation value of
\mathscr{O}_{i}
is obtained by solving the model in Eq. (
3
), any local physical quantities can be evaluated through this formula.
It is notable that
\mathscr{A}_{i}
can be chosen as many-body quantities such as a double occupancy, which is not usually considered for the conventional Kugel-Khomskii model.

The correlation functions are also useful quantities.
When we consider the linear response against a small fictitious field conjugate to
\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}
, the dynamical susceptibilities are given by

\displaystyle\chi_{ij}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\nu)=\int_{0}^{1/T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\quantity[
\la\mathscr{O}_i^\xi(\tau) \mathscr{O}_j^{\xi'}\ra- \la\mathscr{O}_i^\xi\ra\la\mathscr{O}_j^{\xi'}\ra]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\nu\tau},

(5)

where
\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}(\tau)=\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathscr{H}}\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau\mathscr{H}}
, and
\tau
is a Heisenberg picture with imaginary time, and
\nu=2\pi mT
(
m\in\mathbb{Z}
) is a bosonic Matsubara frequency.
We have taken
k_{\mathrm{B}}=1
.
Using Eq. (
4
), the susceptibility can be transformed into the physical susceptibilities defined in terms of the original electron operators.
The information of any spin-orbital excitation is encoded in Eq. (
5

For example, we can obtain the dispersion of the orbiton, which is a quasiparticle describing the excitation of the orbital

;
Ishihara and Maekawa 2000
;
Ishihara 2004

Mean-field theory
.—
Since the obtained localized model contains quantum effects, it is still very hard to be solved.
In the following, we introduce several approximated methods to solve the realistic Kugel-Khomskii model given in Eq. (
3
).
The most fundamental approximation is the mean-field theory.
Defining the effective field
\tilde{H}_{i}^{\xi}=H_{i}^{\xi}-\sum_{j\neq i,\xi^{\prime}}I_{ij}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}M_{j}^{\xi^{\prime}}
, the mean-field Hamiltonian is written as

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{MF}}=-\sum_{i}\sum_{\xi}\tilde{H}_{i}^{\xi}\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sum_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}I_{ij}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}M_{i}^{\xi}M_{j}^{\xi^{\prime}}.

(6)

We have defined
M_{i}^{\xi}=\langle\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}\rangle_{\mathrm{MF}}
where the expectation value is taken as
\langle\cdots\rangle_{\mathrm{MF}}=\Tr(\cdots\epn^{-\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{MF}}/T})/\Tr\mathrm{e}^{-\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{MF}}/T}
.
We also evaluate the dynamical susceptibilities with the random phase approximation as

\displaystyle\hat{\chi}(\bm{q},\omega)=\hat{\chi}_{0}(\omega)\quantity[
\hat{1} + \hat{I}(\bm{q}) \hat{\chi}_0(\omega)
]^{-1},

(7)

where the hat (
\,\hat{}\,
) symbol represents the matrix with respect to the index
\xi
, and
\hat{1}
is the identity matrix.
We have defined the local susceptibility by
\hat{\chi}_{0}(\omega)=\hat{\chi}_{ii}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})
which is evaluated by the local mean-field Hamiltonian.

Classical model
.—We can also solve the model by applying the classical approximation to Eq. (
3
).
In this method, we can examine the effect of the non-local correlation.
We employ the path-integral formalism using a coherent state
Nemoto 2000
;
Zhang and Batista 2021
, with which we derive both the classical partition function and equations of motion.
The coherent state is defined for each site
i
by

\displaystyle|\Omega_{i}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}c_{\alpha}(\Omega_{i})|\alpha\rangle_{i},

(8)

where
|\alpha\rangle_{i}
is a quantum state basis.

\Omega_{i}
is a set of local continuous variables:

\Omega_{i}=\{\xi_{1i},\cdots,\xi_{N-1,i},\varphi_{1i},\cdots,\varphi_{N-1,i}\}
, each of which is written as
\Omega_{pi}
(
p=1,\cdots,2(N-1)
)
sup
.
Here
\xi_{1i,\cdots}\in[0,\pi/2]
and
\varphi_{1i,\cdots}\in[0,2\pi)
respectively correspond to the generalized versions of polar angle and azimuthal angle of the spin in the SU(2) model.

The partition function is written as
Z=\int\mathscr{D}\bm{\Omega}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathscr{S}}
, where the action is
Read and Sachdev 1989

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\tau\,\quantity(
\la\bm\Omega|\partial_\tau|\bm\Omega\ra+\la\bm\Omega| \mathscr H_{\rm eff} |\bm\Omega\ra).

(9)

We have defined
|\bm{\Omega}\rangle=\prod_{i}|\Omega_{i}\rangle
at an imaginary time
\tau
.
The quantum-mechanical operator
\mathscr{O}_{i}
is now replaced by the classical variable:

\mathcal{O}^{\xi}(\Omega_{i})=\langle\bm{\Omega}|\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}|\bm{\Omega}\rangle
.
Based on these expressions, the classical model can be rigorously derived by using the coherent state path integral method that omits the Berry phase term, as in the spin model
Auerbach 1994
.
We can also show that the classical free energy is always larger than the quantum one
Lieb 1973
, and it is ensured that the lowest-free-energy state in the classical model is energetically closest to the genuine quantum state.

The model can be numerically simulated by using the classical Monte Carlo method.
We use the local Metropolis update and the replica exchange method which allow us to simulate the systems with various temperatures efficiently
Hukushima and Nemoto 1996
.
In addition, we also apply the over-relaxation update
Creutz 1987
for the more efficient simulation.
The over-relaxation update in the present case consists of microcanonical moves that does not alter the energy.
For the SU(2) case, the local spin vector is rotated around the local effective field by the angle
\pi

Landau and Binder 2021
;
Alonso et al. 1996
.
However, this cannot be directly extended to SU(
N
) case, and the consideration based on the coherent state is needed.

To perform the over-relaxation update for the SU(
N
) case, let us focus on the one lattice site
i
, and then its effective local Hamiltonian is written as

\mathcal{H}_{{\rm loc},i}=-\sum_{\xi}\tilde{H}_{i}^{\xi}\mathcal{O}^{\xi}(\Omega_{i})

where the effect of the surrounding sites is included in

\tilde{H}_{i}^{\xi}=H_{i}^{\xi}-\sum_{j\neq i,\xi^{\prime}}I_{ij}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}(\Omega_{j})
, which is not dependent on
\Omega_{i}
. We can cast it into the coherent state representation as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{{\rm loc},i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}(i)c_{\alpha}^{*}(\Omega_{i})c_{\beta}(\Omega_{i})=\sum_{\gamma}\Lambda_{\gamma}(i)|d_{\gamma}(\Omega_{i})|^{2},

(10)

where the diagonalization is performed in the right-most side by the unitary matrix
V
:
d_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\gamma\alpha}c_{\alpha}
.
It is apparent at this point that the energy does not change by the phase transformation
d_{\gamma}\to d_{\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\gamma}}
for any
\theta_{\gamma}
, with which the coherent state is transformed as
\Omega_{i}\to\Omega_{i}^{\prime}
.
The parameter
\theta_{\gamma}
is determined to minimize the norm of the inner product
\langle\Omega_{i}|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}\rangle
(see Ref.
sup
for more details).
This update makes it efficient to sample different configurations.
We note that the above procedure involving coherent state reproduces the over-relaxation update usually used for the SU(2) case.

Classical equation of motion
.—
Using the path-integral approach, our framework can further address the thermodynamic non-equilibrium state.
The equation of motion itself has already been derived by Zhang-Batista
Zhang and Batista 2021
.
The derivation is based on the Heisenberg equation of motion of
\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi}
which gives
N^{2}-1
equations.
In terms of the parameters of the coherent states, on the other hand, we only need
2(N-1)
equations.
Hence some of those equations should be redundant.
Here, we derive the
2(N-1)
equations based on the principle of the least action of Eq. (
9
)
Auerbach 1994
;
Nagaosa 1999
.
The resultant equation of motion for the local variable is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{q}\mathcal{B}_{pq}(i)\frac{\partial\Omega_{qi}}{\partial\tau}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Omega_{pi}},

(11)

where
\mathcal{H}=\langle\bm{\Omega}|\mathscr{H}_{\rm eff}|\bm{\Omega}\rangle
and the Berry curvature matrix is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{pq}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial c_{\alpha}^{*}(\Omega_{i})}{\partial\Omega_{pi}}\frac{\partial c_{\alpha}(\Omega_{i})}{\partial\Omega_{qi}}-\frac{\partial c_{\alpha}^{*}(\Omega_{i})}{\partial\Omega_{qi}}\frac{\partial c_{\alpha}(\Omega_{i})}{\partial\Omega_{pi}}\right),

(12)

with
p,q=1,\cdots,2(N-1)
.
Changing the time variable as
\tau\to\mathrm{i}t
, we obtain the real-time equation of motion.
Since the analytic form of the Berry curvature matrix is obtained once the specific coherent state is given in Eq. (
8
), the even-dimension antisymmetric matrix
\mathcal{B}
in Eq. (
11
) is
easily inverted numerically.
Thus the explicit equation of motion is obtained for the
2(N-1)
classical variables, and will be used for a non-equilibrium dynamics in a realistic setup.
The relation to the equations in Ref.
Zhang and Batista 2021
is not apparent but can be deduced from the equation

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\xi}}{\partial\tau}=\sum_{pq}\mathcal{B}^{-1}_{pq}(i)\frac{\partial\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\xi}}{\partial\Omega_{pi}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Omega_{qi}},

(13)

which derives from Eq. (
11
).
The right-hand side is reminiscent of the commutator
[\mathscr{O}_{i}^{\xi},\mathscr{H}_{\rm eff}]
.

Summary and outlook
.—
We have proposed the numerical calculation method for generic spin-orbital Mott insulators, and applied it to
5d
-pyrochlore oxides as a demonstration.
A detailed comparison between simulation results and experiments will provide us a deeper understanding of the Mott insulators, which leads to a design of functional materials.

We write the Hamiltonian as

The Hermitian effective Hamiltonian is given by

;

\displaystyle=(\Omega^{\dagger}\Omega)^{-1/2}\Omega^{\dagger}H\Omega(\Omega^{\dagger}\Omega)^{-1/2},

(S1)

where
\Omega

\displaystyle[H_{0},\Omega]=-V\Omega+\Omega V\Omega.

(S2)

We introduce the projection operators

\displaystyle P_{0}\Omega=P_{0},\ \ \ \Omega P_{0}=\Omega.

\displaystyle(\Omega^{\dagger}\Omega)^{-1/2}

(S5)

\Omega

\displaystyle\langle a|\mathscr{H}_{\rm eff}|b\rangle=\langle a|(H_{0}+V)|b\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2}\langle a|V\Big(Q_{0}\frac{1}{E_{a}-H_{0}}+\frac{1}{E_{b}-H_{0}}Q_{0}\Big)V|b\rangle,

.2
Over-relaxation update

We provide a detailed explanation for the over-relaxation update.
We begin with the local Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{{\rm loc}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\xi}\tilde{H}^{\xi}\mathcal{O}(\Omega)

(S30)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}c_{\alpha}^{*}(\Omega)c_{\beta}(\Omega)=\sum_{\gamma}\Lambda_{\gamma}|d_{\gamma}(\Omega)|^{2},

(S31)

where the site index is omitted for simplicity.
The diagonalization is performed in the right-most side by the unitary matrix
V

\displaystyle d_{\gamma}(\Omega)=\sum_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\gamma\alpha}c_{\alpha}(\Omega).

(S32)

It is apparent at this point that the energy does not change by the phase transformation

\displaystyle d_{\gamma}(\Omega)\to d_{\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\gamma}}=\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\gamma}}V^{\dagger}_{\gamma\alpha}c_{\alpha}(\Omega)\equiv\sum_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\gamma\alpha}c_{\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime}),

(S33)

For the efficient update to
\Omega^{\prime}

\Omega

.
For this purpose, we minimize the inner product defined by

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|\Omega^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\gamma}}|d_{\gamma}(\Omega)|^{2}.

(S34)

We define the norm

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\theta]

\displaystyle=|\langle\Omega|\Omega^{\prime}\rangle|^{2},

(S35)

and we find that
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{N}}{\partial\theta_{\gamma}}=0

The set of signs (

.
We search for the solution by considering

This procedure reproduces the over-relaxation update usually used for the SU(2) case.

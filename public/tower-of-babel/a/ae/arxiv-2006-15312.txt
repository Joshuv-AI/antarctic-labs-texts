A Theory of Equivalent Expectation Measures for Contingent Claim Returns
SANJAY K. NAWALKHA, XIAOYANG ZHUO

Abstract
This paper introduces a dynamic change of measure approach for computing the analytical solutions of expected future prices (and therefore, expected returns) of contingent claims over a finite horizon. The new approach constructs hybrid probability measures called the “equivalent expectation measures” (EEMs), which provide the physical expectation of the claim’s future price until before the horizon date, and serve as pricing measures on or after the horizon date. The EEM theory can be used for empirical investigations of both the cross-section and the term structure of returns of contingent claims, such as Treasury bonds, corporate bonds, and financial derivatives.

Estimating expected returns becomes difficult, if not impossible, for most buy-side firms and other buy-and-hold investors—who do not continuously rebalance and hedge their portfolios—without the analytical solutions of expected future prices of the financial securities in their portfolios. This problem is faced not just by sophisticated hedge fund managers who hold complex contingent claims, but also by numerous asset managers at mutual funds, banks, insurance companies, and pension funds, who hold the most basic financial securities, such as the U.S. Treasury bonds and corporate bonds. In an insightful study,
Becker and Ivashina 2015
illustrate this problem in the context of insurance companies, whose asset managers reach for higher (promised) yields, and not necessarily higher expected returns. They find that the “yield-centered” holdings of insurance companies are related to the business cycle, being most pronounced during economic expansions. More generally, it is common to use metrics based upon yield, rating, sector/industry, optionality, and default probability, to make portfolio holding decisions by fixed income asset managers. Since expected returns are not modeled
explicitly

Becker and Ivashina 2015

Part of this widespread problem in the fixed income markets and the financial derivatives markets is due to the lack of a simple and parsimonious framework that can guide the conditional estimation of finite-horizon expected returns. This paper fills this gap in the finance theory.

IV.  Extensions and Conclusion

This section considers some extensions and applications of the EEM theory and then concludes the paper. Until now the paper has focused only on those contingent claim models that admit an analytical solution to the claim’s current price. \mathbb{R}

Here we consider the
{\mathbb{R}}
measure construction when there are multidimensional Brownian motions as the source of uncertainty. Assuming the existence of a risk-neutral measure
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we let the Radon-Nikodým derivative process
{L_{s}}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
be

\displaystyle{L_{s}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{{\mathrm{d}}{\mathbb{Q}}}{{\mathrm{d}}{\mathbb{P}}}\Big|_{{\mathcal{F}}_{s}}=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{s}{\gamma}_{u}{\mathrm{d}}{W_{u}^{\mathbb{P}}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}||{\gamma}_{u}||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u\right),

(IA1)

where
\gamma_{s}=\left(\gamma_{1s},...,\gamma_{Ns}\right)
,
0\leq s\leq T
, is an
N
-dimensional measurable, adapted process satisfying
\int_{0}^{T}\gamma_{iu}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u<\infty
almost surely for

\E\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}||{\gamma}_{u}||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u\right)\right]<\infty

||\gamma_{s}||=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\gamma_{is}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
denotes the Euclidean norm.

Then, similarly to the case of one-dimensional Brownian motion in Appendix
B
, we have the following lemma:

LEMMA IA2
:

In the economy coupled with multidimensional Brownian motion as the source of uncertainty, for a fixed
H
with
0\leq H\leq T
, define a process
{\mathcal{L}_{s}(H)}
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}_{s}(H)}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{s}{\gamma}_{u}(H){\mathrm{d}}{W_{u}^{\mathbb{P}}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}||{\gamma}_{u}(H)||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u\right),

where

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathds{1}}_{\left\{s\geq H\right\}}\gamma_{s}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\gamma_{s},&\text{if}\ \ H\leq s\leq T,\\
\displaystyle 0,&\text{if}\ \ 0\leq s<H.\\
\end{array}\right.

Let

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}(A)\triangleq\int_{A}L_{T}(H;\omega){\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\ \ \text{for all $A\in\mathcal{F}_{T}$}.

Then
{\mathbb{R}}
is a probability measure equivalent to
{\mathbb{P}}
, and
{\mathcal{L}_{s}(H)}
is the Radon-Nikodým derivative process of
{\mathbb{R}}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
.

Moreover, define

\displaystyle{W_{s}^{\mathbb{R}}}

\displaystyle\triangleq{W_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}}+\int_{0}^{s}{\gamma}_{u}(H){\mathrm{d}}u

\displaystyle={W_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}}+\int_{0}^{s}{\mathds{1}}_{\left\{u\geq H\right\}}{\gamma}_{u}{\mathrm{d}}u.

(IA2)

is an
N
-dimensional Brownian motion under the

Proof.

We skip the steps that are consistent with the proof of Lemma
1
. For multidimensional Brownian motions, clearly, we have

\displaystyle\E\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}||{\gamma}_{u}(H)||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\E\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{H}^{T}||{{\gamma}}_{u}||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u\right)\right]\leq\E\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}||{{\gamma}}_{u}||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u\right)\right]<\infty.

In other words,
{\gamma}_{s}(H)

This Brownian motion transformation between

{\mathbb{P}}

N

{\gamma}_{s}(H)
that has the properties i).

is

-adapted for each

satisfies the Novikov condition, which was proven.

For a given

{\gamma}_{s}(H)=0

\mathcal{F}_{s}

\gamma_{s}

{\gamma}_{s}(H)

\mathcal{F}_{s}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left({\gamma}_{iu}(H)\right)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u=\int_{0}^{T}{\mathds{1}}_{\left\{u\geq H\right\}}{\gamma}_{iu}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u=\int_{H}^{T}{\gamma}_{iu}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u\leq\int_{0}^{T}{\gamma}_{iu}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}u<\infty\ \ \text{almost surely},

A. Expected Future Bond Price under the Vasicek Model

For the ease of exposition, this section illustrates the solution of the expected future price of a pure discount bond under the classical one-factor model of
Vasicek 1977
. The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process for the short rate under the Vasicek model is given as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathrm{d}}r_{s}&=\alpha_{r}\left(m_{r}-r_{s}\right){\mathrm{d}}s+\sigma_{r}{\mathrm{d}}W_{s}^{{\mathbb{P}}},\end{aligned}

(IA3)

where
\alpha_{r}
is the speed of mean reversion,
m_{r}
is the long-term mean of the short rate, and
\sigma_{r}
is the volatility of the changes in the short rate. Assuming
{W_{s}^{\mathbb{Q}}}={W_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}}+\int_{0}^{s}{\gamma_{r}}{\mathrm{d}}u
, the short rate process under the
{\mathbb{R}}
measure can be derived using Lemma
1
and Internet Appendix Section
I
.
C
, as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathrm{d}}r_{s}&=[\alpha_{r}\left(m_{r}-r_{s}\right)-{\mathds{1}}_{\{s\geq H\}}\sigma_{r}\gamma_{r}]{\mathrm{d}}s+\sigma_{r}{\mathrm{d}}{W_{s}^{\mathbb{R}}}\\
&=\left[\alpha_{r}\left(m_{r}-r_{s}\right){\mathds{1}}_{\{s<H\}}+\alpha_{r}^{*}\left(m_{r}^{*}-r_{s}\right){\mathds{1}}_{\{s\geq H\}}\right]{\mathrm{d}}s+\sigma_{r}{\mathrm{d}}W_{s}^{{\mathbb{R}}},\end{aligned}

where
\alpha_{r}^{*}=\alpha_{r}
,
m_{r}^{*}=m_{r}-\sigma_{r}\gamma_{r}/\alpha_{r}
.

The expected future price of a
T
-maturity pure discount bond at time
H
is given under the Vasicek model as follows: For all
t\leq H\leq T
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \E_{t}\left[P(H,T)\right]&=\E_{t}^{\mathbb{R}}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{H}^{T}r_{u}{\mathrm{d}}u\right)\right]\\
&=\exp\left(-A^{(b^{*},c^{*})}_{\alpha_{r}^{*},m_{r}^{*}}(T-H)-A^{(b,{c})}_{\alpha_{r},m_{r}}(H-t)-B^{(b,c)}_{\alpha_{r},m_{r}}(H-t)\cdot{r_{t}}\right),\end{aligned}

(IA4)

where
b^{*}=0
,
c^{*}=1
,
b=B^{(b^{*},c^{*})}_{\alpha_{r}^{*},m_{r}^{*}}(T-H)
,
c=0
.
For any well-behaved
b
,
c
,
\alpha
, and
m
,
A^{(b,c)}_{\alpha,m}\left(\tau\right)
and
B^{(b,c)}_{\alpha,m}\left(\tau\right)
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} A^{(b,c)}_{\alpha,m}\left(\tau\right)&=\alpha m\left(bB_{\alpha}{(\tau)}+c\frac{\tau-B_{\alpha}{(\tau)}}{\alpha}\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{r}^{2}\left(b^{2}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\alpha\tau}}{2\alpha}+bc{B_{\alpha}{(\tau)}}^{2}+c^{2}\frac{2\tau-2B_{\alpha}{(\tau)}-\alpha{B_{\alpha}{(\tau)}}^{2}}{{2\alpha}^{2}}\right),\\

with
B_{\alpha}(\tau)=\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha\tau}\right)/\alpha
.
When
H=t
, equation (
IA4
) reduces to the Vasicek bond price solution
(see
Vasicek 1977
, equation 27)
.

To obtain the expected FSPD from expected future prices of options using Theorem

(
Figlewski 2018
)

(

Madan and Milne 1994

and mixture models
(
Melick and Thomas 1997
;
Soderlind and Svensson 1997
, see, e.g.,)
. The non-parametric estimation methods can be classified into three categories: maximum entropy
(
Stutzer 1996
;
Buchen and Kelly 1996

, kernel
(
Aït-Sahalia and Lo 1998

Pritsker 1998

We extend the fast and stable curve-fitting method of

that fits the implied volatilities using the

4
4

4

For an extensive review of the curve-fitting methods, see
Jackwerth 2004

Figlewski 2018

.

We briefly recall the main steps for using the

\bar{\sigma}(K/S_{t})
, using a finite number of options with different strike prices. This allows greater stability since implied volatilities for different strike prices are much closer in size than the corresponding option prices. Let
\left\{K_{1},K_{2},...,K_{N}\right\}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f_{t}(S_{T}=K_{i})&=P(t,T)p_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}^{T}}(S_{T}=K_{i})\\
&=\frac{\partial^{2}C_{\text{BS}}\left(S_{t},K_{i},t,T,r;\hat{\sigma}(K_{i}/S_{t})\right)}{\partial K_{i}^{2}}\\
&\approx\frac{C_{i+1}-2C_{i}+C_{i-1}}{\left(\Delta K\right)^{2}}.\end{aligned}

&=\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{C}_{\text{BS}}\left(S_{t},K_{i},t,T,H,r;\hat{\sigma}(K_{i}/S_{t}),\hat{\mu}(K_{i}/S_{t})\right)}{\partial K_{i}^{2}}\\

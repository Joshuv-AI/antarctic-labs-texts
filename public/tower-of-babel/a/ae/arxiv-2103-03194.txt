Abstract
. We study stability, long-time behavior and moment estimates for stochastic evolution equations with additive Wiener noise and with singular drift given by a divergence type quasilinear diffusion operator which may not necessarily exhibit a homogeneous diffusivity. Our results cover the singular stochastic p p -Laplace equations and, more generally, singular stochastic Φ \Phi -Laplace equations with zero Dirichlet boundary conditions. We obtain improved moment estimates and quantitative convergence rates of the ergodic semigroup to the unique invariant measure, classified in a systematic way according to the degree of local degeneracy of the potential at the origin. We obtain new concentration results for the invariant measure and establish maximal dissipativity of the associated Kolmogorov operator. In particular, we recover the results for the curve shortening flow in the plane by Es-Sarhir, von Renesse and Stannat, NoDEA 16(9), 2012, [ 26 ] , and improve the results by Liu and Tölle, ECP 16, 2011, [ 41 ] .

Lemma 2.10

This result combined with moment estimates for the finite dimensional
Galerkin approximations of (
2.5
) yields the existence
of a unique invariant measure for (
2.5
).

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
0<s\leq 2
, and every
1<\alpha\leq d\frac{2-s}{d-s}
,
there exists
C=C(d,s,{\mathcal{O}})>0
such that for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
,

\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lvert\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}u\rvert^{\alpha}\,dx\right)^{\frac{s^{\ast}}{\alpha}}\leq C\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left\langle D^{2}\Psi(\nabla u)\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u,\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u\right\rangle\,dx\right),

where
s^{\ast}
is as in (
4.1
) and
\Psi
is defined as in (
3.2
).

Proof.

Set
\Lambda_{\min}(x):=\psi^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)\wedge\frac{\psi(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
.
Let
1<\alpha\leq d\frac{2-s}{d-s}
. First, by using Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities,
we get for
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lvert\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}u\rvert^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{2-s}{\alpha}}\

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle D^{2}\Psi(\nabla u)\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u,\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\cdot\Lambda_{\min}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\nabla u)\,dx\right)^{\frac{2-s}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle D^{2}\Psi(\nabla u)\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u,\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u\right\rangle\,dx\right)^{\frac{2-s}{2}}\times\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Lambda_{\min}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}(\nabla u)\,dx\right)^{\frac{(2-s)(2-\alpha)}{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\frac{2-s}{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2-s}}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle D^{2}\Psi(\nabla u)\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u,\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u\right\rangle\,dx+\frac{s}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{s}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Lambda_{\min}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}(\nabla u)\,dx\right)^{\frac{(2-s)(2-\alpha)}{s\alpha}}\right].

We note that
W_{0}^{2,\alpha}({\mathcal{O}})
is continuously embedded into

W_{0}^{1,\frac{s\alpha}{2-\alpha}}({\mathcal{O}})
for
1<\alpha\leq d\frac{2-s}{d-s}
. This embedding, together
with (C4), yields

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Lambda_{\min}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}(\nabla u)\,dx\right)^{\frac{(2-s)(2-\alpha)}{s\alpha}}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{s\alpha}{2-\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{(2-s)(2-\alpha)}{s\alpha}}\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W_{0}^{2,d\frac{2-s}{d-s}}({\mathcal{O}})}^{s^{\ast}}\right).

Choosing
\varepsilon
small enough completes the proof. ∎

We shall prove the existence of the unique invariant measure for the ergodic semigroup and the main concentration result now.

Theorem 4.3
.

There exists a unique invariant Borel probability
measure
\mu
for the semigroup
\{P_{t}\}
of equation (
2.5
) that is concentrated on the subset
W^{2,\alpha}_{0}({\mathcal{O}})\cap W^{1,2}_{0}({\mathcal{O}})\subset L^{2}({\mathcal{O}})
for any
1<\alpha\leq d\frac{2-s}{d-s}
if
d\geq 2
and
\alpha=1
if
d=1
with

\int_{H}\|u\|_{W_{0}^{2,\alpha}({\mathcal{O}})}^{s^{\ast}}\,\mu(du)+\int_{H}\lVert u\rVert_{V}^{s^{\ast}}\,\mu(du)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{H}\langle D^{2}\Psi\left(\nabla u\right)\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla u,\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla u\rangle_{H}\,\mu(du)<+\infty,

where
s^{\ast}
is as in (
4.1
) and
s\in(0,2]
is as
in assumption (C4) and
\Psi
is defined as in (
3.2
).

Proof.

For a complete orthonormal system
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
H=L^{2}({\mathcal{O}})
,
and for
H_{m}:=\operatorname{span}\{e_{k}\;\colon\;1\leq k\leq m\}
,
we consider the (finite dimensional) approximating stochastic differential equation in
H

(4.2)

\begin{split}du^{m}_{t}+Au^{m}_{t}\,dt&=P_{m}B\,dW_{t}\\
u^{m}_{0}&=P_{m}u_{0},\end{split}

where
A
is as in (
2.6
).
Note that here, we cannot use the standard Galerkin approximation
as in
[
25
]
, for there is no guarantee that the nonlinear operator

A
maps
H_{m}
into
V=H_{0}^{1}({\mathcal{O}})
. One option would be to
add an extra smoothing step for the operator
A
as e.g. in
[
31
, Proof of Theorem 3.1]
.
Instead, we follow the strategy from
[
29
, Section 6.3.1]

to find solutions
u^{m}
converging to
u
in
L^{2}(\Omega,C([0,T];H))

with the aim to consider the corresponding invariant measures
\nu_{m}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
J_{n}:=\left(\operatorname{Id}-\frac{\Delta}{n}\right)^{-1}

and
T_{n}:=-\Delta J_{n}=n(\operatorname{Id}-J_{n})
, respectively,
be the resolvent and the smooth Yosida approximation of the nonnegative definite
differential operator
-\Delta=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{i}}
,
respectively. Let
\langle u,v\rangle_{n}:=\langle u,T_{n}v\rangle_{H}
.
The induced
norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{n}
are equivalent to the
H
-norm and
converge monotonically as follows

\lVert u\rVert_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\uparrow}\begin{cases}\lVert u\rVert_{V},&u\in V,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Applying Itô’s formula to
\lVert u^{m}(t)\rVert_{n}^{2}
, we
get

(4.3)

\lVert u_{t}^{m}\rVert_{n}^{2}=\lVert u_{0}^{m}\rVert_{n}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(u_{\tau}^{m}),T_{n}(u_{\tau}^{m})\rangle\,d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert P_{m}B\rVert_{L_{2}(U,H_{m})}^{2}\,d\tau+M_{t}^{n}

where
t\mapsto M_{t}^{n}=2\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{\tau}^{m},P_{m}B\,dW_{\tau}\rangle_{n}

is a local martingale. From
[
7
, Proposition 8.2]
and (C3),
applied to
[
29
, Proposition 7.1]
, it follows that

\langle A(u_{\tau}^{m}),T_{n}(u_{\tau}^{m})\rangle_{H}\geq 0\quad d\tau\text{-a.e}.

We note that the convexity assumption of for the domain
{\mathcal{O}}
enters here, as it is needed to apply
[
7
, Proposition 8.2]
.
By taking expectation, together with Fatou’s lemma, employing the
bounds
\lVert u_{0}^{m}\rVert_{n}\leq\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{V}
and

\lVert P_{m}B\rVert_{L_{2}(U,H^{m})}^{2}\leq\lVert B\rVert_{L_{2}(U,V)}^{2}
,
it follows from (
4.3
) for every
t\geq 1
that

(4.4)

\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\langle D^{2}\Psi(\nabla u_{\tau}^{m})\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\tau}^{m},\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\tau}^{m}\rangle\,dx\,d\tau\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{V}^{2}+\lVert B\rVert_{L_{2}(U,V)}^{2}\right)=:K_{1},

independently of
m
. On the other hand, the application of Itô’s
formula to
t\mapsto\lVert u_{t}^{m}\rVert_{H}^{2}
yields

\lVert u_{t}^{m}\rVert_{H}^{2}=\lVert u_{0}^{m}\rVert_{H}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(u_{\tau}^{m}),u_{\tau}^{m}\rangle_{H}\,d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert P_{m}B\rVert_{L_{2}(U,H_{m})}^{2}\,d\tau+N_{t}^{m}

where
t\mapsto N_{t}^{m}=2\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{\tau}^{m},P_{m}B\,dW_{\tau}\rangle_{H}

and
\langle A(u^{m}),u^{m}\rangle=\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\langle\phi(\nabla u^{m}),\nabla u^{m}\rangle\,dx
.
Note that by (C5) and Lemma
2.10
, we get

\psi(r)\cdot r\geq c\lvert r\rvert^{1\vee(2-s)}-K,

for suitable constants
c>0
and
K\geq 0
. This combined with the
same arguments as before entails that for every
t\geq 1

(4.5)

\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}\lvert\nabla u_{\tau}^{m}\rvert^{1\vee(2-s)}\,dx\,d\tau\right)\leq\frac{1}{2c}\left(\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert B\rVert_{L_{2}(U,H)}^{2}+K\right)=:K_{2}

for a positive constant
K_{2}>0
.

Let us treat the case
d=1
separately first, so let us assume that
{\mathcal{O}}=(0,L)
.
By the above estimates, it is easy to prove that the family of probability measures
\left\{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}P_{\tau}^{\ast}\delta_{u_{0}}\,d\tau\right\}_{t\geq 1}
(where
\delta_{u_{0}}
denotes the Dirac measure in
u_{0}
) is tight in the finite dimensional space
H_{m}
, and we thus find that by the Krylov-Bogoliubov existence theorem
[
18
, Theorem 3.1.1]
that
there exists an invariant measure
\nu_{m}
for the Markov semigroup of (
4.2
) such that for arbitrary
N>0

(4.6)

\int_{H}\int_{I}\left(\lvert\partial_{x}u\rvert^{1\vee(2-s)}dx\wedge N\right)\nu_{m}(du)+\int_{H}\int_{I}\left(\psi^{\prime}(\partial_{x}u)|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}dx\wedge N\right)\nu_{m}(du)\leq K_{3},

where
K_{3}=K_{1}+K_{2}
. An application of the monotone convergence
lemma yields

(4.7)

\sup_{m\geq 1}\int_{H}\int_{I}\lvert\partial_{x}u\rvert^{1\vee(2-s)}\,dx\,\nu_{m}(du)+\sup_{m\geq 1}\int_{H}\int_{I}\psi^{\prime}(\partial_{x}u)|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,dx\,\nu_{m}(du)\leq K_{3}.

Combining (
4.6
) with Lemma
4.1
, we
obtain

(4.8)

\sup_{m\geq 1}\int_{H}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\lvert\partial_{xx}u\rvert\,dx\right)^{s^{\ast}}\,\nu_{m}(du)<+\infty.

Due to the compactness of the embedding
W_{0}^{2,1}(0,L)\subset W_{0}^{1,1}(0,L)
,
we get that the sequence of measures
\{\nu_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
is tight
w.r.t. the
W^{1,1}
-topology. Let
\nu
denote the limit of a weakly
convergent subsequence in the sense of weak convergence of probability measures. For a function
u\in L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1}(B)
, we define its total variation on an open subset

by

[Du](B):=\sup\left\{\int_{B}u\operatorname{div}v\,dx\;:\;v\in C^{1}(\overline{B},\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;\lVert v\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

Recall that
u\in L^{1}(B)
belongs to the space
BV(B)

of functions of bounded variation if
[Du](B)<+\infty
.
Note that

u\mapsto\begin{cases}[D\partial_{x}u](\mathbb{R}),&\text{ if }\partial_{x}u\in BV(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{1}(0,L),\;u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(0,L),\\
+\infty,&\text{ if }\partial_{x}u\in L^{1}(0,L)\setminus BV(\mathbb{R}),\;u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(0,L),\end{cases}

is the lower semi-continuous envelope of

u\mapsto\int_{0}^{L}|\partial_{xx}u|\,dx,\quad u\in W^{2,1}_{0}(0,L),

in
W^{1,1}_{0}(0,L)
.
The lower semi-continuity of

W^{1,1}_{0}(0,L)
-topology,
together with (
4.8

[

\int_{H}\left([D\partial_{x}u](\mathbb{R})\right)^{s^{\ast}}\,\nu(du)<+\infty.

However, by the tightness, it can be seen by standard arguments that the support of
\nu
is contained in
W_{0}^{2,1}(0,L)
and thus

\int_{H}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\partial_{xx}u\,dx\right)^{s^{\ast}}\,\nu(du)<+\infty.

From the boundedness of the embedding of
W_{0}^{2,1}(0,L)
into

it follows that

\int_{H}\lVert u\|_{V}^{s^{\ast}}\,\nu(du)<+\infty.

Hence the support of

is included in
W_{0}^{2,1}(0,L)\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
.

Let us suppose
d\geq 1
now.
By the above estimates, it is easy to prove that the family of probability measures
\left\{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}P_{\tau}^{\ast}\delta_{u_{0}}\,d\tau\right\}_{t\geq 1}
is tight in the finite dimensional space
H_{m}
and thus by the Krylov-Bogoliubov existence theorem
[
18
, Theorem 3.1.1]
,
as the bound (
4.5
) is independent of

, there exists an invariant measure
\nu_{m}
for (
4.2
).
To show uniqueness, note that for any Lipschitz function
F
and

\nu_{m}
-almost every
u_{0},v_{0}
in
H_{m}
, Proposition
3.3

yields (for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and
l=d_{0}=1\vee\frac{d}{2})

that

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}F(u_{\tau})\,d\tau\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}F(v_{\tau})\,d\tau\right)\right\rvert\\
\leq&\operatorname{Lip}(F)\,\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert u_{\tau}-v_{\tau}\rVert_{H}\,d\tau\right)\\
\leq&t^{-\frac{d_{0}}{2}}C\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert u_{\tau}\rVert_{V}^{\frac{sd_{0}}{2}}+\lVert v_{\tau}\rVert_{V}^{\frac{sd_{0}}{2}}\,d\tau\right)\right)\lVert u_{0}-v_{0}\rVert_{H}\\

as

where

may change from line to line. The latter implies uniqueness of the invariant measure

of the ergodic semigroup of (

) by standard arguments, see e.g.
[
41
, Section 2.1, proof of Theorem 1.2 (iii)]
.

Now, it follows from ergodicity that for arbitrary

(4.10)

\int_{H}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\left\langle D^{2}\Psi(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{i}},\nabla u_{x_{i}}\right\rangle dx\wedge L\right)\,\nu_{m}(du)\leq C.

The monotone convergence lemma yields

(4.11)

\sup_{m\geq 1}\int_{H}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\left\langle D^{2}\Psi(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{i}},\nabla u_{x_{i}}\right\rangle dx\right)\,\nu_{m}(du)\leq C.

Combining (
4.11
) with Lemma

, we obtain

\sup_{m\geq 1}\int_{H}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W_{0}^{2,(2-s)\frac{d}{d-s}}}^{s^{\ast}}\,\nu_{m}(du)<+\infty.

Keeping assumption (C6) in mind, the compactness of the embedding

into

for

implies that

The moment estimate for the

The uniqueness of

As a consequence, we obtain the proof of Theorem

Corollary 4.4

(Stochastic
p
-Laplace)

5.
Decay estimate and examples

Proposition
3.3
is applied to prove the following main decay estimate for
the semigroup
\{P_{t}\}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{P_{t}\}
be the semigroup from (
2.7
)
and let
\mu
denote its unique invariant measure. Let
d_{0}:=1\vee\frac{d}{2}
. Let
s^{\ast}=(2-s)\vee\frac{4-s}{2+s}

0<\beta\leq\beta^{\star}:=1\wedge\frac{8-2s}{s(2+s)d_{0}}\in\left[\frac{1}{2d_{0}},1\right]

3.3
, there exist non-negative constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}

and
C_{3}
such that

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\left[t^{\frac{s^{\ast}}{s}}\frac{\lvert P_{t}f(u)-P_{t}f(v)\rvert}{\lVert u-v\rVert_{H}^{\beta}}\right]\leq C_{1}|f|_{\beta}\left(C_{2}+C_{3}\int_{H}\lVert u\rVert_{V}^{s^{\ast}}\,\mu(du)\right),

for every
u,v\in V
and every

that is bounded and
\beta
-Hölder-continuous, i.e.,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in H\\
u\not=v\end{subarray}}\frac{\lvert f(u)-f(v)\rvert}{\lVert u-v\rVert_{H}^{\beta}}=:\lvert f\rvert_{\beta}<+\infty.

Proof.

We use Proposition
3.3
and get for
\frac{\beta}{2}=:\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}\wedge\frac{s^{\ast}}{sd_{0}}=\frac{\beta^{\star}}{2}
,

l:=\frac{s^{\ast}}{\alpha d_{0}s}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\lvert P_{t}f(u)-P_{t}f(v)\rvert=\lvert\mathbb{E}[f(u_{t})-f(v_{t})]\rvert\leq\lvert f\rvert_{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert u_{t}-v_{t}\rVert_{H}^{2\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{2\alpha}t^{-\frac{s^{\ast}}{s}}C(l,\alpha,{\mathcal{O}},d)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left((a|{\mathcal{O}}|)^{\alpha d_{0}}+b^{\alpha d_{0}}\lVert u_{\tau}\rVert_{V}^{\alpha d_{0}s}+b^{\alpha d_{0}}\lVert v_{\tau}\rVert_{V}^{\alpha d_{0}s}\right)\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{l}{d_{0}}}\right]\lVert u_{0}-v_{0}\rVert_{H}^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\beta}t^{-\frac{s^{\ast}}{s}}C(l,\alpha,{\mathcal{O}},d)3^{(\frac{l}{d_{0}}-1)\vee 0}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left((a|{\mathcal{O}}|)^{\frac{s^{\ast}}{s}}+b^{\frac{s^{\ast}}{s}}\lVert u_{\tau}\rVert_{V}^{s^{\ast}}+b^{\frac{s^{\ast}}{s}}\lVert v_{\tau}\rVert_{V}^{s^{\ast}}\right)\,d\tau\right)\lVert u_{0}-v_{0}\rVert_{H}^{\beta}.

Note that
\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert u_{\tau}\rVert_{V}^{s^{\ast}}\,d\tau\right)

converges to
\int_{H}\lVert u\rVert_{V}^{s^{\ast}}\,\mu(du)
due
to the ergodicity of
\{P_{t}\}
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the proof of Theorem
1.2
(ii).

Kolmogorov operator

Set

\kappa(r)=\psi^{\prime}(\left\lvert r\right\rvert)\wedge\frac{\psi(\left\lvert r\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert r\right\rvert},

i.e.
\Lambda_{\min}(x)=\kappa(|x|)
, where
\Lambda_{\min}
is defined as in Lemma
3.2
,
and set

D_{0}:=\left\{u\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\mathcal{O})\;:\;\sqrt{\kappa\left(|\nabla u|\right)}\,\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),1\leq i,j\leq d\right\}.

Recall that Theorem
4.3

yields that the support of the invariant measure
\mu
is included in
D_{0}
, so that

\|\sqrt{\kappa\left(|\nabla u|\right)}\,\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}u\|^{2}_{H}

is well-defined (and finite)
\mu
-a.e., and

(6.1)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{H}\|\sqrt{\kappa\left(|\nabla u|\right)}\,\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}u\|^{2}_{H}\,\mu(du)<+\infty.

The Kolmogorov operator associated with (
1.1
) on sufficiently smooth functions
F
is given by

J_{0}F(u):=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{H}\left(BB^{\ast}D^{2}F(u)\right)-\langle A(u),DF(u)\rangle.

In order to realize
J_{0}
as an operator in
L^{1}(H,\mu)
with domain
D(J_{0}):=C_{b}^{2}(H)
, we need
to impose conditions on
A
ensuring that
\|A(u)\|\in H
. To this end we now pose the following
additional assumption.

Assumption 6.1
.

Suppose that

(C7)

there exists a constant
M>0
such that for every
r\in\mathbb{R},

\left|\psi^{\prime}(\left\lvert r\right\rvert)-\frac{\psi(\left\lvert r\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert r\right\rvert}\right|+\frac{\psi(\left\lvert r\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert r\right\rvert}\leq M\sqrt{\kappa(r)}.

Example 6.2
.

We shall collect the following examples for (C7) and leave their verification to the reader.

(i)

For
p\in(1,2)
, there exists as constant
C(p)>0
such that
\psi(r)=(1+|r|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{p}}r
satisfies (C7) with
M=C(p)
.

(ii)

\psi(r)=\log(1+|r|)\operatorname{sgn}(r)
satisfies (C7) with
M=2
.

(iii)

\psi(r)=\arctan(r)
satisfies (C7) with
M=4
.

Remark 6.3
.

We would like to point out that for the singular
p
-Laplace
\psi(r)=|r|^{p-2}r
, (C7) reduces to

\sqrt{p-1}|r|^{p-2}\leq M|r|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\quad\text{for every $r\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Clearly, the existence of such
M>0
cannot be proved for
p\not=2
.
Neither does the minimal surface flow satisfy (C7).

An immediate implication of (C7) is that for
u\in D_{0}
due to (
3.4
)

\displaystyle|A(u)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\psi^{\prime}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert)-\frac{\psi(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert}\right|\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}u\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\psi(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert}\left\lvert\Delta u\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq M\sqrt{\kappa\left(|\nabla u|\right)}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}u\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some finite constant. Moreover, (
6.1
) now implies that
\|A(u)\|\in L^{2}(H,\mu)
,
so that for
F\in C_{b}^{2}(H)
it follows that
\langle A(u),DF(u)\rangle\in L^{2}(H,\mu)
, hence
J_{0}F(u)\in L^{1}(H,\mu)
, too. From Itô’s formula for applied to
F(u(t))
with regular initial condition, it follows that
\mu
is infinitesimally invariant for
J_{0}
. Therefore, since

J_{0}F^{2}=2FJ_{0}F+\langle BB^{\ast}DF,DF\rangle,\quad F\in D(J_{0}),

also,

\int_{H}FJ_{0}F\,d\mu=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{H}\lVert B^{\ast}DF\rVert^{2}\,d\mu.

Thus
J_{0}
is dissipative in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(H,\mu)
.
By similar arguments as in
[
22
]
one can prove that
J_{0}

is also dissipative in
L^{1}(H,\mu)
. As a consequence, it is closable
and its closure
J:=\bar{J_{0}}
with the domain
D(J)
is dissipative.

Theorem 6.4

(compare with Theorem 4.1 in
[
26
]
)
.

The operator

(J,D(J))
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions on
L^{1}(H,\mu)
.

Proof.

We shall prove that for
\lambda>0
,
\operatorname{range}(\lambda-J)

is dense in
L^{1}(H,\mu)
. To this aim, for
\alpha>0
, consider
the Yosida approximation of
A
defined by

A_{\alpha}(u)=A(J_{\alpha}(u)),\quad\text{where$\quad J_{\alpha}(u)=(\operatorname{Id}+\alpha A)^{-1}(u)$.}

For the sequence
A_{\alpha}
we have the following:

(i)

For any
\alpha>0
,
A_{\alpha}
is dissipative and Lipschitz continuous,

(ii)

\lVert A_{\alpha}(u)\rVert_{H}\leq\lVert A(u)\rVert_{H}
for any

u\in D(A)
.

Note that the function
A_{\alpha}
is not differentiable in general.
Therefore we consider a
C^{1}
-approximation.
For
\alpha,\beta>0

we set

A_{\alpha,\beta}(u):=\int_{H}e^{\beta\Delta}A_{\alpha}(e^{\beta\Delta}u+v)\,\mathcal{N}_{0,\sigma_{\beta}}(dv)

where
\mathcal{N}_{0,\sigma_{\beta}}
is the Gaussian measure on

H
with mean
0
and covariance operator
\sigma_{\beta}:=\int_{0}^{\beta}e^{2\tau\Delta}\,d\tau
, and
e^{\tau\Delta}
,
\tau>0
, denotes the semigroup generated by the Dirichlet Laplace operator on
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
.
Then,
A_{\alpha,\beta}
is dissipative since

\langle A_{\alpha,\beta}(u)-A_{\alpha,\beta}(v),u-v\rangle=\int_{H}\left\langle A_{\alpha}(e^{\beta\Delta}u+w)-A_{\alpha}(e^{\beta\Delta}v+w),e^{\beta\Delta}(u-v)\right\rangle\,\mathcal{N}_{0,\sigma_{\beta}}(dw)\geq 0.

We would like to use the Cameron-Martin theorem to show that
A_{\alpha,\beta}

is differentiable. To this end we first need to check that
e^{\beta\Delta}u\in\operatorname{range}(\sigma_{\beta}^{\frac{1}{2}})
. According to Proposition B.1 in
[
19
]
it suffices to prove
that there exists a constant
M
such that
\|e^{\beta\Delta}v\|^{2}_{H}\leq M\|\sigma_{\beta}^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{H}^{2}

for all
v\in H
. But this follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\|e^{\beta\Delta}v\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\|e^{(\beta-\tau)\Delta}e^{\tau\Delta}v\|_{H}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\|e^{\tau\Delta}v\|_{H}^{2}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle e^{2\tau\Delta}v,v\rangle\,d\tau=\frac{1}{\beta}\langle\sigma_{\beta}v,v\rangle=\frac{1}{\beta}\|\sigma_{\beta}^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{H}^{2}.

An application of the Cameron-Martin theorem yields

\langle DA_{\alpha,\beta}(u),h\rangle=\int_{H}e^{\beta\Delta}A_{\alpha}(e^{\beta\Delta}u+v)\langle\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}e^{\beta\Delta}h,v\rangle\,\mathcal{N}_{0,\sigma_{\beta}}(dv).

and by the Cameron-Martin formula it is
C^{\infty}
differentiable.
Moreover, as
\alpha,\beta\to 0,A_{\alpha,\beta}\to A
pointwise.
Let us introduce the following approximating equation

(6.2)

\displaystyle du_{\alpha,\beta}(t)=-A_{\alpha,\beta}(u_{\alpha,\beta}(t))\,dt+B\,dW_{t},\quad t>0,\quad u_{\alpha,\beta}(0)=u.

Since
A_{\alpha,\beta}
is globally Lipschitz, equation (
6.2
)
has a unique strong solution
(u_{\alpha,\beta}(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Moreover,
by the regularity of
A_{\alpha,\beta}
the process
(u_{\alpha,\beta}(t))_{t\geq 0}

is differentiable on
H
. For any
h\in H
we set
\eta_{h}(t,u):=Du_{\alpha,\beta}(t,u)\cdot h
.
It holds that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\eta_{h}(t,u)=-DA_{\alpha,\beta}(u_{\alpha,\beta}(t,u))\cdot\eta_{h}(t,u),\quad\eta_{h}(0,u)=h.

From the dissipativity of
A_{\alpha,\beta}
, we have that

\langle DA_{\alpha,\beta}(z)h,h\rangle\geq 0,\quad h\in H,z\in D(A).

Hence by multiplying both sides of
6.3
with
\eta_{h}(t,u)

and integrating with respect to
t
, we get

(6.4)

\lVert\eta_{h}(t,u)\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert h\rVert^{2}.

Now, for
\lambda>0
and
F\in C_{b}^{2}(H)
consider the following
elliptic equation

(6.5)

(\lambda-J_{A_{\alpha,\beta}})\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}=F,\ \lambda>0,

where
J_{A_{\alpha,\beta}}
is the Kolmogorov operator corresponding
to the stochastic differential equation (
6.2
). It
is well-known that this equation has a solution
\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}\in C_{b}^{2}(H)

and can be written in the form
\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}=R(\lambda,J_{A_{\alpha,\beta}})F
,
where

R(\lambda,J_{A_{\alpha,\beta}})F(u)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\mathbb{E}(F(u_{\alpha,\beta})(t,u))\,dt

is the pseudo resolvent associated with
J_{A_{\alpha,\beta}}
. Thus
we have

(6.6)

\lVert\lambda\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}.

We have, moreover, for all
h\in H
,

D{\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}}(u)h=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\mathbb{E}\left(DF(u_{\alpha,\beta})(t,u)((Du_{\alpha,\beta})(t,u)h)\right)dt.

Consequently, using (
6.4
), it follows that

\lVert D\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}\rVert\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\lVert DF\rVert_{\infty}.

From (
6.5
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{H}\left(BB^{\ast}D^{2}\varphi(u)\right)+\langle A(u),D\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)\rangle

\displaystyle=F(u)+\langle A(u)-A_{\alpha,\beta}(u),D\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)\rangle,\ \lambda>0,\ u\in D(A).

Using gradient bound (
6.6
), we deduce that for
\alpha,\beta\to 0
,

\int_{H}\lvert\langle A(u)-A_{\alpha,\beta}(u),D\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)\rangle\rvert\,\mu(du)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\lVert DF\rVert_{\infty}\lVert A_{\alpha,\beta}-A\rVert_{L^{2}(H,\mu)}.

By Lebesgue’s theorem
\lVert A_{\alpha,\beta}-A\rVert_{L^{2}(H,\mu)}

converges to
0
as
\alpha,\beta\to 0
. Therefore we deduce that
for
\alpha,\beta\to 0

\lambda\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{H}\left(BB^{\ast}D^{2}\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)\right)+\langle A(u),D\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)\rangle\to F

strongly in
L^{1}(H,\mu)
. This implies that

C_{b}^{2}(H)\subset\overline{(\lambda-J_{0})(D(J_{0}))}.

Since
C_{b}^{2}(H)
is dense in
L^{1}(H,\mu)
, the proof is complete.∎

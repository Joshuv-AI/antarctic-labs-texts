On the convergence of adaptive approximations for stochastic differential equations
James Foster, Andraž Jelinčič

Abstract
In this paper, we study numerical approximations for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) that use adaptive step sizes. In particular, we consider a general setting where decisions to reduce step sizes are allowed to depend on the future trajectory of the underlying Brownian motion. Since these adaptive step sizes may not be previsible, the standard mean squared error analysis cannot be directly applied to show that the numerical method converges to the solution of the SDE. Building upon the pioneering work of Gaines and Lyons, we instead use rough path theory to establish pathwise convergence for a wide class of adaptive numerical methods on general Stratonovich SDEs (with sufficiently smooth vector fields). To our knowledge, this is the first convergence guarantee that applies to standard solvers, such as the Milstein and Heun methods, with non-previsible step sizes. In our analysis, we require the sequence of adaptive step sizes to be nested and the SDE solver to have unbiased Lévy area terms in its Taylor expansion. We conjecture that for adaptive SDE solvers more generally, convergence is still possible provided the scheme does not introduce “Lévy area bias”. We present a simple example where the step size control can skip over previously considered times, resulting in the numerical method converging to an incorrect limit (i.e. not the Stratonovich SDE). Finally, we conclude with an experiment demonstrating the accuracy of Heun’s method and a newly introduced Splitting Path-based Runge-Kutta scheme (SPaRK) when used with adaptive step sizes.

1.2
Organisation of the paper

In Section
2
, we will establish our pathwise convergence result (Theorem
24
) for a wide class of adaptive SDEs approximations. The central idea in our proof methodology is to employ
rough path theory
and first show that piecewise polynomial approximations of Brownian motion converge in the
\alpha\hskip 0.7113pt
-Hölder
metric along a sequence of nested partitions satisfying certain properties. This argument is a natural extension of the analysis presented in
[
72
, Section 13.3.2]
, which presents similar results for piecewise linear approximations of Brownian motion that are defined along a sequence of partitions that are both nested and deterministic.

Once we have
\alpha\hskip 0.7113pt
-Hölder convergence for the piecewise polynomial approximations, we can apply an extension of the
Universal Limit Theorem
from rough path theory
[
22
]
to show that the corresponding sequence of Controlled Differential Equations (CDEs) converges to the (Stratonovich) SDE solution. We show that pathwise convergence is still preserved when small
o(h)
local perturbations are added to the CDE solutions. By considering the local Taylor expansion of the CDEs between discretization times, we obtain the condition (
3
) from Theorem
1
for the numerical method to satisfy.
We conclude Section
2
by showing the no-area Milstein, Heun and Splitting Path Runge-Kutta (SPaRK) methods in Section
1.1
satisfy the convergence condition (
3
).

In Section
3
, we give an example demonstrating that without nested partitions, an adaptive step size control can induce a non-zero bias which prevents convergence.
Here, the decision to reduce a step size is based on whether it increases the value of the numerical solution. In particular, step sizes can “skip over” known values of
W

\displaystyle S_{t+\Delta t}=S_{t}

\displaystyle+\sigma_{t}\big(W_{t+\Delta t}^{1}-W_{t}^{1}\big)+\sigma_{t}\int_{t}^{t+\Delta t}\big(W_{u}^{2}-W_{t}^{2}\big)\circ dW_{u}^{1}+R_{\Delta t}(t),

(20)

where the remainder is
\hskip 0.7113ptR_{\Delta t}(t)=\sigma_{t}\int_{t}^{t+\Delta t}\big(e^{-\frac{1}{2}(u-t)+W_{u}^{2}-W_{t}^{2}}-\big(1+W_{u}^{2}-W_{t}^{2}\big)\big)\circ dW_{u}^{1}\hskip 0.7113pt
.
Therefore by (
20
), approximating
S_{t+\Delta t}
using one step of Heun’s method, with a sufficiently small step size, will result in a mean squared error of
\frac{1}{4}\sigma_{t}^{2}(\Delta t)^{2}+O((\Delta t)^{3})
.
As a consequence, we would expect that the overall accuracy of a numerical method can be improved by decreasing
\Delta t
if
\sigma_{t}
when large and increasing
\Delta t
when
\sigma_{t}
is small.
We observe that solvers which use a previsible step size of the form
\Delta t=\log(1+C\sigma_{t}^{-2})
achieve an order of magnitude more accuracy than they do with constant step sizes. In addition, we also demonstrate that similar convergence rates can be obtained when local errors are estimated via an embedded Runge-Kutta method and subsequently used by a ‘‘Proportional-Integral’’ (PI) adaptive step size controller based on
[
6
,
33
]
. However, due to our counterxample, we modify the PI step size controller so that it does not “skip over” times where Brownian information has previously been sampled.

In Section
5
, we conclude the paper and discuss potential areas of future research.
Among the areas of future research, we would like to extend our convergence analysis to a multidimensional extension of the stochastic Runge-Kutta method proposed in
[
61
]
(which can be viewed as a “Heun’s method for Itô SDEs”).
This would then enable general Itô SDEs to be solved with non-previsible step sizes, but without explicitly requiring vector field derivatives – which is the case for the no-area Milstein method.

Main result

To begin, we will first establish the “rough path” convergence of piecewise polynomial approximations along nested partitions to Brownian motion.

Proposition 13
.

Let
\{D_{n}\}
be the sequence of partitions given by Definition

\hskip 0.7113pt\mesh(D_{n})\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\sigma\Big(\big\{W_{t}:t\in[0,T]\big\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\infty}\Big),

(29)

where

.

Proof.

Since the enhancement function produces

contains the Brownian motion’s increment
\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}1\,dW_{t}=W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}

.

In order to show our approximations of Brownian motion converge in a rough path sense (i.e. in an

Theorem 14
(Nested piecewise unbiased approximations of Brownian motion).

Let
\{D_{n}\}
denote the sequence of partitions and
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}
the corresponding filtration given by Definition
11
. Moreover, we will assume (
27
) and
\hskip 0.7113pt\mesh(D_{n})\rightarrow 0
almost surely.
We define a sequence of approximations
\{\widetilde{W}^{n}\}

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{t}^{n}:=\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\hskip 0.7113pt.

Then for

almost surely and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\widetilde{W}_{t}^{n}-W_{t}\big)^{2}\hskip 0.7113pt\Big]\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a filtration, it follows that
\big\{\widetilde{W}^{n}\big\}=\big\{\mathbb{E}[W\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]\big\}
is a martingale.
Moreover, each

is square-integrable as by Jensen’s inequality and the tower law,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\widetilde{W}_{t}^{n}\big)^{2}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]^{2}\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\Big]=\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t}^{2}\big]=t.

Therefore, by Doob’s martingale convergence theorem, we have that for each

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\big]\hskip 0.7113pt,

(32)

almost surely and in

. Since
\hskip 0.7113pt\mesh(D_{n})\rightarrow 0
almost surely, we can see that
\sigma\big(W_{t}:t\in[0,T]\big)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
by Proposition

is independent of

, and therefore will have no effect on the conditional expectations within our proof.

.

If

Theorem 16

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\int_{s}^{t}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,r}^{n}\big)^{i}\,d\big(\widetilde{W}_{r}^{n}\big)^{j}-\int_{s}^{t}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,r}^{n}\big)^{j}\,d\big(\widetilde{W}_{r}^{n}\big)^{i}\bigg\}_{1\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113pti,\hskip 0.7113ptj\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113ptd}\hskip 0.7113pt,

\displaystyle A_{s,t}:=\bigg\{\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,r}^{i}\circ dW_{r}^{j}-\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,r}^{i}\circ dW_{r}^{j}\bigg\}_{1\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113pti,\hskip 0.7113ptj\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113ptd}\hskip 0.7113pt,

(33)

where

.

Proof.

When

, we see that
\big(\widetilde{A}_{s,t}^{n}\big)^{i,j}=A_{s,t}^{i,j}=0
and there is nothing to prove.
Since
\big(\widetilde{A}_{s,t}^{n}\big)^{i,j}=-\big(\widetilde{A}_{s,t}^{n}\big)^{j,i}
and

Let

.
We first note the following four properties about Brownian motion:

1.

\{W_{t_{k},t}\}_{t\in[t_{k},\hskip 0.7113ptt_{k+1}]}
is independent of
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\hskip 0.7113ptt_{k}]}
and
\{W_{t_{k+1},t}\}_{[t_{k+1},\hskip 0.7113ptT]}\hskip 0.7113pt
.

2.

Brownian motion has independent coordinate processes. That is, when
i\neq j
,
W^{i}
and
W^{j}
are independent.

3.

For
c>0
, the process
t\mapsto\frac{1}{c}W_{c^{2}t}
has the same law as a Brownian motion.

4.

For a fixed

\big\{W_{t}-\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t}|\big\{\int_{0}^{1}s^{\hskip 0.7113ptl}\,dW_{s}\big\}_{0\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113ptl\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113ptq}\big]\big\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is a centered Gaussian process on

and is independent of

. This is a direct consequence of a polynomial expansion of Brownian motion (see, for example,
[

, Theorem 2.2]
or
[

for such details).

Since the information currently known about
W
is given in

, and
D_{n}
contains no further subintervals of
[t_{k},\hskip 0.7113ptt_{k+1}]
for adjacent times
t_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt,t_{k+1}\in D_{n}\hskip 0.7113pt

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{t,t_{k}}^{n}=\mathbb{E}\bigg[W_{t_{k},t}\,\Big|\,\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}t^{\hskip 0.7113ptl}\,dW_{t}\hskip 0.7113pt,\hskip 0.7113pt0\leq l\leq q\bigg]\hskip 0.7113pt,

for

. In addition, it directly follows from the last three properties that the

-th and
j
-th coordinates of

are independent centered Gaussian processes. Hence, the

-th and
j
-th coordinates of

\displaystyle\,\bigg\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}}\Big(W_{t_{k},t(t_{k+1}-t_{k})}-\widetilde{W}_{t_{k},t(t_{k+1}-t_{k})}^{n}\Big)\bigg\}_{t\in[0,1]}\hskip 0.7113pt,

are independent centered Gaussian processes. As before, we note that any additional information in
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is independent of
W
and does not affect conditional expectations.
Therefore, it follows that
\{W_{t_{k},t}^{i}\}_{t\in[t_{k},\hskip 0.7113ptt_{k+1}]}
and
\{W_{t_{k},t}^{j}\}_{t\in[t_{k},\hskip 0.7113ptt_{k+1}]}
are

-conditionally independent processes and

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{t}^{n}=\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t_{k},t}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\big].

Thus for a sequence of uniform partitions
\{\triangle_{m}\}
of
[s,t]
with
\mesh(\triangle_{m})\rightarrow 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{t_{l}\in\triangle_{m}}W_{s,t_{l}}^{i}W_{t_{l},t_{l+1}}^{j}\hskip 0.7113pt\Big|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{l}\in\triangle_{m}}\mathbb{E}\big[W_{s,t_{l}}^{i}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\hskip 0.7113pt\mathbb{E}\big[W_{t_{l},t_{l+1}}^{j}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{l}\in\triangle_{m}}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,t_{l}}^{n}\big)^{i}\big(\widetilde{W}_{t_{l},t_{l+1}}^{n}\big)^{j}.

(34)

Since Itô integrals can be defined as the limit of their Riemann sum
approximations, with convergence taking place is in the standard
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
sense, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{t_{l}\in\triangle_{m}}W_{s,t_{l}}^{i}W_{t_{l},t_{l+1}}^{j}\hskip 0.7113pt\Big|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\,dW_{u}^{j}\hskip 0.7113pt\Big|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigg]\bigg)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{t_{l}\in\triangle_{m}}W_{s,t_{l}}^{i}W_{t_{l},t_{l+1}}^{j}\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\,dW_{u}^{j}\bigg]\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\rightarrow 0,

by Jensen’s inequality and the tower property. Since
W^{i}
and
W^{j}
are independent, we also observe that the Itô and Stratonovich formulations of the integral coincide. That is,

. Hence, by taking the limit of (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\circ dW_{u}^{j}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigg]=\int_{s}^{t}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,u}^{n}\big)^{i}d\big(\widetilde{W}_{u}^{n}\big)^{j}.

(35)

\{F_{n}\}
is a filtration, it follows that the sequence (

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,u}^{n}\big)^{i}d\big(\widetilde{W}_{u}^{n}\big)^{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\circ dW_{u}^{j}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\bigg]=\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\circ dW_{u}^{j}\hskip 0.7113pt,

(36)

as

almost surely and in

We shall now introduce the concepts of rough paths and
\alpha\hskip 0.7113pt
-Hölder regularity. These are the two key ingredients needed to establish SDE (or CDE) solutions as Lipschitz continuous functions of the Brownian motion (or driving path), see
[

Definition 17
.

A rough path is a continuous function
\boldsymbol{X}:\Delta_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
where
\Delta_{T}:=\{(s,t):0\leq s\leq t\leq T\}
. The rough path
\boldsymbol{X}=(1,X,\mathbb{X})
is said to be
\alpha
-Hölder continuous if
\hskip 0.7113pt\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{\alpha\text{-H\"{o}l;[0,T]}}<\infty
where the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha\text{-H\"{o}l;[s,t]}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{\alpha\text{-H\"{o}l;[s,t]}}:=\max\bigg(\sup_{s\leq u<v\leq t}\frac{\|X_{u,v}\|_{2}}{|v-u|^{\alpha}}\,,\sup_{s\leq u<v\leq t}\frac{\|\mathbb{X}_{u,v}\|_{2}}{|v-u|^{2\alpha}}\bigg),

(37)

is finite. Similarly, we define the
\alpha
-Hölder metric between rough paths
\boldsymbol{X}
and
\boldsymbol{Y}
as

\displaystyle d_{\alpha\text{-H\"{o}l;[s,t]}}\big(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{Y}\big):=\max\bigg(\sup_{s\leq u<v\leq t}\hskip-2.13394pt\frac{\|X_{u,v}-Y_{u,v}\|_{2}}{|v-u|^{\alpha}}\,,\hskip-1.42262pt\sup_{s\leq u<v\leq t}\hskip-2.13394pt\frac{\|\mathbb{X}_{u,v}-\mathbb{Y}_{u,v}\|_{2}}{|v-u|^{2\alpha}}\bigg).

(38)

Definition 18
.

We define a “homogeneous” norm
\|\cdot\|
for path increments as

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{X}\|:=\max\Big(\|X\|_{2},\|\mathbb{X}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big),

(39)

where
\boldsymbol{X}=(1,X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the standard Euclidean norm.

Now we have introduced some key definitions from rough path theory, we can strengthen the pointwise and mean squared convergence established in Theorem
14
.
To do so, we will largely follow the rough path analysis detailed in
[

Theorem 19
(Rough path convergence of nested unbiased approximations).

Let
\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}^{n}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}_{\hskip-2.13394pts,t}^{n}\}_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
be the rough path defined by the piecewise polynomial
\widetilde{W}^{n}\hskip-2.13394pt
as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}_{\hskip-2.13394pts,t}^{n}:=\big(1,\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\hskip 0.7113pt,\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}\hskip 0.7113pt\big),

where
\hskip 0.7113pt\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}:=\big\{(\widetilde{W}_{t}^{n})^{i}-(\widetilde{W}_{s}^{n})^{i}\big\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
and
\,\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}:=\big\{\int_{s}^{t}(\widetilde{W}_{s,u}^{n})^{i}\hskip 0.7113ptd(\widetilde{W}_{u}^{n})^{j}\big\}_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\hskip 0.7113pt
. Then

\displaystyle d_{\alpha\text{-H\"{o}l;[0,T]}}(\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}^{n}\hskip-2.84526pt,\boldsymbol{W})\rightarrow 0,

(40)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
almost surely, where
\alpha\in\big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\big)
and
\boldsymbol{W}=\{\boldsymbol{W}_{\hskip-2.13394pts,t}\}_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
denotes the “Stratonovich enhanced” Brownian motion given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{W}_{\hskip-2.13394pts,t}:=\big(1,W_{s,t}\hskip 0.7113pt,\mathbb{W}_{s,t}\hskip 0.7113pt\big),

with
\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}:=\big\{W_{t}^{i}-W_{s}^{i}\big\}_{1\leq i\leq d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\hskip 0.7113pt
and
\hskip 0.7113pt\mathbb{W}_{s,t}:=\big\{\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\circ dW_{u}^{j}\big\}_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\hskip 0.7113pt
.

Proof.

Let
\beta\in(\alpha,\frac{1}{2})
denote a fixed constant. Then by
[
72
, Corollary 13.14 (i)]
, there exists
c>0
(depending on
\beta
and
T
) such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\exp\Big(c\hskip 0.7113pt\|\boldsymbol{W}\|_{\beta\text{-H\"{o}l;[0,T]}}^{2}\Big)\bigg]<\infty.

In particular, this implies that there exists a non-negative random variable
C_{1}
with
\|C_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}<\infty
so that, for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
, the increments of the path
\boldsymbol{W}(\omega)
satisfy

\displaystyle\big\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\hskip-2.13394pts,t}\big\|^{2}\leq C_{1}|t-s|^{2\beta},

for

where
\|\cdot\|
is the norm previously given by (
39
) in Definition
18
. Hence, there exists a non-negative random variable

with

and

\displaystyle-C_{2}|t-s|^{2\beta}\leq\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\circ dW_{u}^{j}\leq C_{2}|t-s|^{2\beta}.

(41)

Since
\mathbb{E}\big[C_{2}|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]
is a martingale, we can apply Doob’s maximal inequality to give

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sup_{0\leq m\leq n}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[C_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{m}\big]\Big)\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}\leq 2\big\|\mathbb{E}\big[C_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}\leq 2\|C_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}.

(42)

Taking the expectation of the inequality (
41

\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and applying equation (
35
) gives

\displaystyle-C_{3}|t-s|^{2\beta}\leq\int_{s}^{t}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,u}^{n}\big)^{i}d\big(\widetilde{W}_{u}^{n}\big)^{j}\leq C_{3}|t-s|^{2\beta},

(43)

where
C_{3}:=\sup_{n\hskip 0.7113pt\geq\hskip 0.7113pt0}\big(\mathbb{E}\big[C_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\big)
is finite almost surely due to the upper bound (

, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{n\hskip 0.7113pt\geq\hskip 0.7113pt0}\bigg\|\int_{s}^{t}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,u}^{n}\big)\otimes d\big(\widetilde{W}_{u}^{n}\big)\bigg\|_{2}\leq C_{4}|t-s|^{2\beta}.

(44)

By a similar martingale argument, the exists a positive random variable
C_{5}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\hskip 0.7113pt\geq\hskip 0.7113pt0}\big\|\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}\leq C_{5}|t-s|^{\beta},

where

is finite almost surely. Thus, letting

\displaystyle\big\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\hskip-2.13394pts,t}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}|t-s|^{\beta},

(45)

\displaystyle\big\|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|

\displaystyle=\max\bigg(\big\|\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt,\bigg\|\int_{s}^{t}\big(\widetilde{W}_{s,u}^{n}\big)\otimes d\big(\widetilde{W}_{u}^{n}\big)\bigg\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)\leq C_{6}|t-s|^{\beta},

(46)

for

. Letting
\mathbb{W}_{s,t}
and
\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}
denote the second iterated integrals of

\widetilde{W}^{n}
over the interval

\displaystyle\frac{\big\|W_{s,t}-\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{\big\|W_{s,t}-\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}}{|t-s|^{\beta}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}\big\|W_{s,t}-\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}\bigg)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}},

\displaystyle\frac{\big\|\mathbb{W}_{s,t}-\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{\big\|\mathbb{W}_{s,t}-\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}}{|t-s|^{2\beta}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}\big\|\mathbb{W}_{s,t}-\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}\bigg)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}.

By (

\displaystyle\frac{\big\|W_{s,t}-\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{7}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}\big\|W_{s,t}-\widetilde{W}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}\bigg)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}},

(47)

\displaystyle\frac{\big\|\mathbb{W}_{s,t}-\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{7}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}\big\|\mathbb{W}_{s,t}-\mathbb{\widetilde{W}}_{s,t}^{n}\big\|_{2}\bigg)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}.

(48)

Note that it immediately follows from the uniform Hölder bounds (
45
) and (
46
) that, almost surely, any subsequence of

\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}{}_{\hskip-2.13394pts,t}^{n}

Since

is uniformly continuous almost surely, it directly follows that it must be zero.
Hence, almost surely,

In particular, this implies that the right hand sides of equations (

\hskip 0.7113ptd_{\alpha\text{-H\"{o}l;}[0,T]}(\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}{}^{n},\boldsymbol{W})\rightarrow 0

2.2
Convergence of “polynomial driven” CDEs with small local errors

Since we have shown that
\big\{\boldsymbol{\widetilde{W}}^{n}\big\}
converges to the Stratonovich Brownian motion
\boldsymbol{W}
in the
\alpha\hskip 0.7113pt
-Hölder metric, we can apply a generalization of the
Universal Limit Theorem
from rough path theory
[
22
, Theorem 4.11]
to show that the associated sequence of controlled differential equations (with the

\widetilde{W}^{n}
) converges to the rough differential equation driven by
\boldsymbol{W}
– which is simply the Stratonovich SDE (
1
).
To this end, we use the following definition to impose smoothness on the vector fields.

Definition 20
.

We say that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{e}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{e}
is
\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma)
with
\gamma>1
if it is bounded with
\lfloor\gamma\rfloor
bounded derivatives, the last being Hölder continuous with exponent
(\gamma-\lfloor\gamma\rfloor)

f
is
\mathrm{Lip}(1)
if it is bounded and Lipschitz continuous.

Theorem 21
(Rough path convergence of piecewise polynomial driven CDEs).

Suppose the SDE drift
f
is
\mathrm{Lip}(1)
and each noise vector field
g_{i}
is
\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma)
for
\gamma\in(2,3)
. Let
\{D_{n}\}
denote a sequence of nested partitions obtained from the Brownian motion using an enhancement function
\mathcal{E}
, with
q\geq 0
fixed, and a refinement function
\mathcal{R}
, according to (
24
). For each (random) partition
D_{n}\hskip 0.7113pt

\widetilde{W}^{n}
be the associated piecewise degree

21

Let
y
be the solution of the SDE (
1
) and, for
n\geq 0
, we define
\widetilde{y}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}

\displaystyle d\hskip 0.7113pt\widetilde{y}_{t}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}

\displaystyle=f(\hskip 0.7113pt\widetilde{y}_{t}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn})\hskip 0.7113ptdt+g(\hskip 0.7113pt\widetilde{y}_{t}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn})\hskip 0.7113ptd\widetilde{W}_{t}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn},

(49)

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{0}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}

\displaystyle=y_{0}\hskip 0.7113pt.

Then, letting
\boldsymbol{y}
and
\boldsymbol{\widetilde{y}}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}

\displaystyle d_{\alpha\text{-H\"{o}l;[0,T]}}(\boldsymbol{\widetilde{y}}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}\hskip-2.84526pt,\boldsymbol{y})\rightarrow 0,

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
19

[

Remark 22
.

Technically speaking, we define the rough path
\boldsymbol{y}=(1,y,\mathbbm{y})
as the limit of
\{\boldsymbol{\widetilde{y}}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}\}_{n\hskip 0.7113pt\geq\hskip 0.7113pt0}
and, by
[

,

can be taken to be the solution of the rough differential equation driven by Stratonovich enhanced Brownian motion. In particular, from

In practice, it is unlikely that each polynomial driven CDE can be solved exactly.
However, if our numerical method approximates the CDE (
49

14

To make this precise, we follow the error analysis presented in
[
23

However, as rough path theory was formulated after
[

]
, we can use an extension of

[

, Theorem 10.29]
to establish a Lipschitz property of CDE flows.

Theorem 23
(CDEs driven by Brownian polynomials produce Lipschitz flows).

As before, let
\{\widetilde{W}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}\}

\displaystyle\|\hskip 0.7113pt\widetilde{x}_{t}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}-\widetilde{y}_{t}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}\|_{2}\leq L\hskip 0.7113pt\|\hskip 0.7113pt\widetilde{x}_{s}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}-\widetilde{y}_{s}^{\hskip 0.7113ptn}\|_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt,

Using the Lipschitz estimate (

Theorem 24
(Pathwise convergence of strong adaptive SDE approximations).

For each partition

Remark 25

If the local errors can be bounded by

Counterexample involving non-nested step sizes

In this brief section, we will present a simple counterexample showing that non-nested adaptive step sizes can potentially prevent the approximation converging to the desired SDE solution.
The idea is simply that by selectively “skipping over” values of the Brownian path, we can induce a local

O(1)

5
Conclusion and future work

The main contribution of this paper shows that standard SDE solvers, such as the Heun and no-area Milstein methods, converge pathwise when used with non-previsible adaptive step sizes (under mild conditions).
This enables us to use embedded methods, which are popular within ODE numerics, to establish local errors for an adaptive step size controller in the general SDE setting. However, we leave further investigations of such adaptive controllers as a future topic.

We also introduce a numerical method called “SPaRK” which uses space-time Lévy area to achieve smaller local errors than standard “increment only” SDE solvers.
This stochastic Runge-Kutta method is compatible with the proposed adaptive step size theory and we empirically demonstrated its efficacy on a two-dimensional SDE.
The results of this paper also lead to further questions regarding adaptive SDE solvers:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg\|g^{\hskip 0.7113pt\prime}(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113ptg(Y_{k})\bigg(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}W_{t_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt,t}\otimes\circ\,dW_{t}-\Big(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}^{\otimes 2}+H_{k}\otimes W_{k}-W_{k}\otimes H_{k}\Big)\bigg)\bigg\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\|g_{j}^{\hskip 0.7113pt\prime}(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113ptg_{i}(Y_{k})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\hskip-2.84526ptW_{t_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt,t}^{i}\circ dW_{t}^{j}-\Big(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}^{i}W_{k}^{j}+H_{k}^{i}W_{k}^{j}-W_{k}^{i}H_{k}^{j}\Big)\bigg)^{2}\Bigg]}_{=\frac{1}{12}h_{k}^{2}\,\text{ by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{foster2023levyarea}{}{}, Theorem 5.4]} and Brownian scaling}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}h_{k}^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\|g_{j}^{\hskip 0.7113pt\prime}(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113ptg_{i}(Y_{k})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big].

On the other hand, two half steps of Heun’s method on
[t_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt,t_{k+1}]
can be expanded as

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}f(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})h_{k}+g(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}+g^{\hskip 0.7113pt\prime}(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\hskip 0.7113ptg(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\bigg(\frac{1}{2}W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\otimes 2}\bigg)+o(h_{k})

\displaystyle=Y_{k}+f(Y_{k})h_{k}+g(Y_{k})\big(W_{k}+W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 24.18483pt+g^{\hskip 0.7113pt\prime}(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113ptg(Y_{k})\bigg(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}^{\otimes 2}+W_{k}\otimes W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\otimes 2}\bigg)+o(h_{k}),

where we now use the notation
W_{k}:=W_{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-W_{t_{k}}
and
W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}:=W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
.
For
i\neq j
, we have
\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}\circ dW_{u}^{j}=\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}^{i}dW_{u}^{j}\hskip 0.7113pt
, and so applying Itô’s isometry gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\hskip-2.84526ptW_{t_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt,t}^{i}\circ dW_{t}^{j}-\Big(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}^{i}W_{k}^{j}+W_{k}^{i}W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{j}+\frac{1}{2}W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{i}W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{j}\Big)\bigg)^{\vskip-2.84526pt2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\hskip-2.84526ptW_{t_{k},t}^{i}\circ dW_{t}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}W_{k}^{i}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{k}^{j}\bigg)^{2}\hskip-2.13394pt+\bigg(\int_{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{k+1}}\hskip-2.84526ptW_{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}},t}^{i}\circ dW_{t}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{i}W_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{j}\bigg)^{\vskip-2.84526pt2}\hskip 0.7113pt\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}h_{k}\bigg)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}h_{k}\bigg)^{2}=\frac{1}{8}\hskip 0.7113pth_{k}^{2}\hskip 0.7113pt.

Hence, the leading term in the mean squared error of the two-step Heun’s method is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}h_{k}^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\|g_{j}^{\hskip 0.7113pt\prime}(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113ptg_{i}(Y_{k})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big].

Thus, provided the step size is sufficiently small, we see that one step of the stochastic Runge-Kutta method (
16
) is more accurate than two steps of Heun’s method (
14
).
In addition, two steps of Heun’s method uses an extra evaluation of the vector fields.

It is also known that Heun’s method achieves second order weak convergence for SDEs with additive noise
[
45
]
(that is, when
g(y)=\sigma
for a constant matrix
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{e\times d}
),

\displaystyle dy_{t}=f(y_{t})\hskip 0.7113ptdt+\sigma\hskip 0.7113ptdW_{t}\hskip 0.7113pt,

where, for the purposes of this section, we will assume that
f
is sufficiently smooth.
For additive noise SDEs, the Splitting Path Runge-Kutta (SPaRK) method becomes

\displaystyle Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}:=Y_{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptf(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113pth_{k}+\sigma\bigg(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}+\sqrt{3}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{k}\bigg),\hskip 14.22636ptZ_{k+1}:=Y_{k}+f(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})h_{k}+\sigma\hskip 0.7113ptW_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt,

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}:=Y_{k}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{6}\hskip 0.7113ptf(Y_{k})+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\hskip 0.7113ptf(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{6}\hskip 0.7113ptf(Z_{k+1})\bigg)h_{k}+\sigma W_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt.

Using the classical Taylor theorem
f(b)=f(a)+f^{\prime}(a)(b-a)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(a)(b-a)^{\otimes 2}+R
with
R:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{2}f^{\prime\prime\prime}(a+t(b-a))(b-a)^{\otimes 3}dt
, we can Taylor expand
Y_{k+1}
to give

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}=Y_{k}

\displaystyle+f(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113pth_{k}+\sigma W_{k}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}f^{\prime}(Y_{k})\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptf(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113pth_{k}+\sigma\bigg(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}+\sqrt{3}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{k}\bigg)\bigg)h_{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{6}\hskip 0.7113ptf^{\prime}(Y_{k})\Big(f(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})h_{k}+\sigma W_{k}\Big)h_{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(Y_{k})\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptf(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113pth_{k}+\sigma\bigg(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}+\sqrt{3}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{k}\bigg)\bigg)^{\otimes 2}\,\bigg)h_{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{6}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(Y_{k})\Big(f(Y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\hskip 0.7113pth_{k}+\sigma\hskip 0.7113ptW_{k}\Big)^{\otimes 2}\,\bigg)h_{k}+O\big(h_{k}^{\frac{5}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=Y_{k}

\displaystyle+f(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113pth_{k}+\sigma W_{k}+f^{\prime}(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113pt\sigma\bigg(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}+H_{k}\bigg)h_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptf^{\prime}(Y_{k})f(Y_{k})h_{k}^{2}+O\big(h_{k}^{\frac{5}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptf^{\prime\prime}(Y_{k})\hskip 0.7113pt\sigma^{\otimes 2}\bigg(\frac{6-\sqrt{3}}{12}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{k}^{\otimes 2}+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{k}\otimes H_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{k}\otimes W_{k}+\sqrt{3}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{k}^{\otimes 2}\bigg)h_{k}\hskip 0.7113pt.

We note that in the above Taylor expansion, we have
\big(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}+H_{k}\big)h_{k}=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}W_{t_{k},t}\hskip 0.7113ptdt
and
\hskip 0.7113pt\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\frac{6-\sqrt{3}}{12}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{k}^{\otimes 2}+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{k}\otimes H_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{k}\otimes W_{k}+\sqrt{3}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{k}^{\otimes 2}\Big)h_{k}\Big]=\frac{1}{2}h_{k}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}W_{t_{k},t}^{\otimes 2}\hskip 0.7113ptdt\Big]
.

Thus, by the standard mean squared error analysis of Milstein and Tretyakov
[
51
]
,
we see that stochastic Runge-Kutta method will converge strongly with rate
O(h^{\frac{3}{2}})
.
Similarly, it is also clear that the method will achieve second order weak convergence.
We refer the reader to
[

]
for more details on the analysis of commutative noise SDEs. For non-additive noise SDEs, it is straightforward to see that the proposed stochastic Runge-Kutta method exhibits the same strong convergence rates as Heun’s method.

Appendix B
Generating increments and integrals of Brownian motion

In this section, we will briefly outline how increments
W_{s,t}
and space-time Lévy areas
H_{s,t}:=\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}W_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113ptdu\hskip 0.7113pt
of Brownian motion can be generated for adaptive SDE solvers.

We emphasise that the results detailed in this section are already well-established in the SDE numerics literature
[
15
,
35
]
and are only included here for completeness.

To begin, we note that for any
u\in[s,t]
, a path increment
W_{s,u}:=W_{u}-W_{s}
can be generated conditional on
W_{s,t}
using standard properties of the Brownian bridge. When
u=\frac{1}{2}(s+t)
, this leads to well-known
Lévy construction of Brownian motion
.

Theorem 33
(Conditional distribution of Brownian increments).

For
s\leq u\leq t
,

\displaystyle W_{s,u}

\displaystyle=\frac{u-s}{t-s}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}+B_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,\hskip 14.22636ptW_{u,t}=\frac{t-u}{t-s}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}-B_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,

where the Brownian bridge increment
B_{s,u}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\frac{(u-s)(t-u)}{t-s}I_{d}\big)
is independent of
W_{s,t}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,(t-s)I_{d}\big)
. When
u=\frac{1}{2}(s+t)
is the midpoint of
[s,t]
, this simplifies to

\displaystyle W_{s,u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}+B_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,\hskip 14.22636ptW_{u,t}=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}-B_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,

where
B_{s,u}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\frac{1}{4}\hskip 0.7113pt(t-s)I_{d}\big)
.

Proof.

Let
B_{s,u}:=W_{s,u}-\frac{u-s}{t-s}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}
. The covariance between
B_{s,u}
and
W_{s,t}
is

\displaystyle\cov(B_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,W_{s,t})

\displaystyle=\cov(W_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,W_{s,t})-\frac{u-s}{t-s}\hskip 0.7113pt\cov(W_{s,t}\hskip 0.7113pt,W_{s,t})

\displaystyle=(u-s)-\frac{u-s}{t-s}\hskip 0.7113pt(t-s)=0.

Since
B_{s,u}
and
W_{s,t}
depend on the same Brownian motion, they are jointly normal,
and therefore independent. The variance of
B_{s,u}
is straightforward to compute.

Extending the theorem given above to include both increments and space-time Lévy areas of the Brownian motion is more involved, but based on a similar argument (which also uses that uncorrelated jointly normal random variables are independent).

Theorem 34
(Conditional distribution of increments and space-time Lévy areas of Brownian motion within an interval,
[
15
, Theorems 6.1.4 and 6.1.6]
).

For
s\leq u\leq t
,

\displaystyle W_{s,u}

\displaystyle=\frac{u-s}{h}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}+\frac{6(u-s)(t-u)}{h^{2}}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}+\frac{2(a+b)}{h}\hskip 0.7113ptX_{1}\hskip 0.7113pt,

\displaystyle W_{u,t}

\displaystyle=\frac{t-u}{h}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}-\frac{6(u-s)(t-u)}{h^{2}}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}-\frac{2(a+b)}{h}\hskip 0.7113ptX_{1}\hskip 0.7113pt,

\displaystyle H_{s,u}

\displaystyle=\frac{(u-s)^{2}}{h^{2}}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}-\frac{a}{u-s}\hskip 0.7113ptX_{1}+\frac{c}{u-s}\hskip 0.7113ptX_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt,

\displaystyle H_{u,t}

\displaystyle=\frac{(u-s)^{2}}{h^{2}}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}-\frac{b}{t-u}\hskip 0.7113ptX_{1}-\frac{c}{t-u}\hskip 0.7113ptX_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt,

where
W_{s,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,hI_{d})\hskip 0.7113pt,H_{s,t}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\frac{1}{12}\hskip 0.7113pthI_{d}\big)
and
X_{1}\hskip 0.7113pt,X_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
are independent,
h=t-s
denotes the length of the interval and the coefficients
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
are given by

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle:=\frac{(u-s)^{\frac{7}{2}}(t-u)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2h\sqrt{(u-s)^{3}+(t-u)^{3}}}\hskip 0.7113pt,\hskip 14.22636ptb:=\frac{(u-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-u)^{\frac{7}{2}}}{2h\sqrt{(u-s)^{3}+(t-u)^{3}}}\hskip 0.7113pt,

\displaystyle\hskip 64.01869ptc:=\frac{\sqrt{3}(u-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}(t-u)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{6\sqrt{(u-s)^{3}+(t-u)^{3}}}\hskip 0.7113pt.

When
u=\frac{1}{2}(s+t)
is the midpoint of the interval
[s,t]
, these formulae simplify to

\displaystyle W_{s,u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}+\frac{3}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}+Z_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,\hskip 14.22636ptW_{u,t}=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptW_{s,t}-\frac{3}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}-Z_{s,u}\hskip 0.7113pt,

\displaystyle H_{s,u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}-\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptZ_{s,u}+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptN_{s,t}\hskip 0.7113pt,\hskip 14.22636ptH_{u,t}=\frac{1}{4}\hskip 0.7113ptH_{s,t}-\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptZ_{s,u}-\frac{1}{2}\hskip 0.7113ptN_{s,t}\hskip 0.7113pt,

where
W_{s,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,hI_{d}),Z_{s,u}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\frac{1}{16}\hskip 0.7113pthI_{d}\big)
and
H_{s,t}\hskip 0.7113pt,N_{s,t}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\frac{1}{12}\hskip 0.7113pthI_{d}\big)
are all independent Gaussian random vectors.

Norm of the determinant for a Gaussian random matrix

In this section, we present the following known result, which was helpful in Section

.

Theorem 35
(
L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
norm of the determinant for a Gaussian random matrix).

For
n\geq 1
, let
A_{n}=\{a_{ij}\}_{1\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113pti,\hskip 0.7113ptj\hskip 0.7113pt\leq\hskip 0.7113ptn}
be an
n\times n
matrix with independent entries
a_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
denote a standard normal random variable. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\hskip 0.7113pt|\det A_{n}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\hskip 0.7113pt|Z|^{n}\big].

(71)

Proof.

Let
p_{n}
denote the probability density function of
|\det A_{n}\hskip 0.7113pt|
. Then the Mellin transform of
p_{n}
is given by
[
10
, Equation (2.3)]
, but with
a=\frac{1}{2}
and
\beta=1
. That is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}p_{n}(x)\hskip 0.7113ptdx=\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}n(s-1)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}(s-1)+\frac{1}{2}j\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}j\big)}\bigg),

where
\Gamma
is the standard Gamma function. Since
\mathbb{E}\big[\hskip 0.7113pt|\det A_{n}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\big]=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\hskip 0.7113ptp_{n}(x)\hskip 0.7113ptdx

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\hskip 0.7113pt|\det A_{n}\hskip 0.7113pt|\hskip 0.7113pt\big]

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}j\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}j\big)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}n}\,\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{2}n}\,\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}(n+1)\big)}{\sqrt{\pi}},

which is the
n

[
74
, Equation (18)]
).

In particular, when
n=2
, we obtain the result used in the proof of Theorem
29
.

Appendix D
A lower bound for the SABR-specific previsible step size

By the construction of the previsible step size (
65
), applying Heun’s method to the SABR model (
64
) with step size
h\equiv h(\nu_{t})
gives a local mean squared error of
\frac{1}{4}Ch
. Since the local errors propagate linearly, the global mean squared error will be
O(C)
.
On the other hand, we can also consider the “average step size” taken by the method.

Theorem 38
.

Let
\nu_{t}:=-\frac{1}{2}t+W_{t}
denote the log-volatility component of (
64
) and let
h(\nu_{t}):=\log(1+Ce^{-2\nu_{t}})
be the previsible step size control with
C>0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[h(\nu_{t})\big]\geq\log(1+Ce^{t}).

(73)

Proof.

We first note that the previsible step size control
x\mapsto h(x)
is convex as

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}h(x)=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}(\log(1+Ce^{-2x}))=\frac{4Ce^{2x}}{(C+e^{2x})^{2}}>0
. Thus, by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[h(\nu_{t})\big]\geq h\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\nu_{t}\big]\big)=h\Big(\hskip-0.7113pt-\frac{1}{2}t\Big)=\log\big(1+Ce^{t}\big).

Hence, if
C
is sufficiently small, the average step size at any
t\in[0,T]
is
O(C)
, and results in a global
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
error of
O(\sqrt{C}\hskip 0.7113pt)
.
This mimics the
O(\sqrt{h}\hskip 0.7113pt)
strong convergence of SDE solvers with a constant step size. However, since (
73
) increases with time, we can see that the variable step size methods tend to take increasingly larger steps.

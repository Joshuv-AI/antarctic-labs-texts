Anosov geodesic flows on surfaces
Hao-Tong Yan The Ohio State University

Abstract




1.
Introduction

This paper is an exposition of the major results of P. Eberlein’s paper,
When is a geodesic flow of Anosov type? I

[
Ebe73
]
, in the special case when the manifold
M
is a surface. We follow Eberlein’s coverage closely, adding details when helpful, and taking advantage of simplifications given by the dimension two case. The objective is to give readers a more tractable introduction to the important topic of Anosov geodesic flows, which highlights key concepts and arguments without worrying about generalizations to arbitrary dimension. As such, this paper is suited for a beginning graduate or advanced undergraduate student who is familiar with basic Riemannian geometry, such as that covered by the first half of
[
dC92
]
.

The main arc of this paper is divided into three parts. Section 2 highlights some basic concepts and results from Riemannian geometry. For more in-depth coverage, refer to
[
dC92
]
. Section 3 details useful intermediary results when the manifold
M
has no conjugate points. The work in these two sections culminate in the statement and proof of the main result in Section 4, which defines an Anosov flow and specifies conditions on Jacobi fields and curvature that are equivalent to the geodesic flow being of Anosov type.

The author would like to thank Andrey Gogolev (The Ohio State University) for advising this project and Jingyin Huang (The Ohio State University) for providing helpful feedback.

2.
Preliminaries

We start with basic definitions and results. While our main theorem assumes
M
is a complete
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold of dimension two, the results in this section are dimension agnostic; hence for now we may assume
M
is a complete
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
with Riemannian metric
\left\langle,\right\rangle
. In all cases,
M
is equipped with the Levi-Civita connection. Let
TM
and
SM
denote the full tangent bundle and unit tangent bundle of
M
, respectively, and let
\pi
be the projection map onto
M
in either case. For any vector
v\in TM
, let
\gamma_{v}
be the unique geodesic in
M
such that
\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(0)=v
, which is guaranteed by completeness of
M
.

A
complete flow

t\to\varphi^{t}:N\to N
on a
C^{\infty}
manifold
N
is a homeomorphism of
(\mathbb{R},+)
into the group of homeomorphisms of
N
. We say the flow is
C^{k}
differentiable,
0\leqslant k\leqslant\infty
, if the map
(t,n)\to\varphi^{t}(n):\mathbb{R}\times N\to N
is
C^{k}
differentiable.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and let
v\in TM
. We define the
geodesic flow

g^{t}:TM\to TM
on
TM
as

g^{t}v=\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(t)\;,

which is the velocity of
\gamma_{v}
at time
t
.

The geodesic flow is a complete
C^{\infty}
flow on
TM
, and also in
SM
, since
g^{t}
leaves
SM
invariant for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
Y
denote the vector field in both
TM
and
SM
induced by the geodesic flow. In other words, for any real-valued function
f:TM\to\mathbb{R}
(or
f:SM\to\mathbb{R}
), we have

Y(v)f=\left.\frac{d}{dt}f(g^{t}v)\right\rvert_{t=0}\;.

Working with this definition, we have for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left(dg^{s}Y(v)\right)f=\left.\frac{d}{dt}f(g^{s}g^{t}v)\right\rvert_{t=0}=\left.\frac{d}{dt}f(g^{t}g^{s}v)\right\rvert_{t=0}=Y(g^{s}v)f\;,

so
dg^{s}Y(v)=Y(g^{s}v)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A vector field
J
along a geodesic
\gamma
of
M
is called a
Jacobi field
if

J^{\prime\prime}(t)+R(\gamma^{\prime}(t),J(t))\gamma^{\prime}(t)=0\;,

where the ticks over
J
denote covariant differentiation along
\gamma
and
R
is the curvature tensor.

By passing to local coordinate systems along points of
\gamma
, we see that this equation yields a system of
n
linear ODEs of the second order, so
J
is uniquely determined by the values
J(0)
and
J^{\prime}(0)
. Therefore, we have
2n
linearly independent Jacobi fields along
\gamma
; let
J(\gamma)
denote their span. Let
J_{0}(\gamma)\subseteq J(\gamma)
be the
(2n-2)
-dimensional subspace of
perpendicular
Jacobi fields
J
along
\gamma
, that is, where
\left\langle J(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right\rangle=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The
tangential
Jacobi fields
J
along
\gamma
can be expressed in the form
J(t)=(\alpha+\beta t)\gamma^{\prime}(t)
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For
a\neq b
, we say that
\gamma(a)
and
\gamma(b)
are
conjugate
if there exists a nonzero Jacobi field
J
along
\gamma
such that
J(a)=J(b)=0
. We say that
M
has
no conjugate points
if there exists no pair of conjugate points along any geodesic in
M
, or in other words, for any geodesic
\gamma
in
M
and any nonzero Jacobi field
J
along
\gamma
,
J(t)=0
for at most one number
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
J
be a Jacobi field along a geodesic
\gamma
. Recall that if
a\neq b
and
\gamma(a)
and
\gamma(b)
are not conjugate, then
J
is uniquely determined by
J(a)
and
J(b)
. Further, if
J
is perpendicular to
\gamma
at any two nonconjugate points, then it is perpendicular everywhere.

Let
N\subseteq M
be a proper
C^{\infty}
Riemannian submanifold of
M
, and
\gamma
be a unit speed geodesic of
M
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
is perpendicular to
T_{\gamma(0)}N
. A Jacobi field
J
along
\gamma
is an
N
-Jacobi field
if
J
is perpendicular to
\gamma
,
J(0)\in T_{\gamma(0)}N
, and
J^{\prime}(0)+S_{\gamma^{\prime}(0)}(J(0))\in(T_{\gamma(0)}N)^{\perp}
, where
S_{\gamma^{\prime}(0)}
is the linear operator given by the second fundamental form on
N
. Equivalently,
J
is an
N
-Jacobi field if it can be written as

J(s)=\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(s,0)\;,

where
r:(-\infty,\infty)\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M
is a
C^{\infty}
variation of the form

r(s,t)=\exp_{\pi Z(t)}(sZ(t))=(\pi\circ g^{s})(Z(t))\;,

where
Z(t)
is a
C^{\infty}
curve in the unit normal bundle of
N
such that
Z(0)=\gamma^{\prime}(0)
. Note that
\partial r/\partial t
denotes shorthand for
dr(\partial/\partial t)
.

If
N
is a point, then a Jacobi field
J
is an
N
-Jacobi field if and only if
J(0)=0
and
J
is perpendicular to
\gamma
. If
N
is a geodesic
\sigma
and
J(0)\neq 0
, then a Jacobi field
J
is an
N
-Jacobi field if and only if
J(0)
is tangent to
\sigma
,
J
is perpendicular to
\gamma
, and
\left\langle J(0),J^{\prime}(0)\right\rangle=0
.

Definition 2.4
.

For an arbitrary proper submanifold
N\subseteq M
and a perpendicular unit speed geodesic
\gamma
, the point
\gamma(a)
,
a\neq 0
, is a
focal point
of
N
along
\gamma
if there exists a nontrivial
N
-Jacobi field
J
along
\gamma
such that
J(a)=0
.
M
is said to have
no focal points
if no geodesic
N=\sigma
has focal points along any unit speed geodesic perpendicular to
\sigma
.

Proposition 2.5
.

M
has no focal points if and only if the following condition is satisfied: For any unit speed geodesic
\gamma
in
M
and any Jacobi field
J
on
\gamma
such that
J(0)=0
and
J^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle J(t),J(t)\right\rangle>0

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

We follow the proof from
[
O’S74
]
. Since
J(0)=0
, we can write
J(t)=J_{1}(t)+\alpha t\gamma^{\prime}(t)
, where
J_{1}
is a perpendicular Jacobi field with
J_{1}(0)=0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle J(t),J(t)\right\rangle=\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle J_{1}(t),J_{1}(t)\right\rangle+2\alpha^{2}t\;,

so it suffices to show the result for nonzero perpendicular Jacobi fields vanishing at
t=0
.

Let
J
be such a Jacobi field, suppose
M
has no focal points, and suppose there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle J(t),J(t)\right\rangle\rvert_{t=t_{0}}=0
. Then
\left\langle J^{\prime}(t_{0}),J(t_{0})\right\rangle=0
and by picking the smallest
t_{0}
with this property, we can ensure that
J(t_{0})\neq 0
. Let
\sigma
be the geodesic with
\sigma^{\prime}(0)=J(t_{0})
, and let
L=J(t_{0}-t)
, so
L
is Jacobi field along the geodesic
\gamma(t_{0}-t)
. Thus
\left\langle L^{\prime}(0),L(0)\right\rangle=0
, and by the above discussion on focal points,
L
is an
N
-Jacobi field with
N=\sigma
. But
L(t_{0})=0
, so
\gamma(t_{0})
is a focal point of
\sigma
along
\gamma(t_{0}-t)
, a contradiction. Since
\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle J(t),J(t)\right\rangle>0
for sufficiently small
t>0
, this contradiction shows that
\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle J(t),J(t)\right\rangle>0
for all
t>0
.

Conversely, suppose
M
has focal points. Then there exist geodesics
\gamma
and
\sigma
with
\gamma(0)
in
\sigma
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
perpendicular to
\sigma
and a non-trivial
N
-Jacobi field
J
with
N=\sigma
such that
J(t_{0})=0
for some
t_{0}>0
. Then
L(t)=J(t_{0}-t)
is a non-trivial perpendicular Jacobi field with
L(0)=0
. But
\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle L,L\right\rangle\rvert_{t=t_{0}}=-2\left\langle J^{\prime}(0),J(0)\right\rangle=0
since
J
is an
N
-Jacobi field.
∎

This immediately yields the following result, which will be useful later.

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose
M
has no focal points and let
\gamma
be a geodesic. Then for
t\geqslant s\geqslant 0
and any Jacobi field
J
along
\gamma
with
J(0)=0
and
J^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, we have

\left\lVert J(t)\right\rVert\geqslant\left\lVert J(s)\right\rVert\;.

We want to define a natural isomorphism between
T_{v}(TM)
and
J(\gamma_{v})
for any complete
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold
M
and any
v\in TM
. For each
v\in TM
,
d\pi:T_{v}(TM)\to T_{\pi v}M
is linear and the kernel of
d\pi
is the
n
-dimensional
vertical subspace
of
T_{v}(TM)
. We define a
connection map

K:T(TM)\to TM
such that for each
v\in TM
,
K:T_{v}(TM)\to T_{\pi v}M
is linear. The kernel of
K
is the
n
-dimensional
horizontal subspace
of
T_{v}(TM)
, and the intersection of the horizontal and vertical subspaces is the zero vector.

Given a vector
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
, let
Z:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to TM
be a
C^{\infty}
curve with initial velocity
\xi
, and let
\alpha=\pi\circ Z:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M
. We define
K(\xi)=Z^{\prime}(0)\in T_{\pi v}M
, where
Z^{\prime}(0)
is the covariant derivative of
Z
along
\alpha
evaluated at
t=0
. By passing to a local coordinate system about
v
, we can show that
K(\xi)
does not depend on the chosen curve
Z
. Note that for
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
, we can choose the curve
Z
to lie in
SM
.

We define a natural metric on
TM
called the
Sasaki metric
, which has the property that the horizontal and vertical subspaces of
T_{v}(TM)
are orthogonal. Given vectors
\xi,\eta\in T_{v}(TM)
, we define

\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle_{v}=\left\langle d\pi\xi,d\pi\eta\right\rangle_{\pi v}+\left\langle K\xi,K\eta\right\rangle_{\pi v}\;.

We can also define this metric on
SM
in the same way, since
d\pi|_{T(SM)}
and
K|_{T(SM)}
both map
T(SM)
to
SM
. From now on, this will be the metric we use whenever dealing with
TM
or
SM
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
p:N\to M
be a surjective local isometry of complete Riemannian manifolds. Then

(1)

P=dp:TN\to TM
is a surjective local isometry carrying
SN
onto
SM
,

(2)

\left\lVert dg^{t}\xi\right\rVert=\left\lVert dg^{t}dP(\xi)\right\rVert
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and any
\xi\in T(TN)
, where
g^{t}
denotes the geodesic flow on both
TN
and
TM
, and

(3)

dPY(v)=Y(Pv)
for any
v\in TN
, where
Y
denotes the vector field defined by
g^{t}
in both
TN
and
TM
.

Proof.

Define the projection maps
\pi_{1}:TN\to N
,
\pi_{2}:TM\to M
, and the connection maps
K_{1}:T(TN)\to TN
and
K_{2}:T(TM)\to TM
. We have

(i)

p\circ\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}\circ P

(ii)

P\circ d\pi_{1}=d\pi_{2}\circ dp

(iii)

P\circ K_{1}=K_{2}\circ dP
, and

(iv)

P\circ g^{t}=g^{t}\circ P
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

which can be verified from the definitions and by passing to local coordinates. Relations (ii) and (iii) show that
P
is a local isometry, surjectivity of
p
guarantees surjectivity of
P
, and the fact that
P(SN)=SM
follows from the fact that
p
is a local isometry. Assertion (2) follows from (iv) and (1), since
\left\lVert dg^{t}dP(\xi)\right\rVert=\left\lVert dP\circ dg^{t}(\xi)\right\rVert=\left\lVert dg^{t}\xi\right\rVert
. Assertion (3) follows from (iv) and working with the explicit definition of
Y(v)
defined after Definition
2.1
.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

For any
v\in TM
and any
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
, let
J_{\xi}
be the unique Jacobi field on
\gamma_{v}
such that
J_{\xi}(0)=d\pi\xi
and
J_{\xi}^{\prime}(0)=K\xi
.

We recall Lemma 3.4 from
[
dC92
]
, which is a symmetry result used to prove Gauss’s Lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a (possibly unbounded) open rectangle. Let
r(u,v):A\to M
be differentiable. Then

\frac{D}{\partial t}\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,t)=\frac{D}{\partial s}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(s,t)\;,

where
D/\partial t
denotes covariant differentiation with respect to
t
along the curve
t\to r(s,t)
for fixed
s
, and
D/\partial s
denotes covariant differentiation with respect to
s
along the curve
s\to r(s,t)
for fixed
t
.

Let
v\in SM
and let
r(s,t)=\exp_{\pi Z(t)}(sZ(t))=(\pi\circ g^{s})(Z(t))
be the variation of
\gamma_{v}
defined in the discussion of focal points above, with the difference that now
Z
is any curve in
TM
with initial velocity
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
. Then for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we claim that

J_{\xi}(s)=\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(s,0)\;.

It suffices to show that
J_{\xi}(0)=\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(0,0)
and
J_{\xi}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{D}{\partial s}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(0,0)
. By direct computation, we have
\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(s,0)=d\pi\circ dg^{s}\xi
, so
\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(0,0)=d\pi\xi=J_{\xi}(0)
. Next, notice that
\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,t)=\gamma_{Z(t)}^{\prime}(s)=g^{s}(Z(t))
, so the preceding lemma gives us that

\frac{D}{\partial s}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(s,t)=\frac{D}{\partial t}\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,t)=\frac{D}{\partial t}g^{s}(Z(t))\;,

which gives us

\frac{D}{\partial s}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(0,0)=Z^{\prime}(0)=K\xi=J_{\xi}^{\prime}(0)\;,

as desired.

If
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
, we may choose the curve
Z
to lie in
SM
; the
u
-parameter curves of the variation are then unit speed geodesics of
M
. We claim that the map
s\to\left\langle J_{\xi}(s),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle
is a constant function if and only if
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
. Indeed, consider

\frac{d}{ds}\left\langle J_{\xi}(s),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle=\left\langle J_{\xi}^{\prime}(s),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle+\left\langle J_{\xi}(s),\frac{D\gamma_{v}^{\prime}}{ds}(s)\right\rangle=\left\langle J_{\xi}^{\prime}(s),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle

since
\gamma_{v}
is a geodesic. We have

\displaystyle\left\langle J_{\xi}^{\prime}(s),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{D}{\partial s}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}(s,0),\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{D}{\partial t}\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,0),\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left.\left\langle\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,t),\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}(s,t)\right\rangle\right\rvert_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left.\left\langle\gamma_{Z(t)}^{\prime}(s),\gamma_{Z(t)}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle\right\rvert_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left.(\left\lVert Z(t)\right\rVert^{2})\right\rvert_{t=0}\;,

which is zero if and only if we can choose
Z(t)
to be a curve in
SM
, which is true if and only if
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
. The following result is now straightforward to verify. For (5) we also need the result that
KY(v)=0
and
d\pi Y(v)=v
for all
v\in TM
, which follows from the definitions.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
v\in TM
. Then

(1)

\xi\to J_{\xi}
is a linear isomorphism of
T_{v}(TM)
onto
J(\gamma_{v})
,

(2)

J_{\xi}(t)=d\pi\circ dg^{t}(\xi)
and
J_{\xi}^{\prime}(t)=K\circ dg^{t}(\xi)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3)

\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
lies in
T_{v}(SM)
for
v\in SM
if and only if
\left\langle K\circ dg^{t}(\xi),g^{t}v\right\rangle=\left\langle J_{\xi}^{\prime}(t),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(t)\right\rangle=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, if and only if
t\to\left\langle J_{\xi}(t),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(t)\right\rangle
is a constant function,

(4)

\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0
for
v\in SM
and
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
if and only if
\left\langle J_{\xi}(t),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(t)\right\rangle=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
Y
is the flow vector field, and

(5)

\left\langle dg^{t}\xi,Y(dg^{t}v)\right\rangle=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, if
v\in SM
and
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
satisfy
\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0
.

Remark 2.11
.

It follows from (2) above that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, any
v\in TM
and any
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
that
\left\lVert dg^{t}\xi\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert J_{\xi}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert J_{\xi}^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert^{2}
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\gamma_{n}
be a sequence of geodesics in
M
, and let
J_{n}
be a sequence of Jacobi fields such that
J_{n}
is defined on
\gamma_{n}
for every
n
. If
v_{n}=\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)
, choose
\xi_{n}\in T_{v_{n}}(TM)
so that
J_{n}=J_{\xi_{n}}
. We say that the Jacobi fields
J_{n}
converge to a Jacobi field
J
on a geodesic
\gamma
if
\xi_{n}\to\xi
in
T(TM)
, where
v=\gamma^{\prime}(0)
,
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
, and
J=J_{\xi}
.

Note that
\xi_{n}\to\xi
in
T(TM)
if and only if
K\xi_{n}\to K\xi
and
d\pi\xi_{n}\to d\pi\xi
. Therefore
J_{n}
converges to
J
if and only if
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)\to\gamma^{\prime}(0)
,
J_{n}(0)\to J(0)
, and
J_{n}^{\prime}(0)\to J^{\prime}(0)
. If
J_{n}\to J
and
(u_{n})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a sequence converging to a finite number
u
, then by (2) of Proposition
2.10
,
J_{n}(u_{n})\to J(u)
and
J_{n}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to J^{\prime}(u)
.

Definition 2.13
.

M
is said to be
compactly homogenous
if there exists a compact set
B\subseteq M
such that
M
is the union of all translates of
B
by the isometries of
M
.

If
M
is homogenous or a Galois Riemannian covering of a compact Riemannian manifold, then
M
is compactly homogenous. Since isometries preserve sectional curvature, all values of the sectional curvature of
M
(or Gaussian curvature if
M
is a surface) are taken at points of
B
, and therefore the sectional curvature of
M
is uniformly bounded above and below.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
M
be compactly homogenous. For each integer
n>0
, let
J_{n}
be a Jacobi field on a geodesic
\gamma_{n}
with initial velocity
v_{n}
. If each of the sequences
\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert
,
\left\lVert J_{n}(0)\right\rVert
, and
\left\lVert J_{n}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert
is uniformly bounded above, then we can find a sequence
\varphi_{n}
of isometries of
M
and a Jacobi field
L
on a geodesic
\sigma
, such that by passing to a subsequence,
L_{n}=d\varphi_{n}J_{n}\to L
in the sense of Definition
2.12
.

Proof.

Let
p_{n}=\pi v_{n}
and choose a sequence
\varphi_{n}
of isometries of
M
so that the sequence
q_{n}=\varphi_{n}p_{n}
is contained in a compact subset of
M
.
L_{n}=d\varphi_{n}J_{n}
is a Jacobi field on
\sigma_{n}=\varphi_{n}\circ\gamma_{n}
, and
L_{n}^{\prime}(0)=d\varphi_{n}J_{n}^{\prime}(0)
. If
w_{n}=\sigma_{n}^{\prime}(0)=d\varphi_{n}v_{n}
, then the sequences
\left\lVert w_{n}\right\rVert
,
\left\lVert L_{n}(0)\right\rVert
, and
\left\lVert L_{n}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert
are uniformly bounded above, since
d\varphi_{n}
is an isometry. Passing to a subsequence, let
q_{n}\to q
in
M
and let
U
be a local coordinate system about
q
. For each
n
, the three vectors
w_{n}
,
L_{n}(0)
, and
L_{n}^{\prime}(0)
lie in
T_{q_{n}}M
, and relative to the induced coordinate system
\pi^{-1}(U)
in
TM
, it is easy to see that the coordinates of each of the sequences
w_{n}
,
L_{n}(0)
, and
L_{n}^{\prime}(0)
are uniformly bounded in absolute value, so they are contained in a compact subset of
TM
. Passing to a further subsequence, let
w_{n}\to w
,
L_{n}(0)\to u
, and
L_{n}^{\prime}(0)\to v
, where
u
,
v
, and
w
are vectors in
T_{q}M
. Let
\sigma=\gamma_{w}
, and let
L
be the Jacobi field on
\sigma
such that
L(0)=u
and
L^{\prime}(0)=v
. Then
L_{n}\to L
by the discussion following Definition
2.12
.
∎

If
\varphi
is an isometry of
M
, then
T_{\varphi}=d\varphi
is an isometry of
TM
, which leaves
SM
invariant. The following result is equivalent to that just proved.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
M
be compactly homogenous. Let
(v_{n})\subseteq TM
and
(\xi_{n})\subseteq T(TM)
be sequences such that
\xi_{n}\in T_{v_{n}}(TM)
for every
n
, and that the sequences
\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert\xi_{n}\right\rVert
are each uniformly bounded above. Then there exists a sequence
\varphi_{n}
of isometries of

such that by passing to a subsequence,
\xi_{n}^{*}=dT_{\varphi_{n}}\xi_{n}
converges to a vector

.

3.
Surfaces without conjugate points

From here on, assume
M
is a
surface
. In this section we continue building our framework in order to prove the main result in §4, and we focus on the case where
M
has no conjugate points. In many cases, we consider the unit tangent bundle
SM
instead of
TM
. Assume all geodesics are unit speed. The main goal of this section is to define the
1
-dimensional subspaces
X_{s}(v)
and
X_{u}(v)
of
T_{v}(SM)
, which, as we will eventually show, coincide precisely with the subspaces
X_{s}^{*}(v)
and
X_{u}^{*}(v)
in the definition of an Anosov flow when certain conditions are met. We will also state and prove several propositions relevant to these subspaces that will be useful in the final section.

We start by defining a useful method of working with the tangent space along a geodesic. Let
\gamma
be a geodesic, and let
E_{1}(s)
,
E_{2}(s)
be a parallel orthonormal frame along
\gamma
with
E_{2}(s)=\gamma^{\prime}(s)
; indeed,
E_{1}(s)
can be defined by parallel transport. Then a perpendicular vector field
J
along
\gamma
can be written as
J(s)=y(s)E_{1}(s)
, and we can identify
J
with the “curve“
s\longmapsto y(s)
in
\mathbb{R}
. Note that
J^{\prime}(s)=y^{\prime}(s)E_{1}(s)
. We call such a frame an
adapted frame field
.

Using an adapted frame field along
\gamma
, consider the following Jacobi equation in local coordinates

f^{\prime\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)f(s)=0\;,

where
\kappa(s)
is the Gaussian curvature. Then the solutions
f(s)
to this equation correspond precisely to the perpendicular Jacobi fields
J(s)=f(s)E_{1}(s)
along
\gamma
.

Next, recall the following useful definition from differential equations.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f(s)
and
g(s)
be differentiable. Then the
Wronskian

W(f,g)(s)
is given by

W(f,g)(s)=f^{\prime}(s)g(s)-f(s)g^{\prime}(s)\;.

We have that for any two solutions
x,y
of (J), the Wronskian is constant, for

\displaystyle W(f,g)^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime\prime}(s)g(s)-f(s)g^{\prime\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=-\kappa(s)f(s)g(s)+\kappa(s)f(s)g(s)

\displaystyle=0\;.

Now we define some Jacobi fields that will be helpful soon. Let
a(s)
be the unique solution to (J) such that

a(0)=0\text{ and }a^{\prime}(0)=1\;.

Since
M
has no conjugate points,
a(s)\neq 0
for all
s\neq 0
. In particular,
a(s)<0
for
s<0
and
a(s)>0
for
s>0
by the above conditions. Next, for
t>0
, let
d_{t}(s)
be the unique solution to (J) such that

d_{t}(0)=1\text{ and }d_{t}(t)=0\;.

In fact, for
s>0
,
d_{t}(s)
is given by

d_{t}(s)=a(s)\int_{s}^{t}a(u)^{-2}du\;,

where the superscript denotes exponentiation, not function composition and inversion. To see this, let
b_{t}(s)
be the integral expression above for
s>0
. Then direct computation shows that
b_{t}(s)
satisfies (J) and that
b_{t}(t)=0
and
b_{t}^{\prime}(t)=-1/a(t)
. Thus for
s>0
,
b_{t}(s)
is the unique solution of (J) such that
b_{t}(t)=0
and
b_{t}^{\prime}(t)=-1/a(t)
. Extend
b_{t}(s)
to all
s\in\mathbb{R}
using the existence and uniqueness theorem on (J). It is left to show that
b_{t}(0)=1
, for then again by the existence and uniqueness theorem, we shall have
b_{t}(s)\equiv d_{t}(s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, we have
W(a,b_{t})(t)=-a(t)b_{t}^{\prime}(t)=1
, and since the Wronskian is constant,

1=W(a,b_{t})(0)=b_{t}(0)\;,

as desired.

Next, for
0<r<t
and all
s>0
, we have

d_{t}(s)-d_{r}(s)=a(s)\int_{r}^{t}a(u)^{-2}du\;.

Notice that since
a(s)>0
for
s>0
,
d_{t}(s)
is strictly increasing in
t
for fixed
s>0
. By direct computation,

d_{t}^{\prime}(0)-d_{r}^{\prime}(0)=\int_{r}^{t}a(u)^{-2}du\;,

which is positive and strictly increasing in
t
. We are now ready for the following result, which will be useful for defining
X_{s}(v)
and
X_{u}(v)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

With the definitions from above, we have

(1)

\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{t}^{\prime}(0)
exists.

(2)

Let
d(s)
be the solution of (J) such that

d(0)=1\text{ and }d^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{t}^{\prime}(0)\;.

Then
d(s)=\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{t}(s)
.

(3)

d(s)\neq 0
for all
s
.

Proof.

This proof is an adaptation of Lemma 1 in
[
Gre58
]
. For (1), it suffices to show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{t}^{\prime}(0)-d_{r}^{\prime}(0)
exists for fixed
r
with
0<r<t
. Given
q>0
, we have by direct computation that the solution
d_{-q}
of (J) for which
d_{-q}(0)=1
and
d_{-q}(-q)=0
can be given by

d_{-q}(s)=a(s)n_{t,-q}+d_{t}(s)

for any
t>0
, where

n_{t,-q}=-a(-q)^{-1}d_{t}(-q)\;.

Notice that the choice of
t
does not matter by uniqueness of
d_{-q}
. Next, we claim that
n_{t,-q}
is positive. To see this, we have
a(s)<0
when
s<0
, as previously mentioned. Next, since
d_{t}(0)=1
and
d_{t}(t)=0
, the fact that
M
has no conjugate points implies
d_{t}(s)>0
for
s<t
, so
d_{t}(-q)>0
. By direct computation, we have

d_{-1}^{\prime}(0)-d_{t}^{\prime}(0)=n_{t,-1}>0

for any
t>0
. Thus
d_{t}^{\prime}(0)-d_{r}^{\prime}(0)
is increasing in
t
and bounded above by
d_{-1}^{\prime}(0)-d_{r}^{\prime}(0)
, and hence the desired limit exists. The fact that
d(s)=\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{t}(s)
follows from the fact that the solutions of (J) depend continuously on the initial conditions.

Finally, we show that
d(s)\neq 0
for all
s
. Assume toward contradiction that
d(t_{0})=0
for some
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
d(0)=1
by definition, we have that
t_{0}\neq 0
, and by uniqueness of the solutions of (J), we have
d(s)\equiv d_{t_{0}}(s)
. Since
d_{t}(s)
is strictly increasing in
t
for
t>0
for fixed
s>0
, we have
d_{t_{0}}(s)>d_{t}(s)
for
t>0
and
s>0
, which means
t_{0}
cannot be positive. Thus
t_{0}=-q_{0}
for some
q_{0}>0
, so
d(s)\equiv d_{-q_{0}}(s)
. Notice that since
d_{-q}^{\prime}(0)=n_{t,-q}+d_{t}^{\prime}(0)
, we have
d_{-q}^{\prime}(0)
is continuous in
q
for
q>0
. Let
U
be a positive interval containing
q_{0}
. By the existence and uniqueness theorem, we have that the map
q\longmapsto d_{-q}^{\prime}(0)
sends
U
homeomorphically onto an interval containing
d_{-q}^{\prime}(0)
. Since
\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{t}^{\prime}(0)=d^{\prime}(0)=d_{-q_{0}}^{\prime}(0)
, this implies that
d_{t}^{\prime}(0)=d_{-q}^{\prime}(0)
for some
t>0
and
q>0
, a contradiction by the existence and uniqueness theorem. Thus
d(s)
is nonvanishing.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

For
s>0
,
d(s)
can be given by

d(s)=a(s)\int_{s}^{\infty}a(u)^{-2}du\;.

Corollary 3.4
.

Again with the definitions from above, we have

(1)

\lim_{t\to-\infty}d_{t}^{\prime}(0)
exists.

(2)

Let
\overline{d}(s)
be the solution of (J) such that

\overline{d}(0)=1\text{ and }\overline{d}^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{t\to-\infty}d_{t}^{\prime}(0)\;.

Then
\overline{d}(s)=\lim_{t\to-\infty}d_{t}(s)
.

(3)

\overline{d}(s)\neq 0
for all
s
.

Proof.

Apply the results from the preceding proposition to the reverse geodesic
\gamma(-s)
.
∎

Next, for any number
t\neq 0
, we define a linear map
\xi\to\xi_{t}:T_{v}(TM)\to T_{v}(TM)
for every
v\in TM
. Given a vector
v\in TM
and a vector
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
, let
J(s)
be the unique Jacobi field along
\gamma_{v}
such that

J(0)=d\pi(\xi)\text{ and }J(t)=0\;.

Let
\xi_{t}\in T_{v}(TM)
be the vector that corresponds to
J
under our isomorphism, i.e., we have
J=J_{\xi_{t}}
. Thus
\xi_{t}
is the unique vector such that

d\pi(\xi_{t})=J_{\xi_{t}}(0)=d\pi(\xi)\text{ and }d\pi\circ dg^{t}(\xi_{t})=J_{\xi_{t}}(t)=0\;.

The kernel of this map is
\ker d\pi
, which is vertical subspace of
T_{v}(TM)
.

For
v\in SM
it is not true in general that this map leaves
T_{v}(SM)
invariant; in fact, for
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
we have
\xi_{t}\in T_{v}(SM)
if and only if
\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0
. Indeed, if
\xi_{t}\in T_{v}(SM)
, then
s\to\left\langle J_{\xi_{t}}(s),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle
is a constant function by Proposition
2.10
(3), but since
J_{\xi_{t}}(t)=0
, we have
\left\langle J_{\xi_{t}}(s),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(s)\right\rangle\equiv 0
, and by Proposition
2.10
(5),
\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0
. Conversely, suppose
\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0
. Since
KY(v)=0
and
d\pi Y(v)=v
, we have

0=\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=\left\langle d\pi(\xi),v\right\rangle=\left\langle d\pi(\xi_{t}),v\right\rangle=\left\langle J_{\xi_{t}}(0),\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(0)\right\rangle\;.

Since
J_{\xi_{t}}(t)=0
, we also have that it is perpendicular at
t
, and hence perpendicular everywhere. Thus again by Proposition
2.10
(3),
\xi_{t}\in T_{v}(SM)
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
v\in SM
. We define

(i)

X_{s}(v)=\left\{\xi\in T_{v}(SM)\text{ such that }\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0\text{ and }\xi_{t}\to\xi\text{ as }t\to\infty\right\}
, and

(ii)

X_{u}(v)=\left\{\xi\in T_{v}(SM)\text{ such that }\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0\text{ and }\xi_{t}\to\xi\text{ as }t\to-\infty\right\}
.

These are called the
stable
and
unstable subspaces
determined by
v
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\gamma
be a unit speed geodesic in
M
with initial velocity
v
. We define
J_{s}(\gamma)
and
J_{u}(\gamma)
to be the images in
J(\gamma)
of
X_{s}(v)
and
X_{u}(v)
, respectively, under our isomorphism. These are called the
stable
and
unstable subspaces
of perpendicular Jacobi fields along
\gamma
.

Remark 3.7
.

(1) Let
\xi\in X_{s}(v)
. Since
J_{\xi_{t}}
is perpendicular, we have that relative to an adapted frame field
E_{1},E_{2}
along
\gamma
with
E_{2}(s)=\gamma^{\prime}(s)
,
J_{\xi_{t}}
can be given by

J_{\xi_{t}}(s)=cd_{t}(s)E_{1}(s)

for an appropriately chosen
c\in\mathbb{R}
so that
d\pi\xi=cd_{t}(0)E_{1}(0)=cE_{1}(0)
. Notice that
c
does not depend on
t
. Taking
t\to\infty
shows that

J_{\xi}(s)=cd(s)E_{1}(s)\;.

For
\xi\in X_{u}(v)
, we have by similar reasoning that
J_{\xi}(s)=c\overline{d}(s)E_{1}(s)
. Therefore, we can express
J_{s}(\gamma)
and
J_{u}(\gamma)
as

(i)

J_{s}(\gamma)=\left\{cd(s)E_{1}(s):c\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
and

(ii)

J_{u}(\gamma)=\left\{c\overline{d}(s)E_{1}(s):c\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
.

Clearly these are 1-dimensional vector subspaces of
J(\gamma)
, which means
X_{s}(v)
and
X_{u}(v)
are1-dimensional vector subspaces of
T_{v}(SM)
.

(2) Let
\gamma
be a unit speed geodesic in
M
. If
\kappa\equiv 0
, then
J_{s}(\gamma)=J_{u}(\gamma)=
the vector space of all perpendicular parallel vector fields on
\gamma
. It will follow by Theorem
4.2
that for a compact surface
M
without conjugate points the geodesic flow on
SM
is of Anosov type if and only if
J_{s}(\gamma)\cap J_{u}(\gamma)=\left\{0\right\}
for every unit speed geodesic
\gamma
in
M
.

Proposition 3.8
.

If
p:N\to M
is a surjective local isometry of complete Riemannian manifolds and
M
has no conjugate points, then
N
has no conjugate points, and

dPX_{s}(v)=X_{s}(Pv)\text{ and }dPX_{u}(v)=X_{u}(Pv)

for every
v\in SN
, where
P=dp:TN\to TM
.

Proof.

N
has no conjugate points since for any Jacobi field
J
on a geodesic
\gamma
in
N
,
PJ
is a Jacobi field on the geodesic
p\circ\gamma
in
M
. If
\pi_{1}:TN\to N
and
\pi_{2}:TM\to M
are the projection maps, then
p\circ\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}\circ P
. We also have
P\circ g^{t}=g^{t}\circ P
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
g^{t}
is the geodesic flow on both
TN
and
TM
. From these two relations it follows that
(dP\xi)_{t}=dP(\xi_{t})
for any
t\neq 0
, any
v\in TN
, and any
\xi\in T_{v}(TN)
. If
\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0
, then
\left\langle dP\xi,Y(Pv)\right\rangle=0
by Proposition
2.7
. Therefore
dPX_{s}(v)=X_{s}(Pv)
and
dPX_{u}(v)=X_{u}(Pv)
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

We have

(1)

If
S:SM\to SM
is the map which takes a vector
v
to
-v
, then
X_{u}(-v)=dSX_{s}(v)
and
X_{s}(-v)=dSX_{u}(v)
, and

(2)

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and any
v\in SM
,
dg^{t}X_{s}(v)=X_{s}(g^{t}v)
and
dg^{t}X_{u}(v)=X_{u}(g^{t}v)
.

Proof of (1).

Let
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
and
t\neq 0
. It suffices to show that
dS(\xi_{t})=(dS\xi)_{-t}
. By definition of
(dS\xi)_{-t}
, it suffices to show that
d\pi\circ dS(\xi_{t})=d\pi\circ dS(\xi)
and
d\pi\circ dg^{-t}\circ dS(\xi_{t})=0
. The first condition follows from the fact that
\pi\circ S=\pi
. The second condition also requires the fact that
S\circ g^{a}=g^{-a}\circ S
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, which we have by

S\circ g^{a}(v)=S(\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(a))=-\gamma_{v}^{\prime}(a)=\gamma_{-v}^{\prime}(-a)=g^{-a}(-v)=g^{-a}\circ S(v)\;.

∎

Before we prove (2), we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
v\in SM
be given. Then there exist numbers
b=b(v)>0
and
t_{0}=t_{0}(v)>0
such that if
\xi\in T_{v}(TM)
satisfies
d\pi\circ dg^{t}(\xi)=0
for some
t\geqslant t_{0}
, then
\left\lVert K\xi\right\rVert\leqslant b\left\lVert d\pi\xi\right\rVert
.

Proof.

We may assume that
d\pi\xi\neq 0
, or otherwise the Jacobi field
J_{\xi}\equiv 0
since it vanishes at
0
and
t\geqslant t_{0}>0
, and
K\xi=J_{\xi}^{\prime}(0)=0
. First, we consider the case when
J_{\xi}
is perpendicular to
\gamma_{v}
. Relative to an adapted frame field along
\gamma_{v}
, if
d\pi\circ dg^{t}(\xi)=0
for some
t>0
, we have
J_{\xi}=cd_{t}(s)E_{1}(s)
, with
c
chosen so that
cd_{t}(0)E_{1}(0)=cE_{1}(0)=d\pi\xi
. Thus we have

\left\lVert K\xi\right\rVert=\left\lVert J_{\xi}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert=\left\lvert d_{t}^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert\left\lvert c\right\rvert=\left\lvert d_{t}^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert\left\lVert d\pi\xi\right\rVert\;.

Since
d_{t}^{\prime}(0)\to d^{\prime}(0)
as
t\to\infty
, we can choose
t_{0}=t_{0}(v)>1
so that
\left\lvert d_{t}^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert\leqslant 1+\left\lvert d^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert
for
t\geqslant t_{0}
. Setting
b=1+\left\lvert d^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert
, if
d\pi\circ dg^{t}(\xi)=0
for some
t\geqslant t_{0}
, then
\left\lVert K\xi\right\rVert\leqslant b\left\lVert d\pi\xi\right\rVert
.

If
J
is an arbitrary Jacobi field on
\gamma
, we may write
J(s)=J_{1}(s)+J_{2}(s)
, where
J_{1}
is a perpendicular Jacobi field and
J_{2}
is a tangential Jacobi field of the form
J_{2}(s)=(\alpha s+\beta)\gamma^{\prime}(s)
for suitable constants
\alpha
and
\beta
. If
J(t)=0
for some
t\geqslant t_{0}
, then
J_{1}(t)=J_{2}(t)=0
, and hence
\alpha=-\beta/t
. Since
t\geqslant t_{0}>1
, we have
\left\lVert J_{2}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert=\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert=\left\lVert J_{2}(0)\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert J_{1}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert\leqslant b\left\lVert J_{1}(0)\right\rVert
. Since
b\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert K\xi\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert J^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert J_{1}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert J_{2}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant b^{2}\left\lVert J_{1}(0)\right\rVert^{2}+b^{2}\left\lVert J_{2}(0)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=b^{2}\left\lVert J(0)\right\rVert^{2}\;,

and the result follows.
∎

Proof of (2).

Let
v\in SM
and let

, where

. Therefore

Proposition 3.11
.

Assume the Gaussian curvature
\kappa
of
M
is bounded from below, so
\kappa>-k^{2}
for some
k>0
, and let
J
be a perpendicular Jacobi field on a unit speed geodesic

in

. Then
\left\lVert J^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert\leqslant k\coth(ks)\left\lVert J(s)\right\rVert
for every
s>0
.

To show this, we first state and prove two lemmas, the first of which will also be important later.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
r(s)
be a real-valued function defined for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
r(s)\geqslant-k^{2}
for all
s
for some
k>0
. If
u(s)
is a solution of the Ricatti equation

u^{\prime}(s)+u(s)^{2}+r(s)=0\;,

defined everywhere, then
|u(s)|\leqslant k
.

Proof.

This proof is an adaptation of Lemma 2.1 in
[
Gre54
]
. Let
b>k
and suppose there exists
s_{0}
with
u(s_{0})>b
. Then there is a solution
v(s)
of

v^{\prime}(s)=b^{2}-v(s)^{2}

for which
v(s_{0})=u(s_{0})
. For a suitably chosen constant
d
, we have
v(s)=b\coth(bs-d)
. Subtracting the above by (R), we have

v^{\prime}(s)-u^{\prime}(s)=b^{2}+r(s)-v(s)^{2}+u(s)^{2}\;.

For
s=s_{0}
, we have
v^{\prime}(s_{0})-u^{\prime}(s_{0})=b^{2}+r(s)>0
, so

v(s)\leqslant u(s)

in at least a left-sided neighborhood
s_{0}-\varepsilon\leqslant s\leqslant s_{0}
of
s_{0}
. This, however, implies that the inequality holds for
s\leqslant s_{0}
, since for any
s_{1}
where
v(s_{1})=u(s_{1})
, we have that
v^{\prime}(s_{1})-u^{\prime}(s_{1})>0
, and so
v(s)\leqslant u(s)
for a left-sided neighborhood of
s_{1}
. Now if we observe the behavior of
v(s)
, we notice that
\lim_{s\to d/b^{+}}v(s)=\infty
, so
\lim_{s\to d/b^{+}}u(s)=\infty
, but this contradicts the fact that
u(s)
is defined everywhere. Since
b
can be taken arbitrarily close to
k
, we have
u(s)\leqslant k
.

For the lower bound, suppose there exists
s_{0}
with
u(s_{0})<-b
. We then consider
w(s)-u(s)
, where
w(s)=b\coth(bs-c)
with
c
chosen such that
w(s_{0})=u(s_{0})
. Using arguments similar to the above, we have
w^{\prime}(s_{0})-u^{\prime}(s_{0})>0
, so
w(s)\geqslant u(s)
for a right-sided neighborhood
s_{0}\leqslant s\leqslant s_{0}+\varepsilon
of
s_{0}
, and hence for all
s\geqslant s_{0}
. Since
\lim_{s\to c/b^{-}}v(s)=-\infty
, we also have
\lim_{s\to c/b^{-}}u(s)=-\infty
, which again contradicts the fact that
u(s)
is defined everywhere. Thus
u(s)\geqslant-k
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

If
u(s)
is a solution of the Ricatti equation (R) defined for all
s>0
, then
\left\lvert u(s)\right\rvert\leqslant k\coth(ks)
for all
s>0
.

Proof.

The first part of this proof is an adaptation of Lemma 3 in
[
Gre58
]
. For fixed
s_{0}>0
, choose a number
d>0
such that
u(s_{0})<k\coth(ks_{0}-d)
. We show that
u(s)<k\coth(ks-d)
for
s\geqslant s_{0}
. We have
v(s)=k\coth(ks-d)
is a solution to the equation

v^{\prime}(s)+v(s)^{2}-k^{2}=0\;,

defined for
s>d/k>0
. If
f(s)=u(s)-v(s)
, then
f(s_{0})<0
. If
f(s)=0
for some
s>s_{0}
, let
s_{1}
be the first such value. Differentiating at
s_{1}
, we obtain

f^{\prime}(s_{1})=v(s_{1})^{2}-u(s_{1})^{2}-r(s_{1})-k^{2}=-r(s_{1})-k^{2}<0\;.

This means that
f(s)>0
in some left-sided neighborhood
s_{1}-\varepsilon<s<s_{1}
of
s_{1}
, but since
s_{0}<s_{1}
and
f(s_{0})<0
, this contradicts the fact that
s_{1}
is the first point after
s_{0}
for which
f(s)=0
. Thus
u(s)<k\coth(ks-d)
for all
s\geqslant s_{0}
.

Next, for each integer
n>0
, choose a number
d_{n}>0
such that
u(1/n)<k\coth(k/n-d_{n})
. If
s>0
is given, then
s>1/n
for sufficiently large
n
, and the above argument shows that
u(s)<k\coth(ks-d_{n})
. Since
d_{n}<k/n
,
d_{n}\to 0
and we have
u(s)\leqslant k\coth(ks)
. Finally, the argument in the last paragraph of the proof of Lemma
3.12
works if
u(s)
is defined only for
s>0
; we assume
s_{0}>0
and contradict the fact that
u(s)
is defined for
s>0
, which then gives us that
u(s)\geqslant-k
for any
s>0
, completing the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.11
.

Let
\gamma
be a unit speed geodesic in
M
. Relative to an adapted frame field along
\gamma
, we consider the solution
a(s)
to equation (J) defined earlier in this section. Let

be a perpendicular Jacobi field on

such that

Proposition 3.14
.

Assume the Gaussian curvature
\kappa
of
M
is bounded from below, so
\kappa>-k^{2}
for some
k>0
. Let
\gamma
be a unit speed geodesic in
M
. For any
R>0
we can find a number
T=T(R,\gamma)>0
such that
\left\lVert J(s)\right\rVert\geqslant R\left\lVert J^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert
for
s\geqslant T
, where
J
is any perpendicular Jacobi field on
\gamma
such that
J(0)=0
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case where
\left\lVert J^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert=1
. As before, we have
J(s)=\pm a(s)E_{1}(s)
relative to an adapted frame field
E_{1},E_{2}
along
\gamma
with
E_{2}(s)=\gamma^{\prime}(s)
. Thus we want to show that
\left\lvert a(s)\right\rvert\geqslant R
for
s\geqslant T
.

For any
s>0
, let
m(s)=d^{\prime}(0)-d_{s}^{\prime}(0)=\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\infty}a(u)^{-2}du
, and let
u(s)=a^{\prime}(s)a(s)^{-1}
and
v(s)=d^{\prime}(s)d(s)^{-1}
. Then
u(s)
and
v(s)
are solutions defined for
s>0
of the Ricatti equation
u^{\prime}(s)+u(s)^{2}+\kappa(s)=0
. Since
d(s)=a(s)m(s)
, we have
d^{\prime}(s)=a^{\prime}(s)m(s)+a(s)m^{\prime}(s)=a^{\prime}(s)m(s)-a(s)^{-1}
, and consequently

u(s)-v(s)=a(s)^{-2}m(s)^{-1}\;.

Choosing
s_{0}>0
such that
k\coth ks\leqslant 2k
for
s\geqslant s_{0}
, we have by Lemma
3.13
that

\left\lvert a(s)^{-2}m(s)^{-1}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert u(s)\right\rvert+\left\lvert v(s)\right\rvert\leqslant 4k

for
s\geqslant s_{0}
, or in other words, that

\left\lvert a(s)\right\rvert=1/\left\lvert a(s)^{-1}\right\rvert\geqslant(4k\left\lvert m(s)\right\rvert)^{-1/2}

for
s\geqslant s_{0}
. Let
R>0
be given. Since
m(s)\to 0
as
s\to\infty
, we may choose
T>s_{0}>0
such that
s\geqslant T
implies
\left\lvert m(s)\right\rvert\leqslant 1/(4kR^{2})
, hence giving us
\left\lvert a(s)\right\rvert\geqslant R
for
s\geqslant T
.
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

Assume that
\kappa>-k^{2}
for some
k>0
. Then for any
v\in SM
and any
\xi\in X_{s}(v)
or
X_{u}(v)
we have
\left\lVert K\xi\right\rVert\leqslant k\left\lVert d\pi\xi\right\rVert
.

Proof.

First let
\xi\in X_{s}(v)
. Relative to an adapted frame field along
\gamma
, we have
\xi
corresponds to
J_{\xi}=cd(s)E_{1}(s)
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Further, for
t\neq 0
,
\xi_{t}
corresponds to
J_{\xi_{t}}(s)=cd_{t}(s)E_{1}(s)
. By reversing our geodesic and shifting by
t
, i.e., by considering the geodesic
\gamma(t-s)
instead of
\gamma(s)
, we have the corresponding Jacobi field
L_{\xi_{t}}(s)=J_{\xi_{t}}(t-s)=d_{t}(t-s)E_{1}(t-s)
. In particular, we have
L_{\xi_{t}}(0)=J_{\xi_{t}}(t)=0
, so by Proposition
3.11
, we have

\left\lVert L_{\xi_{t}}^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert\leqslant k\coth(ks)\left\lVert L_{\xi_{t}}(s)\right\rVert

for
s>0
. Setting
s=t
, we have

\left\lVert J_{\xi_{t}}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert=\left\lVert L_{\xi_{t}}^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert\leqslant k\coth(kt)\left\lVert L_{\xi_{t}}(t)\right\rVert=k\coth(kt)\left\lVert J_{\xi_{t}}(0)\right\rVert\;.

By our isomorphism, we have

\left\lVert K\xi_{t}\right\rVert\leqslant k\coth(kt)\left\lVert d\pi(\xi_{t})\right\rVert=k\coth(kt)\left\lVert d\pi\xi\right\rVert\;.

Taking
t\to\infty
yields the desired result. A similar approach shows the case where
\xi\in X_{u}(v)
.
∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Assume that
\kappa>-k^{2}
for some
k>0
. Let
v\in SM
and let
\xi\in T_{v}(SM)
be such that
\left\langle\xi,Y(v)\right\rangle=0
and
\left\lVert d\pi\circ dg^{t}\xi\right\rVert
is bounded above for all
t\geqslant 0
(resp. for all
t\leqslant 0
). Then
\xi\in X_{s}(v)
(resp.
\xi\in X_{u}(v)
).

Proof.

Suppose
\left\lVert d\pi\circ dg^{t}\xi\right\rVert\leqslant A
for some
A>0
. For any
t>0
,
\xi_{t}\in T_{v}(SM)
by Proposition
2.10
(3), since
J_{\xi_{t}}
is perpendicular at 0 and
t
and hence everywhere. Thus
\xi-\xi_{t}\in T_{v}(SM)
. By linearity,
J_{\xi-\xi_{t}}(0)=J_{\xi}(0)-J_{\xi_{t}}(0)=0
and

, which is perpendicular by assumption, so
J_{\xi-\xi_{t}}
is also perpendicular everywhere. By Proposition
3.14
, for each integer

we have

\left\lVert\xi-\xi_{t}\right\rVert=\left\lVert K(\xi-\xi_{t})\right\rVert=\left\lVert J_{\xi-\xi_{t}}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert\leqslant\left\lVert J_{\xi-\xi_{t}}(s)\right\rVert/n

\left\lVert\xi-\xi_{t}\right\rVert\leqslant\left\lVert J_{\xi-\xi_{t}}(t)\right\rVert/n=\left\lVert J_{\xi}(t)\right\rVert/n=\left\lVert d\pi\circ dg^{t}\xi\right\rVert/n\leqslant A/n\;,

so

∎

Proposition 3.17
.

Assume that
\kappa>-k^{2}
for some
k>0

A>0
and
s_{0}\geqslant 0
so that for any perpendicular Jacobi vector field
J

J(0)=0

The reverse direction follows immediately from the preceding result. For the forward direction, let

4.
Anosov equivalences

We are finally able to state and prove the main result of this paper. First we need the definition of an Anosov flow.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi^{t}
be a complete
C^{\infty}
flow on a (complete)
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold
N
of dimension
n\geqslant 3
. Then the flow is said to be
Anosov
or of
Anosov type
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

The vector field
Y=\left.\frac{\partial\varphi^{t}}{\partial t}\right\rvert_{t=0}
defined by the flow never vanishes on
N
.

(2)

For each
n\in N
the tangent space
T_{n}N
splits in to a direct sum

T_{n}N=X_{s}^{*}(n)\oplus X_{u}^{*}(n)\oplus Z(n)

(
\dim X_{s}^{*}=k>0,\dim X_{u}^{*}=l>0,\dim Z=1
), where
Z(n)
is generated by
Y(n)
, i.e.,
Z(n)=\left\{cY(n):c\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
, and there exist positive number
a
,
b
, and
c
such that

(i)

for any
\xi\in X_{s}^{*}(n)
,

\left\lVert d\varphi^{t}\xi\right\rVert\leqslant a\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert e^{-ct}\text{ for }t\geqslant 0\text{ and }\left\lVert d\varphi^{t}\xi\right\rVert\geqslant b\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert e^{-ct}\text{ for }t\leqslant 0\;,\text{ and}

(ii)

for any
\eta\in X_{u}^{*}(n)
,

\left\lVert d\varphi^{t}\eta\right\rVert\leqslant a\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert e^{ct}\text{ for }t\leqslant 0\text{ and }\left\lVert d\varphi^{t}\eta\right\rVert\geqslant b\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert e^{ct}\text{ for }t\geqslant 0\;.

We now state the main theorem. For the rest of this section we assume that
M
is a complete,
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold of dimension two without conjugate points such that the universal cover
H
is compactly homogenous. Let
g^{t}
denote the geodesic flow on
TM
,
SM
,
TH
, and
SH
. Since
M
has the same values for the Gaussian curvature as
H
, we have that
\kappa
is bounded, so there exists a number
k>0
such that
\kappa>-k^{2}
in
M
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M
be a surface without conjugate points such that universal cover
H
of
M
is compactly homogenous. Then the following properties are equivalent:

(1)

The geodesic flow on
SM
is of Anosov type.

(2)

For every
v\in SM
,
X_{s}(v)\cap X_{u}(v)=\left\{0\right\}
.

(3)

For every
v\in SM
,
T_{v}(SM)=X_{s}(v)\oplus X_{u}(v)\oplus Z(v)
, where
Z(v)
is the one-dimensional subspace generated by
Y(v)
.

(4)

There exists no nonzero perpendicular Jacobi field
J
on a unit speed geodesic
\gamma
of
M
such that
\left\lVert J(t)\right\rVert
is bounded for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

If, further,
M
has no focal points, the above conditions are also equivalent to the following:

(5)

There exists no nonzero perpendicular parallel Jacobi field
J
on a unit speed geodesic
\gamma
of
M
.

(6)

Every geodesic of
M
passes through a point of negative Gaussian curvature.

We shall prove this theorem in multiple steps, which will require intermediary results.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
M
admits no nonzero perpendicular Jacobi field
J
on a unit speed geodesic
\gamma
such that
\left\lVert J(t)\right\rVert
is bounded above for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a constant
A>0
such that if
J
is a nonzero perpendicular Jacobi field on a unit speed geodesic
\gamma
such that
J(0)=0
, then
\left\lVert J(t)\right\rVert\geqslant A\left\lVert J(s)\right\rVert
for any numbers
t\geqslant s\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this result for the universal cover
H
of
M
. Indeed, the covering map is a surjective local isometry, so Jacobi fields along a geodesic in
M
are projections of Jacobi fields along geodesics in
H
.

Assume toward contradiction that the proposition is false. Then there exist nonzero perpendicular Jacobi fields
J_{n}
on unit speed geodesics
\gamma_{n}
in
H
, along with sequences
1\leqslant s_{n}\leqslant t_{n}
such that for each integer
n>0
, we have
J_{n}(0)=0
and
\left\lVert J_{n}(t_{n})\right\rVert\leqslant(1/n)\left\lVert J_{n}(s_{n})\right\rVert
. By linearity, we may assume
\left\lVert J_{n}^{\prime}(0)\right\rVert=1
.

By the extreme value theorem, we can choose a sequence
(u_{n})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that
0\leqslant u_{n}\leqslant t_{n}
and
\left\lVert J_{n}(s)\right\rVert\leqslant\left\lVert J_{n}(u_{n})\right\rVert
for every
0\leqslant s\leqslant t_{n}
. We claim that the
u_{n}
are lower bounded by some
\delta>0
. For if this were not the case, then
u_{n}\to 0
by passing to a subsequence. By Proposition
2.14
, there exists a sequence
\varphi_{n}
of isometries of
H
and a Jacobi field
L
on a unit speed geodesic
\gamma
in
H
such that
L_{n}=d\varphi_{n}J_{n}\to L
by passing to a subsequence. By continuity,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert J_{n}(u_{n})\right\rVert=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert L_{n}(u_{n})\right\rVert=\left\lVert L(0)\right\rVert=0\;.

On the other hand, since

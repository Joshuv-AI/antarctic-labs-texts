Three arithmetic sites
Lieven Le Bruyn

Abstract
. Two new arithmetic sites are introduced, based on dynamical Belyi maps and Conway’s big picture, respectively. We relate these to arboreal Galois representations, Bost-Connes data, and the original arithmetic site due to Connes and Consani.



Renewed interest in the presheaf topos of a monoid category (the one object category with given endomorphism monoid) was spurred by the discovery of Connes and Consani, see
[
4
]
, that in the special case of
A=\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
, the multiplicative monoid of strict positive numbers, the points of the so called
arithmetic site
, that is, of the corresponding presheaf topos

\mathbf{points}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}})=\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{f}/\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}

are the finite adèle classes, a horrific topological space, better studied with the tools coming from non-commutative geometry.

In this note we will introduce two more such sites and investigate their interplay with the arithmetic site. The first one,
Belyi’s site

\widehat{\mathbf{B}}
, is constructed from the monoid of dynamical Belyi maps, as in
[
1
]
, and is relevant to the study of the absolute Galois group
Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, whereas the arithmetic site is relevant to the abelianisation
Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})^{ab}
. The second one,
Conway’s site

\widehat{\mathbf{C}}
, is constructed from a monoid structure on a subset of
\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
, classifying projective classes of commensurable integral lattices of rank two, and is relevant to the description of groups appearing in moonshine, see for example
[
5
]
or
[
11
]
.

We will study the points of these sites by constructing suitable localic covers of them. We will construct sheaves on these covers, relevant in the study of
arboreal Galois representations
, as in
[
3
]
(in the
\widehat{\mathbf{B}}
case), and of
Bost-Connes data
, as introduced in
[
13
]
(in the
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}
case). Moreover, these three arithmetic sites are related via a triangle of geometric morphisms

\textstyle{\widehat{\mathbf{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\widehat{\mathbf{A}}}

1.
The arithmetic site and its localic cover

In order to illustrate the strategy we will use later on in studying the Belyi and Conway sites, let us recall our approach to the original arithmetic site
\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
, see
[
9
]
for more details.

With
\mathbf{A}_{L}
we denote the poset category of the poset structure on
\mathbb{N}_{+}^{\times}
determined by reverse division, that is,
\mathbf{A}_{L}
has an object
[k]
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{\times}
and a unique morphism

\textstyle{[k]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{[l]}
whenever
l|k
. The covariant functor
\pi:\mathbf{A}_{L}\rTo\mathbf{A}
sending the morphism
\textstyle{[k]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{[l]}
to the endomorphism
\tfrac{k}{l}
induces a geometric morphism between the presheaf toposes

\pi~:~\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}}\rTo\widehat{\mathbf{A}}

and, as
\mathbf{A}_{L}
is a poset-category,
\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}}
is a
locale
which we call the
localic cover
of
\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
. In
[
9
]
it is shown that the points of
\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}}
are in one-to-one correspondence with subsets
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{+}^{\times}
closed under division and taking least common multiples. The
supernatural numbers

\mathbb{S}
is the multiplicative semigroup consisting of formal products
s=\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{s_{p}}
where
p
runs over all prime numbers
\mathbb{P}
and
s_{p}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, so we have a one-to-one correspondence

\mathbf{points}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}})=\mathbb{S}\qquad S\mapsto s=\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{max(d~:~p^{d}\in S)}

We equip
\mathbb{S}
with the
localic topology
having as its sets of opens the subsets

\mathbb{X}_{l}(\cup_{i}n_{i}\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+})=\{s\in\mathbb{S}~:~\exists i~:~n_{i}|s\}

By
[
10
, Theorem 1]
we know that the locale
\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}}
is equivalent to the topos of sheaves of sets
\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbb{S},loc)
of the topological space
\mathbb{S}
, equipped with the localic topology.

The upshot being that we can associate to every
S\in\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}}
, that is to every contravariant functor
S~:~\mathbf{A}_{L}\rTo\mathbf{Sets}
a sheaf
\mathcal{S}\in\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbb{S},loc)
of which the stalk
\mathcal{S}_{s}
in an infinite supernatural number
s\in\mathbb{S}-\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
extends the functor as
S([n])=\mathcal{S}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times~}_{+}
. Here a couple of examples, see also
[
6
, Remark 2.10 and §4.5]
:

Finite fields:
Fix a prime number
p
and let
S
be the functor

S~:~\mathbf{A}_{L}\rTo\mathbf{Sets}\qquad\begin{cases}[n]\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\\
\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 8.77896pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-8.77896pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{[n]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 32.77896pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 32.77896pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{[m]}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rInto\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\end{cases}

for the canonical embedding of fields obtained from taking invariants under the Frobenius. The stalk of the coresponding sheaf
\mathcal{S}
at a supernatural number
s\in\mathbb{S}-\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
is then the (infinite) algebraic extension

\mathcal{S}_{s}=\cup_{n|s}\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

It is well known that algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
correspond up to isomorphism to supernatural numbers in this way.

UHF-algebras:
Let
S
be the functor

S~:~\mathbf{A}_{L}\rTo\mathbf{Sets}\qquad\begin{cases}[n]\mapsto M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\\
\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 8.77896pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-8.77896pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{[n]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 32.77896pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 32.77896pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{[m]}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\mapsto M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\rInto M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\end{cases}

UHF-algebras are closures of chains of matrix algebras

M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\rInto M_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\rInto\ldots

with
n_{1}|n_{2}|\ldots
, and are known to be classified up to isomorphism by supernatural numbers, which in our set-up corresponds to taking the stalk
\mathcal{S}_{s}
of the corresponding sheaf
\mathcal{S}\in\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbb{S},loc)
.

The geometric morphism
\pi:\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}}\rTo\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
induces a surjection on the level of points

\mathbb{S}=\mathbf{points}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}})\rOnto\mathbf{points}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}})=[\mathbb{S}]

by modding out all finite supernaturals, that is,
[\mathbb{S}]
is the set of classes for the equivalence relation

s\sim s^{\prime}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\exists n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times~}_{+}~:~n.s=m.s^{\prime}

and it is easy to verify that indeed
[\mathbb{S}]
coincides with the set of finite adèle classes
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{f}/\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
. This equivalence relation implies that the points of the arithmetic site correspond to
stable isomorphism classes
for the objects classified by a functor
S\in\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{L}}
. For example, two algebraic extentions of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(resp. two UHF-algebras)
\mathcal{S}_{s}
and
\mathcal{S}_{s^{\prime}}
are stably isomorphic if there exist
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
such that

\mathcal{S}_{s}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{s^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\qquad(resp.\quad\mathcal{S}_{s}\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathcal{S}_{s^{\prime}}\otimes M_{m}(\mathbb{C})~).

It is known that stable isomorphism classes of UHF-algebras are its Morita equivalence classes. As noncommutative spaces are Morita equivalence classes of
C^{*}
-algebras, we can view (the points of) the arithmetic site as the
moduli space
of the noncommutative spaces corresponding to UHF-algebras.

2.
Conway’s site and its localic cover

In
[
5
]
John H. Conway introduced his
big picture
which is a graph with vertices the left coset classes
\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
, where
PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
is the group of all elements in
PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
with strictly positive determinant, and
\Gamma
is the modular group
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. We can identify the set
\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
with
\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
via the assignment

\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\rTo\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})\qquad X=(M,\frac{g}{h})\mapsto\Gamma.\begin{bmatrix}M&\frac{g}{h}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=\Gamma.\alpha_{X}

For two elements
X,Y\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
let
d_{XY}
be the smallest positive rational number such that
d_{XY}.\alpha_{X}\alpha_{Y}^{-1}\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The
hyper-distance
between the two elements is then

\delta(X,Y)=det(d_{XY}.\alpha_{X}\alpha_{Y}^{-1})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

Conway showed in
[
5
, p. 329]
that this definition is symmetric and that the
log
of the hyper-distance is a proper distance function on
\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
.

The poset-category
\mathbf{C}_{L}
has as its objects
X\in\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
and a unique arrow
Y\rTo X
if and only if
\delta(1,X)\leq\delta(1,Y)
and
\delta(1,Y)=\delta(1,X).\delta(X,Y)
where
1
is the class of the identity matrix.
Conway’s big picture
is the graph derived from
\mathbf{C}_{L}
by only drawing an edge for each arrow
Y\rTo X
such that
\delta(X,Y)
is a prime number. An object
M=(M,0)
for
M\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
is called a
number-class
.

Conway showed that each
X=(M,\frac{g}{h})
has exactly
p+1
neighbours in the big picture at hyper-distance
p
for any prime number
p
. These are the classes

X_{k}=(\frac{M}{p},\frac{g}{hp}+\frac{k}{p}~\text{mod}~1)~\quad\text{for $0\leq k<p$}~\quad~\text{and}~\quad X_{p}=(pM,\frac{pg}{h}~\text{mod}~1)

It is convenient to consider these
p
-neighbours as the classes corresponding to the matrices obtained by multiplying
\alpha_{X}

on the left
with the matrices

P_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{p}&\frac{k}{p}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}~\quad~\text{for $0\leq k<p$, and}~\quad~P_{p}=\begin{bmatrix}p&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

The classes at hyper-distance a
p
-power from
1
form a
p+1
-valent tree in the big picture, and the big picture itself ’factorizes’ as a product of these
p
-adic trees, see
[
5
, p. 332]
or
[
11
, Lemma 3]
. That is, the class (or object)
X
at hyper-distance
p^{k}
from
1
corresponds to a unique product
P_{i_{b}}.\ldots.P_{i_{2}}.P_{i_{1}}P_{p}^{a}
of the matrices
P_{i}
with
0\leq i<p
and
P_{p}
introduced before with
a+b=k
. Here,
p^{a}
is the maximal number-class on the unique path in the big picture from
X
to
1
. The matrices
P_{i}
with
0\leq i<p
generate a free monoid and satisfy the relation
P_{p}.P_{i}=id
. Matrices
P_{i}
and
Q_{j}
corresponding to different prime numbers
p
and
q
satisfy a
meta-commutation relation
.

Lemma 1
.

For distinct primes
p
and
q
and for all
0\leq i<p
and
0\leq j<q
there exist unique
0\leq k<p
and
0\leq l<q
such that

P_{i}.Q_{j}=Q_{l}.P_{k}\quad\text{and further we have}\quad P_{p}.Q_{j}=Q_{a}.P_{p}~\text{when $a=pj~\text{mod}~q$}

Proof.

P_{i}.Q_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{pq}&\frac{iq+j}{pq}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

and as
0\leq iq+j<pq
we have unique
0\leq k<p
and
0\leq l<q
such that
iq+j=lp+k
. ∎

Lemma 2
.

An object
X=(M,\frac{g}{h})
in
\mathbf{C}_{L}
at hyperdistance
N=p^{k}\times q^{l}\times\ldots\times r^{s}=\delta(1,X)
for prime numbers
p<q<\ldots<r
can be identified uniquely with a product

P_{i_{1}}P_{i_{2}}\ldots P_{i_{b}}Q_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{2}}\ldots Q_{j_{d}}\ldots R_{z_{1}}R_{z_{2}}\ldots R_{z_{v}}P_{p}^{a}Q_{q}^{c}\ldots R_{r}^{u}=L_{X}.K_{X}

for unique
0\leq i_{a}<p
,
0\leq j_{b}<q,\ldots,0\leq z_{u}<r
and with
a+b=k,c+d=l,\ldots,u+v=s
.

Proof.

A path in the big picture from
1
to
X=(M,\frac{g}{h})
of minimal length can be viewed as a product (left multiplication)
X_{l}X_{l-1}\ldots X_{2}X_{1}
with each
X_{i}
one of the matrices
P_{a},Q_{b},\ldots,R_{u}
. Now use the meta-commutation relations of the previous lemma.
∎

Here,
K_{X}=P_{p}^{a}Q_{q}^{b}\ldots R_{r}^{u}
is the unique number-class at minimal hyper-distance from
X=(M,\frac{g}{h})
. For a
number-like class

X=(M,\frac{g}{h})
, that is with
M\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
, we have
K_{X}=Mh
and
N=Mh^{2}
.

Proposition 1
.

The points of
\widehat{\mathbf{C}_{L}}
are in one-to-one correspondence with equivalence classes of finite or infinite sequences
X_{1}\geq X_{2}\geq\ldots
of classes in
\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
under the equivalence relation

X_{1}\geq X_{2}\geq\ldots\sim Y_{1}\geq Y_{2}\geq\ldots\Leftrightarrow\forall~i,\exists~j,k~:~X_{i}\geq Y_{j},~Y_{i}\geq X_{k}

In particular, the finite points correspond to the objects in
\mathbf{C}_{L}
.

Proof.

The points of the presheaf topos of a poset-category are the upwards closed subsets of the poset satisfying the condition that it contains for any two of its elements an element smaller or equal to both. To any sequence
X_{1}\geq X_{2}\geq\ldots
we associate the subset
\{X\in\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})~|~\exists i~:~X\geq X_{i}\}
. The claims follow from this.
∎

In
[
13
]
M. Marcolli and G. Tabuada developed a broad generalisation of the classical Bost-Connes system, where roots of unity are replaced by a
Bost-Connes datum

(\Sigma,\sigma_{n})
consisting of

(1)

an Abelian group
\Sigma
, possibly equipped with a continuous action of a Galois group
G
, and

(2)

a family of commuting
G
-equivariant group endomorphisms
\sigma_{n}~:~\Sigma\rTo\Sigma
such that
\sigma_{n}\circ\sigma_{m}=\sigma_{n\times m}
.

An illustrative example is taking
\Sigma=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
, the algebraic numbers with the canonical Galois action, and with
\sigma_{n}
the power map
x\mapsto x^{n}
, see
[
13
]
for more instances. In many interesting examples the Bost-Connes datum
(\Sigma,\sigma_{n})
will satisfy the following additional properties

(3)

the kernel of
\sigma_{n}
is the cyclic group of order
n
, and

(4)

there is a family of set-maps
s_{n}~:~\Sigma\rTo\Sigma
commuting among themselves and with the
\sigma_{m}
such that their restriction to
\Sigma_{n}=Im(\sigma_{n})
is a set-theoretic section of the image map

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{n}}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s_{n}}
\textstyle{0}

and
\Sigma
is the disjoint union of
C_{n}
-translates of the subset
s_{n}(\Sigma_{n})
.

Let us denote the group operation on
\Sigma
additively, and consider for each prime number
p
maps on
\Sigma
(identified as vectors via
x\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}x\\
1\end{bmatrix}
) by left-multiplication by the ’matrices’ for
0\leq i<p

\mathcal{P}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}s_{p}&x_{i,p}\\

0&1\end{bmatrix}

where
C_{p}=Ker(\sigma_{p})=\{0=x_{0,p},x_{1,p},\ldots,x_{p-1,p}\}
. That is, we have for all
x\in\Sigma

\mathcal{P}_{i}.x=s_{p}(x)+x_{i,p}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{P}_{p}.x=\sigma_{p}(x)

As in
[
13
]
, let
\rho_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\rTo P(\Sigma)
be the map sending an element

to the set of its pre-images under

, then we have
\rho_{p}(x)=\{\mathcal{P}_{0}.x,\mathcal{P}_{1}.x,\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{p-1}.x\}
. If we want these operators to satisfy the same meta-commutation relations as the
P_{i}
and

we have to impose

(5)

for any two prime numbers
p
and

, for all
0\leq i<p
and
0\leq j<q
we have for the unique

and

for

.

.

Let
(\Sigma,\sigma_{p},s_{p})

.

The fiber

The mental image of the geometric morphism

with the inclusion

corresponding to the zero section.

We define the
Conway monoid

C
, having

P_{0},\ldots,P_{p-1}
for each prime

, with
\{P_{0},\ldots,P_{p-1}\}
generating a free monoid of rank

. For different prime numbers

It follows from lemma

from

are objects in

at minimal distance from

With
\mathbf{C}
we denote the corresponding monoid-category, whose presheaf-topos
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}
will be
Conway’s site
.
Because the Conway monoid
C
is left cancellative, we have a covariant functor
\pi:\mathbf{C}_{L}^{\prime}\rTo\mathbf{C}
which assigns to the unique morphism
Y\rTo X
between two objects of
\mathbf{C}_{L}^{\prime}
such that
Y\leq X
the unique element
Z\in C
such that
Y=Z\ast X
.

We will call the points of
\mathbf{C}_{L}^{\prime}
the
non-commutative supernatural numbers
and denote them with
\mathbb{S}_{nc}
. That is,
\mathbb{S}_{nc}
is the set of equivalence classes of finite or infinite products
\ldots\ast A_{2}\ast A_{1}
in
C
under the equivalence relation

\ldots A_{2}\ast A_{1}\sim\ldots B_{2}\ast B_{1}\Leftrightarrow\forall i,\exists j,k,U,V~:~\begin{cases}B_{j}\ast\ldots\ast B_{1}=U\ast A_{i}\ast\ldots\ast A_{1}\\
A_{k}\ast\ldots\ast A_{1}=V\ast B_{i}\ast\ldots\ast B_{1}\end{cases}

This functor induces a surjection on the points of the corresponding presheaf toposes, giving us that

\mathbf{points}(\widehat{\mathbf{C}})=[\mathbb{S}_{nc}]

where the equivalence relations on noncommutative supernatural numbers is given by restricting to their tails, that is

[\ldots\ast A_{2}\ast A_{1}]\sim[\ldots\ast B_{2}\ast B_{1}]\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\exists i,j~:~[\ldots\ast A_{i+1}\ast A_{i}]=[\ldots\ast B_{j+1}\ast B_{j}]

The hyper-distance
\delta(1,X)
from
X
to
1
gives a morphism of monoids
\delta:C\rTo\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
and hence a geometric morphism to the arithmetic site
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}\rTo\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
.

Connes and Consani equip the arithmetic site
\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
with a structure sheaf
\mathbb{Z}_{max}=(\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\},max,+)
(the fundamental semi-ring in characteristic
1
, with addition
x\oplus y=max(x,y)
and multiplication
x\otimes y=x+y
) in order to restrict the large group of auto-equivalences of the topos
\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
, coming from permutations among prime numbers in the monoid
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
.

The geometric morphism
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}\rTo\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
can be seen as an alternative to this rigidification problem. The only monoid morphism of
\mathbb{N}^{\times~}_{+}
which lifts to a monoid morphism of the Conway monoid
C
, compatible with the morphism
\delta
, is the identity map because a class at hyperdistance
p
must be mapped to another one at hyperdistance
p
.

3.
Belyi’s site and some localic subcovers

A
(dynamical) Belyi map
is a rational map
B:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\rTo\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
defined over a number field with exactly three ramification points which we assume to be
\{0,1,\infty\}
(and such that
B(0)=0,B(1)=1
and
B(\infty)=\infty
), see for example
[
1
]
or
[
15
]
. If
B^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\rTo\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
is another dynamical Belyi map, then so is their composition
B^{\prime}\circ B
, that is, dynamical Belyi maps form a monoid. We will restrict here to the submonoid of dynamical Belyi
polynomials
and call it the
Belyi monoid

B_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
or
B
if we restrict to polynomials defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

To a (dynamical) Belyi map
B
we associate its
(framed) dessin (d’enfant)
which is a bicolored graph
D(B)
drawn on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}=S^{2}
with

•

as its
black vertices
the inverse images
B^{-1}(0)
, (with the vertex
0
labeled),

•

as its
white vertices
the inverse images
B^{-1}(1)
, (with the vertex
1
labeled),

•

as its edges the inverse images
B^{-1}(0,1)
of the open interval
(0,1)
.

If
B
is a (dynamical) Belyi polynomial, then
D(B)
is a (framed)
tree
dessin, that is, a bicolored planar tree.

Two bicolored planar trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
are said to be
combinatorial equivalent
is there is a color-preserving tree isomorphism

\gamma:V(T)\rTo V(T^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma:E(T)\rTo E(T^{\prime})

preserving the orientation around each vertex. That is, for any vertex
v
of
T
if
c_{v}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
is the cyclic order of edges encountered when walking counter-clockwise around
v
, then the cyclic order of edges encountered when walking counter-clockwise around
\gamma(v)
must be
c_{\gamma(v)}=(\gamma(e_{1}),\ldots,\gamma(e_{k}))
. A
framed tree dessin
is a bicolored planar tree such that a black vertex is labeled
0
and a white vertex is labeled
1
. Two framed tree dessins
T
and
T^{\prime}
are
isomorphic
if there is a combinatorial equivalence
\gamma:V(T)\rTo V(T^{\prime})
such that
\gamma(0)=0
and
\gamma(1)=1
.

Let
Aut(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})=\{L=\alpha x+\beta:\alpha\not=0\}
be the group of non-constant linear polynomials under composition. We say that two Belyi polynomials
B
and
B^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if
B^{\prime}=B\circ L
for some
L\in Aut(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. G. Shabat proved that
B\leftrightarrow D(B)
gives a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of Belyi polynomials and combinatorial equivalence classes of tree dessins, see
[
8
, Thm. 2.2.9]
.

Proposition 4
.

Every framed tree dessin
T
is isomorphic to the framed tree dessin
D(B)
of a unique (!) dynamical Belyi polynomial
B
.

Proof.

Forgetting the framing of
T
for a moment, there is Belyi polynomial
P\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
such that
T
and the dessin
D(P)
are combinatorially equivalent by Shabat’s result. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the vertices of
D(P)
corresponding to the labeled vertices
0
and
1
of
T
under the equivalence. Consider

L=(\beta-\alpha)x+\alpha\in Aut(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})

then
B=P\circ L
is a Belyi polynomial such that
B(0)=P(\alpha)=0
and
B(1)=P(\beta)=1
, whence
B
is a dynamical Belyi polynomial with framed tree dessin isomorphic to
T
. To prove unicity, assume
B^{\prime}
is another dynamical Belyi polynomial such that
D(B^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
D(B)
. Then, by Shabat’s result there exists an
L=\alpha x+\beta\in Aut(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
B^{\prime}=B\circ L
. But then, the combinatorial equivalence on vertices is given by

L~:~V(D(B^{\prime}))\rTo V(D(B))

and in particular
L(0)=0
and
L(1)=1
whence
\beta=0
and
\alpha=1
whence
B^{\prime}=B
.
∎

.

Proposition 5
.

The Belyi monoids
B_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
B
are both left- and right-cancellative, that is, for all dynamical Belyi polynomials

In fact, we have the stronger result that if
deg(B)=deg(B_{1})
and if

B^{\prime}\circ B=B"\circ B_{1}\Rightarrow B^{\prime}=B"~\text{and}~B=B_{1}

Proof.

If

and
B^{\prime}\circ B=B"\circ B_{1}

and
\beta=0
and therefore

Abstract

We prove the existence and regularity of the isochron map for stable invariant manifolds of a large class of evolution equations.
Our results apply in particular to the isochron map of reaction-diffusion equations and neural field equations.
Using the regularity properties proven here, we prove a strong Itô formula for the isochronal phase of stochastically perturbed travelling waves and other patterns appearing in SPDEs driven by white noise, even for SPDEs that only admit mild solutions.

1
Introduction

This note presents simple arguments on the existence and regularity of the isochron map for a class of dynamical systems
(\phi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on a separable Banach space in the vicinity of a stable, normally hyperbolic invariant manifold
\Gamma
.
The isochron map was introduced for stable limit cycles of dynamical systems in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
[
15
]
and
[
33
]
.
Our framework encompasses infinite dimensional dynamical systems, in particular reaction-diffusion systems and neural field equations.
From the regularity properties of the isochron map proven here, we obtain a strong Itô formula for the isochronal phase of stochastic perturbations of reaction-diffusion systems, even when these systems only admit a mild solution theory.

The proofs in this note are short and general, and recover results on the isochron map proven elsewhere in the literature using different techniques
[
1
]
.
As will be seen, while
[
1
]
requires the invariant manifold to be smooth, our arguments only require
\Gamma
to be
C^{1}
.
Additionally, we make no assumptions on the spectral properties of the generator of the linearization of
\phi_{t}
about
\Gamma
.
This is of particular interest in the case where
\Gamma
is the orbit of a non-commutative Lie group (such as
SE(n)
when
\Gamma
consists of spiral wave solutions to a parabolic PDE), where trying to track the linearized dynamics along
\Gamma
can lead to significant complications, as noted in the introduction of
[
24
]
.

As a consequence, the approach taken in this note may lead to significant generalizations of results obtained for travelling pulses and other transient patterns appearing in stochastic evolution equations using other notions of “phase”.
For instance, those studied in neural field equations by
[
20
,
25
,
26
]
, and in reaction-diffusion equations by
[
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
31
]
.
The approach of this note also allows us to prove a strong Itô formula for the isochronal phase of SPDEs with only mild solutions, which is stronger than the mild Itô formula proven using more complicated techniques in
[
11
,
19
]
.

2
Setup

We consider an evolution equation of the form

\partial_{t}x\,=\,Lx+N(x)\,\eqqcolon\,V(x),

(1)

and its stochastic perturbation

dX\,=\,\left(LX+N(X)\right)\,dt+\sigma B\,dW.

(2)

Here,
L
is a linear operator generating a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Hilbert space
H
,
N
is a nonlinearity defined on a subspace of
H
,
B
is a linear operator on
H
,
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a cylindrical Wiener process, as in
[
12
, Chapter 4.1.2]
, and
\sigma\geq 0
.
We make the following assumptions on the deterministic dynamics of (
1
).

Assumption 1
.

The system (
1
) satisfies the following.

1.

N
is a nonlinearity defined on a dense subset
E
of
H
such that
E
is a Banach space with norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E}
, and the embedding
E\hookrightarrow H
is dense and continuous.

N
is four times continuously Fréchet differentiable
in the topology of
E
, with first and second Fréchet derivatives in this topology denoted
DN
and
D^{2}N
.

2.

L
is a linear operator with a dense domain of definition
D(L)
in
H
, and generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
H
that restricts to
E
.
Moreover, letting
\left\|\cdot\right\|
denote the norm of
H
, there exists
\omega>0
such that
\sup\left\{\left\|\Lambda_{t}\right\|,\,\left\|\Lambda_{t}\right\|_{E}\right\}\,\leq\,e^{-\omega t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Let the flow map of (
1
) be
(t,x)\,\mapsto\,\phi_{t}(x)
, and suppose that the flow can be expressed by the variation of constants formula as

\phi_{t}(x)\,=\,\Lambda_{t}x+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{t-s}N(\phi_{s}(x))\,ds.

Since the nonlinearity
N
is
C^{4}
in the topology of
E
, the flow map is
C^{3}
in this topology.
The first and second derivatives of
\phi_{t}
at
x_{0}
in directions
x,y\in E
are denoted

x\,\mapsto\,D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[x],\qquad(x,y)\,\mapsto\,D^{2}\phi_{t}(x_{0})[x,y].

(3)

We now state our assumptions on the flow of (
1
).
Specifically, we assume the existence of a stable invariant manifold
\Gamma
of (
1
) that models spatiotemporal patterns.
In particular, the following guarantees that the dynamics of (
1
) is not chaotic on
\Gamma
.

Assumption 2
.

The deterministic system (
1
) has a stable, finite dimensional, normally hyperbolic invariant manifold
\Gamma
, as defined in
[
4
, Condition (H3)]
.

\Gamma
is parameterized by a manifold
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
We write
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{S}}
, and let
B(\Gamma)
denote the basin of attraction of
\Gamma
in
H
.
Moreover, the following hold.

1.

\phi_{t}
and
\phi_{t}^{-1}
are Lipschitz on
\Gamma
with uniform in time Lipschitz constants.

2.

D\phi_{t}(\gamma_{\alpha})
is invertible with uniformly bounded inverse for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{S}
.

3.

The map
\alpha\mapsto\gamma_{\alpha}
is continuously differentiable with derivative
D\gamma_{\alpha}
, and
D\gamma_{\alpha}
is invertible with a uniformly bounded inverse.

Definition 2.1
.

Assumption
2
implies that for each
x\in B(\Gamma)
there is a unique
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{S}
such that

\left\|\phi_{t}(x)-\phi_{t}(\gamma_{\pi(x)})\right\|\,\xrightarrow[t\rightarrow\infty]{}\,0,

(4)

providing a well-defined map
\pi:B(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
.
The existence and uniqueness of this map is proven in Theorem
3.1
.
We refer to
\pi
as the
isochron map
of
\Gamma
.

Throughout this note, for
\delta>0
we define
\Gamma_{\delta}\,\coloneqq\,\left\{x\in E\,:\,\left\|x-\gamma_{\pi(x)}\right\|_{E}\leq\delta\right\}\subset E
.
When considering the SDE (
2
), we require the following additional assumptions.

Assumption 3
.

Assumption
1
holds, and the SDE (
2
) satisfies the following.

1.

There exists a unique
E
-valued mild solution
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to (
2
), satisfying

X_{t}\,=\,\Lambda_{t}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{t-s}N(X_{s})\,ds+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{t-s}B\,dW_{s},\qquad X_{0}\in H,

(5)

for
X_{0}\in E
and all
t<\tau_{blowup}
, where
\tau_{blowup}
is in
(0,\infty]
almost surely.

2.

For
x\in H
,
(\phi_{t}(x))_{t>0}
is continuous in
D(L)
, equipped with the graph norm of
L
.

3.

There exists an orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
H
consisting of eigenfunctions of
L
, and
e_{k}\in E
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Letting
\lambda_{k}
denote the eigenvalue of
-L
corresponding to
e_{k}
,

K_{s,r}\,\coloneqq\,\sup_{t\in[s,r]}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}\,<\,\infty.

(6)

and for
y\in E\subset H
, written
y=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}y^{k}e_{k}
in
H
, the following converges in
E
:

L\Lambda_{t}y\,=\,\Lambda_{t}Ly\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}y^{k}\Lambda_{t}Le_{k}.

(7)

Assumption
3
is needed for our strong Itô formula.
We remark that the assumption is weaker than requiring
X_{t}\in D(L)
or that
E=H
, so that the Itô formulas of
[
2
,
9
,
19
]
do not apply to (
2
).
See Remark
2.2
below.
The condition (
6
) is only needed when the noise in (
2
) is white in space, but not when the noise is trace class.

Note that with additive noise, solutions of (
2
) may be pushed out of
B(\Gamma)
in finite time.
We therefore define the exit time of the solution from
B(\Gamma)
as

\tau\,\coloneqq\,\min\left\{\tau_{blowup},\,\inf\left\{t>0\,:\,X_{t}\in E/B(\Gamma)\right\}\right\}.

(8)

Since the geometry of
B(\Gamma)
is generally unknown and may be very complicated, we will sometimes restrict our attention to
\Gamma_{\delta}
for
\delta>0
.
We define the exit time from
\Gamma_{\delta}
as

\tau_{\delta}\,\coloneqq\,\min\left\{\tau_{blowup},\,\inf\left\{t>0\,:\,\left\|X_{t}-\gamma_{\pi(X_{t})}\right\|_{E}=\delta\right\}\right\}.

(9)

When
\Gamma
consists of fixed points of
\phi_{t}
, estimates on the distribution of
\tau_{\delta}
can be found in
[
27
]
.
See also
[
5
]
.
Further work is needed to estimate the distribution of
\tau
for more general stable invariant manifolds.
We let
\pi_{t}=\pi(X_{t})
be the isochronal phase of
X_{t}
for
t<\tau
.

Remark 2.2
.

Two classes of examples of (
1
) motivate the present discussion.
The first is the class of reaction-diffusion systems of the form

\partial_{t}x\,=\,(\Delta-a)x+N(x),

(10)

where
a>0
and
N
is a vector of polynomials.
With
L=\Delta-a
and
O
bounded, we can show that (
10
) satisfies Assumptions
1
&
3
.
For instance if
O=[0,\ell]
with periodic boundaries, take
H=L^{2}(O)
and
E=C(O)
, the space of continuous functions on
O
with the supremum norm.
In this case, it is more convenient to index the eigenpairs by
\mathbb{Z}
rather than
\mathbb{N}
.
We then have
e_{k}(x)=\sin\left(\frac{2k\pi}{\ell}x\right)
and
\lambda_{k}=\frac{4\pi^{2}k^{2}}{\ell^{2}}
for
k<0
, and
e_{k}(x)=\cos\left(\frac{1k\pi}{\ell}x\right)
and
\lambda_{k}=\frac{4\pi^{2}k^{2}}{\ell^{2}}
for
k\geq 0
.
Hence
\left\|e_{k}\right\|_{E}\leq 1
, and

\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}\,\leq\,\exp\left(-\frac{4\pi^{2}k^{2}t}{\ell^{2}}-at\right)\quad\text{ for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $t>0$}.

For fixed
r>s>0
we then have
K_{s,r}\,=\,\sup_{t\in[s,r]}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}\,<\,\infty
, verifying (
6
).
Assumption
3
(b) holds by the regularity of solutions to the heat equation, which can be proven as in
[
14
, Theorem 2.3.1]
.
The regularity of the heat equation also immediately implies (
7
), since
L\Lambda_{t}y
is clearly equal to
\Lambda_{t}Ly
, as defined in (
7
) for
y\in E
, and in this case the graph norm of
L
is just
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{C^{2}}
.
Finally, when (
10
) is perturbed by additive white noise, Assumption
3
(a) is verified using the local inversion theorem, as in
[
12
]
, since in this case the stochastic convolution
\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{t-s}B\,dW_{s}
is contained in
E
.

The second class of examples in which we are interested in is a class of integro-differential equations, with dynamics at
\xi\in O
described as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}x(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\,-x(t,\xi)+\int_{O}\omega(\xi,\zeta)f(x(t,\zeta))\,d\zeta,

(11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}y(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\,-\varepsilon^{-1}y(t,\xi)+x(t,\xi).

where
\omega
and
f
are typically bounded and Lipschitz.
These equations are frequently employed in neuroscience (where they are typically referred to as
neural field equations
)
[
8
]
and ecology
[
30
]
.
Mild solutions to stochastic perturbations of (
11
) only make sense when the perturbing noise is trace class
[
23
]
, and in this case Assumption
3
is satisfied.
With
L=[-1,-\epsilon^{-1}]
, Assumptions
1
(a), (b), are satisfied, though (c) is not.
However, Assumption
1
(c) is only needed to prove the Itô formula for the isochronal phase when
W
is not trace class, as can be seen in the arguments of Section
3
below.
As stochastic neural field equations only make sense for trace class noise, the fact that (
11
) does not satisfy Assumption
1
(c) is not an issue.

For reaction-diffusion or neural field equations, the main examples of
\Gamma
that we have in mind are stationary or travelling waves
[
3
,
21
,
28
]
, and spiral waves in excitable media, as studied in
[
6
,
7
]
on unbounded domains and in
[
34
]
on bounded spatial domains – see also
[
24
]
.
We are also interested in the case of stationary patterns that remain stationary under translation or rotation
[
10
,
28
,
32
]
.
So long as (
10
) or (
11
) possesses a sufficiently regular invariant manifold
\Gamma
, and the parameterization
\alpha\mapsto\gamma_{\alpha}
is non-degenerate, these examples satisfy Assumption
2
.
For instance, if
\Gamma
consists of travelling wave solutions on a periodic domain, this is the case.

3
Regularity of the Isochron Map & a Strong Itô Formula

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Assumptions
1
&
2
, there exists a unique function
\pi:B(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying (
4
).
This function is twice continuously Fréchet differentiable at each
x_{0}\in B(\Gamma)\subset E
in the topology of
E
.

Proof.

We begin by proving that
\pi:B(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
is well-defined.
Since
\Gamma
is a stable manifold of (
1
), taking any sequence of positive numbers
\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
monotonically decreasing to zero, there exists a sequence of times
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
monotonically increasing to infinity and a sequence of closed sets
U_{n}\subset\Gamma
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma\in U_{n}
, we have

\left\|\phi_{t_{n}}(x)-\gamma\right\|_{E}\,\leq\,\epsilon_{n}/2M_{1},

(12)

for some constant
M_{1}
.
The collection
\{\phi_{t_{n}}^{-1}U_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
constitutes a sequence of non-empty, closed, nested subsets of the complete metric space
E
.
Moreover, using the bi-Lipschitz condition of
\phi_{t}
on
\Gamma
and (
12
), we see that
\diam\left(\phi_{t_{n}}^{-1}U_{n}\right)\,\leq\,\epsilon_{n}
.
In particular, the diameter of
\phi_{t_{n}}^{-1}U_{n}
tends to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
By Cantor’s Intersection Theorem, there is a unique
\gamma_{*}
such that
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\phi_{t_{n}}^{-1}U_{n}\,=\,\{\gamma_{*}\}
.
Since

\alpha\mapsto\gamma_{\alpha}
is invertible,
there is a unique
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\gamma_{*}=\gamma_{\pi(x)}
.

To prove that
\pi
is
C^{2}
, note that our assumptions on
N
implies that
x\mapsto\phi_{t}(x)
is
C^{3}
on
B(\Gamma)\cap E
in the topology of
E
.
Since
C^{3}
is a Banach space, the map

x\,\mapsto\,\gamma_{\pi(x)}\,=\,\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{t}(x)

is
C^{3}
on
B(\Gamma)\cap E
.
By assumption,
(r\mapsto\gamma_{r}):\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Gamma
is invertible with
C^{2}
inverse.
Denoting this inverse by
\gamma^{-1}
, the map
x\,\mapsto\,\pi(x)\,=\,\gamma^{-1}(\gamma_{\pi(x)})
is
C^{2}
on
B(\Gamma)\cap E
. ∎

We
denote the first and second derivatives of the isochron map at
x_{0}\in B(\Gamma)\cap E
in the directions
x,y\in E
by
D\pi(x_{0})x
and
D^{2}\pi(x_{0})[x,y]
.
The following theorem yields additional regularity of the isochron map when Assumption
1
holds, showing in particular that its second derivative is trace class, in some sense.

Theorem 3.2
.

Under Assumptions
1
&
2
, there exists
M_{\pi}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\pi(x_{0})[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,<\,M_{\pi},

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\pi(x_{0})e_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,<\,M_{\pi},

(13)

\displaystyle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\pi(x_{0})e_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{2}\,<\,M_{\pi},

uniformly in
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\delta}
.
Moreover,
these quantities are Lipschitz continuous in
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\delta}
.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, define
\tilde{\pi}:\Gamma_{\delta}\rightarrow\Gamma
as
\tilde{\pi}(x)\coloneqq\gamma_{\pi(x)}
, and note that

\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))\,=\,\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))\qquad\forall x\in\Gamma_{\delta},\,\,\,t>0.

(14)

We show that (
13
) is satisfied by
\pi
replaced with
\tilde{\pi}
.
Then, since the map
\alpha\mapsto\gamma_{\alpha}
is
C^{2}
with uniformly bounded first derivative, (
13
) follows.
From (
14
) and Assumption
2
,

\displaystyle D\tilde{\pi}(x)y\,

\displaystyle=\,[D\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))]^{-1}D\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))D\phi_{t}(x)y,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ and }

(15)

\displaystyle D^{2}\tilde{\pi}(x)[y,z]\,

\displaystyle=\,[D\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))]^{-1}\bigg(D^{2}\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))[D\phi_{t}(x)y,D\phi_{t}(x)z]

(16)

\displaystyle\qquad+D\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))D^{2}\phi_{t}(x)[y,z]-D^{2}\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))[D\tilde{\pi}(x)y,D\tilde{\pi}(x)z]\bigg).

We now let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}\coloneqq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E\rightarrow E}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}\coloneqq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E\times E\rightarrow E}
.
Since
D\phi_{t}(x)^{-1}
and
D\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))
are bounded operators, it follows that for some
C>0
independent of
x\in B(\Gamma)
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|D\tilde{\pi}(x)e_{k}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\,\left\|D\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))^{-1}D\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))\right\|_{1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x)e_{k}\right\|_{E}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq\,C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x)e_{k}\right\|_{E}.

Similarly, since
D\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))^{-1}
,
D^{2}\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))
,
D\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))
, and
D^{2}\phi_{t}(x)
are bounded uniformly in
x\in\Gamma_{\delta}
, for some
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
independent of
x\in B(\Gamma)
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\tilde{\pi}(x)[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{1}\,\leq\,\left\|D\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))^{-1}\right\|_{E}\bigg(\left\|D^{2}\tilde{\pi}(\phi_{t}(x))\right\|_{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x)e_{k}\right\|_{E}^{2}

(18)

\displaystyle+\left\|D\tilde{\pi}_{E}(\phi_{t}(x))\right\|_{1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}(x)[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle+\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}(\tilde{\pi}(x))\right\|_{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\tilde{\pi}(x)e_{k}\right\|_{E}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\,C_{1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x)e_{k}\right\|_{E}^{2}+C_{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}(x)[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}+C_{3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\tilde{\pi}(x)e_{k}\right\|^{2}_{E}.

Hence, Theorem
3.2
immediately follows as a corollary to the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under Assumptions
1
&
2
, there exists
M_{\phi}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
t>0
and small
\delta>0
,
the following hold uniformly in
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\delta}
and
s\in(0,t)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,<\,M_{\phi},

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}^{2}\,<\,M_{\phi},\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,<\,M_{\phi}.

Moreover,
these quantities are Lipschitz continuous in
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\delta}
.

Proof.

We consider the evolution equations of the operators defined in (
3
), written as

\displaystyle D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[x]\,=\Lambda_{t}D\phi_{0}(x_{0})[x]+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{t-s}DN(\phi_{s}(x_{0}))D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[x]\,ds,

(20)

\displaystyle D^{2}\phi_{t}(x_{0})[x,y]\,=\,\Lambda_{t}D^{2}\phi_{0}(x_{0})[x,y]+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{t-s}DN(\phi_{s}(x_{0}))D^{2}\phi_{s}(x_{0})[x,y]\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{t-s}D^{2}N(\phi_{s}(x_{0}))\big[D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[x],D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[y]\big]\,ds,

where
DN
and
D^{2}N
are the first and second Fréchet derivatives of
N
.

Now, observe that
D\phi_{0}(x_{0})[x]=x
.
Applying Tonelli’s theorem,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Lambda_{t-s}DN(\phi_{s}(x_{0}))\right\|_{1}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds

for some
C_{0}>0
which is independent of
x_{0}
(using Assumption
1
, and the fact that
N
is uniformly Lipschitz on
\Gamma_{\delta}
in the topology of
E
).
Taking
s\in(0,t)
and letting
r\mapsto K_{s,r}
be the non-decreasing function defined in (
6
), we apply Grönwall’s inequality to obtain, for arbitrary
r>t
,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,\leq\,K_{s,r}e^{C_{0}t}\,<\,\infty.

(21)

Furthermore, fixing
s<t<r
, (
21
) implies a local integrability of the sum,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds\,=\,\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds\,<\,\frac{K_{s,r}}{C_{0}}e^{C_{0}t}.

We remark that the constants appearing in this bound are independent of
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\delta}
, implying the first uniform bound (
19
).

Similarly, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}^{2}+2C_{0}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle+C_{0}^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds\right)^{2}.

The first and second sums are bounded, respectively, by (
6
) & (
21
).
Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,K_{s,r}^{2}+2K_{s,r}^{2}e^{C_{0}t}+C_{0}^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,K_{s,r}^{2}\left(1+2e^{C_{0}t}\right)+C_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}^{2}\,ds,

for arbitrary
s<t<r
.
Applying Grönwall’s inequality, we find that
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}^{2}
is finite and locally integrable in
t>0
.
We remark that the constants appearing in the second bound of (
19
) obtained by these arguments are independent of
x_{0}
.

Once we have the first two uniform bounds in (
19
), we can similarly obtain the third.
Since
D^{2}\phi_{0}(x_{0})[x,x]=x
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\Lambda_{t}e_{k}\right\|_{E}+C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds

\displaystyle+C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}^{2}\,ds.

By our previous results, the first and third sum in the above are finite.
Therefore for arbitrary
s<t<r
we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,\leq\,L_{s,r}+C\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{s}(x_{0})[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,ds

for another constant
L_{s,r}>0
.
We may therefore apply Grönwall’s inequality again, to obtain the third bound in (
19
) with constants independent of
x_{0}
.

To see Lipschitz continuity of the maps defined by taking
x_{0}
to the values in (
19
), we remark that the first part of this proposition implies that the finite sums

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,<\,M_{\phi},\qquad\qquad\quad\,\,\,\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,<\,M_{\phi},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|D\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k}]\right\|_{E}^{2}\,<\,M_{\phi},\quad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}(x_{0})[e_{k},e_{k}]\right\|_{E}\,<\,M_{\phi},

converge uniformly in
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\delta}
to the series in (
19
) as
K\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence we need only prove that the finite sums are Lipschitz.
But, from the fact that
x_{0}\mapsto\phi_{t}(x_{0})
is
C^{3}
in the topology of
E
(since
N
is
C^{4}
), we know that each of the summands is Lipschitz in
\Gamma_{\delta}
.
∎

We now prove a strong Itô formula for
\pi(X_{t})
that holds for
t<\tau
.
We need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let Assumptions
2
&
3
hold.
For each
t>0
, the Itô integral

\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}D\pi(X_{s})B\,dW_{s}

(22)

is well-defined as an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued random variable.

Proof.

For a collection of independent identically distributed Brownian motions
\{\beta^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, write
W_{t}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}e_{k}\beta^{k}_{t}
.
Of course, this sum does not converge in any sense as an
H
-valued random variable, but as an
H_{0}
-valued random variable for some Hilbert space
H_{0}\supset H
(see
[
12
, Chapter 4]
for details).
However, we need not specify
H_{0}
here.
Indeed, the integral (
22
) can be made sense of as the limit in expectation of the finite sums

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}D\pi(X_{s})Be_{k}\,d\beta_{s}^{k}\,\eqqcolon\,\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{\beta}_{t}^{k},

which converge in mean square.
Using Theorem
3.2
and Itô’s isometry, we obtain

\displaystyle\ExpOp\left[\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{\beta}_{t}^{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\ExpOp\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\|D\pi(X_{s})Be_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{2}\,ds\right]

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\,t\sup_{x\in\Gamma_{\delta}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\pi(x)e_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{2}\,<\,tM_{\pi}M_{B}.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let Assumptions
2
&
3
hold.
Then,

D\pi(x)Ly\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}y^{k}D\pi(x)Le_{k}

exists in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for all
x,y\in E
, where
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are the
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
basis coefficients of
y
in
H
.
Moreover,
\partial_{t}\pi(\phi_{t}(x))=D\pi(\phi_{t}(x))V(\phi_{t}(x))
.

Proof.

Write
y=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}y^{k}e_{k}
, converging in
H
.
Note that the sum
Ly=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}y^{k}\lambda_{k}e_{k}
does not necessarily converge in
H
, but in a larger Hilbert space
H_{L}
.
Recall that for
t>0
and
x\in E
,
D\pi(x)=M(x)D\phi_{t}(x)
for a bounded linear operator
M(x)
on
E
.
Hence,
\left\|D\pi(x)Ly\right\|_{E}\leq c\left\|D\phi_{t}(x)Ly\right\|_{E}
for some
c>0
, and

\left\|D\phi_{t}(x)Ly\right\|_{E}\,\leq\,\left\|\Lambda_{t}Ly\right\|_{E}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Lambda_{t-s}DN(\phi_{s}(x))\right\|_{1}\left\|D\phi_{s}(x)Ly\right\|_{E}\,ds.

By Assumption
3
(c), an application of Grönwell’s inequality completes the proof of the convergence of
D\pi(x)Ly
.
The statement
\partial_{t}\pi(\phi_{t}(x))=D\pi(\phi_{t}(x))V(\phi_{t}(x))
then follows from the chain rule.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let Assumptions
2
&
3
hold.
Then,

\displaystyle\pi(X_{t\wedge\tau})

\displaystyle=\,\pi(X_{0})+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}D\pi(X_{s})V(X_{s})\,ds+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})[Be_{k},Be_{k}]\,ds

(24)

\displaystyle+\sigma\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}D\pi(X_{s})B\,dW_{s},

and all of the terms in the above expression are well-defined in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof.

Take
t>0
and a partition of
[0,t]
with boundary points
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}
.
Let
t^{*}\coloneqq t\wedge\tau
and
t_{i}^{*}\coloneqq t_{i}\wedge\tau
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
.
By Theorem
3.1
,
\pi(X_{t^{*}})
may be written as

\pi(X_{t^{*}})\,=\,\pi(X_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big(D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})[X_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-X_{t^{*}_{i}}]+D^{2}\pi(w_{i})[X_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-X_{t^{*}_{i}},X_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-X_{t^{*}_{i}}]\Big),

(25)

where
w_{i}=a_{i}X_{t^{*}_{i}}+(1-a_{i})X_{t^{*}_{i+1}}
for some
a_{i}\in[0,1]
.
Since
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a mild solution of (
2
),

\displaystyle X_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-X_{t^{*}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\,\left[\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-t^{*}_{i}}-I\right]X_{0}+\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}N(X_{s})\,ds+\sigma\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\,U^{1}_{i}+U^{2}_{i}+\sigma U_{i}^{3}.

Inserting this into (
25
), we then have

\displaystyle\pi(X_{t^{*}})-\pi(X_{0})

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[U^{1}_{i}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{M}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[U^{2}_{i}\right]+\sigma\sum_{i=1}^{M}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[U_{i}^{3}\right]

(26)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{M}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[U^{1}_{i}+U^{2}_{i},U^{1}_{i}+U^{2}_{i}\right]+2\sigma\sum_{i=1}^{M}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[U^{1}_{i}+U^{2}_{i},U_{i}^{3}\right]

\displaystyle+\sigma^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{M}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[U^{3}_{i},U^{3}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I+II+III+IV+V+VI.

For a fixed partition
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
with mesh size
h>0
, choose arbitrary
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and let
(\hat{X}_{s})_{s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}
be defined by the deterministic flow started at
X_{t_{i}}
,

\hat{X}_{t_{i}}\,=\,X_{t_{i}},\quad\hat{X}_{s}\,=\,\Lambda_{s-t_{i}}X_{t_{i}}+\int_{t_{i}}^{s}\Lambda_{s-r}N(\hat{X}_{r})\,dr\quad\text{ for }\quad s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}].

By Assumption
3
(b), note that
(\hat{X}_{s})_{s\in[t_{i}^{*},t_{i+1}^{*}]}
exists as a continuous path in
D(L)
(in the topology of the graph norm of
L
), and hence

\sup_{s\in[t_{i}^{*},t_{i+1}^{*}]}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}h^{-1}\left\|\left(\Lambda_{h}-I\right)\hat{X}_{s}\right\|_{E}\,<\,\infty.

Consequently, we have the following fact, which will prove to be useful below:

\sup_{s\in[t_{i}^{*},t_{i+1}^{*}]}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}h^{-1/2}\left\|\left(\Lambda_{h}-I\right)\hat{X}_{s}\right\|_{E}\,=\,0.

(27)

We now show that
(I+II)\rightarrow\,\int_{0}^{t^{*}}D\pi(X_{s})V(X_{s})\,ds
as
h\rightarrow 0
.
To do so, we apply the second order Taylor’s theorem to
\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}})
centered at
\hat{X}_{t_{i}}
to obtain

D\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}\right]+D^{2}\pi(U^{5}_{i})\left[\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}},\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}\right]\,=\,\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}})-\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}),

where
U^{5}_{i}
is defined for some
a_{i}^{5}\in[0,1]
as

U_{i}^{5}\,\coloneqq\,a_{i}^{5}\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}+(1-a_{i}^{5})U^{4}_{i}.

By Grönwall’s inequality and the Lipschitz property of
N
(on
\Gamma_{\delta}
), it can be seen that

\left\|X_{s}-\hat{X}_{s}\right\|_{E}\,\sim\,O(h^{2})\quad\text{ for }\,\,\,s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}].

(28)

It then follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}-\left[(\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-t^{*}_{i}}-I)X_{t^{*}_{i}}+\int_{t_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}N(X_{s})\,ds\right]\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\,K_{0}h\sup_{s\in[t^{*}_{i},t^{*}_{i+1}]}\left\|\hat{X}_{s}-X_{s}\right\|_{E}\,\leq\,K_{1}h^{2}

for some constants
K_{0},K_{1}>0
.
Using (
27
), for all
t\in(t_{i},t_{i+1})
we have,

\left\|\hat{X}_{t^{*}}-\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}\right\|_{E}\,\sim\,o(h^{1/2}).

(29)

Hence, using Lemma
3.5
each summand in
(I+II)
is estimated as

\displaystyle D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[(\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-t^{*}_{i}}-I)X_{t^{*}_{i}}+\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t_{i+1}-s}N(X_{s})\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=\,-D^{2}\pi(U^{5}_{i})\left[\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}},\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}\right]+O(h^{2})+\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}})-\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}})

\displaystyle=\,o(h)+\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i+1}})-\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}})\,=\,o(h)+hD\pi(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}})V(\hat{X}_{t^{*}_{i}}).

Noting that
\hat{X}_{t_{i}^{*}}=X_{t_{i}^{*}}
, summing over
\{t_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{M}
, and taking
h\rightarrow 0
yields the result.

We now show that
III\rightarrow\,\int_{0}^{t^{*}}D\pi(X_{s})B\,dW_{s}
.
To do so, note that a Taylor expansion of
\Lambda_{t_{i+1}-s}
about
s=t_{i+1}
yields

\Lambda_{t_{i+1}-s}\,=\,I+L\Lambda_{u}(t_{i+1}-s)

(30)

for some
u\in(s,t_{i+1})
.
Hence, for arbitrary
x\in\Gamma_{\delta}

\displaystyle D\pi(x)\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle=\,\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}D\pi(x)\left(I+(t_{i+1}-s)L\Lambda_{u}\right)B\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle=\,D\pi(x)B[W_{t_{i+1}^{*}}-W_{t_{i}^{*}}]+\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}(t_{i+1}-s)D\pi(x)L\Lambda_{u}B\,dW_{s}.

By Lemma
3.5
,
D\pi(x)L\Lambda_{u}B:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a bounded linear operator,
which we may identify with an element of
H
.
Hence, applying the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy from
[
29
]
,

\displaystyle\ExpOp_{x}\left[\left\|\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}(t_{i+1}-s)D\pi(x)L\Lambda_{u}B\,dW_{s}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,h\ExpOp_{x}\left[\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}\left\|D\pi(x)L\Lambda_{u}B\right\|^{2}_{H^{m}}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,h^{2}\left\|D\pi(x)L\right\|^{2}\left\|B\right\|^{2}e^{-2\omega t_{i}^{*}}.

Therefore for small
h>0
and any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

D\pi(X_{t_{i}^{*}})\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B\,dW_{s}\,=\,D\pi(X_{t_{i}^{*}})B[W_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-W_{t^{*}_{i}}]+o\left(h^{2}\right).

Observing that
K=\lfloor t/h\rfloor
, we have the following in probability

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i=0}^{K}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B\,dW_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i=0}^{K}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})B[W_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-W_{t^{*}_{i}}]+o\left(h^{1/2}\right)\,\xrightarrow[h\rightarrow 0]{}\,\int_{0}^{t^{*}}D\pi(X_{s})B\,dW_{s}.

We now show that
VI\rightarrow\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})\left[Be_{k},Be_{k}\right]\,ds
as
h\rightarrow 0
.
By (
30
),

\displaystyle\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}Be_{k},\,\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}Be_{k}\right]\,ds

(31)

\displaystyle=\,\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg(D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[Be_{k},Be_{k}\right]+2D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[L\Lambda_{u}Be_{k},Be_{k}\right](t^{*}_{i+1}-s)

\displaystyle+D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[L\Lambda_{u}Be_{k},L\Lambda_{u}Be_{k}\right](t^{*}_{i+1}-s)^{2}\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle=\,(t^{*}_{i+1}-t^{*}_{i})\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[Be_{k},Be_{k}\right]+O(h^{2}).

Itô’s isometry and (
31
) then imply that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\ExpOp\Bigg[D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}\Lambda_{t_{i+1}^{*}-s}B\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}e_{k}\,d\beta_{s}^{k},\,\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}\Lambda_{t_{i+1}^{*}-s}B\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}e_{\ell}\,d\beta_{s}^{\ell},\right]

(32)

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[\Lambda_{t_{i+1}^{*}-s}Be_{k},\,\Lambda_{t_{i+1}^{*}-s}Be_{k}\right]\,ds\Bigg]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\,\left\|\pi\right\|_{C^{2}}\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\ExpOp\left[\left\|\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}\Lambda_{t_{i+1}^{*}-s}Be_{k}\,d\beta_{s}^{k}\right\|^{2}-\int_{t_{i}^{*}}^{t_{i+1}^{*}}\left\|\Lambda_{t_{i+1}^{*}-s}Be_{k}\right\|^{2}\,ds\right]\right|\,=\,0,

where we have used (
31
) to justify exchanging the sums and expectation in (
32
).
Taking (
31
) & (
32
) together, summing over
k\in\{1,\ldots K\}
, and letting
h\rightarrow 0
, we have convergence in probability of
VI
.
Finally,
IV
and
V
tend to zero as
h\rightarrow 0
by (
27
) and (
28
).
∎

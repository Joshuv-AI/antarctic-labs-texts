Quasiprobability distribution functions from fractional Fourier transforms
Jorge A. Anaya-Contreras, A. Zúñiga-Segundo, and Héctor M. Moya-Cessa ∗ *

Abstract
We show, in a formal way, how a class of complex quasiprobability distribution functions may be introduced by using the fractional Fourier transform. This leads to the Fresnel transform of a characteristic function instead of the usual Fourier transform. We end the manuscript by showing a way in which the distribution we are introducing may be reconstructed by using atom-field interactions.

1
Introduction

It has been already shown that quasiprobability distribution
functions may be reconstructed by the measurement of atomic
properties in ion-laser interactions
[
1
]
and two-level atoms
interacting with quantized fields
[
2
,
3
]
. Such measurements of the wave
function are realized usually by measuring atomic observables,
namely, the atomic inversion and polarization.

Although the first quasiprobability distribution functions were introduced in the quantum realm
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
7
]
, they may be also used to analyze classical signals
[
9
,
10
]
.

Ideal interactions, i.e., without taking into account an environment, have shown to lead to the reconstruction of the
Wigner function
[
3
]
by taking advantage of its expression in terms of the parity operator. However, the interaction of a system with its
environment
[
11
]
leads to
s
-prametrized quasiprobability distribution
functions
[
12
,
13
,
14
]

F(\alpha,s)=\frac{2}{\pi(1-s)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{s+1}{s-1}\right)^{k}\langle k|\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\hat{\rho}\hat{D}(\alpha)|k\rangle

(1)

where
\hat{D}(\alpha)=\exp(\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\hat{a})
, with
\hat{a}
and

\hat{a}^{\dagger}
the annihilation and creation operators of the
harmonic oscillator, respectively, is the Glauber displacement
operator
[
15
]
. The state
\hat{D}(\alpha)|k\rangle=|\alpha,k\rangle
is a so-called displaced number state
[
16
]
. Note that, in order to reconstruct a given quasiprobability function it is needed to do displace the system by an amplitude
\alpha
and then measure the diagonal elements of the displaced density matrix.

The parameter
s
defines different orderings and therefore different quasiprobability distribution functions (QDF). The Glauber-Sudarshan
P
-function
[
15
,
17
]
is given for
s=1
, and is used to obtain averages of functions of
normal
ordered creation and annihilation operators;
s=-1
gives the Husimi
Q
-function, used to obtain averages of functions of
anti-normal
ordered creation and annihilation operators, while
s=0
is used for the
symmetric
ordering and gives the Wigner function.

Equation (
1
) may be rewritten as

F(\alpha,s)=\frac{2}{\pi(1-s)}Tr\left\{\left(\frac{s+1}{s-1}\right)^{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\hat{\rho}\hat{D}(\alpha)\right\},

(2)

that, by using the commutation properties under the symbol
of trace, and the system in a
pure
state
|\psi\rangle
, may be casted into

\displaystyle F(\alpha,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi(1-s)}Tr\left\{\hat{D}(\alpha)\left(\frac{s+1}{s-1}\right)^{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\hat{\rho}\right\}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi(1-s)}\langle\psi|\hat{D}(\alpha)\left(\frac{s+1}{s-1}\right)^{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)|\psi\rangle.

Recent studies have openned the possibility of measuring, instead
of observables, non-Hermitian operators
[
18
]
. It would be plausible
then that such measurements could be related to complex
quasiprobability distributions like the McCoy-Kirkwood-Rihaczek-Dirac
distribution functions
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
19
]
.

In this contribution we would like to introduce other kind of
complex quasiprobabilities that, although they could be introduced
simply by taking
s
as a complex number, we introduce them in a
formal way by considering the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT)
[
20
,
21
,
22
]

of a signal. Then, by writing the Dirac-delta function in terms of
its FrFT, we are able to write a general expression for complex
quasiprobability distributions in terms of the Fresnel transform.
Indeed, the representation of these complex quasiprobability
distributions in terms of a Fresnel transform implies that they
are solutions of a paraxial wave equation
[
3
]
. Finally, by using an effective Hamiltonian for the atom-field interaction, we show how this quasiprobability distribution function may be reconstructed.

2
Fractional Fourier Transform

Up to a phase, the fractional Fourier Transform of a signal
\psi(x)
can be written by the following expression
[
20
,
21
,
22
]

\mathcal{F}_{\omega}\left[\psi(x)\right]=\exp\left(-i\omega\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\right)\psi(x)\;,

(4)

that may be expressed in terms of an integral transform as

\mathcal{F}_{\omega}\left[\psi(x)\right]=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}K(x,x^{\prime};\omega)\psi(x^{\prime}),

(5)

where

K(x,x^{\prime};\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i}}\sqrt{\frac{e^{i\omega}}{\sin\omega}}\exp\left[i\frac{x^{2}}{2}\cot\omega+i\frac{x^{\prime 2}}{2}\cot\omega-ixx^{\prime}\csc\omega\right]\,.

(6)

Then, if we consider equation (
6
) as a propagator, Dirac’s delta distribution function takes the form

\displaystyle\delta(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime\prime}K(x,x^{\prime\prime};-\omega)K(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};\omega)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\sin\omega}e^{i\frac{x^{\prime 2}}{2}\cot\omega-i\frac{x^{2}}{2}\cot\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime\prime}e^{ix^{\prime\prime}(x-x^{\prime})\csc\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\exp\left[i\frac{x^{\prime 2}}{2}\cot\omega-i\frac{x^{2}}{2}\cot\omega\right]\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime\prime}\exp\left[ix^{\prime\prime}(x-x^{\prime})\right]\,.

Now, if we apply the fractional Fourier transform to the Dirac delta function we obtain

\mathcal{F}_{\omega}\left[\delta(x-y)\right]=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}K(x,x^{\prime};\omega)\delta(x^{\prime}-y)=K(x,y;\omega).

(8)

Then, applying the inverse fractional Fourier transform to equation (
8
) we obtain an alternative representation of the Dirac delta distribution function

\displaystyle\delta(x-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{-\omega}\left[\mathcal{F}_{\omega}\left[\delta(x-y)\right]\right]=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime\prime}K(x,x^{\prime\prime};-\omega)K(x^{\prime\prime},y;\omega)

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\exp\left[i\frac{y^{2}}{2}\cot\omega-i\frac{x^{2}}{2}\cot\omega\right]\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\exp\left[ix^{\prime}(x-y)\right]\,.

From the above equation it may be seen that there is a phase multiplying the usual integral representation of the Dirac delta function, that although could be omitted by using properties of the delta function, we keep in order to obtain a quasiprobability distribution function as a fractional Fourier (Fresnel) transform of the characteristic function.

3
Probability distribution in the phase space

We define
J(q,p)
a probability distribution in the phase space as

J(q,p)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dq^{\prime}dp^{\prime}\mathcal{P}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})\delta(q^{\prime}-q)\delta(p-p^{\prime})\,,

(10)

then, using equation (
9
), the distribution
J(q,p)
may be rewritten as

\displaystyle J(q,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}e^{i\frac{q^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha-i\frac{p^{2}}{2}\cot\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dudv\,e^{iup-ivq}\,\times

(11)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle{Tr}\left\{\hat{\rho}e^{iv\hat{q}-iu\hat{p}+i\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2}\cot\beta-i\frac{\hat{q}^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha}\right\}\,,

and because

\displaystyle e^{iv\hat{q}-iu\hat{p}+i\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2}\cot\beta-i\frac{\hat{q}^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{u^{2}}{2}\tan\beta}e^{i\frac{v^{2}}{2}\tan\alpha}e^{iu\tan\beta\hat{q}}e^{-iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}}\times

(12)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2}\cot\beta-i\frac{\hat{q}^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha}e^{iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}}e^{-iu\tan\beta\hat{q}}\,,

equation (
11
) takes the form

\displaystyle J(q,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}e^{i\frac{q^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha-i\frac{p^{2}}{2}\cot\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dudv\,e^{iup-ivq}e^{-i\frac{u^{2}}{2}\tan\beta}e^{i\frac{v^{2}}{2}\tan\alpha}\,\times

(13)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}e^{iu\tan\beta\hat{q}}e^{-iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}}e^{i\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2}\cot\beta-i\frac{\hat{q}^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha}e^{iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}}e^{-iu\tan\beta\hat{q}}\right\}\,,

that by using the equivalence

e^{iu\tan\beta\hat{q}}e^{-iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}}=e^{\frac{i}{2}uv\tan\alpha\tan\beta}e^{iu\tan\beta\hat{q}-iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}}\,,

(14)

may be casted into the expression

\displaystyle J(q,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\,e^{i\frac{q^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha-i\frac{p^{2}}{2}\cot\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dudv\,e^{iup-ivq}e^{-i\frac{u^{2}}{2}\tan\beta}e^{i\frac{v^{2}}{2}\tan\alpha}\,\times

(15)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}e^{iu\tan\beta\hat{q}-iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}}e^{i\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2}\cot\beta-i\frac{\hat{q}^{2}}{2}\cot\alpha}e^{iv\tan\alpha\hat{p}-iu\tan\beta\hat{q}}\right\}\,.

3.1
Case
\cot\alpha=-\cot\beta=\pi

The above quasiprobability distribution function is defined for a range of parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
, however, for the sake of simplicity, we will consider the case
\cot\alpha=-\cot\beta=\pi
.

We may relate the quasiprobability distribution function
J(q,p)
to the Wigner function, by noting that, for
\cot\alpha=-\cot\beta=\pi
, equation (
15
) has the form

\displaystyle J(q,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}i}e^{i\pi\left(\frac{p^{2}}{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dudv\,e^{iup-ivq}e^{i\frac{u^{2}}{2\pi}+i\frac{v^{2}}{2\pi}}\times

(16)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}e^{-i\frac{u\hat{q}}{\pi}-i\frac{v\hat{p}}{\pi}}(-1)^{\hat{n}}e^{i\frac{u\hat{q}}{\pi}+i\frac{v\hat{p}}{\pi}}\right\}.

According to trace representation of Wigner function
[
14
]

W\left(\frac{v}{\pi},-\frac{u}{\pi}\right)=Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,\frac{1}{\pi}\,e^{-i\frac{u\hat{q}}{\pi}-i\frac{v\hat{p}}{\pi}}(-1)^{\hat{n}}e^{i\frac{u\hat{q}}{\pi}+i\frac{v\hat{p}}{\pi}}\right\},

(17)

we write the distribution
J(q,p)
as the Fresnel transform of the Wigner function

J(q,p)=\frac{1}{4\pi i}e^{i\pi\left(\frac{p^{2}}{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dudv\,e^{iup-ivq}e^{i\frac{u^{2}}{2\pi}+i\frac{v^{2}}{2\pi}}W\left(\frac{v}{\pi},-\frac{u}{\pi}\right)\,.

(18)

It is easy to show that the quasiprobability distribution (
18
) can be normalized

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dqdp\,J(q,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dxdy\,\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dpe^{ixp}\right]\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dqe^{-iyq}\right]e^{-i\frac{x^{2}}{2\pi}-i\frac{y^{2}}{2\pi}}Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,e^{iy\hat{q}-ix\hat{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dxdy\,\delta(x)\delta(y)e^{-i\frac{x^{2}}{2\pi}-i\frac{y^{2}}{2\pi}}Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,e^{iy\hat{q}-ix\hat{p}}\right\}=\frac{\pi}{2}Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\right\}=\frac{\pi}{2}\,.

(19)

Therefore, for normalization reasons, the quasiprobability distribution is finally given in the form

J(q,p)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,dv\,e^{iup-ivq}e^{-i\frac{u^{2}}{2\pi}-i\frac{v^{2}}{2\pi}}Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,e^{iv\hat{q}-iu\hat{p}}\right\}\,,

(20)

that, by applying the change of variables
\beta=u/\sqrt{2}+iv/\sqrt{2}
takes the form

J(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int d^{2}\beta\,e^{\alpha\beta^{*}-\alpha^{*}\beta}e^{-\frac{i}{\pi}|\beta|^{2}}Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,\hat{D}(\beta)\right\}\,,

(21)

with
\alpha=q/\sqrt{2}+ip/\sqrt{2}
.

From the above expression it is direct to show that the Wigner
function

W(\alpha)=\int d^{2}\beta e^{\alpha\beta^{*}-\alpha^{*}\beta}\{\hat{\rho}\hat{D}(\beta)\},

(22)

and the function
J(\alpha)
may be easily related by the
differential relation

J(\alpha)=\exp\left\{\frac{i}{\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha\partial\alpha^{*}}\right\}W(\alpha).

(23)

The above quasiprobability function may be written as a trace by noting that

\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int d^{2}\beta\,\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\pi}|\beta|^{2}\right)\hat{D}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2i+\pi}\left(\frac{2i-\pi}{2i+\pi}\right)^{\hat{n}}

(24)

that leads to the trace representation of
J(q,p)

J(q,p)=\frac{1}{2i+\pi}Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,\hat{D}(\alpha)\,\left(\frac{2i-\pi}{2i+\pi}\right)^{\hat{n}}\,\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\right\}\,.

(25)

Last equation allows us to show that
J(q,p)
is correctly normalized, for this we do the double integration

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}J(q,p)dqdp

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\frac{2}{\pi+2i}\int\,d^{2}\alpha\hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{D}^{\dagger}\left(\alpha e^{i\theta}\right)e^{i\theta\hat{n}}\right\}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,\hat{A}\,e^{i\theta\hat{n}}\right\}\,,

where we have defined

e^{i\theta}=\frac{2i-\pi}{2i+\pi}\,,

(27)

and

\displaystyle\hat{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi+2i}\int d^{2}\alpha\,e^{i\sin\theta|\alpha|^{2}}\hat{D}\left(\alpha\left(1-e^{i\theta}\right)\right)

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int\int d^{2}z_{1}d^{2}z_{2}\left|z_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle z_{2}\right|B(z_{1},z_{2},z^{*}_{1},z^{*}_{2})\,,

with

\displaystyle B(z_{1},z_{2},z^{*}_{1},z^{*}_{2})=\frac{2}{\pi+2i}\int d^{2}\alpha\,e^{i\sin\theta|\alpha|^{2}}\langle z_{1}|\hat{D}\left(\alpha\left(1-e^{i\theta}\right)\right)|z_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{2\langle z_{1}|z_{2}\rangle}{\pi+2i}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\,d\alpha_{x}\exp\left(-\left(1-e^{i\theta}\right)\alpha_{x}^{2}+\alpha_{x}\left(\left(1-e^{i\theta}\right)z^{*}_{1}-\left(1-e^{-i\theta}\right)z_{2}\right)\right)\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\,d\alpha_{y}\exp\left(-\left(1-e^{i\theta}\right)\alpha_{x}^{2}+i\alpha_{y}\left(\left(1-e^{i\theta}\right)z^{*}_{1}+\left(1-e^{-i\theta}\right)z_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\langle z_{1}|z_{2}\rangle}{\pi+2i}\frac{\pi}{1-e^{i\theta}}\left[-\left(1-e^{-i\theta}\right)z^{*}_{1}z_{2}\right]=\left(-\frac{|z_{1}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\left|z_{2}e^{-i\theta}\right|^{2}}{2}+z^{*}_{1}\left(e^{-i\theta}z_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\langle z_{1}|e^{-i\theta\hat{n}}|z_{2}\rangle\,.

(29)

By replacing equation (
3.1
) into equation (
28
) we obtain

\hat{A}=e^{-i\theta\hat{n}}\,,

(30)

that shows that equation (
26
) is correctly normalized

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}J(q,p)dqdp=Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\,e^{-i\theta\hat{n}}\,e^{i\theta\hat{n}}\right\}=Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}\right\}=1\,.

(31)

Kirkwood distribution and
J(q,p)
distribution

Being the QDF
J(q,p)
and Kirkwood distributions complex functions we show now some differences between them.

The Kirkwood distribution is defined as
[
8
,
19
,
23
,
24
]

K(q,p)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,dv\,e^{iup-ivq}e^{i\frac{uv}{2}}Tr\left\{\hat{\rho}e^{iv\hat{q}-iu\hat{p}}\right\}\,,

(32)

or an alternative way to write it as an expectation value

25

K(q,p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}e^{\frac{q^{2}}{2}+\frac{p^{2}}{2}+iqp}\langle-i\sqrt{2}p|e^{\frac{\hat{a}^{2}}{2}}\hat{\rho}e^{-\frac{\hat{a}^{\dagger\,2}}{2}}|\sqrt{2}q\rangle.

(33)

4.1
Number state

The Kirkwood
K(q,p)
and
J(q,p)
distributions for number state
\left|n\right\rangle
, are represented by the following equations

K_{n}(q,p)=\frac{i^{n}}{2^{n}n!\pi\sqrt{2}}e^{-\frac{q^{2}}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{2}+iqp}H_{n}(q)H_{n}(p)

(34)

and

\displaystyle J_{n}(q,p)=\frac{1}{2i+\pi}\left(\frac{2i-\pi}{2i+\pi}\right)^{n}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi(q^{2}+p^{2})}{2i+\pi}\right)L_{n}\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}(q^{2}+p^{2})}{4+\pi^{2}}\right)\,,

(35)

where,
H_{n}(x)
and
L_{n}(x)
are Hermite and Laguerre polynomials, respectively.

\displaystyle K_{\pm}(q,p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{q^{2}}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{2}+iqp\right)}{2\pm 2\mathrm{Re}\langle\alpha_{1}|\alpha_{2}\rangle}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}_{1}+p^{2}_{1})+(qq_{1}-pp_{1})\right)\times

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{q^{2}}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{2}+iqp\right)}{2\pm 2\mathrm{Re}\langle\alpha_{1}|\alpha_{2}\rangle}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}_{2}+p^{2}_{2})+(qq_{2}-pp_{2})\right)\times

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\frac{i}{2}q_{2}(p_{2}+2p)+\frac{i}{2}p_{2}(q_{2}-2q)\right)

\displaystyle\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{q^{2}}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{2}+iqp\right)}{2\pm 2\mathrm{Re}\langle\alpha_{1}|\alpha_{2}\rangle}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}_{1}+p^{2}_{2})+(qq_{1}-pp_{2})\right)\times

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\frac{i}{2}q_{2}(p_{2}+2p)+\frac{i}{2}p_{1}(q_{1}-2q)\right)

\displaystyle\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{q^{2}}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{2}+iqp\right)}{2\pm 2\mathrm{Re}\langle\alpha_{1}|\alpha_{2}\rangle}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}_{2}+p^{2}_{1})+(qq_{2}-pp_{1})\right)\times

respectively.

5
Reconstruction of distribution
J(\alpha)

It is not difficult to show that the real part of QDF
J(\alpha)
may be measured. This can be achieved by measuring the atomic polarization in the dispersive interaction
between an atom and a quantized field
[
3
]
, whose
Hamiltonian reads

\hat{H}=-\chi\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\hat{\sigma}_{z},

(40)

with
\hat{\sigma}_{z}=|e\rangle\langle e|-|g\rangle\langle g|
, the
Pauli matrix corresponding to the atomic inversion operator, where

|g\rangle
and
|e\rangle
represent the ground and excited
states of the two-level atom. The parameter

\chi
is the dispersive coupling constant. The above Hamiltonian yields the evolution operator

\hat{U}(t)=\exp\{-i\chi t\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\hat{\sigma}_{z}\},

(41)

from where we can obtain the evolved wavefunction
|\psi(t)\rangle=\hat{U}(t)|\psi(0)\rangle
, that allows the calculation of averages of different observables.

The average of observable
\hat{\sigma}_{x}=|e\rangle\langle g|+|g\rangle\langle e|
then can be obtained for an arbitrary
initial field, which we conveniently write as

|\psi_{F}(0)\rangle=\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)|\phi(0)\rangle
and the atom is
initially in a superposition of atomic states,
|\psi_{A}(0)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|g\rangle+|e\rangle)
. Then we write

\langle\hat{\sigma}_{x}(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle\phi(0)|\hat{D}(\alpha)\exp\{2i\chi t\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\}\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)|\phi(0)\rangle+c.c.\right).

(42)

It is also easy to show that the imaginary part of the QDF may be
associated to the observable
\hat{\sigma}_{y}=i(|e\rangle\langle g|-|g\rangle\langle e|)

\langle\hat{\sigma}_{y}(t)\rangle=\frac{i}{2}\left(\langle\phi(0)|\hat{D}(\alpha)\exp\{2i\chi ta^{\dagger}a\}\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)|\phi(0)\rangle-c.c.\right).

(43)

If we set the interaction time
t=\frac{\arctan\frac{4\pi}{\pi^{2}-4}}{2\chi}
, we obtain that

Re\{J(\alpha)\}\propto\langle\hat{\sigma}_{x}\rangle,\qquad Im\{J(\alpha)\}\propto\langle\hat{\sigma}_{y}\rangle.

(44)

Therefore, by measuring the polarizations
\hat{\sigma}_{x}
and
\hat{\sigma}_{y}
we are able to measure the QDF
J(\alpha)
.

6
Conclusions

We have introduced a set of parametrized (in terms of
\alpha
and
\beta
) quasiprobability
distribution functions, equation (
15
), by using the fractional Fourier transform.
This has lead us to generalize QDF to Fresnel transforms of the characteristic function instead of their usual Fourier transforms. We
have also shown how such QDF may be recosntructed in the dispersive
atom-field interaction. We have also given a (differential)
relation that allows the calculation of the newly introduced QDF
from the Wigner function.

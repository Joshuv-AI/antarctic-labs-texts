Orientably-Regular p p -Maps and Regular p p -Maps


Abstract
A map is called a p p -map if it has a prime p p -power vertices. An orientably-regular (resp. A regular ) p p -map is called solvable if the group G + G^{+} of all orientation-preserving automorphisms (resp. the group G G of automorphisms) is solvable; and called normal if G + G^{+} (resp. G G ) contains the normal Sylow p p -subgroup. In this paper, it will be proved that both orientably-regular p p -maps and regular p p -maps are solvable and except for few cases that p ∈ { 2 , 3 } p\in\{2,3\} , they are normal. Moreover, nonnormal p p -maps will be characterized and some properties and constructions of normal p p -maps will be given.

Introduction

A
map
is a cellular decomposition of a closed surface.
An alternative way to describe maps is to consider them as cellular embeddings of graphs into closed surfaces.
By an
automorphism of a map

{\cal M}
we mean an automorphism of the
underlying graph

{\cal G}

which extends to a self-homeomorphism of the surface.
These automorphisms form a subgroup
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
of the automorphism group
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal G})
of
\cal G
.
It is well-known that
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
acts semi-regularly on the set of all flags (in most cases, which are incident vertex-edge-face triples).
If the action is regular, then we call the map as well as the corresponding embedding
regular
.

In the case of orientable supporting surface, if the group
\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
of all orientation-preserving
automorphisms of
{\cal M}
acts regularly on the set of arcs (incident vertex-edge pairs) of
{\cal M}
, then we call
{\cal M}
an
orientably-regular
map.
Such maps fall into two classes: those that admit also orientation-reversing automorphisms,
which are called
reflexible
, and those that do not, which are called
chiral
. Therefore, a reflexible map is a regular map but a chiral map is not.

One of the central problems in topological graph theory is to classify all the regular maps of given underlying graphs.
In a general setting, the classification problem was treated in
[
8
]
.
However, the problem has been solved only for a few particular classes of graphs.
For instance, complete graphs, complete bipartite graphs, complete multipartite graphs,
n
-dimensional cubes
Q_{n}
and so on.
Moreover, there are closed connections between regular maps and other mathematical theories such as group theory, hyperbolic geometry and complex curves and so on.
During the past forty years, there have been abundant references on regular maps, see
[
13
,
15
,
22
]
for an overview.

Definition 1.1

A map is called a
p
-map if the number of vertices is
p^{k}
, where
p
is prime and
k\geq 1
.

In this paper, we shall concentrate on orientably-regular
p
-maps and regular
p
-maps.

Many known orientably-regular maps and regular maps are
p
-maps. For instance, every orientably-regular map of a complete graph must be a
p
-map, see
[
13
]
.
Every
n
-dimensional hypercube
Q_{n}
contains
2^{n}
vertices, whose orientably-regular maps and nonorientable regular maps were classified in
[
1
]
and
[
17
]
, respectively.
Every regular map of complete bipartite graph
K_{2^{e},2^{e}}
has
2^{e+1}
vertices and a classification of orientably-regular embeddings of this graph played an important role in a final classification of
K_{n,n}
, see
[
3
,
4
,
14
,
16
]
. Orientably-regular maps whose automorphism group
G
is nilpotent can be concluded to that when
G
is a 2-group and of course, these maps have a 2-power vertices, see
[
2
,
19
]
.

One will see that the studies of
p
-maps
is very closed to that of finite
p
-groups and its automorphisms as well. In other words, the studies of
p
-maps stimulate us to focus on some related
p
-groups and
it might pose some new research problems for group theorists.

Regular embeddings of simple graphs of order
p
,
p^{2}
and
p^{3}
have been classified, see
[
5
,
6
,
23
]
. During the studying of these maps, we obtained some new observations, ideas, methods and conjectures.
this motivates us to pay more attention on
p
-maps. The aim of this paper is to give a basic theoretical characterization for orientably-regular
p
-maps and regular
p
-maps.

As usual, by
\mathbb{D}_{k}
,
O_{p}(G)
and
H\lhd G
, we denote the dihedral group of order
k
, the maximal normal
p
-subgroup of
G
, and a normal subgroup
H
of
G
, respectively.
First we introduce the following concepts.

Definition 1.2

An orientably-regular (resp. A regular )
p
-map
{\cal M}
is called solvable if
\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
(resp.
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
) is solvable; and called normal if

\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
(resp.
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M}))
contains the normal Sylow
p
-subgroup.

Remark 1.3

Suppose that
{\cal M}
is an orientably-regular
p
-map which is reflexible. Then it is regular. Since
|\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M}):\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})|=2
, it follows that
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
is solvable if and only if

\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
is solvable. Take a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
. If
p\neq 2
, then
P\lhd\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
if and only if
P\lhd\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
.
If
p=2
, then
P\lhd\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
implies that
P\cap\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
is the normal Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
. However, the converse is not true.
For example, consider the regular tetrahedron: the Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})\cong A_{4}
is normal but every Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})\cong S_{4}
is nonormal.

Now we are ready to state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1.4

Let
{\cal M}
be an orientably-regular
p
-map or a regular
p
-map. Then

(1)

{\cal M}
is solvable;

(2)

{\cal M}
is normal, except for the following two cases:

(2.1)

p=2
,
G/O_{2}(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rtimes\mathbb{D}_{4}
, where

is odd.

(2.2)

p=3
,
G/O_{3}(G)\cong S_{4}
.

It follows from Theorem
1.4
that an orientably-regular
p
-map or a regular
p
-map can be nonnormal only if

or 3. To give a description in details for these nonnormal
p
-maps, we
introduce three families of (multi)graphs and maps as well.

(i) By
{\cal D}_{d}
we denote the
d
-dipoles, which is the graph of two vertices joined by
d
parallel edges. Every orientably-regular map of
{\cal D}_{d}
is isomorphic to

(see
[
20
]
), where

and

(ii) By

we denote the

and

in a disc and a sphere, respectively (see

(iii) By

Theorem 1.5

Suppose that

is a nonnormal orientably-regular

-map. Let

or

For a normal orientably-regular

2
Preliminary Results

In this section, we shall give a brief description for orientably-regualr and regular maps and list some known group theoretical results used in this paper.

2.1
Regular maps and orientably-regular maps

(1)
Regular Maps.

A regular map can be described in the following way.

Definition 2.1

For a given finite set
F
and three fixed-point-free involutory permutations
t,r,\ell
on
F
, a quadruple

{\cal M}={\cal M}(F;t,r,\ell)
is called a
combinatorial map
if they satisfy two conditions:
(1)
t\ell=\ell t
; (2) the group
\langle t,r,\ell\rangle
acts transitively on
F.

For a given combinatorial map
{\cal M}={\cal M}(F;t,r,\ell),

F
is called the
flag
set,

t,r,\ell
are called
transversal, rotary, and longitudinal involution,
respectively.
The group
\langle t,r,\ell\rangle
is called the
monodromy group
of
{\cal M}
, denoted by
\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})
.
We define the
vertices, edges
and
face-boundaries
of
{\cal M}
to be the
orbits of the subgroups
\langle t,r\rangle
,
\langle t,\ell\rangle
and
\langle r,\ell\rangle
, respectively.
The incidence in
{\cal M}
is defined by a nontrivial set intersection.
To ensure that the underlying graph has no multiple edges, we let
\langle t,r\rangle\cap\langle t,r\rangle^{\ell}=\langle t\rangle
.

The map
{\cal M}
in Definition
2.1
is
unoriented
. Clearly, the even-word subgroup
\langle tr,r\ell\rangle

of
\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})
has index at most 2. If the index is 1, then
{\cal M}
is said to be
nonorientable
.
If the index is 2, then one may fix an orientation for
{\cal M}
and so
{\cal M}
is said to be

orientable
, while the group
\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
of all orientation-preserving automorphisms
acts transitively on all arcs is exactly
\langle tr,r\ell\rangle.

Given two maps
{\cal M}_{1}={\cal M}(F_{1};t_{1},r_{1},\ell_{1})
and
{\cal M}_{2}={\cal M}_{2}(F_{2};t_{2},r_{2},\ell_{2}),
a bijection
\phi
from
F_{1}
to

F_{2}
is called a
map isomorphism
if
\phi t_{1}=t_{2}\phi,

\phi r_{1}=r_{2}\phi
and
\phi\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}\phi.
In particular,
if
{\cal M}_{1}={\cal M}_{2}={\cal M},
then
\phi
is called an
automorphism
of
{\cal M}.

The automorphisms of
{\cal M}
form a group
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
which is called the
automorphism group
of the map
{\cal M}
. By the definition of map isomorphism, we have
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})=C_{S_{F}}(\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})),
the centralizer of
\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})
in

S_{F}
. It follows from the transitivity of
\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})
on
F
that
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
acts semi-regularly on
F.
If the action is
regular, we call the map
{\cal M}

regular
. As a consequence of a result in permutation group theory
(see
[
11
, I.Theorem 6.5]
), we get that for a regular map
{\cal M},
the two associated permutation groups
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
and
\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})

can be viewed as the right regular representations
R(G)
and left regular representations
L(G)
of an abstract group
G\cong\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})\cong\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})
mutually centralizing each other in
S_{F}
.
If there is no confusion we also identify the elements
R(g)
and
L(g)
with the elements
g\in G
,
so that
{\cal M}\cong{\cal M}(G;t,r,\ell)
, which is called an
algebraic map
.
Thus the subgroups
\langle t,r\rangle
,
\langle t,\ell\rangle
and
\langle r,\ell\rangle
, respectively, stand for the
stabilizer of a vertex, an edge and a face which are mutually incident.
Finally, two maps
{\cal M}(G;t_{1},r_{1},\ell_{1})\cong{\cal M}(G;t_{2},r_{2},\ell_{2})
if and only if there
exists an automorphism
\sigma
of
G
such that
t_{1}^{\sigma}=t_{2},

r_{1}^{\sigma}=r_{2}
and
\ell_{1}^{\sigma}=\ell_{2}
(see
[
15
]
).

(2)
Orientably-regular maps.

An orientably-regular map can be described in the following direct way.

Definition 2.2

For a given finite set
D
and two fixed-point-free permutations
r,\ell
on
D
where
\ell
is an involutory, a triple

{\cal M}={\cal M}(D;r,\ell)
is called a
combinatorial orientable map
if
\langle r,\ell\rangle
acts transitively on
D
.

For a given map
{\cal M}={\cal M}(D;r,\ell),

D
is called the
arc
set,

r,\ell
are called
local rotation and arc-revision involution,
respectively.
The group
\langle r,\ell\rangle
is called the
monodromy group
of
{\cal M}
, denoted by
\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})
.
We define the
vertices, edges
and
face-boundaries
of
{\cal M}
to be the
orbits of the cyclic subgroups
\langle r\rangle
,
\langle\ell\rangle
and
\langle r\ell\rangle
, respectively.
The incidence in
{\cal M}
is defined by a nontrivial set intersection.

Similarly, one may define isomorphisms and automorphisms of
{\cal M}
and know that

\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
acts semi-regularly on
D
. If the action is
regular, we call the map
{\cal M}

regular
. In this case,
the two associated permutation groups
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
and
\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})

can be viewed as the right regular representations
R(G)
and left regular representations
L(G)
of an abstract group
G=\langle r,\ell\rangle\cong\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})\cong\hbox{\rm Mon}({\cal M})
mutually centralizing each other in
S_{D}
so that the map is denoted by
{\cal M}(G;r,\ell)
.
Moreover, two maps
{\cal M}(G;r_{1},\ell_{1})\cong{\cal M}(G;r_{2},\ell_{2})
if and only if there
exists an automorphism
\sigma
of
G
such that
r_{1}^{\sigma}=r_{2}
and
\ell_{1}^{\sigma}=\ell_{2}
.

-Maps

From now on let

be an orientably-regular (resp. regular)

-map of order

and valency

, where

. Let

and

(resp.
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})

Pick up a vertex

\langle r,t\rangle

The following lemma is a well-known fact and here we give a proof for a sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.1

P
acts transitively on
V
so that
G=PG_{v}=PH
.

Proof
Set
|G|=p^{n}m
where
(p,m)=1
. For any

. Since

|PG_{v}|=|P||G_{v}|/|P\cap G_{v}|\geq p^{n}m=|G|
, forcing that

.

In this section we show Theorem
1.4
.(1), that is the solvability of
{\cal M}.

To do that, it suffices to prove the following two lemmas formulated in the language of abstract groups.

Lemma 3.2

Let
G
be a group having a cyclic subgroup

of index a

-power. Then

is solvable.

Proof
As mentioned before,
PH=G=HP
and both

and

are nilpotent. By Proposition

again,

is solvable.

Lemma 3.3

Let
G
be a group having a subgroup
H\cong\mathbb{D}_{2n}
of index a
p
-power. Then
G
is solvable.

Proof
Now
G=PH
, where

. In what follows, we deal with two cases when

and

is odd, separately.

Case 1:

Let

be the maximal subgroup of odd order in

. Then

is cyclic and

. By Proposition
2.4
,
G
is solvable.

Case 2:
p
is odd.

For the contrary, suppose
G
is insolvable. Let
G
be a minimal counterexample.
Suppose that
G
is nonabelian simple. In Lemma
2.5
, a classification of finite nonabelian simple groups containing a subgroup

of index

is given. Checking it, we know that
the subgroup

cannot be a dihedral group. So in what follows, we assume that
G
is not simple.
Then
G
contains a normal subgroup

such that

. We shall show the solvability of

by showing that both

and

Step 1:
The solvability of

.

From
|G:H|=|G:HN||HN:H|=p^{k}
for some integer

, we know that
|G:HN|
is a
p

|G/N:HN/N|=|G:HN|
, it follows that

has a subgroup

of index a

-power..

Since

and

If

Step 2:
The solvability of

Note that

First suppose that

Second suppose that

For some terminologies and notations used below, the readers may see them in the end of Section 1.

Lemma 4.1

Suppose

such that
\ell
is an involution,
|r|
is even and
|G:\langle r\rangle|=p^{k}
, where
p
is odd. Then

contains a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup.

Proof
Let
H=\langle r\rangle
and
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
so that
G=PH
and
|r|
is even. By Lemma
2.7
,
G
is solvable. For the contrary, assume that
P
is nonnormal in
G

O_{p}(G)<P
.
Let
\overline{G}=G/O_{p}(G)
. Then
O_{p}(\overline{G})=1
. Set
H_{1}
be the
p^{\prime}
-Hall subgroup of
H
. Then
\overline{G}=\overline{P}\overline{H}=\overline{P}\overline{H}_{1}

Since
O_{p}(\overline{G})=1
and
|G|=p^{k}|H|

F(\overline{G})
of
\overline{G}
is contained in
\overline{H}_{1}
. Since
\overline{G}
is solvable and
\overline{H}_{1}
is cyclic, it follows from Proposition
2.7
(ii) that
\overline{H}_{1}\leq C_{\overline{G}}(F(\overline{G}))\leq F(\overline{G})
, which gives
F(\overline{G})=\overline{H}_{1}.
In particular,

\overline{H}_{1}\lhd\overline{G}
, forcing
C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{H}_{1})\unlhd\overline{G}
.

Since
\overline{H}\leq C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{H}_{1})

C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{H}_{1})
contains a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\overline{G}
. Since

2
-subgroups of
\overline{G}
and
then contains all involutions of
\overline{G}
. Now we have
\overline{H},\overline{\ell}\leq C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{H}_{1}),

, which implies
\overline{P}\unlhd\overline{P}\overline{H}_{1}=\overline{G}
, that is

, a contradiction.

Lemma 4.2

Suppose
G=\langle t,r,\ell\rangle
such that
t,r,\ell
are involutions,
t\ell=\ell t
and
\langle r,t\rangle\cong\mathbb{D}_{2n}
where
|G:\langle r,t\rangle|=p^{k}
for an odd prime

.
Then

is isomorphic to either
S_{4}
or a Sylow
2
-group of

. Moreover, if

G/O_{2^{{}^{\prime}}}(G)\not\cong S_{4}
.

Proof
Suppose
4\nmid|G|
. Then
|G|=2m
for some odd integer

and so
G
has a unique subgroup of order
m
, that is
O_{2^{\prime}}(G)
. Clearly,
G/O_{2^{\prime}}(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, as desired. Remark that
this group corresponds to a degenerated case for regular maps when
\ell=t
.

Suppose
4||G|
. Then
G
contains a dihedral Sylow
2
-subgroup. By Proposition
2.6
, we get
G/O_{2^{{}^{\prime}}}(G)
isomorphic to
A_{7}
, a group
N
such that
\hbox{\rm PSL}(2,q)\leq N\leq\hbox{\rm P$\Gamma$L}(2,q)
with

being an odd prime power or a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
.

Since
G
is solvable and
\hbox{\rm PSL}(2,q)
is simple when

, the first case was excluded and the second case happens only if

or
{\rm P\Gamma L}(2,3)\cong S_{4}
. Note that
A_{4}
cannot be generated by its involutions, we get that
G/O_{2^{{}^{\prime}}}(G)
is isomorphic to either
S_{4}
or a Sylow
2
-subgroup of

.

Suppose that

where

Lemma 4.3

With the notations in Lemma
4.2
, suppose that
G/O_{2^{{}^{\prime}}}(G)
is isomorphic to a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
, where

is odd. Then

contains a normal Sylow

-subgroup.

Proof
Under the hypothesis, we have

where

is a characteristic subgroup of odd order of

and

which is either

, where

is the
\{p,2\}^{\prime}
-Hall subgroup of

Remind that by Lemma

Assume that

. Then
P=L\lhd G
. So in what follows, we assume that

.
Let
\overline{G}=G/O_{p}(G)
. For the contrary, suppose that
P\ntriangleleft G
, that is
\overline{P}\neq\overline{1}
. Then we first derive the following two facts:

Fact 1:
F(\overline{L})={\overline{H}_{1}.}

Form
\overline{G}=G/O_{p}(G)=\overline{L}\overline{H},
we get
O_{p}(\overline{L})=1
, which in turn implies the Fitting subgroup
F(\overline{L})
of
\overline{G}
is contained in

-Hall subgroups of
\overline{L}
.
Since

is a
p^{\prime}
-Hall subgroup of

and also a

-Hall subgroup of

.
Since
\overline{G}
is solvable and
\overline{H}_{1}

.(ii) that

O_{p^{\prime}}(\overline{L})\leq\overline{H}_{1}\leq C_{\overline{L}}(O_{p^{\prime}}(\overline{L}))\leq C_{\overline{L}}(F(\overline{L}))\leq F(\overline{L})\leq O_{p^{\prime}}(\overline{L}),

\overline{H}_{1}=F(\overline{L})\lhd\overline{G}.

Fact 2: There exists a
\overline{Q}\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{q}(F(\overline{L}))
for some

2p\mid|\overline{G}:C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{Q})|

.

Since

, we get

\overline{Q}\hbox{\rm\,char\,}F(\overline{L})\hbox{\rm\,char\,}\overline{L}\lhd\overline{G}

\overline{Q}\unlhd\overline{G}

. Therefore,

contains one of Sylow
p
-subgroups (resp. Sylow 2-subgroups) if and only if it contains all Sylow
p
-subgroups (resp. all involutions).
Since in
\overline{H}\cong\mathbb{D}_{2n}

\overline{P}

Come back to our proof. By Proposition
2.3

N_{\overline{G}}(\overline{Q})/C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{Q})=\overline{G}/C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{Q})\lessapprox\hbox{\rm Aut}(\overline{Q}).

In this section, we deal with 3-maps.

Lemma 5.1

Suppose that

is an orientably-regular 3-map or a regular

Proof
From Lemma

Following Lemma

The proof will be divided into the following two steps.

Step 1: Show

and
{\cal M}
is nonnormal and nonorientable.

Since
G/O_{2^{\prime}}(G)\cong S_{4}
, we get

where

and
H_{1}
is the
\{3,2\}^{\prime}
-Hall subgroup of

, which is cyclic. Let

. Then

. Since
(|LH_{1}/L|,2\cdot 3)=1

. Since

If

. Therefore

Suppose that

.

Suppose

. Then there exists a prime divisor

where

. Set
\overline{G}=G/O_{3}(G)
.
Then we first show the following two facts.

Fact 1:

The proof is completely the same as that in Fact 1 of Lemma
4.3

Fact 2: For any
\overline{Q}\in Syl_{q}(F(\overline{L}))

, we have
6\mid|\overline{G}:C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{Q})|

By Fact 1,

Let
\overline{Q}\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{q}(F(\overline{L}))

.

Since
\overline{Q}\hbox{\rm\,char\,}F(\overline{L})\hbox{\rm\,char\,}\overline{L}

\overline{Q}\unlhd\overline{G}
,

\overline{C}:=C_{\overline{G}}(\overline{Q})\unlhd\overline{G}.

Since
[\overline{Q},\overline{S}]\neq\overline{1}

is not contained in
\overline{C}
. By the normality of
\overline{C}
, we know that
\overline{C}
cannot contain any Sylow 2-subgroup of
\overline{G}

Suppose that
\overline{C}

must be trivial. In particular,

Note that

7
Affine
p
-Maps

By Theorem
1.4
, every orientably-regular
p
-map or regular
p
-map is normal, with some exceptions for
p=\{2,3\}
. In this section, we concentrate on normal
p
-maps.

Suppose
{\cal M}
is a normal orientably-regular
p
-map or a normal regular
p
-map and let
G=\hbox{\rm Aut}^{+}({\cal M})
or
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
. Take
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Then

P\lhd G
. In general case, a
p
-group may contain some characteristic subgroups, which induce blocks on the set
V
of all vertices, in other words, the induced action of
G^{V}
on
V
is imprimitive.
Therefore, it is a framework to determine normal
p
-maps such that
G^{V}
is primitive.

We use
c(P)
to denote the nilpotent class of
P
and set
\Omega_{1}(P):=\langle x\in P|x^{p}=1\rangle
, note that
\Omega_{1}(P)=\{x\in P|x^{p}=1\}
when
p>2
and
c(P)\leq 2
. Recall that
H_{G}
denotes the core of
H
in
G
.

Proposition 7.1

Suppose that
{\cal M}
is a normal orientably-regular
p
-map with
p^{k}
vertices where
k\geq 2
. Let
G=\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})=\langle r,\ell\rangle
,
H=\langle r\rangle
a point stabilizer,
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
and
P_{0}=H_{G}\cap P
.
Suppose that
G^{V}
is primitive. Then we have

(1)

P=P_{0}\times T
, where
T\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{p}
; or

(2)

P=E\ast P_{0}
, where
P_{0}=Z(P)
and
E
is an extra-special
p
-group, where
E
can be chosen as
\Omega_{1}(P)
if
p>2
. In particular,
k
is always even in this case.

Proof
Suppose that
G^{V}
acts primitively on
V
. Since
G
is solvable,
G^{V}
is solvable too. By Proposition
2.8
,
G^{V}
must be of affine type and
V
can be identified with a vector space of dimension
k
. Thus
\langle\overline{r}\rangle
acts irreducibly on
V
,
|\overline{r}|\bigm|{p^{k}-1}
and
|\overline{r}|\nmid{p^{s}-1}
for any
1\leq s<k
, which implies

P/P_{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{p}
so that
G/H_{G}=\overline{P}\rtimes\langle\overline{r}\rangle
. Moreover,
P_{0}\leq Z(G)
, as
H/H_{G}\leq C_{G}(P_{0})/H_{G}\lhd G/H_{G}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{k}\rtimes H/H_{G}
, a primitive group.

In what follows we deal with two cases separately.

(1)
P
is abelian

Since
P
is abelian,
P_{0}
is cyclic and
P/P_{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{k}
, we know that either
P=P_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{p}
or
P=P_{1}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}_{p}
where
P_{1}
is cyclic and
|P_{1}:P_{0}|=p
. In fact, the second case cannot happen. Otherwise,

\Omega_{1}(P)
is a characteristic subgroup of
P
, which induces blocks. By the primitivity of
G^{V}
, we get that either
\Omega_{1}(P)
fixes
V
pointwise which means
\Omega_{1}(P)\leq P_{0}
or
\Omega_{1}(P)
acts transitively on
V
, but these two cases cannot happen. So
P=P_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{p}
, as shown in (1) of the lemma.

(2)
P
is nonabelian

Since a characteristic abelian subgroup is not transitive on
V
and it induces blocks on
V
, it must be contained in
P_{0}
. In particular,
Z(P)\leq P_{0}
, and then
Z(P)=P_{0}
, which is cyclic. By Proposition
2.10
we deduce that
P
is a central product of two groups
S
and
E
, where
S
and
E
are given below according to
p>2
and
p=2
, separately.

Step 1:
p>2
.

By Proposition
2.10
,
S
is a cyclic subgroup and
E
is an extra-special
p
-group of exponent
p
.

If
S=1
, then
P
is an extra-special
p
-group, as desired. Suppose that
S>1
. First note that
S\leq Z(P)=P_{0}
, we thus have
Z(P)=S(Z(P)\cap E)
by Dedekind’s lemma. But from
1<|Z(P)\cap E|\leq|Z(E)|=p
, we derive that
Z(P)\cap E\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, which implies that
S=Z(P)=P_{0}
. Since
{\rm exp}(E)=p
, we have
E\leq\Omega_{1}(P)
by definition. If there are elements
e\in E,s\in S
such that
|es|\leq p
, then we must have both
|e|\leq p
and
|s|\leq p
. Since any element
s\in S
with
|s|\leq p
is contained in
E
, we conclude that any element of order
p
in
P
lies in
E
, thus
\Omega_{1}(P)=E
, as required.

Step 2:
p=2

By Proposition
2.10
,
E
is either identity or an extra-special
2
-group; and
S
is either a cyclic
2
-group or a
2
-group of maximal class.

If
|P_{0}|=2
, then
P
is an extra-special
2
-group and we can write
P=P_{0}\ast P
. So we suppose that
|P_{0}|\geq 4
from now on. If
P
is a cyclic
2
-group or a maximal class
2
-group, then
P
would contain an index
2
characteristic cyclic subgroup containing
P_{0}
which is neither transitive on vertices nor contained in the core of
H
, a contradiction. Thus
E
is an extra-special
2
-group.

Now we show that
S
can not be a maximal class
2
-group. Assume the contrary. Set
P_{0}=\langle x\rangle
, where
x=es
for some
e\in E
and
s\in S
. Then, we have
e\in Z(E),s\in Z(S)
and either
|e|\geq|x|\geq 4
or
|s|\geq|x|\geq 4
, but the center of both an extra-special
2
-group and a maximal class
2
-group are of order
2
, a contradiction.
Thus
S
is cyclic and clearly we have
S\leq P_{0}
.

Finally, we show that
S=P_{0}
. Since
S\leq P_{0}
, we have
P_{0}=S(P_{0}\cap E)
. Since
P_{0}=Z(P)
, we must have
1<|P_{0}\cap E|\leq|Z(E)|=2
, which implies that
P_{0}\cap E\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and then
S=P_{0}
.

Note that
P/P_{0}\cong E/Z(E)
and the order of an extra-special
p
-group is always of the form
p^{2m+1}
for some positive integer
m
, the statement
k
is even follows, as required.

Proposition 7.2

Suppose that
{\cal M}
is a normal regular
p
-map with
p^{k}(k\geq 2)
vertices such that
G=\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal M})
acts primitively on vertices. Let

be a point stabilizer,

. Then

is even and either

(1)

; or

(2)

, where

and

-group.

Proof
Note that if
{\cal M}
is a normal regular
2
-map such that
G
acts primitively on vertices, then
G
is a
2
-group with
H
maximal which is of less interest, thus our assumption

excludes that possibility. Now
p
must be odd and the proofs for (1) and (2) are similar to those in Lemma
7.1
, reminding a slight difference here is that we need to invoke Proposition 2.3 to prove that
P_{0}=Z(P)
in case

is nonabelian. Here we only need to prove that

is even.
It suffices to consider the case
G
acts faithfully and primitively on vertices. Since
G
is solvable, Proposition
2.8
can be applied to show that

must be of affine type.

Write
G=T\rtimes G_{\alpha}
where

and

is a point (vector) stabilizer which is an irreducible subgroup of

. Let

be the cyclic subgroup of

with index 2.

Suppose that

fixes some nonzero proper subspace of

with minimal dimension, then

is also a subspace fixed by

for some

by assumption on

and irreducibility of

implies that

and then

is even.

Now we may suppose that

is irreducible on

[
11
, page 196,theorem 3.10]

is conjugate to a subgroup of some Singer subgroup. However, the normalizer of any such subgroup in

is isomorphic to the semilinear group

where

is a cyclic subgroup of order

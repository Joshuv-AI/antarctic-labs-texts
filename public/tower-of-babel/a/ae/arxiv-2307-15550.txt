Complex hyperbolic Gromov-Thurston metrics and almost 1 4 \frac{1}{4} -pinched Kähler manifolds
Barry Minemyer

Abstract
. In this paper we construct an almost negatively 1 4 \frac{1}{4} -pinched Riemannian metric on a class of compact manifolds that, via previous work, was already known to be Kähler and not locally symmetric. This is the first known example of such manifolds and, via the result of Hernandez [ 7 ] and Yau and Zheng [ 14 ] , these manifolds cannot admit a negatively quarter-pinched Riemannian metric. This metric is also interesting because it is the complex hyperbolic analogue to the famous pinched metric constructed by Gromov and Thurston in [ 6 ] .



1.
Introduction

Via Mostow rigidity
[
10
]
one knows that any compact Kähler manifold with negatively
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched holomorphic sectional curvature (meaning the curvatures of all complex lines lie in the interval
[-1,-1/4]
) must be isometric to a quotient of the complex hyperbolic space
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Even more surprisingly, by Hernandez
[
7
]
and Yau and Zheng
[
14
]
it holds that any Kähler manifold with a negatively
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched
Riemannian
metric must likewise be isometric to a locally symmetric manifold.

Mostow rigidity of course holds in the hyperbolic case, but there are now various examples of Riemannian manifolds whose sectional curvatures are pinched arbitrarily close to
-1
, but which are not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold.
The first known examples were the branched cover manifolds constructed by Gromov and Thurston in
[
6
]
, but one now obtains many more examples from Ontaneda’s smooth hyperbolization
[
12
]
.

Very little is known about pinched metrics in the Kähler setting.
If a Kähler metric has holomorphic sectional curvatures that are
\varepsilon
-close to being negatively
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched, meaning that all curvatures lie in the
\varepsilon
neighborhood of
[-1,-1/4]
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, then the underlying manifold must be “close” to being locally symmetric in a sense made explicit by Dereax and Seshadri in
[
4
]
.
But it is unknown if any such manifolds exist (outside of the locally symmetric ones).
In light of the results in
[
7
]
and
[
14
]
, it is natural to ask if there are even any Kähler manifolds which admit an almost negatively
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched Riemannian metric but are not locally symmetric?
The main result of this paper is the construction of the first known examples in this latter category.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist Kähler manifolds that admit a Riemannian metric that is
\varepsilon
-close to being negatively
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched and are not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold.

A few quick remarks about the Theorem.
As mentioned in the abstract, the Riemannian metric in Theorem
1.1
is the complex hyperbolic analogue to the famous Gromov-Thurston metric in
[
6
]
.
The Riemannian metric from the Theorem is generally not Kähler.
Also, for the remainder of this paper we use the interval
(-4-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon)
instead of
(-1-\varepsilon,-1/4+\varepsilon)
purely for notational convenience.
One can adjust the curvatures between these two intervals by appropriately scaling the metric.

The new construction in this paper is the almost negatively
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched metric in Theorem
1.1
.
The remainder of the Theorem can be obtained from the literature as follows.
Following Stover and Toledo in
[
13
]
, let
\Gamma<\text{Isom}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n})
be a cocompact congruence arithmetic lattice of simple type, and let
M=\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
It is known that such a manifold
M
contains an immersed (complex) codimension one totally geodesic submanifold
N
.
One can then find a finite congruence cover
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
of
(M,N)
with
N^{\prime}
embedded in
M^{\prime}
.
Let
M^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Our almost
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched Riemannian metric will require a large normal injectivity radius about the totally geodesic submanifold.
Suppose that, for a fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we require a normal injectivity radius of
R
.
By
[
5
]

M^{\prime}
contains only finitely many homotopy classes of closed geodesics with length less than
R
.
Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is residually finite, we can pass to a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
where we remove elements of
\Gamma^{\prime}
that correspond to each of these closed geodesics.
In the resulting pair
(M^{\prime\prime},N^{\prime\prime})
,
N^{\prime\prime}
will still be embedded and will now have normal injectivity radius of at least
R
.
Let us quickly note that this is the only place where we require
M
to be compact.
If the submanifold
N
can be constructed to have a sufficiently large normal injectivity radius, then the ambient manifold
M
in this construction need not be compact.

By Theorem 5.1 of
[
13
]
there exists an integer
d>2
and a further finite cover
(\tilde{M},\tilde{N})
of
(M^{\prime\prime},N^{\prime\prime})
such that the
d
-fold ramified branched cover
X
of
\tilde{M}
about
\tilde{N}
is a smooth manifold.
This manifold
X
is the manifold described in Theorem
1.1
.
Note that
\tilde{N}
will still have a normal injectivity radius of at least
R
within
\tilde{M}
, and therefore the ramification locus will still have a normal injectivity radius of at least
R
in
X
.
Stover and Toledo in
[
13
]
also prove that
X
is not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold (Theorem 1.5 in
[
13
]
).
The fact that
X
admits a Kähler metric is due to Zheng in
[
15
]
.

The metric constructed by Zheng in
[
15
]
actually has negative holomorphic sectional curvature (and thus all sectional curvatures are negative), but this metric generally cannot be altered to be almost
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched.
Since this metric is Kähler, if it were close to
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched then the ratio of the Chern numbers of manifolds which contain this metric would be close to the same ratios of complex hyperbolic manifolds by
[
4
]
.
But Zheng’s metric can be applied to the surfaces developed by Hirzebruch in
[
8
]
.
Hirzebruch computes formulas for the ratio of the two Chern numbers for these surfaces, and for
n\neq 1
they differ from that of a complex hyperbolic manifold (see also Section 3 of
[
15
]
).
Zheng’s metric is the regular complex hyperbolic metric outside of a small tubular neighborhood of the ramification locus of the branched cover, and has a small additive “fix” within this tubular neighborhood.
But, for the degree
d
large, any hope of constructing a metric with pinched curvature would require one to fix a large angle defect in a small interval while keeping tight control of the sectional curvature tensor.
As such, the author believes that any almost
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched metric on
X
will require a modification on a large normal injectivity radius of the ramification locus.

Lastly, Stover and Toledo do not compute the Chern numbers of
X
when proving that
X
is not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold.
They instead give an argument based on the normal bundle of the ramification locus.
So it is unknown if the bounds on the difference between the ratio of Chern numbers of
X
and of a complex hyperbolic manifold developed in
[
4
]
are satisfied.
It seems likely to the author that there exists an example of a branched cover
X
which satisfies Theorem
1.1
but whose ratio of chern numbers is not close to that of a complex hyperbolic manifold, but again this is not known.

Outline of the construction of the metric
g

In what follows, all constructions are at the level of the universal cover.
The constructed metric will descend to the manifold
X
as long as we end up with the pullback metric by the end of the normal injectivity radius of the ramification locus.
We also assume that the ramification locus is connected, and thus is a copy of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
in the universal cover.
If it is disconnected, then we just apply the constructed metric about each component.

Let us quickly review the metric constructed by Gromov and Thurston in
[
6
]
.
Let

\lambda=h^{2}\mathfrak{h}_{n-2}+v^{2}d\theta^{2}+dr^{2}.

where
\mathfrak{h}_{n-2}
denotes the hyperbolic metric on the core copy of
\mathbb{H}^{n-2}
.
The
h^{2}\mathfrak{h}_{n-2}
component of
\lambda
is the horizontal fiber and is tangent to the
r
-tube about the base copy of
\mathbb{H}^{n-2}
.
It is well-known that
\lambda
is the hyperbolic metric
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
written in polar coordinates about a totally geodesic codimension two copy of
\mathbb{H}^{n-2}
if we choose the warping functions
h(r)=\cosh(r)
and
v(r)=\sinh(r)
.
If we let
h(r)=\cosh(r)
and
v(r)=d\sinh(r)
then
\lambda
will be the pullback metric on the entire branched cover, which will be everywhere Riemannian except on the ramification locus.
At these points the metric will have a cone angle of
2d\pi
.

If one chooses an orthonormal basis for
\lambda
at any point and computes the components of the curvature tensor (Theorem
2.1
below), one first sees that all mixed terms vanish.
In the curvature formulas for the coordinate planes, every appearance of
v
happens in the denominator of a fraction.
There is then a
v^{\prime}
or a
v^{\prime\prime}
in the numerator of each of these fractions.
So whether
v
is equal to
\sinh(r)
or
d\sinh(r)
, the curvatures are still identically equal to
-1
.
Gromov and Thurston then define
v(r)=\sigma(r)\sinh(r)
where
\sigma(r):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function that very slowly increases from
1
to
d
.
For
\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
sufficiently small this produces a pinched negatively curved metric which is hyperbolic about a tube of the ramification locus, and which slowly warps to the full hyperbolic metric on each page of the branched cover.

The metric that we construct on the
d
-fold branched cover of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
about a copy of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
is similar, but the situation is now much more complicated.
The associated warped product metric is

\mu=h^{2}\mathfrak{c}_{n-1}+\frac{1}{4}v^{2}d\theta^{2}+dr^{2}.

where
\mathfrak{c}_{n-1}
denotes the complex hyperbolic metric on the core copy of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
.
A major difference between the hyperbolic and complex hyperbolic metrics is that the horizontal fiber is now not integrable, meaning that it is not everywhere tangent to the
r
-tube about
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
.
One obtains the complex hyperbolic metric
\mathfrak{c}_{n}
with the substitutions
h(r)=\cosh(r)
and
v(r)=\sinh(2r)
in the definition of
\mu
.
The non-integrability of this metric leads to nonzero structure constants (equation
2.3
below), which create (among other issues) non-zero mixed terms for the sectional curvature tensor (Theorem
2.2
below).

There now exist terms in various components of the curvature tensor where
v
appears without a corresponding
v^{\prime}
or
v^{\prime\prime}
.
But each of these terms contains a structure constant.
So the first part of our warping process (Theorem
3.2
below) is to slowly “unwind” the complex hyperbolic metric making all structure constants zero.
This is equivalent to slowly turning the horizontal fiber of the metric until it is tangent to the
r
-tube about the core copy of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-2}
.
By turning this fiber slowly we can keep the curvatures pinched near
[-4,-1]
.
The almost
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched metric in Theorem
1.1
is not Kähler, and this is the part of the process that is the reason for this.

The resulting metric has an integrable horizontal fiber like the hyperbolic metric, but the horizontal fiber is still a warped copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{n-1}
and the vertical fiber is still
\sinh(2r)
-warped.
We call this metric the
integrable complex hyperbolic metric

g_{I}
.
We can then increase the angle with this metric in an almost identical way as to what was described above with the Gromov-Thurston metric while keeping the curvature pinched (Theorem
3.5
).
This leaves us with a copy of the metric
g_{I}
on each page of the branched cover.

The final step is to “rewind” the metric
g_{I}
back to the complex hyperbolic metric
\mathfrak{c}_{n}
on each page of the branched cover (Corollary
3.3
).
This process is exactly backwards of the unwinding procedure discussed above.
This results in a copy of the original complex hyperbolic metric on each page of the branched cover, which will descend to a pinched negatively curved metric on
X
.

2.

\frac{1}{4}
-pinched curvature.

]

[

]

Curvature formulas for

\text{span}(X_{1}^{h},X_{2}^{h},\ldots,X_{2n-2}^{h})
in a manner that is independent of the choice of our original frame since
\alpha
only depends on
r
.
Thus, this metric is really well-defined on all of
E\times(0,\infty)
.
Also, note that
g=\mathfrak{c}_{n}
when
r<r_{1}
and
g=g_{I}
for
r>r_{2}
.

To show that the metric
g
is almost
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched, we need to compute the Lie brackets of the basis
(X_{i})
of the horizontal fiber.
We will show that, for
\delta
sufficiently small and
r_{1}
and
r_{2}-r_{1}
sufficiently large, we can
C^{2}
-approximate
g
by a metric which satisfies Lemma
2.3
.
Then, since sufficiently
C^{2}
-small changes in a Riemannian metric cause arbitrarily small changes to its sectional curvature, the proof will be complete.

To start, note that all vector fields are invariant with respect to
\theta
, and therefore
[X_{i},\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}]=0
for all
i
.
Next, consider all pairs
(\check{X}_{i},\check{X}_{j})
that are not a holomorphic pair (that is, where
\{i,j\}\neq\{k,k+1\}
for some odd
k
).
These vector fields were chosen independently of each other, and so
[X^{h}_{i},X^{c}_{j}]=0=[X^{c}_{i},X^{h}_{j}]
.
From this it follows that
[X_{i},X_{j}]=0
.

We now consider a holomorphic pair
(\check{X}_{i},\check{X}_{i+1})
.
In what follows we assume that the structure constant for this pair is
+2
instead of
-2
.
If the sign is reversed, then we just swap the order of the indices.
We have

\displaystyle[X_{i},

\displaystyle X_{i+1}]=[\alpha X_{i}^{c}+(1-\alpha)X_{i}^{h},\alpha X_{i+1}^{c}+(1-\alpha)X_{i+1}^{h}]

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}[X_{i}^{c},X_{i+1}^{c}]+\alpha(1-\alpha)\left([X_{i}^{c},X_{i+1}^{h}]+[X_{i}^{h},X_{i+1}^{c}]\right)+(1-\alpha)^{2}[X_{i}^{h},X_{i+1}^{h}]

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\alpha(1-\alpha)\left([X_{i}^{c},X_{i+1}^{h}]+[X_{i}^{h},X_{i+1}^{c}]\right)

To analyze the mixed Lie brackets in this equation, write
X_{i}^{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n-1}a_{i,j}X_{j}^{c}
for some locally defined coefficient functions
a_{i,j}
and where
X_{2n-1}^{c}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
.
When we plug this sum into the Lie bracket
[X_{i}^{h},X_{i+1}^{c}]
and evaluate at
q=(p,\theta,r)
, the only term that does not zero out is
[a_{i,i}X_{i}^{c},X_{i+1}^{c}]
since we chose our frame so that
[\check{X}_{i},\check{X}_{i+1}]_{p}=0
.
Therefore,

[X_{i}^{h},X_{i+1}^{c}]=a_{i,i}[X_{i}^{c},X_{i+1}^{c}]=2a_{i,i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}.

Note that, for
r
large, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}e^{2r}

\displaystyle\approx\mathfrak{h}_{2n}(X_{i}^{h},X_{i}^{h})\geq\mathfrak{h}_{2n}(a_{i,i+1}X_{i+1}^{c},a_{i,i+1}X_{i+1}^{c})

\displaystyle=(a_{i,i+1})^{2}\mathfrak{h}_{2n}(X_{i+1}^{c},X_{i+1}^{c})\approx(a_{i,i+1})^{2}\frac{1}{4}e^{2r}.

and therefore
|a_{i,i}|\leq 1+\gamma
for some
\gamma>0
small.
Analogously, this all also holds for the Lie bracket
[X_{i}^{c},X_{i+1}^{h}]
where we write
X_{i+1}^{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n-1}b_{i+1,j}X_{j}^{c}
.
We therefore have

\displaystyle\bigr|[X_{i},X_{i+1}]\bigr|

\displaystyle=\biggr|2\alpha^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\alpha(1-\alpha)\left(2b_{i+1,i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+2a_{i,i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq|2\alpha^{2}+4\alpha(1-\alpha)+\eta|\biggr|\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\biggr|

where
\eta
is a small error term due to
\gamma
above that can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
r
sufficiently large.

Note that, ignoring
\eta
, the coefficient within the absolute values on the right-hand side of the inequality reduces to
4\alpha-2\alpha^{2}
.
If we let
b(r)=4\alpha(r)-2\alpha^{2}(r)
, we see that
b(r_{1})=2
,
b(r_{2})=0
, and
b
is decreasing over
[r_{1},r_{2}]
.
Thus, the metric in equation (
3.1
) satisfies the condition on the structure constants for Lemma
2.3
.
One can check that, if the values for both
a_{i,i+1}
and
b_{i+1,i}
are both
-1
(or any values for each between
-1
and
1
), this coefficient still falls in the interval
(-2-\eta,2+\eta)
for sufficiently large
r
and sufficiently small
\gamma
.

The last Lie brackets that we need to consider are the Lie brackets of the form
[X_{i},\partial r]
where
\partial r=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
.
This is easier to consider with respect to an orthonormal basis
(Y_{i})
where
Y_{i}=\frac{1}{\cosh(r)}X_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 2n-2
.
We then compute

\displaystyle[Y_{i},\partial r]

\displaystyle=-\left[\partial r,\frac{\alpha}{\cosh(r)}X_{i}^{c}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\cosh(r)}X_{i}^{h}\right]

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\cosh(r)}-\frac{\alpha\sinh(r)}{\cosh^{2}(r)}\right)X_{i}^{c}-\left(\frac{-\alpha^{\prime}}{\cosh(r)}-\frac{(1-\alpha)\sinh(r)}{\cosh^{2}(r)}\right)X_{i}^{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\cosh(r)}(X_{i}^{h}-X_{i}^{c})+\tanh(r)Y_{i}.

With respect to both the hyperbolic and complex hyperbolic metric one has that
[Y_{i},\partial r]=\tanh(r)Y_{i}
.
So the “error term” in the above summand is
\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\cosh(r)}(X_{i}^{h}-X_{i}^{c})
.
If this term were equal to
0
, then Lemma
2.3
would apply and our proof would be complete.
But the vectors
X_{i}^{h}
and
X_{i}^{c}
grow at a rate of approximately
\cosh(r)
with respect to all of the metrics here.
So this error term can be made as small as possible by requiring that
\delta
be chosen sufficiently small.
We can then
C^{2}
-approximate this metric by one which satisfies Lemma
2.3
to complete the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exist positive constants
r_{3}<r_{4}
and a Riemannian metric
g
defined on
E\times[r_{3},\infty)
such that

(1)

g
restricted to
E\times\{r_{3}\}
is the metric
g_{I}
.

(2)

g
restricted to
E\times(r_{4},\infty)
is the metric
\mathfrak{c}_{n}
.

(3)

all sectional curvatures of
g
lie in the interval
(-4-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Just apply the procedure in the proof of Theorem
3.2
backwards.
Note that this metric is only defined on
E\times[r_{3},\infty)
for some
r_{3}>0
large since
g_{I}
may not have almost pinched negative curvature for small values of
r
.
∎

3.2.
The
d
-fold integrable complex hyperbolic metric
{\bf g_{d}}

Let
d\geq 2
be a positive integer.
The
d
-fold integrable complex hyperbolic metric

g_{d}
is defined as
h(r)=\cosh(r)
and
v(r)=d\sinh(2r)=2d\sinh(r)\cosh(r)
in equation (
2.5
), and setting all structure constants defined in (
2.3
) identically equal to zero.
So as a metric, we have that

g_{d}=\cosh^{2}(r)\mathfrak{c}_{n-1}+\frac{d^{2}}{4}\sinh^{2}(2r)d\theta^{2}+dr^{2}.

Note that this metric has a cone angle of
2d\pi
about the core copy of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
.
For the metric
g_{d}
to be complete, we need to restrict it to
E\times(r_{0},\infty)
for some
r_{0}>0
.

Remark 3.4
.

The curvature formulas for
g_{d}
are exactly the same as the curvature formulas for
g_{I}
.
When all structure constants are
0
in Theorem
2.2
, then the extra “d” that is inserted in any denominator with a
v
will cancel with the same
d
in the numerator that comes from either a
v^{\prime}
or a
v^{\prime\prime}
.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
we know that, given
\varepsilon>0
, all sectional curvatures of
g_{d}
lie in
(-4-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon)
for
r
sufficiently large.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exist positive constants
r_{2}<r_{3}
and a Riemannian metric
g
defined on
E\times[r_{2},\infty)
such that

(1)

g
restricted to
E\times\{r_{2}\}
is the metric
g_{I}
.

(2)

g
restricted to
E\times(r_{3},\infty)
is the metric
g_{d}
.

(3)

all sectional curvatures of
g
lie in the interval
(-4-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

This proof is very similar to the construction in Section 2 of
[
6
]
.
Define a function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to[1,d]
which satisfies

•

\sigma(r)=1
for all
r\leq r_{2}
.

•

\sigma(r)=d
for all
r\geq r_{3}
.

•

\sigma^{\prime}(r)<\delta
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(r)<\delta
for all
r
.

Given any fixed
\delta>0
, such a function
\sigma
clearly exists provided
r_{2}
and
r_{3}-r_{2}
are sufficiently large.

We now define the metric
g
on
E\times[r_{2},\infty)
to be the metric in Theorem
2.2
with the substitutions
h(r)=\cosh(r)
and
v(r)=\sigma(r)\sinh(2r)=2\sigma(r)\sinh(r)\cosh(r)
.
Using the notation of Theorem
2.2
, up to the symmetries of the curvature tensor, the nonzero components of the sectional curvature tensor with respect to
g
are

\displaystyle R_{i,j,i,j}=-1\hskip 40.0ptR_{i,2n,i,2n}=-1

\displaystyle R_{i,2n-1,i,2n-1}=-1-\tanh^{2}(r)-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\tanh(r)

\displaystyle R_{i,i+1,i,i+1}=-1-\frac{3}{\cosh^{2}(r)}\hskip 40.0ptR_{2n-1,2n,2n-1,2n}=-4-\frac{4\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\coth(r)-\frac{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}{\sigma}

\displaystyle R_{i,i+1,2n-1,2n}=R_{i,2n-1,i+1,2n}=R_{i,2n,i+1,2n-1}=0

\displaystyle R_{i,i+1,k,k+1}=2R_{i,k,i+1,k+1}=-2R_{i,k+1,i+1,k}=-\frac{2}{\cosh^{2}(r)}

Since
\tanh(r)
and
\coth(r)
both approach
1
as
r
approaches
\infty
, one sees that these curvature formulas approach those of the metric
g_{I}
for
\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
sufficiently small.
That is, for
\delta
chosen sufficiently small.
Thus, all sectional curvatures of
g
will lie in the interval
(-4-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon)
for appropriately chosen
r_{2}
,
r_{3}
, and
\delta
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Via the discussion in the Introduction, all that is left to prove is the construction of the metric
g
on the
d
-fold ramified cover of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
about
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
with all curvatures almost
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched.
This really follows immediately from Theorem
3.2
, Theorem
3.5
, and Corollary
3.3
, but we give a quick rigorous argument here.

Define
r_{1}
as in Theorem
3.2
.
Choose
r_{2}
to be the larger of those from Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.5
and
r_{3}
to be the larger of those from Theorem
3.5
and Corollary
3.3
.
Finally, choose
r_{4}
as in Corollary
3.3
as needed for the chosen
r_{3}
.
Let
g_{1}
be the metric from Theorem
3.2
, let
g_{2}
be the metric from Theorem
3.5
, and let
g_{3}
be the metric from Corollary
3.3
.

Define a metric
g
on
E\times(0,r_{3}]
by

g=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{c}_{n}&\text{if }r\leq r_{1}\\
g_{1}&\text{if }r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}\\
g_{2}&\text{if }r_{2}\leq r\leq r_{3}\end{cases}

and note that
g
is well-defined when

, and that all sectional curvatures of

lie in the interval

.

At this point, the metric

restricted to

has total angle of

about the core copy of
\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
.
Subdivide

into

.
Then for each

about

over all of

.
So to finish our construction of

On

Note that this agrees with the previous definition for

.
As long as the ramification locus of

∎

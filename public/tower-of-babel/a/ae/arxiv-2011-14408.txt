Residuation in twist products and pseudo-Kleene posets
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
M. Busaniche, R. Cignoli, C. Tsinakis and A. M. Wille showed that every residuated lattice induces a residuation on its full twist product. For their construction they used also lattice operations. We generalize this problem to left-residuated groupoids which need not be lattice-ordered. Hence, for the full twist product we cannot use the same construction. We present another appropriate construction which, however, does not preserve commutativity and associativity of multiplication. Hence we introduce so-called operator residuated posets to obtain another construction which preserves the mentioned properties, but the results of operators on the full twist product need not be elements, but may be subsets. We apply this construction also to restricted twist products and present necessary and sufficient conditions under which we obtain a pseudo-Kleene operator residuated poset.

Introduction

M. Busaniche and R. Cignoli (
[
2
]
) as well as by C. Tsinakis and A. M. Wille (
[
7
]
) showed that if
(L,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
is a residuated lattice then
\cdot
and
\rightarrow
can be used to define binary operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
on the full twist product of
(L,\leq)
such that the resulting structure becomes a residuated lattice again. For the construction of such operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
they used the lattice operations
\vee
and
\wedge
. When going from lattices to residuated posets, the natural question arises whether also in this case the corresponding twist product can be equipped with certain operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
(without using lattice operations) such that the resulting structure is residuated again. We solve this problem in the positive. We define suitable operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
on the full twist product such that the arising structure becomes a left-residuated groupoid again. Unfortunately, this construction does preserve neither commutativity nor associativity of the original structure
(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
. Hence, we try another approach where instead of operations we use certain operators
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
in such a way that the full twist product becomes an operator residuated poset and commutativity as well as associativity of the original operation
\cdot
are preserved. As the authors already showed in
[
4
]
, any poset
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,\leq)
can be embedded into a pseudo-Kleene poset
(P_{a}(\mathbf{Q}),\leq,{}^{\prime})
where
(P_{a}(\mathbf{Q}),\leq)
is a certain subposet of the full twist product of
\mathbf{Q}
. This motivated us to investigate whether our construction of the operators
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
can be extended also to this case, i.e. whether we can determine for a bounded commutative residuated monoid
(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,0,1)

\mathbf{Q}
can be embedded. We characterize those left-residuated posets for which our construction is possible.

2
Preliminaries

The concept of a Kleene lattice (alias Kleene algebra) was introduced by J. A. Kalman (
[
6
]
), see also
[
5
]
. Recall that a
Kleene lattice
is a distributive lattice
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
with an involution
′
satisfying the so-called
normality condition
, i.e. the identity

x\wedge x^{\prime}\leq y\vee y^{\prime}.

This concept was generalized by the first author in
[
3
]
:
\mathbf{L}
is called a
pseudo-Kleene lattice
if it satisfies the above identity, but it need not be distributive.

Let
(P,\leq)
be a poset,
a,b\in P
and
A,B\subseteq P
. Then the lower cone
L(A)
of
A
and the upper cone
U(A)
of
A
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle L(A)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid x\leq A\},

\displaystyle U(A)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid x\geq A\}.

Here
x\leq A
means
x\leq y
for all
y\in A
and, similarly,
x\geq A
means
x\geq y
for all
y\in A
. The expression
A\leq B
means
x\leq y
for all
x\in A
and
y\in B
. Instead of
L(\{a,b\})
and
L(U(A))
we simply write
L(a,b)
and
LU(A)
, respectively. Analogously, we proceed in similar cases. Let
\max A
denote the set of all maximal elements of
(A,\leq)
. A unary operation
′
on
P
is called

•

antitone
if
x,y\in P
and
x\leq y
imply
y^{\prime}\leq x^{\prime}
,

•

an
involution
if it satisfies the identity
x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
.

The concept of a pseudo-Kleene lattice was generalized by the authors in
[
4
]
for posets as follows:

A
pseudo-Kleene poset
is a poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime})
with an antitone involution satisfying the condition

L(x,x^{\prime})\leq U(y,y^{\prime})

for all
x,y\in P
. A
Kleene poset
is a distributive pseudo-Kleene poset. Recall that a
poset

(P,\leq)
is called
distributive
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent LU-identities:

\displaystyle L(U(x,y),z)

\displaystyle\approx LU(L(x,z),L(y,z)),

\displaystyle U(L(x,y),z)

\displaystyle\approx UL(U(x,z),U(y,z)).

In
[
4
]
it was shown that an arbitrary poset can be embedded into a pseudo-Kleene poset by means of the so-called
twist construction
:

The
full twist product
of a poset
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,\leq)
is the poset
(Q^{2},\leq)
where

(x,y)\leq(z,v)\text{ if and only if }x\leq z\text{ and }v\leq y

for all
(x,y),(z,v)\in Q^{2}
. We have

\displaystyle L((x,y),(z,v))

\displaystyle=L(x,z)\times U(y,v),

\displaystyle U((x,y),(z,v))

\displaystyle=U(x,z)\times L(y,v)

for all
(x,y),(z,v)\in Q^{2}
.

3
Left-residuated groupoids

We will investigate when a residuated poset can be transferred to a residuated full twist product. For this purpose we will use the twist construction. For residuated lattices such a transfer was already published in
[
2
]
by using a construction developed in
[
7
]
.

From now on, let
(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
denote a poset with constant
1
endowed with two binary operations
\cdot
and
\rightarrow
. For our next investigations, consider the following conditions.

(1)

x\leq y
implies
z\cdot x\leq z\cdot y
(right-isotony),

(2)

x\leq y
implies
x\cdot z\leq y\cdot z
(left-isotony),

(3)

x\cdot y\leq z

x\leq y\rightarrow z

(4)

x\leq y

z\rightarrow x\leq z\rightarrow y

(5)

x\leq y

y\rightarrow z\leq x\rightarrow z

(6)

x\cdot 1\approx x

for all

Lemma 3.1
.

For

(ii)

and
(6)

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in Q
.

(i)

If
a\leq b
then every one of the following statements implies the next one:

\displaystyle b\rightarrow c

\displaystyle\leq b\rightarrow c,

\displaystyle(b\rightarrow c)\cdot b

\displaystyle\leq c,

\displaystyle(b\rightarrow c)\cdot a

\displaystyle\leq c,

\displaystyle b\rightarrow c

\displaystyle\leq a\rightarrow c.

(ii)

We have
a\cdot b\leq a\cdot 1=a
and
a\cdot b=b\cdot a\leq b
.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
with associative
\cdot
to satisfy
(2)
and
(3)
. Then it satisfies

(8)

(x\cdot y)\rightarrow z\approx x\rightarrow(y\rightarrow z)
.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in Q
. Then every of the following statements implies the next one:

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\rightarrow c

\displaystyle\leq(a\cdot b)\rightarrow c,

\displaystyle((a\cdot b)\rightarrow c)\cdot(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle\leq c,

\displaystyle(((a\cdot b)\rightarrow c)\cdot a)\cdot b

\displaystyle\leq c,

\displaystyle((a\cdot b)\rightarrow c)\cdot a

\displaystyle\leq b\rightarrow c,

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\rightarrow c

\displaystyle\leq a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c).

Moreover, every one of the following statements implies the next one:

\displaystyle a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c)

\displaystyle\leq a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c),

\displaystyle(a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c))\cdot a

\displaystyle\leq b\rightarrow c,

\displaystyle((a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c))\cdot a)\cdot b

\displaystyle(a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c))\cdot(a\cdot b)

Together with

which follows from

we obtain

Now we show that every left-residuated groupoid naturally induces a left-residuated groupoid on its full twist product.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
be a poset with binary operations
\cdot
and
\rightarrow
and a constant
1

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(z,v)

\displaystyle:=(x\cdot f(z,v),g(z,v)\rightarrow y),

\displaystyle(x,y)\Rightarrow(z,v)

\displaystyle:=(f(x,y)\rightarrow z,v\cdot g(x,y))

\displaystyle(Q^{2},\leq,\odot,\Rightarrow,(a,b))\text{ satisfies (3)},

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(z,v)\leq(t,w)\text{ is equivalent to }(x,y)\leq(z,v)\Rightarrow(t,w),

\displaystyle(x\cdot f(z,v)\leq t\text{ and }w\leq g(z,v)\rightarrow y)\text{ is equivalent to}

\displaystyle\quad\quad(x\leq f(z,v)\rightarrow t\text{ and }w\cdot g(z,v)\leq y),

\displaystyle(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)\text{ satisfies (3)}.

\displaystyle(Q^{2},\leq,\odot,\Rightarrow,(a,b))\text{ satisfies (6)},

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(a,b)\approx(x,y),

\displaystyle(x\cdot f(a,b),g(a,b)\rightarrow y)\approx(x,y),

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1,1\rightarrow y)\approx(x,y),

\displaystyle x\cdot 1\approx x\text{ and }1\rightarrow y\approx y,

\displaystyle(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)\text{ satisfies (6) and (7)}.

4
Operator residuated posets

One can easily see that the left-residuated groupoid
(Q^{2},\leq,\odot,\Rightarrow,(a,b))
from Theorem
3.6
need neither be commutative nor associative even if
(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
has this property. Hence, we define the next concept.

Definition 4.1
.

An operator residuated poset is an ordered six-tuple
(Q,\leq,\odot,\Rightarrow,0,1)
such that

(i)

(Q,\leq,0,1)
is a bounded poset,

(ii)

\odot
and
\Rightarrow
are mappings from
Q^{2}
to
2^{Q}

(
so-called operators
)
,

(iii)

x\odot y\approx y\odot x
,

(iv)

\bigcup\limits_{u\in x\odot y}(u\odot z)=\bigcup\limits_{u\in y\odot z}(x\odot u)

(
operator associativity
)
,

(v)

x\odot y\leq z
if and only if
x\leq y\Rightarrow z

for all
x,y,z\in Q
.

The following result shows that when using an operator residuated structure on the full twist product, commutativity and associativity of the original bounded left-residuated groupoid are preserved.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(Q,\leq,\cdot,\rightarrow,0,1)
be a bounded commutative residuated monoid and

(Q^{2},\leq,\odot,\Rightarrow,(0,1),(1,0))

(Q^{2},\leq)
is the full twist product of
(Q,\leq)
and the operators
\odot
and
\Rightarrow

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(z,v)

\displaystyle:=\{(x\cdot z,x\rightarrow v),(x\cdot z,z\rightarrow y)\},

\displaystyle(x,y)\Rightarrow(z,v)

\displaystyle:=\{(x\rightarrow z,x\cdot v),(v\rightarrow y,x\cdot v)\}

is an operator residuated poset and the mapping

(Q,\leq)

(Q^{2},\leq)

Let

(i)

(ii)

and

are mappings from

to

We must prove (iii) – (v) of Definition

(iii)

We have

\displaystyle(a,b)\odot(c,d)

\displaystyle=\{(a\cdot c,a\rightarrow d),(a\cdot c,c\rightarrow b)\}=\{(c\cdot a,c\rightarrow b),(c\cdot a,a\rightarrow d)\}=

\displaystyle=(c,d)\odot(a,b).

(iv)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{(x,y)\in(a,b)\odot(c,d)}((x,y)\odot(e,f))

5
Pseudo-Kleene posets

It was shown by the authors in
[
4
]
that every poset
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,\leq)
can be embedded into a pseudo-Kleene one. For this we use a certain modification of the full twist product construction as follows.

Let
a\in Q
and define

\displaystyle P_{a}(\mathbf{Q})

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y)\in Q^{2}\mid L(x,y)\leq a\leq U(x,y)\},

\displaystyle(x,y)\leq(z,v)

\displaystyle(x,y)^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=(y,x)

Theorem 5.1
.

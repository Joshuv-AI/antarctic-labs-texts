On estimation of the convergence rate to invariant measures in Markov Branching Processes with possibly infinite variance and allowing immigration Thanks: © 2023 Imomov A.A
Azam A. IMOMOV

Abstract
. The paper discusses the continuous-time Markov Branching Process allowing Immigration. We are considering a critical case for which the second moment of offspring law and the first moment of immigration law are possibly infinite. Assuming that the nonlinear parts of the appropriate generating functions are regularly varying in the sense of Karamata, we prove theorems on convergence of transition functions of the process to invariant measures. We deduce the speed rate of these convergence providing that slowly varying factors are with remainder.

1.
Introduction

We continue our discussion of the population growth model called the continuous-time Markov Branching Process
allowing Immigration (MBPI), which we considered in the paper
[
5
]
. Recall that this process can
have a simple physical interpretation: the population size changes not only as a result of reproduction and
disappearance of existing individuals, but also at the random stream of inbound “extraneous” individuals of
the same type from outside. Namely, the process develops by the following scheme. Each individual existing at
time
t\in{\mathcal{T}}:=[0,+\infty)
independently of his history and of each other for a small time interval

(t,t+\varepsilon)
transforms into
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\backslash\{1\}
individuals with
probability
a_{j}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)
and, with probability
1+a_{1}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)

stays to live or makes evenly one descendant (as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
); where

\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}
is the set of natural numbers. Here
\left\{{a_{j}}\right\}

are intensities of individuals’ transformation that
a_{j}\geq 0
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\backslash\{1\}
and

0<a_{0}<-a_{1}=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\backslash\{1\}}{a_{j}}<\infty.

Independently of these
for each time interval
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
new individuals inter the population with probability

b_{j}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)
and, immigration does not occur with probability
1+b_{0}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)
.
Immigration intensities
b_{j}\geq 0
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

0<-b_{0}=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}{b_{j}}<\infty.

Newly arrived individuals undergo transformation in accordance with the reproduction law generated by intensities

\left\{{a_{j}}\right\}
; see
[
11
, p. 217]
. Thus, the process under consideration is completely
determined by infinitesimal generating functions(GFs)

f(s):=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}{a_{j}s^{j}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad g(s):=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}{b_{j}s^{j}}\qquad\parbox{68.28644pt}{for {} $s\in{[0,1)}$.}

Denote
X(t)
the population size at the time
t\in{\mathcal{T}}
in MBPI. This is homogenous
continuous-time Markov chain with state space
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
and transition functions

p_{ij}(t):=\mathbb{P}_{i}\bigl\{{X(t)=j}\bigr\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{X(t+\tau)=j\,\bigl|\,{X(\tau)=i}\bigr.\right\}

for all
i,j\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
\tau,t\in{\mathcal{T}}
.

We devote the paper to the critical case only, i.e.
f^{\prime}(1-)=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}{ja_{j}}=0
, and
observe limit behaviours of transition functions
p_{ij}(t)
as
t\to{\infty}
. Pakes
[
9
]
was one
of the first to study invariant measures for MBPI with finite variance and found an integral form of GF of invariant
measures. He has proved that limits
{\pi}_{j}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\lambda}{p_{ij}(t)}
exist independently on
j
, iff

\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}{a_{j}j^{2}\ln{j}}<\infty
and
\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}{b_{j}j\ln{j}}<\infty
,
where
\lambda={{2g^{\prime}(1-)}\bigl/{f^{\prime\prime}(1-)}\bigr.}
, besides the set
\left\{{\pi}_{j},j\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}
presents
an invariant measure for MBPI. In accordance with the results of the paper
[
7
]
the invariant measure of
MBPI can also be constructed by the strong ratio limit property of transition functions but slightly different in appearance.
Namely, the set of positive numbers
\bigl\{\upsilon_{j}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}{{p_{0j}(t)}/{p_{00}(t)}}\bigr\}
is an
invariant measure. Moreover it can be seen a close relation between the sets
\left\{{\pi}_{j},j\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}

and
\left\{\upsilon_{j},j\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}
, and their GFs
\pi{(s)}=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\pi_{j}s^{j}}

and
\mathcal{U}(s)=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\upsilon_{j}s^{j}}
. In fact, they are really only different versions
of the same limit law. So, it is easy to see
\mathcal{U}(s)=\pi{(s)}\bigl/\pi{(0)}\bigr.
, and this is consistent with
uniqueness, up to a multiplicative constant, of the invariant measure of MBPI.

In this issue an exceptional interest represents an estimation of the rate of convergence to invariant measures. In
our previous report
[
5
]
the rate of convergence of
t^{\lambda}{p_{ij}(t)}
to
{\pi}_{j}
, for all

i,j\in\mathcal{S}
, was studied under the condition
\max\bigl\{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(1-),g^{\prime\prime}(1-)\bigr\}<\infty
. It was
found ibidem that the convergence rate is
\mathcal{O}\bigl({{{\ln t}/t}}\bigr)
as
t\to{\infty}
.

Throughout the paper, we adhere to the following conditions concerning

f(s)
and
g(s)
which are our
Basic assumptions
:

[formula]

f(s)=(1-s)^{1+\nu}\mathcal{L}\left({{{1}\over{1-s}}}\right),

and

[formula]

g(s)=-(1-s)^{\delta}{\ell}\left({{{1}\over{1-s}}}\right)

, where

\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
,

[

]

[

. Basic assumptions imply that the offspring distribution belongs to
the domain of attraction of the
(1+\nu)
-stable law, and the immigration distribution belongs
to the domain of attraction of the
\delta
-stable law. Besides, by the criticality of our process,
assumption
[f_{\nu}]
implies that
2b:=f^{\prime\prime}(1-)=\infty
. If
b<\infty
then representation
[f_{\nu}]

holds with
\nu=1
and
\mathcal{L}(t)\to b
as
t\to\infty
. Similarly, GF
g(s)
of the form
[g_{\delta}]

generates the immigration law, having the
\delta
-order moment. But if
g^{\prime}(1-)<\infty
then the assumption

[g_{\delta}]
will be fulfilled with
\delta=1
and
\mathcal{\ell}(t)\to{g^{\prime}(1-)}
as
t\to\infty
.

By perforce we allow to forcedly put forward an additional requirement for
\mathcal{L}(x)
and
{\ell}(x)
. So we can write

[formula]

{{\mathcal{L}\left({\lambda x}\right)}\over{\mathcal{L}(x)}}=1+{\mathcal{O}}\bigl(\alpha(x)\bigr)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $x\to\infty$}

for each
\lambda>0
, where
\alpha(x)
is known positive decreasing function so that
\alpha(x)\to 0
as

x\to\infty
. In this case

is called

with remainder

Wherever we exploit the condition

we will suppose that

\alpha(x)={\mathcal{O}}\left({{{\mathcal{L}\left(x\right)}\over{x^{\nu}}}}\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $x\to\infty$.}

Similarly, we also allow a condition

[formula]

{{{\ell}\left({\lambda x}\right)}\over{{\ell}(x)}}=1+{\mathcal{O}}\bigl(\beta(x)\bigr)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $x\to\infty$}

for each

2.
Main Results

Put into consideration GF

{\mathcal{P}}_{i}(t;s):=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{p_{ij}(t)s^{j}}.

It is not difficult to see that (see
[
9
]
)

(2.1)

{\mathcal{P}}_{i}(t;s)=\Bigl(F(t;s)\Bigr)^{i}\exp\left\{{\int\limits_{0}^{t}{g\bigl({F(u;s)}\bigr)du}}\right\},

where
F(t;s)
is GF of Markov Branching Process initiated by single individual without immigration. Since

F(t;s)\to 1
as
t\to\infty
uniformly in
s\in[0,d]
,
d<1
(see Lemma
1
below), it suffice
to consider
{\mathcal{P}}(t;s):={\mathcal{P}}_{0}(t;s)
. Then under Basic assumptions, due to the
Kolmogorov’s backward equation
{\partial{F}}\bigl/{\partial{t}}\bigr.=f\left(F\right)
, from (
2.1
) follows

(2.2)

{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)=\exp\left\{{\int\limits_{s}^{F(t;s)}{{{g(u)}\over{f(u)}}\,du}}\right\}.

In view of Basic assumptions, the integrand

(2.3)

{{g(u)}\over{f(u)}}=-{\left(1-u\right)^{\gamma-1}\textsf{\emph{L}}{\left({1}\over{1-u}\right)}},

where
\gamma:=\delta-\nu
and

\textsf{\emph{L}}(t):={{\ell(t)}\over{\mathcal{L}(t)}}\,\raise 0.8pt\hbox{.}

All appearances, the three cases can be divided concerning the classification of
\mathcal{S}
, depending on a sign
of
\gamma
. By virtue of (
2.3
), integral
\int\nolimits_{s}^{1}{\left[{g(u)}\left/{f(u)}\right.\right]du}

converges if
\gamma>0
, and diverges if
\gamma<0
. Thus and so, as it was shown in
[
7
]
, that

\mathcal{S}
is
positive-recurrent
if
\gamma>0
, and it is
transient
if
\gamma<0
. The special
case
\gamma=0
implies that
g(s)=f^{\prime}(s)
and that
\textsf{\emph{L}}(t)\to 1+\nu
as
t\to\infty
. And we get
another population process called
Markov Q-process
instead of MBPI. We refer the reader to
[
4
]

and
[
6
]
for the details on the Markov Q-process; see also
[
1
, pp. 56–58]
and
[
8
]

for the discrete-time case.

Our main results appear only for the case
\gamma\neq 0
in the following two theorems. Put

{\tau}(t):={({\nu}t)^{1/\nu}\over{\mathcal{N}(t)}}\qquad\parbox{22.76228pt}{and}\qquad T(t):={\bigl(\tau(t)\bigr)}^{|\gamma|},

where
{\mathcal{N}}(x)
is
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
defined in Lemma
1
below.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\gamma>0
. Then
{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)
converges to the function

U(s)=\exp\left\{\int_{s}^{1}{\frac{g(u)}{f(u)}du}\right\}

for
s\in[0,1)
, and its power series expansion

U{(s)}=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{u_{j}s^{j}}
generates an invariant distribution

\left\{u_{j},j\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}
for MBPI. The convergence is uniform over compact subsets
of
[0,1)
. In addition, if assumptions
[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}]
and
[{\ell}_{\delta}]
hold, then

(2.4)

{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)=U(s)\left(1+{\Delta(t;s)}{{\mathcal{K}}}\bigl({\tau}(t)\bigr)\right),

where
{\mathcal{K}}(x)={\mathcal{L}}^{-{{\delta}/{\nu}}}(x){\ell(x)}
and the function

{\mathcal{N}}(x)
is
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
defined in (
3.2
) below, herewith

\Delta(t;s)={{\,1\,}\over{\gamma}}\,{{{1}}\over{\bigl(\lambda(t;s)\bigr)^{{\gamma}/\nu}}}+\mathcal{O}\left({{\ln\bigl[{\Lambda}({1-s})\lambda(t;s)\bigr]}\over{\bigl(\lambda(t;s)\bigr)^{{\delta/\nu}}}}\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

where
\lambda(t;s)=\nu{t}+{\Lambda}^{-1}\left({1-s}\right)

and
\Lambda(y)=y^{\nu}\mathcal{L}\left(1/y\right)
.
The transition functions are given by

(2.5)

p_{ij}(t)=u_{j}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left({{K(t)}\over{t^{\gamma/\nu}}}\right)\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

where
K(t)
is
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
.

Another asymptotic property comes out for
{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)
when
\gamma<0
.
We can easily verify that under Basic assumptions

-{{\,\ln{p_{00}(t)}\,}\over{T(t)}}\sim{{\,1\,}\over{|\gamma|}}{\textsf{\emph{L}}{\bigl(\tau{(t)}\bigr)}}\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$.}

This asymptotic formula shows that
{{\bigl({T(t)}\bigr)^{-1}}\ln{p_{00}(t)}}
is asymptotically
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
,
and it suggests that we should look for a limit as

of the function

.
First wee need to discuss
{\textrm{SV}}_{\infty}
property of
\textsf{\emph{L}}(t)
. In accordance with
Slowly varying theory, functions
{\ell}(\cdot)
and
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
are positive. Then by virtue

[
2

\blacktriangleright

[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}]

\Longleftrightarrow

{\mathcal{L}(x)}=C_{\mathcal{L}}+{\mathcal{O}}\bigl(\alpha(x)\bigr)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}\hfill[C_{\mathcal{L}}]

\blacktriangleright

[{\ell}_{\delta}]

\Longleftrightarrow

{{\ell}(x)}=C_{\ell}+{\mathcal{O}}\bigl(\beta(x)\bigr)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}\hfill[C_{\ell}]

where
C_{\mathcal{L}},C_{\ell}
are positive constants and functions
\alpha(x),\beta(x)
are in

[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}]
and
[{\ell}_{\delta}]
. We then can reveal the fact that

(2.6)

\textsf{\emph{L}}(t)={{\ell(t)}\over{\mathcal{L}(t)}}={C_{\textsf{\emph{L}}}}+\mathcal{O}\left({{{\ell}(t)}\over{t^{\delta}}}\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

since
\delta<\nu
, where
C_{\textsf{\emph{L}}}={{C_{\ell}}\left/{C_{\mathcal{L}}}\right.}
. Note this
requirement put forward for
\textsf{\emph{L}}(t)
is quite possible. Especially, we reach an “excellent result”

. Namely the following explicit-form
of

was found in
[
3
]
:

(2.7)

\pi(s)=\exp\left\{{{\,1\,}\over{(1-s)^{|\gamma|}}}+{\int\limits_{s}^{1}{\left[{{{g(u)}\over{f(u)}}+{|\gamma|\over{(1-u)^{1+|\gamma|}}}}\right]du}}\right\}.

Now in the following theorem we determine the convergence rate of
e^{T(t)}{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)
to
\pi(s)
.

.

Let
\gamma<0

in (

where

s\in[0,r]
,

, and the

). In addition,

and

3.
Auxiliaries

In this section, we provide some auxiliary assertions that will be essentially used in the proof of our theorems.

First we are interested in asymptotic representation of GF of Markov branching processes
Z(t)
without
immigration. Let
F(t;s)=\mathbb{E}\left[s^{Z(t)}\bigl|{Z(0)=1}\bigr.\right]
is GF of the process
initiated by single individual. Put
R(t;s):=1-F(t;s)
. The following result called the Basic lemma of
the theory of critical Markov branching processes, has been proved in
[
4
]
. We provide
it in slightly different form below, taking from its proof.

Lemma 1
.

If the condition
[f_{\nu}]
holds then

(3.1)

{{1}\over{R(t;s)}}={{({\nu}t)^{1/\nu}}\over{\mathcal{N}(t)}}\cdot\left[{1+{{\mathcal{M}(s)}\over{t}}}\right]^{1/\nu}

for all
s\in[0,1)
, where
{\mathcal{N}}(x)
is
{SV}_{\infty}
such that

(3.2)

{\mathcal{N}}^{\,\nu}(t)\cdot\mathcal{L}\left({{{({\nu}t)^{{1/\nu}}}\over{\mathcal{N}(t)}}}\right)\longrightarrow 1\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

and
\mathcal{M}(s)
is GF of invariant measures of MBP having the form of

\mathcal{M}(s)=\int\limits_{1}^{{{1}/{(1-s)}}}{{{dx}\over{x^{1-\nu}\mathcal{L}(x)}}}\,\raise 0.9pt\hbox{.}

Let’s introduce a function

\Lambda(y):=y^{\nu}\mathcal{L}\left({{{\,1\,}\over{y}}}\right)={{f(1-y)}\over{y}}

for
y\in(0,1]
. Note that the function
y\Lambda(y)
is positive, tends to zero and has a monotone derivative so that

{{y\Lambda^{\prime}(y)}\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{{y\Lambda^{\prime}(y)}{\Lambda(y)}}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}{\Lambda(y)}}\to\nu

as
y\downarrow 0
; see
[
2
, p. 401]
. Thence it is natural to write

(3.3)

{{y\Lambda^{\prime}(y)}\over{\Lambda(y)}}=\nu+\delta(y),

where
\delta(y)
is continuous and
\delta(y)\to 0
as
y\downarrow 0
.
Since
\Lambda(1)=\mathcal{L}(1)=a_{0}
formula (
3.3
) yields

\Lambda(y)=a_{0}y^{\nu}\exp{\int\limits_{1}^{y}{{{\delta(u)}\over{u}}du}}.

Therefore we have

\mathcal{L}\left({{{\,1\,}\over{y}}}\right)=a_{0}\exp{\int\limits_{1}^{y}{{{\delta(u)}\over{u}}du}}.

Substituting
u={{1}/{t}}
in last integrand gives

\mathcal{L}\left(x\right)=a_{0}\exp{\int\limits_{1}^{x}{{{\varepsilon(t)}\over{t}}dt}},

where
\varepsilon(t)=-\delta(1/t)
and
\varepsilon(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
Combination of last equation with
[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}]
entails

\int\limits_{x}^{\lambda x}{{{\varepsilon(t)}\over t}dt}=\ln\left[1+{\mathcal{O}}\bigl(\alpha(x)\bigr)\right]={\mathcal{O}}\bigl(\alpha(x)\bigr)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $x\to\infty$}

for each
\lambda>0
. Applying the mean value theorem to the left-hand side of last equality, we can assert
that
\varepsilon(x)={\mathcal{O}}\left({\alpha(x)}\right)
and thus the condition
[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}]
entails

(3.4)

\delta(y)={\mathcal{O}}\left({\alpha\left({\,1\,}\over{y}\right)}\right)\quad\parbox{56.9055pt}{{as} {} $y\downarrow 0$.}

The following result is a modification of Lemma
1

and it also will essentially be required in our discussions.

Lemma 2
.

Let assumptions
[f_{\nu}]
and
[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}]
hold. Then

(3.5)

{{1}\over{\Lambda\left(R(t;s)\right)}}-{{1}\over{\Lambda\left(1-s\right)}}=\nu{t}+{\mathcal{O}}{\bigl(\ln{\nu(t;s)}\bigr)}\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

where
\nu(t;s)={\Lambda{\left(1-s\right)}}\nu{t}+1
.

Proof.

From (
3.3
) we write

(3.6)

{{R\Lambda{{}^{\prime}}\left(R\right)}\over{\Lambda\left(R\right)}}=\nu+\delta\left(R\right)

since
R:=R(t;s)\to{0}
as
t\to{\infty}
. By the backward Kolmogorov equation
{\partial{F}\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{{\partial{F}}{\partial{t}}}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}{\partial{t}}}=f\left({F}\right)

and considering representation
\left[{f_{\nu}}\right]
, the relation (
3.6
) becomes

{{d\Lambda\left(R\right)}\over{dt}}=-{{\Lambda\left(R\right)}\over{R}}f\left(1-R\right)\bigl(\nu+\delta\left(R\right)\bigr)=-{{\Lambda^{2}\left(R\right)}}\bigl(\nu+\delta\left(R\right)\bigr).

Therefore

(3.7)

{d}\left[{{1}\over{\Lambda\left(R\right)}}-\nu{t}\right]=\delta\left(R\right)dt.

Integrating (
3.7
) over
[0,t)
we reach to the following equation:

(3.8)

{{1}\over{\Lambda\left(R(t;s)\right)}}-{{1}\over{\Lambda\left(1-s\right)}}=\nu{t}+{\int\limits_{0}^{t}{{\delta\left(R(u;s)\right)}du}},

where
\delta(y)
is in (
3.3
). Now we should calculate integral in (
3.8
).
Considering (
3.4
) we write

(3.9)

{\int\limits_{0}^{t}{{\delta\left(R(u;s)\right)}du}}={\int\limits_{0}^{t}{{\mathcal{O}}{\bigl({\Lambda\left(R(u;s)\right)}\bigr)}du}}.

Needless to say that
R(t;s)\to{0}
as
t\to\infty
uniformly in
s\in[0,1)
, due
to (
3.1
). Therefore, since
\Lambda(y)\to 0
as
y\downarrow 0
, the integral
in the right-hand side of (
3.9
) is
{o}(t)
as
t\to\infty
. Hence

{\Lambda\left(R(t;s)\right)}={{{1}}\over{\lambda(t;s)}}+{o}{\left({{{1}}\over{\lambda(t;s)}}\right)}\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

where
\lambda(t;s)=\nu{t}+{\Lambda}^{-1}\left({1-s}\right)
. Therefore

{\int\limits_{0}^{t}{{\mathcal{O}}{\bigl({\Lambda\left(R(u;s)\right)}\bigr)}du}}={\mathcal{O}}\left({\int\limits_{0}^{t}{{\Lambda\left(R(u;s)\right)}du}}\right)={\mathcal{O}}\bigl({\ln{\nu(t;s)}}\bigr)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$.}

This together with (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) entails (
3.5
).
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
{L}(t)
is
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
with remainder
\varrho(t)
. Then for
\sigma>0

(3.10)

{\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}{{y^{-(1+\sigma)}{L}(y)dy}}}={{\,1\,}\over{\sigma}}{1\over{\,t^{\sigma}}}{L}(t)\left({1+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\varrho(t)\bigr)}\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$.}

Proof.

Undoubtedly
\int_{1}^{\infty}{u^{-(1+\sigma)}du}=1/{\sigma}
. Considering this fact
and making the substitution
y:=ut
in the integrand of (
3.10
), we write

(3.11)

{\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}{{y^{-(1+\sigma)}{L}(y)dy}}}={{\,1\,}\over{\sigma}}{{L(t)}\over{t^{\sigma}}}\left[1+{\sigma}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}{\left[{{L(ut)}\over{L(t)}}-1\right]u^{-(1+\sigma)}du}\right].

By definition of
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
-function with remainder, the expression in brackets of
integrand on right-hand side of (
3.11
) tends to
0
as
t\to\infty
uniformly in
u>{1}

(by Uniform Convergence Theorem for
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
-functions
[
2
, Theorem 1.5.2]
)
with the speed rate
\mathcal{O}\bigl(\varrho(t)\bigr)
. Thus we have (
3.10
).

The Lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let conditions
[\mathcal{L}_{\nu}]
and
[{\ell}_{\delta}]
hold and
\gamma>0
. Then

(3.12)

\int\limits_{x}^{1}{{{g(u)}\over{f(u)}}\,du}={{\,1\,}\over{\gamma}}{{g(x)}\over{\,\Lambda{\left({1-x}\right)}}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\bigl({\Lambda{\left({1-x}\right)}}\bigr)\right)\quad\parbox{56.9055pt}{{as} {} $x\uparrow 1$.}

Proof.

It follows from Basic assumption that

(3.13)

\mathcal{I}(x):=\int\limits_{x}^{1}{{{g(u)}\over{f(u)}}\,du}=-{\int\limits_{{1}/{(1-x)}}^{\infty}}{y^{-(1+\gamma)}{\textsf{\emph{L}}}(y)dy},

where
\textsf{\emph{L}}(t)={{\ell(t)}/{\mathcal{L}(t)}}
as before.
In our conditions, we can easily make sure that

{{{\textsf{\emph{L}}}(ut)}\over{{\textsf{\emph{L}}}(t)}}-1=\mathcal{O}\left({{{\mathcal{L}}(t)}\over{t^{\nu}}}\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$}

uniformly in
u>0
. On right-hand side of (
3.13
) we can directly use of the formula (
3.10
)
with
t={{1}/{(1-x)}}
and
r(t)=\mathcal{O}\left({{{\mathcal{L}}(t)}\bigl/{t^{\nu}}\bigr.}\right)
.
Then

\mathcal{I}(x)=-{{\,1\,}\over{\gamma}}{{\textsf{\emph{L}}(t)}\over{t^{\gamma}}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left({{\mathcal{L}(t)}\over{t^{\nu}}}\right)\right]\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$.}

Now returning to primary designations, we get to (
3.12
).

The Lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Rewrite (

) as follows:

(4.1)

{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)=U(s)\exp\left\{{\int\limits_{1}^{F(t;s)}{{{g(u)}\over{f(u)}}\,du}}\right\},

where

(4.2)

U(s)=\exp\left\{{\int\limits_{s}^{1}{{{g(u)}\over{f(u)}}\,du}}\right\}.

First we see that due to (
2.3
) the integral in (
4.1
) converges for

and becomes
0

as
t\to\infty

{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)
converges to
U(s)
as

uniformly over compact
subsets. Now, using the functional equation
F(t+\tau;s)={F\bigl(t;{F(\tau;s)}\bigr)}

(see
[
9
, p. 134]
), it follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}(t+\tau;s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}(\tau;s)\cdot\exp\left\{{\int\limits_{\tau}^{t+\tau}{g\left({F(u;s)}\right)du}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}(\tau;s)\cdot\exp\left\{{\int\limits_{0}^{t}{g\left({F\bigl(u;{F(\tau;s)}\bigr)}\right)du}}\right\}={\mathcal{P}}(\tau;s)\cdot{\mathcal{P}}\bigl(t;{F(\tau;s)}\bigr),

and taking limit as

(4.3)

{U}\bigl(F(\tau;s)\bigr)={\frac{1}{{\mathcal{P}}(\tau;s)}}\,{U}(s)\qquad\parbox{79.6678pt}{{for any} {} $\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}$.}

Writing the power series expansion
{U}(s)=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{{u}_{j}s^{j}}
, the equation (
4.3
)
entails an invariant property
{u}_{j}=\sum\nolimits_{i\in{\mathcal{S}}}{{u}_{i}p_{ij}(\tau)}
. Obviously
{U}(1-)=1

and hence the function in (
4.2
) generates an invariant distribution
\left\{u_{j},j\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}
for MBPI.

Now we pass to the proof of (
2.4
). Using (
3.12
) in (
4.1
) we obtain

(4.4)

{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)=U(s)\exp\Bigl\{{-I(t;s)}\Bigr\},

where

(4.5)

I(t;s)={{\,1\,}\over{\gamma}}{{g{\bigl(F(t;s)\bigr)}}\over{\Lambda{\bigl(R(t;s)\bigr)}}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl({\Lambda{\left(R(t;s)\right)}}\Bigr)\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$.}

Next, we have to use the asymptotic expansion of
R(t;s)
. Relation (
3.5
) implies

(4.6)

{{1}\over{\Lambda\left(R(t;s)\right)}}=\lambda(t;s)\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left({{\ln{\left[\Lambda{(1-s)}\lambda(t;s)\right]}}\over{\lambda(t;s)}}\right)\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

and therefore

(4.7)

{R(t;s)}={{\mathcal{N}(t;s)}\over{\left(\lambda(t;s)\right)^{1/\nu}}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left({{\ln{\left[\Lambda{(1-s)}\lambda(t;s)\right]}}\over{\lambda(t;s)}}\right)\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\to\infty$,}

where
\lambda(t;s)=\nu{t}+{\Lambda}^{-1}\left({1-s}\right)
and
\mathcal{N}(t;s)={\mathcal{L}^{-{1/\nu}}}\bigl(1/{R(t;s)}\bigr)
. Since
g(s)
is form of
[g_{\delta}]
, using (
4.6
) and (

) we obtain

(4.8)

{{g{\bigl(F(t;s)\bigr)}}\over{\Lambda{\bigl(R(t;s)\bigr)}}}=-{{\mathcal{N}^{\delta}(t;s)}\over{\left(\lambda(t;s)\right)^{{\gamma}/{\nu}}}}{\ell{\left({1}\over{R(t;s)}\right)}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left({{\ln{\left[\Lambda{(1-s)}\lambda(t;s)\right]}}\over{\lambda(t;s)}}\right)\right)

as
t\to\infty
. It it is easy to verify that the function

is asymptotically
equivalent to the
\textrm{SV}_{\infty}
-function
\mathcal{N}(t)
defined in Lemma

.

Asymptotic formula (

), (

)

Proof of Theorem

.

We write

(4.9)

\displaystyle{e^{T(t)}}{\mathcal{P}}(t;s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{{\bigl(\tau(t)\bigr)^{|\gamma|}}+{\int\limits_{0}^{t}{g\left({F(u;s)}\right)du}}\right\}

Exploring Local Norms in Exp-concave Statistical Learning
Nikita Puchkin, Nikita Zhivotovskiy

Abstract
We consider the problem of stochastic convex optimization with exp-concave losses using Empirical Risk Minimization in a convex class. Answering a question raised in several prior works, we provide a O ⁡ ( d n + 1 n ​ log ⁡ ( 1 δ ) ) O\left(\frac{d}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right) excess risk bound valid for a wide class of bounded exp-concave losses, where d d is the dimension of the convex reference set, n n is the sample size, and δ \delta is the confidence level. Our result is based on a unified geometric assumption on the gradient of losses and the notion of local norms.



1
Problem statement

We consider a stochastic convex optimization problem, that is, minimization of the objective

F(w)=Pf(w,Z)

over a compact
convex
set
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here
Z
is a random element with a distribution
P
supported on a set
\mathsf{Z}
, and
Pf(w,Z)
stands for the expectation of
f(w,Z)
with respect to
Z\sim P
. Since
P
is usually unknown, the optimizer

w^{*}\in\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}F(w)

(1)

cannot be computed explicitly. Instead, a learner has an access to a sample
S_{n}=\{Z_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant n\}
with independent elements distributed according to
P
. The most natural and popular strategy in this case is to replace the expectation with respect to
Z\sim P
by the sample mean and study an empirical risk minimizer (ERM)

\widehat{w}\in\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\widehat{F}(w),

(2)

where

\widehat{F}(w)=P_{n}f(w,Z)=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f(w,Z_{i})\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$.}

(3)

Here and further in the paper,
P_{n}
stands for the empirical measure, corresponding to the sample
S_{n}
. We are interested in tight high probability bounds on the excess risk. Our main result will imply a bound of the form

F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})=O\left(\frac{d}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right),

for a general class of bounded exp-concave losses.

Contributions.

Our contribution combines two ideas: specific assumptions on the loss function and a careful probabilistic analysis utilizing empirical process techniques. We propose a general assumption on loss functions that applies to most commonly used exp-concave losses in the bounded setup.
Our main result, Theorem
3.4
, addresses the question raised explicitly in a series of papers
(
Mahdavi et al. 2015
;
Koren and Levy 2015
;
Mehta 2017
;
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
;
Yang et al. 2018
)
by further exploring the concept of local norms. Despite the presence of established empirical process techniques in related contexts, our approach handles the loss functions exhibiting data-dependent curvature. Compared to the existing localized analysis for the losses with (data-independent) curvature
(
Mendelson 2002
;
Sridharan et al. 2008
;
Koltchinskii 2011
)
, we do not assume any linear structure, so the common approach based on computing the local complexity of the linear class is not applicable in our situation. Similarly, some more recent applications of empirical process techniques to exp-concave losses
(
Vijaykumar 2021
)
do not fully exploit the local geometric structure of the reference set and thus lead to suboptimal bounds in our setup. We discuss this in more detail in Section
4
.

Notation.

Throughout the paper, the notation
f\lesssim g
or
g\gtrsim f
means that for some universal constant
c>0
we have
f\leqslant cg
. For any set
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\textrm{Int}(\mathcal{W})
denotes its interior. For a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix
\mathsf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{A}}
stands for a seminorm, induced by
\mathsf{A}
:

\|w\|_{\mathsf{A}}=\sqrt{w^{\top}\mathsf{A}w},\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$}.

Finally, for any two positive semidefinite matrices
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
of the same size the inequalities
\mathsf{A}\preceq\mathsf{B}
and
\mathsf{B}\succeq\mathsf{A}
mean that
(\mathsf{B}-\mathsf{A})
is positive semidefinite.

Structure of the paper.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we make a brief overview of related works. In Section
3
, we introduce our geometric assumption, present our main result, Theorem
3.4
, and discuss its implications. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.4
. It heavily relies on a technical statement about the supremum of a certain empirical process, Lemma
4.2
. We provide its proof in Section
5
. Proofs of auxiliary results appear in Appendix.

2
Related work

Theoretical properties of the empirical risk minimizer
\widehat{w}
, especially its generalization ability, were extensively studied in the contexts of stochastic convex optimization and statistical learning theory. Given the extensive body of relevant literature, we review only a subset of the most relevant papers. Early results in this direction include the analysis of linear regression with squared loss via local Rademacher complexities (see, e.g.,
(
Mendelson 2002
)
and
(
Koltchinskii 2011
, Section 5.1)
).
In particular,
Koltchinskii 2011
shows that, in the problem of bounded linear regression in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the excess squared risk of a ERM scales as
O(d/n)
with high probability. In contrast to the standard results on learning general VC-type classes under various margin conditions (see, e.g.,
(
Bartlett et al. 2005
;
Massart and Nédélec 2006
;
Zhivotovskiy and Hanneke 2018
)
, and
(
Koltchinskii 2011
, Section 5.1, Example 3)
), the linear regression specific rate
O(d/n)
avoids an extra
\log n
factor and exhibits tighter bounds on generalization ability of ERM. The reason for that is a linear structure of the reference set, which allows to bound its local Rademacher complexity using the standard Cauchy-Schwarz or Fenchel inequalities (see
(
Koltchinskii 2011
, Proposition 3.2)
and
(
Kakade et al. 2008
, Theorem 1)
).

Many researchers have further explored the possibility of achieving an excess risk bound better than
O(d\log n/n)
for problems with specific local structures in the reference set. Recent investigations in stochastic approximation lead to numerous tight in-expectation
O(d/n)
bounds on the excess risk of particular optimization algorithms (see, e.g.,
(
Moulines and Bach 2011
;
Bach and Moulines 2013
;
Woodworth and Srebro 2021
)
to mention a few). The current paper focuses on high probability bounds, which typically require a more technical analysis.
In a study by
Sridharan et al. 2008
, the authors extended the analysis of local Rademacher complexities to the case of regularized strongly convex losses,
f(w,z)=\ell(y,w^{\top}x)+R(w)
, where
z=(x,y)
,
\ell
is convex with respect to its second argument for any
y
, and
R(w)
is strongly convex. Similar to an example in
(
Koltchinskii 2011
, Section 5.1, Example 1)
, the key to the proof was a bound on the local Rademacher complexity of a linear class, as demonstrated in Lemma 7 in
(
Sridharan et al. 2008
)
. The PAC-Bayesian localization technique was introduced by
Audibert and Catoni 2010
;
Audibert and Catoni 2011
to obtain high-probability bounds on the squared excess risk of ERM in the bounded linear regression problem. These authors achieved a rate of
O((d+\log(1/\delta))/n)
. This approach has also been used in
(
Zhang 2006
)
in the context of density estimation. Related to our study,
Klochkov and Zhivotovskiy 2021
proved a dimension-free excess risk bound of
O(\log(n)\log(1/\delta)/n)
with high probability for arbitrary strongly convex and Lipschitz losses, using the uniform stability of ERM. We also would like to mention that excess risk bounds can be derived from regret bounds for online learning algorithms via the online-to-batch conversion. We refer to
(
Kakade and Tewari 2008
;
Gaillard and Wintenberger 2018
;
Van der Hoeven et al. 2022
)
for related high probability upper bounds. However, in our context, the online-to-batch approach yields risk bounds with additional logarithmic factors, which makes it unsuitable for our purposes.

This paper focuses on stochastic convex optimization problems using a broad class of exp-concave losses. Our goal is to provide tight high probability bounds
O((d+\log(1/\delta))/n)
on the excess risk of ERM under mild assumptions on the function
f(w,z)
. In the context of exp-concave losses, this question was directly addressed in a series of papers and turned out to be quite challenging.
It appears that one also needs to make some additional assumptions on the loss function that allows to capture the local geometry of the reference set. Several works have achieved the desired
O(d/n)
rate in expectation (e.g.,
(
Koren and Levy 2015
;
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
;
Zhang and Zhou 2019
)
), but transforming this guarantee into a high probability bound results in suboptimal
O((d\log n+\log(1/\delta))/n)
guarantees. Several groups of authors
(
Mahdavi et al. 2015
;
Koren and Levy 2015
;
Mehta 2017
;
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
;
Yang et al. 2018
)
have questioned the possibility of achieving the optimal
O((d+\log(1/\delta))/n)
high probability excess risk bound. The best bound in this direction is given by the confidence boosting approach suggested by
Mehta 2017
, which replaces ERM with an aggregate of independently trained ERMs, resulting in a bound of
O(d\log(1/\delta)/n)
. We additionally refer to the recent papers by
Mourtada and Gaïffas 2022
and
Bilodeau et al. 2021
where a detailed survey of relevant results is provided.

The cornerstone of the analysis of empirical risk minimizers in learning problems with the squared loss (or more general strongly convex losses) is localization. Certain fixed point equations appeared in influential works
(
Van de Geer 1990
;
Birgé and Massart 1993
;
Yang and Barron 1999
)
and then became familiar to the machine learning community thanks to the papers
(
Koltchinskii 2006
;
Bartlett et al. 2005
)
. We refer to the monographs
(
Van de Geer 2000
;
Koltchinskii 2011
)
and the paper
(
Mendelson 2002
)
for examples of application of local entropy and local Rademacher complexity to the analysis of linear regression with squared loss.
Finally, we note that the analysis of the local structure of the reference set in the context of density estimation has its roots in the classical works of
LeCam 1973
and
Birgé 1983
. For related upper bounds, we refer to Section 32.2.3 in the forthcoming book by
Polyanskiy and Wu 2023
.

3
Main results and examples

In this section, we present our main result.
Throughout the paper, we assume that the loss function
f(w,z)
satisfies the following condition.

Assumption 3.1
.

For any training sample
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
there is positive semidefinite matrix
\mathsf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, which may depend on the sample, such that

•

the expectation of
\mathsf{H}
is finite;

•

\widehat{F}(w)
given by (
3
) is
\sigma
-strongly convex on
\mathcal{W}
with respect to the seminorm, induced by
\mathsf{H}
; that is, for any
u,v\in\mathcal{W}
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, the next inequality holds almost surely:

\widehat{F}\big(\alpha u+(1-\alpha)v\big)\leqslant\alpha\widehat{F}(u)+(1-\alpha)\widehat{F}(v)-\frac{\sigma\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2}\|u-v\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2};

(4)

•

for any
u,v\in\mathcal{W}
, the empirical
L_{2}
-distance between
f(u,Z)
and
f(v,Z)
satisfies the inequality

P_{n}\big(f(u,Z)-f(v,Z)\big)^{2}\leqslant L^{2}\|u-v\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}.

Here,
\sigma
and
L
are positive constants that do not depend on
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
.

When
\mathsf{H}
is the identity matrix, we simply say that
\widehat{F}
is a strongly convex function. Similarly, when
\mathsf{H}
is the identity matrix, the second part of this assumption corresponds to the standard Lipschitz property.
Though Assumption
3.1
may appear similar to the commonly used assumptions of strong convexity and Lipschitzness, the introduction of the data-dependent matrix
\mathsf{H}
broadens the range of applications of our model. In particular, it includes regularized objectives, considered in
(
Sridharan et al. 2008
)
, and generalized linear models (see, e.g.,
(
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
)
), where the loss function has a form

f(w,z)=\ell(w^{\top}x,y).

(5)

Here
z=(x,y)
is a feature-label pair,
w
and
x
belong to bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\ell
is
L
-Lipschitz and
\sigma
-strongly convex with respect to the first argument for all
y
. In this case,
\widehat{F}(w)
is
\sigma
-strongly convex with respect to the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
, induced by

\mathsf{H}=(X_{1}X_{1}^{\top}+\dots+X_{n}X_{n}^{\top})/n
(see Example
3.6
).
Our setup covers both of the most commonly used generalized linear models: linear regression, which corresponds to
f(w,z)=(y-w^{\top}x)^{2}
, and logistic regression, which corresponds to
f(w,z)=\log(1+e^{-yw^{\top}x})
with
y\in\{-1,1\}
.

Under mild differentiability assumptions,
\widehat{F}
demonstrates exponential concavity, as shown in the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
\widehat{F}
satisfies Assumption
3.1
and let the loss function
f(w,z)
be twice differentiable with respect to the first argument for all
z\in\mathsf{Z}
. Then
\widehat{F}
is
(\sigma/L^{2})
-exponentially concave on
\mathcal{W}
. That is,
e^{-\sigma\widehat{F}/L^{2}}
is concave on
\mathcal{W}
.

However, our assumptions are slightly stronger than exp-concavity alone. In fact, our assumption implies the boundedness of the loss.

Proposition 3.3
.

Under Assumption
3.1
, we have
|f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)|\leqslant 4L^{2}/\sigma
for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
and almost surely with respect to
Z
.

Observe that this result does not necessarily imply the boundedness of the set
\mathcal{W}
. This distinguishes our assumptions from the more restrictive strongly convex Lipshitz assumption, which implies both the boundedness of the loss and
\mathcal{W}
. In fact, the proof of Proposition
3.3
yields that

\|w-\widehat{w}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\leqslant\frac{4}{\sigma}\left(\widehat{F}(w)-\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})\right)\leqslant\frac{32L^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$}.

If
\det(\mathsf{H})\neq 0
, then
\mathcal{W}
is bounded. However, in general, the matrix
\mathsf{H}
is not assumed to be nondegenerate. Hence,
\mathcal{W}
may potentially be unbounded under our assumption. The proofs of Propositions
3.3
and
3.2
are deferred to Appendix
A
. After listing these basic properties, we are ready to present our main result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a compact convex set.
Under Assumption
3.1
, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, it holds that, with probability at least
1-\delta
, the ERM estimator (
2
) satisfies

F(\widehat{w})-\min\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}F(w)\lesssim\frac{L^{2}(d+\log(1/\delta))}{\sigma n}.

Theorem
3.4
answers a natural question raised in several previous papers. The most relevant to our result are the papers of
Koren and Levy 2015
;
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
, where the authors obtained optimal in-expectation bounds of order
O(d/n)
, and of
Mahdavi et al. 2015
;
Mehta 2017
;
Yang et al. 2018
, where the authors proved
O((d\log n+\log(1/\delta))/n)
high probability upper bounds. We will now discuss the differences in assumptions made on the loss functions.
In
(
Koren and Levy 2015
;
Yang et al. 2018
)
, the authors required the gradient
\partial f(w,z)/\partial w
be Lipschitz on
\mathcal{W}
.
Koren and Levy 2015
also asked if the smoothness of the gradient is necessary for obtaining fast rates for ERM. A partial answer was given in
(
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
)
, where the authors considered generalized linear models with the losses of the form (
5
), where
\ell(\cdot,y)
is Lipschitz and strongly convex with respect to its first argument for all
y
’s. Under these assumptions, the authors showed that the expected excess risk of ERM can decay as fast as
O(d/n)
. Our result strictly improves the one of
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
. As it was discussed earlier, our Assumption
3.1
is more general than the requirements of
Gonen and Shalev-Shwartz 2018
. At the same time, we derive a rate optimal
O((d+\log(1/\delta))/n)
high probability excess risk bound. Concerning the papers
(
Mahdavi et al. 2015
;
Mehta 2017
)
, the authors work under more general assumptions than Assumption
3.1
. Namely, they only assume that the loss
f(w,z)
is bounded, Lipschitz and
\sigma
-exponentially concave with respect to
w\in\mathcal{W}
for all
z\in\mathsf{Z}
. It is not clear, if it is possible to get rid of extra
\log n
factors in their high probability bounds. However, we would like to note that our setup covers all interesting bounded loss functions arising in exp-concave learning with convex reference sets.

Theorem
3.4
has several implications. We discuss them in the examples below.

Example 3.5

(Linear regression with squared loss)
.

Fix
R>0
.
Assume that the observations
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
have a form
Z_{i}=(X_{i},Y_{i})
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, where
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
have distributions supported on
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|\leqslant 1\}
and
[-R,R]
, respectively. Consider
\mathcal{W}=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|w\|\leqslant R\}
and the quadratic loss function
f(w,z)=(y-w^{\top}x)^{2}
. Note that
f(w,z)
fulfils Assumption
3.1
with the matrix
\mathsf{H}=(X_{1}X_{1}^{\top}+\dots+X_{n}X_{n}^{\top})/n

and the constants
\sigma=2
,
L=4R
. According to Theorem
3.4
, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, an empirical risk minimizer
\widehat{w}
satisfies the inequality

F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\lesssim\frac{R^{2}(d+\log(1/\delta)}{n}

with probability at least
1-\delta
.
This agrees with the upper bound of
Koltchinskii 2011
, based on local Rademacher complexities, and the lower bound of
Shamir 2015
, who showed that for any estimator
\widetilde{w}\in\mathcal{W}
there exists a data distribution over
\mathcal{B}(0,1)\times[-R,R]
such that

\mathbb{E}F(\widetilde{w})-F(w^{*})\gtrsim R^{2}d/n
.

Example 3.6

(Logistic regression)
.

Again, assume that the observations
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
have a form
Z_{i}=(X_{i},Y_{i})
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, where
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Y_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
. Fix
R>0
and let the distribution of
X_{i}
’s be supported on
\mathcal{B}(0,R)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|\leqslant R\}
. Consider
\mathcal{W}=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|w\|\leqslant B\}
and the logistic loss

f(w,z)=\log(1+e^{-yw^{\top}x}).

Direct calculations show that the map
u\mapsto\log(1+e^{u})
is
1
-Lipschitz and
e^{-BR}
-strongly convex on
[-BR,BR]
. Hence, the logistic loss meets Assumption
3.1
with the matrix

\mathsf{H}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}

and the constants
\sigma=e^{-BR}
,
L=1
. Theorem
3.4
implies that, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that

F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\lesssim\frac{e^{BR}(d+\log(1/\delta))}{n}.

(6)

We refer to
(
Hazan et al. 2014
)
, where the authors discuss the inavoidability of the
e^{BR}
factor for ERM. We note that the bound (
6
) has not appeared in the literature before.

Furthermore, our bound covers strongly convex and Lipschitz losses, regardless of any linear structure in the loss function. In
(
Klochkov and Zhivotovskiy 2021
)
, the authors used the uniform stability argument to prove that, if
f(\cdot,z)
is
L
-Lipschitz and
\sigma
-strongly convex for all
z\in\mathsf{Z}
, then, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that

F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\lesssim\frac{L^{2}\log n\log(1/\delta)}{\sigma n}.

(7)

Taking together their result and Theorem
3.4
, we get the following bound.

Corollary 3.7

(Strongly convex and Lipschitz losses)
.

Assume that, for any
z\in\mathsf{Z}
, the loss function
f(\cdot,z)
is
L
-Lipschitz and
\sigma
-strongly convex on
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is a compact convex set. Then, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that

F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\lesssim\frac{L^{2}\min\{d+\log(1/\delta),\;\log n\log(1/\delta)\}}{\sigma n}.

This gives the best known high probability upper bound in this standard setup. Recent results on algorithmic stability
(
Bousquet et al. 2020
;
Klochkov and Zhivotovskiy 2021
)
leave the question if the
\log n
term can be removed in the bounds such as (
7
). Our Corollary (
3.7
) shows that if this factor cannot be removed, then any demonstration of this must occur in the regime
d+\log(1/\delta)\gtrsim\log n\log(1/\delta)
.

On logarithmic factors and improper learning.

We focus on the sharp analysis of ERM in a convex reference set
\mathcal{W}
. Nevertheless, recent work has demonstrated that the use of so-called
improper
estimators — estimators that predict values outside of the reference set
\mathcal{W}
— can lead to significant improvements in the dependence on some parameters. These estimators are typically based on aggregating the functions within certain
non-convex
reference sets constructed by truncating the original functions that correspond to
\mathcal{W}
. Such improvements are known in both logistic regression
(
Kakade and Ng 2004
;
Foster et al. 2018
;
Mourtada and Gaïffas 2022
)
and regression with the squared loss
(
Forster and Warmuth 2002
;
Vaškevičius and
Zhivotovskiy 2023
;
Mourtada et al. 2021
)
. However, it should be noted that the improper learning setup poses a considerable challenge for the localized analysis utilized in this paper. Specifically, the lower bound of
Rakhlin et al. 2017
indicates that, in the context of bounded regression with a non-convex reference set, the lower bound of
\Omega(\log(n)/n)
cannot be surpassed even with the use of improper learners. At present, there is no known analysis that can yield high probability excess risk bounds of order
O((d+\log(1/\delta))/n)
for any improper learner in non-convex reference sets.

4
Proof of Theorem
3.4

Our proof is making most of the standard arguments on the Laplace transform of
shifted
or
offset
empirical processes. A similar approach was exploited by many authors
Wegkamp 2003
;
Lecué and Rigollet 2014
;
Liang et al. 2015
;
Zhivotovskiy and Hanneke 2018
;
Kanade et al. 2022
in the statistical setup, though their analysis is specific to strongly convex losses or binary losses under additional probabilistic assumptions. In
(
Vijaykumar 2021
)
, the author generalized the approach of
Liang et al. 2015
to study the properties of ERM under exp-concave losses, though their analysis only focuses on getting the
O(1/n)
rate of convergence and fails to capture the local structure of the reference set. In our context their result would give an additional
\log n
factor, which is known to be achievable using an
\varepsilon
-net argument
(
Mehta 2017
)
. The key point of our analysis is based on relating Assumption
3.1
with the localized covering numbers of the set
\mathcal{W}
with respect to the data-dependent semi-norm induced by
\mathsf{H}
. Two papers that are worth mentioning on the topic of online learning and exp-concave losses, which utilize sequential offset processes, are
(
Rakhlin and Sridharan 2015
)
and
(
Bilodeau et al. 2020
)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumption
3.1
,
let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex monotonously increasing function. Then, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\Phi\big(F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\big)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Phi\left(4\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right),

where
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}
are i.i.d. Rademacher random variables.

The proof of Lemma
4.1
is postponed to Appendix
A.3
.
From now on, we focus our attention on the shifted multiplier process of the form

P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\sigma\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}/8,w\in\mathcal{W}
,
and consider exponential moments of its supremum. In Section
5
, we prove our main technical bound.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f(w,z)
satisfy Assumption
3.1
.
Set
\lambda=\sigma n/(32eL^{2})
.
Then, for all
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}\leqslant e+e^{3d}+14e^{2048(1+e)^{2}d/e}.

Let us briefly describe the idea of the proof of Lemma
4.2
. We use the standard peeling argument and represent the whole set
\mathcal{W}
as the union

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}[0,r]\cup\left(\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{W}[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]\right),

where
r>0
is a fixed number and, for any
b\geqslant a\geqslant 0
,
\mathcal{W}[a,b]
is defined as

\mathcal{W}[a,b]=\left\{w\in\mathcal{W}:a\leqslant\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leqslant b\right\}.

After this step, we essentially reduce the problem to the analysis of localized offset process, that is, the process of the form

P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2},

where
w
runs over a local set
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
, rather than over the whole set
\mathcal{W}
. However, the peeling argument alone is not sufficient to avoid unnecessary
\log n
factors, and one should be careful at this point. In
(
Mehta 2017
, Theorem 1)
and
(
Liang et al. 2015
, Lemma 6, Lemma 7)
, the authors use the
\varepsilon
-net argument, which leads to a suboptimal result in our situation. The main difference of our approach from the previous works is hidden in the proof Lemma
5.1
, where we combine the localized analysis with chaining and Talagrand’s inequality to derive a tight upper bound on the exponential moment

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right\}.

Given that we consider the geometry of the loss in defining
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
, deriving an upper bound on its covering number is relatively straightforward. We provide this bound in Lemma
5.2
. The key point of this approach, which sets it apart and enables improvement over existing results, is the decision not to use the naive
\varepsilon
-net argument.

Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
immediately imply that

\log\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\lambda(F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*}))\right\}\lesssim d\quad\text{with $\lambda=\frac{\sigma n}{128eL^{2}}$.}

Hence, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, the following inequality holds, with probability at least
1-\delta
:

F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\lesssim\frac{L^{2}(d+\log(1/\delta))}{\sigma n}.

The claim follows.

∎

Proof of Lemma
4.2

Our proof relies on the classical peeling argument. For any
b\geqslant a\geqslant 0
define

\mathcal{W}[a,b]=\left\{w\in\mathcal{W}:a\leqslant\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leqslant b\right\}.

Let
r>0
be a positive real number to be specified later. Since the function
\Phi(x)=e^{\lambda x}
takes only positive values, the expectation of interest does not exceed

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}

(8)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}.

Thus, we have bounded the exponential moment of the supremum of the empirical process by the sum of exponential moments of localized processes.
The main ingredient in the proof of Lemma
4.2
is Lemma
5.1
, which provides an upper bound on the exponential moment of the supremum of a localized set without introducing additional logarithmic factors.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f(w,Z)
satisfy Assumption
3.1
. Let

B=\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\operatornamewithlimits{esssup}\limits_{z\in\mathsf{Z}}|f(w,z)-f(w^{*},z)|.

Then, for any
\lambda\geqslant 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 64\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{B^{2}\lambda^{2}e^{B\lambda/n}}{2n}\left(\frac{128Lr}{B}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{L^{2}r^{2}}{B^{2}}\right).

If, in addition,
\lambda\leqslant n/B
, then

\log\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right\}\leqslant 64(1+e)\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{e\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{2n}.

We defer the proof of Lemma
5.1
to Section
5.1
and focus on finishing the proof of Lemma
4.2
. Note that Proposition
3.3
implies that
B\leqslant 4L^{2}/\sigma
under Assumption
3.1
. Since we consider
\lambda=\sigma n/(32eL^{2})
, the conditions of the second part of Lemma
5.1
are satisfied. Applying it to the first term in the right-hand side of (

), we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right\}

(9)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{64(1+e)\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{e\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{2n}\right\}.

Next, fix a non-negative integer
k
and consider

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}.

In contrast to (
5
), dropping the negative term leads to a suboptimal bound in this situation. At the same time, the following simple observation allows us to obtain a tight bound.
By the definition of
\mathcal{W}[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]
, we have
\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}\geqslant 2^{k}r
for any
w\in\mathcal{W}[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right]-4^{k-1}\sigma\lambda r^{2}/2\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,2^{k+1}r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right]-4^{k-1}\sigma\lambda r^{2}/2\right\}.

Applying Lemma
5.1
again, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,2^{k+1}r]}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right]-4^{k-1}\sigma\lambda r^{2}/2\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{64(1+e)\;2^{k+1}\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{e\cdot 4^{k+1}\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{2n}-4^{k-1}\sigma\lambda r^{2}/2\right\}

(10)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{128(1+e)\;2^{k}\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{2e\cdot 4^{k}\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{n}-4^{k-1}\sigma\lambda r^{2}/2\right\}.

Taking (
5
), (
5
), and (
5
) together, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{64(1+e)\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{e\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{2n}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{128(1+e)\;2^{k}\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{2e\cdot 4^{k}\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{n}-4^{k-1}\sigma\lambda r^{2}/2\right\}.

The remainder of the proof relies on standard computations and on making the appropriate choice for the parameters
\lambda
and
r
.
Let us recall that
\lambda=\sigma n/(32eL^{2})
.
It is easy to observe that such a choice ensures

\frac{2e\cdot 4^{k}\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{n}=\frac{4^{k}\sigma\lambda r^{2}}{16}.

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\exp\left\{64(1+e)\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{e\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{2n}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{128(1+e)\;2^{k}\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{2e\cdot 4^{k}\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{n}-4^{k-1}\sigma\lambda r^{2}/2\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{64(1+e)\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{\sigma\lambda r^{2}}{64}\right\}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{128(1+e)\;2^{k}\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}-4^{k-2}\sigma\lambda r^{2}\right\}.

Choosing

r=\frac{L}{\sigma}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}},

we obtain that

64(1+e)\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{\sigma\lambda r^{2}}{64}=\frac{64(1+e)\lambda L^{2}d}{\sigma n}+\frac{\lambda L^{2}d}{64\sigma n}=\frac{2(1+e)d}{e}+\frac{d}{2048e}<3d,

and, for any
k\geqslant 0
, it holds that

128(1+e)\;2^{k}\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}-4^{k-2}\sigma\lambda r^{2}\leqslant\frac{\lambda L^{2}d}{\sigma n}\left(128(1+e)\;2^{k}-4^{k-2}\right).

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{3d}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{\frac{\lambda L^{2}d}{\sigma n}\left(128(1+e)\;2^{k}-4^{k-2}\right)\right\},

(11)

where
\lambda=\sigma n/(32eL^{2})
. It remains to bound the sum

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{\frac{\lambda L^{2}d}{\sigma n}\left(128(1+e)\;2^{k}-4^{k-2}\right)\right\}.

Note that, if
k\geqslant 14
, then
128(1+e)\;2^{k}<2^{k+9}\leqslant 4^{k-2}/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{\frac{\lambda L^{2}d}{\sigma n}\left(128(1+e)\;2^{k}-4^{k-2}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum\limits_{k=0}^{13}\exp\left\{\frac{\lambda L^{2}d}{\sigma n}\left(128(1+e)\;2^{k}-4^{k-2}\right)\right\}+\sum\limits_{k=14}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{4^{k}\lambda L^{2}d}{32\sigma n}\right\}.

(12)

Applying the standard bound for the quadratic function

128(1+e)\;2^{k}-4^{k-2}=\left.\left(128(1+e)x-\frac{x^{2}}{16}\right)\right|_{x=2^{k}}\leqslant 256^{2}(1+e)^{2},

we deduce that the first term in the right-hand side does not exceed

14\exp\left\{\frac{256^{2}(1+e)^{2}\lambda L^{2}d}{\sigma n}\right\}=14\exp\left\{\frac{2048(1+e)^{2}d}{e}\right\}.

(13)

For the second one, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=14}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{4^{k}\lambda L^{2}d}{32\sigma n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{4^{k+14}d}{32^{2}e}\right\}\leqslant\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{4^{14}\;kd}{32^{2}e}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{4^{9}\;kd}{e}\right\}\leqslant e.

(14)

The inequalities (
5
), (
5
), (
13
), and (
5
) imply that

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right\}\leqslant e+e^{3d}+14e^{2048(1+e)^{2}d/e}.

The claim follows.

5.1
Proof of Lemma
5.1

We start with an upper bound on the expected value of the supremum. Let
\mathcal{F}[0,r]=\{f(w,z)-f(w^{*},z):w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]\}
. According to Assumption
3.1
, we have

P_{n}\big(f(u,Z)-f(v,Z)\big)^{2}\leqslant L^{2}\|u-v\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $u,v\in\mathcal{W}$.}

This yields that, for any
\gamma>0
,
a
(\gamma/L)
-net in
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
with respect to the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
induces a
\gamma
-net in
\mathcal{F}[0,r]
with respect to the empirical
L_{2}
-norm.
Indeed, let
\mathcal{W}_{\gamma/L}[0,r]
be a
(\gamma/L)
-net in
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
. Consider any
g\in\mathcal{F}[0,r]
. By the definition of
\mathcal{F}[0,r]
, there exists
w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]
, such that
g(z)=f(w,z)
. Let
w_{\gamma/L}
be the closest to
w
element of
\mathcal{W}_{\gamma/L}[0,r]
. By the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{\gamma/L}[0,r]
, we have

\|w-w_{\gamma/L}\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leqslant\gamma/L.

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{P_{n}\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)^{2}}-\sqrt{P_{n}\big(f(w_{\gamma/L},Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{P_{n}\big(f(w,Z)-f(w_{\gamma/L},Z)\big)^{2}}\leqslant L\|w-w_{\gamma/L}\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leqslant\gamma.

Hence, the covering number of
\mathcal{F}[0,r]
with respect to the empirical
L_{2}
-norm does not exceed the one of
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
with respect to the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
.
Applying the standard chaining technique, we obtain that

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\leqslant\frac{12}{\sqrt{n}}\int\limits_{0}^{Lr}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{W}[0,r],\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}},\gamma/L)}{\rm d}\gamma.

(15)

We use the following lemma to bound the covering number of
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
u\in(0,r]
, it holds that

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{W}[0,r],\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}},u)\leqslant\left(6r/u\right)^{d}.

The proof of Lemma
5.2
is moved to Section
A.4
. Substituting the bound of Lemma
5.2
into the Dudley integral (
15
), we obtain that

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\leqslant\frac{12}{\sqrt{n}}\int\limits_{0}^{Lr}\sqrt{d\log(6Lr/\gamma)}{\rm d}\gamma.

Substituting
\gamma
with
6Lre^{-u}
, we get

\int\limits_{0}^{Lr}\sqrt{\log(6Lr/\gamma)}{\rm d}\gamma=6Lr\int\limits_{\log 6}^{+\infty}\sqrt{u}e^{-u}{\rm d}u\leqslant 6Lr\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\sqrt{u}e^{-u}{\rm d}u=6Lr\;\Gamma(3/2)=3Lr\sqrt{\pi}.

Therefore, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\leqslant 12\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\cdot 3Lr\sqrt{\pi}\leqslant 64Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

(16)

Since
|\varepsilon(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z))|\leqslant B
almost surely, we can apply Talagrand’s concentration inequality for the supremum of the empirical process to

\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big).

We use an upper bound on the exponential moment of the supremum, which follows from
(
Bousquet 2002
, Theorems 2.1 and 2.3)
:

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\big(f(w,Z_{i})-f(w^{*},Z_{i})\big)\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\big(f(w,Z_{i})-f(w^{*},Z_{i})\big)+V_{n}\big(e^{B\lambda}-B\lambda-1\big)\quad\text{for all $\lambda\geqslant 0$},

(17)

where

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{B}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)+\frac{1}{B^{2}}\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\frac{2}{B}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)+\frac{nL^{2}r^{2}}{B^{2}}.

(18)

Combining the inequalities (
16
), (
5.1
), (
5.1
) with the bound

e^{x}-x-1\leqslant\frac{x^{2}e^{x}}{2},
which holds for all
x\geqslant 0
,
we obtain for any
\lambda\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left\{\lambda\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]}P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant 64\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{B^{2}\lambda^{2}e^{B\lambda/n}}{2n}\left(\frac{128Lr}{B}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{L^{2}r^{2}}{B^{2}}\right).

If
\lambda\leqslant n/B
, then

64\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{B^{2}\lambda^{2}e^{B\lambda/n}}{2n}\left(\frac{128Lr}{B}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{L^{2}r^{2}}{B^{2}}\right)\leqslant 64(1+e)\lambda Lr\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{e\lambda^{2}L^{2}r^{2}}{2n}.

The claim follows.

∎

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have established a tight high-probability upper bound on the excess risk of Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM), specifically
O(d/n+\log(1/\delta)/n)
, for a subclass of exp-concave losses. The key aspect of our analysis is the convexity of the domain
\mathcal{W}
, though it does not necessitate boundedness. Nevertheless, it is important for the loss function
f(w,Z_{i})
to remain bounded for every
w\in\mathcal{W}
and all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. This assumption proves instrumental in Lemma
5.1
, when
we are applying Talagrand’s inequality for the suprema of empirical processes. The counterpart to Talagrand’s inequality in the unbounded case, as indicated in
(
Adamczak 2008
)
, might introduce an extra
\log n
factor. Currently, it remains unclear whether a similar
O(d/n+\log(1/\delta)/n)
bound can be achieved in the unbounded scenario. While applications like logistic regression can mitigate the unbounded case to the bounded one with clipping (see
(
Foster et al. 2018
)
), this results in nonconvex classes, making our localized analysis inapplicable. Moreover, as noted in Section
3
, the additional
\log n
factor is sometimes inavoidable when dealing with a non-convex reference set (see
(
Rakhlin et al. 2017
, Theorem 6)
). Understanding the geometry of non-convex classes, particularly in relation to proper and improper learners, appears to be a promising direction for future research.

Proof of Proposition
3.2

It is enough to check that

\nabla^{2}e^{-\sigma\widehat{F}(w)/L^{2}}\preceq\mathsf{O}

for all
w
from the interior of
\mathcal{W}
. Here
\mathsf{O}
is a matrix with zero entries. This is equivalent to

\frac{\sigma}{L^{2}}\nabla\widehat{F}(w)\nabla\widehat{F}(w)^{\top}\preceq\nabla^{2}\widehat{F}(w)\quad\text{for all $w\in\text{Int}(\mathcal{W})$}.

The strong convexity of
\widehat{F}
with respect to the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
implies that the function
\widehat{F}(w)-\sigma\|w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}
is convex on
\mathsf{H}
. The last is equivalent to the inequality

\nabla^{2}\left(\widehat{F}(w)-\sigma\|w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\succeq\mathsf{O}\quad\text{for all $w\in\text{Int}(\mathcal{W})$}

or simply
\nabla^{2}\widehat{F}(w)\succeq\sigma\mathsf{H}
for all
w\in\text{Int}(\mathcal{W})
. On the other hand, it holds that

\big(\widehat{F}(u)-\widehat{F}(v)\big)^{2}\leqslant P_{n}\big(f(u,Z)-f(v,Z)\big)^{2}\leqslant L^{2}\|u-v\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $u,v\in\mathcal{W}$.}

\nabla\widehat{F}(w)\nabla\widehat{F}(w)^{\top}\preceq L^{2}\mathsf{H}\quad\text{for all $w\in\text{Int}(\mathcal{W})$}.

Hence, for any
w
in the interior of
\mathcal{W}
, we have

\frac{\sigma}{L^{2}}\nabla\widehat{F}(w)\nabla\widehat{F}(w)^{\top}\preceq\sigma\mathsf{H}\preceq\nabla^{2}\widehat{F}(w).

Proof of Proposition
3.3

If Assumption
3.1
holds, then Jensen’s inequality immediately implies that

\big(\widehat{F}(u)-\widehat{F}(v)\big)^{2}\leqslant P_{n}\big(f(u,Z)-f(v,Z)\big)^{2}\leqslant L^{2}\|u-v\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $u,v\in\mathcal{W}$.}

Thus,
\widehat{F}
is
L
-Lipschitz on
\mathcal{W}
with respect to the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
.
On the other hand, the strong convexity of
\widehat{F}
yields that

\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})\leqslant\widehat{F}\left(\frac{\widehat{w}+w}{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{F}(w)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|\widehat{w}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$,}

where the first inequality follows from the convexity of
\mathcal{W}
and the definition of
\widehat{w}
. Hence,

\widehat{F}(w)\geqslant\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})+\frac{\sigma}{4}\|\widehat{w}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$.}

As a result, we obtain that

\left(\widehat{F}(w)-\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})\right)^{2}\leqslant L^{2}\|\widehat{w}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\leqslant\frac{4L^{2}}{\sigma}\left(\widehat{F}(w)-\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})\right)\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$.}

Consequently,

\left|\widehat{F}(w)-\widehat{F}(w^{*})\right|\leqslant\max\left\{\widehat{F}(w)-\widehat{F}(\widehat{w}),\widehat{F}(w^{*})-\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})\right\}\leqslant\frac{4L^{2}}{\sigma}\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$.}

(19)

We show that
|f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)|\leqslant 4L^{2}/\sigma
almost surely. Assume the opposite. Let
\mathsf{Z}_{>}\subseteq\mathsf{Z}
be a set of all such

that

. If
\mathsf{Z}_{>}
has a positive probability measure, then we have
\widehat{F}(w)-\widehat{F}(w^{*})>4L^{2}/\sigma
on
\mathsf{Z}_{>}^{\otimes n}
, which contradicts (
19
). Hence,
f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\leqslant 4L^{2}/\sigma
almost surely. Similarly, we can prove that
f(w^{*},Z)-f(w,Z)\leqslant 4L^{2}/\sigma
almost surely.

A.3
Proof of Lemma
4.1

Note that

\displaystyle F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})

\displaystyle=2(P-P_{n})\big(f(\widehat{w},Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)+P\big(f(w^{*},Z)-f(\widehat{w},Z)\big)

\displaystyle\quad+2P_{n}\big(f(\widehat{w},Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big).

According to Assumption
3.1
,
\widehat{F}
is strongly convex with respect to the seminorm, induced by the matrix
\mathsf{H}
.
Taking into account the convexity of
\mathcal{W}
and the equation (
4
), we obtain that

\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})\leqslant\widehat{F}\left(\frac{\widehat{w}+w}{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{F}(w)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|\widehat{w}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$.}

Therefore, it holds that

\widehat{F}(w)\geqslant\widehat{F}(\widehat{w})+\frac{\sigma}{4}\|\widehat{w}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$.}

(20)

Similarly, we deduce that the following inequality holds for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
:

F(w^{*})=\mathbb{E}\widehat{F}(w^{*})\leqslant\mathbb{E}\widehat{F}\left(\frac{w^{*}+w}{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\widehat{F}(w^{*})+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\widehat{F}(w)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\mathbb{E}\|w^{*}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}.

Consequently, we have

F(w)\geqslant F(w^{*})+\frac{\sigma}{4}\mathbb{E}\|w^{*}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $w\in\mathcal{W}$.}

(21)

Observe that
\mathbb{E}\|w^{*}-w\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}=\|w^{*}-w\|_{\mathbb{E}\mathsf{H}}^{2}
.
Then the inequalities (
20
) and (
21
) imply that

P\big(f(w^{*},Z)-f(\widehat{w},Z)\big)\leqslant-\frac{\sigma}{4}\|\widehat{w}-w^{*}\|_{\mathbb{E}\mathsf{H}}^{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad P_{n}\big(f(\widehat{w},Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)\leqslant-\frac{\sigma}{4}\|\widehat{w}-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}.

Thus, it holds that

F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\leqslant 2(P-P_{n})\big(f(\widehat{w},Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{4}\|\widehat{w}-w^{*}\|_{\mathbb{E}\mathsf{H}}^{2}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\widehat{w}-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}.

The last expression does not exceed

2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[(P-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathbb{E}\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right].

We are ready to apply the symmetrization argument. Let
Z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,Z_{n}^{\prime}
be independent copies of
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
, and let
\mathsf{H}^{\prime}=\mathsf{H}(Z^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Z^{\prime}_{n})
be the corresponding matrix from Assumption
3.1
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Phi\left(2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[(P-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathbb{E}\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Phi\left(2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(P_{n}^{\prime}-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right]\right),

where
P_{n}^{\prime}
denotes the expectation with respect to the uniform measure on
Z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,Z_{n}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
stands for the expectation with respect to
Z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,Z_{n}^{\prime}
. Using Jensen’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Phi\left(2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[(P-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathbb{E}\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\Phi\left(2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[(P_{n}^{\prime}-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right]\right).

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}
be i.i.d. Rademacher random variables. Note that, whatever the realization of
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}
is,

(P_{n}^{\prime}-P_{n})\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)

has the same distribution as

(P_{n}^{\prime}-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\Phi\left(2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[(P_{n}^{\prime}-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Phi\left(2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[(P_{n}^{\prime}-P_{n})\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Phi\left(4\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right),

where the last line is due to the triangle inequality. Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Phi\big(F(\widehat{w})-F(w^{*})\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Phi\left(2\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[(P-P_{n})\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\left(\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathbb{E}\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Phi\left(4\sup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\left[P_{n}\varepsilon\big(f(w,Z)-f(w^{*},Z)\big)-\frac{\sigma}{8}\|w-w^{*}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\right]\right).

The claim follows.

A.4
Proof of Lemma
5.2

Let
\mathsf{H}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathsf{H})}\lambda_{j}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}

be the eigendecomposition of
\mathsf{H}
. Here we assume that
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\lambda_{\text{rank}(\mathsf{H})}>0
, and the vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{\text{rank}(\mathsf{H})}
form an orthonormal system.
Define the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of
\mathsf{H}

\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathsf{H})}\lambda_{j}^{-1}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}

and the square roots

\mathsf{H}^{1/2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathsf{H})}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathsf{H})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}.

It is easy to observe that

\Pi=\mathsf{H}^{1/2}\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\text{rank}(\mathsf{H})}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}.

is nothing but the orthogonal projector onto the image of
\mathsf{H}^{1/2}
.
Let
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
be the smallest
u
-net of the Euclidean ball
\mathcal{B}(0,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to the Euclidean norm. It is known that
N\leqslant(6r/u)^{d}
. We show that
\left\{w^{*}+\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}a_{1},\dots,w^{*}+\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}a_{N}\right\}
is a
u
-net of
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
with respect to the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
.
For this purpose, fix any
w\in\mathcal{W}[0,r]
and let

a=\mathsf{H}^{1/2}(w-w^{*}).

By the definition of
\Pi
and
a
,
\Pi a=a
.
Find the closest to
a
element amongst
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
(with respect to the Euclidean distance). Without loss of generality, assume that it is
a_{1}
.
Denote
w^{*}+\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}a_{1}
by
w_{1}
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\|w-w_{1}\|_{\mathsf{H}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathsf{H}^{1/2}(w-w_{1})\|=\|a-\mathsf{H}^{1/2}(w_{1}-w^{*})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|a-\mathsf{H}^{1/2}\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}a_{1}\right\|=\|a-\Pi a_{1}\|=\|\Pi a-\Pi a_{1}\|\leqslant\|a-a_{1}\|\leqslant u.

Hence,
\left\{w^{*}+\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}a_{1},\dots,w^{*}+\left(\mathsf{H}^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}a_{N}\right\}
is a
u
-net of
\mathcal{W}[0,r]
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
. The claim follows.

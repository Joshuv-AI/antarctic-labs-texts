Solving stochastic equations with unbounded nonlinear perturbations Thanks: The first author was supported by National Center for Scientific and Technical Research (CNRST), Morocco.
M. Fkirine and S. Hadd

Abstract
. This paper is interested in semilinear stochastic equations having unbounded nonlinear perturbations in the deterministic part and/or in the random part. Moreover the linear part of these equations is governed by a not necessarily analytic semigroup. The main difficulty with these equations is how to define the concept of mild solutions due to the chosen type of unbounded perturbations. To overcome this problem, we first proved a regularity property of the stochastic convolution with respect to the domain of “admissible” unbounded linear operators (not necessarily closed or closable). This is done using Yosida extensions of such unbounded linear operators. After proving the well-posedness of these equations, we also establish the Feller property for the corresponding transition semigroups. Several examples like heat equations and schrödinger equations with nonlocal perturbations terms are given. Finally, we give an application to a general class of semilinear neutral stochastic equations.

Introduction

We are concerned with the well-posedness and Feller property of two classes of abstract stochastic equations under unbounded nonlinear perturbations. We will work with generators that are not necessarily analytic, so we do not assume any maximal regularity property for the linear part of the stochastic equation. More precisely, we consider the following abstract stochastic equations

\displaystyle dX(t)=AX(t)dt+\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{B}X(t))dW(t),\quad X(0)=\xi,\quad t\in[0,T],

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX(t)=[AX(t)+F(\mathscr{C}X(t))]dt+\mathscr{M}(X(t))dW(t),&t\in[0,T]\\

We end this paper with an application to semilinear stochastic equations of neutral type. We will use product spaces to introduce a semigroup approach to the well-posedness of such equations.

Regularity of the stochastic convolutions for admissible operators

Remark 2.2

Let

Theorem 2.4

Let

(A)
, and let

For all

\displaystyle\begin{split}&W_{A}^{\Phi}(t)\in D(C_{\Lambda}),\quad a.e.\,t\geq 0,\;\mathbb{P}-a.s,\quad and\cr&\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\left\|C_{\Lambda}W_{A}^{\Phi}(t)\right\|^{2}dt\leq\gamma(\alpha)^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\|\Phi(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt.\end{split}

(2.2)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}^{2}(0,\alpha;H)
be the space of all predictable process
\Phi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to L_{2}^{0}(V,H)
such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{\alpha}:=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\|\Phi(s)\|^{2}_{2}ds<+\infty.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{N}^{2}(0,\alpha;H)
and
\beta>\omega_{0}(A)
. By the same arguments as in
[
25
]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\|C\lambda R(\lambda,A)W_{A}^{\Phi}(t)\|^{2}dt\leq\gamma(\alpha)^{2}\||\lambda R(\lambda,A)\Phi(s)|\|_{\alpha}.

(2.3)

This implies that for
\lambda,\mu\in(\beta,\infty),

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\|C\lambda&R(\lambda,A)W_{A}^{\Phi}(t)-C\mu R(\mu,A)W_{A}^{\Phi}(t)\|^{2}dt\cr&\leq\gamma(\alpha)^{2}\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\Phi(s)-\mu R(\mu,A)\Phi(s)\|_{\alpha}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)

\displaystyle\Phi(s)-\mu R(\mu,A)\Phi(s)\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\lambda_{k}\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\Phi(s)e_{k}-\mu R(\mu,A)\Phi(s)e_{k}\|^{2}.

Since

, then
(\lambda R(\lambda,A)\Phi(s))_{\lambda>\beta}
is a Cauchy sequence in

, and hence
(\lambda R(\lambda,A)\Phi(s))_{\lambda>\beta}

\mathcal{N}^{2}(0,\alpha;H)
as well. Now, the inequality (
2.4

(C\lambda R(\lambda,A)W_{A}^{\Phi}(\cdot))_{\lambda>\beta}
is a Cauchy sequence in

. Thus there exists
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\rho(A)\cap\mathbb{R}
such that the sequence
\left(C\lambda_{k}R(\lambda_{k},A)W_{A}^{\Phi}(t)\right)_{k}
converges for a.e

,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. Consequently,
W_{A}^{\Phi}(t)\in D(C_{\Lambda})
for a.e
t\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.

Remark 2.5

.

Now, let

\displaystyle X:=\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{n}~~\text{in}~~{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H),

\displaystyle Y:=\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{\Lambda}X_{n}~~\text{in}~~L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H).

It is clear that
\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(C_{\Lambda}X_{n}(s))ds
converges to
\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(Y(s))ds
as
n\to\infty
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Thus,

X(t)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(Y(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(u(s))ds,

and
X(t)\in D(C_{\Lambda})
for
t\geq 0
a.e,
\mathbb{P}
almost surely. Moreover, we have

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|C_{\Lambda}X_{n}(s)-C_{\Lambda}X(s)\|^{2}\leq c(T)^{2}k^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|C_{\Lambda}X_{n}(s)-Y(s)\|^{2}ds,

for all
T\geq 0
. It follows that
Y(t)=C_{\Lambda}X(t)
and

X(t)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(C_{\Lambda}X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(u(s))dW(s)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let assumptions
(S)
,
(A)
and
(L)
be satisfied. For any initial process
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0},H),
the semilinear stochastic equation (
1.2
) has a unique mild solution
X(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
such that

\displaystyle X(t)\in D(C_{\Lambda}),\quad\text{a.e}~t\geq 0,~\mathbb{P}\text{-almost surely},

\displaystyle X(t)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(C_{\Lambda}X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(X(s))dW(s).

Proof.

Define the map
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
by

\displaystyle(\mathscr{R}u)(t):=X(t,u),

where
X(t,u)
is given by (
3.3
). It suffices to prove that the map
\mathscr{R}
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
. To this end, consider
u,v\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
. A similar calculations as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
lead to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|C_{\Lambda}X(s,u)-C_{\Lambda}X(s,v)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2c(T)^{2}k^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|C_{\Lambda}X(s,u)-C_{\Lambda}X(s,v)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\gamma(T)^{2}k^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-v(s)\|^{2}ds,

for any
t\in[0,T]
. We have
\lim_{t\to 0}c(t)=0
, then we can choose
T>0
such that
2c(T)^{2}k^{2}<\frac{1}{2}
, this implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|C_{\Lambda}X(s,u)-C_{\Lambda}X(s,v)\|^{2}ds\leq 4k^{2}T\gamma(T)^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\|u(t)-v(t)\|^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\|\left(\mathscr{R}u\right)(t)-\left(\mathscr{R}v\right)(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2M^{2}k^{2}e^{2|\beta|T}T\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|C_{\Lambda}X(s,u)-C_{\Lambda}X(s,v)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2M^{2}k^{2}e^{2|\beta|T}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-v(s)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\zeta_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\|u(t)-v(t)\|^{2},

where
\zeta_{T}:=8M^{2}k^{4}e^{2|\beta|T}\gamma(T)^{2}T^{2}+2M^{2}k^{2}e^{2|\beta|T}T
. We choose
T
small enough such that
T=max\{T>0,\quad 2c(T)^{2}k^{2}<\frac{1}{2}~~\text{and}~~\zeta_{T}<1\}
and consequently, by the contraction principle, there exists a unique
v\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
such that

v(t)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(C_{\Lambda}X(s,v))ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(v(s))dW(s).

The case of general
T>0
can be treated by considering the equation in intervals
[0,T_{0}]
,
[T_{0},2T_{0}]
,
\dots
with
T_{0}
such that
T_{0}=max\{T_{0}>0,\quad 2c(T_{0})^{2}k^{2}<\frac{1}{2}~~\text{and}~~\zeta_{T_{0}}<1\}
. On the other hands, from Lemma
3.1
, for all
u\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
there exists a unique
X(\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
such that (
3.3
) is satisfied. Thus, by the uniqueness, there exists a unique
X(\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
such that

\displaystyle X(t)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(C_{\Lambda}X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(X(s))dW(s).

∎

We end this section by giving some examples that satisfy our abstract results. In the following we give two examples, the first example is devoted to kind of Kardar-Parizi-Zhang equation. The second is devoted to the Schrödinger equation with non-local integral term.

Example 3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set and put
H=L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
. We consider the following nonlinear initial value problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX(t,x)=\left[\Delta X(t,x)+f\left((a.\nabla+c)X(t,x)\right)\right]dt\cr\hskip 85.35826pt+b(X(t,x))dW(t,x),&x\in\mathscr{O},t\in[0,T],\\
X(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\mathscr{O},t\in[0,T],\\
X(0,x)=\xi(x),&x\in\mathscr{O},\xi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{O}),\end{cases}

(3.5)

where
a\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
c\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})
and
W(t)
is
Q
-Wiener process on
V=H=L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
. We assume that
f
,
b
are real valued functions and globally Lipschitz. Define
A:D(A)\to H
to be

\displaystyle A\phi=\Delta\phi,\qquad D(A)=H^{2}(\mathscr{O})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O}).

So that
-A
is a strictly
positive densely defined operator on
H
. It is known that
A
generates a strongly continuous and diagonalizable semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
H
. We select
H_{\frac{1}{2}}=D((-A)^{\frac{1}{2}})=H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O})
. We now define the following operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}z=a\cdot\nabla z+cz,\quad z\in H_{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle(F\phi)(x)=f(\phi(x)),\quad x\in\mathscr{O},

\displaystyle(\mathscr{M}(\phi)u)(x)=b(\phi(x))u(x),\quad x\in\mathscr{O},\quad u\in H.

Thus, the system (
3.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX(t)=\left[AX(t)+F\left(\mathscr{C}X(t)\right)\right]dt+\mathscr{M}(X(t))dW(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
X(0)=\xi.\end{cases}

Moreover, it is shown in
[
37
, Example 5.1.4]
that
C:=\mathscr{C}
with domain
D(C)=D(A)
satisfies the condition
(A)
. Also as in Remark
2.2
we have
C_{\Lambda}=\mathscr{C}
.
So, for each
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0},H)
, (
3.5
) has a unique mild solution
X(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
such that

\displaystyle X(t)\in H_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{a.e}~t\geq 0,\mathbb{P}\text{-almost surely},

\displaystyle X(t)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(\mathscr{C}X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(X(s))dW(s).

Example 3.6
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega=\overline{\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}}
,
\Gamma_{0}\cap\Gamma_{1}=\emptyset
. We consider the following semilinear stochastic Schrodinger equation with non-local integral term

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX(t,x)=\left[i\Delta X(t,x)+f\left(i\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\Upsilon(x,y)X(t,y)dy\right)\right]dt\cr\hskip 85.35826pt+b(X(t,x))dW(t,x),&x\in\Omega,t\in[0,T],\\
X(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\Gamma_{0},t\in[0,T],\\
\frac{\partial X}{\partial\nu}(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\Gamma_{1},t\in[0,T],\\
X(0,x)=\xi(x),&x\in\Omega,\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega),\end{cases}

(3.6)

where
\nu
is the unit normal vector,
\Upsilon:\overline{\Omega}\times\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function and
W(t)
is
Q
-Wiener process on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. As usual, we write the equation in an abstract form. We introduce the space
H=L^{2}(\Omega)
. We define the operators

\displaystyle A=i\Delta,\quad D(A)=\left\{g\in H^{2}(\Omega),\qquad g_{|\Gamma_{0}}=\left.\frac{\partial g}{\partial\nu}\right|_{\Gamma_{1}}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}g=i\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\Upsilon(\cdot,y)g(y)dy,\quad g\in H^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle(F\phi)(x)=f(\phi(x)),\quad x\in\Omega,

\displaystyle(\mathscr{M}(\phi)u)(x)=b(\phi(x))u(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\quad u\in H,

and rewrite (
3.6
) as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX(t)=\left[AX(t)+F\left(\mathscr{C}X(t)\right)\right]dt+\mathscr{M}(X(t))dW(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
X(0)=\xi.\end{cases}

It is well known that the operator
A
generates an unitary group
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
H
. Moreover, it is shown in
[
27
, Example 4.2]
that the operator
C:=\mathscr{C}
with domain
D(C)=D(A)
satisfies the condition
(A)
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.4
, the equation (
3.6
) has unique mild solution
X(\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];H)
such that

\displaystyle X(t)\in D(C_{\Lambda}),\quad\text{a.e}~t\geq 0,\mathbb{P}\text{-almost surely},

\displaystyle X(t)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(C_{\Lambda}X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(X(s))dW(s).

4.
The Feller property of the transition semigroup

We are interested here in the continuous dependence of the mild solution of (
1.2
) on the initial data and the boundedness of the moments. Specifically, we have the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
, there exists
C_{T}>0
such that, for arbitrary
\xi
,
\eta\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0},H)
and
t\in[0,T]
, the following estimate holds

\mathbb{E}\|X(t,\xi)-X(t,\nu)\|^{2}\leq C_{T}\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\nu\|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\xi
,
\eta\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0},H)
and set
M=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|T(t)\|
. Let
T_{0}>0
such that
3k^{2}c(T_{0})^{2}<\frac{1}{2}
. By the uniqueness of the solution we have that

X(t,\xi)=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T_{0}]}X_{1}(t,\xi)+\mathbbm{1}_{[T_{0},2T_{0}]}X_{2}(t,\xi),\quad\forall t\in[0,2T_{0}],

where,

\displaystyle X_{1}(t,\xi)=T(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)

\displaystyle F(C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(\xi,s))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}(X_{1}(s,\xi))dW(s)

and

\displaystyle X_{2}(t,\xi)=T(t-T_{0})X_{1}(T_{0},\xi)

\displaystyle+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}T(t-s)F(C_{\Lambda}X_{2}(s,\xi))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}T(t-s)\mathscr{M}\left(X_{2}(s,\xi)\right)dW(s).

For all
t\in[0,T_{0}]
we have

\displaystyle\|C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 3\|C_{\Lambda}T(s)(\xi-\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+3\left\|C_{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{s}T(s-\sigma)\left[F(C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(\sigma,\xi))-F(C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(\sigma,\eta))\right]d\sigma\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+3\left\|C_{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{s}T(s-\sigma)\left[\mathscr{M}(X_{1}(\sigma,\xi))-\mathscr{M}(X_{1}(\sigma,\eta))\right]dW(\sigma)\right\|^{2}.

Using Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.4
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 3\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+3k^{2}c(T_{0})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\eta)\right\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+3k^{2}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|X_{1}(s,\xi)-X_{1}(s,\eta)\right\|^{2}ds.

Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds\cr&\qquad\leq 6\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\eta\|^{2}+6k^{2}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|X_{1}(s,\xi)-X_{1}(s,\eta)\right\|^{2}ds,\end{split}

(4.1)

for all
t\in[0,T_{0}]
. Repeating the same argument as above and using a change of variables, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|C_{\Lambda}X_{2}(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X_{2}(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq 6\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\mathbb{E}\|X(T_{0},\xi)-X(T_{0},\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+6k^{2}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|X_{2}(s,\xi)-X_{2}(s,\eta)\right\|^{2}ds,

for all
t\in[T_{0},2T_{0}]
. Moreover, simple calculations lead to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X(T_{0},\xi)-X(T_{0};\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 3M^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\mu\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+3T_{0}M^{2}k^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X_{1}(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+3M^{2}k^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|X_{1}(s,\xi)-X_{1}(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds.

Using (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X(T_{0},\xi)-X(T_{0};\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(3M^{2}+18T_{0}M^{2}k^{2}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\mu\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(3M^{2}k^{2}+18M^{2}k^{2}T_{0}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|X_{1}(s,\xi)-X_{1}(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds.

It follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|C_{\Lambda}X_{2}(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X_{2}(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds\cr&\qquad\leq 6\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\left(3M^{2}+18M^{2}k^{2}T_{0}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\eta\|^{2}\\
&\qquad+6\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\left(3M^{2}k^{2}+18M^{2}k^{2}T_{0}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left\|X_{1}(s,\xi)-X_{1}(s,\eta)\right\|^{2}ds\\
&\qquad+6k^{2}\gamma(T_{0})^{2}\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|X_{2}(s,\xi)-X_{2}(s,\eta)\right\|^{2}ds.\end{split}

(4.2)

Thus, from (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|C_{\Lambda}X(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds\cr&\qquad\leq C_{T_{0},1}\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\eta\|^{2}+C_{T_{0},2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|X(s,\xi)-X(s,\eta)\right\|^{2}ds\end{split}

(4.3)

for all
t\in[0,2T_{0}]
, where
C_{T_{0},1}
,
C_{T_{0},2}
are constants. This process can be repeated to obtain (
4.3
) for all
t\in[0,T]
,
T>0
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X(t,\xi)-X(t,\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3M^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle+3M^{2}k^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|C_{\Lambda}X(s,\xi)-C_{\Lambda}X(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle+3M^{2}k^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|X(s,\xi)-X(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds,

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Now, by using the inequality (
4.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X(t,\xi)-X(t,\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T,3}\mathbb{E}\|\xi-\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{T,4}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|X(s,\xi)-X(s,\eta)\|^{2}ds,

where
C_{T,3}
,
C_{T,4}
are constants. Using Gronwall’s lemma, the estimate of the theorem follows.
∎

With the use of Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
4.1
, we can now prove the Feller property of the associated semigroup associated to the stochastic evolution equation (
1.2
). In fact, under the condition of Theorem
3.4
, we define

\displaystyle P_{t}\phi(x):=\mathbb{E}(\phi(X(t,x))),\quad t\in[0,T],\;x\in H,

where
\phi(\cdot)
is bounded and Borel measurable function. In addition, we select

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{b}(H):=\left\{\phi:H\to\mathbb{R}:\phi\quad\text{is bounded and continuous function}\right\}.

We have the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under the hypotheses
(S)
,
(A)
and
(L)
we have

\displaystyle P_{t}\mathcal{C}_{b}(H)\subset\mathcal{C}_{b}(H)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

5.
Applications to semilinear stochastic equations of neutral type

We consider the following semi-linear stochastic equation of neutral type

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d(X(t)-\mathscr{D}X_{t})=[A(X(t)-\mathscr{D}X_{t})+F(LX_{t})]dt\cr\hskip 149.37697pt+B(X_{t})dW(t),&t\in[0,T]\\
\left(X(t)-\mathscr{D}X_{t}\right)_{|_{t=0}}=\xi,\qquad X_{0}=\phi,\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
A:D(A)\subset H\to H
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Hilbert space
H
, the process
X:[-r,\infty)\to X
and its history
X_{t}:=X(t+\cdot):[-r,0]\to H
is defined for any
t\geq 0
by

\displaystyle X_{t}(\theta)=\begin{cases}X(t+\theta),&-t\leq\theta\geq 0,\cr\phi(t+\theta),&-r\leq\theta\leq-t.\end{cases}

The initial conditions
X(0)=\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0},H)
,
\phi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}([-r,0],H)
and
W(t)
is a real standard Wiener process. Here
F:H\to H
,
B:L^{2}([-r,0],H)\to H
are nonlinear Lipschitz continuous and
L,\mathscr{D}:W^{1,2}([-r,0],H)\to H
are the following Riemann-Stieltjes integrals

\displaystyle L\phi:=\int_{-r}^{0}d\mu(\theta)\phi(\theta),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{D}\phi:=\int_{-r}^{0}d\nu(\theta)\phi(\theta),

(5.2)

where
\nu,\mu:[-r,0]\to\mathcal{L}(H)
are of bounded variation and non-atomic at zero; that is

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}|\mu|([-\epsilon,0])=0,\qquad\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}|\nu|([-\epsilon,0])=0.

Definition 5.1
.

A pair
(X(t),X(t+\cdot))
is a solution of (
5.1
) if there exist extensions
\tilde{\mathscr{D}}
and
\tilde{L}
of
\mathscr{D}
and
L
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle X_{t}\in D(\tilde{\mathscr{D}})\cap D(\tilde{L})\quad\text{for a.e}~t\geq 0,\mathbb{P}-\text{almost surely},\;and

\displaystyle X(t)=\tilde{\mathscr{D}}X_{t}+T(t)\xi+\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)F(\tilde{L}X_{s})ds+\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)B(X_{s})dW(s).

The equation (
5.1
) was treated by Webb
[
38
, Section 4]
in the particular case
\mathscr{D}\equiv B\equiv 0
. The method employed consists in constructing a semigroup of nonlinear operators which may be associated with the solutions of this equation.

In order to reformulate the equation (
5.1
) to the equation (
1.2
), we select

\displaystyle Z(t):=X(t)-\mathscr{D}X_{t},\qquad t\geq 0.

(5.3)

Then the neutral equation (
5.1
) becomes boundary valued stochastic equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dZ(t)=[AZ(t)+F(LX_{t})]dt+B(X_{t})dW(t),&t\in[0,T]\\
Z(0)=\xi,\qquad X_{0}=\phi\\
X(t)=Z(t)+\mathscr{D}X_{t},&t\geq 0.\end{cases}

(5.4)

We now introduce product spaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=H\times L^{2}([-r,0],H),\qquad\mathcal{Z}:=D(A)\times W^{1,2}([-r,0],H).

The space
\mathcal{H}
is endowed with the following norm

\displaystyle\left\|(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\right\|:=\|x\|_{H}+\|g\|_{L^{2}([-r,0],H)}

On the other hand, we introduce the operators

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}:=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&\frac{d}{d\theta}\end{pmatrix},\quad D(\mathfrak{A}):=\left\{(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathcal{Z}:\psi(0)=x+\mathscr{D}\psi\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&L\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}:D(\mathfrak{A})\to\mathcal{H},

\displaystyle Qg=g^{\prime},\qquad D(Q)=\{g\in W^{1,2}([-r,0],H):g(0)=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},\quad\mathcal{F}(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})=(\begin{smallmatrix}F(x)\\
0\end{smallmatrix}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},\quad\mathscr{M}(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})=(\begin{smallmatrix}B(g)\\
0\end{smallmatrix}).

Moreover, we introduce the following new state

\displaystyle\varrho(t)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Z(t)\\
X_{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\qquad t\geq 0.

With the above notation, the problem (
5.4
) is reformulated as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\varrho(t)=[\mathfrak{A}\varrho(t)+\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{P}\varrho(t))]dt+\mathscr{M}(\varrho(t))dW(t),&t\in[0,T]\\
\varrho(0)=(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\
\phi\end{smallmatrix}).\end{cases}

(5.5)

Theorem 5.2
.

Let assumption
(S)
be satisfied. The neutral equation (
5.1
) admits a unique solution
(X(t),X(t+\cdot))
such that

\displaystyle X_{t}\in D(\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda})\cap D(L_{0,\Lambda})\quad\text{for a.e}~t\geq 0,\mathbb{P}-\text{almost surely},\;and

\displaystyle X(t)=\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}X_{t}+T(t)\xi+\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)F(L_{0,\Lambda}X_{s})ds+\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)B(X_{s})dW(s),

where
L_{0,\Lambda}
and
\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}
are the Yosida extensions of
L
and
\mathscr{D}
with respect to
Q
, respectively.

Proof.

It is known that (see e.g.
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
) that the operator
\mathfrak{A}
is the generator of a
C_{0}
–semigroup
(\mathfrak{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{H}
. On the other hand, as
F:H\to H
and
B:L^{2}([-r,0],H)\to H
are Lipschitz functions, then it is so for the functions
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathscr{M}
. Hence the condition
{\bf(L)}
is satisfied for the equation (
5.5
). In order to apply Theorem
3.4
, it is necessary to show that the operators
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathcal{P}
satisfy the condition
{\bf(A)}
. It is known (see e.g.
[
11
, Chap.II]
) that
Q
generates the left shift semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}([-r,0],H)
given by

\displaystyle(S(t)g)(\theta):=\begin{cases}g(t+\theta),&t+\theta\leq 0,\\
0,&t+\theta>0,\end{cases},

for
t\geq 0,

\theta\in[-r,0]
, and
g\in L^{2}([-r,0],H)
. On the other hand, we define the operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad D(\mathcal{A})=D(A)\times D(Q),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=\left(I\;\;\mathscr{D}\right):D(\mathcal{A})\to H.

Clearly,
\mathcal{A}
generates a diagonal strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathcal{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{H}
. As
\mathscr{D}_{0}=\mathscr{D}
with domain
D(\mathscr{D}_{0})=D(Q)
is admissible for
Q,
it follows that
\mathcal{C}
is admissible for
\mathcal{A}
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}
the Yosida extension of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, it is proved in
[
19
, Theorem 19]
, that
{\rm Im}(\mathfrak{T}(t))\subset D(\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda})
for almost every
t\geq 0,
and for any
\alpha>0
there exists a constant
\kappa(\alpha)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{T}(\cdot)(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\right\|_{L^{2}([0,\alpha],X)}\leq\kappa(\alpha)\left\|(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\right\|,\qquad\forall(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathcal{H}.

(5.6)

Clearly we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}=\left(I\;\;\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}\right),\quad D(\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda})=H\times D(\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}),

(5.7)

where
\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}
is the Yosida extension of
\mathscr{D}
with respect to
Q
.

In order to give an expression of the semigroup
\mathfrak{T}
which can help us to prove the admissibility of
\mathcal{P}
for
\mathfrak{A},
we select

\displaystyle\left(\Upsilon_{t}u\right)(\theta)=\begin{cases}u(t+\theta),&t+\theta\geq 0,\cr 0,&t+\theta<0\end{cases},

for any
t\geq 0,\;\theta\in[-r,0]
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H)
.
By using

[
19
, Theorem 19]
, the semigroup
\mathfrak{T}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}(t)(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})=\begin{pmatrix}T(t)x\\
S(t)g+\Upsilon_{t}\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{T}(\cdot)(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\end{pmatrix}

(5.8)

for any
t\geq 0
and
(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathcal{H}
. We select,

\displaystyle L_{0}:=L_{|D(Q)}.

Denote by
L_{0,\Lambda}
the Yosida extension of
L_{0}
with respect to
Q
. According to
[
17
, Theorem 3]
,
L_{0}
is an admissible operator for
Q,

{\rm Im}(\Upsilon_{t})\subset D(L_{0,\Lambda})
and

\displaystyle\|L_{0,\Lambda}\Upsilon_{t}u\|_{L^{2}([0,\alpha],H)}\leq c_{\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,t],H)},

(5.9)

for any
\alpha>0,

u\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H)
and some constant
c_{\alpha}>0
. On the other hand, by using
[
18
, Lemma 3.6]
, we have
W^{1,2}([-r,0],H)\subset D(L_{0,\Lambda})
and
L_{0,\Lambda}\equiv L
on
W^{1,2}([-r,0],H)
. Thus, for any
(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\in D(\mathfrak{A}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\mathfrak{T}(t)(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&L_{0,\Lambda}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\mathfrak{T}(t)(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}L_{0,\Lambda}S(t)g+L_{0,\Lambda}\Upsilon_{t}\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{T}(\cdot)(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus the admissibility of
\mathcal{P}
for
\mathfrak{A}
now follows from the admissibility of
L_{0}
for
Q
, and the inequalities (
5.6
) and (
5.9
). Thus the condition
(A)
holds as well. Now by Theorem
3.4
, the semi-linear stochastic equation (
5.5
) (hence the neutral stochastic equation (
5.1
)) has a unique mild solution
\varrho\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}([0,T];\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varrho(t)=\mathfrak{T}(t)(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\
\phi\end{smallmatrix})+\int^{t}_{0}\mathfrak{T}(t-s)&\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{A}}\varrho(s))ds\cr&+\int^{t}_{0}\mathfrak{T}(t-s)\mathscr{M}(\varrho(s))dW(s)\end{split}

(5.10)

for any
t\geq 0
,
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0},H)
and
\phi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}([-r,0],H)
.

Let us now give an explicit expression to the first component of
\varrho(t)
. In fact, from (
5.6
) we
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}
is an admissible operator for
\mathfrak{A}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Z(t)\\

almost surely. On the other hand, the relation (

) is a feedback low associated with the system (

) and can be reformulated as

\displaystyle X(t)=\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}\varrho(t)=Z(t)+\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}X_{t}

(5.11)

for a.e.
t\geq 0
. Moreover, by using similar arguments as in
[
19
]
, one can see that
\Xi:=H\times[D(L_{0,\Lambda})\cap D(\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}]\subset D(\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{A}})
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&L_{0,\Lambda}\\

(5.12)

Now by combining (

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle=X(t)-\mathscr{D}_{0,\Lambda}X_{t}

\displaystyle=T(t)\xi+\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)F(L_{0,\Lambda}X_{s})ds+\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)B(X_{s})dW(s).

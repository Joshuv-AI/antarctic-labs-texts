Renormings preserving local geometry at countably many points in spheres of Banach spaces and applications
Andrés Quilis

Abstract
. We develop tools to produce equivalent norms with specific local geometry around infinitely many points in the sphere of a Banach space via an inductive procedure. We combine this process with smoothness results and techniques to solve two open problems posed in the recently published monograph [ GMZ22 ] by A. J. Guirao, V. Montesinos and V. Zizler. Specifically, on the one hand we construct in every separable Banach space admitting a C k C^{k} -smooth norm an equivalent norm which is C k C^{k} -smooth but fails to be uniformly Gâteaux in any direction; and on the other hand we produce in c 0 ​ ( Γ ) c_{0}(\Gamma) for any infinite Γ \Gamma a C ∞ C^{\infty} -smooth norm whose ball is dentable but whose sphere lacks any extreme points.

1.
Introduction

In an infinite-dimensional Banach space, the existence of equivalent norms with different properties related to differentiability and convexity has a profound impact on the structure and geometry of the space and its dual. This has lead to Renormings in Banach spaces being a very active and interesting topic of research in Banach space theory. We refer the reader to
[
DGZ93
,
God01
]
and to the recent monograph
[
GMZ22
]
for a deep study of this subject, and as a sample of the wealth of results in it. For a selection of relatively recent articles dealing with concepts close the topics we discuss in this note we point to
[
Ryc05
,
JR07
,
HJ09
,
HMZ12
,
HT14
,
HR17
]
.

Differentiability and convexity qualities of a given norm in a Banach space are related to the geometry of its unit sphere. Among these properties, some relate to the local shape around a specific point in the sphere, while others ask for certain uniformity on the entire set. As a central example to this note, notice that to check that the norm is Fréchet differentiable, we only have to look at a small enough neighborhood of each point in the sphere individually. On the other hand, to obtain that the norm is uniformly Gâteaux in a particular direction, the size of the neighborhood that we check cannot depend on the point of the sphere we are considering, but only on the quality of the approximation to the derivative.

There are many examples of norms in Banach spaces satisfying strong local properties but failing their uniform counterpart. A well known example is the fact that a Banach space admitting a uniformly rotund norm is necessarily superreflexive (see e.g.: Theorem IV.4.1 in
[
DGZ93
]
), while the class of Banach spaces admitting a locally uniformly rotund norm is considerably larger (see e.g.: Chapter VII in
[
DGZ93
]
), including for instance all weakly Lindelöf determined spaces or all duals of Asplund spaces.

More relevant to our discussion is the result proven in
[
BF93
]
by Borwein and Fabián, which states that in every separable Banach space there exists an equivalent norm which is uniformly Gâteaux but is not everywhere Fréchet differentiable.

In this note we develop a process to construct norms in Banach spaces satisfying strong local properties of smoothness, which fail to be uniformly Gâteaux differentiable in any direction. In the special case of
c_{0}(\Gamma)
, the same process allows us to obtain a
C^{\infty}
-smooth norm locally dependent on finitely many coordinates (LFC), which moreover satisfies the somewhat opposite property of having a dentable unit ball.

Briefly and intuitively, the strategy used to produce our renormings consists in defining countably many equivalent norms in a Banach space, all of which satisfy the desired local properties of smoothness but with decreasing quality around respective specific points. The final step of the process consists in combining all of these renormings into a single one which preserves the local geometry of each individual renorming at their respective critical region. The idea to make this combination possible is to define the sequence of individual renormings using an almost biorthogonal sequence of pairs
\{(x_{n},f_{n})\}\subset S_{X}\times S_{X^{*}}
, where moreover
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is weak
∗
null. This will ensure that the different renormings do not overlap each other excessively in their critical regions and that the final renorming is indeed equivalent to the original one.

Let us now state the two specific applications that we obtain:

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space with a
C^{k}
-smooth norm with
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then
X
admits an equivalent
C^{k}
-smooth norm which is not uniformly Gâteaux in any direction. Moreover, the unit ball in this norm is dentable.

Theorem B
.

For every infinite set
\Gamma
, the space
c_{0}(\Gamma)
admits an equivalent norm which is
C^{\infty}
-smooth and LFC whose unit ball is dentable. Moreover, this norm is not uniformly Gâteaux in any direction.

The motivation for these applications comes from the open problems chapter of the monograph
[
GMZ22
]
. Specifically, the two previous theorems answer the two following questions respectively:

Question 1

(Page 496, 52.1.2.1 in
[
GMZ22
]
)
.

Can every infinite-dimensional separable reflexive space be renormed by a Fréchet differentiable norm which is not uniformly Gâteaux?

Indeed, it is well known that every separable reflexive space admits a Fréchet smooth (and thus
C^{1}
-smooth) renorming (see e.g.
[
DGZ93
]
), so Theorem
A
solves this question in particular.

Question 2

(Page 497, 52.1.3.3 in
[
GMZ22
]
)
.

Is there a norm on
c_{0}
which is
C^{\infty}
-smooth, and whose ball is dentable but whose sphere has no extreme points?

This second question is answered by Theorem
B
since the sphere of an LFC norm in an infinite-dimensional Banach space cannot have any extreme points.

The authors of
[
GMZ22
]
also include a weaker version for each one of these two questions: namely, they ask whether every infinite-dimensional separable superreflexive space can be renormed by a Fréchet smooth norm which is not uniformly Fréchet (Page 496, 52.1.2.4); and if
c_{0}
can be renormed with a
C^{2}
-smooth norm whose ball is dentable (Page 498, 52.1.4.2). Hence, both of these problems are also now solved.

Let us finish the introduction by explaining the structure of this note. The content is divided into three sections: Section 2 is dedicated to fixing the notation and giving preliminary definitions. Section 3 contains the construction of the basic renorming from which we will obtain the final result. We study this renorming by giving quantitative estimates on the speed of convergence for the derivative of the norm at specific points, and on the diameter of certain slices of the unit ball. Finally, in section 4 we use known and deep results in Banach space theory and smoothness to develop the process to combine the individual renormings in the previous section into one final renorming in a suitable way.

Given a norm

3.
A renorming with suitable local properties

In this section we define an equivalent norm in any Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
associated to a pair
(x_{0},f_{0})\in S_{\|\cdot\|}\times S_{\|\cdot\|^{*}}
such that
\langle f_{0},x_{0}\rangle=1
and to a positive number
0<\delta<1/2
. After defining this norm we will obtain some differentiability and dentability estimates.

Fix a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
. For each equivalent norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
in
X
, each
f\in S_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}^{*}}
and each
0<r<1
, define the sets:

\displaystyle C^{+}(f,r,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})

\displaystyle=\{y\in X\colon\langle f,y\rangle\geq r{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\},~\text{and}

\displaystyle C^{-}(f,r,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})

\displaystyle=\{y\in X\colon\langle f,y\rangle\leq-r{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\},

which are closed convex cones in
X
. We define as well the cone
C(f,r,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})=C^{+}(f,r,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})\cup C^{-}(f,r,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})
. We will write
C^{+}(f,r)
,
C^{-}(f,r)
and

to denote the cones in the starting norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let us state a simple fact about some points in the complement of these cones that will be useful in the last section:

Fact 1
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space, let
f_{0}\in S_{\|\cdot\|^{*}}
and let
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
. If
x\in X
satisfies that
|\langle x,f_{0}\rangle|<\frac{\|x\|}{8}
, then
y\notin C(f_{0},1-\delta)
for all
y\in B_{\|\cdot\|}\left(x,\frac{\|x\|}{8}\right)
.

Proof.

The proof of this fact is elementary.

3.1.
Definition of the norm

We proceed to define the unit ball of the desired norm. Consider a pair
(x_{0},f_{0})\in S_{\|\cdot\|}\times S_{\|\cdot\|^{*}}
with
\langle f_{0},x_{0}\rangle=1
and fix
0<\delta<1/2
for the rest of the section. Consider the set:

\displaystyle B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=\overline{\text{conv}}\left(\left(B_{\|\cdot\|}\setminus C\left(f_{0},1-\delta\right)\right)\bigcup\left\{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0},-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}\right\}\right),

which is a closed absolutely convex set contained in the unit ball
B_{\|\cdot\|}
. We are going to show that this set is the unit ball of an equivalent norm in
X
. To this end, let us define an auxiliary function which will be used throughout the rest of the section:

For
y\in X
, notice that there exists
t_{0}\geq 0
such that

(1-\delta)\|y+(t-\langle f_{0},y\rangle)x_{0}\|\leq t

for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Since
(1-\delta)\|y-\langle f_{0},y\rangle x_{0}\|\geq 0
, by continuity we obtain that there exists a non-negative scalar
\psi(y)
so that

\psi(y)=(1-\delta)\|y+(\psi(y)-\langle f_{0},y\rangle)x_{0}\|.

Moreover, we have that if
(1-\delta)\|y+(t-\langle f_{0},y\rangle)x_{0}\|\leq t
for some
t\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
, then
(1-\delta)\|y+(t+\varepsilon-\langle f_{0},y\rangle)x_{0}\|<t+\varepsilon
, which implies that
\psi(y)
is the unique non-negative number satisfying its defining equality for every
y\in X
.

We can thus consider the well defined function
\psi\colon X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
as the map such that
\psi(y)
is the unique non-negative number satisfying
\psi(y)=(1-\delta)\|y+(\psi(y)-\langle f_{0},y\rangle)x_{0}\|
. Moreover, a similar reasoning provides that
\psi
is a subadditive function. We have as well that
\psi
is positively homogeneous, i.e.: for every
\lambda\geq 0
and every
y\in X
it holds that
\psi(\lambda y)=\lambda\psi(y)
.

Additionally, it follows from the definition that for every
y\in X
and every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that
\psi(y+\lambda x_{0})=\psi(y)
; in other words,
\psi
is invariant by translations in the direction of
x_{0}
. Importantly, we also obtain that
\psi(y)=0
if and only if
y=\langle f_{0},y\rangle x_{0}
for every
y\in X
.

1
shows the geometric intuition behind the function
\psi
.

With the definition of the function
\psi
fixed for the rest of the section, and with its basic properties we have discussed, we can now find a better description of the norm we are constructing:

Lemma 3.1
.

The set
B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
as defined above is the unit ball of the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
defined by:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=\begin{cases}\|y\|,~&\text{if }y\notin C\left(f_{0},1-\delta\right),\\
\frac{\psi(y)}{1-\delta}+\frac{\langle f_{0},y\rangle-\psi(y)}{1-\frac{\delta}{2}},&\text{if }y\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta),\\
\frac{\psi(-y)}{1-\delta}+\frac{\langle f_{0},-y\rangle-\psi(-y)}{1-\frac{\delta}{2}},&\text{if }y\in C^{-}(f_{0},1-\delta),\end{cases}

which is an equivalent norm in
(X,\|\cdot\|)
with

\|\cdot\|\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta}\|\cdot\|.

Proof.

Since
\psi(0)=0
, the definition of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
is not problematic at this point and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|0\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=0
. Notice as well that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
is non-negative for all
y\in X
because
\psi(y)\leq\langle f_{0},y\rangle
for all
y\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
.

We start by proving that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=0
if and only if
y=0
. We have already shown that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|0\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=0
. Suppose now that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=0
for some
y\in X
. If
y\notin C\left(f_{0},1-\delta\right)
a contradiction follows from the fact that
\|\cdot\|
is a norm. On the other hand, if
y\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
, then the inequality
\psi(y)\leq\langle f_{0},y\rangle
implies both that
\psi(y)=0
and
\langle f_{0},y\rangle=0
. The first condition means that
y=\langle f_{0},y\rangle x_{0}
and with the second we obtain that
y=0
. The final possibility when
y\in C^{-}(f_{0},1-\delta)
follows from the previous case.

Since
\psi
is positively homogeneous and
-\lambda C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)\subset C^{-}(f_{0},1-\delta)
and
-\lambda C^{-}(f_{0},1-\delta)\subset C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
for every
\lambda\geq 0
, we have that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
is homogeneous.

To finish proving that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
is a norm, it only remains to show that it satisfies the triangle inequality. We will do this by proving that
B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=\{y\in X\colon{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq 1\}
, and the triangle inequality will follow by a straightforward convexity argument. Moreover, this shows as well that
B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
is the unit ball of the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
.

Suppose that
y\in B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
. Then
\|y\|\leq 1
. If
y\notin C\left(f_{0},1-\delta\right)
, we trivially have that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=\|y\|\leq 1
. If
y\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
, write
y=\lambda z+(1-\lambda)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}
for some
z\in B_{\|\cdot\|}
with
|\langle f_{0},z\rangle|<(1-\delta)\|z\|
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Using the properties of the map
\psi
and the fact that
\psi(y)\leq\langle f_{0},y\rangle
and
\psi(z)\geq\langle f_{0},z\rangle
, we obtain that

\lambda\leq\frac{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)+\psi(z)-\langle f_{0},z\rangle}

and thus we can write

y=\mu z_{1}+(1-\mu)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0},

where
0\leq\mu=\lambda\left(1+\frac{\psi(z)-\langle f_{0},z\rangle}{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}\right)\leq 1
and

z_{1}=\frac{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)+\psi(z)-\langle f_{0},z\rangle}(z+(\psi(z)-\langle f_{0},z\rangle)x_{0})\in B_{\|\cdot\|}.

Notice that
\langle f_{0},z_{1}\rangle=(1-\delta)\|z_{1}\|
and thus
\psi(z_{1})=\langle f_{0},z_{1}\rangle
.
Then, using the definition of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
and the properties of
\psi
we have that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}

\displaystyle=1-\mu+\mu\left(\frac{\psi(z_{1})}{1-\delta}+\frac{\langle f_{0},z_{1}\rangle-\psi(z_{1})}{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mu+\mu\left(\frac{\psi(z_{1})}{1-\delta}\right)\leq 1.

A similar argument shows that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq 1
if
y\in C^{-}(f_{0},1-\delta)
as well. Hence
B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\subset\{y\in X\colon{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq 1\}
.

To show the converse inclusion, it suffices to prove that
y\in B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
whenever
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=1
, again by virtue of the homogeneity of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
. Given such a vector
y\in X
, if
y\notin C\left(f_{0},1-\delta\right)
there is nothing to prove. Suppose then first that
y\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
. We may write

y=a\frac{1-\delta}{\psi(y)}\left(y+(\psi(y)-\langle f_{0},y\rangle)x_{0}\right)+b\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0},

where
a=\frac{\psi(y)}{1-\delta}
and
b=\frac{\langle f_{0},y\rangle-\psi(y)}{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}
. Notice that
a
and
b
are positive numbers such that
a+b={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=1
, and that the vector
\frac{1-\delta}{\psi(y)}\left(y+(\psi(y)-\langle f_{0},y\rangle)x_{0}\right)
lies in the closure of the set
B_{\|\cdot\|}\setminus C\left(f_{0},1-\delta\right)
. This shows that
y\in B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
. We may follow a similar process to show that
y\in B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
whenever
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}=1
and
y\in C^{-}(f_{0},1-\delta)
.

It remains to show that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
is an equivalent norm. We have already shown that its unit ball is contained in the ball
B_{\|\cdot\|}
. Hence, we will finish the proof by showing that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq 1
for all
y\in(1-\delta)B_{\|\cdot\|}
. If
y\in(1-\delta)B_{\|\cdot\|}
and
y
does not belong to
C(f_{0},1-\delta)
then
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq 1
holds trivially. Otherwise, if
y\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
, using that
\langle f_{0},y\rangle\geq\psi(y)
we have that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq\frac{\langle f_{0},y\rangle}{1-\delta}\leq 1.

The same holds for
y\in C^{-}(f_{0},1-\delta)
, and the result is proven.
∎

3.2.
Quantitative estimates in the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}

First we obtain an estimate on the “non-differentiability” of the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
at the point
\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
h\in S_{\|\cdot\|}
, the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}+th\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}-{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}}{t}\geq\frac{\delta-(4-\delta)|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}{2\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)(2-\delta)}

holds for all
0<t<\frac{\delta\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}{(1-\delta)+|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}
.

Proof.

Fix
h\in S_{\|\cdot\|}
. First we obtain a lower bound on the value
\psi(h)
. Specifically, we get

\displaystyle\psi(h)

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\|h+\psi(h)x_{0}-\langle f_{0},h\rangle x_{0}\|

\displaystyle\geq(1-\delta)\left(1-\psi(h)-|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|\right),

from where we deduce that

\psi(h)\geq\frac{1-\delta}{2-\delta}\left(1-|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|\right).

Let
0<t<\frac{\delta\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}{(1-\delta)+|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}
. Then we have that
\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}+th
belongs to the cone
C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
. Hence:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}+th\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}

\displaystyle=1+t\left(\frac{\delta\psi(h)}{2(1-\delta)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}+\frac{\langle f_{0},h\rangle}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1+t\left(\frac{\delta(1-|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|)-2(2-\delta)|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}{2(2-\delta)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=1+t\left(\frac{\delta-(4-\delta)|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}{2(2-\delta)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}\right).

The desired inequality follows immediately.
∎

We can also get an upper bound on the diameter of the slices generated by the functional
f_{0}
close to the point
\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}
:

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
0<\varepsilon<\delta/2
we have that

\text{diam}_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}}\left(S\left(B_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}},f_{0},1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\right)\leq\left(\frac{8-2\delta}{\delta}\right)\varepsilon.

Proof.

Fix
x\in S\left(B_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}},f_{0},1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\varepsilon\right)
. Since
\langle f_{0},x\rangle>1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\varepsilon>1-\delta
and
x\in B_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
, we necessarily have that
x\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\delta)
. Therefore, there exists
z\in B_{\|\cdot\|}
with
|\langle f_{0},z\rangle|<(1-\delta)\|z\|
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
such that

x=\lambda z+(1-\lambda)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}.

Hence, we have that

1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\varepsilon<\langle f_{0},x\rangle\leq\lambda(1-\delta)+(1-\lambda)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right),

from which we obtain the bound
\lambda<\frac{2}{\delta}\varepsilon
. Now, a simple computation yields:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq\lambda\left(2-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\leq\left(\frac{4-\delta}{\delta}\right)\varepsilon.

A straightforward use of the triangle inequality finishes the proof.
∎

The third estimate we obtain of the new norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
quantifies how much larger this norm is than the starting norm
\|\cdot\|
around
\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}
. The proof follows directly from the definition:

Lemma 3.4
.

If
x\in C\left(f_{0},1-\frac{\delta}{4}\right)
, then

\|x\|\leq\frac{4-2\delta}{4-\delta}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}.

Proof.

Clearly, it is enough to prove the result for
x\in C^{+}(f_{0},1-\frac{\delta}{4})
. In this case, we have that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\psi(x)}{\delta(1-\delta)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}+\frac{\langle f_{0},x\rangle}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4-\delta}{4-2\delta}\|x\|,

because
\psi(x)
is non-negative.
∎

In
c_{0}(\Gamma)
, the canonical biorthogonal system
\{e_{\gamma};e_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
produces norms with an additional property. We say that a norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
in
c_{0}(\Gamma)
is a
lattice norm
if
\|x\|\leq\|y\|
whenever
x,y\in c_{0}
and
|x_{\gamma}|\leq|y_{\gamma}|
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Clearly the usual supremum norm on
c_{0}(\Gamma)
is a lattice norm.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a set and let
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
. The norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(e_{\gamma_{0}},e_{\gamma_{0}}),\delta}
is a lattice norm.

Proof.

Notice that

(1)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{0}),\delta}=\frac{\delta\psi(x)}{2(1-\delta)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}+\frac{x_{\gamma_{0}}}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}

for all
x\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
with
x\in C^{+}(e_{\gamma_{0}},1-\delta)
.

Let
x
and
y
be two points in
c_{0}(\Gamma)
such that
|x_{\gamma}|\leq|y_{\gamma}|
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. We may assume without loss of generality that both
x_{\gamma_{0}}
and
y_{\gamma_{0}}
are non-negative, since
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(e_{\gamma_{0}},e_{\gamma_{0}}),\delta}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|-x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(e_{\gamma_{0}},e_{\gamma_{0}}),\delta}
for all
x\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
. We can assume as well that
x\in C^{+}(e_{\gamma_{0}},1-\delta)
, since otherwise
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{0}),\delta}=\|x\|_{\infty}\leq\|y\|_{\infty}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{0}),\delta}
and we are done.

Suppose then that
x\in C^{+}(e_{\gamma_{0}},1-\delta)
. Since
\psi(x)=(1-\delta)\|x+(\psi(x)-x_{\gamma_{0}})e_{\gamma_{0}}\|_{\infty}
and the
\gamma_{0}
-th coordinate of
x+(\psi(x)-x_{\gamma_{0}})e_{\gamma_{0}}
is exactly
\psi(x)
, it follows that

\|x+(\psi(x)-x_{\gamma_{0}})e_{\gamma_{0}}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\gamma\neq\gamma_{0}}|x_{\gamma}|.

The same argument can be applied to
y+(\psi(y)-y_{\gamma_{0}})e_{\gamma_{0}}
to obtain that

\|y+(\psi(y)-y_{\gamma_{0}})e_{\gamma_{0}}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\gamma\neq\gamma_{0}}|y_{\gamma}|.

Therefore,
\psi(x)\leq\psi(y)
. If
y\in C^{+}(e_{\gamma_{0}},1-\delta)
, simply applying equation (
1
) yields the result. Otherwise, if
y_{\gamma_{0}}<(1-\delta)\|y\|_{\infty}
, we have that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{0}),\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\psi(x)}{2(1-\delta)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}+\frac{x_{\gamma_{0}}}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle<\frac{\frac{\delta}{2}\|y\|_{\infty}}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}+\frac{(1-\delta)\|y\|_{\infty}}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}=\|y\|_{\infty}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{0}),\delta},

which finishes the proof.

∎

3.3.
Approximations to the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}

We finish this section by showing that a good enough approximation of the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}
also satisfies similar estimates to the ones appearing in Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
. The proof of the following result is a straightforward computation.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\eta>0
, and let
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\eta}
be a norm on
X
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\eta}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}\leq(1+\eta){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\eta}
. Then we have:

(i)

For all
h\in S_{\|\cdot\|}
, the inequality:

D\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\eta},\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0},h,t\right)\geq\left(\frac{2}{1+\eta}\right)\left(\frac{\delta-(4-\delta)|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}{2\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)(2-\delta)}-\frac{\eta}{|t|}\right)

holds for all
0<|t|<\frac{\delta\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}{(1-\delta)+|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}
.

(ii)

For all
\varepsilon>0
,

\text{diam}_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}}\left(S\left(B_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{\eta}},f_{0},1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\right)\leq\left(\frac{8-2\delta}{\delta}\right)\left(\varepsilon+\eta\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\right).

Proof.

For
(i)
, we obtain first the following inequality for a fixed
h\in S_{\|\cdot\|}
and
0<t<\frac{\delta\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}{(1-\delta)+|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}
using Lemma
3.2
:

\displaystyle\frac{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}+th\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\eta}-{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\eta}}{t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1+\eta}\left(\frac{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}+th\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}-{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)x_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}-\eta}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1+\eta}\left(\frac{\delta-(4-\delta)|\langle f_{0},h\rangle|}{2\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)(2-\delta)}-\frac{\eta}{t}\right).

Since the previous inequality also holds for
-h
, item
(i)
follows immediately.

Part
(ii)
follows from the fact that

\frac{1}{1+\eta}S\left(B_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{\eta}},f_{0},1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\subset S\left(B_{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{(x_{0},f_{0}),\delta}},f_{0},1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon+\eta\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}{1+\eta}\right)

and Lemma
3.3
.
∎

4.
Constructing the final renorming

The following lemma allows us to combine a pair of smooth norms into one equally smooth norm that coincides with one of the initial ones at specific regions. No new ideas are introduced in our proof, which is a simple application of Corollary 1.1.23 in
[
Rus18
]
by Russo, where the reader can find the background for this type of smoothening techniques.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, let
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, and let
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{1}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}
be two equivalent
C^{k}
-smooth norms in
X
. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists another equivalent
C^{k}
-smooth norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
such that for all
x\in X
:

(i)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{i}
if
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{j}\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{i}
, with
i\neq j\in\{1,2\}
.

(ii)

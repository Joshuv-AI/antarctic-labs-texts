Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon (NLKG) and sinh-Gordon equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial^{2}_{t}u+u-\Delta u+f(u)=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
u(x,t)
is a complex valued function of
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and

f(u)=\pm u^{1+\alpha},\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
or
f(u)=\sinh u-u,\,\sinh u=(e^{u}-e^{-u})/2

NLKG is a basic model in nonlinear relativistic equation of mathematical physics
[
48

64

for

,
\alpha<\infty
for
d=1,2
. Ginibre and Velo
[

showed the global well-posedness of finite energy solutions in the energy subcritical case. The existence of the scattering operators in the energy subcritical cases were studied by Strauss
[

in dimensions 1 and 2 for large data, respectively. In 3D energy critical case

[

and by Grillakis
[

for the general data. In the energy critical case
\alpha=4/(d-2)

The main purpose of this paper is to study NLKG (

1.1
Function spaces
E^{\sigma,s}

be the Schwartz space and

\mathscr{S}_{1}

and their linear combinations

1.2
Critical and super-critical spaces

First, let us observe that if
u
is a solution of NLKG (
1
) with
f(u)=\pm u^{\alpha+1}
,
then
u_{\lambda}(x,t):=\lambda^{2/\alpha}u(\lambda x,\lambda t)
solves

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial^{2}_{t}v+\lambda^{2}v-\Delta v\pm v^{\alpha+1}=0,\\
v(x,0)=u_{0,\lambda}(x),\ v_{t}(x,0)=u_{1,\lambda}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
u_{0,\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2/\alpha}u_{0}(\lambda x),\ u_{1,\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2/\alpha+1}u_{1}(\lambda x).
From the scaling argument, we see that
(u_{\lambda},\partial_{t}u_{\lambda})|_{t=0}
is invariant in homogeneous Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{\sigma_{c}}\times\dot{H}^{\sigma_{c}-1}

(\dot{H}^{\sigma}:=(-\Delta)^{-\sigma/2}L^{2})
, where

\displaystyle\sigma_{c}:=d/2-2/\alpha.

(7)

For any
\lambda>0
,
H^{\sigma_{c}}\times H^{\sigma_{c}-1}
is said to be a
critical Sobolev space
for NLKG (
1.2
).
H^{\sigma}\times H^{\sigma-1}
with
\sigma>\sigma_{c}
(
\sigma<\sigma_{c}
) is said to be the
subcritical (supercritical) Sobolev space
. Any Banach function space
X
satisfying
H^{\sigma}\times H^{\sigma-1}\subset X
for some
\sigma<\sigma_{c}
is said to be a
supercritical space
for NLKG.
The inclusion (
4
) implies that any supercritical Sobolev space
H^{\kappa}\times H^{\kappa-1}
(
\kappa<\sigma_{c}
) is a subspace of
E^{{\sigma},s}\times E^{{\sigma}-1,s}
if
s<0,\,{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}
. So,
E^{{\sigma},s}\times E^{{\sigma}-1,s}
with
s<0,\,{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}
is a supercritical space.

Main results

For our purpose, we introduce the following resolution spaces (see Section

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R};B^{\sigma,s}_{p,q})}=\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}2^{\sigma jq}\|2^{s|\nabla|}\triangle_{j}u\|^{q}_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{1/q}.

1.5
Ideas in the proof of main results

Now we indicate the crucial ideas how to obtain the solutions of NLKG in
E^{\sigma,s}
.
Recall that one cannot solve nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation in supercritical Sobolev spaces
H^{\sigma}\times H^{\sigma-1}
, since the nonlinear estimate is problematic and the ill-posedness occurs in supercritical Sobolev spaces
H^{\sigma}\times H^{\sigma-1}
with
\sigma<\sigma_{c}
. However,
E^{\sigma,s}
type spaces have some good algebraic structures when the frequency is localized in the first octant, so that the nonlinear estimates become available in
E^{\sigma,s}
type spaces in the case
s<0
.

Let
\varphi\in E^{\sigma,s}
. Observing that
\lim_{b\to-\infty}\|\varphi\|_{E^{\sigma,b}}=0
, we immediately get some
s_{0}<s
such that
\|\varphi\|_{E^{\sigma,s_{0}}}\leqslant\delta\ll 1
. So, we can solve NLKG and sinh-Gordon by the small data arguments as in
[
59
]
and in Nakamura and Ozawa
[
40
]
. However,
s_{0}=s_{0}(\varphi)
will depends on both
\delta
and
\varphi
and it is unbounded for all
\varphi\in E^{\sigma,s}
(see Section
5
). In order to get
s_{0}=s_{0}(\|\varphi\|_{E^{\sigma,s}})
, we use the scaling argument and
NLKG (
1
) becomes Eq. (
1.2
) after scalings. Observing the scaling solutions
u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2/\alpha}u(\lambda x,\lambda t)
of Eq. (
1.2
) in supercritical spaces
H^{\sigma}
(say,
\sigma>1
), we have

\displaystyle\|(u_{\lambda},\partial_{t}u_{\lambda})|_{t=0}\|_{H^{\sigma}\times H^{\sigma-1}}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma-d/2+2/\alpha}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{\sigma}\times H^{\sigma-1}}\to 0,\ \ \lambda\to\infty.

It follows that the scaling NLKG can have very small initial data in supercritical Sobolev spaces. The above observation is also adapted to the supercritical space
E^{\sigma,s}\times E^{\sigma-1,s}
(
s<0
). As
\lambda\to\infty
,
(u_{\lambda},\partial_{t}u_{\lambda})|_{t=0}
will vanish in
E^{\sigma,s}\times E^{\sigma-1,s}
if
(\widehat{u}_{0},\widehat{u}_{1})
is supported in the first octant away from the origin. So, we can try to solve Eq. (
1.2
) by assuming that initial data in
E^{\sigma,s}\times E^{\sigma-1,s}
is sufficiently small.

Since Eq. (
1.2
) contains a parameter
\lambda^{2}
, we have to consider the decaying estimates for the semi-group
U_{\lambda}(t):=e^{\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-\Delta}}
by treating
\lambda
as a variable parameter. By considering its frequency-localized version, we find that the decaying estimates of
U_{\lambda}(t)
depend on
\lambda
, which become worse (better) in the lower (higher) frequency than those of
U_{1}(t)=e^{\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{I-\Delta}}
.

After obtaining the time decay of
U_{\lambda}(t)
, the Strichartz estimates of
U_{\lambda}(t)
can be obtained by
TT^{*}
arguments. Since
U_{\lambda}(t)
has different decaying estimates for the lower and higher frequency, it follows that the the Strichartz estimates of
U_{\lambda}(t)
are also different for lower and higher frequencies. On the other hand, in order to handle the supercritical data in
E^{\sigma,s}
, a new type of function spaces
B^{\sigma,s}_{p,q}
and
\widetilde{L}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R},B^{\sigma,s}_{p,q})
are involved in the Strichartz estimates, see Sections
2
and
3
.

One needs to further make the nonlinear mapping estimates in
\widetilde{L}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R},B^{\sigma,s}_{p,q})
. Since the Strichartz estimates in the lower and higher frequency are different, we need to handle the nonlinearity in lower and higher frequency spaces in a separate way.

Finally, the main results can be shown by contraction mapping arguments.

0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
\inf_{\varphi\in E^{\sigma,s}}b(\varphi,\varepsilon)=-\infty
.

However, if we use the scaling argument in the paper or assume that

{\rm supp}\,\widehat{\varphi}\,\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{I}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon_{0})
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, then we can realize that

\inf_{\varphi\in E^{\sigma,s}}b(\varphi,\varepsilon)<-\infty
. In the later case
b(\varphi,\varepsilon)=b(\|\varphi\|_{E^{\sigma,s}},\varepsilon_{0})
.

Appendix A
Higher order derivatives

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
p(r)=(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha}
, where
\mu>0
is a variable parameter. Then there exist
a_{k,j}:=a_{k,j}(\alpha,k)

(j=0,...,k)
which are independent of
\mu>0
such that

\displaystyle p^{(2k)}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum^{k}_{j=0}a_{k,j}\mu^{2j}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-k-j},

(248)

\displaystyle p^{(2k+1)}(r)

\displaystyle=2r\sum^{k}_{j=0}(\alpha-k-j)a_{k,\,j}\mu^{2j}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-k-j-1}.

(249)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle|p^{(\ell)}(r)|\leqslant C_{k}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-\ell/2},\ \ \forall\,\ell\,\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

(250)

Proof.

Differentiating (
248
), we get (
249
). So, it suffices to show (
248
) by induction. The case
k=0
is obvious. Now let us assume that (
248
) holds. We calculate
p^{(2k+2)}(r)
. Taking the derivative to (
249
),

\displaystyle p^{(2k+2)}(r)=

\displaystyle 2\sum^{k}_{j=0}(\alpha-k-j)a_{k,j}\mu^{2j}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-k-j-1}

\displaystyle+4\sum^{k}_{j=0}(\alpha-k-j)(\alpha-k-1-j)a_{k,j}\mu^{2j}r^{2}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-k-j-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum^{k}_{j=0}(\alpha-k-j)(2\alpha-2k-2j-1)a_{k,j}\mu^{2j}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-(k+1)-j}

\displaystyle-4\sum^{k+1}_{j=1}(\alpha-k-j+1)(\alpha-k-j)a_{k,j-1}\mu^{2j}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-(k+1)-j}.

(251)

Putting

\displaystyle a_{k+1,0}=

\displaystyle 2a_{k,0}(\alpha-k)(2\alpha-2k-1),\ \ a_{k+1,k+1}=-4a_{k,k}(\alpha-k)(\alpha-k+1),

\displaystyle a_{k+1,j}=

\displaystyle 2a_{k,j}(\alpha-k-j)(2\alpha-2k-2j-1)-4a_{k,j-1}(\alpha-k-j)(\alpha-k-j+1)

for
j=1,...,k
, we get (
248
) and (
249
), as desired.
By (
248
) and (
249
), we see that

\displaystyle|p^{(2k)}(r)|\leqslant C_{k}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-k},\ \ |p^{(2k+1)}(r)|\leqslant C_{k}(\mu^{2}+r^{2})^{\alpha-k-1/2},

(252)

which means that (
250
) holds.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
h(r),\,\Phi(r)
be smooth functions. Denote

\displaystyle\sigma^{(1)}(h)=(h\Phi)^{\prime},\ \ \sigma^{(k+1)}(h)=(h\sigma^{(k)}(h))^{\prime},\ \ k=1,2,...

(253)

Then we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{(\ell)}(h)=\sum_{\alpha_{1}+...+\alpha_{\ell}\leqslant\ell}C_{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{\ell}}h^{(\alpha_{1})}...h^{(\alpha_{\ell})}\Phi^{(\ell-\alpha_{1}-...-\alpha_{\ell})}.

(254)

Proof.

By Leibnitz rule, we have the result for
k=1
. The general result can be obtained by induction. Indeed, assume that (
254
) holds. Then we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{(\ell+1)}(h)=\sum_{\alpha_{1}+...+\alpha_{\ell}\leqslant\ell}C_{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{\ell}}\frac{d}{dr}(hh^{(\alpha_{1})}...h^{(\alpha_{\ell})}\Phi^{(\ell-\alpha_{1}-...-\alpha_{\ell})}).

(255)

Using the Leibnitz rule, we have the result, as desired.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\left(f^{-1}\right)^{(k)}=\frac{1}{f^{k+1}}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+...+\alpha_{k}=k}C_{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k}}f^{(\alpha_{1})}...f^{(\alpha_{k})}.

(256)

Proof.

By Leibnitz rule and induction, we have
the result, as desired.
∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
3.5

We only sketch the proofs of (
91
) and (
92
). By (
3.3
) one has that for
2^{j}\lesssim\lambda
,

\displaystyle\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f\|_{p}\lesssim\lambda^{2/\gamma(\theta,p)}2^{2j(1-\theta)\delta(p)}\int^{t}_{0}|t-\tau|^{-2/\gamma(\theta,p)}\|f(\tau)\|_{p^{\prime}}d\tau.

(257)

Using Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality,

\displaystyle\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda^{2/\gamma(\theta,p)}2^{2j(1-\theta)\delta(p)}\|f\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}}.

(258)

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\int\left(\triangle_{j}U_{\lambda}(t)u_{0},f(t)\right)dt\right|\leqslant\left\|\int\triangle_{j}U_{\lambda}(-t)f(t)dt\right\|_{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}.

(259)

By duality,

\displaystyle\left\|\int\triangle_{j}U_{\lambda}(-t)f(t)dt\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}}\left\|\int\triangle_{j}U_{\lambda}(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2/\gamma(\theta,p)}2^{2j(1-\theta)\delta(p)}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}}.

(260)

By (
259
) and (
260
),

\displaystyle\left|\int\left(\triangle_{j}U_{\lambda}(t)u_{0},f(t)\right)dt\right|\lesssim\lambda^{1/\gamma(\theta,p)}2^{j(1-\theta)\delta(p)}\|f\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}}\|u_{0}\|_{2}.

(261)

By duality, we have (
91
). Using an analogous way as in (
260
),

\displaystyle\left\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda^{1/\gamma(\theta,r)}2^{j(1-\theta)\delta(r)}\|f\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,r)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

(262)

In view of (
258
),

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1/\gamma(\theta,r)}2^{-j(1-\theta)\delta(r)}\left\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f(t)\right\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,r)}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda^{1/\gamma(\theta,r)}2^{j(1-\theta)\delta(r)}\|f\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,r)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

(263)

For any
p\in[2,r]
, there exists
\eta\in[0,1]
such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\eta}{2}+\frac{\eta}{r}.

It follows that

\delta(p)=(1-\eta)\delta(2)+\eta\delta(r),\ \ \frac{1}{\gamma(\theta,p)}=\frac{1-\eta}{\gamma(\theta,2)}+\frac{\eta}{\gamma(\theta,r)}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1/\gamma(\theta,p)}

\displaystyle 2^{-j(1-\theta)\delta(p)}\left\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f(t)\right\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\left\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\right)^{1-\eta}\left(\lambda^{-1/\gamma(\theta,r)}2^{-j(1-\theta)\delta(r)}\left\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f(t)\right\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,r)}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\right)^{\eta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1/\gamma(\theta,r)}2^{j(1-\theta)\delta(r)}\|f\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,r)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

(264)

Again, in view of the duality,

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1/\gamma(\theta,p)}2^{-j(1-\theta)\delta(p)}\left\|\triangle_{j}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f(t)\right\|_{L^{\gamma(\theta,p)}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}.

(265)

If
r\in[2,p]
, by making an interpolation between (
258
) and (
265
), we can obtain the result, as desired. This finishes the proof of (
91
) and (
92
).

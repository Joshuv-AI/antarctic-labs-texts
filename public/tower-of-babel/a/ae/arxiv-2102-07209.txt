Stability of the bulk gap for frustration-free topologically ordered quantum lattice systems
Bruno Nachtergaele, Robert Sims, Amanda Young

Abstract
. We prove that uniformly small short-range perturbations do not close the bulk gap above the ground state of frustration-free quantum spin systems that satisfy a standard local topological quantum order condition. In contrast with earlier results, we do not require a positive lower bound for finite-system spectral gaps uniform in the system size. To obtain this result, we extend the Bravyi-Hastings-Michalakis strategy so it can be applied to perturbations of the GNS Hamiltonian of the infinite-system ground state.



1.
Introduction

One of the characteristic properties of gapped topologically ordered ground state phases of quantum many-body systems
is the stability of the spectral gap above the ground state with respect to small perturbations of the Hamiltonian.
Stability results for the ground state gap have a long history. The first result that included the stability of the gap of the AKLT chain is due to Yarotsky
[
48
]
.
The approach we follow in this paper has a much broader range of applicability; it was introduced by Bravyi, Hastings, and Michalakis
[
10
]
and further developed in
[
11
,
32
,
21
,
38
]
.
Other approaches have been introduced in recent years
[
14
,
17
,
15
,
16
]
.
These new approaches can also treat some cases of models with unbounded on-site Hamiltonians, see
[
38
, Section 1]
for a more detailed
discussion. The Bravyi-Hastings-Michalakis strategy, however, is the only approach that
handles general cases with non-trivial topological order.

One obstacle to proving spectral gaps for topological insulators is the common occurrence of gapless edge states.
Spectral analysis for interacting many-body systems is usually carried out for finite systems for which edge states typically imply that there is no spectral gap uniform in the system size. Nevertheless, there may be a bulk gap,
meaning excitations away from the boundary of the system have energy bounded below uniformly in the system size. The goal of this work is to prove stability for the bulk gap in a way that does not require the assumption of a uniform positive lower bound in the spectrum of finite systems. Previously, it was shown how certain cases can be handled by considering sequences of finite systems with suitable boundary conditions. For example, such an approach may
work if the edge states are absent in the model considered with periodic boundary conditions
[
32
,
38
]
.
In general, however, there may not be a suitable boundary condition that ‘gaps out’ the boundary modes or we may
not know whether such a boundary condition exists. Systems defined on a quasicrystal structure, for example, may
be an instance where no simple way of removing gapless edge modes is available
[
30
]
. In our approach here, we only assume that the infinite system described in the GNS representation of the ground state has a gap.
Under natural assumptions consistent with the current state-of-the-art for ground state stability, we prove that sufficiently small but extensive perturbations do not close the gap.

We adapt the strategy of Bravyi, Hastings, and Michalakis
[
10
,
11
,
32
]
and use the techniques we developed in
[
37
,
38
]
to handle the infinite system setting. From a certain perspective, and apart from the technical aspects to deal with
unbounded Hamiltonians, the infinite system setting allows for a simplification in the statement of conditions and the main result. In particular, the local topological quantum
order (LTQO) condition is simpler to state directly for the infinite system. The LTQO property is well know to hold for one-dimensional systems with MPS ground
states
[
38
, Appendix B]
. It is also well-established for Kitaev’s quantum double models
[
26
,
13
]
and the Levin-Wen string-net model

[
28
,
44
]
. LTQO was recently also shown to hold for the AKLT model on a decorated hexagonal lattice
[
31
]
.

For concreteness, we work in the quantum spin system setting but, using the arguments of
[
36
]
, our approach is applicable to lattice fermion systems too.

The assumption that the bulk Hamiltonian has a gap in the spectrum above the ground state appears in several
important recent works. For example, the construction of an index for the classification of symmetry-protected topological phases in the works of Ogata and co-authors makes use of this assumption
[
33
,
40
,
39
,
41
]
. Other examples are in the recent work on adiabatic theorems for infinite many-body systems
[
2
,
3
,
23
]
. All of these works use the same general setting as described here in Section
2
. In addition to the main stability result, we also prove Theorem
3.6
, which shows that a differentiability assumption introduced in
[
33
]
and also used in later work
[
43
,
42
,
39
,
41
]
is always satisfied.

2.
Setup and statement of the main results

2.1.
Setup and notation

The models considered in this work are defined on a
\nu
-regular discrete metric space
(\Gamma,d)
, for some
\nu>0
. This means that there exists
\kappa>0
so that for all
x\in\Gamma,n\geq 1
,
|b_{x}(n)|\leq\kappa n^{\nu}
, where
b_{x}(n)=\{y\in\Gamma\mid d(x,y)\leq n\}
. For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)
, the finite subsets of
\Gamma
, and

n\geq 0
, we also define the sets
\Lambda(n)
by

(2.1)

\Lambda(n)=\bigcup_{x\in\Lambda}b_{x}(n).

The algebra of local observables of the system is the usual

{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}=\bigcup_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}
. Here,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}
is the matrix algebra
\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}M_{d_{x}}

with
d_{x}
the dimension of the spin at
x
. The
C^{*}
-algebra of quasi-local observables
{\mathcal{A}}
is the completion of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
with respect to the operator norm. For
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
, the
support
of
A
,
denoted by
\operatorname{supp}A
, is the smallest
X\subset\Gamma
such that
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
.
For any
X\subset\Gamma
,
\Pi_{X}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
is the conditional expectation with respect to the tracial state
\rho
on
{\mathcal{A}}
:

(2.2)

\Pi_{X}=\rho\restriction_{\Gamma\setminus X}\otimes{\rm id}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{X}}.

In particular, for local
A
,
\Pi_{X}(A)
is a normalized partial trace.

We are specifically interested in systems defined on
infinite

\Gamma
and often want to consider
approximations
A_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda_{n}}
of
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
, where
\Lambda_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)

is an increasing sequence of finite volumes such that
\bigcup_{n}\Lambda_{n}=\Gamma
. We call such
a sequence
(\Lambda_{n})
an
increasing and absorbing sequence
(IAS).
It will often be important to have an estimate for the speed of convergence of
A_{n}\to A
,
in terms of a non-increasing function
g:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
that vanishes at infinity, which we call a
decay function
.
In this paper we will only use decay functions that satisfy a moment condition of the form

(2.3)

\sum_{n\geq 0}(n+1)^{\mu}g(n)<\infty,\ \mbox{ for some }\ \mu\geq\nu.

In particular, we will often work with decay functions of the form

(2.4)

g(r)=\frac{1}{(1+r)^{\xi}}e^{-ar^{\theta}},\quad\xi\geq 0,a\geq 0,\theta\in[0,1],

which share the property

(2.5)

g(n)g(m)\leq Cg(n+m),\for{forall}n,m\geq 0,

for some constant
C
.

Consider an IAS
(\Lambda_{n})
in
(\Gamma,d)
, and a decay function
g
.
Define a norm
\|\cdot\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}
on
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
and a Banach space
{\mathcal{A}}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}
by

(2.6)

\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}=\|A\|+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{\|A-\Pi_{\Lambda_{n}}(A)\|}{g(n)}\right),\quad{\mathcal{A}}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}=\overline{{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}}^{\|\cdot\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}}.

For a proof that
{\mathcal{A}}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}
is the Banach space of all
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
for which
\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}<\infty
, see
[
33
]
. In fact,
{\mathcal{A}}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}
is a Banach
*
-algebra.

For each
x\in\Gamma
,
\Lambda_{n}:=b_{x}(n)
defines a IAS. In this case we set
\|\cdot\|_{(b_{x}(n)),g}=\|\cdot\|_{x,g}
. Define the set

(2.7)

{\mathcal{A}}^{g}:=\bigcup_{x\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{A}}^{(b_{x}(n)),g}.

For any decay function
g
satisfying (
2.5
),
any two norms from
\{\|\cdot\|_{x,g}\mid x\in\Gamma\}
are equivalent. Hence, for all
x\in\Gamma

(2.8)

{\mathcal{A}}^{(b_{x}(n)),g}={\mathcal{A}}^{g}.

In this case,
{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
is a Banach
*
-algebra. Elements
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
are called
g
-local
.

We will often also assume that a decay function
g
is uniformly summable over
\Gamma
, i.e.,

(2.9)

\|g\|_{1}:=\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{y\in\Gamma}g(d(x,y))<\infty\,,

and additionally, that there is a constant
C>0
such that

(2.10)

\sum_{z\in\Gamma}g(d(x,z))g(d(z,y))\leq Cg(d(x,y)),\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\Gamma.

Any decay function
g
satisfying (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) will be called an
F
-function. For any
\nu
-regular
\Gamma
,
the following are
F
-functions appearing in this work:

(2.11)

F(r)=\frac{1}{(1+r)^{\xi}}e^{-ar^{\theta}},\quad\xi>\nu+1,a\geq 0,\theta\in(0,1].

In the case
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
, which is
\nu
-regular, (
2.11
) defines an
F
-function for all
\xi>\nu
.
For a discussion of these examples and some basic inequalities, see
[
37
, Appendix]
.

Assumption 2.1

(Initial Interaction)
.

We assume the initial model is defined by a finite-range, uniformly bounded, frustration-free interaction
h
given in terms of a family
h=\{h_{x}\}_{x\in\Gamma}

which satisfies:

i.

There is a number
R\geq 0
, called the interaction radius, for which

h_{x}^{*}=h_{x}\in\mathcal{A}_{b_{x}(R)}
for all
x\in\Gamma
.

ii.

These terms are uniformly bounded in the sense that

(2.12)

\|h\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\|h_{x}\|<\infty\,.

iii.

The interaction is
frustration-free
, meaning that
h_{x}\geq 0
for all
x\in\Gamma
and for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
,

(2.13)

{\rm min\,spec}(H_{\Lambda})=0\quad\mbox{where}\quad H_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\in\Lambda:}}{{{\rm supp}(h_{x})\subset\Lambda}}}h_{x}\,.

The frustration-free condition implies that the ground state space is
{\rm ker}(H_{\Lambda})

for any finite volume
\Lambda
. Moreover,
\psi\in{\rm ker}(H_{\Lambda})
if and
only if
\psi\in{\rm ker}(h_{x})
for each
x\in\Gamma
with
{\rm supp}(h_{x})\subset\Lambda
.
Thus, denoting by
P_{\Lambda}
the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm ker}(H_{\Lambda})
, for any
\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda
, one has

(2.14)

P_{\Lambda}P_{\Lambda_{0}}=P_{\Lambda_{0}}P_{\Lambda}=P_{\Lambda}\,.

For such a model, the derivation
\delta_{0}
determining the infinite system dynamics is given by

(2.15)

\delta_{0}(A)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda(R)}[h_{x},A]\quad\mbox{for any }A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}\mbox{ and }\Lambda\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma).

It is a standard result that there is a closed derivation extending
\delta_{0}
, which we also denote by
\delta_{0}
,
with domain
\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta_{0})
for which
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
is a core
[
9
, Theorem 6.2.4]
(note that the factor
i
is absorbed in the definition
of the derivation in this reference). The system dynamics
is then the strongly continuous one-parameter group of
C^{*}
-automorphisms
\{\tau^{(0)}_{t}\mid t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
satisfying

(2.16)

\frac{d}{dt}\tau_{t}^{(0)}(A)=i\tau_{t}^{(0)}(\delta_{0}(A))\quad\mbox{ for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}.

In fact, this differential equation holds for all
A\in\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta_{0})
. Two other general properties are:

i.

\tau_{t}^{(0)}(\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta_{0}))\subset\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta_{0})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

ii.

\tau_{t}^{(0)}(\delta_{0}(A))=\delta_{0}(\tau_{t}^{(0)}(A))
for all
A\in\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta_{0})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

More generally, quantum spin models can be defined by an interaction on
\Gamma
which, by definition, is a map

\Phi:{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
, with the property that

\Phi(X)^{*}=\Phi(X)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
for all
X\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)
.
For any decay function
g
, an interaction norm is defined by

(2.17)

\|\Phi\|_{g}=\sup_{x,y\in\Gamma}g(d(x,y))^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma):\\
x,y\in X\end{subarray}}\|\Phi(X)\|\,.

When the above quantity is finite for some interaction
\Phi
, the function
g
is said to measure the decay of
\Phi
.
If
g
is an
F
-function, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{g}
is called an
F-norm
.
If
g
is summable, in the sense of (
2.9
), and
\|\Phi\|_{g}<\infty
,
then a closable derivation on
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
can be defined by setting

(2.18)

\delta(A)=\sum_{Y,Y\cap X\neq\emptyset}[\Phi(Y),A]\quad\mbox{for }A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}\mbox{ with }{\rm supp}(A)\subset X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)\,.

One can prove conditions that guarantee that the derivation
\delta
defined on
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
is a generator of a strongly continuous dynamics
given by automorphisms of
{\mathcal{A}}

[
8
,
9
]
. In practice, however, one usually directly proves
the existence of the thermodynamic limit of the Heisenberg dynamics
\{\tau_{t}\mid t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
.
Standard results along these lines
prove the existence of the dynamics for
\Phi
in a suitable Banach space of
interactions
[
46
,
9
,
47
]
starting from a convergent series for small
|t|
.
An alternative approach, based on Lieb-Robinson bounds
[
29
]
, was introduced by Robinson
[
45
]
.
Lieb-Robinson bounds can be derived for any interaction
\Phi
with a finite
F
-norm

[
34
]
, and this allows one to extend the results for existence of the dynamics beyond
the Banach spaces of interactions
{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}
introduced by Ruelle
[
46
]
. These ideas
are important for the construction of the spectral flow automorphisms
[
5
]
.
This and some other generalizations relevant for the present work are discussed in detail in
[
37
]
.

Recall that infinite-volume ground states associated to
\delta
are those states
\omega
on
{\mathcal{A}}
that satisfy

(2.19)

\omega(A^{*}\delta(A))\geq 0\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}.

In the case of a frustration-free model as in (
2.13
), a state
\omega
is called a zero-energy ground state, or a frustration free ground state,
if
\omega(h_{x})=0
for all
x\in\Gamma
. It is easy to see that a zero-energy ground state satisfies (
2.19
).

Let
({\mathcal{H}},\pi,\Omega)
be the GNS triple of
\omega
.
For the GNS representation of a ground state, as in (
2.19
),
there exists a unique, non-negative self-adjoint operator

H
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, with dense domain
\mathop{\rm dom}H
, satisfying
H\Omega=0
and

(2.20)

\pi(\tau_{t}(A))=e^{itH}\pi(A)e^{-itH}\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}\mbox{ and }t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The full domain of
H
is seldom described explicitly. However, for all systems we consider in this paper,

\pi({\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc})\Omega
is a core for
H
.

The
(GNS) gap
of the model in the state
\omega

is defined as

(2.21)

{\rm gap}(H)=\sup\{\gamma>0\mid(0,\gamma)\cap\mathop{\rm spec}(H)=\emptyset\}.

If the set on the RHS is empty, one defines
{\rm gap}(H)=0
. We say that a ground state
\omega
is gapped if
{\rm gap}(H)>0
.

The equivalence of the following two conditions is easy to verify:

i.

For some
\gamma>0
,
\omega
satisfies

(2.22)

\omega(A^{*}\delta(A))\geq\gamma\omega(A^{*}A)\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}\mbox{ with }\omega(A)=0;

ii.

The ground state of the GNS Hamiltonian
H
is unique and
{\rm gap}(H)\geq\gamma
.

A case of special interest is when
\Gamma
is infinite and describes the bulk of a physical model while the same system on a subset of
\Gamma
with a boundary would describe an edge. In the first situation we will refer to the GNS gap as the
bulk gap
of the system. A model with the same interaction restricted to a subspace of
\Gamma
describing an edge, may have a vanishing gap while the bulk gap is positive. This is precisely the situation of interest here.

We will use that the GNS representation
\pi
is an isometry. This follows from the fact that
{\mathcal{A}}
is simple
[
18
, Theorem 5.1]
, which implies that
\ker\pi=\{0\}
.

2.2.
Main results

We now state the assumptions for the main results.

Assumption 2.2

(Bulk gap)
.

We assume
\gamma_{0}:={\rm gap}(H_{0})>0
, where
H_{0}
is the GNS Hamiltonian of an infinite-volume, zero-energy ground state
\omega_{0}
of a finite-range,
uniformly bounded, frustration free interaction
\{h_{x}\}
as in Assumption
2.1
.

We also need to impose a condition that the local gaps do not close too fast. There generally is some
freedom in choosing the family of finite volumes on which to impose this condition. We will assume that there is
a family
{\mathcal{S}}=\{\Lambda(x,n)\mid n\geq 0,x\in\Gamma\}\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)
, with
b_{x}(n)\subset\Lambda(x,n)
for all
x
and
n
,
and an associated family of partitions of
\Gamma
which
separates
{\mathcal{S}}
and has at most polynomial growth
. Concretely, this means
there is a family of sets

{\mathcal{T}}=\{{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\mid n\geq 0\}
and positive numbers
c
and
\zeta
, such that for each

n\geq 0
,
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}=\{{\mathcal{T}}_{n}^{i}:i\in{\mathcal{I}}_{n}\}
is a partition of
\Gamma
satisfying
|{\mathcal{I}}_{n}|\leq cn^{\zeta}
and

(2.23)

\Lambda(x,n)\cap\Lambda(y,n)=\emptyset\;\text{ for all }\;x,y\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n}^{i}\;\text{with}\;x\neq y.

In such cases, we say that
{\mathcal{T}}
is of
(c,\zeta)
-polynomial growth.

As an example, in the case of
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
, we may take for
\Lambda(x,n)
the
\ell^{\infty}
-ball of radius
n
centered at
x
,
define
{\mathcal{I}}_{n}=\Lambda(0,n)
and, for each
i\in{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
, set

(2.24)

{\mathcal{T}}^{i}_{n}=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}|x_{j}=i_{j}\mod 2n+1,i=1,\ldots,\nu\}.

Assumption 2.3

(Local gaps)
.

For an interaction
\{h_{x}\}
of range
R
, we assume there exist families
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
, such that
{\mathcal{T}}

separates
{\mathcal{S}}
and is of
\zeta
-polynomial growth, and an exponent
\alpha\geq 0
and constant
\gamma_{1}>0
, and
such that the finite-volume Hamiltonians satisfy:

(2.25)

\mathop{\rm spec}(H_{\Lambda(x,n)})\subset\{0\}\cup[n^{-\alpha}\gamma_{1},\infty)\quad\mbox{for all }x\in\Gamma\mbox{ and }n\geq R.

It is important here that the local gaps are allowed to vanish in the limit of infinite system size. For example, certain types of topologically
ordered two-dimensional systems are expected to have chiral edge modes with an energy of order
L^{-1}
on a finite volume of diameter
L
.
Whether or not such edge modes occur in frustration-free systems, however, is not clear. For the class of systems studied in
[
27
]
,
the authors find that finite-volume gaps of a system with gapless edge modes in the thermodynamic limit would have to decay at least as fast
as
L^{-3/2}
. Other results of this type are in
[
19
,
24
,
1
]
.
This is consistent with the gapless boundary modes found in a class of toy models called Product Vacua with Boundary States which are of order
L^{-2}

[
4
,
6
]
.
In any case, regardless of the possible values of the exponent
\alpha
, we will prove stability of the
bulk
gap.

The next assumption was introduced in the form we use here in
[
32
]

where it is called
Local Topological Quantum Order
(LTQO).

Assumption 2.4

(LTQO)
.

There is a decay function
G_{0}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, with

(2.26)

\sum_{n\geq 0}n^{q}G_{0}(n)<\infty\mbox{ for some }q>2(\nu+\zeta+\alpha),

and such that for all
m\geq k\geq 0,x\in\Gamma
, and
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(k)}
, the ground state projections satisfy

(2.27)

\|P_{b_{x}(m)}AP_{b_{x}(m)}-\omega_{0}(A)P_{b_{x}(m)}\|\leq\|A\|(1+k)^{\nu}G_{0}(m-k).

As explained in detail in
[
38
, Section 8]
, if both the initial Hamiltonian and the
perturbation (see below) have a local gauge symmetry, only observables
A
that commute with this symmetry
need to satisfy (
2.27
). Other discrete symmetries can be treated similarly (see

[
38
, Section 8]
). Therefore, the stability results proved here (Theorems
2.8

and
2.9
) will also hold for symmetry-protected topological phases.

It is an interesting observation that the GNS Hamiltonians associated to frustration free models which satisfy Assumption
2.4
automatically
have a unique ground state. This is the content of the following propostion.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\omega_{0}
be an infinite-volume, zero-energy ground state of a frustration-free model satisfying Assumption
2.4
.
The kernel of the GNS Hamiltonian
H_{0}
is one-dimensional.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, let us assume there is a unit vector
\psi\in{\rm ker}(H_{0})
with

\langle\psi,\Omega\rangle=0
. To simplify notation let us denote by

\tilde{A}:=\pi_{0}(A)
the representative of
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
in the GNS space.
The LTQO condition (
2.27
) can be restated in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
as follows: for all
m\geq k\geq 0
,
x\in\Gamma
, and
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(k)}
,

(2.28)

\|\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\tilde{A}\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}-\langle\Omega,\tilde{A}\Omega\rangle\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\|\leq\|A\|(1+k)^{\nu}G_{0}(m-k)\,.

First, since
\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc})\Omega
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
, there is
x\in\Gamma
and operators

A_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(n)}
for which
\psi_{n}=\tilde{A}_{n}\Omega\to\psi
. In this case,

(2.29)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Omega,\tilde{A}_{n}\Omega\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Omega,\psi_{n}\rangle=\langle\Omega,\psi\rangle=0.

Thus, there is
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
|\langle\Omega,\tilde{A}_{n}\Omega\rangle|\leq 1/3
for all
n\geq n_{1}
.

Next, since
{\rm ker}(H_{0})\subset{\rm ker}(\tilde{H}_{b_{x}(m)})
for all
m\geq 0
and
x\in\Gamma
, we have

(2.30)

\langle\psi,\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\tilde{A}_{n}\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\Omega\rangle=\langle\psi,\tilde{A}_{n}\Omega\rangle=\langle\psi,\psi_{n}\rangle\,

for all
m\geq 0
. Since
1=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\psi,\psi_{n}\rangle
, there is some
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
for which

|\langle\psi,\psi_{n}\rangle|\geq 2/3
for all
n\geq n_{2}
. Combining this with (
2.30
), we find that
for any
m\geq n\geq n_{2}
,

(2.31)

2/3\leq\|\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\tilde{A}_{n}\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\|.

It follows that for
n\geq\max\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
,

(2.32)

1/3\leq\|\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\tilde{A}_{n}\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}-\langle\Omega,\tilde{A}_{n}\Omega\rangle\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(m)}\|,

a contradiction to (
2.28
), as the decay function
G_{0}
satisfies
G_{0}(m)\to 0
as

.
∎

Next, we turn to the perturbations of the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
. We consider

\Phi(x,n)^{*}=\Phi(x,n)\in\mathcal{A}_{b_{x}(n)}
for all

.

.
By regrouping, we need only consider those terms with

.

Assumption 2.6

(Short-range perturbation)
.

There is a constant
\|\Phi\|\geq 0
,

, and

\|\Phi(x,n)\|\leq\|\Phi\|e^{-an^{\theta}}\quad\mbox{ for all }n\geq R.

Remark 2.7
.

Assume
\Phi
satisfies Assumption
2.6
.
As indicated in (

), for any

and
\xi>\nu
, the function

given by

F(r)=\frac{e^{-a^{\prime}r^{\theta}}}{(1+r)^{\xi}}\quad\mbox{for all }r\geq 0,

is an
F
-function on
\Gamma
. Let

and note that for any

(2.35)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\Gamma}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\geq R:}}{{x,y\in b_{z}(n)}}}\|\Phi(z,n)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq d(x,y)}\sum_{z\in b_{y}(n)\cap b_{x}(n)}\|\Phi(z,n)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\|\Phi\|\sum_{n\geq d(x,y)}n^{\nu}e^{-an^{\theta}}\leq CF(d(x,y))

with

, with

as in (

).

The focus of this work is to analyze the stability of the bulk gap under the presence of perturbations given by an anchored interaction
\Phi
satisfying Assumption

. We consider perturbed Hamiltonians of the form

(2.36)

Clearly,

is bounded and self-adjoint, and so

defines for all

In the next several sections we will prove the following theorem, which establishes that the spectral gap of

remains open for small

Theorem 2.8

(Stability of the gap uniformly in the perturbation region)
.

Suppose that

–
2.4
, and

, there exists

, such that for all real

,
|s|<s_{0}(\gamma)
, and

\mathop{\rm spec}H(\Lambda,s)\subset\{E(\Lambda,s)\}\cup[E(\Lambda,s)+\gamma,\infty)

with

as in (

) and

(2.39)

We remark that the quantity

only depends on the values of
\kappa
and
\nu

of the lattice,
\|h\|
, the gap
\gamma_{0}
, the parameters in Assumption
2.3
,

\Phi
.
From the arguments in this paper, one can derive an explicit lower bound for

.

We also investigate the situation where the perturbation region
\Lambda
tends to all of
\Gamma

Consider any IAS
(\Lambda_{n})
. We will denote by

As discussed in
[

(respectively, the

Our second result is then concerned with the ground state and its gap for a family of extensive perturbations.
In particular, the uniformity of the stability result in Theorem

Theorem 2.9

(Stability of the bulk gap)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

, let

and

In particular, the GNS Hamiltonian

3.
Quasi-locality, Domains and Local Decompositions

The strategy used here for proving spectral gap stability of infinite systems relies in an essential way
on quasi-locality properties of the observables, the dynamics, and several transformations defined in
terms of the dynamics. Quasi-locality of observables is the topic of Section
3.1
. In Section
3.1.1
, we recall general methods for making strictly local approximations of both quasi-local observables and maps. The specific quasi-local maps and estimates used in the stability proof are discussed in Sections
3.1.2
-
3.1.4
. Their corresponding counterparts in the GNS representation are treated in Section
3.2
.
In Section
3.3
, we prove how the action of certain unbounded operators on a dense domain can expressed as limits of sequences of bounded operators with finite support.
In Section
3.4
we prove a local bound on the generator of the quasi-adiabatic evolution which, while not used in this work, is important for applications.

3.1.
Quasi-Locality

We first recall some general features of quasi-locality estimates and then turn to some important examples relevant for this work.

3.1.1.
Quasi-locality estimates.

If for
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, and
\epsilon>0

(3.1)

\|[A,B]\|\leq\epsilon\|B\|\,,\quad\mbox{whenever }B\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma\setminus X}^{\rm loc}\,,

the following estimate was shown in
[
12
,
35
]
:

(3.2)

\|A-\Pi_{X}(A)\|\leq\epsilon\,.

A linear map
{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is said to be
quasi-local
with constant
C\geq 0
, power
p\geq 0
, and decay
function
G
if

(3.3)

\|[{\mathcal{K}}(A),B]\|\leq C|X|^{p}\|A\|\|B\|G(d(X,Y))\mbox{ for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{X}\mbox{ and }B\in{\mathcal{A}}_{Y}\,.

Using (
3.2
), for such a map
{\mathcal{K}}
and
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(k)}
, we have

(3.4)

\|{\mathcal{K}}(A)-\Pi_{b_{x}(k+n)}({\mathcal{K}}(A))\|\leq C|b_{x}(k)|^{p}\|A\|G(n).

When the corresponding decay function
G
is summable, this estimate guarantees the absolute convergence of telescopic sums, i.e. for any
n_{0}\geq 0
,

(3.5)

{\mathcal{K}}(A)=\Pi_{b_{x}(k+n_{0})}({\mathcal{K}}(A))+\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\left(\Pi_{b_{x}(k+n)}-\Pi_{b_{x}(k+n-1)}\right)({\mathcal{K}}(A))

since the terms satisfy

(3.6)

\|\left(\Pi_{b_{x}(k+n)}-\Pi_{b_{x}(k+n-1)}\right)({\mathcal{K}}(A))\|\leq 2C|b_{x}(k)|^{p}\|A\|G(n-1)\quad\mbox{for }n\geq 1.

A common choice is
n_{0}=0
and we adopt the notation

(3.7)

{\mathcal{K}}(A)=\sum_{n\geq k}\Delta_{b_{x}(k)}^{n}({\mathcal{K}}(A))\quad\text{where}\quad\Delta_{b_{x}(k)}^{n}=\begin{cases}\Pi_{b_{x}(k)},&n=k\\
\Pi_{{b_{x}(n)}}-\Pi_{{b_{x}(n-1)}},&n\geq k+1.\end{cases}

We now review four quasi-local maps as well as a few of their important properties that are used in the stability argument. For more details of these maps see
[
37
]
. Throughout Sections
3.1.2
–
3.2
we work under the assumptions of Theorem
2.8
.

3.1.2.
Dynamics

It is well-known that the unperturbed dynamics
\tau_{t}^{(0)}
defined as in (
2.16
)
satisfies an exponential Lieb-Robinson bound
[
29
]
. Namely, for every
\mu>0
there exists
C_{\mu}>0
and
v_{\mu}>0
such that the bound

(3.8)

\|[\tau_{t}^{(0)}(A),B]\|\leq C_{\mu}\min(|X|,|Y|)\|A\|\|B\|e^{-\mu(d(X,Y)-v_{\mu}|t|)}

holds for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
, and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

It is easy to check that the perturbed interaction
h+s\Phi{\restriction_{\Lambda}}

has a finite
F
-norm for the same
F
as
\Phi
, and that this
F
-norm is uniformly bounded in
|s|\leq 1
and
\Lambda
.
As a consequence, there are
C_{F}>0
and
v_{F}>0
, independent of
s
and
\Lambda
,
such that for any choice of
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
,

(3.9)

\|[\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A),B]\|\leq C_{F}\|A\|\|B\|e^{v_{F}|t|}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Y}F(d(x,y))

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
, and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Since each
sV_{\Lambda}
is bounded and self-adjoint,
[
9
, Proposition 5.4.1]
implies that

(3.10)

\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A)=(K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)})^{*}\tau_{t}^{(0)}(A)K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}\mbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}

where
\{K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}\,|\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a one-parameter family of unitaries on
{\mathcal{A}}
which are uniquely defined as the

{\mathcal{A}}
-valued solution of

(3.11)

\frac{d}{dt}K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}=-i\tau_{t}^{(0)}(sV_{\Lambda})K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}\quad\mbox{with}\quad K_{0}^{(\Lambda,s)}={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\,.

These unitaries are quasi-local as, for any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
and
t>0
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|[K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)},A]\|=\|(K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)})^{*}AK_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}-A\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{du}\left((K_{u}^{(\Lambda,s)})^{*}AK_{u}^{(\Lambda,s)}\right)\,du\right\|\,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|[\tau_{u}^{(0)}(sV_{\Lambda}),A]\|\,du\,.

An application of (
3.8
) then shows that for any
\mu>0
and
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
with
X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma\setminus\Lambda)
,

(3.13)

\|[K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)},A]\|\leq C_{\mu}|s||\Lambda|\|V_{\Lambda}\|\|A\||t|e^{\mu v_{\mu}|t|}e^{-\mu d(\Lambda,X)}

for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
for any exponential
g
, by (
3.2
).

3.1.3.
Weighted Integral Operators

Fix
\gamma>0
. For each
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define two
weighted integral operators
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}^{\Lambda}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
by

(3.14)

{\mathcal{F}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A)w_{\gamma}(t)\,dt\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A)W_{\gamma}(t)\,dt\,,

where the real-valued functions
w_{\gamma},W_{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, are defined in
[
37
, Section VI.B]
.
In particular, they decay faster than any stretched exponential.
Both of these maps depend on the choice of
\gamma
through their weight functions,
w_{\gamma}
and
W_{\gamma}
respectively, but
we suppress this in the notation. Arguing as in

[
37
, Section VI.E.1]
, see also
[
38
, Section 4.3.2]
, we find that for all
A\in{\mathcal{A}}

(3.15)

\|{\mathcal{F}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)\|\leq\|A\|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|{\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)\|\leq\|W_{\gamma}\|_{1}\|A\|\,,

i.e. these maps are bounded uniformly with respect to
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
.
Moreover, they are uniformly quasi-local in the sense that for each
{\mathcal{K}}\in\{{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\}
there is a
decay function
G_{{\mathcal{K}}}
such that: for any choice of
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, we have

(3.16)

\sup_{s\in[-1,1]}\|[{\mathcal{K}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A),B]\|\leq 2\|A\|\|B\||X|G_{{\mathcal{K}}}(d(X,Y))

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
. As shown in
[
37
, Lemma 6.10–6.11]
, the decay functions

G_{{\mathcal{K}}}
can be made explicit. For our purposes here, we need only stress that they can be taken independent of

\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
and
s\in[-1,1]
, and with decay faster than any power. Thus,
for any
\mu\geq 0
,

(3.17)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{\mu}G_{{\mathcal{K}}}(n)<\infty\,.

3.1.4.
The Spectral Flow

Fix
\gamma>0
. For each
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, denote by

(3.18)

D(\Lambda,s)={\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tau^{(\Lambda,s)}_{t}(V_{\Lambda})W_{\gamma}(t)dt

with
{\mathcal{G}}^{\Lambda}_{s}
as defined in (
3.14
).
Clearly,
D(\Lambda,s)
is self-adjoint and
s\mapsto D(\Lambda,s)
is uniformly bounded by (
3.15
).

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
fixed, the
strong derivative of
s\mapsto\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}
is given by the Duhamel formula
[
37
, Proposition 2.7]
:

(3.19)

\frac{d}{ds}\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A)=i\int_{0}^{t}\tau_{r}^{(\Lambda,s)}([V_{\Lambda},\tau_{t-r}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A)])\,dr\,.

Using (
3.19
), one obtains the norm continuity of
s\mapsto D(\Lambda,s)
from the following estimate:

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|D(\Lambda,s)-D(\Lambda,s_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|\tau^{(\Lambda,s)}_{t}(V_{\Lambda})-\tau^{(\Lambda,s_{0})}_{t}(V_{\Lambda})\||W_{\gamma}(t)|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|V_{\Lambda}\|^{2}|s-s_{0}|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|t||W_{\gamma}(t)|\,dt.

Given these properties of
D(\Lambda,s)
, there is a unique solution of

(3.21)

\frac{d}{ds}U(\Lambda,s)=-iD(\Lambda,s)U(\Lambda,s)\quad\mbox{with}\quad U(\Lambda,0)={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\,,

which is given by unitaries in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Using similar arguments as in (
3.13
) with (
3.16
) and (
3.18
), one can show that for
s>0

(3.22)

\|[U(\Lambda,s),A]\|\leq\int_{0}^{s}\|[{\mathcal{G}}_{r}^{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}),A]\|\,dr\leq 2s\|A\||\Lambda|\|V_{\Lambda}\|G_{{\mathcal{G}}}(d(X,\Lambda))

for any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
with
X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma\setminus\Lambda)
.
Thus,
U(\Lambda,s)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
for some
g
with finite moments of all orders by (
3.17
).

The
spectral flow
is then the family of inner automorphisms on
{\mathcal{A}}
induced by
U(\Lambda,s)
:

(3.23)

\alpha^{\Lambda}_{s}(A)=U(\Lambda,s)^{*}AU(\Lambda,s)\quad\mbox{ for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Quasi-locality of this map is then a consequnece of a Lieb-Robinson bound. To this end, first rewrite the generator as

(3.24)

D(\Lambda,s)={\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda})=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\geq R:}}{{b_{x}(n)\subset\Lambda}}}{\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(\Phi(x,n))\,

using (
2.37
). Applying the conditional expectations and telescopic sum from (
3.7
), we further write

(3.25)

D(\Lambda,s)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{m\geq R}\Psi_{\Lambda}(x,m,s)\quad\mbox{where}\quad\Psi_{\Lambda}(x,m,s)=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R\leq n\leq m:}}{{b_{x}(n)\subset\Lambda}}}\Delta_{b_{x}(n)}^{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(\Phi(x,n)))\,.

Arguing as in
[
38
, Appendix A]
, there is a decay function
G_{\Psi}
and a positive number
\|\Psi\|_{G_{\Psi}}
such that
for all
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
,
s\in[-1,1]
,
x\in\Lambda
and
k\geq R
,

(3.26)

\sum_{m\geq k}\|\Psi_{\Lambda}(x,m,s)\|\leq\|\Psi\|_{G_{\Psi}}G_{\Psi}(k)\,.

One can be explicit about estimates for
G_{\Psi}
, see
[
38
, Corollary A.3]
, but for our purposes,
we only need that is has finite moments of all orders.
Given (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), well-known Lieb-Robinson
bounds imply the existence of a decay function
G_{\alpha}
so that
for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
,

(3.27)

\|[\alpha^{\Lambda}_{s}(A),B]\|\leq s\|A\|\|B\||X|G_{\alpha}(d(X,Y))

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

G_{\alpha}
is independent of
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
and has finite moments of all orders.

3.2.
In the GNS space

The spectral perturbation arguments are carried out in the GNS representation of the reference state
\omega_{0}
.
The quasi-local maps discussed in the previous subsection
can be lifted to the GNS representation and we now present the necessary properties we will need in this setting.

3.2.1.
Dynamics

As discussed in Section
2.1
, the unperturbed dynamics
\tau_{t}^{(0)}
is implemented in the GNS representation of
\omega_{0}

by the GNS Hamiltonian
H_{0}
, as in (
2.20
). We further show that the perturbed dynamics
\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}

is implemented in the GNS representation of
\omega_{0}
by the Hamiltonian

(3.28)

H(\Lambda,s)=H_{0}+\pi_{0}(sV_{\Lambda}),s\in{\mathbb{R}},

from (
2.36
). Specifically,

(3.29)

\pi_{0}(\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A))=e^{itH(\Lambda,s)}\pi_{0}(A)e^{-itH(\Lambda,s)}\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}\mbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Applying the GNS representation to the interaction picture representation (
3.10
) gives

(3.30)

\pi_{0}(\tau_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}(A))=(\pi_{0}(K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}))^{*}e^{itH_{0}}\pi_{0}(A)e^{-itH_{0}}\pi_{0}(K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)})\,.

Then, (
3.29
) follows by observing that

(3.31)

e^{-itH(\Lambda,s)}=e^{-itH_{0}}\pi_{0}(K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)})\,,

as by (
3.11
)
\tilde{K}_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}:=\pi_{0}(K_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)})
is the unique, unitary solution of

(3.32)

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{K}_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}=-ie^{itH_{0}}\pi_{0}(sV_{\Lambda})e^{-itH_{0}}\tilde{K}_{t}^{(\Lambda,s)}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\tilde{K}_{0}^{(\Lambda,s)}={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}.

3.2.2.
Weighted Integral Operators

For any
\gamma>0
,
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}

we map the weighted integral operators of (
3.14
) to the GNS space by defining
\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{s}^{\Lambda}
and

\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{s}^{\Lambda}
by

(3.33)

\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{itH(\Lambda,s)}Ae^{-itH(\Lambda,s)}w_{\gamma}(t)\,dt\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{itH(\Lambda,s)}Ae^{-itH(\Lambda,s)}W_{\gamma}(t)\,dt

for all
A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Using (
3.29
), it is clear that

(3.34)

\pi_{0}({\mathcal{F}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A))=\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(\pi_{0}(A))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi_{0}({\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A))=\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{s}^{\Lambda}(\pi_{0}(A))\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}\,.

3.2.3.
The Spectral Flow

For fixed
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)
, following
[
37
, Section VI.A]
we define a norm-continuous family of unitaries
\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})

as the unique solution of

(3.35)

\frac{d}{ds}\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)=-i\tilde{D}(\Lambda,s)\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)\quad\mbox{with}\quad\tilde{U}(\Lambda,0)={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\,,

where

(3.36)

\tilde{D}(\Lambda,s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{itH(\Lambda,s)}\pi_{0}(V_{\Lambda})e^{-itH(\Lambda,s)}W_{\gamma}(t)\,dt\,.

The
spectral flow
associated with
H(\Lambda,s)
is the family of automorphisms of
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
defined by

(3.37)

\tilde{\alpha}_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)^{*}A\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s).

This is Hastings’ quasi-adiabatic evolution
[
20
,
22
]
.

By (
3.34
) it is clear that
\tilde{D}(\Lambda,s)=\pi_{0}(D(\Lambda,s))
with
D(\Lambda,s)
as in (
3.18
) and, hence, by the uniqueness of the unitary solution
of (
3.35
),
\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)=\pi_{0}(U(\Lambda,s))
, where
U(\Lambda,s)
is as in (
3.21
). Therefore,
\pi_{0}
lifts the spectral flow in
{\mathcal{A}}
to the GNS space:

(3.38)

\pi_{0}(\alpha_{s}^{\Lambda}(A))=\tilde{\alpha}_{s}^{\Lambda}(\pi_{0}(A))\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}\,.

Recall that
E(\Lambda,s)
denotes the ground state energy of
H(\Lambda,s)
.
Under our assumptions the ground state space of
H(\Lambda,0)=H_{0}
is one-dimensional by
Proposition
2.5
, and
\gamma_{0}:=\mathrm{gap}(H_{0})
is strictly positive.
By standard results, see e.g
[
25
]
, for
|s|
sufficiently small the kernel of
H(\Lambda,s)-E(\Lambda,s){\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}

is one-dimensional and the ground state gap does not immediately close. More precisely, for any
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0})
,
there is
s_{0}^{\Lambda}(\gamma)>0
so that

(3.39)

{\rm gap}(H(\Lambda,s)):=\sup\{\delta>0:(E(\Lambda,s),E(\Lambda,s)+\delta)\cap{\rm spec}(H(\Lambda,s))=\emptyset\}\geq\gamma,

for all
|s|\leq s_{0}^{\Lambda}(\gamma)
.
An application of
[
37
, Theorem 6.3]
shows that

(3.40)

\tilde{\alpha}_{s}^{\Lambda}(P^{\Lambda}(s))=P^{\Lambda}(0)\,\quad\mbox{whenever }|s|\leq s_{0}^{\Lambda}(\gamma)\,,

where by
P^{\Lambda}(s)
we denote the orthogonal projection onto the ground state space of
H(\Lambda,s)
.

For any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the
state
\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}
given by

(3.41)

\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=\omega_{0}(\alpha_{s}^{\Lambda}(A))\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}\,

is a vector state in the GNS space:

(3.42)

\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=\langle\Omega(\Lambda,s),\pi_{0}(A)\Omega(\Lambda,s)\rangle\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}\,,

where
\Omega(\Lambda,s)=\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)\Omega\in{\mathcal{H}}
. By our assumptions,
P^{\Lambda}(0)=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|
.
An application of (
3.40
) then shows that

(3.43)

P^{\Lambda}(s)=\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|\tilde{U}(\Lambda,s)^{*}=|\Omega(\Lambda,s)\rangle\langle\Omega(\Lambda,s)|\quad\mbox{for all }|s|\leq s_{0}^{\Lambda}(\gamma),

and thus
\Omega(\Lambda,s)
is the ground state of
H(\Lambda,s)
.

Finally, we recall that with the parameters
\gamma
and
s
as above that the weighted integral operator
\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{s}^{\Lambda}
from (
3.33
) satisfies the relation

(3.44)

\left[\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}^{\Lambda}_{s}(A),|\Omega(\Lambda,s)\rangle\langle\Omega(\Lambda,s)|\right]=0\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})\,.

See, e.g.
[
37
, Lemma 6.8]
, for a proof of this property.

3.3.
On Domains

Recall that
(\Gamma,d)
is a
\nu
-regular metric space.
Let
F
be an
F
-function on
(\Gamma,d)
, and
\Phi
an
interaction with
\|\Phi\|_{F}<\infty
. As in Section
2
, let
\delta^{\Phi}
be the closed derivation
with dense domain
{\rm dom}(\delta^{\Phi})\subset{\mathcal{A}}
, and which satisfies

(3.45)

\delta^{\Phi}(A)=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma):}}{{Y\cap X\neq\emptyset}}}[\Phi(Y),A]\quad\mbox{for any }A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{X}\,.

Although the sum on the right-hand-side above may be infinite, it is absolutely convergent when
\Phi

has a finite
F
-norm. In fact,
\delta^{\Phi}
is locally bounded:

(3.46)

\|\delta^{\Phi}(A)\|\leq 2\|F\|\|\Phi\|_{F}|X|\|A\|\quad\mbox{for all }X\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma),A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{X},

see Example 4.7 of
[
37
, Section IV.B.1]
.
We have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be
\nu
-regular,
F
an
F
-function on
(\Gamma,d)
, and
g
a decay function with a finite
\nu
-moment, i.e.,

(3.47)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{\nu}g(n)<\infty\,.

For any interaction
\Phi
on
\Gamma
with
\|\Phi\|_{F}<\infty
,
we have that
{\mathcal{A}}^{g}\subset\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta^{\Phi})
.

Proof.

For
n\geq 1
, and
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
, for some
x\in\Gamma
, and observables

A_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(n)}
satisfying
\|A-A_{n}\|\leq\|A\|_{x,g}g(n)
.
In this case, the bound
\|A_{n+1}-A_{n}\|\leq 2\|A\|_{x,g}g(n)
is clear. Using (
3.46
) and
\nu
-regularity of
\Gamma
, we conclude

(3.48)

\|\delta^{\Phi}(A_{n+1})-\delta^{\Phi}(A_{n})\|\leq 4\|F\|\|\Phi\|_{F}\kappa\|A\|_{x,g}(n+1)^{\nu}g(n).

Thus, for all
m<n
,

(3.49)

\|\delta^{\Phi}(A_{n})-\delta^{\Phi}(A_{m})\|\leq 4\kappa\|A\|_{x,g}\|F\|\|\Phi\|_{F}\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}(k+1)^{\nu}g(k).

Since we assumed that
g
has a finite
\nu
-moment, this implies that
\delta^{\Phi}(A_{n})
is a
Cauchy sequence. Since
A_{n}\to A
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
is a core for
\delta^{\Phi}
, it follows
that
A\in\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta^{\Phi})
.
∎

Given the assumptions of Theorem
2.8
,
Lemma
3.1
clearly applies to the derivation
\delta_{0}
.
Using that
H_{0}\Omega=0
, one readily checks the relation

(3.50)

e^{itH_{0}}\pi_{0}(A)\Omega=\pi_{0}(\tau_{t}^{(0)}(A))\Omega\,,

from which the inclusion
\pi_{0}(\mathop{\rm dom}(\delta_{0}))\Omega\subset\mathop{\rm dom}(H_{0})
is clear.
As a result, if
g
is a decay function with a finite
\nu
-moment, then

\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{g})\Omega\subset\mathop{\rm dom}(H_{0})=\mathop{\rm dom}(H(\Lambda,s))
for any

\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
U(\Lambda,s)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
for some
g
with finite moments of all of orders by (
3.22
), it follows that

\pi_{0}(AU(\Lambda,s))\Omega\in\mathop{\rm dom}(H(\Lambda,s))
, for any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

A consequence of this is a gap inequality for the perturbed ground state
\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}
from (
3.41
). Namely, we show that
for
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0}),\Lambda\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\Gamma)
, and
|s|\leq s_{0}^{\Lambda}(\gamma)
:

(3.51)

\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}(A^{*}\delta_{s}^{\Lambda}(A))\geq\gamma\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}(A^{*}A),\mbox{ for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}\mbox{ with }\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}(A)=0.

To see this, fix
|s|\leq s_{0}^{\Lambda}(\gamma)
. Since
\Omega(\Lambda,s)
is the unique ground state of
H(\Lambda,s)
,

(3.52)

\langle\psi,(H(\Lambda,s)-E(\Lambda,s){\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}})\psi\rangle\geq\gamma\langle\psi,\psi\rangle

for all
\psi\in{\rm dom}(H_{0})
with
\langle\Omega(\Lambda,s),\psi\rangle=0
.
In particular, if
\omega^{\Lambda}_{s}(A)=0
for some
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
, then (
3.52
) holds for

\psi=\pi_{0}(A)\Omega(\Lambda,s)=\pi_{0}(AU(\Lambda,s))\Omega
since

\langle\Omega(\Lambda,s),\psi\rangle=\langle\Omega(\Lambda,s),\pi_{0}(A)\Omega(\Lambda,s)\rangle=\omega_{s}^{\Lambda}(A).

Then (
3.51
) follows from rewriting (
3.52
).

It will be important that on an appropriate dense domain, the action of the unbounded Hamiltonians can be expressed as a
limit of finite-volume quantities. This is the content of the next lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
({\mathcal{H}},\pi_{0},\Omega)
be the GNS representation of
\omega_{0}
, an infinite-volume, zero energy, ground state of a frustration free model
as in Assumption
2.1
. For any decay function
g
with a finite
\nu
-moment and any IAS
(\Lambda_{n})
,

(3.53)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi=H_{0}\psi\quad\mbox{ for all }\psi\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{g})\Omega\,,

where
H_{\Lambda_{n}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda_{n}}
is as in (
2.13
) and
H_{0}
is the GNS Hamiltonian.

Proof.

Note that (
3.53
) is trivially satisfied for
\psi=\pi_{0}(A)\Omega
, for
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
since

(3.54)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(A)\Omega=\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi_{0}([H_{\Lambda_{n}},A])\Omega=\pi_{0}(\delta_{0}(A))\Omega=H_{0}\pi_{0}(A)\Omega.

For the first equality we used
\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\Omega=0
, which is a consequence of the frustration-free property. Then, by the finite-range
condition on the unperturbed model,
[H_{\Lambda_{n}},A]
becomes constant for
n
sufficiently large.

Take
\psi=\pi_{0}(A)\Omega
for any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
. By the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
, there exists
x\in\Gamma
and observables
A_{m}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(m)}

so that
\|A-A_{m}\|\leq\|A\|_{x,g}g(m)
for all
m\geq 1
, and so the vectors
\psi_{m}:=\pi_{0}(A_{m})\Omega
satisfy

(3.55)

\|\psi-\psi_{m}\|\leq\|A\|_{x,g}g(m).

Moreover, since the interaction
h
is uniformly bounded with range
R
, it follows from (
3.45
) and
\nu
-regularity that for any
k\geq 1
,

\|\delta_{0}(A_{\ell+1}-A_{\ell})\|\leq\sum_{x\in b_{x}(\ell+1+R)}\|[h_{x},A_{\ell+1}-A_{\ell}]\|\leq 2\kappa^{2}R^{\nu}(\ell+1)^{\nu}\|h\|_{\infty}\|A_{\ell+1}-A_{\ell}\|

where we use the bound
|b_{x}(n+m)|\leq|b_{x}(n)||b_{x}(m)|
. Then, by the last equality of (
3.54
), one finds that
(H_{0}\psi_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is Cauchy as

(3.56)

\|H_{0}\psi_{m}-H_{0}\psi_{k}\|\leq\|\delta_{0}(A_{m})-\delta_{0}(A_{k})\|\leq D\sum_{\ell=k}^{m-1}(\ell+1)^{\nu}g(\ell)\,.

where we set
D=4\kappa^{2}R^{\nu}\|h\|_{\infty}\|A\|_{x,g}
. Since
H_{0}
is closed, and
\psi\in\mathop{\rm dom}(H_{0})
by Lemma
3.1
and the subsequent discussion, the bound

(3.57)

\|H_{0}\psi-H_{0}\psi_{k}\|\leq D\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}(\ell+1)^{\nu}g(\ell)\,

follows immediately from (
3.55
)-(
3.56
).

In the case of a local Hamiltonian, using again the first equality in (
3.54
), a similar argument shows that for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi-\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi_{k}\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi_{m}-\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi_{k}\|

(3.58)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\|[H_{\Lambda_{n}},A_{m}-A_{k}]\|\leq D\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}(\ell+1)^{\nu}g(\ell)\,.

Putting all of this together, one finds that for any
n\geq 1
and each
k\geq 1
,

(3.59)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi-H_{0}\psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi-\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi_{k}\|+\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\psi_{k}-H_{0}\psi_{k}\|+\|H_{0}\psi_{k}-H_{0}\psi\|

For
k\geq 1
sufficiently large, (
3.57
) and (
3.58
) guarantee that the first and last term above can be made arbitrarily small.
Given such a
k
, the middle term vanishes for
n
sufficiently large, see the comment following (
3.54
). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma
3.2
also trivially applies to the perturbed system

\lim_{n}\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}}+sV_{\Lambda})\psi=H_{0}\psi+s\pi_{0}(V_{\Lambda})\psi=H(\Lambda,s)\psi\quad\mbox{for all }\psi\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{g})\Omega.

.

then for any frustration free interaction

\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma):}}{{X\cap b_{x}(k)\neq\emptyset,X\cap\Lambda_{n}^{c}\neq\emptyset}}}\|\pi_{0}(\Phi(X))\psi_{k}\|\leq\|\Phi\|_{F}(1+g(0))\|A\|_{x,g}\sum_{y\in b_{x}(k),z\in\Lambda_{n}^{c}}F(d(y,z))

We now investigate how the weighted integral operator

from

.
To begin, we prove an analogue of the desired statement for the unperturbed dynamics; this is

below. To this end, assume

To simplify notation, let us also write

\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{u}(A)=e^{iuH_{0}}Ae^{-iuH_{0}}.

Our first result is as follows.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be
\nu
-regular,
g
be a decay function with a finite
2\nu
-moment, and
w:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy
(
3.62
). For each choice of IAS
(\Lambda_{n})
,
the weighted integral operator
\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
from (
3.63
) satisfies

(3.65)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}(\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}}))\psi=H_{0}\psi\quad\mbox{ for all }\psi\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{g})\Omega\,,

where
H_{\Lambda_{n}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda_{n}}
is as in (
2.13
).

Proof.

Fix an IAS
(\Lambda_{n})
and take
\psi=\pi_{0}(A)\Omega
for some
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g}
.
We can rewrite the convergence claimed in (
3.65
) as the convergence of integrals
of a sequence of functions
f_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathcal{H}}
given by

(3.66)

f_{n}(u)=w(u)\tilde{\tau}_{u}^{(0)}(\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}}))\psi\quad\mbox{for all }u\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Since
H_{0}\Omega=0
, the above can be re-written as

(3.67)

f_{n}(u)=w(u)e^{iuH_{0}}\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A))\Omega

using (

). We claim that there is a decay function
g_{\tau}
with a finite
\nu
-moment

\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{g_{\tau}}
for all

. Given this, Lemma
3.2
applies
and we find that

(3.68)

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(u)=w(u)e^{iuH_{0}}H_{0}\pi_{0}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A))\Omega=w(u)H_{0}\psi\quad\mbox{for all }u\in\mathbb{R}\,.

By (
3.62
), the integral of this limit coincides with the right-hand-side of

Let us first prove the existence of a decay function

as claimed. Fix

. In this case, there is

,

,
and observables
A_{m}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(m)}
for which

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
u\in\mathbb{R}
and for any

, set

(3.69)

A_{n}(u)=\Pi_{n}(\tau_{u}^{(0)}(A_{\lceil n/2\rceil}))\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(n)}

where, to ease notation, we have written

, for the conditional expectation from Section
3.1.1
.
A straightforward estimate shows that for any
\mu>0
,

(3.70)

\displaystyle\|\tau_{u}^{(0)}(A)-A_{n}(u)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\tau_{u}^{(0)}(A)-\tau_{u}^{(0)}(A_{\lceil n/2\rceil})\|+\|\tau_{u}^{(0)}(A_{\lceil n/2\rceil})-A_{n}(u))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cg(n/2)+\kappa C_{\mu}e^{\mu(v_{\mu}|u|+1)}(1+g(0))\|A\|_{x,g}(n/2+1)^{\nu}e^{-\mu n/2}

where we used (
3.8
) and (
3.4
) for the final bound. The existence of the decay function

follows from the moment condition on

and the decay of the exponential term..

We now turn to finding a dominating function for

. Recall that for any

,

(3.71)

Inserting this decomposition of

into (

and each

(3.72)

\|f_{n}(u)\|\leq|w(u)|\left(\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}}))\Omega\|+\sum_{k=m_{0}+1}^{\infty}\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(B_{k}))\Omega\|\right)\,.

Now, by the zero-energy property of the ground state
we find the bound

(3.73)

\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(A)\Omega\|\leq\kappa(k+R)^{\nu}\|h\|_{\infty}\|A\|\quad\mbox{for all }A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(k)}\,,

which we stress is uniform in

. This suggests a mechanism for bounding the first term in (

).
Let
\ell_{0}\geq m_{0}
and write

(3.74)

\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}})=\sum_{\ell\geq\ell_{0}}\Delta_{\ell_{0}}^{\ell}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}}))\,,

where we have used the short-hand
\Delta_{m}^{\ell}
for

as in (

).
For
\ell=\ell_{0}
, the bound

(3.75)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\Delta_{\ell_{0}}^{\ell_{0}}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}})))\Omega\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\Pi_{\ell_{0}}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}})))\Omega\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\|h\|_{\infty}(\ell_{0}+R)^{\nu}\|A_{m_{0}}\|

follows from (
3.73
). For
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, the estimate

(3.76)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\Delta_{\ell_{0}}^{\ell}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}})))\Omega\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\|h\|_{\infty}(\ell+R)^{\nu}\|\Delta_{\ell_{0}}^{\ell}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}}))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\kappa^{2}\|h\|_{\infty}C_{\mu}m_{0}^{\nu}(\ell+R)^{\nu}\|A_{m_{0}}\|e^{-\mu(\ell-m_{0}-1-v_{\mu}|u|)}

follows from another application of (

We conclude that

\displaystyle\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}}))\Omega\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\geq\ell_{0}}\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\Delta_{\ell_{0}}^{\ell}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}})))\Omega\|

(3.77)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|h\|_{\infty}\|A_{m_{0}}\|\left((\ell_{0}+R)^{\nu}+2\kappa C_{\mu}m_{0}^{\nu}\sum_{\ell\geq\ell_{0}+1}(\ell+R)^{\nu}e^{-\mu(\ell-m_{0}-1-v_{\mu}|u|)}\right).

If we now take
\ell_{0}=\lceil v_{\mu}|u|+m_{0}\rceil
, then we have found that there is

for which

(3.78)

\|\pi_{0}(H_{\Lambda_{n}})\pi_{0}(\tau_{-u}^{(0)}(A_{m_{0}}))\Omega\|\leq K\|h\|_{\infty}\|A_{m_{0}}\|\left(m_{0}^{2\nu}+|u|^{\nu}+(m_{0}|u|)^{\nu}+1\right)

and here

.

The terms
B_{k}
in (
3.72
) can be estimated similarly.
Regarding
k
as
m_{0}
and arguing as in (

3.4.
Proof of bounded differentiability of the spectral flow

At this point we pause to prove a result in the setting of this paper that is useful for related considerations concerning gapped insulators and symmetry protected phases.
For this purpose we consider differentiable curves of interactions
[0,1]\ni s\mapsto\Phi(s)
, for which we assume a finite norm of the following form:

(3.88)

\|\Phi\|^{1}_{F}=\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\sup_{x,y\in\Gamma}\frac{1}{F(d(x,y))}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma):}}{{x,y\in X}}}\left(\|\Phi(X,s)\|+|X|\|\Phi^{\prime}(X,s)\|\right),

where for each finite
X\subset\Gamma
and
0\leq s\leq 1
,
\Phi(X,s)^{*}=\Phi(X,s)\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
\Phi(X,\cdot):[0,1]\to\mathcal{A}_{X}
is
differentiable, and
F
is an
F
-function of stretched exponential decay as in (
2.11
).
For any differentiable curves of interactions
\Phi
with finite norm
\|\Phi\|^{1}_{F}
, there is an
F
-function
\tilde{F}
, and an
s
-dependent interaction

\Psi(s)
with

\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}:=\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\|\Psi(s)\|_{\tilde{F}}<\infty,

that generates the infinite volume spectral flow automorphisms
\alpha_{s}

[
37
, Section VI.E.2]
.
We further know by
[
37
, Theorem 3.9]
, that
this infinite volume spectral flow is differentiable on
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}^{\rm loc}
with

(3.89)

\frac{d}{ds}\alpha_{s}(A)=i\sum_{X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}\alpha_{s}([\Psi(X,s),A])\quad\mbox{for all }A\in\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}^{\rm loc}\,.

Our aim is the show that

\alpha^{\prime}_{s}:=\frac{d}{ds}\alpha_{s}

is a bounded map when defined on a suitable Banach algebra of
g
-local obervables.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose
g
is a decay function and
(\Lambda_{n})
an IAS that satisfy

(3.90)

\sum_{n\geq 1}|\Lambda_{n+1}|g(n)<\infty.

Then, the derivative
\alpha^{\prime}_{s}
of the spectral flow
\alpha_{s}
is a well-defined bounded linear map
{\mathcal{A}}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}\to{\mathcal{A}}
, satisfying

(3.91)

\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(A)\|\leq 2\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}\left(|\Lambda_{1}|+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Lambda_{n+1}|g(n)\right)\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}\,,

with
\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}
as defined in (
2.9
).

Proof.

We start by noting that for any local observable
A\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
with
Y\subset\Gamma
finite, we have the following estimate:

(3.92)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{d}{ds}\alpha_{s}(A)\right\|\leq\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X:}}{{X\cap Y\neq\emptyset}}}\|[\Psi(X,s),\alpha_{s}(A)]\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|A\|\sum_{y\in Y}\sum_{z\in\Gamma}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X:}}{{y,z\in X}}}\|\Psi(X,s)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|A\|\sum_{y\in Y}\sum_{z\in\Gamma}\tilde{F}(d(y,z))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}|Y|\|A\|

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}
. By (
2.6
),

(3.93)

\|A-\Pi_{n}(A)\|\leq\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}g(n)

which implies that the sequence
\{\Pi_{n}(A)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
A
(in norm) at a rate governed by
g
.
Define a map
\alpha^{\prime}_{s}:\mathcal{A}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}\to\mathcal{A}
by setting

(3.94)

\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d}{ds}\alpha_{s}\left(\Pi_{n}(A)\right)\quad\mbox{for all }A\in\mathcal{A}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}\,.

Note that for any strictly local observable
A
,
\Pi_{n}(A)=A
if
n
is sufficiently large, and thus this
definition agrees with the standard definition of the derivative of
\alpha_{s}(A)
for
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc}
.

Now, consider integers
1\leq M<N<\infty
. For any observable
A
,

(3.95)

\Pi_{N}(A)-\Pi_{M}(A)=\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}\left(\Pi_{n+1}(A)-\Pi_{n}(A)\right)

and for
A\in\mathcal{A}_{g}
, we also have that for any
n\geq 1
,

(3.96)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{n+1}(A)-\Pi_{n}(A)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{n+1}(A)-A\|+\|A-\Pi_{n}(A)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}\left(g(n+1)+g(n)\right)\leq 2\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}g(n)

In the above, we have used (
3.93
) and the fact that
g
is non-increasing.
We conclude that

(3.97)

\displaystyle\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{N}(A))-\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{M}(A))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}\left(\Pi_{n+1}(A)-\Pi_{n}(A)\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}|\Lambda_{n+1}|\|\Pi_{n+1}(A)-\Pi_{n}(A)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}|\Lambda_{n+1}|g(n)\,.

Here, for the second inequality above we used (
3.92
), and the final bound comes from (
3.96
).
We conclude that whenever
g
satisfies (
3.90
), the sequence
\{\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{n}(A))\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is norm Cauchy; hence,
norm convergent. This shows that
\alpha^{\prime}_{s}
as given in (
3.94
) is well-defined and equals the derivative of
\alpha_{s}(A)
, for
A\in{\mathcal{A}}^{(\Lambda_{n}),g}
.
Moreover, for any
N\geq 1
,

(3.98)

\displaystyle\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{N}(A))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{1}(A))\|+\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{N}(A))-\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{1}(A))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}|\Lambda_{1}|\|\Pi_{1}(A)\|+4\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}|\Lambda_{n+1}|g(n)

and therefore,

(3.99)

\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(A)\|=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\alpha^{\prime}_{s}(\Pi_{N}(A))\|\leq 2\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{F}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{1}\left(|\Lambda_{1}|+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Lambda_{n+1}|g(n)\right)\|A\|_{(\Lambda_{n}),g}

as claimed.
∎

This proves that Assumption 1.2 (vii) in
[
33
]
(see also
[
7
,
41
]
) is always satisfied under the assumptions made in that reference. We note that in the same way one obtains the extension of the invariants constructed by Ogata in
[
42
,
39
,
41
]
from models with finite-range interactions to the setting with interactions of stretched exponential decay.

4.

The crux of the stability strategy introduced in

and

.

The proof of Theorem

Lemma 4.5

Let

is the decay function from Assumption

To begin, one uses the LTQO property (

(4.51)

\left|\|\tilde{A}\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(n)}\|-\|\tilde{A}P_{\Omega}\|\right|\leq\|A\|\sqrt{(1+m)^{\nu}G_{0}(n-m)}\;\mbox{ for all }\;A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(m)}

where

Recalling that
({\mathcal{H}},\pi_{0},\Omega)
is the GNS representation of the unperturbed ground state

As
\pi_{0}

\|\tilde{A}\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(n)}\|^{2}=\|\pi_{0}(AP_{b_{x}(n)})\|^{2}=\|P_{b_{x}(n)}A^{*}AP_{b_{x}(n)}\|.

\displaystyle\left|\|\tilde{A}\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(n)}\|-\|\tilde{A}P_{\Omega}\|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\|P_{b_{x}(n)}A^{*}AP_{b_{x}(n)}\|-\omega_{0}(A^{*}A)\|P_{b_{x}(n)}\|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|^{2}(1+m)^{\nu}G_{0}(n-m),

which establishes (
4.51

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=R}^{m}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(n)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=R}^{m}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)P_{\Omega}\right\|+\left\|\sum_{k=R}^{m}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)\right\|\sqrt{(1+m)^{\nu}G_{0}(n-m)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=R}^{m}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)P_{\Omega}\right\|+2sG_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(x,R)\sqrt{(1+m)^{\nu}G_{0}(n-m)}

where the last inequality follows from Theorem
4.2
as
\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)\|\leq 2\|\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}(x,k,s)\|\leq 2sG_{\Lambda}(x,k)
.

The remaining operator norm from (
4.53
) can then be trivially bounded in terms of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)
from (
4.49
) as follows:

(4.54)

\left\|\sum_{k=R}^{m}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)P_{\Omega}\right\|\leq\left\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)P_{\Omega}\right\|+\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)P_{\Omega}\right\|.

Once again applying Theorem
4.2
then shows that

(4.55)

\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)P_{\Omega}\right\|\leq 2\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}(x,k,s)\right\|\leq 2sG_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(x,m+1),

and, moreover,
[\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s),P_{\Omega}]=[\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}(x,s),P_{\Omega}]=0
. As a result,
\left\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)P_{\Omega}\right\|=0
since

(4.56)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)P_{\Omega}=P_{\Omega}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)P_{\Omega}=\left\langle\Omega,\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)\Omega\right\rangle P_{\Omega}=0,

where the last inequality follows from (
4.48
)-(
4.49
). Thus, inserting (
4.55
) into (
4.53
) proves (
4.50
).
∎

We now prove Theorem
4.1
, which uses both Lemma
4.5
and the frustration-free property.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Fix
x\in\Gamma
and recall that
P_{\Omega}=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|
. Since
[\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s),P_{\Omega}]=0
, one can write

\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)=P_{\Omega}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)P_{\Omega}+({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega})\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega})=({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega})\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega}),

where the last equality uses (
4.56
). The terms
\tilde{\Phi}^{(2)}(x,m,s)
are defined by decomposing
{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega}
in terms of the finite volume ground state projections
\tilde{P}_{n}:=\tilde{P}_{b_{x}(n)}\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(n)})
.

First, note that
\tilde{P}_{n}
converges strongly to
P_{\Omega}
for all
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
by the frustration-free and LTQO properties.
As a consequence, the collection of operators

(4.57)

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{n}=\begin{cases}{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{R},&n=R\\
\tilde{P}_{n-1}-\tilde{P}_{n},&n\geq R+1\end{cases}

forms a family of orthogonal projections that are mutually orthogonal and sum to
{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega}
. That is,

(4.58)

\tilde{E}_{n}^{*}=\tilde{E}_{n},\quad\tilde{E}_{n}\tilde{E}_{m}=\delta_{m,n}\tilde{E}_{n},\quad\text{and}\quad({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega})\psi=\sum_{n\geq R}\tilde{E}_{n}\psi\quad\forall\;\psi\in{\mathcal{H}},

where the second equality holds since the frustration-free property implies
\tilde{P}_{n}\tilde{P}_{m}=\tilde{P}_{m}
for
m\geq n
. Moreover, it trivially holds that

(4.59)

{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m}=\sum_{n=R}^{m}\tilde{E}_{n}.

Using (
4.49
), the above properties imply that for all
\psi,\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

(4.60)

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi,({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega})\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\Omega})\psi\right\rangle=\sum_{k,m,n\geq R}\left\langle\phi,\tilde{E}_{n}\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}^{(1)}(x,k,s)\tilde{E}_{m}\psi\right\rangle

We note that the triple sum of operators actually converges absolutely in norm, and so the operator equality holds in the norm sense.

Each term
\Phi^{(2)}(x,m,s)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(m)}
will be defined as a sum of two self-adjoint terms

\pi_{0}(\Phi^{(2)}(x,m,s))=\Theta_{1}(x,m,s)+\Theta_{2}(x,m,s)

each of which is annihilated by the ground state projection
\tilde{P}_{m}
. Fix
k\geq R
, and use the properties in (
4.58
)-(
4.59
) to write

(4.61)

\sum_{n,m\geq R}\tilde{E}_{n}\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,k,s)\tilde{E}_{m}=({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{2k})\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,k,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{2k})+\sum_{m>2k}\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m},

where
\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m}=\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m}^{*}
is defined by

(4.62)

\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m}=\tilde{E}_{m}\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,k,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m-1})+({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m})\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,k,s)\tilde{E}_{m}\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(m)}).

Self-adjointness follows from noting that
{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m}={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m-1}+\tilde{E}_{m}
.

For each
m\geq R
, define
\Theta_{1}(x,m,s)\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{b_{x}(m)}),
by

(4.63)

\Theta_{1}(x,m,s)=\begin{cases}({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m})\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,m/2,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m}),&m\mbox{ even }\\
0,&m\mbox{ odd }\end{cases}

These operators are self-adjoint, satisfy
\Theta_{1}(x,m,s)\tilde{P}_{m}=0
, and Theorem
4.2
implies that their norm is bounded from above by
2sG_{\Lambda}(x,m/2)
as for
m
even:

(4.64)

\|\Theta_{1}(x,m,s)\|\leq\|\Phi^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,m/2,s)\|\leq 2sG_{\Lambda}(x,m/2).

For the
\Theta_{2}
terms, one sums the remaining terms
\sum_{m>2k}\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m}
over
k
, and then uses the indicator function
\chi_{m>2k}
to exchange the summations as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq R}\sum_{m>2k}\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq R}\sum_{m>2R}\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m}\chi_{\{m>2k\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m>2R}\sum_{R\leq k<m/2}\tilde{\Phi}_{k,m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m>2R}\Theta_{2}(x,m,s)

where, for
m>2R
one recalls (
4.62
) and defines

(4.65)

\Theta_{2}(x,m,s)=\sum_{k=R}^{\lceil m/2\rceil-1}\tilde{E}_{m}\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,k,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m-1})+({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m})\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,k,s)\tilde{E}_{m}.

This definition is extended by setting
\Theta_{2}(x,m,s)=0
for
R\leq m\leq 2R
. One sees that
\Theta_{2}(x,m,s)\tilde{P}_{m}=0
by applying
\tilde{P}_{m}\tilde{P}_{m-1}=\tilde{P}_{m}
to verify

\tilde{E}_{m}\tilde{P}_{m}=(\tilde{P}_{m}-\tilde{P}_{m-1})\tilde{P}_{m}=0.

Finally, applying Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{2}(x,m,s)\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\sum_{k=R}^{\lceil m/2\rceil-1}\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\omega}(x,k,s)({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{m-1})\right\|

(4.66)

\displaystyle\leq 4s\left(G_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(x,\lceil m/2\rceil)+G_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(x,R)\sqrt{\lceil m/2\rceil^{\nu}G_{0}(m/2)}\right)

where
G_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(x,m)=\sum_{n\geq m}G_{\Lambda}(x,n)
.

Therefore, setting
\tilde{\Phi}^{(2)}(x,m,s)=\Theta_{1}(x,m,s)+\Theta_{2}(x,m,s)
for all
m
, we have constructed self-adjoint operators such that

(4.67)

W(\Lambda,s)=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{m\geq R}\tilde{\Phi}^{(2)}(x,m,s).

Moreover, these operators satisfy that for all
m\geq R
and
x\in\Gamma
,

\tilde{\Phi}^{(2)}(x,m,s)\tilde{P}_{m}=\tilde{P}_{m}\tilde{\Phi}^{(2)}(x,m,s)=0

and, combining (
4.64
) and (
4.66
), their norms can be bounded above by

(4.68)

\|\tilde{\Phi}^{(2)}(x,m,s)\|\leq 2sG_{\Lambda}^{(2)}(x,m)

where

(4.69)

G_{\Lambda}^{(2)}(x,m)=G_{\Lambda}(x,m/2)+2G_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(x,\lceil m/2\rceil)+2G_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(x,R)\sqrt{\lceil m/2\rceil^{\nu}G_{0}(m/2)}.

The absolute summability of the series in (
4.67
) is a direct consequence of
G_{0}
being summable as well as that both
G_{\Lambda}
and
G_{\Lambda}^{(1)}
satisfy (
4.5
). For
G_{\Lambda}^{(1)}
this can easily be seen from the fact that
G_{\Lambda}
is a combination of functions with finite moments of all orders, see specifically (
4.35
), (
4.38
) and (
4.46
).
∎

5.
Proving Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
via a form bound for the GNS Hamiltonian

In this section, we generalize
[
38
, Theorem 3.8]
, which was itself based off
[
32
, Proposition 2]
, so that it is applicable to the setting of infinite systems in their GNS representation. Afterwards, we apply the form bound in conjunction with Theorem
4.1
to prove Theorem
2.8
. We then conclude with the proof of Theorem
2.9
, which follows as a consequence of Theorem
2.8
.

5.1.
The Michalakis-Zwolak relative form bound

The form bound result is stated in the setting described in Section
2
and, in particular, under Assumption
2.3
. Thus, there is a family of sets

\mathcal{S}=\left\{\Lambda(x,n)|x\in\Gamma,n\geq 0\mbox{ s.t. }b_{x}(n)\subseteq\Lambda(x,n)\right\},

accompanied with
a family
\mathcal{T}=\{\mathcal{T}_{n}:n\geq 0\}
of separating partitions of
(c,\zeta)
-polynomial growth,
for which the associated finite volume Hamiltonians satisfy

(5.1)

H_{\Lambda(x,n)}\geq\gamma(n)P_{\Lambda(x,n)},\mbox{ for all }n\geq R\,.

Moreover, the local gaps are further assumed to satisfy
\gamma(n)\geq\gamma_{1}/n^{\alpha}

for some
\gamma_{1}>0
and
\alpha\geq 0
.

Theorem 5.1

(Michalakis-Zwolak
[
32
]
)
.

Let
H_{0}
be the GNS Hamiltonian associated with a zero-energy ground state of an initial system satisfying Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
, and let
V\in{\mathcal{A}}
be a perturbation associated with an absolutely-summable, anchored interaction on
(\Gamma,d)
. That is, there exist
\Phi(x,n)^{*}=\Phi(x,n)\in\mathcal{A}_{b_{x}(n)}
for all
x\in\Gamma
and
n\geq R
such that

(5.2)

V=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{n\geq R}\Phi(x,n),\qquad\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{n\geq R}\|\Phi(x,n)\|<\infty.

In addition, assume that terms of
V
annihilate the finite-volume ground state projections of the initial system, i.e.

(5.3)

\Phi(x,n)P_{b_{x}(n)}=P_{b_{x}(n)}\Phi(x,n)=0\quad\mbox{for all }x\in\Gamma\mbox{ and }n\geq R\,,

and that there is a decay function
G
with finite
(\zeta+\alpha)
-moment such that
\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\|\Phi(x,n)\|\leq G(n)\,.

Then, for all
\psi\in\mathop{\rm dom}{H_{0}}
,

(5.4)

\left|\left\langle\psi,\pi_{\omega}(V)\,\psi\right\rangle\right|\;\leq\;\beta\left\langle\psi,H_{0}\,\psi\right\rangle\quad\text{where}\quad\beta=c\sum_{n\geq R}\frac{n^{\zeta}G(n)}{\gamma(n)}\leq\frac{c}{\gamma_{1}}\sum_{n\geq R}n^{\zeta+\alpha}G(n).

In keeping with the notation from the previous sections, denote by
\tilde{A}=\pi_{0}(A)
the image of any observable
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
under the GNS representation
({\mathcal{H}},\pi_{0},\Omega)
of the zero-energy ground state
\omega_{0}
. The proof of Theorem
5.1
follows closely the argument proving
[
38
, Theorem 3.8]
, with the proviso that one must check that the infinite operator sums replacing the finite operator sums from
[
38
]
are well-defined.

To this end, let
n\geq R
, and consider the
n
-th separating partition
\mathcal{T}_{n}=\{\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\}
.
For each
i\in\mathcal{I}_{n}
and any choice of
x,y\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}
, one has that

(5.5)

[\tilde{H}_{\Lambda(x,n)},\tilde{H}_{\Lambda(y,n)}]=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad[\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(x,n)},\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(y,n)}]=0\,.

This follows since the corresponding algebra elements
H_{\Lambda(x,n)},H_{\Lambda(y,n)}\in{\mathcal{A}}
are supported on disjoint sets (and similarly for the ground state projections), which carries over to the GNS space by the homomorphism property.

With
n\geq R
and
i\in\mathcal{I}_{n}
fixed, denote by
C_{n}^{i}
the collection of all configurations associated to

\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}
. More precisely,

(5.6)

C_{n}^{i}=\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}}=\left\{\sigma:\sigma=\{\sigma_{x}\}\mbox{ where }\sigma_{x}\in\{0,1\}\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\right\}\,.

For each
\sigma\in C_{n}^{i}
, we define
|\sigma|
by

(5.7)

|\sigma|=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}}\sigma_{x}.

Recall that
\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc})\Omega
is a
dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\psi=\tilde{A}\Omega
for some
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, and set
\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(x,n)}
. In this case, one has that

(5.8)

\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\psi=\psi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\psi=0\,

whenever
\Lambda(x,n)
satisfies
\Lambda(x,n)\cap X=\emptyset
. It is also clear that for
any
X\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, the set of sites
\{x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}:\Lambda(x,n)\cap X\neq\emptyset\}
is finite.
Thus, for any
\psi\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc})\Omega
, define the operator
S(\sigma)
by

(5.9)

S(\sigma)\psi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}}\left[\sigma_{x}\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}+(1-\sigma_{x})\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\right]\psi&\mbox{if }|\sigma|<\infty,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Note that, if
|\sigma|<\infty
, then at most finitely many of these factors act non-trivially, and
moreover, by (
5.5
), all factors above commute. Since
\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc})\Omega
is
dense, there is a unique extension of
S(\sigma)
to an element of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega})

for each
\sigma\in C_{n}^{i}
. One checks that these operators satisfy:

(5.10)

S(\sigma)^{*}=S(\sigma),\quad S(\sigma)S(\sigma^{\prime})=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}S(\sigma),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{\sigma\in C_{n}^{i}}S(\sigma)={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\,.

Since
\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(x,n)}
, by (
5.5
) one clearly has that

(5.11)

[\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)},\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(y,n)}]=0\quad\mbox{for all }\;x,y\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\,

and as a result, also

(5.12)

[\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)},S(\sigma)]=0\quad\mbox{for each }x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\mbox{ and all }\;\sigma\in C_{n}^{i}.

For
\sigma\in C_{n}^{i}
with
|\sigma|<\infty
, (
5.11
) implies

0=[\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)},S(\sigma)]\psi
for all
\psi\in\pi_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{\rm loc})\Omega
and, hence, for all
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
. If
|\sigma|=\infty
, then the commutator is zero by definition.
Combining (
5.10
) and (
5.12
), we find that

(5.13)

S(\sigma)\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}S(\sigma^{\prime})=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}S(\sigma)=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(x,n)})S(\sigma)=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\sigma_{x}S(\sigma)

for all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in C_{n}^{i}
and each
x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}
.

We use these families of orthogonal projections in the following proof.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

In the GNS representation, the terms of
\tilde{V}
can be rearranged using the family of separating partitions from Assumption
2.3
as

(5.14)

\tilde{V}=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{n\geq R}\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)=\sum_{n\geq R}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\tilde{V}_{n}^{i},\qquad\tilde{V}_{n}^{i}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}}\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)

since
V
is absolutely summable. As a result, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
, one has the bound

(5.15)

|\langle\psi,\tilde{V}\psi\rangle|\leq\sum_{n\geq R}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}|\langle\psi,\tilde{V}_{n}^{i}\psi\rangle|\quad\mbox{with}\quad|\langle\psi,\tilde{V}_{n}^{i}\psi\rangle|\leq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}}|\langle\psi,\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)\psi\rangle|\,.

Now, since
b_{x}(n)\subset\Lambda(x,n)
for all
\Lambda(x,n)\in\mathcal{S}
,
the frustration free property implies

(5.16)

P_{\Lambda(x,n)}=P_{b_{x}(n)}P_{\Lambda(x,n)}=P_{\Lambda(x,n)}P_{b_{x}(n)},

and so by (
5.3
) it follows that that for each
x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}
, the term
\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)
satisfies

(5.17)

[\tilde{\Phi}(x,n),\tilde{P}_{\Lambda(y,n)}]=0\quad\mbox{for all }y\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}.

Arguing as in (
5.12
)-(
5.13
) above, one then finds that for all
x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}
and
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in C_{n}^{i}

(5.18)

[\tilde{\Phi}(x,n),S(\sigma)]=0\quad

and, moreover,

\displaystyle S(\sigma)\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)S(\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}S(\sigma)\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\sigma_{x}S(\sigma)\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)

(5.19)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}S(\sigma)\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}S(\sigma)\,.

As a consequence, one can use (
5.10
) and (
5.19
) to bound

(5.20)

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)\psi\rangle|\leq\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}|\langle\psi,S(\sigma)\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)S(\sigma^{\prime})\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}|\langle\psi,S(\sigma)\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}S(\sigma)\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle G(n)\sum_{\sigma}\|\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}S(\sigma)\psi\|^{2}.

Summing these orthogonal projections again, one obtains

(5.21)

\sum_{\sigma}\|\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}S(\sigma)\psi\|^{2}=\sum_{\sigma}\langle\psi,\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}S(\sigma)\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,\tilde{Q}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\psi\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma(n)}\langle\psi,\tilde{H}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\psi\rangle

where, the final bound follows since the representation
preserves positivity and the operator inequality
\gamma(n)Q_{\Lambda(x,n)}\leq H_{\Lambda(x,n)}
holds in the algebra.

Given the above, one concludes that for any
\psi\in{\rm dom}(H_{0})
,

(5.22)

|\langle\psi,\tilde{V}_{n}^{i}\psi\rangle|\leq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}}|\langle\psi,\tilde{\Phi}(x,n)\psi\rangle|\leq\frac{G(n)}{\gamma(n)}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}}\langle\psi,\tilde{H}_{\Lambda(x,n)}\psi\rangle\leq\frac{G(n)}{\gamma(n)}\langle\psi,H_{0}\psi\rangle

where the last inequality uses that
\Lambda(x,n)\cap\Lambda(y,n)=\emptyset
for distinct
x,y\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}
. Returning to (
5.15
), since these partitions satisfy the
(c,\zeta)
-polynomial growth bound it is clear that

(5.23)

|\langle\psi,\tilde{V}\psi\rangle|\leq\sum_{n\geq R}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}|\langle\psi,\tilde{V}_{n}^{i}\psi\rangle|\leq\beta\langle\psi,H_{0}\psi\rangle\,,

with
\beta
as in (
5.4
). This completes the proof.
∎

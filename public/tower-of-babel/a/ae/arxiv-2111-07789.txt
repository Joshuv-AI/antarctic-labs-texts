Abstract
The super-weak force combines three simple extensions of the Standard Model, one in gauge sector, one in fermion sector and one in scalar sector. Each of these extensions are well motivated by their rich phenomenology. Combined to a single framework, they can explain several open questions in particle physics and cosmology: the origin of dark matter, cosmic inflation, matter-antimatter asymmetry, neutrino masses and vacuum stability. We discuss the effects of the model on neutrino masses and phenomenology in the case where the heaviest sterile neutrinos have a GeV scale mass.

Introduction

The incompleteness of the standard model (SM) of particle physics, when confronted with Nature, is a well-known fact. While the SM performs suspiciously well on the description of particle interactions, the discovery of neutrino oscillations in vacuum and matter - among other phenomena - has made abundantly clear that the SM must be extended to include new interactions. Even simple extensions may lead to a rich phenomenology. These include extensions on the scalar, fermion and gauge sectors. One could extend the model by an extra singlet scalar boson, a heavy neutral lepton or by a small gauge group. All three of these possibilities are taken into account and combined in a single framework which is called the
super-weak model

2.1
Gauge extension

The gauge group of the super-weak model is

\text{SU}(3)_{\text{c}}\otimes\text{SU}(2)_{\text{L}}\otimes\text{U}(1)_{Y}\otimes\text{U}(1)_{z},

(1)

that is, the SM group is extended by an extra U(1) group. The kinetic terms of the U(1)
{}_{Y}\otimes
U(1)
z
sector of the group can be described with the Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}^{\text{U(1)}}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}F^{\prime\mu\nu}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}F^{\mu\nu}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu},

(2)

where
F_{\mu\nu}
and
F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}
correspond to the field strength tensors of U(1)
Y
and U(1)
z
. The model exhibits kinetic mixing, driven by a small real dimensionless coupling
\varepsilon
. The covariant derivative acting on the fermion field
f
can be written as

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{U(1)}=\partial_{\mu}-\text{i}(y^{f}g_{y}B_{\mu}+z^{f}g_{z}B^{\prime}_{\mu}),

(3)

when we suppress the non-Abelian contribution. The
y^{f}
and
z^{f}
are the hypercharge and U(1)
z
charge of
f
. Equivalently we may choose a basis where the kinetic mixing is absent. Then, the covariant derivative can be written as

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{U(1)}=\partial_{\mu}-\text{i}(y,z)\begin{pmatrix}g_{y}&-\eta g_{z}^{\prime}\\
0&g_{z}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varepsilon^{\prime}&\sin\varepsilon^{\prime}\\
-\sin\varepsilon^{\prime}&\cos\varepsilon^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\binom{\hat{B}_{\mu}}{\hat{B^{\prime}}_{\mu}},

(4)

where we have denoted
\eta=\varepsilon g_{y}/g_{z}
and
g_{z}^{\prime}=g_{z}/\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}
. The rotation angle
\varepsilon^{\prime}
is not physical, since it can be absorbed to re-defined gauge fields. The mass eigenstates (
A_{\mu},Z_{\mu},Z^{\prime}_{\mu})
are related to these fields via a rotation,

\begin{pmatrix}\hat{B}_{\mu}\\
W^{3}_{\mu}\\
\hat{B^{\prime}}_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{W}&-\cos\theta_{Z}\sin\theta_{W}&-\sin\theta_{Z}\sin\theta_{W}\\
\sin\theta_{W}&\cos\theta_{Z}\cos\theta_{W}&\cos\theta_{W}\sin\theta_{Z}\\
0&-\sin\theta_{Z}&\cos\theta_{Z}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{\mu}\\
Z_{\mu}\\
Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where
\theta_{W}
is the weak mixing angle and
\theta_{Z}
is the
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing angle. The interactions of a neutral vector boson
V=Z
or
Z^{\prime}
with fermion
f
can be written as a chiral decomposition:

\Gamma^{\mu}_{Vff}=-\text{i}e\gamma^{\mu}\left(C^{R}_{Vff}P_{R}+C^{L}_{Vff}P_{L}\right),\quad P_{L,R}=\half(\textbf{1}\mp\gamma_{5}).

(6)

The coefficients corresponding to the coupling of a vector boson to chiral fermion fields can be written in a simple form of rotation:

\displaystyle-e\binom{C^{L,R}_{Zff}}{C^{L,R}_{Z^{\prime}ff}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{Z}&\sin\theta_{Z}\\
-\sin\theta_{Z}&\cos\theta_{Z}\end{pmatrix}\binom{T_{3}^{f}+Q^{f}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}{-(y^{f}g_{y}^{\prime}+z^{f}g_{z}^{\prime})}

(7)

Clearly the coefficient corresponding to

T_{3}^{f}

2.2
Scalar extension

The scalar sector of the super-weak model consists of the SM Higgs SU(2) doublet
\phi
with charges
(y_{\phi},z_{\phi})=(1/2,1)
and a complex singlet scalar
\chi
with charges
(y_{\chi},z_{\chi})=(0,-1)
. The relevant Lagrangian corresponding to them is

\mathcal{L}_{\text{scalar}}=|D_{\mu}\phi|^{2}+|D_{\mu}\chi|^{2}-\mu_{\phi}^{2}|\phi|^{2}-\mu_{\chi}^{2}|\chi|^{2}-\lambda_{\phi}|\phi|^{4}-\lambda_{\chi}|\chi|^{4}-\lambda|\phi|^{2}|\chi|^{2},

(8)

and we parametrize the fields after spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) in
R_{\xi}
gauge as

\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\binom{-i\sqrt{2}\sigma^{+}}{v+h^{\prime}+i\sigma_{\phi}},\quad\chi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(w+s^{\prime}+i\sigma_{\chi}\right),

(9)

where
v\simeq 246.22
GeV and
w
are the vacuum expectation values and the fields
h^{\prime},s^{\prime},\sigma_{\chi}
and
\sigma_{\phi}
are real. The fields
\sigma_{\phi}
and
\sigma_{\chi}
correspond to the Goldstone bosons. To obtain physical fields, we perform standard field rotations

\displaystyle\binom{h}{s}=Z_{S}(\theta_{S})\binom{h^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}},\quad\binom{\sigma_{Z}}{\sigma_{Z^{\prime}}}=Z_{G}(\theta_{G})\binom{\sigma_{\phi}}{\sigma_{\chi}},\quad Z_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{X}&\sin\theta_{X}\\
-\sin\theta_{X}&\cos\theta_{X}\end{pmatrix}

(10)

where
\theta_{S}
and
\theta_{G}
are the scalar and Goldstone mixing angles, given by the relations

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{S}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda vw}{\lambda_{\phi}v^{2}-\lambda_{\chi}w^{2}},

\displaystyle\tan\theta_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}}{M_{Z}}\tan\theta_{Z}.

(11)

2.3
Fermion extension

The fermion sector of the super-weak model is extended with three sterile massive Majorana neutrinos
N_{R}=(\nu_{4},\nu_{5},\nu_{6})
. We denote the active SM neutrinos as
\nu_{L}=(\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau})
in the flavor basis and
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
in the mass basis. The Majorana mass term corresponding to
N_{R}
can not be directly included in the Lagrangian, since
N_{R}
has a nonzero U(1)
z
charge. Instead, such a mass term is generated dynamically by SSB. The gauge invariant Yukawa interactions of the neutrinos are given by Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\nu}=-\overline{N_{R}}Y_{\nu}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}L_{L\alpha}\phi_{\beta}-\half\overline{N_{R}}Y_{N}(N_{R})^{c}\chi+\text{h.c.},

(12)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are SU(2)
L
indices and
\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
. After SSB, the neutrino mass terms and neutrino-scalar interaction terms are generated. Defining the
3\times 3
Dirac and Majorana mass matrices

M_{D}=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}Y_{\nu},\quad M_{N}=\frac{w}{\sqrt{2}}Y_{N},

(13)

the neutrino mass terms can be collected in a form

\mathcal{L}_{m}^{\nu}=-\half\left(\nu_{L},\>(N_{R})^{c}\right)C\begin{pmatrix}0&M_{D}^{T}\\
M_{D}&M_{N}\end{pmatrix}\binom{\nu_{L}}{(N_{R})^{c}},

(14)

which has the exact form of type-I seesaw mechanism. The light neutrino mass matrix
M_{L}=-M_{D}M_{N}^{-1}M_{D}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}
can be obtained by block-diagnonalizing the full
6\times 6
neutrino mass matrix
M
via a unitary matrix
U
:

U^{T}MU=U^{T}\begin{pmatrix}0&M_{D}^{T}\\
M_{D}&M_{N}\end{pmatrix}U=M_{\text{diag}}=\text{diag}(m_{1},\dots,m_{6}),

(15)

where the masses
m_{1},\dots,m_{6}
correspond to neutrinos
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{6}
. Writing the diagonalizing matrix as

U=\binom{U_{L}}{U_{R}^{*}},

(16)

where
U_{L}
and
U_{R}
are semiunitary
3\times 6
matrices, it is straightforward to derive the conditions

U_{L}U_{L}^{\dagger}=U_{R}U_{R}^{\dagger}=\textbf{1}_{3},\quad U^{\dagger}U=U_{L}^{\dagger}U_{L}+U_{R}^{T}U_{R}^{*}=\textbf{1}_{6}

(17)

for them.

The loop-induced corrections to light neutrino masses receive additional contributions in the super-weak model compared to the standard seesaw scenario.
A priori

\delta M_{L}=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=1,2}\left(3(Z_{G})_{k1}\frac{M_{V_{k}}}{v^{2}}F\left(M_{V_{k}}^{2}\right)+(Z_{S})_{k1}\frac{M_{S_{k}}}{v^{2}}F\left(M_{S_{k}}^{2}\right)\right)

(18)

Here we have denoted
(V_{1},V_{2})=(Z,Z^{\prime})
and
(S_{1},S_{2})=(h,s)

3\times 3

F

F_{ij}\equiv F_{ij}\left(M^{2};U_{L},\{m_{a}\}_{a=1}^{6}\right)=\sum\limits_{a=1}^{6}(U_{L}^{*})_{ia}(U_{L}^{\dagger})_{aj}m_{a}\left(\frac{m_{a}}{M}\right)^{2}\frac{\ln\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{M^{2}}}{\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{M^{2}}-1}

3.2
Active-sterile mixing

We use the Casas-Ibarra parameterization to write the Yukawa matrix
Y_{\nu}
in terms of the neutrino mixing matrix elements
U_{ij}^{\text{PMNS}}
and neutrino masses
m_{1},\dots,m_{6}
as

Y_{\nu}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}U^{\text{PMNS,*}}\sqrt{M^{\text{diag}}_{L}}(-iR^{T})\sqrt{M_{N}}.

(20)

The
3\times 3
active-sterile mixing matrix can the be written as

U_{\text{as}}=\begin{pmatrix}U_{e4}&U_{e5}&U_{e6}\\
U_{\mu 4}&U_{\mu 5}&U_{\mu 6}\\
U_{\tau 4}&U_{\tau 5}&U_{\tau 6}\end{pmatrix}=m_{D}^{*}m_{R}^{-1\dagger}=U^{\text{PMNS}}\sqrt{M_{L}^{\text{diag}}}iR^{T}\sqrt{M_{R}^{-1}}

(21)

The matrix
R
is an arbitrary complex orthogonal matrix. Here we assume that
R
is real, hence it can be parametrized as

R=\begin{pmatrix}c_{12}&-s_{12}&0\\
s_{12}&c_{12}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}c_{13}&0&s_{13}&\\
0&1&0\\
-s_{13}&0&c_{13}\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&c_{23}&-s_{23}\\
0&s_{23}&c_{23}\end{pmatrix},

(22)

where
c_{ij}=\sqrt{1-s_{ij}^{2}}
and
s_{ij}\in[0,1]
.
The usual choice is
R=\textbf{1}_{3}
, corresponding to the expected mixing from seesaw mechanism. However, this mixing may be enhanced when the matrix
R
is allowed to vary. This enables us to generated several orders of magnitude larger mixing. To illustrate the expected enhancement by our parametrization, we performed a random scan over
s_{12},s_{13},s_{23}\in[0,1]
,
m_{4}\in[10,50]
keV and
m_{5}\in[1.5,5]
GeV and calculated the weight of electron flavour in sterile states,
U_{e}^{2}\equiv|U_{e4}|^{2}+|U_{e5}|^{2}+|U_{e6}|^{2}
. We repeated the scan also in the case where
s_{12}=s_{23}=0
. The result is in Fig.
3
.

3.3
Nonstandard interactions

Consider the tree-level active neutrino - charged fermion scattering process

\nu_{\ell}+f\rightarrow Z^{\prime*}\rightarrow\nu_{\ell}+f,\quad f=e,u,d.

These processes are manifestations of the super-weak force. For the purpose of analyzing neutrino scattering, the virtual
Z^{\prime}
can be integrated out as long as its mass is at least about 10 MeV, which is the case in our model. The resulting operators have the well-known form of neutral current nonstandard neutrino interactions (NSI). They are a set of effective nonrenormalizable dimension-6 operators of (V
-
A)(V
\pm
A) form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{NSI}}

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{2}G_{F}\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{f,C}(\overline{\nu_{\ell}}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{\ell})(\overline{f}\gamma_{\mu}P_{C}f).

(23)

Here
G_{F}
is the Fermi coupling,
\ell,\ell^{\prime}=e,\mu,\tau
,
C=L,R
, and
P_{C}
are the chiral projection operators. The NSI parameters
\varepsilon^{f,C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
are dimensionless and in general complex. In the super-weak model they are real:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{f,C}_{\ell\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{2}}{2M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\left(eC^{L}_{Z^{\prime}\nu\nu}\right)\left(eC^{X}_{Z^{\prime}ff}\right),

(24)

with chiral couplings given in Eq.
7
. We define
\varepsilon^{f}_{\ell\ell}=\varepsilon^{f,L}_{\ell\ell}+\varepsilon^{f,R}_{\ell\ell}
. The NSI in the super-weak model are flavour-universal:
\varepsilon^{f}_{ee}=\varepsilon^{f}_{\mu\mu}=\varepsilon^{f}_{\tau\tau}\equiv\varepsilon^{f}
. Summing over the contributions of NSI on neutrino propagation in matter and assuming neutrality of matter, the electron and proton contribution to NSI vanishes due to suitable U(1)
z
charge assignments. The resulting NSI is simply
[
5
]

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{m}=-\frac{v^{2}}{8M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\frac{N_{n}}{N_{e}}&\left(g_{y}^{\prime}\cos\theta_{Z}-\frac{g_{L}}{\cos\theta_{W}}\sin\theta_{Z}\right)\\
&\times\left((g_{y}^{\prime}-g_{z}^{\prime})\cos\theta_{Z}-\frac{g_{L}}{\cos\theta_{W}}\sin\theta_{Z}\right).\end{split}

(25)

The NSI effect is further suppressed in neutrino oscillation experiments due to active-sterile mixing, but unsuppressed in neutrino scattering expriments. The region defined by
|\varepsilon^{m}|<\mathcal{O}(0.1)
is consistent with experimental limits. This region corresponds to
|\theta_{Z}|<\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
and excludes low values of VEV of singlet scalar
\chi

[
5
]
.

4
Conclusions

We have demonstrated the experimental feasability of detecting the effects of the neutrino sector in the super-weak model. Neutrino masses are generated – after spontaneous symmetry breaking of an extra scalar field
s
– by type-I seesaw mechanism. We have checked that the one-loop correction to active neutrino masses induced by gauge, scalar and Goldstone bosons are tiny. The active-sterile mixing may be enhanced by several orders of magnitude compared to usual choice of
R=\textbf{1}_{3}
, allowing the near-future experiments to access a significant region in the parameter space of the model. The neutral current NSI effects may be large, which allows their detection via neutrino scattering experiments. Thus, the super-weak model may be probed independently from multiple different sectors.

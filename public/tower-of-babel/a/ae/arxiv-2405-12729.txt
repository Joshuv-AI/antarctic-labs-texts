Conditions for tractability of the weighted L p L_{p} -discrepancy and integration in non-homogeneous tensor product spaces
Erich Novak, Friedrich Pillichshammer

Abstract
We study tractability properties of the weighted L p L_{p} -discrepancy. The concept of weighted discrepancy was introduced by Sloan and Woźniakowski in 1998 in order to prove a weighted version of the Koksma-Hlawka inequality for the error of quasi-Monte Carlo integration rules. The weights have the aim to model the influence of different coordinates of integrands on the error. A discrepancy is said to be tractable if the information complexity, i.e., the minimal number N N of points such that the discrepancy is less than the initial discrepancy times an error threshold ε \varepsilon , does not grow exponentially fast with the dimension. In this case there are various notions of tractabilities used in order to classify the exact rate. For even integer parameters p p there are sufficient conditions on the weights available in literature, which guarantee the one or other notion of tractability. In the present paper we prove matching sufficient conditions (upper bounds) and neccessary conditions (lower bounds) for polynomial and weak tractability for all p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . The proofs of the lower bounds are based on a general result for the information complexity of integration with positive quadrature formulas for tensor product spaces. In order to demonstrate this lower bound we consider as a second application the integration of tensor products of polynomials of degree at most 2.



Keywords:
Weighted discrepancy, numerical integration, curse of dimensionality, tractability, quasi-Monte Carlo

Introduction and main result

For a set
\mathcal{P}

N

\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}({\boldsymbol{t}})=\frac{|\{k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\ :\ {\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\in[{\boldsymbol{0}},{\boldsymbol{t}})\}|}{N}-t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{d},

for
{\boldsymbol{t}}=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})
in
[0,1]^{d}
, where
[{\boldsymbol{0}},{\boldsymbol{t}})=[0,t_{1})\times[0,t_{2})\times\ldots\times[0,t_{d})
.

The classical
L_{p}
-star discrepancy of a point set is defined as the
L_{p}
-norm of the local discrepancy function. See, e.g.,
[
2
,
8
,
16
]
. It is well-known that this
L_{p}
-star discrepancy is intimately related to the worst-case error of quasi-Monte Carlo rules for numerical integration
via so-called Koksma-Hlawka type inequalities.
See, e.g.,
[
12
,
15
,
17
,
21
]
. In view of this important application in 1998 Sloan and Woźniakowski
[
23
]
introduced a new concept of discrepancy, the so-called
weighted

L_{p}
-discrepancy with the aim to model the influence of different coordinates of integrands on the error more sensitively.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive real numbers, so-called coordinate weights. For
d\in\mathbb{N}
let
[d]:=\{1,2,\ldots,d\}

For a parameter

L_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},N}(\mathcal{P}):=\left(\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{p/2}\int_{[0,1]^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}|\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}(({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}},1))|^{p}\,{\rm d}{\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{1/p},

(1)

where, for a vector
{\boldsymbol{t}}=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}
and for
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]
we write

for the projection of

\mathfrak{u}
, i.e.,
{\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}}=(t_{j})_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}
and
({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}},1)
is the
d
-dimensional vector
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}=(\widetilde{t}_{1},\widetilde{t}_{2},\ldots,\widetilde{t}_{d})
, where
\widetilde{t}_{j}=t_{j}

and
\widetilde{t}_{j}=1
, if

.

The weighted
L_{\infty}
-discrepancy can be introduced with the usual adaptions (see
[
23
]
), but is not considered in the present paper.

Nowadays, the weighted

-discrepancy is a well studied quantity in literature (see, for example,
[

,
13
,
14
,
21
,
23

. It is well-known that there exists a close relation to numerical integration. This issue will be discussed in Section

For

the

-th minimal weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy in dimension

is defined as

{\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d):=\min_{\mathcal{P}}L_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},N}(\mathcal{P}),

where the minimum is extended over all
N
-element point sets
\mathcal{P}
in
[0,1)^{d}
. This quantity is then compared with the initial discrepancy
{\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)
, which is the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy of the empty point set. It is well known and easy to check that for the initial weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy for
p\in[1,\infty)
we have

{\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{p+1}\right)^{1/p}.

(2)

See, e.g.,
[
23
]
for
p=2
and
[
14
]
for general finite
p
.

It is reasonable to ask for the minimal number
N
of nodes that is necessary in order to achieve that the
N
-th minimal weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is smaller than
\varepsilon
times
{\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)
for a threshold
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. This quantity is called the inverse of the
N
-th minimal weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy, which is, for
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, formally defined as

N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d):=\min\{N\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ {\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d)\leq\varepsilon\ {\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)\}.

In a more general context, see Section
2
, the inverse of the weighted discrepancy is known as “information complexity”.

The question is now how fast
N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)
increases, when
d\rightarrow\infty
and
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Such questions are typically studied in the field “Information Based Complexity”. For general information on this and related questions we refer to the trilogy
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. In this context, the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is said to be intractable, if
N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)
has the unfavourable property to grow at least exponentially fast in
d
and/or
\varepsilon^{-1}
for
d\rightarrow\infty
and
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. If
N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)
grows at least exponentially fast in
d
for a fixed
\varepsilon>0

C>1
such
that
N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)\geq C^{d}
for infinitely
many
d\in\mathbb{N}
, then the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is said to suffer from the curse of dimensionality.

If, on the other hand,
N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)
grows at most sub-exponentially fast in
d
and
\varepsilon^{-1}
for
d\rightarrow\infty
and
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, then the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is said to be tractable and there are various notions of tractability in order to classify the grow rate of
N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)
more accurately. We consider the three most important ones. The weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is said to be

•

weakly tractable, if

\lim_{d+\varepsilon^{-1}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)}{d+\varepsilon^{-1}}=0;

•

polynomially tractable, if there exist numbers
C,\sigma>0
and
\tau\geq 0
such that

N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)\leq Cd^{\tau}\varepsilon^{-\sigma}\quad\mbox{for all $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $d\in\mathbb{N}$;}

•

strongly polynomially tractable, if there exist numbers
C,\sigma>0
such that

N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)\leq C\varepsilon^{-\sigma}\quad\mbox{for all $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $d\in\mathbb{N}$.}

(3)

The infimum of all
\sigma>0
such that a bound of the form (
3
) holds is called the
\varepsilon
-exponent of strong polynomial tractability.

The problem is to characterize the weight sequences
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
with respect to intractability or tractability and, in the latter case, with respect to the specific notions of tractability of the
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
-weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy.

For the
L_{2}
-case, i.e.,
p=2
, the situation is well understood. Let
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of coordinate weights. It is shown in
[
23
]
that the
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
-weighted
L_{2}
-discrepancy is

•

polynomially tractable, if and only if
\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}<\infty
;

•

strongly polynomially tractable, if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}<\infty
. In this case, the
\varepsilon
-exponent of strong polynomial tractability is at most 2.

The notion of weak tractability was introduced only several years after the
publication of
[
23
]
. However, it follows easily from the results in
[
23
]
that the weighted
L_{2}
-discrepancy is weakly tractable, if and only if
\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}=0
.

For
p
different from 2 we only have partial results consisting of sufficient conditions for even integer
p

that for even integer

the weighted

-discrepancy is

•

polynomially tractable, if
\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}<\infty
;

•

strongly polynomially tractable, if
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}<\infty
. In this case, the
\varepsilon
-exponent of strong polynomial tractability is at most 2.

Again, it follows easily from the results in
[
14
]
that for even
p
the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is weakly tractable, if
\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}=0
.

So for every even integer
p
we have sufficient conditions for the common tractability notions. In this paper we prove necessary conditions for tractability of weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy. Like in

Theorem 1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of coordinate weights in
(0,1]
. A necessary condition for any notion of tractability of the
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
-weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{j}=0
. In particular, a necessary condition for

•

strong polynomial tractablity is

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}<\infty;

(4)

•

polynomial tractablity is

\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}<\infty;

(5)

•

weak tractability is

\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}=0.

(6)

This result will follow from a lower bound on the inverse of the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy, which in turn follows from a more general result about the integration problem in the weighted anchored Sobolev space with a
q
-norm that will be introduced and discussed in the following Section

5

Remark 1
.

It is worth to remark that the conditions for tractability are
much more relaxed in the case of weighted
L_{\infty}
-discrepancy,
better known as weighted star-discrepancy. There are a lot of papers dealing with sufficient conditions for strong polynomial tractability, see
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
,
11
]
. The currently mildest condition on the weights

can be found in the paper

by Aistleitner who showed that if

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\exp(-c\gamma_{j}^{-2})<\infty

(7)

{\rm disc}_{\infty,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d)\leq\frac{C_{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}}{\sqrt{N}}\quad\mbox{for all $d,N\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Consequently, the weighted star-discrepancy for such weights is strongly polynomially tractable, with
\varepsilon
-exponent at most 2. A typical sequence
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
satisfying condition (

) is
\gamma_{j}=\widehat{c}/\sqrt{\log j}
for some
\widehat{c}>0
for

(put
\gamma_{1}=1
).
For such weights we can at best achieve weak tractability for the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy
for finite

.

The observed difference corresponds to the different behavior of classical
L_{p}
-star discrepancy for
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p=\infty
, which suffers from the curse of dimensionality if
p\in(1,\infty)
, see
[
18
]
, and which is polynomially tractable if
p=\infty
, see
[
9
]
.

2
Relation to numerical integration

It is known that the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is related to multivariate integration (see, for example,
[
14
,
13
,
21
,
23
]
). We briefly summarize the most important facts needed for the present paper.

From now on let
p,q\geq 1
be Hölder conjugates, i.e.,
1/p+1/q=1
. Let
W_{q}^{1}([0,1])
be the space of absolutely continuous functions whose first derivatives belong to the space
L_{q}([0,1])
. For a generic weight
\gamma>0
equip this space with the norm

\|f\|_{1,\gamma,q}:=\left(|f(0)|^{q}+\frac{1}{\gamma^{q/2}}\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{q}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{1/q}

(8)

and let

F_{1,\gamma,q}:=\{f\in W_{q}^{1}([0,1])\ :\ \|f\|_{1,\gamma,q}<\infty\}.

For dimension
d>1
and a positive sequence of coordinate weights
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
consider the
d
-fold tensor product space

F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}=F_{1,\gamma_{1},q}\otimes F_{1,\gamma_{2},q}\otimes\ldots\otimes F_{1,\gamma_{d},q}.

This space can be described as the space of all functions from the
d
-fold tensor product space

W_{q}^{(1,1,\ldots,1)}([0,1]^{d})=W_{q}^{1}([0,1])\otimes W_{q}^{1}([0,1])\otimes\ldots\otimes W_{q}^{1}([0,1]),

which is the Sobolev space of functions on
[0,1]^{d}
that are once differentiable in each variable and whose first derivative
\partial^{d}f/\partial{\boldsymbol{x}}
has finite
L_{q}
-norm, where
\partial{\boldsymbol{x}}=\partial x_{1}\partial x_{2}\ldots\partial x_{d}
, and which is equipped with the tensor-product norm

\|f\|_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}=\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]}\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{q/2}}\int_{[0,1]^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\left|\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}f_{\mathfrak{u}}(({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}},0))\right|^{q}\,{\rm d}{\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{1/q},

(9)

where
({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}},0)
is defined analogously to
({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}},1)
with
1
replaced by
0
.

Consider multivariate integration

I_{d}(f):=\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})\,{\rm d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\quad\mbox{for $f\in F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}$}.

We approximate the integrals
I_{d}(f)
by linear algorithms of the form

A_{d,N}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}),

(10)

where
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{2},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N}
are in
[0,1)^{d}
and
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}
are real weights that we call integration weights. If
a_{1}=a_{2}=\ldots=a_{N}=1/N
, then the linear algorithm (
10
) is a so-called quasi-Monte Carlo algorithm, and we denote this by
A_{d,N}^{{\rm QMC}}
.

The worst-case error of an algorithm (
10
) is defined by

e(F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q},A_{d,N})=\sup_{f\in F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}\atop\|f\|_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}\leq 1}\left|I_{d}(f)-A_{d,N}(f)\right|.

(11)

For a quasi-Monte Carlo algorithm
A_{d,N}^{{\rm QMC}}
it is known that

e(F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q},A_{d,N}^{{\rm QMC}})=L_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},N}(\overline{\mathcal{P}}),

where
L_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},N}(\overline{\mathcal{P}})
is the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy of the point set

\overline{\mathcal{P}}=\{\boldsymbol{1}-{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\ :\ k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\},

(12)

where
\boldsymbol{1}-{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
is defined as the component-wise difference of the vector containing only ones and
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
, see, e.g.,
[
14
, Section 1]
,
[
23
, Section 3]
and
[
21
, Section 9.5.1]
. We mention that we have chosen to anchor the norms (
8
) and (
9
), respectively, in
0
whereas in
[
14
]
and
[
23
]
the anchor 1 is used. This change is not a big deal and results in the appearence of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
in the above results rather than directly the point set
\mathcal{P}
.

For general linear algorithms (
10
) the worst-case error is the so-called generalized weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy. Here, for points
\mathcal{P}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{2},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N}\}
and corresponding coefficients
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}\}
the discrepancy function is

\overline{\Delta}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{t}})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}{\bf 1}_{[\boldsymbol{0},{\boldsymbol{t}})}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k})-t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{d}

for
{\boldsymbol{t}}=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})
in
[0,1]^{d}
and, for
p\in[1,\infty)
, the generalized
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
-weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy is

\overline{L}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},N}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{A}):=\left(\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{p/2}\int_{[0,1]^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}|\overline{\Delta}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{A}}(({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}},1))|^{p}\,{\rm d}{\boldsymbol{t}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{1/p}.

If
a_{1}=a_{2}=\ldots=a_{N}=1/N
, then we are back to the classical definition of weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy in Section
1
.

Now a natural extension of the QMC-setting to arbitrary linear algorithms yields

e(F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q},A_{d,N})=\overline{L}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},N}(\overline{\mathcal{P}},\mathcal{A}),

where
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
is like in (
12
) and
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}\}
consists of exactly the coefficients from the given linear algorithm (
10
). See
[
21
, Section 9.5.1]
for the case
p=2
.

From this point of view we now study the more general problem of numerical integration in the weighted space
F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}
rather than the
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
-weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy (which corresponds to quasi-Monte Carlo algorithms – although with suitably “reflected” points). We consider linear algorithms where we restrict ourselves to non-negative weights
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
and hence QMC-algorithms
are included in the present setting.

We define the
N
-th minimal worst-case error as

e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d):=\min_{A_{d,N}}|e(F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q},A_{d,N})|,

where the minimum is extended over all linear algorithms of the form (
10
) based on
N
function evaluations along points
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{2},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N}
from
[0,1)^{d}
and with non-negative weights
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\geq 0
. Note that for all
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d)\leq{\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d).

(13)

The initial error is

e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)=\sup_{f\in F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}\atop\|f\|_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}\leq 1}\left|I_{d}(f)\right|.

We call a function
f\in F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}
a worst-case function, if
I_{d}(f/\|f\|_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q})=e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let
q\in(1,\infty]
and
p\in[1,\infty)
with
1/p+1/q=1
. Then we have

e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{j}}{p+1}\right)^{1/p}

and the worst-case function in
F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}
is given by
h_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=h_{1,\gamma_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots h_{1,\gamma_{d}}(x_{d})
for
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}
, where
h_{1,\gamma}(x)=1+\gamma\frac{1-(1-x)^{p}}{p}
. Furthermore, we have

\int_{0}^{1}h_{1,\gamma}(t)\,{\rm d}t=1+\frac{\gamma^{p/2}}{p+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|h_{1,\gamma}\|_{1,\gamma,q}=\left(1+\frac{\gamma^{p/2}}{p+1}\right)^{1/q}.

(14)

Proof.

Since we are dealing with tensor products of one-dimensional spaces it suffices to prove the result for
d=1
. Let
\gamma>0
be a generic weight. For
f\in F_{1,\gamma,q}
we have

\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{1}\left(f(0)+\int_{0}^{x}f^{\prime}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)\,{\rm d}x=f(0)+\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(t)g(t)\,{\rm d}t,

where
g(t)=1-t
. Applying Hölder’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,{\rm d}x\right|\leq

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\|f\|_{1,q}\|g\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\frac{1}{\gamma^{1/2}}\|f\|_{1,q}\gamma^{1/2}\|g\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1,\gamma,q}\left(1+\gamma^{p/2}\|g\|_{L_{p}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Hence

\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(x)}{\|f\|_{1,\gamma,q}}\,{\rm d}x\right|\leq\left(1+\gamma^{p/2}\|g\|_{L_{p}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}=\left(1+\frac{\gamma^{p/2}}{p+1}\right)^{1/p}.

(15)

Now consider
h_{1,\gamma}(x)=1+\gamma\frac{1-(1-x)^{p}}{p}
. It is elementary to check that (
14
) holds true. Hence, with
f(x)=h_{1,\gamma}(x)
we obtain equality in (
15
). Thus,

e_{q,\gamma}(0,1)=\left(1+\frac{\gamma^{p/2}}{p+1}\right)^{1/p}

and
h_{1,\gamma}
is a worst-case function.
∎

Note that for all Hölder conjugates
q\in(1,\infty]
and
p\in[1,\infty)
and for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
we have

e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)={\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d).

Now we define the information complexity as the minimal number of function evaluations necessary in order to reduce the initial error by a factor of
\varepsilon
.
For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
put

N^{{\rm int}}_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(\varepsilon,d):=\min\{N\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d)\leq\varepsilon\ e_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)\}.

We stress that
N^{{\rm int}}_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(\varepsilon,d)

is a kind of restricted complexity since we only allow positive quadrature formulas.

From (
13
), (
2
) and Lemma
3
it follows that for all Hölder conjugates
q\in(1,\infty]
and
p\in[1,\infty)
and for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)^{d}
we have

N^{{\rm int}}_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(\varepsilon,d)\leq N^{{\rm disc}}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(\varepsilon,d).

Hence, Theorem
1
follows from the following more general results.

Theorem 4
.

For every
q
in
(1,\infty)
put

\tau_{p}:=\frac{2p-(p+1)(1+2^{p/(p+1)}-2^{1/(p+1)})}{4p^{2}+6p+(p+1)(1+2^{p/(p+1)}-2^{1/(p+1)})},

(16)

where
p
is the Hölder conjugate of
q
. Then
\tau_{p}>0
and for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
we have

N^{{\rm disc}}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(\varepsilon,d)\geq N_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm int}}(\varepsilon,d)\geq(1-2\varepsilon)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\tau_{p}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\right)^{1/q}.

The proof of Theorem
4
will be given in Section
4
. It will follow from a general lower bound on the information complexity of the integration problem in (not necessarily homogeneous) linear tensor products of spaces of univariate functions for positive linear rules. This general result will be explained in Section
3
.

The notions of weak, polynomial and strong polynomial tractability for integration in
F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}

are defined in the same way as for the weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy
in Section
1
with the inverse discrepancy

N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)
replaced by the
information complexity
N^{{\rm int}}_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(\varepsilon,d)
.
We obtain the following tractability conditions for the integration problem
with positive quadrature formulas from Theorem
4
.
The proof of Corollary
5
will be given in Section
4
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and let
p
denote the Hölder conjugate of
q
.
For coordinate weights

{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in
(0,1]
we have:

•

A necessary condition for any notion of tractability for integration in

F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}
for positive (integration) weights is
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{j}=0
.

•

A necessary condition for
strong polynomial tractablity is

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}<\infty.

(17)

If
q
of the form

, then this condition
is also sufficient.

•

For every

.

.

3
A general lower bound

In this section we provide a general lower bound on the information complexity of the integration problem in (not necessarily homogeneous) tensor products of spaces of univariate functions. This general result will be used to prove Theorem
4
(and thus Theorem
1
). In Section
5
we will provide another application of this powerful result.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
let
(F^{(j)},\|\cdot\|^{(j)})
be normed spaces of univariate integrable functions over the Borel measurable domain
D^{(j)}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and let, for
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
(F_{d},\|\cdot\|_{d})
be the
d
-fold tensor product space

F_{d}:=F^{(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes F^{(d)},

which consists of functions over the domain
D_{d}:=D^{(1)}\times\ldots\times D^{(d)}
, equipped with a crossnorm
\|\cdot\|_{d}
, i.e., for elementary tensors of the form
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=f_{1}(x_{1})\cdots f_{d}(x_{d})
with
f_{j}:D^{(j)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for
j\in[d]
, we have
\|f\|_{d}=\|f_{1}\|^{(1)}\cdots\|f_{d}\|^{(d)}
.

Consider multivariate integration

I_{d}(f):=\int_{D_{d}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})\,{\rm d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\quad\mbox{for $f\in F_{d}$}.

The so-called initial error of this integration problem is

e(0,d)=\|I_{d}\|=\sup_{f\in F_{d}\atop\|f\|_{d}\leq 1}\left|I_{d}(f)\right|.

(20)

We assume that for every
j\in[d]
there exists a so-called worst-case function
h^{(j)}
in
F^{(j)}
, which is a function that satisfies
I_{1}(h^{(j)})=\|h^{(j)}\|^{(j)}e_{j}(0,1)
,
where
e_{j}(0,1)
is the initial error for univariate integration in
F^{(j)}
.
Furthermore, we also assume that the tensor product
h_{d}({\boldsymbol{x}}):=h^{(1)}(x_{1})\cdots h^{(d)}(x_{d})
is a worst-case function for
d>1
and hence
e(0,d)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}e_{j}(0,1)
is the initial error for integration in
F_{d}
.

We approximate integrals by linear algorithms of the form

A_{d,N}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}),

(21)

where
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{2},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N}
are in
D_{d}
and
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}
are non-negative integration weights
1
1

1

We confess that non-negativity of integration weights constitutes a certain restriction which is in general not legitimated by theory, but which is often favored by practitioners
because of the stability of positive quadrature formulas.
.
The worst-case error of an algorithm is defined by

e(F_{d},A_{d,N})=\sup_{f\in F_{d}\atop\|f\|_{d}\leq 1}\left|I_{d}(f)-A_{d,N}(f)\right|.

We define the
N
-th minimal worst-case error for positive linear rules as

e(N,d):=\min_{A_{d,N}}e(F_{d},A_{d,N}),

where the minimum is extended over all linear algorithms of the form (
21
) based on
N
function evaluations along points
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{2},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N}
from
D_{d}
and with non-negative integration weights
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
define

N(\varepsilon,d)=\min\{N\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ e(N,d)\leq\varepsilon\,e(0,d)\}.

We stress that
N(\varepsilon,d)
is a kind of restricted complexity since we only allow positive quadrature formulas.

The following result can be seen as the non-homogeneous extension of
[
18
, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem 6
.

For every
j\in[d]
: assume that there exists a worst-case function
h^{(j)}
in
F^{(j)}
and assume that
h_{d}({\boldsymbol{x}})=h^{(1)}(x_{1})\cdots h^{(d)}(x_{d})
for
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in D_{d}
is a worst-case function in
F_{d}
.

Assume further that for every
y\in D^{(j)}
there exists a function
s_{y}^{(j)}\in F^{(j)}
such that
s_{y}^{(j)}\geq 0
and
s_{y}^{(j)}(y)=h^{(j)}(y)
. Put

\alpha_{j}:=\max_{y\in D^{(j)}}\|s_{y}^{(j)}\|^{(j)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta_{j}:=\max_{y\in D^{(j)}}\int_{D^{(j)}}s_{y}^{(j)}(x)\,{\rm d}x,

(22)

and

{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j},\quad{\boldsymbol{\beta}}:=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\beta_{j},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widetilde{C}_{d}:=\min\left(\frac{\|h_{d}\|_{d}}{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}},\frac{I_{d}(h_{d})}{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\right).

(23)

Then

N(\varepsilon,d)\geq\widetilde{C}_{d}\,(1-2\varepsilon)\quad\mbox{for all $\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)$ and $d\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Remark 2
.

When applying Theorem
6
, strive for functions
s_{y}^{(j)}
such that
\alpha_{j}<\|h^{(j)}\|^{(j)}
and
\beta_{j}<\int_{D^{(j)}}h^{(j)}(x)\,{\rm d}x
, which yields that

\min\left(\frac{\|h^{(j)}\|^{(j)}}{\alpha_{j}},\frac{I_{1}(h^{(j)})}{\beta_{j}}\right)>1\quad\mbox{at least for many $j\in[d]$.}

The smaller
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
are, the larger is the lower bound
\widetilde{C}_{d}
.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Consider an algorithm
A_{d,N}
of the form (
21
) based on nodes
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N}
in
D_{d}
and with non-negative integration weights
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\geq 0
. Let
x_{i,j}
be the
j
-th coordinate of the point
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
j\in[d]
. For
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
we define functions

P_{i}({\boldsymbol{x}})=s_{x_{i,1}}^{(1)}(x_{1})s_{x_{i,2}}^{(2)}(x_{2})\cdots s_{x_{i,d}}^{(d)}(x_{d}),\quad\mbox{for ${\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in D_{d}$.}

In order to estimate the error of
A_{d,N}
we consider the two functions
h_{d}
and
f^{*}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{i}
. Since
A_{d,N}
is a positive rule we have
A_{d,N}(f^{*})\geq A_{d,N}(h_{d})
. Then we have the error estimate

e(F_{d},A_{d,N})\geq\frac{\left(I_{d}(h_{d})-I_{d}(f^{*})\right)_{+}}{2\max(\|h_{d}\|_{d},\|f^{*}\|_{d})},

(24)

which is trivially true if
I_{d}(h_{d})\leq I_{d}(f^{*})
and which is easily shown if
I_{d}(h_{d})>I_{d}(f^{*})
, because then

\displaystyle\left(I_{d}(h_{d})-I_{d}(f^{*})\right)_{+}\leq

\displaystyle I_{d}(h_{d})-A_{d,N}(h_{d})+A_{d,N}(f^{*})-I_{d}(f^{*})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\max(\|h_{d}\|_{d},\|f^{*}\|_{d})\,e(F_{d},A_{d,N}).

According to (
22
) we have
\|f^{*}\|_{d}\leq N{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and
I_{d}(f^{*})\leq N{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
. Inserting into (
24
) yields

e(N,d)\geq e(F_{d},A_{d,N})\geq\frac{(I_{d}(h_{d})-N{\boldsymbol{\beta}})_{+}}{2\max(\|h_{d}\|_{d},N{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}=e(0,d)\frac{(1-N{\boldsymbol{\beta}}/I_{d}(h_{d}))_{+}}{2\max(1,N{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}/\|h_{d}\|_{d})}.

(25)

Now let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and assume that
e(N,d)\leq\varepsilon\,e(0,d)
. This implies

2\,\varepsilon\max\left(1,N\frac{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}{\|h_{d}\|_{d}}\right)\geq\left(1-N\frac{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{I_{d}(h_{d})}\right)_{+}.

(26)

If
N\leq\widetilde{C}_{d}
, where
\widetilde{C}_{d}
is from (
23
), then we obtain

N\geq\frac{I_{d}(h_{d})}{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\,(1-2\varepsilon)\geq\widetilde{C}_{d}\,(1-2\varepsilon).

If
N>\widetilde{C}_{d}
, then we trivially have
N\geq\widetilde{C}_{d}\,(1-2\varepsilon)
.
This yields

N(\varepsilon,d)\geq\widetilde{C}_{d}\,(1-2\varepsilon),

and we are done.
∎

4
The proofs of Theorem
4
and Corollaries
5
and
2

In this section we present the proofs of Theorem
4
(and hence of Theorem
1
) and subsequently that of Corollary
5
and Corollary
2
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We apply Theorem
6
to the specific setting in Section
2
. Let
(F^{(j)},\|\cdot\|^{(j)})=(F_{1,\gamma_{j},q},\|\cdot\|_{1,\gamma_{j},q})
and
(F_{d},\|\cdot\|_{d})=(F_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q},\|\cdot\|_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q})
. According to Lemma
3
a worst-case function in
F_{1,\gamma_{j},q}
is

h_{1,\gamma_{j}}(x)=1+\gamma_{j}\frac{1-(1-x)^{p}}{p}.

Let
a\in(0,1)
that will be specified later and define

s_{y}^{(j)}(x)=1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{p}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{1,1}(x)+h_{1,2,(0)}(x)&\mbox{if $y\leq a$,}\\
h_{1,1}(x)+h_{1,2,(1)}(x)&\mbox{if $y\geq a$,}\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle h_{1,1}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1-(1-a)^{p}}{a}\min(x,a),

\displaystyle h_{1,2,(0)}(x)=

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{[0,a]}(x)\left((1-(1-x)^{p})-\frac{x}{a}(1-(1-a)^{p})\right),

\displaystyle h_{1,2,(1)}(x)=

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{[a,1]}(x)\left((1-(1-x)^{p})-(1-(1-a)^{p})\right).

Observe that
h_{1,1}(x)+h_{1,2,(0)}(x)+h_{1,2,(1)}(x)=1-(1-x)^{p}
and that
h_{1,2,(0)}
and
h_{1,2,(1)}
have a disjoint support, namely
[0,a)
and
(a,1]
, respectively.

Obviously,
s_{y}^{(j)}
is non-negative, belongs to
F_{1,\gamma_{j},q}
and satisfies
s_{y}^{(j)}(y)=h^{(j)}(y)
for all
y\in[0,1]
. Thus we can apply Theorem
6
, which yields, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,

N_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm int}}(N,d)\geq\widetilde{C}_{d}\,(1-2\varepsilon),

with
\widetilde{C}_{d}
like in (
23
). It remains to estimate
\widetilde{C}_{d}
.

To begin with, it is elementary to compute

\left\|1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{p}(h_{1,1}+h_{1,2,(0)})\right\|_{1,\gamma_{j},q}^{q}=1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\frac{1-(1-a)^{p+1}}{p+1}

and

\left\|1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{p}(h_{1,1}+h_{1,2,(0)})\right\|_{1,\gamma_{j},q}^{q}=1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\frac{(1-a)^{p+1}}{p+1}.

Thus, both quantities coincide if we choose

a=1-\frac{1}{2^{1/(p+1)}}

(27)

and then

\alpha_{j}=\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{2(p+1)}\right)^{1/q}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{2(p+1)}\right)^{1/q}.

Hence

\frac{\|h_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}\|_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},q}}{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{p+1}}{1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{2(p+1)}}\right)^{1/q}\geq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{2p+3}\right)^{1/q},

where in the last step we used that
\gamma_{j}\leq 1
.

Furthermore,

\beta_{j}=1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{p}\max\left(\int_{0}^{1}h_{1,1}(x)+h_{1,2,(0)}(x)\,{\rm d}x,\int_{0}^{1}h_{1,1}(x)+h_{1,2,(1)}(x)\,{\rm d}x\right).

For
a
like in (
27
) we have

\int_{0}^{1}h_{1,1}(x)+h_{1,2,(0)}(x)\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{2}\frac{p}{p+1}

and

\int_{0}^{1}h_{1,1}(x)+h_{1,2,(1)}(x)\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{2}\frac{p}{p+1}+\frac{1+2^{p/(p+1)}-2^{1/(p+1)}}{4}.

Hence

\beta_{j}=1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{1/p}}{2(p+1)}\left(1+\frac{p+1}{2p}(1+2^{p/(p+1)}-2^{1/(p+1)})\right).

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{I_{d}(h_{d,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}})}{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{p+1}}{1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{1/p}}{2(p+1)}\left(1+\frac{p+1}{2p}(1+2^{p/(p+1)}-2^{1/(p+1)})\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\tau_{p}\right),

where we again used that
\gamma_{j}\leq 1
and where
\tau_{p}
is defined in (
16
). Since
\frac{p+1}{2p}(1+2^{p/(p+1)}-2^{1/(p+1)})<1
it is clear that
\tau_{p}>0
(note that
p\in(1,\infty)
).

In any case, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{q}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\tau_{p}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\right)^{1/q}

and this finishes the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
5
.

We use standard arguments. Assume first that there exists a real
\Gamma>0
such that
\gamma_{j}\geq\Gamma
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then it follows from Theorem
4
that

N_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm int}}(\varepsilon,d)\geq(1-2\varepsilon)\left(1+\tau_{p}\Gamma^{p/2}\right)^{d/q}.

This means that the integration problem suffers from the curse of dimensionality. Thus,
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{j}=0
is a necessary condition for tractability.

Now suppose that
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}=\infty
. Since
\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+\tau_{p}\gamma_{j}^{p/2})\geq 1+\tau_{p}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}
it follows that for
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
we have

\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}N_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm int}}(\varepsilon,d)=\infty

and hence we cannot have strong polynomial tractability. Thus, (
17
) is a necessary condition for strong polynomial tractability.

Next suppose that
\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}=\infty
. Note that
0<\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\tau_{p}\leq\tau_{p}<1
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\tfrac{1}{x}\log(1+x)=1
. Hence, there exists a positive real
\ell
such that
\log(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\tau_{p})>\ell\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\tau_{p}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. This gives

\log\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\tau_{p}\right)^{1/q}\geq\frac{\ell\tau_{p}}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}

and hence

\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\tau_{p}\right)^{1/q}\geq\exp\left(\frac{\ell\tau_{p}}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\right)=d^{\frac{\ell\tau_{p}}{q}\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}.

Hence,
N_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm int}}(\varepsilon,d)
goes to infinity faster than any power of
d
and we cannot have polynomial tractability. This means that (
18
) is a necessary condition for polynomial tractability.

Finally, similarly to the above it follows from Theorem
4
that

\log N_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm int}}(\varepsilon,d)\geq\frac{\tau_{p}\ell}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}

and hence

\frac{\log N_{q,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm int}}(\varepsilon,d)}{d+\varepsilon^{-1}}\geq\frac{\tau_{p}\ell}{q}\frac{d}{d+\varepsilon^{-1}}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}.

Thus, weak tractability implies (
19
).

The sufficient conditions follow directly from
Corollary
2
, which will be proven immediately afterwards.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2
.

The sufficiency of (
4
) for strong polynomial tractability for
even
integers
p
was shown in
[
14
]
. It remains to prove the sufficient conditions for polynomial- and weak tractablilty, respectively, for general
p\in[1,\infty)
. To this end, we first show a general upper bound on the inverse of the
N
-th minimal weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. According to the definition in (
1
) we have

L_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},N}(\mathcal{P})\leq\left(\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{p/2}\left(D_{N}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{u}})\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p},

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{u}}
is the set of projections of the points from
\mathcal{P}
to the coordinate directions which belong to
\mathfrak{u}
, and
D_{N}^{\ast}
is the usual star-discrepancy of a point set, i.e.,
D_{N}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P})=\|\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}\|_{L_{\infty}}
. It is shown in
[
10
, Proof of Theorem 1]
that there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that for every
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an
N
-point set
\mathcal{P}
in
[0,1)^{d}
such that

D_{N}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{u}})\leq C\max(1,\sqrt{\log d})\,\sqrt{\frac{|\mathfrak{u}|}{N}}\quad\mbox{for all non-empty $\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]$.}

Therefore, and since
|\mathfrak{u}|\leq 2^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
for
\mathfrak{u}\not=\emptyset
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(N,d)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\frac{\max(1,\sqrt{\log d})}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{p/2}2^{|\mathfrak{u}|p/2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\frac{\max(1,\sqrt{\log d})}{\sqrt{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}2^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}.

This estimate together with the observation that
{\rm disc}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}(0,d)>1
implies that

N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\,C^{2}\,\max(1,\log d)\,\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}2^{p/2}\right)^{2/p}.

(28)

This is the promised estimate of the inverse of the
N
-th minimal weighted
L_{p}
-discrepancy. We employ (
28
) in order to prove sufficiency of the proposed conditions on the weights.

First, assume that (
5
) holds. Then for every
\delta>0
there exists a
d_{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
d\geq d_{\delta}
we have

\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}<A+\delta.

Then, for
d\geq d_{\delta}
, we get

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}2^{p/2}\right)\leq\exp\left(2^{p/2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}\right)=d^{2^{p/2}(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2})/\log d}\leq d^{2^{p/2}(A+\delta)}.

Therefore, there exists a positive real
c_{\delta}
such that we have

\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{p/2}2^{p/2}\right)\leq c_{\delta}\,d^{2^{p/2}(A+\delta)}\quad\mbox{for all $d\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Inserting in (
28
) we obtain

N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)\leq C^{2}\,c_{\delta}^{2/p}\,\max(1,\log d)\,d^{2^{1+p/2}(A+\delta)/p}\,\varepsilon^{-2}

and hence we have polynomial tractability.

Finally, assume that (
6
) holds. For
d\geq 2
we have

\frac{N_{p,{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}^{{\rm disc}}(\varepsilon,d)}{d+\varepsilon^{-1}}\leq\frac{2\log\varepsilon^{-1}+2\log C+\log\log d+\frac{2}{p}2^{p/2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{p/2}}{d+\varepsilon^{-1}},

where the right hand side tends to 0 whenever
d+\varepsilon^{-1}\rightarrow\infty
, according to (
6
). Hence we have weak tractability.
∎

Unfortunately, because of the annoying
\log d
-term, we cannot use (
28
) in order to prove sufficient conditions for strong polynomial tractability. Using (
28
), then, for
p\in[1,\infty)
, condition (
4
) only implies polynomial tractability, however, with
\varepsilon
-exponent zero
2
2

2

More precisely, we have generalized
(T,\Omega)
-tractability with
T(\varepsilon^{-1},d)=\varepsilon^{-1}(1+\log d)
and
\Omega=[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}
. See
[
20
, Section 4.4.3]
for the notion of generalized tractability.
.

5
A further application of Theorem
6
: integration of polynomials

We give another application in order to demonstrate the power of Theorem
6
. Here we study integration of
C^{\infty}
-functions, in particular, we restrict to real polynomials of degree at most two over the unit interval.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive coordinate weights and let
q\in(1,\infty)
. Sticking to the notation in Section
3
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
consider
F^{(j)}=P_{2}
as the space of all real polynomials of degree at most 2 as functions over
[0,1]
with finite “weighted”
q
-norm

\|f\|^{(j)}=\|f\|_{q,\gamma_{j}}:=\left(\|f\|_{L_{q}}^{q}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}\left(\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{q}}^{q}+\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{q}}^{q}\right)\right)^{1/q}.

Consider integration
I_{d}(f)=\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})\,{\rm d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
for
f
in the tensor product space
F_{d}
. This problem is studied in
[
21
, Section 10.5.4]
(for the Hilbert-space case
q=2
) and in
[
19
]
(for general
q\in(1,\infty)
), but in both instances only for the unweighted setting (i.e.,
\gamma_{j}\equiv 1
). It is shown that then
the problem suffers from the curse of dimensionality. Hence it is no real restriction when we restrict ourselves to coordinate weights
\gamma_{j}\in(0,1]
in the following.

The initial error for the present problem is
e(0,d)=1
and the (univariate) worst-case function is
h^{(j)}(x)=1
for
x\in[0,1]
for every
j
. Obviously
\|h^{(j)}\|_{q,\gamma_{j}}=1
and
\int_{0}^{1}h^{(j)}(x)\,{\rm d}x=1
.

We can exploit Theorem
6
in order to show the following result.

Theorem 7
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive coordinate weights at most one and let
q\in(1,\infty)
. Consider integration over the unit-cube in the
d
-fold tensor product of spaces
(P_{2},\|\cdot\|_{q,\gamma_{j}})
. Put

\rho_{q}:=\frac{u_{q}}{12}-(2u_{q})^{q}\frac{q+2}{q+1},

(29)

with some positive number
u_{q}
that is strictly less than
\left(\frac{1}{12\cdot 2^{q}}\frac{q+1}{q+2}\right)^{1/(q-1)}
.

Then we have
\rho_{q}>0
and

N(\varepsilon,d)\geq(1-2\varepsilon)\,\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}\rho_{q}\right)^{1/q}\quad\mbox{for all $\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)$ and $d\in\mathbb{N}$}.

In particular, considering integration with positive rules, a necessary condition for

•

strong polynomial tractability is

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}<\infty;

•

polynomial tractability is

\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}<\infty;

•

weak tractability is

\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}=0.

Proof.

In order to apply Theorem
6
we define, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{y}^{(j)}(x):=1-c_{j}(x-y)^{2}
,
x\in[0,1]
, with some quantity
c_{j}\in(0,1/2)
that will be specified later. Then
s_{y}^{(j)}
is non-negative, belongs to
F^{(j)}
and, obviously,
s_{y}^{(j)}(y)=1=h^{(j)}(y)
for every
y\in[0,1]
. Thus, Theorem
6
implies

N(\varepsilon,d)\geq\widetilde{C}_{d}\,(1-2\varepsilon)\quad\mbox{for all $\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)$ and $d\in\mathbb{N}$},

where
\widetilde{C}_{d}=\min\left({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{-1},{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{-1}\right)
, with
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
as defined in (
23
). It remains to estimate these two quantities.

To begin with, it is easy to show that

I_{1}(s_{y}^{(j)})=1-c_{j}\left(y^{2}-y+\frac{1}{3}\right)

and hence
\beta_{j}=1-\frac{c_{j}}{12}
and
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-\frac{c_{j}}{12}\right)
.

In order to estimate
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
we need to estimate
\|s_{y}^{(j)}\|_{q,\gamma_{j}}
. Since
s_{y}^{(j)}(x)\in[0,1]
and
q>1
we have

\|s_{y}^{(j)}\|_{L_{q}}^{q}=\int_{0}^{1}|s_{y}^{(j)}(x)|^{q}\,{\rm d}x\leq I_{1}(s_{y}^{(j)})\leq\beta_{j}=1-\frac{c_{j}}{12}.

Furthermore, since
(s_{y}^{(j)}(x))^{\prime}=2c_{j}(y-x)
and
(s_{y}^{(j)}(x))^{\prime\prime}=-2c_{j}
we have

\|(s_{y}^{(j)})^{\prime}\|_{L_{q}}^{q}=\int_{0}^{1}|2c_{j}(y-x)|^{q}\,{\rm d}x=(2c_{j})^{q}\frac{y^{q+1}+(1-y)^{q+1}}{q+1}\leq\frac{(2c_{j})^{q}}{q+1}

and

\|(s_{y}^{(j)})^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{q}}^{q}=\int_{0}^{1}|-2c_{j}|^{q}\,{\rm d}x=(2c_{j})^{q}.

Hence

\|s_{y}^{(j)}\|_{q,\gamma_{j}}^{q}\leq 1-\frac{c_{j}}{12}+\frac{(2c_{j})^{q}}{\gamma_{j}}\frac{q+2}{q+1}.

For
q\in(1,\infty)
a necessary condition such that the latter is strictly less than 1, i.e., strictly less than
\|h^{(j)}\|_{q,\gamma_{j}}
, is

c_{j}<\left(\frac{1}{12\cdot 2^{q}}\frac{q+1}{q+2}\right)^{1/(q-1)}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}.

Now we fix the initially unspecified quantity
c_{j}
by setting
c_{j}:=u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
u_{q}
is a positive real that is strictly less than
\left(\frac{1}{12\cdot 2^{q}}\frac{q+1}{q+2}\right)^{1/(q-1)}
. Note that
c_{j}\in(0,1/2)
for
q\in(1,\infty)
, where we use that
\gamma_{j}\leq 1
. Then

\alpha_{j}\leq\left(1-\frac{u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}}{12}+\frac{(2u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)})^{q}}{\gamma_{j}}\frac{q+2}{q+1}\right)^{1/q}.

Note that also

\beta_{j}=1-\frac{u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}}{12}\leq\left(1-\frac{u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}}{12}+\frac{(2u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)})^{q}}{\gamma_{j}}\frac{q+2}{q+1}\right)^{1/q}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\left(\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}},\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\beta_{j}}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-\frac{u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}}{12}+\frac{(2u_{q}\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)})^{q}}{\gamma_{j}}\frac{q+2}{q+1}\right)^{-1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}\rho_{q}\right)^{-1/q}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{1/(q-1)}\rho_{q}\right)^{1/q},

where
\rho_{q}
is defined in (
29
). Note that
\rho_{q}>0
, because
u_{q}
is strictly less than
\left(\frac{1}{12\cdot 2^{q}}\frac{q+1}{q+2}\right)^{1/(q-1)}
.

The necessary conditions for the three notions of tractability follow from (
30
) in the same way like in the proof of Corollary
5
.
∎

We leave it as an open problem to show that the necessary conditions of Theorem
7
are also sufficient.

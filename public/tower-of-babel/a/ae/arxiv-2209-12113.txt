Longest and Shortest Factorizations in Embedding Dimension Three
Baian Liu, JiaYan Yap

Abstract
For a numerical monoid ⟨ n 1 , … , n k ⟩ \langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle minimally generated by n 1 , … , n k ∈ ℕ n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}} with n 1 < ⋯ < n k n_{1}<\cdots<n_{k} , the longest and shortest factorization lengths of an element x x , denoted as L ⁡ ( x ) L(x) and ℓ ⁡ ( x ) \ell(x) , respectively, follow the identities L ⁡ ( x + n 1 ) = L ⁡ ( x ) + 1 L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1 and ℓ ⁡ ( x + n k ) = ℓ ⁡ ( x ) + 1 \ell(x+n_{k})=\ell(x)+1 for sufficiently large elements x x . We characterize when these identities hold for all elements of numerical monoids of embedding dimension three.



1
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of natural numbers including 0. We will study factorizations on
numerical monoids
, which are cofinite submonoids of
{\mathbb{N}}
. Numerical monoids can be presented using
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gcd(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})=1
as generators:

\langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle=\{a_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}n_{k}\mid a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.

The
embedding dimension
of a numerical monoid
M
is the minimum
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that there exists
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that
M=\langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle
.

Each element of
\langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle
can be obtained using different
{\mathbb{N}}
-linear combinations of the generators
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
. We collect this information in a set and study certain invariants on it.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle
be a numerical monoid minimally generated by
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n_{1}<\cdots<n_{k}
. Take an element
x\in\langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle
. We define the
set of factorizations of
x
to be

\mathsf{Z}(x)=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}\mid a_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}n_{k}=x\}.

For each factorization
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathsf{Z}(x)
, we define its
length
to be

\absolutevalue{(a_1, \dots, a_k)}=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}.

The
set of factorization lengths
for
x
are then defined to be

\mathcal{L}(x)=\{\absolutevalue{(a_1, \dots, a_k)}\mid(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathsf{Z}(x)\}.

Then the
maximum factorization length of
x

and the
minimum factorization length of
x

is defined as

L(x)=\max\mathcal{L}(x)\text{\quad and \quad}\ell(x)=\min\mathcal{L}(x),

respectively.

The invariants
L(x)
and
\ell(x)
satisfy a linear-like formula given that
x
is a sufficiently large element of the numerical monoid, as provided by the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
1
, Theorems 4.2 and 4.3]

Let
\langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle
be a numerical monoid with
n_{1}<\cdots<n_{k}
. The following statements hold.

1.

If
x>(n_{1}-1)n_{k}-n_{1}
, then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
.

2.

If
x>(n_{k}-1)n_{k-1}-n_{k}
, then
\ell(x+n_{k})=\ell(x)+1
.

These formulas hold for sufficiently large elements, but for small elements, the formulas can still hold in certain numerical monoids. We want to find some conditions for a numerical monoid
M
under which the equality
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
holds for all
x\in M
. Similarly, we want to see when
\ell(x+n_{k})=\ell(x)+1
holds for all
x\in M
. This question was posed in
[
2
, p. 334]
.

If we first restrict ourselves to a numerical monoid
M
of embedding dimension two, we find that the equalities
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
and
\ell(x+n_{k})=\ell(x)+1
always hold for all
x\in M

[
2
, p. 334]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension two with
n_{1}<n_{2}
. Then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
and
\ell(x+n_{2})=\ell(x)+1
for all
x\in M
.

For this reason, we restrict ourselves to numerical monoids of embedding dimension three.

2
Betti Elements

An important invariant of numerical monoids is the set of Betti elements. We use this set to better describe numerical monoids of embedding dimension three.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a numerical monoid. Let
x\in M
be an element and we define
\nabla_{x}
as a graph with vertices
\mathsf{Z}(x)
and two distinct vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Z}(x)
are connected by an edge if and only if
v\cdot v^{\prime}\neq 0
. We call
x
a
Betti element
if
\nabla_{x}
is not connected, and we will use
\textup{Betti}(M)
to denote the set of Betti elements of
M
.

For a numerical monoid
\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
of embedding dimension three with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
, we can define for each
i=1,2,3
the numbers

c_{i}=\min\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}\mid\text{there exists $(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathsf{Z}(kn_{i})$ such that $a_{i}=0$}\}.

Using these numbers
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
, we can describe the set of Betti elements of a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
3
, p. 582]

For a numerical monoid
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
of embedding dimension three, we have

\textup{Betti}(M)=\{c_{1}n_{1},c_{2}n_{2},c_{3}n_{3}\}.

The elements in the set above are not necessarily distinct. The number of Betti elements of a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three can be determined by the form of the generators.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
3
, p. 583]

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three. Then
\absolutevalue{\textup{Betti}(M)}\leq 2
if and only if

\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\}=\{am_{1},am_{2},bm_{1}+cm_{2}\}

for some
a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
1<m_{1}<m_{2}
,
\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
,
a\geq 2
,
b+c\geq 2
, and
\gcd(a,bm_{1}+cm_{2})=1
.

Remark 2.4
.

In
[
3
, p. 583]
, the case of one Betti element and two Betti elements are separated. The case of two Betti elements is listed as above. In the case when
\absolutevalue{\Betti(M)}=1
, we have
\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\}=\{s_{2}s_{3},s_{1}s_{3},s_{1}s_{2}\}
for some positive pariwise coprime integers
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
. This has the same form as above by setting
a=s_{3},m_{1}=s_{2},m_{2}=s_{1},b=s_{1},c=0
.

In particular, if
M
is a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three and the three minimal generators are pairwise coprime, then
\absolutevalue{\textup{Betti}(M)}=3
. However, the converse is not true in general.

Due to how
c_{i}
is defined, there exist
r_{12},r_{13},r_{21},r_{23},r_{31},r_{32}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}n_{1}=r_{12}n_{2}+r_{13}n_{3},

\displaystyle c_{2}n_{2}=r_{21}n_{1}+r_{23}n_{3},

\displaystyle c_{3}n_{3}=r_{31}n_{1}+r_{32}n_{2}.

In
[
4
, Lemma 1]
, it was shown that each
r_{ij}>0
if the generators are pairwise coprime. Moreover, the following lemma establishes a relationship between these coefficients.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
4
, Lemma 3]

For every
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
,

c_{i}=r_{ji}+r_{ki}

If the numerical monoid
\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
has
\gcd(n_{i},n_{j})=1
for
i\neq j
, then the coefficients
r_{ij}
are unique. The numerical monoid can also be recovered from these invariants.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
4
, Theorem 9]

Let
X
be the set consisting of all numerical monoids of embedding dimension three having the form
\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
such that
\gcd(n_{i},n_{j})=1
if
i\neq j
and
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
. Let
Y
be the set of
3\times 3
matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{12}&a_{13}\\
a_{21}&0&a_{23}\\
a_{31}&a_{32}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
a_{12},a_{13},a_{21},a_{23},a_{31},a_{32}\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
, and the numbers
A_{1}=a_{12}a_{13}+a_{12}a_{23}+a_{13}a_{32},A_{2}=a_{13}a_{21}+a_{21}a_{23}+a_{23}a_{31}
, and
A_{3}=a_{12}a_{31}+a_{21}a_{32}+a_{31}a_{32}
have the properties that
A_{1}<A_{2}<A_{3}
and
\gcd(A_{i},A_{j})=1
if
i\neq j
. There exists a bijection between
X
and
Y

given by the assignments

\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&r_{12}&r_{13}\\
r_{21}&0&r_{23}\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{12}&a_{13}\\
a_{21}&0&a_{23}\\
a_{31}&a_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\langle A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\rangle.

This bijection can be extended to all numerical monoids of embedding dimension three with three Betti elements. The proofs of
[
4
, Lemmas 2, 3, 4, and 5]
do not rely on the fact that the generators are pairwise coprime. However, they do rely on the result of
[
4
, Lemma 1]
, which was proven using the fact that the generators are pairwise coprime. We provide here the result of
[
4
, Lemma 1]
under the weaker assumption that there are three Betti elements.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three such that
\absolutevalue{\Betti(M)}=3
. Then
r_{12},r_{13},r_{21},r_{23},r_{31},r_{32}
are positive integers.

Proof.

We suppose for a contradiction that
r_{ij}=0
for some
i,j
. Then for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, we have

c_{i}n_{i}=r_{ik}n_{k}.

By the minimality of
c_{k}
, we obtain
r_{ik}\geq c_{k}
. Since
\absolutevalue{\Betti(M)}=3
, we cannot have
c_{i}n_{i}=c_{k}n_{k}
. Thus,
r_{ik}>c_{k}
. Subtracting
c_{k}n_{k}=r_{ki}n_{i}+r_{kj}n_{j}
from
c_{i}n_{i}=r_{ik}n_{k}
and rearranging, we get that

(c_{i}-r_{ki})n_{i}=(r_{ik}-c_{k})n_{k}+r_{kj}n_{j}.

Since
r_{ik}-c_{k}>0
, we have that
c_{i}-r_{ki}\geq c_{i}
by the minimality of
c_{i}
, implying that
r_{ki}=0
. Repeating this argument gives us

\displaystyle c_{i}n_{i}

\displaystyle=r_{ik}n_{k},

\displaystyle c_{k}n_{k}

\displaystyle=r_{kj}n_{j},

\displaystyle c_{j}n_{j}

\displaystyle=r_{ji}n_{i}.

However, the product of all three will yield
c_{i}c_{k}c_{j}=r_{ik}r_{kj}r_{ji}
, but this is impossible as
r_{ik}>c_{i}
and similarly
r_{kj}>c_{k}
and
r_{ji}>c_{j}
. Thus,
r_{ij}>0
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three such that
\absolutevalue{\Betti(M)}=3
. Then

\displaystyle n_{1}

\displaystyle=r_{12}r_{13}+r_{12}r_{23}+r_{13}r_{32}

\displaystyle n_{2}

\displaystyle=r_{13}r_{21}+r_{21}r_{23}+r_{23}r_{31}

\displaystyle n_{3}

\displaystyle=r_{12}r_{31}+r_{21}r_{32}+r_{31}r_{32}.

Proof.

This follows from the proofs of
[
4
, Lemmas 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5]
, noting that the assumption that the generators are pairwise coprime is not needed except to obtain the result of
[
4
, Lemma 1]
, which can be obtained under the assumption that
\absolutevalue{\Betti(M)}=3
by the previous lemma.
∎

This allows us to establish a bijection extending that of Theorem
2.6
. To state the extended bijection, we extend the definition of a 0-matrix found in
[
4
, p. 492]
.

Definition 2.9
.

A
0-matrix
is a
3\times 3
matrix of the form

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{12}&a_{13}\\
a_{21}&0&a_{23}\\
a_{31}&a_{32}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
a_{12},a_{13},a_{21},a_{23},a_{31},a_{32}\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
are such that
\langle A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\rangle
is a numerical monoid with embedding dimension three and three Betti elements, with

\displaystyle A_{1}=a_{12}a_{13}+a_{12}a_{23}+a_{13}a_{32},

\displaystyle A_{2}=a_{13}a_{21}+a_{21}a_{23}+a_{23}a_{31},

\displaystyle A_{3}=a_{12}a_{31}+a_{21}a_{32}+a_{31}a_{32}.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
X
be the set consisting of all numerical monoids with embedding dimension three and three Betti elements. Let
Y
be the set 0-matrices. There exists a bijection between
X
and
Y
given by the assignments

\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&r_{12}&r_{13}\\
r_{21}&0&r_{23}\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{12}&a_{13}\\
a_{21}&0&a_{23}\\
a_{31}&a_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\langle A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\rangle.

Proof.

The map from matrices to numerical monoids is injective due to
[
4
, Theorem 8]
, again noting that the result holds under the weaker condition of three Betti elements. This map surjective due to Lemma
2.8
.
∎

Given a numerical monoid
M
with embedding dimension three and exactly three Betti elements, we can use the bijection in Theorem
2.10
to obtain a 0-matrix, which we call the
0-matrix associated with
M
.

3
Longest and Shortest Factorization Lengths

In this section, we consider numerical monoids
\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
of embedding dimension three with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
. We first establish a few results to narrow down where exceptions to
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
or
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
can occur. We then apply these results separately to numerical monoids of embedding dimension three with three Betti elements and those with at most two Betti elements.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\rangle
be a numerical monoid with
n_{1}<\cdots<n_{k}
of embedding dimension
k
. The followings statements hold.

1.

Suppose for
x\in M
there exists
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathsf{Z}(x+n_{1})
with
a_{1}>0
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=L(x+n_{1})
. Then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
.

2.

Suppose for
x\in M
there exists
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathsf{Z}(x+n_{k})
with
a_{k}>0
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=\ell(x+n_{k})
. Then
\ell(x+n_{k})=\ell(x)+1
.

Proof.

We always have
L(x)+1\leq L(x+n_{1})
since any factorization
(a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{k}^{\prime})
of
x
of the longest length gives us a factorization
(a_{1}^{\prime}+1,a_{2}^{\prime}\dots,a_{k}^{\prime})
of
x+n_{1}
.

On the other hand, since
a_{1}>0
, the factorization
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
of
x+n_{1}
gives us that
(a_{1}-1,a_{2}\dots,a_{k})
is a factorization of
x
. Thus,
L(x+n_{1})-1\leq L(x)
.

Combining the two inequalities yields

L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1.

The proof for the case involving the shortest factorization length is similar.
∎

Bringing our focus to a numerical monoid
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
of embedding dimension three with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
, we can find exceptions to
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
by only considering
x\in M
such that
x+n_{1}\in\langle n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
. It turns out only one of those elements needs to be tested to see if there exists an
x\in M
such that
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
. Similarly, we look at
x\in M
such that
x+n_{3}\in\langle n_{1},n_{2}\rangle
to find exceptions to
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
. We will first need a definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three. Define

\alpha(M)=\min\{b\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid bn_{2}-n_{1}\in M\}\text{\quad and \quad}\beta(M)=\min\{b\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid bn_{2}-n_{3}\in M\}.

This definition allows us to state the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
. The following statements hold.

1.

There exists
x\in M
with
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
if and only if
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})\neq L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
.

2.

There exists
x\in M
with
\ell(x+n_{3})\neq\ell(x)+1
if and only if
\ell(\beta(M)n_{2})\neq\ell(\beta(M)n_{2}-n_{3})+1
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the forward direction. Suppose that there exists
x\in M
with
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
. Write

x+n_{1}=an_{1}+bn_{2}+cn_{3},

where
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a+b+c=L(x+n_{1})
. Then
a=0
by Lemma
3.1
. Since
x\in M
, we also have a factorization

x+n_{1}=An_{1}+Bn_{2}+Cn_{3},

with
A,B,C\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
A>0
. Because
A>0
, the factorization
(A,B,C)
of
x+n_{1}
cannot be of the longest length. This yields

L(x+n_{1})=b+c>A+B+C.

We want to show that
c<C
. Suppose on the contrary that
c\geq C
. Suppose further that
b\geq B
. Then
bn_{2}+cn_{3}=An_{1}+Bn_{2}+Cn_{3}
becomes

(b-B)n_{2}+(c-C)n_{3}=An_{1}.

Since
n_{1}<n_{2},n_{3}
, we have
(b-B)+(c-C)<A
, implying
b+c<A+B+C
, a contradiction. Now consider the case when
c\geq C
and
b<B
. Then
bn_{2}+cn_{3}=An_{1}+Bn_{2}+Cn_{3}
becomes

(c-C)n_{3}=An_{1}+(B-b)n_{2}.

This implies
c-C<A+(B-b)
and therefore
b+c<A+B+C
, a contradiction.

Therefore,
c<C
, which means
bn_{2}=An_{1}+Bn_{2}+(C-c)n_{3}
. Since
A>0
, we have that
bn_{2}-n_{1}\in M
and thus
b\geq\alpha(M)
.

Now write

\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1}=a^{\prime}n_{1}+b^{\prime}n_{2}+c^{\prime}n_{3}

with
a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}=L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})
. We add
n_{1}+(b-\alpha(M))n_{2}+cn_{3}
to both sides to obtain

bn_{2}+cn_{3}=(a^{\prime}+1)n_{1}+(b^{\prime}+b-\alpha(M))n_{2}+(c^{\prime}+c)n_{3}.

Because
a^{\prime}+1>0
, we have that
b+c>a^{\prime}+1+b^{\prime}+b-\alpha(M)+c^{\prime}+c
, which implies that

L(\alpha(M)n_{2})\geq\alpha(M)>a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}+1=L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1.

Thus,
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})\neq L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
. The proof for the result about the shortest factorization length is similar.
∎

To investigate further, we would like to determine some properties of the invariants
\alpha(M)
and
\beta(M)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
. Then

1.

for all
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})
, we have
b=0
;

2.

if
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})\neq L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
, then
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})=\alpha(M)
;

3.

L(\alpha(M)n_{2})\neq L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
if and only if for all
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2})
with
b=0
, we have
a+c<\alpha(M)
;

4.

if
\alpha(M)=2
or
\alpha(M)=n_{1}
, then
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})=L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
; and

5.

\alpha(M)\leq n_{1}
.

Analogously, we have

1.

for all
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(\beta(M)n_{2}-n_{3})
, we have
b=0
;

2.

if
\ell(\beta(M)n_{2})\neq\ell(\beta(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
, then
\ell(\beta(M)n_{2})=\beta(M)
;

3.

\ell(\beta(M)n_{2})\neq\ell(\beta(M)n_{2}-n_{3})+1
if and only if for all
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(\beta(M)n_{2})
with
b=0
, we have
a+c>\beta(M)
;

4.

if
\beta(M)=n_{3}
, then
\ell(\beta(M)n_{2})=\ell(\beta(M)n_{2}-n_{3})+1
; and

5.

\beta(M)\leq n_{3}
.

Proof.

We will prove the statements about
\alpha(M)
. The proofs for the statements about
\beta(M)
are similar.

1.

Let
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})
. We have that
\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1}-bn_{2}=(\alpha(M)-b)n_{2}-n_{1}\in M
. Because of the minimality of
\alpha(M)
, we must have
b=0
.

2.

Now suppose that
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})\neq L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
. Let
(A,B,C)
be a factorization of
\alpha(M)n_{2}
such that
A+B+C=L(\alpha(M)n_{2})
. Then
A=0
by Lemma
3.1
. We must have
C=0
since
C>0
will not maximize
B+C
. Thus,
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})=\alpha(M)
.

3.

Still supposing that
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})\neq L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
, we take
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2})
with
b=0
. If
a=0
, then
cn_{3}=\alpha(M)n_{2}
implies
a+c=c<\alpha(M)
. If
a>0
, then
a+c<L(\alpha(M)n_{2})=\alpha(M)
by Lemma
3.1
.

On the other hand, suppose
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})=L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
. Let
(A,B,C)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})
be a factorization such that
A+B+C=L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})
. By item 1,
B=0
. Then
(A+1,0,C)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2})
and
A+1+B+C=L(\alpha(M)n_{2})
. Therefore,
(A+1)+C\geq\alpha(M)
since
(0,\alpha(M),0)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2})
.

4.

If
\alpha(M)=2
, then there does not exist
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2})
with
b=0
and
a+c<2
, since this would require
(1,0,0)
or
(0,0,1)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2})
. This implies
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})=L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
by item 3.

If
\alpha(M)=n_{1}
, then
(n_{2},0,0)\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)n_{2})
and
n_{2}>\alpha(M)
, so
L(\alpha(M)n_{2})=L(\alpha(M)n_{2}-n_{1})+1
by item 3.

5.

n_{1}n_{2}-n_{1}=(n_{2}-1)n_{1}\in M
.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Notice that
\beta(M)=2
does not guarantee
\ell(\beta(M)n_{2})=\ell(\beta(M)n_{2}-n_{3})+1
unlike the analogous statement for
\alpha(M)=2
in item 4 of Proposition
3.4
. For example, if
M=\langle 6,23,34\rangle
, we calculate that
\beta(M)=2
, but
\ell(2\cdot 23)=2
and
\ell(2\cdot 23-34)+1=3
.

Now we focus on numerical monoids with embedding dimension three and three Betti elements. We now make use of the bijection in Theorem
2.10
. Using this bijection, we can determine whether a numerical monoid
\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
of embedding dimension three with exactly three Betti elements has an exception to
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
given its associated 0-matrix.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three such that
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
and
\absolutevalue{\Betti(M)}=3
. Let
A=(r_{ij})
be the 0-matrix associated with
M
as in Theorem
2.10
. The following statements hold.

1.

The identity
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
holds for all
x\in M
if and only if
r_{12}+r_{32}\leq r_{21}+r_{23}
.

2.

The identity
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
holds for all
x\in M
if and only if
r_{12}+r_{32}\geq r_{21}+r_{23}
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
(3), there exists
x\in M
such that
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
if and only if for all
(a,b,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(mn_{2})
with
b=0
, we have
a+c<\alpha(M)
. Let
c_{2}
be the invariant defined in Section
2
. Since
r_{12}>0
, we have
c_{2}=\alpha(M)
. Because
\absolutevalue{\Betti(M)}=3
, Theorem
2.10
implies that the only possibility is that
a=r_{21}
and
c=r_{23}
. We also have
r_{12}+r_{32}=c_{2}
by Lemma
2.5
. Thus,
a+c<\alpha(M)
is equivalent to

r_{12}+r_{32}>r_{21}+r_{23}.

The proof for the statement about
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
is similar.
∎

This theorem allows us to produce numerical monoids of embedding dimension three with large generators that have exceptions to
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
or
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
.

Example 3.7
.

We can verify that the 0-matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&4321&6\\
2000&0&89\\
7001&1234&0\end{pmatrix}

is associated with the numerical monoid
\langle 417899,813089,41358555\rangle
,
which has three Betti elements. Here,
\alpha(M)=5555
,

L(5555\cdot 813089)=5555,\text{\quad and \quad}L(5555\cdot 813089-417899)=2088,

with the latter coming from the factorization
5555\cdot 813089-417899=1999\cdot 417899+89\cdot 41358555
.

Now assume that
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
is a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three and
\absolutevalue{\textup{Betti}(M)}\leq 2
. By Theorem
2.3
, the monoid
M
can be written in the form
\langle am_{1},am_{2},bm_{1}+cm_{2}\rangle
for some
a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and whether there is an exception to
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
or
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
can be determined from this form.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
be a numerical monoid of embedding dimension three and
\absolutevalue{\textup{Betti}(M)}\leq 2
. Write
M
in the form

M=\langle am_{1},am_{2},bm_{1}+cm_{2}\rangle

for some
a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
1<m_{1}<m_{2}
,
\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
,
a\geq 2
,
b+c\geq 2
, and
\gcd(a,bm_{1}+cm_{2})=1
. The following statements hold.

1.

If
am_{1}<am_{2}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}
, then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
.

2.

If
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
b\neq 0
, then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
a\leq b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})
.

3.

If
bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{1}<am_{2}
, then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}\leq a+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{1}-c
.

4.

If
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
b=0
, then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil a\leq\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil c+m_{2}-m_{1}
.

For the shortest factorization lengths, the following statements hold.

1.

If
am_{1}<am_{2}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}
, then
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}\geq\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{2}-b+a
.

2.

If
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
c\neq 0
, then
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
a\geq b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor(m_{1}-m_{2})
.

3.

If
bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{1}<am_{2}
, then
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
for all
x\in M
.

4.

If
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
c=0
, then
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil a\geq\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil b-m_{2}+m_{1}
.

Proof.

From
[
3
, p. 582]
, since
\absolutevalue{\textup{Betti}(M)}\leq 2
, we have that

\Betti(M)=\{a(bm_{1}+cm_{2}),am_{1}m_{2}\}

with

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}(a(bm_{1}+cm_{2}))=

\displaystyle\left\{\left(b-\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor m_{2},c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor m_{1},0\right),\dots,(b-m_{2},c+m_{1},0),(b,c,0),\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\right\}

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\left\{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(b+m_{2},c-m_{1},0),\dots,\left(b+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor m_{2},c-\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor m_{1},0\right),(0,0,a)\right\}

and

\mathsf{Z}(am_{1}m_{2})=\{(m_{2},0,0),(0,m_{1},0)\}.

Here, we make no assumptions about the ordering of the numbers
am_{1},am_{2},bm_{1}+cm_{2}
yet. A factorization
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathsf{Z}(x)
means
a_{1}\cdot am_{1}+a_{2}\cdot am_{2}+a_{3}\cdot(bm_{1}+cm_{2})=x
. Once we make assumptions about the ordering of the generators, factorizations will be written so that the corresponding generators are in ascending order.

Let
c_{i}
be the invariant defined in Section
2
. Suppose that
n_{i}=bm_{1}+cm_{2}
. Then
c_{i}=a
by Proposition
2.2
. Otherwise,
c_{i}(bm_{1}+cm_{2})=am_{1}m_{2}
with
c_{i}<a
, but
a
divides neither
c_{i}
nor
bm_{1}+cm_{2}
.

Suppose that
n_{j}=am_{1}
. Then
c_{j}=m_{2}
by Proposition
2.2
. If this is not the case, we would have
a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})=c_{j}\cdot am_{1}<am_{1}m_{2}
. Based on the factorizations of
a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})
, this requires
c-\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor m_{1}=0
, so
m_{1}|c
. Thus,

a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})=\left(b+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor m_{2}\right)am_{1}>m_{2}\cdot am_{1},

a contradiction. Similarly, if
n_{k}=am_{2}
, then
c_{k}=m_{1}
.

Now we analyze each case using Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
(3), comparing
\alpha(M)
and the length of the longest factorization of
\alpha(M)n_{2}
without involving
n_{2}
.

1.

Suppose that
am_{1}<am_{2}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}
. Then we have
\alpha(M)=m_{1}
. This is because
c_{2}=m_{1}
and we have
m_{1}\cdot am_{2}-am_{1}=(m_{2}-1)\cdot am_{1}\in M
. Since
\mathsf{Z}(am_{1}m_{2})=\{(m_{2},0,0),(0,m_{1},0)\}
, we have that
x\in M
such that
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
if and only if
m_{2}+0<m_{1}
, which never occurs since
m_{1}<m_{2}
.

2.

Now assume that
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
b\neq 0
. Then
\alpha(M)=a
due to the facts that
c_{2}=a
and
\left(b,0,c\right)\in\mathsf{Z}(a(bm_{1}+cm_{2}))
with
b\neq 0
. From the factorizations of
a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})
, the longest factorization of the form
(a_{1},0,a_{3})
is
\left(b+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor m_{2},0,c-\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor m_{1}\right)
, which has length
b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})
. Therefore, there is an exception to
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
a>b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})
.

3.

In this case, we assume that
bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{1}<am_{2}
. We claim that
\alpha(M)=b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}
. We first check that

\left(b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}\right)am_{1}-a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})=\left(\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{1}-c\right)am_{2}\in M,

so there exists
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in Z\left(\left(b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}\right)am_{1}\right)
such that
a_{1}\neq 0
. On the other hand, there exist
a_{1},a_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{1}>0
such that

\alpha(M)\cdot am_{1}=a_{1}(bm_{1}+cm_{2})+a_{3}\cdot am_{2}.

Then we have
\alpha(M)m_{1}=k(bm_{1}+cm_{2})+a_{3}m_{2}=kbm_{1}+(kc+a_{3})m_{2}
, where
k=\frac{a_{1}}{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
since
\gcd(a,bm_{1}+cm_{2})=1
. Since
\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
, we have that

\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)m_{1})=\left\{(\alpha(M),0),(\alpha(M)-m_{2},m_{1}),\dots,\left(\alpha(M)-\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha(M)}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor m_{2},\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha(M)}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor m_{1}\right)\right\}

in
\langle m_{1},m_{2}\rangle
. Therefore,
\alpha(M)-gm_{2}=kb
and
gm_{1}=kc+a_{3}
for some
g\in{\mathbb{N}}
. This implies that
g\geq\frac{c}{m_{1}}
so
g\geq\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil
. Now we see that
\alpha(M)=kb+gm_{2}\geq b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}
. Thus,
\alpha(M)=b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}
.

We now want to show that
\left(a,0,\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{1}-c\right)
is the longest factorization of
\alpha(M)\cdot am_{1}
without involving
am_{1}
. Suppose again that we have
(a_{1},0,a_{3})\in\mathsf{Z}(\alpha(M)\cdot am_{1})
with
a_{1}>0
. Then from before, we have
\alpha(M)=kb+gm_{2}
, where
k=\frac{a_{1}}{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
and
g\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
g\geq\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil
. Assume for a contradiction that
a_{1}>a
. Then
k\geq 2
, so
\alpha(M)>b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}
, which is impossible. Thus,
\left(a,0,\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{1}-c\right)
is the longest factorization of
\alpha(M)\cdot am_{1}
without involving
am_{1}
, and we can conclude that
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}\leq a+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{1}-c
.

4.

Suppose that
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
b=0
. Let
a^{\prime}=m_{2}
,
m_{1}^{\prime}=c
,
m_{2}^{\prime}=a
,
b^{\prime}=0
, and
c^{\prime}=m_{1}
. We now have
c^{\prime}m_{2}^{\prime}<a^{\prime}m_{1}^{\prime}<a^{\prime}m_{2}^{\prime}
.

Now we verify that these parameters are valid. We have that
1<m_{1}^{\prime}<m_{2}^{\prime}
since
cm_{2}<am_{2}
. It must be that
\gcd(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime})=1
because otherwise
\gcd(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\neq 1
. Moreover,
a^{\prime}=m_{2}\geq 2
and
b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}=m_{1}\geq 2
. Lastly,
\gcd(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}m_{1}^{\prime}+c^{\prime}m_{2}^{\prime})=\gcd(m_{2},am_{1})=1
since
\gcd(a,cm_{2})=1
and
\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
.

We are in case 3 with this new form. Therefore,
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if
b^{\prime}+\left\lceil\frac{c^{\prime}}{m_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rceil m_{2}^{\prime}\leq\left\lceil\frac{c^{\prime}}{m_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rceil m_{1}^{\prime}+a^{\prime}-c^{\prime}
, which happens if and only if

\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil a\leq\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil c+m_{2}-m_{1}.

For the shortest factorization lengths, we do a similar analysis.

1.

Under the assumptions of
am_{1}<am_{2}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}
, we calculate
\beta(M)=c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}
. We know there exist
a_{1},a_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{3}>0
such that

\beta(M)\cdot am_{2}=a_{1}\cdot am_{1}+a_{3}(bm_{1}+cm_{2}).

Due to the fact that
\gcd(a,bm_{1}+cm_{2})=1
, dividing by
a
yields

\beta(M)m_{2}=(a_{1}+kb)m_{1}+kcm_{2},

where
k=\frac{a_{3}}{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
. Now we have that
\beta(M)-gm_{1}=kc
and
gm_{2}=a_{1}+kb
for some
g\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The latter implies that
g\geq\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil
and thus

\beta(M)=kc+gm_{1}\geq c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}.

Furthermore, we have

\left(c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}\right)am_{2}-a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})=\left(\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{2}-b\right)am_{1}\in M,

proving that
\beta(M)=c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}
.

Now we claim that
\left(\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{2}-b,0,a\right)
is the shortest factorization of
\beta(M)\cdot am_{2}
without involving
am_{2}
. Suppose we have such a factorization of
\beta(M)\cdot am_{2}
. Then we have
a_{1},a_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{3}>0
such that
\beta(M)\cdot am_{2}=a_{1}\cdot am_{1}+a_{3}(bm_{1}+cm_{2})
. Following the calculations that were previously done, we get that
\beta(M)=kc+gm_{1}
, where
g\geq\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil
and
k=\frac{a_{3}}{a}
. If
(a_{1},0,a_{3})
is a strictly shorter factorization than
\left(\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{2}-b,0,a\right)
, then
a_{3}>a
, so
k\geq 2
. This implies that
\beta(M)=kc+gm_{1}>c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}=\beta(M)
, a contradiction. Thus,
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
for all
x\in M
if and only if

c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}\geq\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{2}-b+a.

2.

Consider the case where
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
c\neq 0
. Then
c_{2}=a
and
(b,0,c)\in\mathsf{Z}(a(bm_{1}+cm_{2}))
with
c\neq 0
, so
\beta(M)=a
. Now we look at all the factorizations of
a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})
and see that there is an exception to
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
if and only if

\beta(M)<b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor(m_{1}-m_{2})

since
\left(b-\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor m_{2},0,c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor m_{1}\right)
is the shortest factorization of
a(bm_{1}+cm_{2})
not involving
bm_{1}+cm_{2}
.

3.

Assuming
bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{1}<am_{2}
, we calculate
\beta(M)=m_{2}
since
c_{2}=m_{2}
and
m_{2}\cdot am_{1}-am_{2}=(m_{1}-1)am_{2}\in M
. Then by the factorizations of
\beta(M)\cdot am_{1}=am_{1}m_{2}
, we have an exception to
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
if and only if
\beta(M)=m_{2}<m_{1}
, which is impossible.

4.

Now suppose that
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
c=0
. Then we can check that the parameters
a^{\prime}=m_{1},m_{1}^{\prime}=a,m_{2}^{\prime}=b,b^{\prime}=m_{2},c^{\prime}=0
are valid parameters that put this numerical monoid in case 1. We then apply the result of case 1.

∎

We now provide some examples of numerical monoids of embedding dimension three with at most two Betti elements that satisfy the inequalities in Theorem
3.8
and some numerical monoids that do not satisfy the inequalities, showing that the inequalities in Theorem
3.8
are necessary to state. We start with cases for
L(x)
first.

Example 3.9
.

In this example, we consider the case where
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
b\neq 0
.

The numerical monoid
\langle 15,19,21\rangle
has parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(3,1,2,5,7)
. These parameters satisfy
a\leq b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})
, so
L(x+15)=L(x)+1
for all
x
.

However,
\langle 33,34,55\rangle

(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(11,3,5,3,5)
. These parameters do not satisfy
a\leq b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})
. In fact,

but

.

Example 3.10
.

Here, we are looking at
bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{1}<am_{2}
.

The monoid
\langle 23,33,44\rangle
has parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(11,1,5,3,4)

b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}\leq a+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{1}-c
, so
L(x+23)=L(x)+1
for all
x
.

On the other hand, the monoid
\langle 8,9,15\rangle
has parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(3,1,1,3,5)
. These parameters do not satisfy
b+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{2}\leq a+\left\lceil\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rceil m_{1}-c
. We have
L(6\cdot 9)=6
but
L(6\cdot 9-8)+1=5
.

Example 3.11
.

Now we consider the case where
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
b=0
.

For the monoid
\langle 15,22,33\rangle
, we have the parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(3,0,2,5,11)
. This satisfies
\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil a\leq\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil c+m_{2}-m_{1}
, so
L(x+15)=L(x)+1
for all
x
.

The monoid
\langle 65,66,143\rangle
has parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(13,0,6,5,11)
. This does not satisfy
\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil a\leq\left\lceil\frac{m_{1}}{c}\right\rceil c+m_{2}-m_{1}
. In this monoid, we have
L(13\cdot 66)=13
but
L(13\cdot 66-65)+1=12
.

Now we see examples for
\ell(x)
.

Example 3.12
.

We look at
am_{1}<am_{2}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}
.

Consider the monoid
\langle 35,40,52\rangle
. This monoid is given by the parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(5,4,3,7,8)
. With these parameters, the inequality
c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}\geq\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{2}-b+a
holds, so
\ell(x+52)=\ell(x)+1
for all
x
.

Now consider
\langle 35,55,61\rangle
, which is given by
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(5,4,3,7,11)
. The inequality
c+\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{1}\geq\left\lceil\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rceil m_{2}-b+a
does not hold. In particular, we have
\ell(10\cdot 55)=10
but
\ell(10\cdot 55-61)+1=12
.

Example 3.13
.

Now consider the case
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
c\neq 0
.

The parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(3,2,1,5,7)
yield the monoid
\langle 15,17,21\rangle
. These parameters satisfy
a\geq b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})
, so
\ell(x+21)=\ell(x)+1
for all
x
.

The monoid
\langle 15,23,24\rangle
has parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(3,3,1,5,8)
. These parameters do not satisfy
a\geq b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})
. We see that
\ell(3\cdot 23)=3
but
\ell(3\cdot 23-24)+1=4
.

Example 3.14
.

Consider the case where
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
and
c=0
.

We have the monoid
\langle 12,16,21\rangle
. This monoid is given by the parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(3,4,0,4,7)
. This satisfies
\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil a\geq\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil b-m_{2}+m_{1}
. Therefore,
\ell(x+21)=\ell(x)+1
for all
x
.

The monoid
\langle 35,75,77\rangle
is given by the parameters
(a,b,c,m_{1},m_{2})=(7,15,0,5,11)
. The inequality
\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil a\geq\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil b-m_{2}+m_{1}
does not hold. We have
\ell(7\cdot 75)=7
but
\ell(7\cdot 75-77)+1=9
.

Putting the results about numerical monoids of embedding dimension three together, we find that there is only a particular family of numerical monoids
\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
of embedding dimension three such that exceptions to both formulas
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
and
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
are found.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
M=\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle
with
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3}
be a numerical monoid with embedding dimension three. Then
L(x+n_{1})=L(x)+1
for all
x\in M
or
\ell(x+n_{3})=\ell(x)+1
for all
x\in M
.

Proof.

If
M
has three Betti elements, then an exception to both formulas about the longest and shortest factorization lengths would require
r_{12}+r_{32}>r_{21}+r_{23}
and
r_{12}+r_{32}<r_{21}+r_{23}
, which is impossible.

Now we assume that
M
has at most two Betti elements. Using the notation and results of Theorem
3.8
, we see that we must have
am_{1}<bm_{1}+cm_{2}<am_{2}
in order to have a chance to have
x,y\in M
such that
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
and
\ell(y+n_{3})\neq\ell(y)+1
. If
b\neq 0
and
c\neq 0
, then there exist
x,y\in M
such that
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
and
\ell(y+n_{3})\neq\ell(y)+1
if and only if

b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})<a<b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor(m_{1}-m_{2}),

but this implies
\left\lfloor\frac{c}{m_{1}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})<\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor(m_{1}-m_{2})
, which is impossible since the left-hand side is positive and the right-hand side is negative. If
b\neq 0
and
c=0
, then there exist
x,y\in M
such that
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
and
\ell(y+n_{3})\neq\ell(y)+1
if and only if

a>b+c+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{m_{2}}\right\rfloor(m_{2}-m_{1})\text{\quad and \quad}\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil a<\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil b-m_{2}+m_{1}.

This implies that
\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil b-m_{2}+m_{1}>\left\lceil\frac{m_{2}}{b}\right\rceil b
, which implies that
m_{1}>m_{2}
, a contradiction. Similarly, assuming
b=0
,
c\neq 0
, and that there exist
x,y\in M
such that
L(x+n_{1})\neq L(x)+1
and
\ell(y+n_{3})\neq\ell(y)+1
leads to a contradiction.
∎

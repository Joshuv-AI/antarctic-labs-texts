Values distribution of holomorphic curves on annuli into projective varieties with hypersurface targets
Si Duc Quang, Tran An Hai

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper has twofold. The first is to establish a Cartan-Nochka theorem for holomorphic curves from annuli into projective varieties intersecting hypersurfaces in subgeneral position with truncated counting functions. The second is to give a second main theorem for such curves with arbitrary families of hypersurfaces, where the truncation level of the counting functions is explicitly estimated not depending on the number of hypersurfaces. As an application, we give a uniqueness theorem for such curves sharing a few hypersurfaces in a projective variety ignoring multiplicity. Our results generalize and improve the recent previous results in this topic, such as [ 10 , 12 , 18 ] .

1.
Introduction and Main results

The values distribution theory for non-constant meromorphic functions on the complex plane was initiated by Nevanlinna
[
6
]
in 1926. And then, the theory of Nevanlinna had been developed to the case of higher dimension by many authors. Especially, in 1933, Cartan
[
1
]
established the most important second main theorem for linearly nondegenerate meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and hyperplanes in general position. By introducing the notion of Nochka weights, in 1983 Nochka
[
7
]
extended the result of Cartan to the case of hyperplanes in subgeneral position. He proved the following second main theorem.

Theorem A
(cf.
[
7
,
8
]
).
Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a linearly nondegenerate meromorphic mapping and
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
a family of hyperplanes of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position. We have

\|\ (q-2N+n-1)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}N^{[n]}(r,\nu_{(H_{i},f)})+o(T_{f}(r)).

Here, the notation “
\|\ P
” means the assertion
P
holds for every
r\in(0,+\infty)
outside a finite linear measure set;
T_{f}(r)
and
N^{[n]}(r,\nu_{(H_{i},f)})
denote the characteristic function of
f
and the truncated (to level
n
) counting function of the divisor
f^{*}H_{i}
, respectively (see
[
9
]
for the definition); and the family
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
is said to be in
N
-subgeneral position if
\bigcap_{j=0}^{N}H_{i_{j}}=\varnothing
for any
1\leq i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{N}\leq q
.

Recently, Quang and An
[
11
]
have extended Theorem A to the case of hypersurface targets. In order to state their result we need to recall the following. Let
V
be a complex projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k\ (k\leq n)
and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\ (q\geq k+1)
hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. The family
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
is said to be in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
if
V\cap(\bigcap_{j=0}^{N}Q_{i_{j}})=\varnothing
for any
1\leq i_{0}<\cdots<i_{N}\leq q
. We say that the family
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
is in
general position
with respect to
V
if it is in
k
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
.

Denote by
I(V)
the ideal of homogeneous polynomials in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
defining
V
. For a positive integer
d
, denote by
H_{d}
the
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space of all homogeneous polynomials in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree
d
, including the zero polynomial, and define

I_{d}(V):=\dfrac{H_{d}}{I(V)\cap H_{d}}\text{ and }H_{V}(d):={\mathrm{dim}}I_{d}(V).

Then
H_{V}(d)
is called the Hilbert function of
V
. Each element of
I_{d}(V)
which is an equivalent class of an element
D\in H_{d},
is denoted by
[D]
and it defines a hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, denoted by
D^{*}
. A meromorphic mapping
f
from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
V
is said to be nondegenerate over
I_{d}(V)
if there is no
D\in H_{d}
with
[D]\neq 0
such that
f({\mathbb{C}}^{m})\subset D^{*}
. In
[
11
]
, Quang and An proved the following result.

Theorem B
(cf.
[
11
]
).
Let
V
be a complex projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k\ (k\leq n)
.
Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be hypersurfaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
with
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
. Let
d
be the least common multiple of
d_{i}^{\prime}
s, i.e.,
d=lcm(d_{1},\ldots,d_{q})
. Let
f
be a holomorphic curve of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
V
such that
f
is nondegenerate over
I_{d}(V)
. Then, we have

\biggl\|\ \left(q-\dfrac{(2N-k+1)H_{V}(d)}{k+1}\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d_{i}}N^{[H_{V}(d)-1]}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)})+o(T_{f}(r)).

In this theorem, the number
\frac{(2N-k+1)H_{V}(d)}{k+1}
is called the above bound of the total defect. We note that, in 1979, B. Shiffman
[
17
]
conjectured that if
f
is an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic curve from
{\mathbb{C}}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
then the total defect of
f
with a family of hypersurfaces in general position is bounded above by
n+1
. In 2004, M. Ru
[
15
]
solved this conjecture by establishing a second main theorem for algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic curves and families of hypersurfaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in general position, where the total defect is bounded above exactly by
n+1
. Later on, in 2009, M. Ru
[
16
]
generalizes his results to the case of hypersurfaces in general position with respect to a projective variety. Recently, by introducing the notion of distributive constant of an arbitrary family of hypersurfaces, Quang
[
12
]
extended the results of Ru to the more general case. We recall the following definition.

Theorem C
(cf.
[
12
]
).
Let
V
be a projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k\leq n
, and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. The distributive constant of the family
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
with respect to
V
is defined by

\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}:=\underset{\varnothing\neq\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}}{\max}\frac{\sharp\Gamma}{k-{\mathrm{dim}}\left(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma}Q_{j}\right)\cap V}.

In
[
12
]
, Quang proved the following second main theorem.

Theorem D
(cf.
[
12
]
).
Let
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension
k\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
be a family of hypersurfaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with the distributive constant
\Delta
with respect to
V
,
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
, and
d
the least common multiple of
d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}
. Let
f
be an algebraically non-degenerate meromorphic mapping from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
V
. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\bigl\|\ \left(q-\Delta(n+1)-\epsilon\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d_{i}}N^{[M_{0}]}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)}),

where
M_{0}=\left\lfloor d^{n^{2}+n}\deg(V)^{n+1}e^{n}\Delta^{n}(2n+4)^{n}(n+1)^{n}(q!)^{n}\epsilon^{-n}\right\rfloor
.

Here, by
\lfloor x\rfloor
we denote the largest integer not exceeding the real number
x
.

The purpose of this article is to generalize Theorem B and Theorem D to the case of holomorphic curves from an annulus
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}|\ \frac{1}{R_{0}}<|z|<R_{0}\}\ (1<R_{0}\leq+\infty)
into a projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Our results are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V
be a complex projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k\ (k\leq n)
and
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
hypersurfaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
with
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
. Let
d
be the least common multiple of
d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}
. Let
f
be a holomorphic curve from
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})\ (1<R_{0}\leq+\infty)
into
V
such that
f
is nondegenerate over
I_{d}(V)
. Assume that
q>\dfrac{(2N-k+1)H_{V}(d)}{k+1}.
Then, we have

\left(q-\dfrac{(2N-k+1)H_{V}(d)}{k+1}\right)T_{0}(r,f)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d_{i}}N^{[H_{V}(d)-1]}_{0}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)})+S_{f}(r).

Here, the characteristic function
T_{0}(r,f)
, the counting function
N^{[m]}_{0}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)})
and the small term
S_{f}(r)
are defined in Section 2. In order to optimal the above bound of the total defect, we generalize and improve Theorem D to the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension
k\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
be a family of hypersurfaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with the distributive constant
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
with respect to
V
,
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
, and
d
the least common multiple of
d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}
. Let
f
be an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic curve from
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})\ (1<R_{0}\leq+\infty)
into
V
. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,

(q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}(n+1)-\epsilon)T_{0}(r,f)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d_{i}}N^{[L_{0}-1]}_{0}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)})+S_{f}(r),

where
L_{0}=\left\lfloor d^{n^{2}+n}(\deg V)^{n+1}e^{n}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}^{n}(2n+5)^{n}((n+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{n}\right\rfloor
.

We would like to emphasize that, in the above result the truncation level
L_{0}
depends on
V,n,\mathcal{Q}
and
\epsilon
, but does not depend on
q
as in Theorem D. Here, in order to estimate the truncation level
L_{0}
not depending on
q
, we will use the new below bound of Chow weight in
[
12
]
(see Lemma 4.2) and a new technique proposed in
[
14
]
to control the error term occurring when estimating the Hilbert weights.

For the last purpose of this paper, we will give an application of Theorem
1.1
. We prove the following uniqueness theorem for holomorphic curves on annuli sharing hypersurfaces. Our last result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
V
be a complex projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k\ (k\leq n)
. Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
and
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
. Let
d
be the least common multiple of
d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}
. Let
f
and
g
be admissible holomorphic curves from
\mathbb{A}(R_{0})
into
V
which are nondegenerate over
I_{d}(V)
. Assume that

(i)
{\mathrm{dim}}f^{-1}(Q_{i}\cap Q_{j})=\varnothing
for every
1\leq i<j\leq q,

(ii)
f=g
on
\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}(f^{-1}(Q_{i})\cup g^{-1}(Q_{i})).

Then the following assertions hold:

a) If
q>\dfrac{2(H_{V}(d)-1)}{d}+\dfrac{(2N-k+1)H_{V}(d)}{k+1},
then
f=g;

b) If
q>\dfrac{2(2N-k+1)H_{V}(d)}{k+1}
, then there exist
N+1
hypersurfaces
Q_{i_{0}},\ldots,Q_{i_{N}}
,
1\leq i_{0}<\cdots<i_{N}\leq q
, such that

\dfrac{D^{d/d_{i_{0}}}_{i_{0}}({\bf f})}{D^{d/d_{i_{0}}}_{i_{0}}({\bf g})}=\cdots=\dfrac{D^{d/d_{i_{N}}}_{i_{N}}({\bf f})}{D^{d/d_{i_{N}}}_{i_{N}}({\bf g})},

where
D_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
is a homogeneous polynomial defining
Q_{i}
with
\deg D_{i}=d_{i}
,
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}
are reduced representations of
f
and
g
, respectively.

In the case where
d=1
, Theorem
1.3
b) gives us the following uniqueness theorem for admissible holomorphic curves into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, which may be linearly degenerate, sharing few hyperplanes in general position.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in general position. Let
f
and
g
be admissible holomorphic curves of
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Assume that

(i)
{\mathrm{dim}}f^{-1}(H_{i}\cap H_{j})=\varnothing
for every
1\leq i<j\leq q,

(ii)
f=g
on
\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}(f^{-1}(H_{i})\cup g^{-1}(H_{i})).

If
q>2(2n-k+1)
, where
k
is the dimension of the smallest linear subspace containing the image of

, then
f=g.

Our results not only extend the previous result in the case of mappings on
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, but also generalize and improve the recent results for the case of holomorphic curves on annuli and hypersurfaces targets of other authors, such as Phuong-Padaphet
[
10
]
and Yang-Zhu
[
18
]
. The paper is organized as follows. The basic notions in Nevanlinna theory is recalled in Section 2. The Cartan-Nochka theorem on annuli for hypersurface targets (Theorem
1.1
) is proved in Section 3. Theorem
1.2
is proved in Section 4. In the last section, we will prove the uniqueness theorem (Theorem
1.3
) and the Corollary
1.4

2.
Basic notions and auxiliary results from Nevanlinna theory on annuli

(a) Nevanlinna theory for meromorphic functions on annuli.

For a divisor
\nu
on
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
, which we may regard as a function on
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
with values in
{\mathbb{Z}}
whose support is a discrete subset of
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0}),
and for a positive integer
M
(maybe
M=+\infty
), we define the counting function of
\nu
as follows

N_{0}^{[M]}(r,\nu)=\int\limits_{\frac{1}{r}}^{1}\dfrac{n_{0}^{[M]}(t)}{t}dt+\int\limits_{1}^{r}\dfrac{n_{0}^{[M]}(t)}{t}dt\quad(1<r<R_{0}),

\displaystyle\text{ where }n_{0}^{[M]}(t)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{1\leq|z|\leq t}\min\{M,\nu(z)\}&\text{ if }1\leq t<R_{0},\\
\sum\limits_{t\leq|z|<1}\min\{M,\nu(z)\}&\text{ if }\dfrac{1}{R_{0}}<t<1.\end{cases}

For brevity we will omit the character
[M]
if
M=+\infty
.

For a meromorphic function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
, denote by
\nu^{0}_{\varphi}
(resp.
\nu^{\infty}_{\varphi}
) the divisor of zeros (resp. divisor of poles) of
\varphi
. The divisor of
\varphi
is defined by
\nu_{\varphi}:=\nu^{0}_{\varphi}-\nu^{\infty}_{\varphi}.
We define the proximity function of
\varphi
by

m_{0}(r,\varphi)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|\varphi(\dfrac{1}{r}e^{i\theta})|d\theta+\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|d\theta-\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|\varphi(e^{i\theta})|d\theta.

The Nevanlinna characteristic function of
f
is defined by

T_{0}(r,\varphi)=m_{0}(r,\varphi)+N_{0}(r,\nu^{\infty}_{\varphi}).

Throughout this paper, we denote by
S_{f}(r)
quantities satisfying

(i)

in the case
R_{0}=+\infty
,

S_{\varphi}(r)=O(\log(rT_{0}(r,\varphi)))

for
r\in(1,+\infty)
except for a set
E
such that
\int_{E}r^{\lambda-1}dr<+\infty
for some
\lambda\geq 0
,

(ii)

in the case
R_{0}<+\infty
,

S_{\varphi}(r)=O(\log(\dfrac{T_{0}(r,\varphi)}{R_{0}-r}))\text{ as }r\longrightarrow R_{0}

for
r\in(1,R_{0})
except for a set
E^{\prime}
such that
\int_{E^{\prime}}\dfrac{dr}{(R_{0}-r)^{\lambda+1}}<+\infty
for some
\lambda\geq 0
.

The function
\varphi
is said to be admissible if it satisfies

\underset{r\longrightarrow+\infty}{\mathrm{limsup}}\dfrac{T_{0}(r,\varphi)}{\log r}=+\infty\text{ in the case }R_{0}=+\infty

or

\underset{r\longrightarrow R_{0}^{-}}{\mathrm{limsup}}\dfrac{T_{0}(r,\varphi)}{-\log(R_{0}-r)}=+\infty\text{ in the case }1<R_{0}<+\infty.

Thus for an admissible meromorphic function
\varphi
on the annulus
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
, we have
S_{\varphi}(r)=o(T_{0}(r,\varphi))
as
r\longrightarrow R_{0}
for all
1\leq r<R_{0}
except for the set
E
or the set
E^{\prime}
mentioned above, respectively (cf.
[
2
]
).

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma on logarithmic derivatives
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
)
.

Let
\varphi
be a nonzero meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
. Then for each positive integer
k
we have

m_{0}\left(r,\dfrac{\varphi^{(k)}}{\varphi}\right)=S_{\varphi}(r)\ (1\leq r<R_{0}).

Here by
\varphi^{(k)}
we denote the derivative of order
k
of the function
f
.

Theorem 2.2

(First main theorem
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
)
.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
. Then for each
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
we have

T_{0}(r,\varphi)=T_{0}\left(r,\dfrac{1}{\varphi-a}\right)+S_{\varphi}(r)\ (1\leq r<R_{0}).

(b) Nevanlinna theory for holomorphic curves on annuli.

Let
f
be a holomorphic mapping from an annulus
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with a reduced representation
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
. The characteristic function of
f
is defined by

T_{0}(r,f)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log\|{\bf f}(re^{i\theta})\|d\theta+\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log\|{\bf f}(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta})\|d\theta-\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log\|{\bf f}(e^{i\theta})\|d\theta,

where
\|{\bf f}\|=(|f_{0}|^{2}+\cdots+|f_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Let
Q
be a hyperpsurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of degree
d
and
D
a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
defining
Q
, i.e.,
Q=\{(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})|D(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=0\},
where

D(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{d}}a_{I}x^{I}

with
\mathcal{I}_{d}=\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}|i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=d\},x^{I}=x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
for
I=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{I}_{d}
. Define

D({\bf f})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{d}}a_{I}f^{I},

where
f^{I}=f_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots f_{n}^{i_{n}}
for
I=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})
. Then the divisor of
D({\bf f})
does not depend on the choice of the reduced representation
{\bf f}
and the defining homogeneous polynomial
D
and we denote it by
\nu_{(Q,f)}
. The proximity function of
f
with respect to
Q
is defined by

\displaystyle m_{0}(r,f,Q)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log\dfrac{\|{\bf f}(re^{i\theta})\|^{d}}{|D({\bf f})(re^{i\theta})|}d\theta+\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log\dfrac{\|{\bf f}(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta})\|^{d}}{|D({\bf f})(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta})|}d\theta-\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log\dfrac{\|{\bf f}(e^{i\theta})\|}{|D({\bf f})(e^{i\theta})|}d\theta.

By Jensen’s formula, we have the First Main Theorem as follows

dT_{0}(r,f)=m_{0}(r,f,Q)+N_{0}(r,\nu_{(Q,f)}).

3.
Cartan-Nochka theorem for hypersurfaces

The following lemma on Nochka weights for hypersurfaces is due to Quang-An
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Cf.
[
11
, Lemma 3]
)
.

Let
V
be a complex projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k\ (k\leq n)
. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
be
q\ (q>2N-k+1)
hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
of the common degree
d.
Then there are positive rational constants
\omega_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
satisfying the following:

i)
0<\omega_{i}\leq 1,\ \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
,

ii) Setting
\tilde{\omega}=\max_{j\in Q}\omega_{j}
, one gets

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}=\tilde{\omega}(q-2N+k-1)+k+1.

iii)
\dfrac{k+1}{2N-k+1}\leq\tilde{\omega}\leq\dfrac{k}{N}.

iv) For
R\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
with
\sharp R=N+1
, then
\sum_{i\in R}\omega_{i}\leq k+1
.

v) Let
E_{i}\geq 1\ (1\leq i\leq q)
be arbitrarily given numbers. For
R\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
with
\sharp R=N+1
, there is a subset
R^{o}\subset R
such that
\sharp R^{o}=\mathrm{rank}\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in R^{o}}=k+1
and

\prod_{i\in R}E_{i}^{\omega_{i}}\leq\prod_{i\in R^{o}}E_{i}.

The next lemma due to Quang-An
[
11
]
is stated for the case of the meromorphic mappings on
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
but it automatically holds for the case of holomorphic curves on annuli.

Lemma 3.2

(cf.
[
11
, Lemma 4]
)
.

Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in R}
be a set of hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of the common degree
d
and let
f
be a holomorphic curve of
\mathbb{A}(R_{0})
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Assume that
\bigcap_{i\in R}Q_{i}\cap V=\varnothing
. Then there exist positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

\alpha\|{\bf f}\|^{d}\leq\max_{i\in R}|D_{i}(f)|\leq\beta\|{\bf f}\|^{d},

where
{\bf f}
is a reduced representation of

and
D_{i}\ (i\in R)
is a homogeneous polynomial defining

of degree
d
.

In order to proof Theorem
1.1
, we first prove the general form of second main theorem as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f
be a linearly nondegenerate holomorphic curve from
{\mathbb{A}}(R_{0})\ (1<R_{0}\leq+\infty)
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with a reduced representation
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
. Let
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a set of linear form in

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\max_{K_{2}}\sum_{i\in K_{2}}\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta})\|}{|L_{i}({\bf f}(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta}))|}d\theta

is linear independent, and

is the wronskian of

Proof.

By adding more linear forms if necessary, we suppose that rank

the set of all subsets

, there is a positive constant

We set

By Lemma

(1)

On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\max_{K_{1}}\sum_{i\in K_{1}}\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(re^{i\theta})\|}{|L_{i}({\bf f}(re^{i\theta}))|}d\theta

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\max_{K_{2}}\sum_{i\in K_{2}}\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta})\|}{|L_{i}({\bf f}(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta}))|}d\theta

\displaystyle+N_{0}(r,\nu_{W})\leq(n+1)T_{0}(r,f)+S_{f}(r).

The theorem is proved.

4.
Second main theorem for arbitrary families of hypersurfaces

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a projective subvariety of dimension
k
and degree
\delta
. For
\textbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{\geq 0}
we write
{\bf x}^{\bf a}

. Let

{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]_{u}

(including

) in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
. For

and define the Hilbert function

of

\displaystyle H_{X}(u):={\mathrm{dim}}{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]_{u}/(I_{X})_{u}.

Let
{\bf c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n})
be a tuple in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{\geq 0}
and let

be the Chow weight of

with respect to
{\bf c}
. The

\displaystyle S_{X}(u,{\bf c}):=\max\sum_{i=1}^{H_{X}(u)}{\bf a}_{i}\cdot{\bf c},

form a basis of

3
]
.

(see
[

By applying Theorem

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}}-\frac{(2n+1)(n+1)\delta}{u}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{q}(N_{0}(r,\nu_{(Q_{j},f)})-N_{0}^{[n_{u}]}(r,\nu_{(Q_{j},f)}))

5.
Uniqueness theorem for holomorphic curves on annuli

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Replacing
D_{i}
by
D_{i}^{d/d_{i}}
if necessary, without loss of generality, we may assume that
d_{i}=d
for all
1\leq i\leq q.

a) Suppose that
f\neq g
. Then there exist two indexes

with
0\leq s<t\leq n

By the assumption (ii) of the theorem, we have

on
\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}(\mathrm{Zero}D_{i}({\bf f})\cup\mathrm{Zero}D_{i}({\bf g}))
. Therefore, we have

This follows that

\displaystyle N_{0}(r,\nu^{0}_{H})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{q}N^{[1]}_{0}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)}).

On the other hand, by the definition of the characteristic function and by the Jensen formula, we have

\displaystyle N_{0}(r,\nu^{0}_{H})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|(f_{s}g_{t}-f_{t}g_{s})(re^{i\theta})|d\theta+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|(f_{s}g_{t}-f_{t}g_{s})(\frac{1}{r}e^{i\theta})|d\theta+O(1)

\displaystyle 2(T_{0}(r,f)+T_{0}(r,g))

b) Suppose that

Proof of Corollary

1.
Classical Sidon sets

Let
A
be a subset of an additive abelian group or semigroup
\Lambda
.
For every positive integer
h
, let
A^{h}
be the set
of all
h
-tuples of elements of
A
.
The symmetric group
S_{h}
acts on the set
A^{h}
by permutation of coordinates:
For all
\sigma\in S_{h}
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}
, we define

(1)

\sigma\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}\right)=\left(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(h)}\right).

The orbits of this action define an equivalence relation
\sim
on
A^{h}
:
For
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}
and
(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})\in A^{h}
, we have

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\sim(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})

if and only if there is a permutation
\sigma\in S_{h}
such that

a^{\prime}_{i}=a_{\sigma(i)}\qquad\text{for all $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$.}

Let
[a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}]
denote the orbit of the
h
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})

and let

A^{h}/S_{h}=\left\{[a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}]:(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}\right\}.

be the set of equivalence classes of
A^{h}
.
The number of
h
-tuples in the orbit
[a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}]
is at most
h!

and is equal to
h!
if and only if the coordinates of the
h
-tuple are distinct.

Consider the linear form

\varphi=\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{h}.

If
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\sim(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
, then for some permutation
\sigma\in S_{h}
we have

\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}=a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(h)}=\varphi(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h}).

Thus, the function
[\varphi]
defined by

[\varphi]([a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}])=\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}

is a well-defined function on the orbit space
A^{h}/S_{h}
.

The
h
-fold sumset
of
A
is the set
hA
of all sums of
h
not necessarily distinct elements of
A
:

\displaystyle hA

\displaystyle=\left\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}:(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}):(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{[\varphi]([a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}]):[a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}]\in A^{h}/S_{h}\right\}

We define two representation functions for the
h
-fold sumset
hA
: For
w\in\Lambda
,

\displaystyle r_{A,h}(w)

\displaystyle=\card\left\{[a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}]\in A^{h}/S_{h}:a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}=w\right\}

and

\displaystyle R_{A,h}(w)

\displaystyle=\card\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}:a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}=w\right\}.

Because the coordinates of the
h
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})
are not necessarily distinct, we have

R_{A,h}(w)\leq h!\ r_{A,h}(w)

for all
w\in\Lambda
.

The set
A
is a
classical Sidon set of order
h
or a
B_{h}
-set

if
r_{A,h}(w)\leq 1
for all
w\in\Lambda
.
The set
A
is a
classical Sidon set of order
h
and multiplicity
g

or, simply, a
B_{h}[g]
-set
if
r_{A,h}(w)\leq g
for all
w\in\Lambda
.

For every integer
m\geq 2
we also define the
modular representation function

r^{(m)}_{A,h}(w)=\card\left\{[a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}]\in A^{h}/S_{h}:a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}\equiv w\pmod{m}\right\}.

The set
A
is a
classical Sidon set of order
h
modulo
m

if
r^{(m)}_{A,h}(w)\leq 1
for all
w\in\Lambda
.

If
A
is a classical Sidon set of order
h
modulo
m
for some
m\geq 2
, then

A
is a classical Sidon set of order
h
.

Halberstam and Roth
[
3
]

and O’Bryant
[
6
]
are excellent surveys of results on Sidon sets obtained before 2004.

2.
The Bose-Chowla argument

Consider classical Sidon sets for the group
\mathbf{Z}
of integers.
Let
F_{h}(n)
denote the largest Sidon set of order
h
contained in the
set of consecutive integers
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
A simple counting argument shows that
F_{h}(n)\ll n^{1/h}
and so

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{h}(n)}{n^{1/h}}<\infty.

Bose and Chowla
[
2
]
proved that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{h}(n)}{n^{1/h}}>0.

Their main result is the following.

Theorem 1

(Bose-Chowla)
.

Let
h\geq 2
and let
q
be a prime power.
Let
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q}\}
be a finite field with
q
elements
and let
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}
be an extension field of
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
of degree
h
.
Let
\theta
be a generator of the cyclic group
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
.
For all
\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
, there is a unique integer
a_{j}\in\{1,2,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}
such that

\theta^{a_{j}}=\theta-\lambda_{j}.

The set

A=\left\{a_{j}:j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\right\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}

is a classical Sidon set of order
h
modulo
q^{h}-1
and cardinality
q
.

Proof.

The element
\theta
generates the field extention
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}/{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
,
and so the minimal polynomial of
\theta
has degree
h
.
Because
\theta
has order
{}^{q^{h}-1}
in the cyclic group
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
, for all integers
u
and
v

we have
\theta^{u}=\theta^{v}
if and only if
u\equiv v\pmod{q^{h}-1}
.

Note that
\theta\notin{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
because
h\geq 2
and
\theta
generate
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
.
Thus,
\theta-\lambda_{j}\neq 0
for all
\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.
It follows that for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
there is a unique integer

a_{j}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}
such that

\theta^{a_{j}}=\theta-\lambda_{j}.

If
a_{j}=0
, then
1=\theta^{0}=\theta-\lambda_{j}
and

\theta=\lambda_{j}+1\in{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
, which is absurd.
Therefore,
a_{j}\in\{1,2,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}
.

We have
a_{j}=a_{k}

if and only if
\theta-\lambda_{j}=\theta^{a_{j}}=\theta^{a_{k}}=\theta-\lambda_{k}

if and only if
j=k
. It follows that the set

A=\left\{a_{j}:j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\right\}

has cardinality
q
.

Let
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})
and
(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
be
h
-tuples of elements of
A
such that

a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{h}\qqand a^{\prime}_{1}\leq a^{\prime}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a^{\prime}_{h}.

There exist unique elements
\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{j}\in{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
such that

\theta^{a_{j}}=\theta-\lambda_{j}\qqand\theta^{a^{\prime}_{j}}=\theta-\lambda^{\prime}_{j}

for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,h\}
. If

a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{h}\equiv a^{\prime}_{1}+a^{\prime}_{2}+\cdots+a^{\prime}_{h}\pmod{q^{h}-1}

then

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{h}(\theta-\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{h}\theta^{a_{j}}=\theta^{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{h}}=\theta^{a^{\prime}_{1}+a^{\prime}_{2}+\cdots+a^{\prime}_{h}}=\prod_{j=1}^{h}\theta^{a^{\prime}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{h}(\theta-\lambda^{\prime}_{j}).

The polynomial

f(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{h}(t-\lambda_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{h}(t-\lambda^{\prime}_{j})

is either the zero polynomial or a nonzero polynomial of degree at most
h-1
with coefficients in
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.
Moreover,

f(\theta)=0.

The minimal polynomial of
\theta
has degree
h
, and so
\theta
is not a root of a nonzero polynomial of degree less than
h
.
Therefore,
f(t)
must be the zero polynomial, and so the polynomials

\prod_{j=1}^{h}(t-\lambda_{j})
and
\prod_{j=1}^{h}(t-\lambda^{\prime}_{j})
have the same roots with the same multiplicities.
Thus,
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{h})
is a permutation of
(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{h})
, and so

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
.
Equivalently, if
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}
and

(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})\in A^{h}
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\neq(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
, then

a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{h}\not\equiv a^{\prime}_{1}+a^{\prime}_{2}+\cdots+a^{\prime}_{h}\pmod{q^{h}-1}

and so
A
is a classical Sidon set of order
h
modulo
q^{h}-1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1
.

For every prime power
q
and every integer
h\geq 2
,

F_{h}(q^{h}-2)\geq q.

Proof.

A classical Sidon set of order
h
modulo
q^{h}-1
is a classical Sidon set of order
h
.
∎

Theorem 2

(Bose-Chowla)
.

For every integer
h\geq 2
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{h}(n)}{n^{1/h}}\geq 1.

Proof.

It has been known since Hoheisel
[
5
]
(and, more recently, Heath-Brown
[
4
]
)
that there is a real number
\alpha

with
0<\alpha<1
such that
if
p
and
p^{\prime}
are consecutive primes, then
p^{\prime}-p<p^{\alpha}
.

For every integer
n\geq 2^{h}
, let
p
be the largest prime such that

p\leq n^{1/h}
and let
p^{\prime}
be the smallest prime such that
p^{\prime}>n^{1/h}
.
The primes
p
and
p^{\prime}
are consecutive, and so, by Hoheisel,

p\leq n^{1/h}<p^{\prime}\leq p+p^{\alpha}\leq p+n^{\alpha/h}

The function
F_{h}(n)
is increasing.
Applying Corollary
1
with
q=p
, we obtain

F_{h}(n)\geq F_{h}(p^{h})\geq F_{h}(p^{h}-2)\geq p\geq n^{1/h}-n^{\alpha/h}.

Therefore,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{h}(n)}{n^{1/h}}\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{1/h}-n^{\alpha/h}}{n^{1/h}}=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{(1-\alpha)/h}}\right)=1.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Sidon systems for linear forms

Fix an integer
h\geq 2
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})
be an
h
-tuple of sets of integers and let

A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}=\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}):a_{i}\in A_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,h\right\}.

For nonzero integers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{h}
, we consider the linear form

\varphi=c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h}

and the set

\varphi(\mathcal{A})=\left\{\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}):(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}\right\}.

For every integer
n
, the
representation function

R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(n)

counts the number of
h
-tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}
such that

\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=n
.

Let
W
be a finite or infinite set of integers.
The
h
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system for
W

if
R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(w)\leq 1
for all
w\in W
.
If
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system for
W
,
then the statements

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h},\qquad(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}

and

\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=\varphi(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})=w\in W

imply

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h}).

More generally, the
h
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system of multiplicity
g
for
W

if
R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(w)\leq g
for all
w\in W
.

The
h
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system
if
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system
for
\mathbf{Z}
,
that is, if
R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(n)\leq 1
for all integers
n
.
The
h
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system of multiplicity
g

if
R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(n)\leq g
for all integers
n
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})
be an
h
-tuple of finite sets of integers. We have

\prod_{i=1}^{h}|A_{i}|=\sum_{w\in\varphi(\mathcal{A})}R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(w)

If
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system, then

\sum_{w\in\varphi(\mathcal{A})}R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(w)=|\varphi(\mathcal{A})|.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system of mutiplicity

,
then

\sum_{w\in\varphi(\mathcal{A})}R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(w)\leq g|\varphi(\mathcal{A})|.

For example, let

be integers such that

for all
i=1,\ldots,h

and let

. Let
W=[0,d-1]
.
Consider the finite sets

and the linear form

\varphi=x_{1}+d_{1}x_{2}+d_{1}d_{2}x_{3}+\cdots+d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{h-1}x_{h}.

We have

for all

and

.

4.
The size of
\varphi
-Sidon systems

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})
be an
h
-tuple of sets of integers
and let
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{h})
be an
h
-tuple of integers.
For
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
, the translate of the set
A_{i}
by the integer
t_{i}
is the set

A_{i}+t_{i}=\{a_{i}+t:a_{i}\in A_{i}\}

and the translate of
\mathcal{A}
by
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{h})
is the
h
-tuple

\mathcal{A}+\mathbf{t}=\mathcal{A}+(t_{1},\ldots,t_{h})=(A_{1}+t_{1},\ldots,A_{h}+t_{h}).

Let
\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}x_{i}
, where the coefficients
c_{i}
are nonzero integers and let

For all
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}+t_{1},\ldots,a_{h}+t_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}(a_{i}+t_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}a_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}t_{i}

\displaystyle=\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})+\varphi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{h})

\displaystyle=\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})+t^{*}.

Thus,
\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=b
if and only if
\varphi(a_{1}+t_{1},\ldots,a_{h}+t_{h})=b+t^{*}
, and so

R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(b)=R_{\mathcal{A}+\mathbf{t},\varphi}(b+t^{*}).

It follows that for
h
-tuples of nonempty finite sets of integers or
h
-tuples
of nonempty sets of integers that are bounded below,
it suffices to consider only
h
-tuples of sets of nonnegative integers
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})

.

Let
\varphi=c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h}
, where the coefficients

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})

If
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system of multiplicity

|A_{i}|=q
for all

, then

(2)

q^{h}=\prod_{i=1}^{h}|A_{i}|\leq g(2Cn+1).

For every positive integer
g
, let
F_{\varphi,g}(n)
denote the largest integer

such that there exists

\varphi

\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})

. Inequality (

5.
Constructing large
\varphi
-Sidon systems

Lemma 1
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
h\geq 2
. There exists an integer
u_{p}
such that

(4)

\gcd(u_{p},p)=\gcd(u_{p}^{h}-1,p)=1

if and only if
p-1
does not divide
h
.

Proof.

Because
p
is prime we have
\gcd(u,p)=1\text{ or }p
for all integers
u
.
If
p-1
divides
h
, then
h=(p-1)t
for some integer
t
. For all integers
u\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}

we have
u^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
and so

u^{h}\equiv\left(u^{p-1}\right)^{t}\equiv 1\pmod{p}

and

\gcd(u^{h}-1,p)=p.

Thus, if
p-1
divides
h
, then no integer satisfies (
4
).

Suppose that
p-1
does not divide
h
.
For every primitive root
u_{p}
modulo
p
we have

u_{p}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and
u_{p}^{h}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}

and so
u_{p}
satisfies (
4
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
h\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{P}(h)
be the finite set of primes
p
such that
p-1
divides
h
.
Let
\varphi=c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h}
be a linear form whose coefficients
c_{1},\ldots,c_{h}

are nonzero integers such that
\gcd(c_{i},p)=1
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
and
p\in\mathcal{P}(h)
.
There exist positive integers
u
and
Q
with
\gcd(u,Q)=1
such that every prime

q
in the infinite arithmetic progression
u\pmod{Q}
satisfies

\gcd(q^{h}-1,c_{i})=1

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{h})
be the finite set of primes
p

such that
p
divides

for some

and let

If

, then

and so

does not divide

,
and so, by Lemma

, there is an integer

that satisfies (

).
By the Chinese remainder theorem, there is an integer

such that

for all

.
We have
\gcd(u,p)=\gcd(u_{p},p)=1
for all

and so
\gcd(u,Q)=1
.
By Dirichlet’s theorem, there are infinitely many primes
q

such that

q\equiv u\pmod{Q}.

For all
p\in\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{h})
we have

\gcd(q^{h}-1,p)=\gcd(u^{h}-1,p)=\gcd(u_{p}^{h}-1,p)=1

and so

\gcd(q^{h}-1,c_{i})=1

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Consider the linear form

\varphi=c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h}

where
h\geq 2
and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{h}
are nonzero integers.
Let
q
be a prime such that

\gcd(q^{h}-1,c_{i})=1

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
.
There is an
h
-tuple
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})
of sets of integers with

A_{i}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}\qqand|A_{i}|=q

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
such that
\mathcal{A}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon system of multiplicity at most
h!
.
Equivalently,

(5)

F_{\varphi,h!}(q^{h}-2)\geq q.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements and let
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}
be an extension field of
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
of degree
h
. The multiplicative group
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
is cyclic
of order
q^{h}-1
. Let
\theta
be a generator of
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
.
For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
we have
\gcd(q^{h}-1,c_{i})=1

and so
\theta^{c_{i}}
is also a generator of the cyclic group
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
.

Let
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q}\}
.
The inequality
h\geq 2
implies that
\theta\notin\mathbf{F}_{q}

and so, for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
,
we have
\theta\neq\lambda_{j}
. Equivalently,

\theta-\lambda_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
.
For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
, the element
\theta^{c_{i}}
generates
\mathbf{F}_{q^{h}}^{\times}
.
It follows that there is a unique integer

a_{i,j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}

such that

\theta^{c_{i}a_{i,j}}=\theta-\lambda_{j}.

If
a_{i,j}=0
, then
\theta=\lambda_{j}+1\in{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
, which is absurd. Therefore,

a_{i,j}\in\{1,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
.
Moreover, if
1\leq j<k\leq q
, then
\lambda_{j}\neq\lambda_{k}
and so
a_{i,j}\neq a_{i,k}
.
The set

A_{i}=\left\{a_{i,j}:j=1,\ldots,q\right\}

is a subset of
\{1,\ldots,q^{h}-2\}
of cardinality
q
for all
i=1,\ldots,h
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})
.
For all
(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j_{h}})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}
we have

\displaystyle\theta^{\varphi\left(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j_{h}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}a_{i,j_{i}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{h}\theta^{c_{i}a_{i,j_{i}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{h}(\theta-\lambda_{j_{i}})=f(\theta)

where

f(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{h}(t-\lambda_{j_{i}})=t^{h}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\lambda_{j_{i}}\right)t^{h-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{h}\prod_{i=1}^{h}\lambda_{j_{i}}

is a monic polynomial of degree
h
with coefficients in
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.

Similarly, for
(a_{1,j^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j^{\prime}_{h}})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}
we have

\displaystyle\theta^{\varphi\left(a_{1,j^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j^{\prime}_{h}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{h}(\theta-\lambda_{j^{\prime}_{i}})=g(\theta)

where

g(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{h}(t-\lambda_{j^{\prime}_{i}})=t^{h}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\lambda_{j^{\prime}_{i}}\right)t^{h-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{h}\prod_{i=1}^{h}\lambda_{j^{\prime}_{i}}

is also a monic polynomial of degree
h
with coefficients in
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.

The relation
\varphi\left(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j_{h}}\right)=\varphi\left(a_{1,j^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j^{\prime}_{h}}\right)

implies

f(\theta)=\theta^{\varphi\left(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j_{h}}\right)}=\theta^{\varphi\left(a_{1,j^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,a_{h,j^{\prime}_{h}}\right)}=g(\theta)

and so
\theta
is a root of the polynomial
f(t)-g(t)
.
If
f(t)\neq g(t)
, then
f(t)-g(t)
is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most
h-1
.
This is impossible because the minimal polynomial of
\theta
has degree
h
.
Therefore,

\prod_{i=1}^{h}(t-\lambda_{j_{i}})=f(t)=g(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{h}(t-\lambda_{j^{\prime}_{i}})

and so
(\lambda_{j^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{j^{\prime}_{h}})
is a permutation of

(\lambda_{j_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{j_{h}})
and
(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{h})
is a permutation of

(j_{1},\ldots,j_{h})
. There are at most
h!
such permutations.
It follows that for every integer
w
there are at most
h!
elements

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{h}

such that
\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=w
.
Therefore,
R_{\mathcal{A},\varphi}(w)\leq h!
and
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})

is a
\varphi
-Sidon system of multiplicity at most
h!
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Let
h\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{P}(h)
be the finite set of primes
p
such that
p-1
divides
h
.
Let
\varphi=c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h}
be a linear form whose coefficients
c_{1},\ldots,c_{h}

are nonzero integers such that for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
and
p\in\mathcal{P}(h)
. Then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{\varphi,h!}(n)}{n^{1/h}}\geq 1.

Proof.

There is an analog of Hoheisel’s theorem for sufficiently large primes in arithmetic progressions.
Let
u
and
Q
be relatively prime positive integers.
Baker, Harman, and Pintz
[
1
]
proved that there
is a real number
\alpha=\alpha(u,Q)
with
0<\alpha<1
such that if
p
and
p^{\prime}

are sufficiently large consecutive primes in the arithmetic progression

u\pmod{Q}
, then
p^{\prime}-p<p^{\alpha}
.

By Lemma
2
,there exist positive integers
u
and
Q
with
\gcd(u,Q)=1

such that every prime

q
in the infinite arithmetic progression
u\pmod{Q}
satisfies

\gcd(q^{h}-1,c_{i})=1

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
.

For every sufficiently large integer
n
, let
p
be the largest prime such that
p\equiv u\pmod{Q}
and

p\leq n^{1/h}
. Let
p^{\prime}
be the smallest prime such that
p\equiv u\pmod{Q}
and
p^{\prime}>n^{1/h}
.
Then
p
and
p^{\prime}
are consecutive primes in the arithmetic progression
u\pmod{Q}
, and so

p\leq n^{1/h}<p^{\prime}\leq p+p^{\alpha}\leq p+n^{\alpha/h}.

Applying inequality (
5
) from Theorem
3

with
q=p
, we obtain

F_{\varphi,h!}(n)\geq F_{\varphi,h!}(p^{h})\geq F_{\varphi,h!}(p^{h}-2)\geq p\geq n^{1/h}-n^{\alpha/h}.

Therefore,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{\varphi,h!}(n)}{n^{1/h}}\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{1/h}-n^{\alpha/h}}{n^{1/h}}=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{(1-\alpha)/h}}\right)=1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Another remark on a result of Ding-Jost-Li-Wang Journal: ***
Xiaobao Zhu

Abstract
Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a compact Riemann surface, h h be a positive smooth function on M M . It is well known that the functional J ⁡ ( u ) = 1 2 ​ ∫ M | ∇ u | 2 ​ d ​ v g + 8 ​ π ​ ∫ M u ​ d ​ v g − 8 ​ π ​ log ​ ∫ M h ​ e u ​ d ​ v g J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+8\pi\int_{M}udv_{g}-8\pi\log\int_{M}he^{u}dv_{g} achieves its minimum under Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition. This result was generalized to nonnegative h h by Yang and the author. Later, Sun and Zhu (arXiv:2012.12840) showed the Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition is also sufficient when h h changes sign, which was reproved later by Wang and Yang (J. Funct. Anal. 282: Paper No. 109449, 2022) and Li and Xu (Calc. Var. 61: Paper No. 143, 2022) respectively using a flow approach. The aim of this note is to give a new proof of Sun and Zhu’s result. Our proof is based on the variational method and the maximum principle.

Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface,
h
a smooth function on
M
. For simplicity, we assume in this paper that the area of
M
equals one.
In 1974, Kazdan-Warner
[
9
]
asked, under what kind of conditions on
h
, the equation

\displaystyle\Delta u=8\pi-8\pi he^{u}

(1.1)

has a solution. In literature, one calls it the Kazdan-Warner problem. Easy to find, a necessary condition to the Kazdan-Warner problem
is
\max_{M}h>0
.
Twenty three years later, Ding, Jost, Li and Wang
[
5
]
attacked this problem successfully.
By using variational method and blow-up analysis, they gave a sufficient condition to the Kazdan-Warner problem.

In
[
5
]
, the authors assumed
h>0
and minimized the functional

\displaystyle J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+8\pi\int_{M}udv_{g}

in the function space

\displaystyle H_{1}=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}(M):\int_{M}he^{u}dv_{g}=1\right\}.

Notice that critical points of
J
in
H_{1}
are solutions of equation (
1.1
). The classical Moser-Trudinger inequality (cf.
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
)

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u}dv_{g}\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{M}udv_{g}+C,

(1.2)

tells us that
J
is bounded from below but not coercive. To see for what kind of
h
the functional
J
can achieve its minimum, they considered the perturbed functional

\displaystyle J_{\epsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+(8\pi-\epsilon)\int_{M}udv_{g}-(8\pi-\epsilon)\log\int_{M}he^{u}dv_{g}

(1.3)

where
\epsilon>0
. By the Moser-Trudinger inequality (
1.2
) one knows,
J_{\epsilon}
is coercive and achieves its minimum at some
u_{\epsilon}\in H_{1}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta u_{\epsilon}=(8\pi-\epsilon)-(8\pi-\epsilon)he^{u_{\epsilon}}.

(1.4)

If
u_{\epsilon}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}(M)
, then
u_{\epsilon}
converges to
u_{0}
which minimizes
J
. Or else,
u_{\epsilon}
blows up, one has
\lambda_{\epsilon}=\max_{M}u_{\epsilon}=u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})\rightarrow+\infty
and

x_{\epsilon}\rightarrow p
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. (In this note, we do not distinguish sequence and its subsequence.) Denote
r_{\epsilon}=e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon}/2}
. They showed the following facts.

Fact 1.

u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon}+r_{\epsilon}x)-\lambda_{\epsilon}\rightarrow-2\log(1+\pi h(p)|x|^{2})
in
C_{\text{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Fact 2.

u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\rightarrow G_{p}(x)
weakly in
W^{1,q}(M)
for any
1<q<2
and in
C^{2}_{\text{loc}}(M\setminus\{p\})
, where
\overline{u_{\epsilon}}=\int_{M}u_{\epsilon}dv_{g}

and
G_{p}
is the Green function which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta G_{p}=8\pi-8\pi\delta_{p},\\
\int_{M}G_{p}dv_{g}=0.\end{cases}

(1.5)

In a normal coordinate system around
y
, one has

\displaystyle G_{y}(x)=-4\log r+A_{y}+b_{1}x_{1}+b_{2}x_{2}+c_{1}x_{1}^{2}+2c_{2}x_{1}x_{2}+c_{3}x_{2}^{2}+O(r^{3}),

(1.6)

where
r=\text{dist}(x,y)
.

Fact 3.
In
M\setminus B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})
,
u_{\epsilon}\geq G_{x_{\epsilon}}-\lambda_{\epsilon}-2\log(\pi h(p))-A_{p}+o_{R}(1)+o_{\epsilon}(1)
.

We remark that Fact 3 is based on the maximum principle.

With these estimates, they proved for positive
h
that

\displaystyle\inf_{W^{1,2}(M)}J(u)\geq C_{0}=-8\pi-8\pi\log\pi-4\pi\max_{M}(A_{p}+2\log h(p)).

(1.7)

Then they constructed a sequence
\phi_{\epsilon}\in W^{1,2}(M)
such that
J(\phi_{\epsilon})<C_{0}
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, provided that

\displaystyle\Delta\log h(p_{0})+8\pi-2K(p_{0})>0,

(1.8)

where
p_{0}
is the maximum point of
A_{p}+2\log h(p)
on
M
and
K
is the Gauss curvature of
(M,g)
.
We call (
1.8
) as the Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition. When
h\geq 0
and is positive somewhere,
Yang and the author
[
13
]
proved that the blow-up can not happen on zero points of
h
, and showed the Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition is sufficient to solve (
1.1
).
The proof is based on a concentration lemma and some elliptic estimates.

Later, using the flow introduced by Castéras
[
2
,
3
]
, Li and Zhu
[
7
]
and Sun and Zhu
[
10
]
reproved the existence result in
[
5
]
and
[
13
]
respectively, Wang and Yang
[
12
]
showed on a symmetric Riemann surface, equations like (
1.1
) can be solved under (
1.8
) when
h
changes sign.
Recently, using another
flow, Li and Xu
[
8
]
proved that when
h
changes sign, the Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition is still sufficient to solve (
1.1
).

In this paper, we would like to give a direct proof which is based on the variational method and the maximum principle to the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface,
h
a smooth function on
M
which is positive somewhere. Denote
M_{+}=\{x\in M:h(x)>0\}
. Suppose the Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition
(
1.8
) is satisfied on
M_{+}
. Then the functional
J
attains its minimum in
H_{1}
and consequently the Kazdan-Warner equation (
1.1
) has a smooth solution.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
11
]
, the authors first asserted Theorem
1.1
. They claimed without much more details that if
u_{\epsilon}
blows up, it must happen at a single point
p
and
h(p)>0
. Also, they said that the maximum principle does not work since
h
changes sign. Instead, they used the capacity estimate to deal with the energy on the neck domain.

The aim of this paper is two fold. First, we give the details on
the claim that if
u_{\epsilon}
blows up, it must happen at a single point
p
and
h(p)>0
.
Second, we find that the maximum principle still works after some modified arguments, so we could still use the maximum principle to deal with the energy on the neck domain like in
[
5
]
. Our method also works for the equation with singular sources, we will present this in a forthcoming paper.

We organized this paper as follows: In Sect. 2, we shall derive an explicit lower bound of
J
in
H_{1}
when
u_{\epsilon}
blows up;
In Sect. 3, we prove the main theorem. Throughout this paper, sequence and its subsequence will
not be distinguished and the constant
C
may change from line to line.

2
The lower bound

In this section, we shall derive an explicit lower bound of
J
in
H_{1}
when
u_{\epsilon}
blows up,
so we assume
u_{\epsilon}
blows up throughout this section. First of all, we have

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

C_{1}\leq\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\leq C_{2}.

Proof.

Since
u_{\epsilon}\in H_{1}
, one has
\int_{M}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}=1
and then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\max_{M}h}\leq\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}.

Since
H_{1}\neq\emptyset
, we choose
u_{0}\in H_{1}
, then
J_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})=\inf_{H_{1}}J_{\epsilon}(u)\leq J_{\epsilon}(u_{0})\to J(u_{0})\leq C
, then the Moser-Trudinger inequality (
1.2
) and Jensen’s inequality yield

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}}+C

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}J_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})+\frac{\epsilon}{8\pi}\overline{u_{\epsilon}}+C

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{8\pi}\log\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+C.

So we have
\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\leq C
.
∎

By Lemma
2.1
, one knows that Lemma 2.3 in
[
5
]
holds, i.e.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
1<q<2
,
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{q}\leq C_{q}
.

Concerning
u_{\epsilon}
blows up, we have the following equivalent characterizations.

Lemma 2.3
.

There holds

\displaystyle\lambda_{\epsilon}\rightarrow+\infty\Leftrightarrow\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\rightarrow+\infty\Leftrightarrow\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\rightarrow-\infty.

Proof.

Since
J_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})
is bounded, the second equivalent relation is obvious. We put our attention on the first one.
If
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\rightarrow+\infty
, then one has
\lambda_{\epsilon}\rightarrow+\infty
by Lemma 2.4 in
[
5
]
.
If
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\leq C
, then
\overline{u_{\epsilon}}
is bounded. One has
e^{u_{\epsilon}}
is bounded in
L^{q}(M)
for any
q\geq 1
by the Moser-Trudinger inequality.
Then by the standard elliptic estimates, we have
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}
is bounded. Therefore
\lambda_{\epsilon}\leq C
. This ends the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We call
u_{\epsilon}
blows up, if one of the three items in Lemma
2.3
holds.

By Brezis-Merle’s lemma (
[
1
]
, Theorem 1) and following elliptic estimates as the proof of Lemma 2.8 in
[
5
]
, one has

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a domain. If for some
\gamma\in(0,1/2)

\int_{\Omega}|h|e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}<\frac{1}{2}-\gamma,

then

\|u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\|_{L_{\text{loc}}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Due to Lemma
2.5
, we define the blowup set of
u_{\epsilon}
as

\displaystyle S=\{x\in M:~\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|h|e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\geq\frac{1}{2}\}.

(2.1)

The following lemma is very important to us. It asserts that
S
is a single point set.

Lemma 2.6
.

S=\{p\}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three parts.

1.
S\neq\emptyset
.

If not,
\forall x\in M
,
\exists\delta=\delta_{x}\in(0,1/2)
,
\exists r=r_{x}\in(0,\text{inj}(M))
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{x}}(x)}|h|e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}<\frac{1}{2}-\delta.

Then Lemma
2.5
tells us that

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{x}/2}(x))}\leq C.

Combining with a finite covering argument, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C.

Multiplying equation (
1.4
) with
u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}
and integrating on
M
, integrating by parts

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\leq C,

where in the last inequality we have used Lemma
2.1
. This contradiction with
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\rightarrow+\infty
tells us that
S
is not empty.

2.
\sharp S=1
.

If not, since
S\neq\emptyset
, we have
\sharp S\geq 2
. Choosing
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in S
, one has for sufficiently small
r
that

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\geq\frac{1}{2C_{2}|h|_{\text{max}}},~~\frac{\int_{B_{r}(x_{2})}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\geq\frac{1}{2C_{2}|h|_{\text{max}}}.

Then by the distribution lemma of Chen-Li (
[
4
]
, Theorem 2.1), we obtain

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\leq(\frac{1}{32\pi}+\epsilon^{\prime})\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}}+C.

Hence

\displaystyle C\geq J_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}+(8\pi-\epsilon)\overline{u_{\epsilon}}-(8\pi-\epsilon)\log\int_{M}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}-(8\pi-\epsilon)\log h_{\text{max}}-(8\pi-\epsilon)(\frac{1}{32\pi}+\epsilon^{\prime})\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{6}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}-C.

It follows
\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}\leq C
, this contradiction tells us that
\sharp S=1
.

3.
S=\{p\}
.

Recall that
u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})=\max_{M}u_{\epsilon}
, and
x_{\epsilon}\rightarrow p\in M
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

If
p\notin S
, then
\exists\delta_{p}>0
and
r_{p}\in(0,\text{inj}(M))
such that

\int_{B_{r_{p}}(p)}|h|e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}<\frac{1}{2}-\delta_{p}.

Then by Lemma
2.5
, one has

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{p}/2}(p))}\leq C.

Hence

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})\leq C+\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\leq C,

where in the last inequality we have used Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
2.1
.
On the other hand, for
x\in S
and small
r

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})=\max_{M}u_{\epsilon}\geq\max_{B_{r}(x)}u_{\epsilon}\rightarrow+\infty~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

This contradiction tells us that
p\in S
, concluding we know
S=\{p\}
.
∎

Still, we can show that at the blow-up point,
h
is positive. Namely,

Lemma 2.7
.

h(p)>0
.

Proof.

By the definition of
S
and Lemma
2.1
, one has
h(p)\neq 0
. Now we just need to exclude
h(p)
is negative. We do this by the maximum principle.
If
h(p)<0
, then
h(x_{\epsilon})<0
for sufficiently small
\epsilon
. Therefore, at
x_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\Delta u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})=(8\pi-\epsilon)(1-h(x_{\epsilon})e^{u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})})>0.

This contradicts with the fact that
u_{\epsilon}
attains its maximum at
x_{\epsilon}
. So we have
h(p)>0
.
∎

We choose a local normal coordinate system around
p
, denote
r_{\epsilon}=e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon}/2}
and define

\displaystyle\varphi_{\epsilon}(x)=u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon}+r_{\epsilon}x)-\lambda_{\epsilon}.

Since
h(p)>0
, the proof of Lemma 2.5 in
[
5
]
tells us that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\epsilon}(x)\rightarrow\varphi_{0}(x)=-2\log(1+\pi h(p)|x|^{2})~~\text{in}~~C_{\text{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.2)

Calculating directly, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h(p)e^{\varphi_{0}}dx=1.

(2.3)

Since
S=\{p\}
, for any
x\in M\setminus\{p\}
, there exists a
\gamma_{x}\in(0,1/2)
and a small
r_{x}\in(0,\text{dist}(x,p)/2)
such that

\int_{B_{r_{x}}(x)}|h|e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}<\frac{1}{2}-\gamma_{x}.

By Lemma
2.5
,
\|u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{B_{r_{x}/2}(x)}}\leq C
, then by Lemma
2.3
we have

u_{\epsilon}(x)\leq C+\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\rightarrow-\infty
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. So for any
\Omega\subset\subset M\setminus\{p\}
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|h|e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\rightarrow 0~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.4)

By (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we can see that,
he^{u_{\epsilon}}
converges to
\delta_{p}
in the sense of measure. Therefore
u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\rightarrow G_{p}(x)
weakly in
W^{1,q}(M)
for any
1<q<2
, where
G_{p}
is the Green function satisfying (
1.5
), since
G_{p}
is the only solution of (
1.5
) in
W^{1,q}(M)
. Lemma
2.5

and (
2.4
) yield that for any
\Omega\subset\subset M\setminus\{p\}
,

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C.

This inequality together with the standard elliptic estimates yields that

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\rightarrow G_{p}~~\text{in}~~C_{\text{loc}}^{2}(M\setminus\{p\})~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.5)

Remark 2.8
.

Even with a lot of effort, (
2.2
) and (
2.5
) show Fact 1 and Fact 2 in the introduction are still right when
h
changes sign.
However, Fact 3 in the introduction is out of reach since
h
changes sign and the maximum principle does not work on the whole
M\setminus B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})
.

However, since
h(p)>0
, we can still use the maximum principle on a small neck neighborhood of
x_{\epsilon}
to build the estimate like Fact 3. Fortunately, it is enough to estimate the energy on the neck domain.

First, we need to show

Lemma 2.9
.

There exists a constant
C_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\epsilon}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\geq-C_{3}.

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.4
) with
u_{\epsilon}
and integrating on
M
, one has

\displaystyle-\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}=(8\pi-\epsilon)\overline{u_{\epsilon}}-(8\pi-\epsilon)\int_{M}he^{u_{\epsilon}}u_{\epsilon}dv_{g}

(2.6)

Since
u_{\epsilon}\in H_{1}
, one has by (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle 2J_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle(8\pi-\epsilon)\int_{M}he^{u_{\epsilon}}(u_{\epsilon}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}})dv_{g}.

(2.7)

It follows from Lemma
2.5
that
\|u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M\setminus B_{\delta}(p))}\leq C
, from Lemma
2.3
that
\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\rightarrow-\infty
, so

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B_{\delta}(p)}he^{u_{\epsilon}}(u_{\epsilon}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}})dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B_{\delta}(p)}he^{u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}}e^{\overline{u_{\epsilon}}}(u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}})dv_{g}+2\int_{M\setminus B_{\delta}(p)}he^{u_{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}}e^{\overline{u_{\epsilon}}}\overline{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o_{\epsilon}(1)~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.8)

By (
2.4
),

\int_{M\setminus B_{\delta}(p)}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}=o_{\epsilon}(1)~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

This together with
u_{\epsilon}\in H_{1}
tells us that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}=1+o_{\epsilon}(1)~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.9)

Choose
\delta>0
small such that
h>0
in
B_{\delta}(p)
, one obtains by (
2.9
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}he^{u_{\epsilon}}(u_{\epsilon}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}})dv_{g}\leq(\lambda_{\epsilon}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}})\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}he^{u_{\epsilon}}=(\lambda_{\epsilon}+\overline{u_{\epsilon}})(1+o_{\epsilon}(1))~~\text{as}~~\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.10)

Taking (
2
) and (
2.10
) into (
2.7
), we complete the proof by noticing
J_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})
is bounded.
∎

In the following, we let
\delta>0
small enough such that
h>0
and (
1.6
) holds for
G_{x_{\epsilon}}
in
B_{\delta}(x_{\epsilon})
.
Recall that
r_{\epsilon}=e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon}/2}
and
R>0
. Due to the maximum principle, we have the following estimate on the neck domain.

Lemma 2.10
.

There exists a constant
C_{4}
, such that in
B_{\delta}(x_{\epsilon})\setminus B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}\geq G_{x_{\epsilon}}-\lambda_{\epsilon}-C_{4}+o_{\epsilon}(1)+o_{R}(1).

Proof.

It is clear that for any constant
C

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{\epsilon}-G_{x_{\epsilon}}+\lambda_{\epsilon}-C)=-\epsilon-(8\pi-\epsilon)he^{u_{\epsilon}}<0~~\text{in}~~B_{\delta}(x_{\epsilon})\setminus B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon}).

(2.11)

By (
2.2
) and (
1.6
) one has on
\partial B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})
that

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\epsilon}-2\log(1+\pi h(p)R^{2})+o_{\epsilon}(1),

\displaystyle G_{x_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=-4\log(Rr_{\epsilon})+A_{x_{\epsilon}}+o_{\epsilon}(1).

Then

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}=G_{x_{\epsilon}}-\lambda_{\epsilon}-2\log\frac{1+\pi h(p)R^{2}}{R^{2}}-A_{x_{\epsilon}}+o_{\epsilon}(1)~~\text{on}~~\partial B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon}).

(2.12)

By (
2.5
) and (
1.6
) one has on
\partial B_{\delta}(x_{\epsilon})
that

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\overline{u_{\epsilon}}+G_{p}+o_{\epsilon}(1),

\displaystyle G_{x_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=G_{p}+o_{\epsilon}(1).

Then we have

u_{\epsilon}=\overline{u_{\epsilon}}+G_{x_{\epsilon}}+o_{\epsilon}(1)~~\text{on}~~\partial B_{\delta}(x_{\epsilon}).

This and Lemma
2.9
yield

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}\geq G_{x_{\epsilon}}-\lambda_{\epsilon}-C_{3}+o_{\epsilon}(1)~~\text{on}~~\partial B_{\delta}(x_{\epsilon}).

(2.13)

From (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), one can choose a large number
C_{4}
such that for small enough
\epsilon>0
and large enough
R>0

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}\geq G_{x_{\epsilon}}-\lambda_{\epsilon}-C_{4}~~\text{on}~~\partial\left(B_{\delta}(x_{\epsilon})\setminus B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})\right).

(2.14)

Combining (
2.11
) and (
2.14
), the lemma follows by the maximum principle.
∎

We still need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.11
.

For any
\alpha>1
, there holds

\displaystyle\lambda_{\epsilon}+\alpha\overline{u_{\epsilon}}\leq C.

Proof.

Let
\alpha>1
be fixed. By the Moser-Trudinger inequality (
1.2
) one has

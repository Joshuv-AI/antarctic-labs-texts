Short retractions of CAT(1) spaces
Alexander Lytchak, Anton Petrunin

Abstract
We construct short retractions of a CAT(1) space to its small convex subsets. This construction provides an alternative geometric description of an analytic tool introduced by Wilfrid Kendall. Our construction uses a tractrix flow which can be defined as a gradient flow for a family of functions of certain type. In an appendix we prove a general existence result for gradient flows of time-dependent locally Lipschitz semiconcave functions, which is of independent interest.

Introduction

Recall that a subset

Let

1.1. Theorem.

Suppose

(b)

These observations became a useful tool to study the Dirichlet problem and its relatives;

Our original goal was to make this tool transparent for geometers.

Appendix A
Time-dependent gradient flow

Here we prove the existence, uniqueness and contractivity of the gradient flow for a time-dependent family of functions.
The proof relies mostly on the corresponding statements for time-independent families — with minor conditions on the space.
The same proof works nearly without changes for spaces with lower curvature bound
and it should work in nearly any space with well defined angles between geodesics starting at one point.

The scalar product

A.1. Proposition.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(\kappa)
space and

f
a Lipschitz and semiconcave function defined on an open set
\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f\subset{\mathcal{U}}
.
Then for any point
p\in\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f

there is a unique
f
-gradient curve
t\mapsto\alpha(t)
with initial point
\alpha(0)=p
defined on a maximal semiopen interval
[0,T)
for some
T\in(0,\infty]
.
Moreover if
T<\infty
then
\alpha
escapes from any closed set
K\subset\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f
.

Time-dependent gradient flow.

Our next aim is to define a gradient flow for a time-dependent family of functions and prove an analog of Proposition
A
for this flow.
Let
f_{t}
be a family of functions defined on open subsets
\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f_{t}
of
\mathcal{U}
.
More precisely, we assume that the parameter
t
lies in a real interval
\mathbb{I}
and

\Omega=\{\,{(x,t)\in\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{I}}\,:\,{x\in\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f_{t}}\,\}

is an open subset in
\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{I}
.

A family of functions
f_{t}
is called
Lipschitz
if
the function
(x,t)\mapsto f_{t}(x)
is
L
-Lipschitz for some constant
L
.

A family of functions
f_{t}
will be called
semiconcave
if
the function
x\mapsto f_{t}(x)
is
\lambda
-concave for each
t
.
A family
f_{t}
is called
locally semiconcave
if for each
(p_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega
there is a neighborhood
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that the restriction of
f_{t}
to
\Omega^{\prime}
is
\lambda
-concave.

Note that one cannot expect that a direct generalization of equation
➋
holds for any family of functions
f_{t}
.

For example, consider a
1
-Lipschitz curve
\alpha
in the real line.
It is reasonable to assume that
\alpha
is an
f_{t}
-gradient curve for the family
f_{t}(x)=-|x-\alpha(t)|
.
(Indeed
\alpha
can be realized as a limit of gradient curves for a family of functions obtained by smoothing
f_{t}
.)
On the other hand,
\alpha^{+}(t)
might be undefined,
but even if it is defined,
\alpha^{+}(t)\neq 0
in general, while
\nabla_{\alpha(t)}f_{t}\equiv 0
.

Instead we define
f_{t}
-gradient curve
as a Lipschitz curve
\alpha
that satisfies the following inequality
for any point
p
, time
t
, and
small
\varepsilon>0
:

\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t+\varepsilon)\leqslant\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t)-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}d_{\alpha(t)}f_{t}({\uparrow}_{\![{\alpha(t)}{p}]})+o(\varepsilon),

➌

where
{\uparrow}_{\![{q}{p}]}\in\mathrm{T}_{q}
denotes a unit tangent vector at
q
in the direction of
p

(if there is no geodesic
[\alpha(t)\,p]
then we impose no condition).

If
\alpha^{+}(t)=\nabla_{\alpha(t)}f_{t}
for all
t
, then
➌
holds;
it follows from
➊
.
On the other hand, the example above shows that the converse does not hold;
that is,
➌
generalizes the definition
➋
.
Our defining inequality
➌
is closely related to the so called
evolution variational inequality

[
27
, Thm 4.0.4(iii)]
.

A.2. Distance estimate.

Let
f_{t}
and
h_{t}
be two families of
\lambda
-concave functions on a
\mathrm{CAT}(\kappa)
space
\mathcal{U}
and
s\geqslant 0
.
Assume
f_{t}
and
h_{t}
have common domain
\Omega\subset{\mathcal{U}}\times\mathbb{R}
and
|f_{t}(x)-h_{t}(x)|\leqslant s
for any
(x,t)\in\Omega
.
Assume
t\mapsto\alpha(t)
and
t\mapsto\beta(t)
are
f_{t}
- and
h_{t}
-gradient curves respectively defined on a common interval
t\in[a,b)
; set
\ell(t)=\penalty|\alpha(t)-\penalty\beta(t)|_{\mathcal{U}}
.
If for all
t
a minimizing geodesic
[\alpha(t)\,\beta(t)]
lies in
\{\,{x\in{\mathcal{U}}}\,:\,{(x,t)\in\Omega}\,\}
, then

\ell^{\prime}(t)\leqslant\lambda{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\ell(t)+2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}s/\ell(t),

➍

assuming that the left hand side is defined.
Moreover

\ell(t)^{2}+\tfrac{2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}s}{\lambda}\leqslant(\ell(a)^{2}+\tfrac{2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}s}{\lambda}){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}e^{2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\lambda{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}(t-a)}.

➎

In particular the inequalities hold for any
t\in\mathbb{I}
if
\Omega\supset B(p,2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}r)\times\mathbb{I}
and
\alpha(t),\beta(t)\in\penalty B(p,r)
for any
t\in\mathbb{I}
.

Note that if
f_{t}=h_{t}
then
s=0
;
in this case the second inequality can be written as

\ell(t)\leqslant\ell(a){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}e^{\lambda{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}(t-a)}.

➏

In particular it implies uniqueness of the future of gradient curve with given initial data.
This inequality also makes it possible to estimate the distance between two gradient curves for close functions.
In particular, it implies convergence of
f_{t}^{n}
-gradient curves if a sequence of
L
-Lipschitz and
\lambda
-concave families
f^{n}_{t}
converges uniformly as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Fix a time moment
t
and set
f=f_{t}
and
h=h_{t}
.
Let
p
be the midpoint of the geodesic
[\alpha(t)\beta(t)]
.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,\ell(t)]\to\mathcal{U}
be an arc length parametrization of
[\alpha(t)\beta(t)]
from
\alpha(t)
to
\beta(t)
.
Note that
d_{\alpha(t)}f({\uparrow}_{\![{\alpha(t)}{p}]})
is the right derivative of
f\circ\gamma
at
0

and
-d_{\alpha(t)}h({\uparrow}_{\![{\beta(t)}{p}]})
is the left derivative of
h\circ\gamma
at
\ell(t)
.
Since
f
and
h
are
\lambda
-concave,

\displaystyle f(\beta(t))

\displaystyle\leqslant f(\alpha(t))+\ell(t){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}d_{\alpha(t)}f({\uparrow}_{\![{\alpha(t)}{p}]})+\tfrac{1}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\lambda{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\ell(t)^{2},

\displaystyle h(\alpha(t))

\displaystyle\leqslant h(\beta(t))+\ell(t){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}d_{\alpha(t)}h({\uparrow}_{\![{\beta(t)}{p}]})+\tfrac{1}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\lambda{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\ell(t)^{2},

Adding these inequalities up and taking into account that

for any

d_{\alpha(t)}f({\uparrow}_{\![{\alpha(t)}{p}]})+d_{\alpha(t)}h({\uparrow}_{\![{\beta(t)}{p}]})\geqslant\lambda{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\ell(t)+2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}s/\ell(t).

Applying the triangle inequality and the definition of gradient curve at
p
, we get that

\displaystyle\ell(t+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=|\alpha(t+\varepsilon)-\beta(t+\varepsilon)|\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant|\alpha(t+\varepsilon)-p|+|\beta(t+\varepsilon)-p|\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant|\alpha(t)-p|-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}d_{\alpha(t)}f({\uparrow}_{\![{\alpha(t)}{p}]})+|\beta(t+\varepsilon)-p|-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}d_{\beta(t)}h({\uparrow}_{\![{\beta(t)}{p}]})+o(\varepsilon)=

\displaystyle=\ell(t)-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}(\lambda{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\ell(t)+2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}s/\ell(t))+o(\varepsilon)

for
\varepsilon>0
; hence the statement.

Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are Lipschitz,
t\mapsto\ell(t)
is a Lipschitz function.
By Rademacher’s theorem, its derivative
\ell^{\prime}
is defined almost everywhere and it satisfies the fundamental theorem of calculus.
Therefore
➍
implies
➎
.
∎

A.3. Proposition.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(\kappa)
space.
Suppose
f_{t}
is a family of
\lambda
-concave functions for
t\in[a,b)
and
\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f_{t}\supset B(z,2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}r)
for some fixed
z\in\mathcal{U}
,
r>0
and any
t
.

Let
\alpha\colon[a,b)\to B(z,r)
be Lipschitz.
Then
\alpha
is an
f_{t}
-gradient curve if and only if

\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t+\varepsilon)\leqslant\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t)-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\left[\frac{f_{t}(p)-f_{t}\circ\alpha(t)}{|p-\alpha(t)|}-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}|p-\alpha(t)|\right]+o(\varepsilon)

➐

for any
t\in[a,b)
and
p\in B(z,r)\setminus\{\alpha(t)\}
.

Proof.

Note that geodesics
[\alpha(t)p]
lie in
\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f_{t}
for any
t
.

Since
f_{t}
is
\lambda
-concave, we have

d_{\alpha(t)}f_{t}({\uparrow}_{\![{\alpha(t)}{p}]})\geqslant\frac{f(p)-f\circ\alpha(t)}{|p-\alpha(t)|}-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}|p-\alpha(t)|.

Hence the only-if part follows.

Given a point
p\in\mathcal{U}
and
t
,
consider a point
\bar{p}\in[\alpha(t)p]
.
Applying
➐
for
\bar{p}
and the triangle inequality, we get

\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t+\varepsilon)\leqslant\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t)-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\left[\frac{f(\bar{p})-f\circ\alpha(t)}{|\bar{p}-\alpha(t)|}-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}|\bar{p}-\alpha(t)|\right]+o(\varepsilon).

By taking
\bar{p}
close to
\alpha(t)
,
the value
\tfrac{f(\bar{p})-f\circ\alpha(t)}{|\bar{p}-\alpha(t)|}-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}|\bar{p}-\alpha(t)|
can be made arbitrary close to

.
Therefore, given

, the following inequality

\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t+\varepsilon)\leqslant\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\alpha(t)-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}d_{\alpha(t)}f_{t}({\uparrow}_{\![{\alpha(t)}{p}]})+\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\delta

.
Therefore
➌
holds.
∎

A.4. Theorem.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(\kappa)
space and

\{f_{t}\}
a family of function defined on an open set
\Omega=\{\,{(x,t)\in\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{R}}\,:\,{x\in\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f_{t}}\,\}
.
Suppose that
f_{t}
is Lipschitz and locally semiconcave.
Then for any time moment
a
and initial point
p\in\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f_{a}
there is a unique
f_{t}
-gradient curve
t\mapsto\alpha(t)
defined on a maximal semiopen interval
[a,b)
.
Moreover if
b<\infty
, then
(\alpha(t),t)
escapes from any closed set
K\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a Lipschitz constant of
f_{t}
.
Fix
b>a
sufficiently small so that
\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits f_{t}\supset\penalty B(p,\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}L)
for any
t\in[a,b)
.
Consider sequence
a=t_{0}<t_{1}\dots<t_{n}=\penalty b
and a piecewise constant family of functions on
B(p,\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}L)
defined by
\hat{f}_{t}=f_{t_{i}}
if
t_{i}\leqslant t<t_{i+1}
.

Note that
\hat{f}_{t}
is time-independent on each interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1})
.
Applying
A
recursively on each interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1})
, we get that the proposition holds for
\hat{f}_{t}
.
That is, there is a unique
\hat{f}_{t}
-gradient curve
\hat{\alpha}
that starts at
p
and defined on the interval
[a,b)
.

The distance estimates (
A
) show that as the partition gets finer, the gradient curves
\hat{\alpha}
form a Cauchy sequence; denote its limit by
\alpha
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\hat{\alpha}(t+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\hat{\alpha}(t)-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\left[\frac{\hat{f}_{t}(p)-\hat{f}_{t}\circ\hat{\alpha}(t)}{|p-\alpha(t)|}-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}|p-\hat{\alpha}(t)|\right]+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{p}\circ\hat{\alpha}(t)-\varepsilon{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}\left[\frac{f_{t}(p)-f_{t}\circ\hat{\alpha}(t)-2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}s}{|p-\alpha(t)|}-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}|p-\hat{\alpha}(t)|\right]+o(\varepsilon)

where

s=\sup_{t,x}\{|f_{t}(x)-\hat{f}_{t}(x)|\}.

Since
s\to 0
as
\hat{\alpha}\to\alpha
, we get that
➐
holds for
\alpha
;
that is,
\alpha
is an
f_{t}
-gradient curve.

It proves a short time existence.
Applying this argument recursively we can find a gradient curve defined on a maximal interval
[a,b)
.
Uniqueness of this curve follows from the distance estimate
➏
.

Note that
\alpha
is
L
-Lipschitz.
In particular, if
b<\infty
, then
\alpha(t)\to p^{\prime}
as
t\to b
.
If
(p^{\prime},b)\in\Omega
, then we can repeat the procedure, otherwise
\alpha
escapes from any closed set in
\Omega
.
∎

Appendix B
Another way

Here we indicate an alternative way to prove Theorem
1
.
The idea is taken from the proof of Kirszbraun’s theorem; see
[
28
, 5.1]
or
[
9
, 9.4.1 and 9.6.5]
.

Proof of
1
.

Set
\mathring{\mathcal{U}}=\mathop{\rm Cone}\nolimits\mathcal{U}
.
Denote by
\mathring{K}
the subcone of
\mathring{\mathcal{U}}
spanned by
K
.
The space
\mathcal{U}
is the unit sphere in
\mathring{\mathcal{U}}
with angle metric;
that is,
\mathcal{U}=\{\,{z\in\mathring{\mathcal{U}}}\,:\,{|z|=1}\,\}
and
|u-v|_{\mathcal{U}}=\measuredangle[o\,{}^{u}_{v}]
, where
o
denotes the tip of the cone
\mathring{\mathcal{U}}
.

Note that
\mathring{\mathcal{U}}
is a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space and
\mathring{K}
forms a convex closed subset of
\mathring{\mathcal{U}}
.
In particular, for any point
x
there is unique point
\hat{x}\in\mathring{K}

that minimize the distance to
x
;
that is,
|\hat{x}-x|=\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{K}(x)
.

Consider the ray
\alpha_{o}\colon t\mapsto t{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}p
in
\mathring{\mathcal{U}}
.
Note that given
s\in\mathring{\mathcal{U}}

the geodesics
[s\ t{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\penalty\hskip 0.5pt}p]
converge as

to a ray,
say
\alpha_{s}\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathring{\mathcal{U}}
that is parallel to
\alpha_{o}
.

Note that if
|x|=1
, then
|\hat{x}|\leqslant 1
.
By assumption for any
k\in\mathring{K}
the function
t\mapsto|\alpha_{k}(t)|
is monotonically increasing.
Therefore there is unique value

such that

|\alpha_{\hat{x}}(t_{x})|=1

set

and define the map

as

.

Evidently

is a retraction of
\mathcal{U}
to

;
that is,

for any

and

\Phi(k)=k
for any

.

It remains to show that

is short which can be done by straightforward lengthy computations.
We leave it as an advanced exercise to the reader.
∎

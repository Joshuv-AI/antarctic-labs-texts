The Coulomb gauge in non-associative gauge theory
Sergey Grigorian

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to extend existence results for the Coulomb gauge from standard gauge theory to a non-associative setting. Non-associative gauge theory is based on smooth loops, which are the non-associative analogs of Lie groups. The main components of the theory include a finite-dimensional smooth loop 𝕃 \mathbb{L} , its tangent algebra 𝔩 , \mathfrak{l}, a finite-dimensional Lie group Ψ \Psi , that is the pseudoautomorphism group of 𝕃 \mathbb{L} , a smooth manifold M M with a principal Ψ \Psi -bundle 𝒫 \mathcal{P} , and associated bundles 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} and 𝒜 \mathcal{A} with fibers 𝕃 \mathbb{L} and 𝔩 \mathfrak{l} , respectively. A configuration in this theory is defined as a pair ( s , ω ) \left(s,\omega\right) , where s s is a section of ℚ \mathbb{Q} and ω \omega is a connection on 𝒫 \mathcal{P} . The torsion T ( s , ω ) T^{\left(s,\omega\right)} is the key object in the theory, with a role similar to that of a connection in standard gauge theory. The original motivation for this study comes from G 2 G_{2} -geometry, and the questions of existence of G 2 G_{2} -structures with particular torsion types. In particular, given a fixed connection, we prove existence of configurations with divergence-free torsion, given a sufficiently small torsion in a Sobolev norm.



1
Introduction

The main goal of this work is to extend results on
existence of Coulomb gauge transformations from standard gauge theory to a

non-associative
setting. One of highly successful areas at the
intersection of differential geometry, analysis, and mathematical physics is
gauge theory, which, as it is well-known, is the study of connections on
bundles with particular Lie groups as the structure groups. In
[
26
]
, the author initiated a theory of smooth loops, which are
non-associative analogs of Lie groups, and began the development of gauge
theory based on loops, i.e. a non-associative gauge theory. The key example
of a non-associative smooth loop is the loop of unit octonions. A
non-associative gauge theory has the following components:

1.

A finite-dimensional smooth (right) loop
\mathbb{L}
, i.e. a smooth
manifold with a right multiplication diffeomorphism
R_{p}
defined for
every
p\in\mathbb{L}
and a distinguished identity element
1\in\mathbb{L},
with tangent algebra
\mathfrak{l}
at identity.

2.

A finite-dimensional Lie group
\Psi
that is the
pseudoautomorphism
group of
\mathbb{L}
, a non-associative generalization
of an automorphism group.

3.

A smooth manifold
M
with a principal
\Psi
-bundle
\mathcal{P},

and associated bundles
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{A}
with fibers
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathfrak{l},
respectively.

4.

A configuration is defined by a pair
\left(s,\omega\right),
where

s
is a section of
\mathcal{Q}
and
\omega
is a connection on
\mathcal{P}.
Together they define the
torsion
T^{\left(s,\omega\right)},

which is an
\mathcal{A}
-valued
1
-form on
M.
The torsion is then the
key object in the theory, in the same way that a connection is the key
object in standard gauge theory.

5.

In addition to standard gauge transformations of
\omega
by
\Psi,

we now also have transformations of
s
induced by loop multiplication. Both
of these kinds of transformations induce transformations of the torsion.

The original motivation for studying non-associative gauge theories comes
from
G_{2}
-geometry
[
24
]
. A
G_{2}
-structure on a
7
-dimensional Riemannian manifold is a reduction of the structure group of
the orthornormal frame bundle from
SO\left(7\right)
to
G_{2},
which is
the automorphism group of the octonions. A further relationship between
G_{2}
-structures and the octonions is that unit norm sections of an
octonion bundle parametrize
G_{2}
-structures that are associated with the
same metric, also known as
isometric
G_{2}
-structures. A defining
characteristic of a
G_{2}
-structure is its torsion, and one of the
unanswered questions in
G_{2}
-geometry is which torsion types are
admissible within a fixed metric class. One of the main goals in the theory
of
G_{2}
-structures is to obtain existence results for torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures, similar to the Yau’s Theorem
[
49
]
, that settled the
existence question for Calabi-Yau manifolds. While this goal is formulated
in terms of
G_{2}
-structures, the real question is the existence of a
Riemannian metric with holonomy group equal to
G_{2}.
The fact that for
each metric there exists an entire family of compatible
G_{2}
-structures
presents a degeneracy in this problem. Some of the existing approaches
involve Laplacian flows of
G_{2}
-structures
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
23
,
21
,
33
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
, with the hope of a flow eventually converging to a torsion-free
G_{2}
-structure. As shown in
[
21
]
, the Laplacian flow of a
generic
G_{2}
-structure has a component that moves within a metric class,
and that component is precisely given by
\mathop{\rm div}T.
Laplacian
flows have been more successful for
closed
G_{2}
-structures, in
which case
\mathop{\rm div}T
automatically vanishes, and thus the
degeneracy is resolved. More generally, however, this degeneracy is the
source of non-parabolicity of Laplacian flows, such as in the case of
co-closed

G_{2}
-structures
[
21
]
. Therefore, the
condition
\mathop{\rm div}T=0
can be regarded as a
gauge-fixing
condition
. Moreover, in
[
24
]
, it was found that on a
compact manifold,
G_{2}
-structures with
\mathop{\rm div}T=0
are
precisely the critical points of the
L^{2}
-norm of the torsion when
restricted to a fixed metric class. As shown in
[
24
,
26
]
, within the loop bundle framework, this is the precise
analog of the Coulomb gauge condition.

Existence of
G_{2}
-structures with divergence-free torsion has been
studied from different perspectives by several authors: Bagaglini in
[
3

author in

-structures (or more generally, geometric structures in

-structure

The interpretation of isometric

-structures as an octonionic
non-associative gauge theory allows to adapt some gauge theory techniques in
this setting. Moreover, without much additional effort, more general loops
can be considered, with potential wider-reaching applications.

Theorem A

Suppose
\mathbb{L}
is a smooth compact loop with tangent
algebra
\mathfrak{l}
and pseudoautomorphism group
\Psi.
Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a closed, smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension

and let
\mathcal{P}
be a
\Psi

and let
\mathcal{A}
be the associated vector bundle to
\mathcal{P}
with fibers
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{l}
. Let
\omega
be a smooth connection on
\mathcal{P}.
Also, suppose

is a non-negative integer and

such

kr>n.
Then, there exist constants

such that if

is
a smooth defining section for which

\left\|T^{\left(s,\omega\right)}\right\|_{W^{k-1,r}}<\delta,

then there exists a section

such that

\left(d^{\omega}\right)^{\ast}T^{\left(As,\omega\right)}=0

and

\left\|T^{\left(As,\omega\right)}\right\|_{W^{k-1,r}}<K\left\|T^{\left(s,\omega\right)}\right\|_{W^{k-1,r}}\left(1+\left\|T^{\left(s,\omega\right)}\right\|_{W^{k-1,r}}^{k-1}\right).

(1.1)

If moreover,
\left(k-1\right)r\geq n,
then
A
is smooth.

For
G_{2}
-structures, this gives the following result for existence of

-structures with divergence-free torsion.

Theorem B

Suppose

Suppose

\left\|T^{\left(V\right)}\right\|_{W^{k,r}}<K\left\|T\right\|_{W^{k,r}}\left(1+\left\|T\right\|_{W^{k,r}}^{k}\right).

(1.2)

The results presented in this paper are of interest and importance in their
own right, but perhaps even more crucially, they show that some well-known
results and techniques from classical gauge theory can be reinterpreted and
adapted in a non-associative setting. In particular, this may open the door
to some analogues of Uhlenbeck compactness and a better understanding of the
torsion of non-associative gauge theories. Furthermore, a non-associative
version of Yang-Mills equations can be considered. Moreover, any such
advances will give immediate results in
G_{2}
-geometry.

Smooth Loops

For a detailed introduction to

Definition 2.1

A
loop
\mathbb{L}
is a set with a binary operation
p\cdot q
with
identity
1,
and compatible left and right quotients
p\backslash q
and
p/q
, respectively.

In particular, existence of quotients is equivalent to saying that for any
q\in\mathbb{L},
the left and right product maps
L_{q}
and
R_{q}
are
invertible maps. Restricting to the smooth category, we obtain the
definition of a smooth loop.

Definition 2.2

A
smooth loop
is a smooth manifold
\mathbb{L}
with a loop structure
such that the left and right product maps are diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{L}.

Remark 2.3

In this paper we will not use the left quotient, so in fact everything that
follows also holds true for smooth
right
loops, i.e. where only the
right quotient is defined, but the left product is not necessarily
invertible. However, for brevity, we will keep referring to
loops
,
rather than
right loops
. As Example
2.4
below shows,
smooth right loops are plentiful and easy to construct.

Example 2.4

Suppose
G
is a Lie group with a Lie subgroup
H
and
consider the left quotient
K=\scalebox{-1}[1]{$\nicefrac{{\scalebox{-1}[1]{$G$}}}{{\scalebox{-1}[1]{$H$}}}$}
. Suppose
\sigma:K\longrightarrow G
is a section of
G
, regarded as a bundle over
K.
In
particular,
\sigma
maps each right coset to a particular representative
of that coset. Suppose
\sigma\left(H\right)=1.
A product structure on
K
is then given by

\left(Ha\right)\circ\left(Hb\right)=Ha\left(\sigma\left(Hb\right)\right).

(2.1)

Consider the equation
\left(Hx\right)\circ\left(Ha\right)=Hb,
Since
\sigma
is a section, we can see right away that we have a unique solution

Thus,
\left(\mathbb{L},\circ\right)
has
right
division, and is thus a
right loop

43
]

to obtain a full loop structure, more structure is needed.

Definition 2.5

A
pseudoautomorphism
of a smooth loop
\mathbb{L}

is a diffeomorphism
h:\mathbb{L}\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}
for which there
exists another diffeomorphism

such that

h\left(pq\right)=h^{\prime}\left(p\right)h\left(q\right).

(2.2)

In particular,

and
h^{\prime}\left(1\right)=1.
The element

From (

see the following property of

with respect to quotients:

h^{\prime}\left(p/q\right)=h\left(p\right)/h\left(q\right).

(2.3)

It is easy to see that the sets of pseudoautomorphisms and partial
pseudoautomorphisms are both groups. Denote the former by
\Psi
and the
latter by
\Psi^{\prime}
. We also see that the
automorphism

of the loop

is the subgroup

which is the

On smooth loops, we can define an analog of the Lie group Maurer-Cartan form.

(

For each

As shown in

\displaystyle\left[\xi,\gamma\right]^{\left(p\right)}

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{\exp\left(t\xi\right)}^{\left(p\right)}\gamma\right)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dtd\tau}\exp\left(t\xi\right)\circ_{p}\exp\left(\tau\gamma\right)\right|_{t,\tau=0}

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau d\tau^{\prime}}\left(A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\circ_{s}A\left(t\right)\right)\circ_{s}\left(B\left(\tau\right)\right)/_{s}A\left(t\right)\right|_{\tau^{\prime},\tau=0}

\displaystyle-\left.\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau d\tau^{\prime}}(\left(\left(A\left(t\right)\circ_{s}\left(B\left(\tau\right)\right)\right)/_{s}A\left(t\right)\right)\circ_{s}\left(A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\circ_{s}A\left(t\right)\right))/_{s}A\left(t\right)\right|_{\tau^{\prime},\tau=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\rho_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\left[\xi,A\left(t\right),\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)}+\left.\frac{d}{d\tau^{\prime}}\left(A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\circ_{s}\left(\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\eta\circ_{s}A\left(t\right)\right)\right)/_{s}A\left(t\right)\right|_{\tau^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle-\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}(\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\eta\circ_{s}\left(A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\circ_{s}A\left(t\right)\right))/_{s}A\left(t\right)\right|_{\tau=0}.

Using (

\left.\frac{d}{d\tau^{\prime}}\left(A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\circ_{s}\left(\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\eta\circ_{s}A\left(t\right)\right)\right)/_{s}A\left(t\right)\right|_{\tau^{\prime}=0}=\left.\frac{d}{d\tau^{\prime}}A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\circ_{A\left(t\right)s}x\left(t\right)\right|_{\tau^{\prime}=0}

\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}(\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\eta\circ_{s}\left(A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\circ_{s}A\left(t\right)\right))/_{s}A\left(t\right)\right|_{\tau=0}=\left.\frac{d}{d\tau^{\prime}}x\left(t\right)\circ_{A\left(t\right)s}A\left(\tau^{\prime}\right)\right|_{\tau^{\prime}=0}.

Using (

\frac{dx\left(t\right)}{dt}=\left[\xi,x\left(t\right)\right]^{\left(A\left(t\right)s\right)}-\left(\rho_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\left[\xi,A\left(t\right),\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)}.

(2.35)

This is an inhomogeneous linear first order ODE. The homogeneous part is
precisely (
2.26

2.30

For the estimate, suppose
\mathbb{L}
is compact. Then, using (
2.30
) and (
2.29
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}-U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\xi\right|\int_{0}^{t}\left|U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\left(U_{\tau\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\right|\left|\left(\rho_{A\left(\tau\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\left(\left[\cdot,A\left(\tau\right),\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)\right|d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\xi\right|\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\left|\xi\right|\left(t-\tau\right)}\left|\left(\rho_{A\left(\tau\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\left(\left[\cdot,A\left(\tau\right),\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)\right|d\tau.

However,
\left(A,s\right)\mapsto\left|\rho_{A}^{-1}\left(\left[\cdot,A,\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)\right|
is a
real-valued function on a compact manifold, and hence there exists a
constant

which is the supremum of this function over
\mathbb{L}\times\mathbb{L}.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}-U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\left|\xi\right|\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\left|\xi\right|\left(t-\tau\right)}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\left(e^{C\left|\xi\right|t}-1\right).

Renaming the constant

we get (
2.31
).

Now if
\left(\mathbb{L},\circ_{s}\right)
is left-alternative, the
second term on the right hand side of (

\left[\xi,A\left(t\right),\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)}=\left[\xi,\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right),\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)}=0.

Then,
\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\eta

satisfies the homogeneous equation, so the solution is just
\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\eta=U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\eta.

From (
2.18
), we have

\left[\xi,x\left(t\right)\right]^{\left(A\left(t\right)s\right)}=\left[\xi,x\left(t\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}+\left(\rho_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}a_{s}\left(\xi,x\left(t\right),A\left(t\right)\right).

If
\left(\mathbb{L},\circ_{s}\right)
is left-power-alternative, then
first of all, the associator is skew-symmetric in the first two entries, so

a_{s}\left(\xi,x\left(t\right),A\left(t\right)\right)=-2\left[x\left(t\right),\xi,A\left(t\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)},

however due to right-power-alternativity,
\left[x\left(t\right),\xi,\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}=0.
Hence, in this

\left[\xi,x\left(t\right)\right]^{\left(A\left(t\right)s\right)}=\left[\xi,x\left(t\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}.

Then,
x\left(t\right)
satisfies the first order homogeneous ODE with
constant coefficients:

\frac{dx\left(t\right)}{dt}=\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\left(x\left(t\right)\right),

so
U_{\xi}\left(t\right)=\exp\left(t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right)
and hence the solution is now

x\left(t\right)=\exp\left(t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right)\eta.

Corollary 2.26

If
\left(\mathbb{L},\circ_{s}\right)
is
power-associative, then

1.

\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\xi=\xi
,

2.

\exp_{s}\left(2t\xi\right)=\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\circ_{s}\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right).

Proof.
From (
2.30
),

\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\xi=U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\xi+U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\int_{0}^{t}\left(U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\rho_{A\left(\tau\right)}^{-1}\left(\left[\xi,A\left(\tau\right),\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)d\tau.

(2.36)

However, by power-associativity (
2.16
),

\left[\xi,A\left(\tau\right),\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}=\left[\xi,\exp_{s}\left(\tau\xi\right),\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}=0.

Hence, using Lemma
2.23
, we obtain

\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A\left(t\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\xi=U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\xi=\xi.

For the second part, define

r\left(t\right)=\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\circ\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right).

Then, informally, we write

\displaystyle\frac{dr\left(t\right)}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\circ_{s}\left(\xi\circ_{s}\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\xi\circ_{s}\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\right)\circ_{s}\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right).

Now using power-associativity, we see that
\xi
associates with
\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)
and since
\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)}^{\left(s\right)}\xi=\xi,
moreover
\xi
and
\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)
commute. Hence, we can rewrite

\frac{dr\left(t\right)}{dt}=2\xi\circ_{s}\left(\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\circ_{s}\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)\right)=2\xi\circ_{s}r\left(t\right).

The solution is thus

r\left(t\right)=\exp_{s}\left(2t\xi\right),

so by uniqueness of solutions we then have the needed equality.

\mathfrak{p\cong h}_{s}\oplus\mathfrak{l}^{\left(s\right)}
.

Lemma 2.33
(
[
26
, Lemma 3.33 and Lemma 3.35]
)

Suppose
\xi\in\mathfrak{p}
and
\eta,\gamma\in\mathfrak{l},
then

\displaystyle\xi\cdot\left[\eta,\gamma\right]^{\left(s\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\xi\cdot\eta,\gamma\right]^{\left(s\right)}+\left[\eta,\xi\cdot\gamma\right]^{\left(s\right)}+a_{s}\left(\eta,\gamma,\varphi_{s}\left(\xi\right)\right)

(2.48a)

\displaystyle\xi\cdot\varphi_{s}\left(\eta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\cdot\varphi_{s}\left(\xi\right)+\varphi_{s}\left(\left[\xi,\eta\right]_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)+\left[\varphi_{s}\left(\xi\right),\varphi_{s}\left(\eta\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}.

(2.48b)

Similarly as for Lie groups, we may define a Killing form
K^{\left(s\right)}
on
\mathfrak{l}^{\left(s\right)}
. For
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{l}
, we have

K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\xi,\eta\right)=\mathop{\rm Tr}\left(\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\circ\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\eta}^{\left(s\right)}\right),

(2.49)

where
\circ
is just composition of linear maps on
\mathfrak{l}
and
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\left(\cdot\right)=\left[\xi,\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)}
. Clearly
K^{\left(s\right)}
is a symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{l}.
In
[
26
]

it is shown that for
h\in\Psi,
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{l}
it
satisfies
K^{\left(h\left(s\right)\right)}\left(h_{\ast}^{\prime}\xi,h_{\ast}^{\prime}\eta\right)=K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\xi,\eta\right)
.

General criteria for a loop algebra to admit a non-degenerate Killing form
are currently not known, but it is known
[
37
]
that for a
semisimple

Malcev
algebra, the Killing form is non-degenerate. A

Malcev
algebra is the tangent algebra of a Moufang loop and is an

alternative
algebra that also satisfies the following identity
[
36
,
42
]
:

\left[\xi,\gamma,\left[\xi,\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right]^{\left(s\right)}=\left[\left[\xi,\gamma,\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)},\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}.

(2.50)

Moreover, in this case,
K^{\left(s\right)}
is
\mathfrak{p}
-invariant
and
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{\left(s\right)}
-invariant
[
26
]
. Suppose
s\left(t\right)=\exp_{s}\left(t\gamma\right)s,
then from (
2.24
), we see that generally,

\displaystyle\frac{dK^{\left(s\left(t\right)\right)}}{dt}\left(\xi,\xi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathop{\rm Tr}\left(\left[\xi,\left[\xi,\cdot\right]^{\left(s\left(t\right)\right)}\right]^{\left(s\left(t\right)\right)}\right)\right)

(2.51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathop{\rm Tr}\left(\left[\xi,a_{s\left(t\right)}\left(\xi,\cdot,\gamma\right)\right]^{\left(s\left(t\right)\right)}\right).

In the special case of
\mathbb{L}
being a Moufang loop, and thus every
\mathfrak{l}^{\left(s\right)}
being a Malcev algebra, we have the
following.

Lemma 2.34

Suppose
\mathbb{L}
is a Moufang loop. Then,
K^{\left(s\right)}
is independent of
s
and for each
\gamma\in\mathfrak{l},

then map
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\gamma}^{\left(s\right)}
is
skew-adjoint with respect to
K^{\left(s\right)}.

Proof.
If
\mathbb{L}
is Moufang, then any
\left(\mathbb{L},\circ_{s}\right)

is also a Moufang loop, and hence for any
s
,
\mathfrak{l}^{\left(s\right)}
is a Malcev algebra. Since a Malcev algebra is alternative,
a^{\left(s\right)}\left(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right)=2\left[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)},
and is moreover totally skew-symmetric.
In particular, the Malcev identity (
2.50
) can be written as

a_{s}\left(\xi,\gamma,\left[\xi,\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)=\left[a_{s}\left(\xi,\gamma,\eta\right),\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}.

(2.52)

In particular, taking the trace, we get

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Tr}a_{s}\left(\xi,\gamma,\left[\xi,\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Tr}\left[a_{s}\left(\xi,\gamma,\cdot\right),\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}

(2.53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathop{\rm Tr}\left[a_{s}\left(\xi,\gamma,\left[\xi,\cdot\right]\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Then, (
2.51
) gives
\frac{dK^{\left(s\left(t\right)\right)}}{dt}\left(\xi,\xi\right)=0.
This shows that
K^{\left(s\right)}
is
constant on
\mathbb{L}.

For the second part, from the generalized Jacobi identity (
2.21
), we
obtain

\displaystyle K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\left[\gamma,\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)},\xi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\eta,\left[\gamma,\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)

(2.54)

\displaystyle+\mathop{\rm Tr}\left[\eta,a_{s}\left(\cdot,\xi,\gamma\right)+a_{s}\left(\xi,\gamma,\cdot\right)+a_{s}\left(\gamma,\cdot,\xi\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}

\displaystyle+\mathop{\rm Tr}\left[\xi,a_{s}\left(\cdot,\eta,\gamma\right)+a_{s}\left(\eta,\gamma,\cdot\right)+a_{s}\left(\gamma,\cdot,\eta\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}.

However, for an alternative algebra, this simplifies to

\displaystyle K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\left[\gamma,\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)},\xi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\eta,\left[\gamma,\xi\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right)

(2.55)

\displaystyle+3\mathop{\rm Tr}\left[\eta,a_{s}\left(\cdot,\xi,\gamma\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}+3\mathop{\rm Tr}\left[\xi,a_{s}\left(\cdot,\eta,\gamma\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}.

The second line is symmetric in
\xi
and
\eta
, so it is sufficient to
consider the case
\xi=\eta.
Indeed, for
\xi=\eta,
using (
2.53
), this vanishes, so we get

K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\gamma}^{\left(s\right)}\eta,\xi\right)=-K^{\left(s\right)}\left(\eta,\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{\gamma}^{\left(s\right)}\xi\right).

(2.56)

Remark 2.35

Note that in Lemma
2.34
, we only used the trace
of the Malcev identity. The non-degeneracy of the Killing form in a
semi-simple Malcev algebra also hinges on the property (
2.56
), same
as for semi-simple Lie algebras. This suggests that weaker conditions could
be sufficient for these key properties.

3
Loop-valued maps

Let
M
be a smooth,
n
-dimensional manifold and let
s:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}
be a smooth
map. The map
s
can be used to define a product on
\mathbb{L}^{\prime}
-valued maps from
M
and a corresponding bracket on
\mathfrak{l}
-valued
maps. Indeed, let
A,B:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\prime}
and
\xi,\eta:M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{l}
be smooth maps, then at each
x\in M
,
define

\displaystyle\left.As\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{x}s_{x}\in\mathbb{L}

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\left.A\circ_{s}B\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{x}\circ_{s_{x}}B_{x}\in\mathbb{L}

(3.1b)

\displaystyle\left.A/_{s}B\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{x}/_{s_{x}}B_{x}\in\mathbb{L}

(3.1c)

\displaystyle\left.\left[\xi,\eta\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\xi_{x},\eta_{x}\right]^{\left(s_{x}\right)}\in\mathfrak{l.}

(3.1d)

In particular, the bracket
\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)}

defines the map
b_{s}:M\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{l.}
We also have the corresponding associator
\left[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right]^{\left(s\right)}
and the left-alternating
associator map
a_{s}:M\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{l}.
Similarly, define the
map
\varphi_{s}:M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{l}.

Then, similarly as for maps to Lie groups, we may define the (right)
Darboux derivative

\theta_{s}
of
s,
which is an
\mathfrak{l}
-valued
1
-form on
M
given by the pull-back
s^{\ast}\theta
of the
Maurer-Cartan form on
\mathbb{L}

[
45
]
. In particular, at
every
x\in M
,

\left.\left(\theta_{s}\right)\right|_{x}=\rho_{s\left(x\right)}^{-1}\left.ds\right|_{x},

(3.2)

and for any vector
X\in T_{x}M

\left.\left(\theta_{s}\right)\right|_{x}\left(X\right)=\left.\theta\right|_{s\left(x\right)}\left(\left.ds\right|_{x}\left(X\right)\right)\in\mathfrak{l.}

It is then clear that
\theta_{s}
, being a pullback of
\theta
,
satisfies the loop Maurer-Cartan structural equation (
2.19
). In
particular, for any vectors
X,Y\in T_{x}M
,

d\theta_{s}\left(X,Y\right)-\left[\theta_{s}\left(X\right),\theta_{s}\left(Y\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}=0.

(3.3)

We can then calculate the derivatives of these maps (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.1
(
[
26
, Theorem 3.51]
)

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and suppose
s\in C^{\infty}\left(M,\mathbb{L}\right)\
and
A,B\in C^{\infty}\left(M,\mathbb{L}^{\prime}\right),
then

\displaystyle d\left(As\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{s}\left(dA\right)+\lambda_{A}\left(ds\right)

(3.4a)

\displaystyle d\left(A\circ_{s}B\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{B}^{\left(s\right)}\left(dA\right)+\lambda_{A}^{\left(s\right)}\left(dB\right)+\left[A,B,\theta_{s}\right]^{\left(s\right)}

(3.4b)

\displaystyle d\left(A/_{s}B\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{B}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}dA-\left(\rho_{B}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\left(\rho_{A/_{s}B}^{\left(s\right)}dB\right)

(3.4d)

\displaystyle-\left(\rho_{B}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\left[A/_{s}B,B,\theta_{s}\right]^{\left(s\right)}

The
\mathfrak{l}\otimes\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}
-valued map
\varphi_{s}:M\longrightarrow

\mathfrak{l}\otimes\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}

(3.6)

where
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}\

\mathfrak{p}
and
\cdot
denotes the action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}
on
\mathfrak{l}
.

The Killing form
K^{\left(s\right)}:M\longrightarrow\mathop{\rm Sym}\nolimits^{2}\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}

dK^{\left(s\right)}\left(\xi,\xi\right)=2\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\left(\left[\xi,a_{s}\left(\xi,\cdot,\theta_{s}\right)\right]^{\left(s\right)}\right).

(3.7)

Given
A:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\prime}
and
s:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{L},
as shown in
[
26

, we have the following
expression for

\theta_{As}=\mathop{\rm Ad}\nolimits_{A}^{\left(s\right)}\theta_{s}+\left(\rho_{A}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}dA.

(3.8)

Moreover, let us consider the evolution equation satisfied by
\theta_{A\left(t\right)s}
for
A\left(t\right)=\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right)

for some
\xi:M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{l}.
This gives us the following.

Lemma 3.2

Let
A\left(t\right)=\exp_{s}\left(t\xi\right),

for
\xi\in C^{\infty}\left(M,\mathfrak{l}\right),

\frac{d\theta_{A\left(t\right)s}}{dt}=\left[\xi,\theta_{A\left(t\right)s}\right]^{A\left(t\right)s}+d\xi

(3.9)

\theta_{A\left(t\right)s}=U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\theta_{s}+\left(U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\int_{0}^{t}\left(U_{\tau\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}d\tau\right)d\xi.

(3.10)

Moreover, if
\mathbb{L}
is compact and given a metric on
M
and an inner
product on
\mathfrak{l},

\left|\theta_{A\left(t\right)s}\right|\leq e^{Ct\left|\xi\right|}\left(\left|\theta_{s}\right|+t\left|d\xi\right|\right).

(3.11)

Proof.
We will write symbolically

\frac{dA}{dt}=\xi\circ_{s}A,

so

\displaystyle\frac{d\theta_{A\left(t\right)s}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(d\left(As\right)/As\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(\frac{dA}{dt}s\right)/As

\displaystyle-\left(\left(d\left(As\right)/As\right)\cdot\left(\frac{dA}{dt}s\right)\right)/As

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(\left(\xi\circ_{s}A\right)s\right)/As

\displaystyle-\left(\theta_{As}\cdot\left(\left(\xi\circ_{s}A\right)s\right)\right)/As

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(\xi\left(As\right)\right)/As

\displaystyle+\left(\theta_{As}\cdot\left(\xi\left(As\right)\right)\right)/As

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\xi+\left(\xi\cdot d\left(As\right)\right)/As

\displaystyle-\theta_{As}\circ_{As}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\xi+\left[\xi,\theta_{As}\right]^{\left(As\right)}

Solving this ODE, with

\left|\theta_{A\left(t\right)s}\right|\leq\left|U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right|\left|\theta_{s}\right|+\left|d\xi\right|\int_{0}^{t}\left|U_{t\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\left(U_{\tau\xi}^{\left(s\right)}\right)^{-1}\right|d\tau,

but from (

since

First, let us recall Sobolev spaces

of functions between manifolds.

Lemma 3.8

Suppose
M
is a compact
n
-dimensional manifold and
suppose
N
is an
l
-dimensional manifold. Let
k
be a non-negative
integer and
r\geq 0
such that
kr>n.
Let
\Phi:N\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2l}
be a smooth embedding (by Whitney Embedding Theorem) and suppose
\left\{\left(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\right)\right\}
is an atlas for

N.
Suppose
f:M\longrightarrow N
is a continuous map. Then, the following
are equivalent:

1.

f\in W^{k,r}\left(M,N\right)

2.

\Phi\circ f\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{R}^{2l}\right)

3.

\phi_{\alpha}\circ f\in W^{k,r}\left(\phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(U_{\alpha}\right),\mathbb{R}^{l}\right)
for any chart
\left(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\right)

4.

f^{\ast}\theta_{G}

\in W^{k-1,r}\left(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}\right)
in case when if
N=G,
a compact Lie group, with Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{g}\
and
\theta_{G}
is the Maurer-Cartan form on
G.

In particular, conditions (2) and (3) are independent of the choice of the
embedding
\Phi
and the atlas
\left(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\right),

respectively.

Note that the condition
kr>n
is needed in Lemma
3.8
due
to the Sobolev embedding
W^{k,r}\subset C^{0}
for
kr>n.
We will prove a
characterization of loop-valued
W^{k,p}
-maps in terms of the loop

3.8
.

Lemma 3.9

Suppose
M
is a compact
n
-dimensional manifold and
suppose
\mathbb{L}
is a smooth loop of dimension
l,
with tangent algebra

\mathfrak{l}
and
\mathfrak{l}
-valued Maurer-Cartan form
\theta
. Let
k
be a non-negative integer and
r\geq 0
such that
kr>n.
Suppose
s:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}
is a continuous map. Then,
s\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}\right)
if and only if
\theta_{s}\in W^{k-1,r}\left(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{l}\right).

Proof.
Suppose
s\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}\right).
By Lemma
3.8
, if
\left\{\left(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\right)\right\}
is an atlas for
\mathbb{L},
then for each chart
\left(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\right),

\phi_{\alpha}\circ s\in W^{k,r}\left(\phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(U_{\alpha}\right),\mathbb{R}^{l}\right).
Now,
\left\{s^{-1}\left(U_{\alpha}\right)\right\}
is
an open cover of
M,
but using compactness of
M,
let
\left\{s^{-1}\left(U_{i}\right)\right\}
be an finite subcover, and suppose
\left\{u_{i}\right\}
is a smooth partition of unity subordinate to this
subcover. Then, we can write

\displaystyle\theta_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta s_{\ast}=\sum_{i}\left(u_{i}\theta\right)s_{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(\left(u_{i}\theta\right)\left(\phi_{i}^{-1}\right)_{\ast}\right)\left(\left(\phi_{i}\right)_{\ast}s_{\ast}\right).

For each
i
,
\left(\phi_{i}\right)_{\ast}s_{\ast}=\left(\phi_{i}\circ s\right)_{\ast}\in W^{k-1,r}\left(\phi_{i}^{-1}\left(U_{i}\right),\mathbb{R}^{l}\right).
On the other hand
\ \left(u_{i}\theta\right)\left(\phi_{i}^{-1}\right)_{\ast}
is a smooth
function, and hence composition with it is a continuous map
W^{k-1,r}\left(\phi_{i}^{-1}\left(U_{i}\right),\mathbb{R}^{l}\right)\longrightarrow W^{k-1,r}\left(\phi_{i}^{-1}\left(U_{i}\right),T^{\ast}U_{i}\otimes\mathfrak{l}\right)
(using
[
48
, Lemma B.8]
). Overall, we
see that each term of this finite sum is bounded in the
W^{k-1,r}
norm,
and thus
\theta_{s}\in W^{k-1,r}\left(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}\right).

Conversely, suppose now
\theta_{s}\in W^{k-1,r}\left(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}\right).
We will use item (2) in Lemma
3.8

to show that
s\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}\right).
This adapts the
proof of
[
48
, Lemma B.5]
. Let
\Phi:\mathbb{L}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2l}
be a smooth embedding, so that
\Phi\circ s
is continuous. In particular,
\Phi\circ s\in L^{r}\left(M,\mathbb{R}^{2l}\right).
Now, let
x\in M
and consider

\displaystyle\left.d\left(\Phi\circ s\right)\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.d\Phi\right|_{s\left(x\right)}\left.ds\right|_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left.d\Phi\right|_{s\left(x\right)}\rho_{s\left(x\right)}\right)\left(\rho_{s\left(x\right)}^{-1}\left.ds\right|_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(s\left(x\right)\right)\left(\theta_{s}\right)_{x},

where, for each
p\in\mathbb{L},
we have the linear map
E\left(p\right)=\left.d\Phi\right|_{p}\rho_{p}:\mathfrak{l}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2l}
, and the map
p\mapsto

E\left(p\right)
is a smooth map from
\mathbb{L}
to
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits\left(\mathfrak{l},\mathbb{R}^{2l}\right).
Thus, we can write

d\left(\Phi\circ s\right)=\left(E\circ s\right)\theta_{s},

with
E\circ s
being bounded in the operator norm, since
s
is continuous.
Hence, there exists a constant
C>0,
such that

\left\|d\left(\Phi\circ s\right)\right\|_{L^{r}}\leq C\left\|\theta_{s}\right\|_{L^{r}}\leq C\left\|\theta_{s}\right\|_{W^{k-1,r}}.

This shows that
\Phi\circ s\in W^{1,r}\left(M,\mathbb{R}^{2l}\right).

To show further that
\Phi\circ s\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{R}^{2l}\right),

k\geq 2,
similar estimates are obtained by considering higher
derivatives.

Theorem 3.10

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold. Suppose
kr>n=\dim M.
Let
s\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}\right)
and
\xi\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathfrak{l}\right),

A=\exp_{s}\left(\xi\right).
Then,

\left\|\theta_{As}\right\|_{W^{k-1,r}}\lesssim e^{Ck\left\|\xi\right\|_{C^{0}}}\left(\Theta^{k}+\Theta\right),

(3.32)

where
\Theta=\left\|\theta_{s}\right\|_{W^{k-1,r}}+\left\|\xi\right\|_{W^{k,r}}.

Similarly, if
X=X\left(1\right),
where
X\left(t\right)
is
1
-parameter family of
\mathfrak{l}
-valued maps that satisfies (
3.29
).
Then,

\left\|X\right\|_{W^{k,r}}\lesssim e^{C\left(k+1\right)\left\|\xi\right\|_{C^{0}}}\left(\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{W^{k,r}}+\left\|Y\right\|_{W^{k,r}}\right)\left(\Theta^{k+1}+\Theta\right)

Proof.
From Lemma
3.5
, for each
j\leq k,
we have the pointwise
estimate

\left|\nabla^{j-1}\theta_{As}\right|\lesssim e^{Cj\left|\xi\right|}p_{j-1},

p_{j-1}=\sum_{J_{j-1}}\left|\theta_{s}\right|^{i_{1}}\left|\nabla\theta_{s}\right|^{i_{2}}...\left|\nabla^{j-1}\theta_{s}\right|^{i_{j}}\left|d\xi\right|^{k_{1}}\left|\nabla d\xi\right|^{k_{2}}...\left|\nabla^{j-1}d\xi\right|^{k_{j}},

(3.33)

with
J_{j-1}=\left\{\left(i_{1},...,i_{j},k_{1},...,k_{j}\right)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2j}:\sum_{m=1}^{j}mi_{m}+\sum_{m=1}^{j}mk_{m}=j\right\}.

Now, from Lemma

, if
\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k^{\prime}}q_{i^{\prime}}m_{i^{\prime}}\leq k,

\left\|\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k^{\prime}}A_{i^{\prime}}^{m_{i^{\prime}}}\right\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k^{\prime}}\left\|A_{i^{\prime}}\right\|_{W^{k-q_{i^{\prime}},r}}^{m_{i^{\prime}}}.

3.33

\left|\nabla^{i-1}d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\left\|p_{j-1}\right\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{J_{j-1}}\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{j-1}\left\|\nabla^{i^{\prime}-1}\theta_{s}\right\|_{W^{k-i^{\prime},r}}^{i_{i^{\prime}}}\prod_{i^{\prime\prime}=1}^{j}\left\|\nabla^{i^{\prime\prime}-1}d\xi\right\|_{W^{k-i^{\prime\prime},r}}^{k_{i^{\prime\prime}}}

\displaystyle e^{\left(j+1\right)C\left\|\xi\right\|_{C^{0}}}\sum_{J_{j-1}^{\prime}}\left\|\left|\theta_{s}\right|^{i_{1}}\left|\nabla\theta_{s}\right|^{i_{2}}...\left|\nabla^{j-1}\theta_{s}\right|^{i_{j-1}}\left|d\xi\right|^{k_{1}}\left|\nabla d\xi\right|^{k_{2}}...\left|\nabla^{j-1}d\xi\right|^{k_{j}}\times\right.

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{j}X\right\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{\left(j+1\right)C\left\|\xi\right\|_{C^{0}}}\sum_{J_{j-1}^{\prime}}\left(\left\|\nabla^{j^{\prime\prime}}X_{0}\right\|_{W^{k-j^{\prime\prime},r}}+\left\|\nabla^{j^{\prime\prime}}Y\right\|_{W^{k-j^{\prime\prime},r}}\right)\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{j-1}\left\|\nabla^{i^{\prime}-1}\theta_{s}\right\|_{W^{k-i^{\prime},r}}^{i_{i^{\prime}}}\times

Corollary 3.11

Suppose
A\in C^{0}\left(M,\mathbb{L}^{\prime}\right)
and
s\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}\right)
, where
kr>n=\dim M.
Then,
A\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}^{\prime}\right)
if and only if
\theta_{As}\in W^{k-1,r}\left(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{l}\right)
.

Proof.
The map
\mu:\mathbb{L}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{L}\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}
given by
\left(A,s\right)\mapsto As
is smooth, hence the composition
with
\mu
is a continuous map from
W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{L}\right)
to

If
A\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}^{\prime}\right),
then since

As\in W^{k,r}\left(M,\mathbb{L}\right),
and hence from Lemma
3.9

\theta_{As}\in W^{k-1,r}\left(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{l}\right),
and thus
\theta_{A}^{\left(s\right)}\in W^{k-1,r}\left(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{l}\right).

Lemma A.1

Let

be positive integers and

,
for a positive real number

Also, suppose

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}A_{j}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}\left\|A_{j}\right\|_{W^{k-q_{j},p}}^{m_{j}}.

Proof.
Let
k^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{k^{\prime\prime}}q_{j}m_{j}\leq k.

Then suppose

Thus, from Hölder’s inequality, we have

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}A_{j}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}\left\|A_{j}\right\|_{L^{m_{j}p_{j}}}^{m_{j}}.

\frac{q_{j}}{k^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{p}{p_{j}m_{j}}\leq 1,
and hence

\displaystyle\frac{k-q_{j}}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{n}\left(1-\frac{q_{j}}{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{k}{n}\left(1-\frac{q_{j}}{k^{\prime\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{n}\left(1-\frac{p}{p_{j}m_{j}}\right).

Since by assumption,

, we obtain

\frac{k-q_{j}}{n}>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{j}m_{j}}.

Using a version of the Sobolev Embedding Theorem, this shows that indeed,

and (

) follows.

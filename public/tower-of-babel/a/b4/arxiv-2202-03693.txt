Consistency between black hole and mimetic gravity – Case of ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) -dimensional gravity –
Shin’ichi Nojiri, G. G. L. Nashed

Abstract
We show that the mimetic theory with the constraint g ρ ​ σ ​ ∂ ρ ϕ ​ ∂ σ ϕ = 1 g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\phi\partial_{\sigma}\phi=1 cannot realize the black hole geometry with the horizon(s). To overcome such issue, we may change the mimetic constraint a little bit by ω ⁡ ( ϕ ) ​ g ρ ​ σ ​ ∂ ρ ϕ ​ ∂ σ ϕ = − 1 , \omega(\phi)g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\phi\partial_{\sigma}\phi=-1, where ω ⁡ ( ϕ ) \omega(\phi) is a function of the scalar field ϕ \phi . As an example, we consider ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) -dimensional mimetic gravity with the mimetic potential and construct black hole (BH) solutions by using this modified constraint. We study three different classes: In the first class, we assume the Lagrange multiplier and mimetic potential are vanishing and obtain a BH solution that fully matches the BH of GR despite the non-triviality of the mimetic field which ensures the study presented in JCAP 01 (2019) 058 . In the second class, we obtain a BH having constant mimetic potential and a non-trivial form of the Lagrange multiplier. In the third class, we obtain a new BH solution with non-vanishing values of the mimetic field, the Lagrange multiplier, and the mimetic potential. In any case, the solutions correspond to the space-time with only one horizon but we show that the formalism for the constraint works.

II
Inconsistency between mimetic gravity with black hole geometry

The term “mimetic DM” in
(3+1)
-dimensional space-time is delivered in the scientific society by Mukhanov and Chamseddine
Chamseddine et al. 2014
.

In this paper, we consider the black hole space-time but the mimetic constraint (
2
) is not consistent with the black hole
space-time with horizon(s).
In (
2
), we choose the minus sign as follows,

\displaystyle g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\phi\partial_{\sigma}\phi=-1\,.

(5)

Even if we choose the plus sign in (
2
), the situation is not changed.
We consider the
D
dimensional sphericaly symmetric and static space-time
with following line element,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=b(r)dt^{2}-\frac{dr^{2}}{b_{1}(r)}-r^{2}d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}\,.

(6)

Here
d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}
is the line element of
D-2
dimensional unit sphere.
If we may also assume
\phi=\phi(r)
, the mimetic constraint (
5
) has the following form

\displaystyle b_{1}\left(\phi^{\prime}\right)^{2}=1\,.

(7)

The equation has no solution if
b_{1}
is negative,
b_{1}<0
.
In the case of black hole geometry,
b
vanishes and changes its signature at the horizon.
As we will see below, to avoid the curvature singularity,
b_{1}
also must vanish at the horizon.
Therefore even if
b_{1}
is positive,
b_{1}
is negative in general, as in the Schwarzschild black hole or the BTZ black hole, which conflicts with Eq. (
7
).
This tells that the mimetic theory with the constraint (
5
) cannot realize the black hole geometry with the horizon(s).

In order to avoid the above problem, we may change the mimetic constraint in (
5
), a little bit as in (
3
),

\displaystyle\omega(\phi)g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\phi\partial_{\sigma}\phi=-1\,.

(8)

Here
\omega(\phi)
is a function of
\phi
.
If
\omega(\phi)
is positive, we may define a new scalar field
\tilde{\phi}
by
\tilde{\phi}=\int d\phi\sqrt{\omega(\phi)}
,
the constraint (
8
) is reduced to the form of (
5
),

\displaystyle g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{\phi}=-1\,.

(9)

The signature of
\omega(\phi)
can be, however, changed in general.
If we may also assume
\phi=\phi(r)
and the space-time is given by (
6
),
instead of (
7
), the constraint (
8
) has the following form,

\displaystyle b_{1}\omega(\phi)\left(\phi^{\prime}\right)^{2}=1\,.

(10)

Then for a solution of
\phi
where
\omega(\phi)
is positive when
b_{1}
is positive and
\omega(\phi)
is negative when
b_{1}
is negative,
the constraint (
10
) is consistent even inside the horizon.
As an example, we consider a simple case where

\displaystyle\omega(\phi)=\frac{1}{\phi}\,.

(11)

Near the horizon,
b_{1}
should behaves as

\displaystyle b_{1}(r)\sim b_{0}\left(r-r_{\mathrm{h}}\right)\,.

(12)

Here
r_{\mathrm{h}}
is the radius of the horizon and
b_{0}
is a positive constant.
Then a solution of (
10
) with (
11
) is given by

\displaystyle\phi\sim\frac{r-r_{\mathrm{h}}}{b_{0}}\,.

(13)

Then the scalar field
\phi
and therefore
\omega(\phi)
change the sign at the horizon and
Eq. (
10
) is consistent even inside the horizon.

In case there are several horizons, the problem might not be solved only by the choice in (
11
).
A way to solve the problem in this case, we may choose

\displaystyle\omega(\phi)=b_{1}(\phi)\,.

(14)

In this case, the solution of (
10
) is simply given by

\displaystyle\phi=r\,.

(15)

Therefore it is clear that the problem is solved by the choice of (
14
).
However, this choice may look rather artificial because it looks like we have assumed the solution from the beginning.
Anyway, the possibility of the choice (
14
) tells that a model is realizing the solution of Eq. (
10
).

IV
Summary and Discussion

The topic of DM became a hot topic based on the data coming from observations of astrophysics as well as cosmology.
To explain the issue of dark matter, scientists supposed two directions:
The first one is to amend the field equation of GR and the second direction is to amend the standard model by building a new particle kind.
Many studies have explained that the two directions are equal
Sebastiani et al. 2017
.
Additionally, it is familiar in the frame of amended theories of gravity that they have new degrees of freedom in addition to the usual massless graviton of GR.
As we discussed in Section
I
that the target of the mimetic theory of gravity was mimetic DM, and was a good theory to explain the presence of cold DM.
We have shown, however, that the mimetic theory with the constraint
g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\phi\partial_{\sigma}\phi=-1

cannot realize the black hole geometry with the horizon(s).
To bypass such problem, we changed the mimetic constraint as
\omega(\phi)g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\phi\partial_{\sigma}\phi=-1,

where
\omega(\phi)
is a function of the scalar field
\phi
.
Because we can choose
\omega(\phi)
changes its signature at the horizon, the constraint becomes consistent with the existence of the horizon(s).
As applications of the formalism of the constraint, we considered the mimetic gravity in
(2+1)
-dimensional space-time and found that the
formalism works well.

In future work, we may consider the time-dependent black hole solution or the black hole solution in the expanding universe, which is difficult to be found
even in other gravity theories.
In the case of the mimetic theory, the vector
(\partial_{\alpha}\phi)
is time-like when we consider the cosmology and
the vector is space-like when we consider the static object like a black hole.
If we use the original mimetic constraint given by Eq. (
2
), it is difficult to include both the time-like vector and the space-like vector for
(\partial_{\alpha}\phi)

but by introducing
\omega(\phi)
as in Eq. (
3
), it becomes possible to include both the time-like and the space-like vectors in a solution,
which may help to construct the time-dependent black hole solution or the black hole solution in the expanding universe.

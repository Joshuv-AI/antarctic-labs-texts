Universal constraints on semigroups of hyperbolic isometries
Argyrios Christodoulou

Abstract
A semigroup generated by a finite collection of isometries of the hyperbolic plane is called semidiscrete if its elements do not accumulate at the identity transformation in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets. In this paper we obtain geometric constraints on the generators of a finitely-generated semigroup which imply that the semigroup is semidiscrete.

Introduction

This paper studies semigroups of conformal isometries of the hyperbolic plane. For a semigroup generated by a finite collection of isometries, we calculate explicit geometric constraints on its generators which imply that elements of the semigroup do not accumulate at the identity transformation. Semigroups of Möbius transformations have been previously studied by Fried, Marotta and Stankiewitz
[
3
]
, as a particular branch of the theory of semigroups of rational functions initiated by Hinkkanen and Martin
[
4
]
. Our approach follows techniques similar to those used by Jacques and Short
[
5
]
, who further developed the material in
[
3
]
, and incorporates well-known results from the theory of Fuchsian groups. Also, the work of Avila, Bochi and Yoccoz
[
1
]
on uniformly hyperbolic finitely-valued
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-cocycles is closely related to the theory of semigroups and at the end of this section we will discuss how our main result can be used to infer the uniform hyperbolicity of a cocycle.

Let us start by setting up some notation. The group
\mathcal{M}
of conformal automorphisms of the upper half-plane

The non-identity elements of

By the term

.

M\leqslant 4\max\limits_{m,n}\left\{\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\,\lvert+\log\frac{\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1}{\lvert\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\rvert}\right\}+3,

(3.2)

and the maximum can be taken over all pairs
f_{m},f_{n}
with
C_{m,n}\neq-1
(recall that
C(f,g)=-1
implies that the axes of
f
and
g
cross orthogonally).

If
\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert<1
, then

\displaystyle\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\,\lvert

\displaystyle+\log\frac{\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1}{\lvert\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\rvert}=\log\frac{\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1}{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\log\frac{4}{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1\right)}

\displaystyle=\log 4-\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\left(\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert-1\right)<5+\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}(C_{m,n}-1)\rvert\,\rvert.

On the other hand, for
1<\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\leqslant 4
we have

\displaystyle\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\,\lvert

\displaystyle+\log\frac{\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1}{\lvert\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\rvert}=\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1\right)-\log\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\log 12-\log\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)=\log 12-\log\frac{12\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)}{12}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\log 12-\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1\right)\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)

\displaystyle=2\log 12+\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert(\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert-1)\rvert<5+\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}(C_{m,n}-1)\rvert\,\rvert.

Finally, assuming that
\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert>4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\,\lvert

\displaystyle+\log\frac{\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1}{\lvert\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\rvert}=\log\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1\right)-\log\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)

\displaystyle<\left\lvert\log\left\lvert C_{m,n}\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}+1\right)\left(\sqrt{\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert}-1\right)\right\rvert\,\right\rvert<\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}(C_{m,n}-1)\rvert\,\rvert,

where the last two steps are obtained by using the triangle inequality.

Combining these last inequalities with (
3.2
) yields

M<4\max\limits_{m,n}\left\{\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}(C_{m,n}-1)\rvert\,\rvert\right\}+20+3,

where the maximum can be taken over all pairs
f_{m},f_{n}
.

The only case left to consider now is when a number of generators of
S
share the same attracting fixed point (the case where they share the same repelling fixed point is identical). Suppose, without loss of generality, that
\alpha(f_{1})=\alpha(f_{2})=\dots=\alpha(f_{k})
for some
k<n
, and all other fixed points of the generators are distinct. Also, assume that

\tau(f_{j})>4\max\left\{\lvert\log\lvert C_{m,n}(C_{m,n}-1)\rvert\,\rvert\right\}+23,\quad\text{for }j=1,2,\dots,n,

where the maximum is taken over all pairs
f_{m},f_{n}
with
C_{m,n}\neq 0
. Then, as we saw earlier, for each
j=1,2,\dots,n
there exist open intervals
A^{j},B^{j}
, with disjoint closures, such that
f_{j}
maps the complement of
B^{j}
inside
A^{j}
.

Observe that in this case
\cap_{i=1}^{k}A^{i}\neq\emptyset
. Also, as the constant
M
does not take into account pairs of generators with zero cross ratio, it is possible that
A^{m}\cap B^{n}\neq\emptyset
, for some
m,n\leqslant k
. However, since we assumed that
\tau(f_{i})>23>\log 5
for
i=1,2,\dots,k
, Lemma
2.8
implies that there exist intervals
A,B
, such that
f_{i}
maps the complement of
B
inside
A
, for all
i=1,2,\dots,k
. Hence, for
i=1,2,\dots,k
, each
f_{i}
maps the complement of
B^{i}\cap B
inside
\left(\cup_{i=1}^{k}A^{i}\right)\cap A
, and these intervals are disjoint for all
i=1,2,\dots,k
. In addition,
B^{i}\cap B
and
\left(\cup_{i=1}^{k}A^{i}\right)\cap A
are disjoint from all
A^{j}
and
B^{j}
, for all
i=1,2,\dots,k
and
j=k+1,k+2,\dots,n
. Therefore, the semigroup
S
maps the union of
\left(\cup_{i=1}^{k}A^{i}\right)\cap A
with
\cup_{j=k+1}^{n}A^{j}
strictly inside itself, completing our proof.

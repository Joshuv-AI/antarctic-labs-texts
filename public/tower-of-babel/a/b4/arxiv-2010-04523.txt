1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a Banach space, a bounded operator
T:X\rightarrow X
is called
power-bounded when
\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\left\|T^{n}\right\|<\infty
. In this
case the spectrum of
T
is contained in the closed unit ball
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. We say that
T
is polynomially bounded when it satisfies an estimate

(1.1)

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq C\sup\{|P(z)|:z\in\mathbb{D}\},\quad P\text{ polynomial. }

When
X=H
is an Hilbert space and
T
is a contraction, that is

\left\|T\right\|\leq 1
(which is a power-bounded operator) it is well-known
that
T
satisfies (
1.1
) with
C=1
. This is the so-called
von Neumann’s inequality. However even on Hilbert space we cannot expect
that any power-bounded operator satisfies (
1.1
) (see
[
12
]
). A natural
question is whether one can obtain similar estimates, for power-bounded operators,
replacing the norm uniform in the right-hand side of (
1.1
) by an another function norm. An answer is given by Peller in

[
13
]
for power-bounded operators on Hilbert space. If
T
is such operator, then
it satisfies

(1.2)

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})},\quad P\text{ polynomial. }

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})}
is an appropriate Besov norm (see section 3).

In the first part of this paper we deal with operators which satisfy the discrete Gomilko
Shi-Feng condition: an operator
T:X\rightarrow X
satisfies this condition if the spectrum of
T

is included in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and

(1.3)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{2}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt\leq\frac{C}{(r^{2}-1)}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|,\quad r>1,x\in X,x^{*}\in X^{*}.

This condition was first introduced in
[
7
]
and
[
15
]
.The continuous case had been introduced before in
[
6
]
and studied extensively in
[
4
]
,
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
.
We will show that this condition implies power boundedness and that the converse is true when
X=H

is an Hilbert space. We will be able to show (see section 3) that a bounded operator
T
on a general Banach space
X
satisfies an estimate (
1.2
) if and only if
T
satisfies the discrete Gomilko Shi-Feng condition (
1.3
).
Furthermore one of the main results of this paper is to show that the
discrete Gomilko Shi-Feng condition is equivalent to the boundedness of the set

(1.4)

\Big\{(r-1)\phi^{\prime}(T):r>1\text{ and }\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})\text{ with }\sup\{|\phi(z)|:z\in r\mathbb{D}\}\leq 1\Big\}.

This is weaker than (
1.1
) in the sense that if an
operator
T:X\rightarrow X
satisfies (
1.1
) then (
1.4
) is bounded. The converse is false even on
Hilbert space.

The boundedness of
(
1.4
)
is a way to characterize power bounded
operators on Hilbert space or operators with discrete Gomilko Shi-Feng
condition on general Banach space. However (
1.4
) is not a usual
bounded functional calculus because we have an estimate of
\left\|\phi^{\prime}(T)\right\|
and not

\left\|\phi(T)\right\|
.

The second part of this article (section 4) is devoted to
‘
\gamma
-versions’ of the previous results. By a ‘
\gamma
-version’, we mean
replacing operator norm boundedness by the
so-called stronger notion of
\gamma
-boundedness (see
[
8
]
and references therein).
This section starts with some basics about

\gamma
-boundedness,
\gamma
-operators and
\gamma^{\prime}
-operators (see
[
9
]
).
We will introduce a
\gamma
-analogue of the discrete Gomilko Shi-Feng condition for a
bounded operator
T:X\rightarrow X
with spectrum included in

\overline{\mathbb{D}}
as follows : there exists
C>0
such that for any

N\in\mathbb{N}
, for any
r_{1},\ldots,r_{N}>1
, and for any

x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and
x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{N}^{*}\in X^{*}
, we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle(r_{k}+1)(r_{k}-1)R(r_{k}e^{it},T)^{2}x_{k},x_{k}^{*}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\left\|(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

We will show that
T
satisfies this condition if and only if the set in (
1.4
) is
\gamma
-bounded.
Moreover, we will show that
T
is power
\gamma
-bounded, that is the set

\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is
\gamma
-bounded, if and only if
T
satisfies (
1.5
).
It is important to notice that these results are stated without any assumption on
X
.
We will conclude this paper with a
\gamma
-version of
[
13
, Corollary 3.7]
.

Finally we give a few notation to be used along this paper. We write
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
for the natural numbers and for
N\in\mathbb{N}
we write
\mathbb{N}_{N}=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
the first N natural numbers. For any Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, we
denote by
B(X,Y)
the algebra of all bounded operators from
X
into
Y
equipped with the operator norm, and we set
B(X):=B(X,X)
.
For
T\in B(X)
we denote by
\sigma(T)
the spectrum of

T
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\sigma(T)
, we put

R(\lambda,T)=(\lambda I_{X}-T)^{-1}
the resolvent operator. We let
\mathbb{D}
and
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
respectively
the open and closed unit disk. Also
the open disk of radius
r
centered at
0
and will be denote
by
r\mathbb{D}
while the closed disk of radius
r
centered
at
0
and will be denoted by

r\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Also. For any
r\in\mathbb{R}
we denote
by
H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
the space of all bounded analytic functions

\phi:r\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. This is a Banach space for
the norm

\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}:=\sup\{|\phi(z)|:z\in r\mathbb{D}\}.

2.
Discrete Gomilko Shi-Feng condition and derivative functional calculus.

2.1.  Discrete Gomilko Shi-Feng condition

Let
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. For
r>1
,
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\phi^{(m)}(T)
the operator obtained with Riesz-Dunford calculus. Since
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
then for each

1<\rho<r
,
\phi^{(m)}\in H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})
and therefore the Riesz-Dunford calculus can be applied to the function
\phi^{(m)}
and the operator
T
. We obtain

(2.1)

\phi^{(m)}(T)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho e^{it}\phi^{(m)}(\rho e^{it})R(\rho e^{it},T)dt\in B(X),\quad 1<\rho<r.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for each
r>1
and
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
, one has

(2.2)

\phi^{(m)}(T)=\frac{m!}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho e^{it}\phi(\rho e^{it})R(\rho e^{it},T)^{m+1}dt,\quad 1<\rho<r.

Proof.

Let
r>1
,
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
and
1<\rho<r
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{d}{dt}R(\rho e^{it},T)^{n}=-in\rho e^{it}R(\rho e^{it},T)^{n+1}.

Using (
2.1
) it follows by
m
integrations by parts,

\displaystyle\phi^{(m)}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho e^{it}\phi^{(m-1)}(\rho e^{it})R(\rho e^{it},T)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\ldots=\frac{m!}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho e^{it}\phi(\rho e^{it})R(\rho e^{it},T)^{m+1}dt.

∎

We now investigate the discrete Gomilko Shi-Feng condition which appears in
[
7
]
in the case
m=1
. The case
m>1
is inspired by
[
4
, Proposition 6.3.]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. We say that
T\in B(X)
has the property
(GFS)_{m}
if
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and there exists
C>0
such that

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{m+1}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt\leq\frac{C}{(r+1)(r-1)^{m}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|,\quad r>1,x\in X,x^{*}\in X^{*}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
T
with
(GFS)_{m}
. Then
T
is power bounded.

Proof.

Let
\phi(z)=\frac{z^{n+m}}{(n+1)\ldots(n+m)}
, then
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
for any
r>1
with

\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}=\frac{r^{n+m}}{(n+1)\ldots(n+m)}

and furthermore
\phi^{(m)}(z)=z^{n}
. By (
2.2
), one has for
r>1
,

T^{n}=\frac{m!}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}re^{it}\phi(re^{it})R(re^{it},T)^{m+1}dt.

It follows, for
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

\displaystyle|\langle T^{n}x,x^{*}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{rm!}{2\pi}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{m+1}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cr^{n+m+1}m!}{2\pi(n+1)\ldots(n+m)(r+1)(r-1)^{m}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

Therefore,

\left\|T^{n}\right\|\leq\frac{m!Cr^{n+m+1}}{2\pi(n+1)\ldots(n+m)(r-1)^{m}}.

Letting
r=1+\frac{1}{n}
one obtains

\frac{m!Cr^{n+m+1}}{2\pi(n+1)\ldots(n+m)(r-1)^{m}}=\frac{m!Cn^{m}(1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}(1+\frac{1}{n})^{m+1}}{2\pi(n+1)\ldots(n+m)}.

But for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\frac{n^{m}}{(n+1)\ldots(n+m)}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\bigg(1+\frac{1}{n}\bigg)^{m+1}\leq 2^{m+1}

and it is well-known that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{n}=e.

Finally, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\|T^{n}\right\|\leq\frac{2^{m}m!Ce}{\pi}.

∎

Remark 2.4
.

We recall that
T\in B(X)
is a Ritt operator if
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and there exists
C\geq 0
such that

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\textbackslash{\overline{\mathbb{D}}},\quad\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|\leq\displaystyle\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|}.

Ritt operators play a prominent role in the theory of functional calculus. Indeed they have a specific
H^{\infty}
functional calculus (see
[
11
]
and references therein).

It is easy to check that a Ritt operator has
(GFS)_{1}
. Indeed let
T
a Ritt operator and
r>1
, let
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{2}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{C}{|re^{it}-1|^{2}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{C}{r^{2}+1-2rcos(t)}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|dt.

Now by the residue method (see for example
[
5
, p.99]
)

(2.5)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{r^{2}+1-2rcos(t)}dt=\frac{2\pi}{(r+1)(r-1)},

therefore one has

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{2}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt\leq\frac{2\pi C}{(r+1)(r-1)}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

We will need the following stability property. We skip the easy proof.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
S\in B(X)
which has
(GFS)_{m}
. If
T
is similar to
S
, that is there exists
U\in B(X)
invertible such that
T=USU^{-1}
, then
T
has
(GFS)_{m}
.

We will see that
T
has
(GFS)_{m}
if and only if
T
has
(GFS)_{1}
. Let us begin by one implication. For the reverse implication we will use a derivative functional calculus that we introduce in the next sub-section.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
m\geq 1
an integer and
T\in B(X)
. If
T
has
(GFS)_{m}
then
T
has
(GFS)_{k}
for each
1\leq k\leq m
.

Proof.

Let
r>1
and assume (
2.3
). We show by downward induction that for
1\leq k\leq m
,

(2.6)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{k+1}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt\leq\frac{Cm}{k(r+1)(r-1)^{k}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|,\quad x\in X,x^{*}\in X^{*}.

Let
x\in X
,
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. Suppose that (
2.6
) is satisfied for
k
with
1<k+1\leq m
. Using the fact that
\frac{d}{dr}R(re^{it},T)^{n}=-ne^{it}R(re^{it},T)^{n+1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and Fubini’s Theorem, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle R(re^{it},T)^{k}x,x^{*}\rangle dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{r}^{+\infty}\langle ke^{it}R(ue^{it},T)^{k+1}x,x^{*}\rangle dudt

(2.7)

\displaystyle=k\int_{r}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{it}\langle R(ue^{it},T)^{k+1}x,x^{*}\rangle dtdu.

It follows using (
2.6
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{k}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\leq k\int_{r}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(ue^{it},T)^{k+1}x,x^{*}\rangle|dtdu

\displaystyle\leq k\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{Cm}{k(u+1)(u-1)^{k}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{Cm}{(r+1)(u-1)^{k}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|du

\displaystyle=\frac{Cm}{(k-1)(r+1)(r-1)^{k-1}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

∎

2.2.
{m}
-derivative bounded functional calculus

We begin by giving a basic result which is a straighforward consequence of Cauchy inequalities.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
r>0
and
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
. Then for each
\rho<r
,
\phi^{(m)}\in H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})
with

(2.8)

\left\|\phi^{(m)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})}\leq\frac{m!}{(r-\rho)^{m}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

This Lemma justifies the following definition.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
T
is said to have

m
-derivative bounded functional calculus
if there exists
C>0
such that

(2.9)

\left\|\phi^{(m)}(T)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{(r-1)^{m}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}\;\text{ for each $\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})$ \text{ and } $r>1$}.

Remark 2.9
.

It is easy to check that (
2.9
) is equivalent to:

\left\|\phi^{(m)}\big(rT\big)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{(1-r)^{m}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\;\text{ for each $\phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})$ \text{ and } $0<r<1$}.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. The following assertions are equivalent for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(i)

T has
(GFS)_{m}
;

(ii)

T has
{1}
-derivative bounded functional calculus;

(iii)

T has
{m}
-derivative bounded functional calculus.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: According to Proposition
2.6

T
has
(GFS)_{1}
. Let
r>\rho>1
and
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
. Then using (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\left\|\phi^{\prime}(T)\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\sup}\underset{\left\|x^{*}\right\|=1}{\sup}\Big|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle\rho e^{it}\phi(\rho e^{it})R(\rho e^{it},T)^{2}x,x^{*}\rangle dt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})}}{2\pi}\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\sup}\underset{\left\|x^{*}\right\|=1}{\sup}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(\rho e^{it},T)^{2}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\rho}{2\pi(\rho-1)(\rho+1)}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})}\leq\frac{C}{2\pi(\rho-1)}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

The second to last inequality comes from (
2.3
) with
m=1
. Letting
\rho\rightarrow r
yields

\left\|\phi^{\prime}(T)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{2\pi(r-1)}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Let
1<\rho<r
and
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
. According to
Lemma
2.7

\phi^{(m-1)}\in H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})
so applying the

{1}
-derivative bounded functional calculus to
\phi^{(m-1)}
and again Lemma
2.7
one has

\displaystyle\left\|\phi^{(m)}(T)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|(\phi^{(m-1)})^{\prime}(T)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{\rho-1}\left\|\phi^{(m-1)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(m-1)!C}{(\rho-1)(r-\rho)^{m-1}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

Taking
\rho=\frac{r+1}{2}
yields

\left\|\phi^{(m)}(T)\right\|\leq\frac{2^{m}(m-1)!C}{(r-1)^{m}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Let
r>1
,
1<\rho<r
,
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. There exists a measurable function
\varepsilon:[0,2\pi]\rightarrow\{z\in\mathbb{C},\;|z|=1\}
such that for each
t\in[0,2\pi)

|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{m+2}x,x^{*}\rangle|=\varepsilon(t)\langle R(re^{it},T)^{m+2}x,x^{*}\rangle.

Let define
\phi(z):=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(m+1)!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\varepsilon(t)}{(re^{it}-z)^{2}}dt
for
z\in\rho\mathbb{D}
. Then
\phi\in H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})
, with

(2.10)

\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})}\leq\frac{2\pi}{(m+1)!(r+\rho)(r-\rho)}.

Indeed for each
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, using (
2.5
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{|re^{it}-\rho e^{i\theta}|^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{|\frac{r}{\rho}e^{it}-1|^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\rho^{2}(\frac{r}{\rho}+1)(\frac{r}{\rho}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{(r+\rho)(r-\rho)}.

Moreover

\phi^{(m)}(z)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\varepsilon(t)}{(re^{it}-z)^{m+2}}dt,\;z\in\rho\mathbb{D}.

By Fubini’s Theorem one has

\phi^{(m)}(T)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varepsilon(t)R(re^{it},T)^{m+2}dt,

and finally using (
2.9
) and (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{m+2}x,x^{*}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varepsilon(t)\langle R(re^{it},T)^{m+2}x,x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\langle\phi^{(m)}(T)x,x^{*}\rangle|\leq\left\|\phi^{(m)}(T)\right\|\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(\rho-1)^{m}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\rho\mathbb{D})}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi C}{(m+1)!(\rho-1)^{m}(r-\rho)(r+\rho)}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi C}{(m+1)!(r+1)(\rho-1)^{m}(r-\rho)}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

Taking
\rho=\frac{r+1}{2}
one obtains

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{m+2}x,x^{*}\rangle|\leq\frac{2^{m+2}\pi C}{(m+1)!(r+1)(r-1)^{m+1}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

Thus
T
has
(GFS)_{m+1}
and hence by Proposition
2.6
,
T
has
(GFS)_{m}
.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

The condition
(GFS)_{m}
does not depend of
m\in\mathbb{N}
. In other words, if
T\in B(X)
satisfies condition
(GFS)_{m}
for one
m\in\mathbb{N}
then it has
(GFS)_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
T\in B(X)
. If
T
satisfies one of the three conditions of Theorem
2.10
then we will say that
T
is a Gomilko Shi-Feng operator or GFS operator.

When
X
is an Hilbert space one obtains the following characterisation.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
T\in B(H)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. The following assertions are equivalent,

(i)

T
is power-bounded;

(ii)

there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
r>1
,

\displaystyle(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\|R(re^{it},T)x\right\|^{2}dt\leq C\left\|x\right\|^{2}\quad(x\in H)

\displaystyle(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\|R(re^{it},T)^{*}y\right\|^{2}dt\leq C\left\|y\right\|^{2}\quad(y\in H);

(iii)

T is a GFS operator.

Proof.

The implication
(iii)\implies(i)
is given by Proposition
2.3
.

(i)\implies(ii)
: Let
r>1
, one has

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\|R(re^{it},T)x\right\|^{2}dt=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{n}x}{(re^{it})^{n+1}}\right\|^{2}dt.

The Fourier-Plancherel Theorem gives

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{n}x}{(re^{it})^{n+1}}\right\|^{2}dt=2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\left\|T^{n}x\right\|}{r^{n+1}}\bigg)^{2}.

Then taking
M=\underset{n}{\sup}\{\left\|T^{n}\right\|\}
, we have

2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\left\|T^{n}\right\|}{r^{n+1}}\bigg)^{2}\leq 2\pi M^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2(n+1)}}=\frac{2\pi M^{2}}{r^{2}-1},

whence the first inequality in
(ii)
. Since
T^{*}
is also power bounded, the second inequality in
(ii)
holds as well.

(ii)\implies(iii)
: by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{2}x,x^{*}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)x,R(re^{it},T)^{*}x^{*}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\|R(re^{it},T)x\right\|^{2}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\|R(re^{it},T)^{*}x^{*}\right\|^{2}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}-1}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

If follows that
T
has
(GFS)_{1}
, and so
T
is a GFS operator. ∎

Remarks 2.14
.

(1)

We say that
T\in B(X)
is polynomially bounded when there exists
C>0
such that for each polynomial
P
one has

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|P\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}.

It is easy to show that if
T
is polynomially bounded, then
T
is a GFS operator. Indeed let
P
be a polynomial and
r>1
. Then according to Lemma
2.7
and the assumption that
T
is polynomially bounded:

\left\|P^{(m)}(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|P^{(m)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\frac{Cm!}{(r-1)^{m}}\left\|P^{(m)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

The converse implication is false.
In fact, on any infinite-dimensional Hilbert space there exists a power bounded
operator which is not polynomially bounded (see
[
12
]
). But, according to Corollary
2.13
, in a Hilbert space an operator
is power bounded if and only if it is a GFS operator.

(2)

When
X
is not an Hilbert space, the implication
(i)\implies(iii)
in
Corollary (
2.13
) is false. Indeed according to
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
,
if
X
is a reflexive Banach space and
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\mathbb{T}
then
T
is a scalar type spectral operator if and only if
T

and
T^{-1}
have
(GFS)_{1}
. Let
U
denote the shift operator defined
on
l_{p}(\mathbb{Z})

(1<p<\infty)
by

(2.11)

U((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})=(x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

It is well-known that for
1<p\neq 2<\infty
,
U
is not a scalar type spectral operator. It
follows, that either
U
or
U^{-1}
does not have
(GFS)_{1}
. Further
U
and
U^{-1}
are similar. Hence by Proposition
2.5
,
U
does not have
(GFS)_{1}
. Thus
U
is a power bounded operator which is not a GFS operator.

3.
Polynomial Besov calculus on general Banach spaces

We denote by
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
the Banach algebra of all holormorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

(3.1)

\int_{0}^{1}\underset{t\in[0,2\pi)}{\sup}|f^{\prime}(ue^{it})|du<\infty,

endowed with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}=\int_{0}^{1}\underset{t\in[0,2\pi)}{\sup}|f^{\prime}(ue^{it})|du+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}.

We remark that by a change of variables
u=\frac{1}{r}
one has

(3.2)

\int_{0}^{1}\underset{t\in[0,2\pi)}{\sup}|f^{\prime}(ue^{it})|du=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\underset{t\in[0,2\pi)}{\sup}\Big|f^{\prime}\Big(\frac{e^{it}}{r}\Big)\Big|dr.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
T\in B(H)
be a power-bounded operator. According to
[
13
, Theorem 3.8.]
there exists
C>0
such that for each polynomial
P
one has

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

Now since the set of polynomials is dense in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
the bounded algebra homomorphism
P\rightarrow P(T)
extends to a bounded algebra homomorphism on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
.

We now consider the issue of obtaining a similar bounded Besov calculus on an arbitrary Banach space
X
. Ideas are based on
[
3
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T\in B(X)
be a
GFS
operator. Then there exists
C>0
such that for each polynomial
P
,

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider
P_{n}(z)=z^{n}

Applying (
2.2
) with
m=1
and
\phi(z)=\frac{z^{n+1}}{n+1}
, one has the following representation,

T^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi(n+1)}\int_{0}^{2\pi}r^{n+2}e^{i(n+2)t}R^{2}(re^{it},T)dt,

for any
r>1
. We deduce that for any
x\in X,x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

\displaystyle\langle T^{n}x,x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=2n(n+1)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{2n+3}}dr\langle T^{n}x,x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=2n(n+1)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{2n+3}}\frac{1}{2\pi(n+1)}\int_{0}^{2\pi}r^{n+2}e^{i(n+2)t}\langle R^{2}(re^{it},T)x,x^{*}\rangle dtdr

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(n+2)t}\langle R^{2}(re^{it},T)x,x^{*}\rangle dtdr

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{3it}}{r^{2}}\frac{e^{i(n-1)t}}{r^{n-1}}\langle R^{2}(re^{it},T)x,x^{*}\rangle dtdr

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{3it}}{r^{2}}P_{n}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{e^{it}}{r}\Big)\langle R^{2}(re^{it},T)x,x^{*}\rangle dtdr,

where, in the first equality, we have used the following identity

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{2n+3}}dr=\frac{1}{2n(n+1)}.

Now let
P
be a polynomial. Using the linearity of derivative and integration, and previous calculations one obtains

(3.4)

\langle P(T)x,x^{*}\rangle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{3it}}{r^{2}}P^{\prime}\Big(\frac{e^{it}}{r}\Big)\langle R^{2}(re^{it},T)x,x^{*}\rangle dtdr+P(0)\langle x,x^{*}\rangle.

Since
T
has
(GFS)_{1}
there exists
C>0
such that

(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle R(re^{it},T)^{2}x,x^{*}\rangle|dt\leq C\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|,\quad r>1,x\in X,x^{*}\in X^{*}.

Therefore combining this with (
3.2
),

(3.5)

|\langle P(T)x,x^{*}\rangle|\leq C^{\prime}\Big(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big|P^{\prime}\Big(\frac{e^{it}}{r}\Big)\big|dr+|P(0)|\Big)\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|\leq C^{\prime}\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|,

which proves the result.

∎

The above proof leads to the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T\in B(X)
a
GFS
operator. Then the mapping
P\rightarrow P(T)
on the set of polynomials can be extended uniquely to a bounded homomorphism from Besov algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
to the algebra of bounded operators
B(X)
. When
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
let
f(T)
denote the operator obtained by this extension.

Then for each
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
one has the following representation:
for
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
:

(3.6)

\langle f(T)x,x^{*}\rangle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{3it}}{r^{2}}f^{\prime}\Big(\frac{e^{it}}{r}\Big)\langle R^{2}(re^{it},T)x,x^{*}\rangle dtdr+\langle f(0)x,x^{*}\rangle.

Proof.

The first assertion is straighforward since the set of polynomials is dense in Besov algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
. Let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and denote by
f_{\mathcal{B}}(T)
the operator defined for
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
by

\langle f_{\mathcal{B}}(T)x,x^{*}\rangle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}(r^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{3it}}{r^{2}}f^{\prime}\Big(\frac{e^{it}}{r}\Big)\langle R^{2}(re^{it},T)x,x^{*}\rangle dtdr+\langle f(0)x,x^{*}\rangle.

The calculation at the end of the proof of Proposition
3.1
shows that this is a well-defined element of
B(X,X^{**})
.
Now let
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of polynomials such that

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

By definition of
f(T)
, one has for
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}

\langle P_{n}(T)x,x^{*}\rangle\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\langle f(T)x,x^{*}\rangle.

Furthermore by the same arguments leading to the estimate (
3.5
) one has for
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

|\langle(f_{\mathcal{B}}(T)-P_{n}(T))x,x^{*}\rangle|\leq C\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

We conclude that
f(T)=f_{\mathcal{B}}(T)
and therefore we have shown equality (
3.6
).
∎

In general when we extend a functional calculus to a larger class, we do not know what the operators
obtained on this larger class look like. The above Theorem has more value because we do not only extend
polynomial calculus to the Besov algebra but also we are able to give a good representation of
operators obtained by extension.

The next result is a converse to Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. If there exists
C>0
such that for each polynomial
P

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{B}},

is a GFS operator.

Proof.

Let
r>1
and
\phi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
. Then
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, indeed by (
2.8
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\underset{t\in[0,2\pi)}{\sup}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(se^{it})|ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(s\mathbb{D})}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{(r-s)^{2}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{r(r-1)}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

It follows

\left\|\phi^{\prime}(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\frac{4C}{(r^{2}-1)}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})}.

Therefore
T
has
{1}
-derivative bounded functional calculus, hence
T
is a GFS operator.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

It is known (see
[
16
, Lemma 2.3.7]
for details) that there exists
C>0
such that for each polynomial of degree
N
one has

\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq Clog(N+2)\left\|P\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}.

Therefore if
T
has
(GFS)_{1}
then there exists
C>0
such that for each polynomial of degree
N
,

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq Clog(N+2)\left\|P\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}.

4.
Generalizations involving
\gamma
-boundedness

This main goal of this section is to obtain an extension of Corollary
2.13
to Banach spaces. We have noticed that it is not possible to replace
H
by a Banach space
X
without additional assumptions. We need assumptions on the operator
T
. We prove an analogue of Corallary
2.13
if
\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is
\gamma
-bounded. We start with some background on the so-called
\gamma
-spaces introduced by Kalton and Weis in
[
9
]
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces and
we let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent
complex valued standard Gaussian variables on some probability space
\Sigma
. We denote by
G(X)
the closure of

\big\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\,:\,x_{k}\in X,\,n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}

in
L^{2}(\Sigma,X)
. For
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
, we let

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}:=\bigg(\underset{\Sigma}{\mathop{\text{\Huge$\int$}}\nolimits}{\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}(\lambda)x_{k}\right\|^{2}d\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

denote the induced norm.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset B(X,Y)
be a set of operators. We say that

\mathcal{T}
is
\gamma
-bounded if
there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that for all finite sequences

(T_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{T}
and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset X
, the following inequality holds:

(4.1)

\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes T_{n}x_{n}\right\|_{G(Y)}\leq C\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes x_{n}\right\|_{G(X)}.

The least admissible constant in the above inequality is called the

\gamma
-bound of
\mathcal{T}
and we denote this quantity by
\gamma(\mathcal{T})
.
If
\mathcal{T}
fails to be
\gamma
-bounded, we set
\gamma(\mathcal{T})=\infty
.

The notion of
\gamma
-boundedness is stronger than uniform boundedness, indeed using definition of
\gamma
-boundedness with
N=1
it is easy to see that
\gamma
-boundedness implies uniform boundedness.
We will use also two important facts which we sum up in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset B(X,Y)
be a
\gamma
-bounded set. Then

(1)

the closure
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{so}
of
\mathcal{T}
in the
strong operator topology is
\gamma
-bounded with

\gamma(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{so})=\gamma(\mathcal{T})
.

(2)

The absolute convex hull of
\mathcal{T}
,
absconv(\mathcal{T})
is
\gamma
-bounded with
\gamma(absconv(\mathcal{T}))=\gamma(\mathcal{T})
.

We now turn to the definition of
\gamma
-spaces.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space.
A linear operator
T:H\rightarrow X
is called
\gamma
-summing if

\left\|T\right\|_{\gamma}:=\sup\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes Th_{n}\right\|_{G(X)}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all finite orthonormal systems
\{h_{1},...,h_{n}\}
in
H
.
We let
\gamma_{\infty}(H;X)
denote the space of all
\gamma
-summing operators and
we endow it with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\gamma}
. Then

\gamma_{\infty}(H;X)
is a Banach space. Clearly any finite rank (bounded) operator
is a
\gamma
-summing operator. We let
\gamma(H;X)
be
the closure in
\gamma_{\infty}(H;X)
of the space of finite rank operators from
H
into
X
.

We let
\gamma^{\prime}_{\infty}(H;X)
be the space of bounded operators
T:H\mapsto X
such that

\left\|T\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(H;X)}=\sup\{trace(T^{*}\circ S)\;|\;S:H\mapsto X^{*},\,\left\|S\right\|_{\gamma(H;X)\leq 1},\,dim\,S(H)<\infty\}

and we denote by
\gamma^{\prime}(H;X)
the closure of the finite dimensional operators in
\gamma^{\prime}_{\infty}(H;X)
. See
[
9
, section 5]
for details about spaces
\gamma^{\prime}(H;X)
and
\gamma_{\infty}^{\prime}(H;X)
. When
X
is
K
-convex (see
[
8
, Section 7.4.]
for details about
K
-convexity) then one has

\gamma^{\prime}(H;X^{*})=\gamma(H;X^{*}).

Let
(S,\mu)
be a measure space. We say that a function

f:S\rightarrow X
is weakly
L^{2}
if for each
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,
the function
s\mapsto\langle f(s),x^{*}\rangle
is measurable and belongs to
L^{2}(S)
.
If
f:S\rightarrow X
is measurable and weakly
L^{2}
, one can define an operator
\mathbb{I}_{f}:L^{2}(S)\to X
,
given by

\mathbb{I}_{f}(g):=\int_{S}g(s)f(s)d\mu,\quad g\in L^{2}(S),

where this integral is defined in the Pettis sense.
We let
\gamma(S;X)
(resp.
\gamma^{\prime}(S,X)
) be the space of all measurable and weakly
L^{2}

functions
f:S\rightarrow X
such that
\mathbb{I}_{f}

belongs to
\gamma(L^{2}(S);X)
(resp.
\gamma^{\prime}(L^{2}(S);X)
). We endow it with
\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma(S;X)}:=\left\|\mathbb{I}_{f}\right\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(S);X)}
(resp.
\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(S;X)}:=\left\|\mathbb{I}_{f}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(L^{2}(S);X)}
).

The next result is an inequality of Hölder type
[
9
, Corollary 5.5.]
.

Theorem 4.3

(
\gamma
-Hölder inequality)
.

If
f:S\rightarrow X
and
g:S\rightarrow X^{*}
belongs to

\gamma(S;X)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(S;X^{*})
, respectively, then
\langle f,g\rangle
belong to
L^{1}(S)
and we have

\left\|\langle f,g\rangle\right\|_{L^{1}(S)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma(S;X)}\left\|g\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(S;X^{*})}.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and
x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{N}^{*}\in X^{*}
. By construction,

(4.2)

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}=\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}.

As a particular case of Theorem
4.3
one has

\big|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x_{k},x^{*}_{k}\rangle\big|\leq\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\left\|(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

Further this inequality is optimal, namely

(4.3)

\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}=\sup\Big\{\big|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x_{k},x^{*}_{k}\rangle\big|:(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\subset X^{*},\,\left\|(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}\leq 1\Big\}

and

(4.4)

\left\|(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}=\sup\Big\{\big|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle z_{k},x_{k}^{*}\rangle\big|:(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\subset X,\,\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\leq 1\Big\}.

Furthermore for each sequence
(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X^{*}
, one has

(4.5)

\left\|(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N};X^{*})}=\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\left\|(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

We finally state an extension result (
[
8
, Theorem 9.6.1.]
and
[
9
, Remark 5.4.]
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
H
and
K
be Hibert spaces and
U\in B(H^{*},K^{*})
. Then
U\otimes I_{X}
extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator
\widetilde{U}\in B(\gamma(H;X),\gamma(K;X))
of the same norm.

Moreover
U\otimes I_{X^{*}}
extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator
\widehat{U}\in B(\gamma^{\prime}(H;X^{*}),\gamma^{\prime}(K;X^{*}))
of the same norm.

4.1.  Power
\gamma
-bounded operators

Definition 4.6
.

Let
T\in B(X)
be power bounded. We say that
T
is power
\gamma
-bounded if the set
\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is
\gamma
-bounded. When
T
is power
\gamma
-bounded we will denote by
C_{\gamma}
the
\gamma
-bound
\gamma(\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\})
.

Example 4.7
.

The shift operator
U
defined by (
2.11
) is obviously power bounded but for
1<p\neq 2<\infty
,
U
is not power
\gamma
-bounded. Assume that
1<p<2
and let
(\delta_{0})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
the sequence defined by
\delta_{0}(0)=1
and

\delta(k)=0
for each
k\neq 0
. Then one has

\left\|\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|U^{(i-1)}\delta_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{l_{p}}=n^{\frac{1}{p}}

whereas

\left\|\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\delta_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{l_{p}}=n^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The proof in the case
2<p<\infty
is similar.

Example 4.8
.

A Ritt operator
T
is called
\gamma
-Ritt if the set

\{(\lambda-1)R(\lambda,T):\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\overline{\mathbb{D}}\}

is
\gamma
-bounded. It turns out
T
is
\gamma
-Ritt if and only if the two sets

\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{n(I-T)T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}

are
\gamma
-bounded. It follows that any
\gamma
-Ritt operator is power
\gamma
-bounded. In
[
2
]
we construct a Ritt operator such that the set
\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is not
\gamma
-bounded (in particular
T
is not
\gamma
-Ritt). An interesting problem which is still open is to construct a Ritt operator which is not
\gamma
-Ritt but which is power
\gamma
-bounded.

Example 4.9
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space,
m:\Omega\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}
an element of
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
1\leq p<\infty
. We define the bounded multiplier
T_{m}\in B(L^{p}(\Omega))
by

(T_{m}f)(s):=m(s)f(s),\quad s\in\Omega.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
one has

(T_{m})^{n}=T_{m^{n}}.

4.4.  Characterization of power
\gamma
-bounded on Banach space
X

In the following, the space
L^{2}((0,2\pi))
will be equipped with the norm

\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(s)|^{2}ds,\quad f\in L^{2}((0,2\pi)).

Therefore, the Fourier-Parseval operator

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathcal{F}&:&L^{2}([0,2\pi))&\to&l^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
&&f&\mapsto&(c_{n}(f))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\\
\end{array}

is an isometry. Here
c_{n}(f)
is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient defined by

c_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(t)e^{-int}dt.

We give a characterization of power
\gamma
-bounded operators.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. Following assertions are equivalent :

(i)

The operator
T
is power
\gamma
-bounded;

(ii)

The spectrum set
\sigma(T)
is included in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and there exists a constant
C>0

such that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
r_{1},\ldots,r_{N}>1
,
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and

x^{*}_{1},\ldots,x^{*}_{N}\in X^{*}
the functions
(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k}

and
(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T^{*})x^{*}_{k}
are in

\gamma([0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
and
\gamma^{\prime}([0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})
respectively, and
satisfy

(4.8)

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma([0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\leq C\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}

and

(4.9)

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T^{*})x^{*}_{k}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}([0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}\leq\left\|(x_{k}^{*})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})};

(iii)

T
is a
\gamma
-GFS operator.

Proof.

(iii)\implies(i)
is Proposition
4.12
.

(i)\implies(ii)
: Recall that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
one has

R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{n}x_{k}}{(r_{k}e^{it})^{n+1}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{n}x_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}e^{-i(n+1)t}.

We apply Lemma
4.5
with
H=l^{2}_{N}\overset{2}{\otimes}L^{2}((0,2\pi))
,
K=l^{2}_{N}\overset{2}{\otimes}l^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
U:H\rightarrow K
defined by

U\big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}e_{k}\otimes f_{k}\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}e_{k}\otimes(c_{n}(f_{k}))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\quad f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in L^{2}((0,2\pi)).

We obtain that
(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{T^{n}x_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}
belongs to
\gamma(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
and

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma([0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}=\left\|(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{T^{n}x_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}.

But, one has by (
4.2
)

\left\|(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{T^{n}x_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\gamma_{k,n}\otimes T^{n}x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)},

where
(\gamma_{k,n})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N},n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
is a family of independant complex valued standard Gaussian variables.
Using the
\gamma
-boundedness of
\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
it follows

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\gamma_{k,n}\otimes T^{n}x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}\leq C_{\gamma}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\gamma_{k,n}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}.

Furthermore
\left\|\Bigg(\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\Bigg)_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\right\|_{l^{2}}=1
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
therefore

\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\gamma_{k,n}\Big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}

is a sequence of independent standard Gaussian variables, which implies

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\gamma_{k,n}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}=\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}.

Hence one obtains (
4.8
).

Likewise, using Lemma
4.5
we obtain
(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}
belongs to
\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})
and

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T^{*})x^{*}_{k}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}([0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}=\left\|(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x^{*}_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

Furthermore, one has by (
4.5
)

\left\|(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x^{*}_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}=\lim\limits_{M\rightarrow\infty}\left\|(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x^{*}_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{M}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

But, by (
4.4
)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left\|(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x^{*}_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{M}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big\langle x_{n,k},\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x^{*}_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\big\rangle\Big|:\left\|(x_{n,k})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{M}\cup\{0\},k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{M}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N},X)}\leq 1\Big\},

and for
M\in\mathbb{N}
, using Theorem
4.3
,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x_{n,k},\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x^{*}_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T}^{n}x_{n,k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}},x_{k}^{*}\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T}^{n}x_{n,k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{M}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N},X)}}\left\|(x_{k}^{*})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

By previous computations which leads to (
4.8
), for each
M\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\left\|\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T}^{n}x_{n,k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{M}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\leq C_{\gamma}\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}.

This implies

\left\|(n,k)\mapsto\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}\frac{{T^{*}}^{n}x^{*}_{k}}{r_{k}^{n+1}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{M}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}\leq C_{\gamma}\left\|(x_{k}^{*})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

Hence one obtains (
4.9
).

(ii)\implies(iii)
: Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
r_{1},\ldots,r_{N}>1
,
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and
x^{*}_{1},\ldots,x^{*}_{N}\in X^{*}
. One has

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle(r_{k}^{2}-1)R(r_{k}e^{it},T)^{2}x_{k},x_{k}^{*}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle(r_{k}^{2}-1)R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k},R(r_{k}e^{it},T)^{*}x_{k}^{*}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle=\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\langle\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k},\sqrt{r_{k}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)^{*}x_{k}^{*}\rangle\right\|_{L^{1}((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N})}.

By Theorem
4.3

) and (

),

\displaystyle\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\langle\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k},\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)^{*}x_{k}^{*}\rangle\right\|_{L^{1}((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\left\|\sqrt{r_{k}^{2}-1}R(r_{k}e^{it},T)^{*}x_{k}^{*}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

Hence
T
has

-

Remark 4.19
.

When

4.5.  Peller calculus

We denote by
H^{p}
the classical Hardy space over the unit disk. Let
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
be the set of all function
F:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that there exist two sequences
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
C(\mathbb{T})
and
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
H^{1}
satisfying

(4.12)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{1}<\infty

and

(4.13)

\forall z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}},\quad F(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}\star h_{k}(z).

We endow
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
with the norm

\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\inf\big\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{1}\big\}

where the infimum runs over all sequences
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
C(\mathbb{T})
and
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
H^{1}
satifsying (
4.12
) and (
4.13
). It is known (see
[
13
, Lemma 3.6.]
) that with this norm
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
is a Banach algebra for pointwise multiplication.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
T\in B(H)
be a power-bounded operator. According to
[
13
, Theorem 3.5.]
or
[
14
, Proposition 4.11]
there exists
C>0
such that for each polynomial
P
one has

\left\|P(T)\right\|\leq C\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}.

Now since the set of polynomial is dense in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
the bounded algebra homomorphism
P\rightarrow P(T)
extends to a bounded algebra homomorphism from
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
into
B(H)
. Our aim is now to give a
\gamma
-version of this result.

Theorem 4.20
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. Let
T\in B(X)
be a power
\gamma
-bounded operator. Then the set

\{P(T):P\text{ is a polynomial with }\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq 1\}

is
\gamma
-bounded.

Proof.

We adapt an argument from
[
14
, Proposition 4.16.]
. First we show that the set

\Gamma=\{f\star(uv)(T):f\in C(\mathbb{T}),u,v\text{ are polynomials},\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\left\|u\right\|_{2}\left\|v\right\|_{2}\leq 1\}

is
\gamma
-bounded. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in C(\mathbb{T})
and let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}
,
v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}
be polynomials with
\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{2}\leq 1
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
,
x^{*}_{1},\ldots,x^{*}_{N}\in X^{*}
. One has, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}

\langle\big(f_{k}\star(u_{k}v_{k})\big)(T)x_{k},x^{*}_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f_{k}(e^{is})\langle u_{k}(e^{-is}T)x_{k},v_{k}(e^{-is}T)^{*}x^{*}_{k}\rangle ds.

It follows, using Theorem
4.3
, that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\langle\big(f_{k}\star

\displaystyle(u_{k}v_{k})\big)(T)x_{k},x^{*}_{k}\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{|f_{k}(e^{is})|}{\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{2}}|\langle u_{k}(e^{-is}T)x_{k},v_{k}(e^{-is}T)^{*}x^{*}_{k}\rangle|ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\big|\big\langle\frac{u_{k}(e^{-is}T)x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}},\frac{v_{k}(e^{-is}T)^{*}x_{k}^{*}}{\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{2}}\big\rangle\big|ds

\displaystyle=\left\|(s,k)\mapsto\langle\frac{u_{k}(e^{-is}T)x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}},\frac{v_{k}(e^{-is}T)^{*}x_{k}^{*}}{\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{2}}\big\rangle\right\|_{L^{1}((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|(s,k)\mapsto\frac{u_{k}(e^{-is}T)x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}}\right\|_{\gamma((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\left\|(s,k)\mapsto\frac{v_{k}(e^{-is}T)^{*}x^{*}_{k}}{\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{2}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

By Lemma
4.5
and the
\gamma
-boundedness of
\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
one obtains

\displaystyle\left\|(s,k)\mapsto\frac{u_{k}(e^{-is}T)x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}}\right\|_{\gamma((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}

\displaystyle=\left\|(n,k)\mapsto c_{n}(u_{k})T^{n}\Big(\frac{x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}}\Big)\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}\otimes T^{n}\Big(\frac{c_{n}(u_{k})x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}}\Big)\right\|_{G(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}\otimes\frac{c_{n}(u_{k})x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}}\right\|_{G(X)}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
,
\displaystyle\frac{\left\|(c_{n}(u_{k}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\right\|_{l^{2}}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}}=1
, therefore

\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{n,k}\otimes\frac{c_{n}(u_{k})x_{k}}{\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}}\right\|_{G(X)}\leq\left\|(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}.

Then arguing as in the proof of Theorem
4.18
,

\left\|(s,k)\mapsto\frac{v_{k}(e^{-is}T)^{*}x^{*}_{k}}{\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{2}}\right\|_{\gamma((0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}\leq C_{\gamma}\left\|(x^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}}\right\|_{\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}.

This shows that
\Gamma
is
\gamma
-bounded.

Now if
P=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f_{k}\star h_{k}
is a polynomial with
f_{k}\in C(\mathbb{T})
,
h_{k}\in H^{1}
and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{1}\leq 1
then using the facts that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

h_{k}
can be written as a product
h_{k}=u_{k}v_{k}
with
u_{k},v_{k}\in H^{2}
and
\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{1}=\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{2}

and that the set of polynomials is dense in
H^{2}
one obtains that
P\in\overline{absconv(\Gamma)}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|}
.
Finally one has the following inclusion

\{P(T):P\text{ is a polynomial with }\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq 1\}\subset\overline{absconv(\Gamma)}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|},

and the latter set is
\gamma
-bounded thanks to Proposition
4.2
whence the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.21
.

Since the set
\{P(T):P\text{ is a polynomial with }\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq 1\}
is
\gamma
-bounded it is uniformly bounded. Therefore, since polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
, the homomorphism

u:P\mapsto P(T)

extends to a bounded algebra homomorphism from
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
into
B(X)
. Theorem
4.20
implies that this homomorphism is
\gamma
-bounded, that is
\{u(f):f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}):\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq 1\}
is
\gamma
-bounded.

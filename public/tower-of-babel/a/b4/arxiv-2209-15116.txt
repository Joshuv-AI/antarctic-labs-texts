Tropical adic spaces I: The continuous spectrum of a topological semiring
Netanel Friedenberg, Kalina Mincheva

Abstract
. Towards building tropical analogues of adic spaces, we study certain spaces of prime congruences as a topological semiring replacement for the space of continuous valuations on a topological ring. This requires building the theory of topological idempotent semirings, and we consider semirings of convergent power series as a primary example. We consider the semiring of convergent power series as a topological space by defining a metric on it. We check that, in tropical toric cases, the proposed objects carry meaningful geometric information. In particular, we show that the dimension behaves as expected. We give an explicit characterization of the points in terms of classical polyhedral geometry in a follow up paper.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context of this work

Tropical varieties are often described as combinatorial shadows of algebraic varieties. However, they do not naturally carry any scheme structure. In tropical geometry most algebraic computations are done on the classical side, using the algebra of the original variety. The theory developed so far has explored the geometric aspect of tropical varieties as opposed to the underlying (semiring) algebra and there are still many commutative algebra tools and notions without a tropical analogue.

In recent years, there has been a lot of effort dedicated to developing the necessary tools for semiring commutative algebra and tropical scheme theory using many different frameworks.
Among these are prime congruences
[

[

[

[

The goal of these programs is to endow the varieties with extra structure analogous to the extra structure schemes carry over classical varieties.
This, in turn, would allow for the further exploration of the properties of tropicalized spaces independent of their classical algebraic counterparts.

One peculiarity of these frameworks is that the framework itself is purely algebraic, but the theory does not recover the scheme points or classical points of a variety. Instead, some of these theories can reconstruct the underlying topological space of the Berkovich analytification of an affine variety. Motivated by this discrepancy, we work towards developing an analytic theory of tropical spaces, namely tropical adic spaces. The present paper is the first stage of this program.

Tropical methods have gained a lot of popularity in recent years because they provide a new set of purely combinatorial tools which have been used to approach classical problems, notably in the study of curves and their moduli spaces

One of the motivations for seeking scheme structure on a tropical variety is to get better control over the tropical objects via a functorial construction. Some potential applications of this would be as follows.

(1) While tropical geometry has been very useful in enumerative geometry, there are situations where there are tropical sub-objects that cannot be realized as tropicalizations of algebraic sub-objects and situations where multiple algebraic sub-objects tropicalize to the same tropical sub-object. By building a tropical theory with more nuanced algebraic information, one would be able both to distinguish non-realizable tropical sub-objects and to count tropical sub-objects with multiplicities.

(2) Tropical methods have been very successful in showing that specific subsets of moduli spaces of curves are nonempty by constructing algebraic curves with certain properties. These constructions proceed by first finding an abstract tropical curve with corresponding properties. However, the existing notions of abstract tropical varieties in higher dimensions (such as those found in

(3) One fundamental limitation on the use of tropical geometry so far has been that the tropicalization of a variety is dependent upon an embedding of that variety in a toric variety. Therefore, not every property of the tropicalization is an inherent property of the variety. However, the Abel-Jacobi map of a totally degenerate pointed curve canonically embeds it into its Jacobian, which in turn can be tropicalized. Unfortunately, no existing tropical scheme theory encompasses this tropicalization, as it inherently uses non-archimedean analytic geometry. Our theory, however, will be analytic in nature and so should enable us to recover more information about (and invariants of) the curve.

(4) Toroidal morphisms between toroidal embeddings are a ubiquitous class of morphisms of varieties. They are used, for example, in semisistable reduction results (see

In this and other contexts, the way toroidal embeddings are used is to transform a question about morphisms of varieties into a question about morphisms of cone complexes. However, it is not known what property of a map of cone complexes should correspond to properness of the map of toroidal embeddings. We expect that our theory will provide a semiring version of the valuative criteria of properness which will allow us to translate properness of a toroidal morphism into a condition on the proposed tropical adic spaces.

As the previous sentence alludes, they treat valuations as maps from a ring to a particular totally ordered abelian group while our valuations are morphisms in the category of semirings.

Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

By a
semiring

R
we mean a commutative semiring with multiplicative unit. That is,

is a set which is a commutative monoid with respect to each of two binary operations: an addition operation

Definition 2.2
.

A

is a semiring in which all nonzero elements have a multiplicative inverse.

Definition 2.3

We call a semiring

for all

If

With respect to this order,

is the least upper bound of

and

Some of the idempotent semifields that we use throughout the paper are:

•

The

is the semifield with two elements

, where

is the multiplicative identity,

•

The

•

The semifield

and the operations are the restrictions of the operations on

•

The semifield

Throughout this paper all monoids, rings and semirings are assumed to be commutative. All rings and semirings have a multiplicative unit. Whenever we use the word semiring,
we refer to an additively idempotent semiring.
Our focus will be on totally ordered semifields which are different from

Let

3.
The continuous spectrum
\operatorname{Cont}A
of a topological semiring
A

In this section we define the continuous spectrum of a topological semiring
A
in terms of continuity of a morphism
A\to K
with
K
a totally ordered semifield. We start by defining a topology on any totally ordered semifield. The results in this section rely heavily on the assumption that the semifields we consider are infinite, i.e., not equal to
\mathbb{B}
.

On any totally ordered semifield
K

we define the
Huber topology

as follows.
6
6

6

This is a standard construction for totally ordered abelian groups in adic geometry first defined in
[
35
]
; one recent reference is Remark 1.17 in
[
65
]
. This is the finest topology that makes a surjective valuation on a field continuous when we give the field the valuation/metric topology. That is, if
F
is a field with a surjective valuation to
S
(i.e.,
F
has value group
S^{\times}
) and valuation ring
R
, then the Huber topology is the quotient topology on
S\cong F/R^{\times}
.
A subset
U
of
K
is open if and only if either
0_{K}
is not in
U
or if
U
contains
[0_{K},a]
for some
a\in K^{\times}
. A base for this topology is given by the sets
\{a\}
and
[0_{K},a]
for all
a\in K^{\times}
. It is routine to check that this topology makes
K
into a topological semiring, and that it is Hausdorff.
Whenever we consider a totally ordered semifield as a topological semiring, it will be using
the Huber topology
unless specifically stated otherwise. We start by considering when a homomorphism of totally ordered semifields is continuous.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi:S\to K
be a homomorphism of totally ordered semifields. The following are equivalent.

(1)

The homomorphism
\varphi
is continuous.

(2)

For every
a\in K^{\times}
there is some
b\in S^{\times}
such that
\varphi(b)\leq a
.

(3)

For every
a\in K^{\times}
there is some
b\in S^{\times}
such that
\varphi(b)<a
.

Proof.

We first show that
(1)
and
(2)
are equivalent.
Because any homomorphism of semifields has trivial ideal-kernel, for any
a\in K^{\times}
we automatically have that
\varphi^{-1}(a)
is open. So
\varphi
is continuous if and only if
\varphi^{-1}([0_{K},a])
is open for all
a\in K^{\times}
. Since
\varphi^{-1}([0_{K},a])
contains
0_{S}
, this happens exactly if
\varphi^{-1}([0_{K},a])
contains
[0_{S},b]
for some
b\in S^{\times}
. This, in turn, is equivalent to having
\varphi([0_{S},b])\subset[0_{K},a]
. Because
\varphi
, as a homomorphism of semirings, sends
0_{S}
to
0_{K}
and respects the ordering, this happens if and only if
\varphi(b)\leq a
.

Clearly
(3)
implies
(2)
. For the other direction, note that, for any
a\in K^{\times}
, there is some
a^{\prime}\in K^{\times}
with
a^{\prime}<a
. If
a>1_{K}
we can take
a^{\prime}=a^{-1}
, if
a<1_{K}
we can take
a^{\prime}=a^{2}
, and if
a=1_{K}
such an
a^{\prime}
exists because
K\neq\mathbb{B}
. Then by
(2)
there is some
b\in S^{\times}
such that
\varphi(b)\leq a^{\prime}<a
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Any surjective homomorphism of totally ordered semifields is continuous.

Proof.

This follows because any surjective homomorphism satisfies
(2)
of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
.
Then any continuous homomorphism
\varphi:S\to K
of totally ordered semifields is injective.

Proof.

It suffices to show that, for
S
a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
,
(3)
of Lemma
3.1
implies that
\varphi
is injective. Since
\varphi
is a homomorphism of semifields it has trivial ideal-kernel, so
\varphi
is injective if and only if the corresponding map
\varphi^{\times}:S^{\times}\to K^{\times}
is injective. Since
S\subseteq\mathbb{T}
, the only proper convex subgroup of
S^{\times}
is
\{1_{S}\}
. So if
\varphi^{\times}
were not injective, it would have to map all of
S^{\times}
to
1_{K}
. But this contradicts
(3)
of Lemma
3.1
applied with

.
∎

The following proposition concerns the continuity of an arbitrary function
\varphi:X\to K
with
K
a totally ordered semifield and
X
a topological space. In practice we will apply it when
\varphi
is a homomorphism of totally ordered semifields. While we only need this result for these cases, the greater generality makes it easier to state and prove.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a topological space, and let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be totally ordered semifields. Suppose
\varphi:X\to K
is a function,
\psi:K\to K^{\prime}
is an injective homomorphism of totally ordered semifields, and
\psi\circ\varphi:X\to K^{\prime}
is continuous. Then
\varphi
is continuous.

Proof.

Consider
a\in K^{\times}
.
Since
\psi^{-1}(\psi(a))=\{a\}
,
\varphi^{-1}(a)=(\psi\circ\varphi)^{-1}(\psi(a))
, which is open in
X
because
\psi(a)\neq 0_{K^{\prime}}
and
\psi\circ\varphi
is continuous.

So it suffices to show that
\varphi^{-1}([0_{K},a])
is open in
X
for each
a\in K^{\times}
. Since
\psi
is injective and order-preserving,
[0_{K},a]=\psi^{-1}([0_{K^{\prime}},\psi(a)])
. So
\varphi^{-1}([0_{K},a])=(\psi\circ\varphi)^{-1}([0_{K^{\prime}},\psi(a)])
, which is open in
X
because
\psi\circ\varphi
is continuous.
∎

In what follows, we make use of classical results on totally ordered abelian groups. For the basics of, and our choice of notation for, totally ordered abelian groups we refer the reader to Appendix
A.1
.
Let
G
be a totally ordered abelian group, written multiplicatively. A
lexicographic product decomposition of

is a way of writing

as a product of abelian groups with
G_{1}
and

totally ordered abelian groups such that the order on

is the lexicographic order on

.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{K^{\prime}}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\scriptstyle{\psi^{\prime}}

of homomorphisms of totally ordered semifields. Suppose there are lexicographic product decompositions of

and
(K^{\prime})^{\times}
such that
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
identify
S^{\times}
with the first factor in each decomposition. Then
\varphi
is continuous.

Proof.

Identifying

with its image in each of

and

and

as

and

for some totally ordered abelian groups

and

. Since
\varphi|_{K^{\times}}:K^{\times}\to(K^{\prime})^{\times}
is a homomorphism of totally ordered abelian groups, there are homomorphisms
\phi_{11}:S^{\times}\to S^{\times}
,
\phi_{22}:G\to G^{\prime}
,
\phi_{21}:S^{\times}\to G^{\prime},
and
\phi_{12}:G\to S^{\times}
such that, for all

and

\varphi(b_{1},b_{2})=(\phi_{11}(b_{1})\phi_{12}(b_{2}),\phi_{21}(b_{1})\phi_{22}(b_{2}))
. Because the above diagram commutes and we have used
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
to identify

with subgroups of

and
(K^{\prime})^{\times}
, we have
\phi_{11}=\mathrm{id}_{S^{\times}}
and
\phi_{21}
is the constant function

. Thus,

\varphi(b_{1},b_{2})=(b_{1}\cdot\phi_{12}(b_{2}),\phi_{22}(b_{2}))
.

We claim that
\phi_{12}=1_{S^{\times}}
. To see this, fix
g\in G
. For any
s\in S^{\times}
which is greater than

, we have
(s,1_{G})>(1_{S},g)
, so
(s,1_{G^{\prime}})=\varphi(s,1_{G})\geq\varphi(1_{S},g)=(\phi_{12}(g),\phi_{22}(g))
. By the definition of the lexicographic order this tells us that

; since this is true for all

, we must have

. The same statement is also true if we replace

Say

(b_{1},\phi_{2}(b_{2}))\leq_{lex}(a_{1},a_{2})
. Pick any

which is less than

and set

\varphi(b)=(b_{1},\phi_{22}(1_{G}))=(b_{1},1_{G^{\prime}})\leq_{lex}(a_{1},a_{2})

∎

The goal of this section is to develop tools for the study of

Lemma 3.22

Let

be a sub-semifield of

and let

\textstyle{\mathbb{T}}

By Theorem

,
\kappa(P)^{\times}
, and

as totally ordered abelian groups.

Because

, Lemma

tells us that for any

such that

there is no

Suppose there were some

such that

for some

. Then the convex subgroup

of
\kappa(P)^{\times}

only in

. In particular,
\kappa(P)^{\times}/G\neq\mathbb{B}
. So the totally ordered semifield

is a quotient of

, and by Corollary

\kappa(P)\to S^{\prime}
is continuous. Thus, the congruence-kernel

of the map

is a prime congruence which strictly contains

, and satisfies

, contradicting the maximality of

.

Thus, for any
s\in S^{\times}
and

with

there are natural numbers

such that

is an archimedean totally ordered abelian group, this implies that

has only one archimedean class, i.e.,

is archimedean. Thus, by Theorem

embeds into

∎

Remark 3.23
.

We will show later, in Proposition

The next proposition will allow us to use Hahn embeddings to prove statements about containments between primes in
\operatorname{Cont}{A}
. Before that, we make a simple observation.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
\varphi:K\to K^{\prime}
be a homomorphism of totally ordered semifields and let
a,b\in K
.

(1)

If
a\leq b
then
\varphi(a)\leq\varphi(b)
.

(2)

If
\varphi(a)<\varphi(b)
then
a<b
.

Proof.

(1) Since
\varphi
, as a homomorphism of semirings, preserves addition, and the relation
\leq
is defined in terms of addition,
\varphi
preserves
\leq
.

(2) This is the contrapositive of (1), with the roles of
a
and
b
flipped.
∎

Proposition 3.25

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, and let
A
be a topological
S
-algebra. Let

with
P^{\prime}
maximal with respect to inclusion. Let
I
be the set of archimedean classes of
\kappa(P)
, which by Lemma
3.19
has a least element. Let
\text{\LARGE$\pi$}:\mathbb{T}^{(I)}\to\mathbb{T}
be the canonical projection. By Proposition
3.21
and Lemma
3.22
there are semiring homomorphisms
\varphi_{P}:A\to\mathbb{T}^{(I)}
and
\varphi_{P^{\prime}}:A\to\mathbb{T}
such that

is the congruence-kernel of
\varphi_{P}
,
P^{\prime}
is the congruence-kernel of
\varphi_{P^{\prime}}

if and only if
\varphi_{P^{\prime}}=\text{\LARGE$\pi$}\circ\varphi_{P}

If
\varphi_{P^{\prime}}=\text{\LARGE$\pi$}\circ\varphi_{P}

\varphi_{P}
, is contained in

, which is the congruence-kernel of
\varphi_{P^{\prime}}
.
So suppose

. We wish to find a pair

By hypothesis, there is some

. On the other hand, fixing some

\varphi_{P^{\prime}}(a)=\text{\LARGE$\pi$}\circ\varphi_{P}(a)\in\mathbb{T}^{\times}\backslash\{1_{\mathbb{T}}\}

and
\text{\LARGE$\pi$}\circ\varphi_{P}(f)

, and the existence of

in

. We now consider two cases.

.

\varphi_{P^{\prime}}(f)>\varphi_{P^{\prime}}(a)^{m/n}>\text{\LARGE$\pi$}\circ\varphi_{P}(f)

.
∎

Corollary 3.28
.

Let

be a sub-semifield of

and let

be a topological

-algebra. If

Let

be the unique maximal element of

containing

. Since

is also the unique maximal element of

gives us that, once we choose

Definition 3.29

If

is a topological semiring, we let

denote the set of those

which have trivial ideal-kernel. Similarly, if

is a topological semiring and

-algebra we let

denote the set of those

We use the notation

Remark 3.33

Proposition 3.34
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
,
A
be a topological
S
-algebra, and

.
If there is an embedding
\kappa(P)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}
, then

is maximal with respect to inclusion.

Suppose

\operatorname{Cont}A

By Corollary

there is a surjection

. The congruence-kernel of

is a prime congruence on
\kappa(P)

has archimedean rank 1 (as it embeds into

3.2.
\operatorname{Cont}
as a topological space

Let
A
be a topological semiring. We now endow
\operatorname{Cont}A
with the topology generated by the sets

U(f,g):=\{P\in\operatorname{Cont}A\ :\ |f|_{P}\leq|g|_{P}\neq 0_{\kappa(P)}\}

for
f,g\in A
. This is analogous to the topology defined on the continuous spectrum of a ring; see
[
35
, Section 0]
or
[
65
, Sections 4.1 and 7.2]
. We give
\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}A\subseteq\operatorname{Cont}A
the subspace topology.

Lemma 3.39
.

The sets
U(f,g)
form a basis for the topology on
\operatorname{Cont}A
.

Proof.

Note that
U(1,1)=\operatorname{Cont}A
. So it suffices to show that, for any
f,f^{\prime},g,g^{\prime}\in A
,
U(f,g)\cap U(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})=U(\tilde{f},\tilde{g})
for some
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\in A
.

Straightforward computation shows that
U(f,g)\cap U(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})=U(fg^{\prime},gg^{\prime})\cap U(gf^{\prime},gg^{\prime})
. Since
|fg^{\prime}+gf^{\prime}|_{P}=|fg^{\prime}|_{P}+|gf^{\prime}|_{P}
is the maximum of
|fg^{\prime}|_{P}
and
|gf^{\prime}|_{P}
in
\kappa(P)
, we have that
U(fg^{\prime},gg^{\prime})\cap U(gf^{\prime},gg^{\prime})=U(fg^{\prime}+gf^{\prime},gg^{\prime})
.
∎

We now consider specializations in
\operatorname{Cont}A
.

Proposition 3.40
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
A
be a topological
S
-algebra. For any
P,P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}A
,
P^{\prime}
is a specialization of
P
if and only if
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
.

Proof.

Note that
P^{\prime}
is a specialization of
P
if and only if whenever
P^{\prime}\in U(f,g)
, we also have
P\in U(f,g)
. Equivalently,
P^{\prime}
is a specialization of
P
if and only if, for all
f,g\in A
,
|f|_{P^{\prime}}\leq|g|_{P^{\prime}}\neq 0
implies
|f|_{P}\leq|g|_{P}\neq 0
.

If
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
, then, by
Corollaries
3.28
and
3.35
,
whenever
|f|_{P^{\prime}}\leq|g|_{P^{\prime}}\neq 0
we have
|f|_{P}\leq|g|_{P}\neq 0
, so
P^{\prime}
is a specialization of
P
.

Now suppose
P^{\prime}
is a specialization of
P
. Fix
(f,g)\in P^{\prime}
; we wish to show that
(f,g)\in P
. If
|g|_{P^{\prime}}\neq 0
, then
|f|_{P^{\prime}}\leq|g|_{P^{\prime}}\neq 0
and
|g|_{P^{\prime}}\leq|f|_{P^{\prime}}\neq 0
, so
|f|_{P}\leq|g|_{P}
and
|g|_{P}\leq|f|_{P}
. Thus,
(f,g)\in P
. If, on the other hand,
|g|_{P^{\prime}}=0
, we still have that, for all
\varepsilon\in S^{\times}
,
(f+\varepsilon,g+\varepsilon)\in P^{\prime}
and
|g+\varepsilon|_{P^{\prime}}\neq 0
. So, by the previous case,
(f+\varepsilon,g+\varepsilon)\in P
. Since
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(f+\varepsilon,g+\varepsilon)=(f,g)
and
P
is closed, we get
(f,g)\in P
.
∎

Corollary 3.41
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
A
be a topological
S
-algebra. Then
\operatorname{Cont}A
is Kolmogorov, i.e.,
\text{T}_{0}
.

Proof.

If
P,P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}A
are topologically indistinguishable, then they are specializations of each other. By Proposition
3.40
,
this implies that
P=P^{\prime}
.
∎

3.3.
\operatorname{Cont}
as a functor

We now want to consider
\operatorname{Cont}A
and its variations as functors. To do this, we need to define several categories.
We denote the category of topological spaces as
\mathcal{T}\text{op}
.

Definition 3.42
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
. We define four categories as follows.

•

The objects of
\mathcal{A}\text{lg}_{S}
are
S
-algebras. The morphisms are all
S
-algebra homomorphisms, i.e., semiring homomorphisms
A\to B
which make the diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

commute.

•

The objects of
\mathcal{T}\text{op}\mathcal{A}\text{lg}_{S}
are topological
S
-algebras. The morphisms are all continuous
S
-algebra homomorphisms.

•

The objects of
\mathcal{A}\text{lg}^{\circ}_{S}
are
S
-algebras. The morphisms are those
S
-algebra homomorphisms which have trivial ideal-kernel.

•

The objects of
\mathcal{T}\text{op}\mathcal{A}\text{lg}^{\circ}_{S}
are topological
S
-algebras. The morphisms are those continuous
S
-algebra homomorphisms with trivial ideal-kernel.

Proposition 3.43
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
.
Then
\operatorname{Cont}:\mathcal{T}\text{op}\mathcal{A}\text{lg}_{S}\to\mathcal{T}\text{op}
is a contravariant functor.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:A\to B
be a morphism in
\mathcal{T}\text{op}\mathcal{A}\text{lg}_{S}
and let
P\in\operatorname{Cont}B
. Then
\varphi^{*}(P)
is a prime congruence on
A
and that
\varphi
induces an injective homomorphism
A/\varphi^{*}(P)\hookrightarrow B/P
. So to get
\varphi^{*}(P)\in\operatorname{Cont}A
we just need to show that
A/\varphi^{*}(P)\neq\mathbb{B}
and the homomorphism
A\to\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))
given by
f\mapsto|f|_{\varphi^{*}(P)}
is continuous.

The injective homomorphism
A/\varphi^{*}(P)\to B/P
induces an injective homomorphism on the residue semifields
\iota=\iota_{P}:\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))\hookrightarrow\kappa(P)
, which makes the diagram

\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{A}}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{B}}
\textstyle{\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{\kappa(P)}

commute. Since
P\in\operatorname{Cont}B
, Lemma
3.13
gives us that the map
S\to B\to\kappa(P)
is injective. By the commutativity of the diagram, this map is
S\to A\to\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))\hookrightarrow\kappa(P)
, so we get that
S\to A\to\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))
is injective. In particular,
\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))\neq\mathbb{B}
, so
A/\varphi^{*}(P)\neq\mathbb{B}
.

Since
\varphi
and
\pi_{B}
are continuous,
\iota\circ\pi_{A}=\pi_{B}\circ\varphi
is continuous. Since
\iota
is an injective homomorphism of totally ordered semifields, Proposition
3.4
gives us that
\pi_{A}
is continuous. Thus,
\varphi^{*}(P)\in\operatorname{Cont}A
.

We have thus shown that
\varphi:A\to B
induces a function
\varphi^{*}:\operatorname{Cont}B\to\operatorname{Cont}A
. To get that
\varphi^{*}
is continuous, it suffices to show that, for any

,

is open in
\operatorname{Cont}B
. In fact, we claim that
(\varphi^{*})^{-1}(U(f,g))=U(\varphi(f),\varphi(g))
. To see this, consider

. Note that
P\in(\varphi^{*})^{-1}(U(f,g))
if and only if
|f|_{\varphi^{*}(P)}\leq|g|_{\varphi^{*}(P)}\neq 0_{\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))}
. Since
\iota_{P}:\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))\to\kappa(P)
is an injective homomorphism of totally ordered semifields,
|f|_{\varphi^{*}(P)}\leq|g|_{\varphi^{*}(P)}\neq 0_{\kappa(\varphi^{*}(P))}
if and only if
\iota_{P}\left(|f|_{\varphi^{*}(P)}\right)\leq\iota_{P}\left(|g|_{\varphi^{*}(P)}\right)\neq 0_{\kappa(P)}
. By the commutativity of the diagram above, this happens if and only if
|\varphi(f)|_{P}\leq|\varphi(g)|_{P}\neq 0_{\kappa(P)}
, i.e., if and only if

. Thus,
(\varphi^{*})^{-1}(U(f,g))=U(\varphi(f),\varphi(g))
and so
\varphi^{*}
is continuous.

The verification that the map
\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{*}
makes
\operatorname{Cont}
into a contravariant functor is routine, and is left to the reader.
∎

4.
The semiring
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
of power series convergent at a prime
P

Let
S\neq\mathbb{B}
be a totally ordered semifield and let
M
be a monoid.
We
define the set of series with coefficients in
S
and exponents in
M
to be

S\llbracket M\rrbracket:=\left\{f=\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}\ :\ f_{u}\in S\right\}.

If
S=\mathbb{T}
and
M=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we view this as an infinite analogue of the tropical Laurent polynomial semiring. However, unlike the set of tropical Laurent polynomials, this set of series does not form a semiring. It does, however, form an
\mathbb{T}
-semimodule with term-by-term addition.
On the other hand, if
M=\mathbb{N}^{n}
then
S\llbracket M\rrbracket=S\llbracket x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rrbracket
is an
S
-algebra. If
S=\mathbb{T}
then this semiring is called the semiring of
tropical power series
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
S(\neq\mathbb{B})
be a totally ordered semifield and
M
be a monoid. Let
P
be a prime congruence on
S[M]
. A series
f=\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}\in S\llbracket M\rrbracket

converges
at
P
if for any
b\in S[M]/P
,
b\neq 0_{S}
, the set

\{u\in M\;:\;|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq b\}

is finite.
We denote the
set of series that converge at
P

by
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
.

By definition, every polynomial converges at
P
. In general, the set
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
contains infinite sums, as in the following examples.

Example 4.2
.

Let
S=\mathbb{T}
,
M=\mathbb{Z}
and let
P
be the prime congruence with defining matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}
. Then

\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=\left\{f=\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}\;:\;f_{u}\to 0_{\mathbb{T}}\text{ as }u\to\pm\infty\right\}.

\diamondsuit

Example 4.3
.

Let
S=\mathbb{T}
,
M=\mathbb{N}
and let
P
be the prime congruence with defining matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&-\infty\end{pmatrix}
. Then
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=\mathbb{T}\llbracket x\rrbracket
, the semiring of tropical power series.

\diamondsuit

Example 4.4
.

Let
S=\mathbb{T}
,
M=\mathbb{N}^{n}
and let
P
be the prime congruence with defining matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}
. Then

\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}f_{u}x^{u}\in\mathbb{T}\llbracket x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rrbracket\;:\;f_{u}\to 0_{\mathbb{T}}\text{ as }|u|\to\infty\right\}

is the
tropical Tate algebra
.

\diamondsuit

Note that, in general,
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is an
S
-sub-semimodule of
S\llbracket M\rrbracket
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
S
be a totally ordered semifield and let
M
be a monoid. Let
A=S[M]
and suppose that
P,P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}A
satisfy
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
. Then
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=\operatorname{Cnvg}(P^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Since
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
, Corollary
3.35
gives us that, for
g,h\in A
, if
|g|_{P}\geq|h|_{P}
then
|g|_{P^{\prime}}\geq|h|_{P^{\prime}}
, and if
|g|_{P^{\prime}}>|h|_{P^{\prime}}
then
|g|_{P}>|h|_{P}
.

Let
f\in S\llbracket M\rrbracket
be a series
f=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}
that converges at
P
. For any
b\in(A/P^{\prime})\backslash\{0_{A}\}\subseteq\kappa(P^{\prime})^{\times}
, by Lemma
3.15
we know that there is some
a\in S^{\times}
such that
|a|_{P^{\prime}}<b
. So

\displaystyle\{u\in M\;:\;|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq b\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{u\in M\;:\;|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}>|a|_{P^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}>|a|_{P}\},

which is finite by the convergence of
f
at
P
.

Suppose that
f
converges at
P^{\prime}
. If
b\in(A/P)\backslash\{0_{A}\}\subseteq\kappa(P)^{\times}
, by Lemma
3.13
there is some
a\in S^{\times}
with
|a|_{P}<b
. Then

\displaystyle\{u\in M\;:\;|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq b\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{u\in M\;:\;|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq|a|_{P}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{u\in M\;:\;|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq|a|_{P^{\prime}}\},

and because
|a|_{P^{\prime}}\neq 0_{A/P^{\prime}}
, the convergence of
f
at
P^{\prime}
gives us that this last set is finite.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
S
be a totally ordered semifield, let
M
be a monoid, and let
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
. Then
f=\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}\in S\llbracket M\rrbracket
converges at
P
if and only if for every element
b\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
the set
\{u\in M\,:\,|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq b\}
is finite.

Proof.

Since the map
S[M]/P\to\kappa(P)
is injective, the “only if” direction is clear. Let
f
converge at
P
. Given
b\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
, there is some
s\in S^{\times}
such that
|s|_{P}\leq b
. This implies that
\{u\in M\,:\,|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq b\}\subseteq\{u\in M\,:\,|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq|s|_{P}\}
, but the bigger set is finite because
|s|_{P}\in(S[M]/P)\backslash\{0_{S}\}
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
S
be a totally ordered semifield, let
M
be a monoid, and let
P
be a prime on
S[M]
. For any
f=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
and any nonempty subset
X\subseteq M
,
there is a maximal element
\displaystyle\sum_{u\in X}|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
of the set
\{|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\,:\,u\in X\}
.
Furthermore, the set
\{v\in X\,:\,|f_{v}\chi^{v}|_{P}=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in X}|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\}

Proof.

Fixing
u_{0}\in X
, let
b=|f_{u_{0}}\chi^{u_{0}}|_{P}
. Then the set
X^{\prime}:=\{u\in X\;:\;|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq b\}
is finite and nonempty.
Thus, the largest of the finitely many values
|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
for

is also the largest value
\displaystyle\sum_{u\in X}|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
of
|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
for

.
Finally,
\{v\in X\,:\,|f_{v}\chi^{v}|_{P}=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in X}|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\}
is finite because it is contained in

.
∎

Given two series

and

in

, we say that

if, for each

5.
The metric topology on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and, as usual, we will assume that

is infinite, i.e., not

. Let
M
be a monoid, and let
P
be a prime congruence on

. We define a (generalized) ultrapseudometric on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
as follows. Given

and

, we set

d(f,g)=d_{P}(f,g):=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
f_{u}>g_{u}\end{subarray}}\left|f_{u}\chi^{u}\right|_{P}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
g_{u}>f_{u}\end{subarray}}\left|g_{u}\chi^{u}\right|_{P}\in S[M]/P\subseteq\kappa(P),

which makes sense by Lemma
4.7
. Note that

is the maximum of the values
|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}+|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
for
u\in M
such that
f_{u}\neq g_{u}
, so we can also write

d_{P}(f,g)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
f_{u}\neq g_{u}\end{subarray}}|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}+|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
f_{u}\neq g_{u}\end{subarray}}|(f_{u}+g_{u})\chi^{u}|_{P}.

Here we use the fact that
|\cdot|_{P}
is a semiring homomorphism and so respects addition.
We say that
d_{P}:\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\to\kappa(P)
is an ultrapseudometric because, for all
f,g,h\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
,

\displaystyle d_{P}(f,g)

\displaystyle\geq 0_{\kappa(P)},

\displaystyle d_{P}(f,f)

\displaystyle=0_{\kappa(P)},

\displaystyle d_{P}(f,g)

\displaystyle=d_{P}(g,f),

and

\displaystyle d_{P}(f,h)

\displaystyle\leq d_{P}(f,g)+d_{P}(g,h)=\max(d_{P}(f,g),d_{P}(g,h)).

If

has trivial ideal-kernel, then

is an ultrametric, in the sense that, in addition to the above properties, for any

,
d_{P}(f,g)=0_{\kappa(P)}
if and only if

.

Remark 5.1
.

The above definition is motivated as follows. The distance
d_{P}(f,g)
is the smallest value of
|f_{1}|_{P}+|g_{1}|_{P}
over all
f_{1},g_{1},h\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
such that
f=h+f_{1}
and
g=h+g_{1}
. This is realized by choosing

h=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in M}\min(f_{u},g_{u})\chi^{u},\ f_{1}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
f_{u}>g_{u}\end{subarray}}f_{u}\chi^{u},\text{ and }g_{1}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
g_{u}>f_{u}\end{subarray}}g_{u}\chi^{u}.

Thus, the definition of
d_{P}(f,g)
gives a way of replicating what “
|f-g|_{P}
” would be if it made sense.

Alternatively, we can motivate this definition in the following way. Suppose that
K
is a field with a nontrivial (non-archimedean) valuation
v:K\twoheadrightarrow S
and
M
is finitely generated (e.g. toric). We extend
v
to a map
v_{1}:K[M]\twoheadrightarrow S[M]
defined by taking the valuation of coefficients, and composing with
|\cdot|_{P}:S[M]\to\kappa(P)
gives a norm
|\cdot|
on
K[M]
. The completion
K\langle M\rangle_{P}
of
K[M]
with respect to this norm admits a natural map
v_{2}:K\langle M\rangle_{P}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
extending

. (When

and

is given by the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}
, this is coefficient-wise valuation from a Tate algebra to a tropical Tate algebra.) Given

d_{P}(f,g)=\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in v_{2}^{-1}(f)\\
G\in v_{2}^{-1}(g)\end{subarray}}|F-G|
, as is realized by picking
H\in v_{2}^{-1}(h)
,

, and

and letting

and

. Because of this, we can say that

is the quotient metric on

as a quotient of

In the same way that a usual metric induces a topology,

induces a topology on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. Similarly, the definition of a Cauchy sequence transfers over to our setup in the obvious way.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
,
M
a monoid, and
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
. Then the addition on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is continuous.

Proof.

The addition on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
will be the map

\mathfrak{Z}:\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\rightarrow\operatorname{Cnvg}(P).

We will show that the inverse of every open ball in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is open in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
, where
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
has the product topology.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
and
r\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
. The preimage of
B(f,r)
is the set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}(B(f,r))

\displaystyle=\left\{(g,h)\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\ :\ d_{P}(g+h,f)<r\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(g,h)\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\ :\ \displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
g_{u}+h_{u}\neq f_{u}\end{subarray}}|(g_{u}+h_{u}+f_{u})\chi^{u}|_{P}<r\right\}.

We want to show that, around each point
(g,h)\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}(B(f,r))
,
there is a product
\mathcal{B}
of open balls such that, for all
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}
, we have
g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}=\mathfrak{Z}(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in B(f,r)
. We first investigate when
g+h\in B(f,r)
. Consider the sets

U_{gr}=\left\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq r\right\}\text{\; and\; }U_{lr}=\left\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}<r\right\}.

With this notation,
g+h\in B(f,r)
if and only if the following condition holds: for all
u\in U_{lr}
, we have
|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P},|h_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}<r
and, for all
u\in U_{gr}
, we have
g_{u}+h_{u}=f_{u}
.

Now we show that, if
g+h\in B(f,r)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in B(g,r)\times B(h,r)
, then
g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}\in B(f,r)
.

For
u\in U_{lr}
we either have
g_{u}=g^{\prime}_{u}
or
g_{u}\neq g^{\prime}_{u}
. In the first case we get that
|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}=|g^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}<r
. In the second case, where
g_{u}\neq g^{\prime}_{u}
, both
|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
and
|g^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
contribute to the distance
d_{P}(g,g^{\prime})
. Since
g^{\prime}\in B(g,r)
, we conclude that

|g^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\leq|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}+|g^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\leq d_{P}(g,g^{\prime})<r.

In either case, we obtain that
|g^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}<r
. Similarly, we observe that
|h^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}<r
.

For
u\in U_{gr}
we can assume without loss of generality that
g_{u}=f_{u}
and that
h_{u}\leq f_{u}
. With this assumption we have that
|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}=|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq r
. If
g^{\prime}_{u}\neq g_{u}
, then

d_{P}(g^{\prime},g)\geq|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}+|g^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq|g_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq r,

but this cannot happen. So we conclude that
g^{\prime}_{u}=g_{u}=f_{u}
.
Looking at
h^{\prime}
, we either have
|h^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}<r
or
|h^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq r
. In the first case, since
|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq r
, we have

|h^{\prime}_{u}|_{P}|\chi^{u}|_{P}=|h^{\prime}_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}<r\leq|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}=|f_{u}|_{P}|\chi^{u}|_{P}.

Since
|\chi^{u}|_{P}\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
, the above inequality implies that

|h^{\prime}_{u}|_{P}<|f_{u}|_{P}
and so
h^{\prime}_{u}<f_{u}
.
In the second case,
|h_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq r
and, since
h^{\prime}\in B(h,r)
, we conclude that
h^{\prime}_{u}=h_{u}\leq f_{u}
. Either way,
h^{\prime}_{u}\leq f_{u}
and, since we already know that
g^{\prime}_{u}=f_{u}
, we obtain that
g^{\prime}_{u}+h^{\prime}_{u}=f_{u}
. Thus,
g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}\in B(f,r)
.
∎

We use the following lemma to prove that the multiplication on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is continuous.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be a toric monoid, and let
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
. For any
g,g^{\prime},h\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
,
d_{P}(gh,g^{\prime}h)\leq d_{P}(g,g^{\prime})|h|_{P}
.

Proof.

For
u\in M
, let

c_{u}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in M\\
v+w=u\end{subarray}}g_{v}h_{w}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{u}^{\prime}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in M\\
v+w=u\end{subarray}}g^{\prime}_{v}h_{w},

so

d_{P}(gh,g^{\prime}h)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
c_{u}\neq c_{u}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|(c_{u}+c_{u}^{\prime})\chi^{u}|_{P}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{P}(g,g^{\prime})=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
g_{u}\neq g_{u}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|(g_{u}+g_{u}^{\prime})\chi^{u}|_{P}.

Fix
u\in M
with
c_{u}\neq c_{u}^{\prime}
; we want to show that
|(c_{u}+c_{u}^{\prime})\chi^{u}|_{P}\leq d_{P}(g,g^{\prime})|h|_{P}
. We assume without loss of generality that
c_{u}>c_{u}^{\prime}
, so
c_{u}+c_{u}^{\prime}=c_{u}
. Pick
v_{0},w_{0}\in M
attaining the maximum in
c_{u}=\displaystyle\sum_{v+w=u}g_{v}h_{w}
, i.e.,
v_{0}+w_{0}=u
and
c_{u}=g_{v_{0}}h_{w_{0}}
. Then

|(c_{u}+c_{u}^{\prime})\chi^{u}|_{P}=|g_{v_{0}}h_{w_{0}}\chi^{u}|_{P}
,
so we want to show that
|g_{v_{0}}h_{w_{0}}\chi^{u}|_{P}\leq d(g,g^{\prime})|h|_{P}
. If
h_{w_{0}}=0_{S}
this is clear, so assume
h_{w_{0}}\in S^{\times}
. Hence, we can divide the inequality
g_{v_{0}}h_{w_{0}}=c_{u}>c_{u}^{\prime}\geq g^{\prime}_{v_{0}}h_{w_{0}}
by
h_{w_{0}}
to get
g_{v_{0}}>g^{\prime}_{v_{0}}
. In particular,
|(g_{v_{0}}+g_{v_{0}}^{\prime})\chi^{v_{0}}|_{P}\leq d_{P}(g,g^{\prime})
. Thus,

|g_{v_{0}}h_{w_{0}}\chi^{u}|_{P}=|g_{v_{0}}\chi^{v_{0}}|_{P}\ |h_{w_{0}}\chi^{w_{0}}|_{P}=|(g_{v_{0}}+g^{\prime}_{v_{0}})\chi^{v_{0}}|_{P}\ |h_{w_{0}}\chi^{w_{0}}|_{P}\leq d_{P}(g,g^{\prime})|h|_{P}.

∎

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and
M
a toric monoid. For any prime
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
, the multiplication on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is continuous.

Proof.

The multiplication on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
will be the map

m:\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\rightarrow\operatorname{Cnvg}(P).

We will show that the inverse image of every open ball in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is open in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)^{2}
with the product topology.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
and
r\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
. The preimage of the ball of radius
r
centered at
f
is the set

m^{-1}(B(f,r))=\left\{(g,h)\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\times\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\ :\ d_{P}(gh,f)<r\right\}.

Analogously to proving that addition is continuous, it suffices to show that if
gh\in B(f,r)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in B(g,r_{1})\times B(h,r_{2})
then
g^{\prime}h^{\prime}\in B(f,r)
, for some choices
r_{1},r_{2}\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
. We will consider 3 cases:

Case 1
:
g=h=0
. In this case we obtain that

|f|_{P}=d_{P}(0,f)=d_{P}(gh,f)<r.

The last inequality holds since
gh\in B(f,r)
. If
r\leq 1
then
r^{2}\leq r
so we set
r_{1}=r
and ensure that
r_{1}^{2}\leq r
. Otherwise, if
r\geq 1
then
1^{2}\leq r
so we set
r_{1}=1
and again ensure that
r_{1}^{2}\leq r
.
Either way, set
r_{2}=r_{1}
. If
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in B(g,r_{1})\times B(h,r_{2})=B(0,r_{1})\times B(0,r_{1})
then

|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|_{P}=|g^{\prime}|_{P}|h^{\prime}|_{P}<r_{1}^{2}\leq r,

and so

d_{P}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime},f)\leq|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|_{P}+|f|_{P}<r+r=r.

Case 2
:
g=0
but
h\neq 0
. In this case we still have that

|f|_{P}=d_{P}(0,f)=d_{P}(gh,f)<r.

Set
r_{1}=r/|h|_{P}
and
r_{2}=|h|_{P}
. For
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in B(g,r_{1})\times B(h,r_{2})=B(0,r_{1})\times B(h,r_{2})
we have that

|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|_{P}=|g^{\prime}|_{P}|h^{\prime}|_{P}<r_{1}|h^{\prime}|_{P}\leq r_{1}(r_{2}+|h|_{P}),

where the last inequality holds since
h^{\prime}\in B(h,r_{2})
. Thus, we obtain that

d_{P}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime},f)\leq|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|_{P}+|f|_{P}<r_{1}(r_{2}+|h|_{P})+r=r+r=r,

where the second-to-last equality is a consequence of our choice of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
.

Case 3
:
g,h\neq 0
. Set
r_{1}=r/|h|_{P}
and
r_{2}=\min(|h|_{P},r/|g|_{P})
. Let
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in B(g,r_{1})\times B(h,r_{2})
. Then, by applying the triangle inequality twice, we get that

d_{P}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime},f)\leq d_{P}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime},gh)+d_{P}(gh,f)\leq d_{P}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime},g^{\prime}h)+d_{P}(g^{\prime}h,gh)+d_{P}(gh,f).

By Lemma
5.10
, this gives us

\displaystyle d_{P}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime},f)

\displaystyle\leq d_{P}(h^{\prime},h)|g^{\prime}|_{P}+d_{P}(g^{\prime},g)|h|_{P}+d_{P}(gh,f)

\displaystyle<r_{2}|g^{\prime}|_{P}+r_{1}|h|_{P}+r

\displaystyle\leq r_{2}(|g|_{P}+r_{1})+r_{1}|h|_{P}+r

\displaystyle=r_{2}|g|_{P}+r_{1}r_{2}+r_{1}|h|_{P}+r=r.

∎

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be a monoid, and let
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
. Then the map
S\to S[M]\to\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is continuous.

Proof.

For any
f\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
and
r\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
, we want to show that
B(f,r)\cap S
is open in
S
. If
0_{S}
is not in
B(f,r)\cap S
then
B(f,r)\cap S
is open.

So suppose
0_{S}\in B(f,r)
, and fix some
a\in S^{\times}
with
|a|_{P}<r
. Then

\displaystyle B(f,r)\cap S

\displaystyle=B(0_{S},r)\cap S

\displaystyle=\{c\in S\ :\ |c|_{P}<r\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\{c\in S\ :\ |c|_{P}\leq|a|_{P}\}

\displaystyle=[0_{S},a],

so
B(f,r)\cap S
is open in
S
.
∎

Remark 5.13
.

Let
S
be a subsemifield of
\mathbb{T}
,
M
a monoid, and
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
. In the induced topology by
d_{P}
on the space of monomials of
S[M]
, every point except for
0_{S}
is isolated. Indeed,
f,g
be distinct monomials (terms) in
S[M]
. Then by definition

d_{P}(f,g)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
f_{u}\neq g_{u}\end{subarray}}|(f_{u}+g_{u})\chi^{u}|_{P}=|f|_{P}+|g|_{P}\geq|f|_{P}.

This shows that the only monomial in the open ball around
f
of radius
|f|_{P}
is
f
itself.

This statement is analogous to the fact that, in any totally ordered semifield, every nonzero point is isolated.

6.
The canonical extension of
P
to a prime on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)

Let
S\neq\mathbb{B}
be a subsemifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be a toric monoid, and let
P
be a prime in
\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
.

Definition 6.1
.

The
canonical extension

Q_{P}
of
P
to
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is the congruence-kernel of the map
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|\cdot|_{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S[M]/P
.
Since
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|\cdot|_{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S[M]/P
is surjective, there is a natural identification of
\kappa(P)
with
\kappa(Q_{P})
, under which we have that
|\sum\limits_{u}f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{Q_{P}}=\max\limits_{u}|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}
.

Our first result about
Q_{P}
is more easily first seen as a result about ultrapseudometrics.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
K
be a totally ordered semifield and let
d:X^{2}\to K
be an ultrapseudometric. For any
a\in X
, the map
d(a,\cdot):X\to K
is continuous in the topology on
X
induced by
d
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(x)=d(a,x)
.
It suffices to show that
\varphi^{-1}(r)
and
\varphi^{-1}\left([0_{K},r]\right)
are open in
X
for
r\in K^{\times}
.

Say
x\in\varphi^{-1}(r)
. If
y\in B(x,r)
then
d(x,y)<r=d(a,x)
, so
\varphi(y)=d(a,y)=d(a,x)+d(x,y)=d(a,x)=r
.

Now consider
x\in\varphi^{-1}([0_{K},r])
. If
y\in B(x,r)
then
\varphi(y)=d(a,y)\leq d(a,x)+d(x,y)\leq r+r=r
, i.e.,
\varphi(y)\in[0_{K},r]
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

For any
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
, we have
Q_{P}\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
.

Proof.

Since the map
|\cdot|_{P}:\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\to\kappa(P)
has trivial ideal-kernel, it suffices to show that this homomorphism is continuous. But
|\cdot|_{P}=d_{P}(0_{S},\cdot)
, so Lemma
6.2
tells us that this is continuous.
∎

For any
Q\in\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))
, Proposition
3.46
tells us that the restriction
Q|_{S[M]}
of

to
S[M]
is in
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
.
We now work towards showing that
Q_{P}
is the unique element of
\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))
whose restriction to
S[M]
is
P
.
First, we require an auxiliary topological result.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
P
be a prime congruence in
\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
,
and let
Q_{P}
denote the canonical extension of
P
to
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. If we view

From Proposition
6.3

5.6

(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

and

(f_{n},g_{n})

.

The uniqueness of

as an element of
\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))
whose restriction to

is

will now follow from a more general uniqueness result which will also be used in the proof of the main theorem in Section

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
S
be a subsemifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
\varphi:A\to B
be a homomorphism of
S
-algebras with
B
a topological
S
-algebra. Suppose that the closure
\bar{P}
of
(\varphi\times\varphi)(P)
in
B^{2}
is a prime congruence on
B
,
\varphi^{*}(\bar{P})=P
, and that the map
A/P\to B/\bar{P}
induced by
\varphi
is an isomorphism. Then
\bar{P}
is the unique closed prime on
B
whose pullback to
A
is
P
.

Proof.

Note that, since
\varphi^{*}(\bar{P})=P
, we must have
\ker\varphi\subseteq P
. Hence, by quotienting
A
by
\ker\varphi
, we may assume without loss of generality that
A
is an
S
-subalgebra of

. Since

(f_{0},f)\in\bar{P}

Suppose that
Q
is a closed prime congruence on

such that

. Since
Q
is closed and contains

, we have

.

Consider
(f,g)\in Q
. So there exist
f_{0},g_{0}\in A
such that
(f_{0},f)
and
(g_{0},g)
are both in
\bar{P}\subseteq Q
. By symmetry and transitivity of
Q
, we get that
f_{0}\sim_{Q}f\sim_{Q}g\sim_{Q}g_{0}
gives us
(f_{0},g_{0})\in Q\cap A^{2}=P
. So
f\sim_{\bar{P}}f_{0}\sim_{\bar{P}}g_{0}\sim_{\bar{P}}g
, giving
(f,g)\in\bar{P}
. Hence
Q\subseteq\bar{P}
.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

For any

, Proposition
6.5

whose restriction to

.

We start by considering the closures in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)^{2}
of primes on
S[M]
. These are not necessarily congruences, since they do not need to be transitively closed.

Notation 7.1
.

For primes
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
and
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
we will denote by
\bar{P^{\prime}}
the topological closure of
P^{\prime}
in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)^{2}
.

Example 7.2

(Motivating example)
.

Let
P
and
P^{\prime}
be prime congruences on
\mathbb{T}[x^{\pm 1}]
with defining matrices
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
respectively. Consider two sequences of polynomials for
n\geq 0
,

f_{n}=1_{\mathbb{T}}+t^{-n}x^{n}\text{, and }g_{n}=t^{-n}x^{n}.

Both of these sequences converge in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. More precisely,

d_{P}(1_{\mathbb{T}},f_{n})=d_{P}(1_{\mathbb{T}},1_{\mathbb{T}}+t^{-n}x^{n})=|t^{-n}x^{n}|_{P}=t^{-n}.

Thus, as
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d_{P}(1_{\mathbb{T}},f_{n})=0_{\mathbb{T}}
, and so
f_{n}
converges to
1_{\mathbb{T}}
in the
P
norm. Similarly, we check that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d_{P}(0_{\mathbb{T}},g_{n})=0_{\mathbb{T}}
, so the sequence
g_{n}
converges to
0_{\mathbb{T}}
. Since the pairs of polynomials
(f_{n},g_{n})
are elements of
P^{\prime}
, we get that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(f_{n},g_{n})=(1_{\mathbb{T}},0_{\mathbb{T}})
belongs to
\bar{P^{\prime}}
.
Thus, any congruence that contains the closure of
P^{\prime}
contains
(1_{\mathbb{T}},0_{\mathbb{T}})
and is therefore all of
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)^{2}
.
In particular, there is no closed prime on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
which contains
P^{\prime}
.

\diamondsuit

We abstract the idea of this example into the following lemma.

Lemma 7.3
.

Consider a maximal prime
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
and any prime
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
. If there is a monomial
m
such that
|m|_{P}<1_{S}\leq|m|_{P^{\prime}}
, then
there is no closed prime of
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
which contains
{P^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Assume there is such an
m
, and suppose there were a closed prime congruence
Q\supseteq P^{\prime}
on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. We can consider two sequences,
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by

f_{n}=1_{S}+m^{n}\text{ and }g_{n}=m^{n}.

Notice that
(f_{n},g_{n})\in P^{\prime}
. To see what these sequences converge to, compute

d_{P}(1_{S},f_{n})=d_{P}(1_{S},1_{S}+m^{n})=|m^{n}|_{P}=|m|_{P}^{n}.

Since
|m|_{P}<1_{S}
and is in
\mathbb{T}
,
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d_{P}(1_{S},f_{n})=0_{S}
, and so
f_{n}
converges to
1_{S}
in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. Similarly, we check that
d_{P}(0_{S},g_{n})=0_{S}
, so the sequence
g_{n}
converges to
0_{S}
.
Thus,
(f_{n},g_{n})
converges to
(1_{S},0_{S})
, which tells us that
(1_{S},0_{S})\in\bar{P}^{\prime}\subseteq Q
, contradicting the assumption that
Q
is prime.
∎

In the following theorem, we classify which primes on
S[M]
occur as the restriction of some
Q\in\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))
. We use the above lemma to rule out those primes that do not arise this way. First, we need to introduce the following condition on primes.

Definition 7.4
.

For prime congruences
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
and
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
, we say that
P^{\prime}
satisfies
\text{property }(*)
with respect to
P
if there exists a nonzero
c\in S
such that, for any term
m\in S[M]
,
c|m|_{P}\geq|m|_{P^{\prime}}
.

Theorem 7.5

(
\overset{\symking}{}
)
.

Fix,
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
. The map
\iota^{*}:\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))\rightarrow\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
induced by the inclusion
\iota:S[M]\rightarrow\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is a bijection onto the set of those prime congruences in
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
that satisfy
\text{property }(*)
with respect to
P
.

Before we prove the theorem we look at special cases of interest.

Corollary 7.6
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
M=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, so
S[M]=S[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
is the Laurent polynomial semiring over
S
. If
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
is a maximal prime, then the map
\iota:S[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]\rightarrow\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
induces a bijection onto the set of primes
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
.

Proof.

In this case
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]=\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
and every
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
satisfies
\text{property }(*)
with respect to
P
. To see this, fix
c\in S^{\times}
greater than
1_{S}
and note that, for any term
m
,
c|m|_{P}=c\text{\LARGE$\pi$}(|m|_{P^{\prime}})>|m|_{P^{\prime}}
by (
1
).

Now it suffices to show that, if
P^{\prime}\not\subseteq P
, then
P^{\prime}
is not in the image of
\iota^{*}
.
Notice that
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
if and only if for all terms
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, for which
|m_{1}|_{P^{\prime}}\leq|m_{2}|_{P^{\prime}}
, we have that
|m_{1}|_{P}\leq|m_{2}|_{P}
. So, if
P^{\prime}\not\subseteq P
, there are terms
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
violating the above conditions. Consider

and observe that
|m|_{P^{\prime}}>|1_{S}|_{P^{\prime}}
and
|m|_{P}<|1_{S}|_{P}
.
Now apply Lemma
7.3
to this

to conclude that there is no closed prime of
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
containing
{P^{\prime}}
.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
M=\mathbb{N}^{n}
, so
S[M]=S[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial semiring over
S
. Consider
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. By Corollary
3.16
we can pick defining matrices

U=\begin{pmatrix}1&p_{1}&\ldots&p_{n}\\
0&*&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&*&\cdots&*\end{pmatrix}\text{\; and\; }U^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1&p_{1}^{\prime}&\cdots&p_{n}^{\prime}\\
0&*&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&*&\cdots&*\end{pmatrix}

for

and
P^{\prime}
, respectively, where

has entries in
\mathbb{R}
and
U^{\prime}
has entries in

. Then

is in the image of the injective map
\iota^{*}:\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))\rightarrow\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
if and only if

for all

.

Remark 7.8
.

One technicality we need to take into account in the above corollary is that in order to compare the norms of terms taken with respect to different primes we need to use matrices of the same size. Thus, for the purpose of stating the corollaries we will assume that every prime corresponding to a matrix of size
k\times(n+1)
is defined by a square matrix, whose remaining
n+1-k
rows are all 0. Each matrix gives a Hahn embedding as in Example
3.8
and adding the extra rows creates a joint Hahn embedding as in Proposition
3.38
.

.

Let

and

have defining matrices

, as in the statement. So for

\begin{pmatrix}b\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}+_{\mathbb{R}}U\begin{pmatrix}a\\

, so

. For any term

Remark 7.9
.

Recall from Section

that, if the defining matrix of a prime on

has

as an entry then all other entries in that column are also

. Equivalently, we can remove the columns with

. The following proposition will allow us to assume, in the proof of Theorem

is maximal in

Proposition 7.10
.

Let

\check{P}
is maximal and

, and let

. Then

\text{property }(*)

\text{property }(*)

.

Suppose that there exists a

such that
c|m|_{P}\geq|m|_{P^{\prime}}
for all terms

. Fix any
d\in S
greater than
1_{S}
and let
\check{c}=cd
. For any term

we have

\check{c}|m|_{\check{P}}=cd\text{\LARGE$\pi$}(|m|_{P})>c|m|_{P}\geq|m|_{P^{\prime}}.

For the other direction, suppose that there is some

such that
\check{c}|m|_{\check{P}}\geq|m|_{P^{\prime}}
for all terms
m\in S
. Fix any

in

and let

. For any term

c|m|_{P}=d\check{c}|m|_{P}>\check{c}\text{\LARGE$\pi$}(|m|_{P})=\check{c}|m|_{\check{P}}\geq|m|_{P^{\prime}}.

∎

Proposition 7.11
.

If
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
satisfies
\text{property }(*)
with respect to a prime
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
, then
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\subseteq\operatorname{Cnvg}(P^{\prime})
. In this case, the inclusion map
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\to\operatorname{Cnvg}(P^{\prime})
is uniformly continuous.

Proof.

Fix
f\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. For any nonzero element
b\in S[M]/P^{\prime}
we want to show that
\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq b\}
is finite. Since
b\in\kappa(P^{\prime})^{\times}
, there exists a nonzero element
d\in S
such that
|d|_{P^{\prime}}<b
, whence

\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq b\}\subseteq\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq|d|_{P^{\prime}}\}.

Since
P^{\prime}
satisfies
\text{property }(*)
, there is a
c\neq 0_{S}
such that, for any
u\in M
with
|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq|d|_{P^{\prime}}
,

c|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq|d|_{P^{\prime}}=|d|_{P}.

Dividing by
c
we obtain that
|f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq|d/c|_{P}
. This implies that

\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\geq|d|_{P^{\prime}}\}\subseteq\{u\in M\ :\ |f_{u}\chi^{u}|_{P}\geq|d/c|_{P}\},

where the second set is finite, since
f\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
.
Thus, we conclude that
f\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P^{\prime})
.

Given
\varepsilon\in\kappa(P^{\prime})^{\times}
, we want to show that there is a
\delta\in\kappa(P)^{\times}
such that, if

with

, then
d_{P^{\prime}}(f,g)<\varepsilon
. Note that

\displaystyle d_{P^{\prime}}(f,g)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
f_{u}\neq g_{u}\end{subarray}}\left(|f_{u}|_{P^{\prime}}+|g_{u}|_{P^{\prime}}\right)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\

Thus, picking any
\delta\in S^{\times}
which is less than

will work.
∎

The above proposition is used two ways. First, it guarantees that the
P^{\prime}
-leading terms of

form a polynomial as pointed out in Remark

. Second, the continuity statement will be used to deduce that
\bar{P}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
.

Remark 7.12
.

By Proposition
7.11
every

has a leading term with respect to the ordering induced by
P^{\prime}
, and there are finitely many such leading terms.

The following result is a technical tool that we use in the subsequent lemma to give an algebraic characterization of
\bar{P}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 7.13
.

Let
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
be a prime that satisfies
\text{property }(*)
with respect to a prime
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
. Fix
f\in\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
and let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be any sequence of polynomials in
S[M]
converging to
f
in
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\prime}
the set of leading terms of
f
with respect to the preorder induced by
P^{\prime}
, and let
f^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\prime}}m
. Then there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
n\geq n_{0}
, we have that

(f^{\prime},f_{n})\in P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Write
f=f^{\prime}+f^{\prime\prime}
so that the supports of
f^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime\prime}
are disjoint. By Remark
7.12
,
f^{\prime}
is a polynomial. Since
P^{\prime}
satisfies
\text{property }(*)
, we can fix a nonzero
c\in S
such that
c|m|_{P}\geq|m|_{P^{\prime}}
for all terms

. For some

and for all

,

In particular, for all

we can write

with the supports of
f^{\prime}
and
f_{n}^{\prime\prime}
disjoint. Consider a term

of

If

, where

is the coefficient of

in

, then

|\mu|_{P^{\prime}}\leq c|\mu|_{P}\leq c\ d_{P}(f_{n},f)<|f^{\prime}|_{P^{\prime}}.

If

is a term of

, so

and thus

implying that

. Since

, this says that
(f^{\prime},f_{n})\in P^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 7.14
.

Let
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
be a prime that satisfies
\text{property }(*)

Proof.

Suppose
(f,g)
is in

. By Proposition

, we can pick a sequence

in

of pairs of polynomials converging to

For the other direction, let

We continue the assumption that

Example 7.17
.

Suppose

\begin{pmatrix}1&w\\
*&*\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
*&*\end{pmatrix}

Example 7.18
.

In the contexts of Examples

Lemma 7.20

Let

and

First assume that

8.
The dimension of
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)

Throughout this section we continue to assume that
S\neq\mathbb{B}
is a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and
M=\sigma^{\vee}\cap\Lambda
for
\Lambda
a lattice and
\sigma
a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}=\Lambda^{*}\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

In this section we study the dimension of the semiring of convergent power series at a prime. We compute it exactly when the coefficients are in
\mathbb{T}
. When the coefficients are in a strict sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, we provide bounds on the dimension. In that case we give examples showing that both equality and strict inequality are possible.

In
[
41
]
the
dimension
of a semiring
A
, denoted
\dim A
, is defined to be the number of strict inclusions in a chain of prime congruences on
A
of maximal length. We refine this notion in the next definition.

Definition 8.1
.

When
S\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is a sub-semifield and
A
is a
S
-algebra, the
relative dimension of
A
over

S
is the number
\dim_{S}A
of strict inclusions in a longest chain of prime congruences in
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{A}
.

Moreover, if
A
is a topological
S
-algebra, the
topological dimension of
A
is the number
\dim_{\mathrm{top}}A
of strict inclusions in a longest chain of prime congruences in
\operatorname{Cont}{A}
.

If there is no finite upper bound on the length of chains of prime congruences, we say that the dimension (topological or relative) is infinite.

Remark 8.2
.

We will be most interested in the cases when
A
is a polynomial or Laurent polynomial semiring with coefficients in a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, the semirings of convergent power series, and their quotients. In these cases, there is always a longest (finite) chain of prime congruences. For polynomial or Laurent polynomial semiring this existence was proven in
[
41
, Theorem 4.14]
and for semirings of convergent power series we will see this in Corollary
8.6
.

We illustrate the distinction between dimension and relative dimension in the following examples.

Example 8.3
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
. Then, as per Example
3.12
,
\dim_{S}S
is the number of nontrivial proper convex subgroups of
S^{\times}
, which is 0. On the other hand,
\dim S
is equal to the number of (possibly trivial) proper convex subgroups of
S^{\times}
which is 1.

\diamondsuit

Example 8.4
.

Let
A=\mathbb{T}[x]
. Then
\dim A=2
but
\dim_{\mathbb{T}}A=1
. The same happens when
\mathbb{T}
is replaced by any sub-semifield.

\diamondsuit

We now state the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 8.5
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
,
M
a toric monoid, and
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
then

\begin{split}\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})+\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)&\geq\dim_{S}S[M]\\
&\geq\max\{\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}),\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\}.\end{split}

This immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 8.6
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
,
M
be a toric monoid, and
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
. Then

\dim_{S}S[M]\geq\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\geq\dim_{S}S[M]-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}).

Remark 8.7
.

If
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then, given a
k\times(n+1)
matrix
C
for
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
, one can compute
\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})
as follows. Let
V
denote the rational subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
generated by all but the first column of
C
. Let
W
be the rational subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
consisting of vectors of the form
\begin{pmatrix}q\alpha\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}
with
q\in\mathbb{Q}
and
t^{\alpha}\in S
. Then
\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(V+W)/W
.

We also have the following corollary, computing the topological dimension of
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
exactly in the case of
\mathbb{T}
coefficients.

Corollary 8.8
.

If
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{T}}{\mathbb{T}[M]}
, then

\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=\dim_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{T}[M].

Proof.

Let
\check{P}
be the unique maximal element of
\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{T}}{\mathbb{T}[M]}
over
P
. Then
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=\operatorname{Cnvg}(\check{P})
and
\kappa(\check{P})^{\times}/\mathbb{T}^{\times}
is the trivial group. The result follows immediately from Theorem
8.5
.
∎

We will first prove Theorem
8.5
in the case when
M
is a group. Since we have the running hypothesis that
M
is a toric monoid, this means
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. In this case, the statement of Theorem
8.5
reduces to the following proposition.

Proposition 8.9
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be a group, and let
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
be maximal. Then

\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})+\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=\dim_{S}S[M].

First we need the following easy lemma. We let
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
denote the multiplicative monoid of (nonzero) terms of
S[M]
; if
M
is a group, then
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
is a group. Note that, if
A
is a semiring and
\mathcal{G}
is a set of additive generators for
A
, then, for any prime congruence
P
on
A
, every equivalence class in
A/P
has a representative in
\mathcal{G}\cup\{0_{A}\}
.

Lemma 8.10
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be a group, and let
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
. Letting
\pi_{P}:\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])\to\kappa(P)^{\times}
be the natural map, we have

\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))+\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})=\operatorname{rk}(M).

Proof.

Note that
\operatorname{Terms}(S[{M}])
is just
S^{\times}\times M
.
Since every nonzero equivalence class in
S[M]/P
has a representative which is a unit,
S[M]/P
is a semifield, so
S[M]/P=\kappa(P)
.
This results in the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\ker(\pi_{P})\rightarrow\operatorname{Terms}(S[{M}])\rightarrow\kappa(P)^{\times}\rightarrow 0.

Since
\pi_{P}
maps
S^{\times}
injectively into
\kappa(P)^{\times}
we can quotient out by
S^{\times}
to get

(3)

0\rightarrow\ker(\pi_{P})\rightarrow M\rightarrow\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}\rightarrow 0.

We can take the ranks of the abelian groups in sequence (
3
) to obtain

\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))+\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})=\operatorname{rk}(M).

∎

In order to construct the chains of primes necessary to show Proposition
8.9
, we use the following proposition.

Proposition 8.11
.

Let
M
be a group. A relation
\preceq
on the terms of
S[M]
is the same as
\leq_{P}
for a prime
P
on
S[M]
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\preceq
is a total preorder,

(2)

\preceq
is multiplicative, and

(3)

\preceq
respects the order on
S
.

Moreover,
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
if and only if

(4)

for any term
a\chi^{u}
of
S[M]
,
\exists b\in S^{\times}
, such that
b\prec a\chi^{u}
.

Proof.

By definition the relation
\leq_{P}
satisfies all these conditions, so it suffices to show the opposite direction.
Conditions (1), (2) and (3) guarantee that the quotient of
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
by the equivalence relation
a\sim b
if and only if
a\preceq b
and
a\succeq b
is a totally ordered abelian group. Thus, the congruence
P
that
\sim
generates on
S[M]
has
S[M]/P
a totally ordered semifield, giving us that
P
is a prime congruence.
The last condition
is equivalent to the congruence being in

\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
by Lemma
3.15
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
8.9
.

For
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}{S[M]}
let
\pi_{P}
be the natural map from

\operatorname{Terms}(S[{M}])

to the multiplicative group
\kappa(P)^{\times}
of the residue semifield.
From Lemma
8.10
we already know that
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))+\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})=\operatorname{rk}(M)
.
By
[
42
, Theorem 3.16]
,
\dim_{S}S[M]=\operatorname{rk}(M)
.
Thus, it remains only to show that
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))=\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P).

To show that
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))\geq\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
, let
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
and consider the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Terms}(S[{M}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa(P^{\prime})^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\pi_{P})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{P^{\prime}}}

in which the first row is exact.

Since
\kappa(P^{\prime})^{\times}
is a totally ordered abelian group, it is torsion-free, so
\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}})
is saturated in

\operatorname{Terms}(S[{M}])
.
Since
\ker(\pi_{P})
is a subgroup of

\operatorname{Terms}(S[{M}])

containing
\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}})
, we get that
\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}})
is saturated in
\ker(\pi_{P})
. Thus, if
P^{\prime}\subsetneq P
, then
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}}))<\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))
. In particular, if we have a chain of primes

P\supsetneq P^{\prime}\supsetneq P^{\prime\prime}\supsetneq\ldots\supsetneq P^{(n)},

then

\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))>\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}}))>\cdots>\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P^{(n)}}))\geq 0,

so
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))\geq n
.
By Corollary
7.6
, pullback along the inclusion map
S[M]\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
gives a bijection from
\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))
to the primes contained in
P
.
So
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))\geq\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
.

Towards proving the opposite inequality, let
\mathfrak{E}:\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])\to M
be the group homomorphism taking a term to its exponent, i.e.,
\mathfrak{E}(a\chi^{u})=u
. In particular, the kernel of
\mathfrak{E}
is
S^{\times}
. So, because
\ker(\pi_{P})\cap S^{\times}=\{1_{S}\}
,
\operatorname{rk}\big(\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P}))\big)=\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))
.

As usual, let
N=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. For any
w\in N\setminus\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P}))^{\perp}
, we can specify a prime
P^{\prime}\subsetneq P
by the multiplicative preorder
\leq_{P^{\prime}}
on
\text{Terms}\ S[{M}]
given as follows:

a\chi^{u}\leq_{P^{\prime}}b\chi^{v}\Longleftrightarrow a\chi^{u}<_{P}b\chi^{v}\text{ or }(a\chi^{u}\equiv_{P}b\chi^{v}\text{ and }\left<w,u\right>\leq\left<w,v\right>).

We claim that for such a pair
P
and
P^{\prime}
we have that
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}}))=\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))-1.
To see this, note that
a\chi^{u}\in\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}})
if and only if
a\chi^{u}\in\ker(\pi_{P})
and
\left<w,u\right>=0
. Thus,
\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}}))=\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P}))\cap w^{\perp}
.
Since
w
is not 0 on
\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P}))
, we have

\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P^{\prime}}))=\operatorname{rk}\left(\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P}))\cap w^{\perp}\right)=\operatorname{rk}\left(\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P})\right))-1=\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))-1,

as claimed.
Now denote by
k
the rank of
\ker(\pi_{P})
and observe that
k=0
if and only if
N=\mathfrak{E}(\ker(\pi_{P}))^{\perp}
. So we can apply the above construction recursively
k
times to obtain a chain of primes

P\supsetneq P^{\prime}\supsetneq P^{\prime\prime}\supsetneq\ldots\supsetneq P^{(k)}.

Thus,
\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\geq k=\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))
and so, combining the two inequalities, we get the desired statement.
∎

Remark 8.12
.

The above proof also gives a result in the case where
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
is not necessarily maximal. Here we are still assuming that
S
is a subsemifield of
\mathbb{T}
and
M
is a group. In this case, the proof shows that
\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{P}))
is the maximum length of a chain under
P
, where
\pi_{P}:\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])\to\kappa(P)^{\times}
is the natural map.

Definition 8.13
.

The
height
of a prime congruence
P
on a semiring
A
is the maximum length
\operatorname{ht}(P)
of a chain
P_{0}\subsetneq P_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq P_{k}=P
of primes under
P
.

With this terminology, Remark
8.12
says that, when
S
is a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the height of any
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
is
\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{rk}(\ker\pi_{P})=n-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})
. We can also extend this to the case of a toric monoid.

Lemma 8.14
.

Let
S
be sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
M\subseteq\Lambda
be a toric monoid corresponding to a cone
\sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. For any
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
,
\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{rk}(\Lambda)-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})
.

Proof.

Because
P
has trivial ideal-kernel, it extends to a unique prime
\widetilde{P}\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[\Lambda]
and the same applies to all of the primes contained in
P
. Since all the primes below
\widetilde{P}
arise this way, we have
\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{ht}(\widetilde{P})=n-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(\widetilde{P})^{\times}/S^{\times})=n-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})
.
∎

We can also extract another result for the case of toric monoids. First we require a lemma.

Lemma 8.15
.

Let
S
be sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
M\subseteq\Lambda
be a toric monoid corresponding to a cone
\sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Fix
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
and let
\widetilde{P}
be the corresponding prime congruence on
S[\Lambda]
. Then the preorder
\leq_{P}
on
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
is an order if and only if
\leq_{\widetilde{P}}
is an order on
\operatorname{Terms}(S[\Lambda])
.

Proof.

Note that
\leq_{P}
and
\leq_{\widetilde{P}}
are orders if and only if the functions
\pi_{P}:\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])\to\kappa(P)^{\times}
and
\pi_{\widetilde{P}}:\operatorname{Terms}(S[\Lambda])\to\kappa(\widetilde{P})^{\times}
, respectively, are injective.

Under the identification
\kappa(P)\cong\kappa(\widetilde{P})
,
\pi_{P}
is the restriction of
\pi_{\widetilde{P}}
to
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])\subseteq\operatorname{Terms}(S[\Lambda])
, so if
\pi_{\widetilde{P}}
is injective then so is
\pi_{P}
.

Now suppose
\pi_{P}
is injective and
a\chi^{u},a^{\prime}\chi^{u^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{Terms}(S[\Lambda])
are such that
\pi_{\widetilde{P}}(a\chi^{u})=\pi_{\widetilde{P}}(a^{\prime}\chi^{u^{\prime}})
. Since
\sigma^{\vee}
is a full-dimensional rational (not necessarily strongly convex) cone in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
, we can pick
\mu\in\Lambda
which is in the interior of
\sigma^{\vee}
. So for any sufficiently large
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
u+r\mu,u^{\prime}+r\mu\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap\Lambda=M
. Now

\displaystyle\pi_{P}(a\chi^{u+r\mu})

\displaystyle=\pi_{\widetilde{P}}(a\chi^{u})\pi_{\widetilde{P}}(\chi^{r\mu})

\displaystyle=\pi_{\widetilde{P}}(a^{\prime}\chi^{u^{\prime}})\pi_{\widetilde{P}}(\chi^{r\mu})

\displaystyle=\pi_{P}(a^{\prime}\chi^{u^{\prime}+r\mu}),

so
a\chi^{u+r\mu}=a^{\prime}\chi^{u^{\prime}+r\mu}
. Thus
a=a^{\prime}
and
u=u^{\prime}
, showing that
\pi_{\widetilde{P}}
is injective.
∎

Corollary 8.16
.

Let
S
be sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
M\subseteq\Lambda
be a toric monoid corresponding to a cone
\sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. For any
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
, the preorder
\leq_{P}
on
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
is an order if and only if
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
and
\operatorname{ht}(P)=0
.

Proof.

Note that
\leq_{P}
is an order if and only if the map
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])\to\kappa(P)
is injective. Since the ideal-kernel of
S[M]\to\kappa(P)
is generated by its intersection with
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
, whenever
\leq_{P}
is an order we have
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
. Therefore, it suffices to show that, if
P
has trivial ideal-kernel, then
\leq_{P}
is an order if and only if
\operatorname{ht}(P)=0
.

When
P\in\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}_{S}S[M]
, it corresponds to a prime
\widetilde{P}
on
S[\Lambda]
. By Lemma
8.15
,
\leq_{P}
is an order if and only if
\leq_{\widetilde{P}}
is an order. This happens exactly if the group homomorphism
\pi_{\widetilde{P}}:\operatorname{Terms}(S[\Lambda])\to\kappa(P)^{\times}
is injective, i.e.,

0=\operatorname{rk}(\ker(\pi_{\widetilde{P}}))=\operatorname{ht}(\widetilde{P})=\operatorname{ht}(P).

∎

We now continue with the toric case and move towards proving Theorem
8.5
. In studying chains of primes, we will use the stratification given in the following proposition.

Proposition 8.17
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
\Lambda
be a finitely generated free abelian group,
let
\sigma
be a cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
,
and let
M=\sigma^{\vee}\cap\Lambda
. There is a partition of
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
into subsets
V_{\tau}
for
\tau\leq\sigma
such that

(1)

if
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
are in
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
, then they are in the same
V_{\tau}
,

(2)

V_{\tau}\cong\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[\Lambda\cap\tau^{\perp}]
, and

(3)

under the map
\Phi:\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]\to N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
defined in Section
7.1
,
V_{\tau}
is the inverse image of the stratum of
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
corresponding to
\tau
.

Moreover, this partition is given by the equivalence relation on
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
given by
P\sim P^{\prime}
if
P
and
P^{\prime}
have the same ideal-kernel.

Remark 8.18
.

Recall that the stratification of
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
is defined as follows. Given
\tau\leq\sigma
, the stratum of
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{T})
corresponding to
\tau
consists of those
\phi:M\to\mathbb{T}
such that
\phi^{-1}(0_{\mathbb{T}})=\{u\in M\;:\;u\notin\tau^{\perp}\}
.

Proof of Proposition
8.17
.

For
\tau\leq\sigma
we let
K_{\tau}
be the set of those terms
a\chi^{u}
in
S[M]
such that
u\notin\tau^{\perp}
and let
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}=\left\langle m\sim 0_{S}\;:\;m\in K_{\tau}\right\rangle
be the congruence on
S[M]
generated by setting each element of
K_{\tau}
to be
0_{S}
. For the zero cone
\tau=\{0_{N_{\mathbb{R}}}\}
, we have
K_{\tau}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}=\Delta
is the trivial congruence on
S[M]
.

For any prime
P
on
S[M]
there is a face
\tau\leq\sigma
such that the restriction of the ideal-kernel of
P
to the set of terms
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
is
K_{\tau}
. We let
V_{\tau}
denote the set of such
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
.

If
P\in V_{\tau}
then
P\supseteq\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
and, by Remark

, the ideal-kernel of
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
is the same as the ideal-kernel of
P
. Thus, for
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
,
P\in V_{\tau}
if and only if
P\supseteq\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
and the prime
\widetilde{P}
on
S[M]/\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
corresponding to
P
has trivial ideal-kernel. By Proposition
2.17
, this exactly says that
P
corresponds to a prime congruence on
\operatorname{Frac}(S[M]/\mathcal{K}_{\tau})
. So we have

\displaystyle V_{\tau}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Cont}_{S}\operatorname{Frac}(S[M]/\mathcal{K}_{\tau})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Cont}_{S}\operatorname{Frac}(S[\sigma^{\vee}\cap\Lambda\cap\tau^{\perp}])

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[\Lambda\cap\tau^{\perp}].

This proves (2).

If
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
are in
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
then Corolary
3.28
tells us that
P
and
P^{\prime}
have the same ideal-kernel, so they are in the same
V_{\tau}
. So we have shown (1).

Towards (3), recall that
\Phi
is defined as follows. Given
P
, let
\check{P}
be the maximal element of
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
containing
P
, so there is a canonical embedding
\kappa(\check{P})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}
. Then
\Phi(P)
is the map
M\hookrightarrow S[M]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{\check{P}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\kappa(\check{P})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}
. Note that
a\chi^{u}
is in the ideal-kernel of
P
if and only if it is in the ideal-kernel of
\check{P}
, which in turn is equivalent to

being in
\big(\Phi(P)\big)^{-1}(0_{\mathbb{T}})
.
Thus
P\in V_{\tau}
if and only if
\Phi(P)
is in the stratum of
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
corresponding to
\tau
.
∎

Proposition 8.19
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
\Lambda
be a finitely generated free abelian group,
let
\sigma
be a cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
,
and let
M=\sigma^{\vee}\cap\Lambda
. Then

\dim_{S}S[M]=\operatorname{rk}\Lambda.

Proof.

Let
\{V_{\tau}\ :\ \tau\leq\sigma\}
be the partition of
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
from Proposition
8.17
. By part (1) of that proposition,
\dim_{S}S[M]
is the maximum over
\tau
of the largest possible number of strict inclusions in a chain of primes in
V_{\tau}
. Part (2) then implies that this is
\dim_{S}S[M]=\max\{\dim_{S}S[\Lambda\cap\tau^{\perp}]\ :\ \tau\leq\sigma\}
. By
[
42
, Theorem 3.16]
we know that
\dim_{S}S[\Lambda\cap\tau^{\perp}]=\operatorname{rk}(\Lambda\cap\tau^{\perp})=\operatorname{rk}(\Lambda)-\dim\tau
, so we conclude that
\dim_{S}S[M]=\operatorname{rk}(\Lambda)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
8.5
.

Write
M=\sigma^{\vee}\cap\Lambda
for some cone
\sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
P
has trivial ideal-kernel, it has a unique extension
\widetilde{P}
to
S[\Lambda]
. The induced homomorphism
\kappa(P)\to\kappa(\widetilde{P})
is an isomorphism. With this notation, we have the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Terms}\ S[{M}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Terms}\ S[{\Lambda}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(S[M]/P)\setminus\{0_{S}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa(\widetilde{P})^{\times}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\widetilde{P}}}

From Lemma
8.10
, we know that
\operatorname{rk}\Lambda=\operatorname{rk}(\ker\pi_{\widetilde{P}})+\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}),
but
\operatorname{rk}\Lambda=\dim_{S}S[M]
by Proposition
8.19
, implying that
\dim_{S}S[M]\geq\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})
. Since, by Theorem
7.5
, the map
\iota^{*}:\operatorname{Cont}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))\to\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
induced by
\iota:S[M]\to\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
is an injection,
we also have that
\dim_{S}S[M]\geq\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. Combining these statements we get the second half of the theorem, namely

\dim_{S}S[M]\geq\max\{\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P),\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})\}.

It remains to show the inequality
\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})+\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\geq\dim_{S}S[M]
. We have
\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{rk}(\Lambda)-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})
by Lemma
8.14
.
Considering the canonical extensions of primes under
P
to
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
as in Example
6.8
, we get
\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\geq\operatorname{ht}(Q_{P})=\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{rk}(\Lambda)-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})
, where
Q_{P}
is the canonical extension of
P
.
∎

Corollary
8.8
provides us with examples where equality is attained in both inequalities of Theorem

. The following examples show that we can have a strict inequality on one side with equality on the other or strict inequality on both sides.

Example 8.20
.

Let
S=\mathbb{Q}_{\max}
and
A=\mathbb{Q}_{\max}[x]
, corresponding to the cone

.
By Proposition

,
\dim_{S}A=1
.
Take
P
to be the prime congruence on

with defining matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}
.
The residue semifield
\kappa(P)
is
(\mathbb{Q}+\mathbb{Z}\sqrt{2})_{\max}
and so
\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})=1
. We claim that
\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=1
. Since we have
\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\leq\dim_{S}A=1
it suffices to exhibit a chain of primes of length one on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
. By Theorem
7.22
, the primes on

defined by the matrices
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
extend to primes of
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
.

\diamondsuit

Example 8.21
.

Let
S=\mathbb{Q}_{\max}
and let
\sigma=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(-1,-1)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this case, we have

A=\mathbb{Q}_{\max}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}]=\mathbb{Q}_{\max}[xy,(xy^{-1})^{\pm 1}].

By Proposition
8.19
,
\dim_{S}A=2
.
Take
P
to be the prime congruence determined by the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt{2}&\sqrt{3}\end{pmatrix}
. For this

we have that
\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})=2
.
However, one can show that
\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)=1
. To see the last statement, let
P^{\prime}
be the prime on
A
with defining matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}
. Here, we get

by letting the matrix define a prime on

and then pulling back along the inclusion

. We know that

extends to a prime congruence on
\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)
by Theorem

\operatorname{ht}(P^{\prime})=2-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P^{\prime})^{\times}/S^{\times})=2-1=1

on

corresponding to a maximal prime

. If

then

comes from a point of

, and so

. On the other hand, if
Q^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{Cont}^{\circ}(\operatorname{Cnvg}(P))
, then

corresponds to a prime of a toric

, as in the proof of Proposition

. So in this case we also get that
\operatorname{ht}(Q^{\prime})=\operatorname{ht}(P^{\prime})\leq 1
. Thus,
\dim_{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Cnvg}(P)\leq 1
.

\diamondsuit

Example 8.22
.

Let
S=\mathbb{Q}_{\max}
and let

The last statement of this section is a refinement of Theorem

in the case when

Proposition 8.23
.

Let

Appendix A
Totally ordered abelian groups and Hahn’s embedding theorem

A.1.
Totally ordered abelian groups

Recall that a totally ordered abelian group is an abelian group with a total order such that addition preserves the order.
For any totally ordered abelian group
G
, written additively, and for any
a,b\in G
with
a,b>0_{G}
, we write
a\gg b
whenever
a>n\cdot b
for all natural numbers
n
.

We say that
a,b>0_{G}
are
archimedean equivalent
, and write
a\sim b
, if neither
a\gg b
nor
b\gg a
. This gives an equivalence relation, whose equivalence classes are called the
archimedean equivalence classes
of
G
. We order the set of archimedean classes
[a]
of
G
“backwards”, i.e.,
[a]<[b]
if
a\gg b
.

The following definitions can be made in greater generality of nonabelian groups, but we make the definitions only in the context in which we use them.

Definition A.1
.

A
convex subgroup

H
of a totally ordered abelian group
G
is a subgroup of
G
which is also a convex subset with respect to the order.

See
[
65
, Remark and Definition 1.7]
for equivalent definitions of a convex subgroup.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be totally ordered abelian groups. A homomorphism of totally ordered abelian groups from
G
to
G^{\prime}
is a group homomorphism
\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}
which preserves the order. The congruence-kernel of such a homomorphism is a convex subgroup of
G
.

Definition A.2
.

A totally ordered abelian group
G
, is called
archimedean
if, for all
a,b>0_{G}
, there exists an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\cdot a\geq b
. That is,
G
is archimedean if it has only one archimedean equivalence class.

Archimedean totally ordered abelian groups have no nontrivial proper convex subgroups.

The following classical statement is a consequence of Theorem 1 and Proposition 2 in Chapter 4 of
[
25
]
.

Theorem A.3
.

Let
G
be an archimedean totally ordered abelian group. Then there is an injective homomorphism of totally ordered abelian groups from
G
into the additive group of
\mathbb{R}
(with the usual ordering). Moreover, this embedding is unique up to multiplication by a scalar.

The following statement is well-known, but the details of the construction can be found in
[
27
]
.
Given a totally ordered abelian group
(G,+,\leq)
, the set
G\cup\{-\infty\}
equipped with the operations
(\max,+)
forms an totally ordered semifield.
This plays a key role in the following theorem, whose proof is a routine exercise. Let
\mathcal{TOS}
denote the category of totally ordered semifields with semiring homomorphisms, and let
\mathcal{TOAG}
denote the category of totally ordered abelian groups with order-preserving group homomorphisms.

Theorem A.4
.

The map taking a totally ordered semifield
S
to the totally ordered abelian group
S^{\times}
is an equivalence of categories
\mathcal{TOS}\to\mathcal{TOAG}
. An inverse functor of this functor is given by
G\mapsto G\cup\{-\infty\}
.

A.2.
Hahn’s embedding theorem

Let
G
be a totally ordered abelian group, written additively. Recall that, for
x\in G
with
x>0_{G}
,
[x]
is the archimedean class of
x
. Also, for any
y\in G
, we have
|y|:=\max(y,-y)
.

Let
I
be a totally ordered set. The
lexicographic function space on
I
, denoted
\mathbb{R}^{(I)}
, is the subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{I}
consisting of functions
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\operatorname{supp}(f):=\{i\in I:f(i)\neq 0_{\mathbb{R}}\}\subseteq I

is well-ordered. Using the lexicographic order makes
\mathbb{R}^{(I)}
a totally ordered abelian group.
This order is given by specifying that
f<g
exactly when
f(i_{0})<g(i_{0})
, where
i_{0}
is the least element of
\{i\in I:f(i)\neq g(i)\}
.

Theorem A.5

(Hahn’s embedding theorem
+\varepsilon
)
.

be a totally ordered abelian group, and let

.
Furthermore, suppose that we have an archimedean abelian group

and embeddings

,

Let

Appendix B
A universal topology on semirings

Recall that a topological semiring is a semiring that is also a topological space on which the addition and multiplication maps are continuous.
The main content of this appendix is the following theorem.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
\psi:X\to A
be a function with
X
a topological space and
A
a semiring. Then there is a topology
\tau
on
A
such that
(A,\tau)
is a topological semiring and, for any semiring homomorphism
\varphi:A\to B
with
B
a topological semiring,
\varphi
is continuous with respect to
\tau
if and only if the composition
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
is continuous. Moreover,
\psi:X\to(A,\tau)
is continuous.

To prove Theorem
B.1
, we need a few elementary lemmas from point-set topology.

If
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y
is a function from a set
X
to a topological space
Y
, recall that the initial topology on
X
(with respect to
\varphi
) is the coarsest topology on
X
which makes
\varphi
continuous. Explicitly, a subset of
X
is open in this topology if and only if it is of the form
\varphi^{-1}(U)
with
U\subset Y
open.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\varphi:A\to B
be a homomorphism of semirings with
B
a topological semiring. The initial topology on
A
with respect to
\varphi
makes
A
into a topological semiring.

Proof.

We show that the multiplication map
m:A\times A\to A
is continuous; the proof that the addition map is continuous is analogous.

Note that we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{A\times A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{m}
\scriptstyle{\varphi\times\varphi}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{B\times B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{m}
\textstyle{B.}

If
V\subset A
is open, then
V=\varphi^{-1}(U)
for some open set
U\subset B
. Then we have that
m^{-1}(V)=(\varphi\times\varphi)^{-1}(m^{-1}(U))
. Since
m:B\times B\to B
and
\varphi\times\varphi:A\times A\to B\times B
are both continuous,
(\varphi\times\varphi)^{-1}(m^{-1}(U))
is open in
A\times A
.
∎

Given a set
\mathfrak{T}
of topologies on a set
X
, the supremum of
\mathfrak{T}
is the smallest topology
\tau
which contains every

\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}
.
Explicitly, if
\mathfrak{T}
is nonempty, the open sets of
\tau
are arbitrary unions of finite intersections of sets in
\bigcup_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathcal{T}
. That is,
\bigcup_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathcal{T}
is a subbase for
\tau
.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
\varphi:X\to Y
be a function, let
X
be a topological space, and let
\mathfrak{T}
be a set of topologies on
Y
, each of which make
\varphi
continuous. Let
\tau
be the supremum of
\mathfrak{T}
. Then the map
\varphi:X\to(Y,\tau)
is continuous.

Proof.

Every open set in
(Y,\tau)
is a union of finite intersections of sets in
\bigcup_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathcal{T}
. Since unions and intersections are preserved by taking inverse images, and because
\varphi^{-1}(U)
is open in
X
for each
U\in\bigcup_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathcal{T}
, we get that the inverse image of any open set in
(Y,\tau)
is open in
X
.
∎

For any topology
\mathcal{T}
on a set
X
, we let
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}
denote the product topology on
X\times X
.

Lemma B.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{T}
be a collection of topologies on a semiring
A
such that
(A,\mathcal{T})
is a topological semiring for every
\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}
. Let
\tau
be the supremum of
\mathfrak{T}
. Then
(A,\tau)
is a topological semiring.

Proof.

We will show that the multiplication map
m:A\times A\to A
is continuous with respect to
\tau
; the proof that the addition map is continuous is analogous. Since
\bigcup_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathcal{T}
is a subbase for
\tau
, it suffices to check that
m^{-1}(U)
is in
\tau\times\tau
for every
U\in\bigcup_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathcal{T}
. Given such a
U
, fix
\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}
such that
U\in\mathcal{T}
. Then
m^{-1}(U)\in\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\subseteq\tau\times\tau
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{T}
be the set of topologies on
A
which are the initial topology on
A
with respect to some semiring homomorphism
\varphi:A\to B
with
B
a topological semiring and
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
continuous. Note that
\mathfrak{T}
is not empty because it contains the trivial topology
\{\emptyset,A\}
. Let
\tau
be the supremum of
\mathfrak{T}
.

Lemma
B.2
tells us that
(A,\mathcal{T})
is a topological semiring for each
\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}
. Thus, by Lemma
B.4
, we have that
(A,\tau)
is a topological semiring.

Since
\psi:X\to(A,\mathcal{T})
is continuous for all
\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{T}
, Lemma
B.3
gives us that the map
\psi:X\to(A,\tau)
is continuous. In particular, if

be the initial topology on

. Since

∎

Remark B.5
.

The proofs in this appendix do not use the full structure of semirings. The same argument goes through for an arbitrary topological algebraic structure, by which we mean an algebraic structure with a topology on the underlying set which makes the operations continuous. In particular, these results apply to non-idempotent semirings.

Appendix C
Transcendence degree of a totally ordered semifield extension

We propose a definition of transcendence degree for an extension of totally ordered semifields based on the definition of algebraic closure for semifields and we relate it to a quantity that occurs in our study of dimension in
8
.

Definition C.1
.

Let
L/S
be an extension of totally ordered semifields. We say that the elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in L
are
algebraically dependent over
S
if there is a
b\in S
and some integers
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
, not all
0
, such that
a_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}=b
.
If
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in L
are not algebraically dependent over
S
, then we say that they are
algebraically independent over
S
.

Note that, if
\overline{S}

is
the algebraic closure of
S
in
L
, then
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are algebraically independent over
S
if and only if they are algebraically independent over
\overline{S}
.

The extension of
S
generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is
\{ba_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}:b\in S,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
because any polynomial (sum) will equal one of its terms. We will denote it by
S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

Proposition C.2
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in L
be algebraically independent over
S
. Then
a_{n+1}
is algebraic over
S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
if and only if the set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n+1}\}
is algebraically dependent over
S
.

Proof.

If
a_{n+1}
is algebraic over
S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, then
a_{n+1}^{u_{n+1}}=c
, for some nonzero
u_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c\in S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, then
c=ba_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}
, for some
b\in S
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
and so
b=a_{1}^{-u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{-u_{n}}a_{n+1}^{u_{n+1}}
implying that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n+1}
are algebraically dependent over
S
.

For the other direction,
say
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}
are algebraically dependent over
S
, so for some
b\in S

a_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n+1}^{u_{n}+1}=b
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, not all 0. Since
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are algebraically independent over
S
, then
u_{n+1}\neq 0
, and so
a_{n+1}^{u_{n+1}}=ba_{1}^{-u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{-u_{n}}
,
showing
that
a_{n+1}^{u_{n+1}}
is algebraic over
S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
∎

Definition C.3
.

An extension
L/S
of totally ordered semifields

has a finite transcendence degree

if there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in L
such that
L=\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
.

Proposition C.4
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in L
be algebraically independent over
S
. Then
L=\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
if and only if
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is maximal among algebraically independent sets over
S
.

Proof.

By definition,
L\neq\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
if and only if there is some
a_{n+1}\in L
which is not algebraic over
S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. By Proposition
C.2
, this is equivalent to there being some
a_{n+1}\in L
such that
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\}
is algebraically independent over
S
. But this exactly says that
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is not maximal among algebraically independent sets over
S
.
∎

Proposition C.5
.

Let
L=\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
. Then
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is algebraically independent if and only if
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is minimal among sets
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}\subseteq L
, such that
L=\overline{S(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})}
.

Proof.

The set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is not minimal if and only if, for some
i
,
a_{i}\in\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,\hat{a_{i}},\ldots a_{n})}
, where
\{a_{1},\ldots,\hat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{n}\}
denotes the set

with
a_{i}
removed from it. This is equivalent to saying that there is some

and

such that
a_{i}^{u_{i}}=b\prod_{j\neq i}a_{j}^{-u_{j}}
, for some

and

. This happens exactly if

, with

and

not all 0, i.e., if

.
∎

Definition C.6
.

The set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is minimal such that
L=\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
.

Lemma C.7
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in L^{\times}
. Then
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are algebraically independent over
S
if and only if each element of
L^{\times}
can be written as
ca_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}
with
c\in S^{\times}
and
u_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
in at most one way. That is, if
ca_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}=da_{1}^{z_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{z_{n}}
implies
c=d
and
u_{i}=z_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Suppose that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are algebraically dependent, so there are
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c\in S
, not all zero, such that
a_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}=c
. Then
c^{-1}a_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}=1_{S}=1_{S}a_{1}^{0}\cdots a_{n}^{0}
with some
u_{i}\neq 0
.

Now suppose that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are algebraically independent and
ca_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}=da_{1}^{z_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{z_{n}}
with
c,d\in S^{\times}
and
u_{i},z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
a_{1}^{u_{1}-z_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}-z_{n}}=\frac{d}{c}\in S^{\times}
, so
u_{i}-z_{i}=0
for all
i
. Thus
z_{i}=u_{i}
and
\frac{d}{c}=a_{1}^{0}\cdots a_{n}^{0}
, so
d=c
.
∎

Proposition C.8
.

Let
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
be transcendence bases of
L/S
. Then for some
1\leq j\leq m
the set
\{b_{j},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is a transcendence basis.

Proof.

We will first show that, if
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
are transcendence bases of
L/S
, then, for some
1\leq j\leq m
,
b_{j}^{v}=ca_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}
with
c\in S
,
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
v,u_{1}\neq 0
.
Suppose this were not true. Since each
b_{j}\in\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
there is
v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
b_{j}^{v_{j}}\in S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, so we can write
b_{j}^{v_{j}}=c_{j}a_{1}^{u_{j,1}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{j,n}}=c_{j}a_{2}^{u_{j,2}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{j,n}},
where the last equality holds because
u_{j,1}=0
by hypothesis. By raising the previous equality to large enough power, we can assume without loss of generality that all
v_{j}
are equal to some
v
, i.e.,
b_{j}^{v}=c_{j}a_{2}^{u_{j,2}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{j,n}}
.
Since
a_{1}\in\overline{S(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})}
, we have
a_{1}^{w}=db_{1}^{z_{1}}\cdots b_{m}^{z_{m}}
for some
d\in S
and
w,z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
w>0
. Thus,

a_{1}^{wv}=d^{v}(b_{1}^{v})^{z_{1}}\cdots(b_{m}^{v})^{z_{m}}=d^{v}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(c_{j}a_{2}^{u_{j,2}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{j,n}})^{z_{j}},
so
a_{1}\in\overline{S(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})}
, contradicting the fact that the set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is algebraically independent.

Note that
a_{1}\in\overline{S(b_{j},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})}
because
a_{1}^{-u_{1}}=cb_{j}^{-v}a_{2}^{u_{2}}\cdots a_{n}^{u_{n}}
with
u_{1}\neq 0
. Thus

\overline{S(b_{j},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})}\supseteq\overline{S(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})}=L.

Since the set
\{a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is algebraically independent, to show that
\{b_{j},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is algebraically independent, it suffices to see that
b_{j}\not\in\overline{S(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})}
. Assume for contradiction that
b_{j}^{w}=da_{2}^{z_{2}}\cdots a_{n}^{z_{n}}
for some
d\in S^{\times}
and
w,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
w\neq 0
. Then

c^{w}a_{1}^{wu_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{wu_{n}}=b_{j}^{wv}=d^{v}a_{2}^{vz_{2}}\cdots a_{n}^{vz_{n}}

where
wu_{1}\neq 0
, which is impossible by Lemma
C.7
.
∎

Corollary C.9
.

If
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
are transcendence bases of
L/S
, then
m=n
.

Proof.

Proposition
C.8
shows that the transcendence bases of
L/S
contained in

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}

form the bases of a matroid. Thus, any two transcendence bases have the same size.
∎

Definition C.10
.

Let
L/S
be an extension of totally ordered semifields which has a finite transcendence degree. Then the
transcendence degree of
L/S
, denoted
\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/S)
, is the size of any transcendence basis of
L/S
.

Proposition C.11
.

If either
\operatorname{rk}(L^{\times}/S^{\times})
or
\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/S)
is finite, then
\operatorname{rk}(L^{\times}/S^{\times})=\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/S)
.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/S)=n
be finite. Let
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
be a transcendence basis for
L/S
, and let
\left<a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right>
denote the subgroup of
L^{\times}/S^{\times}
generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. Then the group
(L^{\times}/S^{\times})/\left<a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right>\cong L^{\times}/S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})^{\times}
is torsion and
\operatorname{rk}(L^{\times}/S^{\times})\leq n=\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/S).

On the other hand, since
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are algebraically independent over
S
, they are
\mathbb{Z}
-linearly independent in
L^{\times}/S^{\times}
, so
\operatorname{rk}(L^{\times}/S^{\times})\geq n=\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/S)
.
Now assume that
\operatorname{rk}(L^{\times}/S^{\times})
is finite; we need to show that
\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/S)
is also finite.
There are elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in L^{\times}
such that
(L^{\times}/S^{\times})/\left<a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\right>\cong L^{\times}/S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})^{\times}
is torsion.
This shows that
L=\overline{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})}
, showing that
L/S
has a finite transcendence degree.
∎

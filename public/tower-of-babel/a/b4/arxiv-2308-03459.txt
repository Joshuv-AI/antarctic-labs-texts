Non-metrizable manifolds and contractibility
Mathieu Baillif

Abstract
We investigate whether non-metrizable manifolds in various classes can be homotopy equivalent to a CW-complex (in short: heCWc), and in particular contractible. We show that a non-metrizable manifold cannot be heCWc if it has one of the following properties: it contains a countably compact non-compact subspace; it contains a copy of an ω 1 \omega_{1} -compact subset of an ω 1 \omega_{1} -tree; it contains a non-Lindelöf closed subspace functionally narrow in it. (These results hold for more general spaces than just manifolds.) We also show that the positive part of the tangent bundle of 𝕃 + \mathbb{L}_{+} is not heCWc (for any smoothing). These theorems follow from stabilization properties of real valued maps. On a more geometric side, we also show that the Prüfer surface, which has been shown to be contractible long ago, has an open submanifold which is not heCWc. On the other end of the spectrum, we show that there is a non-metrizable contractible Type I surface.

The true and honest reason is that we happen to like to think about these problems.

Milnor showed in

If one releases the metric assumption, this is not true anymore as

Of course, any space with a non-trivial homotopy group is non-contractible.
It was at first not clear to us whether contractible non-metrizable manifolds do exist at all.
We then noticed that examples were found a long time ago by Calabi and Rosenlicht:
the Prüfer surface and some of its variants are contractible.
(The original source is
[

While manifolds can be widly different one from another, they share the local properties of the Euclidean space.
In particular, any manifold is locally compact and thus Tychonoff, locally (path) connected and locally second countable.
In addition, metric manifolds are normal
(even perfectly normal and monotonically normal, see e.g.

second countable and thus Lindelöf and paracompact.
Lindelöfness and paracompactness are actually

[

In metric spaces, Lindelöfness is equivalent to being
\omega_{1}
-compact, and compactness to countable compactness.
(Recall that a space is
\omega_{1}
-compact
or has

iff its closed discrete subsets are at most countable.
A countably compact space is
\omega_{1}
-compact.)
Non-metrizable manifolds, on the contrary, can exhibit some but not all of these features,
and the mere existence of non-metrizable manifolds with such-and-such properties sometimes depends on the axioms of set theory.
For example, perfectly normal manifolds do exist under
CH
and do not under

\neg

(due to M.E. Rudin and P. Zenor, see e.g.

Given this variety, we thought it might be interesting to see
whether contractible non-metrizable manifolds exist in such-and-such class, or on the other hand
if such-and-such topological property

.
Some results are already available, for instance

Theorem 1.1

[

.

A locally metrizable space (in particular, a manifold) containing a copy of
\omega_{1}

On the other side of the spectrum, the contractible
Prüfer surface is neither normal nor
\omega_{1}
-compact.
(A version of it is realcompact, though, as shown by A. Mardani

It contains an open (Lindelöf) Euclidean set with a non-metrizable (equivalently, non-Lindelöf) closure.
In a terminology dating to P. Nyikos, it is in the

In a loose sense, what makes it non-metrizable
lies just at the boundary of some perfectly nice Euclidean open contractible set. We happen to be able to

yielding the contractibility.

The other broad class of non-metrizable manifolds are the

Denote as usual by

Loosely speaking again, these manifolds ‘grow slowly’

of it such that any countably compact subset of

is compact. Notice also
that the proof goes through if one replaces

by any connected sequential space.
Theorem

is an immediate consequence of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Manifolds are C-closed and each point has a Lindelöf (open) neighborhood.
Let thus

be a manifold containing a countably compact non-compact subspace

.
Let

be a CW-complex, and
f:M\to W

be given.
Since

is countably compact and CW-complexes are paracompact and C-closed,

is compact,
hence so is

.
It follows that

cannot be homotopic to

by Theorem

∎

We close this section with a consequence of Theorem
2.1
under
the locally compact trichotomy axiom

due to Eisworth and Nyikos
[

]
.
Recall that a space is
\omega
-bounded
iff any countable set has compact closure.

states that a locally compact space is either a countable union
of
\omega
-bounded spaces, or contains an uncountable closed discrete set,
or has a countable subset with non-Lindelöf closure.
This axiom
holds for instance under

or in models of the form

, see

[

]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
LCT).

Let

be locally compact, C-closed,
\omega_{1}
-compact and non-Lindelöf.
If
X
is heCWc, then
X
does not contain a closed Type I subspace.

Proof.

Recall that we assume our Type I spaces to be non-Lindelöf.
It is well known that in a locally compact space, each point has an open Lindelöf neighborhood.
If
Y\subset X
is closed and of Type I, the closure (in
Y
, hence in
X
) of any Lindelöf subset of

is Lindelöf.
Hence by

is a countable union of countably compact subsets

, which are thus closed in

.
Since one of the

must be non-compact,
Theorem
2.1
shows that

is not heCWc.
∎

3
A Type I space containing a closed non-Lindelöf subspace functionally narrow in it is non-contractible

Let
X
be of Type I, hence
X=\cup_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}}X_{\alpha}
with

X_{\alpha}
open,
\overline{X_{\alpha}}
Lindelöf and
\overline{X_{\alpha}}\subset X_{\beta}
whenever
\alpha<\beta
.
The cover by the
X_{\alpha}

if
X_{\alpha}=\cup_{\beta<\alpha}X_{\beta}
for each limit
\alpha
.
Each chain cover indexed by ordinals
<\omega_{1}
can be made canonical by adding members whenever needed.
(Note: our terminology differs slightly from that of Nyikos in
[
27

who uses the terms

instead
of canonical cover.)
Two canonical covers agree on a club set of
\alpha

A subset of
X
is
bounded
if it is contained in some

and
unbounded
otherwise.
A map between Type I spaces is

if its range is bounded and unbounded otherwise.
A

of (the canonical cover of)

is a map

such that
\sigma(\overline{X_{\alpha+1}}-X_{\alpha})\subset[\alpha,\alpha+1]\subset\mathbb{L}_{\geq 0}

If

is regular, then

is Tychonoff, each
\overline{X_{\alpha}}
is normal and there is a slicer of

(see
[
6
, Lemmas 2.2–2.3]

The following is proved in
[
6
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X=\cup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}X_{\alpha}
be Type I and

be club. Then the following are equivalent.

(a) For each

with

unbounded,
the set

of

such that

is club,

(b) For each

with

unbounded,

is bounded in
X

A closed subspace

of

Whether a Type I space

contains a non-Lindelöf
subset functionally narrow in it can be a tricky question: any closed unbounded copy of
\omega_{1}

is functionally narrow in

, but there are spaces containing discrete
subspaces functionally narrow in them as well, and an Aronszajn tree (see the definition
in Section

) does not
contain any uncountable subspace functionally narrow in it. What we prove is the following.

Theorem 3.2
.

A regular Type I space containing a closed non-Lindelöf subset functionally narrow in it is not heCWc.

For the proof, we need a fact about CW-complexes.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
W
be a CW-complex, and

be an uncountable subset of

(all

are distinct). Then, either there is a cell

such that

is uncountable, or there is an uncountable

and for each

an open

is a discrete family.

Recall that a family

of subsets of

. This implies in particular that for any

\cup\{\overline{F}\,:\,F\in\mathcal{G}\}

We can assume that

lies in an

If

Since any CW-complex is collectionwise normal, it implies in particular
that

can be expanded into a discrete collection of open sets, which gives us

.
∎

Let

4
Tangent bundles of
\mathbb{L}_{+}
are non-contractible

We will use the terminology for Type I spaces defined at the beginning of the previous section.
The following theorem shows that a topologically very interesting
class of surfaces with trivial homotopy groups does not contain contractible ones.
Given a smoothing of the longray
\mathbb{L}_{+}
, write

for the resulting tangent bundle.
P. Nyikos has extensively studied these spaces in
[
28
]

and showed that they have very different topological properties depending on the smoothing.
We refer to this paper for precise definitions. Since we will just use properties shared by
all tangent bundles of
\mathbb{L}_{+}
, we only need to recall
that there is a bundle projection

such that

is a canonical cover for

and

is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for each

is a Type I surface.
(Here and in the remainder of this section, we view
\omega_{1}
as a subset of
\mathbb{L}_{+}
whenever convenient.)

contains a copy of
\mathbb{L}_{+}
(the

disconnects the surface in two homeomorphic subsurfaces

, none of which contain a copy of
\mathbb{L}_{+}
.
Depending on the smoothing, they may contain a copy of
\omega_{1}
.
It is important to note that

is also homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, thus
we slightly abuse the notation by denoting the restriction of

to

also by
\pi
.
We say that a function

with domain

if there is some club

of

such that

is constant on

Theorem 4.1
.

If

is given such that

is Lindelöf for some

,
then

is almost constant for each
t\in[0,1]
.

An immediate corollary is:

Corollary 4.2
.

Neither

nor

are heCWc.

Proof.

The homotopy groups of
T^{+}
are all trivial since any compact subset is contained in
\pi^{-1}((0,\alpha))

(which is contractible)
for some
\alpha
.
If
T^{+}
was heCWc it would then be contractible,
which would yield an homotopy between
\pi:T^{+}\to\mathbb{L}_{+}
and a constant map.
Theorem
4.1
impedes it, proving the result for
T^{+}
.
The result for
TL^{+}
follows from

1.1
since it contains a copy of
\omega_{1}
.
∎

Following
[
28
]
, call a subset of
T^{+}

if it intersects all the fibers above some stationary subset of
\omega_{1}
.
We are going to use the following two results of P. Nyikos.

(
[

)
.

(i) If

is a large open set of

, then
\overline{U}
contains

for a club subset

of

.

(ii) If

is open and contains
\pi^{-1}(C)

for some club

of
\omega_{1}
, then given

of
\omega_{1}
such that

is constant on

.

Actually, Nyikos proves part (ii) only for

, but his proof works as well in our case.

Let

7.2
Isotopies and the mapping class group

Our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 7.4
.

(1) The mapping class group of the Prüfer surface
\mathsf{P}
(which is contractible) has cardinality
\geq\mathfrak{c}
.

(2) There is a contractible Type I surface with mapping class group of cardinality
\geq\mathfrak{c}
.

Recall that two homeomorphisms are
isotopic
if there is a homotopy
h_{t}
between them which is an homeomorphism
for each
t\in[0,1]
.
The
mapping class group

\text{\rm MG}(M)
of a topological space
X
is the group of homeomorphisms of
X
quotiented by
the isotopy classes.
Theorem
7.4
shows a sharp contrast between metrizable and non-metrizable surfaces:
two homeomorphisms of a metrizable surface are homotopic if and only if they are isotopic
(except for some trivial exceptions). To our knowledge this is due to D.B.A Epstein
[
15
]
.

We shall prove (2) in detail, and then explain why (1) holds as well.
We first recall some definitions from
[
27
]
.
Given a non-metrizable Type I manifold
M
with canonical cover
\langle M_{\alpha}\,:\,\alpha\in\omega_{1}\rangle
,
the tree of non-metrizable-component
boundaries
\Upsilon(M)
is defined as
the set of topological boundaries
\partial C
such that
C
is a non-metrizable connected component of
M-M_{\alpha}

for some
\alpha\in\omega_{1}
, with the following order:

\sigma\geq\tau
if
\sigma
is a subset of a component whose boundary is
\tau
.
(Of course,
\Upsilon(M)
depends on the choice of the canonical cover, but this is not inconsequential for our
considerations.)
Recall that an
\omega_{1}
-tree
T
is
normalized
if
T_{\geq x}
is uncountable for each
x\in T
.
Since we only take non-metrizable components,
\Upsilon(M)
is normalized,
and
if one starts with a normalized
T
, then

\Upsilon(M_{T})=T
for the surface
M_{T}
constructed in Section
7.1
.

Given a tree
T
, we say that a map
T\to T
is an
automorphism
of
T
if it is an order preserving bijection.
A map
f:M\to M
of a Type I manifold
M

(with some given canonical cover)
is a
\Upsilon
-automorphism if it is a homeomorphism of
M
such that for all

\sigma\in\Upsilon(M)
,

f(\sigma)=\tau
for some
\tau\in\Upsilon(M)
, and if the induced map
\widehat{f}:\Upsilon(X)\to\Upsilon(X)
is an
automorphism. Being a
\Upsilon
-automorphism is a strong assumption, however it is not difficult to
see that any homeomorphism of a Type I manifold induces an
\Upsilon
-automorphism of
\Upsilon(M)
for a
well choosen canonical cover.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
M=\cup_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}}M_{\alpha}
be a Type I manifold.
If
f,g:M\to M
are
\Upsilon
-automorphisms such that

\widehat{f}\not=\widehat{g}
, then

f
and
g
are non isotopic.

We shall not give the shortest proof of this lemma but rather an argument which can be easily adapted for the Prüfer surface.

Proof.

Let

h_{t}
be a homotopy such that
h_{0}=f
,
h_{1}=g
. Let
\sigma\in\Upsilon(M)
be such that

\widehat{f}(\sigma)\not=\widehat{g}(\sigma)
. We will show that
h_{t}
cannot be an isotopy.
Since

\widehat{f},\widehat{g}
are automorphisms,
\widehat{f}(\tau)\not=\widehat{g}(\tau)
for all
\tau\geq\sigma

in
\Upsilon(M)
, and
both
\widehat{f}(\sigma)
and
\widehat{g}(\sigma)
belong to the same level
\gamma
of
\Upsilon(M)
. By
definition of
\Upsilon(M)
,
there is some
\alpha
such that
\widehat{f}(\tau)
and
\widehat{g}(\tau)
are in
different connected components of

M-M_{\alpha}
for all
\tau>\sigma
. Thus, for
\tau\geq\sigma
and all

y\in f(\tau)\subset M
,
there is an open (connected) interval

I_{y}\subset[0,1]
such that
h_{t}(y)\in M_{\alpha}
for
t\in I_{y}
.
Now, since
\Upsilon(M)
is normalized, for all
\beta\in\omega_{1}
with
\beta>\gamma
,
we may choose
\tau_{\beta}
in the
\beta
-level of
\Upsilon(M)
and

y_{\beta}
in
f(\tau_{\beta})
.
Since
[0,1]
is second countable, there
is an interval
J
contained in
I_{y_{\beta}}

for uncoutably many
\beta
.
Thus, for
t\in J
,
h_{t}
sends an unbounded and therefore non-metrizable
subset of
M
inside
M_{\alpha}
,
which is metrizable. Hence,
h_{t}
cannot be a homeomorphism, and
h_{t}
is not an isotopy.
∎

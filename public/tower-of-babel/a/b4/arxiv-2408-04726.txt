Random search with resetting in heterogeneous environments
Luiz Menon Jr. 1 , and Celia Anteneodo 1,2

Abstract
We investigate random searches under stochastic position resetting at rate r r , in a bounded 1D environment with space-dependent diffusivity D ⁡ ( x ) D(x) . For arbitrary shapes of D ⁡ ( x ) D(x) and prescriptions of the associated multiplicative stochastic process, we obtain analytical expressions for the average time T T for reaching the target (mean first-passage time), given the initial and reset positions, in good agreement with stochastic simulations. For arbitrary D ⁡ ( x ) D(x) , we obtain an exact closed-form expression for T T , within Stratonovich scenario, while for other prescriptions, like Itô and anti-Itô, we derive asymptotic approximations for small and large rates r r . Exact results are also obtained for particular forms of D ⁡ ( x ) D(x) , such as the linear one, with arbitrary prescriptions, allowing to outline and discuss the main effects introduced by diffusive heterogeneity on a random search with resetting. We explore how the effectiveness of resetting varies with different types of heterogeneity.

The introduction of stochastic resetting in random search problems has gained significant attention over the last decade

This strategy, involving the resetting of the state or position of a stochastic dynamics can significantly decrease the average time required to reach a target or boundary. It has been shown to accelerate molecular dynamics calculations

There are regions in parameter space where resetting can increase the mean time to reach the target, or more generally the mean first-passage time (MFPT), indicating that resetting can hinder rather than facilitate the search process.
But, in other cases, resetting can be favorable,
existing an optimal resetting rate that minimizes the average time to reach the target in a bounded domain

Recent advances highlighted the benefits of resetting strategies in bounded domains

18

In these studies, the environment typically has uniform diffusivity, while in many real cases the diffusion coefficient may be space-dependent.

Then, we address the problem of search with resetting in a confined (one-dimensional) environment with a space-dependent diffusion coefficient

\displaystyle x(t+\delta t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-r\delta t}}{{=}}&x(t)+\sqrt{2D(x^{*})}\eta(t)\delta t,\\

In the Itô form, Eq. (

\displaystyle x(t+\delta t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-r\delta t}}{{=}}&x+\frac{2-A}{2}D^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{2D(x)\delta t}\,\eta(t)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\delta t}}{{=}}&x_{r}\,,\end{array}\right.

where

We consider that the target is located at

The Fokker-Planck equation associated to Eq. (

[

]
and stochastic resetting
[

]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ }{\partial t}p(x,t|x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\ }{\partial x}\left\{D(x)^{1-\frac{A}{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}[D(x)^{\frac{A}{2}}p(x,t|x_{0})]\right\}

\displaystyle-rp(x,t|x_{0})+r\delta(x-x_{r}),

(6)

where
x_{0},\;x_{r}\in(0,L]

To study first-passage statistics, we follow the usual approach, which is to consider the backward Fokker-Planck equation, where

is a variable, namely

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Q(x_{0},t)

\displaystyle=D(x_{0})^{\frac{A}{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}\left\{D(x_{0})^{1-\frac{A}{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}Q(x_{0},t)\right\}

(8)

This ordinary differential equation, plus boundary conditions, will be the starting point for our analytical derivations of the MFPT, which is a measure of the efficiency of the search.

The details for solving Eq. (

\displaystyle T(y_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{z_{t}}{z_{r}}\right)^{\frac{A}{2}}U_{A}(z_{t},z_{L})-\left(\frac{z_{0}}{z_{r}}\right)^{\frac{A}{2}}U_{A}(z_{0},z_{L})}{rU_{A}(z_{r},z_{L})},\hskip 14.22636pt

(13)

\displaystyle U_{A}(v,w)\equiv K_{\frac{A}{2}}\left(v\right)I_{\frac{A-2}{2}}(w)+I_{\frac{A}{2}}(v)K_{\frac{A-2}{2}}(w),

III.1
Stratonovich scenario (

Within the Stratonovich framework, it is possible to obtain a closed expression of the MFPT for arbitrary profiles, as follows.
Setting

\sqrt{D(x_{0})}\left[\sqrt{D(x_{0})}T^{\prime}(x_{0})\right]^{\prime}+rT(x_{r})-rT(x_{0})=-1.

For inhomogeneous media,

but it becomes finite for

Final remarks

We studied the performance of stochastic resetting in environments with space-dependent diffusivity.
For arbitrary
D(x)
, we obtained analytical expressions for the MFPT (
T
), which are exact within Stratonovich framework, and approximate for other prescriptions. We also obtained closed-form results for particular forms of
D(x)
such as linear diffusivity, for arbitrary interpretation.
In addition, we discussed several extensions such as the situation of two absorbing boundaries, the limit of semi-infinite domain, and the possibility of
x_{r}\neq x_{0}
.

Depending on the characteristics of the heterogeneity, stochastic resetting may be effective for reset positions further away from the target than in the homogeneous case, and
the average time
T
for reaching the target can be shorter.
The time
T
is typically shorter than in the homogeneous case for diffusivities that decay away from the target (i.e., higher diffusivity close to the target), with stronger effects the more anticipating the process is.
The opposite occurs for diffusivities that increase away from the target.
Furthermore, within the Stratonovich framework, highly oscillatory profiles degrade efficiency, even more so the higher the wavenumber.

Several extensions are open for future work. One of them is the study of probability density functions of arrival times. Another possible continuation is to consider a space-dependent resetting rates,
r(x)
, for example governed by
D(x)
.

MFPT for linear profiles

To solve Eq. (
10

\tau(y_{0})=\frac{1}{r}\left[1+rT(y_{r})\right]-T(y_{0}),

(35)

which, substituted into Eq. (

\tau^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})+\frac{1-A}{y_{0}+\lambda}\tau^{\prime}(y_{0})-r\,\tau(y_{0})=0,

(36)

where
\lambda=2\sqrt{D_{0}-\alpha L/2}/\alpha

This type of differential equation can be converted into a Bessel equation by means of the ansatz

\tau(y_{0})=(y_{0}+\lambda)^{\frac{A}{2}}f(y_{0}),

(37)

which, substituted into Eq. (
36
), leads to

f^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})+\frac{f^{\prime}(y_{0})}{y_{0}+\lambda}-\left[r+\left(\frac{A}{2(y_{0}+\lambda)}\right)^{2}\right]f(y_{0})=0.

(38)

This equation can be identified as a Bessel equation of order
A/2
in the variable

\sqrt{r}(y_{0}+\lambda)
. Its general solution is expressed as a linear combination of the modified Bessel functions of the
first ans second kinds
I_{\frac{A}{2}}

and
K_{\frac{A}{2}}
, respectively, as follows

f(y_{0})=c_{1}K_{\frac{A}{2}}[\sqrt{r}(y_{0}+\lambda)]+c_{2}I_{\frac{A}{2}}[\sqrt{r}(y_{0}+\lambda)],

(39)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are constants determined by the boundary conditions.
We can now express the general solution for
T(y)
as

\displaystyle T(y_{0})=\frac{1}{r}\left[1+rT(y_{r})\right]

\displaystyle-(y_{0}+\lambda)^{\frac{A}{2}}\left[c_{1}K_{\frac{A}{2}}[\sqrt{r}y_{0}+\lambda)]+c_{2}I_{\frac{A}{2}}[\sqrt{r}(y_{0}+\lambda)]\right].

(40)

From the reflective boundary condition
\left.\frac{dT(y_{0})}{dy_{0}}\right|_{y_{L}}=0
, we obtain

c_{1}=c_{2}\frac{I_{\frac{A-2}{2}}[\sqrt{r}(y_{L}+\lambda)]}{K_{\frac{A-2}{2}}[\sqrt{r}(y_{L}+\lambda)]}.

(41)

Now, our solution has the form

\displaystyle T(y_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\left[1+rT(y_{r})\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2}\,(y_{0}+\lambda)^{\frac{A}{2}}U_{A}\left(\sqrt{r}(y_{0}+\lambda),\sqrt{r}(y_{L}+\lambda)\right)}{K_{\frac{A-2}{2}}[\sqrt{r}(y_{L}+\lambda)]},

where,

\displaystyle U_{A}(v,w)=K_{\frac{A}{2}}(v)I_{\frac{A-2}{2}}(w)+I_{\frac{A}{2}}(v)K_{\frac{A-2}{2}}(w),

as defined in Eq. (
14

which can be substituted in Eq. (

Appendix B
Asymptotic approximations

B.1
Large
r
(
r>>1
)

To obtain the solution of Eq. (
8
), for large values of
r
, we start by using the transformation introduced by Eq. (
35
), and defining the perturbative parameter
\varepsilon=1/\sqrt{r}
.
So, Eq. (
8
) becomes

\varepsilon^{2}D(x_{0})\tau^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})+\varepsilon^{2}\left(1-\frac{A}{2}\right)D^{\prime}(x_{0})\tau^{\prime}(x_{0})-\tau(x_{0})=0.

(44)

Now we aim to solve this equation, for small values of
\varepsilon
. For this purpose, we consider the WKB expansion

\tau(x_{0})=\exp\left[{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}S_{n}(x_{0})}\right],

(45)

and apply it into Eq. (
44
), obtaining

\displaystyle D(x)\left[(S_{0}^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon(S_{0}^{\prime\prime}+2S_{0}^{\prime}S_{1}^{\prime})+\dots\right]

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{A}{2}\right)D^{\prime}(x)\left[\varepsilon S_{0}^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{2}S_{1}^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{3}S_{2}^{\prime}+\dots\right]=1.

This yields

\displaystyle S_{0}=\pm\,y(x_{0})=\pm\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\sqrt{D(x^{\prime})}},

(47)

and

\displaystyle S_{1}=\frac{A-1}{4}\ln\left[D(x_{0})\right].

(48)

Then, the WKB approximation at first-order reads

T(x_{0})=\frac{1}{r}+T(x_{r})+D(x_{0})^{\frac{A-1}{4}}\sum_{m=\pm}c_{m}\exp{\left[m\,\sqrt{r}\,y(x_{0})\right].}

(49)

Finally, applying the boundary conditions, we arrive at Eq. (
III.2.1
).

First note that the WKB coefficient
S_{0}
naturally leads to the change of variables defined in Eq. (
11
) and used to transform Eq. (
8
) into an homogeneous diffusion equation with the addition of a force term, i.e., Eq. (
10
).

Furthermore,
when
A=1
(Stratonovich), the coefficient
S_{1}
vanishes, and actually all the upper-order ones, in which case
the exact solution given by Eq. (
18
) is recovered,
meaning that the WKB result is exact for
A=1
.

B.2
Small
r
(
r<<1
)

We seek a solution for Eq. (
8
) in the form of a perturbative series of the form

T(x_{0})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}T_{n}(x_{0}).

(50)

The zeroth-order term
T_{0}(x_{0})
satisfies the following differential equation

D(x_{0})^{\frac{A}{2}}\left[D(x_{0})^{1-A/2}T^{\prime}_{0}(x_{0})\right]^{\prime}+1=0,

(51)

whose explicit solution is given by
[
39
]

\displaystyle T_{0}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}D(x^{\prime\prime})^{-\frac{A}{2}}dx^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{x_{0}}D(x^{\prime})^{-1+\frac{A}{2}}dx^{\prime}

(52)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{0}}D(x^{\prime\prime})^{-1+\frac{A}{2}}\int_{0}^{x^{\prime\prime}}D(x^{\prime})^{-\frac{A}{2}}dx^{\prime}dx^{\prime\prime}.\;\;\;\;

To obtain the high-order terms
T_{n}(x_{0})
, we substitute
Eq. (
50
) into Eq. (
8
),
obtaining the recursion relation

D(x_{0})^{\frac{A}{2}}\left[D(x_{0})^{1-A/2}T^{\prime}_{n}(x_{0})\right]^{\prime}=T_{n-1}(x_{0})-T_{n-1}(x_{r}),

(53)

which allows to obtain the

(54)

where the Green function

\left[D(x_{0})^{1-A/2}G^{\prime}(x_{0}|\xi)\right]^{\prime}=\delta(x_{0}-\xi),

(55)

and the explicit form of the solution is

that verifies the boundary conditions of the problem.

The determination of α \alpha -spectroscopic factors and ANC of 16 O states using 12 C( 20 Ne, 16 O) 16 O reaction at E l ​ a ​ b E_{lab} =150 MeV incident energy
Ashok Kumar Mondal 1, C. Basu 1, S. Adhikari 2, C. Bhattacharya 3, T. K. Rana 3, S. Kundu 3, S. Manna 3, R. Pandey 3, P. Roy 3, A. Sen 3, J. K. Meena 3, A. K. Saha 3, J. K. Sahoo 3, Dipali Basak 1, Tanmay Bar 1, Haridas Pai 1, A. Bisoi 4, A. K. Mitra 1, Piyasi Biswas 1

Abstract
The 12 C( 20 Ne, 16 O) 16 O α \alpha -transfer reaction at E l ​ a ​ b E_{lab} =150 MeV is first time used to determine the ANC of the 6.92 MeV and 7.12 MeV states of 16 O. The 20 Ne+ 12 C potential parameters are also obtained from elastic scattering. The direct reaction code FRESCO is used to determine the α \alpha -spectroscopy factor ( S α S_{\alpha} ) of the three states of 16 O (6.92 MeV, 7.12 MeV and 11.52 MeV) and ANC of the two states (6.92 MeV and 7.12 MeV) of 16 O. The extracted ANC and S α S_{\alpha} are compareable to previous measurements.

I
Introduction

The Asymptotic Normalization Co-efficient (ANC) method [1,2] is an indirect method to study astrophysical reactions at low energies. Such reactions take place in stars at relative energies much below the Coulomb barrier of the two interacting nuclei (except in neutron induced reactions). As a result, the cross-sections are very small and their direct measurement with reasonable accuracy is very difficult or almost impossible with presently available techniques. R-matrix extrapolation [3] of the cross-section measured at higher energy to the Gamow energy is the solution of this problem. Additionally, if the main component of the capture cross-section is external capture then the determination of the ANC can evalute the S-factor at zero energy.
The
12
C(
\alpha
,
\gamma
) reaction is such a reaction for which the ANC method is most suitable [4]. The rate of this reaction greatly affects the resulting ratio of
12

16

10^{-17}

12

)

\alpha

Indirect method uses transfer reactions to determine the ANC of the bound states of the nuclei of interest. In this work, the transfer reaction (
12

20

16

\alpha

\alpha

(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega})_{Expt}=S_{1}S_{\alpha}(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega})_{Theo}

(1)

where

is the spectroscopic factor for the
\alpha

16

20

is the
\alpha

12

16
O. The square of the ANC (C
2
) of a particular state is related to the alpha spectroscopic factor (

) via the single particle ANC b
2
as

C^{2}=S_{\alpha}b^{2}

(2)

The single particle ANC
b

16

Derivatives of Complete Weight Enumerators and New Balance Principle of Binary Self-Dual Codes
Vassil Yorgov

Abstract
Let K = 1 2 ​ [ 1 1 1 − 1 ] . K=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\ 1&-1\end{bmatrix}. It is known that the complete weight enumerator W \ W of a binary self-dual code of length n n is an eigenvector corresponding to an eigenvalue 1 of the Kronecker power K [ n ] . K^{[n]}. For every integer t , 0 ≤ t ≤ n , t,~0\leq t\leq n, we define the t-th derivative W < t > W_{<t>} of W W in such a way that W < t > W_{<t>} is in the eigenspace of 1 \ 1 of the matrix K [ n − t ] . K^{[n-t]}. For large values of t , t, W < t > W_{<t>} contains less information about the code but has smaller length while W < 0 > = W W_{<0>}=W completely determines the code. We compute the derivative of order n − 5 n-5 for the extended Golay code of length 24, the extended quadratic residue code of length 48, and the putative [72,24,12] code and show that they are in the eigenspace of 1 \ 1 of the matrix K [ 5 ] . K^{[5]}. We use the derivatives to prove a new balance equation which involves the number of code vectors of given weight having 1 in a selected coordinate position. As an example, we use the balance equation to eliminate some candidates for weight enumerators of binary self-dual codes of length eight.

Derivatives of the Complete Weight Enumerator

Let
\rho=\sqrt{2}-1
and
\mu=-\sqrt{2}-1.
The quadratic extension
Q(\rho)
has automorphism group of order two generated by the automorphism
\Phi:Q(\rho)\rightarrow Q(\rho)
defined with
\Phi(\rho)=\mu.

Definition 3

For every integer
t,

0\leq t\leq n,
the
t
-th derivative
of
W
is the row vector
W_{<t>}
of length
2^{n-t}
having an entry with
label
v\in F^{n-t}
determined by

W_{<t>}[v]=\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(u)}W[uv]

where
wt(u)
is the Hemming weight of
u
and
uv
is the concatenation of
u
and
v.

For example, if
u=(1,1,0,1)
and
v=(1,0),
then
uv=(1,1,0,1,1,0).
It
follows that every derivative
W_{<t>}
has nonnegative entries from the
quadratic extension
Q(\rho).
Particularly,
W_{<n>}
is determined by the
weight distribution of
C.

Lemma 4

W_{<n>}=\sum_{u\in F^{n}}\rho^{wt(u)}W[u]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k}\rho^{k}

where
A_{k}
is the number of weight
k
vectors in
C.

Thus
W_{<n>}\in Q(\rho)
and contains less information about
C
than the
weight distribution of
C.
On the other end,
W_{<0>}=W
determines the
code
C
completely.

Lemma 5

For every integer
t,

0\leq t\leq n-1
and for every
v\in F^{n-t-1},
we have
W_{<t+1>}[v]=W_{<t>}[(0)v]+\rho W_{<t>}[(1)v].

Proof.
The proof is straightforward:

\displaystyle W_{<t+1>}[v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u^{\prime}\in F^{t+1}}\rho^{wt(u^{\prime})}W[u^{\prime}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(u(0))}W[u(0)v]+\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(u(1))}W[u(1)v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(u)}W[u(0)v]+\rho\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(u)}W[u(1)v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{<t>}[(0)v]+\rho W_{<t>}[(1)v].

Less formally,
W_{<t+1>}=

W_{<t>}[0]+\rho W_{<t>}[1]
where
W_{<t>}[0]

is the first half and
W_{<t>}[1]
is the second half of
W_{<t>}
.

The next lemma shows that every entry of the second half of
W_{<t>}

depends only on a corresponding entry from the first half of
W_{<t>}.

Lemma 6

For every integer
t,

0\leq t\leq n-1
and for every
v\in F^{n-t-1},
we have
W_{<t>}[(1)\overline{v}]=(-1)^{wt(v)}\rho^{t}\Phi(W_{<t>}[(0)v])
where
\overline{v}
is the complementary vector of
v
and

\Phi
is the automorphism of
Q(\rho)
defined above.

Proof.
As the all one vector is in the code
C,
for any
u\in F^{t}
we have
W[\overline{u}(1)\overline{v}]=W[u(0)v]
where
\overline{u}
and
\overline{v}
are the complementary vectors of
u
and
v.
By Definition
3
,

\displaystyle W_{<t>}[(1)\overline{v}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\overline{u}\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(\overline{u})}W[\overline{u}(1)\overline{v}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\overline{u}\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(\overline{u})}W[u(0)v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{t-wt(u)}W[u(0)v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{t}\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{-wt(u)}W[u(0)v]

We check that
\rho^{-1}=-\mu.
Because
C
contains only even weight
vectors,
W[u(0)v]=0
when
wt(u)
and
wt(v)
have different parities.
Therefore

\displaystyle W_{<t>}[(1)\overline{v}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{t}\sum_{u\in F^{t}}(-\mu)^{wt(u)}W[u(0)v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{t}(-1)^{wt(v)}\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\mu^{wt(u)}W[u(0)v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{t}(-1)^{wt(v)}\Phi\left(\sum_{u\in F^{t}}\rho^{wt(u)}W[u(0)v]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{wt(v)}\rho^{t}\Phi\left(W_{<t>}[(0)v]\right)

Theorem 7

For every integer
t,

0\leq t\leq n,
the
t
-th derivative

W_{<t>}
belongs to the eigenspace of
1
of
K^{[n-t]}.

Proof.
The statement of the theorem is true for
W_{<0>}=W
from Lemma
2
.
Thus
WK^{[n]}=W.
Since
W=(W[0]W[1])
(recall that this is the
concatenation of
W[0]
and
W[1])
and

K^{[n]}=K\otimes K^{[n-1]}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}K^{[n-1]}&K^{[n-1]}\\
K^{[n-1]}&-K^{[n-1]}\end{bmatrix}

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(W[0]+W[1])K^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W[0]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(W[0]-W[1])K^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W[1].

By adding and then subtracting these equations, we obtain

\displaystyle W[0]K^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(W[0]+W[1]\right)

\displaystyle W[1]K^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(W[0]-W[1]\right).

Now it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\left(W[0]+\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)W[1]\right)K^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(W[0]+W[1]\right)+\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(W[0]-W[1]\right)

\displaystyle W[0]+\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}W[1]

\displaystyle W[0]+\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)W[1].

Hence,

From this, we proof similarly that

is in the eigenspace of
1
of
K^{[n-2]}.

Using Lemma
5
, we complete the proof by induction on

3
A Balance Principle for Self-Dual Codes

Theorem 8

Let
t
be a selected coordinate position of
C,
let
\delta\in F=\{0,1\},

and let
A_{k,\delta}
be the cardinality of the set

\left\{v\in C~|~wt(v)=k\text{ and }v[t]=\delta\right\}.

Then
\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k,1}\rho^{k-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k,0}\rho^{k+1}
and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k,1}\rho^{k-1}=\frac{1+\rho}{4}W_{<n>}.
Particularly,
\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k,1}\rho^{k-1}
does not depend on the selection of
t.

Proof.
Let
t=n.
From Theorem 6,
W_{<n-1>}=(W_{<n-1>}(0)W_{<n-1>}(1))
is in the
eigenspace of
1
of
K.
which is generated by vector
(1,\rho).

Therefore,
W_{<n-1>}(1)=\rho W_{<n-1>}(0).
Since

\displaystyle W_{<n-1>}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in F^{n-1}}\rho^{wt(u)}W[u(1)]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k,1}\rho^{k-1}

\displaystyle W_{<n-1>}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in F^{n-1}}\rho^{wt(u)}W[u(0)]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k,0}\rho^{k}

we obtain the first equality of the Theorem. From Lemma 5,
W_{<n>}=W_{<n-1>}(0)+\rho W_{<n-1>}(1).
Thus
W_{<n>}=W_{<n-1>}(0)+\rho^{2}W_{<n-1>}(0).
Using Lemma 4, we obtain
\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k}\rho^{k}=(1+\rho^{2})W_{<n-1>}(0)
and
W_{<n-1>}(0)=\frac{1}{1+\rho^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k}\rho^{k}.
Then
W_{<n-1>}(1)=\frac{\rho}{1+\rho^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k}\rho^{k}.
Since
\frac{\rho}{1+\rho^{2}}=\frac{1+\rho}{4},
we obtain the second equality of the Theorem.

If
t\neq n,
we apply a permutation to the coordinates of
C
which sends
t
to
n.
Since equivalent codes have the same weight distribution, the
statement of the Theorem holds for
t\neq n.

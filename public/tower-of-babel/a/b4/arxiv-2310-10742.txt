Mean-field limit of particle systems with absorption
Gaoyue Guo, Milica Tomašević

Abstract
In this work, we consider one-dimensional particles interacting in mean-field type through a bounded kernel. In addition, when particles hit some barrier (say zero), they are removed from the system. This absorption of particles is instantaneously felt by the others, as, contrary to the usual mean-field setting, particles interact only with other non-absorbed particles. This makes the interaction singular as it happens through hitting times of the given barrier. In addition, the diffusion coefficient of each particle is non uniformly elliptic. We show that the particle system admits a weak solution. Through Partial Girsanov transforms we are able to relate our particles with independent stopped Brownian motions, and prove tightness and convergence to a mean-fied limit stochastic differential equation when the number of particles tends to infinity. Further, we study the limit and establish the existence and uniqueness of the classical solution to the corresponding nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation under some continuity assumption on the interacting kernel. This yields the strong well-posedness of the mean-field limit SDE and confirms that our convergence result is indeed a propagation of chaos result. Keywords: interacting particle system, mean-field games, Fokker-Planck equation, propagation of chaos

Introduction

We consider interacting particle systems in
1
-
d
whose components are absorbed as soon as they hit some barrier, saying zero without loss of generality.
Denote by
X^{N,1},\ldots,X^{N,N}
the
N
particles whose dynamics are given as follows: for
i=1,\ldots,N
,

\displaystyle X^{N,i}_{t}=Z^{N,i}+\int_{0}^{t}\iota(X^{N,i}_{s})\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}b\left(s,X^{N,i}_{s},X^{N,j}_{s}\right)\iota(X^{N,j}_{s}){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2}\iota(X^{N,i}_{s}){\rm d}W^{i}_{s},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(1.1)

where
\iota(x):=\mathds{1}_{\{x>0\}}
denotes the indicator function,
b:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable drift and

•

(Z^{N,1},\ldots,Z^{N,N})
is a family of exchangeable real valued random variables;

•

W^{1},\ldots,W^{N}
are independent Brownian motions that are independent of
(Z^{N,1},\ldots,Z^{N,N})
.

Provided that (
1.1
) is well posed, every process
X^{N,i}
is absorbed as soon as it hits zero, i.e.

\displaystyle X^{N,i}_{t}=X^{N,i}_{\tau^{N}_{i}\wedge t},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\tau^{N}_{i}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:~X^{N,i}_{t}\leq 0\}
stands for the first hitting time of
X^{N,i}
at zero.
An equivalent formulation to (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle{\rm d}X^{N,i}_{t}=\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{i}>t\}}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}b\left(t,X^{N,i}_{t},X^{N,j}_{t}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{j}>t\}}{\rm d}t+\sqrt{2}{\rm d}W^{i}_{t}\right],\quad\forall t\geq 0.

We aim to show the propagation of chaos, as
N\to\infty
, of the particle system (
1.1
) towards the following mean-field (non-linear) stochastic differential equation (SDE):

\displaystyle X_{t}=Z+\int_{0}^{t}\iota(X_{s})\left(\int_{(0,\infty)}b(s,X_{t},y)\mu_{t}({\rm d}y)\right){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\iota(X_{s})\sqrt{2}{\rm d}W_{s},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(1.2)

where
\mu_{t}:={\cal L}(X_{t})
stands for the law of
X_{t}
.

It is sometimes mathematically more convenient to study the mean-field limit of an interacting system to avoid, for example, the curse of dimensionality. This is also usually done in mean-field games literature. However, justifying rigorously the propagation of chaos is central to this reasoning. Therefore, the goal of this paper is to show the well-posedness of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), and more importantly to establish the propagation of chaos while
N\to\infty
under suitable hypothesis on the coefficient
b
(essentially continuity and boundedness).

The difficulty when dealing with (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) is that the diffusion coefficient is not uniformly elliptic due to the presence of the hitting time. In addition, we do not have a priori any regularity on the law of the hitting time(s). This in some sens means that the cross-interaction of (
1.1
) is singular, and the strength of this interaction is highly related to the number of active particles. Hence, we are not in the classical framework of
[
29
]
, but we will see that under boundedness and continuity assumption on
b
we can obtain a tightness-consistency result when
N\to\infty.
Enforcing more regularity on
b
, and working on the mean-field limit, we will obtain the strong well-posedness of (
1.2
) that will ensure the propagation of chaos result. We believe that the tightness-consistency result could be adapted to the so called
L^{q}-L^{p}
assumption on
b
with more involved computations as in
[
30
]
. In addition, in such framework strong uniqueness of the mean-field limit without absorbtion holds by
[
26
]
. We leave such possible generalizations of assumptions on
b
to our framework with hitting times for a future work.

.

Let

Strategy of the proof.

For Theorem
2.3
, the proof of the existence for the McKean-Vlasov SDE relies on Schauder’s fixed-point theorem. One delicate point here is that the measure
that solves it accumulates dynamically mass in zero, hence the functional on the space of flows of probability measures for which we would naturally exhibit a fixed point contains an indicator function. This is not continuous for Wasserstein distance for probability measures, so the space on which we exhibit a fixed point is enlarged to take into account this dynamical accumulation of mass in zero. It becomes the product space of flows of probability measures and
\Omega_{T}
.

As for the uniqueness, we study the marginal distributions of any solution
X
to (
1.2
). For
t>0
, we first show that the law of
X_{t}
is of the form
p(t,x)dx+(1-\int p(t,x)dx)\delta_{0}(dx)
, where
p(t,\cdot)
is a sub-probability density satisfying a parametric Fokker-Planck equation whose coefficients depend on
p
itself. We show the one-to-one relation between
X
and such parametric equation, and the uniqueness of
X
is equivalent to that of
p
satisfying this PDE. Due to the non-linearity, we consider PDEs of parametric coefficients and estimate the explicit dependency of their solutions on the
parameters. By introducing a suitably defined operator, we prove that the operator is a contraction and thus the uniqueness result follows.

For Theorem
2.4
, the existence follows through an application of a Girsanov-Cameron-Martin transform. The boundedness of the coefficient
b
easily gives the tightness of the sequence of the empirical measures. The identification of the limit is done through the martingale approach and is more delicate due to the singularity the stopping time introduces in the drift. We will thus regularize
\iota(\cdot)
and show that regularized and non-regularized problems are close. In the latter, it will be crucial to use the fact that individual particles behave like stopped Brownian motions modulo a change of measure. To make such a change uniform in
N
, we adopt the strategy of
[
30
]
and introduce the
Partial Girsanov transforms
(see the discussion after Lemma
3.1
).

We finish this part with a following remark. We believe that the first two parts of Theorem
2.4
could be adapted to the so called
L^{q}-L^{p}
assumption on
b
with more involved computations as in
[
30
]
. We leave possible generalizations of assumptions on
b
for a future work.

3.1
Well-posedness of (
1.1
)

We fix an arbitrary
N\geq 1
and show the well-posedness of (
1.1
) under only Assumption
2.1
-(i). It suffices to
prove that the particle system (
1.1
) has a weak solution on any interval
[0,T]
. Pick some filtered probability space
(E,{\cal E},\mathbb{G}=({\cal G}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{W})
that supports random variables
\overline{Z}^{N,1},\ldots,\overline{Z}^{N,N}
and independent Brownian motions

\overline{W}^{1},\dots,\overline{W}^{N}

such that
{\cal L}(\overline{Z}^{N,1},\ldots,\overline{Z}^{N,N})={\cal L}(Z^{N,1},\ldots,Z^{N,N})
. Define processes
Y^{N,i}
and
\overline{X}^{N,i}
by

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{N,i}:=\overline{Z}^{N,i}+\sqrt{2}\overline{W}_{t}^{i}

and

\displaystyle\overline{X}_{t}^{N,i}:=Y_{\sigma_{i}^{N}\wedge t}^{N,i},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\sigma^{N}_{i}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:~Y^{N,i}_{s}\leq 0\}
. Then a straightforward verification yields

\displaystyle\overline{X}_{t}^{N,i}=\overline{Z}^{N,i}+\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2}\iota(\overline{X}^{N,i}_{s}){\rm d}\overline{W}^{i}_{s},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(3.1)

Set
\overline{X}^{N}_{t}:=(\overline{X}_{t}^{N,1},\ldots,\overline{X}_{t}^{N,N})
and
\overline{W}_{t}:=(\overline{W}_{t}^{1},\ldots,\overline{W}_{t}^{N})
.
For
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, denote

\displaystyle b_{t}^{N,i}(x):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}b\left(t,x_{i},x_{j}\right)\iota(x_{j}).

and
B_{t}^{N}(x):=(b_{t}^{N,1}(x),\ldots,b_{t}^{N,N}(x))
. As
\sup_{t\leq T}\|B_{t}^{N}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{N}\|b\|_{T,\infty}<\infty
, we introduce the probability measure
\mathbb{Q}^{N}\in{\cal P}(\Omega_{T})
via the Radon-Nikodym derivative

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{{\rm d}\mathbb{W}}\bigg|_{T}:=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}B_{u}^{N}(\overline{X}^{N}_{u})\cdot{\rm d}{\overline{W}}_{u}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left|B_{u}^{N}(\overline{X}^{N}_{u})\right|^{2}{\rm d}u\right)=:Z_{T}^{N}.

Applying the Cameron-Martin-Girsanov theorem, see e.g.
[
20
, Thm 4.1 p. 191]
or
[
28
, Thm. 6.4.2 p. 154]
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left(V_{t}:=\overline{W}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}B_{s}^{N}(\overline{X}^{N}_{s}){\rm d}s\right)_{0\leq t\leq T}

is a vector consisting of
N
independent Brownian motions under
\mathbb{Q}^{N}
. In addition, we have in view of
[
20
, Thm 4.1 p. 191]

\displaystyle\overline{X}_{t}^{N,i}=\overline{Z}^{N,i}+\int_{0}^{t}\iota(\overline{X}^{N,i}_{s})\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}b\left(s,\overline{X}^{N,i}_{s},\overline{X}^{N,j}_{s}\right)\iota(\overline{X}^{N,j}_{s}){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2}\iota(\overline{X}^{N,i}_{s}){\rm d}V^{i}_{s},\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

which implies that
\mathbb{Q}:=\mathbb{Q}^{N}\circ(\overline{X}^{N})^{-1}\in{\cal P}(\Omega_{T})
is a weak solution to (

3.2
Tightness of
\{\mu^{N}\}_{\mathbb{N}\geq 1}
and Partial Girsanov transforms

Let us first show the tightness of
\{\mu^{N}\}_{\mathbb{N}\geq 1}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mu^{N}_{0}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Z^{N,i}}
converges in probability to some deterministic measure
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. In addition, let Assumption
2.1
-(i) hold. Then, the sequence of random measures
\{\mu^{N}\}_{\mathbb{N}\geq 1}
is tight in
\mathcal{P}(\Omega_{T})
under
\mathbb{Q}^{N}
.

Proof.

By
[
29
, Prop. 2.2-ii]
, its tightness
results from the tightness of the
intensity
measure
\{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{N}}\mu^{N}(\cdot)\}_{N\geq 1}
.
By symmetry, it suffices to check the
tightness of
\{{\cal L}(X^{N,1}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T)\}_{N\geq 1}
. The boundedness of
b
yields the existence of some constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{N}}[|X_{t}^{N,1}-X_{s}^{N,1}|^{4}]\leq C(t-s)^{4}+C\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{N}}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}\iota(X_{u}^{N,1}){\rm d}W_{u}^{1}\right|^{4}\right]\leq C(t-s)^{4}+C(t-s)^{2},

which fulfills the proof. ∎
We notice here that the fact that
b
is bounded does not only play an important role when showing tightness, but it will also be crucial for the identification of the limit as it enables us to use the Girsanov transform. The fact that particles behave as stopped Brownian motions (up to a Girsanov transform) will be used in what follows. However, passing from the interacting system to the system of independent stopped Brownian motions has a cost that was not uniform in
N
(see the existence section) and as such a full Girsanov transform is not useful when we pass to the limit as
N\to\infty
.
Nevertheless we can use that the individual behaviours are the ones of stopped Brownian motions by introducing

Partial transforms
related to (
1.1
).

For a fixed
r<N
that corresponds to
r
particles that are transformed to independent stopped Brownian motions and whose influence is removed from the other
N-r
particles. The cost of such transforms will be uniform in
N
. Although for the identification of the limit we will only use the case
r=1
, we give them below for an arbitrary
r<N
.

Let us choose, for simplicity, the first
r
particles to be transformed, and consider the following reference system defined on the probability space
(E,{\cal E},\mathbb{G}=({\cal G}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{W}^{(r,N)})
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\hat{X}_{t}^{N,i}=Z^{N,i}+\int_{0}^{t}\iota(\hat{X}^{N,i}_{s}){\rm d}\hat{W}_{s}^{i},\quad i\leq r,t\leq T\\
\displaystyle\hat{X}_{t}^{N,i}=Z^{N,i}+\int_{0}^{t}\iota(\hat{X}^{N,i}_{s}){\rm d}\hat{W}_{s}^{i}\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\iota(\hat{X}^{N,i}_{s})\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=r+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\hat{X}_{s}^{N,i},\hat{X}_{s}^{N,j})\iota(\hat{X}^{N,j}_{s}){\rm d}s,\quad r+1\leq i\leq N,t\leq T.\end{cases}

Obviously, it is well-posed as it can be obtained, as in the previous section, from
\overline{X}
. We now study the change of measure between
\hat{X}
and
X
. The corresponding drift vector is

\beta^{(r)}_{t}(x):=\Big(b_{t}^{N,1}(x),\dots,b_{t}^{N,r}(x),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{r}b(t,x^{r+1},x^{i})\iota(x^{i}),\dots,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{r}b(t,x^{N},x^{i})\iota(x^{i})\Big).

In the sequel we will need uniform w.r.t
N
bounds for moments of

Z_{T}^{(r)}:=\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\beta^{(r)}_{t}(\widehat{X}_{t})\cdot dW_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|\beta^{(r)}_{t}(\widehat{X}_{t})|^{2}dt\right\}.

(3.2)

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
T>0
,
\gamma>0
and
r\geq 1

there exists

C(T,\gamma,r)
s.t.

\forall N\geq 1,\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{W}^{(r,N)}}\exp\left\{\gamma\int_{0}^{T}|\beta^{(r)}_{t}(\widehat{X}_{t})|^{2}dt\right\}\leq C(T,\gamma,r).

Proof.

Observe that

|\beta^{(r)}_{t}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|b_{t}^{N,i}(x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=r+1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b(t,x^{j+1},x^{i})\iota(x^{i})\Big)^{2}\leq r\|b\|^{2}_{\infty}+\frac{(N-r)r^{2}}{N^{2}}\|b\|^{2}_{\infty}

This yields the desired result with
C(T,\gamma,r)=e^{2\gamma Tr\|b\|^{2}_{\infty}}
. ∎

3.3
Identification of the limit

Now we are ready to identify the limit. Having showed that
\{\mu^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is tight, we may extract a convergent subsequence, which is still denoted by
\{\mu^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
, of limit
\mu
. Since
\mu^{N}\to\mu
in law in
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, we have that
\mu^{N}\times\mu^{N}\to\mu\times\mu
.

It remains to prove that
\mu

is a weak solution to (
1.2
).
For any
\varphi\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, the process
(M_{t}^{\varphi})_{t\geq 0}
, defined in the martingale problem, should be a
\mu
martingale. To this end, it suffices to show that for all
t>s>0
, all continuous bounded function
\Phi\in C(\Omega_{s})
, we have
\Psi(\mu)=0
a.s., where
for
\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbb{Q}):=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\Phi((F_{r})_{r\in[0,s]})\Big(\varphi(F_{t})-\varphi(F_{s})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\int_{s}^{t}\iota(F_{u})\Big[\varphi^{\prime\prime}(F_{u})+\varphi^{\prime}(F_{u})\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(u,F_{u},y)\iota(y)\mathbb{Q}_{u}({\rm d}y)\Big]{\rm d}u\Big)\Big].

We observe that for any
\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, it holds that
\Psi(\mathbb{Q})=\Theta(\mathbb{Q}\otimes\mathbb{Q})

Under the assumptions of Theorem

Proof.

The proof of Proposition

Let

\displaystyle\Phi(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-y^{2}/2}{\rm d}y,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

Lemma 4.2
.

{\cal P}^{L}_{T}\times\Omega^{L}_{T}

Proof.

The compactness of

{\cal P}^{L}_{T}

, note that there exists a weakly convergent subsequence of
(\nu_{t}^{n})_{n\geq 1}
as it is uniformly integrable. Using the diagonal arguments, one may extract some subsequence, still denoted by
(\nu^{n})_{n\geq 1}
for the sake of simplicity, such that

converges weakly for each

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{2}\nu_{t}^{n}({\rm d}x)\leq L,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],~n\geq 1,

the above weak convergence is indeed the convergence under

\lim_{m,n\to\infty}{\cal W}(\nu^{m}_{t},\nu^{n}_{t})=0
for all

\displaystyle\lim_{m,n\to\infty}{\cal W}(\nu^{m}_{t},\nu^{n}_{t})\leq\lim_{m,n\to\infty}\big({\cal W}(\nu^{m}_{t_{k}},\nu^{m}_{t})+{\cal W}(\nu^{m}_{t_{k}},\nu^{n}_{t_{k}})+{\cal W}(\nu^{n}_{t_{k}},\nu^{n}_{t})\big)\leq 2L|t_{k}-t|^{1/6},

which shows that

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists some constant

Proof.

Take an arbitrary

\displaystyle{\cal W}({\cal L}(Y_{\tau\wedge t}),{\cal L}(Y_{\tau\wedge(t+\Delta t)}))\leq\mathbb{E}[|Y_{\tau\wedge(t+\Delta t)}-Y_{\tau\wedge t}|]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\tau\wedge t}^{\tau\wedge(t+\Delta t)}B_{s}{\rm d}s+\sqrt{2}{\rm d}W_{s}\right|\right]\leq C\sqrt{\Delta t}.

Hereafter,

Applying Girsanov’s theorem with

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\mathbb{Q}}{{\rm d}\mathbb{P}}\Big|_{t}:=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{s}{\rm d}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{s}^{2}{\rm d}s\right),\quad\mbox{where }\lambda_{s}:=\frac{B_{s}}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle\alpha(t)-\alpha(t+\Delta t)=\mathbb{P}[\tau>t]-\mathbb{P}[\tau>t+\Delta t]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\Big(\frac{{\rm d}\mathbb{P}}{{\rm d}\mathbb{Q}}\Big)_{t+\Delta t}\Big(\iota\Big(\inf_{0\leq r\leq t}(Z+\int_{0}^{r}\sqrt{2}{\rm d}W^{\mathbb{Q}}_{s})\Big)-\iota\Big(\inf_{0\leq r\leq t+\Delta t}(Z+\int_{0}^{r}\sqrt{2}{\rm d}W^{\mathbb{Q}}_{s})\Big)\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

Lemma 4.11
.

There exists
R>0
such that

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|+|J_{2}(t)|\leq R(s-t)^{1/2}

and

\displaystyle|I_{3}(t)|+|J_{3}(t)|\leq R(s-t)^{1/2}\Delta_{s}.

Proof.

As the estimation of
I_{2}
/
J_{2}
can be seen as a particular case of that of
I_{3}
/
J_{3}
, we only deal with
I_{3}
/
J_{3}

.

\displaystyle I_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{t}\big(\tilde{p}\otimes G^{(k)}\big)(t,0,s,y)-\partial_{t}\big(\tilde{q}\otimes H^{(k)}\big)(t,0,s,y)\big){\rm d}y\right|.

Next,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\big(\tilde{p}\otimes G^{(k)}\big)(t,0,s,y){\rm d}y=\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\left(\int_{t}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{p}(t,0,u,z)G^{(k)}(u,z,s,y){\rm d}z{\rm d}u\right){\rm d}y

and similarly

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\big(\tilde{q}\otimes H^{(k)}\big)(t,0,s,y){\rm d}y

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{(k)}(t,0,s,y){\rm d}y+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\tilde{q}(t,0,u,z)H^{(k)}(u,z,s,y){\rm d}z{\rm d}u{\rm d}y.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\big(\tilde{p}\otimes G^{(k)}\big)(t,0,s,y){\rm d}y-\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\big(\tilde{q}\otimes H^{(k)}\big)(t,0,s,y){\rm d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(G^{(k)}(t,0,s,y)-H^{(k)}(t,0,s,y)\big){\rm d}y\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{t}\tilde{p}(t,0,u,z)-\partial_{t}\tilde{q}(t,0,u,z)\big)H^{(k)}(u,0,s,y){\rm d}z{\rm d}u{\rm d}y\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{t}\tilde{p}(t,0,u,z)-\partial_{t}\tilde{q}(t,0,u,z)\big)\big(H^{(k)}(u,z,s,y)-H^{(k)}(u,0,s,y)\big){\rm d}z{\rm d}u{\rm d}y\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\tilde{q}(t,0,u,z)\big(H^{(k)}(u,z,s,y)-G^{(k)}(u,z,s,y)\big){\rm d}z{\rm d}u{\rm d}y\right|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{31}+I_{32}+I_{33}+I_{34}.

For

, one has by Lemma

\displaystyle I_{31}\leq k\Delta_{s}\frac{R^{k}\Gamma(1/2)^{k-1}}{\Gamma((1+k)/2)}(s-t)^{k/2-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(r(s-t),y){\rm d}y\leq k\Delta_{s}\frac{R^{k}\Gamma(1/2)^{k-1}}{\Gamma((1+k)/2)}(s-t)^{k/2-1}.

(4.14)

For
I_{32}
, one has

\displaystyle I_{32}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{t}\tilde{p}(t,0,u,z)-\partial_{t}\tilde{q}(t,0,u,z)\big)H^{(k)}(u,0,s,y){\rm d}z{\rm d}u{\rm d}y\right|

(4.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{s}H^{(k)}(u,0,s,y){\rm d}y{\rm d}u\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{t}\tilde{p}(t,0,u,z)-\partial_{t}\tilde{q}(t,0,u,z)\big){\rm d}z\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{s}\big|H^{(k)}(u,0,s,y)\big|{\rm d}y{\rm d}u\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{t}\tilde{p}(t,0,u,z)-\partial_{t}\tilde{q}(t,0,u,z)\big){\rm d}z\right|\right|

For

\displaystyle|H(t,x,s,y)-H(t,0,s,y)|=\left|\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{x}H(t,z,s,y){\rm d}z\right|

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{x}\left[\left(\frac{\sigma(t,z)\partial_{x}\sigma(t,z)}{\big(1+\beta(t)\big)^{2}}+B(t,x)\right)\partial^{2}_{xx}\tilde{q}(t,z,s,y)+\frac{\sigma(t,z)^{2}-\sigma(t,y)^{2}}{2\big(1+\beta(t)\big)^{2}}\partial^{3}_{xxx}\tilde{q}(t,z,s,y)\right]{\rm d}z\right|

\displaystyle R\int_{0}^{x}\phi(r(s-t),y-z))\left(\frac{1}{s-t}+\frac{|y-z|}{(s-t)^{3/2}}\right){\rm d}z.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|H(t,x,s,y)-H(t,0,s,y)|{\rm d}y\leq R\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{x}\phi(r(s-t),y-z))\left(\frac{1}{s-t}+\frac{|y-z|}{(s-t)^{3/2}}\right){\rm d}z{\rm d}y\leq\frac{R|x|}{s-t}.

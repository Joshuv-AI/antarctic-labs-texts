Abstract
. We prove that for any nondegenerate dendrite D D there exist topologically mixing maps F : D → D F:D\to D and f : [ 0 , 1 ] → [ 0 , 1 ] f:[0,1]\to[0,1] , such that the natural extensions (aka shift homeomorphisms) σ F \sigma_{F} and σ f \sigma_{f} are conjugate, and consequently the corresponding inverse limits are homeomorphic. Moreover, the map f f does not depend on the dendrite D D , and can be selected so that the inverse limit lim ← ​ ( D , F ) \underleftarrow{\lim}(D,F) is homeomorphic to the pseudo-arc. The result extends to any finite number of dendrites. Our work is motivated by, but independent of, the recent result of the first and third author on conjugation of Lozi and Hénon maps to natural extensions of dendrite maps.

Preliminaries

A map is a continuous function. Given a map
f:X\to X
on a compact metric space
X
,
we let

(1)

\underleftarrow{\lim}(X,f)=\{\big(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,\big)\in X^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:x_{i}\in X,x_{i}=f(x_{i+1}),\text{ for any }i\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\}

\underleftarrow{\lim}(X,f)

with bonding map

metric induced from the

product metric
in
X^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
The map

is said to be

if for any two nonempty open sets

there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
. The map

is said to be

if for any two nonempty open sets
U,V\subset X
there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
for all
n>N
. The map
f
is said to be
topologically exact
, or
locally eventually onto
, if for every nonempty open set
U
there exists an
n
such that
f^{n}(U)=X
. It is evident from the definitions that topological exactness implies mixing, which implies transitivity. A map
F:Y\to Y
is said to be
semi-conjugate
to
f
if there exists a surjective map
\varphi:Y\to X
such that
f\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ F
. If in addition
\varphi

is a homeomorphism then
F
is said to be
conjugate
to
f
. A
continuum
is a compact and connected metric space that contains at least two points. A
dendrite
is a locally connected continuum
D
such that for all

x,y\in D
there exists a unique (possibly degenerate)
arc in
D
with endpoints
x
and
y
. We denote this arc by
xy
. The arcs
xy
and
yx
are the same as sets. We assume that
xy
is oriented from
x
to
y
if this is needed. So,
x
and
y
are the first and the last points, respectively, of

. An
end point
of

is a point

such that
D\setminus\{e\}
is connected. The set of all end points of
D
will by denoted by

. A

is a point such that

has at least three components. For an arbitrary
x\in D
and arbitrary positive number
\epsilon
, by

we will denote the open ball in
D
with center at

and radius
\epsilon
. In the present paper, a dendrite with finitely many branch points will be called a

. An

is a dendrite with no branch points.

If

and

are real numbers, by

we understand the closed interval between

and

, regardless whether

or

. Similarly as in the case of dendrites, we use the order of endpoints to indicate the orientation of the interval. We do not use the notation

and get the result that
f
has the small folds property.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

There exists a topologically mixing map
f
of
\left[0,1\right]
onto itself such that the inverse limit space

D
, there exist onto maps

. Moreover, the map

of

f
and
F
are conjugate, and the inverse limit space
\varprojlim\left(D,F\right)
is the pseudo-arc.

Proof.

The theorem follows easily from Lemma

, Theorem

and Proposition

.
∎

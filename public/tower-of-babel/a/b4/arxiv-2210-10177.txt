1.
Introduction

For an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
F
, the Mordell-Weil theorem states that the abelian group
E(F)
of
F
-rational points on
E
is finitely generated. One consequence of this is that its torsion subgroup
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
is finite. In fact, a celebrated result of Merel
[
Mer96
, Corollaire]
showed that the size
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
is uniformly bounded in the degree of
F
; more precisely, for each integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
there exists a bound
B(d)
on torsion subgroup sizes
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
over all elliptic curves
E_{/F}
where
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d
.

Sharp values of
B(d)
are only known for
d\leq 3
, via a complete classification of torsion groups of elliptic curves
[
Maz77
,
KM88
,
Kam92a
,
Kam92b
,
DEvH+21
]
. However, Merel
[
Mer96
]
gave an explicit upper bound on prime power divisors of
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
in terms of
d
, which was later strengthened by Parent
[
Par99
, Corollaire 1.8]
: if
p^{n}\mid\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
then
p^{n}\leq 129(5^{d}-1)(3d)^{6}
. This gives bounds
B(d)
which are larger than exponential in the degree
d
.

It is a folklore conjecture that there exist polynomial bounds on torsion groups of elliptic curves over number fields. More precisely:

Conjecture 1
.

[
CCS13
]

There exist constants
C,\alpha>0
such that for all elliptic curves
E_{/F}
one has
\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq C\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{\alpha}
.

Parent’s bounds above
[
Par99
]
are more than an exponential factor away from this conjecture. However, several results in the literature support this conjecture once we restrict certain parameters of our elliptic curves. For example, for any elliptic curve
E_{/F}
with integral
j
-invariant, Hindry and Silverman have shown that
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq 1977408\cdot d\log(d)
when
d:=[F:\mathbb{Q}]>1

[
HS99
, Théorème 1]
. In a stricter case, if
E
has complex multiplication (CM) then Clark and Pollack have shown that
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq C\cdot d\log\log d
when
d>2
, where
C\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is some absolute, effectively computable constant
[
CP15
, Theorem 1]
.

There are also polynomial bounds for elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant:
Clark and Pollack have shown that for each
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any elliptic curve
E_{/F}
whose
j
-invariant
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
, one has that the exponent
1
1

1

Given a finite group
(G,+)
, its
exponent

\exp G
is the least integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
nG=0
. When
G
is abelian, its exponent is equal to the largest possible order of an element in
G
.

\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq C_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{3/2+\epsilon}
, and thus
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq C_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{5/2+\epsilon}

[
CP18
, Theorem 1.3]
.
An identical result holds when one assumes the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) and replaces
\mathbb{Q}
with a number field
F_{0}
which contains no Hilbert class field of any imaginary quadratic field

[
CP18
, Theorem 1.6]
.

The principal result of this paper constructs polynomial bounds on orders of torsion points (and thus torsion groups) of non-CM elliptic curves
E_{/F}
within a fixed geometric isogeny class. Recall that an
isogeny
between elliptic curves
E
and
E^{\prime}
is a nonconstant algebraic map
\phi\colon E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
which preserves basepoints. We say that
\phi
is
F
-rational if
E
,
E^{\prime}
and
\phi
are defined over
F
. As an adjective, “geometric” will mean
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-rational, where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is a fixed algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem 1
.

Fix a number field
F_{0}
and a non-CM elliptic curve
{E_{0}}_{/F_{0}}
. Then for each
\epsilon>0
there exist constants
c_{\epsilon}:=c_{\epsilon}(E_{0},F_{0}),C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon}(E_{0},F_{0})>0
such that for any elliptic curve
E_{/F}
geometrically isogenous to
{E_{0}}_{/F_{0}}
, one has both

\exp E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}

and

\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq C_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1+\epsilon}.

Remark 1
.

In Theorem
1
, The power “
1/2+\epsilon
” in the exponent bound
c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}
is optimal “up to
\epsilon
”, since for any elliptic curve
E_{/F}
, any integer
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and any torsion point
R\in E[N]
, one always has
[F(R):F]\leq N^{2}
.

Remark 2
.

We must assume in Theorem
1
that our elliptic curves are non-CM to have the
torsion group exponent
bound
c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}
hold. Indeed, one can show using e.g.
[
BC20
, Corollary 1.8]
that for any imaginary quadratic field
K
, the geometric isogeny class of elliptic curves with CM field
K
contains infinitely many elliptic curves
E_{/F}
with
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
arbitrarily large and
\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]>[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. Despite this, as noted earlier there is an asymptotically sharp bound on the size of
full torsion groups
of CM elliptic curves: one always has
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq C\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]\log\log[F:\mathbb{Q}]
when
[F:\mathbb{Q}]>2
for some absolute, effectively computable constant
C\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

[
CP15
, Theorem 1]
.

With Remark
2
in mind, we will assume for the rest of this paper that our elliptic curves have no geometric CM.
A key step for us in polynomially bounding torsion from a non-CM geometric isogeny class
\mathcal{E}
will be to relate the adelic indices of two rationally isogenous non-CM elliptic curves (this is Corollary
4
).

In contrast to
[
CP18
, Theorem 1.3]
, the collection of elliptic curves in Theorem
1
will contain curves whose
j
-invariants
j^{\prime}
have arbitrarily large degrees
[\mathbb{Q}(j^{\prime}):\mathbb{Q}]
. However, both Theorem
1
and
[
CP18
, Theorem 1.3]
are part of a natural uniformity conjecture on torsion groups that is motivated by our current understanding of Galois representations of rational elliptic curves.

Conjecture 2
.

There exist constants
C,\alpha>0
such that for all elliptic curves
E_{/F}
geometrically isogenous to some elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, one has
\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq C\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{\alpha}
.

This is a special case of Conjecture
1
. There is recent work which suggests its tractability: a result of Bourdon and Najman
[
BN
, Proposition 4.1]
can be used to show that when
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
is odd and
E_{/F}
is
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isogenous to a rational elliptic curve, one has
\exp E(F)\leq 720720\sqrt{35}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2}
, and thus
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq 1441440\sqrt{35}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2}
. On the other hand, if one assumes a uniformity conjecture of Zywina on indices of adelic Galois representations of non-CM elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}

[
Zyw
, Conjecture 1.3]
, then Conjecture
2
follows “up to
\epsilon
” with the same bounds as in Theorem
1
; the principal difference is that the constants in Theorem
1
will change and depend only on
\epsilon
.

2.
Results on Galois Representations of Non-CM Elliptic Curves

2.1.
Some profinite group theory

In this section, we will show that a result of Greenberg on
\ell
-adic Galois representations
[
Gre12
, Proposition 2.1.1]
has a proof which applies to
n
-adic representations for composite
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, after some modifications. We will then use this composite version to prove that rationally isogenous non-CM elliptic curves have adelic Galois representations with equal indices in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
, a fact we will use in our proof of Theorem
1
; this is recorded as Corollary
4
.

Before we prove this adelic index result, we will prove a few general facts about subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
. For each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we will denote by
\pi_{n}\colon\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\twoheadrightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
the mod-
n
reduction map, and by
\pi_{n^{\infty}}\colon\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\twoheadrightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
the
n
-adic reduction map.

By profinite group theory, for any subgroup
G\subseteq\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
one has that
G
is open in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
iff
G
has finite index in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
, iff
G
contains contains an open neighborhood
U(M):=\ker\pi_{M}
for some
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. When
G
is open, we will call the least such
M
for which
U(M)\subseteq G
the
level of
G
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
\emph{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
.

(1)

One has for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
that
U(n)\subseteq G
iff
G=\pi_{n}^{-1}(\pi_{n}(G))
.

(2)

If
U(n)\subseteq G
then

[\emph{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G]=[\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}):\pi_{n^{\infty}}(G)]=[\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}):\pi_{n}(G)].

(3)

Suppose that
G
is open, and let
G^{\prime}\subseteq\emph{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
be another open subgroup. If for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
one has

[\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}):\pi_{n^{\infty}}(G)]=[\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}):\pi_{n^{\infty}}(G^{\prime})]

then one has the equality

[\emph{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G]=[\emph{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G^{\prime}].

Proof.

For part a., suppose first that
U(n)\subseteq G
. To check that
G=\pi_{n}^{-1}(\pi_{n}(G))
, we note that the containment
\subseteq
is clear. For the reverse containment, observe that if
x\in\pi_{n}^{-1}(\pi_{n}(G))
then
\pi_{n}(x)\in\pi_{n}(G)
, and so
\pi_{n}(x)=\pi_{n}(g)
for some
g\in G
; in particular,
xg^{-1}\in\ker\pi_{n}\subseteq G
, whence we have
x\in G
.
For the converse, assume that
\pi_{n}^{-1}(\pi_{n}(G))=G
. Then we have
U(n)=\pi_{n}^{-1}(\{I\})\subseteq G
, where
I\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
is the identity matrix.

Part b. follows from the general fact that if
f\colon G_{0}\rightarrow K
is a group homomorphism and
G\subseteq G_{0}
is a subgroup containing the kernel
\ker f
, then a set of coset representatives
\{f(g_{i})\}_{i}
for
f(G)
in
f(G_{0})
lifts to a set of coset representatives
\{g_{i}\}_{i}
for
G
in
G_{0}
.
In particular, when
G\subseteq\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
is a subgroup with
U(n)\subseteq G
, one has that
[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G]=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}):\pi_{n}(G)]
. It also follows that
[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G]=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}):\pi_{n^{\infty}}(G)]
, via the containment
\ker\pi_{n^{\infty}}\subseteq G
(the map
\pi_{n}
factors through
\pi_{n^{\infty}}
).

For part c., let us set
N:=\textrm{lcm}(M,M^{\prime})
where
M
and
M^{\prime}
are the levels of
G
and
G^{\prime}
respectively. Since
U(N)\subseteq U(M)\subseteq G
and
U(N)\subseteq U(M^{\prime})\subseteq G^{\prime}
, by part b. we have both

[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G]=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}):\pi_{N^{\infty}}(G)]

and

[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G^{\prime}]=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}):\pi_{N^{\infty}}(G^{\prime})].

Thus, our hypothesis implies that
[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G]=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):G^{\prime}]
.
∎

2.2.
A composite version of a result of Greenberg

Our next goal is to prove a composite version of
[
Gre12
, Proposition 2.1.1]
. Given an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, let us recall that the
ring of
n
-adic integers
is

\mathbb{Z}_{n}:=\varprojlim_{\ell\mid n,k\geq 1}\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{k}\mathbb{Z}\cong\prod_{\ell\mid n}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}.

Following this, the
ring of
n
-adic numbers
is

\mathbb{Q}_{n}:=\prod_{\ell\mid n}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}.

For a free
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
-module
V
of finite rank, we call the
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-span of any basis of
V
a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattice.

Proposition 3
.

Fix a positive integer
n
. Let
V
be a free finite rank
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
-module. Suppose that
G
is a compact open subgroup of
\emph{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V)
. If
T
and
T^{\prime}
are two
G
-invariant
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattices in
V
, then

[\emph{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T):G]=[\emph{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T^{\prime}):G].

Proof.

Suppose that
V
is free of rank
d
over
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
. Fixing a basis for
V
, one has an isomorphism
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V)\cong\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})\cong\prod_{\ell\mid n}\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
.

For each prime
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the group
\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is a locally compact topological group, and thus has a left Haar measure. In fact, since
\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is a reductive
\ell
-adic group it is also
unimodular
: every left Haar measure is also a right Haar measure
[
Glö96
, Theorem 5.1]
. It follows then that the finite product
\prod_{\ell\mid n}\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})
is also unimodular for composite
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Fix a Haar measure
\mu
on
\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})
; since
G
is compact open in
\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})
, we have
\mu(G)>0
, so we may assume that
\mu(G)=1
.
Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattice
T
in
V
, we can identify
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T)\cong\textrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
once we choose a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-basis for
T
. For any
\sigma\in\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V)
one has that
\sigma(T)
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattice; this gives us an action of
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V)
on the set of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattices in
V
. This action is clearly transitive, and the stabilizer of any
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattice
T
is
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T)
. Additionally,
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T)
is a compact open subgroup of
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V)
, and
G
is contained in
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T)
and has finite index. Since
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T)
is a finite disjoint union of left cosets of
G
, and since
\mu(G)=1
and
\mu
is left invariant, it follows that

(1)

\mu(\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T))=[\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T):G].

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be
G
-invariant
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattices of
V
.
Since
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V)
acts transitively on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattices, there exists
\sigma\in\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V)
with
\sigma(T)=T^{\prime}
. It follows then that
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T^{\prime})=\sigma\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T)\sigma^{-1}
. As
\mu
is both left and right invariant, we conclude that
\mu(\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T^{\prime}))=\mu(\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T))
, which by (
1
) implies our result.
∎

2.3.
Galois representations of elliptic curves

Given an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
F
, for each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
the absolute Galois group
G_{F}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
acts on the
n
-torsion subgroup
E[n]
of
E
. This action is described by the
mod-
n
Galois representation of
E
,
denoted by

\rho_{E,n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}(E[n]).

Since
E[n]
is a free
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank two, choosing a basis
\{P,Q\}
for
E[n]
gives an isomorphism
\textrm{Aut}(E[n])\cong\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
; we will often work with a basis implicitly, suppressing dependence on one.

The action of
G_{F}
on each torsion subgroup
E[n]
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
induces an action on their inverse limit
T(E):=\varprojlim E[n]
, called the
adelic Tate module of
E_{/F}
. Since each
E[n]
is a free rank two
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-module, it follows that
T(E)
is free of rank two over the profinite integers
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}:=\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. The action of
G_{F}
on
T(E)
is called the
adelic Galois representation of
E_{/F}
,
denoted by

\rho_{E}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}_{\hat{\mathbb{Z}}}(T(E)).

This also describes the action of
G_{F}
on the full torsion subgroup
E[\textrm{tors}]
.
Choosing a basis for
T(E)
gives an isomorphism
\textrm{Aut}_{\hat{\mathbb{Z}}}(T(E))\cong\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Assume hereafter that our elliptic curves are non-CM; then it follows by
[
Ser72
, Théorème 2]
that the image
\rho_{E}(G_{F})
is open in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
. We say that the
adelic level of
E_{/F}
is the level of
\rho_{E}(G_{F})
as a subgroup of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
.
By abuse of notation, we will often suppress its dependence on
E
and
F
.

Given an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, let us define the
n
-adic Tate module of
E_{/F}
as
T_{n}(E):=\varprojlim_{k\geq 1}E[n^{k}]
. We have that
T_{n}(E)
is a free rank two
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-module.
The
n
-adic representation of
E_{/F}
is the action of
G_{F}
on
T_{n}(E)
, denoted by

\rho_{E,n^{\infty}}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T_{n}(E)).

This also describes the action of
G_{F}
on the
n
-primary torsion subgroup
E[n^{\infty}]:=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}E[n^{k}]=\sum_{\ell\mid n}E[\ell^{\infty}]
.
Since
\rho_{E,n^{\infty}}(G_{F})
is a projection of
\rho_{E}(G_{F})
, it is open in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
.

The action of
G_{F}
on
T_{n}(E)
extends naturally to an action on the rational
n
-adic Tate module
V_{n}(E):=T_{n}(E)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}
. We can realize
\rho_{E,n^{\infty}}(G_{F})
as finite-index subgroup of
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(T_{n}(E))
, the latter of which is a compact open subgroup of
\textrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(V_{n}(E))
.

Suppose two elliptic curves
E_{/F}
and
E^{\prime}_{/F}
are
F
-isogenous; let us write this isogeny as
\phi\colon E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
. Choose an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
; then this isogeny induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}[G_{F}]
-module homomorphism
\phi\colon T_{n}(E)\rightarrow T_{n}(E^{\prime})
. In fact, we have a short exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}[G_{F}]
-modules,

0\rightarrow T_{n}(E)\xrightarrow{\phi}T_{n}(E^{\prime})\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0,

for some finite module
C
.
Tensoring this sequence to
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
shows that the rational Tate modules
V_{n}(E)
and
V_{n}(E^{\prime})
are isomorphic
G_{F}
-modules, and so
T_{n}(E)
and
T_{n}(E^{\prime})
may be realized as
G_{F}
-stable
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-lattices in
V_{n}(E)
. By Proposition
3
, this implies that

[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}):\rho_{E,n^{\infty}}(G_{F})]=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}):\rho_{E^{\prime},n^{\infty}}(G_{F})].

Since
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
was arbitrary, we have proven the following key result after applying Lemma
2
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
E_{/F}
and
E^{\prime}_{/F}
be
F
-isogenous non-CM elliptic curves. Then one has

[\emph{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):\rho_{E}(G_{F})]=[\emph{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):\rho_{E^{\prime}}(G_{F})].

Let us note one more fact about Galois representations of elliptic curves with a rational torsion point.
For each integer
n\geq 2
, we define a distinguished subgroup of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
,

B_{1}(n):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\right\}.

When an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
has an
F
-rational order
n
torsion point, it follows that the image
\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})
is contained in
B_{1}(n)
up to conjugacy. This implies the index divisibility

[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}):B_{1}(n)]\mid[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}):\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})].

The former index can be written more explicitly. Let us recall Euler’s phi function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and the Dedekind psi function
\psi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, both arithmetic multiplicative functions defined on prime powers via
\varphi(\ell^{k})=\ell^{k-1}(\ell-1)
and
\psi(\ell^{k})=\ell^{k-1}(\ell+1)
respectively.

Lemma 5
.

For
n\geq 2
one has

[\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}):B_{1}(n)]=\varphi(n)\psi(n).

Proof.

See e.g.
[
CGPS22
, §7.2]
.
∎

3.
Polynomial Bounds on Torsion

We are ready to prove the main result of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

To recapitulate Theorem
1
, we will show that for any fixed non-CM elliptic curve
{E_{0}}_{/F_{0}}
, for all
\epsilon>0
there exist constants
c_{\epsilon}:=c_{\epsilon}(E_{0},F_{0}),C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon}(E_{0},F_{0})>0
such that for all elliptic curves
E_{/F}
geometrically isogenous to
E_{0}
, one has both

\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}

and

\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq C_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1+\epsilon}.

First, observe that the desired bound on
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
will follow from the desired bound on the exponent
\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
, via the divisibility

\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\mid(\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}])^{2}

(one can take
C_{\epsilon}:=c_{\epsilon/2}^{2}
).
To this end, our proof will focus on bounding
\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
.

Let us write
n:=\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
. Then up to conjugacy we have
\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})\subseteq B_{1}(n)
, so by Lemma
5
we get

(2)

\varphi(n)\psi(n)\mid[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}):\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})].

By
[
LFN20
, Lemma 3.1]
there exists a(n at worst) quadratic extension
L/FF_{0}
for which
E
and
E_{0}
are
L
-isogenous.
Thus, Corollary
4
implies that

(3)

[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):\rho_{E}(G_{L})]=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):\rho_{E_{0}}(G_{L})].

Since the extension
F_{0}(E_{0}[\textrm{tors}])/F_{0}
is normal, so is the extension
L(E_{0}[\textrm{tors}])/L
, and we have

\textrm{Gal}(L(E_{0}[\textrm{tors}])/L)\cong\textrm{Gal}(F_{0}(E_{0}[\textrm{tors}])/L\cap F_{0}(E_{0}[\textrm{tors}]))

(this general fact is e.g.
[
Mil
, Proposition 7.15]
). Since
\rho_{E_{0}}(G_{L})\cong\textrm{Gal}(L(E_{0}[\textrm{tors}])/L)
, we see that
\rho_{E_{0}}(G_{L})
is a subgroup of
\rho_{E_{0}}(G_{F_{0}})
of index
[L\cap F_{0}(E_{0}[\textrm{tors}]):F_{0}]
. Thus, we deduce that

(4)

[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):\rho_{E_{0}}(G_{L})]\mid[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):\rho_{E_{0}}(G_{F_{0}})]\cdot[L:F_{0}].

Finally, since
[FF_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]=[FF_{0}:F]\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]\mid[F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]
and
[L:F_{0}]\mid 2[FF_{0}:F_{0}]
, we find that

[L:F_{0}]\mid 2([F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]-1)!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}].

Combining this fact with (
2
), (
3
) and (
4
), we conclude that

(5)

\varphi(n)\psi(n)\mid 2I([F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]-1)!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}],

where
I:=[\textrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}):\rho_{E_{0}}(G_{F_{0}})]
is the adelic index of our fixed elliptic curve
{E_{0}}_{/F_{0}}
.

One can check directly that
\psi(n)>n
for any
n>1
.
Fixing an
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, by
[
HW08
, Theorem 327]
there exists a constant
b_{\epsilon}>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
one has

\varphi(n)>b_{\epsilon}\cdot n^{1-\epsilon}.

Thus, from (
5
) we deduce that

n^{2-\epsilon}<2Ib_{\epsilon}^{-1}([F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]-1)!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}].

Since
n:=\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
, we conclude that

\exp E(F)[\textrm{tors}]<c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}

where
c_{\epsilon}:=c_{\epsilon}(E_{0},F_{0}):=(2Ib_{\epsilon}^{-1}([F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]-1)!)^{1/(2-\epsilon)}
,
which is the desired upper bound on the exponent of
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
.
∎

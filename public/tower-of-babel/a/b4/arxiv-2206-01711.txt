Entropy and entanglement in a bipartite quasi-Hermitian system and its Hermitian counterparts
Abed Alsalam Abu Moise Graham Cox Marco Merkli

Abstract
We consider a quantum oscillator coupled to a bath of N N other oscillators. The total system evolves with a quasi-Hermitian Hamiltonian. Associated to it is a family of Hermitian systems, parameterized by a unitary map W W . Our main goal is to find the influence of W W on the entropy and the entanglement in the Hermitian systems. We calculate explicitly the reduced density matrix of the single oscillator for all Hermitian systems and show that, regardless of W W , their von Neumann entropy oscillates with a common period which is twice that of the non-Hermitian system. We show that generically, the oscillator and the bath are entangled for almost all times. While the amount of entanglement depends on the choice of W W , it is independent of W W when averaged over a period. These results describe some universality in the physical properties of all Hermitian systems associated to a given non-Hermitian one.

2
Quasi-Hermitian systems

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
. An operator
\eta
is said to be positive, denoted as
\eta>0

\langle\psi|\eta\psi\rangle>0
for all nonzero
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}
. This is equivalent with saying that
\eta^{\dagger}=\eta
and all eigenvalues of
\eta
are strictly positive. Here
A^{\dagger}
is the adjoint of the operator
A
, defined by
\langle\psi|A\phi\rangle=\langle A^{\dagger}\psi|\phi\rangle
for all
|\phi\rangle,|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}
. An operator
H
on
{\mathcal{H}}
is called (
\eta
-)
quasi-Hermitian
if there exists a positive operator
\eta>0
such that

H^{\dagger}=\eta H\eta^{-1}.

(1)

Quasi-hermiticity is a special case of
pseudo-hermiticity
, where (
1
) holds with an invertible (but not necessarily positive) Hermitian operator
\eta
. Pseudo- and quasi-hermitian Hamiltonians arise in
PT
-symmetric quantum theory, see for instance
[
31
]
and references therein.

2.1
Hermitian counterparts

Let
H
be a non-Hermitian operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
, a candidate for the Hamiltonian of a physical system. In order to obtain a Hermitian quantum theory, one could either:

(1)

Modify the inner product of
{\mathcal{H}}
to
\langle\cdot|\eta\,\cdot\rangle
for some
\eta>0
(called a metric operator), such that
H
becomes Hermitian in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
with this new inner product; or

(2)

Take a similarity transformation (invertible map)
S
such that the transformed
h=SHS^{-1}
is Hermitian in the original Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.

If
H
is quasi-Hermitian, then both options (1) and (2) are possible, but neither the metric nor the similarity transform in options (1) and (2) are unique. To explore this non-uniqueness, we first notice that any quasi-Hermitian
H
is diagonalizable
[
7
,
10
,
21
]
. More precisely,

H=\sum_{n=1}^{N}E_{n}|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|,

(2)

where the
E_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
are the eigenvalues and the
\{|\psi_{n}\rangle,|\phi_{n}\rangle\}_{n=1}^{N}
form a complete bi-orthonormal family, meaning that
\langle\psi_{k}|\phi_{l}\rangle=\delta_{kl}
and
\sum|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
. Let us consider the case where all eigenvalues
E_{n}
are distinct for simplicity (a discussion including degenerate eigenvalues can be done similarly, but this is not our focus here). Then the decomposition (
2
) is unique, it is the spectral representation of the operator
H
, and the
P_{n}\equiv|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|
are the uniquely defined (generally not orthogonal) spectral projections. The vectors
|\psi_{n}\rangle
and
|\phi_{n}\rangle
, however, are determined only up to a joint scaling
|\phi_{n}\rangle\mapsto z_{n}|\phi_{n}\rangle
and
|\psi_{n}\rangle\mapsto\frac{1}{\overline{z_{n}}}|\psi_{n}\rangle
, with
0\neq z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
arbitrary.

\bullet
First let us explore the option (1). A metric
\eta
is called a

if

is
\eta

be a linear operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
, with adjoint

, so the adjoint of

is

. It follows that a given
\eta>0

if and only if

, that is, if and only if
H
is Hermitian acting on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
. It is well known (see e.g.
[

,
10

) that

\eta\mbox{\ \, is a metric for $H$}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\eta=\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}|\phi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|\mbox{\quad for some $x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}>0$},

(3)

where the

are the vectors appearing in (

). The multitude of metrics obtained by varying the

in (

) naturally appears due to the fact that

One answer is that the metric is fixed provided that instead of just

, one chooses a whole irreducible family of operators to be Hermitian observables. Namely, it is shown in

On the other hand, if interested only in the single observable

Next, let us investigate option (2) for

Let

One readily sees that (

where

is any unitary and where for a positive operator

is the unique positive operator whose square equals

Once

h_{W,T}=W\sqrt{\eta T}\,H\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta T}}W^{\dagger}.

We stress with this notation that

In this sense, the choice of

In this work we take the following approach: We start with a given quasi-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
and an arbitrary metric

for

as the physical Hilbert space. We analyze the class of all associated

and only consider

for all

\eta

2.2
States, reduced states, von Neumann entropy

Consider now a fixed metric
\eta
, so that the physical Hilbert space is
{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
and
H
is Hermitian on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
,
H^{\ddagger}=H
. Then
e^{-itH}
is the unitary Schrödinger dynamics on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
. The average of an observable
A
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
in the state
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
is given by

\langle\psi|A\psi\rangle_{\eta}=\langle\psi|\eta A\psi\rangle={\rm tr}\big(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\eta A\big)={\rm tr}\big(\widetilde{\rho}A\big),

(9)

where

\widetilde{\rho}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\eta

(10)

is a density matrix on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\eta}
(a positive, trace-one operator). This
\widetilde{\rho}
is called the ‘generalized density matrix’ in
[
28
]
.
It is important to point out that the trace in (
9
) is a purely algebraic quantity: it is the sum of the eigenvalues of the operator, and therefore does not depend on the choice of metric.

To arrive at a Hermitian Hamiltonian, it is necessary to make a choice for the unitary
W
in (
8
). The associated Hermitian Hamiltonian
h_{W}
is then given by (
8
). Let

|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-itH}|\psi(0)\rangle,\quad|\phi(t)\rangle=e^{-ith_{W}}|\phi(0)\rangle

(11)

be the evolution of the initial states
|\psi(0)\rangle
,
|\phi(0)\rangle
with respect to
H
and
h_{W}
, respectively. The states are related by

|\phi(t)\rangle=S|\psi(t)\rangle,\quad S=W\sqrt{\eta},

(12)

and the density matrices associated to these vector states for the non-Hermitian (see (
10
)) and the Hermitian systems are

\rho_{H}(t)=|\psi(t)\rangle\langle\psi(t)|\eta\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho_{h_{W}}(t)=|\phi(t)\rangle\langle\phi(t)|,

(13)

respectively.
(We adopt the notation
\rho_{h_{W}}
and
\rho_{H}
for the density matrices on the Hermitian and non-Hermitian sides of the problem from
[
13
]
.) It is clear from (
12
) that

\rho_{{h_{W}}}(t)=S|\psi(t)\rangle\langle\psi(t)|S^{\dagger}=S|\psi(t)\rangle\langle\psi(t)|(S^{\dagger}S)S^{-1}=S\rho_{H}(t)S^{-1}.

(14)

It follows that
\rho_{h_{W}}(t)
and
\rho_{H}(t)
have the same eigenvalues, and hence the same von Neumann entropy,
{\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{h_{W}}(t))={\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{H}(t))
, where

{\mathcal{E}}(\rho)=-{\rm tr}\big(\rho\ln\rho\big)=-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\ln\lambda_{i}

(15)

and
\{\lambda_{i}\}
are the eigenvalues of
\rho
.

Consider now a bipartite system with
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}
(‘system’ and ‘bath’). We consider the
reduced states
(denoted by an overbar) defined by tracing out the degrees of freedom of the subsystem
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}
,

\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)={\rm tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}}\big(\rho_{H}(t)\big),\qquad\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t)={\rm tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}}\big(\rho_{h_{W}}(t)\big).

(16)

In some recent works
[
13
,
17
,
18
,
5
]
, the dynamics of a bipartite system generated by a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

is studied, with particular focus on the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)
. The strategy proposed in those works is to examine the entropy of
\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t)
as a proxy for that of
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)
. In this respect, however, one should observe the following facts:

1.

The operator
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)
always satisfies
{\rm tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}}(\bar{\rho}_{H}(t))=1
, but for some choices of
\eta
the eigenvalues of
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)
can be complex, in which case it is not a valid density matrix.

2.

Even if the metric
\eta
is chosen such that
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)
is a density matrix, for generic choices of
W
the von Neumann entropies
{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{\rho}_{H}(t))
and
{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t))
are not the same. The latter in fact depends on the choice of
W
.

To understand the normalization of the trace mentioned in fact 1. above, we observe (using
{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{\rm S}
as the system observable) that

{\rm tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}}(\bar{\rho}_{H}(t))={\rm tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}}(\bar{\rho}_{H}(t){\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{\rm S})={\rm tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}}\big(\rho_{H}(t)({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{\rm S}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{\rm B})\big)={\rm tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}}(\rho_{H}(t))=1.

If

, then
\bar{\rho}_{h}=S_{\rm S}\bar{\rho}_{H}S_{\rm S}^{-1}
and so the spectra and thus the von Neumann entropies of

\bar{\rho}_{H}

These difficulties are resolved in the next section, where we study the concrete model used in
[
13

,
a
is coupled to a ‘bath’ of
N
independent oscillators with creation and annihilation operators
q_{i}^{\dagger}
,
q_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
.
The total Hilbert space of the
N+1
oscillators is

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\rm S}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\rm B},

(17)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\rm S}
is the space of a single oscillator and
\mathcal{H}_{\rm B}
is that of the other
N
. As in the previous section, we denote the inner product by
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
and let

\dagger
denote the adjoint in this inner product.
The commutation relations are
[a,a^{\dagger}]=1=[q_{i},q^{\dagger}_{i}]
, and all operators belonging to different oscillators commute. This open quantum system, non-Hermitian model was used in
[
13
]
.

3.1
The quasi-Hermitian system

The coupled total system–bath Hamiltonian is

H=\nu N_{\rm tot}+(g+\kappa)\sqrt{N}\,a^{\dagger}Q+(g-\kappa)\sqrt{N}\ aQ^{\dagger},

(18)

where
\nu>0
and
g,\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
are parameters and

N_{\rm tot}=a^{\dagger}a+\sum_{n=1}^{N}q_{n}^{\dagger}q_{n},\qquad Q=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}q_{n}.

(19)

Due to the different prefactors of
\kappa
in the interaction term of (
18
),
H
is
\dagger
-Hermitian if and only if
\kappa=0
.

The ‘uncoupled’ (
g=\kappa=0
) Hamiltonian is simply
\nu N_{\rm tot}
, a multiple of the total number operator
N_{\rm tot}
. As
H
commutes with
N_{\rm tot}
, each eigenspace of
N_{\rm tot}
, with a fixed number of excitations (in the system plus the bath) is left invariant. Denote by
|0_{\rm S}0_{\rm B}\rangle
the ‘vacuum’ zero excitation state, where all oscillators are in the ground state. The single excitation space is defined as

{\mathcal{E}}_{1}={\rm span}\big\{|1_{\rm S}0_{\rm B}\rangle,|0_{\rm S}1_{1}\rangle,|0_{\rm S}1_{2}\rangle,\ldots,|0_{\rm S}1_{N}\rangle\big\},

(20)

where
|1_{\rm S}0_{\rm B}\rangle=a^{\dagger}|0_{\rm S}0_{\rm B}\rangle
and
|0_{\rm S}1_{i}\rangle=q^{\dagger}_{i}|0_{\rm S}0_{\rm B}\rangle
for
i=1,\ldots,N
. When
H
is applied to a vector in
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
the result is again a vector in
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
. Moreover, due to the collective, symmetric nature of the system-bath interaction in (
18
),
H
leaves the even smaller space

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}={\rm span}\big\{|e_{\rm S}\rangle,\ |e_{\rm B}\rangle\big\}

(21)

invariant, where

|e_{\rm S}\rangle=|1_{\rm S}0_{\rm B}\rangle,\qquad|e_{\rm B}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|0_{\rm S}1_{n}\rangle.

(22)

Those two vectors describe states in which a single excitation is either in
{\rm S}
(the state
|e_{\rm S}\rangle
) or in
{\rm B}
, collectively spread over the
N
bath oscillators (the state
|e_{\rm B}\rangle
).
Therefore, we may view
H
as an operator on
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
. When we do this we denote it by
H_{1}
, which has the form

H_{1}=\nu{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+(g-\kappa)\sqrt{N}\ |e_{\rm B}\rangle\langle e_{\rm S}|+(g+\kappa)\sqrt{N}\ |e_{\rm S}\rangle\langle e_{\rm B}|.

(23)

The eigenvalues of
H_{1}
are

\omega_{\pm}=\nu\pm\omega,\qquad\omega=\sqrt{N}\sqrt{g^{2}-\kappa^{2}},

(24)

which are real for
\kappa^{2}\leq g^{2}
and (purely imaginary) complex conjugates for
\kappa^{2}>g^{2}
. See
[
13
]
for a discussion of the
PT
symmetry of
H
. The operator
H_{1}
is diagonalizable except at the transition points defined by
\kappa^{2}=g^{2}\neq 0
, where
H_{1}
reduces to a Jordan block. Note that increasing the number
N
of oscillators in the bath simply amounts to speeding up the dynamics (the frequency
\omega
) by a factor
\sqrt{N}
.

We consider the ‘
PT
-symmetry unbroken regime’
\kappa^{2}<g^{2}
, so that
\omega_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For definiteness we take
g>0
(the case
g<0
can be dealt with in the same fashion), so

0\leq|\kappa|<g,

(25)

which is equivalent to
g+\kappa>0
and
g-\kappa>0
. Then we have
\omega>0
and

a_{1}=\sqrt{g+\kappa}>0,\quad a_{2}=\sqrt{g-\kappa}>0,

(26)

where the equalities in (
26
) define the quantities
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
.
The two linearly independent (not normalized) eigenvectors of
H_{1}
and its adjoint
H_{1}^{\dagger}
are

|v_{\pm}\rangle\propto a_{1}|e_{\rm S}\rangle\pm a_{2}|e_{\rm B}\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad|v^{*}_{\pm}\rangle\propto a_{2}|e_{\rm S}\rangle\pm a_{1}|e_{\rm B}\rangle,

respectively. They satisfy
H_{1}|v_{\pm}\rangle=\omega_{\pm}|v_{\pm}\rangle
and
H^{\dagger}_{1}|v^{*}_{\pm}\rangle=\omega_{\pm}|v^{*}_{\pm}\rangle
. Note that
|v_{\pm}^{*}\rangle
denote the eigenvectors of
H^{\dagger}
, not to be confused with the complex conjugates of the eigenvectors
|v_{\pm}\rangle
of
H
. We normalize the vectors as

|v_{\pm}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}}|e_{\rm S}\rangle\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}}|e_{\rm B}\rangle\Big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad|v^{*}_{\pm}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}}|e_{\rm S}\rangle\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}}|e_{\rm B}\rangle\Big).

(27)

Then
\{|v_{\pm}\rangle,|v^{*}_{\pm}\rangle\}
is a bi-orthonormal basis, satisfying
\langle v^{*}_{\pm}|v_{\mp}\rangle=0
and
\langle v^{*}_{\pm}|v_{\pm}\rangle=1
, and the operator
H_{1}
can be written as

H_{1}=\omega_{+}|v_{+}\rangle\langle v^{*}_{+}|+\omega_{-}|v_{-}\rangle\langle v^{*}_{-}|.

(28)

Using this, one easily finds

\displaystyle e^{-itH_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-it\omega_{+}}|v_{+}\rangle\langle v^{*}_{+}|+e^{-it\omega_{-}}|v_{-}\rangle\langle v^{*}_{-}|

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-it\nu}\cos(\omega t){\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}-ie^{-it\nu}\sin(\omega t)\Big(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}|e_{\rm S}\rangle\langle e_{\rm B}|+\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}|e_{\rm B}\rangle\langle e_{\rm S}|\Big).

We consider initial states which are vectors in
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
, as defined in (
21
), so the dynamics generated by
H
is entirely given by the operator
H_{1}
from (
23
). We still consider the regime (
25
), so that the spectrum of
H_{1}
consists of two distinct real eigenvalues. Comparing (
2
), (
3
) and (

), we see that

is quasi-Hermitian and the set of all associated metrics is

{\mathcal{M}}_{+}=\big\{\eta=x_{1}|v_{+}^{*}\rangle\langle v_{+}^{*}|+x_{2}|v_{-}^{*}\rangle\langle v_{-}^{*}|\ :\ x_{1},x_{2}>0\big\}.

(30)

Written as a matrix in the basis
\{|e_{\rm S}\rangle,|e_{\rm B}\rangle\}
, we obtain from (
27
)

\eta=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}(x_{1}+x_{2})\,a_{2}/a_{1}&x_{1}-x_{2}\\
x_{1}-x_{2}&(x_{1}+x_{2})\,a_{1}/a_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(31)

This is diagonal exactly when
x_{1}=x_{2}
. As we will see in Section

, this is equivalent to
\eta
being the
restriction to
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
of a product metric
\Lambda_{\rm S}\otimes\Lambda_{\rm B}
on

. And as we discuss below after (
39
), this is also equivalent to the reduced system state
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)
in (
38
) being a positive operator.

3.2

Fix an

4.1

Entanglement of system and bath oscillators

The total Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}
in (
17
) is bipartite, one part being the singled-out oscillator (system), the other being the remaining
N
oscillators (bath). We say that a nonzero vector
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}
is of
product form
, or
disentangled
, if
|\psi\rangle=|\psi_{\rm S}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{\rm B}\rangle
for some
|\psi_{\rm S}\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm S}
and some
|\psi_{\rm B}\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}
. We call a nonzero
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}

entangled
if it is not of product form. The notion of being entangled or not does not depend on the metric determining the inner product of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Nevertheless, the physical interpretation of entanglement in terms of independence of the subsystems
{\rm S}
and
{\rm B}
does depend on the metric. The physical manifestation of disentangled states is the independence of the two subsystems
{\rm S}
and
{\rm B}
. Namely, if the inner product of
{\mathcal{H}}
is given by a metric of the form

\eta=\Lambda_{\rm S}\otimes\Lambda_{\rm B}
(a particular example being
{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{\rm S}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{\rm B}
), then
measurement outcomes
of observables on either of the subsystems are
independent random variables
. This follows because expectation values of observables
O_{\rm S}\otimes O_{\rm B}
in a state
|\psi_{\rm S}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{\rm B}\rangle
split into products,

\langle\psi_{\rm S}\otimes\psi_{\rm B}|\eta(O_{\rm S}\otimes O_{\rm B})\psi_{\rm S}\otimes\psi_{\rm B}\rangle=\langle\psi_{\rm S}|\Lambda_{\rm S}O_{\rm S}\psi_{\rm S}\rangle\langle\psi_{\rm B}|\Lambda_{\rm B}O_{\rm B}\psi_{\rm B}\rangle.

However, those random variables become dependent (correlated) if
\eta
is not of product form, because then their average will not split into a product of a system term times a bath term.

In the model defined in Section
3
, the metrics
\eta
we consider are restrictions to the subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
of product metrics
\Lambda_{\rm S}\otimes\Lambda_{\rm B}
of
{\mathcal{H}}
(c.f. (
31
) and Section
A.1
). The physical meaning of
{\rm S}{\rm B}
entanglement in terms of subsystem independence does therefore not depend on the choice of
\eta
within this class. In other words, measurement outcomes of system and bath observables in

are independent or dependent, according to whether
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
is disentangled or not, regardless of the choice of
\eta
. It is then sensible to investigate the SB entanglement in pure states belonging to the subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
for all
\eta
.

Any vector

is of the form

|\psi\rangle=A|e_{\rm S}\rangle+B|e_{\rm B}\rangle,\qquad A,B\in\mathbb{C}.

6
Conclusion

The Dyson map assigns to a given quasi-Hermitian quantum system an associated Hermitian system in a non-unique way. We quantify the non-uniqueness by means of a metric operator
\eta
and a unitary map
W
. The physical properties of the Hermitian systems depend on the choice of
W
, and it is not obvious how to capture the dynamics of the original quasi-Hermitian system in its Hermitian counterparts – unless there happens to be some universality throughout the Hermitian family. We describe an aspect of universality for a quasi-Hermitian open system consisting of a single oscillator coupled to a bath of
N
oscillators. We show that there is a unique metric operator for which the reduced state of the system (single oscillator) is a well-defined density matrix. Using this metric, we construct all Hermitian systems obtained from the quasi-Hermitian one by varying
W
. We find that the entropy of the single oscillator in the Hermitian system evolves periodically in time with exactly
double the period
of the corresponding entropy of the quasi-Hermitian system, independently of
W
. We further show that averaged over one time period, the entanglement between the oscillator and the bath is independent of the choice of
W
.

[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{11}\Lambda_{\rm S}p_{11}+[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{22}\Lambda_{\rm S}p_{22}=[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{11}[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{11}\ p_{11}+[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{22}[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{22}\ p_{22}.

(77)

Since
[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{11},[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{22}>0
we get from (
77
) that
\Lambda_{\rm S}p_{jj}=[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{jj}p_{jj}
for
j=1,2
, so
\Lambda_{\rm S}|e_{j}\rangle=[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{jj}|e_{j}\rangle
. Hence the restriction of
\Lambda_{\rm S}
to
{\rm span}\{|e_{1}\rangle,|e_{2}\rangle\}
is diagonal,
\Lambda_{\rm S}=[\Lambda_{1}]_{11}p_{11}+[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{22}p_{22}+\Lambda^{\perp}_{\rm S}
, where
\Lambda_{\rm S}^{\perp}
is the block acting on the orthogonal complement of that span. By taking the partial trace over
{\rm S}
in (
75
) and (
76
) and proceeding analogously, we see that
\Lambda_{\rm B}=[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{11}q_{11}+[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{22}q_{22}+\Lambda_{\rm B}^{\perp}
. It follows that
(\Lambda_{\rm S}\otimes\Lambda_{\rm B})|v_{jj}\rangle=[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{jj}[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{jj}\,|v_{jj}\rangle
for
j=1,2
,
so

\eta=[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{11}[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{11}\,|v_{11}\rangle\langle v_{11}|+[\Lambda_{\rm S}]_{22}[\Lambda_{\rm B}]_{22}\,|v_{22}\rangle\langle v_{22}|

is diagonal.

A.2
Conditions for
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)=\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t)
and
\mathcal{E}(\bar{\rho}_{H}(t))=\mathcal{E}(\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t))

In this section we assume the metric
\eta
is of the form (
30
) with
x_{1}=x_{2}=x>0
. Recall the formulas (
41
) and (
51
) for the quasi-Hermitian and Hermitian density matrices.

First we ask when the two reduced density matrices coincide. We show that the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)=\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t)
for all
t
in an open interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and all
A,B\in\mathbb{C}
;

\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)=\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t)
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and all
A,B\in\mathbb{C}
;

3.

There are two real phases
\Phi_{1}
,
\Phi_{2}
, such that

1.
\Rightarrow
3. : Assume that
\bar{\rho}_{H}(t)=\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t)
for

for

\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\cos(2\omega t)+\xi_{3}\sin(2\omega t)=0\mbox{\quad for all $t\in I$},

with

. As the constant function and the sine and cosine are three independent functions, and since (

) holds for all

in an interval, we conclude that
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=\xi_{3}=0
. Since
\xi_{1}=0
for all
A,B
and

, we have

. The coefficients

. Then due to (

\Rightarrow

\Rightarrow

4.

\mathcal{E}(\bar{\rho}_{H}(t))=\mathcal{E}(\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t))
for all

in an open interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and all

\mathcal{E}(\bar{\rho}_{H}(t))=\mathcal{E}(\bar{\rho}_{h_{W}}(t))

6. : Start by looking at the function
\mathcal{E}(q)=-q\ln(q)-(1-q)\ln(1-q)

A.3
About the Dyson map

The idea of mapping a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian to a Hermitian one was originally presented by Dyson in the context of the theory of magnetization
[
8
,
9
]
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
and let
H
be an operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
that is not necessarily Hermitian with respect to
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
. Denote by
|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-itH}|\psi(0)\rangle
the solution of the evolution equation

i\partial_{t}|\psi(t)\rangle=H|\psi(t)\rangle.

(79)

Next, let
S(t)
be a differentiable family of operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
S(t)
is invertible for each
t
, and set

|\varphi(t)\rangle=S(t)|\psi(t)\rangle.

(80)

Passing from
|\psi(t)\rangle
to
|\varphi(t)\rangle
represents a (possibly time-dependent) change of variables. The evolution equation for
|\varphi(t)\rangle
is

i\partial_{t}|\varphi(t)\rangle=h(t)|\varphi(t)\rangle,

(81)

with

h(t)=S(t)HS(t)^{-1}+i\dot{S}(t)S(t)^{-1},

(82)

the dot being the time derivative. Conversely, if
|\varphi(t)\rangle
solves (
81
) then
|\psi(t)\rangle
solves (
79
). Equation (
82
) is called the
time-dependent Dyson equation

[
13
]
. By means of
S(t)
, one may thus equivalently solve (
79
) or (
81
). If
H
is not Hermitian, one can look for
S(t)
such that the resulting
h(t)
is Hermitian, hence trading a non-Hermitian problem with constant Hamiltonian
H
for a Hermitian problem with time-dependent Hamiltonian
h(t)
. One readily sees that

h(t)^{\dagger}=h(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i\partial_{t}\big(S^{\dagger}(t)S(t)\big)=H^{\dagger}\big(S^{\dagger}(t)S(t)\big)-\big(S^{\dagger}(t)S(t)\big)H.

(83)

The operator
\eta(t)=S(t)^{\dagger}S(t)
is automatically positive, so
\eta(t)
is a family of metrics. The equation for
\eta(t)
, according to (
83
), is

i\partial_{t}\eta(t)=H^{\dagger}\eta(t)-\eta(t)H.

(84)

This is called the
quasi-Hermiticity relation
in
[
11
]
; note that it simplifies to (
1
) if
\eta
does not depend on time. It is clear that (
84
) has a unique solution for any initial condition
\eta(0)
, namely

\eta(t)=e^{-itH^{\dagger}}\eta(0)e^{itH},

(85)

and that
\eta(t)
is positive for all times if and only if it is positive at some
t_{0}
.

A strategy to study the dynamics generated by a non-Hermitian
H
is to find a transformation
S(t)
such that
h(t)
, as given by (
82
), is Hermitian, and then analyze the dynamics of this Hermitian system using usual quantum theoretical methods. Finding
S(t)
for a specific Hamiltonian
H
is not easy, however. It often involves making a judicious ansatz containing parameters that must solve rather complicated differential equations, which are obtained by imposing the self-adjointness of
h(t)
. This can be done explicitly for some models
[
4
,
11
,
13
,
24
,
27
,
15
,
16
]
.

Given
H
, we seek
all
possible
S(t)
, and the resulting Hermitian Hamiltonians
h(t)
, with the sole requirement that
\eta(t)=S(t)^{\dagger}S(t)
is positive and satisfies the quasi-Hermiticity relation (
84
). The solution
\eta(t)
is uniquely determined by the initial condition
\eta(0)
, which we may choose to be any positive, invertible operator. The most general form of
S(t)
is thus

S(t)=W(t)\sqrt{\eta(0)}e^{itH},

(86)

where
W(t)
is any unitary family
and
\sqrt{\eta(0)}
denotes the unique positive operator squaring to
\eta(0)
. The
h(t)
associated to (
86
) by (
82
) is

h(t)=i\dot{W}(t)W(t)^{\dagger}.

(87)

Note that we are entirely free to choose
W(t)
. For instance, given an arbitrary
A=A^{\dagger}
, the choice
W(t)=e^{-itA}
yields
h(t)=A
. This means any time-independent Hermitian
h
can be obtained from a suitable choice of
S(t)
. A particularly simple choice is
S(t)=e^{itH}
, which results from undoing the dynamics
e^{-itH}
(going backwards in time) and has
h=0
.

More generally, suppose
A(t)
is a continuous family of operators, and let
W(t)
solve the differential equation

i\dot{W}(t)=A(t)W(t).

(88)

It is easily shown that if
A(t)=A(t)^{\dagger}
for all
t
and the initial condition
W(0)
is unitary, then the solution
W(t)
is unitary for all
t
. Choosing this
W(t)
, we find from (
87
) the Hermitian Hamiltonian
h(t)=i\dot{W}(t)W(t)^{\dagger}=i\dot{W}(t)W(t)^{-1}=A(t)
. This means any time-
dependent
Hermitian
h(t)
can also be obtained from a suitable choice of
S(t)
.

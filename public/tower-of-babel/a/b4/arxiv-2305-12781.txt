Uniform estimates for conforming Galerkin method for anisotropic singularly perturbed elliptic problems
David Maltese a and Chokri Ogabi b

Abstract
In this article, we study some anisotropic singular perturbations for a class of linear elliptic problems. A uniform estimates for conforming Q 1 Q_{1} finite element method are derived, and some other results of convergence and regularity for the continuous problem are proved.

Introduction

The numerical study of singular
perturbation
problems keeps
an important place in numerical analysis
. Consider an elliptic problem
P_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})=0
where
\epsilon\in(0,1]
is a small parameter. Let
P_{\epsilon,h}(u_{\epsilon,h})=0
be the numerical approximation of the continuous problem
P_{\epsilon}
.
The
estimates
obtained by a classical analysis, for instance, by the Céa’s lemma, is of the form

\|u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon}\|_{\Omega}\leq C\frac{h}{\epsilon^{\alpha}},

(1)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
is some norm on a suitable space on
\Omega
. To ensure a good numerical approximation of the exact solution
u_{\epsilon}
when
\epsilon
is very small, then one must take
h
much smaller than
\epsilon
, which is impractical from the numerical point of view. For some kind of numerical scheme which called asymptotically preserved, that is when we have
\lim_{\epsilon}\lim_{h}u_{\epsilon,h}=\lim_{h}\lim_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon,h}
, one can obtain
\epsilon-
uniform
estimates
. In
[
1
]
J. Sin gave a simple method to obtain such
estimates
. The idea is combining (
1
) with
another estimate
of the form

\|u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon}\|_{\Omega}\leq C(\epsilon^{\beta}+h^{\gamma}),

(2)

to obtain the
\epsilon-
uniform
estimate

\|u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon}\|_{\Omega}\leq Ch^{\min(\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta},\gamma)}.

For isotropic singular perturbations, which model diffusion phenomena in isotropic medium, many authors studied conforming and non conforming Galerkin methods for the following problem

-\epsilon^{2}\Delta u_{\epsilon}+au_{\epsilon}=f\text{, with }u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
\Omega

is a square
or a cube and
f
is sufficiently regular (of class
C^{2}
) with some compatibility condition, that is when
f
is zero on the edge of the vertices of
\Omega
. For instance, in
[
2
]
a uniform
estimate
of the form
|||u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon}|||_{\Omega}=O(h^{1/2})
is proved, where
|||\cdot|||_{\Omega}
is a variant of the energy norm. Some other quasi-uniform logarithmic
estimates
have been proved, see for instance the references
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and those cited therein.

V_{h}=V_{h}^{1}\otimes(V_{h}^{2}\otimes V_{h}^{3})=(V_{h}^{1}\otimes V_{h}^{2})\otimes V_{h}^{3}
, the first equality corresponds to the case

, and the second equality corresponds to the case
q=2
.

Now, we are ready to prove the following:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega=(0,1)^{N}
, with
N\in\{2,3\}
. Assume that
A
satisfies (
8
), (
10
), and (
13
). Let
f
such that (
5
) is satisfied, then there exist a positive constant
C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}>0
such that

\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}(h^{1/4}+\epsilon^{1/2}).

In particular, if we assume in addition that
f\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
then we have

\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}(h+\epsilon).

We process by several steps.

Step 1.

Let
u_{h}
be the solution of the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\Omega}A_{22}\nabla_{X_{2}}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{X_{2}}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}I_{h}(f)\text{ }vdx,\quad\forall v\in V_{h}\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ }\\
u_{h}\in V_{h}.\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\end{array}\right.

(49)

We have the following

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that assumptions of Proposition
2.5
hold then:

- For
f\in H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
we have

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}\times\epsilon.

(50)

- For
f\in H^{2}(\Omega)
we have

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\,f,A,\Omega}}(\epsilon+h^{1/3}).

(51)

Proof.

1) Suppose that
f\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, then
I_{h}(f)\in V_{h}
. Therefore, according to Lemma
2.3
and Remark
2.4
with
g
and
V
replaced by
I_{h}(f)
and
V_{h}
respectively, we have

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,A,\Omega}}\left(\left\|\nabla_{X_{1}}I_{h}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{q}}+\left\|I_{h}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\times\epsilon.

Since
I_{h}(f)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then by using Poincaré’s inequality (
22
) we obtain

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,A,\Omega}}\times\left\|\nabla_{X_{1}}I_{h}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{q}}\times\epsilon.

(52)

In the other hand (
42
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{X_{1}}I_{h}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{q}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{N}h\left\|f\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{N}\|f\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

(53)

Therefore, from (
52
) and (
53
) yields

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,\Omega,A}}\epsilon.

(54)

2) Now, we suppose that
f\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, from Lemma
1.5
we use the decomposition
f=f_{\delta}^{1}+f_{\delta}^{2}
, and let
u_{\epsilon,h}^{i}
,
u_{h}^{i}\text{, }i=1,2
be the solutions of (
44
) and (
49
) respectively with
f
replaced by
f_{\delta}^{i}
. The linearity of the equations and the operator
I_{h}
gives

u_{\epsilon,h}=u_{\epsilon,h}^{1}+u_{\epsilon,h}^{2}\text{ and }u_{h}=u_{h}^{1}+u_{h}^{2}

(55)

As in (
52
) we have

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}^{1}-u_{h}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,\Omega,A}}\left\|\nabla_{X_{1}}I_{h}(f_{\delta}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{q}}\times\epsilon.

(56)

According to (
42
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{X_{1}}I_{h}(f_{\delta}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{q}}\leq\left\|f_{\delta}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{N}h\left\|f_{\delta}^{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

and therefore, by applying Lemma
1.5
we get

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{X_{1}}I_{h}(f_{\delta}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{q}}\leq C_{{}_{f,\Omega}}\Big(1+h\delta^{-3/2}\Big).

Combining this with (
56
) to obtain

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}^{1}-u_{h}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}\Big(1+h\delta^{-3/2}\Big)\times\epsilon.

(57)

In the other hand, testing by
u_{\epsilon,h}^{2}
and
u_{h}^{2}
is the corresponding formulations (
44
) and (
49
), with
f
replaced by
f_{\delta}^{2}
, we obtain this basic
estimate

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}^{2}-u_{h}^{2})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,\Omega}}\|f_{\delta}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(58)

Now, applying Lemma
1.5
to the right hand side of (
58
) we obtain

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}^{2}-u_{h}^{2})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,\Omega}}\times\delta^{1/2}

(59)

The combination of (
55
), (
57
) and (
59
) gives, by the triangle inequality, the following

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}\Big(\epsilon+h\delta^{-3/2}\epsilon+\delta^{1/2}\Big).

(60)

Finally, since
\delta
is arbitrary in
(0,1)
, then by setting
\delta=h^{2/3}
in the previous inequality we get

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}\Big(\epsilon+h^{1/3}\Big).

(61)

∎

Step 2.

We denote
w_{h}
the solution to following the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\Omega}A_{22}\nabla_{X_{2}}w_{h}\cdot\nabla_{X_{2}}vdx=\int_{\Omega}f\text{ }vdx,\quad\forall v\in V_{h}\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ }\\
w_{h}\in V_{h}.\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\end{array}\right.

(62)

We have the following

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that assumptions of Proposition
2.5
hold, then:

- If
f\in H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, we have :

\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}-u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}h

- If
f\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, we have:

\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}-u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}h^{1/4}

Proof.

1) Suppose that
f\in H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
:

By using the classical Céa’s Lemma we obtain from (
19
) and (
62
) the following

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}-u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq\frac{\left\|A_{22}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\lambda}\inf_{v\in V_{h}}\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(v-u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Now, according to Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.1
we have
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, then
I_{h}(u)\in V_{h}
. Therefore, from the above inequality we obtain

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}-u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq\frac{\left\|A_{22}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\lambda}\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(I_{h}(u)-u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

Finally, by applying (
42
) and Theorem
1.8
to the right hand side of the above inequality, we get

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}-u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}h.

2)Suppose that
f\in H^{2}(\Omega)
:

We use the decomposition trick of Lemma
1.5
, and let
w_{h}^{i}
,
u^{i}
,

be the solutions of (
62
) and (
19
) with
f
replaced by
f_{\delta}^{i}
respectively. As in the previous case we have

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}^{1}-u^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq\frac{\left\|A_{22}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\lambda}\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(I_{h}(u^{1})-u^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and the application of (

) to the right hid side of the previous inequality gives

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}^{1}-u^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\left\|A_{22}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\lambda}\left\|u^{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}h,

and by Theorem
1.8
we get

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}^{1}-u^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,A,\Omega}}\left\|f_{\delta}^{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}h,

whence by applying Lemma
1.5

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}^{1}-u^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}\delta^{-3/2}h,

Testing with

Step 3.

.

2.5

\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{{}_{f,\Omega}}h.

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}\leq\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}+\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{h}-w_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(w_{h}-u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}+\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u-u_{\epsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N-q}}

2.7

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{X_{2}}(u_{\epsilon,h}-u_{\epsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{{}_{\lambda,f,A,\Omega}}h^{1/5},\text{ when }f\in H^{2}(\Omega),

Remark 2.9
.

\epsilon

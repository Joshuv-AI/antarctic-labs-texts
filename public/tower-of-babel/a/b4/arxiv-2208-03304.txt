Introduction

Let
K
be a totally real algebraic number field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We associate to
K
the embeddings
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n}
into
\mathbb{R}
, that necessarily fix
\mathbb{Q}
. A quadratic form
f:K^{m}\to K
is defined by

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\sum_{k,l=1}^{m}f_{kl}x_{k}x_{l}.

We say that
f
is positive-definite if

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\sum_{k,l=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}(f_{k,l})x_{k}x_{l}

is positive definite for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

Quadratic forms of the form
ax^{2}
, where
a\in K
, are called unary forms. We say that
a
is a totally positive element of
K
if the form
ax^{2}
is positive definite, and we denote
K_{>>0}
the set of totally positive elements of
K
. Note that for all
a\in K_{>>0}
,
\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})
corresponds to a positive-definite quadratic form in
n
variables with rational coefficients. This is known as the trace-form of the unary form
ax^{2}
. For each
a\in K_{>>0}
, we will use the notation

\displaystyle\mu(a)=\min_{x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\setminus\{0\}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(a)=\{x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}:\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})=\mu(a)\}.

If
a^{\prime}=au^{2}
for some
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, we say that the unary form
a^{\prime}x^{2}
is equivalent to the form
ax^{2}
, and we denote this relation by
a\sim a^{\prime}
. Note that since the action of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
fixes
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we have
\mu(a)=\mu(a^{\prime})
,
|\mathcal{M}(a)|=|\mathcal{M}(a^{\prime})|
and each element of
\mathcal{M}(a)
is equivalent to an element of
\mathcal{M}(a^{\prime})
, and vice versa. Finally, we say that
K
is a
A
-reducible if for every
a\in K_{>>0}
, if
x\in\mathcal{M}(a)
then
|\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x)|\leq A
. In particular, if
A=1
we say that
K
is a unit reducible field.

A unary form
ax^{2}
is said to be perfect if it is uniquely determined by
\mu(a)
and
\mathcal{M}(a)
. It is immediately clear that if
ax^{2}
is a perfect unary form, then any unary form of the type
\lambda a^{\prime}x^{2}
is a perfect unary form, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
and
a^{\prime}\sim a
. In this way, we may classify perfect unary forms up to scaling and equivalence, or in other words, by their homothety classes. It is known that for number fields of fixed dimension, the number of classes of perfect forms can grow arbitrarily large (see e.g.
[
7
]
, in which it is shown that the number of classes of perfect unary forms for real quadratic fields can become arbitrarily large).

In this paper, we follow a similar argument to van Woerden in
[
6
]
in order to determine an upper bound on the number of homothety classes of perfect unary forms for an arbitrary totally real number field. Our result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field with field discriminant
\Delta_{K}
, regulator
R_{K}
and degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
n_{K}
denote the number of homothety classes of perfect unary forms in
K
. Then

\displaystyle n_{K}\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{K}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}|\Delta_{K}|\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2n}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{4},

where

•

\eta_{K}=0
if
K
is a unit reducible field,

•

\eta_{K}=\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{2}R_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}
if
K
is not unit reducible and
2\leq n\leq 11
,

•

\eta_{K}=\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n-1}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\frac{1}{1000}\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{3}\right)\right)^{2-n}R_{K}
otherwise,

and

•

\theta_{K}=\frac{4\log(A)^{2}}{n-1}
, if
K
is
A
-reducible.

We will also prove that any totally real field
K
is
A
-reducible, where

\displaystyle A\leq n^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{|\Delta_{K}|}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right).

This leads us to the following more general theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field with field discriminant
\Delta_{K}
, regulator
R_{K}
and degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
n_{K}
denote the number of homothety classes of perfect unary forms in
K
. Then

\displaystyle n_{K}\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{K}^{2}+\rho_{K}}}|\Delta_{K}|\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2n}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{4},

where
\eta_{K}
is as defined before, and

•

\rho_{K}=0
if
K
is unit reducible,

•

\rho_{K}=\frac{4}{n-1}\log\left(n^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{|\Delta_{K}|}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)^{2}\approx\frac{4}{n-1}\log(\sqrt{|\Delta_{K}|})^{2}
otherwise.

Proof of Theorem
1

We will define the set
K_{>>0}^{1}
as the subset of elements in
K_{>>0}
such that
\mu(a)=1
. Moreover, denote
\mathcal{F}_{K}
the fundamental region of
K_{>>0}
, that is, the set of elements such that for all
a\in K_{>>0}
, there exists an
a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
such that
a\sim a^{\prime}
. For any perfect unary form
ax^{2}
, we will use the notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(a)=\left\{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{M}(a)}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{2}:\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}\right\},

which is called the Voronoi cone of
a
. Denote
P_{K}
the set of
a\in K_{>>0}
such that
ax^{2}
determine all the distinct homothety classes of perfect unary forms. It was proven by the work of Koecher
[
4
]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{K}=\bigcup_{a\in P_{K}}\mathcal{V}(a),

From now on, we will use the notation
\mathcal{V}^{1}(a)=\mathcal{V}(a)\cap K_{>>0}^{1}
for any perfect unary form
ax^{2}
. First, we want to prove the following useful lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that
ax^{2}
and
bx^{2}
are perfect unary forms in
K
, and
a,b
belong to distinct homothety classes. Then
\text{Int}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a))\cap\text{Int}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(b))=\phi
, where Int means the interior of the cone generated by
\mathcal{V}(a)
or
\mathcal{V}(b)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
c\in\text{Int}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a))\cap\text{Int}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(b))
, and
c\neq 0
. We want to show that if this holds, then
a=b
, which is a contradiction. Since
c\in\text{Int}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a))
, there exist
\lambda_{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle c=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{M}(a)}\lambda_{x}x^{2},

so

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(bc)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{M}(a)}\lambda_{x}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(bx^{2})\geq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{M}(a)}\lambda_{x}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{M}(a)}\lambda_{x}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})=\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ac).

Similarly, since
c\in\text{Int}(\mathcal{V}(b))
we can deduce that
\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(bc)\leq\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ac)
, so it must hold that
\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ac)=\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(bc)
. Then

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}((a-b)c)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{M}(b)}\lambda_{x}(\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})-1)=0.

Given that
\lambda_{x}>0
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}(b)
, it holds that
\mathcal{M}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{M}(a)
. By a similar argument, we deduce that
\mathcal{M}(a)\subseteq\mathcal{M}(b)
, and so
\mathcal{M}(a)=\mathcal{M}(b)
. Since
\mu(a)=\mu(b)
, by the perfectness of
ax^{2}
, it must hold that
a=b
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Denote
\mathcal{F}_{K}^{1}=\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap K_{>>0}^{1}
. Then by the lemma above,

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(\mathcal{F}_{K}^{1})=\text{Vol}\left(\bigcup_{a\in P_{K}}\mathcal{V}^{1}(a)\right)=\sum_{a\in P_{K}}\text{Vol}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a))\geq n_{K}\min_{a\in P_{K}}\text{Vol}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a)),

(here by volume, we mean the volume of the geometric object attained after canonically embedding into the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
).
First, we will determine an upper bound for
\text{Vol}(\mathcal{F}_{K}^{1})
. Let
T_{n}
denote the set of elements
x
in
{\mathbb{R}^{+}}^{n}
such that
|x|_{1}\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(\mathcal{F}_{K}^{1})\leq\text{Vol}(K_{>>0}^{1})\leq\text{Vol}(T_{n})=\frac{1}{n!},

(since every element
a\in K_{>>0}
will be mapped to
{\mathbb{R}^{+}}^{n}
under the canonical embedding, by the definition of totally positive elements).

Before finding a lower bound for
\text{Vol}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a))
, we will prove the following lemmas.

Lemma 4
.

For all totally real,
A
-reducible fields
K
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and unit group
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
respectively,

\displaystyle\max_{a\in K_{>>0}}\left(\frac{\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(au^{2})}{\mu(a)}\right)\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{K}^{2}+\theta_{K}}},

where
\eta_{K}
and
\theta_{K}
are defined as in Theorem
1
.

Proof.

When
K
is unit reducible,
\eta_{K}=0
follows by definition. For non-unit reducible fields, suppose that
b\in K_{>>0}
is such that

\displaystyle\max_{a\in K_{>>0}}\left(\frac{\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(au^{2})}{\mu(a)}\right)=\frac{\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(bu^{2})}{\mu(b)}.

Suppose that
x\in\mathcal{M}(b)
, and associate to
K
the embeddings
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}
that send
K
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(bu^{2})}{\mu(b)}=\frac{\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{x^{2}}bx^{2}\right)}{\mu(b)}\leq\frac{\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\max_{i}\sigma_{i}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(bx^{2})}{\mu(b)}

\displaystyle=\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\max_{i}\sigma_{i}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)=\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}\max_{i}e^{|\log(\sigma_{i}(u^{2}/x^{2}))|}\leq\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}e^{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log(|\sigma_{i}(u^{2})|)-\log(|\sigma_{i}(x^{2})|))^{2}}}.

Consider the embedding
\Log:K\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Log(y)=(\log(|\sigma_{1}(y)|),\log(|\sigma_{2}(y)|),\dots,\log(|\sigma_{n}(y)|)
. Then
\Log(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})\triangleq\Lambda_{K}
generates a lattice of rank
n-1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(which is known as the log-unit lattice), such that, for any
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\Lambda_{K}
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=0
. Hence, any element of
\Lambda_{K}
lies in the plane
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=0
.

Given that
K
is
A
-reducible by assumption,
\Log(x^{2})
lies in the plane
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=C
, where
0\leq C\leq 2\log(A)
. Let
m
denote the plane
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=0
that lies closest to
\Log(x^{2})
. Then the maximum possible distance between
m
and
\Log(x^{2})
is
\frac{2\log(A)}{\sqrt{n-1}}
, in terms of the

norm in the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The distance between
m
and any point of
\Lambda_{K}
, in terms of the
l_{2}

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, cannot be greater than the covering radius of
\Lambda_{K}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, which we denote
\mu(\Lambda_{K})
. Then, using Pythagoras’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log(|\sigma_{i}(u^{2})|)-\log(|\sigma_{i}(x^{2})|))^{2}}}\leq e^{\sqrt{\mu(\Lambda_{K})^{2}+\theta_{K}}}.

It remains to find a bound on
\mu(\Lambda_{K})
. In
[
2
]
, it was proven that for any lattice
\Lambda

\mu(\Lambda)\leq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}|\det(\Lambda)|^{\frac{1}{d}}
for

. Since
|\det(\Lambda_{K})|=R_{K}
, this yields the result stated in the lemma for
n\leq 11
.

Finally, we need to consider the case
d>11
. Denote
\lambda_{i}
the
i
th successive minima of
\Lambda_{K}
, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. It is easily shown that

\displaystyle\mu(\Lambda_{K})\leq\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{2}\lambda_{n-1}.

(1)

In
[
3
]
, it was proven that

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\frac{1}{1000}\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{3}.

(2)

By Minkowski’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}\leq\gamma_{n-1}^{n-1}R_{K},

where
\gamma_{n}
denotes Hemite’s constant of rank

[
1
]
, it is known that for any

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}},

so combined with

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\frac{1}{1000}\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{3}\right)^{n-2}\leq\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n-1}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{2}R_{K},

which yields the required result after rearranging the inequality above and using inequality
1
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
a\in K_{>>0}
Then there exists an
a^{\prime}\in K_{>>0}
such that
a\sim a^{\prime}
and for all
x\in\mathcal{M}(a)
,

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x^{2})\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{K}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{n}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{2}{n}},

where
\eta_{K}
,
\theta_{K}
are defined identically as in theorem
1
.

Proof.

By theorem 6 in
[
5
]
, and by definition of the algebraic Hermite’s constant
\gamma_{K}
, for any
a\in K_{>>0}
it holds that

\displaystyle\mu(a)\leq\gamma_{K}\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\gamma_{n}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Given that
a
is totally positive, clearly
a^{-1}
is also totally positive, and so repeating this argument, we get

\displaystyle\mu(a)\mu(a^{-1})\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{n}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{2}{n}}.

Assume without loss of generality that
\mu(a)=1
, so

\displaystyle\mu(a^{-1})\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{n}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{2}{n}}.

We may also assume without loss of generality that
a
is such that

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a^{-1})\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{k}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}\mu(a^{-1}),

as otherwise, by Lemma
4
, there exists an
a^{\prime}
such that this inequality holds and
a\sim a^{\prime}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a^{-1})\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{k}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{n}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{2}{n}}.

Let
A
denote the diagonal
n
-dimensional matrix with entries
\sigma_{1}(a),\sigma_{2}(a),\dots,\sigma_{n}(a)
, and denote
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}

. Then

\displaystyle\min_{i}e_{i}=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\mathbf{y}^{T}A\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}}\leq\frac{\mathbf{x}^{T}A\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}},

\mathbf{x}=(\sigma_{1}(x),\sigma_{2}(x),\dots,\sigma_{n}(x))

. Note that
\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x^{2})=\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}

\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})=\mathbf{x}^{T}A\mathbf{x}
, so

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x^{2})\leq\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})\max_{i}\frac{1}{e_{i}}\leq\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{e_{i}}=\mu(a)\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a^{-1})

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a^{-1})\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{k}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{n}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{2}{n}},

as required.
∎

\mathcal{V}(a)
. Note that we can pick
n

\mathcal{M}(a)
(call this set

-span is equal to

. Say
M_{a}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}

\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x^{2})\leq e^{\sqrt{\eta_{K}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{n}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a))\geq\text{Vol}\left(\text{conv}\left(\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{\sigma(x_{i}^{2})}{\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x_{i}^{2})}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{n!}|\det(U)|,

\displaystyle U=\left\{\frac{\sigma(x_{i}^{2})}{\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x_{i}^{2})}\right\}_{i=1}^{n},

\displaystyle|\det(U)|=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x_{i}^{2})}\cdot|\det(\sigma(x_{i}^{2}))|_{i=1}^{n}\geq\frac{\Delta_{K}}{e^{n\sqrt{\eta_{k}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2n}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{4}|\Delta_{K}|^{2}}

Finally, this yields

\displaystyle n_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\text{Vol}(\mathcal{F}_{K}^{1}}{\min_{a\in P_{K}}\text{Vol}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(a))}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{n!}}{\frac{1}{n!e^{n\sqrt{\eta_{k}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2n}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{4}|\Delta_{K}|}}

\displaystyle=e^{n\sqrt{\eta_{K}^{2}+\theta_{K}}}|\Delta_{K}|\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2n}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{4},

which proves Theorem

Proof of Theorem

The only result we need to prove Theorem
2

Lemma 6
.

Let
K
be a totally real field and suppose that
a\in K_{>>0}
. Then

\displaystyle\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x)\leq n^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{|\Delta_{K}|}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right).

Suppose that

\displaystyle\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x^{2})=\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})\right)^{n}\leq\frac{1}{n^{n}}\left(\gamma_{K}\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq n^{-n}\gamma_{n}^{n}|\Delta_{K}|\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\leq n^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{|\Delta_{K}|}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(2+\frac{n}{2}\right)\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a).

The result follows by dividing through by
\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)
.

Theorem

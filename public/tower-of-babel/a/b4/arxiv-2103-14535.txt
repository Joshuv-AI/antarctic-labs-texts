Global solutions for the Muskat problem in the scaling invariant Besov space B ˙ ∞ , 1 1 \dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1} Thanks: MSC Classification: 35R35, 35Q35, 35A01, 35A02.
Huy Q. Nguyen

Abstract
. The one-phase and two-phase Muskat problems with arbitrary viscosity contrast are studied in all dimensions. They are quasilinear parabolic equations for the graph free boundary. We prove that small data in the scaling invariant homogeneous Besov space B ˙ ∞ , 1 1 \dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1} lead to unique global solutions. The proof exploits a new structure of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator which allows us to implement a robust fixed-point argument. As a consequence of this method, the initial data is only assumed to be in B ˙ ∞ , 1 1 \dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1} and the solution map is Lipschitz continuous in the same topology. For the general Muskat problem, the only known scaling invariant result was obtained in the Wiener algebra (plus an L 2 L^{2} assumption) which is strictly contained in B ˙ ∞ , 1 1 \dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1} .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Muskat problem

The two-phase Muskat problem concerns the dynamics of the free interface between two immiscible fluids permeating a porous medium. The fluids have different constant densities
\rho^{\pm}
and, in general, different viscosities
\mu^{\pm}
. Assume that the free interface is the graph of a time-dependent function
\eta(x,t)
, i.e.

\Sigma_{t}=\{(x,\eta(x,t)):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\},

so that the fluids occupy the regions

\Omega^{+}_{t}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:y>\eta(x,t)\},\quad\Omega^{-}_{t}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:y<\eta(t,x)\}.

The fluid motion is modeled by Darcy’s law:

(1.1)

\mu^{\pm}u^{\pm}+\nabla_{x,y}p^{\pm}=-(0,\rho^{\pm}),\quad\cnx_{x,y}u^{\pm}=0\quad\text{in}~\Omega^{\pm}_{t},

Here
u^{\pm}
are the velocity fields,
p^{\pm}
are the pressure fields, and we have normalized the gravity constant to
1
for notational simplicity. The fluids are assumed to be quiescent at great depths,

(1.2)

\lim_{y\to\pm\infty}u^{\pm}(x,y)=0.

The normal velocity is continuous across the interface, i.e.

(1.3)

u^{+}\cdot n=u^{-}\cdot n\quad\text{on}~\Sigma_{t}\quad\text{on }\Sigma_{t},

where
n=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\eta|^{2}}}(-\nabla\eta,1)
is the upward pointing unit normal to
\Sigma_{t}
. Then the interface moves with the fluids:

(1.4)

\partial_{t}\eta=\sqrt{1+|\nabla\eta|^{2}}u^{-}\cdot n|_{\Sigma_{t}}.

Neglecting the effect of surface tension, the pressure is continuous at the interface, i.e.

(1.5)

p^{+}=p^{-}\quad\text{on}~\Sigma_{t}.

We shall refer to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) as the two-phase Muskat problem. When the top phase corresponds to vacuum, i.e.
\mu^{+}=\rho^{+}=0
, the two-phase Muskat problem reduces to the one-phase Muskat problem, in which case (
1.5
) becomes

(1.6)

p^{-}=0\quad\text{on}~\Sigma_{t}.

1.2.
Main result

A remarkable feature of the Muskat problem is that it can be recast as a
quasilinear
nonlocal parabolic equation for the free boundary
\eta
. When the initial data is sufficiently smooth, local well-posedness for large data and global well-posedness for small data were established in
[
21
,
11
,
22
,
16
,
19
,
5
,
6
,
17
,
18
,
12
]
. It turns out that the problem is invariant with respect to the rescaling

\eta(x,t)\to\lambda^{-1}\eta(\lambda x,\lambda t)\quad\forall\lambda>0.

Therefore, it is natural to develop well-posedness theories in function spaces that respect this scaling invariance. These include, among others, the Lipschitz space
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the Wiener algebra
\mathbb{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p
,
q\in[1,\infty]
. The latter includes the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as a special case.

There have been significant developments on well-posedness theories in or close to scaling invariant spaces. Local well-posedness for large data arbitrarily close to scaling (“scaling
+\varepsilon
”) is quite well understood. The case of no viscosity contrast (
\mu^{+}=\mu^{-}
) was treated in
[
15
]
with
W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R})
data for all
p\in(1,\infty]
, and in
[
32
]
with
H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})
data. The latter was then improved to the homogeneous space
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}+}(\mathbb{R})

[
1
]
, which is natural since the PDE annihilates constants. For
arbitrary viscosity contrast
and in
all dimensions
, local well-posedness was obtained in
[
33
]
for
H^{1+\frac{d}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
data. See also
[
1
]
for the one-phase case. The method developed in
[
33
]
could also handle the effect of surface tension
[
34
]
and the zero surface tension limit
[
23
]
at the same regularity level. The only existing scaling invariant (modulo low-frequency assumptions) existence and uniqueness results are
[
3
,
4
]
for the case of
no viscosity contrast
. They were obtained for data in
H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
and
W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\dot{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, respectively.

On the other hand, global well-posedness for small data is more developed owing to the parabolic nature of the Muskat problem. The first result of this type is
[
14
]
which considers the equal viscosities case with data in the subcritical space
H^{3}(\mathbb{R})
but small (with explicit bound) in the Wiener norm

(1.7)

\|\eta_{0}\|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi||\widehat{f}(\xi)|d\xi.

This Wiener framework was then sucessfully employed to study a variety of problems such as the Navier-Stokes equations
[
31
]
, the Landau and non-cutoff Boltzmann equations
[
25
]
, the Peskin problem
[
30
]
. The results in
[
14
]
were extended to 3D in
[
13
]
. Smallness in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
implies smallness of the slope, i.e. of
\|\eta_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}
. Still for the case of no viscosity contrast, if the initial Lipschitz norm is smaller than an explicit number and the profile grows sublinearly at infinity, then there exists a unique global classical solution
[
8
,
9
]
. Recent developments can address arbitrarily large slopes
[
20
,
29
,
4
]
and even infinite slope
[
3
]
. Precisely,
[
20
,
29
,
4
]
consider Lipschitz data that are small in the critical Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\in\{1,2\}
, whereas the Lipschitz condition is removed in
[
3
]
for
d=1
. As far as the general case of arbitrary viscosity contrast is concerned, to the best of our knowledge, the only scaling invariant result is
[
27
]
for data in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and small in
\mathbb{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\in\{2,3\}
. See also
[
28
]
for the global stability of bubbles. The case of viscosity contrast, including the one-phase problem, is more practical and mathematically challenging since in addition to the evolution equation for the free boundary, one has to invert a nonlocal operator for the vorticity amplitude at the free boundary. Unlike the case of equal viscosities, there is no currently available
L^{\infty}
or Lipschitz maximum principle when the viscosity contrast is nonzero. See
[
27
]
for the equations and further discussions. For the one-phase problem, by taking advantage of the maximum principle for the slope, it is proved in
[
24
]
that for any periodic Lipschitz initial data, there exists a global strong solution which is also the unique viscosity solution.

The purpose of this paper is to provide an alternative scaling invariant framework for the general Muskat problem: the Besov framework through the Dirichlet-Neumann operator. To ensure the finiteness of the slope as in the Wiener framework, we consider the scaling invariant Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}+1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p\in[1,\infty]
. The
largest
space in this class is
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which
strictly contains

\mathbb{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

(1.8)

\|\cdot\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}.

For example, the function
f(x)=\cos(\sqrt{2}x)+\cos x
belongs to
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
but not to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Note that
f
is neither periodic nor decaying at infinity.

We establish global well-posedness for small data in
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
by exploiting a
semilinear-like structure
of the problem for such data. Our starting point is the reformulation
[
33
]
of the general Muskat problem in terms of the Dirichlet-Neumann operators
G^{\pm}(\eta)
associated to the fluid domains
\Omega^{\pm}
. Precisely, for a given function
f
, if
\phi^{\pm}
solve

(1.9)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x,y}\phi^{\pm}=0\quad\text{in}~\Omega^{\pm},\\
\phi^{\pm}=f\quad\text{on}~\Sigma,\\
\nabla_{x,y}\phi\to 0\quad\text{as }y\to\pm\infty,\end{cases}

then

(1.10)

G(\eta)^{\pm}f:=\sqrt{1+|\nabla\eta|^{2}}\frac{\partial\phi^{\pm}}{\partial n}.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
33
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
.

1.
(u,p,\eta)
solve the one-phase Muskat problem if and only if
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
obeys the equation

(1.11)

\partial_{t}\eta+\frac{\rho^{-}}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)\eta=0.

2.
(u^{\pm},p^{\pm},\eta)
solve the two-phase Muskat problem if and only if

(1.12)

\partial_{t}\eta=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-},

where
f^{\pm}:=p^{\pm}|_{\Sigma}+\rho^{\pm}\eta
satisfy

(1.13)

\begin{cases}f^{+}-f^{-}=(\rho^{+}-\rho^{-})\eta,\\
\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)f^{+}-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-}=0.\end{cases}

We denote throughout this paper

\kappa=\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}},\quad\llbracket\rho\rrbracket=\rho^{-}-\rho^{+}.

It will be shown that
\kappa
is the dissipation coefficient.

Our main result is the following global well-posedness in Hadamard’s sense for small data in
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider either the one-phase or the two-phase Muskat problem in the stable case
\rho^{+}<\rho^{-}
. Let
\eta_{0}\in\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\geq 1
, be an initial datum. Then there exist positive constants
\delta
and
C
, both depending only on
d
, such that the following holds: if
\|\eta_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq\delta
then for any
T>0
, (
1.11
) and (
1.12
)-(
1.13
) have a unique solution
\eta\in C([0,T];\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
satisfying

(1.14)

\|\eta\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}([0,T];\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1})}+\kappa\|\eta\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}^{2}_{\infty,1})}\leq C\|\eta_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}.

Here
\widetilde{L}^{q}([0,T];\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{s})
denotes the Chemin-Lerner space. Moreover, if
\eta^{1}
and
\eta^{2}
are two solutions as described above, then for all
T>0
,

(1.15)

\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}([0,T];\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1})}+\kappa\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}^{2}_{\infty,1})}\leq C\|\eta^{1}(0)-\eta^{2}(0)\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}.

Theorem
1.2
does not require any additional assumption on low frequencies of initial data. This is natural since for any constant
c
,
\eta+c
is a solution if
\eta
is a solution. This and the Lipschitz continuity of the solution map in
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
follow as a direct consequence of the fact that our solutions are constructed by means of a
fixed-point argument
. Since we do not rely on explicit contour equations
[
19
,
27
]
, the proof of Theorem
1.2
works regardless of the dimension and
extends to the periodic setting
. We stress that the smallness
\delta
is independent of all parameters of the problem.

Remark 1.3
.

It would be interesting to investigate the long time decay and instant analyticity of the solutions constructed in Theorem
1.2
. However, in order to elucidate the main ideas, we do not pursue these issues in the current paper.

1.3.
The method of proof

By virtue of Proposition
1.1
, the analysis of the Muskat problem reduces to that of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator. To fix ideas let us consider first the one-phase equation (
1.11
) in which the nonlinearity
G^{-}(\eta)\eta
is fully nonlinear. In
[
33
]
, a precise structure of
G^{-}(\eta)f
is required to handle large data. Precisely, we proved that for subcritical Sobolev regularity,
G^{-}(\eta)f
can be decomposed into a first order elliptic (paradifferential) operator acting on
f
plus a transport (paradifferential) operator acting on
\eta
, and a lower order remainder. On the other hand, for small data, it turns out that the following different but simpler structure suffices

(1.16)

G^{-}(\eta)f=|D_{x}|f+R^{-}(\eta)f,

where
R^{-}(\eta)f
contains only quadratic and higher nonlinearities. The definition of
R^{-}(\eta)f
involves
\eta
and
v:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{-}\to\mathbb{R}
, where the latter is the harmonic potential
\phi
, defined by (
1.9
), in the flattened domain
\{(x,y):y<0\}
. We shall prove that
v
can be found as the unique fixed point in Chemin-Lerner spaces of some operator
\mathcal{T}
depending on
\eta
(see (
3.16
)), provided that
\eta
is small in
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
. Moreover,
R^{-}(\eta)f
satisfies the good Besov bound

(1.17)

\displaystyle\|R^{-}(\eta)f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{\infty,1}}+C\|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{\infty,1}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

Interestingly, at the same
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
regularity,
R^{-}(\eta)f
has the contraction property

(1.18)

\displaystyle\|[R^{-}(\eta_{1})-R^{-}(\eta_{2})]f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{\infty,1}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}+C\|\eta_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{\infty,1}}+C\|\eta_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{\infty,1}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}},\quad\eta_{\delta}=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}.

Now in view of (
1.16
), the one-phase equation (
1.11
) can be written in the Duhamel form

(1.19)

\eta(t)=e^{-\kappa t|D_{x}|}\eta_{0}-\kappa\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)|D_{x}|}(R^{-}(\eta)\eta)(\tau)d\tau.

This is reminiscent of mild solutions for the semilinear Navier-Stokes equations
[
26
,
10
]
but our nonlinearity
R^{-}(\eta)\eta
is implicit and not purely quadratic. Nevertheless, by virtue of the boundedness (
1.17
) and the contraction (
1.18
) of
R^{-}(\eta)f
, a unique fixed point
\eta
of (
1.19
) can be easily obtained in the space
\widetilde{L}^{\infty}([0,T];\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1})\cap\widetilde{L}^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{2})
for any
T>0
, provided that
\|\eta_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}\leq\delta(d)\ll 1
. In other words, for small data, the quasilinear Muskat problem can be
treated as a semilinear equation
. The above fixed point argument allows us to bypass any additional assumption on low frequencies of data (for instance,
\eta_{0}\in L^{2}
in
[
27
]
) and prove at once the Lipschitz continuity of the solution map in the top topology of
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
.

Regarding the two-phase problem, owing to the linearization (
1.16
) we find that
f^{-}
, the solution of (
1.13
), can be again obtained as the unique fixed point of some operator, provided that
\eta
is small in
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
. Moreover,
f^{-}
has the same Besov regularity as
\eta
, so that the two-phase equation (
1.12
) can be analyzed exactly the same as the one-phase equation (
1.11
).

A review of homogeneous Besov spaces is given in Section
2
. Section
3
is devoted to the linearization, boundedness and contraction properties of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator in Besov spaces. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is given in Section
4
. After the preliminaries in Section
2
, the remainder of the paper is self-contained.

2.
A review of homogeneous Besov spaces

2.1.
Homogeneous Besov spaces

Proposition 2.1

(
[
7
, Proposition 2.10]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
. There exists a radial function
\psi
valued in
[0,1]
and belonging to
C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,\frac{4}{3}))
such that the following hold

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\supp\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\cap\supp\varphi(2^{-j^{\prime}}\cdot)=\emptyset\quad\text{for}~|j-j^{\prime}|\geq 2,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\leq 1\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

Definition 2.2
.

The Littlewood-Paley dyadic block
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
is defined by the Fourier multiplier
\dot{\Delta}_{j}u=\varphi(2^{-j}D_{x})u
. The low-frequency cut-off operator
\dot{S}_{j}
is defined by

(2.4)

\dot{S}_{j}=\sum_{j^{\prime}\leq j-1}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}.

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
respectively denote the set of polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the space of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is well-known that

\text{I}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Definition 2.3
.

1) For
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the following norm is finite

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|2^{sj}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}.

2) For
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,
(p,q,r)\in[1,\infty]^{3}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Chemin-Lerner norm is defined by

\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\|2^{sj}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}.

We then define the Chemin-Lerner space
\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to be the space of tempered distributions
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}<\infty
.

Remark 2.4
.

The inequality (
1.8
) holds since for
u\in\mathbb{A}^{1}
we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j}\|\psi_{j}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j}\psi_{j}(\xi)|\widehat{u}(\xi)|d\xi\leq\frac{4}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{j}(\xi)|\xi||\widehat{u}(\xi)|d\xi=\frac{4}{3}\|u\|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}.

For all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
,
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space (see
[
35
]
, page 240). The following proposition can be proved analogously to Theorem 2.25
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,q,r)\in[1,\infty]^{3}
,
\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a Banach space and satisfies the Fatou property: for any sequence
(u_{n})
bounded in
\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, there exist a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})
and
u\in\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that

(2.5)

\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{n_{k}}=u\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;B^{s}_{p,r})}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;B^{s}_{p,r})}.

By Minkowski’s inequality, we have

(2.6)

\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}(I;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}~~\text{if}~~q\leq r,\quad\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}\geq\|u\|_{L^{q}(I;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}~~\text{if}~~q\geq r.

We shall need to following lemma to prove the continuity in time of solutions.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s,~s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
with
s\geq s^{\prime}
. If
u\in\widetilde{L}^{\infty}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\partial_{z}u\in\widetilde{L}^{1}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s^{\prime}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, then
u\in C([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Proof.

We first note that if
v\in\widetilde{L}^{q}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p,r})
then
\dot{S}_{j}v\in\widetilde{L}^{q}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p,r})
for all
s_{2}\geq s_{1}
and all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Applying this we obtain

\dot{S}_{j}u\in\widetilde{L}^{\infty}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1}),\quad\partial_{z}\dot{S}_{j}u=\dot{S}_{j}\partial_{z}u\in\widetilde{L}^{1}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1})=L^{1}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1}),

whence
\dot{S}_{j}u\in C([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1})
. It then suffices to prove that the sequence of
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1}
-valued functions
\dot{S}_{j}u
converges to
u
uniformly on
[0,T]
as
j\to\infty
. Indeed, since
u-\dot{S}_{j}u=\sum_{k\geq j}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u
we have

\|u-\dot{S}_{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1})}\leq\sum_{k\geq j}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{sk}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}\dot{\Delta}_{k}u\|_{L^{\infty}([a,b];L^{p})}\leq C\sum_{k\geq j-3}2^{sk}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}u\|_{L^{\infty}([a,b];L^{p})}

which converges to
0
as
j\to\infty
because it is the tail of the series convergent to
\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}([a,b];\dot{B}^{s}_{p,1})}
.
∎

The following product rules and nonlinear estimates shall be used frequently.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
7
, Corollary 2.54]
)
.

For
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,
(p,r,q)\in[1,\infty]^{3}
and
s>0

3.1.
Linearization and boundedness

We study the Dirichlet-Neumann operators associated to the fluid domains
\Omega^{\pm}
. The time variable is forgotten throughout. In
\Omega^{-}

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x,y}\phi=0\quad\text{in}~\Omega^{-},\\
\phi=f\quad\text{on}~\Sigma,\\
\nabla_{x,y}\phi\to 0\quad\text{as}~y\to-\infty.\end{cases}

The Dirichlet-Neuman operator associated to

is (formally) defined by

G^{-}(\eta)f=\sqrt{1+|\nabla\eta|^{2}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n},

where we recall that

is the upward-pointing unit normal to

\phi
solves the elliptic problem (

) with
\Omega^{-}
replaced by

by the right-hand side of (

Note that

is inward-pointing for
\Omega^{+}

is a nonpositive operator. We shall only state results for
G^{-}(\eta)
since corresponding results for

are completely parallel.

Assume that
\phi
is a smooth solution of (

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times J\ni(x,z)\mapsto(x,\varrho(x,z))\in\Omega^{-}
, where

and

(3.3)

\varrho(x,z)=z+H(x,z),\quad H(x,z)=e^{z|D_{x}|}\eta(x),\quad(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times J.

Clearly,
\varrho(x,0)=\eta(x)
and
\varrho(x,z)\to-\infty
as

. We have
\partial_{z}\varrho(x,z)=1+e^{z|D_{x}|}|D_{x}|\eta(x)
and

\|e^{z|D_{x}|}|D_{x}|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times J)}\leq C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}

where we have used Lemma
2.9
. This implies

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a positive constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(d)\in(0,1)
such that if

\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq c_{0}\quad\text{then}\quad\partial_{z}\varrho\geq\frac{1}{2}

and thus the mapping

is a Lipschitz diffeomorphism.

A direct calculation shows that if

then

(3.5)

\cnx_{x,z}(\mathcal{A}\nabla_{x,z}\widetilde{g})(x,z)=\partial_{z}\varrho(\Delta_{x,y}g)(x,\varrho(x,z))

with

(3.6)

\mathcal{A}=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{z}\varrho\text{I}&-\nabla_{x}\varrho\\
-(\nabla_{x}\varrho)^{T}&\frac{1+|\nabla_{x}\varrho|^{2}}{\partial_{z}\varrho}\end{bmatrix}=\text{I}+\begin{bmatrix}(|D_{x}|H)\text{I}&-\nabla_{x}H\\
-(\nabla_{x}H)^{T}&\frac{|\nabla_{x}H|^{2}-|D_{x}|H}{1+|D_{x}|H}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
\phi
is harmonic in
\Omega^{-}

satisfies

\cnx_{x,z}(\mathcal{A}\nabla_{x,z}v)(x,z)=0\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{d}\times J,

or equivalently,

(3.8)

\Delta_{x,z}v=\partial_{z}Q_{a}[v]+|D_{x}|Q_{b}[v]\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{d}\times J,

where

(3.9)

\displaystyle Q_{a}[v]=\nabla_{x}H\cdot\nabla_{x}v-\frac{|\nabla_{x}H|^{2}-|D_{x}|H}{1+|D_{x}|H}\partial_{z}v,

\displaystyle Q_{b}[v]=\mathcal{R}_{x}\big(\nabla_{x}H\partial_{z}v-|D_{x}|H\nabla_{x}v\big),\quad\mathcal{R}_{x}=|D_{x}|^{-1}\cnx_{x}.

Then the Dirichlet-Neumann operator can be expressed in terms of

as

\displaystyle G(\eta)f

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1+|\nabla_{x}\varrho|^{2}}{\partial_{z}\varrho}\partial_{z}v-\nabla_{x}\varrho\cdot\nabla_{x}v\Big)|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=\Big[\Big(1+\frac{|\nabla_{x}H|^{2}-|D_{x}|H}{1+|D_{x}|H}\Big)\partial_{z}v-\nabla_{x}H\cdot\nabla_{x}v\Big]|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{z}v-Q_{a}[v])|_{z=0}.

By factorizing

we obtain from (

\displaystyle(\partial_{z}+|D_{x}|)w=|D_{x}|(Q_{b}[v]-Q_{a}[v]).

In terms of

Since

and

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

and

be two norm spaces such that

and the following property holds. There exist
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in(0,1)^{2}
and

such that for all

\displaystyle\|K(u)\|_{E_{1}}\leq\alpha_{1}\|u\|_{E_{1}},

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|K(u)\|_{E_{2}}\leq\alpha_{2}\|u\|_{E_{2}}+A\|u\|_{E_{1}}.

Then for any

there exists a unique fixed point

of the mapping

.

Proof.

By the Banach contraction principle, (
3.18
) implies that

has a unique fixed point

. Moreover,
u_{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}
in
E_{1}
, where
u_{n+1}=u_{0}+K(u_{n})
. For
\beta_{1}>(1-\alpha_{1})^{-1}
, using (

) and induction we find that
\|u_{n}\|_{E_{1}}<\beta_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{E_{1}}
for all

. Next, for

we claim that

and if it is true for

then by (
3.19

\displaystyle\|u_{n+1}-u_{0}\|_{E_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{2}\|u_{n}\|_{E_{2}}+A\|u_{n}\|_{E_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{2}(R+\|u_{0}\|_{E_{2}})+A\beta_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{E_{1}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2}(1+\beta_{2})\|u_{0}\|_{E_{2}}+(\alpha_{2}M+A\beta_{1})\|u_{0}\|_{E_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{2}\|u_{0}\|_{E_{2}}+M\|u_{0}\|_{E_{1}}=R.

Thus
(u_{n})
is bounded in

, so that the Fatou property imposed on

We first prove a simple fixed-point lemma tailored to our problem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space, and let
\nu>0

the closed ball of radius
\nu

\nu\mathcal{F}(2\nu)\leq\frac{1}{2}

and the following conditions hold.

•

For all

,
\mathcal{B}(x,\cdot)
is linear and

(4.2)

\|\mathcal{B}(x,y)\|\leq\mathcal{F}(\|x\|)\|x\|\|y\|\quad\forall y\in B_{\nu}.

•

For all

,

(4.3)

\|\mathcal{B}(x_{1},y)-B(x_{2},y)\|\leq\mathcal{F}(\|x_{1}\|+\|x_{2}\|)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\|y\|.

Then, there exists
\delta=\delta(\nu,\mathcal{F})>0
small enough such for all
x_{0}\in E
with norm less than
\delta
,

has a unique fixed point
x_{*}
in
B_{\nu}
with
\|x_{*}\|\leq 2\|x_{0}\|
.

Proof.

For all
x,~y\in B_{\nu}
, combining (
4.2
) and (

) gives

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(x,x)-\mathcal{B}(y,y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{B}(x,x-y)\|+\|\mathcal{B}(x,y)-\mathcal{B}(y,y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}(\|x\|)\|x\|\|x-y\|+\mathcal{F}(\|x\|+\|y\|)\|x-y\|\|y\|.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{\nu}{2})
be such that
2\delta\mathcal{F}(2\delta)<1
. For
\|x_{0}\|<\delta
, we construct the fixed point of
x_{0}+\mathcal{B}(x,x)
by the iterative scheme
x_{n+1}=x_{0}+\mathcal{B}(x_{n},x_{n})
,
n\geq 0
. Using (
4.2
) and induction we find that
\|x_{n}\|<2\|x_{0}\|<\nu
for all
n\geq 0
. Then, (
4.4
) implies

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{B}(x_{n},x_{n})-\mathcal{B}(x_{n-1},x_{n-1})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\mathcal{F}(2\delta)\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|+2\delta\mathcal{F}(4\delta)\|x_{n}-x_{n_{1}}\|.

Choosing
\delta
smaller so that
2\delta\mathcal{F}(2\delta)+2\delta\mathcal{F}(4\delta)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain
\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x_{n}-x_{n_{1}}\|
. Thus the sequence
(x_{n})
converges to
x_{*}\in E
. In particular,
\|x_{*}\|\leq 2\|x_{0}\|<\nu
and it follows from (
4.4
) that
\|\mathcal{B}(x_{n},x_{n})-\mathcal{B}(x_{*},x_{*})\|\to 0
. Therefore, letting

in the iterative scheme yields

. If
x^{\prime}
is another fixed point in
B_{\nu}
, then by (

) and (

),

\|x_{*}-x^{\prime}\|=\|\mathcal{B}(x_{*},x_{*})-\mathcal{B}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime})\|\leq 2\delta\mathcal{F}(2\delta)\|x_{*}-x^{\prime}\|+\nu\mathcal{F}(2\nu)\|x-y\|\leq\frac{3}{4}\|x_{*}-x^{\prime}\|,

provided that
2\delta\mathcal{F}(2\delta)\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Therefore,
x_{*}
is the unique fixed point in
B_{\nu}
.

) we have

\|e^{-\kappa t|D_{x}|}\eta_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\leq C_{1}\|\eta_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}},\quad C_{1}=C_{1}(d).

Applying the parabolic estimate (
2.11
) with
(q_{1},q_{2})=(\infty,1)
and
(q_{1},q_{2})=(1,1)
, we obtain

\|\mathcal{B}(\eta,f)\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\leq C\kappa\|R^{-}(\eta)f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1})}=C\kappa\|R^{-}(\eta)f\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1})}.

Recall from (
3.30
) that

\|R^{-}(\eta)f\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}\leq C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\||D_{x}|f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}+C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}\||D_{x}|f\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}.

It follows that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\eta,f)\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\||D_{x}|f\|_{L^{1}_{t}\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}+C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{L^{1}_{t}\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}\||D_{x}|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\|f\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])},\quad C=C(d),

where we have used (
2.6
) in the second inequality.

Similarly, we have

\|\mathcal{B}(\eta_{1},f)-\mathcal{B}(\eta_{2},f)\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\leq C\kappa\|R^{-}(\eta_{1})f-R^{-}(\eta_{2})f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1})}.

Recall the contraction estimate (
3.49
):

\displaystyle\|R^{-}(\eta_{1})f-R^{-}(\eta_{2})f\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{A}_{1}\||D_{x}|\eta_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\||D_{x}|f\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}+C\||D_{x}|\eta_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\||D_{x}|f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}+C\||D_{x}|\eta_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}\||D_{x}|f\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}},

where
C=C(d)
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\||D_{x}|\eta_{1}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\||D_{x}|\eta_{2}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}}
. We deduce

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\eta_{1},f)-\mathcal{B}(\eta_{2},f)\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\eta_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}+2\Big)\|\eta_{\delta}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\|f\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}.

In view of (
4.8
) and (
4.9
),
\mathcal{B}
satisfies the conditions (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) in Lemma
4.1
with
\mathcal{F}(z)=C(z+2)
. Finally, choosing
c_{*}
smaller if necessary so that
\nu=c_{*}/2
satisfies (
4.1
), we can apply Lemma
4.1
to conclude the proof.
∎

Let
\eta_{0}^{j}\in\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
,
j=1,2
be initial data with norm less than
\delta
given in Lemma
4.2
. Denoting by
\eta^{j}
the corresponding solutions. Then the estimates (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) imply

\displaystyle\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{X^{1}_{k}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-\kappa t|D_{z}|}(\eta^{1}_{0}-\eta^{2}_{0})\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}+\|\mathcal{B}(\eta^{1},\eta^{1})-\mathcal{B}(\eta^{2},\eta^{2})\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta^{1}_{0}-\eta^{2}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}+C\|\eta^{1}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T]}

\displaystyle+C\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\|\eta^{2}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])},

where we have used that
\|\eta^{j}\|_{X^{1}_{\kappa}([0,T])}\leq 2\delta<1
. Therefore, choosing
\delta
smaller if necessary we obtain the stability estimate

(4.10)

\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{X^{1}_{k}([0,T])}\leq C\|\eta^{1}_{0}-\eta^{2}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}}\quad\forall T>0,\quad C=C(d).

This shows that the solution map is Lipschitz continuous in the topology of
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
, thereby establishing global well-posedness in the strong sense of Hadamard.

4.2.
The two-phase problem

Let us recall the Dirichlet-Neumann reformulation of the two-phase Muskat problem:

(4.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-},

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f^{+}-f^{-}=-\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\eta,\\
\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)f^{+}-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-}=0.\end{cases}

Substituting
f^{+}
from the first equation in (
4.12
), the second equation becomes

\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-}=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)f^{-}-\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)\eta.

Then setting

(4.13)

R^{\pm}(\eta)g=G^{\pm}(\eta)g\mp|D_{x}|g,

we obtain after arranging terms

(4.14)

|D_{x}|f^{-}=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\mu^{-}R^{+}(\eta)-\mu^{+}R^{-}(\eta))f^{-}-\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}G^{+}(\eta)\eta.

Plugging this into the linearization
G^{-}(\eta)f^{-}=|D_{x}|f^{-}+R^{-}(\eta)f^{-}
, it follows in view of (
4.11
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(R^{+}(\eta)+R^{-}(\eta))f^{-}-\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|\eta+\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}R^{+}(\eta)\eta.

In the stable case
\rho^{+}<\rho^{-}
,
\kappa=\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}>0
and
\eta
obeys the nonlinear fractional heat equation

(4.15)

\partial_{t}\eta+\kappa|D_{x}|\eta=-\kappa(R^{+}(\eta)+R^{-}(\eta))\frac{f^{-}}{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket}+\kappa R^{+}(\eta)\eta.

In order for (
4.15
) to be a self-contained equation, it remains to determine
f^{-}
from
\eta
. From (
4.14
) we see that
f^{-}
is a fixed point of

(4.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\eta)g=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\mu^{-}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{+}(\eta)-\mu^{+}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{-}(\eta))g-\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|^{-1}G^{+}(\eta)\eta.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
r\geq 0
and let
\eta\in\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}\cap\dot{B}^{r+1}_{\infty,1}
. If
\eta
satisfies (
3.27
) then
\mathcal{K}(\eta)
has a unique fixed point
f^{-}
in
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}\cap\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}
and

(4.17)

\|f^{-}\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}}\leq C(d,r)\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}}.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
3.3
with
E_{1}=\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}
,
E_{2}=\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}
and

u_{0}=-\frac{\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|^{-1}G^{+}(\eta)\eta\in E_{1}\cap E_{2}

since, under condition (
3.27
), (
3.48
) gives

\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}},\quad C=C(d,r).

It then suffices to prove that the mapping

g\mapsto\mathcal{K}_{1}(g):=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\mu^{-}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{+}(\eta)-\mu^{+}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{-}(\eta))g

satisfies (
3.18
) and (
3.19
). Indeed, under condition (
3.27
) with
c_{*}
smaller if necessary, we can apply (
3.30
) to have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{1}(\eta)g\|_{\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\||D_{x}|^{-1}R^{-}(\eta)\|_{\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}}+\||D_{x}|^{-1}R^{+}(\eta)\|_{\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}}+C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}},\quad C=C(d,r).

Thus there exists a unique fixed point
g\in\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}\cap\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}
for
\mathcal{K}
; moreover,

(4.18)

\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}}+C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{1+r}_{\infty,1}}+C\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}.

When
r=0
, combining (
4.18
) and (
3.27
) yields

\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C(d)\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}.

Plugging this into (
4.18
) and using (
3.27
) again we find that for
r\geq 0
,

\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}}\leq C(d,r)\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{\infty,1}}

which completes the proof.
∎

Assume that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
satisfy (
3.27
), and
f^{-}_{j}
is the fixed point of
\mathcal{K}(\eta_{j})
. Set
\eta_{\delta}=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
and
f_{\delta}=f_{1}-f_{2}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\||D_{x}|f_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|R^{+}(\eta_{1})f^{-}_{1}-R^{+}(\eta_{2})f^{-}_{2}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{r}}+\|R^{-}(\eta_{1})f^{-}_{1}-R^{-}(\eta_{2})f^{-}_{2}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{r}}

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa\|G^{+}(\eta_{1})\eta_{1}-R^{+}(\eta_{2})\eta_{2}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{r}}.

Using the boundedness (
3.30
) for
R^{\pm}(\eta_{j})
and the contraction (
3.49
) for
R^{\pm}(\eta_{1})-R^{\pm}(\eta_{2})
, we easily deduce

(4.19)

\||D_{x}|f_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{r}}\leq C\llbracket\rho\rrbracket\big(\mathcal{A}_{r}\||D_{x}|\eta_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{0}}+\||D_{x}|\eta_{\delta}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{r}}\big),

where
\mathcal{A}_{r}
is given by (
3.50
).

With
f^{-}
given in Proposition
4.3
and satisfying the estimates (
4.17
) and (
4.19
), the nonlinearities
R^{\pm}(\eta)f^{-}
and
R^{+}(\eta)\eta
in (
4.15
) obey the same bounds as for
R^{-}(\eta)\eta
in the one-phase equation (
4.5
). Therefore, the proof of Lemma
4.2
applies, leading to the global well-posedness for the the two-phase problem with small data in
\dot{B}_{\infty,1}^{1}
.

Fano fibrations and DK conjecture for relative Grassmann flips
Marco Rampazzo

Abstract
. Given a vector bundle ℰ {\mathcal{E}} on a smooth projective variety B B , the flag bundle ℱ ​ l ​ ( 1 , 2 , ℰ ) {\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}}) admits two projective bundle structures over the Grassmann bundles 𝒢 ​ r ​ ( 1 , ℰ ) {\mathcal{G}}r(1,{\mathcal{E}}) and 𝒢 ​ r ​ ( 2 , ℰ ) {\mathcal{G}}r(2,{\mathcal{E}}) . The data of a general section of a suitably defined line bundle on ℱ ​ l ​ ( 1 , 2 , ℰ ) {\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}}) defines two varieties: a cover X 1 X_{1} of B B , and a fibration X 2 X_{2} on B B with general fiber isomorphic to a smooth Fano variety. We construct a semiorthogonal decomposition of the derived category of X 2 X_{2} which consists of a list of exceptional objects and a subcategory equivalent to the derived category of X 1 X_{1} . As a byproduct, we obtain a new full exceptional collection for the Fano fourfold of degree 12 12 and genus 7 7 . Any birational map of smooth projective varieties which is resolved by blowups with exceptional divisor ℱ ​ l ​ ( 1 , 2 , ℰ ) {\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}}) is an instance of a so-called Grassmann flip: we prove that the DK conjecture of Bondal–Orlov and Kawamata holds for such flips. This generalizes a previous result of Leung and Xie to a relative setting.



1.
Introduction

The role of the derived category of coherent sheaves as an invariant has been object of study for decades: the remarkable properties it exhibits in the context of Fano varieties
[
BO01
]
and K3 surfaces
[
Orl97
]
motivates the attempts to relate it to other geometric data, such as the class in the Grothendieck ring of varieties
[
BC00
,
KS16
]
, the Hodge structure and the isomorphism/birational class
[
OR18
,
BCP18
]
. About the latter, several counterexamples rule out the possibility that this can be true in general, but it is conjectured that the derived category should behave like a birational invariant if we restrict our attention to a specific class of maps, called K-equivalences and K-inequalities. The conjecture, proposed by Bondal–Orlov
[
BO02
]
and Kawamata
[
Kaw02
]
, can be formulated as:
1

1

Note that several authors call “DK-conjecture” the statement about K-equivalences, without considering K-inquealities.

Conjecture 1.1

(DK conjecture)
.

Consider a K-inequality
\mu:{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{X}}_{2}
, i.e. a birational map resolved by two morphisms
g_{i}:{\mathcal{X}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
such that
g_{2}^{*}K_{{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\sim_{\operatorname{lin}}g_{1}^{*}K_{{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}+D
, where
D
is an effective divisor. Then, there is a fully faithful functor
\Phi:D^{b}({\mathcal{X}}_{1})\xhookrightarrow{\,\,\,\,\,}D^{b}({\mathcal{X}}_{2})
. Moreover, assume that
\mu
is a K-equivalence, i.e.
D=0
. Then,
\Phi
is an equivalence of categories.

Generalizing the work of Kanemitsu
[
Kan22
]
on the so-called simple K-equivalences, Leung and Xie
[
LX23
]
introduce the notion of
simple flips
or
simple K-inequalities
, i.e. K-inequalities as in Conjecture
1.1
such that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are blowups of smooth centers with the same exceptional divisor. For all such flips, the exceptional divisor is a family of special Fano varieties with two projective bundle structures which we call
generalized roofs
: these varieties are classified in the homogeneous case
[
FKMR23
,
LX23
]
. Flips associated to generalized homogeneous roofs are called
generalized Grassmann flips
).

Evidence for the DK conjecture, for generalized Grassmann flips (and flops) has been found in
[
BO95
,
Kaw02
,
Nam03
,
Seg16
,
Mor22
,
Ued19
,
Har21
]
for the flop case, and
[
BO95
,
LX19
,
LX23
]
for the flip case. All generalized Grassmannian flips which have been addressed (with
D\neq 0
) are such that the exceptional divisor is itself a generalized roof, i.e. the family is over a single point: in this paper, instead, we focus on the class of simple flips with exceptional divisor isomprphic to a partial flag bundle
{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})
over an arbitrary smooth base
B
, i.e. a locally trivial fibration with fiber isomorphic to
F(1,2,N)
for some
N
. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider a Grassmann flip
\mu:{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{X}}_{2}
such that the exceptional divisor of the flip is isomorphic to a flag bundle
{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})
over a smooth projective base. Then
D^{b}({\mathcal{X}}_{1})\subset D^{b}({\mathcal{X}}_{2})
, i.e.
\mu
satisfies the DK-conjecture.

This is a generalization of the main result of
[
LX19
]
to the relative setting over a smooth base. As in the original work, the proof is based on constructing fully faithful embeddings of
D^{b}({\mathcal{X}}_{1})
and
D^{b}({\mathcal{X}}_{2})
in
D^{b}({\mathcal{X}})
, such that the semiorthogonal complements are generated by full exceptional collections of (pushforwards of) vector bundles on the exceptional divisor, and then proving the existence of an embedding of the semiorthogonal complements via mutations of exceptional objects. This is done with a diagrammatic technique called “chess game”, which allows to easily visualize the mutations, otherwise very cumbersome to write. This kind of approach first appeared in
[
Kuz07
,
Tho18
]
, and then in the proof of the main results of
[
LX19
,
LX23
]
: instead of directly generalizing the proof of
[
LX19
]
, to the relative setting, we propose a modified and simplified version of the chess game. Besides the Grassmann flip, the geometry of
{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})
allows to introduce some interesting pairs of varieties, of which we describe the relation at the level of derived categories, by means of the same kind of chess game. Consider a general hyperplane section
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})
. Such variety has two contractions, with special fibers, respectively, over two smooth varieties
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
. These are subvarieties of Grassmann bundles, and are zero loci of pushforwards of the section defining
{\mathcal{M}}
. Under the mild assumption
2.2
, by using the fact that the derived categories of
X_{i}
admit fully faithful embeddings in
D^{b}({\mathcal{M}})
, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be fibrations over
B
as above. Then there is a fully faithful functor
\Psi:D^{b}(X_{1})\xhookrightarrow{\,\,\,\,\,}D^{b}(X_{2})
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
, which is articulated in Proposition
3.5
and Proposition
3.6
, allows to construct explicit semiorthogonal decompositions for
D^{b}(X_{2})
containing
D^{b}(X_{1})
as an admissible subcategory, and to describe the semiorthogonal complement in terms of pullbacks of
D^{b}(B)
twisted by appropriate objects. If we assume the further conditions
2.3
and
2.4
, the varieties
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
can be described as fibrations over
B
where
X_{1}
is a cover and the general fiber of
X_{2}
is a smooth Fano variety.
We provide an infinite series of examples of such embeddings, focusing on the cases where
B
, the
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
’s and
{\mathcal{X}}
are rational homogeneous varieties of type
A
, and the
X_{i}
’s are cut by general sections of homogeneous vector bundles. In particular, one of such examples provides an alternative full exceptional collection of length
16
for the Fano fourfold of degree
12
and genus
7
.

Structure of the paper

In section

2.
Flag bundles and fibrations

2.1.
Notation

We shall work over the field of complex numbers. Given a general section
s
of a vector bundle
E
on a variety
X
, we call
Z(s)\subset X
the zero locus of
s
. Moreover, for every
x\in X
,
E_{x}
denotes the fiber of
E
over the point
x
. We say a variety
Y
has a projective bundle structure over
X
if there is an isomorphism
Y\simeq{\mathbb{P}}(E)
for some vector bundle
E
on some variety
X
, sometimes the projective bundle will be denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}(E\longrightarrow X)
. We use Grothendieck’s convention for projectivizations: given
\pi:{\mathbb{P}}(E)\longrightarrow X
, there is a line bundle
L
on
{\mathbb{P}}(E)
such that
L|_{\pi^{-1}(x)}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\pi^{-1}(x)}(1)
and such that
\pi_{*}L\simeq E
. We denote by
G(k,V_{n})
the Grassmannian parametrizing
k
-linear spaces in a fixed vector space
V_{n}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. To unburden the notation, we sometimes use the expression
G(k,n)
. Similarly, we call
F(k_{1},\dots,k_{m},V_{n})
the (partial) flag variety parametrizing
m
-tuples
(V_{k_{1}},\dots,V_{k_{m}})
such that
V_{k_{1}}\subset V_{k_{2}}\subset\dots\subset V_{k_{m}}\subset V_{n}
. To denote cohomology of vector bundles, we write a direct sum of vector spaces shifted by the negative of the appropriate degree, e.g.
H^{\bullet}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},{\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(-n-1))\simeq{\mathbb{C}}[0]\oplus{\mathbb{C}}[-n]
. The bounded derived category of coherent sheaves of a smooth projective variety
X
will be indicated as
D^{b}(X)
.

2.2.
The flag bundle

Consider a vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}\longrightarrow B
of rank
2n+\epsilon
over a smooth, projective base
B
, for
n\geq 2
and
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
, where the choice of notation is due to the fact that the proofs of Propositions
3.5
and
3.6
depend on the parity of the rank of
B
. Then, call
{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})
the associated flag bundle over
B
, i.e. the locally trivial fibration with fiber
F(1,2,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})
for every
b\in B
. Similarly, the associated Grassmann bundle of
k
-subspaces in
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
for every
b
will be called
{\mathcal{G}}r(k,{\mathcal{E}})
(in particular, one has
{\mathcal{G}}r(1,{\mathcal{E}})={\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}})
). We have the following commutative diagram, where all maps are locally trivial:

(2.1)

Recall that

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}_{k}\longrightarrow r_{k}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}\longrightarrow 0

Now, consider the line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}:=p_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\vee}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}
. Then, one has
p_{1*}{\mathcal{L}}\simeq{\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2n+\epsilon-2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}

\begin{split}\omega_{{\mathcal{G}}r(k,{\mathcal{E}})}&=\det({\mathcal{U}}_{k}\otimes{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}^{\vee})\\

Moreover, by combining the relative Euler sequence and the relative tangent bundle sequence associated to

\omega_{{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})}={\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\otimes(2n+\epsilon-1)}\otimes\det{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes(2n+\epsilon-3)}.

Remark 2.1
.

The fibration
{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})\longrightarrow B
is an example of a family of
generalized homogeneous roofs
, i.e. rational homogeneous varieties of Picard rank two which admit two projective bundle structures. These objects are generalizations of the homogeneous roofs introduced in
[
Kan22
]
: they have been classified in
[
FKMR23
]
in the context of derived categories of Fano varieites and, independently, in
[
LX23
]
in the context of flips, while a classification for non-homogeneous cases has yet to be found.

2.3.
Two fibrations over
B

Consider now a general section
s\in H^{0}({\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}}),{\mathcal{L}})
and call
{\mathcal{M}}\in{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})
its zero locus. Define
X_{k}:=Z(p_{k*}s)
for
k=1,2
. By adjunction, one finds:

\begin{split}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}&={\mathcal{U}}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\otimes(2n+\epsilon-2)}\otimes\det{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes(2n+\epsilon-3)}\\
\omega_{X_{1}}&=({\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\vee})^{\otimes(2n+\epsilon-3)}\otimes\det({\mathcal{E}})^{\otimes(2n+\epsilon-3)}\\
\omega_{X_{2}}&=(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(3-2n-\epsilon)}\otimes\det({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee})^{\otimes 2}.\end{split}

In general, there is no guarantee that the general section of
{\mathcal{L}}
cuts a smooth variety, or even that
{\mathcal{L}}
has global sections. However,
{\mathcal{L}}
is defined up to pullbacks of line bundles from
B
, and therefore we have some freedom to choose it so that it has global sections (this choice, in light of Equation
2.2
, is a consequence of a choice of a twist of
{\mathcal{E}}
by a line bundle on
B
). By restricting
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, the varieties
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
inherit fibration structures over
B
. To achieve some control on the fibers, we introduce the following conditions on our setup.

Condition 2.2
.

The bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
is basepoint free.

Condition 2.3
.

The restriction map

\rho_{b}:H^{0}({\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}}),{\mathcal{L}})\longrightarrow H^{0}(F(1,2,{\mathcal{E}}_{b}),{\mathcal{O}}(1,1))

is surjective.

Condition 2.4
.

The dimension of
B
is smaller than
\binom{2n+\epsilon+1}{2}-2n-\epsilon
.

In fact, assuming that Conditions
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.4
hold, one has:

Lemma 2.5
.

X_{1}
is a smooth
N:1
cover of
B
, where:

(2.3)

N=\sum_{i=0}^{2n+\epsilon-1}(-3)^{2n+\epsilon-i-1}2^{i}\binom{2n+\epsilon}{i}.

X_{2}
is a smooth fibration over
B
, with general fiber isomorphic to a Fano variety of codimension two and coindex three in
G(2,2n+\epsilon)
.

Proof.

Take
\gamma_{i}:=r_{i}|_{X_{i}}
. Given any
b\in B
, the expected codimension of the fibers
\gamma_{i}^{-1}(b)
, being zero loci of the restrictions
{\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2n+\epsilon-2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}|_{r_{1}^{-1}(b)}\simeq{\mathcal{Q}}_{G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})}^{\vee}(2)
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}|_{r_{2}^{-1}(b)}\simeq{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})}^{\vee}(1)
is clear, along with the coindex of the general fiber of
\gamma_{2}
. In particular, by Condition
2.2
the generality of
s
implies that
{\mathcal{M}}
,
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
are smooth, and by Condition
2.3
we impose that the general fiber of
\gamma_{2}
is smooth of expected dimension, and the general fiber of
\gamma_{1}
is a set of
N
distinct points. To determine
N
, we just need to compute the degree of the top Chern class of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})}^{\vee}(2)
. Twisting the dual tautological sequence of
G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})
one has

(2.4)

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}_{G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})}^{\vee}(2)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{b}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(2)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(3)\longrightarrow 0,

and therefore, if we call
H
the hyperplane class on
G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})
:

\begin{split}c({\mathcal{Q}}_{G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})}^{\vee}(2))&=(1+2H)^{2n+\epsilon}/(1+3H)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{2n+\epsilon}\binom{2n+\epsilon}{i}(2H)^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{2n+\epsilon-1}(-3H)^{j}\end{split}

where the polynomial is truncated at degree
2n+\epsilon-1
because
H^{2n+\epsilon}=0
. The degree of the top Chern class is precisely the coefficient of the term of maximal degree, and it can be easily computed to be exactly
N
. Let us now rule out the existence of positive dimensional fibers for
\gamma_{1}
. Call
{\mathbb{H}}
the space of global sections of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b})}^{\vee}(2)
. First, consider the variety:

{\mathcal{V}}=\{(b,\sigma)\in B\times{\mathbb{H}}:\dim(Z(\sigma_{b}))>0\}.

Then, one has the obvious projections

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces pr_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces pr_{2}}

Hence, by computing the dimension of

, one easily sees that for every

the codimension of the space of sections of

with positive dimensional zero loci is equal to the codimension in

of the subset over which

has positive dimensional fibers. We can read from the sequence

that sections of

are elements of the kernel of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f:H^{0}(G(1,{\mathcal{E}}_{b}),{\mathcal{E}}_{b}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(2))}

are either linearly dependent, or are annihilated by a degree one syzygy. The first case is represented by sections lying in the kernel of a morphism

By comparing the parameter counts

cannot have positive dimensional fibers, but this is exactly Condition

, which we assume to hold.

3.
A semiorthogonal decomposition for

D^{b}({\mathcal{G}}r(2,{\mathcal{E}}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle{\mathcal{B}},\dots,{\mathcal{B}}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(n-2)},{\mathcal{A}}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(n-1)},\dots,{\mathcal{A}}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(2n-1)}\rangle&\epsilon=0\\

where
{\mathcal{A}}=\{{\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee},\dots,\Sym^{n-2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}\}
and
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathcal{A}},\Sym^{n-1}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}\}
.
Summing all up, we will find the following semiorthogonal decomposition for
\epsilon=1
:

D^{b}({\mathcal{M}})=\langle i_{2*}\bar{q}_{2}^{*}D^{b}(X_{2}),q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}},\dots,q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{B}}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}\rangle,

and the following for
\epsilon=0

\begin{split}D^{b}({\mathcal{M}})=\langle&i_{2*}\bar{q}_{2}^{*}D^{b}(X_{2}),q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}},\dots,q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{B}}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(n/2-1)}\otimes{\mathcal{L}},\\
&q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(n/2)}\otimes{\mathcal{L}},\dots,q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}\rangle.\end{split}

Thus, we are ready to compare the semiorthogonal complements of

and

inside

. However, the number of components grows wildly with

[

3.2.
Extending bundles from the fibers

Before introducing our version of the chess game, we need some technical results about mutations of subcategories of
D^{b}({\mathcal{M}})
. We begin by recalling a different description of the flag bundle. Consider a principal
G
-bundle
{\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow B
, where
G=SL(2n+\epsilon)
. Call
P_{1,2}\subset G
the parabolic subgroup given by the elements of this form:

p=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda_{1}&\times&\times\\
0&\lambda_{2}&\times\\
0&0&h\end{array}\right)\in SL(2n+\epsilon)

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
h\in GL(2n+\epsilon)
and the
\times
’s denote submatrices on which we impose no condition. One has
F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)=G/P_{1,2}
. Then, we can construct a locally trivial fibration
{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow B
with fiber
F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
(i.e. our flag bundle) by taking
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{V}}\times^{G}G/P_{1,2}
, where the notation
\times^{G}
denotes the quotient of the product by the equivalence relation
(g.t,v)\simeq(t,g.v)
for all
g\in G
and
(t,v)\in{\mathcal{V}}\times G/P_{1,2}
. Note that the choice
G=SL(2n+\epsilon)
is purely motivated by writing the explicit description of the parabolic subgroup of a flag variety of type
A
: on the other hand, the construction of homogeneous vector bundles, together with their extension to generalized flag bundles, works for any choice of
G/P
.

Let us call
\pi:{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow B
the map induced by the structure map
{\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow B
. Then, for every
b\in B
we have
\pi^{-1}(b)\simeq F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
. Recall that, given a principal
H
-bundle
{\mathcal{W}}
over a variety
X
, there is the following exact functor from the category of
H
-modules to the category of vector bundles over
X
(see
[
Nor82
, Section 2.2]
, or the survey
[
BN06
, Page 8]
):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H-\operatorname{Mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Vect}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{W}}\times^{H}(-)}

which sends the
H
-module
R
to the vector bundle
{\mathcal{W}}\times^{H}R
. In our case,
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{V}}\times^{G}G
is a principal
P_{1,2}
-bundle over
{\mathcal{Z}}
, because
{\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}/P_{1,2}
is a principal
P_{1,2}
-bundle and
{\mathcal{V}}/P_{1,2}\simeq{\mathcal{V}}\times^{G}G/P_{1,2}\simeq{\mathcal{F}}
(see, for instance,
[
Mit06
, Proposition 3.5]
). This allows to construct an exact functor:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1,2}-\operatorname{Mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Vect}({\mathcal{F}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}\times^{G}G\times^{P_{1,2}}(-)}

Moreover, it is well-known that there exists an equivalence of categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1,2}-\operatorname{Mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Vect}^{P}(G/P_{1,2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{V}}_{G,P_{1,2}}}

which sends a
P_{1,2}
-module
H
to the
P_{1,2}
-homogeneous vector bundle
{\mathbb{V}}_{G,P_{1,2}}(H)=G\times^{P_{1,2}}H
. In particular,
{\mathbb{V}}_{G,P_{1,2}}^{-1}
is an exact functor. Summing all up we can construct an exact functor
{\mathfrak{F}}
sending homogeneous vector bundles over
G/P_{1,2}=F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
to vector bundles over
{\mathcal{F}}
:

(3.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Vect}^{P_{1,2}}(G/P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Vect}({\mathcal{F}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1,2}-\operatorname{Mod}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{F}}:={\mathcal{V}}\times^{G}G\times^{P_{1,2}}(-)\circ{\mathbb{V}}_{G,P_{1,2}}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{V}}_{G,P_{1,2}}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}\times^{G}G\times^{P_{1,2}}(-)}

In particular, we have
{\mathcal{O}}(xh_{1}+yh_{2})={\mathfrak{F}}({\mathcal{O}}(x,y)
for all
x,y
. Moreover, since
{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee}
is homogeneous (although not irreducible), it is easy to see that
\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee}(x,y)={\mathfrak{F}}(\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(x,y))
for all
x,y
.

3.3.
The chess game - rules

3.3.1.
Mutations of blocks

Let us fix the following notation, where we omit pullbacks:
{\mathcal{O}}(xh_{1}+yh_{2}):=({\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\vee})^{\otimes x}\otimes(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes y}
. In particular, one has
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}(h_{1}+h_{2})
. Let us also introduce the subcategories:

\begin{split}\mathbf{S}^{m}_{x,y}:=&\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(xh_{1}+yh_{2})\otimes q_{1}^{*}r_{1}D^{b}(B)\\
\mathbf{A}^{m}_{x,y}:=&\langle\mathbf{S}^{0}_{x,y},\mathbf{S}^{1}_{x,y},\dots,\mathbf{S}^{m}_{x,y}\rangle.\end{split}

In this language, we enunciate the following technical lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

For
0\leq r\leq\min(t-2,n-1)
and
r\neq t-1
one has:

{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t,0}}\mathbf{S}^{r}_{-1,1}\simeq\mathbf{S}^{r}_{-1,1}.

Moreover, for
r=t-1
:

{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t,0}}\mathbf{S}^{t-1}_{-1,1}\simeq\mathbf{S}^{t}_{0,0}.

Proof.

Let us start by considering the following adjoint pair of functors:

\begin{split}f:&E\longmapsto\rho^{*}E\otimes\iota^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(th_{1})\\
f^{!}:&R\longmapsto\rho_{*}R{\mathcal{H}}om_{\mathcal{M}}(\iota^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(th_{1}),R)\end{split}

where
f
is a fully faithful embedding of
D^{b}(B)
in
D^{b}({\mathcal{M}})
), the “
!
” symbol denotes the right adjoint,
\iota:{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
is the embedding as a hypersurface and
\rho:=r_{i}\circ p_{i}|_{{\mathcal{M}}}
. Now, any object in
\mathbf{S}^{r}_{-1,1}
has the form
\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E
for some
E\in D^{b}(B)
, and its mutation through
\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t,0}
is defined by the distinguished triangle

(3.6)

\begin{split}ff^{!}(\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)\longrightarrow\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E\longrightarrow\\
\longrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t,0}}(\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})).\end{split}

Then, by adjunction:

(3.7)

\begin{split}f^{!}(\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)\simeq\hskip 150.0pt\\
\simeq\rho_{*}R{\mathcal{H}}om_{\mathcal{M}}(\iota^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(th_{1}),\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)\\
\simeq\rho_{*}\iota^{*}R{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})}({\mathcal{O}}(th_{1}),\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)\\
\simeq r_{i*}p_{i*}\iota_{*}\iota^{*}R{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})}({\mathcal{O}}(th_{1}),\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E).\end{split}

Let us focus our attention on the term
R{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})}({\mathcal{O}}(th_{1}),\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)
. For any
b\in B
one has

\begin{split}(r_{i*}p_{i*}R{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})}({\mathcal{O}}(th_{1}),\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E))_{b}\simeq\\
\simeq H^{\bullet}((r_{i}\circ p_{i})^{-1}(b),\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(1+t)h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E|_{(r_{i}\circ p_{i})^{-1}(b)})\\
\simeq H^{\bullet}(F(1,2,2n+\epsilon),i_{*}i^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1-t,1))\\
\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{M}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{O}}(t,0),\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1,1))\end{split}

where
i
is the embedding of
M
in
F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
. If
r\neq t-1
the latter is zero by Lemma
A.4
, and hence
R{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{F}}l(1,2,{\mathcal{E}})}({\mathcal{O}}(th_{1}),\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)
vanishes identically. Therefore, the third term in the triangle
3.6
is the cone over the zero map, and hence it will be isomorphic to the second one, proving the first part of the claim. Let us now consider the case
r=t-1
. Back to Equation
3.7
, we can further manipulate such expression obtaining:

\begin{split}f^{!}(\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)&\simeq r_{i*}p_{i*}\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(1+t)h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)\\
\end{split}

Let us resolve
\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E
by the Koszul resolution of
{\mathcal{M}}
(up to twists by pullbacks from
B
):

\begin{split}0\longrightarrow\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(2+t)h_{1})\otimes\rho^{*}E\longrightarrow\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(1+t)h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)\longrightarrow\\
\longrightarrow\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(1+t)h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E\longrightarrow 0\end{split}

Here, by
A.4
and the same argument as above, we have for all
b
:

\begin{split}(r_{i*}p_{i*}\Sym^{t-1}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(2+t)h_{1})\otimes\rho^{*}E)_{b}&=0\\
(r_{i*}p_{i*}\Sym^{t-1}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(1+t)h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)_{b}&\simeq{\mathbb{C}}[-1]\end{split}

which tells us that
r_{i*}p_{i*}\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-(1+t)h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)
is a line bundle on
B
shifted by
-1
. Hence, up to twists by pullbacks of line bundles on
B
, one has
ff^{!}(\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2})\otimes\rho^{*}E)\simeq\rho^{*}E\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(th_{1})
, and therefore the triangle
3.6
reduces to a short exact sequence where
{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t,0}}(\iota^{*}\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{2}^{\vee}(-h_{1}+h_{2}))
is the extension. Such sequence is a twist of
A.3
, and hence we conclude the proof of the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
0\leq m\leq r\leq n-2
one has:

\begin{split}{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbf{S}^{m}_{0,0}}\mathbf{S}^{r}_{-1,1}&\simeq\mathbf{S}^{r}_{-1,1}\\
{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathbf{S}^{r}_{-1,1}}\mathbf{S}^{m}_{0,0}&\simeq\mathbf{S}^{m}_{0,0}.\end{split}

Proof.

The argument follows the same exact steps of the proof of Lemma
3.1
. In particular, with the same local analysis, we see that the relevant Ext computations boil down to terms of the form:

\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}(\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee},\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}),

which have no cohomology by Lemma
A.6
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For
r+1\leq t\leq n-2
there is a sequence of mutations realizing the following equality:

(3.8)

\begin{split}\langle\mathbf{S}^{0}_{0,0},\dots,\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t,0},\mathbf{A}^{r-1}_{-1,1}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{A}^{r-1}_{-1,1},\mathbf{A}^{r-1}_{0,0},\mathbf{S}^{0}_{r,0},\dots,\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t,0},\rangle\end{split}

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
we can move each block of the shape
\mathbf{S}^{m}_{-1,1}
immediately to the right of
\mathbf{S}^{0}_{m+1,0}
. Then, we apply Lemma
3.1
again, mutating the former a further step to the left, where it becomes
\mathbf{S}^{m+1}_{0,0}
The last step consists in reordering the blocks so that we obtain the RHS of Equation
3.8
: this can be done thanks to Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

One has the following short exact sequence on

:

(A.1)

Proof.

For

reduces to the simple embedding of tautological bundles. To prove the assertion for higher

acts on a sequence

as follows:

(A.2)

0\longrightarrow\wedge^{k}A\longrightarrow\wedge^{k-1}A\otimes B\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\wedge^{k-l}A\otimes\Sym^{l}B\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\Sym^{k}B\longrightarrow\Sym^{k}C\longrightarrow 0.

The proof follows by applying Equation
A.2
to the embedding of tautological bundles: note that the first bundle has rank one, thus all its higher wedge powers are zero, giving the expected short exact sequence.

Lemma A.4
.

For

\operatorname{Ext}_{M}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{O}}(t,0),\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1,1))\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}{\mathbb{C}}[-1]&r=t-1\\

(1,1)

Proof.

By the Koszul resolution of

, the computation boils down to

\begin{split}\operatorname{Ext}_{F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{O}}(t,0),\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1,1))\\
\operatorname{Ext}_{F(1,2,2n+\epsilon}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{O}}(t,0),\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-2,0))\end{split}

and hence to the cohomology of
\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-t-1,1)
and
\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-t-2,0)
. In light of the iterate resolution of symmetric powers of Corollary
A.3
, we just need to prove that, for
0\leq\alpha\leq r
, the bundle
{\mathcal{O}}(-t-1-r+2\alpha,1+r-\alpha)
has no cohomology except for
{\mathbb{C}}[-1]
for
\alpha=r=t-1
, and
{\mathcal{O}}(-t-2-r+2\alpha,r-\alpha)
has no cohomology at all. We apply the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem: the weight associated to the first bundle, once we add the sum of fundamental weights, can be expressed as:

\omega+\rho=(-t-r+2\alpha,2+r-\alpha,1,\dots,1)

where the number of
1

2n+\epsilon-3
. Observe that the first coordinate is always negative and the second one is always posytive or zero. If it is zero the bundle has no cohomology, otherwise we proceed by applying the Weyl reflection associated to the first simple root, finding:

s_{1}(\omega+\rho)=(t+r-2\alpha,2-t+\alpha,1,\dots,1).

The first coordinate is always positive, while the only way we can make the second coordinate positive as well is to choose
\alpha=r=t-1
, and this leads to a
{\mathbb{C}}[-1]
term in the cohomology. Let us assume now
\alpha\leq r\leq t-2

s_{2}\circ s_{1}(\omega+\rho)=(2+r-\alpha,-2+t-\alpha,3-t+\alpha,1,\dots,1).

Observe that if

, we eventually obtain a weight with all non negative coordinates and a zero coordinate, by simply repeating the step of applying the Weyl reflection which changes sign to the unique negative coordinate. The inequality we want can be rewritten as
t-\alpha\leq 2n+\epsilon-1
. However, by our assumptions we have
t-\alpha\leq t\leq 2n-3+\epsilon
.

Let us now turn our attention to
{\mathcal{O}}(-t-2-r+2\alpha,r-\alpha)
. Its weight is:

\omega+\rho=(-t-1-r+2\alpha,1+r-\alpha,1,\dots,1)

where the number of
1
’s is
2n+\epsilon-3
. Again the first coordinate is always negative and the second one is always positive or zero. In the first case there is no cohomology, otherwise we apply the Weyl reflection associated to the first simple root:

s_{1}(\omega+\rho)=(t+1+r-2\alpha,-t+\alpha,1,\dots,1).

We essentially proceed as above: there is no cohomology if we can prove that our assumptions imply
t-\alpha\leq 2n+\epsilon-3
, which is true because
t-\alpha\leq t\leq 2n-3+\epsilon
. This proves the claim.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

For
t\leq 2n-3+\epsilon
and
0\leq r\leq\min(t-1,n-1)
one has:

\operatorname{Ext}_{M}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{O}}(t,0),\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1,1))\simeq 0.

Proof.

The approach is the same as for the previous lemma, hence we will be brief. By Corollary
A.3
, we need to show that the bundles
{\mathcal{O}}(t+2\alpha,-r-\alpha)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(-1+t+2\alpha,-1-r-\alpha)
on
F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
have no cohomology for
0\leq\alpha\leq r
. If we add
\rho
to the weight of the first one, we have

\omega+\rho=(1+t+2\alpha,1-r-\alpha,1,\dots,1)

where the first coordinate is positive and the second is negative. By applying the second Weyl reflection (i.e. the one associated to the second simple root) we get

s_{2}(\omega+\rho)=(2-r+t+\alpha,-1+r+\alpha,2-r-\alpha,1,\dots,1).

where the number of
1
’s is
2n+\epsilon-4
. As above, we see that there cannot be cohomology if
-2+r+\alpha\leq 2n+\epsilon-4
, i.e.
r+\alpha\leq 2n+\epsilon-2
. But by assumption
r+\alpha\leq 2r\leq 2n-2
.

Consider now the second bundle: if we dd
\rho
to its weight we have

\omega+\rho=(t+2\alpha,-r-\alpha,1,\dots,1)

and as above:

s_{2}(\omega+\rho)=(t-r+\alpha,r+\alpha,1-r-\alpha,1,\dots,1)

where again the number of
1
’s is
2n+\epsilon-4
. Hence we need to prove that
-1+r+\alpha\leq 2n+\epsilon-3
, which holds by our assumptions.

Lemma A.6
.

For
0\leq m\leq r\leq n-2
the following vanishing holds:

\operatorname{Ext}_{M}^{\bullet}(\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee},\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee})=0.

Proof.

As usual, we start with:

\begin{split}\operatorname{Ext}_{M}^{\bullet}(\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee},\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1,1))=\\
=H^{\bullet}(M,\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}\otimes\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1,1-m))\end{split}

The bundle in the RHS is resolved by the following bundles on
F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
:

\begin{split}\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}\otimes\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-1,1-m)\\
\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}\otimes\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(-2,-m)\end{split}

The first has no cohomology because its pushforward to
G(2,2n+\epsilon)
, by projection formula, can be seen as a product of symmetric powers of
{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}
times the pushforward of
{\mathcal{O}}(-1,0)
, and the latter is identically zero. Let us now turn to the second bundle. By Serre duality, up to shifting by the dimension of
F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)
, computing its cohomology is equivalent to compute the cohomology of:

\begin{split}\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}\otimes\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}(2,m)\otimes\omega_{F(1,2,2n+\epsilon)}\\
=q^{*}{\mathcal{H}}om_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}(\Sym^{m}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee}(2n+\epsilon-1),\Sym^{r}{\mathcal{U}}_{G(2,2n+\epsilon)}^{\vee})\end{split}

Hence, if the latter has no cohomology, we are done, and this can be easily checked by considering the semiorthogonality conditions of the full exceptional collection for
G(2,2n+\epsilon)
of
[
Kuz08
]
.

Stability Results for Bounded Stationary Solutions of Reaction-Diffusion-ODE Systems Journal: ArXiv
Chris Kowall, Anna Marciniak-Czochra, Finn Münnich

Abstract
Reaction-diffusion equations coupled to ordinary differential equations (ODEs) may exhibit spatially low-regular stationary solutions. This work provides a comprehensive theory of asymptotic stability of bounded, discontinuous or continuous, stationary solutions of reaction-diffusion-ODE systems. We characterize the spectrum of the linearized operator and relate its spectral properties to the corresponding semigroup properties. Considering the function spaces L ∞ ​ ( Ω ) m + k , L ∞ ​ ( Ω ) m × C ​ ( Ω ¯ ) k L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k} and C ​ ( Ω ¯ ) m + k C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k} , we establish a sign condition on the spectral bound of the linearized operator, which implies nonlinear stability or instability of the stationary pattern.

Introduction

Reaction-diffusion-type equations form a class of pattern formation models arising from ecological and biological applications. Interaction of diffusive and non-diffusive processes leads to reaction-diffusion equations coupled with ordinary differential equations (reaction-diffusion-ODE models)
[
22
,
25
,
30
,
42
,
49
]
. The lack of diffusion may cause emergence of bounded model solutions with low spatial regularity and, in particular, patterns with jump-discontinuities in space
[
20
,
22
,
26
]
. Analysis of the pattern formation phenomena usually focuses on existence and asymptotic stability of spatially heterogeneous stationary solutions. While stability of linearized systems is well-established in the case of reaction-diffusion-ODE systems, the passage to nonlinear stability is accomplished only for specific models or under restriction of the regularity of the stationary solution
[
10
,
26
,
32
,
42
,
46
]
. To close this gap, this paper concerns nonlinear stability and nonlinear instability of bounded stationary solutions of reaction-diffusion-ODE systems, accounting for both, discontinuous and continuous solutions. Among others, the established general approach allows applications to systems with spatially heterogeneous parameters arising from modeling of processes in heterogeneous habitats
[
9
,
22
,
42
]
.

Mathematical challenges

Linearization at a stationary solution of a reaction-diffusion-ODE system leads us to an unbounded linear (degenerated differential) operator and a nonlinear remainder on an infinite-dimensional function space. Usually, the linear operator is well-studied on a function space such as
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k},1<p<\infty,
which is less regular than the domain of the nonlinear part, where the latter may even neither be well-defined nor continuous in the less regular space
[
15
,
23
]
. In such infinite-dimensional cases, linear stability does not necessarily reflect the properties of the nonlinear model. There exist examples of linear exponentially stable stationary solutions that become nonlinearly unstable
[
53
]
, and conversely, of linear exponentially unstable stationary solutions stabilized by the nonlinearity
[
36
]
.

In case of bounded stationary solutions of a reaction-diffusion-ODE system, there exists an instability result on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
using a spectral gap of the linear operator studied on
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, see
[
32
]
based on results of
[
15
]
. Since in the case of reaction-diffusion-ODE systems the spectrum of the linear operator might not solely consist of discrete eigenvalues, such a spectral gap condition is not always fulfilled. Moreover, classical nonlinear stability results as in
[
38
,
47
]
only apply in the case where the linear and the nonlinear operator is studied on the same function space. Consequently, in the case of a continuous steady state, the choice of the space
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
is expedient for stability and instability results.
In the case of jump-discontinuous stationary solutions, analysis of the problem takes place on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
.
While the nonlinearity is Fréchet differentiable on that space, the study of the linear operator is more delicate. The linear operator does not generate a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, however, this semigroup still features analytic properties for
t>0
. A priori, it is not clear how to link the spectral properties of the linear operator to the growth properties of the corresponding semigroup. As shown in this paper, the growth of the semigroup can still be controlled by the spectral bound of the linear operator.
We adapt the works
[
38
,
47
]
, using continuity of solutions to the nonlinear problem for
t>0
, to finally accomplish the transfer from linear to nonlinear stability and instability.

State of the art

Spectral analysis of a linear reaction-diffusion-ODE operator is presented in
[
10
,
32
]
in the case of a system of ODEs coupled to one reaction-diffusion equation. It is well-known that the spectrum consists of a (possibly non-discrete) part determined by a multiplication operator corresponding to the ODE subsystem and a remainder of eigenvalues of the full linear operator. In the presence of a spectral gap and a positive spectral bound of the linearized operator,
[
32
]
derives nonlinear instability of discontinuous, bounded steady states. Instability of all sufficiently regular non-constant stationary solutions of certain reaction-diffusion-ODE systems is shown in
[
10
,
17
,
26
,
32
,
44
]
, caused by a positive spectral bound of a linearized operator. This instability result seems to be an effect of the coupling of the ODEs to a single reaction-diffusion equation in a homogeneous environment. It is well-known that a scalar reaction-diffusion equation on a convex domain features a similar instability result for homogeneous environments, see references in
[
10
]
. However, in heterogeneous environments, there exist continuous non-constant stationary solutions of reaction-diffusion-ODE systems which are stable
[
22
,
42
]
. In accordance with classical reaction-diffusion systems, a similar stabilizing effect is possible when ODEs are coupled to a system of reaction-diffusion equations.

Asymptotic stability of constant stationary solutions is considered in
[
46
,
51
]
, based on a linearization, and in
[
17
,
21
,
45
]
, based on the knowledge of a specific Lyapunov function. Asymptotic stability is also studied in
[
22
]
for constant and non-constant stationary solutions of a reaction-diffusion-ODE model with heterogeneous model parameters. Furthermore, concerning exponential stability, explicit error estimates are obtained for certain reaction-diffusion-ODE models in
[
20
,
26
,
42
]
. The most recent nonlinear stability result for a jump-discontinuous bounded steady state of a reaction-diffusion-ODE system using linearization has been established using explicit estimates on the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
for
k=1
, under an additional stability assumption on the scalar diffusive model component,
[
11
]
.

Problem formulation

The aim of this paper is a comprehensive nonlinear stability theory for bounded, continuous and jump-discontinuous, solutions of coupled reaction-diffusion-ODE systems with spatially heterogeneous parameters,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}&=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\qquad\mathbf{u}(\cdot,0)=\textbf{u}^{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}-\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta\mathbf{v}&=\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\qquad\mathbf{v}(\cdot,0)=\textbf{v}^{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}&=\mathbf{0}\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{split}

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{u}:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mathbf{v}:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{k},m,k\in\mathbb{N},
are vector-valued functions on the domain
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
endowed with zero Neumann boundary conditions for each diffusive component. The vector
\mathbf{n}
denotes the outward unit normal on
\partial\Omega
. We consider a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., an open and connected set, with a Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega\in C^{0,1}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The Laplace operator
\Delta
is defined componentwise and is multiplied by a diagonal matrix
\mathbf{D}^{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{k\times k}
with positive entries. The model nonlinearities

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{R}^{m+k}\times\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{g}:\mathbb{R}^{m+k}\times\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}

are given functions which may depend on the unknown solution and the space variable, further details are given in Assumption
2
below. Recall that solutions to reaction-diffusion-ODE system (
1.1
) may feature low regularity in space, even in homogeneous environments
[
19
,
31
]
. Hence, mild solutions are considered in this work, similar to
[
37
]
. Accordingly, we assume the following local Lipschitz condition

Assumption 1

(Local Lipschitz condition)
.

Let the function
\mathbf{h}=(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\cdot)
be measurable in
x\in{\overline{\Omega}}
for every fixed
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+k}
. Moreover, for every bounded set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+k}\times\overline{\Omega}
, let there exist a constant
L(B)>0
such that for all
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x),(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z},x)\in B
there holds

\displaystyle|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)|

\displaystyle\leq L(B),

\displaystyle|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z},x)|

\displaystyle\leq L(B)\left(|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{y}|+|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{z}|\right).

If Assumption
1
is satisfied by
(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
, existence and uniqueness of a mild solution is guaranteed by
[
37
, Part II, Theorem 1]
. In view of the linearization procedure, we make the following stronger regularity assumption

Assumption 2

(Nonlinearity)
.

Let the nonlinearities
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
of system (
1.1
) be once continuously differentiable with respect to the unknown variables
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
. Furthermore, we assume that the local Lipschitz condition (Assumption
1
) holds for
(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
and its derivatives
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}),\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
.

Assumption
2
is, for instance, satisfied by functions
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
which are twice continuously differentiable with respect to the unknown variables
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
and which do not depend explicitly on the space variable
x
.

In the case of a continuous steady state of system (
1.1
), we assume the following analog of Assumption
2
.

Assumption 3

(Continuous case)
.

Let Assumption
2
hold, based on the local Lipschitz condition (Assumption
1
) in which we replace the term
measurable in
x\in\overline{\Omega}
by the term
continuous in
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Main objectives of the work

In this work, we consider a bounded stationary solution
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
of system (
1.1
), i.e., a bounded solution of the problem

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{0}&=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
-\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta\mathbf{v}&=\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\qquad\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}=\mathbf{0}\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{split}

(1.2)

Note that elliptic regularity from
[
33
]
implies that the component
\overline{\mathbf{v}}
is at least Hölder continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
. We compare solutions of system (
1.1
) with a solution of system (
1.2
) with respect to the uniform topology induced by
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
or
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
and, in the case of a continuous stationary solution
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
, additionally with respect to the norm on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
. This work provides a unified approach to study nonlinear stability and instability of bounded steady states of system (
1.1
) using linearization.

While the spectrum is purely discrete in the case of classical reaction-diffusion systems, we may lose the spectral gap in the case of a reaction-diffusion-ODE system. Hence, results from
[
32
]
are not applicable to discontinuous stationary solutions in the absence of a spectral gap. Motivated by this observation, we show nonlinear instability in the case of a positive spectral bound of the linearized operator but regardless of a spectral gap condition. This result is achieved for systems of reaction-diffusion equations coupled to a system of ODEs, including discontinuous and continuous patterns.

Since the patterns emerging in reaction-diffusion-ODE models may be both, spatially jump-discontinuous and continuous stationary solutions,
[
20
,
22
,
26
,
42
]
, the second objective of the current study is to establish a rigorous nonlinear stability result that can be applied to the different classes of patterns. More precisely, we show in different functional settings that a negative spectral bound of the linearized operator implies a local exponential stability result.
The robust notion of stability allows considering discontinuous and continuous perturbations of the steady state. Hence, the results are applicable to study stability of continuous patterns of reaction-diffusion-type systems such as
[
2
,
22
,
25
,
30
,
40
]
, including classical reaction-diffusion systems, and jump-discontinuous patterns of reaction-diffusion-ODE systems
[
20
,
22
,
26
,
42
,
49
]
. We indicate possible generalizations of the results in Remark
3.9
.

Linearization

Existing linear stability and instability principles, e.g.,
[
12
, Ch. VII, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
23
, Theorem 5.1.1]
,
[
47
, Proposition 4.17]
, and
[
38
, Theorem 1]
, strongly depend on the choice of the underlying function spaces, e.g.,
L^{p}(\Omega)
or
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
as in
[
32
]
or, in more abstract words, the domain of the fractional operator
(aI-\mathbf{D}\Delta)^{\alpha}
for some
a,\alpha>0

[
23
, Definition 1.5.4]
.

By
[
19
,
31
]
, the ODE component of a stationary solution of problem (
1.1
) may exhibit jump-discontinuities in space. Thus, we consider a steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. We consider a linearization of the reaction-diffusion-ODE system at a steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial t}=\mathbf{L}\xi+\mathbf{N}(\xi),\qquad\xi(0)=\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}

(2.1)

for
\xi=(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})-(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})
. Nonlinear stability of the stationary solution depends on the properties of the linear part
\mathbf{L}
which induces a semigroup and of the nonlinear part
\mathbf{N}
. For a general discontinuous steady state, the nonlinear operator is not well-defined as an operator from
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
to itself. Hence, admitting general bounded steady states, we consider the nonlinear remainder
\mathbf{N}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. It is well-defined and satisfies a certain estimate for small arguments, see Lemma
2.2
. Accordingly, to deduce nonlinear stability from the linearization, we are interested in growth estimates of the corresponding semigroup restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. Since the semigroup is neither analytic nor strongly continuous on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, validity of the spectral mapping theorem is a priori not guaranteed. Nevertheless, we will show in Proposition
2.14
that the growth bound of this semigroup equals the spectral bound of its generator. As the growth of the semigroup determines linear and nonlinear stability, we additionally characterize the spectrum of the linearized operator in Proposition
2.3
.

Whereas
[
10
]
uses a linearization in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)
, we depart from this approach and start from a linearization in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
in the case of a discontinuous steady state. The generated semigroups are both analytic, however, the latter space allows considering a restriction of the generators and its semigroups to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
on which the spectrum remains the same. While the semigroup restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
is not strongly continuous, a further restriction leads to an analytic semigroup on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. The fact that the semigroup operators restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
are the limit of regularized semigroup operators acting on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
allows deriving semigroup estimates on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. These results enable us studying nonlinear stability of bounded, discontinuous steady states in Section
3
from results obtained for its linearization. In the case of a continuous steady state, the spectral analysis on the function space
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
cannot easily be deduced from a restriction of the linear operator considered on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
. The analysis is performed similar to the case
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k}
studied in
[
27
, Proposition 5.13]
, however, on the function space
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
. Details are deferred to Proposition
2.10
.

The linearization of system (
1.1
) at a bounded steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
is induced by the operator

\displaystyle(\mathbf{L}\xi)(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}\Delta\xi(x)+\mathbf{J}(x)\xi(x),\quad x\in\Omega.

(2.2)

Here, we use the Jacobian of
(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{f}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}(x),\overline{\mathbf{v}}(x),x)&\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{f}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}(x),\overline{\mathbf{v}}(x),x)\\
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{g}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}(x),\overline{\mathbf{v}}(x),x)&\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{g}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}(x),\overline{\mathbf{v}}(x),x)\end{pmatrix}=:\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{\ast}(x)&\mathbf{B}_{\ast}(x)\\
\mathbf{C}_{\ast}(x)&\mathbf{D}_{\ast}(x)\end{pmatrix},

(2.3)

evaluated at the steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})
. Moreover, the diagonal diffusion matrix
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{D}^{v})
consists of
m
zeroes and the diagonal matrix
\mathbf{D}^{v}
. Boundedness of the steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})
results in a multiplication operator
\mathbf{J}
induced by the Jacobian in formula (
2.3
). In the case of
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, the linearized operator
\mathbf{L}
is a bounded perturbation of the sectorial operator
\mathbf{D}\Delta
on
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, hence, generates an analytic semigroup. However, to solve nonlinear problems, we need boundedness of the ODE component. For this reason, we consider the product space

Z_{p}:=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}.

On the space
Z_{p}
, unfortunately, the multiplication operator induced by the Jacobian
\mathbf{J}
is unbounded. Hence, it is not trivial that the linearized operator
\mathbf{L}
still generates a strongly continuous semigroup on
Z_{p}
for a certain domain induced by the degenerated differential operator
\mathbf{D}\Delta
. In order to define the domain of the unbounded operator
\mathbf{D}\Delta
on the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
, we use the fact that
\mathbf{D}\Delta
is the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
. Indeed, using the heat semigroup

from Proposition

, we can define the semigroup
\mathbf{S}(t)=(\mathbf{S}^{u}(t),\mathbf{S}^{v}(t))
by its components

for

, provided

The semigroup

is time-independent and analytic on

for

. Clearly, its generator

\mathbf{D}\Delta:=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta):L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{p})^{k}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let the linear operator
\mathbf{L}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})\subset Z_{p}=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\to Z_{p}
be defined by

\displaystyle(\mathbf{L}\xi)(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta\xi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{1}(x)+\mathbf{B}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{2}(x)\\
\mathbf{C}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{1}(x)+\mathbf{D}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix},\quad x\in\Omega,

(2.5)

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}):=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{p})^{k}
(see Proposition

\mathcal{D}(A_{p})

\mathbf{D}^{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{k\times k}
is diagonal and
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}

. Let

Proof.

In order to show that the operator
\mathbf{L}
generates an analytic semigroup on
Z_{p}
, we apply perturbation theory

14
, Ch. III, Theorem 2.10]
. Although
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
are assumed to be bounded matrices which compose the Jacobian
\mathbf{J}
, the operator
\mathbf{D}\Delta
is perturbed by a an unbounded multiplication operator
\mathbf{J}
on
Z_{p}
. Nevertheless, we will show that
\mathbf{J}
is bounded with respect to the operator
\mathbf{D}\Delta
in the sense of
[

Z_{p}
by

The Jacobian
\mathbf{J}
consists of the bounded multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
on

, while the multiplication operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\ast}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{B}_{\ast})\subset L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}

(2.6)

is unbounded, densely defined and closed for the domain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{B}_{\ast})=\{\xi_{2}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\mid\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\xi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\}.

Indeed, the subset
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)^{k}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{B}_{\ast})
is dense in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
. To show closedness, take a sequence
(\psi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{B}_{\ast})
which converges in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
to some
\psi\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
such that
\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\psi_{j}\to\Psi
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
for some
\Psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
. Due to boundedness of the multiplication operator in

[

, we have

\|\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\psi-\Psi\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbf{B}_{\ast}(\psi-\psi_{j})\|_{p}+\|\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\psi_{j}-\Psi\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\|_{\infty}\|\psi-\psi_{j}\|_{p}+\|\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\psi_{j}-\Psi\|_{p}.

Since the right-hand side converges to

as

, we obtain
\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\psi=\Psi
a.e. in
\Omega
. Thus,
\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\psi=\Psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
,
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{B}_{\ast})
and
(\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{B}_{\ast}))
is closed. The remaining multiplication operators

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\ast}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\to L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{D}_{\ast}:L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\to L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}

are bounded by the continuous embedding

and

To show

-boundedness of the operator

in the sense of

, we use the continuous embedding
\mathcal{D}(A_{p})\hookrightarrow C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
from Lemma

for

from

. Ehrling’s compactness lemma in

implies that for each

there exists a constant

such that

\|\xi_{2}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}}\leq\varepsilon\|\xi_{2}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})^{k}}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\xi_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}}\qquad\forall\;\xi_{2}\in C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})^{k}.

Since

is also a bounded multiplication operator on

by

, the embedding

\|\mathbf{J}\xi\|_{p}\leq C\|\mathbf{J}\xi\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\mathbf{J}\|_{\infty}\|\xi\|_{\infty}.

Herein, we denoted by

and

This inequality yields that

\mathbf{D}\Delta

[

generates an analytic semigroup on

Lemma 2.2
.

Let Assumption
2
hold. For a steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
of system (
1.1
), define the nonlinear operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}

\displaystyle\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},\quad\mathbf{N}(\xi)(x)=\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)-\mathbf{h}(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}},x)-\nabla\mathbf{h}(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}},x)\xi

for
\xi=(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})-(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}}),x\in\Omega
. Then, the operator
\mathbf{N}
is locally Lipschitz continuous and satisfies
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
. Moreover, there exist constants
\rho,C>0
such that the superlinear decay estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{N}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}\qquad\forall\;\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},\|\xi\|_{\infty}<\rho

(2.8)

holds as
\xi\to 0
.

Proof.

Using Taylor’s expansion, we write for
\mathbf{h}=(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
and
\xi=(\mathbf{u}-\overline{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v}-\overline{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},x)-\mathbf{h}(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}},x)

\displaystyle=\nabla\mathbf{h}(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}},x)\xi+\mathbf{H}(\xi,x),

where the remainder
\mathbf{H}=(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G})
is given due to the mean value theorem by

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(\xi,x)=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\mathbf{h}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}+t\xi_{1},\overline{\mathbf{v}}+t\xi_{2},x)-\nabla\mathbf{h}(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}},x)\;\mathrm{d}t\cdot\xi.

(2.9)

The nonlinear operator
\mathbf{N}
in equation (
2.1
) then reads

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{f}(\overline{u}+\xi_{1},\overline{v}+\xi_{2},\cdot)-\mathbf{f}(\overline{u},\overline{v}.\cdot)\\
\mathbf{g}(\overline{u}+\xi_{1},\overline{v}+\xi_{2},\cdot)-\mathbf{g}(\overline{u},\overline{v},\cdot)\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{\ast}(\cdot)\xi_{1}+\mathbf{B}_{\ast}(\cdot)\xi_{2}\\
\mathbf{C}_{\ast}(\cdot)\xi_{1}+\mathbf{D}_{\ast}(\cdot)\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{H}(\xi,\cdot)

for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. To prove estimate (
2.8
) for some
\rho>0
and all
\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
with
\|\xi\|_{\infty}<\rho
, we use the local Lipschitz continuity of the gradients of
\mathbf{h}
from Assumption
1
. This assumption allows to estimate the integral in (
2.9
) and we obtain some constant
C=C(\rho)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{N}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}\qquad\forall\;\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},\|\xi\|_{\infty}<\rho.

Note that this constant
C
also depends on the bounded steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})
. Additionally, local Lipschitz continuity transfers from
\mathbf{h}
to
\mathbf{N}
.
∎

Spectral properties

In this section, we first characterize the spectrum of the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
defined in Lemma
2.1
. Unfortunately, the proof in
[
32
]
seems not adaptable to systems with more than one reaction-diffusion equation. The current proof is adapted from
[
28
, Proposition B.2]
, using the close relation of the reaction-diffusion-ODE system (
1.1
) to its shadow limit approximation. Note that the methods used to analyze the spectrum of the linearized operator on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
are also applicable to different function spaces such as
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k}
in
[
27
]
or
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
in Proposition
2.10
. In addition to the spectral characterization, we study restrictions of the operator
\mathbf{L}
to the function spaces
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Due to elliptic regularity theory and
p
-independence of the spectrum of multiplication operators, the spectrum of the operator
\mathbf{L}
equals on all these function spaces for all
p\geq 2
big enough. The basic idea of restricting the operator
\mathbf{L}
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
is the possibility to determine the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
by means of the spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L})
of the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
. This issue is addressed in Section
2.2
which is devoted to semigroup properties.

Recall formula (
2.5
) with bounded coefficients
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
. In order to determine spectral elements
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{L})
, we study the problem

(\lambda I-\mathbf{L})\xi=\psi\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}\qquad\qquad\,(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\xi_{1}-\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\xi_{2}=\psi_{1},\\
-\mathbf{C}_{\ast}\xi_{1}+(\lambda I-\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta-\mathbf{D}_{\ast})\xi_{2}=\psi_{2}\end{cases}

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Let us denote by
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
the multiplication operator induced by
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}

[
28
, Appendix B.2]
. The following result is inspired by the case considered in
[
10
, Section 4]
concerning a system of
m+1
equations. Recall the following characterization of
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
; there exists a null set
N\subset\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\overline{\bigcup_{x\in\Omega\setminus N}\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast}(x))}

and the whole spectrum is essential in the sense of Schechter, i.e., for every
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
the operator
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
is not a Fredholm operator of index zero
[
28
, Appendix B.2]
. For definition and properties of essential spectra and the discrete spectrum
\sigma_{d}
, we refer to
[
24
, Section 7.1]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
be matrix-valued functions which have entries in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
according to the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
defined by (
2.5
) on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
for some
n^{\ast}<p<\infty
. Then, using the same notation for the multiplication operator induced by
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
, we have
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\cap\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})=\emptyset
and

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{L})=\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\mathop{\dot{\cup}}\Sigma

for some set of eigenvalues
\Sigma\subset\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})
. If
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is a connected set in
\mathbb{C}
, then
\Sigma
coincides with the discrete spectrum
\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
of
\mathbf{L}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the properties of essential spectra established in the book of Jeribi
[
24
]
. We use the notion of essential spectra from there.
We work here with 3 concepts of the essential spectrum, i.e. the Wolf spectrum defined by

\displaystyle\sigma_{e4}(\mathbf{L})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lambda I-\mathbf{L}\text{ is not a Fredholm operator}\},

the Schechter spectrum which can be characterized as follows

\displaystyle\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lambda I-\mathbf{L}\text{ is not a Fredholm operator with index }0\}

(see
[
24
, Proposition 7.1.1]
) and lastly the Browder spectrum (see
[
24
, Section 7.1]
for a definition) that provides the whole set

\displaystyle\sigma_{e6}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma(\mathbf{L})\setminus\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L}).

Here,
\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
denotes the discrete spectrum

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lambda\text{ is an isolated eigenvalues of }\mathbf{L}\text{ with a finite algebraic multiplicity}\}.

It holds
\sigma_{e4}(\mathbf{L})\subset\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})\subset\sigma_{e6}(\mathbf{L})\subset\sigma(\mathbf{L})
.

For the Wolf spectrum it was shown in
[
28
, Appendix B.2]
that
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e4}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
.
Consequently, we have
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
, since

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e4}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\subset\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\subset\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast}).

In the next step, we want to show that
\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})
.
To this end, we apply results established in
[
24
, Theorem 10.1.3 (i)]
for an operator in a block form

\displaystyle L=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{on }X_{1}\times X_{2},

where we need some good properties of the resolvent of
A
.

Using the notation of the book
[
24
]
for the operator
\mathbf{L}
given by (
2.5
), we take
A:=\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta+\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
on
X_{1}:=L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
.
Since
A
is a bounded perturbation of the generator of the heat semigroup from Proposition
A.1
,
A
is a densely defined, closed operator
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 1.4]
. Furthermore,
\rho(A)\not=\emptyset
by
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 1.10]
. From
[
13
, Theorem 1.6.3]
,
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 4.29]
and perturbation theory from
[
14
, Ch. III, Theorem 1.12]
, we obtain compactness of the resolvent
R(\lambda,A)
.

Moreover, we have the bounded multiplication operator
D:=\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
on
X_{2}:=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
.
The remaining operators
B:=\mathbf{C}_{\ast}:X_{2}\to X_{1}
and
C:=\mathbf{B}_{\ast}:X_{1}\to X_{2}
consist of a bounded and an unbounded, closed and densely defined multiplication operator, respectively; see operators in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
).

This means the above block setting is obtained by a permutation of the operator matrix
\mathbf{L}
. Such a permutation does not change the essential spectra which can be seen as follows.
Let us consider a permutation matrix
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+k)\times(m+k)}
with
\mathbf{P}^{2}=I
that interchanges the diffusive subsystem with the ODE subsystem. As an operator,
\mathbf{P}
is an isomorphism from
L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
. Then
\lambda I-\mathbf{L}
is a Fredholm operator with index zero if and only if
\lambda I-\tilde{\mathbf{L}}
is Fredholm with index zero where
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}=\mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}
, hence
\sigma_{e4}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma_{e4}(\tilde{\mathbf{L}})
and
\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma_{e5}(\tilde{\mathbf{L}})
. The former is a consequence of the fact that the invertible operators
\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{-1}
are (bounded) Fredholm operators and
\lambda I-\tilde{\mathbf{L}}=\mathbf{P}^{-1}(\lambda I-\mathbf{L})\mathbf{P}
is a reversible composition with Fredholm operators
[
24
, Theorem 2.2.40]
.

These properties allow applying
[
24
, Theorem 10.1.3 (i)]
. The theorem provides the equivalence
\sigma_{ei}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma_{ei}(S_{0})
for
i=4,5
for an operator
S_{0}
obtained by a decomposition of the bounded operator

\displaystyle S(\mu):=D-C(A-\mu I)^{-1}B:X_{2}\to X_{2}

for
\mu\in\rho(A)
.
Moreover, the theorem
[
24
, Theorem 10.1.3 (i)]
yields
\sigma_{e6}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma_{e6}(S_{0})
in case of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma_{e5}(S_{0})
is a connected set and
neither
\rho(S_{0})
nor
\rho(S(\mu))
is empty.
In our case,
S(\mu)
can be decomposed into the bounded operator
S_{0}=\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
and a compact operator
M(\mu)=S(\mu)-S_{0}
.
Hence,
\rho(S_{0})
and
\rho(S(\mu))
are both not empty in our case. Thus, connectedness of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma_{e5}(S_{0})=\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is sufficient for
\sigma_{e6}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma_{e6}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
.

Since
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
is bounded, it is left to show compactness of

\displaystyle M(\mu)=-C(A-\mu I)^{-1}B:X_{2}\to X_{2}.

By
[
1
, Theorem 1.34]
and Lemma
A.2
, the embedding
\iota:(\mathcal{D}(A_{p})^{k},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta})\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{k}
is compact for

, using the graph norm of the operator
\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta
. Recall that the graph norm of

and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta}
are equivalent for
\mu\in\rho(A)
. Since
\mu\in\rho(A)
, the operator
R_{\mu}\psi=-(A-\mu I)^{-1}\psi
yields a bounded isomorphism
R_{\mu}:L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\to(\mathcal{D}(A_{p})^{k},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta})
with bounded inverse
\mu I-A
. Lastly, consider the restriction
C_{|}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
of the operator
C
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{k}
. Then, since
C_{|},B
and
R_{\mu}
are bounded, the operator
M(\mu)=C_{|}\iota R_{\mu}B
is compact
[
5
, Appendix B]
.
Thus,
\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma_{e5}(S_{0})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is a consequence of
[
24

.

Using the relation
\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})\subset\sigma_{e6}(\mathbf{L})
for the Browder essential spectrum

, we obtain

\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})\subset\sigma(\mathbf{L})\setminus\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L}).

Since
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L})
, the remainder of the spectrum

Finally, if
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is a connected set,
[
24

\displaystyle\Sigma=\sigma(\mathbf{L})\setminus\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma(\mathbf{L})\setminus\sigma_{e6}(\mathbf{L})=\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L}).

A_{\infty}
is defined in Lemma
A.3
. However,
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
is not densely defined on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
.

Proof.

Closedness of the part
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
follows from
[
5
, Section 3.10, p. 184]
since
\mathbf{L}
is closed and we have the continuous embedding
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
. The identity
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{\infty})^{k}
can be deduced from the relations of
A_{p}
and
A_{\infty}
considered in Lemma
A.2
and
A.3
. Finally, by Lemma
A.2
, we obtain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Hence, the inclusion
\overline{\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}\subsetneq Z_{\infty}
holds and the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
cannot be densely defined on
Z_{\infty}
.
∎

Since the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
is not densely defined on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
,
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
cannot generate a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}

[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 1.4]
. However, it satisfies a similar resolvent estimate as in the case of a generator of an analytic semigroup. The following result can be compared to
[
14
, Ch. III, Theorem 2.10]
, and to
[
5
, Corollary 3.7.17]
for non-continuous holomorphic semigroups. Such an estimate will enable us to identify the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
with the spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
of the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

There exist a closed sector
\Sigma_{\omega}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\mathrm{arg}\,\lambda|\leq\omega\}
for some angle
\frac{\pi}{2}<\omega<\pi
and constants
M,r>0
such that
\Sigma_{\omega}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\lambda|>r\}\subset\rho(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})

with the resolvent estimate

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\|\leq\frac{M}{|\lambda|}\qquad\forall\;\lambda\in\Sigma_{\omega}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\lambda|>r\}.

(2.11)

Proof.

Let us start with the operator
\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{k}
. Applying the estimate from Lemma
A.3
to each component
\mathbf{D}^{v}_{j}\Delta
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
j=1,\dots,k
, we obtain a resolvent estimate of the form

\displaystyle\|(\lambda I-\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta)^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{k}}\leq\frac{\tilde{M}}{|\lambda|}\qquad\forall\;\lambda\in\Sigma_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}.

(2.12)

This is similar to sectorial operators on some sector
\Sigma_{\omega}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\mathrm{arg}\,\lambda|\leq\omega\}
with angle
\omega>\frac{\pi}{2}
. Apart from a dense domain, the operator
\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta
restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{k}
satisfies all other conditions of sectoriality in
[
14
, Ch. II, Definition 4.1]
. Since the zero operator on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
is also sectorial, the operator
\mathbf{D}\Delta
restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
satisfies a resolvent estimate similar to (
2.12
). By
[
14
, Ch. III, Lemma 2.6]
, the bounded perturbation
\mathbf{J}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
implies a similar resolvent estimate for the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}=\mathbf{D}\Delta+\mathbf{J}
. More precisely, as in
[
35
, Ch. 3, Theorem 2.1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\|\leq\frac{2\tilde{M}}{|\lambda|}\qquad\forall\;\lambda\in\Sigma_{\omega}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\lambda|>r\}

for
r=2\|\mathbf{J}\|_{\infty}\tilde{M}>0
and
\tilde{M}
from estimate (
2.12
).
∎

Let us now consider a further restriction of the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
to its subspace of strong continuity. The above resolvent estimate for the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
shows existence of a generator of an analytic semigroup on the closed subspace
Z_{c}:=\overline{\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})}
of
Z_{\infty}=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, see Lemma
2.12
and compare
[
29
, Corollary 3.1.24]
. In fact, the space
(Z_{c},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is again a Banach space with continuous embedding
Z_{c}\hookrightarrow Z_{\infty}
. Let us define the part of
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
in
Z_{c}
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{c}\xi

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{L}_{\infty}\xi\qquad\forall\;\xi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{c}):=\{\xi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\cap Z_{c}\mid\mathbf{L}_{\infty}\xi\in Z_{c}\}.

(2.13)

We obtain the following result for
\mathbf{L}_{c}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The operator
\mathbf{L}_{c}
defined in (
2.13
) is closed and densely defined on the space
Z_{c}=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. The operator
\mathbf{L}_{c}
is the part of
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
resp.
\mathbf{L}
in
Z_{c}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
is a closed operator on
Z_{\infty}
by Lemma
2.5
, the continuous embedding
Z_{c}\hookrightarrow Z_{\infty}
yields closedness of the part
\mathbf{L}_{c}

[
5
, Section 3.10, p. 184]
. By the inclusion
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\subset Z_{c}\subset Z_{\infty}
, we obtain that
\mathbf{L}_{c}
is also the part of
\mathbf{L}
in
Z_{c}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{c})=\{\xi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})\cap Z_{c}\mid\mathbf{L}\xi\in Z_{c}\}.

In order to show that
\mathbf{L}_{c}
is densely defined, it remains to verify
\overline{\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{c})}=Z_{c}
. In view of estimate (
2.11
) for
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
on
Z_{\infty}
, this follows from
[
5
, Lemma 3.3.12]
applied to the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
and
Z_{c}=\overline{\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})}
. Finally, we show the equality
Z_{c}=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Using the restriction of the Laplacian
A_{p}
to
C(\overline{\Omega})
from Lemma

L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{c})^{k}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Taking the closure with respect to the norm

yields
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}=\overline{\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})}=Z_{c}
.
∎

The next result makes use of the fact that we consider the operator
\mathbf{L}
on the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
and not on
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. Equality of the ODE domain and elliptic regularity allows showing equality of the spectrum of
\mathbf{L}
and its parts
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
and
\mathbf{L}_{c}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
, respectively.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be the linear operator defined in Lemma
2.1
. Let the restrictions
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
and
\mathbf{L}_{c}
be defined as in (
2.10
) and (
2.13
), respectively. Then, the spectra
\sigma(\mathbf{L}),\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
, and
\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{c})
coincide. Furthermore, the resolvents can be determined by the restrictions

\displaystyle R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})=R(\lambda,\mathbf{L})_{|L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}}\quad\text{and}\quad R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{c})=R(\lambda,\mathbf{L})_{|L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}}\quad\forall\;\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{L}).

Proof.

By Corollary
2.4
, we know that the spectrum of
\mathbf{L}
is independent of
p
. Hence, we fix
n^{\ast}<p<\infty
and prove the equality of the spectra for this specific
p
by applying
[
5
, Proposition 3.10.3]
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have
\|z\|_{p}\leq\max\{|\Omega|,1\}\|z\|_{\infty}
for all
z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
with a constant only depending on the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
but not on
p\geq 1
. This shows the continuous embeddings

L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}.

Moreover, Lemma
A.2
yields
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
for
p>n^{\ast}
. This shows invariance of the sets
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
for the resolvent
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L})
for each
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{L})
. Note that the resolvent set
\rho(\mathbf{L})
is not empty by Proposition
2.3
. Finally, we apply
[
5
, Proposition 3.10.3]
to obtain the above equalities.
∎

In the case of a continuous stationary solution of system (
1.1
), the operator
\mathbf{L}
can be restricted to the space
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
, too. If Assumption
3
holds, we obtain the restriction of the operator
\mathbf{L}
by a bounded perturbation of the degenerated differential operator
\mathbf{D}\Delta
on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
be a stationary solution of system (
1.1
) and assume that the coefficient matrices
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
in formula (

) have entries in

. The part of the operator
\mathbf{L}
, defined in Lemma
2.1
, in

is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{c}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}^{c})=C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{c})^{k}\subset C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}\to C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k},\ \xi\mapsto\mathbf{L}\xi,

see Lemma
A.4
for definition of
\mathcal{D}(A_{c})
. The operator
\mathbf{L}^{c}
is closed and densely defined on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
and generates a strongly continuous semigroup on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
which is even analytic. This semigroup coincides with the restriction of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
defined in Lemma
2.1
onto

.

Proof.

Since
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
is closed, we obtain closedness of the part of
\mathbf{L}
in
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
. Note that the Jacobian
\mathbf{J}
consists of continuous entries for a continuous steady state. In this way, one verifies the identity

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}^{c})=\{\xi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})\cap C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}\mid\mathbf{L}\xi\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}\}=C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{c})^{k}.

By Lemma
A.4
, the operator
\mathbf{L}^{c}
is densely defined. Recall for

that

\displaystyle(\lambda I-\mathbf{L})\xi=\psi\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\begin{cases}\qquad\qquad\quad\;\xi_{1}=(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}_{\ast})^{-1}(\psi_{1}+\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\xi_{2}),\\
(\lambda I-\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta)\xi_{2}=\psi_{2}+\mathbf{C}_{\ast}\xi_{1}+\mathbf{D}_{\ast}\xi_{2}.\end{cases}

(2.14)

From this explicit form of the resolvent

, we infer that

is an invariant subspace for the resolvent
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L})
. Moreover, the part of
\mathbf{L}
on the space

satisfies the resolvent estimate (
2.11
). By
[

, Theorem 1.6]
, this part is the generator of an analytic semigroup on

. Finally, we apply
[
35
, Ch. 4, Theorem 5.5]
to see that the analytic semigroup on

is just a restriction of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
.
∎

Unfortunately, methods used in the proof of Proposition
2.8
only imply
\sigma(\mathbf{L}^{c})\subset\sigma(\mathbf{L})
due to minor regularity of the ODE component in definition of
\mathbf{L}
.
Analog to Proposition
2.3
, where a similar spectral decomposition is verified for the operator
\mathbf{L}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
, we obtain the following spectral characterization. We are now able to conclude that the spectrum of the operator
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{L}^{c}
coincide, too.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{D}^{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{k\times k}
. Let
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
be matrix-valued functions with entries in
C(\overline{\Omega})
according to the linear operator
\mathbf{L}^{c}
defined in Lemma
2.9
on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
. Then, the spectrum of the operator
\mathbf{L}^{c}
is given by

\sigma(\mathbf{L}^{c})=\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\mathop{\dot{\cup}}\Sigma=\sigma(\mathbf{L}),

where
\sigma(\mathbf{L})
is characterized in Proposition
2.3
.

Proof.

Since
A_{c}
has compact resolvent on
C(\overline{\Omega})
by Lemma
A.4
, the proof of Proposition
2.3
literally applies to characterize the spectrum of
\mathbf{L}^{c}
. It is again sufficient to show
\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{L}^{c})
for the Schechter essential spectrum, since we infer
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{e5}(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
from Proposition
D.3
. Using the notation of Proposition
2.3
, we consider
X_{2}:=C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
and
X_{1}:=C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
instead and, hence, only bounded multiplication operators are used on these spaces apart from the elliptic operator. This yields
\sigma(\mathbf{L}^{c})=\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\mathop{\dot{\cup}}\Sigma^{c}
for some set of eigenvalues
\Sigma^{c}\subset\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L}^{c})
.

The inclusion
\sigma(\mathbf{L}^{c})\subset\sigma(\mathbf{L})
is clear from
\mathcal{D}(A_{c})\subset\mathcal{D}(A_{p})
and the fact that the spectrum of the multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
is the same on

and
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
by Proposition

and
[
28
, Appendix B.2]
. Hence, it remains to verify
\Sigma\subset\Sigma^{c}
. For
\lambda\in\Sigma\subset\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
, there exists an eigenfunction
\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{p})^{k}
which is an element of
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
for

. Using the explicit form (
2.14
) of the resolvent leads to

\xi_{1}=(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}_{\ast})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\xi_{2}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
and hence
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L}^{c})
by
\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(A_{c})^{k}
.
∎

2.2
Semigroup properties

In the case of a continuous stationary solution, it is a simple consequence of the spectral mapping theorem for analytic semigroups that the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
restricted to
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
equals the spectral bound of the linear operator
\mathbf{L}^{c}
defined in Lemma
2.9
.
The aim of this section is to connect the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
to the spectral bound of the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
.
This allows showing stability results in Section
3
in the case of a discontinuous stationary solution. We make use of the parts
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
in
Z_{\infty}=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
\mathbf{L}_{c}
in
Z_{c}=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
of the operator
\mathbf{L}
defined in Lemma
2.1
. Since
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is analytic on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
by Lemma
2.1
, the spectral mapping theorem

\mathrm{e}^{\sigma(\mathbf{L})t}=\sigma(\mathbf{T}(t))\setminus\{0\}\qquad\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

is valid
[
14
, Ch. IV, Corollary 3.12]
. From this, we infer that the spectral bound

s(\mathbf{L}):=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\mid\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{L})\}

of the operator
\mathbf{L}
equals the growth bound
w_{0}^{p}
of
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
, i.e.,

s(\mathbf{L})=w_{0}^{p}:=\inf\{w\in\mathbb{R}\mid\exists\,M_{w}\geq 1\;\text{such that}\;\|\mathbf{T}(t)\|_{p}\leq M_{w}\mathrm{e}^{wt}\;\text{for all}\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}.

First, we show that the analytic semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
from Lemma
2.1
is
Z_{\infty}
-invariant for
n^{\ast}=\max\{n/2,2\}<p<\infty
and can be restricted to
Z_{\infty}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

The semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
defined as the restriction of
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
is not strongly continuous. However, the map
t\mapsto\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k})
is norm-continuous for all
t>0
and is exponentially bounded, i.e., there exist constants
M\geq 1
,
w\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\|\leq M\mathrm{e}^{wt}\qquad\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(2.15)

Proof.

Since
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is an analytic semigroup on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
, it follows that
\mathbf{T}(t)
maps into
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})
for all
t>0

[
5
, Corollary 3.7.21]
. Recall
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
for
p>n^{\ast}
from Proposition
2.8
. Since
\mathbf{T}(0)=I
, we obtain
\mathbf{T}(t)L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
for all
t\geq 0
and the restricted semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is well-defined.

According to
[
43
, Theorem 1.6]
, the resolvent estimate in Lemma
2.6
implies existence of a semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{|}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
of bounded operators on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
which is generated by the closed operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
. Moreover, the semigroup can be extended to an analytic semigroup on some sector in
\mathbb{C}
which includes the positive half-line
\{t\mid t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
, compare also
[
5
, Corollary 3.7.17]
. By
[
5
, Definition 3.2.5]
, this semigroup is strongly continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and exponentially bounded. However, the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{|}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
with
\mathbf{T}_{|}(0):=I
is not strongly continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by Lemma
2.5
. Analyticity of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
yields that
\mathbf{T}(t)
is given by a complex contour integral over the resolvent
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L})
and a similar formula is valid for
(\mathbf{T}_{|}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
in terms of
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})

[
43
, Theorem 1.6]
. Since the restriction of the resolvent
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L})
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
equals
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
by Proposition
2.8
, we obtain that
(\mathbf{T}_{|}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
and
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
coincide on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. Note that we can choose the same path of integration in view of
\rho(\mathbf{L})=\rho(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
from Proposition
2.8
. By analyticity of
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
, it follows that
t\mapsto\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k})
is norm-continuous for
t>0
. ∎

To show equality of the growth bound
w_{0}^{\infty}
of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
with the spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L})
, we use the fact that each semigroup operator

is the limit of regularized semigroup operators
[
7
]
. This regularization is obtained by a further restriction of the semigroup

to the closed subspace

of
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. Although it could not be verified that the Jacobian
\mathbf{J}
is a
\mathbf{D}\Delta
-bounded perturbation on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
, the operator
\mathbf{L}_{c}
is the generator of an analytic semigroup.

Lemma 2.12
.

The semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}

). Moreover, the semigroup

.

Proof.

We already considered the restriction of
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}

. Recall

\displaystyle R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{c})=R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})_{|Z_{c}}=R(\lambda,\mathbf{L})_{|Z_{c}}\qquad\forall\;\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{L}).

(2.16)

Since

, the resolvent estimate derived in Lemma
2.6

for

[

, Theorem 1.6]
.

In order to deduce that

is the restriction of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
to
Z_{c}
, we apply
[
35
, Ch. 4, Theorem 5.5]
. In view of the generator property of the part
\mathbf{L}_{c}
of
\mathbf{L}
in
Z_{c}
, it remains to verify that the space

is invariant for the resolvent

. However, this follows from (
2.16
), and we obtain
\mathbf{T}(t)_{|Z_{c}}=\mathbf{T}_{c}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. As the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
defined in Lemma
2.11
is a restriction of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, we also infer
\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)_{|Z_{c}}=\mathbf{T}_{c}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

The semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is determined by its restriction on the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
due to the pointwise limit

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\xi=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathbf{T}_{c}(t)\lambda R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\xi\qquad\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}.

(2.17)

Proof.

According to
[
5
, p. 125]
and properties derived for the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
in Lemma
2.11
, the family
(\mathbf{U}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
of linear, bounded operators on
Z_{\infty}
given by

\mathbf{U}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(\tau)\;\mathrm{d}\tau\qquad\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

is a once integrated semigroup
[
5
, Definition 3.2.1]
. Moreover, following
[
29
, Lemma 2.1.6]
, the semigroup
(\mathbf{U}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is generated by
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
and the resolvent is given by

R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}\mathbf{U}(\tau)\;\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}\mathbf{T}_{|}(\tau)\;\mathrm{d}\tau\qquad\forall\;\lambda>w.

By growth estimate (
2.15
), the once integrated semigroup
(\mathbf{U}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
satisfies estimates of the resolvent derivatives in
[
7
, Lemma 1]
. By choosing
\omega=w+\varepsilon
and
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
with
\varphi(t)=M\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon t}
for a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
and recalling estimate (
2.11
), we have verified all assumptions of
[
7
, Theorem 3]
. By analyticity of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{c}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
, there holds
D=Z_{c}
in
[
7
, Lemma 2]
and condition e) in
[
7
, Theorem 3]
applies for
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\subset Z_{c}
. Then the limit (
2.17
) is a consequence of the latter theorem, see property (3.5) in
[
7
, Theorem 3]
.
∎

From this proof it is clear that the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is the non-continuous semigroup constructed in
[
7
, Theorem 3]
, which is uniquely determined by the operator
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
. Finally, the latter result can be used to relate the spectral bound of the operator
\mathbf{L}_{c}
to the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
.

Proposition 2.14
.

The spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L})
of the operator
\mathbf{L}
equals the growth bound
w_{0}^{\infty}
of the restricted semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
.

Proof.

Let us first show
w_{0}^{\infty}\leq s(\mathbf{L})
. By
[
14
, Ch. IV, Corollary 3.12]
, the spectral mapping theorem holds for the analytic semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{c}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
. Proposition
2.8
implies
s(\mathbf{L})=s(\mathbf{L}_{c})=w_{0}^{c}
for the growth bound
w_{0}^{c}
of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{c}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
. Consequently, by definition of the growth bound, for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}_{c}(t)\xi\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{(s(\mathbf{L})+\varepsilon)t}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\qquad\forall\;\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k},t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

Using the limit (
2.17
) as well as the resolvent estimate (
2.11
), we deduce for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
that

\|\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\xi\|_{\infty}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\|\mathbf{T}_{c}(t)\lambda R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\xi\|_{\infty}\leq MC_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{(s(\mathbf{L})+\varepsilon)t}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\quad\forall\;\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}.

This estimate shows that the growth bound
w_{0}^{\infty}
of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
satisfies the inequality
w_{0}^{\infty}\leq s(\mathbf{L})+\varepsilon
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields the first claim
w_{0}^{\infty}\leq s(\mathbf{L})
.

It remains to prove the converse inequality
s(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\leq w_{0}^{\infty}
. Let us assume that
s(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})>w_{0}^{\infty}
. Consider an arbitrary
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>w
, where
w\in\mathbb{R}
with
w_{0}^{\infty}<w<s(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
. From the definition of the growth bound
w_{0}^{\infty}
, we know that there is a constant
M\geq 1
such that

\|\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{wt}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

This estimate and continuity of the map
t\mapsto\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k})
for
t>0
implies that the improper Riemann integral

R(\lambda)f:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(\tau)f\;\mathrm{d}\tau

exists for each
f\in Z_{\infty}=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
with the estimate
\|R(\lambda)\|_{\infty}\leq M/(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda-w)
. We follow the proof of
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 1.10]
to obtain a contradiction to
w<s(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
. We will verify that
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
and
R(\lambda)
coincides with the resolvent of
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
at
\lambda
.

Since
Z_{\infty}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
, we can consider the integral
R(\lambda)f
as an element in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
for each
f\in Z_{\infty}
. Using the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
, one shows that
R(\lambda)f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})
with

(\lambda I-\mathbf{L})R(\lambda)f=f,

see the proof of
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 1.10]
. This implies that
R(\lambda)
is the right inverse of
\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
. For the left inverse, let us consider
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
and the sequence
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset Z_{\infty}
given by the proper integrals

f_{j}:=\int_{0}^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(\tau)f\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

This sequence converges to
R(\lambda)f
in
Z_{\infty}
by definition of
R(\lambda)
.
Considering again the generator
(\mathbf{L},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}))
of
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
,
[
14
, Ch. II, Lemma 1.9]
implies
f_{j}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
with

(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})f_{j}=(\lambda I-\mathbf{L})f_{j}=\int_{0}^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}\mathbf{T}(\tau)(\lambda I-\mathbf{L})f\;\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(\tau)(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})f\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

By definition of
R(\lambda)
, this shows that
(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})f_{j}\to R(\lambda)(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})f
in
Z_{\infty}
as
j\to\infty
. Closedness of
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
as shown in Lemma
2.5
implies
R(\lambda)f=\lim_{j\to\infty}f_{j}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
with

(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})R(\lambda)f=(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\lim_{j\to\infty}f_{j}=\lim_{j\to\infty}(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})f_{j}=R(\lambda)(\lambda I-\mathbf{L}_{\infty})f.

In summary,
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
with resolvent
R(\lambda,\mathbf{L}_{\infty})=R(\lambda)
. The arbitrariness of
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>w
and
w_{0}^{\infty}<w<s(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})
finally yields
s(\mathbf{L}_{\infty})\leq w_{0}^{\infty}
.
∎

3
Nonlinear stability analysis

After a linearization at a stationary solution
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
of the reaction-diffusion-ODE system (
1.1
), we reduced our considerations to equation (
2.1
) with the shift
\xi=(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})-(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})
and the corresponding trivial steady state
\xi\equiv\mathbf{0}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial t}=\mathbf{L}\xi+\mathbf{N}(\xi),\qquad\xi(0)=\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}.

Recall from Appendix
C
that a mild solution of this equation satisfies the implicit integral equation

\displaystyle\xi(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{T}(t)\xi^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{T}(t-\tau)\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

It is well-known that the control of the growth of the semigroup is fundamental for estimating the solution of the nonlinear problem. However, even for the linear case asymptotic stability and exponential asymptotic stability are non-equivalent concepts
[
51
]
. The detailed examination of the operator
\mathbf{L}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and its corresponding semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
allows considering the implicit integral equation on this space, too. Recall that the nonlinear term
\mathbf{N}
can be controlled on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
by estimates given in Lemma
2.2
. The nonlinear operator
\mathbf{N}
is locally Lipschitz continuous and satisfies
\mathbf{N}(\xi)=o(\|\xi\|_{\infty})
as
\|\xi\|_{\infty}\to 0
, i.e., there exist a constant
\rho>0
and a continuous increasing function
b:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
b(0)=0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{N}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq b(\rho)\|\xi\|_{\infty}\qquad\forall\;\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},\|\xi\|_{\infty}<\rho.

(3.1)

This indicates that the behavior of a solution to equation (
2.1
) is mainly governed by the growth of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. Recall that the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
is not strongly continuous. However, this semigroup still has a smoothing property for
t>0
and the growth bound is determined by the spectral bound of
\mathbf{L}
, i.e., the equality
s(\mathbf{L})=w_{0}^{\infty}
holds by Proposition
2.14
. This leads us to the stability result in Theorem
3.3
and the instability result in Theorem
3.7
in the case of a discontinuous stationary solution. Note that both proofs rely on temporal continuity of the solution in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
for
t>0
. For this reason, we use the following notion of stability in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, similar to
[
29
, Ch. 9, p. 337]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The trivial solution
\xi\equiv\mathbf{0}
of equation (
2.1
) is called
nonlinearly stable
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that an upper bound
\|\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}<\delta
for initial values
\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
implies existence and uniqueness of a mild solution
\xi
in the sense of Definition
C.1
which satisfies

(i)

the solution
\xi
is defined globally in time, i.e.,
T_{\max}=\infty
;

(ii)

\|\xi(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

The trivial solution
\xi\equiv\mathbf{0}
is called
nonlinearly unstable
if
it is not nonlinearly stable. Furthermore, a stationary solution
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
of system (
1.1
) is called nonlinearly stable (nonlinearly unstable) if the corresponding trivial solution of equation (
2.1
) is nonlinearly stable (nonlinearly unstable).

If the steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
has a discontinuity, then a sufficiently small
\delta
-neighborhood in Definition
3.1
consists only of discontinuous initial conditions
\xi^{0}
as well.
Note that this notion of stability is stronger than in
[
15
, Section 2]
, in which condition (ii) holds only for almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Further note that possible jump-discontinuities of the ODE component
\overline{\mathbf{u}}
are not cut out in the used topology in Definition
3.1
. Hence, the notion of this work differs from
(\varepsilon,A)
stability considered in
[
20
,
42
,
48
]
. That approach is used for a partly continuous approximation of the steady state but excludes a neighborhood of the discontinuity of the steady state.

Remark 3.2
.

The regularity of the mild solution strongly depends on the regularity of the initial condition. Depending on the regularity of the initial perturbation, we consider three different notions of stability – stability in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
, Lyapunov stability in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
or in
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
.

Recall that a solution of equation (
1.1
) with initial values
\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
may be discontinuous at time
t=0
with respect to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, see Proposition
C.2
. Nevertheless,

\xi\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k})\cap C(\mathbb{R}_{>0};L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k})

by analyticity of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
from Lemma
2.11
and
[
5
, Proposition 1.3.4]
.

In order to determine Lyapunov stability in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
, problem (
2.1
) is endowed with initial values
\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Since the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
restricted to the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
is strongly continuous by Lemma
2.12
, the solution
\xi
of system (
2.1
) satisfies
\xi\in C([0,T_{\max});L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k})
by Proposition
C.2
and
[
5
, Proposition 1.3.4]
. Hence, the inequality in condition (ii) of Definition
3.1
holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
in the classical sense of Lyapunov.

In the case of a continuous steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
, one may consider stability as in Definition
3.1
or Lyapunov stability in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Under Assumption
3
, we may also consider Lyapunov stability in
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
, see Corollary
3.4
and Corollary
3.8
. In this case, initial conditions
\xi^{0}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
are prescribed. By Lemma
2.9
and validity of estimate (
2.8
) for the nonlinear remainder in
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
, the solution
\xi
of problem (
2.1
) satisfies
\xi\in C([0,T_{\max});C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k})
. Here, we use a Picard iteration in
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
in the proof of Proposition
C.2
, compare also
[
35
, Ch. 6, Theorem 1.4]
. Note that, compared to stability with respect to
W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{m+1}
considered in
[
10
,
32
]
, we allow for a larger class of perturbations in Definition
3.1
.

The works
[
11
,
26
,
42
]
consider Lyapunov stability in the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})
as perturbations are smoother for the diffusing component. We briefly depict the possibility of perturbing a stationary solution with bounded initial conditions that are small with respect to the norm on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
or
L^{p}(\Omega)^{m+k}
for a certain model in Remark
4.4
. Since the nonlinear remainder even does not need to be well-defined on these spaces, this kind of perturbations is far from being understood. It remains an open question whether the transfer from linear to nonlinear stability is possible on these spaces. In that respect, we also refer to numerical simulations illustrated in
[
26
, Figure 4]
.

3.1
Stability of steady states

This section is devoted to stability of a bounded stationary solution
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
of the reaction-diffusion-ODE system (
1.1
). We will show that a negative spectral bound of the linearized operator
\mathbf{L}
in (
2.1
) implies a local exponential stability result. Consequently, unlike the specific study
[
22
]
, we do not treat the degenerated case in which
0\in\sigma(\mathbf{L})
. The section is based on the stability result
[
47
, Proposition 4.17]
. Since stability of a continuous steady state can be deduced from stability in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
or
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
, we focus on the case of a discontinuous steady state for which the choice of the function space is challenging. The nonlinear operator
\mathbf{N}
defined by equation (
2.1
) is not well-defined on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
for discontinuous steady states, while the nonlinear operator is continuous on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. On this space, however, the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is not strongly continuous and
[
47
]
cannot directly be applied. Nevertheless, we can adapt the proof by applying semigroup estimates derived in the last section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider a steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
of system (
1.1
). Let Assumption
2
be satisfied. If the linearized operator
\mathbf{L}
from Lemma
2.1
has a negative spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L})<0
, then, the steady state is nonlinearly stable in the sense of Definition
3.1
. More precisely, we obtain local exponential stability in the sense that there exist some constants
\delta,\beta>0
and
M\geq 1
such that for initial conditions
(\mathbf{u}^{0},\mathbf{v}^{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
with
\|(\mathbf{u}^{0},\mathbf{v}^{0})-(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
the mild solution
\xi=(u,v)
of system (
1.1
) is globally defined and satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t),\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t))-(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\|_{\infty}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{-\beta t}\|(\mathbf{u}^{0},\mathbf{v}^{0})-(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\|_{\infty}\quad\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(3.2)

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
47
, Proposition 4.17]
to the linearized equation (
2.1
) at
\xi=\mathbf{0}
. The proof is based on the choice of some constants
0<w<-w_{0}^{\infty}=-s(\mathbf{L})
and
M=M_{w}\geq 1
such that

\|\mathbf{T}(t)\xi\|_{\infty}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{-wt}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\qquad\forall\;\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

In view of estimate (
3.1
), we can choose
r>0
such that
\|\mathbf{N}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{w}{2M}\|\xi\|_{\infty}
for all
\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
with
\|\xi\|_{\infty}<r
. Let
\delta=\frac{r}{M}
and fix
\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
with
\|\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}<\delta
. By Remark
3.2
, there exists a unique mild solution

\xi\in C([0,T_{\max});L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k})\cap C((0,T_{\max});L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k})

of problem (
2.1
) on a maximal existence interval
[0,T_{\max})
, where
T_{\max}
depends on the initial conditions
\xi^{0}
. Let us define

\displaystyle T:=\sup\{t>0\mid\|\xi(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\infty}\leq r\ \text{for all}\ 0<\tau\leq t\}.

Since
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is not strongly continuous, it is not obvious that
T<T_{\max}
is a positive real number. The mild solution
\xi
satisfies the implicit integral formulation (
C.2
). Estimating each term yields

\displaystyle\|\xi(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t-\tau)\mathbf{N}(\xi(\tau))\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq M\mathrm{e}^{-wt}\|\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}+M\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-w(t-\tau)}\|\mathbf{N}(\xi(\tau))\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}\tau,

where the last terms are continuous in
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and smaller than
r
for
t=0
. This shows
T>0
and we can further estimate the solution
\xi
for
0\leq t<T
. Continuity of
\mathbf{N}
shows that the estimate
\|\mathbf{N}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{w}{2M}\|\xi\|_{\infty}
holds as long as
\|\xi\|_{\infty}\leq r
, hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{wt}\|\xi(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq M\|\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}M\mathrm{e}^{w\tau}\|\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq M\|\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}+\frac{w}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{w\tau}\|\xi(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

Since
\tau\mapsto\xi(\cdot,\tau)
is Bochner integrable on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, the function
\tau\mapsto\|\xi(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\infty}
is a bounded, Lebesgue integrable function on
[0,T)
. By Gronwall’s lemma, it follows

\displaystyle\|\xi(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq M\|\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{w}{2}t}\leq r\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{w}{2}t}\leq r\qquad\forall\;0\leq t<T.

If
T<T_{\max}
, we would have
\|\xi(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\to r
for
t\to T
by continuity of the solution
\xi\in C((0,T_{\max});L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k})
and definition of
T
. However, this contradicts the estimate
\|\xi(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq r\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{w}{2}t}\to r\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{w}{2}T}<r
for
t\to T
. This shows
T\geq T_{\max}
and no blow-up is possible as
t
reaches
T_{\max}
from below. By Proposition
C.2
(iii), it follows
T=T_{\max}=\infty
. Hence, the exponential decay rate in estimate (
3.2
) can be chosen to be
\beta=w/2>0
.
∎

Using the notion of Lyapunov stability on certain subspaces of
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, we gain the following result.

Corollary 3.4
.

Stability with respect to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
implies Lyapunov stability in the subspace
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Furthermore, concerning a continuous steady state in
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
under Assumption
3
, Lyapunov stability in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
implies Lyapunov stability in
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
.

Proof.

This is due to regularity of the solution discussed in Remark
3.2
and the fact that the same norm is used for each of these spaces.
∎

3.2
Instability of steady states

In the nonlinear case, there are mainly two ways showing instability via linearization.

Superlinear decay of the nonlinear reaction term in combination with a non-empty intersection of the spectrum with the complex right half-plane is sufficient for instability, see
[
38
, Theorem 1]
or
[
12
, Ch. VII, Theorem 2.3]
for bounded operators. Unfortunately, both the nonlinear term and the semigroup generated by the linearized operator need to be continuous on the same function space
X
. This seems to be the reason why
[
32
, Theorem 2.1]
uses the space
X=W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{m+1}
, which requires a regular steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})\in X
for the nonlinear term to be well-defined. In order to reduce regularity of the steady state, the authors of
[
32
]
choose a spectral gap condition described next.

A growth estimate of the nonlinear reaction term with respect to two different norms and a spectral gap next to the imaginary axis may lead to instability, see
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
or
[
12
, Ch. VII, Theorem 2.2]
for bounded operators. The former method is used to provide the instability result
[
32
, Theorem 2.11]
which can be generalized to systems and discontinuous, bounded steady states in view of Proposition
2.3
.

In this section, we aim for a generalization of the results from
[
32
]
to bounded, discontinuous and continuous, steady states of the reaction-diffusion-ODE system (
1.1
) without requiring a spectral gap. We use the semigroup approach of
[
38
]
combined with the notion of a mild solution from
[
37
]
to show instability. First, we aim to verify semigroup estimates from
[
38
, Lemma 2, 3]
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
in the case of a discontinuous stationary solution. Besides continuity of the solution for positive times, such estimates are essential for showing instability, see Theorem
3.7
.

To obtain instability, let the spectrum of the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
defined in Lemma
2.1
meet the right complex half-plane
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0\}
, i.e., we assume
s(\mathbf{L})>0
. In view of Proposition
2.8
,
s(\mathbf{L}_{c})=s(\mathbf{L})>0
and the spectral radius of the restricted semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{c}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
satisfies
r(\mathbf{B})=\mathrm{e}^{w_{0}^{c}}>1
for
\mathbf{B}:=\mathbf{T}_{c}(1)

[
14
, Ch. IV, Proposition 2.2]
. We choose some
\mu\in\sigma(\mathbf{B})
on the outer boundary of the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbf{B})
and obtain the following result similar to
[
38
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mu=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\in\sigma(\mathbf{T}_{c}(1))
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
satisfy
|\mu|=r(\mathbf{T}_{c}(1))=\mathrm{e}^{s(\mathbf{L})}
and
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda=s(\mathbf{L})>0
. Then for each
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a function
\mathbf{v}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}_{c}(m)\mathbf{v}-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda m}\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}<\gamma\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}

holds. Moreover, the same function
\mathbf{v}
fulfills

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}_{c}(t)\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}\leq 2K\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{Re}\lambda\,t}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}\qquad\forall\;0\leq t\leq m,

where
K:=\sup\{\|\mathbf{T}_{c}(s)\|_{\infty}\mid 0\leq s\leq 1\}<\infty
.

Proof.

Apply
[
38
, Lemma 2]
to the bounded operator
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{T}_{c}(1)
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
, see Lemma
2.12
.
∎

While Lemma
3.5
provides initial data which lead us to instability, the following inequalities are used for an estimation of the implicit integral equation of the mild solution in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda=s(\mathbf{L})>0
. Then for each
\varepsilon>0
there is a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{Re}\lambda\,t}\leq\|\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{(\mathrm{Re}\lambda+\varepsilon)t}\qquad\;\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

Proof.

Proposition
2.14
shows
w_{0}^{\infty}=s(\mathbf{L})
for the growth bound
w_{0}^{\infty}
of
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
. Applying
[
14
, Ch. IV, Lemma 2.3]
to the exponentially bounded semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
yields
w_{0}^{\infty}=\log r(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(1))
, similar to the proof of
[
14
, Ch. IV, Proposition 2.2]
. Hence, the definition of the spectral radius implies

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{Re}\lambda}=|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}|=r(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(1))=\lim_{m\to\infty}\|\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(m)\|^{1/m}.

The rest is analog to the proof of
[
38
, Lemma 3]
.
∎

Proposition
2.14
shows the relation
w_{0}^{\infty}=s(\mathbf{L})
between the growth bound
w_{0}^{\infty}
of the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and the spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L})
of the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
. As a consequence, a positive spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L})
implies a positive growth bound

, and hence instability.

Theorem 3.7
.

Consider a steady state
(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbf{v}})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
of system (
1.1
). Let Assumption
2
be satisfied. If the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
from Lemma
2.1
has a positive spectral bound
s(\mathbf{L})>0
, then the steady state is nonlinearly unstable in the sense of Definition
3.1
and in the sense of Lyapunov in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
.

Proof.

The proof is basically analog to the proof of
[
38
, Theorem 1]
, where a linearized equation of the form (
2.1
) is considered. In the following we depict only main adaptions of the proof. We consider the restricted semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{c}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
in Lemma
3.5
to provide bounded initial data
\mathbf{v}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
that lead us to instability. Assuming stability to the contrary, a global mild solution in the sense of Definition
C.1
satisfies

\xi\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k})\cap C(\mathbb{R}_{>0};L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}).

However, the restricted semigroup
(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is not strongly continuous on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. Nevertheless, from boundedness of the solution
\xi
, we infer that

\displaystyle t\mapsto\xi(\cdot,t)-\mathbf{T}(t)\mathbf{v}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{T}(t-\tau)\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))\;\mathrm{d}\tau\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}

is continuous in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and equals
\mathbf{0}
in
t=0

[
5
, Proposition 1.3.4]
. Using
\sigma=s(\mathbf{L})>0
, we define

\displaystyle T:=\sup\{t>0\mid\|\xi(\cdot,\tau)-\mathbf{T}(\tau)\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2|\mu|}\delta\mathrm{e}^{\sigma\tau},\|\xi(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\infty}<\frac{\rho}{2}\ \text{for all}\ 0<\tau\leq t\}.

Whereas the first condition can be satisfied by continuity for all sufficiently small
t
, the second condition needs more arguments. Using Lemma
3.5
and
3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\xi(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{T}(t)\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{T}(t-\tau)\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2K\mathrm{e}^{\sigma t}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}C_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{(\sigma+\varepsilon)(t-\tau)}\|\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

The right-hand side is continuous in
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and equals
2K\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}=2K\delta
in
t=0
. In order to ensure
T>0
in above definition, we choose in case of
2K>1

0<\delta<\min\left\{\delta_{0},k^{-1},\frac{\rho}{4K},1\right\}

rather than
0<\delta<\min\{\delta_{0},k^{-1},\frac{\rho}{2},1\}
for initial conditions
\mathbf{v}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
with
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}=\delta
. Apart from these modifications, the remainder of the proof is identical to
[
38
]
. It is shown that there is some time point at which the solution leaves the presumed invariant region. This leads to a contradiction to stability in the sense of Definition
3.1
, and hence instability of the zero solution.

Concerning instability in the sense of Lyapunov in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
, we allow only perturbations in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
. Thus, the proof remains the same. Remark
3.2
yields continuity of the mild solution.
∎

The characterization of the spectrum of the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
in Proposition
2.3
can be used to determine the sign of the spectral bound

. Note that this spectrum is independent of the parameter

by Corollary

. Recall that a spectral value

of the ODE subsystem with

provides an instability result in the sense of Definition

Remark 3.9

4
Applications

In this section, we show applicability of the developed theory to the analysis of pattern formation on two basic examples of reaction-diffusion-ODE problems exhibiting unstable continuous and stable discontinuous stationary solutions. We start with some general properties of the linearized model. To this end, we focus on the spectrum of the linearized operator
\mathbf{L}
defined in Lemma
2.1
, i.e.,
\mathbf{L}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
given by

\displaystyle(\mathbf{L}\xi)(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta\xi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{1}(x)+\mathbf{B}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{2}(x)\\
\mathbf{C}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{1}(x)+\mathbf{D}_{\ast}(x)\xi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times\mathcal{D}(A_{p})^{k}
for
p>n^{\ast}=\max\{n/2,2\}
. Recall that the coefficients
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
are matrices with entries in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. If
s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})>0
, then it follows
s(\mathbf{L})>0
by Proposition
2.3
. In general,
s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})<0
does not imply a negative spectral bound of
\mathbf{L}
, see Theorem
4.2
below. In such a case, the next lemma is helpful for a reaction-diffusion-ODE system consisting of one ordinary and one partial differential equation. This result generalizes
[
19
, Corollary 3.10]
,
[
11
, Proposition 4.3]
and is adapted from
[
48
, Section 4]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle A_{\ast}(x)\leq-c,\quad D_{\ast}(x)\leq 0,\quad A_{\ast}(x)D_{\ast}(x)-B_{\ast}(x)C_{\ast}(x)>0

(4.1)

holds for almost every
x\in\Omega
. Then, the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
defined in Lemma
2.1
satisfies
s(\mathbf{L})<0
.

Proof.

By the characterization of
\sigma(A_{\ast})
in
[
28
, Appendix B.2]
, it follows
s(A_{\ast})<0
.
We have
\sigma(\mathbf{L})=\sigma(A_{\ast})\cup\Sigma
by Proposition
2.3
. Moreover, the resolvent
\rho(A_{\ast})
is connected in
\mathbb{C}
since
A_{\ast}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
\Sigma\subset\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})
is discrete by Proposition
2.3
.
Hence, it is left to prove
\sup_{\lambda\in\Sigma}\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<0
.
Although
\Sigma
is discrete, we have to find a constant
c_{0}>0
such that
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\leq-c_{0}<0
for all
\lambda\in\Sigma=\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})\cap\rho(A_{\ast})
. For each
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})\cap\rho(A_{\ast})
we find an eigenfunction
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})\setminus\{(0,0)\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}=(\mathbf{L}-\lambda I)\xi=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
D^{v}\Delta\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}(A_{\ast}-\lambda I)\xi_{1}+B_{\ast}\xi_{2}\\
C_{\ast}\xi_{1}+(D_{\ast}-\lambda I)\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\lambda\in\rho(A_{\ast})
, we can solve the first equation to obtain
\xi_{1}=(\lambda I-A_{\ast})^{-1}B_{\ast}\xi_{2}
.
This relation shows
\xi_{2}\not\equiv 0
, since
\xi_{2}\equiv 0
would imply
\xi_{1}\equiv 0
. However, this contradicts
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})
with eigenfunction
\xi\not\equiv\mathbf{0}
.

Similar to Corollary
2.4
, a substitution of
\xi_{1}
in the second equation leads us to the following elliptic boundary value problem

\displaystyle-D^{v}\Delta\xi_{2}=\left(\frac{B_{\ast}C_{\ast}}{\lambda-A_{\ast}}+D_{\ast}-\lambda\right)\xi_{2}

for
\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(A_{p})\setminus\{0\},p>n^{\ast}
. According to Lemma
A.2
, this problem can be seen as a weak formulation for
\xi_{2}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Multiplying this equation with the complex conjugate of
\xi_{2}
and integrating yields

\displaystyle D^{v}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\xi_{2}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{B_{\ast}C_{\ast}}{\lambda-A_{\ast}}+D_{\ast}-\lambda\right)|\xi_{2}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

(4.2)

We write
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+i\lambda_{2}
for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and consider the imaginary and real part of equation (
4.2
), i.e.,

\displaystyle-\lambda_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{B_{\ast}C_{\ast}}{|\lambda-A_{\ast}|^{2}}+1\right)|\xi_{2}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x=0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A(x,\lambda)|\xi_{2}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x=D^{v}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\xi_{2}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\geq 0

(4.4)

with

\displaystyle A(x,\lambda)=\frac{B_{\ast}(x)C_{\ast}(x)(\lambda_{1}-A_{\ast}(x))}{|\lambda-A_{\ast}(x)|^{2}}+D_{\ast}(x)-\lambda_{1}.

Equation (
4.3
) implies that
\lambda_{2}
is uniformly bounded. In fact,
\lambda_{2}\not=0
and

\displaystyle\frac{B_{\ast}C_{\ast}}{|\lambda-A_{\ast}|^{2}}+1>0\qquad\text{a.e. in }\;\Omega

leads to a contradiction since
\xi_{2}\not\equiv 0
. So, in case
\lambda_{2}\not=0
, we may assume that the last inequality does not hold almost everywhere in
\Omega
, i.e., there is a measurable set
\Omega_{1}
with
|\Omega_{1}|>0
such that

\frac{B_{\ast}C_{\ast}}{|\lambda-A_{\ast}|^{2}}+1\leq 0\qquad\text{a.e. in }\;\Omega_{1}.

Hence, boundedness of the coefficients
B_{\ast},C_{\ast}
implies uniform boundedness of the imaginary part
\lambda_{2}
as
\lambda_{2}^{2}\leq\|B_{\ast}C_{\ast}\|_{\infty}
. Since
\Sigma
is a discrete set and the imaginary parts of
\lambda\in\Sigma
are uniformly bounded, it remains to show
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<0
for all
\lambda\in\Sigma
.

For this reason, let us assume to the contrary that there exists
\lambda\in\Sigma
with
\lambda_{1}=\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq 0
. We will show that such a spectral value cannot exist under above assumption (
4.1
). Let us consider the real part of equation (
4.2
). We aim to show
A(\cdot,\lambda)<0
a.e. in
\Omega
to obtain a contradiction to relation (
4.4
), since
\xi_{2}\not\equiv 0
.
With
|\lambda-A_{\ast}|^{2}=(\lambda_{1}-A_{\ast})^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle A(\cdot,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{\ast}C_{\ast}(\lambda_{1}-A_{\ast})}{|\lambda-A_{\ast}|^{2}}+D_{\ast}-\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(A_{\ast}-\lambda_{1})[(A_{\ast}-\lambda_{1})(D_{\ast}-\lambda_{1})-B_{\ast}C_{\ast}]+(D_{\ast}-\lambda_{1})\lambda_{2}^{2}}{|\lambda-A_{\ast}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(A_{\ast}-\lambda_{1})[\lambda_{1}^{2}-(A_{\ast}+D_{\ast})\lambda_{1}+A_{\ast}D_{\ast}-B_{\ast}C_{\ast}]+(D_{\ast}-\lambda_{1})\lambda_{2}^{2}}{|\lambda-A_{\ast}|^{2}}.

Recall that
A_{\ast}-\lambda_{1}\leq-c<0,D_{\ast}-\lambda_{1}\leq 0
for
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
. Moreover, assumption (
4.1
) guarantees that

\lambda_{1}^{2}-(A_{\ast}+D_{\ast})\lambda_{1}+A_{\ast}D_{\ast}-B_{\ast}C_{\ast}\geq A_{\ast}D_{\ast}-B_{\ast}C_{\ast}>0.

All in all,
A(x,\lambda)<0
for almost every
x\in\Omega
and, using monotony of the Lebesgue integral, relation (
4.4
) yields a contradiction since
\xi_{2}\not\equiv 0
. Hence, there exists no
\lambda\in\Sigma
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq 0
.
∎

4.1
Hysteresis model

We start examples with a basic reaction-diffusion-ODE model exhibiting discontinuous patterns. Introduced in
[
26
]
as a prototype of such phenomena, it is called the
hysteresis model
, because the jump-discontinuous solutions emerge due to co-existence of multiple constant stationary solutions for the ODE subsystem. In particular, it requires a hysteresis effect in the nullclines of the model nonlinearities. A minimal model with such properties takes a two-component form of (
1.1
) with the following choice of functions
f
and
g
,

\displaystyle f(u,v)=v-p(u),\qquad g(u,v)=\alpha u-\beta v

(4.5)

for
\alpha,\beta>0
, and a polynomial
p
of degree three with only one real root at
u=0
. Furthermore, we assume that there are three distinct intersection points of
f=0
and
g=0
with non-negative coordinates

\displaystyle S_{0}=(0,0),\quad S_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2}).

Additionally, we prescribe the slope
p^{\prime}(0)>\alpha/\beta
of the polynomial
p
to obtain an S-hysteresis effect which can be observed in the nullclines of the model nonlinearities regarding the
(v,u)
-plane. We choose
\Omega=(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}
. A generalization to heterogeneous environments can be found in
[
42
]
. In the remainder of this subsection, we show application of our nonlinear stability and instability results, which provides insights into the model dynamics, significantly simplifies the proofs originally provided in
[
26
]
and allows extending them from the linear to nonlinear analysis.

Let
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
be a bounded stationary solution. Then, the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
defined in (
2.5
) is given by

\displaystyle(\mathbf{L}\xi)(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
D^{v}\Delta\xi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}-p^{\prime}(\overline{u}(x))&1\\
\alpha&-\beta\end{pmatrix}\xi.

We consider two cases.

Bistable case

The polynomial
p
is strictly increasing.

Hysteresis case

The model exhibits S-hysteresis, i.e., the polynomial
p
is non-monotone with
\lim_{u\to\infty}p(u)=\infty
. Let
H=(u_{H},v_{H})
and
T=(u_{T},v_{T})
denote the local maximum and minimum of
p
, respectively. We assume that the coordinates of
H
and
T
are positive with
u_{T}>u_{H}
.

Using
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
and Lemma
4.1
, one verifies in both cases that the constant steady states
S_{0},S_{2}
are nonlinearly stable in the sense of Definition
3.1
and
S_{1}
is nonlinearly unstable for system (
1.1
) endowed with nonlinearities (
4.5
). Hence, we focus on non-homogeneous stationary solutions. Recall that in the bistable case, the equation
f(\overline{u},\overline{v})=0
can be uniquely solved with respect to
\overline{u}
, i.e.,
\overline{u}=h(\overline{v})
for some smooth function
h
. By elliptic regularity from Lemma
A.2
, the steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
is at least continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
.

Theorem 4.2

(Instability of all continuous patterns)
.

In the bistable case, every non-homogeneous stationary solution
(\overline{u},\overline{v})\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{2}
of system (
1.1
) with model nonlinearities (
4.5
) is nonlinearly unstable in the sense of Lyapunov in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
,
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
, and in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Proof.

Since
p
is strictly increasing,
K=\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}p^{\prime}(\overline{u}(x))>0
by continuity of the component
\overline{u}
. Hence, we obtain
A_{\ast}(x)\leq-c<0
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
for some
c>0
. In order to apply Corollary
3.8
, one has to prove existence of
\lambda\in\Sigma=\sigma(\mathbf{L})\cap\rho(A_{\ast})\subset\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0
. However, this is shown in the proof of
[
26
, Theorem 2.2]
for
\Omega=(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}
.
∎

We note that
[
10
, Theorem 2.8]
applies in this context to the convex domain
\Omega=(0,1)
. Hence, the single reaction-diffusion equation destabilizes the continuous steady state. However, in the hysteresis case, there exist various stable non-homogeneous patterns which are discontinuous in space.

In the hysteresis case, it is shown in
[
26
, Section 3]
for the one-dimensional case that all stationary, spatially heterogeneous solutions have a jump-discontinuity in the ODE component
\overline{u}
. For existence of such solutions, we refer to
[
26
, Section 3]
. The following result originates from
[
26
, Theorem 3.2]
. Denote by
h_{H},h_{0}
and
h_{T}
the corresponding branches of
f(\overline{u},\overline{v})=0
solved with respect to
\overline{u}
.
For
v_{j}\in(v_{T},\min\{v_{H},v_{2}\})
there exists a unique monotone increasing solution
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
with jump at
v_{j}
. Hence,
\overline{u}
is of the form

\displaystyle\overline{u}(x)=\begin{cases}h_{H}(\overline{v}(x))&\text{if}\quad\overline{v}(x)<v_{j},\\
h_{T}(\overline{v}(x))&\text{if}\quad\overline{v}(x)>v_{j}.\end{cases}

For such a steady state one can prove
s(A_{\ast})\leq 0
. However,
s(A_{\ast})\leq 0
is not sufficient for stability of the steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
. The next theorem states a sufficient condition for stability, compare its analog
[
26
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Theorem 4.3

(Nonlinear stability of a discontinuous pattern)
.

Consider a stationary solution
(\overline{u},\overline{v})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times C(\overline{\Omega})
of system (
1.1
) with model nonlinearities (
4.5
) in the hysteresis case. Assume that
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
satisfies the stability condition
K:=\operatornamewithlimits{essinf}_{x\in\Omega}p^{\prime}(\overline{u}(x))>\frac{\alpha}{\beta}
. Then,
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
is nonlinearly stable in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists
c>0
such that there holds

\displaystyle A_{\ast}

\displaystyle=-p^{\prime}(\overline{u})\leq-c,\quad D_{\ast}=-\beta\leq 0,\quad A_{\ast}D_{\ast}-B_{\ast}C_{\ast}=p^{\prime}(\overline{u})\beta-\alpha>0\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad\Omega.

Hence, we can apply Lemma
4.1
which yields
s(\mathbf{L})<0
.
Theorem
3.3
implies that
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
is stable.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

For the hysteresis model determined by (
4.5
), the nonlinear remainder
\mathbf{N}
is also locally Lipschitz continuous as an operator on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
since it is given by
\mathbf{N}(\xi)=(N_{1}(\xi_{1}),0)
. The operator
\mathbf{N}
even satisfies the decay estimate (
2.8
) in this space. Since
\mathbf{L}
generates an analytic semigroup for
p\geq 2,p>n/2
, we may apply the results
[
38
, Theorem 1]
and
[
47
, Proposition 4.17]
for Lyapunov instability and stability of a stationary solution in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
.

However, if we look at small perturbations of a stationary solution in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
, the situation is different. Although linear stability analysis is the same as for
\mathbf{L}
on the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
, the nonlinearities are not Fréchet differentiable, even not well-defined as an operator on
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
. Using
[
11
, Theorem 2.6]
, one can derive that each small neighborhood (with respect to the
L^{p}
norm) of the constant, linearly stable steady state
(0,0)
contains infinitely many discontinuous stationary solutions in the hysteresis case. Hence, the constant steady state cannot be locally asymptotically stable in
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
. It remains open whether the steady state
(0,0)
is nonlinearly stable in
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
.

4.2
DDI-hysteresis model

In this section, we consider the
DDI-hysteresis model
that, additionally to the hysteresis-based mechanism for patterns with jump-discontinuities, exhibits diffusion-driven instability (Turing instability). As the latter is a diffusion-dependent bifurcation leading to the loss of stability of a spatially constant steady state, the system describes
de novo
formation of the discontinuous patterns and can be considered as a prototype of the model of Turing mechanism leading to
far-from-equilibrium
patterns due to instability of all regular Turing patterns.
The model is given by system (
1.1
) consisting of one ordinary and one partial differential equation with nonlinearities

\displaystyle f(u,v)=-u-uv+m_{1}\frac{u^{2}}{1+ku^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad g(u,v)=-\mu v-uv+m_{2}\frac{u^{2}}{1+ku^{2}}

(4.6)

for some constants
k,\mu,m_{1},m_{2}>0
with
m_{1}>2\sqrt{k}
. The
DDI-hysteresis model
originates from
[
20
]
, for details see also
[
19
, Section 3.6.1]
. In the remainder of this subsection, we explore the underlying pattern formation phenomena applying the developed theory. In particular, we show nonlinear stability of the jump-discontinuous solutions, which originally was shown only for the linearized model in a restrictive setting of
(\varepsilon,A)
stability.

For a bounded steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
, the linear operator defined in Lemma
2.1
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
D^{v}\Delta\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}+J(\overline{u},\overline{v})\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where the Jacobian reads

\displaystyle J(\overline{u},\overline{v})=\begin{pmatrix}-(1+\overline{v})+m_{1}\frac{2\overline{u}}{(1+k\overline{u}^{2})^{2}}&-\overline{u}\\
-\overline{v}+m_{2}\frac{2\overline{u}}{(1+k\overline{u}^{2})^{2}}&-(\mu+\overline{v})\end{pmatrix}.

Following
[
20
, Section 3.2]
, we compute non-negative steady states
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
. Since a stationary solution
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
satisfies in particular
f(\overline{u},\overline{v})\equiv 0
, it holds
\overline{u}(x)=0
or

\displaystyle-(1+\overline{v}(x))(1+k\overline{u}(x)^{2})+m_{1}\overline{u}(x)=0

(4.7)

for
x\in\Omega
. Hence, there are three branches of solutions for
\overline{u}
, namely
u_{0}(v):=0
defined for all
v\geq 0
, and

\displaystyle u_{\pm}(v):=\frac{1}{2k(1+v)}\left(m_{1}\pm\sqrt{m_{1}^{2}-4k(1+v)^{2}}\right)

defined for
0\leq v\leq v_{r}:=\frac{m_{1}}{2\sqrt{k}}-1
. We have
v_{r}>0
since
m_{1}>2\sqrt{k}
.
It holds
u_{-}(v)u_{+}(v)=\frac{1}{k}
,

\displaystyle u_{+}(v)\geq\frac{m_{1}}{2k(1+v)}\geq\frac{m_{1}}{2k(1+v_{r})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}},

and
u_{+}(v)>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
for
v<v_{r}
. This yields
0<u_{-}(v)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
for
0\leq v\leq v_{r}
and
u_{-}(v)<\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
for
0\leq v<v_{r}
. We obtain the following instability result, compare
[
20
, Section 3.3]
.

Theorem 4.5

(Instability of patterns)
.

Let
(\overline{u},\overline{v})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times C(\overline{\Omega})
,
\overline{u},\overline{v}\geq 0
, be a stationary solution of problem (
1.1
) with the nonlinearities
f
and
g
given by equation (
4.6
) and
m_{1}>2\sqrt{k}
.
If there exists a measurable subset
M\subset\Omega
,
|M|>0
, such that for almost every
x\in M
the stationary solution
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
is of class
u_{-}
and
\overline{v}(x)<v_{r}
resp.
\overline{u}(x)<\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
, then the steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
is nonlinearly unstable in the sense of Definition
3.1
and in the sense of Lyapunov in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
.

Proof.

To investigate stability of the steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
, we first analyze the spectrum of the multiplication operator
A_{\ast}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{\infty}(\Omega))
induced by

\displaystyle A_{\ast}(x)=-(1+\overline{v}(x))+m_{1}\frac{2\overline{u}(x)}{(1+k\overline{u}(x)^{2})^{2}},\qquad x\in\Omega.

By
[
28
, Appendix B.2]
, there exists a null set
N\subset\Omega
such that

\sigma(A_{\ast})=\overline{\{A_{\ast}(x)\mid x\in\Omega\setminus N\}}.

For
x\in\Omega
, the steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
is of the form
\overline{u}(x)=u_{i}(\overline{v}(x))
for
i\in\{0,-,+\}
. If
\overline{u}(x)=u_{0}(\overline{v}(x))=0
, then
A_{\ast}(x)=-(1+\overline{v}(x))\leq-1<0
and
s(A_{\ast})<0
. For
\overline{u}(x)\neq 0
, we may use relation (
4.7
).
In the case of
\overline{u}(x)=u_{-}(\overline{v}(x))
, this yields

\displaystyle A_{\ast}(x)

\displaystyle=(1+\overline{v}(x))\left(-1+\frac{2}{1+k\overline{u}(x)^{2}}\right)\geq-1+\frac{2}{1+k(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}})^{2}}\geq 0

and
A_{\ast}(x)>0
if
\overline{v}(x)\neq v_{r}
, i.e.,
\overline{u}(x)\neq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
.
In the remaining case
\overline{u}(x)=u_{+}(\overline{v}(x))
, we obtain
A_{\ast}(x)\leq 0
and
A_{\ast}(x)<0
if
\overline{v}(x)\neq v_{r}
, i.e.,
\overline{u}(x)\neq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
. In summary, it follows
s(A_{\ast})>0
from above calculations and, by Proposition
2.3
,
s(\mathbf{L})>0
. Finally, apply Theorem
3.7
.
∎

To get a statement about stability, we further have to study the remainder of the spectrum, i.e.,
\Sigma=\sigma(\mathbf{L})\cap\rho(A_{\ast})\subset\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})
, which is discrete since
\rho(A_{\ast})
connected. We aim to apply Lemma
4.1
in this case. The following theorem can be compared to the stability results obtained in
[
19
, Theorem 3.21]
.

Theorem 4.6

(Nonlinear stability of patterns)
.

Let
(\overline{u},\overline{v})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times C(\overline{\Omega})
,
\overline{u},\overline{v}\geq 0
, be a stationary solution of system (
1.1
) with functions
f
and
g
given by (
4.6
). We assume that
\overline{u}
is only of class
u_{0}
and
u_{+}
and, moreover,
u_{s}:=\mathrm{essinf}_{x\in\Omega,\;\overline{u}(x)\text{ of class }u_{+}}\overline{u}(x)>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
for
2\sqrt{k}<m_{1}<m_{2}
. Then, the stationary solution
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
is nonlinearly stable in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Proof.

In order to apply Theorem
3.3
, we prove
s(\mathbf{L})<0
with the help of Lemma
4.1
. For
x\in\Omega
with
\overline{u}(x)=u_{0}(x)
, we already know
A_{\ast}(x)\leq-1<0
. Furthermore, it holds
D_{\ast}(x)\leq 0
.
For the determinant of
J(\overline{u},\overline{v})
, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{\ast}(x)D_{\ast}(x)-B_{\ast}(x)C_{\ast}(x)

\displaystyle=(1+\overline{v}(x))(\mu+\overline{v}(x))=\mu>0,

since
\mu,D^{v}>0
imply
\overline{v}=0
by uniqueness of solutions of the stationary elliptic problem.

Next, let us consider
x\in\Omega
with
\overline{u}(x)=u_{+}(x)
. Since

\overline{u}(x)=u_{+}(\overline{v}(x))\geq u_{s}>1/\sqrt{k}

holds for a.e.
x
, we estimate

\displaystyle A_{\ast}(x)

\displaystyle\leq-1+\frac{2}{1+ku_{s}^{2}}=:-\tilde{c}<0\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{\ast}(x)=-(\mu+\overline{v}(x))\leq 0.

(4.8)

It is left to investigate the determinant of
J(\overline{u},\overline{v})(x)
.
Using chain rule, we derive

\displaystyle\det J(u_{+}(v),v)

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}f(u_{+}(v),v)\partial_{v}g(u_{+}(v),v)-\partial_{v}f(u_{+}(v),v)\partial_{u}g(u_{+}(v),v)

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}f(u_{+}(v),v)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}g(u_{+}(v),v)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}u_{+}(v)\partial_{u}g(u_{+}(v),v)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\partial_{v}f(u_{+}(v),v)\partial_{u}g(u_{+}(v),v)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{u}g(u_{+}(v),v)\left(\partial_{u}f(u_{+}(v),v)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}u_{+}(v)+\partial_{v}f(u_{+}(v),v)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{u}f(u_{+}(v),v)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}g(u_{+}(v),v)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{u}g(u_{+}(v),v)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}f(u_{+}(v),v)+\partial_{u}f(u_{+}(v),v)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}g(u_{+}(v),v)

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}f(u_{+}(v),v)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}g(u_{+}(v),v),

where we used
f(u_{+}(v),v)=0
in the last step. First, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}u_{+}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{-u_{+}(v)}{1+v}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{m_{1}^{2}-4k(1+v)^{2}}}\leq\frac{-u_{+}(v)}{1+v}.

Since
m_{2}>m_{1}>0
and
v\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}g(u_{+}(v),v)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}\left[-\mu v+u_{+}(v)\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}v-v+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\mu+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}u_{+}(v)\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}(1+v)-v\right)+u_{+}(v)\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\mu-\frac{1}{1+v}u_{+}(v)\leq-\mu<0.

In view of (
4.8
), we find a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle A_{\ast}(x)\leq-c,\quad D_{\ast}(x)\leq 0\quad\text{and}\quad A_{\ast}(x)D_{\ast}(x)-B_{\ast}(x)C_{\ast}(x)\geq c

holds for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.
An application of Lemma
4.1
yields
s(\mathbf{L})<0
. Finally, Theorem
3.3
implies stability of the steady state
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
.
∎

Appendix A
Heat semigroup

The preceding sections are based on properties of the heat semigroup. We define the heat semigroup
(S_{\Delta}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
according to
[
13
,
34
]
and study basic properties on the spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
C(\overline{\Omega})
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega\in C^{0,1}
,
z^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique variational solution
z\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};L^{2}(\Omega))
of the homogeneous heat equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}-\Delta z

\displaystyle=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{>0},\qquad z(\cdot,0)=z^{0}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\qquad\frac{\partial z}{\partial\mathbf{n}}=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

The solution is given by the heat semigroup
(S_{\Delta}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\subset\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega))
,

\displaystyle z(x,t)=(S_{\Delta}(t)z^{0})(x),\qquad x\in\Omega.

(A.1)

Moreover, the heat semigroup defined by (
A.1
) can be extended to a contraction semigroup
(S_{\Delta,p}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
on
L^{p}(\Omega)
for each
1\leq p\leq\infty
, which is strongly continuous for
1\leq p<\infty
. For
1<p<\infty
, the semigroup is analytic and
S_{\Delta,p}(t)
is compact for
t>0
. Consequently, the generator
A_{p}:\mathcal{D}(A_{p})\subset L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega)
of
(S_{\Delta,p}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
has compact resolvent for
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

Contractivity of the semigroup is shown in
[
13
, Theorem 1.3.9]
. By
[
13
, Theorems 1.4.1, 1.4.2]
,
(S_{\Delta}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is a strongly continuous semigroup for each
1\leq p<\infty
, which is even analytic for
p>1
. Compactness of the semigroup operators follows from
[
13
, Theorem 1.6.3]
. Finally, compactness of the resolvent
R(\lambda,A_{p})
for each
\lambda\in\rho(A_{p})
is a consequence of
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 4.29]
.
∎

For
A_{2}
, the domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{2})
is given in
[
13
]
by the following characterization of a weak solution, i.e.,

\displaystyle w\in\mathcal{D}(A_{2})

\displaystyle\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\;\text{and there exists a function}\;f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;\text{such that}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad(\nabla w,\nabla\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(f,\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\;\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega).

As usual in the weak sense, we identify
A_{2}w=f
and obtain the graph norm estimate

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}=\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(f,w)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|A_{2}w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\quad\forall\;w\in\mathcal{D}(A_{2}).

From this characterization it is clear that the set

H_{N}^{2}(\Omega):=\{w\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)\mid\partial_{\mathbf{n}}w=0\;\text{a.e. in}\;\Omega\}

is included in
\mathcal{D}(A_{2})
. In general, elliptic regularity is restricted for low-regular boundaries such as
\partial\Omega\in C^{0,1}

[
16
, Section 4.4.3]
. By the characterization of contraction semigroups via maximal dissipative generators in
[
5
, Theorem 3.4.5]
, we obtain
H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)\subsetneq\mathcal{D}(A_{2})
for certain Lipschitz boundaries. As shown in
[
52
, Theorem 2.15]
, there holds
\mathcal{D}(A_{2})=H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)
in case of
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}
since
[
16
, Theorem 2.4.1.3]
is used.

Let us next characterize the domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{p})
of the generator
A_{p}
of the heat semigroup on
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
p>2
. We mention that, for Lipschitz domains, we might have elements in
\mathcal{D}(A_{p})
which are not in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, although these functions are Hölder continuous
[
33
,
50
]
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
and the semigroup
(S_{\Delta,p}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
be defined as in Proposition
A.1
with generator
A_{p}:\mathcal{D}(A_{p})\subset L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then,
A_{p}
is the part of the operator
A_{2}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(A_{p})=\{w\in\mathcal{D}(A_{2})\cap L^{p}(\Omega)\mid A_{2}w\in L^{p}(\Omega)\}.

(A.2)

Moreover, for
p>n/2
, the embedding
\mathcal{D}(A_{p})\hookrightarrow C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
is continuous for some
\gamma>0
with

\displaystyle\|w\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\left(\|w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|A_{p}w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)\qquad\forall\;w\in\mathcal{D}(A_{p}).

(A.3)

Proof.

Since the semigroups on
L^{p}(\Omega)
are consistent due to
[
13
, Theorem 1.4.1]
, we obtain that the heat semigroup on
L^{p}(\Omega)
is the restriction of the semigroup
(S_{\Delta}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
defined on
L^{2}(\Omega)
onto
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
p\geq 2
. Hence,
[
35
, Ch. 4, Theorem 5.5]
shows equality (
A.2
).

The domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{2})
is implicitly defined by an elliptic problem. Hence, we focus on the problem
A_{2}w=f
for
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
in order to prove an embedding for
\mathcal{D}(A_{p})
endowed with the graph norm. We apply
[
33
, Proposition 3.6]
for
p>n/2
to obtain an embedding into the Hölder space
C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
for a certain
\gamma>0
which depends on
\Omega
. Estimate (
A.3
) follows from the continuous embedding
L^{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
for
p\geq 2
.
∎

Next we consider a restriction of the Laplace operator to the space
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, i.e., we define

\displaystyle A_{\infty}:\mathcal{D}(A_{\infty})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)

as the part of
A_{p}
defined in Lemma
A.2
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. From

\mathcal{D}(A_{\infty})=\{u\in\mathcal{D}(A_{p})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\mid A_{p}u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\}

we infer that this operator is again closed as the generator
A_{p}
of the heat semigroup is closed on
L^{p}(\Omega)

[
5
, Section 3.10, p. 184]
. However,
A_{\infty}
is not densely defined due to the inclusion
\mathcal{D}(A_{p})\subset C(\overline{\Omega})
from Lemma
A.2
. This means that
(A_{\infty},\mathcal{D}(A_{\infty}))
cannot be a generator of a strongly continuous semigroup by
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 1.4]
. Note that continuity of the heat semigroup restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
only fails in
t=0
and also analyticity is preserved if we exclude
t=0
. In fact, following
[
29
, Corollary 3.1.24]
, there exists a corresponding resolvent estimate for
A_{\infty}
as for sectorial operators. Unfortunately, this estimate is shown under higher regularity assumptions on the boundary. But using heat kernel estimates from
[
34
]
, we derive a corresponding resolvent estimate next.

Lemma A.3
.

The operator
(A_{\infty},\mathcal{D}(A_{\infty}))
is closed on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, there exist an angle
\frac{\pi}{2}<\omega<\pi
, a closed sector
\Sigma_{\omega}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\mathrm{arg}\,\lambda|\leq\omega\}
and a constant
M>0
such that
\Sigma_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}\subset\rho(A_{\infty})
holds with the resolvent estimate

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,A_{\infty})\|\leq\frac{M}{|\lambda|}\qquad\forall\;\lambda\in\Sigma_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.

It is well-known that there exists such a resolvent estimate for the spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
p<\infty

[
52
, Theorem 2.12]
. Nevertheless, constants in these estimates deteriorate as
p\to\infty
. Hence, we choose another strategy which is used for continuous functions in
[
43
, Section 5.5]
. Therefore, let us start with the analytic heat semigroup
(S_{\Delta}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
generated by the Laplace operator defined on
L^{2}(\Omega)
, see Proposition
A.1
.
[
13
, Theorem 2.4.4]
shows that
(S_{\Delta}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is hypercontractive and maps
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for each
t>0
. Thus for each
t>0
,
x\in\Omega
, the map
z\mapsto S_{\Delta}(t)z(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{\ast}
can be represented by a function
p(t,x,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the so called heat kernel, such that

(S_{\Delta}(t)z)(x)=\int_{\Omega}p(t,x,y)z(y)\;\mathrm{d}y\qquad\forall\;z\in L^{2}(\Omega),x\in\Omega.

We use an estimation of the heat kernel from
[
13
, Theorem 3.2.9]
, more precisely, there exist constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}>0
such that

0\leq p(t,x,y)\leq C_{1}m(t)^{-n/2}\exp\left(\frac{-|x-y|^{2}}{C_{2}t}\right)\qquad\forall\;x,y\in\Omega,t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

where
m(t)=\min\{1,t\}
. By
[
34

, this estimate can be extended to some sector

\displaystyle R(\lambda,A_{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}S_{\Delta}(\tau)\;\mathrm{d}\tau\quad\forall\;\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0

Appendix B
Spectral characterization of the reaction-diffusion-ODE operator

We already characterized the spectrum of the operator
\mathbf{L}
in Proposition
2.3
by splitting the spectrum into the two components

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{L})=\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\mathop{\dot{\cup}}\Sigma

where
\Sigma
is a set of eigenvalues and
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is the spectrum of the corresponding multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}
. In the case where the resolvent set
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is connected, then
[
24
, Theorem 10.1.3 (i)]
additionally yields that
\Sigma
is a discrete set of eigenvalues. However, when
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is not a connected set,
\Sigma
might include a non-discrete set of the point spectrum of
\mathbf{L}

[
18
, Example 4.3]
. We aim to further characterize the set
\Sigma
of eigenvalues of
\mathbf{L}
and to show that the spectral bound of
\mathbf{L}
is determined by the spectral bound
s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
and the spectral bound of a discrete set of eigenvalues within the unbounded connected component
\Lambda_{\infty}
of
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{D}^{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{k\times k}
. Let
\mathbf{L}
be the partly diffusive operator defined in (
2.5
) on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
for bounded coefficient matrices
\mathbf{A}_{\ast},\mathbf{B}_{\ast},\mathbf{C}_{\ast},\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
and a finite
n^{\ast}<p<\infty
. Then

\Sigma:=\sigma(\mathbf{L})\cap\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})=\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})\;\dot{\cup}\;\Sigma_{0}

where
\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
is the discrete spectrum of
\mathbf{L}
and
\Sigma_{0}
is a union of bounded (open) connected components of
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
consisting of non-isolated eigenvalues of
\mathbf{L}
. In case that
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is a connected set in
\mathbb{C}
, then
\Sigma_{0}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

By calculations in Corollary
2.4
we infer that, for
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
, invertibility of
\lambda I-\mathbf{L}
is equivalent to invertibility of the elliptic operator

\displaystyle\lambda I-\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta-\mathbf{D}_{\ast}-\mathbf{C}_{\ast}(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}_{\ast})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{\ast}.

To distinguish discrete parts within the spectral set
\Sigma=\sigma(\mathbf{L})\cap\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
we apply an analytic Fredholm theorem. For this reason we use that invertibility of this elliptic operator is equivalent to invertibility of
I-\mathbf{C}(\lambda)
where

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(\lambda)=R(\lambda_{0},L_{1})\left((\lambda_{0}-\lambda)I+\mathbf{C}_{\ast}(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}_{\ast})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{\ast}\right)

(B.1)

for some
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(L_{1})
,
L_{1}:=\mathbf{D}^{v}\Delta+\mathbf{D}_{\ast}
. From
[
13
, Theorem 1.6.3]
and
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 4.29]
, we obtain compactness of the resolvent
R(\lambda_{0},L_{1})
for
L_{1}
defined on
L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
. Hence,
\mathbf{C}(\lambda)
is compact for each
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
and
\mathbf{C}
is analytic since the resolvent mapping of
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
is.
Note that for
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\cap\rho(L_{1})
the operator
\mathbf{C}(\lambda)
can be chosen similar to
[
18
, Theorem 4.2]
, i.e.,

\mathbf{C}(\lambda)=R(\lambda,L_{1})\mathbf{C}_{\ast}(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}_{\ast})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{\ast}.

In the case where
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is connected, we have seen in Proposition
2.3
that
\Sigma_{0}=\emptyset
. In the case where the open set
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is not connected, there exists an at most countable number of open connected components
\Lambda_{i}
,
i\in I
, which are pairwise disjoint and form a partition of
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
. The components are chosen to be maximal, i.e. for all
i,j\in I
with
i\neq j
the set
\Lambda_{i}\cup\Lambda_{j}
is not connected. Moreover, there exists exactly one unbounded connected component denoted by
\Lambda_{\infty}
since
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is bounded. The remaining
\Lambda_{i}
,
i\in I\setminus\{\infty\}
, are bounded components. Now, we are able to apply the analytic Fredholm theorem on each of the components
\Lambda_{i}
. Either there holds
1\in\sigma(\mathbf{C}(\lambda))
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
or there exists
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
such that
1\in\rho(\mathbf{C}(\lambda))
.

In the first case, we recall from Proposition
2.3
that
\Lambda_{i}\subset\Sigma
is a set of eigenvalues of
\mathbf{L}
. As
\Lambda_{i}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
,
\Lambda_{i}\subset\sigma_{e6}(\mathcal{L})
is a set of non-isolated eigenvalues, hence
\Lambda_{i}\cap\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})=\emptyset
. We define the open set
\Sigma_{0}
as the disjoint union of all such
\Lambda_{i}
.

In the second case where
I-\mathbf{C}(\lambda)
is invertible for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
, the Gohberg-Shmul’yan theorem
[
24
, Theorem 2.5.13]
applies and shows that
\Lambda_{i}\cap\Sigma
is a discrete set of eigenvalues of
\mathbf{L}
. Due to the separation of the connected components by the closed set
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
, eigenvalues
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}\cap\Sigma
are also isolated values of
\sigma(\mathbf{L})
. Since
\lambda I-\mathbf{L}
is a Fredholm operator of index zero, we find
\Lambda_{i}\cap\Sigma\subset\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
for each such component
\Lambda_{i}
. We recall from Proposition
2.3
that
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\cap\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})=\emptyset
and, hence, we characterized all parts of
\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
.

Note that in any case, the unbounded connected component
\Lambda_{\infty}
satisfies
\Lambda_{\infty}\cap\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})\subset\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
. In fact, since the multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
is bounded, the operator
I-\mathbf{C}(\lambda)
resp.
\lambda I-\mathbf{L}
is invertible for some large
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{L})\cap\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
by
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 1.10]
.
∎

When
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
is connected, then
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
coincides with the only unbounded connected component
\Lambda_{\infty}
. Then it is well-known that the spectral bound of the reaction-diffusion-ODE operator
\mathbf{L}
is determined by the spectral bound of
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
and the spectral bound of the discrete part
\Sigma=\Lambda_{\infty}\cap\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})

[
32
]
. In general, the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbf{L})
might consist of further discrete or non-discrete bounded components of
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
. However, their spectral bound can be controlled in terms of the spectral bound of the surrounding spectrum
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
.

Corollary B.2
.

Let the assumptions of Proposition
B.1
hold true and denote by
\Lambda_{\infty}
the unbounded open connected component of
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
. Further, let us denote by

s_{\infty}:=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}\cap\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})\}

the spectral bound of the discrete set of eigenvalues within
\Lambda_{\infty}
.
Then the spectral bound of
\mathbf{L}
is determined by

\displaystyle s(\mathbf{L})=\max\{s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast}),s_{\infty}\}.

(B.2)

Proof.

The spectral bound of
\mathbf{L}
is given by
s(\mathbf{L})=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\mid\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{L})\}.
Let us decompose
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
again into open connected components
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
, where exactly one of these is unbounded, denoted by
\Lambda_{\infty}
. We define

s_{b}=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\cap\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})\;\text{for some bounded connected component}\;\Lambda\;\text{of}\;\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})\}.

By Proposition
2.3
and Proposition
B.1
it is clear that

s(\mathbf{L})=\max\{s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast}),s_{\infty},s_{b}\}.

This proves
\max\{s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast}),s_{\infty}\}\leq s(\mathbf{L})
. For the reverse inequality we show
s_{b}\leq s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
.
Therefore, let us take a bounded open connected component
\Lambda
of the resolvent set
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
which is completely surrounded by
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
. Then
s(\Lambda):=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
cannot be attained within the open set
\Lambda
, however, the supremum is attained on the boundary
\partial\Lambda
which is a subset of
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
. Thus, we have
s_{b}\leq s(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
which proves the claim.
∎

Let us note that the discrete sets
\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
or
\Lambda_{\infty}\cap\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L})
are not necessarily closed. However, all accumulation points in
\mathbb{C}
lie on the boundary of the connected components in which the discrete sets are located. Since this boundary is a subset of
\partial\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
, we infer that these accumulation points of
\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{L})
are included in
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\ast})
. Compare also
[
25
, Section 2.1.2]
in the case
\Sigma_{0}=\emptyset
.

Appendix C
Existence of mild solutions

In the case of continuous functions, existence and uniqueness of mild solutions for the corresponding equation (
2.1
) is well established
[
35
, Ch. 6, Theorem 1.4]
. In this section, we present a proper notion and an existence and uniqueness result of a mild solution to system (
2.1
) with discontinuous components, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial t}=\mathbf{L}\xi+\mathbf{N}(\xi),\qquad\xi(0)=\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}.

This is in accordance with the theory developed in
[
37
]
. The analytic semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
from Lemma
2.1
can be restricted to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
by Lemma
2.11
. However, strong continuity of the semigroup is lost in the limit
p\to\infty

[
37
, Part I, Lemma 2]
. Nevertheless, due to regularizing effects of the heat semigroup, a solution to equation (
2.1
) can be obtained by a Picard iteration in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}

[
37
, Part II, Theorem 1]
. The result in
[
37
]
is based on an integral representation

\displaystyle\xi(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{S}(t)\xi^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-\tau)\big(\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))+\mathbf{J}\xi(\cdot,\tau)\big)\;\mathrm{d}\tau,

(C.1)

with the analytic contraction semigroup
(\mathbf{S}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
generated by
\mathbf{D}\Delta
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k}
, see (
2.4
). In order to relate stability properties of the steady state to its linearization
\mathbf{L}
, it is necessary to obtain an integral representation with the analytic semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
generated by
\mathbf{L}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\xi(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{T}(t)\xi^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{T}(t-\tau)\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

(C.2)

To show existence and uniqueness of a mild solution, we use estimate (
2.8
) for the nonlinear term
\mathbf{N}
as well as growth estimate (
2.15
) for the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
. A solution of equation (
2.1
) is understood analog to the mild sense of Rothe, compare
[
37
, Part II, Definition 2]
.

Definition C.1
.

Let
0<T\leq\infty
. A mild solution of problem (
2.1
) on the interval
[0,T)
for initial datum
\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
is a measurable function

\xi:\Omega\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{m+k}

satisfying for all
t\in(0,T)

(i)

\xi(\cdot,t)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
and
\sup_{s\in(0,t)}\|\xi(\cdot,s)\|_{\infty}<\infty
;

(ii)

the integral representation

\xi(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{T}(t)\xi^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{T}(t-\tau)\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,\tau))\;\mathrm{d}\tau,

where the integral is an absolutely converging Bochner integral in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
.

The local-in-time mild solution fulfills
\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})^{m+k}
for each finite
T<T_{\max}
, where
[0,T_{\max})
is the maximal time interval of existence derived in the next Proposition
C.2
. This is achieved via a Picard iteration in the Banach space
(L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
, similar to
[
37
, Part II, Theorem 1]
. Boundedness of the solution and norm-continuity of the analytic semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
for

yields a mild solution
\xi\in C([0,T_{\max});L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{k})
. The regularity of the semigroup for

even implies
\xi\in C((0,T_{\max});L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k})
, as a consequence of Lemma
2.11
. We refer to Remark
3.2
for more comments on the regularity of solutions depending on the regularity of the initial condition.

Proposition C.2
.

Let the nonlinear operator
\mathbf{N}
defined in (

) satisfy the local Lipschitz condition
1
and let the semigroup
(\mathbf{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}

satisfy the growth estimate
\|\mathbf{T}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{wt}
for some

(i)

For all
\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
there exists a

(ii)

If we consider

0<\inf\{T(\xi^{0})\mid\xi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k},\|\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}\leq U_{0}\}

(iii)

The existence interval can be chosen to be maximal, i.e.,
\mathrm{(i)}

Let

for some

.
Choose an arbitrary real number

and consider the bounded set

by Assumption

. Herein, we denote

the open ball with radius

around
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+k}
. Choose

such that

MU_{0}\mathrm{e}^{wT}+\mathrm{e}^{(L(B)M+w)T}-1\leq U,

(C.3)

where this particular choice is due to the following proof. As the case

is already considered in
[

, we assume

in the following.

We use a Picard iteration to obtain the mild solution

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{T}(t)\xi^{0},\qquad\xi_{i+1}(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{T}(t)\xi^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{T}(t-s)\mathbf{N}(\xi_{i}(\cdot,s))\;\mathrm{d}s

(C.4)

for

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\xi_{i}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}&\leq U,\\

(C.5)

for all

and

via induction. Norm-continuity of the semigroup

(\mathbf{T}_{\infty}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
yields

\|\xi_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}=\|\mathbf{T}(t)\xi^{0}\|_{\infty}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{wt}U_{0}<U\qquad\text{for}\quad t\in[0,T],

where we used the choice of
T
in (
C.3
). Continuity of
\mathbf{N}
implies that
\mathbf{N}\circ\xi_{1}
is also (Bochner) measurable. Estimates for the nonlinearity from Assumption
1
lead us to integrability, i.e.,
s\mapsto\mathbf{N}(\xi_{1}(\cdot,s))\in L^{1}([0,T];L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k})
. By
[
5
, Proposition 1.3.4]
and continuity of the semigroup from Lemma
2.11
, the convolution in definition of
\xi_{2}
is well-defined as a Bochner integral in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
or
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m}\times C(\overline{\Omega})^{k}
with the estimate

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}M\mathrm{e}^{w(t-s)}\|\mathbf{N}(\xi_{1}(\cdot,s))\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq L(B)M\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{w(t-s)}\;\mathrm{d}s=\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{ws}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha+w)s}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha+w)t}-1

since
\alpha=ML(B)\geq 0
and
w\geq 0
.
This proves the base case for estimates (
C.5
). If the estimates hold true for a particular
i\in\mathbb{N}
, this implies

\displaystyle\eta_{i+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}M\mathrm{e}^{w(t-s)}\|\mathbf{N}(\xi_{i+1}(\cdot,s))-\mathbf{N}(\xi_{i}(\cdot,s))\|_{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{w(t-s)}\eta_{i}(s)\;\mathrm{d}s\leq\alpha^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{w(t-s)}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(\alpha r)^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}\mathrm{e}^{wr}\;\mathrm{d}r\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{w}\mathrm{e}^{wt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(\alpha r)^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}\;\mathrm{d}r-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{w}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(\alpha r)^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}\mathrm{e}^{wr}\;\mathrm{d}r=\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(\alpha s)^{i}}{i!}\mathrm{e}^{ws}\;\mathrm{d}s,

where we used partial integration twice. We demonstrated only the case
w>0
,
w=0
is even simpler.
By definition of
\xi_{i+1}
in (
C.4
) and
T
in (
C.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\xi_{i+1}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq M\mathrm{e}^{wt}U_{0}+ML(B)\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{w(t-s)}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq M\mathrm{e}^{wT}U_{0}+ML(B)\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(ML(B)+w)s}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq M\mathrm{e}^{wT}U_{0}+\mathrm{e}^{(L(B)M+w)T}-1\leq U

for all
t\in[0,T]
, since
\alpha=ML(B)\geq 0
and
w\geq 0
. Finally, it is left to prove the estimate for the sum of
\eta_{j}
in (
C.5
). The induction step follows from

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}\eta_{j}(t)\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}\frac{(\alpha s)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\mathrm{e}^{ws}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha+w)s}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha+w)t}-1.

For fixed
t\in[0,T]
, these results imply convergence of

\xi_{i+1}(\cdot,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}(\xi_{j+1}-\xi_{j})(\cdot,t)+\xi_{1}(\cdot,t)\to\xi(\cdot,t):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(\xi_{j+1}-\xi_{j})(\cdot,t)+\xi_{1}(\cdot,t)

as
i\to\infty
. The latter series converges absolutely in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k}
, i.e.,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|(\xi_{j+1}-\xi_{j})(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\eta_{j}(t)\leq\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha+w)t}-1,

by convergence of the partial sums in (
C.5
). We even have convergence
\xi_{i}\to\xi
in the Banach space
L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})^{m+k}
and
L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{m+k})
. This is a consequence of the estimates

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(\xi_{i}-\xi)(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\eta_{j}(t)\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(\alpha s)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\mathrm{e}^{ws}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\alpha s}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}\frac{(\alpha s)^{j}}{(j)!}\right)\mathrm{e}^{ws}\;\mathrm{d}s\to 0\quad(i\to\infty).

where the exponential series converges uniformly on the bounded interval
[0,T]
.
Using the above estimate of
\xi_{1}
and inequalities (
C.5
), we obtain an estimate for
\xi
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\xi(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha+w)t}-1+M\mathrm{e}^{wt}U_{0}\right)\leq U.

Due to uniform convergence, the limit
\xi
is (Bochner) measurable and a mild solution of the integral equation

\xi(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{T}(t)\xi^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{T}(t-s)\mathbf{N}(\xi(\cdot,s))\;\mathrm{d}s\qquad\forall\;t\in[0,T].

Uniqueness of mild solutions follows from Gronwall’s inequality and local Lipschitz continuity from Assumption
1
. For the proof of (ii) and (iii), we refer to
[
37
, p. 114]
.
∎

Equivalence of the implicit integral formulations (
C.1
) and (
C.2
) can be shown using

, Equation (4.100)]
for

,
\mathbf{D}\Delta

. Continuity of

Appendix D
Multiplication operator on uniformly continuous functions

Each matrix-valued function
\mathbf{A}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m\times m}
induces a corresponding multiplication operator

\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{A}}:C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}\to C(\overline{\Omega})^{m},\qquad\mathbf{z}\mapsto\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}

where
(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z})(x):=\mathbf{A}(x)\mathbf{z}(x)
for each
\mathbf{z}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
. Since
\|\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{A}}\|\leq\|\mathbf{A}\|_{\infty}
, this is a bounded, linear operator. Let us simply write
\mathbf{A}
instead of
\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{A}}
in the following. The knowledge of the spectrum of a multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}
allows us to characterize the spectrum of the partly diffusive operator
\mathbf{L}^{c}
in Proposition
2.10
.

Lemma D.1
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m\times m}
for
m\in\mathbb{N},
and let
\mathbf{A}
denote its corresponding bounded multiplication operator on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
. Then the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbf{A})
is essential in the sense that each
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{A})
is characterized by the property

\displaystyle\forall\,\varepsilon>0\;\exists\,\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega\;\text{open set}\;\exists\,\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}:\|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A})\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})^{m}}\leq\varepsilon|\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}|.

(D.1)

This means
\sigma(\mathbf{A})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\text{condition}\;\eqref{essspectrumC2}\;\text{is satisfied}\}=:A_{\mathrm{ess}}
and, furthermore,
\sigma(\mathbf{A})
consists of approximate eigenvalues only.

Proof.

First, let us verify
A_{\mathrm{ess}}\subset\sigma_{ap}(\mathbf{A})
. We assume
\lambda\in A_{\mathrm{ess}}
and consider the sequence
(\xi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\subset C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
defined by

\xi_{\varepsilon}=\eta_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}/|\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}|

for a
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
with
0\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
and
\eta_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
on some small ball
\overline{B_{\delta}(y)}\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}

[
8
, Lemma 9.3]
. This construction implies
\|\xi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}}=1
and

\|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A})\xi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}}=\|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A})\xi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})^{m}}\leq\varepsilon.

Thus,
\lambda\in A_{\mathrm{ess}}
is also an approximate eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}
.

In order to show
\sigma(\mathbf{A})\subset A_{\mathrm{ess}}
, we verify the converse inclusion
\mathbb{C}\setminus A_{\mathrm{ess}}\subset\rho(\mathbf{A})
. For
\lambda\notin A_{\mathrm{ess}}
, we find a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for all open sets
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
and vectors
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
there holds

\|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A})(\cdot)\mathbf{v}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega_{0}})^{m}}\geq\varepsilon|\mathbf{v}|.

By contradiction, this implies the pointwise estimate

|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A})(x)\mathbf{v}|\geq\varepsilon|\mathbf{v}|\qquad\forall\;x\in\overline{\Omega},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Consequently, the matrices
(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x))^{-1}
imply a bounded multiplication operator on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
which turns out to be the inverse of
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}
, hence
\lambda\in\rho(\mathbf{A})
.
∎

Proposition D.2
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m\times m}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the spectrum of the bounded multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}
on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{A})=\overline{\bigcup_{x\in\Omega}\sigma(\mathbf{A}(x))}.

(D.2)

Proof.

In order to verify equality (
D.2
), we use a continuity argument. Recall that
\sigma(\mathbf{A})
is a closed set in
\mathbb{C}
by
[
5
, Corollary B.3]
. In view of the closedness of
A_{\mathrm{ess}}=\sigma(\mathbf{A})
, it suffices to show

\bigcup_{x\in\Omega}\sigma(\mathbf{A}(x))\subset A_{\mathrm{ess}}

to obtain one inclusion in (
D.2
). For this reason consider
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{A}(x_{0}))
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Then there exists a normalized eigenvector
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
|\mathbf{v}_{0}|=1
such that
(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x_{0}))\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
. By continuity, we obtain a small neighborhood
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega
of
x_{0}
such that

\sup_{x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x))\mathbf{v}_{0}|\leq\varepsilon=\varepsilon|\mathbf{v}_{0}|,

hence
\lambda\in A_{\mathrm{ess}}
.

For the reverse inclusion in (
D.2
), we show that
\lambda\notin\overline{\bigcup_{x\in\Omega}\sigma(\mathbf{A}(x))}
implies
\lambda\notin A_{\mathrm{ess}}
. Indeed, if
\lambda
is not in the closed set
\overline{\bigcup_{x\in\Omega}\sigma(\mathbf{A}(x))}
and
x\in\Omega
, every matrix
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x)
is invertible in
\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
, i.e., we find a constant
C_{x}>0
such that

|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x))\mathbf{v}|\geq C_{x}|\mathbf{v}|\qquad\forall\;\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Let us verify that there exists a lower bound
C_{x}\geq C>0
which is independent of
x\in\Omega
. We assume to the contrary that there exists a sequence
(x_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega
and a sequence of normalized eigenvectors
(\mathbf{v}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
|\mathbf{v}_{\ell}|=1
satisfying
|(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x_{\ell}))\mathbf{v}_{\ell}|\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
. However, this yields a contradiction, since
\lambda
is arbitrarily close to the spectrum of the matrices
\mathbf{A}(x_{\ell})
as
\ell\to\infty
. Uniformity of the constant
C>0
implies that
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}
is bounded from below on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
and, thus,
\lambda\notin A_{\mathrm{ess}}
by definition (
D.1
).
∎

In order to characterize the spectrum of the part of the operator
\mathbf{L}
on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m+k}
in Proposition
2.10
, we use again that the spectrum of the multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}_{\ast}
is essential in the sense of Wolf.
This is a consequence of the following refined characterization of the spectrum of the multiplication operator
\mathbf{A}
.

Proposition D.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m\times m}
for
m\in\mathbb{N},
and let
\mathbf{A}
denote its corresponding bounded multiplication operator on
C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}
. Then the point spectrum of
\mathbf{A}
is given by

\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{A})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Gamma_{\lambda}\;\text{has non-empty interior}\;\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\circ}\},

(D.3)

where the compact set
\Gamma_{\lambda}
is defined by

\Gamma_{\lambda}=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}\mid\det(\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x))=0\}.

Moreover, the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbf{A})
is essential in the sense of Wolf, i.e.,

\sigma(\mathbf{A})=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathbf{A}):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lambda I-\mathbf{A}\;\text{is not a Fredholm operator}\}.

Proof.

Note that
\Gamma_{\lambda}
is compact by continuity of the determinant and entries of the matrix
\mathbf{A}(x)
. First, let us verify characterization (
D.3
) for
\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{A})
. On the one hand, let
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{A})
with a corresponding eigenfunction
\mathbf{f}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
\mathbf{f}(x)\not=\mathbf{0}
for some
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. By continuity of
\mathbf{f}
and (path-)connectedness of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we may choose a non-empty open set
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
for which
\mathbf{f}(x)\not=\mathbf{0}
holds for all
x\in\tilde{\Omega}
. Then, for each
x\in\tilde{\Omega}
, the vector
\mathbf{f}(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
is an eigenvector of the matrix
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}(x)
. Hence,
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Gamma_{\lambda}
and
\Gamma_{\lambda}
has non-empty interior with
\tilde{\Omega}=\tilde{\Omega}^{\circ}\subset\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\circ}
.

On the other hand, if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\circ}\not=\emptyset
, we can construct eigenvectors
\mathbf{v}(x)
corresponding to
\mathbf{A}(x)
and the constant eigenvalue
\lambda
such that the function
\mathbf{v}
is continuous on some closed ball
\overline{B_{\delta}(y)}\subset\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\circ}
. This follows from Gaussian elimination using elementary row operations which consists of multiplying, adding or swapping continuous entries. Once we obtained such eigenvectors, we use the idea of proof of
[
18
, Theorem 2.5]
. For arbitrary
\phi\in C(\overline{B_{\delta}(y)})
with
\phi\not\equiv 0
on the open ball
B_{\delta/2}(y)
, we define the function

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},\quad\mathbf{f}(x)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{v}(x)\phi(x)\eta(x)&\text{for}\quad x\in B_{\delta}(y),\\
\mathbf{0}&\text{else}\end{cases}

for a cut-off function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(y))
with
\eta_{|B_{\delta/2}(y)}\equiv 1
. Then,
\mathbf{f}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
satisfies
(\lambda I-\mathbf{A})\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{0}
, i.e.,
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{A})
.

It remains to show that
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}
is not Fredholm for all
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{A})
.

If
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{A})
, we infer
\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{A})\subset\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathbf{A})
from an infinite-dimensional kernel of
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}
, analog to
[
18
, Proposition 3.2]
. Indeed, from above calculations we see that this kernel contains a subspace which is isomorphic to
C(\overline{B_{\delta/2}(y)})
and, hence, is infinite-dimensional.

If
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{A})\setminus\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{A})
, the injective operator
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}
cannot be bounded from below since
\lambda\in\sigma_{ap}(\mathbf{A})
by Lemma
D.1
. Thus,
\lambda I-\mathbf{A}
cannot have closed range by
[
8
, Theorem 2.19, Remark 18]
and
\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathbf{A})
.
∎

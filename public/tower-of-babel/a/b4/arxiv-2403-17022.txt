Full Geometric Interpretation of Complex Blades, Determinants and Gramians
André L. G. Mandolesi

Abstract
Complex determinants, Gramians and blades (simple or decomposable multivectors) are related to volumes, but not in the same way as real ones. We present their complete geometric interpretations, including complex orientations. We also link real and complex concepts via a reality index, which measures the lack of holomorphy of real subspaces of ℂ n \mathds{C}^{n} . These results lead to volumetric Pythagorean theorems, a new complex Hodge star, and interpretations for Grassmannian metrics, among other uses.



Keywords:
complex; determinant; Gramian; blade; multivector; orientation; reality index; volumetric Pythagorean theorem

MSC:
15A15; 15A75

Introduction

Real determinants, Gramians (Gram determinants) and blades (simple or decomposable multivectors) have well known geometric interpretations in terms of volumes and orientations
[
17
,
19
,
20
]
.
Complex ones have similar meanings, but with crucial differences which are not usually discussed.
Their relation to volumes changes, and a full interpretation needs a natural, yet uncommon, idea of complex orientation.
Real and complex concepts are linked by a reality index,
which indicates the lack of holomorphy of a real subspace of
\mathds{C}^{n}
in terms of how much the complex structure dislocates it, as measured by a disjointness angle,
or, for a real plane, its Kähler or holomorphy angle
[
4
,
18
]
.

The complex case follows from the real one via some algebraically simple lemmas linking real and complex determinants.
As the geometry behind them is not clear, we also give a geometric proof.
Since the lemmas are fairly known, some of our results may be obvious for experts,

who perhaps simply did not find them useful.
Our experience shows otherwise:
they give volumetric Pythagorean theorems
[
12
]
with important implications for Quantum Theory
[
13
]
;
a simpler Hodge star operator for complex spaces
[
15
]
;
interpretations for Fubini-Study and Cauchy-Binet metrics in Grassmannians
[
16
]
;
etc.

In those articles, we interpreted the norm of complex blades without giving a proof, as we thought it was known.
After being asked for a reference, we were surprised that a thorough search of the literature and the internet returned only discussions about complex determinants in Mathematics forums
[
9
,
11
]
.
We also detected many doubts about such determinants, used by researchers of different areas who might not be familiar with the results needed for their interpretation.
Lack of a geometric understanding of complex blades may also be a reason why complex exterior algebras are underused in complex geometry, with complexified real differential forms taking the place of purely complex ones
[
8
]
.
This article aims to fill this void in the literature.

For real vectors, the Gramian is the squared volume of their parallelotope

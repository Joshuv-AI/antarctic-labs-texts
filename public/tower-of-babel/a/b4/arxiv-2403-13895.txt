Quotients of L L -functions: degrees n n and n − 2 n-2
Ravi Raghunathan

Abstract
. If L ⁡ ( s , π ) L(s,\pi) and L ⁡ ( s , ρ ) L(s,\rho) are the Dirichlet series attached to cuspidal automorphic representations π \pi and ρ \rho of GL n ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) {\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}) and GL n − 2 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) {\rm GL}_{n-2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}) respectively, we show that F 2 ​ ( s ) = L ⁡ ( s , π ) / L ⁡ ( s , ρ ) F_{2}(s)=L(s,\pi)/L(s,\rho) has infinitely many poles. We also establish analogous results for Artin L L -functions and other L L -functions not yet proven to be automorphic. Using the classification theorems of [ Rag20 ] and [ BR20 ] , we show that cuspidal L L -functions of GL 3 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) {\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}) are primitive in 𝔊 {\mathfrak{G}} , a monoid that contains both the Selberg class 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} and L ⁡ ( s , σ ) L(s,\sigma) for all unitary cuspidal automorphic representations σ \sigma of GL n ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) {\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}) .

In Theorem

has a finite number of poles in

such that we have an equality of partial

, for a large enough set of finite places

. In many cases, including those involving Artin

L

For the standard automorphic
L
-functions, this distinctness is a consequence of the theory of Rankin-Selberg convolutions developed by Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika and, more specifically, the results of Jacquet and Shalika in
[
JS81b
]
.
For other
L
-functions, we are sometimes able to reduce the situation to the automorphic case, or impose additional conditions to get the desired outcomes.

Recall that one way of proving the automorphy of Dirichlet series is to use
a
converse theorem
, that is, a theorem that asserts that a Dirichlet series is automorphic if it satisfies certain analytic properties and satsfies suitable functional equations. Our strategy for proving the automorphy of
F_{2}
is to show that if
F_{2}\in\mathfrak{G}
, it necessarily satisfies the hypotheses of the celebrated
converse theorem
of Weil (
[
Wei67
]
), or its analogues, which we will refer to collectively as
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type converse theorems.

If the function
G_{2}
has infinitely many zeros, Theorem
1.1
follows almost immediately, so we can assume that
G_{2}
has only finitely many zeros.
A priori
,
G_{2}
does not appear to have the form of the factors that arise in
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type converse theorems, where there are no gamma functions in the denominator. An elementary but careful analysis shows that the factors in the denominator actually cancel with those in the numerator leaving the correct form upto a factor of a rational function. Removal of this factor requires a choice of a suitable test vector for the archimedean integrals associated to the representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
,
and an invocation of the local functional equation for these integrals. This is one of the main ways in which our proof improves on earlier work.

A second technical difficulty is overcome using a modification of Booker’s theorem in
[
Boo03
]
so that it holds in the class
\mathfrak{G}
(and, in fact, in the even larger class
{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
): crucially we allow quotients of the usual gamma factors to appear in the functional equation. This will allow us to treat character twists of Dirichlet series of degree 2 satsifying even more general functional equations than the classical Hecke and Maass functional equations (and our treatment gives a uniform proof across different cases; we do not follow the more recent
[
BL22
]
).

We should note that a stronger version of Theorem
4.1
of this paper will show that
F_{2}
has infinitely many poles in the critical strip without assuming the condition that
G_{2}
has finitely many zeros. Our primary aim in this paper was to prove that the
L
-functions of cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
are primitive for which we require only the weaker Corollary
1.2
. The strengthening of Theorem
4.1
is work in progress.

In addition to the above, we will need to use the stability results for gamma factors proved by Jacquet and Shalika in
[
JS85
]
in order control the
\varepsilon
-factors that occur in the functional equations of the character twists of
F_{2}(s)
, as well as a number of other inputs from the theory of automorphic
L
-functions and their integral representations. We will discuss these issues in further detail in Section
2
where we outline the strategy of the proof.

Finally, we note that our techniques are unlikely to generalise further without significant breakthroughs in at least two directions. For instance, for a pair
(\pi,\rho)
where
\pi\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{\circ}
and
\rho\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n-3}
, we would require the analogue of Booker’s theorem in
[
Boo03
]
for
L
-functions of degree
3
, which so far seems to be outside the scope of our current technology. If the difference in degrees between
L(s,\pi)
and
L(s,\sigma)
is greater than
3
, there is simply no analogue of Weil’s converse theorem available which guarantees the automorphy of the
L
-function from the knowledge of only the character twists. Even when the difference in the degrees is exactly three, we will need an improvement on the converse theorem of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika formulated for the
L
-functions of admissible representations. These have Euler factors at each prime
p
which are reciprocals of polynomials in
p^{-s}
, but our methods require a theorem for series of degree
3
that may not have this property.

2.
The strategy of the proof and organisation of the paper

We give an outline of our proof of Theorem
1.1
and also describe the structure of the paper.

In Section
3
, we give the precise definitions
of the various classes of Dirichlet series
\mathcal{S}
,
\BOONDOX{S}^{\#}
,
\mathfrak{G}
,
{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
,
{\mathfrak{A}}
, and
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
defined axiomatically in the spirit of the Selberg class
\mathcal{S}
, and other variants that appear in this paper.

Assume now that
F_{2}(s)=L(s,\pi)/L(s,\rho)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}\in\mathfrak{G}
.
Given the definition of
F_{2}
, this amounts to assuming that
F_{2}
has at most a finite number of poles in
{\mathbb{C}}
. All the other conditions for membership in
\mathfrak{G}
are easily seen to be satisfied because of the properties of
L(s,\pi)
and
L(s,\rho)
.
If
G_{2}(s)=L(s,\pi_{\infty})/L(s,\rho_{\infty})
has infinitely many zeros, it is easy to see that
F_{2}
has (infinitely many) poles at those zeros, so we focus on the case when
G_{2}
has only finitely many zeros. As mentioned in the introduction, the main task is to establish that the function
F_{2}
satisfies the hypotheses of the converse theorem of Weil, or that of its analogues for
L
-functions having archimedean factors arising from principal series representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(recall that the series in Weil’s theorem are assumed to have archimedean factors that arise from (limits of) discrete series representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
). We will refer to the archimedean factors in the two cases together as “
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type” factors and to the corresponding converse theorems as “
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type” (converse) theorems. We emphasise that these converse theorems do not require that the Dirichlet series in question have Euler products.

We first show that the twisted series
F_{2}(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi(n)a_{n}n^{-s}
which are
a priori
only meromorphic are actually entire for all primitive Dirichlet characters
\chi
, a necessary input for the converse theorem. This is carried out in Section
4
using the ideas of Booker
[
Boo03
]
but in the more general context of the class
{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
. This has the virtue of giving both a more general result as well as avoiding case by case analyses.

In Section
5
we describe the local and global
L
-functions and
associated to automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. We also record many of the foundational theorems for these
L
-functions primarily due to Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika via the method of integral representations, Shahidi, Kim-Shahidi and Gelbart-Shahidi using the Langlands-Shahidi method, and the work of Moeglin-Waldspurger which combines both approaches. These allow us to conclude that
L(s,\pi)\in\mathfrak{G}
if
\pi\in\BOONDOX{T}_{n}
and, in fact, satisfy the more restrictive form of the functional equation (
5.14
). We obtain slightly weaker conclusions for the tensor product, exterior square and symmetric square
L
-functions, which will nonetheless suffice for our purposes.

As mentioned above, the
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type converse theorem demands that the archimedean factors
G_{2}(s,\chi)
that appear in the functional equation of
F_{2}(s,\chi)
should be the archimedean
L
-functions associated to an irreducible admissible representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, but
a priori
, we can only conclude that the
G(s,\chi)
are quotients of the gamma functions defined in (
5.3
) and (
5.6
).
The passage from such quotients to the
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type gamma factors is done in two stages which we describe below (recall that we are assuming that
G_{2}
has finitely many zeros!).

In Lemma
6.2
of Section
6
we show that this is true upto a factor of a rational function, that is, if
F_{2}\in\mathfrak{G}
,
G(s,\chi_{\infty})=R(s,\chi_{\infty})L(s,\tau_{\infty}\times\chi_{\infty})
for some irreducible admissible representation
\tau_{\infty}
of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and some rational function
R(s,\chi_{\infty})
.
The arguments involve elementary but careful analyses of the poles of the relevant gamma functions. Then, in Theorem
7.2
of Section
7
, we analyse the archimedean local integrals associated to irreducible admissible representations
\tau_{\infty}
of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and show that by choosing suitable test vectors for these integrals we can obtain the factor
P(s,\chi_{\infty})L(s,\sigma_{\infty}\times\chi_{\infty})
for any polynomial
P(s,\chi_{\infty})
. The (archimedean) local functional equation then allows us to eliminate the factor
R(s,\chi_{\infty})
from the global functional equation.

Finally, the converse theorem of
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type requires a degree of control on the
\varepsilon
-factors that appear in the functional equation of
F_{2}(s,\chi)
. This
technical problem can be overcome by twisting
F_{2}(s)
by a character
\chi_{0}
that is sufficiently highly ramified at a set of finite places (depending on
\pi
and
\rho
), and then using a stability result due to Jacquet and Shalika for
\varepsilon
-factors. This process is described in Section
8
and guarantees that the
\varepsilon
-factors are those that appear in the hypotheses of the converse theorem.

In Section
9
we state the precise
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type converse theorem that we will use. We also state Theorem
9.6
and complete its proof, an easy task, since all the ingredients are already in place. Theorem
1.1
follows almost immediately.

Section

is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
and its corollaries after reviewing our results from
[
Rag20
]
and
[
BR20
]
classifying the elements of
{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
of degrees less than
2
. This concludes the main part of the paper.

The remaining sections deal with the applications and extensions of Theorem
1.1
.
Section
11
discusses pairs of
L
-functions of symmetric powers as concrete instances of Theorem
1.1

when
G_{2}
has finitely many zeros, and how these results improve
on existing ones. Section
12
treats Artin
L
-functions
and other examples of
L
-functions which are not known to be
automorphic. Finally, in Section

, we are
able to show that

has infinitely

3.
Various axiomatically defined classes of Dirichlet series

We recall the definitions of the various classes of Dirichlet series
that appear in this paper. Let
s=\sigma+it\in{\mathbb{C}}
and let
F(s)
be a non-zero meromorphic
function on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
We consider the following conditions on
F(s)
.

(P1)

The function
F(s)
is given by a
Dirichlet series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}

with abscissa of absolute convergence
\sigma_{a}\geq 1/2
.

(P2)

There is a polynomial
P(s)
such that
P(s)F(s)

extends to an entire function, and such that in any
vertical strip
\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{2}
,

P(s)F(s)\ll e^{|t|^{\rho}}

for some
\rho>0
.

(P3)

There exist a real number
Q>0
, a complex number
\omega
,
and a function
G(s)
of the form

G(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{r^{\prime}}\Gamma(\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}s+\mu_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{-1},

(3.1)

where
\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
,
\mu_{j},\mu_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
and
\Gamma(s)
denotes the usual gamma function, such that

\Phi(s):=Q^{s}G(s)F(s)=\omega\overline{\Phi(1-\bar{s})}.

(3.2)

(P4)

The function
F(s)
can be expressed as a product

F(s)=\prod_{p}F_{p}(s)
, where

\log F_{p}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{p^{k}}}{p^{ks}}

(3.3)

with
|b_{p^{k}}|\leq Cp^{k\theta}
for some
\theta>0
and some
constant
C>0
.

In
[
Rag20
]
we introduced the monoid (under multiplication of functions) of non-zero meromorphic functions
F

satisfying (P1)-(P3) and denoted it by
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
. The monoid of functions
F\in{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
also satisfying (P4) is called
{\mathfrak{A}}
.

We have also previously introduced the submonoid
\mathfrak{G}\subset{\mathfrak{A}}
(see
[
Rag20
]
) consisting of Dirichlet series satisfying the following additional properties.

(G1)

The abscissa of absolute convergence satisfies
\sigma_{a}\leq 1
.

(G3)

The archimedean parameters satisfy the bounds

\re\left(-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}\right),\re\left(-\frac{\mu_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}}{\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\right)<\frac{1}{2},\,\,1\leq j\leq r,\,\,1\leq j^{\prime}\leq r^{\prime}.

(3.4)

(G4)

The non-archimedean parameters satisfy
\theta<1/2
.

Thus,
F\in\mathfrak{G}
if
F
satisfies (G1), (P2), (G3) and (G4).
Given
F(s)
satisfying an equation of the form (
3.2
), we define
the degree of
F
to be

d_{F}=2(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}-\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{r^{\prime}}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime})
.
We recall Theorem 4.1 of
[
Rag20
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
F\in{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
satisfies
(
3.2
) for two functions

G^{(1)}(s)
and
G^{(2)}(s)
of the form given by (
3.1
),
let
d^{(1)}=2\sum_{i=1}^{{r}_{i}}\lambda_{i}^{(1)}
and

d^{(2)}=2\sum_{i=1}^{{r}_{i}}\lambda_{i}^{(2)}
. Then

d^{(1)}=d^{(2)}
. If we further assume that
F\in{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
,
G_{1}(s)=cG_{2}(s)
for some constant

.

The theorem says that

does not depend on the choice of

in equation (

). Thus, the notation
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{d}
for set of series

We recall that the extended Selberg class
\BOONDOX{S}^{\#}
consists of the series in
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
satisfying

\sigma_{a}\leq 1
,

for some integer

,

\re(-\mu_{j})\leq 0

. In particular, elements of

satisfy (G1) and (G3). The subset of series in

with an Euler product satisfying (P4) and (G4) is the Selberg class

. Thus, we see that

. We denote by

(resp.
\mathfrak{G}_{d}
,
\BOONDOX{S}^{\#}_{d}
,

) the set of series in
{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
(resp.
\mathfrak{G}
,

As we have remarked in
[

]
, the conditions (G3) and (G4) impose a uniform bound of
1/2
on both the archimedean and non-archimedean local parameters, perhaps making the class
\mathfrak{G}
a more aesthetically satisfying choice. By contrast, the Selberg class demands
\re(-\mu_{j})\leq 0
, but
\theta<1/2
. The condition (G3) ensures that there are no trivial zeros of
F
on the critical line
\re(s)=1/2
.

For
F(s)\in\mathfrak{G}
and
S
a finite set of primes, we define the partial
L
-function (or partial Dirichlet series) by

F_{S}(s)=\prod_{p\notin S}F_{p}(s).

If
F(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
and
\chi
is a
Dirichlet character, we define the twisted series

F(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)a_{n}}{n^{s}}.

(3.5)

We note that if
F(s)=F_{1}(s)F_{2}(s)
is a product of two Dirichlet series,

F(s,\chi)=F_{1}(s,\chi)F_{2}(s,\chi).

(3.6)

It is a conjecture of Selberg that the class
\mathcal{S}
is closed under twisting by
Dirichlet characters. We expect that the same is true for the class
\mathfrak{G}
(in fact, one would conjecture
that
\mathfrak{G}=\mathcal{S}
). In what follows we may sometimes require
F(s)
to satisfy the following property:

(G5)

For every non-principal Dirichlet character
\chi
, the series

F(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}\frac{\chi(n)a_{n}}{n^{s}}
extends to a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}
and is holomorphic on the line
\re(s)=1
.

We could have formulated our results somewhat more generally and avoided (to some extent) invoking (P4) (and (G4)), since many of the necessary inputs have been proved for the much larger class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
. However, the sets
\BOONDOX{S}^{\#}_{0}
and
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{0}
are quite large (and coincide), and this prevents a clean formulation of factorisation results. In contrast
\mathfrak{G}_{0}=1
(see Theorem
10.1
of this paper), so we do not have to worry about degree zero elements.

The main (and conjecturally, the only) examples of Dirichlet series in the various classes above arise from automorphic
L
-functions. Let
\sigma
be a unitary automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{K})
, where
K
is a number field,
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
its ring of adèles, and
m=[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]
its degree. We will denote by
L(s,\sigma)
the standard
L
-function associated to
\sigma
. As we will see in Theorem
5.7
of Subsection
5.4
,
L(s,\sigma)
belongs to
\mathfrak{G}
, and its degree is
mn
(although Theorem
5.7
is stated only for
\sigma\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, it is valid over number fields). This the main reason we choose to work in in
\mathfrak{G}
. In contrast, since the Generalised Ramanujan Conjecture (at infinity) is very far from being proved for these
L
-functions, they are not known to belong to
\mathcal{S}
. The only known examples are
L
-functions of Hecke characters of number fields, those arising in some way (e.g. as symmetric powers) from holomorphic cuspidal eigenforms (of any level) or Maass cuspidal eigenforms of small level, or those arising from sufficiently nice Galois representations. We will discuss these automorphic
L
-functions in greater detail in Section
5
.

4.
An analogue of Booker’s theorem for the class
\mathfrak{G}

The aim of this section is to prove the following variant of the main theorem of
[

]

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
F(s)\in{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}_{2}
and that it satisfies (G5).
Then

We first explain how Theorem

The main (and only) theorem of
[

]
is the special case of Theorem
4.1
for

For our purposes however, we need a theorem that allows the factors

to be much more general. In particular, they should be allowed to have gamma functions in the denominators (that is, we allow for factors with

cannot assume that the Dirichlet series have Euler products. The theorems of
[

]
for

[

for

corresponds to the equation for classical Maass forms avoid the assumption of an Euler product for

However, these two cases do not account even for all the factors

allows us to uniformly treat not only all factors that arise from automorphic representations of

We should acknowledge that the theorems of

do not require the holomorphy hypothesis made in (G5) and are sharper since they require twisting by a much smaller (but still, infinite) set of characters.
They also do not require the condition (G1), but only the weaker (P1). We have not attempted to modify those proofs to suit our more general setting: our arguments would have become considerably longer without allowing any new applications. More recently, the authors of
[
BL22

treat the Weil functional equation and generalise certain aspects to the Selberg class. The proof of Theorem

]
.

Finally, we remark that the condition that

is as in Theorem

is the quotient of

and

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}-\sum_{j^{\prime}}^{r^{\prime}}\lambda^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}=1

Corollary 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of the theorem,
if
\chi\neq 1
is a primitive character,
\Phi(s,\chi)=G(s,\chi)F(s,\chi)
is entire.

Proof.

We note
G(s,\chi)
is holomorphic in
\re(s)\geq 1/2
by (G3). Hence, by (G1) and (G5),
\Phi(s,\chi)
is holomorphic in
\re(s)\geq 1
.

Suppose that
\Phi(s,\chi)
has a pole at
s=\beta
with

. By the theorem above, we know that

has no poles in the strip

5.
Local and global
L
-functions of automorphic representations

We will quickly recall some more facts about automorphic
L
-functions. We will assume that we are working over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, though most of the theorems we state below are valid over arbitrary number fields. If
\sigma\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, it is known that
\sigma=\otimes^{\prime}_{v}\sigma_{v}
, the restricted direct product of irreducible admissible representations
of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}}_{v})
, where
v
runs over the set of (finite and infinite) places of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We let
\chi=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\chi_{v}
be a Dirichlet character viewed as an idèle class character. We also fix a global additive character
\psi=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\psi_{v}
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
which is trivial on
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

5.1.
Non-archimedean local factors

By the work of Godement-Jacquet in
[
GJ72
]
we can attach a local
L
-function
L(s,\sigma_{p})
to the representation
\sigma_{p}
.
Explicitly, it has the form

L(s,\sigma_{p})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\alpha_{i}(p)p^{-s})}

(5.1)

for complex numbers
\alpha_{i}(p)
,
1\leq i\leq n
. For all but finitely many
primes
p
we have
\alpha_{i}(p)\neq 0
, for
1\leq i\leq n
. These are called the unramified places of
\sigma
or the places where
\sigma_{v}
is unramified.
The remaining finitely many places are said to be the places where
\sigma
is ramified. We denote this set of places by
S_{\sigma}
. If
\chi=\otimes^{\prime}_{p}\chi_{p}
is a primitive Dirichlet character, we may attach the
L
-factor to the twist
\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p}
of
\sigma_{p}
by
\chi_{p}
.
Whenever
p\notin S_{\sigma}\cup S_{\chi}
, the twisted
L
-function has the form

L(s,\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\chi(p)\alpha_{i}(p)p^{-s})}.

Recall that we may also associate an
\varepsilon
-factor

\varepsilon(s,\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p},\psi_{p})=r(\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p},\psi_{p})K(\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p},\psi_{p})^{1/2-s}

to each pair
(\sigma_{p},\chi_{p})
. Here
r(\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p},\psi_{p})
is a complex number of absolute value
1
, and
K(\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p},\psi_{p})=p^{n_{p,\chi}}
, for some integer
n_{p,\chi}\geq 0
which depends on
\psi_{p}
. It is a fact that the local
\varepsilon
-factors are identically
1
at the primes
p
where both
\sigma_{p}
and
\chi_{p}
are unramified and, in fact,
r(\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p},\psi_{p})=1=K(\sigma_{p}\times\chi_{p},\psi_{p})
at these places.

We will really need to know the
\varepsilon
-factor only for characters.
For a character
\chi_{p}
of conductor
P=p^{e}
, the
\varepsilon
-factor is given by

\varepsilon(s,\chi_{p})=(-1)^{\epsilon_{\chi_{p}}}\tau(\chi)P^{-s},

(5.2)

where
\tau(\chi)
is the Gauss sum associated to the character
\chi_{p}
and the additive character
\psi_{p}
. Thus

r(\chi_{p},\psi_{p})=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{P^{1/2}}
and
K(\chi_{p},\psi_{p})=P
.

5.2.
Archimedean local factors

We may also define
L
-factors at the infinite place. They have the form

L(s,\sigma_{\infty})=\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}(s+\epsilon_{l}+\nu_{l})\prod_{l=r_{1}+1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\Gamma_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left(s+\frac{k_{l}-1}{2}+\nu_{l}\right),

(5.3)

where

\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}(s)=\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)~\text{and}~\Gamma_{{\mathbb{C}}}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s).

(5.4)

Here, the
\nu_{l}:=\nu_{l}(\sigma_{\infty})
are complex numbers and
\epsilon_{l}:=\epsilon_{l}(\sigma_{\infty})
is
1
or
0
according to whether or not the
sign representation of
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
arises in the relevant representation of the Weil group. The
k_{l}:=k_{l}(\sigma_{\infty})\geq 1
are integers for
r_{1}+1\leq l\leq r_{1}+r_{2}
and
r_{1}+2r_{2}=n
. The condition that
\sigma
be unitary yields

\sum_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\re(\nu_{l})+\sum_{l=r_{1}+1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}2\re(\nu_{l})=0.

(5.5)

Assume now that
\chi
is a Dirchlet character, so
\chi_{\infty}
is either the trivial or sign character.
For the representations
\sigma_{\infty}\times\chi_{\infty}
, the corresponding

L
-factors are

L(s,\sigma_{\infty}\times\chi_{\infty})=\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}(s+\epsilon_{l,\chi}+\nu_{l})\prod_{l=r_{1}+1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\Gamma_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left(s+\frac{k_{l}-1}{2}+\nu_{l}\right),

(5.6)

where
\epsilon_{l,\chi}=\epsilon_{l}+\epsilon_{\chi}\pmod{2}

In each of the cases that we considered, we obtained either a polynomial or the reciprocal of a polynomial for the relevant quotients. We may thus proceed inductively starting with

Thus, after canceling all the factors in the denominator out and relabelling the indices if necessary, we see that

G_{2}(s,\chi)=\begin{cases}R_{0}(s)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(s+\epsilon_{1,\chi}+\nu_{1}\right)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(s+\epsilon_{2,\chi}+\nu_{2}\right)\quad\text{or}\\
R_{0}(s)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left(s+\frac{k-1}{2}+\nu_{0}\right).\end{cases}

where

is a quotient of polynomials,
\epsilon_{i,\chi}\in\{0,1\}
,
-1/2<\re(\nu_{i})<1/2
,

,

, and where
\chi=\chi_{\infty}
is either the trivial or sign character. We further note that all the zeros and poles of
R_{0}(s)
lie in the half-plane

\re(s)<1/2
. Further, we can obviously assume that
p_{0}(s)
and
q_{0}(s)
are coprime.

We analyse the first case a little further.
Since
\tau
and
\rho
are both assumed unitary, we know that

\re(\nu_{1})+\re(\nu_{2})=0
.
If
\epsilon_{1,\chi}+\nu_{1}\neq\epsilon_{1,\chi}+\nu_{2}\pm 1
, we

\re(\nu_{1})\neq 0

, and we may assume (after relabelling the two factors if necessary) that

\epsilon_{2,\chi}=1

\re(\nu)=0

A similar analysis of the second case of (

\re(\nu_{0})=0

To prove the second assertion, if

with the largest real part, then

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume that
\pi=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\pi_{v}
and
\rho=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\rho_{v}
are irreducible admissible representations of

has at most a finite number of poles. Then

has at most a finite number of zeros.

Proof.

Since

satisfies (P1) and (P4). Since we have assumed that

\pi_{\infty}
and
\tau_{\infty}
are unitary, they satisfy (

). Hence,

satisfies (G3). Using the duplication formula for the gamma function, we see that

\Gamma_{{\mathbb{C}}}(s+a)=\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(s+a\right)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(s+a+1\right).

Note that the poles and zeros of

lie on lines parallel to the

9.
A
{\rm GL}_{2}

The final ingredient we will require is a version of Weil’s converse theorem valid for all Dirichlet series satisfying a

{\rm GL}_{2}

We recall that if

F_{S}(s)=\sum_{(n,p)=1\,\text{if $p\in S$}}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}.

This is obviously consistent with the earlier notation for partial
L

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
\tau_{\infty}
be an irreducible unitary representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. We denote by

the weight of
\tau_{\infty}
(resp. parity) if it is a (limit of) discrete series representation (resp. principal series representation). Let
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed and let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
(N,D)=1
be arbitrary. Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
and assume that
\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
We will assume that
F(s)
is a Dirichlet series satisfying (P1), (P2), and the following condition.

(W3)

There exists a Dirichlet series
F_{1}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{-s}
satisfying (P1), such that for every primitive Dirichlet character
\chi\pmod{D}
the functions

\Phi(s,\chi)=L(s,\tau_{\infty}\times\chi_{\infty})F(s,\chi)\,\,\text{and}\,\,L(s,\tilde{\tau}_{\infty}\times\chi_{\infty}^{-1})F_{1}(s,\chi)

are entire and satisfy functional equations of the form

\Phi(s,\chi)=\psi(D)N^{1/2-s}\chi(N)\varepsilon(s,\chi)^{2}\tilde{L}(1-s,\sigma_{\infty}\times\chi_{\infty})F_{1}(1-s,\bar{\chi}).

(9.1)

Then there exist distinct automorphic representations
\tau_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
, with
L(s,\tau_{i,\infty})=L(s,\tau_{\infty})
for all

1\leq i\leq r
, such that

F_{S}(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}L_{S}(s,\tau_{i})

(9.2)

for some constants
c_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
.

Remark 9.2
.

A slightly stronger version of the theorem above was formulated and proved by Weil in
[
Wei67
]
when
\sigma
is a discrete series or a limit of discrete series representation. In this case Weil’s theorem asserts that

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi_{0}(n)a_{n}e^{2\pi inz}
is a modular form of level
N
and nebentypus
\psi
of a suitable weight
k
. Since the modular eigenforms give a basis of the space of all modular forms of level
N
, nebentypus
\psi
and weight
k
, and since each eigenform corresponds to an automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we see that the conclusion of our theorem follows.

Remark 9.3
.

When
\tau_{\infty}
is a principal series representation, Weil’s proof essentially goes through with minimal changes. Weil’s proof hinges on producing an elliptic element
M\in{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
of infinite order such that
(g|_{\gamma}-\psi(D)g)|_{M}=g|_{\gamma}-\psi(D)g
, where
g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{2\pi inz}
, the candidate modular form of which
F_{1}(s)
will be the Mellin tranform, for all
\gamma
in a set of elements generating
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. This can be deduced from the functional equations for sufficiently many character twists of
F
.
On the other hand he shows that any holomorphic function
f
, such that
f|_{M}=f
must be identically zero. Although,
f
is holomorphic in Weil’s theorem, the same argument works for real analytic
f
as well, which is what the candidate automorphic form will be in general. Indeed, what one uses is only the fact that the torus generated by
M
has a limit point and the principle of analytic continuation for power series.

Remark 9.4
.

Note that in one sense the functional equation (
9.1
) is actually more general than the one satisfied by the elements of
{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
because it allows the Dirichlet series
F_{1}(s)
to be arbitrary.

Remark 9.5
.

As is well known, Theorem
9.1
requires only that the conditions on the twists
\Phi(s,\chi)
hold for a suitably chosen finite set of primitive characters
\chi
(see
[
PŠ75
]
). Invoking this stronger form of the theorem does not simplify our proof.

\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
, and a finite set of finite places
S
of
{\mathbb{Q}}

F_{S}(s)=L_{S}(s,\tau)

(9.3)

Proof.

Since
F_{2}\in\mathfrak{G}
and satisfies (G5) by assumption, we see that the hypotheses of Theorem

are satisfied. It follows that
F_{2}(s,\chi)
is holomorphic in the strip
0<\re(s)\leq 1

is entire if
\chi\neq 1

4.3
.

Let
\chi_{0}
be the (primitive) character chosen in Section

and recall that

. By our remarks above,
F_{0}(s)
is holomorphic in

and

has only finitely many zeros, it follows from Lemma

G_{0}(s)=G_{2}(s,\chi_{0})

F_{0}(s)
has only finitely many poles, since these poles (which lie outside the strip

G_{0}(s)
, since

Thus, we can now assume that
F_{0}(s)=L(s,\pi\times\chi_{0})/L(s,\rho\times\chi_{0})
extends meromorphically to all of
{\mathbb{C}}
with at most finitely many poles. Replacing
F_{2}
by
F_{0}
in the arguments of the first paragraph of the proof, we see that

F_{0}\in\mathfrak{G}
. Now, let
\chi

. It
follows that

F_{0,S}(s)=L_{S}(s,\pi\times\chi_{0})/L_{S}(s,\rho\times\chi_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}L_{S}(s,\tau_{i}^{\prime}),

for suitable unitary automorphic representations

. Since

(9.4)

for

\pi
is admissible and unramified outside

,

We now deduce Theorem

.

As we have already observed before, we can assume that

Applications to the Primitivity of cuspidal
L
-functions

In this section we will deduce Theorem
1.3
from Corollary
1.2
. We will need previously known classification results for the classes
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
and
\mathfrak{G}
to do this. In
[
Rag20
]
and
[
BR20
]
these are often formulated for series in the larger class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
. We sometimes state only the versions relevant to the class
\mathfrak{G}
below.

Recall that
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{d}
(resp.
\mathfrak{G}_{d}
) is the class of series in
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
(resp.
\mathfrak{G}
) of degree

. The four important classfication theorems for elements of

of small degree recorded below
are Theorems 4.3, 4.7 and 5.1 of
[

]
and Theorem 1.1 of
[
BR20
]
respectively.

Theorem 10.1
.

We have
\mathfrak{G}_{0}=\{1\}
.

Theorem 10.2
.

If
0<d<1
, then
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{d}=\emptyset
.

Theorem 10.3
.

The set
\mathfrak{G}_{1}
consists of the functions of the form

L(s+iA,\chi)
, where
A\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\chi
is a primitive Dirichlet character.

Theorem 10.4
.

If
1<d<2
, then
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{d}=\emptyset
.

An easy consequence of Theorems
10.1
and
10.2
is Corollary 6.2 of

[
Rag20
]
:

Theorem 10.5
.

Every element of
\mathfrak{G}
can be factored in primitive elements.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The theorems above allow us to reduce the proof of Theorem
1.3
to
Corollary
1.2
. Indeed, suppose
\pi\in{\mathcal{A}}_{3}^{\circ}
. Then
L(s,\pi)\in\mathfrak{G}_{3}
. By Theorem

10.5
, we know that
L(s,\pi)
must factor into primitive elements. By Theorems
10.1
and
10.2
, the degree of the smallest non-trivial factor must be at least
1
.
Thus, there can be at most three non-trivial factors. There can be no factor of degree between
1
and
2
by Theorem
10.4
, so we see that the only possible factorisations are

L(s,\pi)=f_{1}(s)f_{2}(s)f_{3}(s),

where
f_{j}(s)\in\mathfrak{G}_{1}
for
1\leq j\leq 3
, or

L(s,\pi)=f_{1}(s)f_{2}(s),

with
f_{1}(s)\in\mathfrak{G}_{1}
and
f_{2}(s)\in\mathfrak{G}_{2}
.
In either case, Theorem
10.3
asserts that
f_{1}(s)=L(s+iA_{1},\chi_{1})
for
A_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and a primitive Dirichlet character
\chi_{1}
. We have

L(s,\pi)=L(s+iA_{1},\chi_{1})F_{2}(s),

(10.1)

where
F_{2}(s)\in\mathfrak{G}_{2}
with
F_{2}=f_{2}f_{3}
in the first case above, or

in the second case. If
\rho=\chi_{1}\norm^{-iA}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}^{\circ}
,

, contradicting Corollary

1.2
.
∎

11.
Comparing the zero sets of
L
-functions

Theorem
1.1
asserts that there are infinitely many zeros
(counted with multiplicty) of
L(s,\rho)

which are not zeros (counted with multiplicty) of
L(s,\pi)
. More precisely, let
m_{\theta}

denote the multiplicity of a zero
\theta
of
L(s,\sigma)
, where

\sigma\in\BOONDOX{T}
. We let

S_{\sigma}=\{(\theta,m_{\theta})\,|\,L(\theta,\sigma)=0\}.

Then the theorem asserts that
|S_{\rho}\setminus S_{\pi}|=\infty
.

When
G_{2}
has finitely many zeros, the theorem tells us that there are infinitely many non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\rho)
which are not zeros of
L(s,\pi)
in the critical strip. Let

S_{c,\sigma}=\{s\in S_{\sigma}\,|\,0<\re(s)<1\}
. We record this reformulation separately as

Corollary 11.1
.

Let
\pi\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{\circ}
and let
\rho\in\BOONDOX{T}_{n-2}
. If
G_{2}(s)=L(s,\pi_{\infty})/L(s,\rho_{\infty})
has at most a finite number of zeros, then

|(S_{c,\rho}\setminus S_{c,\pi})|=\infty
.

Proof.

We know that the poles of
F_{2}
outside the critical strip are necessarily zeros of
G_{2}
, since
\Phi_{2}(s)
is holomorphic when
\re(s)\leq 0
. Since there are at most finitely many of these, we see by Theorem
1.1
that there are infinitely many poles of
F_{2}
in the critical strip.
∎

In
[
Rag99
]
and
[
Rag10
]
, we treated the special case of the corollary above when
G_{2}(s)=L(s,\tau_{\infty})
for some unitary representation
\tau_{\infty}
of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, that is, when
R(s)=1
, and when
F_{2}(s)
had conductor
1
(or, more precisely, when
\pi
and
\rho
had the same conductor).

Special cases of
\pi\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{\circ}
and
\rho\in\BOONDOX{T}_{n-1},\BOONDOX{T}_{n}

were treated in
[
Rag99
]
, and then considerably generalised in

[
Boo15
]
and still further generalised in Theorem 7.1 of
[
Rag20
]
. This last theorem has no restrictions on the archimedean factors or even on the sizes of the Satake parameters, and is thus applicable to the tensor product, exterior square and symmetric square
L
-functions. We refer to the introduction of
[
Rag99
]
for the previous history of comparing zero sets of
L
-functions.

We give one class of examples to illustrate the generality of Corollary
11.1
and how it improves on the existing results.

Example 11.2
.

Let
\tau
be a unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Let

\pi={\sym}^{n}\tau
,
n\geq 0
, and
\rho=\sym^{n-2}\tau
,
n\geq 2
, the automorphic symmetric power lifts of

\tau
(if they exist). These examples are particularly interesting for the following reason. When
\tau_{p}
is unramified
let
\alpha_{p}
and
\alpha_{p}^{-1}
be its Satake parameters. In this case the local Euler factor of
F_{2}(s)
at
p
has the form

F_{2,p}(s)=(1-\alpha_{p}^{n+1}p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\alpha_{p}^{-n-1}p^{-s})^{-1}.

Thus, we may view the local Euler factor as the local
L
-function of an admissible representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
.

When
\tau_{\infty}
is a principal series representation (this case corresponds to representations associated to Maass cusp forms), the symmetric power lifts
{\sym}^{n}\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}_{n+1}
are known to exist for
n\leq 4
(
[
GJ76
]
,
[
KS02
]
,
[
Kim03
]
). In this case, one checks easily that
G_{2}(s)
has the form

\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\varepsilon+ni\nu}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\varepsilon-ni\nu}{2}\right)

which clearly has no zeros. Kim and Shahidi give precise criteria for the cuspidality of
\pi
in
[
KS02
]
. In this case Corollary
11.1
can be stated as

Corollary 11.3
.

Let
\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{\circ}
such that
\tau_{\infty}
is a
principal series representation and assume that
\pi={\sym}^{n}\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{\circ}
for
2\leq n\leq 4
, and that
\rho={\sym}^{n-2}\tau
. Then,
|(S_{c,\rho}\setminus S_{c,\pi})|=\infty
.

When
F_{2}
has conductor 1, this was treated in
[
Rag10
]
and when
n=2
, this is the work of
[
NO20
]
.

When
\tau_{\infty}
is a (limit of) discrete series representation and
\tau
is not CM, the
n
-th symmetric power lifts are known to exist and be cuspidal for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
by the work of Newton and Thorne
[
NT21
]
. As observed in Remark
5.6
, these symmetric power
L
-functions satisfy the Generalised Ramanujan Conjecture at all places.
In this case, we can check that
G_{2}(s)
has the form
P(s)L(s,\tau_{\infty})
for some polynomial
P(s)\not\equiv 1
if
n\geq 4
, so
G_{2}(s)
has at most finitely many zeros.
We record this special case of Corollary
11.1
as

Corollary 11.4
.

Let
\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{\circ}
such that
\tau_{\infty}
is a (limit of) discrete series representation and
\tau
is not CM, and let
\pi={\sym}^{n}\tau
and
\rho={\sym}^{n-2}\tau
. Then for
n\geq 3
,
|S_{c,\rho}\setminus S_{c,\pi}|=\infty
.

Note that for the corollary above we really do need to allow for a polynomial factor to appear in the expression for
G_{2}(s)
.
To summarise, for every
n\geq 3
, the Euler product of
F_{2}(s)
in the case above has the same form as the Euler product of a a global admissible representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, so
F_{2}(s)
is the (partial)
L
-function of an admissible representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. It also satisfies (G1), (G3) and (G4) but has infinitely many poles (in the critical strip) and thus does not satisfy (G2).

Remark 11.5
.

The case

in the corollary above requires a somewhat different argument which we will give in Section

.

Remark 11.6
.

In

12.
Relaxing the condition of automorphy

In this section we will give examples of pairs of
L
-functions
L(s,\pi)
and
L(s,\rho)
which have not been shown to be automorphic but for which we can nonetheless prove that
F_{2}(s)=L(s,\pi)/L(s,\rho)
has infinitely many poles (in the critical strip).
Theorem
9.6
is applicable in a very wide variety of situations and will allows us to conclude that outside of a finite number of primes,
F_{2}
coincides with the
L
-function of an automorphic representation.
It is possiblle to give a large number of pairs of
L
-functions for which we can extend the results of Theorem
1.1
. We content ourselves with two sets of examples, the first involving Artin
L
-functions, and the second involving the tensor product
L
-functions.

Corollary 12.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible three dimensional Artin representation and let
\rho
be a (unitary twist of) a Dirichlet character, and suppose that
G_{2}
has only finitely many zeros. Then

|S_{c,\rho}\setminus S_{c,\pi}|=\infty
.

Proof.

Artin representations give rise to global admissible representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. The irreducibility of
\pi
implies that
F_{2}
satisfies (G5), and the other hypotheses of Theorem
9.6
follow easily from the well known properties of Artin
L
-functions. It follows from Theorem
9.6
that for some finite set of places
S
, we have

L_{S}(s,\pi)=L_{S}(\rho)L_{S}(s,\tau).

(12.1)

Twisting both sides of the equation above by
\rho^{-1}
, we see that the left hand side is holomorphic on the line
\re(s)=1
, while the right hand side has a pole at
s=1
, yielding a contradiction.
∎

The corollary above generalises the work of Hochfilzer and Oliver in
[
HO22
]
where
\rho
is taken to be the trivial character and
\pi
is in a somewhat restricted subset of representations. One reason we are able to handle arbitrary characters
\rho
is our use of the stability results of Theorem
8.1
.

Corollary
12.1
indicates a possible strategy for showing the irreducibility of certain Artin, or more generally, Galois representations, since the reducibility of a representation implies that its
L
-function factorises as a product Galois
L
-functions. However, I was unable to come up with any concrete non-trivial examples where this strategy might be exploited.

Corollary
12.1
permits the following generalisation.

Corollary 12.2
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible Artin representation of dimension
n
and let
\rho\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n-2}
. If
G_{2}
has at most finitely many zeros,
|S_{c,\rho}\setminus S_{c,\pi}|=\infty
.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to the previous case. As before we get

L_{S}(s,\pi)=L_{S}(s,\rho)L(s,\tau)

by using Theorem
9.6
. Thus,
\pi=\rho\boxplus\tau

outside of a finite number of places
S
. We twist both sides above by
\tilde{\pi}
to obtain

L_{S}(s,\pi\times\tilde{\pi})=L_{S}(s,\rho\times\tilde{\rho})L_{S}(s,\rho\times\tilde{\tau})^{2}L_{S}(s,\tau\times\tilde{\tau}).

The irreducibility of
\pi
shows that
L_{S}(s,\pi\times\tilde{\pi})
has a simple pole at
s=1
, while the right hand side has at least a double pole at
s=1

∎

As a variant on the theme above, we can assume that
\rho
is also an Artin representation of dimension
n-2
but that
G_{2}(s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
. Then,
\tau
will arise from a weight
1
form, and under some additional hypotheses, will correspond to an Artin representation of dimension
2
. But this will contradict the irreducibility of
\pi
. Other variations involving the
L
-functions of (compatible families of) Galois representations can also be formulated.

We record a second situation in which Theorem
1.1
does not suffice, but where Theorem
9.6
works.

Corollary 12.3
.

Let
\pi_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n_{i}}^{\circ}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, and suppose that
n_{1}n_{2}=n_{3}+2
and
n_{2}n_{3}>6
. Assume that
\pi_{2}
is tempered at all places, and that there is at least one prime
p
where
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{3}
are all unramified, and such that at least one of the local parameters
\alpha_{\pi_{3},p}
(as in (
5.1
)) is not a parameter of
\pi_{1,p}
or
\pi_{2,p}
. If
G_{2}(s)=L(s,\pi_{1,\infty}\times\pi_{2,\infty})/L(s,\pi_{3,\infty})
has at most finitely many zeros, then
|S_{c,\pi_{3}}\setminus S_{c,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}}|=\infty
.

Proof.

Note that if
n_{1}n_{2}\leq 6
, the corollary already follows from Theorem
1.1
, since
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
is known to be automorphic in these cases (
[
Ram00
]
,
[
KS02
]
). As we have recorded in Theorem
5.9
,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
satisfies all the hypotheses of Theorem
9.6
. Thus, if the quotient
F_{2}(s)
has only finitely many poles in
{\mathbb{C}}
, there is finite set
S
of primes such that

L_{S}(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})=L_{S}(s,\pi_{3})L_{S}(s,\tau)

(12.2)

with
\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
. One checks easily in the proof of Theorem
9.6
that we choose
S
so that
p\notin S
.
Since one of the local
parameters
\alpha_{\pi_{3},p}
(as in (
5.1
)) is not a parameter of
\pi_{1,p}
or
\pi_{2,p}
, we know that

L_{S}(s,\pi_{3})L_{S}(s,\tau)\neq L_{S}(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
, giving a contradiction.
∎

In view of Remark
5.11
, we can formulate similar corollaries involving the exterior and symmetric square
L
-functions.

13.
An example when
G_{2}
has infinitely many zeros

When
\pi,\rho\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}
and
G_{2}
has infinitely many zeros, we know that
F_{2}
has infinitely many poles in
{\mathbb{C}}
, but our arguments thus far have not been able to show that infinitely many of these lie in the critical strip
0<\re(s)<1
. In this section, we will be able to obtain this more subtle conclusion for one class of pairs.

Let
\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{\circ}
such that
\tau
is not CM and
\tau_{\infty}
is a (limit of) discrete series representation of weight
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In this
case, we have

L(s,{\sym}^{2}\tau_{\infty})=\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{s+k+1}{2}\right)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{s+k-1}{2}\right),

(13.1)

while
L(s,{\sym}^{0}\tau_{\infty})=L(s,1_{\infty})=\Gamma(s/2)
, where
1
denotes the trivial representation of
{\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. If
k
is odd,

G_{2}(s)=\frac{L(s,{\sym}^{2}\tau_{\infty})}{L(s,{\sym}^{0}\tau_{\infty})}=[(s+k-2)(s+k-3)\cdots s]\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{s+k+1}{2}\right)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)

has only finitely many zeros, so this is a special case of Theorem
1.1
. When
k
is even,
G_{2}
has infinitely many zeros, and does not fit into our usual template. Nonetheless, we can proceed as follows.

As before, we denote the quotient
L(s,{\sym}^{2}\tau)/\zeta(s)
by
F_{2}(s)
. Recall that
{\sym}^{2}\tau
is cuspidal by the work of Gelbart and Jacquet in
[
GJ76
]
.
Suppose
F_{2}
has finitely many poles. Then, by the arguments we have seen before,
F_{2}\in{\mathfrak{G}}^{\#}
and it satisfies (G5). By Theorem
4.1
,
F_{2}(s,\chi)
has no poles in the strip
0<\re(s)\leq 1
for every non-principal character
\chi
.

Let
\chi_{1}\neq 1
be an even primitive Dirichlet character and let
F_{3}(s)=L(s,\chi_{1})F_{2}(s)
. We let
G_{3}(s)=L(s,\chi_{1,\infty})G_{2}(s)=L(s,{\sym}^{2}\tau_{\infty})
.
Since
L(s,\chi)
is entire,
F_{3}(s,\chi_{1}\times\chi)
has no poles in the strip
0<\re(s)\leq 1
for any primitive Dirichlet character

\chi\neq 1
. Outside of the critical strip, the poles of
F_{3}(s,\chi_{1}\times\chi)
are necessarily among the zeros of

G_{2}(s,\chi_{1}\times\chi)
. It follows that
G_{3}(s,\chi_{1}\times\chi)F_{3}(s,\chi_{1}\times\chi)
is entire.

Recall that
F_{2,p}(s)=(1-\alpha_{p}^{2}p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\alpha_{p}^{-2}p^{-s})^{-1}
at every place where
\tau
is unramified.
After twisting by a primitive Dirichlet character
\chi_{0}
that is sufficiently highly ramified at the places
R
where
\tau
and
\chi_{1}
are ramified, and unramified outside
R
, we can assume that
(\chi_{1,p}\times\chi_{0,p})\boxplus(\tau_{p}\times\chi_{0,p})
is a ramified principal series representation, and that
F_{3,p}(s,\chi_{0})=1
for
p\in R
. For any prime
p
, we set

\pi_{3,p}=(\chi_{1,p}\times\chi_{0,p})\boxplus(\tau_{p}\times\chi_{0,p}).

The factor
G_{3}(s,\chi_{0,\infty})
determines an irreducible admissible representation
\pi_{3,\infty}
of
{{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{R}})}
.
Let
\pi_{3}=\otimes_{v}\pi_{v}^{\prime}
. If
\chi
is a Dirichlet character unramified at all the places of
R
, and
T
is the
set of places where it is ramified,
\pi_{3}\times\chi
is unramified outside of
S=R\bigcup T
. It follows that
\pi_{3}\times\chi
is an irreducible admissible representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Clearly
\Lambda(s,\pi_{3}\times\chi)=G_{3}(s,\chi)F_{3}(s,\chi)
is entire of order
1
and satisfies a functional equation of the form (
5.14
). Thus, all the hypotheses of the converse theorem of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika for
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
in
[
JPSS79
]
are satisfied, so
\pi_{3}
is quasi automorphic. This leads to the equation

L_{S}(s,{\sym}^{2}\tau)L_{S}(s,\chi_{0})=\zeta_{S}(s)L_{S}(s,\pi_{3}^{\prime})

for some
\pi_{3}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{3}
. The right hand side above obviously has a pole at
s=1
, while the left hand side is holomorphic at
s=1
, giving a contradiction.
We have thus proved

Theorem 13.1
.

Let
\tau\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{\circ}
. Assume that
\tau
is not CM and that
\tau_{\infty}
is a (limit of) discrete series representation. Then
L(s,{\sym}^{2}\tau)/\zeta(s)
has infinitely many poles in the strip
0<\re(s)<1
.

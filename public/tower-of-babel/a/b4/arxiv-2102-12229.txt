Convergence in the maximum norm of ADI-type methods for parabolic problems
S. González-Pinto and D. Hernández-Abreu

Abstract
Results on unconditional convergence in the Maximum norm for ADI-type methods, such as the Douglas method, applied to the time integration of semilinear parabolic problems are quite difficult to get, mainly when the number of space dimensions m m is greater than two. Such a result is obtained here under quite general conditions on the PDE problem in case that time-independent Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed. To get these bounds, a theorem that guarantees, in some sense, power-boundeness of the stability function independently of both the space and time resolutions is proved.



1
Introduction

The present article considers the numerical solution of ODE systems

\dot{U}=D\,U+g(t),\quad U(0)=U_{0},\quad t\in[0,t^{*}],\qquad D:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}D_{j},\quad g(t):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{j}(t),

(1)

stemming from the spatial discretization by using finite differences (or finite volumes) of semilinear parabolic PDEs with constant diffusion coefficients
\beta_{j}>0
and an initial condition and Dirichlet Boundary Conditions (BCs) of the form

\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}u(t,\vec{x})=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}\,\partial_{x_{j}x_{j}}u(t,\vec{x})+c(t,\vec{x}),\quad t\in[0,t^{*}],\quad\vec{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})^{\top}\in\mathcal{I}:=(0,1)^{m},\\
u(0,\vec{x})=u_{0}(\vec{x}),\;\vec{x}\in\partial\mathcal{I};\qquad u(t,\vec{x})=h(t),\;t\in(0,t^{*}],\;\vec{x}\in\partial\mathcal{I}.\end{array}

(2)

Here,
c(t,\vec{x})
is a source term and we assume that its discretization is entirely included in
g_{1}(t)
, so that
g_{j}(t),\>j=2,\ldots,m
, consist only of contributions from the boundary conditions in the
j
-direction. In particular, we shall be concerned with time-independent Dirichlet boundary conditions, in which case the vectors
g_{2}(t),\ldots,g_{m}(t)
are constant.

To prove convergence in the maximum norm for many numerical methods of splitting type applied to (
1
), it is customary to get uniform bounds for
\|R(\tau D_{1},\ldots,\tau D_{m})^{n}\|_{\infty},\;n=1,2,\ldots,t^{*}/\tau
, where
\tau>0
is the time stepsize and
R(\tau D_{1},\ldots,\tau D_{m})
is a rational mapping acting on the matrices
\tau D_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq m
. Typically, we have
D_{j}=\beta_{j}\,(I_{N_{m}}\otimes\ldots\otimes L_{j}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{N_{1}})
, with
L_{j}=\mbox{\rm tridiag}(1,-2,1)/\Delta x_{j}^{2}
when second order central differences are considered in the spatial discretization of (
2
). Here, we denote the spacing
\Delta x_{j}=1/(N_{j}+1)
, where
N_{j}
is the number of equidistant grid-points on the
j
-direction, and
\otimes
stands for the Kronecker product of matrices. It should be observed that the matrices
D_{j}
pairwise commute. Such methods of splitting type when applied to (
1
) typically produce a recursion for the global errors
E_{n}:=U_{n}-U(t_{n})
,
n\geq 0
, of the form
E_{n+1}=RE_{n}+S_{n}
, where
U_{n}
stands for the numerical solution at
t_{n}=n\tau
,
S_{n}
denotes the local error and
R
is the stability matrix associated to the numerical integrator. For ADI-type integrators the stability matrix depends on
D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}
(see, e.g.,
[
10
, Sec. II.2.3]
). A relevant example is

R(\tau D_{1},\ldots,\tau D_{m})=I+\Pi(\theta)^{-1}\tau D,\quad D=\sum_{j=1}^{m}D_{j},

(3)

where
\Pi(\theta)=(I-\theta\tau D_{1})\cdots(I-\theta\tau D_{m})
, which has the associated
stability function of
m
complex variables

R(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m})=1+\frac{z}{Q},\quad z:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j},\quad Q:=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\theta z_{j}).

(4)

For the choice

\theta=1/2
, this is the stability matrix of the Peaceman-Rachford method (when
m=2
), also the one of the Douglas scheme (
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
, p. 373]
)
and the one of the one-stage AMF-W-method

[
4
]
. Furthermore, the stability matrix of the so-called
Hundsdorfer–Verwer scheme
[
10
, Section IV.5.2]
,
which is a
2
-stage W-method
of order
2
in general, and of order
3
for
\theta=(3+\sqrt{3})/6
,
is given by

R(\tau D_{1},\ldots,\tau D_{m})=I+2\Pi(\theta)^{-1}\tau D-\Pi(\theta)^{-2}\tau D+\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\Pi(\theta)^{-1}\tau D\bigr)^{2}.

In this case the stability function is given by

R(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m})=1+\frac{2z}{Q}+\frac{z^{2}-2z}{2Q^{2}}

(with
z
and
Q
defined in (
4
)). The power boundedness in the maximum norm of some
R
-stability functions was already considered in
[
5
]
. However, for
m\geq 3
the power bound there obtained is not uniform, since it allows a logarithmic growth in terms of
n
or
N

[
5
]
, i.e.,

\|R(\tau D_{1},\ldots,\tau D_{m})^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{m}\min\{(\ln(n+1))^{m},\>(\ln(N+1))^{m}\},\;\tau>0,\;n\tau\leq t^{*},\;\;N=\max\{N_{j},\>j=1,\ldots,m\}.

With this power bound, convergence results in the maximum norm of size
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p}|\log(\tau)|^{m})
, when the local errors are of size
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p+1})
can be obtained. However, with power bounds of the stability matrix as the one in (
5
), it can be shown convergence of size
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p})
in case of time independent BCs in (
2
). In Section
2
, we prove a result related to the power boundedness for rational functions. This result is applied in Section
3
to show unconditional convergence in the maximum norm for some ADI-methods. In Section 4, numerical experiments are included to illustrate the orders of convergence regarding the PDE solution for some relevant ADI-type methods.

2
Bounds in the maximum norm for rational functions

We look for bounds in the maximum norm of the form

\begin{array}[]{c}\|D^{-1}R(\tau D_{1},\tau D_{2},\ldots,\tau D_{m})^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq C<\infty,\quad t_{n}=n\tau,\;n=0,1,2,\ldots,\;\tau>0,\\[6.0pt]
D=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}D_{j},\quad D_{j}=\beta_{j}(I_{m}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{j+1}\otimes L_{j}\otimes I_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{1}),\\[6.0pt]
L_{j}=\frac{1}{\Delta x_{j}^{2}}\hbox{\rm tridiag}(1,\>-2,\>1)_{N_{j}},\quad I_{j}\;\hbox{\rm is the identity matrix of dimension }N_{j},\quad\Delta x_{j}=\frac{1}{N_{j}+1},\end{array}

(5)

where
\displaystyle R(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})
is a rational function (or a mapping when acting on the matrices
D_{j}
) of
m
complex variables that is
A_{m}(\alpha)
-stable, i.e.

\begin{array}[]{c}|R(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})|\leq 1,\quad\forall z_{j}\in\mathcal{W}(\alpha):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\>|\arg(-z)|\leq\alpha\}\cup\{0\},\quad\hbox{\rm for some }0<\alpha\leq\pi/4.\end{array}

(6)

Of course, if (
6

\bar{\alpha}\in(0,\pi/2]
then it also holds for
\alpha=\min\{\bar{\alpha},\>\pi/4\}
.

Theorem 1

If

is a rational function that satisfies (
6

K
only depending on
\alpha
and
m
such that (
5

C=K/\hat{\beta},\>\hat{\beta}=:\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\beta_{j}>0

For any matrix

(7)

(8)

\|(zI_{j}-\tau L_{j})^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{8\tau-|z|},\quad\forall|z|<8\tau,

(9)

and

\|(zI_{j}-\tau L_{j})^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{|z|-4\tau/\Delta x_{j}^{2}},\quad\forall|z|>4\tau/\Delta x_{j}^{2}>0.

(10)

Proof.
The formula in (

To show (

\displaystyle\|(zI_{j}-\tau L_{j})^{-1}\|_{\infty}=\|(\tau L_{j})^{-1}(I_{j}-z\tau^{-1}L_{j}^{-1})^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\tau^{-1}\|L_{j}^{-1}\|_{\infty}}{1-|z|\tau^{-1}\|L_{j}^{-1}\|_{\infty}}\leq\frac{\tau^{-1}8^{-1}}{1-|z|\tau^{-1}8^{-1}}=\frac{1}{8\tau-|z|}.

\Box

Lemma 2

Assume that for any positive integer
m
we have that

z_{j}=-r_{j}e^{{\rm i}\theta_{j}},\quad r_{j}>0,\;-\alpha\leq\theta_{j}\leq\alpha,\;(j=1,2,\ldots,m),\quad 0\leq\alpha\leq\pi/4.

(11)

Then

\displaystyle|\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}|\geq\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{m}|z_{j}|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|z_{j}|\geq\sqrt{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}|z_{j}|^{1/m}.

(12)

Proof.
The last two inequalities in (

|z_{1}+z_{2}|^{2}=|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+2{\rm Re}(z_{1}\overline{z}_{2})\geq|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}.

Hence,
s_{2}:=z_{1}+z_{2}
fulfils
|s_{2}|\geq\sqrt{|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}}
and it has an angle
|\hat{\theta}_{2}|\leq\alpha

(\leq\pi/4)
with the negative
x-
axis. In particular,
s_{2}
takes the form (
11
). Then, adding a new complex number
z_{3}
(
11
) and using the same argument we deduce that
s_{3}:=s_{2}+z_{3}
fulfils
|s_{3}|\geq\sqrt{|s_{2}|^{2}+|z_{3}|^{2}}\geq\sqrt{|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+|s_{3}|^{2}}
and it has an angle
|\hat{\theta}_{3}|\leq\alpha

(\leq\pi/4)
with the negative
x-

\Box

3
Convergence in the uniform norm of some ADI-type methods

)-(

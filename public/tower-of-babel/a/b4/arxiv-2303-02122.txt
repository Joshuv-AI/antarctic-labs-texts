Abstract
. A well-known result of R. Pol states that a Banach space X X has property ( 𝒞 \mathcal{C} ) of Corson if and only if every point in the weak*-closure of any convex set C ⊆ B X ∗ C\subseteq B_{X^{*}} is actually in the weak*-closure of a countable subset of C C . Nevertheless, it is an open problem whether this is in turn equivalent to the countable tightness of B X ∗ B_{X^{*}} with respect to the weak*-topology. Frankiewicz, Plebanek and Ryll-Nardzewski provided an affirmative answer under MA + ¬ CH \mathrm{MA}+\neg\mathrm{CH} for the class of 𝒞 ⁡ ( K ) \mathcal{C}(K) -spaces. In this article we provide a partial extension of this latter result by showing that under the Proper Forcing Axiom ( PFA \mathrm{PFA} ) the following conditions are equivalent for an arbitrary Banach space X X : (1) X X has property ℰ ′ \mathcal{E}^{\prime} ; (2) X X has weak*-sequential dual ball; (3) X X has property ( 𝒞 \mathcal{C} ) of Corson; (4) ( B X ∗ , w ∗ ) (B_{X^{*}},w^{\ast}) has countable tightness. This provides a partial extension of a former result of Arhangel’skii. In addition, we show that every Banach space with property ℰ ′ \mathcal{E}^{\prime} has weak*-convex block compact dual ball.

A Banach space
X

\mathcal{C}

X

\mathcal{C}

Theorem

(Pol
[

A Banach space
X
has property
(\mathcal{C})
if and only if every point in the weak*-closure of a convex set
C\subseteq B_{X^{\ast}}
is in the weak*-closure of a countable subset of
C

Recall that a topological space
T
is said to have
countable tightness
if for every subspace
F
of
T
, every point in the closure of
F
is in the closure of a countable subspace of
F
. Thus, if the dual ball of a Banach space
X
has countable tightness (with respect to the weak*-topology) then
X
has property
(\mathcal{C})
of Corson. A natural question is whether the reverse implication holds.
To the best of our knowledge, this question is open (see
[
11

14

12

Both countable tightness and property
(\mathcal{C})
of Corson are closely related to several classical sequential properties. Before diving into deeper waters let us fix the notation and state some of the topological notions under consideration.
All topological spaces considered in the manuscript are assumed to be Hausdorff. The symbol
w^{\ast}
will be reserved to denote the weak* topology of a Banach space.
Recall that a topological space
T
is said to be
sequentially compact
if every sequence in
T
contains a convergent subsequence; whereas it is said to be
Fréchet-Urysohn
(FU for short) if for every subspace
F
of
T
, every point in the closure of
F
is the limit of a sequence in
F
. Thus, every FU compact space is sequentially compact and has countable tightness.
For our purpose and to follow the standard terminology in the field, let us just say that a Banach space is said to have
weak*-angelic dual
if it has weak*-FU dual ball.

In this article we shall pay particular attention to a property which is between being FU and having countable tightness.
A topological space
T
is said to be
sequential
if any sequentially closed subspace is closed. Recall that a set is said to be
sequentially closed
if it coincides with its sequential closure, i.e. with the set of all limits of sequences in the set.
It is immediate that every FU space is sequential and that every sequential compact space is sequentially compact.
It is a well-known fact that every sequential space has countable tightness. However, whether the converse implication holds for the class of compact spaces is known as the
Moore-Mrowka Problem
, which is undecidable on the grounds of ZFC. On the one hand, Balogh proved in
[
3
]
that under the
Proper Forcing Axiom
(PFA) a compact space has countable tightness if and only if it is sequential. On the other hand, Fedorchuk
[
10
]
provided a consistent example of a compact space with countable tightness with no convergent subsequences, hence non-sequentially compact. Later variations of Balogh’s theorem not bearing on PFA (hence, bypassing the use of large cardinal) have been obtained by Dow
[
8
]
and, more recently, by Dow and Eisworth
[
9
]
.

Thereby for a compact space

The last property considered in this article is a convex version of sequential compactness:

Property

Let us start this section showing that property
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
implies property
(\mathcal{C})
.

Lemma 2
.

If a Banach space
X
has property
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
then
X
has property
(\mathcal{C})
of Corson.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq B_{X^{*}}
be a convex set and define

S_{0}:=\{x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}\mid\mbox{there is a countable set }D\subseteq S\mbox{ such that }x^{*}\in\overline{D}^{w^{*}}\}.

We have to prove that
\overline{S}^{w^{*}}=S_{0}
. Notice that
S_{0}
is sequentially closed hence, as
X
has property
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and
S\subseteq S_{0}\subseteq\overline{S}^{w^{*}}
, it suffices to show that
S_{0}
is convex.
Let
x^{*},y^{*}\in S_{0}
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Then, there exist countable sets
\{x_{n}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{y_{n}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
in
S
such that
x^{*}\in\overline{\{x_{n}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{w^{*}}
and
y^{*}\in\overline{\{y_{n}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{w^{*}}
. We claim that

\lambda x^{*}+(1-\lambda)y^{*}\in\overline{\{\lambda x_{n}^{*}+(1-\lambda)y_{m}^{*}\mid n,m\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{w^{*}}.

Let
U
be any neighborhood of
\lambda x^{*}+(1-\lambda)y^{*}
. Then, there exist neighborhoods
U_{1}
of
x^{*}
and
U_{2}
of
y^{*}
such that

\lambda U_{1}+(1-\lambda)U_{2}:=\{\lambda z_{1}^{*}+(1-\lambda)z_{2}^{*}\mid z_{1}^{*}\in U_{1},~z_{2}^{*}\in U_{2}\}\subseteq U.

Since
x^{*}\in\overline{\{x_{n}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{w^{*}}
and
y^{*}\in\overline{\{y_{n}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{w^{*}}
, there are
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{n}^{*}\in U_{1}
and
y_{m}^{*}\in U_{2}
, so
\lambda x_{n}^{*}+(1-\lambda)y_{m}^{*}\in\lambda U_{1}+(1-\lambda)U_{2}\subseteq U
, as desired.
∎

The goal of this section is to prove the following result, which was part of the first’s author Ph.D. thesis:

Theorem 3
.

Every Banach space with property
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
has weak*-convex block compact dual ball.

Given a Banach space
X
and a set
C\subseteq B_{X^{*}}
we denote by
S(C)
its weak*-sequential closure; i.e.

S(C)
is the set of limits of weak*-convergent sequences in
C
. It is immediate that
\overline{C}^{\|\cdot\|}\subseteq S(C)
for every set
C\subseteq B_{X}
. Notice that
S(C)
might not be sequentially closed. Nevertheless, the situation can be even worse, since the weak*-sequential closure of a bounded convex set
C
might not be norm-closed (see
[
2
, Example 5.2]
).
This fact motivates the following auxiliary lemma which will be used in the proof of Theorem
3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with property
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
C\subseteq B_{X^{\ast}}
a convex subset. If the weak*-sequential closure of
C
is equal to
\overline{C}^{\|\cdot\|}
then
\overline{C}^{w^{\ast}}=\overline{C}^{\|\cdot\|}
.

Proof.

Obviously
\overline{C}^{\|\cdot\|}
is contained in
S(C)
. Suppose
S(C)=\overline{C}^{\|\cdot\|}
but
\overline{C}^{w^{\ast}}\neq\overline{C}^{\|\cdot\|}
. Then
S(C)
is not weak*-closed. Since
X
has property
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
, there exists a sequence
x_{n}^{\ast}\in S(C)
which converges to a point
x^{\ast}\notin S(C)
. It follows from the equality
S(C)=\overline{C}^{\|\cdot\|}
that there exists
y_{n}^{\ast}\in C
such that
\|y_{n}^{\ast}-x_{n}^{\ast}\|<\frac{1}{2^{n}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

y_{n}^{\ast}=x_{n}^{\ast}+(y_{n}^{\ast}-x_{n}^{\ast})
is weak*-convergent to
x^{\ast}
. Thus
x^{\ast}\in S(C)
in contradiction with our assumption.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
A\subseteq B_{X^{\ast}}
a norm-separable weak*-closed set. Then
A
is weak*-metrizable.

Proof.

Set
D=\{x_{n}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq A
a countable norm-dense set and fix
x_{i,j}\in B_{X}
for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{\|x_{i}^{*}-x_{j}^{*}\|}{2}\leq x_{i}^{*}(x_{i,j})-x_{j}^{*}(x_{i,j})
.
If
x^{\ast},y^{\ast}\in A
are different then we can take
x^{*}_{i},x^{*}_{j}\in D
with
\|x^{\ast}-x_{i}^{*}\|<\frac{\|x^{\ast}-y^{\ast}\|}{8}
and
\|y^{\ast}-x_{j}^{*}\|<\frac{\|x^{\ast}-y^{\ast}\|}{8}
. Then
\|x_{i}^{*}-x_{j}^{*}\|>\frac{3\|x^{*}-y^{*}\|}{4}
and

x^{*}(x_{i,j})-y^{*}(x_{i,j})>x_{i}^{*}(x_{i,j})-\frac{\|x^{\ast}-y^{\ast}\|}{8}-x_{j}^{*}(x_{i,j})-\frac{\|x^{\ast}-y^{\ast}\|}{8}\geq

\geq\frac{\|x_{i}^{*}-x_{j}^{*}\|}{2}-\frac{\|x^{\ast}-y^{\ast}\|}{4}>0.

Thus, the set
\{x_{i,j}\mid i,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
gives a countable family of weak*-continuous functions on the weak*-compact
A
which separates points of
A
. Thus,
A
is weak*-metrizable.
∎

\leq\frac{1}{2^{r}}+\biggl(\frac{2^{r}}{2^{r}-1}-1\biggr)=\frac{1}{2^{r}}+\frac{1}{2^{r}-1},

where the last inequality follows from

\sum_{k\in I_{r+1}}\beta_{k}^{n_{r}}=1-\sum_{k\in I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup\dots\cup I_{r}}\beta_{k}^{n_{r}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{2^{r}}=\frac{2^{r}-1}{2^{r}}.

Since
\|z_{n_{r}}^{\ast}-u_{r+1}^{\ast}\|
converges to zero, we conclude that
(u_{r}^{\ast})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
is a convex block subsequence of
(x_{n}^{\ast})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which is weak*-convergent with the same limit than
(z_{n}^{\ast})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

The question of whether property

In

]
an example of a Banach space with property

Question 10
.

Is it consistent with
\mathrm{ZFC}
that properties
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
coincide?

Furthermore, yet another example of a Banach space with property
\mathcal{E}
but without weak*-angelic dual was constructed in
[
2
]
, also under CH. Once again, it seems to be unknown whether it is consistent with ZFC that these two properties coincide.

Finally, concerning properties
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
(\mathcal{C})

Question 11
.

(1)

Does every Banach space with property
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
have weak*-sequential dual ball?

(2)

Does every Banach space with property
(\mathcal{C})
of Corson have dual ball with countable tightness (with respect to the weak*-topology)?

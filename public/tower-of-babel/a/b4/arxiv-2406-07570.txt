Corrigendum to: Elliptic Boundary Value Problems with Fractional Regularity Data: The First Order Approach
Alex Amenta, Pascal Auscher, Moritz Egert

Abstract
. This is a corrigendum to the monograph “Elliptic Boundary Value Problems with Fractional Regularity Data: The First Order Approach” by the first two authors. It addresses two major imprecisions in the preliminary material.

A correction on interpolation results

The interpolation results, in particular in Section 2.3, require two main adjustments. To this end, it is instructive to consider the exponent
\mathbf{p}
in the
(j,\theta)
-plane and distinguish three regions as in the figure below: The finite range
j(\mathbf{p})>0
, the infinite range
j(\mathbf{p})\leq 0
and the duality range
0\leq j(\mathbf{p})<1
.

Figure 1.
Exponent regions
: The gray duality range described by
0\leq j(\mathbf{p})<1
corresponds to the spaces that can be obtained as duals of Banach spaces with finite exponent
\mathbf{p}
(that is,
j(\mathbf{p})>0
). It lies in the intersection of finite exponents and infinite exponents with
j(\mathbf{p})=0
. The geometrically interpolating exponent
[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\theta}
captures the interpolation behaviour of function spaces if
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
.

The first adjustment is that interpolation of tent spaces,
Z
-spaces and homogeneous smoothness spaces only translates to taking convex combinations of exponents in the
(j,\theta)
-plane, if we restrict ourselves to the right half-plane with
j(\mathbf{p})\geq 0
.

For example, real interpolation of a Besov space
\dot{B}^{\infty,\infty}_{s+\alpha}
with
\dot{B}^{p,p}_{t}
for finite
p
always yields a Besov space with finite exponent — no matter how far to the left the exponent
\mathbf{p}=(\infty,s;\alpha)
, corresponding to
(-\alpha/n,s)
, lies in the
(j,\theta)
-plane, compare with the references in Section 2.3.

The additional assumption
j(\mathbf{p})\geq 0
is a (harmless) restriction for results on tent and Hardy–Sobolev spaces.

For
Z
-spaces, we see from Definition 2.15 that
Z^{\infty,s;\alpha}=Z^{\infty,s+\alpha;0}
, and the same applies to Besov spaces. Hence, the same space can have different parametrisations in the
(j,\theta)
-plane and for a correct formulation of interpolation results we have to pick the unique one with
j(\mathbf{p})\geq 0
. In other words, when
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}
are infinite with
0=j(\mathbf{p})
and
\mathbf{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{q}
, then we have by definition that
Z^{\mathbf{q}}=Z^{\mathbf{p}}
. The distinct
Z
-spaces are only those with
j(\mathbf{p})\geq 0
. The same applies for the Besov scale.

The second adjustment is connected to a general strategy of proof for interpolation results in three steps: first one checks the finite range, then gets the duality range by duality methods for interpolation functors, and finally one gets all exponents by Wolff reiteration because the finite range and the duality range have a substantial overlap. The issue is that no Wolff reiteration is known for the complex interpolation method of Kalton–Mitrea!

The note
[
5
]
contains a proof of the usual Wolff reiteration theorem for four
p
-convex quasi-Banach function spaces, where only one of the ‘exterior spaces’ needs to be separable, provided that all four spaces have the so-called Fatou property. For details, we refer to
[
5
]
, whereas for the purpose of this corrigendum it suffices to note that tent- and
Z
-spaces are covered if one of the ‘exterior exponents’ is finite, which is the case in the scenario described above. This is how Wolff reiteration for complex interpolation spaces has to be understood in the context of Section 2.3.

We come to the concrete changes in the text that follow from the above two main adjustments and their consequences. All other results in the monograph remain true as stated with the sole exception of Theorem 7.8, for which we describe the modification further below.

Changes in Section 2.3

The corrected statement and proof for real interpolation is as follows. We need to assume
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
, also in order to justify duality for real interpolation.

Theorem 2.30 (Real interpolation of tent spaces: full range).

Suppose that
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
are exponents with
\theta(\mathbf{p})\neq\theta(\mathbf{q})
and
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
, and
0<\eta<1
.
Then

(T^{\mathbf{p}},T^{\mathbf{q}})_{\eta,p_{\eta}}=Z^{[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\eta}},

where
p_{\eta}=i([\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\eta})
.

Proof.

For finite exponents this is Theorem 2.18.
Let now
1<i(\mathbf{p}),i(\mathbf{q})\leq\infty
. Since
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
, we have that
T^{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}},T^{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}
are Banach spaces. Hence, in this case the claim follows by writing

(T^{\mathbf{p}},T^{\mathbf{q}})_{\eta,p_{\eta}}=((T^{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}})^{\prime},(T^{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}})^{\prime})_{\eta,p_{\eta}}=(T^{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}},T^{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}})_{\eta,p_{\eta}^{\prime}}^{\prime}

via the duality theorem for real interpolation [22, Theorem 3.7.1], using that
T^{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\cap T^{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}
is dense in both
T^{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
and
T^{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}
, and then noting that

p_{\eta}^{\prime}=i([\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\eta})^{\prime}=i([\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime}]_{\eta}).

The full result follows by Wolff reiteration [76, Theorem 1] which, for the real method holds in the quasi-Banach setting.
∎

In the subsequent result on real interpolation, the same strategy has to be used. For finite exponents one uses reiteration (but the one allowing quasi-Banach spaces in [22, Theorem 3.11.4]). Then duality and Wolff reiteration complete the argument as before. The assumption
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
is needed again, however, as in Theorem 2.30, they can both be infinite:

Proposition 2.31 (Real interpolation of
Z
-spaces).

Let
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
be exponents with
\theta(\mathbf{p})\neq\theta(\mathbf{q})
and
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
, and let
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Then

(Z^{\mathbf{p}},Z^{\mathbf{q}})_{\eta,p_{\eta}}=Z^{[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\eta}},

where
p_{\eta}=i([\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\eta})
.

Also in Proposition 2.33 we need
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
. The strategy is once again the same and the case of finite exponents is what is being deferred to Section 2.6. Duality for the complex method on Banach spaces requires one space to be reflexive, which explains why the assumption that one exponent is finite has to be maintained as in the original text.

Proposition 2.32 (Complex interpolation of
Z
-spaces).

Let
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
be exponents with
j(\mathbf{p}),j(\mathbf{q})\geq 0
and equality for at most one of them, and let
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Then

[Z^{\mathbf{p}},Z^{\mathbf{q}}]_{\eta}=Z^{[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\eta}}.

The restriction to exponents with
j(\mathbf{p})\geq 0
also requires some more care in the proof of the mixed embedding in Theorem 2.34 because in the proof
\mathbf{r}=[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{2}
may not satisfy
j(\mathbf{r})\geq 0
even if
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
are finite. A three step strategy as above is once again necessary and we give a corrected proof of the unchanged statement.

Theorem 2.34 (Mixed embeddings).

Let
X_{0},X_{1}\in\{T,Z\}
and let
\mathbf{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{q}
with
\mathbf{p}\neq\mathbf{q}
.
Then we have the embedding

(X_{0})^{\mathbf{p}}\hookrightarrow(X_{1})^{\mathbf{q}}.

Proof.

We first treat the case that
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
are finite. When
X_{0}=X_{1}=T
, this is Theorem 2.11.

Let
\mathbf{r}=[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{1+\epsilon}
with
\epsilon>0
small enough to guarantee that
\mathbf{r}
is still finite. Then
\mathbf{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{r}
and
[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r}]_{(1+\epsilon)^{-1}}=\mathbf{q}
(by Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2).
Then we have embeddings
T^{\mathbf{p}}\hookrightarrow T^{\mathbf{p}}
(trivially) and
T^{\mathbf{p}}\hookrightarrow T^{\mathbf{r}}
(Theorem 2.11).
Hence

T^{\mathbf{p}}\hookrightarrow(T^{\mathbf{p}},T^{\mathbf{r}})_{(1+\epsilon)^{-1},i([\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r}]_{(1+\epsilon)^{-1}})}=Z^{[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r}]_{(1+\epsilon)^{-1}}}=Z^{\mathbf{q}}

by Theorem 2.30, using that
\mathbf{p}\neq\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{q}
imply
\theta(\mathbf{p})\neq\theta(\mathbf{q})
.
Similarly, putting
\mathbf{s}=[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{-1}
, we have
T^{\mathbf{s}}\hookrightarrow T^{\mathbf{q}}
and
T^{\mathbf{q}}\hookrightarrow T^{\mathbf{q}}
, so

Z^{\mathbf{p}}=(T^{\mathbf{s}},T^{\mathbf{q}})_{1/2,i([\mathbf{s},\mathbf{q}]_{1/2})}\hookrightarrow T^{\mathbf{q}}.

Finally, putting
\mathbf{t}=[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{1/2}
and using the previous results, we have

Z^{\mathbf{p}}\hookrightarrow T^{\mathbf{t}}\hookrightarrow Z^{\mathbf{q}},

which completes the proof for finite exponents.

The next case is that
\mathbf{p^{\prime}}
and
\mathbf{q^{\prime}}
are both finite. Then the claim follows from the first case by duality and this covers the infinite exponents and those in the duality range, see the figure above.

The remaining case is that
\mathbf{q}
is infinite and
\mathbf{p}
is finite but not in the duality range. Then the connecting segment contains a finite exponent
\mathbf{r}=[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]_{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,1)
in the duality range. Combining the previous two cases yields

(X_{0})^{\mathbf{p}}\hookrightarrow(X_{0})^{\mathbf{r}}\hookrightarrow(X_{1})^{\mathbf{r}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Changes in Section 4.3

The interpolation results of Section 2.3 directly correspond to the interpolation results for adapted spaces in Theorem 4.28. Hence, this theorem should be read as follows:

Theorem 4.28 (Interpolation of completions).

Fix
0<\eta<1
and
\psi\in\Psi_{\infty}^{\infty}
.
Let
\mathbf{p}_{0}
and
\mathbf{p}_{1}
be exponents, and set
\mathbf{p}_{\eta}:=[\mathbf{p}_{0},\mathbf{p}_{1}]_{\eta}
.

(i)

Suppose
j(\mathbf{p}_{0}),j(\mathbf{p}_{1})\geq 0
, with equality for at most one exponent.
Then we have the identification

[\psi\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{0}},\psi\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{1}}]_{\eta}=\psi\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{\eta}}.

(ii)

Suppose
j(\mathbf{p}_{0}),j(\mathbf{p}_{1})\geq 0
, with equality for at most one exponent.
Then we have the identification

[\psi\mathbf{B}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{0}},\psi\mathbf{B}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{1}}]_{\eta}=\psi\mathbf{B}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{\eta}}.

(iii)

Suppose
j(\mathbf{p}_{0}),j(\mathbf{p}_{1})\geq 0
and that
\theta(\mathbf{p}_{0})\neq\theta(\mathbf{p}_{1})
.
Then we have the identification

(\psi\mathbf{X}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{0}},\psi\mathbf{X}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{1}})_{\eta,i(\mathbf{p}_{\eta})}=\psi\mathbf{B}_{A}^{\mathbf{p}_{\eta}}.

All of these statements have analogues for spectral subspaces.

Changes in Section 6.5

The interpolation results of Section 2.3 and Section 4.3 have been joined in the proof of Theorem 6.41. Hence, also in this theorem, (ii) needs the additional assumption
j(\mathbf{p_{1}})\geq 0
and in (iii) it is needed that
j(\mathbf{p_{0}}),j(\mathbf{p_{1}})\geq 0
.

Changes in Section 7.1

Theorem 7.8 is the only result, where infinite exponents mistakenly appear by interpolation between infinite and finite exponent. In fact, Šneĭberg’s extrapolation theorem [66] only applies to interior points of complex interpolation scales. This result only works for finite exponents. On the other hand, the restriction
i(\mathbf{p})>1
in the Besov case is unnecessary because complex interpolation for
Z
-spaces was also obtained in the quasi-Banach range in Proposition 2.32. (Section 2.6 was finalised at the very end of composing the monograph.) Theorem 7.8 and the succeeding remark should therefore be modified as follows.

Theorem 7.28 (Extrapolation of well-posedness)

Let
B=\hat{A}
, and suppose
\mathbf{p}\in J(\mathbf{X},DB)
is a finite exponent with
\theta(\mathbf{p})\in(-1,0)
.
Suppose that
(P_{\mathbf{X}})_{A}^{\mathbf{p}}
is well-posed.
Then there exists a
(j,\theta)
-neighbourhood
O_{\mathbf{p}}
of
\mathbf{p}
such that for all
\mathbf{q}\in O_{\mathbf{p}}
with
\theta(\mathbf{q})\leq 0
,
(P_{\mathbf{X}})_{A}^{\mathbf{q}}
and
(P_{\mathbf{X}})_{A}^{\mathbf{p}}
are mutually well-posed.

Remark 7.29

With
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{H}
, a corresponding result is true for
\theta(\mathbf{p})\in\{-1,0\}
, but well-posedness is only obtained for
\mathbf{q}
near
\mathbf{p}
with
\theta(\mathbf{q})=\theta(\mathbf{p})
. The proof uses the same argument.

It should be noted that the subsequent results (Corollary 7.10 and Theorem 7.11) are not affected by the modification above since they only use finite exponents anyway.

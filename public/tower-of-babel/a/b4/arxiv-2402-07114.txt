Towards Quantifying the Preconditioning Effect of Adam
Rudrajit Das, Naman Agarwal, Sujay Sanghavi, Inderjit S. Dhillon

Abstract
There is a notable dearth of results characterizing the preconditioning effect of Adam and showing how it may alleviate the curse of ill-conditioning – an issue plaguing gradient descent (GD). In this work, we perform a detailed analysis of Adam’s preconditioning effect for quadratic functions and quantify to what extent Adam can mitigate the dependence on the condition number of the Hessian. Our key finding is that Adam can suffer less from the condition number but at the expense of suffering a dimension-dependent quantity. Specifically, for a d d -dimensional quadratic with a diagonal Hessian having condition number κ \kappa , we show that the effective condition number-like quantity controlling the iteration complexity of Adam without momentum is 𝒪 ⁡ ( min ⁡ ( d , κ ) ) \mathcal{O}(\min(d,\kappa)) . For a diagonally dominant Hessian, we obtain a bound of 𝒪 ⁡ ( min ⁡ ( d ​ d ​ κ , κ ) ) \mathcal{O}(\min(d\sqrt{d\kappa},\kappa)) for the corresponding quantity. Thus, when d < 𝒪 ⁡ ( κ p ) d<\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{p}) where p = 1 p=1 for a diagonal Hessian and p = 1 / 3 p=1/3 for a diagonally dominant Hessian, Adam can outperform GD (which has an 𝒪 ⁡ ( κ ) \mathcal{O}(\kappa) dependence). On the negative side, our results suggest that Adam can be worse than GD for a sufficiently non-diagonal Hessian even if d ≪ 𝒪 ⁡ ( κ 1 / 3 ) d\ll\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{1/3}) ; we corroborate this with empirical evidence. Finally, we extend our analysis to functions satisfying per-coordinate Lipschitz smoothness and a modified version of the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition.

4.2
Fixed Points of Adam

Here we shall characterize the asymptotic point(s) to which Adam may converge.

Definition 2

(

)
.

\lim_{k\to\infty}f(\bm{x}_{k})=f(\overline{\bm{x}})

We have the following result regarding the fixed point(s)

of Adam for

Theorem 6

(
Fixed point(s) of Adam
)
.

\overline{\bm{x}}=\bm{x}_{*}
is a trivial fixed point. There may exist non-trivial fixed points depending on the value of
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}=\delta\geq 0
(note that this also covers the case of the constant sequence
\delta_{k}=\delta

\forall

k\geq 0

•

If

.

•

If

5
Beyond Quadratics: Per-Coordinate Smoothness and a Modified Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) Condition

Here we focus on functions that satisfy per-coordinate Lipschitz smoothness and a modified version of the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition. Let us begin by defining the standard global version (with respect to the coordinates) of smoothness and the standard PL condition.

Definition 3

(
Smoothness
)
.

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
is said to be
L
-smooth if
\forall

\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that
\|\nabla f(\bm{y})-\nabla f(\bm{x})\|_{2}\leq L\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}\|_{2}
.

Definition 4

(
Polyak- Łojasiewicz (PL) Condition
(
Karimi et al., 2016
)
)
.

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
with
f^{*}:=\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\bm{x})
is said to be
\mu
-PL if
\exists

\mu>0
such that:

\|\nabla f(\bm{x})\|_{2}^{2}\geq 2\mu(f(\bm{x})-f^{*})\text{ }\forall\text{ }\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Theorem 7

(
GD: Smooth and PL
(
Karimi et al., 2016
)
)
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
is
L
-smooth and
\mu
-PL, and let
\kappa=\frac{L}{\mu}
be the condition number.
Suppose our initialization is
\bm{x}_{0}
. Set the step-size
\alpha=\frac{1}{L}
. Then,
f(\bm{x}_{\widehat{K}})-f^{*}\leq\varepsilon
in

\widehat{K}=\mathcal{O}\Bigg({\kappa}\log\Bigg(\frac{f(\bm{x}_{0})-f^{*}}{\varepsilon}\Bigg)\Bigg)

iterations of GD.

Remark 10
.

Similar to the quadratic case (
Theorem

1
), the iteration complexity of GD depends on the condition number, i.e.,
\kappa=\frac{L}{\mu}
.

We will now introduce

smoothness and a

version of the PL condition for functions satisfying per-coordinate smoothness under which we show that Adam can do better than GD.

Definition 5

(
Per-Coordinate Smoothness
)
.

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
-smooth if for all
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for each coordinate

Similar assumptions have been made in the literature

Definition 6

Suppose

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)})^{2}}{\sqrt{2L_{i}}}\geq\sqrt{2\tilde{\mu}}\big(f(\bm{x})-f^{*}\big)\text{ }\forall\text{ }\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{ where $f^{*}=\min_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\bm{z})$.}

\bm{x}_{\tau}=\bm{x}+\tau(\bm{y}-\bm{x})
for
\tau\in[0,1]
. Using the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have:

\displaystyle\big|f(\bm{y})-\big(f(\bm{x})+\nabla f(\bm{x})^{\top}(\bm{y}-\bm{x})\big)\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\big(\nabla f(\bm{x}_{\tau})-\nabla f(\bm{x})\big)^{\top}(\bm{y}-\bm{x})d\tau\Big|

(200)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big|\Big(\nabla f(\bm{x}_{\tau})^{(i)}-\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)}\Big)({y}^{(i)}-{x}^{(i)})\Big|d\tau

(201)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}\big|{x}_{\tau}^{(i)}-x^{(i)}\big|\big|{y}^{(i)}-{x}^{(i)}\big|d\tau

(202)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}\big({y}^{(i)}-{x}^{(i)}\big)^{2}\tau d\tau

(203)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{L_{i}}\big({y}^{(i)}-{x}^{(i)}\big)^{2}

(204)

Thus,

f(\bm{y})\leq f(\bm{x})+\nabla f(\bm{x})^{\top}(\bm{y}-\bm{x})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{L_{i}}\big({y}^{(i)}-{x}^{(i)}\big)^{2}.

(205)

∎

Lemma 11
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
is
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
-smooth. Then for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
i\in[d]
, we have:

{(\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)})^{2}}\leq 2L_{i}\big(f(\bm{x})-\bar{f}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})\big),

where
\bar{f}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})={\min}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}\text{ }f(\bm{x}+h\bm{e}_{i})
. As a result, we also have:

{(\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)})^{2}}\leq 2L_{i}\big(f(\bm{x})-{f}^{*}\big),

where
f^{*}=\min_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\bm{z})
.

Proof.

From
Lemma

10
, we have for any
\bm{x},\bm{y}
:

f(\bm{y})\leq g(\bm{y}):=f(\bm{x})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)}\big({y}^{(i)}-{x}^{(i)}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{L_{i}}\big({y}^{(i)}-{x}^{(i)}\big)^{2}.

(206)

Let
\bar{f}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x}):={\min}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}\text{ }f(\bm{x}+h\bm{e}_{i})
and
\bar{g}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x}):={\min}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}\text{ }g(\bm{x}+h\bm{e}_{i})
; note that
\bar{f}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})\leq\bar{g}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})

\forall

\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
g(\bm{x}+h\bm{e}_{i})=f(\bm{x})+\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)}h+\frac{{L_{i}}h^{2}}{2}
,
we obtain:

\bar{g}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})=f(\bm{x})-\frac{\big(\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)}\big)^{2}}{2L_{i}}.

(207)

Thus,

\bar{f}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})\leq f(\bm{x})-\frac{\big(\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)}\big)^{2}}{2L_{i}}.

(208)

Rearranging this, we get:

{(\nabla f(\bm{x})^{(i)})^{2}}\leq 2L_{i}\big(f(\bm{x})-\bar{f}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})\big).

(209)

The other bound follows from the fact that
f_{i}^{*}\leq\bar{f}^{*}_{i}(\bm{x})
for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

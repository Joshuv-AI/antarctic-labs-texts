On the differentiability of interval functions Journal: Information Sciences
Walter F. Mascarenhas

Abstract
Two articles published by Information Sciences discuss the derivatives of interval functions in the sense of Svetoslav Markov. The authors of these articles tried to characterize for which functions and points such derivatives exist. Unfortunately, their characterization is inaccurate. This article describes this inaccuracy and explains how it can be corrected.

Introduction

In his 1979 article
[
1
]
,
Markov presented a Calculus for
interval functions
of a real variable,
i.e., functions
F
of the form

{F}\!\left(t\right)=[{f}\!\left(t\right),{g}\!\left(t\right)]
for

f,g:\Omega\subset\mathds{R}{}\rightarrow\mathds{R}{}
with
{f}\!\left(t\right)\leq{g}\!\left(t\right)
for

t\in\Omega
. (
\mathds{R}{}
is the set of real numbers.)
Assuming that
x
is in the interior of
\Omega
, as we
will ways do, he uses the operator
\ominus
, defined by

[\underline{a},\overline{a}]\ominus[\underline{b},\overline{b}]=\left[\,{\min}\!\left(\underline{a}-\underline{b},\overline{a}-\overline{b}\right),{\max}\!\left(\underline{a}-\underline{b},\overline{a}-\overline{b}\right)\right],

the usual division operator
/
for intervals and numbers, and the Hausdorff metric

{\mathrm{dist}}\!\left([\underline{a},\overline{a}],[\underline{b},\overline{b}]\right)={\max}\!\left(\,{\left|{\,\underline{a}-\underline{b}\,}\right|},\,{\left|{\,\overline{a}-\overline{b}\,}\right|}\,\right),

to define the derivative of
F
at
x
as

{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right):=\lim_{t\rightarrow x}\frac{{F}\!\left(t\right)\ominus{F}\!\left(x\right)}{t-x}.

(1)

The relevance of Markov’s
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
, in theory and in practice, was latter discussed in

[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, and readers unaware of it should read these articles
and some of their references.

Markov showed that if
f
and
g
are differentiable at
x
then

{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)=\left[\,{\min}\!\left({f}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)\right),\,{\max}\!\left({f}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)\right)\,\right],

and he also showed that
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
may exist in cases in which

{f}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
or
{g}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
does not exist. To illustrate this fact,
in page 331 of
[
1
]
, he considered the directional derivatives

{f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)=\lim_{t\uparrow 0}\frac{{f}\!\left(t\right)-{f}\!\left(x\right)}{t-x}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 28.45274pt{f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{{f}\!\left(t\right)-{f}\!\left(x\right)}{t-x}

and claimed that if
{f}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)
and
{g}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)

exist then
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
exists when

{f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)={g}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)\hskip 28.45274pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 28.45274pt{g}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)={f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right).

(2)

The authors of
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
misunderstood page 331
of
[
1
]
and this misunderstanding lead them to “prove”
a “theorem” which in our notation reads like

Inaccurate Theorem 1

(inaccurate) Theorem 6 in
[
2
]
, (inaccurate) Theorem 1 in
[
3
]
.
The function
{F}\!\left(t\right)=[{f}\!\left(t\right),{g}\!\left(t\right)]

has a derivative
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
at
x
if AND ONLY IF one of the following
cases hold:

(a)

The functions
f
and
g
are differentiable at
x
.

(b)

The derivatives
{f}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)
and

{g}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)

exist and Equation
\left({\ref{dpm}}\right)
holds.
\blacktriangle

Markov did not state the “ONLY IF” part of this “theorem.” In fact,
the next lemma shows that this part of “Theorem 1” is false:

Lemma 1

Let
{\mathds{Q}}{}
be the set of rational numbers.
The function
{F}\!\left(t\right)=[{f}\!\left(t\right),{g}\!\left(t\right)]
for

{f}\!\left(t\right):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{r}t\ \mathrm{if}\ t\in{\mathds{Q}}{}\cap\left({-1,1}\right),\\
0\ \mathrm{if}\ t\in\left({-1,1}\right)\setminus{}{\mathds{Q}}{}.\end{array}\right.\hskip 28.45274pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 28.45274pt{g}\!\left(t\right):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{r}1\ \mathrm{if}\ t\in{\mathds{Q}}{}\cap\left({-1,1}\right),\\
t+1\ \mathrm{if}\ t\in(-1,1)\setminus{}{\mathds{Q}}{}.\end{array}\right.

is such that
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(0\right)=[0,1]
, but

{f}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(0\right)
and
{g}^{\prime}_{\pm}\left(0\right)
do not exist.
\blacktriangle

The “IF” part of Theorem
1
, which is correct, follows from
the next theorem:

Theorem 2

If the right derivatives
{f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)
and
{g}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)
exist
then the right derivative

{F}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right):=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{{F}\!\left(t\right)\ominus{F}\!\left(x\right)}{t-x}

exists and

{F}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)=[{\min}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)\right),{\max}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)\right)].

(3)

Similarly, if the left derivatives

{f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)
and
{g}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)
exist
then the left derivative

{F}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right):=\lim_{t\uparrow 0}\frac{{F}\!\left(t\right)\ominus{F}\!\left(x\right)}{t-x}

exists and

{F}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)=[{\min}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)\right),{\max}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)\right)].\hfill\blacktriangle{}

(4)

Theorem
2
can be proved with the same
technique used by Markov to prove his Theorem 3.
We omit this proof for the sake of brevity, and because our
goal is to provide conditions under which we can fix the
“ONLY IF” part of “Theorem 1.”

The most general condition on
f
and
g
which we could find in order
to fix “Theorem 1” is the existence of
functions
\alpha,\beta,c,d:\Omega\rightarrow\mathds{R}{}

such that

\alpha,\beta
and
c
are continuous in
\Omega
,
{\alpha}\!\left(t\right)\neq{\beta}\!\left(t\right)
for
t\in\Omega
,

\alpha
and
\beta
are bounded,
d
is differentiable at
x
, with
{d}\!\left(x\right)={d}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)=0
and

{\alpha}\!\left(t\right){f}\!\left(t\right)+{\beta}\!\left(t\right){g}\!\left(t\right)={c}\!\left(t\right)+{d}\!\left(t\right).

(5)

This condition is reasonable and applies to most practical situations.
For instance, it holds if
f
is continuous in
\Omega
, in which case we can take
\alpha=1
,
\beta=0
,
c=f
and
d=0
.
It also holds when the length of
{F}\!\left(t\right)
,
{g}\!\left(t\right)-{f}\!\left(t\right)
, is continuous, in which case we can take

\alpha=-1
,
\beta=1
,
c=g-f
and
d=0
.
We formalize these results in the next theorem, which is
proved with other results in Section Proofs.

Theorem 3

Consider functions
\alpha,\beta,c,d:\Omega\rightarrow\mathds{R}{}

and
\mu>0
such that
{d}\!\left(x\right)=0
and

{\left|{{\alpha}\!\left(t\right)}\right|}+{\left|{{\beta}\!\left(t\right)}\right|}\leq\mu

and
\mu{\left|{\,{\alpha}\!\left(t\right)-{\beta}\!\left(t\right)\,}\right|}\geq 1

.
If
{F}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)

exists, Equation
\left({\ref{cond}}\right)
holds for

t\in\Omega\cap(x,+\infty)
,

\alpha,\beta
and
c
are
continuous in
\Omega\cap(x,+\infty)
and
{d}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(0\right)=0
,
then
{f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)
and
{g}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)
exist.
If
{F}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)

exists, Equation
\left({\ref{cond}}\right)
holds for

,

\alpha,\beta
and
c
are continuous in
\Omega\cap(-\infty,x)
and
{d}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(0\right)=0

then
{f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)
and
{g}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)

\blacktriangle

The last two theorems lead to a proper “ONLY IF” part for “Theorem 1”:

Corollary 4

If
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
exists and there exist functions
\alpha,\beta,c,d:\Omega\rightarrow\mathds{R}{}

such that
\alpha
,
\beta
and
c
are continuous,

{\alpha}\!\left(t\right)\neq{\beta}\!\left(t\right)
for

,

{d}\!\left(x\right)={d}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)=0

\alpha f+\beta g=c+d

{f}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)
and

{g}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)
exist and

\displaystyle{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\,{\min}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)\right),\,{\max}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{-}\!\left(x\right)\right)\,\right]

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\,{\min}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)\right),\,{\max}\!\left({f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right),{g}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)\right)\,\right].

In particular, if

{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
exists and
f
or

the directional derivatives
{f}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)
and
{g}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)

\left({\ref{ufa}}\right)

\blacktriangle

Combining this corollary with the next lemma, we end this introduction
with the simplest version of the ONLY IF part of “Theorem 1” that we could find:

Lemma 5

If
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
exists then
{f}\!\left(t\right)
and
{g}\!\left(t\right)
are continuous at
x
.
\blacktriangle

Corollary 6

If
{F}^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)
exists for all
x
in an open interval
(a,b)

then the directional derivatives
{f}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)
and

{g}^{\prime}_{\pm}\!\left(x\right)
exist for all

and satisfy Equation
\left({\ref{ufa}}\right)
.

\blacktriangle

The Denjoy-Young-Saks family of theorems
show that, in Corollary
6
,
the set
{\cal{S}}
of

at which

or

is not differentiable is “tiny.”
Formally, the Lebesgue measure of
{\cal{S}}
is zero and it
is of first category
(in Baire’s sense.) Actually, it is
\sigma

[
5

Thanks to the results above, it is quite likely that if
the inaccurate
“ONLY IF” part of “Theorem 1” was ever used then it caused no harm,
because their hypothesis would hold and ensure the validity
of the conclusions derived from “Theorem 1.” However, we still
believe that the minor technical point discussed in this article deserves
the attention of the potential users of “Theorem 1.”

,

Let us now prove that
{f}^{\prime}_{+}\!\left(x\right)=a

In fact, given
\epsilon>0
, Equations
\left({\ref{equd}}\right)
and
\left({\ref{eqod}}\right)

t_{\epsilon}\in(x,x+\delta)

such that if
t\in(x,t_{\epsilon})

Therefore, if
t\in(x,t_{\epsilon})

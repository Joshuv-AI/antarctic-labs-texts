Inverse problem in femtoscopic correlation functions: The 𝑻 𝒄 ​ 𝒄 ​ ( 𝟑𝟖𝟕𝟓 ) + T_{cc}(3875)^{+} state
M. Albaladejo, A. Feijoo, I. Vidaña, J. Nieves, E. Oset

Abstract
We study here the inverse problem of starting from the femtoscopic correlation functions of related channels and analyze them with an efficient tool to extract the maximum information possible on the interaction of the components of these channels, and the existence of possible bound states tied to this interaction. The method is flexible enough to accommodate non-molecular components and the effect of missing channels relevant for the interaction. We apply the method to realistic correlation functions for the D ∗ ⁣ + ​ D 0 D^{*+}D^{0} and D ∗ 0 ​ D + D^{*0}D^{+} channels derived consistently from the properties of the T c ​ c ​ ( 3875 ) + T_{cc}(3875)^{+} and find that we can extract the existence of a bound state, its nature as a molecular state of the D ∗ ⁣ + ​ D 0 D^{*+}D^{0} and D ∗ 0 ​ D + D^{*0}D^{+} channels, the probabilities of each channel, as well as scattering lengths and effective ranges for the channels, together with the size of the source function, all of them with a relatively good precision.

II
Formalism: correlation functions

We start with a brief discussion about the correlation functions, and how the
D^{\ast}D
interaction enters into their calculation. We consider the coupled channels
D^{\ast+}D^{0}
and
D^{\ast 0}D^{+}
. As discussed in Ref.
Vidaña et al. 2023
(where the reader is referred for further details), for the
S
-wave interaction relevant in the limited range of values of the c.m. momentum of the outgoing
D^{\ast}D
system, the correlation functions can be written as:

\displaystyle C_{D^{0}D^{*+}}(p_{D^{0}})=1+4\pi\,\theta(\Lambda-p_{D^{0}})\int_{0}^{\infty}drr^{2}S_{12}(r)

\displaystyle\times\Big\{\left\lvert j_{0}(p_{D^{0}}r)+T_{11}(s)\widetilde{G}_{1}(r;s)\right\rvert^{2}

(1)

\displaystyle+\left\lvert T_{12}(s)\widetilde{G}_{2}(r;s)\right\rvert^{2}-j^{2}_{0}(p_{D^{0}}r)\Big\}\,~,

\displaystyle C_{D^{+}D^{*0}}(p_{D^{+}})=1+4\pi\,\theta(\Lambda-p_{D^{+}})\int_{0}^{\infty}drr^{2}S_{12}(r)

\displaystyle\times\Big\{\left\lvert j_{0}(p_{D^{+}}r)+T_{22}(s)\widetilde{G}_{2}(r;s)\right\rvert^{2}

(2)

\displaystyle+\left\lvert T_{12}(s)\widetilde{G}_{1}(r;s)\right\rvert^{2}-j^{2}_{0}(p_{D^{+}}r)\Big\}\,~,

where
T_{ij}(s)
are the
T
-matrix elements of the coupled channel system, to be further discussed below, and
s\equiv E^{2}
is the invariant mass squared of the
D^{\ast}D
pair. Furthermore,
p_{D^{0}}
and
p_{D^{+}}
are the c.m. momenta of the
D^{0}D^{\ast+}
and
D^{+}D^{\ast 0}
channels, respectively, given by:

\displaystyle p_{D^{0}}\equiv q

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(s,m_{D^{0}}^{2},m_{D^{*+}}^{2})}{2\sqrt{s}}\,,

(3a)

\displaystyle p_{D^{+}}\equiv k

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(s,m_{D^{+}}^{2},m_{D^{*0}}^{2})}{2\sqrt{s}}\,

(3b)

being
\lambda(a,b,c)=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-2ab-2ac-2bc
the well known Källen function. The zeroth order spherical Bessel function has been denoted by
j_{0}(z)
.

S_{12}(r)
is the so-called source function, assumed to be spherically symmetric and usually parameterized as a gaussian function:

S_{12}(r)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{4\pi})^{3}R^{3}}\mbox{exp}\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{4R^{2}}\right)\,,

(4)

with
R
a parameter representing the size of the system, typically in the range
1
to
5\,\text{fm}
, depending on the transverse mass of the initial colliding system,
i.e.
,
pp
or heavy ions collisions. We shall see that in the inverse problem that we solve, we are also able to get this parameter.

Finally, the function
\widetilde{G}_{i}(r;s)
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{i}(r;s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\!\!\!dqH_{i}(q)\,\frac{j_{0}(qr)}{s-\left(\omega_{1}^{(i)}(q)+\omega_{2}^{(i)}({q})\right)^{2}+i\epsilon}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle H_{i}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{\omega^{(i)}_{1}(q)+\omega^{(i)}_{2}(q)}{2\omega^{(i)}_{1}(q)\,\omega^{(i)}_{2}(q)}\,,

(6)

where
\omega^{(i)}_{j}(q)=\sqrt{m_{j}^{(i)2}+q^{2}}
, which for practical purposes is better evaluated using

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}^{(i)}(r;s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dq\,H_{i}(q)\frac{j_{0}(qr)-j_{0}(p_{i}r)}{s-\left(\omega_{1}^{(i)}(q)+\omega_{2}^{(i)}({q})\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+j_{0}(p_{i}r)G_{i}(s),

(7)

with:

G_{i}(s)=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dq\,H_{i}(q)\frac{1}{s-\left(\omega_{1}^{(i)}(q)+\omega_{2}^{(i)}({q})\right)^{2}+i\epsilon}\,.

(8)

The advantage of this form is that, since
\sqrt{s}=\omega^{(i)}_{1}(p_{i})+\omega^{(i)}_{2}(p_{i})
, the first integral in Eq. (
7
) has no longer a singularity, while the second integral is a standard two-point loop function regularized with a cutoff
\Lambda
, that can be evaluated analytically as can be found in Ref.
Oller et al. 1999
(in the erratum).

III
Formalism:
T
-matrix models

III.1
Simplified method to obtain the scattering lengths

We start with a transition potential, which we do not need to specify:

V_{ij}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{11}&V_{12}\\[2.84544pt]
V_{21}&V_{22}\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

(9)

and the scattering amplitude given by:

T=[1-VG]^{-1}\,V\,,

(10)

with
G
a diagonal matrix with elements given by the loop function [Eq. (
8
)] of the intermediate pseudoscalar-vector channels,
G\equiv\text{diag}\left[G_{1}(s),G_{2}(s)\right]
. The value of
\Lambda
in the
G_{i}(s)
function indicates the range of the interaction in momentum space
Gamermann et al. 2010
;
Song et al. 2022
.

In this part we ignore the
D^{*}
width, which is shown in Ref.
Dai et al. 2023
to have a very small effect on the scattering lengths. We also ignore in this part the effect of the effective range
r_{0}
, also shown to be moderate in the study of
Vidaña et al. 2023
. With the normalization of the
G
function of Eq. (
8
) suited to the field theoretical formulation one has
Dai et al. 2023

\displaystyle T_{11}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{8\pi\sqrt{s}}{-\frac{1}{a_{1}}-iq}\,,

(11a)

\displaystyle T_{22}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{8\pi\sqrt{s}}{-\frac{1}{a_{2}}-ik}\,,

(11b)

where
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
are, respectively, the scattering lengths of the
D^{*+}D^{0}
and
D^{*0}D^{+}
channels and neglecting the
D^{*}
width,
a_{1}
is real, but
a_{2}
is complex because
D^{*0}D^{+}
can decay into
D^{*+}D^{0}
. At the
D^{*0}D^{+}
threshold,
k=0
and the only imaginary part comes from
G_{D^{*+}D^{0}}
in Eq. (
10
),
{\rm Im}G_{D^{*+}D^{0}}=-q/8\pi\sqrt{s}
. Ignoring terms in
q^{2}
, consistently with also not considering the terms in
r_{0}q^{2}/2
of the effective range expansion, we can write

-\frac{1}{a_{2}}=b_{2}-ib^{\prime}_{2}q\,.

(12)

Let us recall that the c.m. differential cross section in the present normalization is given by
Mandl and Shaw 1985

\frac{d\sigma_{i\leftarrow j}}{d\Omega}=\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{s}\frac{p_{j}}{p_{i}}|T_{ij}|^{2}

(13)

with
p_{i}
,
p_{j}
the initial and final momenta respectively, and the optical theorem states
Mandl and Shaw 1985

{\rm Im}T_{22}=-2k\sqrt{s}\sigma_{\rm tot}\,~,\qquad\sigma_{\rm tot}=\sigma_{22}+\sigma_{1\leftarrow 2}\,.

(14)

Using Eqs. (
11
), (
12
), (
13
), and (
14
), we immediately find that

\sigma_{1\leftarrow 2}=\sigma_{\rm tot}-\sigma_{22}=\frac{1}{k}\frac{4\pi b^{\prime}_{2}q}{b_{2}^{2}+(b^{\prime}_{2}q+k)^{2}}\equiv\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\frac{4\pi}{s}\frac{q}{k}|T_{12}|^{2}.

(15)

We see that by having
T_{11}
and
T_{22}
one cannot get
T_{12}
, but we can deduce
\left\lvert T_{12}\right\rvert^{2}
, which is exactly the magnitude we need for the correlation function. Thus, one can express
|T_{12}|^{2}
as

|T_{12}|^{2}=64\pi^{2}s\frac{b^{\prime}_{2}}{b_{2}^{2}+(b^{\prime}_{2}q+k)^{2}}\,.

(16)

Below the second threshold
k
is purely imaginary (
k\to i\kappa
) and then the denominator of
Eq. (
16
) becomes
(b_{2}+\kappa)^{2}+b^{\prime 2}_{2}q^{2}
.

III.2
K
-matrix formalism

A quite general parameterization of the
T
-matrix is the
K
-matrix parameterization,

T^{-1}(s)=K^{-1}(s)-i\rho(s)~,

(17)

where
\rho(s)
is a diagonal matrix with elements

\rho_{ii}(s)=-\frac{p_{i}(s)}{8\pi\sqrt{s}}=\text{Im}\,G_{i}(s)~.

(18)

This parameterization fulfills unitarity as long as the
K
-matrix elements do not have a right-hand cut. Therefore, they can be rational functions, or functions with left-hand cuts, or a combination thereof. For our particular problem, we consider the simplest case in which the
K
-matrix elements are just constants.

Determination of the complete information on the interaction

For this part we follow the strategy used in Ref.

\displaystyle\ket{D^{*}D,I=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{D^{*+}D^0}-\ket{D^{*0}D^+}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\ket{D^{*}D,I=1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{D^{*+}D^0}+\ket{D^{*0}D^+}\right)\,.

V
Conclusions

In this work we investigated the potential of the inverse problem in the femtoscopic correlation functions, in which starting from the correlation functions of some related channels we determine the interaction between these channels. The data are analyzed by means of a coupled channel unitary scheme which has the freedom to accommodate missing channels relevant to the interaction, as well as components of some genuine states of non-molecular nature. This leaves a few parameters to be determined from the data, together with the parameter that gives the source size. We applied the method to pseudo-data of the
D^{*+}D^{0}
and
D^{*0}D^{+}
correlation functions and extracted from there all the possible information. The results obtained are remarkable and we found that using these data, all above the threshold of the two channels, we could determine that there was a bound state of the system, that this state was of molecular nature, with negligible room for a non-molecular state. We determined the scattering length and the effective range for the two channels, the probabilities to have each of the channels in the wave function of the bound state and also, remarkably, the size of the source parameter,
R
, all of them with a good precision and in agreement with the experimental information used to constrain the
T
-matrix employed in the generation of the synthetic data (correlation functions).

In order to better appreciate the precision in the magnitudes obtained with the general method, we also used two other different methods, one very simple which does not require to use any intermediate interaction potentials between the channels, and which provides the scattering amplitudes at a qualitative level. Another one, based on the common
K
-matrix approach, provides much better results for the scattering amplitudes, but however does not allow to obtain the molecular probabilities which rely upon
\partial G/\partial s
, and
\text{Re}\,G
is not fully accounted for in the
K
-matrix approach.

In conlusion, the full method proposed here is ideal to analyze correlation functions and extract from them valuable information on hadron interactions, which will be useful in the future to study the interaction of hadron states which cannot be formed otherwise, as well as to determine the nature of bound states or resonances.

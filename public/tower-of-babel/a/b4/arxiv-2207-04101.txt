On the Maximum Sigma Index of
k

Abstract




Let
G
be a graph with edge set
E(G)
. Denote by
d_{w}
the degree of a vertex
w
of
G
. The sigma index of
G
is defined as
\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}
. A connected graph of order
n
and size
n+k-1
is known as a connected
k
-cyclic graph. Abdo, Dimitrov, and Gutman [Discrete Appl. Math. 250 (2018) 57–64] characterized the graphs having the greatest sigma index over the family of all connected graphs of a fixed order. The primary goal of the present note is to determine graphs possessing the greatest sigma index from the class of all connected
k
-cyclic graphs of a fixed order.

Keywords:
irregularity; Albertson index; sigma index; topological index.

AMS Subject Classification:
05C07, 05C90.

1
Introduction

Only finite graphs are considered in this study. The graph-theory terms that are used in this study, without providing their definitions, may be found in
[
8
,
16
,
9
]
.

Although, regular graphs are usually thought of as graphs with the same vertex degrees (this definition of regular graphs is used throughout this paper), but a more general perspective is: graphs with some common property across their structure. Since the beginning of graph theory, regular graphs have been a major topic of research.
On the other hand, the study of graphs possessing a characteristic opposite to regularity has also attracted a much attention from researchers over the last couple of decades; such a study may be regarded as irregularity in graphs
[
4
]
. To the best of authors’ knowledge, the first paper devoted thoroughly to the concept of irregularity was written by Bezhad and Chartrand
[
7
]

For a graph
G
, its irregularity measure (IM) is a non-negative graph invariant fulfilling the property: every component of
G
is regular if and
only if
IM=0
. Irregularity measures play a significant role in chemistry
[
20
,
14
]
and in network theory

[
10
,
11
,
12
,
21
]
.
One of the best known irregularity measures is the one introduced by Albertson
[
3
]
(which is often referred to as the Albertson index):

A(G)=\sum_{vw\in E(G)}|d_{v}-d_{w}|

where
E(G)
represents the edge set of a graph
G
and
d_{u}
denotes the degree of a vertex
u
in
G
. In order to overcome the Albertson index’s several drawbacks, Abdo et al.
[
1
]
devised an extended version of the Albertson index and named it as the total irregularity index. For a graph
G
, its total irregularity index is defined as

irr_{t}(G)=\sum_{\{v,w\}\subseteq E(G)}|d_{v}-d_{w}|.

The present paper is mainly concerned with the sigma index
[
13
,
15
]
, that is another variant of the Albertson index. For a graph
G
, its sigma index is denoted by
\sigma(G)
and is defined as

\sigma(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}.

The study of the sigma index was explicitly initiated by Gutman et al.
[
15
]
where they not only established some fundamental properties of the sigma index but also proved that the sigma index of every graph is an even integer and constructed graph classes for which this index attains every positive even integer. Réti
[
19
]
compared the sigma index with a couple of well-known irregularity measures and reported several interesting properties of this index.
Some additional mathematical characteristics of the sigma index can be found in the recent paper
[
17
]
where the general Albertson index was proposed and studied. The main motivation of the present paper comes from the article
[
2
]
where the graphs having the greatest sigma index were characterized over the family of all connected graphs of a fixed order. In this paper, the graphs possessing the greatest sigma index over the class of all connected
k
-cyclic graphs of a fixed order are determined, where a connected
k
-cyclic graph is a connected graph of order
n
and size
n+k-1
.

2
Main Results

For a vertex
v
in a graph
G
, denote by
N(v)
the the set of all those vertices of
G
that are adjacent to
v
. The members of
N(v)
are known as neighbors of
v
. A vertex
u\in V(G)
of degree zero (one) is known as an isolated vertex (pendent vertex, respectively). The star graph with
n
vertices is denoted by
S_{n}
. A graph of size zero is known as an edgeless graph.

Lemma 1

For any graph
G
of order
n
and size
m
, with
0\leq m\leq n-1
, the following inequality holds

\sigma(G)\leq m(m-1)^{2}

with equality if and only if
G
is either an edgeless graph
(
for
m=0
)
or it consists of the star
S_{m+1}
together with
n-m-1
isolated vertices
(
for
m\geq 1
)
.

Proof:

The integer
n
is chosen to be fixed. The result is proved by using the mathematical induction on
m
. For
m=0
, the graph
G
is the edgeless graph and for
m=1
the graph
G
consists of the star
S_{2}
together with
n-2
isolated vertices; in both cases, it holds that
\sigma(G)=m(m-1)^{2}=0
and thence the induction starts.
Next, assume that
m=k\geq 2
. Choose an edge
uv\in E(G)
such that
d_{u}\geq d_{v}
and

d_{u}-d_{v}=\max\big\{|d_{w}-d_{w^{\prime}}|:~w^{\prime}w\in E(G)\big\}.

(1)

Take
Z=N(u)\cap N(v)
,
X=N(u)\setminus(Z\cup\{v\})
, and
Y=N(v)\setminus(Z\cup\{u\})
. Then, one has

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\sigma(G-uv)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}+\sum_{x\in X}\Big[(d_{u}-d_{x})^{2}-(d_{u}-1-d_{x})^{2}\Big]

(2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{y\in Y}\Big[(d_{v}-d_{y})^{2}-(d_{v}-1-d_{y})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{z\in Z}\Big[(d_{u}-d_{z})^{2}-(d_{u}-1-d_{z})^{2}+(d_{v}-d_{z})^{2}-(d_{v}-1-d_{z})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}+\sum_{x\in X}\Big[2(d_{u}-d_{x})-1\Big]+\sum_{y\in Y}\Big[2(d_{v}-d_{y})-1\Big]

\displaystyle+2\sum_{z\in Z}\Big[(d_{u}-d_{z})+(d_{v}-d_{z})-1\Big],

where every degree notation represents the degree in
G
(not in
G-uv
).
By making use of Equation (
1
) in Equation (
2
), one gets

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\sigma(G-uv)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}+\sum_{x\in X}\Big[2(d_{u}-d_{v})-1\Big]+\sum_{y\in Y}\big[2(d_{v}-d_{v})-1\big]

(3)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{z\in Z}\Big[2(d_{u}-d_{v})-1\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}+\big(|X|+|Y|+2|Z|\big)\big[2(d_{u}-d_{v})-1\big].

Since
|X|+|Y|+2|Z|=d_{u}+d_{v}-2
, from (
3
) it follows that

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\sigma(G-uv)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(d_{u}-d_{v})^{2}+\big(d_{u}+d_{v}-2\big)\big[2(d_{u}-d_{v})-1\big].

(4)

Since
k\geq 2
, one has
d_{u}\geq 2
.
Note that the function
\phi
defined by

\phi(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}-t_{2})^{2}+\big(t_{1}+t_{2}-2\big)\big[2(t_{1}-t_{2})-1\big],

with
k\geq t_{1}\geq t_{2}\geq 1
and
t_{1}\geq 2
, is strictly increasing in
t_{1}
and strictly decreasing in
t_{2}
. This implies that
\phi(t_{1},t_{2})\leq\phi(k,1)
with equality if and only if
t_{1}=k,t_{2}=1,
and thence (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\sigma(G-uv)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(k-1)^{2}+(k-1)(2k-3)

(5)

Because of the inductive hypothesis, (
5
) yields

\displaystyle\sigma(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(k-1)^{2}+(k-1)(2k-3)+(k-1)(k-2)^{2}=k(k-1)^{2}.

(6)

Take
E^{\prime}\subseteq E(G)
such that if
rs\in E^{\prime}
then either
r=u
or
s=v
.
From (
3
), (
4
), (
5
), and (
6
),
we observe that the equation
\sigma(G)=k(k-1)^{2}
holds if and only if

(i).

the equation
d_{u}-d_{v}=|d_{r}-d_{s}|
is satisfied for every edge
rs\in E^{\prime}
,

(ii).

the vertex
u
has the degree
k
and the vertex
v
is pendent,

(iii).

the graph
G-uv
consists of the star
S_{k}
together with
n-k
isolated vertices.

Therefore, the induction (and hence the proof) is completed.

\square

The maximum degree of a graph
G
is denoted by
\Delta(G)
.

Lemma 2

If
G
is a graph possessing the greatest sigma index over the family of all connected
k
-cyclic graphs of a fixed order
n
, then
\Delta(G)=n-1
.

Proof:

For
n\leq 3
, the result trivially holds. In what follows, we assume that
n\geq 4
.
Contrarily, assume that
v\in V(G)
such that
d_{v}=\Delta(G)\leq n-2
. Then,
G
contains vertices
v^{\prime},u_{1}
such that
v^{\prime}v,v^{\prime}u_{1}\in E(G)
but
vu_{1}\not\in E(G)
. Take
N(v^{\prime})\setminus N(v):=\{v,u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{p}\}
where
p\geq 1
. Also, take
A:=N(v)\setminus(N(v^{\prime})\cup\{v^{\prime}\})
and
B:=N(v)\cap N(v^{\prime})
.
Construct a new graph
G^{\prime}
from
G
by dropping the edges

u_{1}v^{\prime},u_{2}v^{\prime},\ldots,u_{p}v^{\prime}
and inserting the edges
u_{1}v,u_{2}v,\ldots,u_{p}v
.
In the remaining proof, by the vertex degree
d_{t}
we mean degree of the vertex
t
in the graph
G
.
One has

\displaystyle\sigma(G^{\prime})-\sigma(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\Big[(d_{v}+p-d_{a})^{2}-(d_{v}-d_{a})^{2}\Big]

(7)

\displaystyle+\sum_{b\in B}\Big[(d_{v}+p-d_{b})^{2}-(d_{v}-d_{b})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{b\in B}\Big[(d_{v^{\prime}}-p-d_{b})^{2}-(d_{v^{\prime}}-d_{b})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Big[(d_{v}+p-d_{u_{i}})^{2}-(d_{v^{\prime}}-d_{u_{i}})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+(d_{v}+2p-d_{v^{\prime}})^{2}-(d_{v}-d_{v^{\prime}})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\Big[(d_{v}+p-d_{a})^{2}-(d_{v}-d_{a})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{b\in B}2p(d_{v}-d_{v^{\prime}}+p)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Big[(d_{v}+p-d_{u_{i}})^{2}-(d_{v^{\prime}}-d_{u_{i}})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+4p(d_{v}-d_{v^{\prime}}+p).

Since
d_{v}=\Delta(G)
, from (
7
) it follows that
\sigma(G^{\prime})-\sigma(G)>0
, which is a contradiction to the maximality of
\sigma(G)
as
G^{\prime}
is also a connected
k
-cyclic graph with
n
vertices.

\square

In order to prove the main results, we also need the following known result that is similar to Lemma
1
.

Lemma 3

[
18
]
For any graph
G
of order
n
and size
m
, with
1\leq m\leq n-1
, the following inequality holds

M_{1}(G)\leq m(m+1)

with equality if and only if
G
consists of the star
S_{m+1}
together with
n-m-1
isolated vertices.

For
n\geq 4
, let
H_{n,k}
be the graph constructed from the star graph
S_{n}
by inserting
k
edge(s) between
u\in V(S_{n})
and
k
other pendent vertices, where
u
is a fixed pendent vertex of
S_{n}
and
k\geq 1
. Also, we take
S_{n}=H_{n,0}
, see
[
6
]
.

Figure 1:
The
k
-cyclic graph
H_{n,k}
.

Theorem 4

For
0\leq k\leq n-2
with
n\geq 4
, if
G
is connected
k
-cyclic graph with
n
vertices, then

\sigma(G)\leq(n-1)(n-2)^{2}-2k(2n-5)+k^{2}(k-1),

with equality if and only if
G=H_{n,k}
.

Proof:

If the inequality
\Delta(G)<n-1
holds, then by using the transformation (successively) used in the proof of Lemma
2
, one obtains a connected
k
-cyclic graph
G^{*}
of order
n
and maximum degree
n-1
such that
\sigma(G)<\sigma(G^{*})
. Thereby, it enough to prove the result for the case when
\Delta(G)=n-1
. The result trivially holds for
k=0,1,
because there is a unique connected graph of order
n
and maximum degree
n-1
in each such case. Thus, in what follows, assume that
k\geq 2
and
\Delta(G)=n-1
. Take a vertex
v\in V(G)
such that
d_{v}=n-1
. Note that the size
|E(G-v)|
of the graph
G-v
is
k
, where
2\leq k\leq n-2=|V(G-v)|-1
. If
d^{\prime}_{w}
and
d_{w}
are the degrees of a vertex
w\in V(G-v)
in
G-v
and
G
, respectively, then
d^{\prime}_{w}=d_{w}-1
for every
w\in V(G-v)
. If
E^{\prime}=E(G-v)
and
V^{\prime}=V(G-v)
, then one has

\displaystyle\sigma(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in V^{\prime}}(d_{v}-d_{a})^{2}+\sum_{uw\in E^{\prime}}(d_{u}-d_{w})^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in V^{\prime}}(n-d^{\prime}_{a}-2)^{2}+\sum_{uw\in E^{\prime}}(d^{\prime}_{u}-d^{\prime}_{w})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)(n-2)^{2}-4k(n-2)+M_{1}(G-v)+\sigma(G-v).

By using Lemmas
1
and
3
in (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\sigma(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n-1)(n-2)^{2}-4k(n-2)+k(k+1)+k(k-1)^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)(n-2)^{2}-2k(2n-5)+k^{2}(k-1),

where the equality sign in (
9
) holds if and only if the graph
G-v
consists of the star
S_{k+1}
together with
n-1-k-1(=n-k-2)
isolated vertices; that is, if and only if
G=H_{n,k}
.

\square

We observe that the graph
H_{n,k}
exists whenever
0\leq k\leq n-2
. Thus, Theorem
4
has the following direct consequence.

Corollary 1

If
0\leq k\leq n-2
and
n\geq 4
, then
H_{n,k}
uniquely possess the maximum sigma index over the collection of all connected
k
-cyclic graphs of a fixed order
n
.

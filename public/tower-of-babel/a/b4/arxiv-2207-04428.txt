Well-posedness of a 2D gyrokinetic model with equal Debye length and Larmor radius
Pierre-Antoine Giorgi, Maxime Hauray

Abstract
We study here a 2D gyrokinetic model obtained in [ 5 ] , which naturally appears as the limit of a Vlasov-Poisson system with a very large external uniform magnetic field in the finite Larmor radius regime, when the typical Larmor radius is of order of the Debye length. We show that the Cauchy problem for that system is well-posed in a suitable space, provided that the initial condition satisfies a standard uniform decay assumption in velocity. Our result relies on a stability estimate in Wasserstein distance of order one between two solutions of the system. That stability estimate directly implies the uniqueness (in an appropriate space) of solution to the Cauchy problem. An extension of the stability estimate to the case of a regularized interaction allows to prove the existence of solutions, as limits of solutions of a similar system with regularized interactions.

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{{\mathbf{1}}_{|v|\leq|x|}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{{\mathbf{1}}_{|v_{*}|\leq|x_{*}|}}{|x_{*}|^{2}}\right)\frac{|x-x_{*}|}{2}.

The
I_{2}
term is bounded using a geometric argument, called “‘rope argument” in
[
12
]
. In fact, it is non zero only if
|x|-|v|
has a positive sign and
|x_{*}|-|v_{*}|
a negative one. This happens only if
\delta=|x-x_{*}|+|v-v_{*}|
is not too small. Precisely, in that case

|x|-|v|\leq(|x|-|v|)-(|x_{*}|-|v_{*}|)=(|x|-|x_{*}|)-(|v|-|v_{*}|)\leq|x-x_{*}|+|v-v_{*}|=\delta

by the triangular inequality. It implies that

I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{|x|}{\mathbf{1}}_{|v|\leq|x|\leq|v|+\delta}.

The term
I_{2}^{\ast}
is bounded in the same way that
I_{2}
, exchanging the role of
(x,v)
and
(x_{*},v_{*})
:

I_{2}^{\ast}\leq\frac{1}{|x_{*}|}{\mathbf{1}}_{|v_{*}|\leq|x_{*}|\leq|v_{*}|+\delta}.

Summing up the above bounds on
I_{1},I_{2},I_{2}^{\ast}
, we obtain the claimed inequality.
∎

We now state two very useful lemmas, about the possibility to bound some specific integral with the help of the
L^{\infty}_{\gamma}
norm. They will allow us to bound integrals of the terms appearing in the r.h.s. of the bound of Lemma
2.2
: Lemma
2.3
will be useful for the first term, and Lemma
2.4
for the second and third terms.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g
be a function in
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
x,v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Then,

\int_{|w-v|<|y-x|}\frac{g({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)}{2\pi|y-x|^{2}}\leq\bigl(\|g\|_{1}\|g\|_{\infty}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\kappa_{\gamma}\|g\|_{\gamma},

the last bound being valid for
\gamma>2
, and if
\|g\|_{\gamma}<\infty
.
\kappa_{\gamma}
stands for the for the constant introduced in (
8
).

Proof.

Picking up an
R>0
and using Fubini Theorem, we can simply bound

\displaystyle\int_{|v-w|<|x-y|<R}\frac{g({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)}{|x-y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{|x-y|<R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|x-y|^{2}}\underbrace{\int_{|x-y|>|v-w|}{\mathrm{d}}w}_{=\pi|x-y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\pi\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{|x-y|<R}{\mathrm{d}}y=\pi^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}R^{2}.

And also easily

\int_{|x-y|\geq R}\frac{g({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)}{|x-y|^{2}}\leq\frac{\|g\|_{1}}{R^{2}}.

Summing these two inequalities, we obtain

\int_{|v-w|<|x-y|}\frac{g({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)}{|x-y|^{2}}\leq\pi^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}R^{2}+\frac{\|g\|_{1}}{R^{2}},

an inequality valid for any
R>0
. It turns out that the optimal choice for
R
is
R_{m}^{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\|g\|_{1}}{\pi^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}}}
, in which case the bound is equal to
2\pi\bigl(\|g\|_{1}\|g\|_{\infty}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. A quantity smaller than
2\pi\kappa_{\gamma}\|g\|_{\gamma}
thanks to (
8
). Dividing by
2\pi
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\gamma>2
,
g
be a probability distribution in
L^{\infty}_{\gamma}
,
x,v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\delta>0
. Then,

\int_{|v-w|\leq|x-y|<|v-w|+\delta}\frac{g({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)}{2\pi|y-x|}\leq\kappa_{\gamma}\|g\|_{\gamma}\delta.

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{|v-w|\leq|x-y|<|v-w|+\delta}\frac{g({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)}{|y-x|}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{(1+|w|)^{\gamma}}\int_{|v-w|<|y-x|<|v-w|+\delta}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|x-y|}

\displaystyle=\|g\|_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{(1+|w|)^{\gamma}}\int_{|v-w|}^{|v-w|+\delta}2\pi{\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\delta\|g\|_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{(1+|w|)^{\gamma}}=2\pi\kappa_{\gamma}\|g\|_{\gamma}\delta.

The integral in
y
on the first line is in fact computed after a radial change of variable. Dividing by
2\pi
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Proposition 2.1

Proof.

We only write the proof for the bound of

{\mathcal{A}}[f]

and

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{V}}[f](x,v)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|x-y|}\int_{|v-w|<|x-y|}f(y,w)\,{\mathrm{d}}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\int_{|x-y|<R}+\int_{|x-y|\geq R}\right]\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|x-y|}\int_{|v-w|<|x-y|}f(y,w)\,{\mathrm{d}}w

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

2.3
Transport and continuity equation, pushforward and preservation of norms.

We recall here the standard resolution of the transport and continuity equation, applied to our particular case. In the meantime, we state precisely some classical definition that will play an important role later.

The advection fields defined by (
2
) are divergence free. A case which allows to rewrite the transport equation (
1
) in a conservative form, also called a
continuity equation

\partial_{t}f_{t}+\divg_{x}({\mathcal{V}}[f_{t}]f_{t})+\divg_{v}({\mathcal{A}}[f_{t}]f_{t})=0.

(10)

When the field
{\mathcal{V}}[f_{t}]
and
{\mathcal{A}}[f_{t}]
are Lipschitz, the continuity equation is usually solved with the help of the characteristics, that is the trajectories associated to ODE driven by the vector field
({\mathcal{V}}[f_{t}],{\mathcal{A}}[f_{t}])
:

X_{t}(z)=x+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{V}}[f_{s}](X_{s}(z),V_{s}(z)){\mathrm{d}}s,\qquad V_{t}(z)=v+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{V}}[f_{s}](V_{s}(z),V_{s}(z)){\mathrm{d}}s.

(11)

where
z=(x,v)
. the collection of mapping
Z_{t}=(X_{t},V_{t})
for
t\geq 0
is called the
flow
associated to the vector field.

As a consequence of the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem
7
7

7

which applies here as the vector field is assumed to be Lipschitz in position-velocity, with a constant that is locally integrable in time
, the mapping
Z_{t}
is invertible, with in fact a continuous inverse.
At a given time
t
,
Z_{t}^{-1}
is in fact the flow associated to the the time reversed vector field
(s,x,v)\to\left(-{\mathcal{V}}[f_{t-s}],-{\mathcal{A}}[f_{t-s}]\right)
. It satisfies
Z_{t}^{-1}=Z^{rev}_{t,t}
with

Z^{rev}_{t,s}(z)=z-\int_{0}^{s}\left({\mathcal{V}}[f_{t-u}],{\mathcal{A}}[f_{t-u}]\right)(Z^{rev}_{t,u}(z))\,{\mathrm{d}}u,

(12)

Such properties could be found in standard book about ODE, like
[
13
]
.

In the case where the vector field is divergence free, a property that is always true in our setting, then the flow is volume preserving. This means precisely that for any continuous test function
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with bounded support, and any time
t\geq 0

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\varphi(Z_{t}(z)){\mathrm{d}}z=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\varphi(z){\mathrm{d}}z.

(13)

To see why this property is related to the preservation of volume, use indicatrix function of set
A
(with neglectable boundary) instead of continuous function in the above equality, and get
\mathrm{Vol}(Z_{t}^{-1}(A))=\mathrm{Vol}(A)
.

For the sake of completeness, we redefine the notion of pushforward.

Definition 3
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})
and
T:{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
a measurable map. Then the pushforward of
f
by the map
T
is defined as the probability measure denoted
T\#f
such that for any measurable set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
,
T\#f(A)=f(T^{-1}(A))
. Equivalently, for any continuous test function
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the following equality holds

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\varphi(z)\left[T\#f\right]({\mathrm{d}}z)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\varphi(T(z))f({\mathrm{d}}z).

(14)

When the map
T
is volume preserving and has a measurable inverse
T^{-1}
, and
f
has a density (still denoted by
f
here), then

T\#f
also has a density and

(T\#f)(z)=f(T^{-1}(z)).

The last point is a consequence of the measure preserving property (
13
) applied to the equality (
14
) defining the pushforward.

The solution of the continuity equation writes as the pushforward of the initial condition by the flow. It is also classical that the divergence free property implies the preservation of
L^{p}
norms of solutions along time. We prove this rigorously in the next proposition.

According to Proposition

.
In that case, the mappings

4
A new proof of the existence of solutions

In this section, we prove Theorem
2
, namely the existence of global weak solutions to (
1
). Our proof is quite different from the one in
[
4
]
, which is the consequence of a physical derivation of the model using compactness methods. Here we start from an ad-hoc mollification of the force kernel, not physically motivated, and provide quantitative stability estimates in the Wasserstein metric between solutions to the mollified equation and solutions to the limit one.
The estimates proved here are quite similar to the stability estimate (
9
) for the limit model proved in the previous section: the novelty is to treat the fact that we use kernels with different mollification parameters, and that we need to carefully estimate the (small) errors that the mollification introduce.

First, we will precise the mollification of the kernel that we shall use, and construct unique and regular solutions to a mollified equation. We will then show that when the mollification parameter goes to zero, the sequence of solutions is indeed a Cauchy sequence for the Wasserstein metric, and eventually its limit will be shown to be a weak solution to (
1
).

4.1
Mollification of the kernels

We assume that
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})\cap L^{\infty}_{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})
, for a
\gamma>2
.
We start by smoothing the interaction potential
K
defined in (
3
) and its derivative
J
defined in (
4
). We introduce a family of mollifier

\displaystyle\forall\,(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4},\;\chi_{\varepsilon}(x,v)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}\chi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\chi\left(\frac{v}{\varepsilon}\right),

(30)

built with a function
\chi
such that

\chi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\quad\supp(\chi)\subset B(0,1),\quad\chi\geq 0,\quad\int\chi=1.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we define the mollified potential
K_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\varepsilon}*K
and kernel

J_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\varepsilon}*J,\quad\text{i.e. }J_{\varepsilon}(x,v)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\chi\left(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\right)\chi\left(\frac{v-w}{\varepsilon}\right)J(y,w){\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}w.

(31)

The following lemma shows that the mollified kernels are bounded and Lipschitz continuous.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0

\left\|J_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|\chi\|_{\infty}\pi}{\varepsilon},\qquad\left\|\nabla J_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|\nabla\chi\|_{\infty}\pi}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Proof.

By definition,

\displaystyle\left|J_{\varepsilon}(x,v)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B(0,1)^{2}}\chi({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)J(x-\varepsilon y,v-\varepsilon w)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,1)^{2}}\frac{\chi({\mathrm{d}}y,{\mathrm{d}}w)}{|x-\varepsilon y|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\chi\|_{\infty}}{2\pi\varepsilon}\int_{B(0,1)^{2}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}w}{\left|\frac{x}{\varepsilon}-y\right|}=\frac{\|\chi\|_{\infty}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{B\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},1\right)}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|y|}\leq\frac{\|\chi\|_{\infty}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|y|}=\frac{\|\chi\|_{\infty}\pi}{\varepsilon},

where we have used a kind of rearrangement inequality : the integral on a radial function on a unit ball is maximal when the ball is centered at the origin

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{B(z,1)}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|y|}=\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}y}{|y|}=2\pi.

Using
\nabla_{x}\left(\chi_{\varepsilon}\ast J\right)=(\nabla_{x}\chi_{\varepsilon})\ast J
and
\nabla_{v}\left(\chi_{\varepsilon}\ast J\right)=(\nabla_{v}\chi_{\varepsilon})\ast J
, and doing the same computation as above gives the bound for
\nabla J_{\varepsilon}
, with an extra power of
\varepsilon
coming from the derivation of
\chi_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

\varepsilon>0

\|f\ast\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\gamma}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2\gamma}\|f\|_{\gamma}.

Proof.

With the help of inequality (
17
), it comes :

\displaystyle(f\ast\chi_{\varepsilon})(x,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\chi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)f(x-y,v-w)\,{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}w

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\chi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\frac{\|f\|_{\gamma}}{(1+|x-y|)^{\gamma}(1+|v-w|)^{\gamma}}\,{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}w

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f\|_{\gamma}}{(1+|x|)^{\gamma}(1+|v|)^{\gamma}}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\chi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)(1+|y|)^{\gamma}(1+|w|)^{\gamma}\,{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}w.

Then

(f\ast\chi_{\varepsilon})(x,v)\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2\gamma}\|f\|_{\gamma}}{(1+|x|)^{\gamma}(1+|v|)^{\gamma}}.

Multiplying by
(1+|x|)^{\gamma}(1+|v|)^{\gamma}
and taking the supremum on
(x,v)
, we obtain the statement of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

and so does the

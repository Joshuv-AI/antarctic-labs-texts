On minimizers in the liquid drop model
Otis Chodosh, Ian Ruohoniemi

Abstract
. We prove that round balls of volume ≤ 1 \leq 1 uniquely minimize in Gamow’s liquid drop model.



1.
Introduction

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
measurable we define the liquid drop functional

(1)

\mathcal{E}(\Omega):=P(\Omega)+D(\Omega).

where

(2)

D(\Omega):=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|}

is the Coulomb energy and
P(\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\Div X:X\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3}),|X|\leq 1\right\}
is the perimeter of
\Omega
.

The functional (
1
) was introduced by Gamow in 1928 as a model for the nucleus of an atom
[
Gam30
]
. The minimization problem

(3)

E(V):=\inf_{|\Omega|=V}\mathcal{E}(\Omega).

has recently received considerable attention for its connections with various problems in mathematical physics. We refer to the recent survey
[
CMT17
]
for further discussion.

One basic feature of (
1
) is the fact that the perimeter
P(\Omega)
is minimized at a round ball (by the isoperimetric inequality) while the Coulomb term
D(\Omega)
is maximized at a round ball (by Riesz rearrangement
[
Rie30
]
). Thus, the two terms are in competition in the minimization problem (
3
). A well-known conjecture suggests that the perimeter completely dominates in (
3
) until the Coulomb energy becomes strong enough to prevent the existence of minimizers.

To state the conjecture precisely, write
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
for the unit ball and set

(4)

V_{*}:=\frac{2-2^{\frac{2}{3}}}{2^{\frac{2}{3}}-1}\frac{|B_{1}|P(\partial B_{1})}{\frac{1}{2}\iint_{B_{1}\times B_{1}}|x-y|^{-1}dxdy}=5\frac{2-2^{\frac{2}{3}}}{2^{\frac{2}{3}}-1}\approx 3.51.

The significance of (
4
) is that for
V>V_{*}
, the configuration of two balls of volume
V/2
placed infinitely far apart has less energy than a single ball of volume
V
.

We then have (cf.
[
CP11
]
):

Conjecture 1
.

(a)

For
V\leq V_{*}
, the round ball of volume
V
uniquely minimizes
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
among measurable sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\Omega|=V
.

(b)

For
V>V_{*}
no minimizer exists.

Our main result confirms (a) for the range
V\leq 0.28V_{*}
:

Theorem 1
.

Round balls
B_{R}
with
|B_{R}|\leq 1
uniquely minimize
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
among measurable sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\Omega|=|B_{R}|
.

Previously Knüpfer–Muratov proved
[
KM13
,
KM14
]
(see also
[
BC14
,
Jul14
,
MZ15
,
FFM+15
,
CNT22
]
) proved that balls uniquely minimize
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
among sets of sufficiently small volume
V
. It seems that Theorem
1
is the first result concerning (a) in Conjecture
1
to go beyond the the “isoperimetric dominated” (small volume) regime where
D(\Omega)\ll P(\Omega)
in (
1
) allows the repulsive term
D(\Omega)
to be treated as a lower-order term (cf.
[
KM13
, p. 1132]
). Along these lines, it appears to be the first quantitative estimate (cf.
[
MZ15
]
).

1.1.
Boundary-interior Coulomb interaction

A crucial ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1
is the following sharp inequality (see Theorem
6
)

(5)

|\Omega|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{12\pi}P(\Omega)\iint_{\Omega\times\partial\Omega}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|}.

Equality holds here if and only if
\Omega
is a round ball. The proof of (
5
) uses Santaló’s formula from integral geometry. The significance of this inequality for us is that the integral appearing above arises in the first variation of
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
in a natural way.

Similar considerations (cf.
11
) also yield mean convexity of critical points of
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
for a certain range of volumes (Proposition
14
) and an improved bound for the minimal binding energy per particle (Corollary
19
).

2.
The Newtonian potential

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
measurable, we recall that

(6)

v_{\Omega}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|}

is the
Newtonian potential
of
\Omega
.

Lemma 2
.

The Newtonian potential of a round ball satisfies

v_{B_{R}}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi}{3}(3R^{2}-|x|^{2})&|x|\leq R\\
\frac{4\pi R^{3}}{3|x|}&|x|\geq R.\end{cases}

In particular, the Coulomb energy satisfies
D(B_{R})=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{15}R^{5}
.

Proof.

Note that
v_{B_{R}}(0)=4\pi\int_{0}^{R}rdr=2\pi R^{2}
. Moreover,
v_{B_{R}}
is rotationally invariant and solves
\Delta v_{B_{R}}=-4\pi\chi_{B_{R}}
. Thus,
\frac{\partial^{2}v_{B_{R}}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial v_{B_{R}}}{\partial r}=-4\pi
for
r\in(0,R)
. This implies that
v_{B_{R}}(r)=\frac{2\pi}{3}(3R^{2}-r^{2})
for
r\leq R
. Arguing similarly for
r>R
(and using
\lim_{r\to\infty}v_{B_{R}}(r)=0
) we find
v_{B_{R}}(r)=\frac{4\pi R^{3}}{3r}
for
r\geq R
. This proves the first assertion and the second follows from

D(B_{R})=2\pi\int_{0}^{R}\frac{2\pi}{3}(3R^{2}-r^{2})r^{2}dr=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{15}R^{5}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3
.

For any measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\Omega|=|B_{R}|
, the Newtonian potential satisfies
v_{\Omega}(x)\leq v_{B_{R}}(0)=2\pi R^{2}
.

Proof.

Apply the “bathtub principle”
[
LL01
, Theorem 1.14]
with
f(y)=-\frac{1}{|x-y|}
and
G=|\Omega|
.
∎

3.
The outer-minimizing property

We say that a measurable Caccioppoli set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is
outer-minimizing
if any
\Omega\subset\hat{\Omega}
measurable has
P(\Omega)\leq P(\hat{\Omega})
. We say that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
measurable is
strictly outer-minimizing
if any
\Omega\subset\hat{\Omega}
measurable with
P(\hat{\Omega})\leq P(\Omega)
must have
|\hat{\Omega}\setminus\Omega|=0
. In this section we establish the following (conditional) outer-minimizing property for minimizers of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proposition 4
.

Fix
V=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}\leq 1
and assume that
E(V)=\mathcal{E}(B_{R})
. If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
has
|\Omega|=V
and
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)=E(V)
then
\Omega
is strictly outer-minimizing.

Proof.

Consider
\Omega\subset\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
measurable with
P(\hat{\Omega})\leq P(\Omega)
. Write
|\hat{\Omega}|=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}(1+\alpha^{3})
for some
\alpha\geq 0
. Our goal is to prove that
\alpha=0
.

Write
\Gamma:=\hat{\Omega}\setminus\Omega
and
\tilde{\Omega}=(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{1}{3}}\hat{\Omega}
. Note that
|\tilde{\Omega}|=|\Omega|
and
|\Gamma|=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}\alpha^{3}
. The assumption
P(\hat{\Omega})\leq P(\Omega)
implies

\displaystyle P(\tilde{\Omega})

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{2}{3}}P(\hat{\Omega})\leq(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{2}{3}}P(\Omega).

Recalling the definition of the Newtonian potential
v_{\Omega}
in (
6
) we have

\displaystyle D(\tilde{\Omega})

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}D(\hat{\Omega})

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}D(\Omega)+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}\int_{\Gamma}v_{\Omega}(x)dx+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}D(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{2}{3}}D(\Omega)+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}2\pi R^{2}|\Gamma|+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}D(B_{\alpha R})

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{2}{3}}D(\Omega)+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}R^{5}\alpha^{3}+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}\frac{16\pi^{2}}{15}R^{5}\alpha^{5}.

In the third line we used the estimate
(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}\leq(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{2}{3}}
, Lemma
3
, and the fact that balls maximize
D(\cdot)
for fixed volumes. Since
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)=E(V)\leq\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\Omega})
we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Omega)\leq(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{2}{3}}\mathcal{E}(\Omega)+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}R^{5}\alpha^{3}+(1+\alpha^{3})^{-\frac{5}{3}}\frac{16\pi^{2}}{15}R^{5}\alpha^{5}.

Rearranging and using
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)=E(V)=\mathcal{E}(B_{R})
we find

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(1+\alpha^{3}-(1+\alpha^{3})^{\frac{5}{3}}\right)\mathcal{E}(B_{R})+\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}R^{5}\alpha^{3}+\frac{16\pi^{2}}{15}R^{5}\alpha^{5}.

Using
\mathcal{E}(B_{R})=4\pi R^{2}+\frac{16\pi^{2}}{15}R^{5}
this can be simplified to read

(7)

0\leq\left(1+\alpha^{3}-(1+\alpha^{3})^{\frac{5}{3}}\right)\left(\frac{15}{\pi}R^{-3}+4\right)+10\alpha^{3}+4\alpha^{5}.

Since
\frac{15}{\pi}R^{-3}\geq 20
and
1+\alpha^{3}-(1+\alpha^{3})^{\frac{5}{3}}<0
we find

(8)

0\leq 12\left(1+\alpha^{3}-(1+\alpha^{3})^{\frac{5}{3}}\right)+5\alpha^{3}+2\alpha^{5}.

We now use (
8
) to prove that
\alpha=0
. Since
(1+\alpha^{3})^{\frac{5}{3}}\geq 1+\frac{5}{3}\alpha^{3}
, we obtain

0\leq-8\alpha^{3}+5\alpha^{3}+2\alpha^{5}

which gives

(9)

\alpha>0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\alpha^{2}\geq\frac{3}{2}.

On the other hand we have that

(1+\alpha^{3})^{\frac{5}{3}}=\alpha^{5}(1+\alpha^{-3})^{\frac{5}{3}}\geq\alpha^{5}+\frac{5}{3}\alpha^{2}.

Using this instead in (
8
) we find

0\leq 12-20\alpha^{2}+17\alpha^{3}-10\alpha^{5}:=f(\alpha).

We note that
f(1)=-1
. Moreover,

f^{\prime}(\alpha)=-\alpha(40-51\alpha+50\alpha^{3}):=-\alpha g(\alpha).

It’s easy to check that
g(1)=39
. Since

g^{\prime}(\alpha)=150\alpha^{2}-51\geq 0

for
\alpha\geq 1
we find that
g(\alpha)>0
for
\alpha\geq 1
. Thus
f^{\prime}(\alpha)<0
for
\alpha\geq 1
. Finally, this implies that
f(\alpha)<0
for
\alpha\geq 1
, so (
8
) implies that
\alpha<1
. Combined with (
9
) this gives
\alpha=0
as claimed.
∎

.

The above proof could be extended (with some modifications) to cover the range

4.1.
Santaló’s formula

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a compact set with smooth boundary. Let
U:=\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\subset T\Omega
denote the unit sphere bundle over
\Omega
. We write
U_{x}:=\{x\}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
for the fiber over
x
. For
\nu(x)
the
inwards
pointing unit normal to
\partial\Omega
, we set

U^{+}:=\{(x,\sigma)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}:\left\langle\sigma,\nu(x)\right\rangle>0\}

and
U_{x}^{+}:=U^{+}\cap U_{x}
. Below we often write
u=(x,\sigma)\in U^{+}
and
\cos u=\left\langle\sigma,\nu(x)\right\rangle
. Observe that
U
(resp.
U^{+}
) is a smooth submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
and thus inherits a Riemannian metric. We will write
du
for the induced volume measure on both manifolds. We define a function
\ell:U\to[0,\infty)
defined by

\ell(x,\sigma):=\sup\{t\geq 0:x+s\sigma\in\Omega\textrm{ for all }0\leq s\leq t\}.

The following result is the main tool used in the proof of Theorem
6
.

Theorem 8

(Santaló)
.

Set
\Phi((x,\sigma),t)=(x+t\sigma,\sigma)
for
t<\ell(x,\sigma)
. Then,

\int_{U}f(u)du=\int_{U^{+}}\int_{0}^{\ell(u)}f(\Phi(u,t))\cos u\,dtdu

for any
f\in L^{1}(U)
.

This is proven in
[
San04
, pp. 336–338]
(see also
[
Ber65
, p. 286]
) (in the more general setting of the unit sphere bundle on a Riemannian manifold). We sketch the proof of Theorem
8
in Appendix
A
. In the sequel will only ever use the following special case.

Proposition 9
.

For
\alpha>-1
we have

\int_{U}\ell(u)^{\alpha}du=\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{\alpha+1}\cos u\,du.

Proof.

We take
f(u)=\ell(u)^{\alpha}
in Theorem
8
. Note that for
u\in U^{+}
and
t\in(0,\ell(u))
,
\ell(\Phi(u,t))=\ell(u)-t
. Thus

\int_{U}\ell(u)^{\alpha}du=\int_{U^{+}}\int_{0}^{\ell(u)}(\ell(u)-t)^{\alpha}\,\cos u\,dtdu=\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{\alpha+1}\cos u\,du.

This completes the proof.
∎

Taking
\alpha=0
yields the following commonly used expression:

Corollary 10
.

We have

|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}||\Omega|=\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)\cos u\,du.

4.2.
Integral estimates for Coulomb energies

We now show how Santaló’s formula can be used to prove Theorem
6
.

Lemma 11
.

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
compact with smooth boundary we have

\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{3}\cos u\,du\leq 12D(\Omega).

Proof.

For
x\in\Omega
, the volume form
dy
in spherical coordinates centered at
x

(r,\sigma)\in(0,\infty)\times U_{x}
is
r^{2}drd\sigma
(where
d\sigma
is the volume form on the unit sphere
U_{x}
). This gives

\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|}\geq\int_{U_{x}}\int_{0}^{\ell(x,\sigma)}r^{-1}r^{2}drd\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{x}}\ell(x,\sigma)^{2}d\sigma.

Integrating with respect to
x
, we obtain

\displaystyle D(\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\int_{U_{x}}\ell(x,\sigma)^{2}\,d\sigma dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{U}\ell(u)^{2}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{3}\cos u\,du.

We used Santaló’s formula (in the form of Proposition
9
) in the final equality above.
∎

Using this we can now prove the first inequality in Theorem
6
.

Proof of (
11
).

Corollary
10
gives

4\pi|\Omega|=\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)\cos u\,du.

Thus, using Hölder’s inequality we find

\displaystyle(4\pi)^{3}|\Omega|^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{U^{+}}\cos u\,du\right)^{2}\left(\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{3}\cos u\,du\right)

\displaystyle=P(\Omega)^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{+}}\left\langle e_{3},\sigma\right\rangle\,d\sigma\right)^{2}\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{3}\cos u\,du

\displaystyle=\pi^{2}P(\Omega)^{2}\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{3}\cos u\,du\leq 12\pi^{2}P(\Omega)^{2}D(\Omega)

where we used Lemma
11
in the final step and wrote
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{+}=\{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}:\left\langle e_{3},\sigma\right\rangle>0\}
. To complete the proof, we note that if equality held in Hölder’s inequality then
\ell(u)=K
for a.e.
u\in U^{+}
and
K
some fixed constant. This cannot occur, so the inequality at that step must be strict. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 12
.

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
compact with smooth boundary

\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{2}\,du\leq 2D^{\partial}(\Omega).

Proof.

For
x\in\partial\Omega
, we can use spherical coordinates centered at
x
to write

\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|}\geq\int_{U_{x}^{+}}\int_{0}^{\ell(x,\sigma)}r^{-1}r^{2}drd\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{x}^{+}}\ell(x,\sigma)^{2}d\sigma.

Integrating with respect to
x\in\partial\Omega
completes the proof.
∎

Using this we can now prove the second inequality in Theorem
6
.

Proof of (
12
).

Corollary
10
gives

4\pi|\Omega|=\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)\cos u\,du.

Thus, using Hölder’s inequality we find

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}|\Omega|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{U^{+}}\cos^{2}u\,du\right)\left(\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle=P(\Omega)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{+}}\left\langle e_{3},\sigma\right\rangle^{2}\,d\sigma\right)\left(\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{3}P(\Omega)\left(\int_{U^{+}}\ell(u)^{2}du\right)=\frac{4\pi}{3}P(\Omega)D^{\partial}(\Omega)

Rearranging this proves the inequality. It is easy to see that equality holds when
\Omega
is a ball. Conversely, if equality holds in the application of Hölder’s inequality we find that
\ell(u)=K\cos u
for a.e.
u\in U^{+}
so
\Omega
must be a round ball. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
The first variation of energy

In this section we compute the first variation of
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
and particular deduce the value of the Lagrange multiplier at a volume constrained critical point following the scaling method of
[
BdC84
]
. Such a calculation has appeared previously in several places in the literature including
[
BC14
, Theorem 3.3]
,
[
Fra19
, Lemma 12]
, and
[
Jul17
, Lemma 2]
.

In this paper we write
H_{\partial\Omega}
for the sum of the principal curvatures (not the average).

Lemma 13
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a compact set with smooth boundary that’s stationary with respect to
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
for volume preserving variations then

H_{\partial\Omega}+v_{\Omega}=\frac{2P(\Omega)}{3|\Omega|}+\frac{5D(\Omega)}{3|\Omega|}

along
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

The first variation of area implies that
H_{\partial\Omega}+v_{\Omega}=\lambda
is constant along
\partial\Omega
. It thus remains to determine
\lambda
. For
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
(for some
\delta>0
small) we denote
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
the family of regions obtained by flowing
\partial\Omega
with unit normal speed (to the outside). Then, we choose
\eta_{t}
so that
t\mapsto\eta_{t}\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
has constant volume. Because
\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}|\tilde{\Omega}_{t}|=P(\Omega)
we find

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\eta_{t}=-\frac{P(\Omega)}{3|\Omega|}

(using the first variation of volume). Thus

\mathcal{E}(\eta_{t}\tilde{\Omega}_{t})=\eta_{t}^{2}P(\tilde{\Omega}_{t})+\eta_{t}^{5}\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\tilde{\Omega}_{t}\times\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|}.

Differentiating this at
t=0
and using the first variation formulae from the proof of
[
BC14
, Theorem 3.3]
we find

0=-\frac{2P(\Omega)^{2}}{3|\Omega|}-\frac{5P(\Omega)}{6|\Omega|}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|}+\int_{\partial\Omega}(H_{\partial\Omega}(x)+v_{\Omega}(x))dx.

Using
H_{\partial\Omega}(x)+v_{\Omega}(x)=\lambda
is constant, we have proven the assertion.
∎

The following result is not used in the proof of Theorem
1
but may be of some independent interest.

Proposition 14
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a compact set with smooth boundary stationary with respect to
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
for variations fixing
|\Omega|=V\leq\frac{4}{3}\left(\frac{10}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx 2.43
. Then
\partial\Omega
is connected and has positive mean curvature
H_{\partial\Omega}>0
.

Proof.

Write
|\Omega|=V=|B_{R}|
. Combining Lemmas
3
and
13
with (
11
) we find

\displaystyle H_{\partial\Omega}+2\pi R^{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{2P(\Omega)}{3|\Omega|}+\frac{80\pi|\Omega|^{2}}{9P(\Omega)^{2}}\geq 2\left(\frac{10\pi}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},

where we used AM-GM in the last step. Thus,
H_{\partial\Omega}>0
for
R\leq\left(\frac{10}{3\pi^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}
. This completes the proof of mean-convexity.

Finally, if
\partial\Omega
has more than one component, then there will be some component
\Sigma
so that
\Sigma=\partial\Gamma
for a bounded open component of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega
. By touching
\Sigma
from the outside by a sphere, we see that the mean curvature of
\Sigma
is positive at some
p\in\Sigma
, when measured with respect to
\Gamma
. However, this means that the mean curvature at
p
when measured with respect to
\Omega
is negative. This contradicts the above estimate.
∎

6.
Roundness via the Minkowski inequality

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a compact set with smooth boundary. We say that
\Omega
satisfies the
Minkowski inequality
if

(13)

(16\pi P(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\partial\Omega}(x)dx.

Minkowski proved
[
Min03
]
that if
\Omega
is convex then (
13
) holds. We will need the following result of Huisken (see
[
AFM22
, Corollary 1.3]
for a proof):

Theorem 15
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a compact outer-minimizing set with smooth boundary then
\Omega
satisfies the Minkowski inequality (
13
). If equality holds then
\Omega
is a round ball.

It’s conjectured that “outer-minimizing” can be relaxed to “mean-convex.” See
[
GL09
,
DHMT16
,
Gla22
,
CEK23
,
Bre21
]
for related results.

Proposition 16
.

Suppose that a compact set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
has smooth boundary and minimizes
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
among measurable regions with fixed volume
|\Omega|=V\leq 1
. If
\Omega
satisfies the Minkowski inequality (
13
) then
\Omega
agrees with a round ball.

Proof.

Write
|\Omega|=V=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}
. Lemma
13
yields

\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\partial\Omega}(x)dx+D^{\partial}(\Omega)=\frac{2P(\Omega)^{2}}{3|\Omega|}+\frac{5P(\Omega)}{3|\Omega|}D(\Omega)

Combining this with the Minkowski inequality (
13
) (assumed to hold) and the isoperimetric-type inequality from (
12
), we find

(16\pi P(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{12\pi|\Omega|^{2}}{P(\Omega)}\leq\frac{2P(\Omega)^{2}}{3|\Omega|}+\frac{5P(\Omega)}{3|\Omega|}D(\Omega)

Because
\Omega
minimizes
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
among sets of the same volume, we have
D(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{E}(B_{R})-P(\Omega)
. Thus, we arrive at the expression

(14)

(16\pi P(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{12\pi|\Omega|^{2}}{P(\Omega)}\leq\frac{5P(\Omega)}{3|\Omega|}\mathcal{E}(B_{R})-\frac{P(\Omega)^{2}}{|\Omega|}.

Note that
x:=P(\Omega)\geq 4\pi R^{2}
by the isoperimetric inequality with equality only for the round ball. We will show that (
14
) implies that
x=4\pi R^{2}
. Using

\mathcal{E}(B_{R})=4\pi R^{2}+\frac{16\pi^{2}}{15}R^{5}

the inequality (
14
) can be written as

f_{R}(x):=-\frac{3}{4\pi}R^{-3}x^{2}+\left(5R^{-1}+\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{2}\right)x-4\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{64\pi^{3}}{3}R^{6}x^{-1}\geq 0.

We will check that
f_{R}(4\pi R^{2})=0
,
f^{\prime}(4\pi R^{2})\leq 0
(as long as
V\leq 1
), and
f^{\prime\prime}_{R}(x)<0
for
x\geq 4\pi R^{2}
. From this, we see that
x=4\pi R^{2}
as desired.

Since all inequalities used above hold as equalities at the round ball, we deduce that
f_{R}(4\pi R^{2})=0
. Next, we compute

f^{\prime}_{R}(4\pi R^{2})=-2R^{-1}+\frac{8\pi}{3}R^{2}

so
f^{\prime}_{R}(4\pi R^{2})\leq 0
for
V=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}\leq 1
. Finally, we compute

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}_{R}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2\pi}R^{-3}+\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{-\frac{3}{2}}-\frac{128\pi^{3}}{3}R^{6}x^{-3}.

Using
x^{-\frac{3}{2}}\leq(4\pi R^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}
, we can estimate

-\frac{3}{2\pi}R^{-3}+\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{-\frac{3}{2}}\leq-\frac{11}{8\pi}R^{-3},

implying that
f^{\prime\prime}_{R}(x)<0
for
x\geq 4\pi R^{2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Recall the definition of

is continuous (cf.
[

). Let
E_{B}(V):=\mathcal{E}(B_{(\frac{3V}{4\pi})^{\frac{1}{3}}})
denote the profile corresponding to the energy of the round ball of volume

(clearly
E_{B}(V)
is also continuous). Let

(15)

V_{B}:=\sup\{V>0:E(V^{\prime})=E_{B}(V^{\prime})\textrm{ for all $V^{\prime}\leq V$}\}.

By
[
KM14
, Theorem 3.2]
(cf.
[
BC14
,
Jul14
,
FFM+15
]
) we have that
V_{B}>0
. Moreover, by
[
BC14
, Theorem 2.10]
if
V<V_{B}
then the ball uniquely minimizes
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
for fixed volume
V
(and minimizes for
V=V_{B}

Lemma 17
.

If
V_{B}<V_{*}
there is
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
compact with smooth boundary so that
|\Omega|=V_{B}
,
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)=E(V_{B})
but
\Omega
is not a round ball.

Proof.

By
[
FN21
, Theorem 1]
and the definition of
V_{B}
there are
\Omega_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that minimize
\mathcal{E}(\Omega_{j})
among sets of fixed volume so that
|\Omega_{j}|\searrow V_{B}
but
\Omega_{j}
are not round balls. By
[
KM14
, Lemma 7.2]
we may assume that the sets
\Omega_{j}
are uniformly essentially bounded. Thus, by standard compactness results for sets of finite perimeter (cf.
[
Sim83
, §2.6]
) we can pass to a subsequence with
\Omega_{j}\to\Omega
in
L^{1}
and
P(\Omega)\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}P(\Omega_{j})
. As in
[
FL15

we have
D(\Omega_{j})\to D(\Omega)
. Thus, by continuity of
E(\cdot)
we find

\mathcal{E}(\Omega)\leq E(V_{B}),

so
\Omega
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
among sets of volume
V_{B}
. By
[
BC14
, Theorem 2.7]
, we can assume that
\Omega
is compact with smooth boundary.

Suppose that
\Omega
was a round ball (up to a set of measure zero). Then, the strict stability of the ball (in the range of volumes under consideration) implies that
\Omega_{j}
must also be a round ball for

sufficiently large (see the proof of
[
BC14
, Theorem 2.10]
). This contradicts our assumption that the

\varepsilon>0
so that round balls of volume
\leq 1+\varepsilon
uniquely minimize
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
among competing regions of fixed volume.

.

As observed above, it suffices to prove that
V_{B}
(defined in (
15
)) satisfies
V_{B}>1
. For the sake of contradiction, assume that

. By Lemma

there exists

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
8

Write
U^{\circ}
for the interior of
U
and

\tilde{U}:=\{(u,t)\in U^{+}\times(0,\infty):t<\ell(u)\}.

Then, we define

\Phi:\tilde{U}\to U^{\circ},\qquad\Phi:((x,\sigma),t)\mapsto(x+t\sigma,\sigma).

The map
\Phi
is a diffeomorphism onto its image and
U^{\circ}\setminus\Phi(\tilde{U})
has measure zero. We now compute the Jacobian of
\Phi
at
p:=((x,\sigma),t)\in T\tilde{U}
. We can assume that
T_{x}\partial\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\{0\}
. Note that (using the splitting
T_{p}\tilde{U}=T_{x}\partial\Omega\times T_{\sigma}\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and
T_{\Phi(p)}U=T_{x+t\sigma}\mathbb{R}^{N}\times T_{\sigma}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
):

\displaystyle d\Phi_{p}(v,0,0)

\displaystyle=(v,0)

\displaystyle d\Phi_{p}(0,w,0)

\displaystyle=(tw,w)

\displaystyle d\Phi_{p}(0,0,1)

\displaystyle=(\sigma,0).

In particular, if we choose an orthonormal basis
w_{1},\dots,w_{N-1}
for
T_{\sigma}\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
and use
e_{1},\dots,e_{N}
for the standard basis on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we find that the matrix of
d\Phi_{p}
becomes

\left(\begin{matrix}\Id_{(N-1)\times(N-1)}&*&*\\
0_{1\times(n-1)}&t&\left\langle\sigma,e_{N}\right\rangle\\
0_{(N-1)\times(N-1)}&\Id_{(N-1)\times(N-1)}&0_{(N-1)\times 1}\end{matrix}\right)

which has determinant
\pm\left\langle\sigma,e_{N}\right\rangle=\pm\cos u
. The assertion thus follows from the change of variables formula.

A Hodge-theoretic proof of Hwang’s theorem on base manifolds of Lagrangian fibrations
Benjamin Bakker, Christian Schnell

Abstract
. We give a Hodge-theoretic proof of Hwang’s theorem, which says that if the base of a Lagrangian fibration of an irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold is smooth, it must be projective space.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be an irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
, and let

f:X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration over a base of dimension
n
. In this
note, we reprove the following well-known result of Hwang
[
Hwa08
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Hwang)
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration of an irreducible holomorphic symplectic
manifold of dimension
2n
. If
B
is smooth, then
B\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

The proof that we present below is inspired by Hwang’s original argument, but perhaps
clarified by the consistent use of Hodge theory, especially the theory of
variations of Hodge structure. There are two other important inputs. The first is the
following enhancement of Mori’s characterization of projective space, due to Cho,
Miyaoka, and Shepherd-Barron
[
CMSB02
]
and Kebekus
[
Keb02
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Cho–Miyaoka–Shepherd-Barron, Kebekus)
.

Let
Y
be a uniruled smooth projective variety and suppose there is a dense
Zariski open subset
Y^{\circ}\subseteq Y
such that every nonconstant rational
curve
g\colon C\to Y
meeting
Y^{\circ}
satisfies
-\deg g^{*}\omega_{Y}\geq\dim Y+1
. Then
Y
is isomorphic to projective space.

The second input is a result of Voisin
[
Voi18
]
about the
variation of Hodge structure on the first cohomology of the fibers of a Lagrangian fibration.
Let
B^{\circ}\subseteq B
be the open set over which the Lagrangian fibration

f
is smooth, and
f^{\circ}:X^{\circ}\to B^{\circ}
the base change of
f
to
B^{\circ}
.
It is known that the discriminant locus
D=B\setminus B^{\circ}
is a nonempty
divisor. We denote by
V=R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}
the polarized variation of Hodge
structure of weight
1
on
B^{\circ}
; by
\mathcal{V}
the underlying flat vector
bundle; by
\nabla
the Gauss-Manin connection on
\mathcal{V}
; and by
F^{1}\mathcal{V}=f^{\circ}_{*}\Omega_{X^{\circ}/B^{\circ}}^{1}
the Hodge bundle of type
(1,0)
.

Theorem 1.3

(Voisin)
.

V\otimes\mathbb{R}
is irreducible as a real variation of Hodge structure.

The anticanonical degree of a curve (as well as its deformation theory) is linked to
the variation
V
since contraction with the symplectic form yields a natural
isomorphism
\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}\cong F^{1}\mathcal{V}
. Work of Matsushita

[
Mat05
]
extends this to an isomorphism between
\mathscr{T}_{B}
and the
“canonical extension” of
F^{1}\mathcal{V}
in codimension
one, and this will be an important ingredient in our approach.

It is known by
[
Mat99
]
that
B
is a Fano manifold (and therefore
uniruled). The strategy is to
assume the condition in
1.2
is not satisfied, so that there is a low
degree rational curve meeting
B^{\circ}
, and conclude from this that: (1) the curve
deforms to cover

B
; and (2) the variation
V
must have a nontrivial fixed part when restricted to

C
. We then obtain a nontrivial splitting of
V
on the universal family of curves
and thus on a finite cover of
B
. To finish, we show that this splitting descends to
B^{\circ}
,
where it of course contradicts
1.3
. In our approach, the first step is proved using a Hodge module version of
Matsushita’s result combined with a straightforward functoriality property of Hodge
modules to show that
g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
is semi-positive (see
2.2
) and
that its maximal trivial quotient comes from the fixed part of the variation (see

2.4
). As the low degree curve which is assumed to exist may
a priori not deform, it can intersect the discriminant divisor in its singular
locus, and the Hodge module perspective provides a version of Matsushita’s theorem
that works in higher codimension. The descent step is straightforward in the case
that
f
is non-isotrivial (using the result of
[
Bak22
]
, see
Remark

6.3
), but we do not actually use this. Instead, we prove the descent in general using a rather tricky argument which is a reinterpretation of an argument from Hwang’s paper using Hodge theory to simplify the steps. Note that the descent step must in some sense be subtle—in fact, it is the most difficult part of our proof—as it is
easy to construct examples where
V
splits off a factor on a finite cover that is
constant on a covering family of rational curves (see
Remark

7.1
).

Remark 1.4
.

The recent preprint by Li and Tosatti
[
LT23
]
contains another proof of
Hwang’s theorem that follows the same overall strategy, but has a more analytic
flavor. Instead of Hodge theory, Li and Tosatti rely on the theory of special Kähler
manifolds
[
Fre99
]
. They use the curvature of the special Kähler metric on

B^{\circ}
to show that the restriction of
\mathscr{T}_{B}
to a rational curve of minimal
degree is semipositive, and that any trivial quotient of
g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
actually
comes from a subbundle that is flat with respect to the Chern connection of the
metric. For the descent step at the end of the proof, however, they depend on one of
the results from our paper.

Outline

In
Section

2
we prove the results on the semipostivity of
g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
and the relation between the maximal trivial quotient and the fixed part of the variation. We give two proofs: the first in the projective case is more geometric, using results of Kollár; the second as a consequence of the functoriality of Hodge module extensions. In
Section

3
we make some general remarks relating subvariations to foliations on
B
. In
Section

4
we use the existence of a low degree curve to construct a nontrivial splitting of the variation on a finite cover. In
Section

5
, we study the geometry of the subvarieties swept out by connected components of the family of rational curves through a given point. (For the sake of exposition, we reprove all the necessary results, which are due to Araujo
[
Ara06
]
and Hwang
[
Hwa07
]
, even though this makes the paper longer.) In
Section

6
, we describe the main ingredient for descending the splitting to
B
, using ideas from
[
Hwa08
]
. Finally, we prove
1.1
in
Section

7
.

Notation and terminology

By a
rational curve

g:C\to B
we always mean a nonconstant morphism from
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If the image meets the open subset
B^{\circ}
, we denote by

g^{\circ}:C^{\circ}:=g^{-1}(B^{\circ})\to B^{\circ}
the restriction of
g
, and in general
the superscript “
\circ
” always stands for objects that are related to
B^{\circ}
.
We denote by
V_{C}:=(g^{\circ})^{*}V
the pullback of
the variation, and likewise for any pullback. We typically use Roman letters
V,W

to denote local systems and script letters
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}
to denote the
associated flat vector bundles.

2.
Restriction of the tangent bundle to rational curves

In this section, we use results from Hodge theory to analyze the splitting behavior
of the tangent bundle
\mathscr{T}_{B}
on rational curves in
B
that meet the smooth locus

B^{\circ}
of the Lagrangian fibration
f\colon X\to B
. Here it will be more
convenient to work with the variation of Hodge structure on
R^{2n-1}f_{*}^{\circ}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}
instead of the one on
R^{1}f_{*}^{\circ}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}
. This
makes no essential difference, because the Hard Lefschetz theorem says that, after
tensoring by
\mathbb{R}
, the two variations are isomorphic up to a Tate twist by

\mathbb{R}(-n+1)
; the isomorphism is induced by cup product with the
(n-1)
-th power of a
Kähler form on
X
. We will therefore change the notation slightly, and—in this
section only—use the symbol
V
for the variation of Hodge structure on
R^{2n-1}f_{*}^{\circ}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}
. The relevant Hodge bundle is then
F^{n}\mathcal{V}=R^{n-1}f_{*}^{\circ}\omega_{X^{\circ}/B^{\circ}}
; it is of course isomorphic to
f_{*}^{\circ}\Omega_{X^{\circ}/B^{\circ}}^{1}
.

Our starting point is the following result by Matsushita
[
Mat05
]
,
which describes the tangent bundle of
B
in terms of Hodge theory.

Theorem 2.1
.

There is an isomorphism
R^{n-1}f_{*}\omega_{X/B}\cong\mathscr{T}_{B}
, whose restriction
to
B^{\circ}
is the natural isomorphism induced by the holomorphic symplectic
form.

Proof.

Matsushita proves that the natural isomorphism on
B^{\circ}
extends to
R^{n-1}f_{*}\omega_{X}\cong\Omega_{B}^{n-1}\cong\omega_{B}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{B}
. Tensoring both
sides by
\omega_{B}^{-1}
then gives the desired result. Matsushita only states the
theorem for projective
X
, but his proof carries over to the case
where
X
is a compact Kähler manifold. For an alternative proof, see

[
Sch23
, §4]
.
∎

Let
g\colon C\to B
be a nonconstant morphism from
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose image
intersects
B^{\circ}
. Like any vector
bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the pullback
g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
decomposes as

(1)

g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}\cong\mathscr{O}(a_{1})\oplus\dotsb\oplus\mathscr{O}(a_{n})

for certain integers
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}
. Our first result is that
g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
is
semi-positive.

Proposition 2.2
.

The pullback
g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
is semi-positive, in the sense that
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\geq 0
.

We will first present a geometric proof in the case when
X
is projective;
afterwards, we will give a Hodge-theoretic proof that also works when
X
is a
compact Kähler manifold. Let us denote by
h\colon Y\to C
the base change of
the Lagrangian fibration, and
let
r\colon\tilde{Y}\to Y
be a resolution of singularities of
Y
that is an
isomorphism over the preimage of
C^{\circ}=g^{-1}(B^{\circ})
. For dimension
reasons, the composition
\tilde{h}=r\circ h\colon\tilde{Y}\to C
is still flat of
relative dimension
n
. We get a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

in which the square is Cartesian. The key step in the proof is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

There is a morphism of sheaves

R^{n-1}\tilde{h}_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}/C}\to g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}

that restricts to the natural isomorphism over the open subset
C^{\circ}=g^{-1}(B^{\circ})
.

Proof.

Before giving the proof, we need to review a few facts about relative dualizing
sheaves and base change. Here we probably have to assume that
X
is projective
(although it is known by
[
Cam21
]
that Lagrangian
fibrations are projective locally in the analytic topology on the base). In this algebraic setting, a good reference for duality theory is

[
Sta18
,
Tag ODWE
]
.
To begin with,
B
is smooth and
f
is flat,
and so all fibers of
f
are local complete intersections in
X
and therefore
Gorenstein. Since
f
is proper and flat, it follows that the relative dualizing
sheaf
\omega_{X/B}
is isomorphic to
\omega_{X}\otimes f^{*}\omega_{B}^{-1}
, and
that the relative dualizing complex is
\omega_{X/B}[n]
. Moreover, in this
situation, the relative dualizing sheaf commutes with arbitrary base change.

Now we begin the proof. Flatness of
f
and the base change property for the
relative dualizing sheaf give us an isomorphism

g^{*}\mathbf{R}f_{*}\omega_{X/B}\cong\mathbf{R}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}.

Since all the higher direct images
R^{i}f_{*}\omega_{X/B}
are locally free (by
Matsushita’s theorem), we get in particular that

(2)

g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}\cong g^{*}R^{n-1}f_{*}\omega_{X/B}\cong R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}.

Consider now the resolution of singularities
r\colon\tilde{Y}\to Y
. The trace map
gives us a morphism
r_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}}\to\omega_{Y}
that is an isomorphism over
the smooth locus of
Y
and therefore injective. The induced morphism

(3)

R^{n-1}\tilde{h}_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}/C}\to R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}

is of course still an isomorphism over
C^{\circ}
. We then get the desired
morphism by composing (
3
) and (
2
); it is
clearly the natural isomorphism over
C^{\circ}
.
∎

We can now prove that
g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
is always semi-positive.

Proof of
2.2
.

From
2.3
, we get a short exact sequence

0\to R^{n-1}\tilde{h}_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}/C}\to g^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}\to\mathscr{Q}\to 0,

in which
\mathscr{Q}
is supported on the finite set
C\setminus C^{\circ}
and therefore
a torsion sheaf. This reduces the problem to the semi-positivity of
R^{n-1}\tilde{h}_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}/C}
. Since
\tilde{h}\colon\tilde{Y}\to C
is a Kähler fiber space over a
curve, this is the content of a classical result by Fujita
[
Fuj78
]
. Alternatively, one can argue using Kollár’s results
[
Kol86a
]
about higher direct images of dualizing sheaves. Since
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have

R^{i}h_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}/C}\otimes\mathscr{O}(-1)\cong R^{i}h_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}}\otimes\mathscr{O}(1),

and this bundle has no higher cohomology by Kollár’s vanishing theorem (which
applies to any morphism from a compact Kähler manifold to a projective
variety). But on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, this is only possible if
R^{i}h_{*}\omega_{\tilde{Y}/C}
is
semi-positive.
∎

We also need to understand the trivial part in the decomposition (
1
), at
least in the case of generic rational curves. Let us therefore assume from now on
that
g\colon C\to B
is an immersed rational curve that intersects the
discriminant divisor
D=B\setminus B^{\circ}
transversely. The
preimage
Y=X\times_{C}B
is then already smooth, and (
2
) gives
us an isomorphism

g^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}\cong R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}.

Suppose that in the decomposition (
1
), we have
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{r}=0
and

a_{r+1},\dotsc,a_{n}>0
. In that case, the bundle
g^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}
has a
canonical trivial quotient

g^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}\to\mathscr{O}_{C}^{\oplus r}.

On the open subset
C^{\circ}=g^{-1}(B^{\circ})
, we have a polarized variation of
Hodge structure of weight
2n-1
, whose underlying local system is
R^{2n-1}h^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y^{\circ}}
; here
h^{\circ}\colon Y^{\circ}\to C^{\circ}
is the restriction of
h
. Our next result identifies the maximal trivial quotient of
g^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}

as coming from the fixed part of the variation of Hodge structure on
R^{2n-1}h^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y^{\circ}}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The fixed part of the variation of Hodge structure on
R^{2n-1}h^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y^{\circ}}
has rank
exactly
2r
, and the Hodge bundle of type
(n,n-1)
of the fixed part projects
isomorphically to the maximal trivial quotient of
g^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}
.

Proof.

To simplify the notation, let us set
V_{C}=R^{2n-1}h^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y^{\circ}}
,
and let us denote by
\mathcal{V}_{C}
the underlying flat vector bundle, and by
F^{n}\mathcal{V}_{C}
the Hodge bundle of
type
(n,n-1)
. Since
h
has relative dimension
n
, the restriction of
R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}
to the open subset
C^{\circ}
is canonically isomorphic to
F^{n}\mathcal{V}_{C}
. In fact, more is true. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{C}^{>-1}
denote Deligne’s canonical
extension of the flat vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{C}
; it is uniquely characterized by the
condition that the connection on
\mathcal{V}_{C}
has logarithmic poles on
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{C}^{>-1}
and
that the residues of the connection at each point of
C\setminus C^{\circ}
have
eigenvalues contained in the interval
(-1,0]
. With this notation, Kollár

[
Kol86a
]
proves that

(4)

R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}\cong\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{C}^{>-1}\cap j_{*}(F^{n}\mathcal{V}_{C}),

where
j\colon C^{\circ}\to C
is the inclusion.

The fixed part of
V_{C}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure of type
(n,n-1)+(n-1,n)
, and so its dimension is an even number, say
2s
. The fixed part then
contributes a trivial summand of rank
2s
to the canonical extension

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{C}^{>-1}
, and, due to (
4
), a trivial summand of rank
s
to
the bundle
R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}
. This gives
r\geq s
. To prove that
r=s
, we need to argue that any nontrivial morphism of the form

(5)

R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}\to\mathscr{O}_{C}

must come from the fixed part of
V_{C}
. This is a straightforward computation with
duality. First, we observe that since
C
is a curve, the derived pushforward of
the line bundle
\omega_{Y/C}
decomposes as

\mathbf{R}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}R^{i}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}[-i]

in the derived category of coherent sheaves on
C
. (This kind of result is due to
Kollár
[
Kol86b
]
in general, but only needs elementary
homological algebra in the case of curves.) Consequently,
R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}
is a direct summand of
\mathbf{R}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}[n-1]
.

Grothendieck duality for the morphism
h\colon Y\to C
yields

\operatorname{Hom}_{C}\Bigl(\mathbf{R}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C}[n-1],\mathscr{O}_{C}\Bigr)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{Y}\Bigl(\omega_{Y/C}[n-1],\omega_{Y/C}[n]\Bigr)\cong H^{1}(Y,\mathscr{O}_{Y}).

After substituting in the above formula for the direct image and remembering that

H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})=0
(because
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
), we get an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{C}\Bigl(R^{n-1}h_{*}\omega_{Y/C},\mathscr{O}_{C}\Bigr)\cong H^{1}(Y,\mathscr{O}_{Y}).

Now
Y
is a compact Kähler manifold, and so by classical Hodge theory, the
morphism
H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{C})\to H^{1}(Y,\mathscr{O}_{Y})
is surjective. After some
compatibility checking, it follows that any morphism as in (
5
) comes
about in the following way: at any point
c\in C^{\circ}
, the restriction of
(
5
) is the linear functional
H^{n,n-1}(Y_{c})\to\mathbb{C}

obtained by integration against the restriction of a fixed class in
H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{C})

to the fiber
Y_{c}=h^{-1}(c)
. Since restriction to the smooth fibers of
h\colon Y\to C
determines a morphism of variations of Hodge structure

H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Q})\otimes R^{2n-1}h^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y^{\circ}}\to R^{2n}h^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y^{\circ}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{C}(-n),

we conclude (by the semi-simplicity of polarized variations of Hodge structure)
that the fixed part of
R^{2n-1}h^{\circ}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y^{\circ}}
must have rank at least
2r
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is possible that the construction with relative dualizing sheaves and base
change still works in the analytic setting, but we have not been able to
find a reference for the necessary technical results.

In the remainder of this chapter, we explain how one can modify the argument above to
make it work when
X
is just a compact Kähler manifold. The proof relies
on a few basic properties of Hodge modules
[
Sai88
,
Sai90
]
. Let
M
denote
the polarized Hodge module of weight
\dim B+(2n-1)
with strict support
B
,
associated to the variation of Hodge structure
V=R^{2n-1}f_{*}^{\circ}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}

on
B^{\circ}
by
[
Sai90
, Thm. 3.21]
. Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the (regular
holonomic) left
\mathscr{D}_{B}
-module underlying
M
, and let
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
be its
Hodge filtration. By Saito’s direct image theorem
[
Sai88
, Thm. 5.3.1]
(and

[
Sai91
]
for the constant Hodge module on a compact Kähler manifold), we
have
F_{-n-1}\mathcal{M}=0
, and

\omega_{B}\otimes F_{-n}\mathcal{M}\cong R^{n-1}f_{*}\omega_{X}.

Together with
2.1
, this implies that
F_{-n}\mathcal{M}\cong\mathscr{T}_{B}
,
and so we can also think of the tangent sheaf as being part of a Hodge module on
B
.
We may sometimes refer to the coherent
\mathscr{O}_{B}
-module
F_{-n}\mathcal{M}
as the “Hodge
module extension” of
F^{n}\mathcal{V}
.

There is an alternative description of the coherent sheaf
F_{-n}\mathcal{M}
using
resolution of singularities. Let
\mu\colon\tilde{B}\to B
be a log resolution of the
discriminant divisor
D=B\setminus B^{\circ}
that is an isomorphism over the open
subset over which
D
is a divisor with normal crossings, hence in particular
over
B^{\circ}
. Let
\mathcal{V}
denote the flat vector bundle underlying the variation
of Hodge structure
V=R^{2n-1}f_{*}^{\circ}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}
, and let
F^{n}\mathcal{V}=R^{n}f_{*}^{\circ}\omega_{X^{\circ}/B^{\circ}}
be the Hodge bundle of type

(n,n-1)
. As before, let
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{>-1}
denote Deligne’s canonical extension of the
flat vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
; this is the unique extension of
\mathcal{V}
to a vector bundle
on
\tilde{B}
on which the connection has logarithmic poles with residues having
eigenvalues in the interval
(-1,0]
. By Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem, the Hodge
bundle
F^{n}\mathcal{V}
extends to a subbundle
F^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{>-1}=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{>-1}\cap j_{*}(F^{n}\mathcal{V})
, where
j\colon B^{\circ}\to\tilde{B}
is the inclusion. According to

[
Sai91
, Thm. 2.4 and (2.2.2)]
, we then have

(6)

\omega_{B}\otimes F_{-n}\mathcal{M}\cong\mathbf{R}\mu_{*}\Bigl(\omega_{\tilde{B}}\otimes F^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{>-1}\Bigr).

This morphism is an isomorphism over the open subset where
D=B\setminus B^{\circ}
is a divisor with normal crossings.

We can now present the alternative proof of
2.2
. Let

be a rational curve meeting the smooth locus

of the Lagrangian

be its extension to a polarized Hodge module of

. Let
(\mathcal{M}_{C},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}_{C})
be the underlying filtered left

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{C}^{>-1}
for Deligne’s canonical extension, then for the same reason as above,

F_{-n}\mathcal{M}_{C}\cong F^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{C}^{>-1},

V_{C}

.

There is a morphism of sheaves

This kind of morphism is constructed in

greater generality. We present here a more elementary argument
that reduces the input from the theory of Hodge modules to a minimum. Recall
from (

this is a morphism of sheaves

By construction, this morphism is an isomorphism outside the exceptional divisor
of

The rational curve

intersects the smooth locus

, and
therefore lifts uniquely to a rational curve
\tilde{g}\colon C\to\tilde{B}
, making the
following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

After pulling back (

This gives us an injective morphism from the semi-positive vector bundle

Now suppose that the rational curve

3.
Foliations and variations of Hodge structure

The purpose of this section is to prove a couple of small technical results that relate the
variation of Hodge structure on
V=R^{1}f_{*}^{\circ}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}
to the theory of
foliations. Here
f\colon X\to B
is again a Lagrangian fibration from a compact
Kähler manifold
X
.

Recall that
\mathcal{V}
denotes the underlying flat vector bundle,
\nabla\colon\mathcal{V}\to\Omega_{B^{\circ}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}
the Gauss-Manin connection, and
F^{1}\mathcal{V}=f_{*}^{\circ}\Omega_{X^{\circ}/B^{\circ}}^{1}
the Hodge bundle. We have
F^{1}\mathcal{V}\cong\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}
, with the isomorphism induced by contraction with the holomorphic
symplectic form
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})
. We are going to need the following
result by Freed
[
Fre99
]
that describes the Lie bracket on the
tangent bundle in terms of the connection.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any two holomorphic vector fields
\xi,\eta\in\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}
, one has

\nabla_{\xi}(\eta)-\nabla_{\eta}(\xi)=[\xi,\eta].

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq B^{\circ}
be an open subset and
\xi,\eta\in H^{0}(U,\mathscr{T}_{B})

be two holomorphic vector fields. Since
f^{\circ}\colon X^{\circ}\to B^{\circ}

is a fiber bundle, we can lift them to smooth vector fields
\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta}\in A^{0}\bigl(f^{-1}(U),\mathscr{T}_{X}\bigr)
; then
[\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta}]
is a smooth lifting of the
Lie bracket
[\xi,\eta]
. The isomorphism
\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}\cong F^{1}\mathcal{V}

takes the holomorphic vector field
\xi
to the fiberwise cohomology class of the
smooth
(1,0)
-form

\Omega_{\tilde{\xi}}=\cont(\tilde{\xi})(\sigma)\in A^{1,0}\bigl(f^{-1}(U)\bigr),

obtained by contracting the vector field
\tilde{\xi}
against the holomorphic symplectic
form. In these terms, the Gauss-Manin connection
\nabla_{\xi}(\eta)\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{V})
is given by taking the fiberwise cohomology class of the smooth
1
-form

\cont(\tilde{\xi})(d\Omega_{\tilde{\eta}})\in A^{1}\bigl(f^{-1}(U)\bigr).

Now we compute that, for any smooth vector field
\lambda
on
f^{-1}(U)
, one has

\displaystyle\Bigl(

\displaystyle\cont(\tilde{\xi})(d\Omega_{\tilde{\eta}})-\cont(\tilde{\eta})(d\Omega_{\tilde{\xi}})\Bigr)(\lambda)=d\Omega_{\tilde{\eta}}(\tilde{\xi},\lambda)-d\Omega_{\tilde{\xi}}(\tilde{\eta},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\tilde{\xi}\cdot\sigma(\tilde{\eta},\lambda)-\lambda\cdot\sigma(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})-\sigma\bigl(\tilde{\eta},[\tilde{\xi},\lambda]\bigr)\Bigr)-\Bigl(\tilde{\eta}\cdot\sigma(\tilde{\xi},\lambda)-\lambda\cdot\sigma(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})-\sigma\bigl(\tilde{\xi},[\tilde{\eta},\lambda]\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sigma\bigl([\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta}],\lambda\bigr)+\lambda\cdot\sigma(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta}),

using the coordinate-free formula for the exterior derivative, and the identity

\displaystyle 0=(d\sigma)(\lambda,\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})

\displaystyle=\lambda\cdot\sigma(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})+\tilde{\xi}\cdot\sigma(\tilde{\eta},\lambda)+\tilde{\eta}\cdot\sigma(\lambda,\tilde{\xi})

\displaystyle+\sigma\bigl(\lambda,[\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta}]\bigr)+\sigma\bigl(\tilde{\xi},[\tilde{\eta},\lambda]\bigr)+\sigma\bigl(\tilde{\eta},[\lambda,\tilde{\xi}]\bigr).

This proves that
\cont(\tilde{\xi})(d\Omega_{\tilde{\eta}})-\cont(\tilde{\eta})(d\Omega_{\tilde{\xi}})=\Omega_{[\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta}]}+d\bigl(\sigma(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})\bigr)
, which then gives the desired
identity in the first cohomology of the fibers of the Lagrangian fibration.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Since
\mathcal{V}^{1,0}\cong\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}
and
\mathcal{V}^{0,1}\cong\Omega_{B^{\circ}}^{1}
, this also implies that the Higgs field

\overline{\nabla}\colon\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}\otimes\mathcal{V}^{1,0}\to\mathcal{V}^{0,1}

is symmetric in its two arguments. This fact is a special case of the “cubic
condition” of Donagi and Markman
[
DM96
]
. The identity in

Lemma

3.1
is actually equivalent to the statement that the hermitian metric
on
B^{\circ}
induced by the Hodge metric on the bundle
\mathcal{V}^{1,0}
is a Kähler
metric
[
Fre99
]
.

The formula for the Lie bracket allows us to produce foliations from
subvariations of
V
, using the fact that
F^{1}\mathcal{V}\cong\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}
is the
tangent bundle of
B^{\circ}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\colon U\to B^{\circ}
be a holomorphic mapping that is locally
biholomorphic, and let
W
be a subvariation of Hodge structure of
V_{U}=p^{*}V
.
Then the distribution given by the Hodge bundle
F^{1}\mathcal{W}\subseteq F^{1}\mathcal{V}_{U}
is
integrable, hence defines a foliation on
U
.

Proof.

Since
p
is locally biholomorphic, we have
F^{1}\mathcal{V}_{U}=p^{*}(F^{1}\mathcal{V})\cong p^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B^{\circ}}\cong\mathscr{T}_{U}
. Because
W
is a subvariation of Hodge structure,
the vector bundle
\mathcal{W}
is preserved by the Gauss-Manin connection on
\mathcal{V}_{U}
.
From
\mathcal{W}\cap F^{1}\mathcal{V}_{U}=F^{1}\mathcal{W}
, we get

[\xi,\eta]=\nabla_{\xi}(\eta)-\nabla_{\eta}(\xi)\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{W})\cap H^{0}(U,F^{1}\mathcal{V}_{U})=H^{0}(U,F^{1}\mathcal{W})

for any two holomorphic vector fields
\xi,\eta
in the subspace
H^{0}(U,F^{1}\mathcal{W})\subseteq H^{0}(U,\mathscr{T}_{U})
. This proves that the distribution is integrable.
∎

We are also going to need the following simple fact about the integrability of the
sum of two integrable distributions.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F
and
G
be holomorphic foliations of rank
d
on a complex manifold,
whose sum
F+G
is a foliation of rank
2d
. Take a leaf
L
of
F
, and
form the union of the leaves of
G
through points of
L
. Then the
resulting submanifold is a leaf of
F+G
.

Proof.

Fix a point on
L
, and let
S
be the unique leaf of
F+G
through that point;
then
S
is a (locally defined) complex submanifold of dimension
2d
, and since

F\subseteq F+G
, it contains
L
. The restriction
G|_{S}
is a foliation on

S
, and so
S
is locally the union of leaves of
G
. For dimension reasons,
those leaves of
G|_{S}
that pass through points of
L
fill up all of
S
,
and this gives the desired result.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The proof of the lemma shows that the construction is actually symmetric in
F
and

G
: if we start from a leaf of
G
that is contained in
S
, and form the union
of the leaves of
F
through its points, we get the same submanifold
S
as
before.

Lastly, we need to analyze the behavior of the tangent bundle at smooth points of the
discriminant locus
D
. It is known that
D
is always a divisor in
B

[
HO09
, Prop. 3.1]
, and that the local monodromy at any smooth point of

is always nontrivial
[
Hwa08
, Prop. 3.2]
. Our proof does not actually
rely on these two facts, but it is nevertheless useful to keep them in mind. The next
result says that if the local monodromy at a smooth point of

has finite order,
then it looks like the local monodromy in an isotrivial elliptic fibration on a K3
surface.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that, in a neighborhood of a given point
b\in B
, the discriminant locus

D
is smooth of dimension
n-1
, and that the local monodromy around
D
has
finite order. Then the local monodromy transformation has exactly two nontrivial
eigenvalues, which are a
4
th or
6
th root of unity and its complex conjugate.

Proof.

Let
U
be a neighborhood of the point in question, and suppose that
D\cap U
is
smooth and defined by a nontrivial holomorphic function
h\in\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}_{B})
. Let
V_{U}

denote the restriction of the variation of Hodge structure
V=R^{1}f_{*}^{\circ}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\circ}}
to this open subset. By Matsushita’s theorem, the isomorphism

\mathscr{T}_{U^{\circ}}\cong F^{1}\mathcal{V}
extends to an isomorphism

\mathscr{T}_{U}\cong F^{1}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{U}^{>-1}

between the tangent bundle of

and the subbundle
F^{1}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{U}^{>-1}=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{U}^{>-1}\cap j_{*}(F^{1}\mathcal{V}_{U})
of the canonical extension of
(\mathcal{V}_{U},\nabla)
along the open embedding
j\colon U^{\circ}\to U
.

Since the monodromy transformation
T
has finite order, all of its eigenvalues
are roots of unity. Moreover, the weight
filtration defined by the logarithm of the unipotent part of
T
is trivial, and
so the limiting mixed Hodge structure at

is a polarized Hodge structure of
type
(1,0)+(0,1)
, and

is an automorphism of this Hodge structure

[
Sch73
, Thm. 6.16]
. By choosing a basis that is adapted to the Hodge
decomposition and the eigenspace decomposition of

, we obtain a local frame

v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n},v_{n+1},\dotsc,v_{2n}\in\Gamma\bigl(U,\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{U}^{>-1}\bigr)

for the canonical extension, with the property that

\nabla(v_{j})=\alpha_{j}\frac{dh}{h}\otimes v_{j},

where
\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(-1,0]
. As explained in

[
CK89
, §2]
(and in
[
SS22
]
for the case of complex variations of
Hodge structure), we can then find a collection of holomorphic functions

f_{j,k}
near the point

, such that the

sections

define a local frame for the bundle
F^{1}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{U}^{>-1}
, after shrinking the open
set
U
if necessary. Because of the isomorphism
\mathscr{T}_{U}\cong F^{1}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{U}^{>-1}
,
we may also view

as holomorphic vector fields on

that
generate the tangent bundle
\mathscr{T}_{U}
.

We now apply the formula for the Lie bracket in
Lemma

3.1
. It gives

[s_{i},s_{j}]=\nabla_{s_{i}}(s_{j})-\nabla_{s_{j}}(s_{i})\equiv\alpha_{j}\frac{dh(s_{i})}{h}\cdot s_{j}-\alpha_{i}\frac{dh(s_{j})}{h}\cdot s_{i}\mod v_{n+1},\dotsc,v_{2n},

and because of the special shape of the sections

, the
congruence is strong enough to imply that

[s_{i},s_{j}]=\alpha_{j}\frac{dh(s_{i})}{h}\cdot s_{j}-\alpha_{i}\frac{dh(s_{j})}{h}\cdot s_{i}.

Now the left-hand side is again a holomorphic vector field on

, and so we
deduce that, for every pair of indices

, either

, or

divides

. This last condition means that the vector field

is
tangent to the hypersurface

. Since not all the vector fields can be

, we conclude that at most one of

can
be nonzero; in fact, exactly one of them has to be nonzero, because the monodromy

is nontrivial.

To prove the remaining assertion, we observe that

is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
and
that

. Therefore

has exactly two nontrivial eigenvalues, which are
roots of unity and complex conjugates of each other. By checking the possible
values of Euler’s

-function, it is then easy to see that the two eigenvalues
must be either
4
th or
6
th roots of unity.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Suppose that

and

.
The identity in (

) shows that the

holomorphic vector fields

are tangent to

and

.

By the same method, one can show that if

is the

), then

, and the limiting mixed Hodge structure

and dimension

. This is

[
HO09
]
.

4.
Deformation theory and the universal family of curves

We first explain why
2.2
and
2.4
, together with
some basic deformation theory, imply that
V
splits off a factor universally
computing the fixed part of the variation of Hodge structure on any rational curve of
minimal degree, at least on a certain finite cover. We recommend
Kollár’s book
[
Kol96
]
for the necessary background on rational
curves. Throughout,
B
is the smooth base of a Lagrangian fibration,
B^{\circ}
is
the complement of the discriminant locus, and

.

Recall that
\omega^{\vee}_{B}=\det\mathscr{T}_{B}
is ample
[

]
. Suppose

is a rational curve meeting

and of minimal anticanonical degree among such

that
g_{0}^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}
is
semi-positive; consequently, we must have

\deg g_{0}^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}=d+1

for some integer

. (If the degree is
\geq n+2
, then Mori’s
Bend and Break technique produces a rational curve of smaller degree through every

, contradicting minimality.) In view of

, our goal
will be to show that

. As

is Fano, it is known that a very
general rational curve of minimal anticanonical degree deforms to cover
B
and moreover the pullback of the tangent
bundle decomposes as

(11)

g_{0}^{\ast}\mathscr{T}_{B}\cong\mathscr{O}(2)\oplus\mathscr{O}(1)^{\oplus(d-1)}\oplus\mathscr{O}^{\oplus(n-d)}.

By
2.4
, the restriction of the variation of Hodge structure

to

2(n-d)
.

We work with the (normalized) moduli space of rational curves
\RatCurves^{n}(B)
,
following Kollár’s notation
[
Kol96
, II.2.5]
. Let

be the open

that intersect

and belong to the same connected component as

. The universal family

, and we denote by

the evaluation morphism. We use the notation

.

For a point

and the obstructions for

. This group vanishes by

, and so

is smooth at such a point, of dimension

Moreover, as

is globally generated and by the

intersects

Remark 4.1
.

The rational curves in our family are free, and so a generic curve

is an immersion

, but not necessarily an
embedding. Of course, we will eventually show that

and that

the space of lines in

, but one has to be careful not to make any

Let us summarize the conclusions that we have drawn from

and
the deformation theory of rational curves.

Lemma 4.2

The moduli space

is smooth of dimension

, the universal curve

is a

, and the evaluation morphism

We denote by

the base change

, and by

the projection to the

, any rational curve of anticanonical

that meets

must be irreducible and therefore cannot

is proper.

Now the general idea is the following. Suppose that

, which means that the

on

has a nontrivial fixed part on a

, constructed from the Stein factorization of

, on which the pullback of

\mathcal{C}^{\circ}

Consider the pullback

of the variation of Hodge structure to

\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
, and its direct image
\pi_{*}^{\circ}V_{\mathcal{C}}
. By general

. Let

decomposes as in

; but this does not matter for the time being.)
By the semi-simplicity of polarized variations of Hodge structure, we get a canonical
splitting of rational variations of Hodge

and rank

, as we now explain.

, or rather its image in the moduli space

, parametrizes all those rational curves in our family that pass

. In the case of projective space, the space of lines through a given

7.
Proof of the main theorem

At this point, we can prove the main theorem very quickly by putting together the
results from the previous five sections.

Proof of
1.1
.

By way of contradiction, assume

is not
isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. By
1.2
, there must be a minimal degree rational

meeting

with

. By

, since

contains a copy of

, it follows that
g_{0}^{*}\mathscr{T}_{B}
has a trivial factor, so by

and

, we obtain a nontrivial
splitting of the variation of Hodge structure

. A general curve

meets the boundary transversely, and since

, it must meet every
irreducible component of
the boundary that has dimension

. By
6.1
it follows that

p\colon Z\to B
is unramified in codimension 1, and because

is normal by
construction,

must be an étale cover by purity of the branch locus

[
Nag59
]
. Since
B
is simply connected, it follows that

is an
isomorphism; this means that there is a nontrivial splitting of

itself, which
contradicts Voisin’s result in

.
∎

Remark 7.1
.

The fact that
M
was a full deformation space of a
family of rational curves admitting a splitting was crucial to the argument.
Indeed, consider
S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
an elliptic K3 surface, and
X=S^{[n]}\to B=\mathbb{P}^{n}
the Hilbert scheme of
n
points. Then taking the cover

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
obtained by quotienting
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}
by
S_{n-1}
, we
have a covering family of rational curves (namely the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
fibers) along which
the pullback of
V
has a fixed part (of rank

), and the cover

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
splits completely over the open part of these curves.
These curves are all tangent to the diagonal of

however, so this does not
imply
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
is unramified (and indeed it is not), and it
also does not imply that the splitting descends (and indeed it does not).

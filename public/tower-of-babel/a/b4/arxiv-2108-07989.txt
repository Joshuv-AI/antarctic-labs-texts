Asymptotic behavior of least energy solutions to the Finsler Lane-Emden problem with large exponents
Sadaf Habibi 1, Futoshi Takahashi 2

Abstract
. In this paper we are concerned with the least energy solutions to the Lane-Emden problem driven by an anisotropic operator, so-called the Finsler N N -Laplacian, on a bounded domain in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . We prove several asymptotic formulae as the nonlinear exponent gets large. Key words: Finsler Lane-Emden problem, Finsler Laplacian, Least energy solution 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J35, 35J60

1.
Introduction

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer.
In this paper, we study the following Lane-Emden problem driven by an anisotropic operator
Q_{N}
:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}&-Q_{N}u=u^{p}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,\\
&u>0\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,\\
&u=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

p>1
is any positive number, and
Q_{N}
is a quasilinear operator,
so-called
the Finsler
N
-Laplacian
, defined by

Q_{N}u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(H(\nabla u)^{N-1}H_{\xi_{i}}(\nabla u)\right).

Here
H\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
is any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
H_{\xi_{i}}(\xi)=\frac{\partial H(\xi)}{\partial\xi_{i}}
.
We assume that
H^{N}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\rm Hess}\left(H^{N}(\xi)\right)
is positive definite for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\xi\neq 0
.
Note that
Q_{N}u
can be written as

Q_{N}u={\rm div}\left(\nabla_{\xi}(\frac{1}{N}H(\xi)^{N})\Big|_{\xi=\nabla u}\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}(\nabla u)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},

where
a_{ij}(\nabla u)={\rm Hess}(\frac{1}{N}H^{N}(\xi))_{i,j}\Big|_{\xi=\nabla u}
.
If
H(\xi)=|\xi|
(the Euclidean norm),
then
Q_{N}u
coincides with the
N
-Laplacian
\Delta_{N}u={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{N-2}\nabla u)
of a function
u
.
In this case, the problem (
1.1
) was treated by Ren and Wei
[
21
]

[
22
]
when
N=2
,
and in
[
23
]
for general
N\geq 2
.
Ren and Wei
[
23
]
considered the least energy solution
u_{p}
of the following quasilinear problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&-\Delta_{N}u=u^{p}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,\\
&u>0\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,\\
&u=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
They studied the asymptotic behavior of
u_{p}
as the nonlinear exponent
p\to\infty
,
and proved that the least energy solutions remain bounded in
L^{\infty}
-norm regardless of
p
.
When the dimension
N=2
, they showed that the least energy solutions must develop one “peak” in the interior of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
that is, the shape of graph of
u_{p}
looks like a single spike as
p\to\infty
.
Moreover they showed that this peak point must be a critical point of the Robin function of the domain.
For other generalizations of this problem to various situations, see for example,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
.

Now, main aim of the paper is to extend the results of Ren and Wei
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
to the anisotropic problem (
1.1
).

As in
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
,
we restrict our attention to the least energy solutions to (
1.1
) constructed as follows:

Consider the constrained minimization problem:

(1.2)

C_{p}=\inf\{\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{N}dx:u\in W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}|u|^{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}dx=1\}.

Since the Sobolev imbedding
W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\Omega)
is compact for any
p>1
,
we have at least one minimizer
\underline{u}_{p}
for the problem (
1.2
), where

\underline{u}_{p}\in W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega),\|\underline{u}_{p}\|_{p+1}=1
.
As
|\underline{u}_{p}|\in W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega)
also achieves
C_{p}
, we may assume
\underline{u}_{p}>0
.
Note that
Q_{N}(cu)=c^{N-1}Q_{N}(u)
for a constant
c>0
.
Thus if we define

u_{p}=C_{p}^{\frac{1}{p+1-N}}\underline{u}_{p},

then
u_{p}
solves
(
1.1
)
and
C_{p}=\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{p})^{N}dx/(\int_{\Omega}|u_{p}|^{p+1}dx)^{\frac{N}{p+1}}
.
Standard regularity argument implies that any weak solution
u\in W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies

u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
We call
u_{p}
the
least energy solution
to (
1.1
).

Our first result is the following
L^{\infty}
-bound of least energy solutions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{p}
be a least energy solution to (
1.1
). Then there exist
C_{1},C_{2}
(independent of
p
),
such that

0<C_{1}\leq\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}<\infty

for
p
large enough.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to\infty}p^{N-1}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{p})^{N}dx=\lim_{p\to\infty}p^{N-1}\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{p+1}dx=\left(\frac{Ne\beta_{N}}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}

where
\beta_{N}=N(N\kappa_{N})^{\frac{1}{N-1}}
,
\kappa_{N}=|\mathcal{W}|
is the volume (with respect to the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure) of the unit Wulff ball associated with the dual norm
H^{0}
of
H
:

\mathcal{W}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:H^{0}(x)<1\}.

On the asymptotic behavior of the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
u_{p}
, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u_{p}
be a least energy solution to (
1.1
).
Then it holds that

1\leq\limsup_{p\to\infty}\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}.

To state further results, we need some definitions.
Set

(1.3)

v_{p}=\frac{u_{p}}{(\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{p}dx)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}.

Then
v_{p}
is a weak solution of

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-Q_{N}v_{p}=f_{p}(x)=\frac{u_{p}^{p}}{\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{p}dx}\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
v_{p}>0\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
v_{p}=0\quad\text{on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

For
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},X,Y\neq 0,X\neq Y
, we put

d(X,Y)=\frac{\left(H^{N-1}(X)(\nabla_{\xi}H)(X)-H^{N-1}(Y)(\nabla_{\xi}H)(Y)\right)\cdot(X-Y)}{H^{N}(X-Y)},

and

(1.5)

d_{N}=\inf\{d(X,Y)\ |\ X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},X,Y\neq 0,X\neq Y\},

where
X\cdot Y=\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}Y_{j}
denotes the usual inner product for
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
As in
[
32
]
Lemma 5.1, we can obtain the estimate

\min\{\frac{\lambda}{\beta^{N}},1\}\leq d_{N}\leq 1

where
\lambda
is the least eigenvalue of
{\rm Hess}\left(\frac{1}{N}H^{N}(\xi)\right)
, which is positive by the assumption (
2.2
)
and
\beta
is as in (
2.3
), see §
2
.
Also define

(1.6)

\displaystyle L_{0}=\limsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{p\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}{\left(\frac{N}{N-1}e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\right)},\quad L_{1}=d_{N}^{-\left(\frac{1}{N-1}\right)}L_{0}.

For a sequence
v_{p_{n}}
of
v_{p}
, we define the
blow-up set

S
of
\{v_{p_{n}}\}
as usual:

S=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:\exists\mbox{a subsequence}\;v_{p_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}},\exists\{x_{n}\}\subset\Omega\;\mbox{s.t.}\ x_{n}\to x\;\mbox{and}\;v_{p_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x_{n})\to\infty\}.

In the following,
\sharp A
denotes the cardinality of a set
A
and
[\cdot]
denotes the Gauss symbol.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a smooth bounded domain.
Then for any sequence
v_{p_{n}}
of
v_{p}
with
p_{n}\to\infty
, the blow-up set
S
of
v_{p_{n}}
is non-empty.
Also there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
v_{p_{n}}
) such that the estimate

\sharp(S\cap\Omega)\leq\left[\frac{e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}{d_{N}}\right]

holds true for this subsequence.

Assume
S\cap\Omega=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\}\subset\Omega
.
Then we have

(i)

f_{n}=\frac{u_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}}{\int_{\Omega}u_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}dx}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}

in the sense of Radon measures of
\Omega
,
where

\gamma_{i}\geq\left(\frac{\beta_{N}}{L_{1}}\right)^{N-1}

and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\leq 1
.

(ii)

v_{p_{n}}\to G
in
C^{1}_{loc}(\Omega\setminus(S\cap\Omega))
for some function
G
satisfying

\begin{cases}-Q_{N}G=0\quad&\text{in}\,\Omega\setminus(S\cap\Omega),\\
G=+\infty\quad&\text{on}\,S\cap\Omega,\\
G=0\quad&\text{on}\,\partial\Omega\setminus(\partial\Omega\cap S).\end{cases}

(iii)

\|u_{p_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for any compact set
K\subset\Omega\setminus(S\cap\Omega)
.

In
[
23
]
, Ren and Wei obtained an estimate of the number of interior blow-up set

\sharp(S\cap\Omega)\leq\left[\frac{1}{d_{N}}\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}\right]

when
H(\xi)=|\xi|
case.
Since
e^{x}<\frac{1}{1-x}
for
x\in(0,1)
, we check that
e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}<\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}
for all
N\geq 2
.
Thus the estimate in Theorem
1.3
is better than that in
[
23
]

even when
H(\xi)
coincides with the Euclidean norm
|\xi|
.
Also, Theorem
1.2
seems new even for
H(\xi)=|\xi|
and
N>2
case.

Finally, we prove that if the blow-up set consists of one point, it must be an interior point of
\Omega
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
\sharp S=1
and
S=\{x_{0}\}
,
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Then
x_{0}\in\Omega
must hold.

In §

In §

In §

In §

Finally in §

2.
Notations and basic properties

Let
H
be any
norm
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.,

H
is convex,
H(\xi)\geq 0
and
H(\xi)=0
if and only if
\xi=0
, and
H
satisfies

(2.1)

H(t\xi)=|t|H(\xi),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

By (
2.1
),
H
must be even:
H(-\xi)=H(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Throughout of the paper, we also assume that
H\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
,
H^{N}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and

(2.2)

{\rm Hess}\left(H^{N}(\xi)\right)
is positive definite for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\xi\neq 0
.

Since all norms on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
are equivalent to each other, we see the existence of positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

(2.3)

\alpha|\xi|\leq H(\xi)\leq\beta|\xi|,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The dual norm of
H
is the function
H^{0}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

H^{0}(x)=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\xi\cdot x}{H(\xi)}.

It is well-known that
H^{0}
is also a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and satisfies the inequality

\frac{1}{\beta}|x|\leq H^{0}(x)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}|x|,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The set

\mathcal{W}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,H^{0}(x)<1\}

is called the
Wulff ball
, or the
H^{0}
-unit ball, and we denote
\kappa_{N}=\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{W})
,
where
\mathcal{H}^{N}
denotes the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We also denote
\mathcal{W}_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,|\,H^{0}(x)<r\}
for any
r>0
.

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the
anisotropic
H
-perimeter
of a set
E
with respect to
\Omega

is defined as

P_{H}(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E\cap\Omega}{\rm div}\sigma dx\ :\ \sigma\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}),H^{0}(\sigma(x))\leq 1\right\}.

If
E
is Lipschitz, then it holds
P_{H}(E,\Omega)=\int_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}H(\nu)d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
,
where
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of the set
E
and
\nu(x)
is the measure theoretic outer unit normal of
\partial^{*}E
(see
[
16
]
).
Also we have
P_{H}(\mathcal{W},\mathbb{R}^{N})=N\kappa_{N}
.
For more explanation about the anisotropic perimeter, see
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
.

Here we just recall some properties of
H
and
H^{0}
.
These will be proven by using the homogeneity property of
H
and
H^{0}
,
see
[

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H
be a Finsler norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then the following properties hold true:

(1)

|\nabla_{\xi}H(\xi)|\leq C
for any
\xi\neq 0
.

(2)

\nabla_{\xi}H(\xi)\cdot\xi=H(\xi)
,
\nabla_{x}H(x)\cdot x=H(x)
for any
\xi\neq 0
,
x\neq 0
.

(3)

\left(\nabla_{\xi}H\right)(t\xi)=\frac{t}{|t|}\left(\nabla_{\xi}H\right)(\xi)
for any
\xi\neq 0
,
t\neq 0
.

(4)

H\left(\nabla H^{0}(x)\right)=1
.
H^{0}\left(\nabla_{\xi}H(\xi)\right)=1
.

(5)

H^{0}(x)\left(\nabla_{\xi}H\right)\left(\nabla_{x}H^{0}(x)\right)=x
.

Finally, given a smooth function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
the Finsler Laplace operator
of
u
(associated with
H
) is defined by

\displaystyle Qu(x)

\displaystyle={\rm div}\left(H(\nabla u(x))\left(\nabla_{\xi}H\right)(\nabla u(x))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(H(\xi)H_{\xi_{j}}(\xi)\Big|_{\xi=\nabla u(x)}\right)

and, more generally, for any
1<q<\infty
,
the Finsler
q
-Laplace operator

Q_{q}
by

Q_{q}u(x)=\mbox{div}\left(H^{q-1}(\nabla u(x))(\nabla_{\xi}H)(\nabla u(x))\right).

If we assume that
{\rm Hess}(H^{q}(\xi))
is positive definite on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
Q_{q}
becomes a uniformly elliptic operator locally.

We collect here several useful facts.

Theorem 2.2
.

(Finsler Trudinger-Moser inequality
[
32
]
)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
. Let
u\in W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfy
\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{N}dx\leq 1
.
Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on the dimension
N
such that

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\beta|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\right)dx\leq C|\Omega|

holds for any
\beta\leq\beta_{N}=N(N\kappa_{N})^{\frac{1}{N-1}}
.
Furthermore,
\beta_{N}
is optimal in the sense that there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{n})^{N}dx\leq 1
,
such that
\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\beta|u_{n}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\right)dx\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
for
\beta>\beta_{N}
.

Next is the unique existence of the Green function for the Finsler
p
-Laplacian.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
32
]
)

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain containing the origin.
Define
\Omega^{*}=\Omega\setminus\{0\}
and

\Gamma(x)=\begin{cases}&C(p,N)(H^{0}(x))^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}\quad\text{for}\quad 1<p<N,\\
&C(N)\log\frac{1}{H^{0}(x)}\quad\text{for}\quad p=N,\end{cases}

where
C(p,N)=\frac{p-N}{p-1}(N\kappa_{N})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
and
C(N)=(N\kappa_{N})^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}
.
Then there exists a unique function
G(\cdot,0)\in C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega^{*})
with
|\nabla G|\in L^{p-1}(\Omega)
,
G/\Gamma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-Q_{p}G(\cdot,0)=\delta_{0}&\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
G(\cdot,0)=0&\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Moreover,
g=G-\Gamma
satisfies
g\in C(\Omega)
and
\lim_{x\to 0}H^{0}(x)\nabla g(x)=0
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a smooth bounded domain.
Then the following statements are true.

(1)

Let
u\in W^{1,N}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of

-Q_{N}u=f
in
\Omega
, where
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
for some
q>1
.
Then for any subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime},q,N)>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}\right)

holds.

(2)

Let
u\in W^{1,N}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of
-Q_{N}u=f
in
\Omega
.
Suppose
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq a
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq b
for some
a,b<\infty
.
Then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\Omega)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)

and for any subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime},a,b,\alpha)>0
such that

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C

holds.
If, in addition,
u
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition
u=\phi
on
\partial\Omega
where
\phi\in C^{1,\beta}(\partial\Omega)
,
\beta\in(0,1)
,
then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
holds.

(3)

(Harnack inequality)

Let
u\in W^{1,N}(\Omega)
be a nonnegative weak solution of
-Q_{N}u=f
in
\Omega
.
Suppose
\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq b
for some
q>1
.
Then for any subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime},q,b)>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\Omega^{\prime}}u(x)\leq C\left(1+\inf_{x\in\Omega^{\prime}}u(x)\right)

holds.

Next is the result from
[

]
(Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2).

Theorem 2.5
.

(Finsler Brezis-Merle type inequality
[
33
]
)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
.

(1)

Suppose
u
is a weak solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-Q_{N}u=f(x)&\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
u=0&\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\beta_{N})
where
\beta_{N}=N(N\kappa_{N})^{\frac{1}{N-1}}
,
it holds that

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{(\beta_{N}-\varepsilon)|u(x)|}{\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}\right)dx\leq\frac{\beta_{N}}{\varepsilon}|\Omega|.

(2)

Suppose
u
and
v
are weak solutions to

-Q_{N}u=f(x)>0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega

and

-Q_{N}v=0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega\quad v=u\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,

respectively. Then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\beta_{N})
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{(\beta_{N}-\varepsilon)d_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}|u(x)-v(x)|}{\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}\right)dx\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{\varepsilon},

where

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx<+\infty
.
In fact, since
z_{p}\to z
in
C^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we obtain

1_{\Omega_{p}}(x)\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(x)}{p}\right)^{p}\to e^{z(x)}

pointwisely for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
1_{\Omega_{p}}
is the characteristic function of
\Omega_{p}
.
By using Fatou’s lemma and Hölder’s inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{p}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(x)}{p}\right)^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{p^{N-1}}{(u_{p}(x_{p}))^{N-1}}\int_{\Omega}(u_{p}(y))^{p}dy

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{p^{N-1}}{(u_{p}(x_{p}))^{N-1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u_{p}(y))^{p+1}dy\right)^{p/(p+1)}|\Omega|^{1/(p+1)}

\displaystyle\leq C<\infty

where we have used the facts that
\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{p+1}dy=\frac{O(1)}{p^{N-1}}
by Lemma
3.3
and
u_{p}(x_{p})\geq C_{1}>0
by Theorem
1.1
.
Hence, we check that the limit function satisfies

(5.4)

\begin{cases}&-Q_{N}z=e^{z}\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&z\leq 0,\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx<\infty.\end{cases}

In the case (ii), almost the same proof works, and we see that the limit function
z
is a solution of

(5.5)

\begin{cases}&-Q_{N}z=e^{z}\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0}),\\
&z\leq 0,\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0}),\\
&z=-\infty,\quad\text{on}\,\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0}),\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0})}e^{z}dx<\infty.\end{cases}

Now we prove the following lemma.
The case
N=2
was treated by Ding (see
[
7
]
) when
H(\xi)=|\xi|
, and by Wang and Xia
[
32
]
for general
H(\xi)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
z
is a
C^{1}
weak solution of (
5.4
), then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx\geq\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}N^{N}\kappa_{N}.

If
z
is a
C^{1}
weak solution of (
5.5
), then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0})}e^{z}dx\geq\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}N^{N}\kappa_{N}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we use a level set argument.
First, we assume
z
is a solution of (
5.4
).
Put

\Omega_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ :\ z(x)>t\},\quad\mu(t)=|\Omega_{t}|.

Integration by parts on
\Omega_{t}
leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}e^{z}dx=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}Q_{N}zdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}H^{N-1}(\nabla z)(\nabla_{\xi}H)(\nabla z)\cdot\frac{\nabla z}{|\nabla z|}ds_{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\frac{H^{N}(\nabla z)}{|\nabla z|}ds_{x}.

By the Finsler isoperimetric inequality (
4.6
) and Hölder’s inequality, we see

\displaystyle N\kappa_{N}^{1/N}|\Omega_{t}|^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq P_{H}(\Omega_{t},\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\frac{H(\nabla z)}{|\nabla z|}ds_{x}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\frac{H^{N}(\nabla z)}{|\nabla z|}ds_{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\frac{ds_{x}}{|\nabla z|}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}e^{z}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}},

here we have used coarea formula

\mu(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{\{x:z(x)=s\}}\frac{ds_{x}}{|\nabla z|}ds.

Thus we have

\mu(t)\leq\left\{\frac{1}{N\kappa_{N}^{1/N}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}e^{z}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\right\}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\max z}e^{t}\mu(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{N\kappa_{N}^{1/N}}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\max z}e^{t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}e^{z}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}(-\mu^{\prime}(t))dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{N\kappa_{N}^{1/N}}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\max z}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}e^{z}dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{N\kappa_{N}^{1/N}}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}},

which implies

\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}N^{N}\kappa_{N}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx.

The proof when
z
is a solution to (
5.5
) is similar,
since the boundary condition
z=-\infty
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0})
assures that all level sets of
z
are confined in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0})
.

By the change of variables, we have

(5.6)

p^{N-1}\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{p+1}(y)dy=u_{p}^{N}(x_{p})\int_{\Omega_{p}}\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(x)}{p}\right)^{p+1}dx.

Let us take
\limsup_{p\to\infty}
of both sides of (
5.6
).
Then we see

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\text{LHS of }\eqref{change}=\left(\frac{Ne}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}N^{N}\kappa_{N}

by Lemma
3.3
.
On the other hand, Fatou’s lemma and Lemma
5.1
implies

\displaystyle\limsup_{p\to\infty}\text{RHS of }\eqref{change}

\displaystyle\geq(\limsup_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{p}))^{N}\times\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{z}dx\quad&\text{when case (i)}\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0})}e^{z}dx\quad&\text{when case (ii)}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq(\limsup_{p\to\infty}u_{p}(x_{p}))^{N}\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}N^{N}\kappa_{N}.

Hence, we have

e^{N-1}\geq(\limsup_{p\to\infty}\|u_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})^{N}.

which implies Theorem
1.2

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem

.
Given any sequence

of
p

,
u_{n}=u_{p_{n}}
.

\displaystyle v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}=\frac{u_{n}}{(\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p_{n}}dx)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}},\quad\lambda_{n}=\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p_{n}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}},

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=\frac{u_{n}^{p_{n}}}{\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p_{n}}dx},

\displaystyle L_{0}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p_{n}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}{\left(\frac{N}{N-1}e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\right)},\quad L_{1}=d_{N}^{-\left(\frac{1}{N-1}\right)}L_{0}.

First, we prove

for any sequence
v_{n}=v_{p_{n}}
of

.
Indeed, by Theorem
1.1
, we have
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq C_{1}>0

.
Let
x_{n}\in\Omega
be a point such that
u_{n}(x_{n})=\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

.

.

Next, as in
[
6

[

]

[

where
\beta_{N}=N(N\kappa_{N})^{\frac{1}{N-1}}

We put

\displaystyle R_{L}(\delta)=\{x_{0}\in\Omega:x_{0}\;\mbox{is a}\;(L,\delta)\text{-regular point}\},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{L}(\delta)=\Omega\setminus R_{L}(\delta).

We call a point in

-irregular point

.
Note that
(L,\delta)
-regular, or
(L,\delta)
-irregular points are automatically interior points of
\Omega
.
Also note that if
x_{0}\in\Sigma_{L}(\delta)
, then we have

(6.1)

\mu(\{x_{0}\})\geq\left(\frac{\beta_{N}}{L+3\delta}\right)^{N-1}.

Since

), we see that

and

.

Next Lemma is the key to analyze the interior blow-up set

.

Lemma 6.1
.

(smallness of
\mu
implies boundedness)

Let
x_{0}
be a
(L_{1},\delta)
-regular point of a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
where
L_{1}
is defined in (
1.6
).
Then
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}))
for some
R_{0}>0
.

Proof.

Key point in the proof is to get the following pointwise estimate

(6.2)

f_{n}(x)<\exp\left((L_{1}+\delta/2)d_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}v_{n}(x)\right),\quad x\in\Omega.

In checking (
6.2
), we use the elementary inequality

(6.3)

\frac{\log x}{x}\leq\frac{\log y}{y}\quad\text{for any}\;0<x\leq y\leq e.

Let

\alpha_{n}=\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p_{n}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}}=\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}},

and recall that
\lambda_{n}=O\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}}\right)
by Corollary
4.1
, so
\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}=O\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.
Thus we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}

by Theorem
1.2
.
From this, we see that for any small
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
,

\frac{u_{n}(x)}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}}\leq\alpha_{n}\leq e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}<e

holds for any
x\in\Omega
and for large
n
.
Therefore by (
6.3
), we have for fixed small
\varepsilon>0

\frac{\log\left(\frac{u_{n}(x)}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}}\right)}{\frac{u_{n}(x)}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}}}\leq\frac{\log\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\leq\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)\frac{1}{e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}+\varepsilon

for large
n
.
Hence

\displaystyle\log f_{n}(x)=p_{n}\log\frac{u_{n}(x)}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}}

\displaystyle\leq p_{n}\left(\frac{u_{n}(x)}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}}\right)\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}+\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=p_{n}\lambda_{n}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}e^{-\frac{N-1}{N}}+\varepsilon\right)\frac{v_{n}(x)}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{N}{N-1}e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}L_{1}d_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}+\varepsilon\right)\left(\frac{N-1}{N}e^{-\frac{N-1}{N}}+2\varepsilon\right)v_{n}(x),

here we have used
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p_{n}}}=1
and

p_{n}\lambda_{n}\leq\frac{N}{N-1}e^{\frac{N-1}{N}}L_{1}d_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}+\varepsilon

for large
n
by the definition of
L_{1}
.
Therefore

\log f_{n}(x)\leq\left((L_{1}+\delta/2)d_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\right)v_{n}(x)

holds if we choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough.
This proves the pointwise estimate (
6.2
).

Next, by the use of Brezis-Merle theory for the Finsler
N
-Laplacian, we obtain the integral estimate

(6.4)

\int_{B_{R_{1}/2}(x_{0})}\exp\left((L_{1}+\delta)d_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}v_{n}(x)\right)dx\leq C

for some
R_{1}>0
small and

independent of
n
, here
x_{0}
is a
(L_{1},\delta)
-regular point.

Indeed, by the definition of
(L_{1},\delta)
-regular point,
we can find

such that

\int_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})}f_{n}dx\leq\left(\frac{\beta_{N}}{L_{1}+2\delta}\right)^{N-1}.

Also by Theorem
2.5
(i) and the fact that
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=1
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left((\beta_{N}-\varepsilon)v_{n}(x)\right)dx\leq\frac{\beta_{N}}{\varepsilon}|\Omega|

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\beta_{N})
.
From this, we obtain

(6.5)

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}\leq C

where

is independent of
n
.
Next, let
\phi_{n}
be a weak solution of

-Q_{N}\phi_{n}=0\quad\text{in}\,B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})\quad\phi_{n}=v_{n}\quad\text{on}\,\partial B_{R_{1}}(x_{0}).

Then by Theorem
2.5

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R_{1}}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}<\frac{\beta_{N}}{L_{1}+2\delta}
,
we have

(6.6)

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left((L_{1}+\delta)d_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}|v_{n}(x)-\phi_{n}(x)|\right)dx\leq C

if we choose

[
33

Theorem 3.2) and Serrin’s estimates Theorem

\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}/2}(x_{0}))}\leq\|v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}/2}(x_{0}))}\leq C\|v_{n}\|_{L^{N}(B_{R_{1}}(x_{0}))}\leq C

Combining this with (

), we obtain the desired integral estimate (

).

Comparing (

) and (

where

.
Therefore, Serrin’s regularity estimate Theorem

(i) again implies that

.

If

Finally, since

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem

.

Proof.

Assume the contrary that

,
where

with

For

on

(7.1)

\displaystyle\frac{N}{p_{n}+1}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}dx=\int_{\partial(\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}))}\frac{u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}}{p_{n}+1}(x-y)\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int_{\partial(\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}))}H^{N}(\nabla u_{n})(x-y)\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial(\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}))}\left(H^{N-1}(\nabla u_{n})(\nabla_{\xi}H)(\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nu(x)\right)(x-y)\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}.

In order to remove the integral terms involving

[

Define

\rho_{n}=\frac{\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}H^{N}(\nabla u_{n})(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}}{\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}H^{N}(\nabla u_{n})\nu(x_{0})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}}

and put

We assume

so small such that

for

Then we have that
\rho_{n}\leq 2R
.
By the definition of

and
\rho_{n}

\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}H^{N}(\nabla u_{n})(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}\equiv 0

for all

.
Also since

on
\partial\Omega
and
u_{n}>0
in
\Omega
, we see

.
By using these, we see (
7.1
) with

becomes

(7.2)

\displaystyle\frac{N}{p_{n}+1}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}dx=\frac{1}{p_{n}+1}\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}H^{N}(\nabla u_{n})(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))}\left(H^{N-1}(\nabla u_{n})(\nabla_{\xi}H)(\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nu(x)\right)(x-y_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}(x)ds_{x}.

Multiplying
(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}})^{N}
to both sides of (
7.2
) and recalling
v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}
, we have

(7.3)

\displaystyle\frac{N}{p_{n}+1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\right)^{N}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{n}+1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\right)^{N}\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}H^{N}(\nabla v_{n})(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))}\left(H^{N-1}(\nabla v_{n})(\nabla_{\xi}H)(\nabla v_{n})\cdot\nu(x)\right)(x-y_{n})\cdot\nabla v_{n}(x)ds_{x}

\displaystyle=I+II+III.

We estimate the terms
I,II,III
on the right-hand side of (
7.3
) as follows:

\displaystyle|I|=\frac{1}{p_{n}+1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\right)^{N}\big|\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{O(p_{n}^{N})}{p_{n}^{N-1}(p_{n}+1)}\|p_{n}^{N-1}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))}\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}|(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)|ds_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{O(p_{n}^{N})}{p_{n}^{N-1}(p_{n}+1)}\|p_{n}^{N-1}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))}O(R^{N-1}).

We note that since
S\cap\Omega=\phi
by assumption,

f_{n}=\frac{u_{n}^{p_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}^{N-1}}\to 0

uniformly on compact sets in
\Omega
and

p_{n}^{N-1}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}(x)\leq\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}p_{n}^{N-1}u_{n}^{p_{n}}(x)\leq C\frac{u_{n}^{p_{n}}(x)}{\lambda_{n}^{N-1}}\leq Cf_{n}(x)

by Theorem
1.1
and the fact that
\lambda_{n}=O(\frac{1}{p_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
.
Thus we have

\|p_{n}^{N-1}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty

and thus

\lim_{R\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}|I|=0.

Also, by Theorem
1.3
(ii), we have

v_{n}\to G
in
C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\Omega\setminus(S\cap\Omega))
.
Thus we have
H^{N}(\nabla v_{n})=O(1)
on
\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
, which implies

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Big|\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}H^{N}(\nabla v_{n})(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)ds_{x}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}|(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)|ds_{x}\leq O(1)O(R^{N-1}),

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))}\left(H^{N-1}(\nabla v_{n})(\nabla_{\xi}H)(\nabla v_{n})\cdot\nu(x)\right)(x-y_{n})\cdot\nabla v_{n}(x)ds_{x}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}|(x-y_{n})\cdot\nu(x)|ds_{x}\leq O(1)O(R^{N-1}).

Therefore we have

\lim_{R\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}|II|=\lim_{R\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}|III|=0.

From these, we obtain

(7.4)

\lim_{R\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}(\text{RHS of \eqref{local_Pohozaev3}})=0.

On the other hand,
recall

\displaystyle z_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{n}}{u_{n}(x_{n})}\left(u_{n}(\varepsilon_{n}x+x_{n})-u_{n}(x_{n})\right),

\displaystyle x\in\Omega_{R,n}=\frac{(\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}))-x_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}},

where
\varepsilon_{n}^{N}p_{n}^{N-1}u_{n}(x_{n})^{p_{n}+1-N}\equiv 1
.
Then we see from Fatou’s lemma, Theorem
1.1
, and Lemma
5.1
, that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}))}p_{n}^{N-1}u_{n}^{p_{n}+1}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(x_{n})^{N}\int_{\Omega_{R,n}}\left(1+\frac{z_{n}(x)}{p_{n}}\right)^{p_{n}+1}dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}^{N}\int_{U}e^{z}dx\geq C_{1}^{N}\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}N^{N}\kappa_{N}

where
u=\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}(s_{0})
for some
s_{0}>0
according to the cases

\frac{{\rm dist}(x_{n},\partial\Omega_{R,n})}{\varepsilon_{n}}\to+\infty
or

\frac{{\rm dist}(x_{n},\partial\Omega_{R,n})}{\varepsilon_{n}}\to s_{0}
.
Note that our assumption
\sharp S=1
assures that we can choose
x_{n}
as a maximum points of
u_{n}
.
From this and the fact that
\lambda_{n}=O(\frac{1}{p_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
, we have

(7.5)

\lim_{R\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}(\text{LHS of \eqref{local_Pohozaev3}})\geq C>0

for some positive constant
C>0
independent of
n
.

Clearly (
7.5
) contradicts to (
7.4
), and we conclude that
x_{0}\not\in\partial\Omega
.

Finally, as a corollary, we prove the following.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
R>0
and let
\{u_{p}\}
be a sequence of least energy solutions to

(7.6)

\begin{cases}&-Q_{N}u_{p}=u_{p}^{p}\quad\mbox{in}\;\mathcal{W}_{R},\\
&u_{p}>0\quad\mbox{in}\;\mathcal{W}_{R},\\
&u_{p}=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{W}_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,H^{0}(x)<R\}
.
Then the blow-up set
S
of
v_{p}
satisfies
S\cap\mathcal{W}_{R}=\{0\}
, and

\displaystyle u_{p}\to G(\cdot,0)\quad\text{in }C^{1}_{loc}(\mathcal{W}_{R}\setminus\{0\})

where
G
is the unique Green function on
\mathcal{W}_{R}
obtained in Theorem
2.3
, and

\displaystyle f_{p}=\frac{u_{p}^{p}}{\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}u_{p}^{p}dx}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\delta_{0}

in the sense of Radon measures on
\mathcal{W}_{R}
, along the full sequence.

Proof.

The usual method of moving plane to prove the symmetry of solutions is not applicable in the anisotropic situation.
However, we can use Theorem 4.1 in
[
5
]
under the convexity and
C^{1}
-assumption of the map
\xi\mapsto H^{N}(\xi)
.
(Note that the key point of the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
5
]
is the Pohozaev identity Theorem 4.2 in
[
5
]
for
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
-weak solutions,
which is valid by the above assumptions).
Thus we assure that any positive solution
u_{p}
to (
7.6
) is
Finsler-radial
,
that is, all level sets of
u_{p}
are homothetic to
\mathcal{W}_{R}
for any
p>1
.
Let
S
be the blow-up set of
v_{p}
. Then we see that
S\cap\mathcal{W}_{R}=\{0\}
.
Indeed, if there were a point
x_{0}\in S\cap\mathcal{W}_{R}
, then all points on the level set of
u_{p}
passing through
x_{0}
must be blow-up points of
v_{p}
,
which contradicts to the fact that
\sharp(S\cap\mathcal{W}_{R})
is finite.
Thus by Theorem
1.3
, we see

\displaystyle v_{p}\to G(\cdot,0)\quad\text{in }C^{1}_{loc}(\mathcal{W}_{R}\setminus\{0\})

for some function
G
along a subsequence.
The limit function must be the unique Green function constructed in Theorem
2.3
,
and by the uniqueness, the convergence is true for the full sequence.

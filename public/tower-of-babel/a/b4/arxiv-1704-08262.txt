Soft Functions for Generic Jet Algorithms and Observables at Hadron Colliders Preprint: DESY 16-137 MIT–CTP 4823 NIKHEF 2016-035 August 9, 2026 – 22:29
Daniele Bertolini, Daniel Kolodrubetz, Duff Neill, Piotr Pietrulewicz, Iain W. Stewart, Frank J. Tackmann, and Wouter J. Waalewijn

Abstract
We introduce a method to compute one-loop soft functions for exclusive N N -jet processes at hadron colliders, allowing for different definitions of the algorithm that determines the jet regions and of the measurements in those regions. In particular, we generalize the N N -jettiness hemisphere decomposition of ref. Jouttenus:2011wh in a manner that separates the dependence on the jet boundary from the observables measured inside the jet and beam regions. Results are given for several factorizable jet definitions, including anti- k T k_{T} , XCone, and other geometric partitionings. We calculate explicitly the soft functions for angularity measurements, including jet mass and jet broadening, in p ​ p → L + 1 pp\to L+1 jet and explore the differences for various jet vetoes and algorithms. This includes a consistent treatment of rapidity divergences when applicable. We also compute analytic results for these soft functions in an expansion for a small jet radius R R . We find that the small- R R results, including corrections up to 𝒪 ⁡ ( R 2 ) \mathcal{O}(R^{2}) , accurately capture the full behavior over a large range of R R .

Introduction

Exclusive jet processes, i.e. those with a fixed number of hard signal jets in the final state, play a crucial role in the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) physics program. Many important processes, such as Higgs or
W/Z
boson production or diboson production, are measured in different exclusive jet bins. Furthermore, jet substructure techniques have become increasingly important both in Standard Model and in new physics analyses, and the associated observables often exploit the properties of a fixed number of subjets. Theoretical predictions at increasingly high precision are needed to match the increasing precision of the data. Compared to color-singlet final states, the presence of jets makes perturbative QCD calculations more challenging and the singularity structure more complicated. Furthermore, a fixed number of jets is imposed through a jet veto, which restricts the phase space for additional collinear and soft emissions, and generates large logarithms that often need to be resummed to obtain predictions with the best possible precision.

Soft Collinear Effective Theory (SCET)
Bauer:2000ew
;
Bauer:2000yr
;
Bauer:2001ct
;
Bauer:2001yt
provides a framework to systematically carry out the resummation of logarithms to higher orders by factorizing the cross section into hard, collinear, and soft functions, and then exploiting their renormalization group evolution. Schematically, the cross section for
pp\to N\text{ jets}
factorizes for many observables in the singular limit as

\displaystyle\sigma_{N}=H_{N}\times\left[B_{a}B_{b}\prod_{i=1}^{N}J_{i}\right]\otimes S_{N},

(1)

where the hard function
H_{N}
contains the virtual corrections to the partonic hard scattering process, the beam functions
B_{a,b}
contain parton distribution functions and describe collinear initial-state radiation. The jet functions
J_{i}
describe final-state radiation collinear to the direction of the hard partons, and the soft function
S_{N}
describes wide-angle soft radiation. The resummation of large logarithms is achieved by evaluating each component at its natural scale and then renormalization-group evolving all components to a common scale. For an interesting class of observables, the jet and beam functions are of the inclusive type and do not depend on the precise definition of the jet regions. They are known for a variety of jet and beam measurements, typically at one loop or
beyond
Bauer:2003pi
;
Fleming:2003gt
;
Becher:2006qw
;
Becher:2009th
;
Becher:2010pd
;
Larkoski:2014uqa
;
Stewart:2010qs
;
Berger:2010xi
;
Becher:2010tm
;
Becher:2012qa
;
Chiu:2012ir
;
Gehrmann:2014yya
;
Gaunt:2014xga
;
Gaunt:2014cfa
;
Luebbert:2016itl
. Hard functions are also known for many processes at one loop or beyond (see e.g. ref.
Moult:2015aoa
and references therein). In this paper, we focus on determining the soft functions that appear for a wide class of jet algorithms and jet measurements. The resummation at NLL
′
and NNLL requires the soft function at one loop. Compared to the beam and jet functions, the perturbative calculation of the soft function generally requires a more sophisticated setup, since it depends not only on the measurements made in the jet and beam regions, but also on the angles between all jet and beam directions and the precise definition of the jet boundaries.

N
-jettiness
Stewart:2010tn
is a global event shape that allows one to define exclusive
N
-jet cross sections in a manner that is particularly suitable for higher-order analytic resummation. The calculation of the one-loop soft function for exclusive
N
-jet processes using

-jettiness has been carried out for arbitrary

Jouttenus:2011wh

-jettiness is used both as the algorithm to partition the phase space into jet and beam regions and as the measurement performed on those regions. To simplify the calculation, the version of

-jettiness used in ref.

\displaystyle\text{thrust-like $N$-jettiness:}\qquad{\mathcal{T}}_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\min_{m}\left\{\frac{2q_{m}\cdot p_{i}}{Q_{m}}\right\}=\sum_{i}\min_{m}\left\{\frac{n_{m}\cdot p_{i}}{\rho_{m}}\right\}\,.

(2)

This is essentially a generalization of beam thrust
Stewart:2009yx
to the case of
N
jets. In eq. (
2

of all particles that are part of the hadronic final state, and the minimization over

N

or lightlike vectors

is the jet energy. The directions

N

2

This definition of
N
-jettiness depends only on the choices of jet directions

and measure factors

For the cross section with a measurement of

, the

;

N
-jettiness can also be used more generally as a means of defining an exclusive jet algorithm, which partitions the particles in an event into a beam region and a fixed number of

Stewart:2010tn
;

(3)

{\mathcal{T}}_{N}(\{\hat{n}_{m}\})=\sum_{i}p_{Ti}\min\bigl\{d_{1}(p_{i}),\ldots,d_{N}(p_{i}),d_{a}(p_{i}),d_{b}(p_{i})\bigr\}\,.

Different choices of the

(5)

Given a common beam region with a single beam measure

Constructing a full jet algorithm requires in addition to the partitioning an infrared-safe method to determine the jet axes
\hat{n}_{m}
. This could be done by simply taking the directions of the
N

N
-jettiness based jet algorithm, the axes can be obtained by minimizing
N
-jettiness itself over all possible axes,

{\mathcal{T}}_{N}=\min_{\hat{n}_{1},\ldots,\hat{n}_{N}}{\mathcal{T}}_{N}(\{\hat{n}_{m}\})\,,

(6)

Thaler:2011gf
;
Stewart:2015waa

For the calculations in this paper, we consider a very general set of distance measures for determining the partitioning into jet and beam regions as in eq. (
4

different

2.2
. For the measured observables we consider the generic version of
N
-jettiness variables,
{\mathcal{T}}^{(m)}
, given by

{\mathcal{T}}^{(m)}=\sum_{i\in\,{\rm region}\,m}f_{m}(\eta_{i},\phi_{i})\,p_{Ti}\,.

(7)

Here,
\eta_{i}
,
\phi_{i}
, and
p_{Ti}
denote the pseudorapidity, azimuthal angle, and transverse momentum
of particle

2.1
. When considering a single beam region we have a common beam measurement
{\mathcal{T}}^{(0)}={\mathcal{T}}^{(a)}+{\mathcal{T}}^{(b)}
. Earlier analytic calculations of
N
-jettiness cross sections have all been done for the case where the observable and partitioning measure coincide,

, in which case the total
N

{\mathcal{T}}_{N}=\sum_{m}{\mathcal{T}}^{(m)}

The exact definition of the axes
\hat{n}_{m}
is irrelevant for the calculation of the soft function. For our purposes we can therefore separate the jet-axes finding from the partitioning and measurement, and we will assume predetermined axes obtained from a suitable algorithm. However, one should make sure to use recoil-free axes

In this paper, we determine factorization theorems, which describe the singular perturbative contributions in the
{\mathcal{T}}_{N}\to 0
limit for these generic versions of

-jettiness. We then establish a generalized hemisphere decomposition for computing the corresponding one-loop soft function. We carry out the computations explicitly for a number of interesting cases.
As underlying hard process we consider color-singlet plus jet production, and we discuss results for generic angularities as jet measurements. For the beam measurement we discuss different types of jet vetoes, including beam thrust, beam
C

(8)

Here

The associated jet and beam axes are normalized lightlike directions, and are given in terms of these coordinates by

The

{\mathcal{T}}^{(m)}(p_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{\mu}_{i}\to E_{i}n^{\mu}_{m}}}{{\longrightarrow}}c_{m}\,\bigl(n_{m}\cdot p_{i}\bigr)^{\frac{\beta_{m}}{2}}\bigl(\bar{n}_{m}\cdot p_{i}\bigr)^{1-\frac{\beta_{m}}{2}}\,,

Defining

\displaystyle f_{a}(\eta_{i},\phi_{i})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{i}\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}c_{a}\,e^{(1-\gamma)\eta_{i}}\,,\qquad f_{b}(\eta_{i},\phi_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{i}\to-\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}c_{b}\,e^{-(1-\gamma)\eta_{i}}\,,

\displaystyle f_{m\geq 1}(\eta_{i},\phi_{i})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\eta_{i},\phi_{i})\to(\eta_{m},\phi_{m})}}{{\longrightarrow}}c_{m}\,(2\cosh\eta_{m})^{1-\beta_{m}}\Bigl[(\eta_{i}-\eta_{m})^{2}+(\phi_{i}-\phi_{m})^{2}\Bigr]^{\frac{\beta_{m}}{2}}\,.

We will discuss several examples in secs.
3

4

Jet algorithms

Given a set of jet and beam axes
\{n_{m}\}
, the partitioning of the phase space into jet and beam regions
is determined by the distance measures
d_{m}(p_{i})

3

m

d_{m}(p_{i})<d_{j}(p_{i})

For

R
and the jet transverse momentum
p_{T,m}^{J}

2.3

and for well-separated jets and beams and sufficiently large jet radii, the differential cross section in the

m

and do not play a role for the partitioning of the event. Furthermore, the recoil on the location of the jet axes due to soft emissions is power suppressed for the description of the soft dynamics.

2.3
Factorization for different observable choices

In this section we display the form of the factorized cross section for

denotes a recoiling color-singlet state, with generic observables in the limit

{\rm SCET}_{\rm I}
-type and
{\rm SCET}_{\rm II}
-type cases. For notational simplicity we assume that the same observable is measured in each jet region (which asymptotically behaves like eq. (

We will mainly focus on the properties of the relevant soft function, which also
encodes all dependence of the singular cross section on the distance measure used for the partitioning.

The scaling of the modes in the effective theory follows in general from the constraints on radiation imposed by the
N

{\mathcal{T}}^{(m)}
in eq. (

The parametric scaling of the collinear and soft modes is then given by

\displaystyle n_{a,b}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle p_{n_{a,b}}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim p_{T}^{J}\,(\lambda^{\frac{4}{\gamma}},1,\lambda^{\frac{2}{\gamma}})_{n_{a,b}}\,,

\displaystyle n_{m\geq 1}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\qquad p_{n_{m}}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim p_{T}^{J}\,(\lambda^{\frac{4}{\beta}},1,\lambda^{\frac{2}{\beta}})_{n_{m}}\,,

soft:

\displaystyle\quad\times\theta\Bigl(n_{a}\cdot p-\frac{n_{J}\cdot p}{\rho_{J}^{R}}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle F^{B}_{aJ}(\{k_{l}\},\{d_{n}\},\eta_{J},p)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\delta\bigl(k_{B}-p_{T}f_{B}(\eta)\bigr)\,\delta(k_{J})-\delta(k_{B})\,\delta\bigl(k_{J}-p_{T}f_{J}(\eta,\phi)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\times\theta\bigl(d_{J}(\eta,\phi)-d_{B}(\eta)\bigr)\,\theta\Bigl(n_{a}\cdot p-\frac{n_{J}\cdot p}{\rho_{J}^{R}}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle F^{J}_{aJ}(\{k_{l}\},\{d_{n}\},\eta_{J},p)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\delta(k_{B})\,\delta\bigl(k_{J}-p_{T}f_{J}(\eta,\phi)\bigr)-\delta\bigl(k_{B}-p_{T}f_{B}(\eta)\bigr)\,\delta(k_{J})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\times\theta\bigl(d_{B}(\eta)-d_{J}(\eta,\phi)\bigr)\,\theta\Bigl(\frac{n_{J}\cdot p}{\rho_{J}^{R}}-n_{a}\cdot p\Bigr)\,.

(70)

Here the expanded measurement of the beam region observable in the jet region is denoted by
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}}}_{B}=p_{T}\tilde{f}_{B}(\eta-\eta_{J})
with

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{B}(\eta-\eta_{J})\equiv f_{B}(\eta_{J})\,e^{\eta_{J}-\eta}=\frac{n_{a}\cdot p}{p_{T}}\,f_{B}(\eta_{J})\,e^{\eta_{J}}\,.

(71)

\displaystyle S_{aJ}(\{k_{l}\},\{d_{n}\},\eta_{J},p)

\displaystyle=S_{aJ}^{\text{whole}}(\{k_{l}\},\eta_{J},p)-\tilde{S}_{J<a}^{B}(\{k_{l}\},\eta_{J})+S_{J<a}(\{k_{l}\},R,\eta_{J})

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta S_{J<a}^{B}(\{k_{l}\},R,\eta_{J})+\sum_{m=J,B}S_{aJ}^{m}(\{k_{l}\},\{d_{n}\},\eta_{J})\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\,\Delta I^{B}_{1,aJ}(f_{B},R,\eta_{J})\,\delta(k_{B})\,\delta(k_{J})\,,

with

\displaystyle\Delta I^{B}_{1,aJ}(f_{B},R,\eta_{J})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\eta\,\frac{e^{\eta-\eta_{J}}}{\cosh(\eta-\eta_{J})-\cos\phi}\,\ln\biggl(\frac{e^{\eta_{J}}f_{B}(\eta_{J})}{e^{\eta}f_{B}(\eta)}\biggr)\,\theta\Bigl(n_{a}\cdot p-\frac{n_{J}\cdot p}{\rho_{J}^{R}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\theta(R-1)\biggl[\int^{R-1}_{0}\mathrm{d}x\,h_{1}(f_{B},\eta_{J},x)+\int^{R+1}_{R-1}\mathrm{d}x\,h_{2}(f_{B},R,\eta_{J},x)\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\theta(1-R)\int^{R+1}_{1-R}\mathrm{d}x\,h_{2}(f_{B},R,\eta_{J},x)\,,

(79)

where we have defined the integration variable

\displaystyle h_{1}(f_{B},\eta_{J},x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2x}{\lvert x^{2}-1\rvert}\,\ln\biggl(\frac{f_{B}(\eta_{J})}{xf_{B}(\eta_{J}+\ln x)}\biggr)\,,

\displaystyle h_{2}(f_{B},R,\eta_{J},x)

\displaystyle=\biggl[1-\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\biggl(\frac{|x-1|}{x+1}\sqrt{\frac{(1+x)^{2}-R^{2}}{R^{2}-(x-1)^{2}}}\biggr)\biggr]\,h_{1}(f_{B},\eta_{J},x)\,.

(80)

\eta\to\eta_{J}
and

\mathcal{O}(R^{2})

5
Conclusions

In this paper we worked out a general setup to calculate one-loop soft functions for exclusive
N
-jet processes at hadron colliders. This method applies to any jet algorithm that satisfies soft-collinear factorization, and for generic infrared- and collinear safe jet measurements and jet vetoes, as long as they reduce to an angularity in the limit where they approach the jet/beam axis. The soft function is calculated using a hemisphere decomposition of the phase space, extending the approach that was used in ref.
Jouttenus:2011wh
to calculate the
N
-jettiness soft function. The divergences are extracted analytically, such that numerical computations only arise for the finite terms.

We also demonstrated how the method works in practice, providing explicit expressions for single jet production
pp\to L+1\text{ jet}
for several cases: angularities as jet measurements, beam thrust,
C
-parameter, and transverse momentum as jet vetoes, and anti-
k_{T}
and XCone as jet algorithms. We optimized our method by expanding the finite corrections in the jet radius
R
, obtaining a fully analytical result in the limit
R\ll 1
. It turns out that the remaining (numerical) contributions are rather small, even for relatively large values of
R
, thus improving the stability.

With the soft functions discussed in this paper, one can calculate resummed cross-section at NNLL or NLL
′
accuracy for exclusive jet processes at the LHC. This same soft function also enters in jet substructure calculations, see e.g. the 2-jettiness calculation of ref.
Feige:2012vc
, and the subtraction techniques could prove useful for other jet substructure calculations as found in ref.
Larkoski:2015kga
.

A.1
Hemisphere soft function correction

We perform the calculation of the jet hemisphere correction for the boost-invariant angularities defined in eq. (
58

\displaystyle S_{J<a}(\{k_{l}\},\rho,\eta_{J})\!=-2\Bigl(\frac{\mu^{2}e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{\epsilon}\!g^{2}\!\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{n_{a}\cdot n_{J}}{(n_{a}\cdot p)(n_{J}\cdot p)}\,2\pi\delta(p^{2})\theta(p^{0})\,F_{J<a}(\{k_{m}\},\rho,\eta_{J},p),

(93)

with the size of the hemisphere adjusted by the parameter
\rho
and the measurement given by

\displaystyle F_{J<a}(\{k_{m}\},\rho,\eta_{J},p)=\delta\bigl(k_{J}-p_{T}\mathcal{R}_{J}^{\beta}\bigr)\,\theta\Bigl(n_{a}\cdot p-\frac{n_{J}\cdot p}{\rho}\Bigr)\,\delta(k_{B})\,,

(94)

in analogy to eq. (
4.2

Here
\mathcal{R}_{J}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{sJ}
denotes the distance of the soft emission with momentum
p^{\mu}
with respect to the jet direction in azimuth-rapidity space as defined in eq. (
2.2
).
Let us define the momentum projection
p_{k}
along a generic light-like direction
n_{k}
and the angular distance between two light-like directions
\hat{s}_{ij}
as

\begin{split}p_{k}&\equiv n_{k}\cdot p\,,\qquad\hat{s}_{ij}\equiv\frac{n_{i}\cdot n_{j}}{2}=\frac{1-\cos\theta_{ij}}{2}\,.\end{split}

(95)

For any
ij
-dipole, the gluon four-momentum can be decomposed as

p^{\mu}=\frac{p_{i}}{2\hat{s}_{ij}}n_{j}^{\mu}+\frac{p_{j}}{2\hat{s}_{ij}}n_{i}^{\mu}+p_{\perp_{ij}}^{\mu}\,,

(96)

with the integration measure given by

\mathrm{d}^{4-2\epsilon}p=\frac{p_{\perp_{ij}}^{1-2\epsilon}}{2\hat{s}_{ij}}\,\mathrm{d}p_{i}\,\mathrm{d}p_{j}\,\mathrm{d}p_{\perp_{ij}}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega_{2-2\epsilon}\,.

(97)

The boost-invariant jet angularity can be expressed in this basis, by first writing

\displaystyle p_{T}\mathcal{R}_{J}^{\beta}=(2p_{j}\cosh\eta_{j})^{\beta/2}(p_{T})^{1-\beta/2},

(98)

and then substituting

\displaystyle p_{T}=\frac{p_{\perp_{ij}}\mathcal{G}(q,\phi)}{q}\quad{\rm with}\,\,q=\frac{p_{j}}{p_{\perp_{ij}}}\,.

(99)

The function
\mathcal{G}(q,\phi)
is given in general by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(q,\phi)=

\displaystyle\biggl(\hat{s}_{aj}+\frac{\hat{s}_{ai}}{\hat{s}_{ij}}\,q^{2}-2\sqrt{\frac{\hat{s}_{aj}\hat{s}_{ai}}{\hat{s}_{ij}}}\,q\cos\phi\,\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\hat{s}_{bj}+\frac{\hat{s}_{bi}}{\hat{s}_{ij}}\,q^{2}-2\sqrt{\frac{\hat{s}_{bj}\hat{s}_{bi}}{\hat{s}_{ij}}}\,q\cos(\phi-\Delta\phi_{ij})\,\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(100)

Here
\phi
is the azimuthal angle in the two-dimensional
\perp_{ij}
-space, and
\Delta\phi_{ij}
is the difference in azimuth (with respect to the beam axis) between the dipole directions
i
and
j
. Thus the jet angularity can be written as

p_{T}\mathcal{R}_{J}^{\beta}=p_{\perp_{ij}}\,q^{\beta-1}\,[\mathcal{G}(q,\phi)]^{1-\beta/2}\,(2\cosh\eta_{J})^{\beta/2}\,.

(101)

Let us specialize to the case
i=a
and
j=J
. The hemisphere phase space is given by

\displaystyle\text{Hemisphere $J<a$:}\quad\theta(q_{0}-q),\quad q_{0}=\sqrt{\rho\,\hat{s}_{aJ}}\,,

(102)

with
\hat{s}_{aJ}=e^{-\eta_{J}}/(2\cosh\eta_{J})
. For the case
\beta>1
, dimensional regularization regulates all the divergences. Using the basis of eq. (
96
), after the trivial integrations and changing variable from
p_{J}
to
q
, eq. (
93
) reads

\displaystyle S_{J<a}(\{k_{l}\},\rho,\eta_{J})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2g^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}}\Bigl(\frac{\mu^{2}e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{\epsilon}\,\frac{(2\cosh\eta_{J})^{\beta\epsilon}}{k_{J}^{1+2\epsilon}}\,\delta(k_{B})

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\mathrm{d}\Omega_{2-2\epsilon}\int_{0}^{q_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}q\,}{q^{1-2\epsilon(\beta-1)}}\bigl[\mathcal{G}(q,\phi)\bigr]^{\epsilon(2-\beta)}\,.

(103)

Performing the integrals and expanding in
\epsilon

\displaystyle S_{J<a}(\{k_{l}\},\rho,\eta_{J})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\,\frac{\delta(k_{B})}{\beta-1}\biggl[\frac{8}{\mu\,r^{\beta-1}}\,\mathcal{L}_{1}\biggl(\frac{k_{J}}{\mu\,r^{\beta-1}}\biggr)-\frac{4}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\mu\,r^{\beta-1}}\,\mathcal{L}_{0}\biggl(\frac{k_{J}}{\mu\,r^{\beta-1}}\biggr)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{q_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{q}\ln\left[2\cosh\eta_{J}\,\mathcal{G}(q,\phi)\right]=\theta(r-1)\ln^{2}r\,.

(105)

Setting
\rho=\rho_{R}^{J}(R,\eta_{J})
as defined in eq. (
68

and thus the result in eq. (
75

\beta=1

) that an additional rapidity regulator is needed as

A.2
Corrections at
\mathcal{O}(R^{2})

Here we outline the analytic calculation of the soft function corrections in eq. (

\mathcal{O}(R^{2})
in the small jet radius expansion. A similar computation has been performed in ref.

for a jet mass measurement in dijet processes close to the kinematic threshold. We give the results for a conical (anti-
k_{T}
) jet with the
measurement of arbitrary jet angularities and general smooth jet vetoes (including in addition the beam thrust case).

First, we consider the contributions from the beam-beam dipole. Here the
\mathcal{O}(R^{2})
corrections are the leading contributions that account for the jet region. Since the deviations between the jet boundaries for different partitionings are in addition power suppressed by the jet radius all sets of distance measures discussed in sec.
2
lead to the same result at
\mathcal{O}(R^{2})

The term
s_{ab,B}
in eq. (
4.3

s_{ab,\delta}

In fact, for conical jets and a transverse momentum veto, i.e.

vanish, so that eq. (
A.2
) provides already the exact one-loop result for this case.

Next, we discuss the contributions from the beam-jet dipole, which in general differ for different partitionings. The corrections for real radiation inside the jet region can be written as

\displaystyle S_{aJ}^{(J)}=-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\,e^{\gamma_{E}\epsilon}\mu^{2\epsilon}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon)}\,\frac{1}{k_{J}^{1+2\epsilon}}\,\Bigl[I_{aJ,R\ll 1}^{(J)}+\underbrace{(I_{aJ}^{(J)}-I_{aJ,R\ll 1}^{(J)})}_{=\Delta I_{aJ}^{(J)}}\Bigr]\,,

\displaystyle S_{aJ}^{(B)}=-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\,e^{\gamma_{E}\epsilon}\mu^{2\epsilon}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon)}\,\frac{1}{k_{B}^{1+2\epsilon}}\,\Bigl[I_{aJ,R\ll 1}^{(B)}+\underbrace{(I_{aJ}^{(B)}-I_{aJ,R\ll 1}^{(B)})}_{=\Delta I_{aJ}^{(B)}}\Bigr]\,.

Expanding the integrand in

We remark that for jet vetoes which are not boost invariant, all of the dipoles, in particular also the jet-jet dipole, depend on the individual jet rapidities. For multijet processes or an additional recoiling color singlet state the soft function depends in addition on the separation of the jets in azimuth. The analytic computation for these cases is significantly more involved.

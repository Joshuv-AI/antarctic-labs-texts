ON A GENERALIZATION OF STRONGLY η \eta -CONVEX FUNCTIONS VIA FRACTAL SETS
Zaroni Robles 1 , José E. Sanabria 2 ∗ and Rainier V. Sánchez C. 3

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to study a generalization of strongly η \eta -convex functions using the fractal calculus developed by Yang [ 10 ] , namely generalized strongly η \eta -convex function. Among other results, we obtain some Hermite-Hadamard and Fejér type inequalities for this class of functions.

Introduction and preliminaries

Convex sets, convex functions and their generalizations are important in
applied mathematics, especially in nonlinear programming and optimization theory. For
example in economics, convexity plays a fundamental role in equilibrium and duality
theory. The convexity of sets and functions has been the subject of many studies in recent years,
among which we can mention several generalized variants;
as done by Awan et al.
[
1
]
when they introduced the class of
strongly
\eta
-convex functions as natural generalization of
\eta
-convex functions due to Gorji et al
[
6
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

[
1
]

A function
f:I=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be is said to be strongly
\eta
-convex with respect to
\eta
:
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and modulus
c\geq 0
, if

f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq f(y)+t\eta(f(x),f(y))-ct\left(1-t\right)\left(x-y\right)^{2},

for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
.

The concept of local fractional calculus (also called fractal calculus) has received considerable attention
for its application in non-differentiable problems of science and engineering. In
the sense of Mandelbrot, a fractal set is the one whose Hausdorff dimension strictly exceeds the topological dimension (see
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
). Many researchers studied the properties of functions
on fractal space and constructed many kinds of fractional calculus by using different approaches (see

[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
10
]
). In particular, Yang
[
10
]
stated the analysis of local fractional functions
on fractal space systematically, which includes local fractional
calculus and the monotonicity of functions. The theory of fractal sets developed by Yang establishes the following. For
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we have the following
\alpha
-type sets:

\mathbb{Z}^{\alpha}=\left\{0^{\alpha},\pm 1^{\alpha},\pm 2^{\alpha},...,\pm n^{\alpha},...\right\}(\textrm{integer numbers $\alpha$-type}).

\mathbb{Q}^{\alpha}=\left\{m^{\alpha}=\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\alpha}:p,q\in\mathbb{Z},q\neq 0\right\}\textrm{ (rational numbers $\alpha$-type)}.

\mathbb{J}^{\alpha}=\left\{m^{\alpha}\neq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\alpha}:p,q\in\mathbb{Z},q\neq 0\right\}\textrm{ (irrational numbers $\alpha$-type)}
.

\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}=\mathbb{Q^{\alpha}\cup}\mathbb{J}^{\alpha}\textrm{ (real numbers $\alpha$-type)}
.

We call fractal set to
\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{\alpha}
and any subset of it. The following facts are
found in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
.

If
a^{\alpha}
,
b^{\alpha}
and
c^{\alpha}
belong to the set

\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{\alpha}
of
\alpha
-type real numbers, then we have the following properties:

(1)

a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}
and
a^{\alpha}b^{\alpha}
belong to the set
\mathbb{R^{\alpha}}
.

(2)

a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}=b^{\alpha}+a^{\alpha}=(a+b)^{\alpha}=(b+a)^{\alpha}
.

(3)

a^{\alpha}+\left(b^{\alpha}+c^{\alpha}\right)=\left(a^{\alpha}+b{}^{\alpha}\right)+c{}^{\alpha}
.

(4)

a^{\alpha}b^{\alpha}=b^{\alpha}a^{\alpha}=(ab)^{\alpha}=(ba)^{\alpha}
.

(5)

a^{\alpha}\left(b^{\alpha}c^{\alpha}\right)=\left(a^{\alpha}b^{\alpha}\right)c^{\alpha}
.

(6)

a^{\alpha}\left(b^{\alpha}+c^{\alpha}\right)=a^{\alpha}b^{\alpha}+a^{\alpha}c^{\alpha}
.

(7)

a^{\alpha}+0^{\alpha}=0^{\alpha}+a^{\alpha}=a^{\alpha}
and
a^{\alpha}1^{\alpha}=1^{\alpha}a^{\alpha}=a^{\alpha}
.

It is important to note that in this theory the number
(a^{2})^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{\alpha}
will be
represented by
a^{2\alpha}
.

Now we introduce some basic definitions about the local factional calculus.

Definition 1.2
.

[
10
]

A non-differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
,
x\rightarrow f(x)
is called local fractional continuous at
x_{0}
, if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, such that

|f(x)-f(x_{0})|<\varepsilon^{\alpha}

holds for
|x-x_{0}|<\delta
, where
\varepsilon,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. If a function f is local fractional continuous on an interval
I
,
we denote
f\in C_{\alpha}(I)
.

Definition 1.3
.

[
10
]

The local fractional derivative of
f(x)
of order
\alpha
at
x=x_{0}
is defined by

f^{(\alpha)}(x_{0})=\left.\dfrac{d^{\alpha}f(x)}{dx^{\alpha}}\right|_{x=x_{0}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\dfrac{\Delta^{\alpha}(f(x)-f(x_{0}))}{(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}},

where
\Delta^{\alpha}(f(x)-f(x_{0}))\cong\Gamma(1+\alpha)(f(x)-f(x_{0}))
and
\Gamma
is the familiar Gamma
function.

Let
f^{(\alpha)}(x)=D^{\alpha}_{x}f(x)
. If there exists
f^{((k+1)\alpha)}(x)=\overbrace{D^{\alpha}_{x}\cdots D^{\alpha}_{x}}^{k+1\mbox{ times}}f(x)
for any
x\in I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, then we denote
f\in D_{(k+1)\alpha}(I)
, where
k=0,1,2,\ldots
.

Definition 1.4
.

[
10
]

Let
f\in C_{\alpha}[a,b]
. The local fractional integral of
f
on the interval
[a,b]
of order
\alpha
(denoted by
{\,\,}{}_{a}I_{b}^{(\alpha)}f
) is defined by

{\,\,}{}_{a}I_{b}^{(\alpha)}f(t)=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\int_{a}^{b}f(t)(dt)^{\alpha}=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\lim_{\Delta t\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f(t_{j})(\Delta t_{j})^{\alpha},

with
\Delta t=\max\{\Delta t_{0},\Delta t_{1},\ldots,\Delta t_{N-1}\}
and
\Delta t_{j}=t_{j+1}-t_{j}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,N-1
, where
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{i}<\cdots<t_{N-1}<t_{N}=b
is a partition of the interval
[a,b]
.

Here, it follows that
{\,\,}{}_{a}I_{b}^{(\alpha)}f=0
if
a=b
and
{\,\,}{}_{a}I_{b}^{(\alpha)}f=-{\,\,}{}_{b}I_{a}^{(\alpha)}f
if
a<b
. If
{\,\,}{}_{a}I_{x}^{(\alpha)}f
there exits for any
x\in[a,b]
, then it is denoted by
f\in I_{x}^{(\alpha)}[a,b]
.

2.
Main results

In this section we introduce the definition of generalized strongly
\eta
-convex function and establish some relevant inequalities.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
f:I=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
is said to be
generalized strongly
\eta
-convex
with respect to
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
and modulus
c\geq 0
, if

f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq f(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(f(x),f(y))-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}\left(1-t\right)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha},

for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
.

The family of all generalized strongly
\eta
-convex functions in an interval
I=[a,b]
is denoted by
\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
; which is,

\eta\mbox{-}GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)=\{f:I=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}|f\mbox{ is a generalized strongly }\eta\mbox{-convex function}\}.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that for particular cases of the numbers
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
c\geq 0
, and the function
\eta
, we recover well known classical concepts
of convex functions as is shown below.

(1)

If
\alpha=1
, then generalized strongly
\eta
-convex functions are strongly
\eta
-convex functions.

(2)

If
c=0
, then generalized strongly
\eta
-convex functions are generalized
\eta
-convex functions.

(3)

If
\alpha=1
and
c=0
, then generalized strongly
\eta
-convex functions are
\eta
-convex functions.

(4)

If
\eta(x^{\alpha},y^{\alpha})=x^{\alpha}-y^{\alpha}
, then generalized strongly
\eta
-convex functions are generalized strongly convex functions.

If
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
and
x=y
then
f(x)\leq f(x)+t^{\alpha}\eta(f(x),f(x))
; that is,
0^{\alpha}\leq t^{\alpha}\eta(f(x),f(x))
and hence,
0^{\alpha}\leq\eta(f(x),f(x))
. Also, if
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
and
t=1
then
f(x)\leq f(y)+\eta(f(x),f(y))
, which implies that
f(x)-f(y)\leq\eta(f(x),f(y))
. On the other hand, if
f\in GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
and
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
is a function which satisfies the equality
\eta(a^{\alpha},b^{\alpha})\geq a^{\alpha}-b^{\alpha}
for all
a^{\alpha},b^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
, then

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}f(x)+(1-t)^{\alpha}f(y)-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}\left(1-t\right)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(y)+t^{\alpha}[f(x)-f(y)]-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}\left(1-t\right)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(f(x),f(y))-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}\left(1-t\right)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha}.

This shows that
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
.

Example 2.3
.

For a function
f\in GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
, we may find another function
\eta

other than the function
\eta(x^{\alpha},y^{\alpha})=x^{\alpha}-y^{\alpha}
such that
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
. Indeed, since the function
g(x)=x^{2\alpha}
is generalized convex, by
[
9
, Theorem 2.8]
it follows that the function
f(x)=g(x)+c^{\alpha}x^{2\alpha}=g(x)+c^{\alpha}g(x)
is generalized strongly convex. If
\eta(x^{\alpha},y^{\alpha})=2^{\alpha}x^{\alpha}+y^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(tx+(1-t)y)+c^{\alpha}g(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))+c^{\alpha}\left(tx+(1-t)y\right)^{2\alpha}\,\,\,\,\mbox{\, \, \, \, (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{SanR}{}{}, Example 2.3]})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))+c^{\alpha}\left(t^{2}x^{2}+t(1-t)2xy+(1-t)^{2}y^{2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))+c^{\alpha}\left(t^{2}(x-y)^{2}-t(x-y)^{2}+tx^{2}-ty^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))+c^{\alpha}\left(t(t-1)(x-y)^{2}+tx^{2}-ty^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha}+c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}x^{2\alpha}-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}y^{2\alpha}+c^{\alpha}y^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha}+c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}(2^{\alpha}x^{2\alpha}+y^{2\alpha})+c^{\alpha}y^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha}+c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}\eta(g(x),g(y))+c^{\alpha}g(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(y)+t^{\alpha}\eta(f(x),f(y))-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\left(x-y\right)^{2\alpha}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\in[0,1]
. This shows that
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
. Note that
f
is generalized strongly
\eta
-convex with respect to every
\eta(x^{\alpha},y^{\alpha})=a^{\alpha}x^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}y^{\alpha}
with
a^{\alpha}\geq 1^{\alpha}
,
b^{\alpha}\geq-1^{\alpha}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f\in D_{\alpha}(I)
be a generalized strongly
\eta
-convex function. If
x\in I
is the minimum
of
f
, then

f^{\alpha}(x)\dfrac{\left(y-x\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}\geq 0\Rightarrow\eta\left(f(y),f(x)\right)\geq c^{\alpha}\left(y-x\right)^{2\alpha}.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in I
is the minimum of
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}(I)
,
then
f(x)\leq f(y)
for all
y\in I
. Since
I
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}
, then
y_{t}=x+t(y-x)\in I
for all
y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
. Thus, we have
0^{\alpha}\leq f(x+t(y-x))-f(x)
and dividing above inequality by
t^{\alpha}
, we obtain that

0^{\alpha}\leq\dfrac{f(x+t(y-x))-f(x)}{t^{\alpha}}

Putting
h=t(y-x)
, we have
t=\dfrac{h}{y-x}
and hence,

0\leq\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h^{\alpha}}(y-x)^{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)[f\left(x+h\right)-f\left(x\right)]}{h^{\alpha}}\frac{\left(y-x\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}.

Letting
h\rightarrow 0
we have

0\leq f^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{\left(y-x\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}.\,\hskip 113.81102pt(1)

Since
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}([a,b])
, then

\displaystyle f(x+t(y-x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x)+t^{\alpha}\eta(f(y),f(x))-c^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}(y-x)^{2\alpha},

which implies that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)f(x+t(y-x))-f(x)}{t^{\alpha}}\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta(f(y),f(x))-c^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}(y-x)^{2\alpha}.

Taking limit on both sides as
t\rightarrow 0
, we obtain that

\displaystyle f^{(\alpha)}(x)\dfrac{(y-x)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta(f(y),f(x))-c^{\alpha}(y-x)^{2\alpha}.

By (1), we conclude that
\eta(f(y),f(x))-c^{\alpha}(y-x)^{2\alpha}\geq 0
and hence,

\eta(f(y),f(x))\geq c^{\alpha}(y-x)^{2\alpha}
.

The following result is a new refinement of the Hermite-Hadamard type inequality for generalized strongly
\eta
-convex functions.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}([a,b])
. If
\eta(.,.)
is bounded from above by
M_{\eta}^{\alpha}
on
f([a,b])\times f([a,b])

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)-\left(\frac{M_{\eta}}{2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{(b-a)^{\alpha}}\left[{\,\,}{}_{a}I_{b}^{(\alpha)}f(x)-\dfrac{c^{\alpha}}{4^{\alpha}}(b-a)^{3\alpha}A\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2^{\alpha}}+\Gamma(1+\alpha)\left[\frac{\eta(f(a),f(b))+\eta(f(b),f(a))}{2^{\alpha}}B\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.c^{\alpha}\left(b-a\right)^{2\alpha}\left(B-A\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2^{\alpha}}+\Gamma(1+\alpha)\left[M_{\eta}^{\alpha}B-c^{\alpha}\left(b-a\right)^{2\alpha}\left(B-A\right)\right],

donde

and

Proof.

Since
f\in\eta
-
GSC^{c}_{\alpha}([a,b])

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b}{4}-\frac{t(b-a)}{4}+\frac{a+b}{4}+\frac{t(b-a)}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{a+b-t(b-a)}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{a+b+t(b-a)}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b-t(b-a)}{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}\eta\left(f\left(\frac{a+b+t(b-a)}{2}\right),f\left(\frac{a+b-t(b-a)}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{c}{4}\right)^{\alpha}\left(t(b-a)\right)^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b-t(b-a)}{2}\right)+\left(\frac{M_{\eta}}{2}\right)^{\alpha}-\left(\dfrac{c}{4}\right)^{\alpha}\left(t(b-a)\right)^{2\alpha}

,

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b-t(b-a)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)-\left(\frac{M_{\eta}}{2}\right)^{\alpha}+\left(\dfrac{c}{4}\right)^{\alpha}t^{2\alpha}(b-a)^{2\alpha},

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b+t(b-a)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)-\left(\frac{M_{\eta}}{2}\right)^{\alpha}+\left(\dfrac{c}{4}\right)^{\alpha}t^{2\alpha}(b-a)^{2\alpha}.

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha}}{(b-a)^{\alpha}}\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,(dx)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha}}{(b-a)^{\alpha}}\left[\displaystyle\int_{a}^{(a+b)/2}f(x)\,(dx)^{\alpha}+\displaystyle\int_{(a+b)/2}^{b}f(x)\,(dx)^{\alpha}\right]

-

\displaystyle f(b)+\eta(f(a),f(b))\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)\cdot\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+2\alpha)}

\displaystyle c^{\alpha}\left(b-a\right)^{2\alpha}\Gamma(1+\alpha)\left[\dfrac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+2\alpha)}-\dfrac{\Gamma(1+2\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+3\alpha)}\right]=A_{1}.

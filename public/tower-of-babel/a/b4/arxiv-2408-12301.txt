Compact star in noninteger power model of f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity
Yong-Xiang Cui, Zu Yan, Kota Numajiri, Taishi Katsuragawa, Shin’ichi Nojiri

Abstract
We investigate compact stars in the noninteger power (NIP) model of f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity theory, which includes the higher-curvature correction to the Einstein-Hilbert action. The mass-radius relation of the compact stars in the NIP model predicts large deviations from those in the general relativity in the low-mass region, potentially allowing us to test the NIP model by future astrophysical observations. We also study the nonvanishing scalar hair surrounding the compact star and demonstrate that the chameleon mechanism works efficiently. They result in distinct scalar profiles inside and outside the star, which implies screening the fifth force mediated by the scalar field.

II
NIP model of
f(R)
gravity

II.1
Metric description

By replacing the Einstein-Hilbert action with the generic function of the scalar curvature
R
,
f(R)
,
the action of the gravitational field with matter field
\Psi
can be obtained

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,f(R)\,+S_{m}(g_{\mu\nu},\Psi)\,,

(1)

where
\kappa^{2}=8\pi G
with the gravitational constant
G
.
Variation of Eq. (
1
) with respect to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
leads to the field equation in
f(R)
gravity:

\displaystyle f_{R}(R)R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}f(R)g_{\mu\nu}+(g_{\mu\nu}\square-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})f_{R}(R)=\kappa^{2}T_{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

where
f_{R}(R)=df/dR
, and
T_{\mu\nu}
is the energy-momentum tensor defined by varying the action of matter
S_{M}

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{M}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\,.

(3)

Operating the covariant derivative to Eq. (
2
), we find the conservation law for
T_{\mu\nu}
,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=0\,.

(4)

By multiplying
g^{\mu\nu}
with both-hand sides of Eq. (
2
),
the trace part of the field equation can be read as

\displaystyle\square f_{R}(R)=\frac{1}{3}\left[2f(R)-Rf_{R}(R)+\kappa^{2}T\right]\,,

(5)

where
T=T^{\mu}_{\mu}
is the trace of the energy-momentum tensor.
Note that the curvature
R
can take nontrivial distribution even in the vacuum (
T_{\mu\nu}=0
).

In this work, we employ a function form for
f(R)
as follows
[
56
]
:

\displaystyle f(R)=R+a\,R^{1+\frac{1}{b}}\,.

(6)

Here,
a
and
b
are model parameters, and
b>1
.
The parameter
b
is dimensionless, while the parameter
a
has dimension controlled by
b
.
In the unit of length
L
, the dimensions for each quantity are
[R]=L^{-2}
, and
[a]=L^{2/b}
.
This model has a form similar to the
R^{2}
model from the viewpoint of higher-order correction of
R
to GR.
By taking the limit
a\rightarrow 0
or
b\rightarrow 1
,
GR or the
R^{2}
model are recovered, respectively.
Note that taking the limit
b\rightarrow\infty
,
the NIP model approximates the GR,
f(R)\rightarrow(1+a)R
,
up to the redefinition of the gravitational constant.

We also comment on the potential origin of this correction
[
56
]
.
This theory can be seen as the low-energy effective theory of dilatonic gravity defined by the action

\displaystyle S_{\phi}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\phi R-\frac{\omega(\phi)}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi-V(\phi)\right),

(7)

with a power-law-type potential

\displaystyle V(\phi)=V_{0}\phi^{n}.

(8)

The NIP correction term of the curvature Eq. (
6
) can be obtained by neglecting the kinetic term in the above action and eliminating
\phi
using the field equation.

Substituting the above NIP model into Eqs. (
2
) and (
5
),
the field equation and its trace part are written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa^{2}T_{\mu\nu}&=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}\\
&\qquad+aR^{\frac{1}{b}}\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{b}\right)R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}\right]+a\left(1+\frac{1}{b}\right)(g_{\mu\nu}\square-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})R^{\frac{1}{b}}\,,\end{split}

(9)

and

\displaystyle a\left(1+\frac{1}{b}\right)\square R^{\frac{1}{b}}=\frac{1}{3}\left[R+a\left(1-\frac{1}{b}\right)R^{1+\frac{1}{b}}+\kappa^{2}T\right]\,.

(10)

One can find in that Eq. (
10
),
the scalar curvature
R
obeys the Klein-Gordon type equation,
although
R
is determined by algebraic relation
R=-\kappa^{2}T
in the GR.
This result indicates a scalar DOF in the
f(R)
gravity theory.
The presence of the scalar DOF in
f(R)
characterizes the deviations from GR, and as we will see later, the original TOV equation receives significant changes.
We note that in Eq. (
9
), we separated the corrections to GR from the Einstein tensor.
The second line in Eq. (
9
) represents the scalar DOF originating from the gravity sector.

II.2
Scalar-tensor description in Jordan frame

To show the scalar DOF explicitly,
we extract the scalar field from the action (
1
) and describe
f(R)
gravity theory in terms of the scalar-tensor theory.
Defining a new scalar field, called the scalaron, by

\displaystyle\Phi\equiv f_{R}(R)\,,

(11)

we find that the action (
1
) is rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[\Phi R-Y(\Phi)\right]\,+S_{m}(g_{\mu\nu},\Psi)\,,

(12)

where
Y(\Phi)
is defined as

\displaystyle Y(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\tilde{R}(\Phi)\Phi-f(\tilde{R}(\Phi))\,.

(13)

As in Eq. (
12
),
the
f(R)
gravity theory can be regarded as GR nonminimally coupling the scalaron field.
It is now apparent that in addition to two DOFs from the Einstein-Hilbert action,
there is an additional DOF originating from the scalaron field
[
60
]
.

The field equation with respect to metric can be obtained
by varying the action (
12
) with respect to
g_{\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\Phi}(T_{\mu\nu}+T^{(\Phi)}_{\mu\nu})\,.

(14)

T^{(\Phi)}_{\mu\nu}
is an effective energy-momentum tensor of the scalar field,

\displaystyle T^{(\Phi)}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\left[\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\Phi-g_{\mu\nu}\square\Phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}Y(\Phi)\right]\,.

(15)

Moreover, the variation of the action (
12
) with respect to
\Phi
leads to
R=Y_{\Phi}(\Phi)
, where
Y_{\Phi}=\partial Y/\partial\Phi
.
Combining it with the trace of Eq. (
14
), we obtain the field equation with respect to the scalaron field:

\displaystyle\square\Phi=\frac{dV_{\mathrm{eff}}}{d\Phi}\,.

(16)

where we define the effective potential
V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Phi,T)
of the scalar field:

\displaystyle\frac{dV_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Phi,T)}{d\Phi}\equiv\frac{1}{3}\left[\Phi R(\Phi)-2Y(\Phi)+\kappa^{2}T\right]\,.

(17)

We note that
V_{\mathrm{eff}}
explicitly depends on
T
, and we denote it by
V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Phi,T)
.

Finally, we consider the effective mass of the scalaron field.
The mass is related to the second derivative potential given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}V_{\mathrm{eff}}}{d\Phi^{2}}&\equiv\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{d\Phi}\left[\Phi R(\Phi)-2Y(\Phi)+\kappa^{2}T\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{F_{R}(R)}{F_{RR}(R)}-R\right]\,.\end{split}

(18)

By convention, the second derivative at the potential minimum is used to define the effective mass,

\displaystyle m_{\Phi}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\left.\frac{d^{2}V_{\mathrm{eff}}}{d\Phi^{2}}\right|_{{\Phi}=\Phi_{\min}}\,,

(19)

where the minimum of the effective potential can be calculated by using the stationary condition,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dV_{\mathrm{eff}}}{d\Phi}=0\right|_{{\Phi}=\Phi_{\min}}\,.

(20)

Note that for a given
T
, we can search for
\Phi=\Phi_{\min}
at the potential minimum.

We consider the field equation of the scalaron and observe the chameleon mechanism
[
58
,
59
]
in the NIP model.
By substituting Eq. (
6
) into Eq. (
11
),
the scalaron field is given as

\displaystyle\Phi=1+a\left(1+\frac{1}{b}\right)R^{\frac{1}{b}}\,.

(21)

Here, the right-hand side of Eq. (
14
) suggests that
G/\Phi=G/f_{R}
gives the effective gravitational constant.
To avoid the antigravity (repulsive gravitational interaction), we impose
f_{R}>0
.
This condition indicates that the scalar curvature
R
should satisfy the following condition:

\displaystyle\left\{\ \begin{aligned} 0\leq\,&R\,\leq\left(\frac{b}{\left\lvert a\right\rvert(1+b)}\right)^{b}\quad&\text{for}\ a<0\,,\\
&0\leq R\quad&\text{for}\ a>0\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(22)

We obtain the field equation for the scalaron field (
16

\displaystyle\square\Phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left\{\frac{(b-1)\Phi+2}{b+1}\left[\frac{b(\Phi-1)}{a(b+1)}\right]^{b}+\kappa^{2}T\right\}\,,

(23)

and the effective potential
V_{\mathrm{eff}}
(
17
) is given as

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Phi,T)=\frac{1}{3}\left\{\frac{\left[(b-1)\Phi+3\right](\Phi-1)}{(b+1)(b+2)}\left[\frac{b(\Phi-1)}{a(b+1)}\right]^{b}+\kappa^{2}T\Phi\right\}\,.

(24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{\Phi}^{2}&=\frac{b[1+(b-1)\Phi_{\min}]}{3a(b+1)}\left[\frac{b(\Phi_{\min}-1)}{a(b+1)}\right]^{b-1}\,.\end{split}

III
Static and spherically symmetric star under NIP gravity

III.1
The configuration of system

We consider a static and spherically symmetric star composed of the perfect fluid.
The geometry inside and outside the star is characterized by the following spacetime metric
g_{\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-e^{2\nu(r)}dt^{2}+e^{2\lambda(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,,

(26)

where
x^{\mu}=\{t,r,\theta,\varphi\}
,
and
d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}
is the line element of the ywo-sphere.
The
(t,t)
and
(r,r)
components of the metric,
\nu(r)
and
\lambda(r)
, depend only on the radial coordinate
r
.

The conserved energy-momentum tensor inside the star is defined as

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=[\epsilon(r)+p(r)]u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+p(r)g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(27)

where
\epsilon(r)
and
p(r)
are the total energy density and the pressure, respectively.

u^{\mu}=\{e^{-\nu},0,0,0\}
is the four velocity of static fluid.
The radius of the star
r=r_{s}
is defined as the radial coordinate where the pressure vanishes
p(r_{s})=0
.
The matter composing compact stars is assumed to be the perfect fluid,
and it is characterized by the EOS
p=p(\rho)
that describes the physical properties of the matter.
In this work, we use the (piecewise) polytrope EOS
[
65
]
:

\displaystyle p(\rho)=K\rho^{\Gamma}\,.

(28)

Here
a
,
K
, and
\Gamma
are parameters characterizing the EOS.
The total energy density
\epsilon(r)
and rest-mass density
\rho(r)
are related through the first law of thermodynamics,
and for polytrope EOS, we find the following relation

\displaystyle\epsilon(\rho)=(1+a)\rho+\frac{K}{\Gamma-1}\rho^{\Gamma}\,.

(29)

When implementing the numerical calculation with the piecewise polytope EOS,
we define the discrete intervals and assign the index
i
for
a
,
K
, and
\Gamma
in each interval.

We impose the junction condition at the surface of the compact star
r=r_{s}
.
For the matter sector, we assume

\displaystyle\left[T_{\mu\nu}\right]=0\,,

(30)

where
\quantity[\dots]
denotes the jump over some timelike hypersurface.
This condition is also consistent with our assumption of the polytope EOS, and we do not study the thin-shell collapse with a delta-function-like discontinuity.
For the gravity sector,
there are many existing works
[
66
,
67
,
68
]
that have discussed the junction condition in
f(R)
gravity.
The first and second fundamental forms across a timelike hypersurface are the same as the usual junction condition in GR
[
69
]

\displaystyle\left[g_{\mu\nu}\right]=0\,,\quad\left[K_{\mu\nu}\right]=0\,,

(31)

where
K_{\mu\nu}
is extrinsic curvature.
If this condition is violated, then the spacetime is singular at this surface.
Furthermore, the field equation Eqs. (
5
) or (
16
) of additional scalar DOF demands the curvature continuity conditions
[
70
]

\displaystyle\left[R\right]=0\,,\quad\left[\nabla_{\mu}R\right]=0\,.

(32)

These conditions indicate no discontinuity at the surface for the metric, matter field, and curvature. Because the numerical solution of inner and outer are calculated independently, these conditions should
be imposed when they are connected.

Modified TOV equations

We derive the modified TOV equation in the NIP model.
First of all,

r

\displaystyle 0=p^{\prime}+\nu^{\prime}\left(\epsilon+p\right)\,,

Substituting the spacetime metric

in Eq. (

&\quad\left[e^{2\lambda}\left(ab^{2}r^{2}R^{\frac{1}{b}+3}-2b^{2}R^{2}\left(a(b+1)R^{\frac{1}{b}}-b\kappa^{2}r^{2}\epsilon+b\right)\right)+2b^{2}R^{2}\left(a(b+1)R^{\frac{1}{b}}+b\right)\right.\\
&\hskip 133.72795pt\left.+2ab(b+1)rR^{\frac{1}{b}+1}\left(2R^{\prime}+rR^{\prime\prime}\right)-2a\left(b^{2}-1\right)r^{2}R^{\frac{1}{b}}R^{\prime 2}\right]\,,\end{split}

(34)

36
).
To complete the system, we impose a specific EOS capable of determining the relation between the total energy density
\epsilon(r)
and the pressure
p(r)
.
Thus, we need to solve four ordinary differential equations (ODEs) for four functions
\lambda(r)
,
\nu(r)
,
R(r)
, and
p(r)
with the assumed EOS.

III.3
Asymptotic solution and scalar hair

To consider the boundary condition outside the star, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the spacetime.
We begin with a realistic assumption that the spacetime geometry shows asymptotic flatness, where the scalar curvature
R
is expected to go to zero as
r\rightarrow\infty
.
Based on this assumption, Eq. (
21
) suggests the scalaron field
\Phi
goes to unity in vacuum.
This behavior is actually consistent with the physical insight that the scalaron field rolls down the effective potential and reaches
\Phi=1
,
which also satisfies the stationary condition in the vacuum.

The scalaron characterizes the deviation from GR, and
\Phi=f_{R}=1
corresponds to the GR limit.
Thus, the NIP model approaches GR far from the compact star,
as the scalaron field approaches
\Phi=1
.
Although the
f(R)
gravity does not possess Birkhoff-Jebsen’s theorem in general,
the NIP model asymptotically shows it.
Similar results can also be found in the
R^{2}
model
[
60
]
.
Then, the spacetime solution is the asymptotically Schwarzschild one at
r\rightarrow\infty
:

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}e^{2\nu(r)}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}e^{-2\lambda(r)}=1-\frac{2M}{r}\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}R(r)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}R^{\prime}(r)=0\,.

(38)

The constant
M
corresponds to the Schwarzschild mass.
We can also define a mass within radius
r
as

\displaystyle m(r)\equiv\frac{r}{2}\left(1-e^{-2\lambda(r)}\right).

(39)

where
m(\infty)
equals to the Schwarzschild mass
M
.
It should be noted that in the
f(R)
gravity
m(r_{s})
does not provide the proper stellar mass as an observable since it does not include the contribution of the scalaron (or the curvature), which stems from the absence of Birkhoff-Jebsen’s theorem.

Although the one-way integration method to be adopted does not need to identify the form of the asymptotic solution,
we briefly discuss the asymptotic behavior of the scalaron field
\Phi
outside the compact star.
Assuming the asymptotic Minkowski spacetime and a vacuum
T=0
, far enough away from the compact star,
we find Eq. (
23
) reduces into the following form:

\displaystyle\Box(\Phi-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(b-1)}{3(b+1)}\left[\frac{b}{a(b+1)}\right]^{b}(\Phi-1)^{b+1}+\frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{b}{a(b+1)}\right]^{b}(\Phi-1)^{b}\,.

(40)

We expect
\Phi\approx 1
away from the compact star,
and when
0<\Phi-1\ll 1
and
b>1
, we find
(\Phi-1)^{b+1}\ll(\Phi-1)^{b}
.
Thus, the scalaron field equation around
\Phi=1
at the leading order is given as

\displaystyle(\Phi-1)^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2}{r}(\Phi-1)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{b}{a(b+1)}\right]^{b}(\Phi-1)^{b}\,.

(41)

The above equation determines the scalaron behavior around
\Phi\approx 1
.

When
a
is very large
a\gg 1
, the right-hand side of Eq. (
41
) is negligible.
In this case, Eq. (
41
) approximates the Klein-Gordon equation for a free particle, and the scalaron field behaves like the Coulomb potential,
\Phi(r)\sim C_{1}/r+C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are integration constants.
When
a
is very small
a\ll 1
,
the right-hand side of Eq. (
41
) is dominant.
Then, the scalaron may rapidly go to the potential minimum in vacuum
\Phi(r)\rightarrow 1
, corresponding to the GR limit.
We can expect that the scalaron field
\Phi(r)
is a decreasing function with respect to
r
and may decay more quickly for the smaller
a
.

III.4
Boundary conditions

We solve the modified TOV equations as a multiboundary value problem and specify boundary conditions at
r=0
and
r=\infty
.
We have four differential equations with an EOS, and three of them are the first-order ODEs (
33
), (
34
), and (
35
) for
p(r)
,
\lambda(r)
, and
\nu(r)
.
One of them is a second-order ODE for the curvature
R(r)
.
Thus, we need to specify five boundary conditions.
To avoid the conical singularity at the center of star
r=0
, we demand that

\displaystyle\lambda(0)=0\,,\quad R^{\prime}(0)=0\,.

(42)

We still have three undetermined values at the center of the star,

\displaystyle\nu(0)=\nu_{c}\,,\quad R(0)=R_{c}\,,\quad p(0)=p_{c}\,.

(43)

Equation (
38
) determines
\nu_{c}
and
R_{c}
so that
\nu(r)
and
R(r)
approach the Schwarzschild solution at the radial infinity.
Note that the EOS gives the central pressure
p_{c}
when the central rest-mass density
\rho_{c}
is specified.
Thus,
R_{c}
and
\nu_{c}
are determined by the boundary conditions at radial infinity, and we treat them as the shooting parameters.

To solve the modified TOV equation smoothly,
we need to impose the asymptotic behaviors around the center.
By expanding the solution of the Eqs. (
33
)–(
36
) around
r=0
,
four functions behave as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lambda(r)\simeq\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}r+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}r^{2}\,,&\nu(r)\simeq\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}r+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{2}r^{2}\,,\\
&R(r)\simeq R_{0}+R_{1}r+\frac{1}{2}R_{2}r^{2}\,,&p(r)\simeq p_{0}+p_{1}r+\frac{1}{2}p_{2}r^{2}\,.\end{aligned}

(44)

Substituting them into Eqs. (
34
)–(
36
) determines the each coefficient:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{0}=0\,,\quad\nu_{0}=\nu_{c}\,,\quad R_{0}=R_{c}\,,\quad p_{0}=p_{c}\,,\quad\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{c}\,,\end{split}

(45)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{1}=\nu_{1}=R_{1}=p_{1}=0\,,\end{split}

(46)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{2}&=\frac{2bR_{0}+4b\kappa^{2}\epsilon_{0}+6b\kappa^{2}p_{0}+a(2b+1)R_{0}^{\frac{1}{b}+1}}{18\left[a(b+1)R_{0}^{\frac{1}{b}}+b\right]}\,,\\
\nu_{2}&=-\frac{bR_{0}-4b\kappa^{2}\epsilon_{0}-6b\kappa^{2}p_{0}+a(b+2)R_{0}^{\frac{1}{b}+1}}{18\left[a(b+1)R_{0}^{\frac{1}{b}}+b\right]}\,,\\
R_{2}&=\frac{\left[a(b-1)R_{0}^{\frac{1}{b}+1}-b\kappa^{2}\epsilon_{0}+3b\kappa^{2}p_{0}+bR_{0}\right]bR_{0}^{\frac{b-1}{b}}}{9a(b+1)}\,,\\
p_{2}&=\frac{(p_{0}+\epsilon_{0})\left[bR_{0}-4b\kappa^{2}\epsilon_{0}-6b\kappa^{2}p_{0}+a(b+2)R_{0}^{\frac{1}{b}+1}\right]}{18\left[a(b+1)R_{0}^{\frac{1}{b}}+b\right]}\,.\end{split}

(47)

Regarding boundary conditions at
r=\infty
, we rely on Eq. (
38
) to determine
\nu_{c}
and
R_{c}
.
In summary, we determine the five boundary conditions given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lambda(0)=0\,,\quad p(0)=p_{c}\,,\quad R^{\prime}(0)=0\,,\\
&\nu(\infty)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\,,\quad R(\infty)=0\,,\end{split}

(48)

and
p_{c}
is given by hand.
We use the general shooting method; from the origin
r=0
, we start the shooting until the solution satisfies the two boundary conditions in the second line of Eq. (
48
).

Numerical implementations

To numerically solve the modified TOV equations, we consider the radial direction divided into three regions.
The first one is inside the star,
r\in\left[0,r_{s}\right]
, where
r_{s}
is the surface of the star.
For outside the star,
r\in\left(r_{s},\infty\right)
,
we define the radius
r_{\Phi}
where the scalaron field is small enough to be neglected.
Thus, we have
\left[0,r_{s}\right]
,
\left(r_{s},r_{\Phi}\right]

\left(r_{\phi},\infty\right)
.
The origin point in the shooting method is set as
r_{c}=10^{-3}r_{g}
.

In the above configuration,
we solve Eqs. (
33
) – (
36
) from
r_{c}
to
r_{\Phi}
.
We use the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method supported by the package
SciPy
in P
ython
to solve the ODE system.
First, we define the dimensionless variables by the following characteristic scales:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&M_{g}=M_{\odot}\simeq 1.99\times 10^{33}~\mathrm{g}\,,\\
&r_{g}=\frac{GM_{\odot}}{c^{2}}\simeq 1.48\times 10^{5}~\mathrm{cm}\,,\\
&\rho_{g}=\frac{M_{g}}{r_{g}^{3}}\simeq 6.18\times 10^{17}~\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}\,,\\
&p_{g}=\frac{M_{g}c^{2}}{r_{g}^{3}}\simeq 5.55\times 10^{38}~\mathrm{g\,cm^{-1}s^{-2}}\,,\\
&R_{g}=\frac{\rho_{g}G}{c^{2}}\simeq 4.59\times 10^{-11}~\mathrm{cm^{-2}}\,,\\
&m_{\Phi g}=R_{g}^{\frac{1}{2}}\simeq 0.34~\mathrm{eV}\,.\end{split}

(49)

For the center
r=r_{c}
,
we constantly change the value of

with given the
\nu_{c}
and

to solve the ODE system
until it reaches the condition (

to satisfy the Schwarzschild solution.
In addition, for the EOS

[
65
]
of SLy EOS
[

]

There is the upper value for

does not exceed the light speed
c^{2}

The positive chameleon mass in the Einstein frame demands a boundary of central pressure
p_{c}

For the value of the parameter

and

we take

Note that we choose the parameter

to be on the astrophysical scale.
One could derive limits on the parameter from cosmological observations by assuming higher energy scales and adapting the higher-order curvature term to inflation.
However, we consider the lower energy scales, especially the compact star scale, due in part to numerical difficulties.

V
Conclusion

We have studied the compact stars in the NIP model of
f(R)
gravity, compared with known results in GR and
R^{2}
model of
f(R)
gravity.
Based on the scalar-tensor description in the Jordan frame,
we have formulated the modified TOV equations in the static and spherically symmetric spacetime and discussed the boundary conditions and asymptotic behavior of the scalaron field analytically.
We have solved the modified TOV equations numerically with the shooting method and investigated the scalaron distribution, its effective mass around the compact star, and the M-R relation of the compact star.

Regarding scalaron field distribution surrounding the compact star, the scalaron field takes a nearly constant value inside the star and starts decreasing by the power law near the surface.
This behavior can be understood in terms of the chameleon mechanism.
Although we can find similar behavior in the
R^{2}
model,
the
R^{2}
model does not possess the chameleon mechanism, and the scalaron field shows the exponential decay
e^{-mr}
where
m
represents the constant scalaron mass in the
R^{2}
model.
Moreover, comparing the numerically obtained solution
\Phi_{\mathrm{sol}}
with the field value at the effective potential minimum
\Phi_{\min}
, we have observed a significant difference between these two quantities inside and outside the compact stars.
This result indicates the nonlinear effects.

We have also considered the scalaron contributions to the stellar mass.
The evaluations of the energy condition of the scalaron field indicate that the scalaron field can behave like the quintessence field inside the compact star, while like baryonic matter outside the star.
Consequently, the scalaron field in the interior region can reduce the stellar mass significantly for lower
\rho_{c}
cases, although near the surface and outside the star it increases the mass.
These multiple contributions of the scalar field to the compact star lead to the peculiar shape of the M-R relation.
The above results are very similar to the existing results
[
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
43
,
46
,
47
,
60
]
in the
R^{2}
model, and it would be the common features in
f(R)
gravity that includes the higher-curvature correction.

In addition to the scalar hair surrounding the compact star,
we have investigated the M-R relation in the NIP model, compared with the results in the GR and
R^{2}
mode.
We have observed the rotation of the M-R curves, which is similar to the
R^{2}
model.
However, the M-R curves in the low-mass regions largely deviate from those in the GR and
R^{2}
model, which would be the characteristic nature of the NIP model.
It should be noted that we do not constrain the value of
a
and
b

because of the degeneracy problem
[
56
]
.
We cannot distinguish the ambiguity in EOS and that in gravitational theory in the M-R relation,
Thus, we need further observational data to separately constrain the model parameters in the
f(R)
gravity theory.
Although we cannot clearly constrain the value of
a
and
b
based on the M-R relation,
the characteristic M-R relation in the low-mass region may allow us to distinguish the NIP model from GR qualitatively.

SCALAR-TENSOR DESCRIPTION IN EINSTEIN FRAME

We briefly review the NIP model in the Einstein frame.
Although we discussed the physical measurements in the Jordan frame,
we can discuss more about the scalaron field by performing the Weyl transformation of the metric:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\Phi\,g_{\mu\nu}\equiv\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\kappa\tilde{\Phi}}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(51)

Here,
\tilde{\Phi}
is the scalaron field in the Einstein frame and related to
\Phi
in the Jordan frame by

\displaystyle\Phi=\exp[\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\kappa\tilde{\Phi}]\,.

(52)

According to the Weyl transformation,
the action Eq. (
12
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\quantity[\frac{\Tilde{R}}{2 \kappa^{2}}
- \frac{1}{2} \partial^{\alpha} \Tilde{\Phi} \partial_{\alpha} \Tilde{\Phi}
-U(\Tilde{\Phi})]\,.

(53)

The scalaron potential in the Einstein frame
U
is defined as

\displaystyle U(\tilde{\Phi})=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\kappa\tilde{\Phi}}Y(\tilde{\Phi})

(54)

This action comprises the Einstein-Hilbert action and minimally coupled canonical scalar field with potential
U(\tilde{\Phi})
.
The whole DOF of the gravitational field is apparently
2+1
.
This frame is often called the Einstein frame in contrast with the Jordan frame with the action Eq. (
1
).

By variation of the gravitational action (
53
) and the matter action,
we can derive the field equations for
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{\Phi}

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{R}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\kappa^{2}\quantity(\Tilde{T}_{\mu\nu} + \Tilde{T}^{\Tilde{\Phi}}_{\mu\nu}),

(55)

\displaystyle\tilde{\square}\tilde{\Phi}=U_{\tilde{\Phi}}(\tilde{\Phi})+\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{6}}\tilde{T}\,,

(56)

where
\tilde{T}^{\tilde{\Phi}}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}
are defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{T}^{\tilde{\Phi}}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\Phi}\partial_{\nu}\tilde{\Phi}-\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\quantity[
\frac{1}{2} \partial^{\alpha} \Tilde{\Phi} \partial_{\alpha} \Tilde{\Phi}
-U(\Tilde{\Phi})
]

(57)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{2}{\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}}\frac{\delta S_{M}}{\delta\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\kappa\tilde{\phi}}\>T_{\mu\nu}\,.

(58)

The tilde denotes the quantities in the Einstein frame.

One can also discuss the chameleon mechanism in the Einstein frame.
The effective potential
U_{\mathrm{\mathrm{eff}}}(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{T})
in the Einstein frame is defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\square}\tilde{\Phi}&=U_{\tilde{\Phi}}(\tilde{\Phi})+\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{6}}\tilde{T}\\

(59)

Note that
T
without tilde denotes the Jordan frame quantity.
The chameleon mass of the scalar field in the Einstein frame is

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{\tilde{\Phi}}^{2}&\equiv\evaluated{\pdv[2]{U_{\mathrm{\mathrm{eff}}}}{\Tilde{\Phi}}}_{\Tilde{\Phi}=\Tilde{\Phi}_{\min}}\\
&=\frac{1}{3F_{R}\left(R_{\min}\right)}\left(\frac{F_{R}\left(R_{\min}\right)}{F_{RR}\left(R_{\min}\right)}-R_{\min}\right)\\

(60)

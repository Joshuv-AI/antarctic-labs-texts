The limiting spectral distribution of the sample canonical correlation matrix
Xiaozhuo Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the spectral properties of the sample canonical correlation (SCC) matrix under the alternative hypothesis to provide a more comprehensive description of the association between two sets of variables. Our research involves establishing the relationship between the eigenvalues of the SCC matrix and the block correlation matrix, as well as proving the universality of the Stieltjes transform of the limiting spectral distribution (LSD) of the block correlation matrix. By combining the results from the normal case, we establish the limiting spectral distribution (LSD) of the SCC matrix with a general underlying distribution under the arbitrary rank alternative hypothesis. Finally, we present several simulated examples and find that they fit well with our theoretical results.

Model and Relationship

When we encounter multivariables or high dimensional data, principal component analysis as a traditional research method for data reduction and interpretation, fought against data explosion by focusing on the significant information reflected in original data.
However, when confronted with more complex problems in practical applications such as statistical learning, wireless communications, financial economics and population genetics, Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) was proposed to identify and quantify the associations between two sets of variables.

Traditional multivariate analysis textbooks
[
9
,
10
,
14
,
1
]
use matrix language to deconstruct the CCA problem and draw a heuristic conclusion: the key quantity that reflects the correlation between two groups of variables is the characteristic space of the canonical correlation matrix. Specifically, for
p+q
dimensional general population
(\tilde{{\bf x}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bf y}}^{\prime})^{\prime}
with mean
\bf 0
, the covariance between
\tilde{{\bf x}}
and
\tilde{{\bf y}}
has the following expression

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}ar\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{{\bf x}}\\
\tilde{{\bf y}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{xx},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{xy}\\
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{yx},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{yy}\end{pmatrix}.

The aimed canonical correlation coefficients can be characterized as the square roots of the eigenvalues of the population canonical correlation (PCC) matrix
\mathcal{C}_{xy}=\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{xx}^{-1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{xy}\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{yy}^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{yx}\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{xx}^{-1/2}
. According to scale invariance, we can simply assume that the covariance matrix of the population
({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})^{\prime}
with mean
\bf 0
has the following structure:

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}ar\begin{pmatrix}{\bf x}\\
{\bf y}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bf I}_{p},&\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\\
\boldsymbol{\Lambda},&{\bf I}_{q}\end{pmatrix}.

For sample matrices
{\bf X}_{p\times n}
and
{\bf Y}_{q\times n}
from population
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
respectively, write
{\bf S}_{xx}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime}
,
{\bf S}_{yy}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime}
, and
{\bf S}_{xy}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf X}{\bf Y}^{\prime}
.
Then, we define the sample canonical correlation (SCC) matrix as
\mathcal{S}_{xy}={\bf S}_{xx}^{-1/2}{\bf S}_{xy}{\bf S}_{yy}^{-1}{\bf S}_{yx}{\bf S}_{xx}^{-1/2}
to spy on the information of population canonical correlations.

We pause to review some important studies on the spectrum of the SCC matrix.
Under the null hypothesis, i.e.,
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent, Yang and Pan established the LSD of SCC matrix
[
16
]
and the central limiting theorem(CLT) for linear spectral statistics (LSS)
[
17
]
. Under finite rank case of alternative hypothesis, i.e., the fixed rank of
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
, Bao et al.
[
6
]
gave the asymptotic distribution of spiked eigenvalues and extreme eigenvalues with Gaussian entries, then Yang and Ma showed the universality of the above results in
[
15
,
13
]
.
For the general alternative hypothesis, i.e., the arbitrary rank of
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
, Bai et al.
[
2
]
proposed a new way to transform the eigenvalues of the SCC matrix to the function of that of the noncentral Fisher matrix under Gaussian assumption. Then under the same condition, Hou et al.
[
11
]
gave the CLT for spiked eigenvalues of the SCC matrix via the corresponding results of noncentral Fisher matrix. Now we face the problem of asymptotic results for the SCC matrix under alternative hypothesis and general underlying distribution.

Noticed it is hard to extend the results to the data matrices with generally distributed entries using the idea in
[
2
]
, because it depended crucially on the equivalence of uncorrelation and independence of normal distribution. Essentially, the goal of CCA is to seek the relationship between the block
{\bf x}
and block
{\bf y}
in random vector
({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})^{\prime}
, which is the same as that of the block correlation matrix. Block correlation matrix dated back to Bao et al.
[
5
]
, which was developed by sample correlation matrix. Recently the results
[
7
]
about this concept are expanded to general cases, for example, no matter whether
k
is fixed or divergent, more mild conditions for dimensional size ratio.

Our approach adapts the same strategy as in
[
16
]
. The key innovation is to find the relationship between the SCC matrix and block correlation matrix and to get the exact expression for Stieltjes transform of the LSD of the SCC matrix. Now we focus on case
k=2
to calculate the relationship between the SCC matrix and block correlation matrix. We construct the block correlation matrix
{\bf B}
for sample matrices
{\bf X}_{p\times n}
and
{\bf Y}_{q\times n}
, where

\displaystyle{\bf B}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{0},&({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime},&{\bf X}{\bf Y}^{\prime}\\
{\bf Y}{\bf X}^{\prime},&{\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{0},&({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\bf I}_{p},&({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf X}{\bf Y}^{\prime}({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf Y}{\bf X}^{\prime}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}},&{\bf I}_{q}\end{pmatrix}.

The block correlation matrix presented above is a natural extension of the sample correlation matrix in block form.
Considering the Green function
G_{b}(z)=({\bf B}-z)^{-1}
of
{\bf B}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{b}(z)=\left[\begin{pmatrix}{\bf I}_{p},&({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf X}{\bf Y}^{\prime}({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf Y}{\bf X}^{\prime}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}},&{\bf I}_{q}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}z{\bf I}_{p},&\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{0},&z{\bf I}_{q}\end{pmatrix}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}(1-z){\bf I}_{p},&({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf X}{\bf Y}^{\prime}({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf Y}{\bf X}^{\prime}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}},&(1-z){\bf I}_{q}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}[(1\!-\!z){\bf I}_{p}\!-\!\frac{1}{1\!-\!z}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf X}{\bf P}_{y}{\bf X}^{\prime}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}]^{-1},&\ast\\
\ast,&[(1\!-\!z){\bf I}_{q}\!-\!\frac{1}{1\!-\!z}({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf Y}{\bf P}_{x}{\bf Y}^{\prime}({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}]^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-z)\begin{pmatrix}[(1\!-\!z)^{2}{\bf I}_{p}\!-\!({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf X}{\bf P}_{y}{\bf X}^{\prime}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}]^{-1},&\ast\ast\\
\ast\ast,&[(1\!-\!z)^{2}{\bf I}_{q}\!-\!({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf Y}{\bf P}_{x}{\bf Y}^{\prime}({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}]^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Taking trace for the matrix on both sides of the equation, that is

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}G_{b}=(z-1)[{\rm Tr}G_{xy}((1-z)^{2})+{\rm Tr}G_{yx}((1-z)^{2})],

where the function
\frac{1}{p}{\rm Tr}G_{xy}(z):=\frac{1}{p}{\rm Tr}(\mathcal{S}_{xy}-z)^{-1}
is the Stieltjes transform of the SCC matrix, and
\frac{1}{q}{\rm Tr}G_{yx}(z):=\frac{1}{q}{\rm Tr}(\mathcal{S}_{yx}-z)^{-1}
is similar. Notice that
\mathcal{S}_{xy}
and
\mathcal{S}_{yx}
share the same non-zero eigenvalues, so the limit of
\frac{1}{q}{\rm Tr}G_{xy}
under the alternative hypothesis can be converted to the limit of
\frac{1}{p+q}{\rm Tr}G_{b}
. This implies the spectral distributions of
{\bf B}
and the SCC matrix have some relationship at a global level.

Considering the correlation coefficients matrix
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
, we can assume the entries of
{\bf W}_{p\times n}
and
{\bf Y}_{q\times n}
are i.i.d., and

\displaystyle{\bf X}_{p\times n}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{p\times q}{\bf Y}_{q\times n}+\boldsymbol{\Gamma}{\bf W}_{p\times n},\ {\bf Y}_{q\times n}={\bf Y}_{q\times n}

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}={\bf I}_{p}-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\prime}
.
Because of the scale invariance in form (
1
), we replace
{\bf X},{\bf Y},{\bf W}
in (
1
) with the
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\bf X},\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\bf Y},\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\bf W}
, but still expressed as
{\bf X},{\bf Y},{\bf W}
, respectively.

Main Result

Theorem 1
.

Denote the ordered non-zero eigenvalues of
\mathcal{S}_{xy}
by
l_{1}^{2}\geq l_{2}^{2}\geq\dots\geq l_{\min\{p,q\}}^{2}
, then the
p+q

\displaystyle\{\underbrace{1+l_{1},\dots,1+l_{\min\{p,q\}}}_{\min\{p,q\}},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{\max\{p,q\}-\min\{p,q\}},\underbrace{1-l_{\min\{p,q\}},\dots,1-l_{1}}_{\min\{p,q\}}\}.

Moreover, at the global level, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}G_{b}=(z-1)[{\rm Tr}G_{xy}((1-z)^{2})+{\rm Tr}G_{yx}((1-z)^{2})].

Theorem 2
.

Under Assumptions
1
-
3
, we have the LSDs of the SCC matrix and its corresponding block correlation matrix are universal. Then Stieltjes transform of the LSD of the SCC matrix is the unique solution of the following equation set:

\displaystyle G_{1}(z,m)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2}(1-z)[(1-z)m-1]}{1-c_{2}+c_{1}[(1-z)m-1]}=\int\left[\frac{n}{q}\frac{x^{2}}{1-x^{2}}-\frac{G_{2}(z,m)}{G_{1}(z,m)}\right]^{-1}dH(x)

(2)

\displaystyle G_{1}(z,m)=

\displaystyle 1-c_{1}-c_{1}[(1-z)m-1]+\frac{c_{1}(1-c_{2})(1-z)[(1-z)m-1]}{1-c_{2}+c_{1}z[(1-z)m-1]}

\displaystyle G_{2}(z,m)=

\displaystyle\frac{1-c_{1}-c_{2}+c_{1}(1-z)m}{c_{2}(1-z)}\left[1-\frac{(1-z)(1-c_{1})}{1-c_{2}-c_{1}z+c_{1}zm(1-z)}\right].

Here we roughly introduce the proof strategy. Under normal case, we can transform the SCC matrix to noncentral Fisher matrix, and through the study of the noncentral Fisher matrix, we have the corresponding results for the SCC matrix. Intuitively, the result of the LSD should be universality, now the restriction is that the relationship does not work under non-Gaussian case. We choose another transformation which holds under the general case, that is, the block correlation matrix under some appropriate alternative hypothesis. Given the result of the normality in

]

{\bf B}
. Notice the non-zero eigenvalues of

equal that of
{\bf H}
, where

\displaystyle{\bf H}={\bf X}^{\prime}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\prime})^{-1}{\bf X}+{\bf Y}^{\prime}({\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\prime})^{-1}{\bf Y}:={\bf P}_{x}+{\bf P}_{y}.

So in subsequent sections, we mainly prove that the LSD of
{\bf H}
is universal.

3
Expectation convergence

In this section, we will show the difference of
{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}
(
{\rm tr}
means normalized trace as
\frac{1}{n}{\rm Tr}
) between Gaussian and general case in the sense of expectation. There are two random parts contained in matrix
{\bf H}
, then we substitute them step by step. Here the substitute method is not Lindeberg’s method but another continuous method widely used in
[
17
,
12
]
.
Write
{\bf W}(s)=s^{1/2}{\bf W}+(1-s)^{1/2}\hat{{\bf W}}
, where the matrix
\hat{{\bf W}}
with standard normal entries is independent with
{\bf W}
. Let
{\bf H}_{N}
and
{\bf H}(s)
denote the matrix in which
{\bf W}
is replaced by
\hat{{\bf W}}
and
{\bf W}(s)
respectively, but
{\bf Y}
is still under general distribution. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}-\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}_{N}-z)^{-1}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}(s)-z)^{-1}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}(s)-z)^{-2}\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{x(s)}}{\partial s}ds.

Similar to the derivative of a matrix by a scalar, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{x(s)}}{\partial s}=\frac{\partial[{\bf X}(s)]^{\prime}\left({\bf X}(s)[{\bf X}(s)]^{\prime}\right)^{-1}{\bf X}(s)}{\partial s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}({\bf I}-{\bf P}_{x(s)})\left(\frac{{\bf W}}{\sqrt{s}}\!-\!\frac{\hat{{\bf W}}}{\sqrt{1\!-\!s}}\right)^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}{\bf Q}(s)+\frac{1}{2}{\bf Q}(s)^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\left(\frac{{\bf W}}{\sqrt{s}}\!-\!\frac{\hat{{\bf W}}}{\sqrt{1\!-\!s}}\right)({\bf I}-{\bf P}_{x(s)})

where by definition
{\bf Q}(s)=({\bf X}(s){\bf X}(s)^{\prime})^{-1}{\bf X}(s)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}(s)-z)^{-2}\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{x(s)}}{\partial s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}\left(\frac{{\bf W}}{\sqrt{s}}\!-\!\frac{\hat{{\bf W}}}{\sqrt{1\!-\!s}}\right)^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}{\bf Q}(s)({\bf H}(s)-z)^{-2}({\bf I}-{\bf P}_{x(s)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}W_{ji}\Phi_{ji}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-s}}\sum_{ij}\hat{W}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}\right]

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{ji}:=[\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}{\bf Q}(s)({\bf H}(s)-z)^{-2}({\bf I}-{\bf P}_{x(s)})]_{ji}.

(3)

So

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}\!-\!z)^{-1}\!-\!\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}_{N}\!-\!z)^{-1}=\!-\!\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}W_{ji}\Phi_{ji}\!-\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{1\!-\!s}}\sum_{ij}\hat{W}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}\right]ds.

Then we need to calculate
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}W_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
and
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-s}}\sum_{ij}\hat{W}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
respectively. Here
\hat{W}_{ji}
is from standard normal distribution, so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{ij}\hat{W}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}\frac{\partial\Phi_{ji}}{\partial\hat{W}_{ji}}

exactly holds. By performing basic matrix calculus, we conclude the term with
\kappa_{2}
in
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}W_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
and
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-s}}\sum_{ij}\hat{W}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
are exactly same, see more details for Lemma
2
. So the terms with third and higher order cumulants of
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}W_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
are remained in their difference, next we bound it via the following formulas:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}W_{ji}\Phi_{ji}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-s}}\sum_{ij}\hat{W}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}e_{1}

where the term
e_{1}
results from cumulant expansion and

\displaystyle e_{1}=O(1)\mathbb{E}[|W_{ji}|^{3}I_{\{|W_{ji}|>t\}}]\sup_{W_{ji}\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{ji}}{\partial W^{2}_{ji}}\right|+O(1)\mathbb{E}|W_{ji}|^{3}\sup_{|W_{ji}|<t}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{ji}}{\partial W^{2}_{ji}}\right|.

Note that all terms in
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{ji}}{\partial W^{2}_{ji}}
can be expressed in terms of the following factors

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}({\bf X}(s){\bf X}(s)^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}]_{jj},[\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}{\bf Q}(s)G^{b}{\bf Q}^{\prime}(s)\boldsymbol{\Gamma}]_{jj},[G^{b}]_{ii},[{\bf P}_{x(s)}G^{b}]_{ii},

\displaystyle[{\bf P}_{x(s)}G^{b}{\bf P}_{x(s)}]_{ii},[\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}{\bf Q}(s)G^{b}]_{ji},[\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}{\bf Q}(s)G^{b}{\bf P}_{x(s)}]_{ji}

where
b=0,1,2
and
G=({\bf H}(s)-z)^{-1}
(we omit
s
without aumbiguity).

According to the Assumption
2
, we have
\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\|
is bounded. For any fixed
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
,
\|G(z)\|
has the trivial bound
\frac{1}{v}
. Here we need Lemma
3
to bound
\|{\bf Q}\|
with high probability. Meanwhile from the moment assumption on matrix entries, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|W_{ji}|^{3}I_{\{|W_{ji}|>t\}}]=o\left(\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\right)\mbox{ and }\mathbb{E}|W_{ji}|^{3}=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\right).

Considering there is at least one off-diagonal element in term
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{ji}}{\partial W^{2}_{ji}}
, and choosing one of off-diagonal element denoted

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}[|W_{ji}|^{3}I_{\{|W_{ji}|>t\}}]\sup_{W_{ji}\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{ji}}{\partial W^{2}_{ji}}\right|=\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|o(\frac{1}{n^{3/2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|^{2}}\sqrt{np}\times o(\frac{1}{n^{3/2}})=o(\frac{1}{n}),

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}|W_{ji}|^{3}\sup_{|W_{ji}|<t}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{ji}}{\partial W^{2}_{ji}}\right|=\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|O(\frac{1}{n^{3/2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|^{2}}\sqrt{np}\times O(\frac{1}{n^{3/2}})=O(\frac{1}{n}).

This implies
\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}-\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}_{N}-z)^{-1}=O(\frac{1}{n})
.
Now we need to confirm that after replacing
{\bf Y}
with
\hat{{\bf Y}}
, whose elements are with standard normal distribution, the limit of the quality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}-\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}_{N}-z)^{-1}=-\int_{0}^{1}O(\frac{1}{n})ds=O(\frac{1}{n}),

where the notion
{\bf H}
is the matrix in which
{\bf W}
with Gaussian case and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}-\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}_{N}-z)^{-1}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}(t)-z)^{-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}(t)-z)^{-2}\left[\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{x(t)}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{y(t)}}{\partial t}\right]dt.

Set

\displaystyle{\bf Y}(t)=\sqrt{t}{\bf Y}+\sqrt{1-t}\hat{{\bf Y}},\ {\bf X}(t)=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}{\bf Y}(t)+\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\hat{{\bf W}},\ {\bf U}(t)=({\bf Y}(t){\bf Y}^{\prime}(t))^{-1}{\bf Y}(t),

by the derivative calculation of a matrix by a scalar, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{x(t)}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial{\bf X}^{\prime}(t)({\bf X}(t){\bf X}^{\prime}(t))^{-1}{\bf X}(t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bf I}\!-\!{\bf P}_{x(t)})\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}{\bf Y}\!-\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{1\!-\!t}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\hat{{\bf Y}}\right)^{\prime}{\bf Q}(t)\!+\!\frac{1}{2}{\bf Q}(t)^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}{\bf Y}\!-\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{1\!-\!t}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\hat{{\bf Y}}\right)({\bf I}\!-\!{\bf P}_{x(t)})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{y(t)}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial{\bf Y}^{\prime}(t)({\bf Y}(t){\bf Y}^{\prime}(t))^{-1}{\bf Y}(t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}({\bf I}-{\bf P}_{y(t)})\left(\frac{{\bf Y}}{\sqrt{t}}\!-\!\frac{\hat{{\bf Y}}}{\sqrt{1\!-\!t}}\right)^{\prime}{\bf U}(t)+\frac{1}{2}{\bf U}(t)^{\prime}\left(\frac{{\bf Y}}{\sqrt{t}}\!-\!\frac{\hat{{\bf Y}}}{\sqrt{1\!-\!t}}\right)({\bf I}-{\bf P}_{y(t)}).

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}(t)-zI)^{-2}\left[\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{x(t)}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{y(t)}}{\partial t}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{ij}Y_{ji}\Psi_{ji}^{l}\!-\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}\sum_{ij}\hat{Y}_{ji}\Psi_{ji}^{l}\right]

,

Similarly, we need to calculate
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{ij}Y_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}
and
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}\sum_{ij}\hat{Y}_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}
respectively. Here
\hat{Y}_{ji}
is standard normal distribution, so we have exactly

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{ij}\hat{Y}_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}\frac{\partial\Psi^{l}_{ji}}{\partial\hat{Y}_{ji}}.

From the arguments in Lemma
4
, we conclude the term with
\kappa_{2}
in
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{ij}Y_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}
and
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}\sum_{ij}\hat{Y}_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}
are exactly same for

. So we need to calculate and bound the terms with third and later cumulants of
\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{ij}Y_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{ij}Y_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1\!-\!t}}\sum_{ij}\hat{Y}_{ji}\Psi^{l}_{ji}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}e_{2}^{l},

\displaystyle e_{2}^{l}=O(1)\mathbb{E}[|Y_{ji}|^{3}I_{\{|Y_{ji}|>t\}}]\sup_{Y_{ji}\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi^{l}_{ji}}{\partial Y^{2}_{ji}}\right|+O(1)\mathbb{E}|Y_{ji}|^{3}\sup_{|Y_{ji}|<t}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi^{l}_{ji}}{\partial Y^{2}_{ji}}\right|,

where the term
e_{2}^{l}
results from cumulant expansion.

We notice that the bound of
\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi^{l}_{ji}}{\partial Y^{2}_{ji}}\right|
is only related with its property (at least one off-diagonal element factor), not with the specific form. Similarly,

\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}e_{2}^{l}=O(\frac{1}{n}).

4
Martingale difference decomposition

Define
\mathbb{E}_{k}=\mathbb{E}(\cdot|w_{1},y_{1},\dots,w_{k},y_{k})
, then we have

\displaystyle{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}-\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1}){\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1})[{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}-{\rm tr}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-1}],

where

\displaystyle{\bf H}^{(k)}={\bf P}_{x}^{(k)}+{\bf P}_{y}^{(k)}=({\bf X}^{(k)})^{\prime}[{\bf X}^{(k)}({\bf X}^{(k)})^{\prime}]^{-1}{\bf X}^{(k)}+({\bf Y}^{(k)})^{\prime}[{\bf Y}^{(k)}({\bf Y}^{(k)})^{\prime}]^{-1}{\bf Y}^{(k)},

and

\displaystyle{\bf X}={\bf X}^{(k)}+x_{k}e_{k}^{\prime},\ {\bf Y}={\bf Y}^{(k)}+y_{k}e_{k}^{\prime},\ {\bf X}^{(k)}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}{\bf Y}^{(k)}+\boldsymbol{\Gamma}{\bf W}^{(k)}.

By the calculation in Lemma
5
, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\bf H}-{\bf H}^{(k)}={\bf P}_{x}-{\bf P}_{x}^{(k)}+{\bf P}_{y}-{\bf P}_{y}^{(k)}=\mbox{the sum of 8 rank 1 matrices}:=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}.

Let
(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k})_{n\times 8}
,
(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k})_{8\times n}
denote the sets of column, row vector respectively, see Lemma
5
for more details. And by the formula (0.7.4.1) of
[
8
]
, we have

\displaystyle({\bf H}\!-\!z)^{-1}\!-\!({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-1}=\!-\!({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}({\bf I}_{8}\!+\!\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-1}.

There exists a deterministic matrix
{\bf M}
w.r.t. random vectors
y_{k},w_{k}
, which approximates
{\bf I}_{8}+\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}
in the expectation sense by Lemma
6
.
For this matrix with fixed dimension, we have

\displaystyle{\bf I}_{8}\!+\!\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}\!=\!{\bf M}\!+\!\tilde{{\bf M}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad({\bf I}_{8}\!+\!\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k})^{-1}={\bf M}^{-1}\!+\!\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}

where
\mathbb{E}|\tilde{{\bf M}}_{ij}|^{8}=O(\frac{1}{n})
and
\mathbb{E}|(\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}})_{ij}|^{4}=O(\frac{1}{n})
.
Then martingale decomposition term becomes

\displaystyle{\rm tr}({\bf H}\!-\!z)^{-1}\!-\!{\rm tr}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!-\!\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}{\bf M}_{ij}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\!+\!\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{ij}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}.

First we consider the remainder term

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{ij}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\right|>\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{ij}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|(\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{ij}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left|\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{ij}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}=1}^{8}\left|\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{ij}\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right|^{2}n^{2\delta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{n^{2\delta}}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}=1}^{8}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{ij}|^{4}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{{\bf M}^{-1}}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}|^{4}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(1-\delta)}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{n^{1+2\delta}}\right)<\infty,

where the first two inequalities are due to Chebyshev’s inequality and Burkholder inequality, respectively, and we define the event

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\!=\!\{\mbox{for any }i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},|\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!zI)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}|<n^{\delta}\ \mbox{and }|\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j^{\prime}}({\bf H}^{(k)}\!-\!z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i^{\prime}}|<n^{\delta}\}.

The specific forms of

include

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}_{k}G^{2}\chi_{k},\ \omega^{\prime}_{k}G^{2}\omega_{k},\ \chi^{\prime}_{k}G^{2}\omega_{k},\ e^{\prime}_{k}G^{2}\chi_{k},\ e^{\prime}_{k}G^{2}\omega_{k},\ e^{\prime}_{k}G^{2}e_{k}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the formula (2.3) of
[
3
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{c})\leq\frac{8^{4}}{n^{2\delta}}\mathbb{E}\left|\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j^{\prime}}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i^{\prime}}\right|^{2}=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2\delta}}\right).

The remainder term can be ignorable in the sense of almost sure. Now we consider the main term

\displaystyle\delta_{k}=\frac{1}{n}(\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1})\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}{\bf M}_{ij}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}(\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1})\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}{\bf M}_{ij}^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}-{\rm tr}({\bf H}^{(k)}-z)^{-2}\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k,j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{k}\right|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{k}\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left|\delta_{k}\right|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)<\infty.

The first two inequality are due to Markov inequality and Burkholder inequality, respectively, and the next equality is due to the fact
\mathbb{E}|\delta_{k}|^{2}=O(\frac{1}{n^{3}})
. By Borel-Cantelli Lemma, we have
{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}-\mathbb{E}{\rm tr}({\bf H}-z)^{-1}\overset{a.s.}{\to}0.

Lemma 1

Fluids in random media and dimensional augmentation
John Cardy

Abstract
We propose a solution to the puzzle of dimensional reduction in the random field Ising model, asking: to what random problem in D = d + 2 D=d+2 dimensions does a pure system in d d dimensions correspond? For a continuum binary fluid and an Ising lattice gas, we prove that the mean density and other observables equal those of a similar model in D D dimensions, but with infinite range interactions and correlated disorder in the extra two dimensions. There is no conflict with rigorous results that the finite range model orders in D = 3 D=3 . Our arguments avoid the use of replicas and perturbative field theory, being based on convergent cluster expansions, which, for the lattice gas, may be extended to the critical point by the Lee-Yang theorem. Although our results may be derived using supersymmetry, they follow more directly from the matrix-tree theorem.

Statement of the main result.

The mean densities of a non-random binary fluid, or of a lattice gas, in

-(1/\pi{l}^{2})V^{\prime}(r,r^{\prime};\rho^{2}/{l}^{2})

In fact

Discussion.

We have given a concrete answer to the question posed at the beginning: a non-random fluid in
d
dimensions with finite range 2-body potentials is precisely equivalent to one in a quenched random medium in
d+2
dimensions, in the limit when the range
{l}
of the interactions and correlated randomness in the two extra dimensions tends to infinity. It is not precisely equivalent to a fluid with only finite range interactions and impurity correlations, to which the arguments
im
;
imb
that
D_{l}=2
apply.

However the present study does not immediately shed light on why dimensional reduction fails for
D<5
in short range models.
SUSY implies dimensional reduction even non-perturbatively

, so presumably interactions which break it explicitly should become relevant at the SUSY fixed point, as concluded in Ref.
krt

{l}\to\infty
limit, but also through an explicit fine-tuning between the random field covariance
\propto-V
and the interaction potential
\propto-V^{\prime}
.

The relation of the first with the earlier field theory approaches may be seen in the
D
-dimensional version of (
3

\rho\to{l}\rho
and
V\to{l}^{2}
, which modifies the measure to
e^{-{l}^{2}{\cal H}}

{l}\to\infty
then formally leads to the classical equations, the tree Feynman diagrams

and conjectured Parisi-Sourlas supersymmetry and dimensional reduction. In fact
{l}^{-2}
may be identified as the dangerously irrelevant variable in that approach, which is responsible for the breaking of hyperscaling. It is difficult to see how such a mechanism could fail as
D
is lowered. More likely is that the breaking of fine-tuning becomes relevant. It would be interesting to carry out numerical simulations on finite-range models in lower dimensions with fine-tuning of the random field covariance.

Finally, we have pointed out that dimensional shift by two, which may be seen as a consequence of supersymmetry

;
ps2

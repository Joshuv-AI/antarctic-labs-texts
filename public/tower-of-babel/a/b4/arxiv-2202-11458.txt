Initial data on big bang singularities in symmetric settings
Hans Ringström

Abstract
. In a recent article, we propose a general geometric notion of initial data on big bang singularities. This notion is of interest in its own right. However, it also serves the purpose of giving a unified perspective on many of the results in the literature. In the present article, we give a partial justification of this statement by rephrasing the results concerning Bianchi class A orthogonal stiff solutions and solutions in the 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} -Gowdy symmetric vacuum setting in terms of our general geometric notion of initial data on the big bang singularity.

1.1.
Quiescent initial data on the singularity

In the case of the Einstein-scalar field equations with a cosmological constant
\Lambda
,
quiescent initial data on the singularity take the form
(\bar{M},\bar{h},\mathscr{K},\Phi_{a},\Phi_{b})
. Here
\bar{M}
is a
3
-dimensional manifold,
\bar{h}
is a
Riemannian metric on
\bar{M}
,
\mathscr{K}
is a
(1,1)
-tensor field on
\bar{M}
and
\Phi_{a}
and
\Phi_{b}
are scalar functions on
\bar{M}
. We think of
\mathscr{K}

as an endomorphism of the tangent bundle of
\bar{M}
. Assume the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
to be real and distinct, denote them by
p_{A}
and assume them to be
ordered so that
p_{1}<p_{2}<p_{3}
. Let
\{\mathscr{X}_{A}\}
be a corresponding local frame of eigenvector fields of
\mathscr{K}
; i.e.,
\mathscr{K}\mathscr{X}_{A}=p_{A}\mathscr{X}_{A}

(no summation). Assume, moreover,
\{\mathscr{X}_{A}\}
to be unit vector fields with respect to
\bar{h}
. These conditions define the local vector fields

\mathscr{X}_{A}
up to a sign. This means that the functions
\gamma^{A}_{BC}
, given by
[\mathscr{X}_{B},\mathscr{X}_{C}]=\gamma^{A}_{BC}\mathscr{X}_{A}
, are well defined up to a sign.
Introducing
\mathscr{O}^{A}_{BC}:=(\gamma^{A}_{BC})^{2}
, it is thus clear that
\mathscr{O}^{A}_{BC}
are globally well defined smooth functions on
\bar{M}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
(\bar{M},\bar{h})
be a smooth
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
\mathscr{K}
be a smooth
(1,1)
-tensor field on

\bar{M}
and
\Phi_{a}
and
\Phi_{b}
be smooth functions on
\bar{M}
. Then
(\bar{M},\bar{h},\mathscr{K},\Phi_{a},\Phi_{b})
are

non-degenerate quiescent initial data on the singularity for the Einstein-scalar field equations with a cosmological constant
\Lambda

if, with notation as above,

(1)

\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}=1
and
\mathscr{K}
is symmetric with respect to
\bar{h}
.

(2)

\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}^{2}+\Phi_{a}^{2}=1
and
\mathrm{div}_{\bar{h}}\mathscr{K}=\Phi_{a}d\Phi_{b}
.

(3)

The eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
are distinct.

(4)

\mathscr{O}^{k}_{ij}
vanishes in a neighbourhood of
\bar{x}\in\bar{M}
if
p_{k}(\bar{x})\leq 0
and
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
.

Remark 2
.

That
\mathscr{K}
is symmetric with respect to
\bar{h}
means that if
p\in\bar{M}
and
\xi,\zeta\in T_{p}\bar{M}
, then
\bar{h}(\mathscr{K}\xi,\zeta)=\bar{h}(\xi,\mathscr{K}\zeta)
.
This condition implies that the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
are real.

Remark 3
.

The definition is a special case of
[
28
, Definition 10]
which holds for spacetime dimensions
d+1
,
d\geq 3
.

1.2.
Developments corresponding to data on the singularity

Given non-degenerate quiescent initial data on the singularity as in Definition
1
, the goal is to demonstrate that there is a unique
corresponding maximal globally hyperbolic development, say
(M,g,\phi)
. Exactly how the data on the singularity correspond to a solution depends
on the choice of foliation of the relevant spacetime. Here we focus on the case of foliations with a vanishing shift vector field and uniformly
diverging mean curvature, though constructing, e.g., Gaussian foliations is also a possibility (cf.
[
28
]
for a discussion of this case).
The goal is to prove that there is a unique maximal
globally hyperbolic development, say
(M,g,\phi)
, solving the Einstein-scalar field equations with a cosmological constant
\Lambda
such that the
following holds. There is a
0<t_{+}\in\mathbb{R}
and a diffeomorphism
\Psi
from
\bar{M}\times(0,t_{+})
to an open subset of
(M,g)
such that

\Psi^{*}g
can locally be represented as

(1)

(\Psi^{*}g)=-N^{2}dt\otimes dt+\textstyle{\sum}_{A,B}b_{AB}\theta^{-2p_{\max\{A,B\}}}\mathscr{Y}^{A}\otimes\mathscr{Y}^{B},

where
N
is a strictly positive function (the
lapse function
),
\theta
is the mean curvature of the leaves of the foliation,
\{\mathscr{X}_{A}\}

is a local basis of eigenvector fields of
\mathscr{K}
and
\{\mathscr{Y}^{A}\}
is the dual basis. Moreover,
\mathscr{X}_{A}
corresponds to the eigenvalue
p_{A}
and
the
p_{A}
are ordered so that
p_{1}<p_{2}<p_{3}
. Here, we require
\theta
to diverge to infinity uniformly as
t\rightarrow 0+
. Let
\bar{K}
be the
Weingarten map of the leaves of the foliation (i.e., the second fundamental form with one index raised by the induced metric);
\mathcal{K}:=\bar{K}/\theta
be
the
expansion normalised Weingarten map
; and
\check{h}
be defined by

\check{h}:=\textstyle{\sum}_{A,B}b_{AB}\mathscr{Y}^{A}\otimes\mathscr{Y}^{B};

note that
\check{h}
is globally well defined independent of the choice of local frame. Then we require the following correspondence between
the solution and the data on the singularity

(2)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\mathcal{K}=\mathscr{K},\ \ \ \lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\check{h}=\bar{h},\ \ \ \lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}(\hat{U}\phi)=\Phi_{a},\ \ \ \lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}(\phi+\Phi_{a}\ln\theta)=\Phi_{b},

where
\hat{U}:=\hat{N}^{-1}\partial_{t}
and
\hat{N}:=\theta N
. In practice, the exact function space in which this convergence takes place should be specified.
Moreover, in certain circumstances it might be natural to require a particular rate of convergence; cf.
[
28
]
for a more detailed discussion.
It might also be necessary to impose additional conditions on the foliation in order to ensure existence and uniqueness.

1.3.
Comments on the conditions

It is of interest to comment on the conditions appearing in Definition
1
. We do so in the form of the following remarks.

Remark 4
.

The condition that
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}=1
arises from (
2
) and the fact that
\mathrm{tr}\mathcal{K}=1
by definition. The conditions

\mathrm{div}_{\bar{h}}\mathscr{K}=\Phi_{a}d\Phi_{b}
and
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}^{2}+\Phi_{a}^{2}=1
are what remains of the momentum and Hamiltonian constraints
respectively.

Remark 5
.

In terms of
\Phi_{a}
and the
p_{A}
, the conditions
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}=1
and
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}^{2}+\Phi_{a}^{2}=1
translate to

(3)

\textstyle{\sum}_{A}p_{A}=1,\ \ \ \textstyle{\sum}_{A}p_{A}^{2}+\Phi_{a}^{2}=1

respectively. In vacuum, these conditions correspond to the requirement that the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
satisfy the Kasner relations.

Remark 6
.

Due to the framework developed in
[
27
]
, we expect the last criterion in Definition
1
to be necessary in order to
obtain quiescence. However, if
p_{1}>0
, this condition is void.

Remark 7
.

In general, there is not a global frame of eigenvector fields of
\mathscr{K}
. However, by going to a finite covering space (of
\bar{M}
), if necessary,
the frame can be assumed to be global; cf.
[
26
, Lemma A.1, p. 201]
. In that setting, the equality (
1
)
holds globally.

Remark 8
.

The requirement of non-degeneracy is unfortunate. However, in the case of Gaussian foliations, it might be possible to avoid it;
cf.
[
28
, Remark 26]
.

1.4.
Spatially homogeneous setting

It is of interest to interpret the results in the spatially homogeneous setting in the light of the above notion of initial data on the singularity.

Regular initial data.
In the
3+1
-dimensional spatially homogeneous setting, there are three cases to consider: left
invariant initial data on a unimodular Lie group (Bianchi class A); left invariant initial data on a non-unimodular Lie group (Bianchi class B); and
initial data on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
invariant under the isometry group of the standard Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
(Kantowski-Sachs).
Here we focus solely on Bianchi class A with orthogonal perfect fluid matter. To be more precise, the corresponding initial data are defined as follows
(cf.
[
25
, Definition 9, p. 607]
):

Definition 9
.

Bianchi class A orthogonal perfect fluid initial data
for Einstein’s equations consist of the following: a connected
3
-dimensional
unimodular Lie group
G
; a left invariant metric
\bar{g}
on
G
; a left invariant symmetric covariant
2
-tensor field
\bar{k}
on
G
; and a constant

\rho_{0}\geq 0
satisfying

\displaystyle\bar{S}-\bar{k}^{ij}\bar{k}_{ij}+(\mathrm{tr}_{\bar{g}}\bar{k})^{2}=

\displaystyle 2\rho_{0}

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{i}\mathrm{tr}_{\bar{g}}\bar{k}-\overline{\nabla}^{j}\bar{k}_{ij}=

\displaystyle 0.

Developments.
Given Bianchi class A orthogonal perfect fluid initial data for Einstein’s equations and a constant
\gamma\in(2/3,2]
, there
is a corresponding Bianchi class A development with equation of state
p=(\gamma-1)\rho
; cf.
[
21
, Definition 21.1, p. 489]
. The metric
takes the form

(4)

g=-dt\otimes dt+\textstyle{\sum}_{i}a_{i}^{2}(t)\xi^{i}\otimes\xi^{j}

on
G\times I
, where
I
is an open interval. Moreover, the stress energy tensor of the orthogonal perfect fluid takes the form

T:=(\rho+p)dt\otimes dt+pg.

In the above expressions,
\rho
denotes the
energy density
and
p
the
pressure
;

a_{i}
are smooth positive functions of
t
;
\{e_{i}\}
is a basis of the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
of
G
;
\{\xi^{i}\}
is the dual basis of
\{e_{i}\}
; and there are constants
n_{k}
such that

(5)

[e_{i},e_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}n_{k}e_{k}

(no summation on
k
). The constants
n_{k}
correspond to a classification of the Lie algebras: all
n_{i}=0
(Bianchi type I); all but one of the

n_{i}=0
(Bianchi type II); two
n_{i}
non-zero, different signs (Bianchi type VI
0
); two
n_{i}
non-zero, same signs (Bianchi type
VII
0
); all
n_{i}
non-zero, not all same sign (Bianchi type VIII); all
n_{i}
non-zero, all have the same sign (Bianchi type IX).

The above developments are globally hyperbolic; cf.
[
21
, Lemma 21.4, p. 490]
. Moreover, in
case
\rho_{0}
appearing in Definition
9
is non-zero, the spacetime Ricci tensor, contracted with itself, diverges in
directions that are not geodesically complete; cf.
[
21
, Lemma 22.3, p. 497]
. This means that the developments are

C^{2}
-inextendible if
\rho_{0}\neq 0
. In particular, they are the maximal globally hyperbolic developments of the initial data. In case

\rho_{0}=0
, then, for a given time direction, either the mean curvature diverges or the spacetime is geodesically complete. This means that
the corresponding developments are inextendible as globally hyperbolic developments; cf.
[
24
, Proposition 18.16, p. 203]
. For a complete
picture in the vacuum setting, see
[
24
, Theorem 24.12, p. 258]
.

Correspondence between orthogonal stiff fluids and massless scalar fields in the spatially homogeneous setting.
The orthogonal perfect
fluids considered above include
dust
(
\gamma=1
),
radiation
(
\gamma=4/3
) and
stiff fluids
(
\gamma=2
). In what follows, it is of
interest to keep in mind that a stiff fluid can be interpreted as a massless scalar field and vice versa. To be more precise, let
\phi
be a massless
scalar field on
(M,g)
, where
g
is of the form (
4
) and
M:=G\times I
, and assume that
\phi
only depends on
t
. Then,
defining
\rho=\dot{\phi}^{2}/2
, the stress energy tensor associated with
\phi
can be interpreted as the stress energy tensor of an orthogonal
stiff fluid. Alternately, given an orthogonal stiff fluid solution with non-vanishing energy density
\rho
(note that if
\rho=0
at one point in time,

\rho=0
for all
t
), we can define a scalar field by integrating the relation
\phi_{t}=(2\rho)^{1/2}
.

Data on the singularity in the Bianchi class A setting.
Due to the above equivalence between massless scalar fields and orthogonal stiff fluids,
and since the results to which we appeal are stated in the stiff fluid setting, we use the following formulation of data on the singularity
in the Bianchi class A setting with a vanishing cosmological constant.

Definition 10
.

Let
G
be a
3
-dimensional unimodular Lie group,
\bar{h}
be a left invariant Riemannian metric on
G
,
\mathscr{K}
be a left invariant

(1,1)
-tensor field on
G
and
\omega
be a non-negative real number. Then
(G,\bar{h},\mathscr{K},\omega)
are

non-degenerate quiescent Bianchi class A initial data on the singularity for the Einstein-orthogonal stiff fluid equations
if

(1)

\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}=1
and
\mathscr{K}
is symmetric with respect to
\bar{h}
,

(2)

\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}^{2}+2\omega/3=1
and
\mathrm{div}_{\bar{h}}\mathscr{K}=0
,

(3)

the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
are distinct,

(4)

\mathscr{O}^{k}_{ij}=0
if
p_{k}\leq 0
, where
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
.

Remark 11
.

Here
p_{A}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
, and they are constants due to the left invariance of
\mathscr{K}
.
In case
\omega=0
, we speak of non-degenerate quiescent Bianchi class A initial data on the singularity for the Einstein vacuum equations.

Before discussing the existence of initial data on the singularity, as well as the existence and uniqueness of corresponding developments, it is
useful to rephrase the conditions. To begin with, due to
[
24
, Corollary 19.14, p. 211]
, the conditions that
\mathscr{K}
is symmetric with
respect to
\bar{h}
and that
\mathrm{div}_{\bar{h}}\mathscr{K}=0
are equivalent to the existence of a basis
\{e_{i}\}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that

(6)

\bar{h}(e_{i},e_{j})=\delta_{ij},\ \ \ \mathscr{K}e_{i}=p_{i}e_{i},\ \ \ [e_{i},e_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}n_{k}e_{k},

where there is no summation on
i
in the second equality, no summation on
k
in the third equality,
\epsilon_{123}=1
and
\epsilon_{ijk}
is antisymmetric
under permutations of the indices. Here
p_{i}
and
n_{k}
are constants. Note that the
p_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
. Introducing

p_{\pm}
by

p_{+}:=\frac{3}{2}\left(p_{2}+p_{3}-\frac{2}{3}\right),\ \ \ p_{-}:=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(p_{2}-p_{3}),

then the relation
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}=1
is automatically satisfied and the relation
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}^{2}+2\omega/3=1
can be rephrased

\omega+p_{+}^{2}+p_{-}^{2}=1.

Finally, the fourth condition can be rephrased as saying that
n_{k}=0
if
p_{k}\leq 0
. Note that one particular consequence of the last
observation is that there are only initial data on the singularity of Bianchi types VIII and IX in case all the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{K}
are
strictly positive. Since this is not possible for
\omega=0
, we conclude that for Bianchi types VIII and IX, there are no non-degenerate
quiescent Bianchi class A initial data on the singularity for the Einstein vacuum equations. This corresponds to the fact that all Bianchi
type VIII and IX vacuum solutions that are not locally rotationally symmetric have oscillatory singularities; cf.
[
20
]
for details.

Consider Bianchi class A orthogonal stiff fluid initial data and let
(M,g,\rho)
be the corresponding orthogonal stiff fluid development, where

M:=G\times I
,

g
takes the form (
4
) and
\rho
is the energy density. In the vacuum setting, assume that the development is neither of Bianchi
type VIII nor IX; that it is not a quotient of Minkowski space; and that it does not admit an extension through a Cauchy horizon. Then, given an
appropriate time orientation, the mean curvature of the hypersurfaces of spatial homogeneity, say
\theta

\infty

0

\infty

-\infty

t_{+}\leq\infty

t

0

\theta>0

\Omega=3\rho/\theta^{2}
and the expansion normalised Weingarten map associated with
g

\mathcal{K}

Under the above assumptions,
\Omega
and
\mathcal{K}
converge to limits, say
\omega
and
\mathscr{K}
respectively, as
t\rightarrow 0+
. These limits are such
that
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}=1
and
\mathrm{tr}\mathscr{K}^{2}+2\omega/3=1

\{e_{i}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}

1.5.
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-Gowdy symmetric vacuum spacetimes

As a first step beyond the spatially homogeneous setting, it is natural to consider
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-Gowdy symmetric vacuum spacetimes. For the purposes of the
present discussion,
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-Gowdy metrics are taken to be of the form

(11)

g=t^{-1/2}e^{\lambda/2}(-dt^{2}+d\vartheta^{2})+te^{P}(dx+Qdy)^{2}+te^{-P}dy^{2},

where
\lambda
,

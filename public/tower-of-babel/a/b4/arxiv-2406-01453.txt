Abstract
. The Cauchy-Szegő singular integral is a fundamental tool in the study of holomorphic H p H^{p} Hardy space. But for a kind of Siegel domains, the Cauchy-Szegő kernels are neither product ones nor flag ones on the Shilov boundaries, which have the structure of nilpotent Lie groups 𝒩 \mathscr{N} of step two. We use the lifting method to investigate flag-like singular integrals on 𝒩 \mathscr{N} , which includes these Cauchy-Szegő ones as a special case.The lifting group is the product 𝒩 ~ \tilde{\mathscr{N}} of three Heisenberg groups, and naturally geometric or analytical objects on 𝒩 \mathscr{N} are the projection of those on 𝒩 ~ \tilde{\mathscr{N}} . As in the flag case, we introduce various notions on 𝒩 \mathscr{N} adapted to geometric feature of these kernels, such as tubes, nontangential regions, tube maximal functions, Littlewood-Paley functions, tents, shards and atoms etc. They have the feature of tri-parameters, although the second step of the group 𝒩 \mathscr{N} is only 2 2 -dimensional, i.e. there exists a hidden parameter as in the flag case. We also establish the corresponding Calderón reproducing formula, characterization of L p ​ ( 𝒩 ) L^{p}(\mathscr{N}) by Littlewood-Paley functions, L p L^{p} -boundedness of tube maximal functions and flag-like singular integrals and atomic decomposition of H 1 H^{1} Hardy space on 𝒩 {\mathscr{N}} .

A

[
29

2.
Lifting and transference

2.1.
The lifting group
\tilde{\mathscr{N}}

We write a point of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
as
\mathbf{g}_{\mu}:=(\mathbf{z}_{\mu},t_{\mu})
with

t_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
and

(2.1)

\mathbf{z}_{\mu}=({z}_{\mu 1},\ldots,{z}_{\mu n_{\mu}})\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mu}},\qquad{\rm where}\qquad{z}_{\mu j}=x_{\mu j}+\mathbf{i}x_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)},

j=1,\ldots,n_{\mu}
. Since

{\rm Im}\langle\mathbf{z}_{\mu},\mathbf{z}_{\mu}^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mu}}(x_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}x^{\prime}_{\mu j}-x_{\mu j}x^{\prime}_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)})
,

(2.2)

\begin{split}Y_{\mu j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu j}}+2x_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{\mu}},\qquad\qquad Y_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}=\frac{\partial\hskip 22.76219pt}{\partial x_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}}-2x_{\mu j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{\mu}},\end{split}

j=1,\ldots,n_{\mu}
, are left invariant vector fields on the Heisenberg group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
for
\mu=1,2,3
. Then

(2.3)

\left[Y_{\mu j},Y_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}\right]=-4\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{\mu}},

and all other brackets vanish. The
sub-Laplacian
on the Heisenberg group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
is

\widetilde{{\triangle}}_{\mu}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{2n_{\mu}}Y_{\mu j}^{2}.
The dilation of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
is denoted as

\delta_{a}(\mathbf{z}_{\mu},t_{\mu})=(a\mathbf{z}_{\mu},a^{2}t_{\mu})
for
a>0
. Sometimes we write it briefly as
a(\mathbf{z}_{\mu},t_{\mu})
.

We will use the following
pseudodistance
on the Heisenberg group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
:

d_{\infty}(\mathbf{h}_{\mu},\mathbf{g}_{\mu})=\|\mathbf{h}_{\mu}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{\mu}\|_{\infty}

where
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is given by

\|(\mathbf{z}_{\mu},t_{\mu})\|_{\infty}=\max\{|x_{\mu 1}|,\ldots,|x_{\mu(2n_{\mu})}|,|t_{\mu}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
for
\mathbf{z}_{\mu}
given by (
2.1
).
Balls on the Heisenberg group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
are
{B}_{\mu}(\mathbf{g}_{\mu},r_{\mu}):=\{{\mathbf{h}}_{\mu}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu};\|{\mathbf{h}}_{\mu}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{\mu}\|_{\infty}<{r}_{\mu}\}
for some
\mathbf{g}_{\mu}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
and
{r}_{\mu}>0
. If denote by
\Box_{a}^{(\mu)}

the cube

(-a,a)^{2n_{\mu}}:=\{\mathbf{z}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mu}};|{x}_{\mu j}|<a\}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mu}}
,
and by
I_{b}
the interval
(-b,b)
, then

{B}_{\mu}(\mathbf{0}_{\mu},r_{\mu})=\Box_{r_{\mu}}^{(\mu)}\times I_{r_{\mu}^{2}}

is a cuboid, where
\mathbf{0}_{\mu}
is the origin of the Heisenberg group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
.
Let
\tau_{\mu}:{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}\rightarrow{\mathscr{N}}
be the (embedding) homomorphism from the Heisenberg group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
(
\mu=1,2,3
) into
\mathscr{N}
:

(2.4)

\begin{split}\tau_{1}(\mathbf{z}_{1},t_{1})&:=(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{0}_{2},\mathbf{0}_{3},t_{1},0),\\
\tau_{2}(\mathbf{z}_{2},t_{2})&:=(\mathbf{0}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2},\mathbf{0}_{3},0,t_{2}),\\
\tau_{3}(\mathbf{z}_{3},t_{3})&:=(\mathbf{0}_{1},\mathbf{0}_{2},\mathbf{z}_{3},t_{3},t_{3}).\end{split}

Proposition 2.1
.

(1)
\pi
in (
1.11
) is a homomorphism of groups.

(2)
\tau_{\mu}({\mathscr{H}}_{\mu})
commutes with
\tau_{\nu}({\mathscr{H}}_{\nu})
for
\mu\neq\nu
.

Proof.

(1) It is direct to see
\pi(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u})\pi(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})=\pi((\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u})(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}))
by the multiplication laws of
\mathscr{N}
and
\tilde{\mathscr{N}}
.

(2) It is direct to check
\tau_{\mu}(\mathbf{z}_{\mu},t_{\mu})\tau_{\nu}(\mathbf{z}_{\nu},t_{\nu})=\tau_{\nu}(\mathbf{z}_{\nu},t_{\nu})\tau_{\mu}(\mathbf{z}_{\mu},t_{\mu})
for

\mu\neq\nu
by the multiplication law (
1.5
) of
\mathscr{N}
and the definition of
\tau_{\mu}
’s in (
2.4
).
∎

\tau_{1}({\mathscr{H}}_{1})
is a normal subgroup of
{\mathscr{N}}
by

(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{0}_{2},\mathbf{0}_{3},t_{1},0)(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{t}^{\prime})^{-1}=(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{0}_{2},\mathbf{0}_{3},t_{1}+2\Phi_{1}(\mathbf{z}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{z}_{1}),0)\in\tau_{1}({\mathscr{H}}_{1}),

for any
(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\in{\mathscr{N}}
. Similarly,
\tau_{2}({\mathscr{H}}_{2})
and
\tau_{3}({\mathscr{H}}_{3})
are also normal subgroups of
{\mathscr{N}}
.
Thus,
{\mathscr{V}}_{\alpha}:=\mathbb{C}^{n_{\alpha}+n_{3}}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha=1,2
, are quotient groups:

{\mathscr{N}}/\tau_{1}({\mathscr{H}}_{1})\cong{\mathscr{V}}_{2},\qquad{\mathscr{N}}/\tau_{2}({\mathscr{H}}_{2})\cong{\mathscr{V}}_{1},

which are isomorphic to the Heisenberg groups with
multiplications given by

(2.5)

(\mathbf{z}_{\alpha},{\mathbf{z}}_{3},t_{\alpha})(\mathbf{z}_{\alpha}^{\prime},{\mathbf{z}}_{3}^{\prime},t_{\alpha}^{\prime})=\Big(\mathbf{z}_{\alpha}+\mathbf{z}_{\alpha}^{\prime},{\mathbf{z}}_{3}+{\mathbf{z}}_{3}^{\prime},t_{\alpha}+t_{\alpha}^{\prime}+\Phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{z}_{\alpha},\mathbf{z}_{\alpha}^{\prime})+\Phi_{3}(\mathbf{z}_{3},\mathbf{z}_{3}^{\prime})\Big).

The embedding
\tau_{\mu}
maps left invariant vector fields
Y_{\mu j}
’s in (
2.2
) on the group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
to left invariant vector fields
X_{\mu j}
’s on the group
\mathscr{N}
, which are given by

(2.6)

\begin{split}X_{\mu j}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu j}}+2x_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{\mu}},\qquad\qquad\qquad X_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}=\frac{\partial\hskip 22.76219pt}{\partial x_{\mu(n_{\mu}+j)}}-2x_{\mu j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{\mu}},\qquad\mu=1,2,\\
X_{3j}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3j}}+2x_{3(n_{3}+j)}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}\right),\qquad X_{3(n_{3}+j)}=\frac{\partial\hskip 22.76219pt}{\partial x_{3(n_{3}+j)}}-2x_{3j}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}\right).\end{split}

The sub-Laplacian on the group
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
induces an operator
{\triangle}_{\mu}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{2n_{\mu}}X_{\mu j}^{2}
on
the group
\mathscr{N}
.

The normalized Haar measure on
\mathscr{N}

is the Lebesgue measure, which is denoted by
d\mathbf{g}
. The
convolution on
\mathscr{N}
is defined as

(2.7)

f_{1}*f_{2}(\mathbf{g}):=\int_{\mathscr{N}}f_{1}(\mathbf{h})f_{2}(\mathbf{h}^{-1}\mathbf{g})d\mathbf{h}=\int_{\mathscr{N}}f_{1}(\mathbf{g}\mathbf{h}^{-1})f_{2}(\mathbf{h})d\mathbf{h},

by taking coordinates transformation
\mathbf{h}^{-1}\mathbf{g}\rightarrow\mathbf{h}
, which preserving the Haar measure. The same formulae hold for
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mu}
. For a function
f
on a nilpotent group, denote

\breve{f}(\mathbf{g}):=f(\mathbf{g}^{-1}).

An
L^{p}
-function on
{\mathscr{N}}
can be lifted to an
L^{p}
-function on
\tilde{\mathscr{N}}
by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
f\in L^{p}(\mathscr{N})
, define

(2.8)

f^{\sharp}(\mathbf{z},t_{1},t_{2},u):=2\chi(t_{1}+t_{2})f(\mathbf{z},t_{1}+u,t_{2}+u)

where
\chi
is a non-negative smooth function supported on
[1/2,1]\cup[-1,-1/2]
such that
\chi(t)=\chi(-t)
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi=1
. Then
f^{\sharp}\in L^{p}(\tilde{\mathscr{N}})
and
\pi_{*}f^{\sharp}=f
. Moreover,
f^{\sharp}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathscr{N}})
if

f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathscr{N}})
.

Proof.

This is because

\begin{split}(\pi_{*}f^{\sharp})(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t})&=f(\mathbf{z},t_{1},t_{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}}2\chi(t_{1}+t_{2}-2u)du=f(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t}),\\
\|f^{\sharp}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\tilde{\mathscr{N}})}&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{z}dt_{1}dt_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(\mathbf{z},t_{1},t_{2})|^{p}2^{p}\chi^{p}(t_{1}+t_{2}-2u)du=C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathscr{N}})}^{p},\end{split}

by definition and changing variables, where
C_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}2^{p-1}\chi^{p}(u)du
.

Suppose that
f
is compactly supported. If
f^{\sharp}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t},u)\neq 0
, then by definition (
2.8
), there exists
M>0
such that

|\mathbf{z}|<M
,
|t_{1}+u|<M
,
|t_{2}+u|<M
and

|t_{1}+t_{2}|<1
. Thus,

|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq|t_{1}+u|+|t_{2}+u|<2M.

Consequently,
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|,|u|<M+1/2
. Namely,
f^{\sharp}
is compactly supported.
∎

2.2.
A general transference theorem

The
transference method (cf. e.g.
[
1
,
8
,
10
,
17
,
29
]
and the references therein) works for our lifting.
Let

\tilde{T}
be a measurable function on

\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
with compact support.
Define an operator
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}

acting on functions on
\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(F)(\mathbf{z},\tilde{\mathbf{t}}):=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{t}}-\tilde{\mathbf{s}})F(\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}},

for
(\mathbf{z},\tilde{\mathbf{t}})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Next define the projection of the kernel
\widetilde{T}
by
\pi

as

(2.9)

{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{{T}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},t_{1}-u,t_{2}-u,u)du,

for
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Given a
L^{1}
-function
f
on

\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, define

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(f)(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t}):&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{s})f(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{w}d\mathbf{s}.\end{split}

Lemma 2.2
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

\mathcal{T}(f)\circ\pi=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(f\circ\pi)
.

Proof.

Note that

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(f\circ\pi)(\mathbf{z},\tilde{\mathbf{t}})&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{{T}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{t}}-\tilde{\mathbf{s}})f(\mathbf{w},\tilde{s}_{1}+\tilde{s}_{3},\tilde{s}_{2}+\tilde{s}_{3})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{w}d\mathbf{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{{T}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{t}_{1}+\tilde{t}_{3}-s_{1}-u,\tilde{t}_{2}+\tilde{t}_{3}-s_{2}-u,u)f(\mathbf{w},s_{1},s_{2})du,\end{split}

by taking coordinates transformation
s_{\alpha}=\tilde{s}_{\alpha}+\tilde{s}_{3},

\alpha=1,2
,
u=\tilde{t}_{3}-\tilde{s}_{3}
, which preserve the Lebesgue
measure. On the other hand, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(f)\circ\pi(\mathbf{z},\tilde{\mathbf{t}})&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{t}_{1}+\tilde{t}_{3}-{s}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2}+\tilde{t}_{3}-{s}_{2})f(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{w}d\mathbf{s}.\end{split}

The identity follows.
∎

Let
R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}
be the action of the group
\mathbb{R}^{3}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by

R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})=f(\pi(\mathbf{0},\tilde{\mathbf{s}})^{-1}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t}))
, i.e.

(2.10)

R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t}):=f(\mathbf{w},t_{1}-\tilde{s}_{1}-\tilde{s}_{3},t_{2}-\tilde{s}_{2}-\tilde{s}_{3}),

for fixed
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. It is a
representation
of the group
\mathbb{R}^{3}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
, i.e.

(2.11)

R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime}}\circ R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)=R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime}+\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f),

for any
\tilde{\mathbf{s}},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Obviously, we have

(2.12)

\|R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

We now have the following transference result generalizing Nagel-Stein
[
29
, Theorem 5.1.1]
, which shows that
a priori
L^{p}
bound for
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}

gives the same bound for
\mathcal{T}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\tilde{T}
be a measurable function on

\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
with compact support, and there is a constant
A_{p}
such that

\|\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(F)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq A_{p}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},

for any
F\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Then the operator
\mathcal{T}
satisfies for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\|{\mathcal{T}}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq A_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Proof.

Note that for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

(2.13)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(f)(\mathbf{z},{\mathbf{t}})&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},t_{1}-s_{1}-u,t_{2}-s_{2}-u,u)f(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{w}d\mathbf{s}du\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{{T}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{s}_{1},\tilde{s}_{2},\tilde{s}_{3})f(\mathbf{w},t_{1}-\tilde{s}_{1}-\tilde{s}_{3},t_{2}-\tilde{s}_{2}-\tilde{s}_{3})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}})R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\end{split}

by using definition (
2.10
) and taking coordinates transformation
\tilde{s}_{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}-s_{\alpha}-u
,
\alpha=1,2
,
\tilde{s}_{3}=u
, which preserve the Lebesgue measure.
Moreover, for fixed
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(2.14)

\begin{split}R_{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}(\mathcal{T}(f))(\mathbf{z},{\mathbf{t}})&=\mathcal{T}(f)(\mathbf{z},t_{1}-\tilde{u}_{1}-\tilde{u}_{3},t_{2}-\tilde{u}_{2}-\tilde{u}_{3})\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}})R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},t_{1}-\tilde{u}_{1}-\tilde{u}_{3},t_{2}-\tilde{u}_{2}-\tilde{u}_{3})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}})R_{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}+\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}})R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\end{split}

by using (
2.10
), (
2.11
), (
2.13
) and taking transformation
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathbf{s}}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the
(compact) projection onto
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of the compact support of the function

\tilde{T}
. Given
\epsilon>0
, choose a (large) bounded
open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
so that

\frac{|V+E|}{|V|}<1+\epsilon.

Then we get

\begin{split}\|{\mathcal{T}}(f)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}&=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}\|R_{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}(\mathcal{T}(f))\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}d\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\\
&=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}d\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|R_{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}(\mathcal{T}(f))(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t})|^{p}d\mathbf{z}d\mathbf{t}\\
&=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}d\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}})R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\right|^{p}d\mathbf{z}d\mathbf{t}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{t}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}})R_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})\chi_{V+E}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}})d\mathbf{w}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\right|^{p}d\mathbf{z}d\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\end{split}

by using (
2.12
) (
2.14
) and the kernel
\tilde{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}})

vanishing unless
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\in V+E
.
If denote

\tilde{F}_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{w},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}):=R_{-\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})\chi_{V+E}(-\tilde{\mathbf{s}})

and take the transformation
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\rightarrow-\tilde{\mathbf{s}}
in the last integral, we get

\begin{split}\|{\mathcal{T}}(f)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}&\leq\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{t}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(\tilde{F}_{\mathbf{t}})(\mathbf{z},-\tilde{\mathbf{u}})\right|^{p}d\mathbf{z}d\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\\
&=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(\tilde{F}_{\mathbf{t}})\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}d\mathbf{t}\\
&\leq\frac{A_{p}^{p}}{|V|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|\tilde{F}_{\mathbf{t}}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}d\mathbf{t},\end{split}

by the assumption of the theorem.
But

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|\tilde{F}_{\mathbf{t}}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}d\mathbf{t}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|R_{-\tilde{\mathbf{s}}}(f)(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{t})\chi_{V+E}(-\tilde{\mathbf{s}})\right|^{p}d\mathbf{w}d\mathbf{t}=|V+E|\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},\end{split}

by using (
2.12
) again. It follows that

\begin{split}\|{\mathcal{T}}(f)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}&\leq{A_{p}^{p}}(1+\epsilon)\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},\end{split}

which completes the proof by taking
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.
∎

2.3.
Transference of Cauchy-Szegő kernels

As in Corollary
A.1
in the appendix, under the identification
\tilde{\iota}:\mathscr{U}\rightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{U}}}
in (
A.12
), the Cauchy-Szegő
kernel
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}
on the domain
\tilde{\mathscr{N}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}
can written as

(2.15)

\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{g})=\prod_{\mu=1}^{3}\widetilde{{S}}_{\mu}(\varepsilon_{\mu},\mathbf{g}_{\mu}),

for
\mathbf{g}=(\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2},\mathbf{g}_{3})\in\mathscr{H}_{1}\times\mathscr{H}_{2}\times\times\mathscr{H}_{3}
and
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
,
where

(2.16)

\widetilde{{S}}_{\mu}(\varepsilon_{\mu},\mathbf{g}_{\mu})=\frac{c_{\mu}}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{\mu}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{\mu}-\mathbf{i}t_{\mu})^{n_{\mu}+1}},\qquad c_{\mu}:=\frac{n_{\mu}!}{4(\frac{\pi}{2})^{n_{\mu}+1}},

is the
Cauchy-Szegő kernel on the domain
\mathscr{H}_{\mu}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}=\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mu}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, if we write

\mathbf{g}_{\mu}=(\mathbf{z}_{\mu},t_{\mu})\in\mathscr{H}_{\mu}
. Its push-forward is

\begin{split}\pi_{*}(\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}})(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t})&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{z},t_{1}-u,t_{2}-u,u)du\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{c_{1}}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}-\mathbf{i}(t_{1}-u))^{n_{1}+1}}\frac{c_{2}}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}-\mathbf{i}(t_{2}-u))^{n_{2}+1}}\frac{c_{3}}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{3}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{3}-\mathbf{i}u)^{n_{3}+1}}du\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\int_{\mathbf{i}\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-|{\mathbf{z}}_{3}|^{2}-\varepsilon_{3})^{n_{3}+1}}d\zeta\end{split}

by changing the integral to a contour integral (
\mathbf{i}u\rightarrow\zeta
),
where

f(\zeta)=2\pi c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}(-1)^{n_{3}+1}\frac{1}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}-\mathbf{i}t_{1}+\zeta)^{n_{1}+1}}\frac{1}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}-\mathbf{i}t_{2}+\zeta)^{n_{2}+1}}

is holomorphic on the right half plane
\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C};{\rm Re}\,\zeta>-\min(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\}
. Now consider the
semicircle

\Gamma_{R}:=\{Re^{i\theta};\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\}

&\lesssim C_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\left\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}}\left|f*{\varphi}_{\mathbf{r}}\right|^{2}\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{r}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{p}\lesssim C_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\end{split}

by using Fefferman-Stein vector-valued maximal
inequality
[
11

and Theorem
1.3
. Then

\begin{split}\|f*K_{\varepsilon}^{\flat}\|_{p}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}}\|T_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}f\|_{p}\frac{d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{r^{\prime}}}&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}}\prod_{\mu=1}^{3}\left(\frac{1}{r_{\mu}^{\prime}}\wedge r_{\mu}^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\frac{d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{r^{\prime}}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{p}.\end{split}

by Minkowski’s inequality.
The theorem follows by taking

∎

Recall that

where

It is direct to check

Similarly, the

2.16

Proof.

6.1.

[

,

To see the convergence of the series

, recall that for

Appendix A
Calculation of the Cauchy-Szegő kernels on some Siegel domains

As in
[
39
, Section 3.1]
, we calculate the Cauchy-Szegő kernel by using general Gindikin’s formula.

For a regular cone
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
(i.e. it is a nonempty open convex with vertex at
0
and
containing no entire straight line), an
Hermitian form

\Psi:\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
is said to be
\Omega
-positive
if
\Psi(z,z)\in\overline{\Omega}
for any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\Psi(z,z)=0
only if
z=0
. For the Siegel domain
\mathcal{D}
defined by
(
1.1
), its
Shilov boundary

{\mathcal{S}}
is the CR submanifold defined by the equation

\operatorname{Im}\zeta^{\prime\prime}-\Psi(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})=0,
which has the structure of a nilpotent Lie group of step two.

For
f\in H^{2}(\mathcal{D})
, it is well known
[
25
]
that its boundary value

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Omega\\
y\rightarrow 0\end{subarray}}f(\zeta^{\prime},x+\mathbf{i}y+\mathbf{i}\Psi(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}))

exists in
L^{2}({\mathcal{S}})

and its
L^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
norm equals to
\|f\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{D})}
. Thus,

H^{2}(\mathcal{D})
can be identified with a closed subspace of
L^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
.
The
Cauchy-Szegő projection

\mathcal{P}:L^{2}(\mathcal{S})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathcal{D})
is the orthogonal projection to this closed subspace. It has a reproducing kernel
S(\zeta,\eta)
, the
Cauchy-Szegő kernel

[
16
]
, which is holomorphic
in
\zeta\in\mathcal{D}
.
Namely, for
f\in H^{2}(\mathcal{D})
, we have the reproducing formula:

(A.1)

f(\zeta)=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}S(\zeta,\eta)f(\eta)d\beta(\eta),

where
d\beta
is the measure corresponding to
dxd\zeta^{\prime\prime}
in (
1.2
). For
f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
,

(A.2)

\mathcal{P}f(\zeta^{\prime},x+\mathbf{i}\Psi(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}))=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Omega\\
y\rightarrow 0\end{subarray}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}S(\zeta^{\prime},x+\mathbf{i}y+\mathbf{i}\Psi(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}),\eta)f(\eta)d\beta(\eta).

The
dual cone

\Omega^{*}
is the set of all
\lambda\in(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{*}
such that
\langle\lambda,x\rangle>0
for all

x\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\}
.
For
\lambda\in\Omega^{*}
, denote
B_{\lambda}(\zeta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}):=4\langle\lambda,\Psi(\zeta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\rangle
, an
Hermitian form on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, whose
associated Hermitian matrix is also denoted by
B_{\lambda}
. The explicit formula for
S(\zeta,\eta)

[
25
, Theorem
5.1]
is known
as

(A.3)

S(\zeta,\eta)=\int_{\Omega^{*}}e^{-2\pi\langle\lambda,\rho(\zeta,\eta)\rangle}\det B_{\lambda}\,d\lambda,

for
\zeta=(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{D},\eta=(\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{S}
, where

\rho(\zeta,\eta)=\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}-\overline{\eta^{\prime\prime}}}{\mathbf{i}}-2\Psi(\zeta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
is polarized form of
2\operatorname{Im}\zeta^{\prime\prime}-2\Psi(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
.

The domain (
1.4
) is a Siegel domain defined by the regular cone
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\Omega^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
m=2
,
N:=n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
, and the Hermitian form given by

\Psi({\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime})=\Big(\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{1},{\mathbf{z}}_{1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{3},{\mathbf{z}}_{3}^{\prime}\rangle,\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{2},{\mathbf{z}}_{2}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{3},{\mathbf{z}}_{3}^{\prime}\rangle\Big),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard Hermitian inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mu}}
.
\Psi
is
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
-positive,
and
\rho=(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
with

(A.4)

\rho_{\alpha}(\zeta,\eta)=\frac{w_{\alpha}-\overline{w^{\prime}_{\alpha}}}{\mathbf{i}}-2\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{\alpha},{\mathbf{z}}_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle-2\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{3},{\mathbf{z}}_{3}^{\prime}\rangle,

for
\zeta=(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})=({\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{w}})\in\mathcal{D}
,

\eta=(\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime})=({\mathbf{z}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
Then,

B_{\lambda}({\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime})=4\lambda_{1}\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{1},{\mathbf{z}}_{1}^{\prime}\rangle+4\lambda_{2}\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{2},{\mathbf{z}}_{2}^{\prime}\rangle+4(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\langle{\mathbf{z}}_{3},{\mathbf{z}}_{3}^{\prime}\rangle
,
i.e.

B_{\lambda}=4\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda_{1}I_{n_{1}}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}I_{n_{2}}&0\\
0&0&(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})I_{n_{3}}\end{matrix}\right).

Thus,

\det B_{\lambda}=4^{N}\lambda_{1}^{n_{1}}\lambda_{2}^{n_{2}}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{n_{3}}
. Recall that for
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm Re}\,\theta>0
,

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-2\pi s\theta}s^{m}ds=\frac{m!}{(2\pi\theta)^{m+1}}.

It follows that

(A.5)

\begin{split}S(\zeta,\eta)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}e^{-2\pi\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\lambda_{\alpha}\rho_{\alpha}(\zeta,\eta)}4^{N}\lambda_{1}^{n_{1}}\lambda_{2}^{n_{2}}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{n_{3}}d\lambda\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{3}}\binom{n_{3}}{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}e^{-2\pi\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\lambda_{\alpha}\rho_{\alpha}(\zeta,\eta)}4^{N}\lambda_{1}^{n_{1}+k}\lambda_{2}^{n_{2}+n_{3}-k}d\lambda\\
&=\frac{1}{4^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2})^{N+2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{3}}\binom{n_{3}}{k}\frac{(n_{1}+k)!}{\rho_{1}(\zeta,\eta)^{n_{1}+k+1}}\frac{(n_{2}+n_{3}-k)!}{\rho_{2}(\zeta,\eta)^{n_{2}+n_{3}-k+1}}.\end{split}

We need to transform the Cauchy-Szegő kernel (
A.5
) on
\mathcal{D}
to the one on the flat model
\mathscr{U}:=\mathscr{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.
The identification
\iota:\mathscr{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is given by the quadratic mapping

(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})\mapsto(\mathbf{z},{w}_{1}+\mathbf{i}(|{\mathbf{z}}_{1}|^{2}+|{\mathbf{z}}_{3}|^{2}),{w}_{2}+\mathbf{i}(|{\mathbf{z}}_{2}|^{2}+|{\mathbf{z}}_{3}|^{2})).

Corollary A.1
.

The Cauchy-Szegő kernel on the domain
\mathscr{U}
is
S((\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf{g}),\mathbf{g}^{\prime})=S_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}((\mathbf{g}^{\prime})^{-1}\mathbf{g}))
with

(A.6)

S_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{h}):=\frac{1}{4^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2})^{N+2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{3}}\binom{n_{3}}{k}\frac{(n_{1}+k)!}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{1}|^{2}+|{\mathbf{z}}_{3}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}-\mathbf{i}t_{1})^{n_{1}+k+1}}\frac{(n_{2}+n_{3}-k)!}{(|{\mathbf{z}}_{2}|^{2}+|{\mathbf{z}}_{3}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}-\mathbf{i}t_{2})^{n_{2}+n_{3}-k+1}},

\mathbf{h}=(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{t})\in\mathscr{N}
,

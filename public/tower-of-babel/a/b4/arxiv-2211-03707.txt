A causal characterisation of Sp ell + ​ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
Jakob Hedicke

Abstract
. We show that the natural conjugation invariant cone structure on the linear symplectic group Sp ⁡ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{Sp}(2n) is globally hyperbolic in the positively elliptic region Sp ell + ​ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n) . This answers a question by Abbondandolo, Benedetti and Polterovich and shows a formula for a bi-invariant Lorentzian distance function defined by these authors for elements in this region. Moreover we give a characterisation of the positively elliptic region and of − i ​ d ∈ Sp ⁡ ( 2 ​ n ) -id\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n) in terms of the causality of this cone structure.



1.
Main results

Let
\omega
be the canonical symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, given in standard coordinates
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1}\cdots,y_{n})
by

\omega=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}.

The
linear symplectic group

\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
can be defined as the group of linear maps of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
that preserve
\omega
.
The group
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
is a smooth Lie-group whose Lie-algebra is given by

\mathfrak{sp}(2n):=\{X\in\mathrm{Mat}(2n,\mathbb{R})|\omega(\cdot,X\cdot)\text{ is symmetric}\}.

It is well known that the positive definite symmetric bilinear forms induce a convex cone in the Lie-algebra of
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
given by

\mathfrak{sp}^{+}(2n):=\{X\in\mathfrak{sp}(2n)|\omega(\cdot,X\cdot)\text{ is positive definite}\}.

Positive paths of symplectic maps, i.e. paths whose tangent vector lies in
\mathfrak{sp}^{+}(2n)
play a key role in the study of linear Hamiltonian systems and their stability (see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
5
,
12
]
).

The cone
\mathfrak{sp}^{+}(2n)
induces a bi-invariant closed cone structure
C\subset T\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
in the sense of
[
13
]
by setting

C(id):=\{X\in\mathfrak{sp}(2n)|\omega(\cdot,X\cdot)\text{ is positive semi-definite}\}

and
C(W):=WC(id)=C(id)W
for
W\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
(see. e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
12
]
).
This cone structure is the unique bi-invariant closed cone structure on
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
that is a proper subset of
T\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
.
In
[
2
]
the authors define a natural bi-invariant Lorentz-Finsler metric
G
on
C
.

Note that while
(\mathrm{Sp}(2),G)
is isometric to the
3
-dimensional anti-de Sitter space
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
, for
n>1
the Lorentz-Finsler metric
G
is not induced by a Lorentzian metric.
In particular
\partial C
is not smooth and
G
is smooth in the interior of
C
, but only extends continuously to
0
at
\partial C
.

Moreover in
[
2
]
it is shown, that the timelike geodesics of
G
are of the form
e^{tX}W
, where
W\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
and
X\in\mathfrak{sp}^{+}(2n)
.
It follows that
(\mathrm{Sp}(2n),G)
is totally vicious (i.e. there is a closed timelike curve through every point) since given
W\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
, the curve
e^{tJ}W
is timelike and closed.
Here
J
denotes the standard
\omega
-compatible complex structure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Of particular interest for the theory of linear Hamiltonian systems are strongly stable systems.
These are linear Hamiltonian systems whose solution remains bounded for all times such that this property survives small perturbations of the system (see
[
5
]
).

Such systems can be studied in terms of Krein-theory
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
In particular a linear symplectic map gives rise to a strongly stable Hamiltonian system if all its (complex) eigenvalues lie on
S^{1}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
and the Krein form
\kappa(v,w):=\langle iJv,w\rangle
is positive definite on the (complex) eigenspaces of eigenvalues with positive imaginary part.
Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard Hermitian product on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
and
J
the canonical complex structure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

The set of all such symplectic maps is an open subset of
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
called the
positively elliptic region

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
.

Equivalently
W\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
if and only if there exists a splitting of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
into symplectic planes
V_{1},\cdots,V_{n}
and
\omega
-compatible complex structures
J_{k}
on
V_{k}
such that

W=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{n}e^{\theta_{k}J_{k}},

where
\theta_{k}\in(0,\pi)
(see
[
2
, Proposition ii.2.]
).

The most important causal condition in the study of cone structures is global hyperbolicity.
A closed cone structure
(M,C)
is globally hyperbolic if and only if there exists a Cauchy surface, i.e. an embedded hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
such that any inextensible curve tangent to the cone intersects
\Sigma
in a unique point.
In the following we show

Theorem 1.1
.

The positively elliptic region
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
is globally hyperbolic.

This answers
[
2
, Question J.5.]
.
Moreover as a consequence we can generalise
[
2
, Theorem J.1 (i)]
.
Recall that in
[
2
]
the authors introduced a bi-invariant Lorentz-Finsler metric on the interior of
C
.
It is defined on
\mathfrak{sp}^{+}(2n)
as

G(X):=\det(X)^{\frac{1}{2n}}.

This allows to define the Lorentzian-length of a curve tangent to
\mathrm{Int}(C)
.

Consider the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
be the unique lift of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
whose boundary contains the identity in
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
.
The cone structure on
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
lifts to a causal cone structure on
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
(see
[
2
, Theorem I.1.]
), i.e. a cone structure without any closed curves tangent to the cone.
The Lorentz-Finsler metric lifts to
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
and induces a Lorentzian distance function
\mathrm{dist}_{G}
defined for
w_{1},w_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
as the supremum over the lengths of all curves tangent to
C
connecting
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
(see e.g.
[
3
]
for a detailed treatment of Lorentzian distances and their properties).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
w
be in the closure of
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
with eigenvalues

\{e^{\pm i\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{\pm i\theta_{n}}\}
for
\theta_{k}\in[0,\pi]
.
Then

\mathrm{dist}_{G}(id,w)=\sqrt[n]{\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{n}}.

Similar arguments provide a causal characterisation of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
and
-id\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
.
Denote by
I^{+}(w)
and
I^{-}(w)
the chronological future and past of
w\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
, i.e. the set of points that are connected to
w
by a curve tangent to
\mathrm{Int}(C)
and
-\mathrm{Int}(C)
, respectively.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\widetilde{-id}
be the unique lift of
-id
lying on the boundary of
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
.
Then

i)

\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}=I^{+}(id)\cap I^{-}(\widetilde{-id})
.

ii)

If
I^{+}(id)\cap I^{-}(w)\neq\emptyset
is globally hyperbolic for some
w\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
then
w
lies in the closure of
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
.

iii)

If
I^{+}(id)\cap I^{-}(w)=\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
then
w=\widetilde{-id}
.

Remark
.

Theorem
1.3
i) and ii) in particular show that
I^{+}(id)\cap I^{-}(w)\subset\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
, i.e.
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
is the union of all sets of the form
I^{+}(id)\cap I^{-}(w)
, where
w\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
is such that
I^{+}(id)\cap I^{-}(w)
is globally hyperbolic.

2.
Causality theory for closed cone structures

In this section we recall some basic facts about causality theory and cone structures.
Closed cone structures are a natural generalisation of Lorentzian manifolds.

Definition 2.1
.

A closed proper cone
C\subset V
in a vector space
V
is a closed convex subset of
V\setminus\{0\}
with non-empty interior such that
\lambda v\in C
if
v\in C
and
\lambda>0
.
A closed proper cone structure on a smooth manifold
M
is a closed subset
C\subset TM
such that
C(p):=C\cap T_{p}M
is a closed proper cone for all
p\in M
.

Remark
.

The set

C:=\bigcup\limits_{W\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}C(W)

defines a closed proper cone structure on
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
.
This cone structure is Lipschitz in the sense of
[
6
,
13
]
.

Similar to Lorentzian geometry, a cone structure on a smooth manifold
M
gives rise to the notion of causality.
A smooth curve
\gamma
is called
timelike
if
\gamma^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{Int}(C)
,
null
if
\gamma^{\prime}\in\partial C\setminus\{0\}
and
causal
if
\gamma^{\prime}\subset C
.
Then the
chronological future and past
of a point
p
can be defined as

\displaystyle I^{+}(p):=\{q\in M|\text{ there exists a timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\}

\displaystyle I^{-}(p):=\{q\in M|\text{ there exists a timelike curve from $q$ to $p$}\}

and the
causal future and past
as

\displaystyle J^{+}(p):=\{q\in M|\text{ there exists a causal curve from $p$ to $q$}\}\cup\{p\}

\displaystyle J^{-}(p):=\{q\in M|\text{ there exists a causal curve from $q$ to $p$}\}\cup\{p\}.

Finally the
future/past horismos
of
p
is defined as
E^{\pm}(p):=J^{\pm}(p)\setminus I^{\pm}(p)
.
Given a set
S\subset M
we define
I^{\pm}(S)
and
J^{\pm}(S)
to be the union of all chronological (causal) futures/pasts of points in
S
.

The cone structure
(M,C)
is called
causal
if there are no closed causal loops and
strongly causal
if every point
p\in M
admits an open neighbourhood
U
such that any causal curve with endpoints in
U
is contained in
U
(
U
is causally convex).
Obviously strongly causal cone structures are causal.
If there exists a time function on
(M,C)
, i.e. a continuous function strictly increasing along causal curves then we call
(M,C)

stably causal
.
Note that stably causal cone structures are strongly causal.

The strongest causal condition for cone structures is global hyperbolicity.
A cone structure
(M,C)
is called
globally hyperbolic
if it is causal and the sets
J^{+}(p)\cap J^{-}(q)
are compact for all
p,q\in M
.
Global hyperbolicity in particular implies the existence of a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
, i.e. a hypersurface in
M
that is intersected in a unique point by any inextensible causal curve and a smooth splitting
M\cong\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
(see e.g.
[
4
,
13
]
).

Given a cone structure one can define the notion of null geodesic.
In the case of Lorentzian metrics this notion coincides (up to parametrisation) with the usual one.

Definition 2.2
.

A null geodesic of a cone structure
C
is a continuous curve
\gamma\colon I\rightarrow N
that is locally horismotic.
This means that for every
t_{0}\in I
and any open neighbourhood
U
of
\gamma(t_{0})
there exists an open neighbourhood
\gamma(t_{0})\in V\subset U
such that if
\gamma([t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon]\cap I)\subset V
, then
\gamma(s_{1})\in E_{C_{U}}^{+}(\gamma(s_{0}))
for all
s_{0},s_{1}\in[t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon]\cap I
with
s_{0}<s_{1}
.
Here
C_{U}
denotes the restriction of the cone structure
C
to the neighbourhood
U
.

In the case of strongly causal cone structures a null geodesic is just a curve
\gamma(t)
with the property that for any
t_{0}
there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that
\gamma(t_{2})\in E^{+}(\gamma(t_{1}))
for all
t_{0}-\epsilon<t_{1}\leq t_{2}<t_{0}+\epsilon
.

3.
The positively elliptic region and the Maslov quasimorphism

In this section we introduce some basic facts about
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
and the Maslov quasimorphism
\mu\colon\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined on the universal cover of the symplectic group.

Recall that by
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
we denote the linear symplectic group, i.e. the group of linear maps preserving the canonical symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
As mentioned in the introduction the positively elliptic region
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
can be defined as an open subset of strongly stable symplectic maps.
Another characterisation can be given in terms of Krein theory (see e.g.
[
5
]
):

The canonical symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
can be extended to a skew-hermitian form

\omega\colon\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{C}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n}.

The
Krein form
can be defined as

\kappa:=-i\omega.

Note that
\kappa
is a Hermitian form on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
.

Consider
W\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
as a linear map on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
.
Given a complex eigenvalue
\lambda
, the form
\kappa
is non-degenerate on the generalised eigenspace
E_{\lambda}
if and only if
\lambda
lies on the unit circle.
Another eigenvalue of
W
is
\overline{\lambda}
.
If
\lambda
lies on the unit circle and
\kappa
has signature
(p,q)
on
E_{\lambda}
, it has signature
(q,p)
on
E_{\overline{\lambda}}
.
In particular the signature of
\kappa
on
E_{\pm 1}
is always
(p,p)
.

Definition 3.1
.

The positively elliptic region
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
is defined as the set of
W\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
such that all (complex) eigenvalues of
W
are contained in
S^{1}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
and
\kappa
is positive definite on
E_{\lambda}
if and only if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
W
with positive imaginary part.

Due to the definiteness of
\kappa
on the eigenspaces, the positively elliptic region forms an open subset of
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
.
Moreover all maps in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
are diagonalisable as
[
2
, Proposition ii.2.]
shows.
The proposition states that the exponential map
\mathrm{exp}\colon\mathfrak{sp}(2n)\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
maps the set

\mathfrak{sp}(2n)_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{sp}^{+}(2n)|\sigma(X)\subset i(-\pi,\pi)\}

diffeomorphically to
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
.
Moreover
W\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
if and only if there exists a
W
-invariant splitting of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
into symplectic planes
V_{k}
such that

W=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{n}e^{\theta_{k}J_{k}}.

Here
\theta_{k}\in(0,\pi)
and
J_{k}
denotes an
\omega
-invariant complex structure on
V_{k}
.

Due to the non-degeneracy of the Krein form causal paths behave particularly nice on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
(
[
5
]
).
For instance it is known that along a timelike curve the eigenvalues with positive imaginary part rotate counterclockwise, while the once with negative imaginary part rotate clockwise on
S^{1}
.
This means that the
\theta_{k}
in the splitting above are increasing along causal curves.
Moreover any causal path
W(t)
can leave
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
only once the eigenvalue
-1
is attained.

The behaviour of eigenvalues along a causal curve can be estimated using the Maslov quasimorphism which we briefly introduce.
For details see e.g.
[
1
,
2
]
.

Denote by
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
the universal cover of the symplectic group.
The universal cover has a natural group structure with neutral element being a lift of the identity.
We denote the neutral element by
id
and by
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
the unique lift of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
such that
id
lies on the boundary.

The Maslov quasimorphism

\mu\colon\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

was introduced in
[
7
]
as the unique homogeneous real quasimorphism whose restriction to the lift of
\mathrm{U}(n)\subset\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
agrees with the complex determinant.
Here the term quasimorphism means that there exists a constant
C
such that

|\mu(vw)-\mu(v)-\mu(w)|\leq C

for all
v,w\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
.
In
[
2
]
this quasimorphism is used to construct a time function on
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
.

For
W\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
define

\nu(W):=(-1)^{m}\prod\limits_{\lambda\in\sigma(W)\cap S^{1}\setminus\{\pm 1\}}\lambda^{p(\lambda)},

where
2m
is the total algebraic multiplicity of all negative real eigenvalues and
(p(\lambda),q(\lambda))
the signature of
\kappa
on
E_{\lambda}
.
As pointed out in
[
2
]
the Maslov quasimorphism can be computed as the unique continuous function satisfying

\mu(w)=e^{2\pi i\nu(W)},\mu(id)=0.

Here
W
denotes the projection of
w
to
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
.

Given
W\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
denote by
\{\theta_{1}^{W},\cdots,\theta_{n}^{W}\}
the arguments of the eigenvalues of
W
with positive imaginary part.
Let
w\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)}
be the lift of
W
.
Then

\mu(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\theta^{W}_{1}+\cdots+\theta^{W}_{n}).

4.
Proofs

As before for
W\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
denote by
\{\theta_{1}^{W},\cdots,\theta_{n}^{W}\}
the arguments of the eigenvalues of
W
with positive imaginary part.
Define

\displaystyle\tau\colon\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle W\mapsto\sum\limits_{i}\ln(\theta^{W}_{i})-\ln(\pi-\theta^{W}_{i})

Lemma 4.1
.

The function
\tau
is a continuous time function on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
, i.e.
\tau
is strictly increasing along causal curves.

Remark
.

In fact the proof of Theorem
1.1
implies that
\tau
is a Cauchy time function, i.e. its level sets are (topological) Cauchy surfaces.

Proof.

Let
W(t)
be a causal curve in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
.
It follows from
[
5
, Proposition 2]
that
\tau
is non-decreasing along
W(t)
.
Since
W^{\prime}(t)\in C(W(t))
there exists an eigenvector
x
for some eigenvalue
e^{i\theta}
of
W(t)
with
\omega(x,W^{\prime}(t)x)>0
and
\kappa(x,x)=1
.
Following the proof of
[
5
, Proposition 2]
take a sequence
t_{k}\searrow t
.
Then there exists a sequence of eigenvalues
e^{i\theta_{k}}
for
W(t_{k})

with
\theta_{k}\rightarrow\theta
and a sequence of eigenvectors
x_{k}
with
\kappa(x_{k},x_{k})=1
and
x_{k}\rightarrow x
.

As pointed out in
[
5
]
it follows that

\omega\left(\frac{W(t)-W(t_{k})}{t-t_{k}}x,x_{k}\right)=\frac{(e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta_{k}})}{t-t_{k}}\omega(x,x_{k}).

Taking the limit this implies that for
t_{k}
close to
t
we have that
\theta_{k}>\theta
, i.e.
\tau(W(t_{k}))>\tau(t)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X\in C
and
W_{0}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
.
Then there exist
0<c_{1},c_{2}<\infty
such that
e^{tX}W_{0}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
for all
t\in(-c_{1},c_{2})
and
e^{-c_{1}X}W_{0},e^{c_{2}X}W_{0}\notin\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
.
In particular

\lim\limits_{t\searrow-c_{1}}\tau(e^{tX}W_{0})=-\infty,\lim\limits_{t\nearrow c_{2}}\tau(e^{tX}W_{0})=\infty

Proof.

Let
\theta_{1}(t),\cdots,\theta_{n}(t)
be the arguments of the Krein positive eigenvalues of
e^{tX}W_{0}
.

1. case:

X
has an eigenvalue
\lambda\neq 0
.

Let
w(t)\subset\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}(2n)}
be a lift of
e^{tX}
starting at
id
.
Then the Maslov quasimorphism satisfies
\mu(w(t))=tc
for some
c>0
, i.e.
\mu(w)
diverges.
Let
w_{0}
be a lift of
W_{0}
.
Since
\mu
is a quasimorphism there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that
\mu(w(t)w_{0})\in(\mu(w_{0})+tc-C,\mu(w_{0})+tc+C)
.
Hence
\mu(w(t)w_{0})
diverges.
It follows that
e^{tX}W_{0}
takes the eigenvalues
1
and
-1
for infinitely many
t
, in particular it leaves
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
in the future and in the past for finite
t
.

2. case:
The only eigenvalue of
X
is
0
.

In this case
X
is nilpotent since one has
(X)^{2n}=0
.
It follows that

e^{tX}W=W+tXW+\cdots+\frac{t^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}(X)^{2n-1}W.

In particular the characteristic polynomial
P_{t}
is a monic polynomial of degree
2n
whose coefficients are polynomials in
t
.
Write

P_{t}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2n}+P_{2n-1}(t)\lambda^{2n-1}+\cdots+P_{1}(t)\lambda+1.

Then due to
4.1
at least one of the eigenvalues of
e^{tX}W
moves counter-clockwise on the unit circle, i.e. there exists some non-constant
P_{k}(t)
.

Over
\mathbb{C}
the polynomial
P_{t}(\lambda)
can be factorized, i.e. the coefficients
P_{i}(t)
depend polinomially on the eigenvalues of
e^{tX}W
.
Since
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}|P_{k}(t)|=\infty
, it follows that at least one of the eigenvalues of
e^{tX}W
diverges for
t\rightarrow\pm\infty
.
In particular
e^{tX}W
leaves
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
for finite
t
in the future and in the past.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The positively elliptic region
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
is causally geodesically connected, i.e. for
W_{0}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
and
W_{1}\in J^{+}(W_{0})
there exists
X\in C
with
W_{1}=e^{X}W_{0}
and
e^{sX}W_{0}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
for
s\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Claim: For
W_{0}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
and
W_{1}\in I^{+}(W_{0})
we have that
W_{1}W_{0}^{-1}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
.

Suppose not.
Let
W(t)
be a timelike curve in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{+}(2n)
with
W(0)=W_{0}
and
W(1)=W_{1}
.
Due to

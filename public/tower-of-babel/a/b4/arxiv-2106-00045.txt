Existence results for a generalized fractional boundary value problem in b b -metric space
Faouzi Haddouchi

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with a class of nonlinear boundary value problem involving fractional derivative in the φ \varphi -Riemann-Liouville sense. Some Properties of the Green’s function for this problem are mentioned. By means of the Banach contraction principle in b b -metric space and the technique of the γ \gamma – ψ \psi Geraghty contractive maps, existence and uniqueness results are obtained. Two examples are given to support the theoretical results.

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}\big[(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-2}-(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-1}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}\Lambda(\eta)(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-2}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

For
t\leq s\leq\eta
, one has

\displaystyle\mu\Gamma(\alpha)\mathcal{G}(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\big[(\alpha-1)\varphi^{\prime}(1)(\varphi(1)-\varphi(s))^{\alpha-2}-\beta(\varphi(\eta)-\varphi(s))^{\alpha-1}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\Big[(\alpha-1)\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}(1-\Lambda(1))^{\alpha-2}-\beta[\Phi(\eta)]^{\alpha-1}(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-1}\Big]

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\Big[\beta[\Phi(\eta)]^{\alpha-1}(1-\Lambda(1))^{\alpha-2}-\beta[\Phi(\eta)]^{\alpha-1}(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-1}\Big]

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\beta[\Phi(t)\Phi(\eta)]^{\alpha-1}\Big[(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-2}-(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-1}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta[\Phi(t)\Phi(\eta)]^{\alpha-1}(1-\Lambda(\eta))^{\alpha-2}\Lambda(\eta)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

For
\eta\leq s\leq t
, one has

\displaystyle\mu\Gamma(\alpha)\mathcal{G}(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}(\alpha-1)\varphi^{\prime}(1)(\varphi(1)-\varphi(s))^{\alpha-2}-\mu(\varphi(t)-\varphi(s))^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}(1-\Lambda(1))^{\alpha-2}-\mu[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}(1-\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}(1-\Lambda(1))^{\alpha-2}

\displaystyle-(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}(1-\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}+\beta[\Phi(\eta)\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}(1-\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}\Big[(1-\Lambda(1))^{\alpha-2}-(1-\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\Big]

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}\Big[(1-\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-2}-(1-\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-2}(1-\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-2}\Lambda(t)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

Clearly, for
\max\{\eta,t\}\leq s
,
\mathcal{G}(t,s)>0
.

Therefore,
\mathcal{G}(t,s)>0
for
t,s\in(0,1)
.

(iii) Since
\varphi
is a strictly increasing function, then by (
2.3
), it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\mu\Gamma(\alpha)\max_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{G}(t,s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)[\Phi(t)]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)(\varphi(1)-\varphi(s))^{\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)\varphi^{\prime}(1)(\varphi(1)-\varphi(s))^{\alpha-2},\ \text{for all}\ s\in(0,1).

Therefore

\max_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\leq\frac{(\alpha-1)\varphi^{\prime}(1)(\varphi(1)-\varphi(s))^{\alpha-2}}{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)},\ \text{for all}\ s\in(0,1).

The proof is completed.
∎

3.
Main results

By
X=\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R})
we denote the set of continuous functions. Let
d:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be given by

d(x,y)=\|(x-y)^{2}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}(x(t)-y(t))^{2}.

Then,
(X,d,r)
is a complete
b
-metric space with
r=2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:X\rightarrow X
be the operator defined as

(3.1)

\mathcal{A}u(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)f(s,u(s))ds,\ t\in[0,1].

Clearly,
u
is a solution of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) if and only if
u
is a fixed point of operator
\mathcal{A}
.

We now list suitable conditions on the nonlinearity function
f(t,u)
.

(H1)

f\in\mathcal{C}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
;

(H2)

There exists real-valued function not identically zero
g\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that for all

t\in[0,1]
and
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

|f(t,u)-f(t,v)|\leq g(t)|u-v|.

Our first result on the existence and uniqueness of solutions is based on the Banach contraction principle in a
b
-metric space.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
(H1)-(H2) hold. Then the fractional boundary value problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) has a unique solution on
[0,1]
provided that

(3.2)

\|g\|_{\infty}<\frac{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)}{\sqrt{2}\big[\Phi(1)\big]^{\alpha-1}\varphi^{\prime}(1)}.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.9
-
2.10
, for
u,v\in X
, and
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\big|(\mathcal{A}u)(t)-(\mathcal{A}v)(t)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\Big(f(s,u(s))-f(s,v(s))\Big)ds\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\Big|f(s,u(s))-f(s,v(s))\Big|ds\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)g(s)\big|u(s)-v(s)\big|ds\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\|g\|_{\infty}\sqrt{d(u,v)}ds\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{(\alpha-1)\|g\|_{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(1)}{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\big(\varphi(1)-\varphi(s)\big)^{\alpha-2}\varphi^{\prime}(s)ds\Bigg]^{2}d(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{(\alpha-1)\|g\|_{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(1)}{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\alpha-1}(\varphi(1)-\varphi(0))^{\alpha-1}\Bigg]^{2}d(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\|g\|_{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(1)\big[\Phi(1)\big]^{\alpha-1}}{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigg)^{2}d(u,v)

By denoting
\lambda=\Big(\frac{\|g\|_{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(1)[\Phi(1)]^{\alpha-1}}{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)}\Big)^{2},
we get

d(\mathcal{A}u,\mathcal{A}v)\leq\lambda d(u,v).

From (
3.2
) and by means of Theorem
2.5
, we claim that the operator
\mathcal{A}
has a unique fixed point.
The proof is completed.
∎

The second result concerns the existence of positive solutions for the
\varphi
-Riemann-Liouville fractional differential equation
(
1.5
)-(
1.6
) via the
\gamma
–
\psi
-Geraghty
contractive type mappings. To begin with, we make the following assumption:

(H3)

f\in C([0,1]\times\mathbb{R^{+}},\mathbb{R}^{+})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
(H3) holds. If the following assumptions are satisfied:

(i)

There exists
f:[0,1]\times\mathbb{R^{+}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R^{+}}
such that

\displaystyle\big|f(s,u(s))-f(s,v(s))\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)}{\varphi^{\prime}(1)\big[\Phi(1)\big]^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\theta\big(\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\big)},\ s\in[0,1],

where
\psi\in\Psi
and
\theta\in\Theta
;

(ii)

There exists
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
and
\tau:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\tau\big(u_{0}(t),\mathcal{A}u_{0}(t)\big)\geq 0,\ t\in[0,1];

(iii)

For
t\in[0,1]
and
u,v\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
,
\tau\big(u(t),v(t)\big)\geq 0
implies

\tau\big(\mathcal{A}u(t),\mathcal{A}v(t)\big)\geq 0;

(iv)

If
\{u_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}([0,1])
with
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
\mathcal{C}([0,1])
, and
\tau(u_{n},u_{n+1})\geq 0
, then
\tau(u_{n},u)\geq 0
,

then the fractional boundary value problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) has at least one positive solution.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
,
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
is a solution of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) if and only if
u
is a solution of the integral equation

u(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)f(s,u(s))ds,\ t\in[0,1].

Clearly, the fixed points of
\mathcal{A}
coincide with the solution of fractional boundary value problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
be such that
\tau\big(u(t),v(t)\big)\geq 0
for
t\in[0,1]
.

Using (i) and Lemma
2.10
, we get

\displaystyle\big|(\mathcal{A}u)(t)-(\mathcal{A}v)(t)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\Big(f(s,u(s))-f(s,v(s))\Big)ds\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\Big|f(s,u(s))-f(s,v(s))\Big|ds\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\mu\Gamma(\alpha)}{\varphi^{\prime}(1)\big[\Phi(1)\big]^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\theta\big(\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\big)}ds\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\theta\big(\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\big).

Hence

\|(\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{8}\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\theta\big(\psi\big(\|(u-v)^{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\big).

Let
\gamma:\mathcal{C}([0,1])\times\mathcal{C}([0,1])\rightarrow\mathbb{R^{+}}
be defined by

\gamma(u,v)=\begin{cases}1,&\tau\big(u(t),v(t)\big)\geq 0,\ t\in[0,1],\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

So for
u,v\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
with
\tau\big(u(t),v(t)\big)\geq 0
,
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma(u,v)\psi\big(8d(\mathcal{A}u,\mathcal{A}v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8d(\mathcal{A}u,\mathcal{A}v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\big(\psi(d(u,v))\big)\psi(d(u,v)),\ \theta\in\Theta.

This proves
\mathcal{A}
is a
\gamma-\psi
-Geraphty mapping.

Now, from (iii), we get for
u,v\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
,

\displaystyle\gamma(u,v)\geq 1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\tau\big(u(t),v(t)\big)\geq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\tau\big(\mathcal{A}u(t),\mathcal{A}v(t)\big)\geq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\gamma\big(\mathcal{A}u,\mathcal{A}v\big)\geq 1.

Thus,
\mathcal{A}
is
\gamma
-admissible.

By (ii), there exists
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
such that
\gamma\big(u_{0},\mathcal{A}u_{0}\big)\geq 1
. Utilizing (iv) and Theorem
2.8
, there exists
u^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1])
such that
u^{\star}=\mathcal{A}u^{\star}
. We have proved that
u^{\star}
is a solution of the problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).
∎

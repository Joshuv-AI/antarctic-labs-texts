Cohomology rings of the moduli of one-dimensional sheaves on the projective plane
Yakov Kononov, Woonam Lim, Miguel Moreira, Weite Pi

Abstract
. We initiate a systematic study on the cohomology rings of the moduli stack 𝔐 d , χ \mathfrak{M}_{d,\chi} of semistable one-dimensional sheaves on the projective plane. We introduce a set of tautological relations of geometric origin, including Mumford-type relations, and prove that their ideal is generated by certain primitive relations via the Virasoro operators. Using BPS integrality and the computational efficiency of Virasoro operators, we show that our geometric relations completely determine the cohomology rings of the moduli stacks up to degree 5 5 . As an application, we verify the refined Gopakumar–Vafa/Pandharipande–Thomas correspondence for local ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} in degree 5 5 . Furthermore, we propose a substantially strengthened version of the P = C P=C conjecture, originally introduced by Shen and two of the authors. This can be viewed as an analogue of the P = W P=W conjecture in a compact and Fano setting.

Introduction

0.1.
Overview and main results.

The primary purpose of this paper is twofold. First, we initiate a systematic study on the cohomology rings of the moduli stack
\mathfrak{M}_{d,\chi}
of semistable one-dimensional sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, combining a variety of ideas and tools including geometric relations, Virasoro representations, and BPS integrality. Our methods produce explicit presentations of a number of new cohomology rings in degrees
d\leq 5
. Second, we propose a substantially strengthened version of
the
P=C
conjecture
introduced by Shen and two of the authors
[
KPS23
]
, extending it to all cohomological degrees. This can be viewed as an analogue of the
P=W
conjecture in a compact, Fano setting, and has various structural implications on both sides of the equality. We verify this conjecture, among other predictions, on the cohomology rings we obtain. Throughout, we work over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

Fix two integers
d
and
\chi
with
d\geq 1
. We consider the moduli stack of sheaves

\mathfrak{M}_{d,\chi}:=\{\textrm{semistable }{F}\in\mathrm{Coh}(\mathbb{P}^{2})\mid[\mathrm{supp}({F})]=d\cdot H,\;\;\chi({F})=\chi\},

where
H
is the class of a line,
\mathrm{supp}({F})
denotes the Fitting support, and the stability condition is with respect to the slope

\mu({F})=\frac{\chi({F})}{c_{1}({F})\cdot H}\in\mathbb{Q}.

This moduli stack is smooth and admits a good moduli space
M_{d,\chi}
parametrizing semistable sheaves up to S-equivalence. When
d
and
\chi
are coprime, the good moduli map
\pi:\mathfrak{M}_{d,\chi}\to M_{d,\chi}
is a trivial

-gerbe and induces an isomorphism of rings

(1)

H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi},\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{*}(M_{d,\chi},\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{*}(B\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{Q}).

Thus, study of the cohomology rings of
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
subsumes that of the coprime moduli spaces
M_{d,\chi}
. The latter has applications to enumerative geometry which we explain below. We remark that our approach is inductive and understanding the cohomology rings of
M_{d,\chi}
makes use of the moduli stacks
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}}
with non-coprime
(d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
as well.

The study of the good moduli spaces
M_{d,\chi}
dates back to the works of Simpson
[
Sim94
]
and Le Potier
[
LP93a
]
. We refer to
[
PS23
, Introduction]
for a brief overview of various directions of study toward this moduli space. One of the motivations to investigate

comes from enumerative geometry. Briefly speaking, a certain

on the intersection cohomology

defines interesting curve counting invariants called

for local
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, i.e., the Calabi–Yau threefold

[

is a smooth projective variety and its intersection cohomology coincides with singular cohomology. Furthermore, the perverse filtration in this case is characterized by the cup product with a base ample class, cf. Section

The above discussion motivates the following problem:

Problem 0.1

Study the cohomology rings of the moduli stacks

0.1.1.
The
P=C
conjecture.

We turn to the second goal of the paper and introduce the
P=C
conjecture next.
The moduli spaces
M_{d,\chi}
admit a proper map to the complete linear system of degree
d
curves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, called the Hilbert–Chow map

h:M_{d,\chi}\longrightarrow|d\cdot H|

sending a sheaf to its Fitting support. Associated to such a proper map, there is a filtration
P_{\bullet}\mathit{IH}^{\ast}(M_{d,\chi})
called the
perverse filtration
, cf.
[
BBD82
]
, which encodes interesting information about the topology of the fibration
h
. As mentioned before, numerical data of this filtration (more precisely, dimensions of
\textup{gr}^{P}_{i}\mathit{IH}^{i+j}(M_{d,\chi})
) defines the refined Gopakumar–Vafa invariants for local
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

On the other hand, when
d
and
\chi
are coprime, there is a natural multiplicative filtration
C_{\bullet}H^{\ast}(M_{d,\chi})
called the
Chern filtration
, defined using the (normalized) tautological classes
c_{k}(j)
, which serve as generators for our rings; see (
4
) and (
37
) for precise definitions. In
[
KPS23
]
, Shen and two of the authors proposed that the perverse and Chern filtrations should match up to cohomological degree
2d-4
. In Section
6.1
, we shall upgrade this ‘local’ version into a statement on the entire cohomology ring as follows.

Conjecture 0.6
.

For coprime
d\geq 1
and
\chi\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have
P_{\bullet}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi},\mathbb{Q})=C_{\bullet}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi},\mathbb{Q})
.

We refer readers to
[
MSY23
]
for a discussion on the history and development of various
P=C
phenomena. In particular, it plays a key role in the recently proved
P=W
conjecture of de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini
[
dCHM12
,
MS22
,
HMMS22
,
MSY23
]
by serving as an intermediate step
P=C=W
; the equality
P=C
is considered the most difficult one. Note that, by the spectral correspondence
[
BNR89
]
, Higgs bundles on a curve
\Sigma
are in correspondence with one-dimensional sheaves on the non-compact and symplectic surface
T^{*}\Sigma
. Thus Conjecture
0.6
can be viewed as an analogue of the
P=W
conjecture for the compact and Fano surface
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. One important different feature, however, is that we do not have a multiplicative
splitting
of the perverse filtration on
M_{d,\chi}
as on the moduli of Higgs bundles; this fails already for
d=3
.

As a consequence of this conjecture, structures of either the perverse or the Chern filtration can be transported to the other side. In particular, Conjecture
0.6
would imply the multiplicativity of the perverse filtration and a curious Hard Lefschetz symmetry for the Chern filtration. See Remark
6.7
for a detailed discussion.

Again, Theorem
0.2
allows us to verify this conjecture in low degrees.

Corollary 0.7
.

The
P=C
conjecture holds up to degree
5
.

In the rest of the introduction, we explain our main ingredients for the proof of Theorem
0.2
in some more detail.

0.2.
Mumford and geometric relations.

The original Mumford relations are formulated over
M_{r,d}(\Sigma)
, the moduli space of semistable holomorphic bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
over a fixed algebraic curve
\Sigma
of genus
g\geq 2
. Mumford conjectured that such relations form a complete set in the rank two case
[
AB83
]
. A similar but more general construction of the relations called
generalized Mumford relations
is carried out in
[
EK04
]
.

Roughly speaking, the Mumford-type relations are obtained by constructing certain vector bundles built from the universal family over the moduli spaces, and taking the (vanishing) Chern classes of degrees beyond the rank of the bundle. Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem expresses the Chern classes in terms of tautological classes on the moduli space, thus giving relations in the cohomology ring. More precisely, the constructions in
[
AB83
]
and
[
EK04
]
rely on the following vanishing results:

(i)

Higher cohomology of the bundles due to positivity.

(ii)

Hom (and Ext) groups between semistable bundles due to slope differences.

In the following, we reserve the name
Mumford relations
(
\mathsf{MR}
) for the first type, and call the second type
generalized Mumford relations
(
\mathsf{GMR}
) in accordance with the original terminology. For the moduli space
M_{r,d}(\Sigma)
, Mumford’s conjecture in the rank two case was first proved by Kirwan
[
Kir92
]
, while Earl–Kirwan
[
EK04
]
showed that the generalized Mumford relations are complete for general rank coprime to the degree.

The study of Mumford-type relations for sheaves on (del Pezzo)
surfaces
was initiated in
[
PS23
]
and played a key role in the proof of
[
LMP23
, Theorem 1.2]
; see Remark
2.13
. One primary goal of the current paper is to pursue this idea more systematically. In Section
2
, we carry out general constructions of the two Mumford-type relations for the moduli stack
\mathfrak{M}_{d,\chi}
. In addition, there is a third type of relations, which we name
base relations
(
\mathsf{BR}
), coming from the fibration structure of
\mathfrak{M}_{d,\chi}
over the base
|d\cdot H|
. We shall refer to all of these as
geometric relations.
In light of the results in
[
Kir92
,
EK04
]
, it is natural to ask the following question:

Question 0.8
.

Are the geometric relations complete for general
\mathfrak{M}_{d,\chi}
?

The answer to Question
0.8
turns out to be positive for all moduli stacks (and spaces) in Theorem
0.2
,
except for

M_{5,1}
. In fact, we will see that Mumford and generalized Mumford relations are already complete for these moduli stacks. The situation for
M_{5,1}
is more subtle: the geometric relations are, somewhat surprisingly, not complete in the literal sense, but they are still sufficient to determine the entire ring structure when combined with Poincaré duality. We refer to Section
5.2.5
for a detailed explanation.

0.3.
Virasoro representations.

The Virasoro constraints predict a rich set of relations among tautological invariants of moduli spaces in various settings. It is named so because the predicted relations are described by certain representations of the half of the Virasoro algebra
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}:=\textup{span}\{{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\,|\,n\geq-1\}
, where
\mathsf{L}_{n}
are certain operators (cf. Section
3.1
for the definition of these operators in our setting) satisfying the Virasoro relations

[\mathsf{L}_{n},\mathsf{L}_{m}]=(m-n)\,\mathsf{L}_{n+m}.

The Witten–Kontsevich theorem
[
Wit91
,
Kon92
]
was the first instance of the Virasoro constraints. This was conjecturally generalized to Gromov–Witten theory
[
EHX97
]
. After being transported to the sheaf side via the GW/PT correspondence
[
MOOP22
]
, there have been rapid developments in the sheaf-theoretic Virasoro constraints
[
Mor22
,
vB23
,
BLM22
,
Boj24
,
LM24
]
.

For the moduli spaces of our interest, namely
M_{d,\chi}
, the Virasoro constraint was first conjectured in
[
BLM22
]
and proven for the coprime cases in
[
LM24
]
using quiver representations. The Virasoro constraints is a priori a statement about top degree relations. However, it was observed in
[
LM24
]
that the constraints imply that a representation of
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}
on the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}_{d,\chi}
(see Section
1.1
) factors through the surjective homomorphism

0.4.
BPS integrality.

One of the most subtle and important aspects in the study of tautological rings is whether a given set of relations is complete, as inquired in Question
0.8
. When the moduli space is a smooth projective variety, Poincaré duality provides an easy criterion for the completeness of a set of relations. But unless we are in such a case, it is in general difficult to determine whether the relations we have found are complete.

One practical method is to compute the Poincaré series of the moduli stacks (and spaces) in question. In this paper, we use the BPS integrality formula of Mozgovoy–Reineke
[
MR15
]
to obtain the Poincaré series of the moduli stacks
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
.
Roughly speaking, the BPS integrality formula relates the virtual Poincaré series of the moduli stacks and the virtual intersection Poincaré polynomials of the moduli spaces for a fixed slope
\mu\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. In order to derive an equality of the actual Poincaré series of the moduli stacks, we need the following result, cf. Theorem
4.4
.

Theorem 0.11
.

The cohomology ring
H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi},{\mathbb{Q}})
is tautologically generated and pure. Furthermore, the cycle class map from Chow to cohomology is an isomorphism.

This theorem shows in particular that
H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi},{\mathbb{Q}})
is of
(p,p)
-type. The corresponding property for
\mathit{\mathit{IH}}^{*}(M_{d,\chi},{\mathbb{Q}})
was proven by Bousseau
[
Bou22
]
. Therefore, the BPS integrality formula implies an equality between the shifted (intersection) Poincaré series

(2)

\sum_{(d,\chi)\in\Lambda_{\mu}}\overline{E}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi},q)\cdot e^{(d,\chi)}=\mathrm{PE}\left(\frac{-q^{1/2}}{1-q}\cdot\sum_{(d,\chi)\in\Lambda_{\mu}\backslash\{(0,0)\}}\overline{\mathit{IE}}(M_{d,\chi},q)\cdot e^{(d,\chi)}\right)

where
\mathrm{PE}(-)
denotes the plethystic exponential. Note that on the right hand side we have moduli spaces
M_{d,\chi}
that are not necessarily smooth, whose (intersection) cohomology is a priori more mysterious. Nonetheless, the
\chi
-independence theorem of Maulik–Shen
[
MS23
, Theorem 0.1]
gives an isomorphism of graded vector spaces

(3)

\mathit{IH}^{*}(M_{d,\chi})\simeq\mathit{IH}^{*}(M_{d,\chi^{\prime}})

for any
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
not necessarily coprime to
d
. Thanks to this isomorphism, we can use Equation (
2
) to effectively compute the Poincaré series of
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
in terms of the Poincaré polynomials of the smooth moduli spaces
M_{d^{\prime},1}
where
(d,\chi)=k(d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
for some
k\geq 1
. The latter can be computed directly (at least for
d\leq 5
) using geometric relations and the Poincaré duality criterion; see Section
5.2
. Knowing the Poincaré series then allows us to check whether our geometric relations are complete for the moduli stacks.

0.5.
Further directions.

We outline a few directions of future research.

(i)

The relations in
H^{*}(M_{5,1},\mathbb{Q})
that cannot be obtained as geometric relations, cf. Section
5.2.5
, seem rather mysterious to us. It is natural to ask if one can obtain these relations by other geometric means, with the aim of proving a result similar to
[
EK04
]
.

(ii)

The
P=C
conjecture gives various predictions on the perverse and the Chern filtrations on
M_{d,\chi}
, see Remark
6.7
. Confirming these predictions independently would in turn give more evidence to the conjecture. In particular, Remark
6.7
(ii) and (iv) are closely related to the theme of finding relations in this paper. We will prove in Section
2.3
a subset of the vanishing Chern monomials predicted by Remark
6.7
(iv), and we expect similar methods to establish more results along this line.

(iii)

In the setting of Higgs bundles on curves, there is a generalized version of the
P=W
conjecture for stacks by
[
Dav23
, Section 1.3]
. Motivated by this, we provide strong numerical evidence toward a
stacky

P=C
conjecture in our setting. More precisely, it is verified that the graded dimensions of the perverse and the Chern filtrations match in all degrees for the moduli stacks we compute, cf. Theorem
6.9
. A systematic study of the stacky
P=C
conjecture would likely require a better understanding of the interaction between BPS integrality and the Chern filtration.

1.1.

Our goal in this paper is to study the cohomology rings

Let

2.
Geometric relations

We will now focus on proving three families of relations — we call these families
Mumford relations
(
{\mathsf{MR}}
),
generalized Mumford relations
(
{\mathsf{GMR}}
) and
base relations
(
{\mathsf{BR}}
) — among the generators of the descendent algebra. In other words, we will construct explicit ideals
{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}_{d,\chi}
,
{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{GMR}}}_{d,\chi}
and
{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{BR}}}_{d,\chi}
, and show that

{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}_{d,\chi},{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{GMR}}}_{d,\chi},{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{BR}}}_{d,\chi}\subset{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}\,.

We will denote by
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{Geom}}_{d,\chi}
the ideal generated by
{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}_{d,\chi},{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{GMR}}}_{d,\chi},{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{BR}}}_{d,\chi}
. As we will explain, the determination of
{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{GMR}}}_{d,\chi}
requires knowledge of the cohomology rings of
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}}
for ‘smaller’ values
(d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
, leading to an inductive approach to understanding this ideal and more generally the cohomology ring structure.

Having defined these ideals, the natural question is the following (cf. Question
0.8
):

Question 2.1
.

Do we have
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{Geom}}_{d,\chi}={\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}
?

If the answer to this question were positive, we would have a recursive algorithm to compute the cohomology rings of
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
. We will look into Question
2.1
in Section
5.2
for small
d
; it turns out that the answer is positive for
d\leq 4
and
(d,\chi)=(5,2)
, but negative for
(d,\chi)=(5,1)
.

We work at the level of stacks where universal sheaves exist, but this also gives relations for the good moduli spaces
M_{d,\chi}
when
\gcd(d,\chi)=1
by Lemma
1.16
. More precisely, if we define

I^{\bullet}_{d,\chi}=\eta({\mathfrak{I}}^{\bullet}_{d,\chi})\quad\textup{for \,}\bullet={\mathsf{MR}},{\mathsf{GMR}},{\mathsf{BR}},{\mathsf{Geom}}

then we have
I^{\mathsf{Geom}}_{d,\chi}\subset I_{d,\chi}
.

2.1.
Mumford relations

As explained in Section
0.2
, one general approach to proving geometric relations on the cohomology of moduli spaces is to construct some vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
out of the moduli data (in our case, the universal sheaf
{\mathbb{F}}
) with a rank
r
that we can compute by Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch; then the vanishing of Chern classes

c_{j}({\mathcal{V}})=0\quad\textup{for }j>r\,

may give non-trivial relations. Usually
{\mathcal{V}}
is first constructed as a complex, and then shown to be a vector bundle by some cohomological vanishing. Mumford relations follow this strategy applied to the following vanishing result:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
F
be a semistable sheaf of type
(d,\chi)
. Assume

\chi\geq g_{d}\coloneqq\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2},

then
H^{1}(F)=H^{2}(F)=0
.

Proof.

This is proven in
[
DM11
, Proposition 2.1.3]
, but we provide a proof for completeness. The vanishing
H^{2}(F)=0
is obvious since
F
is one-dimensional. Let
C^{\prime}
be the scheme-theoretic support of
F
, thus
0<d^{\prime}:=\deg(C^{\prime})\leq d
. Suppose that
H^{1}(F)\neq 0
, we can pick an element
s\neq 0
in the dual

H^{1}(C^{\prime},F)^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}(F,\omega_{C^{\prime}})=\mathrm{Hom}(F,\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}(-3+d^{\prime})).

This gives rise to a non-zero morphism

s:F\to\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}(-3+d^{\prime}),

whose image we denote by
G(-3+d^{\prime})
with
G\subset\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}
. By the semistability of
F
, we have

(9)

\frac{\chi}{d}\leq\frac{\chi(G(-3+d^{\prime}))}{\deg G}=\frac{\chi(G)}{\deg G}-3+d^{\prime}.

To estimate the RHS, we consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime\prime}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}_{C_{\mathrm{pure}}^{\prime\prime}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{0.}}

Here
C^{\prime\prime}
is a closed subvariety of
C^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{pure}}}
is the quotient of
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime\prime}}
by its torsion part
T
. Thus it is the structure sheaf of a planar curve of degree
0\leq d^{\prime\prime}<d^{\prime}
. We have

\frac{\chi(G)}{\deg G}\leq\frac{\chi(\widetilde{G})}{\deg G}=\frac{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{pure}}})}{d^{\prime}-d^{\prime\prime}}.

Using the formula
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C_{d}})=1-g_{d}=1-\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}
for any degree
d
planar curve, an elementary computation shows that

\max_{0\leq d^{\prime\prime}<d^{\prime}\leq d}\left(\frac{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{pure}}})}{d^{\prime}-d^{\prime\prime}}-3+d^{\prime}\right)=\frac{d-3}{2}.

It follows from (
9
) that
\chi\leq\frac{d(d-3)}{2}
, but this contradicts our assumption
\chi\geq\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}
.
∎

By the cohomology and base change theorem, it follows that for
(d,\chi)
with
\chi\geq g
the complex
Rp_{\ast}{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{b}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})
is actually a vector bundle with fibers
H^{0}(F)
over
[F]\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
. Moreover, the rank of this vector bundle is
\chi
. It follows that
c_{j}(Rp_{\ast}{\mathcal{F}})=0
for every
j>\chi\geq g
. To write down this formula in terms of descendents, Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch yields

{\mathrm{ch}}(Rp_{\ast}{\mathcal{F}})=p_{\ast}\big({\mathrm{ch}}({\mathcal{F}}){\mathrm{td}}(T_{p})\big)=p_{\ast}\big({\mathrm{ch}}({\mathcal{F}})q^{\ast}{\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})\big)\,,

so
{\mathrm{ch}}_{j}(Rp_{\ast}{\mathcal{F}})
is given by the geometrical realization of
{\mathrm{ch}}_{j}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))
. Thus by Newton’s identity, the total Chern class
c(Rp_{\ast}{\mathcal{F}})
is the geometric realization of

\exp\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!{\mathrm{ch}}_{j}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))\right).

The upshot of the above discussion is the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
j>\chi\geq g
. Then

\left[\exp\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!{\mathrm{ch}}_{j}({\mathrm{td}}(\mathbb{P}^{2}))\right)\right]_{2j}\in{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}\,.

For a fixed
(d,\chi)
, we can use the isomorphisms
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}\cong{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\pm\chi+kd}
to obtain relations by Proposition
2.3
if we take sufficiently large
k
. Given an arbitrary
(d,\chi)
, a choice of signs
\pm
and
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
j>\pm\chi+kd\geq g
, we get a relation

{\mathsf{MR}}_{d,\chi}^{\pm,k,j}\in{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}

which corresponds to

\left[\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!{\mathrm{ch}}_{k}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))\right)\right]_{2j}\in{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\pm\chi+kd}\,.

under the ismomorphism
{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}\cong{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\pm\chi+kd}
.

We define the ideal

{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{MR}}_{d,\chi}:=\big\langle{\mathsf{MR}}_{d,\chi}^{\pm,k,j}\big|\,j>\pm\chi+kd\geq g\big\rangle\subset{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}\,.

The relations of the form

2.2.
Generalized Mumford relations

Following the same strategy but using cohomological vanishing provided by stability, we can construct a generalized version of Mumford relations.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
F,F^{\prime}
be semistable sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of topological types
(d,\chi)
and
(d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
, respectively. Suppose that

(13)

\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{d^{\prime}}<\frac{\chi}{d}<\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{d^{\prime}}+3\,.

Then we have

\operatorname{Hom}(F,F^{\prime})=\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(F,F^{\prime})=0\,.

Proof.

The vanishing
\mathrm{Hom}(F,F^{\prime})=0
follows from the first inequality in (
13
) and the semistability of
F
and
F^{\prime}
. To see that
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(F,F^{\prime})=0
, we use Serre duality

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(F,F^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(F^{\prime},F\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-3))^{\vee},

which vanishes due to the second inequality in (
13
).
∎

Remark 2.9
.

There are several seemingly different ways to produce generalized Mumford relations. First, one can easily check that

and look at

Definition 2.11
.

We say that
(d^{\prime},n^{\prime})\prec(d,n)
if either
d^{\prime}<d
, or
d^{\prime}=d
and
\gcd(d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})<\gcd(d,\chi)
. We define the ideal of generalized Mumford relations to be

{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{GMR}}_{d,\chi}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})\prec(d,\chi)\\
\textrm{satisfying }\eqref{eq: inequalitygmr}\end{subarray}}{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}_{\alpha}\subset{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}.

Remark 2.12
.

As pointed out in Section
5.2.5
, the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{Geom}}_{5,1}
turns out to be strictly smaller than
{\mathfrak{I}}_{5,1}
. On the other hand, we have
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{Geom}}_{5,2}={\mathfrak{I}}_{5,2}
. Thus from an algorithmic point of view, it could make sense to extend
\prec
so that
(5,2)\prec(5,1)
and include
{\mathfrak{I}}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},5,2}_{5,1}
in the geometric relations. We have not been able to check if this produces the missing relations in
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{Geom}}_{5,1}
due to the computational complexity involved.

Remark 2.13
.

On the moduli space level, the generalized Mumford relations
I_{d,\chi}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},1,\chi^{\prime}}
already played a fundamental role in some recent developments understanding the cohomology rings. For example, one key step in Theorem
1.9
(i) consists of showing that these relations express all (normalized) tautological classes in terms of the
3d-7
generators, and Theorem
1.7
follows from a careful study of the three relations in Theorem
1.9
(ii), which can be produced by
I_{d,\chi}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},1,\chi^{\prime}}
. The current paper builds on these progresses and explore the full power of generalized Mumford relations; indeed, they make up the majority of all geometric relations we formulate.

2.3.
Base relations

The set of relations that we discuss now have a different nature; they use the Hilbert–Chow morphism
h\colon{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}\to|d\cdot H|
in a crucial way. Recall that
|d\cdot H|={\mathbb{P}}^{b}
where
b=d(d+3)/2
and that
{\mathrm{ch}}_{1}(H^{2})=c_{0}(2)
is the pull-back of the hyperplane class via
h
. It follows that
\mathrm{ch}_{1}(H^{2})^{b+1}=0
. More generally, the following is true:

Proposition 2.14
.

For any
i_{1},\ldots,i_{b+1}\geq 1
,

{\mathrm{ch}}_{i_{1}}(H^{2})\cdots{\mathrm{ch}}_{i_{b+1}}(H^{2})\in{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}

is a tautological relation.

Proof.

By the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem, the Chern character
\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{F}})
is a cycle pushed forward from
\mathrm{supp}({\mathcal{F}})\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Recall the geometric realization

{\mathrm{ch}}_{i}(H^{2})=\Big[p_{\ast}\big({\mathrm{ch}}({\mathcal{F}})\cdot q^{\ast}H^{2})\Big]_{2i}\in H^{2i}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}).

If we pick a point
{\mathsf{p}}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
to represent the class
H^{2}
, then
q^{*}H^{2}
can be identified with
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}\times\{{\mathsf{p}}\}
, and the class
\mathrm{ch}_{i}(H^{2})
is a push-forward from the intersection
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})\cap({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}\times\{{\mathsf{p}}\})
. In particular, a sheaf
[F]\in[\mathrm{ch}_{i}(H^{2})]\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
must be supported at the point
{\mathsf{p}}
. Now we pick
b+1
points
{\mathsf{p}}_{1},{\mathsf{p}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathsf{p}}_{b+1}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
in general position, then there are no degree
d
curves passing through all of them. It follows that

{\mathrm{ch}}_{i_{1}}(H^{2})\cdots{\mathrm{ch}}_{i_{b+1}}(H^{2})=0.\qed

We define the ideal of base relations

{\mathfrak{I}}^{\mathsf{BR}}_{d,\chi}:=\left\langle{\mathrm{ch}}_{i_{1}}(H^{2})\cdots{\mathrm{ch}}_{i_{b+1}}(H^{2})\mid i_{1},\ldots,i_{b+1}\geq 1\right\rangle\subset{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}.

Note that the base relations all have cohomological degrees
\geq 2(b+1)
.

3.
Virasoro representations

In this section, we introduce the Virasoro operators and explain how they can be used to study the cohomology ring of the moduli stacks and spaces. We explain the Virasoro constraints for moduli spaces
M_{d,\chi}
with
\gcd(d,\chi)=1
proven in
[
LM24
]
and an important consequence: the ideal of tautological relations is preserved by the Virasoro operators, hence inducing a representation of
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}
on
H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})
. In fact, such a property is proven in
loc. cit.
for any
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
with
(d,\chi)
not necessarily coprime. Second, we refine the preservation of the ideal under the Virasoro operators to the ideal of each type
{\mathsf{MR}}
/
{\mathsf{GMR}}
/
{\mathsf{BR}}
/
{\mathsf{Geom}}
. Furthermore, we prove that each ideal is generated by suitable primitive elements under the Virasoro operators. Working with the Virasoro operators and primitive relations is computationally much more effective than working directly with the entire ideals.

3.1.
Virasoro operators and Virasoro constraints

Recall the definition of the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
from Section
1.1
. The Virasoro operators
\{{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\}_{n\geq-1}
acting on
{\mathbb{D}}
is defined as a sum
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}={\mathsf{R}}_{n}+{\mathsf{T}}_{n}
of the two operators:
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}
is a derivation operator such that

{\mathsf{R}}_{n}({\mathrm{ch}}_{i}(\gamma)):=i(i+1)\cdots(i+n){\mathrm{ch}}_{i+n}(\gamma)

and
{\mathsf{T}}_{n}
is a multiplication operator by the element

{\mathsf{T}}_{n}:=\sum_{a+b=n,\,a,b\geq 0}\sum_{i}a!b!\,(-1)^{2-d_{i}^{L}}{\mathrm{ch}}_{a}(\gamma_{i}^{L})\cdot{\mathrm{ch}}_{b}(\gamma_{i}^{R}),

where
\gamma_{i}^{L}
and
\gamma_{i}^{R}
are the same as in (
14
). Note that
{\mathsf{L}}_{-1}={\mathsf{R}}_{-1}
agrees with Definition
1.2
. One can check that the operators satisfy the Virasoro bracket relations

[{\mathsf{R}}_{n},{\mathsf{R}}_{m}]=(m-n){\mathsf{R}}_{n+m},\quad[{\mathsf{L}}_{n},{\mathsf{L}}_{m}]=(m-n){\mathsf{L}}_{n+m}.

The weight zero Virasoro operator is defined as a combination of the Virasoro operators

{\mathsf{L}}_{{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}}:=\sum_{n\geq-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n+1)!}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\circ{\mathsf{L}}_{-1}^{n+1}:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}\,.

Virasoro constraints in sheaf theory have been studied in
[
BLM22
]
building on the previous works
[
MOOP22
,
Mor22
,
vB23
]
. A proof of the Virasoro constraints for the moduli spaces
M_{d,\chi}
with
\gcd(d,\chi)=1
is given in
[
LM24
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
LM24
]
)
.

Given
d
and
\chi
coprime, we have

\int_{M_{d,\chi}}{\mathsf{L}}_{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}(D)=0\quad\text{for all}\;\;D\in{\mathbb{D}}\,.

For concrete calculations, it is useful to record here the normalized formulation of the Virasoro constraints (cf.
[
BLM22
, Conjecture 2.15]
) in terms of the generators
c_{k}(j)
. It has been explained in Section
1.2
how the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}
is identified with the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{1}(1),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
. In particular, the operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}
and
{\mathsf{T}}_{n}
can be interpreted as operators acting on
{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{1}(1),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
, and we have

{\mathsf{R}}_{n}(c_{k}(j))=(k+j-1)\cdots(k+j-1+n)c_{k+n}(j)\,.

As discussed in Section
1.2
, there is a normalized realization

{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{1}(1),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]\to H^{*}(M_{d,\chi})

that sends

to zero. The normalized Virasoro operators

on

are given by

{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\delta}={\mathsf{R}}_{n}+{\mathsf{T}}_{n}-\frac{(n+1)!}{d}{\mathsf{R}}_{-1}\circ c_{n+1}(1)\,.

Corollary 3.2
.

Given
d
and
\chi
coprime, we have

\int_{M_{d,\chi}}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\delta}(D)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad n\geq 0,\;\,D\in{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{1}(1),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]\,.

Proof.

This follows from the standard form of the Virasoro constraints in Theorem

and
[

. Note that the definition of the realization of

also involves the twisting operators

from Section

and that these commute with the Virasoro operators by

, Lemma 2.19]
.
∎

3.2.
Virasoro representation

Recall from the last section that we have a representation of the half of the Virasoro algebra
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}
on
{\mathbb{D}}
. On the other hand, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}_{d,\chi}\rightarrow H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})\rightarrow 0\,.

It turns out that as a consequence of Virasoro constraints, one can show that the representation of

descends to the cohomology of the stack.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
LM24
]
)
.

The representation of
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}

preserves the ideal of relations

. Hence, the operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}
descend to the cohomology

, i.e. there is a dashed arrow completing the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi}

Remark 3.4
.

Note that
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}
acts on
{\mathbb{D}}
via either
\{{\mathsf{R}}_{n}\}_{n\geq-1}
or

trivially preserves the ideal

\{{\mathsf{R}}_{n}\}_{n\geq-1}
.

Remark 3.5
.

The most natural way to prove a statement like Theorem
3.3
is to utilize the approximation of
H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})
by the cohomology of moduli spaces of Joyce–Song pairs
P^{N}_{d,\chi}
, see the proof of Theorem
4.4
. If we knew the pair Virasoro constraints (cf.
[
BLM22
, Conjecture 2.18]
) for Joyce–Song pairs, we could deduce that
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}
descends to the cohomology
H^{*}(P^{N}_{d,\chi})
, and hence to the cohomology of the stack by approximation. The Virasoro constraints for
P^{N}_{d,\chi}
are equivalent to the Virasoro constraints for the Joyce invariant classes
[M_{d,\chi}]^{\mathrm{inv}}
for every
(d,\chi)
, but currently we only know these unconditionally when
\gcd(d,\chi)=1
. This problem is worked around in
[
LM24
]
by identifying
{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi}
with a stack of quiver representations and approximating it by framed quiver representations, which are the analogue of Joyce–Song pairs in the quiver context.

We can also make a statement for the cohomology of the good moduli spaces by using the normalized realization. We have an exact sequence

0\to I_{d,\chi}\to{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]\to H^{*}(M_{d,\chi})\to 0\,.

Define the derivation
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}
on
{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{1}(1),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
by

{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}={\mathsf{R}}_{n}-\frac{(n+1)!}{d}c_{n+1}(1)\circ{\mathsf{R}}_{-1}\,.

This derivation satisfies the property that
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}(c_{1}(1))=0
for every
n\geq 0
, so it induces an operator on
{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
which we denote in the same way. Note that
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\delta}={\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}+{\mathsf{T}}_{n}^{\delta}
where
{\mathsf{T}}_{n}^{\delta}={\mathsf{T}}_{n}-\frac{(n+1)!}{d}c_{n}(1)
is linear.

The following proposition is immediate:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
{\mathfrak{I}}\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{1}(1),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
be an ideal closed under the action of the operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}
,
n\geq-1
. Then the ideal
I\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
obtained as the image of
{\mathfrak{I}}
by the quotient setting
c_{1}(1)
to 0 is closed under the operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}
,
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}
and
{\mathsf{R}}_{-1}
preserve the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}
, the operator
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}
also preserves the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}
, so the induced operator
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}
on the quotient
{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
preserves the ideal
I
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
d,\chi
be coprime and
n\geq 0
. The derivation
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}
preserves the ideal
I_{d,\chi}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
above, this is a consequence of the stacky analogue (Theorem
3.3
) and Lemma
1.16
. However, it is instructive to give a proof directly from the normalized form of the Virasoro constraints, which we do below.

Suppose that
D\in I_{d,\chi}
and let
E\in{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
be arbitrary. By the Virasoro constraints in the form of Corollary
3.2
,

0=\int_{M_{d,\chi}}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\delta}(D\cdot E)=\int_{M_{d,\chi}}{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}(D)\cdot E+\int_{M_{d,\chi}}D\cdot{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\delta}(E)=\int_{M_{d,\chi}}{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}(D)\cdot E\,.

Since
E
was arbitrary and the realization map
{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]\to H^{*}(M_{d,\chi})
is surjective, we conclude by Poincaré duality that
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}(D)\in I_{d,\chi}
.
∎

3.3.
Preservation of geometric relations

In this subsection, we study preservation of the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bullet}_{d,\chi}
under the
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}
-representation for each
\bullet={\mathsf{MR}},{\mathsf{GMR}},{\mathsf{BR}},{\mathsf{Geom}}
. The
{\mathsf{BR}}
case is trivial, and the
{\mathsf{Geom}}
case follows from the other three cases by definition. So we study the
{\mathsf{MR}}
and
{\mathsf{GMR}}

3.3.1.
Mumford relations

We prove here the following proposition:

Proposition 3.8
.

The ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}
is closed under the action of
U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
. More is true: for each choice of sign
\pm
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the ideal generated by
{\mathsf{MR}}_{d,\chi}^{\pm,k,j}
for
j>\pm\chi+kd
is closed under the action of
U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
.

Proof.

The Virasoro operators interact well with respect to the isomorphisms
{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}\cong{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\pm\chi+kd}
, see Proposition
1.8
(ii), so it is enough to treat the case
\pm=+
and
k=0
. To simplify notation, we write
A_{j}={\mathsf{MR}}_{d,\chi}^{+,0,j}
. Recall that
A_{j}
is the degree
2j
term of

A=\exp\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(-1)^{\ell-1}(\ell-1)!{\mathrm{ch}}_{\ell}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))\right)\,.

To compute
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}(A_{j})
, we apply
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}
to the exponential:

{\mathsf{R}}_{n}(A)=\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(-1)^{\ell-1}(\ell+n)!{\mathrm{ch}}_{\ell+n}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))\right)A\,

hence

{\mathsf{R}}_{n}(A_{j})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell-1}(\ell+n)!{\mathrm{ch}}_{\ell+n}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))A_{j-\ell}\,.

On the other hand, we also have

(j+n)A_{j+n}={\mathsf{R}}_{0}(A_{j+n})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j+n}(-1)^{\ell-1}\ell!{\mathrm{ch}}_{\ell}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))A_{j+n-\ell}\,.

Assume first that
n\geq 0
. Comparing the two equations we find that

(15)

{\mathsf{R}}_{n}(A_{j})=(-1)^{n}(j+n)A_{j+n}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+\ell}\ell!{\mathrm{ch}}_{\ell}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))A_{j+n-\ell}\,.

If
j>\chi
, then
j+n-\ell\geq j>\chi
, so clearly the right hand side is also in
{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}
. When
n=-1
, we obtain

{\mathsf{R}}_{-1}(A_{j})=\big({\mathrm{ch}}_{0}({\mathrm{td}}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}))-j+1\big)A_{j-1}=\big(\chi-j+1\big)A_{j-1}\,.

If
j>\chi+1
, then
j-1>\chi
and
A_{j-1}\in{\mathfrak{I}}_{d,\chi}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}
; when
j=\chi+1
the factor
\chi-j+1
vanishes, so
{\mathsf{R}}_{-1}(A_{\chi+1})=0
is also in the ideal.
∎

By the definition of
I_{d,\chi}^{\mathsf{MR}}
and Proposition
3.6
the corollary below follows:

Corollary 3.9
.

The ideal
I_{d,\chi}^{\mathsf{MR}}
is closed under the action of the operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}
for
n\geq 0
.

3.3.2.
Generalized Mumford relations

We proceed now to prove that the generalized Mumford relations are also preserved by the action of the Virasoro operators.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\alpha=(d,\chi)
,
\alpha^{\prime}=(d^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
satisfy (
13
). Then the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}
is closed under the action of
U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
.

Proof.

Recall that the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}
is generated by relations of the form
{\mathsf{GMR}}^{\alpha^{\prime},j,A}_{\alpha}
where
j>dd^{\prime}
and
A\in H_{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
. They are obtained by starting with the formal class
C_{j}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
, realizing the second factor
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\to H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha}^{\prime})
and then integrating against
A
. The crucial ingredient in the proof is Theorem

, which we prove in the appendix. This is a combinatorial identity which interacts
C
with the action of the Virasoro operators; it states that we have an equality

(16)

\displaystyle({\mathsf{R}}_{n}\otimes\textup{id})(C_{j})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=-1}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k+1}(\textup{id}\otimes{\mathsf{R}}_{k})(C_{j+n-k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq a+b\leq n}\sum_{i}(-1)^{d_{i}^{L}+1}\frac{a!(n-a)!}{(n-a-b)!}\big({\mathrm{ch}}_{a}(\gamma_{i}^{L})\otimes{\mathrm{ch}}_{b}(\gamma_{i}^{R})\big)C_{j+n-a-b}

in
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
. Since the operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{k}
descend to
H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
by Theorem
3.3
, the equality (
16
) can be also viewed in
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\otimes H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
. Since
H_{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
is dual to
H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
, we have dual operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\dagger}\colon H_{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})\to H_{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
on homology. Applying
\textup{id}\otimes\int_{A}
to (
16
) we obtain

(17)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{R}}_{n}({\mathsf{GMR}}^{\alpha^{\prime},j,A}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=-1}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k+1}{\mathsf{GMR}}^{\alpha^{\prime},j+n-k,{\mathsf{R}}_{k}^{\dagger}(A)}_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq a+b\leq n}\sum_{i}(-1)^{d_{i}^{L}+1}\frac{a!(n-a)!}{(n-a-b)!}{\mathrm{ch}}_{a}(\gamma_{i}^{L}){\mathsf{GMR}}_{\alpha}^{\alpha^{\prime}\!,\,j+n-a-b,\,{\mathrm{ch}}_{b}(\gamma_{i}^{R})\cap A}\,.

Note that for
k\leq n
and
a+b\leq n
we have
j+n-k,\,j+n-a-b\geq j>dd^{\prime}
, so we conclude that
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}({\mathsf{GMR}}^{\alpha^{\prime},j,A}_{\alpha})\in{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}
.∎

Once again, a similar statement can be made at the level of the good moduli space:

Corollary 3.11
.

The ideal
I_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}
is closed under the action of the operators
{\mathsf{R}}_{n}^{\delta}
for
n\geq 0
.

3.4.
Primitive relations

Knowing that
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bullet}_{\alpha}
is closed under the action of
\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1}
can provide a strong simplification on the computations. We now proceed to present and explain a considerably smaller set of generators of the ideals
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{\bullet}
when we regard them as a
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\otimes U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
-module, rather than just as an ideal in
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}
. Here, the algebra structure of
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\otimes U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
is induced from that of
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}
and
U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
together with the commutation rule
[{\mathsf{R}}_{k},D]={\mathsf{R}}_{k}(D)
.

Definition 3.12
.

We define the set
\mathfrak{P}^{\bullet}_{\alpha}
of primitive relations of three types as follows:

(i)

\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha}^{\mathsf{MR}}
is the set of relations
{\mathsf{MR}}^{\pm,k,j}_{\alpha}
with
\pm\chi+kd\geq g
and
j=\pm\chi+kd+1
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}
is the set of relations
{\mathsf{GMR}}^{\alpha^{\prime},j,A}_{\alpha}
with either

j=dd^{\prime}+1\textup{ or }\big(j=dd^{\prime}+2\textup{ and }\deg(A)=dd^{\prime}+2\big)\,.

(iii)

\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha}^{\mathsf{BR}}
consists of the single relation
{\mathrm{ch}}_{1}(H^{2})^{b+1}
.

Clearly each of these sets are contained in the corresponding ideals including non-primitive relations. For example,
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{\mathsf{MR}}
is generated by all relations
{\mathsf{MR}}^{\pm,k,j}_{\alpha}
with
j\geq\pm\chi+kd+1
, but
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha}^{\mathsf{MR}}
consists only of the first Chern class beyond the rank. It turns out that the primitive relations are enough to generate all the relations once we know that the ideals are closed under the Virasoro action.

Theorem 3.13
.

The ideals
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{MR}}},{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}},{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{BR}}}
are the smallest ideals containing
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}
,
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}
,
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{BR}}}
, respectively, that are closed under the action of
U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
.

Proof.

We have already shown that the ideals
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{MR}}}
,
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{GMR}},\alpha^{\prime}}
,
{\mathfrak{I}}_{\alpha}^{{\mathsf{BR}}}
are closed under the action of
U(\mathsf{Vir}_{\geq-1})
and they clearly contain the primitive relations, so it is enough to show that they are the smallest such ones.

We start with the ideal of Mumford relations. As we did in the proof of Proposition
3.8
, it is enough to consider
k=0
and
\pm=+
. Considering equation (
15
) for
n=1
, it is clear that if
A_{j}
is in some ideal closed under
{\mathsf{R}}_{1}
, then
A_{j+1}
is also in such ideal. Since

4.
BPS integrality

In this section, we recall cohomological Hall algebras (CoHA in short) and BPS integrality of Mozgovoy–Reineke
[
MR15
]
. By establishing tautological generation and purity of the cohomology of the moduli stacks, we relate the Poincaré series of the moduli stacks to the intersection Poincaré polynomials of the good moduli spaces and vice versa. As applications, we prove structural formulas for the Poincaré series of the moduli stacks and free generation of cohomology rings in low degrees.

4.1.
Cohomological Hall algebra

Denote the monoid of topological types of one-dimensional sheaves by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{SES}_{\mu}}

6.

In this final section, we formulate the strengthened version of the
P=C
conjecture and verify several predictions on the cohomology rings of the moduli spaces (mostly

) in Theorem

. Again, it suffices to verify each of the conjectures for

.

6.1.
The
P=C
conjecture.

6.1.1.
The perverse filtration

We start by recalling the construction of the perverse filtration
[
dC16
, Lecture 5]
; see also
[
KPS23
, Section 1.1]
. Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper morphism between irreducible varieties. For simplicity, we assume that
X
and
Y
are both smooth and projective. The perverse truncation functors
[
BBD82
]
filter the derived push-forward complex
Rf_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X}
, thus inducing an increasing filtration

P_{0}H^{*}(X)\subset P_{1}H^{*}(X)\subset\cdots\subset H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})

called the perverse filtration. Intuitively, it measures the complexity of the fibers of the proper map
f:X\to Y
.

We recall the following useful properties of the perverse filtration:

(i)

Let
\eta
be an ample class on
Y
, and denote by
\xi:=f^{*}\eta
its pull-back to
X
. Then the perverse filtration on
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is completely determined by the multiplication by
\xi
operator; see
[
dCM05
, Proposition 5.2.4]
and
[
KPS23
, Proposition 1.1]
for the explicit formula.

(ii)

Let
r
be the
defect of semismallness
of
f
:

r:=\dim X\times_{Y}X-\dim X

When
f
is equidimensional, this is simply the relative dimension
\dim X-\dim Y
. Then the perverse filtration
P_{\bullet}H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})
stabilizes in
2r
steps, i.e.
P_{2r}H^{*}(X)=H^{*}(X)
.

(iii)

The graded pieces
\mathrm{gr}^{P}_{\bullet}H^{*}(X)
of the perverse filtration satisfy two Hard Lefschetz type symmetries
[
dCM05
, Theorem 2.1.4]
. They arise respectively from the pull-back of an ample class on
Y
and a relative ample class on
X
.

Consider the moduli space
M_{d,\chi}
with
\gcd(d,\chi)=1
and the Hilbert–Chow map

h:M_{d,\chi}\longrightarrow|d\cdot H|.

This is a proper map with generic fibers being abelian varieties. Its topology is encoded in the perverse filtration
P_{\bullet}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi},\mathbb{Q})
. This is one side of the
P=C
conjecture.

6.1.2.
The Chern filtration

We turn to the Chern side next. Recall that the (local) Chern filtration
C_{\bullet}H^{\leq 2d-4}(M_{d,\chi})
was defined in
[
KPS23
, Section 0.2]
for coprime
M_{d,\chi}
.
By definition, it involves only the normalized tautological
generators
in Theorem
1.9
(i). In order to extend this to a filtration on the entire cohomology rings, it is natural to include all normalized tautological
classes.
We thus define the (global) Chern filtration

(37)

C_{k}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi})\coloneqq\mathrm{span}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{s}c_{k_{i}}(j_{i})\,\Bigg|\,\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}\leq k\right\}\subset H^{*}(M_{d,\chi},\mathbb{Q})

where we impose no restriction on the cohomological degree, and
c_{k_{i}}(j_{i})
is allowed to be any normalized class.
15
15

15

This corrects the mismatch between perversity and the Chern degrees in
[
KPS23
, Remark 2.10]
.
Equivalently, this is the smallest multiplicative filtration where each class
c_{k}(j)
lies in the
k
-th step of the filtration.

The definition above has the advantage of being explicit and computable, but apparently relies on the normalization in Section
1.2
. Recall that the normalized classes are closely related to the weight zero descendent algebra via the identification
{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]\simeq{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha,{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}}
, where
\alpha=(d,\chi)
as usual. We now explain how the latter can be used to give a more canonical definition of the Chern filtration, without any choice of normalization. To motivate this, we note that the previous definition (
37
) can be viewed as being induced from a parallel Chern filtration
C_{\bullet}{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
on the free polynomial ring, via the surjective map

{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]\twoheadrightarrow H^{*}(M_{d,\chi}).

We can also define the Chern filtration
C_{\bullet}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})
for the stacks in the same way, using a surjective map from
C_{\bullet}{\mathbb{Q}}[c_{0}(2),c_{1}(1),c_{2}(0),c_{1}(2),\cdots]
for any
(d,\chi)
.

Definition 6.1
.

The Chern filtration
\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}
is the minimal multiplicative filtration such that

{\mathrm{ch}}_{k}(H^{j})\in\widetilde{C}_{k-j+1}\,{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}
for any
k>0
and
j=0,1,2
. The Chern filtration on the weight zero descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha,{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}}
is defined by restriction, i.e.

\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha,{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}}=\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\cap{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha,{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}}\,.

Remark 6.2
.

Since
\xi({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}(H^{j}))=p_{*}\big({\mathrm{ch}}_{k-j+2}({\mathcal{F}})\cdot q^{*}H^{j}\big)
, the Chern filtration index
(k-j+1)
is one less than the Chern character degree of the universal sheaf we take in the realization. The shift by one can be motivated by the codimension of the sheaves we parametrize.

Remark 6.3
.

The Chern filtrations are preserved under the isomorphism

\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{D}}_{e^{\rho}\cdot\alpha}\xrightarrow{{\mathsf{F}}_{\rho}}\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}

from Section
1.2.2
. Indeed, we have
{\mathsf{F}}_{\rho}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}(H^{j}))={\mathrm{ch}}_{k}(H^{j}\cdot e^{\rho})\in\widetilde{C}_{k-j+1}{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}
, and the multiplicativity shows the inclusion
{\mathsf{F}}_{\rho}(\widetilde{C}_{\bullet})\subseteq\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}
. The other inclusion follows from the same argument applied to
{\mathsf{F}}_{-\rho}
. The same statement holds for the weight zero descendent algebra.

Definition 6.4
.

We define the Chern filtration on the cohomology of the moduli stacks and spaces to be

\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})\coloneqq\textup{Im}(\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha}\overset{\xi}{\longrightarrow}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})),\quad\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi})\coloneqq\textup{Im}(\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{D}}_{\alpha,{\mathrm{wt}_{0}}}\overset{\xi}{\longrightarrow}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi}))

where
\gcd(d,\chi)=1
is assumed for the moduli space.

The next proposition justifies the same choice of terminology for the above definitions.

Proposition 6.5
.

The two definitions of the Chern filtration match, i.e.

C_{\bullet}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})=\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\chi})\quad\textup{and}\quad C_{\bullet}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi})=\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(M_{d,\chi})

Remark 6.7
.

Before presenting more evidence, we discuss a number of structural implications of Conjecture

on both sides of the equality.

(i)

The perverse filtration is in general

(ii)

Appendix A
An identity in the quadratic descendent algebra

, we have

\displaystyle K_{a,b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-1}^{a+b-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k+1}(a+b-k-1)!(b)_{k+1}

\displaystyle=a!b!\sum_{k=-1}^{b-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k+1}\binom{a+b-k-1}{a}=-a!b!\binom{a+b-n-1}{b}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{b-1}\frac{a!(n-a)!}{(n-a-b)!}

where the penultimate identity is (
47
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the following identity holds:

(a)_{m}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}(a+b-\ell)_{m-\ell}(b)_{\ell}\,.

Proof.

This is a polynomial identity in
a
and
b
, so it is enough to prove it for
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
a,b>m
. In this case, we may replace the falling factorials by a quotient of factorials, and the identity we want can be rewritten as

(47)

\binom{a+b-m}{b}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}\binom{a+b-\ell}{a}\,.

This follows by taking the
z^{a+b}
coefficient in the identity of generating series

\frac{z^{a}}{(1-z)^{a+1-m}}=(1-z)^{m}\frac{z^{a}}{(1-z)^{a+1}}.\qed

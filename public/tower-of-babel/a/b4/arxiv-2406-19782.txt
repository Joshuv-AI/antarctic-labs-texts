Abstract
. We exhibit generically nonreduced components of the Hilbert scheme of at least 21 21 points on a smooth variety of dimension at least four. The result was announced in [ Jel23 ] and answers a question [ Ame10 , Problem 3.8] . The method is similar to the one of [ JŠ22 , §6] .



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over a field
\Bbbk
.
The Hilbert scheme of
d
points
\Hilb_{d}(X)
is a moduli space of central importance, with
applications to combinatorics
[
Hai01
,
Hai03
]
, algebra, enumerative
geometry
[
Ric22
]
, and
classical algebraic geometry
[
Bea83
]
. Many of the applications are limited to
the case when
\dim X\leq 2
as in this case
\Hilb_{d}(X)
is
smooth
[
Fog68
]
. For a good and gentle introduction to Hilbert schemes,
see
[
Ber12
]

or
[
MS05
, Chapter 18]
. See
also
[
Jel23
]
for a list of open problems.

The possible singularities of
\bigsqcup_{d}\Hilb_{d}(X)
for
\dim X\geq 3
are only
partially understood. A point
[Z]\in\Hilb_{d}(X)
is smooth for every

Z\subseteq X
which can be embedded into a smooth surface. As a very
particular case, this implies that
\Hilb_{d}(X)
is smooth for
d\leq 3
. In contrast, the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb_{d}(X)
is singular for every

d\geq 4
and
\dim X\geq 3
, in fact for every
x\in X
, any degree

d
subscheme

Z\subseteq V(\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})
gives a singular point
[
MS05
, Cor 18.30]
.

The singularities in the case
\dim X=3
are constrained as the
Hilbert scheme is a critical
locus
[
DS09
]
. Understanding the
singularities is a very active research area, see for
example
[
GGGL23
,
RS23
,
KJS23
,
Rez23
]
.

The singularities in the case
\dim X\geq 16
can be almost
arbitrary: the Hilbert scheme satisfies Murphy’s Law
up to retraction, see
[
Jel20
]
.
For important singularity types, such as nonreduced ones, sharper bounds on
\dim X
are known.
Szachniewicz
[
Sza21
]
proved that
\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{6})

is nonreduced for every
d\geq 13
; it has an embedded component.
See
[
Erm12
,
Sch23
]
for some
results in similar direction on fixed loci.

One instance where
up to retraction
cannot be ignored is when we consider generic
nonreducedness.
In particular, the results above do not prove that the
Hilbert scheme has any generically nonreduced components.
Proving that
such components do exist and already in codimension four is the main aim of the current article.

1.1.
Generic nonreducedness

We work over a field of characteristic zero, in particular over a
perfect field. An irreducible component of a
finite type
\Bbbk
-scheme is either
generically smooth
, that is,
its general point is smooth, or
generically nonreduced
which
means that every point is nonreduced.

The problem is that generic nonreducedness does not propagate along
retractions.
For example, consider

\frac{\Bbbk[\![y]\!]}{(y^{2})}\hookrightarrow\frac{\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!]}{(xy,y^{2})}.

The source of this map is generically nonreduced, while the target is
generically reduced. Geometrically speaking, the above map comes from
a retraction of
V(xy,y^{2})\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}
onto
V(y^{2})\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{1}
by contracting the
x
axis:

Therefore, from
[
Jel20
]
it does not follow
that
\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{16})
admits generically nonreduced
components. Neither it follows from subsequent paper of
Szachniewicz
[
Sza21
]
.
In contrast, in the paper
[
JŠ22
]
the authors show that

\Quot_{8}(\mathbb{A}^{4})
has a generically nonreduced component.

The aim of the present note is to apply the method of
Jelisiejew-Šivic to the case of the Hilbert scheme and show the
following theorem, which resolves
[
Ame10
, Problem 3.8]
. Let

\mathcal{L}_{H}\subseteq\Hilb_{\mathrm{pts}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
denote the locus
of
[Z]
such that
Z=\Spec(A)
is an irreducible scheme
corresponding to the local algebra
A
with Hilbert function
H_{A}=H
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic zero and let
H=(1,4,10,s)
for
s\in\{6,7,8,9\}
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{H}\subseteq\Hilb_{15+s}(\mathbb{A}^{4})
is an
irreducible component, and this component, with the scheme
structure inherited from the Hilbert scheme, is generically
nonreduced. Therefore, the Hilbert scheme

\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{4})
admits generically nonreduced components
for all
d\geq 21
.

Prior to Theorem
1.1
it was not known whether

\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{4})
or
\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{5})
are
reduced

for all
d
.
It remains an open question whether
\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{3})
is nonreduced
for
d
high enough and whether this scheme has generically nonreduced
components, see
[
Jel23
, Problem XIV]
.

There are three main steps of the argument. First, the locus

\mathcal{L}_{H}
is closed for the functions
H
as in theorem. Moreover,
it is contained in a dominant Białynicki-Birula cell, which
implies that on an open subset
U\subseteq\Hilb_{15+s}(\mathbb{A}^{4})

containing
\mathcal{L}_{H}
, the Hilbert scheme admits
a retraction
\pi\colon U\to U^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
which maps any point

[\Spec(S/I)]\in U
to
\Spec(S/\operatorname{in}(I))
, where
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{4}]
and
\operatorname{in}(I)
is the ideal of top degree forms.

Second, primary obstruction yields quadratic equations
for the fibre

\pi^{-1}([Z])\subseteq\left(T_{\Hilb_{15+s}(\mathbb{A}^{4}),[Z]}\right)_{<0}.

Third, for a chosen
[Z_{0}]\in\mathcal{L}_{H}
we computer-check using
Macaulay2
that
the quadrics alone cut out a
4
-dimensional scheme in the affine
space
\left(T_{\Hilb_{15+s}(\mathbb{A}^{4}),[Z_{0}]}\right)_{<0}
. It follows that
\dim\pi^{-1}([Z_{0}])\leq 4
.
The fibre
\pi^{-1}([Z_{0}])
is a cone and has a translation action by

\mathbb{A}^{4}
, so the fibre is equal to
\{Z_{0}+v\ |\ v\in\mathbb{A}^{4}\}
as a set
and hence
\mathcal{L}_{H}
contains an open neighbourhood of
[Z_{0}]
,
so this locus
is a component. A syzygetic argument shows that
the containment
T_{\mathcal{L}_{H},[Z]}\subseteq T_{\Hilb_{15+s}(\mathbb{A}^{4}),[Z]}
is strict for every
[Z]\in\mathcal{L}_{H}
, hence
\mathcal{L}_{H}
cannot be generically reduced.

1.2.
Open questions and possible generalizations

Consider now
\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
and the unique very
compressed Hilbert function
H=H_{n,d}
given by the condition
that there exists a
\delta
such that

H_{n,d}(i)=\begin{cases}\binom{n+i-1}{i}=\dim\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{i}&\mbox{
for }i<\delta\\
0&\mbox{ for }i>\delta\\
d-\sum_{i=0}^{\delta-1}\binom{n+i-1}{i}&\mbox{ for }i=\delta\end{cases}

The locus
\mathcal{L}_{H}\subseteq\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
is irreducible and closed also in this more general
case. We then have three possibilities for a general
[Z]\in\mathcal{L}_{H}
:

SMOOTH

the scheme
[Z]
has only trivial negative tangents, so

\mathcal{L}_{H}
is a component and
[Z]
is a smooth point
of
\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
on this component,

DEFORMS

the scheme
[Z]
has nontrivial negative tangents and
some of them “integrate”, that is,
the fibre
\pi^{-1}([Z])
, which is a cone, contains more points than just

\mathbb{A}^{n}
. In this case
\mathcal{L}_{H}
is not an
irreducible component and without additional information we cannot say much about
whether its points are reduced in
\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
.

NONRED

the scheme
[Z]
has nontrivial negative tangents and

\pi^{-1}([Z])
is, as a topological space, equal to

\mathbb{A}^{n}
. In this case
\mathcal{L}_{H}
is a
generically nonreduced component.

Example 1.2
.

By
[
CEVV09
]
the case
SMOOTH
occurs for example for
H=(1,4,3)
. The case
DEFORMS
occurs for example for
H=(1,4,4)
. The case
NONRED
occurs for
H
as in
Theorem
1.1
.

We stress that above we look at a general point of

\mathcal{L}_{H}
. This makes a difference: for example for
H=(1,6,6)
the case
SMOOTH
occurs, so
\mathcal{L}_{H}
is a
generically smooth component, however
Szachniewicz
[
Sza21
]
found an embedded component of

\Hilb_{13}(\mathbb{A}^{6})
inside
\mathcal{L}_{H}
. It is a
completely open problem to understand whether having an embedded
component is typical or exceptional for
\mathcal{L}_{H}
which fall
into the
SMOOTH
case.

One motivation to discuss the more general situation is the case

n=3
, the Hilbert scheme of
\mathbb{A}^{3}
. Taking
d=96
and

H=H_{3,96}
we
get that
\mathcal{L}_{H}
is too big to fit in the smoothable
component of
\Hilb_{96}(\mathbb{A}^{3})
,
see
[
Iar72
]
. A syzygetic argument, see
Lemma
3.2
below, also shows
that the case
SMOOTH
cannot hold. Moreover, it is known
that the fibre
\pi^{-1}([Z])
for a general
[Z]
is cut out by
quadrics only. Actually, this holds whenever

(T^{2}_{[Z]})_{<-2}=0
, where
T^{2}_{[Z]}\subseteq\Ext^{1}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
is the Schessinger’s functor, see
[
Har10
, Chapter 3]
.
It is possible to obtain the quadrics explicitly using

Macaulay2
. However, the Gröbner basis computation
necessary for determining
\dim\pi^{-1}([Z])
is out of reach, at
least using standard algorithms. We warn the reader that it is not
clear, even intuitively, whether we should expect
NONRED

or
DEFORMS
in this case, since it may be that

\mathcal{L}_{H}
lies in the closure of a compressed (not very
compressed) component similar to the ones discussed
in
[
Iar84
]
.

The question about
H=(1,4,10,s)
in
[
Ame10
]
is also formulated
for
s=10
. In this case one could try the approach above, however
there are
50
negative tangents (see
Lemma
3.2
below) and the approach is
infeasible on our hardware. Of course, perhaps this is
only a question of computational cost, however we prefer to leave
the case
s=10
open, in the hope that it will stimulate further
progress on understanding the Yoneda multiplication in

\Ext^{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
and in particular the primary obstruction.

3.
Preliminaries

We work over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. The
characteristic assumption will be used mostly for justifying the
computations (we believe that the result holds for most
characteristics). Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring and
\mathbb{A}^{n}=\Spec(S)
. For a subscheme
Z\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n}
we denote by

I_{Z}
its ideal and by
\mathcal{O}_{Z}=S/I_{Z}
its coordinate ring.

Proposition 3.1
.

The tangent space to
[Z]\in\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
is
given by
\Hom_{S}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
. This space is canonically
isomorphic to
\Ext^{1}_{S}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
.

Proof.

A self-contained proof for
\Hom_{S}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
can be found
in
[
Str96
]
; also the Ext functor naturally
appears there. The isomorphism

\Hom_{S}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})\to\Ext^{1}_{S}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})

follows from the long exact sequence obtained by applying

\Hom_{S}(-,\mathcal{O}_{Z})
to
0\to I_{Z}\to S\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to 0
.
∎

Further in the paper, when discussing
\Hom_{S}(-,-)
and
\Ext_{S}^{\bullet}(-,-)
, we drop the
subscript
S
from the notation.

If
I_{Z}
is presented as

S^{\oplus d_{2}}\to S^{\oplus d_{1}}\to I_{Z}\to 0,

then
\Hom(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
is the kernel of the natural map

\Hom(S^{\oplus d_{1}},\mathcal{O}_{Z})\to\Hom(S^{\oplus d_{2}},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
.
Computing this kernel is best performed with
a computer.

We propose one example, which is straightforward, but it will
be important in the following. Recall that when
I_{Z}
is
graded, also the tangent space
\Hom_{S}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
is graded
with

\Hom_{S}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{i}=\left\{\varphi\colon I_{Z}\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}\ |\ \varphi((I_{Z})_{j})\subseteq(\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{i+j}\mbox{ for
all }j\right\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
[Z]\in\Hilb_{15+s}(\mathbb{A}^{4})
is given by a

homogeneous
ideal
I_{Z}
and that
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
is very
compressed with Hilbert function
(1,4,10,s)
. Suppose
further that
I_{Z}
is generated by cubics.
Then

\dim\Hom(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{0}=(20-s)s\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dim\Hom(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{-1}\geq 4s^{2}-55s+200.

Proof.

By assumption, the presentation of
I_{Z}
is

S(-5)^{\beta}\oplus S(-4)^{4\cdot(20-s)-35}\to S(-3)^{20-s}\to I_{Z}\to 0.

Let us first look at degree zero.
If we consider the full linear space
\Hom_{\Bbbk}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{0}
, then any relation
between generators of
I_{Z}
is mapped to
(\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{\geq 4}=0
, so
\Hom_{\Bbbk}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{0}=\Hom(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{0}
.

Let us now look at degree one. By similar considerations,
for every linear map
\varphi\in\Hom_{\Bbbk}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{-1}
, the image of
S(-5)^{\beta}
is zero and the
image of
S(-4)^{4\cdot(20-s)-35}
is contained in
the
s
-dimensional space
(\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{3}
. Thus, the relations
in the presentation yield at most
s\cdot\left(4\cdot(20-s)-35\right)
linear-algebraic conditions on the images of
minimal homogeneous generators and so

\dim\Hom(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{-1}\geq 10\cdot(20-s)-s\cdot\left(4\cdot(20-s)-35\right)=4s^{2}-55s+200,

as claimed.
∎

Proposition 3.3

(Very compressed
loci)
.

Let
H
be any very compressed Hilbert function and

\delta
be the largest index such that
H(\delta)\neq 0
. Then the
very compressed locus
\mathcal{L}_{H}
is closed in

\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{n}\times\Gr(H(\delta),\binom{n-1+\delta}{\delta})
and has dimension

n+\binom{n-1+\delta}{\delta}-H(\delta).

Proof.

A point
[Z]\in\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
lies in

\mathcal{L}_{H}
if and only if, first, the support of

[Z]
is a single point
z
and, second, the ideal
I_{Z}

is contained in
\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{\delta}
. The first condition is
closed and the second is closed provided that the first
one is satisfied. The description of
\mathcal{L}_{H}
as a
product is immediate, see
[
Sza21
, Proposition 2.27]
.
∎

s
such that
I_{s}\neq 0

and
(\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{\geq s+1}\neq 0
. Pick a set of minimal
generators of
I_{Z}
and let
g
be an element of lowest
degree. Pick a socle element
h\in\mathcal{O}_{Z}
of highest
degree. Then
\deg(h)\geq s+1>s\geq\deg(g)
. There
exists a homomorphism
\varphi\colon I_{Z}\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}
which
satisfies
\varphi(g)=h
and sends all other
minimal generators to zero. It follows that
\varphi
is
homogeneous of strictly positive degree, equal to

\deg(h)-\deg(g)
.
∎

3.3.
Primary obstructions

Primary obstructions govern the order two part of
deformation theory and can be computed explicitly. We
discuss them below.

Consider two tangent vectors at a point
[Z]\in\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. They yield maps

\varphi_{i}\colon\Spec\left(\frac{\Bbbk[\varepsilon_{i}]}{(\varepsilon_{i}^{2})}\right)\to\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),

for
i=1,2
and two elements

\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in T_{\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),[Z]}\simeq\Ext^{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z}\right).

The two tangent vectors span an at most

2
-dimensional space and the corresponding morphism is

\varphi_{12}\colon\Spec\left(\frac{\Bbbk[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]}{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})^{2}}\right)\to\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),

which restricts to
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}
in the natural
way. We may ask when
\varphi_{12}
does extend to a map

\widetilde{\varphi_{12}}

from
\Spec\left(\Bbbk[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]/(\varepsilon_{1}^{2},\varepsilon_{2}^{2})\right)
. Deformation
theory
[
FGI+05
, Chapter 5]

implies that an extension exists if and only if an
obstruction

ob_{\varphi_{12},\widetilde{\varphi}_{12}}\in\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})

vanishes. The key observation is that we can describe
the obstruction explicitly.

Theorem 3.9

(
[
JŠ22
, Theorem 4.18]
)
.

The obstruction
ob_{\varphi_{12},\widetilde{\varphi}_{12}}
is equal to

\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}+\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1},

where
\circ

denotes Yoneda’s multiplication in
\Ext^{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
applied to
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
.

Let
\mu\colon\Sym^{2}\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})\to\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})

be given by

\mu(\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}):=\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}+\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}.

Consider its transpose

\mu^{\vee}\colon\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}\to\left(\Sym^{2}\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})\right)^{\vee}\simeq\Sym^{2}\left(\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}\right).

As explained in
[
JŠ22
, §4.2]
,
Theorem
3.9
yields the following
corollary. Recall that
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}
is
the cotangent space at
[Z]\in\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Consider the complete local ring

(\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),[Z]},\mathfrak{m}_{[Z]})
. Its truncation to second order satisfies

\frac{\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),[Z]}}{\mathfrak{m}_{[Z]}^{3}}\simeq\frac{\Sym^{\bullet}\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}}{\im\left(\mu^{\vee}\colon\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}\to\Sym^{2}\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}\right)+\left(\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}\right)^{3}}.

3.4.
Primary obstructions and Białynicki-Birula
decompositions

As written, Corollary
3.10
does
not directly involve the dimension of the local ring.
Moreover, we would like to apply it for the fibre of the
Białynicki-Birula decomposition. Both subtleties “cancel out”:
restriction to the fibre gives us an
\mathbb{N}
-grading
which allows to pass from the third neighbourhood to the
full complete local ring.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
[Z]\in\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-fixed

\Bbbk
-point and consider its Białynicki-Birula fibre
\Spec(A)
.
Assume that the subspace

\left(T_{\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),[Z]}\right)_{\leq-2}

is zero.
Then there is a surjection of graded algebras

\frac{\Sym^{\bullet}\left(\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{<0}\right)^{\vee}}{\im(\mu^{\vee}\colon\left(\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{<0}\right)^{\vee}\to\Sym^{2}\left(\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{<0}\right)^{\vee})}\twoheadrightarrow A.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
we already know that there is an open

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-stable neighbourhood
[Z]\in U\subseteq\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
such that the Białynicki-Birula fibre

\pi^{-1}([Z])
is contained in
U^{+}
. Using
Proposition
3.5
we conclude
that
\pi^{-1}([Z])
is a spectrum of an

\mathbb{N}
-graded algebra
B
with
B_{0}=\Bbbk
.

We now employ Corollary
3.10
.
To make the notation lighter, we put
E:=\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
.
The complete local ring
\hat{B}
of
[Z]\in\Spec(B)
is a quotient of the
complete local ring of
[Z]\in\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. Hence,
also the truncation
\hat{B}/\mathfrak{m}^{3}
is a quotient of
the truncation of

\frac{\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\Hilb_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),[Z]}}{\mathfrak{m}^{3}}\simeq\frac{\Sym^{\bullet}E^{\vee}}{\im\left(\mu^{\vee}\colon\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{\vee}\to\Sym^{2}E^{\vee}\right)+\left(E^{\vee}\right)^{3}}.

The cotangent space of
[Z]\in\Spec(B)
has no
nonpositive weights, so
\hat{B}/\mathfrak{m}^{3}
is in fact a
quotient of

\frac{\Sym^{\bullet}\left(E_{<0}\right)^{\vee}}{\im\left(\mu^{\vee}\colon\left(\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{<0}\right)^{\vee}\to\Sym^{2}\left(E_{<0}\right)^{\vee}\right)+\left(\left(E_{<0}\right)^{\vee}\right)^{3}}

We now lift this from infinitesimal second order to a
more global situation using the
\mathbb{N}
-grading.
Consider the map of graded rings

p\colon\Sym^{\bullet}E_{<0}\to B.

By Example
3.6
,
this map is an isomorphism on cotangent spaces. Since

B
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded with
B_{0}=\Bbbk
, the map

p
is a surjection, by induction on the degree.
Moreover, again thanks to the grading and to the fact
that all
E_{<0}=E_{-1}
, we obtain
an isomorphism
\hat{B}/\mathfrak{m}^{3}\simeq B/B_{\geq 3}
.

We summarize the situation on
a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sym^{\bullet}E_{<0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{\Sym^{\bullet}\left(E_{<0}\right)^{\vee}}{\im\left(\mu^{\vee}\colon\left(\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})_{<0}\right)^{\vee}\to\Sym^{2}\left(E_{<0}\right)^{\vee}\right)+\left(\left(E_{<0}\right)^{\vee}\right)^{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{\hat{B}}{\mathfrak{m}^{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{B}{B_{\geq 3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

This shows that the image
p(\im\mu^{\vee})
in
B_{2}
is zero.
The claim follows.
∎

.

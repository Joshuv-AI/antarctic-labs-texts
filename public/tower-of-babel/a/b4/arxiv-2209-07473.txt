Wandering domains in ℂ × ℝ \mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
Ramanpreet Kaur

Abstract
. Approximation theory of entire functions has been used to demonstrate the construction of a map on ℂ × ℝ \mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R} having wandering domains. We also present suitable modification to this construction that helps in obtaining maps with similar characteristics such as infinitely many attracting Fatou components, infinitely many wandering domains having common paths, etc.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
30D05, 37F10

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

In this note, we shall consider the map

F:\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
which is of the following form:

(1)

F(z,w)=(h_{1}(z,w),h_{2}(w)),

where
h_{1}(z,w)
is a transcendental entire function in the variables
z,w
and
h_{2}(w)
is a real valued function. For the construction of the required map, first we shall recall few definitions and results. The Fatou set of
F
, denoted by
\mathscr{F}(F)
, is the largest open set where the family of iterates
\{F^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
F
is normal. A connected component of the Fatou set is called a Fatou component. The complement of the Fatou set is called as Julia set. For more details about the Fatou-Julia theory in several variables, one can refer to
[
3
,
5
]
and the references therein.

In one variable, for a rational function of degree
d\geq 2
, every Fatou component is pre-periodic
[
9
]
. In particular, the periodic Fatou component, say
U
, is one of the following forms.

(1)

Attracting: every point of the component converges to periodic point
z_{0}\in U
under iteration of the function. In this case, the multiplier at the periodic point has modulus less than 1;

(2)

Parabolic: every point of the component converges to periodic point
z_{0}\in\partial U
under iteration of the function;

(3)

Siegel disk: the function on
U
is conformally conjugate to an irrational rotation of a unit disk. In this case, the multiplier at the periodic point has modulus 1;

(4)

Herman ring: the function on
U
is conformally conjugate to an irrational rotation of a standard annulus.

In contrast to this, a transcendental entire function does not have Herman rings. Indeed they may have

(1)

Baker domains: a Fatou component
U
for which the orbit of every point of
U
converges to
z_{0}
and the function is not analytic at
z_{0}
. It is clear that for transcendental entire functions,
z_{0}=\infty
;

(2)

Wandering domains: a Fatou component
U
which is not pre-periodic
[
2
]
.

This led to the study of the classification of Fatou components in higher variables. Several authors have discussed about these components for maps of certain types, for example, recurrent Fatou components of a transcendental Hénon map
[
1
]
, Fatou components of attracting skew products
[
6
]
, etc.

In this note, we shall restrict ourselves to wandering domains corresponding to a map
F
mentioned in
Equation

1
.
We make use of approximation theory to construct an example of wandering domains in
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
. In fact, the construction of such a map is inspired from the construction of a Carleman set used by Singh
[
7
]
. We construct a general Carleman set in one variable. This leads us to construct an example of wandering domains in
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
. We shall prove the following

Theorem 1
.

There exists a mapping
F:\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
having escaping wandering domains.

To apply approximation theory, let us first recall the definition of a Carleman set (in
\mathbb{C}
). For a relatively closed subset
G

of
\mathbb{C}
, and

\mathbb{C}(G)=\{f:G\to\mathbb{C}\mid f\text{ is continuous on }G\text{ and analytic on }G^{\circ}\}.

We call
G
as a Carleman set in
\mathbb{C}
if for any
f\in\mathbb{C}(G)
and for any positive, continuous function
\epsilon
on
G
, there exists an entire function
g
such that

|f(z)-g(z)|<\epsilon(z)\text{ for every }z\in G.

The following is a sufficient condition for a set to be a Carleman set.

Theorem 2

(Nersesjen,
[
4
]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a closed proper subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Then
G
is a Carleman set in
\mathbb{C}
if and only if
G
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus G
is connected.

(ii)

\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus G
is locally connected at
\infty
.

(iii)

For every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
, there exists a neighborhood
V
of
\infty
in
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
such that no components of
G^{\circ}
intersects both
K
and
V
.

2.
Preparation for the Main Result

To construct the desired map, first we construct an infinite number of pairwise disjoint domains with certain properties, so that no two domains are subsets of a single Fatou component of
F
. We prove that our desired map
F
with wandering domains have the following form:

F(z,w)=(e^{f_{1}(z)}+\delta e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)},e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}),\text{ for every }(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}.

Here,
f_{1},f_{2}
are entire functions (existence of such functions is shown during the construction of a Carleman set) and
\delta\in(0,1)
is chosen suitably (one of the conditions is
\frac{17\delta}{16}<\frac{9}{8}
).

Construction of a Carleman set in one variable:

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

B_{k}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-\delta_{k}|\leq l_{k}\},

where the sequences
\{\delta_{k}\}
and
\{l_{k}\}
are chosen such that

•

\{\delta_{k}\}
and
\{l_{k}\}
are strictly increasing real sequences converging to infinity.

•

l_{1}
satisfies
\frac{17\delta}{16}<\frac{l_{1}}{8}
.

•

B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
for every
i\not=j
.

•

B_{1}\cap G_{1}=\emptyset
, where
G_{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-1|\leq 3\}
.

Now, choose a sequence
\{m_{k}\}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\delta_{k}+l_{k}<m_{k}<\delta_{k+1}-l_{k+1}.

For such a sequence, define

M_{k}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\text{Re}z=m_{k}\},k\in\mathbb{N}.

Further, define

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z+r_{k}|\leq s_{k}\},k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\text{ and, }L_{k}

\displaystyle:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\text{Re}z=-t_{k}\},k\in\mathbb{N}

where,

•

\{-r_{k}\}
is chosen to be a decreasing real sequence converging to
-\infty
.

•

\{s_{k}\}
is chosen such that
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
for every
i\not=j
.

•

\{t_{k}\}
is chosen such that
(-r_{k+1}+s_{k+1})<-t_{k}<(-r_{k}+s_{k})
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Using the continuity of the exponential function
z\mapsto e^{z}
, we have

(a)

for every
\epsilon_{k}=\frac{l_{k+1}}{4}
, there exists
\delta^{\prime}_{k}>0
such that

|z-\log(\delta_{k+1})|<\delta^{\prime}_{k}\text{ implies that }|e^{z}-\delta_{k+1}|<\frac{l_{k+1}}{4}.

(b)

As
\frac{s_{1}}{8}>0
, there exists
\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{1}>0
such that

|z-\pi\iota+\log(r_{1})|<\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{1}\text{ implies that }|e^{z}+r_{1}|<\frac{s_{1}}{8}.

(c)

there exists
\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{2}>0
such that

|z|<\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{2}\text{ implies that }|e^{z}-1|<\frac{1}{16}.

Defining the functions
g(z)
and
\epsilon(z)
:

Let,

g(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log(\delta_{k+1}),&z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\pi\iota+\log(r_{1}),&z\in A_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\log 1,&z\in G_{1}\cup\left(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{k}\cup L_{k})\right),\end{array}\right.

and

\epsilon(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta^{\prime}_{k},&z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{1},&z\in A_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{2},&z\in G_{1}\cup\left(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{k}\cup L_{k})\right).\end{array}\right.

Further, let
G=G_{1}\cup\left(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(B_{k}\cup M_{k}\cup A_{k}\cup L_{k})\right)
(See Figure 1). By
Theorem

2
,
G
is a Carleman set. Since
g\in\mathbb{C}(G)
, there exists an entire function
f_{1}(z)
such that

|f_{1}(z)-g(z)|<\epsilon(z),\text{ for every }z\in G.

In particular, we get that

(d)

|f_{1}(z)-\log(\delta_{k+1})|<\delta^{\prime}_{k}
, whenever
z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(e)

|f_{1}(z)-\pi\iota+\log(r_{1})|<\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{1}
, whenever
z\in A_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}.

(f)

|f_{1}(z)-1|<\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{2}
, whenever
z\in G_{1}\cup\left(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{k}\cup L_{k})\right)
.

This further implies that

(g)

|e^{f_{1}(z)}-\delta_{k+1}|<\frac{l_{k+1}}{4}
, whenever
z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(h)

|e^{f_{1}(z)}+r_{1}|<\frac{s_{1}}{8}
, for every
z\in A_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

|e^{f_{1}(z)}-1|<\frac{1}{16},
for
z\in G_{1}\cup(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{k}\cup L_{k}))
.

Now, using continuity of
e^{w}
at origin, there exists
d_{1}>0
such that whenever
|w-\log 1|<d_{1}
, we have
|e^{w}-1|<\frac{1}{16}.

Further, define

g_{1}(w)=\log 1
and
\epsilon_{1}(w)=d_{1},\text{ for }w\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mathbb{R}
is a Carleman set
[
4
]
, there exists an entire function
f_{2}
such that

|f_{2}(w)-g_{1}(w)|<\epsilon_{1}(w),\text{ for every }w\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular,

|f_{2}(w)-\log 1|<d_{1},\text{ for every }w\in\mathbb{R}.

This further implies that

|e^{f_{2}(w)}-1|<\frac{1}{16},\text{ for every }w\in\mathbb{R}.

From here, we can easily observe that

|e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}|=|e^{f_{2}(w)}|<\frac{17}{16},\text{ for every }w\in\mathbb{R}.

L_{k},M_{k},B_{k}
(in the proof of
Theorem

1
) and that of
A_{k}
(in the proof of
Proposition

) under the function
e^{f_{1}(z)}
, for

.

Proof of
Theorem

1
.

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be defined as above. Let

F(z,w)=(e^{f_{1}(z)}+\delta e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)},e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}),\text{ for }(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}.

For wandering domains of the above map, define

\displaystyle B_{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:|z-\delta_{k}|\leq l_{k},|w|<\frac{l_{k}}{8\delta}\},k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle M_{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:\text{Re}z=m_{k},w\in\mathbb{R}\},k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle A_{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:|z+r_{k}|\leq s_{k},|w|\leq\frac{l_{1}}{4}\},k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle L_{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:\text{Re}z=-t_{k},w\in\mathbb{R}\},k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\text{and }G_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:|z-1|\leq 3,|w|\leq\frac{9}{8\delta}\}.

First, we shall show that for every
(z,w)\in B_{k}^{\prime}
, we have
F(z,w)\in B_{k+1}^{\prime}
. For this, suppose that
(z,w)\in B_{k}^{\prime}
. By definition of
F
, we have
F(z,w)=(e^{f_{1}(z)}+\delta w,e^{f_{2}(w)})
. Consider,

\displaystyle|e^{f_{1}(z)}+\delta e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}-\delta_{k+1}|

\displaystyle\leq|e^{f_{1}(z)}-\delta_{k+1}+\delta|e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l_{k+1}}{4}+\delta\frac{17}{16}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l_{k+1}}{4}+\frac{l_{k+1}}{8}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l_{k+1}}{2}.

Also,

\displaystyle|e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{17}{16}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l_{k+1}}{4\delta}.

This shows that
F(B_{k}^{\prime})\subset B_{k+1}^{\prime}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,
F
maps
B_{k}^{\prime}
into a smaller poly disc inside
B_{k+1}^{\prime}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, assume that
(z,w)\in G_{2}\cup(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{k}^{\prime}\cup L_{k}^{\prime}))
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle|e^{f_{1}(z)}+\delta e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}-1|

\displaystyle\leq|e^{f_{1}(z)}-1|+\delta|e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16}+\delta\frac{17}{16}

\displaystyle<3.

Also,

\displaystyle|e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}|

\displaystyle<\frac{17}{16}

\displaystyle<\frac{9}{8\delta}.

This, in particular shows that
F(M_{k}^{\prime})\subset\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z-1|<2,|w|<\frac{9}{8\delta}\}\subset G_{2}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since, each
B_{k}^{\prime}
is disjoint and
F(M_{k}^{\prime})\subset G_{2}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
N_{k}
be the Fatou component of
F
containing
B_{k}^{\prime}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By the construction of
F
, it is clear that each
N_{k}
is an escaping wandering domain of
F
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Proposition 3
.

There exists a mapping
F
on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
having infinitely many attracting Fatou components.

Proof.

Consider
B_{k}
and
M_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
as we defined earlier. Now we shall follow the following steps:

Step1: Using continuity of
e^{z}
,
there exists
h_{k}^{\prime}>0
such that

|z-\log(\delta_{k})|<h_{k}^{\prime}\text{ implies that }|e^{z}-\delta_{k}|<\frac{l_{k}}{4}.

Step2: Define functions
g(z)
and
\epsilon(z)
as follows:

g(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log(\delta_{k}),&z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\log 1,&z\in G_{1}\cup(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}M_{k}),\end{array}\right.

and

\epsilon(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{l_{k}}{4},&z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{2},&z\in G_{1}\cup(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}M_{k}).\end{array}\right.

For such functions, using the definition of a Carleman set, we have

|f_{1}(z)-\log(\delta_{k})|<\delta^{\prime}_{k},\text{ whenever }z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}.

This further implies that

|e^{f_{1}(z)}-\delta_{k}|<\frac{l_{k}}{4}.

Similarly, for
z\in G_{1}\cup(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}M_{k})
, we have

|e^{f_{1}(z)}-1|<3.

Now, define
B_{k}^{\prime}
and
M_{k}^{\prime}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
as we did earlier. Consider,

\displaystyle|e^{f_{1}(z)}+\delta e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}-\delta_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq|e^{f_{1}(z)}-\delta_{k}|+\delta|e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l_{k}}{4}+\frac{17\delta}{16}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l_{k}}{4}+\frac{l_{k}}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{l_{k}}{2},

and

\displaystyle|e^{\text{Re}f_{2}(w)}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{17}{16}

\displaystyle<\frac{l_{k}}{8\delta}.

These inequalities show that
F(B_{k}^{\prime})\subset B_{k}^{\prime}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., every
B_{k}^{\prime}
is being mapped by
F
into a smaller poly disc inside
B_{k}^{\prime}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
C_{k}
be the Fatou component containing
B_{k}^{\prime}
which is an attracting Fatou component. Since,
F(G_{2}\cup(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}M_{k}^{\prime}))\subset G_{2}
and each
B_{k}^{\prime}
is disjoint, we have
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\not=j
. This gives us the existence of an infinite number of attracting Fatou components.
∎

Recall that two wandering domains
C_{0}
and
V_{0}
are said to have a common path if
C_{m}=V_{n}
for some
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
C_{m}
and
V_{n}
are the Fatou components containing
F^{m}(C_{0})
and
F^{n}(V_{0})
respectively. If
C_{m}\not=V_{n}
for any
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, then
C_{0}
and
V_{0}
are said to have distinct paths.

Proposition 4
.

There exists a mapping
F
on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
with infinitely many wandering domains having a common path.

Proof.

For this, we shall make use of the domains
A_{k}^{\prime},k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Firstly, using continuity of
e^{z}
there exists
\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{3}>0
such that

|z-\log(\delta_{1})|<\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{3}\text{ implies that }|e^{z}-\delta_{1}|<\frac{l_{1}}{4}.

Define the function
g(z)
and
\epsilon(z)
as

g(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log(\delta_{1}),&z\in A_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\log(\delta_{k+1}),&z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\log 1,&z\in G_{1}\cup\left(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{k}\cup L_{k})\right),\end{array}\right.

and

\epsilon(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{l_{1}}{4},&z\in A_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\delta^{\prime}_{k},&z\in B_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\frac{1}{4},&z\in G_{1}\cup\left(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{k}\cup L_{k})\right).\end{array}\right.

Now, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

A_{k}^{\prime}:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:|z-r_{k}|\leq s_{k},|w|\leq\frac{l_{1}}{4}\}.

Arguing as in the previous manner, we can observe that

|e^{f_{1}(z)}+\delta e^{f_{2}(z)}-\delta_{1}|<\frac{l_{1}}{2}
and
|e^{f_{2}(w)}|<\frac{l_{1}}{4}
, for every
(z,w)\in A_{k}^{\prime}
. This means that
F(A_{k}^{\prime})\subset B_{1}^{\prime}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, if
D_{k}
is the Fatou component containing
A_{k}^{\prime}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
C_{1}
(the Fatou component containing
B_{1}^{\prime}
) is a common path for these wandering domains.
∎

Remark 5
.

From the above proposition, one can observe that by modifying functions
g(z),\epsilon(z)
and by suitably using domains, we can have

•

An
F
with wandering domains each having a distinct path.

•

F
having wandering domains with a common path as well as having wandering domains with distinct paths.

The general class of Wasserstein Sobolev spaces: density of cylinder functions, reflexivity, uniform convexity and Clarkson’s inequalities
Giacomo Enrico Sodini

Abstract
. We show that the algebra of cylinder functions in the Wasserstein Sobolev space H 1 , q ​ ( 𝒫 p ​ ( X , 𝖽 ) , W p , 𝖽 , 𝔪 ) H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}),W_{p,{\sf d}},\mathfrak{m}) generated by a finite and positive Borel measure 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} on the ( p , 𝖽 ) (p,{\sf d}) -Wasserstein space ( 𝒫 p ​ ( X , 𝖽 ) , W p , 𝖽 ) (\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}),W_{p,{\sf d}}) on a complete and separable metric space ( X , 𝖽 ) (X,{\sf d}) is dense in energy. As an application, we prove that, in case the underlying metric space is a separable Banach space 𝔹 \mathbb{B} , then the Wasserstein Sobolev space is reflexive (resp. uniformly convex) if 𝔹 \mathbb{B} is reflexive (resp. if the dual of 𝔹 \mathbb{B} is uniformly convex). Finally, we also provide sufficient conditions for the validity of Clarkson’s type inequalities in the Wasserstein Sobolev space.

1.
Introduction

The study of Sobolev spaces on metric measure spaces is a well established area of interest in metric geometry, we refer to
[
18
,
19
,
22
,
25
]
for a general treatment of the subject. It is thus important to provide examples (as concrete as possible) of such Sobolev spaces built on relevant metric spaces; the study carried out in
[
12
]
goes precisely in this direction: the authors analyze the properties of the
2
-Sobolev space on the
2
-Wasserstein space
[
29
,
30
,
2
,
24
]
of probability measures on a separable Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
.

Let us also mention that, besides being interesting from a purely theoretical point of view, the study of Sobolev spaces on spaces of probability measures has also important applications to functional analysis over spaces of probability measures. Indeed, problems such as evolutionary games or Kolmogorov equations in nonlinear filtering have been treated so far by classical notions of solutions, lacking weak formulations. In this regard, providing a functional analytic framework in which to set those problems may be very relevant. We refer to the introduction of
[
12
]
for a more detailed list of applications and references.

In this work we generalize the results in
[
12
]
considering a general complete and separable metric space
(X,{\sf d})
, instead of a Hilbert space, and we treat general exponents
p,q
both in the order of the Wasserstein distance and in the Sobolev space (instead of
p=q=2
). Before entering into the details of the present work, let us briefly recall the definition of metric Sobolev space and the results of
[
12
]
that are the starting point for our analysis.

Among the possible approaches to Sobolev spaces on metric measure spaces (for example the Newtonian one
[
26
,
5
]
), here we focus on the approach, strictly related to the ideas of Cheeger
[
7
]
, that is contained in the work of Ambrosio, Gigli and Savaré
[
4
]
where they define the following concept of
q
-relaxed (
q\in(1,+\infty))
gradient: given a metric measure space
(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
, we say that
G\in L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is a
q
-relaxed gradient of
f\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
if there exist a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
and
\tilde{G}\in L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
such that

(1)

f_{n}\to f
in
L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
and
\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{\sf d}f\rightharpoonup\tilde{G}
in
L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
,

(2)

\tilde{G}\leq G

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
X
,

where, for
f\in\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
, the asymptotic Lipschitz constant of
f
is defined as

(1.1)

\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{\sf d}f(x):=\limsup_{y,z\to x,\ y\neq z}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|}{{\sf d}(y,z)},\quad x\in X.

The
q
-Cheeger energy of
f\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is then defined as

\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(f):=\int_{X}|{\mathrm{D}}f|_{\star,q}^{q}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m},

where
|{\mathrm{D}}f|_{\star,q}
is the minimal relaxed gradient of
f
i.e. the element of minimal norm in the set of relaxed gradients of
f
. It is well known that the
q
-Cheeger energy can be also characterized as the relaxation of the so called pre-
q
-Cheeger energy

\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(f):=\int_{X}(\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{\sf d}f)^{q}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m},\quad f\in\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d}),

in the sense that

(1.2)

\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(f)=\inf\left\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(f_{n}):(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d}),\,f_{n}\to f\text{ in }L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})\right\}.

The Sobolev space
à la Cheeger

H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is thus the vector space of functions
f\in L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
with finite Cheeger energy endowed with the norm

|f|^{q}_{H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})}:=\int_{X}|f|^{q}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}+\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(f)

which makes it a Banach space. A remarkable result
[
4
]
is the so called strong approximation property by Lipschitz functions: if
f\in H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
then there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
such that

(1.3)

f_{n}\to f,\quad\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{\sf d}f_{n}\to|{\mathrm{D}}f|_{\star,q}\text{ in }L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m}).

In
[
12
]
the authors provide a general criterion for the validity of the same property where, instead of the whole
\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
, the approximating sequence can be taken from a suitable subalgebra
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
satisfying

\mathbbm{1}\in\mathscr{A},\quad\text{for every $x_{0},x_{1}\in X$ there exists $f\in\mathscr{A}$:\quad$f(x_{0})\neq f(x_{1})$},

where
\mathbbm{1}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is the constant function equal to
1
. This is equivalent to say that
\mathscr{A}
is
dense in energy in the Sobolev space
. In particular (see Theorem
2.9
below or
[
12
, Theorem 2.12]
for the proof) it is proven that an equivalent condition is that for every
y\in X
it holds

(1.4)

|{\mathrm{D}}{\sf d}_{y}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}\leq 1\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e. in }X,

where
{\sf d}_{y}(x):={\sf d}(x,y)
and
|{\mathrm{D}}\cdot|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}
is a suitably
\mathscr{A}
-adapted notion of minimal relaxed gradient (cf. Definition
2.1
). The second part of
[
12
]
is devoted to apply the criterion in (
1.4
) to the
L^{2}
-Kantorovich-Rubinstein-Wasserstein (in brief, Wasserstein) space on a separable Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
, denoted by
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{H}),W_{2})
, with the algebra
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{H}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{H})\big)
of cylinder functions (cf. Definition
4.1
), which is the algebra of functions of the form

(1.5)

F(\mu)=\psi\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}}\phi_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\mu,\dots,\int_{\mathbb{H}}\phi_{N}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{H}),

where
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\phi_{n}\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{H})
for
n=1,\dots,N\in\mathbb{N}
. The density of
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{H}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{H})\big)
in
H^{1,2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{H}),W_{2},\mathfrak{m})
(here
\mathfrak{m}
is a finite and positive Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{H})
) is particularly interesting since cylinder functions come already with some structure; in particular to any function
F
as in (
1.5
) we can associate a notion of gradient given by

(1.6)

{\mathrm{D}}F(\mu,x):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\partial_{n}\psi\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}}\phi_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\mu,\dots,\int_{\mathbb{H}}\phi_{N}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right)\nabla\phi_{n}(x),\quad(\mu,x)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{H})\times\mathbb{H}.

It is proven in
[
12
, Proposition 4.9]
that, for every
F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{H}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{H})\big)
, we have

(1.7)

\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{2}(F)=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{H})\times\mathbb{H}}|{\mathrm{D}}F(\mu,x)|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu),

so that the pre-Cheeger energy satisfies the parallelogram identity (cf.
[
12
, Subsection 4.2]
) and thus forces the Cheeger energy to be a quadratic form. This amounts to say that the Sobolev space
H^{1,2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{H}),W_{2},\mathfrak{m})
is a Hilbert space, a crucial property in the theory of metric Sobolev spaces
[
21
,
27
,
28
,
4
,
14
]
.

The aim of this work is, following the approach of
[
12
]
, to study the properties of the more general class of Wasserstein Sobolev spaces
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
(
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
), where
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
is a separable Banach space,
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}})
is the
(p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
-Wasserstein space on it and
\mathfrak{m}
is positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
. First of all, in Proposition
4.7
we provide a generalization of the representation in (
1.7
): if
F
is a cylinder function in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B})
as in (
1.5
), then

(1.8)

\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(F)=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\|{\mathrm{D}}F(\mu,x)\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\right)^{q/p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu),

where
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
is the conjugate exponent of
p
,
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is the dual norm in
\mathbb{B}^{*}
and
{\mathrm{D}}F
is adapted from (
1.6
) in the obvious way. Notice that, differently from the representation (
1.7
), the right hand side of (
1.8
) doesn’t coincide with the
q
-th power of a
L^{q}
norm on a suitable space but it is rather the
q
-th power of the norm in the
L^{q}
-direct integral of a family of Banach spaces (see Subsection
5
for more details).

Approaching first the case in which
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\|\cdot\|
is a sufficiently regular norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(cf.(
3.3
)), we are able to prove the density of
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
in the corresponding Sobolev space (first part of Theorem
4.15
) adapting the techniques of
[
12
]
(in particular using (
1.4
) and the representation in (
1.8
)) to this more general situation: this requires more sophisticated results (which may be interesting by themselves) in terms of the properties of Kantorovich potentials which are no longer convex if
p\neq 2
(see Section
3.1
).

The density of cylinder functions is then extended to the Sobolev-Wasserstien space corresponding to an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by an approximation procedure and then to an arbitrary separable Banach space
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
via a standard embedding technique in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
and finite dimensional projections (second part of Theorem
4.15
and Corollary
4.19
, respectively). The precise statement of the result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space; then the algebra of cylinder functions
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
is dense in
q
-energy in the Sobolev space
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
.

Let us also mention that we are able to extend the density result (Theorem
4.18
) to an arbitrary complete and separable metric space
(X,{\sf d})
where, instead of using
{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}
functions (which of course are not available) to generate cylinder functions on
\mathcal{P}(X)
, we consider a sequence
(\phi_{k})_{k}\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
such that

{\sf d}(x,y)=\sup_{k}|\phi_{k}(x)-\phi_{k}(y)|,\quad\text{ for every }x,y\in X,

and we use the smallest unital algebra containing
(\phi_{k})_{k}
.

In the same spirit of the Hilbertianity result in
[
12
]
, we are led to study which properties of the Banach space
\mathbb{B}
are transferred to the Sobolev space, thanks to the density of cylinder functions provided by the above Theorem
1.1
. This is the case of uniform convexity and the validity of some Clarkson’s type inequalities. The argument we adopt for such properties is similar: since the pre-Cheeger energy of a cylinder function as in (
1.8
) corresponds to the
q
-th power of a norm in a suitable Banach space, it is sufficient to prove that such norm enjoys the uniform convexity (resp. the validity of Clarkson’s type inequalities) to obtain that the pre-Cheeger satisfies the same property; thanks to the density of cylinder functions such a property is extended to the Cheeger energy and thus to the whole Sobolev norm (Theorems
5.4
and
5.10
).
For what concerns reflexivity the argument is somehow standard: again using the representation of the pre-Cheeger energy in (
1.8
), we can isometrically embed the Sobolev space into a reflexive Banach space (Theorem
5.2
). The precise statement regarding reflexivity and uniform convexity is reported below (we refer to Subsection
5.1
for the results related to Clarkson’s type inequalities).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space. If
\mathbb{B}
is reflexive (resp. its dual is uniformly convex), then the Sobolev space
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
is reflexive (resp. uniformly convex).

2.
Metric Sobolev spaces and density of subalgebras

In this section we describe the general metric setting and we list a few results of
[
12
]
which are the starting point of our analysis. For this whole section, we fix a separable metric space
(X,{\sf d})
, a finite and positive Borel measure
\mathfrak{m}
on
(X,{\sf d})
(the triple
(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is called a Polish metric-measure space), an exponent
q\in(1,+\infty)
and a unital and separating subalgebra
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{b}(X,{\sf d})
i.e. satisfying

(2.1)

\mathbbm{1}\in\mathscr{A},\quad\text{for every $x_{0},x_{1}\in X$ there exists $f\in\mathscr{A}$:\quad$f(x_{0})\neq f(x_{1})$},

where
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{b}(X,{\sf d})
is the space of real valued and bounded
{\sf d}
-Lipschitz functions on
X
and
\mathbbm{1}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is the constant function equal to
1
.

If
(Y,{\sf d}_{Y})
is another complete and separable metric space and
f:X\to Y
is a Borel function, we define the finite (with the same total mass of
\mathfrak{m}
) and Borel measure
f_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}
on
(Y,{\sf d}_{Y})
as

(2.2)

f_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}(B)=\mathfrak{m}(f^{-1}(B))\quad\text{ for every Borel set }B\subset Y.

We define the
{\sf d}
-asymptotic Lipschitz constant of
f\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{b}(X,{\sf d})
as

(2.3)

\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{\sf d}f(x):=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(f,{\mathrm{B}}_{{\sf d}}(x,r),{\sf d})=\limsup_{y,z\to x,\ y\neq z}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|}{{\sf d}(y,z)},\quad x\in X,

where
{\mathrm{B}}_{{\sf d}}(x,r)\subset X
denotes the
{\sf d}
-open ball centered at
x
with radius
r>0
and,
for
A\subset X
, the quantity
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(f,A,{\sf d})
is defined as

\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(f,A,{\sf d}):=\sup_{x,y\in A,\,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{{\sf d}(x,y)}.

We denote by
L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
the space of real valued and Borel measurable functions on
X
, identified up to equality
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. and, analogously, by
L^{r}(X,\mathfrak{m})
the usual Lebesgue spaces of real valued,
r
-summable and Borel measurable functions, identified up to equality
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.,
r\in[1,+\infty]
. We endow
L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
with the topology of the convergence in
\mathfrak{m}
-measure, while
L^{r}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is endowed with the usual norm,
r\in[1,+\infty]
. When dealing with vector-valued or extended real-valued functions we also specify the codomain in the notation for
L^{r}
-spaces, i.e. we write
L^{r}(X,\mu;Y)
, where
Y
is a (subset of a) Banach space. The following is the definition of relaxed gradient we adopt
[
4
,
3
,
25
]
(see also
[
26
,
5
]
for a different approach).

Definition 2.1

(
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient)
.

We say that
G\in L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is a
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient of
f\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
if
there exist a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathscr{A}
and
\tilde{G}\in L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
such that:

(1)

f_{n}\to f
in
\mathfrak{m}
-measure and

\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{\sf d}f_{n}\to\tilde{G}
weakly in
L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
;

(2)

\tilde{G}\leq G

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
X
.

The next result is a simple but important property of relaxed gradients
[
4
,
3
,
25
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If

f\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
has a
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient then there exists a unique element of minimal
L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
-norm in

S(f):=\Big\{G\in L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m}):\text{$G$ is a $(q,\mathscr{A})$-relaxed gradient of $f$}\Big\}.

Thanks to Theorem
2.2
the next definition is well posed.

Definition 2.3

(Minimal relaxed gradient)
.

Let
f\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
be such that
f
has a
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient. Its relaxed gradient with minimal
L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
-norm is denoted by
|{\mathrm{D}}f|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}
and called minimal
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient of
f
.

Definition 2.4

(Cheeger energy and Sobolev space)
.

We call
D^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
the set of functions in
L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
with
a
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient and we define the
(q,\mathscr{A})
-Cheeger energy as

(2.4)

\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q,\mathscr{A}}(f):=\int_{X}|{\mathrm{D}}f|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}^{q}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)\quad\text{for every $f\in D^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})$},

with
\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q,\mathscr{A}}(f):=+\infty
if
f\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})\setminus D^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A}).

The Sobolev space
H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
is defined as

L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})\cap D^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
. The Sobolev norm of
f\in H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
is defined as

\|f\|_{H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})}^{q}:=\|f\|_{L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})}^{q}+\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q,\mathscr{A}}(f).

In the next theorem we collect a few properties of relaxed gradients and Sobolev spaces that will be useful (for a more comprehensive list and references to the proofs, see
[
12
]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

(1)

The set

S:=\Big\{(f,G)\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})\times L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m}):\text{$G$ is a $(q,\mathscr{A})$-relaxed gradient of $f$}\Big\}

is convex and it is closed with respect to
to the product topology of the convergence in
\mathfrak{m}
-measure and
the weak convergence in
L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
.
In particular, the restriction
S_{r}:=S\cap L^{r}(X,\mathfrak{m})\times L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is weakly closed in

L^{r}(X,\mathfrak{m})\times L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
for every
r\in(1,+\infty)
.

(2)

(Strong approximation) If

f\in D^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
takes values in a closed
(possibly unbounded)
interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
then
there exists a sequence
f_{n}\in\mathscr{A}
with values in

(2.5)

f_{n}\to f\text{ $\mathfrak{m}$-a.e.\penalty\ in $X$},\quad\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{\sf d}f_{n}\to|{\mathrm{D}}f|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}\text{ strongly in }L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m}).

If moreover
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathfrak{m})
for some
r\in[1,+\infty)
then
we can also find a sequence as in (
2.5
) converging strongly
to
f
in
L^{r}(X,\mathfrak{m})
.

(3)

(Pointwise minimality)
If
G
is a
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient of

f\in L^{0}(X,\mathfrak{m})
then
|{\mathrm{D}}f|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}\leq G

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
X
.

(4)

(Truncations) If
f_{j}\in D^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
,
j=1,\cdots,J
,
then
also the functions
f_{+}:=\max(f_{1},\cdots,f_{J})
and

f_{-}:=\min(f_{1},\cdots,f_{J})
have
(q,\mathscr{A})
-relaxed gradient
and

(2.6)

\displaystyle|{\mathrm{D}}f_{+}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}=|{\mathrm{D}}f_{j}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{$\mathfrak{m}$-a.e.\penalty\ on }\{x\in X:f_{+}=f_{j}\},

(2.7)

\displaystyle|{\mathrm{D}}f_{-}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}=|{\mathrm{D}}f_{j}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{$\mathfrak{m}$-a.e.\penalty\ on }\{x\in X:f_{-}=f_{j}\}.

Wasserstein spaces

We devote the first part of this section to a few general properties of Wasserstein spaces (see

If

the space of Borel probability measures on

the set

\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X):\int_{X}{\sf d}^{p}(x,x_{0}){\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)<+\infty\text{ for some (and hence for all) }x_{0}\in X\right\}.

Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
the set of transport plans between
\mu
and
\nu
is denoted by
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
and defined as

\Gamma(\mu,\nu):=\left\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in\mathcal{P}(X\times X):\pi^{1}_{\sharp}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=\mu,\,\pi^{2}_{\sharp}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=\nu\right\},

where
\pi^{i}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{i}
for every
(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X
and
\sharp
denotes the push forward operator as in (
2.2

(p,{\sf d})
-Wasserstein distance
W_{{p},{\sf d}}
between
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d})
is defined as

W_{{p},{\sf d}}^{p}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\left\{\int_{X\times X}{\sf d}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}:{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)\right\}.

It is well known that the infimum above is attained in a non-empty and
convex set
\Gamma_{o,p,{\sf d}}(\mu,\nu)\subset\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
and that the
(p,{\sf d})
-Wasserstein space
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}),W_{{p},{\sf d}})
is complete and separable, if
(X,{\sf d})
is complete and separable. The Kantorovich duality for the Wasserstein distance states that

(3.1)

\frac{1}{p}W_{{p},{\sf d}}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu+\int_{X}\psi\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu:(\varphi,\psi)\in\text{Adm}_{{\sf d}^{p}/p}(X)\right\}\quad\text{ for every }\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}),

where
\text{Adm}_{{\sf d}^{p}/p}(X)
is the set of pairs
(\varphi,\psi)\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}(X)\times{\mathrm{C}}_{b}(X)
such that

\varphi(x)+\psi(y)\leq\frac{1}{p}{\sf d}^{p}(x,y)\quad\text{ for every }x,y\in X.

It is easy to check that for every
f\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d})
we have

(3.2)

\int_{X}f\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mu-\nu)\leq\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(f,X)W_{p,{\sf d}}(\mu,\nu);

in fact, choosing
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in\Gamma_{o,p,{\sf d}}(\mu,\nu)
and setting

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}f\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mu-\nu)=\int(f(x)-f(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(x,y)\leq L\int{\sf d}\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\leq L\Big(\int{\sf d}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\Big)^{1/p}=LW_{p}(\mu,\nu).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
R,I>0
and let
(\varphi_{n})_{n},(\psi_{n})_{n}
be sequences of functions such that

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\frac{1}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}-\psi_{n}(y)\right\},\quad

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(y)=\inf_{x\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)}\left\{\frac{1}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}-\varphi_{n}(y)\right\},

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(0)

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)}\varphi_{n}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{d}\geq-I

for every
x\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
, every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\varphi_{n}
is locally (w.r.t.
x\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
) uniformly (w.r.t.
n\in\mathbb{N}
) bounded and Lipschitz.

Proof.

The proof is strongly based on
[
11
]
(see also the similar approach in
[
13
, Proposition C.3]
) and it is divided in two claims.
For the whole proof, we extend
\varphi_{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
simply setting

\varphi_{n}(x)=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\frac{1}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}-\psi_{n}(y)\right\}\ \text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Claim 1
. The sequence
\varphi_{n}
is locally (w.r.t.
x\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
) uniformly (w.r.t.
n\in\mathbb{N}
) bounded.

Proof of claim 1
. Since
\varphi_{n}(x)\leq\frac{1}{p}\|x\|^{p}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is enough to prove that
\varphi_{n}
is locally uniformly bounded from below in
{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
. Let us suppose by contradiction that there exist some
\bar{x}\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
and a sequence
(x_{n})_{n}\subset{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
such that
x_{n}\to\bar{x}
and
\varphi_{n}(x_{n})\to-\infty
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose some
y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\varphi_{n}(x_{n})+1\geq\frac{1}{p}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|^{p}-\psi_{n}(y_{n})\geq-\psi_{n}(y_{n})

implying that
\psi_{n}(y_{n})\to+\infty
. Thus, since
\varphi_{n}(\bar{x})\in\mathbb{R}
and

\varphi_{n}(\bar{x})\leq\frac{1}{p}\|\bar{x}-y_{n}\|^{p}-\psi(y_{n}),

we get that
\|\bar{x}-y_{n}\|\to+\infty
which in particular gives that
\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|\to+\infty
. Let us define now the curves
\gamma_{n}:[0,\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

\gamma_{n}(t)=\left(1-\frac{t}{\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}\right)x_{n}+\frac{t}{\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}y_{n},\quad t\in[0,\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|].

Since
\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|\to+\infty
, we can assume that
\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|\geq 1
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and define

C_{n}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{ there exists }t\in[0,1]\text{ such that }\|x-\gamma_{n}(t)\|\leq\frac{t}{2}\right\},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

We claim that
\sup_{C_{n}}\varphi_{n}\to-\infty
as
n\to+\infty
. Indeed if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in[0,1]
is such that
\|x-\gamma_{n}(t)\|\leq t/2
, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi_{n}(x)&\leq\frac{1}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}-\psi_{n}(y_{n})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{p}\left(\|x-\gamma_{n}(t)\|+\|\gamma_{n}(t)-y_{n}\|\right)^{p}-\psi_{n}(y_{n})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{p}\left(t/2+\|\gamma_{n}(t)-y_{n}\|\right)^{p}-\psi_{n}(y_{n})\\
&=\frac{1}{p}\left|\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|-t/2\right|^{p}-\psi_{n}(y_{n})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{p}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|^{p}-\frac{t}{2}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|^{p-1}-\psi_{n}(y_{n})\\
&\leq\varphi_{n}(x_{n})+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{t}{2}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|^{p-1}\\
&\leq\varphi_{n}(x_{n})+1,\end{split}

where we have used the elementary inequality

(a-b)^{p}\leq a^{p}-pa^{p-1}b^{p}\quad\text{ for every }0\leq a\leq b.

Letting

, we obtain the claim. Up to passing to a (unrelabeled) subsequence, we can thus assume that

\varphi_{n}(x)\leq-n\quad\text{for every }x\in C_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\bar{x}\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
there exists some

such that

. Let

be such that
\|x_{n}-\bar{x}\|<\varepsilon/2
for every

and let

; it is not difficult to check that the truncated sets

for every

. Then for every

\displaystyle-I-\frac{1}{p}\mathsf{m}_{p}^{p}(\mathcal{L}^{d}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)}\left(\varphi_{n}(x)-\frac{1}{p}\|x\|^{p}\right){\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{C_{n}^{\delta}}\left(\varphi_{n}(x)-\frac{1}{p}\|x\|^{p}\right){\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{C_{n}^{\delta}}\left(-n-\frac{1}{p}\|x\|^{p}\right){\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n\omega_{d-1}\delta^{d}}{d2^{d-1}\eta^{d-1}},

where
\omega_{d-1}
is the
\mathcal{L}^{d-1}
-measure of the Euclidean

-dimensional unit ball and
\eta>0
is a constant such that

\|x\|\leq\eta|x|\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Passing to the limit as

gives that
I=\infty
, a contradiction.

Claim 2
. For every
K\subset\subset{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
there exists

such that

\varphi_{n}(x)=\inf_{y\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,M_{K})}\left\{\frac{1}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}-\psi_{n}(y)\right\}\quad\text{for every $x\in K$ and for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$, }

is locally (w.r.t.
x\in{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
) uniformly (w.r.t.

Proof of claim 2
. Let
K\subset\subset{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
be fixed; we claim that there exists
C_{K}>0
such that, if

are such that

\varphi_{n}(x)+1\geq\frac{1}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}-\psi_{n}(y),

then

. Let us define

where

and

\displaystyle S_{K_{\ell}}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\varphi_{n}(x):(x,n)\in K_{\ell}\times\mathbb{N}\right\}<+\infty,

\displaystyle I_{K_{\ell}}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\varphi_{n}(x):(x,n)\in K_{\ell}\times\mathbb{N}\right\}>-\infty,

where we have used claim 1 to ensure that the supremum and the infimum are finite. Let us consider
(x,y,n)\in K\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}
as above. To prove that

is the sought constant, it is enough to consider the case in which

. If we define

∎

and
\|v_{k}\|\to+\infty
, we get a contradiction, proving our first claim. We set
C_{K}:=\max\{K,D_{K}\}
and we prove that
\partial^{c}\psi_{n}(\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,K)})\subset\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,C_{K})}\times\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,C_{K})}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
(y,x)\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n}(\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,K)}
and we define
\lambda:=\psi_{n}(y)-\frac{1}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}
, we have that
(x,\lambda,y,n)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,K)}\times\mathbb{N}
and satisfies (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) by the very definition of
\partial^{c}\psi_{n}
so that by the above claim we get that
\|x\|\leq D_{K}\leq C_{K}
and of course
\|y\|\leq K\leq C_{K}
. This concludes the proof of the claim.

Claim 2
. We have that

\partial^{c}\psi(y)\neq\emptyset,\quad\lim_{j}\sup\left\{\mathsf{dist}(x,\partial^{c}\psi(y)):x\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)\right\}=0\quad\text{ for every }y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof of claim 2
. Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be fixed; observe that, if
(x_{j})_{j}
is any sequence such that
x_{j}\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
(there exists at least one such a sequence by claim 1), then by claim 1
\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)
are uniformly (w.r.t
j\in\mathbb{N}
) bounded so that we can extract a subsequence
k\mapsto j_{k}
such that
x_{j_{k}}\to x
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By the very definition of
c
-superdifferential, we have that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\psi_{n(j_{k})}(z)\leq\psi_{n(j_{k})}(y)+\frac{1}{p}\|z-x_{j_{k}}\|^{p}-\frac{1}{p}\|y-x_{j_{k}}\|^{p}\quad\text{ for every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Passing to the limit as
k\to+\infty
, we get that
x\in\partial^{c}\psi(y)
, so that
\partial^{c}\psi(y)\neq\emptyset
. This proves in particular that from any sequence of points
(x_{j})_{j}
such that
x_{j}\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can extract a subsequence converging to an element of
\partial^{c}\psi(y)
. Let us come now to the proof of the limit. We prove that from any (unrelabeled) subsequence of
n(j)
we can extract a further subsequence such that we have the convergence above. For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can find some
x_{j}\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)
such that

\mathsf{dist}(x_{j},\partial^{c}\psi(y))+\frac{1}{j}\geq\sup\left\{\mathsf{dist}(x,\partial^{c}\psi(y)):x\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)\right\}.

Reasoning as above, we find a subsequence
k\mapsto j_{k}
such that
x_{j_{k}}\to x\in\partial^{c}\psi(y)
as
k\to+\infty
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0=\mathsf{dist}(x,\partial^{c}\psi(y))

\displaystyle=\lim_{k}\mathsf{dist}(x_{j_{k}},\partial^{c}\psi(y))+\frac{1}{j_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{k}\sup\left\{\mathsf{dist}(x,\partial^{c}\psi(y)):x\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j_{k})}(y)\right\}.

This concludes the proof of the claim.

Claim 3
. We have that
\nabla\psi_{n(j)}\to\nabla\psi

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof of claim 3
. Let
A:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{$\psi$ and $\psi_{n(j)}$ are differentiable at $y$ for every $j\in\mathbb{N}$}\right\}
; notice that
A
has full
\mathcal{L}^{d}
measure. Let
y\in A
; since
\psi
is differentiable at
y
we know by
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
that any
x\in\partial^{c}\psi(y)
will satisfy
\|\nabla\psi(y)\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}=\|\nabla h(x-y)\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}=\|x-y\|^{p}
so that
\partial^{c}\psi(y)
is contained in
\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,C)}
for some
C>0
. On the other hand, claim 1 implies that there exists some constant
D>0
such that
\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)\subset\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,D)}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us define
M>0
as the uniform modulus of continuity of the map
x\mapsto\nabla h(x-y)
in the compact set
\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,C+D)}
. By claim 2, for every
\varepsilon>0
we can find
J_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, if
j\geq J_{\varepsilon}
, then

\mathsf{dist}(x,\partial^{c}\psi(y))\leq\sup\left\{\mathsf{dist}(x,\partial^{c}\psi(y)):x\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)\right\}<\varepsilon/M\quad\text{ for every }x\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y).

This means that for every
j\geq J_{\varepsilon}
and every
x_{j}\in\partial^{c}\psi_{n(j)}(y)
there exists some
z_{j}\in\partial^{c}\psi(y)
such that
\|x_{j}-z_{j}\|<\varepsilon/M
. Using again
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
and the fact that both
\psi_{n(j)}
and
\psi
are differentiable at
y
, we have that
\nabla\psi_{n(j)}(y)=\nabla h(x_{j}-y)
and
\nabla\psi(y)=\nabla h(z_{j}-y)
for every
j\geq J_{\varepsilon}
. Then

\left|\nabla\psi_{n(j)}(y)-\nabla\psi(y)\right|=\left|\nabla h(x_{j}-y)-\nabla h(z_{j}-y)\right|\leq M\|x_{j}-z_{j}\|<\varepsilon

for every
j\geq J_{\varepsilon}
. This concludes the proof of the claim.
∎

4.
The Wasserstein Sobolev space

H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}),W_{p,{\sf d}},\mathfrak{m})

The aim of this section is to study the
q
-Sobolev space on the
(p,{\sf d})
-Wasserstein space on a separable and complete metric space
(X,{\sf d})
. In particular we will show at the end of this section that the algebra of cylinder functions generated by a sufficiently rich algebra of Lipschitz and bounded functions on
(X,{\sf d})
is dense in
q
-energy in the Sobolev space
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}),W_{p,{\sf d}},\mathfrak{m})
. For the whole section
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
are fixed exponents.

4.1.
Cylinder functions on
(X,{\sf d})
and their differential in Banach spaces

Let
(X,{\sf d})
be a complete and separable metric space. We extend the definition of cylinder function in
[
12
]
to this more general setting. To every
\phi\in\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
we can associate the functional
\mathsf{L}_{\phi}
on
\mathcal{P}(X)

(4.1)

\mathsf{L}_{\phi}:\mu\to\int_{X}\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu

which belongs to
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{b}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X,{\sf d}),W_{p,{\sf d}})
thanks to (
3.2
). If
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{N})\in\big(\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})\big)^{N}
, we denote by

\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}:=(\mathsf{L}_{\phi_{1}},\cdots,\mathsf{L}_{\phi_{N}})
the corresponding map
from
\mathcal{P}(X)
to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Definition 4.1

(
\mathscr{E}
-cylinder functions)
.

Let
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}_{b}\nolimits(X,{\sf d})
be an algebra of functions; we say that a function
F:\mathcal{P}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathscr{E}
-
cylinder function
if there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N})\in\mathscr{E}^{N}
such that

(4.2)

F(\mu)=\psi(\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu))=\psi\big(\mathsf{L}_{\phi_{1}}(\mu),\cdots,\mathsf{L}_{\phi_{N}}(\mu)\big)\quad\text{for every }\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X).

We denote the set of such functions by
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(X),\mathscr{E}\big)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Since for every
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in\mathscr{E}^{N}

the range of
\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
is always contained in the
bounded set

[-M,M]^{N}
where
M:=\max_{i=1,\dots,d}\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}
,
also functions
F=\psi\circ\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
with
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
belong
to
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(X),\mathscr{E}\big)
. Indeed it is enough to consider a function

\tilde{\psi}\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
coinciding with
\psi
on

[-M,M]^{N}
and equal to
0
outside
[-M-1,M+1]^{N}

so that
F=\tilde{\psi}\circ\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
.
In particular
every function of the form
\mathsf{L}_{\phi}
,
\phi\in\mathscr{E}
,
belongs to
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(X),\mathscr{E}\big)
.

We use the notation
\mathcal{D}_{X}
for the Borel set

(4.3)

\mathcal{D}_{X}:=\Big\{(\mu,x)\in\mathcal{P}(X)\times X:x\in\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mu)\Big\}.

We introduce now the definition of differential of a cylinder function, still following
[
12
]
, in case
(X,{\sf d})=(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
, where
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach space and
{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}
is the distance induced by
\|\cdot\|
. In this case we denote by
{\mathrm{C}}^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{B})
the space of bounded, continuously differentiable and Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{B}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
; then, given
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in({\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B}))^{N}
such that
F=\psi\circ\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
, we define the Wasserstein differential of
F
conditioned to
(\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
,

{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}:\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{B}}}\to\mathbb{B}^{*}
, as

(4.4)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu,x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\partial_{n}\psi\left(\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu)\right)\nabla\phi_{n}(x),\quad(\mu,x)\in\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{B}}}.

We will also denote by
{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu]
the
function
x\mapsto{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu,x)
and we will set

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu]\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu](x)\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\right)^{1/p^{\prime}},\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),

where
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is the dual norm to
\|\cdot\|
.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
, let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}\in\Gamma_{o,p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{B},\mu_{0};(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|))
. We define the curves
\mu,\nu:[0,1]\to\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
as

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mu_{t}

\displaystyle:={\sf x}^{t}_{\sharp}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}},\quad

\displaystyle t\in[0,1],

(4.7)

\displaystyle\nu_{t}

\displaystyle:=({\mbox{\boldmath$i$}}_{\mathbb{B}}+tu)_{\sharp}\mu_{0},\quad

\displaystyle t\in[0,1],

where
{\sf x}^{t}:\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}
is the map defined as
{\sf x}^{t}(x_{0},x_{1}):=(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1}
, for every
(x_{0},x_{1})\in\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}
and
t\in[0,1]
. If
F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
, given
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in({\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B}))^{N}
such that
F=\psi\circ\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
, then

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{[0,1]\ni t\to s}\frac{F(\mu_{t})-F(\mu_{s})}{t-s}=\int_{\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}}{\langle}{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu_{s},{\sf x}^{s}(x_{0},x_{1})),x_{1}-x_{0}{\rangle}{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(x_{0},x_{1}),

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{[0,1]\ni t\to s}\frac{F(\nu_{t})-F(\nu_{s})}{t-s}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}{\langle}{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\nu_{s},x),u(x){\rangle}{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{s}(x),

for every
s\in[0,1]
. This is a simple consequence of the chain rule and the regularity of
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
.

Remark 4.5
.

It is not difficult to check that

(4.10)

{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\text{ is continuous in }\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B})\times\mathbb{B}

with respect to the natural product (narrow and norm) topology.

In principle

{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
may depend on the choice of
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in({\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B}))^{N}

used to represent
F
. In Proposition
4.7
we show
that for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})

the function
{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu]
is uniquely characterized in
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mu)
so that
{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
is
uniquely characterized by
F
in
\mathcal{D}^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{B}}\cap(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\times\mathbb{B})
. We will be then able to remove the subscript in
{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
and denote it simply by
{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F
.

The following Lemma is proved in
[
12
]
and will be useful in the proof of Proposition
4.7
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
Y
be a Polish space
and let
G:\mathcal{P}(Y)\times Y\to[0,+\infty)
be a bounded and
continuous function. If

(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{P}(Y)
narrowly converging (i.e. converging in duality with continuous and bounded functions on
Y
)
to
\mu
as
n\to+\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}G(\mu_{n},y){\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}(y)=\int_{Y}G(\mu,y){\mathrm{d}}\mu(y).

The following proposition corresponds to
[
12
, Proposition 4.9]
and the proof is quite similar but, because of a few differences, we still report it here.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space and let
F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
; then, if
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in({\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B}))^{N}
are such that
F=\psi\circ\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
, we have

\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu]\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}=\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}}F(\mu)\quad\text{ for every }\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}).

In particular,
\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu]\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}
does not depend on the choice of the
representation of
F

and
{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
just depends on
F
on
\overline{\mathcal{D}^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}
(cf. Remark
4.5
).

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
and let
(\mu^{\prime}_{n},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\times\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
with
\mu^{\prime}_{n}\neq\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n}
be such that
(\mu^{\prime}_{n},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})\to(\mu,\mu)
in
W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}
and

\lim_{n}\frac{\left|F(\mu^{\prime}_{n})-F(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})\right|}{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu^{\prime}_{n},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})}=\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}}F(\mu).

Let
(\mu_{n}^{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be the curves defined as in (
4.6
) for plans
{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{n}\in\Gamma_{o,p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu^{\prime}_{n},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})
; we have

\displaystyle\left|F(\mu^{\prime}_{n})-F(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}}{\langle}{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu_{n}^{t},{\sf x}^{t}(x_{0},x_{1})),x_{1}-x_{0}{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{n}(x_{0},x_{1})\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu_{n}^{t},x)\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}^{t}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{n}(x_{0},x_{1})\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu^{\prime}_{n},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu_{n}^{t},x)\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}^{t}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

where the first equality comes from (
4.8
). Dividing both sides by
W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu^{\prime}_{n},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})
, we obtain

\frac{\left|F(\mu^{\prime}_{n})-F(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})\right|}{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu^{\prime}_{n},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n})}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu_{n}^{t},x)\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}^{t}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Observe that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{n}\to({\mbox{\boldmath$i$}}_{\mathbb{B}},{\mbox{\boldmath$i$}}_{\mathbb{B}})_{\sharp}\mu
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B})

so that
\mu_{n}^{t}\to\mu
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B})
for every
t\in[0,1]
.
We can pass to the limit as
n\to+\infty
the above inequality using the dominated convergence Theorem and Lemma
4.6
with

G(\mu,x):=\left\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu,x)\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}},\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),\,x\in\mathbb{B}.

We hence get

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}}F(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}}\left\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu,x)\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}=\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu]\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}.

To prove the other inequality we consider a countable dense subset
E:=\{x_{n}\}_{n}
of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{B}
and, for every
\varepsilon>0
, the maps
N_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{B}^{*}\to\mathbb{N}
and
j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}:\mathbb{B}^{*}\to\mathbb{B}
defined as

\displaystyle N_{\varepsilon}(x^{*})

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:{\langle}x^{*},x_{n}{\rangle}\geq\|x^{*}\|_{*}-\varepsilon\right\},\quad

\displaystyle x^{*}\in\mathbb{B}^{*},

\displaystyle j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}(x^{*})

\displaystyle:=\|x^{*}\|^{p^{\prime}/p}x_{N_{\varepsilon}(x^{*})},\quad

\displaystyle x^{*}\in\mathbb{B}^{*}.

It is not difficult to check that
j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}
is measurable: if we define the sets

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{B}^{*}:{\langle}x^{*},x_{n}{\rangle}\geq\|x^{*}\|_{*}-\varepsilon\right\},\quad

\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle:=A_{1},

\displaystyle U_{n}

\displaystyle:=A_{n}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}A_{i},\quad

\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N},\,n>1,

then
\{U_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable measurable partition of
\mathbb{B}^{*}
and
j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}
can be written as
j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}(x^{*})=\|x^{*}\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}/p}x_{n}
if
x\in U_{n}
, so that
j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}
is measurable. Moreover it is obvious that

(4.11)

\|j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}(x^{*})\|^{p}=\|x^{*}\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}},\quad{\langle}j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}(x^{*}),x^{*}{\rangle}\geq\|x^{*}\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}-\varepsilon\|x^{*}\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}/p},\quad\text{for every }x^{*}\in\mathbb{B}^{*}.

Let us now consider the maps
T,u_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}
defined as

T(x):={\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu](x),\quad u_{\varepsilon}(x):=j_{p^{\prime},\varepsilon}(T(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{B},

and the curve
(\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
defined as in (
4.7
) with
u_{\varepsilon}
as above (notice that
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|))
since it is Borel measurable and
\|u_{\varepsilon}(x)\|^{p}=\|T(x)\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}<C<+\infty
for every
x\in\mathbb{B}
). By (
4.9
), we get that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{F(\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})-F(\mu)}{t}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}{\langle}{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu,x),u_{\varepsilon}(x){\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\geq\|T\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))}-C^{1/p}\varepsilon,

where we used (
4.11
). Moreover

\frac{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu,\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})}{t}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|))}=\|T\|^{p^{\prime}/p}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))}\quad\text{ for every }t\in(0,1].

Thus

\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}}F(\mu)\geq\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{F(\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})-F(\mu)}{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}(\mu,\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})}\geq\|T\|^{p^{\prime}-p^{\prime}/p}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))}-\frac{C^{1/p}\varepsilon}{\|T\|^{p^{\prime}/p}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))}}.

Passing to
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}
we obtain the sought inequality and this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Note that the inequality

\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}}F(\mu)\leq\|{\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}[\mu]\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}\quad\text{ for every }\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})

holds even if the Banach space
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\||)
is non-separable, since separability is not used in the first part of the proof.

Thanks to Proposition
4.7
the following definition is well posed.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space. For every
F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
, we define

(4.12)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F(\mu,x)

\displaystyle:={\mathrm{D}}F_{\psi,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\mu,x),\quad(\mu,x)\in\overline{\mathcal{D}^{p}},

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F[\mu]\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}

where

,

, and

is as in Remark

.

4.2.
The density result in
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|)
for any norm

In this whole subsection (apart from Theorem
4.15
) we focus again on a finite dimensional Banach space with a sufficiently regular norm. To this aim we fix a dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
and a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying (
3.3
). As we did in Subsection
3.1
, we work on the complete and separable metric space
(\mathbb{R}^{d},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
, where
{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}
is the distance induced by
\|\cdot\|
, and the corresponding
(p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
-Wasserstein space. To simplify the notation, as we did in Subsection
3.1
, in this subsection, we will simply write
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
W_{p}
and
\Gamma_{o,p}
, omitting the dependence on
{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}
. For the rest of this subsection
\mathfrak{m}
is a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Recall that for a bounded Lipschitz function

F:\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
the pre-Cheeger energy (cf. (
2.9
)) associated to
\mathfrak{m}
is defined by

(4.14)

\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(F)=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big(\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{W_{p}}F(\mu)\big)^{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu).

Thanks to Proposition
4.7
, if
F
is a cylinder
function in
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
, we have a nice equivalent expression

(4.15)

\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(F)=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F[\mu]\|^{q}_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F(\mu,x)\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\right)^{q/p^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu),

where
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is the dual norm induced by
\|\cdot\|
. Notice that
(\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q})^{1/q}
is not simply the
L^{q}
-
L^{p^{\prime}}
mixed norm of
{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F
in a Bochner space, since the measures
\mu
w.r.t. the inner norm is computed varies, but rather the norm in the direct
L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathfrak{m})
-integral of the Banach spaces
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))
(see e.g.
[
17
,
10
]
or Section
5
).

We adopt the notation
\mathscr{A}:=\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
and we devote this subsection to the proof of Theorem
4.15
. The following Lemmas are the obvious adaptation of
[
12
, Lemma 4.14, Lemma 4.15]
and their proofs are the same, and thus omitted.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
F_{n}
be a sequence of functions in
D^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{p},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathfrak{m})
such
that

and

|{\mathrm{D}}F_{n}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}
are uniformly bounded in every bounded set
of
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let

be Borel functions in

L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathfrak{m})
,

nonnegative.
If

(4.16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(\mu)=F(\mu),\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}|{\mathrm{D}}F_{n}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}(\mu)\leq G(\mu)\quad\text{$\mathfrak{m}$-a.e.\penalty\ in $\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$},

then
F\in H^{1,{q}}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{p},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
and
|{\mathrm{D}}F|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}\leq G
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\phi\in{\mathrm{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be satisfying the growth conditions

(4.17)

\phi(x)\geq A\|x\|^{p}-B,\quad\|\nabla\phi(x)\|_{*}\leq C(\|x\|^{p-1}+1)\quad\text{for
every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

for given positive constants
A,B,C>0

and let
\zeta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
{\mathrm{C}}^{1}
nondecreasing
function whose derivative has compact support.
Then
the function

F(\mu):=\zeta\circ\mathsf{L}_{\phi}

is Lipschitz in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it
belongs to

H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{p},\mathfrak{m};\mathscr{A})
, and

(4.18)

|{\mathrm{D}}F|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}(\mu)\leq\zeta^{\prime}(\mathsf{L}_{\phi}(\mu))\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits{\kappa}=\overline{{\mathrm{B}}(0,1)}
(here
{\mathrm{B}}(0,1)
is the unit
d
-dimensional Euclidean ball) ,
\kappa(x)\geq 0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\kappa(x)>0
for every
x\in{\mathrm{B}}(0,1)
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\kappa{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{d}=1
and
\kappa(-x)=\kappa(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let us define, for every
0<\varepsilon<1
, the standard mollifiers

\kappa_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\kappa(x/\varepsilon),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Given
\sigma\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
0<\varepsilon<1
, we define

\sigma_{\varepsilon}:=\sigma\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon}.

Notice that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
W_{p}(\sigma_{\varepsilon},\sigma)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Moreover, if
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we have

(4.19)

W_{p}(\sigma_{\varepsilon},\sigma^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})\leq W_{p}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\quad\text{ for every }0<\varepsilon<1

and it is easy to check that, if we set

(4.20)

C_{\varepsilon}:=\mathsf{m}_{p}(\kappa_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{d}),

then we have

(4.21)

\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu)+C_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ for every }0<\varepsilon<1.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We define the continuous functions
W_{\nu},W_{\nu}^{\varepsilon},F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}:{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle W_{\nu}(\mu):=W_{p}(\mu,\nu),\quad W_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu):=W_{\nu}(\mu_{\varepsilon}),\quad F^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\mu):=\frac{1}{p}(W_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu))^{p},\quad\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The proof of the following proposition follows the one of
[
12
, Proposition 4.17]
but since the exponent
p
may be different from
2
, the estimates are more complicated and thus reported in full.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
,
\delta,R>0
, let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits{\nu}=\overline{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)}
and
\nu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)\geq\delta\mathcal{L}^{d}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)
, and let
\zeta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
{\mathrm{C}}^{1}
nondecreasing function whose derivative has compact support. Then

(4.22)

|{\mathrm{D}}(\zeta\circ F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon})|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}(\mu)\leq\zeta^{\prime}(F^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\mu))W_{p}^{p-1}(\nu,\mu_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{ for }\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e. }\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}:=\{\mu^{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
be a dense and countable set
in
{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and let us set, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varphi_{h}:=\Phi(\nu,\mu^{h}_{\varepsilon})
,
\varphi_{h}^{*}:=\Phi^{*}(\nu,\mu^{h}_{\varepsilon})
(see Theorem
3.1
),

a_{h}:=\int_{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)}\varphi_{h}{\mathrm{d}}\nu,\quad u_{h}:=\varphi_{h}^{*}+a_{h},\quad G_{k}(\mu):=\max_{1\leq h\leq k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{h}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon},\quad\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Notice that, by (
3.11
),
u_{h}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu_{\varepsilon})
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Claim 1
. It holds

\lim_{k\to+\infty}G_{k}(\mu)=F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu)\quad\text{ for every }\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof of claim 1
. Since
G_{k+1}(\mu)\geq G_{k}(\mu)
for every
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we have that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}G_{k}(\mu)=\sup_{k}G_{k}(\mu)=\sup_{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{h}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ for every }\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By the definition of
\varphi_{h}
and
\varphi_{h}^{*}
we have, for every
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
h\in\mathbb{N}
, that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{h}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{h}^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\int_{{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)}\varphi_{h}(y){\mathrm{d}}\nu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}W_{p}^{p}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\nu)

\displaystyle=F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu).

This proves that
\sup_{k}G_{k}(\mu)\leq F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu)
for every
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Clearly, if
\mu\in\mathcal{G}
, this is an equality. By (
3.16
) there exists a constant
D_{p,R}
such that, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{h}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{h}{\mathrm{d}}\mu^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{h}^{*}{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq D_{p,R}W_{p}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\mu^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})(1+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu_{\varepsilon})+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}))

\displaystyle\leq D_{p,R}W_{p}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})(1+2C_{\varepsilon}+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu)+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu^{\prime})),

where we used (
4.19
) and (
4.21
). We hence deduce that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}

(4.23)

\left|G_{k}(\mu)-G_{k}(\mu^{\prime})\right|\leq D_{p,R}W_{p}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})(1+2C_{\varepsilon}+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu)+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu^{\prime}))\quad\text{ for every }\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Choosing
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
and passing to the limit as
k\to+\infty
we get from (
4.23
) that

\left|\lim_{k\to+\infty}G_{k}(\mu)-F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\prime})\right|\leq D_{p,R}W_{p}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})(1+2C_{\varepsilon}+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu)+\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu^{\prime}))\text{ for every }\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}.

Using the density of
\mathcal{G}
and the continuity of
\mu^{\prime}\mapsto F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\prime})
we deduce that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}G_{k}(\mu)=F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu)\quad\text{ for every }\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

proving the first claim.

Claim 2
.
If
H_{k}:=\zeta\circ G_{k}
and
u_{h,\varepsilon}:=u_{h}\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon}
, it holds

|{\mathrm{D}}H_{k}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)\leq\big(\zeta^{\prime}(G_{k}(\mu))\big)^{p^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla u_{h,\varepsilon}\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu=\big(\zeta^{\prime}(G_{k}(\mu))\big)^{p^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{*}\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu,

for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
\mu\in B_{h}^{k}
, where

Proof of claim 2
. For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, (
3.13
) and (
3.12
) (also using (
3.15

(4.24)

u_{h,\varepsilon}(x)\geq\frac{1}{4p}\|x\|^{p}+a_{h}-A_{p,R,\varepsilon},\quad\|\nabla u_{h,\varepsilon}(x)\|_{*}\leq A_{p,R,\varepsilon}(1+\|x\|^{p-1})\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

is a constant depending only on
p,R,\varepsilon

we can apply Theorem
2.5
(4) and conclude the proof of
the second claim.

Claim 3
. For every
R>0
there exists a constant

such
that

(4.25)

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla u_{h,\varepsilon}\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq C\quad\text{whenever }\mathsf{m}_{p}(\mu)\leq R.

Proof of Claim 3
.
It is sufficient to use (
4.24

Claim 4
. Let
(h_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{N}
be an increasing sequence and
let
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. If
\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{h_{n}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}=F_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\mu)

\limsup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla(\varphi_{h_{n}}^{*}\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\leq W_{p}^{p}(\nu,\mu_{\varepsilon}).

Proof of claim 4
. Since

\left\|\nabla(\varphi_{h_{n}}^{*}\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon})(x)\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\leq\left(\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h_{n}}^{*}\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon}\right)(x)\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla(\varphi_{h_{n}}^{*}\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h_{n}}^{*}\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}\ast\kappa_{\varepsilon}\right){\mathrm{d}}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h_{n}}^{*}\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}.

It is then enough to prove that

(4.26)

\limsup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h_{n}}^{*}\right\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq W_{p}^{p}(\nu,\mu_{\varepsilon}).

\nu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)\geq\delta\mathcal{L}^{d}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\mathrm{B}}_{\|\cdot\|}(0,R)

By the convergence of

(4.28)

We can thus apply Proposition

Claim 5
. It holds

\limsup_{k}|{\mathrm{D}}H_{k}|_{\star,q,\mathscr{A}}(\mu)\leq\zeta^{\prime}(F^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\mu))W_{p}^{p/p^{\prime}}(\nu,\mu_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{ for $\mathfrak{m}$-a.e. }\mu\in{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof of claim 5
. Let

\mathfrak{m}

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\iota(X),{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|^{\prime}})
.
By Theorem
2.5
(1)-(3), we obtain (
4.45
), concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
. Then the algebra
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
is dense in
q
-energy in

.

Proof.

It is enough to consider the functions of the form
\phi_{k}(x):={\langle}x_{k}^{*},x{\rangle}
,
x\in\mathbb{B}
, where
\{x_{k}^{*}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a subset of unit sphere in
\mathbb{B}^{*}
separating the points in
\mathbb{B}
. Notice that each
\phi_{k}
belongs to
{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})
.
∎

Corollary 4.20
.

Let
(\mathbb{M},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{M},{\sf d}_{g})
, where
{\sf d}_{g}
is the Riemannian distance induced by

. Then the algebra

-energy in

4.34
) for

.
∎

5.
Reflexivity, uniform convexity and Clarkson’s inequalities

The aim of this section is to study how some properties of a Banach space (or its dual) pass to the Wasserstein Sobolev space built on it. In the whole section
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
is a fixed separable Banach space and
\mathfrak{m}
is a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
.

To study the properties of the pre-Cheeger energy as in (
4.15
) we will use the concept of
L^{q}
-direct integral of Banach spaces (
[
10
,
17
]
) (see also the related notion of Banach bundle
[
23
]
), that we introduce here briefly in the form that it is best suited to our needs. Let
\mathscr{V}
be the vector space of functions

\mathscr{V}:=\left\{f:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}^{*}:f\text{ is measurable and }\int_{\mathbb{B}}\|f(x)\|_{*}^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)<+\infty\text{ for $\mathfrak{m}$-a.e. }\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\right\}.

It is not difficult to check that, for every
f\in\mathscr{V}
, the map
M_{f}:\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

M_{f}(\mu):=\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))},\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),

is measurable.
Let us denote by
\|\cdot\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}
the
{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))}
-norm on the completion of
\mathscr{V}_{\mu}:=\mathscr{V}/\sim_{\mu}
, where
\sim_{\mu}
is the equivalence relation of the equality
\mu
-a.e.. We say that
s:\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\to\bigsqcup_{\mu}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}
(i.e.
s(\mu)\in\mathscr{V}_{\mu}
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
) is a simple function if there exist a finite measurable partition of
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
,
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
, and values
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathscr{V}
such that
s(\mu)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{A_{k}}(\mu)f_{k}
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
. A function
G:\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\to\bigsqcup_{\mu}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}
is said to be Bochner measurable if there exists a sequence of simple functions
(s_{k})_{k}
such that
\|G(\mu)-s_{k}(\mu)\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}\to 0
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
. The direct integral of
(\mathscr{V}_{\mu})_{\mu}
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
is the vector space of Bochner measurable functions
G:\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\to\bigsqcup_{\mu}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}
modulo equivalence
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. and it is denoted by
\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)
. The
L^{q}
direct integral
\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)_{L^{q}}
of
(\mathscr{V}_{\mu})_{\mu}
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
is the subspace of
\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)
consisting of those functions
G
such that the (measurable) map
\mu\mapsto\|G(\mu)\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}
is in
L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m})
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space. Then
\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)_{L^{q}}
is a Banach space. If, in addition,
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
is reflexive (resp.
(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
is uniformly convex), then
\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)_{L^{q}}
is reflexive (resp. uniformly convex). Finally, if
F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
, then
{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F\in\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)_{L^{q}}
.

Proof.

The completeness property can be found in
[
10
, Proposition 3.2]
(see also
[
17
]
where the notion of direct integral was introduced for the first time).

If
(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
is uniformly convex, then we can use
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
, together with the fact that
L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m})
is uniformly convex and that the modulus of convexity of
\mathscr{V}_{\mu}
is larger than the one of
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|))
, being
\mathscr{V}_{\mu}
just a subspace of the latter, and the fact that the modulus of convexity of the Bochner space
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|))
does not depend on
\mu
but only on
p^{\prime}
and on the modulus of convexity of the uniformly convex space
(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
.

If
\mathbb{B}
is reflexive, then also
\mathbb{B}^{*}
and thus
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))
is reflexive so that we can apply
[
17
, Theorem 6.19]
, also noting that, obviously,
L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m})
is reflexive.

Let now
F=\psi\circ\mathsf{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
, with
\psi\in{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in({\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B}))^{N}
, for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
; notice that we are looking at
{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F
as the map sending
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
to
{\mathrm{D}}_{p}[\mu]:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}^{*}
. Of course
{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F[\mu]\in\mathscr{V}_{\mu}
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
and it is uniformly bounded. Moreover it is Bochner measurable since, in order to approximate it with simple functions, it is enough to approximate every term of the form
\partial\psi_{n}(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\phi_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\mu,\dots,\int_{\mathbb{B}}\phi_{N}{\mathrm{d}}\mu)
with
\mathbb{R}
-valued simple function
(s_{k}^{n})_{k}
on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
(which is possible since this map is measurable) so that

s_{k}(\mu):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}s_{k}^{n}(\mu)\nabla\phi_{n},\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),

is a sequence of simple functions approximating
{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable reflexive Banach space, let
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
be (not necessarily conjugate) fixed exponents and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
. Then the Sobolev space
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
is reflexive.

Proof.

It is sufficient to provide a linear isometry
\iota
from
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
into a reflexive Banach space. For simplicity let us denote by

X:=L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m}),\quad Y:=\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)_{L^{q}}

and let us define
{\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}\subset X\times Y
as the closure in
X\times Y
(endowed with the norm
\|(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}^{q}=\|x\|_{X}^{q}+\|y\|_{Y}^{q}
) of

\{(F,{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F):F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)\}.

Denoting by
\pi^{X}:X\times Y\to X
the projection
\pi^{X}(x,y)=x
for every
x\in X
, we define the sections of
G
by

{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}_{\mathfrak{m}}F:=\{G\in Y:(F,G)\in{\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}\},\quad F\in\pi^{X}({\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}).

It is clear that
G
is closed and convex so that it is also weakly closed; let us show that
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})\subset\pi^{X}({\mbox{\boldmath$G$}})
: if
F\in H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
, by Corollary
4.19
, we can find a sequence
(F_{n})_{n}\subset\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
such that

F_{n}\to F,\quad\mathop{\rm lip}\nolimits_{W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}}}F_{n}\to|{\mathrm{D}}F|_{\star,q}\quad\text{ in }X.

By Proposition
4.7
, we have that
\|{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F_{n}\|_{Y}
is uniformly bounded so that, by the reflexivity of
Y
, we can find a subsequence of
(F_{n_{k}})_{k}
such that

(F_{n_{k}},{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F_{n_{k}})\rightharpoonup(F,G)\text{ in }X\times Y.

This gives that
F\in\pi^{X}({\mbox{\boldmath$G$}})
and that
\|G\|_{Y}\leq\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F)
. Moreover, if we consider
F\in H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
and
G\in{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}_{\mathfrak{m}}F
, then there exists
(F_{n})_{n}\subset\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
such that
(F_{n},{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F)\to(F,G)
in
X\times Y
so that
\|G\|_{Y}=\lim_{n}\|{\mathrm{D}}_{p}F_{n}\|_{Y}=\lim_{n}\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F_{n})\geq\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F)
so that

\min_{G\in{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}_{\mathfrak{m}}F}\|G\|_{Y}=\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F),\quad F\in H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m}).

We define
{\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}_{0}:={\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}/(\{0\}\times{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}_{\mathfrak{m}}0)
with the quotient norm
\|\cdot\|_{Y,0}
: elements of
{\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}_{0}
are equivalence classes
[F]=\{(F,G):G\in{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}_{\mathfrak{m}}F\}
and the quotient norm is simply given by

\|[F]\|_{Y,0}=\inf_{G\in{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}_{\mathfrak{m}}F}\left(\|F\|^{q}_{X}+\|G\|_{Y}^{q})\right)^{1/q}=\left(\|F\|^{q}_{X}+\min_{G\in{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}_{\mathfrak{m}}F}\|G\|_{Y}^{q})\right)^{1/q}=\|F\|_{H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})}.

The linear isometry is thus given by
\iota:H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})\to{\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}_{0}
defined as

\iota(F)=[F],\quad F\in H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m}).

Since closed subspaces of reflexive spaces are reflexive and quotients of reflexive spaces are reflexive,
{\mbox{\boldmath$G$}}_{0}
is reflexive and this concludes the proof.
∎

The next result
[
31
, Lemma 2]
provides a quantitative version of the uniform convexity property that will pass from the pre-Cheeger energy to the Cheeger energy.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(\mathscr{W},\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{W}})
be a Banach space. Then
(\mathscr{W},\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{W}})
is uniformly convex if and only if for every
t\in(1,+\infty)
there exists a strictly increasing and continuous function
g_{t}:[0,2]\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\left\|\frac{x+y}{2}\right\|_{\mathscr{W}}^{t}+\left(\|x\|_{\mathscr{W}}\vee\|y\|_{\mathscr{W}}\right)^{t}g_{t}\left(\frac{\|x-y\|_{\mathscr{W}}}{\|x\|_{\mathscr{W}}\vee\|y\|_{\mathscr{W}})}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{\mathscr{W}}^{t}+\frac{1}{2}\|y\|_{\mathscr{W}}^{t}\quad\text{ for every }x,y\in\mathscr{W}.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space such that
(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
is uniformly convex, let
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
be (not necessarily conjugate) fixed exponents and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
. Then the Sobolev space
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
is uniformly convex.

Proof.

By the uniform convexity of
\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}^{\bigoplus}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)_{L^{q}}
provided by Proposition
5.1
and using Proposition
5.3
, we have that for every
t\in(1,+\infty)
there exists a continuous and strictly increasing function
g_{t}:[0,2]\to[0,+\infty)
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}\left(\frac{1}{2}(F+G)\right)^{t/q}&+\left(\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F)\vee\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(G)\right)^{t}g_{t}\left(\frac{\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F-G)}{\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F)\vee\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(G)}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{t/q}(F)+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{t/q}(G)\quad\text{ for every }F,G\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big),\end{split}

where we are able to identify the norm in the direct integral and the
q
-th root of the pre-Cheeger energy thanks to Proposition
4.7
.
If
F,G\in H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot}},\mathfrak{m})
, thanks to Corollary
4.19
, we can find sequences
(F_{n})_{n},(G_{n})_{n}\subset\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
such that

F_{n}\to F,\quad G_{n}\to G\quad\text{ in }L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m}),\quad\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(F_{n})\to\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(F),\quad\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(G_{n})\to\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(G).

By the lower semicontinuity of the Cheeger energy w.r.t. the
L^{q}
convergence and the continuity and monotonicity of
g_{t}
, we obtain that (
5.1
) holds for
\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}
instead of
\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}
. By Proposition
5.3
we obtain that
\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}
is uniformly convex, in the sense that, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that, whenever
F,G\in H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{p,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathfrak{m})
are such that
\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F)=\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(G)=1
and
\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F-G)\geq\varepsilon
, then
\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}(F+G)<2(1-\delta(\varepsilon))
. Since the
L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|},\mathfrak{m})
-norm is uniformly convex, the
q
-sum (i.e. the
q
-th root of the sum of the
q
-powers) of it with
\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}^{1/q}
is uniformly convex (see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 1]
). This means that the Sobolev norm is uniformly convex and concludes the proof.
∎

Given a separable Banach space
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
and a positive and finite Borel measure
\mathfrak{m}
on
\mathbb{B}
, we can consider the measure
\mathsf{D}_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}
on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
, where
\mathsf{D}:\mathbb{B}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
is defined as

\mathsf{D}(x):=\delta_{x},\quad x\in\mathbb{B}.

It is immediate to check that, for every
q\in(1,+\infty)
, the Sobolev spaces
H^{1,q}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|},\mathfrak{m})
and
H^{1,q}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),W_{2,{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}},\mathsf{D}_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m})
are isomorphic, see also
[
12
, Section 5.2]
. Notice that the choice
p=2
is irrelevant, since
\mathsf{D}(\mathbb{B})\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
for every
p\in[1,+\infty]
.
As a consequence of this observation and Theorems
5.2
and
5.4
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space, let
q\in(1,+\infty)
be a fixed exponent, and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}
. If
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
is reflexive (resp. if
(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
is uniformly convex), then
H^{1,q}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|},\mathfrak{m})
is reflexive (resp. uniformly convex).

We remark that the statement regarding reflexivity in the above corollary was already known (see e.g.
[
25
, Corollary 5.3.11]
) while the part concerning uniform convexity is new, at least to our knowledge.

5.1.
Clarkson’s type inequalities

In this subsection we show how one can deduce Clarkson’s type inequalities for the Sobolev norm starting from analogous inequalities that are satisfied for the dual norm in the separable Banach space
\mathbb{B}
. The following inequality was introduced for the first time by
[
6
]
and it is a generalization of the classical Clarkson’s inequalities
[
8
]
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
V
be a real vector space and let
r,s\in(1,+\infty)
. We say that a functional
\mathcal{J}:V\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies the
(r,s)
-Boas inequality (
(r,s)
-(BI)) in
V
if

\left(\mathcal{J}(u+v)^{r}+\mathcal{J}(u-v)^{r}\right)^{1/r}\leq 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{J}(u)^{s}+\mathcal{J}(v)^{s}\right)^{1/s}\quad\text{ for every }u,v\in V.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
V
be a real vector space, let
q\in(1,+\infty)
and let
\mathcal{J}_{1},\mathcal{J}_{2}:V\to[0,+\infty)
be two functionals satisfying the
(r,s)
-(BI) in
V
for some
r,s\in(1,+\infty)
such that
s\leq q\leq r
. Then the functional
\mathcal{J}:V\to[0,+\infty)
defined as

\mathcal{J}(u):=\left(\mathcal{J}_{1}(u)^{q}+\mathcal{J}_{2}(u)^{q}\right)^{1/q},\quad u\in V,

satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI) in
V
as well.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in V
; let us set

\displaystyle a_{0}:=\mathcal{J}_{1}(u+v),\quad

\displaystyle b_{0}:=\mathcal{J}_{1}(u-v),\quad

\displaystyle c_{0}:=\mathcal{J}_{2}(u+v),\quad

\displaystyle d_{0}:=\mathcal{J}_{2}(u-v),

\displaystyle a:=\mathcal{J}_{1}(u),\quad

\displaystyle b:=\mathcal{J}_{1}(v),\quad

\displaystyle c:=\mathcal{J}_{2}(u),\quad

\displaystyle d:=\mathcal{J}_{2}(v).

Since
\mathcal{J}_{1}
and
\mathcal{J}_{2}
satisfy the
(r,s)
-(BI) in
V
, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left(a_{0}^{r}+b_{0}^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(a^{s}+b^{s}\right)^{1/s},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left(c_{0}^{r}+d_{0}^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(c^{s}+d^{s}\right)^{1/s}.

Then

\displaystyle 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{J}(u)^{s}+\mathcal{J}(v)^{s}\right)^{1/s}

\displaystyle=2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left((a^{q}+c^{q})^{s/q}+(b^{q}+d^{q})^{s/q}\right)^{1/s}

\displaystyle=2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(((a^{s})^{q/s}+(c^{s})^{q/s})^{s/q}+((b^{s})^{q/s}+(d^{s})^{q/s})^{s/q}\right)^{1/s}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(\left(a^{s}+b^{s}\right)^{q/s}+\left(c^{s}+d^{s}\right)^{q/s}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\left(a_{0}^{r}+b_{0}^{r}\right)^{q/r}+\left(c_{0}^{r}+d_{0}^{r}\right)^{q/r}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\left(\left((a_{0}^{q})^{r/q}+(b_{0}^{q})^{r/q}\right)^{q/r}+\left((c_{0}^{q})^{r/q}+(d_{0}^{q})^{r/q}\right)^{q/r}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\geq\left((a_{0}^{q}+c_{0}^{q})^{r/q}+(b_{0}^{q}+d_{0}^{q})^{r/q}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{J}(u+v)^{r}+\mathcal{J}(u-v)^{r}\right)^{1/r},

where we have used the triangular inequality for the
\alpha
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
first with
\alpha=q/s
(from the second to the third line) and then with
\alpha=r/q
(from the fifth to the sixth line), and the inequalities (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) to pass from the third to the fourth line.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a Polish metric-measure space, let
\mathscr{A}
be a separating unital subalgebra of

\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{b}(X,{\sf d})
satisfying (
2.12
) and let
q,r,s\in(1,+\infty)
. If
(\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q})^{1/q}
satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI) in
\mathscr{A}
, then
(\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q})^{1/q}
satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI) in
H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
. If in addition
r^{\prime}\leq s\leq q\leq r
, then the Sobolev norm in
H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI).

Proof.

The proof that
(\mathsf{C\kern-1.0ptE}_{q})^{1/q}
satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI) in
H^{1,q}(X,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is the same of
[
12
, Theorem 2.15]
for the case
r=s=q=2
. If in addition we know that
r^{\prime}\leq s\leq q\leq r
, then the
L^{q}(X,\mathfrak{m})
-norm satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI) in

[

, Theorem 1]
so that we can apply Lemma
5.7
and conclude that the Sobolev norm satisfies the

In the next Proposition
5.9
we show how to deduce the
(r,s)
-(BI) for the pre-Cheeger energy from the same inequality for the
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))
norm. Instead of stating our hypothesis in terms of the validity of a Clarkson’s inequality in
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
, we state it directly in terms of the (BI) in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))
: the two notions are different in general, we refer to
[
16
]
for the treatment of the relation between them.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a positive and finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
; suppose that there exists
(r,s)
with
1<s\leq q\leq r
such that the
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))
-norm satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI) (cf. Definition
5.6
) in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
. Then the functional
\mathcal{J}:\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)\to[0,+\infty)
defined as

\mathcal{J}(F):=(\mathsf{pC\kern-1.0ptE}_{q}(F))^{1/q},\quad F\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big),

satisfies the
(r,s)
-(BI) in
\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
.

Proof.

Let
F,G\in\mathfrak{C}\big(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}),{\mathrm{C}}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{B})\big)
. We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}
the
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B},\mu;(\mathbb{B}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}))
-norm for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
and we set

U_{\mu}(x):={\mathrm{D}}_{p}F(\mu,x),\quad V_{\mu}(x):={\mathrm{D}}_{p}G(\mu,x),\quad(\mu,x)\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})\times\mathbb{B}.

By hypothesis we have

\left(\|U_{\mu}+V_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{r}+\|U_{\mu}-V_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{r}\right)^{1/r}\leq 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(\|U_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{s}+\|V_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{s}\right)^{1/s}

for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})
.
Computing the
L^{q}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m})
-norm on both sides and applying Minkowski inequalities in
L^{q/r}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m})
and in
L^{q/s}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|}),\mathfrak{m})
(notice that
0<q/r\leq 1
and
q/s\geq 1
), we get

\displaystyle\left(\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}\|U_{\mu}+V_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)^{r/q}+\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}\|U_{\mu}-V_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)^{r/q}\right)^{1/r}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}\|U_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)^{s/q}+\left(\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{B},{\sf d}_{\|\cdot\|})}\|V_{\mu}\|_{*,p^{\prime},\mu}^{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(\mu)\right)^{s/q}\right)^{1/s}

i.e.

\left(\mathcal{J}(F+G)^{r}+\mathcal{J}(F-G)^{r}\right)^{1/r}\leq 2^{1/s^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{J}(F)^{s}+\mathcal{J}(G)^{s}\right)^{1/s},

by Proposition
4.7
.
∎

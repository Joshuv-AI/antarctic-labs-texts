Some measure-theoretical and dynamical properties of successive minima on the space of unimodular lattices
Hao Xing

Abstract
In this article, we study the relation between lattice basis and successive minima and give an estimate for the measure-theoretical distribution of successive minima. As consequences, we also discuss some logarithm laws associated to higher sucessive minima.

v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{d}
of
\Lambda
such that

\|v_{1}\|=\lambda_{1}(\Lambda),\|v_{2}\|_{d}\asymp_{d}\lambda_{2}(\Lambda),\dots,\|v_{d}\|\asymp_{d}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda).

Here
A\asymp_{d}B
means there exist positive constants
c_{d},C_{d}
depending only on
d
such that

c_{d}|A|\leq|B|\leq C_{d}|A|.

Proof.

We shall prove this by induction on
d
. The case
d=1
is obvious.

Assume the statement holds for lattices with rank less than or equal to
d-1
. For a rank
d
lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
v_{1}
be any nonzero vector in
\Lambda
satisfying
\|v_{1}\|=\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)
and let
\pi_{1}
be the projection of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
onto
v_{1}^{\perp}
, the hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
orthogonal to
v_{1}
as in the previous lemma.

Now applying the induction hypothesis to the
d-1
dimensional hyperplane
v_{1}^{\perp}
and the rank
d-1
lattice
\pi_{1}(\Lambda)
contained in
v_{1}^{\perp}
yields a basis
w_{2}\dots w_{d}
of
\pi_{1}(\Lambda)
with

\|w_{2}\|=\lambda_{1}(\pi_{1}(\Lambda)),\|w_{3}\|\asymp_{d}\lambda_{2}(\pi_{1}(\Lambda)),\dots,\|w_{d}\|\asymp_{d}\lambda_{d-1}(\pi_{1}(\Lambda)).

By the monotonicity of
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}s
, we know

\|w_{2}\|\lesssim_{d}\cdots\lesssim_{d}\|w_{d}\|.

Our next step is to choose some
v_{2},\dots,v_{d}
in
\Lambda
as preimages of
w_{2},\dots,w_{d}
under
\pi_{1}
such that
v_{1},v_{2}\dots,v_{d}
form a basis of
\Lambda
. We start by choosing
v_{2},\cdots,v_{d}
to be any
d-1
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\pi_{1}(v_{j})=w_{j},2\leq j\leq d.

It follows that
v_{1},\cdots,v_{d}
are
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent and thus form an
\mathbb{R}
-linear basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For any
v\in\Lambda
,

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(v)

\displaystyle=n_{2}w_{2}+\cdots+n_{d}w_{d}

\displaystyle=n_{2}\pi_{1}(v_{2})+\cdots+n_{d}\pi_{1}(v_{d})

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(n_{2}v_{2}+\cdots+n_{d}v_{d}).

So
\pi_{1}[v-(n_{2}v_{2}+\dots+n_{d}v_{d})]=0
and

v=n_{2}v_{2}+\dots+n_{d}v_{d}+tv_{1},

for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
. But since
v\in\Lambda
,
tv_{1}\in\Lambda
and thus
t=0
or
\pm 1
since

by our choice is a minimal nonzero vector.

Therefore,
\Lambda=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{v_{1},\dots v_{d}\}
. Namely
v_{1},\dots,v_{d}
indeed form a basis for
\Lambda
.

Observe that replacing each
v_{j}
with
v_{j}+n_{j}v_{1},n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
does not change the nature that
v_{1},\cdots,v_{d}
form a basis of
\Lambda
. Since
v_{j}=w_{j}+t_{j}v_{1}
for some
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, by carefully choosing

we may assume
t_{j}\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.

It follows that for
2\leq j\leq d
,

\displaystyle\|w_{j}\|\leq\|v_{j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{j}\|+|t_{j}|\|v_{1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{j}\|+\frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\|w_{2}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})\|w_{j}\|,

where the second inequality follows from the previous lemma with
\pi_{1}(v_{2})=w_{2}
. So
\|v_{j}\|\asymp_{d}\|w_{j}\|,j=2\dots,d
.

Next, we observe that
\lambda_{j-1}(\pi_{1}(\Lambda))\leq\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)
. This is because if
v_{1},v_{2}^{\prime},\dots v_{j}^{\prime}
represent the first
j
successive minima vectors in
\Lambda
, then their projection images (excluding
\pi_{1}(v_{1})=0
),
\pi_{1}(v_{1}),\pi_{1}(v_{2}^{\prime}),\dots\pi_{1}(v_{j}^{\prime})
are still linearly independent in
v_{1}^{\perp}
and

\begin{cases}\|\pi_{1}(v_{2}^{\prime})\|\leq\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)\\
~~~~~\vdots\\
\|\pi_{1}(v_{j}^{\prime})\|\leq\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)\\
\end{cases}\,,

which implies
\lambda_{j-1}(\pi_{1}(\Lambda))\leq\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)
. Therefore

\|v_{j}\|\asymp_{d}\|w_{j}\|\asymp_{d}\lambda_{j-1}(\pi_{1}(\Lambda))\leq\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)

for
j=2,\dots,d
.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\|v_{1}\|,\dots,\|v_{j}\|\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\max\{\|w_{2}\|,\dots,\|w_{j}\|\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\|w_{j}\|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j-1}(\pi_{1}(\Lambda)).

Therefore

\|v_{j}\|\asymp_{d}\lambda_{j}(\Lambda),j=1,2,\dots d.

The proof is complete by the induction hypothesis.
∎

1.2
Continuity of successive minima

Next, we study the continuity of successive minima on the space of lattices.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
b\in\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and
\|b\|_{op}
denotes the operator norm of
b
, then we have for all
i=1,2,\dots,d
and unimodular lattice
\Lambda
, the inequality

\lambda_{i}(b\Lambda)\leq\|b\|_{op}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)

(1.3)

Proof.

For
i=1,2,\dots,d
, let
v_{1},\dots,v_{i}
denote the
i
linearly independent vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\|v_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda).

Consider the vectors
bv_{1},\dots,bv_{i}
. Since
b\in\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
,
bv_{1},\dots,bv_{i}
are again linearly independent. From
\|bv_{i}\|\leq\|b\|_{op}\|v_{i}\|
it follows that
bv_{1},\dots,bv_{i}
are contained in a ball of radius
\|b\|_{op}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
. So it follows that
\lambda_{i}(b\Lambda)\leq\|b\|_{op}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
. ∎

Theorem 1.10
.

\lambda_{i}(\cdot)
are continuous functions on the space of unimodular lattices
\mathcal{L}
for
i=1,2,\cdots d
.

Proof.

We may identify
\mathcal{L}
with the homogeneous space
G/\Gamma:=\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})

By the Lemma
1.9
, we have for any

,

\frac{1}{\|b\|_{op}}\lambda_{i}(b\Lambda)\leq\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)\leq\|c\|_{op}\lambda_{i}(c^{-1}\Lambda).

(1.4)

For any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}
, we may write
\Lambda=g\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some
g\in\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
, identified with
g\Gamma
. For any convergent sequence of lattices

g_{i}\Gamma\to g\Gamma,t\to\infty

(1.5)

which is equivalent to the convergence

.

Let

denote any right-invariant metric on

and define a metric

on

For each

, namely

It follows that the condition

. Therefore, by replacing the representative

in
g_{j}\Gamma

g_{i}\gamma
. We may assume

for the equation

.

Now taking

.
∎

1.3
Sucessive minima and the dual lattice

Recall that for a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with basis
\{\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}\}
, since
\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
are linearly independent, from linear algebra we know there exist vectors
\textbf{b}_{1}^{*},\cdots\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}
, call
dual vectors
to

\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
, such that

\langle\textbf{b}_{i},\textbf{b}_{i}^{*}\rangle=\begin{cases}0&i\neq j\\
1&i=j\end{cases}.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-span of dual basis vectors, namely
\Lambda^{*}:=\text{Span}\{\textbf{b}_{1}^{*},\cdots\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}\}
, is called the
dual (or polar or reciprocal) lattice
to the lattice
\Lambda
.

Although defined through basis, it turns out that the dual lattices are independent of the choice of basis of the original lattice.

Proposition 1.11
.

The dual lattice
\Lambda^{*}
consists of all vectors
\textbf{b}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\langle\textbf{b}^{*},\textbf{b}\rangle
is an integer for all
b
in
\Lambda
. As a consequence,
\Lambda^{*}
is also the dual of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
be a basis of the lattice
\Lambda
and their duals be
\textbf{b}_{1}^{*},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}
. For any
\textbf{b}\in\Lambda
and any
\textbf{c}\in\Lambda^{*}
, suppose

\textbf{b}=s_{1}\textbf{b}_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}\textbf{b}_{d},\text{ and }\textbf{c}=t_{1}\textbf{b}_{1}^{*}+\cdots+t_{d}\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}

with integer coefficients
s_{i},t_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,d.
We have immediately that

\langle\textbf{b},\textbf{c}\rangle=s_{1}t_{1}\cdots+s_{d}t_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}.

On the other hand, if
\textbf{b}^{*}=u_{1}\textbf{b}_{1}^{*}+\cdots+u_{d}\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\langle\textbf{b}^{*},\textbf{b}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z},
for any
\textbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then in particular this holds for
\textbf{b}=\textbf{b}_{i}
, for any
i=1,2,\cdots,d
and thus

u_{i}=\langle\textbf{b}^{*},\textbf{b}_{i}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore
\textbf{b}^{*}\in\Lambda^{*}
.

∎

The dual lattice operator commutes nicely with an invertible linear transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

(T\Lambda)^{*}=T^{*}\Lambda^{*},

where

is the inverse of the transpose of

and
\Lambda^{*}
is the dual lattice to

.

Proof.

If
\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
is a basis of
\Lambda

\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,T\textbf{b}_{d}

is a basis of
T\Lambda

satisfy

\begin{bmatrix}{}^{t}(T\textbf{b}_{1})^{*}\\
\vdots\\
{}^{t}(T\textbf{b}_{1})^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}T\textbf{b}_{1}&\cdots&T\textbf{b}_{d}\end{bmatrix}=I_{d}

But on the other hand,

\begin{bmatrix}{}^{t}({}^{t}T^{-1}\textbf{b}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
{}^{t}({}^{t}T^{-1}\textbf{b}_{d})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}T\textbf{b}_{1}&\cdots&T\textbf{b}_{d}\end{bmatrix}=I_{d}

So by the uniqueness of inverse matrix,
T^{*}\textbf{b}_{i}={}^{t}T^{-1}\textbf{b}_{i}=(Tb_{i})
, for any

(T\Lambda)^{*}=T^{*}\Lambda^{*}.

∎

The measure-theoretical distribution of
\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
in the space of unimodular lattices

2.1
Haar measure on the space of unimodular lattices

In this subsection we shall recall a few definitions and results on Siegel sets and the probability Haar measure on the space of unimodular lattices, identified with
G/\Gamma:=\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
.

The main reference for the following is
[
BM00
]
Chapter V and
[
Fol15
]
Section 2.6.

Let
K:=\text{SO}(d,\mathbb{R})
,

A:=\{\text{diag}(a_{1},...a_{d}):a_{1}\cdots a_{d}=1,a_{i}>0,\forall i=1,2,\dots,d\},

the diagonal subgroup of
\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
with positive entries and

N:=\{(n_{ij})\in\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R}):n_{ii}=1,n_{ij}=0,\forall i<j\},

the subgroup of upper triangular unipotent matrices in
G
. We have

Theorem 2.1

(Iwasawa Decomposition)
.

The product map

K\times A\times N\to G,(k,a,n)\to kan

is a homeomorphism.

Definition 2.2

(Siegel Sets in
\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
)
.

A Siegel Set in
\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a set
\Sigma_{t,u}
of the form

\Sigma_{t,u}:=KA_{t}N_{u},

where
t,u>0
and
A_{t}
and
N_{u}
are given by

A_{t}:=\left\{\text{diag}(a_{1},...a_{d})\in A:\frac{a_{i}}{a_{i+1}}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}},i=1,2,...d-1\right\}

and

N_{u}:=\left\{(n_{i}j)\in N:|n_{ij}|\leq u,\forall i<j\right\}

It turns out that
\Sigma_{t,u}
can cover the fundamental domain of
G:=\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
under the action of
\Gamma:=\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
:

Theorem 2.3
.

For
t\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}
and
u\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we have
G=\Sigma_{t,u}\Gamma
. As a result
\Sigma_{t,u}
contains a fundamental domain of
G/\Gamma
.

Another important fact about Siegel sets is that it only intersects finitely many of its
\Gamma
-translates

Theorem 2.4
.

Fix
t
and
u
, then for all but finitely many
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have

\Sigma_{t,u}\gamma\cap\Sigma_{t,u}=\varnothing.

In particular, all but finitely many
\gamma\in\Gamma
satisfies

\Sigma_{t,u}\cap F\gamma=\varnothing.

Now we turn to look at the Haar measure on
G
. Let
B=AN\cong N\rtimes_{c}A
(note that as sets
AN=NA
) be the semidirect product of
A
and
N
with conjugation as action:

c:A\to\text{N},a\mapsto c_{a},

where
c_{a}(n)=ana^{-1}
. In other words, the product in
AN
is defined by

a_{1}n_{1}\cdot a_{2}n_{2}:=(a_{1}a_{2})(n_{1}a_{1}n_{2}a_{1}^{-1}).

Proposition 2.5
.

da:=\frac{da_{1}}{a_{1}}\dots\frac{da_{d-1}}{a_{d-1}}
, with the right hand side identified with the standard Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, is a bi-invariant Haar measure on
A
.

Proof.

For
a^{\prime}=\text{diag}(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},\dots,a_{d}^{\prime})\in A
, we have
a^{\prime}a=\text{diag}(a_{1}^{\prime}a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}a_{2},\dots,a_{d}^{\prime}a_{d})
. Hence,

d(a^{\prime}a)=\frac{d(a_{1}^{\prime}a_{1})}{a_{1}^{\prime}a_{1}}\cdots\frac{d(a_{d-1}^{\prime}a_{d-1})}{a_{d-1}^{\prime}a_{d-1}}=da.

∎

Proposition 2.6
.

dn:=\prod_{i<j}dn_{ij}
, with the right hand side identified with the standard Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d(d-1)/2}
, is a bi-invariant Haar measure on
N
.

Proof.

For
n^{\prime}=(n^{\prime}_{ij})\in N
, the
(i,j)
-th entry of
(n_{ij}^{\prime})(n_{ij})
is

n_{ij}+(n_{i,i+1}^{\prime}n_{i+1,j}+\cdots+n_{i,j-1}^{\prime}n_{j-1,j})+n_{ij}^{\prime},

whose partial derivative w.r.t.
n_{ij}
is
1
. So by the
\frac{d(d-1)}{2}
-dimensional change of variable formula with Jacobian the identity matrix, we obtain the left invariance
d(n^{\prime}n)=dn
. The right invariance is similar.

∎

Proposition 2.7
.

\rho(a)dadn
is a right invariant Haar measure on
B
, where the coefficient
\rho(a):=\prod_{i<j}\frac{a_{i}}{a_{j}}
.

Proof.

For
a^{\prime}n^{\prime},an\in N\rtimes_{c}A=:B
,
and for any continuous function
f
with compact support on
AN
, identified with
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d(d-1)/2}
via the previous propositions,

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{N}f(ana^{\prime}n^{\prime})\rho(a)dadn=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{N}f(aa^{\prime}a^{\prime-1}na^{\prime}n^{\prime})\rho(a)dadn

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{N}f(aa^{\prime}(a^{\prime-1}na^{\prime})n^{\prime})\rho(a)dadn.

Making a change of variable
n\mapsto a^{\prime}na^{\prime-1}
, whose Jacobian can be easily computed as
\rho(a^{\prime})=\prod_{i<j}\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{a_{j}^{\prime}}
, this is equal to

\int_{N}\int_{A}f(aa^{\prime}nn^{\prime})\rho(a)d(a^{\prime}na^{\prime-1})da=\int_{N}\int_{A}f(aa^{\prime}nn^{\prime})\rho(a)\rho(a^{\prime})dnda.

Making change of variables
a\mapsto aa^{\prime-1}
and then
n\to nn^{\prime-1}
and noticing that
da,dn
are bi-invariant and that
\rho
is a group character, the above is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{N}\int_{A}f(ann^{\prime})\rho(aa^{\prime-1})\rho(a^{\prime})dnda=

\displaystyle\int_{N}\int_{A}f(an)\rho(a)dnda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{N}f(an)\rho(a)dadn.

This proves the right invariance of the measure
\rho(a)dadn
on
B
.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
dk
denote a (finite) Haar measure on
K
. If we identify
G=\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
with
KB=KAN
via the Iwasawa decomposition (Theorem

), then
\rho(a)dkdadn
gives a bi-invariant Haar measure on

.

Now we define the Haar measure on
G/\Gamma
:

Theorem and Definition 2.9

(Haar meaure on
G/\Gamma
)
.

Let
F
be any compactly supported continuous function on
G/\Gamma
, then there exists a compacted supported continuous function
f
on
G

F(g\Gamma):=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(g\gamma).

Define

\int_{X}F(g\Gamma)d(g\Gamma):=\int_{G}f(g)dg.

(2.1)

The right hand side
\int_{G}f(g)dg
is independent of the choice of

by unfolding the integral using the quotient integral formula (Theorem 2.51 in
[
Fol15
]
). Therefore by the theory of Radon measures on locally compact Hausdorff spaces (
[
Fol07
]

defines a left
G

where
\hat{f}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)dx
is the Fourier transform of
f
at
0
.

Proof of Claim.

Indeed, notice that
f
is
K
-invariant (rotation invariant), so

f(te_{1})=f(x),\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ with }\|x\|=t.

By the spherical coordinate in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=r\cos(\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle x_{2}

\displaystyle=r\sin(\varphi_{1})\cos(\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle x_{3}

\displaystyle=r\sin(\varphi_{1})\sin(\varphi_{2})\cos(\varphi_{3})

\displaystyle\,\,\,\vdots

\displaystyle x_{d-1}

\displaystyle=r\sin(\varphi_{1})\cdots\sin(\varphi_{d-2})\cos(\varphi_{d-1})

\displaystyle x_{d}

\displaystyle=r\sin(\varphi_{1})\cdots\sin(\varphi_{d-2})\sin(\varphi_{d-1}).

where
0\leq\phi_{d-1}\leq 2\pi
and
0\leq\phi_{i}\leq\pi
for all
i\leq d-1
.
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)dx=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,\pi]^{d-1}\times[0,2\pi]}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\left|\det\frac{\partial(x_{i})}{\partial\left(r,\varphi_{j}\right)}\right|dr\,d\varphi_{1}\,d\varphi_{2}\cdots d\varphi_{d-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,\pi]^{d-1}\times[0,2\pi]}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(re_{1})r^{d-1}\sin^{d-2}(\varphi_{1})\sin^{d-3}(\varphi_{2})\cdots\sin(\varphi_{d-2})\,dr\,d\varphi_{1}\,d\varphi_{2}\cdots d\varphi_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(S^{d-1})\int_{0}^{\infty}f(re_{1})r^{d-1}dr.

∎

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{G/\Gamma}F(g\Gamma)d(g\Gamma)=

\displaystyle f(0)\mu(G/\Gamma)+\text{Vol}(S^{d-1})\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z}))\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(te_{1})t^{d-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(0)\mu(G/\Gamma)+\hat{f}(0)\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z}))\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(0)\mu(G/\Gamma)+\hat{f}(0)\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z}))\zeta(d).

(1.3)

In other to find
\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z}))
, we shall look at the dual version of the above equation.

For any
g\in G
,
g\mathbb{Z}^{d}
defines a lattice and its dual lattice is
g^{*}\mathbb{Z}^{d}={}^{t}g^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(Proposition
1.12
). But the automorphism

*:G\to G,g\mapsto g^{*}

clearly preserves the Haar measure on
G
and
\gamma\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\gamma^{*}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. So it also preserves the Haar measure on
G/\Gamma
.

On the other hand, by the Poisson summation formula:

F(g\Gamma)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(gv)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{f}(g^{*}v)=:\hat{F}(g^{*}).

Since
\hat{\hat{f}}(0)=f(0)
, by replacing
f
in the recursion equation
1.3
by
\hat{f}
, we have

\displaystyle f(0)\mu(G/\Gamma)+\hat{f}(0)\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z}))\zeta(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G/\Gamma}F(g\Gamma)d(g\Gamma)=\int_{G/\Gamma}\hat{F}(g\Gamma)d(g\Gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{f}(0)\mu(G/\Gamma)+\hat{\hat{f}}(0)\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z}))\zeta(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{f}(0)\mu(G/\Gamma)+f(0).

Since we have chosen
f(0)=\hat{f}(0)
at the beginning, this yields:

\text{Vol}(G/\Gamma)=\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z}))=\zeta(d)\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z}))=\zeta(d)\text{Vol}(G_{1}/\Gamma_{1}),

for
d>2
and with our discussion above (before the claim),

\text{Vol}(\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}))=\zeta(2).

By induction, we have

\text{Vol}(G/\Gamma)=\zeta(d)\cdots\zeta(d-k+1)\text{Vol}(G_{k}/\Gamma_{k})

This gives the constant we need for the generalized Siegel formula as well as

\text{Vol}(G/\Gamma)=\zeta(d)\cdots\zeta(2).

STFT spectral loss for training a neural speech waveform model
Shinji Takaki, Toru Nakashika, Xin Wang, Junichi Yamagishi

Abstract
This paper proposes a new loss using short-time Fourier transform (STFT) spectra for the aim of training a high-performance neural speech waveform model that predicts raw continuous speech waveform samples directly. Not only amplitude spectra but also phase spectra obtained from generated speech waveforms are used to calculate the proposed loss. We also mathematically show that training of the waveform model on the basis of the proposed loss can be interpreted as maximum likelihood training that assumes the amplitude and phase spectra of generated speech waveforms following Gaussian and von Mises distributions, respectively. Furthermore, this paper presents a simple network architecture as the speech waveform model, which is composed of uni-directional long short-term memories (LSTMs) and an auto-regressive structure. Experimental results showed that the proposed neural model synthesized high-quality speech waveforms.

Introduction

Research on speech waveform modeling is advancing because of neural networks
[
1
,
2
]
. The WaveNet
[
2
]
directly models waveform signals and demonstrates excellent performance. The WaveNet can also be used as a vocoder, which converts acoustic features, e.g., a mel-spectrogram sequence, into speech waveform signals
[
3
,
4
]
. Such a neural speech waveform model used as a vocoder can be integrated into a text-to-speech synthesis system, and it has been shown that it outperforms conventional signal-processing-based vocoders
[
5
]
. Neural speech waveform models will be essential components for many speech synthesis applications.

There have been several investigations of neural speech waveform models about output distribution and training criteria. A categorical distribution for discrete waveform samples and cross entropy are introduced to train the original WaveNet
[
2
]
. The mixture of logistic distribution and a discretized logistic mixture likelihood are also used to train the improved WaveNet
[
6
]
. The parallel-WaveNet
[
6
]
and the ClariNet
[
7
]
use a distilling approach to transfer the knowledge of auto-regressive (AR) WaveNet to a simpler non-AR student model. The distilling approach typically introduces an amplitude spectral loss as the auxiliary loss in the distillation in order to avoid generating devoiced/whisper voices. We believe that losses using short-time Fourier transform (STFT) spectra can be used beyond the distillation, and they can be used for training speech waveform models themselves. Because it is known that (complex-valued) STFT spectra represent speech characteristics well, the spectra would be useful for efficient training of neural waveform models.

In this paper, we propose a new loss using the STFT spectra for the aim of training a high-performance neural speech waveform model that predicts raw continuous speech waveform samples directly (rather than the auxiliary loss used for the distillation process). Because not only amplitude spectra but also phase spectra represent speech characteristics
[
8
]
, the proposed loss considers both amplitude and phase spectral losses obtained from generated speech waveforms. Also, we give interpretation based on probability distributions about training using the proposed loss. This interpretation provides us better understanding of the proposed method and its relationship to various spectral losses such as Kullback–-Leibler divergence or Itakura–Saito divergence
[
9
,
10
]
. This paper also presents a simple network architecture as a speech waveform model. The proposed simple network is composed of uni-directional long short-term memories (LSTMs) and an auto-regressive structure unlike the WaveNet, which uses a relatively complicated convolutional neural network (CNN) with stacked dilated convolution.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 of this paper presents a neural speech waveform and the proposed loss to train it. Section 3 describes the proposed network architecture. Experimental results are presented in Section 4. We conclude in Section 5 with a summary and mention of our future work.

2.2
Amplitude spectral loss

A loss function for an amplitude spectrum of frequency bin

\displaystyle E^{(amp)}_{t,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{A}_{t,n}-A_{t,n})^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{A}_{t,n}-(\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}.

We then obtain a partial derivative of Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{E^{(amp)}_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left.+\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\overline{\boldsymbol{W}}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{E^{(ph)}_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}

2.4
Loss function for model training

A partial derivative of the amplitude loss function (Eq. (
9

\theta_{t,n}
), and that of the phase loss function (Eq. (
12

\overline{Y}

In this paper, a combination of amplitude and phase spectral loss functions is used for training a neural speech waveform model.

\displaystyle E^{(sp)}=\sum_{t,n}(E^{(amp)}_{t,n}+\alpha_{t,n}E^{(ph)}_{t,n}),

(13)

where,
\alpha_{t,n}

for training a neural speech waveform model.

\alpha_{t,n}=0
:
Using only the amplitude spectral loss function for model training.

\alpha_{t,n}=1
:
Using a simple combination of the amplitude and phase spectral loss functions.

\alpha_{t,n}=v_{t}
:
Here,
v_{t}
represents a voiced/unvoiced flag (1:voiced, 0:unvoiced). In this case, we assume that phase spectra in unvoiced parts are random values, and hence the phase spectral loss computed in unvoiced parts is omitted from model training.

2.5
Interpretation based on probability distributions

First, Eq. (
7
) and Eq. (
10
) can be rewritten as follows.

\displaystyle E^{(amp)}_{t,n}

\displaystyle=\log P_{g}(\hat{A}_{t,n}\mid\hat{A}_{t,n},1)-\log P_{g}(\hat{A}_{t,n}\mid A_{t,n},1)

(14)

\displaystyle E^{(ph)}_{t,n}

\displaystyle=1-\cos(\hat{\theta}_{t,n}-\theta_{t,n})

\displaystyle=\log P_{vm}(\hat{\theta}_{t,n}\mid\hat{\theta}_{t,n},1)-\log P_{vm}(\hat{\theta}_{t,n}\mid\theta_{t,n},1),

(15)

where,
P_{g}(\cdot)
and
P_{vm}(\cdot)
are probability density functions of the Gaussian distribution and the von Mises distribution.

\displaystyle P_{g}(x\mid\mu,\sigma^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

(16)

\displaystyle P_{vm}(x\mid\mu,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(\beta\cos(x-\mu))}{2\pi I_{0}(\beta)}

(17)

I_{0}(\beta)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order
0
.

From relationships of Eqs. (
7
), (
10
), (
14
), and (
2.5
), minimization of Eq. (
13
) is equivalent to that of the log negative likelihood
L
below.

\displaystyle L=-\log\prod_{t,n}

\displaystyle P_{g}(\hat{A}_{t,n}\mid A_{t,n},1)P_{vm}^{\alpha_{t,n}}(\hat{\theta}_{t,n}\mid\theta_{t,n},1).

(18)

In other words, the amplitude and phase spectra of a neural network’s outputs represent mean parameters of the Gaussian distribution and the von Mises distribution, respectively. Thus, optimizing a neural network on the basis of the proposed loss function is the maximum likelihood estimation that uses the above probabilistic functions for the speech waveform model.

In this initial investigation we used the Gaussian distribution and the von Mises distribution to define loss functions for amplitude and phase spectra. But we can easily define other meaningful spectral losses by replacing the distributions with other distributions. For instance, if we use Poisson or exponential distribution instead of Gaussian distribution, we can define the Kullback–-Leibler divergence or Itakura–Saito divergence as an amplitude spectral loss
[
9
,
10
]
. Also, we can easily define a different loss function for phase spectra by replacing the von Mises distribution with other distributions such as the generalized cardioid distribution
[
11
]
, the generalized version of the von Mises distribution.

5
Conclusion

We proposed a new STFT spectral loss to train a high-performance speech waveform model directly. We also presented a simple network architecture for the model, which is composed of uni-directional LSTMs and an auto-regressive structure. Experimental results showed that the proposed model can synthesize high-quality speech waveforms.

This is a part of our sequential work. In our next paper
[
13
]
, we will show that the above model can be improved further to achieve performance comparable with the WaveNet. Our other future work includes training based on other time-frequency analysis such as modified discrete cosine transform.

Details of the partial derivatives

The Wirtinger derivative is used to calculate the partial derivative with respect to a complex value
z
in the complex domain as,

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{dz}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{\mathcal{R}(z)}}-i\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{\mathcal{I}(z)}}\right),

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{d\overline{z}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{\mathcal{R}(z)}}+i\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{\mathcal{I}(z)}}\right).

(21)

If
E
is a real function, the complex gradient vector is given by

\displaystyle\nabla E

\displaystyle=2\frac{dE}{d\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}}

(22)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{z})}}+i\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{z})}}.

(23)

For a non-analytic function, the chain rule is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{x}}=\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{u}}\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{x}}+\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial{\overline{u}}}\frac{\partial{\overline{u}}}{\partial{x}}.

(24)

A.1
Derivative of amplitude spectral loss
E^{(amp)}_{t,n}

Given the amplitude spectral loss

\displaystyle E^{(amp)}_{t,n}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{A}_{t,n}-A_{t,n})^{2}.

(25)

According to the chain rule, we can compute the derivative

\frac{\partial{E^{(amp)}_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}=({A}_{t,n}-\hat{A}_{t,n})\cdot\frac{\partial{{A}_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}.

(26)

For
\frac{\partial{{A}_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}
, since we know that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{A_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{(\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})^{\frac{1}{2}}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}+\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\overline{\boldsymbol{W}}_{t,n})\boldsymbol{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\overline{\boldsymbol{W}}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot 2\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A_{t,n}}\cdot\mathcal{R}(Y_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H})

(31)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(\frac{Y_{t,n}}{A_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H})

(32)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(\exp({i\theta_{t,n}})\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H})

(33)

where,

and

Note that, from Eq. (

) to (

), because

\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{y}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}=(\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}+\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\overline{\boldsymbol{W}}_{t,n})\boldsymbol{y}.

From Eqs. (

) to (

), we use the fact that

Based on Equation

A.2
Derivative of phase spectrum loss
E^{(ph)}_{t,n}

Based on the definition of
E^{(ph)}_{t,n}
, we get

\displaystyle E^{(ph)}_{t,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|1-\exp(i(\hat{\theta}_{t,n}-\theta_{t,n}))\right|^{2}

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|1-\exp(i(\hat{\theta}_{t,n}))\frac{1}{\exp(i(\theta_{t,n}))}\right|^{2}

(38)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|1-\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}\right|^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}\right)\overline{\left(1-\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}\right)}

(40)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}}-\left(\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}+\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}}\right)\right)

(42)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+1-\left(\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}+\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}{\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}}\right)\right)

(43)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{{Y_{t,n}}}+\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}{\frac{{A_{t,n}}}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}}\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}Y+\hat{Y}\overline{Y}\right).

(45)

Note that
\hat{A}_{t,n}
,
{A}_{t,n}
and
\hat{Y}\overline{Y}+\overline{\hat{Y}}Y\in\mathbb{R}
while
\hat{Y}_{t,n}
and
{Y}_{t,n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Also note that
\overline{{Y}}_{t,n}{Y}_{t,n}={A}_{t,n}^{2}
and
\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\hat{Y}_{t,n}=\hat{A}_{t,n}^{2}
.
Thus, we can calculate
\frac{\partial{E^{(ph)}_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}
as follows.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{E^{(ph)}_{t,n}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial{\hat{A}_{t,n}^{-1}A_{t,n}^{-1}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}Y_{t,n}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\overline{Y}_{t,n}\right)+\frac{1}{\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}}\frac{\partial\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}Y_{t,n}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\overline{Y}_{t,n}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{y}}}\right)

(46)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}^{3}}\left(Y_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}+\overline{Y}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\right)\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}Y_{t,n}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\overline{Y}_{t,n}\right)+\frac{1}{\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}}\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)\right)

(47)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}^{3}}\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}Y_{t,n}^{2}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}A_{t,n}^{2}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}+\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}A_{t,n}^{2}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\overline{Y}_{t,n}^{2}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\right)+\frac{1}{\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}}\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)\right)

(48)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}}\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\frac{Y_{t,n}}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}+\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\frac{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}{Y_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\right)+\frac{1}{\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}}\left(\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}+\hat{Y}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)\right)

(49)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2\hat{A}_{t,n}A_{t,n}}\left(\hat{Y}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}-\hat{Y}_{t,n}\frac{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}{Y_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}+\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}-\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}\frac{Y_{t,n}}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)\right)

(50)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}{A_{t,n}}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}-\frac{1}{Y_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\right)+\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{Y_{t,n}}{A_{t,n}}\left(\frac{1}{Y_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}-\frac{1}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)\right)

(51)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{A_{t,n}}{Y_{t,n}}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}-\frac{1}{Y_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}\right)+\frac{\overline{\hat{Y}}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{A_{t,n}}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\left(\frac{1}{Y_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}-\frac{1}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)\right)

(52)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}\left(\frac{\hat{Y}_{t,n}}{\hat{A}_{t,n}}\frac{A_{t,n}}{Y_{t,n}}\left(\frac{1}{Y_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{\top}-\frac{1}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)\right)

(53)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}\left(\exp(i(\angle\hat{Y}_{t,n}-\angle Y_{t,n}))\right)\mathcal{I}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right)

(54)

\displaystyle=\sin(\angle\hat{Y}_{t,n}-\angle Y_{t,n})\mathcal{I}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{Y}_{t,n}}\boldsymbol{W}_{t,n}^{H}\right).

(55)

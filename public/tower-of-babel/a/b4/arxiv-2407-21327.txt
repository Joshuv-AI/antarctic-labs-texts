Introduction

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>2
,

the ring of Witt-vectors over
k
with
\varphi
its Frobenius automorphism,

its fraction field, and

the ring of power series over

in
d

be a “small base ring,” a ring that is
p
-adically completed étale over the Tate algebra,
W(k)\langle t_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,t_{d}^{\pm 1}\rangle
(i.e. the ring of restricted Laurent series over
W(k)
), for some

. Let

be an integer such that

When the base ring is
W(k)
, Fontaine and Laffaille introduced in
[

]
strongly divisible

-lattices, which we now call Fontaine-Laffaille modules, to study
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattices in crystalline representations of

Denote this category by
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p},[0,r]}^{\mathrm{cris}}(G_{K})
. We first recall the definition of a Fontaine-Laffaille module in the classical setting:

Definition 1
.

A finite free Fontaine-Laffaille module over
W(k)
is a triple
(M,\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M),\varphi_{i})
where:

•

M
is a finite free module over
W(k)

•

\{\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing filtration of
M
such that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i+1}(M)
is a direct summand of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)
for all
i
,
\mathrm{Fil}^{0}(M)=M
,
\mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}(M)=0.

•

\varphi_{i}
are
\varphi
-semilinear maps
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\to M
whose restriction to
\mathrm{Fil}^{i+1}(M)
is
p\varphi_{i+1}.

•

\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}M)=M.

Let
\mathrm{MF}^{\mathrm{ff},[0,r]}(W(k))
denote the category of finite free Fontaine-Laffaille modules over
W(k)
. These modules classify lattices within crystalline representations as in the following theorem:

Theorem 2

(
[
FL82
]
)
.

When

the functor given by

T_{\mathrm{cris}}(M)\coloneqq(\mathrm{Fil}^{r}(A_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{W(k)}M))^{\varphi_{r}=1}

is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{MF}^{\mathrm{ff},[0,r]}(W(k))\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p},[0,r]}^{\mathrm{cris}}(G_{K}).

The work of Faltings in
[
Fal89
]
extended this theory to more general base rings. Our goal is to extend the above theorem to the setting where the base ring is
R
and to the setting of a small base ring
T
.

Let us first recall the representation category in the relative case. Let
G_{R}
denote
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{R}[p^{-1}]/R[p^{-1}])=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}R\left[\frac{1}{p}\right],\eta)

is a fixed geometric point and

is the union of finite
R
-subalgebras

of a fixed algebraic closure of
\mathrm{Frac}(R)
such that

is étale over
R[p^{-1}].
Let
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p},[0,r]}^{\mathrm{cris}}(G_{R})
denote the category of crystalline

representations of

with Hodge-Tate weights in

and we make the analogous definitions over the base ring

.

Using recent results of Du, Liu, Moon, and Shimizu in
[
Du+24
]
and the category of finite, free Fontaine-Laffaille modules in the relative case first introduced by Faltings in
[
Fal89
]

]

9

Theorem 3
.

Let
r
be an integer satisfying
0\leq r\leq p-2
and let
\mathcal{T}
denote either the base ring
R
or
T
. The functor

T_{\mathrm{cris}}:\mathrm{MF}^{\mathrm{ff},[0,r]}_{\nabla}(\mathcal{T})\to\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p},[0,r]}^{\mathrm{cris}}(G_{\mathcal{T}})

given by

T_{\mathrm{cris}}(M)\coloneqq(\mathrm{Fil}^{r}(A_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{T})\otimes_{\mathcal{T}}M))^{\varphi_{r}=1}

is an equivalence of categories.

Showing this functor is fully faithful is routine, and the difficulty lies in showing essential surjectivity and specifically associating a Fontaine-Laffaille module to a given lattice inside a crystalline representation.

The key input of
[
Du+24
]
is the existence of a Kisin module
\mathfrak{M}
over the ring
\mathfrak{S}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}\llbracket u\rrbracket
associated to an object of
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p},[0,r]}^{\mathrm{cris}}(G_{\mathcal{T}})
. The ring
\mathfrak{S}
comes equipped with a Frobenius map
\varphi_{\mathfrak{S}}
extending the Frobenius on
\mathcal{T}
. We can then define the associated Fontaine-Laffaille module as
\mathfrak{M}/u\mathfrak{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{T},\varphi_{\mathfrak{S}}}\mathcal{T}
, but the Kisin module alone is not enough to obtain the full data of a Fontaine-Laffaille module over
\mathcal{T}
.

In the classical case, Breuil in
[
Bre02
]
studied
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
lattices in crystalline representations of

using strongly divisible
S
-modules. To obtain the full data of our Fontaine-Laffaille module, we define
S
in the relative setting to be the
p
-adic completion of the divided power envelope of
\mathfrak{S}
taken with respect to
(E(u))
with
E(u)=u-p
or
u+p
. The ring
S
comes with a canonical divided power filtration and additional structure which will descend to our proposed Fontaine-Laffaille module.

To show the main theorem holds over
R
, we will verify that the data properly descends through the use of two base change maps relating our
R
-module back to the classical theory. Let
k_{g}
be the perfection of
\mathrm{Frac}(R/pR)
and
R_{g}\coloneqq W(k_{g})
, which gives rise to a flat embedding
R\hookrightarrow R_{g}
. Let
\overline{b}:R\to W(k)
denote reduction moduo
(t_{1},\cdots,t_{d})
. A result of
[
LMP23
]
allows us to verify that

is an object of
\mathrm{MF}^{\mathrm{ff},[0,r]}_{\nabla}(R)
by checking that

and

are Fontaine-Laffaille modules over
W(k_{g})
and
W(k)
in the classical sense.

To show the main theorem holds over
T
, we obtain a result analogous to that of
[
LMP23
]
where we can check that

is Fontaine-Laffaille at the
p
-adic completion of localizations at maximal ideals. This enables us to use a base change

and reduce to the setting where the base ring is

.

Recently Würthen in
[
Wür23
]
proved this result in the setting of a smooth
p
-adic formal scheme over a mixed characteristic complete discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field. Our work uses different methods to focus on the case where the base ring is more limited, and it is routine to extend the results from a small base ring to accommodate the setting of a smooth,
p
-adic formal scheme.

The work of Imai, Kato, and Youcis in
[
IKY24
]
,developed simultaneously, uses different methods to obtain a different fully faithful functor between lattices inside crystalline representations and Fontaine-Laffaille modules in a relative setting. Their functor has the advantage of being extendable beyond the Fontaine-Laffaille range, and it yields objects in a nicer category. Our functor has the advantage of being essentially surjective. They also place additional assumptions on the prismatic
F
-crystal, and their technique has not yet been applied to the case where the base ring is a power series ring.

2.
Preliminary Results on some Categories and Functors in the Relative Setting

2.1.
Base Ring Conventions

Much of integral
p
-adic Hodge Theory has been established in the relative case in far more generality than Fontaine-Laffaille theory. We present the most general setting we will consider here.

Recall
k
is a perfect field of characteristic
p>2.
Let
K^{tr}
be a totally ramified extension of
K=\mathrm{Frac}(W(k))
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K^{tr}}
. Fix
\pi
a uniformizer of
K^{tr}
and let
E(u)\in W(k)[u]
denote the monic minimal polynomial of
\pi.
Write
W(k)\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1}\rangle
for the
p
-adic completion of the Laurent polynomial ring
W(k)\left[T_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1}\right]
.

Convention 4
.

In order to reference results with maximal generality, we will say
\mathcal{R}
is a
general base ring
if
\mathcal{R}
is a
p
-adically complete
\mathcal{O}_{K^{tr}}
-algebra which is of the form
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{O}_{K^{tr}}\otimes_{W(k)}\mathcal{R}_{0}
where
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is an integral domain obtained from
W(k)\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1}\rangle
by a finite number of iterations of the operations:

•

p
-adic completion of an étale extension

•

p
-adic completion of a localization;

•

completion with respect to an ideal containing
p
.

Our new results will hold for a subset of these general base rings which we describe next:

Convention 5
.

We will say a general base ring
T
is
small
if it is
p
-adically completed étale over
W(k)\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1}\rangle
for some
d
.

The key strategy for showing our results over small base rings will be to reduce to the case of the power series ring
R=W(k)\llbracket t_{1},\cdots,t_{d}\rrbracket
due to the local structure of small base rings described in the following lemma:

Lemma 6
.

Let
T
be a small base ring that is
p
-adically compaeted étale over
W(k)\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1}\rangle
and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal (note that
p\in\mathfrak{m}
). Then
\widehat{T_{\mathfrak{m}}}
, the
p
-adic completion of the localization of
T
at
\mathfrak{m}
, is isomorphic to
W(k)\llbracket t_{1},\cdots,t_{d}\rrbracket
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal of
T
, and let
\widehat{T_{\mathfrak{m}}}
denote the
p
-adic completion of the localization of
T
at
\mathfrak{m}.
The ring
T
is a regular Noetherian ring, and thus
\widehat{T_{\mathfrak{m}}}\cong W(k)\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket\cong R
by Cohen’s Structure Theorem.
∎

We will try to reserve the letter
R
in usual font for referring to such a power series ring, the letter
T
in usual font for a small base ring, and the stylized
\mathcal{R}
for a general base ring. We will use the stylized
\mathcal{T}
to concisely state results that hold for a small base ring or the power series ring
R
.

When we refer to the “classical” theory we will be referring to the setting where the base ring is
K
: the historically typical setting of
p
-adic Hodge Theory.

We will now describe the Frobenius structure and the category of Galois representations for a general base ring. We will let
\varphi_{\mathcal{R}}
denote the lift of Frobenius on
\mathcal{R}/W(k)
uniquely determined by
\varphi(T_{i})=T_{i}^{p}
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{R}}
denote the union of finite
\mathcal{R}
-subalgebras
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
of a fixed algebraic closure of
\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{R})
such that
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[p^{-1}]
is étale over
\mathcal{R}[p^{-1}]
. Set
G_{\mathcal{R}}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathcal{R}}/\mathcal{R}[p^{-1}])
. Let
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(G_{\mathcal{R}})
denote the category of finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
vector spaces with continuous
G_{\mathcal{R}}
action, and let
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{\mathrm{ff}}(G_{\mathcal{R}})
denote the full category of finite free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
modules equipped with a continuous action of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{R}}
.

Remark 7
.

This describes the Frobenius structure for a general base ring, but we will actually take
\varphi_{R}(t_{i})=(t_{i}+1)^{p}-1
for our base ring
R
in order to use the results of
[
LMP23
]
as stated. This is not significant, as we will show the relevant category of Fontaine-Laffaille modules defined below is independent of the choice of Frobenius.

2.2.
Important Base Change Maps

Our main technique for developing the Fontaine-Laffaille Theory for new base rings will be to base change to rings where the Fontaine-Laffaille Theory is already known. We describe the important base change maps here.

2.2.1.
Shilov Point Base Changes

Let
k_{g}
be the perfection of
\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{R}/p\mathcal{R})
and let
\mathcal{R}_{g}\coloneqq W(k_{g})
. We have a
\varphi
-compatiable, flat embedding
\mathcal{R}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}_{g}
. Set
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{R},g}\coloneqq W(k_{g})\llbracket u\rrbracket
, and let
S_{\mathcal{R},g}
be the
p
-adically completed divided power envelope of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{R},g}
with respect to
E(u)
which comes equipped with a
\varphi
structure and PD-filtration.

As the Shilov point above forgets the data of the connection
\nabla
, we will also need to consider a second Shilov point with imperfect residue field. Let
\mathcal{R}_{L}
denote the
p
-adic completion of the localization
\mathcal{R}_{(p)}
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{L}
is a complete discrete valuation ring with imperfect residue field. We define
\mathfrak{S}_{L}=\mathcal{R}_{L}\llbracket u\rrbracket
and
S_{L}
to be the
p
-adically completed divided power envelope of
\mathfrak{S}_{L}
with respect to
(E(u)).

2.2.2.
Closed Fiber Base Change of a Power Series Ring

In the case of our base ring
R
, we also have a natural projection
\pi:R\to W(k)
given by
t_{i}\mapsto 0
for every
i
. Showing that the Fontaine-Laffaille Theory holds for
R
will amount to checking it is compatible with the Shilov point base change and the closed fiber base change.

2.2.3.
Small Base Ring Base Changes

We will now discuss a base change map from a small base ring
T
to
R
suggested by Lemma
6
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(p,f_{1},\cdots,f_{d})
be a maximal ideal of
T
(recall
T
is regular), and let
\widehat{T_{\mathfrak{m}}}
denote the
p
-adic completion of the localization of
T
at
\mathfrak{m}
which is isomorphic to
\widehat{T_{\mathfrak{m}}}\cong W(k)\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket\cong R
by Lemma
6
.

This gives us a flat base change ring homomorphism
b_{\mathfrak{m}}:T\to\widehat{T_{\mathfrak{m}}}\cong R
. In order to make this map Frobenius equivariant, though, we must consider
\varphi_{R}
to be the lift of the Frobenius on
W(k)
which acts on the
X_{i}
as
\varphi_{R}(X_{1})=b_{\mathfrak{m}}(\varphi_{T}(f_{i}))
. For example, if
\mathfrak{m}=(p,t_{1}-1,\cdots,t_{d}-1)
, then we must take
\varphi_{R}(X_{i})=(X_{i}+1)^{p}-1.

By Lemma
12
below, there is a canonical equivalence between the categories of finite, free Fontaine-Laffaille modules over
R
with different Frobenii, so this choice will not impact our results.

2.8.

In this section we will work over

For

2.8.4.
The functor from Fontaine-Laffaille modules to Breuil modules

In the classical setting, it has already been established as the main result of

that there is a “direct” equivalence of categories between Fontaine-Laffaille modules and Breuil modules. We rely on this equivalence when reducing the relative setting to the classical setting. There is an equivalence of categories between Kisin modules and lattices inside of crystalline representations. If

[
FL82
]
.

In the relative setting there is an analogous “obvious” functor from Fontaine-Laffaille modules to Breuil modules.

Definition 31

admits a unique
\varphi
-compatible section

s:M\to\mathscr{M}.

Proof.

To attain this section we base change to the Shilov point obtaining the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M[\frac{1}{p}]\ }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}[\frac{1}{p}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{g}[\frac{1}{p}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}_{g}[\frac{1}{p}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s\otimes R_{g}}

Note that
s\otimes R_{g}
determines a section
M_{g}[\frac{1}{p}]\to\mathscr{M}_{g}[\frac{1}{p}]
. By
[
Gao19
, Prop 3.2.3]
this section is unique and it takes
M_{g}
, and thus
M
, into
\mathscr{M}_{g}
. Tracing
M
through the top of the diagram, we see that the image of
M
in
\mathscr{M}_{g}[\frac{1}{p}]
is contained in
\mathscr{M}[\frac{1}{p}].

From Lemma
22
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{g}\cap\mathscr{M}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathscr{M}\otimes\left(S_{g}\cap S\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{M}\otimes S.

We can verify
S_{g}\cap S\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]=S
term by term. If we take
x
in the intersection, we can write it as

x=\sum\frac{E(u)^{i}}{i!}\sum a_{ij}u^{j}

with
a_{ij}\in W(k_{g}).
We can also write it as

x=\sum\frac{E(u)^{i}}{i!}\sum b_{ij}u^{j}

with
b_{ij}\in W(k)[\frac{1}{p}].
Comparing term-by-term we can see that
a_{ij}=b_{ij}.

∎

Lemma 44
.

The map
1\otimes s:S\otimes_{R}M\to\mathscr{M}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
S\otimes_{R}M
and
\mathscr{M}
are finite, free
S
-modules of the same rank, it suffices to show that
1\otimes s
is surjective. Moreover,
S
is a local ring, so by Nakayama’s Lemma it suffices to show that
1\otimes s
is surjective after reducing modulo the maximal ideal of
S
which is the ideal generated by
p,t_{1},\cdots,t_{d}
,
u
, and the divided powers
\frac{E(u)^{i}}{i!}
. After reducing modulo the maximal ideal,
1\otimes s
becomes the identity map which is obviously surjective.
∎

We now define the filtration and
\varphi_{i}
structure on
M
.

Definition 45
.

Realizing
M
as a submodule of
\mathscr{M}
via the section
s
, we can define

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\coloneqq\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\cap M.

Definition 46
.

We set
\varphi_{M,i}=\varphi_{\mathscr{M},i}\big|_{M}
.

Note that the filtration on
\mathscr{M}
induces a filtration
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)
on
M
for which the composite
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\to\mathrm{Fil}^{i-1}(M)\xrightarrow{\varphi_{M,i-1}}M
is
p\varphi_{M,i}
. By the corresponding properties on
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}
(see Definition
25
), it is easy to see that
\mathrm{Fil}^{0}M=M
and
\mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}M=\{0\}.

Thus
M
has the structure of an object in
\mathrm{MF}_{\mathrm{big}}^{\mathrm{f}}(R)
. We rely on Proposition
11
to show that we have an object of
\mathrm{MF}^{\mathrm{ff}}(R)
in the following section.

3.2.
M\otimes_{R}R_{g}\cong M_{g}
as Fontaine-Laffaille modules

We need to show that the filtration data on the tensor product
M\otimes_{R}R_{g}
is compatible with the filtration on
M_{g}
. The difficulty in doing this is establishing a base change theorem for the Breuil module
\mathscr{M}
. The ring
S_{g}
is very large, so treating
\mathscr{M}
as an
R
-module and tensoring with
R_{g}
over
R
will not get us back
\mathscr{M}_{g}
: we would need to
p
-adically complete
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}.
The fact
S
is non-Noetherian makes
p
-adic completion difficult to work with.

Instead, we can work over the ring
S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)
which is Noetherian when
i<p
. This will allow us to decompose
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
and show that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})
is contained in the nicer piece.

Lemma 47
.

Let
i
be a nonnegative integer less than
p
. Then we can decompose
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
as follows:

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M}_{g})=\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}+\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g}).

Proof.

Another way to express the definition of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}
is as a kernel in the exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{M}\to\left(S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\right)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}.

It follows quickly from the definition of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}
that
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)
is contained in the kernel, so we get an exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}/\left(\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\right)\to\mathscr{M}/\left(\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\right)\to\left(S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\right)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}.

We can now tensor with
R_{g}
which maintains the exactness since
R_{g}
is a flat
R
-module. As a further conesequence of the flatness of
R_{g}
, the tensor product commutes with quotients and we obtain the exact sequence:

0\to\frac{\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}}{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\otimes_{R}R_{g})}\to\frac{\mathscr{M}}{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S))}\otimes_{R}R_{g}\to\left(\left(S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\right)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}\right)\otimes_{R}R_{g}

We also have on the Shilov point the exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}_{g}\to\mathscr{M}_{g}\to(S_{g}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{g}}\mathfrak{M}_{g}

where the rightmost map restricts to the rightmost map of exact sequence
(1)
via the embedding
\mathscr{M}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}_{g}
. We can also see that
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}
is in the kernel, so this gives an exact sequence

0\to\frac{\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}_{g}}{\left(\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}\right)}\to\frac{\mathscr{M}_{g}}{\left(\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}\right)}\to(S_{g}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{g}}\mathfrak{M}_{g}.

Now note that

\frac{\mathscr{M}_{g}}{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}}\cong\frac{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}S_{g}}{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}}\cong\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\frac{S_{g}}{\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}}

since
\mathscr{M}
is a finite, free
S
-module. Also,

\frac{\mathscr{M}}{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S}\otimes_{R}R_{g}\cong\mathscr{M}\otimes\frac{S}{\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)}\otimes_{R}R_{g}.

Since
S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S
is a finite, free
R
-module and
S_{g}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}
is a finite, free-
R_{g}
module of the same rank, the rightmost maps of
(2)
and
(3)
are the same with the same domain. Thus the kernels must be the same.

Since
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\otimes_{R}R_{g}\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g})
, we obtain that an element in
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}_{g}
is determined by an element of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}
up to an element in
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}
, completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

We now want to use this decomposition to show that elements of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})
cannot arise containing any
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g})\otimes_{S}\mathscr{M}
components. Formally, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 48
.

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})
, which we know to be a subset of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M}_{g}).
This means by Lemma
47
we can write
x=y+z
with
y\in\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g})\otimes_{S}\mathscr{M}
and
z\in\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}
. Since
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})\subset M_{g}\cong M\otimes_{R}R_{g}
, we have that
y=z-x\in\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}.
We claim
y\in(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\otimes_{S}\mathscr{M})\otimes_{R}R_{g}\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}.

Let
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
be an
S
-basis of
\mathscr{M}.
Since
y\in\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g})\otimes_{S}\mathscr{M}
, we can write

y=\sum_{m=1}^{n}s_{m}e_{m}

with
s_{m}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g}).
Since
y\in\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}
, we can write

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k,m,j}\frac{E(u)^{k}}{k!}\right)e_{m}\right]\otimes r_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left((a_{k,m,j}\otimes r_{j})\frac{E(u)^{k}}{k!}\right)\right]e_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(a_{k,m,j}\otimes r_{j})\right)\frac{E(u)^{k}}{k!}\right]e_{m}

with
a_{k,m,j}\in R[u]
and
r_{j}\in R_{g}.
Thus,

s_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(a_{k,m,j}\otimes r_{j})\right)\frac{E(u)^{k}}{k!}.

Then since
s_{m}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g})
, we must have

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(a_{k,m,j}\otimes r_{j})\right)=0

for
k<i,
and then it is clear that
y\in(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\otimes_{S}\mathscr{M})\otimes_{R}R_{g}\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}
and the lemma is proven.
∎

Theorem 49
.

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})\cong\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{g}.

Proof.

We know that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})\subset M\otimes_{R}R_{g}\cong M_{g}
and, by Lemma
48
,
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}.
By Lemma
22
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g})

\displaystyle\subset\left(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}\right)\cap\left(M\otimes_{R}R_{g}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\cap M\right)\otimes_{R}R_{g}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{g}.

The inclusion in the other direction is easy to establish. Clearly
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{g}
is inside
M_{g}
and
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M})\otimes_{R}R_{g}
since
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)
is a subset of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M})
by definition,
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\subset M
and
R_{g}
is a flat
R
-module. Thus we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{g}\subset(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M})\otimes_{R}R_{g})\cap M_{g}.

But
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M})\otimes_{R}R_{g}
as a subset of
\mathscr{M}_{g}
is in the kernel of
1\otimes\varphi:\mathscr{M}_{g}\to S_{g}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{g}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{g}}\mathfrak{M}_{g}
, so we have

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{g}\subset(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M}_{g}))\cap M_{g}=\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g}).

∎

Now that we have shown the compatibility of the filtrations,
M\otimes_{R}R_{g}
has the full Fontaine-Laffaille module structure of
M_{g}
. We thus conclude:

Theorem 50
.

M\otimes_{R}R_{g}
is a Fontaine-Laffaille module over
R_{g}
, and
M\otimes_{R}R_{g}\cong M_{g}
as Fontaine-Laffaille modules.

Proof.

We established in Theorem
23
an isomorphism
M\otimes_{R}R_{g}\cong M_{g}
compatible with Frobenius. We established in Theorem
49
that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{g}\cong\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(M_{g}).
Since
M_{g}
carries the structure of a Fontaine-Laffaile module, we have completed the proof of this theorem.
∎

3.3.
M\otimes_{R}W(k)\cong M_{0}
as Fontaine-Laffaille Modules

The strategy in this section is similar to the strategy in the above section where we analyze the Breuil module filtration to understand the Fontaine-Laffaille module filtration, but we have to deal with the additional concern that
W(k)
is not a flat
R
-module, which means analyzing various terms of the form
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(-,W(k))
. These Tor terms aren’t too concerning because the barrier to
W(k)
being a flat
R
-module is, effectively,
t_{i}
-torsion, which does not show up in most of our modules.

Lemma 51
.

We have (for
i<p
):

(a)
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(S/R,W(k))=0

(b)
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(\mathscr{M}/M,W(k))=0

(c)
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M},W(k))=0

(d)
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(\mathscr{M}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M}),W(k))=0

Proof.

For (a), we compute
\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{1}(S/R,W(k))=0
by recognizing that

0\rightarrow R\rightarrow S\rightarrow S/R\rightarrow 0

is a free resolution of
S/R
since
S
is a free
R
-module. When tensoring with
W(k)
, it is clear that the first map remains injective, so indeed
\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{1}(S/R,W(k))=0
.

For (b), by Lemma
44
, we have that
\mathscr{M}/M
is a direct sum of finitely many copies of
S/R
, so
\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{1}(\mathscr{M}/M,W(k))=0
by part (a).

For part (c), we observe that
S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S
is (for
i<p
) a free
R
-module and hence a free
W(k)
-module. We also know
\mathfrak{M}
is a free
\mathfrak{S}
-module, giving us our conclusion.

For Part (d), we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}\rightarrow S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}.

Below in Lemma
52
, we justify that the cokernel of this map

\mathscr{M}\to S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}
is a finite free
R
-module, hence a free
W(k)
-module.

Tensoring the induced sequence

0\to\mathscr{M}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M})\to S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}

with
W(k)
then applying Lemma
52
and part (c) gives us the result.

∎

The following lemma completes the proof of (c):

Lemma 52
.

Let

be the map induced by
1\otimes\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}}:\mathscr{M}=S\otimes_{\varphi,\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}\to S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}.
Then
\mathrm{coker}(f)
is a finite, free
R
-module.

Proof.

Write
\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}
for
\mathfrak{S}\otimes_{\varphi,\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}
. We have an exact sequence

0\to\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{M}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to 0

Tensoring with
S
, we obtain

S\otimes_{\varphi,\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}\to S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}\to S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to 0

where the first map is
1\otimes\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}}.
Then we obtain the following diagram projecting to the
S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S)
level:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\otimes_{\varphi,\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\otimes\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(*)}

from which we conclude that

is surjective and that
\mathrm{coker}(f)=S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}.
By Lemma

and the proof of Lemma

(c) which justifies that
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S,W(k))=0
and allows us to commute the base change with the quotient,
\mathrm{coker}(f)\otimes_{R}W(k)\cong S_{0}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{0}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{0}}\mathfrak{M}_{0}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{0}.

By tensoring
0\to\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{0}\to\mathfrak{M}_{0}\to\mathfrak{M}_{0}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to 0
with

, we find that

is the cokernel of
f_{0}:\mathscr{M}_{0}\to S_{0}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{0}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}
induced by

. If

, by

GLS14
, Theorem 4.20]
we know that

and

where

are the Hodge-Tate weights from which it is clear that
S_{0}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{0}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{0}}\mathfrak{M}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}=\mathrm{coker}(f_{0})
is a finite free
W(k)
-module of rank, say,
m
.

Let
e_{1},\cdots,e_{m}
be a basis of
\mathrm{coker}(f_{0})
as a
W(k)
-module. By Nakayama’s Lemma, we can lift it to
\widetilde{e_{1}},\cdots,\widetilde{e_{m}}
which generate
\mathrm{coker}(f)
as an
R
-module. Analogously, we can see that
\mathrm{coker}(f)\otimes_{R}R_{g}\cong S_{g}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(S_{g})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{g}}\mathfrak{M}_{g}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{g}
which, by the same reasoning replacing

with

, is a free

-module of rank

. The rank is the same since

arises from a crystalline representation. A crystalline representation in the relative case is Hodge-Tate and will have the same Hodge-Tate weights on all of its fibers, so the decomposition of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{0}
will use the same Hodge-Tate weights
h_{i}
as the analogous decomposition of
\mathfrak{M}_{g}/\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}_{g}.
Then the images of
\widetilde{e_{1}},\cdots,\widetilde{e_{m}}
in

must generate

. Therefore they are linearly independent over

is a finite, free

-module.
∎

On the Shilov point we had to be concerned that

is very large: it is not true that
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}R_{g}=\mathscr{M}_{g}
, we would need an additional

-adic completion which does not behave nicely over non-Noetherian rings. On the closed fiber we have a little more freedom since

is smaller than

and this concern is not present on the closed fiber. We can thus obtain a stronger version of Lemma

more easily:

Lemma 53
.

We have

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}(\mathscr{M}_{0})=\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}W(k).

Proof.

We have the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\rightarrow\mathscr{M}\xrightarrow{f}(S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathrm{coker}(f)\to 0

from the definition of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}
, and let

be the labelled map. Factoring through the kernel, we obtain the short exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{M}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\to(S/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathrm{coker}(f)\to 0.

We now base change to
W(k)
, noting that
\mathrm{coker}(f)
is a finite, free
R
-module by Lemma

and the tensor produdct commutes with the quotients by Lemma

. We thus obtain the exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{M}_{0}/(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}W(k))\to(S_{0}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{0})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{0}}\mathfrak{M}_{0}\to\mathrm{coker}(f)\otimes_{R}W(k)\to 0.

As
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}_{0}
is exactly the kernel of
\mathscr{M}_{0}\to(S_{0}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i}S_{0})\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{0}}\mathfrak{M}_{0}
inside of
\mathscr{M}_{0}
and
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{R}W(k)
is contained in this kernel, the proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

Now that we can reduce the Breuil module filtration to the classical case, we will introduce a second filtration on
M
which behaves a little more nicely under base change. In the classical case, the two filtrations agree, which is what we will show in our power series ring setting.

Definition 54
.

Let

f_{p}:\mathscr{M}\to M

be the map determined by
u\mapsto p
. Note this map sends
\frac{E(u)^{i}}{i!}
to
0
. Define
\mathrm{F}^{i}M
to be the image of
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}
under
f_{p}
.

Lemma 55
.

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}M=\mathrm{F}^{i}M

Proof.

We first show that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}M\subset\mathrm{F}^{i}M
. Recall that we set
\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{M}[p^{-1}]
and
D=M[p^{-1}]
upon which we can utilize the rational theory. Set
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}D\coloneqq\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{D}\cap D.
We have the exact sequence

0\to\frac{\mathrm{Fil}^{i-1}\mathscr{D}}{(\mathrm{Fil}^{p}S)\mathscr{D}}\xrightarrow{\cdot E}\frac{\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{D}}{(\mathrm{Fil}^{p}S)\mathscr{D}}\xrightarrow{f_{p}}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}D\to 0.

The kernel of
f_{p}:\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{D}\to\mathrm{Fil}^{i}D
is
(\mathrm{Fil}^{1}S)\mathrm{Fil}^{i-1}\mathscr{D}
by
[
Du+24
, Lemma 4.31]
, and removing the
\mathrm{Fil}^{p}S
part ensures that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i-1}\mathscr{D}
will give the entire kernel. Recalling from the rational theory that

\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}=\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{D}\cap\mathscr{M},

we obtain the exact sequence

(1)

\displaystyle 0\to\frac{\mathrm{Fil}^{i-1}\mathscr{M}}{(\mathrm{Fil}^{p}S)\mathscr{M}}\to\frac{\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathscr{M}}{(\mathrm{Fil}^{p}S)\mathscr{M}}\xrightarrow{f_{p}}\mathrm{F}^{i}M\to 0.

the base change in the first two terms is as written due to Theorem

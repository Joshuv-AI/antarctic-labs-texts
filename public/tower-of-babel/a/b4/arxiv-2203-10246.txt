[

,
1
,
13
,
14
,

]
). But for subnormal operators, it does not hold. Asymmetric version of Fuglede - Putnam theorem for bounded subnormal operators,
M
-hyponormal operators and dominant operators are studied by Furuta (
[

,
9
]
) Moore (
[

]
) and Stampfli (
[
23
]
) respectively. The study of unbounded operators is one of the major research area in the field of operator theory. In most of the cases operators involved in quantum mechanics, differential equations are unbounded.

Let
\mathcal{D}(T),~ker(T),~ran(T)
and
\mathcal{G}(T)
denotes the domain, nullspace, range and graph of
T
respectively. Let

be the polar decomposition of

, where

.

in to the closed subspace

An operator

For example,
T:l^{2}(\mathbb{N})\longrightarrow l^{2}(\mathbb{N})

be defined by

the following inclusion hold:

hyponormal\subset M-hyponormal.

A densely defined operator

M
-hyponormal if

\mathcal{D}(T)\subset\mathcal{D}(T^{*})

2.
Fuglede-Putnam Theorem

A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
invariant
under
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})

if for any
x\in\mathcal{D}(T)\cap\mathcal{M}
, then
Tx\in\mathcal{M}
. ie., if
\mathcal{M}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, we define
T|_{\mathcal{M}}
is an operator on
\mathcal{M}
with domain

\mathcal{D}(T|_{\mathcal{M}})=\{x\in\mathcal{D}(T)\cap\mathcal{M}:Tx\in\mathcal{M}\}~~\mbox{and}~~T|_{\mathcal{M}}~~x=Tx,~~x\in\mathcal{D}(T|_{\mathcal{M}}).

If
\mathcal{M}
reduce
T
to an operator
B,
then
B=T|_{\mathcal{M}}
. Throughout the paper we present known result as proposition. In the following result we show that part of closed densely defined
M
- hyponormal operator is again
M
-hyponormal.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})
be a densely defined
M
-hyponormal operator and let
\mathcal{M}
be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
which is invariant under T.
Then
T|_{\mathcal{M}}
is a closed
M
-hyponormal operator.

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{D}(T|_{\mathcal{M}})=\mathcal{D}(T)\cap\mathcal{M}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{D}(T|_{\mathcal{M}})
and
P
be an orthogonal projection on to
\mathcal{M}
. Then

\displaystyle\parallel(T|_{\mathcal{M}}-\lambda I)^{*}x\parallel

\displaystyle=\parallel P(T-\lambda I)^{*}x\parallel

\displaystyle\leq M\parallel(T-\lambda I)x\parallel

\displaystyle=M\parallel(T|_{\mathcal{M}}-\lambda I)x\parallel.

Hence
T|_{\mathcal{M}}
is a closed
M
-hyponormal operator.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})
be a densely defined
M
-hyponormal operator. Then there exist a contraction

such that
\dfrac{1}{M}(T-\lambda)\subseteq(T-\lambda)^{*}C_{\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

Proof.

Define
K:ran(T-\lambda)\rightarrow ran(T^{*}-\overline{\lambda})
by

\displaystyle K\left((T-\lambda)x\right)=\dfrac{1}{M}(T^{*}-\overline{\lambda})x,~~\mbox{for all}~~~x\in\mathcal{D}(T).

Since
T
is
M
-hyponormal,
K
is a contraction with
K(T-\lambda)\subseteq\dfrac{1}{M}(T^{*}-\overline{\lambda}).
Now we extend
K
to

such that

K^{\prime}(T-\lambda)\subseteq\dfrac{1}{M}(T^{*}-\overline{\lambda})
. Then the contraction

defined by
Ax=0~~
for all
x\in{\overline{ran(T-\lambda)}}^{\perp}
is an extension of

. Hence

A(T-\lambda)\subseteq\dfrac{1}{M}(T^{*}-\overline{\lambda}).

Therefore, we get

\dfrac{1}{M}(T-\lambda)\subseteq(T-\lambda)^{*}A^{*}.

If we put
A^{*}=C_{\lambda},
then
\dfrac{1}{M}(T-\lambda)\subseteq(T-\lambda)^{*}C_{\lambda}.

∎

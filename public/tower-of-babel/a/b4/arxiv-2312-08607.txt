Parabolic Anderson model with rough initial condition: continuity in law of the solution
Xiao Liang

Abstract
The parabolic Anderson model (PAM) is one of the most interesting and challenging SPDEs related to various physical phenomena, and can be described mathematically as a stochastic heat equation driven by linear multiplicative noise. In this paper, we consider PAM with initial condition given by a signed Borel measure on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . The forcing term under investigation is examined in two cases: (i) the regular noise, with the spatial covariance given by the Riesz kernel of order α ∈ ( 0 , d ) \alpha\in(0,d) in spatial dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 ; (ii) the rough noise, which is a fractional noise in space with Hurst index H < 1 / 2 H<1/2 and d = 1 d=1 . In both cases, the noise is assumed to be colored in time and we consider a general initial condition. The objective of this article is to show that the solution is continuous in law with respect to the spatial noise parameter. The similar problem for the constant initial condition has been recently studied in [ 6 ] .

Introduction and Main Results

In this article, we consider the following stochastic heat equation:

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)+u(t,x)\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\[8.00003pt]
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot),\end{cases}

(1)

with initial condition given by a signed Borel measure
u_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-a|x|^{2}}|u_{0}|(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty,\text{ for all }a>0.

(2)

This general initial condition, known as “rough initial condition”, was introduced in
[
7
]
, and has been considered in the analysis of the stochastic heat equation in several other papers; see e.g.
[
3
,
8
,
9
]
. Here
|u_{0}|:=u_{0,+}+u_{0,-}
, where
u_{0}=u_{0,+}-u_{0,-}
is the Jordan decomposition and
u_{0,\pm}
are two non-negative Borel measure with disjoint support.

We assume that
W
is a space-time homogeneous Gaussian noise. More precisely,
W=\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
is a zero-mean Gaussian process, defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, with the following covariance structure:

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\cdot)(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(s,\cdot)(\xi)}\gamma_{0}(t-s)dtds\mu(d\xi)=:\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

(3)

where
\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\cdot)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\varphi(t,x)dx
is the Fourier transform of the function
\varphi(t,\cdot)
,
\xi\cdot x
is the Euclidean product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\gamma_{0}
is a non-negative and non-negative definite, i.e. for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi\ast\widetilde{\phi})(t)\gamma_{0}(t)dt\geq 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}
the Hilbert space defined as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. In this setting, the noise
W
can be extended to an isonormal Gaussian process
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{H}\}
, and the solution can be defined using Malliavin calculus.

By the Bochner-Schwartz theorem, there exists a tempered measure
\mu_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma_{0}
is the Fourier transform of
\mu_{0}
in the space
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi(t)\phi(s)\gamma_{0}(t-s)dtds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\tau)|^{2}\mu_{0}(d\tau),

(4)

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of
\mathbb{C}
-valued rapidly decreasing
C^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{F}\phi
is the Fourier transform of
\phi
, given by
\mathcal{F}\phi(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau t}\phi(t)dt
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. We assume that
there exists a function
g_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty]
such that

\mu_{0}(d\tau)=g_{0}(\tau)d\tau.

(5)

We will be interested in the temporal covariance function

\gamma_{0}(t)=\alpha_{H_{0}}|t|^{2H_{0}-2}

(6)

with
H_{0}\in(1/2,1)
, in which case
g_{0}(\tau)=c_{H_{0}}|\tau|^{1-2H_{0}}
, with
\alpha_{H_{0}}=H_{0}(2H_{0}-1)
and

c_{H_{0}}=\frac{\Gamma(2H_{0}+1)\sin(\pi H_{0})}{2\pi}.

(7)

In this case,
\gamma_{0}
, we say that the noise
W
is fractional in time with index
H_{0}>1/2
.

Similarly, there exists a tempered measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\gamma
is the Fourier transform of
\mu
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e. for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\gamma(x-y)dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

(8)

The spatial correlation pattern of the noise is defined by this spatial spectral measure
\mu
. Typically, this measure is assumed to be tempered, non-negative, and non-negative definite. We will consider two cases:

\mu(d\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi&\mbox{with $\alpha\in(0,d)$ and $d\geq 1$ ({Case I: the regular case})}\\
c_{H}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi&\mbox{with $H\in(0,1/2)$ and $d=1$ ({Case II: the rough case})}\end{array}\right.

In Case II, the constant
c_{H}
is defined in (
7
).

We are now ready to give the definition of the solution to equation (
1

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a process
u=\{u(t,x),(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is a
(mild Skorohod) solution
of equation (
1
) if for any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
u(t,x)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable,
\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{2}<\infty
and solves the following equation:

u(t,x)=w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)u(s,y)W(\delta s,\delta y),

where the stochastic integral is interpreted in the Skorohod sense and
w(t,x)
is the solution of the homogeneous heat equation with initial data
u_{0}
:

w(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t}(x-y)u_{0}(\mathrm{d}y).

(9)

Note that if
u_{0}\geq 0
, then (
2
) is the necessary and sufficient condition for
w(t,x)
to be finite. By Lemma B.2 of
[
3
]
, we know that
w(t,x)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence
w(t,x)
is uniformly bounded in any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We recall that
G_{t}(x)
is the fundamental solution of the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}\right),

and the Fourier transform of
G_{t}
in any dimension
d
is given by
\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}/2}
. Moreover, we note that
|w(t,x)|\leq w_{+}(t,x)
, where

w_{+}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t}(x-y)|u_{0}|(\mathrm{d}y),

(10)

and it can be shown that condition (
2
) is equivalent to

w_{+}(t,x)<\infty\quad\text{ for all }t>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The solution to equation (
1
) is understood in
Skorohod
sense. We recall that
\delta:\text{Dom}(\delta)\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is the divergence operator with respect to
W
which is defined as the adjoint of the Malliavin derivative
D
with respect to
W
. If
u\in\text{Dom}(\delta)
, we use the notation

\delta(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)W(\delta t,\delta x)

and we say
\delta(u)
is the
Skorohod integral
of
u
with respect to
W
. Further elaboration on the subject of Malliavin calculus is available in
[
13
]
.

Using the methodology introduced in
[
11
]
, we know that if it exists, the solution of equation (
1
) has the series expansion:

u(t,x)=w(t,x)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}\big(f_{t,x,n}\big)\quad\mbox{in}\quad L^{2}(\Omega),

(11)

the terms of these series being orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Here
I_{n}
is the multiple Wiener integral of order
n
with respect to
W
, and
f_{t,x,n}
is given by: for all
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t
,

\displaystyle f_{t,x,n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n})

\displaystyle=G_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})w(t_{1},x_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\cdots G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})G_{t_{1}}(x_{1}-x_{0})u_{0}(\mathrm{d}x_{0})

where the second expression of this kernel is obtained by (
9
). By the orthogonality of the terms in this series, the necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of solution is

\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}|I_{n}\big(f_{t,x,n}\big)|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\,\|\widetilde{f}_{t,x,n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty.

where

\widetilde{f}
is the symmetrization of the function
f
, defined by:

\widetilde{f}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}f(t_{\rho(1)},x_{\rho(1)},\ldots,t_{\rho(n)},x_{\rho(n)}),

and
S_{n}
is the set of permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

In what follows, we will need the following result which gives the explicit expression of the Fourier transform of the kernel
f_{t,x,n}
in the space variables.

Lemma 1.2

(Lemma 2.5 of
[
3
]
)
.

For any
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t=t_{n+1}
and for any
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}f_{t,x,n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\exp\bigg\{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}\xi_{j}\Big|^{2}\bigg\}\exp\bigg\{-\frac{i}{t}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\xi_{j}\Big)\cdot x\bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\Big\{-i\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{t_{j}}{t}\Big)\xi_{j}\Big]\cdot x_{0}\Big\}G_{t}(x-x_{0})u_{0}(\mathrm{d}x_{0}).

In Case I with
\gamma_{0}
arbitrary, a sufficient condition for the existence of the solution the heat equation (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

which is equivalent to

(12)

(see

Remark 1.5
.

We note that in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
, the convergence is only on compact sets of the form

and

The proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
follow the same strategy. We first prove the convergence of the corresponding finite-dimensional distributions and tightness. In both cases, we need to guarantee that all noise processes are defined on the same probability space, for all parameter values
\alpha
, or
H
. To construct all noise processes
(W^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in(0,d)}

(W^{H})_{H\in(0,1/2)}

\mu_{0}
has density (see (
5

[
6
]
for this construction of noise, based on a complex-valued Gaussian white noise. To prove tightness, we apply Kolmogorov-Centsov theorem. For this, we derive some moment estimates for the increments of the solution, ensuring that all bounds remain uniformly bounded for parameter values (
\alpha
or
H
) in a compact set.

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{s_{k}})\bigg|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}/2}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha^{*}/2}\bigg|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}

\Gamma_{0,t}=\int_{-t}^{t}\gamma_{0}(s)ds
. It suffices to show that the integral appearing in the upper bound above converges to
0
as

, i.e.

T_{n}:=\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\big|\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\big|^{2}\left|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}/2}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha^{*}/2}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\to 0.

Note that the integrand converges pointwise to
0
on
T_{k}(t)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}
, as
n\to\infty
.
To apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem, it suffices to find an integrable bound for the term depending on
\alpha_{n}
:

g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}):=\big|\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}},

and we need to find a function
g
on
T_{k}(t)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}
, such that

g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})\leq g(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})\quad\mbox{for all $n$},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}g(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}<\infty.

Recall that
\max\{d-2,0\}<\alpha^{\ast}<d
. Fix numbers
a
and
b
such that
\max\{d-2,0\}<a<\alpha^{\ast}<b<d
. Since
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha^{\ast}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

a\leq\alpha_{n}\leq b
for all
n\geq N
. A natural candidate for

is

\displaystyle g(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})

\displaystyle:=\big|\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(|\xi_{j}|^{-b}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|\leq 1\}}+|\xi_{j}|^{-a}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|>1\}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq w_{+}^{2}(t,x)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\exp\bigg\{-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{j}t_{i}\xi_{i}\Big|^{2}\bigg\}\Big(|\xi_{j}|^{-b}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|\leq 1\}}+|\xi_{j}|^{-a}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|>1\}}\Big).

(24)

We fix
j=1,\ldots,k
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\big|\sum_{i=1}^{j}t_{i}\xi_{i}\big|^{2}}\Big(|\xi_{j}|^{-b}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|\leq 1\}}+|\xi_{j}|^{-a}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|>1\}}\Big)\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{|\xi_{j}|\leq 1}|\xi_{j}|^{-b}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}+\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\big|t_{j}\xi_{j}+\eta\big|^{2}}|\xi_{j}|^{-a}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{c_{d}}{d-b}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}|t_{j}\xi_{j}|^{2}}|\xi_{j}|^{-a}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}=\frac{c_{d}}{d-b}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\xi_{j}^{2}}|\xi_{j}|^{-a}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{c_{d}}{d-b}+\bigg(\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\bigg)^{-\frac{d-a}{2}}K_{d,a}=:C\Big[1+\bigg(\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\bigg)^{-\frac{d-a}{2}}\Big]

(25)

where
c_{d}
given in (
16
) and
K_{d,a}
are given in (
19
). Observe that
for any
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{k}<t_{k+1}=t
, we have
\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\leq t_{j+1}-t_{j}\leq t
. Therefore we have
\Big(\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\Big)^{-\frac{d-a}{2}}\geq t^{-\frac{d-a}{2}},

and multiplying by
t^{\frac{d-a}{2}}
on both sides, we obtain:

1\leq t^{\frac{d-a}{2}}\Big(\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\Big)^{-\frac{d-a}{2}}

which implies

\displaystyle 1+\Big(\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\Big)^{-\frac{d-a}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\Big)^{-\frac{d-a}{2}}\Big(t^{\frac{d-a}{2}}+1\Big).

(26)

Using relations (
24
), (
25
) and (
26
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}g(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq w^{2}(t,x)C^{k}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\Big)^{-\frac{d-a}{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{2-(d-a)}{2}\big)^{n+1}}{\Gamma\Big((n+1)(\frac{2-(d-a)}{2})\Big)}t^{n(\frac{2-(d-a)}{2})-\frac{d-a}{2}}

where for the last line, we used Lemma
2.3
.

Theorem 2.5

(Theorem 1.3 of
[
3
]
)
.

Let

be the solution of equation (

that satisfying (

Then for any

\displaystyle\|u^{\alpha}(t,x)-u^{\alpha}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\|_{p}\leq C\big(|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{1-\beta}\big),

(27)

Consequently, for any

(28)

where the last equation is obtained using the change of variables

Note that

such that
\max\{d-2,0\}<a<b<d
.

The following result will be used several times in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 3.5 of
[
3
]
)
.

For any

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\exp\bigg\{-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}{t_{k+1}t_{k}}\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}\xi_{j}\bigg|^{2}\bigg)\bigg\}\prod_{k=1}^{n}|\xi_{k}|^{-\alpha}d\xi_{k}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}k_{\alpha}\bigg(\frac{2(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\bigg).

By (

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}k_{\alpha}\bigg(\frac{2(t_{j+1}-t_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=C^{(1)}_{d,\alpha}\Big(\frac{2(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}}{t_{2}}\Big)^{-(d-\alpha)/2}\ldots C^{(1)}_{d,\alpha}\Big(\frac{2(t-t_{n})t_{n}}{t}\Big)^{-(d-\alpha)/2}

(29)

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that

Proof.

We proceed with a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem 1.3 of

Step 1 (time increments).

\displaystyle\Big|\mathcal{F}\big(f_{t+h,x,n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\cdot)-f_{t,x,n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\cdot)\big)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}})\Big|\leq K_{1}+K_{2}

Relation (

) follows.

Step 2. (tightness)
The fact that the sequence
(u^{\alpha_{n}})_{n\geq 1}
is tight in
C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
follows by Proposition 2.3 of
[
14
]
, using Theorem
2.7
above.

3
Rough noise

In this section, we consider equation (
1
) driven by a Gaussian noise
W
where the temporal covariance function
\gamma_{0}
is given by (
6
), and the measure
\mu
is given by (
14
). The existence of the solution was proved in
[
4
]
.

To emphasize the dependence on the parameter
H
, we denote the noise, the Hilbert space, and the solution, by
W^{H},\mathcal{H}^{H},u^{H}
, respectively. Denoting the multiple integral of order
n
with respect to
W^{H}
as
I_{n}^{H}
, the series expansion (
11
) takes the following form:

u^{H}(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}^{H}(f_{t,x,n}).

Similarly to Section 4 of
[
6
]
, all Gaussian processes
\{W^{H}\}_{H\in(0,1/2)}
can be constructed on the same probability space. Let
\widehat{W}
be the
\mathbb{C}
-valued Gaussian measure given by (
17
).
We recall the following representation of the multiple integral with respect to the process
W^{H}
: for any
t\mapsto\varphi(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

I_{k}^{H}(\varphi)=c_{H_{0}}^{k/2}c_{H}^{k/2}\int_{(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})^{k}}\mathcal{F}_{t}[\mathcal{F}_{x}\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\bullet)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})](\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}})\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\tau_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{0}}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\widehat{W}(d\tau_{1},d\xi_{1})\ldots\widehat{W}(d\tau_{k},d\xi_{k}).

(44)

We will use the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 3.1 of
[
5
]
)
.

Let
G
be the fundamental solution of the heat equation, the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}\xi
is finite if and only if
\alpha>-1
and in this case,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}\xi=\Gamma\Big(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\Big)\ t^{-(1+\alpha)/2}.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma A.1 in
[
4
]
)
.

Suppose that
\alpha_{i}>-1
,
\beta_{i}>-1
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
, and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})+k+1+\alpha_{k+1}>0\,\text{ for all }k=1,\ldots,n-1.

(45)

Then by setting
t_{n+1}=t
,
|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}
and
|\beta|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}
, we have that

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}):=\int_{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}(t_{i+1}-t_{i})^{\beta_{i}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(\beta_{i}+1)}{\Gamma\big(|\alpha|+|\beta|+n+1\big)}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})+k+1+\alpha_{k+1}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})+k+1\big)}t^{|\alpha|+|\beta|+n}.

We will use a similar approximation technique as in Section
2
. More precisely, we study first the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-continuity in
H
of the multiple integral of
I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})
for fixed
k
. This is achieved by the following lemma. Note that the proof is the same as in the proof of Lemma 4.2 of
[
6
]
, the difference being that here we use a different expression for the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}f_{t,x,k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\cdot)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})
, due to the general initial condition. The following inequality is also required, which is a consequence of the Littlewood-Hardy inequality: for any
\varphi\in L^{1/H_{0}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k})
,

\alpha_{H_{0}}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2H_{0}-2}\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\varphi(\boldsymbol{s_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}d\boldsymbol{s_{k}}\leq b_{H_{0}}^{k}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}|\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})|^{1/H_{0}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right)^{2H_{0}},

(46)

where
b_{H_{0}}>0
depends only on
H_{0}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\ell=\max(3/4-H_{0},0)
.
If
H_{n}\to H^{*}\in(\ell,1/2)
, then

Q_{n}:=\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H_{n}}(f_{t,x,k})-I_{k}^{H^{*}}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$},

for any
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma 4.2 of
[
6
]
, by (
46
), we infer that

\displaystyle Q_{n}\leq k!\,b_{H_{0}}^{k}\left(\int_{T_{k}(t)}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right)^{2H_{0}}.

where

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\overline{\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{s_{k}})}\left|c^{k}_{H_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c^{k}_{H^{\ast}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{\ast}}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}

and
\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})
is defined by (
23
) with
d=1
.

Therefore, it suffices to show that:

\int_{T_{k}(t)}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

(47)

For this, we apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem. We will show that

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$},

(48)

and there exists a function
h_{k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})
such that

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\leq h_{k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\quad\mbox{and}\int_{T_{k}(t)}h_{k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}<\infty.

(49)

We first prove (
48
). By the change of variables
z_{i}=t_{i}\xi_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,j
, followed by
\eta_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}z_{i}
for
j=1,\ldots,k
(with
\eta_{0}=0
), we have

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\leq w_{+}^{2}(t,x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\exp\Big(-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{j}t_{i}\xi_{i}\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\bigg|c^{k/2}_{H_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\xi_{j}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c^{k/2}_{H^{\ast}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\xi_{j}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{\ast}}\bigg|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle=w_{+}^{2}(t,x)\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{-1}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\exp\Big(-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{j}z_{i}\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\bigg|c^{k/2}_{H_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{H_{n}-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|z_{j}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c^{k/2}_{H^{\ast}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{H^{\ast}-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|z_{j}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{\ast}}\bigg|^{2}d\boldsymbol{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=w_{+}^{2}(t,x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\exp\Big(-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\Big|\eta_{j}\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\bigg|c^{k/2}_{H_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{H_{n}-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c^{k/2}_{H^{\ast}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{H^{\ast}-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{\ast}}\bigg|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}.

(50)

We denote by
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
the integrand in the last integral above. Clearly,
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. So relation (
48
) will follow by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, as long as we show that there exists a function
B(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
such that

B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq B(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}B(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}<\infty.

Note that
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq 2\big(B_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})+B_{2}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\big)
, where

B_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\exp\Big(-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\big|\eta_{j}\big|^{2}\Big)c^{k}_{H_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{2H_{n}-2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}\big|^{1-2H_{n}}

and
B_{2}
has a similar expression with
H_{n}
replaced by
H^{*}
. It suffices to consider
B_{1}^{(n)}
.

Fix numbers
a
and
b
such that

\ell<a<H^{*}<b<\frac{1}{2}.

(51)

Then
H_{n}\in[a,b]
for
n
large enough.
Since the constant
c_{H}
defines a continuous function of
H
,
c_{H_{n}}
is bounded by a constant
c
.
We use the inequality

\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H}\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}\in A_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}|^{(1-2H)a_{j}},

(52)

where
A_{k}
is a set of multi-indices
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
such that

a_{1}\in\{1,2\}
,
a_{n}\in\{0,1\}
,
a_{j}\in\{0,1,2\}
for
j=1,\ldots,k-1
,
\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}=k
, and
{\rm card}(A_{k})=2^{k-1}
. Note that
|\eta_{j}|^{(1-2H)a_{j}}\leq f_{a}(|\eta_{j}|)
for any
j=1,\ldots,n
, where
a
is the constant from
\{0,1,2\}
and the functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}
are defined as follows:
f_{0}(r)=1
for any
r>0
,

f_{1}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{1-2a}&\mbox{if $r\geq 1$}\\
1&\mbox{if $r\in(0,1)$}\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{2}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{2(1-2a)}&\mbox{if $r\geq 1$}\\
1&\mbox{if $r\in(0,1)$}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for any
H_{n}\in[a,b]
,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}t^{2H_{n}-2}_{j}

\displaystyle=\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}t^{-1}_{j}\Big)^{2-2H_{n}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(t^{-1}_{j}\vee 1\big)^{2-2a}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(t_{j}\wedge 1\big)^{2a-2}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(t_{j}\vee 1\big)^{2a-2}.

It follows that:

\displaystyle B_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq c^{k}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j}\vee 1)^{2a-2}\Big)\sum_{\mathbb{a}\in A_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\exp\Big(-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\Big|\eta_{j}\Big|^{2}\Big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}f_{a_{j}}(|\eta_{j}|)=:F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}).

By the same argument,
B_{2}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
. Hence,
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq 2F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
and so,

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}.

(53)

It remains to prove that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}<\infty
. We give below an estimate for this integral which will be used for the proof of relation (
49
). Note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}=c^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j}\vee 1)^{2a-2}\sum_{\mathbb{a}\in A_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}I(a_{j}),\ \mbox{with}\ I(a_{j})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}|\eta_{j}|^{2}}f_{a_{j}}(|\eta_{j}|)d\eta_{j}.

We use the same arguments for the study of the upper bound of
I(a_{j})
in the proof of Lemma 4.2 in
[
6
]
with
t_{j+1}-t_{j}
replaced by
\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{t_{j}t_{j+1}}
. Moreover, we have

\begin{cases}(t_{j}\vee 1)^{2a-2}(t_{j}t_{j+1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}}_{j+1}\frac{1}{(t_{j}\vee 1)^{2-2a}}(t_{j}\vee 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}}_{j+1},&\text{ since }\frac{1}{2}<2-2a,\\[8.00003pt]
(t_{j}\vee 1)^{2a-2}(t_{j}t_{j+1})^{1-a}\leq t^{1-a}_{j+1}\frac{1}{(t_{j}\vee 1)^{1-a}}(t_{j}\vee 1)^{1-a}\leq t^{1-a}_{j+1},&\text{ since }1-a<2-2a,\\[8.00003pt]
(t_{j}\vee 1)^{2a-2}(t_{j}t_{j+1})^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}\leq t^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}_{j+1}\frac{1}{(t_{j}\vee 1)^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}}(t_{j}\vee 1)^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}\leq t^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}_{j+1},&\text{ since }\frac{3-4a}{2}<2-2a.\end{cases}

Since
\frac{1}{2}<1-a<\frac{3-4a}{2}
,
t^{\frac{1}{2}}_{j+1}
,
t^{1-a}_{j+1}
and
t^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}_{j+1}
are all dominated by
(T\vee 1)^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle(t_{j}\vee 1)^{2a-2}I(a_{j})

\displaystyle\leq c_{a}t^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}_{j+1}\Big\{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{1}{2}}+(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-(1-a)}+(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq c_{a}c_{t,a}(T\vee 1)^{\frac{3-4a}{2}}\,(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}.

Since
\text{card}(A_{k})=2^{k-1}
, we obtain:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}\leq 2^{k-1}c^{k}c^{k}_{a}c^{k}_{t,a}(T\vee 1)^{k\cdot\frac{3-4a}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}.

(54)

This proves the integrability of
F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
and so, relation (
48
) follows by the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

Now we prove (
49
). By relations (
53
) and (
54
), we have

\displaystyle\Big(A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(2^{k+1}c^{k}c_{a}^{k}c^{k}_{t,a}(T\vee 1)^{k\cdot\frac{3-4a}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}=:h_{k}(\mathbb{t}).

Note that
h_{k}(\mathbb{t})
is integrable over the symplex
T_{k}(t)
since
-\frac{3-4a}{4H_{0}}>-1
which is equivalent to
a>\frac{3}{4}-H_{0}
. This finishes the proof of (
49
) and the justification of the application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

∎

We now prove some moment estimates for the increments of the solution
u^{H}
, uniform in
H
, which will be used below for the proof of tightness of the family
(u^{H})_{H\in[a,b]}
. In particular, Theorem
3.4
shows that the process
u^{H}
has a continuous modification. We will work with this modification.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\ell=\max(3/4-H_{0},0)
. Let
[a,b]
be a compact set in
[0,1]
such that:

\ell<a<b<\frac{1}{2}.

(55)

Let
u^{H}
be the solution of the heat equation (
1
) with noise
W^{H}
. For any
p\geq 2
,
T>0
,
c_{0}\in(0,\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2H_{0}})
and

0<\theta<2H_{0}(1-c_{0})+a-1,

(56)

there exist a constant
C>0
such that for any
t,t^{\prime}\in[t_{0},T]
and
x,x^{\prime}\in K
,

\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)-u(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|^{p}\leq C\Big(|t-t^{\prime}|^{p\theta/2}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{p\theta}\Big).

Proof.

Step 1 (time increments).
Let
t\in[t_{0},T]
and
h>0
be such that
t+h\in[t_{0},T]
. As in the proof of Theorem
2.7
,

\displaystyle\big\|u^{H}(t+h,x)-u^{H}(t,x)\big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{2}{n!}\Big(A_{n}^{H}(t,h)+B_{n}^{H}(t,h)\Big)\right)^{1/2},

where

A_{n}^{H}(t,h)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{t+h,x,n}1_{[0,t]^{n}}-\widetilde{f}_{t,x,n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
and
B_{n}^{H}(t,h)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{t+h,x,n}1_{[0,t+h]^{n}-[0,t]^{n}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
.

We examine
A_{n}^{H}(t,h)
. Note that by inequality (
46
),

\displaystyle A^{H}_{n}(t,h)

\displaystyle\leq b^{n}_{H_{0}}\bigg(\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\big(\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\big)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{t}}\bigg)^{2H_{0}},

(57)

where the function
\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
is defined as follows: if
\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\in[0,t]^{n}
is fixed and
\rho
is the permutation of
1,\ldots,n
such that
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}
with
t_{\rho(n+1)}=t
, and

\displaystyle\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\mathcal{F}\Big(f_{t+h,x,n}(t_{\rho(1)},\cdot,\ldots,t_{\rho(n)},\cdot)-f_{t,x,n}(t_{\rho(1)},\cdot,\ldots,t_{\rho(n)},\cdot)\Big)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}})\Big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}}.

Assume for simplicity that
t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
. To estimate
\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
, we use estimates (
31
), (
34
) and (
35
), for the increments of the Fourier transform appearing above. Hence, there exists a constant
C^{(1)}
depending on
t_{0}
,
\theta
and
T
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq h^{\theta}C^{(1)}\big(t-t_{n}\big)^{-\theta}I^{(n)}_{t/2}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}}/2)

(58)

where

\displaystyle I^{(n)}_{t}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}}):=c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\exp\bigg\{-\frac{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}{t_{k+1}t_{k}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\xi_{i}\Big|^{2}\bigg\}|\xi_{k}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}}.

(59)

and
t_{n+1}=t
.

We study
I^{(n)}_{t}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
. Using the change of variables
z_{i}=t_{i}\xi_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, followed by
\eta_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}
for
k=1,\ldots,n
, we have:

\displaystyle I^{(n)}_{t}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{n}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}\Big)^{2H-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\exp\bigg\{-\frac{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}{t_{k+1}t_{k}}|\eta_{k}|^{2}\bigg\}|\eta_{1}|^{1-2H}\prod_{k=2}^{n}\big(|\eta_{k}|^{1-2H}+|\eta_{k-1}|^{1-2H}\big)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{n}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}\Big)^{2H-2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\Big(-\frac{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}{t_{k+1}t_{k}}|\eta_{k}|^{2}\Big)|\eta_{k}|^{\alpha_{k}}\mathrm{d}\eta_{k}\bigg\},

where for the last inequality, we used relation (
52
) and the set
D_{n}^{(H)}
is the set of multi-indices
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
with
\alpha_{j}=(1-2H)a_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{a_{n}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in A_{n}
. Using Lemma
3.1
to compute the
d\eta_{k}
integrals above with
k=1,\ldots,n
, we have:

\displaystyle I^{(n)}_{t}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{n}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}\Big)^{2H-2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma\Big(\frac{1+\alpha_{k}}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\Big)^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{k}}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{H,1}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}t^{\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{2}}t_{1}^{\frac{4H-3+\alpha_{1}}{2}}\Big(\prod_{k=2}^{n}t_{k}^{\frac{4H-2+\alpha_{k-1}+\alpha_{k}}{2}}\Big)\prod_{k=1}^{n}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{k}}{2}}

(60)

where for the second inequality we used the constant
C_{H,1}
defined by

C_{H,1}=c_{H}\max\bigg\{\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big),\Gamma\Big(1-H\Big),\Gamma\Big(\frac{3-4H}{2}\Big)\bigg\}.

(61)

Both
c_{H}
and
C_{H,1}
can be uniformly bounded for all
H\in[a,b]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}t^{\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{k}}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{\widetilde{\beta}_{k}}\bigg).

(80)

A similar estimate holds for arbitrary
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in[0,t]^{n}
with
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}
for same permutation
\rho\in S_{n}
.

We now integrate
\big(\Psi^{H}_{t,z,n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\big)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}
over
[0,t]^{n}
. For this, we use Lemma
3.2
. For the first integral, we use (
69
) and for the second integral we use (
77
). Note that, the polynomial function of
t
in the right side of (
69
), respectively (
77
), is
t^{|\widetilde{\alpha}|+|\widetilde{\beta}|+n}=t^{n\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}
and the factor
t^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}
cancels when we multiplying the integral by
t^{\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}
, as required when integrating the right hand side of (
80
) with respect to
\boldsymbol{t_{n}}
. Using the same estimates above, we see that

\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\Big(\Psi^{H}_{t,z,n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq|z|^{\frac{\theta}{H_{0}}}\frac{\big(C^{(9)}\big)^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{\frac{a-1}{2H_{0}}}}.

We conclude that

\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}C_{n}^{H}(t,z)\right)^{1/2}\leq C|z|^{\theta},

where

is a constant depending on

,

,

,

,

,

and

.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:
Step 1. (finite dimensional convergence)
In this step, we prove that:

\big(u^{H_{n}}(t_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,u^{H_{n}}(t_{k},x_{k})\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\big(u^{H^{*}}(t_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,u^{H^{*}}(t_{k},x_{k})\big),

for any
(t_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,(t_{k},x_{k})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
. It is enough to prove that for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
,
u^{H_{n}}(t,x)\to u^{H^{*}}(t,x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we approximate
u^{H}(t,x)
by the partial sum:

u_{m}^{H}(t,x)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}I_{n}^{H}(f_{t,x,k}).

Then
u_{m}^{H^{\ast}}(t,x)\to u^{H^{\ast}}(t,x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
m\to\infty
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.3
, for any
m\geq 1
fixed,

u_{m}^{H_{n}}(t,x)\to u_{m}^{H*}(t,x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
. So, it remains to prove that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}|u_{m}^{H_{n}}(t,x)-u^{H_{n}}(t,x)|^{2}\to 0

as
m\to\infty
. We choose values
a
and
b
such that (
51
) holds. Since
H_{n}\to H^{*}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a<H_{n}<b
for all
n\geq N
. Therefore, it suffices to prove that:

\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\mathbb{E}|u_{m}^{H}(t,x)-u^{H}(t,x)|^{2}=\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\sum_{k\geq m+1}\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $m\to\infty$}.

(81)

To prove (
81
), we need to estimate
\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}
. Those estimates have been done in
[
4
]
, but we need to revisit them here since we are interested in obtaining a uniform bound in
H
. First, note that by Littlewood-Hardy inequality (
46
),

\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}=k!\,\|\widetilde{f}_{t,x,k}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\otimes k}}^{2}\leq k!\,b_{0}^{k}\left(\int_{[0,t]^{k}}A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right)^{2H_{0}},

(82)

where
A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})=c_{H}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\mathcal{F}\widetilde{f}_{t,x,k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\bullet)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}
.

Next, we would like to find an upper bound for
A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})
. For this, using the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem 2.2 of
[
6
]
(the study of
A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})
), with
\mathcal{F}G_{t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}}(\eta_{j})
replaced by
\mathcal{F}G_{(t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)})/t_{\rho(j+1)}t_{\rho(j)}}(\eta_{j})
, we obtain:

\displaystyle A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\leq\frac{c_{H}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{\rho(j)}\Big)^{2H-2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\Big(-\frac{t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}}{t_{\rho(j+1)}t_{\rho(j)}}|\eta_{j}|^{2}\Big)|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\eta_{j}\bigg\}.

where
D_{k}^{(H)}
be the set of multi-indices
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
with
\alpha_{j}=(1-2H)a_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in A_{k}

Using Lemma
3.1
, we have:

\displaystyle A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{H}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{\rho(j)}\Big)^{2H-2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma\Big(\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}}{t_{\rho(j+1)}t_{\rho(j)}}\Big)^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{k}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{H,1}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}^{(H)}}t^{\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{2}}t_{\rho(1)}^{\frac{4H-3+\alpha_{1}}{2}}\Big(\prod_{j=2}^{k}t_{\rho(j)}^{\frac{4H-2+\alpha_{k-1}+\alpha_{k}}{2}}\Big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{k}}{2}}

where for the second inequality we used the constant
C_{H,1}
defined by (
61
).

Coming back to (
82
), we obtain that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\leq b^{2}_{H_{0}}w_{+}^{2}(t,x)C_{H,1}^{k}(k!)^{2H_{0}-1}\bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}^{(H)}}t^{\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\bigg)^{2H_{0}}

where
\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
are the same as in (
75
). The last integral is calculated by Lemma
3.2
, the integral is

\displaystyle\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{\rho(j)}^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}}(t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)})^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1}+1)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma(\widetilde{\beta}_{j}+1)}{\Gamma\big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\beta}_{j}+k+1\big)}\gamma_{k-1}t^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\beta}_{j}+k}

Similarly as in (
77
), those factors appears in above can be bounded uniformly in
H\in[a,b]
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\sum_{k\geq m+1}\mathbb{E}\big|I^{H}_{k}(f_{k}(\cdot,t,x))\big|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\geq m+1}C^{k}\frac{(t\vee 1)^{k(2H_{0}+b-1)}}{(k!)^{a}}.

The last term clearly converges to
0
as
m\to\infty
.
This concludes the proof of (
81
).

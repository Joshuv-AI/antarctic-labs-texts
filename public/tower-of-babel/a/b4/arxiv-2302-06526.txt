Nonlocal-interaction vortices
Margherita Solci

Abstract
We consider sequences of quadratic non-local functionals, depending on a small parameter ε \varepsilon , that approximate the Dirichlet integral by a well-known result by Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu. Similarly to what is done for hard-core approximations to vortex energies in the case of the Dirichlet integral, we further scale such energies by | log ⁡ ε | − 1 |\log\varepsilon|^{-1} and restrict them to S 1 S^{1} -valued functions. We introduce a notion of convergence of functions to integral currents with respect to which such energies are equi-coercive, and show the converge to a vortex energy, similarly to the limit behaviour of Ginzburg-Landau energies at the vortex scaling. MSC Classification: 49J45, 35Q56, 74A70, 46E39 Keywords: nonlocal energies, topological singularities, discrete approximations, Γ \Gamma -convergence

Introduction

In their seminal paper
[
9
]
, Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu have shown, among many other results, that sequences of nonlocal energies depending on a vanishing parameter
\varepsilon
approximate Sobolev seminorms. In particular if
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\rho
is an integrable positive kernel with compact support, their result implies that the
\Gamma
-limit of
energies of the form

F^{BBM}_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\rho\Bigl({|x-y|\over\varepsilon}\Bigr){|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}\,dxdy,

(1)

defined on functions
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, is an explicit constant depending only on
\rho
and the dimension
d
times the Dirichlet integral. For a general variational approach to convolution-type energies modeled on functionals (
1
) we refer to
[
4
]
. Note that we may rewrite such type of functionals in the form

F^{BBM}_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\rho\Bigl({|x-y|\over\varepsilon}\Bigr){|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\over|x-y|^{2}}\,dxdy,

(2)

up to considering the kernel
\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}
instead of
\rho
in (
1
), which is a notation closer to that of
[
9
]
.

Functionals such as those considered above, and more in general functionals of nonlocal type, have recently been used to study a number of variational problems, where the role of gradient terms is played by either finite differences, or fractional seminorms, or nonlocal gradients. This has been used to give non-local approximation of elastic energies or sharp-interface theories often using a singular-perturbation approach (see
e.g.

[
3
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
18
]
). Our objective is to extend the use of nonlocal energies in order to treat models with topological singularities such as those arising in the asymptotic analysis of Ginzburg-Landau energies
[
8
,
19
]
. To that end, we consider an open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
and sequences of nonlocal-interaction energies defined for vector functions
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
|u(x)|=1
almost everywhere as

F_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\rho\Bigl({|x-y|\over\varepsilon}\Bigr){|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}\,dxdy.

(3)

In light of the Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu result, heuristically this can be regarded as a singular perturbation of Dirichlet energies with the constraint
|u|=1
.
This approach can be compared with the
hard-core
approach to sequences of energies of Ginzburg-Landau type at the
vortex scaling
(see
[
8
,
6
]
).
In that case, one considers scaled Dirichlet integrals on a domain where we have removed from
\Omega
a number of small balls close to possible singularities.
In our case, contrary to the Sobolev-space hard-core model, it is not necessary to remove such small balls since no regularity on
u
is needed.
We will show that energies
F_{\varepsilon}
are approximated as
\varepsilon\to 0
by a vortex energy defined on (
\pi
times) integral
(d-2)
-currents, as in the case of Ginzburg-Landau functionals
[
8
,
14
,
15
,
19
]
.
A similar approximation of vortex energies has been shown in
[
5
]
using lattice energies, proving a connection with the so-called
XY
model. In a sense energies (
3
) are a formal continuous version of those lattice energies, with a major difference, that in (
3
) we do not assume any regularity on the functions
u
, while lattice energies are essentially defined on piecewise-affine functions, with restrictions due to the lattice structure. As a consequence, we have more flexibility in the use of functionals (
3
) in dimension
d\geq 3
(see
[
5
, Section 4.3.2]
for a discussion on technical issues in the lattice case).

A loose explanation of our asymptotic result can be given as follows. For ease of notation we consider the case
d=2
, in which case integral
(d-2)
-currents in
\Omega
are measures of the form

\mu=\pi\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},\qquad d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\hbox{ and }x_{i}\in\Omega,

(4)

and we take
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
in order to simplify changes of variables. Using the change of variables
\xi={x-y\over\varepsilon}
we rewrite the energies as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\rho(|\xi|)}\Bigl({1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|^{2}\,dx\Bigr)\,d\xi.

(5)

The inner integral can be analyzed by discretization. In the case
\xi=|\xi|e_{1}
we can estimate

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt{1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon|\xi|(k+[0,1]^{2})}|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{|\xi|^{2}\over|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Bigl|{1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\xi|^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon|\xi|(k+[0,1]^{2})}(u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x))\,dx\Bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\xi|^{2}\over|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|u^{\varepsilon,\xi}_{k+e_{1}}-u^{\varepsilon,\xi}_{k}|^{2},

where

u^{\varepsilon,\xi}_{k}={1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\xi|^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon|\xi|(k+[0,1]^{2})}u(x)\,dx.

Repeating the same argument for
\xi=|\xi|e_{2}
and taking into account double counting, we can use the discrete energies
X_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,\xi})

in the lower bound, with

X_{\varepsilon}(v)={1\over|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2},

(6)

where
v\colon\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
v_{i}=v(\varepsilon i)
, and
\langle i,j\rangle
denotes summation of nearest neighbours in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. An analog estimate holds for arbitrary
\xi
upon using suitable discretization on rotated lattices. In
[
5
]
the analysis of energies of form (
6
) when
v_{i}\in S^{1}
has been carried out, showing their equi-coerciveness with respect to the flat convergence of the Jacobians of interpolations, implying that the limit energies are defined on vortex measures. In the heuristic argument just illustrated the results of
[
5
]
cannot directly be applied, since the averages
u^{\varepsilon,\xi}_{k}
in general do not lie in
S^{1}
. Furthermore, in principle the limit measure
\mu
might depend on
\xi
. In order to overcome these structural complexities, a Compactness Lemma will ensure that indeed the discrete functions
u^{\varepsilon,\xi}
do converge, up to subsequences, in the same sense as that obtained in
[
5
]
to a vortex measure independent of
\xi
. This result will allow to give a definition of convergence of
u_{\varepsilon}
to
\mu
even though the functions
u_{\varepsilon}
themselves may be not weakly differentiable. Note that the compactness result will be proved using the fact that the piecewise-affine interpolation of
u^{\varepsilon,\xi}
can be seen as an average of piecewise-affine interpolation of
S^{1}
-valued maps to almost all of which we may apply a compactness argument.

Once a compactness result is achieved, we compute the
\Gamma
-limit with respect to the convergence above. Proceeding in the heuristic argument, we can use Fatou’s Lemma to give a lower bound as

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq{1\over 4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}X_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,\xi})d\xi,

where the factor
4
comes from the fact that
\xi
,
-\xi
,
\xi^{\perp}
, and
-\xi^{\perp}
are all taken into account in the definition of
X_{\varepsilon}
. The asymptotic analysis in
[
5
]
ensures that
X_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converge to
4\pi\sum_{i}|d_{i}|
(
d_{i}
being the coefficients of the limit vortex measure
\mu
related to
u_{\varepsilon}
), when the discrete functions are
S^{1}
-valued. If this were true also for
u^{\varepsilon,\xi}
then we would conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}}d\xi\,\sum_{i}|d_{i}|.

Again, as in the compactness argument, this can be proven using the fact that the piecewise-affine interpolation of
u^{\varepsilon,\xi}
can be seen as an average of piecewise-affine interpolation of
S^{1}
-valued maps.
The optimality of the lower bound can be then shown by a direct computation. It is worth mentioning that, while recovery sequences in the case of Ginzburg-Landau energies use a smoothening argument, and the discrete approach uses suitable interpolations of the function
{x\over|x|}
, whose singularity gives a simple vortex in
0
, in our case this function can be directly used as a recovery sequence.

In the paper all results are proven for energies (
3
) in the general
d
-dimensional case, in which the limit is defined on currents of the form
\mu=\pi M
, where
M
is a
(d-2)
-integral current. The form of the
\Gamma
-limit is then

F(\mu)={2\pi\over d}\|M\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}}d\xi,

with
\|M\|
the mass of the current in
\Omega
. Note that
\|M\|=\sum_{i}|d_{i}|
in the
2
-dimensional case.

The plan of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we introduce the necessary notions about currents, in order to define the space of integral currents in which we frame our asymptotic analysis. In the same section, after recalling the results obtained in

[
5
]
for the
XY
model, we give the main definition of convergence of functions to integral currents via the flat convergence of the Jacobians of the interpolation of the averages, and we finally
state our main compactness and
\Gamma
-convergence result. In Section
3
we prove the key compactness lemma, which states the equi-coerciveness of energies
F_{\varepsilon}
with respect to the convergence of the averages introduced in the previous section. The proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence part of the main theorem is given in Section
4
, subdivided into the proof of the lower and upper bounds.

Lemma 2.1

(a criterion for equivalent flat-converging sequences

Let

\displaystyle\int_{U}|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{U}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx\leq C|\log\varepsilon|

2.2
Discrete energies

We will use a compactness and a convergence result for discrete energies defined on

Let
\Omega

For

M=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}d_{\ell}\delta_{x_{\ell}}
and

\Phi(M)=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}|d_{\ell}|.

2.3
A notion of convergence for averages

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open Lipschitz domain. For each
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\varepsilon>0
we define a discretization
I_{\varepsilon}(u)\colon\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as follows

I_{\varepsilon}(u)(\varepsilon i)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{i}\cap\Omega}u(x)\,dx,

(11)

where
Q_{\varepsilon}^{i}=\varepsilon i+\varepsilon[0,1]^{d}
.

We consider
a
1
-periodic triangulation of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with vertices in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

such that its restriction to the unit cube is given by
d!
simplices, each of which has
d
edges coinciding with the edges of the cube (the Kuhn decomposition of the cube, see
e.g.

[
17
, Lemma 1]
).
This triangulation corresponds to a family of piecewise-affine functions
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
with
\lambda_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
and such that
\lambda_{k}(k)=1
, the support of
\lambda_{k}
is the union of the elements of the triangulation containing
k
and
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda_{k}(x)=1
for all
x
.
Given a discrete function
v\colon\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define a piecewise-affine interpolation by setting

A_{\varepsilon}(v)(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda_{k}\Big({x\over\varepsilon}\Big)v(\varepsilon k).

(12)

Note that if the dimension
d
is equal to
2
, then the Kuhn decomposition is simply the subdivision of each square in two triangles, which will make the proofs easier to follow in that case.

We now introduce a notion of convergence of functions to integral currents. It involves the use the notion of Jacobian (of interpolations) for functions which in general are not weakly differentiable.
Its use will be justified by a compactness result (see Theorem
2.6
(i) in the next section).

Definition 2.4

(Convergence)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a sequence in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and let
\mu=\pi M
, where

M
is an integral
(d-2)
-current.
The sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}

converges to

\mu
if
for every
U\subset\subset\Omega
we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\bf F}_{U}(\star J(A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})))-\mu)=0,

where
{\bf F}_{U}
is the flat norm in
U
and
\star J
is the Jacobian current.

Remark 2.5
.

If
v^{\varepsilon}\colon\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to S^{1}
is such that the sum
X_{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon})
defined in (
6
) is equibounded, then Lemma
2.2
implies that, up to subsequences, there exists
\mu
as above such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\bf F}_{U}(\star J(A_{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon})))-\mu)=0

for every
U\subset\subset\Omega
.
In our case, we cannot directly use that result since the discrete functions
I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
are not
S^{1}
-valued.

2.4
The main result

We are now in the position to state our main result of compactness and
\Gamma
-convergence,
which will be proved in the following sections.

Theorem 2.6

(Compactness and
\Gamma
-convergence)
.

Let
\rho\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a fixed kernel with compact support such that

(a)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\,dx<+\infty;

(b)

\rho(t)\geq\rho_{0}>0
in a neighbourhood of
0
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open Lipschitz bounded domain, and for all
\varepsilon>0
let
F_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{1}(\Omega;S^{1})\to[0,+\infty]
be defined by

F_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\rho\Bigl({|x-y|\over\varepsilon}\Bigr){|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}\,dxdy.

Then, we have the following results.

(i)

(equi-coerciveness of
F_{\varepsilon}
)

If
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is a sequence such that

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is equibounded,
then, up to subsequences, there exists an integral
(d-2)
-current
M

such that
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
converges to
\mu=\pi M
in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

(ii)

(lower bound)

If
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
converges to
\mu=\pi M
in the sense of Definition
2.4
, then

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq C_{\rho}\|M\|,\ \ \hbox{ where }\ C_{\rho}={2\pi\over d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\,d\xi.

(13)

(iii)

(upper bound)
For every integral
(d-2)
-current
\mu=\pi M
, there exists a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
converging to
\mu=\pi M
in the sense of Definition
2.4
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=C_{\rho}\|M\|.

Items (ii) and (iii) in Theorem
2.6
state that the sequence
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}

\Gamma
-converges with respect to the convergence in Definition
2.4
to the functional

F(\mu)=C_{\rho}\|M\|,\ \ \hbox{ where }\ C_{\rho}={2\pi\over d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\,d\xi.

with domain

D(F)=\{\mu=\pi M:M\ \hbox{\rm integral }(d-2)\hbox{\rm-current}\}
.

3
The compactness result

In this section we prove item (i) of Theorem
2.6
; that is, the key compactness result with respect to the convergence of the Jacobian currents of interpolations of discrete averages of functions with bounded energies defined in Definition
2.4
. This is the analog of Lemma
2.2
in which instead the Jacobian currents of interpolations of discrete functions are taken into account. That lemma cannot directly be applied to discrete averages since the latter do not take values in
S^{1}
. We will use the fact that it can nevertheless be applied to discretizations defined on translated lattices for almost all translations.

In order to prove the equi-coerciveness of the family
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}
defined in (
3
),
by scaling and comparison it is sufficient to treat the case

F_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\chi_{[-1,1]^{d}}\Bigl({x-y\over\varepsilon}\Bigr){|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}\,dxdy

(14)

with
u\in L^{1}(\Omega;S^{1})
.
For future reference, we restate the compactness result as follows.

Lemma 3.1

(Compactness)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open Lipschitz domain and let

\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a sequence such that

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is equibounded, where
F_{\varepsilon}
is defined by
(
14
).
Then, up to subsequences, there exists an integral
(d-2)
-current
M

such that
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
converges to
\mu=\pi M
in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

Proof.

We first consider the two-dimensional case, in which a simplified notation makes proofs clearer.

Let
u\in L^{1}(\Omega;S^{1})
and let
U\subset\subset\Omega
be an open set.
For
\varepsilon
small enough, using a triangular argument we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt{1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}\Big|u(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1})-u(x)\Big|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}\Big|u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}\Big)-u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\xi\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\xi\Big)-u(x)\Big|^{2}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq{2\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}\Bigl(\Big|u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}\Big)-u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\xi\Big)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\Big|u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\xi\Big)-u(x)\Big|^{2}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq{32\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U+\varepsilon[0,1]^{2}}|u(x+\varepsilon\eta)-u(x)|^{2}\,dx\,d\eta

\displaystyle\leq{32\over\varepsilon^{4}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\chi_{[-1,1]^{2}}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=32F_{\varepsilon}(u),

and hence for all
u\in L^{1}(\Omega;S^{1})
and
U\subset\subset\Omega
we get

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt{1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}|u(x+\varepsilon e_{1})-u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{2\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}\Big|u(x+\varepsilon e_{1})-u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}\Big)\Big|^{2}\,dx+{2\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}\Big|u\Big(x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{1}\Big)-u(x)\Big|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\,F_{\varepsilon}(u)

(15)

for
\varepsilon
small enough.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be such that
\sup_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<+\infty
.
For
U\subset\subset\Omega
and
\varepsilon>0
,
we set

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(U)=\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\varepsilon k+[-2\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]^{2}\subset\subset\Omega,\ \varepsilon k+[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^{2}\cap U\neq\emptyset\}.

Upon using in (
3
) any set containing
\{x\in\Omega:\hbox{\rm dist}(x,U)\leq 2\sqrt{2}\varepsilon\}
,
which we may suppose to be compactly contained in
\Omega
,
in the place of
U
,
we get the estimate

\displaystyle C\,F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{1\over\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\varepsilon(k+[0,1]^{2})}|u_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon e_{1})-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z+\varepsilon e_{1})-u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z)|^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}|u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z+\varepsilon e_{1})-u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z)|^{2}\,dz

for
\varepsilon
small enough.
The same argument can be also used with
e_{2}
in the place of
e_{1}
.
Now, we define a family of discrete functions

u^{\varepsilon,z}\colon\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to S^{1}
given by
u^{\varepsilon,z}_{k}=u^{\varepsilon,z}(\varepsilon k)=u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z)
, after extending
u_{\varepsilon}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by setting
u_{\varepsilon}=0
outside
\Omega
for convenience.
It follows that

\int_{[0,1]^{2}}X_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,z};U)\,dz\leq C\,F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}),

(17)

where for
v\colon\varepsilon\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\to S^{1}
we have set

X_{\varepsilon}(v;U)=\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\langle i,j\rangle\\
i,j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}|v(\varepsilon i)-v(\varepsilon j)|^{2}.

We first outline the arguments of the rest of the proof.
In order to prove the claim we
will use Lemma
2.2
to obtain that, up to subsequences, the sequence of piecewise-affine functions
\{A_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,z})\}
converges to a measure
\mu^{z}
of the form

\mu^{z}=\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{z}}d_{\ell}^{z}\delta_{x^{z}_{\ell}}

in the sense that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\bf F}_{U}(\star J(A_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,z}))-\mu^{z})=0,

(18)

and at the same time (in order to have a common subsequence) we apply Lemma
2.1
to deduce that, up to subsequences, the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
converges to one of such
\mu^{z}
. Note that as a consequence we prove that such
\mu^{z}
are in fact almost all independent of
z
.

As a first step, in order to compare
u^{\varepsilon,z}
and
A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))
we introduce the following auxiliary functions. For any
z\in[0,1]^{2}
we consider the piecewise-affine function which interpolates on the values of
u_{\varepsilon}
on the lattice
\varepsilon z+\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
; that is, the values of
u^{\varepsilon,z}
. We set

u_{\varepsilon}^{z}(x)=A_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,z})(x-\varepsilon z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon z}{\varepsilon}\Big)u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon(k+z))=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon z}{\varepsilon}\Big)u_{k}^{\varepsilon,z}.

Note that if (
18
) holds then
\{u_{\varepsilon}^{z}\}
still converges to the same
\mu^{z}
, in the sense that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\bf F}_{U}(\star J(u^{z}_{\varepsilon})-\mu^{z})=0.

(19)

In order to apply Lemma
2.1
, we have to estimate
the
L^{2}
-norm of
A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))-u^{z}_{\varepsilon}
and of its gradient.
To this end, we note that

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{z}(x)|^{2}=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\Big|\frac{u_{k+e_{1}}^{\varepsilon,z}-u_{k}^{\varepsilon,z}}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\frac{u_{k+e_{2}}^{\varepsilon,z}-u_{k}^{\varepsilon,z}}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2}&\hbox{ for }\ x\in\varepsilon z+\varepsilon k+\varepsilon Q^{-}\\
\displaystyle\Big|\frac{u_{k+e_{1}+e_{2}}^{\varepsilon,z}-u_{k+e_{1}}^{\varepsilon,z}}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\frac{u_{k+e_{1}+e_{2}}^{\varepsilon,z}-u_{k+e_{2}}^{\varepsilon,z}}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2}&\hbox{ for }\ x\in\varepsilon z+\varepsilon k+\varepsilon Q^{+},\end{cases}

(20)

where
k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
we have set
Q^{-}=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in[0,1]^{2}:x_{1}+x_{2}\leq 1\}
and
Q^{+}=[0,1]^{2}\setminus Q^{-}
.
By (
16
) and (
20
) we get

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{z}(x)|^{2}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\Big|\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z+\varepsilon e_{1})-u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z)}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 88.2037pt+\Big|\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z+\varepsilon e_{2})-u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon z)}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2}\Big)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CF_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

Similarly, noting that by Jensen’s inequality we have

\int_{\varepsilon(k+[0,1]^{2})}|u_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon e_{1})-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\geq\varepsilon^{2}|(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon e_{1})-(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))(\varepsilon k)|^{2}

for
k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
, we deduce that

\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}|\nabla A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq CF_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

(21)

In particular it follows that

\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}|\nabla A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{z}|^{2}\,dx\,dz\leq C\,F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

(22)

As for the
L^{2}
-norm of
A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))-u_{\varepsilon}^{z}
, we start by estimating
u_{\varepsilon}^{z}-u_{\varepsilon}
. We have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}|u_{\varepsilon}^{z}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}\Big|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon z}{\varepsilon}\Big)\big(u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon(k+z))-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\Big|^{2}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon z}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big|u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon(k+z))-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\Big|^{2}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{U}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon z}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big|u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon(k+z))-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\Big|^{2}\,dz\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{j}}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon z}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big|u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon(k+z))-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\Big|^{2}\,dz\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{j}}\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{x+[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\chi_{[-1,1]^{2}}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)|u_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

(23)

In this inequality, we used the fact that only an equibounded number of
\lambda_{k}
is different from
0
at fixed
x
and that
\lambda_{k}\leq 1
.

Recalling (
11
) and (
12
), we can write

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\big)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{k}}u_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lambda_{k}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\big)u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon z+\varepsilon k)\,dz\ =\ \int_{[0,1]^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}^{z}(x+\varepsilon z)\,dz.

Hence, proceeding with estimates as in (
3
),

\displaystyle\int_{U}|A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}\Big|\int_{[0,1]^{2}}(u_{\varepsilon}^{z}(x+\varepsilon z)-u_{\varepsilon}(x))\,dz\Big|^{2}\,dx

(24)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{U}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}^{z}(x+\varepsilon z)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dz\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

In particular, this estimate and (
3
)
imply that

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}|A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)))-u^{z}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\,dz\leq CF_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

(25)

By estimates (
17
), (
22
) and (
25
), applying Fatou’s Lemma, we deduce that
for almost all
z\in[0,1]^{2}

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(X_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,z};U)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}|A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)))-u^{z}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 105.2751pt+\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{U}|\nabla A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))-\nabla u^{z}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\Big)<+\infty.

(26)

Recalling Lemma
2.2
,
for almost all
z\in[0,1]^{2}
there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}(z)
and a measure
\mu^{z}
such that
u^{\varepsilon_{j},z}\to\mu^{z}
and

\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}|\log\varepsilon_{j}|}\int_{U}|A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)))-u^{z}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon_{j}|}\int_{U}|\nabla A_{\varepsilon_{j}}(I_{\varepsilon_{j}}(u_{\varepsilon_{j}}))-\nabla u^{z}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq C.

By Lemma
2.1
we obtain that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}{\bf F}_{U}(\star J(A_{\varepsilon_{j}}(I_{\varepsilon_{j}}(u_{\varepsilon_{j}})))-\mu^{z})=0;

(27)

that is,
\{u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\}
converges to
\mu^{z}
in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

The proof in the general case
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is exactly the same as in the
2
-dimensional case, with the difference that all triangular arguments must be repeated
d
times, a factor
\varepsilon^{d-2}
appears in the definition of the discrete functional
X_{\varepsilon}
and we have to use the properties of the Kuhn triangulation in the computation of the gradient of
u_{\varepsilon}^{z}
, generalizing (
20
). We omit the details since they only result in a heavier notation.

Remark 3.2

(Independence from the discretization)
.

We now remark that Definition
2.4
is in fact independent from the choice of the discretization.
We make this statement precise in the case
d=2
, the general case following with minor modifications.

For a fixed
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}

we consider the lattice
\mathbb{Z}_{\xi}^{2}=\mathbb{Z}\xi\oplus\mathbb{Z}\xi^{\perp}
,
where
\xi^{\perp}=(\xi_{2},-\xi_{1})
and the convergence of a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
obtained by a discretization on this lattice.
Namely, for each
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\varepsilon>0
we define
I^{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u)\colon\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as follows

I_{\varepsilon}^{\xi}(u)(\varepsilon k)=\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{k,\xi}\cap\Omega}u(x)\,dx,

(28)

where
Q_{\varepsilon}^{k,\xi}=\varepsilon k+\varepsilon Q^{\xi}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}
and
Q^{\xi}=[0,1]\xi+[0,1]\xi^{\perp}
.

Then, we consider a fixed triangulation of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with vertices in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}
and the corresponding family of piecewise-affine functions
\{\lambda_{k}^{\xi}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}}
with
\lambda_{k}^{\xi}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
and such that
\lambda_{k}^{\xi}(k)=1
, the support of
\lambda_{k}^{\xi}
is the union of the elements of the triangulation containing
k
and
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}}\lambda_{k}(x)=1
for all
x
.
Given a discrete function
v\colon\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, following (
12
) we define a piecewise-affine interpolation by setting

A_{\varepsilon}^{\xi}(v)(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}}\lambda_{k}^{\xi}\Big({x\over\varepsilon}\Big)v(\varepsilon k).

(29)

The key argument of Lemma
3.1
is the comparison of the functions
u_{\varepsilon}^{z}
with
u_{\varepsilon}
as in (
3
), and is obtained thanks to (
3
).
We can repeat the arguments leading to (
3
) with
\xi
in the place of
e_{1}
, up to changing the constants, as follows.
For fixed
\xi
and
z\in[0,1]^{2}
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we set

u^{\varepsilon,z,\xi}_{i}=u^{\varepsilon,z,\xi}(\varepsilon i)=u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon z_{1}\xi+\varepsilon z_{2}\xi^{\perp}+\varepsilon i_{1}\xi+\varepsilon i_{2}\xi^{\perp}),

(30)

and let
u^{z,\xi}_{\varepsilon}
denote the corresponding piecewise-affine interpolation from the lattice
\varepsilon z_{1}\xi+\varepsilon z_{2}\xi^{\perp}+\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}
.
Note that
u^{\varepsilon,z,\xi}_{i}
are defined for

, so that they differ form

by the linear transformation carrying
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\xi}
, up to a small translation.
Then we obtain the estimates analogous to (

) and (
24
) with
A^{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(I^{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))
in the place of
A_{\varepsilon}(I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))
, and we can conclude that

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\int_{[0,1]^{2}}X_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon,z,\xi};U^{\varepsilon}_{z,\xi})\,dz+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{U}|A^{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(I^{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)))-u^{z,\xi}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\,dz

4
Proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence result

We can now prove the
\Gamma
-convergence result. We recall that

\rho\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a fixed kernel with compact support such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\,dx<+\infty
.
Moreover, we suppose that
\rho
is larger than a strictly positive constant in a neighbourhood of
0
; then, upon scaling, it is not restrictive to suppose that

\rho\geq\rho_{0}\chi_{[-1,1]^{d}},

(33)

so that we can directly apply the compactness result of Lemma
3.1
.
We also recall that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open Lipschitz bounded domain, and
F_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{1}(\Omega;S^{1})\to[0,+\infty]
is defined by

F_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\rho\Bigl({|x-y|\over\varepsilon}\Bigr){|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}\,dxdy.

The proof of the lower bound uses a discretization approach, rewriting the energies as integrals on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]^{d}
of lattice energies parameterized on the integration variable, which can be analyzed separately, while a direct computation is used for the upper bound.

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq{\mathcal{L}}^{d-2}(\omega)\pi\int_{B_{1}}\rho(|\xi|)(|\langle\xi,e_{1}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle\xi,e_{2}\rangle|^{2})\,d\xi.

Noting that

\int_{B_{1}}\rho(|\xi|)|\langle\xi,e_{1}\rangle|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{B_{1}}\rho(|\xi|)|\langle\xi,e_{1}\rangle|^{2}\,d\xi={1\over d}\int_{B_{1}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\,d\xi,

we deduce that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\mu)\leq{\mathcal{L}}^{d-2}(\omega){2\pi\over d}\int_{B_{1}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\,d\xi=C_{\varrho}\|M\|,

(40)

where
C_{\rho}
is defined as in (
13
).

(b)
The general case.
We consider a
(d-2)
-dimensional current
M
such that spt
(M)\subset\subset\Omega
and is a polyhedral boundary; that is, there exists a
(d-1)
-dimensional polyhedral current
L
such that
\partial L=M
. Then, we can repeat the arguments for a single vortex up to considering in the place of
v(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}
a function which plays the same role around each vortex.
Indeed,
by
[
2
, Theorem 9.6]
(see also
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 3]
) there exist a map
\tilde{u}\colon\overline{\Omega}\to S^{1}
and a finite union
S
of
(d-3)
-dimensional simplices which contains all the
(d-3)
-dimensional faces of
L
, and
\delta,\gamma>0
such that

(i)

\tilde{u}\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;S^{1})
and
\star J\tilde{u}=M
;

(ii)

\tilde{u}
is locally Lipschitz in
\Omega\setminus(S\cup M)
and there exists
p<\frac{3}{2}
such that

|D\tilde{u}(x)|=O\Big(\frac{1}{{\rm dist}(x,M)}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{1}{{\rm dist}(x,S)^{p}}\Big);

(41)

(iii)

for every
(d-2)
-dimensional face
F
of
M
, setting

U(F,\delta,\gamma)=\Big\{x\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(x,F)\leq\min\Big\{\delta,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}}\Big\}\,{\rm dist}(x,\partial F)\Big\},

then

\tilde{u}(x)=\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|}\ \ \hbox{\rm for }\ x\in U(F,\delta,\gamma),

(42)

where we have identified the
(d-2)
-dimensional affine space containing
F
with
\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
and written
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a pair
(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
, omitting the dependence on
F
(see
[
2
, Section 4.1]
).

In order to treat each singularity separately, we localize our energies as follows.
For all
V\subset\Omega
, we set

F_{\varepsilon}(u;V)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{V\times V}\rho\Bigl({|x-y|\over\varepsilon}\Bigr){|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}\,dxdy.

Now, we take
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\tilde{u}(x)
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
We can subdivide the computation of
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
into a computation “close” to
M
; that is, in each set
U(F,\delta,\gamma)
, which will give the main contribution, and in the rest, which will be small.
Indeed, for each
F
, by (
42
) we can reduce to the computations above for a single vortex, obtaining

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};U(F,\delta,\gamma))\leq C_{\rho}\|M\|(F).

(43)

Now we have to prove that the contribution in the rest of the domain is negligible.
To that end, we introduce the notation

D^{\eta}=\{x\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(x,D)<\eta\}

for each
\eta>0
and
D\subset\Omega
.
With fixed
\eta>0
, let
U=U(\eta)
be an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

M\cup S\subset U\subset\subset S^{\eta}\cup\bigcup_{F}U(F,\delta,\gamma),

where the union is taken over all
(d-2)
-dimensional faces of
M
.
We first note that, since
\tilde{u}
is Lipschitz in
\Omega\setminus\overline{U}
, we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\Omega\setminus\overline{U})=0.

(44)

Then, we consider the neighbourhood of the
(d-3)
-dimensional part of the boundary,
showing that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};S^{\eta})=O(\eta)

(45)

as
\eta\to 0
. This estimate can be achieved by estimating separately interactions close to
S
(in a neighbourhood of order
\varepsilon
) and in the remaining of the set.
To that end, let
T\geq 2
be such that spt
(\rho)\subset B_{T}
;
since
|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq 2
, we have

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};S^{\eta}\cap(S\cup M)^{2T\varepsilon})\leq\frac{C}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

(46)

As for
S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{(T+1)\varepsilon}
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188ptF_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{(T+1)\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(|\xi|)\int_{S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{(T+1)\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}|\tilde{u}(x+\varepsilon\xi)-\tilde{u}(x)|^{2}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(|\xi|)\int_{S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{(T+1)\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{1}|D\tilde{u}(x+t\varepsilon\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\,dt\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{\varepsilon}}|D\tilde{u}(w)|^{2}\,dw\,dt\,d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(|\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\,d\xi\int_{S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{\varepsilon}}|D\tilde{u}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

Now, by using estimate (
41
) we get

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{(T+1)\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{S^{\eta}\setminus(S\cup M)^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{1}{{\rm dist}(x,S)^{2p}}+\frac{1}{{\rm dist}(x,M)^{2}}\Big)\,dx

(47)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|M\|(S^{\eta})

(see
[
5
, Lemma 3]
).
By (
46
) and (
47
) we obtain (
45
).

By gathering estimates (
43
), (
44
) and (
45
), we can conclude
that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(\sum_{F}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};U(F,\delta,\gamma))+F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\Omega\setminus\overline{U})+F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};S^{2\eta})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\rho}\|M\|+O(\eta).

By the arbitrariness of
\eta>0
, the claim is achieved.

Finally, we can remove the hypothesis on
M
by using the following density result (see
e.g.

[
5
, Proposition 2]
and
[
1
,
2
]
).

Proposition 4.1

(density of polyhedral boundaries with multiplicity
1
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
T
be an integral boundary locally in
\Omega
with dimension
h<d
and
\|T\|<+\infty
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{T_{n}\}
of polyhedral boundaries in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with multiplicity
1
and
spt
(T_{n})\subset\subset\Omega
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}{\bf F}_{U}(T_{n}-T)=0
for any
U\subset\subset\Omega

and
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\|T_{n}\|=\|T\|
.

Using this proposition, we can construct a recovery sequence by a diagonal argument, concluding the proof.

Use of algebraic dual representations in domain decomposition methods for Darcy flow in 3D domains
V. Jain, A. Palha, M. Gerritsma

Abstract
In this work we use algebraic dual representations in conjunction with domain decomposition methods for Darcy equations. We define the broken Sobolev spaces and their finite dimensional counterparts. In addition, a global trace space is defined that connects the solution between the broken spaces. Use of dual representations results in a sparse metric free representation of the constraint on divergence of velocity, the pressure gradient term and on the continuity constraint across the sub domains. To demonstrate this, we solve two test cases: i) manufactured solution case, and ii) industrial benchmark reservoir modelling problem SPE10. The results demonstrate that the domain decomposition scheme, although with more unkowns, requires less memory and simulation time as compared to the continuous Galerkin formulation.

Introduction

Since the early days of computational fluid dynamics there has been an immense increment in computational power.
However, the need for development of fast numerical algorithms has persisted consistently.
To that motive, the class of domain decomposition (DD) methods has played an important role in reducing the run times and memory requirements for numerical simulations.
The general aim for these methods is that the domain of the problem is broken up into a set of many small independent sub domains.
New degrees of freedom are introduced, the Lagrange multipliers, that impose the appropriate continuity constraints across these domains.
It is then possible to solve for the coupled system of Lagrange multiplier equations only, which is a smaller system compared to the continuous unbroken formulation.
The local solution in the sub domains is obtained independently of each other as a function of the solution of the Lagrange multipliers.
This process reduces the computational burden of the problem in the sense that it is less demanding in terms of required memory and computational time, without compromising on the accuracy of the results.
Over the last decades, many variants of DD methods have been developed, for example, hybrid method
[
1
]
, mortar method
[
2
]
, FETI method
[
3
,
4
]
, DPG method
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, Steklov-Poincaré method
[
8
,
9
]
, etc.
For a comprehensive discussion of these methods we refer the reader to the review papers
[
10
,
11
]
.
The two main challenges for DD methods are: i) to find a subset of suitable finite dimensional spaces such that the system matrices are not singular, i.e. they do not produce spurious kinematic modes
[
12
]
, especially with respect to the trace spaces of the Lagrange multipliers; and ii) how to efficiently solve the global system of Lagrange multiplier equations which can become large for practical applications and is characterized by high condition numbers.
The primary objective of this paper is to address the first challenge, i.e. to define the framework and extend the use of algebraic dual representations introduced in
[
13
]
for DD formulation of Darcy flow.

The DD formulation used in this work is based on the hybrid method which is a form of discontinuous Galerking formulation.

It is shown that the use of algebraic dual representation results in a sparse metric free representation for the divergence constraint on the velocity, the pressure gradient and the continuity constraint across the sub domain, even for high order spectral element method.
We also see that the continuity constraints are local to the boundary face of adjoining sub domain elements.
This construction is similar to the use of dual spaces in
[
2
]
, where they have non-confirming sub domain boundaries and the continuity constraints are also local to the elements on the adjoining sub domain boundaries only.

In this paper we use spectral elements of order
N=1,2,3
and demonstrate the advantage of using DD formulation with algebraic dual representations using two test cases.
The first test case is a manufactured solution taken from
[
14
]
on a randomly deformed smooth domain.
Using this case we first show that using the DD formulation gives the same solution as the continuous formulation and have speed-up in simulation times.
It is observed that on mesh refinement the speed-up in simulation time is larger in the case of high order elements (see Table
3
).
We also show optimal rates of convergence for varying mesh refinements.
The second test case is a benchmark reservoir modelling test case SPE10
[
15
]
that demonstrates the use of this formulation on a practical application.
The purpose of this work is fourfold: i) to demonstrate that the algebraic dual representations can be extended to the framework of DD methods without compromising on the accuracy of results, ii) to show that using DD method we can go to higher levels of mesh refinement with same memory hardware, iii) the use of DD formulation is more efficient in terms of simulation time, and iv) to show the applicability of the method for industry benchmark problems such as the SPE10 case.

Broken Sobolev spaces

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Let

be the space of square integrable functions and

\begin{array}[]{ll}H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)&:=\left\{p\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right):\mathrm{grad}\ p\in[L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)]^{3}\right\}\\[4.73611pt]
H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)&:=\left\{\bm{v}\in[L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)]^{3}:\mathrm{div}\ \bm{v}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)\right\}\end{array}\;.

The trace spaces of

is defined as

\begin{array}[]{ll}H^{1/2}\left(\partial\Omega\right)&:=\left\{\lambda\in L^{2}\left(\partial\Omega\right):\exists\ p\in H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad\lambda=p|_{\partial\Omega}\right\}\\[4.73611pt]
\end{array}\;,

and we denote by
H^{-1/2}\left(\partial\Omega\right)
its dual space.

Let
\Omega
be broken into

with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}
,
i=1\;,\ldots\;,T
, such that

\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1,\ldots\;,T}\mathcal{M}_{i}\qquad\mbox{with,}\qquad\mathcal{M}_{i}\subset\Omega\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\overset{\circ}{\mathcal{M}}\ _{i}\bigcap\overset{\circ}{\mathcal{M}}\ _{j}=\emptyset\quad\mbox{for}\quad i\neq j\;.

(1)

Let
\Omega_{T}
be the set of sub domains, and
\partial\Omega_{T}
be the set of boundaries of these sub domains defined as

\Omega_{T}=\left\{\mathcal{M}_{i}\right\}_{i=1,\ldots\;,T}\qquad\qquad\partial\Omega_{T}=\left\{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\right\}_{i=1,\ldots\;,T}\;.

We define the broken Sobolev spaces for the set of sub domains
\Omega_{T}
as

\displaystyle L^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right):=

\displaystyle\prod_{\mathcal{M}\in\Omega_{T}}L^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)

\displaystyle H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega_{T}\right):=

\displaystyle\prod_{\mathcal{M}\in\Omega_{T}}H\left(\mathrm{div\;;\mathcal{M}}\right)\;.

\displaystyle H^{-1/2}\left(\partial\Omega_{T}\right):=

\displaystyle\prod_{\Gamma\in\partial\Omega_{T}}H^{-1/2}\left(\Gamma\right)

Let the set of boundary faces,
\partial\Omega_{e}
be defined as

\partial\Omega_{e}=\left\{\gamma_{ij}=\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\bigcap\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{j}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad i<j\right\}\;.

\partial\Omega_{\Gamma}=\left\{\gamma_{i}=\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\bigcap\partial\Omega\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad i=1,\ldots,T\right\}\;.

Then we define the trace space

H^{1/2}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)=\left\{\lambda|_{\gamma}\in L^{2}\left(\gamma\right),\gamma\in\partial\Omega_{e}\right\}=\prod_{\Gamma\in\partial\Omega_{e}}H^{1/2}\left(\Gamma\right)\;.

Notation

-inner product by

A set of coordinates is denoted by

\mathcal{N}_{2}^{k}\left(\bm{u}\right)\\[4.73611pt]

We will Lemma 2 from

Lemma 1

If

and

\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\Psi}^{3-k}\left(\bm{x}\right)\Psi^{k}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega=\mathbb{I}\;,

(2)

where

Corollary 1

The inner product between variables of primal and dual representation is vector dot product of expansion coefficients.

Proof.

Let

, the the inner product is given by

\int_{\Omega}p\ q\ \mathrm{d}\Omega=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{3-k}\left(q\right)^{\intercal}\left(\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\Psi}^{3-k}\left(\bm{x}\right)^{\intercal}\Psi^{k}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega\right)\mathcal{N}^{k}\left(p\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:primal_dual_int}}}{{=}}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{3-k}\left(q\right)^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}^{k}\left(p\right)\;.

∎

3
Finite dimensional spaces

In this section we will define the finite dimensional spaces for hexahedral elements in 3D domains.
The domain
\Omega
is discretized into a mesh that consists of points, edges, surfaces and volumes.
For
N>1
each element also consists of a GLL mesh.
We will first introduce the finite dimensional spaces for the domain
\Omega
as defined in
[
13
]
.
The basis functions for domain
\Omega
are obtained from mapping of basis functions on a reference domain
\widehat{\Omega}=[-1,1]^{3}
.
For further details on construction we refer the reader to
[
13
, §4.5]
.
Here we directly use the mapped basis functions and the relevant properties of these spaces.
We will use the primal representations to define finite element spaces: i)
D\left(\Omega\right)\subset H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)
, ii)
S\left(\Omega\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
, iii)
D_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\subset H^{-1/2}\left(\partial\Omega\right)
, and dual representations to define the finite element spaces: i)
\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
, ii)
\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\subset H^{1/2}\left(\partial\Omega\right)
.

These spaces will then be used to define the broken finite dimensional spaces that will be used for DD formulation.

3.1
Primal representations

3.1.1
Finite element space
D\left(\Omega\right)\subset H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)

Let
\bm{u}
be the flux component of a vector field.
For an element
\bm{u}\in D\left(\Omega\right)
, let
\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)
,
\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)
form the row vector of basis functions and the column vector of expansion coefficients, given as

\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\left(\bm{\epsilon}^{(2)}_{1}\left(\bm{x}\right)\quad\bm{\epsilon}^{(2)}_{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)\quad\ldots\quad\bm{\epsilon}^{(2)}_{d_{2}}\left(\bm{x}\right)\right)\;,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{N}_{1}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\mathcal{N}_{2}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\vdots\\[4.73611pt]
\mathcal{N}_{d_{2}}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\end{array}\right)\;,\qquad\qquad\mbox{respectively.}

(3)

Here,
\mathcal{N}^{2}_{i}\left(\bm{u}\right)=\int_{f_{i}}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\ \mathrm{dA}
denotes the net flux
\bm{u}
through the face
f_{i}
.

Then we can represent
\bm{u}
as

\bm{u}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;.

Using this notation, for two elements
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in D\left(\Omega\right)
the
L^{2}
-inner product is given as

\left(\bm{u}\;,\bm{v}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\bm{u}^{\intercal}\bm{v}\mathrm{d}\Omega={\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}\left(\int_{\Omega}{\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)}^{\intercal}\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega\right)\ \mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)={\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)\;,

where,
\mathbb{M}^{(2)}:=\int_{\Omega}{\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)}^{\intercal}\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega
is the mass matrix associated with the basis functions
\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)
.

If there is a symmetric positive definite permeability tensor
\mathbb{K}
, then the weighted inner product is given as

\left(\bm{u}\;,\bm{v}\right)_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}=\int_{\Omega}\bm{u}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{v}\mathrm{d}\Omega={\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}\left(\int_{\Omega}{\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\left(\bm{x}\right)\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega\right)\ \mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)={\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)\;,

where
\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}:=\int_{\Omega}{\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\left(\bm{x}\right)\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega
is the mass matrix of the weighted inner product.

3.1.3
D_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\subset H^{-1/2}\left(\partial\Omega\right)

The trace space

If

If

\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\Psi^{2}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;.

where tr is the trace operator.

For construction of inclusion matrix, see

The inclusion matrix is a sparse metric-free matrix, that consists of

Algebraic dual representations

3.2.1
Finite dimensional space
\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)

For the finite dimensional space

For any element
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q}\in\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega\right)

\widetilde{\Psi}^{0}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\Psi^{3}\left(\bm{x}\right)\left(\mathbb{M}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}\;,\qquad\text{and}\;,\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q}\right)=\mathbb{M}^{(3)}\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(q\right)\;.

-inner product between the elements

\left(q\;,p\right)=\int_{\Omega}q^{\intercal}p\ \mathrm{d}\Omega={\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(p\right)\;.

(8)

-inner product in (

) requires the evaluation of mass matrix, whereas the

in (

) requires only the vector product between the expansion coefficients which makes the discrete system more sparse and easier to set up.

3.2.2
Finite dimensional trace space

For an element
\lambda\in\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)
if
\widetilde{\Psi}_{b}^{0}\left(\bm{x}\right)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)

\lambda
as

\lambda\left(\bm{x}\right)=\widetilde{\Psi}^{0}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)\;.

(9)

where

\widetilde{\Psi}^{0}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\Psi^{2}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)\left(\mathbb{M}_{b}^{(2)}\right)^{-1}\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)=\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{b}\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\lambda\right)\;,\qquad\mbox{with,}\qquad\mathbb{M}_{b}^{(2)}=\int\Psi^{2}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)^{\intercal}\Psi^{2}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;.

(10)

The inner product between the elements
\lambda\;,\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\in\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\times D_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)
, is given by

\left(\lambda\;,\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\right)=\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)^{\intercal}\left(\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\Psi}^{0}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)^{\intercal}\Psi^{2}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega\right)\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\right)=\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)^{\intercal}\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\right)\;.

(11)

3.3
The gradient of dual representations

For a scalar field
p\in\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega\right),
and
\hat{p}\in\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)
its values on the domain boundary, the gradient operation for dual representations is defined as
[
13
, Def. 19]
,
\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}:\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega\right)\times\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\rightarrow\widetilde{D}\left(\Omega\right)
, such that

\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}\left(p\;,\hat{p}\right)\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega=-\int_{\Omega}p\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{p}\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma\qquad\qquad\forall\bm{u}\in D\left(\Omega\right)\;.

(12)

The expansion coefficients of
\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}\ \left(p,\hat{p}\right)
are given as

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{1}\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}\left({p}\;,\hat{p}\right)\right)=-{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)+\mathbb{N}_{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\hat{p}\right)\;,

(13)

expanded in the basis
\widetilde{\Psi}^{1}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\mathbb{M}^{(2)}\Psi^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)

3.4
Broken finite dimensional spaces

In this section we define the finite dimensional subset of Broken Sobolev spaces.

3.4.1
Finite dimensional space
D\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\subset H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega_{T}\right)

For the set of domains
\Omega_{T}
we define the finite element space
D\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\subset H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega_{T}\right)
as

D\left(\Omega_{T}\right):=\prod_{\mathcal{M}\in\Omega_{T}}D\left(\mathcal{M}\right)\;.

(14)

For any two elements
\bm{u}\;,\bm{v}\in D\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, and symmetric positive definite permeability tensor
\mathbb{K}
, the weighted
L^{2}
-inner product is given by

\left(\bm{u}\;,\bm{v}\right)_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}}}=\int_{\Omega}\bm{u}^{\intercal}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}}\bm{v}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\bm{u}^{\intercal}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}}\bm{v}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}={{\mathcal{N}}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}{\mathbb{M}}^{(2)}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}}}{\mathcal{N}}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)\;,

(15)

where,
\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)
and
\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)
are the column vector of assembled expansion coefficients of all the sub domains and the mass matrix
\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}
is given as

{\mathbb{M}}^{(2)}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}},1}\\[4.73611pt]
&\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}},2}\\[4.73611pt]
&&\ddots\\[4.73611pt]
&&&\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}},T}\end{array}\right]\;,\qquad\text{with,}\qquad\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}},i}=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}{\Psi^{2}_{i}\left(\bm{x}\right)}^{\intercal}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{K}^{-1}}\left(\bm{x}\right)}\Psi^{2}_{i}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}\;,

(16)

where
\Psi_{i}^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)
are the basis functions associated to the finite dimensional space
D\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
.

3.4.2
Finite dimensional space
S\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)

For the set of domains
\Omega_{T}
we define the finite element space
S\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
as

S\left(\Omega_{T}\right):=\prod_{\mathcal{M}\in\Omega_{T}}S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)\;.

(17)

For elements

, the

- inner product is given by

\left(p,q\right)=\int_{\Omega}p^{\intercal}q\ \mathrm{d}\Omega=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}p^{\intercal}q\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}={\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(p\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(3)}\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(q\right)\;,

(18)

where
\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(p\right)
,
\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(q\right)
are the column vectors of assembled expansion coefficients of all the sub domains, and the mass matrix
\mathbb{M}^{(3)}
is the block diagonal mass matrix given by

\mathbb{M}^{(3)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{M}^{(3)}_{1}\\[4.73611pt]
&\mathbb{M}^{(3)}_{2}\\[4.73611pt]
&&\ddots\\[4.73611pt]
&&&\mathbb{M}^{(3)}_{T}\end{array}\right]\;,\qquad\text{with,}\qquad\mathbb{M}^{(3)}_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}{\Psi^{3}_{i}\left(\bm{x}\right)}^{\intercal}\Psi^{3}_{i}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}\;,

(19)

where
\Psi_{i}^{3}\left(\bm{x}\right)
are the basis associated with the finite dimensional space
S\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
.

3.4.3
Finite dimensional space
D_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{T}\right)\subset H^{-1/2}\left(\partial\Omega_{T}\right)

For the set of boundaries
\partial\Omega_{T}
we define the trace space
D_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{T}\right)\subset H^{-1/2}\left(\partial\Omega_{T}\right)

D_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{T}\right):=\prod_{\mathcal{M}\in\Omega_{T}}D_{b}\left(\partial\mathcal{M}\right)\;.

(20)

3.4.4
Finite dimensional space
\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)

The algebraic dual representation of
S\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
is denoted by
\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
and defined as

\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right):=\prod_{\mathcal{M}\in\Omega_{T}}\widetilde{S}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)\;.

(21)

Let
p,q\in S\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\times\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)

\left(p,q\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}q^{\intercal}p\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}={\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(q\right)}^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(p\right)\;,

(22)

where,
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(q\right)\;,\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(p\right)
are the column vectors of assembled expansion coefficients.

3.4.5
Finite dimensional space
\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)\subset H^{1/2}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)

Let
\gamma\in\partial\Omega_{e}
be any boundary face of the sub domains.
Let
D_{b}\left(\gamma\right)
be the space of restriction of basis of

to
\gamma

\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\gamma\right)

Then the finite dimensional sub space

\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)=\prod_{\gamma\in\partial\Omega_{e}}\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\gamma\right)\;.

(23)

For elements
\lambda,\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\in\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)\times D_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{T}\right)

\left(\lambda,\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}}\lambda^{\intercal}\mathrm{tr}\ \bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)^{\intercal}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{2}\left(\mu\right)\;,

(24)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)
,
\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\mu\right)

If
\mu=\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}|_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}}

\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\mu\right)=\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)=\mathbb{N}_{2}^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;,

(25)

and we can write (

\left(\lambda,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)=\widetilde{B}^{0}\left(\lambda\right)^{\intercal}\mathbb{N}_{2}^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;,

(26)

where,
\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)

\mathbb{N}_{2}^{\intercal}

3.4.6
The divergence operator for broken Sobolev spaces

Let
q,\bm{u}\in\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\times D\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
be the elements of the broken finite dimensional spaces.
Then the divergence operation on the vector field
\bm{u}
is given by

\left(q\;,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}q\ \mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}={\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(q\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{E}^{3,2}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\qquad\forall q\in\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\;,

(27)

where,
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(q\right),\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)
are the column vectors of the assembled expansion coefficients, and
\mathbb{E}^{3,2}
is the assembled divergence operator.
If
\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}
is the discrete representation of divergence operator on the domain
\mathcal{M}_{i}
, then we have

\mathbb{E}^{3,2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{1}\\[4.73611pt]
&\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{2}\\[4.73611pt]
&&\ddots\\[4.73611pt]
&&&\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{T}\end{array}\right]\;.

(28)

If the topology of sub domain discretizations is also the same, see Example
1
, then we have that
\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{1}=\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{2}=\ldots=\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{T}
.
In this paper we have only used the case with the same topology for all the sub domains, therefore

\mathbb{E}^{3,2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}\\[4.73611pt]
&\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}\\[4.73611pt]
&&\ddots\\[4.73611pt]
&&&\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}\end{array}\right]\;,

(29)

where
\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}
is the topological divergence operator for any of the sub domains.

3.4.7
The gradient of dual representations

Let
p
be a scalar field represented by algebraic dual representation
\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, and
\hat{p}
be the boundary value of the scalar field on the sub domain boundary faces
\gamma\in\partial\Omega_{e}

The gradient operation for dual representations
p\;,\hat{p}\in\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\times\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)
is then defined as,
\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}:\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\times\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)\rightarrow\widetilde{D}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, such that

\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}\left(p\;,\hat{p}\right)\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}\left(p\;,\hat{p}\right)\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left(-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}p\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}}\hat{p}\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma\right)\quad\forall\bm{u}\in D\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\;.

(30)

For the pair of elements
p\;,\hat{p}\in\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\times\widetilde{D}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)
the
H^{1}
-norm is then defined as

\|p\|^{2}_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}=\|p\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\|\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}\left(p,\hat{p}\right)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\{\|p\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)}+\|\widetilde{\mathrm{grad}}\left(p,\hat{p}\right)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)}\right\}\;.

(31)

4
Model anisotropic diffusion problem

In this section we will use the broken finite dimensional spaces defined in Section
3
to derive the algebraic formulation for DD formulation of Darcy problem.

The equations for Darcy problem in the domain
\Omega
are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bm{u}+\mathbb{K}\ \mathrm{grad}\ p&=0\\[4.73611pt]
\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}&=f\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{with}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=\hat{u}&\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{N}\\[4.73611pt]
p=\hat{p}&\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{D}\\[4.73611pt]
\Gamma_{N}\cap\Gamma_{D}=\emptyset&\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma_{N}\cup\Gamma_{D}=\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.\;,

(32)

where
\bm{u}
is the velocity,
\mathbb{K}
is the symmetric positive definite permeability tensor,
p
is the pressure,
f
is the given right hand side term,
\bm{n}
is the outward unit normal vector,
\hat{u}
is the given velocity boundary condition imposed on Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
and
\hat{p}
is the pressure boundary condition imposed on the Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}
.

The Lagrange functional for continuous formulation of Darcy equations is given by

\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{u},p;f,\hat{p},\hat{u}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\bm{u}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega-\int_{\Omega}p\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}-f\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}\hat{p}\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}-\hat{u}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;.

(33)

The algebraic system for (
33
), using algebraic dual representations of
[
13
]
, is given by

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}&-{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\\[4.73611pt]
-\mathbb{E}^{3,2}&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-{\mathbb{N}_{2}}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\hat{p}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
-\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(f\right)\end{array}\right]\;.

(34)

The above system can be solved for unknowns of
p
, and the unknowns of
\bm{u}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}^{3,2}{\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(f\right)+\mathbb{E}^{3,2}{\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}}^{-1}\mathbb{N}_{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\hat{p}\right)\right)

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}}^{-1}\left({\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)-\mathbb{N}_{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\hat{p}\right)\right)

(36)

For the DD formulation of (
32
) we break the domain
\Omega
into
T
sub domains, see (
1
), and we use the Lagrange multipliers
\lambda
to enforce the required continuity across the sub domains.
The weak formulation for (
32
) is then obtained using the Lagrange functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{u},p,\lambda;f,\hat{p},\hat{u}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\{\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\frac{1}{2}\bm{u}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}p\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}-f\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\setminus(\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N})}\lambda\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma\right.

(37)

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{N}}\lambda\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}-\hat{u}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{D}}\hat{p}\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma\right\}\;,

where, on the left hand side, the first term is the kinetic energy term, the second term imposes the constraint on divergence of velocity field
\bm{u}
, the third term imposes continuity of flux across the sub domain faces, the fourth term imposes the Neumann boundary condition and the fifth term imposes the Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Lemma 2
.

The Lagrange multipliers are pressure boundary values on sub domains.

Proof.

In (
37
) if we take variations with respect to
\bm{u}
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\{\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\bm{v}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{v}\right)p\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\setminus{\Gamma_{D}}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\lambda\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{D}}\hat{p}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma\right\}=0\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega_{t}\right)\;.

For any sub domain
\mathcal{M}_{i}
, we have

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\bm{v}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{v}\right)p\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\setminus{\Gamma_{D}}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\lambda\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{D}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\hat{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma=0\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\;.

If the solution is sufficiently smooth, then using integration by parts on the second term, we get

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\bm{v}^{\intercal}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}-\left(-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\bm{v}^{\intercal}\left(\mathrm{grad}\ p\right)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)p\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma\right)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\setminus{\Gamma_{D}}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\lambda\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{D}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\hat{p}\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma=0\quad\forall\bm{v}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\;.

Now, combining the first and the second term, and the third, fourth and fifth term we get

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\bm{v}^{\intercal}\left(\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}+\mathrm{grad}\ p\right)\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\setminus{\Gamma_{D}}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\left(\lambda-p\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{D}}\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)\left(p-\hat{p}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma=0\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\;.

This implies that
\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}+\mathrm{grad}\ p=0
in the
L^{2}
-sense, i.e. almost everywhere, and
\lambda-p=0
in the
H^{1/2}
-sense, i.e. between the sub domains and
p=\hat{p}
along
\Gamma_{D}
.
As this should hold for all
\bm{v}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
, we have that
\lambda=p
, i.e. the Lagrange multipliers are the pressure boundary values on the sub domain boundaries,
\partial\mathcal{M}_{i}
.
∎

The optimality conditions at continuous level for the Lagrange functional (
37
) are given by: For given
f\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
,
\hat{p}\in H^{1/2}\left(\Gamma_{D}\right)
,
\hat{u}\in H^{-1/2}\left(\Gamma_{N}\right)
, find
\bm{u}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
,
p\in L^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
,
\lambda\in H^{1/2}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\{\begin{array}[]{clllll}\left(\bm{v},\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\right)&-\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{v},p\right)&+\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},\lambda\right)&=-\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},\hat{p}\right)&\forall\bm{v}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
-\left(q,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)&&&=-\left(q,f\right)&\forall q\in L^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\left(\mu,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)&&&=\left(\mu,\hat{u}\right)&\forall\mu\in H^{1/2}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)\end{array}\right.\;.

(38)

The finite dimensional problem is then given by: For given
f\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
,
\hat{p}\in H^{1/2}\left(\Gamma_{D}\right)
,
\hat{u}\in H^{-1/2}\left(\Gamma_{N}\right)
, find
\bm{u}\in D\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
,
{p}\in\widetilde{S}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
,
{\lambda}\in\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\{\begin{array}[]{clllll}\left(\bm{v},\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\right)&-\left(\mathrm{div}\ \bm{v},{p}\right)&+\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},{\lambda}\right)&=-\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},{\hat{p}}\right)&\forall\bm{v}\in D\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
-\left({q},\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)&&&=-\left({q},f\right)&\forall q\in\widetilde{S}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\left({\mu},\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)&&&=\left({\mu},\hat{u}\right)&\forall{\mu}\in\widetilde{D}_{b}\left(\partial\Omega_{e}\right)\end{array}\right.\;.

(39)

In (
39
) we see that all terms, except one - the weighted inner product term, are the inner product between the primal and the dual representations.
Consequently, these terms do not require evaluation of (dense) mass matrices.
The only matrices associated with these terms are the sparse, metric-free divergence operator, or the inclusion matrix.
See also (
40
).

4.1
The algebraic formulation

The inner product for the left hand side terms of (
39
) are evaluated as

\displaystyle\left(\bm{v},\mathbb{K}^{-1}\bm{u}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inner_D}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\left({q}\;,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inner_div}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left({q}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{E}^{3,2}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\left({\mu}\;,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inner_trace}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\mu}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{N}_{2}^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;,

and the inner product for the right hand side terms of (
39
) are evaluated as

\displaystyle\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\;,{\hat{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inner_trace}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{N}_{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\hat{p}}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}={\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{v}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{N}_{2}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{0}_{b}\left(\bm{x}\right)\hat{p}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma}\;,

\displaystyle\left({q}\;,f\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inner_S_dual}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left({q}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(f\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}={\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left({q}\right)}^{\intercal}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\Psi}^{0}\left(\bm{x}\right)f\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega}\;,

\displaystyle\left({\mu}\;,\hat{u}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inner_trace2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\mu}\right)}^{\intercal}\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\hat{u}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}={\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\mu}\right)}^{\intercal}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\Psi_{b}^{2}\left(\bm{x}\right)\hat{u}\left(\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma}\;.

Using above relations we can write the algebraic formulation for (
39
) as

\left[\begin{array}[]{lcc}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}&-{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}&\mathbb{N}_{2}\\[4.73611pt]
-\mathbb{E}^{3,2}&0&0\\[4.73611pt]
\mathbb{N}_{2}^{\intercal}&0&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{N}^{2}\left(\bm{u}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left({p}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\lambda}\right)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\mathbb{N}_{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\hat{p}}\right)\\[4.73611pt]
-\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(f\right)\\[4.73611pt]
\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\hat{u}\right)\end{array}\right]\;.

(40)

In (
40
), we see that the matrices
\mathbb{E}^{3,2}
,
\mathbb{N}_{2}
, are sparse and metric-free.
By metric-free, we mean independent of the size of the elements, the shape of the elements (orthogonal or highly curved) or the order of the approximation.
All the metric dependence is contained in the basis functions and therefore in the mass matrix
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}
.
Using static condensation (
40
) can be solved efficiently for the trace variables
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\lambda}\right)
only.
We first solve for a global system of
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\lambda}\right)
by

\left(\mathbb{N}_{2}^{\intercal}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{N}_{2}\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\lambda}\right)=-\mathcal{B}^{2}\left(\hat{u}\right)+\mathbb{N}_{2}^{\intercal}\left({\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\left({\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{N}^{3}\left(f\right)-\mathbb{A}\mathbb{N}_{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left({\hat{p}}\right)\right)

(41)

where,

\mathbb{A}={\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}}^{-1}-{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\left({\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\right)^{-1}{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}}{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1}}^{(2)}}^{-1}\;.

The matrix
\mathbb{A}
is block diagonal and can be constructed efficiently by evaluating for each sub domain separately as

\mathbb{A}_{i}={\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1},i}^{(2)}}^{-1}-{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1},i}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}_{i}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\left({\mathbb{E}_{i}^{3,2}}{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1},i}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}_{i}^{3,2}}^{\intercal}\right)^{-1}{\mathbb{E}_{i}^{3,2}}{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1},i}^{(2)}}^{-1}\;.

(42)

The inverse matrices in (
42
) are symmetric, positive definite and are evaluated using Cholesky decomposition.
The left hand side of (
41
) is also symmetric, positive definite and the
\lambda
system is solved using Cholesky decomposition.
The local degrees of freedom in sub domains
\mathcal{M}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,T
are then evaluated as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}_{i}\left({p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1},i}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}}^{\intercal}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{N}_{i}^{3}\left(f\right)+\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1},i}^{(2)}}^{-1}{\mathbb{N}_{2}}_{i}\left({\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i}^{0}\left({\hat{p}}\right)+\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i}^{0}\left({\lambda}\right)}\right)\right)

(43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{2}_{i}\left(\bm{u}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{K}^{-1},i}^{(2)}}^{-1}\left({\mathbb{E}^{3,2}_{i}}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{i}^{0}\left({p}\right)-{\mathbb{N}_{2}}_{i}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\left(\hat{p}\right)_{i}+\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i}^{0}\left({\lambda}\right)\right)\right)\;.

(44)

The inverse terms in (
43
) and (
44
) are already evaluated in (
42
), and therefore evaluation of expansion coefficients of velocity and pressure field is simply a matrix multiplication step.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we have presented the use of algebraic dual spaces for DD method for Darcy flow.
We have defined the broken Sobolev spaces and their finite dimensional counterparts.
We have also defined the global finite dimensional trace space that connects the broken spaces.
These spaces are used to solve the DD formulation of Darcy equations.

It is shown that using algebraic dual representations the matrix representation of continuity constraint across the sub domains becomes sparse and metric-free.

The first test case is a manufactured solution where it is shown that i) the results from continuous formulation and DD formulation are precisely the same and that DD formulation also has optimal rate of convergence of errors, ii) the DD formulation is more memory efficient, iii) the DD formulation is more efficient in terms of simulation run times.
In the second test case we show that DD scheme can be used to reduce the memory requirements and solve for large practical applications.

We have demonstrated that algebraic dual spaces can be used with DD schemes.
In future this work will be extended to broken
H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
and
H\left(\mathrm{curl};\Omega\right)
spaces, to address problems such as vector Laplacian, Stokes flow, and linear elasticity.

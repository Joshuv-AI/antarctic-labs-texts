\displaystyle{\sf(Conservative)\quad}\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}&=\partial_{p}H(x,p,t)\\

\displaystyle{\sf(Dissipative)\quad}\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}&=\partial_{p}H(x,p,t)\\

\dot{I}&=-\partial_{t}H(x,p,t)-f^{\prime}(t)u-f(t)I\\

Although this model might seem very special, there are several examples in the solar system for which such a model yields a good description of the motion, at least in a first approximation, and anyhow represents a first step toward the understanding of the problem. For instance, in the pairs

In Sec.

2.3
Pumping of the swing

The pumping of a swing is usually modeled as a rigid object forced to rotate back and forth at the lower ends of supporting ropes. After a series of approximations and reasonable simplifications, the pumping of the swing can be characterized as a harmonic oscillator with driving and parametric terms
Ca2
. Therefore, this model has a typical meaning in understanding the dynamics of motors.

As shown in Fig.
3
, the length of the ropes supporting the swinger is
l
, and
s
is the distance between the center of mass of the swinger to the lower ends of the rope. The angle of the supporting rope to the vertical position is denoted by

\theta=\theta_{0}\cos\omega t
. So we get the equation of the motion by

\displaystyle(l^{2}-2ls\cos\theta+s^{2}+R^{2})\ddot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-gl\sin\phi+gs\sin(\phi+\theta)-ls\sin\theta\dot{\theta}^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle+(ls\cos\theta-s^{2}-R^{2})\ddot{\theta}-2ls\sin\theta\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi},\quad\phi\in\mathbb{T}

where

is the gravity index and
mR^{2}
is the moment of inertia of the center (

is the mass of swinger).
We can see that by reasonable adjustment of

parameters, this system can be dissipative, accelerative or critical.

Notice that numerical research of this equation for

has been done by numerical experts in a bunch of papers, see

for a survey of that. Those results successfully simulate the swinging at small to modest amplitudes. As the amplitude grows these results become less and less accurate, and that’s why we resort to a theoretical analysis in this paper.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta\tau}\int^{\tau+\Delta\tau}_{\tau}e^{F_{\delta}(\sigma)}(L(\gamma^{-}_{x,t}(\sigma),\dot{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}(\sigma),\sigma)+c(H))\mbox{d}\sigma.

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\Delta\tau\to 0}\frac{e^{F_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)-e^{F_{\delta}(\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau)}{\Delta\tau}

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{\Delta\tau\to 0}\frac{e^{F_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)-e^{F_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau)+e^{F_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau)-e^{F_{\delta}(\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau)}{\Delta\tau}

\displaystyle=e^{F_{\delta}(\tau)}\varlimsup_{\Delta\tau\to 0}\bigg(\frac{v_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)-v_{\delta}(\tau)}{\Delta\tau}\bigg)+e^{F_{\delta}(\tau)}f_{\delta}(\tau)v_{\delta}(\tau).

Since

and

is continuous,
there exists

such that

and

.

For

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\Delta\tau\to 0}\bigg(\frac{v_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)-v_{\delta}(\tau)}{\Delta\tau}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq L(\gamma^{-}_{x,t}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}(\tau),\tau)+c(H)-f_{\delta}(\tau)v_{\delta}(\tau)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\Delta\tau\to 0}\bigg(\frac{e^{F_{0}(\tau+\Delta\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau+\Delta\tau)-e^{F_{0}(\tau)}v_{\delta}(\tau)}{\Delta\tau}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{F_{0}(\tau)}(L(\gamma^{-}_{x,t}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}(\tau),\tau)+c(H)).

From
v_{\delta}(s_{1})=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle e^{F_{0}(t)}v_{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{s_{1}}e^{F_{0}(\tau)}(L(\gamma^{-}_{x,t},\dot{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t},\tau)+c(H))\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle=e^{F_{0}(t)}u_{0}^{-}(x,t)-e^{F_{0}(s_{1})}u_{0}^{-}(\gamma^{-}_{x,t}(s_{1}),s_{1}).

Note that

is equi-bounded. By a similar approach of the proof of Lemma

is equi-Lipschitz.
∎

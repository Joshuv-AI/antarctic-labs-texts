A priori and a posteriori error analysis of the Crouzeix-Raviart and Morley FEM with original and modified right-hand sides
Carsten Carstensen, and, Neela Nataraj

Abstract
This article on nonconforming schemes for m m harmonic problems simultaneously treats the Crouzeix-Raviart ( m = 1 m=1 ) and the Morley finite elements ( m = 2 m=2 ) for the original and for modified right-hand side F F in the dual space V ∗ := H − m ​ ( Ω ) V^{*}:=H^{-m}(\Omega) to the energy space V := H 0 m ​ ( Ω ) V:=H^{m}_{0}(\Omega) . The smoother J : V nc → V J:V_{\mathrm{nc}}\to V in this paper is a companion operator, that is a linear and bounded right-inverse to the nonconforming interpolation operator I nc : V → V nc I_{\mathrm{nc}}:V\to V_{\mathrm{nc}} , and modifies the discrete right-hand side F h := F ∘ J ∈ V nc ∗ F_{h}:=F\circ J\in V_{\mathrm{nc}}^{*} . The best-approximation property of the modified scheme from Veeser et al. (2018) is recovered and complemented with an analysis of the convergence rates in weaker Sobolev norms. Examples with oscillating data show that the original method may fail to enjoy the best-approximation property but can also be better than the modified scheme. The a posteriori analysis of this paper concerns data oscillations of various types in a class of right-hand sides F ∈ V ∗ F\in V^{*} . The reliable error estimates involve explicit constants and can be recommended for explicit error control of the piecewise energy norm. The efficiency follows solely up to data oscillations and examples illustrate this can be problematic.

Theorem 3.3

(characterization of best-approximation constant)
.

Proof.

Step 1 recalls

given for completeness.
Any

with the abbreviation
\sup_{w,w_{\mathrm{nc}}}

(w,w_{\mathrm{nc}})\in(V_{\mathrm{nc}}^{\perp}\times V_{\mathrm{nc}})\setminus\{0\}

Step 2 establishes
\|P\|_{L(\widehat{V})}=\|P\|_{L(V_{\rm c};V_{\mathrm{nc}})}
for the conforming companion finite element space

.
Since

\widehat{v}\in\widehat{V}

onto the (finite-dimensional, whence closed) subspace

in the Hilbert space

with orthogonality written as

and

and
\widehat{v}-P\widehat{v}\perp V_{\rm c}

.
This and

imply

|\!|\!|P\widehat{v}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}=|\!|\!|Pv_{\rm c}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}\leq\|P\|_{L(V_{\rm c};V_{\mathrm{nc}})}|\!|\!|v_{\rm c}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}\leq\|P\|_{L(V_{\rm c};V_{\mathrm{nc}})}|\!|\!|\widehat{v}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}.\qed

\|P\|_{L(V_{\rm c};V_{\mathrm{nc}})}=\|J\|
with linear algebra. Given any basis

of

Then

and

in coefficient vectors

5
Comments and examples of data in
H^{-{m}}(\Omega)

5.1
The space
H^{-m}(\Omega)

The space
H^{-m}(\Omega)
is the dual to
H^{m}_{0}(\Omega)
and is a real Hilbert space. It is known that for any functional
F\in H^{-m}(\Omega)
, there exist
f_{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq m
such that

F(v)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}v{\rm\,dx}\text{ for all }v\in H^{m}_{0}(\Omega)

(5.1)

(with multi-index notation
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
and

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
for the partial derivatives

\partial_{\alpha}v:=\partial^{|\alpha|}v/(\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x_{1}\cdots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}x_{n})
etc).
The family
(f_{\alpha}:|\alpha|\leq m)
of
L^{2}(\Omega)
functions with (
5.1
) is
not
unique. For instance,
given any
{F}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
one may solve
(-1)^{m}\Delta^{m}u={F}
for a weak solution
u\in H^{m}_{0}(\Omega)
and
consider
G:=D^{m}u\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{(n^{m})})
with

F(v)=a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}G:D^{m}v{\rm\,dx}\text{ for all }v\in H^{m}_{0}(\Omega).

Hence
f_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}F\text{ if }\alpha=(0,0,\cdots,0)\\
0\text{ if }1\leq|\alpha|\leq m\end{cases}

and

f_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}0\text{ if }|\alpha|\leq m-1\\
{G_{\alpha}}\text{ if }|\alpha|=m\end{cases}
with
G_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}u
define two sets of data
(f_{\alpha}:|\alpha|\leq m)

on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) for the same functional
F
. It is also known

[
49
, Lemma 15.9, p.78]
or
[
32
, Thm.1. in 5.9, p.253]
for
m=1
that
\|F\|_{H^{-m}(\Omega)}

is the infimum of
\sqrt{\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|f_{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}
for all
(f_{\alpha}:|\alpha|\leq m)
with (
5.1
).

It cannot be overemphasized that the preprocessing of the data set
(f_{\alpha}:|\alpha|\leq m)
in (
5.1
) is a subtle issue that essentially depends on the way the right-hand side
F
is provided. For instance, point forces may apply, a right-hand side
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
for
m\geq 2
and line integrals may be modeled for
m\geq 1
. The main idea will
be explained for a model example with the right-hand side
F\in H^{-m}(\Omega)
,
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
in (
5.1
) of the form

F(v):=\int_{\Omega}G:D^{m}v{\rm\,dx}+\int_{\Omega}gv{\rm\,dx}\quad\text{for all }v\in V=H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)

(5.2)

for given
G\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{2^{m}})
and
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Consider the vector space
H({\rm div}^{m},\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{2^{m}})
of all
L^{2}
functions
Q\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{2^{m}})

such that the
m
-th distributional divergence
{\rm div}^{m}Q\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is a Lebesgue function:

q\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is called the
m
-th divergence of some
Q\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{2^{m}})
if, for all
v\in D(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}Q:D^{m}v{\rm\,dx}=(-1)^{m}\int_{\Omega}qv{\rm\,dx}.

This holds for all
v\in H^{m}_{0}(\Omega)
as well (by density of
D(\Omega)
in
H^{m}_{0}(\Omega)
) and one writes
q=(-1)^{m}{\rm div}^{m}\>Q
. Hence any
Q
and any
v\in H^{m}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfy

F(v)=\int_{\Omega}(G-Q):D^{m}v{\rm\,dx}+\int_{\Omega}(g+{\rm div}^{m}\>Q)\>v{\rm\,dx}

(5.3)

In other words, the data
G
and
g
of
F
in (
5.2
) can be modified to
G^{\prime}:=G-Q
and

g^{\prime}:=g+{\rm div}^{m}\>Q
without changing the action of
F
on the continuous level.
This data preprocessing may reduce the data oscillations as they arise e.g. in the a posteriori error control.

5.2
Point forces
(m=2)

The mechanical model often involves point loads. The Dirac delta functional
\delta_{z}\in H^{-2}(\Omega)
is the evaluation of the continuous test function
u\in V=H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C({\overline{\Omega}})
at a fixed point

, called atom of the delta functional. Unlike for
m=1

6.3
Example with dominating data oscillations

The data-oscillation term
\|G-\Pi_{0}G\|
dominates the error zero and makes the error estimates useless in the following alarming example.
Suppose
m=2
(the situation is simpler for
m=1
) and choose some function
z_{\rm c}\in S^{1}(\mathcal{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
Given
z_{\rm c}
, consider any

with

\int_{E}z{\rm\,ds}=\int_{E}z_{\rm c}{\rm\,ds}
for all edges
E\in\E
.
(This can be done, e.g, by volume bubble functions added to

or other functions that do not change the above edge integrals.)
Let

and

in (
5.5
).
Since
\Pi_{0}\>{\rm Curl}\>z={\rm Curl}_{\mathrm{pw}}\>I_{\mathrm{CR}}z={\rm Curl}\>z_{\rm c},
it follows that

for all
v\in V
and

.
The first identity follows immediately from an integration by parts and the second from a piecewise integration by parts; for instance, any

satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G:D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}v_{\mathrm{M}}{\rm\,dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}{\rm sym\;Curl}\>z:D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathrm{M}}{\rm\,dx}=\int_{\Omega}{\rm Curl}\>z:D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathrm{M}}{\rm\,dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}{\rm Curl}\>z_{\rm c}:D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathrm{M}}{\rm\,dx}=\sum_{E\in\E}\int_{E}\nu_{E}\cdot\langle{\rm Curl}\>z_{\rm c}\rangle_{E}[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathrm{M}}]_{E}{\rm\,ds}

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\E}\left(\int_{E}[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathrm{M}}]_{E}{\rm\,ds}\right)\cdot\langle{\rm Curl}\>z_{\rm c}\rangle_{E}\nu_{E}{\rm\,ds}=0

(because
[{\rm Curl}\>z_{\rm c}]_{E}\times\nu_{E}=0
and
\langle{\rm Curl}\>z_{\rm c}\rangle_{E}\nu_{E}=0
on
E

while
\int_{E}[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathrm{M}}]_{E}{\rm\,ds}=0
). This latter orthogonality property implies
u=0=u_{\mathrm{M}}
for the scheme (
2.2
) as well as for (
3.9
).
On the other hand,

\|G-\Pi_{0}G\|=\|{\rm sym\;Curl}\;(z-z_{\rm c})\|
. If
\|G-\Pi_{0}G\|_{L^{2}(T)}=0
for
T\in\mathcal{T}
, then

satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm sym\;Curl}\>w=0
a.e. in
T
. This is equivalent to
w_{1,2}=0=w_{2,1}
and
w_{1,1}=w_{2,2}
.
Hence
(w_{2},w_{1})
is divergence-free and there exists some
\beta\in H^{2}(T)
with

(w_{2},w_{1})={\rm\nabla}\beta=(\partial\beta/\partial x_{1},\partial\beta/\partial x_{2}).
Consequently,
\beta
satisfies
\partial^{2}\beta/\partial x_{j}^{2}=0
for

. Since
\beta
is harmonic, it is smooth in
T
. Without loss of generality, assume

. For

, an integration along the line

{\rm conv}\{0,{x_{1}}\}\times\{x_{2}\}

shows
\beta(x)=\beta(0,x_{2})+\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\beta_{,1}(\xi,x_{2})\>{\rm d}\xi
(with the abbreviation
\beta_{,j}:=\partial\beta/\partial x_{j}

\beta_{,ij}:=\partial^{2}\beta/\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}
,
j=1,2

for the partial derivatives). Since
\beta_{,11}=0
,

for a.e.

. Hence
\beta(x)=\beta(0,x_{2})+x_{1}\beta_{,1}(0,x_{2})
. The same argument applies for an integration along
\{x_{1}\}\times{\rm conv}\{0,{x_{2}}\}
and leads to

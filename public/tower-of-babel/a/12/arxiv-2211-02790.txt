Existence of positive solutions for a parameter fractional p p -Laplacian problem with semipositone nonlinearity
Emer Lopera, Camila López, Raúl E. Vidal

Abstract
. In this paper we prove the existence of at least one positive solution for the nonlocal semipositone problem { ( − Δ ) p s ​ ( u ) = λ ​ f ​ ( u ) in Ω u = 0 in ℝ N − Ω , \displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}(u)&=&\lambda f(u)&\text{in}\ \ \Omega\\ u&=&0&\text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}-\Omega,\end{array}\right. whenever λ > 0 \lambda>0 is a sufficiently small parameter. Here Ω ⊆ ℝ N \Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N} a bounded domain with C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} boundary, 2 ⩽ p < N 2\leqslant p<N , s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) and f f superlineal and subcritical. We prove that if λ > 0 \lambda>0 is chosen sufficiently small the associated Energy Functional to the problem has a mountain pass structure and, therefore, it has a critical point u λ u_{\lambda} , which is a weak solution. After that we manage to prove that this solution is positive by using new regularity results up to the boundary and a Hopf’s Lemma.

1.
Introduction

We are interested in the study of the existence of positive solutions to the problem

(1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}(u)&=&\lambda f(u)&\text{in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&\text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}-\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
N>2
is an integer,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary,
s\in(0,1)
,
1<p
and
sp<N
and
\lambda>0
. Besides
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function and
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}
is the
s
-fractional
p
-Laplacian operator defined as

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u(x)=2\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}y.

Let us denote by
p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-sp}
the fractional critical Sobolev exponent. For any Lebesgue measurable set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|U|
will stand for the Lebesgue measure of
U
.
In this work we will assume that there exist
p-1<q<\min\{\frac{sp}{N}p_{s}^{*},p_{s}^{*}-1\}
,
A,B>0
such that

(2)

\begin{array}[]{lll}A(s^{q}-1)\leqslant&f(s)\leqslant B(s^{q}+1)&\text{for}\ \ s>0\\
&f(s)=0&\text{for}\ \ s\leqslant-1\end{array}.

Let us define

F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, there exist
A_{1},C_{1},B_{1}>0
such that

(3)

F(u)\leqslant B_{1}(|u|^{q+1}+1)\qquad\text{for all}\ \ u\in\mathbb{R}

and

(4)

A_{1}(u^{q+1}-C_{1})\leqslant F(u)\qquad\text{for all}\ \ u\geqslant 0.

Let us also assume that
f
satisfies an Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz type condition. More specifically, we will assume that there exist
\theta>p
and
M\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

(5)

sf(s)\geqslant\theta F(s)+M.

Remark 1
.

The existence of at least one solution to our problem can be stated under the assumption
q\in(p-1,\,p_{s}^{*}-1)
. The restriction
p-1<q<\min\{\frac{sp}{N}p_{s}^{*},p_{s}^{*}-1\}
is necessary to prove the positiveness of this.

The aim of this paper is to prove the following result.

Theorem 1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let us assume that
\Omega
is a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary. Then there is
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
problem (
1
) has at least one positive weak solution
u_{\lambda}\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1).

This result extends the one in
[
5
]
where the authors considered the problem for the
p
-Laplacian operator, (
2\leqslant p<N
). The difficulties to prove the positiveness of the solutions for Dirichlet problems with semipositone type nonlinearities are well documented, see for example
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
and references therein. Such issues persist in the nonlocal case. To the best of our knowledge this is the first result on the existence of positive solutions for a semipositone nonlinearity with the fractional
p
-Laplacian.
In
[
7
]
, the authors studied the problem (
1
) with
p=2
,
f(u)=u^{q}-1
, (semipositone) but
0<q<1
. Indeed, they proved the existence of at least one positive solution if
\lambda>0
is sufficiently large. In
[
1
]
, the authors proved the existence of positive solutions of a problem of semipositone type for the
\Phi
-Laplacian through Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

Throughout this paper,
C
will denote positive constant, not the same at each occurrence.

2.
Fractional frame

Definition 1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
1\leq p<\infty
and let

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y<\infty\right\}

be the fractional Sobolev space endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{s,p}=(\|u\|_{p}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p})^{1/p},

where

[u]^{p}_{s,p}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

is the Gagliardo seminorm and for every
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
,
\|\cdot\|_{q}
is the norm in
L^{q}(\Omega)
.

With this norm,
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Banach space.
We shall work in the closed subspace

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0~\textrm{a.e in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}-\Omega\right\}

which can be equivalently renormed by setting
\|u\|=[u]_{s,p}
. The equivalence of this norms is a consequence of the Sobolev embedding theorem (see
[
8
]
).

Let us set for all
s\in\mathbb{R}

\Phi_{p}(s)=|s|^{p-2}s.

A weak solution to the problem (
1
)
is a function
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
such that for all
\varphi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=\lambda\int_{\Omega}f(u)\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

We shall give to this problem a variational approach. Then, for each
\lambda>0
let us define the functional
E_{\lambda}:W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
as

(6)

E_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y-\lambda\int_{\Omega}F(u)\mathrm{d}x.

Observe that
E_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}F(u)\mathrm{d}x
.
It is well known that
E_{\lambda}\in C^{1}
and its derivative is given by

(7)

\langle E^{\prime}_{\lambda}u,\,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y-\lambda\int_{\Omega}f(u)\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, the critical points of
E_{\lambda}
turns out to be the weak solutions of problem (
1
).

3.
Preliminary results

In this section we shall establish some lemmas that guarantee that
E_{\lambda}
has a critical point,
u_{\lambda}
, whenever
\lambda>0
is sufficiently small. After that, we present some lemmas concerning the regularity of
u_{\lambda}
. Finally we prove our main result.
The positive number

r:=\frac{1}{q+1-p},

will be use repeatedly throughout this paper. Let
\varphi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
be a positive function with
\|\varphi\|=1
and let

c:=\bigg(\frac{2}{pA_{1}\|\varphi\|_{q+1}^{q+1}}\bigg)^{r}>0.

Finally, let us define
\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega^{c})
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Lemma 1
.

There exists
\lambda_{1}>0
such that if
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1})
then
E_{\lambda}(c\lambda^{-r}\varphi)\leqslant 0
.

Proof.

Let
l=c\lambda^{-r}
. From the growth behaviour of
F
(see (
4
)) and the fact that
\|\varphi\|=1
we have

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{\lambda}(l\varphi)&=\frac{1}{p}\|l\varphi\|^{p}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}F(l\varphi)\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leqslant\frac{l^{p}}{p}\|\varphi\|^{p}-\lambda A_{1}l^{q+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{q+1}\mathrm{d}x+\lambda A_{1}C_{1}|\Omega|\\
&\leqslant\frac{l^{p}}{p}-\lambda A_{1}l^{q+1}\|\varphi\|^{q+1}_{q+1}+\lambda A_{1}C_{1}|\Omega|.\end{split}

Thus, if
0<\lambda<\big(\frac{c^{p}}{2pA_{1}C_{1}|\Omega|}\big)^{1/(1+rp)}=:\lambda_{1}
, then

(9)

E_{\lambda}(l\varphi)\leqslant-\frac{c^{p}}{2p}\lambda^{-rp}\leqslant 0.

∎

Lemma 2
.

There exist
\tau>0
,
c_{1}>0
and
0<\lambda_{2}<1
such that if
\|u\|=\tau\lambda^{-r}
then
E_{\lambda}(u)\geq c_{1}(\tau\lambda^{-r})^{p}
for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
with
\|u\|=\lambda^{-r}\tau
, by the Sobolev embedding theorem, there exists
K_{1}>0
such that for all
v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
,
\|v\|_{q+1}\leq K_{1}\|v\|
, define
\tau=\min\{(2pK_{1}^{q+1}B_{1})^{-r},c\}
then,

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}F(u)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}(\lambda^{-r}\tau)^{p}-\lambda B_{1}\|u\|_{q+1}^{q+1}-\lambda B_{1}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}(\lambda^{-r}\tau)^{p}-\lambda B_{1}(K_{1}\|u\|)^{q+1}-\lambda B_{1}|\Omega|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}(\lambda^{-r}\tau)^{p}-\lambda B_{1}K_{1}^{q+1}(\lambda^{-r}\tau)^{q+1}-\lambda B_{1}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{-rp}\left(\frac{\tau^{p}}{2p}-\lambda^{1+rp}|\Omega|B_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{-rp}\frac{\tau^{p}}{4p}

taking
c_{1}=\frac{1}{4p}
and
\lambda_{2}:=\tau^{p/(1+rp)}(4pB_{1}|\Omega|)^{-1/(1+rp)}
we obtain the result.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
\lambda_{3}=\min\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}
. Then, there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{3})
the functional

Proof.

First of all, we will prove that

Moreover, from (
7

-\|u_{n}\|^{p}-\|u_{n}\|\leqslant-\lambda\int_{\Omega}f(u_{n})u_{n}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\text{for all}\,n>\nu.

Let
K>0
such that for all

|E_{\lambda}(u_{n})|\leqslant K

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{p}\|u_{n}\|^{p}-\frac{\lambda}{\theta}\int_{\Omega}f(u_{n})u_{n}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda}{\theta}M|\Omega|&\leqslant\frac{1}{p}\|u_{n}\|^{p}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}F(u_{n})\mathrm{d}x\\

Using (

is bounded in

Then, since

Using again that
u

(13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(y))((u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}=0.

On the other hand, taking into account the Hölder inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi_{p}(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))-\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}((u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}-\frac{\Phi_{p}(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}+\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\right]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geqslant\|u_{n}\|^{p}-\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}\|u\|-\|u_{n}\|\|u\|^{p-1}+\|u\|^{p}

\displaystyle=([\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}-\|u\|^{p-1})(\|u_{n}\|-\|u\|)\geqslant 0.

12

u_{n}\to u
strongly in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. This proves that

satisfies the Palais-Smale condition.

Let us observe that, from (
8

From Lemmas

\psi_{j}(x,y):=\frac{|w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}},

is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
. Therefore, following the same procedure made in Lemma
3
to prove the strong convergence of
\{u_{n}\}
(see Lemma
7
in the appendix), we conclude that it converges to

\psi(x,y):=\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}},

in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N}).
Then there exists
h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
such that
|\psi_{j}(x,y)|\leqslant h(x,y)
, a.e.
(x,y)
. Hence, from the Young’s inequality, for all
\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{|w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)|^{p-1}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)|^{p-1}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N+sp}{p^{\prime}}}|x-y|^{\frac{N+sp}{p}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\frac{|w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)|^{(p-1)p^{\prime}}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}+\frac{1}{p}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}(h(x,y))^{p}+\frac{1}{p}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}},

where
p^{\prime}
stands for the conjugate Hölder exponent of
p
.
Since the last function belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
, by the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem we have

(21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p-2}(w(x)-w(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)|^{p-2}(w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{j}f(u_{\lambda_{j}}(x))\|u_{\lambda_{j}}\|_{\infty}^{1-p}\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}z(x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Observe that we also proved that
w_{j}\to w
in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, and thus
w\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. This proves the claim. Thus
w
is a supersolution of the
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}(w)=0
in
\Omega
. Since
\Omega
has
C^{1,1}
boundary then it satisfies the interior ball condition (see Theorem 1.0.9 in
[
2
]
). Therefore, by Theorems 1.4 and 1.5 of
[
6
]
we have
w>0
in
\Omega
and for all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,

\liminf_{x\to x_{0}}\frac{w(x)}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{B_{R}(x)}}>0,

where
B_{R}\subseteq\Omega
and
x_{0}\in\partial B_{R}
.
From Lemma
6
(see appendix), there exists
j
sufficiently large such that
w_{j}>0
in
\Omega.
Absurd.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let us assume that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary and
\frac{w_{j}}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}}\to\frac{w}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}}
in
C^{\beta}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
with
w(x)=w_{j}(x)=0
, for all
j
and all
x\in\partial\Omega
. Let us assume that
w>0
in
\Omega
and for all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega

(22)

m:=\liminf_{x\to x_{0}}\frac{w(x)}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{B_{R}}(x)}>0.

Then there exists

j
such that
w_{j}(x)>0
for all
x\in\Omega
.

Proof.

First of all, let us emphasize that, since
\frac{w}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}}\in C^{\beta}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
, then for all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,
\frac{w(x_{0})}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{\Omega}(x_{0})}
is well defined in terms of limits.
Now, let
B_{R}\subseteq\Omega
be an interior ball such that
x_{0}\in\partial B_{R}
and let be
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
x\in B_{R}\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
,

\frac{w(x)}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{B_{R}}(x)}>\frac{m}{2}.

Let us pick up a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
B_{R}\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
in the segment joining
x_{0}
and the center of
B_{R}
and such that
x_{n}\to x_{0}
. So that for all
n
,
x_{n}-x_{0}
is orthogonal to
\partial B_{R}
and
\partial\Omega
and
\mathrm{d}_{B_{R}}(x_{n})=\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}(x_{n})
. Therefore

\frac{w(x_{0})}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{\Omega}(x_{0})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{w(x_{n})}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{\Omega}(x_{n})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{w(x_{n})}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{B_{R}}(x_{n})}\geqslant\frac{m}{2}>0.

And, obviously,
\frac{w(x)}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{\Omega}(x)}>0

for all
x\in\Omega
. Thus
\frac{w}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{\Omega}}
is positive in the compact

\overline{\Omega}.
Let

(23)

\varepsilon:=\min\frac{w}{\mathrm{d}^{s}_{\Omega}}>0.

Let
\Omega_{1}
be a nonempty open set such that
\overline{\Omega}_{1}\subseteq\Omega
.
We claim that there exists
j
such that for all
x\in\overline{\Omega_{1}}
,
w_{j}(x)>0
. Indeed, there exists
j
sufficiently large such that

\bigg\|\frac{w}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}}-\frac{w_{j}}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}}\bigg\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

In particular for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}_{1}

-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leqslant\frac{w_{j}(x)}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}(x)}-\frac{w(x)}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}(x)}.

Then, for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}_{1}

\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leqslant\frac{w(x)}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}(x)}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}<\frac{w_{j}(x)}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}(x)}.

Which proves the claim. Finally, we will prove that for all
x\in\Omega-\overline{\Omega_{1}}
,
w_{j}(x)>0
. Let us argue by contradiction. If there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega-\overline{\Omega_{1}}
such that
w_{j}(x_{0})\leq 0
, then, by the intermediate Value Theorem, there is
z_{0}\in\Omega-\overline{\Omega_{1}}
such that
w_{j}(z_{0})=0
. Thus, from (
23
) and the definition of
\varepsilon_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{w(z_{0})}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}(z_{0})}-\frac{w_{j}(z_{0})}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}(z_{0})}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{w}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}}-\frac{w_{j}}{\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}^{s}}\bigg\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Absurd.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
\{w_{j}\}
be a bounded sequence in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}(w_{j})&=&\lambda_{j}g(w_{j})~\textrm{in}&\Omega\\
w_{j}(x)&=&0~~~~~~~~~\textrm{in}&\mathbb{R}^{N}-\Omega,\end{array}\right.

with
\{\lambda_{j}g(w_{j})\}
bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then
w_{j}
converges strongly in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
\{w_{j}\}
is bounded in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, then, up to a sub-sequence,
\{w_{j}\}
converges weakly to the function
v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Since
p<q+1<p_{s}^{*}
, then
w_{j}\to v
(strongly) in
L^{q+1}(\Omega)
. As
\{\lambda_{j}g(w_{j})\}
bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, applying the Hölder inequality this implies that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}\int_{\Omega}g(w_{j})(w_{j}-v)\mathrm{d}x=0.

Then, since
J^{\prime}_{\lambda_{j}}(w_{j})=0
(where
J_{\lambda}
is the associated Energy Functional to this problem), we have

(24)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\Phi_{p}(w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y))((w_{j}-v)(x)-(w_{j}-v)(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}=0.

Using again that
v
is the weak limit of
w_{j}
we have

(25)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\Phi_{p}(v(x)-v(y))((w_{j}-v)(x)-(w_{j}-v)(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}=0.

Thus, from the same argument that we use in the proof of Lemma
3
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi_{p}(w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y))-\Phi_{p}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}((w_{j}-v)(x)-(w_{j}-v)(y))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geqslant(\|w_{j}\|^{p-1}-\|v\|^{p-1})(\|w_{j}\|-\|v\|)\geqslant 0.

From (
24
), (
25
) we obtain

\lim_{j\to\infty}(\|w_{j}\|^{p-1}-\|v\|^{p-1})(\|w_{j}\|-\|v\|)=0,

which implies

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|w_{j}\|=\|v\|.

Since
w_{j}\rightharpoonup v
, then
w_{j}\to v
strongly in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
.
∎

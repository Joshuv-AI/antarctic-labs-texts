Abstract
.
We determine all affinely homogeneous
hypersurfaces
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
whose Hessian is (invariantly)
of constant rank 2,

including the simply transitive ones
.

We find 34 inequivalent terminal branches yielding each to a nonempty
moduli space of homogeneous models of
hypersurfaces
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
sometimes parametrized by a certain complicated algebraic variety,
especially for the 15 (over 34) families of models which are

simply transitive
.

We employ the
power series method of equivalence
,
which captures invariants at the origin,
creates branches, and infinitesimalizes calculations.

In Lie’s original classification spirit, we describe
the found homogeneous models by listing explicit
Lie algebras of infinitesimal transformations,
sometimes parametrized by absolute invariants
satisfying certain algebraic equations.

Introduction

The goal of this article is to determine all affinely homogeneous
Hessian rank 2
hypersurfaces
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
,

including the simply transitive ones
.
This problem has been studied, among
other things,
by Mozhei
[
33
]
who found
the
multiply transitive models
,
and also by Wermann
[
36
]
for the special affine group.

About affinely homogeneous submanifolds,
the literature is extensive,
a few references being
[
12
,
9
,
15
,
36
,
18
,
3
,
6
,
30
,
21
]
.
Information about history, context, perspective, problems,
can be found there  —
see
also these
articles’ bibliographies.

To be specific, in
\mathbb{R}^{4}\ni(x,y,z,u)
, a local analytic
hypersurface
S^{3}
can be graphed,
after an affine transformation, as:

\displaystyle u\,=\,F\big(x,y,z\big)\,=\,\sum_{i+j+k\geqslant 0}\,F_{i,j,k}\,x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}\,=\,\sum_{\mu\geqslant 0}\,\sum_{i+j+k=\mu}\,F_{i,j,k}\,x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}\,=\,F_{0}+F_{1}+F_{2}+\cdots,

with
F
real-analytic in some
neighborhood of the origin
(0,0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The Hessian matrix:

{\sf Hessian}_{F}\,:=\,\left[\!\begin{array}[]{ccc}F_{xx}&F_{xy}&F_{xz}\\
F_{yx}&F_{yy}&F_{yz}\\
F_{zx}&F_{zy}&F_{zz}\end{array}\!\right],

has invariant rank
[
30
, Sec. 2]
.

Hypothesis 1.1
.

At every point
(x,y,z)
, assume

{\sf rank}\,{\sf Hessian}_{F}=2
.

After an elementary affine transformation
(Section
2
),
order 1 terms in
F
disappear, and
two inequivalent normalizations for the quadratic terms
F_{2}

exist at order 2:

\begin{array}[]{cc}&F_{2}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2a}&x\,y\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2b}&x^{2}+y^{2}\end{array}

with
x^{2}-y^{2}
equivalent to the more convenient
x\,y
.
At order 2,
these are the 2 existing branches,
labelled
2a
,
2b
,
similarly as in
[
21
]

where 7 branches exist at order 2.

Beyond order 2, further branches exist, exhibited
just before stating the main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Up to the action of a finite subgroup of
{\sf Aff}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
, all the
possible affinely homogeneous hypersurfaces
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
having
constant Hessian rank 2 are represented by means of 3 lists.

\bullet

The list of branching trees appearing above.

\bullet

The list of occurring linear representations appearing
in Section
10
.

\bullet

The list of (truncated) normal forms together with
their associated
transitive Lie algebras of vector fields,
appearing in
Sections
11
and
12
.

Our principal technique is the

Power Series Method of Equivalence
,
inspired from Élie Cartan’s method.
Part I of
[
21
]
describes in details
the features of this (new, computationally economical) method.

Problem 15.2 in
[
21
]
emphasizes
that there is currently no satisfactory theory
which provides necessary and sufficient conditions
on a given transitive Lie algebra of vector fields
that are symmetries of a given type of geometric structures,
in order that, after a change of coordinates belonging
to the initial group, the geometric
structure becomes neatly expressed by means of either
polynomials or
/
and usual transcendental functions, like
exponentials, logarithms, trigonometric functions.

This is why, similarly as in
[
21
]
,
we abandon in this paper the search for closed forms
of the found homogeneous models.

Hypersurfaces
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}

Under
{\sf Aff}(4,\mathbb{R})

So, let us start.
After translation, an affine transformation of
\mathbb{R}^{4}

fixes the origin. Consider therefore an invertible linear map

(x,y,z,u)\longmapsto(p,q,r,v)
:

\begin{aligned} p&\,:=\,a_{1,1}\,x+a_{1,2}\,y+a_{1,3}\,z+b_{1}\,u,\\
q&\,:=\,a_{2,1}\,x+a_{2,2}\,y+a_{2,3}\,z+b_{2}\,u,\\
r&\,:=\,a_{3,1}\,x+a_{3,2}\,y+a_{3,3}\,z+b_{3}\,u,\\
v&\,:=\,c_{1}\,x+c_{2}\,y+c_{3}\,z+d\,u,\end{aligned}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{with}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\,\neq\,\left|\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&a_{1,3}&b_{1}\\
a_{2,1}&a_{2,2}&a_{2,3}&b_{2}\\
a_{3,1}&a_{3,2}&a_{3,3}&b_{3}\\
c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&d\end{array}\!\right|.

Also, consider two local analytic hypersurfaces passing through
the origin, graphed as:

u\,=\,F(x,y,z)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ {\scriptstyle{(F(0,0,0)\,=\,0)}}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ v\,=\,G(p,q,r)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ {\scriptstyle{(0\,=\,G(0,0,0))}},

with convergent series:

F\,=\,\sum_{i+j+k\geqslant 1}\,F_{i,j,k}\,x^{i}\,y^{j}\,z^{k},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ G\,=\,\sum_{i+j+k\geqslant 1}\,G_{i,j,k}\,p^{i}\,q^{j}\,r^{k}.

The concerned general linear transformation of
{\sf GL}(4,\mathbb{R})

maps the left hypersurface
\{u=F\}

to the right hypersurface
\{v=G\}

if and only if a
fundamental equation:

\displaystyle 0\,\equiv\,{\sf eqFG}(x,y,z),

(2.1)

holds identically in
\mathbb{R}\{x,y,z\}
, where:

\displaystyle{\sf eqFG}

\displaystyle:=\,-\,c_{1}\,x-c_{2}\,y-c_{2}\,z-d\,F(x,y,z)+G\Big(a_{1,1}x+a_{1,2}y+a_{1,3}z+b_{1}F(x,y,z),

\displaystyle a_{2,1}x+a_{2,2}y+a_{2,3}z+b_{2}F(x,y,z),

\displaystyle a_{3,1}x+a_{3,2}y+a_{3,3}z+b_{3}F(x,y,z)\Big),

so that:

0\,=\,{\sf eqFG}\,=\,\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathscr{N}_{i,j,k}\Big(a_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,b_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,c_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,d,\,\,F_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,G_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}}\Big)\,x^{i}\,y^{j}\,z^{k}.

For all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
, the coefficient of
x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}
in

{\sf eqFG}
can, in a standard way, be denoted as:

\displaystyle\big[x^{i}\,y^{j}\,z^{k}\big]{\sf eqFG}\,:=\,\mathscr{N}_{i,j,k}\,=\,0,

and we will indicate the corresponding indices

{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf i}
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf j}
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf k}
over the equal
sign:

0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf i,j,k}}{\,\,=\,\,}}\mathscr{N}_{i,j,k}.

We will proceed by increasing:

{\sf order}\,:=\,i+j+k.

Two simple affine transformations of
\mathbb{R}_{x,y,z,u}^{4}

and of
\mathbb{R}_{p,q,r,v}^{4}
erase
\ast\,x^{1}+\ast\,y^{1}+\ast\,z^{1}

and
\ast\,p^{1}+\ast\,q^{1}+\ast\,r^{1}
:

u\,=\,{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}+{\rm O}_{x,y,z}(2),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \begin{aligned} v\,=\,{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}+{\rm O}_{p,q,r}(2),\end{aligned}

so that order 1 terms are
normalized
to be
{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Stabilization of order 1 terms holds if and only if

0=c_{1}=c_{2}=c_{3}
:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&a_{1,3}&b_{1}\\
a_{2,1}&a_{2,2}&a_{2,3}&b_{2}\\
a_{3,1}&a_{3,2}&a_{3,3}&b_{3}\\
c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&d\end{array}\right]^{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 0}}\,\,\,\leadsto\,\,\,\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&a_{1,3}&b_{1}\\
a_{2,1}&a_{2,2}&a_{2,3}&b_{2}\\
a_{3,1}&a_{3,2}&a_{3,3}&b_{3}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&d\end{array}\right]^{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 1}}.

Proof.

Just read (
2.1
) at order 1:

0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 1,0,0}}{\,\,=\,\,}}-\,c_{1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 0,1,0}}{\,\,=\,\,}}-\,c_{2},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 0,0,1}}{\,\,=\,\,}}-\,c_{3}.\qed

Quadratic Normalizations and Isotropy Reductions

At order 2, a quadratic form
F_{2}
appears:

u\,=\,F_{2}+{\rm O}_{x,y,z}(3)\,=\,F_{2,0,0}\,x^{2}+F_{1,1,0}\,x\,y+F_{1,0,1}\,x\,z+F_{0,2,0}\,y^{2}+F_{0,1,1}\,y\,z+F_{0,0,2}\,z^{2}+\cdots,

At the origin,
the Hessian matrix is:

\left[\!\begin{array}[]{ccc}2F_{200}&F_{110}&F_{101}\\
F_{110}&2F_{020}&F_{011}\\
F_{101}&F_{011}&2F_{002}\end{array}\!\right]\,\,\,\sim\,\,\,\left[\!\begin{array}[]{ccc}2F_{200}&F_{110}&0\\
F_{110}&2F_{020}&0\\
0&0&0\end{array}\!\right],

and due to Hypothesis
1.1
,
after an elementary affine transformation
in the
(x,y,z)
-space,
only its upper-left
2\times 2
block is nonzero,
and of rank 2.

It is well known that, under the group

{\sf GL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ni\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\big)
, with
x\longmapsto\alpha\,x+\beta\,y
and
y\longmapsto\gamma\,x+\delta\,y
,
any quadratic form
F_{2,0}\,x^{2}+F_{1,1}\,xy+F_{0,2}\,y^{2}

of rank 2 can be normalized in two inequivalent ways as:

\begin{array}[]{cc}\,\,{\sf Hyperbolic}&\,\,{\sf Elliptic}\\
x\,y&x^{2}+y^{2}\end{array}

the quadratic form
x\,y
, of signature
(1,1)
,
being equivalent to
x^{2}-y^{2}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

At order 2, two nonequivalent branches exist:

\displaystyle\text{\bf Branch 2a}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ u

\displaystyle\,=\,x\,y\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \,+{\rm O}_{x,y,z}(3),

\displaystyle\text{\bf Branch 2b}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ u

\displaystyle\,=\,x^{2}+y^{2}+{\rm O}_{x,y,z}(3).

∎

Once a normalization has been made in the left
space
\mathbb{R}^{4}\ni(x,y,z,u)
,
always, it can also be made
exactly the same
in the right
space
(p,q,r,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
:

\displaystyle\text{\bf Branch 2a}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ v

\displaystyle=\,p\,q\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \,+{\rm O}_{p,q,r}(3),

\displaystyle\text{\bf Branch 2b}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ v

\displaystyle=\,p^{2}+q^{2}+{\rm O}_{p,q,r}(3).

Principle 3.2
.

At each order, every performed normalization will always
be instantly achieved on both hypersurfaces

\{u=F\}
and
\{v=G\}
.
∎

To terminate order 2, we must determine the group reductions
in the two branches.

Lemma 3.3
.

Stabilization of order 2 terms
holds with the group reductions:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&a_{1,3}&b_{1}\\
a_{2,1}&a_{2,2}&a_{2,3}&b_{2}\\
a_{3,1}&a_{3,2}&a_{3,3}&b_{3}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&d\end{array}\right]^{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 1}}\,\,\,\leadsto\,\,\,\begin{aligned} {}&{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2a:}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&b_{1}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&a_{2,2}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&b_{2}\\
a_{3,1}&a_{3,2}&a_{3,3}&b_{3}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}a_{1,1}a_{2,2}}\end{array}\right]^{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2}}\\
{}&{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2b:}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}-a_{2,1}}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&b_{1}\\
a_{2,1}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}a_{1,1}}&{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}&b_{2}\\
a_{3,1}&a_{3,2}&a_{3,3}&b_{3}\\

with nonzero determinants:

a_{1,1}^{2}\,a_{2,2}^{2}\,a_{3,3}\,\neq\,0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\,\neq\,\big(a_{1,1}^{2}+a_{2,1}^{2}\big)^{2}\,a_{3,3}.

Read
2.1
at order 2, for branch
{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2a}

and for branch
{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2b}

\begin{aligned} &0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 2,0,0}}{\,\,=\,\,}}a_{1,1}\,a_{2,1},\\
&0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 1,1,0}}{\,\,=\,\,}}a_{1,1}\,a_{2,2}+a_{1,2}\,a_{2,1}-d,\\
&0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 0,2,0}}{\,\,=\,\,}}a_{1,2}\,a_{2,2},\\

Infinitesimal Affine Transformations

A general affine vector field writes:

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\,\ \ \big(T_{1}+A_{1,1}\,x+A_{1,2}\,y+A_{1,3}\,z+B_{1}\,u\big)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x}

\displaystyle+\big(T_{2}+A_{2,1}\,x+A_{2,2}\,y+A_{2,3}\,z+B_{2}\,u\big)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

\displaystyle+\big(T_{3}+A_{3,1}\,x+A_{3,2}\,y+A_{3,3}\,z+B_{3}\,u\big)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

\displaystyle+\big(U_{0}+C_{1}\,x+C_{2}\,y+C_{3}\,z+D\,u\big)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial u}.

It is tangent to
\big\{u=F(x,y,z)\big\}

if and only if:

0\,\equiv\,{\sf eqLF}(x,y,z)\,=:\,L\big(-\,u+F(x,y,z)\big)\Big|_{u=F(x,y,z)},

identically as power series in
\mathbb{R}\{x,y,z\}
.
With increasing orders
\mu=0,1,2,3,\dots
,
this
{\sf eqLF}
may be expanded:

0\,=\,{\sf eqLF}\,=\,\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\,\,\sum_{i+j+k=\mu}\,\mathscr{V}_{i,j,k}\Big(T_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,U_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,A_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,B_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,C_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,D_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}},\,\,F_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}}}\Big)\,x^{i}\,y^{j}\,z^{k},

so that, for all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
:

0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf i,j,k}}{\,\,=\,\,}}\mathscr{V}_{i,j,k}.

Such a vector field
L
is tangent to:

u\,=\,{\color[rgb]{0.37,0,0}\bf 0}+{\rm O}_{x,y,z}(2),

if and only if:

0{\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0.37,0}\bf 0,0,0}}{\,\,=\,\,}}-\,U_{0}.

The key constraint of transitivity:

{\sf Span}\,\big(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x},\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y},\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\big)\,=\,T_{\sf origin}S\,=\,{\sf Span}\,L\big|_{\sf origin}\,=\,{\sf Span}\,\Big(T_{1}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}+T_{2}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}+T_{3}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\Big),

forces to always keep
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
,
T_{3}

absolutely free  —  never solved.

Introduction

Throughout this paper we consider finite, undirected and simple graphs. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set

V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
. An edge
e\in E(G)
with end vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
is denoted by

v_{i}v_{j}
. For
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, we denote by
d_{i}
the degree of the vertex
v_{i}
in
G
. As usual, let
P_{n}
,
S_{n}
and

C_{n}
be the path, the star and the cycle of order
n\geq 3
. Let
S_{n}+e
with
n\geq 3
denote the unicyclic graph obtained
from
S_{n}
by adding an edge. The spectral radius of a complex matrix
M
is the largest value among the modules of all eigenvalues of
M
. In the case that
M
is real symmetric, its spectral radius is exactly the largest eigenvalue and we denote it by
\displaystyle\rho(M)
. The spectral radius of the adjacency matrix

A(G)=(a_{i,j})
of
G
is referred as the spectral radius of
G
.

In 1975, a so-called Randić index was proposed
[
18
]
, it is
defined as

\displaystyle R_{-1}(G)=\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)}\frac{1}{d_{i}d_{j}}.

The Randić matrix
R(G)=(r_{i,j})
of
G
, where
\displaystyle r_{i,j}=\frac{1}{d_{i}d_{j}}
if
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
and 0 otherwise, seems to be
first time used in 2005 by Rodríguez, who referred to it as the “weighted adjacency matrix”
[
19
]
and
the “degree adjacency matrix”
[
20
]
. Moreover, the role of Randić matrix in the Laplacian theory was
clarified in
[
3
,
2
]
. The Randić spectral radius was studied in
[
14
,
17
,
3
,
2
]
and the references cited therein.

In 1994, Yang et al.
[
24
]
proposed the extended matrix of
G
, denoted by
A_{ex}(G)=(c_{i,j})
, where

\displaystyle c_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{d_{i}}{d_{j}}+\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}\Big)
if
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
and
0
otherwise.
The corresponding topological index is the symmetric division deg index, which is proposed by Vukičević
[
22
]

and
written as

\displaystyle SDD(G)=\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)}\Big(\frac{d_{i}}{d_{j}}+\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}\Big).

The extended spectral radius were studied in
[
1
,
9
,
23
]
and the references cited therein.

In 1998, Estrada, Torres, Rodríguez and Gutman
[
11
]
introduced the atom-bond connectivity
(ABC)
index, defined as

\displaystyle ABC(G)=\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)}\sqrt{\frac{d_{i}+d_{j}-2}{d_{i}d_{j}}}.

The ABC matrix of G, put forward by Estrada
[
10
]
, is defined to be
\Omega(G)=(\omega_{i,j})
, where

\displaystyle\omega_{i,j}=\sqrt{\frac{d_{i}+d_{j}-2}{d_{i}d_{j}}}
if
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
and
0
otherwise. Chen
[
7
]
showed
that for
any tree
T
of order
n\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{n+1}\leq\rho(\Omega(T))\leq\sqrt{n-2},

with left (right, respectively) equality if and only if
T\cong P_{n}
(
T\cong S_{n}
, respectively), which was conjectured
in
[
6
]
. Recently, Li and Wang
[
16
]
proved that for an unicyclic graph
G
of order
n\geq 4
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}=\rho(\Omega(C_{n}))\leq\rho(\Omega(G))\leq\rho(\Omega(S_{n}+e)),

with equality if and only if
T\cong C_{n}
for the lower bound, and if and only if
T\cong S_{n}+e
for the upper bound,
which was conjectured in
[
12
]
. Yuan and Du characterized the bicyclic graphs with the largest and second largest
ABC
spectral radius, which are just the unique two bicyclic graphs of order
n
with maximum degree
n-1
for
n\geq 7
[
25
]
.

In 2015, Shegehall and Kanabur
[
21
]
proposed the arithmetic-geometric index of
G
, which is defined as

\displaystyle AG(G)=\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)}\frac{d_{i}+d_{j}}{2\sqrt{d_{i}d_{j}}}.

Recently, Zheng et al.
[
26
]
considered the arithmetic-geometric matrix (AG matrix) of
G
, denoted by

A_{ag}(G)=(h_{i,j})
,
where
\displaystyle h_{i,j}=\frac{d_{i}+d_{j}}{2\sqrt{d_{i}d_{j}}}
if
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
and 0 otherwise. We denote
\rho(A_{ag}(G))
by
\rho_{ag}(G)
, and call it
the arithmetic-geometric spectral radius (
AG
spectral radius) of
G
. Moreover, Zheng et al.
[
26
]
gave several bounds for
the AG spectral radius in terms of the maximum degree, the minimum degree of
G
, the Randić index
\displaystyle R_{-1}(G)
and the first Zagreb index
M_{1}(G)=\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E}(d_{i}+d_{j})
. Guo and Gao
[
13
]
offered several other
bounds for the
AG
spectral radius as well.

In this paper, we consider the
AG
spectral radius of trees and unicyclic graphs. We prove that:
(1) for any tree
T
of order
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle 2\cos\frac{\pi}{n+1}\leq\rho_{ag}(P_{n})\leq\rho_{ag}(T)\leq\rho_{ag}(S_{n})=\frac{n}{2},

with equality if and only if
T\cong P_{n}
for the lower bound, and if and only if
T\cong S_{n}
for the upper
bound.

(2) for any unicyclic graph
G
of order
n\geq 3
,

\displaystyle 2=\rho_{ag}(C_{n})\leq\rho_{ag}(G)\leq\rho_{ag}(S_{n}+e),

the lower (upper, respectively) bound is attained if and only if
T\cong C_{n}
(
T\cong S_{n}+e
, respectively) and

\displaystyle\rho_{ag}(S_{n}+e)<\frac{n}{2}
for
n\geq 7
.

Abstract
Motivated by Andrews’ partitions with initial repetitions, we derive parity formulas for several functions for this class of partitions. In many cases, we present an infinite family of Ramanujan-like congruences modulo 2.



Modulo 2 congruences for partitions with initial repetitions

1
Introduction

A partition of
n
is a representation
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}
where
\lambda_{i}
’s are positive integers with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}\geq 1
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}=n
. The summand
\lambda_{i}
is called a part of
\lambda
, and the number of times this summand appears is called its multiplicity. Mostly, we use the multiplicity notation in which different parts appear with their multiplicities. We may write

\lambda=(\mu_{1}^{m_{1}},\mu_{2}^{m_{2}},\ldots,\mu_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}})
in which
m_{i}
is the multiplicity of the part
\mu_{i}
and
\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\cdots>\mu_{\ell}
.
At times, restrictions are made on
\mu_{i}
’s and
m_{i}
’s. In this case, we are said to have restricted partitions which induce restricted partition functions. For more examples on this subject, one can refer to
[
4
]
. One interesting example is the case of partitions with initial repetitions, introduced by George Andrews
[
1
]
. Andrews defined a partition with initial
k
-repetitions as one in which if
j
appears at least
k
times, all positive integers less than
j
appear at least
k
times. This definition entails that all parts greater than
j
have their multiplicites strictly less than
k
. Using generating functions, Andrews proved that the number of partitions of
n
with initial
k
-repetitions is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into parts with multiplicies not more than
2k-1
. A bijective proof of this identity was established by W. Keith
[
2
]
, and later, a simpler version thereof was given in
[
3
]
.

Much of the work in this area has dwelt on partition identities and combinatorial bijections. In this paper, we study a class of partitions with initial repetitions with respect to parity. We derive modulo 2 congruences for partition functions associated with partitions in this category. We recall the following notation:

For
a,q\in\mathbb{C}
and
n
a positive integer,
(a;q)_{n}=(1-q)(1-aq)(1-aq^{2})\cdots(1-aq^{n-1})
and

(a;q)_{0}=1
. If
|q|<1
, we have
(q;q)_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n})
and so

(a;q)_{n}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}}.

Throughout our discussion, we assume that
|q|<1
. Some of the
q
-identities which will be useful include:

\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}z^{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})(1+zq^{n})(1+z^{-1}q^{n-1})

(1.1)

for
z\neq 0
.
Other useful
q
-identities:

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{n}}{1+q^{n}},

(1.2)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}-n}z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}(z;q)_{n}}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-zq^{n})^{-1},

(1.3)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(2n+1)q^{n(n+1)/2}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{3}.

(1.4)

The results in this paper arise by examining the following identities of Rogers-Ramanujan type due to Slater (see
[
5
]
):

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n+1)}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{4n})(1-q^{4n-1})(1-q^{4n-3}),

(1.5)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{5n})(1-q^{5n-2})(1-q^{5n-3}),

(1.6)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{2n})}{(1-q^{2n-1})}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{6n})(1-q^{6n-2})(1-q^{6n-4}),

(1.7)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q^{2})_{n+1}(q^{4};q^{4})_{n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{6n})(1+q^{6n-1})(1+q^{6n-5}),

(1.8)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{2n})}{(1+q^{2n-1})}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q)_{2n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{6n})(1+q^{6n-2})(1+q^{6n-4}),

(1.9)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q;q)_{2n+1}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{7n})(1-q^{7n-1})(1-q^{7n-6}),

(1.10)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q;q)_{2n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{7n})(1-q^{7n-2})(1-q^{7n-5}),

(1.11)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q;q)_{2n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{7n})(1-q^{7n-3})(1-q^{7n-4}),

(1.12)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{2n})}{(1+q^{2n+1})}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{8n})(1-q^{8n-3})(1-q^{8n-5}),

(1.13)

\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n+2)}(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q)_{2n+1}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{12n})(1-q^{12n-2})(1-q^{12n-10}).

(1.14)

We present our results in Section 2.

2
Modulo 2 congruences

Unless otherwise specified, all congruence equations involving
q
-series are taken modulo 2. So the statement
a\equiv b
, where
a
and
b
are two expressions involving
q
-series, shall mean
a\equiv b\pmod{2}
. We start by investigating a variation of Andrews’ partitions with initial 2-repetitions as follows:

Let
c_{1}(n)
be the number of partitions of
n
in which either

(a)

all parts are distinct

or

(b)

there is an odd repeated part
2j-1
and all positive integers less than
2j-1
appear as repeated parts, any part greater than
2j
is distinct.

Then, we do get the following parity formula for
c_{1}(n)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{1}(n)\equiv 1\pmod{2}\iff n=j(j+1)/2,j\geq 0.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{1}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{1+1+2+2+\ldots+(2n-1)+(2n-1)}}{(q;q)_{2n}}\prod\limits_{j=2n+1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{1+1+2+2+\ldots+(2n-1)+(2n-1)}}{(q;q)_{2n}}\prod\limits_{j=2n+1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n^{2}-2n}}{(q;q)_{2n}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2n}(1+q^{j})}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n^{2}-2n}}{(q;q)_{2n}(-q;q)_{2n}}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n^{2}-2n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{2n}}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n^{2}-2n}}{(q^{4};q^{4})_{n}(q^{2};q^{4})_{n}}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\prod\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2+4n}}\,\,\,\,\,\,(z=q^{2},q:=q^{4}\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\eqref{cauchy})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\equiv(q;q)^{3}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}.

∎

Let
c_{2}(n)
be the number of partitions of
n
in which there exists
j\geq 1
such that
j
appears exactly
j
times and it is the only part less than
2j+1
, even parts
\geq 2j+2
are distinct, odd parts
\geq 2j+1
appear unrestricted. Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{2}(5n+2)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{2}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{n^{2}}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{n^{2}}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{n^{2}}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}+(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{n^{2}}(-q;q^{2})_{n}(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}+(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{n^{2}}(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(2n+1)q^{n(n+1)/2}

\displaystyle\equiv\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{8n-3}\right)\left(1-q^{8n-5}\right)\left(1-q^{8n}\right)+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad(\text{by}\,\,\eqref{eq36}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\eqref{cube})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{4n^{2}+n}+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}\,\,\,\,(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{jacobi}).

Since there is no integer
n
such that
4n^{2}+n\equiv 2\pmod{5}
or
n(n+1)/2\equiv 2\pmod{5}
, it must follow that

c_{2}(5n+2)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.
∎

For instance, there are two partitions of 7 enumerated by
c_{2}(7)
. These are:
(6,1)
and
(3^{2},1)
. Thus
c_{2}(7)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.

Let
c_{3}(n)
be the number of partitions of
n
in which, there is a positive integer
j
such that 1 appears with multiplicities
j^{2}
or
j^{2}+1
, odd parts
>1
are distinct, all even parts are distinct and those
>2j
are at least
4j+4
in size and divisible by 4, no even integer in the set
\{2j+2,2j+4,\ldots,4j+2\}
appears as a part. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{3}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{1+1+\cdots+1(n^{2}\,\text{times})}(1+q)(-q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q^{4n+4};q^{4})_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(1+q)(-q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(-q;q)_{\infty}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}-\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\equiv(q^{2};q^{6})_{\infty}(q^{4};q^{6})_{\infty}(q^{6};q^{6})_{\infty}+\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\,\,\,(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{eq23})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}\,\,\,\,\,(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{jacobi}).

Note that, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
n(3n+1)\equiv 0,2,4\pmod{5}
and for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
n(n+1)/2\equiv 0,1,3\pmod{5}
. More specifically, we have

n(3n+1)\equiv 0\pmod{5}\iff n\equiv 0,3\pmod{5}
,
n(3n+1)\equiv 2\pmod{5}\iff n\equiv 4\pmod{5}
,
n(3n+1)\equiv 4\pmod{5}\iff n\equiv 1,2\pmod{5}
,
n(n+1)/2\equiv 0\pmod{5}\iff n\equiv 0,4\pmod{5}
,
n(n+1)/2\equiv 1\pmod{5}\iff\,\,\,n\equiv 1,3\pmod{5}
and
n(n+1)/2\equiv 3\pmod{5}\,\,\iff\,\,n\equiv 2\pmod{5}
.

Hence

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{3}(5n+1)q^{5n+1}\equiv\sum_{n\geq 1,n\equiv 1,3\pmod{5}}q^{n(n+1)/2}\pmod{2},

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{3}(5n+2)q^{5n+2}\equiv\sum_{n\equiv 4\pmod{5}}q^{n(3n+1)}=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{(5n+4)(15n+13)}\pmod{2},

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{3}(5n+3)q^{5n+3}\equiv\sum_{n>1,n\equiv 2\pmod{5}}q^{n(n+1)/2}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{(5n+2)(5n+3)/2}

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{3}(5n+4)q^{5n+4}\equiv\sum_{n\equiv 1,2\pmod{5}}q^{n(3n+1)}

so that we have the following.

Theorem 2.3
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{3}(5n+1)\equiv 1\pmod{2}\,\,\,\,\text{iff}\,\,\,\,n=\frac{j(j+1)-2}{10},\,\,\,j\geq 1\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,j\equiv 1,3\pmod{5},

c_{3}(5n+2)\equiv 1\pmod{2}\,\,\,\,\text{iff}\,\,\,\,n=\frac{(5j+4)(15j+13)-2}{5},\,\,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

c_{3}(5n+3)\equiv 1\pmod{2}\,\,\,\,\text{iff}\,\,\,\,n=\frac{(5j+2)(5j+3)-6}{10},\,\,j\geq 0,

c_{3}(5n+4)\equiv 1\pmod{2}\,\,\,\,\text{iff}\,\,\,\,n=\frac{j(3j+1)-4}{5},\,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,j\equiv 1,2\pmod{5}.

Recall that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{3}(n)q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}.

Observe that none of the exponents
n(3n+1)
or
n(n+1)/2
is congruent to 5, 7, 9 modulo 11. Thus, we have:

Theorem 2.4
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{3}(11n+5)\equiv 0\pmod{2},

c_{3}(11n+7)\equiv 0\pmod{2},

c_{3}(11n+9)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Let
c_{4}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
in which either

(a)

all parts are even and distinct

or

(b)

there is an even part
2j
which appears twice, all positive even integers
<2j
appear twice, any even part larger than
2j
is actually
\geq 4j+2
and distinct, odd parts are distinct and at most
2j-1
in part size.

Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{4}(n)\equiv\begin{cases}1\pmod{2},&n=(7j^{2}+3j)/2,j\in\mathbb{Z};\\
\\
0\pmod{2},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{4}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}+\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{2+2+4+4+\cdots+2n+2n}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(1+q^{2i-1})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{4n+2j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n(n+1)}(-q;q^{2})_{n}(-q^{4n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n(n+1)}(q;q^{2})_{n}(q^{4n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n(n+1)}(q;q^{2})_{n}\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{2n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{2n}}(q^{4n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n(n+1)}(q;q^{2})_{n}\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{2n}}

\displaystyle=(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n(n+1)}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q)_{2n}(-q;q)_{2n}}

\displaystyle=(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q;q)_{2n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{7n})(1+q^{7n-2})(1+q^{7n-5})\quad\,\,\,(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{eq32})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{(7n^{2}+3n)/2}.

∎

Let
c_{5}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
in which either

(a)

all parts are even and distinct

or

(b)

odd parts appear twice or thrice and are gap-free, even parts are distinct and the smallest even part is
\geq
2(the largest odd part) + 4.

Then parity of
c_{5}(n)
can be deduced from the following.

Theorem 2.6
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{5}(n)\equiv\begin{cases}1\pmod{2},&n=(7j^{2}+j)/2,j\in\mathbb{Z};\\
\\
0\pmod{2},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

It is clear that

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}c_{5}(n)q^{n}=(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}q^{1+1+3+3+\ldots+2n-1}(-q;q^{2})_{n}(-q^{4n+2};q^{2})_{\infty},

and by a similar manipulation as in Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}c_{5}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q;q)_{2n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{7n})(1+q^{7n-3})(1+q^{7n-4})\quad(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{eq33})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{(7n^{2}+n)/2}.

∎

Let
c_{6}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
in which either

(a)

1 is the only odd integer that may appear and even parts are distinct

or

(b)

there is an even part
2j
that appears twice, all positive even integers
<2j
appear twice, any even part larger than
2j
is actually
\geq 4j+2
and distinct, odd parts are
\leq 2j+1
and those
\leq 2j-1
are distinct.

We have:

Theorem 2.7
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{6}(n)\equiv\begin{cases}1\pmod{2},&n=(7j^{2}+5j)/2,j\in\mathbb{Z};\\
\\
0\pmod{2},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The generating function for the partition function in question is

\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{6}(n)q^{n}=\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{1-q}+\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{2+2+4+4+\ldots 2n+2n}\frac{(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{1-q^{2n+1}}(-q^{4n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}.

However, by a similar manipulation as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{6}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(-q;q)_{2n+1}}\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{7n})(1+q^{7n-1})(1+q^{7n-6})\quad(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{eq31})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{(7n^{2}+5n)/2}.

∎

Let
c_{7}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
in which either

(a)

even parts are
\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and distinct, and 1 is the only odd integer that may appear

or

(b)

the largest even part
2j
appears exactly twice if
2j\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, and appears twice or thrice if
2j\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, all positive even integers
<2j
and
\equiv 0\pmod{4}
are repeated exactly twice, and those
<2j
and
\equiv 2\pmod{4}
appear twice or thrice, any even part larger than
2j
that is divisible by 4 is actually at least
4j+4
in part size and distinct, even parts that are
>2j
and
\equiv 2\pmod{4}
are distinct and
2j+1
is the only odd integer that may appear.

The following result follows:

Theorem 2.8
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{7}(n)\equiv\begin{cases}1\pmod{2},&n=3j^{2}+2j,j\in\mathbb{Z};\\
\\
0\pmod{2},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}c_{7}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}}{1-q}+(-q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2(2+4+6+\cdots+2n)}}{1-q^{2n+1}}(-q^{4n+4};q^{4})_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{1-q^{2n+1}}(q^{4n+4};q^{4})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{1-q^{2n+1}}(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q^{4};q^{4})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(1-q^{2n+1})(q^{4};q^{4})_{n}}(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(1-q^{2n+1})}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q^{2})_{n}(q^{4};q^{4})_{n}}(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(q;q^{2})_{n+1}}\frac{(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{4};q^{4})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{6n})(1+q^{6n-5})(1+q^{6n-1})\,\,\,(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{eq27})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{3n^{2}+2n}.

∎

Let
c_{8}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
in which either

(a)

all parts are even and distinct

or

(b)

there is the largest odd part
2j-1
which appears once, all positive odd integers
\leq j
appear once or twice , all positive odd integers
>j
appear once, even parts

\leq j
are distinct, and those
>j
are distinct and actually
\geq 2j+2
in size.

We obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

c_{8}(49n+r)\equiv 0\pmod{2},

where
r=6,20,27,34,41,48.

Proof.

It is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{8}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}+\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{1+3+5+\ldots+2n-1}(1+q)(1+q^{2})\ldots(1+q^{n})(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n^{2}}(-q;q)_{n}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n^{2}}\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}(-q;q)_{n}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n^{2}}\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv(q;q)_{\infty}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{5n})(1+q^{5n-2})(1+q^{5n-3})\,\,\,(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{eq18})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)/2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(5n+1)/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(q^{n(6n+1)}+q^{(2n+1)(3n+2)}\right)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(q^{n(10n+1)}+q^{(2n+1)(5n+3)}\right).

Since the exponents in
\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(q^{n(6n+1)}+q^{(2n+1)(3n+2)}\right)
are congruent to

0,1,2,5,7,8,12,14,15,19,21,22,26,28,29,33,35,36,40,42,43,47

modulo 49 and the exponents in
\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(q^{n(10n+1)}+q^{(2n+1)(5n+3)}\right)
are congruent to

0,2,3,7,9,10,11,14,16,17,21,23,24,28,30,31,35,37,38,42,44,45

modulo 49, it follows that the product

\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(q^{n(6n+1)}+q^{(2n+1)(3n+2)}\right)\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(q^{n(10n+1)}+q^{(2n+1)(5n+3)}\right)
has no exponent congruent to 6, 20, 27, 34, 41, 48 modulo 49. Thus

\sum_{n\geq 0}^{\infty}c_{8}(49n+r)q^{49n+r}\equiv 0\pmod{2}

where
r=6,20,27,34,41,48
.
∎

.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{9}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=(-q^{2};q)_{\infty}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(-q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv(1+2q)(-q^{2};q)_{\infty}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(-q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(1+q)(-q^{2};q)_{\infty}+q(-q^{2};q)_{\infty}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(-q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}+q(-q^{2};q)_{\infty}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(-q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(-q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}(q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{n^{2}}(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=(q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+q^{5n-2}\right)\left(1+q^{5n-3}\right)\left(1-q^{5n}\right)\,\,\,(\text{by}\,\,\,\,\eqref{eq18})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\frac{5n^{2}+n}{2}}.

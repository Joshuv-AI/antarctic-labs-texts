1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Given
N\geq 2
and
p\in(1,N)
, denote the homogeneous Sobolev space
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The Sobolev inequality states as

(1.1)

\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\geq\mathcal{S}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

for some
\mathcal{S}>0
, where
p^{*}:=\frac{pN}{N-p}
.
It is well known that Aubin
[
1
]
and Talenti
[
33
]
found the optimal constant and the extremals for inequality (
1.1
). Indeed, equality is achieved precisely by the functions (up to scalar multiplications)

(1.2)

V_{\lambda,z}(x):=\lambda^{\frac{N-p}{p}}V(\lambda(x-z)),\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\lambda>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where

V(x)=\gamma_{N,p}(1+|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}})^{-\frac{N-p}{p}},\quad\mbox{for some constant}\quad\gamma_{N,p}>0,

which solve the related Sobolev critical equation

(1.3)

-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=u^{p^{*}-1},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

All the solutions to equation (
1.3
) are indeed the only ones of (
1.2
). Caffarelli et al.
[
9
]
proved the claim when
p=2
without the restricted condition
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The case
p\neq 2
has been firstly solved by Guedda and Véron
[
18
]
for the radial case, where the authors classified all the positive radial solutions and successively by Damascelli et al.
[
12
]
when
\frac{2N}{N+2}\leq p<2
, by Damascelli and Ramaswamy
[
13
]
and Vétois
[
34
]
when
1<p<2
and finally by Sciunzi
[
28
]
for the remaining case, namely when
2<p<N
. And recently, Catino et al.
[
10
]
and Ou
[
25
]
proved the claim when
p\neq 2
without the restricted condition
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some special cases.

Pistoia and Vaira
[
26
]
proved that the solution
V
of equation (
1.3
) is non-degenerate in the sense that all solutions of equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle-{\rm div}(|\nabla V|^{p-2}\nabla\varphi)-(p-2){\rm div}(|\nabla V|^{p-4}(\nabla V\cdot\nabla\varphi)\nabla V)=\left(p^{*}-1\right)V^{p^{*}-2}\varphi,

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, are linear combination of the functions

Z_{0}(x)=\frac{N-p}{p}V+x\cdot\nabla V,\quad Z_{i}(x)=\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{i}},\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

Here
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the weighted Sobolev space which is defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla V|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We note that Pistoia and Vaira
[
26
]
obtained the above conclusion by proving that
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously, where
L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the set of measurable functions
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
whose norm

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{p^{*}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore, Figalli and Neumayer
[
15
]
proved that
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
compactly when
2\leq p<N
then they showed the solutions of (
1.4
) in
L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are linear combination of the functions

Z_{0}
and
Z_{i}

(i=1,\ldots,N)
. Figalli and Zhang
[
16
]
proved that
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
compactly for all
1<p<N
and the non-degenerate conclusion in
[
15
]
also holds.

Now, let us consider the stability of inequality (
1.1
). For
p=2
, Brezis and Lieb in
[
4
]
asked the question whether a remainder term - proportional to the quadratic distance of the function
u
to be the manifold
\mathcal{M}_{0}:=\{cV_{\lambda,z}:c\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0,z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}
- can be added to the right hand side of (
1.1
). This question was answered affirmatively by Bianchi and Egnell
[
3
]
by using spectral estimate combined with Lions’ concentration and compactness principle (see
[
21
]
), which reads that there exists constant
c_{\mathrm{BE}}>0
such that

(1.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\mathcal{S}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\geq c_{\mathrm{BE}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

After that, this result was extended later to the biharmonic case by Lu and Wei
[
22
]
and the case of arbitrary high order in
[
5
]
, and the whole fractional order case was proved in
[
6
]
.
Furthermore, Rădulescu et. al
[
27
]
gave the remainder terms of Hardy-Sobolev inequality for exponent two.
Wang and Willem
[
35
]
studied Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities (see
[
8
]
) with Lebesgue-type remainder terms, see also
[
2
,
14
,
31
]
for remainder terms of weighted Hardy inequalities.
Recently, Wei and Wu
[
36
]
established the stability of the profile decompositions to a special case of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality and also gave the gradient type remainder term. It is worth to mention some recent works, Dolbeault et al.
[
11
]
obtained the lower bound estimate for sharp constant
c_{\mathrm{BE}}
obtained in (
1.6
), and König
[
19
]
gave its upper bound that is
c_{\mathrm{BE}}<4/(N+4)
, and König
[
20
]
proved that
c_{\mathrm{BE}}<2-2^{\frac{N-2}{N}}
and there exist a minimizer for
c_{\mathrm{BE}}
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

While for
p\neq 2
, it needs much delicate analysis to deal with the stability of inequality (
1.1
). Cianchi et al.
[
7
]
first proved a stability version of Lebesgue-type for every
1<p<N
, Figalli and Neumayer
[
15
]
proved the gradient stability for the Sobolev inequality when
p\geq 2
, Neumayer
[
24
]
extended the result in
[
15
]
to all
1<p<N
. It is worth to mention that very recently, Figalli and Zhang
[
16
]
obtained the sharp stability of critical points of the Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) for all
1<p<N
which reads

\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}-\mathcal{S}^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq c_{\mathrm{FZ}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\right)^{\max\{2,p\}},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

for some constant
c_{\mathrm{FZ}}>0
,
furthermore the exponent
\max\{2,p\}
is sharp, and this can be understood as a weak form of Bianchi-Egnell type

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\mathcal{S}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\geq c_{\mathrm{FZ}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\max\{2,p\}}.

1.2.
Problem setup and main results

In present paper, we extend the works of Figalli and Zhang
[
16
]
to a weighted case, and Rădulescu et. al
[
27
]
to
p
-Laplace case, that is,
we consider the gradient stability of the following Hardy-Sobolev inequality

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\geq\mathcal{S}_{\beta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

for some constant
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}>0
, where

1<p<N,\quad 0<\beta<p,\quad p^{*}_{\beta}=\frac{p(N-\beta)}{N-p}.

In
[
17
]
, Ghoussoub and Yuan gave the optimal constant
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
and proved that the equality is achieved precisely by the functions (up to scalar multiplications):

(1.8)

U_{\lambda}(x):=\frac{C_{N,p,\beta}\lambda^{\frac{N-p}{p}}}{(1+\lambda^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-p}{p-\beta}}},\quad\mbox{where}\quad C_{N,p,\beta}=\left[(N-\beta)\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\frac{N-p}{p(p-\beta)}},

for some
\lambda>0
.
Moreover the functions
U_{\lambda}
are the only radial solutions (up to scalings) of

-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\frac{u^{p^{*}_{\beta}-1}}{|x|^{\beta}},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Inspired by the work of Pistoia and Vaira
[
26
]
, our first result concerns the non-degeneracy property of the solution
U:=U_{1}
in suitable space. Let us
denote the weighted Sobolev space
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the set of measurable functions with respect to the norm

(1.9)

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Denote also
C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the space of compactly supported functions of class
C^{1}
that are zero in a neighborhood of the origin, then we define the weighted Sobolev space
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the completion of
C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

(1.10)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and note that this norm is equivalent to (
1.5
) replacing
V
by
U
.

Remark 1.1
.

As stated in
[
16
, Remark 3.1]
, it is important for us to consider weighted that are not necessarily integrable at the origin, since
|\nabla U|^{p-2}\sim|x|^{\frac{(p-2)(1-\beta)}{p-1}}\not\in L^{1}(B_{1})
for
p\leq\frac{N+2(1-\beta)}{N+1-\beta}
. This is why, when defining weighted Sobolev spaces, we consider the space
C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so that gradients vanish near zero. Of course, replacing
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by
C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
plays no role in the case
p>\frac{N+2(1-\beta)}{N+1-\beta}
.

We first give a crucial compact embedding result as follows.

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose
1<p<N
and
0<\beta<p
. The space
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
compactly embeds into
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Based on above compact embedding theorem, we will prove that
U
is non-degenerate in
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This leads to study the problem:

(1.11)

\displaystyle-{\rm div}(|\nabla U|^{p-2}\nabla v)-(p-2){\rm div}(|\nabla U|^{p-4}(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)\nabla U)=(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}v,

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
v\in L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
1<p<N
and
0<\beta<p
. Then the space of solutions of problem (
1.11
) has dimension
1
and is spanned by
(\frac{N-p}{p}U+x\cdot\nabla U)
.

Next is the main result of the present paper, which is the gradient stability of inequality (
1.7
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
1<p<N
and
0<\beta<p
. Then there exists constant
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(N,p,\beta)>0
such that for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

(1.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\mathcal{S}_{\beta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}\geq\mathcal{B}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\gamma},

where
\gamma={\max\{p,2\}}
,
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}:=\{cU_{\lambda}:c\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0\}
is the set of extremal functions for Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.7
).

Remark 1.5
.

By using perturbation methods as those in
[
16
]
, it is easy to verify that the exponent
\gamma
in Theorem
1.4
is sharp.

In fact, if
1<p<2
, let us fix
U:=U_{1}
and consider first
u_{n}(x):=\left(\frac{|x|}{|A_{n}x|}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}U(A_{n}x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and
u_{n}(0):=U(0)
, where
A_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
denotes the diagonal matrix

A_{n}=\mathrm{diag}\left(1,\ldots,1,1+\frac{1}{n}\right).

Then it is not difficult to check that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\geq(1+n^{-1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+C_{1}(N,p,\beta)n^{-2}

and

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}=(1+n^{-1})^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}\leq(1+n^{-1})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}},

for
n
sufficiently large, thus the left hand side of (
1.12
) behaves as
n^{-2}
. While the right side of (
1.12
) behaves as
n^{-\gamma}
, that is,

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\gamma}\sim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla(U(A_{n}x)-U(x))|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\bar{x}_{N}}\bar{x}_{N}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle n^{-\gamma},

hence (
1.12
) cannot hold with
\gamma<2
.

On the other hand, if
p\geq 2
, let us fix
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
a nontrivial function, here
B_{1}=B(\mathbf{0},1)
denotes the unit ball centered at the origin, and consider now

\tilde{u}_{n}(x):=\left(\frac{|x|}{|x_{n}+x|}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}\left(U(x)+\varphi(x_{n}+x)\right),

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{-x_{n}\}
, and
\tilde{u}_{n}(-x_{n}):=U(-x_{n})+\varphi(0)
,
where
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a sequence of points satisfying
|x_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. One can check that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+r_{n,1}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x+r_{n,2}

with
|r_{n,1}|+|r_{n,2}|\leq C(|\nabla U(x_{n})|+U(x_{n}))\leq CU(x_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Hence, choosing a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
such that
U(x_{n})\ll\varepsilon_{n}\ll 1
, the functions

\hat{u}_{n}(x):=\left(\frac{|x|}{|x_{n}+x|}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}\left(U(x)+\varepsilon_{n}\varphi(x_{n}+x)\right)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{-x_{n}\}
, and
\hat{u}_{n}(-x_{n}):=U(-x_{n})+\varepsilon_{n}\varphi(0)

satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\hat{u}_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon_{n}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+o(\varepsilon_{n}^{p})

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\hat{u}_{n}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon_{n}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x+o(\varepsilon_{n}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}).

Thanks to these facts, one easily deduces that the left hand side of (
1.12
) behaves as
\varepsilon_{n}^{p}
, while the right hand side of (
1.12
) behaves as
\varepsilon^{\gamma}_{n}
. Hence (
1.12
) cannot hold with
\gamma<p
.

Remark 1.6
.

In this paper, to handle the general case
1<p<N
with
0<\beta<p
and obtain the remainder terms, as stated in
[
16
]
, we need to consider three cases
1<p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
,
\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}<p<2
, and
2\leq p<N
by using different arguments.
In fact, note that
p^{*}_{\beta}=\frac{p(N-\beta)}{N-p}\leq 2
implies
p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
,
p^{*}_{\beta}>2
implies
p>\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
, respectively. Moreover,
\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}<2
is equivalent to
\beta<2
. Therefore, when
1<p<2
, we will split this problem into two cases:

(i)

1<p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
;

(ii)

\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}<p<2
.

The reason why we consider the above two cases is that, it needs some appropriate algebraic inequalities which requires to compare
p
and
p^{*}_{\beta}
with
2
, see Lemmas
A.1
and
A.2
. However,
2\leq p<N
implies
p^{*}_{\beta}>2
, and the
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm is stronger than any weighted
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm (see (
1.9
)), so that we can deal with this case directly.

2.
Compact embedding theorem

We first establish the following Poincaré type inequalities which will be useful later.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<p<N
and
0<\beta<p
. Then there exists
C=C(N,p,\beta)>0
such that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Also, there exists
\vartheta=\vartheta(N,p,\beta)>0
such that, for any
\rho\in(0,1)
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{C}{\rho^{\vartheta}}\int_{B_{\rho}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq C|\log\rho|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\rho}}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

To prove (
2.1
), we can assume by approximation that
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as in
[
15
, Lemma 4.2]
. Define

\mathfrak{F}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\mathcal{S}_{\beta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}}.

We know
U
is a local minimum of the functional
\mathfrak{F}
, then

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d\epsilon^{2}}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\mathfrak{F}(U+\epsilon\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+p(p-2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-4}(\nabla U\cdot\nabla\varphi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\mathcal{S}_{\beta}\Bigg[p\left(\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}-1\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}-2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}U\varphi\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+p(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg].

Noting that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-4}(\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}-2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}U\varphi\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\geq 0,

these imply that

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\mathcal{S}_{\beta}p(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\beta}}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-p(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

due to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x=\mathcal{S}_{\beta}^{\frac{p^{*}_{\beta}}{p^{*}_{\beta}-p}}.

Thus (
2.1
) holds.

To prove (
2.2
), we can also assume by approximation that
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and we apply the Sobolev inequality with radial weights. More precisely, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq

\displaystyle c\left(\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{-\frac{q\beta}{2}}|\varphi|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}},

where
q=q(N,p,\beta)>2
.
In fact, when
0<\beta\leq 1
,
|\nabla U|^{p-2}\geq c
inside
B_{1}
, it follows by Hardy-Sobolev inequality (see
[
17
]
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq c_{1}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq c_{2}\left(\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{-\frac{q\beta}{2}}|\varphi|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}},

where
q=q(N,p,\beta)=\frac{2N}{N-2+\beta}>2
, thus (
2.4
) holds. Otherwise, if
1<\beta<p
,
|\nabla U|^{p-2}\geq c|x|^{\frac{(2-p)(\beta-1)}{p-1}}
inside
B_{1}
, then applying (
2.1
) and the Sobolev inequality with radial weights (see
[
23
, Section 2.1]
) we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq

\displaystyle c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}+|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{2}\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{-\beta}\left(\varphi^{2}+|x|^{1+\frac{\beta-1}{p-1}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{2}\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{-\beta}\left(\varphi^{2}+|x|^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{3}\int_{B_{1}}\left(\left(|x|^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\varphi\right)^{2}+|x|^{2}\left|\nabla\left(|x|^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\varphi\right)\right|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{4}\left(\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{-\frac{q\beta}{2}}|\varphi|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}},

where
q=q(N)>2
, then (
2.4
) holds.
Therefore, by Hölder inequality, for any
\rho\in(0,1)
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{B_{\rho}}|x|^{-\beta}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\rho^{N(1-\frac{q}{2})}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}|x|^{-\frac{q\beta}{2}}|\varphi|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\rho^{N(1-\frac{q}{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

as desired.

To prove (
2.3
), we define

\displaystyle\eta_{\rho}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&{\rm for}\ \ |x|<\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}},\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{2\log|x|-|\log\rho|}{|\log\rho|},&{\rm for}\ \ \rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq|x|\leq\rho^{-1},\\[5.69054pt]
1,&{\rm for}\ \ |x|>\rho^{-1},\end{array}\right.

and
\phi_{\rho}:=\eta_{\rho}\varphi\in C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
We note that, since

U(r)\sim(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{N-p}{p-\beta}},\quad U^{\prime}(r)\sim(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{N-\beta}{p-\beta}}r^{\frac{1-\beta}{p-1}},

then

|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\sim(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2},\quad|\nabla U|^{p-2}\sim(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}}r^{\frac{(1-\beta)(p-2)}{p-1}}.

Thanks to Fubini’s theorem and using polar coordinates,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\phi_{\rho}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}r^{N-1-\beta}(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\phi_{\rho}(r\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}r^{N-1-\beta}(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int^{+\infty}_{r}|\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)||\nabla\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}r^{N-1-\beta}(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}

\displaystyle\quad\times|\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)||\nabla\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)|\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}t^{N-\beta}(1+t^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)||\nabla\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta.

Thus, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\phi_{\rho}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C_{5}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}t^{N+1-\beta}(1+t^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\nabla\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}t^{N-1-\beta}(1+t^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and since the last term in the right hand coincides with
\|\phi_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
(up to a multiplicative constant), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\phi_{\rho}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}t^{N+1-\beta}(1+t^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\nabla\phi_{\rho}(t\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{7}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2-\beta}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\nabla\phi_{\rho}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\rho}}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\phi_{\rho}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2-\beta}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\nabla\phi_{\rho}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2-\beta}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}\eta_{\rho}^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2-\beta}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\nabla\eta_{\rho}|^{2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}}}|x|^{2-\beta}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+4C_{3}|\log\rho|^{-2}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\rho}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}}}|x|^{-\beta}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\rho^{\frac{p-\beta}{2(p-1)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+C_{4}|\log\rho|^{-2}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\rho}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Thus, combining with (
2.1
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\rho}}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\rho^{\frac{p-\beta}{2(p-1)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+C_{4}|\log\rho|^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{5}|\log\rho|^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

due to
\frac{p-\beta}{2(p-1)}>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
, (
2.3
) holds.
∎

2.1.
Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with uniformly bounded norm. It follows from (
2.1
) that
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
are uniformly bounded as well.

Since both
|\nabla U|^{p-2}
and
|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}
are locally bounded away from zero and infinity in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, by Rellich-Kondrachov Theorem and a diagonal argument we deduce that, up to a subsequence, there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\varphi_{n}\rightharpoonup\varphi
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
locally in
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\})
.

Also, it follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that, for any
\rho\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\rho^{\vartheta},\quad\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\rho}}}|x|^{-\beta}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{|\log\rho|^{2}}.

We conclude the proof by defining the compact set
K_{\rho}:=\overline{B_{\frac{1}{\rho}}}\setminus B_{\rho}
and applying the strong convergence of
\varphi_{n}
in
K_{\rho}
, together with the arbitrariness of
\rho
(that can be chosen arbitrarily small).
∎

As mentioned in
[
16
]
, because of the crucial inequality
A.5
, we shall see that Proposition
1.2
allows us to deal with the case
\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}<p<2
when we show the stability of Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.7
). However, when
1<p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
which implies

, we will need a much more delicate compactness result that we now present.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
with
0<\beta<p
. Let
\{v_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq 1,

where
\varepsilon_{n}\in(0,1)
is a sequence of positive numbers converging to
0
. Then, up to a subsequence,
v_{n}
convergence weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to some
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Also, given any constant
C_{1}\geq 0
it holds

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

We follow the arguments as those in
[
16
, Lemma 3.4]
.
Up to replacing
v_{n}
by
|v_{n}|
, we can assume that
v_{n}\geq 0
. Note that
p<p^{*}_{\beta}\leq 2
under our assumption
1<p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
with
0<\beta<p
.

Observe that, under Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\|U\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\varepsilon_{n}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

that combined with (
2.7
) gives

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C.

Thus, up to a subsequence,
v_{n}
converges weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and also a.e. to some function
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence, to conclude the proof, we need to show the validity of (
2.8
).

We first prove it under the assumption that
\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}\leq\zeta U
with some small constant
\zeta=\zeta(N,p,\beta,C_{1})\in(0,1)
be determined. Later, we will remove this assumption.

\bullet

Step 1: prove of (
2.8
) when
\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}\leq\zeta U
. Since
\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}
is bounded by
\zeta U\leq U
, we have that
1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\leq 2
, thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}(U+\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p^{*}_{\beta}-p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{p-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Recall that

(2.10)

|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\sim(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}-2},\quad|\nabla U|^{p-2}\sim(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)(p-2)}{p-\beta}}r^{\frac{(1-\beta)(p-2)}{p-1}}.

Moreover, from the following Hardy-Poincaré inequality, see Lemma
B.1
, we deduce that: For any
p>1
and
\xi\geq 1
, and any compactly supported function
w\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, one has

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{p}|x|^{-\beta}\left[\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\xi-1}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w|^{p}\left[\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\xi}\mathrm{d}x.

By approximation, we can apply this inequality with

\displaystyle\xi=1+\frac{(2-p^{*}_{\beta})(N-p)}{(p-1)(p-\beta)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad w=w_{n}:=\left(\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{p-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus, by
|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\sim\left[\left(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\xi-1}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}(U+\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{p}\left[\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\xi}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\times\left[\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|\nabla U|}{U^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|}{U}\right)^{p}+\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{p-2}|v_{n}|^{2-p}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\right]\mathrm{d}x

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|U|^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}\left[|v_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\zeta|\nabla U|}{U}+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|}{U}\right)^{p}+|v_{n}|^{2-p}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\right]\mathrm{d}x,

where, in the last inequality, we have used that
0\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\leq\zeta<1
.

We now apply (
B.11
) to the last integrand in (
2.1
) with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}
,
r=|x|
,
a=|v_{n}|
,
b=|\nabla v_{n}|
. In this way, thanks to (
2.1
) and since
U+\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}\leq 2U
, we deduce that for any
\varepsilon_{0}>0
there exists
\zeta=\zeta(\varepsilon_{0})\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2-p^{*}_{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}(U+\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{p}\left[\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\xi}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon_{0})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Thus, fixing
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough such that
C\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, it follows from (
2.7
) and the inequality above that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{p}\left[\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\xi}\mathrm{d}x

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C.

In particular, the sequence
w_{n}
is uniformly and locally bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{p^{*}}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Since
1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\sim 1
, this implies that
|v_{n}|^{\frac{2}{p}}
is locally in
L^{p^{*}}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Combining this higher integrability estimate with the a.e. convergence of
v_{n}
to
v
, by dominated convergence we deduce that, for any
R>1
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

as
n\to\infty
(recall that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
).

Also, since
1<p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
and
0<\beta<p
it follows that
N>\max\{\beta,2-\beta\}
, therefore

\frac{(p-N)(p^{*}_{\beta}-2-p)}{p-1}-\beta+N=\frac{N-2p+\beta(N-p+1)}{p-1}=\frac{N+\beta N+\beta-(\beta+2)p}{p-1}>0.

This allows us to apply Hardy-Poincaré inequality (see
[
16
, Lemma A.1]
) to
v_{n}
with

\alpha=\frac{(N-p)(p^{*}_{\beta}-2-p)}{p-1}+\beta<N,

namely,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|v_{n}|^{p}|x|^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}|x|^{-\alpha+p}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall R>1.

Then similarly to (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{p-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{(p-N)(p^{*}_{\beta}-2-p)}{p-1}+p-\beta}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|\nabla U|}{U^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|}{U}\right)^{p}+\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{U}\right)^{p-2}|v_{n}|^{2-p}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{(p-N)(p^{*}_{\beta}-2-p)}{p-1}+p-\beta}\left[|v_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\zeta|\nabla U|}{U}+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|}{U}\right)^{p}+|v_{n}|^{2-p}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\right]\mathrm{d}x.

Then applying (

) with

,

,

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0})R^{-\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle CR^{-\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle CR^{-\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}.

Combining this bound with (

) and (

) holds. This concludes the proof under assumption that
\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}\leq\zeta U
with

\bullet

Step 2: proof of (

) in the general case
. Throughout this part, we assume that

Same as

, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle C\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

For readers’ convenience, we explain the reason.
Observe that,
\zeta U
is a supersolution for the operator

namely

. Therefore, multiplying

by

and integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\zeta|\nabla\varphi|)^{p-2}\zeta\nabla U\cdot\nabla(\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}-\zeta U)_{+}\mathrm{d}x

(2.17)

\displaystyle\quad+(p-2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\zeta|\nabla\varphi|)^{p-3}\zeta^{2}|\nabla U|\nabla U\cdot\nabla(\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}-\zeta U)_{+}\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

Also, by the convexity of

we have

F_{t}(z)+\nabla F_{t}(z)\cdot(z^{\prime}-z)\leq F_{t}(z^{\prime}),\quad\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad t\geq 0.

Hence, applying this inequality with

,

, and

, it follows by (

) that

\displaystyle C\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}(|\nabla U|+\zeta|\nabla U|)^{p-2}\zeta^{2}|\nabla U|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+2\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}(|\nabla U|+\zeta|\nabla U|)^{p-2}\zeta\nabla U\cdot\nabla(\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}-\zeta U)_{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+(p-2)\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}(|\nabla U|+\zeta|\nabla U|)^{p-3}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\times\zeta^{2}|\nabla U|\nabla U\cdot\nabla(\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}-\zeta U)_{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

thus (

We now write

\displaystyle v_{n,1}:=\min\left\{v_{n},\frac{\zeta U}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right\},\quad v_{n,2}:=v_{n}-v_{n,1}.

Note that, as a consequence of (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n,1}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n,1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n,2}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n,1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq C.

In particular we deduce that

Let

Case 1

\displaystyle\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,1}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x>

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\eta}_{n}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}|x|^{-\beta}\left(v_{n}-\frac{\zeta U}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)_{+}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\eta}_{n}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left(1+O(\varepsilon_{n}^{\eta})\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n,1})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n,1}|^{2}}|v_{n,1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

Case 2
. Assume now

(2.21)

\displaystyle\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,1}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon^{-\eta}_{n}\int_{\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x.

We claim that

(2.22)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x=O(\varepsilon_{n}^{\eta}).

To prove this, we denote
D_{n}:=\{\varepsilon_{n}\nabla v_{n}>\zeta U\}

E_{n}:=\left\{|\nabla v_{n,2}|\leq\frac{|\nabla U|}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right\}\cap D_{n},\quad F_{n}:=\left\{|\nabla v_{n,2}|>\frac{|\nabla U|}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right\}\cap D_{n}.

Then, since
v_{n,2}=v_{n}-v_{n,1}\equiv 0
inside
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D_{n}
,
D_{n}=E_{n}\cup F_{n}
, and
|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n,2}|\leq 2|\nabla U|
inside

, it follows by Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{D_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{E_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{F_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{E_{n}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{E_{n}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}+\int_{F_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\int_{E_{n}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n,2}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{E_{n}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}+\int_{F_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

Also, using (
2.10
) and condition (
2.21
) together with Hölder inequality,
taking

(2.24)

\displaystyle Q:=\frac{pN-\beta}{p(N-1)-(p-\beta)\nu}>1

for any
\nu
satisfying
\frac{(p-1)N}{N-\beta}<\nu<\frac{p(N-1)}{p-\beta}
(since
\beta<p<N
),
we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{E_{n}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{E_{n}}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)p}{p-\beta}}|x|^{\frac{(1-\beta)p}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\int_{E_{n}}\left((1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)p}{p-\beta}+\nu}|x|^{\frac{(1-\beta)p}{p-1}}\right)^{Q}\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{1}{Q}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{E_{n}}(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{\nu Q}{Q-1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}

\displaystyle C\left[\int_{D_{n}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}}{\zeta U}\right)^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\left((1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)p}{p-\beta}+\nu}|x|^{\frac{(1-\beta)p}{p-1}}\right)^{Q}\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{1}{Q}}

\displaystyle C\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\int_{D_{n}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{Q}}

(2.25)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{p^{*}_{\beta}-\eta}\int_{D_{n}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{Q}},

where we used that
\frac{(p-\beta)}{(p-1)}\frac{\nu Q}{Q-1}>N
and that

U^{-p^{*}_{\beta}}\left((1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{-\frac{(N-\beta)p}{p-\beta}+\nu}|x|^{\frac{(1-\beta)p}{p-1}}\right)^{Q}\leq C|x|^{-\beta}.

Therefore, introducing the notation

N_{n,2}:=\int_{E_{n}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n,2}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

by Hardy-Sobolev inequality, (

), and (
2.1

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}_{n}\left[N_{n,2}^{\frac{p^{*}_{\beta}}{2}}\left(\int_{E_{n}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(2-p)p^{*}_{\beta}}{2p}}+\left(\int_{F_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p^{*}_{\beta}}{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}_{n}\Bigg[N_{n,2}^{\frac{p^{*}_{\beta}}{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-\eta}_{n}\int_{D_{n}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(2-p)p^{*}_{\beta}}{2pQ}}

(2.26)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{F_{n}}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg],

where in the last inequality we used (

Suppose first that

Then, since

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-p}_{n}\int_{F_{n}}(|\nabla U|+\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla v_{n,2}|)^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n,2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

Set

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2+(2-\eta)\theta}_{n}N_{n,2}^{\frac{p^{*}_{\beta}}{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}_{n}\int_{D_{n}}|x|^{-\beta}|v_{n,2}|^{p^{*}_{\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\theta}.

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n}|^{2}}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}\frac{(U+C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}v_{n,1})^{p^{*}_{\beta}}}{U^{2}+|\varepsilon_{n}v_{n,1}|^{2}}|v_{n,1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right|

First of all, let us rewrite the linear equation (

\displaystyle-|x|^{2}\Delta v-(p-2)\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}x_{i}x_{j}-\frac{(p-2)(N-\beta)}{1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}(x\cdot\nabla v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)C_{N,p,\beta}^{p^{*}_{\beta}-p}\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{2-p}\frac{|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{2}}v\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad v\in L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Indeed a straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(|\nabla U|^{p-2}\nabla v)+(p-2)\mathrm{div}(|\nabla U|^{p-4}(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)\nabla U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla U|^{p-2}\Delta v+\nabla(|\nabla U|^{p-2})\cdot\nabla v+(p-2)|\nabla U|^{p-4}(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)\Delta U

\displaystyle+(p-2)(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)(\nabla(|\nabla U|)\cdot\nabla U)+(p-2)|\nabla U|(\nabla(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)\cdot\nabla U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla U|^{p-2}\Delta v+(p-2)(p-4)|\nabla U|^{p-6}(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)(\nabla U\nabla(\nabla U)\cdot\nabla U)

\displaystyle+(p-2)|\nabla U|^{p-4}\left[(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)\Delta U+2(\nabla U\nabla(\nabla U)\cdot\nabla v)+(\nabla U\nabla(\nabla v)\cdot\nabla U)\right],

and

\displaystyle\nabla U=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{N,p}|x|^{\frac{2-p-\beta}{p-1}}x}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-\beta}{p-\beta}}},\quad(x\cdot\nabla U)=-\frac{c_{N,p}|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-\beta}{p-\beta}}},

\displaystyle(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{N,p}|x|^{\frac{2-p-\beta}{p-1}}(x\cdot\nabla v)}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-\beta}{p-\beta}}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\Delta U=

\displaystyle\frac{-c_{N,p}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-\beta}{p-\beta}}}\left\{\left(\frac{2-p-\beta}{p-1}+N\right)|x|^{\frac{2-p-\beta}{p-1}}-\frac{\frac{N-\beta}{p-1}|x|^{\frac{2-2\beta}{p-1}}}{1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}\right\},

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{j=1}\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}_{N,p}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{2(N-\beta)}{p-\beta}}}\left\{\frac{1-\beta}{p-1}|x|^{\frac{2(2-p-\beta)}{p-1}}-\frac{\frac{N-\beta}{p-1}|x|^{\frac{4-p-3\beta}{p-1}}}{1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}\right\}x_{i},

where
c_{N,p}:=C_{N,p,\beta}\frac{N-p}{p-1}
. Here,

\displaystyle(\nabla U\nabla(\nabla v)\cdot\nabla U):=

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i,j}\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{c^{2}_{N,p}|x|^{\frac{2(2-p-\beta)}{p-1}}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{2(N-\beta)}{p-\beta}}}\sum^{N}_{i,j}\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}x_{i}x_{j},

\displaystyle(\nabla U\nabla(\nabla U)\cdot\nabla v):=

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i,j}\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}_{N,p}(x\cdot\nabla v)}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{2(N-\beta)}{p-\beta}}}\left\{\frac{1-\beta}{p-1}|x|^{\frac{2(2-p-\beta)}{p-1}}-\frac{\frac{N-\beta}{p-1}|x|^{\frac{4-p-3\beta}{p-1}}}{1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}\right\},

\displaystyle(\nabla U\nabla(\nabla U)\cdot\nabla U):=

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i,j}\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-c^{3}_{N,p}|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{3(N-\beta)}{p-\beta}}}\left\{\frac{1-\beta}{p-1}|x|^{\frac{2(2-p-\beta)}{p-1}}-\frac{\frac{N-\beta}{p-1}|x|^{\frac{4-p-3\beta}{p-1}}}{1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}\right\}.

Then by using the standard spherical decomposition and making the change of variable
r\mapsto r^{\frac{p}{p-\beta}}
, we can characterize all solutions to the linearized problem (
3
).

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We make the standard partial wave decomposition of (
3
), namely

(3.2)

v=v(r,\theta)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\varphi_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\theta),

where
r=|x|
,
\theta=\frac{x}{|x|}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, and

\varphi_{k}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}v(r,\theta)\Psi_{k}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta.

Here
\Psi_{k}(\theta)
denotes the
k
-th spherical harmonic, i.e., it satisfies

(3.3)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\Psi_{k},

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with the standard metric and
\lambda_{k}
is the
k
-th eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
. It is well known that

(3.4)

\lambda_{k}=k(N-2+k),\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

whose multiplicity is
\frac{(N+2k-2)(N+k-3)!}{(N-2)!k!}
and that

\mathrm{Ker}(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}+\lambda_{k})=\mathbb{Y}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N})|_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}},

where
\mathbb{Y}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the space of all homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree
k
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It is standard that
\lambda_{0}=0
and the corresponding eigenfunction of (
3.3
) is the constant function. The second eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=N-1
and the corresponding eigenfunctions of (
3.3
) are
x_{i}/|x|
,
i=1,\ldots,N
.

The following results can be obtained by direct calculation,

\displaystyle\Delta(\varphi_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\theta))=

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\frac{N-1}{r}\varphi^{\prime}_{k}\right)+\frac{\varphi_{k}}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Psi_{k}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\frac{N-1}{r}\varphi^{\prime}_{k}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{r^{2}}\varphi_{k}\right).

It is easy to verify that

\frac{\partial(\varphi_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\theta))}{\partial x_{i}}=\varphi^{\prime}_{k}\frac{x_{i}}{r}\Psi_{k}+\varphi_{k}\frac{\partial\Psi_{k}}{\partial\theta_{l}}\frac{\partial\theta_{l}}{\partial x_{i}},

hence

(3.6)

\begin{split}x\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\theta))=\sum^{N}_{i=1}x_{i}\frac{\partial(\varphi_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\theta))}{\partial x_{i}}=\varphi^{\prime}_{k}r\Psi_{k}+\varphi_{k}\frac{\partial\Psi_{k}}{\partial\theta_{l}}\sum^{N}_{i=i}\frac{\partial\theta_{l}}{\partial x_{i}}x_{i}=\varphi^{\prime}_{k}r\Psi_{k},\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial^{2}(\varphi_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\theta))}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}x_{i}x_{j}=

\displaystyle 2\varphi^{\prime}_{k}r\frac{\partial\Psi_{k}}{\partial\theta_{l}}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{\partial\theta_{l}}{\partial x_{i}}x_{i}+\varphi_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{k}}{\partial\theta_{l}\partial\theta_{m}}\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial\theta_{l}}{\partial x_{i}}x_{i}\frac{\partial\theta_{m}}{\partial x_{j}}x_{j}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial\Psi_{k}}{\partial\theta_{l}}\varphi_{k}\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial^{2}\theta_{l}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}x_{i}x_{j}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{k}r^{2}\Psi_{k}=\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{k}r^{2}\Psi_{k},

due to

\begin{split}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{\partial\theta_{l}}{\partial x_{i}}x_{i}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial^{2}\theta_{l}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}x_{i}x_{j}=0,\quad l=1,\ldots,N-1.\end{split}

Then putting together (
3.2
), (
3.1
), (
3.6
) and (
3.1
) into (
3
), the function
v
is a solution of (
3
) if and only if
\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{W}
is a classical solution of the system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(p-1)\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}}{r}\left[(N-1)+\frac{(p-2)(N-\beta)}{1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}\right]-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{r^{2}}\varphi_{k}\\[11.38109pt]
+(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)C_{N,p,\beta}^{p^{*}_{\beta}-p}\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{2-p}\frac{r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}-2}}{\left(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}\right)^{2}}\varphi_{k}=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad r\in(0,\infty),\\[11.38109pt]
\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(0)=0\quad\mbox{if}\quad k=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi_{k}(0)=0\quad\mbox{if}\quad k\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{W}:=\{\omega\in C([0,\infty))|\int^{\infty}_{0}r^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\omega(r)|^{2}r^{N-1}\mathrm{d}r<\infty\}
.
We use the change of variable:
r=s^{q}
with
q=p/(p-\beta)
, and let

\eta_{k}(s)=\varphi_{k}(r),

that transforms (
3.1
) into the following equations for any
\eta_{k}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(3.12)

\eta^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\frac{\eta^{\prime}_{k}}{s}\left(\frac{K-1}{p-1}+\frac{(p-2)K}{(p-1)(1+s^{\frac{p}{p-1}})}\right)-\frac{q^{2}\lambda_{k}}{(p-1)s^{2}}\eta_{k}+\frac{K(Kp-K+p)}{(p-1)^{2}}\frac{s^{\frac{p}{p-1}-2}}{(1+s^{\frac{p}{p-1}})^{2}}\eta_{k}=0.

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}:=\{\omega\in C([0,\infty))|\int^{\infty}_{0}W^{\frac{Kp}{K-p}-2}|\omega(s)|^{2}s^{K-1}ds<\infty\}
,
W(s)=U(r)
and

K=\frac{p(N-\beta)}{p-\beta}>p.

Here we have used the fact

q^{2}(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)C_{N,p,\beta}^{p^{*}_{\beta}-p}\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{2-p}=\frac{K(Kp-K+p)}{p-1},

where
C_{N,p,\beta}
is given in (
1.8
).

Fix
K
let us now consider the following eigenvalue problem

(3.13)

\eta^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{s}\left(\frac{K-1}{p-1}+\frac{(p-2)K}{(p-1)(1+s^{\frac{p}{p-1}})}\right)-\frac{\mu}{(p-1)s^{2}}\eta+\frac{K(Kp-K+p)}{(p-1)^{2}}\frac{s^{\frac{p}{p-1}-2}}{(1+s^{\frac{p}{p-1}})^{2}}\eta=0.

When
K
is an integer we can study (
3.13
) as the linearized operator of the equation

-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla W|^{p-2}\nabla W)=K\left(\frac{K-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}W^{\frac{Kp}{K-p}-1},\quad W>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{K}).

around the standard solution
W(x)=(1+|x|^{p/(p-1)})^{-(K-p)/p}
(note that we always have
K>p
). In this case, as in
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
(see also
[
26
]
), we have that

(3.14)

\mu_{0}=0;\quad\mu_{1}=K-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta_{0}(s)=\frac{(p-1)-s^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{(1+s^{\frac{p}{p-1}})^{\frac{K}{p}}};\quad\eta_{1}(s)=\frac{s^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(1+s^{\frac{p}{p-1}})^{\frac{K}{p}}}.

Moreover, even
K
is not an integer we readily see that (
3.14
) remains true. Therefore, we conclude that (
3.12
) has nontrivial solutions if and only if

q^{2}\lambda_{k}\in\{0,K-1\},

where
\lambda_{k}
is given in (
3.4
). If
q^{2}\lambda_{k}=0
then
k=0
. Moreover, if
q^{2}\lambda_{k}=K-1
, that is

(3.15)

\left(\frac{p-\beta}{p}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{p(N-\beta)}{p-\beta}-1\right]=k(N-2+k)\quad\mbox{for some}\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

However, since
0<\beta<p
, it is easy to verify that

0<\left(\frac{p-\beta}{p}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{p(N-\beta)}{p-\beta}-1\right]<N-1,

which leads to (
3.15
) cannot happen. Therefore, we deduce that (
3.1
) only admits one solution

(3.16)

\varphi_{0}(r)=\frac{(p-1)-r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}{(1+r^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-\beta}{p-\beta}}}.

That is, the space of solutions of (
3
) has dimension
1
and is spanned by

v_{0}(x)=\frac{(p-1)-|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p-\beta}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-\beta}{p-\beta}}}.

Since
v_{0}\sim\frac{\partial U_{\lambda}}{\partial\lambda}|_{\lambda=1}=\frac{N-p}{p}U+x\cdot\nabla U
, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is complete.

4.
Spectral analysis

Let us consider the following eigenvalue problem

(4.1)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{U}[v]=\mu|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}v\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad v\in L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{U}[v]:=-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla U|^{p-2}\nabla v)-(p-2)\mathrm{div}(|\nabla U|^{p-4}(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)\nabla U).\end{split}

When
p=2
, the eigenvalue problem (
4.1
) was classified by Smets and Willem
[
32
]
. Then combining Theorem
1.3
with
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
, thanks to Proposition
1.2
, making the change
r\mapsto r^{\frac{p}{p-\beta}}
we deduce directly the following conclusion.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
1<p<N
and
0<\beta<p
. Let
\mu_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,
denote the eigenvalues of (
4.1
) in increasing order. Then
\mu_{1}=(p-1)
is simple and the corresponding eigenfunction is
\zeta U
with
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mu_{2}=p^{*}_{\beta}-1
and the corresponding eigenfunction is
\zeta(\frac{N-p}{p}U+x\cdot\nabla U)
with
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Furthermore, the Rayleigh quotient characterization of eigenvalues implies

\mu_{3}=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{L}_{U}[v]v\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x}:v\perp\mathrm{Span}\left\{U,\ \frac{N-p}{p}U+x\cdot\nabla U\right\}\right\}>p^{*}_{\beta}-1.

In particular, Proposition
4.1
implies

(4.2)

\displaystyle T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}=\mathrm{Span}\left\{U,\ \frac{N-p}{p}U+x\cdot\nabla U\right\},

where
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}:=\{cU_{\lambda}:c\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0\}
is set of extremal functions for Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.7
).
From Proposition
4.1
, we directly obtain

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
1<p<N
and
0<\beta<p
. Then there exists a constant
\tau=\tau(N,p,\beta)>0
such that for any function
v\in L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
orthogonal to
T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
, it holds that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}+(p-2)|\nabla U|^{p-4}(\nabla U\cdot\nabla v)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x\geq\left[(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)+2\tau\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Enlightened by
[
16
]
, we give the following remark which will be important to give a meaning to the notion of “orthogonal to
T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
” for functions which are not necessarily in
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Remark 4.3
.

For any
\xi\in T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
it holds
U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}\xi\in L_{\beta}^{\frac{p^{*}_{\beta}}{p^{*}_{\beta}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left(L_{\beta}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)^{\prime}
, here
L_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the set of measurable functions with the norm
\|\varphi\|_{L_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|\varphi|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
.
Hence, by abuse of notation, for any function
v\in L_{\beta}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we say that
v
is orthogonal to
T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
in
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}v\xi\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall\xi\in T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}.

Note that, by Hölder inequality,
L_{\beta}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
p^{*}_{\beta}\geq 2
.
Hence, the notion of orthogonality introduced above is particularly relevant when
p^{*}_{\beta}<2
(equivalently,
p<\frac{2N}{N+2-\beta}
). We also observe that, by Sobolev embedding theorem, the previous remark gives a meaning to the orthogonality to
T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
for functions in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
since the Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.7
) implies
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L_{\beta}^{p^{*}_{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously.

4.1.
Improvements of spectral gap

Same as
[
16
, Proposition 3.8]
, the goal of this subsection is improving the spectral gap obtained in Proposition
4.2
, that is, we will give the following spectral gap-type estimates for
p\geq 2
and
1<p<2
, respectively.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
2\leq p<N
and
0<\beta<p
. Given any
\gamma_{0}>0
, there exists
\overline{\delta}=\overline{\delta}(N,p,\beta,\gamma_{0})>0
such that for any function
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
orthogonal to
T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
in
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
\|v\|\leq\overline{\delta}
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}+(p-2)|\omega|^{p-2}(|\nabla(U+v)|-|\nabla U|)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq&\left[(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)+\tau\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

where
\tau>0
is given in Proposition
4.2
, and
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{2N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is defined in analogy to Lemma
A.1
:

\displaystyle\omega=\omega(\nabla U,\nabla(U+v))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla U,&{\rm if}\ \ |\nabla U|<|\nabla(U+v)|,\\[8.53581pt]
\left(\frac{|\nabla(U+v)|}{|\nabla U|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}\nabla(U+v),&{\rm if}\ \ |\nabla(U+v)|\leq|\nabla U|.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If the statement of this lemma fails, then there exists a sequence
0\not\equiv v_{n}\to 0
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with
v_{n}
orthogonal to
T_{U}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}+(p-2)|\omega_{n}|^{p-2}(|\nabla(U+v_{n})|-|\nabla U|)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x

(4.4)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left[(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)+\tau\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where
\omega_{n}
corresponds to
v_{n}
as in the statement.
Let

\varepsilon_{n}:=\|v_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\widehat{v}_{n}=\frac{v_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}}.

Note that, since
p\geq 2
, it follows by Hölder inequality that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\to 0,

hence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
.
Since
\|\widehat{v}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=1
, Proposition
1.2
implies that, up to a subsequence,
\widehat{v}_{n}\rightharpoonup\widehat{v}
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\widehat{v}_{n}\rightarrow\widehat{v}
in
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\widehat{v}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Also, since
p\geq 2
, it follows from (
4.1
) that

1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left[(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)+\tau\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

then we deduce that

\|\widehat{v}_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq c

for some
c>0
.

Fix
R>1
which can be chosen arbitrarily large, set

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\{2|\nabla U|\geq|\nabla v_{n}|\}&,\quad\mathcal{S}_{n}:=\{2|\nabla U|<|\nabla v_{n}|\},\\
\mathcal{R}_{n,R}:=\left(B_{R}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{R}}\right)\cap\mathcal{R}_{n}&,\quad\mathcal{S}_{n,R}:=\left(B_{R}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{R}}\right)\cap\mathcal{S}_{n},\end{split}

thus
B_{R}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{R}}=\mathcal{R}_{n,R}\cup\mathcal{S}_{n,R}
.
Since the integrand in the left hand side of (
4.1
) is nonnegative, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{R}}}\left[|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}+(p-2)|\omega_{n}|^{p-2}\left(\frac{|\nabla(U+v_{n})|-|\nabla U|}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x

(4.5)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left[(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)+\tau\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

From Proposition
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle c\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}U^{p^{*}_{\beta}-2}|\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U|^{p-2}|\nabla\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=C_{1},

thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}_{n,R}}\left|\nabla U\right|^{p-2}|\nabla\widehat{v}_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon^{p-2}_{n}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{n,R}}|\nabla\widehat{v}_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{2}.

Then we obtain

\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{n,R}}|\nabla U|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{p-2}}{2^{p}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{n,R}}|\nabla\widehat{v}_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{3},

and since

0<c(R)\leq|\nabla U|\leq C(R)\quad\mbox{inside}\quad B_{R}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{R}},\quad\forall R>1,

for some constants
c(R)\leq C(R)
depending only on
R
, we conclude that

(4.6)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}_{n,R}|\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty,\quad\forall R>1.

Here
|\mathcal{S}_{n,R}|
denote the Lebesgue measure of
\mathcal{S}_{n,R}
. Now, writing

\widehat{v}_{n}=\widehat{v}+\varphi_{n},\quad\mbox{with}\quad\varphi_{n}:=\widehat{v}_{n}-\widehat{v},

since

We argue by contradiction. In fact, if the theorem is false then there exists a sequence

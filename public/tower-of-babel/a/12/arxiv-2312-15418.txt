Abstract
. We investigate how to control optimally a traffic flow through a junction on the line by acting only on speed reduction or traffic light at the junction. We show the existence of an optimal control and, under structure assumptions, provide optimality conditions. We use this analysis to investigate thoroughly the maximization of the flux on a space-time subset and show the existence of an optimal control which is bang-bang.



1.
Introduction

In this note, we investigate how to control optimally a traffic flow through a junction on the line by acting only on speed reduction or traffic light at the junction.

In our setting, the evolution of the density
\rho^{A}
of the vehicles is given by a conservation law of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho^{A}_{t}+(f^{L}(\rho^{A}))_{x}=0&\;\text{in}\;(-\infty,0)\times(0,T),\\[2.84526pt]
\rho^{A}_{t}+(f^{R}(\rho^{A}))_{x}=0&\;\text{in}\;(0,\infty)\times(0,T),\\[2.84526pt]
\rho^{A}_{t}(0,t)\in\mathcal{G}(A)(t)&\;\text{a.e. in}\;(0,T),\\[2.84526pt]
\rho^{A}(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x)&\;\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here
f^{L}:[0,R^{L}]\to\mathbb{R}
and
f^{R}:[0,R^{R}]\to\mathbb{R}
are two strictly concave flux functions vanishing respectively at
0
and
R^{L}
and at
0
and
R^{R}
. The measurable initial condition
\rho_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
\rho_{0}\in[0,R^{L}]
on
(-\infty,0)
and
\rho_{0}\in[0,R^{R}]
on
(0,\infty)
. The time-dependent control is the flux limiter
A=A(t)
which acts on the traffic through the junction condition, that is

\rho^{A}_{t}(0,t)=(\rho^{A}_{t}(0^{-},t),\rho^{A}_{t}(0^{+},t))\in\mathcal{G}(A)(t)\qquad\text{a.e. in}\ (0,T),

where
\rho^{A}_{t}(0^{-},t)
and
\rho^{A}_{t}(0^{+},t)
are understood in the sense of traces, which exist since
\rho^{A}
solves a scalar conservation law with a strictly concave flux. Finally, the time dependent germ
\mathcal{G}(A)(t)
is given by

\mathcal{G}(A)(t)=\left\{e=(e^{L},e^{R})\in Q,\;f^{L}(e^{L})=f^{R}(e^{R})=\min\{-A(t),f^{L,+}(e^{L}),f^{R,-}(e^{R})\}\right\},

where
Q=[0,R^{L}]\times[0,R^{R}]
,
A_{0}=-\min\{\max f^{L},\max f^{R}\}
and
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
denote respectively the increasing and decreasing parts of a given map
f
. We note that is known, see, for example,
[
3
]
, that, for piecewise constant controls
A:[0,T]\to[A_{0},0]
, there exists a unique entropy solution
\rho^{A}
to (
1.1
).

The general optimal control problem is the minimization over all piecewise constant maps
A:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
of a quantity of the form

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(x,t,\rho^{A}(x,t),A(t))dt

(1.2)

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[A_{0},0]\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function with a compact support and convex in the last variable.

Models about the optimal control of traffic flow have attracted considerable attention recently; see, for example, the monograph
[
5
]
and to the survey papers
[
6
,
23
]
.
In fact, several optimal control problems of the type discussed above have been looked at in the literature, some
of which having for more general junction conditions;
see, for instance,
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
24
]
. In particular,
[
17
]
derives optimality conditions assuming the smoothness of the optimal solution. In
[
14
]
, the controls are assumed to be constant in time. Reference
[
15
]
discusses how to minimize the queue length, the total variation of traffic speed (see also
[
16
]
), and general functions of the density as in (
1.2
) for problems on the half-line. As it is explained in
[
15
]
, this later case includes the “travel time”, that is, the average time needed to reach a given position. The main result of
[
15
]
is the existence of optimal solutions when controlling the flux at the entry of the road. In
[
2
]
(see also
[
18
]
), the authors study problems on a junction. Their aim is to optimize integrals in time of a function of the flux at the node, namely, expressions of the form
\psi(f^{L}(\rho^{A}(0^{-},t)),f^{R}(\rho^{A}(0^{+},t))

and prove the existence of an optimal solution under a total variation constraint on the control.

Our objective is to show that the problem consisting in minimizing (
1.2
) has a (relaxed) minimum (Theorem
3.1
), to give optimality conditions in the case where
\psi
is a linear function of
\rho^{A}
or of
f^{L/R}(\rho^{A})
(Theorem
4.2
) and to show that, under suitable structure conditions on
\psi
and the initial condition (including the case of maximizing the flux on a space-time box), the relaxed control is piecewise constant and takes values in
\{0,A_{0}\}
(Theorem
5.1
). Note that this latter result is meaningful in the context of traffic flows. It shows that it is more efficient to stop the traffic by using traffic lights with only a finite number of switches than to slow it down by reducing the speed. We underline however that the optimal solution depends on the choice of both the initial condition and the cost function.

The existence of a solution is a new result since it does not require any BV bound on the control in contrast to other results in the literature
[
2
,
14
]
. The price to pay is that the optimal control is then a priori in
L^{\infty}
and not BV. As a result, it is necessary to interpret the solution in a suitable way (see below). Note, however, that, under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, the relaxed optimal control turns out to be piecewise constant, and thus a “classical” control. With the exception of
[
17
]
, where the optimal solution, that is, the pair
(A,\rho^{A})
) is supposed to be smooth, and
[
14
]
, in which controls are assumed to be constant in time, there is no optimality conditions in the literature. Here we obtain such conditions, at least under specific structure assumptions. Finally, we derive from these conditions that the optimal control is bang-bang for the problem of maximizing the flux in a space-time box.

As the direct analysis of the minimization problem in (
1.2
) is delicate, we rely on the representation of the solution
\rho^{A}
in terms of Hamilton-Jacobi equations. This approach is classical in the literature; see, for example,
[
20
]
and the references therein or
[
5
, section 3.3.1]
for a comprehensive account. It amounts to replacing the density
\rho^{A}
by the Moskowitz function
u^{A}=u^{A}(x,t)
, which, roughly speaking, counts the number of vehicles passed by location
x
before time
t
. Then
\rho^{A}=-u^{A}_{x}
and
u^{A}
is the unique viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation on the junction problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{A}_{t}(x,t)+H^{L}(u^{A}_{x}(x,t))=0\ \;\ \text{in}\;\ \ (-\infty,0)\times(0,T),\\
u^{A}_{t}(x,t)+H^{R}(u^{A}_{x}(x,t))=0\;\ \ \text{in}\;\ \ (0,\infty)\times(0,T),\\
u^{A}_{t}(0,t)+\max\{A(t),H^{L,+}(u^{A}_{x}(0^{-},t)),H^{R,-}(u^{A}_{x}(0^{+},t))\}=0\ \ \;\text{in}\;\ \ (0,T),\\
u^{A}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\ \ \;\text{in}\;\ \ R,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
H^{L/R}(v)=-f^{L/R}(-v)
. Note that
H^{L/R}:(-R^{L/R},0)\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth uniformly convex Hamiltonians with
H^{L/R}(-R^{L/R})=H^{L/R}(0)=0
. The initial condition
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz map such that
u_{0,x}=-\rho_{0}
,
(u_{0})_{x}\in(-R^{L},0)
on
(-\infty,0)
and
u_{0,x}\in(-R^{R},0)
on
(0,\infty)
. The (now relaxed) control
A
is a measurable map taking values in
[A_{0},0]
, where
A_{0}=\max\{\min H^{L},\min H^{R}\}
.

The optimization problem (
1.2
) then consists in minimizing over measurable controls
A
, taking values in
[A_{0},0]
, the cost

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\psi}(x,t,u^{A}_{x}(x,t),A(t))dt,

(1.4)

where
\widetilde{\psi}(x,t,\rho,A)=\psi(x,t,-\rho,A)
is continuous function with a compact support which is convex in the last variable. We summarize saying that we study the optimization problem

\min\{\mathcal{J}(A):A:(0,T)\to[A_{0},0]\ \text{measurable}\},

(1.5)

with
\mathcal{J}(A)
as in (
1.4
).

Note that we restrict our analysis to a single junction on the line. The reason for this is that it is known from
[
13
]
that the junction conditions on a more general junction can be in general much more intricate. For example, the control parameter in the case of one entry and two exit lines could be at each time a function describing how to dispatch the entry to the exits. In addition, the method of proof developed in this paper break down in this three lines model because the equivalence between conservation law and Hamilton-Jacobi equations is lost.

The second strong restriction of the paper is the structure of
\psi
to obtain optimality conditions. If the existence of optimal solution can be derived under the general conditions given above, in order to study the optimality conditions we need to assume the cost functional to be written in the form

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\phi(x,t)u^{A}(x,t)+f(A(t))]dt,

(1.6)

for a suitable continuous map
\phi:\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
with a compact support and
f:[A_{0},0]\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
and convex. The reason for this restriction is that the derivative
u^{A}_{x}
has a very singular dependence with respect to the variations of
A
.
We show, however, that the map
A\to u^{A}(x,t)
has directional derivatives in many directions, which allows to derive optimality conditions. The cost function (
1.4
) can actually be written in the form (
1.6
) when the map
\psi
is an affine function of
\rho
or of
f(\rho)
. This includes the problems consisting in maximizing the flux or minimizing the travel time.

In this Hamilton-Jacobi setting, our main results are: the existence of an optimal minimizer
A\in L^{\infty}([0,T],[A_{0},0])
for the optimization problem (
1.5
) (Theorem
3.1
). Following
[
19
]
(see also
[
1
,
4
,
21
,
22
]
), it is known that, given a piecewise constant control
A
taking values in
[A_{0},0]
, the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.3
) has a unique viscosity solution
u^{A}
and that
\rho^{A}=-u^{A}_{x}
is the unique entropy solution of (
1.1
) (see
[
12
]
). The definition of a solution
u^{A}
, when
A
is just measurable, is made by using a representation formula (see Definition
2
), the equivalence between the two formulations (viscosity solution and representation formula) being known when
A
is piecewise constant. Assuming that the cost function
\mathcal{J}
takes the form (
1.6
), we also obtain an optimality condition on the minimizer (Theorem
4.2
). We then study thoroughly the nature of the maximizer when the problem consists in maximizing the flux on a space-time box of the form
[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{4}]
. Under suitable assumptions on the data, we show that the optimal control is piecewise constant and takes its values in
\{0,A_{0}\}
(Theorem
5.1
). We also give an example in which constant controls are not optimal (Proposition
5.23
).

The paper is organized as follows. We first introduce our standing assumptions and notations (Section
2
). Then we prove the existence of a minimizer to (
1.4
) (Section
3
). Section
4
gives the optimality condition, while Section
5
is devoted to problem of maximization of the flux in a box.

2.
Main definitions and assumptions

Standing assumptions.
Throughout the paper we assume that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}H^{L}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\ \text{and}\ H^{R}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\ \text{ are uniformly convex and}\\[2.84526pt]
\text{ of class $C^{2}-$Hamiltonians}\text{ and there exist}\ R^{L}\ \text{and}\ R^{R}\ \text{such that }\\[2.84526pt]
H^{L}(-R^{L})=H^{L}(0)=0=H^{R}(-R^{R})=H^{R}(0),\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{the initial condition $u_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is Lipschitz continuous and}\\[2.84526pt]
\text{$u_{0,x}\in(-R^{L},0)$ in $\mathbb{R}_{-}$ and $u_{0,x}\in(-R^{R},0)$ in $\mathbb{R}_{+}$,}\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where

A_{0}=\max\{\min_{p}H^{L}(p),\min_{p}H^{R}(p)\}.

We also denote by
\widehat{p}^{L}\in(-R^{L},0)
and
\widehat{p}^{R}\in(-\widehat{p}^{R},0)
the unique point such that
H^{L}(\widehat{p}^{L})=\min_{p}H^{L}(p)
and
H^{R}(\widehat{p}^{R})=\min_{p}H^{R}(p)
. Note that
H^{L}
is decreasing on
[-R^{L},\widehat{p}^{L}]
and increasing on
[\widehat{p}^{L},0]
, and
H^{R}
is decreasing on
[-R^{L},\widehat{p}^{R}]
and increasing on
[\widehat{p}^{R},0]

We write
H^{L,+}
and
H^{L,-}
for the increasing part and decreasing part of
H^{L}
respectively, that is,

H^{L,+}(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{p}{\min}H^{L}(p)&\text{if}\;p\leq\widehat{p}^{L},\\
H^{L}(p)&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.\qquad H^{L,-}(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}H^{L}(p)&\text{if}\;p\leq\widehat{p}^{L},\\[3.41432pt]
\underset{p}{\min}H^{L}(p)&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and we use a symmetrical notation for
H^{R}
.

Definition of a solution to (
1.3
).
Following
[
19
]
(see also
[
4
,
21
,
22
]
), it is known that, for any constant flux limiter
A\in[A_{0},0]
, there exists a unique viscosity solution
u^{A}
to (
1.3
), which can be represented as

u^{A}(x,t)=\inf_{\gamma\in H^{1},\;\gamma(t)=x}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\big(L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}-A{\bf 1}_{\{\gamma=0\}}\big)ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0))\big],

where the Lagrangians
L^{L}
and
L^{R}
are defined, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, by

L^{L}(\alpha)=\sup_{p\in[-R^{L},0]}\{\alpha p-H^{L}(p)\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ L^{R}(\alpha)=\sup_{p\in[-R^{R},0]}\{\alpha p-H^{R}(p)\}.

The existence, uniqueness and representation of a solution can easily be extended to piecewise constant maps
A:[0,T]\to[A_{0},0]
; see, for instance, the construction in
[
13
]
. Here we need to work with measurable maps
A:[0,T]\to[A_{0},0]
. Although it is possible to extend the notion of viscosity solution to this more general framework (see the paper in preparation
[
11
]
), we will not use this point of view here and only work with the representation formula.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A:[0,T]\to[A_{0},0]
be measurable. Then we denote by
u^{A}
the map

u^{A}(x,t)=\inf_{\gamma\in H^{1}(0,t),\;\gamma(t)=x}J^{A}(\gamma),

(2.3)

where

J^{A}(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{t}(L^{R}\big(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)<0\}}-A(s){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)=0\}}\big)ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0)).

It is easy to see that dynamic programming gives that
u^{A}
is Lipschitz continuous and satisfies (
1.3
) in the viscosity sense in
(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})\times(0,T)
. Note also that
u_{x}^{A}\in[-R^{L},0]
and
u^{A}_{t}\in[\min H^{L},0]
a.e. in
\{x<0\}
, while
u_{x}^{A}\in[-R^{R},0]
and
u^{A}_{t}\in[\min H^{R},0]
a.e. in
\{x>0\}
.

3.
Existence of an optimal control

We prove here the existence of a minimizer for the optimal control problem (
1.5
). This also leads to the existence of optimal trajectories for (
2.3
).

3.1.
The existence result

Throughout the section we assume that

\begin{split}&\text{the cost function}\ \widetilde{\psi}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times[A_{0},0]\to\mathbb{R}\ \text{is measurable and bounded,}\\
&\text{has compact support, and is continuous in the last two variables}\\
&\text{and is convex in the last variable.}\end{split}

(3.1)

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
3.1
).
Then, the problem (
1.5
) admits at least one solution.

The proof is given at the end of the section. The key remark is the continuity of the map
A\to u^{A}
with respect to the weak-
\ast
convergence of
A
, a fact which needs justification in view of the highly nonlinear way in which
u^{A}
depend on
A
.
In view of the uniform (in
A
) local semiconcavity of
u^{A}
in
(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})\times(0,T)
, this continuity then easily implies the continuity of the map
A\to u^{A}_{x}
.

The proof of the continuity of
u^{A}
in
A
is based on the following lemma, also proved at the end of the section, which implies that we can always assume that any
\varepsilon-
suboptimal path
\gamma
for
u^{A}(x,t)
is piecewise linear and may vanish on an interval.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) and fix a measurable
A:[0,t]\to[A_{0},0]
. For any
\gamma\in H^{1}((0,t);\mathbb{R})
, there exists
\widetilde{\gamma}\in H^{1}((0,t);\mathbb{R})
and
0\leq a\leq b\leq t
such that

(i)
\widetilde{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)
,

(ii)
J^{A}(\widetilde{\gamma})\leq J^{A}(\gamma)
, and

(iii) either
\widetilde{\gamma}
is a nonvanishing straight-line, or
\widetilde{\gamma}
is affine and does not vanish on
(0,a)
and on
(b,t)
, and
\widetilde{\gamma}\equiv 0
on
[a,b]
.

Moreover,
J^{A}(\widetilde{\gamma})<J^{A}(\gamma)
unless
\gamma=\widetilde{\gamma}
.

We continue with the continuity property of
u^{A}
in
A
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that a sequence
(A^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{\infty}([0,T],[A_{0},0])
converges weakly-
\ast
to
A\in L^{\infty}([0,T],[A_{0},0])
. Then the
u^{A^{n}}
’s converge locally uniformly to
u^{A}
. In addition, for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T]
, there exists an optimal trajectory
\gamma\in H^{1}([0,t],\mathbb{R})
with
\gamma(t)=x
for
u^{A}(x,t)
.

Proof.

Note first that, since the
u^{A^{n}}
’s are uniformly Lipschitz continuous, they converge along subsequences. Without loss of generality, we can assume that the whole sequence
(u^{A^{n}})
converges to some
u
and we check that
u=u^{A}
.

We first observe that
u\leq u^{A}
. Indeed, for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T]
and any
\gamma\in H^{1}([0,t])
such that
\gamma(t)=x
, we have, in view of the weak-
\ast
convergence of the
u^{A^{n}}
’s, that

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}u^{A^{n}}(x,t)\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\int_{0}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}-A^{n}{\bf 1}_{\{\gamma=0\}}\big]ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}-A{\bf 1}_{\{\gamma=0\}}\big]ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0))=J^{A}(\gamma).

Taking the infimum over
\gamma
gives the claim.

For the other inequality, we fix
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T]
.
Let
\gamma^{n}
be a
1/n-
minimizer for
u^{A^{n}}(x,t)
,
that is

J^{A^{n}}(\gamma^{n})\leq u^{A^{n}}(x,t)+1/n.

Following Lemma
3.2
, we may assume that either
\gamma^{n}
is affine and does not vanish on
(0,t)
, or there exist
0\leq a^{n}\leq b^{n}<t
such that
\gamma^{n}
is affine and does not vanish in
(b^{n},1]
,
\gamma
is affine and does not vanish in
[0,a^{n})
and
\gamma^{n}\equiv 0
in
[a^{n},b^{n}]
.

Next we show that
\gamma^{n}(0)
can be assumed to be bounded. For simplicity, we assume that
\gamma^{n}
vanishes on
(0,t)
with
a^{n}>0
, as the other cases can be treated with similar and simpler argument. Recall that

L^{L}(\alpha)=\sup_{p\in[-R^{L},0]}\{p\alpha-H^{L}(p)\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }\;\alpha\geq(H^{L})^{\prime}(0),\\
-R^{L}\alpha&\text{if }\;\alpha\leq(H^{L})^{\prime}(-R^{L}),\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

and the symmetric equality for
L^{R}
.

Since
\gamma^{n}(a^{n})=0
, we have
\gamma^{n}(s)=(a^{n}-s)\gamma^{n}(0)/a^{n}
on
[0,a^{n}]
. Thus

J^{A^{n}}(\gamma^{n})=a^{n}(L^{R}(-\gamma^{n}(0)/a^{n}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma^{n}(0)>0\}}+L^{L}(-\gamma^{n}(0)/a^{n}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma^{n}(0)<0\}})+u_{0}(\gamma^{n}(0))+R^{n},

where

R^{n}=\int_{b^{n}}^{t}(L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}^{n}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}^{n}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}})-\int_{a^{n}}^{b^{n}}A^{n}(s)

is bounded since
\gamma^{n}
is affine between
b^{n}
and
t
, vanishes at
b^{n}
and is equal to
x
at
t
.

On the other hand, if
-\gamma^{n}(0)/a^{n}\leq(H^{R})^{\prime}(-R^{R})
, that is,
\gamma^{n}(0)\geq y^{n}=-a^{n}(H^{R})^{\prime}(-R^{R})>0
, then, as
u_{0}
satisfies
u_{0,x}\geq-R^{R}
in
(0,\infty)
and (
3.2
) holds, we find

\displaystyle J^{A^{n}}(\gamma^{n})

\displaystyle=a^{n}L^{R}(-\gamma^{n}(0)/a^{n})+u_{0}(\gamma^{n}(0))+R^{n}

\displaystyle\geq R^{R}\gamma^{n}(0)+u_{0}(y^{n})+(-R^{R})(\gamma^{n}(0)-y^{n})+R^{n}

\displaystyle=a^{n}L^{R}(-y^{n}/a^{n})+u_{0}(y_{n})+R^{n},

which shows that we can replace
\gamma^{n}(0)
by
y^{n}=-a^{n}(H^{R})^{\prime}(-R^{R})
in this case.

In a symmetric way, if
-\gamma^{n}(0)/a^{n}\geq(H^{L})^{\prime}(0)
, that is,
\gamma^{n}(0)\leq z^{n}=-a^{n}(H^{L})^{\prime}(0)<0
, then, as
u_{0}
is nonincreasing and (
3.2
) holds,

\displaystyle J^{A^{n}}(\gamma^{n})

\displaystyle=a^{n}L^{L}(-\gamma^{n}(0)/a^{n})+u_{0}(\gamma^{n}(0))+R^{n}\geq u_{0}(z^{n})+R^{n}

\displaystyle=a^{n}L^{R}(-z^{n}/a^{n})+u_{0}(z_{n})+R^{n},

which proves that we can replace
\gamma^{n}(0)
by
z^{n}=-a^{n}(H^{L})^{\prime}(0)
.

In conclusion, we can assume that
\gamma^{n}(0)
is bounded.

Taking a subsequence, still labelled for simplicity in the same way, we now assume that the
\gamma^{n}
’s converge on
[0,t]
to some
\gamma
and that
a^{n}
’s and
b^{n}
’s converge to some
a
and
b
respectively, with
0\leq a\leq b<t
.
Note that the
\dot{\gamma}^{n}
’s converge to
\dot{\gamma}
in
L^{1}
.

If
a=0
or
a>0
and
\gamma
does not vanish in
[0,a)
, and if
b=t
or if
\gamma
does not vanish in
(b,t]
, it follows that
\{\gamma=0\}=[a,b]
is the limit of the interval
\{\widetilde{\gamma}^{n}=0\}=[a^{n},b^{n}]
and the same holds for the sets
\{\gamma<0\}
and
\{\gamma>0\}
. Since the
\dot{\gamma}^{n}
’s converge to
\dot{\gamma}
in
L^{1}
,
we can pass directly to the limit in the definition of
u^{A^{n}}(x,t)
to get

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)<0\}}+L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)>0\}}-A(s){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)=0\}}\ ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0))

\displaystyle\geq u^{A}(x,t),

which proves the equality
u(x,t)=u^{A}(x,t)
and the fact that
\gamma
is optimal for
u^{A}(x,t)
in this case.

We now suppose that
a>0
and
\gamma
vanishes somewhere in
(0,a)
, but that
b=t
or that
\gamma
does not vanish in
(b,t]
.
Then, since
\gamma(a)=0
and
\gamma
is affine,
\gamma\equiv 0
on
[0,a]
. Writing
\gamma^{n}(s)=\alpha^{n}s+\beta^{n}
on
(0,a^{n})
, with
\beta^{n}\neq 0
since
\gamma^{n}(0)\neq 0
and
\alpha^{n}\neq 0
since
\gamma^{n}(a^{n})=0

\alpha^{n}\to 0
and
\beta^{n}\to 0
.

Assume next, to fix the ideas, that
\beta^{n}<0
for any
n
up to a subsequence. In view of the definition of
u^{A^{n}}(x,t)
, we have

u^{A^{n}}(x,t)=a^{n}L^{L}(\alpha^{n})-\int_{a^{n}}^{b^{n}}A^{n}(s)ds+u_{0}(\beta^{n})+R^{n},

with

R^{n}=\int_{b^{n}}^{t}\big[L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}^{n}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma^{n}(s)<0\}}+L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}^{n}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma^{n}(s)>0\}}\big]\ ds.

The argument developed above yields that the
R^{n}
’s converge to

R=\int_{b}^{t}\big[L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)<0\}}+L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma(s)>0\}}\big]\ ds.

Thus

u(x,t)=aL^{L}(0)-\int_{a}^{b}A(s)ds+u_{0}(0)+R.

As
L^{L}(0)=-\min_{p}H^{L}(p)\geq-A_{0}\geq-A(s)
for a.e.
s\in[0,a]
, we get

u(x,t)\geq-\int_{0}^{b}A(s)ds+u_{0}(0)+R=J^{A}(\gamma)\geq u^{A}(x,t).

This shows that
u(x,t)=u^{A}(x,t)
and the fact that
\gamma
is optimal for
u^{A}(x,t)
in this case.

If
\beta^{n}>0
up to a subsequence, or if
b<t
and
\gamma
vanishes in
(b,t]
, we argue similarly.

In conclusion, we have proved that
u=u^{A}
and, therefore, that the sequence
(u^{A^{n}})
converges to
u^{A}
. In addition we have also shown the existence of an optimal trajectory
\gamma
for
u^{A}(x,t)
for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T]
.
∎

We continue with the proofs of Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
A^{n}
be a minimizing subsequence. Extracting a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that the
A^{n}
’s converge weakly
-\ast
to some
A\in L^{\infty}([0,T],[A_{0},0])
and, therefore, that the
u^{A^{n}}
’s converge locally uniformly to
u^{A}
.

Since the Hamiltonians are uniformly convex, the
u^{A^{n}}
’s are uniformly locally semiconcave in
(x,t)
on compact subsets of
(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})\times(0,T)
. Thus the bounded sequence
(u^{A^{n}}_{x})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
L^{1}_{Loc}
to
u^{A}_{x}
. The continuity of the compactly supported
\widetilde{\psi}
and its convexity in the last variable, along standard lower semicontinuity arguments, yield that
A
is a minimum of
\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Fix
\gamma\in H^{1}(0,t)
. If
\gamma
does not vanish on
[0,t]
, then we denote
\widetilde{\gamma}
be the linear interpolation between
\gamma(0)
and
\gamma(t)
. By the strict convexity of
L^{L}
, if
\gamma
remains negative, and of
L^{R}
, if
\gamma
remains positive, we have immediately that
J^{A}(\widetilde{\gamma})\leq J^{A}(\gamma)
, with an equality only if
\gamma=\widetilde{\gamma}
.

Let us now assume that
\gamma
vanishes in
[0,t]
and let

a=\sup\{a^{\prime}\in(0,t)\;:\text{$\gamma$ does not vanish in $[0,a^{\prime})$}\},

b=\inf\{b^{\prime}\in(0,t)\;:\text{$\gamma$ does not vanish in $(b^{\prime},t]$}\}.

By convention, we set
a=0
if
\gamma(0)=0
and/or
b=t
if
\gamma(t)=0
. Note that, since
\gamma
vanishes in
[0,t]
,
0\leq a\leq b\leq t
.

We define
\widetilde{\gamma}
as follows: if
a>0
, then
\widetilde{\gamma}
is on
[0,a]
the linear interpolation between
\gamma(0)
and
0
. On
[a,b]
, we set
\widetilde{\gamma}\equiv 0
. Finally, if
b<t
, then
\widetilde{\gamma}
is on
[b,t]
the linear interpolation between
0
and
\gamma(t)
. Note that
\widetilde{\gamma}
is affine and does not vanish on
(0,a)
and on
(b,t)
, and
\widetilde{\gamma}\equiv 0
on
[a,b]
. In addition
\widetilde{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)
.

Using again the strict convexity of
L^{L}
and
L^{R}
we have immediately that

\int_{[0,a]\cup[b,t]}\big[L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}+L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}\big]ds\geq\int_{[0,a]\cup[b,t]}\big[L^{L}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}}){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{\gamma}<0\}}+L^{R}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}}){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{\gamma}>0\}}\big]ds,

with an equality only if
\gamma=\widetilde{\gamma}
on
[0,a]\cup[b,t]
.

We now compare the cost for
\gamma
and
\widetilde{\gamma}
on
[a,b]
. The open set
\{\gamma\neq 0\}\cap(a,b)
is covered by a countable union of disjoint open intervals, and
\gamma=0
at the end points of each of these intervals.

Let
(c,d)
be one of these intervals and assume, without any loss of generality, that
\gamma>0
there. Then using the strict convexity of
L^{R}
and that
\gamma(c)=\gamma(d)=0
, we find

\int_{c}^{d}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}(s))ds>\int_{c}^{d}L^{R}(0)ds+\int_{a}^{b}(L^{R})^{\prime}(0)(\dot{\gamma}^{n}(s)-0)ds=\int_{c}^{d}L^{R}(0)ds,

which, in view of the fact that
L^{R}(0)=\sup_{p\in[-R^{0},0]}-H^{R}(p)\geq-A_{0}\geq-A(s)
a.e. in
s
, further yields

\int_{c}^{d}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}(s))ds>-\int_{c}^{d}A(s)ds.

Arguing similarly for any of the other open intervals, we see that

\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}(s)){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}-A(s){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma=0\}}\big]ds\\[2.84526pt]
\geq-\int_{a}^{b}A(s)ds=-\int_{a}^{b}A(s){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{\gamma}=0\}}ds,\end{split}

with an equality only if
\gamma\equiv 0
in
[a,b]
.

This shows that
J^{A}(\widetilde{\gamma})\leq J^{A}(\gamma)
with equality only if
\gamma=\widetilde{\gamma}
.
∎

3.2.
Structure of optimal trajectories

Given an admissible control, we now investigate the existence optimal trajectories for the optimal control problem (
2.3
) and explain their structure.

The representation formula (
2.3
) implies that, for any admissible control
A
, we have

u^{A}\leq u^{A_{0}}
. We can therefore expect that, for points
(x,t)
at which
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
, optimal trajectories have specific structure. This is exactly what the second part of the following proposition says.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). For any
A\in L^{\infty}([0,T],[A_{0},0])
, there exist an optimal path
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}
in (
2.3
) which is
either affine and does not vanish on
(0,t)
, or there exist
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
such that
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}
is affine and does not vanish on
(0,t_{1})
and on
(t_{2},T)
, and vanishes on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
.
Moreover,
if
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T]
is such that
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
and
\gamma
is optimal for
u^{A}(x,t)
, then
\gamma
vanishes in
(0,t)
and, if

\tau^{+}=\sup\{s\geq 0,\;\gamma(s)=0\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tau^{-}=\inf\{s\geq 0,\;\gamma(s)=0\},

then
\tau^{-}<\tau^{+}
,
u^{A}(0,\tau^{+})<u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{+})
and
u^{A}(0,\tau^{-})=u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{-})
.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
3.2
with Proposition
3.3
easily gives the first claim.

We now assume that
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
. If
\gamma
does not vanish or if
\tau^{-}=\tau^{+}
, then

u^{A}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}\big]ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0))=J^{A_{0}}(\gamma)\geq u^{A_{0}}(x,t),

which contradicts the assumption
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
. Thus
\tau^{-}
and
\tau^{+}
exist and
\tau^{-}<\tau^{+}
.

Assume that
u^{A}(0,\tau^{+})=u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{+})
. Dynamic programming yields that

\displaystyle u^{A_{0}}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau^{+}}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}\big]ds+u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{+})

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau^{+}}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}}\big]ds+u^{A}(0,\tau^{+})=u^{A}(x,t),

which contradicts the assumption
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
. So
u^{A}(0,\tau^{+})<u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{+})
.

We now check the equality
u^{A}(0,\tau^{-})=u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{-})
. If
\tau^{-}=0
, the result is obvious since
u^{A}(\cdot,0)=u^{A_{0}}(\cdot,0)=u_{0}
. We now assume that
\tau^{-}>0
. Then
\gamma
does not vanish on
[0,\tau^{-})
and thus, by dynamic programming,

u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{-})\leq\int_{0}^{\tau^{-}}(L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma<0\}})ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0))=u^{A}(0,\tau^{-})\leq u^{A_{0}}(0,\tau^{-}),

which proves the equality.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that there are in general infinitely many optimal controls for
\mathcal{J}
in (
1.5
). Thus requiring additional conditions on the optimal control is natural. The additional condition in the next result consisting in minimizing
\int_{0}^{T}A
is especially suitable because its means that one wants to perturb the traffic as little as possible. Note that
A\equiv A_{0}
means no speed limit or traffic light.

A consequence of the structure of the optimal trajectories is the following remark.

Lemma 3.6
.

If (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
3.1
) hold and
f
is nondecreasing, then any minimizer
\overline{A}
which in addition minimizes
\int_{0}^{T}\overline{A}
among all the minimizers satisfies

\overline{A}(t)=A_{0}\qquad\text{a.e. in }\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)=u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}.

Proof.

Define

\widetilde{A}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{0}&\text{if }u^{\overline{A}}(0,t)=u^{A_{0}}(0,t),\\[2.84526pt]
\overline{A}(t)&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

It follows, as we show below, that

u^{\overline{A}}=u^{\widetilde{A}}\ \text{ in}\ \mathbb{R}\times(0,T).

(3.3)

Assuming (
3.3
), we first complete the proof of the lemma. Indeed,
f
being nondecreasing yields
\mathcal{J}(\overline{A})\geq\mathcal{J}(\widetilde{A})
. Thus
\widetilde{A}
is also a minimizer,
\overline{A}\geq\widetilde{A}
by construction and, by assumption on
\overline{A}
,
\int_{0}^{T}\overline{A}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{A}
. It follows that
\overline{A}=\widetilde{A}
equals
A_{0}
on
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)=u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
.

We now prove (
3.3
). Since
u^{\overline{A}}\leq u^{\widetilde{A}}\leq u^{A_{0}}
, and thus
u^{\overline{A}}=u^{\widetilde{A}}
in
\{u^{\overline{A}}=u^{A_{0}}\}
, we fix
(x,t)\in(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})\times(0,T)
such that
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
.

Let
\gamma
be optimal for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
. By Proposition
3.4
, we know that there exists
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t
such that
\gamma
is affine and nonvanishing on
(0,t_{1})
and
(t_{2},t)
and vanishes on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
, with
u^{\overline{A}}(0,t_{1})=u^{A_{0}}(0,t_{1})
and
u^{\overline{A}}(0,t_{2})<u^{A_{0}}(0,t_{2})
.

Let
\tau=\sup\{s\;:u^{\overline{A}}(\gamma(s),s)=u^{A_{0}}(\gamma(s),s)\}
. Then
t_{1}\leq\tau<t_{2}
,

\gamma(\tau)=0
and
u^{\overline{A}}(\gamma(\tau),\tau)=u^{A_{0}}(\gamma(\tau),\tau)
, so that
u^{\overline{A}}(\gamma(\tau),\tau)=u^{\widetilde{A}}(\gamma(\tau),\tau)
. Moreover
u^{\overline{A}}(0,s)<u^{A_{0}}(0,s)
on
(\tau,t_{2})
and thus
\widetilde{A}=\overline{A}
on
(\tau,t_{2})
. By dynamic programming,

\displaystyle u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{2}}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\gamma>0}+\int_{t_{2}}^{t}L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\gamma<0}-\int_{\tau}^{t_{2}}\overline{A}(s)ds+u^{\overline{A}}(\gamma(\tau),\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{2}}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\gamma>0}+\int_{t_{2}}^{t}L^{L}(\dot{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\gamma<0}-\int_{\tau}^{t_{2}}\widetilde{A}(s)ds+u^{\widetilde{A}}(\gamma(\tau),\tau)

\displaystyle\geq u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)\geq u^{\overline{A}}(x,t).

Hence
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)=u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)
, which gives the result.

∎

4.
Optimality conditions

In order to derive the optimality conditions for the problem consisting in minimizing (
1.4
), we assume that
\widetilde{\psi}
is linear in
u_{x}
or in
H(u_{x})
with separated dependence in
A
. Then (
1.4
) can be rewritten (after integration by parts) in the form

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t)u^{A}(x,t)dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}f(A(t))dt,

(4.1)

where

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$\phi:\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}$ is a continuous map with a compact support}\\
\hskip-122.85876pt\text{ and $f:[A_{0},0]\to\mathbb{R}$ is convex and $C^{1}$.}\end{array}

(4.2)

The function
f
could also depend on time. We omit this dependence for simplicity of notation. Throughout this section, we fix
\overline{A}
a minimizer for
\mathcal{J}
.

Note that, in the case
f\equiv 0
, the problem is interesting only if
\phi
changes sign. Indeed, if
\phi
is nonpositive, then the optimal solution is
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
, while if
\phi
is nonnegative, then
\overline{A}\equiv 0
since, for any admissible
A
,
u^{0}\leq u^{A}\leq u^{A_{0}}
.

4.1.
Examples

We list a few example leading to functionals of the form (
4.1
).

4.1.1.
Maximization of a weighted density

The goal is to optimize the density against a smooth compactly supported weight
\xi
, that is,

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(x,t)\rho^{A}(x,t)dxdt.

Recalling that
\rho^{A}=-u^{A}_{x}
, the functional can be rewritten, after integration by parts, as

\mathcal{J}(A)=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi_{x}(x,t)u^{A}(x,t)dxdt,

which is of the form (
4.1
).

4.1.2.
Maximization of the flux

In traffic problems, it is natural to optimize the flux on some portion of the exit line. In other words, given a space-time box
(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{1},t_{4})
, the problem is to aim at maximizing

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}f(\rho^{A}(x,t))dxdt,

where
\rho^{A}
solves (
1.1
). In terms of Hamilton-Jacobi equations, this amounts to maximizing

-\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}H(u^{A}_{x}(x,t))dxdt=\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}u^{A}_{t}(x,t)dxdt.

Approximating the indicatrix function
{\bf 1}_{[x_{1},x_{1}]\times[t_{1},t_{4}]}
by a smooth, compactly supported function
\xi
, this boils down to minimizing

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{T}\xi(x,t)u^{A}_{t}(x,t)dxdt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{T}\xi_{t}(x,t)u^{A}(x,t)dxdt,

which is of the form (
4.1
). This problem will be studied in details in Section
5
.

4.1.3.
Minimization of the travel time

In
[
15
]
the authors discuss the optimization of the averaged travel time by acting on the entry condition. Assuming that the exit road is initially empty, that is,
\rho_{0}=0
in
(0,\infty)
, and becomes eventually empty, that is,
\rho(\cdot,\infty)=0
, the average travel time to reach a given position
\overline{x}>0
is given by

\frac{1}{Q_{\rm in}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\tau_{\overline{x}}(x)\rho_{0}(x)dx=\frac{1}{Q_{\rm in}}\int_{0}^{\infty}tf^{R}(\rho^{A}(\overline{x},t))dt,

where
Q_{\rm in}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\rho_{0}^{L}
and
\tau_{\overline{x}}(x)
is the time needed to reach
\overline{x}
from the position
x<0
.

If we now control the junction condition and write the problem in terms of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation, we get

\mathcal{J}(A)=-\frac{1}{Q_{\rm in}}\int_{0}^{\infty}tH^{R}(u^{A}_{x}(\overline{x},t))dt=\frac{1}{Q_{\rm in}}\int_{0}^{\infty}tu^{A}_{t}(\overline{x},t))dt,

where, under the constraint that the road must be eventually empty, we must have
u^{A}(\overline{x},\infty)=u_{\max}=u_{0}(-\infty)
.

Thus, the problem becomes to minimize

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q_{\rm in}}\left(\left[t(u^{A}(\overline{x},\cdot)-u_{\max})\right]_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{A}(\overline{x},t)-u_{\max})dt\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{Q_{\rm in}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{A}(\overline{x},t)-u_{\max})dt.

This quantity is of the form (
4.1
), at least after a suitable regularization. However, the solution is obvious. Indeed, since, for any admissible
A
,
u^{A}\leq u^{A_{0}}
,
\mathcal{J}
is clearly minimal when
A\equiv A_{0}
. The computation above is sketchy, but the proof can easily made rigorous.

4.2.
Statement of the optimality conditions

Before discussing the optimality conditions, we need to introduce notation on the optimal trajectories staying on
\{x=0\}
.

Notation for the optimal trajectories for
u^{\overline{A}}

Let
(x,t)
be a point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}
with
x\neq 0
and
t\in(0,T)
. It follows, from the structure of optimizers in Proposition
3.4
and standard relations between the derivative of
u^{\overline{A}}
and the derivative of the optimal solution, that there exist
t_{2}\geq 0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
such that any optimal trajectory
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}
for
u^{\overline{A}}
is affine on
[t_{2},t]
with either
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}(t_{2})=0
, or
t_{2}=0
and
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}(0)=y
. Note that
t_{2}
and
y
are independent of the choice of the optimal solution. From Proposition
3.4
, we also know that, if
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}(t_{2})=0
, then there exists
t_{1}\in[0,t_{2}]
such that
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}
vanishes on
(t_{1},t_{2})
and is affine and does not vanish on
(0,t_{1})
. Here, however,
t_{1}
may depend on the optimal solution. We denote by
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}
the optimal solution for which
t_{1}
is the smallest, and by
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{-}
the optimal solution for which
t_{1}
is the largest.

Remark 4.1
.

Unfortunately, it seems that the set of points
(x,t)
for which
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{-}\neq\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}
can be of positive measure. This is in contrast with standard optimal control problems with uniformly convex Hamiltonians for which minimizers are a.e. unique. Thus it is not clear that one of the inequalities in the next theorem is an equality, even in the set
\{A_{0}<A<0\}
. We show later in Corollary
4.3
that this can, however, be the case in some part of
(0,T)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
4.2
), and let
\overline{A}
be a minimizer for
\mathcal{J}
defined by (
4.1
).
Then, for a.e.
s\in[0,T]
,

\int_{s}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}dxdt-f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\leq 0\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ \ \{\overline{A}<0\},

and

\int_{s}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{-}(s)=0\}}dxdt-f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\geq 0\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ \ \{\overline{A}>A_{0}\}.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, let
\beta\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
be such that
\beta(s)\geq 0
a.e. and
\beta=0
on
\{\overline{A}\geq-\varepsilon\}
. Then
\big(\overline{A}+h\beta\big)([0,T])\subset[A_{0},0]
for any
h\in(0,\varepsilon/\|\beta\|_{\infty})
, and, hence,
\overline{A}+h\beta
is an admissible control.

Let
(x,t)
be a point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}
with
x\neq 0
and
t\in(0,T)
. By the representation formula for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
, which can be reduced to a finite dimensional minimization problem in view of Proposition
3.4
, and the envelop theorem,
A\to u^{A}
has directional derivatives in any admissible direction, and, thus, in the direction
\beta
, with

\partial_{A}u^{\overline{A}}(\beta)(x,t)=\min_{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}\;\text{optimal for }\;u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)}-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}(s)=0\}}ds.

Since
\beta\geq 0
, the minimum is reached for the optimal trajectory which remains the most at
0
and thus

\partial_{A}u^{\overline{A}}(\beta)(x,t)=-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}ds.

Then,

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{A})(\beta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t)\partial_{A}u^{\overline{A}}(\beta)(x,t)dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\beta(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t)\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}dsdxdt+\int_{0}^{T}f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\beta(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)\big[\int_{s}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}dxdt-f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\big]ds.

As this inequality holds for any
\beta
as above, it follows that

\int_{s}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}dxdt-f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\leq 0\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ \ \{\overline{A}\leq-\varepsilon\}.

We can then conclude that the first inequality holds since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary. The other inequality can be obtained in the symmetrical way, choosing nonpositive perturbations
\beta
.

∎

We introduce next the notion of the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
.
We say that
(a,b)
is the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
if, for any other connected component
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
, we have
b^{\prime}\leq a
. It is not always true that this last connected component exists. We will show, however, below, that this is the case under suitable assumptions.

It turns out that we can improve the optimality conditions on
(a,T]
, when
(a,b)
is the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
. This is the topic of the next corollary, which is a special case of Theorem
4.2
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
4.2
) and let
\overline{A}
be a minimizer for
\mathcal{J}
defined by (
4.1
). If
(a,b)
is the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
, then, for a.e.
s\in(a,T]
,

\int_{s}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}dxdt=f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ \ \{A_{0}<\overline{A}<0\},

\int_{s}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}dxdt\geq f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ \ \{\overline{A}=0\},

and

\int_{s}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}dxdt\leq f^{\prime}(\overline{A}(s))\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ \ \{\overline{A}=A_{0}\}.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
using perturbations
\beta
concentrated on
(a,T)
. Proposition
3.4
implies that
\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}=\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{-}
on
(a,t)
. Hence, we conclude that

\partial_{A}u^{\overline{A}}(\beta)(x,t)=-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}_{x,t}^{+}(s)=0\}}ds,

and we can complete the proof as for Theorem
4.2
.

∎

We conclude this section showing that, under suitable conditions,
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}=\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}
unless two connected components of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
touch.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
4.2
), let
\overline{A}
be a minimizer for
\mathcal{J}
defined by (
4.1
), and suppose that
a_{1}<b_{1}<a_{0}<b_{0}
are such that
(a_{1},b_{1})
and
(a_{0},b_{0})
are connected components of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
with
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)=u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)
in
[b_{1},a_{0}]
. Assume, in addition that, for any
t\in(0,T]
, any optimal trajectory for
u^{A_{0}}(0,t)
is positive on
[0,t)
. Finally, we suppose that
A_{0}=\min_{p}H^{R}(0)
.

Then, for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}
for which there is
s\in[a_{0},b_{0})
with
\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}(s)=0
,
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
does not vanish on
[0,a_{0})
and
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}=\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}
on
[a_{0},t]
.

Proof.

Let
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
for which there is
s\in[a_{0},b_{0})
with
\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}(s)=0
. Then
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0
.

Assume that
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
vanishes at some
\overline{s}<a_{0}
. Then
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
vanishes on
[\overline{s},a_{0}]
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\overline{s}\geq b_{1}
(otherwise we replace
\overline{s}
by
b_{1}
).

Let
\gamma
be optimal for
u^{A_{0}}(0,\overline{s})
. As
u^{A_{0}}(0,\overline{s})=u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{s})
and
\gamma
does not vanish on
(0,\overline{s})
,
J^{A_{0}}(\gamma)=J^{\overline{A}}(\gamma)
and
\gamma
is also optimal for
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{s})
. Then dynamic programming implies that

\widetilde{\gamma}(\tau)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma(\tau)&\text{if }\tau\in[0,\overline{s}],\\[2.84526pt]
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(\tau)&\text{if }\tau\in[\overline{s},t]\end{array}\right.

is also optimal for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
, and, in turn, again
by dynamic programming,
\widetilde{\gamma}_{|_{[0,a_{0}]}}
is optimal for
u^{\overline{A}}(0,a_{0})
.

We know from Lemma
3.6
that
\overline{A}=A_{0}
on
[\overline{s},a_{0}]
and, to fix the ideas, we assume here that
\gamma>0
on
(0,\overline{s})
. Then,

\displaystyle u^{\overline{A}}(0,a_{0})=J^{\overline{A}}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{|_{[0,a_{0}]}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\overline{s}}L^{R}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}})-\int_{\overline{s}}^{a_{0}}\overline{A}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{0}}L^{R}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}})>u^{A_{0}}(0,a_{0})\geq u^{\overline{A}}(0,a_{0}),

which is a contradiction. Note that the third equality above comes from the equality
-\overline{A}=-A_{0}=L^{R}(0)
on
[\overline{s},a_{0}]
and the strict inequality from the strict convexity of
L^{R}
and the fact that
\widetilde{\gamma}
is not a straight line.

Finally, we know that
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}=\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}
until the first time
s_{1}
they touch
\{x=0\}
. Since, in view of the structure of minimizers,
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
and
\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}
vanish on an interval, they must vanish on
[s,s_{1}]
. Then they must vanish on
[a_{0},s]
because
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)
on
(a_{0},s)
. This shows that
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}=\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}
on
[a_{0},t]
.
∎

5.
Maximizing the flux

We analyze here the second example in the previous section which is about optimizing the flux on the exit line which is formulated as follows.

Fix
\xi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T))
with compact support and, for
A:[0,T]\to[A_{0},0]
, the aim is to minimize the functional

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(x,t)f(\rho^{A})(x,t)dxdt,

which, in view of the relationship between the conservation law and the Hamilton-Jacobi equation, is rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(x,t)f(\rho^{A}(c,t))dxdt=\inf_{A}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(x,t)(-H(u^{A}_{x}(x,t)))dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(x,t)u^{A}_{t}(x,t)dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-\xi_{t}(x,t))u^{A}(x,t).

Here we want to increase the traffic in the space-time rectangle
(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{1},t_{4})
, where
x_{1}>0
and
0<t_{1}<t_{4}<T
. For this, we choose
\xi(x,t)=-\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}(t)
to be an approximation of
-{\bf 1}_{(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{1},t_{4})}
, so that

\phi(x,t)=-\xi_{t}(x,t)=\phi(x,t)=\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}^{\prime}(t),

with
\psi_{1}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
continuous, vanishing outside
(x_{1},x_{2})
and positive in
(x_{1},x_{2})
. The
C^{1}-
map
\psi_{2}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is such that
\psi_{2}^{\prime}
vanishes outside the two intervals
(t_{1},t_{2})
and
(t_{3},t_{4})
, is positive on
(t_{1},t_{2})
and negative on
(t_{3},t_{4})
.

We summarize all the above in

\begin{split}\phi:\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)\ &\text{is continuous and compactly supported in}\ (x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{1},t_{4})\ \text{and}\\
&\{\phi>0\}=(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{1},t_{2})\ \text{and}\ \{\phi<0\}=(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{3},t_{4}),\end{split}

(5.1)

and write

\mathcal{J}(A)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x,t)u^{A}(x,t)dxdt.

(5.2)

We will also assume that

\frac{x_{1}}{t_{2}}>\frac{x_{2}}{t_{3}}.

(5.3)

Note that this condition, which is technical, holds if
t_{3}
is large enough or if
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are close. As we will see below, (
5.3
) ensures that no characteristic can cross both
\{\phi>0\}
and
\{\phi<0\}
.

The goal is to show to show the existence of an optimal control
\overline{A}
which is bang-bang, that is, it only takes its values in
0
and
A_{0}
.

In order to prove that this optimal control is piecewise constant, that is, the set
\{\overline{A}=0\}
consists only of a finite number of intervals, we need to add three more technical conditions, which we explain next.

The first, which is often used in the literature, is that
H^{R}
and
H^{L}
have the same minimum value, that is,

\min H^{R}=\min H^{L}=A_{0},

(5.4)

which implies that
L^{R}(0)=L^{L}(0)=-A_{0}
.

The second one is about the behavior of the optimal trajectories for
u^{A_{0}}
. We assume that

for any
t\in(0,T)
,
\gamma\equiv 0
is not optimal for
u^{A_{0}}(0,t)
.

(5.5)

Assuming (
5.4
), it is easily checked that a sufficient condition for (
5.5
) to hold
is that

\begin{split}&\text{ either $u_{0}$ has a right derivative $u_{0,x}(0+)$ at $0$ and $(H^{R})^{\prime}(u_{0,x}(0+))<0$}\\
&\text{or $u_{0}$ has a left derivative $u_{0,x}(0-)$ at $0$ and $(H^{L})^{\prime}(u_{0,x}(0-))>0$.}\end{split}

The last assumption is on the initial condition
\rho_{0}=-u_{0,x}
. It says that
\rho_{0}
cannot oscillate too much between
0
and positive values, that is,

\{x\leq 0\;:u_{0,x}(x)=0\}\;\text{consists a.e. of only finitely many intervals}.

(5.6)

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
5.1
), (
5.3
), (
5.4
), (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) and, for any measurable
A:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{J}(A)
be given by (
5.2
). Then, there exists a minimizer
\overline{A}
which takes values in
\{A_{0},0\}
only and the set
\{\overline{A}=0\}
consists a.e. of at most a finite number of intervals.

Remark 5.2
.

We explain in Remark
5.21
below that, if in addition,

u_{0,x}>0\;\text{a.e. in }(-\infty,0)\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{0,x}<R^{R}\;\text{a.e. in }(0,\infty),

then the set
\{\overline{A}=0\}
is made of at most two intervals.

The proof of the theorem is given at the end of the ongoing section. We first show that, under less restrictive conditions, there exists a bang-bang optimal control
\overline{A}
. However, this control has possibly a countable number of discontinuities. To prove that the number of discontinuities is finite requires some extra work.

For the proof, we start investigating the behavior of the optimal solution starting from the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
, since it controls the structure of the whole problem. Then we obtain the finiteness result by refining the analysis of the connected components.

5.1.
Existence of bang-bang optimal control

We begin with the result about the bang-bang minimizing strategy which requires fewer assumptions.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
5.1
), (
5.3
) and, for any measurable
A:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{J}(A)
be given by (
5.2
). If
\min H^{R}=A_{0}
, then there exists a minimizer
\overline{A}
which takes only values in
\{A_{0},0\}
. In addition, the set
\{\overline{A}=0\}
consists a.e. of at most a countable number of intervals.

Throughout this part we work under the assumption of Theorem
5.3
. The proof, which requires several steps, is given at the end of the subsection.

Since, in principle, there are more than one minimizing controls, it is necessary to introduce some further properties of the minimizers, which will allow us to distinguish among them.

Let

\mathcal{A}=\{\overline{A}\;\text{minimizer of $\mathcal{J}$}\},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{\overline{A}\in\mathcal{A}\;:\int_{0}^{T}\overline{A}=\min_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\int_{0}^{T}A\}

and

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\{\overline{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\;:\int_{0}^{T}s\overline{A}(s)ds=\min_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{1}}\int_{0}^{T}sA(s)ds\}

Requiring that
\overline{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is quite natural as it was explained in Remark
3.5
. The condition that
\overline{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is mostly technical but plays a key role to prove that the control is bang-bang.

From now on we fix
\overline{A}
in
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. As
\overline{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{1}
, we know from Lemma
3.6
that
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
on
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)=u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
. It remains to understand what happens in the set
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
.

The first step in this direction is the next lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

\tau=\sup\left\{s\in[t_{1},t_{2}]:\begin{array}[]{l}\text{there exists}\;(x,t)\in(x_{1},x_{2})\times(s,t_{2}),\\
u^{\overline{A}}\;\text{is differentiable at $(x,t)$,}\\
\text{$u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)$ and $\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}(s)=0$}\end{array}\right\},

(5.7)

with
\tau=0
, if the right-hand side is empty. Then
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
a.e. in
(\tau,T)
.

Proof.

Let

\widetilde{A}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{0}&\text{on $(\tau,T)$,}\\[2.84526pt]
\overline{A}(t)&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Since
A_{0}\leq\widetilde{A}\leq\overline{A}
and
\widetilde{A}=A_{0}
in
[0,\tau]
, it follows that
u^{\overline{A}}\leq u^{\widetilde{A}}\leq u^{A_{0}}
with
u^{\widetilde{A}}=u^{\overline{A}}
in
\{u^{\overline{A}}=u^{A_{0}}\}
and in
[0,\infty)\times[0,\tau]
.

Let now
(x,t)\in(0,\infty)\times(\tau,T)
be a point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}
such that
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
. If
\phi(x,t)<0
, then
\phi(x,t)u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)\geq\phi(x,t)u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)
. If
\phi(x,t)>0
, then
x\in(x_{1},x_{2})
and thus, by the definition of
\tau
,
\{\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}=0\}\subset[0,\tau]
. Recall that
\widetilde{A}=\overline{A}
on
(0,\tau)
, so that

\displaystyle u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=J^{\overline{A}}(\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t})=\int_{0}^{t}(L^{R}(\dot{\widehat{\gamma}}^{-}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\widehat{\gamma}}^{-}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}<0\}}-\overline{A}{\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}=0\}})ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(L^{R}(\dot{\widehat{\gamma}}^{-}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\widehat{\gamma}}^{-}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}<0\}}-\widetilde{A}{\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t}=0\}})ds=J^{\widetilde{A}}(\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x,t})

\displaystyle\geq u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)\geq u^{\overline{A}}(x,t).

This proves that
u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)=u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
in this case.

In conclusion,
\phi(x,t)u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)\leq\phi(x,t)u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
and, therefore,
\mathcal{J}(\widetilde{A})\leq\mathcal{J}(\overline{A})
. Thus
\widetilde{A}
is also optimal and, as
\widetilde{A}\leq\overline{A}
, we obtain
\widetilde{A}=\overline{A}
since
\overline{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{1}
. So
\overline{A}=A_{0}
on
[\tau,T]
.

∎

From now on, we implicitly assume that
\tau
is positive, as Theorems
5.1
and
5.3
obviously hold if
\tau=0
since then
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
.

The next lemma explains the role of (
5.3
).

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(x,t)\in[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[t_{3},t_{4}]
and
\gamma
be optimal for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
. If
\gamma(h)=0
for some
h\in[0,t]
, then
\gamma([h,t])\cap\{\phi>0\}=\emptyset
. Moreover,

\sup\{s\;:\gamma(s)=0\}\geq\tau.

Proof.

In view of the structure of optimal trajectories established in Proposition
3.4
,
there exists
0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<t
such that
\gamma
is a straight line on
[s_{2},t]
, vanishes on
[s_{1},s_{2}]
and is a nonvanishing straight line on
[0,s_{1})
. Therefore
h\in[s_{1},s_{2}]
.

In order to check that
\gamma([h,t])\cap\{\phi>0\}=\emptyset
, it is enough to show that, if
s\in[h,t]\cap(t_{1},t_{2})
, then
\gamma(s)\leq x_{1}
. Let
s\in[h,t]\cap(t_{1},t_{2})
. Then, it follows from the convexity of the map
s^{\prime}\to\gamma(s^{\prime})
on
[s_{1},T]
and the facts that
\gamma(h)=0
and
\gamma(t)=x
, that

\gamma(s)\leq(s-h)x/(t-h)\leq(s-h)x_{2}/(t-h)\leq sx_{2}/t\leq t_{2}x_{2}/t_{3}.

Hence, by assumption (
5.3
),

\gamma(s)\leq t_{2}x_{2}/t_{3}<x_{1},

which shows that
\gamma([h,t])\cap\{\phi>0\}=\emptyset
.

Let us now check that
s_{2}=\sup\{s\;:\gamma(s)=0\}\geq\tau
. If not, in view of the definition of
\tau
, there exist
(x_{n},t_{n})\in(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{1},t_{2})
such that
\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x_{n},t_{n}}
vanishes at
\tau_{n}
and
\tau_{n}\to\tau
. Moreover, we can assume, without loss of generality, that
\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x_{n},t_{n}}
is a straight line on
[\tau_{n},t_{n}]
.

Since
s_{2}<\tau
, it follows that
s_{2}<\tau_{n}
for
n
large. Moreover, we know that
0=\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x_{n},t_{n}}(\tau_{n})<\gamma(\tau_{n})
. Finally, the first part of the proof applied to
h=s_{2}
and
s=t_{n}\in[s_{2},t]\cap(t_{1},t_{2})
yields
\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x_{n},t_{n}}(t_{n})=x_{n}\geq x_{1}>\gamma(t_{n})
.
Thus, we infer that there exists
s\in(\tau_{n},t_{n})
such that
\widehat{\gamma}^{-}_{x_{n},t_{n}}(s)=\gamma(s)
.

But two optimal trajectories cannot cross in
(0,\infty)\times(0,T)
. Thus there is a contradiction and we must have
s_{2}\geq\tau
.

∎

The next lemma gives information about the location of the left point of any
connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
in terms of
\tau
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(a,b)
be any connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
. Then
a<\tau
.

Proof.

If
a\geq\tau
, it follows from Lemma
5.4
that
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
on
[\tau,T]
and, thus, on
(a,b)
.

Fix
\overline{t}\in(a,b)
and let
\gamma
be optimal for
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{t})
. Then from the structure of optimizers,
\gamma
has to vanish on
[a,\overline{t}]
, while dynamic programming gives

\displaystyle u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{t})=-\int_{a}^{\overline{t}}\overline{A}ds+u^{\overline{A}}(0,a)=-\int_{a}^{\overline{t}}A_{0}ds+u^{A_{0}}(0,a)\geq u^{A_{0}}(0,\overline{t}).

Then we must have

\sup\{s:\;\gamma(s)\}\in(a,b)\end{array}\right\}.

(5.15)

Then,
|\mathcal{O}\cap\{\phi>0\}|>0
.

Proof.

Define

\widetilde{A}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{0}&\text{on $(a,b)$,}\\[2.84526pt]
\overline{A}(t)&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Since
\widetilde{A}\leq\overline{A}
we have
u^{\overline{A}}\leq u^{\widetilde{A}}
.
We claim that

u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)=u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)\ \ \text{in}\ \ ((0,\infty)\times(0,T))\backslash\mathcal{O}.

(5.16)

Fix
(x,t)\in((0,\infty)\times(0,T))\backslash\mathcal{O}
, let
\gamma_{x,t}
be an optimal path for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
and set

\tau^{+}=\sup\{s\in[0,t]:\;\gamma_{x,t}(s)=0\}.

In view of the definition of
\mathcal{O}
, observe that
\tau^{+}\notin(a,b)
.

If
\gamma_{x,t}(s)\neq 0
for any
s\in(a,b)
, then

u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)=J^{\overline{A}}(\gamma_{x,t})=J^{\widetilde{A}}(\gamma_{x,t})\geq u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)\geq u^{\overline{A}}(x,t),

which proves (
5.16
) in this case.

We assume next that
\gamma_{x,t}
vanishes at a point in
(a,b)
. This implies that
\tau^{+}\geq b
and, thus, in view of the structure of the minimizers,
\gamma_{x,t}
vanishes on
[b,\tau^{+}]
.

Define

\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,b}(s)&\text{on $[0,b]$,}\\[2.84526pt]
\gamma_{x,t}(s)&\text{on $[b,t]$, }\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,b}
is optimal for
u^{A_{0}}(0,b)
, and recall that, in view of Lemma
5.11
,
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,b}
vanishes only at
t=b
.

Then by dynamic programming and the optimality of
\gamma_{x,t}
, we find

\displaystyle u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma_{x,t}(s)>0\}}-\overline{A}(s){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma_{x,t}(s)=0\}}\big]ds+u^{\overline{A}}(0,b)

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma_{x,t}(s)>0\}}-\widetilde{A}(s){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma_{x,t}(s)=0\}}\big]ds+u^{A_{0}}(0,b)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\big[L^{R}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(s)>0\}}+L^{L}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{x,t}){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(s)<0\}}-\widetilde{A}(s){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(s)=0\}}\big]ds+u_{0}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(0))

\displaystyle=J^{\widetilde{A}}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}),

where the second equality holds because
\overline{A}=\widetilde{A}
on
[b,t]
and
u^{\overline{A}}(0,b)=u^{A_{0}}(0,b)
and the third one because
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,b}
does not vanish on
[0,b)
.

Thus

u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)=J^{\widetilde{A}}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t})\geq u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)\geq u^{\overline{A}}(x,t),

and, hence, (
5.16
) is true also in this case.

Assume that
|\mathcal{O}\cap\{\phi>0\}|=0
. Then, in view of (
5.16
) and the fact that
u^{\overline{A}}\leq u^{\widetilde{A}}
and
\phi\leq 0
a.e. in
\mathcal{O}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\widetilde{A})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{A})=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\phi(x,t)(u^{\widetilde{A}}(x,t)-u^{\overline{A}}(x,t))dxdt\leq 0,

and, thus,
\mathcal{J}(\widetilde{A})\leq\mathcal{J}(\overline{A})
, which proves that
\widetilde{A}
is also optimal.

Moreover, since
\overline{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
, we have
\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{A}\geq\int_{0}^{T}\overline{A}
, which implies that
\overline{A}\equiv\widetilde{A}
since
\overline{A}\geq\widetilde{A}
. It follows
\overline{A}=\widetilde{A}=A_{0}
in
(a,b)
, which, as we show next, is not possible.

Fix
t\in(a,b)
and let
\gamma_{0,t}
and
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,a}
be optimal for respectively
u^{\overline{A}}(0,t)
and
for
u^{A_{0}}(0,a)
. We recall that
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,a}
vanishes only at
t=a
by Lemma
5.11
and define

\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,a}(s)&\text{on $[0,a]$,}\\[2.84526pt]
\gamma_{0,t}(s)&\text{on $[a,t]$.}\end{array}\right.

Assume that
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,a}(0)>0
.
Then, as
\gamma_{0,t}
vanishes on
[a,t]
by Proposition
3.4
and
\overline{A}=A_{0}=-L^{R}(0)
in
(a,b)
,

\displaystyle u^{\overline{A}}(0,t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{a}^{t}A_{0}ds+u^{\overline{A}}(0,a)=\int_{a}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}_{x,t})ds+u^{A_{0}}(0,a)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{x,t})ds+u_{0}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(0))>\int_{0}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma})ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0)),

where
\gamma
is the straight-line between
(0,\widetilde{\gamma}_{x,t}(0))
and
(0,t)
. Note that the second equality holds because
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,a}
is positive on
[0,a)
and the strict inequality because
L^{R}
is strictly convex.

Thus

u^{\overline{A}}(0,t)>\int_{0}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma})ds+u_{0}(\gamma(0))=J^{A_{0}}(\gamma)\geq u^{A_{0}}(0,t),

which is impossible since
u^{A_{0}}\geq u^{\overline{A}}
.

If
\gamma^{A_{0}}_{0,a}(0)<0
, then we argue in the same way with
L^{L}
instead of
L^{R}
using that
A_{0}=-L^{L}(0)
.

The fact that
|\mathcal{O}\cap\{\phi>0\}|>0
now follows.
∎

From now on, we denote by
(\overline{a},\overline{b})
the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
if there is one, and recall that
\tau\in(\overline{a},\overline{b}]
, where
\tau
is defined by (
5.7
).

Let
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}
be the optimal solution for
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{b})
which remains the most on
\{x=0\}
and define

\overline{s}^{-}=\inf\{s:\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}(s)=0\}.

(5.17)

Lemma 5.13
.

Assume (
5.14
). Fix
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
and let
\gamma
be an optimal trajectory for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
. If
\gamma
vanishes at some point in
(0,t)
and
\sup\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\in[\overline{s}^{-},\overline{b}]
, then
\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\subset[\overline{s}^{-},\overline{b}]
. In particular, if
(x,t)\in(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{3},t_{4})
is a point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}
such that
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
, then

\sup\{s:\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0\}\in[\tau,\overline{b})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \inf\{s:\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0\}=\overline{s}^{-}.

Proof.

Fix
\gamma
as in the statement and assume that
s^{+}=\sup\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\in[\overline{s}^{-},\overline{b}]
. Let
s^{-}=\inf\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}
. Then, by the structure of minimizers,
\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}=[s^{-},s^{+}]
.

If we had
s^{-}<\overline{s}^{-}
, then using dynamic programming, we can replace
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}
by
\gamma
on
[0,s^{+}]
to find an optimal trajectory for
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{b})
which remains in
\{x=0\}
longer than
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}
, a contradiction with the definition of
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}
. Thus
s^{-}\geq\overline{s}^{-}
.

Let
(x,t)\in(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{3},t_{4})
be a point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}
such that
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
, and set
s^{+}=\sup\{s:\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0\}
and
s^{-}=\inf\{s:\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0\}
.

Proposition
3.4
yields that
u^{\overline{A}}(0,s^{+})<u^{A_{0}}(0,s^{+})
and Lemma
5.5
implies that
s^{+}\geq\tau
. Thus, if
(a,b)
is the connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
containing
s^{+}
, we have
b\geq\tau
, so that
(a,b)=(\overline{a},\overline{b})
. Note that
s^{-}\leq\overline{a}
.

By the first claim, we also have that
\{s,\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)\}\subset[\overline{s}^{-},\overline{b}]
. If
s^{-}>\overline{s}^{-}
, we can replace
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
by
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}
on
[0,\overline{a}]
to find an optimal solution for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
which remains in
\{x=0\}
longer than
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
, a contradiction to the definition of
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
. Hence
s^{-}=\overline{s}^{-}
.

∎

We investigate next the sign of the quantity

\mathcal{H}(s)=\int_{s}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0\}}dxdt,

which appears in the optimality condition.

Lemma 5.14
.

Assume (
5.14
). The map
s\to\mathcal{H}(s)
is nonincreasing and nonnegative on
(\overline{s}^{-},\tau)
.

Proof.

Let
s\in(\overline{s}^{-},\tau)
and
\delta>0
small so that
s+\delta<\tau
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(s+\delta)+\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0\}}dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{s+\delta}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t)({\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0\}}-{\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s+\delta)=0\}})dxdt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(s+\delta)+(H_{1})+(H_{2}).

For
(H_{1})
, we recall that, for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(s,s+\delta)
such that
\phi(x,t)\neq 0
, we know that
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(t)=x\geq x_{1}
, and, thus, if we choose
\delta
small enough,

\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)\geq x_{1}-\|(H^{R})^{\prime}\|_{\infty}(t-s)\geq x_{1}-\|(H^{R})^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\delta>0,

Thus
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)\neq 0
and, hence,
(H_{1})=0
.

For
(H_{2})
, let
(x,t)
be a point of differentiability of
u^{\overline{A}}
with
\phi(x,t)\neq 0
, such that either
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0
or
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s+\delta)=0
.

If
\phi(x,t)<0
, then, by Lemma
5.13
,
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
vanishes at least on
[\overline{s}^{-},\tau]
. Thus
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s+\delta)=0
and the integrant of
(H_{2})
vanishes for such
(x,t)
.

If
\phi(x,t)>0
, then
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}
vanishes on an interval of the form
[\overline{s}^{-},h]
with
h\geq s
, and, thus, if
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s+\delta)=0
, then
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s)=0
. In this case the integrant of
(H_{2})
at
(x,t)
is nonnegative. Hence
(H_{2})\geq 0
.

In view of the above, we have
\mathcal{H}(s)\geq\mathcal{H}(s+\delta)
, that is, the map
s\to\mathcal{H}(s)
is nonincreasing on
(\overline{s}^{-},\tau)
.

Now recall that, in view of Lemma
5.10
,
\overline{A}\equiv 0
on
(\overline{a},\tau)
. Since
(\overline{a},\overline{b})
is the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
, Corollary
4.3
yields that
\mathcal{H}
is nonnegative on
(\overline{a},\tau)
. As
s\to\mathcal{H}(s)
is nonincreasing on
(\overline{s}^{-},\tau)
, we infer that
\mathcal{H}
is nonnegative on
(\overline{s}^{-},\tau)
.
∎

We proceed with our investigation of the last connected component
(\overline{a},\overline{b})
in the case where
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}
remains in
\{x=0\}
before
\overline{a}
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Assume (
5.14
) and suppose that
\overline{s}^{-}<\overline{a}
.
Then there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that,
n\to\infty
,
t_{n}\to\overline{a}^{-}
and
u^{\overline{A}}(0,t_{n})<u^{A_{0}}(x,t_{n})
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction assuming that there exists
\delta\in(0,\overline{a}-s^{-}]
such that
u^{\overline{A}}(0,t)=u^{A_{0}}(0,t)
for any
t\in[\overline{a}-\delta,\overline{a}]
. Then
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
in
(\overline{a}-\delta,\overline{a})
.

Let
\gamma
be optimal for
u^{A_{0}}(0,\overline{a}-\delta)
. It follows that does
\gamma
does not vanish on
[0,\overline{a}-\delta)
, and, to fix the ideas, we assume that it remains negative, and set

\widetilde{\gamma}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma(s)&\text{if $s\in[0,\overline{a}-\delta]$,}\\[2.84526pt]
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}(s)&\text{if $s\in[\overline{a}-\delta,\overline{b}]$}.\end{array}\right.

Then, since
L^{R}(0)=-A_{0}
and
\widehat{\gamma}_{0,b}^{+}
vanishes on
[\overline{s}^{-},b]
,

\displaystyle u^{A_{0}}(0,\overline{b})

\displaystyle=u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{b})=-\int_{\overline{a}-\delta}^{\overline{b}}A_{0}ds+u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{a}-\delta)=\int_{\overline{a}-\delta}^{\overline{b}}L^{R}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}})ds+u^{A_{0}}(0,\overline{a}-\delta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\overline{b}}L^{R}(\dot{\widetilde{\gamma}})ds+u_{0}(\widetilde{\gamma}(0)\;>\;\int_{0}^{\overline{b}}L^{R}(\dot{\check{\gamma}})ds+u_{0}(\check{\gamma}(0))=J^{A_{0}}(\check{\gamma}),

where
\check{\gamma}
is the straight-line between
(\gamma(0),0)
and
(0,\overline{b})
and the strict inequality follows from the strong convexity of
L^{R}
. This is impossible because
\check{\gamma}
is admissible.
∎

The next lemma states that, if the optimal trajectory starting from
(0,\overline{b})
vanishes outside
[\overline{a},\overline{b}]
, then there exists a one before last connected component of
(\widehat{a},\widehat{b}):=(\overline{s}^{-},\overline{a})
which touches the last one at
\overline{a}
.

Lemma 5.16
.

If
\overline{s}^{-}<\overline{a}
,
then
(\overline{s}^{-},\overline{a})
is a connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
.

Proof.

Let
(t_{n})
be the sequence introduced in Lemma
5.15
and
(a_{n},b_{n})
be the connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
containing

. Then
b_{n}\leq\overline{a}
.

Note also that, since
\overline{s}^{-}=\inf\{s:\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}(s)=0\}
, Proposition
3.4
yields that we have
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\overline{s}^{-})=u^{A_{0}}(0,\overline{s}^{-})
, and, since
u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)
in
(a_{n},b_{n})
, we have

a_{n}\geq\overline{s}^{-}
.

We claim that
\mathcal{H}
is positive in a neighborhood of
a_{n}
. Indeed, let
\delta
be as in the proof of Lemma
5.14
, that is, assume that
\delta<x_{1}/\|(H^{R})^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, and, consider the sets

\mathcal{O}_{n}=\left\{(x,t):\ \begin{array}[]{l}u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)\ \text{and}\\
\text{there exists}\ \gamma\ \text{optimal for }u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)\ \text{and}\\
\sup\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\in(a_{n},b_{n})\end{array}\right\}.

Recall that it follows from Lemma
5.12

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(s_{n}-\delta/2)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(s_{n}+\delta/2)+\int_{s_{n}-\delta/2}^{s_{n}+\delta/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t){\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s_{n}-\delta/2)=0\}}dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{s_{n}+\delta/2}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x,t)({\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s_{n}-\delta/2)=0\}}-{\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{x,t}(s_{n}+\delta/2)=0\}})dxdt

vanishes by the choice of
\delta
. We also know from the proof of Lemma

is nonnegative, and, by construction, it is positive on

.

\mathcal{H}(s_{n}+\delta/2)

\mathcal{H}
is positive on

(\overline{s}^{-},a_{n})
.

It follows from Theorem
4.2
and the positivity of
\mathcal{H}

\overline{A}\equiv 0
on this interval.

5.7
, for any connected component
(a,b)
of
\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}

\overline{A}\equiv 0
on
(a,\tau)
and
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
in

\tau_{n}\in(a_{n},b_{n})

(a_{n},b_{n})
.

. Indeed, otherwise there would be
n^{\prime}>n
such that
t_{n^{\prime}}>b_{n}
. But the argument above shows that
\overline{A}\equiv 0
on
(\overline{s}^{-},a_{n^{\prime}})

, a contradiction to the fact that
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}

.

. Indeed, assuming that
\overline{s}^{-}<a_{n}
, we note that, in view of the fact that

in

. This shows that

\{u^{\overline{A}}(0,\cdot)<u^{A_{0}}(0,\cdot)\}
.
∎

The main consequence of the previous lemma is the confinement of optimal trajectories vanishing in the last connected component.

Corollary 5.17
.

Assume (
5.14
). Let
\gamma
be an optimal path for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
with
(x,t)\in[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[t_{3},t_{4}]
. If
|\{\gamma=0\}|>0
, then the last connected component
(\overline{a},\overline{b})
of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}
exists and either
\{\gamma=0\}\subset[\overline{a},\overline{b}]
or
\{\gamma=0\}\subset[\widehat{a},\overline{b}]
where
(\widehat{a},\overline{a})
is a one before the last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}
.

Proof.

In view of the structure of minimizers, there exists
s^{-}<s^{+}
such that
\{\gamma=0\}=[s^{-},s^{+}]
. Moreover, in view of Lemma
5.5
, we know that
s^{+}\geq\tau
.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,s^{+}-s^{-})
. If
\overline{A}\equiv A_{0}
in
(s^{+}-\varepsilon,s^{+})
, then, by dynamic programming, if
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
is the straight-line connecting
(0,s^{+}-\varepsilon)
and
(x,t)
,

\displaystyle u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{s^{+}}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma})ds-\int_{s^{+}-\varepsilon}^{s^{+}}A_{0}ds+u^{\overline{A}}(s^{+}-\varepsilon,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{s^{+}-\varepsilon}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma})ds+u^{\overline{A}}(s^{+}-\varepsilon,0)>\int_{s^{+}-\varepsilon}^{t}L^{R}(\dot{\gamma}_{\varepsilon})ds+u^{\overline{A}}(s^{+}-\varepsilon,0),

which is a contradiction.

So
\overline{A}\not\equiv A_{0}
in
(s^{+}-\varepsilon,s^{+})
, and, hence, there exists
t_{\varepsilon}\in(s^{+}-\varepsilon,s^{+})
such that
u^{\overline{A}}(0,t_{\varepsilon})<u^{A_{0}}(0,t_{\varepsilon})
.

Let
(a,b)
be the connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}
containing
t_{\varepsilon}
. We know from
Proposition
3.4
that
s^{-}\leq a
, which implies that this connected component does not depend on
\varepsilon
. In addition,
b>t_{\varepsilon}
, thus, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\overline{b}\geq s^{+}\geq\tau
. Thus
(a,b)
must be the last connected component.

Assume now that
\{\gamma=0\}\not\subset[\overline{a},\overline{b}]
and let
\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}
be the optimal trajectory for

which remains the most in

. It follows from Lemma

that
\{\gamma=0\}\subset\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}=0\}
and thus
\{\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}=0\}\not\subset[\overline{a},\overline{b}]
. Then Lemma

states that, if we set
\overline{s}^{-}=\inf\{s,\;\widehat{\gamma}^{+}_{0,\overline{b}}(s)=0\}
, then
(\overline{s}^{-},\overline{a})
is the one before last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}
.
∎

5.3.
On the number of connected components

We finally show in this subsection that, if (
5.14
) and (
5.6
) hold, then the number of connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}
is finite.

Let
\tau
be given by (
5.7
). We define
\alpha\in[0,T]
as follows: If there is no last connected component of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}
, then
\alpha=\tau
. If there is a last connected component
(\overline{a},\overline{b})
, but the component
(\widehat{a},\widehat{b})
before last either does not exist or satisfies
\widehat{b}<\overline{a}
, then we set
\alpha=\overline{a}
. Finally, if
\widehat{b}=\overline{a}
, we set
\alpha=\widehat{a}
.

We first discuss a technical stability result.

Lemma 5.18
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any measurable
A:[0,T]\to[A_{0},0]
with
\|A-\overline{A}\|_{L^{1}}\leq|A_{0}|\delta
, any
(x,t)\in[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[t_{3},t_{4}]
with
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
and for any optimal trajectory
\gamma
for
u^{A}(x,t)
,

\inf\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\geq\alpha-\varepsilon.

Proof.

We first consider the case
A\equiv\overline{A}
. Let
(x,t)\in[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[t_{3},t_{4}]
and
\gamma
be optimal for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
. We show that
\inf\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\geq\alpha
.

If
|\{\gamma=0\}|=0
, then by the structure of minimizers and Lemma
5.5
imply that

\inf\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}=\sup\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\geq\tau\geq\alpha.

If
|\{\gamma=0\}|>0
, then, in view of Corollary
5.17
, the last connected component
(\overline{a},\overline{b})
of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}
exists and either

\inf\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\geq\overline{a}\geq\alpha,

or there exists a one before last connected component
(\widehat{a},\widehat{b})
with
\widehat{b}=\overline{a}
and

\inf\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}\geq\widehat{a}\geq\alpha.

We now show that there exists
C>0
such that, for any
t\in[\alpha,T]
, any optimal trajectory
\gamma
of
u^{\overline{A}}(0,t)

satisfies, for all
s\in[0,s^{-}]
, the bound

|\gamma(s)|\geq C^{-1}(s^{-}-s)/s^{-},

(5.18)

where
s^{-}=\inf\{s:\;\gamma(s)=0\}
.

Indeed, let
\gamma
be such a path. We know that
s^{-}\geq\alpha
. Moreover, by the structure of minimizers,
\gamma
does not vanish on
[0,s^{-})
and
u^{\overline{A}}(0,s^{-})=u^{A_{0}}(0,s^{-})
. Thus,
\gamma_{|_{[0,s^{-}]}}
is an optimal path for
u^{A_{0}}(0,s^{-})
. Then Lemma
5.11
and a compactness argument yield the existence of some
C>0
, which is independent of
\gamma
, such that
|\gamma(0)|\geq C^{-1}
. Since
\gamma_{|_{[0,s^{-}]}}
is a straight line, (
5.18
) follows.

In view of stability in
L^{1}
of the paths
A
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any measurable
A:[0,T]\to[A_{0},0]
with
\|A-\overline{A}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\delta|A_{0}|
, for any
(x,t)\in[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[t_{3},t_{4}]
and for any optimal trajectory
\gamma
for
u^{A}(x,t)
, there exists an optimal trajectory
\widetilde{\gamma}
for
u^{\overline{A}}(x,t)
such that
\|\gamma-\widetilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq(CT)^{-1}\varepsilon
, where
C
is the constant in (
5.18
).

Let
s^{-}=\inf\{s:\;\widetilde{\gamma}(s)=0\}
. We know that
s^{-}\geq\alpha
. Then, by (
5.18
),

|\gamma(s)|\geq|\widetilde{\gamma}(s)|-(CT)^{-1}\varepsilon\geq C^{-1}(s^{-}-s)/s^{-}-(CT)^{-1}\varepsilon.

If
\gamma(s)=0
for some
s\leq s^{-}
, then
s\geq s^{-}-s^{-}T^{-1}\varepsilon\geq\alpha-\varepsilon
.

The proof is now completed.
∎

We show next a “local finiteness” result for the number of connected components of
\{u^{\overline{A}}<u^{A_{0}}\}\cap(0,\alpha-\varepsilon)

Lemma 5.19
.

Assume (
5.14
) and let

be defined as above. Then, for any

(a,b)
with

, there exists

such that

Proof.

Fix

and let

be as in Lemma

\overline{A}(s)&{\rm otherwise},\end{array}\right.

We claim that

in

Then

5.4.
An example

We present here a class of examples for which the optimal controls cannot be constant and equal to
0
or to
A_{0}
.

For simplicity of notation, we assume here that
H^{L}=H^{R}
and thus remove the exponents “L” and “R” in the notation. We recall that
H:[-R,0]\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly convex with
H(0)=H(-R)=0
and
H
has a minimum at
\widehat{p}
.

Proposition 5.23
.

Assume in addition to (
5.14
)
that
u_{0}(x)=px
, where
p\in(-R,\widehat{p})
. If

\begin{split}&(i)\;x_{2}\leq H(p)t_{1}/p,\ (ii)\;t_{2}H^{\prime}(0)/(H^{\prime}(0)-H(p)/p)<t_{3},\ \text{and}\\
&(iii)\;-t_{2}A_{0}/(H(p)-A_{0})<t_{3},\end{split}

(5.19)

then
A\equiv A_{0}
and
A\equiv 0
are not optimal.

We strongly use below that the traffic is congested in the entry line, that is,
p\in(-R,\widehat{p})
.
As we show in the following proof, the first condition in (
5.19
) implies that the control is active on the whole set
\{\phi\neq 0\}
, which is a natural condition. Conditions (ii) and (iii) hold if
t_{3}
is large enough.

Proof.

Define the control

A=\begin{cases}0\ \text{on}\ [0,t_{2}],\\
A_{0}\ \text{in}\ (t_{2},T].\end{cases}

Recall that
u^{A}\leq u^{A_{0}}
and
u^{A_{0}}(x,t)=px-H(p)t
. Then, using dynamic programming for the interval
(t_{2},T)
, we find

\displaystyle u^{A}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\min\{-Rx{\bf 1}_{\{x\leq 0\}},px-H(p)t\}\ \text{if}\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,t_{2}],\\[3.41432pt]
\displaystyle\min\{\min_{y\in[0,H(p)t_{2}/p]}(t-t_{2})L((x-y)/(t-t_{2}))\ ,\ px-tH(p)\}&\ \text{if}\ (x,t)\in[0,\infty)\times[t_{2},T].\end{array}\right.

We first claim that
\mathcal{J}(A)<\mathcal{J}(A_{0})
. Note that

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}(A_{0})-\mathcal{J}(A)&=\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{H(p)t/p}\phi(x,t)(px-H(p)t)dxdt\\
&\qquad+\int_{t_{2}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\phi(x,t)(u^{A_{0}}(x,t)-u^{A}(x,t))dxdt.\end{split}

(5.20)

Since, by (
5.19
)(i),
x_{2}\leq H(p)t_{1}/p
, it follows that

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2})\times(t_{1},t_{2})=\{\phi>0\}

\displaystyle\subset\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,t_{2}],\;x<H(p)t/p\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,t_{2}],\;u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)\},

and, thus, the first integral in (
5.20
) is positive.

We now fix
(x,t)
such that
t>t_{2}
and
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
. Then,

u^{A}(x,t)=\min_{y\in[0,H(p)t_{2}/p]}(t-t_{2})L((x-y)/(t-t_{2}))<px-tH(p).

Let
y
be a minimum point
\min_{y\in[0,H(p)t_{2}/p]}(t-t_{2})L((x-y)/(t-t_{2}))
. In view of the facts that
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
and
u^{A}(H(p)t_{2}/p,t_{2})=u^{A_{0}}(H(p)t_{2}/p,t_{2})
, it follows that
y\in[0,H(p)t_{2}/p)
.

In the case where the minimum point
y
is in
(0,H(p)t_{2}/p)
, we have
L^{\prime}((x-y)/(t-t_{2}))=0
, and thus

\min_{y\geq 0}(t-t_{2})L(x-y/(t-t_{2}))=0
and
x-y=(t-t_{2})H^{\prime}(0).

Then
0=u^{A}(x,t)
and
u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
implies that

0\leq x<H(p)t/p\ \text{and}\ x>(t-t_{2})H^{\prime}(0).
It follows that

H^{\prime}(0)(t-t_{2})<H(p)t/p
, which in turn yields that
t<t_{2}H^{\prime}(0)/(H^{\prime}(0)-H(p)/p))
, and, in view of (
5.19
)-(ii),
that
t<t_{3}
.

If the minimum point
y
is zero, then

u^{A}(x,t)<u^{A_{0}}(x,t)
implies, using the convexity of
L
,

px-H(p)t>(t-t_{2})L(x/(t-t_{2}))\geq\widehat{p}x-(t-t_{2})H(\widehat{p})=\widehat{p}x-(t-t_{2})A_{0},

so that

(\widehat{p}-p)x+(H(p)-A_{0})t<-t_{2}A_{0}.

Since, in view of (
5.19
)-(iii), we have
t_{3}>-t_{2}A_{0}/(H(p)-A_{0})
and, hence,
t\leq t_{3}
.

The considerations above show that
\{u^{A}<u^{A_{0}}\}\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[t_{2},T])\subset\{\phi\geq 0\}
, and, hence, the second integral in (
5.20
) is nonnegative. Thus
\mathcal{J}(A_{0})>\mathcal{J}(A)
and the constant control
A_{0}
is not optimal.

We check next that the constant control
0
is not optimal. Note that

u^{0}(x,t)=\min\{-Rx{\bf 1}_{\{x\leq 0\}},px-H(p)t\}\qquad\text{for }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T].

Hence, since
x_{2}\leq H(p)t_{1}/p
,

\{\phi\neq 0\}\subset[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{4}]\subset\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,t_{2}],\;x\leq H(p)t/p\}\subset\{u^{0}(x,t)=0\}.

One the other hand we have seen that
u^{A}=u^{0}
in
\mathbb{R}\times[0,t_{2}]
, while, in
(x_{1},x_{2})\times[t_{3},t_{4}]
,
u^{A}=u^{A_{0}}>u^{0}
. This proves that

\mathcal{J}(0)>\mathcal{J}(A)
and, thus, the constant control
0
is not optimal.

∎

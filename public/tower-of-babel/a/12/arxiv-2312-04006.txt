A representation theorem for set-valued submartingales
Luc T. Tuyen, Vu T. Luan

Abstract
The integral representation theorem for martingales has been widely used in probability theory. In this work, we propose and prove a general representation theorem for a class of set-valued submartingales. We also extend the stochastic integral representation for non-trivial initial set-valued martingales. Moreover, we show that this result covers the existing ones in the literature for both degenerated and non-degenerated set-valued martingales.

For set-valued stochastic processes, we refer the readers to several works of

Specifically, the authors have showed in an M-type 2 Banach space that the set-valued stochastic integral becomes a set-valued submartingle and the Castaing representation for the set-valued stochastic integral is also obtained.
It is shown in

that for every set-valued martingale

We note, however, that for any set-valued martingale

[
15

holds for single-valued martingales only. From this point of view, in some real world applications it is more reasonable to consider the condition

for all
t\geq 0
instead of the equal one, see
[
22
]

The authors of
[
27
]
then proposed a representation theorem for non-degenerated set-valued martingales that holds for a special case,
\displaystyle F_{t}=C+\left\{\int_{0}^{t}g_{s}dB_{s}\right\}

is a bounded, closed and convex subset of a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
and
(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is an

The aim of this work is to fill this gap by presenting a general result, showing that under some reasonable assumptions on the set-valued submartingale

the representation
F_{t}=\displaystyle\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{s}dB_{s}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
holds not only for the degenerated set-valued martingales but also for the non-degenerated one.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

. For

\left\|f\right\|_{p}=\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left\|f(\omega)\right\|^{p}dP\right\}^{1/p},\,1\leq p<\infty.

Denote

Recall that a set-valued mapping

For

Let

Theorem 2.1
.

(

Let

For a set-valued random variable

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
8
]
)
For a set-valued random variable
F\in U[\Omega;\mathbf{K}(\mathfrak{X})]
, there exists a sequence

Moreover, denote by

For a set-valued random variable

A set-valued random process
F=(F_{t})_{t\geq 0}

-adapted convex set-valued process on

Given

(

For every set-valued

.

One can show that Theorem

holds only when

we note that, for a set-valued martingale

Conversely, for a set

is not a non-degenerated set-valued

An extended version of Theorem

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[

)
Let

(ii) There exists an

-valued stochastic process

(iii) There exists a sequence

Remark 2.2
.

It is clear that this result still holds only in a very special case, i.e.,

equals to sum of a set

and a singleton. The representation (i),
\displaystyle M_{t}=E(M_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}G_{s}dB_{s}\,\ a.s.\,\text{for each }t

3
Main results

In this section, we present a new representation theorem for a trivial initial set-valued
\mathbb{F}
-submartingale with natural filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion
B=(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. Finally, we propose an extension of integral representation for non-trivial initial set-valued martingale.

3.1
Integral representation for trivial initial set-valued submartingales

First, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F=(F_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a set-valued
\mathcal{F}
-submartingale in
L^{p}[\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P;

\mathbf{K}_{bc}(\mathfrak{X})]
(
p\geq 1
) with
\mathfrak{X}
is reflexive. Then, there exists a system of
\mathfrak{X}
-valued random variables
\{f_{r,t}^{k}:t\geq 0,r\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

(i)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\geq 0
, the sequence
(f_{r,t}^{k})_{t\geq 0}
is a
\mathfrak{X}
- valued
\mathbb{F}
-martingale.

(ii)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
r,t\geq 0
with
0\leq r\leq t
,
f_{r,t}^{k}\in S_{F_{t}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
.

(iii)

For each
t\geq 0
,
F_{t}(\omega)=cl_{\mathfrak{X}}\{f_{r,t}^{k}(\omega):0\leq r\leq t,k\in\mathbb{N}\}
for all
\omega\in\Omega
. In addition,

S_{F_{t}}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\overline{\text{dec}}\{f_{r,t}^{k}(\omega):0\leq r\leq t,k\in\mathbb{N}\},

where
\text{cl}_{\mathfrak{X}}
denotes the closure taken in
\mathfrak{X}
.

Proof.

For fixed
r\geq 0
, since
F_{r}\in L^{p}\left[\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P:K_{bc}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\right]
and by Castaing representation (Theorem
2.2
), there is a countable family
\left\{f_{r,r}^{k}\in{S}^{p}_{F_{r}}(\mathcal{F}_{r}),k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
such that
F_{r}(\omega)=\text{cl}_{\mathfrak{X}}\left\{f_{r,r}^{k}(\omega):k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
for all
\omega\in\Omega
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\geq 0
, we define a
\mathfrak{X}
-valued martingale
\left(f_{r,t}^{k}\right)_{t\geq 0}
as follows. For
t\leq r
, define
f_{r,t}^{k}=E[f_{r,r}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
.
For
t>r
, since
\mathfrak{X}
is reflexive and due to Eberlein-Smulyan theorem,
F_{t}
is weakly compact for all
t\geq 0
and
\omega\in\Omega
. Thus,

S_{E[F_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}_{r}]}\left(\mathcal{F}_{r}\right)=\left\{E[f|\mathcal{F}_{r}]:f\in S_{F_{t}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)\right\}

is closed in
L^{p}[\Omega,{\mathfrak{X}}]
by
[
3
]
.
Since
\left(F_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a set-valued submartingale, we have

S_{F_{r}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{r}\right)\subset\left\{E[f|\mathcal{F}_{r}]:f\in S_{F_{t}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)\right\}

for every
0\leq r\leq t<\infty
.
Thus, for
f_{r,r}^{k}\in S_{F_{r}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{r}\right)
there exists
f_{r,t}^{k}\in S_{F_{t}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)
such that
E\left[f_{r,t}^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{r}\right]={{f}_{r,r}}
. Hence,
f=\left(f_{r,t}^{k}\right)_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the condition (i) and (ii).

On the other hand, for each
t\geq 0
we have

F_{t}(\omega)=\text{cl}_{\mathfrak{X}}\left\{f_{t,t}^{k}(\omega):k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

for all
\omega\in\Omega
. Since
f_{r,t}^{k}\in S_{F_{t}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)
for all
0\leq r\leq t
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain
F_{t}(\omega)=\text{cl}_{\mathfrak{X}}\left\{f_{r,t}^{k}(\omega):0\leq r\leq t,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
for every
\omega\in\Omega
.
Next, it is clear that
\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{f_{r,t}^{k}:0\leq r\leq t,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subset S_{F_{t}}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
. Conversely, for every
f\in S_{F_{t}}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
and every
\epsilon>0
, the result of F. Hiai and H. Umegaki (
[
8
]
, Lemma 1.3) shows that there exists a finite measurable partition
\left\{A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{n}\right\}
of
\Omega
and a family
\left(f_{r,t}^{k_{i}}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset\left\{f_{r,t}^{k}:k\geq 1\right\}
such that
E\left\|f-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{1_{A_{i}}f_{r,t}^{k_{i}}}\right\|_{\mathfrak{X}}\leq\epsilon
,
which implies that
f\in\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{f_{r,t}^{k}:0\leq r\leq t,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
.
Thus,
S_{F_{t}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)=\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{f_{r,t}^{k}:0\leq r\leq t,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
,
which concludes (iii).

With these preparations at hand, we are now ready to present the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1

(Set-valued submartingale representation theorem)
.

Given
\mathfrak{X}
is a separable reflexive M-type 2 Banach space. Let
F=(F_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with

F_{t}\in L^{2}[\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P;\mathbf{K}_{bc}(\mathfrak{X})]
be a bounded closed convex set-valued
\mathbb{F}
-submartingale defined on a filtered probability space
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
with completed natural filtration
\mathbb{F}=\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
of an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion
B=(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P\right)
.
Suppose that
{F}_{0}=\left\{0\right\}
a.s. and for each
t\geq 0
the Castaining representation
{F}_{t}=\text{cl}\left\{f_{t}^{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
satisfies
E\left[f_{t}^{k}|{\mathcal{F}_{s}}\right]\in S_{F_{s}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{s}\right)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s<t
, then there exists a set
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}\right)
such that
\displaystyle F_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}d{B_{\tau}}
, where the integral is in the sense of generalized set-valued stochastic integral.

Proof.

By using Lemma
3.1
, we have

S_{F_{t}}\left({\mathcal{F}_{t}}\right)=\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{f_{r,t}^{k}:0\leq r\leq t,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

where
\left(f_{r,t}^{k}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a
\mathbb{F}
-martingale for each
r\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since
E\left[f_{t}^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\in S_{F_{s}}\left({\mathcal{F}_{s}}\right)
for all
s<t
and
{F}_{0}=\left\{0\right\}
, for each
r\geq 0
we choose
f_{r,t}^{k}\in S_{F_{t}}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
such that
E[f_{r,r}^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=f_{r,t}^{k}
for all
t<r
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, for each
t\geq 0
we have

S_{F_{t}}\left({\mathcal{F}_{t}}\right)=\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{f_{r,t}^{k}:r\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

(3.1)

where
\left(f_{r,t}^{k}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a
\mathbb{F}
-martingale with
f_{r,0}^{k}=0
for each
r\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

As a result of the traditional martingale representation theorem for every martingale selector
f_{r}^{k}=\left(f_{r,t}^{k}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, there exists exactly one (with respect to the equality a.e.)
g^{f_{r}^{k}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
such that
\displaystyle f_{r,t}^{k}=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{f_{r}^{k}}d{B_{\tau}}
. For
t\geq 0
,

let
\mathcal{G}=\left\{g=\left(g_{t}^{f_{r}^{k}}\right)_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}:r\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
. By the definition of the generalized set-valued stochastic integrals, we have

S_{\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{G}}d{B_{s}}}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{\int_{0}^{t}g_{s}^{f_{r}^{k}}d{B_{s}}:g_{s}^{f_{r}^{k}}\in\mathcal{G}\right\}.

(3.2)

From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we have

{{F}_{t}}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{G}}d{{B}_{s}}.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

We have the following observations

1.

Theorem
3.1
covers the result of Theorem
2.3
because with a zero origin set-valued martingale
\{F_{t}\}
, it degenerates to a singleton,
F_{t}=\{f_{t}\}
. The Castaing representation of
F_{t}
now becomes
F_{t}=cl\{f_{t}\}
where all the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied.

2.

The generalization of Theorem
2.4
may be obtained for non-zero origin set-valued submartingales if the Hukuhara different (H-different)
[
21
]
,
F_{t}{{\ominus}_{H}}F_{0}
, exists for all
t\geq 0
. Then,
F_{t}=F_{0}+(F_{t}\ominus_{H}F_{0})
and thus
F_{t}\ominus_{H}F_{0}
is a zero origin set-valued submartingale. However, the existence of H-different does not always hold for every sets. Therefore, the integral representation for non-zero origin set-valued submartingales is still a open problem.

3.

The condition
E\left[f_{t}^{k}|{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\right]\in S_{F_{s}}(\mathcal{F}_{s})
in Theorem
3.1
is not sufficient for the submartingale
F=(F_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to become a set-valued martingale. This means that the theorem is not in the generated case. To illustrate this remark, we give the following examples.

Example 3.1
.

Given
u,v\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
are two different stochastic processes. Let
\displaystyle f_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}d{B_{s}}
and
\displaystyle g_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}{v_{s}}dB_{s}.
For each
t\in[0,T]
, we define

F_{t}=\text{cl}\left\{\lambda{{f}_{t}}+\left(1-\lambda\right){{g}_{t}}:\lambda\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\right\}.

Then,
\{F_{t}\},t\in[0,T]
is a non-degenerated set-valued submartingale satisfying all conditions of Theorem
3.1
and thus its integral representation holds.

Indeed, it is clear that
F_{t}=[\min\{f_{t},g_{t}\},\max\{f_{t},g_{t}\}]
.
As both
\min\{f_{t},g_{t}\}
and
\max\{f_{t},g_{t}\}
are real-valued supermartingales,
F_{t}
is a non-degenerate interval-valued submartingale. Since
F_{0}=\{0\}
a.s.,
\{F_{t}:t\in[0,T]\}
is not an interval-valued martingale.

One can see that
{{F}_{t}}=\text{cl}\left\{h_{t,t}^{\lambda}:\lambda\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\right\}
with

h_{t,t}^{\lambda}=h_{t}^{\lambda}=\lambda{{f}_{t}}+\left(1-\lambda\right){{g}_{t}}.

Notice that

\text{cl}\left\{h_{t,t}^{\lambda}:\lambda\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\right\}=\text{cl}\left\{h_{r,t}^{\lambda}:\lambda\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q},0\leq r\leq t\right\}
since
h_{r,t}^{\lambda}\in{S}_{F_{t}}\left({\mathcal{F}_{t}}\right)
for all
0\leq r\leq t
, where
h_{r,t}^{\lambda}
is designed as in Lemma
3.1
. From
h_{0,0}^{\lambda}=0
a.s, we have
h_{r,0}^{\lambda}=0
a.s for all
r\geq 0
. On the other hand, for every
s<r
,

E\left[h_{r,r}^{\lambda}|{\mathcal{F}_{s}}\right]=E\left[\lambda{{f}_{r}}+\left(1-\lambda\right){g_{r}}|{\mathcal{F}_{s}}\right]

=\lambda{f_{s}}+\left(1-\lambda\right){g_{s}}=h_{s,s}^{\lambda}

\in S_{F_{s}}\left({\mathcal{F}_{s}}\right)
.
Hence,
\{F_{t}:t\in[0,T]\}
satisfies all the conditions of Theorem
3.1
.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle S_{F_{t}}(\mathcal{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{h_{t}^{\lambda}:\lambda\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{dec}}\left\{\mathop{\int}_{0}^{t}\left(\lambda{u_{s}}+(1-\lambda){v_{s}}\right)d{B_{s}}:\lambda\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\right\}

\displaystyle=S_{\int_{0}^{t}Gd{B_{s}}}(\mathcal{F}_{t}),

where

G={{\left({{G}_{t}}\right)}_{t\geq 0}}={{\left(\left\{\lambda{{u}_{t}}+(1-\lambda){{v}_{t}}:\lambda\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\right\}\right)}_{t\geq 0}}.

Thus,
\displaystyle{F}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Gd{B_{s}}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\eta_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}dB_{s}
where
u\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a stochastic process. We denote by
\mathbf{B}
a countable set in
\mathfrak{X}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
that
B=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}:i^{2}+j^{2}\leq 1\}
. Then,
\mathbf{B}
is dense in
\mathfrak{X}
and
cl(\mathbf{B})
is the unit ball in
\mathfrak{X}
. For each
t\geq 0
, let

F_{t}=cl\{\eta_{t}\mathbf{B}\},

where the closure is taken in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then,
\{F_{t},t\geq 0\}
is also a non-degenerated set-valued submartingale satisfying all conditions of Theorem
3.1
and thus its integral representation holds.

Indeed,
F_{0}=\{0\}
a.s. is obviously. From
\mathbf{B}
is countable, the Castaing representation of
F_{t}
can be written by
F_{t}=cl\{f_{t}^{k}=\eta_{t}b_{k}:b_{k}\in B,k\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Hence, we have
E[f_{t}^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]=E[\eta_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]b_{k}=\eta_{s}b_{k}\in S_{F_{s}}(\mathcal{F}_{s})
. Moreover, since
E[\eta_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]b=E[\eta_{t}b|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
for all
b\in\mathbf{B}
, we have
E[\eta_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\mathbf{B}\subset E[\eta_{t}\mathbf{B}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
and thus
E[F_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\supset F_{s}
. From this fact and the notice that
cl(\mathbf{B})
is the unit ball in
\mathfrak{X}
,
\{F_{t},t\geq 0\}
is a non-degenerated set-valued submartingale. Now, all the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied.

3.2
Integral representation for non-trivial initial set-valued martingales

The primary challenge in applying set-valued random variables to practical problems lies in the fact that a set-valued martingale with a trivial initial value (i.e., a singleton) will degenerate into a singleton. Therefore, our focus is exclusively on non-trivial initial set-valued martingales.
However, the stochastic integral used in Theorems
2.3
,
3.1
and
2.4
unfortunately assumes zero initial value.
Even that with certain assumptions, the set-valued martingale
(M_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
can be represented in the form
\displaystyle M_{t}=E(M_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}G_{s}dB_{s}
; and so
(G_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
will degenerate to singleton, which is still being a special case of set-valued martingales.

To establish a stochastic integral representation for general initial set-valued martingales, it is necessary to extend the stochastic integral on the expanded space
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}:=\mathfrak{X}\times\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
. The added element
\mathfrak{X}
in
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
will store the initial values of set-valued martingales.
In this section, we will work with
\mathfrak{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and so
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})
.

Building on the work presented in
[
1
]
, we now streamline the definition of the set-valued stochastic integral on
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
. Given an element
(x,\varphi)\in\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
, for
t\in[0,T]
, define the mapping
\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}
by setting
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(x,\varphi)=x+\int_{0}^{t}\varphi_{\tau}dB_{\tau}
. It is clear that
(\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(x,\varphi))_{t\in[0,T]}
is a continuous
\mathbb{F}
-martingale. Let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2})
(the family of all nonempty subsets of
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
). For each
t\in[0,T]
, there exists only one
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable set-valued random variable
F_{t}:\Omega\to\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
S_{F_{t}}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})
(see Theorem
2.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2})
. For
t\in[0,T]
, there exists only one
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable set-valued random variable on
L^{2}[\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t};\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]
, denoted by
\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dBs
, such that
S_{\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dBs}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})
. The set-valued random variable
\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dBs
is called the generalized set-valued stochastic integral of
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
.

Remark 3.2
.

1.

If
\mathcal{G}=\{x\}\times G
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
G\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
, then
\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dBs=\{x\}+\int_{0}^{t}GdB_{s}
. Hence, when
x=0
we have
\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dBs=\int_{0}^{t}GdB_{s}
.

2.

Since
\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})
is a family of
\mathbb{F}
-martingale,
\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dBs
is generally a set-valued submartingale. Indeed, we have

S^{2}_{E[\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dB|\mathcal{F}_{s}]}(\mathcal{F}_{s})=\text{cl}\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\}.

(3.3)

We will show that
\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\}
is
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-decomposable. Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\}
. For any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
, there exists
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})
such that
\xi_{1}=E[\psi_{1}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
and
\xi_{2}=E[\psi_{2}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
. We have

\displaystyle I_{A}\xi_{1}+I_{\Omega\setminus A}\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=I_{A}E[\psi_{1}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]+I_{\Omega\setminus A}E[\psi_{2}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

\displaystyle=E[I_{A}\psi_{1}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]+E[I_{\Omega\setminus A}\psi_{2}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

\displaystyle=E[I_{A}\psi_{1}+I_{\Omega\setminus A}\psi_{2}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

\displaystyle\in\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\}.

(3.4)

The relation “
\in
” in equation (
2.
) is due to
I_{A}\psi_{1}+I_{\Omega\setminus A}\psi_{2}\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})
,
A\in\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset\mathcal{F}_{t}
, and the decomposability of
\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})
.

Thus,
\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\}
is
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-decomposable. On the other hand, for all
(x,\varphi)\in\mathcal{G}
, we have
\mathcal{J}_{B}^{s}(x,\varphi)=E[\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(x,\varphi)|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
. Hence,

\mathcal{J}_{B}^{s}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\}.

From the definition of decomposable hull of a set, we have

\text{dec}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{s}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\},

implying that

\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{s}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\text{cl}\left\{E[\psi|\mathcal{F}_{s}]:\psi\in\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G})\right\}.

(3.5)

In view of (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), we have

S^{2}_{E[\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dB|\mathcal{F}_{s}]}(\mathcal{F}_{s})\supseteq\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{s}(\mathcal{G})=S^{2}_{\int_{\Theta}^{s}\mathcal{G}dB}(\mathcal{F}_{s}).

Therefore,
\displaystyle E\left[\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dB|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\supseteq\int_{\Theta}^{s}\mathcal{G}dB
. This implies that
\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}dBs
is a set-valued submartingale.

Let
M=(M_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a convex uniformly square integrably bounded set-valued
\mathbb{F}
-martingale. We denote by
MS(M)
the set of all square integrably bounded
\mathbb{F}
-martingale selectors of
M
and
P_{t}[MS(M)]
the
t
th-sector of
MS(M)
. As a result of traditional martingale representation, for every martingale selector
f=(f_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in MS(M)
, there exists only one
(x^{f},\varphi^{f})\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
such that
\displaystyle f_{t}=x^{f}+\int_{0}^{t}\varphi_{\tau}^{f}dB_{\tau}
a.s. for every
t\in[0,T]
. Let us denote it by
\mathcal{G}^{M}=\left\{(x^{f},\varphi^{f})\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}:f\in MS(M)\right\}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M=(M_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a convex uniformly square integrably bounded set-valued
\mathbb{F}
-martingale. Then, for every
t\in[0,T]
,

M_{t}=\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}^{M}dB_{\tau}\ \,a.s.

Proof.

For each
t\in[0,T]
, by the definition of
\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}^{M}dB_{\tau}
we have

S_{\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}^{M}dB}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\overline{\text{dec}}\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G}^{M}).

It is clear that
\mathcal{J}_{B}^{t}(\mathcal{G}^{M})=P_{t}[MS(M)]
. Hence,

S_{\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}^{M}dB}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\overline{\text{dec}}P_{t}[MS(M)].

Using the result of [Proposition 3.1,
[
15
]
], we get
\overline{\text{dec}}P_{t}[MS(M)]=S_{M_{t}}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
.
Thus,

S_{\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}^{M}dB}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=S_{M_{t}}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t}),

showing that
\displaystyle M_{t}=\int_{\Theta}^{t}\mathcal{G}^{M}dB
a.s.
∎

By using

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have shown that every set-valued martingale with zero origin degenerates to the singleton case.
To avoid the degenerated case of set-valued martingales, we have introduced a general integral representation theorem for non-degenerated set-valued submartingales based on certain assumptions. Finally, we also present an extended stochastic integral representation for non-singleton initial set-valued martingales.
Using this work allows one to recover the previous results.

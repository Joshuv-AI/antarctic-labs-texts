From Torch to Projector: Fundamental Tradeoff of Integrated Sensing and Communications
Yifeng Xiong, Fan Liu, Kai Wan, Weijie Yuan, Yuanhao Cui, Giuseppe Caire

Abstract
snc ( snc ) have been historically developed in parallel. In recent decade, they have been evolving from separation to integration, giving rise to the isac ( isac ) paradigm, that has been recognized as one of the six key 6G usage scenarios. Despite the plethora of research works dedicated to isac signal processing, the fundamental performance limits of snc remain widely unexplored in an isac system. In this tutorial paper, we attempt to summarize the recent research findings in characterizing the performance boundary of isac systems and the resulting snc tradeoff from an information-theoretical viewpoint. We begin with a folklore “torch metaphor” that depicts the resource competition mechanism of snc . Then, we elaborate on the fundamental capacity-distortion (C-D) theory, indicating the incompleteness of this metaphor. Towards that end, we further elaborate on the snc tradeoff by discussing a special case within the C-D framework, namely the Cramér-Rao bound (CRB)-rate region. In particular, snc have preference discrepancies over both the subspace occupied by the transmitted signal and the adopted codebook, leading to a “projector metaphor” complementary to the isac torch analogy. We also present two practical design examples by leveraging the lessons learned from fundamental theories. Finally, we conclude the paper by identifying a number of open challenges.

Capacity-Distortion Theory

To answer these questions, one has to rely on a more general analytical framework, that applies to both estimation and detection tasks and can handle favorably all reasonable sensing performance metrics. The capacity-distortion theory

III-A

Rate-Distortion Region

As the name suggests, the capacity-distortion theory investigates the tradeoff between communication capacity and sensing distortion. Originally proposed by Shannon in the context of rate-distortion theory for lossy data compression
[
17
]
, distortion refers to a wide range of functions taking the form of
d(\bm{\seta},\hat{\bm{\seta}})

\bm{\seta}
and its estimate
\hat{\bm{\seta}}
. Due to the randomness of the communication message, the
isac
waveform (codeword)
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}^{N}
is also random. To reflect the sensing performance over a relatively long period of time, a common practice is to use the expectation of the distortion, instead of its instantaneous values, as the performance metric. For example, in estimation tasks, the squared Euclidean distance
d(\bm{\seta},\hat{\bm{\seta}})=\|\bm{\seta}-\hat{\bm{\seta}}\|^{2}
is a widely applied distortion function, whose expectation is the
mse
(
mse
). In binary (e.g. target presence) detection problems in which the task is to determine the value of a binary variable
\seta\in\{0,1\}
, a valid distortion function is the Hamming distance given by
d(\seta,\hat{\seta})=\seta\oplus\hat{\seta}
, whose expectation is related to commonly used detection metrics, including the detection probability
P_{\rm D}
and the false alarm rate
P_{\rm FA}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{\seta\oplus\hat{\seta}\}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt=(1\oplus 1)\mathbb{P}\{\hat{\seta}=1|\seta=1\}+(0\oplus 0)\mathbb{P}\{\hat{\seta}=0|\seta=0\}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91692pt+(1\oplus 0)\mathbb{P}\{\hat{\seta}=1|\seta=0\}+(0\oplus 1)\mathbb{P}\{\hat{\seta}=0|\seta=1\}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt=1-P_{\rm D}+P_{\rm FA}.

IV-B

CRB with Random but Known Nuisance Parameters

Since the communication performance of (
18
a) can be directly measured by the mutual information
I(\bm{\mathsfbr{X}};\bm{\mathsfbr{Y}}_{\rm c}|\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{\rm c})
, we are now in a position to rethink the sensing performance evaluation in ISAC systems. Indeed, in conventional radar systems, probing signals are typically deterministic, well-designed with good ambiguity properties. In ISAC systems, on the other hand, the transmit signal randomly varies from block to block given the communication data embedded in the signal. This imposes a unique challenge in defining the CRB, which now becomes a function of the random signal
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
.

One possible approach would be to treat
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
as a nuisance parameter, and either consider it as a part of the unknown sensing parameter, or integrate it out of the likelihood function describing the observations, leading to classical hybrid or marginal CRB expressions, respectively. Nonetheless, neither of these methods grasps the fundamental feature of the ISAC system, that the random signal
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
is
known
to the sensing
rx
, and is thus loose in general. To that end, we resort to a Miller-Chang type CRB
[
23
]
by computing the CRB for a given instance of
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
, and take the expectation over
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
. For any weakly unbiased estimator of
\bm{\seta}
, the MSE is lower bounded by the Miller-Chang Bayesian CRB in the form of

(19)

where the expectation is taken with respect to
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
, and
\bm{\mathsfbr{J}}_{\bm{\seta}|\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
denotes the Bayesian Fisher Information Matrix (BFIM) of
\bm{\seta}
given by

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsfbr{J}}_{\bm{\seta}|\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}

\displaystyle:=\!\mathbb{E}\!\left\{\!\frac{\partial\ln p_{\bm{\mathsfbr{Y}}_{\rm s}|\bm{\mathsfbr{X}},\bm{\seta}}(\bm{Y}_{\rm s}|\bm{X},\bm{\eta})}{\partial\bm{\eta}}\frac{\partial\ln p_{\bm{\mathsfbr{Y}}_{\rm s}|\bm{\mathsfbr{X}},\bm{\seta}}(\bm{Y}_{\rm s}|\bm{X},\bm{\eta})}{\partial\bm{\eta}^{\rm T}}\bigg|\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}\right\}

(20)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{\partial\ln p_{\bm{\seta}}(\bm{\eta})}{\partial\bm{\eta}}\frac{\partial\ln p_{\bm{\seta}}(\bm{\eta})}{\partial\bm{\eta}^{\rm T}}\right\}.

More precisely, the BFIM

Invariant cones for strange attractors of Lozi, Hénon and Belykh type maps
D. A. Grechko, V. N. Belykh, N. V. Barabash

Abstract
We consider strange attractors of two dimensional generalized map with one nonlinearity [ 1 , 2 ] such that Lozi, Hénon and Belykh maps are particular cases of it. We describe technique of invariant expanding and contracting cones creation for study of hyperbolic properties. Theorems of singular hyperbolic attractors for new modifications of Lozi, Hénon and Belykh-type maps are presented.

Introduction

In this paper we consider map

f:\;\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the form
[
1
,
2
]

f:\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{x}=x+y+g(x),\\
\bar{y}=\lambda\big(y+g(x)\big),\end{array}

(1)

where
0<\lambda<1
and
g(x)
is a piecewise smooth function.
This map arised as a model of phase-locked loop (PLL)
[
2
,
3
]
.
Namely, the simplest version of PLL models has the form of ODE system

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=u+\omega_{1}g(x),\\
\dot{u}=-\lambda_{0}u+\omega_{2}g(x),\end{array}

(2)

where
\omega_{1,2}
,
\lambda_{0}
are positive paramters, and
g(x)
is sine-like periodic function serving the output of PLL phase detector.

The discrete time model of PLL is similar to result of applying Euler method with discretely defined time derivatives in (
2
)

\begin{array}[]{l}x_{i+1}=x_{i}+hu_{i}+h\omega_{1}g(x_{i}),\\
u_{i+1}=u_{i}-h\lambda_{0}u_{i}+h\omega_{2}g(x_{i}).\end{array}

(3)

The variable
u
and parameters
\lambda_{0}
,
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
change in (
3
)

hu=y,\quad\lambda=1-\lambda_{0}h,\quad h\omega_{1}=a,\quad\omega_{2}h^{2}=\lambda a

(4)

leads to map (
1
).

Now we demonstrate that this map has a universal character.

1.1
Sine-Gordon equation

For the steady-state equation of Sine-Gordon type

(1-\lambda)\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}=\sin u

we consider the following discrete version

(1-\lambda)(u_{j}-u_{j-1})+u_{j+1}-2u_{j}+u_{j-1}=\sin u_{j}

or equation

u_{j+1}-u_{j}-\lambda(u_{j}-u_{j-1})=\sin u_{j}.

(5)

If we set
u_{j}=x_{j}
,
\lambda(u_{j}-u_{j-1})=y_{j}

then equation (
5
) implies that

\begin{array}[]{l}x_{j+1}=x_{j}+y_{j}+\sin x_{j},\\
y_{j+1}=\lambda(y_{j}+\sin x_{j}),\end{array}

and we obtain the dynamical system generated by map (
1
) with
g(x)=\sin x
.

1.2
Standard map

For
\lambda=1
,
g(x)=k\sin x
map (
1
) becomes area preserving Chirikov standard map
[
4
]
playing essential role in theory of the conservative chaos.

1.3
Zaslavsky map

Zaslavsky map
[
5
,
6
]
has the form

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{z}=\eta(z+a\sin\theta),\\
\bar{\theta}=\theta+\omega+z+a\sin\theta,\end{array}

where
a
,
\eta
and
\omega
are parameters.
If we set
\theta=x
,
z=y-\omega\eta
,
g(x)=\omega(1-\eta)+a\sin x
,
\eta=\lambda

then Zaslavsky map takes the form (
1
).

1.4
Lozi-Hénon map

Consider famous 2-D map

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{x}=y+1-aU(x),\\
\bar{y}=bx,\end{array}

(6)

which is Hénon map
[
7
,
8
]
for
U(x)=x^{2}
and the Lozi map
[
9
]
for
U(x)=|x|
.
Variable and parameter change in (

(x,y)\rightarrow(x,y-bx),\quad\lambda=-b,

leads the Lozi-Hénon map to the form (
1
) with the following function

g(x)=1-(1+\lambda)x-aU(x).

In this paper which is based on the results from
[
1
]
we present sufficient conditions of hyperbolicity for map (
1
) (section
2
).
As an example of applying these conditions we consider the Lozi map written in the form of map (
1
) (section
3

In section

Finally in section

Conditions of hyperbolicity

Invariant expanding and contracting cones play an essential role in the theory of hyperbilocity
[
13
,
14
,
12
,
21
]
, being the main tool of proven hyperbolic properties of certain maps
[
10
,
15
,
1
,
16
,
19
,
17
,
18
]
.
In this section we present detailed technique of cones construction briefly presented in
[
1
]
.

Definition 1

Assume that a smooth map
F:\;G\rightarrow G
, where
G
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, has the same in each point of
G
invariant cones
K^{u}
and
K^{s}

[
1
]
such that
DFK^{u}\subset K^{u}
and
DF^{-1}K^{s}\subset K^{s}
, where linearization
DF
is expanding in
K^{u}
and contracting in
K^{s}
for any point of
G
.
Then map
F
is called hyperbolic.

Note that despite of usual notation of hyperbolicity
[
12
,
11
]
this definition has a strong restriction that cones
K^{u}
and
K^{s}
are one and the same in each point of region
G
.

Consider map (
1
) in
region
G=G^{-}\cup G^{+}
where
G^{-(+)}
corresponds to pieces of function
g(x)
monotonicity with negative (positive, respectively) derivative
g^{\prime}(x)
.
In other words, we consider piecewise smooth map (
1
) with bounded away from zero derivative
g^{\prime}(x)

g^{\prime}(x)\triangleq d(x),\quad|d(x)|\geq\varepsilon>0,

for each point of
G
.

Theorem 1

Let the conditions

\begin{array}[]{ll}d(x)\geq\varepsilon>0&\textrm{for }x\in G^{+},\\
\\
d(x)<-2(1+\lambda)&\textrm{for }x\in G^{-},\end{array}

(7)

hold.
Then map (
1
) is hyperbolic in region
G
covered by the next invariant unstable and stable cones

K^{u}=\left\{0<\frac{u_{2}}{u_{1}}<\lambda\right\},\quad K^{s}=\left\{-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}<\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}<\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\right\},

(8)

where
u_{1,2}
are local coordinates in each point of
G
.

Proof.

To construct cones
K^{u,s}
we consider variational equations as a linearized map

L:\quad\bar{\textbf{u}}=A(x)\textbf{u},

(9)

where

\textbf{u}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\textbf{u}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{u}_{1}\\
\bar{u}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad A(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1+d(x)&1\\
\lambda d(x)&\lambda\end{pmatrix}.

(10)

Eigenvalues
\mu_{1,2}(x)
and eigenvectors
\textbf{V}_{1,2}(x)=\big(1,\alpha_{1,2}(x)\big)
of matrix
A(x)
take the form

\begin{array}[]{l}\mu_{1,2}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+d(x)+\lambda\pm\sqrt{(1+d(x)+\lambda)^{2}-4\lambda}\right),\\
\\
\alpha_{1,2}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1-d(x)+\lambda\pm\sqrt{(1+d(x)+\lambda)^{2}-4\lambda}\right).\end{array}

(11)

Under conditions (
7
) of Theorem
1
eigenvalues (
11
) satisfy
the following conditions

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mu_{1}(x)>1,&0<\mu_{2}(x)<1,&\textrm{for}\;d(x)>0,\\
\\
-1<\mu_{1}(x)<0,&\mu_{2}(x)<-1,&\textrm{for}\;d(x)<-2(1+\lambda),\end{array}

(12)

which imply that each point of
G
is of saddle type.

From formulas (
11
) satisfying conditions (
12
) it follows that the eigenvectors define the next directions

\begin{array}[]{ll}\textbf{V}_{1}\big|_{G^{+}}\;\textrm{and}\;\textbf{V}_{2}\big|_{G^{-}}\;\textrm{-- unstable directions},\\
\\
\textbf{V}_{2}\big|_{G^{+}}\;\textrm{and}\;\textbf{V}_{1}\big|_{G^{-}}\;\textrm{-- stable directions}.\end{array}

(13)

The angle boundaries of eigenvectors are defined by edge values of coordinates
\alpha_{1,2}(x)
.
Directions of the angle turn are defined by derivatives of
\alpha_{1,2}(x)
with respect to
d(x)

\alpha_{1,2}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1\pm\frac{1+d(x)+\lambda}{\sqrt{(1+d(x)+\lambda)^{2}-4\lambda}}\right)

which gives inequalities

\begin{array}[]{rrl}\alpha_{1}^{\prime}(x)>0,&\alpha_{2}^{\prime}(x)<0&\textrm{for}\;d\geq\varepsilon>0,\\
\\
\alpha_{1}^{\prime}(x)<0,&\alpha_{2}^{\prime}(x)>0&\textrm{for}\;d<-2(1+\lambda).\end{array}

Due to (
11
) we obtain the next intervals of monotonic change of
\alpha_{1,2}
as functions of
d(x)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{1}(x)\in(0,\lambda),\\
\alpha_{2}(x)\in(-1+\lambda,-\infty),\end{array}&\textrm{for}\;d(x)\in(0,\infty),\\
\\
\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{1}(x)\in(\infty,1+\lambda),\\
\alpha_{2}(x)\in(\lambda,2\lambda),\end{array}&\textrm{for}\;d(x)\in(-\infty,-2(1+\lambda))\end{array}

(14)

Thus, the family of all possible eigenvectors
\textbf{V}^{u}
and
\textbf{V}^{s}
in region
G
for
d(x)>0
and
d(x)<-2(1+\lambda)

are bounded by the next edge vectors (see Fig.
1
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\textbf{V}_{bot}^{u}=(1,0),&\textbf{V}_{up}^{u}=(1,2\lambda),\\
\\
\textbf{V}_{left}^{s}=(-1,1-\lambda),&\textbf{V}_{right}^{s}=(1,1+\lambda).\end{array}

(15)

As a result using these edge vectors we obtain the form of unstable and stable invariant cones (
8
).

\Box

Theorem 2

Let

be a trapping domain,

, of map

Hamid Reza Moradi, Shigeru Furuichi and Mohammad Sababheh

Abstract
. The main goal of this article is to present new inequalities for the spectral geometric mean A ​ ♮ t ​ B A\natural_{t}B of two positive definite operators A , B A,B on a Hilbert space. The obtained results complement many known inequalities for the geometric mean A ​ ♯ t ​ B A\sharp_{t}B . In particular, explicit comparisons between A ​ ♮ t ​ B A\natural_{t}B and A ​ ♯ t ​ B A\sharp_{t}B are given, with applications towards Ando-type inequalities and Ando-Hiai inequalities for A ​ ♮ t ​ B A\natural_{t}B and some other consequences.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. If
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
is such that
\left<Ax,x\right>\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
,
A
is said to be positive semi-definite, and we write
A\geq 0
. If in addition
A
is invertible, it is positive definite, and we write
A>0
. The class of positive definite operators in
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
will be denoted by
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
.
The ordering
A\geq B
means
A-B\geq 0
. In addition, if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
\alpha\leq A\leq\beta
if
\alpha I\leq A\leq\beta I
where
I
is the identity operator.

The weighted geometric mean of
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
is defined by the equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle A\sharp_{t}B=A^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(A^{-\frac{1}{2}}BA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{t}A^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

When
t=\frac{1}{2}
, we simply write
A\sharp B
instead of
A\sharp_{\frac{1}{2}}B.

An interesting geometric meaning of
A\sharp B
is that it is a midpoint of
A
and
B
for a natural Finsler metric (Thompson’s part metric) on the cone of positive definite operators
[
16
,
17
]
.

The geometric mean
\sharp_{t}
is a special case of operator means. Recall that an operator mean
\sigma
on
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
is a binary operation defined by

\displaystyle A\sigma B=A^{\frac{1}{2}}f\left(A^{-\frac{1}{2}}BA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)A^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is an operator monotone function, with
f(1)=1
.
Examples of other operator means are the weighted arithmetic and harmonic mean, defined respectively by

\displaystyle A\nabla_{t}B=(1-t)A+tB\;{\text{and}}\;A!_{t}B=\left((1-t)A^{-1}+tB^{-1}\right)^{-1},0\leq t\leq 1.

It is well known that

(1.2)

\displaystyle A!_{t}B\leq A\sharp_{t}B\leq A\nabla_{t}B,\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

In
[
5
]
, the spectral geometric mean was defined by

A{{\natural}}B={{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}A{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then the weighted spectral geometric mean was defined in
[
12
]
by

A{{\natural}_{t}}B={{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}A{{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}},0\leq t\leq 1.

In fact, simple manipulations of the above definition implies that
A{{\natural}_{t}}B
is a unique positive definite solution
X
of the following equation

{{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}={{A}^{-1}}\sharp X.

We refer the reader to
[
10
]
as a recent reference for further algebraic and geometric meaning of
A\natural_{t}B
.

Our main target in this article is to present new inequalities for
A\natural_{t}B
, in a way that complements those inequalities known for
A\sharp_{t}B
.

In particular,

•

The relations between
\left<A\natural_{t}Bx,x\right>
and
\left<Ax,x\right>^{1-t}\left<Bx,x\right>^{t}
are given.

•

Upper and lower bounds of
A\natural_{t}B
in terms of
A\sharp_{t}B
are given.

•

Ando-type inequalities are given for
\natural_{t}
, where the relation between
\Phi(A\natural_{t}B)
and
\Phi(A)\natural_{t}\Phi(B)
are described in both ways.

•

Kantorovich-type inequality is presented to find an upper bound of
\Phi\left(A^{-1}\right)\natural\Phi(A)^{-1}.

•

An Ando-Hiai inequality for the spectral geometric mean is given to describe the relation between
\left(A\natural_{t}B\right)^{r}
and
A^{r}\natural_{t}B^{r}
.

•

A detailed discussion of the Kantorovich constant is presented, as a by product of our results.

For this, we need to remind the reader of some well-established results for
A\sharp_{t}B.
We also refer the reader to the obtained inequalities for spectral geometric mean that simulates the corresponding result for the geometric mean. This will help the reader better follow the order of the results.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
1
]
Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
, and let
\Phi
be a unital positive linear map on
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\Phi\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)\leq\Phi\left(A\right){{\sharp}_{t}}\Phi\left(B\right).

In particular, for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

(1.3)

\left\langle A{{\sharp}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle\leq{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{1-t}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}.

We refer the reader to Theorems
2.1
and
2.3
below for the spectral geometric mean versions of Lemma
1.1
.

In this paper, we use the generalized Kantorovich constant for
0<m<M
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

K\left(m,M,t\right)=\frac{\left(m{{M}^{t}}-M{{m}^{t}}\right)}{\left(t-1\right)\left(M-m\right)}{{\left(\frac{t-1}{t}\frac{{{M}^{t}}-{{m}^{t}}}{m{{M}^{t}}-M{{m}^{t}}}\right)}^{t}}.

It is known that this recovers the (original) Kantorovich constant when
t=-1
and
t=2
, that is,
K(m,M,2)=K(m,M,-1)=\dfrac{(M+m)^{2}}{4Mm}
, whose constant was appeared in
[
9
, p.142]
as the so-called Kantorovich inequality. In Section
3
, we study the generalized Kantorovich constant is defined by

(1.4)

K(x,t):=\frac{(x^{t}-x)}{(t-1)(x-1)}\left(\frac{t-1}{t}\frac{x^{t}-1}{x^{t}-x}\right)^{t},\quad x>0,\,\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

We see that
K(x,1,t)=K(x,t)
.
We use the same symbol
K
as the generalized Kantorovich constant. However, this never creates confusion for the readers by the different numbers of their variables.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
2
]

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that
mA\leq B\leq MA
for some scalars
0<\text{}m\leq M
, and let
\Phi
be a unital positive linear map. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\Phi\left(A\right){{\sharp}_{t}}\Phi\left(B\right)\leq\frac{1}{K\left(m,M,t\right)}\Phi\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right).

The spectral geometric mean version of Lemma
1.2
is stated below in Theorem
2.4
.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
11
]

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
such that
{{m}_{1}}\leq A\leq{{M}_{1}}
,
{{m}_{2}}\leq B\leq{{M}_{2}}
and let
\Phi
be a unital positive linear map on
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. Then

\Phi\left(A\right)\sharp\Phi\left(B\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{{{M}_{1}}{{M}_{2}}}+\sqrt{{{m}_{1}}{{m}_{2}}}}{2\sqrt[4]{{{M}_{1}}{{m}_{1}}{{M}_{2}}{{m}_{2}}}}\Phi\left(A\sharp B\right).

In particular, for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\sqrt{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}\leq\frac{\sqrt{{{M}_{1}}{{M}_{2}}}+\sqrt{{{m}_{1}}{{m}_{2}}}}{2\sqrt[4]{{{M}_{1}}{{m}_{1}}{{M}_{2}}{{m}_{2}}}}\left\langle A\sharp Bx,x\right\rangle.

The Choi-Davis inequality is stated next
[
3
,
4
]
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
f:J\to\mathbb{R}
be an operator convex function, and let
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
be a unital positive linear mapping. Then

(1.5)

f(\Phi(A))\leq\Phi(f(A)),

for all self-adjoint operators
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with spectra in the interval
J
.

In particular,
\Phi(A)^{-1}\leq\Phi\left(A^{-1}\right),
for any invertible self-adjoint operator
A
.

The inequality (
1.5
) is reversed when
f
is operator concave.

In particular, if
0\leq t\leq 1,
then

\Phi(A^{t})\leq\Phi(A)^{t},

for any positive definite operator
A
.

Lemma 1.5
.

[
13
]
Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
be such that
m\leq A\leq M
, and let
\Phi
be a positive linear map on
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{t}}\leq\frac{1}{K\left(m,M,t\right)}\Phi\left({{A}^{t}}\right)

In particular, for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{t}}\leq\frac{1}{K\left(m,M,t\right)}\left\langle{{A}^{t}}x,x\right\rangle

Lemma 1.6
.

[
14
]

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
such that
0<m\leq A\leq M
and let
\Phi
be a positive unital linear mapping. Then

\Phi\left(A^{-1}\right)\leq\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}\Phi(A)^{-1}.

In particular, if
x\in\mathcal{H}
is any unit vector, then

\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle\leq\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{-1}},

for any positive definite
A
.

We refer the reader to Corollary
2.4
below for the corresponding inequality that the spectral geometric mean fulfills, similar to Lemma
1.6
.

While the inequality
A\leq B
does not imply
A^{2}\leq B^{2}
, for arbitrary self-adjoint operators in general, the following useful weaker version holds.

Lemma 1.7
.

[
6
]

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that
0<A\leq B
and
m\leq A\leq M
. Then

{{A}^{2}}\leq\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}{{B}^{2}}.

Lemma 1.8
.

[
18
]
Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that
m\leq A,B\leq M
for some scalars
0\leq m\leq M
, and let

. Then for any

,

{{\left(A{{\nabla}_{t}}B\right)}^{r}}\leq\frac{1}{K\left(m,M,t\right)}{{A}^{r}}{{\nabla}_{t}}{{B}^{r}}.

Lemma 1.9
.

[
15
]
Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be a positive semi-definite operator. If

is a unit vector, then

(i)

{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{r}}\leq\left\langle{{A}^{r}}x,x\right\rangle
for all

.

(ii)

\left\langle{{A}^{r}}x,x\right\rangle\leq{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{r}}
for all

(iii)

{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{r}}\leq\left\langle{{A}^{r}}x,x\right\rangle

\Phi(A\sharp B)^{t}\leq\left(\Phi(A)\sharp\Phi(B)\right)^{t},0\leq t\leq 1

2.
Main results

In this section, we present our main results, where we show some comparisons between the geometric and spectral geometric means first, then we discuss some Ando-type inequalities for the spectral geometric mean.

2.1.
Some comparisons between
A\sharp_{t}B
and
A\natural_{t}B

The first result is the spectral geometric version of (
1.3
). For this, we notice first that if
m,M
are positive scalars and
A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{+}
are such that
m\leq A,B\leq M
, then
M^{-1}\leq A^{-1}\leq m^{-1}
, which in turns imply

(2.1)

\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}}\leq A^{-1}\sharp B\leq\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}},

as a consequence of the monotone property of the geometric mean.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
be such that
m\leq A,B\leq M
, and let
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\left\langle A{{\natural}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle\leq\frac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{1-t}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}.

Proof.

Using (
2.1
) and by conjugation with
(A^{-1}\sharp B)^{t}
we get

\frac{{{m}^{1+t}}}{{{M}^{t}}}\leq X:={{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}A{{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}\leq\frac{{{M}^{1+t}}}{{{m}^{t}}}.

On the other hand, we know that
A{{\natural}_{t}}B
is a unique positive definite solution
X
of the following equation

(2.2)

{{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}={{A}^{-1}}\sharp X.

Let
X=A{{\natural}_{t}}B
. For any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\xi\sqrt{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle\left\langle Xx,x\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}\sharp Xx,x\right\rangle\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{10})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq{{\left\langle\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)x,x\right\rangle}^{t}}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{6})}

\displaystyle\leq{{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle}^{\frac{t}{2}}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{\frac{t}{2}}}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{11})},

where
\xi=\dfrac{2\sqrt{{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}}{{{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}}
.
Thus,

\left\langle Xx,x\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{{{\xi}^{2}}}{{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle}^{t-1}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}},

which implies,

\left\langle A{{\natural}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{{{\xi}^{2}}}{{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle}^{t-1}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}.

Again by Lemma
1.9
,

\left\langle A{{\natural}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{{{\xi}^{2}}}{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{1-t}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}.

∎

The relation between weighted spectral geometric mean, arithmetic mean, and harmonic mean is stated in the following result, in a way that simulates (
1.2
) for the geometric mean. This follows from Theorem
2.1
, the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean, and the fact that
{{\left({{A}^{-1}}{{\natural}_{t}}{{B}^{-1}}\right)}^{-1}}=A{{\natural}_{t}}B
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)^{+}
be such that
0<m\leq A,B\leq M
, and let
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then

\frac{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}A{{!}_{t}}B\leq A{{\natural}_{t}}B\leq\frac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}A{{\nabla}_{t}}B.

Remark 2.1
.

The inequalities in Corollary
2.1
can be improved as follows. Recall the following refinement of the weighted arithmetic–geometric mean inequality
[
19
]

{{\left(\frac{{{\left(1+h\right)}^{2}}}{4h}\right)}^{r}}{{a}^{1-t}}{{b}^{t}}\leq\left(\left(1-t\right)a+tb\right)

where
h={b}/{a}\;
and
r=\min\left\{t,1-t\right\}
. Let
x
be a unit vector. If we replace
a
and
b
by
\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle
and
\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle
with
m\leq A\leq m^{\prime}<M^{\prime}\leq B\leq M
and noting that the function

f\left(h\right)=\frac{{{\left(1+h\right)}^{2}}}{4h}

is increasing for
h\geq 1,
we get

{{\left(\frac{{{\left(M^{\prime}+m^{\prime}\right)}^{2}}}{4M^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\right)}^{r}}{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{1-t}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}\leq\left\langle\left(A{{\nabla}_{t}}B\right)x,x\right\rangle.

Thus,

A{{\natural}_{t}}B\leq\frac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}{{\left(\frac{4M^{\prime}m^{\prime}}{{{\left(M^{\prime}+m^{\prime}\right)}^{2}}}\right)}^{r}}A{{\nabla}_{t}}B,

whenever
m\leq A\leq m^{\prime}<M^{\prime}\leq B\leq M
. The same inequality holds when
m\leq B\leq m^{\prime}<M^{\prime}\leq A\leq M
.

The following result intends to give a relationship between the weighted spectral geometric mean and the weighted geometric mean.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that
0<m\leq A,B\leq M
, and let
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then

A{{\natural}_{t}}B\leq\frac{1}{K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}\frac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B.

Proof.

If
m\leq A,B\leq M
, then
\frac{m}{M}A\leq B\leq\frac{M}{m}A
. In this case, by Lemma
1.2
, we get

\Phi\left(A\right){{\sharp}_{t}}\Phi\left(B\right)\leq\frac{1}{K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}\Phi\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right),

for any positive unital linear mapping
\Phi.

In particular,

{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{1-t}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}\leq\frac{1}{K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}\left\langle A{{\sharp}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle

for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
.
Now, by Theorem
2.1
, we have

A{{\natural}_{t}}B\leq\frac{1}{K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}\frac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B.

∎

A reverse of Theorem
2.1
can be stated as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that
0<m\leq A,B\leq M
, and let
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{1-t}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}\leq\frac{1}{{{K}^{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}},\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}},t\right)}{{\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}}}\left\langle A{{\natural}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle,

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle\left\langle Xx,x\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}\sharp Xx,x\right\rangle\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{11})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq K\left(\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}},\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}},t\right){{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}\sharp Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{12})}

\displaystyle\geq K\left(\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}},\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}},t\right){{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\right)}^{t}}{{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle}^{\frac{t}{2}}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{\frac{t}{2}}}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{10})}.

Thus,

\left\langle A{{\natural}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle\geq{{K}^{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}},\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}},t\right){{\left(\frac{4Mm}{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}\right)}^{t}}{{\left\langle{{A}^{-1}}x,x\right\rangle}^{t-1}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}.

Now, by Lemma
1.6
, we have

{{\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle}^{1-t}}{{\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle}^{t}}\leq\frac{1}{{{K}^{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}},\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}},t\right)}{{\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}}}\left\langle A{{\natural}_{t}}Bx,x\right\rangle,

as desired.
∎

Now we are ready to present a reversed version of Corollary (
2.2
).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that
0<m\leq A,B\leq M
, and let
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then

A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\leq\frac{1}{{{K}^{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}},\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}},t\right)}{{\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}}}A{{\natural}_{t}}B.

2.2
and
2.3
is given in Proposition

. The proposition is stated later in this paper due to the detailed computations of the generalized Kantorovich constant, which we prefer to state independently to avoid disturbing the readers.

2.2.
Ando-type inequalities for
A\natural_{t}B

We begin this section by presenting the following Ando-type inequality, similar to the celebrated result given in Lemma
1.1
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be positive definite such that
0<m\leq A,B\leq M
and let
\Phi
be a positive unital linear mapping. Then

\Phi(A\natural_{t}B)\leq\beta\Phi(A)\natural_{t}\Phi(B),0\leq t\leq 1,

where
\beta=\dfrac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2t}}{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{4}}}{{{4}^{2+t}}{{M}^{2+3t}}{{m}^{2+3t}}}
.

Proof.

Let
X=A\natural_{t}B
and
Y=\Phi(A)\natural_{t}\Phi(B).
Then

\displaystyle\Phi\left(A^{-1}\sharp X\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\left(A^{-1}\sharp B\right)^{t}\right)\;\;({\text{by}}\;\eqref{eq_for_spec})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi\left(A^{-1}\sharp B\right)^{t}\;\;\;({\text{by\;Lemma}}\;\ref{lemm_choi})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Phi\left(A^{-1}\right)\sharp\Phi(B)\right)^{t}\;\;({\text{by}}\;\eqref{needed_eq_11})

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{1}\Phi(A)^{-1}\sharp\Phi(B)\right)^{t}\;\;({\text{by\;Lemma}}\;\ref{2})

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(\Phi(A)^{-1}\sharp\Phi(B)\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\frac{t}{2}}\Phi(A)^{-1}\sharp Y,

where
{{C}_{1}}=\dfrac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}
. On the other hand, using the reversed Ando’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi\left(A^{-1}\sharp X\right)

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\Phi(A^{-1})\sharp\Phi(X)\;\;({\text{by\;Lemma}}\;\ref{10})

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\Phi(A)^{-1}\sharp\Phi(X)\;\;({\text{by\;Lemma}}\;\ref{lemm_choi})

where
{{C}_{2}}=\dfrac{2\sqrt{{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}}{{{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}}
. Thus, we have shown that

\displaystyle C_{2}\Phi(A)^{-1}\sharp\Phi(X)\leq C_{1}^{\frac{t}{2}}\Phi(A)^{-1}\sharp Y.

This leads to

\displaystyle C_{2}\left(\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(X)\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{1}^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}Y\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, by Lemma
1.7
, we infer that

\displaystyle C_{2}^{2}\left(\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(X)\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq C_{1}^{t}K_{2}\left(\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}Y\Phi(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

where
{{K}_{2}}=\dfrac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}
, which in turns implies

\displaystyle\Phi(A\natural_{t}B)\leq\frac{C_{1}^{t}K_{2}}{C_{2}^{2}}\Phi(A)\natural_{t}\Phi(B).

This completes the proof.
∎

Having shown Theorem
2.3
as an Ando-type inequality for the spectral geometric mean, the following theorem intends to give a reversed version of Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be positive definite such that
0<m\leq A,B\leq M
and let
\Phi
be a positive unital linear mapping. Then

\Phi\left(A\right){{\natural}_{t}}\Phi\left(B\right)\leq\frac{1}{{{K}^{2}}\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2t}}\frac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}\Phi\left(A{{\natural}_{t}}B\right).

Proof.

Let
X=A\natural_{t}B
. Then

\displaystyle\Phi\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp X\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi\left({{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}\right)\quad({\text{by}}\;\eqref{eq_for_spec})

\displaystyle\geq K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)\Phi{{\left({{A}^{-1}}\sharp B\right)}^{t}}\quad({\text{by\;Lemma}}\;\ref{12})

\displaystyle\geq K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right){{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\Phi\left({{A}^{-1}}\right)\sharp\Phi\left(B\right)\right)}^{t}}\quad({\text{by\;Lemma}}\;\ref{5})

\displaystyle=K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right){{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\right)}^{t}}{{\left(\Phi\left({{A}^{-1}}\right)\sharp\Phi\left(B\right)\right)}^{t}}

\displaystyle\geq K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right){{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\right)}^{t}}{{\left(\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{-1}}\sharp\Phi\left(B\right)\right)}^{t}}\;({\text{by\;Lemma}}\;\ref{lemm_choi})

\displaystyle=K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right){{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\right)}^{t}}\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{-1}}\sharp Y,

where
Y=\Phi(A)\natural_{t}\Phi(B)
; see (
2.2
). This implies

\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{-1}}\sharp Y\leq\frac{1}{K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{t}}\Phi\left({{A}^{-1}}\right)\sharp\Phi\left(X\right).

Therefore,

{{\left(\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}Y\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{K\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{t}}{{\left(\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Phi\left(X\right)\Phi{{\left(A\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

By Lemma
1.7
,

\Phi\left(A\right){{\natural}_{t}}\Phi\left(B\right)\leq\frac{1}{{{K}^{2}}\left(\frac{m}{M},\frac{M}{m},t\right)}{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2t}}\frac{{{\left({{M}^{1+t}}+{{m}^{1+t}}\right)}^{2}}}{4{{M}^{1+t}}{{m}^{1+t}}}\Phi\left(A{{\natural}_{t}}B\right),

as desired.
∎

An upper bound of
\Phi(A)\sharp_{t}\Phi(A)^{-1}
in terms of the Kantorovich constant is usually stated as the Kantorovich inequality. In the following, we present this inequality for the spectral geometric mean.

Corollary 2.4
.

(Operator Kantorovich inequality for spectral geometric mean) Let

.

Let

be such that

, and let

Theorem 2.5
.

(Ando-Hiai inequality for spectral geometric mean) Let

{{\left(A{{\natural}_{t}}B\right)}^{r}}\leq\min\{\kappa_{1}(m,M,r,t,\lambda),\kappa_{2}(m,M,r,t,\lambda)\}{{A}^{r}}{{\natural}_{t}}{{B}^{r}},

.

Proof.

, we replace

and

3.
On the generalized Kantorovich constant

In our discussion of the above inequalities, different constants have shown up. This section attempts to give a detailed discussion of these constants. This helps better understand the stated results. The conclusion of this section is a hidden relation between Corollaries
2.2
and
2.3
.

Due to the technical nature of the proofs of the results in this section, we have added the proofs in the appendix at the end of this paper. So, results in Section 3 will be presented without proofs so that the reader can easily follow without distraction.

In the following proposition, we present a monotonicity behavior of the generalized Kantorovich constant
K(x,t)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The generalized Kantorovich constant
K(x,t)
satisfies the following monotonicity properties in
x
.

(i)

If
t<0
or
t>1
, then
K(x,t)
is monotone decreasing when
0<x<1
, and monotone increasing when
x>1
.
K(x,t)
takes the minimum value
1
when
x=1
.

(ii)

If
0<t<1
, then
K(x,t)
is monotone increasing when
0<x<1
, and monotone decreasing when
x>1
.
K(x,t)
takes the maximum value
1
when
x=1
.

Remark 3.1
.

The case
0<x<1
in Proposition
3.1
can be proven by the fact
K(1/x,t)=K(x,t)
for any
t>0
and
x>0
,
[
7
, Theorem 2.54 (i)]
.

Proposition
3.1
can be stated equivalently in the following form, where

(3.1)

K(m,M,t):=\frac{(mM^{t}-Mm^{t})}{(t-1)(M-m)}\left(\frac{t-1}{t}\frac{M^{t}-m^{t}}{mM^{t}-Mm^{t}}\right)^{t},\quad(0<m<M)

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
0<m_{1}<M_{1}
and
0<m_{2}<M_{2}
such that
\dfrac{M_{1}}{m_{1}}\leq\dfrac{M_{2}}{m_{2}}
.
If
0<t<1
, then
K(m_{1},M_{1},t)\geq K(m_{2},M_{2},t)
. If
t<0
or
t>1
, then
K(m_{1},M_{1},t)\leq K(m_{2},M_{2},t)
.

To better understand these constants, and to reach our goal, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

(I)

Let
0<t<1
. For
x>1
, the following inequality holds

(3.2)

\frac{x+1}{x-1}>(1-t)^{2}\left(\frac{x+x^{t}}{x-x^{t}}\right)+t^{2}\left(\frac{x^{t}+1}{x^{t}-1}\right).

For
0<x<1
, we have the inequality (
3.3
) below.

(II)

Let
t>1
or
t<0
. For
x>1
, the following inequality holds

(3.3)

\frac{x+1}{x-1}<(1-t)^{2}\left(\frac{x+x^{t}}{x-x^{t}}\right)+t^{2}\left(\frac{x^{t}+1}{x^{t}-1}\right).

For
0<x<1
, we have the inequality (
3.2
) above.

Applying Lemma
3.1
, we have the following bounds of Kantorovich constant
K(x,t)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Define
L(x,t):=\left(\dfrac{x^{t}+x}{x+1}\right)\left(\dfrac{x^{t}+1}{x^{t}+x}\right)^{t}
for
x>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(I)

If
0\leq t\leq 1
, then

(3.4)

L(x,t)\leq K(x,t),\quad(x>0).

(II)

If
t\geq 1
or
t\leq 0
, then

(3.5)

L(x,t)\geq K(x,t),\quad(x>0).

Note that we have

L(x,1/2)=\frac{\sqrt{x}(\sqrt{x}+1)^{2}}{x+1}\leq\frac{2\sqrt[4]{x}}{\sqrt{x}+1}=K(x,1/2)

and

L(x,2)=L(x,-1)=\frac{(x^{2}+1)^{2}}{x(x+1)^{2}}\geq\frac{(x+1)^{2}}{4x}=K(x,2)=K(x,-1)

for special cases.

Corollary 3.2
.

(I)

For
0\leq t\leq 1
, we have

(3.6)

K(x^{2},t)\leq K^{2}(x,t),\quad(x>0).

(II)

For
t\geq 1
or
t\leq 0
, we have

(3.7)

K(x^{2},t)\geq K^{2}(x,t),\quad(x>0).

Now we are ready to present the relation between Corollaries
2.2
and
2.3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that

, and let

and

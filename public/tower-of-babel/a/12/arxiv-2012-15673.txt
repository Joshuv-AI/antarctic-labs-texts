Representations of quantum groups arising from the Stokes phenomenon
Xiaomeng Xu

Abstract
In this paper we prove that the quantum Stokes matrices of the quantum differential equation at a second order pole give rise to representations of the quantum group U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) . We explain our results from the viewpoint of deformation quantization of the classical Stokes matrices at a second order pole. As a consequence, we can get a dictionary between the theory of Stokes phenomenon and the theory of quantum groups. We briefly discuss several such correspondences, and outline the generalization of our results to all classical types of Lie algebras and to the quantum differential equation at an arbitrary order pole.



1
Introduction

The Stokes phenomenon states that a solution of a meromorphic linear ordinary differential equation may have different asymptotic expressions as
z
approaches an irregular singularity
z_{0}
from different sectorial regions around
z_{0}
. See the books
[
6
,
44
,
67
]
for a detailed introduction to the Stokes phenomenon. The discontinuous jump of the asymtotics around
z_{0}
can be measured by the Stokes matrices. In the past decades, the Stokes phenomenon/matrices of meromorphic linear systems of differential equation at a second order pole have played an important role in many subjects of mathematics and physics. However the Stokes matrices are highly transcendental and thus hard to study.
In this paper, we find the algebraic structures hidden behind the Stokes phenomenon. The importance of the result is that it enables us to apply the algebraic methods/ideas to solve some open analysis problems for the linear systems and the associated isomonodromy deformation equations. In particular, in later works, we solve the long time asymptotics and the associated Riemann-Hilbert problem
[
72
,
61
]
, the nonlinear connection problems
[
74
]
, and give the characterization of the WKB analysis
[
5
,
72
]
, the classification of the soliton solutions and so on. In the following, let us explain the main result of this paper.

Let us take the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
over the field of complex numbers, and its universal enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})
generated by
\{e_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
subject to the relation
[e_{ij},e_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}e_{il}-\delta_{li}e_{kj}
. Let us take the
n\times n
matrix
T=(T_{ij})
with entries valued in
U({\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})

T_{ij}=e_{ij},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
denote the set of
n\times n
diagonal matrices with distinct eigenvalues. Given any finite-dimensional irreducible representation
L(\lambda)
of
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
with highest weight
\lambda
, let us consider the quantum confluent hypergeometric system

\frac{dF}{dz}=h\Big(u+\frac{T}{z}\Big)\cdot F,

(1)

for
F(z)\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
an
n\times n
matrix function with entries in
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
. Here

is a complex parameter,
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
is seen as an
n\times n
matrix with scalar entries in
U({\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})
, and the action of the coefficient matrix on

is given by matrix multiplication and the representation of
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
.

1.1
Main results

Nonresonant case
h\notin\mathbb{Q}

Let us first assume
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
. The equation (
1
) is then nonresonant and thus has a unique formal solution
\hat{F}
around

z=\infty
. See Proposition
2.1
for a proof. The standard theory of resummation (see e.g,
[

,
44
,
67
]

S_{h\pm}(u)\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

Theorem 1.1
.

(c.f. Theorem
4.1
)
For any fixed
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, the map (with
q=e^{\pi\mathrm{i}h}
)

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{q}(u):U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})&\rightarrow{\rm End}(L(\lambda))~;\\
e_{i}&\mapsto\frac{S_{h+}(u)_{i,i}^{-1}\cdot S_{h+}(u)_{i,i+1}}{q^{-1}-q},\\
f_{i}&\mapsto\frac{S_{h-}(u)_{i+1,i}\cdot S_{h-}(u)_{i,i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},\\
q^{h_{i}}&\mapsto S_{h+}(u)_{i,i}\end{split}

(2)

defines a representation of the Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
on the vector space
L(\lambda)
. Here recall that
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is a unital associative algebra with generators
q^{\pm h_{i}},e_{j},f_{j},

1\leq j\leq n-1,1\leq i\leq n
and relations:

•

for each
1\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq j\leq n-1
,

q^{h_{i}}q^{-h_{i}}=q^{-h_{i}}q^{h_{i}}=1,\ q^{h_{i}}e_{j}q^{-h_{i}}=q^{\delta_{ij}}q^{-\delta_{i,j+1}}e_{j},\ q^{h_{i}}f_{j}q^{-h_{i}}=q^{-\delta_{ij}}q^{\delta_{i,j+1}}f_{j};

•

for each
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
,

[e_{i},f_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{q^{h_{i}-h_{i+1}}-q^{-h_{i}+h_{i+1}}}{q-q^{-1}};

•

for
|i-j|=1
,

e_{i}^{2}e_{j}-(q+q^{-1})e_{i}e_{j}e_{i}+e_{j}e_{i}^{2}=0,

f_{i}^{2}f_{j}-(q+q^{-1})f_{i}f_{j}f_{i}+f_{j}f_{i}^{2}=0,

and for
|i-j|\neq 1
,
[e_{i},e_{j}]=0=[f_{i},f_{j}]
.

The theorem associates to any representation
L(\lambda)
of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})

\mathcal{S}_{q}(u)
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
on the same vector space

Resonant case

It is convenient to think of (

Its irregular term

Theorem 1.2
.

For any fixed

By pursuing the heuristics of the above algebraic structure of the Stokes matrices, we are led to rather interesting results. On the one hand, Theorem

1.2
Motivation: deformation quantization of the classical Stokes matrices at second order poles

In this subsection, we explain our original motivation and explain the main theorem from the viewpoint of deformation quantization.

Let us consider the classical confluent hypergeometric system for a function
f(z)\in{\rm GL}_{n}

\frac{df}{dz}=\left(u+\frac{A}{z}\right)\cdot f,

(3)

where
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, and
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

In
[
9
]
, Boalch interpreted the dual Poisson Lie group
GL_{n}^{*}
of
GL_{n}
as the space of Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)\in GL_{n}
of (
3
), and proved that the irregular Riemann-Hilbert map (also known as Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff map) from the de Rham to the Betti moduli space

\nu(u):\mathfrak{gl}_{n}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\rightarrow GL_{n}^{*};~A\mapsto(S_{-}(u,A),S_{+}(u,A))

(4)

associating the Stokes matrices to the confluent hypergeometric system (
3
) parameterized by
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, is a Poisson map.

In terms of the r-matrix formulation of the Poisson brackets on the dual Poisson Lie group, Boalch’s theorem can be rewritten via the classical RLL formulation (see
[
2
, Formula (235)]
):

Theorem 1.3
.

[
9
]

For any fixed
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, the matrix valued function
M(u,A):=S_{-}(u,A)S_{+}(u,A)\in GL_{n}
of
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\{M^{1}\otimes M^{2}\}=r_{+}M^{1}M^{2}+M^{1}M^{2}r_{-}-M^{1}r_{+}M^{2}-M^{2}r_{-}M^{1}.

(5)

Here

\displaystyle r_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

\displaystyle r_{-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}-\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

and the tensor notation is used: both sides are
\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
valued functions on

A\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
,
M^{1}:=M(u)\otimes 1
,
\nu^{2}:=1\otimes\nu(u)
, and the
ij,kl
coefficient of the matrix
\{M^{1},M^{2}\}
is
defined as the Poisson bracket
\{M(u)_{ij},M(u)_{kl}\}
of the functions on the
canonical linear Poisson space
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{\ast}\cong\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
.

The theorem gives rise to a distinguished (complex version of the) Ginzburg-Weinstein linearization of the dual Poisson Lie group
[
30
]
. In the literature, there are many proofs of the Ginzburg-Weinstein linearization theorem, from the perspectives of cohomology calculation, Moser’s trick in symplectic geometry, Stokes phenomenon, Gelfand-Tsetlin integrable systems, the quantum algebras and so on, see e.g.,
[
30
,
1
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
21
]
.
It is natural to ask if these seemingly rather different methods are related to each other in some deep manner. We have been working on this problem with the belief that the connection between the different methods will bring new insights into the above mentioned subjects. In a series of works, we clarified the relations between these constructions. In particular, in
[
71
]
, we pointed out a relation between the Boalch’s construction and the Enriquez-Etingof-Marshall construction. It motivates us to study the deformation of the classical Stokes matrices. In the following, let us explain the idea.

On the one hand, it is known that (see e.g., Proposition
5.3
) the classical equation (
3
) has a unique formal solution

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(z)=\widehat{h}(z)e^{{uz}}z^{\delta A},\ \ \ \text{with}\ \widehat{h}=1+h_{1}z^{-1}+h_{2}z^{-1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(6)

where
\delta A
is the diagonal part of the matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1,...,n}
, and the
(k,l)
-entry
h_{m,kl}\in{\mathbb{C}}
of the
m
-th coefficient
h_{m}\in{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is given by a degree
2m
polynomial in the variables
\{a_{ij}\}
. The formal power series
\hat{h}(z)
itself already encodes all the information. Thus the observation is that the Stokes phenomenon of (
3
) for all
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is encoded in the sequence of functions

\Big\{h_{m}(u,A)\in Sym(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\Big\}.

(7)

On the other hand, following Proposition
5.1
, the equation (
1
) has a formal solution

\displaystyle\widehat{F}(z)=\widehat{H}(z)e^{{huz}}z^{h\delta T},\ \ \ \text{with}\ \widehat{H}=1+H_{1}z^{-1}+H_{2}z^{-1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(8)

where
\delta T
is the diagonal part of
T
, and the
(k,l)
-entry
H_{m,kl}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
of the
m
-th coefficient
H_{m}
is given by (the evaluation on the representation
L(\lambda)
of) a filtered degree
2m
element in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. Thus the Stokes phenomenon of (
1
) is encoded in the sequence

\Big\{H_{m}(hu,hT)\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\Big\}.

Following Proposition
5.1
and
5.3
, the quantum coefficients
H_{m}
satisfy the same recursive relation as the classical coefficients
h_{m}
(provided replacing
A
and
\delta A
by
T
and
\delta T
for all
l=1,...,k
).
Thus, the quantum equation (
1
) provides us a canonical deformation quantization of the sequence (
7
) from the Poisson algebra
Sym(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
to the quantized algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
(see Section
5
): for all positive integer
m
and
k,l=1,...,n
, we have a canonical lift

\Big\{h_{m,kl}(u,A)\in Sym(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\Big\}\rightarrow\Big\{H_{m,kl}(hu,hT)\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\Big\}.

(9)

The Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)
and
S_{h\pm}(u)
of (
3
) and (
1
) are obtained via the Borel-Laplace transforms of the power series
\sum_{m}h_{m}z^{-m}
and
\sum_{m}H_{m}z^{-m}
respectively. And the Borel-Laplace transforms are compatible with the deformation quantization in (
9
). Therefore, after taking
completion, the Stokes matrices
S_{h\pm}(u)
of (
22
), seen as
n\times n
matrices with entries valued in
\hat{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\llbracket h\rrbracket
(or in any
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
) are understood as the deformation quantization of the classical Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)\in\widehat{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of (
3
), seen as
n\times n
matrix valued analytic functions of the parameter
A\in{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}^{*}
.

Remark 1.4
.

More directly, using the associated function method, see e.g.,
[
6
, Chapter 9]
and
[
47
,
77
]
, the Stokes matrices can be obtained from the regularized limit of the matrix
h_{m}
and
H_{m}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
respectively (as a consequence, the Poisson brackets between the entries of Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)
, as functions on
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}^{*}
, can be obtained by taking the limit of the Poisson brackets between the entries of the coefficient matrices
h_{m}(u,A)
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, see
[
60
]
).

That is considering the Stokes matrices of (
1
) leads to a canonical lift for all
i,j=1,...,n

\Big\{S_{\pm}(u,A)_{ij}\in\widehat{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\Big\}\rightarrow\Big\{S_{h\pm}(u)_{ij}\in\widehat{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\llbracket h\rrbracket\Big\}.

(10)

By this reason, we also call
S_{h\pm}(u)
the quantum Stokes matrices.

Now the irregular Riemann-Hilbert map
\nu(u)
in (
4
) pulls back the entry coordinates on the space of Stokes matrices to the highly transcendental functions
\{S_{\pm}(u,A)_{ij}\}
on
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}^{*}
. This collection of functions is the data of the irregular Riemann-Hilbert map. By (
10
), the (quantum) Stokes matrices of (
1
) is seen as a quantization/deformation of the irregular Riemann-Hilbert map in (
4
).

For fixed
u
, this collection of functions
\{S_{\pm}(u,A)_{ij}\}
is a new coordinate system around a small neighbourhood of
0\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}^{*}
(here we take the upper and lower entries of the upper and lower triangular Stokes matrices respectively). Theorem
1.3
states that in terms of the new coordinates
\{S_{\pm}(u,A)_{ij}\}
, the canonical linear Poisson bracket on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}^{*}
takes the nonlinear (but still algebraic) classical r-matrix form. That is the Poisson bracket is linear under the entry coordinates of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}^{*}
, and nonlinear but still has a nice form under the transcendental coordinates
\{S_{\pm}(u,A)_{ij}\}
.

After the deformation quantization, the coordinates
\{S_{\pm}(u,A)_{ij}\}
become elements
\{S_{h\pm}(u)_{ij}\}
in
\hat{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\llbracket h\rrbracket
or by taking a representation in
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
. Now the question is if the associative algebra product in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
or the ordinary composition in
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
, between these elements
S_{h\pm}(u)_{ij}
, takes a nice form. Given Theorem
1.3
, we expect that the entries of (quantum) Stokes matrices
S_{h\pm}(u)
satisfy the quantum RLL relation. In the paper we prove such a statement as in Theorem
1.1
(see Section
3
for the RLL relation and its equivalence with Theorem
1.1
).

The important idea conveyed in the discussion above is that, the deformation/quantization of the irregular Riemann-Hilbert map, thought of as a collection of transcendental functions on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}^{*}
, is just a collection of generators in the associative algebra or
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
. In a sense, it already quantizes the Poisson structures involved. We stress that to see if this collection of elements satisfy natural algebraic relations (like the RLL relation) is a different question.

Forgetting the transcendental Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff map, one can study the quantization of the algebraic Poisson structures on the moduli space in a purely algebraic way. The quantization of the moduli space of flat connections on a Riemann surface
has been extensively studied, mainly motivated by a relation to the Chern-Simons theory and knot invariants
[
57
,
66
,
68
,
69
]
. The quantization of Poisson structures on certain moduli spaces closely related to the dual Poisson Lie group (i.e., the space of Stokes matrices) was studied in
[
15
,
16
]
, with the help of the Fock-Goncharov coordinates
[
25
]
. In particular, the monodromy matrices are explicitly expressed in terms of the Fock-Goncharov coordinates. Then the quantum monodromy matrices, defined by the same expression provided the Fock-Goncharov coordinates are replaced by their natural quantization and the proper ordering is accounted for (see
[
16
,
17
,
26
]
), are proved to satisfy the quantum R-matrix type relations
[
15
,
16
]
. It is seen as a quantization of the Betti moduli space. To the best of our knowledge, the quantization of the Riemann-Hilbert(-Birkhoff) maps from the Betti to the de Rham moduli spaces, as a (highly transcendental) homomorphism between associative algebras, is missing in the literature. In particular, it is an interesting question to see if the quantum monodromy matrices in
[
15
,
16
]
have a transcendental construction, i.e., can be realized as the monodromy data of certain differential equations, and then to see if they are related to the Stokes matrices of the quantum confluent hypergeometric systems.

On the one hand, the WKB method, named after Wentzel, Kramers, and Brillouin, is for approximating solutions of a differential equation whose highest derivative is multiplied by a small parameter (other
names, including Liouville, Green, and Jeffreys are sometimes attached to this method).

On the other hand, a

{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}

[

Let us take the shift of argument subalgebra

Let us now make the following WKB asymptotic expansion assumption.

Assumption 1
.

For
h\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and

, the action of the off-diagonal entry

of the quantum Stokes matrix on the eigenbasis vectors

(11)

,

is the biggest in the collection

Thus, the WKB approximation of

naturally defines an operator

on the generic elements of

by picking the unique leading term in (

\displaystyle\widetilde{e_{k}}v_{a}(u):=v_{c}(u),\ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ \phi^{(k)}_{ac}(u)={\rm max}\{\phi^{(k)}_{ab}(u)~|~{b\in I}\}.

(12)

Deformation of the classical Stokes matrices: higher order poles and quantum irregular Riemann-Hilbert maps

The Poisson geometric nature of the irregular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, beyond the second order pole case, was developed in
[
10
]
. Let us take a positive integer
k
, and consider the differential equations for a function
f(z)\in{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\frac{df}{dz}=\left(\frac{u}{z^{k+1}}+\frac{A_{k}}{z^{k}}+\cdots+\frac{A_{2}}{z^{2}}+\frac{A_{1}}{z}\right)\cdot f,

(20)

where
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, and
A_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
for all
i=1,...,k
. The differential equation has a pole of order
k+1
at
z=0
.

On the one hand, for fixed
u
, the moduli space of the differential equations (
20
) can be described as follows. Let us take the Lie group
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})
and let
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})={\rm Lie}({\rm GL}_{n}^{(k)})
be its Lie algebra, which contains elements of the form
X(t)=X_{0}+X_{1}t+\cdots+X_{k-1}t^{k-1}
with
A_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Then the elements
A(t)
of the dual
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
will be written as

A(t)=\frac{A_{k}}{t^{k}}+\cdots+\frac{A_{2}}{t^{2}}+\frac{A_{1}}{t}

(21)

via the pairing with
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})
given by
\langle A(t),X(t)\rangle:={\rm Res}_{0}(A,X)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(A_{j},X_{j-1})
. Note that the equation (
20
) is parameterized by such
A(t)
.

On the other hand, in this higher order pole case, the Stokes phenomenon of the equation (
20
) at the irregular singularity
z=0
is characterized by
k
pairs of Stokes matrices, denoted by
\{S_{\pm}^{(j)}(u,A(t))\}_{j=1,...,k}
(with
S_{+}^{(j)}
and
S_{-}^{(l)}
invertible upper and lower triangular and having the same diagonal part for all
j,l=1,...,k
). See e.g.,
[
7
,
10
]
. Let us introduce the space of Stokes matrices

\mathcal{M}^{(k)}:=\{(S_{-}^{(j)},S_{+}^{(j)})_{j=1,...,k}\in(B_{-}\times B_{+})^{k}~|~\delta(S_{\pm}^{(l)})=\delta(S_{-}^{(j)})\ \text{for all }l,j\}.

Now the dual
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
is equipped with the canonical linear Poisson structure induced from the Lie algebra structure, and
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}
is equipped with the Poisson structure inherited from the irregular Atiyah-Bott construction
[
10
]
. Then

Theorem 1.9
.

[
10
]

The irregular Riemann-Hilbert (also known as Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff) map

\nu(u):\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{(k)}~;~A(z)\mapsto\Big(S_{-}^{(1)},S_{+}^{(1)},...,S_{-}^{(k)},S_{+}^{(k)}\Big)

associating the Stokes matrices to the differential equation (
20
), is a locally analytic Poisson isomorphism.

In the case
k=1
, Theorem
1.9
recovers Theorem
1.3
.
Here like in the
k=1
case, it is useful to think that there is only one Poisson structure, i.e., the canonical liner one on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
. The map
\nu(u)
pulls back the entry coordinates on the space of Stokes matrices to the transcendental functions
\{S^{(j)}_{\pm}(u,A(t))_{il}\}
on
A(t)\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
. Theorem
1.9
then states that for fixed
u
, under the new coordinate system
\{S^{(j)}_{\pm}(u,A(t))_{il}\}
around a neighbourhood of
0\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
, the canonical linear Poisson bracket takes an algebraic form (the explicit form was computed in
[
11
]
and
[
41
]
). From this viewpoint, a quantization of the map
\nu(u)
is a lift of the functions
\{S^{(j)}_{\pm}(u,A(t))_{il}\}
on
A(t)\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
to elements in the completion of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})).
Motivated by Theorem
1.1
, let us discuss how to do it in a canonical way.

Remark 1.10
.

In
[
11
]
, an explicit description of the Poisson structure on
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}
was derived in the framework of quasi-Hamiltonian geometry
[
3
]
. In particular, the explicit quasi-Hamlitonian two form on an enlarged (framed) moduli space of Stokes matrices, induced from the Atiyah-Bott construction, was given. After the
{\rm GL}_{n}
-reduction of the global framing, one gets the explicit algebraic Poisson structure on the space
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}
of Stokes matrices. In the meanwhile, the expression of the Poisson/symplectic structure on
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}
was derived in
[
41
]
via a Hamiltonian approach.

In a next work, we will consider a quantum analog of the equation (
20
). Recall that the equation (
20
) was parameterized by the dual
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
equipped with the Lie-Poisson (canonical linear Poisson) bracket, the above discussion in the case
k=1
then implies that the quantum analog should encode the generators of the universal enveloping algebra
U\big({\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})\big)
.
Thus we will consider the equation

\frac{dF}{dz}=h\left(\frac{u}{z^{k+1}}+\frac{T_{[k]}}{z^{k}}+\cdots+\frac{T_{[2]}}{z^{2}}+\frac{T_{[1]}}{z}\right)\cdot F,

(22)

where each
T_{[m]}
is an
n\times n
matrix with entries valued in the universal enveloping algebra
U\big({\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})\big)

\displaystyle(T_{[m]})_{ij}=e_{ij}t^{m-1},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ \ 1\leq m\leq k.

Let us outline in which sense (

) is a quantization of (
20
). First, the equation (
20
) has a formal power series solution

\hat{w}=({\rm Id}+w_{1}z+w_{2}z^{2}+\cdots)\cdot e^{\int^{z}\big(\frac{u}{t^{k+1}}+\frac{d_{k}}{t^{k}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{1}}{t}\big)dt},

where

are diagonal, and for all positive integers

the entries

of the coefficients

are understood as polynomial functions of the parameter
A(t)\in{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})^{*}
given in (

Second, the equation (
22
) has a formal power series solution

\hat{W}=({\rm Id}+W_{1}z+W_{2}z^{2}+\cdots)\cdot e^{\int^{z}\big(\frac{u}{t^{k+1}}+\frac{D_{k}}{t^{k}}+\cdots+\frac{D_{1}}{t}\big)dt},

where
D_{k},...,D_{1}
are diagonal with entries in

Then by considering the quantum equation, we actually get a canonical lift of all the data (the analytic data are encoded in the coefficients of the formal solution) from the Poisson algebra to the quantized algebra, i.e., for all positive integers

we have the lift

\begin{CD}\Big\{w_{m}=(w_{m,ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes Sym\big({\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})\big)\Big\}\\
@V{h\text{ deformation }}V{}V\\

Taking the Borel-Laplace transform (in the completion of

and the

of (

.

In this way, a quantization of the irregular Riemann-Hilbert map in Theorem

is given by the data of quantum Stokes matrices

.
Furthermore, similar to the

When forgetting the transcendental origin from the quantum Stokes matrices, the abstract algebraic relation itself (like the RLL relation in the

\begin{CD}\nu_{h}(u):~\mathcal{A}^{(k)}@>{\text{quantum Stokes matrices}}>{}>U\big(\mathfrak{{gl}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k})\big)\\

2.1
The unique formal fundamental solution of equation (

) in nonresonant case

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, the ordinary differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dz}=h\Big(u+\frac{T}{z}\Big)\cdot F

(23)

has a unique formal fundamental solution taking the form

\displaystyle\widehat{F}(z)=\widehat{H}(z)e^{{huz}}z^{h\delta T},\ \ \ {\it for}\ \widehat{H}=1+H_{1}z^{-1}+H_{2}z^{-2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(24)

where each coefficient
H_{m}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and
\delta T
denotes the diagonal part of
T
, i.e.,
\delta T=\sum_{k}e_{kk}\otimes E_{kk}.

Proof.

Plugging (
24
) into the equation (
23
) gives rise to the equation for
\widehat{H},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\frac{d\hat{H}}{dz}+\hat{H}\cdot\Big(u+\frac{\delta T}{z}\Big)=\Big(u+\frac{T}{z}\Big)\cdot\hat{H}.

(25)

Comparing the coefficients of
z^{-m-1}
, we see that
H_{m}
satisfies

\displaystyle[H_{m+1},u]=(\frac{m}{h}+T)\cdot H_{m}-H_{m}\cdot\delta T.

(26)

Let
\{E_{kl}\}_{1\leq k,l\leq n}
be the standard basis of
{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Then

T=\sum_{k,l}e_{kl}\otimes E_{kl},\ \text{ and }u=\sum_{i}1\otimes u_{i}E_{ii}.

Plugging
H_{m}=\sum_{k,l}H_{m,kl}\otimes E_{kl}
, with
H_{m,kl}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
, into the equation (
26
) gives rise to

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}(u_{l}-u_{k})H_{m+1,kl}\otimes E_{kl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}\frac{m}{h}H_{m,kl}\otimes E_{kl}+\sum_{k,l,j}e_{kj}H_{m,jl}\otimes E_{kl}-\sum_{k,l}H_{m,kl}e_{ll}\otimes E_{kl}.

(27)

Here
e_{kl}^{\prime}s
are understood as elements in
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
via the given representation. That is for
k\neq l

(u_{l}-u_{k})H_{m+1,kl}=\frac{m}{h}H_{m,kl}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{kj}H_{m,jl}-H_{m,kl}e_{ll}\ \in{\rm End}(L(\lambda)),

(28)

and for
k=l
(replacing
m
by
m+1
in (
27
)),

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{j\neq k}e_{kj}H_{m+1,jk}+\frac{m+1}{h}H_{m+1,kk}+[e_{kk},H_{m+1,kk}]\ \in{\rm End}(L(\lambda)).

(29)

Suppose
H_{m}
is given, let us check that the above recursive relation has a unique solution
H_{m+1}
. First note that, since
u_{k}\neq u_{l}
for
k\neq l
, the identity (
28
) uniquely defines the "off-diangonal" part
H_{m+1,kl}
(
k\neq l
) of
H_{m+1}
from
H_{m}
. Furthermore, since
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
, we have
\frac{m+1}{h}{\rm Id}+{\rm ad}_{e_{kk}}
is invertible on
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
for any integer
m+1
. Thus, the condition (
29
) uniquely defines the "diagonal" part
H_{m+1,kk}
of
H_{m+1}
from the off diagonal part.

Remark 2.2
.

The recursive relation (
26
) can be solved explicitly via the quantum minors of the matrix
T
. See Proposition
5.1
.

2.2
The canonical solutions with a prescribed asymptotics

The radius of convergence of the formal power series
\hat{H}(z)
is in general zero. However, it follows from the general principle of differential equations with irregular singularities that (see e.g.,
[
6
,
44
,
48
]
or the proof of Proposition
6.3
) the Borel resummation (Borel-Laplace transform) of
\hat{H}
gives a holomorphic function in each Stokes supersector around
z=\infty
. In this way, one gets actual solutions of (
23
) on each Stokes supersector. These sectors are determined by the irregular term
hu
of the differential equation as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

The anti-Stokes rays of the equation (
23
) are the directions along which
e^{h(u_{i}-u_{j})z}
decays most rapidly as
z\rightarrow\infty
for some
u_{i}\neq u_{j}
.

Equivalently, the anti-Stokes rays
are the directions between pairs of eigenvalues of
hu
, when plotted in the
{z}
-plane. Therefore, there is an even number of such rays.
In this paper we denote a ray by its argument, thus the anti-Stokes rays of (
23
) are
-{\rm arg}(hu_{i}-hu_{j})
for all the possible
i\neq j
.
For any two real numbers
a,b
, an open sector and a closed sector with opening
angle
b-a>0
are respectively denoted by

S(a,b):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}~|~a<{\rm arg}(z)<b\},\hskip 14.22636pt\overline{S(a,b)}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}~|~a\leq{\rm arg}(z)\leq b\}.

Definition 2.4
.

The Stokes supersectors
are the open regions of
\mathbb{C}

{\rm Sect}_{i}:=S\Big(d_{i}-\frac{\pi}{2},d_{i+1}+\frac{\pi}{2}\Big),

bounded by two adjacent rays
d_{i}-\frac{\pi}{2}
and
d_{i+1}+\frac{\pi}{2}
. Here we label the anti-Stokes rays
d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{2l}
in anticlockwise order (the indices are taken modulo
2l
), and use the same letter
d_{i}
to denote the argument of the ray/direction
d_{i}
.

Let us make a choice of branch of
{\rm log}(z)
on
{\rm Sect}_{0}
, and by convention the branch of
{\rm log}(z)
on
{\rm Sect}_{0}
is extended to
the other sectors. Then the following theorem follows from the general principle of differential equations with irregular singularities (see e.g.,
[
6
,
44
,
48
]
or the proof of Proposition
6.3
).

Theorem 2.5
.

For fixed
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, there is a unique (therefore canonical) holomorphic function
H_{i}:{\rm Sect}_{i}\to{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that the function

F_{i}(z):=H_{i}(z)\cdot e^{huz}z^{h{\delta T}}

satisfies the equation (
1
), and moreover

\displaystyle H_{i}(z)\sim\hat{H}(z),\hskip 8.53581pt\text{as }z\rightarrow\infty\text{ within }{\rm Sect}_{i}.

(30)

We stress that, the actual solution of the differential equation, with the prescribed asymptotic expansion as
z\rightarrow\infty

in a sector whose opening is larger than
\pi
, is unique and this fact is important in what follows later.

2.3
Stokes matrices

Definition 2.6
.

For any
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, the Stokes matrices
S_{h\pm}(u)\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of the equation (

) (with respect

) are the unique matrices such that:

•

If

is continued in a positive sense to

, and

•

If
F_{l}

is continued in a positive sense to

then

.

Remark 2.7
.

The definition of the Stokes matrices involves the auxiliary choices of
initial sector
{\rm Sect}_{0}
and a choice of branch of
{\rm log}(z)
on
{\rm Sect}_{0}
, and the formal monodromy
e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}h\delta T}
usually is separated from the Stokes matrices (thus the latter has one’s along the diagonal). Here we take a different convention that the definition of Stokes matrices includes the data of formal monodromy. The advantage is that in our convention the Stokes matrices themselves satisfy the RLL relations. See Section
3
.

It is convenient to think of
S_{h\pm}(u)
as
n\times n
matrices with entries in
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
. Let us introduce the permutation matrix
1\otimes J\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
associated to the choice of
{\rm Sect}_{0}
. Here the
n\times n
matrix
J
is
defined by
J_{ij}=\delta_{\sigma(i)j}
, where
\sigma
is the permutation of
\{1,...,n\}
corresponding to the
dominance ordering of
\{e^{hu_{1}z},...,e^{hu_{n}z}\}
along the direction
\theta
bisecting the sector
{\rm Sect}(d_{1},d_{l})
. That is

\sigma(i)<\sigma(j)
if and only if

as

along

. Then the prescribed asymptotics of
F_{0}(z)
and
F_{l}(z)
at

ensure that

Lemma 2.8
.

The matrices
S_{h\pm}(u)
are triangular, up to conjugating
by the permutation matrix
1\otimes J
. Furthermore, their diagonal elements are

S_{h+}(u)_{kk}^{-1}=S_{h-}(u)_{kk}=\mathrm{e}^{h\pi\mathrm{i}e_{kk}}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda)),\ \ \text{ for }k=1,...,n.

Proof.

By the asymptotics (
30
) of
H_{\pm}(z)
, Definition
2.6
leads to

e^{huz}z^{h\delta T}\cdot\Big(e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}h\delta T}S_{h+}(u)^{-1}\Big)\cdot e^{-huz}z^{-h\delta T}=H_{l}(z)^{-1}H_{0}(z)\rightarrow 1,

as
z\rightarrow\infty\text{ within }{\rm Sect}_{0}\cap{\rm Sect}_{l}.

Let us write
S_{h+}(u)^{-1}=\sum_{ij}(S_{h+}^{-1})_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Since the exponential
e^{huz}
dominate, we must have, for any
i\neq j
,
e^{h(u_{i}-u_{j})z}(S_{h+}^{-1})_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\rightarrow 0.

It implies that
(e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}h\delta T}S_{h+}^{-1})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
unless
e^{zh(u_{i}-u_{j})}\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\infty
within
{\rm Sect}_{0}\cap{\rm Sect}_{l}
. Thus
S_{h+}(u)^{-1}
is triangular, up to the permutation matrix
1\otimes J
. The argument for
S_{h-}
is the same once the change of the branches of
{\rm log}(z)
is accounted for.

For the sake of convenience, in the rest of the paper, we will assume that
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
and the sector
{\rm Sect}_{0}
are chosen such that the corresponding permutation
matrix is the identity. Under this assumption, the Stokes matrices

and

are upper and lower triangular respectively. That is,
S_{h+}(u)_{ij}=0\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
if
i>j
, and
S_{h-}(u)_{ij}=0\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
if
i<j
.

Remark 2.9
.

There is a factorization of the Stokes matrices

as an ordered product of the Stokes factors

for
i=0,1,...,l-1
(up to a multiplication by

). Here each

is determined by

within

. It is interesting to see if the factorization of the Stokes matrices is related to the factorization of
the universal R–matrices of Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum groups
[

,
42
]
.

3
Stokes matrices satisfy the RLL relation

Let us take the standard R-matrix
R\in{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, see e.g.,
[
32
]
[
56
]
,

R=\sum_{i\neq j,i,j=1}^{n}E_{ii}\otimes E_{jj}+e^{\pi\mathrm{i}h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}+(e^{\pi\mathrm{i}h}-e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}h})\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}.

(31)

The following theorem is the main result of this section.
Note that the Stokes matrices
S_{h\pm}(u)
are
n\times n
matrices with entries
S_{h\pm}(u)_{ij}
in
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
, i.e.,
S_{h\pm}(u)=\sum_{i,j}S_{h\pm}(u)_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Let us introduce

S^{(1)}_{h\pm}(u):=\sum_{i,j}S_{h\pm}(u)_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\otimes 1,\ S^{(2)}_{h\pm}(u):=\sum_{i,j}S_{h\pm}(u)_{ij}\otimes 1\otimes E_{ij},\text{ and }\ R^{12}:=1\otimes R

as elements in
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, the Stokes matrices
S_{h\pm}(u)
of (
1
) satisfy

R^{12}S^{(1)}_{h\pm}(u)S^{(2)}_{h\pm}(u)=S^{(2)}_{h\pm}(u)S^{(1)}_{h\pm}(u)R^{12},

(32)

R^{12}S^{(1)}_{+}(u)S^{(2)}_{-}(u)=S^{(2)}_{+}(u)S^{(1)}_{-}(u)R^{12}.

(33)

This theorem is closely related to the result in
[
73
]
. To prove it, we introduce a partial differential equation for a function
Y(z,t)
with two complex variables. We then introduce six holomorphic solutions
Y_{\pm k}(z,t)
of the equation in six different connected regions
D_{\pm k}
for
k=1,2,3
, specified by a common prescribed asymptotics. In the end, we prove that after the analytic continuation of
Y_{+3}
from the domain
D_{+3}
to the domain
D_{-3}
, along two homotopic paths
D_{+3}\rightarrow D_{+2}\rightarrow D_{+1}\rightarrow D_{-3}
and
D_{+3}\rightarrow D_{-1}\rightarrow D_{-2}\rightarrow D_{-3}
, we have respectively

\displaystyle Y_{-1}(z,t)=Y_{+3}(z,t)\cdot S^{(2)}_{h+}(u)S^{(1)}_{h+}(u)R^{(12)},\text{ for }(z,t)\in D_{-3}.

and

\displaystyle Y_{-1}(z,t)=Y_{+3}(z,t)\cdot R^{(12)}S^{(2)}_{h+}(u)S^{(1)}_{h+}(u),\text{ for }(z,t)\in D_{-3}.

It implies
S^{(2)}_{h+}S^{(1)}_{h+}R^{(12)}=R^{(12)}S^{(2)}_{h+}S^{(1)}_{h+}
. Similarly, we obtain the other identities in Theorem
3.1
.

3.1
Solutions of the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations with irregular singularities

Again, let
L(\lambda)
be the finite dimensional highest weight

U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module. Let us consider a system of equations, for an
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
valued function
Y(z_{1},z_{2})
of two complex variables,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\frac{{\partial Y}}{\partial z}=\Big(u^{(1)}+tu^{(2)}+\frac{T^{(1)}+T^{(2)}+P}{z}\Big)\cdot Y,

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\frac{{\partial Y}}{\partial t}=\Big(zu^{(2)}+\frac{T^{(2)}}{t}+\frac{P}{t-1}\Big)\cdot Y.

(35)

Here
h
is a complex parameter,
u={\rm diag}(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
, and

\displaystyle u^{(1)}=\sum_{i}1\otimes u_{i}E_{ii}\otimes 1,\ u^{(2)}=\sum_{i}1\otimes 1\otimes u_{i}E_{ii},

\displaystyle T^{(1)}=\sum_{k,l}e_{kl}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes 1,\ T^{(2)}=\sum_{k,l}e_{kl}\otimes 1\otimes E_{kl},\ P=-\sum_{k,l}1\otimes E_{kl}\otimes E_{lk},

are elements in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. The action of the coefficient matrix of the system on
Y(z_{1},z_{2})
is given by matrix multiplication and the representation of
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The system of equations (
34
) and (
35
) is compatible.

Proof.

It follows from a direct computation and the identities
[T^{(1)}+T^{(2)},P]=0
,
[T^{(1)},T^{(2)}+P]=0
. Here we remark that there is a minus sign in the expression of
P
.

Remark 3.3
.

The system is equivalent to the two variables Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov (KZ) equations with irregular singularities
[
22
]
. Under the assumption that the eigenvalues of
hu
are purely imaginary, the canonical solutions of the KZ equation with prescribed asymptotics was first studied in a formal setting by Toledano Laredo
[
64
]
. Furthermore, the Stokes phenomenon was used to construct a Drinfeld twist killing the KZ associator. The proof in this section is mainly motivated by
[
64
]
.

In the rest of this section, we fix a
u={\rm diag}(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
. For any fixed
t
, the first equation (
34
) becomes a meromorphic ordinary differential equation with an irregular singularity at
z=\infty
. In the following, we first follow the standard way to produce solutions of equation (
34
) with a prescribed asymptotics at
z=\infty
, and then apply the resulting solutions to solve the system of equations (
34
) and (
35
).

3.1.1
The unique formal solution of equation (
34
) in the nonresonant case

For any
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
let us introduce the
u
dependent region in
\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle D_{u}:=\Big\{t\in\mathbb{C}~|~t\neq\frac{u_{i}-u_{j}}{u_{k}-u_{l}},\text{ for any }i,j,k,l\Big\}.

In the following, we will first consider the case
t\in D_{u}
, and then treat the leftover case by means of analytic continuation.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let us assume that
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
. Then for any fixed
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
and
t\in D_{u}
, the ordinary differential equation (
34
) has a unique formal fundamental solution taking the form

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}(z,t)=\widehat{Q}(z;t)e^{hz(u^{(1)}+tu^{(2)})}z^{h(\delta T^{(1)}+\delta T^{(2)}+\delta P)},

(36)

where
\widehat{Q}=1+Q_{1}z^{-1}+Q_{2}z^{-2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot
, and each coefficient
Q_{m}(t)\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Here
\delta
takes the diagonal part, i.e.,

\delta T^{(1)}=\sum_{k}e_{kk}\otimes E_{kk}\otimes 1,\ \ \ \delta T^{(2)}=\sum_{k}e_{kk}\otimes 1\otimes E_{kk},\ \ \ \delta P=-\sum_{k}1\otimes E_{kk}\otimes E_{kk}.

Proof.

Plugging
\hat{Y}
in (
36
) into the equation (
34
) gives rise to the equation for the formal power series
\widehat{Q}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{h}\frac{d\hat{Q}}{dz}+\hat{Q}\cdot\Big(u^{(1)}+tu^{(2)}+\frac{\delta T^{(1)}+\delta T^{(2)}+\delta P}{z}\Big)\\
=&\Big(u^{(1)}+tu^{(2)}+\frac{T^{(1)}+T^{(2)}+P}{z}\Big)\cdot\hat{Q}.\end{split}

(37)

Comparing the coefficients of
z^{-m-1}
in the two sides of (
37
), we get that the recursion relation

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[Q_{m+1}(t),u^{(1)}+tu^{(2)}]\\
=&\Big(m/h+T^{(1)}+T^{(2)}+P\Big)\cdot Q_{m}(t)-Q_{m}(t)\cdot\Big(\delta T^{(1)}+\delta T^{(2)}+\delta P\Big).\end{split}

(38)

Let us write
Q_{m}=\sum_{i,j,k,l}Q_{m,ijkl}\otimes E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}
in terms of the basis
\{E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\}_{1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n}
of
{\rm End}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes 2}
, where each
Q_{m,ijkl}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
. Then a direct computation shows that the relation (
38
) gives rise to

(1)
For
i\neq j
or
k\neq l
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(u_{j}-u_{i}+tu_{l}-tu_{k})Q_{m+1,ijkl}\\
=&\sum_{j^{\prime}}e_{ij^{\prime}}Q_{m,j^{\prime}jkl}+\sum_{l^{\prime}}e_{kl^{\prime}}Q_{m,ijl^{\prime}l}+\delta_{lj}Q_{m,ijkl}-Q_{m,kjil}\\
&-Q_{m,ijkl}e_{jj}-Q_{m,ijkl}e_{ll}+\frac{m}{h}Q_{m,ijkl}.\end{split}

(39)

(2)
For
i=j
and
k=l
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=&\sum_{i^{\prime}\neq i}e_{ii^{\prime}}Q_{m,i^{\prime}ikk}+\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}e_{kk^{\prime}}Q_{m,iik^{\prime}k}+\delta_{ik}Q_{m,ikki}-Q_{m,kiik}\\
&+[e_{ii}+e_{kk},Q_{m,iikk}]+\frac{m}{h}Q_{m,iikk}.\end{split}

(40)

Let us check that the above recursive relation have a unique solution. First, by the assumption
t\in D_{u}
, i.e.,
t\neq\frac{u_{i}-u_{j}}{u_{k}-u_{l}}
for all
i,j,k,l
, the identity (
39
) uniquely defines the "off-diangonal" part
Q_{m+1,ijkl}
(
i\neq j
or
k\neq l
) of
Q_{m+1}
from
H_{m}
. Second, replacing
m
by
m+1
in the identity (
40
) (the solvability condition of the recursion relation), we get a linear system

\displaystyle[Q_{m+1,iikk},e_{ii}+e_{kk}]-(m+1)/h\cdot Q_{m+1,iikk}

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}\neq i}e_{ii^{\prime}}Q_{m+1,i^{\prime}ikk}+\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}e_{kk^{\prime}}Q_{m+1,iik^{\prime}k}+\delta_{ik}Q_{m+1,ikki}-Q_{m+1,kiik}

for the unknown
Q_{m+1,iikk}\in{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
. Note that the off-diagonal part of
Q_{m+1}
on the right hand side of (
41
) has been determined by the identity (
39
).
Since
h\notin\mathbb{Q}
, we have
(m+1)/h{\rm Id}+{\rm ad}_{e_{ii}+e_{kk}}
is invertible on
{\rm End}(L(\lambda))
. That is, the linear system for
Q_{m+1,iikk}
is nondegenerate and has a unique solution. This concludes the proof.

In the rest of this section, let us fix

Similar to Section

Definition 3.5

\displaystyle Q_{d,d^{\prime}}(z;t)\sim\hat{Q}(z;t),\hskip 8.53581pt\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty\text{ within }{\rm Sect}_{d,d^{\prime}}(t).

(42)

There are two kinds of anti-Stokes rays. One is the set of rays that vary with respect

for some index

(independent of

). Note that the fixed rays coincide with the anti-Stokes rays of equation (

3.1.3
Solutions of the system of equations (

) on different domains

Let
\mathcal{R}\in D_{u}
be any region of the complex

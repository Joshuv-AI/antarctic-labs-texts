Yulia Zaitseva

Abstract
. We give a classification of noncommutative algebraic monoid structures on normal affine varieties such that the group of invertible elements of the monoid is connected, solvable, and has a one-dimensional unipotent radical. We describe the set of idempotents and the center of such a monoid and give a criterion for existence of the zero element.

1.
Introduction

An (affine) algebraic monoid is an irreducible (affine) algebraic variety
X
with an associative multiplication

\mu\colon X\times X\to X,\quad(x,y)\mapsto x*y
,
which is a morphism of algebraic varieties and admits a unity
1\in X
such that
{1*x=x*1=x}
for all
x\in X
. The group of invertible elements
G(X)
of an algebraic monoid
X
is an algebraic group, which is Zariski open in
X
. According to
[
19
, Theorem 3]
, every algebraic monoid
X
with an affine group of invertible elements
G(X)
is an affine monoid. For more information on algebraic monoids, see
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
21
]
.

An affine algebraic monoid
X
is called reductive (solvable, commutative) if
G(X)
is a reductive (solvable, commutative) affine algebraic group. The most developed is the theory of reductive monoids, see e.g. the combinatorial classification of reductive monoids in
[
21
,
18
]
.

By the (co)rank of a monoid
X
we mean the (co)dimension of a maximal torus in
G(X)
. Let the ground field
\mathbb{K}
be algebraically closed and of characteristic zero. In the commutative case, the group
G(X)
splits into the direct product of an algebraic torus
(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{r}
and a commutative unipotent group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{s}
, where
\mathbb{G}_{a}=(\mathbb{K},+)
is the additive group of the ground field.

In
[
1
]
, commutative monoids on affine spaces are studied. In particular,
[
1
, Proposition 1]
gives a classification of commutative monoid structures of ranks
0
,
n-1
, and
n
on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
. This implies a classification of commutative monoids on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
and
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, and
[
1
, Theorem 1]
provides a classification on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
. In
[
20
]
, these classifications are extended to non-closed ground fields of characteristic zero.

In
[
10
]
, a classification of commutative monoid structures of rank
0
,
n-1
, and
n
on normal affine varieties is obtained. It turns out that such an affine algebraic variety is toric, and structures of corank one are described by Demazure roots of the variety. The classification is given in terms of comultiplications
\mu^{*}\colon\mathbb{K}[X]\to\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
:

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}(1\otimes\chi^{e}+\chi^{e}\otimes 1)^{\langle p,u\rangle},

where
p
is the primitive vector on a ray of the cone of
X
and
e
is a Demazure root corresponding to this ray, see Section
3
for definitions. In particular, it implies the classification of commutative monoid structures of any rank for normal affine surfaces.

In
[
6
]
, a classification of noncommutative monoids on affine surfaces is given. In this case,
G(X)
is a semidirect product of
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
and
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. It is proved that the surface is toric and the comultiplication has the form

(1)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}}+\chi^{e_{2}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p,u\rangle},

where
e_{1},e_{2}
are two Demazure roots corresponding to the same ray of the cone of
X
with primitive vector
p
.

In this work, we obtain a classification of noncommutative affine monoids of corank one on normal varieties of an arbitrary dimension. This result generalizes classifications in
[
10
]
and
[
6
]
. Let
X
be such a monoid. In Proposition
1
we show that
X
is toric. It was proved earlier in
[
6
]
for affine monoids of dimension 2.
Then we get a classification of monoid structures of corank one in terms of comultiplications and toric geometry in Theorem
1
, see Section
5
for a proof. It turns out that formula (
1
) works in arbitrary dimension. Theorem
2
is the specialization of the classification to affine spaces. In Theorem
3
, we obtain a description of idempotents in
X
. In contrast to the commutative case, the number of idempotents may be infinite under some conditions on Demazure roots
e_{1},e_{2}
and the cone
\sigma
of the toric variety. In Proposition
3
, we study geometry of the subvariety
E(X)
of all idempotents in
X
. In particular, we show that any irreducible component of
E(X)
is either isomorphic to the affine line or is an isolated point. In Proposition
4
, it is proved that the monoid
X
admits the zero element if and only if
\sigma^{\perp}=0
and
-e_{1},-e_{2}\notin\sigma^{\vee}
.
In Proposition
5
, the system of equations defining the center
Z(X)
of
X
is obtained. It follows that
\dim Z(X)=\dim X-2
; moreover, all irreducible components have the maximal dimension if
X
is an affine space; see Corollaries
3
and
4
. Finally, the connection between irreducible components of
E(X)
and the center is given in Proposition
6
.

2.
Semidirect products and the toric structure

Definition 1
.

An (affine) irreducible normal algebraic variety
X
with a morphism
{X\times X\to X,\,(x,y)\mapsto x*y}
, is called an
(affine) algebraic monoid
if
x*(y*z)=(x*y)*z
for all
x,y,z\in X
and there exists a point
1\in X
called
unity
such that
1*x=x*1=x
.

Let
X
be an affine monoid of dimension
n
. Then the group
G(X)
of invertible elements of
X
is a connected affine algebraic group, which is Zariski open in
X
, see
[
18
, Theorem 1]
and
[
19
, Theorem 5]
. Recall that the
rank
and the
corank
of an affine algebraic group
G
are the dimension and the codimension of a maximal torus in
G
, respectively. By the rank and the corank of a monoid
X
we mean the rank and the corank of the group
G(X)
, respectively.

The aim of the present work is to study affine monoids of corank one. In this case,
G(X)
has no semisimple part since any root subgroup in a semisimple group occurs together with an opposite one.
So
G(X)
has a one-dimensional unipotent radical and
G(X)=\mathbb{G}_{a}\leftthreetimes T
, where
\mathbb{G}_{a}=(\mathbb{K},+)
and
T
is a torus of dimension
n-1
.

By definition of a semidirect product, the group operation in
G(X)
is defined by a homomorphism
\chi\colon T\to\Aut(\mathbb{G}_{a})=\mathbb{K}^{\times}
:

(2)

(\alpha,t]\cdot(\alpha^{\prime},t^{\prime}]=(\alpha+\chi(t)\alpha^{\prime},\,tt^{\prime}],

where
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
t,t^{\prime}\in T
.
We denote this group
G_{\chi}
. Thus

G_{\chi}=\mathbb{G}_{a}\leftthreetimes T,

where
T
is an algebraic torus of dimension
n-1
and the multiplication is defined by (
2
).

Notice that according to (
2
) we have
(\alpha,t]=(\alpha,1](0,t]=\alpha\cdot t
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
,
t\in T
. There is also a dual way to write the elements of
G_{\chi}
. Namely, for an element
(\alpha,t]\in G_{\chi}
consider
\beta=\chi^{-1}(t)\alpha\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
. Then
t\cdot\beta=(0,t](\beta,1]=(\chi(t)\beta,t]=(\alpha,t]
. Thus for any element
g\in G_{\chi}
we have two decompositions

g=(\alpha,t]=\alpha\cdot t=[t,\beta)=t\cdot\beta.

The dual multiplication rule is

[t,\beta)\cdot[t^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})=[tt^{\prime},\chi(t^{\prime})^{-1}\beta+\beta^{\prime}).

The group
G_{\chi}
is commutative if and only if
\chi=0
. Let us denote the center of a group
G
by
Z(G)
.

Lemma 1
.

If
\chi\neq 0
, then
Z(G_{\chi})=\{0\}\times\Ker\chi
.

Proof.

Let
(\alpha,t]\in Z(G_{\chi})
, i.e.

(3)

(\alpha+\chi(t)\alpha^{\prime},\,tt^{\prime}]=(\chi(t^{\prime})\alpha+\alpha^{\prime},\,t^{\prime}t]

for any
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
t^{\prime}\in T
.
Since there exists
t^{\prime}\in T
with
\chi(t^{\prime})\neq 1
, condition (
3
) for
\alpha^{\prime}=0,1
implies
\alpha=0
and
\chi(t)=1
. Conversely, for
(\alpha,t]\in\{0\}\times\Ker\chi
equation (
3
) turns into
(\alpha^{\prime},\,tt^{\prime}]=(\alpha^{\prime},\,t^{\prime}t]
.
∎

Definition 2
.

A
group embedding
of an algebraic group
G
is an irreducible algebraic variety
X
with an open embedding
G\hookrightarrow X
such that the action of
G\times G
on
G
by left and right multiplication can be extended to an action of
G\times G
on
X
.

If
X
is a monoid, then
G(X)\hookrightarrow X
is a group embedding. For affine monoids the converse is also true, i.e. if
X
is an affine group embedding of
G
, then the multiplication on
G
extends to a multiplication
X\times X\to X
in such a way that
G
is the group
G(X)
of invertible elements of
X
, see
[
21
, Theorem 1]
for characteristic zero and
[
18
, Proposition 1]
for the general case.

Recall that a normal irreducible algebraic variety is called
toric
if it admits an effective action of an algebraic torus
\mathbb{T}
with an open orbit, see
[
8
,
12
,
14
]
. In other words, a toric variety is a group embedding of an algebraic torus. The main result of this section is the following statement.

Proposition 1
.

Any affine monoid
X
of corank one is a toric variety. Moreover, the group of invertible elements
G(X)
is invariant with respect to the acting torus
\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

If
\chi=0
, then
G_{\chi}
is commutative and the result follows from
[
2
, Theorem 2 and Lemma 2]
.

Let
\chi\neq 0
. First consider the actions of
G_{\chi}
on itself by left and right multiplication. Let a group homomorphism
\theta\colon T\times T\to\Aut(G_{\chi})
be defined by

\theta(t_{1},t_{2})(g)=t_{1}gt_{2}^{-1}.

An element
(t_{1},t_{2})\in T\times T
belongs to the kernel of
\theta
if and only if
(t_{1},t_{2})
belongs to the diagonal of the center
\diag Z(G_{\chi})=\{(g,g)\mid g\in Z(G_{\chi})\}\subseteq G_{\chi}\times G_{\chi}
. By Lemma
1
,
Z(G_{\chi})=\{0\}\times\Ker\chi\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{a}\leftthreetimes T
, so
\Ker\theta=\diag\Ker\chi
.
Since
T\times T
is a torus, the image

\mathbb{T}=\mathop{\text{Im}}\theta\subseteq\Aut(G_{\chi})

is a torus as well. It is isomorphic to
(T\times T)/\diag\Ker\chi
, so the torus
\mathbb{T}
has dimension
\dim\mathbb{T}=(n-1)+(n-1)-(n-2)=n
.

Thus we have an effective action of
\mathbb{T}
on
G_{\chi}
coming from left and right multiplications of
G_{\chi}
on itself. By definition of a group embedding, this action can be extended to the action on
X
, so there exists an effective action of
\mathbb{T}
on
X
. It is known that any effective action of a torus of dimension coinciding with the dimension of a variety has an open orbit, see
[
9
, Corollaire 1, P. 521]
. We have
\dim\mathbb{T}=\dim G_{\chi}=\dim X=n
, so both actions of
\mathbb{T}
on
G_{\chi}
and
X
have an open orbit.
∎

The idea of the following generalization of Proposition
1
was proposed by Sergey Gorchinskiy and Constantin Shramov. Consider a solvable monoid
X
, i.e. a monoid with a solvable group of invertible elements
G(X)
. Then
G(X)=U\leftthreetimes T
, where
T
is a torus and
U
is the unipotent radical of
G(X)
. In some cases it can be proved that
X
is toric as well.

Namely, let the multiplication in a semidirect product
G=U\leftthreetimes T
be given by a homomorphism
\psi\colon T\to\Aut(U)
:

(u,t]\cdot(u^{\prime},t^{\prime}]=(u\cdot\psi(t)(u^{\prime}),tt^{\prime}],\quad\quad u,u^{\prime}\in U,\;\;t,t^{\prime}\in T.

Definition 3
.

This semidirect product is called
active
if
\dim T+\dim\mathop{\text{Im}}\psi=\dim G
or, equivalently,
\dim\mathop{\text{Im}}\psi=\dim U
.

Example 1
.

Any connected affine algebraic group of corank one is solvable, and the semidirect product
G_{\chi}=\mathbb{G}_{a}\leftthreetimes T
is active if and only if
\chi\neq 0
.

Since
U
is a unipotent group, it is isomorphic to a vector space as an algebraic variety. Let us prove that we can choose coordinates in
U
in such a way that
T
acts on
U
linearly.

Lemma 2
.

There are coordinates in
U
such that
\psi(T)\subseteq\GL(U)
.

Proof.

For
G=U\leftthreetimes T
and its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{u}}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}
, consider the conjugation

\psi\colon G\to\Aut(G),\quad\psi(g)(g^{\prime})=gg^{\prime}g^{-1},

and the adjoint representation

\Ad\colon G\to\Aut({\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\Ad(g)=d\psi(g).

Since
U
is normal in
G
, we have restrictions

\psi\colon T\to\Aut(U)\quad\text{and}\quad\Ad\colon T\to\Aut({\mathfrak{u}})\subseteq\GL({\mathfrak{u}}).

It is known that the exponential map
\exp\colon{\mathfrak{u}}\to U
is bijective and commutes with the differential, i.e.

\exp\circ\,\psi(t)=\Ad(t)\circ\exp
for any
t\in T
. So
\exp
takes coordinates on
{\mathfrak{u}}
to
U
in such a way that
\psi(t)\in\GL(U)
for any
t\in T
.
∎

So one may assume that
\psi(t)=\diag(\chi_{1}(t),\ldots,\chi_{k}(t))\in\GL(U)
, where
\chi_{i},\,1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, are characters of the torus
T
. In these terms, the semidirect product is active if and only if
\chi_{i}
are linearly independent.

Example 2
.

Notice that
\dim\mathop{\text{Im}}\psi\leqslant\dim T
, so for an active semidirect product there is a necessary condition
\dim T\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\dim G
. For example, the solvable group
B
of upper triangular
n\times n
matrices is the semidirect product of the group of unitriangular matrices
U
and the torus of diagonal matrices
T
. It is not active for
n>3
since
\dim T=n<\frac{1}{2}\dim G=\frac{n(n+1)}{4}
. For
n=3
we have
\dim T=3=\frac{1}{2}\dim B
, however, the semidirect product is not active as well since
\psi(\diag(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}))
acts on
U
with linear dependent characters
t_{1}t_{2}^{-1},\,t_{1}t_{3}^{-1},\,t_{2}t_{3}^{-1}
.

Example 3
.

The group

G=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&0&\ldots&0&u_{1}\\
0&t_{2}&\ldots&0&u_{2}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ddots&\ldots&\ldots\\
0&0&\ldots&t_{n-1}&u_{n-1}\\
0&0&\ldots&0&t_{n}\end{pmatrix},\,t_{i}\in\mathbb{K}^{\times},u_{j}\in\mathbb{K}\right\}

is an active semidirect product of the group
U
of unitriangular matrices of
G
and the torus of diagonal matrices
T
since
\psi(T)\subseteq\GL(U)
is generated by linearly independent characters
t_{1}t_{n}^{-1},\,\ldots,t_{n-1}t_{n}^{-1}
.

Proposition 2
.

Any solvable affine monoid
X
with active group of invertible elements is a toric variety. Moreover, the group of invertible elements
G(X)
is invariant with respect to the acting torus
\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Following the proof of Proposition
1
, consider the map
\theta\colon T\times T\to\Aut(G)
and the torus
\mathbb{T}=\mathop{\text{Im}}\theta
. An element
(t_{1},t_{2})\in T\times T
belongs to
\Ker\theta
if and only if
t_{1}=t_{2}\in Z(G)
. Notice that
t=(0,t]\in Z(G)
if and only if
t\in\Ker\psi
. Indeed, if
(\psi(t)(u^{\prime}),\,tt^{\prime}]=(u^{\prime},\,t^{\prime}t]
for any
u^{\prime}\in U
,
t\in T
, then
\psi(t)=\id
. So
\Ker\theta=\diag\Ker\psi
.

Since
\dim T+\dim\mathop{\text{Im}}\psi=\dim G
, the torus
\mathbb{T}
has dimension

\dim(T\times T)-\dim\Ker\theta=\dim T+\dim T-(\dim T-\dim\mathop{\text{Im}}\psi)=\dim G=\dim X.

As above, it follows that the action of
\mathbb{T}
has an open orbit on
G(X)
and
X
.
∎

It would be interesting to investigate affine algebraic monoids with an arbitrary active group of invertible elements, and we plan to do it in a further publication. In this article, we concentrate on affine monoids of corank one.

3.
Preliminaries on toric varieties

3.1.
Polyhedral cones of an affine toric variety

Let us recall basic facts on affine toric varieties; see, for example,
[
12
, Section 1.3]
.
Let
N
be a lattice of rank
n
,
N_{\mathbb{Q}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
be the rational vector space of dimension
n
, and
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
be a strongly convex polyhedral cone. Below we give a construction of an affine toric variety
X_{\sigma}
of dimension
n
corresponding to the cone
\sigma
. It is known that all affine toric varieties arise in this way.

Let
M=\Hom(N,\mathbb{Z})
be the dual lattice,
M_{\mathbb{Q}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
be the corresponding rational vector space, and
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle\colon N_{\mathbb{Q}}\times M_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Q}
be the natural pairing. We can consider
M
as the lattice of characters of a torus
\mathbb{T}=\Hom(M,\mathbb{K}^{\times})
of dimension
n
; for a lattice element
u\in M
, let
\chi^{u}\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
be the corresponding character. Then the dual lattice
N
is identified with the lattice of one-parameter subgroups of the torus
\mathbb{T}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}^{\times}
. Consider the polyhedral cone
\sigma^{\vee}
dual to the cone
\sigma
:

\sigma^{\vee}=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\langle v,u\rangle\geqslant 0\;\text{ for all }v\in\sigma\}.

Then
\sigma^{\vee}
is a cone of full dimension,
S_{\sigma}=M\cap\sigma^{\vee}
is a finitely generated semigroup with
\mathbb{Z}S_{\sigma}=M
, and
\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]=\!\bigoplus\limits_{u\in S_{\sigma}}\!\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}
-algebra.
We define
X_{\sigma}
as the spectrum of the algebra
\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]
.

Consider
\mathbb{K}
as a semigroup with respect to multiplication. Any semigroup homomorphism
S_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{K}
defines an algebra homomorphism
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]\to\mathbb{K}
, which corresponds to a point in
X_{\sigma}
. Conversely, any point in
X_{\sigma}
is defined by some semigroup homomorphism
{S_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{K}}
. The torus
\mathbb{T}
can be identified with the subset
X_{0}
of
X_{\sigma}
as the restrictions of group homomorphisms
M\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
to semigroup homomorphisms
S_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{K}
.

In fact,
X_{0}
is open in
X_{\sigma}
, so any character
\chi^{u}
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
can be identified with a rational function on
X_{\sigma}
. We have the decomposition

(4)

\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]=\!\bigoplus\limits_{u\in S_{\sigma}}\!\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}.

Let an element
t
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
act on
\chi^{u}
by multiplication by
\chi^{u}(t)
. This defines the action of
\mathbb{T}
on
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
and hence the action of
\mathbb{T}
on
X_{\sigma}
.

3.2.
Faces and toric open subsets

Recall that a face of a cone is the intersection of the cone with a supporting hyperplane. There is a bijection between faces of the dual cones
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\vee}
: for any
k
-dimensional face
\tau
of the cone
\sigma
the set

\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\langle v,u\rangle=0\;\;\forall v\in\tau\}\cap\sigma^{\vee}

is an
(n-k)
-dimensional face of
\sigma^{\vee}
, and, conversely, for any face
\gamma
of the cone
\sigma^{\vee}
of dimension
k
the set
\gamma^{\perp}\cap\sigma
is a face of
\sigma
of dimension
n-k
. Faces of dimension one are called
rays
, and faces of codimension one are called
facets
.

Any homomorphism of semigroups
S_{\sigma}\to S_{\sigma^{\prime}}
defines a homomorphism
\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]\to\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]
of algebras and a morphism
\Spec\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]\to\Spec\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]
.
In particular, if
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, then
S_{\tau}\supseteq S_{\sigma}
, which determines a morphism
X_{\tau}\to X_{\sigma}
. It can be proved that the morphism
X_{\tau}\to X_{\sigma}
is an open embedding if and only if
\tau
is a face of the cone
\sigma
. Moreover, in this case
S_{\tau}=S_{\sigma}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}(-u^{\prime})
, where
u^{\prime}\in M
and
\tau=\sigma\cap u^{\prime\perp}
, or, equivalently,
u^{\prime}\in M
is from the relative interior of the dual face
\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
of the cone
\sigma^{\vee}
. Then
\mathbb{K}[S_{\tau}]
is the localization of
\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]
at
\chi^{u^{\prime}}
, which determines an embedding
X_{\tau}\subseteq X_{\sigma}
as a principal open subset. In terms of semigroup homomorphisms,
X_{\tau}
consists of points
S_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{K}
such that the image of
u^{\prime}
is nonzero, or, equivalently, the image of any
u^{\prime}\in M
from the relative interior of
\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
is nonzero. In particular, for all faces
\tau
of
\sigma
, the actions of
\mathbb{T}
on
X_{\tau}
defined in subsection
3.1
are compatible and have the open orbit is
X_{0}
.

3.3.
Torus orbits

It is known that
\mathbb{T}
-orbits on
X_{\sigma}
correspond to faces of the cone
\sigma
, see
[
12
, Section 3.1]
. More precisely, for a face
\tau\subseteq\sigma
denote by
x_{\tau}
the distinguished point in
X_{\sigma}
given by the following semigroup homomorphism
S_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{K}
:

u\mapsto\begin{cases}1\;\text{ if }u\in\tau^{\perp},\\
0\;\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

By
O_{\tau}
we denote the
\mathbb{T}
-orbit of the point
x_{\tau}
; it has dimension
n-\dim\tau
. In terms of semigroup homomorphisms it consists of homomorphisms
S_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{K}
such that

u\mapsto\begin{cases}\mathbb{K}^{\times}\;\text{ if }u\in\tau^{\perp},\\
0\;\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is known that all orbits of
\mathbb{T}
on
X_{\sigma}
are of the form
O_{\tau}
for a face
\tau
of
\sigma
. If
\gamma
is a face of
\tau
, then
O_{\tau}\subseteq\overline{O_{\gamma}}
. For more details, see
[
7
, Theorem 3.2.6]
.

3.4.
Locally nilpotent derivations,
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions, and Demazure roots

For an algebra
A
, a linear operator
\delta\colon A\to A
is a
derivation
if it satisfies the Leibniz rule:

\delta(fg)=\delta(f)g+f\delta(g)

for any
f,g\in A
. A derivation
\delta
is called
locally nilpotent
(LND) if for any
f\in A
there exists a number
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\delta^{n}(f)=0
. The exponential map defines a bijection between locally nilpotent derivations on an algebra
A
and rational
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
A
, see
[
11
, Section 1.5]
.
More precisely, an LND
\delta
on
A
defines the
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action
\alpha\cdot f=\exp(\alpha\delta)(f)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
,
f\in A
; conversely, given an action
\xi\colon\mathbb{G}_{a}\times A\to A
,
(\alpha,f)\mapsto\alpha\cdot f
, we can recover the LND
\delta
as
\left(\frac{d\xi}{d\alpha}\right)|_{\alpha=0}
. For an affine algebraic variety
X
,
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
X
are in bijection with
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
\mathbb{K}[X]
, and so with LNDs on the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X]
.

Let
A=\bigoplus\limits_{u\in S}A_{u}
be graded by a semigroup
S
. A derivation
\delta\colon A\to A
is called
homogeneous
if it maps homogeneous elements to homogeneous ones. It follows from the Leibniz rule that, for a domain
A
, a homogeneous derivation has the
degree

\deg\delta\in\mathbb{Z}S
such that
\delta(A_{u})\subseteq A_{u+\deg\delta}
for any
u\in S
.

Let
X_{\sigma}
be an affine toric variety, and let
p_{i}\in N,\;1\leqslant i\leqslant m
, be primitive vectors on the rays of the cone
\sigma
. For any
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
, denote

\mathfrak{R}_{i}=\{e\in M\mid\langle p_{i},e\rangle=-1,\;\langle p_{j},e\rangle\geqslant 0\;\text{ for all }j\neq i,\;1\leqslant j\leqslant m\}.

The elements of the set
\mathfrak{R}=\!\!\!\bigsqcup\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\!\!\!\mathfrak{R}_{i}
are called the
Demazure roots
of the toric variety
X_{\sigma}
. It is easy to see that if
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
,
\gamma
is a face of
\sigma
, and
e\in\gamma^{\perp}
, then the cone generated by
\gamma
and
p_{i}
is a face of
\sigma
as well.

Demazure roots of
X_{\sigma}
are in one-to-one correspondence with nonzero homogeneous LNDs on the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
with respect to grading (
4
) up to proportionality. Homogeneous LNDs on
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
are, in turn, in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
X_{\sigma}
normalized by the acting torus
\mathbb{T}
, see
[
13
, Theorem 2.7]
. A Demazure root

is the degree of the corresponding LND, and the action of the torus
\mathbb{T}
on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
by conjugation is the multiplication by
\chi^{e}
.

4.
Classification results

Let

\mathbb{K}[X]\to\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
. The comultiplication determines the multiplication, so affine monoids can be described in these terms.

Let us formulate the main result.

Theorem 1

Let

is toric, and the comultiplication
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]\to\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]

(5)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\,(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}}+\chi^{e_{2}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p,u\rangle},

as in equation (

),

\sigma

are Demazure roots corresponding to

, and any Demazure roots

corresponding to the same

.

The group of invertible elements is the toric open subset

isomorphic to the group

for
\chi=\chi^{e_{2}-e_{1}}
.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof is divided into four subsections below. The first three subsections contain the proof of the direct implication of the theorem, and the last subsection provides the converse implication.

5.1.
Preliminary results

Let
X
be an affine monoid of corank one.
By Proposition
1
, the variety
X
is toric with respect to the torus
\mathbb{T}
, and the open subset
G_{\chi}\subseteq X
is invariant with respect to
\mathbb{T}
, i.e. is a toric variety as well. Let
X=X_{\sigma}
for a cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
G_{\chi}=X_{\rho}
for a cone
\rho\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
As an algebraic variety, the group
G_{\chi}=\mathbb{G}_{a}\leftthreetimes T
is isomorphic to the direct product of the line
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and the torus
T
.
Then the cone
\rho\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a ray and the dual cone
\rho^{\vee}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a halfspace.

Notice that the inclusion
G_{\chi}=X_{\rho}\hookrightarrow X=X_{\sigma}
restricts to the identity map on the open orbit of the torus action. So it comes from the inclusion of semigroups
S_{\sigma}\subseteq S_{\rho}
and of cones
\rho\hookrightarrow\sigma
as in subsection
3.2
. Since
X_{\rho}\subseteq X_{\sigma}
is an open embedding, the cone
\rho
is a face of the cone
\sigma
, see subsection
3.2
.
So
\rho
is a ray of the cone
\sigma
.

The action of
\mathbb{T}=\mathop{\text{Im}}\theta
on
G_{\chi}
comes from left and right multiplications of
T
on
G_{\chi}
, see Proposition
1
. If we act on a point
(\alpha,t]\in G_{\chi}
by an element
\theta(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{T}
,
t_{1},t_{2}\in T
, we obtain a point

(6)

\theta(t_{1},t_{2})\bigl((\alpha,t]\bigr)=(0,t_{1}](\alpha,t](0,t_{2}^{-1}]=(\chi(t_{1})\alpha,\,t_{1}tt_{2}^{-1}].

Notice that
\chi(t_{1})=\chi(t_{1}^{\prime})
does not depend on a choice of a representative
\theta(t_{1},t_{2})=\theta(t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}
since
\Ker\theta=\diag\Ker\chi
. So
\theta(t_{1},t_{2})\mapsto\chi(t_{1})
is a character of
\mathbb{T}
. It follows that the coordinate function
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
is homogeneous with respect to grading (
4
) corresponding to the action of
\mathbb{T}
. The function
\boldsymbol{\beta}=\chi^{-1}(t)\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
is homogeneous as well.
Multiplying
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
by an appropriate scalar we may assume that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\chi^{a}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=\chi^{b}
for some
a,b\in S_{\rho}
. Notice that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
and
1/\boldsymbol{\alpha}\notin\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
, whence
a\in S_{\rho}
does not belong to the face
\rho^{\perp}
, i.e.
\langle p,a\rangle>0
.

5.2.
Comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]

Let
G
be the group of invertible elements in
X
. Then
G
is open in
X
, the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X]
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{K}[G]
, and the comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[X]
is the restriction of the comultiplication
\mathbb{K}[G]\to\mathbb{K}[G]\otimes\mathbb{K}[G]
corresponding to the multiplication in the group
G
.

Therefore, we are interested in the comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
.

Lemma 3
.

The comultiplication
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]\to\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]\otimes\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
is given by

(7)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\,(1\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-1}\otimes 1)^{\langle p,u\rangle},

where
u\in S_{\rho}
and
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]=\!\bigoplus\limits_{u\in S_{\rho}}\!\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}
.

Proof.

Consider the orbits of the action of the torus
\mathbb{T}
on
X_{\rho}=G_{\chi}=\mathbb{G}_{a}\leftthreetimes T
. The cone
\rho
has two faces
0
and
\rho
, so according to subsection
3.3
there are two orbits
X_{\rho}=O_{0}\cup O_{\rho}
. By formula (
6
), we see that
O_{0}=\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\neq 0\}
is the open orbit and
O_{\rho}=\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}=0\}=T
is its complement.
The torus
\Hom(M(\rho),\mathbb{K}^{\times})
for the sublattice
M(\rho)=\rho^{\perp}\cap M
in
M
is identified with
O_{\rho}
, so
\mathbb{K}[T]=\mathbb{K}[M(\rho)]
.

Since
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]=\mathbb{K}[T][\boldsymbol{\alpha}]
, the semigroup
S_{\rho}
is generated by the lattice
M(\rho)
and
a\in S_{\rho}
, where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\chi^{a}
.
Notice that
\langle p,u_{1}+u_{2}\rangle=\langle p,u_{1}\rangle+\langle p,u_{2}\rangle
and
\chi^{u_{1}+u_{2}}=\chi^{u_{1}}\chi^{u_{2}}
. Then it is sufficient to check formula (
7
) for
u\in M(\rho)
and for
u=a
.

For
u\in M(\rho)
we obtain
\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}
since
\chi^{u}\in\mathbb{K}[T]
are multiplied as characters in
T\subseteq G_{\chi}
.
Notice that for
u\in M(\rho)
formula (
7
) also turns into
\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}
since
\langle p,u\rangle=0
.

By equation (
2
) we also have

\boldsymbol{\alpha}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes 1+\chi(t)\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha}
.
Let us check that it coincides with formula (
7
) for
\chi^{u}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, i.e. for
u=a
. Notice that

\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes 1+\chi(t)\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha}\,(1\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{-1}+\chi(t)\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{-1}\otimes 1),

where
\chi(t)\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-1}
. The exponent
\langle p,a\rangle
is equal to
1
since the sublattice
M(\rho)=\{u\in M\mid\langle p,u\rangle=0\}
and the element
a\in\rho^{\vee}
generate a semigroup
S_{\rho}
in
M
such that
\mathbb{Z}S_{\rho}=M
.
∎

5.3.
Derivations and Demazure roots

By Lemma
3
, the comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
is the restriction of the comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
, see equation (
7
):

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\,(1\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-1}\otimes 1)^{\langle p,u\rangle}.

Denote
e_{1}=-a
and
e_{2}=-b
, where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\chi^{a}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=\chi^{b}
,
a,b\in S_{\rho}
. We have seen in the proof of Lemma
3
that
\langle p,a\rangle=1
, so
\langle p,e_{1}\rangle=-1
. In the same way
\langle p,e_{2}\rangle=-1
.

Consider two
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
G_{\chi}
coming from left and right multiplication by
\mathbb{G}_{a}\subseteq G_{\chi}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{a}\times G_{\chi}\to G_{\chi},

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\cdot(\alpha,\,t]=(\alpha^{\prime}+\alpha,\,t];

\displaystyle G_{\chi}\times\mathbb{G}_{a}\to G_{\chi},

\displaystyle[t,\beta)\cdot\beta^{\prime}=[t,\beta+\beta^{\prime}).

To obtain the corresponding LNDs on
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]=\mathbb{K}[T][\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=\mathbb{K}[T][\boldsymbol{\beta}]
, we differentiate dual actions of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
on
\mathbb{K}[G_{\chi}]
at
\alpha^{\prime}=0
and
\beta^{\prime}=0
:

\delta_{l}=\frac{d}{d\boldsymbol{\alpha}};\quad\delta_{r}=\frac{d}{d\boldsymbol{\beta}}.

Left and right multiplications on
G_{\chi}
can be extended to
X_{\sigma}
. Since the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
is invariant with respect to the action by left and right multiplication, the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
is also invariant with respect to derivations
\delta_{l}
and
\delta_{r}
. Notice that
\delta_{l}
and
\delta_{r}
are homogeneous LNDs on
\mathbb{K}[X]
with respect to the grading
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]=\!\bigoplus\limits_{u\in S_{\sigma}}\!\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}
with degrees
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, respectively, since
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{-1}=\chi^{e_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-1}=\chi^{e_{2}}
. It follows that
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are Demazure roots of
X_{\sigma}
. Since
\langle p,e_{1}\rangle=\langle p,e_{2}\rangle=-1
, they correspond to the ray
\rho
. Thus we proved that the comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
is given by formula (
5
).

5.4.
The inverse implication

First let us provide the proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G
be an affine algebraic group and
G\hookrightarrow X
be an open embedding into an affine algebraic variety
X
. Let
\mu\colon\mathbb{K}[G]\to\mathbb{K}[G]\otimes\mathbb{K}[G]
be the comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[G]
and
\mu(\mathbb{K}[X])\subseteq\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
. Then the restriction of
\mu
to
\mathbb{K}[X]
defines a comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[X]
that corresponds to some monoid structure on
X
.

Proof.

If
\mu(\mathbb{K}[X])\subseteq\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
, then the dual map
X\times X\to X
is a morphism, which restricts to the group multiplication
G\times G\to G
. Since the former is associative and admits a unity, the same holds for
X\times X\to X
.
∎

Now let
X_{\sigma}
be an affine toric variety,
p
be the primitive vector on a ray
\rho
of the cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
e_{1},e_{2}\in M
be Demazure roots corresponding to
p
. First let us prove that formula (
5
) defines a map to
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
.
We have

(8)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\,(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}}+\chi^{e_{2}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p,u\rangle}=\sum\limits_{i+j=\langle p,u\rangle}\begin{pmatrix}\langle p,u\rangle\\
i\end{pmatrix}\chi^{u+ie_{2}}\otimes\chi^{u+je_{1}}.

Notice that, according to the definition of a Demazure root,
\langle p,u+ie_{2}\rangle=\langle p,u\rangle-i\geqslant 0
, and for the primitive vector
p^{\prime}
on any other ray of the cone
\sigma
we have
\langle p^{\prime},u+ie_{2}\rangle=\langle p^{\prime},u\rangle+i\langle p^{\prime},e_{2}\rangle\geqslant 0+0=0
as well. So
\chi^{u+ie_{2}}\in\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
. Similarly,
\chi^{u+je_{1}}\in\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
.

Consider the natural open embedding of the toric variety
X_{\rho}
with the cone
\rho
into
X_{\sigma}
, see subsection
3.2
. Let us show that the unity of the given multiplication is the point
x_{\rho}
, see subsection
3.3
. Recall that by the definition of this point for
u\in S_{\rho}
we have
\chi^{u}(x_{\rho})=1
if
u\in\rho^{\perp}
and
\chi^{u}(x_{\rho})=0
otherwise. Suppose
x\in X_{\sigma}
,
u\in S_{\sigma}
. Then

\chi^{u}(x*x_{\rho})=\sum\limits_{i+j=\langle p,u\rangle}\begin{pmatrix}\langle p,u\rangle\\
i\end{pmatrix}\chi^{u+ie_{2}}(x)\chi^{u+je_{1}}(x_{\rho})=\chi^{u}(x).

Indeed, the last equality holds since
\langle p,u+je_{1}\rangle=0
if and only if
j=\langle p,u\rangle
.

Let us prove that the set of invertible elements in the monoid
X_{\sigma}
is a subset of
X_{\rho}
. Consider a point
x\in X_{\sigma}
, and assume that there exists the inverse
y=x^{-1}\in X_{\sigma}
. Then for any
u\in S_{\sigma}
in the relative interior of the facet
\rho^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
we have
\langle p,u\rangle=0
, so formula (
8
) gives

\chi^{u}(x*y)=\chi^{u}(x)\chi^{u}(y),

that has to equal
1
since

\chi^{u}(x_{\rho})=\begin{cases}1\;\text{ if }u\in\rho^{\perp},\\
0\;\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

So
\chi^{u}(x)\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
if
u
is in the relative interior of the facet
\rho^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
. According to the description of the open subset
X_{\rho}
in subsection
3.2
, we obtain
x\in X_{\rho}
.

Finally, since
e_{1},e_{2}
are Demazure roots with
\langle p,e_{1}\rangle=\langle p,e_{2}\rangle=-1
, it follows that
-e_{1},-e_{2}\in\rho^{\vee}
, so the characters
\boldsymbol{\alpha}:=\chi^{-e_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}:=\chi^{-e_{2}}
are regular functions on
X_{\rho}
.
The restriction of comultiplication (
5
) to the open subset
X_{\rho}
coincides with formula (
7
), so all points in
X_{\rho}
are invertible and
X_{\rho}\cong G_{\chi}
, where
\chi=\boldsymbol{\alpha}/\boldsymbol{\beta}
.
Thus
X_{\sigma}
is a monoid with the group of invertible elements
X_{\rho}
of corank one by Lemma
4
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

6.

The classification of affine monoids of corank one from Theorem

Let us give the classification of

in

, i.e. elements

Theorem 3
.

In the notation of Theorem

, for any face

of the cone

in the orbit

in

is as follows:

(a)

E_{\gamma}=\{x_{\gamma}\}
if

is a ray of

(b)

E_{\gamma}=\varnothing
if
\rho
is not a ray of
\gamma
and
e_{1},e_{2}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
;

(c)

E_{\gamma}=\varnothing
if
\rho
is not a ray of
\gamma

e_{1},e_{2}\in\gamma^{\perp}

(d)

E_{\gamma}=O_{\gamma}\cap\,\{\chi^{u}=1\;\;\forall u\in\cone(\gamma,\rho)^{\perp}\cap S_{\sigma}\}
otherwise.

Remark 2

In this section, we study the connection of the center with the set of idempotents.

Paths in tournaments, a simple proof of Rosenfeld’s Conjecture
Charbel Bou Hanna

Abstract
Rosenfeld Conjectured [ 7 ] in 1972 that there exists an integer K ≥ 8 K\geq 8 such that any tournament of order n ≥ K n\geq K contains any Hamiltonian oriented path. In 2000, Havet and Thomassé [ 5 ] proved this conjecture for any tournament with exactly 3 exceptions. We give a simplified proof of this fact.

Introduction

A tournament
T
is an orientation of a complete graph. The set of vertices of
T
is denoted by
V(T)
and the set of arcs by
E(T)
.
v(T)
will denote the order of
T
,
|V(T)|
. Sometimes, we write
|T|
instead of

.
T
is said to be an

The out-neighbour (resp. in-neighbour) of a vertex

(resp.
N^{-}_{T}(v)

d^{+}_{T}(v)
(resp.
d^{-}_{T}(v)

(resp.
\delta^{-}(T)

(resp.
\Delta^{-}(T)
) the maximal out-degree (resp. in-degree). Note that
\delta^{-}(T)\leq\Delta^{+}(T)
(resp.
\delta^{+}(T)\leq\Delta^{-}(T)
). A tournament
T
is said to be regular if
d^{+}(v)=d^{-}(v)\,\forall\,v\in V(T)
. A cyclic triangle is a circuit of length 3. We denote by
T^{+}_{4}
a tournament composed of a circuit triangle together with a source. A Paley tournament on 7 vertices is a tournament

such that
V(T)=\{v_{i},1\leq i\leq 7\}
and
(v_{i},v_{j})\in E(T)
if and only if
j-i\equiv 1,\,2
or
4(mod\,7)
. We write
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
whenever
T^{\prime}
is a subtournament of
T
. Let
S\subseteq V(T)
, we denote by
T[S]
the subtournament of
T
induced by
S
. If
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
and
S\subseteq V(T)
, we write
T^{\prime}+S=T[V(T^{\prime})\cup S]
and
T^{\prime}-S=T[V(T^{\prime})-S]
. Let
v\in V(T)
,
d^{+}_{S}(v)=|N^{+}_{T}(v)\cap S|
and
d^{-}_{S}(v)=|N^{-}_{T}(v)\cap S|
. A subset
\{x_{1},...,x_{r}\}
in

will be denoted by

.

Let

be an oriented path, set

.
P
is called an
s

x_{1}
and
x_{s}
are its extremities,
x_{1}
is the origin and
x_{s}
is the end. The length of

P
is said to be directed if all of its arcs are oriented in the same direction. A block of
P
is a maximal (for
\subseteq

P
. The path
P
is said to be of type

and we write

, if
P
is composed of
m
successive blocks
B_{1},...,B_{m}
, such that
l(B_{i})=b_{i}
. Moreover, we write
P=P^{+}(b_{1},...,b_{m})
, if
(x_{1},x_{2})\in E(P)
. Else, we write
P=P^{-}(b_{1},...,b_{m})
.
P
is said to be antidirected if each block of

is of length 1.

Note that if
P=x_{1}...x_{s}=P^{+}(b_{1},...,b_{m})
(resp.
P^{-}(b_{1},...,b_{m})
) and

\rightarrow

x_{i}

\rightarrow

f(x_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}

A path

. Let

A strong tournament is such that any two of its vertices can be joined by a directed path. It is known that any tournament
T
is a transitive union of strong subtournaments (called strong components)
I_{1},\,I_{2},...,I_{t}
that is
(v_{i},v_{j})\in E(T)\,\forall\,i<j

[
2
]

Theorem 1
.

T
is strong if and only if
T
contains a Hamiltonian circuit.

As a consequence of the above theorem, if

, then
T
contains a directed path ending at
x

V(P)=V(I_{1})\cup...\cup V(I_{i})
for every

.

The complement of a digraph

In 1971, Grünbaum

proved the following theorem:

Theorem 2

Any tournament contains any Hamiltonian antidirected path with exactly 3 exceptions: a cyclic triangle (

), a regular tournament on 5 vertices (

Set
\mathcal{T}_{3,5,7}=\{T_{3},T_{5},T_{7}\}

contains a copy of a path

is an origin of a copy of
P

[
7
]
, in 1972, inspired by the work of Grünbaum, conjectured that there exists

such that any tournament of order

contains any Hamiltonian oriented path. The case of directed path being Rédei’s theorem
[
6
]
. Alspach, Rosenfeld
[

and Straight
[

]
proved Rosenfeld’s conjecture on paths of 2 blocks. In 1973, Forcade
[

proved Rosenfeld’s conjecture for any tournament of order

[
10
]
was the first one to give a general answer. He proved, in 1986, that there exists

such that for all

any tournament of order

contains any Hamiltonian oriented path. Havet and Thomassé settled the problem by proving that the three exceptions of Grünbaum are the only tournaments not satisfying Rosenfled’s conjecture. Havet and Thomassé’s proof consists of giving a refinement of a key idea introduced by Thomason saying that any set of
b_{1}+1
vertices in an
n
-tournament contains an origin of any
(n-1)
-path whose first block is of length
b_{1}
. They proved that if

, then
x
or
y
is an origin of a copy of such path where

such that

can be reached from

or

by a directed path
\}|
. This new performance allowed them to remark that proving the existence of an
(n-1)
-oriented path in any
n
-tournament

is equivalent to the existence of any Hamiltonian path
P
in this tournament unless
(T,P)
is one of the 69 exceptions that were verified one by one. In this paper, we give a simple proof of the result without treating all these exceptions.

The main result

In our proof, we are going to use the following theorem due to El-sahili and Ghazo-Hanna
[
8
]

Theorem 3
.

A path lies in a tournament
T
if and only if it lies in
\overline{T}
.

The following Lemmas will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 1

Let

|\{v\in T\,/\,T-v\in\mathcal{T}_{3,5,7}\}|\leq 2

Proof.

Suppose that

,

N^{-}_{T-v_{3}}(v_{1})=N^{-}_{T-v_{1}}(v_{3})

, but
v_{2}\in N^{+}_{T-v_{3}}(v_{1})
and
v_{2}\notin N^{+}_{T-v_{1}}(v_{3})

∎

We may deduce from this Lemma more precise conclusions. Indeed, if

, then
\forall\,x\in T

(Simple Lemma) Let

contains a copy of

.

2.

Any of the vertices of

P
if
T-v\in\mathcal{T}_{3,5,7}
.

Proof.

1.

Let

, then

If

, then we may suppose that a copy of

may be found in

starting at any one of the vertices of

.

∎

By analogy, the above lemma is valid if

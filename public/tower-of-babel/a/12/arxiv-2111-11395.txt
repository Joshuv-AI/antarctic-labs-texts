1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
. The celebrated theorem of Mordell-Weil asserts that the set of
K
-rational points
E(K)
is a finitely generated abelian group. Let
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
denote the torsion subgroup of
E(K)
. Mazur
[
Maz78
]
first completed the classification of all possible torsion subgroups when
K=\mathbb{Q},
which are

(1)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},&1\leq n\leq 12,\ n\neq 11\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/{2n}\mathbb{Z},&1\leq n\leq 4\end{array}

Subsequently, Kamienny
[
Kam92
]
and Kenku-Momose
[
KM88
]
determined the complete list of all the groups arising as
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
where
E
is an elliptic curve over any quadratic number field
K
. These groups are:

(2)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},&1\leq n\leq 18,\ n\neq 17\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/{2n}\mathbb{Z},&1\leq n\leq 6\\
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/{3n}\mathbb{Z},&1\leq n\leq 2\\
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}&.\end{array}

Over cubic fields, the full description of torsion subgroups has been settled only last year
[
DEvH+21
]
. Over quartic fields, while we still do not have a complete classification, analogous to (
1
), we know due to
[
JKP06
]
that what torsion structures are infinitely many times occurring up to isomorphism of elliptic curves. Also,
17
is the largest prime dividing the torsion subgroup over any quartic field by
[
DKSS17
]
.
As a first step toward this direction, we ask “given an elliptic curve
E
over any quadratic number field
K
, if
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
has full 2-torsion, then how does
E(L)_{\text{tor}}
relate with
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
where
[L:K]=2
?”. If
E(K)_{\text{tor}}\subsetneq E(L)_{\text{tor}},
we say that torsion grows.

This leads us to a more fundamental question: whether there is a good bound for the growth. Kwon
[
Kwo97
]
showed that for an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q},
the growth of
E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tor}}
upon quadratic base change, is controlled by the torsion structure realizing on a quadratic twist of the curve
E,
which can be generalized to quadratic number fields which allows us to bound
E(L)_{\text{tor}}/E(K)_{\text{tor}}.
(See Propostion
2.1
below.) In this note, we answer the main objection above for the set
\mathcal{S}
of all the non-cyclotomic imaginary quadratic fields of class number 1, that is

\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-19}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-43}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-67}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-163})\}.
We choose to work over the base fields in
\mathcal{S}
as these are the remaining imaginary quadratic fields of class number 1 for which the classification of torsion structures is known,
[
SS18
]
. The method used in their paper is outlined by Kamienny and Najman
[
KN12
]
. In case
E(K)[2]\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},
we generalize both Kwon’s work in which
K=\mathbb{Q},
and Newman’s work
[
New17
]
in which
K\in\{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}\}.

This note will also complete the author’s earlier work
[
Bal21
]
in which for given
K\in\mathcal{S}
and an elliptic curve
E/K
with trivial
2
-torsion, the growth of odd torsion subgroup
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
is classified upon quadratic base change.

Our methods didn’t explicitly rely upon the facts that
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is UFD and has a finite unit group whereas Kwon’s methods do. Instead, we generalized Newman’s methods more efficiently without using Grobner basis, (see Lemma
3
) as well as used new techniques ranging from the Galois action to the analysis of cyclic isogenies (see Theorem
3.1
),
thus finishing the classification of quadratic torsion growth for the set of all imaginary quadratic number fields of class number 1.

d

\Phi_{K}(d):=\{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}:\exists\ E/K:E(L)_{\text{tor}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\ \text{for}\ [L:K]=d\}

and for a fixed

\displaystyle\Phi_{K}(d,G):=\{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}:\exists\ E/K:E(K)_{\text{tor}}\simeq G,E(L)_{\text{tor}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},\ [L:K]=d\}.

We now give a more detailed overview of the paper. In section 2, we present a number of known results that narrow our focus. For example, an elliptic curve with full
2
-torsion admits a Weierstrass model of the form
y^{2}=x(x+\alpha)(x+\beta)
. Moreover, the existence of points of order
4
,
8
or
3
is then equivalent to finding solutions to certain equations involving
\alpha
and
\beta
. (See Theorem
2.2
). These characterizations will be later used to eliminate torsion points upon base change.

In section 3, we develop a result that is used in section 4. Specifically, we classify certain cyclic
N
-isogenies of elliptic curves (up to isomorphism) defined over any non-cyclotomic quadratic imaginary field of class number 1. The values for
N
of interest are
N=30,40,48.
For a given
N,
the set of
K
-rational points on the modular curve
X_{0}(N)
parametrizes the isomorphism classes of pairs
(E,C)
where
E
is an elliptic curve defined over
K
and
C\subseteq E(\overline{K})
is a Gal
(\overline{K}/K)
-invariant cyclic subgroup of order
N.
If
X_{0}(N)
has genus at least 2, then there are finitely many
K
-rational points, by a renowned result of Faltings. In the cases
N=30,40,48
,
X_{0}(N)
is a hyperelliptic curve with genus 3. The main trick for studying quadratic points on such a curve
X_{0}(N)
is to instead study the rational points on its symmetric square
X_{0}(N)^{(2)}.
This comes down to finding the rational points on the Jacobian
J_{0}(N)(\mathbb{Q})
of
X_{0}(N).
In the light of the fact that
J_{0}(N)(\mathbb{Q})
has rank 0 for the aforementioned values of
N,
Bruin and Najman
[
BN15
]
classified all exceptional quadratic points on
X_{0}(N).
We complemented their work and found that there are no non-exceptional
K
-rational points lying on these curves for any
K
in
\mathcal{S}.

In section 4, we prove the most important ingredient for Theorem
1.1
which is the description of
T_{K}(G)
, the set of finite abelian groups
H
(up to isomorphism) such that there is an elliptic curve
E/K
with
E(K)_{\text{tor}}\simeq G
and
E^{d}(K)\simeq H
for
d\in K.
We study the torsion structures on quadratic twists because if
L=K(\sqrt{d})
and
E/K
is an elliptic curve then
E^{d}(K)_{\text{tor}}
sets a bound for
E(L)_{\text{tor}}
(see Proposition
2.1
). If one wants to study the relation of
E(L)_{\text{tor}}
with
E(K)_{\text{tor}},
the first task is to classify which groups occur as
E^{d}(K)_{\text{tor}}.
To do so, we make use of the isogeny analysis from section 3 and the equivalent characterization of elliptic curves with the prescribed torsion
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/{2n}\mathbb{Z}\ (1\leq n\leq 6)
. Twisting and equating parametrized models of curves give rise to systems of Diophantine equations to solve.

In section 5, we prove our main result Theorem
1.1
. With the classification of twists in hand, we can have a finite list of possible torsion structures arising as
E(L)_{\text{tor}}.
Furthermore, the growth occurs over
L
if and only if there exists
P\in E(K)_{\text{tor}}
with
P\in 2E(L)_{\text{tor}}\backslash\ 2E(K)_{\text{tor}}.
The criteria of the existence of such a point is given in Lemma
1
.
Applying this criteria yields systems of Diophantine equations as in the previous section. The difficulty arises when we deal with torsion points of high order such as 10 or 12. In order to work around this problem, we shifted our perspective and used the galois action on
n
-torsion
E[n].

A natural question arises when analyzing the growth of torsion in a quadratic extension is whether there is an upper bound for the growth. In
[
Kwo97
, Proposition 1]
Kwon answered this question for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}.
We state the generalization of his result as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
Kwo97
]
)
Let K be a quadratic number field,
L=K(\sqrt{d})
a quadratic extension of K and let
\sigma
denote the generator of
Gal(L/K)
. The map

E(L)_{\text{tor}}\xrightarrow{h}E^{d}(K)_{\text{tor}}

P\mapsto P-\sigma(P)

is a homomorphism with kernel
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
and hence induces an injection

E(L)_{\text{tor}}/E(K)_{\text{tor}}\hookrightarrow E^{d}(K)_{\text{tor}}.

The next lemmas play a key role in studying the 2-part torsion of elliptic curves. They will be repeatedly used in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 1
.

(
[
Kna92
]
)
Let E be an elliptic curve over a field k with
char(k)\neq 2,3.
Suppose E is given by

y^{2}=(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)(x-\gamma)

with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in k.
For
P=(x,y)
in
E(k)
, there exists a k-rational point
Q
on E such that 2
Q=P
if and only if
x-\alpha,x-\beta
, and
x-\gamma
are all squares in k.

In this case if we fix the sign of the square roots of
x-\alpha,x-\beta,x-\gamma,
then the
x
-coordinate of
Q
equals either

\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\beta}\pm\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\gamma}\pm\sqrt{x-\beta}\sqrt{x-\gamma}+x

or

-\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\beta}\pm\sqrt{x-\alpha}\sqrt{x-\gamma}\mp\sqrt{x-\beta}\sqrt{x-\gamma}+x

If the criteria of the statement above is satisfied for a point
P,
we say that
P
is halved. The next result is a variant of the result of Ono, see
[
Ono96
]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
New17
]
)
Let
K
be a number field and
E/K
be an elliptic curve with full 2-torsion. Then
E
has a model of the form
y^{2}=x(x+\alpha)(x+\beta)
where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}.

(1)

E(K)
has a point of order 4 iff
\alpha,\beta
are both squares, or
-\alpha,\beta-\alpha
are both squares, or
-\beta,\alpha-\beta
are both squares in
\mathcal{O}_{K}.

(2)

E(K)
has a point of order 8 iff there exists a
z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
z\neq 0
and a pythagorean triple
(u,v,w)
such that

\alpha=z^{2}u^{4},\beta=z^{2}v^{4}

or we can replace
\alpha,\beta
by
-\alpha,\beta-\alpha
or
-\beta,\alpha-\beta
as in the first case.

(3)

E(K)
contains a point of order 3 iff there exists
a,b,z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
with

and
\frac{a}{b}\notin\{-2,-1,-\frac{1}{2},0,1\}
such that
\alpha=a^{3}(a+2b)z^{2}
and
\beta=b^{3}(b+2a)z^{2}.

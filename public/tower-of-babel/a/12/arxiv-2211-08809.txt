Resolvent of vector fields and Lefschetz numbers
Yann Chaubet and Yannick Guedes Bonthonneau

Abstract
. We study the wavefront set of resolvent of arbitrary flows in their region of convergence, to obtain a general formula for their intersection with currents. We provide an application to the topology of surfaces.



Given a chaotic flow
(\varphi_{t})
acting on a closed manifold
M
, the distribution of periodic points, given as the solutions
(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times M
of the equation

\varphi_{t}(z)=z

(1)

have been extensively studied in the literature. To study the periodic orbits, Smale
[
Sma67
]
suggested to introduce dynamical series or zeta functions involving their travel time; those series have proved to be very effective and have given rise to many important works, starting from the seminal paper of Ruelle
[
Rue76
]
. The last decade has seen a series of breakthrough in this topic, in particular with
[
GLP13
]
,
[
DZ16
]
,
[
FT17
]
and
[
DG18
]
.

The modern approach for studying the analytic properties of a dynamical series consists in rewriting it as a distributional pairing or trace involving the resolvent

(s-\mathcal{L}_{X})^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ts}\varphi_{-t}^{*}\mathrm{d}t\ :\ \Omega^{\bullet}(M)\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime\bullet}(M)

of the flow. Here
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
is the space of smooth differential forms on
M
while
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\bullet}(M)
is its dual topological space,
s
is a complex parameter with large real part,
\varphi_{t}^{*}
is the pull-back by the flow and
\mathcal{L}_{X}
is the Lie derivative in the direction of the generator
X=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\varphi_{t}\right|_{t=0}
of
(\varphi_{t})
. However, establishing such a relation requires some justification, and authors often deal with this crucial step in an ad-hoc fashion (see §
1
below). For this reason, we propose in this paper to gather and generalize these statements by providing a formula which expresses dynamical series of a general form in terms of the resolvent of any flow (not necessarily chaotic), see Theorem
3
below. Before stating our results in their most precise form in §
1
, we first present an application about topology of surfaces.

It is somewhat natural to ask what happens if we replace (
1
) by the equation

\varphi_{t}(z)=F(z)

(2)

where
F:M\to M
is a smooth map. While this question can be asked in a very general context, we will focuse on geodesic flows of surfaces. To be more precise, let
\Sigma
be a closed oriented Riemannian surface whose geodesic flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, acting on the unit tangent bundle
M=S\Sigma
, has the Anosov property. The geodesic vector field
X
is the Reeb vector field of the Liouville contact one-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. We denote by
\pi:S\Sigma\to\Sigma
the natural projection and by
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
the Hamiltonian bundle lift of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
to
T^{\ast}S\Sigma
.

Next, let

be a diffeomorphism and
F:S\Sigma\to S\Sigma
be a bundle map over

, in the sense that
\pi\circ F=f\circ\pi
. We will say that

is

to
(\varphi_{t})

N^{*}\mathrm{G}_{F}\cap\Gamma(\varphi)^{\prime}=\{0\}.

(3)

Here

denotes the conormal bundle of the graph
\mathrm{G}_{F}=\{(F(z),z))~|~z\in M\}
of

, while
\Gamma(\varphi)=\Delta(T^{*}M)\cup\overline{\Omega_{+}}
, where

is the diagonal in

and

\Omega_{+}=\bigl\{(\Phi_{t}(z,\xi),(z,\xi))\ |\ (z,\xi)\in T^{*}M,~\langle\xi,X(z)\rangle=0,~t\geqslant 0\bigr\}.

Finally, we denoted
A^{\prime}=\{((y,\eta),(x,-\xi))\ |\ ((y,\eta),(x,\xi))\in A\}
for each

.

Under the condition (
3

Z_{F}=\bigl\{(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\ |\ \varphi_{t}(z)=F(z)\bigr\},

which is the set of points satisfying (
2

1

\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f
and
F
be as above. For
\Re s\gg 1
the integral

\eta(s)=\int_{Z_{F}}e^{-ts}\pi_{M}^{*}\alpha

(4)

converges, where

has a meromorphic continuation to

\lim_{s\to 0}s\eta(s)=\Lambda_{f}+\int_{S\Sigma}(\iota_{X}F_{\ast}\alpha-1)\mathrm{d}\mu,

1.
The wave front set of the resolvent

In this section we assume that
X
a smooth vector field on a closed and oriented manifold
M
of dimension
n
. Denote by
\Omega^{k}(M)
the space of smooth differential
k
-forms and by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime n-k}(M)
the space of
(n-k)
-currents, that is, the topological dual space of
\Omega^{k}(M)
. Then we have
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)=\oplus_{k}\Omega^{k}(M)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\bullet}(M)=\oplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{\prime k}(M)
. For any operator

A:\Omega^{\bullet}(M)\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime\bullet}(M)

preserving the degree, its Schwartz kernel
\mathrm{K}_{A}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime n}(M\times M)
is defined by

\int_{M}(Av)\wedge w=\int_{M\times M}\mathrm{K}_{A}\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}v\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}w,\quad v,w\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M),

where
\pi_{j}:M\times M\to M
is the projection over the first and second factors, respectively.
We can now state our main result

Theorem 2
.

Let
J(t)
be the Schwartz kernel of
e^{tX}=\varphi_{t}^{*}
and
\mathrm{K}(s)
that of
(s-\mathcal{L}_{X})^{-1}
. Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime k}(M\times M)
such that

\WF^{\prime}(u)\cap\Gamma(\varphi)=\emptyset.

Then the product
\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge u
is well defined and for any
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
which is equal to
1
near
0
, we have, for
\Re s
large,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\chi(t/\varepsilon)e^{-ts}J(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)\wedge u=\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge u,

where the convergence holds in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime n+k}(M\times M).
Moreover, the convergence is uniform as long as the wavefront set of
u
lies in a fixed compact subset not intersecting
\Gamma(\varphi)
, with uniform estimates.

Before turning to the proof, let us discuss the main application of this statement. We define the left flow
(\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}_{t})
acting on
M\times M
by

\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}_{t}(x,y)=(\varphi_{t}(x),x),\quad x,y\in M,

denote by
X^{\mathrm{L}}
its generator and set

G_{\varphi}=\{(t,\varphi_{t}(x),x)\ |\ t\geqslant 0,\ x\in M\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times M\times M.

Finally, if
P\subset M_{1}
is an oriented submanifold of a manifold
M_{1}
, we will denote by
[P]\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\bullet}(M_{1})
the associated integration current, which is defined by

\langle[P],\omega\rangle=\int_{P}\iota_{P}^{*}\,\omega,\quad\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M_{1})

where
\iota_{P}:P\hookrightarrow M_{1}
is the inclusion.

The following result allows to express general dynamical series in terms of the resolvent.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
{\mathrm{Q}}\subset M\times M
is a submanifold of dimension
d\geqslant n
, and that its conormal
N^{\ast}({\mathrm{Q}})
intersects
\Gamma(\varphi)^{\prime}
trivially. Then the following holds.

\mathrm{(i)}

Let
\tau:\mathbb{R}\times M^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\pi:\mathbb{R}\times M^{2}\to M^{2}
be the projections over the first and second factor, respectively. Then,
\pi^{-1}({\mathrm{Q}})
intersects
G_{\varphi}
transversally, so that
G_{\varphi}\cap\pi^{-1}({\mathrm{Q}})
is a smooth submanifold of
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M^{2}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

For large
\Re s
, the wedge product
\iota_{X^{\mathrm{L}}}\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge[{\mathrm{Q}}]
is well defined, the current
e^{-s\tau}\left[G_{\varphi}\cap\pi^{-1}({\mathrm{Q}})\right]
lies in the dual of
\pi^{*}\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
, and we have

\iota_{X^{\mathrm{L}}}\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge[{\mathrm{Q}}]=\pi_{*}\left(e^{-s\tau}\left[G_{\varphi}\cap\pi^{-1}({\mathrm{Q}})\right]\right),

where the equality holds in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime 3n-d-1}(M\times M).

Let us briefly explain how Theorem
3
can be used to recover some known formulae in two important situations.

First, for
\varepsilon>0
, let
{\mathrm{Q}}=\Delta_{-\varepsilon}=\{(\varphi_{-\varepsilon}(x),x)~:~x\in M\}
. Assume that
N^{*}{\mathrm{Q}}\cap\Gamma(\varphi)=\{0\}
, which is always satisfied for example if
(\varphi_{t})
is Anosov and
\varepsilon
is small enough. Then

(
ii
)

yields that

\iota_{X^{\mathrm{L}}}\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge[\Delta_{-\varepsilon}]=e^{\varepsilon s}\sum_{\gamma}e^{-s\tau(\gamma)}[\Lambda_{\gamma}^{\varepsilon}],

where the sum runs over all periodic orbits of the flow, and

\Lambda_{\gamma}^{\varepsilon}=\{(\varphi_{t-\varepsilon}(x),\varphi_{t}(x))\ |\ t\in[0,\tau^{\sharp}(\gamma)]\}\subset M\times M.

Here
\tau^{\sharp}(\gamma)
is the primitive period of
\gamma
and
x
is any point in the image of
\gamma
. In particular, if
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(M\times M)
is any smooth one form such that
\iota_{X\times X}\omega=1
, then we get

\langle\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge\iota_{X^{\mathrm{R}}}[\Delta_{-\varepsilon}],\omega\rangle=e^{\varepsilon s}\sum_{\gamma}\tau^{\sharp}(\gamma)e^{-s\tau(\gamma)}.

(1.1)

The above series is, up to the term

, the logarithmic derivative of the so-called Ruelle zeta function, which was shown to admit a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
in
[

]
, and later in
[

]
with semi-classical methods. We emphasize that the convergence of the sum in the right-hand side of (

[

Next, assume that
{\mathrm{Q}}={\mathrm{Q}}_{1}\times{\mathrm{Q}}_{2}

\dim{\mathrm{Q}}_{1}+\dim{\mathrm{Q}}_{2}+1=n
, where
{\mathrm{Q}}_{j}

(

)

\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge\iota_{X^{\mathrm{R}}}[{\mathrm{Q}}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\geqslant 0\\
(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathrm{Q}}_{1}\times{\mathrm{Q}}_{2}\\

Again, we get

\langle\iota_{X^{\mathrm{L}}}\mathrm{K}(s)\wedge[{\mathrm{Q}}],1\rangle=\sum_{\gamma}e^{-s\tau(\gamma)},

(1.2)

where the sum runs over all dynamical arcs

{\mathrm{Q}}_{1}
and

2.
Egorov for flows (semi-classical version)

That the conjugation of a pseudo-differential operator by a (smooth) flow still is pseudo-differential follows from the invariance of the class of pseudo-differential operators under the action of diffeomorphisms. The most usual proof of this fact relies on the so-called Kuranishi trick. While this remains true when the time is large, it is only a qualitative statement. In this section, we will give a quantitative result.

For
X
a smooth vector field on a compact manifold
M
with flow
\varphi_{t}
, we denote by
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)
is maximal Lyapunov exponent, i.e

\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\sup_{z}\max(\|\mathrm{d}\varphi_{t}(z)\|,\|\mathrm{d}\varphi_{-t}(z)\|).

We will need to quantify the action on
L^{2}
, Setting

\mu_{\mathrm{max}}(X)=\limsup_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|e^{tX}\|_{L^{2}}.

Also we define
\Phi=(\Phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
the Hamiltonian lift of
(\varphi_{t})
, acting on
T^{*}M
, which is defined by

\Phi_{t}(z,\xi)=\mathrm{d}\varphi_{t}(z)^{-\top}\xi,\quad(z,\xi)\in T^{*}M,

where
-⊤
denotes the inverse transpose. Let us introduce some usual classes of pseudo-differential operators. The ones whose symbols satisfy
S^{m}
estimates in local charts

|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a|\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\beta|},

form the algebra
\Psi^{0}
. When working with
h
-pseudo-differential operators, for
0\leqslant\delta<1/2
, we will write
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\delta,\,\mathrm{sc}}
if its symbol satisfies the
S^{m}_{\delta}
estimates

|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a|\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta}h^{-\delta(|\alpha|+|\beta|)}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\beta|}.

We can now state our result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
X
be a smooth vector field, and
A\in\Psi^{0}
with principal symbol
a\in S^{0}
. Let
\lambda>\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)
and
\mu>\mu_{\mathrm{max}}(X)
. Given
0\leqslant\delta<1/2
, for
h>0
and
|t|\leqslant\delta|\log h|/\lambda
, we can find
A_{t}
and
R_{t}
such that

e^{tX}Ae^{-tX}=A_{t}+R_{t},

where
R_{t}
is smoothing, with estimates
\|R_{t}\|_{H^{-N}\to H^{N}}=\mathcal{O}(h^{-3N-\mu/\lambda})
, and
A_{t}\in\Psi^{0}_{\delta,\mathrm{sc}}
is a
h
-pseudo-differential operator with principal symbol satisfying
a_{t}=a\circ\Phi_{t}\mod\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}
, and
\WF(A_{t})=\Phi_{-t}(\WF(A))
.

Proof.

We start by choosing a Weyl quantization procedure
\Op
— as in §14 of
[
Zwo12
]
— and write
A=\Op(a)+R_{0}
,
R_{0}
being a
C^{\infty}

h>0
and a cutoff, writing
a=a\chi+a(1-\chi)
, so that
a\chi
is supported in
|\xi|\leqslant 1/h
, and
a(1-\chi)
in
|\xi|>1/2h
.

By rescaling, we observe that
\Op(a(1-\chi))\in\Psi^{0}_{0,\,\mathrm{sc}}
of the form
\Op_{h}(b)
with

b(z,\xi)=[a(1-\chi)](z,\xi/h).

Using the strategy of
[
BR02
]
, we then obtain that as long as
0\leqslant\delta<1/2
and
|t|\leqslant\delta|\log h|/\lambda
, we can find a symbol
b_{t}
satisfying
S_{\delta}^{0}
estimates and such that

e^{tX}\Op_{h}(b)e^{-tX}=\Op_{h}(b_{t})+\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{smoothing}}(h^{\infty}).

We will not detail the arguments of
[
BR02
]

[
DJN21
]
. The two main ingredients in the proof are the following

•

For the range of times allowed,
b\circ\Phi_{t}\in S_{\delta}^{0}
uniformly.

•

We have a product formula, for
c\in S^{m}_{\delta}
:

[X,\Op_{h}(c)]=\frac{h}{i}\Op_{h}(\{p,c\})+\mathcal{O}(h^{2-2\delta}\Psi^{m-2}_{\delta,\mathrm{sc}}).

It is a feature of the proof that

b_{t}=b\circ\Phi_{t}+\mathcal{O}(h^{1-\delta}S^{-1}_{\delta}),

and
b_{t}
shares the support of
b\circ\Phi_{t}
.

Now,
\Op(a\chi)
is
not
semi-classical, so that there is not much microlocal information we can find on
e^{tX}\Op(a\chi)e^{-tX}
for

H^{s}\to H^{r}
norms. Letting

R_{0}^{\prime}=R_{0}+\Op(a\chi),

we get
\|R_{0}^{\prime}\|_{H^{s}\to H^{r}}=\mathcal{O}(h^{s-r})
as
h\to 0
when
r\geqslant s
. Then in the range
|t|\leqslant\delta|\log h|/\lambda
, we find

\displaystyle\|e^{tX}R_{0}^{\prime}e^{-tX}\|_{H^{-N}\to H^{N}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{N}h^{-2N}\|e^{-tX}\|_{H^{N}}\|e^{tX}\|_{H^{-N}}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ch^{-2N}e^{2N\lambda|t|+2\mu|t|}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ch^{-2N(1+\delta)-2\mu\delta/\lambda}\leqslant Ch^{-3N-\mu/\lambda}.

Hence by setting

A_{t}=\Op_{h}(b_{t})\quad\text{and}\quad R_{t}=e^{tX}R_{0}^{\prime}e^{-tX}+e^{tX}\Op_{h}(b)e^{-tX}-A_{t},

the proof is complete.
∎

3.
Proof of main theorem

Using a microlocal partition of unity, we see that Theorem
2
will be a consequence of the following result.

Lemma 5
.

Let
A,B\in\Psi^{0}
be two pseudo-differential operators of order
0
such that

\bigl(\WF(A)\times\WF(B)\bigr)\cap\Gamma(\varphi)=\emptyset.

(3.1)

Also let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
be equal to
1
near
0
. Then, for
N>0
and
\Re s>6N\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)+\mu_{\mathrm{max}}(X)
, we have the convergence

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\chi(t/\varepsilon)Ae^{t(X-s)}B\mathrm{d}t\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}A(s-X)^{-1}B

in the space of bounded operators
H^{-N}\to H^{N}
.

Proof.

We start by an ellipticity argument. For any
\eta>0
,
X
is elliptic in the directions
(z,\xi)
for which
|\langle\xi,X(z)\rangle|>\eta|\xi|
. We can thus decompose

B=B_{\mathrm{ell}}+B_{\mathrm{char}},

with
B_{\mathrm{char}}
microsupported in an arbitrarily small conical neighbourhood of
\{\langle\xi,X\rangle=0\}
, and
B_{\mathrm{ell}}
microsupported in the region of ellipticity of
X
.

By ellipticity, we can find a pseudo
C(s)
of order
-2N
, with same wavefront set as
B_{\mathrm{ell}}
, and
R
a smoothing operator, such that

B_{\mathrm{ell}}=(s-X)^{2N}C(s)+R.

Then we have

5.
Geodesic flow on anosov surfaces

In this section we prove the main result of our article. Beforehand, we will prove a more general statement valid for every Anosov flow.

Assume that

Then it follows from the results of

(5.2)

\tr_{\mathrm{s}}A=\tr^{\flat}_{\mathrm{s}}A,

where

is the supertrace of

\tr_{\mathrm{s}}A=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\tr A|_{\Omega^{k}}.

6.
Examples of maps transverse to an Anosov flow

Let us start by translating the assumption into more easily understable geometric statements. We will assume that

{\mathrm{Q}}=\{(F(z),z)\ |\ z\in M\}

is the graph of a smooth map

. When
X
is an Anosov vector field,

\overline{\Omega_{+}}=\Omega_{+}\cup E_{u}^{\ast}\times E_{s}^{\ast}.

In particular, one gets the following characterization.

Lemma 9
.

N^{\ast}{\mathrm{Q}}\cap\Gamma(\varphi)^{\prime}=\{0\}
if and only if three conditions are satisfied

(i)

has non-degenerate fixed points

(ii)

\mathbb{R}^{+}\times{\mathrm{Q}}
is transverse to

.

(iii)

\dim\bigl(\mathrm{d}F(E_{s}\oplus E_{0})\cap(E_{u}\oplus E_{0})\bigr)\leqslant 1

Observe that both
(i)
and
(ii)
are generic. However
(iii)
is not so easily verified. Indeed, it means that the graph of

is transverse to every leaf of the foliation

M\times M=\bigcup_{x,y}\left(W^{u0}(x)\times W^{s0}(y)\right).

Generically in 3 dimensions, the points where the graph of

is satisfied. Thankfully,

Proof.

Condition
(i)
stems from the fact that

For condition

From this it follows that

(here

Finally, for

, we study the condition

Since

, this means that for every

is tranverse to the foliation

Denoting

the components of

This is equivalent to

∎

\delta&\varepsilon&\nu\end{pmatrix}

for some functions
\lambda,\mu^{\perp},\mu,\delta,\varepsilon,\nu
of
(\tau,\rho,\theta)
, with
\nu=\partial_{\theta}\varphi
and

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\sin\theta\sin\varphi+\cos\theta(-\ell h\chi^{\prime}\sin\varphi+\cos\varphi),

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=-2\partial_{\rho}h(-h^{-1}\sin\theta+\ell\chi^{\prime}\cos\theta)-2h^{-1}\partial_{\rho}h\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}\varphi+\partial_{\rho}\varphi\cos\theta.

Now, write

\varepsilon=2h^{-1}\partial_{h}\sin\theta(1-\partial_{\theta}\varphi)+\partial_{\rho}\varphi\cos\theta-2\ell\chi^{\prime}\partial_{\rho}h\cos\theta.

Equation (
6.2
) yields

\partial_{\theta}\varphi\to 1,\quad\partial_{\rho}\varphi\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi\to\theta

uniformly on
[-2,2]\times\mathbb{R}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
as
\ell\to 0
. Therefore we get

\varepsilon\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\mu,\nu\to 1

as
\ell\to 0.
Consequently, whenever
\ell
is small enough, we have

\mathrm{d}F_{0}(E_{u}\oplus E_{0})\neq E_{s}\oplus E_{0}

by (
6.1
), which is equivalent to the condition
(iii)
. Since
(iii)
is an open condition, any sufficiently small perturbation of
F_{0}
will still satisfy it.

We can find
f
arbitrarily close to
f_{0}
, with non degenerate fixed points
x_{1},\dots,x_{q}
. Let
F_{1}
be a bundle map over
f
, close to
F_{0}
, so that
F_{1}
satisfies
(iii)
.

Next, consider the function

\mathscr{F}:\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times M\to M\times M

defined by

\mathscr{F}(\theta,z)=(R_{\theta}F_{1}(z),z),

and set
F_{\theta}(z)=R_{\theta}\circ F_{1}
. We claim that for a dense set of
\theta
’s,
F_{\theta}
satisfies
(i)
and
(ii)
. For this we will use the tranversality result
[
Lau12
, Théorème p. 93]
.

For point
(i)
, observe that
F_{\theta}
has non degenerate fixed points if and only if
F_{\theta}
has non-degenerate fixed points in each fiber
S_{x_{j}}\Sigma
,
j=1\dots q
. However,

\mathrm{ran}\left(\mathrm{d}\mathscr{F}_{|\mathbb{R}\times S_{x_{j}}\Sigma}\right)=T[S_{x_{j}}\Sigma]^{2}

is tranverse to the diagonal, so that according to
[
Lau12
, Théorème p. 93]
, for almost every
\theta
,
F_{\theta}
has non degenerate fixed points in
S_{x_{j}}\Sigma
.

Let us turn to point
(ii)
. Define for
\tau>0

G_{\tau}=\{(\varphi_{t}(z),z)\ |\ 0\leqslant t<\tau,~z\in M\}\subset M\times M.

Let us prove that
\mathscr{F}
is transverse to
G_{\tau}
. Let
z\in M
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
\tau>t\geqslant 0
such that we have
R_{\theta}F_{1}(x,v)=\varphi_{t}(z)
. For simplicity we set
z_{t}=\varphi_{t}(z)
. If
t=0
, the tranversality of
\mathscr{F}
with
G_{\varphi}
follows from the tranversality with the diagonal, so we assume that
t>0
. At our point of intersection,
\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{d}\mathscr{F})
contains

T_{z_{t}}S_{f(x)}\Sigma\times T_{z}S_{x}\Sigma=\mathrm{vect}\{(V,0),\ (0,V)\}

On the other and,
TG_{\tau}
is generated by
(X,0)
and the vectors

(\mathrm{d}\varphi_{t}(z)W,W),\quad W\in T_{z}M.

In particular,
TG_{\tau}
contains also
(0,X)
. Finally, since
\Sigma
has no conjugate points,
\mathrm{d}\varphi_{t}(V)
always has a non-zero component along
H
when
t>0
, so that
\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{d}\mathscr{F})+TG_{\tau}
contains

(X,0),\ (0,X),\ (V,0),\ (0,V),\ (H,0),\ (0,H),

which generate
T_{(z_{t},z)}(M\times M)
. Taking a sequence
\tau=n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can use again
[
Lau12
, Théorème p. 93]
to conclude. This closes the proof of Proposition
11
.

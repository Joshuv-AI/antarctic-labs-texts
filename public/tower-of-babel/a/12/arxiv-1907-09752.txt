Stabilized subgrid multiscale finite element formulation for advection-diffusion-reaction equation with variable coefficients coupled with Stokes-Darcy equation
Manisha Chowdhury, B.V. Rathish Kumar

Abstract
In this paper subgrid multiscale stabilized finite element method for Advection-Diffusion-Reaction (ADR) equation coupled with Stokes-Darcy flow problem has been studied. Here the advection velocity involved in ADR equation obeys Stokes-Darcy flow equation. In this study the approach of algebraic approximation of stabilization parameter has been considered. Further apriori error estimation has been elaborately carried out.

Statement of the problem

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded domain in

, d=2,3. Here we have worked with two dimensional model for the sake of simplicity in further derivation, but it can be extended to three dimensional model straightforward.

For an incompressible solvent fluid the Stokes-Darcy (or Brinkman) equation representing its flow is to find

\begin{split}-\mu\Delta\textbf{u}+\sigma\textbf{u}+\bigtriangledown p&=\textbf{f}\hskip 5.69054ptin\hskip 5.69054pt\Omega\\
\bigtriangledown\cdot\textbf{u}&=0\hskip 5.69054ptin\hskip 5.69054pt\Omega\\
\textbf{u}&=\textbf{0}\hskip 5.69054pton\hskip 5.69054pt\partial\Omega\\
\end{split}

(1)

where
u

),p and

are velocity, pressure and viscosity of the fluid,
\sigma

And the ADR equation with spatially variable coefficients representing the concentration c of transporting solute in the same domain

such that,

\begin{split}-\bigtriangledown\cdot\tilde{\bigtriangledown}c+\textbf{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown c+\alpha c&=g\hskip 5.69054ptin\hskip 5.69054pt\Omega\\
c&=0\hskip 5.69054pton\hskip 5.69054pt\Omega\\
\end{split}

(2)

where the notation,

are spatially variable diffusion coefficients along x-axis and y-axis respectively,
\alpha

(i)

2.1
Weak formulation

Let us consider the standard space
V=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as the admissible space for both velocity fields and concentration and the space
Q=L^{2}(\Omega)
for pressure.

The weak formulation is to find
(\textbf{u},p)\in V\times V\times Q
and
c\in V
such that

\begin{split}a_{S}(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})-b(\textbf{v},p)+b(\textbf{u},q)&=l_{S}(\textbf{v})\hskip 5.69054pt\forall\textbf{v}\in V\times V,q\in Q\\
a_{T}(c,d)&=l_{T}(d)\hskip 5.69054pt\forall d\in V\end{split}

(3)

where
a_{S}(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})=\int_{\Omega}\mu\bigtriangledown\textbf{u}:\bigtriangledown\textbf{v}+\sigma\int_{\Omega}\textbf{u}\cdot\textbf{v}
and
b(\textbf{v},q)=\int_{\Omega}(\bigtriangledown\cdot\textbf{v})q

a_{T}(c,d)=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\bigtriangledown}c\cdot\bigtriangledown d+\int_{\Omega}d\textbf{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown c+\alpha\int_{\Omega}cd

l_{S}(\textbf{v})=\int_{\Omega}\textbf{f}\cdot\textbf{v}
and
l_{T}(d)=\int_{\Omega}gd

\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\bigtriangledown}E^{A}_{c}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{A}_{c}+\alpha\int_{\Omega}E^{A}_{c}E^{A}_{c}=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(-\bigtriangledown\cdot\widetilde{\bigtriangledown}E^{A}_{c}-\overline{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{A}_{c}+\alpha E^{A}_{c})\tau_{3}(-\bigtriangledown\cdot\widetilde{\bigtriangledown}E^{I}_{c}+\\
\quad\overline{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{I}_{c}+\alpha E^{I}_{c})+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(-\bigtriangledown\cdot\widetilde{\bigtriangledown}E^{A}_{c}-\overline{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{A}_{c}+\alpha E^{A}_{c})\tau_{3}(-\bigtriangledown\cdot\widetilde{\bigtriangledown}E^{A}_{c}+\overline{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{A}_{c}+\alpha E^{A}_{c})\\
\quad-\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\bigtriangledown}E^{I}_{c}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{A}_{c}-\int_{\Omega}E^{A}_{c}\textbf{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{I}_{c}-\int_{\Omega}E^{A}_{c}\textbf{u}\cdot\bigtriangledown E^{A}_{c}\\
=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}+T_{5}\hskip 2.84526pt(say)\hskip 170.71652pt

(20)

We will follow the same procedure as the previous part. Under the same observation let us consider the positive numbers
M_{6k},M_{7k},M_{8k},M_{7k}^{\prime},M_{8k}^{\prime}
as bounds for
E^{A}_{c},\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial x},\frac{\partial^{2}E^{A}_{c}}{\partial x^{2}},\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial x},\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial y}
respectively over each sub-domain.

First simply multiplying the terms of
T_{1}
, then applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality over each of them and finally using bounds for
E^{A}s
we can find estimate of
T_{1}
as follows:

\begin{split}T_{1}&\leq\mid\tau_{3}\mid(\sum_{k=1}^{n_{el}}(D_{1l}M_{8k}+D_{2l}M_{8k}^{\prime}+D_{u1}M_{7k}+D_{u2}M_{7k}^{\prime}+\alpha M_{6k}))\{(D_{1l}+D_{2l})\\
&\quad\|E^{I}_{c}\|_{2}+(\bar{D}_{u1}+\bar{D}_{u2})\|E^{I}_{c}\|_{1}+\alpha\|E^{I}_{c}\|\}\\
&\leq\mid\tau_{3}\mid C(\sum_{k=1}^{n_{el}}(D_{1l}M_{8k}+D_{2l}M_{8k}^{\prime}+D_{u1}M_{7k}+D_{u2}M_{7k}^{\prime}+\alpha M_{6k}))\{(D_{1l}+D_{2l})\\
&\quad+h(\bar{D}_{u1}+\bar{D}_{u2})+h^{2}\alpha\}\|c\|_{2}\end{split}

(21)

Applying similar arguments we have the following bounds

\begin{split}T_{2}&\leq\mid\tau_{3}\mid\sum_{k=1}^{n_{el}}\{(D_{1l}M_{8k}+D_{2l}M_{8k}^{\prime}+D_{u1}M_{7k}+D_{u2}M_{7k}^{\prime}+\alpha M_{6k})(D_{1l}M_{8k}+\\
&\quad D_{2l}M_{8k}^{\prime}+\bar{D}_{u1}M_{7k}+\bar{D}_{u2}M_{7k}^{\prime}+\alpha M_{6k})\}\end{split}

(22)

For estimating next terms we are going to use Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities as follows:

\begin{split}T_{3}&=-\int_{\Omega}(D_{1}\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial x}+D_{2}\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial y}\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial y})\\
&\leq\frac{D_{1l}}{2\epsilon_{3}}\|E^{I}_{c}\|_{1}^{2}+\frac{D_{2l}\epsilon_{3}}{2}(\|\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial x}\|^{2}+\|\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial y}\|^{2})\\
&\leq\frac{D_{1l}}{2\epsilon_{3}}h^{2}\|c\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{D_{2l}\epsilon_{3}}{2}(\|\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial x}\|^{2}+\|\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial y}\|^{2})\\
\end{split}

(23)

\begin{split}T_{4}&=-\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial x}+u_{2}\frac{\partial E^{I}_{c}}{\partial y})E^{A}_{c}\\
&\leq\frac{(u_{1l}+u_{2l})}{2\epsilon_{4}}\|E^{I}_{c}\|_{1}^{2}+\frac{(u_{1l}+u_{2l})}{2}\epsilon_{4}\|E^{A}_{c}\|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{(u_{1l}+u_{2l})}{2\epsilon_{4}}h^{2}\|c\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{(u_{1l}+u_{2l})}{2}\epsilon_{4}\|E^{A}_{c}\|^{2}\end{split}

(24)

Similarly

\begin{split}T_{5}&=-\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial x}+u_{2}\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial y})E^{A}_{c}\\
&\leq\frac{(u_{1l}+u_{2l})}{2\epsilon_{4}}(\|\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial x}\|^{2}+\|\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial y}\|^{2})+\frac{(u_{1l}+u_{2l})}{2}\epsilon_{4}\|E^{A}_{c}\|^{2}\end{split}

(25)

Now combining all the results obtained above into the equation (20) and proceeding as explained at the end of the first part we finally get

\|\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial x}\|^{2}+\|\frac{\partial E^{A}_{c}}{\partial y}\|^{2}+\|E^{A}_{c}\|^{2}\leq C_{2}(c)h^{2}

(26)

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Apriori error estimate
:
Assuming the same condition as in the previous theorem

\begin{split}\|u_{1}-u_{1h}\|^{2}_{V}+\|u_{2}-u_{2h}\|^{2}_{V}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{Q}^{2}+\|c-c_{h}\|_{V}^{2}&\leq C^{\prime}(\textbf{u},p,c)h^{2}\\
\end{split}

(27)

where
C^{\prime}
is a constant depending upon
\textbf{u},p,c
.

Proof.

We prove this result by first applying triangle inequality and then interpolation estimates and the results obtained in the previous theorem as the following

\|u_{1}-u_{1h}\|^{2}_{V}+\|u_{2}-u_{2h}\|^{2}_{V}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{Q}^{2}+\|c-c_{h}\|_{V}^{2}\\
=\|E^{I}_{u1}+E^{A}_{u1}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{I}_{u2}+E^{A}_{u2}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{I}_{c}+E^{A}_{c}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{I}_{c}+E^{A}_{c}\|_{V}^{2}\hskip 56.9055pt\\
\leq\bar{C}(\|E^{I}_{u1}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{I}_{u2}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{I}_{p}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{I}_{c}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{A}_{u1}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{A}_{u2}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{A}_{p}\|_{V}^{2}+\|E^{A}_{c}\|_{V}^{2})\\
\leq C^{\prime}(\textbf{u},p,c)h^{2}\hskip 284.52756pt

(28)

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

This error estimation is not computable as it depends upon the true solution, but it implies convergence of the stabilized method and the order of convergence for this method is 2.

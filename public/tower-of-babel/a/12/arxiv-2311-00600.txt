Cumulant method for weighted random connection models
Nils Heerten, Christian Hirsch, Moritz Otto

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive cumulant bounds for subgraph counts and power-weighted edge length in a class of spatial random networks known as weighted random connection models. This involves dealing with long-range spatial correlations induced by the profile function and the weight distribution. We start by deriving the bounds for the classical case of a Poisson vertex set, and then provide extensions to α \alpha -determinantal processes.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
60F10, 60G55, 60D05.

1.
Introduction

In the random geometric graph, the vertex set is a random set of points in Euclidean space, and any two vertices are connected by an edge if their distance is smaller than a fixed threshold. Such a random geometric graph is a model in a wide variety of applications such as wireless networks. Therefore, its asymptotic behavior for a large number of nodes has been studied extensively in the literature
[
15
]
. However, with the rise of network science, it has become apparent that often this model is not flexible enough to capture more complex structures. Driven by this need, the class of
weighted random connection models (WRCM)
has recently appeared as a promising approach for describing phenomena in large complex networks
[
6
,
7
,
12
]
. Instead of a fixed connection threshold, the WRCM quantify the connectivity via a
profile function
and a
weight distribution
. However, due to the complexity of the models, the analysis of their asymptotic behavior is still in its infancy. In this paper, we study the question of asymptotic normality of suitable functionals on the WRCM. This is relevant in order to put the analysis of data via WRCM on a firm statistical foundation.

One of the main tools in the analysis of asymptotic normality is the method of cumulants
[
3
,
17
]
. Its popularity stems from the level of generality where it is applicable and the variety of implications it draws. Moreover, one obtains specific non-asymptotic bounds for the rate of convergence to the normal distribution. However, the challenge in applying the method of cumulants is that it requires the computation of higher-order integrals of the functional under consideration. This is particularly challenging in settings involving long-range spatial correlations. Recently, an important progress was achieved in
[
14
,
18
]
, where the authors derived a general formula for the cumulants of U-statistics based on Poisson processes, see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
therein. This formula relies on the computation of higher-order integrals, which are particularly accessible for random geometric graphs, where the corresponding integrands are bounded and of bounded support. This is no longer true in the WRCM where the range of the support depends on the profile function and the weight distribution.

While Poisson processes are certainly the simplest model for a random point pattern in Euclidean space, they are inappropriate in settings with substantial spatial correlations. In order to overcome this limitation, one considers determinantal and permanental point processes that are based on a spatial kernel function
[
10
]
. These classes of point processes are particularly well-suited for cumulant-based methods since their cumulant density can be expressed in closed form in terms of the kernel function
[
8
]
. One of the key observations in
[
9
,
11
]
is that the cumulants of U-statistics can again be bounded in terms of higher-order integrals. These integrals are reminiscent to those appearing in
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
but are more complex due to the presence of the kernel function. We show that the results in the Poisson case can be extended to this more complex setting at the cost of weaker bounds on the convergence rate. In this context, we provide a lower bound on the variance of the functional under consideration, which may be of independent interest.

The main contributions of this paper are:

i.

Show asymptotic normality for power-weighted edge lengths and subgraph counts in the WRCM. One of the key achievements of our result is that our bounds are completely transparent in the moment assumptions on the weight distribution and the profile function.

ii.

While
[
18
]
focusses on the case of Poisson processes, we extend the results to
\alpha
-determinantal processes. This relies on expansions derived in
[
8
,
9
,
11
]
.

iii.

Explain how variance lower bounds can be established for the considered functionals.

The rest of the manuscript is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the model and state our main results. Then we discuss certain preliminary results concerning general cumulant computations in Section
3
. Sections
4
,
5
and
6
contain the proofs of the main results. Finally, Section
7
contains some considerations regarding the lower variance bounds.

2.
Model and main results

The main goal of this work is to develop the method of cumulants in the setting of WRCM. One of the core strengths of the cumulant method is that it gives rise to a variety of different limit results as elaborated in
[
3
]
. To make our presentation self contained, we reproduce here these consequences.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of square-integrable random variables, let
\gamma\geqslant 0
be a constant and let
(\Delta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be a positive real-valued sequence. We say that
(X_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
satisfies

•

MDP
(\gamma,(\Delta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1})
if for any positive real-valued sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
with

\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\Delta_{n}^{1/(1+2\gamma)}}=0

the re-scaled random variables
(\widehat{X}_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}:=(a_{n}^{-1}(X_{n}-\mathbb{E}[X_{n}])/\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(X_{n})})_{n\geqslant 1}
satisfy a
moderate deviation principle (MDP)
with speed
a_{n}^{2}
and good rate function
\mathcal{I}(z)=z^{2}/2
. That is, for all Borel sets
A
,

-\inf_{z\in\text{int}(A)}\mathcal{I}(z)\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}a_{n}^{-2}\log\mathbb{P}(\widehat{X}_{n}\in A)\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_{n}^{-2}\log\mathbb{P}(\widehat{X}_{n}\in A)\leqslant-\inf_{z\in\text{cl}(A)}\mathcal{I}(z),

where
\text{int}(A)
and
\text{cl}(A)
stand for the interior and the closure of
A
, respectively
[
2
]
. Sometimes this is also referred to as large deviation principle. However, since in our setting the speed
a_{n}
is of order smaller than
\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(X_{n})}
, the term MDP is more appropriate.

•

CI
(\gamma,(\Delta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1})
if the Bernstein-type concentration inequality

\mathbb{P}\big(\,|X_{n}-\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]|\geqslant z\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(X_{n})}\,\big)\leqslant 2\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{4}\min\Big\{\frac{z^{2}}{2^{1+\gamma}},(z\Delta_{n})^{1/(1+\gamma)}\Big\}\Big)

holds for all
n\geqslant 1
and
z\geqslant 0
,

•

NACC
(\gamma,(\Delta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1})
if a normal approximation bound with Crámer correction holds, that is, if there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
only depending on
\gamma
such that for all
n\geqslant 1
and
z\in[0,c_{0}\Delta_{n}^{1/(1+2\gamma)}]
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\,|X_{n}-\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]|\geqslant z\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(X_{n})}\,\big)=e^{L_{n,z}^{+}}(1-\Phi(z))\Big(1+c_{1}\theta_{n,z}^{+}\frac{1+z}{\Delta_{n}^{1/(1+2\gamma)}}\Big)

and

\mathbb{P}\big(\,|X_{n}-\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]|\leqslant-z\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(X_{n})}\,\big) =e^{L_{n,z}^{-}}(1-\Phi(z))\Big(1+c_{1}\theta_{n,z}^{-}\frac{1+z}{\Delta_{n}^{1/(1+2\gamma)}}\Big)

with
\theta_{n,z}^{+},\theta_{n,z}^{-}\in[-1,1]
and
L_{n,z}^{+},L_{n,z}^{-}\in(-c_{2}z^{3}/\Delta_{n}^{1/(1+2\gamma)},c_{2}z^{3}/\Delta_{n}^{1/(1+2\gamma)})
, where
\Phi
is the distribution function of a standard normal random variable.

We now provide a formal definition of the WRCM considered in this paper, confer
[
6
,
7
,
12
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}:=\{P_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 1}:=\{(X_{i},U_{i})\}_{i\geqslant 1}
be a Poisson point process on the space
W\times[0,\infty)
with intensity measure
t_{n}\mathrm{d}x\otimes\mathbb{P}_{U}(\mathrm{d}u)
for some compact set
W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with interior points and
t_{n}>0
and
\mathbb{P}_{U}
is a probability distribution. Then, in the WRCM, between any pair of points
P_{i},P_{j}
we place an edge independently with probability

(1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(X_{i}-X_{j},U_{i},U_{j}):=\varphi\bigg(\frac{|B_{\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|}|}{\nu_{n}\kappa(U_{i},U_{j})}\bigg),

where we now explain in detail the individual components.

i.

\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is the
profile function
, which is non-increasing and for which we assume the normalization
\int\varphi(t)\mathrm{d}t=1
.

ii.

|B_{\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|}|
is the volume of the Euclidean ball of radius
\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Sometimes in literature, the scaling
\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|^{d}
is used. Since the difference is only in a constant multiple, the two parameterizations are equivalent. For our analysis, the parameterization with the ball-based scaling will simplify substantially the statements and proofs of our main results.

iii.

(\nu_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
is a positive real-valued sequence that is bounded from above. It can be thought of as the volume of a ball around a typical node within which most neighbors of this node are located.

iv.

Finally,
\kappa\colon[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is the
interaction kernel
, which is non-decreasing in both arguments. We assume that
\kappa
is such that
1\leqslant\kappa(u,v)\leqslant uv
for all
u,v\geqslant 0
.

In a broad sense, our cumulant bounds are similar to those considered earlier in the setting of standard random geometric graphs
[
18
]
. However, in the setting of WRCM the convergence will depend sensitively on moment estimates for both the profile function and the weight distribution. To make this precise, we now introduce specific conditions on these quantities.

We assume that
U\geqslant 1
a.s. and that there are constants
c_{U,1}>0
and
c_{U,2}\leqslant 1
such that for all
x\geqslant 1
,

(
MU)

\displaystyle M_{U}(x):=\mathbb{E}[U^{x}]\leqslant c_{U,1}^{x}\Gamma(1+x)^{c_{U,2}},

where
\Gamma(y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{y-1}e^{-t}\mathrm{d}t
is the standard Gamma function. For instance, if
X
is exponentially distributed with some parameter
\lambda>0
, then
U:=X+1
satisfies (
MU
) with
c_{U,2}=1
. Moreover, if
X
is a standard normal random variable and
U\sim X\mid\{X>1\}
, then (
MU
) holds with
c_{U,2}=1/2
. As we will see in Theorem
1
below, the value of the constant
c_{U,2}
is much more important than that of
c_{U,1}
.

The first functional of interest is the subgraph count, which was also considered in
[
18
]
and we recall the definition here. Let
G
be a fixed connected graph on the vertices
\{1,\dots,q\}
. Then, we let
S_{n}(G)
denote
1/q!
times the number of
q
-tuples
(P_{1},\dots,P_{q})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{q}
such that for every edge
\{i,j\}
in
G
, the WRCM has an edge between
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
.

Theorem 1

(Cumulant bounds for subgraph count)
.

Let
d\geqslant 1
and
G
be a fixed connected graph on
q
vertices.
Assume that
U\geqslant 1
a.s. and that condition (
MU
) holds and set
A_{\mathsf{SG}}:=(q-1)(1+c_{U,2})
. Moreover, assume that there exists
v>0
such that for all
n\geqslant 1
,

(
VSG)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}(G))\geqslant v|W|t_{n}^{q}\nu_{n}^{q-1}(1\vee t_{n}\nu_{n})^{q-1}.

Then,
S_{n}(G)
satisfies
\emph{\textbf{MDP}}(A_{\mathsf{SG}},(\beta_{n})_{n})
,
\emph{\textbf{CI}}(A_{\mathsf{SG}},(\beta_{n})_{n})
and
\emph{\textbf{NACC}}(A_{\mathsf{SG}},(\beta_{n})_{n})
with

\beta_{n}:=\frac{\sqrt{v|W|t_{n}(1\wedge t_{n}\nu_{n})^{q-1}}}{q^{3q}(b_{\mathsf{SG}}\vee b_{\mathsf{SG}}^{3}/v)},\quad\text{where}\quad b_{\mathsf{SG}}:=((1\vee c_{U,1}^{2})(q-1)^{c_{U,2}})^{q-1},\quad n\geqslant 1,

Second, for fixed
\tau\geqslant 0
, we consider the
\tau
-power-weighted edge length given by

S_{n}^{(\tau)}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{P_{i}\sim P_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|^{\tau},

where
P_{i}\sim P_{j}
indicates the existence of an edge in the WRCM. Here, we additionally need to impose similar assumptions concerning
\varphi
as we did for the weights in (
MU
). More precisely, we assume that there are constants
c_{\varphi,1}>0
and
c_{\varphi,2}\leqslant 1
such that for all
x\geqslant 0
,

(
MPHI)

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t)t^{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant c_{\varphi,1}^{x}\Gamma(1+x)^{c_{\varphi,2}}\quad\text{and}\quad M_{\varphi}^{\prime}(x):=\sup_{t>0}\varphi(t)t^{x}\leqslant c_{\varphi,1}^{x}\Gamma(1+x)^{c_{\varphi,2}}.

For instance, (
MPHI
) holds if
\varphi
has compact support. Moreover, similarly as for the case of weights, one can also consider the cases of exponential tails where
c_{\varphi,2}=1
and Gaussian tails where
c_{\varphi,2}=1/2
.

Theorem 2

(Cumulant bounds for power-weighted edge lengths)
.

Let
d\geqslant 1
and
\tau\geqslant 0
.
Assume that
U\geqslant 1
a.s. and that conditions (
MU
) and (
MPHI
) hold and set
A_{\mathsf{EL}}:=1+2c_{U,2}(\tau_{d}+1)+c_{\varphi,2}\tau_{d}
, where
\tau_{d}:=\tau/d
. Moreover, assume that there exists
v>0
such that for all
n\geqslant 1
,

(
VEL)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}^{(\tau)})\geqslant v|W|t_{n}^{2}\nu_{n}^{2\tau_{d}+1}(1\vee t_{n}\nu_{n}).

Then, the power-weighted edge length
S_{n}^{(\tau)}
satisfies
\emph{\textbf{MDP}}(A_{\mathsf{EL}},(\beta_{n})_{n})
,
\emph{\textbf{CI}}(A_{\mathsf{EL}},(\beta_{n})_{n})
and
\emph{\textbf{NACC}}(A_{\mathsf{EL}},(\beta_{n})_{n})
with

\beta_{n}:=\frac{\sqrt{v|W|t_{n}(1\wedge t_{n}\nu_{n})}}{64(b_{\mathsf{EL}}\vee b_{\mathsf{EL}}^{3}/v)},\quad\text{where}\quad b_{\mathsf{EL}}:=(c_{\varphi,1}(\tau_{d}\vee 1)^{c_{\varphi,2}})^{\tau_{d}}(c_{U,1}(\tau_{d}+1)^{c_{U,2}})^{2(\tau_{d}+1)},\quad n\geqslant 1.

Note that for
U=1
a.s. and
\varphi(\cdot)=\mathds{1}_{[0,1]}
, the weight-dependent random connection model reduces to the classical random connection model. In this situation,
[
18
, Section 4.2]
establishes the analogue of Theorem
1
. Since we can choose
a=c_{U,1}=1
,
c_{U,2}=0
in this case, Theorem
1
generalizes
[
18
, Corollary 4.2]
to the weight-dependent situation.

We also note that for many real-world datasets one can encounter the setting where the weights only have a finite number of moments. In such situations our results do not apply since the condition (
MU
) is violated.

Finally in Theorem
3
and
4
below, we replace the Poisson point process by an
\alpha
-determinantal point process (
\alpha
-DPP) as input. We note that while a homogeneous Poisson point process is a special case of an
\alpha
-DPP, the convergence rates derived for
\alpha
-DPPs are less sharp than the one for Poisson input. Hence, the results in Theorem
3
and
4
do not supersede the ones in Theorem
1
and
2
.

Recall that for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, a simple point process
\mathcal{P}
is called
\alpha
-determinantal point process (
\alpha
-DPP)
with
covariance kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, if for all
k\geqslant 1
, its
k
th product density
\rho_{k}
is given by

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{\pi\in\text{Per}(k)}\tau^{k-n(\pi)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}K(x_{i},x_{\pi(i)}),

for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\text{Per}(k)
is the set of all permutations of
\{1,\dots,k\}
and
n(\pi)
is the number of cycles in the permutation
\pi
. Important special cases are
determinantal processes
(
\tau=-1
) and
permanental processes
(
\tau=1
).
Following
[
8
, Section 3]
, we assume throughout that the kernel is stationary, i.e.
K(x,y)=K(o,y-x)=K_{0}(y-x)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
K_{0}(x)
is a continuous complex-valued non-negative-definite function, i.e.
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}z_{i}K_{0}(x_{i}-x_{j})\overline{z_{j}}\geqslant 0
for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. This implies that
K_{0}(o)\geqslant 0
,
K_{0}(-x)=\overline{K_{0}(x)}
,
|K_{0}(x)|\leqslant K_{0}(o)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and that the
\alpha
-DPP with kernel
K
is stationary. Moreover, we assume that

(
DPP)

\displaystyle K_{0}(o)\in[0,1]\quad\text{and}\quad \|K_{0}\|_{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{0}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Under these assumptions, it is shown in
[
8
, Theorem 2]
that the
\alpha
-DPP with kernel

is Brillinger-mixing, which says that for all
k\geqslant 1
, its

th cumulant density is absolutely integrable.

Since there is no natural analogue to an increasing intensity parameter for
\alpha
-DPPs, we will instead consider the scenario of a stationary point process
\mathcal{P}
restricted to an increasing sampling window
W_{n}:=n^{1/d}W
. This is as in
[
20
]
. Note that due to the scaling invariance property of a Poisson process, Theorems
1
and
2
could also be formulated in this setup.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we set the stage for the proof of our main results and recall some material from
[

.
To keep the presentation self-contained, in Section

3.1.
Partitions

Partitions play a central role in our application of the method of cumulants, and in the present section, we reproduce some material from
[
13
, Chapter 12.2]
, see also
[
18
, Section 2.4]
. However, since we only deal with single U-statistics and not linear combinations of U-statistics, we can simplify the presentation.

Let
m\geqslant 1
and let
q\geqslant 1
. We define
N_{0}:=0
,
N_{\ell}:=\ell q
,
\ell\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, and
N:=N_{m}
, and put
J_{\ell}:=\{N_{\ell-1}+1,\dots,N_{\ell}\}
,
\ell\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. A partition
\sigma
of
[N]:=\{1,\dots,N\}
is a collection
\{B_{1},\dots,B_{k}\}
of
1\leqslant k\leqslant N
pairwise disjoint non-empty sets, called blocks, such that
B_{1}\cup\dots\cup B_{k}=[N]
. By
\Pi^{m}(q)
, we denote the set of all partitions
\sigma
such that

\max_{B\in\sigma}\max_{\ell\leqslant m}|B\cap J_{\ell}|\leqslant 1,

and by
\Pi^{m}_{\geqslant 2}(q)
the set of all
\sigma\in\Pi^{m}(q)
with
\min_{B\in\sigma}|B|\geqslant 2
.

Every partition
\sigma\in\Pi^{m}(q)
induces a partition
\sigma^{*}
of
\{1,\dots,m\}
in the following way:
k,\ell\in\{1,\dots,m\}
are in the same block of
\sigma^{*}
whenever there is a block
B\in\sigma
such that
|B\cap J_{k}|=1
and
|B\cap J_{\ell}|=1
. Let
\widetilde{\Pi}^{m}(q)
be the set of all partitions
\sigma\in\Pi^{m}(q)
such that
|\sigma^{*}|=1
and define
\widetilde{\Pi}^{m}_{\geqslant 2}(q)
in the same way as
\Pi^{m}_{\geqslant 2}(q)
.

Let
(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X})
be a measurable space. For functions
f^{(\ell)}:\mathbb{X}^{q_{\ell}}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\ell\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we define their tensor product
\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)}:\mathbb{X}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)})(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}):=\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)}(x_{N_{\ell-1}+1},\dots,x_{N_{\ell}}).

For

the function

is obtained by replacing in

all variables that belong to the same block of

3.2.
Method of cumulants

In this section, we introduce the key tool for the proof of the main results, namely the method of cumulants, which originates from
[
17
]
, but also see the survey
[
3
]
. Henceforth,
\mathsf{cum}(X_{1},\,\dots,\,X_{d})
denotes the joint cumulant of the random variables
(X_{1},\,\dots,\,X_{d})
. We recall that with
i
the imaginary unit, this is given as

\mathsf{cum}(X_{1},\,\dots,\,X_{d}):=(-i)^{m}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial t_{1}\cdots\partial t_{m}}\log\phi_{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\mid_{t_{1}=\ldots=t_{m}=0},

where

\phi_{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{m}):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(i\sum_{i\leqslant m}t_{i}X_{i}\Big)\Big]

is the characteristic function of the random vector
(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
. We henceforth always assume that all considered random variables have finite moments of all orders.

The following proposition can be regarded as the application of the method of cumulants to Poisson-based U-statistics and is a variant of
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
. In contrast to the latter, we allow for the additional factor
(m!)^{p}
in assumption (
3
) below. Although we do not need it for our purposes, we note that the statement could be generalized to linear combinations of U-statistics.

Proposition 7

(Variant of Theorem 3.2 in
[
18
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{n})_{n}
be a family of Poisson processes over
\sigma
-finite measure spaces
((\mathbb{X}_{n},\mathcal{X}_{n},\mu_{n}))_{n}
and let
f_{n}:\mathbb{X}_{n}^{q}\to\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be measurable and satisfy the integrability conditions

\max_{i\in\{0,\dots,q\}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{i}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q-i}}f(y_{1},\dots,y_{i},x_{1},\dots,x_{q-i})\,\mu_{n}^{q-i}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{q-i}))\Big)^{2}\,\mu_{n}^{i}(\mathrm{d}(y_{1},\dots,y_{i}))<\infty,

and
\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{n}^{q})}^{2}>0
. Define for
n\geqslant 1
the random variables

Z_{n}:=\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{q})\in\mathcal{P}_{n,\neq}^{q}}f_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{q}).

Assume that there is a positive real-valued sequence
(\beta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
\sigma\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{m}(q)
and
m\geqslant 3
,

(3)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Var}(Z_{n})^{-m/2}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}}(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f_{n}^{(\ell)})_{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{|\sigma|}\Big|\leqslant(m!)^{p}\beta_{n}^{-(m-2)}.

Then
(Z_{n})_{n}
satisfies
MDP
(p+q-1,(c_{q}\beta_{n})_{n})
,
CI
(p+q-1,(c_{q}\beta_{n})_{n})
and
NACC
(p+q-1,(c_{q}\beta_{n})_{n})
with

.

Proof.

According to
[
3
, Section 2]
, we have to find parameters
\gamma\geqslant 0
and

, such that the condition

\Big|\mathsf{cum}_{m}\Big(\frac{Z_{n}}{\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(Z_{n})}}\Big)\Big|\leqslant\frac{(m!)^{1+\gamma}}{\Delta_{n}^{m-2}}

holds, which then implies satisfaction of
MDP
(\gamma,(\Delta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1})
,
CI
(\gamma,(\Delta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1})
and
NACC
(\gamma,(\Delta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1})
. This condition is widely known as the Statulevičius condition. To do so, we follow the arguments in the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
. In particular, we rely on the Wiener Itô integrals
I_{q}(f)
as defined in Section 2.1 ibidem.
By definition of the cumulant, its multilinearity and the triangle inequality we have that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathsf{cum}_{m}\Big(\frac{Z_{n}}{\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(Z_{n})}}\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathsf{Var}(Z_{n})^{m/2}}|\mathsf{cum}(Z_{n},\dots,Z_{n})|\leqslant\frac{1}{\mathsf{Var}(Z_{n})^{m/2}}|\mathsf{cum}(I_{q}(f_{n}),\dots,I_{q}(f_{n}))|.

Now it follows from

that

\displaystyle|\mathsf{cum}(I_{q}(f_{n}),\dots,I_{q}(f_{n}))|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\widetilde{\Pi}^{m}_{\geqslant 2}(q)|\sup_{\sigma\in\tilde{\Pi}^{m}_{\geqslant 2}(q)}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}}(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f_{n}^{(\ell)})_{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{|\sigma|}\Big|.

Thus, we obtain from the first part of
[

that

|\widetilde{\Pi}^{m}_{\geqslant 2}(q)|\leqslant|\Pi^{m}(q)|\leqslant q^{qm}(m!)^{q}.

Putting this together with Assumption (
3
) gives that

\Big|\mathsf{cum}_{m}\Big(\frac{Z_{n}}{\sqrt{\mathsf{Var}(Z_{n})}}\Big)\Big|\leqslant q^{qm}(m!)^{p+q}\beta_{n}^{m-2}\leqslant(m!)^{p+q}(q^{3q}\beta_{n})^{m-2}.

∎

. However, secondly, an edge of type II is now also if

and

are members of the same element in

Proof of Theorem
3
.

First we bound the number of partitions involved in the double sum in (

we have that
|\Pi^{m}(q)|\leqslant q^{qm}(m!)^{q}
. To bound the sum over the partitions
\Pi(q;\sigma)

(m-1)!\leqslant m!
summands. Hence, given

\sum_{r=1}^{|\sigma|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\Pi(q;\sigma)\\
|\rho|=r\end{subarray}}|Q_{1}|!\cdots|Q_{r}|!\leqslant\sum_{r=1}^{|\sigma|}\frac{2^{|\sigma|-1}|\sigma|!}{r!}\leqslant 2^{|\sigma|}|\sigma|!\leqslant(2q)^{qm}(m!)^{q},

where the upper bound of the inner sum holds by

4

and we have used (
7

|\alpha|\leqslant 1

|\mathsf{cum}(S_{n}(G))|
is bounded by

(13)

\displaystyle(2q^{2})^{qm}(m!)^{2q}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\Pi^{m}(q)\\
\rho\in\Pi(q;\sigma)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}}(f_{n}^{\otimes m})_{\sigma}\prod_{Q\in\rho}\sup_{\pi\in\text{Per}(|Q|)}|K({x_{1},x_{\pi(2)}})K({x_{\pi(2)},x_{\pi(3)}})\cdots K({x_{\pi(|Q|)},x_{1}})|\,\mu_{n}^{|\sigma|}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}).

Hence, it remains to bound the individual integrands in (

We construct a spanning tree such that for points
x_{i}

is present in the integral. We put type I edges in the spanning tree that connect the different

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{W_{n}^{|\sigma|}}\prod_{(i,j)\in E_{s,1}}\varphi_{n}(x_{i}-x_{j},U_{i},U_{j})\prod_{(i,j)\in E_{s,2}}|K(x_{i},x_{j})|\,\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{|\sigma|})\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant|W_{n}|\nu_{n}^{|E_{s,1}|}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{(i,j)\in E_{s,1}}\kappa(U_{i},U_{j})\Big]\|K_{0}\|_{1}^{|E_{s,2}|}\leqslant|W_{n}|\nu_{n}^{|E_{s,1}|}(m!)^{(q-1)c_{U,2}}b_{\mathsf{SG}}^{m}\|K_{0}\|_{1}^{|E_{s,2}|}.

where

From here, the assertion follows as in the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

\displaystyle 64^{m}(m!)^{4}\sup_{\sigma\in\Pi^{m}(q)}\sup_{\rho\in\Pi(q;\sigma)}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{W_{n}^{|\sigma|}}\prod_{(i,j)\in E_{1}}\varphi_{n}(x_{i}-x_{j},U_{i},U_{j})|B_{\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|}|^{\tau}\prod_{(i,j)\in E_{s,2}}|K(x_{i},x_{j})|\,\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{|\sigma|})\Big].

\displaystyle 64^{m}(m!)^{4}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod\limits_{(i,j)\in E_{1}\setminus E_{s,1}}(\nu_{n}\kappa(U_{i},U_{j}))^{{\tau m_{ij}}{}}M_{\varphi}^{\prime}(\tau m_{ij})

7.
Variance lower bounds

All of our main results depend on lower bounds for the variance of
S_{n}(G)
and
S_{n}^{(\tau)}
, respectively. In this final section, we elaborate on how these lower bounds can be established. Although the following propositions are stated for the scenario of a fixed point process restricted to a growing sampling window
W_{n}
, they analogously hold in the case of a Poisson process with a growing intensity
t_{n}
on a fixed sampling window
W
.

For some
a>0
let

W_{n}^{-a}:=\{x\in W_{n}:\,B_{(a/|B_{1}|)^{1/d}}(x)\subseteq W_{n}\}

be the set of all
x\in W_{n}
such that a ball centered at
x
with volume
a
is still contained entirely in
W_{n}
.

We start with the the lower bound on
\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}^{(\tau d)})
.

Proposition 8

(Variance lower bounds for power-weighted edge count)
.

For
\alpha\in[0,1]
let
\mathcal{P}
be a stationary
\alpha
-DPP. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\tau\geqslant 0
. Then for
n\geqslant 1
,

\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}^{(\tau d)})\geqslant v|W_{n}|\nu_{n}^{2\tau+1}(1\vee\nu_{n}),

where

v:=\inf_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{|W_{n}^{-\varepsilon\nu_{n}}|}{|W_{n}|}|B_{1}|^{-2\tau}K_{0}(o)^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}t^{2\tau+1}\varphi(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big(1\wedge 2K_{0}(o)\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}t^{2\tau+1}\varphi(t)\mathrm{d}t\,\Big).

Note that if the sequence
(\nu_{n}/n)_{n\geqslant 1}
is bounded, we have that
v>0
. Indeed, our assumptions (that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t)\mathrm{d}t=1
and that
\varphi
is non-increasing) guarantee that there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varphi(\varepsilon)>0
and
\inf_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{|W_{n}^{-\varepsilon\nu_{n}}|}{|W_{n}|}>0
.

Proof of Proposition
8
.

Expanding the second moment of
S_{n}^{(\tau d)}
into three terms, we find that the variance of
S_{n}^{(\tau d)}
is given by

\displaystyle|B_{1}|^{2\tau}\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}^{(\tau d)})

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|^{2\tau}\big(\mathbb{E}[(S_{n}^{(\tau d)})^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[S_{n}^{(\tau d)}]^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{W_{n}^{2}}|B_{\|x-y\|}|^{2\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi_{n}(x-y,U,V)\big]\rho_{2}(x,y)\mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\,+2\int_{W_{n}^{3}}|B_{\|x-y\|}|^{\tau}|B_{\|x-y^{\prime}\|}|^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi_{n}(x-y,U,V)\varphi_{n}(x-y^{\prime},U,V^{\prime})\big]\rho_{3}(x,y,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}(x,y,y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\,+\int_{W_{n}^{4}}|B_{\|x-y\|}|^{\tau}|B_{\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|}|^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi_{n}(x-y,U,V)\varphi_{n}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},U^{\prime},V^{\prime})]\rho_{4}(x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\,-\int_{W_{n}^{4}}|B_{\|x-y\|}|^{\tau}|B_{\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|}|^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi_{n}(x-y,U,V)\varphi_{n}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},U^{\prime},V^{\prime})\big]\rho_{2}(x,y)\rho_{2}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),

where
U,U^{\prime},V,V^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{U}
are independent. Since for an
\alpha
-DPP with
\alpha\geqslant 0
all terms on the right-hand side in (
2
) are non-negative, it holds that
\rho_{4}(x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime})\geqslant\rho_{2}(x,y)\rho_{2}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
for all
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|B_{1}|^{2\tau}\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}^{(\tau d)})

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{W_{n}^{2}}|B_{\|x-y\|}|^{2\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi_{n}(x-y,U,V)\big]\rho_{2}(x,y)\mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{W_{n}^{3}}|B_{\|x-y\|}|^{\tau}|B_{\|x-y^{\prime}\|}|^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi_{n}(x-y,U,V)\varphi_{n}(x-y^{\prime},U,V^{\prime})\big]\rho_{3}(x,y,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}(x,y,y^{\prime}).

We now give lower bounds for the two terms separately, starting with the first one. We use spherical coordinates and exploit that
\kappa(u,v)\geqslant 1
. Since
\rho_{2}(x,y)\geqslant K_{0}(o)^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this gives

\displaystyle\int_{W_{n}^{2}}|B_{\|x-y\|}|^{2\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi_{n}(x-y,U,V)\big]\rho_{2}(x,y)\mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{0}(o)^{2}|W_{n}^{-\varepsilon\nu_{n}}|\nu_{n}^{2\tau+1}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}t^{2\tau+1}\varphi(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Similarly, for the second integral we obtain the lower bound

K_{0}(o)^{3}|W_{n}^{-\varepsilon\nu_{n}}|\,\nu_{n}^{2(\tau+1)}\Big(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}t^{2\tau+1}\varphi(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{2}.

Hence, combining the two bounds yields the assertion.
∎

Next, we consider the variance of the subgraph count
S_{n}(G)
.

Proposition 9

(Variance lower bounds for the subgraph count)
.

For
\alpha\in[0,1]
let
\mathcal{P}
be a stationary
\alpha
-DPP, let
G
be a fixed connected graph on
q
vertices. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then for
n\geqslant 1
,

(15)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}(G))\geqslant v|W_{n}|\nu_{n}^{q-1}(1\vee\nu_{n}^{q-1}),

where

v:=\inf_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{|W_{n}^{-\varepsilon\nu_{n}/2^{d}}|}{|W_{n}|}\varphi(\varepsilon)^{2\binom{q}{2}}K_{0}(o)(\varepsilon K_{0}(o)/2^{d})^{q-1}(1\wedge q(\varepsilon K_{0}(o)/2^{d})^{q-1}).

Proof.

Let
f_{n}
be defined as in the proof of Theorem
1
. Moreover, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
\rho_{\ell}
be the
k
th product density of
\mathcal{P}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}(G))=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{q}\binom{q}{k}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}f_{n}(\mathbf{s})f_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{q-k},\mathbf{t})\rho_{q+k}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mu_{n}^{k}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t})\mu_{n}^{q}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})

(16)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q}}f_{n}(\mathbf{s})f_{n}(\mathbf{t})\rho_{q}(\mathbf{x})\rho_{q}(\mathbf{y})\mu_{n}^{q}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t})\mu_{n}^{q}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}),

where
\mathbf{s}:=(s_{1},\dots,s_{q})
and
x_{i}
is the first coordinate of
s_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,q
, and
\mathbf{s}_{q-k}:=(s_{1},\dots,s_{q-k})
(similarly for
\mathbf{t}
and
\mathbf{y}
). Since for
\alpha>0
all terms on the right-hand side in (
2
) are non-negative, it holds that
\rho_{2q}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\geqslant\rho_{q}(\mathbf{x})\rho_{q}(\mathbf{y})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{qd}
,
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{qd}
. This yields

(17)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}(G))\geqslant\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}\ \binom{q}{k}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}f_{n}(\mathbf{s})f_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{q-k},\mathbf{t})\rho_{q+k}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mu_{n}^{k}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t})\mu_{n}^{q}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}).

To bound the term with
k=0
, note that
f_{n}(\mathbf{x})
is bounded by the indicator that
x_{2},\dots,x_{q}
are all contained in a ball with volume

around

and that the WRCM built on

\varepsilon>0
is chosen such that
\varphi(\varepsilon)>0
and such that for all

and all

, the ball centred at
x
with volume

, this yields the lower bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q}}f_{n}(\mathbf{s})\rho_{q}(\mathbf{x})\mu_{n}^{q}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})\geqslant K_{0}(o)^{q}|W_{n}^{-r_{n}}|p^{\binom{q}{2}}r_{n}^{q-1}.

in (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q-1}}f_{n}(\mathbf{s})f_{n}(s_{1},\mathbf{t})\rho_{2q-1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mu_{n}^{q-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t})\mu_{n}^{q}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})\geqslant K_{0}(o)^{2q-1}|W_{n}^{-r_{n}}|p^{2\binom{q}{2}}r_{n}^{2q-2}.

∎

Remark 10
.

It is a notoriously challenging task to obtain lower bounds on the variance of geometric functionals of (stationary) determinantal point processes (see also the discussion in Remark (iii) after Theorem 1.14 in
[
1
]
). This is due to the fact that
\rho_{2q}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leqslant\rho_{q}(\mathbf{x})\rho_{q}(\mathbf{y})
for
\tau\leqslant 0
, which results in positive and negative terms in the expansion of the variance in (
16
). In the case
q=1
(where
S_{n}
is the number of points in
\mathcal{P}\cap W_{n}
) we have for
\int|K_{0}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=K_{0}(o)
that the variance of
S_{n}
grows for
n\to\infty
as the surface area of
W_{n}
, i.e.
\mathsf{Var}(S_{n})\sim|W_{n}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}
(see
[
5
]
, (4.15)). For instance, this is the case in the Ginibre process, and similar phenomena also occur for
\int|K_{0}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x>K_{0}(o)
. However, if
\int|K_{0}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x<K_{0}(o)
we have that
\mathsf{Var}(S_{n})\sim|W_{n}|
. If
q\geqslant 2
, the situation is more complex, since the asymptotic behavior of the variance of
S_{n}(G)
is determined by the asymptotic properties of
K
,
(\nu_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
as well as the profile function
\varphi
. A concrete scenario in which we can determine the growth rate of
\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}(G))
is when
q=2
,
G
is the connected graph on two vertices,
\nu_{n}\to 0
and the profile function
\varphi
has bounded support. In this case we obtain from (
16
) that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}(G))=\int_{W_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{n}(x_{1}-x_{2},U_{1},U_{2})]\rho_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{W_{n}^{3}}\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{n}(x_{1}-x_{2},U_{1},U_{2})\varphi_{n}(x_{1}-x_{3},U_{1},U_{3})]\rho_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\mathrm{d}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{W_{n}^{4}}\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{n}(x_{1}-x_{2},U_{1},U_{2})\varphi_{n}(x_{3}-x_{4},U_{3},U_{4})](\rho_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})-\rho_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\rho_{2}(x_{3},x_{4}))\mathrm{d}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}).

A detailed analysis of the three different terms that proceeds along the lines of the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
shows that the first term grows at rate
|W_{n}|\nu_{n}
, whereas the second and the third term grow at a rate that is at most
|W_{n}|\nu_{n}^{2}
. Hence, there is some
v>0
such that

\mathsf{Var}(S_{n}(G))\sim v|W_{n}|\nu_{n}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

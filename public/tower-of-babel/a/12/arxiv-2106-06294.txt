Maximum logarithmic derivative bound on quantum state estimation as a dual of the Holevo bound
Koichi Yamagata

Abstract
In quantum estimation theory, the Holevo bound is known as a lower bound of weighed traces of covariances of unbiased estimators. The Holevo bound is defined by a solution of a minimization problem, and in general, explicit solution is not known. When the dimension of Hilbert space is two and the number of parameters is two, a explicit form of the Holevo bound was given by Suzuki. In this paper, we focus on a logarithmic derivative lies between the symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD) and the right logarithmic derivative (RLD) parameterized by β ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \beta\in[0,1] to obtain lower bounds of weighted trace of covariance of unbiased estimator. We introduce the maximum logarithmic derivative bound as the maximum of bounds with respect to β \beta . We show that all monotone metrics induce lower bounds, and the maximum logarithmic derivative bound is the largest bound among them. We show that the maximum logarithmic derivative bound has explicit solution when the d d dimensional model has d + 1 d+1 dimensional 𝒟 \mathcal{D} invariant extension of the SLD tangent space. Furthermore, when d = 2 d=2 , we show that the maximization problem to define the maximum logarithmic derivative bound is the Lagrangian duality of the minimization problem to define Holevo bound, and is the same as the Holevo bound. This explicit solution is a generalization of the solution for a two dimensional Hilbert space given by Suzuki. We give also examples of families of quantum states to which our theory can be applied not only for two dimensional Hilbert spaces.

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\theta}^{i}(x){\rm Tr\,}\rho_{\theta_{0}}M_{x}

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}^{i}\qquad(i=1,\dots,d),

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\theta}^{i}(x){\rm Tr\,}\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta_{0}}M_{x}

(1.8)

{\rm Tr\,}GV_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]\geq{\rm Tr\,}GJ_{\theta_{0}}^{(R)^{-1}}+{\rm Tr\,}\left|\sqrt{G}{\rm Im}J_{\theta_{0}}^{(R)^{-1}}\sqrt{G}\right|=:C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(R)},

(1.10)

for any positive complex matrix

A

A tighter lower bound of

\displaystyle:=\min_{V,B}\left\{{\rm Tr\,}GV;\,V\text{ is a real matrix such that }V\geq Z(B),\,Z_{ij}(B)={\rm Tr\,}\rho_{\theta_{0}}B_{j}B_{i},\right.

(1.11)

\displaystyle\qquad B_{1},\dots,B_{d}\text{ are Hermitian operators on $\mathcal{H}\ $such that }{\rm Tr\,}\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}}B_{j}=\delta_{ij}\},

(1.12)

(see Appendix
B

It is known that the Holevo bound is asymptotically achievable in

,
3
,
4
]
.
Note that the minimization problem over

\displaystyle=\min_{B}\left\{{\rm Tr\,}GZ(B)+{\rm Tr\,}\left|\sqrt{G}{\rm Im}Z(B)\sqrt{G}\right|;\,Z_{ij}(B)={\rm Tr\,}\rho_{\theta_{0}}B_{j}B_{i},\right.

(1.14)

\displaystyle\qquad B_{1},\dots,B_{d}\text{ are Hermitian operators on $\mathcal{H}\ $such that }{\rm Tr\,}\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}}B_{j}=\delta_{ij}\}.

C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(R)}+S_{\theta_{0},G}&\text{otherwise, }\end{cases}

where

2
Maximum logarithmic derivative bound

Let
\mathcal{S}=\left\{\rho_{\theta};\,\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

be a smooth parametric family of density operators on a finite dimensional
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The covariance matrix
V_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]

of an locally unbiased estimator
(M,\hat{\theta})
at
\theta_{0}

satisfies the classical Cramér Rao inequality,

V_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]\geq J_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)^{-1}}

(2.1)

where

J_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)}=\left[\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{\left({\rm Tr\,}\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}}M_{x}\right)\left({\rm Tr\,}\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta_{0}}M_{x}\right)}{{\rm Tr\,}\rho_{\theta_{0}}M_{x}}\right]_{ij}

(2.2)

is the classical Fisher information matrix with respect to the POVM

M
. The equality is achieved when

\hat{\theta}^{i}(x)=\theta_{0}^{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left[J_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)^{-1}}\right]^{ij}\frac{{\rm Tr\,}\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta_{0}}M_{x}}{{\rm Tr\,}\rho_{\theta_{0}}M_{x}}.

(2.3)

Thus the minimization of
{\rm Tr\,}GV_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]
is
reduced to the minimization of
{\rm Tr\,}GJ_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)^{-1}}
for
any
d\times d
real positive matrix
G
. In this section, we consider
lower bounds of
{\rm Tr\,}GJ_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)^{-1}}
directly induced
by monotone metrics.

Let
P:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be an operator monotone function
such that
P(1)=1

[
8
]
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the set of all
linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. A monotone metric at
\theta_{0}\in\Theta

is an inner product
K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(P)}(\cdot,\cdot)
on

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
defined by

K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(P)}(X,Y)={\rm Tr\,}X^{*}\left[(\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}P(\mathbf{L}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{-1}))^{-1}Y\right],

(2.4)

where
\mathbf{L}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}
and
\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}
are
super operators on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}(X)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\theta_{0}}X,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}(X)

\displaystyle=X\rho_{\theta_{0}},

(2.6)

with a strictly positive operator
\rho_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{S}

[
6
,
7
]
.
Note that
\mathbf{L}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}
and
\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}

are commutative so the super operator
(\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}P(\mathbf{L}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{-1}))^{-1}

is well-defined. The monotone metric
K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(P)}(\cdot,\cdot)

has the monotonicity

K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(P)}(X,X)\geq K_{T(\rho_{\theta_{0}})}^{(P)}(T(X),T(X))

(2.7)

under any channel
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
mapping to another Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

The logarithmic derivative
L_{i}^{(P)}
and the Fisher information
matrix
J_{\theta_{0}}^{(P)}
with respect to
P
are

\displaystyle L_{i}^{(P)}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{R}_{\theta_{0}}P(\mathbf{L}_{\theta_{0}}\mathbf{R}_{\theta_{0}}^{-1}))^{-1}\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}}\qquad(i=1,\dots,d),

(2.8)

\displaystyle J_{\theta_{0}}^{(P)}

\displaystyle=\left[K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(P)}(\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}},\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta_{0}})\right]_{ij}=\left[{\rm Tr\,}\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}}L_{j}^{(P)}\right]_{ij}.

(2.9)

Because a linear map
T^{(M)}:X\mapsto{\rm Diag}({\rm Tr\,}XM_{1},{\rm Tr\,}XM_{2},\dots,{\rm Tr\,}XM_{|\mathcal{X}|})

(
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
) is a quantum channel, for any POVM
M
taking values
on
\mathcal{X}=\{1,2,\dots,|\mathcal{X}|\},
the monotonicity (
2.7
)
of
K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(P)}(\cdot,\cdot)
induces a matrix inequality

J_{\theta_{0}}^{(P)}\geq\left[K_{T^{(M)}(\rho_{\theta_{0}})}^{(P)}(T^{(M)}(\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}}),T^{(M)}(\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta_{0}}))\right]_{ij}=J_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)},

(2.10)

where
{\rm Diag}(\cdots)
indicates a diagonal matrix.
The inequality
(
2.10
) implies

\displaystyle{\rm Tr\,}GJ_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)^{-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{{\rm Tr\,}GV;\,V\geq J_{\theta_{0}}^{(P)^{-1}},V\text{ is a real matrix}\}

(2.11)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr\,}GJ_{\theta_{0}}^{(P)^{-1}}+{\rm Tr\,}\left|\sqrt{G}{\rm Im}J_{\theta_{0}}^{(P)^{-1}}\sqrt{G}\right|=:C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(P)},

(2.12)

due to (
1.10
).

To obtain a tighter bound, we consider
maximizing
C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(P)}
with respect to
P
.
In existing studies, such maximization was considered
in several models,
and SLD or RLD bounds were derived
[
9
]
.
In this section, we consider maximization of

C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(P)}
in general models.

In quantum state estimation,
a family of linear functions

\mathcal{F}=\left\{P^{(\beta)}(x)=\frac{1+\beta}{2}x+\frac{1-\beta}{2};\,\beta\in[-1,1]\right\},

(2.13)

is particularly important for monotone metrics among operator monotone
functions

because
the operator monotone function
P
which
maximize the lower bound
C^{(P)}_{\theta_{0}}
is always
in the family
\mathcal{F}
of functions
as we will show in Theorem
2.1
.

We write
K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(\beta)}
,
L_{i}^{(\beta)}
,

J_{\theta_{0}}^{(\beta)}
, and
C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(\beta)}
instead
of
K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(P^{(\beta)})}
,
L_{i}^{(P^{(\beta)})}
,

J_{\theta_{0}}^{(P^{(\beta)})}
, and
C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(P^{(\beta)})}
.
The
\beta
logarithmic derivative
L_{i}^{(\beta)}
is defined
by

\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}}=\frac{(1+\beta)}{2}\rho_{\theta_{0}}L_{i}^{(\beta)}+\frac{(1-\beta)}{2}L_{i}^{(\beta)}\rho_{\theta_{0}}.

(2.14)

Let
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle^{(\beta)}
be an inner
product on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
defined by

\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle^{(\beta)}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr\,}X^{*}\left\{(1+\beta)\rho_{\theta_{0}}Y+(1-\beta)Y\rho_{\theta_{0}}\right\}.

(2.15)

By using this inner product, the
\beta
Fisher information matrix
can be written as

J_{\theta_{0}}^{(\beta)}=\left[K_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}^{(\beta)}(\partial_{i}\rho_{\theta_{0}},\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta_{0}})\right]_{ij}=\left[\left\langle L_{i}^{(\beta)},L_{j}^{(\beta)}\right\rangle^{(\beta)}\right]_{ij}.

(2.16)

Note that
L_{i}^{(0)}
coincides with SLD
L_{i}^{(S)}
, and
L_{i}^{(1)}

coincides with RLD
L_{i}^{(R)}
.

Let us prove the optimality of the family
\mathcal{F}
of functions.
Because any operator monotone function

P:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is differentiable and concave
[
8
]
,
there always exists
\beta\in[-1,1]
such that

\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}P(x)\right|_{x=1}=\frac{1+\beta}{2}

if
P(1)=1
.
Since the line
P^{(\beta)}(x)
is a tangent to the concave function
P(x)
at
x=1
,

P(x)\leq P^{(\beta)}(x)

(2.17)

for any
x\in(0,\infty)
,
and this implies

J_{\theta_{0}}^{(P)}\geq J_{\theta_{0}}^{(\beta)}

(2.18)

and

C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(\beta)}\geq C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(P)}.

(2.19)

Further,
C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(\beta)}=C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(-\beta)}

because
L_{i}^{(\beta)}
is conjugate transpose of
L_{i}^{(-\beta)}
.
Therefore, we do not need to consider operator monotone functions
other than
P^{(\beta)}
for
\beta\in[0,1]
.

Collecting these results, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any locally unbiased estimator
(M,\hat{\theta})
at
\theta_{0}
,
a
d\times d
real positive matrix
G
, and an operator monotone
function
P:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
P(1)=1
and
\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}P(x)\right|_{x=1}=\frac{1+\beta}{2}

with
\beta\in[-1,1]
,

{\rm Tr\,}GV_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]\geq{\rm Tr\,}GJ_{\theta_{0}}^{(M)^{-1}}\geq C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(\left|\beta\right|)}\geq C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(P)}.

(2.20)

From this theorem, we have an inequality

{\rm Tr\,}GV_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]\geq\max_{0\leq\beta\leq 1}C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(\beta)},

(2.21)

and we call the RHS of this inequality the maximum logarithmic derivative
bound.

3
Equivalent expressions of Holevo bound

In this section, we give a simpler form of the Holevo bound by using
a commutation operator. Let
\mathcal{S}=\left\{\rho_{\theta};\,\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

be a smooth parametric family of density operators on a finite dimensional
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

be the commutation operator with respect to a faithful state
\rho_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{S}

on the set of linear operators
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
on
\mathcal{H}
defined by

\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}(X)\rho_{\theta_{0}}+\rho_{\theta_{0}}\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}(X)=\sqrt{-1}(X\rho_{\theta_{0}}-\rho_{\theta_{0}}X),

(3.1)

for
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

[
1
]
. The commutation operator can also
be defined by

\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}}(\mathbf{L}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}-\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}})(\mathbf{L}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}+\mathbf{R}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}})^{-1}.

(3.2)

When
X
is a Hermitian operator,
\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}(X)

is also a Hermitian operator. Through the commutation operator, the

\beta
logarithmic derivatives
\{L_{i}^{(\beta)}\}_{i=1}^{d}

and the corresponding inner product are linked by the following relations:

\displaystyle L_{i}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle=(I+\beta\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}})^{-1}(L_{i}^{(0)})\qquad(i=1,\dots,d),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle A,B\right\rangle^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle A,(I+\beta\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}})(B)\right\rangle^{(0)}\qquad(A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))

(3.4)

for
\beta\in[0,1]
. Note that
I+\beta\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}

is invertible for
\rho_{\theta_{0}}>0
since the operator norm of

\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}
is

\max\{\left|\frac{\lambda-\mu}{\lambda+\mu}\right|;\lambda,\mu\text{ are eigenvalues of }\text{$\rho_{\theta_{0}}$}\}<1
.
For details about the commutation operator
\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}
,
see Appendix
D
. By considering a
\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}

invariant extension
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\supset\mathcal{T}
of the SLD tangent space

\mathcal{T}:={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\,\{L_{i}^{(S)}\}_{i=1}^{d}
, the minimization
problem to define the Holevo bound is simplified as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that a quantum statistical model

\mathcal{S}=\left\{\rho_{\theta};\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

on
\mathcal{H}
has a
\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{\theta_{0}}}
invariant extension
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}

of the SLD tangent space of
\mathcal{S}
at
\theta=\theta_{0}

Let us consider the Lagrangian duality of the quadratic programming

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr\,}G\,{\rm Re}A+2\left|a\right|+2\left\langle\hat{b}\mid\hat{b}\right\rangle t+\left\langle\hat{b}\mid\hat{b}\right\rangle t^{2}-\lambda(\left|a\right|+\left\langle\hat{b}\mid\hat{b}\right\rangle t)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr\,}G\,{\rm Re}A+2\left|a\right|-\lambda\left|a\right|+\left\langle\hat{b}\mid\hat{b}\right\rangle((2-\lambda)t+t^{2}).

Proof of (

In this appendix, we give a proof of (

Lemma A.1
.

d\times d
positive complex matrix
J

,

Appendix B
Derivation of Holevo bound

In this appendix, it is proved briefly that the Holevo bound is lower
than the weighted trace of the covariance of any unbiased estimator.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\left\{\rho_{\theta};\,\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

be a smooth parametric family of density operators on a finite dimensional
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. For a locally unbiased estimator
(M,\hat{\theta})

at
\theta_{0}
and a
d\times d
positive real matrix
G
,

{\rm Tr\,}GV_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]\geq C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(H)},

(B.1)

where
C_{\theta_{0},G}^{(H)}
is the Holevo bound defined by (
1.12
).

Proof.

Let

X^{M,i}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}(\hat{\theta}^{i}(x)-\theta_{0}^{i})M_{x}.

(B.2)

It follows that

V_{\theta_{0}}[M,\hat{\theta}]_{ij}-Z(X^{M})_{ij}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}{\rm Tr\,}\left[(\hat{\theta}^{i}(x)-\theta_{0}^{i}-X^{M,i})\right]\rho_{\theta_{0}}\left[(\hat{\theta}^{j}(x)-\theta_{0}^{j}-X^{M,j})\right]M_{x},

Quantum Squeezing of Slow-Light Solitons
Jinzhong Zhu 1 , Qi Zhang 1 , and Guoxiang Huang 1,2,3

Abstract
We investigate the quantum squeezing of slow-light solitons generated in a Λ \Lambda -shaped three-level atomic system working under condition of electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT). Starting from the Heisenberg-Langevin and Maxwell equations governing the quantum dynamics of atoms and probe laser field, we derive a quantum nonlinear Schrödinger equation controlling the evolution of the probe-field envelope. By using a direct perturbation approach to diagonalize the effective Hamiltonian (where the atomic variables have been eliminated), we carry out a detailed calculation on the quantum fluctuations of a slow-light soliton, expanded as a superposition of the complete and orthonormalized set of eigenfunctions obtained by solving the Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) equations describing the quantum fluctuations. We show that due to the giant Kerr nonlinearity contributed from the EIT effect, significant quantum squeezing of the slow-light soliton can be realized within a short propagation distance. The results reported here are helpful for understanding the quantum property of slow-light solitons and for realizing light squeezing via EIT in cold atomic gases experimentally.

Introduction

Solitons, fascinating nonlinear wavepackets localized in space and time, can form in nonlinear media and propagate over long distances without distortion
Ablowitz2003

Among various solitons studied so far, optical solitons have attracted much attention and have been investigated extensively because of their important applications in optical information processing and transmission
Kivshar2003

Up to now, most of the methods for generating optical solitons are based on the excitations in far-off-resonance media (such as optical fibers). Since such media have generally very small Kerr nonlinearities, to excite the solitons intense electromagnetic radiations are needed. III.1
Quantum NLS equation

We are interested in the nonlinear quantum dynamics of the system, which needs to solve the quantum field equations (

Now we turn to investigate the quantum correction of the slow-light soliton
U_{0}

(18a)

(18b)

, which can provide a complete solution for all possible quantum fluctuations. To simplify our discussion, we use the new variables

\displaystyle{\hat{H}}_{2}=\frac{A_{0}^{2}g^{2}}{4}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\sigma\left[{\hat{w}}^{\dagger}{\hat{L}}{\hat{w}}-{\rm sech}^{2}\,\sigma\,({\hat{w}}{\hat{w}}+{\hat{w}}^{\dagger}{\hat{w}}^{\dagger})\right],

To obtain general solutions for the quantum fluctuation
{\hat{w}}
, a clear and standard way is to find a set of complete and orthogonal eigenfunctions that can be used to expand
{\hat{w}}
as a linear superposition of these eigenfunctions. This is, however, equivalent to diagonalize the Hamiltonian (

\displaystyle{\hat{w}}(s,

\displaystyle\sigma)=\sum_{j}\left[u_{j}(\sigma)\hat{a}_{j}(s)+v_{j}(\sigma)\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}(s)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dk\left[u(\sigma,k)\hat{a}(s,k)+v(\sigma,k)\hat{a}^{\dagger}(s,k)\right].

(20)

Here the first term (summation) and the second term (integration) on the right-hand side are contributed respectively from the discrete and continuum spectra of the excitations created from the soliton background;

u_{j}(\sigma)
,
v_{j}(\sigma)
,
u(\sigma,k)
,

are the corresponding eigen (or mode) functions;
\hat{a}_{j}(s)
,

,

, and
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(s,k)
are the corresponding annihilation operators of photons obeying the commutation relations

and
[\hat{a}(s,k),\hat{a}^{\dagger}(s,k^{\prime})]=\delta(k-k^{\prime})
(with other commutators zero).

The key to diagonalize
{\hat{H}}_{2}
is to find a complete set of the
eigenfunctions
\{u_{j}(\sigma),u(\sigma,k),v_{j}(\sigma),v(\sigma,k)\}
, with which the explicit form of
{\hat{w}}
can be determined. Following the
idea in Ref.

, we assume
u_{q}
and
v_{q}
satisfy
the BdG eigen equations
bdge

\displaystyle\hat{L}u_{q}(\sigma)-2{\rm sech}^{2}\sigma\nu_{q}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{q}u_{q}(\sigma),

(21a)

\displaystyle\hat{L}v_{q}(\sigma)-2{\rm sech}^{2}\sigma u_{q}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{q}v_{q}(\sigma),

(21b)

where
q=j
(

) is for the discrete (continuous) spectrum,

,

. The above BdG equations have the following analytical solutions

\displaystyle u(\sigma,k)=-\frac{k^{2}+2ik\tanh(\sigma)-\tanh^{2}(\sigma)}{\sqrt{2\pi}(k^{2}+1)}e^{ik\sigma},

(22a)

\displaystyle v(\sigma,k)=-\frac{{\rm sech}^{2}(\sigma)}{\sqrt{2\pi}(k^{2}+1)}e^{ik\sigma},

(22b)

for the continuum spectrum with eigen value

(

), and

\displaystyle u_{1}(\sigma)=\frac{2-\sigma\tanh(\sigma)}{2}{\rm sech}(\sigma),

(23a)

\displaystyle u_{2}(\sigma)=\frac{{\tanh(\sigma)+\sigma}}{2}{\rm sech}(\sigma),

(23b)

\displaystyle v_{1}(\sigma)=-\frac{\sigma\tanh(\sigma)}{2}{\rm sech}(\sigma),

(23c)

\displaystyle v_{2}(\sigma)=\frac{{\tanh(\sigma)-\sigma}}{2}{\rm sech}(\sigma),

(23d)

for the discrete spectrum with degenerate eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
.
It can be shown that all the eigenfunctions of both the continuous and the discrete spectra given above constitutes a complete set of eigenfunctions (i.e. Hilbert space), which means that they can be used to expand quantum fluctuations given by the form (
IV.1
). A detailed discussion on the above solutions is presented in Appendix
C

Based on the above analytical results and substituting (

) into (

after carrying out the integrations over

\displaystyle{\hat{H}}_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{0}^{3}g^{2}}{6}+\frac{A_{0}^{2}g^{2}}{4}\left[{\hat{P}}_{2}^{2}-{\hat{Q}}_{1}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\left.-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dk\lambda_{k}\hat{a}^{\dagger}(s,k)\hat{a}(s,k)\right].

(24)

Here
{\hat{Q}}_{j}
and
{\hat{P}}_{j}

(j=1,2)
are respectively
the coordinate operators and momentum operators related to the discrete-spectrum eigenfunctions, defined respectively by

\displaystyle{\hat{Q}}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}_{j}+\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\right),

(25a)

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}i}\left(\hat{a}_{j}-\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\right),

(25b)

which obey the commutation relation
[{\hat{Q}}_{j},{\hat{P}}_{j^{\prime}}]=i\delta_{jj^{\prime}}
.
In this way, the Hamiltonian of the system in the presence of the quantum fluctuations on the soliton background is diagonalized, which is useful for the study of quantum squeezing of the slow-light soliton.

IV.2
Quantum dynamics of slow-light solitons

Based on the diagonalized effective Hamiltonian (
IV.1
), we can easily examine the quantum dynamics of the slow-light soliton. The Heisenberg equation of motion for the operator
\hat{A}
reads

i\partial\hat{A}/\partial s=[\hat{A},\hat{H}_{\rm eff}]
. Taking
\hat{A}
to be
\hat{Q}_{j}(s)
,
\hat{P}_{j}(s)
, and
\hat{a}(s,k)
, we obtain the equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\hat{Q}_{1}=0,

(26a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\hat{P}_{1}-\frac{A_{0}^{2}g^{2}}{2}\hat{Q}_{1}=0,

(26b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\hat{P}_{2}=0,

(26c)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\hat{Q}_{2}-\frac{A_{0}^{2}g^{2}}{2}\hat{P}_{2}=0.,

(26d)

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\hat{a}(s,k)+\frac{A_{0}^{2}g^{2}}{4}\lambda_{k}\hat{a}(s,k)=0.

(26e)

It is easy to get the exact solutions of these equations, given by

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{1}(s)=\hat{Q}_{1}(0),

(27a)

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{1}(s)=(A_{0}^{2}g^{2}s/2)\hat{Q}_{1}(0)+\hat{P}_{1}(0),

(27b)

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{2}(s)=\hat{P}_{2}(0),

(27c)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{2}(s)=(A_{0}^{2}g^{2}s/2)\hat{P}_{2}(0)+\hat{Q}_{2}(0),

(27d)

\displaystyle\hat{a}(s,k)=\hat{a}(0,k)\exp(iA_{0}^{2}g^{2}\lambda_{k}s/4).

(27e)

where
\hat{Q}_{j}(0)
,
\hat{P}_{j}(0)
,
\hat{a}(0,k)
are the values of
\hat{Q}_{j}(s)
,
\hat{P}_{j}(s)
,
\hat{a}(s,k)
at
s=0
, respectively.

From Eqs. (
26
) and their solutions (
27
), we have the following conclusions: (i) The quantum fluctuations contributed by the discrete spectrum display specific characters.
The position operator
\hat{Q}_{1}
(momentum operator
\hat{P}_{2}
) remains unchanged, but it becomes correlated with the momentum operator
{\hat{P}}_{1}
(position operator

) during propagation. Such correlations between

and

(

). (ii) The quantum fluctuation for the continuum-mode

has only a simple effect, i.e. a phase shift
to the same mode caused by the Kerr nonlinearity; no correlation between different modes occurs during propagation.

By using the results (

), (

), and (

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\times\exp\left[i\frac{{\hat{P}}_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{A_{0}}}+i\frac{g\sqrt{A_{0}}}{2}{\hat{P}}_{2}(s)\tau\right],

\displaystyle\hat{A}(s,\tau)=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{g}}{2}\left\{A_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{0}}}\left[1-\frac{A_{0}g}{2}\tau\right.\right.

When obtaining this result, a renormalization technique

From the expression (

The above results can be used to obtain the numerical results of the quantum fluctuations of the soliton. Let

V
Summary

In this work, we have developed a quantum theory of slow-light solitons produced in an EIT-based atomic gas. Starting from the HLM equations which govern the quantum dynamics of the atoms and the quantized probe field, we have derived a quantum NLS equation controlling the evolution of the probe-field envelope. We have constructed an effective Hamiltonian and quantum Heisenberg equation of motion where the atomic variables have been eliminated. By exploiting a direct perturbation approach, we have diagonalized the effective Hamiltonian and carried out a detailed calculation on the quantum fluctuations of a slow-light soliton. These quantum fluctuations are expanded as a linear superposition of the complete and orthonormalized set of eigenfunctions obtained by solving the BdG equations. We have shown that, different from optical-fiber solitons, due to the giant Kerr nonlinearity contributed from the EIT effect, significant squeezing of the slow-light soliton can be obtained within a very short propagation distance both classically (i.e. squeezed soliton width) and quantum mechanically (i.e. squeezed quantum fluctuations). In addition, together with the the squeezing of the slow-light soliton, atomic spin squeezing can also be realized in the system. The results reported here is useful for understanding the quantum property of slow-light solitons and for realizing their quantum squeezing via EIT in cold atomic gases experimentally.

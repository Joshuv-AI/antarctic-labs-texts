2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Valued quivers

A quiver
Q=(I,H,s,t)
consists of the following data: a set
I
of vertices, a set
H
of arrows, and two maps
s,t:H\rightarrow I
such that any arrow
h\in H
starts at
s(h)
and terminates at
t(h)
.

A valued quiver
\Gamma=({\bf I},{\mathbf{H}},s,t;d,m)
consists of the following data: a quiver
({\bf I},{\mathbf{H}},s,t)
and two maps
d:{\bf I}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}^{+},{\bf i}\mapsto d_{\bf i};m:{\mathbf{H}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}^{+},{\bf h}\mapsto m_{\bf h}
such that for any arrow
{\bf h}\in{\mathbf{H}}
,
m_{{\bf h}}
is a common multiple of
d_{s({\bf h})}
and
d_{t({\bf h})}
. We say that
d,m
are the valuations of
\Gamma
on vertices and arrows, respectively. A quiver can be treated as a valued quiver given that the valuations are trivial (
d_{(-)}=1=m_{(-)}
).

We require in this paper that all quivers and valued quivers are finite and have no loops, that is,
I,H,{\bf I},{\mathbf{H}}
are finite sets there is no arrow sharing the same starting and terminal vertex. All quivers and valued quivers shall admit no oriented cycle (a sequence of arrows
h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{s}
such that
t(h_{r})=s(h_{r+1})
for
1\leqslant r\leqslant s-1
and
t(h_{s})=s(h_{1})
) unless stated.

An automorphism
\sigma
of a quiver
Q=(I,H,s,t)
is a pair of bijections
I\rightarrow I
and
H\rightarrow H
, both denoted by
\sigma
, such that for any
h\in H
,
\sigma(s(h))=s(\sigma(h))
,
\sigma(t(h))=t(\sigma(h))
. For example, the identity
\mathrm{Id}_{Q}
consisting of identity maps of
I
and
H
is an automorphism.

Given a quiver
Q=(I,H,s,t)
and an automorphism
\sigma
of
Q
as above, we can define a valued quiver
\Gamma(Q,\sigma)=({\bf I},{\mathbf{H}},s,t;d,m)
as follows: the sets
{\bf I}
and
{\mathbf{H}}
are respectively the sets of
\sigma
-orbits in
I
and
H
; the maps
s,t
are naturally induced; the cardinal number of a
\sigma
-orbit
{\bf i}\in{\bf I}
equals to
d_{\bf i}
; the cardinal number of a
\sigma
-orbit
{\bf h}\in{\mathbf{H}}
equals to
m_{\bf h}
.

2.3.
Cartan datum

A Cartan datum is a pair
(I,(-,-))
consisting of a finite set
I
and a symmetric bilinear form
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\mapsto(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
on the free abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}I
with values in
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that:

(a)

(i,i)\in\{2,4,6,\dots\}
for any
i\in I
;

(b)

2\frac{(i,j)}{(i,i)}\in\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}
for any
i\neq j
in
I
.

There is also an equivalent definition. A Cartan datum is a triple
(I,C,D)
consisting of a finite set
I
and two
I\times I
-matrices
C,D
in
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that:

(a)

C_{ii}=2
for any
i\in I
;
C_{ij}\in\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}
for any
i\neq j
in
I
;

(b)

D
is a diagonal matrix with positive integers in the diagonal and
DC
is symmetric.

The matrix
C
is often called a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix.

The equivalence is given as follow. For a given
(I,(-,-))
, one can obtain
(I,C,D)
by letting
C_{ij}=2\frac{(i,j)}{(i,i)}
and
D_{ii}=\frac{(i,i)}{2}
for all
i,j\in I
. On the other hand, one can obtain
(I,(-,-))
from
(I,C,D)
by setting
(i,j)
to be the
i,j
-entry of
DC
for all
i,j\in I
.

Given a valued quiver
\Gamma=({\bf I},{\mathbf{H}},s,t;d,m)
, we associate it with a generalized Cartan matrix
C=C_{\Gamma}=(C_{{\bf i}{\bf j}})_{{\bf i},{\bf j}\in{\bf I}}
defined as:

C_{{\bf i}{\bf j}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &2,&\text{if}\,{\bf i}={\bf j},\\
&-\sum_{\bf h}m_{\bf h}/d_{\bf i},&\text{if}\,{\bf i}\neq{\bf j}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here the sum
\sum_{\bf h}
is taken over all arrows between
{\bf i}
and
{\bf j}
.

Let
D=D_{\Gamma}=\text{diag}\{d_{\bf i}\}_{{\bf i}\in{\bf I}}
, then one can check that
({\bf I},C,D)
is a Cartan datum. We call it the Cartan datum of
\Gamma
. Need to remind that
({\bf I},C,D)
is independent of the orientation of
\Gamma
, that is, if we exchange the starting and terminal vertices of an arrow in
\Gamma
, we will get the same Cartan datum.

2.4.
Root system and Weyl group

For a Cartan datum
(I,(-,-))
, we can define a simple reflection
s_{i}\in\mbox{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{Z}}I)
for all
i\in I
:

s_{i}(\nu)=\nu-\frac{2(\nu,i)}{(i,i)}i.

The Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
is by definition the subgroup of
\mbox{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{Z}}I)
generated by all
s_{i}
.

Any
w\in{\mathcal{W}}
can be written as an expression
w=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\dots s_{i_{l}}
. We say this expression is reduced if
l
is minimal among all possible expressions of
w
.

Denote by
\Phi_{\rm re}={\mathcal{W}}I
the set of real roots. Then
\Phi_{\rm re}=\Phi_{\rm re}^{+}\sqcup\Phi_{\rm re}^{-}
where
\Phi_{\rm re}^{+}=\Phi_{\rm re}\cap{\mathbb{N}}I
and
\Phi_{\rm re}^{-}=-\Phi_{\rm re}^{+}
. Denote by
\Phi_{\rm im}=\mbox{\rm rad}\,(-,-)
the set of imaginary roots.

2.5.
Euler form

For a given valued quiver
\Gamma
and
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\bf I}
, we define the Euler form

\langle\nu,\nu^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{{\bf i}\in{\bf I}}d_{\bf i}\nu_{\bf i}\nu^{\prime}_{\bf i}-\sum_{{\bf h}\in{\mathbf{H}}}m_{\bf h}\nu_{s({\bf h})}\nu^{\prime}_{t({\bf h})}

and the symmetric Euler form

(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\langle\nu,\nu^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\nu^{\prime},\nu\rangle,

where
\nu=\sum_{{\bf i}\in{\bf I}}\nu_{\bf i}{\bf i},\nu^{\prime}=\sum_{{\bf i}\in{\bf I}}\nu^{\prime}_{\bf i}{\bf i}
.

One can easily check that
({\bf I},(-,-))
is a Cartan datum and equivalent to
({\bf I},C,D)
defined in Section
2.3
. Note that
\langle-,-\rangle
depends on the orientation of
\Gamma
while
(-,-)
does not.

By
[
17
]
, given
V,W\in\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
, it is known that

\langle\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,V,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,W\rangle=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma}(V,W)-\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\Gamma}(V,W).

2.6.
Quantum groups

The quantum group
{\bf U}
(also called the quantized enveloping algebra) associated to a Cartan datum
(I,(-,-))
or
(I,C,D)
as above is a
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-algebra with generators
E_{i},F_{i},i\in I
and
K_{\nu},\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}I
subject to the following relations:

(1)

K_{0}=1
,
K_{\nu}K_{\nu^{\prime}}=K_{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}
,
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}I
;

(2)

K_{\nu}E_{i}=v^{(\nu,i)}E_{i}K_{\nu}
,
K_{\nu}F_{i}=v^{(-\nu,i)}F_{i}K_{\nu}
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}I,i\in\ I
;

(3)

E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}=\delta_{ij}\frac{\tilde{K}_{i}-\tilde{K}_{-i}}{v_{i}-v_{i}^{-1}}
,
i,j\in I
;

(4)

(Quantum Serre relations)
\sum_{p+p^{\prime}=1-C_{ij}}(-1)^{p}E_{i}^{(p)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(p^{\prime})}=0
,
i\neq j
.

Here
v_{i}=v^{d_{i}},[p]_{x}=\frac{x^{p}-x^{-p}}{x-x^{-1}},[p]^{!}_{x}=[p]_{x}[p-1]_{x}\dots[1]_{x},[0]_{x}=1,E_{i}^{(p)}=E_{i}^{p}/[p]^{!}_{v_{i}}.\tilde{K}_{\pm i}=K_{\pm((i,i)/2)i}
.

Let
{\bf U}^{+},{\bf U}^{0},{\bf U}^{-}
be the
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-subalgebras of
{\bf U}
generated by
E_{i},i\in I
;
K_{\nu},\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}I
;
F_{i},i\in I
, respectively. It is well-known that there is a triangular decomposition as vector spaces:
{\bf U}={\bf U}^{+}\otimes{\bf U}^{0}\otimes{\bf U}^{-}
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]
. The integral form
{\bf U}_{\mathcal{A}}^{+}
is by definition the
{\mathcal{A}}
-subalgebra of
{\bf U}
generated by all
E_{i}^{(m)},i\in I,m\geqslant 0
.

The bar involution
\bar{}:{\bf U}^{+}\rightarrow{\bf U}^{+}
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra isomorphism such that
\bar{E_{\bf i}}=E_{\bf i}
and
\bar{v}=v^{-1}
.

2.7.

Following
[

, for any

\langle M\rangle=v_{k}^{-\mathrm{dim}_{k}M+\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{End}_{\Gamma}M}[M]

Let

be an infinite set of finite fields

3.2.
Frobenius folding

To describe
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
via
(Q,\sigma)
, we introduce the notion of Frobenius folding and refer to
[
4
]
for more details.

Let
k
be a finite field and
\bar{k}
be an algebraic closure of
k
. Let
Q
be a quiver (not necessarily of finite or affine type) with an automorphism
\sigma
. Consider the category
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q
of representations of
Q
over
\bar{k}
. By
[
5
]
, there is a self-equivalent functor
(-)^{[1]}
on
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q
such that
\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M^{[1]}=\sigma(\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M)
. The functor
(-)^{[1]}
is called the Frobenius twist functor and denote
(-)^{[n]}=((-)^{[1]})^{n}
.

For
M
in
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q
, let
r
be the minimal positive integer such that
M\cong M^{[r]}
. If
r=1
, we say
M
is
(-)^{[1]}
-stable. By
[
5
]
, there is a Frobenius map
F
on
M
such that the fixed point set
M^{F}
is in
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
and all representations in
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
can be obtained in this way (up to isomorphisms).

Such
M^{F}
is in
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
for a certain
k
-modulation. Since the isomorphism between
{\bf U}^{+}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
does not depend on which
k
-modulation we choose, we abbreviate it.
Also, the isomorphism class of
M^{F}
does not depend on the choice of the Frobenius map
F
.

Moreover, this gives a bijection

\{\text{iso-classes of }\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma\}\leftrightarrow\{\text{iso-classes of }(-)^{[1]}-\text{stable objects in }\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q\}

and a bijection

\displaystyle\{\text{iso-classes of indecomposable modules in }\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma\}\leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{(-)^{[1]}-\text{orbits of iso-classes of indecomposable modules in }\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q\}.

If a full subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q
such that
N\in{\mathcal{C}}
implies
N^{[1]}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then there is a corresponding full subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}^{F}
of
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
consisting of the objects which are isomorphic to
M^{F}
for some
(-)^{[1]}
-stable
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
and a Frobenius map
F
. We say
{\mathcal{C}}^{F}
is the Frobenius folding of
{\mathcal{C}}
. In particular,
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
is the Frobenius folding of
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q
.

3.3.
Representations of a valued tame quiver

For a tame quiver
Q
, denote by
{\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{I}}
the full subcatgory of
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q
consisting of preprojective, regular , preinjective modules, respectively. Denote by
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h}
the full subcategory of
{\mathcal{R}}
consisting of nonhomogeneous and homogeneous regular modules.

Let
K_{r}
be the cyclic quiver of
r
-vertices and
{\mathcal{K}}_{r}(f)=\mbox{\rm Rep}^{0}_{f}K_{r}
be the category of nilpotent representations of
K_{r}
for any field
f
and
r\geqslant 1
. We say a category is a tube of rank
r
over a field
f
if it is equivalent to
{\mathcal{K}}_{r}(f)
. We say a tube is nonhomogeneous if rank
\geqslant 2
and homogeneous if rank is 1.

Then
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh}
is a direct product of finitely many subcategories which are nonhomogeneous tubes over
\bar{k}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h}
is a direct product of infinitely many subcategories which are homogeneous tubes over
\bar{k}
.

For a valued tame quiver
\Gamma=\Gamma(Q,\sigma)
, let
{\mathcal{P}}^{\Gamma},{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma},{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh},{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm h},{\mathcal{I}}^{\Gamma}
be the full subcategory of
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
consisting of preprojective, regular, nonhomogeneous regular, homogeneous regular, preinjective modules, respectively. Let
{\mathcal{P}}^{F},{\mathcal{R}}^{F},{\mathcal{R}}^{F}_{\rm nh},{\mathcal{R}}^{F}_{\rm h},{\mathcal{I}}^{F}
be the Frobenius foldings of
{\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh},{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h},{\mathcal{I}}
.

We have

{\mathcal{P}}^{\Gamma}={\mathcal{P}}^{F},{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}={\mathcal{R}}^{F},{\mathcal{I}}^{\Gamma}={\mathcal{I}}^{F},

and

{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{F}_{\rm nh},{\mathcal{R}}^{F}_{\rm h}\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm h}

since a nonhomogeneous tube could be Frobenius-folded to a homogeneous tube for some
Q
and
\sigma
.

3.4.
Sequence
\underline{{\bf i}}

A doubly infinite sequence

\underline{{\bf i}}=(\dots,{\bf i}_{-1},{\bf i}_{0}|{\bf i}_{1},{\bf i}_{2},\dots)

in
{\bf I}
is called reduced if
s_{{\bf i}_{r}}s_{{\bf i}_{r+1}}\dots s_{{\bf i}_{t-1}}s_{{\bf i}_{t}}
is a reduced expression for all
r,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with

A vertex
{\bf i}\in{\bf I}
is called a sink (resp., source) if there is no arrow starting (resp., terminating) at
{\bf i}
. Let
\sigma_{\bf i}\Gamma
be the valued quiver obtained from
\Gamma
by reversing all arrows connected to
{\bf i}
and replacing
{\mathbb{M}}_{\bf h}
by its
k
-dual for
s({\bf h})={\bf i}
or
t({\bf h})={\bf i}
.
We say that a finite sequence
({\bf i}_{1},\dots,{\bf i}_{m})
is an admissible sink (resp., source) sequence of
\Gamma
if for any
1\leqslant t\leqslant m
,
{\bf i}_{t}
is a sink (resp., source) of
\sigma_{{\bf i}_{t-1}}\dots\sigma_{{\bf i}_{1}}\Gamma
.

We say that
\underline{{\bf i}}
is admissible to
\Gamma
if
\underline{{\bf i}}
is reduced and
({\bf i}_{0},{\bf i}_{-1},\dots,{\bf i}_{-m})
is admissible sink sequence of
\Gamma
and
({\bf i}_{1},{\bf i}_{2},\dots,{\bf i}_{m})
is admissible source sequence of
\Gamma
for all

.

3.5.
Preprojective and preinjective part

For a given doubly infinite sequence
\underline{{\bf i}}
which is admissible to
\Gamma
, set positive real roots

\bm{\beta}_{t}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &s_{{\bf i}_{0}}s_{{\bf i}_{-1}}\dots s_{{\bf i}_{t+1}}({\bf i}_{t}),&\text{if}\,t\leqslant 0,\\
&s_{{\bf i}_{1}}s_{{\bf i}_{2}}\dots s_{{\bf i}_{t-1}}({\bf i}_{t}),&\text{if}\,t>0.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\bm{\beta}_{0}={\bf i}_{0},\bm{\beta}_{1}={\bf i}_{1}
. Then there is a unique (up to isomorphism) indecomposable module
M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t})
in
\mbox{\rm Rep}_{k}\Gamma
for each

such that

\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t})=\bm{\beta}_{t}.

Moreover, we can classify all indecomposable module in
{\mathcal{P}}^{\Gamma}
and
{\mathcal{I}}^{\Gamma}
:

\{\text{iso-classes of indecomposable modules in }{\mathcal{P}}^{\Gamma}\}=\{[M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t})]|t\leqslant 0\},

\{\text{iso-classes of indecomposable modules in }{\mathcal{I}}^{\Gamma}\}=\{[M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t})]|t>0\}.

Note that
\bm{\beta}_{t},t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are not all of the positive real roots if
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}
is nontrivial.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
t_{1}<t_{2}\leqslant 0
or
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
, we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma}(M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t_{1}}),M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t_{2}}))=0

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\Gamma}(M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t_{2}}),M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{t_{1}}))=0

We order these real roots:

(\bm{\beta}_{0}<\bm{\beta}_{-1}<\cdots)<(\cdots<\bm{\beta}_{2}<\bm{\beta}_{1}).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{-}
(resp.,
{\mathcal{G}}_{+}
) be the set of all finitely supported functions
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leqslant 0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
(resp.,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
). For
{\bf c}_{-}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{-}
and
{\bf c}_{+}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{+}
, let

M_{k}({\bf c}_{-})=M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{0})^{\oplus{\bf c}_{-}(0)}\oplus M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{-1})^{\oplus{\bf c}_{-}(-1)}\oplus\dots,

M_{k}({\bf c}_{+})=\dots\oplus M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{2})^{\oplus{\bf c}_{+}(2)}\oplus M_{k}(\bm{\beta}_{1})^{\oplus{\bf c}_{+}(1)}.

Then we have the following classification:

\{\text{iso-classes of modules in }{\mathcal{P}}^{\Gamma}\}=\{[M_{k}({\bf c}_{-})]|{\bf c}_{-}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{-}\},

\{\text{iso-classes of modules in }{\mathcal{I}}^{\Gamma}\}=\{[M_{k}({\bf c}_{+})]|{\bf c}_{+}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{+}\}.

3.6.
Nonhomogeneous regular part

A module in a nonhomogeneous tube
{\mathcal{T}}
is called regular simple if it is simple as an object in
{\mathcal{T}}
.
To charaterize a nonhomogeneous tube

We refer to Section 6 in
[
9

By comparing
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}
with
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh}
for all types of
\Gamma=\Gamma(Q,\sigma)

Lemma 3.2
.

For a nonhomogeneous regular tube
{\mathcal{T}}
in
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}
, a regular simple
L
in
{\mathcal{T}}
can be obtained in the follow manners:

(a)

, where

are regular simples, and

(

) are different tubes in
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh}
such that
\mathrm{rk}\,T=\mathrm{rk}\,{\mathcal{T}}
and

is naturally isomorphic to identity on

(b)

L\cong(L_{1}\oplus L_{2})^{F}
, where
L_{1},L_{2}
are regular simples in the same tube
T
of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh}
, such that
\mathrm{rk}\,T=2\mathrm{rk}\,{\mathcal{T}}\geqslant 4
. Here

is a non-homogeneous tube in
{\rm Rep}_{\bar{k}}Q
.

For example,
\Gamma
of type
\tilde{F}_{41}
has a tube of rank 3, which is obtained by Frobenius-folding the two rank-3 tubes of
Q
of type
\tilde{E}_{6}
, satisfying case

.
For another example, let
n>3
,
\Gamma
of type
\tilde{BD}_{n}
has a tube of rank
n-1
which is obtained by Frobenius-folding the tube of rank
2n-2
of
\tilde{D}_{2n}
, satisfying case
(b)
.

Since
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}
is the Frobenius folding of some nonhomogeneous tubes over
\bar{k}
in
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh}
,
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}
is a direct product of some nonhomogeneous tubes.
Denote by
{\mathcal{T}}_{1},{\mathcal{T}}_{2},\dots,{\mathcal{T}}_{s}(s\leqslant 3)
the nonhomogeneous tubes in
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}
and fix the equivalences
\varepsilon_{t}:{\mathcal{K}}_{r_{t}}(k_{t})\cong{\mathcal{T}}_{t}
where

is the rank of
{\mathcal{T}}_{t}
and

is the endomorphism ring of a simple object in
{\mathcal{T}}_{t}

.

By
[
6

is one-to-one-correspondent to the set
\Pi_{r}
of (cyclic) multisegments. The set

is independent of

and its elements are of the form

with

and almost all of them being zero. We denote

Let

{\mathcal{G}}_{0}=\Pi_{r_{1}}\times\Pi_{r_{2}}\times\dots\times\Pi_{r_{s}},

then for

\{\text{iso-classes of modules in }{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}\}=\{[M_{k}({\bf c}_{0})]|{\bf c}_{0}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\}.

Note that
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
is independent of the field
k
and the choice of the equivalences
\varepsilon_{t}
’s.

A multisegment
\pi\in\Pi_{r}
is called aperiodic if, for each

, there is some

such that
\pi_{i,l}=0
. We denote by
\Pi_{r}^{a}
the set of aperiodic multisegments. Denote

{\mathcal{G}}^{a}_{0}=\Pi_{r_{1}}^{a}\times\Pi_{r_{2}}^{a}\times\dots\times\Pi_{r_{s}}^{a}.

A module
M
in
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm nh}
is called aperiodic if

is isomorphic to

for some
{\bf c}_{0}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a}

The homogeneous regular part

For

3.10.
The order
\preceq

First, we define the lexicographic orderings on
{\mathcal{G}}_{-}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{+}
as follow.
Given
{\bf c}_{-},{\bf d}_{-}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{-}
,
we say
{\bf c}_{-}>_{L}{\bf d}_{-}
if there exists

such that
{\bf c}_{-}(t)>{\bf d}_{-}(t)
and
{\bf c}_{-}(l)={\bf d}_{-}(l)
for all
t<l\leqslant 0
.
Given
{\bf c}_{+},{\bf d}_{+}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{+}
,
we say
{\bf c}_{+}>_{L}{\bf d}_{+}
if there exists
t>0
such that
{\bf c}_{+}(t)>{\bf d}_{+}(t)
and
{\bf c}_{-}(l)={\bf d}_{-}(l)
for all
0<l<t
.

Second, there is a partial order
\leqslant_{G}
on
\Pi_{r}
for each
r\geqslant 2
. For
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{r}
,
\pi\leqslant_{G}\pi^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M_{k}(\pi)=\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M_{k}(\pi^{\prime})
and

\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{r}(k)}(M,M_{k}(\pi))\geqslant\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{r}(k)}(M,M_{k}(\pi^{\prime}))

for all
M\in{\mathcal{K}}_{r}(k)
. There are several equivalent definitions for
\leqslant_{G}
and one can refer to
[
3
]
for details.

The order
\leqslant_{G}
is independent of the field

, so we can define a partial order, still denoted by
\leqslant_{G}
, on
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
such that
{\bf c}_{0}=(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{s})<_{G}{\bf c}_{0}^{\prime}=(\pi_{1}^{\prime},\pi_{2}^{\prime},\dots,\pi_{s}^{\prime})
if and only if
\pi_{t}\leqslant_{G}\pi_{t}^{\prime}
for all
1\leqslant t\leqslant s
but not all equalities hold.

Definition 3.4
.

Define a partial order
\preceq

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

4.2.
Almost orthogonality

Fix a valued quiver
\Gamma
and its Cartan datum
({\bf I},(-,-))
, let
{\bf U}
be the associated quantum group.
Define an algebra structure on
{\bf U}^{+}\otimes{\bf U}^{+}
by

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})(y_{1}\otimes y_{2})=v^{(|x_{2}|,|y_{1}|)}x_{1}y_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{2},

where
|x|\in{\mathbb{N}}{\bf I}
is the grading of homogeneous
x
on
{\bf U}^{+}
such that
|E_{\bf i}|={\bf i}
.
Let
r:{\bf U}^{+}\rightarrow{\bf U}^{+}\otimes{\bf U}^{+}
be the the algebra homomorphism such that

r(E_{\bf i})=E_{\bf i}\otimes 1+1\otimes E_{\bf i}.

The operators
{}_{\bf i}r,r_{\bf i}:{\bf U}^{+}\rightarrow{\bf U}^{+}
are defined such that

r(x)=u_{\bf i}\otimes{}_{\bf i}r(x)

plus terms of other bi-homogeneities, and
r(x)=r_{\bf i}(x)\otimes u_{\bf i}

plus terms of other bi-homogeneities.
One can deduce that

{}_{\bf i}r(xy)={}_{\bf i}r(x)y+v^{({\bf i},|x|)}x{}_{\bf i}r(y),r_{\bf i}(xy)=v^{({\bf i},|y|)}r_{\bf i}(x)y+xr_{\bf i}(y)

for homogeneous
x,y\in{\bf U}^{+}
.

By
[
15
]
, there is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
(-,-):{\bf U}^{+}\times{\bf U}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
such that

(E_{\bf i},E_{\bf j})=\delta_{{\bf i}{\bf j}}(1-v_{\bf i}^{-2})^{-1},(x,y_{1}y_{2})=(r(x),y_{1}\otimes y_{2}),(x_{1}x_{2},y)=(x_{1}\otimes x_{2},r(y))

where the form on
{\bf U}^{+}\otimes{\bf U}^{+}
is defined by
(x\otimes x^{\prime},y\otimes y^{\prime})=(x,x^{\prime})(y,y^{\prime})
.

For a fixed finite field
k
, by
[
10
]
, there is an inner product
(-,-):{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{k}(\Gamma)\times{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{k}(\Gamma)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{k})
defined by

(\langle M\rangle,\langle N\rangle)=\delta_{MN}\frac{v_{k}^{2\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{End}_{\Gamma}M}}{a_{M}},

where
a_{M}=|\mbox{\rm Aut}_{\Gamma}M|
.
This bilinear form is also well-defined on
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
and coincides with the form
(-,-)
on
{\bf U}^{+}
via the isomorphism
{\bf U}^{+}\cong{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
.

By
[
20
]
and
[
10
]
, there is a linear operator

r:{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{k}(\Gamma)\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{k}(\Gamma)\times{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{k}(\Gamma)
defined by

r([L])=\sum_{[M],[N]}v_{k}^{\langle\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,N\rangle}g_{MN}^{L}\frac{a_{M}a_{N}}{a_{L}}[M]\otimes[N],

which is also well-defined on
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
and coincides with the operator
r
on
{\bf U}^{+}
via the isomorphism
{\bf U}^{+}\cong{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
.

Note that for any
{\bf c}_{0}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
, let
L=M_{k}({\bf c}_{0})
, then
g^{L}_{MN}\neq 0
implies that
M,N
have no direct summand in
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}_{\rm h}
, therefore
r([M({\bf c}_{0})])
is well-defined. Since
r
is an algebra homomorphism,
r
is well-defined on
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\Gamma)
.

The operators
{}_{\bf i}r,r_{\bf i}:{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\Gamma)\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\Gamma)
are similarly well-defined, and their restriction on
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
coincide with
{}_{\bf i}r,r_{\bf i}:{\bf U}^{+}\rightarrow{\bf U}^{+}
, respectively.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
({\bf c},t_{\lambda}),({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}
, we have

(N({\bf c},t_{\lambda}),N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}))\in\delta_{{\bf c}{\bf c}^{\prime}}\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}+v^{-1}{\mathbb{Q}}[[v^{-1}]]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}(v).

Proof.

The proof is similar to Proposition 8.8 in
[
21
]
.
∎

Therefore we call the PBW basis
\{N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}\}
almost orthogonal.

Using the method in

, a set

Proposition 5.11
.

For each
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
in
{\mathcal{G}}^{a}

E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
such that

E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}b_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

for some

for some

.

is an

-basis of

.

The basis

Recall that

Proposition 5.12

-basis of

For

5.8.
A property of the PBW basis

We prove here a property of the PBW basis
\{E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
.

We identify
{\bf c}_{-},{\bf c}_{0},{\bf c}_{+}
with
({\bf c}_{-},0,0),(0,{\bf c}_{0},0),(0,0,{\bf c}_{+})
. Also, we simply denote
E({\bf c}_{-})=\langle M({\bf c}_{-})\rangle,E({\bf c}_{+})=\langle M({\bf c}_{+})\rangle
and
E({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})=E((0,{\bf c}_{0},0),t_{\lambda})
for
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
.

Lemma 5.14
.

For
({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a}\times{\mathbb{P}}
, we have

E({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in({\mathcal{G}}_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a})\times{\mathbb{P}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}b_{({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

for some
b_{({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. In other words,
E({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\cap{\mathcal{H}}^{*}({\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

Write
{\bf c}_{0}=(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{s})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}_{0}
. Consider

\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0})=\varepsilon_{1}(E_{\pi_{1}})*\varepsilon_{2}(E_{\pi_{2}})*\dots*\varepsilon_{s}(E_{\pi_{s}}),

where
E_{\pi_{t}}
is the PBW basis element of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}({\mathcal{K}}_{r_{t}}(k_{t}))
defined in
[
6
]
, satisfying

E_{\pi}-\langle M(\pi)\rangle\in\sum_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{lr_{t}}\setminus\Pi_{r_{t}}^{a}\atop\pi^{\prime}<_{G}\pi}{\mathcal{A}}\langle M(\pi^{\prime})\rangle,\forall\pi\in\Pi_{r_{t}}^{a}.

Since
\varepsilon_{t}
sends simple modules to exceptional modules,
\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0})
is in the composition algebra
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
.
Also, by definition of the orderings, we have

\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0})-\langle M({\bf c}_{0})\rangle\in\sum_{{\bf d}_{0}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a}\atop{\bf d}_{0}\prec{\bf c}_{0}}{\mathcal{A}}\langle M({\bf d}_{0})\rangle.

Consider the element
\tilde{H}_{m}
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(\Gamma)
whose
k
-component is

(\tilde{H}_{m})_{k}=\sum_{[M]:M\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma},\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M=m\delta}v_{k}^{-\mathrm{dim}_{k}M}[M].

In fact, we can prove that
\tilde{H}_{m}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\Gamma)
by induction on
m
and that

{\mathfrak{m}}^{m\delta}=\tilde{H}_{m}+\sum_{0\leqslant j<m}A_{j}\tilde{H}_{j}B_{j}

for some
A_{j}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{*}({\mathcal{P}}^{\Gamma})\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
and
B_{j}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}^{\Gamma})\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
. Here
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}({\mathcal{P}}^{\Gamma}),{\mathcal{C}}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}^{\Gamma})
are subalgebras of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(\Gamma)
spanned by
\langle M({\bf c}_{-})\rangle,{\bf c}_{-}\in{\mathcal{G}}^{-}
and
\langle M({\bf c}_{+})\rangle,{\bf c}_{+}\in{\mathcal{G}}^{+}
, respectively.

Let

\tilde{S}_{\lambda}=\det(\tilde{H}_{\lambda_{t}-t+t^{\prime}})_{1\leqslant t,t^{\prime}\leqslant s}

for a partition
\lambda
.
By definition, we have

\tilde{H}_{m}-H_{m}\in\sum_{({\bf c}_{0},t_{\mu}),|\mu|<m}{\mathcal{A}}N({\bf c}_{0},t_{\mu}).

Then

\tilde{S}_{\lambda}-S_{\lambda}\in\sum_{({\bf c}_{0},t_{\mu}),|\mu|<m}{\mathcal{A}}N({\bf c}_{0},t_{\mu}).

Now for
({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}_{0}\times{\mathbb{P}}
, consider

\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})=\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0})*\tilde{S_{\lambda}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\cap{\mathcal{H}}^{*}({\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma}),

then

\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})-N({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})\in\sum_{({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu})\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{P}}\atop({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu})\prec({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}N({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu}).

Assume that this lemma holds for all

({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a}\times{\mathbb{P}}
such that

({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})
.
Write

\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu})\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{P}}\atop({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu})\prec({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}\gamma_{({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu})}N({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu}),\gamma^{-}_{-}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}

then by assumption

\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})-\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a}\times{\mathbb{P}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}\gamma_{({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}E({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})-N({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})\in\sum_{({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu})\in({\mathcal{G}}_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a})\times{\mathbb{P}}\atop({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu})\prec({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}N({\bf d}_{0},t_{\mu}).

Therefore by Proposition
5.11
, we have

\tilde{E}({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})-\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{a}\times{\mathbb{P}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}\gamma_{({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}E({\bf c}^{\prime}_{0},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})=E({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda}).

The left side is clearly in
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\cap{\mathcal{H}}^{*}({\mathcal{R}}^{\Gamma})
, so is the right side.

∎

The following proposition gives a more explicit interpretation for the PBW basis.

Proposition 5.15
.

For
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we have

E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=E({\bf c}_{-})*E({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})*E({\bf c}_{+}).

Proof.

The previous lemma implies that

E({\bf c}_{-})*E({\bf c}_{0},t_{\lambda})*E({\bf c}_{+})-N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

then the proof is completed by Proposition
5.11
.
∎

Two-loop 𝒩 = 1 {\cal N}=1 SYM Amplitudes via SUSY Decomposition and Massive Spinor-Helicity Preprint: UUITP-42/23 HU-EP-23/73-RTG
Henrik Johansson, Gregor Kälin, Gustav Mogull, and Bram Verbeek

Abstract
We obtain a color-kinematics-dual representation of the two-loop four-vector amplitude in a general renormalizable massless 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 SYM theory, including internal matter as chiral supermultiplets. The integrand is constructed to be compatible with dimensional regularization and supersymmetry by employing two strategies (implicitly defining our regularization scheme): supersymmetric decomposition and matching to massive spinor-helicity amplitudes. All internal vector components inherit their D D -dimensional properties by relating them to the previously constructed D ≤ 6 D\leq 6 , 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 SQCD amplitude using supersymmetric decomposition identities of individual diagrams. This leaves only diagrams with internal matter lines as unknown masters, which are in turn constrained on D D -dimensional unitarity cuts by reinterpreting the extra-dimensional momentum components as masses for the chiral supermultiplets. We rely on the massive spinor-helicity formalism and massive on-shell 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 superspace, generalized here to complex masses. Finally, we extend the kinematic numerator algebra to include three-term identities that are dual to color identities linear in the matter Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, as well as two new optional identities satisfied by mass-deformed 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 and 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 SYM theories that preserve 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 supersymmetry. Altogether, these identities makes it possible to completely reduce the two-loop integrand to only two master numerators.

{-}i\frac{\gamma\cdot p-mP_{\rm L}-\overline{m}P_{\rm R}}{p^{2}-m\overline{m}}\,,

(20)

where the kinetic term is
\frac{i}{2}\overline{\Psi}\gamma\cdot\partial\Psi+\frac{1}{2}\overline{m}\overline{\Psi}P_{\rm L}\Psi+\frac{1}{2}m\overline{\Psi}P_{\rm R}\Psi
.
See Appendix
B
for the full Lagrangian.

Using the Majorana spinors we can, for example, compute a color-stripped three-point amplitude between two Majorana fermions and one gluon,

A_{3}(1\Psi,2\Psi,3A^{h=\pm})=\{\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\varepsilon_{3}^{\pm}|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\}=[\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\varepsilon_{3}^{\pm}|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\rangle+\langle\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\varepsilon_{3}^{\pm}|\mathfrak{q}_{2}]=\left\{\begin{matrix}x\langle\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\rangle~~~~~h=+1\\
\frac{1}{mx}[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]~~~~~~h=-1\end{matrix}\right.

(21)

where
mx=\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{3}^{+}\cdot p_{1}
. Since the spinors now include the Grassmann-odd parameters, we should demand that the color-dressed amplitude is Bose symmetric under exchange of
1\leftrightarrow 2
. Without the color factor the above amplitude is anti-symmetric, which fixes the color factor to also be anti-symmetric
T^{A}=-(T^{A})^{t}
. This corresponds to a real representation of the gauge group, which is expected since the Majorana fermions are real.

Consider three-point amplitudes between two Majorana fermions and a scalar. If the interaction is vector-like
\overline{\Psi}\Psi\varphi
, then the amplitude is

A_{3}(1\Psi,2\Psi,3\varphi)=\{\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\}=\langle\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\rangle+[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]

(22)

and if it is axial-like
\overline{\Psi}\gamma_{5}\Psi\tilde{\varphi}
, we get

A_{3}(1\Psi,2\Psi,3\tilde{\varphi})=\{\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\gamma_{5}|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\}=[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]-\langle\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\rangle\,.

(23)

If the interactions are chiral
\overline{\Psi}P_{\rm L}\Psi\phi
, or anti-chiral
\overline{\Psi}P_{\rm R}\Psi\overline{\phi}
, we get

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Psi,2\Psi,3\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{q}_{1}|P_{\rm L}|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\}=\langle\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Psi,2\Psi,3\overline{\phi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{q}_{1}|P_{\rm R}|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\}=[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]\,.

(24)

Note that all the color-stripped fermion-scalar amplitudes are symmetric under exchange of
1\leftrightarrow 2
, which means that the Majorana fermions either have to transform in a pseudo-real representation of the gauge group, or we have to introduce additional flavor structure which compensates for the anti-symmetry of the real-representation generators.

2.4
On-shell
\mathcal{N}=1
superspace

Given fermionic charges
Q_{\alpha}
,
Q^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}
, the
{\cal N}=1
supersymmetry algebra is defined by

\{Q_{\alpha},Q^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}=\sigma_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\cdot P\,,~~~\{Q_{\alpha},Q_{\beta}\}=0\,,~~~\{Q^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}},Q^{\dagger}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=0\,,

(25)

where
P^{\mu}
is the momentum operator
(as there is no R-symmetry manifest, a central charge is not required).
For the situation at hand, where the operators act on a given scattering amplitude, we may replace
Q_{\alpha}
,
Q^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}
and
P
with the total charges of the amplitude. They can then be expressed as sums over the individual charges of the on-shell one-particle states,
Q_{\alpha}=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{i,\alpha}
,
Q^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{q}^{\dagger}_{i,\dot{\alpha}}
,
P=\sum_{i}p_{i}
, thus

\{\mathfrak{q}_{i,\alpha},\mathfrak{q}^{\dagger}_{i,\dot{\alpha}}\}=\sigma_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\cdot p_{i}\,,~~~\{\mathfrak{q}_{i,\alpha},\mathfrak{q}_{i,\beta}\}=0\,,~~~\{\mathfrak{q}^{\dagger}_{i,\dot{\alpha}},\mathfrak{q}^{\dagger}_{i,\dot{\beta}}\}=0\,,

(26)

and all anti-commutators that mix particle labels
i,j
are zero.

We can explicitly realize the algebra by using the massive on-shell spinors and Grassmann variables introduced in the previous section, which gives a
holomorphic representation
of the on-shell superspace:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathfrak{q}_{i,\alpha}&=|i^{a}\rangle_{\alpha}\eta_{i,a}=|\mathfrak{q}_{i}\rangle_{\alpha}\,,\\
\mathfrak{q}_{i,\dot{\alpha}}^{\dagger}&=[i_{a}|_{\dot{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{i,a}}=-\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{i}\,[\mathfrak{q}_{i}|_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.\end{aligned}

(27)

In the last equality in eq. (
27
) we have traded the
\eta
-derivative for a two-fold
\eta
-integral acting on
[\mathfrak{q}_{i}|=\eta^{a}_{i}[i_{a}|
, which gives an integral operator that acts on a test function in the same way as the derivative operator. In general, we prefer to work with Grassmann integrals rather than derivatives.
Thus we have defined the total supercharges (or supermomenta) as

Q=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathfrak{q}_{i}\rangle\,,~~~~~Q^{\dagger}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{i}[\mathfrak{q}_{i}|\,,

(28)

where for convenience we have suppressed the Lorentz spinor indices, and
n
is the number of external particles for the amplitude.

Alternatively, we can convert to an
anti-holomorphic representation
of the on-shell superspace that uses the
\bar{\eta}
variables introduced before. To avoid confusion, we call these alternative versions of the supercharges

\overline{Q}
and
\overline{Q}^{\dagger}
:

\overline{Q}^{\dagger}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\overline{\mathfrak{q}}_{i}|\,,~~~~~\overline{Q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|i^{a}\rangle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}_{i}^{a}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\bar{\eta}_{i}|\overline{\mathfrak{q}}_{i}\rangle\,,

(29)

where
[\overline{\mathfrak{q}}_{i}|\equiv\bar{\eta}_{i}^{a}[i_{a}|
and
|\overline{\mathfrak{q}}_{i}\rangle\equiv|i^{a}\rangle\bar{\eta}_{i,a}
as defined in eq. (
2.3
). These supercharges are equivalent to the holomorphic ones, except that they act on functions that have been Grassman-Fourier transformed from
\eta
to
\bar{\eta}
space. They do not constitute additional supercharges, just a redundancy in our description. We will generally choose to work with the holomorphic
\eta
variables and, when necessary, the Grassman-Fourier transform can be used to map to
\bar{\eta}
variables.

Scattering amplitudes with massive or massless external legs that preserve
{\cal N}=1
supersymmetry will be annihilated by both
Q
and
Q^{\dagger}
, as well as the momentum operator
P
. Since
P
and
Q
are multiplicative operators (not differential or integral operators) they should appear as delta functions
(except at three points, as we will see below).
As is conventional, we define the amplitude
A_{n}
by factoring out the momentum conservation factor
\delta^{4}(P)
, but include the Grassmann delta function:

\delta^{4}(P)A_{n}=\delta^{4}(P)\delta^{2}(Q)a_{n}\,,

(30)

where
a_{n}
is a polynomial in
\eta_{i}^{a}
, such that each monomial term is a little-group invariant and of even degree
2,4,6,\ldots,2n-2
. Using the Grassmann delta function identity
\delta(\eta)=\eta
one can derive an explicit expression for
\delta^{2}(Q)
:

\delta^{2}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\mathfrak{q}_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{j}\rangle\,,

(31)

where it is important to remember that for massive external states terms of the form
\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathfrak{q}_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{i}\rangle=-m_{i}(\eta_{i})^{2}
are non-vanishing.

If all external states are massless then the sum collapses to the usual expression involving massless spinors and Grassmann variables with only one upper index,

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\mathfrak{q}_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{j}\rangle\Big|_{\rm massless}=\sum_{i<j}^{n}\eta_{i}^{1}\langle i\,j\rangle\eta_{j}^{1}\,.

(32)

The
\eta_{i}^{1}
are no longer charged under the massive little group, and instead they carry the global
U(1)
R-symmetry charge of the massless
{\cal N}=1
multiplets.
While it can be important to remember that massive and massless states are quite different in
{\cal N}=1
theories, the definition of
\delta^{2}(Q)
in this section, using the massive spinor-helicity notation parametrized as eq. (
3
), automatically incorporates these subtleties for any configuration of massive and massless legs. So, henceforth when writing
\delta^{2}(Q)
we never need to specify which legs are massless.

2.5
On-shell multiplets for
{\cal N}=1
SYM with matter

As we are interested in
{\cal N}=1
SYM with generic matter couplings,
here we list the on-shell multiplets to be encountered.
Firstly, we will have two massless on-shell
{\cal N}=1
vector multiplets:

V^{+}=A^{+}+\eta^{1}\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}\,,~~~~~~V^{-}=\lambda^{-}+A^{-}\eta^{1}\,,

(33)

where
A^{\pm}
denotes the on-shell gluon polarizations
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}
,
\lambda^{-}
are the on-shell Weyl spinors of the massless gluino.
The
V^{+}
multiplet is manifestly bosonic and the
V^{-}
multiplet fermionic,
which is a peculiarity of the fact that we are working
with an odd amount of supersymmetry.
In particular, to perform a state sum over
the physical polarizations and spinors we use
a one-dimensional Grassmann integral:

\int\!\mathrm{d}\eta^{1}(V^{+}_{\rm L}V^{-}_{\rm R}+V^{-}_{\rm L}V^{+}_{\rm R})\,,

(34)

which gives a Grassmann-even expression overall.

For massive matter we assemble the fermions and scalars into
a long chiral
{\cal N}=1
multiplet:

\Phi=\phi+\eta^{a}\Psi_{a}+(\eta)^{2}\overline{\phi}\,,

(35)

which satisfies
\Phi={\rm FT}\,\overline{\Phi}
,
assuming that
\Psi_{a}
is an on-shell Majorana spinor.
The multiplet thus has two bosonic and two fermionic degrees of freedom, and this is the smallest four-dimensional representation of massive supersymmetric matter. When considering several matter multiplets we add a flavor index to the on-shell multiplet,
\Phi^{A}
, and it is understood that the components all carry the same flavor index.
The sum over the physical states is taken care of by a two-fold Grassmann integration,

\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta\,\Phi_{\rm L}\Phi_{\rm R}\,,

(36)

and the resulting answer is little-group invariant.

In the massless limit the long chiral multiplet
\Phi

can be decomposed into two short chiral multiplets:

\Phi=\Phi^{-}+\Phi^{+}\eta^{2}\,.

(37)

The on-shell Majorana spinor
\Psi_{a}
is separated into its chiral on-shell components:

\Psi_{a}\rightarrow(-\psi^{-},\widetilde{\psi}^{+})
, so

\displaystyle\Phi^{-}

\displaystyle=\phi-\eta^{1}\psi^{-}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi^{+}

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{\psi}^{+}+\eta^{1}\overline{\phi}\,.

(38)

where have identified the helicities and little group indices as:
1=+
and
2=-
.
While these short multiplets are interesting to study separately when calculating amplitudes, ultimately they need to be assigned to a real representation of the gauge group (effectively giving back the long multiplet),
otherwise the theory has a one-loop gauge anomaly.

3
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric amplitudes

In this section we use the on-shell formalism developed in Section
2
to obtain three- and four-point
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric amplitudes.
These will subsequently be used to compute generalized unitarity cuts.
All of our amplitudes are consistent with the supersymmetric Lagrangian given in Appendix
B
.

3.1
Three-point amplitudes

We begin with the massless MHV amplitudes
for three
\mathcal{N}=1
vector multiplets.
For massless particles the spinors in eq. (
3
) reduce to

|i^{a}\rangle=\varepsilon^{ab}|i_{b}\rangle=\left({\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
|i\rangle\\
\end{array}}\right)\,,~~~~~~|i^{a}]=\varepsilon^{ab}|i_{b}]=\left({\begin{array}[]{c}|i]\\
0\\
\end{array}}\right)\,,

(39)

which implies that
|\mathfrak{q}_{i}\rangle=|i\rangle\eta_{i}^{1}

and
|\mathfrak{q}_{i}]=-|i]\eta_{i,1}

using the definitions in eq. (
8
).
The three-point amplitudes are given by familiar expressions:

\displaystyle A_{3}(1V^{-},2V^{-},3V^{+})

\displaystyle=i\delta^{2}(Q)\frac{\left\langle 1\,2\right\rangle^{3}}{\left\langle 1\,2\right\rangle\left\langle 2\,3\right\rangle\left\langle 3\,1\right\rangle}\,,

(40a)

\displaystyle A_{3}(1V^{+},2V^{+},3V^{-})

\displaystyle=-i\overline{\delta^{2}(Q)\eta_{1}^{1}\eta_{2}^{1}\eta_{3}^{1}}\frac{\left[1\,2\right]^{3}}{\left[1\,2\right]\left[2\,3\right]\left[3\,1\right]}=-i\delta(Z_{3})\frac{\left[1\,2\right]^{3}}{\left[1\,2\right]\left[2\,3\right]\left[3\,1\right]}.

(40b)

The long bar denotes CPT conjugation:
we take the complex conjugate (with
(\eta_{a})^{*}=\bar{\eta}^{a}
)
and then Grassmann-Fourier transform with respect each of the external legs (
15
).
This gives rise to the degree-one expression

Z_{3}=\left[1\,2\right]\eta_{3}^{1}+\left[2\,3\right]\eta_{1}^{1}+\left[3\,1\right]\eta_{2}^{1}

in the Grassmann variables,
which conserves supermomentum (
Q=0
) without the need for an
explicit factor
\delta^{2}(Q)
.

For two long chiral multiplets of equal complex mass
m
that interact with a vector multiplet we have two non-zero three-point amplitudes:

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Phi^{(A)},2\Phi^{(B)},3V^{+})

\displaystyle=ix\delta^{AB}\delta^{2}(Q)\,,

(41a)

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Phi^{(A)},2\Phi^{(B)},3V^{-})

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\delta^{AB}}{mx}\delta^{2}(Q)\!\left(\overline{m}\,\eta_{3}^{1}+[3{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}]\right)=i\frac{\delta^{AB}}{x}\overline{\delta^{2}(Q)\eta^{1}_{3}}\,,

(41b)

where
A
,
B
are flavor indices.
The prefactor
x
is given by

mx=\langle q|1|3]/\langle 3\,q\rangle=-\sqrt{2}(p_{1}\cdot\varepsilon_{3}^{+})=i\,A(\phi,\phi,A^{+})
,
where
q^{\mu}
is an arbitrary null momentum and the last equality defines it in terms of a standard scalar-gluon amplitude.
The first amplitude containing
V^{+}
is fixed
by the requirement it have overall Grassmann weight 2;
the second amplitude containing
V^{-}
is
derived from the first via CPT conjugation,
and is also eliminated by
Q^{\dagger}
(
28
).
The kinematic (non-flavor) part of (
41b
) has full Bose symmetry,
yet we have chosen to eliminate
\eta_{2}^{a}
inside the bracket
using supermomentum conservation
Q=0
to give a more compact expression.

The last non-vanishing massive three-point amplitude is the one between three long chiral multiplets of complex masses
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
, respectively:

A_{3}(1\Phi^{(A)},2\Phi^{(B)},3\Phi^{(C)})=i\,T^{ABC}\delta^{2}(Q)\!\left[1+\frac{1}{m_{3}}\!\left([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]\!-\!\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}\!-\!\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2}\right)\right],

(42)

where
(\eta_{i})^{2}\equiv\eta_{i}^{1}\eta_{i}^{2}

and
T^{ABC}
is an arbitrary-flavor
tensor that controls the interaction.
The Grassmann weight-4 part of the amplitude
is derived from the simpler weight-2 part via CPT conjugation;
we have eliminated
\eta_{3}^{a}
inside the bracket using
Q=0
.

Despite the apparent factors of mass in the denominators,
all amplitudes are well behaved in the massless limit.
As explained in Section
2.5
,
the long chiral multiplet decomposes into two short chiral multiplets:

\Phi=\Phi^{-}+\Phi^{+}\eta^{2}
.
From eq. (
41
)
we get the following two massless amplitudes:

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Phi^{-(A)},2\Phi^{+(B)},3V^{+})

\displaystyle=i\delta^{AB}\delta(Z_{3})\frac{\left[2\,3\right]}{\left[1\,2\right]}\,,

(43a)

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Phi^{-(A)},2\Phi^{+(B)},3V^{-})

\displaystyle=i\delta^{AB}\delta^{2}(Q)\frac{\left\langle 1\,3\right\rangle}{\left\langle 1\,2\right\rangle}\,,

(43b)

which have lower Grassmann degree by one unit compared to the massive amplitudes since we have extracted and removed an overall factor of
\eta^{2}_{2}
.

The massive three-chiral amplitude (
42
) has non-uniform Grassmann degree,
so it splits into two sectors in the massless limit.
The two massless amplitudes are

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Phi^{+(A)},2\Phi^{+(B)},3\Phi^{+(C)})

\displaystyle=i\,T^{ABC}\delta(Z_{3})\,,

(44a)

\displaystyle A_{3}(1\Phi^{-(A)},2\Phi^{-(B)},3\Phi^{-(C)})

\displaystyle=i\,T_{ABC}\delta^{2}(Q)\,.

(44b)

Here we have lowered the flavor indices in the all-minus amplitude to emphasize that in the massless limit the two cubic tensors may be taken to be distinct, though related by complex conjugation
(T_{ABC})^{*}=T^{ABC}
.

3.2
Obtaining four-point amplitudes

We build up four-point
\mathcal{N}=1
amplitudes by sewing together the three-point amplitudes derived above,
essentially using a primitive version of BCFW recursion

Britto:2004ap
;
Britto:2005fq
.
(A similar procedure was also used in refs.
Herderschee:2019ofc
;
Herderschee:2019dmc
.)
Usually one should worry about four-point contact
terms that might get lost,
both when sewing three-point amplitudes together
and when applying BCFW recursion
(encoded in the large-
z
scaling behavior).
However, in this case any such ambiguity must correspond to the freedom of adding a local dimensionless
supersymmetric four-point function to our result,
or equivalently the freedom of adding unconstrained quartic interactions to a supersymmetric and renormalizable
{\cal N}=1
Lagrangian.
The
{\cal N}=1
SYM Lagrangian has no such freedom,
hence a supersymmetric and dimensionless
four-point function that has consistent factorization limits
will give a correct four-point amplitude.

Consider sewing together two three-point amplitudes of chiral multiplets.
Assuming that only one internal chiral multiplet
(of flavor 5) contributes,

\displaystyle{\rm Sew}_{12|34}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{5}\,A_{3}(1\Phi^{(1)},2\Phi^{(2)},5\Phi^{(5)})\times A_{3}(-5\Phi^{(5)},3\Phi^{(3)},4\Phi^{(4)})

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iT^{125}T^{534}\delta^{2}(Q)\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{5}\,\delta^{2}(Q_{125})\left[1+\frac{1}{m_{5}}\left([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]-\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}-\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 153.6447pt\!\!\!\times\left[1+\frac{1}{m_{5}}\left([\mathfrak{q}_{3}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]-\overline{m}_{3}(\eta_{4})^{2}-\overline{m}_{4}(\eta_{3})^{2}\right)\right].

We have used
\delta(A)\delta(B)=\delta(A+B)\delta(B)

to extract the overall supermomentum delta function;
the leftover delta function has the argument

Q_{125}=|\mathfrak{q}_{1}\rangle+|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\rangle+|\mathfrak{q}_{5}\rangle
.
As
\eta_{5}^{a}
appears only inside
\delta^{2}(Q_{125})

we simply need to evaluate

\displaystyle\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{5}\,\delta^{2}(Q_{125})=\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{5}\,\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathfrak{q}_{5}\mathfrak{q}_{5}\rangle=-m_{5}\,.

(46)

So the sewing is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\rm Sew}_{12|34}=i\frac{T^{125}T^{534}\delta^{2}(Q)}{m_{5}}&\left(m_{5}+[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]-\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}-\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2}\right)\\
\times&\left(m_{5}+[\mathfrak{q}_{3}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]-\overline{m}_{3}(\eta_{4})^{2}-\overline{m}_{4}(\eta_{3})^{2}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(47)

The result has terms of Grassmann degree 2, 4 and 6.

The terms of degree 2 and 4 are manifestly local after expanding out the product, while the terms of degree 6 have a spurious
1/m_{5}
pole:

\displaystyle\text{degree 2}:

\displaystyle~~\delta^{2}(Q)m_{5}\,,

(48)

\displaystyle\text{degree 4}:

\displaystyle~~\delta^{2}(Q)\big([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]+[\mathfrak{q}_{3}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]-\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}-\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2}-\overline{m}_{3}(\eta_{4})^{2}-\overline{m}_{4}(\eta_{3})^{2}\big)\,,

\displaystyle\text{degree 6}:

\displaystyle~~\frac{\delta^{2}(Q)}{m_{5}}\big([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]-\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}-\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2}\big)\big([\mathfrak{q}_{3}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]-\overline{m}_{3}(\eta_{4})^{2}-\overline{m}_{4}(\eta_{3})^{2}\big)\,.

The degree-2 and 6 terms are related by CPT invariance,
which is not apparent since the complex conjugate mass
\overline{m}_{5}
is not present in either expression.
We can remedy this by using the on-shell condition of the intermediate leg:
m_{5}\overline{m}_{5}=s=(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}
.
This gives an expression that instead has a spurious
s
pole:

\text{degree 6}:~\frac{\delta^{2}(Q)\overline{m}_{5}}{s}([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]-\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}-\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2})([\mathfrak{q}_{3}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]-\overline{m}_{3}(\eta_{4})^{2}-\overline{m}_{4}(\eta_{3})^{2})=\overline{\delta^{2}(Q)m_{5}}\,.

(49)

Using the Grassmann-Fourier transform one can show this is the CPT conjugate of
\delta^{2}(Q)m_{5}
for unrestricted four-point kinematics.
We promote the result of the sewing to
an amplitude which has only an s-channel contribution:

A_{4}(1\Phi^{(1)},2\Phi^{(2)}|3\Phi^{(3)},4\Phi^{(4)})=\frac{{\rm Sew}_{12|34}}{s-\mu_{5}^{2}}\,,

(50)

where
\mu_{5}^{2}=m_{5}\overline{m}_{5}
.
Other channels can be added when needed,
but the above result already constitutes a fully consistent
partial amplitude.

Next we sew together two three-point
amplitudes where one external leg is a massless
V^{+}
vector
and the remaining ones are massive chiral multiplets:

\displaystyle-i\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{5}A_{3}(1\Phi^{(1)},2\Phi^{(2)},5\Phi^{(3)})A_{3}(-5\Phi^{(3)},3\Phi^{(3)},4V^{+})

\displaystyle\qquad=-i\frac{\langle q|3|4]}{\left\langle q\,4\right\rangle m_{3}}\delta^{2}(Q)\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{5}\delta^{2}(Q_{125})\left[1+\frac{1}{m_{3}}\left([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]-\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}-\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=-i\frac{[4|13|4]}{2p_{1}\cdot p_{4}}\delta^{2}(Q)\left[1+\frac{\overline{m}_{3}}{s}\left([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}]-\overline{m}_{1}(\eta_{2})^{2}-\overline{m}_{2}(\eta_{1})^{2}\right)\right]

(51)

\displaystyle\qquad=-i\frac{\delta^{2}(Q)([4|13|4]+\overline{m}_{1}[\mathfrak{q}_{2}4][4\mathfrak{q}_{3}]+\overline{m}_{2}[\mathfrak{q}_{3}4][4\mathfrak{q}_{1}]+\overline{m}_{3}[\mathfrak{q}_{1}4][4\mathfrak{q}_{2}])}{2p_{1}\cdot p_{4}}\,.

For simplicity we have set
T^{123}=1
and the mass of particle 5 is in this case
m_{3}
.
In the last step of eq. (
3.2
) some non-trivial simplifications have brought it to a form that can be promoted to unrestricted kinematics. It is easiest to use an ansatz for this step, where the ansatz is constrained by crossing symmetry of the massive legs. The denominator of the sewing can be recognized as the
t
-channel pole, and the color-ordered amplitude is

A_{4}(1\Phi^{(1)},2\Phi^{(2)},3\Phi^{(3)},4V^{+})\!=\!-i\delta^{2}(Q)\frac{[4|13|4]\!+\!(\overline{m}_{1}[\mathfrak{q}_{2}4][4\mathfrak{q}_{3}]+{\rm cyclic}(123))}{(s-\mu_{3}^{2})(t-\mu_{1}^{2})}\,,

(52)

where
\mu_{i}^{2}=m_{i}\overline{m}_{i}
is the invariant mass square of the internal particles.

As our final example, consider sewing together the massless vector of the three-point amplitudes eqs. (
41a
) and (
41b
). The algebraic steps are not enlightening for this calculation, and it is instead straightforward to write down a very simple ansatz of the correct form. The result has to be local and antisymmetric in legs
1\leftrightarrow 2
and
3\leftrightarrow 4
, which gives

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-i\sum_{h=\pm 1}\int\!\mathrm{d}\eta_{5}^{1}A_{3}(1\Phi^{(1)},2\Phi^{(1)},5V^{h})A_{3}(-5V^{-h},3\Phi^{(3)},4\Phi^{(3)})\\
&\qquad=i\delta^{2}(Q)\big([\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{3}]-[\mathfrak{q}_{2}\mathfrak{q}_{3}]-[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]+[\mathfrak{q}_{2}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]\big)\,.\end{aligned}

(53)

From this we can define an
s
-channel partial amplitude:

A_{4}(1\Phi^{(1)},2\Phi^{(1)}|3\Phi^{(3)},4\Phi^{(3)})=i\delta^{2}(Q)\frac{[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{3}]-[\mathfrak{q}_{2}\mathfrak{q}_{3}]-[\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]+[\mathfrak{q}_{2}\mathfrak{q}_{4}]}{s}\,,

(54)

which is fully gauge invariant without the need to add other channels.

3.3
Four-point amplitudes

Let us now summarize the four-point color-ordered amplitudes.

Any four-point amplitude with at least one massless vector can be shown to obey the rules of color-kinematics duality.

\displaystyle\frac{s-\mu^{2}_{3}}{u-\mu^{2}_{2}}A_{4}(1\Phi,2\Phi,3\Phi,4V)\,.

4.1
Supersymmetric decomposition

The idea behind supersymmetric decomposition is that we can recycle known supersymmetric amplitudes when constructing new amplitudes with less supersymmetry. This works because most of the necessary ingredients are already present in the maximally supersymmetric
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory,
albeit packaged so as to manifest the supersymmetry.
Furthermore, the amplitudes with a high degree of supersymmetry will have properties that makes them easier to calculate, hence they will in general be determined before those of lower supersymmetry.

Here we elaborate on two decomposition routes
for obtaining
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM amplitudes starting from
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM.
The first uses
\mathcal{N}=2

(\mathcal{N}=4)\xrightarrow{SU(4)\rightarrow SU(2)\times U(1)}(\mathcal{N}=2)\xrightarrow{SU(2)\rightarrow U(1)}(\mathcal{N}=1)\,,

(70)

(\mathcal{N}=4)\xrightarrow{SU(4)\rightarrow U(1)\times SU(3)}(\mathcal{N}=1)\,.

(71)

\mathcal{N}=2

SYM theories.

4.2
Color-kinematics duality — general considerations

The idea of color-kinematics duality is to express an
L
-loop
D
-dimensional gauge theory amplitude in terms of only cubic graphs,

\mathcal{A}_{m}^{(L)}=i^{L-1}g_{\text{YM}}^{m+2L-2}\sum_{i\in\{\text{cubic graphs}\}}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{LD}\ell}{(2\pi)^{LD}}\frac{1}{S_{i}}\frac{n_{i}c_{i}}{D_{i}}\,,

(85)

with color factors
c_{i}
, kinematic numerators
n_{i}
, propagator denominators
D_{i}
and symmetry factors
S_{i}
. Then impose that the kinematic numerators fulfil the same Lie-algebraic relations as the color factors, such as the three-term relations coming from Jacobi identities,

\displaystyle c_{i}=c_{j}-c_{k}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad n_{i}=n_{j}-n_{k}\,.

(86)

Kinematic numerators that obey color-kinematics duality are called BCJ numerators, or sometimes color-dual numerators. While eq. (
85
) has several ingredients that depend on the graph, the only a priori undetermined quantities are the kinematic numerators
n_{i}
. The quantities
c_{i}
,
D_{i}
and
S_{i}
are computed by standard Feynman-diagrammatic methods and are considered trivial for our purposes. Due to the richness of algebraic identities between BCJ numerators — possibly supplemented by other relations such as supersymmetric decomposition — the number of independent numerators can be observed to be surprisingly small. Any subset of numerators that can be used to generate all others are called
master numerators
. In later sections we will see how they can be used to greatly simplify the construction of gauge theory multi-loop amplitudes, which is a sufficient reason for studying color-kinematics duality.

A second central reason for finding BCJ numerators is that they can be used to construct gravitational amplitudes through the double copy. Replacing
c_{i}\rightarrow\tilde{n}_{i}
and
g_{\text{YM}}\rightarrow\kappa/2
(the gravitational coupling) in eq. (
85
) gives the gravitational amplitude

\mathcal{M}_{m}^{(L)}=i^{L-1}\Big(\frac{\kappa}{2}\Big)^{m+2L-2}\sum_{i\in\{\text{cubic graphs}\}}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{LD}\ell}{(2\pi)^{LD}}\frac{1}{S_{i}}\frac{n_{i}\tilde{n}_{i}}{D_{i}}\,,

(87)

where
n_{i}
and
\tilde{n}_{i}
are sets of cubic-diagram numerators that may come from two different gauge theories. At least one set of numerators must manifestly obey the duality eq. (
86
), but both sets must come from gauge theories that in principle admit the construction of such numerators.

The reason for imposing constraints on the numerators is that the gravitational amplitude must be invariant under a linearized diffeomorphism transformation,
\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu}
, applied to any external graviton state. Since in the double copy an external graviton state is obtained as a (symmetrized) tensor product of two vector polarizations, a linearized diffeomorphism transformation can be obtained by sending
\varepsilon_{\mu}\rightarrow p_{\mu}
in one set of numerators, say
\tilde{n}_{i}
:
9
9

9

Since the graviton state is symmetric in the Lorentz indices, the transformation should also be applied to the other copy,
n_{i}\big|_{\varepsilon_{\nu}\rightarrow p_{\nu}}
, and then the two transformations may be added.

\sum_{i}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{LD}\ell}{(2\pi)^{LD}}\frac{1}{S_{i}}\frac{n_{i}(\tilde{n}_{i}\big|_{\varepsilon_{\mu}\rightarrow p_{\mu}})}{D_{i}}=0\,.

(88)

This identity is mapped through the double copy, using
n_{i}\rightarrow c_{i}
, to the corresponding Ward identity of the gauge theory,

\sum_{i}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{LD}\ell}{(2\pi)^{LD}}\frac{1}{S_{i}}\frac{c_{i}(\tilde{n}_{i}\big|_{\varepsilon_{\mu}\rightarrow p_{\mu}})}{D_{i}}=0\,.

(89)

So from this consideration we can determine all the algebraic identities that the numerators
n_{i}
must obey,
and they will simply correspond to the necessary Lie-algebra identities that the color factors
c_{i}
must obey in order to guarantee gauge invariance of the gauge theory amplitude.

We use a diagrammatic notation to represent both the kinematical numerators
n_{i}
and color factors
c_{i}
.
When required the (internal and external) legs may be dressed with momentum labels denoting the functional dependence of the corresponding numerator.
Furthermore, many of the diagrammatic identities are to be applied to (tree-level) subgraphs.
Meaning that the numerators, whose subgraphs are related by such identities, obey the corresponding functional constraint.

4.5
Diagram and flavor symmetries of
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM numerators

When working with loop-level numerators it is best assumed that they
fulfill the same automorphism symmetries as the corresponding cubic graphs,
which are labeled by (loop) momenta and particle types.
In physics language we refer to such constraints as imposing (manifest)

crossing symmetry
on the graphs,
as this will guarantee the standard crossing symmetry of the full amplitude
after summing over all graphs and integrating over the loop momenta.
The main practical advantage of having crossing-symmetric numerators is
that we have fewer graphs to work with,
and also fewer consistency checks to perform as most such checks
are related by trivial relabellings.

An example of a crossing symmetry constraint for the double-box (db) diagram is

n_{\mathrm{db}}(1234;\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\hbox{\lower 18.56606pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/boxboxSymm1}}}=\hbox{\lower 18.56606pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/boxboxSymm2}}}=n_{\mathrm{db}}(2143;-\ell_{1},-\ell_{2})\,.

(95)

There are similar (and more complex) constraints for other graphs,
with and without matter, planar and non-planar diagrams.

Next, there are isomorphism identities that relate two different graphs,
which arise due to the existence of a symmetry that is not manifest.
In our case it is the flavor symmetry
that may or may not be broken by the mass spectrum of the chiral matter.

5.5
Two-loop cuts: merging dimensional regularization and SUSY

It is well known that
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM does not have a dimensional uplift to a well-defined supersymmetric theory in
D>4
dimensions.
This property is sometimes naively generalised to the statement that dimensional regularization is not compatible with supersymmetry.
However, such a statement is too broad to be true in general.
There are certainly some supersymmetric theories (
\mathcal{N}>1
) that are fully compatible with dimensional regularization in the dimensional reduction scheme.
The dimensional reduction scheme requires that the integrand is constructed for the higher-dimensional theory and then the dimensional reduction back to four dimensions is parametrised with
\epsilon=(4-D)/2
.

For
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM such a scheme appears not to be available since the higher-dimensional integrand cannot be constructed following a unique prescription.
Nevertheless, one is allowed to supplement the physical constraints that come from the four-dimensional theory with auxiliary constraints that select terms compatible with a higher-dimensional uplifts.
Such constraints and higher-dimensional terms will then be part of the scheme dependence of the regularization.
The auxiliary constraints should ideally be tied to a higher-dimensional theory, such that the usual theorems and properties of a quantum field theory are built in to the higher-dimensional contributions.

For the dimensional regularization scheme used in this paper we demand that the following properties are satisfied:

•

supersymmetric decomposition identities to
D>4

\mathcal{N}=2
SYM obeyed,

•

color-kinematics duality is obeyed for the
D
-dimensional integrand,

•

power counting for the
D
-dimensional integrand is the same as the
D=4
integrand,

•

higher-dimensional momentum is compatible with massive matter amplitudes.

The last point is realized here by matching to cuts in a massive theory.
These cuts include cases where the internal matter are
\mathcal{N}=1
vector multiplets and
\mathcal{N}=1
chiral long multiplets.

5.8
Chiral short matter multiplets

Two-loop numerators associated to short chiral multiplets have not been considered in this paper, but in principle their four-dimensional integrand-level contributions could be computed. While such chiral contributions would give rise to gauge anomalies, and are thus not desirable in a gauge theory, it turns out that they are important for the double-copy construction of supergravity amplitudes
Johansson:2014zca
. Short chiral multiplet gives the ability to better control the supergravity matter content, and the double copy of two chiral contribution need not be anomalous
Johansson:2014zca
. Below we briefly comment on the needed ingredients in their construction.

Numerators coming from short chiral multiplets can be treated analogous to the
\mathcal{N}=2
computations in refs.
Johansson:2017bfl
;
Kalin:2018thp
;
Kalin:2019vjc
;
Kalin:2019qup
after introducing arrows on the matter lines.
We have checked that there are five master numerators – depicted in figure
5
– which is substancially larger than for the amplitude with long chiral multiplets.
The chief reason for this is that in the decomposition identities the matter graphs appear twice with a different momentum configuration and hence this equation cannot be used to functionally solve for them.
Instead, the decomposition identity can be used as a constraint for the ansatz. The free parameters can be chosen, such that the relation between the long and short multiplets eq. (
82
) manifestly holds,
i.e. these numerators add up to the four-dimensional part of the long chiral multiplet numerators presented in the previous subsection.

We leave the computation of the extra-dimensional part of these amplitudes, needed for certain double-copy constructions of pure-supergravity amplitudes

6
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we have used color-kinematics duality to obtain the integrand for the four-vector two-loop

\mathcal{N}=1
SYM amplitude, coupled to chiral matter multiplets,
in arbitrary representations of the gauge group, with chiral self-interactions (see detailed Lagrangians in App.
B
).
Our starting point was a generalization of
the massive spinor-helicity formalism
Arkani-Hamed:2017jhn

and associated on-shell
\mathcal{N}=1
superspace

Herderschee:2019ofc
;
Herderschee:2019dmc

to include complex masses (see Section
2
).
This included a supersymmetric depiction of the long chiral multiplet
\Phi
,
containing a complex scalar and Majorana fermion.
It blends conveniently with the usual massless on-shell formalism which we
used to describe the
\mathcal{N}=1
vector multiplets
V^{\pm}
.

Equipped with the on-shell formalism we proceeded to obtain tree-level amplitudes.
First we wrote down three-point amplitudes involving various combinations
of long chiral multiplets and massless
\mathcal{N}=1
vector multiplets
V^{\pm}

using elementary symmetry considerations (Section
3.1
).
From these we extracted four-point amplitudes via on-shell recursion (Section
3.2
),
correctly capturing the contact terms, such that it matches the unique supersymmetric
\mathcal{N}=1
theory presented in Appendix
B
.
This Lagrangian provided a useful check on the amplitudes;
furthermore we found that the three- and four-point amplitudes satisfy massive versions of the tree-level BCJ amplitude relations, or for pure-matter graphs, the kinematic Jacobi identity
Bern:2008qj
.

Following the by-now standard procedure for
obtaining loop amplitudes via generalized unitarity,
we sewed together the massive tree-level amplitudes to obtain two-loop cuts.
These provided the physical data needed to constrain an ansatz
for the kinematic color-dual numerators involved in the two-loop amplitude.
An important subtlety here was our re-interpretation of masses
in the tree-level amplitudes as extra-dimensional
components of loop momenta in dimensional regularization.
We found that, while the two can usually be straightforwardly identified,
in one particular instance a difference between the kinematic Jacobi identities
in the massive and extra-dimensional settings
creates tension in the numerator relations.
We resolved this tension by introducing a simple
\mu
-term deformation of the cuts
which we associate with the scheme dependence of dimensional regularization — see Section
5.5
for further details.

Also crucial to finding a two-loop color-dual representation was our use of

supersymmetric decomposition identities

(see Sections
4.1
and
4.6
).
Using double-box numerators as a simple example,
one can demand that the
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM numerators with long chiral matter multiplets (unarrowed lines) satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl1}}}&=\hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl2}}}+\hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl3}}}+\hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl4}}}+\hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl5}}}\,,\\

\hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl9}}}&=\hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl10}}}+\hbox{\lower 17.35493pt\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/decompConcl11}}}\,,\end{aligned}

(119)

where colors are used to represent distinct flavor.
Given prior knowledge of the
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD

(for example an
\mathcal{N}=1
SQCD amplitude)
diagrams of this kind are still useful,

These identities were supplemented by other useful off-shell relations.
The presence of cubic matter self-interactions with generic Clebsch-Gordan
coefficients
C_{ijk}
led us to a three-term identity (

whose appearance greatly resembles the Jacobi and commutation relations
demanded by color-kinematics duality involving matter.
Following ref.

we also found it useful to impose a two-term
identity between between graphs with complex matter (

which led to another three-term identity when combined to the full chiral multiplet.
Finally, inspired by the tree-level amplitudes,
we imposed another three-term identity with two pairs of differently flavored

Having obtained an integrand for the two-loop four-vector
amplitude of generic
{\mathcal{N}=1}
SYM with added chiral multiplets,
we aim to use this result to compute the full integrated
{\mathcal{N}=1}
SQCD amplitude
— an example of particular interest among such theories due to its similarity to QCD.
In ref.
DelDuca:2017peo
it was found that —
when tuned to a particular number of chiral multiplets —
this theory obeys the principle of maximal transcendentality
at next-to-leading logarithmic order in the high-energy limit.
It would be interesting to see how much of
this simplicity survives beyond the high-energy limit,
as the analogously tuned result for
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD was observed to break maximal
transcendentality in a very simple way

;
Leoni:2015zxa
;
Duhr:2019ywc
;
Kalin:2019vjc
.
Furthermore, for the two-loop four-vector amplitude in
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD for a generic
number of hypermultiplets a particular choice of infrared subtraction motivated by
structure of the integrand
Kalin:2018thp
;
Kalin:2019vjc
resulted in a finite
amplitude with enhanced transcendentality behavior
Duhr:2019ywc
;
Kalin:2019vjc
.
It would be interesting to see if a similar analysis
is feasible for the
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric theory.

Finally, this amplitude provides us with an excellent means to
study a wider class of two-loop supergravity amplitudes in different dimensions —
and in particular their ultraviolet behavior.
While two-loop Einstein gravity amplitudes are known
to be divergent in five dimensions due to an allowed
R^{4}
counterterm
Goroff:1985th
;
vandeVen:1991gw
,
five-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
supergravity is finite
Bern:2013qca

despite such a counterterm not being ruled out by symmetry considerations

Bossard:2011tq
;
Bossard:2012xs
;
Bossard:2013rza
;
Bern:2010tq
.
An interesting question is therefore whether this “enhanced cancellation”
persists for theories with
0<\mathcal{N}<4
supersymmetries
By double copy with other two-loop
\mathcal{N}<4
gauge theory amplitudes —
such as this one or the two-loop
\mathcal{N}=2
amplitude discussed here and in
refs.
Johansson:2017bfl
;
Kalin:2018thp
;
Kalin:2019vjc
—
we can potentially answer this and other interesting questions.
We emphasize that in order to compute arbitrary matter-coupled gravity theory amplitudes via the double copy one requires gauge-theory amplitudes with short chiral matter multiplets.
Whereas the four-dimensional contributions of these amplitudes can be computed with the same techniques as discussed here, the extra-dimensional pieces, if needed, are not well defined. We leave the computation of these pieces and the study of the double copy for future work.

Finally, we note that with the amplitudes computed here, we are only one step away, using a final superymmetric decomposition, to the non-supersymmetric QCD and pure-gravity amplitudes at two loops. While these amplitudes are well known
Bern:2017puu
;
Abreu:2020lyk
, the interesting testbed question is if the current approaches can be streamlined to the point that they become competitive for non-supersymmetric gauge and gravity theories at two loop and beyond. Indeed, we expect that same techniques and ideas presented here will be directly applicable to such computations.

To obtain a more useful Lagrangian from which one can derive Feynman rules

(133)

This diagonalizes the quadratic terms on
\mathrm{U}^{A}
and
\varphi^{A}

gives rise to the following Lagrangians:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\mathcal{N}=1\text{ m. matter}}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\bigg(|D_{\mu}\varphi^{A}|^{2}+\widetilde{\psi}^{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,i\,/\penalty\hskip-8.53581ptD^{\dot{\alpha}\alpha}\psi^{A}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\overline{m}_{A}\psi^{\alpha A}\psi^{A}_{\alpha}+m_{A}\widetilde{\psi}^{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}\widetilde{\psi}^{\dot{\alpha}A}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-|m_{A}\varphi^{A}|^{2}+g\overline{\varphi}^{A}\lambda^{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}^{A}-g\varphi^{A}\widetilde{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}\widetilde{\psi}^{\dot{\alpha}A}+g\overline{\varphi}^{A}\mathrm{D}\varphi^{A}+|\mathrm{U}^{A}|^{2}\bigg)\,,

(134)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\mathcal{N}=1\text{ Yukawa}}

\displaystyle=g{T}_{ABC}{\rm Tr}\bigg(\overline{m}_{A}\varphi^{A}\overline{\varphi}^{B}\overline{\varphi}^{C}-m_{A}\overline{\varphi}^{A}\varphi^{B}\varphi^{C}-\varphi^{A}\psi^{\alpha B}\psi_{\alpha}^{C}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\overline{\varphi}^{A}\widetilde{\psi}^{B}_{\dot{\alpha}}\widetilde{\psi}^{\dot{\alpha}C}+\varphi^{A}\varphi^{B}\mathrm{U}^{C}-\overline{\varphi}^{A}\overline{\varphi}^{B}\overline{\mathrm{U}}^{C}\bigg)\,,

(135)

which one may add to the pure

\mathcal{N}=1
SYM with adjoint massive matter.
The propagators for
\mathrm{D}
and
\mathrm{U}^{A}

and their contributions give rise to four-point contact terms.

\displaystyle|p\rangle_{\alpha}\,~~~~\psi_{\alpha}(p)\rightarrow|p^{a}\rangle_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\overline{m}}\,p_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}|p^{a}]^{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(136)

In this convention,
\lambda
is a negative helicity spinor, and
\tilde{\lambda}
is a positive helicity spinor.

The on-shell multiplets are given by

\displaystyle V^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{+}+\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}\eta^{1}\,,~~~~~V^{-}=\lambda^{-}+A^{-}\eta^{1}

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\cal N}=1\,{\rm long}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi+\Psi_{a}\eta^{a}+\overline{\varphi}(\eta)^{2}\,,

(137)

where the
SU(2)

and
2=-
.
The
\Psi_{a}
is an on-shell Majorana spinor, which is a superposition of the on-shell Weyl spinors:
\Psi_{a}\sim\psi_{a}+\widetilde{\psi}_{a}
.
In the massless limit one can decompose the Majorana spinor into its chiral fields, of which only the following survive on-shell:

\psi\rightarrow\psi_{1}=-\psi^{-}
and
\widetilde{\psi}\rightarrow\widetilde{\psi}_{2}=\widetilde{\psi}^{+}

B.2
Massive
{\cal N}=1
theories from SUSY decomposition

Let us now specify to a particular
\mathcal{N}=1
theory with adjoint matter, which amounts to picking a specific form of the superpotential eq. (
132
). The theory we will consider is
\mathcal{N}=1^{*}
, which is of particular importance to the supersymmetric decomposition used throughout this paper, as it can be interpreted as a massive deformation of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. The theory consists of an
\mathcal{N}=1
gauge multiplet with three adjoint massive chiral multiplets
\varphi^{A}
. Its superpotential is given by

W_{\mathcal{N}=1^{*}}={\rm Tr}\left(\frac{\overline{m}_{A}}{2}\varphi^{A}\varphi^{A}+\frac{g}{3}{\epsilon}_{ABC}\varphi^{A}\varphi^{B}\varphi^{C}\right)\,,

(139)

from which we see that the
{\cal N}=1^{*}
Yukawa interaction is proportional to the Levi-Civita tensor
{\epsilon}_{ABC}
of
SO(3)
. We decompose the
{\cal N}=1^{*}
SYM Lagrangian as before:

{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1^{*}}={\cal L}_{{\rm pure}\,\,{\cal N}=1\,{\rm SYM}}+{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1\,{\text{m. matter}}}+{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1^{*}\,{\rm Yukawa}}\,,

(140)

where the pure
{\cal N}=1
SYM theory is given by eq. (
127
).
The matter Lagrangian is identical for the 3 flavors of chiral multiplets, and is given by eq. (
B.1
).
As indicated by eq. (
139
), the Yukawa interactions are given by

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1^{*}\,{\rm Yukawa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\epsilon_{ABC}{\rm Tr}\Big(\overline{m}_{A}\varphi^{A}\overline{\varphi}^{B}\overline{\varphi}^{C}-m_{A}\overline{\varphi}^{A}\varphi^{B}\varphi^{C}-\varphi^{A}\psi^{\alpha B}\psi_{\alpha}^{C}

(141)

\displaystyle\hbox{}~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\overline{\varphi}^{A}\widetilde{\psi}^{B}_{\dot{\alpha}}\widetilde{\psi}^{{\dot{\alpha}}C}+\varphi^{A}\varphi^{B}\mathrm{U}^{C}-\overline{\varphi}^{A}\overline{\varphi}^{B}\overline{\mathrm{U}}^{C}\Big)\,,

where we may normalize by setting
\epsilon_{123}=1
.
Much like one can obtain
\mathcal{N}=1^{*}
from a massive deformation of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, one could also obtain a massive
\mathcal{N}=1
theory by deforming
\mathcal{N}=2
SYM. Let us call this theory
\mathcal{N}=1^{**}
SYM. Its matter content is given by an
\mathcal{N}=1
gauge multiplet with a single massive adjoint chiral multiplet. The superpotential is given by

W_{\mathcal{N}=1^{**}}={\rm Tr}\left(\frac{\overline{m}}{2}\varphi\varphi\right)\,,

(142)

from which we see that the Lagrangian is determined entirely by eqs. (
127
) and (
B.1
),

{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1^{**}}={\cal L}_{{\rm pure}\,\,{\cal N}=1\,{\rm SYM}}+{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1\,{\text{m. matter}}}\,,

(143)

and so the theory corresponds to restricting
\mathcal{N}=1^{*}
to a single flavor.

Finally, let us comment on what
\mathcal{N}=2
theories look like in our
{\cal N}=1
language. Note that the pure
{\cal N}=2
SYM theory is given by

{\cal L}_{{\rm pure}\,\,{\cal N}=2\,{\rm SYM}}={\cal L}_{{\rm pure}\,\,{\cal N}=1\,{\rm SYM}}+{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1\,{\rm matter}}

(144)

which, eliminating the auxiliary fields
{\rm D}
and
{\rm F}
via their equations of motion, yields

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{{\rm pure}\,\,{\cal N}=2\,{\rm SYM}}={\rm Tr}\Big(

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+|D_{\mu}\varphi|^{2}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}A}\,i\,[\,/\penalty\hskip-8.53581ptD^{\dot{\alpha}\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}^{A}]

\displaystyle+g\lambda^{\alpha 1}[\lambda_{\alpha}^{2},\overline{\varphi}]-g\widetilde{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}1}[\widetilde{\lambda}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{2},\varphi]-\frac{g^{2}}{4}[\overline{\varphi},\varphi]^{2}\Big)\,,

(145)

where we have changed notation for the fermions
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{1}
,
\psi\rightarrow\lambda_{2}
to make the R-symmetry manifest.

The matter part of
{\cal N}=2^{*}
SYM – the
\mathcal{N}=2

\mathcal{N}=4
SYM – is

{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=2\,{\rm matter}}=\sum_{A=2}^{3}{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1\,{\rm m.matter}}+{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1\,{\rm Yukawa}}\,.

(146)

And thus one may compose

{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=2^{*}}={\cal L}_{{\rm pure}\,\,{\cal N}=2\,{\rm SYM}}+{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=2\,{\text{matter}}}\,,

(147)

Changing the
\mathcal{N}=2

N_{f}
would land on the theory known as
{\cal N}=2

B.3
The Lagrangian for
{\cal N}=1

Another
\mathcal{N}=1
gauge theory of particular interest is
\mathcal{N}=1
super-QCD (SQCD), which constitutes of
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM coupled to
N_{f}

\mathcal{N}=1

\Phi

\widetilde{\Phi}
respectively. We may use them to compose on-shell massive
\mathcal{N}=1

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{N}=1\text{ long}}

\displaystyle=\varphi+\Psi_{a}\eta^{a}+\overline{\widetilde{\varphi}}\,(\eta)^{2}\,,

(148)

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}_{\mathcal{N}=1\text{ long}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\varphi}+\overline{\Psi}^{a}{\eta}_{a}+\overline{\varphi}\,(\eta)^{2}\,,

(149)

where the Dirac spinors
\Psi

\overline{\Psi}

\Psi_{a}=\left(\begin{matrix}\psi\\
\overline{\widetilde{\psi}}\end{matrix}\right)\,,\qquad\overline{\Psi}^{a}=\left(\begin{matrix}\widetilde{\psi}&&\overline{\psi}\end{matrix}\right)\,.\qquad

(150)

\Phi_{\mathcal{N}=1\text{ long}}\xrightarrow{m\to 0}\Phi_{\mathcal{N}=1}+\overline{\widetilde{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}=1}\,{\eta}_{2}

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{N}=1}

\displaystyle=\varphi+\psi\,\eta^{1}\,,

(152)

\displaystyle\overline{\widetilde{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}=1}

\displaystyle=\overline{\widetilde{\psi}}+\overline{\widetilde{\varphi}}\,\eta^{1}\,,

(153)

and similar relations hold for the conjugate superfield.

The theory’s superpotential is given by

W_{\mathcal{N}=1\text{ SQCD}}=\overline{m}_{A}\widetilde{\varphi}_{A}\varphi^{A}\,,

(154)

where
A=1,\dots,N_{f}
is the flavor index of the
\mathcal{N}=1
matter fields. Note that the superpotential vanishes in the massless limit. Since there are no Yukawa-type couplings, we may decompose the Lagrangian as

{\cal L}_{{\cal N}=1\,{\rm SQCD}}={\cal L}_{{\rm pure}\,\,{\cal N}=1\,{\rm SYM}}+{\cal L}_{{\text{matter}}}+\widetilde{\cal L}_{{\text{matter}}}+{\cal L}_{{\text{mass terms}}}\,,

(155)

\mathrm{F}

\mathrm{F}^{A}=\mathrm{U}^{A}-m_{A}\overline{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{A}

\widetilde{\mathrm{F}}_{A}=\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}_{A}-m_{A}\overline{\varphi}_{A}

{\cal L}_{{\text{matter}}}=|D_{\mu}\varphi^{A}|^{2}+\overline{\psi}_{A\,\alpha}i\,/\penalty\hskip-8.53581ptD^{\dot{\alpha}\alpha}\psi^{A}_{\alpha}+g\overline{\varphi}_{A}\lambda^{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}^{A}-g\varphi^{A}\overline{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}\overline{\psi}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{A}+g\overline{\varphi}_{A}\mathrm{D}\varphi^{A}+|\mathrm{U}^{A}|^{2}\,,

(156)

and the matter terms
\widetilde{\cal L}_{\text{matter}}
stemming from the antifundamental multiplet are given by the same Lagrangian, where appropriate tildes are added to the component fields of the chiral multiplet. Finally, the mass terms are given by

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\text{mass terms}}=-|m_{A}\varphi_{A}|^{2}-|m_{A}\widetilde{\varphi}_{A}|^{2}-\frac{\overline{m}_{A}}{2}\widetilde{\psi}_{A}\psi^{A}-\frac{m_{A}}{2}\overline{\psi}_{A}\overline{\widetilde{\psi}}^{A}\,.

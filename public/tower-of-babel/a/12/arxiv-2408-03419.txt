Tamagawa Numbers of Elliptic Curves with an ℓ \ell -isogeny
Alexander J. Barrios, John Cullinan

Abstract
. Let ℓ \ell be an odd prime, and suppose E E is an elliptic curve defined over the rational numbers ℚ \mathbb{Q} . If E E has an ℓ \ell -torsion point, then there has been significant work done on characterizing the ℓ \ell -divisibility of the global Tamagawa number of E E . In this paper, we consider elliptic curves that are ℓ \ell -isogenous to elliptic curves with an ℓ \ell -torsion point and study the ℓ \ell -divisibility of their global Tamagawa numbers.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setup and Motivation

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over the rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
E
admits finitely many rational isogenies
E\to E^{\prime}
, where
E^{\prime}
is another elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. The set of isomorphism classes of isogenous elliptic curves to
E
form the vertices of a graph (the
isogeny graph
of
E
), with edges corresponding to prime-degree isogenies. Isogeny graphs of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
were recently explicitly classified in
[
3
]
.
Isogeny-torsion
graphs are isogeny graphs whose vertices are decorated with the torsion subgroup of the elliptic curve. In
[
10
]
, all possible isogeny-torsion graphs over
\mathbb{Q}
were classified. It is an interesting number theoretic problem to study how the arithmetic properties of elliptic curves vary over members of the same graph.

Here is the setup we are considering. Fix a positive integer
m
and suppose
|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathsf{tors}}|=m
. For each prime
\ell
at which
E
has good reduction, every
\mathbb{Q}
-isogenous elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
to
E
has the property that
|E^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})|
is divisible by
m
. This is due to: (1) the well-known injectivity of rational torsion into
E(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
under reduction modulo
\ell
, (2) the fact that the number of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-points is an isogeny invariant, and (3) that a
\mathbb{Q}
-isogeny descends to an
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-isogeny for all but finitely many
\ell
. However, it is not necessarily the case that all of these
E^{\prime}
must have
|E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathsf{tors}}|=m
. Introducing a little more terminology as in
[
12
]
, we say that
E/\mathbb{Q}

locally has a subgroup of order
m
if
|E(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})|\equiv 0\pmod{m}
for all good primes
\ell
.

Example 1.1
.

The isogeny class
880.h

[
19
]
consists of four elliptic curves:

The curves 880.h1 and 880.h4 locally have subgroups of order 4, but not globally.

We immediately specialize to the case
m=\ell
is a fixed, odd prime number for the remainder of the paper. By Mazur’s torsion theorem
[
21
]
, we are necessarily restricted to
\ell\in\{3,5,7\}
. If

has a point

, then set
\widetilde{E}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E/\langle P\rangle
. In terms of the mod

representation, the difference between the two curves is that if we choose a basis for

such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{im}\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&*\\
0&*\end{pmatrix}\right\},

then up to an ordering of a corresponding basis for

, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{im}\overline{\rho}_{\widetilde{E},\ell}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\

If, furthermore,

is a split Cartan subgroup of

, then (at least one of) the
\ell
-isogenous curves to

\ell
-torsion point (by
[

]
, this restricts

).

Let

be the set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over

that locally have a subgroup of order

, another reason why we take

-divisibility of the global Tamagawa number

of elliptic curves differs between the sets
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\smallsetminus\mathcal{G}_{\ell}
.

There has been a good amount of work done already on studying the
\ell
-divisibility of Tamagawa numbers of elliptic curves with a non-trivial
\ell
-torsion point. For example, in
[
20
]
and
[
6
]
, the authors studied, among other things, the conditions under which

is divisible by
\ell
for

. Specifically, in
[
20
]
, it was shown that if
\ell=7
, then

is divisible by 7 for all

with a rational 7-torsion point, and if
\ell=5
, then
c_{E}
is divisible by 5 unless

is isomorphic to the modular curve

. When
\ell\in\{2,3\}
, there are infinitely many

with a rational
\ell
-torsion point with

coprime to
\ell
, and in
[
6
]
the authors give a complete classification of such curves for which

with at most

exceptions. We also note that the ratio of the local Tamagawa numbers of
\ell
-isogenous elliptic curves is a power of

[

. However, for
\ell=2

In this paper, we study the

-divisibility of global Tamagawa numbers for elliptic curves in

. Loosely speaking, we find that the presence of a local subgroup of order
\ell
has a more nuanced effect on the

-divisibility of the global Tamagawa number than does having a global point of order
\ell
. We now describe our results in detail.

1.2.
Main Results

1.3.
Future Directions

Given that we have explicit conditions for when the global Tamagawa number of
\widetilde{E}
is divisible by
\ell

The groups (

\ell

\mathbb{Q}
. More generally, given an elliptic curve

-divisibility of its global Tamagawa number depends on the image

of the
\ell
-adic representation. Specifically, let
\mathcal{E}
denote the set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
ordered by height; write
\mathcal{E}_{X}
for the subset of

consisting of elliptic curves with height at most

.

Fix a prime number
\ell
, a positive integer

, and a subgroup
G_{\ell}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
. Denote by

N(G_{\ell},n,X)
the number of

such that
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,\ell}
is conjugate to
G_{\ell}
and

. It would be interesting in a future work to determine asymptotics for

for various subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
; our work in this paper, together with the work of
[
6
]
and
[
20
]
can be viewed as a start to this classification. In this particular direction, we would need asymptotic estimates on the number of curves in

with

, which we are currently unable to prove.

2.
Background and Setup

In this section we give a brief overview of the Tamagawa number of an elliptic curve. We do not intend to be encyclopedic and refer the reader to
[
25
]
for detailed background. Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a global field
K
. Let
v
be a non-archimedean place of
K
and denote by
K_{v}
the completion of
K
at
v
. Let
k_{v}
be the residue field at
v
and let
E_{0}(K_{v})
be the points of non-singular reduction of
E(K_{v})
. Then
E_{0}(K_{v})
is a finite subgroup of
E(K_{v})
and the index

c_{v}(E/K)=[E(K_{v}):E_{0}(K_{v})]

is called the local Tamagawa number at
v
. The product
c(E/K)=\prod_{v}c_{v}(E/K)
is the global Tamagawa number of
E/K
.

We now take
K=\mathbb{Q}
for the remainder of the paper. For ease of notation, we write
c_{E,p}
for the local Tamagawa number of
E
at
p
and
c_{E}
for the global Tamagawa number. Tate’s algorithm is a multi-step, terminating process that determines
c_{E,p}
and is laid out with 11 main steps in
[
25
, Ch. IV.9]
(in this paper, when we refer to Step
n
of Tate’s algorithm, we are referring to Silverman’s exposition).

If
E/\mathbb{Q}
is an elliptic curve with Weierstrass equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6},

then the following invariants are well-known:

b_{2}=a_{1}^{2}+4a_{2}

c_{4}=b_{2}^{2}-24b_{4}

b_{4}=2a_{4}+a_{1}a_{3}

c_{6}=36b_{2}b_{4}-216b_{6}-b_{2}^{3}

b_{6}=a_{3}^{2}+4a_{6}

\Delta=9b_{2}b_{4}b_{6}-b_{2}^{2}b_{8}-8b_{4}^{3}-27b_{6}^{2}

b_{8}=a_{1}^{2}a_{6}+4a_{2}a_{6}-a_{1}a_{3}a_{4}+a_{2}a_{3}^{2}-a_{4}^{2}

j=c_{4}^{3}/\Delta

Two elliptic curve
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic
if there exists an isomorphism
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
defined by
\psi(x,y)=\left(ux^{2}+r,u^{3}y+u^{2}sx+w\right)
for

, with
u
non-zero. For such an isomorphism, we write
\psi=\left[u,r,s,w\right]
.

To begin calculating
c_{E,p}
, recall that
E
has bad reduction when
p\mid\Delta
(this is Step
1
), has multiplicative reduction at
p
if
v_{p}(\Delta)>0
and
v_{p}(c_{4})=0
(this is Step
2
), and additive reduction if
v_{p}(\Delta)>0
and
v_{p}(c_{4})>0
(these are steps
3
-
10
). If the algorithm reaches step
11
, then the model for

is not minimal at
p
, and the algorithm must be run again after an admissible change of variables. A by-product of Tate’s Algorithm is the Kodaira-Néron type at a prime
p
of an elliptic curve
E
, which we denote by
\operatorname{typ}_{p}(E)
. The
p
-adic signature
of an elliptic curve

, denoted
\operatorname{sig}_{p}(E)
, is the triple
(v_{p}(c_{4}),v_{p}(c_{6}),v_{p}(\Delta))
. In
[
23
]
, it is shown that if an elliptic curve

is given by a minimal Weierstrass model, then the Kodaira-Néron type at a prime
p
of

is uniquely determined by
\operatorname{sig}_{p}(E)
if

. If

, then additional information is needed to determine the Kodaira-Néron type. All of this is summarized in the online supplement
[
7
]
to
[
6
]
.

If
E
is an elliptic curve with a torsion point of order

, then

admits a unique model over
\mathbb{Q}

[

, Proposition 1.3]
, the so-called Tate normal form,

(2.2)

y^{2}+\left(1-g\right)xy-fy=x^{3}-fx^{2},

where

and

, and

. When

has order

, the corresponding modular curve

has genus

, and this leads to such curves being parameterizable by their Tate normal form with

[

]
. Now let

denote the cyclic group of order

with the property that if

, then there are relatively prime integers

\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic to

, and

[

. Elliptic curves with a torsion point of order

4.

-isogenous rational elliptic curves

-torsion point

Proposition 4.1
.

There are infinitely many elliptic curves

Proof.

By Proposition

, if

-invariant

\left(i\right)

Case 1.

. In particular, half of the primes satisfy this condition. Our next results builds on Proposition

Corollary 4.2
.

For a prime
p
, consider the elliptic curve
\widetilde{E}_{C_{3}^{0}}(p)

(1)

The global Tamagawa number of
\widetilde{E}_{C_{3}^{0}}(p)

for
\frac{2}{3}
of the primes;

(2)

The global Tamagawa number of
\widetilde{E}_{C_{3}^{0}}(p)

\frac{1}{3}

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, the global Tamagawa number

of
\widetilde{E}_{C_{3}^{0}}(p)

\widetilde{c}=\widetilde{c}_{3}\widetilde{c}_{p}
. Note that the condition that

.

Now suppose that

\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)=-1

and

Let

for

\ell
-th power-free such that

\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic to

and
\widetilde{E}_{T}(a,b)

\left(\frac{-\ell g_{\ell}(a,b)}{q}\right)=-1.

By Proposition
2.3
, we may assume that there are relatively prime integers with
a
positive such that

and
\widetilde{E}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic to

(resp.

and

to be positive relatively prime positive integers. Since the global Tamagawa number

7.1.

, Thm. 1.3.1]
the limit
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lim_{X\to\infty}|\mathcal{G}_{\ell,X}|/|\mathcal{L}_{\ell,X}|
exists, and can be interpreted as the conditional probability that an elliptic curve has a global subgroup of order
\ell
given that it locally has a subgroup of order
\ell
. For instance,

\mathcal{P}_{3}=1/2,\ \mathcal{P}_{5}=25/34,\text{ and }\mathcal{P}_{7}=4/(4+\sqrt{7})\approx 0.602.

However, we are unable at this point to predict whether a similar proportion exists for the Tamagawa data we study in this paper. More precisely, we set

\mathcal{T}_{\ell,X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{|\{E\in\mathcal{L}_{\ell,X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{G}_{\ell,X}~:~c_{E}\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\}|}{|\mathcal{L}_{\ell,X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{G}_{\ell,X}|}.

In
[
16
]
, the authors determined asymptotics for the number of elliptic curves of height
\leq X
with a given torsion subgroup. (For example, they show that
|\mathcal{G}_{5,X}|=cX^{1/6}+O(X^{1/12})
, where
c
is a computable constant.) In
[
12
]
the authors showed that
|\mathcal{L}_{\ell,X}|
has the same growth asymptotics and error terms (though with different leading constant), allowing them to compute the limits as
X\to\infty
as ratios of growth constants.

Two items that make proving analogous results for
\mathcal{T}_{\ell,X}
more difficult are

(1)

it is not clear what the correct order of growth is for the denominator
|\mathcal{L}_{\ell,X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{G}_{\ell,X}|
, and

(2)

as the parameters
a
and
b
range over positive, coprime integers, we would need asymptotics on quadratic and cubic forms in
a
and
b
with certain prime factorizations.

With all of that said, we give some data below on
\mathcal{T}_{\ell,X}
for several values of
X
. We observe that the proportions do not appear to be converging to a limit that we can discern from these data.

Note that for
\ell\in\left\{5,7\right\}
, Proposition
5.1
gives infinitely many specializations of
\widetilde{E}_{C_{\ell}}
whose global Tamagawa number is divisible by
\ell
. Theorem
6.1
gives necessary and sufficient conditions on the parameters of
\widetilde{E}_{C_{\ell}}
for determining those specializations whose global Tamagawa number is coprime to
\ell
. While we are unable to prove that there are infinitely many such specializations, Corollaries
6.5
and

show that there are infinitely many such specializations provided that the set

is infinite. Motivated by this, we investigate the

.

We conclude with a discussion of how the data in Table
6.1
was computed. Let
T=C_{\ell}
for
\ell\in\left\{5,7\right\}
, and let
\alpha_{T}=\alpha_{T}(a,b)
and
\beta_{T}=\beta_{T}(a,b)
denote the invariants
c_{4}
and
c_{6}
, respectively, of
E_{T}
. These polynomials are given explicitly in Tables 4 and 5, respectively, of
[
2
]
. Since
E_{T}
is given by a global minimal model, we have that

\operatorname*{ht}(E_{T})=\frac{1}{12}\max\log\left\{\left|\alpha_{T}^{3}\right|,\beta_{T}^{2}\right\}.

Next, let
\mathcal{E}_{T}\
denote the set of
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E
such that
T\hookrightarrow E(\mathbb{Q})
. Then, we have a surjective map
\psi:\left\{\left(a,b\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\gcd(a,b)=1\right\}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{T}
given by
\psi(a,b)=E_{T}(a,b)
. We note that this map is not necessarily injective as distinct pairs
\left(a,b\right)
and
\left(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\right)
may yield the same
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic elliptic curve. However, such duplicates can be removed by considering the reduced minimal models of
\psi(a,b)
and
\psi(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
. Here, we recall that the reduced minimal model of an elliptic curve is unique up to
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphism
[
11
]
. In fact,
[
4
]
gives necessary and sufficient conditions on the parameters of
E_{T}
to determine the reduced minimal model of
E_{T}
. This gives rise to a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{E}_{T}
and
\left\{\left(a,b\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\gcd(a,b)=1\right\}/\sim_{T}
, where
\sim_{T}
is the equivalence relation defined by
\left(a,b\right)\sim_{T}\left(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\right)
if and only if
E_{T}(a,b)
and
E_{T}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
are
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic.

Next, let

\Lambda_{T}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cl}\left\{\left(a,b\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\gcd(a,b)=1,ab\text{ not a }5\text{th-power}\right\}/\sim_{T}&\text{if }T=C_{5},\\
\left\{\left(a,b\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\gcd(a,b)=1\right\}/\sim_{T}&\text{if }T=C_{7}.\end{array}\right.

By the above, Lemma
7.2
, and the fact that there is no isogeny class over
\mathbb{Q}
consisting of two distinct elliptic curves with a
7
-torsion point, we have that
\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{7}
is bijective to
\Lambda_{C_{7}}
. For
T=C_{5}
, we have by
[
12
, Lemma 4.3.3]
that
E_{T}
and
\widetilde{E}_{T}
both have a
5
-torsion point if and only if
ab
is a fifth power. This, coupled with our above discussion and Lemma
7.2
gives a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{5}
and
\Lambda_{C_{5}}
. Finally, the explicit description of
\operatorname*{ht}(E_{T})
allows us to demonstrate that if
\operatorname*{ht}(E_{T})<10
(resp.
20
) for
T=C_{5}
(resp.
C_{7}
), then
a+b<55\ 000
. So to compute the data in Table
6.1
, we first compute
\left\{\left(a,b\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\gcd(a,b)=1,a+b<55\ 000\right\}
. For each pair in this set, we compute the reduced minimal model of the corresponding elliptic curves. After removing duplicates, we obtain subsets
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T}
that are a complete set of representatives for
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T}
. In particular, we obtain a subset in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{\ell,X}
for
X=10
(resp.
20
) if
T=C_{5}
(resp.
C_{7})
. Theorem
6.1
then gives us the required assumptions to find the subset of
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T}
that is
in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathcal{N}_{\ell,X}
.

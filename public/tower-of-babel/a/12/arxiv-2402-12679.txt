Robust Solutions of Nonlinear Least Squares Problems via Min-max Optimization
Xiaojun Chen Department of Applied Mathematics, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Kowloon, Hong Kong, China and C.T. Kelley North Carolina State University, Department of Mathematics, Box 8205, Raleigh, NC 27695-8205, USA

Abstract
This paper considers robust solutions to a class of nonlinear least squares problems using min-max optimization approach. We give an explicit formula for the value function of the inner maximization problem and show the existence of global minimax points. We establish error bounds from any solution of the nonlinear least squares problem to the solution set of the robust nonlinear least squares problem. Moreover, we propose a smoothing method for finding a global minimax point of the min-max problem by using the formula and show that finding an ϵ \epsilon minimax critical point of the min-max problem needs at most O ⁡ ( ϵ − 2 + δ 2 ​ ϵ − 3 ) O(\epsilon^{-2}+\delta^{2}\epsilon^{-3}) evaluations of the function value and gradients of the objective function, where δ \delta is the tolerance of the noise. Numerical results of integral equations with uncertain data demonstrate the robustness of solutions of our approach and unstable behaviour of least squares solutions disregarding uncertainties in the data. Min-max optimization; nonlinear least squares problems; numerical solution of integral equations; nonsmooth nonconvex optimization, complexity

Introduction

Let
\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a continuous function and
\|\cdot\|
denote the Euclidean norm. The nonlinear least squares problem

\min_{\mathbf{x}}\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}

(1)

is widely used to model problems in science and engineering. When the problems contain uncertain or noisy data, it is important to find a robust solution. In this paper, we consider the following min-max problem

\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{y}\|^{2},

(2)

for the robust nonlinear least squares problem
where
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
with
r\leq m
,

\Omega=\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\,:\,\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta\}
, and

\delta\geq 0
.
If
\delta=0
, then problem (
2
) reduces to problem (
1
). In some applications, the perturbation vector has a known structure and can be present as
a finite linear combination of columns of
C
. See
Ghaoui & Lebret 1997
and §5.2 in this paper.

A particular example is from the
first kind Fredholm nonlinear integral equation with noisy
data, which can be written in the following form:

h(x)=\int^{1}_{0}p(x,y)q(x,u(y))\ dy,

(3)

where
h,p,q
are continuous functions.
The low dimensional projection method is a popular numerical method
to find an approximate
solution of (
3
). For example, using

\tilde{u}({\bf x})=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathbf{x}_{i}T_{i-1}(x),

where
T_{i}
is the
i
th degree Chebyshev polynomial on
[0,1]
, we obtain a nonlinear least squares problem (
1
). If
h
contains uncertain or noisy data, we can use
the min-max model (
2
) to
find a robust least squares solution.

Nonlinear least square problems are also often seen in data sciences and machine learning, for example, training convolutional neural network (CNN). Given input and output data sets
\{\mathbf{a}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{{\bf{b}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, the training process of a two-layer CNN
(
Cui et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2023
)

is represented as

\min_{\mathbf{x}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|{\bf{b}}_{i}-\mathbf{U}\sigma(\mathbf{W}\sigma(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{a}_{i}+{\bf{u}})+{\bf{v}})\|^{2},

(4)

where
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times l}
,
{\bf W}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
,

{\bf V}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times\hat{n}}
are the weight matrices,

{\bf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l},{\bf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
are the bias vectors,
\sigma
is an
activation function, e.g., the ReLU activation function

\sigma({\bf z})=\max(0,{\bf z})
. Let

\mathbf{x}=({\rm vec}(\mathbf{U})^{\top},{\rm vec}(\mathbf{V})^{\top},{\rm vec}(\mathbf{W})^{\top},{\bf{u}}^{\top},{\bf{v}}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
n={kl+l^{2}+l\hat{n}+l+k}
and
m=Nk
. When we study a big set of data,
m
can be much bigger than
n
. Problem (
4
) can be written as problem (
1
), which is an overdetermined least squares problem.
When the output data
\{{\bf{b}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\}_{i=1}^{N}
contain noise, the min-max problem (
2
) can be used to find a robust least squares solution.

First order methods including decent-ascent methods have been widely used to find the first order stationary points of min-max problems
(
Diakonikolas et al. 2021
;
Jin et al. 2020
;
Jiang & Chen 2023
;
Yang et al. 2020
)
. However, the first order stationary points defined by gradients of the objective function
f
can lead to incorrect solutions of min-max problems. In subsection 1.2, we show with an example of robust linear least square problems that solving the first order optimality
conditions can lead to incorrect results.

This paper makes three contributions.

•

We use a change of variable in
{\bf y}

to give an explicit formula for the value function of the inner maximization problem

\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})\|_{1}+\|{\bf C}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2}=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

(5)

for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\|{\bf C}\|_{F}

is the Frobenius norm of
{\bf C}
.

•

We propose a smoothing method for finding a global minimax point of the min-max problem by using the function
\varphi
and show that finding an
\epsilon
minimax critical point of the min-max problem (
2
) needs at most
O(\epsilon^{-2}+\delta^{2}\epsilon^{-3})

evaluations of the function value and gradients of the objective function.

•

We give error bounds from any solution of nonlinear least squares problem (
1
) to the set of minimizers of
\varphi.
We give an explicit form
for the difference of
worst-case residual errors of a least squares solution and a robust least squares solution, and show the difference increases linearly as the tolerance of the noise increases.

1.1
Important concepts

In this subsection we review some subtle concepts for min-max problems.
There are several forms of stationarity and they differ in important
ways. These ideas will reappear throughout the paper.

A point
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
is called a

saddle point

(
v. Neumann 1928
)
of problem (
2
) if for
any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbf{y}\in\Omega,
we have

f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y})\leq f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\leq f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{*}).

(6)

A point
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
is called a
local saddle point
of problem (
2
) if (
6
) holds in a neighborhood of

(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})
.

A point
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
is called a
global minimax point

(
Jin et al. 2020
)
of problem (
2
) if

\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y})\leq f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\leq\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{y\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

A point
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
is called a

local minimax point
of problem (
2
), if there exist a

\rho_{0}>0
and a function
\tau:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

satisfying

\tau(\rho)\rightarrow 0
as
\rho\rightarrow 0
,
such that for any
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
and any

(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
satisfying
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|\leq\rho
and
\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{*}\|\leq\rho
, we have

f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y})\leq f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\leq\max_{\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in\{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega:\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{*}\|\leq\tau(\rho)\}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}).

By Theorem 3.11 in
Jiang & Chen 2023
, if
f

is directionally differentiable then
a local minimax point
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega

of problem (
2
) satisfies the following first order condition

\begin{array}[]{ll}d_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*};{\bf{v}})=0,\quad\forall\,{\bf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
d_{\mathbf{y}}f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*};{\bf{u}})\leq 0,\quad\forall\,{\bf{u}}\in\mathrm{T}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{y}^{*}),\end{array}

(7)

where
d_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*};{\bf{v}})
and
d_{\mathbf{y}}f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*};{\bf{u}})

denote the directional derivatives of
f
at
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y}^{*})

along the directions
{\bf{v}}
and
{\bf{u}}
, respectively,
and

\mathrm{T}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{y})=:\left\{\mathbf{w}:\exists~\mathbf{y}^{k}\overset{\Omega}{\to}\mathbf{y},t^{k}\downarrow 0~\text{such that}~\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{y}^{k}-\mathbf{y}}{t^{k}}=\mathbf{w}\right\}

is the tangent cone to
\Omega
at
\mathbf{y}\in\Omega

(
Rockafellar & Wets 2009
, Definition 6.1)
. When
\mathbf{F}
is differentiable, the first order optimality condition of problem (
2
) has the following form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{y})=0\\
\mathbf{y}={\rm Proj}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{y}-\alpha\mathbf{C}^{\top}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{y})),\end{array}

where
\alpha
is any positive scalar.

Since the objective function is continuous and the feasible set
\Omega
is bounded in problem (
2
), the solution set

\Omega^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

is nonempty and bounded for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, the function
\varphi
is well-defined.
However, it is known that solving a convex quadratic maximization over a box feasible set is NP-hard
(
Pardalos & Vavasis 1991
)
.
People often consider the first order optimality condition for nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problem
(
Diakonikolas et al. 2021
;
Jiang & Chen 2023
;
Yang et al. 2020
)
.
However a solution of this first order optimality condition can be meaningless for (
2
), unless the problem has a local minimax problem, for example, convex-concave saddle point problems with cardinality penalties
Bian & Chen 2024
.

1.2
Example

Consider problem (
2
) with
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}
and
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{I}
. Suppose that
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is nonsingular. Then any pair
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=(\mathbf{A}^{-1}({\bf{b}}+\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{y})
with
\mathbf{y}\in\Omega
satisfies
the first order condition (
1.1
). On the other hand,
the optimal solution of the inner maximization problem has the closed form

\mathbf{y}_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{|\mathbf{y}_{i}|\leq\delta}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y})_{i}^{2}=\delta\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&{\rm if}\,(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}})_{i}<0\\
\{-1,1\}&{\rm if}\,(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}})_{i}=0\\
-1&{\rm if}\,(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}})_{i}>0,\end{array}\right.\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

(11)

Hence

\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}=\min_{\mathbf{x}}\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}=n\delta^{2}

at
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})=(\mathbf{A}^{-1}{\bf{b}},\delta{\bf{c}})
,
where
{\bf{c}}
is a vector with
all elements being 1 or -1. In fact, by the
Danskin theorem, the inner function
\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}
is strongly convex.
Hence, the minimization problem has only one solution
\mathbf{x}^{*}=\mathbf{A}^{-1}{\bf{b}}.

It is worth noting that
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
is not a saddle point
of
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2},
for any
\hat{\mathbf{y}}
with
\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i}\in\{\delta,-\delta\}
, since

f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})=n\delta^{2}>f(\mathbf{A}^{-1}({\bf{b}}+\hat{\mathbf{y}}),\hat{\mathbf{y}})=0.

In fact,
f
does not have a saddle point, since

n\delta^{2}=\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\neq\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\min_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=0.

See
Facchinei & Pang 2002
. Moreover, due to the arbitrary choice of
\delta
for the closed form solution
(
11
) of the maximization problem, we can easily find that
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
is not a local saddle point.

On the other hand, we find

\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{y})=f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})=\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{y\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Hence
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
is a global minimax point of
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}
.
However,
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
is not a local minimax point of
f
. Indeed, consider
\hat{\mathbf{y}}=\delta\mathbf{e}
, where
\mathbf{e}
is the vector with
all elements being 1.

Let
\rho<\frac{\delta}{2\|{\bf A}\|}
and
\tau(\rho)<\frac{\delta}{2}
satisfying
\tau(\rho)\to 0
as
\rho\to 0
.
For any
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
satisfying
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|\leq\rho

and
\|\mathbf{y}-\delta\mathbf{e}\|\leq\rho
, we have

|{\bf A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}|_{i}\leq\|{\bf A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}\|=\|{\bf A}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{*})\|\leq\|{\bf A}\|\rho\leq\delta-\frac{\tau(\rho)}{2}

and for
i=1\ldots,n
,

\mathbf{y}_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{|\mathbf{y}_{i}|\leq\delta,|\mathbf{y}_{i}-\delta|\leq\tau(\rho)}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y})_{i}^{2}=\delta\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&{\rm if}\,(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}})_{i}<0\\
1&{\rm if}\,(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}})_{i}=0\\
1&{\rm if}\,0<(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf{b}})_{i}\leq\delta-\frac{\tau(\rho)}{2}.\end{array}\right.

Let
\mathbf{x}^{1}={\bf A}^{-1}({\bf{b}}+\epsilon\mathbf{e})
with
\epsilon\leq\rho/\|{\bf A}^{-1}\mathbf{e}\|
. Then
we obtain
\|\mathbf{x}^{1}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|\leq\rho
and

\displaystyle n\delta^{2}=f(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\max_{\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta,\|\mathbf{y}-\delta\mathbf{e}\|_{\infty}\leq\tau(\rho)}\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{1}-{\bf{b}}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}=\|\epsilon\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\epsilon-\delta)^{2}n\geq\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta,\|\mathbf{y}-\delta\mathbf{e}\|_{\infty}\leq\tau(\rho)}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Hence,
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
is not a local minimax point of
f
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
does not satisfy the first order condition (
1.1
), since
{\bf A}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-{\bf b}-\hat{\mathbf{y}})=-\delta{\bf A}^{\top}\mathbf{e}\neq 0
. On the other hand, if
(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})
satisfies the first order condition (
1.1
), then from the nonsingularity of
{\bf A}
, we have
f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})=0
, which implies

(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})
cannot be a local minimax point or a global
minimax point, since
0=f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\not\geq\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y})>0
.

Note that for nonconvex-nonconcave min-max optimization, a local minimax point must satisfy the first order condition, but a global minimax point is not necessarily a stationary point
(
Jiang & Chen 2023
)
.

In this example we present a min-max problem for robust linear
least squares problems which does not have a saddle point, a local saddle point and a local minimax point, while it has first order stationary points and global minimax points. However, the set of first order stationary points and
the set of global minimax points do not have a common point.

This example also shows that the study of the value function of the inner maximization problem is important. In §
2
, we give a proof of closed form expression (
5
) for the inner
value function. Using the inner value function, we prove the existence of a minimax point of the robust nonlinear least squares problem (
2
). In §
3
, we
apply the formula to expose some connections between
solutions of (
2
) and those of (
1
), and give error bounds from any solution of nonlinear least squares problem (
1
) to the set of minimizers of
\varphi.

In §
4
,
we propose a smoothing method with the formula to obtain a complexity bound
for finding an
\epsilon
minimax critical point of
a global minimax point of (
2
). In §
5
, we compare least squares solutions and
robust least squares solutions in the worst-case, and show the difference of their worst-case residual errors
increases linearly as the tolerance of the noise increases. Moreover, we present numerical results of integral
equations with uncertain data to show the robustness of solutions of our approach.

2
The value function

The study of the inner function of the maximization problem is
very important for solving min-max problem (
2
).
Denote the value function by

\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

We consider a change of variables. Let

{\bf C}={\bf U}_{\bf C}\Sigma_{\bf C}{\bf V}^{\top}_{\bf C}

be the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
{\bf C}
. Define

{\hat{\bf y}}={\bf V}^{\top}_{\bf C}{\bf y}\mbox{ and }{\hat{\bf C}}={\bf U}_{\bf C}\Sigma_{\bf C}.

Then the QR factorization of
{\hat{\bf C}}
is
{\bf U}_{\bf C}\Sigma_{\bf C}
and the
R factor is therefore diagonal. We argue that replacing
{\bf y}
and
{\bf C}

with
{\hat{\bf y}}
and
{\hat{\bf C}}
is consistent with the modeling assumption
that the perturbation
{\bf C}{\bf y}
be small and that rotating the coordinates
for
{\bf y}
does no harm. One can compare the new constraint

\|{\hat{\bf y}}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
with the constraint on
{\bf y}

to see this by

\|{\bf y}\|_{\infty}=\|{\bf V}_{\bf C}{\hat{\bf y}}\|_{\infty}\leq\|{\bf V}_{\bf C}\|_{\infty}\|{\hat{\bf y}}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{r}\delta.

(12)

So we will now assume that the change of variable has been made.

Assumption 2.1

{\bf C}
has full column rank.
Let
{\bf C}={\bf Q}{\bf R}
be the QR factorization of
{\bf C}
, where
{\bf R}
is
(
r\times r
) diagonal with positive diagonal entries.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold.
Then the inner function
\varphi
is given by (
5
).

Proof 2.2
.

We note that the orthogonality of
{\bf Q}
implies that

\|{\bf R}\|_{F}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\bf R}_{jj}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\|{\bf C}\|_{F}=\left(\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{m,r}{\bf C}^{2}_{ij}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence (
5
) is equivalent to

\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})\|_{1}+\|{\bf R}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2}.

(13)

We will prove (
13
).
Our task is to compute the inner function

\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\max_{{\bf y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}.

The orthogonal projection onto the range of
{\bf C}
is

{\bf P}={{\bf Q}}{{\bf Q}}^{\top}

which is an
m\times m
matrix. Since
{\bf P}
is an orthogonal
projector and
{\bf P}{\bf C}={\bf C}
, we have

\|{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}=\|({\bf I}-{\bf P}){\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+\|{\bf P}{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}

(14)

by the Pythagorean theorem. The term
\|({\bf I}-{\bf P}){\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}

does not depend on
{\bf y}
so we have reduced the problem to
computing

\varphi_{0}({\bf x})=\max_{{\bf y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf P}{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}.

(15)

Using the QR factorization again and the orthogonality
of
{{\bf Q}}
we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|{\bf P}{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}&=\|{{\bf Q}}{{\bf Q}}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})-{{\bf Q}}{{\bf R}}{\bf y}\|^{2}\\
&=\|{{\bf Q}}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})-{{\bf R}}{\bf y}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Setting
\xi={{\bf Q}}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})
gives us the diagonal form
of the maximization problem

\varphi_{0}({\bf x})=\max_{{\bf y}\in\Omega}\|\xi-{{\bf R}}{\bf y}\|^{2}.

(16)

We can solve this problem explicitly. Since
{\bf R}
is diagonal with
positive diagonal elements
{\bf R}_{ii}
, we obtain

\mathbf{y}_{i}=\delta\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1&\mbox{if $\xi_{i}>0$}\\
\{-1,1\}&\mbox{if $\xi_{i}=0$}\\
1&\mbox{if $\xi_{i}<0$,}\end{array}\right.\quad i=1,\ldots,r.

Hence, from
\xi={\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})
, we have

\varphi_{0}({\bf x})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(|[{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})]_{j}|+{\bf R}_{jj}\delta)^{2}.

(17)

Here
[\cdot]_{i}
denotes the
i
th component of a vector if the
vector is a complicated expression, such as
{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})
.

Since
{\bf P}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}
we have

\sum_{j=1}^{r}|[{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})]_{j}|^{2}=\|{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}=\|{\bf P}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}

and so, using the orthogonality of
{\bf P}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi({\bf x})&=\|({\bf I}-{\bf P}){\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(|[{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})]_{j}|+{\bf R}_{jj}\delta)^{2}\\
\\
&=\|({\bf I}-{\bf P}){\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+\|{\bf P}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+2\delta\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\bf R}_{jj}|[{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})]_{j}|+(\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\bf R}_{jj}^{2})\delta^{2}\\
\\
&=\|{\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|_{1}+\|{\bf R}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2}.\\
\end{array}

(18)

The last equality is a consequence of the diagonality of
{\bf R}
and the fact
that
{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{\top}={\bf C}^{\top}
.

It is worth noting that the inner function
\varphi
is nonsmooth due to
the term
\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|_{1}
, even if
{\bf F}
is differentiable.

Remark 2.1
From the expression (
5
), the function
\varphi
has the following
properties.

(i)

If
\mathbf{F}
is continuously differentiable, then
\varphi
is locally Lipschitz continuous, directionally differentiable, and its directional derivatives satisfy

\varphi(\mathbf{x};{\bf{d}})=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega^{*}(\mathbf{x})}{\bf{d}}^{\top}{\bf F}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{y}),

where
{\bf F}^{\prime}
is the Jacobian of
{\bf F}
and
\Omega^{*}(\mathbf{x})=
arg
\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}.
If
\Omega^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\{\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\}
is a singleton, then
\varphi
is differentiable at
\mathbf{x}
and

\nabla\varphi(\mathbf{x})={\bf F}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}({\bf F}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{x})).

(ii)

If the set of minimizers of
\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}
is nonempty and bounded, then the set of minimizers of
\varphi
is nonempty and bounded, which can be seen from the relation of the two level sets

(iii)

If

and there is

such that

, then

has a minimizer in
{\bf X}_{\gamma}
, which can be seen from the continuity of

and boundedness of

with

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|^{2}+2\delta(\|{\bf C}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|_{1}-\|{\bf C}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|^{2}>0,\quad\mathbf{x}\not\in{\bf X}_{\gamma}

and

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})\leq\min_{\mathbf{x}\in{\bf X}_{\gamma}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*}).

The function

can have infinitely many minimizers, but only the minimizers at which

has small values
are interesting for the robust least squares problems.
For example,

for

and

is the identity matrix. Any point

, but only at
\mathbf{x}^{*}=\frac{1}{2}
,
\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|<\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})\|

for
\mathbf{x}\not\in{\bf X}_{\gamma}=\{\mathbf{x}^{*}\}
.

(iv)

If
\mathbf{F}
is linear, then
\varphi
is convex and
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is a minimizer of
\varphi
if and only if

0\in\nabla\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|^{2}+2\delta\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}F(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|_{1}.

(19)

If

, then there is
\bar{\delta}>0
such that for any

\delta\geq\bar{\delta}
, (
19
) holds, and thus
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is a solution of
\varphi
. (See Example 5.1). Here int
\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|_{1}
means that the interior of the Clarke subdifferential of

at
\mathbf{x}^{*}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. If
\varphi
has a minimizer, then
problem (

) has a global minimax point and

is a global minimax point of (

) for any

Proof 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{x}^{*}
be a minimizer of

. Since

is continuous and

\mathbf{y}^{*}

3.1
Zero residual

Note that
\varphi({\hat{\bf x}})\leq\varphi({\bf x}^{*})
and

\|{\bf F}({\bf x}^{*})\|^{2}\leq\|{\bf F}({\hat{\bf x}})\|^{2}
for
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\hat{{\bf X}}
and

\mathbf{x}^{*}\in{\bf X}^{*}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi({\hat{\bf x}})&=\|{\bf F}({\hat{\bf x}})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\hat{\bf x}})\|_{1}+\|{\bf C}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2}\\
\\
&\leq\varphi({{\bf x}^{*}})=\|{\bf F}({{\bf x}^{*}})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({{\bf x}^{*}})\|_{1}+\|{\bf C}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2}\\
\\
&\leq\|{\bf F}({\hat{\bf x}})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({{\bf x}^{*}})\|_{1}+\|{\bf C}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2}.\end{array}

(21)

Therefore

\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\hat{\bf x}})\|_{1}\leq\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({{\bf x}^{*}})\|_{1}.

(22)

When
{\bf C}={\bf I}
, we can see that a least squares solution of (
1
) is not
necessarily a least 1-norm solution of
\min_{\bf x}\|{\bf F}({\bf x})\|_{1}

When the equality holds in (

and

. Hence the nonlinear least squares solution set and
the robust nonlinear least squares solution set are the same, that is

.
A special case is the zero residual problem.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. If

, then
({\bf x}^{*},\delta{\bf{c}})
is a global minimax point of (
2
), where
{\bf{c}}
is a vector with
all elements being 1 or -1.

Proof 3.2
.

If

then
the nonsmooth term

Hence the two solution sets
{\bf X}^{*}
and

are the same from (

3.2
Error bounds: linear least squares problems

We first consider the linear least squares problem with

{\bf F}({\bf x})={\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf b}

where
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
{\bf b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let

\varphi_{\rm min}:=\min_{\bf x}\varphi(\mathbf{x}):=\|{\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf b}\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf b})\|_{1}+\|{\bf C}\|^{2}_{F}\delta^{2}.

(23)

Since the objective function of problem (
23
) is piecewise quadratic convex with nonnegative function values, problem (
23
)
has a minimizer
(
Frank & Wolfe 1956
)
.

The subgradient of
\varphi
at
\mathbf{x}
is as follows

\partial\varphi(\mathbf{x})=2{\bf A}^{\top}({\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf b})+2\delta\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf b})\|_{1}.

Let
{\bf B}={\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A}
and
{\bf c}={\bf C}^{\top}{\bf b}
. Then

\displaystyle\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf b})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf B}_{i}{\bf x}-{\bf c}_{i}>0}{\bf B}_{i}^{\top}-\sum_{{\bf B}_{i}{\bf x}-{\bf c}_{i}<0}{\bf B}_{i}^{\top}+\sum_{{\bf B}_{i}{\bf x}-{\bf c}_{i}=0}[\min({\bf B}_{i}^{\top},-{\bf B}_{i}^{\top}),\max({\bf B}_{i}^{\top},-{\bf B}_{i}^{\top})]

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle[-|{\bf B}|^{\top}{\bf e},|{\bf B}|^{\top}{\bf e}],

where
{\bf B}_{i}
is the
i
th row of
{\bf B}
,
|{\bf B}|=(|{\bf B}_{i,j}|)\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}
, and
{\bf e}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
is the vector with all elements being one.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any given
\Gamma>0
, there is

\gamma>0
such that for any

{\bf x}^{*}\in{\bf X}^{*}
with
\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\varphi_{\rm min}\leq\Gamma
,

{\rm dist}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}})\leq 2\delta\gamma^{2}\sqrt{r}\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A}\|.

(24)

Proof 3.4
.

From Theorem 2.7 in
Li 1995
, we have

{\rm dist}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}})\leq\gamma\sqrt{\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\varphi_{\rm min}},

(25)

if
\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\varphi_{\rm min}\leq\Gamma
.
Let
\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\hat{{\bf X}}
such that
\|\mathbf{x}^{*}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|={\rm dist}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}}).

Since
\varphi
is convex, we have

\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq g(\mathbf{x}^{*})^{\top}(\mathbf{x}^{*}-\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\|g(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|\|\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|=\|g(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|{\rm dist}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}}),

where
g(\mathbf{x}^{*})\in\partial\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*})
.

Since
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in{\bf X}^{*},
we have
2{\bf A}^{\top}({\bf A}{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf b})=0
and

\displaystyle\partial\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\bf A}^{\top}({\bf A}{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf b})+2\delta\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}{{\bf x}^{*}}-{\bf b})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\delta\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}{{\bf x}^{*}}-{\bf b})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle 2\delta[-|{\bf B}|^{\top}{\bf e},|{\bf B}|^{\top}{\bf e}].

Hence from (
25
), we find

{\rm dist}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}})\leq\gamma^{2}\|g(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|\leq 2\delta\gamma^{2}\sqrt{r}\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A}\|.

This completes the proof.

From the first order necessary optimality conditions of (
1
) and (
2
) with
{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})={\bf A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf b}
,
we have

{\bf A}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-{\bf b})=0,\quad{\bf A}^{\top}({\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-{\bf b}-{\bf C}\mathbf{y})=0,

for
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in{\bf X}^{*}
and
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\hat{{\bf X}}
, which implies

{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A}(\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}})={\bf C}\mathbf{y}.

If
{\bf A}
has full column rank, then

\|\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq\|({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A})^{-1}{\bf C}\|\|\mathbf{y}\|\leq\sqrt{r}\delta\|({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A})^{-1}{\bf C}\|.

Note that this error bound is the same
O(\delta)
as (
24
). However, Theorem
3.3
does not assume that
{\bf A}
has full column rank.

In the case of overdetermined linear least squares problems, with
m\gg n>r
,
we often have
({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-{\bf b}))_{i}\neq 0,
for
i=1,\ldots,r
, where
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is the
minimum norm solution of the linear least squares problem

\min\|{\bf A}\mathbf{x}-{\bf b}\|^{2}.

Let the SVD of
{\bf A}
be

{\bf A}={\bf U}_{A}\Sigma_{A}{\bf V}_{A}^{\top}.

Then the pseudo-inverse of
{\bf A}
is

{\bf A}^{\dagger}={\bf V}_{A}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}{\bf U}_{A}^{\top}

and the minimum norm solution of the linear least squares problem is

{\bf x}^{*}={\bf A}^{\dagger}{\bf b}={\bf V}_{A}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}{\bf U}_{A}^{\top}{\bf b}=({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\dagger}{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf b}.

Let

\eta=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-{\bf b})|_{i}.

(26)

Proposition 3.5
.

If
\eta>0
, then for any positive scalar
\delta
satisfying

\delta<\frac{\eta}{r\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}^{\top})^{\dagger}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\top}\|_{1}}

(27)

the minimum norm solution of the robust linear least squares problem (
23
) is

\hat{\mathbf{x}}=({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\dagger}({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf b}-\delta\sum_{i=1}^{r}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\top}_{i}{\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-b))_{i}),

(28)

where
({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})_{i}
is the
i
th row of
{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A}
and
s
gn is the sign function.

Proof 3.6
.

First we show that

\min_{1\leq i\leq r}|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-{\bf b})|_{i}\neq 0
,
which implies that
\varphi
is differentiable at
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
.
From the SVD of
{\bf A}
and the form of
\mathbf{x}^{*}
, we have

\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}^{*}-\delta({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\dagger}\sum_{i=1}^{r}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\top}_{i}{\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-b))_{i}.

(29)

Hence for

\displaystyle|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-{\bf b})-{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-{\bf b})|_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A}({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\dagger}\sum_{i=1}^{r}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\top}_{i}{\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-b))_{i}|_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}^{\top})^{\dagger}\sum_{i=1}^{r}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\top}_{i}{\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-b))_{i}|_{i}

\displaystyle\delta r\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}^{\top})^{\dagger}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})^{\top}\|_{1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\eta.

By the definition of
\eta
in (
26
), we find that for

,
|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-{\bf b})|_{i}\neq 0
and

{\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-b))_{i}={\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-b))_{i}.

Now we show
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
is a minimum norm minimizer of

. Since

is a convex function and differentiable at
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
, the first order optimality condition is necessary and sufficient for the optimality, which holds at

as the follows

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi(\hat{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\bf A}^{\top}({\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-b)+2\delta\sum_{i=1}^{r}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})_{i}{\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-b))_{i}

\displaystyle 2{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-2{\bf A}^{\top}b+2\delta\sum_{i=1}^{r}({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf A})_{i}{\rm sgn}({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-b))_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where we use (

is the minimum norm solution of (

).

Remark 3.7
.

If
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})={\bf A}\mathbf{x}^{*}-{\bf b}\neq 0

.

3.3
Error bounds: nonlinear least squares problems

We use the second order growth condition in
Bonnans & Shapiro 2000

to present an error bound from a nonlinear least squares solution to the robust nonlinear least squares
solution set.

We say that the second order growth condition holds at
\hat{{\bf X}}
if there exists a neighborhood
{\cal N}
of
\hat{{\bf X}}
and a constant
\alpha>0
such that

\varphi(\mathbf{x})\geq\varphi_{\rm min}+\alpha[{\rm dist}(\mathbf{x},\hat{{\bf X}})]^{2},\,\,\,\forall\mathbf{x}\,\in{\cal N},

(31)

where
\varphi_{\rm min}=\min_{\mathbf{x}}\varphi(\mathbf{x}).
If
\varphi
is a piecewise convex quadratic function, then (
31
) holds
(
Li 1995
)
.

We say that
{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{F}
is Lipschitz continuous in
{\cal N}
at
\hat{{\bf X}}
if there is
L>0
such that

\|{\bf C}^{\top}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{z}))\|\leq L\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}\|,\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\,\in{\cal N},\mathbf{z}\in\hat{{\bf X}}.

(32)

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose the second order growth condition (
31
) and the Lipschitz continuity condition (
32
) hold. Then for any
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in{\bf X}^{*}\cap{\cal N}
, we have

{\rm dist}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}})\leq 2\delta\sqrt{r}L\alpha^{-1}.

(33)

Proof 3.9
.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\hat{{\bf X}}
such that
\|\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|=
dist
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}}).

From condition (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\varphi(\hat{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|_{1}-\|\mathbf{F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\|-2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|_{1}-2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\mathbf{F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}))\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta\sqrt{r}\|{\bf C}^{\top}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\mathbf{F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta\sqrt{r}L\|\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\delta\sqrt{r}L{\rm dist}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\hat{{\bf X}}),

where the first inequality uses
\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\|\leq\|\mathbf{F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\|.

Together with the second order growth condition (
31
), we find the error bound (
33
).

In this section, we give upper-bounds of the distance between a least squares solution and the solution set of the robust least squares problem. Theorem
3.3
does not reply on a quadratic growth condition and the differentiability.
On the other hand, Theorem
3.8
assumes a quadratic growth condition and the inequality in (
30
) relies on the differentiability of
\varphi
at the least squares solution.

4
Smoothing methods and complexity for minimax problem (

)

The inner maximization function
\varphi
in (

) is a nonconvex composite function with a structured nonsmooth term. In this section, we consider smoothing methods and complexity for minimax problem (
2
). We consider the following minimization problem

\min_{\bf x}\psi({\bf x}):=\|{\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|_{1},

(34)

and assume that
\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a continuously differentiable function in this section.
If
\delta=0
, then problem (
34
) reduces to smooth nonlinear least squares problem (
1
).

4.1
Smoothing function

Using the smoothing convex function
\theta(t,\mu):=\sqrt{t^{2}+4\mu^{2}}
with the smoothing parameter
\mu>0
of
|t|

(
Chen 2012
)
, we define a smoothing function of
\psi
as follows

\Psi({\bf x},\mu)=\|{\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+2\delta\Theta({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}),\mu),

where

\Theta({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}),\mu)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\theta(({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}))_{i},\mu).

(35)

For any fixed
\mu>0
,
\Theta(\cdot,\mu)
is continuously differentiable and

0\leq\Theta({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}),\mu)-\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|_{1}\leq 2r\mu.

(36)

Since
{\bf F}
is continuously differentiable,
\theta(\cdot,\mu)
and
|\cdot|
are convex, we have the gradient consistence
(
Chen 2012
)

{\rm con}\{\lim_{\mathbf{x}\to\bar{\mathbf{x}},\mu\downarrow 0}\nabla\Theta({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}),\mu)\}=\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\bar{{\bf x}})\|_{1},

where “con” denotes the convex hull and
\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x})\|_{1}
is the Clarke gradient of
\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\cdot)\|_{1}
at
{\bf x}

(
Clarke 1990
)
. Hence, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm con}\{{\displaystyle\lim_{\mathbf{x}\to\bar{\mathbf{x}},\mu\downarrow 0}\nabla\Psi({\bf x},\mu)}\}&={\displaystyle\lim_{\mathbf{x}\to\bar{\mathbf{x}}}2\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})+2\delta{\rm con}\{\lim_{\mathbf{x}\to\bar{\mathbf{x}},\mu\downarrow 0}\nabla\Theta({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}),\mu)}\}\\
&=2\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})^{\top}\mathbf{F}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})+2\delta\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\bar{{\bf x}})\|_{1}=\partial\psi(\bar{{\bf x}}).\end{array}

(37)

It is known that if
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is a local minimizer of problem (
34
), then
0\in\partial\psi(\mathbf{x}^{*})

(
Clarke 1990
)
.
A vector
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is called a
Clarke stationary point
of
\psi
if
0\in\partial\psi(\mathbf{x}^{*})
.

From the gradient consistence (
37
), if

\mathbf{x}^{*}
is a local minimizer of problem (
34
), then there are sequences
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}\}
and

\{\mu_{k}\}
such that

\lim_{\mathbf{x}_{k}\to\mathbf{x}^{*},\mu_{k}\downarrow 0}\nabla\Psi({\bf x}_{k},\mu_{k})=0.

For a fixed
\mu>0
, a vector
\mathbf{x}_{\mu}
is called a
stationary point
of
\Psi({\bf x}_{\mu},\mu)

if
\nabla\Psi({\bf x}_{\mu},\mu)=0.
From the gradient consistence, we have

\lim_{\mu\downarrow 0}\nabla\Psi({\bf x}_{\mu},\mu)=0

and if
\{\mathbf{x}_{\mu}\}
is bounded, then there is subsequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{\mu_{k}}\}
of
\{\mathbf{x}_{\mu}\}
such that

\{\mathbf{x}_{\mu_{k}}\}
converges to a stationary point of
\psi
as
\mu_{k}\downarrow 0
.

4.2
Complexity for problem (
34
)

Let the linearization of
\psi
at
\mathbf{x}
be defined as follows

\phi({\bf x},{\bf s})=\|{\bf F}({\bf x})\|^{2}+2({\bf F}^{\prime}({\bf x})^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}))^{\top}{\bf s}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf F}({\bf x})+{\bf F}^{\prime}({\bf x}){\bf s})\|_{1}.

An
\epsilon
critical point of
\psi
is defined in
Cartis et al. 2011
by the linearization of
\psi
as follows.

We said that
{\bf x}^{*}
is an
\epsilon
critical point
of
\psi
if

\phi({\bf x}^{*},0)-\min_{\|{\bf s}\|\leq 1}\phi({\bf x}^{*},{\bf s})\leq\epsilon.

(38)

When
\epsilon=0
in (
38
),

{\bf x}^{*}
is called a
critical point
of
\psi
, that is,

\min_{\|{\bf s}\|\leq 1}\phi({\bf x}^{*},{\bf s})=\phi({\bf x}^{*},0).

(39)

We can verify that if
{\bf x}^{*}
is a local minimizer of
\psi
, then (
39
) holds. Indeed, by Taylor’s theorem, for any small
\alpha\geq 0
, and
{\bf s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0<\|{\bf s}\|\leq 1
, we have

\psi({\bf x}^{*}+\alpha{\bf s})\leq\phi({\bf x}^{*},\alpha{\bf s})+o(\alpha)+o(\alpha)2\delta\|{\bf s}\|=\phi({\bf x}^{*},\alpha{\bf s})+o(\alpha),

and by the convexity of
\phi({\bf x}^{*},\cdot)
, we have

\phi({\bf x}^{*},\alpha{\bf s})=\phi({\bf x}^{*},\alpha{\bf s}+(1-\alpha)0)\leq\alpha\phi({\bf x}^{*},{\bf s})+(1-\alpha)\phi({\bf x}^{*},0).

These two inequalities with
\phi({\bf x}^{*},0)=\psi({\bf x}^{*})
imply

0\leq\psi(\mathbf{x}^{*}+\alpha{\bf s})-\psi(\mathbf{x}^{*})\leq\phi({\bf x}^{*},\alpha{\bf s})+o(\alpha)-\psi(\mathbf{x}^{*})\leq\alpha(\phi({\bf x}^{*},{\bf s})-\psi({\bf x}^{*}))+o(\alpha).

Hence if
{\bf x}^{*}
a local minimizer of
\psi
, then a minimizer
{\bf s}^{*}
of (
39
) must satisfy

\phi({\bf x}^{*},{\bf s}^{*})=\phi({\bf x}^{*},0).

Moreover, using the convexity of
\phi({\bf x}^{*},\cdot)
again, we have

0\in\partial\phi({\bf x}^{*},0)=2{\bf F}^{\prime}({\bf x}^{*})^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}^{*})+2\delta\partial\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}^{*})\|_{1}=\partial\psi({\bf x}^{*}).

(40)

Hence
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is a critical point of
\psi
if and only if
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is a Clarke stationary point.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and
\mu\leq\epsilon/(8r\delta)
. If
\|\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu},\mu)\|

\leq\epsilon/2
, then

\phi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu},0)-\min_{\|{\bf s}\|\leq 1}\phi({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu},{\bf s})\leq\epsilon,

(41)

that is,
\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu}
is an
\epsilon
critical point of
\psi
.

Proof 4.2
.

We verify (
41
) as follows. Let

{\bf s}_{\mu}\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\|{\bf s}\|\leq 1}\phi({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu},{\bf s}).
We have

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu},0)-\phi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu},{\bf s}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu})-\psi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu})-2({\bf F}^{\prime}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu})^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu}))^{\top}{\bf s}_{\mu}-2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}({\bf F}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu})+{\bf F}^{\prime}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu}){\bf s}_{\mu})\|_{1}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-2({\bf F}^{\prime}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu})^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu}))^{\top}{\bf s}_{\mu}-2\delta\Theta({\bf C}^{\top}({\bf F}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu})+{\bf F}^{\prime}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu}){\bf s}_{\mu}),\mu)+2\delta\Theta({\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}^{*}_{\mu}),\mu)+4\delta r\mu

\displaystyle\leq-\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu},\mu)^{\top}{\bf s}_{\mu}+4r\mu\delta

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu},\mu)\|\|{\bf s}_{\mu}\|+4r\mu\delta\leq\epsilon,

where the first inequality uses (
36
), the second inequality uses the
convexity and twice continuously differentiability of
\Theta
and the last inequality uses
\|{\bf s}_{\mu}\|\leq 1

and the assumption of this proposition.

Suppose
{\bf F}^{\prime}(\cdot)^{\top}{\bf F}(\cdot)
and
{\bf F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
are Lipschitz continuous with
constants
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
. Then the gradient of
\Psi
is Lipschitz continuous
with
L_{\Psi}:=\gamma(L_{1}+\delta L_{2}\mu^{-1})
where
\gamma
is a constant which is independent
of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mu
. Hence we can find an
\epsilon
stationary point of the continuously differentiable function
\Psi(\cdot,\mu)
for any given
\mu>0
at most
O(L_{\Psi}\epsilon^{-2})
function evaluations using standard steepest descent methods with linesearch or trust-region safeguards
(
Cartis et al. 2011
;
Cartis et al. 2012
;
Nesterov 2004
)
. Hence from Proposition
4.1
, we can choose
\mu=\epsilon/(8r\delta)
and find
\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu}
such that

\|\Psi(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mu},\mu)\|\leq\epsilon/2
at most
O(L_{\Psi}\epsilon^{-2})
function and gradient evaluations, which means that we can find
an
\epsilon
critical
point of the nonsmooth function
\psi
at most

O(\epsilon^{-2}+\delta^{2}r\epsilon^{-3})
evaluations of
{\bf F}
and
{\bf F}^{\prime}

via a smoothing function. When

is independent of dimension
n,m
, the complexity is
O(\epsilon^{-2}+\delta^{2}\epsilon^{-3})
, which is independent of the dimension
m,n
.
Moreover, if
\epsilon\geq\delta^{2}
, then the complexity bound is
O(\epsilon^{-2})
, which is the lower bound for finding stationary points of smooth nonconvex functions
(
Carmon et al. 2020
;
Cartis et al. 2011
)
and independent of the dimension of the problem.
One of the advantages of smoothing methods is to
make use of efficient optimization software for solving
smooth optimization problems.

Remark 4.3
.

In
Cartis et al. 2011
, Cartis et al propose a trust region method for a composite nonsmooth nonconvex optimization problem

, Gratton et al generated the results in

to inexact function values, and
proposed an adaptive regularization algorithm for problem (

). Problem
(

) and satisfies Assumption
AS.1-AS.4 in
Gratton et al. 2021
when

is continuously differentiable and the level set

is bounded, where

is the initial point of the algorithm. Hence it is interesting to develop efficient codes to implement the algorithms in

;
Gratton et al. 2021
for finding an

satisfying (

), which needs at most

4.3
Complexity for minimax problem (
2
)

For a general min-max problem

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in{\cal Y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

(43)

where
{\cal Y}
is a convex set, the widely used first order optimality condition is

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=0,\quad\quad\mathbf{y}={\rm Proj}_{{\cal Y}}(\mathbf{y}+\alpha\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})),

(44)

where
\alpha
is a positive scaler. The system of nonlinear equations (
44
) is a necessary condition for local minimax points of (
43
)
(
Jiang & Chen 2023
;
Jin et al. 2020
)
. Many algorithms have been developed to find stationary points satisfying (
44
)
(
Diakonikolas et al. 2021
;
Jin et al. 2020
;
Yang et al. 2020
)
. However, as shown in Subsection 1.2, minimax problem (
2
) does not have a local minimax point. Although problem (
2
) has stationary points and minimax points, the set of stationary points and the set of minimax points do not have a common point. Naturally, we have to define a meaningful
stationary point when the min-max problem does not have a local minimax point.

Definition 4.4
.

We say
(\hat{{\bf x}},\hat{{\bf y}})
is an
\epsilon
minimax critical point

of the min-max problem (
2
)
if
(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
satisfies

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathbf{x},0)-\min_{\|{\bf s}\|\leq 1}\varphi({\bf x},{\bf s})\leq\epsilon,

(45)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta}\|\mathbf{C}^{\top}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{y})).

(46)

We say
(\hat{{\bf x}},\hat{{\bf y}})
is a
minimax critical point

of the minimax problem (
2
) if
\epsilon=0
in (

).

Proposition 4.5
.

If
(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{y}})
is a global minimax point of problem (
2
), then
(
45
) holds with
\epsilon=0
, that is
(\hat{{\bf x}},\hat{{\bf y}})
is a minimax critical point
of problem (
2
).

Proof 4.6
.

By the definition of a global minimax point of (
2
), we have

\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}(\hat{{\bf x}})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}\leq\|{\bf F}(\hat{{\bf x}})-{\bf C}\hat{{\bf y}}\|^{2}\leq\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}.

Hence, we find that
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
is a minimizer of
\varphi
as follows,

\varphi(\hat{\mathbf{x}})=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}(\hat{{\bf x}})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}\leq\|{\bf F}(\hat{{\bf x}})-{\bf C}\hat{{\bf y}}\|^{2}\leq\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}=\min_{\mathbf{x}}\varphi(\mathbf{x}).

Following the discussion in Subsection 4.2,
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
is a critical point of

\varphi
, and thus (
45
) holds with
\epsilon=0
.

From
\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}(\hat{{\bf x}})-{\bf C}{\bf y}\|^{2}\leq\|{\bf F}(\hat{{\bf x}})-{\bf C}\hat{{\bf y}}\|^{2}
, we have
\hat{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{{\bf y}\in\Omega}\|{\bf F}(\hat{{\bf x}})-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf y}\|^{2}
.

From Theorem
2.1
, if
Assumption
2.1
holds,
then the inner function
\varphi
is given by (
5
). From Proposition 4.1, we can find a
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
such that (

) holds after at most

O(\epsilon^{-2}+\delta^{2}\epsilon^{-3})
evaluations of
{\bf F}
and
{\bf F}^{\prime}.

Moreover, by Assumption
2.1
,
\mathbf{C}^{\top}\mathbf{C}
is a diagonal matrix with positive diagonal elements, which implies that we can find
\hat{\mathbf{y}}
satisfying (
46
) by

\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i}=\delta\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1&\mbox{if $(\mathbf{C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}))_{i}>0$}\\
\{-1,1\}&\mbox{if $(\mathbf{C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}))_{i}=0$}\\
1&\mbox{if $(\mathbf{C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}))_{i}<0$,}\end{array}\right.\quad i=1,\ldots,r.

Hence, we can have the following theorem for complexity bound of the min-max problem (
2
).

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose Assumption
2.1
holds. We take at most

O(\epsilon^{-2}+\delta^{2}\epsilon^{-3})
evaluations of
{\bf F}
and
{\bf F}^{\prime}
to find an

with

.

The complexity of finding a near-stationary
point of the inner maximization function has been studied in some literature for minimax optimization problems. For example, the complexity results on a two-time-scale gradient descent ascent algorithm (GDA) for solving nonconvex concave minimax problems is provided in
(

)

5
Least squares solutions vs robust least squares solutions in the worst-case residual errors

We call
\mathbf{x}_{ls}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls}
a least squares solution
and a
\lambda
-robust least squares solution, respectively if

\mathbf{x}_{ls}\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}\|{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}

and

\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls}\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}\|{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}+2\lambda\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})\|_{1}.

In this section, we compare least squares solutions and

\lambda
-robust least squares solutions in the worse-case residual
error, that is,

Er_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})=\max_{\|\mathbf{y}\|\leq\delta}\|{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})-{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{y}\|^{2}

and

Er_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})=\max_{\|\mathbf{y}\|\leq\delta}\|{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})-{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{y}\|^{2}

for
\delta\geq 0
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the maximization
problem
\max_{\|\mathbf{y}\|\leq\delta}\|{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf C}^{\top}\mathbf{y}\|^{2}

has a closed form solution

\mathbf{y}_{i}=\delta\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1&\mbox{if $({\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf F}({\bf x}))_{i}\geq 0$}\\
1&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\quad i=1,\ldots,r,

where
{\bf Q}
is the orthogonal matrix in the QR decomposition of
{\bf C}={\bf Q}{\bf R}.

Moreover, by Theorem
2.1
, we have

Er_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})=\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|_{1}+\|{\bf C}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2}

and

Er_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})=\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|^{2}+2\delta\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|_{1}+\|{\bf C}\|_{F}^{2}\delta^{2},

which implies that the worse-case residual errors of a least squares solution
\mathbf{x}_{ls}
and a
\lambda
-robust least squares solution
\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls}
have the following difference

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\lambda}(\delta)&=Er_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})-Er_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})=\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|^{2}\\
\\
&+2\delta(\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|_{1}-\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|_{1}).\end{array}

(47)

Hence, we have the following Proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
\mathbf{x}_{ls}
is not a
\lambda
-robust least squares solution,
then
\Delta_{\lambda}(\delta)
increases linearly with respect
to
\delta
by the rate

2(\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|_{1}-\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|_{1}),
and
\Delta_{\lambda}(\delta)>0
when

\delta>\frac{\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|^{2}}{2(\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|_{1}-\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|_{1})}.

(48)

Proof 5.2
.

If
\mathbf{x}_{ls}
is not a minimizer of

\|{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}+2\lambda\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x})\|_{1}
, then

0\geq\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|^{2}>2\delta(\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|_{1}-\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|_{1}),

where we use the definition of least square solution
\mathbf{x}_{ls}
and

\lambda-
robust least square solution
\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls}
. Hence we have

\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|_{1}-\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|_{1}>0
,
and thus
\Delta_{\lambda}(\delta)
increases linearly with
respect to
\delta
by the rate

2(\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}_{ls})\|_{1}-\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf F}(\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}_{rls})\|_{1})

and
\Delta_{\lambda}(\delta)>0
when (
48
) holds.

Simple example

To illustrate Proposition
5.1
, we consider a simple example.

Example 5.1
Let

By a simple calculation, we find that

,

5.2
Integral equations with uncertain data

We consider the nonlinear integral equation

{\cal G}(u)(x)\equiv\int_{0}^{1}g(x,y)\phi(u(y))\,dy=f(x),

(49)

where

Greens function

g(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}y(1-x),\quad{\rm if}\,\,\ x>y\\

The operator

Gu(x)\equiv\int_{0}^{1}g(x,y)u(y)\,dy

-w^{\prime\prime}=v,\quad w(0)=w(1)=0.

In our example

\phi(u)=\frac{\sin(\pi u)+u^{3}}{1+u^{2}}.

(51)

f(x)=G\phi(u^{*}(x))\quad{\rm with}\quad u^{*}(x)=|x-0.025|

We add an

\min\|G(\phi(u))-f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|du/dx\|_{2}^{2}.

6
Conclusion

We consider the min-max problem (
2
) for robust nonlinear
least squares problems. We use the example in subsection 1.2 to show that the min-max problem (
2
) does not have a local minimax point and the first order optimality
conditions defined by gradients of the objective function can lead to incorrect solutions. We give an explicit formula (
5
)
for the value function of the inner maximization problem in (
2
). We provide error bounds from a solution of
the least squares problem (
1
) to the solution set of the
robust least squares problem. Moreover, we propose a smoothing method for finding a
minimax point of the min-max problem (
2
) by using the formula (
5
).
We show that finding an
\epsilon
minimax critical point of the min-max problem (
2
)
needs at most
O(\epsilon^{-2}+\delta^{2}\epsilon^{-3})

evaluations of the function value and gradient of the
objective function by the smoothing method. Moreover, we give an explicit form
for the difference
\Delta(\delta)
of
worst-case residual errors of a least squares solution and a robust least squares solution, and show
\Delta(\delta)
increases linearly as
\delta
increases. Numerical results of integral equations with uncertain data demonstrate the robustness of solutions of our approach and unstable behaviour of
least squares solutions disregarding uncertainties in the data.

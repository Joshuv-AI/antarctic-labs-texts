Hemivariational Inequalities on Graphs
Nouhayla Ait Oussaid, Khalid Akhlil, Sultana Ben Aadi, Mourad El Ouali, Anand Srivastav

Abstract
. In this paper, a new class of hemivariational inequalities is introduced. It concerns Laplace operator on locally finite graphs together with multivalued nonmonotone nonlinearities expressed in terms of Clarke’s subdifferential. First of all, we state and prove some results on the subdifferentiability of nonconvex functionals defined on graphs. Thereafter, an elliptic hemivariational inequality on locally finite graphs is considered and the existence and uniqueness of its weak solutions are proved by means of the well-known surjectivity result for pseudomonotone mappings. In the end of this paper, we tackle the problem of hemivariational inequalities of parabolic type on locally finite graphs and we prove the existence of its weak solutions.

1.
Introduction

Discrete calculus incorporates the various research works that focus on developing a proper theory for differential operators on discrete spaces with a net separation from the classical continuous calculus. From this perspective, discrete calculus should be differentiated from discretized calculus which concerns the discretization of the continuous framework for numerical and algorithmic purposes. Difference calculus, as a particular case of discrete calculus, is performed generally on the
d-
dimensional lattice graph (or grid)
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some
d\geq 1
and plays the role of an intermediate discipline. Discrete calculus aims then to establish a distinct and coherent core of calculus that operates purely in the discrete space without any reference to an underlying continuous counterpart. The philosophy behind this, is the fact that there is a solid connection between dynamics and the mathematical description of the space where they occur
[
45
,
9
]
.

The spaces of predilection for the discrete calculus are graphs and networks, from which cell complexes arise as general space structures
[
4
]
. The first application of graph theory to the modelling of physical systems came from Kirchhoff, who both developed the basic laws of circuit theory and also made fundamental contributions to graph theory
[
24
]
. Among applications of modern graph theory one can mention manifold learning, filtering (denoising), content extraction, ranking, clustering, and network characterization. The main technique here is to use the data to define weights on the network and then methods are used to formulate content extraction problems as convex energy minimization problems
[
13
,

]
. Nonconvex energy models appears also in data filtering on graphs with explicit discontinuities (rapid data change)
[

and in nonsmooth nonconvex Regularizer in variational models for image restoration and segmentation

After a decade from the development of the theory of nonlinear circuit networks in the sixties, operator theory on infinite graphs and the underlying Sobolev spaces began to be systematically developed as a theoretical core for studying elliptic and parabolic problems on graphs and networks. An embryonic study was initiated by M. Yamasaki and co-authors in

In many physical and social phenomena, the Laplacian operator arises naturally in the mathematical description of diffusion through discrete and continuous media. Discrete diffusion theory

In the development of functional analysis on graphs, the finite difference operator plays a fundamental role. It started with an intuition that goes back to G. Boole
[
3
]
revealing that the operator

\mathcal{I}^{T}\phi(\mathsf{v},\mathsf{w}):=\phi(\mathsf{v})-\phi(\mathsf{w}),\quad\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{V}}

can be looked as a discretized version of the first derivative of the function
\phi
defined in all points of the underlying graph
\mathsf{G}
. In the definition of Sobolev spaces on graphs, the operator
\mathcal{I}^{T}\phi
is an equivalent of the gradient. The parallel between discrete functional calculus and the classical continuous settings can be made by taking into account the following rules: Scalar functions replaces vectors of the node set, vector fields replace vectors of the edge set and gradient of scalar functions at some point replace evaluation of the difference operator at an edge.

The goal of this paper is to formulate a new class of variational-type inequalities consisting of nonmonotone multivalued perturbation of the discrete Laplacian on a locally finite graph. The pseudomonotone term is brought by a nonconvex functional defined by an integral. Let
\mathsf{j}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz function whose Clarke’s subdifferential satisfies a growth condition. The following sum functional

\mathsf{J}(\phi)=\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}(\phi(\mathsf{v}))

can be looked as a discretized version of the well-studied integral functionals of the form

\mathsf{J}(\phi)=\int_{\mathscr{O}}\mathsf{j}(\phi(x))\,d\mu(x)

The primary question in such situation is the relation between the subdifferential of
\mathsf{j}
and
\mathsf{J}
. In the integral functional case, this is what we commonly call Aubin-Clarke theorem. In this paper, we prove its discrete couterpart, that is

\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi)\subset\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\partial\mathsf{j}(\phi(\mathsf{v}))

Having this discrete version of Aubin-Clarke theorem at ones disposal one may formulate elliptic problem as follows

\langle\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}\phi,\psi-\phi\rangle+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}^{\circ}(\phi(\mathsf{v});\psi(\mathsf{v})-\phi(\mathsf{v}))\geq\langle\mathsf{f},\psi-\phi\rangle

(1.1)

where
\mathsf{j}^{\circ}
is the generalized Clarke directional derivative of
\mathsf{j}
and its parabolic counterpart

\langle\phi^{\prime}+\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}\phi,\psi-\phi\rangle+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}^{\circ}(t,\phi(\mathsf{v});\psi(\mathsf{v})-\phi(\mathsf{v}))\geq\langle\mathsf{f},\psi-\phi\rangle

(1.2)

Such problems will be called discrete hemivariational inequalities or hemivariational inequalities on graphs.

The general theory of hemivariational theory is a natural generalization of the classical variational theory where convex energy functionals are involved. Mathematical formulation of many engineering problems reveals cases that lack of monotonicity and corresponds to nonconvex superpotentials which cannot be formulated by the classical variational tools. By applying the mathematical notion of generalized gradient of Clarke
[
6
]
, Panagiotopoulos
[
38
]
introduced for the first time the so-called hemivariational inequalities. Since then, such formulation found applications in many fields, i.e. Navier-Stokes equations
[
25
,
26
]
, boundary value problems
[
1
,
27
]
, frictional contact
[
29
]
, history-dependent problems
[
42
]
, nonlocal problems
[
49
]
to name a few. Different methods are applied for the solvability of hemivariational inequalities, we can mention Galerkin approximation method, critical point theory, surjectivity theorems, extremal solutions method, Rothe approximation method , equilibrium problem method, penalty method..etc. The main assumption on the locally Lipschitz function include Rauch condition, growth condition or unilateral growth condition.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we recall the functional setting on graphs and some concepts from nonsmooth analysis. In Section 3 we extend to the framework of locally finite graphs the Aubin-Clarke Theorem concerning the subdifferentiability of sum functionals. This may serve as a building block for developing variational methods for elliptic and parabolic problems involving the discrete Laplace operator and nonsmooth corresponding energy functional. In Section 3, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the elliptic hemivariational problem on locally finite graphs. The main tool is the well-know surjectivity result for pseudomonotone mappings. Section 4 is devoted to the discrete parabolic hemivariational inequality. The existence of a solution is reached by the use of a surjectivity result for the sum of maximal monotone and pseudomonotone mappings. In the last section, we provide some extensions to the problems discussed in previous sections. It concerns Galerkin scheme for discrete hemivariational inequalities, discrete variational-hemivariational inequalities and discrete quasi-hemivariational inequalities.

2.1.
Sobolev spaces on graphs

For the concepts on graphs used in this paper and the underlying functional analysis which is the theoretical core we deploy in our formulations, we refer to the complete and self-contained book

Let
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E})
be a direct graph, where
\mathsf{V}
is the set of nodes, which is finite or countable set and
E
the set of edges, which is a subset of
\mathsf{V}\times\mathsf{V}
. A weighted graph is a quadruple
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{E},\mathsf{V},\rho,\mu)
where
(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E})
is a direct graph,
\mu:\mathsf{V}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is some given function and
\rho:\mathsf{E}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is some other given function such that
\rho(\mathsf{e})=\rho(\bar{\mathsf{e}})
whenever
\mathsf{e},\,\bar{\mathsf{e}}\in\mathsf{E}
(
\bar{\mathsf{e}}=(\mathsf{w},\mathsf{v})
when
\mathsf{e}=(\mathsf{v},\mathsf{w})
). For
\mathsf{e}=(\mathsf{v},\mathsf{w})
, we note
\mathsf{e}_{-}:=\mathsf{v}
the initial endpoint of
\mathsf{e}
and
\mathsf{e}_{+}:=\mathsf{w}
the terminal endpoint of
\mathsf{e}
and we say that they are adjacent (shortly
\mathsf{v}\sim\mathsf{w}
).

Define

\eta_{\mathsf{ve}}^{+}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1&\quad\text{if }v\text{ is initial endpoint of }\mathsf{e}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.,\qquad\eta_{\mathsf{ve}}^{-}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1&\quad\text{if }v\text{ is terminal endpoint of }\mathsf{e}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

Definition 2.1
.

A weighted graph
\mathsf{G}:=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},\rho,\mu)
is called outward locally finite if its outdegree function satisfies

\mathsf{deg}^{+}(\mathsf{v}):=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\eta_{\mathsf{ve}}^{+}\rho(\mathsf{e})\leq\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{v}}^{+}\quad\text{for all }\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}\text{ and some }\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{v}}^{+}>0.

It is called inward locally finite if its indegree function satisfies

\mathsf{deg}^{-}(\mathsf{v}):=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\eta_{\mathsf{ve}}^{-}\rho(\mathsf{e})\leq\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{v}}^{-}\quad\text{for all }\mathsf{v}\in V\text{ and some }\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{v}}^{-}>0.

It is locally finite if it is both inward and outward locally finite, i.e., if its degree function satisfies

\mathsf{deg}(\mathsf{v}):=\mathsf{deg}^{+}(\mathsf{v})+\mathsf{deg}^{-}(\mathsf{v})\leq\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{v}}\quad\text{ for all }\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}\text{ and some }\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{v}}.

Example 2.2
.

If
\mathsf{G}
is unweighted, then it is locally finite if and only if each node has only finitely incident edges.

Throughout this paper we suppose that
\mathsf{G}:=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},\rho,\mu)
is a locally finite graph such that
\rho(\mathsf{e})>0
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}
,
\mu(\mathsf{v})>0
for all
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}
and
\mu(\mathsf{V})<\infty
. Let
p\in[1,+\infty)
, we denote by
\ell^{p}(\mathsf{E},\rho)
the space of all functions
\varphi:\mathsf{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathsf{E},\rho)}:=\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}|\varphi(\mathsf{e})|^{p}\rho(\mathsf{e})\right)^{1/p}<\infty

or else

\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathsf{E},\rho)}:=\sup_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}|\varphi(\mathsf{e})|\rho(\mathsf{e})<\infty

For

,
\ell^{2}(\mathsf{E},\rho)
is a Hilbert space endowed with the inner product

\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\rho}=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\rho(\mathsf{e})\varphi_{1}(\mathsf{e})\varphi_{2}(\mathsf{e})

Similarly, we denote by
\ell^{p}(\mathsf{V},\mu)
the space of all functions
\phi:\mathsf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\|\phi\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathsf{V},\mu)}:=\left(\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}|\phi(\mathsf{v})|^{p}\mu(\mathsf{v})\right)^{1/p}<\infty

\|\phi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathsf{V},\mu)}:=\sup_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}|\phi(\mathsf{v})|\mu(\mathsf{v})<\infty

For

,

is a Hilbert space endowed with the inner product

\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle_{\mu}=\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\phi_{1}(\mathsf{v})\phi_{2}(\mathsf{v})

We simply write
\ell^{p}(\mathsf{E})
if

and
\ell^{p}(\mathsf{V})
if

.
Define

(\mathcal{I}^{T}\phi)(\mathsf{e})=\phi(\mathsf{e}_{+})-\phi(\mathsf{e}_{-}),\quad\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{V}},\,\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}

The difference operator

can be looked as a discretized version of the first derivative of a function

2.2.
Abstract surjectivity result

Let
E
be a reflexive Banach space with its dual
E^{*}
and
A:D(A)\subset E\rightarrow 2^{E^{*}}
be a multivalued function, where
D(A)=\{u\in E:\,Au\neq\emptyset\}
, stands for the domain of
A
. We say that
A
is
monotone
if
\langle u^{*}-v^{*},u-v\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}\geq 0
for all
u^{*}\in Au
,
v^{*}\in Av
and
u,\,v\in D(A)
. If moreover,
A
has a maximal graph in the sense of inclusion among all monotone operators, then we say that
A
is maximal monotone. We say that
A
is
pseudomonotone
operators if it satisfies the following properties,

(a)

for each
u\in E
, the set
Au
is nonempty, closed and convex in
E^{*}
.

(b)

A
is upper semicontinuous from each finite dimensional subspace of
E
into
E^{*}
endowed with its weak topology;

(c)

if
u_{n}\rightarrow u
weakly weakly in
E
,
u_{n}^{*}\in Au_{n}
and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\limits\,\langle u_{n}^{*},u_{n}-u\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}\leq 0
, then for each
v\in E
there exists
v^{*}\in Au
such that
\langle v^{*},u-v\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\limits\,\langle u_{n}^{*},u_{n}-v\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}
.

For a linear, maximal monotone operator
L:D(L)\subset E\rightarrow E^{*}
, an operator
A
is said to be
pseudomonotone with respect to
D(L)
(or
L-
pseudomonotone) if
(a)
and
(b)
are satisfied and

(c’)

for each sequences
\{u_{n}\}\subset D(L)
and
\{u_{n}^{*}\}\subset E^{*}
with
u_{n}\rightarrow u
weakly in
E
,
Lu_{n}\rightarrow Lu
weakly in
E^{*}
,
u_{n}^{*}\in Au_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{n}^{*}\rightarrow u^{*}
weakly in
E^{*}
and
\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\langle u_{n}^{*},u_{n}-u\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}\leq 0
, we have
u^{*}\in Au
and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle u_{n}^{*},u_{n}\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}=\langle u^{*},u\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}
.

A
is
coercive
if there exists a function
c:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
c(r)\rightarrow\infty
as
r\to\infty
such that
\langle u^{*},u\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}\geq c(\|u\|_{E})\|u\|_{E}
for every
(u,u^{*})\in\mathrm{Graph}(A)
.

Now let
f:E\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous functional. The mapping
\partial_{c}f:E\rightarrow 2^{E^{*}}
defined by

\partial_{c}f(u)=\{u^{*}\in E^{*}:\,\langle u^{*},v-u\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}\leq f(v)-f(u)\text{ for all }v\in E\},

is called the subdifferential of

. Any element

is called a subgradient of

at

. It is a well know fact that

is a maximal monotone operator.

Let
F:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous functional and

. We denote by
F^{\circ}(u;v)
the generalized Clarke directional derivative of

at the point

The generalized Clarke gradient

of

is defined by

\partial F(u)=\{\xi\in E^{*}:\,\langle\xi,v\rangle_{E^{*}\times E}\leq F^{\circ}(u;v)\text{ for all }v\in E\}.

We collect the following properties

(a)

the function
v\mapsto F^{\circ}(u;v)
is positively homogeneous, subadditive and satisfies

|F^{\circ}(u;v)|\leq L_{u}\|v\|_{E}\text{for all }v\in E,

where

is the rank of

.

(b)

(u,v)\mapsto F^{\circ}(u;v)
is upper semicontinuous.

(c)

\partial F(u)
is a nonempty, convex and weakly
∗
compact subset of
E^{*}
with
\|\xi\|_{E^{*}}\leq L_{u}
for all
\xi\in\partial F(u)
.

(d)

for all
v\in E

.

We say that a function

If

3.
Nonconvex sum functionals on graphs

In this section we will prove the discrete counterpart of the Aubin-Clarke theorem concerning the subdifferentiability of nonconvex sum functionals. We consider a function
\mathsf{j}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which satisfies the following hypothesis
\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})
:

\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})_{1}

\mathsf{j}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz.

\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})_{2}

there exists
\alpha_{\mathsf{j}}>0
such that

|z|\leq\alpha_{\mathsf{j}}(1+|s|),\qquad\forall z\in\partial\mathsf{j}(s)

Next we define the superpotential
\mathsf{J}:\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathsf{J}(\phi)=\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\,\mathsf{j}(\phi(\mathsf{v}))

for all
\phi\in\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu)
. The sum functional

\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}+\partial\mathsf{J}:\mathsf{W}_{0}\rightarrow\mathsf{W}_{0}
is pseudomonotone, which completes the proof.
∎

Let’s consider the following additional assumption

\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j}^{0})

There exists
\alpha_{\mathsf{j}^{0}}>0
such that

\mathsf{j}^{0}(s;t-s)+\mathsf{j}^{0}(t;s-t)\leq\alpha_{\mathsf{j}^{0}}|t-s|^{2}

for all
s,\,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under assumptions
\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{G})
,
\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})
and
\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j}^{0})
with

\alpha_{\mathsf{j}^{0}}\vee\alpha_{\mathsf{j}}<\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu},

the weak solution of the Problem (
4.1
) is unique.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be two weak solutions of Problem (
4.1
). It follows then that

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}\phi_{1},\psi-\phi_{1}\rangle+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu\,(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}^{0}(\phi_{1}(\mathsf{v});\psi(\mathsf{v})-\phi_{1}(\mathsf{v}))\geq\langle\mathsf{f},\psi-\phi_{1}\rangle,\,\text{for all }\phi

(4.8)

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}\phi_{2},\psi-\phi_{2}\rangle+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu\,(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}^{0}(\phi_{2}(\mathsf{v});\psi(\mathsf{v})-\phi_{2}(\mathsf{v}))\geq\langle\mathsf{f},\psi-\phi_{2}\rangle,\,\text{for all }\phi

(4.9)

We replace
\phi
in (
4.8
) by
\phi_{2}
and in (
4.9
) by
\phi_{1}
and we sum up the two inequalities. We obtain that

\langle\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}),\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}\rangle+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu\,(\mathsf{v})\{\mathsf{j}^{0}(\phi_{1}(\mathsf{v});\phi_{2}(\mathsf{v})-\phi_{1}(\mathsf{v}))+j^{0}(\phi_{2}(\mathsf{v});\phi_{1}(\mathsf{v})-\phi_{2}(\mathsf{v}))\}\geq 0

It follows that

-\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu}\|\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}\|^{2}_{\mathsf{W}_{0}}+\alpha_{\mathsf{j}^{0}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu\,(\mathsf{v})|\phi_{2}(\mathsf{v})-\phi_{1}(\mathsf{v})|^{2}\geq 0

Thus

(\alpha_{\mathsf{j}^{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu})\|\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}\|^{2}_{\mathsf{W}_{0}}\geq 0

If
\alpha_{\mathsf{j}^{0}}<\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu}
, we have
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}
, which completes the proof.

∎

5.
Existence Result for parabolic problem

For
0<\mathsf{T}<\infty
, we denote by
\mathcal{V}:=L^{2}(0,\mathsf{T};\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu))
the usual time Sobolev space endowed with the norm

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}=\left(\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu)}\,dt\right)^{1/2}

and consider a function
\mathsf{j}:(0,\mathsf{T})\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which satisfies the following hypothesis
\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})
:

\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})_{1}

for each
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
t\mapsto\mathsf{j}(t,r)
is measurable on
(0,\mathsf{T})
,

\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})_{2}

for a.e.
t\in(0,\mathsf{T})
, the functional
r\mapsto\mathsf{j}(t,r)
is locally Lipschitz,

\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})_{3}

there exists
\alpha_{\mathsf{j}}>0
such that

|z|\leq\alpha_{\mathsf{j}}(1+|r|),\qquad\text{for all }z\in\partial\mathsf{j}(t,r)\text{ and a.e. }t\in(0,\mathsf{T}).

We define the superpotential
\mathsf{J}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathsf{J}(\phi)=\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\,\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathsf{j}(t,\phi(t,\mathsf{v}))\,dt

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{V}
. Similarly to Propostion
3.2
, we have the following result

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume the hypothesis
H(\mathsf{j})
is fulfilled. Then the functional
\mathsf{J}
is locally Lipschitz and there exists
\mathsf{c}_{\mathsf{J}}>0
such that the following inequalities hold

\mathsf{J}^{0}(\phi;\psi)\leq\mathsf{c}_{\mathsf{J}}\left(1+\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{V}},\quad\forall\phi,\,\psi\in\mathcal{V}

and

\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{V}}\leq\mathsf{c}_{\mathsf{J}}\left(1+\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right),\quad\forall\theta\in\partial(\mathsf{J}_{|\mathcal{V}})(\phi),\,\phi\in\mathcal{V}

We introduce the function spaces

\mathcal{W}_{0}=L^{2}(0,T;\mathsf{W}_{0}),\qquad\mathcal{M}_{0}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\,|\,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\}

where the time derivative
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}
is understood in the sense of vector-valued distributions. The norm

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}:=\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

make the space
\mathcal{M}_{0}
a Banach space. Moreover, the embeddings
\mathcal{M}_{0}\subset L^{2}(0,T;\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu))
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}\subset C(0,T;\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu))
are compact and continuous, respectively.

The purpose of this section is to prove the existence of solutions for the parabolic hemivariational inequalities on graphs, which can be stated as follows: Find
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
such that

\begin{cases}\,\phi^{\prime}+\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}\phi+\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi)\ni\mathsf{f}&\qquad\text{in }\mathsf{V}\times(0,T)\\
\,\phi(\mathsf{v},0)=\phi_{0}&\qquad\text{in }\mathsf{V}\end{cases}

(5.1)

Definition 5.2
.

We say that
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a weak solution to problem (
5.1
), if
\phi(0,\mathsf{v})=\phi_{0}(\mathsf{v})
in
\mathsf{V}
, and the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\frac{\partial\phi(t,\mathsf{v})}{\partial t}\left(\psi(t,\mathsf{v})-\phi(t,\mathsf{v})\right)\,dt+\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}\phi(t,\mathsf{v})\left(\psi(t,\mathsf{v})-\phi(t,\mathsf{v})\right)\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}^{0}(t,\phi(t,\mathsf{v});\psi(t,\mathsf{v})-\phi(t,\mathsf{v}))\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mathsf{f}(t,\mathsf{v})\left(\psi(t,\mathsf{v})-\phi(t,\mathsf{v})\right)\,dt

for all
\psi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
.

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathsf{f}\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
and assume that the hypotheses
H(\mathsf{G})
and
\mathsf{H}(\mathsf{j})
are fulfilled. If
\mathsf{\alpha}_{\mathsf{J}}<\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu}
, then the problem (
5.1
) admits a weak solution.

Proof.

First, we define the operator
\Lambda:\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{0}
by

\Lambda(\phi)(\psi):=\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\psi(t,\mathsf{v})\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}(\phi(t,\mathsf{v})+\tilde{\phi}_{0}(t,\mathsf{v}))\,dt,

for all
\phi,\,\psi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
and where
\tilde{\phi}_{0}
is such that
\tilde{\phi}_{0}(t,\mathsf{v})=\phi_{0}(\mathsf{v})
for all
(t,\mathsf{v})\in(0,\mathsf{T})\times\mathsf{V}
. From (
4
) we have

\displaystyle\Lambda(\phi)(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\psi(t,\mathsf{v})\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}(\phi(t,\mathsf{v})+\tilde{\phi}_{0}(t,\mathsf{v}))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\overline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\vee\overline{\alpha}_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\|\psi\|_{\mathsf{W}_{0}}\,\|\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathsf{W}_{0}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\overline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\vee\overline{\alpha}_{\mu}\,\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\,\|\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

It follows that

\|\Lambda(\phi)\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\overline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\vee\overline{\alpha}_{\mu}\left(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\right),\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}.

Thus, the operator
\Lambda
is continuous. Moreover, the operator
\Lambda
is strongly monotone. In fact, from (
4
), one can obtain

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda(\phi)-\Lambda(\psi),\phi-\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle\Lambda(\phi),\phi-\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\langle\Lambda(\psi),\phi-\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})(\phi(t,\mathsf{v})-\psi(t,\mathsf{v}))\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}(\phi(t,\mathsf{v})+\tilde{\phi}_{0}(t,\mathsf{v}))\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})(\phi(t,\mathsf{v})-\psi(t,\mathsf{v}))\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}(\psi(t,\mathsf{v})+\tilde{\phi}_{0}(t,\mathsf{v}))\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})(\phi(t,\mathsf{v})-\psi(t,\mathsf{v}))\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}(\phi(t,\mathsf{v})-\psi(t,\mathsf{v}))\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\|\phi(t,.)-\psi(t,.)\|^{2}_{\mathsf{W}_{0}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu}\,\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

for all
\phi,\,\psi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
.

Define the operator
\mathsf{L}:\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{L})\subset\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{0}
by

\mathsf{L}\phi=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t},\qquad\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{L}):=\{\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\,|\,\phi(0)=0\}

which is closed, linear, densely defined and maximal monotone operator
[
48
]
.

Now, we shall prove the hypotheses of the surjectivity theorem.

Claim 1:
The multivalued operator
\Lambda+\partial\mathsf{J}(.+\tilde{\phi}_{0}):\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{W}_{0}}
is bounded and pseudomonotone with respect to
\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{L})
.

In fact, by the properties of Clarke’s subdifferential, we deduce that the set
\Lambda(\phi)+\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0})
is nonempty, closed and convex in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
. By Proposition
5.1
and the continuity of
\Lambda
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Lambda\phi+\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Lambda\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}^{*}}+\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\overline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\vee\overline{\alpha}_{\mu}\left(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\right)+\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\left(1+\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}+(\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\vee\overline{\alpha}_{\mu})(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}})

which implies that
\Lambda+\partial\mathsf{J}(.+\tilde{\phi}_{0}):\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{W}_{0}}
is bounded. Moreover, since
\Lambda
is linear and continuous (hence demicontinuous) and
\partial\mathsf{J}
is uppersemicontinuous from
\mathcal{W}_{0}
to
w-\mathcal{W}_{0}
.

It remains to verify the last condition. let
\{\phi_{n}\}\subset\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{L})
and
\{\phi_{n}^{*}\}\subset\mathcal{W}_{0}
be such that
\phi_{n}\rightarrow\phi
weakly in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
,
\mathsf{L}\phi_{n}\rightarrow\mathsf{L}\phi
weakly in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
,
\phi_{n}^{*}\in\Lambda\phi_{n}+\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi_{n}+\tilde{\phi}_{0})
with
\phi_{n}^{*}\rightarrow\phi^{*}
weakly in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
, and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle\phi_{n}^{*},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\leq 0

(5.2)

Then, we are able to find a sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{W}_{0}
such that
\xi_{n}\in\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi_{n}+\tilde{\phi}_{0})
and

\phi_{n}^{*}=\Lambda\phi_{n}+\xi_{n},\qquad\text{for each }n\in\mathbb{N}

Consequently, from (
5.2
), we get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle\Lambda\phi_{n},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle\xi_{n},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\leq 0

(5.3)

Since
\mathsf{W}_{0}\subset\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu)
and the embedding of
\mathsf{W}_{0}
in
\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu)
is compact, we have that
\phi_{n}
strongly converges to
\phi
in
\mathcal{V}
. Furthermore, one has

\partial(\mathsf{J}_{|\mathcal{W}_{0}})(\phi)\subset\partial(\mathsf{J}_{|\mathcal{V}})(\phi)

(5.4)

which means that

\langle\xi_{n},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}=\langle\xi_{n},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}

(5.5)

Further, from the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
, we have that
\{\xi_{n}\}
is bounded both in
\mathcal{V}
and in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
. Then, by (
5.5
), we pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
to get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\xi_{n},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\xi_{n},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}=0

This convergence combined with (
5.3
) and the monotonicity of
\Lambda
implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}^{2}\leq\mathsf{A}^{-1}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle\Lambda\phi_{n},\phi_{n}-\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\mathsf{A}^{-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Lambda\phi,\phi-\phi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\leq 0

where
\mathsf{A}=\frac{1}{2}\underline{\alpha}_{\gamma}\wedge\underline{\alpha}_{\mu}
. Hence
\phi_{n}\to\phi
strongly in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
. On the other side, the reflexivity of
\mathcal{W}_{0}
and boundedness of
\{\xi_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{W}_{0}
allow to assume, at least for a subsequence, that
\xi_{n}
converges weakly in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
to some
\xi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
.
Since
\partial\mathsf{J}
is upper semicontinuous from
\mathcal{W}_{0}
to
w-\mathcal{W}_{0}
and it has convex and closed values, it is closed from
\mathcal{W}_{0}
to
w-\mathcal{W}_{0}
(see
[
21
, Theorem 1.1.4]
). Therefore, we obtain
\xi\in\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0})
.

To conclude, we have
\phi^{*}=\xi+\Lambda\phi\in\Lambda\phi+\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0})
and

\langle\phi_{n}^{*},\phi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}=\langle\xi_{n}+\Lambda\phi_{n},\phi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\to\langle\xi+\Lambda\phi,\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}=\langle\phi^{*},\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

which means that the operator
\Lambda+\partial\mathsf{J}(.+\tilde{\phi}_{0}):\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{W}_{0}}
is pseudomonotone with respect to
\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{L})
.

Claim 2:
The operator
\Lambda+\partial\mathsf{J}(.+\tilde{\phi}_{0}):\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{W}_{0}}
is coercive.

For all
\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
one has

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda\phi+\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}=\langle\Lambda\phi,\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\langle\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\mathsf{A}\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\mathsf{A}\,\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\langle\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\mathsf{A}\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\mathsf{A}\,\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\|\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0})\|_{\mathcal{V}}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\mathsf{A}\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\mathsf{A}\,\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\left(1+\|\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\mathsf{A}\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\mathsf{A}\,\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\left(\left(\mathsf{A}-\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\right)\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-(\mathsf{A}+\alpha_{\mathsf{J}})\,\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\right)\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq c(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}})\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}

where
c:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
c(r)=\left(\mathsf{A}-\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}\right)r-(\mathsf{A}+\alpha_{\mathsf{J}})\,\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}+\alpha_{\mathsf{J}}
. It is clear that
c(r)\rightarrow\infty
as
r\to\infty
, thus the operator
\Lambda+\partial\mathsf{J}(.+\tilde{\phi}_{0}):\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{W}_{0}}
is coercive.

We are now in a position to apply the surjectivity result. We deduce that there exists a function
\chi\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
with
\chi(0)=0
solving the following inclusion problem

\begin{cases}\mathsf{L}\chi+\Lambda\chi+\partial\mathsf{J}(\chi+\tilde{\phi}_{0})\ni\mathsf{f},\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{W}_{0}\\
\chi(0)=0.\end{cases}

(5.6)

Claim 3:
If
\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a solution to problem (
5.6
), then
\phi=\chi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}
is a weak solution to problem (
5.1
).

Let
\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a solution to problem (
5.6
). Hence
\phi=\chi+\tilde{\phi}_{0}
solves the following problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\Lambda(\phi-\tilde{\phi}_{0})+\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi)\ni\mathsf{f},\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{W}_{0}\\
\phi(0)=\phi_{0}.\end{cases}

(5.7)

By the definition of generalized Clarke subdifferential we obtain
(
5.1
)
. This completes the proof.

∎

6.
Concluding remarks

In this section we give some remarks and extensions of the results proved in previous sections.

(1)

Let
\Phi:\mathsf{W}_{0}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous functional such that
0\in\partial_{C}\Phi(\phi_{0})
, where
\partial_{C}
is the subdifferential in the sense of convex analysis. Suppose additionally that
\phi_{0}\in\mathrm{int}D(\Phi)
. Then, the variational-hemivariational inequality: Find
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
such that

\begin{cases}\,\phi^{\prime}+\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}}\phi+\partial\mathsf{J}(\phi)+\partial_{C}\Phi(\phi)\ni\mathsf{f}&\qquad\text{in }\mathsf{V}\times(0,T)\\
\,\phi(\mathsf{v},0)=\phi_{0}&\qquad\text{in }\mathsf{V}\end{cases}

(6.1)

admits a solution. To prove the existence of Problem (
6.1
), let us consider the functional
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{0}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

\Psi(\phi)=\int_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\Phi(\phi+\tilde{\phi}_{0})\,dt

Now, it suffices to continue on the proof of Theorem
5.3
and prove, additionally, that the operator
\partial_{C}\Psi
is maximal monotone and strongly quasi-bounded with
0\in\partial\Psi(0)
. The existence follows by the surjectivity result stated by Theorem 3.1 in
[
11
]
.

(2)

One can think about an alternative proof of existence in both the elliptic and parabolic problems by using the Galerkin scheme adapted to graph theory context. Let
(\mathsf{G}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a growing family of finite graphs that exhaust
\mathsf{G}
in the sense of
[
31
, Definition 4.1]
and
[
33
, Definition 3.3]
and consider the problem of finding
\phi_{n}
such that

\phi_{n}^{\prime}+\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,\kappa}^{\mathsf{G}_{n}}\phi_{n}+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}_{n}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}_{n}^{\prime}(\phi_{n}(\mathsf{v}))=\mathsf{f}

(6.2)

where
\mathsf{j}_{n}
is a mollification of
\mathsf{j}
. By using techniques from the proof of Theorem 3.6 in
[
16
]
and some standard calculation on the nonlinear term, one can prove that the sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n}
on
\mathsf{G}_{n}
is bounded in
H^{1}(0,\mathsf{T};\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu))
and weak
∗
in
L^{\infty}(0,\mathsf{T};\mathsf{W}_{0})
. By taking a subsequence if necessary, it possible to prove that
(\phi_{n})_{n}
converges to some
\phi
and
\mathsf{j}_{n}^{\prime}(\phi_{n})
converges in
\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu)
to some
\xi
. By the convergence theorem of Aubin and Cellina
[
2
]
, it is clear that
\xi\in\partial\mathsf{j}(\phi)
and by taking the limit in (
6.2
), one can see that
\phi
resolves Problem (
5.3
).

(3)

Let
\mathsf{h}
be some nonnegative continuous function which satisfies with
\mathsf{j}
the following growth condition

|\mathsf{h}(\xi_{1})\xi|\leq c(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|),\quad\text{for all }\xi_{1},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\text{ with }\xi\in\partial\mathsf{j}(\xi_{2})

where
c
is some nonnegative constant. One can prove a version of Aubin-Clarke theorem for discrete functionals in the form:

\mathsf{J}(\phi)=\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{h}(\phi(\mathsf{v}))\mathsf{j}(\phi(\mathsf{v}))

With the above hypotheses we have for
\phi
,
\psi
in
\ell^{2}(\mathsf{V},\mu)
that

\mathsf{J}^{\circ}(\phi;\psi)\leq\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mu(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{v})\mathsf{j}^{\circ}(\phi(\mathsf{v});\psi(\mathsf{v}))

With some modifications, one can prove that the quasi-hemivariational versions of Problems (
5.1
) and (
5.3
) admit weak solutions.

(4)

By using Theorem 3.6 in
[
16
]
, the theory in this paper can be applied for the operator
\mathscr{L}_{\gamma,0}^{\mathsf{G}}
if we assume that
\mathsf{G}
is uniformly locally finite and satisfy the
d-
isoperimetric inequality for some
d\geq 2

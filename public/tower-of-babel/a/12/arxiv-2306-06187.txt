1.
Introduction

The main aim of this article is to prove quantitative spectral inequalities for the Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions. More specifically, we show that the Faber-Krahn inequality is stable, where stability is quantified in terms of Newtonian capacities and Hausdorff contents of positive codimension. Our results hold for subdomains of the Euclidean space, the unit sphere and the hyperbolic space, and they are sharp up to a constant factor. We remark that Theorem
A
, our main result (see below), can be seen as a
geometric
solution of an issue raised by Brasco and De Philippis (
[
BD
, Open Problem 7.23]
), and it foundamentally rests on the
analytic
solution of the same problem by Allen, Kriventsov and Neumayer
[
AKN1
]
.
Our interest in these versions of the Faber-Krahn inequality arise also from applications in connection with the Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman monotonicity formula and with the Carleson
{\varepsilon}^{2}
-conjecture
about tangent points for Jordan domains
in the plane. Indeed, in this paper we also propose a natural extension of Carleson
{\varepsilon}^{2}
-conjecture to higher dimensions
in terms of a square function involving the characteristics of certain spherical domains, and we prove
the necessity of the finiteness of such square function at almost every tangent point. Sufficiency is shown in the companion paper
[
FTV
]
.

1.1.
Quantitative Faber-Krahn inequalities

Given a bounded open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we say that
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
is a Dirichlet eigenfunction of

\Omega
(for the Laplacian) if
u\not\equiv 0
and

-\Delta u=\lambda\,u,

for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. The number
\lambda
is the eigenvalue associated with
u
. It is well known that all the eigenvalues of the Laplacian are positive and the smallest one, i.e., the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
, satisfies

\lambda_{1}=\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx}.

Further the infimum is attained by an eigenfunction
u_{0}
which does not change sign, and so which can be assumed to be non-negative. We will denote by
\lambda_{\Omega}
(or
\lambda(\Omega)
) the first eigenvalue of
\Omega
and by
u_{\Omega}
the associated non-negative eigenfunction, normalized so that
\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
.

The classical Faber-Krahn inequality asserts that among all bounded open sets with a fixed volume, a ball minimizes the first eigenvalue.
Following many previous works (see, for example,
[
HN
]
and
[
Me
]
), Brasco, De Philippis, and Velichkov proved in
[
BDV
]
the following
sharp quantitative version of the Faber-Krahn inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded open set with
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega)=1
and let
B_{0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a ball with
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{0})=1
.
Then

(1.1)

\lambda_{\Omega}-\lambda_{B_{0}}\geq c\,\inf_{B}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega\triangle B)^{2},

where
c
is a positive absolute constant and the infimum is taken over all balls
B
with
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B)=1
.

The inequality above is sharp in the sense that the power
2
on the right hand side cannot be lowered.

The classical Faber-Krahn inequality also holds for subdomains of the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
or the hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. In this context one should take the Laplacian with respect to the Riemannian metric of the space (i.e., the Laplace-Beltrami operator) and one should consider geodesic balls. That is, for subdomains
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
or
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
with a given volume, the minimal
value of
\lambda_{\Omega}
is attained again by a geodesic ball among all open bounded domains of the
same volume. Further, recently Allen, Kriventsov, and Neumayer
[
AKN1
]
have obtained the following quantitative form.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}
be either
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, or
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, and let
\beta>0
.
Let
\Omega
be a relatively open subset of
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}
and let
B_{0}
be a geodesic ball in
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}
such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{0})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega)
.
In the case
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, suppose also that
\beta\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega)\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})-\beta
, and in the case

{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
or
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{H}}^{n}
only assume that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega)\leq\beta
.
Denote by
\lambda_{\Omega}
and
\lambda_{B_{0}}
the respective first Dirichlet eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}
in
\Omega
and
B_{0}
. Then

(1.2)

\lambda_{\Omega}-\lambda_{B_{0}}\geq c(\beta)\,\inf_{B}\Big({\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega\triangle B)^{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}|u_{\Omega}-u_{B}|^{2}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\Big),

where
c(\beta)>0
, the infimum is taken over all geodesic balls
B\subset{\mathbb{M}}^{n}

such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B)={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega)
, and
u_{\Omega}
and
u_{B}
are the corresponding first eigenfunctions in
\Omega
and
B
normalized so that
they are positive and
\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{M}}^{n})}=\|u_{B}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{M}}^{n})}=1
.
In the case
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, (
1.2
) also holds with the infimum over
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
replaced
by the choice of
B
equal to a ball centered at the
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
-barycenter of
\Omega
with
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B)={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega)

(possibly with a different constant
c(\beta)
).

In the theorem we have denoted by
\Delta_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}
the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}
.
For the definition of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
-barycenter of a ball in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, see Section
2.1
. The assumption involving the parameter
\beta
is necessary to prevent the domain from being too big and, in the case
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, also too small.
Remark that in the case
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
one can get an appropriate scaling invariant statement by renormalising. This is not case for
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
or
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.
Notice the presence of the additional term
\int_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}|u_{\Omega}-u_{B}|^{2}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
in the inequality (
1.2
) when compared to (
1.1
). In this term we assume that the functions
u_{\Omega}
and
u_{B}
vanish outside of
\Omega
and
B
respectively.

{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
or

.
We denote by

the geodesic distance in

and by

. We also write

to denote an open ball in

centered in

, with radius

.
Given

and

, we set
\rho B=B_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}(x,\rho r)
and we denote
\delta_{B}(x)={\rm dist}_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}(x,\partial_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}B)

We denote by

, and by

Our first main result in this paper is the following.

.

Given

Then we apply apply (
1.2
) by estimating
{\widetilde{u}}_{B}-{\widetilde{u}}_{\Omega}
from below in terms of the harmonic measure for
{\widetilde{\Omega}}
,
using the fact that
{\widetilde{u}}_{B}(x)\approx{\rm dist}(x,\partial{\widetilde{B}})
while

{\widetilde{u}}_{\Omega}(x)=0
in a large part of
{\widetilde{B}}\cap\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}
.
To relate this estimate to the
terms on the right hand side of (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and (
1.9
), we consider an auxiliary Lipschitz domain
{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}
and we relate the harmonic measure for
{\widetilde{\Omega}}
to the one for

{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}
using the maximum principle.
Lastly we take advantage of the fact that
the behavior of harmonic measure is well understood on Lipschitz domains.

1.2.
The Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman monotonicity formula and the Friedland-Hayman inequality

Given a domain in the unit sphere,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
whose first Dirichlet
eigenvalue is
\lambda_{\Omega}
, the characteristic constant of
\Omega
is the positive number
\alpha_{\Omega}
such that
\lambda_{\Omega}=\alpha_{\Omega}(n-1+\alpha_{\Omega})
.

Recall that the Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman (ACF) monotonicity formula asserts the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
R>0
. Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in W^{1,2}(B(x,R))\cap C(B(x,R))
be nonnegative subharmonic functions such that
u_{1}(x)=u_{2}(x)=0
and
u_{1}\cdot u_{2}\equiv 0
.
Set

(1.10)

J(x,r)=\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{|\nabla u_{1}(y)|^{2}}{|y-x|^{n-1}}dy\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{|\nabla u_{2}(y)|^{2}}{|y-x|^{n-1}}dy\right)

Then
J(x,r)
is an absolutely continuous function of
r\in(0,R)
and

(1.11)

\frac{\partial_{r}J(x,r)}{J(x,r)}\geq\frac{2}{r}\bigl(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\bigr).

where
\alpha_{i}
is the characteristic constant of the open subset
\Omega_{i}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
given by

\Omega_{i}=\bigl\{r^{-1}(y-x):y\in\partial B(x,r),\,u_{i}(y)>0\bigr\}.

Further, for
r\in(0,R/2)
and
i=1,2
, we have

(1.12)

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{|\nabla u_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|y-x|^{n-1}}dy\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{n+1}}\|\nabla u_{i}\|_{L^{2}(B(x,2r))}^{2}.

The Friedland-Hayman
[
FH
]
inequality ensures that, for any two disjoint open subsets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
,

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\geq 0,

so that
J(x,r)
is non-decreasing on
r
, by (
1.11
). In fact, more is known.
By Sperner’s inequality
[
Sp
]
, among all the open subsets with a fixed measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, the one that minimizes the characteristic constant is a spherical ball with the same measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
. That is to say,
if
B_{i}
is a spherical ball such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{i})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i})
and
\bar{\alpha}_{i}
denotes its characteristic constant, then

\alpha_{i}\geq\bar{\alpha}_{i}.

Further, if one of the sets
\Omega_{i}
differs from a hemisphere by a surface measure
h
,
that is,

\Big|{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i})-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})\Big|\geq h,

then

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\geq c\,h^{2}.

In this paper we will deduce other more precise estimates for
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2
using Theorem
B
and Theorem
1.2
.
To state the precise result, we need some additional notation.
Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be open and disjoint and let
H\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be half-space such that
0\in\partial H
. Denote

S_{H,1}={\mathbb{S}}^{n}\cap H,\qquad S_{H,2}={\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\overline{H}.

We denote

V_{c_{0}}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},H,a)=T_{c_{0}}(\Omega_{1},S_{H,1},a)\cup T_{c_{0}}(\Omega_{2},S_{H,2},a).

Notice that if both
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
satisfy the the CDC and
c_{0}
is small enough, then
V_{c_{0}}=(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},H,a)=T_{c_{0}}(S_{H,1}\setminus\Omega_{1})\cup T_{c_{0}}(S_{H,2}\setminus\Omega_{2})
.

For fixed
c_{0}>0
and
a\in(0,1)
, we denote for
0<s<n
,

{\varepsilon}_{s}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})=\inf_{H}\int_{V_{c_{0}}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},H,a)}{\rm dist}(y,\partial H)^{n-s}\,d{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}^{s}(y),

with the infimum taken over all half-space
H
such that
0\in\partial H
.
On the other hand, for
s=n
, we set

{\varepsilon}_{n}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})=\inf_{H}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\big((S_{H,1}\setminus\Omega_{1})\cup(S_{H,2}\setminus\Omega_{2})\big).

We will prove the following:

Theorem C
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
0<s\leq n
.
Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be open and disjoint.
For any
c_{0}>0
and
a\in(0,1)
we have

{\varepsilon}_{s}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})^{2}\lesssim_{s,c_{0},a}\min(1,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2).

Remark that when
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i})\to 0
, we have
\alpha_{i}\to\infty
, and thus
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\to\infty
too.
On the other hand, we always have
{\varepsilon}_{s}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\lesssim 1
by definition. This is the reason for
writing
\min(1,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2)
instead of
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2
on the right hand side of the inequality in Theorem
C
.

1.3.
Carleson’s conjecture

Next we introduce the precise notion of a tangent point for a pair of disjoint open sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.
For a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, a unit vector
u
,
and an aperture parameter
a\in(0,1)
we consider the two sided cone with axis in the direction of
u
defined by

X_{a}(x,u)=\bigl\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:|(y-x)\cdot u|>a|y-x|\bigr\}.

Given disjoint open sets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
x\in\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\Omega_{2}
,
we say that
x
is a tangent point for the pair
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
if
x\in{\partial\Omega}_{1}\cap{\partial\Omega}_{2}
and there exists a unit vector
u
such that, for all

a\in(0,1)
, there exists some
r>0
such that

(\partial\Omega_{1}\cup\partial\Omega_{2})\cap X_{a}(x,u)\cap B(x,r)=\varnothing,

and moreover, one component of
X_{a}(x,u)\cap B(x,r)
is contained in
\Omega_{1}
and the other in
\Omega_{2}
.
The hyperplane
L
orthogonal to
u
through
x
is called a tangent hyperplane at
x
. In case that
\Omega_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}
, we say that
x
is a tangent point for
\Omega_{1}
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
be a Jordan domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and set
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{1}
and

\Omega_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}
.
For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
r>0
, denote by
I_{1}(x,r)
and
I_{2}(x,r)
the longest open arcs of the circumference

\partial B(x,r)
contained in
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, respectively (they may be empty). Then we define

(1.13)

{\varepsilon}(x,r)=\frac{1}{r}\,\max\big(\big|\pi r-{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{1}(x,r))\big|,\,\big|\pi r-{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{2}(x,r))\big|\big).

The Carleson
{\varepsilon}^{2}
-square function is given by

(1.14)

{\mathcal{E}}(x)^{2}:=\int_{0}^{1}{\varepsilon}(x,r)^{2}\,\frac{dr}{r}.

Carleson’s conjecture, now a theorem, asserts the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a Jordan domain, let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{1}
, and let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the
associated square function defined in (
1.14
). Then
the set of tangent points for
\Omega_{1}

coincides with the subset of those points
x\in\Gamma
such that
{\mathcal{E}}(x)<\infty
, up to a set of zero measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
. In particular, the set

G=\{x\in\Gamma:{\mathcal{E}}(x)<\infty\}
is
1
-rectifiable.

Recall here that a set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is called
d
-rectifiable if there are Lipschitz maps

f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

(1.15)

{\mathcal{H}}^{d}\Big(E\setminus\textstyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\Big)=0.

The fact that
{\mathcal{E}}(x)<\infty
for
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-a.e. tangent point in a Jordan curve was proved by Bishop in

[
Bi1
]
(see also
[
BCGJ
]
). The most difficult implication of Theorem
1.5
, i.e, the fact that the set
G
is
1
-rectifiable and tangents to
\Gamma
exist for
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in G
,
was proved more recently by Ben Jaye and the last two authors of this paper
[
JTV
]
.

It is natural to wonder about the existence of a suitable version of Carleson’s conjecture in higher dimensions.
There are two natural questions: which coefficients should replace the coefficients
{\varepsilon}(x,r)
defined in
(
1.13
)? and second, for which open sets
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
should we expect to obtain
a characterization such as the one in Theorem
1.5
? In this theorem, the fact that

\Omega_{1}
is a Jordan domain ensures that
its boundary is connected, which plays an essential role in the proof. Indeed, the arguments in
[
JTV
]
are based on the connectivity of the boundary and they do not extend to higher dimensions (this should not be a surprise, since typically the role of connectivity in the plane is much more relevant than in higher dimensions for many geometric problems).

Regarding the coefficients
{\varepsilon}(x,r)
, in
[
AKN2
]
the authors show a very interesting connection
with the characteristic constants of spherical domains and the Friedland-Hayman inequality, which we proceed to describe. Given two disjoint open sets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, consider the open subsets
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
defined by

(1.16)

\Sigma_{i}=\bigl\{r^{-1}(y-x):y\in\Omega_{i}\cap\partial B(x,r)\bigr\},

and let
\alpha_{i}(x,r)=\alpha_{\Sigma_{i}}
, the characteristic constant of
\Sigma_{i}
. Analogously, set

\lambda_{i}(x,r)=\lambda_{\Sigma_{i}}
.
In
[
AKN2
]
it is remarked that, in the planar case
n=1
,

(1.17)

\alpha_{1}(x,r)+\alpha_{2}(x,r)-2\gtrsim{\varepsilon}(x,r)^{2}.

This is easy to check. Indeed, without loss of generality, assume
x=0
,
r=1
.
Taking into account that the characteristic of a domain decreases as its size increases, we have
\alpha_{I_{i}}=\alpha_{\Sigma_{i}}
and also
\lambda_{I_{i}}=\lambda_{\Sigma_{i}}
,
for
I_{i}:=I_{i}(x,r)
as in (
1.13
).
Let
\gamma_{i}={\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{i})/(2\pi)
. Since the first eigenfunction for
I_{i}
is the function
u_{i}(\theta)=\sin((2\gamma_{i})^{-1}\theta)
(modulo a translation in the torus), we have
\alpha_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{1/2}=(2\gamma_{i})^{-1}
.
Suppose, for example, that
{\varepsilon}(x,r)=\big|\pi-{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{1})\big|
and write
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}(x,r)
.
Let
\tilde{\alpha}_{2}
the characteristic of

{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\setminus\overline{I_{1}}
. Since
I_{2}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\setminus\overline{I_{1}}
, we have

\alpha_{2}\geq\tilde{\alpha}_{2}
. Thus,

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\geq\alpha_{1}+\tilde{\alpha}_{2}-2=\frac{1}{2\gamma_{1}}+\frac{1}{2(1-\gamma_{1})}-2=\frac{1-4\gamma_{1}(1-\gamma_{1})}{2\gamma_{1}(1-\gamma_{1})}=\frac{2(\frac{1}{2}-\gamma_{1})^{2}}{\gamma_{1}(1-\gamma_{1})}\approx\frac{{\varepsilon}(x,r)^{2}}{\gamma_{1}(1-\gamma_{1})},

which completes the proof of (
1.17
), since
\gamma_{1}\in(0,1)
.
Further, in case that
I_{1}(x,r)
and
I_{2}(x,r)
are complementary arcs, arguing as above, one can deduce

\min\big(1,\alpha_{1}(x,r)+\alpha_{2}(x,r)-2\big)\approx{\varepsilon}(x,r)^{2}.

See also
[
Bi2
]
for a very related discussion.

In view of the preceding discussion, a possible generalization of Carleson’s conjecture to higher dimensions
may consist in showing that, for a suitable domain
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
\Omega_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}
, it holds

(1.18)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\min(1,\alpha_{1}(x,r)+\alpha_{2}(x,r)-2)}{r}\,dr<\infty\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\text{$x$ is a tangent point of $\Omega_{1}$,}

for every
x\in\partial\Omega_{1}
, up to a set of zero measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
.

In this paper we prove the implication
\Rightarrow
in the above equivalence (
1.18
) for a very large class of domains. Further, we do not ask the domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
to be complementary.
The precise result is the
following.

Theorem D
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be disjoint Wiener regular domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.
Then, for
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
-a.e. tangent point
x
for the pair
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
,
it holds

(1.19)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\min(1,\alpha_{1}(x,r)+\alpha_{2}(x,r)-2)}{r}\,dr<\infty.

Recall that a domain is called Wiener regular if the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian with continuous data is solvable in that domain. This is a property which is implied by the CDC, which is a strictly stronger condition (see Section
2.3
).
We will prove Theorem
D
by integrating the Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman inequality
(
1.11
) applied to the Green functions
u_{i}
of
\Omega_{i}
and using the fact that the harmonic measure of
\Omega_{i}
satisfies
\omega_{\Omega_{i}}(B(x,r))\gtrsim r^{n}
as
r\to 0
at
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
-a.e. tangent point
x\in\partial\Omega_{i}
, which in turn implies that
J(x,0)>0
:

\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{2}{r}\bigl(\alpha_{1}(x,r)+\alpha_{2}(x,r)-2\bigr)\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{\partial_{r}J(x,r)}{J(x,r)}\,dr=\log\frac{J(x,r_{0})}{J(x,0)}<\infty.

Putting together Theorem
C
and Theorem
D
, for
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
as
above and for
0<s\leq n
, denoting
{\varepsilon}_{s}(x,r)={\varepsilon}_{s}(\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2})
, with
\Sigma_{i}
as
in (
1.16
), we deduce that

\int_{0}^{1}{\varepsilon}_{s}(x,r)^{2}\,\frac{dr}{r}<\infty

for
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
-a.e. tangent point
x
for the pair
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
.

Finally, we remark that in the companion paper
[
FTV
]
we will prove the converse implication in (
1.18
)
when
\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
satisfies the CDC.
The precise result is the following.

Theorem E
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be disjoint open sets. The set of points

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}(x,r)^{2}\,\frac{dr}{r}<\infty

is
n
-rectifiable. In case that the set
\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
satisfies the CDC, then

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\min(1,\alpha_{1}(x,r)+\alpha_{2}(x,r)-2)}{r}\,dr<\infty

is a tangent point for the pair
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
.

We insist on the fact that the CDC for
\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
is a quite mild lower regularity condition.
For example, it holds if
1
1

1

In fact, in
[
Le
]
it is shown that this is also a necessary condition for the CDC.
there exist
s\in(n-1,n+1]
and
c>0
such that

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{\infty}(B(x,r)\cap(\partial\Omega_{1}\cup\partial\Omega_{2}))\geq c\,r^{s}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\partial\Omega_{1}\cup\partial\Omega_{2}$, $0<r\leq 1$.}

Because of the discussion above, we think that this result can be considered as a natural extension
of Carleson’s conjecture to higher dimensions. Observe that a pair
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
consisting of
a Jordan domain in the plane and its complementary, as in
[
JTV
]
, satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
E
.
In fact, in the case of the plane, the above theorem applies to domains much more general than Jordan domains and so it is also new.

We remark that in
[
AKN2
]
the authors propose another possible extension of Carleson’s conjecture to
higher dimensions. Let

a(x,r)^{2}:=|\lambda_{1}(x,r)-n|^{2}+|\lambda_{2}(x,r)-n|^{2}.

Assuming that
\Omega_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}
,
they ask under what minimal assumptions on
\partial\Omega_{1}
the set of those points
x\in\partial\Omega_{1}
such that

(1.20)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{a(x,r)^{2}}{r}\,dr<\infty

coincides with the rectifiable part of
\partial\Omega_{1}
, up to a set of
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
measure zero.
As shown in
[
AKN1
]
, one has

(1.21)

\min(1,a(x,r))^{2}\lesssim\min(1,\alpha_{1}(x,r)+\alpha_{2}(x,r)-2).

Thus the condition (
1.19
) implies that

(1.22)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\min(1,a(x,r))^{2}}{r}\,dr<\infty,

which is equivalent to (
1.20
) for “reasonable” domains, for example when both
\Omega_{1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\Omega_{1}
are connected and have diameter larger than
1
.
By (
1.21
) and Theorem
D
, the condition (
1.22
) holds for tangent points
x\in\partial\Omega_{1}
in the case
when both
\Omega_{1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\Omega_{1}
are Wiener regular. However,
the inequality converse to the one in (
1.21
) does not hold in general, and so
it is not clear to
us if the condition (
1.22
) implies the existence of tangents for domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
such
as the ones in Theorem
E
.

1.4.
A counterexample

For
n\geq 2
, let
u,v:B(0,10)\to{\mathbb{R}}
be two nonnegative continuous functions satisfying

-\Delta u\leq 0
in
\{u>0\}
,
-\Delta v\leq 0
in
\{v>0\}
and
uv=0
in
B(0,10)
.

Define
\Gamma^{*}:=\{x\,:\,J(x,0^{+})>0\}
, where
J(0^{+}):=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}J(x,r)
and
J(x,r)
is the ACF monotonicity formula defined in (
1.10
) relative to
u,v
.
In
[
AKN2
]
, Allen, Kriventsov ans Neumayer prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
AKN2
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
u,v
as above. Then
\Gamma^{*}\cap B(0,1)
is
(n-1)
-rectifiable.

In the same work, see
[
AKN2
, Problem 2.8]
, the authors pose the converse question: suppose that
E=\partial\{u>0\}\cap\partial\{v>0\}
is
(n-1)
-rectifiable. Is it true, then, that
J(x,0^{+})>0
for
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in E
(under some minimal assumptions on
E
and
u,v
)? In the last section of this paper, we provide an example which shows that, without further structural assumptions on the domains, the natural converse to Theorem
1.6
is false. More precisely we construct two Wiener regular domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with
1
-rectifiable boundary and such that, if
E=\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\Omega_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(E)>0
and the limit
J(x,0^{+})
of the ACF functional associated to the Green functions of
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
equals to
0
for
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-almost every point
x\in E
. See Section
9
for the detailed construction. On the other hand, it is implicit in the proof of Theorem
D
that
J(x,0^{+})>0
whenever
x
is a (true) tangent point of
E
and
u
and
v
are the Green functions of disjoint Wiener regular domains (up to a set of
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
measure zero).

Preliminaries

2.1.
Miscellaneous notation

In the paper, constants denoted by
C
or
c
depend just on the dimension and perhaps other fixed
parameters, such as
a,\beta
in Theorem
A
, for example. We will write
a\lesssim b
if there is
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
. We write
a\approx b
if
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
.

Open balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
centered in
x
with radius
r>0
are denoted by
B(x,r)
, and closed balls by

\bar{B}(x,r)
. For an open or closed ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with radius
r
, we write
{\operatorname{rad}}(B)=r
.
Similarly, open and closed balls in
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}
are denoted by
B_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}(x,r)
and

\bar{B}_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}(x,r)
respectively. For an open or closed ball
B\subset{\mathbb{M}}^{n}
with radius
r
, we write
{\operatorname{rad}}_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}(B)=r
.
An open annulus in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
centered in
x
with inner radius
r_{1}
and outer radius
r_{2}
is denoted by

A(x,r_{1},r_{2})
, and the corresponding closed annulus by
\bar{A}(x,r_{1},r_{2})
. Similarly, the analogous annuli in
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}

are denoted by
A_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}(x,r_{1},r_{2})
and
\bar{A}_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}(x,r_{1},r_{2})
, respectively.

We use the two notations
S(x,r)\equiv\partial B(x,r)
for spheres
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
centered in
x
with radius
r
, so that
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}=S(0,1)
.

We call open sets in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
spherical domains, and the geodesic distance in
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}
is written as

{\rm dist}_{{\mathbb{M}}^{n}}
. Notice that, assuming
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
to be embedded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, the geodesic distance

{\rm dist}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}
is comparable to the Euclidean distance in the ambient space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.

Given a set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, the notation
M_{+}(F)
stands for the
set of (positive) Radon measures supported on
F
.
For
s>0
, the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, and the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff
content by
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{\infty}
. Recall that, for any set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{\infty}(E)=\inf\Big\{\sum_{i}{\rm diam}(A_{i})^{s}:E\subset\bigcup_{i}A_{i}\Big\}.

We say that
E
is lower
s
-content regular if there exists some constant
c>0
such that

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{\infty}(B(x,r)\cap E)\geq c\,r^{s}\quad\text{ for all $x\in E$ and $0<r\leq{\rm diam}(E)$.}

The barycenter of a set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined by

x_{E}=\int_{E}x\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(x).

On the other hand, the
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
-barycenter of
E\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
is defined by
x_{E}/|x_{E}|
, with
x_{E}
as above. So this belongs to
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
and it is defined only when
x_{E}\neq 0
.

2.2.
Capacities

The fundamental solution of the minus Laplacian in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, for
n\geq 3
, equals

{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(x)=\frac{c_{n}}{|x|^{n-2}},

where
c_{n}=(n-2){\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. In the plane,
the fundamental solution is

{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\log\frac{1}{|x|}.

For a Radon measure
\mu
, we consider the potential defined by

U_{n-2}\mu(x)={\mathcal{E}}_{n}*\mu(x).

Given a set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(or, more generally,
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
)
we define the capacity
\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)
by the identity

(2.1)

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)=\frac{1}{\inf_{\mu\in M_{1}(F)}I_{n-2}(\mu)},

where the infimum is taken over all
probability
measures
\mu
supported on
F
and
I_{n-2}(\mu)
is the energy

(2.2)

I_{n-2}(\mu)=\iint{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(x-y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)=\int U_{n-2}\mu(x)\,d\mu(x).

For
n\geq 3
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)
is the Newtonian capacity of
F
, and for
n=2
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,

\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(F)
is the Wiener capacity of
F
. Remark that for
n\geq 3
, one also has

(2.3)

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)=\sup\big\{\mu(F):\mu\in M_{+}(F),\,\|U_{n-2}\mu\|_{\infty,F}\leq 1\big\},

where
M_{+}(F)
is the family of all Radon measures supported in
F
.
In the plane, the analogous identity with
n=2
holds when
{\rm diam}F<1
.

It is easy to check that the Newtonian capacity
\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}
is
homogeneous of degree
n-2
when
n\geq 3
. The Wiener capacity is not homogeneous. However, the logarithmic capacity
\operatorname{Cap}_{L}
, defined by

\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(F)=e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(F)}},

is
1
-homogeneous.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open set. We say that
\Omega
satisfies the
n
-dimensional capacity density condition (CDC)
if there exists some constant
c>0
such that, for every
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,{\rm diam}(\Omega))
,

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(B(x,r)\setminus\Omega)\geq c\,r^{n-2}\quad\mbox{in the case $n\geq 3$,}

and

\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(B(x,r)\setminus\Omega)\geq c\,r\quad\mbox{in the case $n=2$.}

For subsets
F\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
and domains
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, we define
\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)
in the same way as
for
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and domains
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(using the fundamental solution of the Laplacian in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
).
We define the
n
-dimensional CDC in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
by asking the following condition for every
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,{\rm diam}(\Omega))
:

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(B_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}(x,r)\setminus\Omega)\geq c\,r^{n-2}\quad\mbox{in the case $n\geq 3$,}

and

\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(B_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}(x,r)\setminus\Omega)\geq c\,r\quad\mbox{in the case $n=2$.}

We will need the following auxiliary results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
or
E\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be compact and
n-2<s\leq n
. In the case
n>2
, we have

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(E)\gtrsim_{s,n}{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}^{s}(E)^{\frac{n-2}{s}}.

In the case
n=2
, we have

\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(E)\gtrsim_{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}^{s}(E)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

The proof of this result is an immediate consequence of Frostman’s Lemma. See
[
Ma
, Chapter 8]
for the case
n>2
, and

[
CTV
, Lemma 4]
for the case
n=2
, for example.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\ell>0
, consider a compact set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and denote
{\widetilde{F}}=F\times[0,\ell]\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.
In the case
n\geq 3
, we have

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-1}({\widetilde{F}})\gtrsim\ell\,\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F).

In the case
n=2
, given
C_{1}\geq 1
, if
{\rm diam}(F)\leq C_{1}\,\ell
, we have

\operatorname{Cap}_{1}({\widetilde{F}})\gtrsim\frac{\ell}{\log\dfrac{2C_{1}\ell}{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(F)}},

with the implicit constant depending on
C_{1}
.

Proof.

By the
n-1
homogeneity of
\operatorname{Cap}_{n+1}(F)
and of the inequalities stated in the lemma we can assume
{\rm diam}(F)<1
, so that
(
2.3
) also holds in the case
n=2
.
Let
\mu\in M_{+}(F)
be such that
U_{n-2}\mu(x)\leq 1
for all
x\in F
and
\mu(F)=\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)
(it is well known that such measure, called equilibrium measure, exists for any compact set
F
). Denote
I_{\ell}=[0,\ell]
.
Consider the product measure
{\widetilde{\mu}}=\mu\times{\mathcal{H}}^{1}|_{I_{\ell}}
, which is supported on
{\widetilde{F}}
.
For
x^{\prime}=(x,x_{n+1})\in{\widetilde{F}}
, we have

(2.4)

U_{n-1}{\widetilde{\mu}}(x^{\prime})=\!\int\!\frac{c_{n}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{n-1}}\,d{\widetilde{\mu}}(y^{\prime})\approx\!\int_{y_{n+1}\in I_{\ell}}\int_{y\in F}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}+|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|^{n-1}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(y_{n+1})d\mu(y).

For each
x,y\in F
, we have

\int_{y_{n+1}\in I_{\ell}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}+|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|^{n-1}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(y_{n+1})\\
=\int_{|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|\leq|x-y|}\!\!\!\cdots\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}|_{I_{\ell}}(y_{n+1})+\int_{|x-y|<|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|\leq\ell}\!\!\!\cdots\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}|_{I_{\ell}}(y_{n+1})=:T_{1}(x,y)+T_{2}(x,y).

Concerning
T_{1}(x,y)
, we have

T_{1}(x,y)\leq\int_{|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|\leq|x-y|}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}|_{I_{\ell}}(y_{n+1})\leq\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}.

Also,

T_{2}(x,y)\leq\int_{|x-y|<|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|\leq\ell}\frac{1}{|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|^{n-1}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}|_{I_{\ell}}(y_{n+1}).

In the case
n\geq 3
, it easily follows also that

T_{2}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}.

Thus,

U_{n-1}{\widetilde{\mu}}(x^{\prime})\lesssim\int_{y\in F}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}\,d\mu(y)\approx U_{n-2}\mu(x)\leq 1.

Consequently,

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-1}({\widetilde{F}})\geq\frac{{\widetilde{\mu}}(F)}{\|U_{n-1}{\widetilde{\mu}}\|_{\infty,{\widetilde{F}}}}\gtrsim{\widetilde{\mu}}({\widetilde{F}})=\ell\,\mu(F)\approx\ell\,\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)\quad\mbox{ for $n\geq 3$}.

In the case
n=2
, if
T_{2}(x,y)\neq 0
, we have

T_{2}(x,y)\leq\log\frac{\ell}{|x-y|}.

Since
|x-y|\leq C_{1}\ell
for any
x,y\in F
, we can always write
T_{2}(x,y)\leq\log\frac{C_{1}\ell}{|x-y|},

and so

T_{1}(x,y)+T_{2}(x,y)\leq 1+\log\frac{C_{1}\ell}{|x-y|}\lesssim\log\frac{2C_{1}\ell}{|x-y|}.

Thus,

\displaystyle U_{1}{\widetilde{\mu}}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{y\in F}\log\frac{2C_{1}\ell}{|x-y|}\,d\mu(y)=\log(2C_{1}\ell)\,\mu(F)+\int_{y\in F}\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\log(2C_{1}\ell)\,\mu(F)+2\pi\,U_{0}\mu(x)\leq\log(2C_{1}\ell)\,\mu(F)+2\pi.

Therefore,

\operatorname{Cap}_{1}(F)\geq\frac{{\widetilde{\mu}}(F)}{\|U_{1}{\widetilde{\mu}}\|_{\infty,{\widetilde{F}}}}\gtrsim\frac{\ell\,\mu(F)}{\log(2C_{1}\ell)\,\mu(F)+2\pi}=\frac{\ell}{\log(2C_{1}\ell)+\frac{2\pi}{\mu(F)}}.

Writing

\frac{2\pi}{\mu(F)}=\frac{2\pi}{\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(F)}=\log\frac{1}{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(F)},

we obtain

\operatorname{Cap}_{1}(F)\gtrsim\frac{\ell}{\log(2C_{1}\ell)+\log\frac{1}{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(F)}}=\frac{\ell}{\log\frac{2C_{1}\ell}{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(F)}}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\ell>0
,
F\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
compact, and let
I_{\ell}
be an interval of length
\ell
contained in
[1/4,2]
. Denote

{\widetilde{F}}=\{x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:x^{\prime}=tx\mbox{ for some $x\in F$ and $t\in I_{\ell}$}\}.

Then we have

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-1}({\widetilde{F}})\gtrsim\ell\,\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)\quad\mbox{in the case $n\geq 3$,}

and, in the case
n=2
, if
{\rm diam}(F)\leq C_{1}\,\ell
,

\operatorname{Cap}_{1}({\widetilde{F}})\gtrsim\frac{\ell}{\log\frac{2C_{1}\ell}{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(F)}}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the one of Lemma
2.2
.
We assume
{\rm diam}(F)<1
, so that
(
2.3
) holds in the case
n=2
. We take

\mu\in M_{+}(F)
be such that
U_{n-2}\mu(x)\leq 1
for all
x\in F
and
\mu(F)=\operatorname{Cap}_{n-2}(F)
.
Then we consider the “product measure”
{\widetilde{\mu}}
defined by

\int f\,d{\widetilde{\mu}}=\int_{t\in I_{\ell}}\int_{x\in F}f(tx)\,d\mu(x)\,dt,\quad\text{ for $f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})$}.

Notice that
{\widetilde{\mu}}
is supported on
{\widetilde{F}}
.

For
x^{\prime}=sx\in{\widetilde{F}}
, with
x\in F
,
s\in I_{\ell}
, we have

U_{n-1}{\widetilde{\mu}}(x^{\prime})=\int\frac{c_{n}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{n-1}}\,d{\widetilde{\mu}}(y^{\prime})=\int_{t\in I_{\ell}}\int_{y\in F}\frac{c_{n}}{|sx-ty|^{n-1}}\,d\mu(y)\,dt

Now we claim that

|sx-ty|\gtrsim|x-y|+|s-t|.

Indeed, we have

|s-t|=||sx|-|ty||\leq|sx-ty|.

Also,

|x-y|=\frac{1}{s}\,|sx-sy|\leq\frac{1}{s}\,\big(|sx-ty|+|ty-sy|\big)\approx|sx-ty|+|t-s|\lesssim|sx-ty|.

Adding the last two inequalities, the claim follows.
Then we deduce

U_{n-1}{\widetilde{\mu}}(x^{\prime})\lesssim\int_{t\in I_{\ell}}\int_{y\in F}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}+|s-t|^{n-1}}\,d\mu(y)dt

Notice now the similarity between this inequality and (
2.4
). Then by the same arguments following (
2.4
)
in the proof of Lemma
2.2
we obtain the desired estimates.
∎

2.3.
Harmonic measure

A bounded open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is Wiener regular if for any continuous
f:{\partial\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}
there exists the solution
of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplace equation. The Wiener criterion characterizes the Wiener regularity of any bounded open. For our purposes, it suffices to know that, in the case
n\geq 2
, if for any
x\in\partial\Omega
there exists some
r_{x}>0
and
c=c(x)>0
such that

(2.6)

\operatorname{Cap}_{n-1}(B(x,r)\setminus\Omega)\geq c\,r^{n-1}\quad\mbox{ for $0<r\leq r_{x}$,}

then
\Omega
is Wiener regular. In the case
n=1
, instead a sufficient condition for Wiener regularity is that
\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(B(x,r)\setminus\Omega)\geq c\,r
for
0<r\leq r_{x}
, with
c=c(x)>0
.

Recall that if
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a bounded Wiener regular open set and
f:\overline{\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}

is continuous and of class
C^{2}
in
\Omega
, then

f(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f\,d\omega_{\Omega}^{x}-\int_{\Omega}\Delta f(y)\,g_{\Omega}(x,y)\,dy,

where
g_{\Omega}
stands for the Green function for
\Omega
.

The following result is well known
2
2

2

In some references, it is called “Bourgain’s lemma”.
. See Section 2.1 from
[
To
]
, for example.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
n\geq 1
there exists
c(n)>0
such that, given an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and a closed ball
B
intersecting
{\partial\Omega}
, it holds

\omega^{x}(B)\geq c(n)\,\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{n-1}(\tfrac{1}{4}B\setminus\Omega)}{{\operatorname{rad}}(B)^{n-1}}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\tfrac{1}{4}B\cap\Omega$
when $n\geq 2$},

and

\omega^{x}(B)\geq c(n)\,\frac{1}{\log\dfrac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B)}{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(\frac{1}{4}B\setminus\Omega)}}\qquad\mbox{ for all $x\in\tfrac{1}{4}B\cap\Omega$ when $n=1$.}

The following two lemmas are valid for the so called NTA domains. However, we only state them for
Lipschitz domains, which suffices for the purposes of this paper. For the proof, see
[
JK
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a Lipschitz domain.
Let

\omega^{p}(B)\approx r(B)^{n-1}\,g_{\Omega}(p,y)\quad\mbox{
for all $p\in\Omega\backslash 2B$ and $y\in B\cap\Omega$,}

with the implicit constant just depending on

The following theorem states the so called “change of pole formula”. Again, this holds for NTA domains (see
[

Lemma 2.8

(Change of pole formula)
.

For

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in K:\,{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\leq{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k}),\\

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in K:\,B_{j}\subset 2^{i+2}B_{k}\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(L\cap B_{j})}{(2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k}))^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(L\cap 2^{i+2}B_{k})}{(2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k}))^{n+1}}\approx\frac{1}{2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})}.

Therefore,

S_{0,1}\lesssim\sum_{k\in K}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})^{2}\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{k})\,\,\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{1}{2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})}\approx\sum_{k\in K}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{k}).

Estimate of
S_{0,2}

Now we assume that
{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})>{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})
.
Let
A\geq 1
be the minimal constant such that
B_{k}\subset AB_{j}
. Notice that
{\operatorname{rad}}(AB_{j})\approx D(B_{j},B_{k})
.
By Lemma
4.5
, using also the doubling property of
\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}
(since
{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}
is a Lipschitz domain), we have

\displaystyle\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{j})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\bigg(\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})}{{\operatorname{rad}}(AB_{j})}\bigg)^{n+a}\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(AB_{j})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\bigg(\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})}{{\operatorname{rad}}(AB_{j})}\bigg)^{n+\alpha}\bigg(\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(AB_{j})}{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})}\bigg)^{n-\alpha}\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{k})\approx\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})^{n+\alpha}}{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})^{n-\alpha}\,D(B_{j},B_{k})^{2\alpha}}\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{k}).

Plugging this estimate into the definition of
S_{0,2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{0,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in K:\\
{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})>{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})^{n+1}\,{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})}{D(B_{j},B_{k})^{n+1}}\,\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})^{n-\alpha}\,D(B_{j},B_{k})^{2\alpha}}{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})^{n+\alpha}}\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{j})

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in K:\\
{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})>{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})^{1-\alpha}\,{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})^{n+1-\alpha}}{D(B_{j},B_{k})^{n+1-2\alpha}}\,\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{j}).

Using that
{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})<{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})
, we get

S_{0,2}\lesssim\sum_{j\in K}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})^{2-2\alpha}\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{j})\,\,\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in K:\,{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})<{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j}),\\
2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\leq D(B_{j},B_{k})<2^{i+1}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})^{n}\,}{D(B_{j},B_{k})^{n+1-2\alpha}}.

Arguing as in (
4.11
), we deduce that for each
i\geq 0

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in K:\,{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})<{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j}),\\
2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\leq D(B_{j},B_{k})<2^{i+1}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})^{n}\,}{D(B_{j},B_{k})^{n+1-2\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\big(2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\big)^{1-2\alpha}}.

Thus, since

,

S_{0,2}\lesssim\sum_{j\in K}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})^{2-2\alpha}\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{j})\,\,\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{1}{\big(2^{i}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\big)^{1-2\alpha}}\approx\sum_{j\in K}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{j})\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{j}).

Gathering the estimates obtained for

and
S_{0,2}
, we obtain

S_{0}\lesssim\sum_{k\in K}{\operatorname{rad}}(B_{k})\,\omega_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{\Gamma}}^{x}(B_{k}),

as wished. Together with (
4.10
), this concludes the proof of the lemma, modulo Claim

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

We have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda(\Omega_{k})=\lambda(\Omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda(B_{k})=\lambda(B).

Proof.

Let
U
be the connected component of
\Omega
such that
\lambda(\Omega)=\lambda(U)
, and let
U_{k}=U\setminus S_{k}=U\cap\Omega_{k}.

Let
u_{k}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(U_{k})
be the Dirichlet eigenfunction associated with
\lambda(U_{k})
, normalized so that
u_{k}\geq 0
and
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(U_{k})}=1
.
We assume that
u_{k}
is extended by
0
to the whole
U
. For any subsequence of
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
, there exists another subsubsequence

\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j}
which converges weakly in
W_{0}^{1,2}(U)
and strongly in
L^{2}(U)
(by the compact embedding
W^{1,2}(U)\subset L^{2}(U)
) to some function
u\geq 0
such that
\|u\|_{L^{2}(U)}=1
. Since
\{\lambda(U_{k_{j}})\}_{j}
is a non-increasing sequence and

\lambda(U_{k_{j}})\geq\lambda(U)
for any

, it also holds
that
\lambda(U_{k_{j}})
converges to some
\alpha\in[\lambda(U),\infty)

Passing to the limit the equation

-\Delta u_{k_{j}}=\lambda_{k_{j}}\,u_{k_{j}},

it follows that, in the weak sense,

-\Delta u=\alpha\,u\quad\mbox{ in $U$}.

Since
u
is a Dirichlet eigenfunction for
U
, which is connected, and moreover
u\geq 0
, it follows that
u
is a Dirichlet eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue for
U
and

\alpha=\lambda(U)=\lambda(\Omega)
.
Consequently,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda(U_{k})=\lambda(\Omega).

Since
U_{k}\subset\Omega_{k}
, this implies that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\lambda(\Omega_{k})\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\lambda(U_{k})=\lambda(\Omega).

On the other hand, since
\Omega_{k}\subset\Omega
, we have
\lambda(\Omega_{k})\geq\lambda(\Omega)
for any

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda(\Omega_{k})=\lambda(\Omega).

The fact that

, we can assume that

and that they are concentric. Then the same arguments used for
\Omega_{k}
work in this case.
∎

as wished, the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B
for
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.

From the preceding lemmas and the validity of Theorem
B
for the sets

, we deduce

6.2.
Domains in hyperbolic space

As for the Euclidean space, the proof of Theorems
A
and
B
when
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{H}}^{n}
is very similar to the case
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}={\mathbb{S}}^{n}
. For this reason, we only sketch the arguments and highlight the main modifications required.

Before getting started, some preliminary remarks: we consider the half-space model of the hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, which is the upper half space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
with the metric
\frac{dx_{1}^{2}+\cdots dx_{n}^{2}}{x_{n}^{2}}
. Unlike the Euclidean case, and similarly to the spherical one, Theorems
A
and
B
are not scale invariant in this geometry. Hence the constants in the estimates naturally depend on the volume of
\Omega
.
On this, see also
[
AKN1
, Remark 1.5]
.

Let
\Omega,B\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be as in Theorems
A
and
B
(so in particular,
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega)={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B)<\beta
),
with
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
computed in the hyperbolic metric. We assume that
B=B_{x}
attains the infimum in
(
1.3
), (
1.4
), or (
1.9
) modulo a factor of
1/2
.
Since the Laplace-Beltrami operator is invariant by isometries, by applying a suitable isometry to
\Omega
we can assume that
B
is centered at the point
(0,\ldots,0,1)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
. From the fact that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B)=\mathrm{vol}(B)<\beta
, it follows
that
4B
is far away from
\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
in the Euclidean metric. So inside
4B
the Euclidean metric and the hyperbolic metric are comparable,
and the Hausdorff measures
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
are comparable when computed with both metrics (with the comparability constant depending on
\beta
in both cases).

Let
\lambda_{\Omega},\lambda_{B}
be the respective first Dirichlet eigenvalues of
\Omega
and
B
, let
u_{\Omega},u_{B}
be the corresponding eigenfunctions, and put
\alpha_{\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}^{1/2}
and
\alpha_{B}=\lambda_{B}^{1/2}
. For
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, we set

{\widetilde{u}}_{\Omega}(x,t):=u_{\Omega}(x)e^{t\alpha_{B}}\,\mbox{ and }\,{\widetilde{u}}_{B}(x,t):=u_{B}(x)e^{t\alpha_{B}}.

These are the extensions of
u_{\Omega}
and
u_{B}
, respectively, analogous to the ones defined at the beginning of Section
3
for the spherical case.

We also consider the cylindrical domains in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
given by

{\widetilde{B}}=B\times(0,1),\,\,\,\,{\widetilde{B}}^{\prime}=B\times(-1,2),\,\,\,\,{\widetilde{\Omega}}=\Omega\times(0,1)\,\mbox{ and }\,{\widetilde{\Omega}}^{\prime}=\Omega\times(-1,2).

It is easy to check that
\Delta_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}}{\widetilde{u}}_{B}=0
, where
\Delta_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}

where

is the diagonal matrix with smooth coefficients defined by

(6.1)

a_{ij}=x_{n}^{2-n}\delta_{i,j}\,\mbox{ if }\,1\leq i,j\leq n,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,a_{ij}=x_{n}^{-n}\delta_{ij}\,\mbox{ otherwise.}

Moreover, note that

(6.2)

\displaystyle L{\widetilde{u}}_{\Omega}(x,t)

\displaystyle=x_{n}^{-n}\left(\Delta_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}{\widetilde{u}}_{\Omega}(x,t)+(\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\Omega}(x)e^{t\alpha_{B}})\right)=x_{n}^{-n}(-\lambda_{\Omega}+\lambda_{B})\,{\widetilde{u}}_{\Omega}(x,t),

which is the analog of (
3.1
) for the current setting. Remark that the multiplicative term

is bounded in

is uniformly elliptic on

The proof continues very similarly to the spherical case. We just highlight some of the differences.

7.
Proof of Theorem
C

Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{1})\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{2})
. For simplicity, we assume that

a=1/2
in the definition of
{\varepsilon}_{s}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
, but minor modifications yield the result for any
a\in(0,1)
.
Let
\bar{\alpha}_{i}
be the characteristic of the spherical cap
B_{i}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
with the same
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
measure as
\Omega_{i}
.
The Friedland-Hayman
[
FH
]
inequality ensures that

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\geq 0
.
Then we write

(7.1)

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2=(\alpha_{1}-\bar{\alpha}_{1})+(\alpha_{2}-\bar{\alpha}_{2})+(\bar{\alpha}_{1}+\bar{\alpha}_{2}-2)\geq 0.

By Sperner’s inequality
[
Sp
]
, among all the open subsets with a fixed measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, the one that minimizes the characteristic constant is a spherical cap with that measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
. Hence,

\alpha_{i}\geq\bar{\alpha}_{i}.

So the three summands on the right hand side of (
7.1
) are non-negative.
Further, if one of the caps
B_{i}
differs from a hemisphere by a surface measure
h_{0}
,
that is,

h_{0}=\max_{i}\Big|{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{i})-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})\Big|,

then

(7.2)

\bar{\alpha}_{1}+\bar{\alpha}_{2}-2\geq c\,h_{0}^{2}.

See Corollary 12.4 from
[
CS
]
, for example.
So to prove Theorem
C
we can assume that, for
i=1,2
,

(7.3)

\Big|{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{i})-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})\Big|\leq\frac{1}{100}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n}),

because otherwise

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\gtrsim 1

and the statement in the theorem is trivial. Observe that (
7.3
) implies that

(7.4)

\beta\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{i})\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})-\beta

for a suitable absolute constant
\beta
.
From this estimate it follows that
\bar{\alpha}_{i}\approx 1
for
i=1,2
. So we can assume that
|\alpha_{i}-\bar{\alpha}_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{\alpha}_{i}
because otherwise the theorem follows trivially from (
7.1
). So
\alpha_{i}\approx\bar{\alpha}_{i}\approx 1
.
Then from the identity
\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+n-1)
(where
\lambda_{i}\equiv\lambda_{\Omega_{i}}
) it follows immediately that

(7.5)

\alpha_{i}-\bar{\alpha}_{i}\approx\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{B_{i}}.

For
i=1,2
, let
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be the barycenter of
\Omega_{i}
.
We assume that the barycenter exists because otherwise this means that
\int_{\Omega_{i}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)=0
, while

\left|\int_{B_{i}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)\right|\gtrsim 1
for the spherical cap
B_{i}
because of (
7.4
) (independently of the choice of its center in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
).
Hence, by the Allen-Kriventsov-Neumayer theorem, using (
7.4
) and (
7.5
), we would obtain

\alpha_{i}-\bar{\alpha}_{i}\approx\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{B_{i}}\gtrsim{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i}\triangle B_{i})^{2}\geq\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{i}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)-\int_{B_{i}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)\bigg|^{2}\gtrsim 1,

which would yield the conclusion of the theorem. The same argument shows that, in fact, we have

\left|\int_{\Omega_{i}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)\right|\approx 1.

From now on we assume that the spherical caps
B_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are centered in the barycenters
x_{i}
of the
\Omega_{i}
’s.
In the case when
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i})+{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{2})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})
it follows easily that
the barycenters of
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are opposite points in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, and
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are complementary balls in

{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.
When the preceding condition does not hold, we need to be a little more careful.

Let

\theta_{0}={\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{1})-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{2}),

and suppose that
\theta_{0}>0
.
For
i=1,2
,

y_{i}=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i})}\int_{\Omega_{i}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y),

so that
x_{i}=y_{i}/|y_{i}|
. Also, let

y_{2}^{*}=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y).

Notice that
y_{2}^{*}=y_{2}
if
\theta_{0}=0
and that

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{1})\,y_{1}+{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})\,y_{2}^{*}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)=0.

So the barycenter
x_{1}
and the point
x_{2}^{*}=y_{2}^{*}/|y_{2}^{*}|
are antipodal points in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.
We also have

\displaystyle|y_{2}-y_{2}^{*}|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{1}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{2})}-\frac{1}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})}\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1}}|y|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})}\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{2}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1}}y\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(y)\bigg|\lesssim\theta_{0}+\theta_{0}\approx\theta_{0}.

Also, let
B_{2}^{*}
be a spherical cap centered in
x_{2}^{*}
with measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{2}^{*})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})
.
Since

|{\operatorname{rad}}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}(B_{2})-{\operatorname{rad}}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}(B_{2}^{*})|\approx|{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{2})-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{2}^{*})|\lesssim\theta_{0},

using also (

), we infer that

(7.6)

On the other hand, from the definition of
\theta_{0}
, it follows that

h_{0}=\max_{i}\big|{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Omega_{i})-\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})\big|\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\theta_{0}.

Then, by (

),

(7.7)

\bar{\alpha}_{1}+\bar{\alpha}_{2}-2\gtrsim\theta_{0}^{2}\gtrsim{\rm dist}_{H}(\partial_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}B_{2},\partial_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}B_{1})^{2}.

To estimate
{\varepsilon}_{s}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
we denote by

be the

-plane that contains

be the

-plane through the origin parallel to

Also, for

\mu(B(y,t))\leq\frac{\mu(I_{j}^{i})}{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{j}^{i})}\,t\lesssim\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{j}^{i})^{1/2}}{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{j}^{i})}\,t\leq t^{1/2}.

From the fact that
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(F_{i})\leq 1/4
, it easily follows that
{\rm dist}(I_{j}^{i},I_{j+1}^{i})\geq 1/(2N_{i})
(we assume that
I_{j}^{i}
and
I_{j+1}^{i}
are consecutive intervals in
F_{i}
). So for
y\in I_{j}^{i}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{j}^{i})\leq t\leq 1/(2N_{i})
, it follows that
B(y,t)\cap F_{i}=B(y,t)\cap I_{j}^{i}
(i.e.,
B(y,t)
does not intersect any interval
I_{k}^{i}
different from
I_{j}^{i}
). Then we have

\mu(B(y,t))\leq\mu(I_{j}^{i})\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(I_{j}^{i})^{1/2}\leq t^{1/2}.

Finally, for
1/(2N_{i})<t\leq 2r
,

\mu(B(y,t))\lesssim\frac{t}{r}\,\|\mu\|=\frac{t\,r^{1/2}}{r}\lesssim t^{1/2}.

So
\mu
satisfies the desired growth condition and then (
9.3
) holds.
∎

Next we will show that
J(x,0+)=0
for
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in E
.
Recall that, for such
x
and for
0<r\leq 1/4
,

J(x,r)=J_{1}(x,r)^{1/2}J_{2}(x,r)^{1/2},

where

J_{i}(x,r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{B(x,r)}|\nabla g_{i}(y,p_{i})|^{2}\,dy.

We assume that the Green functions
g_{i}(\cdot,p_{i})
are extended by
0
away from
\Omega_{i}
. By Caccioppoli’s inequality, since

g_{i}(\cdot,p_{i})
is subharmonic and non-negative in
B(x,8r)
,

(9.4)

J_{i}(x,r)\approx\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|\nabla g_{i}(y,p_{i})|^{2}\,dy\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B(x,2r)}|g_{i}(y,p_{i})|^{2}\,dy\leq\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sup_{y\in B(x,2r)}g_{i}(y,p_{i})^{2}.

To bound
g_{i}(y,p_{i})
in terms of the harmonic measure
\omega_{i}^{p_{i}}
, we appeal to the following lemma:

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a bounded, Wiener regular open set.
Let
\bar{B}
be a closed ball centered at
\partial\Omega
. Let
\omega
be the harmonic measure for
\Omega
and
g
its Green function.
Then,

(9.5)

g(z,y)\lesssim\omega^{z}(8\bar{B})\,\bigg(\log\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(\bar{B})}{\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(\frac{1}{4}\bar{B}\setminus\Omega)}\bigg)^{2}\qquad\mbox{
for all $z\in\Omega\setminus 2\bar{B}$ and $y\in\frac{1}{5}\bar{B}\cap\Omega$.}

For the proof of the lemma, see
[
PT2
, Chapter 7]
.
In the case when
\Omega
is a domain satisfying the CDC condition, the right hand side of (
9.5
) is comparable to
1
. In this situation, the result above appears in
[
AH
]
.
However, in the generality stated above, it is difficult to find the lemma in the literature.

The domains
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
above do not satisfy the CDC condition (roughly speaking, because the holes between the segments
I_{j}^{i}
are two big). However, any closed ball
\bar{B}
with radius at most
1
centered in
E
satisfies

\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(\tfrac{1}{4}\bar{B}\setminus\Omega_{i})\geq\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(\tfrac{1}{4}\bar{B}\cap E)\approx{\operatorname{rad}}(\bar{B})\approx\operatorname{Cap}_{L}(\bar{B}).

Thus, applying Lemma
9.5
to
\Omega_{i}
and the ball
\bar{B}=B(x,10r)
, we get

g_{i}(p_{i},y)\lesssim\omega_{i}^{p_{i}}(\bar{B}(x,80r))\quad\mbox{ for all $y\in B(x,2r)$,}

assuming
r
small enough so that
p_{i}\not\in B(x,20r)
.
Plugging this estimate into (
9.4
), we obtain

(9.6)

J_{i}(x,r)\lesssim\left(\frac{\omega_{i}^{p_{i}}(\bar{B}(x,80r))}{r}\right)^{2}

for all
x\in E
and
r
small enough.
Consequently, by Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\int_{E}J(x,0+)^{1/4}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{r\to 0}\int_{E}J(x,r)^{1/4}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{r\to 0}\bigg[\left(\int_{E}J_{1}(x,r)^{1/2}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{E}J_{2}(x,r)^{1/2}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)\right)^{1/2}\bigg].

Observe now that, for
i=1,2
, by (
9.6
) and Fubini,

\displaystyle\int_{E}J_{i}(x,r)^{1/2}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E}\frac{\omega_{i}^{p_{i}}(\bar{B}(x,80r))}{r}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{y\in{\partial\Omega}:{\rm dist}(y,E)\leq 80r}\frac{1}{r}\int_{x\in E\cap\bar{B}(y,80r)}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)\,d\omega_{i}^{p_{i}}(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\omega_{i}^{p_{i}}(\bar{U}_{80r}(E)),

where
\bar{U}_{t}(F)
stands for the closed
t
-neighborhood of
F
.
So we deduce

\int_{E}J(x,0+)^{1/4}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(x)\lesssim\lim_{r\to 0}\omega_{1}^{p_{1}}(\bar{U}_{80r}(E))\cdot\lim_{r\to 0}\omega_{2}^{p_{2}}(\bar{U}_{80r}(E))=\omega_{1}^{p_{1}}(E)\,\omega_{2}^{p_{2}}(E)=0,

using the outer regularity of the measures
\omega_{i}^{p_{i}}
. Thus,
J(x,0+)=0
for
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in E
, as wished.

Remark 9.2
.

We already mentioned that the domains
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
defined above do not satisfy the CDC. However, they can be modified suitably so that
the CDC holds. For example, we could define
{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{i}=\Omega_{i}\setminus K
, where
K
is the union of a countable family of homothetic copies
of the
1/3
Cantor set located between all consecutive intervals
I_{j}^{i}
,
I_{j+1}^{i}
. That is, in the “hole” in
L_{i}
between the intervals
I_{j}^{i}
,
I_{j+1}^{i}
, we put a homothetic copy of the
1/3
Cantor set with diameter comparable to the size of the hole.
We could also replace the
1/3
Cantor set by
a suitable approximation by intervals, in case we wanted the boundary of
{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{i}
to be made up of a countable family of intervals.
We leave the details for the reader.

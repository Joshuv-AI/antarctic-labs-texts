Optimal Control for Kinematic Bicycle Model with Continuous-time Safety Guarantees: A Sequential Second-order Cone Programming Approach
Victor Freire, Xiangru Xu

Abstract
The optimal control problem for the kinematic bicycle model is considered where the trajectories are required to satisfy the safety constraints in the continuous-time sense. Based on the differential flatness property of the model, necessary and sufficient conditions in the flat space are provided to guarantee safety in the state space. The optimal control problem is relaxed to the problem of solving three second-order cone programs (SOCPs) sequentially, which find the safe path, the trajectory duration, and the speed profile, respectively. Solutions of the three SOCPs together provide a sub-optimal solution to the original optimal control problem. Simulation examples and comparisons with state-of-the-art optimal control solvers are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.



I
Introduction

The vehicle motion planning problem is well-studied. However, fast algorithms tend to lack formal safety guarantees while robust and safe planning methods are usually slow, making them unfit for real-time implementation. The search for a fast and safe planning algorithm is key to achieving provably safe driving autonomy
[
1
,
2
]
.

The literature addressing the motion planning problem for vehicles is vast.
A common approach is the spatio-temporal division of the problem.
On the one hand, most pathfinding (i.e., spatial) algorithms parse the configuration space in search of minimum length or minimum curvature sequences or functions
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. For example, in
[
4
]
, the authors used RRT and B-spline curves to explore the space and generate kinodynamically feasible paths;
however, their approach requires each RRT sampling step to verify kinodynamic feasibility, resulting in increased computation times.
In
[
5
]
, a multi-layer planning framework was proposed where the pathfinding layer uses sampling techniques to modify a global path for obstacle avoidance; however, they rely on nonconvex minimization of the path’s curvature to enforce kinodynamic feasibility. On the other hand, speed profile optimization (i.e., temporal) algorithms usually focus on minimum time and maximum rider comfort while navigating a given path
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. For example,

[
6
]
showed that a minimum-time objective function can be reformulated in terms of the path parameter, and the problem was generalized for certain classes of systems in
[
7
]
.
In
[
8
]
, the authors used the spatio-temporal separation to iteratively optimize the path and the speed profile;
both optimization subproblems are convex but there is ambiguity in the stopping criteria.

The kinematic bicycle model is a widely used model which captures the nonholonomic constraint present in actual vehicle dynamics and has been used in many optimal control problems for vehicle motion planning.
For example,
[
10
]
studied the consistency of using the 3 DOF kinematic bicycle model for motion planning by comparing its results with a 9 DOF
model;

[
11
]
formulated an MPC problem based on the kinematic bicycle model, but the resulting problem is nonconvex and provides no safety guarantees in the continuous-time sense;

[
12
]
demonstrated the high performance of stochastic MPC using the nonlinear bicycle model in a miniature racing environment, but their safety guarantees are given in the probabilistic sense.

In this work, we formulate the vehicle motion planning problem as an optimal control problem using the kinematic bicycle model. In order to ensure that the trajectories satisfy the safety constraints in the continuous-time sense, we propose necessary and sufficient conditions in flat space to guarantee safety in the state space, based on the differential flatness property of the kinematic bicycle model.
We use spatio-temporal separation and the convexity properties of B-splines to relax the original optimal control problem into three sequential SOCPs yielding a path, a trajectory duration, and a speed profile.
We show that the SOCP solutions constitute a suboptimal system trajectory to the original optimal control problem. Notably, SOCPs are a special type of convex optimization problem for which efficient solvers exist, which makes the proposed approach suitable for real-time and embedded applications.
Furthermore, the trajectories generated are proven to satisfy rigorously, in the continuous-time sense, the state and input safety constraints including maximum steering angle, position constraints, maximum velocity and maximum acceleration.

II-C

Second-order Cone Constraints

Second-order cone programs
(SOCPs) are convex optimization problems of the following form
[
20
,
21
]
:

\displaystyle\min\quad

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{f}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}

(6)

s.t.

\displaystyle\|A_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{i}\|_{2}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{c}}_{i}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}+d_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the decision variable,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{f}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
A_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{i}-1)\times n}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}-1}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{c}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. The constraint shown in (
7
) is the
second-order cone
(SOC) constraint. SOCPs can be seen as a generalization of more specialized types of convex optimization problems such as linear programs (LPs), quadratic programs (QPs) and convex, quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs)
[
20
,
21
]
. SOCPs can be solved in polynomial time by interior-point methods, and specialized SOCP solvers also exist
[
22
]
.

II-D

Problem Statement

The motion planning problem of a car is formulated as the following constrained optimal control problem with variable horizon:

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(\cdot),\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(\cdot),t_{f}}\quad

\displaystyle\nu t_{f}+\int_{0}^{t_{f}}L(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t),\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t))\;\mathrm{d}t

(OPT)

s. t.

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}}(t)=f(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t))+g(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t))\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t),

(8a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(0)=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t_{f})=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{f},

(8b)

\displaystyle 0\leq v(t)\leq\overline{v},\quad\forall t\in[0,t_{f}],

(8c)

\displaystyle|\dot{v}(t)|\leq\overline{a},\quad\forall t\in[0,t_{f}],

(8d)

\displaystyle|\gamma(t)|\leq\overline{\gamma},\quad\forall t\in[0,t_{f}],

(8e)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)\in\mathcal{D},\quad\forall t\in[0,t_{f}],

(8f)

where
f
and
g
are defined in the kinematic bicycle model (
1
),
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{f}
are given initial and final states, respectively,
\overline{v}>0
is the speed limit,
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the obstacle-free region,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}\triangleq(x,y)^{T}
is the position vector,
\overline{a}>0
is the maximum acceleration and braking and
0<\overline{\gamma}<\pi/2
is the steering angle limit. The Lagrange cost functional
L:\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is chosen to promote smoothness properties for the trajectory, and the parameter
\nu>0
encodes the tradeoff between time-optimality and smoothness. The optimal control problem (
OPT
) is generally non-convex and computationally demanding to solve in real-time. Furthermore, constraints (
8c
)-(
8f
) are difficult to satisfy strictly in the continuous-time sense.

In this paper, we solve (
OPT
) by proposing a sequential SOCP approach, which guarantees that the constraints (
8c
)-(
8f
) are rigorously satisfied in the continuous-time. While we won’t compromise on safety (feasibility), we will trade optimality for an increase in computational efficiency. Our approach leverages the differential flatness property of the bicycle model and parameterizes flat outputs using a pair of convoluted B-spline curves whose convexity properties will allow us to verify such constraints formally in continuous-time. We consider a separation between space (
\mathbb{R}^{2}
) and time to first find a
path
with desirable properties; then, we use these properties to find a
speed profile
for navigating it. Convoluting the
path
with its
speed profile
results in the flat output trajectory required to recover the state-space trajectory.

III
Safety Constraints Satisfaction in Flat Space

In this section, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions on the flat output trajectory
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}(t)
that can guarantee continuous-time safety in the state-space.

Consider a

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s)\triangleq\big(x(s),y(s)\big)^{T}\in C^{2}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}(t)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}\big(s(t)\big),

(9a)

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\dot{s}(t)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}\big(s(t)\big),

(9b)

\displaystyle\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\ddot{s}(t)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}\big(s(t)\big)+\dot{s}^{2}(t)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}\big(s(t)\big),

(9c)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}\big(s(t)\big)
denotes differentiation of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}
with respect to
s

, and similarly for
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}\big(s(t)\big)
.

Remark 1
.

The parameterization shown in (
9a
) provides a number of benefits. First, the map (
2
) has singularities if

for some

\dot{x}(t)=\dot{y}(t)=0

) allows one to avoid the singularity even in zero-speed situations

]
.
Second, we can now consider the safety of the path
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}
, such as obstacle avoidance and steering angle constraints, independently of the speed profile

In the following, we will overload the notation of the flat map (
2
) as
\Phi(t)\triangleq\Phi\big(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}(t),\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}}(t)\big)=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)
,
\Psi(t)\triangleq\Psi\big(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}(t),\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}}(t),\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}}(t)\big)=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)
,
where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}(t)
and its derivatives are parameterized in (

)-(

).

III-A

Path Safety

Define the

and

, respectively, as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\triangleq\{(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\gamma|\leq\overline{\gamma}<\pi/2\},

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{D}

\displaystyle\triangleq\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}=(x,y)^{T}\in\mathcal{D}\},

(11)

Lemma 1
.

The state-space trajectory
\big(\Phi(t),\Psi(t)\big)\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
for all

if and only if

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|_{2}/\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}^{3}\leq\tan\overline{\gamma}/L,\quad\forall s\in[0,1].

Proof.

It can be shown from the expression of

and the considered parameterization (

\gamma(t)=\arctan\big({L(y^{\prime\prime}\small(s\small)x^{\prime}\small(s\small)-x^{\prime\prime}\small(s\small)y^{\prime}\small(s\small))}/\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}\small(s\small)\|_{2}^{3}\big)
.
Furthermore, over the range

\tan|\gamma|/L={\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|_{2}}/{\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}^{3}}

Observing that
\tan(|\gamma|)\leq\tan(\overline{\gamma}),\gamma\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\iff|\gamma|\leq\overline{\gamma}<\pi/2

∎

Lemma 2
.

The state-space trajectory

.

IV
Convexification of Problem (
FLAT-OPT
)

In this section, we describe a sequential convexification approach for (
FLAT-OPT

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s)
, the second SOCP finds a duration
t_{f}
for the trajectory, and the third SOCP computes a safe velocity profile

(see Figure

FLAT-OPT

.

A

IV-A

B-spline Path Optimization

The following propositions provide convex relaxations to the conditions of Lemma
1
and Lemma

Proposition 3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s)
be a B-spline path and
b_{r,i,j}
be the
(i,j)
-th entry of matrix
B_{r}
defined in Section
II-B
.
If there exists positive constant
\alpha>0
, column unit vector
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and variables
\underline{v}_{\theta},\overline{a}_{\theta},\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that the B-spline path
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s)
satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}^{(1)}_{i}\geq\underline{v}_{\theta},\quad j=1,\ldots,N_{\theta},

(18a)

\displaystyle\big\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}^{(2)}_{i}\big\|_{2}\leq\overline{a}_{\theta},\quad j=2,\ldots,N_{\theta},

(18b)

\displaystyle\big\|\begin{bmatrix}2\alpha&\frac{4\tan\overline{\gamma}}{L}\beta-1\end{bmatrix}^{T}\big\|_{2}\leq 4\beta\tan\overline{\gamma}/L+1,

(18c)

\displaystyle\overline{a}_{\theta}\leq\alpha\underline{v}_{\theta}-\beta,\quad\beta,\underline{v}_{\theta}\geq 0,

(18d)

then the state-space trajectory
\big(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t),\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)\big)\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma},

\forall t\in[0,t_{f}]
where
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
is the
steering angle safety set
defined in (
10
).

Proof.

By Proposition
1
conditions (
18a
)-(
18b
) imply that
\underline{v}_{\theta}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}
and
\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|_{2}\leq\overline{a}_{\theta}
for all
s\in[0,1)
.
Continue by expanding (
18c
) to observe that
0\geq\alpha^{2}-4\beta\tan\overline{\gamma}/L\beta\triangleq\Delta_{v}
.
Notice that
\Delta_{v}
is the discriminant of the
quadratic polynomial in
\underline{v}_{\theta}
given by
p(\underline{v}_{\theta})\triangleq\frac{\tan\overline{\gamma}}{L}\underline{v}_{\theta}^{2}-\alpha\underline{v}_{\theta}+\beta
.
It follows from
\Delta_{v}\leq 0
that the roots of

p(\underline{v}_{\theta})
are either repeated and real, or complex
conjugates. Therefore, the polynomial
p(\underline{v}_{\theta})
does
not change sign. Since
p(0)\geq 0
(because
\beta\geq 0
), we must have that
p(\underline{v}_{\theta})\geq 0
. In particular, we can
now observe that

\overline{a}_{\theta}\!\leq\!\alpha\underline{v}_{\theta}-\beta\!\leq\underline{v}_{\theta}^{2}\tan\overline{\gamma}/L\!\implies\!\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|_{2}\!\leq\!\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}^{2}\tan\overline{\gamma}/L

holds. Now multiply by
\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}\geq 0
in both sides of the implied inequality to establish:

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}^{3}\tan\overline{\gamma}/L\geq\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}\geq\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|_{2}.

The conclusion follows directly from Lemma
1
.∎

.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}
of the B-spline path
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}\in\mathcal{D},\quad j=0,\ldots,N_{\theta},

(19)

then the state-space trajectory
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)\in\mathcal{D}
for all

.

Proof.

Condition (
19
) implies, by Proposition
1
, that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s)\in\mathcal{D}
for all
s\in[0,1)
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
2
.
∎

Remark 2
.

While the obstacle-free space
\mathcal{D}
is generally nonconvex, the convexity assumption is easily relaxed by considering the concept of “safe corridor” (union of convex sets) and enforcing the conditions of Proposition
4
segment-wise instead of globally. The reader is referred to our previous work
[

and to

For fixed values of

are convex and we can formulate the following SOCP to find a safe path

:

\displaystyle\min\;

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\|_{2}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s+\overline{v}_{\theta}-\underline{v}_{\theta}+\overline{a}_{\theta}

(PATH-SOCP)

s. t.

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{0},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{N_{\theta}}=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{f},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}^{(1)}_{1}\!=\!\overline{v}_{\theta}(\cos\psi_{0},\sin\psi_{0})^{T},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}^{(1)}_{N_{\theta}}\!=\!\overline{v}_{\theta}(\cos\psi_{f},\sin\psi_{f})^{T},

\displaystyle\big\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}^{(1)}_{i}\big\|_{2}\leq\overline{v}_{\theta},\quad j=1,\ldots,N_{\theta},

\displaystyle\mbox{\text{\eqref{cvx_gamma_cst} and \eqref{cvx_obs_cst} hold}},

with decision variables

and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{0},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{N_{\theta}}
,
where

is the maximum steering angle,

is the initial/final position vector and

is the initial/final heading angle. Note that the resulting B-spline path

Remark 3
.

Proposition
3
contains two convex relaxations by lower-bounding a norm and a convex, quadratic function. Because of the relaxations, the size of the feasible region of (

and

\alpha=\frac{2\tan\overline{\gamma}}{L}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{f}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{0}\|_{2},\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{f}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{f}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}_{0}\|_{2}}.

IV-B

Temporal Optimization

In this subsection, we find an appropriate trajectory duration

t_{f}
by considering a minimization of both
t_{f}
and the magnitude of the acceleration vector.
Consider a B-spline path
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s)
feasible in (
PATH-SOCP
), and the functions
b(s)\triangleq\dot{s}^{2}
and
a(s)\triangleq\ddot{s}
, which must satisfy the differential condition
b^{\prime}(s)=2a(s)
.
Following
[
6
]
, we have that

t_{f}=\int_{0}^{t_{f}}1\;\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\dot{s}}\;\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{1}b(s)^{-1/2}\;\mathrm{d}s.

(20)

Purely minimizing the trajectory duration given by
t_{f}
results in maximum-speed velocity profiles. We additionally minimize the acceleration to encourage trajectories with mild friction circle profiles by choosing a Lagrange cost functional
[
28
]
:

L\big(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t),\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)\big)\triangleq\dot{v}^{2}(t)+v^{2}(t)\dot{\psi}^{2}(t)=\|\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}.

(21)

We can now write the objective functional entirely in terms of (and convex with respect to) the new functions
a(s)
and
b(s)
as follows:

J\big(a(s),b(s)\big)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{b(s)}}+\|a(s)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s)+b(s)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|^{2}_{2}\;\mathrm{d}s.

We follow a similar procedure as in
[
6
]
and consider
N_{t}+1
points partitioning the interval
[0,1]
into
N_{t}
uniform segments with width
\Delta s\triangleq 1/N_{t}
.
The discretized Lagrange cost functional becomes

L(s_{i},a_{i},b_{i})=\|a_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s_{i})+b_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s_{i})\|^{2}_{2},

where
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are the decision variables representing
a(s_{i})
and
b(s_{i})
, respectively. Assuming that
a(s)
is piecewise constant over each segment
[s_{i},s_{i+1}),i=0,\ldots,N_{t}-1
where
s_{i}\triangleq i\Delta s
, we can exactly evaluate the integral (
20
) to avoid the case when
\dot{s}=0
as shown in
[
6
]
.

We formulate the following SOCP to obtain the trajectory duration
t_{f}
:

\displaystyle\min\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N_{t}-1}2\nu\Delta sd_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{N_{t}}L(s_{i},a_{i},b_{i})

(TIME-SOCP)

s. t.

\displaystyle\big\|(2c_{i},b_{i}-1)^{T}\big\|_{2}\leq b_{i}+1,\quad i=0,\ldots,N_{t},

\displaystyle\big\|(2,\overline{c}_{i}-d_{i})^{T}\big\|_{2}\leq\overline{c}_{i}+d_{i},\;i=0,\ldots,N_{t}-1,

\displaystyle 2\Delta sa_{i}=b_{i}-b_{i-1},\quad i=1,\ldots,N_{t},

\displaystyle b_{0}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(0)\|_{2}^{2}=v_{0}^{2},\quad b_{N_{t}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(1)\|_{2}^{2}=v_{f}^{2},

\displaystyle b_{i}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s_{i})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\overline{v}^{2},\quad i=0,\ldots,N_{t},

\displaystyle\Big|a_{i}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s_{i})\|_{2}+b_{i}f_{i}\Big|\leq\overline{a},\quad i=0,\ldots,N_{t},

\displaystyle\overline{c}_{i}\triangleq c_{i+1}+c_{i},\quad f_{i}\triangleq\big(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s_{i})\big)/\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}(s_{i})\|_{2},

with decision variables
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}
,
i=0,\ldots,N_{t}
. The first two constraints are the SOCP embedding of (
20
) given in
[
6
]
, the third constraint is from the differential constraint relating
a(s)
and
b(s)
, the fourth sets initial and final speeds, the fifth ensures the speed bound is respected and the sixth ensures the acceleration bound is respected. From our assumption that the function
a(s)
is constant over each
[s_{i},s_{i+1})
segment, we can recover the duration of each segment from the constant acceleration equation
\Delta t_{i}=(\sqrt{b_{i}}+\sqrt{b_{i-1}})/a_{i},\;i=1,\ldots,N_{t}.

The overall duration of the trajectory is then
t_{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\Delta t_{i}.

Remark 4
.

The solution of (
TIME-SOCP
) provides a safe speed profile at discrete time instances. If continuous-time safety is not critical, it suffices to stop here and retrieve the discretized state-space solution. In addition, if the desired trajectory duration
t_{f}
is known, one can skip (
TIME-SOCP
) and proceed to the next SOCP after solving (
PATH-SOCP
).

Proposition 6

For any given nonnegative vectors

The first two conditions imply, by Proposition

Expanding the last condition, we determine that for any

\displaystyle|\overline{\epsilon}_{k}\overline{v}_{\theta}|

\displaystyle\geq|\ddot{s}\small(t\small)|\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s\small(t\small))\|_{2}=|a_{t}\small(t\small)|,

\displaystyle|\overline{\kappa}_{k}^{2}\overline{a}_{\theta}|

\displaystyle\geq\big|{\dot{s}^{2}\small(t\small)\big(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s\small(t\small))\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime\prime}(s\small(t\small))\big)}/{\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\theta}}^{\prime}(s\small(t\small))\|}\big|=|a_{n}\small(t\small)|,

VI
Conclusions

In this work we presented a sequential SOCP approach to solve the optimal control problem with a kinematic bicycle model where the state and input trajectories generated are guaranteed to satisfy the constraints in the continuous-time sense. We also compared the performance of the proposed framework to that of state-of-the-art solvers and demonstrated its efficiency in a simulated scenario. In future work, we will leverage the safety guarantees of the proposed framework to achieve safety both at a higher level in the form of objective success and at a lower level in the form of tracking safety guarantees.

Periodic Infinite Frieze Patterns of Type Λ p 1 , … , p n \Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}} and Dissections on Annuli
Esther Banaian, Jiuqi Chen

Abstract
Finite frieze patterns with entries in ℤ ⁡ [ λ p 1 , … , λ p s ] \mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{p_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{p_{s}}] where { p 1 , … , p s } ⊆ ℤ ≥ 3 \{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3} and λ p = 2 ​ cos ⁡ ( π / p ) \lambda_{p}=2\cos(\pi/p) were shown to have a connection to dissected polygons by Holm and Jørgensen. We extend their work by studying the connection between infinite frieze patterns with such entries and dissections of annuli and once-punctured discs. We give an algorithm to determine whether a frieze pattern with entries in ℤ ⁡ [ λ p 1 , … , λ p s ] \mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{p_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{p_{s}}] , finite or infinite, comes from a dissected surface. We introduce quotient dissections as a realization for some frieze patterns unrealizable by an ordinary dissection. We also introduce two combinatorial interpretations for entries of frieze patterns from dissected surfaces. These interpretations are a generalization of matchings introduced by Broline, Crowe, and Isaacs for finite frieze patterns over ℤ \mathbb{Z} .

2.2
Infinite Frieze Patterns of Positive Integers

Tschabold produced infinite frieze patterns by using the same algorithm as Conway and Coxeter but starting with a triangulated once-punctured disc
[

[

we will discuss how the frieze patterns from the outer boundary and inner boundary are related. See Figure

Frieze Patterns of Type

We further study frieze patterns whose quiddity sequence consists of sums of

Definition 5
.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3}
. An infinite frieze pattern is of

\Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}}
if each entry in its quiddity row is the sum of positive integral multiples of
\lambda_{p_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{p_{s}}

For example, the frieze pattern in Figure
3
is Type
\Lambda_{3,4}
.
Given a frieze pattern
\mathcal{F}
of Type
\Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}}
, we can write each entry in the quiddity row in the form of

m_{i-1,i+1}=\sum_{p\in A_{i}}\lambda_{p}

where
A_{i}
is a multiset. For example, given the quiddity cycle
(3,2+\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3})
, we have that
A_{3k}=\{3,3,3\},A_{3k+1}=\{3,3,4\},
and
A_{3k+2}=\{4,6\}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We record some useful results about frieze patterns. First, we study 1-periodic frieze patterns. These results are most conveniently stated in terms of Chebyshev polynomials.

Definition 6
.

Let
U_{k}(x)
denote the
normalized Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
, determined by initial conditions
U_{-1}(x)=0,U_{0}(x)=1
and the recurrence,

U_{k}(x)=U_{1}(x)U_{k-1}(x)-U_{k-2}(x)

For example,
U_{1}(x)=x
and
U_{2}(x)=x^{2}-1
. These normalized Chebyshev polynomials can be obtained by evaluating the ordinary Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind at
\frac{x}{2}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the 1-periodic frieze pattern determined by the quiddity cycle
(x)
, where
x
is an indeterminate. Then,
\mathcal{F}
is an infinite frieze pattern, with the
k
-th row given by
(U_{k}(x))
.

This lemma is quickly proven given the following well-known result, which shows that rows of frieze patterns satisfy a recurrence similar to the Chebyshev recurrence.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{m_{i,j}\}
be a frieze pattern. Then, for all
m_{i,j}
with
|j-i|\geq 2
,

m_{i,j}=m_{i,i+2}m_{i+1,j}-m_{i+2,j}

and dually,

m_{i,j}=m_{j-2,j}m_{i,j-1}-m_{i,j-2}.

Thus, we can study one-periodic frieze patterns by studying evaluations of Chebyshev polynomials. Lemma

Lemma 3
.

Evaluations of Chebyshev polynomials at
\lambda_{p}
are periodic, in the sense that
U_{k+p}(\lambda_{p})=-U_{k}(\lambda_{p})
. In particular,
U_{p-1}(\lambda_{p})=-U_{-1}(\lambda_{p})=0
.

From Lemma

Lemma 4
.

If a sequence of numbers contains more than

Proof.

Suppose we have
m_{0,2}=m_{1,3}=\cdots=m_{p-2,p}=\lambda_{p}

4
Dissections of Once-Punctured Discs and Annuli

Our main goal is to study frieze patterns from dissections of once-punctured discs and annuli. Accordingly, we must describe these surfaces and their dissections more carefully.

We let
A_{n,m}
denote an annulus with
n

m

denote a once-punctured disc with

as the outer boundary. As a convention, we will let

denote a vertex on the outer boundary of either surface; if working with

There are two types of arcs in a dissection of

Definition 7
.

Let

be a dissection of the annulus

As a convention, we will assume there are never peripheral arcs which are incident to the inner boundary. Since our frieze patterns are determined by the number of subgons incident to each vertex, we will consider dissections up to Dehn twist.

We also describe several special types of subgons in a dissection. In general, a dissection can have many subgons which are not one of these types.

Definition 8
.

•

A subgon of size
p

•

A subgon in a dissection which has at least one edge on the outer boundary and at least one vertex on the inner boundary is called an

We will see in Section
5

Definition 9

A dissection of
A_{n,m}
or
S_{n}
is said to be

Importantly, a skeletal dissection will not contain any ears.

Definition 10

An infinite frieze pattern of period

We begin by determining some frieze patterns which are unrealizable.

Proposition 1

(Realizability test)
.

Let
\{m_{i-1,i+1}\}
be such that

for all

is unrealizable if at least one of the following is true.

1.

There exists

Proof.

In a dissection, each pair of adjacent vertices are incident to a common subgon. Therefore, a quiddity sequence from a dissection must have

for all

Similarly, it is impossible for the quiddity sequence from a realizable frieze pattern to have more than

5.1

In order to describe which frieze patterns are realizable by dissections of

A skeletal dissection will always produce a skeletal frieze pattern.

5.3
Cutting

The conditions of Proposition
2
are not sufficient for non-skeletal frieze patterns. For example, the quiddity cycle
(1+\sqrt{2},1,2,1+\sqrt{2})
satisfies all conditions, but we claim it is not realizable. Since this frieze pattern is not skeletal, we can
cut
it in order to make it simpler. Baur, Parsons, and Tschabold introduced the notion of cutting a frieze pattern when proving one of their main results
[
1
]
. They were working with frieze patterns of positive integers and triangulations of surfaces; we extend the notion of cutting to general dissections.

Definition 15
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a frieze pattern with quiddity cycle
(m_{0,2},\ldots,m_{n-1,n+1})

p

5.4
Realization Algorithm

Since we have direct realizability criteria for skeletal frieze patterns in Proposition
2
, we can use cutting to characterize the realizability of arbitrary frieze patterns of Type
\Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}
be any frieze pattern of Type
\Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}}
with a period
n

\mathcal{F}_{0}
is finite, of width

, we must consider the quiddity row of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
as
n
-periodic. If
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is infinite, we can consider any valid period of its quiddity row. We run the following algorithm to determine whether
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is realizable; moreover, if
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is realizable, we will be able to determine which surface can be dissected to realize
\mathcal{F}_{0}
or whether
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is only realizable by a quotient dissection.

Let
i\geq 0
.

1.

If
\mathcal{F}_{i}
satisfies at least one condition of Proposition
1
or has any entries in the quiddity sequence which are less than or equal to 0, it is unrealizable.

2.

If
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is generated by the quiddity cycle
(\lambda_{p})
, then
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is realizable by an empty dissection of a
p
-gon.
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is realizable by a dissection of an
n
-gon.

3.

If
\mathcal{F}_{i}

be the result of cutting
\mathcal{F}_{i}
at one interval. Return to step 1.

4.

If
\mathcal{F}_{i}
passes the realizability test and is skeletal, but does not satisfy Proposition
2
, then
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is realizable by a quotient dissection.
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is realizable by a quotient dissection of
A_{n,m}
.

5.

If
\mathcal{F}_{i}
satisfies Proposition
2
, implying that it passes the realizability test and is skeletal, then
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is realizable by a dissection of an annulus or once-punctured disc.
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is realizable by a dissection of
A_{n,m}
(for some

) or
S_{n}
.

(a)

Lemma
6
provides one way to determine whether
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is realizable by
S_{n}
or

(b)

Proposition
3
gives another method of checking:
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is realizable by

if only if the

(see Theorem

When we end in cases 2,4, or 5, Lemma
7
and Lemma

\mathcal{F}_{0}
is also realizable by the same type of dissection as
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. In each case, these realizations can be constructed by geometrically gluing subgons onto the skeletal realization of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
in the reverse order of the cuts performed to get from

6.1

We now introduce combinatorial interpretations of the entries of frieze patterns of Type
\Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}
from dissected surfaces. Our interpretations will hold in both infinite and finite cases. Each entry in a frieze pattern will correspond to to a sum of weighted

on the surface. We will in fact introduce two distinct weightings for matchings; these two weightings shed light on different features of these frieze patterns.

Definition 17

Let

Traditionally, a matching

Broline, Crowe and Isaacs were the first to connect matchings to frieze patterns

]

6.2
Local Weighting

We now define our first method to weight paths in dissected polygons. We will use
w[a:b]=\mathcal{P}_{a},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{b}
to denote a submatching of
w=\mathcal{P}_{i+1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{j-1}
, where
i+1\leq a\leq b\leq j-1
. If
a>b
, then
w[a:b]=\emptyset
.

Definition 18

(Local Weighting)
.

Given a matching
w=\mathcal{P}_{i+1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{j-1}
in a dissected polygon, we define the local weight
wt_{L}(w)
recursively. Suppose
k
is the largest positive integer such that the first
k
subgons in
w
are the same, i.e.
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}=\mathcal{P}_{i+2}=\cdots=\mathcal{P}_{i+k}\neq\mathcal{P}_{i+k+1}
, for some
1\leq k\leq j-i-1
. Then, we set

wt_{L}(w)=U_{k}(\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|})wt_{L}(w[i+k+1:j-1]).

We set
wt_{L}(\emptyset)=1
.

This weighting is “local” in the sense that it only depends on consecutive occurrences of the same subgon. See Example
6
and Figure
8
for examples of calculating
wt_{L}
.

Lemma
10
gives an equivalent definition of the local weighting by recursively checking if two consecutive subgons in a matching are equal.

Lemma 10
.

Given a matching
w=\mathcal{P}_{i+1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{j-1}
in a dissected polygon, we define
wt_{L^{\prime}}
recursively. If
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}=\mathcal{P}_{i+2},
set

wt_{L^{\prime}}(w)=\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|}wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+2:j-1])-wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+3:j-1]),

and otherwise we set

wt_{L^{\prime}}(w)=\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|}wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+2:j-1]).

We set
wt_{L^{\prime}}(\emptyset)=1
.
Then, for any
w
,
wt_{L}(w)=wt_{L^{\prime}}(w)
.

Proof.

Let
w\in\mathbf{P}_{i,j}
. We will induct on
j-i
.

If
j=i
, there are no matchings in
\mathbf{P}_{i,j}
. If
j=i+1
, then
\mathbf{P}_{i,j}=\{\emptyset\}
, and
wt_{L}(\emptyset)=wt_{L^{\prime}}(\emptyset)=1
. If
j=i+2
, then each matching
w\in\mathbf{P}_{i,j}
is of the form
w=\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
. In this case as well,
wt_{L}(\mathcal{P}_{i+1})=wt_{L^{\prime}}(\mathcal{P}_{i+1})=\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|}
.

Now, suppose we have established the claim for all
w\in\mathbf{P}_{i,j}
with
j\leq i+\ell-1
,
\ell\geq 3
and all possible values of
k
, the number of consecutive subgons at the beginning of a matching which are the same. Let
j=i+\ell
and let
w\in\mathbf{P}_{i,j}
. Suppose the first
k\geq 1
subgons of
w
are the same. If
k=1
, then by induction we immediately have

wt_{L^{\prime}}(w)=\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|}wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+2,j-1])=wt_{L}(w)

Now let
k>1
. Then,

wt_{L^{\prime}}(w)=\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|}wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+2:j-1])-wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+3:j-1])

By induction, we can rewrite the righthand side in terms of
wt_{L}
,

\displaystyle wt_{L^{\prime}}(w)=\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|}wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+2:j-1])-wt_{L^{\prime}}(w[i+3:j-1])

\displaystyle=\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|}U_{k-1}(\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|})wt_{L}(w[i+k+1:j-1])-U_{k-2}(\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|})wt_{L}(w[i+k+1:j-1])

\displaystyle=U_{k}(\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}_{i+1}|})wt_{L}(w[i+k+1:j-1]).

∎

6.3
Traditional Weighting

We now have one combinatorial interpretation for entries of a realizable frieze pattern of Type
\Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}}
. However, there are some matchings
w
such that
wt_{L}(w)<0
, even though the frieze pattern entries appear to be all positive. We introduce another weighting,
wt_{T}
, which satisfies
wt_{T}(w)\geq 0
for all matchings

Definition 19

(Traditional Weighting)
.

Consider a dissected polygon, with vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
. Let
w=(\mathcal{P}_{i+1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{j-1})
be a matching between vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
. For all
\mathcal{P}
in the dissection, let
k_{\mathcal{P}}
be the number of times that
\mathcal{P}
is used in
w
. If for any
\mathcal{P}
,
k_{\mathcal{P}}>|\mathcal{P}|-2
, then
wt_{T}(w)=0
. Otherwise, we set

wt_{T}(w)=\prod_{\mathcal{P}\in w}U_{k_{\mathcal{P}}}(\lambda_{|\mathcal{P}|})

We define this weighting on a dissected polygon; when working with a dissection of
A_{n,m}
or
S_{n}
, we will calculate the weight of a matching from the universal cover.

This weighting scheme is “traditional” in the sense that, in the case of triangulations, it forbids using a triangle more than one time in a matching, as was the case in
[
5
]
and
[
1
]
. In the case of dissections, Bessenrodt also only permitted using a
p
-gon up to
p-2
times in a matching in
[
4
]
. This weighting differs from Bessenrodt’s though, as she used formal variables and did not use Chebyshev polynomials to track using a subgon multiple times.

Remark 5
.

We do not define traditional weighting on a quotient dissection. We do not yet know a consistent way to weight matchings in a quotient dissection, other than with
wt_{L}
, which would give an interpretation of the entries of the corresponding frieze pattern. In particular, we cannot verify that all frieze patterns from quotient dissections have positive entries; see Conjecture
1
.

Positivity

Since
wt_{T}(w)\geq 0
for any matching
w
, it follows from Theorem
9

Corollary 5
.

If
\mathcal{F}=\{m_{i,j}\}
is an infinite frieze pattern from a dissected annulus or once-punctured disc, then for all
i<j
,
m_{i,j}>0
.

Proof.

We must show that, for all
i<j
, there is at least one matching
w\in\mathbf{P}_{i,j}
with
wt_{T}(w)>0
. From Equation
4
, we know that if any edge from
\mathcal{D}
is strictly between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, so that there are ears between the vertices, we can remove these and the sum over matchings using
wt_{T}
will be unchanged. From the proof of Equation
7
, we know the same is true for edges weakly between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
. Thus, it suffices to consider the case where there does not exist an edge
(v_{\ell},v_{m})\in\mathcal{D}
with
i\leq\ell<m\leq j
.

In this case, for any subgon
\mathcal{P}
with vertices strictly between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, a matching can choose
\mathcal{P}
at most
|\mathcal{P}|-2
times. For a concrete example, one could form a matching
\mathcal{P}_{i+1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{j-1}
by setting
\mathcal{P}_{k}
as the subgon which has edge
(v_{k},v_{k+1})
for all

. This is the matching which uses the counterclockwise-most subgon incident to each vertex.

∎

We consider the positivity of the other frieze patterns of Type

Proposition 5
.

Let

Proof.

Baur, Parsons, and Tschabold proved this in the case of positive integers in

. Their proof can be modified for frieze patterns with more general entries. In particular, we can combine the fact that the 1-periodic frieze pattern determined by quiddity row

1
]
applied to

∎

Corollary 6
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{m_{i,j}\}
be an
n
-periodic, infinite frieze pattern. Suppose that the first
n
nontrivial rows are positive; that is
m_{i,j}>0
whenever
|j-i|\leq n+1
. Moreover, suppose that the first growth coefficient,
s_{1}=m_{0,n+1}-m_{1,n}
, satisfies
s_{1}\geq 2
. Then, all entries of
\mathcal{F}
are positive.

Proof.

First, note by Lemma 4.3 of
[
2

that if
s_{1}\geq 2

s_{k}\geq 2
where
s_{k}
is the
k

Next, we recall a special case of Theorem 5.4 of
[
13
]
, known as the Progression Formulas. Let
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
k\in Z_{\geq 1}
. Moreover, first assume
i<j

m_{i,j+(k-1)n}=s_{k-1}m_{i,j}+m_{j,i+k(n-1)}

Similarly, suppose

m_{i,j+kn}=s_{k-1}m_{i,j+n}+m_{j,i+kn}

We see we can write entries which are below the
n
-th row in terms of entries which are in higher rows and growth coefficients. Thus, given that the first
n

\mathcal{F}
are positive.

∎

Note that in the example of an unrealizable frieze pattern with negative entries, the first growth coefficient is
\sqrt{2}
, which is positive but not larger than 2.

We can use Proposition

Conjecture 1

The only frieze patterns of Type
\Lambda_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}}
with negative entries are those which fail the realizability test ( Proposition

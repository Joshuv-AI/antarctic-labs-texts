Masaki KAWAMOTO


Abstract
This paper is concerned with nonlinear Schrödinger equations with a time-decaying harmonic potential. The nonlinearity is gauge-invariant of the long-range critical order. In [ 20 ] and [ 23 ] , it is proved that the equation admits a nontrivial solution that behaves like a free solution with a logarithmic phase correction in the frameworks of both the final state problem and the initial value problem. Furthermore, a modified scattering operator has been established in the case without the potential in [ 15 ] . In this paper, we construct a modified scattering operator for our equation by utilizing a generator of the Galilean transformation. Moreover, we remove a restriction for the coefficient of the potential which is required in [ 23 ] .

Introduction

1.1
Setting and Backgounrd

In this paper, we consider the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u-H(t)u=F(u),

(NLS)

where
(t,x)\in{\bf R}\times{\bf R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
, and
u=u(t,x)
is a complex-valued unknown function.
The operator
H(t)
is defined by

\displaystyle H(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\sigma(t)\frac{|x|^{2}}{2},\quad\sigma(t)\in L^{\infty}({\bf R};{\bf R}).

The coefficient of the potential
\sigma(t)
satisfies the time-decay condition
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2}\sigma(t)\eqqcolon\sigma_{1}\in(-\infty,1/4)
, more precisely see Assumption
1.1
. The nonlinearity
F
is gauge invariant of degree
1+2/d(1-\lambda)
; that is, it is the form of

\displaystyle F(u)=\eta|u|^{p_{c}}u,\quad p_{c}\coloneqq\frac{2}{d(1-\lambda)},\quad\eta\in{\bf R},

(1.1)

where
\lambda=(1-\sqrt{1-4\sigma_{1}})/2<1/2
with
-1/3<\lambda
if
d=3
.

The aim of this paper is to deal with a scattering operator to (
NLS
). As for (
NLS
) with
\sigma(t)\equiv 0
, that is,

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u=G(u),\quad G(u)=\eta|u|^{p}u,

(1.2)

then (
1.2
) admits a nontrivial solution that behaves like a free solution
U_{0}(t)u_{\pm}

as
t\rightarrow\pm\infty
, when
p>2/d
, where
U_{0}(t)=e^{it\Delta/2}
and
u_{\pm}
is called the
final date
. In this sense, we say the case
p>2/d
is the
short-range
.
On the other hand, if
p\leq 2/d
, then (
1.2
) has no solutions that behave like the free solution in
L^{2}
(e.g.,
[
32
,
3
,
33
]
), hence we say the case
p\leq d/2
is the
long-range
. Hence we can regard the exponent
p_{c}^{\prime}\coloneqq 2/d
as the threshold in the sense of scattering problems.
In particular, when
p=p_{c}^{\prime}
, there is the solution that behaves like a free solution with a logarithmic phase correction
U_{N}(t)u_{\pm}:=U_{0}(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\exp\left(-i\eta\left|\mathcal{F}u_{\pm}\right|^{p_{c}}\log|t|\right)\mathcal{F}u_{\pm}

for a suitable given
u_{\pm}
as
t\to\pm\infty
(cf.
[
29
,
12
,
14
]
), where
\mathcal{F}
is the usual Fourier transform.
A scattering problem for nonlinear Schrödinger equations has been considered in two directions.
One direction is the final state problem.
We attempt to construct a solution
u
to (
1.2
) that behaves like a prescribed asymptotics
U_{N}(t)u_{-}
as
t\rightarrow-\infty
in the problem.
The second is the initial value problem that is dealt with a global-in-time solution and asymptotics
U_{N}(t)u_{+}
for large time, specified by given initial data. If we find the solution under the final state problem, then the wave operator
\mathcal{W}_{-}
which is a map from
u_{-}
into
u(0)
can be defined.
On the other hand, we can define the inverse wave operator
\mathcal{W}_{+}^{-1}
from
u(0)
into
u_{+}
, once the initial value problem is solved.
Furthermore, if the range of
\mathcal{W}_{-}
included in the domain of
\mathcal{W}_{+}^{-1}
, then the scattering operator
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\mathcal{W}_{+}^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{-}
can be defined.

The scattering operator on the critical case
p=p_{c}^{\prime}
is called the
modified scattering operator
.
In
[
5
]
, when
d=1
, Carles constructs a modified scattering operator for (
1.2
) on
H^{0,3}\cap H^{1,2}
if
\norm{u_{-}}_{H^{1}\cap H^{0,1}}
is small.
Later on, the operator on the neighborhood centered at the origin in
H^{\alpha,0}\cap H^{0,\alpha}
has been defined in Hayashi and Naumkin
[
15
]
under
d/2<\alpha<\min(1+p_{c}^{\prime},d)
.
This situation such that the operator can be defined on the neighborhood centered at the origin is called
low-energy scattering
(cf.
[
30
,
31
]
).
This kind of modified scattering operator has been treated on other types of nonlinearity in some literature (e.g.,
[
28
,
27
]
).
In terms of (
NLS
), Carles
[
6
]
proves the existence of the scattering operator, provided
\sigma(t)\equiv-\omega^{2}
(
\omega>0
) and
F(u)=\eta|u|^{2\sigma}u
,
\sigma>0
when
d=1,2
and
0<\sigma<2/(d-2)
when
d\geq 3
.

Now we back to the case
\sigma(t)\neq 0
. Under the existence of
\sigma(t)|x|^{2}
, the dispersive effect for free solution ((
NLS
) with
F\equiv 0
) becomes weaker than the case of
\sigma(t)\equiv 0
and a threshold in the sense of scattering is changed from
p_{c}^{\prime}
to
p_{c}=2/(d(1-\lambda))
, (see below). Hence the construction of the scattering operator for (
NLS
) is equivalent to that of the modified scattering operator.
Kawamoto and Muramatsu
[
20
]
study the initial value problem for (
NLS
) with
F(u)\sim\eta|u|^{p_{c}}u
, and they succeed to construct the inverse modified wave operator
{\cal W}^{-1}_{+}
from
u_{0}\in H^{0,\alpha}
to
u_{+}\in L^{2}
.
Also, Kawamoto
[
23
]
proves that the existence of modified wave operators
{\cal W}_{-}
from
H^{0,\alpha}
to
u_{0}\in L^{2}
. Clearly, these results do NOT guarantee
\mathrm{Ran}({\cal W}_{-})\subset{\mathscr{D}}({\cal W}_{+}^{-1})
and hence to construct the modified wave operator is difficult only with employing previous results. Hence the first aim of this paper is to bridge this gap.
Moreover, a strong restriction for
\lambda
has been imposed in
[
20
]
because of a use of the time-weighted Strichartz estimate developed by
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
. Hence the second aim of this paper is to remove the restriction. The modified scattering operator for (
NLS
) has not been discussed and so we attempt to define the operator in this paper under the some smallness conditions which are associated to the low energy condition in the case of
\sigma(t)=0
.

To state the reason why
p\leq p_{c}
becomes long-range, we first let
U(t,s)
be a propagator for
H(t)
, defined by the family of unitary operators
\{U(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in{\bf R}^{2}}
on
L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
such that for all
t,

s
,
\tau\in{\bf R}
,

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}U(t,s)=H(t)U(t,s),\quad i\partial_{s}U(t,s)=-U(t,s)H(s),

\displaystyle U(t,\tau)U(\tau,s)=U(t,s),\quad U(s,s)=\mathrm{Id}_{L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})},\quad U(t,s){\mathscr{D}}(H(s))\subset{\mathscr{D}}(H(s))

hold on
{\mathscr{D}}(H(s))
, i.e.,
u_{0}(t):=U(t,0)u_{0}
solves
i\partial_{t}u_{0}(t)=H(t)u_{0}(t)
with
u(0)=u_{0}\in{\mathscr{D}}(H(0))
. We here mention that
U(t,s)
has the following factorization formula

\displaystyle U(t,0)={\cal M}\left(\frac{\zeta_{2}(t)}{\zeta_{2}^{\prime}(t)}\right){\cal D}(\zeta_{2}(t))\mathcal{F}{\cal M}\left(\frac{\zeta_{2}(t)}{\zeta_{1}(t)}\right),

(1.3)

where
{\cal M}(t)=e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{2t}}
,
\left({\cal D}(t)\phi\right)(x)=(it)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\phi(x/t)
,
\mathcal{F}
is a usual Fourier transform and
\zeta_{j}(t)
,
j=1,2
are solutions to

\displaystyle\zeta_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t)+\sigma(t)\zeta_{j}(t)=0,\quad\begin{cases}\zeta_{1}(0)=1,\\
\zeta_{1}^{\prime}(0)=0,\end{cases}\quad\begin{cases}\zeta_{2}(0)=0,\\
\zeta_{2}^{\prime}(0)=1.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Korotyaev
[
24
]
first discovered similar factorization to (
1.3
) and Kawamoto and Muramatsu
[
20
]
rewrites Korotyaev’s factorization to (
1.3
) to deal with a nonlinear scattering problem on (
NLS
) motivated by Hayashi and Namunkin
[
14
]
(see also
[
6
,
7
,
23
]
). This factorization formula leads to a kind of dispersive estimate

\displaystyle\norm{U(t,0) \phi}_{\infty}\lesssim|\zeta_{2}(t)|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\norm{ \phi}_{1}.

(1.5)

The typical example of
\sigma(t)
in our mind is

\displaystyle\sigma(t)=\begin{cases}\sigma_{0}\quad(|t|<r_{1}),\\
\sigma_{1}|t|^{-2}\quad(|t|\geq r_{1}),\end{cases}\quad r_{1},\sigma_{0}>0.

(1.6)

For this case,
\zeta_{j}(t)
,
|t|\geq r_{1}
can be written as a linear combination of
|t|^{\lambda}
and
|t|^{1-\lambda}
with
\lambda=(1-\sqrt{1-4\sigma_{1}})/2
. Namely, those solutions are

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(t)=b_{1}|t|^{1-\lambda}+b_{1}^{\prime}|t|^{\lambda},\quad\zeta_{2}(t)=b_{2}|t|^{1-\lambda}+b_{2}^{\prime}|t|^{\lambda}.

Hence the time decay estimate becomes
\|U(t,0)\phi\|_{\infty}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}(1-\lambda)}
holds whenever
b_{2}^{\prime}\neq 0
, and this decay is slower than the free case
\sigma(t)\equiv 0
. The fact changes a threshold that the asymptotics involves nonlinear effects coming from
G(u)=\eta|u|^{p}u
with
p>0
.

, where

\displaystyle u_{p}(t)={}

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{1}(t){\cal D}_{1}(t)\widehat{w}(t),\quad\widehat{w}(t)=\widehat{u_{-}}\exp\left(i\frac{\eta}{c_{+}}|\widehat{u_{-}}|^{p_{c}}\log|t|\right).

We denote a new unknown

.
Then one obtains the equation

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}v-H(t)v={}

\displaystyle F(u_{p}+v)-F(u_{p})-\{\left(i\partial_{t}-H(t)\right)u_{p}-F(u_{p})\},

which implies

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\left(U(0,t)v\right)={}

\displaystyle U(0,t)\{F(u_{p}+v)-F(u_{p})\}-\{i\partial_{t}\left(U(0,t)u_{p}\right)-U(0,t)F(u_{p})\}.

Since

is an expected asymptotic profile of

as

.
Therefore, applying (

\displaystyle v(t)=-i\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(t,s)(F(u_{p}(s)+v(s))-F(u_{p}(s)))\,ds+\mathcal{E}(t),

where
\mathcal{E}(t)=\mathcal{A}(t)+\mathcal{E}_{r}(t)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\cal A}(t)={}&-i\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(t,0){\cal M}_{2}(s)^{-1}{\cal M}_{+}^{-1}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{c_{+}|s|}{|\zeta_{2}(s)|^{1/(1-\lambda)}}-1\right)F(\widehat{w}(s))\,\frac{ds}{c_{+}|s|},\\[5.0pt]

R(t)={}&{\cal M}_{1}(t){\cal D}_{1}(t)\left(\mathcal{F}{\cal M}_{2}(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}-1\right).\end{aligned}

(1.17)

Note that we give the derivation of (

) in Appendix

Conversely, if we find a solution to (

is the solution to (

solves

\displaystyle u(t)=u_{p}(t)+i\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(t,s)(F(u(s))-F(u_{p}(s)))\,ds+\mathcal{E}(t).

(1.18)

Hence we attempt to find a unique solution to (

), based on the contraction mapping argument in the complete metric space

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X_{b,R}={}&\left\{\phi\in C\left((-\infty,-r_{0}];L^{2}({\bf R}^{d})\right)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\left\|\phi\right\|_{X_{b}}\leq R\right\},\\
\norm{\phi}_{X_{b}}={}&\sup_{t\leq-r_{0}}\left(|t|^{\frac{\beta}{2}(1-2\lambda)+b}\norm{\phi(t)}_{L^{2}({\bf R}^{d})}+|t|^{b}\norm{U(0,t) \phi(t)}_{H^{0,\beta}({\bf R}^{d})}\right),\\
d(\phi,\psi)={}&\norm{\phi- \psi}_{L^{\infty}(-\infty,-r_{0};L^{2}({\bf R}^{d}))}\end{aligned}

for some

, where

is small enough. If

is small enough and if the external term

with

.

The proof of Theorem

that

increase in time of the small order depending on the size of

.
This kind of technique is originally developed by
[

]
.
In

]
,

is required to control

on

To avoid a use of the condition, we construct the solution on

They also employ (

) instead of (

), because the factor

]
.
Hence, for the reader’s convenience, we give a sketch of the proof of Theorem

]
.

The novelty of the paper is to find the final data

in

with

.
To state our technique, we only handle
d=2
,
3
.
Following
[
14
]
, from (

), we derive

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}(\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,t)u)={}

\displaystyle\eta|\zeta_{2}(t)|^{-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}}|\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,t)u|^{p_{c}}\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,t)u+o(t^{-1}).

Roughly speaking, the first term in the above right-hand side is
\mathcal{O}(t^{-1})
, that is, it is non-integrable with respect to time on
[r_{0},\infty)
.
Hence we introduce a phase modification

\displaystyle v_{p}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathcal{P}(t)\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,t)u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(t)\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\exp\left(i\eta\int_{r_{0}}^{t}\left(|\tau|^{-\chi}+|\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,\tau)u(\tau)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p_{c}}{2}}|\zeta_{2}(\tau)|^{-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}}\,d\tau\right)

for some
\chi>0
.
By the modification, we obtain

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}(\mathcal{F}v_{p})={}

\displaystyle\eta|\zeta_{2}(t)|^{-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}}\mathcal{P}(t)\left(|\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,t)u(t)|^{p_{c}}-\left(|t|^{-\chi}+|\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,t)u(t)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p_{c}}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle{}\times\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}_{+}U(0,t)u(t)+o(t^{-1}).

This implies that there exists
u_{+}\in H^{0,\delta}
such that
v_{p}\rightarrow u_{+}
in
H^{0,\delta}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
Owing to the lack of differentiability of the nonlinearity, the argument in
[
14
]
is not directly applicable, since we need to find the final data in
H^{0,\delta}
.
To overcome the difficulty, we take the above phase modification with the factor
|\tau|^{-\chi}
, which is firstly used by Ginibre-Ozawa
[
12
]
.
The key of the proof is to see that the first term of the above right-hand side, which comes from the modification, is negligible for large time.
To deal with this issue, we develop fundamental inequalities necessary to extract the time decay of the first term.

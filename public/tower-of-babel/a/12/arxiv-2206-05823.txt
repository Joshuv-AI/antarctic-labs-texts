Abstract
. We define a family of arithmetic zero cycles in the arithmetic Chow group of a modular curve X 0 ​ ( N ) X_{0}(N) , for N > 3 N>3 odd and squarefree, and identify the arithmetic degrees of these cycles as q q -coefficients of the central derivative of a Siegel Eisenstein series of genus two. This parallels work of Kudla-Rapoport-Yang for Shimura curves.

1.
Introduction

In a series of work culminating in the book
[
KRY06
]
, Kudla, Rapoport and the third named author studied certain families of arithmetic “special” cycles that live on arithmetic models of Shimura curves. Among their results are
arithmetic Siegel-Weil formulas
in genus one and two, which identify generating series of heights of arithmetic cycles with derivatives of Eisenstein series. The results of
[
KRY06
]
comprise the most fully-developed example in Kudla’s program, which seeks to establish systematic relations between arithmetic cycles on Shimura varieties and automorphic forms; while a substantial body of work has arisen in support of these conjectures, the case of modular curves has been largely overlooked.

In this note, we fill this gap in the literature and prove the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula for arithmetic zero cycles on
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
for odd, squarefree
N
with
N>3
. More precisely, we construct a family of arithmetic zero cycles
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,v)
, viewed in the arithmetic Chow group
\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))
. Our main result, explicated in more detail in
Section

2.5
and
Section

2.6
below, is the identity

(1)

\sum_{T\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\widehat{\mathrm{deg}}\,\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,v)\,q^{T}\ =\ C\cdot E^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}});

here

•

\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{2}
, the Siegel upper half space of genus 2 and
v=\mathrm{Im}(\tau)
;

•

q^{T}=e^{2\pi i\mathrm{tr}(\tau T)}
;

•

C=\frac{1}{24}\prod_{p|N}(p+1)
;

•

\widehat{\mathrm{deg}}
is the arithmetic degree; and

•

E^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}})
is the derivative of the Siegel Eisenstein series, evaluated at the centre of symmetry
s=0
, associated to the quadratic lattice
\mathcal{L}
given by

(2)

\mathcal{L}=\left\{x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b/N\\
c&-a\end{pmatrix}:\,a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},\quad Q_{\mathcal{L}}(x)=Ndet(x)=-Na^{2}-bc.

Our proof of the main result, which proceeds by matching the
q
-coefficients term-by-term, is closely modelled on the arguments in
[
KRY06
]
. For positive definite
T
, it turns out that the cycle
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,v)
is concentrated in a special fibre at a prime
p
, and the geometric side of the theorem factors as the product of a local intersection number, determined by Gross and Keating
[
GK93
]
, and a point count; we then relate this computation to the corresponding coefficient of the Eisenstein series using explicit formulas due to the third named author. An important step is to prove a local arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula (
Proposition

3.3
), which identifies the local intersection number with the central derivative of a local Whittaker function. For non-degenerate
T
of signature
(1,1)
or
(0,2)
, the special cycles are purely archimedean, and the result is essentially a special case of
[
GS19
, Theorem 5.1]
. Finally, for degenerate
T
’s, the identity can be reduced to the genus one case of the Siegel-Weil formula, as considered in
[
DY19
]
.

Finally, we note that there is another quadratic lattice
L
that is naturally associated to
X_{0}(N)
, which is given by

(3)

L=\left\{x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
Nc&-a\end{pmatrix}:\,a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},\quad Q_{L}(x)=det(x)=-a^{2}-Nbc.

The precise relation between
E^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}})
and
E^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{L})
is given by
Proposition

5.3
and (
325
). For the purposes of the computations in the present paper, it seems that the lattice
\mathcal{L}
is more convenient.

2.1.
Modular curves

Throughout, we fix

odd and squarefree. Let

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)

\varphi\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}

where
\mathcal{E}

For later use, we recall the following description of the complex points

where

The group

by

, which descends to an action on

Moreover, we have a
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

(10)

\mathbb{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{D}^{+},\qquad\tau\mapsto\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\,z(\tau)

where

(11)

z(\tau)=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&-\tau^{2}\\
1&-\tau\end{pmatrix}.

This construction gives rise to identifications

(12)

\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{C})\simeq[\Gamma_{0}(N)\big\backslash\mathbb{H}]\simeq[\Gamma_{0}(N)\big\backslash\mathbb{D}^{+}].

Note here we are viewing the quotients as orbifolds.

2.2.
Special cycles

We begin by recalling the construction of special cycles, following
[
DY19
]
or
[
BY09
]
.
For a point
(\varphi\colon E\to E^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)(S)
, for some base scheme
S
, let

(13)

\End(\varphi)=\{x\in\End(E)\ |\ \varphi\circ x\circ\varphi^{-1}\in\End(E^{\prime})\}

and define

(14)

L_{\varphi}\ :=\ \left\{\alpha\in\End(\varphi)\ |\ \alpha+\alpha^{\dagger}=0\right\},

where
\alpha^{\dagger}
is the Rosati dual. We may equip
L_{\varphi}
with the positive definite quadratic form

(15)

Q_{\varphi}(\alpha)=\deg(\alpha)=-\alpha^{2}.

Definition 2.1
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathcal{Z}(m)
denote the moduli stack whose
S
points, for a base scheme
S
, are given by

(16)

\mathcal{Z}(m)(S):=\left\{(\varphi\colon E\to E^{\prime},\alpha)\right\}

where
\alpha\in L_{\varphi}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

Q_{\varphi}(\alpha)=mN
;
and

(b)

the quasi-isogeny
\alpha\circ\varphi^{-1}
is in fact an isogeny from
E^{\prime}
to
E
.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

More generally, in
[
DY19
,
BY09
]
one finds a definition for cycles
\mathcal{Z}(m,\mu_{r})
parametrized by
m\in\frac{1}{4N}\mathbb{Z}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
r^{2}\equiv-4Nm\pmod{4N}
; for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the moduli problem for
\mathcal{Z}(m)
described above coincides with
\mathcal{Z}(m,\mu_{0})
in the notation of loc. cit.

(ii)

If
\alpha\in L_{\varphi}
satisfies condition (b) above, then
Q_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is divisible by
N
. Moreover, if one omits this condition, the resulting moduli problem does not define a divisor on
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
.

There is a natural forgetful map
\mathcal{Z}(m)\to\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
, which allows us to view
\mathcal{Z}(m)
as a cycle on
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
(which, abusing notation, we denote by the same symbol).

Lemma 2.3
.

The cycle
\mathcal{Z}(m)
is a divisor on
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
. Moreover, it is equal to the flat closure of its generic fibre
Z(m)=\mathcal{Z}(m)_{/\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
\mathcal{Z}(m)
is a divisor. This is clear on the generic fibre, so it will suffice to verify this claim in an étale neighbourhood of a point in characteristic
p
. To this end, let
z\in\mathcal{Z}(m)(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, lying above the point
x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, and let
R_{z}
(resp.
R_{x}
) denote the complete étale local rings of
z
and
x
respectively.
Thus we have a surjection

(17)

R_{x}\to R_{z}=R_{x}/J

for some ideal
J
that we would like to show is principal. By Nakayama’s lemma, it will suffice to show that
I=J/mJ
is principal, where
m
is the maximal ideal of
R_{x}
. Note that
I^{2}=0
, so that the surjection

(18)

A:=R_{x}/mJ\to R_{z}

is a square-zero infinitesimal extension.

Let
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
denote the universal diagram over
R_{x}
, and
\widetilde{\varphi}_{A}\colon\mathcal{E}_{A}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A}
(resp.
\widetilde{\varphi}_{z}\colon\mathcal{E}_{R_{z}}\to\mathcal{E}_{R_{z}}^{\prime}
) its base change to
A
(resp.
R_{z}
).

By assumption, we have an action
\alpha_{z}\in\End(\widetilde{\varphi}_{z})
such that

(19)

\delta:=\alpha_{z}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}_{z}^{-1}

is an isogeny
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{R_{z}}\to\mathcal{E}_{R_{z}}
. Moreover, the ideal
I\subset A
is, by definition, the largest ideal such that
\delta
deforms to a homomorphism in
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A/I},\mathcal{E}_{A/I})
.

Now we apply Grothendieck-Messing theory. Consider the Hodge exact sequences

(20)

0\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A})\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A})\to\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A})\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0\to\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\to\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\to 0

for
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A}
and
\mathcal{E}_{A}
respectively. Since the map
A\to R_{z}
is a square-zero thickening, the homomorphism
\delta\colon\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{R_{z}}\to\mathcal{E}_{R_{z}}
induces a canonical
A
-linear map

(21)

H^{1}_{dR}(\mathcal{E}_{A}^{\prime})\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathcal{E}_{A});

composing this with the maps in the Hodge exact sequences above, we obtain a map

(22)

\widehat{\delta}\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A})\to\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{E}_{A}).

Then Grothendieck-Messing theory implies that for any intermediate ring
B
with

(23)

A\to B\to R_{z}

the homomorphism
\delta
lifts to an element of
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{B},\mathcal{E}_{B})
if and only if
\widehat{\delta}\otimes B=0
. This immediately implies that the ideal
I
is given by the vanishing locus of
\widehat{\delta}
, and since
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{A})
and
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{E}_{A})
are free
A
-modules of rank 1, it follows that
I
is principal, as required.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{Z}(m)
is horizontal, i.e. does not contain any irreducible components in finite characteristic. Let
p
be a prime, and suppose
z\in\mathcal{Z}(m)(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
corresponds to
(\varphi\colon E\to E^{\prime},\alpha)
as before. If
p
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-mN})
, then
E
and
E^{\prime}
are necessarily ordinary; if
p
is non-split, then
E
and
E^{\prime}
are both supersingular. However, every irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
contains both ordinary and supersingular points, hence such a component cannot be contained in the support of
\mathcal{Z}(m)
.
∎

We will also require a description of the complex points
\mathcal{Z}(m)(\mathbb{C})

\mathcal{L}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b/N\\
c&-a\end{pmatrix}\Big|a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\ \subset\ \mathcal{V}

where
\mathcal{V}=M_{2}(\mathbb{Q})^{tr=0}

Now given

(25)

\mathbb{D}^{+}_{x}\ :=\ \{z\in\mathbb{D}^{+}\ |\ \langle z,x\rangle=0\}.

If

\mathbb{D}^{+}_{x}
is empty. Otherwise,
\mathbb{D}^{+}_{x}=\{z(\tau_{x})\}

Next, we turn our attention to 0-cycles. Let
\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\vee}=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b/2\\
b/2&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)|a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, suppose

T=\begin{pmatrix}t_{11}&*\\
*&t_{22}\end{pmatrix}\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\vee}

and assume for the moment that

.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}(T)
denote the moduli stack whose
S
points parametrize tuples

(31)

\mathcal{Z}(T)(S)\ :=\ \left\{(\varphi\colon E\to E^{\prime},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\right\}

where

(a)

\alpha_{i}\in L_{\varphi}
for

;

(b)

the moment matrix

To describe the geometric points of this stack, we first recall the following representability criteria. Let

be a quadratic space of dimension

. Define the character

(32)

\chi_{V,\ell}(x)=(x,(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\det V)_{\ell}=(x,-\det V)_{\ell}.

For a given choice of determinant
\det(V)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\times,2}

, distinguished by their Hasse invariants.

Now if

is non-degenerate, we have that

is represented by

if and only if

\epsilon(V)=\epsilon(T)\,\chi_{V}(\det T)

as in (

to be

(34)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Diff}(T,\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\{\ell\text{ finite prime}\ |\ T\text{ is not represented by }\mathcal{V}_{\ell}\}

\displaystyle=\{\ell\ |\ \epsilon_{\ell}(T)=-(-1,N)_{\ell}\cdot(\det(T),-N)_{\ell}\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
T
is non-degenerate.

(1)

If
T
is not positive definite, or if
T>0
and
\#\mathrm{Diff}(T,\mathcal{V})\geq 2
, then
\mathcal{Z}(T)=\emptyset
.

(2)

If
T>0
and
\mathrm{Diff}(T,\mathcal{V})=\{p\}
, then
\mathcal{Z}(T)
is supported in the supersingular locus in the special fibre
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{/\mathbb{F}_{p}}
. In particular,
\mathcal{Z}(T)\to\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
determines a zero cycle.

Proof.

Suppose we have a geometric point
z\in\mathcal{Z}(T)(\mathbb{F})
for an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
, corresponding to a tuple
\varphi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
. Let
V_{\varphi}=\End(\varphi)^{tr=0}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, equipped with the quadratic form
x\mapsto N^{-1}\deg x
. By assumption, we have that
V_{\varphi}
represents
T
. However,
V_{\varphi}
is positive definite, so if
T
is not positive definite, we obtain a contradiction; thus
\mathcal{Z}(T)=\emptyset
if
T
is not positive definite.

Assuming that
T>0
and continuing, we note that
\dim V_{\varphi}\geq 3
, so the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is non-zero and

are supersingular elliptic curves. Setting
p=\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F})
, we may identify

, where
B
is the rational quaternion algebra ramified at
p
and
\infty
, equipped with the quadratic form
N^{-1}\mathrm{nm}
, where
\mathrm{nm}(x)
is the reduced norm. It follows that for

, the space
V_{\varphi,\ell}
is isometric to
\mathcal{V}_{\ell}
, and so
\mathrm{Diff}(T,\mathcal{V})\subset\{p\}
.

Finally, note that
\det(V_{\varphi,p})=\det(\mathcal{V}_{p})
and
\epsilon_{p}(V_{\varphi,p})=-\epsilon_{p}(\mathcal{V}_{p})
. Hence, applying the representability criterion (

), we conclude that
p\in\mathrm{Diff}(T,\mathcal{V})
. As we have already seen that any geometric point of
\mathcal{Z}(T)
is in the supersingular locus, we have concluded the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.3.
Classes in arithmetic Chow groups

Let

(36)

\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{2}\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X})

denote the arithmetic Chow ring, as originally constructed by Gillet and Soulé, see e.g.
[
Sou92
]
. A discussion extending the construction to the case of Deligne-Mumford stacks of dimension two can be found in
[
KRY06
, §2]
.

Roughly, a class in
\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X})
is represented by an
arithmetic cycle

(\mathcal{Z},g)
, where
\mathcal{Z}
is a codimension
n
cycle on
\mathcal{X}
, with
\mathbb{R}
-coefficients, and
g_{Z}
is a Green current for
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e.
g_{Z}
is a current on
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})
of degree
(n-1,n-1)
for which there exists a smooth form
\omega
such that

(37)

\mathrm{dd^{c}}g_{Z}+\delta_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{C})}=[\omega],

holds; here
[\omega]
is the current defined by integration against
\omega
. The rational arithmetic cycles are those of the form
\widehat{\mathrm{div}}(f)=(\mathrm{div}f,i_{*}[-\log|f|^{2}])
, where
f\in\mathbb{Q}(W)^{\times}
for a codimension
n-1
integral subscheme
\iota\colon W\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
, together with classes of the form
(0,\partial\eta+\overline{\partial}\eta^{\prime})
; by definition, the arithmetic Chow group
\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X})
is the quotient of the space of arithmetic cycles by the
\mathbb{R}
-subspace spanned by the rational cycles.

Note that if
n=2
, for dimension reasons
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{C})=0
and Green’s equation (
37
) is devoid of content; in particular, there is nothing linking
\mathcal{Z}
and
g
in this case.

We will also require a generalization of these Chow groups due to Kühn and Burgos-Kramer-Kühn. For a cusp
P
of
X_{0}(N)
, let
\mathcal{P}
denote its flat closure in
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
, and let
\mathcal{S}=\sum\mathcal{P}
denote the cuspidal divisor. In
[
BGKK07
]
, the authors present an abstract framework that allows for more general kinds of Green objects in the the theory of arithmetic Chow groups. One of the examples they present utilizes “pre-log-log forms” relative to a fixed normal crossing divisor. In our case, we take this fixed divisor to be the cuspidal divisor
\mathcal{S}
, and denote by

(38)

\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}})=\bigoplus_{n}\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}})

the corresponding Chow ring. In particular, there is a natural map

(39)

\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\to\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{n}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}}),

an intersection product

(40)

\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}})\times\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}})\to\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}})\qquad(\widehat{Z}_{1},\widehat{Z}_{2})\mapsto\widehat{Z}_{1}\cdot\widehat{Z}_{2},

and a degree map

(41)

\widehat{\mathrm{deg}}\colon\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}})\to\mathbb{R}.

For convenience, we will often abbreviate

(42)

\langle\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{1},\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{2}\rangle=\widehat{\mathrm{deg}}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\cdot\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{2}\right),\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{1},\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{2}\in\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pre}})

for the intersection pairing, which recovers the intersection pairing defined in
[

We will not require precise formulas for any of the aforementioned structures; the interested reader may consult

Remark 2.7
.

Strictly speaking, the constructions in
[

]
and
[

Here we recall the special divisors defined in

Special cycles in codimension two

In this section, we will attach an arithmetic cycle
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,v)
for every
T\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and

We begin with the case that

Lemma

is non-zero, it is supported in the fibre

for a prime

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,v)\ :=\ (\mathcal{Z}(T),0)\ \in\ \widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X});

note that this class is independent of

Next, suppose
T
is non-degenerate and of signature
(1,1)
or
(0,2)
, so that
\mathcal{Z}(T)=\emptyset
. In this case, we define a purely archimedean class via the construction of
[

]

Suppose
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus 2}
is a linearly independent pair of vectors, and consider the Schwartz form

(58)

\nu(x)\in S(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\otimes A^{1,1}(\mathbb{H})

defined in
[
GS19
]
. Explicitly, we have

(59)

\nu(x)\ :=\ \nu^{o}(x)e^{-2\pi(Q(x_{1})+Q(x_{2}))}

where

(60)

\nu^{o}(x)=\psi^{o}(x)\,d\mu

with

(61)

\psi^{o}(x,\tau)=\left(-\frac{1}{\pi}+2\sum_{i=1}^{2}(R(x_{i},\tau)+2Q(x_{i}))\right)e^{-2\pi(R(x_{1},\tau)+R(x_{2},\tau))},\qquad x=(x_{1},x_{2})

and

(62)

d\mu=\frac{du\wedge dv}{v^{2}}

for
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}
; here we are abusing notation and writing
R(x,\tau)=R(x,[z_{\tau}])
under the identification
\mathbb{H}^{\pm}\simeq\mathbb{D}
as in (
10
).

Next, we set

(63)

\Psi(x,\tau)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi^{o}(\sqrt{t}x,\tau)\,\frac{dt}{t}.

If the components of
x
span a space of signature
(1,1)
or
(0,2)
, then
\Psi(x,\cdot)
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{H}
.

Finally, for
T\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
of signature
(1,1)
or
(0,2)
, and

, we choose

with

and set

(64)

\Psi(T,v,\tau)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{L}^{2}\\
T(x)=T\end{subarray}}\Psi(xa,\tau)

and

(65)

\Xi(T,v):=\Psi(T,v,\tau)d\mu(\tau).

The sum converges absolutely to a
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-invariant function on
\mathbb{H}
, and
\Xi(T,v)
defines a smooth form on
Y_{0}(N)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

The form
\Xi(T,v)
is absolutely integrable on
X_{0}(N)
, i.e.

\int_{X_{0}(N)}|\Psi(T,v,\tau)|d\mu(\tau)<\infty.

Proof.

We first claim that it suffices to prove that for any
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega(T)
, the form
\Psi(xa,\tau)d\mu(\tau)
is absolutely integrable on
\mathbb{H}
; indeed, if this is the case, we would have

(66)

\int_{X_{0}(N)}\Psi(T,v,\tau)d\mu(\tau)=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in L^{2}\\
T(x)=T\end{subarray}}\Psi(xa,\tau)\right)d\mu(\tau)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Omega(T)\\
\text{mod}\ \Gamma_{0}(N)\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\Psi(xa,\tau)\,d\mu(\tau),

where we use the fact that the stabilizer of
x
in
\Gamma_{0}(N)
is
\{\pm 1\}
, which acts trivially on
\mathbb{H}
; since there are finitely many
\Gamma_{0}(N)
orbits, the lemma would follow by Fubini.

To show the integrability of
\Psi(xa,\tau)
, fix a matrix
k\in SO(2)
such that

(67)

xak=(x_{1},x_{2})ak=(y_{1},y_{2})

for a tuple
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in V_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that

(68)

T(y)=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{1}&\\
&\delta_{2}\end{pmatrix},

with
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
and
\delta_{1}<0
. It follows from
[
GS19
]
that
\Psi(xa,\tau)=\Psi(y,\tau)
. Furthermore, we have

(69)

\Psi(\gamma y,\tau)=\Psi(y,\gamma\cdot\tau)

for
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, acting diagonally on
V_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Hence, in the integral over
\mathbb{H}
, we may act by an appropriate choice of
\gamma
and assume without loss of generality that

(70)

y_{1}=|\delta_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad y_{2}=|\delta_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
\pm 1&\end{pmatrix}

where the sign in the matrix defining
y_{2}
is
-
if
T
is of signature
(1,1)
, and
+
if
T
is of signature
(0,2)
.

First, suppose
T
has signature
(0,2)
.
In this case, we have

(71)

R_{1}\ :=\ R(y_{1},\tau)=\frac{|\delta_{1}|}{4v^{2}}\cdot|\tau|^{2},\qquad R_{2}:=R(y_{2},\tau)=\frac{|\delta_{2}|}{4v^{2}}\cdot|1-\tau^{2}|^{2}.

Setting
c=\frac{\pi}{2}\min(|\delta_{1}|,|\delta_{2}|)
and writing
\tau=u+iv
, we then have

\displaystyle 2\pi(R_{1}+R_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{v^{2}}\left(|\tau|^{2}+|1-\tau^{2}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{v^{2}}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}+(1-u^{2})^{2}+2v^{2}(1+u^{2})+v^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{v^{2}}\left(v^{2}+(1/2-u^{2})^{2}+3/4+2v^{2}(1+u^{2})+v^{4}\right)

\displaystyle>c\left(3/4\cdot v^{-2}+v^{2}+2u^{2}\right).

This estimate easily implies that the integrals

(72)

\int_{\mathbb{H}}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi t(R_{1}+R_{2})}\frac{dt}{t}d\mu(\tau)<\int_{\mathbb{H}}\frac{1}{2\pi(R_{1}+R_{2})}e^{-2\pi(R_{1}+R_{2})}d\mu(\tau)

and

(73)

\int_{\mathbb{H}}\int_{1}^{\infty}(tR_{i})\cdot e^{-2\pi t(R_{1}+R_{2})}\frac{dt}{t}d\mu(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{H}}\frac{R_{i}}{2\pi(R_{1}+R_{2})}e^{-2\pi(R_{1}+R_{2})}d\mu(\tau)

are finite. So the integral (
66
) is absolutely convergent, which in turn implies the statement of the lemma. The case of signature
(1,1)
is analogous.
∎

The preceding lemma implies that
\Xi(T,v)
defines a current on

so in this case we obtain a class

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,v)=(0,\Xi(T,v))\in\widehat{\mathrm{CH}}{}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X}).

Next, we suppose

has rank 1.
We begin with the following simple observation:

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose
\mathrm{rank}(T)=1
. Then there exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and an element
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

(75)

T={}^{t}\gamma\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\gamma.

Moreover, the integer
t
is uniquely determined by
T
, and is invariant upon replacing

by
{}^{t}\sigma T\sigma
with
\sigma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

Proof.

For the existence, write

, so that

. If
t_{1}=0
, then we may take
\gamma=Id
and
t=t_{2}
. Otherwise, write
-m/t_{1}=a/c
where
a
and

are relatively prime, and choose

such that

; then
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
satisfies
{}^{t}\gamma T\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for some

, as required.

To show that

is uniquely determined, suppose that we have
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with

(76)

{}^{t}\gamma\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

Then
\gamma
is of the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, hence

, which in turn implies that

.
∎

]

2.6.
The main theorem

We begin by briefly reviewing the theory of Siegel Eisenstein series, mostly to fix notation; for details, see
[
KRY06
, §8]
, for example. Let

(82)

\Sp_{r}=\left\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2r}\ |\ {}^{t}g\begin{pmatrix}&1_{r}\\
-1_{r}&\end{pmatrix}g=\begin{pmatrix}&1_{r}\\
-1_{r}&\end{pmatrix}\right\}

viewed as an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.

For a place
v\leq\infty
, let
G_{r,v}
denote the metaplectic cover of
\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})
; as a set, we have
G_{r,v}=\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})\times\mathbb{C}^{1}
with multiplication given by the normalized Leray cocycle (see

KRY06

).
Similarly,
G_{r,\mathbb{A}}=\Mp_{r}(\mathbb{A})
denote the metaplectic cover of
\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{A})
, which can be realized as a restricted direct product of the

. There is a unique splitting
\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{Q})\to G_{r,\mathbb{A}}
whose image we denote by
G_{r,\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
P_{r}=M_{r}N_{r}
denote the standard Siegel parabolic of
\Sp_{r}
; here

(83)

M_{r}=\left\{m(a)=\begin{pmatrix}a&\\
&{}^{t}a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\ \Big|\ a\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N_{r}=\left\{n(b)=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\ \Big|\ b\in\Sym_{r}\right\}.

We denote by
\tilde{P}_{r,\mathbb{A}},\tilde{M}_{r,\mathbb{A}}
and
\tilde{N}_{r,\mathbb{A}}
, the inverse images of
P_{r}(\mathbb{A})
,
M_{r}(\mathbb{A})
, and
N_{r}(\mathbb{A})
under the covering map
G_{r,\mathbb{A}}\to\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{A})
.

Given the quadratic space
\mathcal{V}
as in (
6
), we may define a character
\chi_{\mathcal{V}}
on
\tilde{P}_{\mathbb{A}}
by the formula

(84)

\chi_{\mathcal{V}}\left([m(a)n(b),\epsilon]\right))=\epsilon\left(\det(a),-\det\mathcal{V}\right)_{\mathbb{A}}=\epsilon\left(\det a,-N\right)_{\mathbb{A}},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathbb{A}}
is the global Hilbert symbol. Similarly, for
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we define a character
|\cdot|^{s}
on
\widetilde{P}_{\mathbb{A}}
by

(85)

\left|p\right|^{s}=|\det(a)|^{s}_{\mathbb{A}},\qquad p=[m(a)n(b),\epsilon].

Consider the degenerate principal series representation

(86)

I_{r}(s,\chi_{\mathcal{V}}):=\{\Phi\colon G_{r,\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}\text{ smooth}\ |\ \Phi(pg,s)=\chi_{\mathcal{V}}(p)|p|^{s+\rho}\Phi(g,s)\text{ for all }g\in G_{r,\mathbb{A}},p\in\tilde{P}_{r,\mathbb{A}}\},

with
G_{r,\mathbb{A}}
acting by right translation; here
\rho=\frac{r+1}{2}
.
This representation factors as a restriced direct product

(87)

I_{r}(s,\chi_{\mathcal{V}})={\bigotimes_{v\leq\infty}}^{\prime}I_{r,v}(s,\chi_{\mathcal{V},v}).

where

I_{r,v}(s,\chi_{\mathcal{V},v}):=\{\Phi\colon G_{r,v}\to\mathbb{C}\text{ smooth}\ |\ \Phi(pg,s)=\chi_{\mathcal{V},v}(p)|p|_{v}^{s+\rho}\Phi(g,s)\text{ for all }g\in G_{r,v},p\in\tilde{P}_{r,v}\},

is the local analogue of
I_{r}(s,\chi_{\mathcal{V}})
.

For each finite prime
p
, let
K_{p}=\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and let
\tilde{K}_{p}
denote its inverse image in
G_{r,p}
. Similarly, let
K_{\infty}\simeq U(r)
denote the standard maximal compact subgroup of
\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{R})
, let
\tilde{K}_{\infty}
denote its inverse image in

, and set

We say that a section

(resp.

) is

(resp.

.

In this paper, we will primarily be concerned with following standard sections for

.

For

\displaystyle q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{W^{\prime}_{t,q}(e,\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{1,q})}{c_{q}(\mathcal{V})}=\frac{W^{\prime}_{Nt,q}(e,\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{L}_{1,q})}{c_{q}(V)}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{q+1}{2(q-1)}\frac{W_{Nt,q}(e,\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{L}_{1,q})}{c_{q}(V)}-\frac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\frac{W_{Nt,q}(e,\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{(q)}_{1,q})}{c_{q}(V^{(q)})}-\frac{2}{q^{2}-1}\frac{W_{Nt,q}(e,\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{sp,(q)}_{1,q})}{c_{q}(V^{sp,(q)})}\right)\log q

(iv)

Let
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}
and
g_{\tau,\infty}=[n(u)m(\sqrt{v}),1]\in G_{1,\infty}
denote the corresponding group element. Then for any
\Phi\in I_{1,\infty}(s,\chi^{\prime}_{\infty})
, we have

W_{t,\infty}(g_{N\tau,\infty},s,\Phi)=N^{-\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}W_{Nt,\infty}(g_{\tau,\infty},s,\Phi).

(v)

Globally,

\displaystyle N^{-3/4}W_{t}^{\prime}(g_{N\tau},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{1})

\displaystyle=W_{Nt}^{\prime}(g_{\tau},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{L}_{1})+\left(\sum_{\ell|N}\frac{\log\ell}{\ell-1}\right)W_{Nt}(g_{\tau},\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{L}_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell|N}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{2(\ell+1)}W_{Nt}(g_{\tau},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{(\ell)}_{1})-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}-1}W_{Nt}(g_{\tau},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{sp,(\ell)}_{1})\right)\log\ell

Proof.

First, suppose that
q\nmid N
. The representation density
\alpha_{q}(X,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})
was computed previously, cf. (
207
). On the other hand, we have that for any
\ell|N
, the quadratic spaces
L_{q}
and
L^{(\ell)}_{q}
are both isometric to
(M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{q})^{tr=0},\det)
. Applying
[
Yan98
, Proposition 8.3]
, a short computation reveals

(290)

\alpha_{q}(X,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})=\alpha_{q}(X,Nt,L_{q})=\alpha_{q}(X,Nt,L^{(\ell)}_{q}).

Finally, applying the identity (
199
), which relates the representation densities to Whittaker functionals, yields
(i)
.

When
q|N
, the representation density
\alpha_{q}(X,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})
can be computed explicitly using
[
Yan98
, Theorem 3.1]
; the result is recorded in equations (
209
) and (
210
) above. Comparing these formulas with the explicit formulas in Corollary 8.2 and Proposition 8.3 of
[
Yan98
]
, we find

(291)

\alpha_{q}(X,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})=1-X^{-1}+X^{-1}\alpha_{q}(X,Nt,L_{q})

Evaluating at
X=1
gives

(292)

\alpha_{q}(1,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})=\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q})=\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q}^{(\ell)})

for
\ell\neq q
, and hence, applying (
199
) again gives part
(ii)
of the proposition.

To prove
(iii)
we differentiate (
291
) and evaluate at
X=1
, which yields

(293)

\alpha^{\prime}_{q}(1,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})=\alpha^{\prime}_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q})-\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q})+1.

By
[
Yan98
, Corollary 8.2]
, we have
\alpha_{q}(X,Nt,L_{q})+\alpha_{q}(X,Nt,L_{q}^{(q)})=2
; substituting this identity above, we conclude that

(294)

\alpha^{\prime}_{q}(1,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})=\alpha^{\prime}_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q})-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q})+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L^{(q)}_{q}).

On the other hand, the same corollary implies that

(295)

\frac{2q}{q^{2}-1}\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L^{0}_{q})-\frac{\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q})}{q-1}-\frac{\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q}^{(q)})}{q+1}=0.

Thus

(296)

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{q}(1,t,\mathcal{L}_{q})-\alpha^{\prime}_{q}(1,Nt,L)

\displaystyle=-\frac{q+1}{2(q-1)}\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L_{q})+\frac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L^{(q)}_{q})

(297)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{2q}{q^{2}-1}\alpha_{q}(1,Nt,L^{0}_{q}).

Another appeal to (
199
) gives part
(iii)
of the proposition.

Part
(iv)
follows in the same way as the proof of
Proposition

5.1
(iv).

Finally, we observe that for any quadratic space
W
, the constants
c_{q}(W)
satisfy the product formula
\prod_{q\leq\infty}c_{q}(W)=1
. Combining this observation with the preceding parts of the proposition, a short computation yields

\displaystyle N^{-\frac{3}{2}}W_{t}^{\prime}(g_{\tau},\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=\left(W_{Nt}^{\prime}(g_{\tau},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{L}_{1})+\left(\sum_{\ell|N}\frac{\log\ell}{\ell-1}\right)W_{Nt}(g_{\tau},\frac{1}{2},\Phi^{L}_{1})\right)\frac{c_{\infty}(V)}{c_{\infty}(\mathcal{V})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell|N}\left(-\frac{\ell-1}{2(\ell+1)}W_{Nt}(g_{\tau},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{(\ell)}_{1})\frac{c_{\infty}(V^{(\ell)})}{c_{\infty}(\mathcal{V})}\right)\log\ell

(298)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}-1}W_{Nt}(g_{\tau},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{sp,(\ell)}_{1})\frac{c_{\infty}(V^{sp,\ell})}{c_{\infty}(\mathcal{V})}\right)\log\ell

Since
\mathcal{V}_{\infty}\simeq V_{\infty}\simeq V^{sp,(\ell)}_{\infty}
, we have
c_{\infty}(\mathcal{V})=c_{\infty}(V)c_{\infty}(V^{sp,(\ell)}).

Moreover, we have

(299)

\chi_{\mathcal{V},\infty}(-1)=(-1,-1)_{\infty}=-1=\chi_{\mathcal{V}^{(\ell)},\infty}(-1)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\gamma_{\infty}(\mathcal{V})=-\gamma_{\infty}(V^{(\ell)})

cf.
[
KRY06
, p. 330]
. Thus
c_{\infty}(V^{\ell})=-c_{\infty}(\mathcal{V})
, and the proposition follows.

For a section
\Phi\in I_{2}(s,\chi^{\prime})
, let
E(g,s,\Phi)=\sum_{T}E_{T}(g,s,\Phi)
denote the Fourier expansion of the corresponding Eisenstein series, as in
Section

.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{2}
. Then for any
T\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\vee}
we have

(300)

N^{-\frac{3}{2}}E^{\prime}_{T}(g_{N\tau},0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{2})=E^{\prime}_{NT}(g_{\tau},0,\Phi^{L}_{2})+\sum_{\ell|N}\frac{\ell-1}{2(\ell+1)}E_{NT}(g_{\tau}0,\Phi^{(\ell)}_{2})\log\ell.

Proof.

We note that in general, if
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}_{2}
, we have

(301)

E_{T}(g_{\tau},s,\Phi)=e^{2\pi itr(Tu)}E_{T}(g_{v},s,\Phi).

Thus, we may assume that
\tau=iv
for
v\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{R})_{>0}
, and write
g_{\tau}=g_{v}
in the remainder of the proof.

Now suppose
T
is non-degenerate. Then for any section
\Phi\in I_{2}(s,\chi^{\prime})
, we have

(302)

E_{T}(g,s,\Phi)=W_{T}(g,s,\Phi),

cf.
Proposition

4.1
. On the other hand, recall that Eisenstein series
E(g,s,\Phi^{L}_{2})
and
E(g,s,\Phi^{sp,(\ell)}_{2})
are incoherent, in the sense of
[
Kud97
]
, and so they vanish at
s=0
. In particular,

(303)

W_{NT}(g_{v},0,\Phi^{L})=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad W_{NT}(g_{v},0,\Phi^{sp,(\ell)})=0.

Thus, the proposition follows from
Proposition

5.1
(v) in this case.

Next, suppose that
T
has rank 1. By
Lemma

2.10
, we may choose
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
that
T={}^{t}\gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\gamma
for some
t\neq 0
. Since

(304)

E_{T}(g,s,\Phi)=E_{{}^{t}\gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\gamma}(g,s,\Phi)=E_{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)}(m(\gamma)g,s,\Phi)

for any section
\Phi\in I_{2}(s,\chi^{\prime})
, we may assume without loss of generality that
T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\\
&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Similarly, replacing
v
with
\theta v{}^{t}\theta
for an appropriate choice of
\theta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&*\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we may further assume that

(305)

v=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}v_{1}&\\
&v_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

is diagonal.

For
r=1
or
2
, let
\Phi^{*}_{r}
denote any one of the sections
\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{r}
,
\Phi^{L}_{r}
,
\Phi^{(\ell)}_{r}
or
\Phi^{sp,(\ell)}_{r}
. Note that in all cases,
\Phi^{*}_{r,\infty}=\Phi^{\frac{3}{2}}_{r,\infty}
. In this case, combining

4.11
,
Lemma

4.12
and (

E_{T}(g_{v},s,\Phi^{*}_{2})=v_{1}^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{4}}W_{t}\left(g_{v_{2}},s+\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{*}_{1}\right)+W_{T}(g_{v},s,\Phi^{*}_{2}).

In particular,

(307)

N^{-\frac{3}{2}}E^{\prime}_{T}(g_{Nv},0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{2})=v_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\frac{\log(Nv_{1})}{2}\right)N^{-\frac{3}{4}}W_{t}(g_{Nv_{2}},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{1})+v_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{-\frac{3}{4}}W^{\prime}_{t}(g_{Nv_{2}},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{1})\\
+N^{-\frac{3}{2}}W^{\prime}_{T}(g_{v},0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}).

Applying (
306
),
Proposition

5.1
and
Proposition

5.2
, a short computation gives

\displaystyle N^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{T}(g_{Nv},0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{2})-E^{\prime}_{NT}(g_{v},0,\Phi^{L}_{2})-\sum_{\ell|N}\frac{\ell-1}{2(\ell+1)}E_{NT}(g_{v},0,\Phi^{(\ell)}_{2})\log\ell

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\ell|N}\frac{\ell+1}{2(\ell-1)}\log\ell\right)\left\{v_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}W_{Nt}(g_{v_{2}},\tfrac{1}{2},\Phi^{L}_{1})+W_{NT}(g_{v},0,\Phi^{L}_{2})\right\}

Remark 5.4
.

The preceding proposition can be phrased in more classical language, as follows, cf.
Section

2.6
. If
\Phi^{*}_{2}
is one of the sections
\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{2},\Phi^{L}_{2}
or
\Phi^{(\ell)}_{2}
, and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{2}
, we set

(321)

E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*}_{2})=\det(v)^{-\frac{3}{4}}E(g_{\tau},s,\Phi^{*}_{2}).

Let

(322)

\Gamma_{0}(4N)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Sp_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\ |\ C\equiv 0\pmod{4N}\right\}

denote the usual congruence subgroup. Then it can be verified that
E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*}_{2})
transforms like a Siegel modular form of scalar weight
3/2
and level
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
with character
\chi(\gamma)=\mathrm{sgn}\det(d)
, see
[
KRY06
, §8.5.6]
for a more precise formulation.

Let
M_{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))
denote the space of (non-holomorphic) Siegel modular forms of scalar weight
3/2
and level
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
and character
\chi
, and consider the Hecke operator

(323)

U_{N}\colon M_{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))\to M_{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))

given by

(324)

U_{N}(F)(\tau)=\sum_{u\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})}F|_{3/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&u\\
&N\end{smallmatrix}\right)=N^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{u}F(N^{-1}\tau+N^{-1}u)

Then the previous proposition can be rewritten in classical terms as the identity

(325)

E^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}_{2})=U_{N}\left(E^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{L}_{2})+\sum_{\ell|N}\frac{\ell-1}{2(\ell+1)}E(\tau,0,\Phi^{(\ell)})\log\ell\right).

A new highly nonlinear equation modelling shallow-water waves with constant vorticity
Yu Liu, Xingxing Liu, and, Min Li

Abstract
In this paper we apply the approach of formal asymptotic expansions and perturbation theory to derive a new highly nonlinear shallow-water model from the full governing equations for two dimensional incompressible fluid with constant vorticity. This approximate model is generated by introduction of a larger scaling than the Camassa-Holm one, which is shown to be optimal in the sense that there are no non-local terms appearing in free surface equation. Moreover, we establish the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem in Besov spaces, and give a blow-up criterion, which improve the previous corresponding results in Sobolev spaces. Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q53, 35G25, 35B30 Keywords : Shallow-water waves; Vorticity; Shear flow; Besov spaces; Blow-up.

Introduction

The classical water wave problems embody the Euler equations of motion, the equation of mass conservation and the dynamic and kinematic conditions on the the free surface, and for simplicity the ”no-flow” condition on the flat bottom. Due to the complexity of this coupled system of two-dimensional flow with four unknowns (the horizontal and vertical velocity components, the pressure and the free surface), many simpler models have been established as effective approximations in various specific physical regimes. In the derivation of such models, we usually use the following two fundamental dimensionless parameters: the amplitude parameter
\varepsilon=\frac{a}{h_{0}}
and the shallowness parameter
\mu=\frac{h_{0}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}},
where
h_{0}
is the mean depth of water,
a
and
\lambda
are the typical amplitude and wavelength of the waves, respectively. The parameters
\varepsilon
and
\mu
clearly represent the contributions of the amplitude and the wavelength of wave under consideration. When speaking about shallow-water waves, we always assume
\mu
to be small:
\mu\ll 1.

In the Boussinesq scaling (weakly nonlinear regime):
\mu\ll 1
,
\varepsilon=O(\mu)
,
one can establish the classical weakly nonlinear Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) and Benjamin-Bona-Mahoney (BBM) equations
[
2
,
33
]
, which are good asymptotic approximations of unidirectional solutions of the two-dimensional irrotational water waves
[
12
,
34
]
, but they exhibit behavior that is less nonlinear than dispersive. The two equations play an important role in capturing the existence of solitary water waves
[
22
,
28
]
, and all physically relevant solutions exist globally in time
[
7
,
41
]
. However, the phenomenon of wave breaking (i.e., the solution remains bounded, but its slope becomes unbounded
[
44
]
.) is ubiquitous in nature. Thus, in order to gain insight into the most interesting phenomenon of wave breaking, the models derived for the propagation of shallow-water waves require a transition to full nonlinearity.

When allowing
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
(
\mu
fixed), one obtains a set of linear water wave equations. While
\mu\rightarrow 0
(
\varepsilon
fixed), one finds the fully nonlinear shallow water equations
[
12
]
. Hence the derivation of the water wave models accommodating wave breaking phenomena amounts to considering larger values of
\varepsilon
. Indeed, the following Camassa-Holm (CH) scaling (moderately nonlinear regime):
\mu\ll 1
,
\varepsilon=O(\sqrt{\mu})
is proved to be effective. With this scaling, the CH
[
4
]
and Degasperis-Procesi (DP) equations
[
21
]
are verified as valid approximations to the full governing equations for water waves
[
14
]
. These two equations are completely integrable Hamiltonian system
[
6
,
11
]
. More importantly, the CH and DP equations have both solitary waves interacting like solitons
[
4
,
16
,
17
,
35
,
37
,
39
]
, and in contrast to the KdV and BBM equations, wave breaking solutions
[
9
,
14
,
24
,
38
]
. Recently, inspired by the idea that the larger
\varepsilon
, the stronger nonlinearity, the models with higher-order nonlinear terms have been derived with larger than the one in the CH scaling. A new highly nonlinear shallow water equation within the scaling of
\mu\ll 1
,
\varepsilon=O(\sqrt[4]{\mu})
, along with the local well-posedness and blow-up criterion were obtained in
[
40
]
. Moreover, several integrable equations with cubic nonlinearity as asymptotic models for the propagation of shallow water waves were derived under the scaling of
\mu\ll 1
,
\varepsilon=O(\sqrt[5]{{\mu}^{2}})

[
5
]
.

Note that the above obtained equations under the Boussinesq, CH or even larger scalings, are all presented with the assumption of irrotational fluid motion. However, vorticity is of great importance to incorporate the effects of currents and wave-current interactions in the flow
[
7
]
. Nonzero constant vorticity is adapted to tidal flows
[
42
]
and nonconstant vorticity is the hallmark of highly irregular currents
[
18
]
. In the last few years, the problem of water waves with nonzero vorticity and especially with constant vorticity has been given increasing attention
[
8
,
10
,
13
,
15
,
18
,
30
,
32
,
42
]
and the references therein. Thus the question as to whether these above approximate equations can be appropriate for
the sake of inclusion of an underlying vorticity is an interesting one. R. S. Johnson
[
30
]
showed the CH equation is directly relevant to water waves moving over a
linear shear flow (i.e., constant vorticity in the undisturbed state), and also presented the variation of the depth as the linear shear is varied.
D. Ionescu-Kruse
[
26
]
established the CH equation for unidirectional surface shallow water waves moving over a non-zero vorticity flow by an interplay of variational methods and small-parameter expansions.
H. Wang et al.
[
43
]
derived a generalized CH equation with the effect of underlying linear shear flow from the two-dimensional rotational fluid in the CH scaling.
For two component equations modelling shallow-water waves with constant vorticity, we refer the readers to
[
23
,
27
]
.

In this paper, following
[
30
]
, we formally present the derivation of a highly nonlinear shallow-water model by applying asymptotic expansion method to the
full governing equations for two dimensional flow with an underlying constant vorticity under a larger scaling than the CH one. So a natural question is how to choose an appropriate scaling
that is larger than the CH one and at the same time avoids considerable undertaking. Inspired by the highly nonlinear equations under the assumption of irrotational flow
[
5
,
40
]
,
the scalings
\mu\ll 1
,
\varepsilon=O(\sqrt[5]{{\mu}^{2}})
and

Nevertheless, our calculation confirms that neither of these two scalings is a desired choice. More precisely, on the one hand, within the scaling

it requires us to compute terms up to the order of

, leaving the

-order approximation,

2
Derivation of the model equation

Assume that the two dimensional fluid is incompressible and inviscid with a constant density and zero surface tension. Let
z=0
denote the location of the flat bottom and
h_{0}
be the mean depth, or the undisturbed depth of water. Suppose that
D_{t}=\{(x,z):0<z<h_{0}+\eta(t,x)\}
, where
\eta(t,x)
measures the deviation from the average level. As in
[
28
]
, the pressure of the fluid is written as
P(t,x,z)=P_{a}+\rho g(h_{0}-z)+p(t,x,z)
,
where
P_{a}
is the constant atmospheric pressure, the variable
p
measures the deviation from the hydrostatic pressure distribution,
g
is the constant
Earth’s gravity acceleration and
\rho
is the constant fluid density. The equations governing
the motion of the fluid consist of Euler equations, together with the equation of mass conservation

\displaystyle u_{x}+w_{z}=0,\ \mbox{in}\ D_{t},

and the dynamic and kinematic boundary conditions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p=\rho g\eta,&$on$\ z=h_{0}+\eta(t,x),\\
w=\eta_{t}+u\eta_{x},&$on$\ z=h_{0}+\eta(t,x),\\
w=0,&$on$\ z=0,\end{cases}

where
(u(t,x,z),w(t,x,z))
is the two-dimensional velocity field.

3
Local well-posedness

In the sequel, for notational convenience, we shall consider the following initial value problem of Eq. (

) with more general coefficients

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}+(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}u+\alpha_{3}u^{2})u_{x}=\Lambda^{-2}f(u,u_{x}),&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},\\

.

Let

is continuous from a neighborhood of

As is well-known, for every

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{-2}(\tilde{f}_{2}(t,x)\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}\leq C\|w\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\big(\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,\infty}\cap\mbox{Lip}}^{2}+\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,\infty}\cap\mbox{Lip}}\|v\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,\infty}\cap\mbox{Lip}}+\|v\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,\infty}\cap\mbox{Lip}}^{2}\big).\quad\quad

Thus, by (

(i)
, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{-C\int_{0}^{t}U(\tau)d\tau}\|w(t)\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|w_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}+C\int^{t}_{0}e^{-C\int_{0}^{\tau}U(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\|w\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{6}(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,\infty}\cap\mbox{Lip}}^{i-j}\|v\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,\infty}\cap\mbox{Lip}}^{j-1})\big)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|w_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}+C\int^{t}_{0}e^{-C\int_{0}^{\tau}U(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\|w\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}\ln\big(e+\frac{\|w\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,\infty}}}{\|w\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}}\big)\cdot V(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|w_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}+C\int^{t}_{0}e^{-C\int_{0}^{\tau}U(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\|w\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}\ln\big(e+\frac{V(\tau)}{e^{-C\int_{0}^{\tau}U(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\|w\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}}\big)\cdot V(\tau)d\tau,

where we used Proposition
5.1

(x)
and Young inequality in the third inequality. Denote

to the above inequality, and in view of (

which is the desired inequality (

).

Now it suffices to prove that the solution depends continuously on the initial data

,
i.e.
the map

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq L.

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\leq\frac{2\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}}{1-4C\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}t},\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

where
0<T\leq\frac{1}{3C\big(4\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}+\sum_{i=1,i\neq 2}^{6}2^{2i-2}\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|^{i-1}_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\big)}

\displaystyle T=\frac{1}{8C\big(4(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}+\delta)+\sum_{i=1,i\neq 2}^{6}2^{2i-2}(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}+\delta)^{i-1}\big)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L=4(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}+\delta).

Combining the a priori estimate (
3.12

3.10

\displaystyle\frac{\|\tilde{u}-u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty})}}{e}\leq e^{C(L+L^{2})T}\Big(\frac{\|\tilde{u}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}}{e}\Big)^{\exp(-C(1+2L^{5})\ln(e+(1+2L^{5}))T)},

(3.19)

provided that

\|\tilde{u}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}\leq e^{1-\exp(C(1+2L^{5})\ln(e+(1+2L^{5}))T)}.
In view of the interpolation inequality
(vi)
in Proposition
5.1

u_{0}\rightarrow u(\cdot,u_{0})

Then we show the continuity in
C([0,T];B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1})

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}^{(n)}-\tilde{f}^{(\infty)}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\leq C\big(1+\|u^{(n)}\|^{5}_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}+\|u^{(\infty)}\|^{5}_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\big)\big(\|u^{(n)}-u^{(\infty)}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}+\|u_{x}^{(n)}-u_{x}^{(\infty)}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\big).

\displaystyle\|v_{1}^{(n)}(t)\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}

4
Blow-up scenario

In this section, we will give a more precise blow-up criterion compared with our previous one given in
[
36
]
. It reads

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}
. Assume
T^{\star}
is the maximal existence time of the corresponding solution
u
to Eq. (
3.1
) guaranteed by Theorem
3.1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle T^{\star}<+\infty\Rightarrow\int_{0}^{T^{\star}}\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}d\tau=+\infty.

Proof.

Applying
\Delta_{q}
to Eq. (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}+(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}u+\alpha_{3}u^{2})\partial_{x}\big)\Delta_{q}u=[\alpha_{2}u+\alpha_{3}u^{2},\Delta_{q}]\partial_{x}u+\Delta_{q}\Lambda^{-2}f(u,u_{x}),

(4.1)

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the commutator of the operators. In view of the commutator estimates of Lemma 2.100 in
[
1
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(2^{\frac{3}{2}q}\|[\alpha_{2}u+\alpha_{3}u^{2},\Delta_{q}]\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}})_{q\geq-1}\|_{l^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|(\alpha_{2}u+\alpha_{3}u^{2})_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u-u^{2}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}})

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}.

where we used 1-
D
Moser-type estimate
(viii)
in Proposition
5.1
. Note that
\Lambda^{-2}\in Op(S^{-2})
and
\Lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\in Op(S^{-1})
, we get

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{-2}f(u,u_{x})\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\leq C(\sum_{i=1}^{6}\|u\|^{i-1}_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}.

(4.3)

Going along the lines of the proof of Lemma
5.1
, by using the estimates (
4.2
)-(
4.3
), it yields

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{6}\|u\|^{i-1}_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}d\tau.

It thus follows from Gronwall’s inequality and the embedding
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\exp\big(C\int_{0}^{t}(\sum_{i=1}^{6}\|u\|^{i-1}_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})d\tau\big)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\exp\big(C\int_{0}^{t}(\sum_{i=1}^{6}\|u\|^{i-1}_{H^{1}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})d\tau\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}\exp\big(C\int_{0}^{t}(\sum_{i=1}^{6}\|u_{0}\|^{i-1}_{H^{1}}\exp(C(i-1)\int_{0}^{\tau}\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}dt^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\exp(C\int_{0}^{\tau}\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}dt^{\prime})\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})d\tau\big),

where we used
\|u\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\exp(C\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}d\tau),\ t\in[0,T^{\star})
given in Theorem 3.2 of
[
36
]
.

Now if we assume that
\int_{0}^{T^{\star}}\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\leq M
for some
M>0,
then we have
\int_{0}^{T^{\star}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\leq M
, for finite
T^{\star}
.
Owing to (
4.4
), we find
\|u(T^{\star})\|_{B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}<+\infty,
which contradicts the fact that
T^{\star}
is the maximal existence time of the solution. This completes the proof of
Theorem
4.1
.

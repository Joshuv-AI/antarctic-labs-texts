Invariant curves of quasi-periodic reversible mappings and its application Journal: Journal of Differential Equations
Yan Zhuang, Daxiong Piao, Yanmin Niu

Abstract
We consider the existence of invariant curves of real analytic reversible mappings which are quasi-periodic in the angle variables. By the normal form theorem, we prove that under some assumptions, the original mapping is changed into its linear part via an analytic convergent transformation, so that invariants curves are obtained. In the iterative process, by solving the modified homological equations, we ensure that the transformed mapping is still reversible. As an application, we investigate the invariant curves of a class of nonlinear resonant oscillators, with the Birkhoff constants of the corresponding Poincar e ´ \acute{e} mapping all zeros or not.

). For this purpose, we need to prove for every
n
, there is a transformation
T_{n}
such that
M_{0}
is transformed into
M_{n}
,

M_{n}:\begin{cases}\xi_{1}=\xi+\gamma_{0}+f_{s_{n}}(\xi,\eta),\\
\eta_{1}=\eta+g_{s_{n}}(\xi,\eta),\end{cases}

and

|f_{s_{n}}|_{D_{n}}+|g_{s_{n}}|_{D_{n}}<d_{n}.

(4.1)

By Lemma
3.2
, the existence of transformation
T_{n}
is obtained. Thus we just check (
4.1
) for all
n
.

When
n=0
,
|f_{s_{0}}|_{D_{0}}+|g_{s_{0}}|_{D_{0}}<d=d_{0}
.
Supposing for all
n
, the nonlinear part of
M_{n}
satisfies (
4.1

|f_{s_{n+1}}|_{D_{n+1}}+|g_{s_{n+1}}|_{D_{n+1}}<d_{n+1}.

Firstly, we have to guarantee the conditions of Lemma
3.2
, i.e.,

d_{n}<\min\{\frac{t_{n}-t_{n+1}}{4},\frac{\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1}}{4}\},

\nu=c_{7}d_{n}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})^{-m-\sigma}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}(\frac{1}{t_{n}-t_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1}})<\min\{\frac{t_{n}-t_{n+1}}{4},\frac{\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1}}{4},\frac{1}{5}\},

which means that

(\frac{3}{2})^{n}d_{n}<\min\{\frac{t_{0}}{24},\frac{\rho_{0}}{24}\},

and

c_{7}d_{n}(\frac{t_{0}}{6})^{-m-\sigma}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}\Big((\frac{t_{0}}{6})^{-1}+(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}\Big)(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma+2)}<\min\{\frac{t_{0}}{24},\frac{\rho_{0}}{24},\frac{1}{5}\}.

From the definition of

d_{n}=(\frac{2}{3})^{3n+9}\Big((\frac{3}{2})^{9}d_{0}\Big)^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}
. Obviously,

and
c_{0}
, thus we can choose
\alpha
large enough such that
d_{0}
is sufficiently small, finally all above inequalities hold.
By Lemma
3.2
, we derive

\displaystyle|f_{s_{n+1}}(\xi,\eta)|_{D_{n+1}}+|g_{s_{n+1}}(\xi,\eta)|_{D_{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{6}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}(d_{n}e^{\frac{1}{2}(2s_{n}-1)(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})}+(d_{n})^{2}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})^{-m-\sigma}(\frac{1}{t_{n}-t_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1}}))}{1-\nu}.

On the one hand, we will prove the inequality

\frac{c_{6}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}d_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(2s_{n}-1)(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})}}{1-\nu}<\frac{1}{2}d_{n+1}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{c_{6}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}d_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(2s_{n}-1)(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})}}{1-\nu}

\displaystyle<\frac{5}{4}c_{6}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}(\frac{3}{2})^{n}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}(\frac{2}{3})^{n}(2s_{n}-1)}d_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{4}c_{6}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}(\frac{2}{3})^{n}(2s_{n}-1)}d_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(\frac{3}{2})^{n}d_{n}^{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\Big(c_{8}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}(\frac{2}{3})^{n}(2s_{n}-1)}d_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\Big)d_{n+1},

where
c_{8}>\frac{5}{2}c_{6}
is a positive constant,
we will only verify the term
c_{8}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}(\frac{2}{3})^{n}(2s_{n}-1)}d_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}<1
.
In fact

\displaystyle c_{8}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}(\frac{2}{3})^{n}(2s_{n}-1)}d_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle<\frac{6c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}2^{\alpha}})^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}\Big(\big((\frac{3}{2})^{9}d_{0}\big)^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}(\frac{2}{3})^{3n+9}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle<\Big((\frac{6c_{8}}{\rho_{0}})^{3}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}2^{\alpha}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}\Big((\frac{3}{2})^{9}d_{0}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}\big((\frac{3}{2})^{3n+9}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle<\Big((\frac{6c_{8}}{\rho_{0}})^{3}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}2^{\alpha}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}\Big((\frac{3}{2})^{9}d_{0}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}\big((\frac{3}{2})^{9}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}

\displaystyle=\Big((\frac{6c_{8}}{\rho_{0}})^{3}(\frac{3}{2})^{9}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}2^{\alpha}}(\frac{3}{2})^{9}d_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}

\displaystyle=\Big((\frac{6c_{8}}{\rho_{0}})^{3}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}2^{\alpha}}d_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}.

Therefore when
(\frac{6c_{8}}{\rho_{0}})^{3}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}2^{\alpha}}d_{0}}<1
,
i.e.,

\alpha>\log_{2}^{\frac{12}{\rho_{0}}\ln{\frac{216c_{9}}{\rho_{0}^{3}d_{0}}}},

(4.2)

we have

c_{8}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{6})^{-1}e^{-\frac{\rho_{0}}{12}(\frac{2}{3})^{n}(2s_{n}-1)}d_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}<1,

and

\frac{c_{6}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}d_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(2s_{n}-1)(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})}}{1-\nu}<\frac{1}{2}d_{n+1},

where
c_{9}=c_{8}^{3}
.

On the other hand, we will prove the inequality

\frac{c_{6}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}d_{n}^{2}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})^{-m-\sigma}(\frac{1}{t_{n}-t_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1}})}{1-\nu}<\frac{1}{2}d_{n+1}.

Due to

\displaystyle\frac{c_{6}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}d_{n}^{2}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})^{-m-\sigma}(\frac{1}{t_{n}-t_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1}})}{1-\nu}

\displaystyle<\frac{5}{4}c_{6}\frac{36}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma+1)}d_{n}^{\frac{2}{3}}(\frac{3}{2})^{n}d_{n}^{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{36c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma+1)}d_{n}^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big)d_{n+1},

we turn to prove

\frac{36c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma+1)}d_{n}^{\frac{2}{3}}<1.

(4.3)

The estimate of the left hand in (
4.3
) follows

\displaystyle\frac{36c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma+1)}d_{n}^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{36c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma+1)}\Big(\big((\frac{3}{2})^{9}d_{0}\big)^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}(\frac{2}{3})^{3n+9}\Big)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle<\frac{36c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma-1)-6}\big((\frac{3}{2})^{6}d_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}\big)^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}

\displaystyle<\Big(\frac{36c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{(m+\sigma-1)}(\frac{3}{2})^{6}d_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big)^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{36c_{8}}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})(\frac{3}{2})^{(m+\sigma+5)}d_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big)^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}.

Thus, by the choice of

d_{0}<[\frac{\rho_{0}}{36c_{8}}(\frac{t_{0}}{6})^{m+\sigma}(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{1}{t_{0}})^{-1}(\frac{2}{3})^{(m+\sigma+5)}]^{\frac{3}{2}},

(4.4)

(
4.3
) is established.

From the above discussion, we conclude

|f_{s_{n+1}}(\xi,\eta)|_{D_{n+1}}+|g_{s_{n+1}}(\xi,\eta)|_{D_{n+1}}<d_{n+1},

if (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) hold.
Hence we get the inequality (
4.1
), which completes the induction.

For one thing, the change of variables
T_{n}
can be written as
T_{n}=T_{0}\circ\Delta T_{1}\circ\Delta T_{2}\circ\cdots\Delta T_{n}
, then
T_{n+1}=T_{n}\circ\Delta T_{n+1}
, and the transformation
T_{n+1}
can be expressed as

T_{n+1}:\begin{cases}\theta=\xi+u_{n+1}(\xi,\eta)\\
r=\eta+v_{n+1}(\xi,\eta),\end{cases}

where

u_{n+1}=u_{0}+u_{1}+...+u_{n},\quad\quad v_{n+1}=v_{0}+v_{1}+...+v_{n}.

(4.5)

The convergence of the transformation sequence
\{T_{n+1}\}
is decided by their nonlinear parts (
4.5
).

By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle|u_{n}|_{D_{n}}+|v_{n}|_{D_{n}}<c_{2}d_{n}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})^{-m-\sigma}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{n+1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=c_{2}(\frac{6}{t_{0}})^{m+\sigma}\frac{6}{\rho_{0}}(\frac{3}{2})^{n(m+\sigma+1)}(\frac{2}{3})^{3n+9}\big((\frac{3}{2})^{9}d_{0}\big)^{(\frac{4}{3})^{n}}.

When
d_{0}<(\frac{2}{3})^{m+\sigma+7}
, there is
|u_{n}|_{D_{n}}+|v_{n}|_{D_{n}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. It follows that the sequence
\{u_{n+1}\}
and
\{v_{n+1}\}
are uniformly bounded in
D_{\infty}=\{(\xi,\eta):|\Im\xi|<\frac{t_{0}}{2},|\eta|<\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\}
. Hence, one can choose a subsequence of
\{T_{n}\}
, which converges to a transformation
T
in
D_{\infty}
.

For another, since the nonlinear parts of
M_{n}
satisfies
|f_{n}|_{D_{n}}+|g_{n}|_{D_{n}}<d_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, it implies that the mapping
M_{n}={T_{n}}^{-1}\circ M_{0}\circ T_{n}
tends to a linearized normal form (
1.5
) in

.

In conclusion, there is a convergent transformation
V\circ T
, where

is defined in Lemma
2.5
, such that the mapping (
1.1
) is reduced to the formal normal form (
1.5
).

5
Application

In this section, we will apply Theorem
1.1
to the equation

x^{\prime\prime}+\varphi(x)f(x^{\prime})+\omega^{2}x+g(x)=p(t).

(5.1)

Suppose that

(H_{1})
: functions
\varphi,f,g
and
p
are real analytic in
x,x^{\prime}
and
t
;

(H_{2})
:
f
and
p
are even functions,
p(t+2\pi)=p(t)
;

(H_{3})
:
\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}\varphi(x)=:\varphi(\pm\infty)\in\mathbb{R},\quad\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}x^{4}\varphi^{(4)}(x)=0;

(H_{4})
:
\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}f(x)=:f(+\infty)\in\mathbb{R},\quad\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}x^{4}f^{(4)}(x)=0;

(H_{5})
:
\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}g(x)=:g(\pm\infty)\in\mathbb{R},\quad\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}x^{4}g^{(4)}(x)=0.

Theorem 5.1

Suppose that
(H_{1})-(H_{5})
hold, and

\omega
satisfies

|k\omega^{-1}-l|\geq\frac{c_{0}}{|k|^{\sigma}},

where
c_{0}>0,\sigma>0,k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus 0,l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then for every solution
x(t)
of (
5.1
), we have

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}(|x(t)|+|x^{\prime}(t)|)<+\infty.

In order to obtain the boundedness of all solutions of (
5.1
), it is sufficient to prove that its Poincaré mapping can be written as a twist mapping with small enough perturbations. Under some transformations, if some Birkhoff constants of the Poincaré mapping are not zero, we use classical twist theorem or small twist theorem for reversible mappings to derive the boundedness. Otherwise, if all Birkhoff constants of the Poincaré mapping vanish, we apply Theorem
1.1
to achieve the goal. In the following, we will give the proof of Theorem
5.1
, which is similar to the proof in
Liubounde
and
LiM
. Thus we give the sketch of the proof.

We first rewrite (
5.1
) as

\begin{cases}x^{\prime}=-\omega y\\
y^{\prime}=\omega x+\omega^{-1}\varphi(x)f(\omega y)+\omega^{-1}g(x)-\omega^{-1}p(t).\end{cases}

(5.2)

From
(H_{2})
, it follows that (
5.2
) is reversible with respect to the involution
G(x,y)=(x,-y)
.

By polar coordinates change

x=r\cos\theta,\ y=r\sin\theta,

the system (
5.3
) is transformed into

\begin{cases}r^{\prime}=\omega^{-1}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\sin\theta-\omega^{-1}p(t)\sin\theta\\
\theta^{\prime}=\omega+\omega^{-1}r^{-1}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\cos\theta-\omega^{-1}r^{-1}p(t)\cos\theta.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Observing that

|\omega^{-1}r^{-1}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\cos\theta-\omega^{-1}r^{-1}p(t)\cos\theta|\leq Cr^{-1},

for some
C>0
, we may consider (
5.3
) assuming that
r(t)>2C\omega^{-1}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
along a solution
t\mapsto(r(t),\theta(t))
. Therefore,

\theta^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega>0,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

which means that
t\mapsto\theta(t)
ia globally invertible. Denoting by
\theta\mapsto t(\theta)
the inverse function, we have that
\theta\mapsto(r(t(\theta)),t(\theta))
solves the system

\begin{cases}\frac{dr}{d\theta}=\Phi(r,t,\theta)\\
\frac{dt}{d\theta}=\Psi(r,t,\theta),\end{cases}

(5.4)

where

\Phi(r,t,\theta)=\frac{\omega^{-1}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\sin\theta-\omega^{-1}p(t)\sin\theta}{\omega+\omega^{-1}r^{-1}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\cos\theta-\omega^{-1}r^{-1}p(t)\cos\theta},

\Psi(r,t,\theta)=\frac{1}{\omega+\omega^{-1}r^{-1}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\cos\theta-\omega^{-1}r^{-1}p(t)\cos\theta}.

Now noting that the action, angle and time variables are
r,t
and
\theta
, respectively. Since
\Psi(r,-t,-\theta)=\Psi(r,t,\theta)
and
\Phi(r,-t,-\theta)=-\Phi(r,t,\theta)
, we see that system (
5.4
) is reversible under the transformation
(r,t)\mapsto(r,-t)
.

To estimate error terms, we introduce some notations.

Definition 5.2

: Assume function
f(\theta,r,t)
is
O_{n}(r^{-j})
, if
f
is smooth in
(r,t)
, continue in
\theta
, periodic of period
2\pi
in
\theta
and
t
, moreover

|r^{k+j}\frac{\partial^{k+l}f}{\partial r^{k}\partial t^{l}}|\leq C,\quad 0\leq k+l\leq n,

where
C
is a positive constant.

: Suppose function
f(\theta,r,t)
is
o_{n}(r^{-j})
, if
f
is smooth in
(r,t)
, continue in
\theta
, periodic of period
2\pi
in
\theta
and
t
, moreover

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}r^{k+j}\frac{\partial^{k+l}f}{\partial r^{k}\partial t^{l}}=0,\quad 0\leq k+l\leq n,

uniformly in
(\theta,t)
.

It is obvious that

\Phi(r,t,\theta)\in O_{4}(1),\quad\Psi(r,t,\theta)\in O_{4}(1),

and (
5.4
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}\frac{dr}{d\theta}=\omega^{-2}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\sin\theta-\omega^{-2}p(t)\sin\theta+O_{4}(r^{-1})\\
\frac{dt}{d\theta}=\omega^{-1}-\omega^{-3}r^{-1}\big(\varphi(r\cos\theta)f(\omega r\sin\theta)+g(r\cos\theta)\big)\cos\theta+\omega^{-3}r^{-1}p(t)\cos\theta+O_{4}(r^{-2}).\end{cases}

(5.5)

Since the Poincaré mapping of (
5.5
) is not sufficiently close to a twist map, we need to transform (
5.5
) further.

Let

\lambda=r+S_{1}(\theta,r),\quad t=t,

where

S_{1}(\theta,r)=-\omega^{-2}\int_{0}^{\theta}\big(\varphi(r\cos\phi)f(\omega r\sin\phi)+g(r\cos\phi)\big)\sin\phi d\phi.

Under this transformation, (
5.5
) is transformed into

\begin{cases}\frac{d\lambda}{d\theta}=-\omega^{-2}p(t)\sin\theta+O_{4}(\lambda^{-1}),\\
\frac{dt}{d\theta}=\omega^{-1}-\omega^{-3}\lambda^{-1}\big(\varphi(\lambda\cos\theta)f(\omega\lambda\sin\theta)+g(\lambda\cos\theta)\big)\cos\theta+\omega^{-3}\lambda^{-1}p(t)\cos\theta+O_{4}(\lambda^{-2}).\end{cases}

(5.6)

Introduce a transformation

\lambda=\lambda,\quad\tau=t+S_{2}(\theta,\lambda),

where

S_{2}(\theta,\lambda)=\omega^{-3}\lambda^{-1}\int_{0}^{\theta}\big(\varphi(\lambda\cos\phi)f(\omega\lambda\sin\phi)\cos\phi-\lambda J_{1}(\lambda)\big)+\big(g(\lambda\cos\phi)\cos\phi-\lambda J_{2}(\lambda)\big)d\phi,

with

J_{1}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varphi(\lambda\cos\phi)f(\omega\lambda\sin\phi)\cos\phi d\phi,

J_{2}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(\lambda\cos\phi)\cos\phi d\phi.

By this transformation, (
5.6
) is transformed into

\begin{cases}\frac{d\lambda}{d\theta}=-\omega^{-2}p(\tau)\sin\theta+O_{4}(\lambda^{-1})\\
\frac{d\tau}{d\theta}=\omega^{-1}-\omega^{-3}\big(J_{1}(\lambda)+J_{2}(\lambda)\big)+\omega^{-3}\lambda^{-1}p(\tau)\cos\theta+O_{4}(\lambda^{-2}).\end{cases}

(5.7)

Furthermore, we can find a transformation
(\lambda,\tau)\rightarrow(\lambda,\varsigma)
,
where

\varsigma=\tau+\lambda^{-1}S_{3}(\theta,\tau),

(5.8)

and
S_{3}(\theta,\tau)
is determined by solving equation

\omega^{-3}p(\tau)\cos\theta+\frac{\partial S_{3}}{\partial\theta}+\omega^{-1}\frac{\partial S_{3}}{\partial\tau}=0.

By this transformation, we eliminate
\omega^{-3}\lambda^{-1}p(\tau)\cos\theta
item in the second equation of (
5.7
).

Since

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{k+1}J_{1}^{(k)}(\lambda)=(-1)^{k}k!\frac{1}{\pi}\big(\varphi(+\infty)-\varphi(-\infty)\big)f(+\infty),

and

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{k+1}J_{2}^{(k)}(\lambda)=(-1)^{k}k!\frac{1}{\pi}(g(+\infty)-g(-\infty)),\quad 0\leq k\leq 4,

(
5.7
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}\frac{d\lambda}{d\theta}=-\omega^{-2}p(\varsigma)\sin\theta+O_{4}(\lambda^{-1})\\
\frac{d\varsigma}{d\theta}=\omega^{-1}-\frac{\omega^{-3}}{\pi}\lambda^{-1}\Big(\big(\varphi(+\infty)-\varphi(-\infty)\big)f(+\infty)+\big(g(+\infty)-g(-\infty)\big)\Big)+o_{4}(\lambda^{-1}).\end{cases}

(5.9)

We also recalled (
5.9
) is reversible with respect to
(\lambda,\varsigma)\mapsto(\lambda,-\varsigma)
.

Denote
\lambda=\rho^{-1},
then (
5.9
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}\frac{d\rho}{d\theta}=\omega^{-2}\rho^{2}p(\varsigma)\sin\theta+o_{4}(\rho^{2}),\\
\frac{d\varsigma}{d\theta}=\omega^{-1}-\frac{\omega^{-3}}{\pi}\rho\Big(\big(\varphi(+\infty)-\varphi(-\infty)\big)f(+\infty)+\big(g(+\infty)-g(-\infty)\big)\Big)+o_{4}(\rho).\end{cases}

Therefore, we derive the corresponding Poincaré mapping with the form

\begin{cases}\rho_{1}=\rho_{0}+\rho_{0}^{2}l(\varsigma_{0})+o_{4}(\rho_{0}^{2}),\\
\varsigma_{1}=\varsigma_{0}+\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{1}\rho_{0}+o_{4}(\rho_{0}),\end{cases}

(5.10)

where
\gamma_{0}=2\pi\omega^{-1}
,
l(\varsigma_{0})=\omega^{-2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}p(\varsigma_{0}+\omega^{-1}\theta)\sin\theta d\theta

and

\gamma_{1}=-2\omega^{-3}\big((\varphi(+\infty)-\varphi(-\infty))f(+\infty)+(g(+\infty)-g(-\infty))\big).

(5.11)

From the normal form theory, we see that one of the following two cases occurs.

Case 1. If
(\varphi(+\infty)-\varphi(-\infty))f(+\infty)+(g(+\infty)-g(-\infty))\neq 0
, that is,
\gamma_{1}\neq 0
, then by twist theorem for reversible mappings (see
Liu-1
), there are many invariant curves for
\rho\ll 1
. If
\gamma_{1}=0
, we need to continue looking for changes, such that (
5.10
) is of the following form

\begin{cases}\rho_{1}=\rho_{0}+c_{1}\rho_{0}^{k}+o(\rho_{0}^{k+1}),\\
\varsigma_{1}=\varsigma_{0}+\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{l}\rho_{0}^{l}+o(\rho_{0}^{l+1}),\end{cases}

where
c_{1},\gamma_{1}
are constants and
l>k
. Similarly, as long as the coefficient of the twist term
\gamma_{l}
is not zero, by twist theorem in
Liu-1
, we obtain many invariant curves if
\rho\ll 1
, i.e.,
r\gg 1
, which
implies the existence of quasi-periodic solutions of (
5.1
).

Case 2. There is a change of variables such that (
5.10
) can be transformed into a linear normal form

\begin{cases}\rho_{1}=\rho_{0}\\
\varsigma_{1}=\varsigma_{0}+\gamma_{0}.\end{cases}

By Theorem
1.1
, there are sequence of invariant curves tending to
\rho_{0}=0
.

In conclusion, the mapping (
5.10
) has many invariant curves tending to
\rho_{0}=0
, which means the invariant curves of the Poincaré map of (
5.1
) tend to infinity. Thus for equation (
5.1
), the existence of quasi-periodic solutions is got. Moreover for the initial value lying between two invariant curves, the solution is globally bounded. As the invariant curves tend to infinity, all solutions of (
5.1
) are bounded, therefore we finish the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

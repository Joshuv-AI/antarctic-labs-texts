Abstract
Strong convergence and convergence in probability were generalized to the setting of a Riesz space with conditional expectation operator, T T , in [ Y. Azouzi, W.-C. Kuo, K. Ramdane, B. A. Watson , Convergence in Riesz spaces with conditional expectation operators, Positivity , 19 (2015), 647-657] as T T -strong convergence and convergence in T T -conditional probability, respectively. Generalized L p L^{p} spaces for the cases of p = 1 , 2 , ∞ p=1,2,\infty , were discussed in the setting of Riesz spaces as L p ​ ( T ) {L}^{p}(T) spaces in [ C. C. A. Labuschagne, B. A. Watson , Discrete stochastic integration in Riesz spaces, Positivity , 14 (2010), 859-875]. An R ⁡ ( T ) R(T) valued norm, for the cases of p = 1 , ∞ , p=1,\infty, was introduced on these spaces in [ W. Kuo, M. Rogans, B.A. Watson , Mixing processes in Riesz spaces, Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Application , 456 (2017), 992-1004] where it was also shown that R ⁡ ( T ) R(T) is a universally complete f f -algebra and that these spaces are R ⁡ ( T ) R(T) -modules. In [ Y. Azouzi, M. Trabelsi , L p L^{p} -spaces with respect to conditional expectation on Riesz spaces, Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Application , 447 (2017), 798-816] functional calculus was used to consider L p ​ ( T ) {L}^{p}(T) for p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . The strong sequential completeness of the space L 1 ​ ( T ) {L}^{1}(T) , the natural domain of the conditional expectation operator T T , and the strong completeness of L ∞ ​ ( T ) {L}^{\infty}(T) was established in [ W.-C. Kuo, D. Rodda, B. A. Watson , Sequential strong completeness of the natural domain of Riesz space conditional expectation operators, Proc. AMS , 147 (2019), 1597–1603]. In the current work the T T -strong completeness of L 2 ​ ( T ) {L}^{2}(T) is established along with a Riesz-Fischer type theorem where the duality is with respect to the T T -strong dual. It is also shown that the conditional expectation operator T T is a weak order unit for the T T -strong dual.

Throughout this work

Lemma 3.2
(Riesz-Kantorovich)

The space

is a Riesz space with respect to the partial ordering

{\mathfrak{f}}(x)\leq{\mathfrak{g}}(x)

({\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}})(x):=\sup\{{\mathfrak{f}}(y)+{\mathfrak{g}}(z)\,|\,y,z\in E_{+},y+z=x\}

and

({\mathfrak{f}}\wedge{\mathfrak{g}})(x):=\inf\{{\mathfrak{f}}(y)+{\mathfrak{g}}(z)\,|\,y,z\in E_{+},y+z=x\}

for all

and extending these operators to

[
2

.
Here
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}\downarrow{\mathfrak{0}}
in
E^{*}
if and only if
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(x)\downarrow 0
in
E
for each
x\in E_{+}
.

E^{*}
is Dedekind complete and an
R(T)
-module.

Proof:

If
{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{g}}\in E^{*}
then

{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{g}}\in{\cal L}_{b}(E,R(T))
and hence (as

Further, for

To show that

As

to show that

is the same is

{\mathfrak{f}}\vee_{{\cal L}_{b}(E,R(T))}{\mathfrak{g}}=:{\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}}

{\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}}
is in
E^{*}

Since
{\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}}\in{{\cal L}_{b}(E,R(T))}

For
\alpha\in R(T)_{+}
and

({\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}})(\alpha x):=\sup\{{\mathfrak{f}}(y)+{\mathfrak{g}}(z)\,|\,y,z\in E_{+},y+z=\alpha x\}.

If

\alpha

Now as

Thus

Hence

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}})(\alpha x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{\alpha({\mathfrak{f}}(\beta y)+{\mathfrak{g}}(\beta z)\,|\,y,z\in E_{+},\beta y+\beta z=P_{\alpha}x\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\{\alpha({\mathfrak{f}}(y^{\prime})+{\mathfrak{g}}(z^{\prime})\,|\,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in E_{+},y^{\prime}+z^{\prime}=P_{\alpha}x\}

\displaystyle\sup\{\alpha({\mathfrak{f}}(y^{\prime})+{\mathfrak{g}}(z^{\prime})\,|\,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in E_{+},y^{\prime}+z^{\prime}=x\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha({\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}})(x)

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot({\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}})(x)

\displaystyle\sup\{\alpha\cdot({\mathfrak{f}}(y)+{\mathfrak{g}}(z))\,|\,y,z\in E_{+},y+z=x\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{({\mathfrak{f}}(\alpha y)+{\mathfrak{g}}(\alpha z))\,|\,y,z\in E_{+},y+z=x\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\{({\mathfrak{f}}(\alpha y)+{\mathfrak{g}}(\alpha z))\,|\,y,z\in E_{+},\alpha y+\alpha z=\alpha x\}

\displaystyle\sup\{({\mathfrak{f}}(y^{\prime})+{\mathfrak{g}}(z^{\prime}))\,|\,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in E_{+},y^{\prime}+z^{\prime}=\alpha x\}

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{f}}\vee{\mathfrak{g}})(\alpha x)

4
Completeness

As in the scalar case, we can define a norm on
\hat{E}
, however in the case under consideration here this is an

-valued norm or in the
notation of
[
11
]
a
T
-norm. For
{\mathfrak{f}}\in\hat{E}
we define

\|{\mathfrak{f}}\|=\inf\{k\in R(T)^{+}\,|\,|{\mathfrak{f}}(x)|\leq k\|x\|_{T,2}\mbox{ for all }x\in E\}.

(4.6)

As
R(T)
is Dedekind complete it follows that
\|{\mathfrak{f}}\|\in R(T)^{+}
.

For each
x\in E
,
\{k\in R(T)^{+}\,|\,|{\mathfrak{f}}(x)|\leq k\|x\|_{T,2}\}
is a closed (with respect to order limits in

) convex cone in

. Thus

is convex additive and closed w.r.t. order limits suprema and infima in

.
Thus
Y
has a unique minimal element

\|{\mathfrak{f}}\|:=\inf\bigcap_{x\in E}\{k\in R(T)^{+}\,|\,|{\mathfrak{f}}(x)|\leq k\|x\|_{T,2}\}.

As
\|{\mathfrak{f}}\|\in Y
we have

|{\mathfrak{f}}(x)|\leq\|{\mathfrak{f}}\|\,\|x\|_{T,2}
for each
{\mathfrak{f}}\in\hat{E}
and

.

Theorem 4.1

The dual space
\hat{E}
is strongly complete.

Proof:

Let
({\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
be a strong Cauchy net in
\hat{E}
.
Since

|{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(x)-{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}(x)|\leq\|{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}-{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}\|\,\|x\|_{T,2}

it follows that for each
x\in{L}^{2}(T)
,
({\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(x))
is a Cauchy net in
R(T)
.
Eventually

\displaystyle v_{\alpha}:=\sup_{\beta,\gamma\geq\alpha}\|{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}-{\mathfrak{f}}_{\gamma}\|_{T,2}

exists as an element of
R(T)
and

.
Here by eventually we mean that there is there is
\beta
in the index set of the net, so that for
\alpha\geq\beta
the given statement holds.
So eventually

\displaystyle|{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}(x)-{\mathfrak{f}}_{\gamma}(x)|\leq v_{\alpha}\|x\|_{T,2},\quad\mbox{ for all }\beta,\gamma\geq\alpha,

(4.7)

and the Cauchy net
({\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(x))
is eventually bounded in
R(T)
.
Here the net
(|{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}(x)-{\mathfrak{f}}_{\gamma}(x)|)
is considered as being index by
(\alpha,\beta)

with

meaning
\alpha\leq\alpha_{1}
and
\beta\leq\beta_{1}
.
We may thus define

\underline{\mathfrak{f}}(x):=\liminf_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(x)
,
\overline{\mathfrak{f}}(x):=\limsup_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(x)
in
R(T)
.
Here

So we can set
{\mathfrak{f}}(x):=\overline{\mathfrak{f}}(x)=\underline{\mathfrak{f}}(x)
with
({\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(x))
converging in order to
{\mathfrak{f}}(x)
, see
[
3
]
, and hence

is defined for each

From the linearity of order limits and the linearity of each

is a linear map.
Taking the order limit in (

\displaystyle|{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}(x)-{\mathfrak{f}}(x)|\leq v_{\alpha}\|x\|_{T,2},\quad\mbox{ for all }\beta\geq\alpha,x\in L^{2}(T).

(4.8)

Thus

for all

,
from which we have that

Now (

giving that

converges strongly to

.

Definition 4.2

We are now in a position to give the definition of strong completeness.

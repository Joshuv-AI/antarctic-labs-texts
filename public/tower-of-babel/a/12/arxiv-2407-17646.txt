Generalized Hilbert operators acting on weighted spaces of holomorphic functions with sup-norms
María J. Beltrán-Meneu, José Bonet, Enrique Jordá

Abstract
The behaviour of the generalized Hilbert operator associated with a positive finite Borel measure μ \mu on [ 0 , 1 ) [0,1) is investigated when it acts on weighted Banach spaces of holomorphic functions on the unit disc defined by sup-norms and on Korenblum type growth Banach spaces. It is studied when the operator is well defined, bounded and compact. To this aim, we study when it can be represented as an integral operator. We observe important differences with the behaviour of the Cesàro-type operator acting on these spaces, getting that boundedness and compactness are equivalent concepts for some standard weights. For the space of bounded holomorphic functions on the disc and for the Wiener algebra, we get also this equivalence, which is characterized in turn by the summability of the moments of the measure μ . \mu. In the latter case, it is also equivalent to nuclearity. Nuclearity of the generalized Hilbert operator acting on related spaces, such as the classical Hardy space, is also analyzed.



Keywords:
Generalized Hilbert operator, Weighted Banach spaces of analytic functions, Boundness, Compactness, Nuclearity.

Introduction

Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be the space of holomorphic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
endowed with the topology
\tau_{0}
of uniform convergence on the compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. If
\mu
is a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
, we denote by
\mu_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}d\mu(t)
the
n
-th moment of
\mu
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Here
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}=\{0,1,2,...\}
. For

f\in H(\mathbb{D}),

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

z\in\mathbb{D},
we consider its image by the generalized Hilbert operator

H_{\mu}(f)(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n+k}a_{k}\right)z^{n},\ z\in\mathbb{D},

in case it defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
. The operator
H_{\mu}
is a generalization of the classical Hilbert operator
H
. Indeed, if
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure, then
H_{\mu}=H
and
\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The following related operator will also be considered:

I_{\mu}(f)(z):=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(t)}{1-tz}d\mu(t),

whenever it defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
for
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. It is well known that, if
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{1-t}<\infty,
i.e.,
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}<\infty,
then
I_{\mu}
is well defined and continuous on
H(\mathbb{D}).

These two operators coincide on the polynomials. Therefore, if they are continuous on a space containing the polynomials as a dense subset, then they must coincide.

The Hilbert operator and the generalized Hilbert operators have received much attention in the last decades and their behaviour have been investigated when acting on different spaces of holomorphic functions on the disc. We refer the reader to
[
2
,
12
,
13
,
25
]
and the references therein for the Hilbert matrix and to
[

,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
31
,
33
]
for the generalized Hilbert and other Hankel operators.

The purpose of our article is to investigate the boundedness and compactness of the generalized Hilbert operator
H_{\mu}
and its companion operator
I_{\mu}
when they act on weighted spaces of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
defined by sup-norms, and in particular on the Korenblum type growth Banach spaces
H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty}
and
H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0}
,
\gamma>0
. When
p<1
, the results that we present for the continuity and compactness of
I_{\mu}:H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty}\to H_{v_{p}}^{\infty}
are analogous to those obtained for the Cesàro-type operator in
[
3
]
. When
p\geq 1
the situation is completely different. In particular, we show that the continuity of
I_{\mu}:H_{v_{1}}^{\infty}\to H_{v_{1}}^{\infty}
is equivalent to the compactness of the operator. Both conditions are equivalent to the above mentioned summability of the sequence of moments
(\mu_{n})_{n}
associated to
\mu
. In the last section we show that the summability condition is in fact equivalent to the continuity and also to the compactness of
I_{\mu}
when it acts on the space of bounded holomorphic functions on the disc
H^{\infty}
and in the Wiener algebra
A(\mathbb{T})
. We get
I_{\mu}=H_{\mu}
in these cases. Moreover, we show that the continuity of
I_{\mu}:A(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow A(\mathbb{T})
is equivalent not only to the compactness but also to nuclearity. The equivalence of the summability of
(\mu_{n})_{n}
and the continuity of

was obtained recently by Girela and Merchán in
[
17
]
.

We present some necessary definitions. A
weight

\mathbb{D}
is a radial (

Both are Banach spaces under the norm
\|f\|_{v}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}v(z)|f(z)|,\ f\in H_{v}^{\infty}.
They appear in the study of growth conditions of analytic functions and have been investigated by several authors since the work of Shields and Williams (see eg.
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
26
]
,
[

]

coincides with the closure of the polynomials in

[

A weight

Lemma 1

If the weight

By

c_{1}\frac{1}{v(r)}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}r^{k}\leq c_{2}\frac{1}{v(r)}.

Then the function

belongs to

, and, for each

This implies

\frac{1}{v(r)}\leq(1/c_{1})f_{v}(r)

.
∎

I_{\mu}
as an operator with values in
H(\mathbb{D})

In this section
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is a general Banach space of analytic functions, that is, a Banach space containing the polynomials such that the inclusion
X\hookrightarrow(H(\mathbb{D}),\tau_{0})
is continuous. We analyze, for a general measure

First, observe that, given
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D}),
we get
I_{\mu}(f)(0)=\int_{0}^{1}f(t)d\mu(t)
. So, if

is well defined, then the restriction of

must belong to

In the next proposition we prove that this condition is also sufficient to get
I_{\mu}(f)\in H(\mathbb{D})

Proposition 2

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
. If
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfies that its restriction to
[0,1)
belongs to
L_{1}([0,1),\mu)
, then
I_{\mu}(f)\in H(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in H(\mathbb{D})\cap L_{1}([0,1),\mu)
. For
0\leq r<1,
put
I^{r}_{\mu}(f)(z):=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{f(t)}{1-tz}d\mu(t)\in H(\mathbb{D}).
Given
0<R<1,
we get

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D},|z|\leq R}|I_{\mu}(f)(z)-I_{\mu}^{r}(f)(z)|\leq\frac{1}{1-R}\int_{r}^{1}|f(t)|d\mu(t).

Hence, as
\int_{0}^{1}|f(t)|d\mu(t)<\infty
, we get
I_{\mu}^{r}(f)\rightarrow I_{\mu}(f)
as
r\rightarrow 1
in
\tau_{0}
. Hence,
I_{\mu}(f)\in H(\mathbb{D})
.
∎

If we look now at the Hilbert-type operator, we see that
H_{\mu}(f)(0)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}a_{n}
. So, if
H_{\mu}
is well defined for
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})
, then
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}a_{n}
must be convergent. In the next proposition we prove that if we consider the stronger condition
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}|a_{n}|<\infty
, we even get that
H_{\mu}(f)
and
I_{\mu}(f)
are well defined and coincide.

Proposition 3

Let
X\hookrightarrow(H(\mathbb{D}),\tau_{0})
be a Banach space of analytic functions and let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
. If
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}|a_{n}|<\infty
for each sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
such that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in X
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
H_{\mu}:X\to H(\mathbb{D})
and
I_{\mu}:X\to H(\mathbb{D})
are well defined and
I_{\mu}=H_{\mu}
.

Proof.

On the one hand, as
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is decreasing, by hypothesis
(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n+k}a_{k})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is bounded, thus
H_{\mu}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n+k}a_{k}\right)z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})
for every
f\in X
,
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. On the other hand, the hypothesis yields that the restriction of every
f\in X

belongs to
L_{1}([0,1),\mu)
. Actually, the monotone convergence theorem gives

\int_{0}^{1}|f(t)|d\mu(t)\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}|a_{n}|,
hence
I_{\mu}:X\to H(\mathbb{D})
is well defined by Proposition
2
.
The coincidence of
I_{\mu}
and
H_{\mu}
on
X
follows by the dominated convergence theorem using the Taylor series of
\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-tz}
.
∎

Definition 1

A series

It is known that, if

In order to state the following proposition we introduce some notation. For

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-r_{0}}M\|f_{n}-f\|\leq\varepsilon

Now, let us see that

(i)

When

\frac{1}{v}\in L_{1}([0,1),\mu)

If the weight

(i) Just observe that we can apply Proposition

Now assume that

and

(ii) Let
C>0
be such that
\mu_{n}\leq\frac{C}{n+1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Given

\int_{0}^{1}g(t)d\mu(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\mu_{n}\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+1}\leq C\pi\|f\|_{H^{1}},

and then (a) in Proposition

∎

Theorem 7

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and let
v,w
be weights on
\mathbb{D}.
Consider the following statements:

(i)

C(v,w):=\sup_{0\leq r<1}w(r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{v(t)(1-tr)}<\infty,

(ii)

I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is well defined and continuous.

Then (i) always implies (ii). If we assume in addition that the weight
v
is essential, then (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

Proof.

If we assume (i) then
1/v\in L_{1}([0,1),\mu)
(just take
r=0

5
(i) implies that
I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H(\mathbb{D})

hence

and (ii) is a consequence of the closed graph theorem.

For the converse, we assume (ii) and that the weight

is essential. By Lemma

there are
f_{v}\in H_{v}^{\infty}
and
D(v)>0
such that

for each

w(r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{v(t)(1-tr)}\leq D(v)\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D},|z|=r}w(z)\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{v}(t)}{1-tz}d\mu(t)\right|=D(v)\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D},|z|=r}w(z)|I_{\mu}(f_{v})(z)|.

(3.1)

So, if we assume

we get that the condition in (i) is also necessary.
∎

Proposition 8

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on

be weights on
\mathbb{D}
such that

(ii)

Assume that (i) holds. Then

immediately. We can conclude by Lemma

, proceeding analogously as in
[

.

If we assume that (ii) holds, for each

\max_{|z|=r}|I_{\mu}(z^{k})|=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n+k}r^{n}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}r^{n}=\max_{|z|=r}|I_{\mu}(1)|,

(3.2)

we get

for each

. Since the polynomials

The last assertion follows reasoning analogously as in
[

, Lemma 1]
. ∎

\mu

, where

Proposition 10

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and
v,w
be weights on
\mathbb{D}
such that
1/v\in L_{1}([0,1),\mu)
and
I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}

I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
and
I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
are compact if and only if
I_{\mu}(H_{v}^{\infty})\subseteq H_{w}^{0}.

Proof.

By Proposition
8
,
I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is continuous and the operators
I_{\mu}^{**}
and
I_{\mu}
coincide on

. Therefore

if and only if

. Now
[

implies that this is equivalent to the compactness of

. Finally, Proposition

∎

Theorem 11

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and let
v,w
be weights on
\mathbb{D}.
Assume that
1/v\in L_{1}([0,1),\mu)
. Consider the following statements:

(i)

\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}w(r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{v(t)(1-tr)}=0,

(ii)

I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{0}
is compact.

(iii)

I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is compact and
I_{\mu}(1)\in H_{w}^{0}.

(iv)

I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{0}
is continuous.

is essential, then the four conditions are equivalent.

Proof.

Observe that the hypothesis (i) implies
that
I_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is continuous by Theorem

. Moreover, for each

and

Hence,

. The compactness of
I_{\mu}
follows by Proposition
10
, so we have that (i) implies (ii). (ii) implies (iii) is trivial and (iii) implies (iv) by Propositions
8
and

.

Now, let us assume the weight

there are
f_{v}\in H_{v}^{\infty}
and

∎

Corollary 12

\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on

and let

be weights on

Lemma 13

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
,
\gamma>0,
and
\delta\in(-\gamma,1-\gamma).

The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r)^{\gamma+\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{(1-t)^{\gamma}(1-tr)}<\infty

(ii)

\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r)^{\gamma+\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{(1-tr)^{\gamma+1}}<\infty
.

(iii)

\mu
is a
(1-\delta)
-Carleson measure.

Proof.

(ii) is equivalent to (iii) by
[
3
, Theorem 10]

\frac{1}{1-tr}\leq\frac{1}{1-t}
for every

\mu[t,1)\leq C(1-t)^{1-\delta}.
Thus, using integration by parts and
[

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{(1-t)^{\gamma}(1-tr)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu([0,1))+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\gamma}(1-tr)}\right)^{\prime}\mu([t,1))dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu([0,1))+C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\gamma(1-tr)+r(1-t)}{(1-tr)^{2}(1-t)^{\gamma+\delta}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu([0,1))+C(\gamma+r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1-tr)(1-t)^{\gamma+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu([0,1))+C^{\prime}(\gamma+r)(1-r)^{-(\gamma+\delta)}.

∎

The proof of the next Lemma is completely analogous of that of Lemma

[

Theorem 17

\mu

,

Corollary 21

4

The solid hull and the solid core of

Proposition 22

Proposition 23

5
Hilbert-type operators associated to summable moment measures

In this section we deal with positive finite Borel measures
\mu
on
[0,1)
such that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}<\infty
. This condition implies
\mu
is a vanishing Carleson measure
[
24
, Theorem 3.3.1]
.

Proceeding as in the proof of
[

, Theorem 7]
, we obtain that this condition gives the equivalence of the boundedness and compactness of the Hilbert-type operator on

Theorem 24

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
such that
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{1-t}<\infty
, i.e.
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}<\infty
. Then
I_{\mu}:H^{\infty}\rightarrow H^{\infty}
is compact and
H_{\mu}=I_{\mu}.

Proof.

Let us assume without loss of generality that
\mu([0,1))=1
. By
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
,
I_{\mu}
and
H_{\mu}
are continuous on
H^{\infty}
and
H_{\mu}=I_{\mu}.
By the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 6]
we only need to show that
(\|I_{\mu}(f_{n})\|_{\infty})_{n}
is convergent to 0 whenever
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq B_{H^{\infty}}
is convergent to 0 in
\tau_{0}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. By hypothesis, there is
s_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\int_{s_{0}}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{1-t}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(5.1)

We have that

|I_{\mu}(f)(z)|=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(t)}{1-tz}d\mu(t)\right|

(5.2)

for all
f\in H^{\infty}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
t\in[s_{0},1]
and
f\in B_{H^{\infty}}
we have
|\frac{f(t)}{1-tz}|\leq\frac{1}{1-t}
. Hence, by (
5.1
), for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\left|\int_{s_{0}}^{1}\frac{f_{n}(t)}{1-tz}d\mu(t)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(5.3)

Take
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
n\geq n_{0},

|f_{n}(\omega)|<\frac{\varepsilon(1-s_{0})}{2}
for every
|\omega|\leq s_{0}.
For
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\geq n_{0}
we also have

\left|\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\frac{f_{n}(t)}{1-tz}d\mu(t)\right|\leq\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\frac{\max_{\omega\in s_{0}\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|f_{n}(\omega)|}{1-s_{0}}d\mu(t)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(5.4)

Putting together (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (

) we conclude that
\|H_{\mu}(f_{n})\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
for

, and so,
H_{\mu}:H^{\infty}\rightarrow H^{\infty}
, is compact. ∎

Theorem

permits us to complement

Theorem 25

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1).
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

H_{\mu}:H^{\infty}\rightarrow H^{\infty}
is continuous.

(ii)

H_{\mu}:H^{\infty}\rightarrow H^{\infty}
is compact.

(iii)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{1-t}<\infty.

(iv)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}<\infty.

(v)

H_{\mu}:A(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow A(\mathbb{D})
is continuous.

(vi)

H_{\mu}:A(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow A(\mathbb{D})
is compact.

If the assertions hold, then we get
H_{\mu}=I_{\mu}
on
H^{\infty}
.

Proof.

The equivalence between (i), (iii) and (iv) is given in
[
17
, Proof of Theorem 1.2]
. Theorem
24
yields that (iv) implies (ii), and, since (ii) implies (i) we have the equivalence of the first four statements. Since
A(\mathbb{D})
is a subspace of
H^{\infty}
, we get that (i) implies (v) if we show that (iv) yields
H_{\mu}(A(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq A(\mathbb{D})
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
H_{\mu}(z^{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n+k}z^{n}\in A(\mathbb{D})
. We conclude since the polynomials
\mathcal{P}
are dense in
A(\mathbb{D})
. Finally, (v) implies (iv) since
H_{\mu}(1)(1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}
. Trivially, (vi) implies (v), and (v) and (ii) imply (vi).
∎

In what follows, we study the compactness and nuclearity of the Hilbert-type operator on different spaces of holomorphic functions on which the operator is well-defined under the assumption
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}<\infty.
We recall that given two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, we say that an operator

is

if there exist
(x_{n}^{*})_{n}\subseteq X^{*}
and
(y_{n})_{n}\subseteq Y
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{*}\|\|y_{n}\|<\infty
and
T=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{*}\otimes y_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{*}(\cdot)y_{n}.
Nuclear operators are compact.

In the rest of this section, we consider the following general spaces of analytic functions:

\ell^{p}_{A}:=\{f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D}):\ \|f\|^{p}_{p}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}<\infty\}\text{ for $1\leq p<\infty.$}

\ell^{\infty}_{A}:=\{f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D}):\ \|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{n}|a_{n}|<\infty\}.

These spaces have been thoroughly investigated in
[
10
]
. The most relevant examples are the Wiener algebra (for
p=1
)
\ell^{1}_{A}=A(\mathbb{T})
, and (for
p=2
) the classical Hardy space
\ell^{2}_{A}=H^{2}
. In the rest of the paper we identify a function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\ell^{p}_{A},

1\leq p\leq\infty,
with its coefficients
(a_{n})_{n}
.

Proposition 26

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on

If

for all

with

Lemma 27

Let

∎

For a non increasing sequence

(\|y_{k}^{c}\|_{p^{\prime}})_{k}\in l_{q}
whenever

q\left(r-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)=q\left(r-\frac{p-1}{p}\right)>1.

(5.7)

Since
r>1
and
q\geq 1
, the above inequality holds for
p>1
close enough to 1. Now the result follows directly from Lemma
27
.
For the statements about
\ell^{2}_{A}
, i.e
p=p^{\prime}=2
, we observe that the estimate (
5.7
) is satisfied for every
r>1
if
q=2
. For
q=1
the estimate is satisfied when
r>\frac{3}{2}
.
∎

Theorem 32

Let
\mu
be an
r
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
for some
r>1
.

(i)

H_{\mu}:H^{\infty}\to A(\mathbb{T})
and
C_{\mu}:H^{\infty}\to A(\mathbb{T})
are both well defined nuclear operators.

(ii)

H_{\mu}:H^{2}\to H^{2}
and
C_{\mu}:H^{2}\to H^{2}

H_{\mu}
is self-adjoint.

(iii)

If
r>\frac{3}{2},

H_{\mu}:H^{2}\to A(\mathbb{T})

Proof.

From the continuous inclusion
H^{\infty}\hookrightarrow\ell^{p}_{A}
for each

, we get
(i)
, considering the composition of the inclusion and

. The identification

1.

{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable functions.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{E}})
be a paved set. We say that a function
f\in[0,+\infty]^{X}
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable if we always have
\int_{X}fd\alpha=\int_{X}fd\beta

\alpha
and
\beta

\alpha(H)=\beta(H)
for all
H\in{\mathcal{E}}
. We also say that

is
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable if
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
are
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable.

We will denote by
{\mathcal{M}}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
the class of functions
f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{X}
which are
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable and we set
{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{M}}(X,{\mathcal{E}})\cap[0,+\infty]^{X}
.

The simplest
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable functions are the functions that belong to
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
; indeed if
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset[0.+\infty]
;
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
and
H_{i+1}\subset H_{i}
for all

, we have
\int_{X}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\varphi_{H_{i}}\right)d\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\alpha(H_{i})
for any monotonic set function
\alpha
.

\int_{X}fd\alpha=\int_{X}fd\beta.

. i)
\implies

Remark 1
. Let
(X,{\mathcal{E}})
be a paved set, let
\delta:{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
be a set function such that
\delta(\emptyset)=0
and
\delta(A)\leqslant\delta(B)
for all
A,B\in{\mathcal{E}}
with
A\subset B
. The preceding theorem makes it possible to define in a satisfying manner the integral
\int_{X}\mbox{--- }d\delta
; it is defined by setting
\int_{X}fd\delta=\int_{X}fd\alpha
, where
\alpha:{\mathcal{P}}(X)\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
is a monotonic set function such that
\alpha(H)=\delta(H)
for all
H\in{\mathcal{E}}
, when
f\in{\mathcal{M}}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
and
\int_{X}f^{+}d\alpha<+\infty
or
\int_{X}f^{-}d\alpha<+\infty
. According to this definition of the integral we can analyse the properties of
\delta
by means of the properties of
\int_{X}\mbox{--- }d\alpha
. For example let
(X,{\mathcal{E}})
be a paved set that is stable for
(\cap f,\cup f)
; we will see in Proposition 2 that the family of
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable functions
{\mathcal{M}}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
is a convex cone of functions stable for
(\wedge f,\vee f)
. The following conditions are equivalent

i)

\delta(A\cap B)+\delta(A\cup B)=\delta(A)+\delta(B),\forall A,B\in{\mathcal{E}}
,

ii)

\int_{X}(f+g)d\delta=\int_{X}fd\delta+\int_{X}gd\delta,\forall f,g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
,

iii)

\int_{X}(f\wedge g)d\delta+\int_{X}(f\vee g)d\delta=\int_{X}fd\delta+\int_{X}gd\delta,\forall f,g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
.

If in i), ii) and iii) we change “
=
” to “
\leqslant
” or “
\geqslant
”, we again obtain the equivalence of i), ii) and iii) (see
[
4
]
).

2.

Properties of
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable functions.

From the definition of
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurability and Property ii) of Theorem 1, we obtain the following propositions on the properties of
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable functions.

Proposition 1
. Let
(X,{\mathcal{E}})
be a paved set and let
\psi:[0,+\infty]\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
be a continuous and increasing function. We have

i)

if
\psi(0)=0
,
f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
, then
\psi\circ f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
(note we can dispense with the equality
\psi(0)=0
if
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
);

ii)

if the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
converges uniformly to
f
, then
f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
.

Proposition 2
. Let
(X,{\mathcal{E}})
be a paved set. Then

i)

for
{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
to be stable for
(\wedge f)
(respectively, for
(\vee f)
) it is necessary and sufficient that
{\mathcal{E}}
be stable for
(\cap f)
(respectively, for
(\cup f)
);

ii)

to have
f+g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
for any
f,g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
, it is necessary and sufficient that
{\mathcal{E}}
be stable for
(\cap f,\cup f)
;

iii)

for
{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
to be stable for
(\wedge d)
(respectively, for
(\vee d)
) it is necessary and sufficient that
{\mathcal{E}}
be stable for
(\cap d)
(respectively, for
(\cup d)
); in that case, for
f\in[0,+\infty]
to be
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable it is necessary and sufficient that the set
\{f\geqslant t\}\in{\mathcal{E}}
(respectively,
\{f>t\}\in{\mathcal{E}}
) for all
t\in(0,+\infty)
.

Proof
. We are only going to show property ii). If
f+g
is
{\mathcal{E}}
measurable for all pairs of functions
f,g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
, we have that
\varphi_{A}+\varphi_{B}
is
{\mathcal{E}}
measurable for all
A,B\in{\mathcal{E}}
; it follows therefore that
A\cap B
and
A\cup B\in{\mathcal{E}}
, according to ii) of Theorem 1; that is
{\mathcal{E}}
is stable for
(\cap f,\cup f)
. On the other hand, suppose that
{\mathcal{E}}
is stable for
(\cap f,\cup f)
, let us verify that
{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
is stable for summation. If
g_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\varphi_{A_{i}}
and
g_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\varphi_{B_{i}}
belong to
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
, the function
g_{1}+g_{2}
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
, because we have for all
t\in(0,+\infty)
the equality

\{g_{1}+g_{2}>t\}=\cup_{(a,b)\in Z}(\{g_{1}\geqslant a\}\cap\{g_{2}\geqslant b\}),

where
Z
is the finite set

\{(g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x\in X\mbox{ and }g_{1}(x)+g_{2}(x)>t\}.

If
f,g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
are bounded, there exist two sequences of bounded
{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable functions
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, which converge uniformly, respectively, to
g
and
f
(see Th. 1, property iii)); since
\{g_{n}+f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
converges uniformly to
g+f
, we therefore have that
f+g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
, by property ii) of Proposition 1. Finally, taking into account the equality
\int_{X}(f+g)d\alpha=\lim_{n}\int_{X}(f\wedge n+g\wedge n)d\alpha
, true for any
f,g\in[0,+\infty]^{X}
and for any monotonic set function
\alpha:{\mathcal{P}}(X)\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
, it follows then that
f+g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
, also if
f,g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
are not bounded.
\quad\quad\quad\blacksquare

In particular cases, the class

has new properties. For example, if

that is measurable with respect to the paving comprised of the sets of the form

is continuous, is also bounded. This depends on the

of the pavings in question (see

such that

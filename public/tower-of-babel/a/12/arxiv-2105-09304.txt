Emergence of the Cotton tensor for describing gravity
Junpei Harada

Abstract
It is shown that the Cotton tensor can describe the effects of gravity beyond general relativity. Any solution of the Einstein equations with or without the cosmological constant satisfies the field equations described by the Cotton tensor. It implies that the cosmological constant is an integration constant. A vacuum of a theory is represented by the vanishing of the Cotton tensor, rather than the vanishing of the Ricci tensor. An exact Schwarzschild-like solution for a static and spherically symmetric source is discovered. Although the field equations involve the third order of derivative, it is found that they reduce to the second-order differential equations due to a variational principle.



Introduction

The Cotton curvature tensor, named after Émile Cotton
Cotton 1899
, is a rank-3 tensor that describes the curvature of a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. For
n<3
, it identically vanishes. For
n=3
, it vanishes if and only if a manifold is conformally flat. The Cotton tensor has mainly been used to investigate the three-dimensional manifolds
Cotton 1899
and three-dimensional gravity
Jackiw 2004
;
Deser et al. 2005
. However, as we will see below, it is found that the Cotton tensor can describe the effects of gravity beyond general relativity in four-dimensional spacetime.

In this Letter it is shown that the Cotton tensor emerges as describing the gravitational field equations. This description has a number of advantages. First, any solution of the Einstein equations
with or without
the cosmological constant satisfies the given field equations. It implies that the cosmological constant is an integration constant—there is no need to add it to the field equations or to the action. Second, a vacuum in such a theory is represented by the vanishing of the Cotton tensor, rather than the vanishing of the Ricci tensor. This implies that the field equations in vacuum have more solutions than general relativity—it will be shown that any metric of the Ricci-flat manifolds, the Einstein manifolds, or the conformally flat manifolds is a trivial vacuum solution. As a nontrivial solution, an exact Schwarzschild-like metric for a static and spherically symmetric source is discovered. It is the first discovered nontrivial exact solution of the given field equations. Third, it is found that the field equations described by the Cotton tensor can be derived from the Weyl action of conformal gravity, by varying the action with respect to the connection keeping the metric fixed. Although the field equations involve the third order of derivative, they reduce to the second-order differential equations due to such a variational principle.

Throughout this Letter we work in
n=4
dimensions. The usual Levi-Civita connection is adopted to defined the covariant derivative, though a choice is arbitrary. The signature of metric is
(-,+,+,+)
.

Gravitational field equations

In electromagnetism, the Maxwell’s equations in vacuum are written as
\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=0
, where
\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}
denotes the divergence of a linear curvature. In the case of gravity, there are four possible terms as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},

(1a)

\displaystyle(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})\nabla_{\mu}R,

(1b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(g^{\mu\rho}R^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}R^{\mu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}R^{\nu\rho}+g^{\nu\sigma}R^{\mu\rho}),

(1c)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},

(1d)

where
\nabla_{\mu}
denotes the covariant derivative, and
C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the Weyl curvature tensor
Weyl 1918
which is defined by

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}:=

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{6}(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\nu\rho}g_{\mu\sigma})R

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(g_{\mu\rho}R_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\nu\rho}R_{\mu\sigma}-g_{\mu\sigma}R_{\nu\rho}+g_{\nu\sigma}R_{\mu\rho}).

(2)

It should be also noted that the following Bianchi identity is satisfied (this form may be unfamiliar, but it is useful),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})\nabla_{\mu}R

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\mu}\left(g^{\mu\rho}R^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}R^{\mu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}R^{\nu\rho}+g^{\nu\sigma}R^{\mu\rho}\right)=0.

(3)

Four possible terms (
1a
)–(
1d
) are not linearly independent due to Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
), and therefore the gravitational analog of
\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}
should be a linear combination of arbitrary two terms of Eqs. (
1a
)–(
1d
). If we choose (
1a
) and (
1b
) as two independent terms, then the gravitational analog of
\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}
can be written in the form,

a\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+b(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})\nabla_{\mu}R.

(4)

The coefficients
a
and
b
will be determined soon. We regard this as the left-hand side of the gravitational field equations. The right-hand side of field equations can be similarly written. There are
only
two possible terms as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(g^{\mu\rho}T^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}T^{\mu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}T^{\nu\rho}+g^{\nu\sigma}T^{\mu\rho}),

(5a)

\displaystyle(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})\nabla_{\mu}T,

(5b)

where
T_{\mu\nu}
is the energy-momentum tensor and

. Therefore, the field equations can be written as

\displaystyle a\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+b(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})\nabla_{\mu}R

(6)

\displaystyle=c\nabla_{\mu}(g^{\mu\rho}T^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}T^{\mu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}T^{\nu\rho}+g^{\nu\sigma}T^{\mu\rho})

\displaystyle+d(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})\nabla_{\mu}T,

where
c
and
d
are coefficients.

Let us now determine the coefficients
a
,
b
,
c
, and
d
. Multiplying Eq. (
6
) by
g_{\nu\sigma}
, we find

(a+6b)\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\rho}=2c\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\rho}+(c+3d)\nabla_{\mu}(g^{\mu\rho}T),

(7)

where we have used the identity
g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}R=2\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\nu}
.
We now require that the conservation law of the energy momentum (
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\rho}=0
) is identically satisfied, and then from Eq. (
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle a+6b=0,

(8a)

\displaystyle c+3d=0.

(8b)

We also require that any solution of the Einstein equations satisfies Eq. (
6
). Inserting
T_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-Rg_{\mu\nu}/2
(where we set
8\pi G=1
,
G
is the Newton’s constant) and
T=-R
into the right-hand side of Eq. (

6

c\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-\left(\frac{c}{2}+d\right)(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})\nabla_{\mu}R,

(9)

where the Bianchi identity (
3
) has been used.
Comparing Eq. (
9
) with the left-hand side of Eq. (
6

\displaystyle a=c,

(10a)

\displaystyle b=-\left(\frac{c}{2}+d\right).

(10b)

From Eqs. (
8a
), (

), (
10a
), and (

) (three of them are linearly independent), the coefficients
a
,
b
,
c
, and
d

a=1,\quad b=-\frac{1}{6},\quad c=1,\quad d=-\frac{1}{3},\quad

(11)

where we set
a=1
as a normalization. Thus, we obtained Eq. (
6
) with Eq. (
11
) as the field equations, but it is now convenient to rewrite them in a simple form as follows.

It is convenient to define the rank-4 tensor by

G^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}:=R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{6}(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})R.

(12)

Multiplying
G^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
by
g_{\nu\sigma}
, we find that

g_{\nu\sigma}G^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=R^{\mu\rho}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\rho}R=:G^{\mu\rho},

(13)

where
G^{\mu\rho}
is the Einstein tensor. It is also convenient to define the rank-4 tensor by

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(g^{\mu\rho}T^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}T^{\mu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}T^{\nu\rho}+g^{\nu\sigma}T^{\mu\rho})

(14)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma})T,

where an overall normalization of Eq. (
14
) has been chosen so that the following relation is satisfied,

g_{\nu\sigma}T^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=T^{\mu\rho}.

(15)

Therefore, Eq. (
6
) with Eq. (
11
) can be written as

\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=16\pi G\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma},

(16)

where
8\pi G
is explicitly written, and we have

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\rho}T_{\nu\sigma}-\nabla_{\sigma}T_{\nu\rho})-\frac{1}{6}(g_{\nu\sigma}\nabla_{\rho}T-g_{\nu\rho}\nabla_{\sigma}T).

(17)

It is even more convenient to rewrite Eq. (
16
) as follows. Using Eq. (
12
), we find

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{6}(g_{\nu\sigma}\nabla_{\rho}R-g_{\nu\rho}\nabla_{\sigma}R),

(18)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\rho}R_{\nu\sigma}-\nabla_{\sigma}R_{\nu\rho}-\frac{1}{6}(g_{\nu\sigma}\nabla_{\rho}R-g_{\nu\rho}\nabla_{\sigma}R),

\displaystyle=:C_{\nu\rho\sigma},

where the identity
\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\nabla_{\rho}R_{\nu\sigma}-\nabla_{\sigma}R_{\nu\rho}
has been used, and
C_{\nu\rho\sigma}
is called the Cotton tensor that satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla^{\nu}C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=0,

(19a)

\displaystyle g^{\nu\rho}C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=0,

(19b)

\displaystyle C_{\nu\rho\sigma}+C_{\nu\sigma\rho}=0,

(19c)

\displaystyle C_{\nu\rho\sigma}+C_{\rho\sigma\nu}+C_{\sigma\nu\rho}=0.

(19d)

It should be noted that
C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
denotes the Weyl tensor, and
C_{\nu\rho\sigma}
denotes the Cotton tensor.

Consequently, we obtain the field equations of gravity,

C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=16\pi G\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}.

(20)

Multiplying Eq. (
20
) by
g^{\nu\sigma}
, we can quickly confirm the conservation law of the energy-momentum tensor as

g^{\nu\sigma}C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu}{}_{\rho}=16\pi G\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{\rho}=0.

(21)

Here we should present some remarks. First, any solution of the Einstein equations (
G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}
) satisfies Eq. (
20
). Furthermore, it is obvious that any solution of the Einstein equations with the cosmological constant (
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}
) satisfies Eq. (
20
). This implies that the cosmological constant is an integration constant. Of course, Eq. (
20
) has other solutions which are not solutions of the Einstein equations—Eq. (
20
) has more information than general relativity. It should be noted that the Einstein equations need
not
be satisfied in Eq. (

). These are shown in Fig.

Exact vacuum solutions

In a vacuum, Eq. (
20
) yields

C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=0.

(22)

This should be regarded as a generalization of
R_{\mu\nu}=0
of general relativity.
It is clear from Eq. (
18
) that if
R_{\mu\nu}
is zero, then
C_{\nu\rho\sigma}
vanishes too, so any vacuum solution of the Einstein equations is a solution of Eq. (
22
). We also find from Eq. (
18
) that if
R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
, then
C_{\nu\rho\sigma}
automatically vanishes. Thus, the de Sitter (or the anti–de Sitter) metric is a solution of Eq. (
22
), though there is no cosmological constant in Eq. (
22
).

Furthermore, any metric of the locally conformally flat manifolds satisfies Eq. (
22
). This can be understood as follows. Using the Bianchi identity (
3
), we find

\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=2\nabla_{\mu}C^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=C_{\nu\rho\sigma}.

(23)

This indicates that the divergence of
G^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
and that of
C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
are equivalent (up to a factor two), though

G^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
and
C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
are different. It is clear from Eq. (
23
) that if
C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is zero, then
C_{\nu\rho\sigma}
vanishes too, so any metric of the locally conformally flat manifold
(C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=0)
satisfies Eq. (
22
).

Thus, it is concluded that any metric that satisfies

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\qquad\mbox{(Ricci-flat manifold)}

(24a)

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda g_{\mu\nu},\ \mbox{(Einstein manifold)}

(24b)

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\qquad\mbox{(conformally flat manifold)}

(24c)

is a solution of Eq. (
22
).

C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=0

Let us focus on the Schwarzschild-like metric as

(25)

where

\displaystyle 3e^{-\nu}C_{010}=-\frac{2}{r^{3}}

Action and variational principle

The Weyl tensor in Eq. (
23
) aims our attention to the following Weyl action,

\displaystyle I_{\rm W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{3}R^{2}\right),

where
g=\det(g_{\mu\nu})
, and we have used the identity,

C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-2R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{3}R^{2},

(38)

and we have omitted the topological invariant from the action. The Weyl action (
37
) is a unique gravity action that is invariant under the local conformal transformation
g_{\mu\nu}(x)\rightarrow\Omega^{2}(x)g_{\mu\nu}(x)
. We will show that the Weyl action (
37
) yields
C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=0
, but before showing it, we present some remarks on the conformal gravity.

The gravity theory with the Weyl action (
37
) is usually known as the conformal gravity
Mannheim and Kazanas 1989
. However, our gravity theory is different from conformal gravity, though the action coincides. The difference is originated from not the action but the variational principle; in conformal gravity, one varies the Weyl action (
37
) with respect to the metric, and it yields the rank-2 tensor equations, which are described by the Bach tensor
Bach 1922
;
Mannheim and Kazanas 1989
;
’t Hooft 2015
. Thus, a vacuum of conformal gravity is represented by the vanishing of the Bach tensor. In contrast, Eq. (
22
) is described by the Cotton tensor. This implies that the variational principle should be different from that of conformal gravity.

The variational principle that yields
C_{\nu\rho\sigma}=0
is as follows. Varying the Weyl action (
37
) with respect to the connection keeping the metric fixed, we find

\displaystyle\delta I_{\rm W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}(R_{\mu}{}^{\nu\rho\sigma}\delta R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{3}Rg^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu})

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}C^{\nu\rho\sigma}\delta\Gamma_{\sigma\nu\rho},

where
\delta\Gamma_{\sigma\nu\rho}:=g_{\sigma\lambda}\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\nu\rho}
; we have used the identity

\delta R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\nabla_{\rho}(\delta\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\sigma\nu})-\nabla_{\sigma}(\delta\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\rho\nu}),

(40)

and the boundary condition
\delta\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=0
at the boundary is imposed. Thus, we find that
\delta I_{\rm W}=0
yields
C^{\nu\rho\sigma}=0
. Thus, it is found that the local conformal symmetry of the action (
37
) can be broken in the field equations, as shown in Eq. (
36
), due to a variational principle.

The variation of the connection (with keeping in mind that the metric fixed) is given by

\delta\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\rho\lambda}\left(\delta(\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\lambda})+\delta(\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu\lambda})-\delta(\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu})\right).

(41)

Therefore, we are varying the action with respect to the derivative of the metric keeping the metric itself fixed. As a consequence of this variational principle, the field equations that involve the third order of derivative reduce to the second-order differential equations, as shown in Eq. (
28
). This is a generic property of the theory, and it is a consequence of the variation presented here.

Finally, we present the gravitational analog of the source term
J^{\mu}A_{\mu}
in Maxwell’s theory,

I_{\rm source}=-8\pi G\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}T^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},

(42)

where
T^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is defined by Eq. (
14
), and
T_{\mu\nu}
is a source that does not include the connection. We find that the variation of the action with respect to the connection keeping the metric fixed yields,

\delta I_{\rm source}=-16\pi G\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\delta\Gamma_{\sigma\nu\rho}.

(43)

Therefore, we find that
\delta I_{\rm W}+\delta I_{\rm source}=0
yields Eq. (
20
).

Conclusion

It has been shown that the Cotton tensor can describe the effects of gravity beyond general relativity. A vacuum is represented by the vanishing of the Cotton tensor. An exact Schwarzschild-like solution has been discovered. This theory includes general relativity as part of itself, and has more information on gravity. It is a potentially viable theory of gravity, and it would be worth for further investigations.

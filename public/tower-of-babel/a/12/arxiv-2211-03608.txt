Combining effects ensuring boundedness in an attraction-repulsion chemotaxis model with production and consumption


Abstract
. This paper is framed in a series of studies on attraction-repulsion chemotaxis models combining different effects: nonlinear diffusion and sensitivities and logistic sources, for the dynamics of the cell density, and consumption and/or production impacts, for those of the chemicals. In particular, herein we focus on the situation where the signal responsible of gathering tendencies for the particles’ distribution is produced, while the opposite counterpart is consumed. In such a sense, this research complements the results in [ FLV22 ] and [ CFV22 ] , where the chemicals evolve according to different laws.

1.
Introduction, motivations and state of the art

1.1.
The attractive and the repulsive models

During the last 50 years, precisely since the advent of basic biological models idealizing chemotaxis phenomena, many related variants have attracted the interest of the mathematical community. Indeed, several technical difficulties encouraging and stimulating researchers in the field arise in such problems.

For our purposes, we are interested in a model resulting in a combination of this aggregative signal-production mechanism

(1)

u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)=\nabla\cdot(\nabla u-\chi u\nabla v)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau v_{t}=\Delta v-v+u,\quad\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T_{max}),

and this repulsive signal-consumption one

(2)

u_{t}=\Delta u+\xi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w)=\nabla\cdot(\nabla u+\xi u\nabla w)\quad\textrm{and}\quad w_{t}=\Delta w-uw,\quad\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T_{max}).

Herein
\Omega
is a bounded and smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tau\in\{0,1\}
,
\chi,\xi>0
and
T_{max}>0
. Moreover, to properly interpret these formulations in the context of real biological phenomena, we also specify the following:

denotes a particular cell distribution (populations, organisms) at position
x\in\Omega
and time
t\in(0,T_{max})
, and
v=v(x,t)
and
w=w(x,t)
stand for the concentrations of chemoattractant and chemorepellent (i.e., a chemical signal that causes cells to attract and repel each other, respectively). In this way, system
(
1

1.2.
The attraction-repulsion model: combining effects

The general form of an attraction-repulsion chemotaxis model with logistic source and linear or nonlinear production can be formulated as

(3)

\hskip-5.69046pt\begin{cases}u_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(D(u)\nabla u-S(u)\nabla v+T(u)\nabla w\right)+h(u)&\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T_{max}),\\
\tau_{1}v_{t}=\Delta v+\phi(u,v)&\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T_{max}),\\
\tau_{2}w_{t}=\Delta w+\psi(u,w)&\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T_{max}),\\
u_{\nu}=v_{\nu}=w_{\nu}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T_{max}),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;\tau_{1}v(x,0)=\tau_{1}v_{0}(x),\;\tau_{2}w(x,0)=\tau_{2}w_{0}(x)&x\in\bar{\Omega},\\
\end{cases}

where
D=D(u),S=S(u),T=T(u),h(u)
are functions with a certain regularity and proper behavior, and

\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\{0,1\}

More On the Quasi-Stationary Distribution of the Shiryaev–Roberts Diffusion
Soumik Banerjee and Aleksey S. Polunchenko

Abstract
We consider the classical Shiryaev–Roberts martingale diffusion, ( R t ) t ⩾ 0 (R_{t})_{t\geqslant 0} , restricted to the interval [ 0 , A ] [0,A] , where A > 0 A>0 is a preset absorbing boundary. We take yet another look at the well-known phenomenon of quasi-stationarity (time-invariant probabilistic behavior, conditional on no absorbtion hitherto) exhibited by the diffusion in the temporal limit, as t → + ∞ t\to+\infty , for each A > 0 A>0 . We obtain new upper- and lower-bounds for the quasi-stationary distribution’s probability density function (pdf), q A ​ ( x ) q_{A}(x) ; the bounds vary in the trade-off between simplicity and tightness. The bounds imply directly the expected result that q A ​ ( x ) q_{A}(x) converges to the pdf, h ⁡ ( x ) h(x) , of the diffusion’s stationary distribution, as A → + ∞ A\to+\infty ; the convergence is pointwise, for all x ⩾ 0 x\geqslant 0 . The bounds also yield an explicit upperbound for the gap between q A ​ ( x ) q_{A}(x) and h ⁡ ( x ) h(x) for a fixed x x . By virtue of integration the bounds for the pdf q A ​ ( x ) q_{A}(x) translate into new bounds for the corresponding cumulative distribution function (cdf), Q A ​ ( x ) Q_{A}(x) . All of our results are established explicitly, using certain latest monotonicity properties of the modified Bessel K K function involved in the exact closed-form formula for q A ​ ( x ) q_{A}(x) recently obtained by Polunchenko 2017c . We conclude with a discussion of potential applications of our results in quickest change-point detection: our bounds allow for a very accurate performance analysis of the so-called randomized Shiryaev–Roberts–Pollak change-point detection procedure.

Introduction

This work is a continuation of the recent paper by
Li and Polunchenko 2020
and, too, focuses on the phenomena of quasi-stationarity and stationarity exhibited by one particular version of the Generalized Shiryaev–Roberts (GSR) stochastic process—a time-homogeneous Markov diffusion well-known in the area of quickest change-point detection. See, e.g.,
Shiryaev 1961
;
Shiryaev 1963
;
Shiryaev 1978
;
Shiryaev 2002
;
Shiryaev 2011
;
Shiryaev 2017
,
Pollak and Siegmund 1985
,
Feinberg and Shiryaev 2006
,
Burnaev et al 2009
,
Polunchenko and Sokolov 2016
, and
Polunchenko 2016
;
Polunchenko 2017c
;
Polunchenko 2017a
;
Polunchenko 2017b
. More specifically, the GSR process’ version of interest is the solution
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
of the stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle dR_{t}^{r}

\displaystyle=dt+R_{t}^{r}dB_{t}\;\text{with}\;R_{0}^{r}\coloneqq r\geqslant 0\;\text{fixed},

(1)

where
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is standard Brownian motion in the sense that
\EV[dB_{t}]=0
,
\EV[(dB_{t})^{2}]=dt
, and
B_{0}=0
; the initial value
R_{0}^{r}\coloneqq r\geqslant 0
is often referred to as the process’ headstart. It is straightforward to solve (
1
) and express
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
explicitly as

\displaystyle R_{t}^{r}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{B_{t}-\dfrac{1}{2}t\right\}\left(r+\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle t$}}$}}\nolimits\exp\biggl\{-\left(B_{s}-\dfrac{1}{2}s\right)\biggr\}ds\right),\;\;t\geqslant 0,

so that the set
[0,+\infty)
is easily seen to be the “natural” state space for
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
because
R_{0}^{r}\coloneqq r\geqslant 0
by assumption. Moreover, it is also easily checked that
\EV[R_{t}^{r}-t-r]=0
for any
t,r\geqslant 0
, i.e., the process
\{R_{t}^{r}-t-r\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is a zero-mean martingale. Yet, although
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
has a linear upward trend in time, it is actually a recurrent process with a nontrivial probabilistic behavior in the limit as
t\to+\infty
; cf.
(
Pollak and Siegmund 1985
, p. 270)
. Specifically, if
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
is let run “loose”, i.e., considered on the entire nonnegative half-line, then the limiting (as
t\to+\infty
) behavior of
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
is known as stationarity, and it is characterized by the invariant probability measure whose cumulative distribution function (cdf) and density (pdf), respectively, are

\displaystyle H(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{t\to+\infty}\Pr(R_{t}^{r}\leqslant x)\;\;\text{and}\;\;h(x)\coloneqq\dfrac{d}{dx}H(x),

(2)

where
r\in[0,+\infty)
is fixed. This probability measure has already been found, e.g., by
Shiryaev 1961
;
Shiryaev 1963
, by
Pollak and Siegmund 1985
, and more recently also by
Feinberg and Shiryaev 2006
;
Burnaev et al 2009
;
Polunchenko and Sokolov 2016
, to be the momentless (no moments of orders one and higher) distribution

\displaystyle H(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-\tfrac{2}{x}}{\One_{\left\{x\geqslant 0\right\}}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;h(x)=\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}\,e^{-\tfrac{2}{x}}{\One_{\left\{x\geqslant 0\right\}}}=\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}H(x),

(3)

which is an extreme-value Fréchet-type distribution, and a particular case of the inverse (reciprocal) gamma distribution. See also, e.g.,
Linetsky 2004
and
Avram et al 2013
. As an aside, note that, in view of (
3
), the stationary distribution of the reciprocal of
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
is exponential with mean
1/2
.

However, if all states from a fixed
A>0
and up inside the process’ “natural” state space
[0,+\infty)
are made into absorbing states, then
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
also has a nontrivial probabilistic behavior in the limit as
t\to+\infty
. This behavior is known as quasi-stationarity, and it is characterized by the invariant probability measure whose cdf and pdf, respectively, are

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{t\to+\infty}\Pr(R_{t}^{r}\leqslant x\mid R_{s}^{r}\in[0,A)\;\text{for all}\;0\leqslant s\leqslant t)

(4)

\displaystyle\text{and }\;q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dfrac{d}{dx}Q_{A}(x),

where
r\in[0,A)
is fixed. The existence of this probability measure was formally established, e.g., by
Pollak and Siegmund 1985
, although one can also infer the same result, e.g., from the earlier seminal work of
Mandl 1961
. Moreover, analytic closed-form formulae for both
Q_{A}(x)
and
q_{A}(x)
were recently obtained by
Polunchenko 2017c
, apparently for the first time in the literature; see formulae (
11
) and (
12
) in Section
4
below. Recently these formulae were used by
Polunchenko and Pepelyshev 2018
to compute analytically the quasi-stationary distribution’s Laplace transform, and then also by
Li et al 2019
to find the quasi-stationary distribution’s fractional moment of any real order.

Remark 1

The phenomenon of quasi-stationarity is also exhibited by
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
in another case, viz. when all states from 0 up through a fixed
A>0
inclusive inside the process’ “natural” state space
[0,+\infty)
are made into absorbing states, so that the state space of
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
becomes the set
[A,+\infty)
with absorbtion at the lower end. This case was first investigated in
(
Collet et al 2013
, Section 7.8.2)
. It was also recently analyzed by
Polunchenko et al 2018
who obtained analytically exact closed-form formulae for the corresponding quasi-stationary cdf and pdf.

The quasi-stationary distribution (
4
) and the stationary distribution (
2
) are obviously related: as one would expect, the former converges to the latter as
A\to+\infty
. This was formally shown by
Pollak and Siegmund 1986
, and not only for the GSR process at hand, but for an entire class of stochastically monotone processes. More specifically, it can be deduced from
Pollak and Siegmund 1986
that
Q_{A}(x)\geqslant H(x)
for any fixed
A>0
and any
x\geqslant 0
, and that
\lim_{A\to+\infty}Q_{A}(x)=H(x)
for any fixed
x\geqslant 0
. The question as to the rate of convergence of
Q_{A}(x)
down to
H(x)
as
A\to+\infty
was recently investigated by
Li and Polunchenko 2020
who showed that
(0<)\;\sup_{x\geqslant 0}[Q_{A}(x)-H(x)]=O(\log(A)/A)
, as
A\to+\infty
; see (
16
). The latter result was arrived at by first obtaining new lower- and upper-bounds for
Q_{A}(x)
, of varying tightness and complexity. This work’s main contribution is new lower- and upper-bounds for the pdf
q_{A}(x)
; the bounds for
q_{A}(x)
can be integrated, and thereby be converted into new bounds for
Q_{A}(x)
. In particular, our new bounds for
q_{A}(x)
are tight enough to show that
q_{A}(x)
converges to
h(x)
as
A\to+\infty
, pointwise, for each fixed
x\geqslant 0
. All of the bounds are obtained explicitly with the aid of the formula for
Q_{A}(x)
and that for
q_{A}(x)
latterly offered by
Polunchenko 2017c
, and certain recently discovered monotonicity properties of the modified Bessel
K
function (of the second kind).

The obtained bounds for
Q_{A}(x)
and for
q_{A}(x)
are of importance in quickest change-point detection. Specifically, the process
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
governed by equation (
1
) arises in quickest change-point detection when the aim is to monitor the mean of the process
X_{t}\coloneqq(t-\nu){\One_{\left\{t>\nu\right\}}}+B_{t}
, where
\nu,t\geqslant 0
, observed “live”. Since
\EV[X_{t}]=(t-\nu){\One_{\left\{t>\nu\right\}}}
, it is anticipated that the drift of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
will change from none (zero) to one (per time unit) at time instance
\nu\in[0,+\infty]
referred to as the change-point. The challenge is that
\nu
is
not
known in advance; in particular
\nu=\infty
is a possibility, i.e., the drift of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
may remain zero indefinitely and never change. The mean of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is controlled online by sounding an alarm should (and as soon as) the behavior of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
suggest that possibly
\EV[X_{t}]=t-\nu>0
, i.e.,
t>\nu
; if it is not the case, then the alarm is a false one. More concretely, the so-called GSR quickest change-point detection procedure, set up to control the drift of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, sounds a false alarm at

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{A}^{r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\big\{t\geqslant 0\colon R_{t}^{r}=A\big\}\;\text{with}\;r\in[0,A)\;\text{fixed},\;\text{and}\;\inf\{\varnothing\}=+\infty,

(5)

where the constant
A>0
is selected in advance in accordance with the desired false alarm risk level. Hence
(R_{t}^{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
is the GSR procedure’s detection statistic in the pre-change regime, i.e., for
t\in[0,\nu]
. The definition (
4
) of the quasi-stationary cdf can now be rewritten as
Q_{A}(x)=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\Pr(R_{t}^{r}\leqslant x|\mathcal{S}_{A}^{r}>t)
.

The GSR procedure, identified in the pre-change regime with the stopping time (
5
), was proposed by
Moustakides et al 2011
as a headstarted (i.e., more general) version of the classical quasi-Bayesian Shiryaev–Roberts (SR) procedure that emerged from the independent work of Shiryaev
(
Shiryaev 1961
;
Shiryaev 1963
)
and that of Roberts
(
Roberts 1966
)
. The interest in the GSR procedure (and its variations) is due to its strong (near-) optimality properties. See, e.g.,
Burnaev 2009
,
Feinberg and Shiryaev 2006
,
Burnaev et al 2009
,
Polunchenko and Tartakovsky 2010
,
Tartakovsky and Polunchenko 2010
,
Vexler and Gurevich 2011
, and
Tartakovsky et al 2012
. For example, it is known that if the GSR procedure’s headstart is sampled from the quasi-stationary distribution (
4
), then such a randomization of the GSR procedure makes the latter nearly (to within a vanishingly small additive term) minimax in the sense of
Pollak 1985
. The idea of such a randomization of the GSR procedure and a proof that the randomized GSR procedure is nearly minimax are due to
Pollak 1985
who was concerned with the discrete-time formulation of the problem. For the problem’s continuous-time formulation, the same result was established by
Polunchenko 2017b
who heavily relied on the exact closed-form formulae for
Q_{A}(x)
and
q_{A}(x)
obtained by
Polunchenko 2017c
, as well as on the quasi-stationary distribution’s first two moments, also computed by
Polunchenko 2017c
. The stopping time associated with the randomized GSR procedure is

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\big\{t\geqslant 0\colon R_{t}^{Q}=A\big\}

(6)

\displaystyle\text{ with}\;(R_{t}^{Q})_{t\geqslant 0}\;\text{as in\penalty\ \eqref{eq:Rt_r-def} except}\;R_{0}^{Q}\propto Q_{A}(x),\;\text{and}\;\inf\{\varnothing\}=+\infty,

i.e., the initial (at
t=0
) value of the “original”
R_{t}^{r}
is not a fixed number
r\geqslant 0
, but rather is a random number sampled from the quasi-stationary distribution (
4
).

3
Preliminary background on the quasi-stationary distribution

The quasi-stationary distribution’s pdf and cdf defined by (
4
) can both be expressed analytically and in closed form; see
Polunchenko 2017c
. We now recall the expressions, as they will be key to establishing our main results in the next section. Specifically, it can be deduced from
(
Polunchenko 2017c
, Theorem 3.1)
that if
A>0
is fixed and
\lambda\equiv\lambda_{A}>0
is the smallest (positive) solution of the equation

\displaystyle W_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(\lambda)}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(9)

where

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{1-8\lambda}\;\;\text{so that}\;\;\lambda=\dfrac{1}{8}\left(1-\big[\xi(\lambda)\big]^{2}\right),

(10)

then the quasi-stationary pdf is given by

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{x}}\,\dfrac{1}{x}\,W_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(\lambda)}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)}{e^{-\tfrac{1}{A}}\,W_{0,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(\lambda)}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}},

(11)

and the respective cdf is given either by

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\;\text{if $x\geqslant A$;}\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{x}}\,W_{0,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(\lambda)}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)}{e^{-\tfrac{1}{A}}\,W_{0,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(\lambda)}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)},&\;\text{if $x\in[0,A)$;}\\[22.76219pt]
0,&\;\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(12)

or, equivalently, by

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\;\text{if $x\geqslant A$;}\\[5.69054pt]
\sqrt{\dfrac{A}{x}}\,\dfrac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{x}}\,K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(\lambda)}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)}{e^{-\tfrac{1}{A}}\,K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(\lambda)}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)},&\;\text{if $x\in[0,A)$;}\\[22.76219pt]
0,&\;\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(13)

because

\displaystyle W_{0,b}(z)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\dfrac{z}{\pi}}\,K_{b}\left(\dfrac{z}{2}\right),

(14)

which is
(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964

. Formula (

(

The pdf

is a “singularity-free”, bounded function in both

Remark 2

The definition (
10
) of
\xi(\lambda)
can actually be changed to
\xi(\lambda)\coloneqq-\sqrt{1-8\lambda}
with no effect whatsoever on either equation (
9
), or formulae (

) and (

. This was previously pointed out by

) and (

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{A}+\dfrac{1}{A(1+A)}

\displaystyle<\lambda_{A}<\dfrac{1}{A}+\dfrac{1+\sqrt{4A+1}}{2A^{2}},\;\;\text{for any}\;\;A>0,

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|Q_{A}(x)-H(x)\right|=O\left(\dfrac{\log(A)}{A}\right),

(16)

\displaystyle\quad\text{as}\;A\to+\infty,

i.e.,
Q_{A}(x)
converges to
H(x)
uniformly in
x
, as
A\to+\infty
. The proof makes use of the double-inequality (
15
) and formula (
13
), along with certain latest monotonicity properties of the Bessel
K
function and functionals thereof.

4
Bounds for the quasi-stationary distribution

We are now in a position to start deriving our bounds for the quasi-stationary distribution. To that end, formula (
11
) for the pdf
q_{A}(x)
is poorly suited for our purposes: the Whittaker
W
function involved in (
11
) is a somewhat exotic special function (with
1
as the first index, anyway), and has not received much attention in the literature. Nevertheless, it
is
possible to rid (
11
) of the Whittaker
W
function, and instead express
q_{A}(x)
entirely in terms of the modified Bessel
K
function—a much more extensively studied special function. Better yet, alternative, well-suited for our purposes expressions for
q_{A}(x)
are many. For example, the pdf
q_{A}(x)
is known to satisfy two second-order ordinary differential equations: one called the Kolmogorov forward and the other called the Kolmogorov backward equation. The equations are related, and either one alone, subject to the appropriate boundary and regularity conditions, uniquely defines
q_{A}(x)
. The boundary condition is
q_{A}(A)=0
, which is a Dirichlet-type condition effectively saying that the GSR process is “killed” at
x=A
. The regularity condition is that
q_{A}(x)
as a pdf must integrate to unity over
[0,A]
. Formula (
11
) for
q_{A}(x)
was arrived at by
Polunchenko 2017c
by solving explicitly the forward equation, which is

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}\left[y^{2}q_{A}(y)\right]-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\left[q_{A}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=-\lambda q_{A}(y),\;y\in[0,A],\;A>0,

where
\lambda\equiv\lambda_{A}
is determined by (
9
). If we now integrate this equation with respect to
y
from 0 up through
x\in[0,A]
, then we will get

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}\left\{Q_{A}(x)-\lambda\int_{0}^{x}Q_{A}(t)\,dt\right\}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}},\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

(17)

or equivalently

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}\left\{Q_{A}(x)-1+\lambda\int_{x}^{A}Q_{A}(t)\,dt\right\}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}},\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

(18)

inasmuch as
q_{A}(A)=0
and
Q_{A}(A)=1
, for any
A>0
, due to (
9
), (
11
), and (
12
).

Parenthetically, we note the curious identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}Q_{A}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{A}},\;A>0,

(19)

which comes directly from (
17
) with
x=A
, because again
q_{A}(A)=0
and
Q_{A}(A)=1
for any
A>0
. Identity (
19
) is noteworthy because, with its help, any lower- or upper-bound for
Q_{A}(x)
can be converted into an upper- or, respectively, a lower-bound for
\lambda
, and the behavior of
\lambda_{A}
as a function of
A>0
is of importance in quickest change-point detection; see, e.g.,
Polunchenko 2017b
. For example, by virtue of (
19
), one can get the trivial inequality
\lambda>1/A
,
A>0
, from the trivial bound
Q_{A}(x)\leqslant 1
valid for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and any
A>0
. Getting sharper bounds, such as (
15
) or even better, is also possible, and this question will be explored in the next section.

Now, with virtually no effort one can get a pair of lower- and a pair of upper-bounds for
q_{A}(x)
directly from (
17
) and (
18
). Specifically, since
Q_{A}(x)
is strictly increasing in
x
for any
A>0
, and such that
0\leqslant Q_{A}(x)\leqslant 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
for any
A>0
, from (
17
) we get

\displaystyle l_{A}^{(1)}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}Q_{A}(x)[1-\lambda x]{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}\leqslant q_{A}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

(20)

but

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}Q_{A}(x){\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}\eqqcolon u_{A}^{(1)}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

(21)

and, likewise, from (
18
) we get

\displaystyle l_{A}^{(2)}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}\left[Q_{A}(x)(1+\lambda A-\lambda x)-1\right]{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}\leqslant q_{A}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

but

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}\left[Q_{A}(x)+\lambda A-1-\lambda x\right]{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}\eqqcolon u_{A}^{(2)}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0.

The pair
(l_{A}^{(1)}(x),u_{A}^{(1)}(x))
and the pair
(l_{A}^{(2)}(x),u_{A}^{(2)}(x))
complement each other. Specifically, on the one hand, for the first pair we have
\lim_{x\to 0+}\left[l_{A}^{(1)}(x)\right]=\lim_{x\to 0+}\left[u_{A}^{(1)}(x)\right]=0
, for any fixed
A>0
, but
l_{A}^{(1)}(A)=2(1-\lambda A)/A^{2}<0
, due to the left half of (
15
), and
u_{A}^{(1)}(A)=2/A^{2}>0
, for any
A>0
. That is, the bounds
l_{A}^{(1)}(x)
and
u_{A}^{(1)}(x)
are loose if
x
is sufficiently close to
A
, although note that
\lim_{A\to+\infty}\left[l_{A}^{(1)}(A)\right]=0
and
\lim_{A\to+\infty}\left[u_{A}^{(1)}(A)\right]=0
, because of (
15
). On the other hand, by contrast, for the second pair of bounds we have
l_{A}^{(2)}(A)=u_{A}^{(2)}(A)=0
for any
A>0
, but
\lim_{x\to 0+}\left[l_{A}^{(2)}(x)\right]=-\infty
and
\lim_{x\to 0+}\left[u_{A}^{(2)}(x)\right]=+\infty
, for any fixed
A>0
. That is, the bounds
l_{A}^{(2)}(x)
and
u_{A}^{(2)}(x)
are (too) loose if
x
is close to
0
, no matter whether
A
is small or large. This suggests that one can consider

\displaystyle\max\left\{l_{A}^{(1)}(x),l_{A}^{(2)}(x)\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant q_{A}(x)\leqslant\min\left\{u_{A}^{(1)}(x),u_{A}^{(2)}(x)\right\},\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

although, for simplicity’s sake, we shall just stick to
l_{A}^{(1)}(x)
and
u_{A}^{(1)}(x)
, which, unlike
l_{A}^{(2)}(x)
and
u_{A}^{(2)}(x)
, are singularity-free, and get sharp for all
x
asymptotically, as
A\to+\infty
.

The lowerbound

and the upperbound

We have seen numerical evidence that the lowerbound
l_{A}^{(1)}(x)
and the upperbound
u_{A}^{(1)}(x)
are both tight bounds. They are also tight in a more formal sense, namely in the sense that

\displaystyle\lim_{A\to+\infty}\left[l_{A}^{(1)}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{A\to+\infty}\left[u_{A}^{(1)}(x)\right]=h(x),\;\text{pointwise, for each fixed $x\in\mathbb{R}$},

where
h(x)
is the stationary distribution’s pdf (
2
)–(
3
); here we used (
16
) and (
15
). Put another way, we see that
q_{A}(x)
converges pointwise to
h(x)
, as
A\to+\infty
.

The pair
(l_{A}^{(1)}(x),u_{A}^{(1)}(x))
can be used to lower- and upper-bound the cdf
Q_{A}(x)
. For example, inequality (
21
) may be rewritten as

\displaystyle\dfrac{q_{A}(x)}{Q_{A}(x)}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\log[Q_{A}(x)]

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{2}{x^{2}},\;\;\text{so that}\;\;\int_{x}^{A}\partial_{t}\log[Q_{A}(t)]\leqslant 2\int_{x}^{A}\dfrac{dt}{t^{2}},

whence

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\eqqcolon L_{A}^{(1)}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

(22)

which is a lowerbound for
Q_{A}(x)
that was recently obtained by
Li and Polunchenko 2020
via a different argument. However, the argument used by
Li and Polunchenko 2020
to get the lowerbound
L_{A}^{(1)}(x)
required that
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
. We can now see that that requirement can be lifted. Moreover, the bound
L_{A}^{(1)}(x)
is a cdf in itself, with the corresponding density supported on
[0,A]
.

Likewise, the lowerbound
l_{A}^{(1)}(x)
can be used to get a new upperbound for
Q_{A}(x)
. Specifically, from (
20
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{A}\partial_{t}\log[Q_{A}(t)]

\displaystyle\geqslant-\dfrac{2}{A}+\dfrac{2}{x}-2\log\left(\dfrac{A}{x}\right),

whence

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\left(\dfrac{A}{x}\right)^{2\lambda}\eqqcolon U_{A}^{(1)}(x),\;x\in[0,A],\;A>0.

(23)

Since for any
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
we have
\xi\coloneqq\sqrt{1-8\lambda}\leqslant 1-4\lambda
, so that
1-\xi\geqslant 4\lambda
, we can conclude that the new upperbound
U_{A}^{(1)}(x)
is tighter than the upperbound

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\left(\dfrac{A}{x}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi},\;\;x\in[0,A],\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

previously obtained by
Li and Polunchenko 2020
via a different argument. Moreover, the new upperbound
U_{A}^{(1)}(x)
is valid for
all

A>0
, whereas the proof of the old upperbound due to
Li and Polunchenko 2020
does not go through unless
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
.

Now, let us try to get yet another representation for
q_{A}(x)
, different from (
17
) and (
18
), but again with no other special functions involved other than the modified Bessel
K
function. Such a representation will then allow us to appeal to some of the latest monotonicity properties of the modified Bessel
K
function and functionals thereof, and establish new bounds for
q_{A}(x)
. First recall the identity

\displaystyle W_{\varkappa+1,b}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{z}{2}-\varkappa\right)W_{\varkappa,b}(z)-z\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}W_{\varkappa,b}(z)\right];

cf., e.g.,
(
Slater 1960
, Identity (2.4.24), p. 25)
. Hence

\displaystyle W_{1,b}(x)

\displaystyle=x\left\{\dfrac{1}{2}W_{0,b}(x)-\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}W_{0,b}(x)\right]\right\},

which, on account of (
14
), can subsequently be brought to the form

\displaystyle W_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi x}}\left\{\left(1-\dfrac{x}{2}\right)K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)-\left.\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}(u)\right]\right|_{u=\tfrac{1}{x}}\right\}.

(24)

To proceed, we appeal to the derivative formulae

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}K_{b}(z)

\displaystyle=-K_{b-1}(z)-\dfrac{b}{z}K_{b}(z)=-K_{b+1}(z)+\dfrac{b}{z}K_{b}(z),

(25)

given, e.g., by
(
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2014
, Identities 8.486.12 and 8.486.13, p. 938)
. By substituting (
25
) back over into (
24
) we arrive at two additional equivalent expressions for
W_{1,\xi/2}(2/x)
in terms of the Bessel
K
function:

\displaystyle W_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi x}}\left\{\left[1-\dfrac{x}{2}\left(1-\xi\right)\right]K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)+K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)\right\},

(26)

\displaystyle W_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi x}}\left\{\left[1-\dfrac{x}{2}\left(1+\xi\right)\right]K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)+K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi+1}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)\right\}.

(27)

We hasten to note the identities

\displaystyle\left.\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}(u)\right]\right|_{u=\tfrac{1}{A}}

\displaystyle=\left[1-\dfrac{A}{2}\right]K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right),

\displaystyle K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{A}{2}(1-\xi)-1\right]K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right),

(28)

\displaystyle K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi+1}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{A}{2}(1+\xi)-1\right]K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right),

(29)

which follow at once from (
24
), (
26
), and (
27
), respectively, on account of (
9
); all three hold for
any

A>0
. Also, identity (
28
) and identity (
29
) are the effectively equivalent to one another, because the Bessel
K
function is an even function of its index.

If we now substitute (
24
), (
26
), and (
27
) back into (
11
) we will get the following three equivalent expressions for
q_{A}(x)
, all in terms of the modified Bessel
K
function:

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}\left\{\left[1-\dfrac{x}{2}\right]Q_{A}(x)-\dfrac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{x}}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi x}}\,\left.\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}(u)\right]\right|_{u=\tfrac{1}{x}}}{e^{-\tfrac{1}{A}}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi A}}\,K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)}\right\}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}\left\{\left[1-\dfrac{x}{2}(1-\xi)\right]Q_{A}(x)+\dfrac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{x}}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi x}}\,K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)}{e^{-\tfrac{1}{A}}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi A}}\,K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)}\right\}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}\left\{\left[1-\dfrac{x}{2}(1+\xi)\right]Q_{A}(x)+\dfrac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{x}}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi x}}\,K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi+1}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)}{e^{-\tfrac{1}{A}}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi A}}\,K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)}\right\}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}.

The plan now is to use the foregoing three expressions for
q_{A}(x)
in conjunction with certain recently established monotonicity properties of the modified Bessel
K
function and its functions to get new bounds for
q_{A}(x)
. To that end, consider first
(
Yang and Zheng 2017
, Proposition 4.5, p. 2957)
whereby the function

\displaystyle f_{1}(x;b)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dfrac{x}{1+x}\dfrac{K_{b+1}(x)}{K_{b}(x)}

is strictly increasing in
x
on
(0,+\infty)
for any

. This gives

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1+x}\left[K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi+1}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)\left/K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)\right.\right]

\displaystyle>\dfrac{1}{1+A}\left[K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi+1}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)\left/K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{A}\right)\right.\right],

\displaystyle\qquad x\in(0,A),\;\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

because
\xi\in[0,1]
for
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
.

After some elementary algebra this translates to

\displaystyle l_{A}^{(3)}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}Q_{A}(x)\dfrac{3+\xi}{4}\dfrac{A-x}{A+1}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}\leqslant q_{A}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A\geqslant\tilde{A}.

\displaystyle f_{2}(x;b)

\displaystyle\left[K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)\left/K_{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)\right.\right]

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

To draw a line under this section we point out that one can “iterate” either formula (
17
) or formula (
18
) to obtain new, even tighter, but more complicated, bounds for
q_{A}(x)
; these bounds, in turn, can then be integrated, of course, and thereby be “converted” into bounds for
Q_{A}(x)
. For example, from (
22
) plugged under the integral on the right of (
17
), we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{x}Q_{A}(t)\,dt&=Q_{A}(x)\int_{0}^{x}\dfrac{Q_{A}(t)}{Q_{A}(x)}\,dt\geqslant Q_{A}(x)e^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\tfrac{2}{t}}dt\\
&=Q_{A}(x)\left\{x-2e^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)\right\},\;x\in[0,A],\;A>0,\end{split}

whence

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}Q_{A}(x)\left\{1-\lambda\left[x-2e^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)\right]\right\}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}\eqqcolon u_{A}^{(6)}(x),

(32)

\displaystyle\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

and it is a very sharp upperbound for
q_{A}(x)
; recall that
\E 1(x)
here denotes the exponential integral function (
7
). By virtue of integration, it can be translated into the lowerbound for
Q_{A}(x)
. Specifically, we find:

\displaystyle L_{A}^{(6)}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\exp\left\{2\lambda\left[e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)-e^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)\right]\right\}\leqslant Q_{A}(x),

\displaystyle\qquad x\in[0,A],\;A>0,

which is also a very tight lowerbound for
Q_{A}(x)
, clearly tighter than the lowerbound given by (
22
). It is also worth pointing out that
L_{A}^{(6)}(x)
is a cdf in itself, with the corresponding density’s support being the interval
[0,A]
.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{Q_{A}(x_{1})}{Q_{A}(x_{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{x_{2}}}e^{-\tfrac{2}{x_{1}}}x_{2}^{\tfrac{2}{A}}x_{1}^{-\tfrac{2}{A}},\;0\leqslant x_{1}\leqslant x_{2}\leqslant A,\;A>0,

and thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{x}Q_{A}(t)\,dt&=Q_{A}(x)\int_{0}^{x}\dfrac{Q_{A}(t)}{Q_{A}(x)}\,dt\leqslant Q_{A}(x)e^{\tfrac{2}{x}}x^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\tfrac{2}{t}}t^{-\tfrac{2}{A}}dt\\
&=2Q_{A}(x)e^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{x}\right),\;x\in[0,A],\;A>0,\end{split}

so that

\displaystyle l_{A}^{(6)}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}Q_{A}(x)\left\{1-2\lambda e^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{x}\right)\right\}{\One_{\left\{x\in[0,A]\right\}}}\leqslant q_{A}(x),

\displaystyle\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;A>0,

which is a very sharp lowerbound for
q_{A}(x)
; its sharpness though is offset by the presence of a special function, namely the incomplete Gamma function.

Translating the lowerbound
l_{A}^{(6)}(x)
into an upperbound for
Q_{A}(x)
is somewhat problematic, because
l_{A}^{(6)}(x)
is difficult to integrate with respect to
x
, for

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\log[Q_{A}(x)]&\geqslant\dfrac{2}{x}-\dfrac{2}{A}-4\lambda\int_{x}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{u}}\left(\dfrac{u}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{u}\right)\dfrac{du}{u^{2}}\\
&=\dfrac{2}{x}-\dfrac{2}{A}-2\lambda\int_{\tfrac{2}{A}}^{\tfrac{2}{x}}e^{t}\,t^{-\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},t\right)dt.\end{split}

One way to handle the integral of the incomplete Gamma function is to use the integral representation

\displaystyle e^{t}\,t^{-a}\Gamma(a-1,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{e^{-ty}}{(1+y)^{2-a}}dy,

so that we readily get

\displaystyle\int_{\tfrac{2}{A}}^{\tfrac{2}{x}}e^{t}\,t^{-\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},t\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\tfrac{2}{A}}^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\dfrac{e^{-ty}}{t}dt\right)\dfrac{dy}{(1+y)^{2-\tfrac{2}{A}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}y\right)-\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{x}y\right)\right]\dfrac{dy}{(1+y)^{2-\tfrac{2}{A}}}.

Another option is to use the integral representation

\displaystyle e^{t}\,t^{-a}\Gamma(a-1,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{t\Gamma(2-a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{y^{1-a}e^{-y}}{y+t}dy,

whence

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\tfrac{2}{A}}^{\tfrac{2}{x}}e^{t}\,t^{-\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},t\right)dt&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\tfrac{2}{A}}^{\tfrac{2}{x}}\dfrac{dt}{t(t+y)}\right)y^{1-\tfrac{2}{A}}e^{-y}dy\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\left(\dfrac{1+\tfrac{A}{2}y}{1+\tfrac{x}{2}y}\right)e^{-y}\dfrac{dy}{y^{\tfrac{2}{A}}}.\end{split}

We therefore arrive at the inequality

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\exp\left\{2\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}y\right)-\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{x}y\right)\right]\dfrac{dy}{(1+y)^{2-\tfrac{2}{A}}}\right\}\eqqcolon U_{A}^{(6)}(x),

\displaystyle\qquad x\in[0,A],\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

or equivalently

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\exp\left\{2\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\left(\dfrac{1+\tfrac{A}{2}y}{1+\tfrac{x}{2}y}\right)e^{-y}\dfrac{dy}{y^{\tfrac{2}{A}}}\right\}\eqqcolon U_{A}^{(6)}(x),

\displaystyle\qquad x\in[0,A],\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

and
U_{A}^{(6)}(x)
is a very sharp upperbound, but is not as simple as (
31
).

We presented quite a few lower- and upper-bounds for
q_{A}(x)
as well as for
Q_{A}(x)
. The bounds vary in their complexity and tightness (accuracy): those that are more complex are more tight, and those that are simpler are less accurate. A good compromise, in our opinion, is bounds
l^{(5)}(x)
and
u^{(1)}(x)
for
q_{A}(x)
and bounds
L^{(1)}(x)
and
U^{(5)}(x)
for
Q_{A}(x)
.

5
Discussion

We now illustrate a few applications of the bounds obtained in the previous section for the quasi-stationary pdf
q_{A}(x)
and cdf
Q_{A}(x)
.

Let us first try to use our bounds to quantify the difference between
Q_{A}(x)
and
H(x)
for
x\in(0,A)
with
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
; otherwise, for
x\leqslant 0
we have
Q_{A}(x)=H(x)=0
for any
A>0
, and for
x\geqslant A
we have
Q_{A}(x)=1
for any
A>0
, so
Q_{A}(x)-H(x)=1-e^{-\tfrac{2}{x}}\leqslant 1-e^{-\tfrac{2}{A}}
.

We start with the observation that

\displaystyle Q_{A}(x)-H(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\left(\dfrac{A}{x}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}-H(x)\eqqcolon g(x;A),\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

which is an immediate consequence of (
31
). Fix
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
, restrict
x
to the interval
(0,A)
, and consider the function
g(x;A)
,
which is obviously a bounded and smooth function on
x\in(0,A)
. The first derivative of
g(x;A)
with respect to
x
is

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}g(x;A)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}g(x;A)-\dfrac{2}{Ax}e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x)\left(\dfrac{A}{x}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}},

and it vanishes at
x_{A}^{*}\in(0,A)
that solves the equation

\displaystyle e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\left(\dfrac{A}{x_{A}^{*}}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\left(1-\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=1.

(33)

Equation (
33
) clearly has a unique solution, contained in the interval between 0 and
A
. To see this, fix
A
and vary
x
from
0
up through
A
, and note that, on the on hand, the function
x\mapsto e^{2/A}(A/x)^{2/A}
is strictly decreasing from
+\infty
down to
e^{2/A}
, while, on the other hand, the function
x\mapsto 1/(1-x/A)
is strictly increasing from 1 up to
+\infty
. The two functions definitely “meet” exactly once, at some point
x_{A}^{*}
inside the interval
(0,A)
. Moreover, it is also clear that the function
g(x;A)
is maximized at
x_{A}^{*}\in(0,A)
.

The exact solution
x_{A}^{*}
to equation (
33
) is not possible to find analytically, due to the transcendental nature of the equation. However, it is not too difficult to upperbound
x_{A}^{*}
via an elementary function of
A
. Specifically, since equation (
33
) is equivalent to the equation

\displaystyle\dfrac{2}{A}+\dfrac{2}{A}\log(A)-\dfrac{2}{A}\log(x_{A}^{*})+\log\left(1-\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

and
1-1/x\leqslant\log(x)\leqslant x-1
for
x>0
, and
\log(1-x)\leqslant-x
for
x\in(0,1)
, we find

\displaystyle\log(x_{A}^{*})

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-\dfrac{1}{x_{A}^{*}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\log\left(1-\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A}\right)\leqslant-\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A},

so that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{2}{A}+\dfrac{2}{A}\log(A)-\dfrac{2}{A}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{x_{A}^{*}}\right)-\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A},

whence

\displaystyle(x_{A}^{*})^{2}-2x_{A}^{*}\log(A)-2

\displaystyle\leqslant 0,

and thus

\displaystyle x_{A}^{*}

\displaystyle\leqslant\log(A)+\sqrt{\log^{2}(A)+2}\leqslant 2\log(A)+\dfrac{1}{\log(A)},

because
\sqrt{1-x}\leqslant 1-x/2
for
x\geqslant-1
.

Now, from (
33
) we see that

\displaystyle g(x_{A}^{*};A)

\displaystyle=e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}H(x_{A}^{*})\left(\dfrac{A}{x_{A}^{*}}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}-H(x_{A}^{*})=H(x_{A}^{*})\left(1-\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A}\right)^{-1}-H(x_{A}^{*})

\displaystyle=H(x_{A}^{*})\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A-x_{A}^{*}}\leqslant\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A-x_{A}^{*}}.

We can now claim that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in(0,A)}[Q_{A}(x)-H(x)]

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{x_{A}^{*}}{A-x_{A}^{*}}\leqslant\dfrac{2\log(A)+1/\log(A)}{A-2\log(A)-1/\log(A)}=O\left(\dfrac{\log(A)}{A}\right),

which is consistent with (
16
) proved by
Li and Polunchenko 2020
. By “consistent” we mean that
Li and Polunchenko 2020
merely showed the rate of convergence, i.e., the “
\log(A)/A
” under the “big Oh” symbol, but did not quantify the rate any more specifically. Here we fill in that void.

Next, let us try to use our bounds for
q_{A}(x)
and
Q_{A}(x)
to get a new lowerbound for
\lambda
. To that end, from (
19
) and (
31
) we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{A}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{A}Q_{A}(t)\,dt\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\int_{0}^{A}H(x)\left(\dfrac{A}{x}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}dx=2\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right),

where
\Gamma(a,z)
denotes the incomplete (upper) Gamma function. This gives at once

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\geqslant\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}e^{-\tfrac{2}{A}}\left\{\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right)\right\}^{-1},

which is a very sharp bound, but it has a special function involved in it.

To upperbound
\Gamma(a,z)
we turn to
(
Pinelis 2020
, Proposition 2.11)
, i.e., the inequality

\displaystyle\Gamma(a,x)

\displaystyle<e^{-x}\dfrac{x^{a}(1-a+x)}{(x-a)^{2}-a+2x},\;a<0,\;x>0,

which gives

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{A}{A+1}\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}-1}e^{-\tfrac{2}{A}},

and thus we arrive at the lowerbound

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{A}+\dfrac{1}{A^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda,

which is clearly tighter than the left half of (
15
).

From the lowerbound (
30
) we find

\displaystyle\dfrac{x^{2}}{2}q_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant Q_{A}(x)\left(1-\dfrac{x}{A}\right),\;x\in[0,A],\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

so that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{A}x^{2}q_{A}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{0}^{A}Q_{A}(x)dx-\dfrac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{A}xQ_{A}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{A}Q_{A}(x)dx-\dfrac{1}{A}\left(\dfrac{A^{2}}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{A}x^{2}q_{A}(x)dx\right),

whence

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}x^{2}q_{A}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\dfrac{A(2-\lambda A)}{\lambda(A-1)},\;A\geqslant\tilde{A}.

Now, from
Polunchenko 2017c
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}x^{2}q_{A}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\lambda^{2}A^{2}-2\lambda A+2}{\lambda+\lambda^{2}},

so that we finally arrive at
\lambda^{2}A^{3}-\lambda A^{2}-2\geqslant 0
, whence

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\geqslant\dfrac{1+\sqrt{1+8/A}}{2A}\geqslant\dfrac{1}{A}+\dfrac{2}{A^{2}}\left(1-\dfrac{2}{A}\right),\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

which is tighter than our earlier result because
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
.

Finally, from the upperbound (
32
) and the fact that
q_{A}(A)=0
we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{A}\left\{1-\dfrac{A}{2}e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)\right\}^{-1},

which is a reasonably tight upperbound for
\lambda
.

5.1
Application to Quickest Change-Point Detection

Another important application of our bounds is new bounds for the delay exhibited by the Randomized Shiryaev–Roberts–Pollak (SRP) change-point detection method. Recall that the latter is identified with the stopping time
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q}
given by (
6
).

The worst-possible average detection delay exhibited by
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q}
is given by

\displaystyle\SADD(\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q})

\displaystyle=2\left\{e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)-\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)q_{A}(t)\,dt\right\},\;A>0;

cf.
Feinberg and Shiryaev 2006
. It was shown by
Polunchenko 2017b
that the delay can also be expressed as

\displaystyle\SADD(\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q})

\displaystyle=2\left\{e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)-1+2\lambda\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)Q_{A}(t)\dfrac{dt}{t}\right\},\;A>0,

where
Q_{A}(x)
is as in (
12
) or as in (
13
), and
\lambda
is determined by (
9
).

From (
31
) we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)Q_{A}(t)\dfrac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\int_{0}^{A}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\dfrac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{A}{2}e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)+e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}+1}\Gamma\left(\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right),

because, on account of (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[\dfrac{x^{\varkappa+1}}{\varkappa+1}\E 1(x)\right]

\displaystyle=x^{\varkappa}\E 1(x)-\dfrac{x^{\varkappa}}{\varkappa+1}e^{-x},\;\varkappa\neq-1,

and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\dfrac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tfrac{2}{A}}^{+\infty}u^{\tfrac{2}{A}-1}\E 1(u)\,du

\displaystyle=-\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}-1}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)+\dfrac{A}{2}\Gamma\left(\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right),

for
\lim_{x\to 0}[x^{a}\E 1(x)]=0
,
a>0
, as given, e.g., by
(
Geller and Ng 1969
, Entry 3.5.3, p. 193)
.

We thus have (for
A\geqslant\tilde{A}
)

\displaystyle\SADD(\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\{\lambda A\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right)-(\lambda A-1)e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)-1\right\},

which is a very tight upperbound for
\SADD(\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q})
, but it has the incomplete Gamma function in it. From the recurrence

\displaystyle\Gamma(a+1,x)

\displaystyle=a\Gamma(a,x)+x^{a}e^{-x}

we find

\displaystyle\left(1-\dfrac{2}{A}\right)\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-\tfrac{2}{A}}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)^{-1+\tfrac{2}{A}}-\Gamma\left(\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right),

whence

\displaystyle e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\Gamma\left(-1+\dfrac{2}{A},\dfrac{2}{A}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{\lambda A-1}{2\lambda}+\dfrac{1}{\lambda A},

and

\displaystyle\SADD(\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2(\lambda A-1)\left\{\dfrac{A}{2}-e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)\right\},\;A>\tilde{A},

which is a reasonably tight bound, free of the incomplete Gamma function.

Now, from (
30
) we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)q_{A}(t)dt

\displaystyle\geqslant 2\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)Q_{A}(t)\dfrac{dt}{t^{2}}-\dfrac{2}{A}\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)Q_{A}(t)\dfrac{dt}{t}

whence

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)Q_{A}(t)\dfrac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{2\lambda A}{\lambda A-1}\left(1-2\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)Q_{A}(t)\dfrac{dt}{t^{2}}\right).

On account of (
22
) we further obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)Q_{A}(t)\dfrac{dt}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\int_{0}^{A}e^{\tfrac{2}{t}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)H(t)\dfrac{dt}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\int_{0}^{A}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{t}\right)\dfrac{dt}{t^{2}}=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{A}\,e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right).

Thus we arrive at

\displaystyle\SADD(\mathcal{S}_{A}^{Q})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\{e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)-1+\dfrac{2\lambda}{\lambda A-1}e^{\tfrac{2}{A}}\E 1\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)\right\},\;A\geqslant\tilde{A},

which is a reasonably tight bound as well, but not as tight as the bound we obtained earlier.

6
Concluding remarks

The primary goal of this work was to obtain new lower- and upper-bounds for the pdf,
q_{A}(x)
, as well as for the corresponding cdf,
Q_{A}(x)
, of the quasi-stationary distribution of the Generalized Shiryaev–Roberts (GSR) process. This is of importance in quickest change-point detection, which was the main motivation for us to initiate this work in the first place. We employed the latest monotonicity properties of the modified Bessel
K
function and its functionals to achieve the goal, and offered a host of bounds—lower- and upper-bounds—for ratios
q_{A}(x)/h(x)
and
Q_{A}(x)/H(x)
, where
h(x)
and
H(x)
are, respectively, the pdf and the cdf of the GSR process’ stationary distribution; see (
2
) and (
3
).

However, there is more to the story: it is possible to quantify the difference between
q_{A}(x)
and
h(x)
as well as that between
Q_{A}(x)
and
H(x)

directly
, i.e., obtain an exact closed-form expression for each. Specifically, the idea is to use the following identity

\displaystyle\dfrac{\pi}{8}\dfrac{\Gamma(\sqrt{s}-1/2)}{\Gamma(1+2\sqrt{s})}W_{1,\sqrt{s}}(\max\{x,y\})M_{1,\sqrt{s}}(\min\{x,y\})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\dfrac{xy}{4s-1}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}(x+y)}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\beta\sinh(\pi\beta)}{(1+4\beta^{2})(s+\beta^{2})}W_{1,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}(x)W_{1,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}(y)d\beta,

\displaystyle\qquad\text{valid for}\;\;s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)\cup\{1/4\}\;\;\text{and}\;\;x,y\geqslant 0,

where
M_{a,b}(z)
is the Whittaker
M
function, and
W_{a,b}(z)
is the Whittaker
W
function; cf.
(
Becker 2004
, Identity (52), p. 769)
. It should be noted that the case of
s=1/4
, while covered, has to be treated with care. On the one hand, the integral on the right
is
convergent for
s=1/4
; see
Becker 2004
as well as our analysis below. However, on the other hand, as
s\to 1/4
, two singularities emerge in the identity: one buried in the
\Gamma(\sqrt{s}-1/2)
factor on the left, and the other one buried in the
1/(4s-1)
factor on the right. The two are of the same order and, in the limit, as
s\to 1/4
, end up “undoing” each other, yielding a finite expression for the limiting value of the integral.

With
\sqrt{s}=\xi/2
and recalling that
\xi\in(0,1)
, the above identity takes the form

\displaystyle\dfrac{\pi}{8}\dfrac{\Gamma(\xi/2-1/2)}{\Gamma(1+\xi)}W_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)M_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\dfrac{2}{A}\dfrac{2}{x}\dfrac{1}{-8\lambda}e^{-\tfrac{1}{A}-\tfrac{1}{x}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{4\beta\sinh(\pi\beta)}{(1+4\beta^{2})(1-8\lambda+4\beta^{2})}W_{1,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)W_{1,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)d\beta,

\displaystyle\qquad x\in(0,A),\;A>\tilde{A}.

To make use of this identity we need to recall

\displaystyle\lambda A\,\Gamma\left(\dfrac{\xi-1}{2}\right)W_{0,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)\,M_{1,\tfrac{1}{2}\xi}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)=-\Gamma(\xi+1),

shown in
Polunchenko and Pepelyshev 2018
.

From this and (
3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle q_{A}(x)-h(x)

(34)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{8}{\pi}\dfrac{A\lambda}{x}e^{\tfrac{1}{A}-\tfrac{1}{x}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{4\beta\sinh(\pi\beta)}{(1+4\beta^{2})(1-8\lambda+4\beta^{2})}W_{1,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)W_{1,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)d\beta,

for any
x\in[0,A]
and
A>\tilde{A}
.

We have thus obtained an
exact
representation of the discrepancy between
q_{A}(x)
and
h(x)
valid for any fixed
x\in[0,A]
and
A>\tilde{A}
. The integral on the right is absolutely convergent for any fixed
x\in(0,A]
. This is mentioned in
Becker 2004
.

By integrating both sides of (
34
) with respect to
x
from 0 up through
t\in(0,A]
we obtain the following:

\displaystyle(0\leqslant)\;Q_{A}(x)-H(x)

(35)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{8}{\pi}A\lambda e^{\tfrac{1}{A}-\tfrac{1}{x}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{4\beta\sinh(\pi\beta)}{(1+4\beta^{2})(1-8\lambda+4\beta^{2})}W_{0,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)W_{1,\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\beta}\left(\dfrac{2}{A}\right)d\beta,

where we used the property of the Whittaker
W
function that

\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^{-x/2}W_{0,b}(x)\right)=-\dfrac{e^{-x/2}}{x}W_{1,b}(x).

Formulae (
34
) and (
35
) may lead to new bounds for
|q_{A}(x)-h(x)|
and for
(0\leqslant)\;Q_{A}(x)-H(x)=|Q_{A}(x)-H(x)|
. Getting these bounds comes down to whether or not we can tightly upperbound the integral on the right of (
34
) and that on the right of (
35
). To that end, the problem is that both integrals involve the Whittaker
W
function, which is a somewhat exotic function in the sense that its properties are not as well understood as those of the modified Bessel
K
function.
Hence, using (
34
) and (
35
) to get new bounds for
|q_{A}(x)-h(x)|
and for
(0\leqslant)\;Q_{A}(x)-H(x)=|Q_{A}(x)-H(x)|
will likely prove to be a challenge. Nonetheless, it does seem worthwhile, and, if and once solved, the results will be published in a separate paper.

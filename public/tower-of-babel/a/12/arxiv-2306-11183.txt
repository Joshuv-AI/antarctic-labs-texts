Closed formulas for the factorization of X n − 1 X^{n}-1 , the n n -th cyclotomic polynomial, X n − a X^{n}-a and f ⁡ ( X n ) f(X^{n}) over a finite field for arbitrary positive integers n n
Anna-Maurin Graner University of Rostock, Germany amg.research@posteo.com

Abstract
The factorizations of the polynomial X n − 1 X^{n}-1 and the cyclotomic polynomial Φ n \Phi_{n} over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} have been studied for a very long time. Explicit factorizations have been given for the case that rad ⁡ ( n ) | q w − 1 \rad(n)\mid q^{w}-1 where w = 1 w=1 , w w is prime or w w is the product of two primes. For arbitrary a ∈ 𝔽 q ∗ a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast} the factorization of the polynomial X n − a X^{n}-a is needed for the construction of constacyclic codes. Its factorization has been determined for the case rad ⁡ ( n ) | q − 1 \rad(n)\mid q-1 and for the case that there exist at most three distinct prime factors of n n and rad ⁡ ( n ) | q w − 1 \rad(n)\mid q^{w}-1 for a prime w w . Both polynomials X n − 1 X^{n}-1 and X n − a X^{n}-a are compositions of the form f ⁡ ( X n ) f(X^{n}) for a monic irreducible polynomial f ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X] . The factorization of the composition f ⁡ ( X n ) f(X^{n}) is known for the case gcd ⁡ ( n , ord ⁡ ( f ) ⋅ deg ⁡ ( f ) ) = 1 \gcd(n,\mathrm{ord}(f)\cdot\deg(f))=1 and rad ⁡ ( n ) | q w − 1 \rad(n)\mid q^{w}-1 for w = 1 w=1 or w w prime. However, there does not exist a closed formula for the explicit factorization of either X n − 1 X^{n}-1 , the cyclotomic polynomial Φ n \Phi_{n} , the binomial X n − a X^{n}-a or the composition f ⁡ ( X n ) f(X^{n}) . Without loss of generality we can assume that gcd ⁡ ( n , q ) = 1 \gcd(n,q)=1 . Our main theorem, Theorem 18 , is a closed formula for the factorization of X n − a X^{n}-a over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} for any a ∈ 𝔽 q ∗ a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast} and any positive integer n n such that gcd ⁡ ( n , q ) = 1 \gcd(n,q)=1 . From our main theorem we derive one closed formula each for the factorization of X n − 1 X^{n}-1 and of the n n -th cyclotomic polynomial Φ n \Phi_{n} for any positive integer n n such that gcd ⁡ ( n , q ) = 1 \gcd(n,q)=1 ( 2.5 and 2.6 ). Furthermore, our main theorem yields a closed formula for the factorization of the composition f ⁡ ( X n ) f(X^{n}) for any irreducible polynomial f ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X] , f ≠ X f\neq X , and any positive integer n n such that gcd ⁡ ( n , q ) = 1 \gcd(n,q)=1 ( Theorem 27 ).

Factorization, irreducible polynomials, composition, constacyclic codes, cyclic codes, cyclotomic polynomials,
X^{n}-1

are distinct primes (see
[
Rak+22
]
). In this section we present the explicit factorization of
X^{n}-a
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any positive integer
n
.

Remark 2.1
.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
\gcd(n,q)=1
. Indeed, if
\gcd(n,q)>1
, then
n=\tilde{n}\cdot\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{l}
for positive integers
\tilde{n},l
. Then there exists an element
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b^{\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{l}}=a
and the polynomial
X^{n}-a
equals
(X^{\tilde{n}}-b)^{\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{l}}
.

We prove our results by induction on the number of (not necessarily distinct) prime factors of
n
. As base case we study the polynomial
X^{p}-a
for a prime
p
such that
\gcd(p,q)=1
. Then there exists an element
b\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
such that
b^{p}=a
and the polynomial
X^{p}-a
splits as
\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(X-\zeta_{p}^{j}b)
, where
\zeta_{p}
is a
p
-th primitive root of unity over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By
Theorem

3
, the factorization of
X^{p}-a
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
depends on the degree of the coefficients of
(X-\zeta_{p}^{j}b)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q}\left(X-\zeta_{p}^{j}b\right)=\ord_{\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}b)}(q)
, it is determined by the order of the roots
\zeta_{p}^{j}b
. The following proposition specifies this order for the two cases
p\mid\ord(a)
and
p\nmid\ord(a)
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
and
p
prime such that
\gcd(p,q)=1
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If
p\nmid\ord(a)
, then there exists a positive integer
r
such that
(a^{r})^{p}=a
and
\ord(a^{r})=\ord(a)
.
If
a=1
, then
r=1
, else
r
satisfies
rp\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(a)}
.
Furthermore,
\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}a^{r})=p\cdot\ord(a)
for all
1\leq j\leq p-1
.

(ii)

If
p\mid\ord(a)
, then every root
b\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of the polynomial
X^{p}-a
has order
\ord(b)=p\cdot\ord(a)
.

Proof.

Note that for any element
b\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
such that
b^{p}=a
, the order of
b
satisfies

\ord(b)=\ord(a)\cdot\gcd(\ord(b),p)\in\{\ord(a),\ord(a)\cdot p\}.

(1)

(i)

If
p\nmid\ord(a)

and
\ord(a)>1
,
then there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
rp\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(a)}
and the element
a^{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfies
(a^{r})^{p}=a
.
If
a=1
, then obviously
(a^{1})^{p}=a
and we set
r=1
.
Since
a^{r}
is an element of the multiplicative group
\langle a\rangle\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
, its order divides
\ord(a)
. This fact combined with
eq.

1
yields that
\ord(a^{r})=\ord(a)
. For every
1\leq j\leq p-1
, the element
\zeta_{p}^{j}
has order
p
, which is coprime with
\ord(a)
. Therefore, we can conclude that
\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}a^{r})=p\cdot\ord(a)
for
1\leq j\leq p-1
.

(ii)

If
p\mid\ord(a)
, then with
eq.

1
also
p\mid\ord(b)
. Suppose that
\ord(b)=\ord(a)
. With the fact that
a=b^{p}
, we know that
\ord(a)=\frac{\ord(b)}{\gcd(\ord(b),p)}=\frac{\ord(b)}{p}=\frac{\ord(a)}{p}
, a contradiction. Consequently, our assumption must have been false and we have
\ord(b)=\ord(a)\cdot p
.

∎

Note that, with
Proposition

6
, for a prime
p
such that
p\mid q-1
and
p\nmid\ord(a)
, the polynomial
X^{p}-a
always decomposes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. Furthermore, in both cases,
p\nmid\ord(a)
and
p\mid\ord(a)
, there exists a root of
X^{p}-a
of order
p\cdot\ord(a)
. We can easily derive the following corollary.

Corollary 7
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
and
p
prime such that
p\mid(q-1)
. Then there exists
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b^{p}=a
if and only if
p\cdot\ord(a)\mid(q-1)
.

Proof.

If
p\cdot\ord(a)\mid(q-1)
, then with
Proposition

6
all roots of
X^{p}-a
are elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It remains to argue that if one root
b
of
X^{p}-a
lies in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then all roots
\zeta_{p}^{j}b
for
0\leq j\leq p-1
are elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, because
\zeta_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, the order of every root of
X^{p}-a
divides
q-1
and in particular the order
p\cdot\ord(a)
.
∎

As the following theorem by Serret,
Theorem

8
, shows, the irreducibility of the binomial
X^{t}-a
for a positive integer

depends mainly on the order of
a
. Since

and

give the order of the roots of

, they are the key elements for the theorems to come.

Theorem 8

(
[
LN94
, Theorem 3.75]
)
.

Let
t\geq 2
. Then the binomial
X^{t}-a
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\rad(t)\mid\ord(a)
,

(ii)

\gcd(t,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=1
,

(iii)

4\nmid t
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

2.1
The factorization of
X^{n}-a
for positive integers
n

such that
\mathrm{rad}(n)\mid q-1
and (
4\nmid n
or
q\equiv 1\mod 4
)

The factorization of
X^{n}-a
for the case
\rad(n)\mid q-1
and (
4\nmid n
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
) from
[

]
is given in three separate theorems for three types of

. In this section we give a new proof for this case which, due to its inductive nature, yields one closed formula for all

.

In the next theorem we combine
Corollary

7
and
Theorem

8
to obtain a result which can be applied recursively. More precisely, given an irreducible polynomial
X^{t}-a
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
Proposition

9
gives an easy irreducibility criterion for the polynomial
X^{tp}-a
, where
p
is a prime and divides
q-1
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
,
p
be a prime such that
p\mid(q-1)
and let
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
X^{t}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Further, let
4\nmid tp
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then
X^{tp}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
p\cdot\ord(a)\nmid(q-1)
.

Proof.

We show that
X^{tp}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if there does not exist
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b^{p}=a
. Then
Corollary

7
completes the statement.

Suppose that there exists
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b^{p}=a
. Then the polynomial
X^{tp}-a
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
satisfies
X^{tp}-a=\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(X^{t}-\zeta_{p}^{j}b)
and it is reducible.

Suppose that there does not exist
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b^{p}=a
. Then all roots of
X^{p}-a
lie in true extension fields of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(b)
be a root of
X^{p}-a
. Then since
\zeta_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
\zeta_{p}^{j}b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(b)
for all
1\leq j\leq p-1
. Vice versa,
b=\zeta_{p}^{-j}\cdot\zeta_{p}^{j}b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\zeta_{p}^{j}b)
. Thus, the degree of the minimal polynomial of every root of
X^{p}-a
is equal to
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(b):\mathbb{F}_{q}]
. Since
X^{p}-a
is a product of the minimal polynomials of its roots over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists a positive integer
m
such that
p=m\cdot[\mathbb{F}_{q}(b):\mathbb{F}_{q}]
. The two facts that
p
is prime and
b
is not an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
imply that
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(b):\mathbb{F}_{q}]=p
and
X^{p}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Theorem

8
yields that
p\mid\ord(a)
and
\gcd(p,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=1
. Furthermore, since
X^{t}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
\rad(t)\mid\ord(a)
,
\gcd(t,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=1
. As a direct consequence we obtain that
\rad(tp)\mid\ord(a)
and also
\gcd(tp,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=1
. Since we assumed that
4\nmid tp
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, the third condition of
Theorem

8
is satisfied and we can conclude that
X^{tp}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
∎

Let
p
be a prime such that
4\nmid tp
or

. Then the following corollary states the useful fact that if

is irreducible and

, the polynomial

Corollary 10
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
,
p
be a prime such that
p\mid(q-1)
and let
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
X^{t}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Further, let
4\nmid tp
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then if
p
divides
t
the polynomial
X^{tp}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

Since
X^{t}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
Theorem

8
implies that
\gcd(t,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=1
. If
p\mid t
, then also
\gcd(p,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=1
and
p\cdot\ord(a)
cannot divide
(q-1)
.
Proposition

9

∎

Corollary

significantly. If

, we do not need to compare the properties of

and

as specified in

(i) and (ii).

Using

, the following theorem gives the factorization of

for the case that the polynomial is reducible over

and (

Proposition 11
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
,
p
be a prime such that
p\mid(q-1)
and let
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
X^{t}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Further, let
4\nmid tp
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then if
p\cdot\ord(a)\mid(q-1)
, the factorization of
X^{tp}-a
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

X^{tp}-a=\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(X^{t}-\zeta_{p}^{j}b),

where
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b^{p}=a
.

(i)

If
p\nmid\ord(a)
, then
b=a^{r}
and
\ord(b)=\ord(a)
for a positive integer
r
satisfying
r=1
if
a=1
or
rp\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(a)}
otherwise
.

(ii)

If
p\mid\ord(a)
,
\ord(b)=p\cdot\ord(a)
.

Furthermore,
\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}b)=p\cdot\ord(a)
for all
1\leq j\leq p-1
.

Proof.

From
Proposition

6
and
Proposition

9
follows directly that

X^{tp}-a=\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(X^{t}-\zeta_{p}^{j}b)
and that statements (
i
) and (
ii
) hold. It remains to prove that
X^{t}-\zeta_{p}^{j}b
is irreducible for every
0\leq j\leq p-1
. Since
X^{t}-a
is irreducible, from
Theorem

8
follows that
\rad(t)\mid\ord(a)
and
\gcd(t,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=1
. Consequently,
\rad(t)\mid\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}b)
, because
\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}b)\in\{\ord(a),p\cdot\ord(a)\}
. Furthermore,
\frac{q-1}{\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}b)}
divides
\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)}
which implies that
\gcd(t,\frac{q-1}{\ord(\zeta_{p}^{j}b)})=1
and

is irreducible with
Theorem

8
.
∎

Proposition 12
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
X^{t}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rad(n)\mid\ord(a)
. Further, let
4\nmid tn
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Set
d:=\gcd(n,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})
. Then the factorization of
X^{tn}-a
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(X^{t\cdot\frac{n}{d}}-\zeta_{d}^{j}b),

where
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b^{d}=a
and
\ord(\zeta_{d}^{j}b)=\ord(a)\cdot d
for all
0\leq j\leq d-1
.

Proof.

We use the fact that every positive integer
n
can be written as a product of prime factors and prove the statement by induction on the number of prime factors of
n
.
Base case:
If
n=p
for a prime
p
such that
p\mid q-1
and
p\mid\ord(a)
, the statement follows directly from
Proposition

11
(i).

Induction hypothesis:
Suppose that the statement of
Proposition

13
holds for
n,d,b
such that
\rad(n)\mid\ord(a),d=\gcd(n,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})
and
b^{d}=a
.

Induction step
(n\rightarrow np)
:
Let
p
be a prime such that
p\mid\ord(a)
, then

X^{tnp}-a=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(X^{t\cdot\frac{n}{d}\cdot p}-\zeta_{d}^{j}b).

(2)

If
dp
does not divide
\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)}
, then with
Proposition

9
the polynomial
(X^{t\cdot\frac{n}{d}\cdot p}-\zeta_{d}^{j}b)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for every
0\leq j\leq d-1
. Furthermore, the greatest common divisor of
np
and
\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)}
is also
d
. Consequently,
eq.

2
is the factorization of
X^{tnp}-a
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the statement of
Proposition

12
holds.

If
dp
divides
\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)}
, then
\tilde{d}:=\gcd(np,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=dp
and there exists
\zeta_{\tilde{d}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Furthermore, for every
0\leq j\leq d-1
holds
\ord(\zeta_{d}^{j}b)\cdot p=\ord(a)\cdot d\cdot p\mid q-1
and the polynomial
(X^{t\cdot\frac{n}{d}}-\zeta_{d}^{j}b)
is reducible.
Proposition

11
yields that there exists an element
c_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
c_{j}^{p}=\zeta_{d}^{j}b
and that the factorization of
(X^{t\cdot\frac{n}{d}\cdot p}-\zeta_{d}^{j}b)
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is given by
\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}(X^{t\cdot\frac{n}{d}}-\zeta_{p}^{i}c_{j})
, where
\ord(\zeta_{p}^{i}c_{j})=\ord(\zeta_{d}^{j}b)\cdot p=\ord(a)\cdot d\cdot p=\ord(a)\cdot\tilde{d}
.
Thus, the factorization of
X^{tnp}-a
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

X^{tnp}-a=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}(X^{t\cdot\frac{n}{d}}-\zeta_{p}^{i}c_{j})=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}(X^{t\cdot\frac{np}{\tilde{d}}}-\zeta_{p}^{i}c_{j}).

(3)

Moreover,
c:=c_{0}
satisfies
c^{dp}=(c^{p})^{d}=b^{d}=a
and the factorization of
X^{tnp}-a
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can also be written as:

X^{tnp}-a=\prod_{j=0}^{\tilde{d}}(X^{t\cdot\frac{np}{\tilde{d}}}-\zeta_{\tilde{d}}^{j}c).

(4)

Then for every
0\leq j\leq\tilde{d}-1
holds
\ord(\zeta_{\tilde{d}}^{j}c)=\ord(a)\cdot\tilde{d}
, because every factor in
eq.

4
corresponds to a factor in
eq.

3
.
∎

As in the proof of

Proposition 13
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
X^{t}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rad(n)\mid(q-1)
and
\gcd(n,\ord(a)\cdot t)=1
. Further, let
4\nmid tn
or
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Set
d:=\gcd(n,\frac{q-1}{\ord(a)})=\gcd(n,q-1)
. Then the factorization of
X^{tn}-a
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

\prod_{v\mid\frac{n}{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
\gcd(j,v)=1\end{subarray}}^{d-1}(X^{tv}-\zeta_{d}^{j}a^{rv}),

(5)

where
r\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies
r=1
if
a=1
or
rn\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(a)}
otherwise
and for all applicable
v
and
j
holds

(i)

(\zeta_{d}^{j}a^{rv})^{\frac{n}{v}}=a
,

(ii)

\ord(\zeta_{d}^{j}a^{rv})=\ord(a)\cdot\frac{d}{\gcd(j,d)}
.

Base case:
If
n=p
, then the statement follows directly from
Proposition

11
(ii).

Induction hypothesis:
Suppose that the statement of
Proposition

13
holds for

such that
\rad(n)\mid(q-1),\gcd(n,\ord(a)\cdot t)=1,d=\gcd(n,q-1)
and either

and
r=1
or

.

Induction step (

):
Let
p
be a prime such that
p\mid(q-1)
and
p\nmid\ord(a)\cdot t
.
If
a=1
then set
\tilde{r}=1
so that
(a^{\tilde{r}})^{p}=a
.
Otherwise there exists a positive integer

such that
r^{\prime}p\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(a)}
. Since
\ord(a^{r})=\ord(a)
, this implies that

. We set
\tilde{r}:=rr^{\prime}
and together with

5
we obtain:

X^{tnp}-a=\prod_{v\mid\frac{n}{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
\gcd(j,v)=1\end{subarray}}^{d-1}(X^{tvp}-\zeta_{d}^{j}a^{\tilde{r}pv}),

(6)

where
\tilde{r}
satisfies
r=1
if
a=1
or
\tilde{r}\cdot np=rn\cdot r^{\prime}p\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(a)}
otherwise

\ord(a^{\tilde{r}pv})=\ord(a)

(a^{\tilde{r}pv})^{\frac{n}{v}}=a
. It remains to discuss which of the factors

and
\gcd(np,q-1)=d\cdot p
.

Case

If
\gcd(np,q-1)=d

p\mid n
but
dp\nmid q-1
and also
\ord(a)\cdot d\cdot p\nmid q-1

and

such that
\gcd(j,v)=1

(X^{tvp}-\zeta_{d}^{j}a^{\tilde{r}vp})
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

. Thus, the following expression is a factorization into irreducible factors over

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\mid\frac{n}{d}\\
p\mid v\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
\gcd(j,v)=1\end{subarray}}^{d-1}(X^{tvp}-\zeta_{d}^{j}a^{\tilde{r}vp})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{v}\mid\frac{np}{d}\\
p^{2}\mid\tilde{v}\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
\gcd(j,\tilde{v})=1\end{subarray}}^{d-1}(X^{t\tilde{v}}-\zeta_{d}^{j}a^{\tilde{r}\tilde{v}}),

where

irreducible binomials of this form, we obtain that
\varphi(n)=\frac{n}{{d}^{(s)}}\cdot\varphi({d}^{(s)})
. The factorization of
\Phi_{n}
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
is :

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
\gcd(i,{d}^{(s)}=1)\end{subarray}}^{{d}^{(s)}-1}(X^{\frac{n}{{d}^{(s)}}}-\zeta_{{d}^{(s)}}^{i}).

With
Corollary

16
the factorization of
\Phi_{n}
is given by
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq{d}^{(s)}-1\\
\gcd(i,{d}^{(s)})=1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[X^{\frac{n}{{d}^{(s)}}}-\zeta_{{d}^{(s)}}^{i}\right]
. It remains to prove that for every
i\in\mathcal{I}
, the primitive
{d}^{(s)}
-th root of unity
\zeta_{{d}^{(s)}}^{i}
is a proper element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
. The smallest integer
t
such that
{d}^{(s)}\mid q^{t}-1
satisfies
t=\ord_{{d}^{(s)}}(q)\geq\ord_{\rad({d}^{(s)})}(q)=\ord_{\rad(n)}(q)=w
and from the fact that
{d}^{(s)}
divides
q^{s}-1
follows that
t=\ord_{{d}^{(s)}}\leq s
. If
s=w
, then
t=\ord_{{d}^{(s)}}(q)=s
. If
s=2w
, then
4\mid n
and
q^{w}\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. Thus, with
2.3
(
i
) holds
\gcd(n,q^{s}-1)=\gcd(n,q^{w}-1)\cdot 2^{1+\min\{\nu_{2}(\frac{n}{4}),\nu_{2}(\frac{q+1}{2})\}}
. Consequently,
2\mid\frac{{d}^{(s)}}{{d}^{(w)}}
which implies that
{d}^{(s)}\nmid q^{w}-1
and
s=\ord_{{d}^{(s)}}(q)
. Thus,
\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q}\left(X^{\frac{n}{{d}^{(s)}}}-\zeta_{{d}^{(s)}}^{i}\right)=s
. Since
X^{\frac{n}{{d}^{(s)}}}-\zeta_{{d}^{(s)}}^{i}
is a binomial, its
q
-spin can easily be computed:

\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[X^{\frac{n}{{d}^{(s)}}}-\zeta_{{d}^{(s)}}^{i}\right]=\sum_{l=0}^{s}X^{\frac{n}{{d}^{(s)}}\cdot l}\cdot(-1)^{c_{i}-l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{U}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,c_{i}-1\}\\
|\mathcal{U}|=c_{i}-l\end{subarray}}\prod_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\zeta_{{d}^{(s)}}^{i\cdot q^{u}}.

∎

3
The factorization of
f(X^{n})

In this section we derive
Theorem

27
, a closed formula for the factorization of
f(X^{n})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any monic irreducible polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
,
f\neq X
, and any positive integer
n
such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
, from our main theorem. The polynomial
f=X
is of no interest, since its composition with
X^{n}
always factors as
f(X^{n})=(X)^{n}
. Throughout this section let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
k
,
f\neq X
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
be a root of
f
. Then
Theorem

18
yields the factorization of
X^{n}-\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. Therefore, the conditions from
Section

2
that are posed on
q
are conditions on
q^{k}
in this section. With
Theorem

3
the factorization of
f(X^{n})
is then given by the
q
-spins of all irreducible factors of
X^{n}-\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
.

Remark 3.1
.

As in
Section

2
, we can assume
\gcd(n,q)=1
without loss of generality. Indeed, if
\gcd(n,q)>1
, then
n=\tilde{n}\cdot\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{l}
and there exists an element
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
such that
\gamma^{\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{l}}=\alpha
. Let
\prod_{R}R
be the factorization of
X^{\tilde{n}}-\gamma
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
, then
(\prod_{R}R)^{\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{l}}
is the factorization of
X^{n}-\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. With
Theorem

3
the factorization of
f(X^{n})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
then is given by
(\prod_{R}\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[R\right])^{\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{l}}
.

Furthermore, we can asssume that
f
is monic without loss of generality. Indeed, if
f=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}X^{i}
is not monic, then
a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}\setminus\{1\}
and
f=a_{k}\cdot g
for a monic polynomial
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. If
\prod_{R\in\mathcal{R}}R
is the factorization of
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
a_{k}\cdot\prod_{R\in\mathcal{R}}R
is the factorization of
f
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Remark 20
.

The factorization of
f(X^{n})
for the case
\gcd(n,\ord(\alpha)\cdot k)=1
and
\rad(n)\mid q-1
or
\rad(n)\mid q^{w}-1
for a prime
w
is given in
[
BRS19
]
. In this remark we discuss the implications of this condition so that the differences between the existing and our results become clear. If
\gcd(n,\ord(\alpha))=1
, then there exists a positive integer
r
such that
rn\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(\alpha)}
and the element
\alpha^{r}
satisfies
(\alpha^{r})^{n}=\alpha
. Thus, this is a subcase of the case that there exists an element
\beta
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
such that
\beta^{n}=\alpha
. The authors of
[
BRS19
]
claim that their results are generalizations of the results given in
[
BGO15
]
and
[
WYF18
]
. Though this is technically true, we would like to point out that with
Proposition

5
these factorizations could easily be obtained from the factorizations of
X^{n}-1
in
[
BGO15
,
WYF18
]
.

The condition
\gcd(n,k)=1
implies that the roots of unity appearing in the factorization of
f(X^{n})
(or in fact of
X^{n}-\alpha
) are elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and not
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. Since these roots of unity are determined by
{d}^{(k)}
, this can be seen from the following lemma and
Corollary

22
, which is a corollary of
Lemma

21
.

Lemma 21

(
[
Bey77
, Proposition 1]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime such that
p\mid(q-1)
and let
m
be a positive integer. Then

(i)

If
p
is odd, then
\nu_{p}(q^{m}-1)=\nu_{p}(q-1)+\nu_{p}(m).

(ii)

If
p=2
, then
\nu_{2}(q^{m}-1)=\begin{cases}\nu_{2}(q-1)&\text{ if }m\text{ is odd,}\\
\nu_{2}(q-1)+\nu_{2}(m)+\nu_{2}(q+1)-1&\text{ if }m\text{ is even,}\end{cases}

so that
\nu_{p}(q^{m}-1)=\nu_{p}(q-1)+\nu_{p}(m)
if
q\equiv 1\mod 4
.

Corollary 22
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rad(n)\mid q-1
and
\gcd(n,k)=1
. For a positive integer
w
we set
{d}^{(w)}:=\gcd(n,q^{w}-1)
. Then

(i)

{d}^{(km)}={d}^{(m)}
for every positive integer
m
,

(ii)

{d}^{(2k)}=2^{l}\cdot{d}^{(1)}
, where
l=\min\{\max\{0,\nu_{2}(n)-\nu_{2}(q-1)\},\nu_{2}(q+1)\}
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
p
be a prime divisor of
n
, then if
p
odd, we have
\nu_{p}(q^{km}-1)=\nu_{p}(q^{m}-1)+\nu_{p}(k)=\nu_{p}(q^{m}-1)
. If
p=2
, then
k
is odd, because
\gcd(n,k)=1
, and
\nu_{2}(q^{km}-1)=\nu_{2}(q^{m}-1)
.

(ii)

Let
p
be a prime divisor of
n
, then if
p
odd, we have
\nu_{p}(q^{2k}-1)=\nu_{p}(q-1)+\nu_{p}(2k)=\nu_{p}(q-1)
, because
p\neq 2
and
\gcd(n,k)=1
. If
p=2
, then
\nu_{2}(q^{2k}-1)=\nu_{2}(q-1)+\nu_{2}(2k)+\nu_{2}(q+1)-1=\nu_{2}(q-1)+1+\nu_{2}(q+1)-1=\nu_{2}(q-1)+\nu_{2}(q+1)
.

∎

Remark 23
.

In
[
BRS19
, Theorem 4.1]
the authors do not mention the condition
\gcd(n,\ord(\alpha)\cdot k)=1
. However, without this condition the statement of the theorem would be false. For example with
Lemma

21
the greatest common divisor
d={d}^{(2)}
would equal
{d}^{(1)}\cdot\gcd(n,k)\cdot 2^{l}
, where
l:=\min\{\nu_{2}(\frac{n}{2}),\nu_{2}(q+1)\}
and not satisfy the equation
d=2^{l}\cdot{d}^{(1)}
. Furthermore, the authors prove this result using results from their Section 3 and the condition
\gcd(n,\ord(\alpha)\cdot k)
is set throughout this section.

We use the following lemma in the proof of
Theorem

27
to simplify our formula for the factorization of
f(X^{n})
. It shows that the
q
-spin is transitive.

Lemma 26
.

Let
k,s
be positive integers and
g
be an irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{ks}}
such that
k\mid\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q}\left(g\right)
, then the
q
-spin of
g
satisfies

\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[g\right]=\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[\mathrm{spin}_{q^{k}}\left[g\right]\right].

Proof.

Since
k\mid\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q}\left(g\right)
, there exists a divisor
d
of
s
such that
\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q}\left(g\right)=kd
and
\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q^{k}}\left(g\right)=d
. Then if
g=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}X^{i}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[\mathrm{spin}_{q^{k}}\left[g\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}^{q^{kj}}X^{i}\right]=\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}^{q^{kj+l}}X^{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{kd-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}^{q^{j}}X^{i}=\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[g\right],

because
q^{l}
is a power of
\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and for the coefficients of the polynomial
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}^{q^{kj+l}}X^{i}
we can write i.e.
(a_{i}^{q^{kj}}+a_{\tilde{i}}^{q^{k\tilde{j}}})^{q^{l}}=a_{i}^{q^{kj+l}}+a_{\tilde{i}}^{q^{k\tilde{j}+l}}
.
∎

The next theorem gives a closed formula for the factorization of
f(X^{n})
into monic irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any positive integer
n
such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
and any monic irreducible polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
such that
f\neq X
. It covers all factorizations given in
[
BRS19
]
.

Theorem 27
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
,
f\neq X
, be a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
k
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{\ast}
be a root of
f
. Furthermore, let
n
be a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
and
n=n_{1}\cdot n_{2},
where
\rad(n_{1})\mid\ord(\alpha)
and
\gcd(n_{2},\ord(\alpha))=1
. Let
w=\ord_{\rad(n)}(q^{k})
and set
s:=w
if
4\nmid n
or
q^{kw}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, else set
s:=2w
. For all positive integers
t
we set
{d_{1}}^{(t)}:=\gcd(n_{1},\frac{q^{kt}-1}{\ord(a)})
and
{d_{2}}^{(t)}:=\gcd(n_{2},q^{kt}-1)
. If
4\nmid{d_{1}}^{(s)}
or
q^{k}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then
s_{1}:=\frac{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}{{d_{1}}^{(1)}}
, otherwise
s_{1}:=\frac{2{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}}{{d_{1}}^{(2)}}
. For every
i\in\mathrm{CR}_{q}({d_{2}}^{(s)})
we set
t_{i}:=\min\{t\in\mathbb{N}:\frac{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}{{d_{2}}^{(t)}}\mid i\}
and
c_{i}:=\lcm(t_{i},s_{1})
.

Then there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{ks}}
such that
\beta^{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}=a
and the factorization of
f(X^{n})
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

\prod_{j\in\{0,\ldots,{d_{1}}^{(s)}-1\}/\sim}\;\prod_{v\mid\frac{n_{2}}{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}}\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathrm{CR}_{q^{k}}({d_{2}}^{(s)})\\
\gcd(i,v)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=0}^{\gcd(t_{i},s_{1})-1}S_{(j,v,i,m)},\;

and for all applicable
(j,v,i,m)
the monic irreducible polynomial
S_{(j,v,i,m)}
of degree
k\cdot\frac{n_{1}}{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}\cdot v\cdot c_{i}
and order
\ord(\alpha)\cdot n_{1}\cdot v\cdot\frac{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}{\gcd(i,{d_{2}}^{(s)})}
is defined as

S_{(j,v,i,m)}=\sum_{l=0}^{kc_{i}}X^{\frac{n_{1}}{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}\cdot v\cdot l}\cdot(-1)^{kc_{i}-l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{U}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,kc_{i}-1\}\\
|\mathcal{U}|=kc_{i}-l\end{subarray}}\prod_{u\in\mathcal{U}}(\zeta_{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}^{i\cdot q^{m}}(\zeta_{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}^{j}\beta)^{rv})^{q^{u}},

where
r
is a positive integer satisfying
r=1
if
f=X-1
or
rn_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(\alpha)\cdot{d_{1}}^{(s)}}
otherwise. Furthermore, for all
j,\tilde{j}\in\{0,\ldots,{d_{1}}^{(s)}-1\}
holds
j\sim\tilde{j}
if and only if
\zeta_{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}^{\tilde{j}}=\zeta_{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}^{j\cdot q^{km}}\beta^{q^{km}-1}
for an integer
0\leq m\leq s_{1}-1
.

Proof.

The factorization of
X^{n}-\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is given by
Theorem

18
as

\prod_{j\in\{0,\ldots,{d_{1}}^{(s)}-1\}/\sim}\;\prod_{v\mid\frac{n_{2}}{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}}\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathrm{CR}_{q^{k}}({d_{2}}^{(s)})\\
\gcd(i,v)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=0}^{\gcd(t_{i},s_{1})-1}S_{(j,v,i,m)}

where
S_{(j,v,i,m)}
is the
q^{k}
-spin of the binomial
R_{(j,v,i,m)}:=X^{\frac{n_{1}}{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}\cdot v}-\zeta_{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}^{i\cdot q^{m}}(\zeta_{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}^{j}\beta)^{rv}
with
\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q^{k}}\left(R_{(j,v,i,m)}\right)=c_{i}
. The positive integer
r
satisfies
r=1
if
\alpha=1
, which is equivalent to
f=X-1
, or
rn_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{\ord(\alpha)\cdot{d_{1}}^{(s)}}
otherwise. Then with
Theorem

3
the factorization of
f(X^{n})=f^{Q}
for
Q=\frac{X^{n}}{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is given by

\displaystyle\prod_{j\in\{0,\ldots,{d_{1}}^{(s)}-1\}/\sim}\;\prod_{v\mid\frac{n_{2}}{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}}\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathrm{CR}_{q^{k}}({d_{2}}^{(s)})\\
\gcd(i,v)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=0}^{\gcd(t_{i},s_{1})-1}\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[S_{(j,v,i,m)}\right],

and
\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q}\left(S_{(j,v,i,m)}\right)=k
. With
Lemma

26
holds
\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[S_{(j,v,i,m)}\right]=\mathrm{spin}_{q}\left[R_{(j,v,i,m)}\right]
and
\mathrm{coeffdeg}_{q}\left(R_{(j,v,i,m)}\right)=kc_{i}
. Since
R_{(j,v,i,m)}
is a binomial, its
q
-spin can easily be computed:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{kc_{i}}X^{\frac{n_{1}}{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}\cdot v\cdot l}\cdot(-1)^{kc_{i}-l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{U}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,kc_{i}-1\}\\
|\mathcal{U}|=kc_{i}-l\end{subarray}}\prod_{u\in\mathcal{U}}(\zeta_{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}^{i\cdot q^{m}}(\zeta_{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}^{j}\beta)^{rv})^{q^{u}}

The degree of the
q
-spin of
R_{(j,v,i,m)}
is
kc_{i}\cdot\frac{n_{1}}{{d_{1}}^{(s)}}\cdot v
and its order is the same as the order of
S_{(j,v,i,m)}
, which is
\ord(\alpha)\cdot n_{1}\cdot v\cdot\frac{{d_{2}}^{(s)}}{\gcd(i,{d_{2}}^{(s)})}
.
∎

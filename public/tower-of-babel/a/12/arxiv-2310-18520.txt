On the L r L^{r} -differentiability of Two Lusin Classes and a Full Descriptive Characterization of the H ​ K r HK_{r} -integral
PAUL MUSIAL, VALENTIN SKVORTSOV, PIOTR SWOROWSKI, FRANCESCO TULONE

Abstract
. It is proved that any function of a Lusin-type class, the class of A ​ C ​ G r ACG_{r} -functions, is differentiable almost everywhere in the sense of a derivative defined in the space L r L^{r} , 1 ≤ r < ∞ 1\leq r<\infty . This leads to obtaining a full descriptive characterization of a Henstock-Kurzweil-type integral, the H ​ K r HK_{r} -integral, which serves to recover functions from their L r L^{r} -derivatives. The class A ​ C ​ G r ACG_{r} is compared with the classical Lusin class A ​ C ​ G ACG and it is shown that a continuous A ​ C ​ G ACG -function can fail to be L r L^{r} -differentiable almost everywhere.

1.
Introduction

The well-known classical definition of the Lebesgue integral in terms of absolute continuity of the indefinite integral is an example of a so-called full descriptive characterization of an integral. Definitions of this type are known also for a number of non-absolute generalizations of the Lebesgue integral. The most well-known among them is Lusin’s definition of Denjoy integral (see
[
15
]
).

In general, we say that a
descriptive
characterization of a constructive integration process is obtained if a class of functions is found so that it coincides with the class of the indefinite integrals of all functions integrable in the sense of this process, and if functions of the class are differentiable almost everywhere in a certain sense, the derivative being equal to the integrand almost everywhere. If the differentiability (in the appropriate sense, corresponding to differentiability property of the indefinite integral) of functions of the class is an additional assumption put on the class, then we have only a
partial
descriptive characterization of the considered integral. If on the other hand the corresponding differentiability property is a consequence of the description of the class and not an additional requirement, such a characterization is said to be a
full
descriptive characterization. For example, each continuous function from Lusin’s class
ACG
is differentiable almost everywhere only in the sense of the approximate derivative, and this class gives a full descriptive characterization of the wide Denjoy integral (the
D
-integral, see
[
15
]
). At the same time if we restrict this class by considering only continuous and differentiable (in the usual sense)
ACG
-functions then we obtain a partial descriptive characterization of the so-called Khintchine integral (see
[
5
]
). Descriptive characterizations of other non-absolute integrals are discussed in
[
2
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

In this paper we obtain a full descriptive characterization of a Henstock-Kurzweil-type integral, the
HK_{r}
-integral (defined in 2004 by Musial and Sagher
[
6
]
), which serves to integrate a derivative defined in the space
L^{r}
,
1\leq r<\infty
. This
L^{r}
-derivative was introduced in
[
1
]
by Calderón and Zygmund to be used in some estimates for solutions of elliptic partial differential equations. The
HK_{r}
-integral was defined as an extension of a Perron-type integral, the
P_{r}
-integral, which was defined earlier by L. Gordon
[
3
]
and which also recovers a function from its
L^{r}
-derivative. The
HK_{r}
-integral turned out to be strictly wider than the
P_{r}
-integral (see
[
7
]
). It was also shown in
[
6
]
that the indefinite
HK_{r}
-integral is
L^{r}
-differentiable almost everywhere and belongs to a Lusin-type class of
ACG_{r}
-functions. Some other properties of these integrals are investigated in
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

Another class, also giving a descriptive characterization of the
HK_{r}
-integral, was obtained in
[
8
]
in terms of absolute continuity of so-called
L^{r}
-variational measure generated by a function belonging to
L^{r}
. Both classes, the class of
ACG_{r}
-functions and the class of functions generating absolutely continuous
L^{r}
-variational measure, were known to coincide with the class of the indefinite
HK_{r}
-integrals, but only under the additional assumption that the functions are
L^{r}
-differentiable almost everywhere (see
[
8
]
). The problem of
L^{r}
-differentiability almost everywhere of functions of those classes was left open in
[
8
]
. So in fact only partial descriptive characterizations of the
HK_{r}
-integral were obtained in
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
.

The main aim of the present paper is to prove that any
ACG_{r}
-function is
L^{r}
-differentiable almost everywhere and thereby obtain a full descriptive characterization of the
HK_{r}
-integral (see Theorem
13
in Section
3
). In Section
4
, we construct an example of a function from a Lusin-type class,
[ACG]
, which, even under an additional assumption of continuity, fails to have the property of being
L^{r}
-differentiable almost everywhere for any
r
. Thereby we show that the class
[ACG]
is not contained in the class
ACG_{r}
for any
r
. As a byproduct of this example we get a new proof, based on the
L^{r}
-derivative, of a result, previously obtained in
[
16
]
, stating that the
HK_{r}
-integral does not cover the wide Denjoy integral.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall here the main definitions and facts related to the notion of the
HK_{r}
-integral (see
[
3
,
6
]
).

We work in a fixed closed interval
[a,b]
. An interval
I
is a non-degenerate closed subinterval of
[a,b]
. We assume in all these definitions that the functions
F
belong to the class
L^{r}=L^{r}[a,b]
,
1\leq r<\infty
. We start with
L^{r}
-derivates and the
L^{r}
-derivative.

Definition 1
.

The
upper-right
L^{r}
-derivate
of
F
at
x
, denoted by
D_{r}^{+}F(x)
, is defined as the infimum of all numbers
\alpha
such that

(1)

\left(\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}[F(x+t)-F(x)-\alpha t]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudt\right)^{\!1/r}\!=\,o(h)\quad\text{as}\ h\rightarrow 0^{+}.

If no real number
\alpha
satisfies (
1
), we set
D_{r}^{+}F(x)=+\infty
. The
lower-right
,
upper-left
, and
lower-left
L^{r}
-derivates
of
F
at
x
are defined correspondingly as

\displaystyle D_{+,r}F(x)=-D_{r}^{+}(-F)(x),\quad D_{r}^{-}F(x)=D_{r}^{+}(-F)(-x),

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad D_{-,r}F(x)=-D_{r}^{+}F(-x).

If all four
L^{r}
-derivates of
F
at
x
are equal, the common value, denoted by
F^{\prime}_{r}(x)
, is the
L^{r}
-derivative of
F
at
x
. If
F^{\prime}_{r}(x)
is finite we say that
F
is
L^{r}
-differentiable at
x
and the value of
F^{\prime}_{r}(x)
, say
\alpha
, is uniquely defined by

(2)

\left(\frac{1}{h}\int_{-h}^{h}|F(x+t)-F(x)-\alpha t|^{r}\mkern 1.5mudt\right)^{\!1/r}\!=\,o(h)\quad\text{as}\ h\to 0^{+}.

We need the following property of
L^{r}
-derivates (see
[
3
, Corollary of Theorem 4]
)

Proposition 2
.

If the two upper (or the two lower)
L^{r}
-derivates of a function
F
are less than
+\infty
(resp. greater than
-\infty
) on a set
E
, then almost everywhere on
E
the
L^{r}
-derivative of
F
exists and is finite.

For completeness we give the following definitions; see
[
4
]
for a full treatment.

Definition 3
.

Let
F\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
and let
c\in[a,b]
. The function
F
is said to be
approximately continuous at

c
if there exists a measurable set
E
containing
c
such that
c
is a point of density of
E
and
F|_{E}
is continuous at
c
.

Definition 4
.

Let
F\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
and let
c\in[a,b]
. The function
F
is said to be
approximately differentiable at

c
if there exists a measurable set
E
containing
c
such that
c
is a point of density of
E
and

\lim_{x\rightarrow c,x\in E}\frac{F(x)-F(c)}{x-c}

is finite. The value of this limit is the approximate derivative of
F
at
c
, and we will denote it by
F^{\prime}_{\textup{ap}}(c)
. Note that the value of
F^{\prime}_{\textup{ap}}(c)
does not depend on the choice
of
E
.

To define the
HK_{r}
-integral
[
6
]
we need some auxiliary notions. A
tagged interval
is a pair
(I,x)
where a point
x\in I
is a
tag
. We say that tagged intervals
(I^{\prime},x^{\prime})
and
(I^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
are nonoverlapping if intervals
I^{\prime}
and
I^{\prime\prime}
are nonoverlapping, i.e., they have no inner points in common. A
partition
is any finite collection
\pi
of pairwise nonoverlapping tagged intervals. A
gauge
is a strictly positive function
\delta
on
[a,b]
(or on a subset of
[a,b]
). We say that a tagged interval
(I,x)
is
\delta
-fine
if
I\subset(x-\delta(x),x+\delta(x))
.

A partition is
\delta
-fine
if all its elements are
\delta
-fine.
A partition
\pi
is
tagged in a set

E\subset[a,b]
if
x\in E
for each element
(I,x)
of
\pi
.

The Lebesgue measure on
[a,b]
will be denoted by
\mu
. We recall the definition of the
L^{r}
-Henstock–Kurzweil integral given in
[
6
]
.

Definition 5
.

A function
f\colon[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
L^{r}
-Henstock–Kurzweil integrable
(
HK
r
-integrable
) on
[a,b]
if there exists a function
F\in L^{r}
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a gauge
\delta
such that for any
\delta
-fine partition
\{([c_{i},d_{i}],x_{i})\}_{i}
we have

\sum\limits_{i}\left(\dfrac{1}{d_{i}-c_{i}}\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}\lvert F(y)-F(x_{i})-f(x_{i})(y-x_{i})\rvert^{r}\,dy\right)^{1/r}<\varepsilon.

Definition 6
.

[
6
]

Let
E\subset[a,b]
and
F\in L^{r}
. We say that
F\in AC_{r}(E)
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\eta>0
and a gauge
\delta
defined on
E
such that for any
\delta
-fine partition
\{([c_{i},d_{i}],x_{i})\}_{i}
tagged in
E
and such that

(3)

\sum_{i}(d_{i}-c_{i})<\eta

we have

(4)

\sum_{i}\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}-c_{i}}\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}|F(y)-F(x_{i})|^{r}\,dy\right)^{\!1/r}<\,\varepsilon.

We say that
F
is an
ACG_{r}
-
function
if
[a,b]=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
where
F\in AC_{r}(E_{n})
for all
n
.

Definition 7
.

Let
E\subset[a,b]
. We say that
F\in AC(E)
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\eta>0
such that for any finite collection of nonoverlapping intervals
\{[c_{i},d_{i}]\}_{i}
having endpoints in
E
and such that

\sum_{i}(d_{i}-c_{i})<\eta

we have

\sum_{i}|F(d_{i})-F(c_{i})|<\,\varepsilon.

We say that
F
is an
ACG-function
if
[a,b]=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
, where
F\in AC(E_{n})
for all
n
. If, moreover, all
E_{n}
can be chosen closed, we say
F
is an [
ACG
]
-function
.

Definition 8
.

A function
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
D-integrable
(
integrable in the wide Denjoy sense
) if there exists a
continuous

ACG
-function
F\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F^{\prime}_{\textup{ap}}(x)=f(x)
at almost all
x\in[a,b]
. We then define
\int_{a}^{b}f=F(b)-F(a)
.

Definition 9
.

A function
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
Kubota integrable
if there exists an approximately continuous
[ACG]
-function
F\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F^{\prime}_{\textup{ap}}(x)=f(x)
at almost all
x\in[a,b]
. We then define
\int_{a}^{b}f=F(b)-F(a)
.

We will use the following property of
ACG_{r}
-functions.

Proposition 10
.

[
6
, Corollary 1]
If
F
is an
ACG_{r}
-function, then
[a,b]
can be represented as
[a,b]=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
with
F\in AC(E_{n})
for each
n
.

Recall that
F\in L^{r}
and so is measurable. Hence the above proposition implies that any
ACG_{r}
-function is a measurable
VBG
-function, and so by the Denjoy-Khintchine Theorem (see
[
15
, Chapter VII, Section 4, Theorem 4.3]
) we obtain:

Theorem 11
.

Any
ACG_{r}
-function is approximately differentiable a.e. on
[a,b]
.

3.
L_{r}
-differentiability of
ACG_{r}
-functions

We now state and prove the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 12
.

Any
ACG_{r}
-function
F
is
L_{r}
-differentiable a.e. on
[a,b]
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
11
,
F^{\prime}_{\textup{ap}}
exists and is finite almost everywhere on
[a,b]
. We will show that

(5)

F_{r}^{\prime}(x)=F_{\textup{ap}}^{\prime}(x)\ \ \text{a.e.}

By Lusin’s theorem on
C
-property it is enough to prove (
5
) on a closed set
E
of positive measure such that
F
restricted to
E
is continuous. We can suppose that
F
is approximately differentiable at each point of
E
. Note that the approximate derivative and all
L_{r}
-derivates are measurable (see
[
15
,
3
]
). To simplify computation we consider first the case where
F^{\prime}_{\textup{ap}}
is non-negative on
E
. We start by proving that in this case

(6)

D_{r}^{+}F(x)=F_{\textup{ap}}^{\prime}(x)\ \ \text{a.e.\ on $E$}.

If not, then having in mind the relation between
L^{r}
-derivates and approximate derivates (see
[
3
, Theorem 2]
) we get

(7)

D_{r}^{+}F(x)>F_{\textup{ap}}^{\prime}(x)\geq 0

on a set
E^{\prime}\subset E
of positive measure. We can suppose that
E^{\prime}
is closed. By Definition
4
we can find a set
E^{\prime\prime}\subset E^{\prime}
of positive outer measure where
F\in AC_{r}(E^{\prime\prime})
. By Definition
6
for
\varepsilon=1
there exist on
E^{\prime\prime}
a gauge
\delta
and a positive number
\eta
such that inequality (
4
) holds for each
\delta
-fine partition
\{([c_{i},d_{i}],x_{i})\}_{i}
tagged in
E^{\prime\prime}
and satisfying (
3
). Now having fixed
\delta
corresponding to the chosen
\varepsilon
we can find a set
T\subset E^{\prime\prime}
of positive outer measure on which
\delta(x)\geq\gamma
for some constant
\gamma>0
. Let
\overline{T}
be the closure of
T
and let
W
be the set of two-sided limit points of
\overline{T}
. We then have
\overline{T}=W\cup C
where
C
is countable. As the set
E^{\prime}
is closed we have
W\subset\overline{T}\subset E^{\prime}\subset E
and
\mu(W)>0
.

We show now that the inequality (
4
) with
\varepsilon=2
, i.e., the inequality

(8)

\sum_{i}\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}-c_{i}}\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}|F(y)-F(x_{i})|^{r}\,dy\right)^{\!1/r}<\,2

holds for any
\gamma
-fine partition
\{([c_{i},d_{i}],x_{i})\}_{i}
tagged in
W
, and satisfying (
3
) with the same
\eta
which was found for
\varepsilon=1
and for
\delta
-fine partitions tagged in
E^{\prime\prime}
. Indeed, consider such a
\gamma
-fine partition. By the definition of
W
and by the continuity of
F
with respect to
E
, we can choose, for each
i
, a point
x_{i}^{\prime}\in T
so close to
x_{i}

inside of

[c_{i},d_{i}]
that
([c_{i},d_{i}],x_{i}^{\prime})
is also
\gamma
-fine and
\sum_{i}|F(x_{i})-F(x^{\prime}_{i})|<1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}-c_{i}}\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}|F(y)-F(x_{i})|^{r}\mkern 1.5mudy\right)^{\!1/r}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{i}\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}-c_{i}}\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}|F(y)-F(x_{i}^{\prime})|^{r}\mkern 1.5mudy\right)^{\!1/r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i}\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}-c_{i}}\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}|F(x_{i}^{\prime})-F(x_{i})|^{r}\mkern 1.5mudy\right)^{\!1/r}\!<1+1=2.

So (
8
) must be true for any
\gamma
-fine partition tagged in
W
and satisfying (
3
). We now construct such a
\gamma
-fine partition for which (
8
) fails to be true. This will give us a contradiction proving (
6
).

Let
P\subset W
be the set of density points of
W
. We have
\mu(P)=\mu(W)
. Let
x
be any point of
P
. There exists a set
S_{x}
having
x
as a density point, such that

(9)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\to 0\\
x+t\in S_{x}\end{subarray}}\!\frac{F(x+t)-F(x)}{t}=F_{\textup{ap}}^{\prime}(x)\geq 0.

Note that
x
is also a density point for the set
W\cap S_{x}
. Moreover, we can find a closed set
A_{x}\subset W\cap S_{x}

for which
x
is still a density point. Let the open set
B_{x}
be the complement of
A_{x}
in
[a,b]
. Choose the number
l
so that
D^{+}_{r}(x)>l>F_{\textup{ap}}^{\prime}(x)
. Then by Definition
1
there exists a constant
c_{x}>0
and a sequence
h_{k}\to 0^{+}
such that for each
k

(10)

\left(\frac{1}{h_{k}}\int_{0}^{h_{k}}[F(x+t)-F(x)-lt]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudt\right)^{\!1/r}\!>\,c_{x}h_{k}.

We now put some restrictions on the size of
h_{k}
. We cover
P
by an open set
G\subset(a,b)
such that
\mu(G)<2\mu(P)
, and we assume that all intervals
[x,x+h_{k}]
are subsets of
G
. As
A_{x}\cap[x,x+h]\subset\{x+t:F(x+t)-F(x)<lt\}
for small enough
h
, we can also assume that for each
h_{k}

(11)

\int_{0}^{h_{k}}[F(x+t)-F(x)-lt]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudt\\
=\int_{B_{x}\cap[x,x+h_{k}]}[F(u)-F(x)-l(u-x)]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudu.

Note that this equality in particular implies that in our further consideration we can assume that for all
k
,

(12)

\mu(B_{x}\cap[x,x+h_{k}])>0.

Indeed, otherwise

\int_{0}^{h_{k}}[F(x+t)-F(x)-lt]_{+}^{r}\,dt=0

contradicting the claim that
D^{+}_{r}F(x)>l
.

Fix some natural
n>\max{\{2\mu(P)/\eta,2/\mu(P)\}}
. As
x
is a point of dispersion for the set
B_{x}
we can find
h_{x}\leq\gamma
such that

(13)

\frac{\mu(B_{x}\cap[x,x+h])}{h}<\min{\biggl\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{c_{x}^{r}}{n^{r}}\biggr\}}

holds for
0<h\leq h_{x}
. So we can assume that
h_{k}<h_{x}\leq\gamma
for all
k
. Having in mind (
10
), (
11
), (
12
), and (
13
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B_{x}\cap[x,x+h_{k}])}\int_{B_{x}\cap[x,x+h_{k}]}[F(u)-F(x)-l(u-x)]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudu\right)^{\!1/r}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{h_{k}}{\mu(B_{x}\cap[x,x+h_{k}])}\right)^{\!1/r}\!\cdot\,\left(\frac{1}{h_{k}}\int_{0}^{h_{k}}[F(x+t)-F(x)-lt]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudt\right)^{\!1/r}

(14)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\biggl(\frac{n^{r}}{c^{r}_{x}}\biggr)^{\!1/r}\,\cdot c_{x}h_{k}=nh_{k}.

As
B_{x}
is an open set we have the representation

B_{x}\cap(x,x+h_{k})=\bigcup_{j}\,(a_{j},b_{j}).

Note that
a_{j}\in A_{x}\subset W
and
b_{j}-a_{j}<h_{x}\leq\gamma
for each
j
. So
([a_{j},b_{j}],a_{j})
are
\gamma
-fine intervals tagged in
W
. Using the following obvious inequality for positive numbers

\left(\frac{\sum_{k}a_{k}}{\sum_{k}b_{k}}\right)^{\!1/r}\leq\left(\sum_{k}\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}\right)^{\!1/r}\leq\sum_{k}\biggl(\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}\biggr)^{\!1/r}

(this is true for infinite sums provided the series are convergent), we get

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B_{x}\cap[x,x+h_{k}])}\int_{B_{x}\cap[x,x+h_{k}]}[F(u)-F(x)-l(u-x)]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudu\right)^{\!1/r}\\
&\qquad\leq\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}[F(u)-F(x)-l(u-x)]_{+}^{r}\mkern 1.5mudu\right)^{\!1/r}\\
&\qquad\leq\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}|F(u)-F(a_{j})|^{r}\mkern 1.4mudu\right)^{\!1/r}\\
&\qquad\leq\sum_{j}\left(\frac{1}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}|F(u)-F(a_{j})|^{r}\mkern 1.5mudu\right)^{\!1/r}.\end{split}

(We have taken into account that
F(a_{j})-F(x)<l(a_{j}-x)<l(u-x)
for
u\in(a_{j},b_{j})
). Combining (
15
) with (
14
) we get

(16)

\sum_{j}\left(\frac{1}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}|F(u)-F(a_{j})|^{r}\mkern 1.5mudu\right)^{\!1/r}\geq nh_{k}.

The family of intervals
\{[x,x+h_{k}]\}
for all
x\in P
and all
k
forms a Vitali cover of
P
. Applying the Vitali covering theorem we choose a countable sequence of nonoverlapping intervals
[x_{j},x_{j}+h_{k_{j}}]
which are subsets of
G
and cover
P
up to a set of measure zero. Hence
\mu(P)\leq\,\sum_{j}h_{k_{j}}\leq\mu(G)<2\mu(P)
. We can apply (
16
) to each of these intervals and to the corresponding set
B_{x_{j}}\cap[x_{j},x_{j}+h_{k_{j}}]
. Collecting all intervals constituting
B_{x_{j}}\cap[x_{j},x_{j}+h_{k_{j}}]
for all
j
we obtain a family
\{(I_{i},z_{i})\}_{i}
of nonoverlapping
\gamma
-fine intervals tagged in
P
, and so in
W,
the total length of which is estimated, due to (
13

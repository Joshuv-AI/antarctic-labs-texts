Uniform Estimates for Dirichlet Problems in Perforated Domains
Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
This paper studies the Dirichlet problem for Laplace’s equation in a domain Ω ε , η \Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta} perforated with small holes, where ε \varepsilon represents the scale of the minimal distances between holes and η \eta the ratio between the scale of sizes of holes and ε \varepsilon . We establish W 1 , p W^{1,p} estimates for solutions with bounding constants depending explicitly on the small parameters ε \varepsilon and η \eta . We also show that these estimates are either optimal or near optimal. Keywords : Perforated Domain; Laplace’s Equation; W 1 , p W^{1,p} Estimate; Homogenization. MSC2020 : 35J05; 35J25; 35B27.

Introduction

In this paper we consider the Dirichlet problem for Laplace’s equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f)&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

in a domain

Under some general conditions on

Moreover, the solution satisfies the energy estimate,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\leq A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},\\

We now describe our approach to Theorems

and
1.2

The first step is to establish the estimate (

by using the test functions,

v_{\ell}=\min\{|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2}\},

where

-\Delta u=F+\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

This is a classical method that goes back to J. Moser
[

and was used to establish

The approach was also used in
[
14

in the case

By using a Poincaré inequality for functions in

that vanish on

we are able to deduce the estimate (

Next, we use a localization argument to show that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\left\{(\varepsilon\eta)^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\},

As a result, we obtain an estimate of

Finally, we apply the Riesz-Thorin Interpolation Theorem for

The case

We remark that the approach outlined above works equally well for the bounded perforated domain

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

Suppose that

\int_{Y}|u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{Y}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\cdot\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta^{p-d}&\quad&\text{ if \ \ }1<p<d,\\
&|\ln\eta|^{p-1}&\quad&\text{ if \ \ }p=d,\\
&1&\quad&\text{ if \ \ }d<p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where

depends on

Proof.

This case

The proof for the general case is similar.
We provide a proof for the reader’s convenience.
Using

on
B(0,\eta)

u(x)=u(r\omega)-u(\eta\omega)=\int_{\eta}^{r}\omega\cdot\nabla u(t\omega)dt

for any

\omega=x/|x|

It follows by Hölder’s inequality that

|u(x)|^{p}\leq\int_{\eta}^{r}|\nabla u(t\omega)|^{p}t^{d-1}\,dt\left(\int_{\eta}^{r}t^{-\frac{d-1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1}

for

.
Thus,

\int_{Y}|u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{Y}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\left(\int_{\eta}^{d}t^{-\frac{d-1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1},

from which the inequalities in (
2.1
) follow readily.

Remark 2.3

Proof.

We may assume
\eta\in(0,1/4)
. The case
\eta\in[1/4,1]
follows from the
W^{1,p}
estimate (
2.6
)
for Laplace’s equation in
C^{1}
and Lipschitz domains, by a localization argument.
In fact, the case
\eta\in(0,1/4)
also follows readily from (
2.6
) by a localization argument.
To see this, choose a cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T)
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
in
\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T
,

\varphi=1
in
Y\setminus 2\eta T
, and

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|\leq C\eta^{-1},\ \ \ |\nabla^{2}\varphi|\leq C\eta^{-2}\quad\text{ in }2\eta T\setminus\eta T,

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|+|\nabla^{2}\varphi|\leq C\quad\text{ in }\widetilde{Y}\setminus Y.

Using

-\Delta(u\varphi)=F\varphi+\text{\rm div}(f\varphi)-f\cdot\nabla\varphi-2\,\text{\rm div}(u\nabla\varphi)+u\Delta\varphi

(2.9)

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we obtain

\|\nabla(u\varphi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\{\|f\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|F\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\Delta\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\},

where
d^{\prime}<p<\infty
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{d}
.
This gives

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y\setminus 2\eta T)}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T)}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T)}+\eta^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T)}\right\},

(2.10)

where we have used the observation
\|u\|_{L^{q}(2\eta T\setminus\eta T)}\leq C\eta\|u\|_{L^{p}(2\eta T\setminus\eta T)}
.

Finally, if
\partial T
is
C^{1}
and
u=0
on
\partial T
, it follows from (
2.6
) by a rescaling argument that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(2\eta T\setminus\eta T)}\leq C\left\{\eta^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(3\eta T\setminus\eta T)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(3\eta T\setminus\eta T)}+\eta\|F\|_{L^{p}(3\eta T\setminus\eta T)}\right\}

(2.11)

for
1<p<\infty
.
If
\partial T
is Lipschitz, the same is true under the additional conditions in (
2.3
).
The estimate (
2.11
), together with (
2.10
), yields (
2.8
).
∎

3
Global estimates

Let
\{T_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
be a family of closed subsets of
Y=[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
.
Assume that each
T_{k}
is the closure of a bounded Lipschitz domain and satisfies the condition
(
1.7
).
We also assume that
Y\setminus{T_{k}}
is connected.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.8
).
Then, for any
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
,

\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\cdot\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}&\quad&\text{ if \ \ }d\geq 3,\\
&\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|&\quad&\text{ if \ \ }d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where
0<\varepsilon,\eta\leq 1
and
C
depends on
d
and
c_{0}
in (
1.7
).

Proof.

By rescaling we may assume
\varepsilon=1
.
Assume
d\geq 3
. By applying Poincaré’s inequality (
2.1
), we obtain

\int_{k+(Y\setminus\eta T_{k})}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\eta^{2-d}\int_{k+(Y\setminus\eta T_{k})}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where we have used the assumption
B(0,c_{0})\subset T_{k}
.
The inequality (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon=1
and
d\geq 3
follows by summing the inequalities above over
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
The proof for the case
d=2
is the same.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.16
), where
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Then, for
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

(3.2)

where
C
depends on
\Omega
.
This follows by extending
u
by zero to
\Omega
and applying Poincaré’s inequality for
\Omega
.
Moreover,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\cdot\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}&\quad&\text{ if \ \ }d\geq 3,\\
&\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|&\quad&\text{ if \ \ }d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

for any
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
To see (
3.3
), we may assume
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small.
For otherwise, the desired estimate follows from (
3.2
).
We cover
\Omega
by a family of cubes
\{\varepsilon(k+Y)\}
of size
\varepsilon
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and

\varepsilon(k+Y)\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
.
The case
\varepsilon(k+Y)\subset\Omega
is handled in the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
If
\varepsilon(k_{0}+Y)\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we may find
k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

\varepsilon(k_{1}+Y)\subset\Omega
and

\varepsilon(k_{0}+Y)\subset 5\varepsilon(k_{1}+Y).

It follows from the proof of Lemma
2.1
that

\int_{\varepsilon(k_{0}+Y)}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{5\varepsilon(k_{1}+Y)}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\int_{5\varepsilon(k_{1}+Y)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

for
d\geq 3
,
where we have extended
u
by zero to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
As a result,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

The proof for the case
d=2
is the same.

It is not hard to see that

W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\right\}.

Using Lemma
3.1
and the Lax-Milgram Theorem, one may show that, given
F\in L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and

f\in L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f)&\quad&\text{ in }\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

has a unique solution in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
Moreover, if
d\geq 3
,
the solution satisfies

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\left\{\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{2-d}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\},

(3.5)

and if
d=2
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln(\eta/2)|^{-1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\}.

The constants
C
in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
)
depend only on
d
and
c_{0}
in (
1.7
).

The following is one of the key estimates in this paper.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.8
) and
2\leq p<\infty
.
Given
F\in L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\cap L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and

f\in L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
let
u
denote the weak solution of (
3.4
) in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
Then
u\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\left\{\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{2-d}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\},

(3.7)

where
d\geq 3
and
C
depends on
d
,
p
and
c_{0}
.
If
d=2
, we have

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\left\{\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\}.

(3.8)

Proof.

We give the proof for the case
d\geq 3
.
The proof for the case
d=2
is similar.

For
\ell\geq 1
, let

v_{\ell}=\min\{|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2}\}u,

where
p>2
(the case
p=2
is given by (
3.5
) and (
3.6
)).
Note that

\displaystyle\nabla v_{\ell}

\displaystyle=(p-2)|u|^{p-2}\chi_{\{|u|<\ell\}}\nabla u+\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})\nabla u

\displaystyle=(p-1)|u|^{p-2}\chi_{\{|u|<\ell\}}\nabla u+\ell^{p-2}\chi_{\{|u|\geq\ell\}}\nabla u.

It follows that
v_{\ell}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and

\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{\ell}\,dx=\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}Fv_{\ell}\,dx-\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}f\cdot\nabla v_{\ell}\,dx.

This gives

\displaystyle c\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|F|\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|u|\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|f|\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|\nabla u|\,dx,

where
c
depends on
p
.
By using the Cauchy inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|F|\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|u|\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|f|^{2}\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})\,dx\right\}.

Next, let

w_{\ell}=\min(|u|^{\frac{p}{2}-1},\ell^{\frac{p}{2}-1})u.

Note that
w\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and

|\nabla w_{\ell}|\approx\min(|u|^{\frac{p}{2}-1},\ell^{\frac{p}{2}-1})|\nabla u|.

It follows by Lemma
3.1
and (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|w_{\ell}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla w_{\ell}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\Bigg\{\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\left(\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\left(\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|u|\right)^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}^{2}\left(\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\left(\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})\right)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\Bigg\},

where we have used Hölder’s inequality for the last step.

Finally, we observe that

|w_{\ell}|^{2}=\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|u|^{2},

and thus

\displaystyle\left(\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})|u|\right)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq|w_{\ell}|^{2},

\displaystyle\left(\min(|u|^{p-2},\ell^{p-2})\right)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}

\displaystyle\leq|w_{\ell}|^{2},

where we have used the assumption
p>2
.
As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\left\{\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}^{2/p^{\prime}}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}^{2}\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}^{2-\frac{4}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d})^{p}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}^{p}+C(\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d})^{\frac{p}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon},\eta)}^{p}+(1/2)\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}^{2},

where we have used Young’s inequality for the last step.
Thus, we have proved that

\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|w_{\ell}|^{2}\,dx\leq C(\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d})^{p}\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|F|^{p}\,dx+C(\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d})^{p/2}\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|f|^{p}\,dx,

from which the estimate (
3.7
) follows by letting
\ell\to\infty
and using Fatou’s Lemma.
∎

4
Estimates of
u

For
1<p<\infty
and
\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1]
, we introduce operator norms,

\displaystyle A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=\sup\Big\{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}:

\displaystyle u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\text{ is a weak solution of }-\Delta u=\text{\rm div}(f)\text{ in }\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\text{ with }f\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}=1\Big\},

\displaystyle B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=\sup\Big\{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}:

\displaystyle u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\text{ is a weak solution of }-\Delta u=F\text{ in }\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\text{ with }F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\text{ and }\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}=1\Big\},

\displaystyle C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=\sup\Big\{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}:

\displaystyle u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\text{ is a weak solution of }-\Delta u=\text{\rm div}(f)\text{ in }\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\text{ with }f\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}=1\Big\},

\displaystyle D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=\sup\Big\{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}:

\displaystyle u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\text{ is a weak solution of }-\Delta u=F\text{ in }\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\text{ with }F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\text{ and }\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}=1\Big\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta),B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
,
C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
and
D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
be defined above.
Then

A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=A_{p}(1,\eta),\quad\text{ \ }\quad B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=\varepsilon B_{p}(1,\eta),

(4.5)

C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=\varepsilon\,C_{p}(1,\eta),\quad\text{ \ }\quad D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=\varepsilon^{2}D_{p}(1,\eta).

(4.6)

Proof.

This follows by a simple rescaling, using the observation that
if
-\Delta u=F+\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and

v(x)=u(\varepsilon x)
, then

-\Delta v(x)=\varepsilon^{2}F(\varepsilon x)+\varepsilon\text{\rm div}\{f(\varepsilon x)\}

for
x\in\omega_{1,\eta}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
.
Then

A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=A_{p^{\prime}}(\varepsilon,\eta),\quad B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=C_{p^{\prime}}(\varepsilon,\eta),\quad\text{ and }\quad D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=D_{p^{\prime}}(\varepsilon,\eta),

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
.

Proof.

This follows by a duality argument. Indeed,
let
u,w\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
be weak solutions of

-\Delta u=F+\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and

-\Delta w=G+\text{\rm div}(g)
in
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
, respectively,
where
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and

G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}u\,G\,dx-\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon},\eta}\nabla u\cdot g\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w\,dx

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}w\,F\,dx-\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon},\eta}\nabla w\cdot f\,dx.

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
and
D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
be defined by (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), respectively.

1.

For
2\leq p<\infty
and
\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1]
,

&C\,\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\quad&\text{ for }d=2.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.8)

2.

For
1<p<\infty
and
\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1]
,

D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\,\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}&\quad&\text{ for }d\geq 3,\\
&C\,\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|&\quad&\text{ for }d=2.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.9)

The constants
C
depend only on
d
,
p
and
c_{0}
in (
1.7
).

Proof.

The estimates for
2\leq p<\infty
in (
4.8
) and (

) are given by Theorem
3.3
.
The case

for

∎

In the remaining of this section we shall show that the estimates in Theorem

are sharp.
To this end, we consider a periodically perforated domain,

\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varepsilon(k+\eta T),

(4.10)

where
T
is the closure of a bounded Lipschitz subdomain of
Y
.
We assume that
Y\setminus T
is connected,
B(0,c_{0})\subset T
, and that
dist
(\partial T,\partial Y)\geq c_{0}>0
.
Let
\chi_{\eta}
be a
Y
-periodic function in
H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

-\Delta\chi_{\eta}=\eta^{d-2}\quad\text{ in }\omega_{1,\eta}\quad\text{ and }\quad\chi_{\eta}=0\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega_{1,\eta},

(4.11)

where
\eta\in(0,1]
.
The existence and uniqueness of
\chi_{\eta}
may be proved by using the Lax-Milgram Theorem on the Hilbert space

\{u\in H^{1}_{per}(Y):u=0\text{ in }\eta T\}
, where
H^{1}_{per}(Y)
denotes the closure of the set of
smooth
Y
-periodic functions in
H^{1}(Y)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\chi_{\eta}
be the
Y
-periodic function defined by (
4.11
).
Then, if
d\geq 3
,

\int_{Y}\chi_{\eta}\,dx\approx 1\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(\int_{Y}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\approx\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

(4.12)

If
d=2
, we have

\int_{Y}\chi_{\eta}\,dx\approx|\ln(\eta/2)|\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(\int_{Y}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\approx|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.13)

Proof.

Suppose
d\geq 3
.
Using

\int_{Y}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\,dx=\eta^{d-2}\int_{Y}\chi_{\eta}\,dx,

(4.14)

we obtain

\int_{Y}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\,dx\leq\eta^{d-2}\left(\int_{Y}|\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\left(\int_{Y}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2},

where we have used Poincaré inequality (
2.1
) for
p=2
.
It follows that

\left(\int_{Y}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{Y}\chi_{\eta}\,dx\leq C,

where
C
depends only on
d
and
c_{0}
.
To prove the reverse inequalities, we construct a function
\psi\in H^{1}_{per}(Y)
such that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
in
Y
,

\psi=1
in
Y\setminus C\eta T
,

\psi=0
on
\eta T
, and
|\nabla\psi|\leq C\eta^{-1}
.
Note that

\int_{Y}\psi\,dx\approx 1\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(\int_{Y}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

This yields

\displaystyle\eta^{d-2}\int_{Y}\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Y}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

Hence,
\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\leq C\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}
, which, together with (
4.14
), also gives

\int_{Y}\chi_{\eta}\,dx\geq c>0
.

Consider the case
d=2
.
The proof for the upper bounds is the same.
However, the same choice of
\psi
only produces lower bounds for
\int_{Y}\chi_{\eta}\,dx
and

\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}
by a positive constant
c
.
For optimal lower bounds, we may assume that
\eta
is sufficiently small and

\eta T\subset B(0,C_{0}\eta)\subset B(0,1/4)
.
Let
\psi
be the function in
H^{1}_{per}(Y)
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \psi&=1-\frac{\ln(1/2)}{\ln(C_{0}\eta)}&\quad&\text{ in }Y\setminus B(0,1/2),\\
\psi(x)&=1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln(C_{0}\eta)}&\quad&\text{ for }x\in B(0,1/2)\setminus B(0,C_{0}\eta),\\
\psi&=0&\quad&\text{ in }B(0,C_{0}\eta).\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(4.15)

A direct computation shows that

\int_{Y}\psi\,dx\geq c>0,

and

\int_{Y}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,dx=|\ln(C_{0}\eta)|^{-2}\int_{B(0,1/2)\setminus B(0,C_{0}\eta)}|x|^{-2}\,dx\leq C|\ln\eta|^{-1}.

It follows that

c\leq\int_{Y}\psi\,dx=\int_{Y}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\leq C|\ln\eta|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

As a result, we have proved that
\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\geq c|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2}
.
By (
4.14
), this yields
\int_{Y}\chi_{\eta}\,dx\geq c|\ln(\eta/2)|
.
∎

The next theorem shows that the estimate (

) for

Theorem 4.5
.

Proof.

The upper bounds are given by Theorem

In view of Lemmas
4.1
and

,
to prove the lower bounds, we may assume

and

.

.

in

∎

5
Estimates of
\nabla u

In this section we establish upper and lower bounds for
A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
and
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
, defined by (
4.1
)
and (
4.2
), respectively.
Throughout this section we assume
\{T_{k}\}
are the closures of open subsets of
Y
with uniform
C^{1}
boundaries and satisfy
(
1.7
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.8
).
Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
be a weak solution to the
Dirichlet problem:
-\Delta u=\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and

u=0
on
\partial\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
,
where
f\in L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
1<p<\infty
.
Then

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C_{\delta}\eta^{-d|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|-\delta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

(5.1)

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
,
where
C_{\delta}
depends on
d
,
p
,
\delta
and
\{T_{k}\}
.

Proof.

By rescaling and duality we may assume
\varepsilon=1
and
p>2
.
It follows by Lemma
2.5
that for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\int_{k+(Y\setminus\eta T_{k})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\left\{\eta^{-p}\int_{k+(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T_{k})}|u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{k+(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T_{k})}|f|^{p}\,dx\right\},

where
C
depends on
d
,
p
,
c_{0}
, and the uniform
C^{1}
characters of
\{T_{k}\}
.
By summing the inequalities above over
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
we obtain

\int_{\omega_{1,\eta}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\left\{\eta^{-p}\int_{\omega_{1,\eta}}|u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\omega_{1,\eta}}|f|^{p}\,dx\right\}.

(5.2)

This, together with (
3.7
), gives

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq C_{p}\eta^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}

(5.3)

for any
p>2
and
d\geq 3
.
In the case
d=2
, we may use (
5.2
) and (
3.8
) to obtain

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq C\eta^{-1}|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}

(5.4)

for
p>2
.
Note that for
p=2
, we have the energy estimate,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{1,\eta})}.

(5.5)

Thus, by Riesz-Thorin Interpolation Theorem, if
d\geq 3
and
2<p<p_{0}
,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq C\eta^{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})},

(5.6)

where

\alpha=-\frac{d}{2}\cdot\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

Since

\alpha\to-d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\quad\text{ as }p_{0}\to\infty,

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists
p_{0}>p
such that

\alpha>-d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)-\delta.

As a result, we have proved that if
d\geq 3
,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\delta})}\leq C_{\delta}\,\eta^{-d\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|-\delta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}

(5.7)

for any
p>2
and
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Using (
5.4
), the same argument also yields (
5.1
) for
p>2
in the case
d=2
.
The logarithmic factor can be absorbed into
\eta^{-\delta}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be the same perforated domain as in Theorem
5.1
.
Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
be a weak solution to the
Dirichlet problem:
-\Delta u=F
in
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and

u=0
on
\partial\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
,
where

for some

Then, if

,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\,\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ if }1<p\leq 2,\\
&C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{p}-\delta}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ if }2<p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.8)

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
,
where
C_{\delta}
depends on
d
,
p
,
\delta
and
\{T_{k}\}
.
If
d=2
, one has

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\,\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ if }1<p\leq 2,\\
&C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon\eta^{-1+\frac{2}{p}-\delta}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ if }2<p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.9)

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Since
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=C_{p^{\prime}}(\varepsilon,\eta)
for
1<p<\infty
,
the estimates (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) for the case

are given by Theorem
4.3
.

To treat the case
2<p<\infty
, we may assume
\varepsilon=1
by rescaling.
Suppose
d\geq 3
.
Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\omega_{1,\eta})
be a weak solution of

-\Delta u=F
in
\omega_{1,\eta}
.
Note that by Lemma
2.5
, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\int_{k+(Y\setminus\eta T_{k})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\left\{\eta^{-p}\int_{k+(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T_{k})}|u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{k+(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T_{k})}|F|^{p}\,dx\right\},

(5.10)

where

depends on
d
,
p
,
c_{0}
, and the uniform
C^{1}
characters of
\{T_{k}\}
.
It follows by summation that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\eta^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{1-d}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}

for any
p>2
,
where we have used (
3.7
) for the last inequality.
By Riesz-Thorin Interpolation Theorem, this, together with the

estimate

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq C\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

yields

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq C\eta^{\alpha}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})},

where
2<p<p_{0}
and

\alpha=1-d+\frac{d}{2}\cdot\frac{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

Since

as

, by choosing

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq C_{\delta}\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{p}-\delta}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}

∎

Next we show that

Lemma 5.3

Let

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a cut-off function in

such that

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\varphi=1
in

,

and

Since

This, together with (
5.11
), yields (
5.13
) for
2\leq p<\infty
and
\varepsilon=1
.

The proof for the case
d=2
and
2<p<\infty
is similar.
Indeed,
using (
5.12
), we may show that

\|\nabla(\chi_{\eta}\varphi)\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})}\leq CA_{p}(1,\eta)\big\{R^{\frac{2}{p}+1}+|\ln(\eta/2)|R^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\big\}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}

\displaystyle\leq CA_{p}(1,\eta)\big\{R+|\ln(\eta/2)|R^{-1}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq CA_{p}(1,\eta)|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2},

where we have let
R=C|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2}
.
This, together with (
5.12
), gives (
5.14
) for
2<p<\infty
and
\varepsilon=1
.

Finally, in the case
d=2
and
p=2
, we note that by Lemma
3.1
,

C_{2}(1,\eta)\leq CA_{2}(1,\eta)|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2}.

By Theorem
4.6
,
C_{2}(1,\eta)\geq c|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2}
.
It follows that
A_{2}(1,\eta)\geq c>0
.
∎

The next theorem gives lower bounds for
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
be defined by (
4.2
) for the periodically perforated domain
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
in
(
4.10
).
Then, for
d\geq 3
,

B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} &c\,\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p\leq 2,\\
&c\,\varepsilon\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{p}}&\quad&\text{ for }2<p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.17)

where
c>0
depends only on
d
,
p
and
T
.
If
d=2
, we have

B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} &c\,\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p\leq 2,\\
&c\,\varepsilon\eta^{-1+\frac{2}{p}}&\quad&\text{ for }2<p<\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.18)

Proof.

Since
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=C_{p^{\prime}}(\varepsilon,\eta)
,
the case
1<p\leq 2
is contained in Theorem
4.6
.
To treat the case
2<p<\infty
, we assume
\varepsilon=1
.
As in the proof of Theorem
4.5
, we may use the equation (
4.18
)
to deduce that

\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\leq C\eta^{d-2}B_{p}(1,\eta).

By Lemma
5.3
this leads to

B_{p}(1,\eta)\geq c\,\eta^{2-d}\|\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\geq c\,\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{p}}

for
d\geq 2
.
∎

We are now in a position to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
1<p<\infty
.
It follows from Theorems
4.3
,
5.1
and
5.2
that if
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\cap L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
,
the weak solution of (
1.9
) in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
satisfies the estimates
(
1.10
) and (
1.11
).
By a density argument this implies that for any
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
, the Dirichlet problem (
1.9
) has a solution
u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
,
which satisfies the estimates (
1.10
) and (
1.11
).

To prove the uniqueness, let’s assume that
\Delta u=0
in
\omega_{1,\eta}

and
u=0
on
\partial\omega_{1,\eta}
for some
u\in W^{1,p}(\omega_{1,\eta})
and
p>1
.
By
L^{\infty}
estimates for harmonic functions in Lipschitz domains,

\max_{k+(Y\setminus\eta T_{k})}|u|\leq C_{\eta}\int_{k+(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T_{k})}|u|\,dx,

where
C_{\eta}
depends on
\eta
, but not on
k
.
Since
u\in L^{p}(\omega_{1,\eta})
, we see that
u\in L^{\infty}(\omega_{1,\eta})
and that

u(x)\to 0\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.

By applying the maximum principle in
B(0,R)\cap\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and then letting
R\to\infty
,
we conclude that
u\equiv 0
in
\omega_{1,\eta}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Theorem
1.1
treats the case
d\geq 3
.
The estimates for
d=2
are summarized below.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose
d=2
and
1<p<\infty
.
Let
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be the same as in Theorem
1.1
.
Then, for any
F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and

, the Dirichlet problem
(
1.9
) has a unique solution in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimates,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C_{\delta}\,\eta^{-2|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|-\delta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p\leq 2,\\
&C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon\eta^{-1+\frac{2}{p}-\delta}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C_{\delta}\eta^{-2|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|-\delta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }2<p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.19)

and

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{2}{p}-\delta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p<2,\\
&C\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }2\leq p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.20)

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, where
C
depends on
p
and
\{T_{k}\}
, and
C_{\delta}
also depends on
\delta
.

Proof.

See (
5.1
) and (
5.9
) for (
5.19
), and (
4.8
)-(
4.9
) for (
5.20
).
∎

6
Estimates in a bounded perforated domain

In this section we consider the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
)
in a bounded perforated domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
given by (
1.16
).
Throughout this section, unless indicated otherwise,
we assume that
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain and that

\{T_{k}:\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
are the closures of bounded sub-domains of
Y
with uniform
C^{1}
boundaries.

For
\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1]
,
let
A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
,
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
,
C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
and
D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
be defined by
(
4.1
), (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), but with
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
in the place of
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.16
) and
1<p<\infty
.
For any
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
,
the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimates,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

(6.1)

and

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}.

(6.2)

Proof.

Since
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain, the existence and uniqueness of solutions for (
1.1
)
are given by
[
7
]
.
The estimates (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) follow by linearity and a density argument.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

The three equations in Lemma
4.2
continue to hold for the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.

Proof.

The same duality argument for
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
works equally well for
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
and
D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
be defined by
(
4.3
)
and (
4.4
), respectively, but with

\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
in the place of
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
. Then

1.

For
2\leq p<\infty
,

C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\min(1,\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{2-d}{2}})&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3,\\
&C\min(1,\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2})&\quad&\text{ if }d=2.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.3)

2.

For
1<p<\infty
,

D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\min(1,\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d})&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3,\\
&C\min(1,\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|)&\quad&\text{ if }d=2.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.4)

The constants
C
depend on
d
,
p
,
c_{0}
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

The case
2\leq p<\infty
is given by Theorem
3.4
, while
the case
1<p<2
for
D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
follows from the fact
D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=D_{p^{\prime}}(\varepsilon,\eta)
.
Note that the
C^{1}
conditions for
\Omega
and
\{T_{k}\}
are not needed for (
6.3
) and (
6.4
).
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
be a weak solution of the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
), where

F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
2<p<\infty
.
Then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\left\{(\varepsilon\eta)^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\},

(6.5)

where
C
depends on
d
,
p
,
\Omega
and
\{T_{k}\}
.

Proof.

We claim that
for any
x_{0}\in\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
,

\int_{B(x_{0},c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{B(x_{0},8c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\left((\varepsilon\eta)^{-p}|u|^{p}+|F|^{p}+|f|^{p}\right)\,dx,

(6.6)

where
c_{1}=(c_{0}/100)
.
To see this,
we consider two cases.
In the first case we assume
B(x_{0},2c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
The estimate (
6.6
) then follows by the standard interior estimates for Laplace’s equation.
In the second case we assume
B(x_{0},2c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\neq\emptyset
.
Choose
y_{0}\in B(x_{0},2c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
Then
B(x_{0},c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\subset B(y_{0},3c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)
.
Since
\partial\Omega
is a
C^{1}
domain and
\{T_{k}\}
are the closures of bounded domains with uniform
C^{1}
boundaries,
it follows from (
2.6
) by a localization argument that

\int_{B(y_{0},3c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{B(y_{0},6c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\left((\varepsilon\eta)^{-p}|u|^{p}+|F|^{p}+|f|^{p}\right)\,dx,

(6.7)

where we have used the fact
u=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
Note that
B(y_{0},6c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)\subset B(x_{0},8c_{1}\varepsilon\eta)
.
As a result, (
6.6
) follows from (
6.7
).
Finally, we obtain (
6.5
) by integrating both sides of (
6.6
) in
x_{0}
over
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

and using Fubini’s Theorem.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
be defined by (
4.2
), but with
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
in the place of
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
Then

1.

For
1<p\leq 2
,

B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\min(1,\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{2-d}{2}})&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3,\\
&C\min(1,\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{1/2})&\quad&\text{ if }d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.8)

where
C
depends on
d
,
p
,
c_{0}
and
\Omega
.

2.

For
2<p<\infty
and
d\geq 2
,

B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\leq C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon\eta\big\{\min((\varepsilon\eta)^{-1},\eta^{-\frac{d}{2}})\big\}^{2-\frac{2}{p}+\delta}

(6.9)

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, where
C_{\delta}
depends on
d
,
p
,
\delta
,
\{T_{k}\}
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)=C_{p^{\prime}}(\varepsilon,\eta)
by Lemma
6.2
,
the estimate (
6.8
) follows from (
6.3
).
To prove (
6.9
), we first consider the case
d\geq 3
.
Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
be a weak solution of

-\Delta u=F
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
, where
F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
By (
6.3
) and (
6.5
) we obtain

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C(\varepsilon\eta)^{-1}\big\{\min(1,\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{2-d}{2}})\big\}^{2}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

(6.10)

for any
p>2
.
By Riesz-Thorin Theorem,
this, together with the
L^{2}
estimate,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\min(1,\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{2-d}{2}})\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

yields

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\varepsilon\eta\big\{\min((\varepsilon\eta)^{-1},\eta^{-\frac{d}{2}})\big\}^{1+t}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

where

t=\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

and
2<p<p_{0}
.
Since
t\to 1-\frac{2}{p}
as
p_{0}\to\infty
, by choosing
p_{0}
sufficiently large, we obtain

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon\,\eta\big\{\min((\varepsilon\eta)^{-1},\eta^{-\frac{d}{2}})\big\}^{2-\frac{2}{p}+\delta}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
.
The proof for the case
d=2
is similar (the factor
\ln(\eta/2)
is absorbed by
\eta^{-\delta}
).
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
be defined by (
4.1
), but with
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
in the place of
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
Then

A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\leq C_{\delta}\big\{\min((\varepsilon\eta)^{-2},\eta^{-d})\big\}^{|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|+\delta}

(6.11)

for
d\geq 2
and
1<p<\infty
, where
C_{\delta}
depends on
d
,
p
,
\delta
,
\Omega
and
\{T_{k}\}
.

Proof.

Consider the case
d\geq 3
and
2<p<\infty
.
Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
be a weak solution of

-\Delta u=\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
, where

f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It follows from (
6.5
) and (
6.3
) that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C(\varepsilon\eta)^{-1}\min(1,\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{2-d}{2}})\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}.

By interpolation, this, together with the

estimate
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}
,
yields

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\big\{\min((\varepsilon\eta)^{-1},\eta^{-\frac{d}{2}})\big\}^{t}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

where
t
is the same as in the proof of Theorem
6.5
and
2<p<p_{0}
.
Since

.
The proof for the case

is similar.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

This follows from Lemma

, and

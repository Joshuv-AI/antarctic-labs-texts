The Fokas-Lenells equations: Bilinear approach
Shu-zhi Liu, Jing Wang, Da-jun Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, the Fokas-Lenells equations are investigated via bilinear approach. We bilinearize the unreduced Fokas-Lenells system, derive double Wronskian solutions, and then, by means of a reduction technique we obtain variety of solutions of the reduced equations. This enables us to have a full profile of solutions of the classical and nonlocal Fokas-Lenells equations. Some obtained solutions are illustrated based on asymptotic analysis. As a notable new result, we obtain solutions to the Fokas-Lenells equation, which are related to real discrete eigenvalues and not reported before in the analytic approaches. These solutions behave like (multi-)periodic waves or solitary waves with algebraic decay. In addition, we also obtain solutions to the two-dimensional massive Thirring model from those of the Fokas-Lenells equation. Key Words: Fokas-Lenells equation, bilinear, double Wronskian, nonlocal, real eigenvalue

Introduction

The Fokas-Lenells (FL) equation,

iu_{t}-\nu u_{tx}+\gamma u_{xx}+\sigma|u|^{2}(u+i\nu u_{x})=0,\quad\sigma=\pm 1,

(1)

as a novel generalization of the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation,
was first derived using bi-Hamiltonian structures of the NLS equation
[
1
]
,
where
\nu
and
\gamma
are real parameters.
This equation is integrable and belongs to
the derivative nonlinear Schrödinger (DNLS) hierarchy
[
2
]

that is related to the Kaup-Newell (KN) spectral problem.
It is notable that the FL equation (
1
) is equivalent to the following one
[
3
]

u_{xt}+u-2i\delta|u|^{2}u_{x}=0,~~\delta=\pm 1.

(2)

The latter is derived for modeling propagation of nonlinear pulses in monomode optical fibers
where
u
is assumed to describe the slowly varying envelope of the pulse
[
3
]
.
It is interesting that Eq.(
2
) is a reduced potential form of the first negative member in the KN hierarchy,

\displaystyle u_{xt}+u-2iuvu_{x}=0,

(3a)

\displaystyle v_{xt}+v+2iuvv_{x}=0,

(3b)

by imposing reduction
v=\delta u^{*}
, where
i
is the imaginary unit and
*
denotes complex conjugate.
Both (
1
) and (
2
) can be called the Fokas-Lenells equation,
and in the following we call (
3
) the pKN
(-1)
for convenience.
Note that the pKN
(-1)
system (
3
) is also known as the Mikhailov model
(cf.
[
4
,
5
]
).
It is A.V. Mikhailov in 1976 who first gave a Lax pair for the two-dimensional massive Thirring model
in laboratory coordinates.
Later it was shown that the Lax pair in light-cone coordinates is gauge equivalent to
the KN spectral problem
[
7
]
and solutions of the massive Thirring model
could be obtained by solving Eq.(
2
), i.e. the reduced pKN
(-1)

[
7
,
4
]
.
We will explain how Eq.(
2
) and the massive Thirring model are related in Appendix
A
.

Before Fokas and Lenells’ work, Eq.(
2
) has been solved using
direct linearization approach
[
8
]
.
More recently,
by virtue of a clear integrable background associated with the well studied KN spectral problem,
solutions of the FL equation (

Without using Lax pairs, the FL equation (

determinantal solutions of the bilinear FL equations were constructed in a delicate direct way

Another direct approach was presented in

2
Preliminary

2.1
Integrability of the FL equations

As an integrable background let us recall the relation between the FL equation and the KN hierarchy.
The KN spectral problem reads
[
7
,
43
]

\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}\\
\varphi_{2}\end{pmatrix}_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{i}{2}\lambda^{2}&\lambda q\\
\lambda r&-\frac{i}{2}\lambda^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}\\
\varphi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

from which one can derivative the well known KN hierarchy

\begin{pmatrix}q\\
r\end{pmatrix}_{t_{n}}=L^{n}\begin{pmatrix}-q\\
r\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where the recursion operator
L
is

L=\partial_{x}\begin{pmatrix}-i+2q\partial^{-1}_{x}r&2q\partial^{-1}_{x}q\\
2r\partial^{-1}_{x}r&i+2r\partial^{-1}_{x}q\end{pmatrix},

and
\partial_{x}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
,
\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{x}=\partial_{x}\partial_{x}^{-1}=1
.
Here
\lambda
is the spectral parameter, and both
q
and
r
are functions of
(x,t)
.
When
n=2
, the hierarchy (
6
) yields the second order KN system (KN(2) for short)

\displaystyle q_{t}+iq_{xx}-2(q^{2}r)_{x}=0,

(7a)

\displaystyle r_{t}-ir_{xx}-2(qr^{2})_{x}=0,

(7b)

from which the DNLS equation,

q_{t}+iq_{xx}-2\delta(|q|^{2}q)_{x}=0,~~\delta=\pm 1,

(8)

is obtained by imposing reduction
r=\delta q^{*}
.
When
n=-1
, we have KN
(-1)
equations, i.e.

L\begin{pmatrix}q\\
r\end{pmatrix}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}-q\\
r\end{pmatrix},

which reads( with
t\rightarrow-it
)

\displaystyle q_{t}+\partial^{-1}q-2iq\partial^{-1}(q\partial^{-1}r+r\partial^{-1}q)=0,

(9a)

\displaystyle r_{t}+\partial^{-1}r+2ir\partial^{-1}(q\partial^{-1}r+r\partial^{-1}q)=0.

(9b)

Introduce potentials
(u,v)
by

q=u_{x},~~r=v_{x},

(10)

then (
9
) can easily be written into the local form (
3
).
Thus, the latter is the potential form of the KN
(-1)
equations (
9
).
As we mentioned before, the pKN
(-1)
system (
3
) allows two reductions,

v=\pm u^{*}
and
v(x,t)=\pm u(-x,-t)
.

2.2
Wronskians and some determinantal identities

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be
(N+M)
-th order column vectors

\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{N+M})^{T},~~\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\ldots,\psi_{N+M})^{T},

where elements
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
are smooth functions of
(x,t)

A standard double Wronskian is a determinant of the following form

W^{[N,M]}(\phi;\psi)=|\phi,\partial_{x}\phi,\cdots,\partial_{x}^{N-1}\phi;\psi,\partial_{x}\psi,\cdots,\partial_{x}^{M-1}\psi|.

We introduce short-hand (cf.
[
24
,
45
,
46
]
)

W^{[N,M]}(\phi;\psi)=|\widehat{\phi^{[N-1]}};\widehat{\psi^{[M-1]}}|,

where by
\widehat{\phi^{[N-1]}}
we mean
consecutive columns
(\phi,\partial_{x}\phi,\cdots,\partial_{x}^{N-1}\phi)
.
Without making any confusion, we also employ the conventional compact notation

W^{[N,M]}(\phi;\psi)=|0,1,\cdots,N-1;0,1,\cdots,M-1|=|\widehat{N-1};\widehat{M-1}|

that was introduced in
[
44
]
.

Following the above notations, we introduce four more double Wronskians

\begin{array}[]{l}|\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-1}|=|1,2,\cdots,N;0,1,\cdots,M-1|,\\
|\widehat{N};\widetilde{M-1}|=|0,1,\cdots,N;1,2,\cdots,M-1|,\\
|\overline{N};\widehat{M}|=|2,3,\cdots,N;0,1,\cdots,M|,\\
|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M}|=|1,2,\cdots,N;1,2,\cdots,M|,\end{array}

(12)

which will be employed in presenting solutions of the bilinear FL-equations in next section.

We also need the following identities which will be used in verifying solutions of bilinear equations.

Lemma 1
.

[
47
]

|\mathbf{M},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}||\mathbf{M},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}|-|\mathbf{M},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}||\mathbf{M},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{d}|+|\mathbf{M},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d}||\mathbf{M},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}|=0,

where
\mathbf{M}
is an arbitrary
N\times(N-2)
matrix, and
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{b}
,

\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{d}
are
N
th-order column vectors.

Lemma 2
.

[
48
,
22
]

Let

be a

matrix with

\{\alpha_{j}\}
.
\Gamma=(\gamma_{js})_{N\times N}
is an

operator matrix and each

A_{j}\circ B_{j}=(A_{1,j}B_{1,j},A_{2,j}B_{2,j},\ldots,A_{N,j}B_{N,j}),

in which
A_{j}=(A_{1,j},A_{2,j},\ldots,A_{N,j})^{T}
and
B_{j}=(B_{1,j},B_{2,j},\ldots,B_{N,j})^{T}

are
N
th-order column vectors.

6
Concluding remakks

We have derived solutions for the classical FL equation (
2
) and nonlocal FL equation (
4
)
from bilinear approach.
We introduced new double Wronskian expressions (
16
) that are different from
those of the AKNS hierarchy, the KN equation and the Chen-Lee-Liu equation
(cf.
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
).
The assumption (
17
) with a general
A
for Wronskian entries
and the reduction technique enable us to have a full profile
for the solutions of the FL equations.
One- and two-soliton solutions were illustrated based on analysis in detail.
It is notable that the FL equation (
2
) also allows solutions related to real discrete eigenvalues.
They exhibit (multi-)periodic behavior for distinct eigenvalues and algebraic decayed solitary waves
for those eigenvalues with multiplicity two. The later case was not
found before in the analytic approaches (e.g.
[
2
,
9
,
10
]
)
that are based on analyzing analytic domains of wave functions.

Before Fokas and Lenells, the FL equation (
2
) was already explored around 40 years ago
(cf.
[
4
,
8
]
),
as it could generate solutions to the massive Thirring model arose in relativistic quantum field theory.
In Appendix
A
we will recall the links between the FL equation (
2
) and the
massive Thirring model. As a result, all the solutions we obtained for the FL equation (
2
) can
generate solutions to the massive Thirring model (see Theorem
4
in Appendix
A
).

In this paper solutions are presented in terms of (double) Wronskians.
Solutions in this form and similar forms are usually obtained via Darboux transformations
or bilinear method.
Compared with other popular forms of
N
-soliton solutions (e.g. Hirota’s form using polynomials of
exponential functions given in Appendix
B

and dressed Cauchy matrix form obtained in
[
11
,
15
,
17
]
),
by virtue of their special structure,
Wronskian solutions have advantage in presenting limit solutions, i.e. multiple-pole solutions,
In bilinear approach, such solutions are alternatively obtained by taking, for example,

A
in (
17
) to be composed of Jordan blocks.
One may refer to
[
50
]
(see page 22 of the book) for the limit procedure
and to
[
48
]
for the connections between the LTTMs and limit solutions.
For the multiple-pole solutions in terms of dressed Cauchy matrix form, one may refer to

[
53
]
for the Riemann-Hilbert method and to
[
54
]

for the Cauchy matrix approach.
In addition, by employing double Wronskians, coefficient matrix
A
and bilinear approach,
we have also illustrated an effective reduction technique to obtain solutions for the reduced equations.
In this technique, looking for vectors
\phi
and
\psi
such that
u
and
v
satisfy desired constraints
when they are expressed in terms of double Wronskians
f,g,h
and
s
, is boiled down to solving the
algebraic equation (
24
) (and (
52
) for nonlocal case).
This enables us to approach to new solutions that might be missed before,
for example, the case (2) in Table
1
that corresponds to real eigenvalues and the mixed case related to
(
46
).

Finally, as remarks we list several possible interesting questions arising from the current paper.
The first is to reinvestigate the coupled KN equation (
7
) and the DNLS equation (
8
)
using the double Wronskian structure given in (
16
). Since the couple system (
3
)
is the potential form of the KN
(-1)
system (
9
),
it is possible to get solutions for the coupled KN equation (
7
) from
q=(g/f)_{x},~r=(h/s)_{x}
after
redefining
\phi,\psi
with the dispersion relation of (
7
).
Similar treatment was done in
[
11
]
.
The second is to investigate the FL equations with nonzero backgrounds from bilinear approach.
Note that the assumption (
17
) corresponds to
q=r=0
in the Lax pair.
In addition, it would be interesting to reinvestigate possible analytic domains of wave functions
for the Cauchy problem where
|u(x,t=0)|^{2}
is algebraic decayed as
|x|\to\infty
.
This was not touched in
[
2
,
9
,
10
]
.
Finally, solving the nonlocal FL equation from an analytic approach is also an interesting
problem. Note that the analysis in Sec.
5.1
implies
soliton solutions arise from the eigenvalue distribution
\mathbf{H}_{N}=\mathbf{K}_{N}^{*}

or
\mathbf{H}_{N}=-\mathbf{K}_{N}^{*}
.
However, interactions of 2SS exhibit more varieties in nonlocal case.

\displaystyle e^{\vartheta_{j,s}}=(k_{j}-k_{s})(w_{j}-w_{s}),\quad(j<s=2,3,\ldots,N),

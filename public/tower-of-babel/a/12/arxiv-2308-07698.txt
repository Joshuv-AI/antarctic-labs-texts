Polynomization of the Bessenrodt–Ono type inequalities for A A -partition functions
Krystian Gajdzica, Bernhard Heim, Markus Neuhauser

Abstract
. For an arbitrary set or multiset A A of positive integers, we associate the A A -partition function p A ​ ( n ) p_{A}(n) (that is the number of partitions of n n whose parts belong to A A ). We also consider the analogue of the k k -colored partition function, namely, p A , − k ​ ( n ) p_{A,-k}(n) . Further, we define a family of polynomials f A , n ​ ( x ) f_{A,n}(x) which satisfy the equality f A , n ​ ( k ) = p A , − k ​ ( n ) f_{A,n}(k)=p_{A,-k}(n) for all n ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} and k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} . This paper concerns the polynomization of the Bessenrodt–Ono type inequality for f A , n ​ ( x ) f_{A,n}(x) : f A , a ​ ( x ) ​ f A , b ​ ( x ) > f A , a + b ​ ( x ) , \displaystyle f_{A,a}(x)f_{A,b}(x)>f_{A,a+b}(x), where a a and b b are arbitrary positive integers; and delivers some efficient criteria for its solutions. Moreover, we also investigate a few basic properties related to both functions f A , n ​ ( x ) f_{A,n}(x) and f A , n ′ ​ ( x ) f_{A,n}^{\prime}(x) .

Let us recall some basic definitions and known results.
For a non-negative integer

At first, let us fix some notations and conventions. By

,

\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
we denote
the set of real numbers, the set of positive integers,
and the set of non-negative integers, respectively. For an arbitrary multiset of positive integers

,
\mu(a)
denotes the number of appearances of the
number
a
in
A
. Further, for a fixed finite or infinite unbounded set or multiset of positive integers
A
, the
A
-partition function

is the number of
A
-partitions of

(actually, if

is infinite, we have to require that

for every

, otherwise there will be infinitely many numbers

). In such a setting, we also introduce a family of polynomials associated to the
A
-partition function via the power series expansion (

).

It is straightforward to see that
f_{A,0}(x)=1
and
f_{A,n}(1)=p_{A}(n)
. Furthermore, if we consider the
k
-colored

-partition function

, then we have that
p_{A,-k}(n)=f_{A,n}(k)
for all

and

. Moreover, there are recurrence formulae for the polynomials
f_{A,n}(x)
and
f_{A,n}^{\prime}(x)
, as well.

Proposition 2.1
.

For a fixed set (or multiset)
A
of positive integers, the following identities

(2.1)

f_{A,n}(x)=\frac{x}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{A}(j)f_{A,n-j}(x)

and

(2.2)

f_{A,n}^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,n-j}(x),

are true for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\sigma_{A}(i)
is a sum of those divisors of
i
that belong to
A
.

Proof.

If we denote the left hand side of the equality
(\ref{def:polynomization})
by
F(q,x)
and compare the appropriate coefficients standing next to
q^{n}
of both

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F(q,x)}{\partial q}=\frac{\partial\log{(F(q,x))}}{\partial q}F(q,x)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F(q,x)}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial\log{(F(q,x))}}{\partial x}F(q,x),

we obtain the required property.
∎

Now, it is not difficult to notice that if
1\in A
, then
f_{A,n}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
n
. Otherwise, we have that
f_{A,1}(x)=0
. In order to omit such a confusion, we assume that henceforth

and

occurs exactly one time in

. In addition, that assumption asserts that
\gcd A=1
which is a necessary and sufficient condition to have the
Bessenrodt–Ono type inequality for

is a finite set or multiset, see
[
16
]
. Next, we introduce an additional notation, namely,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{A,n}(x):=f_{A,n+1}(x)-f_{A,n}(x).

The following characterization follows from the above discussion.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
with
x\geq 1
. Then

f_{A,n}(x)\leq f_{A,n+1}(x)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt1\leq f_{A,n}^{\prime}(x)<f_{A,n+1}^{\prime}(x).

For
x>1
, we additionally have that

f_{A,n}(x)<f_{A,n+1}(x).

Proof.

At first, we show that
f_{A,n}(x)\leq f_{A,n+1}(x)
. The proof is by induction on
n
under the assumption that
x\geq 1
. Let us notice that
f_{A,1}(x)=x
and
f_{A,2}(x)=x(x+(1+2s_{2}))/2
, where

is the number of

. Thus, we get that

\displaystyle\Delta_{A,1}(x)\geq\frac{x}{2}(x-1)\geq 0

for every

. Now, we assume that the statement is valid for all

and

, and check its correctness for

. The identity (

Thus, we have that the inequality

is satisfied for every
x\geq 1
. One can also notice that

f_{A,n+1}(1)=p_{A}(n+1)=p_{A}(n)+p_{A\setminus\{1\}}(n+1)\geq f_{A,n}(1).

Hence, the function

is positive for every

holds for all such

Theorem 3.3

For every set

\displaystyle f_{A,2}(3)=\begin{cases}9,&\text{if }2\in A,\\

\displaystyle f_{A,1}(3)f_{A,1}(3)=9\geq f_{A,2}(3).

which is not a difficult task. This ends the proof by the law of induction.
∎

There are a few direct consequences of Theorem
4.1
. Since they are very similar to Corollary

and Remark

, we omit them and proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof.

We show the claim by induction on
a+b
. For
a+b=2
, we have that

\displaystyle f_{A,1}(x)f_{A,1}(x)-f_{A,2}(x)\geq x^{2}-x/2(x+5)=x(x-5)/2,

which is a consequence of the equality

\displaystyle f_{A,2}(x)=\begin{cases}x(x+5)/2,&\text{if }2\text{ appears exactly two times in }A,\\
x(x+3)/2,&\text{if }2\text{ appears exactly one time in }A,\\
x(x+1)/2,&\text{if }2\not\in A.\end{cases}

For
a+b=3
, on the other hand, one can verify that

\displaystyle f_{A,2}(x)f_{A,1}(x)-f_{A,3}(x)=\begin{cases}x\left(x^{2}-10\right)/3,&\text{if }3\text{ occurs three times in }A,\\
x\left(x^{2}-7\right)/3,&\text{if }3\text{ occurs two times in }A,\\
x(x-2)(x+2)/3,&\text{if }3\text{ occurs one time in }A,\\
x(x-1)(x+1)/3,&\text{if }3\not\in A,\end{cases}

and we see that the claim is true for
a+b=3
. Thus, let us suppose that the statement is valid for all
3\leq a+b\leq n
, and investigate whether it is also correct for
a+b=n+1
with
a
and
b
such that
1\leq b\leq a
and
a\geq 2
. Theorem
4.1
asserts that the inequality
f_{A,a}(5)f_{A,b}(5)>f_{A,a+b}(5)
is true for all positive integers
a
and
b
such that
a+b>2
. Now, it suffices to show that the derivative

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(f_{A,a}(x)f_{A,b}(x)-f_{A,a+b}(x)\right)

is positive for all
x\geq 5
, because it maintains that
f_{A,a}(x)f_{A,b}(x)-f_{A,a+b}(x)>0
. Since
f_{A,n}(x)f_{A,0}(x)=f_{A,n}(x)
, we get

\displaystyle f_{A,a}^{\prime}(x)f_{A,b}(x)+f_{A,a}(x)f_{A,b}^{\prime}(x)-f_{A,a+b}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{a}\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a-j}(x)f_{A,b}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{b}\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a}(x)f_{A,b-j}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{a+b}\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a+b-j}(x)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{a}\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a-j}(x)f_{A,b}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{b}\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a}(x)f_{A,b-j}(x)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{a}\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a-j}(x)f_{A,b}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{b}\frac{\sigma_{A}(a+j)}{a+j}f_{A,b-j}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{b}\left(\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a}(x)-\frac{\sigma_{A}(a+j)}{a+j}\right)f_{A,b-j}(x).

Therefore, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a}(x)-\frac{\sigma_{A}(a+j)}{a+j}\geq 0

for each
j
between
1
and
b
. Since
a\geq b
and

, it is clear that

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{A}(j)}{j}f_{A,a}(x)-\frac{\sigma_{A}(a+j)}{a+j}\geq\frac{1}{a}\binom{x+a-1}{a}-4a+1

is valid for each such a

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\binom{x+a-1}{a}-4a+1\geq(a+1)(a+2)(a+3)(a+4)/(24a)-4a+1.

Now, we just examine when the function

\displaystyle\psi(x):=(x+1)(x+2)(x+3)(x+4)-96x^{2}+24x

∎

Pentavalent symmetric graphs of order twice a prime power
Yan-Quan Feng, Jin-Xin Zhou

Abstract
A connected symmetric graph of prime valency is basic if its automorphism group contains no nontrivial normal subgroup having more than two orbits. Let p p be a prime and n n a positive integer. In this paper, we investigate properties of connected pentavalent symmetric graphs of order 2 ​ p n 2p^{n} , and it is shown that a connected pentavalent symmetric graph of order 2 ​ p n 2p^{n} is basic if and only if it is either a graph of order 6 6 , 16 16 , 250 250 , or a graph of three infinite families of Cayley graphs on generalized dihedral groups – one family has order 2 ​ p 2p with p = 5 p=5 or 5 | ( p − 1 ) 5\mid(p-1) , one family has order 2 ​ p 2 2p^{2} with 5 | ( p ± 1 ) 5\mid(p\pm 1) , and the other family has order 2 ​ p 4 2p^{4} . Furthermore, the automorphism groups of these basic graphs are computed. Similar works on cubic and tetravalent symmetric graphs of order 2 ​ p n 2p^{n} have been done. It is shown that basic graphs of connected pentavalent symmetric graphs of order 2 ​ p n 2p^{n} are symmetric elementary abelian covers of the dipole Dip 5 \hbox{\rm Dip}_{5} , and with covering techniques, uniqueness and automorphism groups of these basic graphs are determined. Moreover, symmetric ℤ p n {\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n} -covers of the dipole Dip 5 \hbox{\rm Dip}_{5} are classified. As a byproduct, connected pentavalent symmetric graphs of order 2 ​ p 2 2p^{2} are classified. Key Words: Symmetric graph, Cayley graph, regular covering, normal cover. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C25, 20B25.

Symmetric elementary abelian covers of
\hbox{\rm Dip}_{5}

The main purpose of this paper is to determine basic graphs of pentavalent symmetric graphs of order twice a prime power and to compute their automorphism groups. To do that, we need the so
called regular covering and this was treated in an extensive way
[
36
,
37
,
39
,
41
]
. In this section, we classify symmetric elementary abelian covers of the dipole
\hbox{\rm Dip}_{5}
and compute their automorphism groups by combining Theorem
3.1
.

An epimorphism
{\cal P}:\widetilde{\Gamma}\mapsto\Gamma
of graphs is called a
regular covering projection
or
regular
N
-covering
projection
if
\hbox{\rm Aut}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
has a semiregular subgroup

N
whose orbits on
V(\widetilde{\Gamma})
coincide with the
vertex fibres

{\cal P}^{-1}(v),v\in V(\Gamma)
, and the arc and
edge orbits of
N
coincide with the
arc fibres

{\cal P}^{-1}((u,v)),u\sim v
, and
edge fibres

{\cal P}^{-1}(\{u,v\}),u\sim v
, respectively. In particular, we call
the graph
\widetilde{\Gamma}
a
regular cover
or an
N
-cover
of the graph
\Gamma
, and
N
the
covering
transformation group
. In particular, if
N
is a cyclic or an
elementary abelian group, then we speak of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
as
a
cyclic cover
or an
elementary abelian cover
of

\Gamma
. Let
{\cal P}:\widetilde{\Gamma}\mapsto\Gamma
be a
regular covering projection. An automorphism of
\widetilde{\Gamma}

is said to be
fibre-preserving
if it maps a vertex fibre to a
vertex fibre, and all such fibre-preserving automorphisms form a
group, say
F
, called the
fibre preserving group
. When
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is connected, it is easy
to show that
N
is the kernel of
\hbox{\rm Aut}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
acting on the fibres and
F=N_{\hbox{\rm Aut}(\widetilde{\Gamma})}(N)
. If

\widetilde{\Gamma}
is
F
-arc-transitive, we say that

\widetilde{\Gamma}
is a
symmetric cover
of
\Gamma
.

Two regular covering
projections
{\cal P}:\widetilde{\Gamma}\mapsto\Gamma
and

{\cal P}^{{}^{\prime}}:\widetilde{\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}}\mapsto\Gamma
of a graph

\Gamma
are isomorphic if there exist an automorphism
\alpha\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
and an isomorphism

\widetilde{\alpha}:\widetilde{\Gamma}\mapsto\widetilde{\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}}

\alpha{\cal P}={\cal P}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{\alpha}

\alpha

{\cal P}
and
{\cal P}^{{}^{\prime}}

equivalent

\widetilde{\Gamma}=\widetilde{\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}}

\widetilde{\alpha}
a
lift

\alpha

\alpha

of
\widetilde{\alpha}

{\cal P}
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph and let

be a finite group. Assign to

(u,v)

\zeta(u,v)\in N

\Gamma

N

,

,

by
Proposition
2.5
. It follows that
{\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{2}

(5\ |\ (p\pm 1))
are basic. Finally let
\Gamma={\cal CGD}_{p^{4}}

(p=3
or
p\geq 7)
. Similarly,
\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
has no
normal subgroups of order
p
,
p^{2}
or
p^{3}
(for
p=11
by
MAGMA
[
6
]
and for
p=3,7
or
p\geq 13
by
Theorem
4.3
and Proposition
2.5
), and hence
{\cal CGD}_{p^{4}}

(p=3
or
p\geq 7)
are basic.

On the other hand, let
\Gamma={\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{1}

(p=5
or
5\ |\ (p-1))
,
{\cal CGD}_{p^{3}}

(5\ |\ (p-1))
or
{\cal CGD}_{p^{4}}

(p=2
or
5)

and let
A=\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. To finish the proof, by Theorem
5.2
and Proposition
2.7
, it
suffices to show that
A
has a nontrivial normal subgroup having more than two
orbits. This is true for
\Gamma={\cal CGD}_{5^{2}}^{1},{\cal CGD}_{2^{4}}

or
{\cal CGD}_{5^{4}}
by MAGMA
[
6
]
, and moreover,
\Gamma
is a
normal cover of
{\cal CD}_{5}
,
FQ_{4}
or
{\cal CGD}_{5^{3}}
,
respectively. Let
\Gamma={\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{1}

(5\ |\ (p-1))
. Then

A=\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{1})=R(GD_{p^{2}})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{5}
and
A_{u}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{5}
with
u\in V(\Gamma)
.
The group
R(GD_{p^{2}})
contains a characteristic subgroup of order
p^{2}
, say
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}
, and each subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}
is normal in

R(GD_{p^{2}})
. The number of subgroups of order
p
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}
is

p+1
, and since
5\nmid(p+1)
, at least one of the subgroups of
order
p
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}
is fixed by
A_{u}
. It follows that
A
has a
normal subgroup of order
p
and hence
\Gamma
is a normal cover of

{\cal CD}_{p}

(5\ |\ (p-1))
. Let
\Gamma={\cal CGD}_{p^{3}}

(5\ |\ (p-1))
. Then
R(GD_{p^{3}})
has a characteristic subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{3}
, which has
p^{2}+p+1
subgroups of order
p^{2}
. Since
5\nmid(p^{2}+p+1)
,
A
has a normal
subgroup of order
p^{2}
and
\Gamma
is a normal cover of
{\cal CD}_{p}

(5\ |\ (p-1))
. This completes the proof.

6
Pentavalent Symmetric graphs of order
2p^{2}

As an application of the results obtained in former sections, we classify pentavalent symmetric graphs of order
2p^{2}
.

Theorem 6.1

Let
p
be a prime and
\Gamma
a connected pentavalent symmetric graph
of order
2p^{2}
. Then
\Gamma\cong{\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{1}

(p=5
or
5\ |\ (p-1))
,
{\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{2}

(5\ |\ (p\pm 1))
or
{\cal CD}_{p^{2}}

(5\ |\ (p-1))
.

Proof.
Let
A=\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
and
u\in V(\Gamma)
. By McKay
[
35
]
,
there is no pentavalent symmetric graph of order
8
or
18
. Thus,

p\geq 5
. By Proposition
2.1
,
|A_{u}|\mid 2^{9}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot 5
and hence
|A|=2^{i}\cdot 3^{j}\cdot 5\cdot p^{2}
,
1\leq i\leq 10
,
0\leq j\leq 2
.

First we have an observation.

Observation:
If
p=5
then
A
has a non-abelian Sylow

5
-group.

Suppose that
Q
is an abelian Sylow
5
-subgroup of
A
. Since

|V(\Gamma)|=2\cdot 5^{2}
, we have
|Q|=5^{3}
and
|Q_{u}|=5
. Thus,
Q
has two
orbits, say
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
with
u\in B_{1}
. Since
Q
is abelian,

Q_{u}
fixes every vertex in
B_{1}
and all orbits of
Q_{u}
in
B_{2}

have length
5
. Thus,
\Gamma
contains a subgroup
K_{5,5}
, and by its connectedness,
\Gamma\cong K_{5,5}
, which is contrary to

|\Gamma|=2p^{2}
. The observation follows.

Let
H
be a non-trivial normal abelian
p
-group of an
arc-transitive subgroup
B
of
A
.
Then all orbits of
H
have length
p
or
p^{2}
because
|\Gamma|=2p^{2}
.
Suppose
H_{u}\not=1
. Since
H_{u}\lhd B_{u}
, we have
5\ |\ |H_{u}|
and

p=5
. By Proposition
2.7
,
H
has two orbits on
V(\Gamma)
,
implying that
5^{2}\ |\ |H|
. Consequently,
5^{3}\ |\ |H|
and
H

is an abelian Sylow
5
-subgroup of
A
, which is impossible by
Observation. Thus,
H
is semiregular on
V(\Gamma)
, forcing that

|H|=p
or
p^{2}
. This implies that
H={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}
or

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}
. If
H={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}
then, by Theorem
4.3
,
\Gamma\cong{\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{1}

(p=5
or
5\ |\ (p-1))
or
{\cal CGD}_{p^{2}}^{2}

(5\ |\ (p\pm 1))
, which are the first two families of graphs in the theorem. Thus, we may have the following assumption.

Assumption:
Each non-trivial normal abelian
p
-group of
each arc-transitive subgroup of
A
is semiregular on
V(\Gamma)
and
isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}
.

Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
A
. By Lemma
5.1
and Assumption,
N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Now we prove the following
claim.

Claim:

A
has a semiregular subgroup
L
of order
p^{2}

such that
N\leq L
and
\Gamma
is
N_{A}(L)
-arc-transitive.

By Proposition
2.7
, the quotient graph
\Gamma_{N}
has order
2p

and is
A/N
-arc-transitive. By Proposition
2.5
,
\Gamma_{N}\cong{\cal CD}_{5}

(\cong K_{5,5})
with
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{5})\cong(S_{5}\times S_{5})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
\Gamma_{N}\cong{\cal CD}_{11}
with

\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{11})\cong\hbox{\rm PGL}(2,11)
, or
\Gamma_{N}\cong{\cal CD}_{p}

(p>11
and
5\ |\ (p-1))
with
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{p})\cong D_{p}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{5}
. Since
A/N
is arc-transitive, in each case
A/N

contains a semiregular subgroup of order
p
, say
L/N
. Thus,

10p\mid|A/N|
,
N\leq L
and
L
is a semiregular subgroup of
order
p^{2}
in
A
.

For
p>11
with
5\mid(p-1)
, we have
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{p})\cong D_{p}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{5}
. Since
10p\mid|A/N|
, we have
A/N=\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{p})
and hence
L
is the unique normal Sylow
p
-subgroup of

A
. Thus,
N_{A}(L)=A
and
\Gamma
is
N_{A}(L)
-arc-transitive. For

p=11
,
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{11})=\hbox{\rm PGL}(2,11)
and
L/N
is a Sylow
11
-subgroup of
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{11})
. By ATLAS
[
5
]
,
\hbox{\rm Aut}({\cal CD}_{11})
has a maximal subgroup
M/N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{11}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{10}
such that
L/N\leq M/N
. Thus,
L\lhd M
. Since
M/N\not\leq\hbox{\rm PSL}(2,11)\leq\hbox{\rm PGL}(2,11)
,
M/N
is vertex-transitive and hence arc-transitive on
\Gamma_{N}
. It follows that
M
is arc-transitive on
\Gamma
and
\Gamma
is
N_{A}(L)
-arc-transitive.
For
p=5
,
\Gamma
is a bipartite graph of order
5^{2}
with the orbits of

L
as its bipartite sets. By Lemma
2.8
,
N_{A}(L)

is vertex-transitive. Since Sylow
5
-subgroups of
A
have order

5^{3}
and
|L|=p^{2}
,
N_{A}(L)
contains a Sylow
5
-subgroup of
A
, and hence
\Gamma

is
N_{A}(L)
-arc-transitive.

Now we are ready to finish the proof. Set
B=N_{A}(L)
. By Claim,
\Gamma

is
B
-arc-transitive. Since
L\lhd B
and
L
has two orbits,
\Gamma

is bipartite with its bipartite sets as the orbits of
L
, say
B_{1}

and
B_{2}
with
u\in B_{1}
. By Assumption,
L\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}
. Note
that
L
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
C_{B}(L)
for
p>5
because

p^{3}\nmid|A|
, where
C_{B}(L)
is the centralizer of
L
in
B
.
This is also true for
p=5
by Observation. Thus,
L
is a normal
Sylow
p
-subgroup of
C_{B}(L)
and hence
C_{B}(L)=L\times K
, where

K
is a Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
C_{B}(L)
. In particular,
K
is
characteristic in
C_{B}(L)
. If
K\neq 1
, then
K\unlhd B
because

C_{B}(L)\unlhd B
, and since
p^{2}\nmid|K|
,
K
has more than two
orbits on
V(\Gamma)
. By Proposition
2.7
,
K
is semiregular and
hence
|K|=2
. But the quotient graph
\Gamma_{K}
would have odd
order
p^{2}
and valency
5
, a contradiction. Thus,
K=1
and

C_{B}(L)=L
. Since
B/L=B/C_{B}(L)\lesssim\hbox{\rm Aut}(L)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p(p-1)}
,

B
has a unique normal subgroup of order
2p^{2}
containing
L
, say
R
.
By Lemma
2.8
,
B
contains a regular dihedral
group of order
2p^{2}
containing
L
. By the uniqueness of
R
, we have
R\cong D_{p^{2}}
and hence

\Gamma
is a Cayley graph on
R
. Since
R\lhd B
, Proposition
2.6
implies
\Gamma\cong{\cal CD}_{p^{2}}
(
5\ |\ (p-1)
).

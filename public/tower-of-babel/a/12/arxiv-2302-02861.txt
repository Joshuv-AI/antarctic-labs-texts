Abstract
. The principal eigenvalue for linear elliptic operator has been known to be one of very useful tools to investigate many important partial differential equations. Due to the pioneering works of Berestycki et al. [ 1 , 2 ] , the study of qualitative properties for the principal eigenvalue of nonlocal operators has attracted a lot of attention of the community from theory to application (For examples [ 24 , 25 , 28 , 29 , 31 , 14 , 36 ] ). In this paper, motivated from the study of mathematical modeling the dynamics of infectious diseases in [ 30 , 37 , 38 ] , we analyze the asymptotic properties of the principal eigenvalue of nonlocal inhomogeneous cooperative system with respect to the dispersal rate and dispersal range. This can be done thanks to the deep results of Rainer [ 34 ] , Kriegl and Michor [ 20 ] on the stability of eigenvalue of the variable matrices of zero-order coefficients and extends many results from [ 1 , 22 , 30 ] . Our work provides a fundamental step to investigate the nonlinear system modeling the spreading of the transmitted diseases as mentioned.

Theorem 2.1

Let

such that
\lambda_{1}(x_{0})=\alpha
. Replacing
A(x)-d.I
for

big enough and shrinking the domain

if necessary, we may assume that
\alpha>0
and
\lambda_{1}(x)>0
for all

.

Let
\{x_{k}\}\subset\Omega_{\delta}
be a sequence such that
\bar{\lambda}(A(x_{k}))\geq\lambda_{1}(x_{k})>\alpha-\dfrac{1}{n}>0
for every

. In addition, we may assume that there exists

as

.

Denote by

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\lambda_{1}(x)\|v(x)\|^{2}dx>0;

\displaystyle N_{k}

\displaystyle=\sup_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\|v(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle M_{k}

\displaystyle=2N_{k}\sup_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\|A(x)\|^{2}.

Choose a sequence

with
\epsilon_{k}\to 0^{+}
such that

Now let
\{r_{k}^{\prime}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a sequence such that
0<r_{k}^{\prime}<r_{k}
and

\mathrm{vol}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime}))<\min\left\{\dfrac{\epsilon_{k}A_{k}}{N_{k}(\alpha-1/k)},\dfrac{\epsilon_{k}A_{k}}{2M_{k}}\right\}.

Next, we define
\tilde{f}_{k}(x):=v(x).\chi(|x-x_{k}|)
, where
\chi
is the continuous function satisfying

\chi(t)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\quad|t|<r_{k}^{\prime}\\
0\quad\text{if}\quad|t|>r_{k}.\end{cases}

Since
\lambda_{1}(x)\leq\|A(x)\|
and
|f_{k}(x)^{T}A(x)\tilde{f}_{k}(x)|\leq\|A(x)\|.\|\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)^{T}A(x)\tilde{f}_{k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)^{T}A(x)\tilde{f}_{k}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}v(x)^{T}A(x)v(x)dx-\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\|A(x)\|.\|\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\lambda_{1}(x)\|v(x)\|^{2}dx-\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\|A(x)\|.\|v(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\lambda_{1}(x)\|v(x)\|^{2}dx-\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\lambda_{1}(x)\|v(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\|A(x)\|.\|v(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\lambda_{1}(x)\|v(x)\|^{2}dx-2\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\|A(x)\|.\|v(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq A_{k}-2\sup_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\|A(x)\|\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\|v(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq A_{k}-2N_{k}\sup_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\|A(x)\|\mathrm{vol}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\geq A_{k}-M_{k}\mathrm{vol}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\geq A_{k}(1-\epsilon_{k}/2)

and

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}_{k}\|_{E}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\|\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\|^{2}dx=\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})}\|\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\|\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\|^{2}dx+\int_{B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})}\|\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{A_{k}}{\alpha-1/k}+N_{k}\mathrm{vol}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus B(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{A_{k}}{\alpha-1/k}+\dfrac{\epsilon_{k}A_{k}}{\alpha-1/k}=\dfrac{(1+\epsilon_{k})A_{k}}{\alpha-1/k}.

Therefore, one obtains that

\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)^{T}A(x)\tilde{f}_{k}(x)dx}{\int_{\Omega}\|\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\|^{2}dx}\geq\dfrac{A_{k}(1-\epsilon_{k}/2)}{\dfrac{(1+\epsilon_{k})A_{k}}{\alpha-1/k}}>\alpha-\frac{2}{k}.

By using the convolution of

with a smooth function with small support for each

is

-smooth with

for all

. Therefore, the desired sequene
\{f_{k}\}
is finally defined by
f_{k}:=\tilde{f}_{k}/\|\tilde{f}_{k}\|_{E}
. ∎

2.2.
Variational characterization of the principal eigenvalue

Let us begin with the well-definedness of the principal eigenvalue.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\left({\bf{J}}\right)
holds and
A(x)\in(\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega}))^{N\times N}
with
a_{ij}(x)=a_{ji}(x)>0
whenever
i\neq j
. Then the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})
is well-defined.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{N}}+\bm{\mathcal{A}})
is well-defined. Let us first show that the set

\Lambda:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\colon\exists\varphi\in\mathbf{C}(\overline{\Omega}),\varphi>0,\bm{\mathcal{K}}[\varphi](x)+\lambda\varphi(x)\leq 0\text{ in }\Omega\}

is non-empty. Indeed, let us denote
K(x)=\mathrm{diag}(k_{1}(x),\ldots,k_{N}(x))
by

k_{i}(x):=\int_{\Omega}J_{i}(x-y)dy,\;i=1,\ldots,N.

In addition, we define
\mathbf{K}\colon\mathbf{E}\to\mathbf{C}
given by
\mathbf{K}[\varphi](x)=\left(k_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(x),\ldots,k_{N}(x)\varphi_{N}(x)\right)^{T}
. Then, fix a constant function
\psi=(1,\ldots,1)>0
one always has
\bm{\mathcal{N}}[\psi]=\mathbf{K}[\psi]
. Therefore, for any
\lambda\leq-(N|\bm{\mathcal{A}}|_{\infty}+|\mathbf{K}|_{\infty})
we have

\bm{\mathcal{N}}[\psi](x)+(\bm{\mathcal{A}}+\lambda\bm{\mathcal{I}})[\psi](x)=(\mathbf{K}+\bm{\mathcal{A}}+\lambda\bm{\mathcal{I}})[\psi](x)\leq(\mathbf{K}+\bm{\mathcal{A}}-N|\bm{\mathcal{A}}|_{\infty}\bm{\mathcal{I}}-|\mathbf{K}|_{\infty}\bm{\mathcal{I}})[\psi](x)\leq 0

for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\displaystyle|\bm{\mathcal{A}}|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\overline{\Omega};1\leq i,j\leq N}|a_{ij}(x)|
and
\displaystyle|\mathbf{K}|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\overline{\Omega};1\leq i\leq N}|k_{i}(x)|
. This implies that
\Lambda
is non-empty, as desired.

Next, since
J>0
and
\|A(x)\|
is bounded on
\overline{\Omega}
, for any continuous positive function
\phi
it follows that

\bm{\mathcal{N}}[\psi](x)+(\bm{\mathcal{A}}+|\bm{\mathcal{A}}|_{\infty}\bm{\mathcal{I}})[\phi](x)\geq 0.

Hence, the set
\Lambda
has upper bound and thus
\lambda_{p}
is well-defined.
∎

2.3.
Counterexample

In this subsection, we introduce several examples of nonlocal equation where no positive bounded eigenfunction exists.

In
[
13
, Theorem
5.1
]
, the author considered the following principal eigenvalue problem:

(2.1)

\rho\int_{\Omega}udx+a(x)u=\lambda u,

where
\rho>0
and
a(x)\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies the condition
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma-a(x)}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, where
\sigma=\max_{\overline{\Omega}}a(x)
. Then he proved that if
\displaystyle\rho\int_{\Omega}\frac{dx}{\sigma-a(x)}<1
, then there exists no bounded continuous positive principal eigenfunction
\phi
to the above equation (cf.
[
13
, Theorem
5.1
]
). However, following his proof even no positive eigenfunction
\phi\in L^{1}(\Omega)
exists.

We note that in the equation (
2.1
), by replacing
a
by
a-\sigma
and then dividing both sides of (
2.1
) by
\rho
, it suffices to consider the following equation

(2.2)

\int_{\Omega}udx+a(x)u=\lambda u,

where
a(x)\leq 0
and
\sigma=\max_{\overline{\Omega}}a(x)=0
. In addition, we have the following theorem.

Proposition 2.4
.

(a)

If
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{dx}{-a(x)}<1
, then there exists no bounded positive principal eigenfunction
\phi\in L^{1}(\Omega)
to (
2.2
).

(b)

If
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{dx}{-a(x)}\geq 1
, then for each positive function
\phi\in L^{1}(\Omega)
there exists
\lambda_{\phi}
such that
(\lambda_{\phi},\phi)
is an eigenpair of (
2.2
).

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a positive function
\phi\in L^{1}(\Omega)
that we normalize by
\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)dx=1
. Then, one considers the following equation

\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)dx+a(x)\phi(x)=\lambda\phi(x),\;x\in\Omega.

This is equivalent to

\phi(x)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda-a(x)},\;x\in\Omega,

and hence,
\lambda>0
and

(2.3)

F(\lambda):=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{dx}{\lambda-a(x)}=1.

Note that
\displaystyle F(\lambda)\leq\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{dx}{-a(x)}
. Therefore, if
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{dx}{-a(x)}<1
, then there is no
\lambda\geq 0
satisfying the equation (
2.2
), which proves
(a)
. Thus, we consider the case that
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{dx}{-a(x)}\geq 1
. Then, thanks to the continuity of the function
F(\lambda)
,
F(0)\geq 1
, and
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}F(\lambda)=0
, there exists
\lambda=\lambda_{p}\geq 0
such that
(\lambda_{\phi},\phi)
is an eigenpair of (
2.2
), which proves
(b)
.
∎

A typical example for the function
a(x)
is the function
a(x)=-\sqrt{x}
defined on
\Omega=(0,1/5)
satisfying that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/5}\frac{dx}{-a(x)}=\int_{0}^{1/5}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}<1
. Let us fix
\epsilon\in\Big(0,\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}-1\Big)
. We now consider the following eigenvalue problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/5}J(x-y)u(y)dy-u(x)+\dfrac{2}{3}(1-\sqrt{x})u(x)+\dfrac{1}{3}(1-\sqrt{x})v(x)=\lambda_{p}u(x),&x\in\;[0,1/5],\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/5}J(x-y)v(y)dy-v(x)+\dfrac{1}{3}(1-\sqrt{x})u(x)+\dfrac{2}{3}(1-\sqrt{x})v(x)=\lambda_{p}v(x),&x\in\;[0,1/5],\end{array}\right.

where
J(x)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left((-1,1)\right)
be a nonnegative, symmetric function such that
|J(x)-1|<\epsilon
for all
x\in[0,1/5]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

We have that there exists no positive principal eigenfunction
(u,v)\in L^{1}([0,1/5])\times L^{1}([0,1/5])
to (
2.3
).

Proof.

We ague by contradiction. We suppose otherwise that there exists an eigenpair
((u,v),\lambda_{p})
of (
2.3
).
Then by adding two equations in (
2.3
) we obtain the following equation

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/5}J(x-y)\phi(y)dy-\sqrt{x}\phi(x)=\lambda_{p}\phi(x),\;x\in[0,1/5],

where
\phi(x):=(u(x)+v(x))/2>0
. Without loss of generality, we may normalize that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/5}\phi(x)dx=1
.
Therefore, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/5}\phi(y)dy+\int_{0}^{1/5}\left(J(x-y)-1\right)\phi(y)dy-\sqrt{x}\phi(x)=\lambda_{p}\phi(x),\;x\in[0,1/5].

Since
\int_{0}^{1/5}\phi(y)dy=1
and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1/5}\left(J(x-y)-1\right)\phi(y)dy\right|\leq\int_{0}^{1/5}\left|J(x-y)-1\right|\phi(y)dy<\epsilon\int_{0}^{1/5}\phi(y)dy=\epsilon,\;\forall x\in[0,1/5],

it follows that
\displaystyle 1-\epsilon\leq(\lambda_{p}+\sqrt{x})\phi(x)\leq 1+\epsilon
for all
x\in[0,1/5]
. This implies that
\lambda_{p}\geq 0
and

\phi(x)\leq\frac{1+\epsilon}{\lambda_{p}+\sqrt{x}},\;\forall x\in[0,1/5],

which yields

1=\int_{0}^{1/5}\phi(x)dx\leq\int_{0}^{1/5}\frac{1+\epsilon}{\lambda_{p}+\sqrt{x}}dx\leq\int_{0}^{1/5}\frac{1+\epsilon}{\sqrt{x}}dx=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}(1+\epsilon).

This is a contradiction since
\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}(1+\epsilon)<1
. Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

Hence, by

2.7
)
we induce that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{i}(x-y)\left(|\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y)|-\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y)\right)dxdy=0,i=1,\ldots,N,\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(x)|-\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(x)\right)dx=0
for all
i\neq j
(note that
a_{ij}(x)>0
for any
1\leq i,j\leq N
).
Since the functions under the integrals are continuous and non-negative, we follow that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y)\geq 0,\text{ for all }x,y\in\Omega,1\leq i\leq N.

Now, if there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
\phi_{i}(x_{0})<0
, then from
(
2.16
)
,
we have
-{\phi}\in\mathbf{E}^{+}
. In this case, we can choose
-{\phi}
, which is non-negative eigenfunction. Therefore, we may assume that
\phi\geq 0
. Note that
\phi
cannot be a eigenfunction if
\phi_{1}(x_{0})=0
or
\phi_{2}(x_{0})=0
for some
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
. For if it were, assume
\phi_{1}(x_{0})=0
, putting
x=x_{0}
in equation
(
2.15
)
gives

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}d_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x_{0}-y)\phi_{1}(y)dy+\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_{1j}\phi_{j}(x_{0})&=0,\\
d_{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{2}(x_{0}-y)\phi_{2}(y)dy+(a_{22}-d_{2})\phi_{2}(x_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{2j}\phi_{j}(x_{0})&=-\lambda_{-}\phi_{2}(x_{0})\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
d_{N}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{N}(x_{0}-y)\phi_{N}(y)dy+(a_{NN}-d_{N})\phi_{N}(x_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{jN}\phi_{j}(x_{0})&=-\lambda_{-}\phi_{N}(x_{0})\\
\end{array}\right.

Due to
{\bf{(J)}}
and
\phi_{j}\geq 0
then the first equation of
(
2.4
)
yields
\phi_{1}(x)=0,\forall x\in\Omega
and
\phi_{j}(x_{0})=0
for all
2\leq j\leq N

(
2.4
)
lead to
\phi_{j}(x)=0,\forall x\in\Omega
for all
2\leq j\leq N
. This contradiction proves that
{\phi}>0
. The uniqueness can be obtained by a simple consequence, for if
{\phi}
and
\psi
were different eigenfunctions,
{\phi}-\psi
would be an eigenfunction. But this may change sign, contradicting the positive. In orther to show the conclusion, it is sufficient to prove that
\lambda\leq\lambda_{-}
for any eigenvalue

. Assume by contradiction that

we have
\left\langle\bm{\mathcal{K}}{\varphi},{\varphi}\right\rangle\leq\lambda_{-}\|{\varphi}\|^{2},\;\forall{\varphi}\in E,

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda{\varphi}-\bm{\mathcal{K}}{\varphi},{\varphi}\right\rangle\geq\left(\lambda-\lambda_{-}\right)\|{\varphi}\|^{2}=\alpha\|{\varphi}\|^{2},\forall{\varphi}\in\mathbf{E}\;\text{ with }\alpha>0.

\lambda\bm{\mathcal{I}}-\bm{\mathcal{K}}
is bijective
and thus

\lambda

\bm{\mathcal{K}}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

We have

\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})=\lambda_{p}^{\prime}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})=-\sup\limits_{\left\|{\varphi}\right\|_{E}=1}\left\langle\bm{\mathcal{K}}{\varphi},{\varphi}\right\rangle=\lambda_{v}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})=\lambda_{1}(\bm{\mathcal{K}}).

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
2.6
, one has

\lambda_{p}^{\prime}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\leq\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})=-\sup\limits_{\left\|{\varphi}\right\|_{E}=1}\left\langle\bm{\mathcal{K}}{\varphi},{\varphi}\right\rangle=\lambda_{v}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})=\lambda_{1}(\bm{\mathcal{K}}).

We shall prove that
\lambda_{v}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\leq\lambda_{p}^{\prime}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})
.
Indeed,
let
\lambda>\lambda_{p}^{\prime}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})
, then by definition of
\lambda_{p}^{\prime}
there exists
\psi\geq 0
such that
\psi\in\mathbf{C}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

(2.23)

\bm{\mathcal{K}}[\psi](x)+\lambda\psi(x)\geq 0\quad\text{ in }\quad\Omega.

Since
\Omega
is bounded and
\psi\in\mathbf{C}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\psi\in E:=\left(L^{2}(\Omega\right))^{N}
. So, by taking the scalar product (
2.23
) with
\psi
and integrating over
\Omega
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\bm{\mathcal{K}}[\psi](x),\psi(x)\rangle dx

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\langle\psi(x),\psi(x)\rangle dx

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\langle\bm{\mathcal{K}}[\psi],\psi\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda\langle\psi,\psi\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\dfrac{\langle\bm{\mathcal{K}}[\psi],\psi\rangle_{E}}{\|\psi\|^{2}_{E}}

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda.

This yields that

\lambda_{v}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})=-\sup_{0\not\equiv\psi\in\mathbf{C}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\dfrac{\langle\bm{\mathcal{K}}[\psi],\psi\rangle_{E}}{\|\psi\|^{2}_{E}}\leq\lambda

for all
\lambda>\lambda_{p}^{\prime}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})
. Therefore,
\lambda_{v}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\leq\lambda_{p}^{\prime}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})
. Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

We now establish a maximum principle for the operator

Proposition 2.8

(Maximum principle)
.

Assume
(J)
holds and let
A(x)\in(\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega}))^{N\times N}
be a continuous symmetric matrix-valued function on
\overline{\Omega}
such that

whenever

. If
\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\geq 0
, then for all function
{\varphi}=\left(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\right)\in\mathbf{C}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
satisfying

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}{\varphi}\leq 0,\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle{\varphi}\geq 0,\qquad

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega,

then we have
{\varphi}\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
{\varphi}=\left(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\right)\in\mathbf{C}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
,
{\varphi}\not\equiv 0
satisfies
\varphi_{i}(x)\geq 0
with
i=1,\ldots,N
and
x\in\partial\Omega
and

(2.24)

\displaystyle d_{i}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{i}(x-y)\varphi_{i}(y)dy-d_{i}\varphi_{i}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}(x)\varphi_{j}(x)\leq 0,\;\forall x\in\Omega,\;i=1,\ldots,N.

Let
{\varphi}^{p}=(\varphi_{1}^{p},\ldots,\varphi_{N}^{p})\in\mathbf{C}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
be the corresponding eigenfunction to
\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})
. Then we have
\varphi^{p}>0
and

(2.25)

\displaystyle d_{i}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{i}(x-y)\varphi_{i}^{p}(y)dy-d_{i}\varphi_{i}^{p}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}(x)\varphi_{j}^{p}(x)+\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}}))\varphi_{i}^{p}(x)=0,\;\forall x\in\Omega,\;i=1,\ldots,N.

Let us define

\displaystyle\psi=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N}):=\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{1}^{p}},\ldots,\dfrac{\varphi_{N}}{\varphi_{N}^{p}}\right).

Combining (
2.24
) and (
2.25
), we have

\displaystyle d_{1}\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x-y)\varphi_{1}^{p}(y)(\psi_{1}(y)-\psi_{1}(x))dy-\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)\psi_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x-y)\left(\varphi_{1}(y)-\varphi_{1}^{p}(y)\dfrac{\varphi_{1}(x)}{\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)}\right)dy-\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)\dfrac{\varphi_{1}(x)}{\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x-y)\varphi_{1}(y)dy-\dfrac{\varphi_{1}(x)}{\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)}\left(d_{1}\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x-y)\varphi_{1}^{p}(y)dy+\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x-y)\varphi_{1}(y)dy+\dfrac{\varphi_{1}(x)}{\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{1j}(x)\varphi_{j}^{p}(x)-d_{1}\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x-y)\varphi_{1}(y)dy-d_{1}\varphi_{1}(x)+a_{11}(x)\varphi_{1}(x)+\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_{1j}(x)\varphi_{1}(x)\dfrac{\varphi_{j}^{p}(x)}{\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)}

(2.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_{1j}(x)\varphi_{j}(x)+\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_{1j}(x)\varphi_{1}(x)\dfrac{\varphi_{j}^{p}(x)}{\varphi_{1}^{p}(x)}=\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_{1j}(x)\varphi_{j}^{p}(x)\left(\psi_{1}(x)-\psi_{j}(x)\right),\;\text{for any}\;x\in\Omega.

Similarly, (
2.24
) and (
2.25
), we also obtain that

(2.27)

\displaystyle d_{2}\int_{\Omega}J_{2}(x-y)\varphi_{2}^{p}(y)\left(\psi_{2}(y)-\psi_{2}(x)\right)dy-\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\varphi_{2}^{p}(x)\psi_{2}(x)\leq\sum_{j\neq 2}a_{2j}(x)\varphi_{j}^{p}(x)[\psi_{2}(x)-\psi_{j}(x)].

Since
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N}
are continuous functions in
\overline{\Omega}
,
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N}
achieve at some

a minimum, respectively, i.e
\psi_{j}(x_{j})=\min_{\overline{\Omega}}\psi_{j}(x)
for

. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\psi_{N}(x_{N})\leq\cdots\leq\psi_{1}(x_{1})
. We first prove that
\psi_{1}(x_{1})\geq 0

\psi_{j}(x_{j})<0

\varphi_{j}(x)\geq 0
for all

\psi_{j}(x_{j})<0

By putting

into (

(2.28)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}J_{1}(x_{1}-y)\varphi_{1}^{p}(y)\left(\psi_{1}(y)-\psi_{1}(x_{1})\right)dy-\lambda_{p}(\bm{\mathcal{K}})\varphi_{1}^{p}(x_{1})\psi_{1}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_{1j}(x_{1})\varphi_{j}^{p}(x_{1})\left[\psi_{1}(x_{1})-\psi_{j}(x_{1})\right]\leq\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_{1j}(x_{1})\varphi_{j}^{p}(x_{1})\left[\psi_{1}(x_{1})-\psi_{j}(x_{j})\right].

into (

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{i}(x_{i}-y)\varphi_{i}^{p}(y)\left(\psi_{i}(y)-\psi_{i}(x_{i})\right)dy=0,\;i=1,\ldots,N.

\displaystyle J_{i}(x_{i}-y)(\psi_{i}(y)-\psi_{i}(x_{i}))=0,\text{ for all }y\in\Omega,\ i=1,\ldots,N.

(J)

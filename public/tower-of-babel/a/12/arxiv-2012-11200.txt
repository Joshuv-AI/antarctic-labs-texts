Martingale Decomposition and BSDE on Time Scales
Guofeng Tang

Abstract
In this paper, we present martingale decomposition on time scales. We establish the related backward stochastic dynamic equations on time scales (this paper BS ∇ \nabla E for short, concerning ∇ \nabla -integral on time scales) which unify backward stochastic differential equations and backward stochastic difference equations. We prove the existence and uniqueness theorem of BS ∇ \nabla E. This work can be considered as a unification and a generalization of similar results in backward stochastic difference equations and backward stochastic differential equations.

Time Scales, Martingale Decomposition on time scales,

Now we state the ”fundamental decomposition theorem” for the martingales w.r.t Brownian motion on time scales.

Theorem 3.3
.

For every

, with

is orthogonal to every element of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathds{T},k}^{2,*}

Proof.

Uniqueness
:
Suppose that there exists a process

such that
M_{t}=I_{t}(Y)+N_{t}
, where
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathds{T},k}^{2}
has the property

\langle I(X),N\rangle_{t}=0,\quad\forall X\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathds{T},k\times d}^{2}.

indeed, if we have

and both

and

satisfy the property,
then

is in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathds{T},k}^{2}
with

and
\langle Z\rangle=\langle Z,I(Y^{\prime}-Y^{\prime\prime})\rangle=0

Then

(Similar results as Problem1.5.12 in Karatzas
[
24

The decomposition is unique up to indistinguishability.

Existence

Since
\mathcal{R}_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{T})

Taking conditional expectation under

It remains to show that

or equivalently, that

Note that each martingale has a right continuous modification.
So now we suppose that

According to lemma

,
we only need to prove that

The proof is complete.

.
Take any
(y^{1}(\cdot),z^{1}(\cdot),n^{1}(\cdot)),(y^{2}(\cdot),z^{2}(\cdot),n^{2}(\cdot))\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{T}}^{2}\times M^{2}_{\mathds{T}}\times\mathcal{M}_{\mathds{T},k}^{2,\perp}
,
we denote

(Y^{1},Z^{1},N^{1})=I[(y^{1},z^{1},n^{1})],\quad(Y^{2},Z^{2},N^{2})=I[(y^{2},z^{2},n^{2})].

and
\delta y=y^{1}-y^{2}
,
\delta z=z^{1}-z^{2}
,
\delta n=n^{1}-n^{2}
.
By equation(
14
), we get

\begin{split}&E|\delta Y_{0}|^{2}+E\{\int_{0}^{T}\{\frac{\beta}{2}|\delta Y_{s-}|^{2}+|\delta Z_{s}|^{2}\}e_{\beta}(s,0)\nabla s+\int_{0}^{T}e_{\beta}(s-,0)\nabla[\delta N]_{s}\}\\
\leq&\frac{2}{\beta}E\int_{0}^{T}e_{\beta}(s,0)|g(s,y^{1}_{s-},z^{1}_{s})-g(s,y^{2}_{s-},z^{2}_{s})|^{2}\nabla s\\
\leq&\frac{2}{\beta}E\int_{0}^{T}e_{\beta}(s,0)|L|\delta y_{s-}|+L|\delta z_{s}||^{2}\nabla s\\
\leq&\frac{2}{\beta}E\int_{0}^{T}e_{\beta}(s,0)[2L^{2}|\delta y_{s-}|^{2}+2L^{2}|\delta z_{s}|^{2}]\nabla s.\\
\end{split}

We get

\begin{split}&E\{\int_{0}^{T}\{\frac{\beta}{2}|\delta Y_{s-}|^{2}+|\delta Z_{s}|^{2}\}e_{\beta}(s,0)\nabla s+\int_{0}^{T}e_{\beta}(s-,0)\nabla[\delta N]_{s}\}\\
\leq&\frac{4L^{2}}{\beta}E\int_{0}^{T}e_{\beta}(s,0)[|\delta y_{s-}|^{2}+|\delta z_{s}|^{2}]\nabla s.\end{split}

(17)

Let
\beta=8(1+L^{2})
,

\|\delta Y\|_{\beta,T}^{2}+\|\delta Z\|_{\beta,T}^{2}+\|\delta N\|_{\beta,T}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\|\delta y\|_{\beta,T}^{2}+\|\delta z\|_{\beta,T}^{2}).

We can get that the operator
I
is contractive.
Hence, in this case, there exists a unique fixed point
(Y,Z,N)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{T}}^{2}\times M^{2}_{\mathds{T}}\times\mathcal{M}_{\mathds{T},k}^{2,\perp}
for the map
I
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
a,b,c
be predictable bounded processes, let
\mathcal{E}
be the exponential martingale of the martingale
M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}b_{s}\nabla W_{s}
, and define

\gamma_{t}=\exp(\int_{0}^{t}a_{s}\nabla s),~\Gamma_{t}=\gamma_{t}\mathcal{E}_{t}

Suppose that

•

\mathcal{E}_{t}
is a positive uniformly integrable martingale;

•

E[(sup_{t}\gamma_{t})\mathcal{E}_{T}^{2}]<\infty
.

If the linear backward equation

-\nabla Y_{t}=(a_{t}Y_{t-}+b_{t}Z_{t}+c_{t})\nabla t-Z_{t}\nabla W_{t}-\nabla N_{t},~Y_{T}=\xi

has a solution
(Y,Z,N)
, then
Y
is given by

Y_{t}=E[\xi\frac{\Gamma_{T}}{\Gamma_{t}}+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\Gamma_{s}}{\Gamma_{t}}c_{s}\nabla s|\mathcal{F}_{t}].

Proof.

By Girsanov’s theorem
[
22
]
,

\tilde{W}_{t}=W_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}b_{s}\nabla s

is a Q-martingale, the probability measure Q which has density
\mathcal{E}_{T}
with respect to
\mathbf{P}
on
\mathcal{F}_{T}
.
Under the measure Q, the equation becomes

-\nabla Y_{t}=(a_{t}Y_{t-}+c_{t})\nabla t-Z_{t}\nabla\tilde{W}_{t}-\nabla N_{t},~Y_{T}=\xi.

Applying Itô formula to the process
\gamma Y
, we obtain

\gamma_{t}Y_{t}=\gamma_{0}Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}Z_{s}\nabla\tilde{W}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}\nabla N_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}c_{s}\gamma_{s}\nabla s+S

where
S=\sum_{s\in\left(0,t\right]}(\gamma_{s}Y_{s}-\gamma_{s}Y_{s-}-(\gamma_{s}(Y_{s}-Y_{s-})))=0
.
Since
\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}Z_{s}\nabla\tilde{W}_{s}
and
\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}\nabla N_{s}
are martingales,

\gamma_{t}Y_{t}=E^{Q}[\xi\gamma_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}c_{s}\gamma_{s}\nabla s|\mathcal{F}_{t}],

and consequently

\Gamma_{t}Y_{t}=E[\xi\Gamma_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}c_{s}\Gamma_{s}\nabla s|\mathcal{F}_{t}].

This concludes the proof.
∎

We have the one-dimension comparison result

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(Y^{1},Z^{1},N^{1})
,
(Y^{2},Z^{2},N^{2})
be the corresponding solutions to
(g^{1}(t,Y_{t-}^{1},Z_{t}^{1}),\xi^{1})
,
(g^{2}(t,Y_{t-}^{2},Z_{t}^{2}),\xi^{2})

Assume that

and satisfy (H4),
g^{1}(t,Y_{t-}^{2},Z_{t}^{2})\geq g^{2}(t,Y_{t-}^{2},Z_{t}^{2})~a.s.-\mathbf{P}
.
Then, for any
t
, we have
Y_{t}^{1}\geq Y_{t}^{2}~a.s.-\mathbf{P}

Proof.

We define
\delta Y_{t}=Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2}
,
\delta Z_{t}=Z_{t}^{1}-Z_{t}^{2}
,
\delta N_{t}=N_{t}^{1}-N_{t}^{2}
.
Then
\delta Y
solves the following linear BSDE on time scales

-\nabla\delta Y_{t}=(a_{t}\delta Y_{t-}+b_{t}\delta Z_{t}+c_{t})\nabla t-\delta Z_{t}\nabla W_{t}-\nabla\delta N_{t},~\delta Y_{T}=\xi^{1}-\xi^{2},

where

a_{t}=\frac{g^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{1},Z_{t}^{1})-g^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{2},Z_{t}^{1})}{\delta Y_{t}}1_{\delta Y_{t}\neq 0},

b_{t}=\frac{g^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{2},Z_{t}^{1})-g^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{2},Z_{t}^{2})}{\delta Y_{t}}1_{\delta Z_{t}\neq 0},

c_{t}=g^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t-},Z_{t}^{2})-g^{2}(t,Y_{t-}^{2},Z_{t}^{2}).

According to the linear BSDE on time scales, if the coefficients
a_{t},b_{t},c_{t}
verify the suitable conditions, we have

\Gamma_{t}\delta Y_{t}=E[\Gamma_{T}(\xi^{1}-\xi^{2})+\int_{t}^{T}\delta c_{s}\Gamma_{s}\nabla s|\mathcal{F}_{t}].

Assume now that
\xi^{1}\geq\xi^{2}
, and for any
t
,
c_{t}\geq 0~a.s.-\mathbf{P}
.
Then, for any
t
,
Y_{t}^{1}\geq Y_{t}^{2}~a.s.-\mathbf{P}
.
∎

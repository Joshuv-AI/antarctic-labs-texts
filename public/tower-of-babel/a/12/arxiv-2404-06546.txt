The H i Mass Function of Star-forming Galaxies at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 Astropy ( Astropy Collaboration et al. 2013 ; Astropy Collaboration et al. 2018 ; Astropy Collaboration et al. 2022 )
Aditya Chowdhury, Nissim Kanekar, Jayaram N. Chengalur

Abstract
We present the first estimate, based on direct H i 21 cm observations, of the H i mass function (H i MF) of star-forming galaxies at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 , obtained by combining our measurement of the scaling relation between H i mass ( M H ​ i \rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}} ) and B-band luminosity ( M B \rm{M_{B}} ) of star-forming galaxies with literature estimates of the B-band luminosity function at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 . We determined the M H ​ i − M B \rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}-M_{B}} relation by using the GMRT-CAT z ​ 1 z1 survey of the DEEP2 fields to measure the average H i mass of blue galaxies at z = 0.74 − 1.45 z=0.74-1.45 in three separate M B \rm{M_{B}} subsamples. This was done by separately stacking the H i 21 cm emission signals of the galaxies in each subsample to detect, at ( 3.5 − 4.4 ) ​ σ (3.5-4.4)\sigma significance, the average H i 21 cm emission of each subsample. We find that the M H ​ i − M B \rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}-M_{B}} relation at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 is consistent with that at z ≈ 0 z\approx 0 . We combine our estimate of the M H ​ i − M B \rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}-M_{B}} relation at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 with the B-band luminosity function at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 to determine the H i MF at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 . We find that the number density of galaxies with M H ​ i > 10 10 ​ M ⊙ \rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}>10^{10}~M_{\odot}} (higher than the knee of the local H i mass function) at z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 is a factor of ≈ 4 − 5 \approx 4-5 higher than that at z ≈ 0 z\approx 0 , for a wide range of assumed scatters in the M H ​ i − M B \rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}-M_{B}} relation. We rule out the hypothesis that the number density of galaxies with M H ​ i > 10 10 ​ M ⊙ \rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}>10^{10}~M_{\odot}} remains unchanged between z ≈ 1 z\approx 1 and z ≈ 0 z\approx 0 at ≳ 99.7 \gtrsim 99.7 % confidence. This is the first statistically significant evidence for evolution in the H i MF of galaxies from the epoch of cosmic noon.



I
Introduction

Neutral atomic hydrogen (H
i
) is the primary fuel for star formation in galaxies and a critical component of a galaxy’s baryonic cycle
(
Péroux & Howk 2020
, e.g.)
. Understanding the evolution of the H
i
content of galaxies with cosmological time is thus critical for an understanding of galaxy evolution. A basic descriptor of the H
i
content of galaxies at any epoch is the “H
i
mass function” (H
i
MF), the number density of galaxies of a given H
i
mass as a function of the H
i
mass
(
Bothun 1985
;
Briggs 1990
;
Briggs & Rao 1993
;
Rao & Briggs 1993
;
Zwaan et al. 1997
;
Zwaan et al. 2005
;
Rosenberg & Schneider 2002
;
Hoppmann et al. 2015
;
Jones et al. 2018
, e.g.)
. Over the past two decades, unbiased wide-field H
i
21 cm emission surveys have shown that a Schechter function provides a good fit to the H
i
MF at
z\approx 0
, with an exponential decline at masses above the “knee” of the mass function and a power-law dependence at low H
i
masses
(
Zwaan et al. 2005
;
Jones et al. 2018
, e.g.)
. At present, the most accurate measurement of the H
i
MF in the local Universe is from the ALFALFA survey with the Arecibo Telescope
(
Haynes et al. 2018
)
, yielding a knee H
i
mass of log(
\rm M_{*}/M_{\odot})=9.94\pm 0.05
, a low-mass power-law slope of
\alpha=-1.25\pm 0.1
, and a normalization of
\phi_{*}=(4.7\pm 0.8)\times 10^{-3}
Mpc
-3
dex
-1
, where the errors are dominated by systematic uncertainties due to cosmic variance and the absolute flux density scale
(
Jones et al. 2018
)
.

Unfortunately, the weakness of the H
i
21 cm line has meant that little is known about the H
i
MF at cosmological distances. So far, direct estimates of the H
i
MF via unbiased H
i
21 cm surveys are restricted to
z\approx 0.1

(
Hoppmann et al. 2015
;
Ponomareva et al. 2023
)
. Measuring the H
i
MF at high redshifts is of critical importance to understanding galaxy evolution. Indeed, while different cosmological hydrodynamic simulations
(
Schaye et al. 2015
;
Pillepich et al. 2019
;
Davé et al. 2019
, e.g. SIMBA, IllustrisTNG, EAGLE;)
do reproduce the H
i
MF at
z\approx 0
, the results of these simulations for the H
i
MF are very different at
z\approx 1
and
z\approx 2

(
Davé et al. 2020
, e.g.)
. Measurements of the H
i
MF at high redshifts thus offer an avenue to distinguish between different models of galaxy evolution, and their inbuilt assumptions and subgrid physics.

The inherent weakness of the H
i
21 cm line can be overcome by using the stacking approach, in which the H
i
21 cm emission signals from a sample of galaxies with known spectroscopic redshifts are combined to obtain the average H
i
mass of the sample
(
Zwaan 2000
;
Chengalur et al. 2001
)
. Such H
i
21 cm stacking has been used to characterise the H
i
properties of different galaxy populations out to
z\approx 1.3

(
Bera et al. 2019
;
Bera et al. 2023a
;
Bera et al. 2023b
;
Chowdhury et al. 2020
;
Chowdhury et al. 2021
;
Chowdhury et al. 2022a
;
Chowdhury et al. 2022b
, e.g.)
. Recently,
Bera et al. 2022
demonstrated that H
i
21 cm stacking can also be used to determine the H
i
MF, by combining a measurement of the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation at
z\approx 0.34
(obtained from H
i
21 cm stacking) with the B-band luminosity function (
\phi({\rm M_{B}})
) at the same redshift. This approach, an extension of that used to obtain the early estimates of the H
i
MF in the local Universe
(
Briggs 1990
;
Rao & Briggs 1993
;
Zwaan et al. 2001
, see, e.g., )
yielded the first measurement of the H
i
MF at intermediate redshifts.

In this Letter, we use data from the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope (GMRT) Cold-H
i
AT
z\approx 1
(GMRT-CAT
z1
) survey
(
Chowdhury et al. 2022c
)
to determine the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation for star-forming galaxies at
z\approx 1
. We combine this relation with measurements of the B-band luminosity function at
z\approx 1
to obtain the first measurement of the H
i
MF at
z\approx 1
, at the end of the epoch of cosmic noon.

To determine the

z=0.74-1.45
into three
{\rm M_{B}}
subsamples with
{\rm M_{B}}<-21.3
(bright),
-21.3\leq{\rm M_{B}}<-20.9

-20.9\leq{\rm M_{B}}\leq-20.0

For each

Chowdhury et al. 2022c

21 cm cube, excluding velocity channels covering the central
\pm 250
km s
-1

\pm 1500

-1

21 cm subcubes were then stacked, using a procedure identical to that followed for the “true” H
i
21 cm stack, to obtain a realisation of the stacked H

21 cm cube. This procedure was repeated to produce
10^{4}
stacked H

\begin{split}\log\left[\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}/\textrm{M}_{\odot}\right]=&(-0.156\pm 0.105)\times({\rm M_{B}}+21)\\

In the local Universe, H
i

\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation are obtained from measurements of the H
i

\langle\log\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}\rangle
as a function of
{\rm M_{B}}

(
Dénes et al. 2014
, e.g.)
. The distribution of H
i

(
Catinella et al. 2018
, e.g.)
. In such cases,
\langle\log\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}\rangle
is equal to the logarithm of the median of the H
i
masses of the subsample. The scaling relations obtained in the local Universe from measurements of individual H
i
masses are thus “median” scaling relations. However, in stacking analysis, scaling relations such as the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation of Equation
1
are based on measurements of the average H
i
mass and hence of
\log\langle\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}\rangle
in multiple galaxy subsamples. The scaling relations directly obtained from stacking analyses are thus “mean” scaling relations and are generally different from the median scaling relations obtained from measurements of individual H
i
masses
(
Bera et al. 2022
;
Bera et al. 2023a
;
Chowdhury et al. 2022b
)
.

The above difference between the median scaling relations obtained at
z\approx 0
from H
i

i
21 cm stacking analyses at high redshifts must be accounted for when comparing scaling relations at different redshifts.
Assuming that the distribution of H
i
masses is log-normal with a logarithmic scatter
\sigma
, one obtains

\langle\log\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}\rangle=\log\langle\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}\rangle-(\ln 10/2)\sigma^{2}\,.

(2)

This implies that the intercept of the mean scaling relation (
\alpha_{\mathrm{mean}}
) obtained from a stacking analysis is related to that of the median scaling relation (
\alpha_{\mathrm{med}}
) obtained from individual detections via
\alpha_{\mathrm{med}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{mean}}-(\ln 10/2)\sigma^{2}
. The slopes of the two relations are the same.

III.3
Systematic effects

We note that our measurement of the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation at
z\approx 1
could be affected by incompleteness in the galaxy sample of the DEEP2 survey. We provide below a systematic exploration of possible biases in our measurement of the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation due to such sample incompleteness.

First, the DEEP2 Galaxy Redshift survey targeted galaxies for spectroscopy down to a limiting magnitude R
{}_{\textrm{AB}}=24.1
. The survey selected galaxies from a photometric catalogue with a limiting magnitude that is 1.5 mag. fainter than the threshold for DEEP2 spectroscopy and yielded a redshift success rate that is approximately independent of magnitude and colour
(
Newman et al. 2013
)
. As such, photometric incompleteness of the DEEP2 galaxy sample is unlikely to be an issue for our results.

Next, the R-band selection of the DEEP2 survey yields a rest-frame B-band completeness that is a function of both redshift and colour: for a fixed
{\rm M_{B}}
, the survey is biased toward galaxies with increasingly bluer rest-frame (U-B) colour at higher redshifts
(
Newman et al. 2013
)
. In our current study, we have restricted our sample for H
i
21 cm stacking to galaxies with
{\rm M_{B}}\leq-20
, the B-band magnitude at which the DEEP2 survey is complete to blue star-forming galaxies at
z\lesssim 1

(
Willmer et al. 2006
;
Newman et al. 2013
)
. This implies that the galaxy sample at
z\gtrsim 1
used for the H
i
21 cm stacking could be biased towards bluer galaxies, which could affect our average H
i
mass measurements. However,
Chowdhury et al. 2022a
find that the DEEP2 galaxies in our sample at
z>1
have average stellar masses and average star formation rates (SFRs) consistent with the star-forming main-sequence relation derived from highly complete photometric surveys at these redshifts. This indicates that the average properties of the DEEP2 galaxies in our sample are representative of the general main-sequence galaxy population over
0.74\lesssim z\lesssim 1.45
.

Finally, we note that our choice of weights for galaxies in the three subsamples is based on the distribution of galaxies in the faintest subsample and is hence biased toward the galaxies at
z\lesssim 1
(see Figure
1
) for which the DEEP2 sample is complete at
{\rm M_{B}}\leq-20

(
Newman et al. 2013
)
. Indeed, galaxies at
z>1
make up
\approx 33\%
of each
{\rm M_{B}}
subsample, after the redshift-based weighting, while galaxies at
z>1.2
, for which the effects of incompleteness would be the highest, make up only
\approx 10\%
of each subsample. Our average H
i
mass measurements in the three subsamples are thus likely to be robust to any potential effects of incompleteness in the higher-redshift bins. Overall, we conclude that our measurement of the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation at
z\approx 1
is likely to be robust to the effects of the DEEP2 selection criteria.

IV
Determining the H
i
Mass Function at
z\approx 1

While modern measurements of the H
i
MF in the local Universe have relied on wide-area optically-unbiased H
i
21 cm emission surveys
(
Zwaan et al. 2005
;
Jones et al. 2018
, e.g.)
, the early estimates of the H
i
MF at
z\approx 0
were based on combining the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation with measurements of the B-band luminosity function
\phi({\rm M_{B}})

(
Briggs 1990
;
Rao & Briggs 1993
;
Zwaan et al. 2001
, e.g.)
. These authors performed a simple transformation of variable in
\phi({\rm M_{B}})
, substituting
{\rm M_{B}}
with
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}
via the observed
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation, to obtain
\phi(\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}})

Bera et al. 2022
found that this straightforward approach, which ignores the scatter in the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation, results in an underestimation of the H
i
MF at the high-mass end.
Bera et al. 2022
further demonstrated that combining the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation of
Dénes et al. 2014
with the local B-band luminosity function, and incorporating the measured scatter of the local
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation
(
Dénes et al. 2014
)
, yields an H
i
MF at
z\approx 0
in excellent agreement with that obtained from the ALFALFA survey
(
Jones et al. 2018
)
. In passing, we note that it may also be possible to derive the H
i
MF via other scaling relations such as the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-\textrm{M}_{*}
relation. However, the measured scatter of the local
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation
(
Dénes et al. 2014
, 0.26 dex;)
is far lower than that of other H

z\approx 0

\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-\textrm{M}_{*}

To determine the H

z\approx 1

(

López-Sanjuan et al. 2017

{\rm M_{B}}^{*}
) and the normalisation (
\phi^{*}
) of the B-band luminosity function over
z\approx 0.2-1
can be described, respectively, by
{\rm M_{B}}^{*}=(-21.00\pm 0.03)+(z-0.5)\times(-1.03\pm 0.08)

\log\left[\phi^{*}(10^{-3}{\rm Mpc}^{-3}{\rm mag}^{-1})\right]=(-2.51\pm 0.03)+(z-0.5)\times(-0.01\pm 0.03)

\alpha

\alpha=1.29\pm 0.02

{\rm M_{B}}^{*}
and
\phi^{*}
to estimate the B-band luminosity function of star-forming galaxies at
z=0.97

{\rm M_{B}}
subsamples (see Table

In passing, we emphasize that the above errors on the parameters of the B-band luminosity function from the ALHAMBRA survey include an estimate of systematic uncertainties such as survey incompleteness, cosmic variance, etc. We take into account the above uncertainties while estimating the uncertainty in our measurement of the H

z\approx 1

VI
Summary

In this Letter, we have determined the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation for blue star-forming galaxies at
z\approx 1
, by using the GMRT-CAT
z
1 survey and H
i
21 cm stacking to measure the average H
i
masses of galaxies in three independent
{\rm M_{B}}
subsamples. Our measurement of the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation is consistent with the power-law relation at
z\approx 0

(
Dénes et al. 2014
)
, in both slope and amplitude. We used our
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation, with a scatter assumed to be the same as that at
z\approx 0
, along with the B-band luminosity function of blue galaxies at
z\approx 1
from the ALHAMBRA survey to obtain the first estimate of the H
i
mass function at
z\approx 1
. We find statistically significant evidence that the number density of galaxies with high H
i
masses is higher at
z\approx 1
than in the local Universe. Specifically, the number density of galaxies with
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}>10^{10}~\textrm{M}_{\odot}
(i.e. higher than the knee of the local H
i
mass function) at
z\approx 1
is a factor of
\approx 4-5
higher than that at
z\approx 0
, for a wide range of values of the assumed scatter in the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation. We rule out, at
\gtrsim 99.7
% confidence, the hypothesis that the number density of galaxies with
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}>10^{10}\,\textrm{M}_{\odot}
at
z\approx 1
is the same as that at
z\approx 0
. This is the first clear evidence for a change in the H
i
MF at
z\lesssim 1
. For even higher H
i
masses,
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}>5\times 10^{10}\,\textrm{M}_{\odot}
, the number density at
z\approx 1
is
\approx 9
times higher than that at
z\approx 0
, assuming that the scatter in the
\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}-{\rm M_{B}}
relation at
z\approx 1
is the same as that at
z\approx 0
. The high inferred number density of galaxies at
z\approx 1
with large H
i
reservoirs implies that it may be possible for deep H
i
21 cm emission surveys with today’s radio telescopes to obtain detections of H
i
21 cm emission in individual galaxies at
z\approx 1
, and thus obtain even more direct constraints on the H
i
MF toward the end of the epoch of cosmic noon.

Appendix A

We assume that the average H

\log\rm{M_{H{\textsc{i}}}}=a\,({\rm M_{B}}+21)+c\,,

We fit the above relation to our measurements of the average

z\approx 1
. This should be possible with deeper H

{\rm M_{B}}

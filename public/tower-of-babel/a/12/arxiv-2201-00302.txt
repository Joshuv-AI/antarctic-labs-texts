Entropies of Serre functors for higher hereditary algebras This work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 12371043 and 11971460).
Yang Han

Abstract
For a higher hereditary algebra, we calculate its upper (lower) Serre dimension, the entropy and polynomial entropy of Serre functor, and the Hochschild (co)homology entropy of Serre quasi-functor. These invariants are determined by its Calabi-Yau dimension for a higher representation-finite algebra, and by its global dimension and the spectral radius and polynomial growth rate of its Coxeter matrix for a higher representation-infinite algebra. For this, we will prove the Yomdin type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy for a finite dimensional elementary algebra of finite global dimension. Our calculations imply that the Kikuta and Ouchi’s question on relations between entropy and Hochschild (co)homology entropy has positive answer, and the Gromov-Yomdin type equalities on entropy and Hochschild (co)homology entropy hold, for the Serre functor on perfect derived category and the Serre quasi-functor on perfect dg module category of an indecomposable elementary higher hereditary algebra.

Introduction

A topological dynamical system
(X,f)
consists of a topological space
X
and a continuous function
f:X\to X
. The topological entropy
h_{\mathrm{top}}(f)
measures the complexity of
(X,f)
.
As a categorical analog of topological dynamical system, in
[
7
]
, Dimitrov, Haiden, Katzarkov and Kontsevich introduced categorical dynamical system
({\cal T},F)
which consists of a triangulated category
{\cal T}
and a (triangle) endofunctor
F:{\cal T}\to{\cal T}
of
{\cal T}
, and (categorical) entropy which measures the complexity of a categorical dynamical system.
Roughly speaking, the entropy is the asymptotically exponential growth rate of the complexity of a categorical dynamical system. In
[
12
]
, Fan, Fu and Ouchi introduced (categorical)
polynomial entropy which is the asymptotically polynomial growth rate of the complexity of a categorical dynamical system. Moreover, in
[
21
]
, Kikuta and Ouchi introduced Hochschild (co)homology entropy. Hochschild (co)homology entropy is defined not for a categorical dynamical system, but for a “dg categorical dynamical system”. Meanwhile, Kikuta and Ouchi posed a question (
[
21
, Question 2.13]
): When does the Hochschild (co)homology entropy for a “dg categorical dynamical system” coincide with the entropy for the corresponding categorical dynamical system? In addition to these types of entropies, in
[
11
]
, Elagin and Lunts introduced the upper (lower) Serre dimension of a triangulated category with a (split=classical) generator and a Serre functor, which sometimes is the coefficient of the degree one term of the entropy (
[
11
, Proposition 6.14]
).

As a generalization of representation-finite hereditary algebras, Iyama and Oppermann introduced higher representation-finite algebras in
[
17
]
. As a generalization of representation-infinite hereditary algebras, Herschend, Iyama and Oppermann introduced higher representation-infinite algebras in
[
16
]
.
Meanwhile, they also introduced higher hereditary algebras which are shown to be either higher representation-finite algebras or higher representation-infinite algebras (
[
16
, Theorem 3.4]
). Many classical results in the representation theory of hereditary algebras have higher dimensional analogs for higher hereditary algebras.
Moreover, as a generalization of fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras, Herschend and Iyama introduced twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras in
[
14
]
, which contain higher representation-finite algebras as typical examples (
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
).

In this paper, for a higher hereditary algebra, we will calculate its upper (lower) Serre dimensions, the entropy and polynomial entropy of Serre functor, and the Hochschild (co)homology entropy of Serre quasi-functor.
Given Serre functor and higher hereditary algebra have congenital relationship, the calculations become feasible.

Our main results are the following.

Theorem A.
(= Theorem
3
)
Let
A
be a twisted
\frac{q}{p}
-Calabi-Yau algebra. Then

(1)
the entropy of Serre functor
h_{t}(S)=\frac{q}{p}t
.

(2)
the polynomial entropy of Serre functor
h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{t}(S)=0
.

(3)
the Hochschild (co)homology entropy of Serre quasi-functor
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=0
if we assume further that
A
is elementary.

(4)
the upper (lower) Serre dimension
\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\frac{q}{p}

Theorem A (1), (2) and (4) generalize the corresponding results
[

, 2.6.1]
,
[
12
, Remark 6.3]
and
[
10
, Proposition 3.17]
for fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras. To date, it is not known whether every higher representation-finite algebra, or more general, twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebra, is fractionally Calabi-Yau or not (
[
6
, Question 1.6]
). So Theorem A should have its own place.

From Theorem A and
[

Corollary B.
(= Corollary

Let

(1)

(2)

(3)

h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=0

(4)

Applying the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch type theorem (
[

) and Wimmer’s formula (
[

), we can obtain the following Theorem C which gives the Yomdin type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy.

Theorem C.
(= Theorem

Let

The Theorem C above will be applied to show the following Theorem D.

Theorem D.
(= Theorem
7
)
Let
A
be an elementary
d
-representation-infinite algebra, and

(1)
the entropy of (inverse) Serre functor:
h_{t}(S)=dt+\log\rho(\Phi)

(2)
the polynomial entropy of (inverse) Serre functor:

(3)

Partial results of Theorem D (1), (2) and (4) for representation-infinite hereditary algebras had been obtained in
[

, Theorem 2.17]
,
[
10
, Proposition 4.2]
and
[

. The equalities

and

seem to be new. Serre functor is a categorification of Coxeter matrix. Theorem D (1), (2) and (3) suggest that, for an elementary higher representation-infinite algebra, the entropy of Serre functor and the Hochschild (co)homology entropy of Serre quasi-functor are the categorifications of spectral radius of Coxeter matrix, and polynomial entropy is the categorification of polynomial growth rate of Coxeter matrix, in some sense.

Furthermore, our main results imply that the Kikuta and Ouchi’s question on the relations between entropy and Hochschild (co)homology entropy has positive answer, that is,
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h(S)
, and the Gromov-Yomdin type equalities on entropy and Hochschild (co)homology entropy hold, that is,
h(S)=\log\rho([S])
and
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=\log\rho([H^{0}(\tilde{S})])

on the perfect derived category and the Serre quasi-functor

on the perfect dg module category of an elementary twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebra or an indecomposable elementary higher hereditary algebra.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we will recall the definitions of entropy, polynomial entropy, Hochschild (co)homology entropy and upper (lower) Serre dimension, and their basic properties that we need for later use. Moreover, we will reformulate Hochschild (co)homology entropy so that they are easier to manipulate in our situations. In Section 3, we will calculate these invariants for the Serre functor on the perfect derived category and the Serre quasi-functor on the perfect dg module category of a higher hereditary algebra. We will consider twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras, higher representation-finite algebras, and higher representation-infinite algebras in turn. For this, we will show the Yomdin type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy for a finite dimensional elementary algebra, and prove that Hochschild cohomology entropy and Hochschild homology entropy coincide for the (inverse) Serre quasi-functor on perfect dg module category of a proper smooth dg algebra (Proposition

For a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
admitting a dg enhancement given by a saturated dg category, and an endofunctor
F
of
\mathcal{T}
admitting a dg lift, the polynomial entropy can be calculated by cohomology functors.

Lemma 4
.

(
[
12
, Lemma 2.7]
)

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category admitting a dg enhancement given by a saturated dg category, and
F
an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
admitting a dg lift. Then for any generators
G,G^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{T}
,

h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{t}(F)=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\epsilon_{t}(G,F^{n}(G^{\prime}))-n\cdot h_{t}(F)}{\log n},

where
\epsilon_{t}(X,Y):=\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\cal T}(X,Y[l])\cdot e^{-lt}
for all
X,Y\in{\cal T}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Polynomial entropy has the following properties.

Lemma 5
.

(Shift
[
12
, Lemma 6.1]
)

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category admitting a dg enhancement given by a saturated dg category,
F
an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
admitting a dg lift, and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
h_{t}^{\mathrm{pol}}(F[m])=h_{t}^{\mathrm{pol}}(F)
.
In particular,
h_{t}^{\mathrm{pol}}([m])=0
.

Lemma 6
.

(Inverse
[
12
, Lemma 2.11]
)

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category admitting a Serre functor and a dg enhancement given by a saturated dg category, and
F
an autoequivalence of
\mathcal{T}
admitting a dg lift. Then
h_{t}^{\mathrm{pol}}(F^{-1})=h_{-t}^{\mathrm{pol}}(F)
.
In particular,
h^{\mathrm{pol}}(F^{-1})=h^{\mathrm{pol}}(F)
.

Polynomial growth rate of a linear operator.

Sometimes, we can reduce the polynomial entropy of an endofunctor to the polynomial growth rate of a linear operator (
[
12
, Proposition 4.4]
).
Let
f
be a non-nilpotent linear operator on a finite dimensional complex vector space
V
endowed with some norm
\|-\|
, and
\rho(f)
the
spectral radius
of
f
, that is, the maximal absolute value of eigenvalues of
f
. The
polynomial growth rate
of
f
(
[
12
, Definition 4.1]
) is

s(f):=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|f^{n}\|-n\cdot\log\rho(f)}{\log n}.

Here,
\|f^{n}\|=\sup\limits_{0\neq x\in V}\frac{\|f^{n}(x)\|}{\|x\|}
(See
[
3
, 1.1.10]
). As all norms on the space of matrices are equivalent,
s(f)
is independent of the choice of the norm.

Lemma 7
.

(
[
12
, Lemma 4.2]
)

Let
f
be a non-nilpotent linear operator of a finite dimensional complex vector space endowed with some norm
\|-\|
. Then the polynomial growth rate of
f
is well-defined, and it is precisely one less than the maximal size of the Jordan blocks whose eigenvalues are of maximal absolute value
\rho(f)
. In particular,
s(f)
is a nonnegative integer.

2.3
Hochschild (co)homology entropy

Hochschild (co)homology entropy is defined not for a (triangle) endofunctor of a triangulated category but for a quasi-endofunctor of the perfect dg module category of a proper smooth dg category.

Homotopy category of dg categories.
The category
\mathbf{dgcat}
of (small) dg categories, whose morphisms are dg functors, is a closed symmetric monoidal category with tensor product
\otimes
and internal Hom functor
\mathcal{H}om
. A
quasi-equivalence

F
from a dg category
\mathcal{A}
to a dg category
\mathcal{B}
is a dg functor
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
such that
F(x,y):\mathcal{A}(x,y)\to\mathcal{B}(F(x),F(y))
is a quasi-isomorphism for all objects
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
and the induced functor on homotopy categories
H^{0}(F):H^{0}(\mathcal{A})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{B})
is an equivalence.
The localization
\mathbf{hodgcat}
of
\mathbf{dgcat}
with respect to quasi-equivalences
is a closed symmetric monoidal category with tensor product
\otimes^{L}
and internal Hom functor
\mathcal{RH}om
(
[
19
, Theorem 4.5]
and
[
24
, Theorem 1.3]
).
For any two dg categories
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, let
\mathrm{rep}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
be the full triangulated subcategory of the derived category

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathcal{B})
of dg
\mathcal{A}
-
\mathcal{B}
-bimodules formed by the dg
\mathcal{A}
-
\mathcal{B}
-bimodules
M
such that the derived tensor product functor
-\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}M:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B})
takes the representable dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules to objects which are isomorphic to representable dg
\mathcal{B}
-modules.
Then we have a bijection

\mathbf{hodgcat}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\cong\mathrm{Iso}(\mathrm{rep}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}))

(
[
19
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
24
, Corollary 1.2]
).
Let
\mathrm{rep}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
be the full sub-dg category of the dg
\mathcal{A}
-
\mathcal{B}
-bimodule category
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathcal{B})
whose objects are those of
\mathrm{rep}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
which are cofibrant as dg
\mathcal{A}
-
\mathcal{B}
-bimodules. Then we have an isomorphism

\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\cong\mathrm{rep}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

in
\mathbf{hodgcat}
(
[
19
, Theorem 4.5]
and
[
24
, Theorem 1.3]
).
Furthermore, we have equivalences

H^{0}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}))\simeq H^{0}(\mathrm{rep}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}))\simeq\mathrm{rep}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

and a bijection

\mathbf{hodgcat}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\cong\mathrm{Iso}(H^{0}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}))).
The objects in
\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
or
\mathrm{rep}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
or
H^{0}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}))
or
\mathrm{rep}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
are called
quasi-functors
.

Hochschild (co)homology entropy.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a proper smooth dg category (
[
26
, Definition 2.4]
).
Denote by
\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})
the full sub-dg category of the dg category
\mathrm{Mod}\mathcal{A}
of dg right
\mathcal{A}
-modules, whose objects are the cofibrant perfect dg right
\mathcal{A}
-modules, which is a dg enhancement of the perfect derived category
\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A})
of dg right
\mathcal{A}
-modules.
It follows from
[
26
, Lemma 2.6]
that
\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})
is a saturated dg category.
Applying the formula in
[
25
, Page 298]
, we obtain an isomorphism

\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
in
\mathbf{hodgcat}
,
where
\mathcal{A}^{e}:=\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is the enveloping dg category of
\mathcal{A}
.
Thus we have
inverse Serre quasi-functor

\tilde{S}^{-1}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
which corresponds to the
inverse dualizing complex

\Theta\in\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
(
[
20
, 3.3]
), that is, a cofibrant resolution of
\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{e})\in\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
.
For any quasi-functor
\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
the
i
-th Hochschild cohomology group
of
\tilde{\Phi}
(
[
21
, Definition 2.6]
) is

HH^{i}(\tilde{\Phi}):=H^{i}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))(\mathrm{Id},\tilde{\Phi})),

and the
i
-th Hochschild homology group
of
\tilde{\Phi}
is

HH_{i}(\tilde{\Phi}):=H^{-i}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))(\tilde{S}^{-1},\tilde{\Phi})).

Furthermore, the
Hochschild cohomology entropy
of
\tilde{\Phi}
(
[
21
, Definition 2.9]
) is

h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi}):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}HH^{i}(\tilde{\Phi}^{n}),

and the
Hochschild homology entropy
of
\tilde{\Phi}
is

h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi}):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}HH_{i}(\tilde{\Phi}^{n}).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a proper smooth dg category. A quasi-functor
\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\linebreak\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
is also a quasi-functor in
H^{0}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})))
, which corresponds to a morphism
\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathbf{hodgcat}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
and further a functor
\Phi:=H^{0}(\tilde{\Phi}):\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A})
. The following result compares the Hochschild (co)homology entropy of
\tilde{\Phi}
with the entropy of
\Phi
.

Theorem 2
.

(
[
21
, Theorem 2.10]
)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a proper smooth dg category,
\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
a quasi-endofunctor of
\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})
, and
\Phi:=H^{0}(\tilde{\Phi})
the induced endofunctor of
\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A})
. Then

h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi})\leq h(\Phi)
and
h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi})\leq h(\Phi).

Furthermore, Kikuta and Ouchi posed the following question on relations between entropy and Hochschild (co)homology entropy.

Question 1
.

(
[
21
, Question 2.13]
)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a proper smooth dg category,
\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
a quasi-endofunctor of
\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})
, and
\Phi:=H^{0}(\tilde{\Phi})
the induced endofunctor of
\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A})
. When do the equalities
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi})=h(\Phi)
hold?

It was pointed by Atsushi Takahashi (
[
21
, Page 231]
) that the equalities in Question
1
do not always hold in general. We will provide some positive answers, cf. Remark 1 (1) and Remark 2 (3).

Reformulation of Hochschild (co)homology entropy.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a proper smooth dg category. Under the isomorphism
\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
in
\mathbf{hodgcat}
, any object
\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
corresponds to an object
M\in\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
. In particular, the identity functor
\mathrm{Id}
on
\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})
corresponds to an object
\Delta\in\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
which is a cofibrant resolution of the dg
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
.
Then for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle HH^{i}(\tilde{\Phi}^{n})

\displaystyle=H^{i}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))(\mathrm{Id},\tilde{\Phi}^{n}))

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})(\Delta,M^{\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}n}))

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}(\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},M^{\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}n}))

and

\displaystyle HH_{i}(\tilde{\Phi}^{n})

\displaystyle=H^{-i}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))(\tilde{S}^{-1},\tilde{\Phi}^{n}))

\displaystyle\cong H^{-i}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})(\Theta,M^{\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}n}))

\displaystyle\cong H^{-i}(\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{e}),M^{\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}n}))

\displaystyle\cong H^{-i}(M^{\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}n}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{e}),\mathcal{A}^{e}))

\displaystyle\cong H^{-i}(M^{\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}n}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\cong H^{-i}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}M^{\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}n}).

So Hochschild (co)homology entropy is well-defined for any quasi-endofunctor
\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}))
or
H^{0}(\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})))
or dg
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule
M\in\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
or
\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
, and can be calculated by

h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi})=h^{HH^{\bullet}}(M):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{tdim}_{k}\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(\mathcal{A},M^{\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}n}),

and

h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{\Phi})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(M):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}M^{\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{A}}n}).

2.4
Serre dimensions

Serre dimensions are new invariants of an Ext-finite triangulated category with a generator and a Serre functor, or of a proper smooth dg algebra.

(Inverse) Serre (quasi-)functor.
A triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is
Hom-finite
if
\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)<\infty
for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}
.
A
Serre functor
(
[
4
, Definition 3.1]
) of a Hom-finite triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is an autoequivalence
S:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
such that there is a bifunctorial isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)^{*}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y,S(X))
.
The Serre functor, if it exists, is unique up to isomorphism.
The perfect derived category
\mathrm{per}(A)
of a proper smooth dg algebra
A
has Serre functors.
Indeed,
S:=-\otimes^{L}_{A}A^{*}
is a Serre functor of
\mathrm{per}(A)
with the
inverse Serre functor

S^{-1}:=\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},-)\cong-\otimes^{L}_{A}\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)
as a quasi-inverse.
Let
\nabla
and
\Theta
be any cofibrant resolutions of dg
A
-bimodule
A^{*}
and
\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)
respectively.
The quasi-endofunctors
\tilde{S},\tilde{S}^{-1}\in\mathcal{RH}\mathrm{om}(\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(A),\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(A))
corresponding to
\nabla,\Theta\in\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(A^{e})
are called
Serre quasi-functor
and
inverse Serre quasi-functor
respectively. Note that we have
\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)\cong\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,\mathrm{RHom}_{k}(A^{*},A))\linebreak\cong\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,A^{e})
in
\mathrm{per}(A^{e})
. So the inverse Serre quasi-functor defined here coincides with that in 2.3.

Upper (lower) Serre dimension.

A triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is
Ext-finite
if
\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y[i])<\infty
for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}
.
Let
{\cal T}
be an Ext-finite triangulated category with a generator and a Serre functor
S:{\cal T}\to{\cal T}
. For any two generators
G,G^{\prime}
of
{\cal T}
, the
upper Serre dimension
of
{\cal T}
(
[
11
, Definition 5.3]
) is

\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}{\cal T}:=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\inf\{i\ |\ \mathrm{Hom}_{\cal T}(G,S^{n}(G^{\prime})[i])\neq 0\}}{n},

and the
lower Serre dimension
of
{\cal T}
is

\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}{\cal T}:=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\sup\{i\ |\ \mathrm{Hom}_{\cal T}(G,S^{n}(G^{\prime})[i])\neq 0\}}{n}.

It follows from
[
11
, Lemma 6.3 and Definition 6.4]
that
\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}{\cal T}
and
\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}{\cal T}
are well-defined, i.e., they are independent of the choice of the generators
G
and
G^{\prime}
.

Let
A
be a proper smooth dg algebra. The
upper Serre dimension of
A
is
\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A:=\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}\ \mathrm{per}(A)
, and the
lower Serre dimension of
A
is

\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A:=\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}\ \mathrm{per}(A)
.
It follows from
[
11
, Proposition 5.5]
that

\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\inf\ (A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}}{n},

and

\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\sup\ (A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}}{n},

where for any object
X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(A^{e})
,

\inf X:=\inf\{i\ |\ H^{i}(X)\neq 0\},\hskip 10.00002pt\sup X:=\sup\{i\ |\ H^{i}(X)\neq 0\}.

Moreover, the above two limits are finite.

3
Higher hereditary algebras

In this section, for a higher hereditary algebra, we will calculate its upper (lower) Serre dimension, the entropy and polynomial entropy of Serre functor, and Hochschild (co)homology entropy of Serre quasi-functor. Higher representation-finite algebras are twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras for which these invariants can be calculated easily.

3.1
Twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras

Twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras are a generalization of fractionally Calabi-Yau algebra, and has higher representation-finite algebras as typical examples.

Twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebra.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional algebra of finite global dimension.
Then the
Nakayama functor

\nu=\nu_{A}:=\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(-,A)^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)\to\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)

is isomorphic to the Serre functor
S=S_{A}:=-\otimes^{L}_{A}A^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)\to\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
.
Let
\phi
be an algebra endomorphism of
A
. Then
\phi
induces the restriction functor
\phi^{*}:\mathrm{Mod}A\to\mathrm{Mod}A,M\mapsto M_{\phi}
, where
M_{\phi}
is the right
A
-module defined by the same vector space as
M
but the new right
A
-module action
m\cdot a:=m\phi(a)
for all
m\in M
and
a\in A
. Furthermore,
\phi^{*}
induces the derived functor
\phi^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)\to\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
which is isomorphic to the derived tensor product functor
-\otimes^{L}_{A}A_{\phi}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)\to\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
, where
A_{\phi}
is the twisted
A
-bimodule
A
with the left and right
A
-module actions
b\cdot a\cdot c:=ba\phi(c)
for all
a,b,c\in A
.
A finite dimensional algebra
A
of finite global dimension is
twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau and of Calabi-Yau dimension
\frac{q}{p}
or
twisted
\frac{q}{p}
-Calabi-Yau
(
[
14
, Definition 0.3]
) if there exist a positive integer
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, an integer
q\in\mathbb{Z}
, and an algebra automorphism
\phi
of
A
, such that there is a functorial isomorphism
\nu^{p}\cong\phi^{*}[q]
of autoequivalences on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
, or equivalently,
\nu^{p}(A)\cong A[q]
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
(
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
). When
\phi
is the identity automorphism of
A
,
A
is said to be
fractionally Calabi-Yau and of Calabi-Yau dimension
\frac{q}{p}
or
\frac{q}{p}
-Calabi-Yau
.
(Twisted)
\frac{q}{p}
-Calabi-Yau algebras are also (twisted)
\frac{qk}{pk}
-Calabi-Yau algebras for all positive integers
k
. The converse does not hold in general, but the Calabi-Yau dimension
\frac{q}{p}
is unique as a rational number.

Hochschild (co)homology entropy of (inverse) Serre quasi-functor.
The following result implies that Hochschild cohomology entropy coincides with Hochschild homology entropy for the (inverse) Serre quasi-functor on the perfect dg module category of a proper smooth dg algebra.

Proposition 1
.

Let
A
be a proper smooth dg algebra, and
\tilde{S}
the Serre quasi-functor on the perfect dg module category
\mathrm{per}_{\mathrm{dg}}(A)
of
A
. Then

h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})
and
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})
.

Proof.

Since
A
is a proper smooth dg algebra, we have isomorphisms

\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)\cong\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,\mathrm{RHom}_{k}(A^{*},A))\cong\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,A^{e})

and
A^{*}\otimes^{L}_{A}\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)\cong A
in
\mathrm{per}(A^{e})
.
Then we get isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n})

\displaystyle\cong(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,A^{e})

\displaystyle\cong(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)

\displaystyle\cong A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}{n-1}}

in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(k)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Thus

\displaystyle h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{tdim}_{k}\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n})

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}{n-1}})

\displaystyle=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}).

Next, we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,A^{e})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)

\displaystyle\cong A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)^{\otimes^{L}_{A}{n+1}}

in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(k)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Thus

\displaystyle h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{tdim}_{k}\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n})

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)^{\otimes^{L}_{A}{n+1}})

\displaystyle=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1}).

Now we have finished the proof of Proposition
1
.
∎

Entropies and Serre dimensions for twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras.
Our first main result is the following, in which (1), (2) and (4) generalize the corresponding results
[
7
, 2.6.1]
,
[
12
, Remark 6.3]
and
[
10
, Proposition 3.17]
for fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A
be a twisted
\frac{q}{p}
-Calabi-Yau algebra. Then

(1)
the entropy of Serre functor
h_{t}(S)=\frac{q}{p}t
.

(2)
the polynomial entropy of Serre functor
h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{t}(S)=0
.

(3)
the Hochschild (co)homology entropy of Serre quasi-functor
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=0
if we assume further that
A
is elementary.

(4)
the upper (lower) Serre dimension
\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\frac{q}{p}
.

Proof.

(1) Since
A
is a twisted
\frac{q}{p}
-Calabi-Yau algebra, there is an algebra automorphism
\phi
of
A
such that
S^{p}\cong\phi^{*}[q]
as autoequivalences of
\mathrm{per}(A)
.
In view of
A_{\phi^{n}}\in\mathrm{mod}A
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{t}(\phi^{*})&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\ \mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)}(A,(\phi^{*})^{n}(A)[l])\cdot e^{-lt}\\[11.38109pt]
&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\ \mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)}(A,A_{\phi^{n}}[l])\cdot e^{-lt}\\[11.38109pt]
&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{dim}_{k}A_{\phi^{n}}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{dim}_{k}A=0.\end{array}

Furthermore, from Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
, we obtain
p\cdot h_{t}(S)=h_{t}(S^{p})=h_{t}(\phi^{*}[q])=h_{t}(\phi^{*})+qt=qt
. Thus
h_{t}(S)=\frac{q}{p}t
.

(2) Analogous to
[
12
, Remark 6.3]
.
Since
A
is twisted
\frac{q}{p}
-Calabi-Yau, we have
S^{p}(A)\cong A[q]
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
(
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
).
For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we can write
n=ap+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leq b\leq p-1
.
Then we get

\epsilon_{t}(A,S^{n}(A))=\epsilon_{t}(A,S^{b}(A)[qa])=\epsilon_{t}(A,S^{b}(A))\cdot e^{qat}.

Furthermore, from (1), we obtain

\log\epsilon_{t}(A,S^{n}(A))-n\cdot h_{t}(S)=\log\epsilon_{t}(A,S^{b}(A))-\frac{qb}{p}t.

Since the absolute value of the right hand side is bounded by

\max\limits_{0\leq b\leq p-1}\{|\log\epsilon_{t}(A,S^{b}(A))|\}+|qt|,

which is independent of
n
, we have
h_{t}^{\mathrm{pol}}(S)=0
.

(3) As in the proof of Proposition
1
, we have
\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n})\cong A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}{n-1}}
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(k)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we can write
n-1=ap+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leq b\leq p-1
.
Thus

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{RHom}_{A^{e}}(A,(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n})&\cong A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}{n-1}}\\
&\cong A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}((A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}b}\otimes^{L}_{A}((A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}p})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}a})\\
&\cong A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}((A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}b}\otimes^{L}_{A}(A_{\phi}[q])^{\otimes^{L}_{A}a})\\
&\cong A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}((A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}b}\otimes^{L}_{A}A_{\phi^{a}}[qa])\\
&\cong A_{\phi^{a}}\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}b}[qa],\end{array}

where the third isomorphism holds since
A
is twisted Calabi-Yau,
and the fourth isomorphism follows from
(A_{\phi})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}a}\cong(A_{\phi})^{\otimes_{A}a}\cong A_{\phi^{a}}
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A^{e})
.
Let
P
be a minimal projective resolution of
A
-bimodule
A^{*}
.
Then
\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(A_{\phi^{a}}\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}b}[qa])=\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(A_{\phi^{a}}\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}(A^{*})^{\otimes^{L}_{A}b})=\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(A_{\phi^{a}}\otimes_{A^{e}}P^{\otimes_{A}b})
.
Since
A
is a finite dimensional elementary algebra, the factor algebra
A/\mathrm{rad}A
of
A
modulo its Jacobson radical
\mathrm{rad}A
is a direct sum of finitely many copies of
k
, in particular,
A/\mathrm{rad}A
is separable.
By the assumption that
A
is twisted Calabi-Yau, we know
A
is of finite global dimension.
Applying
[
9

, we obtain
\mathrm{gl.dim}A^{e}<\infty
, in particular,
\mathrm{pd}_{A^{e}}A<\infty
, and further,

is (homologically) smooth.
Thus, as a graded vector space,
P
is finite dimensional.
Since the
i
-th component of the complex

is a quotient vector space of the

\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(A_{\phi^{a}}\otimes_{A^{e}}P^{\otimes_{A}b})

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(A_{\phi^{a}}\otimes_{A^{e}}P^{\otimes_{A}b})

.
By Proposition
1

we have
h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=0
.

(4) For any

in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
. Since

.
Furthermore, we obtain

.
∎

3.2

Higher representation-finite algebras are generalizations of representation-finite hereditary algebras, which are typical examples of twisted fractionally Calabi-Yau algebras.

Higher representation-infinite algebras

Yomdin type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy.
We need the following Wimmer’s formula to calculate Hochschild (co)homology entropy.

Lemma 8
.

(
[
27
, Theorem]
)

Let
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be an
m\times m
complex matrix, and
\rho(M)
the spectral radius of
M
. Then

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|\mathrm{tr}(M^{n})|}=\rho(M).

The following result gives the Yomdin type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy. I do not know whether the Gromov type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy, that is,
h^{HH_{\bullet}}(M)\leq\log\rho(\Psi_{M})
, and the Gromov and Yomdin type inequalities on Hochschild cohomology entropy, i.e.,
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(M)\leq\log\rho(\Psi_{M})

, hold or not.

Theorem 5
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional elementary algebra of finite global dimension,
M
a bounded
A
-bimodule complex, and
\Psi_{M}:=-C_{M}C_{A}^{-1}
the dual Coxeter matrix of
M
. Then

h^{HH_{\bullet}}(M)\geq\log\rho(\Psi_{M}).

Proof.

Applying
[
13
, Theorem 1 (4) and (2)]
and Lemma
8
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\ (\mathrm{tdim}_{k}(A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}M^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}))^{\frac{1}{n}}\\[11.38109pt]
\geq&\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\ |\mathrm{sdim}_{k}(A\otimes^{L}_{A^{e}}M^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n})|^{\frac{1}{n}}\\[11.38109pt]
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(4)}}{{=}}&\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\ |\mathrm{tr}(C^{-1}_{A}\cdot C_{M^{\otimes^{L}_{A}n}})|^{\frac{1}{n}}\\[11.38109pt]
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}&\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\ |\mathrm{tr}(C^{-1}_{A}\cdot C_{M}(C^{-1}_{A}C_{M})^{n-1})|^{\frac{1}{n}}\\[11.38109pt]

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L8}}{{=}}&\rho(C^{-1}_{A}C_{M})=\rho(C_{M}C^{-1}_{A})=\rho(\Psi_{M}).\end{array}

Entropies and Serre dimensions for higher representation-infinite algebras.

the upper (lower) Serre dimension:

K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))_{\mathbb{R}}:=K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}

be the scalar extension of
K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))
and the
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear form

\chi_{\mathbb{R}}:K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))_{\mathbb{R}}\times K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}

the scalar extension of
\chi
. Then
\chi_{\mathbb{R}}
is also non-degenerate.

For any linear operator
f:K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))_{\mathbb{R}}\to K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))_{\mathbb{R}}
,
we define

\|f\|:=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}|\chi_{\mathbb{R}}([P_{i}],f([P_{j}]))|.

Since
\chi_{\mathbb{R}}
is non-degenerate,
\|-\|
is a norm on the space
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))_{\mathbb{R}})
of linear operators on
K_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(A))_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Since
A
is
d
-representation-infinite,
\nu_{d}^{-n}(P_{i})
is isomorphic to an indecomposable
A
-module for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
1\leq i\leq r
. Thus

\begin{array}[]{lll}h_{t}(\nu_{d}^{-1})&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Ext}^{l}_{A}(A,\nu_{d}^{-n}(A))\cdot e^{-lt}&\mbox{(Theorem~\ref{Theorem-Entropy-Cohomology})}\\[11.38109pt]
&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(P_{i},\nu_{d}^{-n}(P_{j}))&(\nu_{d}^{-n}(P_{j})\in\mathrm{mod}A)\\[11.38109pt]
&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}|\chi_{\mathbb{R}}([P_{i}],[\nu_{d}^{-1}]^{n}([P_{j}]))|&(\nu_{d}^{-n}(P_{j})\in\mathrm{mod}A)\\[11.38109pt]
&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|[\nu_{d}^{-1}]^{n}\|\\[11.38109pt]
&=\log\rho([\nu_{d}^{-1}])&\mbox{(Gelfand formula)}\\[11.38109pt]
&=\log\rho([\nu^{-1}])=\log\rho([S^{-1}])=\log\rho(\Phi^{-1}).&([\nu_{d}^{-1}]=(-1)^{d}[\nu^{-1}])\\[11.38109pt]
\end{array}

Here, the Gelfand formula is
\rho(A)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|A^{n}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}
for all complex square matrix
A
(See
[
3
, 1.3.3]
). By Lemma
2
, we have
h_{t}(\nu^{-1})+dt=h_{t}(\nu^{-1}_{d})=\log\rho(\Phi^{-1})
.
Thus
h_{t}(\nu^{-1})=-dt+\log\rho(\Phi^{-1})
.

Similarly, since
A
is
d
-representation-infinite, by
[
16
, Proposition 2.9]
,
we have
\nu_{d}(A)=A^{*}[-d]
and
\nu_{d}^{n}(A^{*})\in\mathrm{mod}A
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Let
I_{1},\cdots,I_{r}
be a complete set of indecomposable injective right
A
-modules.
Then
\nu_{d}^{n}(I_{j})\in\mathrm{mod}A
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
1\leq j\leq r
. Thus

\begin{array}[]{lll}&h_{t}(\nu_{d})\\
=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Ext}^{l}_{A}(A,\nu_{d}^{n}(A))\cdot e^{-lt}&\mbox{(Theorem~\ref{Theorem-Entropy-Cohomology})}\\[11.38109pt]
=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Ext}^{l-d}_{A}(A,\nu_{d}^{n-1}(A^{*}))\cdot e^{-lt}&(\nu_{d}(A)=A^{*}[-d])\\[11.38109pt]
=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Ext}^{q}_{A}(A,\nu_{d}^{n-1}(A^{*}))\cdot e^{-qt}&(e^{-dt}\mbox{ is independent of }n)\\[11.38109pt]
=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(P_{i},\nu_{d}^{n-1}(I_{j}))&[\nu_{d}^{n-1}(I_{j})\in\mathrm{mod}A]\\[11.38109pt]
=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}|\chi_{\mathbb{R}}([P_{i}],[\nu_{d}^{n-1}(I_{j})])|&[\nu_{d}^{n-1}(I_{j})\in\mathrm{mod}A]\\[11.38109pt]
=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}|\chi_{\mathbb{R}}([P_{i}],[\nu_{d}]^{n}([P_{j}]))|&([\nu_{d}]([P_{j}])=(-1)^{d}[I_{j}])\\[11.38109pt]
=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|[\nu_{d}]^{n}\|&\\[11.38109pt]
=&\log\rho([\nu_{d}])&\mbox{(Gelfand formula)}\\[11.38109pt]
=&\log\rho([\nu])=\log\rho([S])=\log\rho(\Phi).&([\nu_{d}]=(-1)^{d}[\nu])\end{array}

By Lemma
2
, we have
h_{t}(\nu)-dt=h_{t}(\nu_{d})=\log\rho(\Phi)
.
So
h_{t}(\nu)=dt+\log\rho(\Phi)
.

From Lemma
3
, we obtain
h(\nu)=h(\nu^{-1})
. Hence
\rho(\Phi)=\rho(\Phi^{-1})
.

(2) Since
A
is
d
-representation-infinite, we have
\nu_{d}^{-n}(A)\in\mathrm{mod}A
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Applying Lemma
4
, (1) and Lemma
7
, we obtain

\displaystyle h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{t}(\nu^{-1}_{d})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L4}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)}(A,\nu_{d}^{-n}(A)[l])\cdot e^{-lt}-n\cdot h_{t}(\nu_{d}^{-1})}{\log n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(P_{i},\nu_{d}^{-n}(P_{j}))-n\cdot h_{t}(\nu_{d}^{-1})}{\log n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}|\chi([P_{i}],[\nu_{d}^{-n}(P_{j})])|-n\cdot h_{t}(\nu_{d}^{-1})}{\log n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|[\nu_{d}^{-1}]^{n}\|-n\cdot\log\rho([\nu_{d}^{-1}])}{\log n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L7}}{{=}}

\displaystyle s([\nu_{d}^{-1}])=s([\nu^{-1}])=s([S^{-1}])=s(\Phi^{-1}).

Similarly,
applying Lemma
4
, (1) and Lemma
7
, we obtain

\displaystyle h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{t}(\nu_{d})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L4}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)}(A,\nu_{d}^{n}(A)[l])\cdot e^{-lt}-n\cdot h_{t}(\nu_{d})}{\log n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)}(A,\nu_{d}^{n-1}(A^{*})[l-d])\cdot e^{-lt}-n\cdot h_{t}(\nu_{d})}{\log n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)}(P_{i},\nu_{d}^{n-1}(I_{j}))-n\cdot h_{t}(\nu_{d})}{\log n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{r}|\chi([P_{i}],[\nu_{d}]^{n}([P_{j}]))|-n\cdot h_{t}(\nu_{d})}{\log n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|[\nu_{d}]^{n}\|-n\cdot\log\rho([\nu_{d}])}{\log n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L7}}{{=}}

\displaystyle s([\nu_{d}])=s([\nu])=s([S])=s(\Phi).

By Lemma
5
and Lemma
6
, we have
h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{t}(\nu)=h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{t}(\nu_{d})=h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{-t}(\nu_{d}^{-1})=h^{\mathrm{pol}}_{-t}(\nu^{-1})
. Furthermore,
s(\Phi)=s(\Phi^{-1})
.

(3) By
[
13
, Lemma 5]
, we have
C_{A^{*}}=C^{T}_{A}
.
Thanks to the Yomdin type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy in Theorem
5
, we obtain

h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(A^{*})\geq\log\rho(\Psi_{A^{*}})=\log\rho(C_{A^{*}}C_{A}^{-1})=\log\rho(C_{A}^{T}C_{A}^{-1})=\log\rho(\Phi)
.
From (1) above, we know
h(S)=\log\rho(\Phi)
. Due to Theorem
2
, we have
h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})\leq h(S)
.
By Proposition
1
, we get
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})
.
Thus
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S})=h(S)=\log\rho(\Phi)
.

Similarly, by
[
13
, Theorem 1 (1) and Lemma 5]
, we have
C_{\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)}=(C^{T}_{A})^{T}C^{-T}_{A}C_{A}=C_{A}C^{-T}_{A}C_{A}
. By the Yomdin type inequality on Hochschild homology entropy in Theorem
5
, we obtain
h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A))\geq\log\rho(\Psi_{\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)})=\log\rho(C_{\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(A^{*},A)}C_{A}^{-1})=\log\rho(C_{A}C_{A}^{-T})=\log\rho(\Phi^{-1})
. From (1) above, we know
h(S^{-1})=\log\rho(\Phi^{-1})=\log\rho(\Phi)
.
It follows from Theorem
2
that
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})\leq h(S^{-1})
.
By Proposition
1
, we get
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})
.
Thus
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{-1})=h(S^{-1})=\log\rho(\Phi^{-1})=\log\rho(\Phi)
.

(4) For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, from
[
16
, Proposition 2.9 (d)]
, we obtain

S^{n}(A)=\nu_{d}^{n-1}(A^{*})[d(n-1)]\in(\mathrm{mod}A)[d(n-1)]

which is a stalk complex concentrating on degree
-d(n-1)
.
Thus we have

\overline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\underline{\mathrm{Sdim}}A=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\inf S^{n}(A)}{n}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\sup S^{n}(A)}{n}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(n-1)}{n}=d.

Now we have finished the proof of the theorem. ∎

Remark 2
.

(1) Theorem
7
(1) generalizes
[
7
, Theorem 2.17]
, and implies that the Gromov-Yomdin type equality on entropy, that is,
h(S)=\log\rho([S])
, holds for the Serre functor on perfect derived category of an elementary higher representation-infinite algebra.

(2) Theorem
7
(2) (cf.
[
12
, Proposition 4.4]
) implies that the polynomial entropy of Serre functor on perfect derived category of an elementary higher representation-infinite algebra has nothing to do with the parameter

(3) Theorem
7
(3) implies that the Kikuta-Ouchi’s question (Question

) has positive answer, that is,
h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{\pm 1})=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{\pm 1})=h(S^{\pm 1})

h^{HH^{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{\pm 1})=\linebreak\log\rho([H^{0}(\tilde{S}^{\pm 1})])=h^{HH_{\bullet}}(\tilde{S}^{\pm 1})
holds, for the (inverse) Serre quasi-functor on perfect dg module category of an elementary higher representation-infinite algebra.

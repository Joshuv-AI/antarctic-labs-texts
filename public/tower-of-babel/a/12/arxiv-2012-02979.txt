Abstract:
In this paper, we investigate the logarithmic nonlinear Schrödinger (LNLS) equation with the parity-time (
\mathcal{PT}
)-symmetric harmonic potential, which is an important physical model in many fields such as nuclear physics, quantum optics, magma transport phenomena, and effective quantum gravity. Three types of initial value conditions and periodic boundary conditions are chosen to solve the LNLS equation with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential via the physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) deep learning method, and these obtained results are compared with ones deduced from the Fourier spectral method. Moreover, we also investigate the effectiveness of the PINNs deep learning for the LNLS equation with
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric potential by choosing the distinct space widths or distinct optimized steps. Finally, we use the PINNs deep learning method to effectively tackle the data-driven discovery of the LNLS equation with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential such that the coefficients of dispersion and nonlinear terms or the amplitudes of
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential can be approximately found.

Keywords:
Logarithmic nonlinear Schrödinger equation;
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric potentials; physics-informed neural networks; solitons

The shared parameters between the neural network

4.1
Discovering the coefficients of
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential

Here we identify the coefficient of potential
V_{0},W_{0}
in the focusing LNLS equation with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential

\displaystyle i\psi_{t}=-\psi_{xx}+(V_{0}x^{2}+iW_{0}x)\psi-\psi\ln(|\psi|^{2}),

(30)

where
V_{0},\,W_{0}
are the unknown real-valued parameters.

Let
\varphi(x,t)=u(x,t)+iv(x,t)
with
u(x,t),\,v(x,t)
being its real and imaginary parts, respectively, and the PINNs
F(x,t)=iF_{u}(x,t)-F_{v}(x,t)
with
-F_{v}(x,t),\,F_{u}(x,t)
being its real and imaginary parts, respectively, be

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}F(x,t):=i\varphi_{t}+\varphi_{xx}-(V_{0}x^{2}+iW_{0}x)\varphi+\varphi\ln(|\varphi|^{2}),\\
F_{u}(x,t):=u_{t}+v_{xx}-W_{0}xu-V_{0}x^{2}v+v\ln(u^{2}+v^{2}),\\
F_{v}(x,t):=v_{t}-u_{xx}+V_{0}x^{2}u-W_{0}xv-u\ln(u^{2}+v^{2}),\end{array}

Then the deep neural network is used to train
\{u(x,t),\,v(x,t)\}
and parameters
(V_{0},\,W_{0})
by minimizing the mean squared error loss

\displaystyle{\rm TL}={\rm TL}_{uv}+{\rm TL}_{D}

(35)

with

\displaystyle{\rm TL}_{uv}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{D}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{D}}\left(|u(x^{j},t^{j})-u^{j}|^{2}+|v(x^{j},t^{j})-v^{j}|^{2}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle{\rm TL}_{D}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{D}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{D}}\left(|F_{u}(x^{j},t^{j})|^{2}+|F_{v}(x^{j},t^{j})|^{2}\right).

where
\{x^{j},\,t^{j},\,u^{j},\,v^{j}\}_{i=1}^{N_{D}}
represents the training data on
u(x,t),\,v(x,t)
, and
u(x^{j},t^{j}),\,v(x^{j},t^{j})
are real and imaginary parts of the exact solution
\varphi(x,t)=u(x,t)+iv(x,t)
given by Eq. (
23
) with
A=V_{0}=1,\,W_{0}=0.2,\,\sigma=-1
in
(x,t)\in[-3,3]\times[0,10]
.

To study the data-driven discovery of the LNLS equation (
30

V_{0},\,W_{0}
, we generate a training data-set by randomly choosing

from the exact solution
\psi(x,t)

. Then the obtained data-set is applied to train an

-layer deep NN with
50
neurons per hidden layer to find the approximate parameters
V_{0},\,W_{0}
in terms of minimizing the mean squared loss of (
35
). We here use the 20000-step Adam optimization and 20000-step L-BFGS optimization.

5
Conclusions and discussions

In conclusion, we have used the PINNs deep learning method to solve the LNLS equation with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential and distinct
initial-boundary value conditions. We find that the deep learning method is powerful in the LNLS equation by comparing these results and ones deduced from the Fourier spectral method. Moreover, we also verify the PINNs learning ability by solving the LNLS equation with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential under the distinct conditions: 1) we fix the optimized step and change the space width; 2) we fix the space width and change the optimized step. Moreover, we also apply the PINNs deep learning method to effectively investigate the data-driven discovery of the LNLS equation with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric harmonic potential. Moreover, the PINNs deep learning method can also be extended to other nonlinear wave equations with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric potentials.

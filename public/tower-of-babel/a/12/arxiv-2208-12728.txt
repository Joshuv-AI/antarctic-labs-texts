Abstract
For linear control systems, the usual state feedback stabilizability has two components: one is a continuous observation mode (i.e., to observe solutions continuously in time), and the other is a class of feedback laws (which is usually the space of all of the linear and bounded operators from a state space to a control space). This paper studies the stabilizability for abstract linear control systems, with a discrete observation mode (i.e., to observe solutions discretely in time) and two different classes of feedback laws. We first characterize these types of stabilizabilities via some weak observability inequalities for the dual systems. Then, we use these characterizations to reveal the connections between these types of stabilizabilities and those with continuous observation mode. Finally, we show some applications of the aforementioned weak observability inequalities.

1.3
Motivation and aim

For the system (
1.1
), we have introduced four types of stabilizability, which are the
(CC)
-stabilizability,

(CP)
T
-stabilizability,
(DC)
T
-stabilizability, and
(DP)
T
-stabilizability, where two different observation modes and two different
classes of feedback laws are involved.
First, as mentioned above, the
(CC)
-stabilizability is characterized by
the weak observability inequality (
1.3
). However,
the characterizations of the stabilizability for the system (
1.1
) with discrete observation modes
have not been examined to the best of our knowledge. This motivated us to characterize the

-stabilizability and the
(DP)

-stabilizability via some weak observability inequalities (which may be different from (

Second, it is important to illustrate
the difference between the discrete and continuous observation modes.
It would also be interesting to reveal the possibly different influences of different classes of feedback
laws on the stabilizability. This inspired us to find relationships between the above-mentioned
four types of stabilizabilities.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
1.8

We organized the proof in several steps.

Step 1. It is trivial that
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
.

Step 2. We prove that
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|u_{F}(t)\|_{U}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\int_{(i-1)T}^{iT}\|F(t)y_{F}((i-1)T)\|^{2}_{U}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C(\omega,T))^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|F(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}(Y;U)}dt\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}e^{-2(i-1)\omega T}\|y_{0}\|_{Y}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}(\omega,T,F)\|y_{0}\|_{Y}^{2},

. Thus,
u_{F}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};U)
.
Third, it follows from (
1.8
) and (

y_{F}(t)=S(t)y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)Bu_{F}(s)ds\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(3.16)

Now, by (
3.13
), (
3.15
), and
(
3.16
), we have that when

and

\displaystyle|\langle y_{0},S(\hat{N}T)^{*}\varphi\rangle_{Y}|

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\langle y_{F}(\hat{N}T),\varphi\rangle_{Y}|+\Big|\int_{0}^{\hat{N}T}\langle u_{F}(s),B^{*}S(\hat{N}T-s)^{*}\varphi\rangle_{U}ds\Big|

\displaystyle\|y_{F}(\hat{N}T)\|_{Y}\|\varphi\|_{Y}+\Big(\int_{0}^{\hat{N}T}\|u_{F}(s)\|_{U}^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\hat{N}T}\|B^{*}S(\hat{N}T-s)^{*}\varphi\|_{U}^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle C(\omega,T)e^{-\hat{N}\omega T}\|y_{0}\|_{Y}\|\varphi\|_{Y}+\sqrt{\widetilde{C}(\omega,T,F)}\|y_{0}\|_{Y}\Big(\int_{0}^{\hat{N}T}\|B^{*}S(\hat{N}T-s)^{*}\varphi\|_{U}^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Noting that
y_{0}\in Y
was arbitrarily taken, by taking
\hat{N}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
such that

\hat{\delta}:=2(C(\omega,T))^{2}e^{-2\hat{N}\omega T}\in(0,1)

. Thus,

(3.18)

We arbitrarily fix
y_{0}\in Y
. Several facts are stated as follows. First, because

A+BF
with

D(A+BF)=D(A)

Second, it is clear that

(3.20)

Third, for an arbitrarily fixed

Almost toric presentations of symplectic log Calabi-Yau pairs
Tian-Jun Li, Jie Min, Shengzhen Ning

Abstract
It’s known ( [ Sym03 ] ) that the union of fibers over elliptic singularities of an almost toric fibered (ATF) symplectic four-manifold gives a symplectic log Calabi-Yau (LCY) divisor. In this note, we show the converse also holds: any symplectic log Calabi-Yau divisor can be realized as the boundary divisor of an almost toric fibration. This realization is canonical once we choose an extra data called the framing on the space of LCY. This is achieved by considering the symplectic analogue of the toric model used by [ GHK15 ] in the algebraic geometrical settings.

A symplectic log Calabi-Yau divisor in a symplectic rational manifold is a cycle of symplectically embedded spheres whose intersections are positively transverse and the sum of homology classes are Poincaré to the first Chern class. After some deformation the intersections can also be made orthogonal. By a symplectic log Calabi-Yau pair (or simply pair), we mean a triple
(X,\omega,D)

Proposition 4

(Symplectic Torelli theorem)
.

If there is an integral isometry
\gamma:H_{2}(X^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})

We will apply the above result in the following particular way. If one already knows two pairs are isomorphic, then after the toric blowup (blowdown) at the corresponding node (component) or non-toric blowup (blowdown) at the corresponding component of the same the size, the outcomes will also be isomorphic.

Lemma 5

(Tautness of toric pairs)

Toric elements in

3.3

Two base diagrams are equivalent if they are only differed by rearrangement of surgery triangles. The base diagram set

is then defined to be the equivalent classes of all bitten Delzant polygons and the above two extra types of diagrams. As what we did in section

can also be equipped with a quiver structure where each arrow corresponds to chopping a corner or adding a new surgery triangle.

Now let’s carefully explain the relation between
\mathbb{BD}
and

through almost toric fibration.

Proposition 10
.

There is a canonical morphism between quivers
\mathbf{B}:\mathbb{BD}\rightarrow\mathbb{LCY}
by taking the boundary divisor.

Proof.

On the level before modulo equivalence relations in both

\mathbb{BD}
and
\mathbb{LCY}
, each base diagram gives a stratified integral affine disk with nodes
(B,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
. According to
[
Sym03
]
Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.4, one can produce an almost toric fibered symplectic four-manifold over the base
(B,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
. Then by
[
Sym03
]
Proposition 8.2, the fibers over
0
and
1
-strata will form a divisor whose homology class is Poincaré dual to the first Chern class. Thus we will obtain a log Calabi-Yau pair.

To make this assignment into a map
\mathbf{B}:\mathbb{BD}\rightarrow\mathbb{LCY}
, we use the following observations. Let’s first assume the base diagram is a bitten Delzant polygon (that is, not of the extra types). For any surgery triangle lying on some edge, by
[
Sym03
]
Theorem 7.4, one always has an embedded symplectic sphere (unique up to isotopy) lying over the curve going from the node to the edge. This sphere intersects the boundary divisor transversally and has self intersection
-1
due to adjunction formula. After blowing down all of those exceptional spheres, we will get a toric divisor. Note that the area sequence and self-intersection sequence of this toric divisor will not change under the rearrangement of surgery triangles on the bitten Delzant polygon. Thus, by tautness of toric pairs (Lemma
5
), we will get an element in
\mathbb{LCY}
. Then by Proposition
4
and the followed illustration therein, the original log Calabi-Yau pair assigned to the bitten Delzant polygon will also represent a well-defined element in
\mathbb{LCY}
.

Now if the base diagram is of extra types, its
\varepsilon
or
(\varepsilon,\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
-shift will be a bitten Delzant polygon. Thus we can firstly obtain an element in
\mathbb{LCY}
corresponding to the shift. The original space, associated to the extra types base diagram, is then the toric blowdowns with sizes
\varepsilon
or
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
of that element. When
\varepsilon
is small enough, the exceptional spheres blown down are distinguished among the components of the log Calabi-Yau divisor. Therefore we will also get a well-defined element in
\mathbb{LCY}
again by Proposition
4
.

To further upgrade this map into a morphism between quivers, firstly notice that the arrow of chopping a corner in
\mathbb{BD}
naturally correspond to the arrow of toric blowup in
\mathbb{LCY}
. To establish the correspondence between adding a surgery triangle and a non-toric blowup, we blow down the exceptional sphere lying over the newly added surgery triangle. It remains to show the divisor after blowdown represents the element in
\mathbb{LCY}
associated to the base diagram before adding the surgery triangle. One then can run a similar argument as above by blowing down all the other exceptional spheres lying over other surgery triangles into some toric pairs and then use tautness of toric pairs and Torelli theorem.
∎

Now we can state the main result of this note:

Theorem 11
.

Given a framing
\mathfrak{f}
on
\mathbb{LCY}
, there is a canonical section
\mathbf{R}_{\mathfrak{f}}:\mathbb{LCY}\rightarrow\mathbb{BD}
of the map
\mathbf{B}:\mathbb{BD}\rightarrow\mathbb{LCY}
.

After picking a framing

\Theta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}\leq 1-\delta_{1}=x,

\Theta_{2}\leq(1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{i})+\delta_{2}+(2a-2)\delta_{3}=1-\delta_{i}+(a-1)(\delta_{1}+2\delta_{3})-(a-1)\delta_{1}+(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1})

\leq 1-\delta_{i}+a-1-(a-1)\delta_{1}=y.

(iv)

If
f_{2}
is the non-toric blowup on
aH-(a-1)E_{1}
,

\Theta_{1}\leq\delta_{3}+(1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})\leq 1-\delta_{1}=x,

\Theta_{2}\leq\delta_{3}+\delta_{4}+(2a-2)\delta_{3}=a(\delta_{1}+2\delta_{3})-(a-1)\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}+(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1})+(\delta_{4}-\delta_{3})

\leq a-(a-1)\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}=y;

if
f_{2}
is the toric blowup on the node between
aH-(a-1)E_{1}
and
H-E_{1}
, then
a_{Y_{1}},b_{Y_{1}}\leq\delta_{3}
,
a_{X_{1}},b_{X_{1}}\leq 1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}
,
a_{X_{1}^{\prime}},b_{X_{1}^{\prime}}\leq\delta_{3}
hold and we have

\Theta_{1}\leq\delta_{3}+(1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})\leq 1-\delta_{1}=x,

\Theta_{2}\leq(1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})+\delta_{3}+(2a-2)\delta_{3}=1-\delta_{2}+(a-1)(\delta_{1}+2\delta_{3})-(a-1)\delta_{1}+(\delta_{3}-\delta_{1})

\leq 1-\delta_{2}+a-1-(a-1)\delta_{1}=y;

otherwise,
a_{Y_{1}},b_{Y_{1}}\leq\delta_{3}
still holds and we have

\Theta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}\leq 1-\delta_{1}=x,

\Theta_{2}\leq\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}+(2a-1)\delta_{3}=a(\delta_{1}+2\delta_{3})-(a-1)\delta_{1}+(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1})

\leq a-(a-1)\delta_{1}=y.

(v)

\Theta_{1}\leq\delta_{3}+(1-\delta_{2}-\delta_{l})\leq 1-\delta_{l}=x,

\Theta_{2}\leq(1-\delta_{l}-\delta_{i})+(1-\delta_{l}-\delta_{j})+\delta_{3}=y-(1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}-\delta_{3})\leq y.

Therefore Lemma
13
gives us an disjoint embedding of all the surgery triangles and we get an element
\mathbb{P}
in
\mathbb{BD}
. When
\mathbb{X}_{\varepsilon}
is equal to
\mathbb{X}
,
\mathbf{R}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathbb{X})
is then defined to be this
\mathbb{P}
. Otherwise, according to our construction of
\mathbb{X}_{\varepsilon}
, there will be one or two (depending on small blowup times) distinguished edges of the bitten Delzant polygon whose affine length are much less than others. As explained in section
3.2
, there will be an extra type diagram
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{BD}
whose
\varepsilon
-shift (or
(\varepsilon,\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
-shift) is
\mathbb{P}
, in which case
\mathbf{R}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathbb{X})
is defined to be this
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
.

To see
\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{R}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathbb{X}))=\mathbb{X}
, first notice that
\mathbf{B}(\mathbb{P})
always gives
\mathbb{X}_{\varepsilon}
due to our construction of
\mathbb{X}_{tor}
in section
2.3
and
\mathbf{B}
in Proposition
10
. So if
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}_{\varepsilon}
the result immediately follows. Otherwise, since the inverse of
\varepsilon
-shift (or
(\varepsilon,\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
-shift) corresponds to
\varepsilon
-replacement
\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathbb{X}_{\varepsilon}
,
\mathbf{B}(\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
will give us
\mathbb{X}
. The proof of Theorem
11
is now completed.

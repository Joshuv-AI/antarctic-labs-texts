Integral Artin motives I: Smooth objects and the ordinary t-structure
Raphaël Ruimy

Abstract
. We link smooth Artin motives to étale local systems and Artin representations. We then construct the ordinary motivic t-structure on Artin motives with integral coefficients and show that the ℓ \ell -adic realization functor is t-exact.

Rigidity for torsion motives

One of the main contrasts between Artin étale motives with rational coefficients and Artin étale motives with integral coefficients is that the category of integral motives will contain many motives which should come from higher dimensional motives with torsion coefficients. This is in fact an instance of the rigidity theorem. Let
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
be the stable
\infty
-category of hypersheaves on the small étale site of
S
with value in the derived category of
R
-modules. We have by
Proposition

1.4.5
a functor

\rho_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

which sends a sheaf to the infinite Tate suspension of the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localization of its extension to the big étale site. This functor is in fact very close to being an equivalence when restricted to torsion objects. More precisely, we define

•

The stable category of
torsion étale motives
(resp.
p^{\infty}
-torsion étale motives
) denoted by
\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},\mathrm{tors}}(S,R)
(resp.
\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},p^{\infty}-\mathrm{tors}}(S,R)
) as the subcategory of the stable category
\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
made of those objects
M
such that the motive
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
(resp.
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]
) vanishes.

•

The stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\mathrm{tors}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
(resp.
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{p^{\infty}-\mathrm{tors}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
) of
torsion étale sheaves
(resp.
p^{\infty}
-torsion étale sheaves
) as the subcategory of the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
made of those sheaves

M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
(resp.

The following result is then a consequence of the Rigidity Theorem

(1)

(2)

One of the main ideas of this text is to deduce information on the t-structures on Artin motives with integral coefficients from the t-structures on Artin motives with rational coefficients and the t-structures on torsion motives.

Smooth Artin motives

The subcategory of dualizable (or lisse) objects of the derived category of
\ell
-adic sheaves plays a key role in the study of
\ell

•

The category
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)
of constructible smooth Artin étale motives defined as the thick subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

S
-schemes.

•

The category
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
of smooth Artin étale motives defined as the localizing subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

An important step towards the answer of the questions above is to describe the categories

The ordinary motivic t-structure

In this paper and its sequel, we will introduce two
homotopy t-structures
on the category
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
whose definitions are inspired by the approach of
[
Pep19b
,
Vai19
,
Ayo07
,
BD17
]
. This paper will focus on the
ordinary homotopy t-structure
while the sequel will focus on the
perverse homotopy t-structure
. The t-structures are defined by giving a family of t-non-positive generators. Our goal will be to determine when those t-structures induce t-structures on the category of constructible objects and when the realization functor is t-exact.

We extend the main results of
[
Pep19a
,
Vai19
]
about the ordinary motivic t-structure to the case of integral coefficients.

Theorem
.

(
Theorem

4.2.7
) Let
S
be a scheme allowing resolution of singularities by alterations and let
R
be a regular good ring.

(1)

The ordinary homotopy t-structure induces a non-degenerate t-structure on the subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)
. We still call this t-structure the
ordinary homotopy
t-structure.

(2)

Assume that the ring
R
is a localization of the ring of integers of a number field
K
. Let
v
be a non-archimedian valuation on
K
and let
\ell
be the prime number such that
v
extends the
\ell
-adic valuation. Then, the reduced
v
-adic realization functor

\overline{\rho}_{v}\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}^{A}(S,R)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S[1/\ell],R_{v})

of
Definition

3.4.1
is t-exact when the left hand side is endowed with the ordinary homotopy t-structure and the right hand side is endowed with the ordinary t-structure.

To prove this result, we first study the case of smooth Artin motives and of torsion motives. We show that the inclusion functors

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

and

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\mathrm{tors}}(S,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

are t-exact when the left hand sides are endowed with their ordinary t-structures and the right hand side is endowed with the ordinary homotopy t-structure (see
Propositions

4.2.1
and
4.1.6
).
To finish the proof, we show that the pullback functors are t-exact (see
Proposition

4.2.5
) and conclude by stratifying any constructible motive by smooth Artin motives.

\mathcal{D}
be a t-category and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be a full stable subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
. If
(\mathcal{D}^{\leqslant 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\mathcal{D}^{\geqslant 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
defines a t-structure on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, we say that
\mathcal{D^{\prime}}
is a
sub-t-category
of
\mathcal{D}
, that the t-structure of
\mathcal{D}

induces a t-structure
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and call the latter the
induced t-structure
.

The following lemma will allow us to define the ordinary t-structure.

Proposition 1.1.3
.

(
[
Lur17
, 1.4.4.11]
) Let
\mathcal{C}
be a presentable stable category. Given a small family
\mathcal{E}
of objects, the smallest subcategory
\mathcal{E}_{-}
closed under small colimits and extensions is the set of non-positive objects of a t-structure.

In this setting, we will call this t-structure the
t-structure generated by

\mathcal{E}
. When a set
\mathcal{E}
of objects has a small set of isomorphism classes, we still call
t-structure generated by

\mathcal{E}
the t-structure generated by a small family of representatives of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proposition 1.1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category endowed with a Grothendieck topology
\tau
and let
\Lambda
be a ring. Denote by
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
the t-category of
\Lambda
-modules in the category of
\tau
-hypersheaves of spectra and denote by

\Lambda(-)\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)

the
\tau
-hypersheafification composed with the Yoneda functor. Then,

(1)

The category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
is generated as a localizing subcategory of itself by the sheaves of the form
\Lambda(X)
where
X
runs through the set of objects of
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
has a small set of isomorphism classes. Then, the t-structure on
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
is generated by the sheaves of the form
\Lambda(X)
where
X
runs through the set of objects of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
is a localization of the category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
of presheaves of
\Lambda
-modules and since every t-non-positive sheaf is the sheafification of a t-non-positive presheaf it suffices to prove the analogous result for the category of presheaves.

The category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
is compactly generated by the sheaves of the form
\Lambda(X)
where
X
runs through the set of objects of
\mathcal{C}
. The first assertion then follows from
[
Nee01
, 8.4.1]
.

We now prove the second assertion. A presheaf
M
is t-positive with respect to the ordinary t-structure on
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
if and only if for object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
, the complex
M(X)
of
\Lambda
-modules is
0
-connected. But since for any object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
, we have

M(X)=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)}(\Lambda(X),M),

this is equivalent to the condition that
M
is t-positive with respect to the t-structure generated by the sheaves of the form
\Lambda(X)
with
X
an object of
\mathcal{C}
. Hence, both t-structures coincide.
∎

The following lemma shows how t-structures generated by a set of objects behave with respect to change of coefficients.

Lemma 1.1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be
\mathbb{Z}
-linear presentable stable category, let
\mathcal{E}
be a small family of objects of
\mathcal{C}
, let
n
be an integer and let
A
be a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
. Denote by
\sigma_{n}\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
and by
\sigma_{A}\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow A
the natural ring morphisms.

If
R=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
or
R=A
and if
\sigma\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow R
is the natural ring morphism, we denote by

\sigma^{*}\colon\mathcal{C}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\colon\sigma_{*}

the adjunction given by
[
Lur17
, 4.6.2.17]
.
Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{R}
the family of objects of
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
made of the
\sigma^{*}M
.

Endow
\mathcal{C}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}
, resp.
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
) with the t-structure generated by
\mathcal{E}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}_{Z/n\mathbb{Z}}
, resp.
\mathcal{E}_{A}
) in the sense of
Proposition

1.1.3
.

(1)

The functors of the form
\sigma_{*}
are t-exact.

(2)

Assume furthermore that if
M
belongs to
\mathcal{E}
and
N
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
, the map

\sigma_{A}^{*}\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,\sigma_{A}^{*}N)

is an equivalence. Then, the functor
\sigma_{A}^{*}
is t-exact.

Proof.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
or
R=A
and let
\sigma\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow R
be the natural ring morphism. The colimit preserving exact functor
\sigma^{*}
sends the generators of the t-structure to t-non-positive objects and is therefore right t-exact. By adjunction, the functor
\sigma_{*}
is therefore left t-exact.

Notice then that if
M
lies in
\mathcal{E}
, the object
\sigma_{n}^{*}M
is the cofiber of the map
M\overset{\times n}{\rightarrow}M.

Therefore the object
\sigma_{n}^{*}M
is a colimit of t-non-positive objects of
\mathcal{C}
and is therefore t-non-positive.

Since the subcategory of t-non-positive objects of the stable category
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}
is the smallest subcategory closed under small colimits and extensions containing the
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
when
M
runs through
\mathcal{E}
, and since the exact functor
(\sigma_{n})_{*}
preserves small colimits, the functor
(\sigma_{n})_{*}
is therefore right t-exact.

On the other hand, write
A=\Sigma^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
with
\Sigma
a multiplicative system. Then, the object
\sigma_{A}^{*}M
is the colimit of the diagram of value
M
with transition maps the multiplications by
s
for
s\in\Sigma
. Hence, it is t-non-positive. Since the functor
(\sigma_{A})_{*}
is also colimit preserving, this yields its t-exactness.

To prove the second assertion, it suffices to show that the exact functor
\sigma_{A}^{*}
is left-t-exact under our assumption. Take
N
a t-non-negative object of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
M
be an object of
\mathcal{E}
. Then, we have

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}}(\sigma_{A}^{*}M,\sigma_{A}^{*}N)=\operatorname{Map}(M,\sigma_{A}^{*}N)

But, by assumption, we have

\operatorname{Map}(M,\sigma_{A}^{*}N)=\sigma_{A}^{*}\operatorname{Map}(M,N).

Thus, the chain complex
\operatorname{Map}(\sigma_{A}^{*}M,\sigma_{A}^{*}N)
is
(-1)
-connected and the object
\sigma_{A}^{*}(N)
is t-non-negative. Therefore, the functor
\sigma_{A}^{*}
is t-exact.
∎

In our study, we will need the following:

Lemma 1.1.6
.

Let
I
be a small set, let
\mathcal{D}
be a t-category. For any index
i
in
I
, let
\mathcal{D}_{i}
be a t-category and let
T_{i}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{i}
be a t-exact functor. Assume that the family
(T_{i})_{i\in I}
is conservative.

Then, an object
M
of
\mathcal{D}
is t-positive (resp. t-negative) if and only if for any index
i
in
I
, the object
T_{i}(M)
is t-positive (resp. t-negative) in
\mathcal{D}_{i}
.

Proof.

The t-exactness of the
T_{i}
yields the "only if" part of the lemma.

We now prove the "if" part. Let
M
be an object of
\mathcal{D}
such that for any
i\in I
, the object
T_{i}(M)
of
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is t-positive. Then, for any
i\in I
, the functor
T_{i}
sends the map
M\rightarrow\tau_{>0}M
to an equivalence since t-exact functors commute with the positive truncation. Therefore, the map
M\rightarrow\tau_{>0}M
is an equivalence and
M
is t-positive. The t-negative case is similar.
∎

1.2.2.
Change of Coefficients

Let
\mathscr{D}
be one of the fibered categories
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
,
\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
,
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
and
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(-_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}})
.
Let
\sigma\colon R\rightarrow R^{\prime}
be a ring homomorphism.

The adjunction

\sigma^{*}\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Mod}_{R^{\prime}}\colon\sigma_{*}

provides a premotivic adjunction

\sigma^{*}\colon\mathscr{D}(-,R)\leftrightarrows\mathscr{D}(-,R^{\prime})\colon\sigma_{*},

except for
\mathscr{D}=\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(-_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}})
where the adjunction exists but is not premotivic.

If
S
is a scheme, we can identify
\mathscr{D}(S,R)
with the category of
R
-modules in
\mathscr{D}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. The functor
\sigma_{*}
is then the forgetful functor, and the functor
\sigma^{*}
is the functor
-\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
.

Finally, the map
\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{*}
comes from the functor

\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathbf{Ab})\xrightarrow{H}\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcalboondox{Sp})\xrightarrow{h(S)\otimes\operatorname{Id}}\mathrm{CAlg}(\widehat{\operatorname{Sm}_{S}}\otimes\mathcalboondox{Sp})\xrightarrow{L_{{\mathbb{A}^{1}}}L_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{SH}(S))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Mod}_{-}}\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{Stb}}).

Proposition 1.2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be one of the fibered categories
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
,
\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
and
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
. Let
\sigma\colon R\rightarrow R^{\prime}
be a ring morphism of the form
\sigma_{A}\colon R\rightarrow R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A
or
\sigma_{n}\colon R\rightarrow R/nR
, where
A
is a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
and
n
is an integer.

(1)

The functor
\sigma^{*}
commutes with the functors
\rho_{\#}
,
\rho^{*}
,
L_{{\mathbb{A}^{1}}}
,
\iota
,
\Sigma^{\infty}
and
\Omega^{\infty}
defined in
Section

1.2.1
.

(2)

Let
n
be an integer, let
S
be a scheme and let
M
and
N
be objects of
\mathscr{D}(S,R)
. Then, the canonical maps

\sigma_{n}^{*}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,\sigma_{n}^{*}N)

\sigma_{n}^{*}\operatorname{Map}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,\sigma_{n}^{*}N)

are equivalences.

(3)

Let
A
be a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
, let
S
be a scheme and let
M
and
N
be objects of
\mathscr{D}(S,R)
. Assume that
M
is constructible in the sense that it is in the thick subcategory generated by representable objects (and Tate twist in the case of non-effective étale motives). Then, the canonical maps

\sigma_{A}^{*}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,\sigma_{A}^{*}N)

\sigma_{A}^{*}\operatorname{Map}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{\mathscr{D}(S,R)}(M,\sigma_{A}^{*}N)

are equivalences.

(4)

If
\mathscr{D}
is one of the categories
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
,
\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
and
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
, the functor
\sigma^{*}
commute with the six functors formalism.

Proof.

The functor
\rho^{*}
is the restriction to the small étale site and therefore, it commutes with the forgetful functor
\sigma_{*}
and with the functor
\sigma^{*}
. Hence, its left adjoint
\rho_{\#}
also commutes with the functor
\sigma^{*}
.

The functor
\iota
is the inclusion of
{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-local sheaves into the fibered category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
. Since the
{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-locality property does not depend on the ring of coefficients, the functor
\iota
commutes with the forgetful functor
\sigma_{*}
. Therefore the functors
L_{{\mathbb{A}^{1}}}
and
\sigma^{*}
commute. Furthermore,
[
CD16
, 5.4.5, 5.4.11]
ensure that if
M
is
{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-local, then so is
\sigma^{*}(M)
which implies that
\iota
and
\sigma^{*}
commute.

Following
[
CD16
, 5.4]
the functor
\sigma^{*}
commutes with geometric sections. Since the functors
\sigma^{*}
and
\Sigma^{\infty}
are colimit-preserving and since by
Proposition

1.1.4
, the category
\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
is generated as a localizing subcategory of itself by geometric sections, those functors commute. The proof of
[
CD16
, 5.5.5]
shows that the functor
\Omega^{\infty}
commutes with the functor
\sigma_{A}^{*}
. Finally, we have exact triangle of functors

\operatorname{Id}\overset{\times n}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Id}\rightarrow\sigma_{n}^{*}

in
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
and
\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}

which induce an exact triangles of functors when applying
\Omega^{\infty}
to the left or to the right

\Omega^{\infty}\overset{\times n}{\rightarrow}\Omega^{\infty}\rightarrow\Omega^{\infty}\sigma_{n}^{*}

\Omega^{\infty}\overset{\times n}{\rightarrow}\Omega^{\infty}\rightarrow\sigma_{n}^{*}\Omega^{\infty}

Hence, the functors
\Omega^{\infty}
and
\sigma_{n}^{*}
commute, which finishes the proof of the first assertion.

The second assertion follows from the exactness of the triangle

\operatorname{Id}\overset{\times n}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Id}\rightarrow\sigma_{n}^{*}

in the categories
\mathscr{D}(S,R)
and
\mathcal{D}(R)
.

In the case where
\mathscr{D}=\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
, the third assertion follows from
[
CD16
, 5.4.11]
. Tensoring with
A
respects
{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-local objects by
[
CD16
, 1.1.11]
. Therefore, the third assertion in the case where
\mathscr{D}=\mathcal{DA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
follows from the same assertion when
\mathscr{D}=\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
. The assertion in both of the cases when
\mathscr{D}=\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
and when
\mathscr{D}=\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(-_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}})
follows from
[
CD16
, 1.1.11]
.

Using
[
CD16
, 5.4.5, 5.4.11, A.1.1.16]
, we see that the six functors commute with
\sigma^{*}
in the case of
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
. We can mimic the proofs of
[
CD16
, 5.4.5, 5.4.11]
in the other cases to prove the last assertion.
∎

Proposition 1.2.3
.

Let

is an equivalence.

Proof.

With the notations of

1.1.2
, the functor

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{na}}\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})

1.2.1
. Since the category
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Therefore, it is an equivalence.
∎

1.3.
The
\ell
-adic Realization and the Bhatt-Scholze Formalism

In this section, we recall the
\ell
-adic formalism.

Let
S
be a scheme and
\ell
be a prime number invertible on
S
. Let
\Lambda
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
or the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. There are several definitions of the
derived category of constructible
\ell
-adic sheaves

\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,\Lambda)
. The first definition was given in
[
BBDG18
]
for
S
of finite type over a field. In
[
Eke07
]
,
[
Ayo14
]
,
[
CD16
, 7.2]
,
[
BS15
]
and
[
ILO14
, XIII.4]
, this definition was extended to all quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes. In this section, we recall the definitions of
[
CD16
, 7.2]
and
[
BS15
]
and explain the link between them. We also recall some usual properties of the category
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,\Lambda)
.

1.3.1.
The
\lambda
-adic realization

We now fix a discrete valuation ring
R
with a local parameter
\lambda
such that the residue field
R/\lambda R
is finite. We denote by
\ell
the latter’s characteristic.

Definition 1.3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
R
-linear category. An object
M
in
\mathcal{C}
is
\lambda
-complete if the natural map

M\rightarrow\lim M\otimes_{R}(R/\lambda^{n}R)

is an equivalence. We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
formed by
\lambda
-complete objects.

We have a
\lambda
-completion functor
\rho_{\lambda}^{0}\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
, which sends an object
M
to the object
\lim M\otimes_{R}(R/\lambda^{n}R)
.

If
S
is a scheme, we let
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(-,R_{\lambda}):=\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(-,R)_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 1.3.2
.

(
[
CD16
, 7.2.11]
)
The premotivic category
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(-,R_{\lambda})
can be endowed with the six functors formalism and satisfies absolute purity. Furthermore, the functor
\rho_{\lambda}^{0}
commutes with the six functors.

We have the following variant of the rigidity theorem according to
[
AGV22
, 2.10.4]
.

Proposition 1.3.3
.

When restricted to the category of
\mathbb{Z}[1/\ell]
-schemes, the adjunction

\rho_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(-_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)_{\lambda}\leftrightarrows\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R_{\lambda})\colon\rho^{!}

is an equivalence.

Finally, we have a constructible version of this variant of the rigidity theorem. We can indeed define the category of constructible
\lambda
-complete objects as follows.

Definition 1.3.4
.

Let
S
be a scheme. A
\lambda
-complete étale sheaf (resp. étale motive) is
constructible
if there exists a stratification of
X
such that the restriction of the sheaf to each stratum is dualizable. We denote by
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{c}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R_{\lambda})
(resp.
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R_{\lambda})
) the full subcategory spanned by constructible objects.

We immediatly conclude that over
\mathbb{Z}[1/\ell]
-schemes, the functor
\rho_{!}
induces an equivalence of monoidal categories

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{c}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R_{\lambda})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R_{\lambda}).

We now denote by
R_{\lambda}
the completion of
R
with respect to the
\lambda
-adic valuation. We now present the Bhatt-Scholze adic formalism in the version developed in
[
HRS20
]
.

Bhatt and Scholze defined the pro-étale topology as follows: a morphism of schemes
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is
weakly étale
if it is flat and if the diagonal morphism
\Delta_{f}\colon X\rightarrow X\times_{Y}X
is flat. The pro-étale site
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
of a scheme
X
is then defined as the category of schemes that are weakly étale over
X
. It is equipped with a site structure where a family
(X_{i}\rightarrow X)_{i\in I}
is considered covering if, for every open affine
U
of
X
, there exists an open affine of
\bigsqcup\limits_{i\in I}X_{i}
that covers
U
.

The topological ring
R_{\lambda}
defines, for any scheme
X
, a sheaf
R_{\lambda,X}
on the proétale site
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
of
X
with values in the category
\operatorname{D}(\mathbb{Z})
. This sheaf is an algebra in the category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathbb{Z})
of pro-étale hypersheaves on
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
.

Definition 1.3.5
.

(1)

We define the presentably monoidal stable category
\mathcal{D}(X,R_{\ell})
of
\lambda
-adic sheaves
as the category of
R_{\lambda,X}
-modules in
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathbb{Z})
.

(2)

A
\lambda
-adic sheaf is
constructible
if there exists a stratification of
X
such that the restriction of this sheaf to each stratum is dualizable. The full subcategory
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(X,R_{\lambda})
of
\mathcal{D}(X,R_{\lambda})
formed by constructible objects is a monoidal subcategory.

Similarly, we can define the category of constructible étale sheaves, denoted by
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{c}(X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
.

To link those categories together, recall that we have a morphism of sites
\nu\colon S_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}\rightarrow S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
which, for any integer
n
, induces a premotivic adjunction

\nu^{*}\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(-_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R/\lambda^{n}R)\leftrightarrows\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(-_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},R/\lambda^{n}R)\colon\nu_{*}.

According to
[
HRS20
, 3.40]
, the latter is an equivalence and we even have the following result.

Proposition 1.3.6
.

Let
X
be a scheme. The mapping
M\mapsto\lim\nu^{*}(M)/\ell^{n}
induces an equivalence

\Psi\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{c}(X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R_{\lambda})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(X,R_{\lambda}).

Definition 1.3.7
.

The
\lambda
-adic realization
is the functor

\rho_{\lambda}=\Psi\rho^{!}\rho_{\lambda}^{0}\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,R_{\ell}).

We can derive the following result from
[
CD16
, 7.2.16]
.

Proposition 1.3.8
.

The functor

\rho_{\ell}\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(-,R)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(-,R_{\ell})

commutes with the six functors when restricted to quasi-excellent schemes and morphisms of finite type between these schemes.

We finish this section by describing the rational version of the adic realization. Let
K
be the fraction field of
R
. We denote by
K_{\lambda}
the completion of
K
for the
\lambda
-adic valuation.

Definition 1.3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
R
-linear category. We define the
K
-linearization
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{R}K
of
\mathcal{C}
as the idempotent completion of the stable
K
-linear category having the same objects as
\mathcal{C}
and such that

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}\otimes_{R}K}(M,N)=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,N)\otimes_{R}K.

We define the category
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(X,K_{\ell})
in the same fashion as
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(X,R_{\ell})
. According to
[
CD16
, 7.2.23]
, we have

\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,K_{\ell})=\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,R)\otimes_{R}K

and

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,K)=\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)\otimes_{R}K.

We then define the
\lambda
-adic realization functor as follows.

Definition 1.3.10
.

The
\lambda
-adic realization
is the functor

\rho_{\lambda}\otimes_{R}K\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,K)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,K_{\lambda})

which is also denoted as
\rho_{\lambda}
.

Since we have a pre-motivic equivalence between étale motives and h-motives, as shown in
[
CD16
, 7.2.24]
, we obtain the following result (the reader is also referred to
[
Ayo14
, 6.6]
).

Proposition 1.3.11
.

The functor

\rho_{\lambda}\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(-,K)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(-,K_{\lambda})

commutes with the six operations when restricted to quasi-excellent schemes and morphisms of finite type between these schemes.

1.3.2.
The
v
-adic Realization

Let
S
be a scheme, let
R
be a localization of the ring of integers of a number field
K
, let
v
be a non-archemedian valuation on the field
K
and let
\ell
be the prime number such that
v
extends the
\ell
-adic valuation. Assuming that the prime number
\ell
is invertible on
S
, we can define an
\ell
-adic realization functor, or in this context more accurately a
v
-adic realization functor

\rho_{v}\colon\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,R_{v})

which preserves constructible objects and respects the six functors formalism.
In the case when
v
is non-negative on

Proposition 2.1.3

(3)

(4)

All those equivalences commute with the six functors formalism.

Proof.

Proof of Assertion (1):

For any prime number
p
, let
\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})
be Prüfer
p

A
is an abelian group, denote by
\underline{A}
the image through the functor

\rho_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

of the constant sheaf with value
A
. We have an exact triangle

\mathbbm{1}_{S}\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}_{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]\rightarrow\underline{\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})},

therefore, if
M
is a
p^{\infty}
-torsion étale motive, we have

M=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\underline{\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})}[-1].

Furthermore, if
p
and

are distinct prime numbers, the canonical map

\mathbb{Z}(q^{\infty})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}(q^{\infty})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]

is an isomorphism.

For any prime number
p
, let
M_{p}
and
N_{p}
be
p^{\infty}
-torsion étale motives. Notice that if
p
is a prime number, we have

\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{ prime}\\
q\neq p\end{subarray}}N_{q}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{ prime}\\
q\neq p\end{subarray}}N_{q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}(q^{\infty})[-1]

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{ prime}\\
q\neq p\end{subarray}}N_{q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}(q^{\infty})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p])[-1]

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{ prime}\\
q\neq p\end{subarray}}N_{q}\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]

Therefore, we get

\operatorname{Map}\left(M_{p},\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{ prime}\\
q\neq p\end{subarray}}N_{q}\right)=0.

Hence, the map

\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\operatorname{Map}(M_{p},N_{p})\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{p\text{ prime}}M_{p},\bigoplus\limits_{p\text{ prime}}N_{p}\right)

is an equivalence. Therefore, the functor

\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},p^{\infty}-\mathrm{tors}}(S,R)\rightarrow\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},\mathrm{tors}}(S,R)

is fully faithful.

We now prove that this functor is essentially surjective. We have an exact triangle

\mathbbm{1}_{S}\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}_{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\underline{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}.

Let now
M
be a torsion étale motive. We get that

Furthermore, we have

\underline{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}=\bigoplus\limits_{p\text{ prime}}\underline{\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})}.

and since the functor
\rho_{!}
commutes with colimits, we therefore get that

M=\bigoplus\limits_{p\text{ prime}}\left(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\underline{\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})}[-1]\right).

As

the motive

-torsion and therefore, the functor

Proposition 2.2.2

Let
S
be a scheme and
R
be a ring. Then, the ordinary t-structure of the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{p^{\infty}-\mathrm{tors}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)

Let
\tau_{\geqslant 0}
be the truncation with respect to the ordinary t-structure of
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)

be an étale sheaf of

-torsion. The functor
\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]

\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, we have

\tau_{\geqslant 0}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]=\tau_{\geqslant 0}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]).

Furthermore, the sheaf
\tau_{\geqslant 0}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/p])

\tau_{\geqslant 0}(M)
is of

-torsion. We conclude by using
[

, 1.3.19]

A product of t-categories is endowed with a t-structure by taking the product of the subcategories of t-non-positive objects as the subcategory of t-non-positive objects and the product of the subcategories of t-non-negative objects as the subcategory of t-non-negative objects.

This allows us to define an ordinary t-structure on

-torsion étale motives.

3.
Description of Smooth Artin Motives and Applications

3.1.
Artin Representations and Lisse Étale Sheaves

3.1.1.
Discrete representations, Artin representations

Recall the following definitions:

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\pi
be a topological group and let
R
be a ring. An
R[\pi]
-module
M
is
discrete
if the action of
\pi
on
M
is continuous when
M
is endowed with the discrete topology. We will denote by
\mathrm{Mod}(\pi,R)
the Grothendieck abelian category of discrete
R[\pi]
-modules.

Remark 3.1.2
.

Keep the same notations. An
R[\pi]
-module is discrete if and only if the stabilizer of every point is open. Equivalently, this means that
M
is the union of all the
M^{U}
for
U
an open subgroup of
\pi
.

If the topological group
\pi
is profinite, this also means that
M
is the union of all the
M^{H}
for
H
a normal subgroup of
\pi
of finite index.

Definition 3.1.3
.

Let
\pi
be a profinite group and let
R
be a ring. An
Artin representation
of
\pi
is a discrete
R[\pi]
-module which is of finite presentation as an
R
-module. We will denote by
\operatorname{Rep}^{A}(\pi,R)
the abelian category of Artin representations.

Remark 3.1.4
.

Keep the same notations. An
R[\pi]
-module is an Artin representation if and only if it is of finite presentation as an
R
-module and the action of
\pi
factors through a finite quotient. Indeed, if
M
is an Artin representation, the stabilizer of a finite generating family is open and thus contains a normal subgroup
N
of finite index. Therefore, the action of
\pi
factors through
\pi/N
.

3.1.2.
Locally constant étale sheaves

Definition 3.1.5
.

Let
S
be a scheme and let
R
be a regular ring. We define:

(1)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Sh}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
of
étale sheaves of
R
-modules
as the heart of the ordinary t-structure on the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
.

(2)

The abelian category
\mathrm{Loc}_{S}(R)
of
locally constant sheaves of
R
-modules
as the subcategory of the abelian category
\operatorname{Sh}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
made of those sheaves which are étale-locally constant with finitely presented fibers.

(3)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Ind}\mathrm{Loc}(S,R)
of
Ind-locally constant sheaves of
R
-modules
as the subcategory of the abelian category
\operatorname{Sh}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
made of those sheaves which are filtered colimits of locally constant sheaves of
R
-modules.

(4)

The
category of lisse étale sheaves

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(S,R)
as the subcategory of dualizable objects of the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
.

(5)

The
category of Ind-lisse étale sheaves

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\mathrm{lisse}}(S,R)
as the localizing subcategory of the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
generated by
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(S,R)
.

These categories are related to each other by the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1.6
.

Let
S
be a scheme and let
R
be a regular ring.

(1)

An étale sheaf is lisse if and only if it is locally constant with perfect fibers.

(2)

The ordinary t-structure on the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
induces a t-structure on the subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(S,R)
whose heart is the abelian category
\mathrm{Loc}_{S}(R)
.

(3)

The ordinary t-structure on the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
induces a t-structure on the subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\mathrm{lisse}}(S,R)
whose heart is the abelian category
\operatorname{Ind}\mathrm{Loc}(S,R)
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from
[
Sta23
, 0FPP]
. To prove the second assertion, notice that, as a consequence of the first assertion, lisse sheaves are bounded with respect to the ordinary t-structure of
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
. Therefore,
[
Rui22
, Lemma 1.2.3]
ensures that it suffices to show that

\operatorname{Loc}_{S}(R)=\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\cap\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)^{\heartsuit}.

The fact that locally constant sheaves with finitely presented fibers have perfect fibers follows from the hypothesis that the ring
R
is regular, since in this case, an
R
-modules is perfect if and only if it is finitely presented by
[
Sta23
, 066Z]
. Moreover, an abelian sheaf with perfect fibers is finitely presented. This finishes the proof of the second assertion.

Any Ind-lisse étale sheaf is a filtered colimit of lisse étale sheaves. Furthermore, by
[
Lur17
, 1.3.5.21]
, the ordinary t-structure of
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
is compatible with filtered colimits. Therefore, if
M=\operatorname{colim}_{i\in I}M_{i}
is an Ind-lisse étale sheaf, where
I
is a filtered simplicial set and each
M_{i}
is lisse, we have

\tau_{\geqslant 0}(M)=\tau_{\geqslant 0}(\operatorname{colim}M_{i})=\operatorname{colim}\tau_{\geqslant 0}(M_{i}).

Therefore, the sheaf
\tau_{\geqslant 0}(M)
is Ind-lisse. Hence, by
[
BBDG18
, 1.3.19]
, the ordinary t-structure of
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
induces a t-structure on the category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\mathrm{lisse}}(S,R)
. An object
L
of the heart of this t-structure is a filtered colimit of lisse sheaves
M_{i}
. But then, we have,

L=\operatorname{colim}M_{i}=H^{0}(\operatorname{colim}M_{i})=\operatorname{colim}H^{0}(M_{i}).

Therefore, the sheaf
L
is Ind-locally constant. Conversely, Ind-locally constant sheaves lie in the heart of the ordinary t-structure and are Ind-lisse. This finishes the proof of the third assertion.
∎

The above proposition shows in particular that
\operatorname{Loc}_{S}(R)
is a weak Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Sh}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
. We have the following characterization of lisse sheaves:

Proposition 3.1.7
.

Let
S
be a connected scheme and let
R
be a regular ring. Let
\xi
be a geometric point of
S
. The fiber functor associated to
\xi
induces an equivalence of abelian monoidal categories

\xi^{*}\colon\mathrm{Loc}_{S}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Rep}^{A}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,\xi),R)

where
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}(S,\xi)
is the étale fundamental group of
S
with base point
\xi
of
[
GR71
, V]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Loc}_{S}
be the Galois category of locally constant sheaves of sets on
S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
. By definition of the étale fundamental group, the fiber functor

\xi^{*}\colon\mathrm{Loc}_{S}\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,\xi)-\mathrm{Set}

is an equivalence.

The constant sheaf
R_{S}(S)
is sent to the set
R
endowed with the trivial action of
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,\xi)
. Thus, the functor
\xi^{*}
induces an equivalence

\mathrm{Loc}_{S}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Rep}^{A}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,\xi),R).

∎

In the case when

is the spectrum of a field

Proposition 3.1.8

Let

The first assertion follows from

The second assertion follows from the first assertion and the description of lisse sheaves

(1).

Furthermore, any complex of discrete

\psi\colon\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Rep}^{A}(G_{k},R))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{perf}}(\mathrm{Mod}(G_{k},R))

It suffices to show that the canonical map

Proposition 3.1.9

Let

(1)

X
is a finite étale

Lemma 3.1.10
.

Let

be a scheme and let
R
be a regular ring and let

\xi
be a geometric point of

through the fiber functor associated to

Rui22
, 1.3.1]
(which is a result of Balmer and Gallauer) the complex

3.2.

Recall that the étale motivic cohomology groups of a scheme

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme, let

be a good ring and let

By

1.2.2

-algebra.

In the first case, by the rigidity theorem

the Prüfer
\ell
-group. Then we have the following result.

Proposition 3.2.3
.

Let
X
be a geometrically unibranch scheme which is the disjoint union of its irreducible components, let
R
be a ring which is flat over
\mathbb{Z}
and in which all the residue characteristics of
X
are invertible and let
e(X)
be the set of residue characteristic exponents of
X
. Then,

H^{n}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,R)=\begin{cases}R^{\pi_{0}(X)}&\text{ if }n=0\\
0&\text{ if }n=1\\
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\left[\bigoplus\limits_{\ell\notin e(X)}\left(\mathbb{Z}(\ell^{\infty})^{b_{n-1}(X,\ell)}\oplus T_{n-1}(X,\ell)\right)\right]&\text{ if }n\geqslant 2\end{cases}

Proof.

As
R
is flat over
\mathbb{Z}
, we may suppose that
R
is the subring

\mathbb{Z}[1/e(X)]=\left\{\frac{n}{p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{m_{r}}}\mid p_{i}\in e(X)\right\}

of
\mathbb{Q}
. We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[1/e(X)]\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{\ell\notin e(X)}\mathbb{Z}(\ell^{\infty})\rightarrow 0.

Hence, by
Proposition

3.2.2
, we get

H^{n}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}[1/e(X)])=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}[1/e(X)]^{\pi_{0}(X)}&\text{ if }n=0\\
0&\text{ if }n=1\\
\bigoplus\limits_{\ell\notin e(X)}\operatorname{colim}_{N}H^{n-1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{N}\mathbb{Z})&\text{ if }n\geqslant 2\end{cases}

This yields the result.
∎

Remark 3.2.4
.

If a complete theory of abelian motives was available, all the
b_{i}(X,\ell)
would coincide. Thus, for
n\geqslant 2
, we would have

H^{n}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,R)=R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\left[\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}[1/e(X)]^{b_{i}(X)}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\ell\notin e(X)}T_{n-1}(X,\ell)\right]

Notice that, using Artin’s Theorem
[
AGV73
, XI.4.4]
or Deligne’s work on Weil’s conjectures (see
[
Del80
]
) all the
b_{i}(X,\ell)
coincide if
X
is defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
or over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Finally, if
X
is a curve over any separably closed field
k
, using
[
AGV73
, IX.4.7]
, we have

H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,R)=(R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}[1/p])^{2g}

H^{3}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,R)=R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}[1/p]

and
H^{n}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,R)=0
for

.

3.3.
Equivalence Between Smooth Artin Motives and Lisse Étale Sheaves over a Regular Base scheme

In this paragraph, we give an explicit description of smooth Artin étale motives over a regular base scheme. This description will not work over a non-regular base scheme, even when it is normal (see
Remark

3.3.2
below).

Theorem 3.3.1
.

Let
R
be a regular good ring and let
S
be a regular scheme. Assume that the residue characteristic exponents of
S
are invertible in
R
.

The functor
\rho_{!}
of
Proposition

1.4.5
induces monoidal equivalences

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R),

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R).

Proof.

We can assume the scheme
S
to be connected. If
V
is an étale cover of
S
, the functor
\rho_{!}
sends the sheaf
R_{S}(V)
to the motive
M_{S}(V)
. Using
Proposition

3.1.9
, the fact that the ring
R
is regular and the fact that
\rho_{!}
commutes with colimits and finite limits, it induces exact functors

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R).

Assuming that the second functor is an equivalence, the first functor is also an equivalence since
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)
is the thick stable subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)
generated by the sheaves of the form
R_{S}(V)
when
V
runs through the set of étale covers of
S
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)
is the thick stable subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
generated by the motives of the form
M_{S}(V)
when
V
runs through the set of étale covers of
S
.

We now prove that the functor

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

induced by
\rho_{!}
is an equivalence. Assuming that the functor
\rho_{!}
is fully faithful, its essential surjectivity follows from the fact that
\mathcal{DM}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
is generated as a localizing subcategory of itsef by the essential image of the functor
\rho_{!}
. Hence, it suffices to prove that the functor above is fully faithful.

Let
\rho^{!}
be the right adjoint of
\rho_{!}
. The full faithfulness of
\rho_{!}
holds for any good ring. It is equivalent to the fact that the ordinary transformation
\alpha\colon Id\rightarrow\rho^{!}\rho_{!}
is an equivalence. Using
Lemmas

1.2.2
and
1.2.1
, we may assume that the ring
R
is either of
\ell
-torsion where
\ell
is invertible in
S
, or a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. The case where
R
is of
\ell
-torsion follows from
[
BH21
, 3.2]
. Therefore, we can assume that the ring
R
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.

The family of functors
\operatorname{Map}(R_{S}(W),-)
when
W
runs through the set of étale
S
-schemes is conservative. Therefore, it suffices to show that for any Ind-lisse étale sheaf
M
and any étale
S
-scheme
W
, the canonical map

\operatorname{Map}(R_{S}(W),M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(R_{S}(W),\rho^{!}\rho_{!}M)

is an equivalence. This is equivalent to the fact that the canonical map

\operatorname{Map}(R_{S}(W),M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(M_{S}(W),\rho_{!}M)

is an equivalence.

Since
R
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, by
[
CD16
, 1.1.4, 1.1.9]
the sheaf
R_{S}(W)
is a compact object of
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
and the motive
M_{S}(W)
is a compact object of
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
. As the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)
generated as a localizing subcategory of itself by objects of the form
R_{S}(V)
where
V
is finite étale over
S
, it therefore suffices to show that for any étale finite scheme
V
over
S
and any étale scheme
W
over
S
, the map

\rho_{W,V}\colon\operatorname{Map}(R_{S}(W),R_{S}(V))\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(M_{S}(W),M_{S}(V))

is an equivalence.

Recall now, that the étale sheaf
R_{S}(V)
is dualizable with itself as a dual. As the functor
\rho_{!}
is monoidal, this implies that the motive
M_{S}(V)
is also dualizable with itself as a dual. Hence, the map
\rho_{W,V}
is an equivalence if and only if the map
\rho_{W\times_{S}V,S}
is an equivalence. As

\rho_{X,S}=\bigoplus\limits_{T\in\pi_{0}(X)}\rho_{T,S},

it suffices to show that the maps
\rho_{T,S}
are equivalences for
T
étale over
S
and connected. But if
T
is étale over
S
, the map
\rho_{T,S}
is an equivalence if and only if the canonical map

\psi_{T}\colon\operatorname{Map}(R_{T}(T),R_{T}(T))\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(\mathbbm{1}_{T},\mathbbm{1}_{T})

is an equivalence.

Such a
T
is irreducible and geometrically unibranch. Therefore, using
Proposition

3.2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(\operatorname{Map}(R_{T}(T),R_{T}(T)))

\displaystyle=H^{n}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(T,R)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}R&\text{ if }n=0.\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover, as such a
T
is also regular (and connected), using
[
Pep19b
, B.5]
,

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(\operatorname{Map}(\mathbbm{1}_{T},\mathbbm{1}_{T}))

\displaystyle=H^{n,0}_{\mathcal{M},\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(T,R)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}R&\text{ if }n=0.\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus, it is sufficient to show that the map
\pi_{0}(\psi_{T})
is non-zero in order to show that it is an equivalence. But
\pi_{0}(\psi_{T})
is the natural map

\operatorname{Hom}(R_{T}(T),R_{T}(T))\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbbm{1}_{T},\mathbbm{1}_{T})

and this map sends the identity of
R_{T}(T)
to the identity of
\mathbbm{1}_{T}
.
∎

a

Proposition 3.3.9
.

Let
S
be a regular scheme and let
R
be a localization of the ring of integers of a number field
K
, let
v
be a non-archemedian valuation on the field
K
and let
\ell
be the prime number such that
v
extends the
\ell
-adic valuation. Assume that
\ell
is invertible on
S
and that all the residue characteristic exponents of
S
are invertible in
R
.

(1)

If
v
is non-negative on
R
, denote by
\lambda
a uniformizer of
R_{(v)}
. Let
\Xi
be the functor

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\lambda}(S,R_{(v)})

that sends an étale sheaf
M
to the
\lambda
-complete sheaf
\lim(M\otimes_{R}R_{(v)})/\lambda^{n}

Then, with the notations of
Section

1.3.2
, the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\lambda}(S,R_{(v)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(S,R_{v})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Xi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{v}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}

is commutative.

Furthermore, when restricted to the subcategory of constructible smooth Artin étale motives, the
v
-adic realization functor is t-exact with respect to the ordinary t-structures
and induces the exact functor

-\otimes_{R}R_{v}\colon\operatorname{Loc}_{S}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}^{smA}(S,R_{v})

between the hearts.

(2)

If
v
is not non-negative on
R
, Let
A
be the subring of
K
on which the valuation
v
is non-negative and let
\lambda
be a uniformizer of
A
. Then, with the notations of
Section

1.3.2
, the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\lambda,c}(S,A)_{(0)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,R_{v})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Xi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{v}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi^{\prime}}

is commutative using the notations of
Section

1.1.2
, if
\Psi^{\prime}
denotes the functor
\Psi_{(0)}
and
\Xi^{\prime}
denotes the functor
\Xi_{(0)}\circ(-\otimes_{R}K)
.

Furthermore, the
v
-adic realization functor is t-exact with respect to the ordinary t-structures and induces the exact functor

-\otimes_{R}R_{v}\colon\operatorname{Loc}_{S}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}^{smA}(S,R_{v})

between the hearts.

Proof.

The second assertion follows from the first assertion. We now prove the first assertion.
Recall that with the notations of
Section

1.3.2
, we have

\rho_{v}=\Psi\circ\rho^{!}\circ\rho_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{CD}}.

But as
\rho^{!}
is a right adjoint functor, it commutes with limits which yields

\rho^{!}\circ\rho_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{CD}}\circ\rho_{!}=\Xi.

To show that the
\ell
-adic realization functor is t-exact when restricted to the subcategory of constructible smooth Artin étale motives, it suffices to show that the functor

\Xi\circ\Psi\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,R_{v})

is t-exact. Let
M
be a locally constant étale sheaf with coefficients in
R
. We have

\Xi(\Psi(M))=\lim\nu^{*}(M)/\lambda^{n}.

It suffices to show that
\lim\nu^{*}(M)/\lambda^{n}
lies in the heart of the ordinary t-structure on
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{c}(S,R_{v})
. Now, by definition of the ordinary t-structure (see
[
BBDG18
, 2.2.13]
) if

H^{i}(\lim\nu^{*}(M)/\lambda^{n})=\lim H^{i}(\nu^{*}(M/\lambda^{n}))=\lim\nu^{*}\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,R/\lambda^{n}R).

Now, the exact sequence

0\rightarrow R\overset{\times\lambda^{n}}{\rightarrow}R\rightarrow R/\lambda^{n}R\rightarrow 0

yields

\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,R/\lambda^{n}R)=\begin{cases}M/\lambda^{n}M&\text{if }i=0\\
M[\lambda^{n}]&\text{if }i=-1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where

denotes the
\lambda^{n}
-torsion of

. As the transition map

is the multiplication by
\lambda
, we get
\lim M[\lambda^{n}]=0
. The result follows.
∎

3.4.
The Conservativity Conjecture for Artin Motives

The conservativity conjecture, proved for Artin motives with rational coefficients in
[

]
, asserts that the
\ell
-adic realization functor
[
CD16
, 7.2.11]
is conservative. With integral coefficients, we need to include realization functors with respect to every
\ell
. Indeed, if
M
is an Artin motive of
p
-torsion and
\ell\neq p
, the
\ell
-adic realization of

vanishes.

Furthermore, the stable category of constructible
\ell
-adic complexes has a non-pathological behavior only when
\ell
is invertible on the base scheme. Therefore, we introduce the following functor.

Definition 3.4.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme, let
R
be a localization of the ring of integers of a number field

, let
v
be a valuation on

and let
\ell
be the prime number such that
v
extends the
\ell
-adic valuation.

The

v
-adic realization functor

is the composition

\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)\overset{j_{\ell}^{*}}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S[1/\ell],R)\overset{\rho_{v}}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{D}(S[1/\ell],R_{v}),

where
j_{\ell}
denotes the open immersion
S[1/\ell]\rightarrow S
and where the functor
\rho_{v}
is the
v
-adic realization functor defined in
Section

1.3.2
.

We still denote by
\bar{\rho}_{v}
the restriction of this functor to the stable subcategory of constructible Artin motives.

A consequence of
Theorem

3.3.1
is the conservativity conjecture in the case of Artin motives.

Proposition 3.4.2
.

Let
S
be a scheme, let
R
be a localization of the ring of integers of a number field
K
. If
v
is a valuation on
K
, denote by

the completion of

with respect to

and by

the completion of
K
with respect to
v
.

(1)

If there is a prime number
\ell
which is invertible on

, let
v
be a valuation which extends the
\ell
-adic valuation then, the functor

\bar{\rho}_{v}\colon\mathcal{DM}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}^{A}(S,K)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(S,K_{v})

is conservative.

(2)

If
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are two distinct prime numbers, let
v
be a valuation which extends the
\ell
-adic valuation and let

be a valuation which extends the
\ell^{\prime}
-adic valuation. Then, when working with coefficients in

The Ordinary Homotopy t-structure on Artin Motives

4.1.
Definition for Non-constructible Artin Motives

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme and let

be a ring. The

is the t-structure generated in the sense of

1.1.3
by the family of the motives
M_{S}^{\operatorname{BM}}(X)
with

quasi-finite over
S
.

We denote with the small letters
{\mathrm{ord}}
the notions related to the ordinary homotopy t-structure on
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)
. For instance, we denote by
{}^{\mathrm{ord}}\tau_{\leqslant 0}
the left truncation with respect to the ordinary homotopy t-structure and by

the ordinary homotopy t-structure itself.
Using the same method as
Proposition

1.4.3
, this t-structure is also generated by family of the motives
M_{S}^{\operatorname{BM}}(X)
with
X
finite (resp. étale) over
S
. Thus, an object

of
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)
is in
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)^{\geqslant n}
if and only if for any étale
S
-scheme
X
, the complex
\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}(M_{S}(X),M)
is
(n-1)
-connected.

Proposition 4.1.2
.

Let
R
be a ring. Let
f
be a quasi-finite morphism of schemes and let
g
be a morphism of schemes. Then, the functors
f_{!}
,
g^{*}
and
-\otimes_{S}M
are right
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-exact.

Proof.

The full subcategory of those Artin motives
N
such that
f_{!}N
,
g^{*}N
and

are
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-non-positive is closed under extensions, small coproducts and negative shifts and contains the set of generators of the t-structure. Thus, by definition of
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
, this subcategory contains any
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-non-positive object. Hence, the functors
f_{!}
,
g^{*}
and
-\otimes M
are right
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-exact.
∎

Corollary 4.1.3
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
f
be a morphism of schemes. Then,

(1)

If
f
is étale, the functor
f^{*}=f^{!}
is
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-exact.

(2)

If
f
is finite, the functor
f_{!}=f_{*}
is
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-exact.

Proof.

If
f
is étale, the functor
f^{!}
is right adjoint to
f_{!}
and therefore left
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-exact. The case where
f
is finite is handled similarly.
∎

Lemma 4.1.4
.

Let
S
be a scheme and let
R
be a ring. Let

be finite over

. Then, the motive

lies in the heart of the ordinary homotopy t-structure.

Proof.

If
f
is finite, the functor

is

-exact by

4.1.3
and therefore, we may assume that

. By definition, we have

\mathbbm{1}_{S}\leqslant_{\mathrm{ord}}0.

Let

be étale over

, and let

be an integer, by definition, we have

But if the integer

is negative, this group vanishes by

and the result follows.
∎

We now explore the compatibility of the ordinary homotopy t-structure with respect to change of coefficients.

Proposition 4.1.5
.

Let
S
be a scheme, let

be a ring, let

be an integer and let

be a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
. Denote by
\sigma_{n}\colon R\rightarrow R/nR
and by

the natural ring morphisms.
Recall the notations of

1.2.2
.
Then,

(1)

The functors

and

are

-exact.

(2)

the functor

is

-exact.

This follows from

1.1.5
and from
Proposition

1.2.1
.
∎

Proposition 4.1.6
.

Let
S
be a scheme and let
R
be a ring. Recall
Proposition

1.4.7
. Then, the inclusion functor

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},\mathrm{tors}}(S,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)

is t-exact when the left hand side is endowed with the ordinary t-structure (see
Definition

2.2.3
) and when the right hand side is endowed with the ordinary homotopy t-structure.

Proof.

If
X
is étale over
S
, the functor
\rho_{!}
maps the étale sheaf
R_{S}(X)
to the motive
M_{S}(X)
which is
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-non-positive. Recall that
Proposition

2.2.1
asserts that the sheaves of the form

with
X
étale over
S
generate the ordinary t-structure on the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
. Since the exact functor

\rho_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)

of
Proposition

1.4.5
is compatible with small colimits, it is therefore right t-exact.

If
p
is a prime number, we denote by
j_{p}\colon S[1/p]\rightarrow S
the open immersion. Recall that by
Proposition

2.1.3
, the functor
j_{p}^{*}
induces an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},p^{\infty}-\mathrm{tors}}(S,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},p^{\infty}-\mathrm{tors}}(S[1/p],R)

with inverse
(j_{p})_{!}=(j_{p})_{*}
.

Now, let
M
be a torsion étale motive. Assume first that

is t-non-positive with respect to the ordinary t-structure. By definition, this means that

M=\bigoplus_{p\text{ prime}}(j_{p})_{!}\rho_{!}N_{p},

where for any prime number
p
,

is a
p^{\infty}
-torsion étale sheaf which is t-non-positive with respect to the ordinary t-structure .

By right t-exactness of the functor
\rho_{!}
, for any prime number

, the motive
\rho_{!}N_{p}
is

-non-positive. Hence, using
Proposition

4.1.2
, so is the motive
(j_{p})_{!}\rho_{!}N_{p}
. Therefore, the motive
M
is
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-non-positive.

Assume now that
M
is t-non-negative with respect to the ordinary t-structure. Let
X
be an étale
S
-scheme. By
Proposition

1.2.4
, we get

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}(M_{S}(X),M)=\bigoplus_{p\text{ prime}}\operatorname{colim}_{n}\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}(M_{S}(X),M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}[-1]).

If
p
is a prime number and if
n
is an integer, denote by
M_{p^{n}}
the motive
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}[-1]
. We have

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}(M_{S}(X),M_{p^{n}})=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R/nR)}(M_{S}(X,R/nR),M_{p^{n}}).

Since by
[
CD16
, A.3.4]
, we have

M_{p^{n}}=(j_{p})_{*}j_{p}^{*}M_{p^{n}},

we get

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R/nR)}(M_{S}(X,R/nR),M_{p^{n}})=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S[1/p]_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R/nR)}(M_{S[1/p]}(X[1/p]),j_{p}^{*}(M_{p^{n}})).

And therefore, we get

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}(M_{S}(X),M_{p^{n}})=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S[1/p]_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)}(R_{S[1/p]}(X[1/p]),\rho^{!}j_{p}^{*}(M_{p^{n}})).

If
p
is a prime number and if

is an integer, the

-torsion étale motive

is t-non-negative with respect to the ordinary t-structure. Therefore, by definition of the ordinary t-structure, the

-torsion étale sheaf
\rho^{!}j_{p}^{*}(M_{p^{n}})
is t-non-negative with respect to the ordinary t-structure. Therefore, the complex
\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S[1/p]_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)}(R_{S[1/p]}(X[1/p]),\rho^{!}j_{p}^{*}(M_{p^{n}}))
is
(-1)
-connected. Hence, the complex
\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}(M_{S}(X),M)
is also
(-1)
-connected. Hence, the motive
M
is
t_{\mathrm{ord}}
-non-negative.
∎

4.2.
The Ordinary Homotopy t-structure on Constructible Artin Motives and the t-exactness of the
\ell
-adic Realization

We begin this section with the case of smooth Artin motives when the residue characteristic exponents of the base scheme are invertible in the ring of coefficients.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let

be a regular good ring and let

be a regular scheme. Assume that the residue characteristic exponents of

are invertible in

.

Then, the functor

of

induces a fully faithful t-exact functor

Proof.

In the proof of

\rho_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)

Therefore, it suffices to show that the exact functor

is left t-exact. Let

be an Ind-lisse étale sheaf and let

be an étale

, we have

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S;R)}(M_{S}(X),\rho_{!}N)=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)}(R_{S}(X),N).

Hence, if the sheaf

is t-non-negative, by
Proposition

1.1.4
, so is the motive
\rho_{!}N
.
Therefore, the functor

\rho_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)

By

3.3.1
, the functor
\rho_{!}
is fully faithful with essential image the stable subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
induces a t-structure on the stable subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)
.

Finally, since the ordinary t-structure on the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}(S_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)
induces by
Proposition

3.1.6
a t-structure on the stable subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(S,R)
, the ordinary homotopy t-structure on the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,R)
by

3.3.1
.
∎

We want to show that the ordinary homotopy t-structure induces a t-structure on constructible Artin motives and to study the properties of the induced t-structure. In the case of Artin motives with rational coefficients, this was done in
[
Pep19a
]
. We will use
Proposition

4.1.6
to extend Pepin Lehalleur’s result to arbitrary regular good rings. The first step is to extend Pepin Lehalleur’s result on the t-exactness of the functor
f^{*}
for any morphism of schemes
f
to a result with intgeral coefficients. To that end, we need to introduce the following auxiliary notion.

Definition 4.2.2
.

Let

be a scheme and let

be a ring. An Artin étale motive

over

with coefficients in

is

-constructible

of

, there is a proper alteration

be an excellent scheme allowing resolution of singularities by alterations and let

Assume first that the ring

But by
[

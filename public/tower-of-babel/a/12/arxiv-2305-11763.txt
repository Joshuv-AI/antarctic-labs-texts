5
Open Questions

In this paper we have determined, among graphs with maximum degree at most
D

maximum order of a clique in
G^{2}

G
is
2-degenerate and (b) when
\textrm{mad}(G)<2\times 2
.
Recall that
f(D)
and
g(D)
are the largest possible sizes of a clique in
G^{2}
when

G
has maximum degree at most
D
and satisfies (a) and (b), respectively.
It is natural to ask when
g(D)=f(D)
.

Question 1
.

For which positive integers
D
does
g(D)=f(D)
?

We can ask analogous questions for larger values of degeneracy
or maximum average degree.

Question 2
.

For each positive integer

\alpha_{k}
†
†
margin:

\alpha_{k}
,
c_{k}

such that there exists a constant
c_{k}

such that if
G
is
k
-degenerate with maximum degree at most
D
, then

\omega(G^{2})\leqslant\alpha_{k}D+c_{k}

Recall that if
G
is
k
-degenerate with
\Delta(G)\leqslant D
, then
G^{2}
has

(2k-1)D-k^{2}
; this is witnessed by any order witnessing
that
G
is
k
-degenerate. Thus,
\alpha_{k}\leqslant 2k-1
.
By extending the ideas in Example
2
, we can also construct such
graphs
G
for which the degeneracy of

is equal to
(2k-1)D-k^{2}

3

However, we believe that
\alpha_{k}<2k-1
. In fact, it is interesting to ask

\lim_{k\to\infty}(2k-1)-\alpha_{k}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}(2k-1)/\alpha_{k}

We suspect the first limit is infinite, but do not have a conjectured value for
the second limit.

Now we consider the larger class of graphs with

(and

Decomposition of linear tensor transformations
Claudio Turchetti

Abstract
One of the main issues in computing a tensor decomposition is how to choose the number of rank-one components, since there is no finite algorithms for determining the rank of a tensor. A commonly used approach for this purpose is to find a low-dimensional subspace by solving an optimization problem and assuming the number of components is fixed. However, even though this algorithm is efficient and easy to implement, it often converges to poor local minima and suffers from outliers and noise. The aim of this paper is to develop a mathematical framework for exact tensor decomposition that is able to represent a tensor as the sum of a finite number of low-rank tensors. In the paper three different problems will be carried out to derive: i) the decomposition of a non-negative self-adjoint tensor operator; ii) the decomposition of a linear tensor transformation; iii) the decomposition of a generic tensor.

\left\langle\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}},\mathchoice{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}\right\rangle=\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{i}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j}}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{k,i}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{i}}(\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{k,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j}})=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textbf{k}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textbf{k}}\geq 0

(31)

where
\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textbf{k}}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{k,i}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{i}}
,
so that from (
26
) it results
\lambda\geq 0
. The operator
\mathchoice{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}_{\textbf{i,j}}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,k}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{j,k}}
satisfies this property as well. In particular for linear indices
\textbf{i}=i
,
\textbf{j}=j
,
\textbf{k}=k
, the operator reduces to the symmetric matrix

{G}_{i,j}={A}_{k,i}{A}_{k,j}=({A}^{T}_{i,k}){A}_{k,j}

(32)

which can be written as
G=A^{T}A
. Similarly, the operator

\mathchoice{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{G^{\prime}}}_{\textbf{i,j}}
reduces to the matrix
G^{T}=AA^{T}
. The matrix
G
represents the well known Gram-matrix, while matrix
G^{T}
its transpose.
Thus, tensor
\mathchoice{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{i,j}}
is the analog in tensor space of Gram-matrix
G
. Assuming
G
has size
(L\times L)
and rank
r
, i.e.
r=rank(G)
, it is well known from geometry that, since
G
is a symmetric non-negative definite matrix, it admits
L
eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i},\hskip 5.69046pti=1:L\}
such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}>{0}=\lambda_{r+1}=\dots=\lambda_{L}
. Thus
G
can be diagonalized as

G_{n,m}U_{m,p}=U_{n,q}\Lambda_{q,p},\hskip 5.69046ptq,p=1:r

(33)

where
U=(u_{1},\dots,u_{r})
is the matrix whose columns are the eigenvectors corresponding to non-zero eigenvalues in the matrix

\Lambda=diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
.
Using orthogonality of matrix
U
, i.e.

U_{m,p}U_{m,p^{\prime}}=\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}
and

\Lambda_{q,p}=\lambda_{p}\delta_{q,p}
, (
33
) becomes

G_{n,m}=\lambda_{p}U_{n,p}U_{m,p}.

(34)

This relationship is called
spectral decomposition
of matrix
G
and represents a fundamental result in matrix analysis, since both principal component analysis (PCA) and singular value decomposition (SVD) are based on this decomposition. Thus it is essential to ask whether a similar relationship can be derived for the class of (
SA-NND
) tensors.

3
Tensor decomposition

Having proven there is a correspondence between self-adjoint tensor operators and symmetric matrices, the aim of this section is to discover that a correspondence to the spectral decomposition
(
34
) exists in tensor space.
To take advantage of these considerations, tensor
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}
to be decomposed will be treated as a linear transformation from tensor space
\mathbb{R}^{J}
to tensor space
\mathbb{R}^{I}
, that is
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{R}^{J}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}^{I}}
, which for linear indices reduces to the matrix
A
. On the basis of this assumption, two cases will be considered first: i) the tensor

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times I}

is real self-adjoint operator and
ii) the tensor

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J}
, with
I\neq J
, is a linear transformation. Using these results, then the most general case of a generic tensor will be treated at the end of the section.

A. Decomposition of a self-adjoint non-negative tensor operator

The first case will be studied here deals with an order-
2d
tensor

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times I}
that represents a linear operator (or endomorphism)

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{R}^{I}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}^{I}}
. Thus, with reference to such a tensor, we state the following proposition.

Proposition1

Let

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times I},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{i}={(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{j}={(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})}

(35)

be a non-negative self-adjoint order-
2d
tensor operator with
{I=I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\ldots\times I_{d}}
, there exists a set of scalars
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}>{0}
, and a set of orthogonal tensors

\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r\}
, such that the following decomposition

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}=\lambda_{p}{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r

(36)

holds.

Proof

First, we want to prove that the following eigenvalue equation

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}=\lambda\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}},

(37)

where
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}
is a self-adjoint operator, admits solutions with
\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
and
\lambda>{0}
. Using index convention introduced in Section 2, Eq. (
37
) is equivalent to the
L
equations

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j}}=\lambda\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i}},\hskip 8.5359pt\textbf{i}\in{I},\hskip 5.69046ptL=dim(I)=I_{1}I_{2}\dots I_{d}

(38)

which can be written as

(\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}-\lambda\delta_{\textbf{i,j}})\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j}}=0,

(39)

where the term on the left

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Y}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\textbf{i}}=(\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}-\lambda\delta_{\textbf{i,j}})\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j}}
represents an order-d tensor. The solutions of (
39
) are independent on how the terms in the equation are arranged as entries in the tensor
\mathchoice{\mathcal{Y}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\textbf{i}}
, meaning that (
39
) is invariant to a one-to-one transformation of subscript vectors.
Assuming indices
\textbf{i},\textbf{j}
are obtained by the inverse vector-to-linear index transformation (
18
) then, substituting the vector indices

\textbf{i}(n)=T(n,:)
,

\textbf{j}(m)=T(m,:)
, (
39
) becomes

(\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i}(n),\textbf{j}(m)}-\lambda\delta_{\textbf{i}(n),\textbf{j}(m)})\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j}(m)}=0

(40)

By defining the
(L\times{L})
matrices

a_{n,m}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i}(n),\textbf{j}(m)},\hskip 5.69046pt\delta_{n,m}=\delta_{\textbf{i}(n),\textbf{j}(m)}

(41)

and the vector

u_{m}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j}(m)},

(42)

thus (
40
) can be re-arranged as

(a_{n,m}-\lambda\delta_{n,m})u_{m}=0

(43)

or equivalently

a_{n,m}u_{m}=\lambda{u_{n}},\hskip 5.69046ptm,n=1:L

(44)

Equation (
44
) is equivalent to

Au=\lambda{u}.

(45)

where the matrix
[A]_{n,m}=a_{n,m}
is a symmetric non-negative definite (
L\times{L}
) matrix. In fact, as
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}
is self-adjoint, then it results

a_{n,m}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i}(n),\textbf{j}(m)}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{j}(m),\textbf{i}(n)}=a_{m,n}

(46)

that proves matrix
a_{n,m}
is symmetric (or self-adjoint). In addition, since

\left\langle\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}},\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}\right\rangle\geq 0
for every tensor
\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}
, it results

0\leq\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i},\textbf{j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j}}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{i}(m)}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i}(m),\textbf{j}(n)}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j}(n)}=v_{m}{A_{m,n}}v_{n}

(47)

for every vector
[v]_{n}=v_{n}
, thus proving the matrix
A
is non-negative.

Assuming
r=rank(A)
, a set of scalars
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}>{0}
exist such that (
45
) holds, thus (
45
) can be rewritten as

A_{n,m}U_{m,p}=U_{n,q}\Lambda_{q,p}

(48)

where
U=(u_{1},\dots,u_{r})
and
\Lambda=diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
.
Using the inverse vector-to-linear index transformation (
16
) we have

U_{n(\textbf{i}),q}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},q},\hskip 8.5359ptA_{n(\textbf{i}),m(\textbf{j})}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}

(49)

and (
48
) becomes

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},q}\Lambda_{q,p},

(50)

or that is the same

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p}}=\lambda_{p}{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p}},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r

(51)

This proves that (
37
) admits solutions with
\lambda>0
. To decompose
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}
, the tensor product of (
51
) and
\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p^{\prime}}

yields

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p^{\prime}}=\lambda_{p}{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p^{\prime}},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r

(52)

and from orthogonality
{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p^{\prime}}=\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}
, it results

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}=\lambda_{l}{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p^{\prime}},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r

(53)

that is

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}=\lambda_{p}{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{j},p},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r

(54)

and this concludes the proof.

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J}
that represents a linear transformation
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{R}^{J}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}^{I}}

Proposition2

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{i}={(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{j}={(j_{1},\ldots,j_{e})}

(55)

where
{I=I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\ldots\times I_{d}}
and
{J=J_{1}\times J_{2}\times\ldots\times J_{e}}

\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p}\in\mathbb{R}^{I},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r\}

,
and a set of scalars

\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{r}>{0}
, such that the following decomposition

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}=\sigma_{p}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},p}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j},p}\hskip 5.69046pt,p=1:r.

Proof

\mathchoice{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{l,j}}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,l}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{J\times J},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{l}=(l_{1},\dots,l_{d})

(57)

\mathchoice{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{l,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j},p}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l},q}\Lambda_{q,p},\hskip 5.69046ptq,p=1:r

(58)

can be derived from (
36
), where

\Lambda_{q,p}=diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
,
and

\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j},p},\hskip 5.69046ptp=1:r\}

is a set of orthogonal tensors in
\mathbb{R}^{J}
.
Since
\lambda_{i}>0
we can pose
\Lambda=S^{2}
with
S_{q,p}=diag(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})
, then multiplying each side of (

\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l},q^{\prime}}

\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l},q^{\prime}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{l,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j},p}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l},q^{\prime}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l},q}S^{2}_{q,p}

and by orthogonality of
\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l},q}
,

,
we have

\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l},q}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,l}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j},p}=S^{2}_{q,p}

(62)

Thus

\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},q},\hskip 5.69046ptq=1:r\}

(63)

is a set of orthogonal tensors in
\mathbb{R}^{I}
. The inner product of two generic terms
\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},s}
and
\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},q}
gives

\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},s}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},q}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},s}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j},p}S^{-1}_{p,q}

and for orthogonality, that is

C. Decomposition of a generic tensor

The previous result can be generalized to an order-
(d+e+f)
tensor
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,l,k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J\times K}

Proposition3

Given

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J\times K},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{i}={(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{j}={(j_{1},\ldots,j_{e})},\hskip 5.69046pt\textbf{k}={(k_{1},\ldots,k_{f})}

(67)

where
{I=I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\ldots\times I_{d}}
,
{J=J_{1}\times J_{2}\times\ldots\times J_{e}}
and
{K=K_{1}\times K_{2}\times\ldots\times K_{f}}
, there exist sets of orthogonal tensors

\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},m}\in\mathbb{R}^{I},\hskip 5.69046ptm=1:M\}
,

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textbf{j},m}\in\mathbb{R}^{J},\hskip 5.69046ptm=1:M\}
,

\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}_{\textbf{k},m}\in\mathbb{R}^{K},\hskip 5.69046ptm=1:M\}
, and a set of scalars

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{M}>{0}
with
M=r_{1}r_{2}
,
such that
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}
can be decomposed as

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}=\lambda_{m}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},m}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textbf{j},m}\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}_{\textbf{k},m},\hskip 5.69046ptm=1:M.

(68)

Proof

The product
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{l,j,k}}

is a self-adjoint operator on
\mathbb{R}^{I}
, thus the eigenvalue equation

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{l,j,k}}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{l},p}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},q}S^{2}_{q,p},\hskip 5.69046ptq,p=1:r_{1}

(69)

admits a set of solutions

\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}=\{\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},q},\hskip 5.69046ptq=1:r_{1}\}

with
S_{q,p}=diag(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r_{1}})
,

\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{r_{1}}>{0}
.
Since the matrix
S_{q,p}
is diagonal full-rank, thus
S^{-1}_{q,p}
exists and multiplying by
\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},q}
(
69
) can be rewritten as

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},q}S^{-1}_{q,p}(\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{l,j,k}}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{l},p}S^{-1}_{q,p})=\delta_{p,q},

(70)

that represents the orthogonal condition for the tensor

\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},q}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{l,j,k}}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{l},p}S^{-1}_{p,q}\in{\mathbb{R}^{J\times K}}.

(71)

Due to orthogonality of
\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{l},p}
we have

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},q}S_{q,p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}

(72)

that represents the decomposition of linear transformation
\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}
from
{\mathbb{R}^{J\times K}}
to
{\mathbb{R}^{I}}
. (
72
) can be further decomposed since the product
\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\textbf{l,k},p}
is a self-adjoint operator on
\mathbb{R}^{J}
, thus the eigenvalue equation

\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{l,k},p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{l},r}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},s}\Lambda^{2}_{s,r},\hskip 5.69046ptr,s=1:r_{2}

(73)

admits a set of solutions
\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}=\{\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},s},\hskip 5.69046pts=1:r_{2}\}
with
\Lambda_{r,s}=diag(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{r_{2}})
,

\gamma_{1}\geq\gamma_{2}\geq\dots\geq\gamma_{r_{2}}>{0}
. The matrix

\Lambda^{-1}_{s,r}
exists and (
73
) can be rewritten as

\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},s}\Lambda^{-1}_{s,r}(\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{l,k},p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{l},r}\Lambda^{-1}_{r,s})=\delta_{r,s}

(74)

that represents the orthogonality condition for the tensor

\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}}_{\textbf{k},p,r}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},s}\Lambda^{-1}_{s,r}.

(75)

By deriving
\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}
we have

\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},s}\Lambda_{s,r}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}}_{\textbf{k},p,r}

(76)

that represents the decomposition of linear transformation
\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}}}_{\textbf{j,k},p}

from
{\mathbb{R}^{K}}
to
{\mathbb{R}^{J}}
. Finally combining (
72
) and (
76
) yields

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}=S_{q,p}\Lambda_{s,r}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},q}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},s}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}}_{\textbf{k},p,r},

(77)

and more concisely

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}=\sigma_{p}\gamma_{p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},p}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},r}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}}_{\textbf{k},p,r},

(78)

since
S_{q,p}=\sigma_{p}\delta_{q,p}
and
\Lambda_{s,r}=\gamma_{r}\delta_{s,r}
.
Now, let us derive the matrix
T
that establishes the transformation between the vector index
\textbf{u}=(p,r),p=1:r_{1},r=1:r_{2}
and the linear index
m=1:M
with
M=r_{1}{r_{2}}
, such that

(p,r)=\textbf{u}(m)=(u_{1}(m),u_{2}(m)).

(79)

Then (
78
) becomes

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}=\sigma_{u_{1}(m)}\gamma_{u_{2}(m)}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},u_{1}(m)}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},u_{2}(m)}\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}}_{\textbf{k},u_{1}(m),u_{2}(m)},

(80)

By defining

\lambda_{m}=\sigma_{u_{1}(m)}\gamma_{u_{2}(m)}

\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},m}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{\textbf{i},u_{1}(m)}

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textbf{j},m}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\textbf{j},u_{2}(m)}

\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}_{\textbf{k},m}=\hat{\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}}_{\textbf{k},u_{1}(m),u_{2}(m)},

(81)

from (
79
) we have

\mathchoice{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\textbf{i,j,k}}=\lambda_{m}\mathchoice{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{U}}_{\textbf{i},m}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textbf{j},m}\mathchoice{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}_{\textbf{k},m},

(82)

and this concludes the proof.

5
Conclusion

In this paper a mathematical framework for exact tensor decomposition, that is able to represent a tensor as the sum of a finite number of low-rank tensors, has been developed. The core of the proposed approach is the derivation of a decomposition for non-negative self-adjoint tensor operators. In particular it has been proven that a correspondence exists between the spectral decomposition of a symmetric matrix and the decomposition of a self-adjoint tensor operator.

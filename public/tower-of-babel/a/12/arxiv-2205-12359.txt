The γ \gamma -Signless Laplacian Adjacency Matrix of Mixed Graphs
Omar Alomari , Mohammad Abudayah , Manal Ghanem

Abstract
The α \alpha -Hermitian adjacency matrix H α H_{\alpha} of a mixed graph X X has been recently introduced. It is a generalization of the adjacency matrix of unoriented graphs. In this paper, we consider a special case of the complex number α \alpha . This enables us to define an incidence matrix of mixed graphs. Consequently, we define a generalization of line graphs as well as a generalization of the signless Laplacian adjacency matrix of graphs. We then study the spectral properties of the signless Laplacian adjacency matrix of a mixed graph. Lastly, we characterize when the signless Laplacian adjacency matrix of a mixed graph is singular and give lower and upper bounds of number of arcs and digons in terms of largest and lowest eigenvalue of the signless Laplacian adjacency matrix.

Mixed Graphs; Signless Adjacency Matrix; Hermitian Adjacency Matrix; Line Graphs; Bipartite Graphs

Introduction

A mixed graph is a triple
(V(X),E(X),\vec{E}(X))
, where
V(X)
is the set of vertices,
E(X)
is the set of unoriented edges (digons) and
\vec{E}(X)
is the set of oriented edges (arcs). Here, one can consider mixed graphs as digraphs, where both ways oriented edges considered as digons. Throughout this paper, an arc from the vertex
u
to the vertex
v
in
\vec{E}
will be denoted by
\vec{uv}
. A digon between the verteces
u
and
v
will be denoted by
uv
. A graph that is obtained from a mixed graph by considering all arcs as digons is called the underlying graph of
X
and will be denoted by
\Gamma(X)
. The degree of a vertex
u
in
X
, denoted by
deg(u)
, is defined to be the degree of
u
in
\Gamma(X)
. By a path (resp. a cycle, a walk) in a mixed graph, we mean a path (resp. a cycle, a walk) in
\Gamma(X)
. For an unoriented graph
(V(G),E(G))
,
L_{G}
denotes the line graph of
G
.

For an unoriented graph
G
, the adjacency matrix of
G
, denoted by

, is the square symmetric
(0,1)
-matrix whose rows and columns correspond to the vertices of
G
and
a_{ij}=1
if the vertices
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
are adjacent. The Laplacian (resp. signless Laplacian) adjacency matrix of a graph

is the square matrix

(resp.
Q(G)=D(G)+A(G)
) where
D(G)=diag(deg(u_{i}):u_{i}\in V(G))
. The incidence matrix

of an undirected graph

is the
(0,1)
-matrix where its rows correspond to the vertices of
G

G
and the entries
b_{ij}
equal
1
if and only if the vertex
u_{i}
and the edge
e_{j}
are incident. The adjacency matrix of a digraph
D
, denoted by
A(D)=(a_{ij})
, is the same as the adjacency matrix of a graph with
a_{ij}=1
if there is an arc from the vertex
u_{i}
to the vertex
u_{j}
. Which means, the adjacency matrix of a directed graph
D
is symmetric if and only if all arcs of
D
are both ways oriented arcs.

3
The
\gamma
-Signless Laplacian Adjacency Matrix

Let
X
be a mixed graph and
Q_{\gamma}
be its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then, the characteristic polynomial of the
\gamma
-signless Laplacian (resp. Hermitian) adjacency matrix of
X
will be denoted by
\chi_{Q}(X,\lambda)
(resp.
\chi_{H}(X,\mu)
). The eigenvalues of the matrix
Q_{\gamma}
(resp.
H_{\gamma}
) are called the
Q
-eigenvalues (resp.
H
-eigenvalues) of
X
. The
Q
-eigenvalues and
H
-eigenvalues (which are all real) of the mixed graph
X
will be denoted by
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
and
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{n}
respectively.

Now, since
Q_{\gamma}=B_{\gamma}B_{\gamma}^{*}
is positive semidefinite, all
Q
-eigenvalues of
X
should be non-negative. Also, since the non-zero eigenvalues of
B_{\gamma}B_{\gamma}^{*}
and
B_{\gamma}^{*}B_{\gamma}
are the same, using Equations
1
and
2
, one can immediately obtain the following:

\chi_{H}(AL_{X},\lambda)=(\lambda+2)^{m-n}\chi_{Q}(X,\lambda+2),

(3)

where,
m=|E(X)\cup\vec{E}(X)|
and
n=|V(X)|
.

Since the diagonal entries of the
\gamma-
signless Laplacian adjacency matrix of a mixed graph
X
are the degrees of the vertices of
X
, we get
tr(Q_{\gamma})=2m
, where
m
is the number of arcs and digons in
X
. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=2m.

Theorem 2

Let
X
be a mixed graph with
|V(X)|=n
,
|\vec{E}(X)\cup E(X)|=m
and
Q_{\gamma}
be its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}=2m+\sum_{uv\in\vec{E}(X)\cup E(X)}(deg(u)+deg(v)).

Proof

Observing that,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{2}=tr(Q_{\gamma}^{2})
we get,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=tr\left((B_{\gamma}^{*}B_{\gamma})^{2}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle=tr\left((2I+H_{\gamma}(AL_{X}))^{2}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle=4m+\sum_{e\in V(AL_{X})}deg_{AL_{X}}(e)

(6)

\displaystyle=4m+\sum_{uv\in\vec{E}(X)\cup E(X)}\left(deg(u)+deg(v)-2\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=2m+\sum_{uv\in\vec{E}(X)\cup E(X)}\left(deg(u)+deg(v)\right),

(8)

where
deg_{AL_{X}}(e)
is the degree of the vertex
e
in
AL_{X}
.

For a connected graph
G
, it is well known that the signless Laplacian adjacency matrix is singular if and only if
G
is a bipartite graph
[
7
]
. The following is a generalization of this theorem.

Theorem 3

Let
X
be a mixed graph and
Q_{\gamma}
be its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then,
Q_{\gamma}
is singular if and only if
X
is a
\gamma
-monostore graph and
\Gamma(X)
is bipartite.

Proof

Suppose that
x=[x_{u}]_{u\in V(X)}
be a non-zero vector, with
Q_{\gamma}x=0
. Then,
B_{\gamma}^{*}x=0
and thus, for every arc
\vec{uv}
or digon
uv
in
X
we have,
x_{v}=-x_{u}h_{uv}
. Now, let
C=u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{k-1}u_{1}
be a cycle in
X
. Then,

\displaystyle x_{u_{1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}h_{u_{1}u_{2}}h_{u_{2}u_{3}}\dots h_{u_{k-1}u_{1}}x_{u_{1}}

(9)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}h_{\gamma}(C)x_{u_{1}}.

(10)

Observing that
X
is weakly connected graph,
x_{u_{1}}\neq 0
. Therefore,

1=(-1)^{k}h_{\gamma}(C).

The fact that this equation is true if and only if
h_{\gamma}(C)=1
and
k
is even concludes the proof.

For a mixed graph
X
it has been proven that spectral radius of the
\gamma
-Hermitian adjacency matrix
H_{\gamma}
of
X
is less than the maximum degree of the vertices of
X
. To be more formal,

\max_{i}|\mu_{i}|\leq\max_{u\in V(X)}\{deg(u)\}.

(11)

An immediate consequence of this result is that, the largest
Q
-eigenvalue of
X
should be less than twice the maximum degree of the vertices of
X
. The following theorem gives a sharper upper bound of the largest
Q
-eigenvalues of
X
.

Theorem 4

Let
X
be a mixed graph and
Q_{\gamma}
be its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then,

\displaystyle\max_{u\in V(X)}\left\{deg(u)\right\}\leq\lambda_{1}(X)\leq\displaystyle\max_{uv\in\vec{E}(X)\cup E(X)}\left\{deg(u)+deg(v)\right\}.

Proof

Observing that the non-zero eigenvalues of
B_{\gamma}B_{\gamma}^{*}
and
B_{\gamma}^{*}B_{\gamma}
are the same,
\lambda_{1}(X)=\mu_{1}(AL_{X})+2
. Using inequality
11
we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\max_{e\in V(AL_{X})}\left\{deg(e)\right\}

(12)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\max_{uv\in\vec{E}(X)\cup E(X)}\left\{deg(u)+deg(v)-2\right\}.

(13)

Therefore,
\lambda_{1}\leq\displaystyle\max_{uv\in\vec{E}(X)\cup E(X)}\left\{deg(u)+deg(v)\right\}
.

For the other side, observing that for every vector
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
||x||=1
we get
x^{*}Q_{\gamma}x\leq\lambda_{1}
. Therefore, for every vertex
u
of
X
we have
deg(u)\leq\lambda_{1}
.

Now, let
M
be a Hermitian matrix. Then, the spectral theorem says that
M
has a set of orthonormal basis
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of its eigenvectors. Which means that there is a unitary matrix
U
such that

U^{*}MU=diag\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}.

Therefore,

M=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{i},

(14)

where
P_{i}=x_{i}x_{i}^{*}
. It is obvious here that the matrix
P_{i}
is a Hermitian idempotent matrix, that is
P_{i}=P_{i}^{*}=P_{i}^{2}
. Moreover, since
UU^{*}=I
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}=I
. Which means,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(P_{i})_{rs}=0,\hskip 25.0pt\text{ if $r\neq s$}

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(P_{i})_{rs}=1,\hskip 25.0pt\text{ if $r=s$}

(16)

Also, since
\sum_{i,j}\overline{x_{i}}x_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{x_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}
and
\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2}|x_{j}|^{2}=|x_{j}|^{2}(1-|x_{j}|^{2})
, we can easily observe the following theorem:

Theorem 5

For any vector
x=[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]^{T}
, if
x^{*}x=1
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\overline{x_{i}}x_{j}\geq 0

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2}|x_{j}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}

(18)

Now, we are ready to give some upper and lower bound of the extreme eigenvalues of the
\gamma
-signless Laplacian adjacency matrix of mixed graphs.

Theorem 6

Let
X
be a mixed graph,
n=|V(X)|
,
m=|E(\Gamma(X))|
and
Q_{\gamma}
its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then,

\frac{n}{2}\lambda_{n}+1\leq m\leq\frac{n}{2}\lambda_{1}-1.

(19)

Proof

Using Equation
14
and Equation
15
we have:

Q_{\gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{i}\hskip 15.0pt\text{ and for }r\neq s,\hskip 15.0pt\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(P_{i})_{rs}=0.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
X
has a digon
uv
. Then,

1=(Q_{\gamma})_{uv}=\overline{(Q_{\gamma})_{uv}}.
Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}Re((P_{i})_{uv})=1,

Setting

c_{i}=\frac{1\pm 2Re((P_{i})_{uv})}{n},

(20)

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{tr(Q_{\gamma})}{n}\pm\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{n}Re((P_{i})_{uv})\lambda_{i}}{n}

(21)

\displaystyle=\frac{2m}{n}\pm\frac{2}{n}.

(22)

On the other hand, using Equation
16
, for
r\in V(X)
we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(P_{i})_{rr}=

(23)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i})_{r}\overline{(x_{i})_{r}}

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(x_{i})_{r}|^{2}.

(25)

Therefore, using Theorem
5
we have,

\sum_{r,s\in V(X),r\neq s}|(x_{i})_{r}|^{2}|(x_{i})_{s}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

And thus,
|(P_{i})_{rs}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Which means,

-\frac{1}{2}\leq Re((P_{i})_{rs})\leq\frac{1}{2}.

So,

0\leq c_{i}\leq\frac{2}{n}

Now, it can be easily seen that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=1
. Therefore,

\lambda_{n}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{1}.

Finally, the fact that
tr(Q_{\gamma})=2m
ends the proof.

Corollary 1

Let
X
be a mixed graph,
n=|V(X)|
and
Q_{\gamma}
its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then,

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}\geq\frac{4}{n}.

Obviously, the lower bound of spread of
Q_{\gamma}
in Inequality

Theorem 7

Let
X
be a mixed graph,
n=|V(X)|
and
Q_{\gamma}
its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then,

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}\geq 2.

(27)

Proof

Let
uv
be any arc or digon in
X
. Then

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=|\left(Q_{\gamma}\right)_{uv}|

(28)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(P_{i})_{uv}\right|.

(29)

Observing that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(P_{i})_{uv}=0
, we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n}}{2}(P_{i})_{uv}\right|

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|(\lambda_{i}-\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n}}{2}\right|\left|(P_{i})_{uv}\right|

(31)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|(P_{i})_{uv}\right|.

(32)

Now, since
P_{i}
is positive semidefinite we have,

\left|(P_{i})_{uv}\right|\leq\sqrt{(P_{i})_{uu}(P_{i})_{vv}}\leq\frac{(P_{i})_{uu}+(P_{i})_{vv}}{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|(P_{i})_{uv}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(P_{i})_{uu}+(P_{i})_{vv}}{2}

(33)

\displaystyle=1.

(34)

Which ends the proof.

The following is a sharper inequality for the right hand side of Theorem
6
.

Theorem 8

Let
X
be a mixed graph,
k=|\vec{E}(X)|
,
l=|E(X)|
and
Q_{\gamma}
be its signless Laplacian adjacency matrix. Then,

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{4l+k}{n}.

Proof

First, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V(X)}(Q_{\gamma})_{uv}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V(X)}deg(u)+\sum_{u,v\in V(X),u\neq v}(Q_{\gamma})_{uv}

(35)

\displaystyle=2(k+l)-k+2l

(36)

\displaystyle=4l+k

(37)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V(X)}(Q_{\gamma})_{uv}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in V(X)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(P_{i})_{uv}

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V(X)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(P_{i})_{uu}+\sum_{u,v\in V(X),u\neq v}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(P_{i})_{uv}

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sum_{u\in V(X)}(P_{i})_{uu}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sum_{u,v\in V(X),u\neq v}(P_{i})_{uv}.

(40)

But, using Equation
15
we have
\sum_{u,v\in V(X),u\neq v}(P_{i})_{uv}=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V(X)}(Q_{\gamma})_{uv}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sum_{u\in V(X)}(P_{i})_{uu}

(41)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{u\in V(X)}(P_{i})_{uu}

(42)

\displaystyle=n\lambda_{1}

(43)

Therefore,

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{4l+k}{n}.

One can easily check that the above inequality is sharp when

Spinorial description for Lorentzian 𝔥 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{hs} -IKKT
& Tung Tran

Abstract
We introduce a novel spinorial description for the higher-spin gauge theory induced by the IKKT matrix model on an FLRW spacetime with Lorentzian signature, called Lorentzian 𝔥 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{hs} -IKKT theory. The new description is based on Weyl spinors transforming under the space-like isometry subgroup S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) SL(2,\mathbb{C}) of the structure group S ​ O ​ ( 2 , 4 ) ≃ S ​ U ​ ( 2 , 2 ) SO(2,4)\simeq SU(2,2) . It allows us to exploit the full power of the spinor formalism in Lorentzian signature, in contrast to a previous formalism based on the compact subgroup S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L × S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) R SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R} of S ​ U ​ ( 2 , 2 ) SU(2,2) . Some cubic vertices of the Yang-Mills sector and the corresponding scattering amplitudes are computed. We observe that the n n -point (for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 ) tree-level amplitudes are typically non-trivial on-shell, but exponentially suppressed in the late-time regime. While Lorentz invariance of the higher-spin amplitudes is not manifest, it is expected to be restored by higher-spin gauge invariance.

. There are two specific sectors that we will focus on in Lorentzian
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT theory:

\bullet

Would-be-massive sector.
Assuming factorization of spinors, i.e.

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha(2s)}=\zeta_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\zeta_{\alpha_{s}}

(5.1)

etc., we propose the following ansatz for the positive and negative helicity polarization tensors associated with the would-be-massive external higher-spin states:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{i+}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\zeta_{\alpha(2s-1)}\tilde{\kappa}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}}}{\langle\kappa_{i}\,\zeta\rangle^{2s-1}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\epsilon^{i-}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{i}_{\alpha(2s-1)}\,\tilde{\zeta}_{\dot{\alpha}}}{[\kappa_{i}\,\zeta]}\,,

(5.2)

where
(\zeta,\tilde{\zeta})
are auxiliary spinors and
(\kappa,\tilde{\kappa})
may be referred to as principal spinors. A priori,
\kappa_{i}^{\alpha}\rho_{i\alpha}\neq 0
and
\tilde{\kappa}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\tilde{\rho}_{i\dot{\alpha}}\neq 0
due to the fact that higher-spin fields in Lorentzian
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT theory carry more dof. compared to conventional higher-spin theories. Nevertheless, the gauge-fixing condition (
4.11
) on twistor space descends to

\displaystyle p^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon^{\pm}_{\beta(2s-2)\alpha\,\dot{\alpha}}=0\,

(5.3)

on spacetime, which should hold for all physical modes. This implies

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha}p^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\tilde{\kappa}_{\dot{\alpha}}=0=\kappa_{\alpha}p^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\tilde{\zeta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(5.4)

or equivalently
\rho_{i}^{\alpha}\simeq\kappa_{i}^{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\simeq\tilde{\kappa}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}
since
(\zeta,\tilde{\zeta})
are auxiliary spinors. As a result, the polarization tensors (
5.2
) reduce to

\displaystyle\epsilon^{i+}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\zeta_{\alpha(2s-1)}\tilde{\rho}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}}}{\langle i\,\zeta\rangle^{2s-1}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\epsilon^{i-}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho^{i}_{\alpha(2s-1)}\,\tilde{\zeta}_{\dot{\alpha}}}{[i\,\tilde{\zeta}]}\,.

(5.5)

It is easy to check that the above representatives of the polarization tensors obey the normalization

\epsilon^{+}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon_{-}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}=-1\,.

(5.6)

Furthermore, for the ansatz (
5.5
), we can obtain the standard eom. for free positive/negative helicity massless higher-spin fields as
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}^{+}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(5.7a)

\displaystyle\partial^{\beta\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{(\beta}{}^{\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}^{-}_{\alpha(2s-1))\,\dot{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(5.7b)

This can be verified directly by plugging in the ansatz (
5.5
).
Note, however, that after imposing (
5.3
), the diagonalized higher-spin fields
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}
have

\displaystyle 4s-(2s-1)=2s+1

(5.8)

degrees of freedom, which correspond to the would-be-massive modes. We thus recover the correct dof. for a massive higher-spin field in four dimension.

\bullet

Massless sector.
To reduce the degrees of freedom for the massless fields
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
to two, we can impose in addition the space-like or unitary gauge conditions:

\displaystyle y^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon^{\pm}_{\beta(2s-2)\alpha\,\dot{\alpha}}=0\,,

(5.9)

on the polarization tensors
\epsilon_{\alpha(2s_{i}-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}^{\pm}
. This space-like gauge conditions reduce further
(2s-1)
dof. from
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
, leaving us with
2
dof of a massless higher-spin gauge field in four dimensions. Thus, after imposing (
5.9
),
\mathbb{A}^{+}
and
\mathbb{A}^{-}
each carries 1 dof. corresponding to two helicities of a massless higher-spin field.

It is crucial to note that in this massless sector, the auxiliary spinors
(\zeta,\tilde{\zeta})
should drop out in any scattering amplitude expressions in the massless sector, as in the standard formulation of massless gauge fields. Below, we show that this is indeed the case. In particular, we can simplify the space-like gauge conditions to:

\displaystyle y^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\zeta_{\alpha}\tilde{\rho}_{i\dot{\alpha}}=0=y^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\rho_{i\alpha}\tilde{\zeta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(5.10)

This will help us eliminate some explicit Lorentz-violating structures of the 3-point scattering amplitudes in the massless sector.

Propagator.

Recall that the kinetic action of the Yang-Mills sector after rescaling is

S_{2}=\sum_{s}\frac{1}{s+1}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}y\,\mathbb{A}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}_{-}\Box\mathbb{A}^{+}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pts\geq 1\,.

(5.11)

Here, the fields comprise all would-be massive as well as massless modes.
Thus, up to normalization, the propagator between positive and negative helicity fields reads

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{A}^{+}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}(p)\mathbb{A}_{-}^{\beta(2s^{\prime}-1)\,\dot{\beta}}(p^{\prime})\rangle\sim\delta^{4}(p+p^{\prime})\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{\delta_{(\alpha_{1}}{}^{(\beta_{1}}\ldots\delta_{\alpha_{2s-1})}{}^{\beta_{2s^{\prime}-1})}\delta_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}}{p^{2}}\,,

(5.12)

where
\delta_{x,y}
’s denote the Kronecker deltas. It is natural to view the
\mathbb{A}_{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}
as chiral fields
[
49
]
, because they have more un-dotted than dotted spinorial indices.

Comment on
n

Computing higher-point amplitudes in the Yang-Mills sector is a non-trivial task. In particular, while some exchange channels vanish at the level of vertices

6
Discussion

In this work, we establish a global spinorial description for Lorentzian
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT theory using Weyl spinors, which transform in the fundamental of the non-compact space-like isometry group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})\simeq SO(1,3)
. We obtain the spacetime action of the
\mathfrak{hs}
-valued Yang-Mills sector in
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT theory in spinorial form, and study some tree-level 3-point higher-spin scattering amplitudes. Remarkably, they can be non-trivial on-shell due to mild Lorentz violation, but are strongly suppressed by a time-dependent coupling
e^{-\frac{3}{2}\tau}
in the late-time and flat regime. These results are consistent with a related study of similar amplitudes in the vectorial formulation of the same model in
[
4
]
.

The technical tools presented in this work should allow in principle to extract all vertices in the remaining sectors and study higher-point tree-level amplitudes, and (eventually) also loop amplitudes of the Lorentzian
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT under consideration. However, a better organization for the spacetime vertices may be needed to simplify this task. In particular, this should allow to explore gravitational interactions between spin-2 fields in Lorentzian
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT and to compare them with standard GR, see also
[
58
]
in a similar context as ours. However, this requires including one-loop amplitudes, since the Einstein-Hilbert action only arises at one loop. That task is postponed to future work.

Given that Lorentz
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT can have non-trivial scatterings from the would-be-massive higher-spin modes, it would be interesting to
see whether these modes can acquire masses from quantum corrections. Intriguingly, we observe that some 3-point amplitudes in the massless sector of the model do not vanish due to Lorentz-violating structures encoded in the Poisson structure
\theta^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}\beta\dot{\beta}}
cf. (
4.20
). Nevertheless, local Lorentz invariance is expected to be recovered from the
\mathfrak{hs}
gauge symmetry in this model, as observed in
[
4
]
.

Let

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathtt{K}\,\frac{\bar{\mu}_{\alpha(s)}\lambda^{\beta(s)}}{\langle\lambda\,\bar{\mu}\rangle^{s}}=-\frac{2\pi}{s+1}\delta_{(\alpha_{1}}{}^{\beta_{1}}\ldots\delta_{\alpha_{s})}{}^{\beta_{s}}\,.

Proof.

The tensor structure on the rhs follows from

∎

Note that (

B.1

Recall that the kinetic action in the local

On non-abelian dp-minimal groups
Atticus Stonestrom (Email: atticusstonestrom@yahoo.com )

Abstract




Abstract:
Let
G
be a dp-minimal group; we prove some consequences of several different hypotheses on
G
. First, if
G
is torsion-free, then it is abelian. Second, if
G
admits a distal f-generic type, then it is virtually nilpotent; we prove this by equipping the quotient of
G
by its FC-center in this case with a valued group structure. Finally, if
G
has the uniform chain condition, for example if
G
is stable, then
G
is virtually solvable.

Introduction

‘Dp-minimality’, first introduced in
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
and then later isolated as a notion in
[
18
]
, is a kind of abstract ‘one-dimensionality’ condition which has proved a very fruitful strengthening of the NIP, both from the perspective of ‘pure’ model theory and from the perspective of its algebraic implications. Here we are concerned with dp-minimal groups. The typical examples, including for instance superstable groups of
U
-rank
1
,
(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^{\omega}
, and the multiplicative and additive groups of
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q}_{p},
and ACVFs, are all virtually abelian, but there exist dp-minimal groups which are not virtually abelian
[
29
]
, and it is an open question, asked for example in
[
4
]
, whether every dp-minimal group is virtually nilpotent. This is known to be the case under several stronger hypotheses. For instance, C-minimal valued groups are virtually nilpotent
[
30
]
,
\omega
-categorical dp-minimal groups are virtually abelian
[
7
]
, profinite dp-minimal groups are virtually abelian
[
6
]
, and left-ordered dp-minimal groups are abelian
[
8
]
.

In this paper we investigate this question under several other hypotheses. First we show that any torsion-free dp-minimal group is abelian; this is the content of Section 4. We then show that any dp-minimal group
G
which admits a distal f-generic type is virtually nilpotent. This is deduced from a more general analysis, in which we construct a valued group structure on the quotient of
G
by its FC-center in such a way that the structure inherited by
G^{00}/Z(G^{00})
is ‘plain’ (see Section 2.1.4); this is the content of Section 5. Finally, we show that any dp-minimal group with the ‘uniform chain condition’, ie in which there is no infinite descending chain of uniformly definable subgroups, is virtually solvable

2.1
Groups

Let
(H,\cdot)
be a group.

2.1.1
Radicals

Recall that the Fitting subgroup of
H
is the subgroup
F(H)\trianglelefteqslant H
generated by all nilpotent normal subgroups of
H
, and that the solvable radical of
H
is the subgroup
R(H)\trianglelefteqslant H
generated by all solvable normal subgroups of
H
. The following can be found for instance in
[
12
]
:

Fact 2.1
.

If
F(H)
is nilpotent, then it is a definable subgroup of
(H,\cdot)
. If
R(H)
is solvable, then it is a definable subgroup of
(H,\cdot)
.

We will freely use the following throughout the paper.

Remark 2.2
.

If
H
is virtually nilpotent (resp. virtually solvable), then the Fitting subgroup (resp. solvable radical) of
H
is nilpotent (resp. solvable). Thus, by Fact
2.1
, a definable group is virtually nilpotent (resp. virtually solvable) iff it has a definable finite-index nilpotent (resp. solvable) subgroup.

2.1.2
Commutators

Throughout this paper we will use the non-standard notation that, for subsets
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\subseteq H
,
[A_{1},\dots,A_{n}]
denotes the set of commutators
\{[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]:a_{i}\in A_{i}\}
; when
A_{i}=\{a\}
is a singleton we will just write
[A_{1},\dots,A_{i-1},a,A_{i+1},\dots,A_{n}]
. The following is standard:

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\subseteq H
are each fixed setwise under conjugation by elements of
H
. Then, for any
k\in\{0\}\cup[n]
, the set of
g\in H
such that
[A_{1},\dots,A_{k},g,A_{k+1},\dots,A_{n}]=\{1\}
is a normal subgroup of
H
.

Proof.

Noting that
[A_{1},\dots,A_{k},g]=[g^{-1},[A_{1},\dots,A_{k}]^{g}]
, and that
[A_{1},\dots,A_{k}]
is fixed setwise under conjugation by elements of
H
, it suffices to show the claim when
k=0
, ie to show that the set of
g\in H
with
[g,A_{1},\dots,A_{n}]=\{1\}
is a normal subgroup of
H
. This follows by induction on
n
; in the case
n=1
the group is just
C_{H}(A_{1})
.

For the inductive step, let
U
denote the set of
u\in H
with
[u,A_{2},\dots,A_{n}]=\{1\}
and let
V
denote the set of
v\in H
with
[v,A_{1},\dots,A_{n}]=\{1\}
. Since
[v^{h},A_{1},\dots,A_{n}]=[v,A_{1},\dots,A_{n}]^{h}
,
V
is fixed setwise under conjugation by elements of
H
. Now, by the inductive hypothesis,
U
is a normal subgroup of
H
. If
g\in V
, then
[g,A_{1}]\subseteq U
, whence
[g^{-1},A_{1}]=[g,A_{1}]^{-g^{-1}}\subseteq U
, so that
g^{-1}\in V
. Finally, if
g,h\in V
, then
[g,A_{1}],[h,A_{1}]\subseteq U
, whence
[g,A_{1}]^{h}[h,A_{1}]\subseteq U
; since
[gh,A_{1}]\subseteq[g,A_{1}]^{h}[h,A_{1}]
we thus have
gh\in V
, as desired.
∎

2.1.3
Torsion-free groups

We will need some facts about torsion-free groups; see for example 5.2.19 and 14.5.9 in
[
21
]
.

Fact 2.4
.

Suppose
H
is nilpotent and torsion-free. Then
H/Z(H)
is torsion-free.

Fact 2.5
.

If
H
is torsion-free and
[H:Z(H)]
is finite, then
H
is abelian.

The following is an easy computation which we put here for convenience.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
H
is abelian-by-torsion and that
a\in H
is a non-torsion element. If
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that
a^{i}\sim a^{j}
, then
|i|=|j|
.

Proof.

Let
g\in H
be such that
(a^{i})^{g}=a^{j}
. For notational convenience, let
f:H\to H
denote the conjugation-by-
g
map. Since
H
is abelian-by-torsion, there is some
n>0
such that
f^{n}(a^{n})=a^{n}
. Let
b=a^{n}
; then
b
is still non-torsion, and we have
f(b)^{i}=f(a^{i})^{n}=a^{jn}=b^{j}
. By induction on
k
it follows that
f^{k}(b)^{i^{k}}=b^{j^{k}}
for all
k\geqslant 1
, since

f^{k+1}(b)^{i^{k+1}}=f(f^{k}(b)^{i^{k}})^{i}=f(b^{j^{k}})^{i}=(f(b)^{i})^{j^{k}}=b^{j^{k+1}}.

On the other hand, we have
b=f^{n}(b)
, and hence
b^{i^{n}}=f^{n}(b)^{i^{n}}=b^{j^{n}}
. Since
b
is non-torsion, thus
i^{n}=j^{n}
, so that indeed
|i|=|j|
.
∎

2.1.4
MC-groups

Recall that
H
is said to be an ‘MC-group’ if it satisfies the descending chain condition on centralizers, ie if the sequence of subgroups
C_{H}(a_{1})\supseteq C_{H}(a_{1},a_{2})\supseteq\dots
stabilizes for every sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
from
H
. We will use the following fact in Section 6; thank you to Frank Wagner for the reference to
[
3
]
.

Fact 2.7
.

Suppose
H
is a torsion MC-group. Then the solvable radical of
H
is solvable.

Proof.

The solvable radical is locally solvable, and (as a subgroup of
H
) it is still a torsion MC-group. The result thus follows from Theorem 2.2 in
[
3
]
.
∎

2.1.5
Valued groups

Let
(\mathcal{P},\leqslant)
be a totally ordered set with a greatest element. A
\mathcal{P}
-valued group structure on
H
is a a surjective map
v:H\to\mathcal{P}
satisfying the following:

1.

v(a)=\max\mathcal{P}
if and only if
a=1
.

2.

v(a^{g})<v(b^{g})
for all
g\in H
and
a,b\in H
with
v(a)<v(b)
.

3.

v(a)=v(a^{-1})
for all
a\in H

4.

v(ab)\geqslant\min\{v(a),v(b)\}
for all
a,b\in H
.

Following
[
30
]
, we will say that
(H,v)
is ‘plain’ if we additionally have
v([a,b])>\max\{v(a),v(b)\}
for all
a,b\in H\setminus\{1\}
. The following is proved in
[
30
]
:

Fact 2.8
.

Suppose
(H,v)
is a plain valued group of finite exponent. Then
H
is locally nilpotent.

2.2.1
NIP groups

In this section

There is a constant

Fact 2.10

Suppose

\phi(x,y)

B\subseteq\mathfrak{C}^{y}

\phi(x,b)
defines a subgroup of

C\subseteq B
such that
\bigcap_{c\in C}\phi(x,c)\subseteq\phi(x,b)

We will also make extensive use of Shelah’s theorem on the existence of
G^{00}

Fact 2.11

There is a partial type

, without parameters, such that

2.2.2
Dp-minimality

There are numerous equivalent characterizations of dp-minimality; see for example
[
9
]
for an introduction. A partial type
\pi(x)
over a small parameter set is said to be ‘dp-minimal’ if the following holds for some (any) parameters
B
over which
\pi
is defined: for any infinite sequences
I,J
mutually indiscernible over
B
, and any realization
a\models\pi(x)
, one of
I
and
J
is indiscernible over
(a,B)
. Then
\pi(x)
is not dp-minimal if and only if there are
L(\mathfrak{C})
-formulas
\phi(x,y)
and
\psi(x,z)
and tuples
b_{i}\in\mathfrak{C}^{y}
and
c_{j}\in\mathfrak{C}^{z}
such that, for all
i,j\in\omega
, the partial type

\pi(x)\wedge\phi(x,b_{i})\wedge\psi(x,c_{j})\wedge\bigwedge_{k\neq i}\neg\phi(x,b_{k})\wedge\bigwedge_{l\neq j}\neg\psi(x,c_{l})

is consistent. We say that the definable group
G
is dp-minimal if the formula
x\in G
is dp-minimal.

The following was noted in
[
27
]
for definable subgroups of dp-minimal groups, and the analogue is true for invariant subgroups:

Fact 2.14
.

Suppose
G
is dp-minimal, and that
A,B\leqslant G
are subgroups invariant over some small model
M
. Then
A\cap B
has bounded index in one of
A
and
B
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
A\cap B
has unbounded index in both
A
and
B
. Then there are sequences
I=(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
in
A
and
J=(b_{j})_{j\in\omega}
in
B
, each indiscernible over
M
, and such that
a_{i}B\cap a_{j}B=Ab_{i}\cap Ab_{j}=\varnothing
for all
i\neq j
. By Ramsey and
M
-invariance of
A,B
we may assume that
I
and
J
are mutually indiscernible over
M
, and then by dp-minimality one of
I
and
J
must be indiscernible over
(M,a_{0}b_{0})
. In the former case, there is an automorphism fixing
(M,a_{0}b_{0})
and taking
a_{0}
to
a_{1}
, and since
a_{0}^{-1}(a_{0}b_{0})\in B
it follows by invariance that
a_{1}^{-1}(a_{0}b_{0})\in B
. But then
a_{1}^{-1}a_{0}\in B
, a contradiction. A symmetric argument, using that
(a_{0}b_{0})b_{0}^{-1}\in A
, gives the result in the latter case.
∎

2.2.3
Invariant types in NIP theories

In this section
assume
only that
T
is NIP. We will need several facts about invariant types under this assumption. Recall from
[
23
]
that, if

is a global type, then

does not fork over

if and only if it is
M

The first is that of generically stable types, introduced in
[
22
]
, then investigated in
[
14
]
and in
[
32
]
(mostly with an NIP hypothesis) and in
[
19
]
(without an NIP hypothesis). A global type
p(x)\in S(\mathfrak{C})

B\subset\mathfrak{C}
over which it is invariant, we have: for any linear order
I
, any Morley sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in I}\models p^{\otimes I}|_{B}
, and any
L(\mathfrak{C})
-formula
\phi(x,c)
, the set
\{i:a_{i}\models\phi(x,c)\}
is either finite or co-finite in

. With the NIP hypothesis on

, the following conditions are all equivalent:

1.

p(x)
is generically stable.

2.

Any Morley sequence of
p(x)
is totally indiscernible.

3.

p(x)
is the limit type of some totally indiscernible sequence.

4.

p(x)
is definable over and finitely satisfiable in any small model over which it is invariant.

5.

For any global type
q(y)
invariant over some small set, we have
p(x)\otimes q(y)=q(y)\otimes p(x)
.

The following is proved in
[

]

Fact 2.18
.

Suppose
p(x)\in S(\mathfrak{C})
is
B
-invariant and generically stable. Then
p(x)|_{B}
is stationary, ie
p(x)
is the unique global extension of

that does not fork over
B
.

generic
; call this (
\ast
). By (
\ast
),
p
is generic, so
G
is generically stable in the sense of Section 6 of
[
14
]
, and by the results proved there the global generic types of
G
are precisely the translates of
p
. Again by (
\ast
), it follows that the f-generic types are precisely the translates of
p
, so the claim follows.
∎

3
Generic Nilpotence

In this section
assume
only that
G
is NIP.

It is well known that, in a stable group, to check virtual nilpotence of class at most
(c-1)
it suffices to check that an independent
c
-tuple of principal generics satisfies
[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]=1
; see eg
[
33
]
. We point out here an analogous fact in the NIP setting, which follows quickly from Fact
2.11
; compare also de Aldama’s proof of the existence of nilpotent envelopes in NIP groups
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose there is a left or right f-generic type
p(x)\in S_{G^{00}}(\mathfrak{C})
invariant over some small model and such that
p^{\otimes c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})\vdash[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]=1
. Then
G
is virtually nilpotent of class at most
(c-1)
.

Proof.

By induction on
c
. In the case
c=0
,
G
must be finite, so the result follows. Now suppose the result holds for
c
, and that
p(x)\in S_{G^{00}}(\mathfrak{C})
is an
M
-invariant f-generic type satisfying
p^{\otimes{(c+1)}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{c+1})\vdash[x_{1},\dots,x_{c+1}]=1
. Let
q(y)
be the pushforward of
p^{\otimes c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
under the map
G^{c}\to G
,
(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})\mapsto[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]
; then
p(x)\otimes q(y)\vdash[x,y]=1
.

If
b\models q|_{M}
and
a\models p_{(M,b)}
, then
a\in C_{G}(b)
; since
\operatorname{tp}(a/b)
is f-generic it follows that
C_{G}(b)
must have finite index in
G
. In particular,
G^{00}(x)\vdash x\in C_{G}(b)
, so by compactness there is a
\varnothing
-definable set
D\supseteq G^{00}
such that
b\in C_{G}(D)
. Since
D
is
\varnothing
-definable, thus
q(y)\vdash y\in C_{G}(D)
.

Now, let
H=C_{G}(C_{G}(D))
. Then
H\supseteq D\supseteq G^{00}
; thus
H
has finite index in
G
, so it suffices to show that
H
is virtually nilpotent of class at most
c
, and we moreover have
p(x)\vdash x\in H
and
q(y)\vdash y\in H
. Since we also have
q(y)\vdash y\in C_{G}(D)
, it follows that
q(y)\vdash y\in Z(H)
.

Let
\bar{p}
be the pushforward of
p
under the quotient map
H\to H/Z(H)
; this is well-defined since
p(x)\vdash x\in H
, and by the above we have
\bar{p}{}^{\otimes c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})\vdash[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]=1
. Since
p
is concentrated on
H^{00}
and the pullback of a type-definable subgroup of bounded index under a quotient map is type-definable and bounded index,
\bar{p}
is concentrated on
(H/Z(H))^{00}
. Moreover, the image of an f-generic set under a quotient map is still f-generic, so
\bar{p}
is f-generic. By the inductive hypothesis applied to the interpretable group
H/Z(H)
, it follows that
H/Z(H)
is virtually nilpotent of class at most
(c-1)
, and taking its pullback to
H
gives the result.
∎

4
Torsion-free case

In this section
assume
that
G
is dp-minimal. We will show that, if
G
has a global bi-f-generic type concentrated on
G^{00}
whose realizations are non-torsion, then
G
is virtually nilpotent of class at most
2
. Moreover, if
G
is torsion-free, then
G
is abelian.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
I=(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is a sequence in
G
such that (i)
a_{i}\not\sim a_{j}
for all
i\neq j
, and (ii)
[a_{i},a_{j}]=1
for all
i,j
. Then, for every sequence
J=(b_{j})_{j\in\omega}
in
G
, there is some
i\neq j
and
k\in\omega
with
[a_{k}^{b_{i}},a_{k}^{b_{j}}]=1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
J
is otherwise, so that
[a_{k}^{b_{i}},a_{k}^{b_{j}}]\neq 1
for all
i\neq j
and
k\in\omega
. By Ramsey and compactness, we may then assume that
I
and
J
are mutually indiscernible sequences. Now, let
n
be given Fact
2.9
. Pick
u_{i},v_{i}
so that
(u_{i})_{i\in[n]}+I+(v_{i})_{i\in[n]}
is still
J
-indiscernible and let
W=\{u_{i},v_{i}:i\in[n]\}
; then
I
and
J
are mutually indiscernible over
W
. Moreover,
D:=C_{G}(W)
is
W
-definable, and by condition (ii) and Fact
2.9
we have
a_{k}\in D\subseteq C_{G}(a_{k})
for all
k\in\omega
.

Now, by condition (i), we have
a_{i}\sim a_{0}^{b_{0}}
iff
i=0
, so
I
is not indiscernible over
a_{0}^{b_{0}}
and hence not over
(W,a_{0}^{b_{0}})
. Thus, by dp-minimality,
J
must be indiscernible over
(W,a_{0}^{b_{0}})
. Since
a_{0}\in D
, we have
b_{0}\models a_{0}^{b_{0}}\in D^{y}
, and thus also
a_{0}^{b_{0}}\in D^{b_{1}}
. But
D\subseteq C_{G}(a_{0})
, so that
a_{0}^{b_{0}}\in C_{G}(a_{0})^{b_{1}}=C_{G}(a_{0}^{b_{1}})
, ie
[a_{0}^{b_{0}},a_{0}^{b_{1}}]=1
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
a\in G(\mathfrak{C})
is non-torsion, and that
J=(g_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is an
a
-indiscernible sequence in
G
. Then
[a^{g_{0}},a^{g_{1}}]=1
.

Proof.

Let
s\subseteq\omega
be the set of all
k\in\omega
such that
[a^{kg_{0}},a^{kg_{1}}]\neq 1
; since
J
is
a
-indiscernible, note that, if
k\in s
, then
[a^{kg_{i}},a^{kg_{j}}]\neq 1
for all
i\neq j
. On the other hand, we have
[a^{i},a^{j}]=1
for all
i,j
, and by Lemma
2.6
and Fact
2.15
we have
a^{i}\not\sim a^{j}
for all
i\neq j\in\omega
, so it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
s
must be finite. On the other hand, letting
b=a^{g_{0}}
and
c=a^{g_{1}}
, we have
[b^{k},c^{k}]=1
for all
k\notin s
, and it follows that
[b,c]=1
. (For example, we may find four pairwise coprime numbers
p,q,r,s\in\omega\setminus s
. Then
b^{p}
and
b^{q}
each lie in
C_{G}(c^{pq})
, so
b\in C_{G}(c^{pq})
, and likewise
b\in C_{G}(c^{rs})
. Now
c^{pq}
and
c^{rs}
each lie in
C_{G}(b)
, so
c\in C_{G}(b)
, as needed.)
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
M
be a small model. Suppose that
p(x)\in S_{G}(\mathfrak{C})
is an
M
-invariant type whose realizations are non-torsion, and that
q(y)\in S_{G^{00}}(\mathfrak{C})
is an
M
-invariant right f-generic type. Then
q(y)\otimes p(x)
and
p(x)\otimes q(y)
each imply
[x,x^{y}]=1
.

Proof.

To see that
q(y)\otimes p(x)
implies
[x,x^{y}]=1
, let
a\models p|_{M}
and let
J=(g_{i})_{i\in\omega}\models q^{\otimes\omega}|_{(M,a)}
. Then
J
is
a
-indiscernible, so by Lemma
4.2

[a^{g_{0}},a^{g_{1}}]=1
and hence
[a,a^{g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}}]=1
. But
q
is right f-generic and concentrated on
G^{00}
, so
g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}\models q|_{(M,a)}
, and the result follows.

To see that
p(x)\otimes q(y)
implies
[x,x^{y}]=1
, let
J=(g_{i})_{i\in\omega}\models q^{\otimes\omega}|_{M}
and let
a\models p|_{(M,J)}
. Since
p
is
M
-invariant and
J
is
M
-indiscernible, also
J
is
(M,a)
-indiscernible. So again by Lemma
4.2
we have
[a,a^{g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}}]=1
. Also, again by right f-genericity,
g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}\models q|_{M}
, and now since
a\models q|_{(M,g_{1}g_{0}^{-1})}
the result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose there is an
M
-invariant bi-f-generic type concentrated on
G^{00}
whose realizations are non-torsion. Then
G
is virtually nilpotent of class at most
2
.

Proof.

Let
q(x)\in S_{G^{00}}(\mathfrak{C})
be bi-f-generic with non-torsion realizations, and let
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}+(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
be a Morley sequence of
q
over
M
. By Lemma
4.3
, we have
[a_{1},a_{1}^{b_{1}}]=1
and
[b_{1},b_{1}^{a_{1}}]=1
. In other words,
[a_{1},b_{1}]\in C_{G}(a_{1})\cap C_{G}(b_{1})
, so that
\langle a_{1},b_{1}\rangle
is nilpotent of class at most
2
. Now, by dp-minimality, one of
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
and
(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is indiscernible over
[a_{1},b_{1}]
. If
(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is, then, since
[a_{1},b_{1},b_{1}]=1
, we have
[a_{1},b_{1},b_{2}]=1
, so the result follows by Lemma
3.1
. So we may assume instead that
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is indiscernible over
[a_{1},b_{1}]
. Since
[a_{1},b_{1},a_{1}]=1
, thus also
[a_{1},b_{1},a_{2}]=1
; since
q
is bi-f-generic and concentrated on
G^{00}
, and
a_{2}\models q|_{(M,a_{1})}
, we have
a_{2}^{a_{1}}\models q|_{(M,a_{1})}
, and since
b_{1}\models q|_{(M,a_{1},a_{2})}
it follows that
[a_{1},b_{1},a_{2}^{a_{1}}]=1
.

Now, again by dp-minimality, one of
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
and
(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is indiscernible over
[b_{1},a_{1}^{b_{1}^{-1}}]
. If
(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is, then, since
[b_{1},a_{1}^{b_{1}^{-1}},b_{1}]=1
, also
[b_{1},a_{1}^{b_{1}^{-1}},b_{2}]=1
, so that
[b_{1},a_{1},b_{2}^{b_{1}}]=1
. Since
q
is bi-f-generic and concentrated on
G^{00}
, and
b_{2}\models q|_{(M,a_{1},b_{1})}
, we have
b_{2}^{b_{1}}\models q|_{(M,a_{1},b_{1})}
, so it follows that
[b_{1},a_{1},b_{2}]=1
. But then
[a_{1},b_{1},b_{2}]=1
and so again by Lemma
3.1
we are done. So we may assume that
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is indiscernible over
[b_{1},a_{1}^{b_{1}^{-1}}]
. Since
[b_{1},a_{1}^{b_{1}^{-1}},a_{1}]=1
, it follows that
[b_{1},a_{1}^{b_{1}^{-1}},a_{0}]=1
, and hence that
[b_{1},a_{1},a_{0}^{b_{1}}]=1
.

Altogether we have shown that, if the desired conclusion does not hold, then
[a,c,b^{a}]=1
and
[c,b,a^{c}]=1
for all
(a,b,c)\models q^{\otimes 3}|_{M}
. By the Hall-Witt identity, we have

[c,b,a^{c}][a,c,b^{a}][b,a,c^{b}]=1,

so it follows that
[b,a,c^{b}]=1
. By one final application of bi-f-genericity of
q
, we have
c^{b}\models q|_{(M,a,b)}
, so that
[b,a,c]=1
, whence
[a,b,c]=1
and the result follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

If
G
is torsion-free, then it is abelian.

Proof.

By Fact
2.16
and Fact
2.13
, let
q(x)\in S_{G^{00}}(\mathfrak{C})
be an
M
-invariant bi-f-generic type, and let
(a,b)\models q^{\otimes 2}|_{M}
. As in Lemma
4.4
, we have that
\langle a,b\rangle
is nilpotent of class at most
2
, so that
[a^{k},b]=[a,b]^{k}=[a,b^{k}]
for all
k\in\omega
. On the other hand, by Fact
2.15
, for some
n>0
, one of
[a^{n},b]=1
and
[a,b^{n}]=1
holds, whence
[a,b]^{n}=1
. Since
G
is torsion-free, thus
[a,b]=1
, and by Lemma
3.1
it follows that
G^{00}
is abelian.

Now, to show the desired result, it suffices by Fact
2.5
to show that
G^{00}\subseteq Z(G)
. Thus fix arbitrary
g\models G
with
g\neq 1
. Let
N
be any small model containing
(M,g)
, and let
c\models q|_{N}
. By Lemma
4.3
applied to the
N
-invariant type
\operatorname{tp}(g/N)
, we have
[g,g^{c}]=1
. On the other hand,
c\models G^{00}
, and
G^{00}
is a normal abelian subgroup of
G
, so also
[c,c^{g}]=1
. Thus
\langle c,g\rangle
is nilpotent of class at most
2
, and so again since
G
is torsion-free and by Fact
2.15
it follows that
[c,g]=1
. So
c\in C_{G}(g)
, and by f-genericity of
q
it follows that
C_{G}(g)
has finite-index in
G
, whence
G^{00}\subseteq C_{G}(g)
. Since
g
was arbitrary, indeed
G^{00}\subseteq Z(G)
, and the result follows.
∎

5
Distal case

In this section, we will study dp-minimal groups admitting a distal f-generic type; by Corollary
2.20
, these are precisely those dp-minimal groups with no generically stable f-generic type. Hence the class of such groups includes not only distal dp-minimal groups, ie dp-minimal groups in which every invariant type is distal, but also, for instance, dp-minimal
\mathcal{P}
-valued groups whenever
(\mathcal{P},\leqslant)
is a total order with no least element.

Many of the results we will prove here were proved for C-minimal valued groups in
[
30
]
. In particular, the results of Section 5.3 and Theorem
5.21
were both proved in the C-minimal case there. The main difference here is that we do not start with a valued group structure on the group.

Throughout this section,
assume
that
G
is a dp-minimal group admitting a distal f-generic type. By Corollary
2.20
, then every f-generic type of
G
is distal, and by Fact
2.13
and Fact
2.17
there is hence a bi-f-generic type
q(x)\in S_{G^{00}}(\mathfrak{C})
which is distal and invariant over any small model; fix this type
q
for the entire section.

5.1
Conjugacy classes are thin

Recall from
[
11
]
that a
\mathfrak{C}
-definable subset
X\subseteq G
with
1\in X=X^{-1}
is called ‘thick’ if, for any sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
indiscernible over the parameters defining
X
, we have
a_{i}^{-1}a_{j}\in X
for all
i,j\in\omega
. In this section we will show that, for any
u\in G\setminus\{1\}
, the set
G\setminus(u^{G}\cup u^{-G})
is thick.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
B\subset\mathfrak{C}
be a parameter set. Suppose
u,g\models G
are such that
\operatorname{tp}(u/B)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g/B)
and that
\operatorname{tp}(u/B)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u^{g}/B)
, and that there is some
n>0
with
[g^{n},u]=1
. If
[g,u]\equiv_{B}u
, then
u=1
.

Proof.

Suppose
[g,u]\equiv_{B}u
. We prove by induction on
k
that
[g^{k},u]\equiv_{B}u
for each
k>0
; taking
k=n
will then give the result. The base case
k=1
is by hypothesis. For the inductive step, assume that
[g^{k},u]\equiv_{B}u
. Then, since
\operatorname{tp}(u/B)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g/B)
, it follows that
[g^{k},u]^{g}\equiv_{B}u^{g}
. By the case
k=1
, we also have
[g,u]\equiv_{B}u
. Now since
\operatorname{tp}(u/B)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u^{g}/C)
, the
2
-tuple
([g,u],u^{g})
has the same type over
B
as
([g,u],[g^{k},u]^{g})
does. But we have
u^{g}\cdot[g,u]=u
, and so the result follows from the identity
[g^{k},u]^{g}[g,u]=[g^{k+1},u]
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M
be a small model, and suppose that
v\in G(M)
and that
u\in v^{G^{00}}
. If
J+g\models q^{\otimes(\omega+1)}|_{(M,u)}
, then
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g/M,J)
and
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u^{g}/M,J)
.

Proof.

That
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g/M,J)
follows immediately from distality of
q
. Now, let
h\models G^{00}
be such that
u=v^{h}
, and let
J^{\prime}+g^{\prime}\models q^{\otimes(\omega+1)}|_{(M,u,h)}
. We have
(u,J^{\prime},g^{\prime})\equiv_{M}(u,J,g)
, so it suffices to show that
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J^{\prime})\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u^{g^{\prime}}/M,J^{\prime})
. Since
h\models G^{00}
and
g^{\prime}\models q|_{(M,u,h,J^{\prime})}
, it follows by left f-genericity of
q
that
hg^{\prime}\models q|_{(M,u,h,J^{\prime})}
. Since
v\in G(M)
, we thus have in particular that
v^{g^{\prime}}\equiv_{(M,J^{\prime})}v^{hg^{\prime}}=u^{g^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, again since
v\in G(M)
, and since
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J^{\prime})\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g^{\prime}/M,J^{\prime})
, we have
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J^{\prime})\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(v^{g^{\prime}}/M,J^{\prime})
, so the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M
be a small model, and suppose that
I=(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is an
M
-indiscernible sequence such that, for some
v\in G(M)
, we have
a_{i}^{-1}a_{j}\in v^{G^{00}}
for all
i<j
. Then
I
is constant.

Proof.

By Ramsey and compactness, it suffices to show the result when
I
has order type
\mathbb{R}
, so replace
I
by a sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{R}}
with the same properties. Now, I claim that, for some
n>0
,
q(x)\vdash[x^{n},a_{i}^{-1}a_{j}]=1
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{R}
; call this (
\star
). If
q(x)\vdash x^{n}=1
for some
n>0
then the claim is clear. Otherwise the realizations of
q(x)
are non-torsion, whence by Lemma
4.4

G^{00}
is nilpotent of class at most
2
; in particular then
[b^{n},c]=[b,c^{n}]
for all
b,c\models G^{00}
and all
n\in\omega
. Since
a_{i}^{-1}a_{j}\models G^{00}
and
q
is concentrated on
G^{00}
, (
\star
) then follows from Fact
2.15
.

Let
J+g\models q^{\otimes(\omega+1)}|_{(M,I)}
, and let
u=a_{0}^{-1}a_{1}
. By Lemma
5.2
, we have
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g/M,J)
and
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u^{g}/M,J)
; moreover, by
5.2
applied to the bi-f-generic distal type
q^{-1}
, we also have
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g^{-1}/M,J)
and
\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u^{g^{-1}}/M,J)
. Call these facts (
\ast
).

Note also that, since
I
is
M
-indiscernible and
q
is
M
-invariant,
I
is
(M,J,g)
-indiscernible. Thus
u\equiv_{(M,J,g)}a_{i}^{-1}a_{j}
for all
i<j\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now,
(a_{i})_{i\in[0,1]}
and
(a_{j})_{j\in[3,4]}
are mutually indiscernible over
(M,a_{2},a_{5},J,g)
. By dp-minimality, one of them is thus indiscernible over
(M,a_{2},a_{5},J,g,a_{0}ga_{3}^{-1})
. First suppose
(a_{i})_{i\in[0,1]}
is indiscernible over that set. Note that

g^{-1}\cdot a_{0}^{-1}\cdot(a_{0}ga_{3}^{-1})\cdot a_{5}=a_{3}^{-1}a_{5}\equiv_{(M,J)}u.

Hence, by indiscernibility, also
g^{-1}a_{1}^{-1}a_{0}ga_{3}^{-1}a_{5}\equiv_{(M,J)}u
, ie
(a_{1}^{-1}a_{0})^{g}(a_{3}^{-1}a_{5})\equiv_{(M,J)}u
. But we have
a_{1}^{-1}a_{0}\equiv_{(M,J,g)}u^{-1}
, hence
(a_{1}^{-1}a_{0})^{g}\equiv_{(M,J)}u^{-g}
, and
a_{3}^{-1}a_{5}\equiv_{(M,J)}u
. Since
\operatorname{tp}(u^{-g}/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J)
, it follows that
u^{-g}u\equiv_{(M,J)}u
, ie that
[g,u]\equiv_{(M,J)}u
. By Lemma
5.1
, using (
\ast
) and (
\star
), we must have
u=1
, so that indeed
I
is constant.

Now suppose
(a_{j})_{j\in[3,4]}
is indiscernible over
(M,a_{2},a_{5},J,g,a_{0}ga_{3}^{-1})
. As above, note that

a_{2}^{-1}\cdot(a_{0}ga_{3}^{-1})\cdot a_{3}\cdot g^{-1}=a_{2}^{-1}a_{0}\equiv_{(M,J)}u^{-1}.

By indiscernibility, thus
a_{2}^{-1}a_{0}ga_{3}^{-1}a_{4}g^{-1}\equiv_{(M,J)}u^{-1}
, ie
(a_{2}^{-1}a_{0})(a_{3}^{-1}a_{4})^{g^{-1}}\equiv_{(M,J)}u^{-1}
. But we have
a_{3}^{-1}a_{4}\equiv_{(M,J,g)}u
, hence
(a_{3}^{-1}a_{4})^{g^{-1}}\equiv_{(M,J)}u^{g^{-1}}
, and
a_{2}^{-1}a_{0}\equiv_{(M,J)}u^{-1}
. Since

\operatorname{tp}(u^{g^{-1}}/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(u/M,J),

it follows that
u^{-1}u^{g^{-1}}\equiv_{(M,J)}u^{-1}
, so that
u^{-g^{-1}}u\equiv_{(M,J)}u
, ie
[g^{-1},u]\equiv_{(M,J)}u
. By Lemma
5.1
applied to the bi-f-generic distal type
q^{-1}
, using (
\ast
) and (
\star
), we must have
u=1
, so that again
I
is constant.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose
I=(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is an indiscernible sequence such that
b_{i}^{-1}b_{j}\sim b_{0}^{-1}b_{1}
for all
i<j
. Then
I
is constant.

Proof.

By Ramsey and compactness, we may find a model
M
such that (i)
G(M)
contains a representative of every coset of
G^{00}
, (ii)
I
is
M
-indiscernible, and (iii) for some
v\in G(M)
, we have
b_{i}^{-1}b_{j}\in v^{G}
for all
i<j
. By (i), there is some
\lambda\in G(M)
such that
b_{i}^{-1}b_{j}\in v^{G^{00}\lambda^{-1}}
for all
i<j
. Let
a_{i}=b_{i}^{\lambda}
. Then
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is
M
-indiscernible, and
a_{i}^{-1}a_{j}\in v^{G^{00}}
for all
i<j
; by Lemma
5.3
,
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
must be constant, so
I
is too.
∎

By considering a Morley sequence we hence obtain the following:

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
M\prec\mathfrak{C}
, and suppose
p(x)\in S_{G}(\mathfrak{C})
is
M
-invariant with
p^{\otimes 2}(x,y)\vdash p(x^{-1}y)
and
p^{\otimes 2}(x,y)\vdash x\sim y
. Then
p(x)\vdash x=1
.

5.2
Conjugacy classes determine types

In this section we will show that any type not concentrated on finitely many conjugacy classes is uniquely determined by the
G^{00}
-conjugacy class of any of its realizations.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
B\subset\mathfrak{C}
is a small set of parameters and that
a\models G
begins a
B
-indiscernible sequence of non-conjugates. Then
a^{g}\equiv_{B}a
for all
g\models G^{00}
.

Proof.

By Ramsey and compactness, we may assume without loss of generality that
B=M
is a model. I first claim that, for any
J+g\models q^{\otimes(\omega+1)}|_{(M,a)}
, we have
a^{g}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
; it suffices for this to show that
a^{g}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
for some
J+g\models q^{\otimes(\omega+1)}|_{(M,a)}
. To see this, let
I=(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
be an
M
-indiscernible sequence with
a_{0}=a
and
a_{i}\not\sim a_{j}
for all
i\neq j
. Let
J\models q^{\otimes\omega}|_{(M,I)}
and let
K=(g_{i})_{i\in\omega}\models q^{\otimes\omega}|_{(M,I,J)}
. Since
I
is
M
-indiscernible and
q
is
M
-invariant,
I
and
K
are mutually indiscernible over
(M,J)
, and thus by dp-minimality one of them is indiscernible over
(M,J,a^{g_{1}})
. Since
a_{i}\sim a^{g_{1}}=a_{0}^{g_{1}}
if and only if
i=0
,
K
must therefore be indiscernible over
(M,J,a^{g_{1}})
, and hence there is some
a_{*}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
such that
a_{*}^{g_{0}}=a^{g_{1}}
. But now
a^{g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}}=a_{*}
, so that
a^{g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
. Since
q
is right f-generic and concentrated on
G^{00}
, and
g_{1}\models q|_{(M,a,J,g_{0})}
, we have
g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}\models q|_{(M,a,J)}
, so the claim follows.

Now, let
J+g\models q^{\otimes(\omega+1)}|_{(M,a)}
. By the claim,
a^{g}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
, and by the claim applied to the bi-f-generic distal type
q^{-1}
also
a^{g^{-1}}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
. On the other hand, by distality of
q
, we have
\operatorname{tp}(a/M,J)\perp^{w}\operatorname{tp}(g/M,J)
, so it follows that
a_{*}^{g}\equiv_{(M,J)}a\equiv_{(M,J)}a_{*}^{g^{-1}}
for all
a_{*}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
. In other words, if
r(x)=\operatorname{tp}(a/M,J)
, then
g
lies in the normalizer
N_{G}(r):=\{h\in G:r(x)^{h}=r(x)\}
.
N_{G}(r)
is an
(M,J)
-invariant subgroup of
G
; since
\operatorname{tp}(g/M,J)=q|_{(M,J)}
and
q
is f-generic, it follows by Fact
2.12
that
G^{00}\subseteq N_{G}(r)
, so that
a^{h}\equiv_{(M,J)}a
for all
h\models G^{00}
. The result follows ‘a fortiori’.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

If
r(x)\in S_{G}(B)
for some
B\subset\mathfrak{C}
and
r
is not concentrated on finitely many conjugacy classes, then
r(x)^{g}=r(x)
for all
g\models G^{00}
. If
B=M
is a model, then the same holds provided
r
is not concentrated on a single conjugacy class.

Corollary 5.8
.

Suppose
p(x)\in S_{G}(\mathfrak{C})
is an
M
-invariant type not concentrated on any conjugacy class. If
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}\models p^{\otimes\omega}|_{M}
, then
(a_{i}^{g_{i}})_{i\in\omega}\models p^{\otimes\omega}|_{M}
for all
g_{i}\models G^{00}
.

Proof.

If
(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
is a Morley sequence of
p
over
M
, then each
b_{i}
starts an
(M,b_{\neq i})
-indiscernible sequence of non-conjugates, so by Lemma
5.6

b_{i}^{g}\equiv_{(M,b_{\neq i})}b_{i}
for all
g\models G^{00}
. Thus by induction on
n\in\omega
we have
(a_{i}^{g_{i}})_{i<n}+(a_{i})_{i\geqslant n}\models p^{\otimes\omega}|_{M}
for all
g_{i}\models G^{00}
, and the result follows. ∎

5.3
Commutators

In this section we will show that, for any
a\models G
, the set of commutators
[a,G^{00}]
is a group; this was proved for C-minimal valued groups in
[
30
]
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
a\models G
such that
q(x)\vdash[a,x]\neq 1
. Then
[a,q]
, ie the pushforward of
q
under the map
x\mapsto[a,x]
, is not concentrated on a single conjugacy class.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise for contradiction that
[a,q](x)\vdash x\sim u
. Let
M
be any small model containing
a,u
, and let
(g_{i})_{i\in\omega}\models q^{\otimes\omega}|_{M}
. Since
q
is right f-generic and concentrated on
G^{00}
, we have
g_{j}g_{i}^{-1}\models q|_{M}
for all
i<j
and hence
[a,g_{j}g_{i}^{-1}]\sim u
for all
i<j
. For
i\in\omega
, let
b_{i}=a^{g_{i}}
, and consider the
M
-indiscernible sequence
I=(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
. For
i<j
, we have

b_{i}^{-1}b_{j}=a^{-g_{i}}a^{g_{j}}=[a,g_{j}g_{i}^{-1}]^{g_{i}}\sim u.

By Lemma
5.4
,
I
is constant, so that
a^{g_{0}}=a^{g_{1}}
and hence
g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}\in C_{G}(a)
. Since
g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}\models q|_{M}
, this contradicts that
q(x)\vdash[a,x]\neq 1
.
∎

By Corollary
5.7
, and since
[a,q]
is just
\operatorname{tp}(1/\mathfrak{C})
if
q(x)\vdash[a,x]=1
, we get the following:

Corollary 5.10
.

For any
M\prec\mathfrak{C}
and any
a\in G(M)
, we have
[a,q|_{M}]=[a,q|_{M}]^{g}
for all
g\models G^{00}
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
a\models G
. Then
[a,g][a,q]=[a,q]
for all
g\models G^{00}
, and
[a,G^{00}][a,q|_{M}]\subseteq[a,G^{00}]
for any small model
M
with
a\in G(M)
.

Proof.

First note the following general fact. Let
r\in S_{G}(M)
be a complete type over some model
M\prec\mathfrak{C}
such that
a\in G(M)
and such that
[a,r]=[a,r]^{g}
for all
g\models G^{00}
. Then, from the identity
[a,g][a,c]^{g}=[a,cg]
, it follows that
[a,g][a,r]=[a,rg]
for all
g\models G^{00}
.

The desired result now follows; indeed by Corollary
5.10
and by the remark above we have
[a,g][a,q|_{M}(x)]=[a,q|_{M}(x)g]
for all
M\prec\mathfrak{C}
with
a\in G(M)
and all
g\models G^{00}
; this shows already that
[a,G^{00}][a,q|_{M}]\subseteq[a,G^{00}]
. Furthermore, since
q
is right f-generic, we have
qg=q
for all
g\in G^{00}(\mathfrak{C})
, so that
q|_{M}(x)g=q|_{M}(x)
whenever
G(M)\ni g
, and hence indeed
[a,g][a,q]=[a,q]
.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

For any
a\models G
, the set of commutators
[a,G^{00}]
is a type-definable subgroup of
G^{00}
, and it is ‘strongly connected’, ie has no proper type-definable subgroups of bounded index.

Proof.

Certainly
[a,G^{00}]
is type-definable, and it is a subset of
G^{00}
since
G^{00}
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Now, let
M
be any small model containing
a
. By Corollary
5.10
, we have
[a,q|_{M}]^{g}=[a,q|_{M}]
for all
g\models G^{00}
; likewise, by Corollary
5.10
applied to the bi-f-generic distal type
q^{-1}
, we also have
[a,q|_{M}^{-1}]^{g}=[a,q|_{M}^{-1}]
for all
g\models G^{00}
. Thus, for any
b\models q|_{M}
, since
q
is concentrated on
G^{00}
we have

\displaystyle[a,b^{-1}]

\displaystyle=[a,b]^{-b^{-1}}\in[a,q|_{M}]^{-b^{-1}}=[a,q|_{M}]^{-1}\text{ and}

\displaystyle[a,b]^{-1}

\displaystyle=[a,b^{-1}]^{b^{-1}}\in[a,q|_{M}^{-1}]^{b^{-1}}=[a,q|_{M}^{-1}],

so that
[a,q|_{M}^{-1}]=[a,q|_{M}]^{-1}
, and it follows that
[a,q|_{M}][a,q|_{M}^{-1}]
is closed under taking inverses. Now, by Fact
2.12
, we have
G^{00}\subseteq q|_{M}^{-1}(x)q|_{M}(x)
. Thus, for any
g\models G^{00}
, there are
b,c\models q|_{M}
with
g=b^{-1}c
, and then we have

[a,g]=[a,c][a,b^{-1}]^{c}\in[a,q|_{M}][a,q|_{M}^{-1}]^{c}=[a,q|_{M}][a,q|_{M}^{-1}],

so that
[a,G^{00}]\subseteq[a,q|_{M}][a,q|_{M}^{-1}]
. Finally, by Lemma
5.11
applied to
q
and to
q^{-1}
, we have

[a,G^{00}][a,q|_{M}][a,q|_{M}^{-1}]\subseteq[a,G^{00}][a,q|_{M}^{-1}]\subseteq[a,G^{00}].

Thus
[a,q|_{M}][a,q|_{M}^{-1}]=[a,G^{00}]
, and it is symmetric and closed under multiplication; in other words, it is a group.

To see that
[a,G^{00}]
is strongly connected, note that, by Lemma
5.11
,
[a,q]
is invariant under left translation by all elements of
[a,G^{00}(\mathfrak{C})]
. If
H\leqslant[a,G^{00}]
is type-definable and of bounded index in
[a,G^{00}]
, then, since
[a,q]
is concentrated on
[a,G^{00}]
, it must be concentrated on some right coset of
H
, and then left-invariance of
[a,q]
forces
H=[a,G^{00}]
.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

For any
a,b\models G
, one of
[a,G^{00}]\supseteq[b,G^{00}]
and
[a,G^{00}]\subseteq[b,G^{00}]
holds.

Proof.

By Fact
2.14
,
[a,G^{00}]\cap[b,G^{00}]
has bounded index in one of
[a,G^{00}]
and
[b,G^{00}]
. Since the latter two groups are strongly connected,
[a,G^{00}]\cap[b,G^{00}]
must be equal to one of them, and the result follows.
∎

Corollary 5.14
.

For any type-definable set
X\subseteq G
, the set of commutators
[X,G^{00}]
is a type-definable strongly connected subgroup of
G^{00}
.

Proof.

[X,G^{00}]
is equal to
\bigcup_{a\models X}[a,G^{00}]
, and by Lemma
5.12
and Corollary
5.13
this is the union of a chain of subgroups of
G^{00}
. So indeed
[X,G^{00}]
is a group, and it is certainly type-definable. To see that it is strongly connected, suppose that
H\leqslant[X,G^{00}]
is a bounded-index type-definable subgroup. Then, for every
a\models X
,
H\cap[a,G^{00}]
has bounded index in
[a,G^{00}]
, hence must be equal to
[a,G^{00}]
. So
H\geqslant[a,G^{00}]
for all
a\models X
and hence
H=[X,G^{00}]
, as desired.
∎

The following now has an identical proof as Proposition 2.4.1 of
[
30
]
:

Lemma 5.15
.

[G^{00},G^{00}]
has finite exponent.

Proof.

Fix
a,b\models G^{00}
; by Lemma
5.12
,
[a,b,G^{00}]
is now a normal subgroup of
G^{00}
. Working in the quotient
G^{00}/[a,b,G^{00}]
, it follows from the identities
[a^{k+1},b]=[a,b][a,b,a^{k}][a^{k},b]
and
[a,b^{k+1}]=[a,b^{k}][a,b][a,b,b^{k}]
that
[a^{k},b]
and
[a,b^{k}]
are each equivalent to
[a,b]^{k}
modulo
[a,b,G^{00}]
for each
k\in\omega
. By Fact
2.15
, there is some
n>0
such that one of
[a^{n},b]
and
[a,b^{n}]
is trivial, and it follows that
[a,b]^{n}\in[a,b,G^{00}]
.

Thus there is some
g\models G^{00}
with
[a,b]^{n}=[a,b,g]
, so that
[a,b]^{n+1}=[a,b]^{g}\sim[a,b]
. By Lemma
2.6
and Fact
2.15
, it follows that
[a,b]
must be a torsion element. Since
a,b\models G^{00}
were arbitrary, thus every element of
[G^{00},G^{00}]
has finite order, and so by compactness
[G^{00},G^{00}]
has finite exponent.
∎

Corollary 5.16
.

If
G
does not have finite exponent, then it is virtually nilpotent of class
\leqslant 2
.

Proof.

If the realizations of
q(x)
are non-torsion, then by Lemma
4.4
we are done, so we may assume that
q(x)\vdash x^{k}=1
for some
k>0
; we aim to show that
G
has finite exponent. By Lemma
5.15
, let
m>0
be the exponent of
[G^{00},G^{00}]
. By Fact
2.12
, every element of
G^{00}
is a difference of two realizations of
q
, and it follows that
G^{00}
has exponent at most
mk
: if
a,b\models G^{00}
have order at most
k
, then
(ab)^{k}
is equivalent to
a^{k}b^{k}=1
modulo
[G^{00},G^{00}]
, so that
(ab)^{k}
lies in
[G^{00},G^{00}]
, and hence
(ab)^{km}=1
. Thus indeed
G^{00}
has exponent at most
n:=mk
.

Now, since
G^{00}
is a group, we have
G^{00}(x)\wedge G^{00}(y)\vdash(xy^{-1})^{n}=1
, and so by compactness there is some
\varnothing
-definable set
D\supseteq G^{00}
such that
(ab^{-1})^{n}=1
for all
a,b\models D
; in other words
c^{n}=1
for all
c\in DD^{-1}
.

Now I claim that
G
is torsion; by compactness the result will follow. Otherwise, there is some non-torsion element
b\models G
. Since every element of
DD^{-1}
is torsion, we have
b^{k}\notin DD^{-1}
for all
k\neq 0
, and hence
b^{i}D\cap b^{j}D=\varnothing
for all
i\neq j\in\omega
. Letting
I=(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
be an indiscernible sequence realizing the EM-type of
(b^{i})_{i\in\omega}
over
\varnothing
, we then have
a_{i}D\cap a_{j}D=\varnothing
for all
i\neq j
; since
D\supseteq G^{00}
, the
a_{i}
thus lie in pairwise distinct cosets of
G^{00}
. Since
I
is indiscernible, this contradicts that
G^{00}
has bounded index in
G
, and the result follows.
∎

5.4
Valued group structure

Suppose that
(H,\cdot)
is an arbitrary group and that
N\trianglelefteqslant H
is a normal subgroup such that the collection
\mathcal{P}=\{[a,N]:a\in H\}
is a family of subgroups of
H
totally ordered by the reverse inclusion relation, which we will denote
\preccurlyeq
. Note that
(\mathcal{P},\preccurlyeq)
has a maximal element
\{1\}=[1,N]
. Now, define a map
v:H/C_{H}(N)\to\mathcal{P}
by
v(a)=[a,N]
; this is well-defined since
[C_{H}(N)a,g]=\{[a,g]\}
for all
a\in H
and
g\in N
. By definition,
v(a)\preccurlyeq v(b)
if and only if
[a,N]\supseteq[b,N]
.

Lemma 5.17
.

In the above circumstances,
(H,v)
is a
\mathcal{P}
-valued group.

Proof.

For property (1), note that, for
a\in H
, we have
v(a)=\max\mathcal{P}
if and only if
[a,N]=\{1\}
, ie if and only if
a\in C_{H}(N)
. For property (2), suppose that
v(a)\prec v(b)
and that
g\in H
is arbitrary. We have
[a,N]\supsetneq[b,N]
, and hence
[a,N]^{g}\supsetneq[b,N]^{g}
. But
N
is a normal subgroup of
H
, so
[a,N]^{g}=[a^{g},N^{g}]=[a^{g},N]
, and likewise
[b,N]^{g}=[b^{g},N]
, so that
v(a^{g})\prec v(b^{g})
as desired. For property (3), fix
a\in H
. We have
[a^{-1},N]=[N,a]^{a^{-1}}=[N,a]
. But
[a,N]
is by hypothesis a subgroup of
H
, hence in particular is closed under inverses, and so
[a^{-1},N]=[a,N]
, whence
v(a)=v(a^{-1})
.

So we need only show property (4), ie that
v(ab)\succcurlyeq\min\{v(a),v(b)\}
for all
a,b\in H
. First suppose that
v(b)=\min\{v(a),v(b)\}
, ie that
[a,N]\subseteq[b,N]
; we wish to show that
[ab,N]\subseteq[b,N]
. By the identity
[ab,g]=[a,g]^{b}[b,g]
, we have
[ab,N]\subseteq[a,N]^{b}[b,N]
. But
[a,N]^{b}\subseteq[b,N]^{b}=[b,N]
, so we have
[ab,N]\subseteq[b,N][b,N]\subseteq[b,N]
, where the second inclusion uses that
[b,N]
is closed under multiplication. In other words
v(ab)\succcurlyeq v(b)
, as desired.

Now suppose that
v(a)=\min\{v(a),v(b)\}
. Using that
v(c)=v(c^{-1})
for all
c\in H
, we have
v(ab)=v(b^{-1}a^{-1})
and
v(b^{-1})=v(b)\succcurlyeq v(a)=v(a^{-1})
. Thus
v(a^{-1})=\min\{v(b^{-1}),v(a^{-1})\}
and so, by the case in the previous paragraph, we have
v(b^{-1}a^{-1})\succcurlyeq v(a^{-1})
. But this means
v(ab)\succcurlyeq v(a)
, as desired.
∎

Now, let us return to the group
G
. The subgroup
N=G^{00}
is normal in
G
, and by Lemma
5.12
and Corollary
5.13
the set
\mathcal{P}=\{[a,G^{00}]:a\models G\}
is a chain of subgroups of
G
. So, by Lemma
5.17
, the map
v:a\mapsto[a,G^{00}]
makes
G/C_{G}(G^{00})
into a
\mathcal{P}
-valued group. Note that, by Fact
2.11
, the group
C_{G}(G^{00})
has a purely group-theoretic description; recall that the FC-center of
G
, which we will denote
\mathrm{FC}(G)
, is the set of all
g\in G
with
[G:C_{G}(g)]<\infty
. This is a characteristic subgroup of
G
, and by Fact
2.11
we have
g\in\mathrm{FC}(G)
if and only if
G^{00}\subseteq C_{G}(g)
. So
C_{G}(G^{00})=\mathrm{FC}(G)
and thus the map
v
makes
G/\mathrm{FC}(G)
into a valued group.

We have
\mathrm{FC}(G)\cap G^{00}=C_{G}(G^{00})\cap G^{00}=Z(G^{00})
. Thus
G^{00}/Z(G^{00})
inherits the valued group structure from
G/\mathrm{FC}(G)
.

Theorem 5.18
.

(G^{00}/Z(G^{00}),v)
is a plain valued group.

Proof.

We need only show that
v
is a plain valuation. Hence fix
a,b\in G^{00}\setminus Z(G^{00})
; we wish to show that
v([a,b])\succ\max\{v(a),v(b)\}
. Since
v([a,b])=v([b,a])
, we may assume without loss that
v(a)=\max\{v(a),v(b)\}
, so that
[a,G^{00}]\subseteq[b,G^{00}]
. We wish to show that
v([a,b])\succ v(a)
, ie that
[a,b,G^{00}]\not\supseteq[a,G^{00}]
. To see this, let
M
be any small model and let
g\models q|_{(M,a,b)}
; it suffices to show that
[a,g]\notin[a,b,G^{00}]
. Suppose otherwise for contradiction that
[a,g]=[a,b,h]
for some
h\models G^{00}
. Now,
[a,g]\models[a,q]|_{(M,a,b)}
, and
b\models G^{00}
, so by Lemma
5.11
we have
[a,b][a,g]\models[a,q]|_{(M,a,b)}
. But

Now by Fact

Corollary 5.19
.

Proof.

If

has finite exponent, then by Fact

and Theorem

5.5
Local nilpotence to nilpotence

The following lemma and its proof work just with the assumption that
G
is dp-minimal; we do not need the existence of a distal f-generic type for it.

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
M\prec\mathfrak{C}
be any model, and suppose
p(x)\in S_{G}(\mathfrak{C})
is an
M
-invariant type with the property that, for some (any)
(a,b)\models p^{\otimes 2}|_{M}
, the group
\langle a,b\rangle
is nilpotent of class at most
(n-1)
. Then there is some
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that
p^{\otimes n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\vdash[x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)}]=1
.

Proof.

Let
(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}+(u_{i})_{i\in\omega}+(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
be a Morley sequence of
p
over some model
M
. Write
I=(a_{i})_{i\in\omega}
and
J=(b_{i})_{i\in\omega}
and
K=(u_{i})_{i\in\omega}
for notational convenience.

By hypothesis,
\langle a_{0},b_{1}\rangle
is nilpotent of class at most
(n-1)
. We will prove by backwards induction on
k
that, for every
k\in[n]\cup\{0\}
, and for all
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in\{a_{0},b_{1}\}
, there is some
\tau\in S_{n-k}
such that
[c_{1},\dots,c_{k},u_{\tau(n-k)},\dots,u_{\tau(1)}]=1
. The base case
n=k
is clear. Now, suppose we have shown the result for
k+1
, and fix
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in\{a_{0},b_{1}\}
.
I
and
J
are mutually indiscernible over
K
, and so by dp-minimality one of
I
and
J
is indiscernible over
(K,[c_{1},\dots,c_{k}])
.

First suppose
I
is indiscernible over that set. By the inductive hypothesis, there is some
\nu\in S_{n-k-1}
such that
[c_{1},\dots,c_{k},a_{0},u_{\nu(n-k-1)},\dots,u_{\nu(1)}]=1
. By indiscernibility of
I
over
(K,[c_{1},\dots,c_{k}])
, it follows that
[c_{1},\dots,c_{k},a_{1},u_{\nu(n-k-1)},\dots,u_{\nu(1)}]=1
. Now if we define
\tau\in S_{n-k}
by taking
\tau(n-k)=1
and taking
\tau(i)=\nu(i)+1
for

then we get the desired result by indiscernibility of
a_{0}+a_{1}+u_{1}+\dots+u_{n-k}+b_{1}
.

Now suppose
J
is indiscernible over that set. By the inductive hypothesis, there is some
\nu\in S_{n-k-1}
such that
[c_{1},\dots,c_{k},b_{1},u_{\nu(n-k-1)},\dots,u_{\nu(1)}]=1
. By indiscernibility of
J
over
(K,[c_{1},\dots,c_{k}])
, it follows that
[c_{1},\dots,c_{k},b_{0},u_{\nu(n-k-1)},\dots,u_{\nu(1)}]=1
. Now if we define
\tau\in S_{n-k}
by taking
\tau(n-k)=n-k
and
\tau(i)=\nu(i)
for
i\in[n-k-1]
then we get the desired result by indiscernibility of
a_{0}+u_{1}+\dots+u_{n-k}+b_{0}+b_{1}
.

So the induction goes through, and when
k=0
we get that
[u_{\sigma(n)},\dots,u_{\sigma(1)}]=1
for some
\sigma\in S_{n}
, giving the desired result.
∎

Now we can obtain the main result.

Theorem 5.21
.

G
is virtually nilpotent.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.19
,

is locally nilpotent, so any global type

concentrated on

6
UCC case

In this section we will study dp-minimal groups with the ‘uniform chain condition’ (ucc), ie with the property that they contain no infinite strictly decreasing sequence of uniformly definable subgroups. The class of ucc groups includes stable groups, groups definable in o-minimal structures, and more generally all rosy groups; see eg
[
10
]
.

After completing this project, we learned that Eran Alouf and Frank Wagner had independently proved Theorem
6.7
in the case that
G
is stable. Since then, Frank Wagner has shared with us a proof that any solvable abelian-by-(finite exponent) group with the intersection chain condition is virtually nilpotent. By Fact
2.15
, Fact
2.10
, and Theorem
6.7
, it follows that dp-minimal ucc groups are in fact virtually nilpotent.

Throughout this section,
assume
that
G
is dp-minimal.

We begin first with a remark that failures of ucc give rise to type-definable subgroups of

that have distal f-generic types, where we define f-genericity for relatively definable subsets of a type-definable group in the natural way.

1

1

Namely, suppose

Remark 6.1

Suppose

By Ramsey and compactness, there is a formula

Now we can begin proving the main result.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
G
has ucc. Then there is an infinite type-definable subgroup

H\leqslant O
for every infinite
\mathfrak{C}
-definable subgroup

Lemma 6.5
.

I claim that, if

By the construction of

Theorem 6.7
.

If
G

Proof.

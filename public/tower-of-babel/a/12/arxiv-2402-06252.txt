Lipschitz bounds for nonuniformly elliptic integral functionals in the plane
Mathias Schäffner

Abstract
. We study local regularity properties of local minimizer of scalar integral functionals with controlled ( p , q ) (p,q) -growth in the two-dimensional plane. We establish Lipschitz continuity for local minimizer under the condition 1 < p ≤ q < ∞ 1<p\leq q<\infty with q < 3 ​ p q<3p which improve upon the classical results valid in the regime q < 2 ​ p q<2p . Along the way, we establish an L ∞ L^{\infty} - L 2 L^{2} -estimate for solutions of linear uniformly elliptic equations in the plane which is optimal with respect to the ellipticity contrast of the coefficients.



1.
Introduction

In this short note, we revisit the question of Lipschitz-regularity for local minimizers of integral functionals of the form

(1)

w\mapsto\mathcal{F}(w;\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}F(\nabla w)\,dx,

where the integrand
F
satisfies so-called
(p,q)
-growth conditions which are described in the following

Assumption 1
.

Let
0<\nu\leq\Lambda<\infty
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
\mu\in[0,1]
be given. Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is convex, locally
C^{2}
-regular in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and satisfies

(2)

\begin{cases}\nu(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq F(z)\leq\Lambda(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+\Lambda(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
|\partial^{2}F(z)|\leq\Lambda(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}+\Lambda(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}},\\
\nu(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle\partial^{2}F(z)\xi,\xi\rangle,\end{cases}

for every choice of
z,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|z|>0
.

We recall that regularity theory for local minimizer of (
1
) under Assumption
1
with
1<p=q
is classical, see e.g.
[
27
]
. A systematic regularity theory in the case
p<q
starts with the seminal contributions of Marcellini
[
31
,
32
]
and became an active field of research (see e.g.
[
35
,
34
]
or the introduction of
[
33
]
for recent overviews on the literature).

Before we state our main result, we recall a standard notion of local minimality in the context of integral functionals with
(p,q)
-growth

Definition 1
.

We call
u\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)
a local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
given in (
1
) if and only if
F(\nabla u)\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
and for every
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
holds
\mathcal{F}(u,\Omega^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{F}(u+\varphi,\Omega^{\prime})
for every
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega^{\prime})
.

The main result of the present paper is

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open bounded domain and suppose Assumption
1
is satisfied with
1<p\leq q<\infty
such that

(3)

q<3p.

Let
u\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{F}
given in (
1
). Then
\nabla u
is locally bounded in
\Omega
. Moreover, there exists
c=c(\Lambda,\nu,p,q)\in[1,\infty)
such that for all
B\Subset\Omega
it holds

(4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq

\displaystyle c\biggl(\fint_{B}F(\nabla u)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}+c\biggl(\fint_{B}F(\nabla u)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{2}{3p-q}}.

Theorem
1
improves upon the by now classical findings of Marcellini
[
32
]
, where Lipschitz-continuity is proven under the relation
q<2p
. More precisely, Marcellini considered integral functionals in arbitrary dimensions
n\geq 2
and proved local Lipschitz continuity for minimizer of (
1
) under the restriction

(5)

\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{2}{n},

that is
q<2p
for
n=2
. Recently, the condition (
5
) for Lipschitz-continuity was relaxed in joint works with Bella
[
5
,
6
]
to

(6)

\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{2}{n-1}

in dimensions
n\geq 3
, but the methods of
[
5
,
6
]
(that are based on
[
4
]
) do not give any improvement compared to Marcellini’s result in dimension
n=2
. Combining Theorem
1
with the findings of
[
5
,
6
]
, we have

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
and suppose Assumption
1
is satisfied with
1<p\leq q<\infty
such that (
6
). Let
u\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{F}
given in (
1
). Then,
u\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Optimality of condition (
3
) in Theorem
1
is not clear. We note that the results of
[
12
,
29
]
imply that in the situation of Theorem
1
local minimizer are locally bounded for every choice of
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, and recall that by Marcellini’s counterexample, see
[
31
, Theorem 6.1]
, this is in general not true in dimensions
n\geq 3
(and exponents
1<p<n-1
and
q<\infty
sufficiently large).

The strategy to prove Theorem
1
is similar to
[
3
,
6
]
and relies on careful estimates for certain
uniformly elliptic
problems. Indeed, one of the main technical achievement in
[
6
]
is to establish a local
L^{\infty}
-
L^{2}
-estimate for solutions of linear uniformly elliptic equations which is essentially optimal with respect to the ellipticity contrast in dimension
n\geq 3
, see
[
6
, Proposition 1]
. Here, we provide the analogous statement in dimension
n=2
, namely

Proposition 1
.

Let
B=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. There exists
c<\infty
, such that the following is true. Let
0<\nu\leq\lambda<\infty
and suppose
a\in L^{\infty}(B;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
satisfies that
a(x)
is symmetric for almost every
x\in B
and is uniformly elliptic in the sense

(7)

\nu|z|^{2}\leq a(x)z\cdot z\leq\Lambda|z|^{2}\quad\mbox{for every $z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ and almost every $x\in B$.}

Let
v\in W^{1,2}(B)
be a subsolution, that is it satisfies

(8)

\int_{B}a\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi\leq 0\qquad\mbox{for all $\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(B)$ with $\varphi\geq 0$.}

Then,

(9)

\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B}v\leq c\biggl(\frac{\Lambda}{\nu}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{4}}\biggl(\fint_{B}(v_{+})^{2}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
v_{+}:=\max\{v,0\}
. Moreover, the exponent
\frac{1}{4}
in (
9
) is optimal.

The proof of Proposition
1
is elementary and only relies on the maximum principle, a suitable one dimensional Poincaré inequality and the Caccioppoli inequality, see Section
3
. However, Proposition
1
(or rather its proof) is crucial for the proof of Theorem
1
. To illustrate the relation between Proposition
1
and Theorem
1
, we consider the case that
F
satisfies Assumption
1
with
p=2<q
: Let
u
be a local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}(\cdot,B_{1})
and assume that
u
is smooth. Standard arguments yield

(10)

{\rm{div}}(A(x)\nabla\partial_{i}u)=0\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad A:=\partial^{2}F(\nabla u).

Hence,
\partial_{i}u
satisfies a linear elliptic equation where
A
satisfies (using (
2
))

|z|^{2}\lesssim Az\cdot z\lesssim(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{q-2})|z|^{2}.

Hence, Proposition
1
(applied to
\partial_{1}u
and
\partial_{2}u
) yields

(11)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\lesssim(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{q-2}{4}})\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}.

Appealing to some well-known iteration arguments, it is possible to absorb the prefactor
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{q-2}{4}}
on the right-hand side in (
11
) provided that
\frac{q-2}{4}<1
, which yields the restriction
q<6
coinciding with (
3
) for
p=2
. Once an a priori Lipschitz estimate for smooth minimizer is established, the proof of Theorem
1
follows by a careful regularization and approximation procedure – for this we closely follow related arguments from
[
18
,
19
]
.

Next, we discuss further related literature: Firstly, we mention the recent paper
[
15
]
, where a variation of
p,q
-growth conditions is considered and local Lipschitz-continuity is proven in dimension two for all exponents
2\leq p<q
. In
[
15
]
, the assumptions (

) are strengthened by assuming an additional relation between the growth of
\partial^{2}F
and
\partial F
. Let us now discuss some possible extensions of Theorem

: Here we assume polynomial growth conditions with

. There are several results (and recent interest) for integrands satisfying more general growth conditions and in particular allowing for nearly linear growth, see e.g.
[
7
,
17
,
20
,

,

,

]
. We expect that the method to prove Theorem

can be combined with the techniques of e.g.
[

,

,

]
to improve (in parts) these results in
n=2
(and for

with the methods of
[

). As mentioned above, we rely in the proof of Theorem

on the maximum principle and thus the argument cannot be used for vectorial problems. It would be interesting to find an argument which is applicable for systems to improve higher integrability results, see e.g.
[
11
,
24
,
37
,
30
]
, for vectorial integral functionals in dimension
n=2
, see
[
8
]
for a full regularity result under Assumption

with

.

In the above discussion, we mostly ignored the rich regularity theory for non-autonomous non-uniformly elliptic integral functionals, we refer again to the recent overviews
[

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with an elementary one dimensional interpolation inequality.

Lemma 1
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval. Then, it holds

(12)

\forall u\in H^{1}(I):\qquad\|u-(u)_{I}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\sqrt{2}\|u-(u)_{I}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
(u)_{I}:=\fint_{I}u(s)\,ds
.

Proof.

By density it suffices to show (
12
) for
u\in C^{1}(I)
and without loss of generality we assume
(u)_{I}=0
. By the integral mean value theorem there exists
y\in I
such that
u(y)=(u)_{I}=0
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have for every
x\in I

u^{2}(x)=2\biggl|\int_{y}^{x}u(s)u^{\prime}(s)\,ds\biggr|\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{2}(I)}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}.

We obtain (
12
) by applying
\sup_{x\in I}
and taking the square-root of the resulting inequality.
∎

In the following, we use Lemma
1
in the form below

Corollary 1
.

There exists
c<\infty
such that the following is true: For every
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
and
\frac{1}{4}\leq\rho<\sigma<1
, it holds

(13)

\inf_{r\in(\rho,\sigma)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{r})}\leq\frac{c}{(\sigma-\rho)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\biggl(\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\rho})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\rho})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\rho})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggr)

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
. For
r\in[0,1]
, we define
u_{r}:\partial B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
as
u_{r}(z)=u(rz)
for
z\in\partial B_{1}
. Clearly,
u_{r}
is weakly differentiable for almost every
r\in[0,1]
and by Fubini’s theorem there exists a Nullset
N\subset[0,1]
such that
u_{r}\in H^{1}(\partial B_{1},\mathbb{R})
for
r\in[0,1]\setminus N
. We have

\displaystyle(\sigma-\rho)^{\frac{1}{2}}\inf_{r\in(\rho,\sigma)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{r})}\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{(\rho,\sigma)\setminus N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{r})}^{2}\,dr\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\biggl(\int_{(\rho,\sigma)\setminus N}\|u_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\,dr\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{(\rho,\sigma)\setminus N}\|u_{r}-(u_{r})_{\partial B_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\,dr\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\biggl(\int_{\rho}^{\sigma}\biggl(\int_{\partial B_{1}}|u(rz)|\,dz\biggr)^{2}\,dr\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{est:1dinterpolation}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{(\rho,\sigma)\setminus N}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{r}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}\|\nabla_{\rm tan}u_{r}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}\,dr\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\rho})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\rho})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\rho})}

and the claim follows.
∎

Finally, we recall here the following classical iteration lemma

Lemma 2

(Lemma 6.1,
[

]
)
.

Let
Z(t)
be a bounded non-negative function in the interval
[\rho,\sigma]
. Assume that for every
\rho\leq s<t\leq\sigma
it holds

Z(s)\leq\theta Z(t)+(t-s)^{-\alpha}A+B,

with
A,B\geq 0
,
\alpha>0
and
\theta\in[0,1)
. Then, there exists
c=c(\alpha,\theta)\in[1,\infty)
such that

Z(s)\leq c((t-s)^{-\alpha}A+B).

Proof of Proposition
1

The proof of estimate (
9
) in Proposition
1
follows by a combination of Corollary
1
and the maximum principle, see e.g.
[
4
, Proposition 3.4]
for related arguments for nonuniformly linear elliptic equations.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Without loss of generality, we only consider
B=B_{1}(0)
and
1=\nu\leq\Lambda
. The general claim follows by standard scaling and translation arguments and multiplying the equation by
\nu^{-1}
.

Step 1.
Let
v\in H^{1}(B)
be a subsolutions in the sense of (
8
). We claim that

(14)

\sup_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}v\lesssim\|v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The maximum principle for subsolution, see e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 8.1]
, yields

\displaystyle\forall r\in[\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{4}]:\quad\sup_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}v\leq\sup_{B_{r}}v\leq\|v_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{r})}

and the claim (
14
) follows from Corollary
1
.

Step 2.
Proof of estimate (
9
). Let
v\in H^{1}(B)
be as in Step 1. By standard density arguments, we can choose
\varphi=v_{+}\eta^{2}
with
\eta\in C_{c}^{1}(B_{1})
in (
8
) and obtain (after standard manipulations)

\int_{B}\eta^{2}|\nabla v_{+}|^{2}\,dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:ellipt}}}{{\leq}}\int_{B}\eta^{2}a\nabla v_{+}\cdot\nabla v_{+}\,dx\leq 4\int_{B}v_{+}^{2}a\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:ellipt}}}{{\leq}}4\Lambda\int_{B}v_{+}^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx.

Choosing the obvious
\eta
, we find

(15)

\|\nabla v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\lesssim\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}.

Inserting (
15
) into (
14
) yields

\sup_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{P1:step1:est0}}}{{\lesssim}}\|v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}+\|v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{P1:step2:est1}}}{{\lesssim}}(1+\Lambda^{\frac{1}{4}})\|v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B)}

which yields the claimed estimate (
9
) (recall
\Lambda\geq 1=\nu
).

Step 3.
Optimality. This follows by the same example used in
[
6
, Remark 3.2]
to show (almost) optimality of a related statement for
n\geq 3
. We briefly recall the example (which is inspired by
[
36
]
).

Consider
v(x):=1+x_{2}^{2}-\Lambda x_{1}^{2}
which clearly satisfies

(16)

-\nabla\cdot a\nabla v=0\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad a:={\rm diag}(1,\Lambda).

Obviously, we have
\sup_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\,v\geq v(0)\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}(v_{+})^{2}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{\Lambda^{-1/2}(1+x_{2}^{2})^{1/2}}(1+x_{2}^{2})^{2}-2(1+x_{2}^{2})\Lambda x_{1}^{2}+\Lambda^{2}x_{1}^{4}\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Lambda^{-1/2}(1-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{5})\int_{-1}^{1}(1+x_{2}^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}\,dx_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{32}{15}\cdot 2^{\frac{5}{2}}\Lambda^{-1/2}.

In particular, we have

\frac{\sup_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}v}{\|v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}}\geq\biggl(\frac{32}{15}\cdot 2^{\frac{5}{2}}\biggr)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{\frac{1}{4}},

which shows that the exponent
\frac{1}{4}
in (
9
) is sharp.
∎

4.
A priori estimate for regularized problems

In this section, we derive Lipschitz estimates for minimizers of certain regularized problems. We introduce the notation

(17)

H_{\mu}(z):=\mu^{2}+|z|^{2},\qquad E_{\mu}(z):=\frac{1}{p}\biggl(H_{\mu}(z)^{\frac{p}{2}}-\mu^{p}\biggr)

and suppose in this section

Assumption 2
.

Let
0<\nu\leq\Lambda<\infty
,
\tilde{\nu}>0
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
\mu\in(0,2]
be given. Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is convex, locally
C^{2}
-regular and satisfies for every choice of
z,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(18)

\begin{cases}\nu(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{p}{2}}+\tilde{\nu}(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{q}{2}}\leq F(z)\leq\Lambda(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{q}{2}}+\Lambda(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
|\partial^{2}F(z)|\leq\Lambda(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{q-2}{2}}+\Lambda(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{p-2}{2}},\\
\left(\nu(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\tilde{\nu}(H_{\mu}(z))^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\right)|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle\partial^{2}F(z)\xi,\xi\rangle.\end{cases}

In contrast to Assumptions
1
, Assumptions
2
implies that
F
satisfies standard
q
-growth conditions. In particular, classical regularity theory applies to local minimizer of (
1
) under Assumption
2
. In the following lemma we provide a Lipschitz estimate for local minimizer of (
1
) under Assumption
2
which is independent of the parameter
\tilde{\nu}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain and suppose Assumption
2
is satisfied with
1<p\leq q<3p<\infty
. Let
u\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{F}
given in (
1
). Then, there exists
c=c(\Lambda,\nu,p,q)\in[1,\infty)
such that for all
B\Subset\Omega
it holds

(19)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{1}{2}B)}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle c\fint_{B}H_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx+c\biggl(\fint_{B}H_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{2}{3p-q}}.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Throughout the proof we write
\lesssim
if
\leq
holds up to a multiplicative constant depending only on
\Lambda,\nu,p
and
q
. By standard scaling and translation arguments it suffices to consider the case
B=B_{1}\Subset\Omega
.

Step 0.
Preliminaries. By Assumption
2
, the integrand
F
satisfies standard
q
-growth. Hence, by classical elliptic regularity, we have that
u\in W^{2,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C_{\rm loc}^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, see e.g.
[
27
]
, and in particular
u
solves the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation

(20)

\nabla\cdot(\partial F(\nabla u))=0\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega$}.

Moreover, we can differentiate the Euler-Lagrange equation (
20
) and obtain that
\partial_{s}u\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
solves

(21)

\nabla\cdot(\partial^{2}F(\nabla u)\nabla\partial_{s}u)=0\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega$}.

Finally, we state a crucial Caccioppoli inequality: There exists
c=c(\Lambda,\nu,p,q)\in[1,\infty)
such that

(22)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{B}}|\nabla E_{\mu}(\nabla u)|^{2}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle c(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{B})}^{q-p})|\tilde{B}|^{-1}\int_{\tilde{B}}|E_{\mu}(\nabla u)|^{2}\,dx,

for all balls
\tilde{B}\subset B
(recall
E_{\mu}(z)
is defined in (
17
)). Note that the
c
in (
22
) is independent of
\tilde{\nu}
. Estimate (
22
) follows directly from
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
(which is based on a more general result derived in
[
3
]
) applied to (
20
) with
A_{0}=\partial F
. Indeed, the symmetry of
\partial A_{0}=\partial^{2}F
is obvious and the estimates on
A_{0}
and
\partial A_{0}
stated in
[
18
, equation (5.16)]
follow from Assumption
2
, where we use that the upper bound on
F
in (
18
) in combination with the convexity of
F
yield

(23)

|\partial F(z)|\leq\Lambda^{\prime}(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{q-1}{2}}+\Lambda^{\prime}(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

with
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda^{\prime}(\Lambda,p,q)
.

Step 1.
In this step, we suppose
B=B_{1}\Subset\Omega
and prove

(24)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{4}})}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle(\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\gamma p}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{p}{2}})\|H_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mu^{p},

where
\gamma:=\frac{q+p}{4p}
. In view of Step 0, we have
u\in W^{1,\infty}(B)
and thus by Assumption
2
the matrix field
\partial^{2}F(\nabla u)
is uniformly elliptic in
B
. Hence, the maximum principle, see e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 8.1]
, applied to equation (
21
) yields for
s\in\{1,2\}
and all
r\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{4}})}^{p}\leq\|\partial_{s}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{r})}^{p}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{r})}^{p}\leq p(\|E_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{r})}+\mu^{p})

(where we use
|t|^{p}\leq(\mu^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq p(E_{\mu}(t)+\mu^{p})
). Thus we obtain with help of Corollary
1
and (
22
)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{4}})}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|E_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|E_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla E_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mu^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{q-p}{4}})\|E_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\mu^{p}.

The claimed estimate (
24
) follows from the elementary interpolation inequality
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}\leq(\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

\|E_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}(\mu^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{2})^{\frac{p}{4}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{p}{2}},

E_{\mu}(t)\leq H_{\mu}(t)^{\frac{p}{2}}
and
\frac{q-p}{4}+\frac{p}{2}=\frac{q+p}{4}=p\gamma
.

Step 2.
Conclusion. Appealing to standard scaling and covering arguments, we deduce from Step 1 the following: For every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<\rho<\sigma
satisfying
B_{\sigma}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
it holds

(25)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\biggl(\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}^{\gamma p}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{p}{2}}\biggr)\frac{\|H_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{1}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sigma-\rho}+\mu^{p}.

From estimate (
25
) in combination with Young inequality and assumption
1<q<3p
in the form
0<\gamma=\frac{q+p}{4p}<\frac{4p}{4p}=1
, we obtain the existence of
c=c(\nu,\Lambda,p,q)\in[1,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}^{p}+c\frac{\|H_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{1}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}}{(\sigma-\rho)^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}}

\displaystyle+c\frac{\|H_{\mu}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{1}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}}{(\sigma-\rho)^{2}}+c\mu^{p}.

The claimed inequality (
19
) (for
B=B_{1}
) now follows from Lemma
2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

Appealing to Lemma
3
, the proof of Theorem
1
follows by approximation arguments. Below, we provide details following closely the scheme described in
[
19
, Section 8]
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Throughout the proof we write
\lesssim
if
\leq
holds up to a multiplicative constant that depends only on
\Lambda,\nu,p
and
q
. We assume that
B_{2}\Subset\Omega
and show

(26)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{p}\lesssim\biggl(\fint_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\biggl(\fint_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{2}{3p-q}}.

Clearly, the conclusion follows from a standard scaling, translation and covering arguments.

Let
\varphi
be a non-negative, radially symmetric mollifier, i.e. it satisfies

\varphi\geq 0,\quad{\rm supp}\;\varphi\subset B_{1},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\,dx=1,\quad\varphi(\cdot)=\widetilde{\varphi}(|\cdot|)\quad\mbox{for some $\widetilde{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$}.

We introduce two regularization parameters
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
, and set

(27)

\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}:=u\ast\varphi_{\varepsilon},\quad F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(z):=F_{\delta}(z)+\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mu+\delta+|z|^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}},\quad F_{\delta}:=F\ast\varphi_{\delta},\quad\sigma_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon^{-1}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}^{q}}.

The integrand
F_{\varepsilon,\delta}
satisfies Assumption
2
for some
2\geq\mu_{\delta}>\mu>0
satisfying
\mu_{\delta}\to\mu
as
\delta\to 0
,
L_{\varepsilon,\delta}\lesssim L
,
\nu\lesssim\nu_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\lesssim\tilde{\nu}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\lesssim\sigma_{\varepsilon}
(see
[
19
, Section 8]
). Moreover, we note that
\partial F(z)\lesssim 1+|z|^{q}
(see (
23
)) implies
|F_{\delta}(z)-F(z)|\lesssim\delta(1+|z|^{q})
and thus

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1):\quad\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\|F_{\delta}(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})-F(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}=0.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})+\frac{(\mu+|\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}}{1+\varepsilon^{-1}+\|\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}^{q}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx

(28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{1+\varepsilon}}F(\nabla u)\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u)\,dx,

where we use in the last two inequality Jensen inequality in combination with
\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}=\nabla u\ast\varphi_{\varepsilon}
and dominated convergence in combination with
F(\nabla u)\in L^{1}(B_{2})
.
We denote by
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in W^{1,1}(B)
the unique function satisfying

(29)

\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\nabla v)\,dx\qquad\mbox{for all $v\in\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}+W_{0}^{1,1}(B)$.}

The bound
H_{\mu}(z)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq H_{\mu_{\delta}}(z)^{\frac{p}{2}}\lesssim F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(z)
and the minimality of
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
yield

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}H_{\mu}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1}}H_{\mu_{\delta}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\lesssim\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\,dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:uepsdelta}}}{{\leq}}\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx.

Since
F_{\varepsilon,\delta}
satisfies Assumption
2
(with
\nu\lesssim\nu_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
L_{\varepsilon,\delta}\lesssim L
), we obtain by Lemma
3

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{claim:L:apriorilipschitz}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}H_{\mu_{\delta}}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx+\biggl(\int_{B_{1}}H_{\mu_{\delta}}(|\nabla u|)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{2}{3p-q}}

(31)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{L:apriorLp}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx+\biggl(\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{2}{3p-q}}.

Estimates (
30
) and (
5
) in combination with (
5
) and
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}+W_{0}^{1,1}(B_{1})
imply that there exist as sequence
(\delta_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
with
\delta_{j}\to 0
and
u_{\varepsilon}\in\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}+W_{0}^{1,1}(B)
such that
u_{\varepsilon,\delta_{j}}\rightharpoonup u_{\varepsilon}
in
W^{1,p}(B_{1})
. Moreover, there exist
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
and
w\in u+W_{0}^{1,p}(B_{1})
such that
u_{\varepsilon_{k}}\rightharpoonup w
in
W^{1,p}(B_{1})
. The weak
∗
lower semicontinuity of norms (
5
) and (
5
) imply

(32)

\displaystyle\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{p}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{limsupFepsueps},\eqref{est:apriorilipschitzum}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u)\,dx+\biggl(\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{2}{3p-q}}

Finally, it remains to show that
u=w
. The convexity of
F
and
\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}}\rightharpoonup\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k}}
imply for given
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k}})\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}})\,dx+\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}|F(\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}})-F_{\delta_{j}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}})|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}}(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon_{k}})\,dx,

where we use in the last inequality the minimality of
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
,
|F(z)-F_{\delta}(z)|\lesssim\delta(1+|z|^{q})
and the fact that by definition of
F_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, we have
\sup_{j}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}^{q}<\infty
. Hence, sending

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla w)\,dx\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k}})\,dx\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}F_{\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{j}}(\nabla\overline{u}_{\varepsilon_{k}})\,dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{limsupFepsueps}}}{{\leq}}\int_{B_{1}}F(\nabla u)\,dx.

The above inequality combined with

and the strict convexity of

implies

and the desired estimate (

∎

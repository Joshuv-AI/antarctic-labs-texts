Subelliptic Operators on Weighted Folland-Stein Spaces
Hung-Lin Chiu

Abstract
. In this paper, we show that the sub-Laplacian of an asymptotically flat pseudo-hermitian manifold defined on a suitable weighted Folland-Stein spaces is an isomorphism. Similarly in dimension 5 5 a certain Dirac-type operator is shown to be an isomorphism, which is used to resolve the CR positive mass problem

1.
Introduction

The CR positive mass problem plays an essential role in CR geometry. As well known, we need a CR positive mass theorem to solve the CR Yamabe problem for the cases which either the CR dimension
n=1
or the CR manifold
M
is spherical with higher CR dimension. When
n=1
, this was shown by Cheng, Malchiodi and Yang
[
3
]
. On the other hand, when
n\geq 2
and
M
is spherical, this was finished by Cheng, Yang and the author
[
2
]
through showing that the developing map is injective. However in the case
n=2
, we need an extra condition on the growth rate of the Green’s function on the universal cover of
M
. So in the case
n=2
, the CR positive mass theorem is not really completed. In the paper
[
1
]
, Cheng and the author showed that for
n=2
,
M
being spherical, if moreover
M
has a spin structure, then we have the CR positive mass theorem built up through a spinorial approach.

Recall that in 1982, E. Witten described a proof of the positive mass theorem using spinors (see
[
8
,
7
]
). Applying a Weitzenbock-type formula to
\psi
satisfying
D^{2}\psi=0
and approaching a constant spinor at infinity and integrating after taking the inner product with
\psi
, we then pick up the
p
-mass from the boundary integral and obtain a Witten-type formula for the
p
-mass. So the non-negativity of
p
-mass follows. Therefore, for the CR positive mass theorem, it suffices to show that the square of the Dirac operator
D^{2}
on some suitable weighted Folland-Stein spaces is an isomorphism. In this paper we prove that both sublaplacian
\Delta_{b}
(see Theorem
3.2
) and
D^{2}
(see Theorem
3.3
) on suitable spaces are isomorphisms.

Operations on Weighted Folland-Stein Spaces

For basic material about the Heisenberg group, we refer the reader to Section
7
Appendix in
[
1
]
. Let
H_{n}
be the Heisenberg group and
Q=2n+2
be the homogeneous dimension of
H_{n}
. The summation convention applies and derivatives along
\mathring{e}_{a},1\leq a\leq 2n
, may be denoted by
subscripts
u_{a}=\mathring{e}_{a}u
. The sub-gradient
\mathring{\nabla}_{b}u=\sum_{a=1}^{2n}u_{a}\mathring{e}_{a}
. The sub-laplacian
\mathring{\Delta}_{b}u=\sum_{a=1}^{2n}(\mathring{e}_{a})^{2}u
.
Set

(2.1)

\rho=(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ \sigma=(1+\rho^{4})^{\frac{1}{4}},

Denote
B_{R}=B_{R}(0)
as the closed Heisenberg ball of radius
R
and center
0
. The annulus is
A_{R}=B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}
, and the exterior domain is

E_{R}=H_{n}\setminus B_{R}
.

On
H_{n}
, we have the dilation operator defined by

(2.2)

R(z,t)=(Rz,R^{2}t)

2.1.
The weighted Folland-Stein spaces

For
\delta\in\R
, we define the weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}_{\delta},L^{\prime p}_{\delta}
with weight
\delta\in\R
as the spaces of measurable functions in
L^{p}_{loc}(H_{n}),L^{p}_{loc}(H_{n}\setminus\{0\})
,
respectively, for which the norms defined by

(2.3)

\|u\|_{p,\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\int_{H_{n}}|\sigma^{-\delta}u|^{p}\sigma^{-Q}dV_{(z,t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},&\ \ p<\infty,\\
\ \ \textrm{ess sup}_{H_{n}}(\sigma^{-\delta}|u|),&\ \ p=\infty,\end{array}\right.

and

(2.4)

\|u\|^{\prime}_{p,\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\int_{H_{n}\setminus\{0\}}|\rho^{-\delta}u|^{p}\rho^{-Q}dV_{(z,t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},&\ \ p<\infty,\\
\ \ \textrm{ess sup}_{H_{n}\setminus\{0\}}(\rho^{-\delta}|u|),&\ \ p=\infty,\end{array}\right.

are finite, where
dV_{(z,t)}=\Theta\wedge(d\Theta)^{n}
and
\Theta=dt+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}(iz^{\beta}d\bar{z}^{\beta}-i\bar{z}^{\beta}dz^{\beta})
.

For
k\in\N\cup\{0\}
, the weighted Folland-Stein spaces
S^{p}_{k,\delta},S^{\prime p}_{k,\delta}
are the spaces of
u
such that

(2.5)

|\nabla^{j}u|\in L^{p}_{\delta-j},L^{\prime p}_{\delta-j},\ \ \textrm{for}\ 0\leq j\leq k,

respectively, with norms

(2.6)

\|u\|_{k,p,\delta}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|\nabla^{j}u\|_{p,\delta-j},

(2.7)

\|u\|^{\prime}_{k,p,\delta}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|\nabla^{j}u\|^{\prime}_{p,\delta-j}

where
\nabla,\nabla^{2},\cdots
denote the subdradient, subhession,
\cdots
for simplcity.

Observe that
C^{\infty}_{0}(H_{n}),C^{\infty}_{0}(H_{n}\setminus\{0\})
are dense in
S^{p}_{k,\delta},S^{\prime p}_{k,\delta}
, respectively, for
\delta<0
and
1\leq p<\infty
and that

L^{p}_{\delta}=L^{p}(H_{n})
for
\delta=-Q/p
.

2.2.
Global weighted inequalities

As R. Bartnik pointed out in
[
4
]
that the indexing chosen for the weights has the advantage that it directly describes the growth at infinity (see (
2.18
) below). In addition, the rescaled function, defined by

(2.8)

u_{R}(z,t)=u(R(z,t)),

will satisfy the estimates (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) which follow by a simple change of variables. We have

(2.9)

\|u_{R}\|^{\prime}_{k,p,\delta}=R^{\delta}\|u\|^{\prime}_{k,p,\delta}.

Similarly, with an obvious notation for norms over subsets of
H_{n}
, we have

(2.10)

\|u\|_{k,p,\delta;A_{R}}\approx R^{-\delta}\|u_{R}\|_{k,p,\delta;A_{1}},

for
R\geq 1
, where ”
\approx
” means ”is comparable to”, independent of
R\geq 1
. The estimate (
2.10
) is the key to proving global weighted inequalities from local inequalities.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty,\ \delta_{2}<\delta_{1}
and
u\in L^{q}_{\delta_{2}}
, then

(2.11)

\|u\|_{p,\delta_{1}}\leq c\|u\|_{q,\delta_{2}},

and hence
L^{q}_{\delta_{2}}\subset L^{p}_{\delta_{1}}
.

Proof.

The estimate (
2.11
) follows directly from the definition and Hölder inequality.
If
p=q<\infty
, we write

\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+Q}}=\frac{|u|^{q}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}q+Q}}\ \frac{1}{\sigma^{(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})q}}.

Then (
2.11
) holds in this case. If
p<q<\infty
, we write

\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+Q}}=\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}p+\frac{Qp}{q}}}\ \frac{1}{\sigma^{(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})p}+\frac{Q(q-p)}{q}}.

Then by Hölder’s inequality

\begin{split}\int_{E_{R}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+Q}}\ dV&\leq\left[\int_{E_{R}}\left(\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}p+\frac{Qp}{q}}}\right)^{q/p}\right]^{p/q}\left[\int_{E_{R}}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})p}+\frac{Q(q-p)}{q}}\right)^{q/(q-p)}\right]^{(q-p)/q}\\
&=\left[\int_{E_{R}}\left(\frac{|u|^{q}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}q+Q}}\right)dV\right]^{p/q}\left[\int_{E_{R}}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})\frac{pq}{q-p}+Q}}\right)dV\right]^{(q-p)/q},\end{split}

where the second term is integrable on
E_{R}
. Therefore (
2.11
) holds. Finally, suppose that
p<q=\infty
, then

\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+Q}}=\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}p}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})p+Q}}\leq\|u\|^{p}_{\infty,\delta_{2}}\ \frac{1}{\sigma^{(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})p+Q}},\ a.e.

Again that
\frac{1}{\sigma^{(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})p+Q}}
is integrable on
H_{n}
, we get (
2.11
). Suppose
p=q=\infty
. We fix a point, since
\sigma\geq 1
, we have that
(\sigma^{-1})^{x}
is a decreasing function, hence

(\sigma^{-1})^{\delta_{1}}\leq(\sigma^{-1})^{\delta_{2}}
. And it is easy to see that if

m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta_{2}}|u(z,t)|>M\}=0

then

m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta_{1}}|u(z,t)|>M\}=0.

Therefore

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\infty,\delta_{1}}&=ess\ sup(\sigma^{-\delta_{1}}|u|)\\
&=\inf\{M\ |\ m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta_{1}}|u(z,t)|>M\}=0\}\\
&\leq\inf\{M\ |\ m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta_{2}}|u(z,t)|>M\}=0\}\\
&=ess\ sup(\sigma^{-\delta_{2}}|u|)=\|u\|_{\infty,\delta_{2}},\end{split}

which gives (
2.11
). Finally, suppose that
p<q=\infty
, since

\begin{split}|\sigma^{-\delta_{1}}u^{p}|\sigma^{-Q}&=|\sigma^{-\frac{\delta_{2}}{p}}u\sigma^{-\delta_{1}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{p}}|^{p}\sigma^{-Q}\\
&=\sigma^{-\delta_{2}}|u|\sigma^{-Q-p\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}\\
&\leq M\frac{1}{\sigma^{Q+(p\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})}},\ \ \textrm{almost everywhere},\end{split}

we have

\begin{split}\int_{H_{n}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+Q}}dV_{(z,t)}&\leq\int_{H_{n}}\frac{M}{\sigma^{Q+(p\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})}}dV_{(z,t)}\\
&\leq C\ ess\ sup(\sigma^{-\delta_{2}}|u|).\end{split}

We thus complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.2

(
Hölder inequality).

If
u\in L^{q}_{\delta_{1}},v\in L^{r}_{\delta_{2}}
and
\delta=\delta_{1}+\delta_{2},\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r},1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
, then

(2.12)

\|uv\|_{p,\delta}\leq\|u\|_{q,\delta_{1}}\|v\|_{r,\delta_{2}},

Proof.

The estimate (
2.12
) follows directly from the definition and Hölder inequality.
If
1<p,q,r<\infty
, then we write

\frac{|uv|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta p+Q}}=\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+\frac{Qp}{q}}}\ \frac{|v|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}p+\frac{Qp}{r}}}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\int_{H_{n}}\frac{|uv|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta p+Q}}dV=\left(\int_{H_{n}}\left(\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+\frac{Qp}{q}}}\right)^{q/p}dV\right)^{p/q}\left(\int_{H_{n}}\left(\frac{|v|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}p+\frac{Qp}{r}}}\right)^{r/p}dV\right)^{p/r}.

This shows (
2.12
). If
p=q<\infty,\ r=\infty
, then

\frac{|uv|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta p+Q}}=\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+Q}}\ \frac{|v|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{2}p}}\leq\frac{|u|^{p}}{\sigma^{\delta_{1}p+Q}}\ \|v\|^{p}_{\infty,\delta_{2}},\ a.e.

This shows (
2.12
). The case
p=q<\infty,\ r=\infty
is similar. In the end, suppose that
p=q=r=\infty
. Let
M_{1},M_{2}
be two numbers such that

m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta_{1}}|u(z,t)|>M_{1}\}=0,\ m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta_{2}}|v(z,t)|>M_{2}\}=0.

Then we have

\sigma^{-\delta}|uv|=\sigma^{-\delta_{1}}|u|\cdot\sigma^{-\delta_{2}}|v|\leq M_{1}M_{2},\ \ \textrm{almost everywhere},

that is,

m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta}|uv|>M_{1}M_{2}\}=0,

which implies

(2.13)

\begin{split}\|uv\|_{\infty,\delta}&=ess\ sup(\sigma^{-\delta}|uv|)\\
&=\inf\{M\ |\ m\{(z,t)\ |\ \sigma^{-\delta}|uv|>M\}=0\}\\
&\leq M_{1}M_{2},\ \ \textrm{for any such}\ M_{1},M_{2}.\end{split}

Then (
2.12
) follows. We thus complete the proof.
∎

The following two estimates, interpolation inequality and Sobolev inequality, follow from the technique of rescaling and applying local estimate:

Proposition 2.3

(
Interpolation inequality).

For any
\varepsilon>0
and any non-negative integer
s
, there is a constant
C=C(Q,p,\delta,s)
such that

(2.14)

\|u\|_{s+1,p,\delta}\leq\varepsilon\|u\|_{s+2,p,\delta}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{0,p,\delta},

for all
u\in S^{p}_{s+2,\delta},1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

The estimates (
2.14
) follows from the technique of rescaling and applying local estimates. By the usual interpolation inequality, we have, for
1\leq p<\infty
,

(2.15)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{s+1,p,\delta;A_{R}}&\leq CR^{-\delta}\|u\|_{s+1,p,\delta;A_{1}}\\
&\leq CR^{-\delta}\left(\varepsilon\|u_{R}\|_{s+2,p,\delta;A_{1}}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\|u_{R}\|_{0,p,\delta;A_{1}}\right)\\
&\leq\varepsilon\|u\|_{s+2,p,\delta;A_{R}}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{0,p,\delta;A_{R}},\end{split}

where
C
is a constant independent of
A_{R}
, for
R\geq 1
.
Therefore, writing
u=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}u_{j}
with
u_{0}=u|_{B_{1}},u_{j}=u|_{A_{2^{j-1}}}
, we see that
(A\preceq B\ \textrm{means}\ A\leq(\textrm{const})B)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{s+1,p,\delta}&=\sum_{k=0}^{s+1}\|\nabla^{k}u\|_{p,\delta-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{s+1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|\nabla^{k}u_{j}\|^{p}_{p,\delta-k}\right)^{1/p}\\
&\preceq\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|\nabla^{k}u_{j}\|^{p}_{p,\delta-k}\right)^{1/p}\preceq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|u_{j}\|^{p}_{s+1,p,\delta}\right)^{1/p}\\
&\preceq\left(\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|u_{j}\|^{p}_{s+2,p,\delta}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|u_{j}\|^{p}_{0,p,\delta}\right)^{1/p},\ \ (\textrm{by}\ \eqref{opw121}),\\
&\preceq\varepsilon\|u\|_{s+2,p,\delta}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{0,p,\delta}.\end{split}

This completes the proof. We remark here that the interpolation inequality also holds when
p=\infty
. But we don’t need it in this paper.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(
Sobolev inequality).

If
u\in S^{q}_{k,\delta}
, then we have

(2.16)

\|u\|_{\frac{Qp}{Q-kp},\delta}\leq C\|u\|_{k,q,\delta},

provided that
Q-kp>0
and
1<p\leq q\leq\frac{Qp}{Q-kp}
.

We also have that

(2.17)

\|u\|_{\infty,\delta}\leq C\|u\|_{k,p,\delta},\ \ \textrm{if}\ Q-kp<0.

and in fact

(2.18)

|u(z,t)|=o(\rho^{\delta})\ \ \textrm{as}\ \ \rho\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that
p^{*}=Qp/(Q-kp)<\infty
, we have

(2.19)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{p^{*},\delta;A_{R}}&=\left(\int_{A_{R}}(\sigma^{-\delta}u)^{p^{*}}\sigma^{-Q}dV_{(z,t)}\right)^{1/p^{*}}\\
&\leq CR^{-\delta}\|u_{R}\|_{p^{*},\delta;A_{1}}\\
&\leq CR^{-\delta}\|u_{R}\|_{k,q,\delta;A_{1}},\end{split}

where, for the last inequality, we have used the usual Sobolev inequality applied to
A_{1}
, and by Hölder’s inequality. Rescaling gives

(2.20)

\|u\|_{p^{*},\delta;A_{R}}\leq C\|u\|_{k,q,\delta;A_{R}}.

Now using the same notation
u_{j}
as above, we see that

(2.21)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{p^{*},\delta}&=\left(\int_{H_{n}}\|u\|^{p^{*}}\sigma^{-\delta p^{*}-Q}dV_{(z,t)}\right)^{1/p^{*}}\\
&=\left(\sum_{j-0}^{\infty}\int_{A_{2^{j-1}}}|u|^{p^{*}}\sigma^{-\delta p^{*}-Q}dV_{(z,t)}\right)^{1/p^{*}}=\left(\sum_{j-0}^{\infty}\|u\|^{p^{*}}_{p^{*},\delta;A_{2^{j-1}}}\right)^{1/p^{*}}\\
&\leq C\left(\sum_{j-0}^{\infty}\|u\|^{p^{*}}_{k,q,\delta;A_{2^{j-1}}}\right)^{1/p^{*}},\ \ (\textrm{by}\ \eqref{opw131})\\
&\leq C\left(\sum_{j-0}^{\infty}\|u\|^{q}_{k,q,\delta;A_{2^{j-1}}}\right)^{1/q},\ \ q\leq p^{*}\\
&=C\|u\|_{k,q,\delta}.\end{split}

Therefore, we obtain (
2.16
). The same rescaling argument implies that

(2.22)

\sup_{A_{R}}|u|\sigma^{-\delta}=\|u\|_{\infty,\delta,A_{R}}\leq C\|u\|_{k,p,\delta;A_{R}},

which gives (
2.17
). Since
\|u\|_{k,p,\delta}<\infty
, we have

\|u\|_{k,p,\delta;A_{R}}\rightarrow 0\ \ \ \textrm{as}\ R\rightarrow\infty,

that is,

\|u\|_{k,p,\delta;A_{R}}o(1)\ \ \ \textrm{as}\ R\rightarrow\infty,

which gives (
2.18
), by means of (
2.22
). We therefore complete the proof.

∎

2.3.
Weighted estimates for sub-elliptic operators

The rescaling argument and the standard local estimates gives estimates in weighted spaces for sub-elliptic operators whose coefficients are well behaved
at infinity.

Definition 2.5
.

The operator
u\rightarrow Pu
defined by

(2.23)

Pu=a^{ij}(z,t)\mathring{e}_{i}\mathring{e}_{j}u+b^{i}(z,t)\mathring{e}_{i}u+c(z,t)u

will be said to be asymptotic to
\mathring{\Delta}_{b}
(at rate
\tau
) if there exists
Q<q<\infty
and
\tau\geq 0
and constants
C_{1},\lambda
such that

(2.24)

\lambda|\zeta|^{2}\leq a^{ij}(z,t)\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\leq\lambda^{-1}|\zeta|^{2},\ \ \textrm{for all}\ (z,t)\in H_{n},\ \zeta=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\zeta_{j}\mathring{e}_{j}\in\xi,

and

(2.25)

\|a^{ij}-\delta_{ij}\|_{1,q,-\tau}+\|b^{i}\|_{0,q,-1-\tau}+\|c\|_{0,q/2,-2-\tau}\leq C_{1},

where
\delta_{ij}
corresponds to the standard sub-laplacian
\mathring{\Delta}_{b}
on
H_{n}
.

It is clear that if
P
is asymptotic to
\mathring{\Delta}_{b}
, then the map
P:S^{p}_{2,\delta}\rightarrow S^{p}_{0,\delta-2}
is bounded for
1\leq p\leq q
and
\delta\in\R
. In fact the following weighted estimate holds.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
P
is asymptotic to
\mathring{\Delta}_{b},1<p\leq q
and
\delta\in\R
. Then there is a constant
C=C(Q,p,q,s,\delta,C_{1},\lambda)
such that if
u\in L^{p}_{\delta}

and
Pu\in S^{p}_{s,\delta-2}
, then
u\in S^{p}_{s+2,\delta}
. Moreover, we have

(2.26)

\|u\|_{s+2,p,\delta}\leq C(\|Pu\|_{s,p,\delta-2}+\|u\|_{p,\delta}).

Proof.

Sub-elliptic regularity applies to show that
u\in S^{p}_{s+2,loc}
, and the remaining conclusions follow from the usual interior
L^{p}
estimates and the rescaling technique.
∎

Observe that the same argument give the estimate (
2.26
) with the
S^{\prime p}_{k,\delta}
norms instead. We now investigate the Fredholm properties of
P
. We need the following lemma:

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix
p\in(1,\infty),p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
, and let
a,b\in\R
be such that
a+b>0
. Suppose that
K^{\prime}(x,y)
is the Kernel

K(x,y)=|x|^{-a}|y^{-1}x|^{-Q+a+b}|y|^{-b},\ \ \textrm{for}\ x\neq y,

For
u\in L^{p}(H_{n})
define

Ku(x)=\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)u(y)dV_{y}.

Then there is a constant
c=c(n,p,a,b)
such that

\|Ku\|_{L^{p}}\leq c\|u\|_{L^{p}}

if and only if
a<Q/p
and
b<Q/p^{\prime}
.

Proof.

To show that the conditions
a<Q/p
and
b<Q/p^{\prime}
are necessary, suppose that
K
is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(H_{n})
. We first define the function

v(x)=\int_{|y|\leq 1}K(x,y)dV_{y}.

Then we have

v(x)=\int_{|y|\leq 1}K(x,y)dV_{y}=\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)u(y)dV_{y},

where

u(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&|y|\leq 1\\
0&|y|>1\end{array}\right.

Since
u(y)\in L^{p}(H_{n})
and
K
is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(H_{n})
, we see that
v(x)
is in
L^{p}(H_{n})
. Next, we study the behavior of
v(x)

at infinity. By the triangle inequality (see
[
5
]
), there exists a constant
\gamma\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{|y|}{|x|}\leq\frac{|y^{-1}x|}{|x|}\leq\gamma(1+\frac{|y|}{|x|}).

Therefore, for
|y|\leq 1
, if
|x|
is large enough, then
K(x,y)\approx\frac{1}{|x|^{Q-b}|y|^{b}}
, and hence
v(x)
behaves like a constant multiple of
|x|^{-Q+b}
. Then
v(x)
belongs to
L^{p}(H_{n})
only if
p(Q-b)>Q
, i.e. only if
b<Q/p^{\prime}
. On the other hand, we define the function
w(y)
by

w(y)=\int_{|x|\leq 1}K(x,y)dV_{x}.

Then, by Fubini’s Theorem and Hölder’s inequality, for
u(y)\in L^{p}(H_{n})
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{H_{n}}w(y)u(y)dV_{y}&=\int_{H_{n}}\left(\int_{|x|\leq 1}K(x,y)u(y)dV_{x}\right)dV_{y}\\
&=\int_{|x|\leq 1}\left(\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)u(y)dV_{y}\right)dV_{x}\\
&\leq\|Ku(x)\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\left(\int_{|x|<1}1\ dV_{x}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\\
&\leq\omega_{n}^{1/p^{\prime}}\|Ku(x)\|_{L^{p}(H_{n})}\leq c\ \omega_{n}^{1/p^{\prime}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(H_{n})},\end{split}

for some constant
c>0
, where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball
B_{1}
. That is,
w(y)
is a bounded linear functional on
L^{p}(H_{n})
, and hence belongs to
L^{p}(H_{n})
. As we discuss the behavior of
v(x)
above, it is easy to see that, for large
|y|
,
w(y)
behaves like a constant multiple of
|y|^{-Q+a}
. Then
w(y)
belongs to
L^{p}(H_{n})
only if
a<Q/p
.

To show that the conditions
a<Q/p
and
b<Q/p^{\prime}
are sufficient, note first that it may be assumed without loss of generality that both
a
and
b
are non-negative. (If, say,
a
is negative, then
b
is positive, and from the triangle inequality
|x|\leq\gamma(|y|+|y^{-1}x|)
for some
\gamma\geq 1
, we have

\frac{|x|}{|y^{-1}x|}\leq\gamma\left(1+\frac{|y|}{|y^{-1}x|}\right),

which implies that

\left(\frac{|x|}{|y^{-1}x|}\right)^{-a}\leq C\left(1+\frac{|y|}{|y^{-1}x|}\right)^{-a},

for some
C>0
. It is seen that

K(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q-b}|y|^{b}}+\frac{C}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q-a-b}|y|^{a+b}}.

The boundedness of
K
in this case then follows immediately from the result in the case in which both
a
and
b
are non-nagative.) And it suffices to show that

(2.27)

\int_{H_{n}}\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)u(y)v(x)dV_{y}dV_{x}\leq C\left(\int_{H_{n}}u(y)^{p}dV_{y}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{H_{n}}v(x)^{p^{\prime}}dV_{x}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}},

hence

Ku(x)=\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)u(y)dV_{y}\in(L^{p^{\prime}})^{*}(H_{n})=L^{p}(H_{n})

with operator norm less than
C\left(\int_{H_{n}}u(y)^{p}dV_{y}\right)^{1/p}
.
By Hölder’s inequailty, and noticing that
K(x,y)
is homogeneous of degree
-Q
, we have

(2.28)

\begin{split}&\ \ \ \ \int_{H_{n}}\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)u(y)v(x)dV_{y}dV_{x}\\
&=\int_{H_{n}}\int_{H_{n}}K^{1/p}u(y)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/pp^{\prime}}K^{1/p^{\prime}}v(x)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{Q/pp^{\prime}}dV_{y}dV_{x}\\
&\leq\left[\int_{H_{n}}\int_{H_{n}}\left(K^{1/p}u(y)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/pp^{\prime}}\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}\left[\int_{H_{n}}\int_{H_{n}}\left(K^{1/p^{\prime}}v(x)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{Q/pp^{\prime}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right]^{1/p^{\prime}}\\
&=P^{1/p}Q^{1/p^{\prime}},\end{split}

where

(2.29)

\begin{split}P&=\int_{H_{n}}\int_{H_{n}}\left(K(x,y)u^{p}(y)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/p^{\prime}}\right)dV_{y}dV_{x}\\
&=\int_{H_{n}}u^{p}(y)\left(\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/p^{\prime}}dV_{x}\right)dV_{y}\\
\end{split}

and

(2.30)

\begin{split}Q&=\int_{H_{n}}\int_{H_{n}}\left(K(x,y)v^{p^{\prime}}(x)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{Q/p}\right)dV_{y}dV_{x}\\
&=\int_{H_{n}}v^{p^{\prime}}(x)\left(\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{Q/p}dV_{x}\right)dV_{x}\\
\end{split}

From (
2.28
),(
2.29
),(
2.30
), we obtain (
2.27
), provided that the integral
\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/p^{\prime}}dV_{x}
has an upper bound which is independent of
y
and
\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{Q/p}dV_{y}
has an upper bound which is independent of
x
. Actually, fixing
y
and by a change of variables, we have

(2.31)

\begin{split}\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/p^{\prime}}dV_{x}&=\int_{H_{n}}|y|^{-Q}K(\frac{x}{|y|},\frac{y}{|y|})\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/p^{\prime}}dV_{x}\\
&=\int_{H_{n}}K(z,y^{*})|z|^{-Q/p^{\prime}}dV_{z},\ z=\frac{x}{|y|},\ \textrm{and}\ y^{*}=\frac{y}{|y|}\\
&=\int_{H_{n}}\frac{1}{|z|^{a+\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}}|(y^{*})^{-1}z|^{Q-(a+b)}}dV_{z},\end{split}

where

(2.32)

\frac{1}{|z|^{a+\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}}|(y^{*})^{-1}z|^{Q-(a+b)}}\approx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{|z|^{a+\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}}}&,x\approx 0\\
\frac{1}{|(y^{*})^{-1}z|^{Q-(a+b)}}&,x\approx 1\\
\frac{1}{|z|^{Q+\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}-b}}&,x\approx\infty\end{array}\right.

Since
a<\frac{Q}{p}
and
b<\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}
, we have that
0<a+\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}<Q,\ 0<Q-(a+b)<Q
and
Q+\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}-b>Q
, thus the integral
\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^{Q/p^{\prime}}dV_{x}
converges, depending on
y^{*}
which belongs to the unit sphere. Therefore, it has an upper bound which is independent of
y
. Similarly,
\int_{H_{n}}K(x,y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{Q/p}dV_{y}
has an upper bound which is independent of
x
. Therefore, we have completed the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose that
\delta<0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
s
is a non-negative integer. Then the map

(2.33)

\mathring{\Delta}_{b}:S^{\prime p}_{s+2,\delta}\rightarrow S^{\prime p}_{s,\delta-2}

is an isomorphism and there is a constant

(2.34)

\|u\|^{\prime}_{s+2,p,\delta}\leq C\|\mathring{\Delta}_{b}u\|^{\prime}_{s,p,\delta-2}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove (
2.34
) for
s=0
. We first assume that
-2n<\delta<0
and show that the distribution inverse has convolution kernel

(2.35)

c_{0}K_{0}(x,y)=c_{0}\Phi_{0}(y^{-1}x)=|y^{-1}x|^{-2n}.

Let us first show that (

) defines a bounded operator from
S^{\prime p}_{0,\delta-2}
to
S^{\prime p}_{0,\delta}
. Lemma
2.7
shows that the kernel

K(x,y)=|x|^{-\delta-\frac{2n+2}{p}}K_{0}(x,y)|y|^{\delta-2+\frac{2n+2}{p}}

when

(2.36)

\begin{split}\|K_{0}u\|^{\prime}_{p,\delta}&=\left(\int_{H_{n}\setminus\{0\}}|K_{0}u(x)|^{p}|x|^{-\delta p-Q}dV_{x}\right)^{1/p}\\
&=\left(\int_{H_{n}\setminus\{0\}}\left|\int_{H_{n}\setminus\{0\}}|x|^{-\delta-\frac{Q}{p}}K_{0}(x,y)u(y)dV_{y}\right|^{p}dV_{x}\right)^{1/p}\\
&=\left(\int_{H_{n}\setminus\{0\}}\left|(KU)(x)\right|^{p}dV_{x}\right)^{1/p},\ \ \textrm{where}\ U(y)=u(y)|y|^{-(\delta-2)-\frac{Q}{p}}\\

&=c\|u\|^{\prime}_{p,\delta-2}.\end{split}

Thus we have shown that

-2n<\delta<0
.
Recall that we have
K_{0}\mathring{\Delta}_{b}u=u
, for all

, so the boundness of
K_{0}

gives

\|u\|^{\prime}_{p,\delta}\leq C\|\mathring{\Delta}u\|^{\prime}_{p,\delta-2},\ \ \ \textrm{for all}\ u\in S^{\prime p}_{0,\delta},

since
C^{\infty}_{0}(H_{n}\setminus\{0\})
is dense. This and estimate (
2.26
) yield (
2.34
).

Now suppose that
\{u_{j}\}\subset S^{\prime p}_{2,\delta},\{f_{j}\}\subset S^{\prime p}_{0,\delta-2}
are sequences such that

and

2.34

is a Cauchy sequence and hence is convergent
to

, since

This map thus has closed range. On the other hand, by (

), the kernel of this map is trivial. Also, by (
2.34
), we have

K_{0}f\in S^{\prime p}_{2,\delta}

and this establishes the isomorphism for the cases

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathring{\Delta}_{b}:&S^{\prime p}_{s+2,\delta_{1}}&\longrightarrow&S^{\prime p}_{s,\delta_{1}-2}\\
&\uparrow&&\uparrow\\

\delta_{2}

The scale-broken estimate (
2.38
) below is the key to proving Fredholm properties.

Theorem 2.9
.

Suppose that
P
is asymptotic to
\mathring{\Delta}_{b}
at rate
\tau>0
and
Q<q<\infty
, and
\delta<0
,
s
is a non-negative integer.
Then, for

, the map

has finite-dimensional kernel and closed range. In addition, for any
u\in S^{p}_{s+2,\delta}
, we have

(2.38)

\|u\|_{s+2,p,\delta}\leq C(\|Pu\|_{s,p,\delta-2}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}),

where
C,R
are constants depending on
P,\delta,Q,p,q
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove (
2.38
) for
s=0
. Define the operator norm

\|P-\mathring{\Delta}_{b}\|_{op}=\sup\{\|(P-\mathring{\Delta}_{b})u\|_{p,\delta-2}\ :\ u\in S^{p}_{2,\delta},\ \|u\|_{2,p,\delta}=1\}

and let
\|\cdot\|_{op,R}
denote the same norm restricted to function with support in
E_{R}=H_{n}\setminus B_{R}
.
For (
2.38
), first, we claim that

(2.39)

\|P-\mathring{\Delta}_{b}\|_{op,R}=o(1),\ \ \textrm{as}\ R\rightarrow\infty.

If
\supp{u}\subset E_{R}
, since
Q<q
,

(2.40)

\begin{split}&\|(P-\mathring{\Delta}_{b})u\|_{p,\delta-2}\\
\leq&\|(a^{ij}(z,t)-\delta_{ij})\partial_{ij}u+b^{i}(z,t)\partial_{i}u+c(z,t)u\|_{p,\delta-2}\\
\leq&\|(a^{ij}(z,t)-\delta_{ij})\partial_{ij}u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}}+\|b^{i}(z,t)\partial_{i}u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}}+\|c(z,t)u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}},\end{split}

where

(2.41)

\|(a^{ij}(z,t)-\delta_{ij})\partial_{ij}u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}}\leq\sup_{|(z,t)|>R}\left\{|a^{ij}(z,t)-\delta_{ij}|\right\}\|\partial_{ij}u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}};

(2.42)

\begin{split}\|b^{i}(z,t)\partial_{i}u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}}&\leq\|b^{i}\|_{Q,-1;E_{R}}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{\frac{Qp}{Q-p},\delta-1},\ \ \textrm{by}\ \eqref{opw11}\\

and, similarly, we have

(2.43)

\|c(z,t)u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}}\leq C\|c\|_{q/2,-2-\tau;E_{R}}\|u\|_{2,p,\delta}.

Substituting (
2.41
),(
2.42
) and (
2.43
) into (
2.40
), we obtain

\begin{split}\|(P-\mathring{\Delta}_{b})u\|_{p,\delta-2}&\leq\sup_{|(z,t)|>R}\left\{|a^{ij}(z,t)-\delta_{ij}|\right\}\|\partial_{ij}u\|_{p,\delta-2;E_{R}}\\
&+C\|b^{i}\|_{q,-1-\tau;E_{R}}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{1,p,\delta-1}+C\|c\|_{q/2,-2-\tau;E_{R}}\|u\|_{2,p,\delta},\end{split}

and hence (
2.39
) holds.

Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2})
be a patch function,
0\leq\chi\leq 1,\chi=1
in
B_{1}
, and set
\chi_{R}(x)=\chi(R^{-1}x)
. Writting

with

a constant to be determined, the sharp estimate (
2.34

\begin{split}\|u_{\infty}\|_{2,p,\delta}&\leq C\|\mathring{\Delta}_{b}u_{\infty}\|_{0,p,\delta-2}\\

in which the second term of the right hand side can be absorbed into the left hand side, for

sufficiently large, and
we estimate

\begin{split}\|Pu_{\infty}\|_{0,p,\delta-2}&=\|Pu-Pu_{0}\|_{0,p,\delta-2}\\
&\leq\|Pu\|_{0,p,\delta-2}+\|2a^{ij}(\partial_{i}u)(\partial_{j}\chi_{R})+(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\chi_{R}+b^{i}\partial_{i}\chi_{R})u\|_{0,p,\delta-2;A_{R}}\\

We therefore get

for

\{u_{j}\}

∎

.

:(S^{p}_{0,\delta-2})^{*}=S^{p^{\prime}}_{2-Q-\delta}

∎

3.
Asymptotically Flat Pseudo-hermitian Manifolds

Definition 3.1
.

A
(2n+1)
-dimensional pseudo-hermitian manifold
(N,J,\theta)
is said to be asymptotically flat pseudo-hermitian if
N=N_{0}\cup N_{\infty}
, with
N_{0}
compact and
N_{\infty}
diffemorphic to
H_{n}\setminus B_{\rho_{0}}
in which
(J,\theta)
is close to
(\mathring{J},\mathring{\theta})
in the sense that

(3.1)

\begin{split}\theta&=(1+c_{n}A\rho^{-2n}+O(\rho^{-2n-1}))\mathring{\theta}+O(\rho^{-2n-1})_{\beta}dz^{\beta}+O(\rho^{-2n-1})_{\bar{\beta}}dz^{\bar{\beta}};\\
\theta^{\alpha}&=O(\rho^{-2n-1})\mathring{\theta}+O(\rho^{-2n-2})_{\bar{\beta}}{}^{\alpha}dz^{\bar{\beta}}+(1+\tilde{c}_{n}A\rho^{-2n}+O(\rho^{-2n-1}))\sqrt{2}dz^{\alpha},\end{split}

for some
A\in\R
and a unitary co-frame
\theta^{\alpha}
in coordinates
(z^{\beta},z^{\bar{\beta}},t)
(called asymptotic coordinates) for
N_{\infty}
on which
\rho=((\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}|z^{\beta}|^{2})^{2}+t^{4})^{1/4}
is defined. We also require the Tanaka-Webster scalar curvature
W\in L^{1}(N)
.

Suppose that
(M,J)
is a
spherical
in a neighborhood of a point
x\in M
. If we blow up at
x
through the Green’s function of the CR invariant sub-Laplacian

G_{x}=\frac{a_{n}}{2\pi}\rho^{-2n}+A_{x}+O(\rho),

which is expressed in terms of the CR normal coordinates, then we obtain an asymptotically flat pseudo-hermitian manifold (see
[
1
]
for more explanation). We denote such a manifold by
N(x)
. In such a case, we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\theta&=(1+c_{n}A_{x}\rho^{-2n}+O(\rho^{-2n-1}))\mathring{\theta};\\
\theta^{\alpha}&=O(\rho^{-2n-1})\mathring{\theta}+(1+\tilde{c}_{n}A_{x}\rho^{-2n}+O(\rho^{-2n-1}))\sqrt{2}dz^{\alpha},\end{split}

where
c_{n}=\frac{4\pi}{na_{n}}
and
\tilde{c}_{n}=\frac{2\pi}{na_{n}}
. Let
\{Z_{\alpha},Z_{\bar{\alpha}},T\}
be the frame dual to
\{\theta^{\alpha},\theta^{\bar{\alpha}},\theta\}
. It is easy to see that

(3.3)

Z_{\alpha}=(1-\tilde{c}_{n}A_{x}\rho^{-2n}+O(\rho^{-2n-1}))\mathring{Z}_{\alpha}+O(\rho^{-2n-2})_{\alpha}{}^{\bar{\beta}}\mathring{Z}_{\bar{\beta}}.

On the other hand, from the structure equations of pseudo-hermitian manifolds, we have the asymptotic behavior of the pseudo-hermitian connection forms

(3.4)

\theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=O(\rho^{-2n-2})\mathring{\theta}+B_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}{}_{\gamma}dz^{\gamma}+C_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}{}_{\bar{\gamma}}dz^{\bar{\gamma}},

where for any fixed
\alpha
, we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}C_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}{}_{\bar{\gamma}}&=O(\rho^{-2n-2}),\ \textrm{for}\ \beta\neq\alpha,\gamma\neq\alpha\\
C_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}&=\frac{-inc_{n}A_{x}z^{\beta}\omega}{\rho^{2n+4}}+O(\rho^{-2n-2}),\ \textrm{for}\ \beta\neq\alpha\\
C_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\bar{\gamma}}&=\frac{-in\tilde{c}_{n}A_{x}z^{\gamma}\omega}{\rho^{2n+4}}+O(\rho^{-2n-2}),\ \textrm{for}\ \gamma\neq\alpha\\
C_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}&=\frac{-in(c_{n}+\tilde{c}_{n})A_{x}z^{\alpha}\omega}{\rho^{2n+4}}+O(\rho^{-2n-2}),\\
\end{split}

and
B_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}{}_{\gamma}=-\overline{C_{\beta}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\bar{\gamma}}}
. Therefore, we have

(3.6)

\Delta_{b}=\left(1+\frac{4\tilde{c}_{n}A_{x}}{\rho^{2n}}\right)\mathring{\Delta}_{b}+\textrm{higher decay order},

which implies that
\Delta_{b}
is asymptotic to
\mathring{\Delta}_{b}
for some
q
with
Q<q<\infty
. We remark that if the manifold
(M,J)
is not spherical, then, in general, formula (
3.6
) does not hold.

As used in Corollary
2.11
, the strong maximum principle implies the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
(M,J)
is a CR manifold which is

3.1.
The Weitzenbock formula for
n=2

In this subsection, we will give an isomorphism theorem for Dirac operators on spinor bundles. More details and notations on the geometry of context of spinors here may be found in
[
1
]
. Suppose
(M,J,\theta)
is a spherical CR manifold with a spin structure
Spin(\xi)
on the contact bundle
\xi=\ker{\theta}
, together with the Levi metric
L_{\theta}
. Let
\Lambda_{R}^{k}(n)
and
\Lambda_{C}^{k}(n)
denote the real and complex vector spaces respectively, spanned by
\{\omega^{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega^{j_{k}}\ |\ 1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq n\}
(view symbols
\omega^{1},\cdots,\omega^{n}
as independent vectors).
Recall, from
[
1
]
, that there is a canonical representation
\mathcal{N}
of the Clifford algebra
C_{2n}(-1)
such that
\Lambda_{C}^{*}(n)
is an irreducible
C_{2n}(-1)
-modules, and
\Lambda_{C}^{even}(n),\Lambda_{C}^{odd}(n)
are two irreducible
Spin(2n)
-modules. We hence have the following three associated vector bundles:

S=Spin(\xi)\times_{\mathcal{N}}\Lambda_{C}^{*}(n),\ S^{+}=Spin(\xi)\times_{\mathcal{N}}\Lambda_{C}^{even}(n),\ S^{-}=Spin(\xi)\times_{\mathcal{N}}\Lambda_{C}^{odd}(n),

which are called spinor bundles. On each spinor bundle there is associated a unique spin connection
\nabla
, constructed from the pseudo-hermitian connection, and hence a Dirac operator, which is denoted by
D_{\xi}
. We have a Weitzenbock-type formula as follows:

D_{\xi}^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+W-2\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}e_{\beta}e_{n+\beta}\nabla_{T},

where
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{2n}\}
is a local orthonormal frame field such that
e_{n+\beta}=Je_{\beta}
for
1\leq\beta\leq n
. Fortunately, due to some algebraic property in the case
n=2
, we reduce the formula to

(3.8)

D_{\xi}^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+W,\ \ \ \textrm{on}\ S^{+}

It is this formula that makes Witten’s approach work on this geometry. Actually we have the following isomorphism theorem for Dirac operators.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
n=2
and suppose that
(M,J)
is a CR manifold which is
spherical
in a neighborhood of a point
x\in M
. We consider the blow up manifold
N(x)
at this point
x
. Suppose that
\frac{q}{q-1}<p\leq q\ (q
is specified as in Theorem
3.2
)
and
-2n<\delta<0
. Then

(3.9)

D^{2}_{\xi}:S^{p}_{s+2,\delta}(S^{+})\rightarrow S^{p}_{s,\delta-2}(S^{+})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\varphi=\varphi^{\lambda}s_{\lambda}
be a spinor spanned by a frame field of spinors
\{s_{\lambda}\}
. We compute

(3.10)

\begin{split}\nabla^{*}\nabla\varphi&=-\sum_{a=1}^{2n}\nabla^{2}_{e_{a},e_{a}}\varphi\\
&=-\sum_{a=1}^{2n}\left(\nabla_{e_{a}}\nabla_{e_{a}}\varphi-\nabla_{\nabla_{e_{a}}e_{a}}\varphi\right)\\
&=-\sum_{a=1}^{2n}(e_{a}e_{a}\varphi^{\lambda}-(\nabla_{e_{a}}e_{a})\varphi^{\lambda})s_{\lambda}+2(e_{a}\varphi^{\lambda})\nabla_{e_{a}}s_{\lambda}+\varphi^{\lambda}\left(\nabla_{e_{a}}\nabla_{e_{a}}s_{\lambda}-\nabla_{\nabla_{e_{a}}e_{a}}s_{\lambda}\right)\\
&=-(\Delta_{b}\varphi^{\lambda})s_{\lambda}+\ \textrm{l.o.t.},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\Delta_{b}f&=tr(\nabla df)=e_{a}e_{a}f-(\nabla_{e_{a}}e_{a})f,\ \ \textrm{for a function}\ f\\
\textrm{l.o.t.}&=2(e_{a}\varphi^{\lambda})\nabla_{e_{a}}s_{\lambda}+\varphi^{\lambda}\left(\nabla_{e_{a}}\nabla_{e_{a}}s_{\lambda}-\nabla_{\nabla_{e_{a}}e_{a}}s_{\lambda}\right).\end{split}

Therefore the principal part of each component of
D_{\xi}^{2}
is the sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{b}
, which is asymptotic to
\mathring{\Delta}_{b}
at rate
\tau>0
.
Therefore,
D_{\xi}^{2}
satisfies the scale-broken estimate (
2.38
), and hence is Fredholm with adjoint

D^{2}_{\xi}=D_{\xi}^{*2}:S^{p^{\prime}}_{s+2,2-Q-\delta}(S^{+})\rightarrow S^{p^{\prime}}_{s,-Q-\delta}(S^{+}).

On the other hand, from (
2.38
) again, we see that
\ker{(D_{\xi}^{2},\delta)}\subset C^{\infty}(N(x))
, thus if
\varphi\in\ker{(D_{\xi}^{2},\delta)}
, then
|\varphi|^{2}\rightarrow 0
at infinity and from (
3.8
) we have

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{b}|\varphi|^{2}=|\nabla_{b}\varphi|^{2}+W|\varphi|^{2}\geq 0.

The strong maximum principle implies that
|\varphi|^{2}=0
. Since that
-2n<\delta<0
if and only if
-2n<2-Q-\delta<0
, this shows that for
-2n<\delta<0
, both
\ker{D_{\xi}^{2}}
and
\ker{D_{\xi}^{*2}}
are trivial, and hence
D_{\xi}^{2}
is an isomorphism.
∎

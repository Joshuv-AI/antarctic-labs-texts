Linear-quadratic stochastic Volterra controls I: Causal feedback strategies
Yushi Hamaguchi, Tianxiao Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we formulate and investigate the notion of causal feedback strategies arising in linear-quadratic control problems for stochastic Volterra integral equations (SVIEs) with singular and non-convolution-type coefficients. We show that there exists a unique solution, which we call the causal feedback solution, to the closed-loop system of a controlled SVIE associated with a causal feedback strategy. Furthermore, introducing two novel equations named a Type-II extended backward stochastic Volterra integral equation and a Lyapunov–Volterra equation, we prove a duality principle and a representation formula for a quadratic functional of controlled SVIEs in the framework of causal feedback strategies.



Keywords:
Linear-quadratic control; stochastic Volterra integral equation; causal feedback strategy.

Introduction

Linear-quadratic (LQ) control problems are special classes of optimal control problems described by a linear state dynamics and a quadratic cost functional. In the continuous time setting, the state dynamics is assumed to be governed by a controlled differential/integral equation. In this paper, we study controlled linear stochastic Volterra integral equations (SVIEs) of the following form:

\begin{split}&X(t)=x(t)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(t,s)X(s)+B(t,s)u(s)+b(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(t,s)X(s)+D(t,s)u(s)+\sigma(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\\
&\hskip 227.62204ptt\in(t_{0},T),\end{split}

(1.1)

where
u
is a control process,
x
is a given deterministic function called the free term (which is also called the forcing term),
W
is a Brownian motion,
A,B,C
and
D
are matrix-valued deterministic coefficients, and
b
and
\sigma
are vector-valued stochastic inhomogeneous terms. The LQ control problem for SVIE (
1.1
), which we call an
LQ stochastic Volterra control problem
, is a problem to find a control process or a strategy which minimize a quadratic cost functional of both
u
and
X
.

The controlled SVIE (
1.1
) is a Volterra-type extension of a controlled linear stochastic differential equation (SDE):

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}X(s)=\{A(s)X(s)+B(s)u(s)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s+\{C(s)X(s)+D(s)u(s)+\sigma(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\\
X(t_{0})=x,\end{cases}

(1.2)

with
x
being a constant. LQ control problems for SDEs were first studied by Wonham
[
31
]
in 1968 and followed by many researchers; see
[
33
, Chapter 6]
and
[
24
]
for systematic studies and the developments of LQ control theory for SDEs. It is well-known that, in the LQ control problems for SDEs, the optimal control
u
has the following state-feedback representation:

u(t)=\Xi(t)X(t)+v(t),\ t\in[t_{0},T],

(1.3)

where
\Xi
is a matrix-valued deterministic function,
v
is a stochastic inhomogeneous term, and
X
is the optimal state process corresponding to
u
. Therefore, given the strategy
(\Xi,v)
, for each initial condition
(t_{0},x)
, we get the following closed-loop system:

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}X^{t_{0},x}(s)=\{A(s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+B(s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\{C(s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+D(s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+\sigma(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\\
X^{t_{0},x}(t_{0})=x,\\
u^{t_{0},x}(s)=\Xi(s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+v(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\end{cases}

(1.4)

or equivalently the SDE

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}X^{t_{0},x}(s)=\{(A(s)+B(s)\Xi(s))X^{t_{0},x}(s)+B(s)v(s)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\{(C(s)+D(s)\Xi(s))X^{t_{0},x}(s)+D(s)v(s)+\sigma(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\\
X^{t_{0},x}(t_{0})=x.\end{cases}

We note that the above system is an equation for the optimal state process
X=X^{t_{0},x}
, and the optimal control process
u=u^{t_{0},x}
is obtained as the outcome of the strategy
(\Xi,v)
by inserting the solution
X^{t_{0},x}
into the expression
u^{t_{0},x}=\Xi X^{t_{0},x}+v
. The closed-loop system (
1.4
) of the controlled SDE satisfies the flow property: for each
0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}<t\leq T
,

X^{t_{0},x}(t)=X^{t_{1},X^{t_{0},x}(t_{1})}(t),\ u^{t_{0},x}(t)=u^{t_{1},X^{t_{0},x}(t_{1})}(t).

This is also called the time-consistency, which ensures that the strategy
(\Xi,v)
is meaningful in dynamic sense. It is well-known that the dynamic optimality of the strategy
(\Xi,v)
is closely related to the well-posedness of a Riccati differential equation and a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE).

Compared to SDEs, the solutions of SVIEs are not Markovian or even not semimartingales in general. These features of SVIEs make some analysis dramatically difficult since we cannot use some fundamental tools of stochastic calculus such as Itô’s formula directly to compute some functionals of SVIEs. Furthermore, SVIEs do not satisfy the flow property. To illustrate this, consider the following (uncontrolled) stochastic Volterra process:

X^{t_{0},x}(t):=x+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}K(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(t_{0},T),

where
K
is a deterministic function, and
x
is an initial constant at time
t_{0}
. This is in particular the case of a fractional Brownian motion. Due to the two time variables structure of the kernel
K
,
X
does not satisfy the flow property in the standard sense: for
t_{0}<t_{1}<t<T
,

X^{t_{0},x}(t)\neq X^{t_{1},X^{t_{0},x}(t_{1})}(t)\ \ \text{where}\ \ X^{t_{1},X^{t_{0},x}(t_{1})}(t):=X^{t_{0},x}(t_{1})+\int^{t}_{t_{1}}K(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s).

The lack of the flow property gives rise to the following question:
What is the “state space” of SVIEs?
Actually, in the above example, the space of constants
x
(the space of values of
X^{t_{0},x}(t)
) is not suitable for the state space since it is not preserved in dynamic sense. To overcome this difficulty,
[
1
,
17
,
25
,
26
]
considered an auxiliary process
\Theta
defined by

\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t):=x+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W(r),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T),

where
\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T):=\{(s,t)\,|\,t_{0}<t<s<T\}
. Then, for any
t_{0}<t_{1}<t<T
, we have the following generalized flow property:

X^{t_{0},x}(t)=X^{t_{1},\Theta^{t_{0},x}(\cdot,t_{1})}(t)\ \ \text{where}\ \ X^{t_{1},\Theta^{t_{0},x}(\cdot,t_{1})}(t):=\Theta^{t_{0},x}(t,t_{1})+\int^{t}_{t_{1}}K(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s).

In the above expression,
\Theta^{t_{0},x}(\cdot,t_{1})=(\Theta^{t_{0},x}(t,t_{1}))_{t\in(t_{1},T)}
can be regarded as the free term of
X^{t_{1},\Theta^{t_{0},x}(\cdot,t_{1})}
evaluated at time
t_{1}
. We note that
\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,\cdot)=(\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t))_{t\in[t_{0},s]}
is a semimartingale for each fixed
s\in(t_{0},T)
, and it holds that
\Theta^{t_{0},x}(t,t)=X^{t_{0},x}(t)
. In the dynamic point of view, the space of the auxiliary process
\Theta(\cdot,\cdot)
can be seen as a state space for the above example. It is worth to mention that, in the theory of mathematical finance, the auxiliary process
\Theta
corresponds to the so-called forward variance which is a financial index available in the market (see
[
12
,
25
]
and references sited therein).

Let us turn to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
). As mentioned in the above example, the sole space of
X(t)
is not sufficient for describing the state space. In view of the generalized flow property mentioned above, it is natural to consider the following auxiliary process:

\Theta(s,t)=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(s,r)X(r)+B(s,r)u(r)+b(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(s,r)X(r)+D(s,r)u(r)+\sigma(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(r)

for
(s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T)
. A particular case of
\Theta(\cdot,\cdot)
was introduced in
[
27
]
to treat LQ stochastic Volterra control problems. In this paper, we call the above process a
forward state process
and regard the pair
(X,\Theta)
as the (generalized) state of the controlled SVIE (
1.1
). Introducing the forward state process
\Theta
to LQ problem also fits into the so-called causal projection approach studied by
[
18
,
21
]
in deterministic LQ Volterra control problems. From this sense, the forward state
\Theta(s,t)
can be seen as the causal projection of the original controlled SVIE (
1.1
) which is determined by information of
X
and
u
up to the current time
t
. Unlike the SDEs case, the above observation inspires us to consider a feedback control process represented by

u(t)=\Xi(t)X(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(s,t)\Theta(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s+v(t)

for some deterministic functions
\Xi
and
\Gamma
and a stochastic inhomogeneous term
v
. We call the triplet
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
a
causal feedback strategy
. By inserting the above expression of the outcome
u
into the controlled SVIE (
1.1
), for each
(t_{0},x)
, we get the following closed-loop system:

\begin{cases}X^{t_{0},x}(t)=x(t)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(t,s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+B(t,s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+b(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(t,s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+D(t,s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+\sigma(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(t_{0},T),\\
\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(s,r)X^{t_{0},x}(r)+B(s,r)u^{t_{0},x}(r)+b(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(s,r)X^{t_{0},x}(r)+D(s,r)u^{t_{0},x}(r)+\sigma(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(r),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T),\\
u^{t_{0},x}(t)=\Xi(t)X^{t_{0},x}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(s,t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s+v(t),\ t\in(t_{0},T).\end{cases}

(1.5)

We note that the above system is a coupled equation for the state process
X^{t_{0},x}
and the forward state process
\Theta^{t_{0},x}
, and it is beyond the typical framework of SVIEs studied in existing works. We call the pair
(X^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x})
satisfying the above system a
causal feedback solution
to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) at
(t_{0},x)
corresponding to the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
.

The purpose of this paper is to formulate and investigate causal feedback solutions of controlled SVIE (
1.1
) systematically. On one hand, the present paper can be seen as a preliminary of our continued paper
[
16
]
, where we go into the main topic of LQ stochastic Volterra control problems and characterize the optimal causal feedback strategy. On the other hand, besides the preliminary, the present paper provides a novel framework for SVIEs which is interesting by its own right in theoretical point of view. The main contributions of this paper are the following three points:

(i)

We show that there exists a unique causal feedback solution to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) for any given causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
and any input condition
(t_{0},x)
(see
2.4
).

(ii)

We prove a
duality principle
for the causal feedback solution (see
3.3
).

(iii)

We derive a
representation formula for a quadratic functional
of the causal feedback solution in terms of the free term
x
(see
4.11
).

Let us make some comments on the contribution (i) compared to existing works. SVIEs with regular coefficients were first studied by Berger and Mizel
[
6
,
7
]
and followed by
[
5
,
19
,
20
]
. Studies of SVIEs with singular coefficients (which include fractional SDEs and fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter less than
1/2
) can be found in
[
11
,
30
,
34
,
35
]
, etc. Recently, in
[
15
]
, the first author of the present paper investigated the algebraic structure of linear SVIEs with singular coefficients and proved a variation of constants formula. In the above works, there are no controls in SVIEs. Optimal control problems of SVIEs were studied by
[
4
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
22
,
23
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
32
]
in the open-loop framework, where feedback strategies were not considered. In the special case of SVIEs with completely monotone and convolution-type kernels, several kinds of feedback representations of the optimal controls were investigated by
[
2
,
8
,
10
]
. Specifically, Abi Jaber, Miller and Pham
[
2
]
studied LQ stochastic Volterra control problems with completely monotone and convolution-type kernels. Based on the so-called Markovian lift approach, which is an infinite-dimensional approach associated with the special structure of the completely monotone kernel, they obtained a kind of a linear feedback representation of the optimal control; see also
[
3
]
for the study on the associated integral operator Riccati equation. Compared to these works, we study linear controlled SVIEs with general (singular and non-convolution-type) coefficients. Our definition of causal feedback strategies, which is motivated from the generalized flow property as mentioned above, is different from the infinite dimensional ones considered in
[
2
,
8
,
10
]
. It is worth to mention that Viens and Zhang
[
25
]
proved the functional Itô formula for non-linear SVIEs with non-convolution-type coefficients and applied it to optimal control theory. The corresponding optimal strategy is similar to our definition of causal feedback strategies, but they did not study the well-posedness of the associated closed-loop system (
1.5
). We emphasize that the closed-loop system (
1.5
) is a coupled system of the original state process
X
and the forward state process
\Theta
, which is beyond the framework of the existing literature of SVIEs. Our contribution (i) ensures the well-posedness of the closed-loop system (
1.5
).

Let us illustrate the motivation of the contributions (ii) and (iii). In typical LQ control problems for SDEs, the controlled dynamics of the state process
X
is described by a linear SDE of the forms of (
1.2
) in the open-loop framework or (
1.4
) in the closed-loop framework. In such problems, the key points are to calculate (in terms of given parameters such as controls, initial states and some inhomogeneous terms) a linear functional of the form

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\psi(t),X(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

(1.6)

with an adapted process
\psi
and a quadratic functional of the form

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle Q(t)X(t),X(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

(1.7)

with a symmetric matrix-valued function
Q
. It is well-known that the linear functional (
1.6
) for the solution
X
to a linear SDE can be calculated by using the duality principle in terms of a BSDE, and the quadratic functional (
1.7
) can be calculated in terms of a Lyapunov differential equation (see
[
24
]
). In contrast, in our continued paper
[
16
]
, we consider LQ stochastic Volterra control problems in the framework of causal feedback strategies, where we are faced with the calculations of a linear functional of the form

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle\psi(t),X^{t_{0},x}(t)\rangle+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\chi(s,t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

(1.8)

and a quadratic functional of the form

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle Q^{(1)}(t)X^{t_{0},x}(t),X^{t_{0},x}(t)\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(2)}(s,t)X^{t_{0},x}(t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\int^{T}_{t}\!\!\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(3)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s_{2},t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s_{1},t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr].\end{split}

(1.9)

Here,
(X^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x})
is the causal feedback solution to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) corresponding to a given causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
,
\psi
and
\chi
are stochastic processes, and
Q^{(1)},Q^{(2)}
and
Q^{(3)}
are matrix-valued deterministic functions. The analyses of (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) serve as basis of the study of LQ stochastic Volterra control problems in
[
16
]
, and these are the main topics of the present paper.

In order to calculate the linear functional (
1.8
), as a counterpart of a BSDE in the Volterra setting, we introduce a novel class of backward stochastic equations which we call a
Type-II extended backward stochastic Volterra integral equation
(Type-II EBSVIE), see (
3.1
). This is a generalization of a class of Type-II backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (Type-II BSVIEs) introduced by Yong
[
32
]
to the framework of causal feedback strategies. We prove the well-posedness of Type-II EBSVIEs and derive a duality principle between the causal feedback solution to a controlled SVIE and the adapted solution to a Type-II EBSVIE.

In order to calculate the quadratic functional (
1.9
), we introduce a system of Lyapunov-type Volterra integro-differential equations which we call a
Lyapunov–Volterra equation
, see (
4.5
). We prove the well-posedness of Lyapunov–Volterra equations. Furthermore, we provide a representation formula for the quadratic functional (
1.9
) of a causal feedback solution to a (homogeneous) controlled SVIE in terms of the free term
x
and the solution to a Lyapunov–Volterra equation.

As mentioned above, the studies on causal feedback strategies including the duality principle and the representation formula for a quadratic functional play fundamental roles in LQ stochastic Volterra control problems. In our continued paper
[
16
]
, we characterize the optimal causal feedback strategy by means of a
Riccati–Volterra equation
, which is a Riccati-type equation closely related to the Lyapunov–Volterra equation, and a Type-II EBSVIE.

(L^{2,2,1}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2,2,1}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T))})
is the Banach space of
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
-valued deterministic functions
f
on
\square_{3}(t_{0},T)
such that
\|f\|_{L^{2,2,1}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T))}<\infty
, where

\|f\|_{L^{2,2,1}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T))}:=\Bigl(\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl(\int^{s_{1}\wedge s_{2}}_{t_{0}}|f(s_{1},s_{2},t)|\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}<\infty.

•

L^{2,2,1}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is the set of
f\in L^{2,2,1}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
such that
f(s_{1},s_{2},t)=f(s_{2},s_{1},t)^{\top}
for a.e.
(s_{1},s_{2},t)\in\square_{3}(0,T)
. It is easy to see that
L^{2,2,1}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a closed subspace of
L^{2,2,1}(\square_{3}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
.

•

\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
is the set of
f\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
satisfying the following two conditions:

–

it holds that

\|f\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T))}:=\underset{t\in(t_{0},T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int^{T}_{t}|f(s,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/2}<\infty;

–

for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite partition
\{U_{i}\}^{m}_{i=0}
of
(t_{0},T)
with
t_{0}=U_{0}<U_{1}<\cdots<U_{m}=T
such that

\underset{t\in(U_{i},U_{i+1})}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int^{U_{i+1}}_{t}|f(s,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/2}<\varepsilon

for each
i\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
.

It is easy to see that
(\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T))})
is a Banach space.

Remark 1.1
.

The space
\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
includes important classes of singular kernels appearing in the study of SVIEs. On one hand, convolution-type kernels of the form
f(t,s)=f_{1}(t-s)f_{2}(s)
,
(t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
, with
f_{1}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
and
f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
is in
\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
. On the other hand, if
f
satisfies the
L^{p}
-integrability

\underset{t\in(t_{0},T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int^{T}_{t}|f(s,t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/p}<\infty

for some
p>2
, then it is in
\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
too.

Throughout this paper,
d\in\mathbb{N}
represents the dimension of state processes, and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
represents the dimension of control processes. We fix a finite terminal time
T\in(0,\infty)
.

We define the set of input conditions by

.

•

In the standing assumption, the coefficients and the inhomogeneous terms are singular and of non-convolution-types. For example,

is allowed to diverge as

and

[

and

[

Based on the discussion in the introductory section, we define the notions of causal feedback strategies and the associated causal feedback solutions of linear controlled SVIEs.

3
Type-II EBSVIEs and duality principle

In this section, for a given causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
and
(\psi,\chi)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we investigate the linear functional (
1.8
) which we rewrite here for readers’ convenience:

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle\psi(t),X^{t_{0},x}(t)\rangle+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\chi(s,t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr].

Here,
(X^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the causal feedback solution to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) at
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
corresponding to
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
. By the linearity of
(X^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x})
with respect to the tuple
(x,b,\sigma,v)\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, together with the estimate (
2.2
), the above term can be seen as a bounded linear functional
\Psi
on the product space:

\Psi(x,b,\sigma,v):=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle\psi(t),X^{t_{0},x}(t)\rangle+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\chi(s,t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr].

Note that the bounded linear functional
\Psi
is determined by
A,B,C,D,\Xi,\Gamma
and
(\psi,\chi)
. Our purpose is to derive the dual representation of
\Psi
.

To do so, we introduce the following (linear)
Type-II extended backward stochastic Volterra integral equation
(Type-II EBSVIE):

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,s)=-\Bigl\{\chi(t,s)+\Gamma(t,s)^{\top}\int^{T}_{s}B(r,s)^{\top}\eta(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r+\Gamma(t,s)^{\top}\int^{T}_{s}D(r,s)^{\top}\zeta(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\zeta(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ (t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),\\
\eta(t,t)=\psi(t)+\int^{T}_{t}(A+B\triangleright\Xi)(r,t)^{\top}\eta(r,t)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{T}_{t}(C+D\triangleright\Xi)(r,t)^{\top}\zeta(r,t)\,\mathrm{d}r,\ t\in(0,T),\end{cases}

(3.1)

with
A+B\triangleright\Xi\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
C+D\triangleright\Xi\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
defined by

(A+B\triangleright\Xi)(t,s):=A(t,s)+B(t,s)\Xi(s),\ (C+D\triangleright\Xi)(t,s):=C(t,s)+D(t,s)\Xi(s)

for
(t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a pair
(\eta,\zeta)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an
adapted solution
to the Type-II EBSVIE (

) if it satisfies

\begin{cases}\eta(t,\theta)=\eta(t,t)+\int^{t}_{\theta}\Bigl\{\chi(t,s)+\Gamma(t,s)^{\top}\int^{T}_{s}B(r,s)^{\top}\eta(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r+\Gamma(t,s)^{\top}\int^{T}_{s}D(r,s)^{\top}\zeta(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 113.81102pt-\int^{t}_{\theta}\zeta(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\\
\eta(t,t)=\psi(t)+\int^{T}_{t}(A+B\triangleright\Xi)(r,t)^{\top}\eta(r,t)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{T}_{t}(C+D\triangleright\Xi)(r,t)^{\top}\zeta(r,t)\,\mathrm{d}r,\end{cases}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and any
\theta\in[0,t]
, a.s.

Remark 3.2
.

The Type-II EBSVIE (

) is a generalization of a class of linear Type-II BSVIEs introduced by Yong
[
32
]
. Indeed, if
\chi=0
and
(\Xi,\Gamma)=(0,0)

or equivalently, by means of the martingale representation theorem,

\eta(t)=\psi(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\{A(r,t)^{\top}\eta(r)+C(r,t)^{\top}\zeta(r,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}r-\int^{T}_{t}\zeta(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W(r),\ t\in(0,T),

for a suitable choice of

for

. This is a linear Type-II BSVIE. In this case, by
[
15

32
]
, the following duality principle holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\psi(t),X(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\eta(t),\varphi^{x,b,\sigma,v}(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\mathbb{E}[\eta(t)],x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\int^{T}_{t}\langle\mathbb{E}_{t}[\eta(s)],b(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\zeta(s,t),\sigma(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}\{B(s,t)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\eta(s)]+D(s,t)^{\top}\zeta(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s,v(t)\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr],

(Q^{(1)},Q^{(2)},Q^{(3)})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{d})\times L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times L^{2,2,1}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\square_{3}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, consider the following term:

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle Q^{(1)}(t)X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t),X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t)\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(2)}(s,t)X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt+\int^{T}_{t}\!\!\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(3)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s_{2},t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s_{1},t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr],\end{split}

(4.1)

where
(X^{t_{0},x}_{0},\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the causal feedback solution to the homogeneous controlled SVIE (
2.1
) at
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
corresponding to the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,0)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
. (
4.1
) can be written as
\Phi^{t_{0}}(x,x)
with

\displaystyle\Phi^{t_{0}}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle Q^{(1)}(t)X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t),X^{t_{0},y}_{0}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(2)}(s,t)X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t),\Theta^{t_{0},y}_{0}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(2)}(s,t)X^{t_{0},y}_{0}(t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int^{T}_{t}\!\!\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(3)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s_{2},t),\Theta^{t_{0},y}_{0}(s_{1},t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

for
x,y\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For each fixed
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, noting the linearity of
(X^{t_{0},x}_{0},\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0})
with respect to
x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the estimate (
2.2
) and the symmetricity of
(Q^{(1)},Q^{(2)},Q^{(3)})
, we see that
\Phi^{t_{0}}
is a bounded symmetric bilinear form on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, there exists a unique self-adjoint bounded linear operator
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}
on
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle Q^{(1)}(t)X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t),X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t)\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(2)}(s,t)X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\int^{T}_{t}\!\!\int^{T}_{t}\langle Q^{(3)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s_{2},t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s_{1},t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\Phi^{t_{0}}(x,x)=\langle\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}x,x\rangle_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}

for any
x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}
denotes the inner product on
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We consider the following natural ansatz of the bounded self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}
:

(\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}x)(t):=P^{(1)}(t)x(t)+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}P^{(2)}(t,r,t_{0})x(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\ t\in(t_{0},T),

with suitable choice of matrix-valued deterministic functions
P^{(1)}
and
P^{(2)}
. The above ansatz is a combination of a multiplicative operator and a Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator. The purpose of this section is to derive a Lyapunov-type dynamics of the pair
(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})

4.1
Preliminaries

First, we introduce a suitable space of the pairs
(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})
and investigate some fundamental properties.

Definition 4.1
.

We denote by
\Pi(0,T)
the set of pairs
(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})
with
P^{(1)}:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
P^{(2)}:\square_{3}(t_{0},T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that

•

P^{(1)}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{d})
;

•

for a.e.
(s_{1},s_{2})\in(0,T)^{2}
,
t\mapsto P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)
is absolutely continuous on
(0,s_{1}\wedge s_{2})
;

•

the function
P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{1}\wedge s_{2}):=\lim_{t\uparrow s_{1}\wedge s_{2}}P(s_{1},s_{2},t)
,
(s_{1},s_{2})\in(0,T)^{2}
, is in
L^{2}((0,T)^{2};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
;

•

the function
\dot{P}^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t):=\frac{\partial P^{(2)}}{\partial t}(s_{1},s_{2},t)
,
(s_{1},s_{2},t)\in\square_{3}(0,T)
, is in
L^{2,2,1}(\square_{3}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
;

•

for a.e.
(s_{1},s_{2},t)\in\square_{3}(0,T)
, it holds that
P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)=P^{(2)}(s_{2},s_{1},t)^{\top}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Observe that
\Pi(0,T)
is a Banach space with the norm

\displaystyle\|P\|_{\Pi(0,T)}:=\underset{t\in(0,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|P^{(1)}(t)|+\Bigl(\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\int^{T}_{0}|P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{1}\wedge s_{2})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\Bigl(\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl(\int^{s_{1}\wedge s_{2}}_{0}|\dot{P}^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)|\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

for
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\Pi(0,T)
. Furthermore, the following holds:

\underset{t\in(0,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|P^{(1)}(t)|+\Bigl(\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{t\in[0,s_{1}\wedge s_{2}]}|P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq\|P\|_{\Pi(0,T)}<\infty.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\Pi(0,T)
. Then for each
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, the map
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}:L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined by

(\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}x)(t):=P^{(1)}(t)x(t)+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}P^{(2)}(t,r,t_{0})x(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\ t\in(t_{0},T),

for
x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is a self-adjoint bounded linear operator on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Noting that
P^{(1)}(t)\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)=P^{(2)}(s_{2},s_{1},t)^{\top}
, for each
x,y\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}x,y\rangle_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle(\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}x)(t),y(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(1)}(t)x(t),y(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),y(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle x(t),P^{(1)}(t)y(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle x(s_{2}),P^{(2)}(s_{2},s_{1},t_{0})y(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle x(t),(\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}y)(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t=\langle x,\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}y\rangle_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}.

Thus,
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}
is self-adjoint.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\Pi(0,T)
. Assume that

\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(1)}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}=0

for any
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
. Then
P=0
in the sense that
P^{(1)}(t)=0
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and
P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)=0
for any
t\in[0,s_{1}\wedge s_{2}]
for a.e.
(s_{1},s_{2})\in(0,T)^{2}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
P^{(1)}=0
. Then
P^{(2)}=0
follows easily. Considering
t_{0}=0
and
x(t):=\sqrt{N}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[\tau,\tau+1/N]}(t)y
,
t\in(0,T)
, with
\tau\in(0,T)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
\tau+1/N<T
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
being arbitrary, we obtain

N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\langle P^{(1)}(t)y,y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\langle P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},0)y,y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}=0.

Thus, we have

N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}P^{(1)}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},0)\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}=0.

By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
, the first term tends to
P^{(1)}(\tau)
as
N\to\infty
. On the other hand, for any
\tau\in(0,T)
, the second term can be estimated as

\Bigl|N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},0)\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr|\leq\Bigl(\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}|P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},0)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},

and the right-hand side tends to zero as
N\to\infty
. Thus, we have
P^{(1)}(\tau)=0
for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Derivations of Lyapunov–Volterra equations

By using the notations defined in the last subsection, we derive a Lyapunov-type dynamics of
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\Pi(0,T)
and investigate the quadratic functional (
4.1
). Before stating the main theorem, we prove the following key lemma, which is helpful not only for understanding how to derive the Lyapunov-type equation, but also for the study of LQ stochastic Volterra control problems.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\Pi(0,T)

(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(1)}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}(\sigma^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}\sigma)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}D)(t)u(t),u(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+2\Bigl\langle(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}C)(t)X(t)+\int^{T}_{t}(B^{\top}{\ltimes}P)(s,t)\Theta(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}\sigma)(t),u(t)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\langle(C^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}C)(t)X(t),X(t)\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle(P{\rtimes}A)(s,t)X(t),\Theta(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

Proof.

First, we note that

for a.e.

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle D(t,s)^{\top}P^{(1)}(t)D(t,s)u(s),u(s)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+2\langle D(t,s)^{\top}P^{(1)}(t)C(t,s)X(s)+B(t,s)^{\top}P^{(1)}(t)\Theta(t,s)+D(t,s)^{\top}P^{(1)}(t)\sigma(t,s),u(s)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\langle C(t,s)^{\top}P^{(1)}(t)C(t,s)X(s),X(s)\rangle+2\langle P^{(1)}(t)A(t,s)X(s),\Theta(t,s)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+2\langle C(t,s)^{\top}P^{(1)}(t)\sigma(t,s),X(s)\rangle+2\langle P^{(1)}(t)b(t,s),\Theta(t,s)\rangle\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr].

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(1)}(t)X(t),X(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\\

&\hskip 14.22636pt+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}D(s,t)^{\top}P^{(1)}(s)D(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,u(t),u(t)\Bigr\rangle\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+2\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}D(s,t)^{\top}P^{(1)}(s)C(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,X(t)+\int^{T}_{t}B(s,t)^{\top}P^{(1)}(s)\Theta(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt+\int^{T}_{t}D(s,t)^{\top}P^{(1)}(s)\sigma(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,u(t)\Bigr\rangle\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}C(s,t)^{\top}P^{(1)}(s)C(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,X(t),X(t)\Bigr\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle P^{(1)}(s)A(s,t)X(t),\Theta(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+2\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}C(s,t)^{\top}P^{(1)}(s)\sigma(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,X(t)\Bigr\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle P^{(1)}(s)b(s,t),\Theta(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr].\end{split}

(4.2)

Next, we note that
P^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)=P^{(2)}(s_{2},s_{1},t)^{\top}
for any

for a.e.

. Applying Itô’s formula to

for a.e.

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}[\langle P^{(2)}(s,t,t)X(t),\Theta(s,t)\rangle]=2\mathbb{E}[\langle P^{(2)}(s,t,t)\Theta(t,t),\Theta(s,t)\rangle]

\displaystyle=2\langle P^{(2)}(s,t,t_{0})x(t),x(s)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle\dot{P}^{(2)}(s,t,r)\Theta(t,r),\Theta(s,r)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\langle P^{(2)}(s,t,r)\Theta(t,r),A(s,r)X(r)+B(s,r)u(r)+b(s,r)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\langle P^{(2)}(s,t,r)(A(t,r)X(r)+B(t,r)u(r)+b(t,r)),\Theta(s,r)\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\langle P^{(2)}(s,t,r)(C(t,r)X(r)+D(t,r)u(r)+\sigma(t,r)),C(s,r)X(r)+D(s,r)u(r)+\sigma(s,r)\rangle\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\langle P^{(2)}(s,t,t_{0})x(t),x(s)\rangle+\langle P^{(2)}(t,s,t_{0})x(s),x(t)\rangle

In this appendix, we prove our main Theorems

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}})
be a Banach space,
(\mathcal{Y},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}})
be a normed space, and
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a continuous map. Suppose that a map
F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
satisfies the following two properties:

•

Lipschitz continuity: There exists a constant
L>0
such that

\|F(X_{1})-F(X_{2})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq L\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}

for any
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
.

•

A priori estimate: There exists a constant
K>0
such that, for any
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{Y}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, two solutions
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
of the equations

X_{i}=\mathcal{T}(\lambda F(X_{i})+\varphi_{i}),\ i=1,2,

satisfy

\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq K\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

Then, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{Y}
, there exists a unique solution
X\in\mathcal{X}
to the equation

X=\mathcal{T}(F(X)+\varphi).

Proof.

The uniqueness of the solution follows from the a priori estimate. We prove the existence by
the method of continuation
. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be a number such that
N\geq 2(1+KL)
. For each
n\in\{0,1,\dots,N\}
, we say that (P
n
) holds if for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{Y}
, there exists a unique solution
X\in\mathcal{X}
to the equation
X=\mathcal{T}(\frac{n}{N}F(X)+\varphi)
. We shall show that (P
N
) holds. Clearly, (P
0
) holds. Assume that (P
n
) holds for some
n\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
. Fix an arbitrary
\varphi\in\mathcal{Y}
. By virtue of the assumption (P
n
), we can define a sequence
X_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
with
i\in\mathbb{N}
inductively as the solution to the equation

X_{i}=\mathcal{T}\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}F(X_{i})+\frac{1}{N}F(X_{i-1})+\varphi\Bigr),

with the convention that
X_{0}:=0
. By the a priori estimate and the Lipschitz continuity of
F
, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\|X_{i+1}-X_{i}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\frac{K}{N}\|F(X_{i})-F(X_{i-1})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\frac{KL}{N}\|X_{i}-X_{i-1}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|X_{i}-X_{i-1}\|_{\mathcal{X}},

where the last inequality follows from
N\geq 2(1+KL)
. The above estimate implies that
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence on the Banach space
\mathcal{X}
, and thus the limit
X:=\lim_{i\to\infty}X_{i}
exists. Noting the continuity of
F
and
\mathcal{T}
, we obtain

X=\mathcal{T}\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}F(X)+\frac{1}{N}F(X)+\varphi\Bigr)=\mathcal{T}\Bigl(\frac{n+1}{N}F(X)+\varphi\Bigr).

Since
\varphi\in\mathcal{Y}
is arbitrary, we see that (P
n+1
) holds. By the induction, we see that (P
N
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

F
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We prove the a priori estimate. Let
L:=\|A\|_{L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}+\|C\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}
and
0=U_{0}<U_{1}<\cdots<U_{m}=T
be a partition of
(0,T)
satisfying the requirement in the assertion. We emphasize that such a partition exists since
A\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
C\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
be fixed, and assume that
X_{1},X_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy
X_{i}=\lambda F(X_{i})+\varphi_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Define
\bar{X}:=X_{1}-X_{2}
and
\bar{\varphi}:=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
. Then we have
\bar{X}=\lambda F(\bar{X})+\bar{\varphi}
. For
i\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
, we have

\bar{X}(t)=\bar{\varphi}(t)+\lambda\{F^{1}_{i}(\bar{X})(t)+F^{2}_{i}(\bar{X})(t)\},\ t\in(U_{i},U_{i+1}),

where

\displaystyle F^{1}_{i}(\bar{X})(t):=\int^{U_{i}}_{0}A(t,s)\bar{X}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{U_{i}}_{0}C(t,s)\bar{X}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(U_{i},U_{i+1}),

\displaystyle F^{2}_{i}(\bar{X})(t):=\int^{t}_{U_{i}}A(t,s)\bar{X}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{U_{i}}C(t,s)\bar{X}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(U_{i},U_{i+1}).

It is easy to see that

\|F^{1}_{i}(\bar{X})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\leq L\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,U_{i})},\ \|F^{2}_{i}(\bar{X})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{i},U_{i+1})}.

Thus, we have

\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\leq 2\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{i},U_{i+1})}+2L\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,U_{i})}\leq 2\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}+2L\sum^{i-1}_{j=0}\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{j},U_{j+1})}.

By the discrete Gronwall inequality, we have

\max_{i\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}}\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\leq 2\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}\sum^{m}_{j=1}(1+2L)^{j-1}\leq 2m(1+2L)^{m-1}\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)},

and thus

\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}\leq\sum^{m-1}_{i=0}\|\bar{X}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\leq 2m^{2}(1+2L)^{m-1}\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}.

Therefore, the a priori estimate holds. By
A.1
, for any
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the SVIE
X=F(X)+\varphi
has a unique solution
X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

The following technical lemma reveals the structure of the causal feedback solution of a linear controlled SVIE.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
be fixed. For each
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
, a triplet
(\Xi^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x},u^{t_{0},x})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)
is a causal feedback solution to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) if and only if the following holds:

\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}X^{t_{0},x}(t)\\
u^{t_{0},x}(t)\end{pmatrix}=\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}(t),\ t\in(t_{0},T),\\
\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{(A(s,r),B(s,r))\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}(r)+b(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{(C(s,r),D(s,r))\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}(r)+\sigma(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(r),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T),\end{cases}

(A.1)

where
\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d+\ell})

\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}(t)=\Phi(t)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\mathbb{A}(t,s)\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\mathbb{C}(t,s)\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(t_{0},T).

(A.2)

Here,
\Phi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d+\ell})
,
\mathbb{A}\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{(d+\ell)\times(d+\ell)})
and
\mathbb{C}\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{(d+\ell)\times(d+\ell)})
are defined by

\displaystyle\Phi(t):=\begin{pmatrix}x(t)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}b(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\sigma(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\\

with

and

for

By using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we can easily show that

\Phi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d+\ell}),\ \mathbb{A}\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{(d+\ell)\times(d+\ell)})\ \text{and}\ \mathbb{C}\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{(d+\ell)\times(d+\ell)}).

Let
(X^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x},u^{t_{0},x})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)
be a causal feedback solution to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) at
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
corresponding to the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
. By using the (stochastic) Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle u^{t_{0},x}(t)

\displaystyle=\Xi(t)X^{t_{0},x}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(s,t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s+v(t)

\displaystyle=\Xi(t)x(t)+\Xi(t)\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(t,s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+B(t,s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+b(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\Xi(t)\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(t,s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+D(t,s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+\sigma(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(s,t)x(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(s,t)\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(s,r)X^{t_{0},x}(r)+B(s,r)u^{t_{0},x}(r)+b(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(s,t)\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(s,r)X^{t_{0},x}(r)+D(s,r)u^{t_{0},x}(r)+\sigma(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(r)\,\mathrm{d}s+v(t)

\displaystyle=x^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}b^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\sigma^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)+v(t)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}(A^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t,s),B^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t,s))\begin{pmatrix}X^{t_{0},x}(s)\\
u^{t_{0},x}(s)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}(C^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t,s),D^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t,s))\begin{pmatrix}X^{t_{0},x}(s)\\
u^{t_{0},x}(s)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)

for a.e.
t\in(t_{0},T)
, a.s. Therefore, the process
\mathbb{X}^{t_{0},x}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d+\ell})
defined by the first equality in (
A.1
) satisfies the SVIE (

A.1
) holds.

Conversely, if

solves the SVIE (

(X^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x},u^{t_{0},x})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)
defined by (

) is the causal feedback solution of the controlled SVIE (

). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

For any
(\psi,\chi)\in\mathcal{Y}:=L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a unique adapted solution
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathcal{X}:=L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the trivial Type-II EBSVIE

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\eta(t,s)=-\chi(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\zeta(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ (t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),\\
\eta(t,t)=\psi(t),\ t\in(0,T).\end{cases}

Furthermore, the solution map
\mathcal{T}:(\psi,\chi)\mapsto(\eta,\zeta)
is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{Y}
to
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

We construct the adapted solution. Define
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\eta(t,s):=\mathbb{E}_{s}\Bigl[\psi(t)+\int^{t}_{s}\chi(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr],\ (t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T).

Also, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, define
\zeta(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,t;\mathbb{R}^{d})
via the martingale representation theorem:

\psi(t)+\int^{t}_{0}\chi(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\psi(t)+\int^{t}_{0}\chi(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]+\int^{t}_{0}\zeta(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s).

Then we have
\eta(t,t)=\psi(t)
,
t\in(0,T)
, and

\eta(t,s)=\eta(t,t)+\int^{t}_{s}\chi(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r-\int^{t}_{s}\zeta(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W(r),\ (t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T).

Thus, the pair
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathcal{X}=L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an adapted solution to the trivial Type-II EBSVIE. From the construction, we get

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\int^{t}_{0}|\zeta(t,s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\!\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl|\int^{t}_{0}\zeta(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl|\psi(t)+\int^{t}_{0}\chi(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\chi\|_{L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}.

Also, by Doob’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|\eta(t,s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}=\Bigl(\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|\eta(t,s)|^{2}\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\Bigl|\mathbb{E}_{s}\Bigl[\psi(t)+\int^{t}_{s}\chi(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]\Bigr|^{2}\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\psi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}+2\|\chi\|_{L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}.

The uniqueness of the adapted-solution and the linearity of the solution map
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
are clear. The boundedness of
\mathcal{T}
follows from the above estimates. This completes the proof.
∎

Existence and uniqueness of the adapted solution:
We define a map

by

\displaystyle F(\eta,\zeta):=(F_{1}(\eta,\zeta),F_{2}(\eta,\zeta)),

\displaystyle F_{1}(\eta,\zeta)(t):=\int^{T}_{t}(A+B\triangleright\Xi)(r,t)^{\top}\eta(r,t)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{T}_{t}(C+D\triangleright\Xi)(r,t)^{\top}\zeta(r,t)\,\mathrm{d}r,\ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle F_{2}(\eta,\zeta)(t,s):=\Gamma(t,s)^{\top}\int^{T}_{s}B(r,s)^{\top}\eta(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r+\Gamma(t,s)^{\top}\int^{T}_{s}D(r,s)^{\top}\zeta(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r,\ (t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),

for
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathcal{X}
. It is easy to see that
F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a bounded linear operator. Also, consider the bounded linear operator
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
defined in
A.6
. Observe that, for each
(\psi,\chi)\in\mathcal{Y}
,
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathcal{X}
is an adapted solution to the Type-II EBSVIE (
3.1
) if and only if the following holds:

(\eta,\zeta)=\mathcal{T}(F(\eta,\zeta)+(\psi,\chi))

In order to apply
A.1
, we have to show the a priori estimate.

A priori estimate of the adapted solution:
Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
be fixed. For each
(\psi_{i},\chi_{i})\in\mathcal{Y}
with
i=1,2
, assume that
(\eta_{i},\zeta_{i})\in\mathcal{X}
satisfy the equations

(\eta_{i},\zeta_{i})=\mathcal{T}(\lambda F(\eta_{i},\zeta_{i})+(\psi_{i},\chi_{i})),\ i=1,2.

Define
(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta}):=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})\in\mathcal{X}
and
(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi}):=(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2},\chi_{1}-\chi_{2})\in\mathcal{Y}
. We will show the estimate

\|(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta})\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq K\|(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

Here and the rest of this proof,
K>0
denotes a universal constant which does not depend on
\lambda
or
(\psi_{i},\chi_{i})
. Observe that
(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta})
is an adapted solution to the Type-II EBSVIE (
3.1
) with the coefficients
A,B,C,D
replaced by
\lambda A,\lambda B,\lambda C,\lambda D
and
(\psi,\chi)
replaced by
(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})
. Let
(X_{1},\Theta_{1})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the causal feedback solution to the homogeneous controlled SVIE (
2.1
) with the coefficients
(\lambda A,\lambda B,\lambda C,\lambda D)
, the zero input condition
(t_{0},x)=(0,0)\in\mathcal{I}
, and a causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
with
v\in\mathcal{U}(0,T)
being arbitrary. By the duality principle (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}\{\lambda B(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\eta}(s,t)+\lambda D(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\zeta}(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s,v(t)\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl\{\langle\bar{\psi}(t),X_{1}(t)\rangle+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{\chi}(s,t),\Theta_{1}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr].

By
2.4
, noting
A.5
, the right hand side of the above equality is estimated as

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl\{\langle\bar{\psi}(t),X_{1}(t)\rangle+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{\chi}(s,t),\Theta_{1}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{\psi}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}+\|\bar{\chi}\|_{L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}\|\Theta_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}

\displaystyle\leq K\|(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\|v\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}.

Therefore, we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl|\int^{T}_{t}\{\lambda B(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\eta}(s,t)+\lambda D(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\zeta}(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{\ell})\\
\|v\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}\{\lambda B(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\eta}(s,t)+\lambda D(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\zeta}(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s,v(t)\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq K\|(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

By using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\lambda F_{2}(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta})\|_{L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Gamma\|_{L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl|\int^{T}_{t}\{\lambda B(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\eta}(s,t)+\lambda D(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\zeta}(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq K\|(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

Next, for a given
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
(X_{2},\Theta_{2})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the causal feedback solution to the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) with the coefficients
(\lambda A,\lambda B,\lambda C,\lambda D)
, the inhomogeneous terms

b(t,s)=\lambda(A+B\triangleright\Xi)(t,s)\varphi(s),\ \sigma(t,s)=\lambda(C+D\triangleright\Xi)(t,s)\varphi(s),\ (t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),

the input condition
(t_{0},x)=(0,0)
, and the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,0)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
. Then the duality principle (
3.2
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl\langle\int^{T}_{t}\{\lambda(A+B\triangleright\Xi)(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\eta}(s,t)+\lambda(C+D\triangleright\Xi)(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\zeta}(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s,\varphi(t)\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{\eta}(s,t),b(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{\zeta}(s,t),\sigma(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl\{\langle\bar{\psi}(t),X_{2}(t)\rangle+\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{\chi}(s,t),\Theta_{2}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr].

Noting that
\|b\|_{L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}+\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}\leq K\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}
, by the same arguments as above, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\lambda F_{1}(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{0}\Bigl|\int^{T}_{t}\{\lambda(A+B\triangleright\Xi)(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\eta}(s,t)+\lambda(C+D\triangleright\Xi)(s,t)^{\top}\bar{\zeta}(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq K\|(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

From the above estimates, we have

\|\lambda F(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq K\|(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})\|_{\mathcal{Y}},

and hence the desired a priori estimate holds:

\|(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta})\|_{\mathcal{X}}=\|\mathcal{T}(\lambda F(\bar{\eta},\bar{\zeta})+(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi}))\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq K\|(\bar{\psi},\bar{\chi})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

By
A.1
, for any
(\psi,\chi)\in\mathcal{Y}=L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a unique adapted solution
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathcal{X}=L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the Type-II EBSVIE (
3.1
). This completes the proof.
∎

A.3
Proof of
4.11
: Lyapunov–Volterra equations

We prove
4.11
. The representation formula (
4.6
) follows from
4.9
. The idea of the proof of the well-posedness of the Lyapunov–Volterra equation (
4.5
) is similar to the one of
3.3
.

Proof of
4.11
.

The representation formula (
4.6
):
By
4.9
, it is trivial that if
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\Pi(0,T)
satisfies the Lyapunov–Volterra equation (
4.5
), then the representation formula (
4.6
) holds.

Existence and uniqueness of the solution:
Let
\mathcal{X}:=\Pi(0,T)
and

\mathcal{Y}:=L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{d})\times L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times L^{2,2,1}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\square_{3}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}).

Define
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
by
\mathcal{T}(Q):=(\mathcal{T}_{1}(Q),\mathcal{T}_{2}(Q))
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}(Q)(t):=Q^{(1)}(t),\ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}(Q)(s_{1},s_{2},t):=Q^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2})\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\triangle_{2}(0,T)}(s_{1},s_{2})+Q^{(2)}(s_{2},s_{1})^{\top}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\triangle_{2}(0,T)}(s_{2},s_{1})-\int^{s_{1}\wedge s_{2}}_{t}Q^{(3)}(s_{1},s_{2},\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt(s_{1},s_{2},t)\in\square_{3}(0,T),

for
Q=(Q^{(1)},Q^{(2)},Q^{(3)})\in\mathcal{Y}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
is a bounded linear operator. Also, define
F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
by

\displaystyle F(P):=(F^{(1)}[\Xi;P],F^{(2)}[\Xi,\Gamma;P],F^{(3)}[\Gamma;P])

for
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\mathcal{X}=\Pi(0,T)
. By
4.6
,
F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a bounded linear operator. For each
Q=(Q^{(1)},Q^{(2)},Q^{(3)})\in\mathcal{Y}
,
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\mathcal{X}
is a solution to the Lyapunov–Volterra equation (
4.5
) if and only if the following equation holds:

P=\mathcal{T}(F(P)+Q).

In order to apply
A.1
, we have to show the a priori estimate.

A priori estimate of the solution:
Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
be fixed. For each
Q_{i}=(Q^{(1)}_{i},Q^{(2)}_{i},Q^{(3)}_{i})\in\mathcal{Y}
with
i=1,2
, assume that
P_{i}=(P^{(1)}_{i},P^{(2)}_{i})\in\mathcal{X}
satisfy the equations

P_{i}=\mathcal{T}(\lambda F(P_{i})+Q_{i}),\ i=1,2.

Define
\bar{P}:=(\bar{P}^{(1)},\bar{P}^{(2)}):=(P^{(1)}_{1}-P^{(1)}_{2},P^{(2)}_{1}-P^{(2)}_{2})\in\mathcal{X}
and
\bar{Q}:=(\bar{Q}^{(1)},\bar{Q}^{(2)},\bar{Q}^{(3)}):=(Q^{(1)}_{1}-Q^{(1)}_{2},Q^{(2)}_{1}-Q^{(2)}_{2},Q^{(3)}_{1}-Q^{(3)}_{2})\in\mathcal{Y}
. We will show the estimate

\|\bar{P}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq K\|\bar{Q}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

Here and the rest of this proof,
K>0
denotes a universal constant which does not depend on
\lambda
or
Q_{i}
. Observe that
\bar{P}=(\bar{P}^{(1)},\bar{P}^{(2)})
is a solution to the Lyapunov–Volterra equation (
4.5
) with the coefficients
A,B,C,D
replaced by
\lambda A,\lambda B,\sqrt{\lambda}C,\sqrt{\lambda}D
and
Q=(Q^{(1)},Q^{(2)},Q^{(3)})
replaced by
\bar{Q}=(\bar{Q}^{(1)},\bar{Q}^{(2)},\bar{Q}^{(3)})
. For each
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, define
\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}:L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}:=\bar{P}^{(1)}(t)x(t)+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\bar{P}^{(2)}(t,r,t_{0})x(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\ t\in(t_{0},T),

for
x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By
4.3
,
\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}
is a self-adjoint bounded linear operator on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Now let
(X,\Theta)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the causal feedback solution to the homogeneous controlled SVIE (
2.1
) with the coefficients
(\lambda A,\lambda B,\sqrt{\lambda}C,\sqrt{\lambda}D)
, a given input condition
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
, and the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,0)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
. By the representation formula (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}x,x\rangle_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\bar{P}^{(1)}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\bar{P}^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle\bar{Q}^{(1)}(t)X(t),X(t)\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{Q}^{(2)}(s,t)X(t),\Theta(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\int^{T}_{t}\!\!\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{Q}^{(3)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)\Theta(s_{2},t),\Theta(s_{1},t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr].

By
2.4
, noting
A.5
, the right hand side of the above equality is estimated as

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle\bar{Q}^{(1)}(t)X(t),X(t)\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{Q}^{(2)}(s,t)X(t),\Theta(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\int^{T}_{t}\!\!\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{Q}^{(3)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)\Theta(s_{2},t),\Theta(s_{1},t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{Q}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\|X\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T)}+2\|\bar{Q}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}\|X\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t_{0},T)}\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T))}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\|\bar{Q}^{(3)}\|_{L^{2,2,1}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\square_{3}(0,T))}\|\Theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\mathrm{c}}(\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T))}

\displaystyle\leq K\|\bar{Q}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\|x\|^{2}_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}.

Therefore, the operator norm
\|\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
of
\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}
is estimated as

\|\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
\|x\|_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\langle\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t_{0}}x,x\rangle_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}|\leq K\|\bar{Q}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

We note that, for any
M=(M_{1},\dots,M_{d_{1}})\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
\bar{P}{\rtimes}M\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is written by

(\bar{P}{\rtimes}M)(s,t)=((\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t}M_{1}(\cdot,t))(s),\dots,(\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t}M_{d}(\cdot,t))(s)),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),

and thus

\|\bar{P}{\rtimes}M\|_{L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}\leq K\|\bar{Q}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\|M\|_{L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}.

Similarly, for any
M=(M_{1},\dots,M_{d})\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
N=(N_{1},\dots,N_{d})\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
M^{\top}{\ltimes}\bar{P}{\rtimes}N\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is written by

(M^{\top}{\ltimes}\bar{P}{\rtimes}N)(t)=(\langle M_{k}(\cdot,t),\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{t}N_{\ell}(\cdot,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(t,T)})_{k,\ell},\ t\in(0,T),

and thus

\|M^{\top}{\ltimes}\bar{P}{\rtimes}N\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq K\|\bar{Q}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\|M\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}\|N\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T))}.

Noting the definition (
4.4
) of the coefficients
(F^{(1)},F^{(2)},F^{(3)})
, we see that

\|F(\bar{P})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}=\|(F^{(1)}[\Xi;\bar{P}],F^{(2)}[\Xi,\Gamma;\bar{P}],F^{(3)}[\Gamma;\bar{P}])\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq K\|\bar{Q}\|_{\mathcal{Y}},

and thus the desired a priori estimate holds:

\|\bar{P}\|_{\mathcal{X}}=\|\mathcal{T}(\lambda F(\bar{P})+\bar{Q})\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq K\|\bar{Q}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

By
A.1
, for any
Q=(Q^{(1)},Q^{(2)},Q^{(3)})\in\mathcal{Y}=L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{d})\times L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times L^{2,2,1}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\square_{3}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, there exists a unique solution
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\mathcal{X}=\Pi(0,T)
to the Lyapunov–Volterra equation (
4.5
). This completes the proof.
∎

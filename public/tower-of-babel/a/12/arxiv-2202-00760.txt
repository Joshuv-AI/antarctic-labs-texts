Exact boundary controllability and exact boundary synchronization for a coupled system of wave equations with coupled Robin boundary controls


Abstract
In this paper, we consider the exact boundary controllability and the exact boundary synchronization (by groups) for a coupled system of wave equations with coupled Robin boundary controls. Owing to the difficulty coming from the lack of regularity of the solution, we confront a bigger challenge than that in the case with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary controls. In order to overcome this difficulty, we use the regularity results of solutions to the mixed problem with Neumann boundary conditions by Lasiecka and Triggiani ( [ 6 ] ) to get the regularity of solutions to the mixed problem with coupled Robin boundary conditions. Thus we show the exact boundary controllability of the system, and by a method of compact perturbation, we obtain the non-exact boundary controllability of the system with fewer boundary controls on some special domains. Based on this, we further study the exact boundary synchronization (by groups) for the same system, the determination of the exactly synchronizable state (by groups), as well as the necessity of the compatibility conditions of the coupling matrices.

2
Regularity of solutions with Neumann boundary conditions

Similarly to the problem of wave equations with Neumann boundary conditions, a problem with Robin boundary conditions no longer enjoys the hidden regularity as in the case with Dirichlet boundary conditions. As a result, the solution to problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) with Robin boundary conditions is not smooth enough in general for the proof of the non-exact boundary controllability of the system. In order to overcome this difficulty, we should deeply study the regularity of solutions to wave equations with Neumann boundary conditions. For this purpose, we will review some existing results with Neumann boundary conditions.

Consider the following second order hyperbolic problem
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with boundary
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{tt}+A(x,\partial)y=f&\hbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu_{A}}=g&\text{on}\quad(0,T)\times\Gamma,\\
t=0:~~y=y_{0},\quad y_{t}=y_{1}&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle A(x,\partial)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+c_{0}(x),

(2.2)

in which

with

, where

is a positive constant; moreover,

is the outward normal derivative associated with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu_{A}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial{y}}{\partial x_{i}}\nu_{j},

\nu=(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{n})^{T}

\Gamma
.

Define the operator

\displaystyle{\cal A}=A(x,\partial),\quad{\cal D}({\cal A})=\big\{y\in H^{2}(\Omega):\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu_{A}}=0~\text{on}~\Gamma\big\}.

3
Well-posedness of a coupled system of wave equations with coupled Robin boundary conditions

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded domain or a parallelepiped as mentioned before. We now prove the well-posedness of problem (
1.7
) and (
1.8
).

Let
\Phi=(\phi^{(1)},\cdots,\phi^{(N)})^{T}.
We first consider the following adjoint system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Phi^{\prime\prime}-{\Delta}\Phi+A^{T}\Phi=0\hskip 59.75095pt~&\hbox{in}\quad(0,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}\Phi+B^{T}\Phi=0\hskip 85.35826pt~&\hbox{on}\quad(0,+\infty)\times\Gamma\\
\end{cases}

(3.1)

with the initial data

t=0:\quad\Phi=\widehat{\Phi}_{0},\quad\Phi^{\prime}=\widehat{\Phi}_{1}\quad\hbox{in}~~\Omega,

(3.2)

where
A^{T}
and
B^{T}
denote the transpose of
A
and
B
, respectively.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth bounded domain or a parallelepiped. Assume furthermore that
B
is similar to a real symmetric matrix. Then for any given
(\widehat{\Phi}_{0},\widehat{\Phi}_{1})\in(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N}\times(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N}
, the adjoint problem (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) admits a unique weak solution

(\Phi,\Phi^{\prime})\in C^{0}_{loc}([0,+\infty);(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N}\times(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N})

(3.3)

in the sense of
C_{0}
-semigroup, where
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}
are defined by (
1.2
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
B
is a real symmetric matrix.

We formulate system (
3.1
) into the following variational form:

\int_{\Omega}(\Phi^{\prime\prime},\widehat{\Phi})dx+\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\Phi,\nabla\widehat{\Phi}\rangle dx+\int_{\Gamma}(\Phi,B\widehat{\Phi})d\Gamma+\int_{\Omega}(\Phi,A\widehat{\Phi})dx=0

(3.4)

for any given test function
\widehat{\Phi}\in(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N}
, where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, while

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product of
\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}(\mathbb{R})
.

Recalling the following interpolation inequality (
[
25
]
)

\int_{\Gamma}|\phi|^{2}d\Gamma\leqslant c\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega),

we have

\int_{\Gamma}(\Phi,B\Phi)d\Gamma\leqslant\|B\|\int_{\Gamma}|\Phi|^{2}d\Gamma\leqslant c\|B\|\|\Phi\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N}}\|\Phi\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N}},

then it is easy to see that

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\Phi,\nabla\Phi\rangle dx+\int_{\Gamma}(\Phi,B\Phi)d\Gamma+\lambda\|\Phi\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N}}^{2}\geqslant c^{\prime}\|\Phi\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N}}^{2}

for some suitable constants
\lambda>0
and
c^{\prime}>0
.
Moreover, the non-symmetric part in (
3.4
) satisfies

\int_{\Omega}(\Phi,A\widehat{\Phi})dx\leqslant\|A\|\|\Phi\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N}}\|\widehat{\Phi}\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N}}.

By Theorem 1.1 of Chapter 8 in
[
22
]
(p.151), the variational problem (
3.4
) with the initial data (
3.2
) admits a unique solution
\Phi
with the smoothness (
3.3
). The proof is complete.
∎

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle(\widehat{U}_{1},-\widehat{U}_{0}),(\widehat{\Phi}_{0},\widehat{\Phi}_{1})\rangle\!\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(DH(\tau),\Phi(\tau))dxdt,

.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

is similar to a real symmetric matrix. For any given

4
Regularity of solutions with coupled Robin boundary conditions

In this section, we will improve the regularity results for Robin problem by means of the regularity results for Neumann problem mentioned in Section

Theorem 4.1
.

When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth bounded domain or a parallelepiped, for any given

and any given

1.8

(U,U^{\prime})\in C^{0}([0,T];(H^{\alpha}(\Omega))^{N}\times(H^{\alpha-1}(\Omega))^{N})

(4.1)

and

(4.2)

where

, and
\alpha
is defined by (
2.6
). Moreover, the linear mapping

We first consider the case that
\Omega

]

Noting (

) and (

) in Lemma

\widehat{U}_{0}\equiv\widehat{U}_{1}\equiv 0

Let

Defining

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi^{\prime\prime}-\Delta\phi=-(e,AU)&\hbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\Omega,\\

(4.5)

(Equation (

By Theorem

the solution

to the following problem with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi^{\prime\prime}-\Delta\psi+b(\psi)=-e^{\lambda h}(e,AU)&\hbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\Omega,\\

satisfies

(\psi,\psi^{\prime})\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)).

(4.9)

Next, we consider the following problem with inhomogeneous Neumann boundary conditions but without internal force terms:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi^{\prime\prime}-\Delta\psi+b(\psi)=0&\hbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}\psi=e^{\lambda h}(e,DH)&\hbox{on}\quad(0,T)\times\Gamma,\\

By (

) and (

) in Lemma

, we have

(\psi,\psi^{\prime})\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{\alpha}(\Omega)\times H^{\alpha-1}(\Omega))

and

\psi|_{\Sigma}\in H^{2\alpha-1}(\Sigma)=H^{2\alpha-1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2\alpha-1}(\Gamma)),

(4.12)

where

is given by the first formula of (

). Since this regularity result holds for all the eigenvectors of

, and all the eigenvectors of
B^{T}
constitute a set of basis in

, we get the desired (

We next consider the case that

is a parallelepiped. Although the boundary is only piecewise smooth, however, using direct eigenfunction expansions as for Theorem 6.1N.1 in

.
∎

5
Exact boundary controllability and non-exact boundary controllability

In this section, we will study the exact boundary controllability and the non-exact boundary controllability for the coupled system (
1.7
) of wave equations with coupled
Robin
boundary controls. We will prove that, for a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, when the number of boundary controls is equal to

, the number of state variables, system (

) is exactly controllable for any given initial data
(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\in({\mathcal{H}}_{1})^{N}\times({\mathcal{H}}_{0})^{N}
, while, for a parallelepiped in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
M=\text{rank}(D)<N
, namely, the number of controls needed is less than the number of variables, system (
1.7
) is not exactly controllable in
({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}
.

5.1
Exact boundary controllability

Definition 5.1
.

System (
1.7
) is exactly null controllable in the space
(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N}\times(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N}
, if there exists a positive constant
T>0
, such that for any given

(\widehat{U}_{1},\widehat{U}_{0})\in(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N}\times(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N}
, there exists a boundary control

H\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M}
, such that problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) admits a unique weak solution

satisfying the final condition

t=T:\quad U=U^{\prime}=0.

(5.1)

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
M=\text{rank}(D)=N
. For a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, system (
1.7
) is exactly controllable at a certain time
T>0
, and the boundary control continuously depends on the initial data:

\displaystyle\|H\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{N})}\leq c\|(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\|_{({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}},

(5.2)

where

is a positive constant.

Proof.

We first consider the corresponding problem (
1.3
) and (
1.8
). By Lemma
1.1
and Remark

, for any given initial data
(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\in({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}
, there exists a boundary control
\widehat{H}\in L_{loc}^{2}(0,+\infty;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{N})
with compact support in
[0,T]
, such that system (
1.3
) with Neumann boundary controls is exactly controllable at the time
T
, and the boundary control
\widehat{H}
continuously depends on the initial data:

\displaystyle\|\widehat{H}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{N})}\leq c_{1}\|(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\|_{({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}},

(5.3)

where
c_{1}>0
is a positive constant.

Noting that

,
D
is invertible and the boundary condition in system (
1.3
)

\partial_{\nu}U=D\widehat{H}\quad\text{on}~(0,T)\times\Gamma

can be rewritten as

\partial_{\nu}U+BU=D(\widehat{H}+D^{-1}BU)\overset{\text{def.}}{=}DH\quad\text{on}~(0,T)\times\Gamma.

Thus, problem (

)–(
1.8
) with (

given by

(5.6)

where

is the solution to problem (
1.3
) and (
1.8
) with (

), realizes the exact boundary controllability of system (

).

It remains to check that

given by (

) belongs to the control space
L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{N})
with continuous dependence (
5.2
).
By the regularity result given in Theorem

(in which we take B=0), the trace
U|_{\Sigma}\in(H^{2\alpha-1}(\Sigma))^{N}
, where
\alpha
is defined by the first formula of (

). Since

, we have

. Moreover, still by Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{N})}\leq c_{2}\big(\|(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\|_{({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}}+\|\widehat{H}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{N})}\big),

(5.7)

where
c_{2}>0
is another positive constant.
By the well-posedness theorem given in Theorem

, it is easy to see that system (

) is exactly controllable by the boundary control function

. Moreover, noting (

), (

∎

Remark 5.1
.

The parallelepiped domain is only piecewise smooth, however, the angles between the corners in a parallelepiped are all equal to
\pi/2
, then, by Grisvard’s results in
[
2
]
(see also p.534 of
[
1
]
),
the Laplacian
\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition and
L^{2}(\Omega)
data has the
H^{2}(\Omega)
-regularity for a parallelepiped
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\leqslant 3
. Therefore, the exact controllability Theorem 5.1 is still valid at least in this case.

5.2
Non-exact boundary controllability

Differently from the case with Neumann boundary controls, the non-exact boundary controllability for the coupled system with coupled Robin boundary controls in a general domain is still an open problem. Fortunately, for some special domains, the solution to problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) may possess higher regularity. In particular, when
\Omega
is a parallelepiped, the optimal regularity of trace
U|_{\Sigma}
almost reaches
(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma))^{N}
. This benefits a lot in the proof of the non-exact boundary controllability for the system with fewer boundary controls. We first give the following result of compactness, then we use it to prove the main Theorem
5.2
in this section that for a parallelepiped
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
M=\text{rank}(D)<N
, then system (
1.7
) is not exactly null controllable.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a parallelepiped. Let
{\cal L}
be a compact linear mapping from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, and let

{\cal R}
be a compact linear mapping from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-\alpha}(\Gamma))
, where
\alpha
is defined by (
2.6
). Then, for any given
T>0
, there exists
\theta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, such that the solution to the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w={\cal L}\theta&\hbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}w={\cal R}\theta&\hbox{on}\quad(0,T)\times\Gamma,\\
t=0:\quad w=0,\quad w^{\prime}=\theta&\hbox{in}~\quad\Omega\end{array}\right.

(5.8)

doesn’t satisfy the final condition

w(T)=w^{\prime}(T)=0.

(5.9)

Proof.

For any given
\theta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, by Lemma
2.3
, the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\phi^{\prime\prime}-\Delta\phi=0&\hbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}\phi=0&\hbox{on}\quad(0,T)\times\Gamma,\\
t=0:\quad\phi=\theta,\quad\phi^{\prime}=0&\hbox{in}\quad\Omega\end{array}\right.

(5.10)

admits a unique solution
\phi
.
By (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) in Lemma
2.3
, we have

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq c\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(5.11)

and

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{\alpha-1}(\Gamma))}\leq c\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(5.12)

where
\alpha
is given by (
2.6
).

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, problem (
5.8
) admits a unique solution
w
. Assume by contradiction that (
5.9
) holds. Then taking the inner product with
\phi
on both sides of (
5.8
) and integrating by parts, it is easy to get

\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\cal L}\theta\phi dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}{\cal R}\theta\phi d\Gamma.

(5.13)

Noting (
5.11
)–(
5.12
), we then have

\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c(\|{\cal L}\theta\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|{\cal R}\theta\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-\alpha}(\Gamma))})

(5.14)

for all
\theta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
which contradicts the compactness of
\cal L
and
\cal R
. ∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
M=\text{rank}(D)<N
. Assume furthermore that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a parallelepiped. Then, no matter how large
T>0
is, system (
1.7
) is not exactly null controllable in the space
({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}
.

Proof.

Assume that
M=\text{rank}(D)<N
. Then there exists an
e\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that
D^{T}e=0
. Take the special initial data

t=0:\quad U=0,\quad U^{\prime}=e\theta

(5.15)

for system (
1.7
). Assume by contradiction that the system is exactly controllable at the time
T>0
. Then for any given
\theta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a boundary control
H\in L^{2}(0,T,(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M})
, such that the corresponding solution satisfies

U(T)=U^{\prime}(T)=0.

(5.16)

Let

w=(e,U),\qquad{\cal L}\theta=-(e,AU),\qquad{\cal R}\theta=-(e,BU)|_{\Sigma}.

(5.17)

Noting that
D^{T}e=0
, we see that
w
satisfies problem (
5.8
) and the final condition (
5.9
).

By Theorem
5.1
, in order to prove Theorem
5.2
, it suffices to show that the linear mapping
{\cal L}
is compact from
L^{2}(\Omega)
into
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, and
{\cal R}
is compact from
L^{2}(\Omega)
into
H^{1-\alpha}(\Sigma)
, where
\alpha
is given by (
2.6
).

Since system (
1.7
) with special initial data (

) is exactly null controllable, the linear mapping

is continuous from

into
L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M})
. By Theorem

, the mapping

is continuous from

into
C^{0}([0,T];(H^{\alpha}(\Omega))^{N})\cap C^{1}([0,T];(H^{\alpha-1}(\Omega))^{N})
. Besides, by Lions’ compact embedding theorem (Theorem 5.1 in
[
23
]
, p68), the following embedding

L^{2}(0,T;(H^{\alpha}(\Omega))^{N})\cap H^{1}(0,T;(H^{\alpha-1}(\Omega))^{N})\}\subset L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Omega))^{N})

\mathcal{L}
is compact from

into

On the other hand, by (

When

is compact from

into

. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

We obtain the non-exact boundary controllability for system (
1.7
) with coupled Robin boundary controls in a parallelepiped
\Omega
when there is a lack of boundary controls.
The main idea is to use the compact perturbation theory which has a higher requirement on the regularity of the solution. The improved regularity (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) with
\alpha=3/4-\epsilon
for a parallelepiped domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a consequence of Lasiecka-Triggiani’s sharp estimation for Neumann problem in
[
5
]
.

On the other hand, the parallelepiped domain is only piecewise smooth. However, by Remark
5.1
, the well-posedness Theorem
4.1
and the exact controllability Theorem
5.1
are still valid for a parallelepiped
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\leqslant 3
. Nevertheless, since Theorem
5.2
takes the assumption that the system is exactly controllable, so, it is valid for all parallelepiped
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
without any restriction on the dimension
n
.

In what follows, all the results on the synchronization will be established in a smooth bounded domain, while the parallelepiped domain will be only used to examine the necessity of compatibility conditions (see §7 below). Theorem
5.2
on the non-exact controllability can be regarded as a start in this direction. How to generalize this result to the general domain is still an open problem.

Exact boundary synchronization by
p
-groups

Based on the results of the exact boundary controllability and the non-exact boundary controllability, we continue to study the

p
-groups for system (

will show that in order to obtain the exact boundary synchronization by

Let

into

\displaystyle(u^{(1)},\cdots,u^{(n_{1})}),\quad(u^{(n_{1}+1)},\cdots,u^{(n_{2})}),\cdots,(u^{(n_{p-1}+1)},\cdots,u^{(n_{p})}).

(6.2)

Definition 6.1
.

System (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable by
p
-groups at the time

in the space
({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}
, if for any given initial data
(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\in({\cal H}_{1})^{N}\times({\cal H}_{0})^{N}

to problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) satisfies

\displaystyle t\geq T:\quad u^{(i)}=u_{r}(t,x),\qquad n_{r-1}+1\leq i\leq n_{r},~1\leq r\leq p,

where,

, being unknown a priori, is called the corresponding exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups.

Remark 6.1
.

In particular, when
p=1
, system (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable. The following theorems in this section also work in that case.

For the given division
0=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{p}=N
, let
S_{r}
be an
(n_{r}-n_{r-1}-1)\times(n_{r}-n_{r-1})
full row-rank matrix:

\displaystyle S_{r}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&-1&&&\\
&1&-1&&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&1&-1\end{array}\right),\qquad 1\leq r\leq p,

and let

be the following
(N-p)\times N
matrix of synchronization by

-groups:

\displaystyle C_{p}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}S_{1}&&&\\
&S_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&S_{p}\end{array}\right).

Evidently, we have

\displaystyle\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})=\hbox{Span}\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{p}\},

where for

(e_{r})_{i}=\begin{cases}1,\qquad n_{r-1}+1\leq i\leq n_{r},\\

Thus, the exact boundary synchronization by

-groups (
6.3
) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle t\geq T:\quad C_{p}U\equiv 0

(6.13)

or

\displaystyle t\geq T:\quad U=\sum_{r=1}^{p}u_{r}e_{r}.

(6.14)

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth bounded domain. Let

be the

-groups defined by (

)–(

). Assume that both

and

Then there exists a boundary control matrix

satisfying

such that system (

) is exactly synchronizable by

-groups, and the corresponding boundary control

possesses the following continuous dependence:

\displaystyle\|H\|_{L^{2}(0,T,(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{N-p})}\leq c\|C_{p}(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N-p}\times(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N-p}},

where

is a positive constant.

Proof.

Since both

and

satisfy the conditions of

-compatibility (

), by Lemma 3.3 in
[

, there exist matrices

and

)

with the initial data:

Since

Remark 6.2
.

Noting Theorem

The well-posedness of the reduced problem (

.

(7.2)

Proof.

Since system (

) is exactly synchronizable by

-groups, taking the special initial data (

) for any given

, the solution

to problem (

) and (

) satisfies (
6.3
) (or (

)) under boundary control

.
Let

We easily get

) for

Remark 7.1
.

The condition of
C_{p}
-compatibility (
7.7
) is equivalent to the fact that there exist constants
\alpha_{rs}~(1\leq r,s\leq p)
such that

\displaystyle Ae_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{sr}e_{s}\quad 1\leqslant r\leqslant p,

(7.18)

or
A
satisfies the following row-sum condition by blocks:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=n_{r-1}+1}^{n_{r}}a_{ij}=\alpha_{sr},\quad n_{s-1}+1\leq i\leq n_{s},\quad 1\leq r,s\leq p.

(7.19)

In particular, when

\displaystyle\alpha_{sr}=0,\quad 1\leq r,s\leq p,

(7.20)

we say that

satisfies the zero-sum condition by blocks.
In this case, we have

\displaystyle Ae_{r}=0,\quad 1\leq r\leq p.

(7.21)

7.2
Condition of

-compatibility for the boundary coupling matrix

Comparing with the internal coupling matrix

-compatibility for the boundary coupling matrix

Let

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=(0,\cdots,\overset{(i)}{1},\cdots,0)^{T},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N

(7.22)

be a set of classical orthogonal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and let

\displaystyle V_{r}=\text{Span}\{\varepsilon_{n_{r-1}+1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n_{r}}\},\qquad 1\leq r\leq p.

\displaystyle e_{r}\in V_{r},\quad 1\leqslant r\leqslant p.

(7.24)

In what follows, we will discuss the necessity of the condition of
C_{p}
-compatibility for the boundary coupling matrix
B
under the assumption that
Ae_{r}\in V_{r}
and

.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a parallelepiped.
Assume that
M=\text{rank}(D)=N-p
and

Ae_{r}\in V_{r},\quad Be_{r}\in V_{r}\quad(1\leqslant r\leqslant p).

(7.25)

If system (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable by
p
-groups, then the boundary coupling matrix

should satisfy the following condition of

-compatibility:

B\text{Ker}(C_{p})\subseteq\text{Ker}(C_{p}).

(7.26)

Proof.

By (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{p}(u^{\prime\prime}_{r}e_{r}-\Delta u_{r}e_{r}+u_{r}Ae_{r})=0&\hbox{in }(T,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\

)–(

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u^{\prime\prime}_{r}e_{r}-\Delta u_{r}e_{r}+u_{r}Ae_{r}=0&\hbox{in }(T,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\partial_{\nu}u_{r}e_{r}+u_{r}Be_{r}=0&\hbox{on }(T,+\infty)\times\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

(7.32)

then it is easy to check that

Let

(7.34)

We have (
7.14

Again, since system (

7.3
Conditions of
C_{2}
-compatibility

In this section, we will study the necessity of the condition of

-compatibility (when

) for
B
for a specific example, where the restricted condition on

given in (

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a parallelepiped. Assume that the coupling matrix
A
satisfies the zero-sum condition by blocks (
7.21
). Assume furthermore that system (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable by 2-groups with
M=\text{rank}(D)=N-2
. Then the coupling matrix
B
necessarily satisfies the condition of
C_{2}
-compatibility:

B\text{Ker}(C_{2})\subseteq\text{Ker}(C_{2}).

(7.38)

Proof.

By the exact boundary synchronization by 2-groups of (
1.7
), we have

t\geq T:\quad U=e_{1}u_{1}+e_{2}u_{2}\quad\hbox{in }\Omega.

(7.39)

Noting (
7.21
), as
t\geq T
we have

AU=Ae_{1}u_{1}+Ae_{2}u_{2}=0,

then it is easy to see that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}U^{\prime\prime}-\Delta U=0&\hbox{in}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}U+BU=0&\hbox{on}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

(7.40)

Let
P
be a matrix such that
\widehat{B}=PBP^{-1}
is a real symmetric matrix. Denote

u=(u_{1},u_{2})^{T}.

(7.41)

Taking the inner product on both sides of (
7.40
) with
P^{T}Pe_{i}
for
i=1,2
, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}Lu^{\prime\prime}-L\Delta u=0&\hbox{in}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
L\partial_{\nu}u+\Lambda u=0&\hbox{on}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Gamma,\end{array}\right.

(7.42)

where the matrices
L
and
\Lambda
are given by

L=(Pe_{i},Pe_{j})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Lambda=(\widehat{B}Pe_{i},Pe_{j}),\quad 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 2,

(7.43)

respectively. Clearly,
L
is a symmetric and positive definite matrix and
\Lambda
is a symmetric matrix.

Taking the inner product on both sides of (
7.42
) with
L^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and denoting
w=L^{\frac{1}{2}}u
, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w=0&\hbox{in}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}w+\widehat{\Lambda}w=0&\hbox{on}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Gamma,\end{array}\right.

(7.44)

where
\widehat{\Lambda}=L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda L^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is also a symmetric matrix.

On the other hand, taking the inner product with
C_{2}
on both sides of the boundary condition on
\Gamma
in system (
1.7
) and noting (
7.39
), we get

t\geq T:\qquad C_{2}Be_{1}u_{1}+C_{2}Be_{2}u_{2}\equiv 0\qquad\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma.

(7.45)

We claim that
C_{2}Be_{1}=C_{2}Be_{2}=0
, namely,
B
satisfies the condition of
C_{2}
-compatibility (
7.38
).

Otherwise, without loss of generality, we way assume that
C_{2}Be_{1}\not=0
.
Then it follows from (
7.45
) that there exists a non-zero vector
D_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

t\geqslant T:\quad D_{2}^{T}u\equiv 0\quad\hbox{on }\Gamma.

(7.46)

Denoting

\widehat{D}^{T}=D_{2}^{T}L^{-\frac{1}{2}},

we then have

t\geq T:\qquad\widehat{D}^{T}w=0\qquad\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma,

(7.47)

in which
w=L^{\frac{1}{2}}u
.
By the multiplier method, we can prove that the following Kalman’s criterion

\hbox{rank}(\widehat{D},\widehat{\Lambda}\widehat{D})=2

(7.48)

is sufficient for the unique continuation of system (
7.44
) under the observation (
7.47
) on the infinite horizon
[T,+\infty)
(see Theorem 3.22 and Remark 3.12 in
[
27
]
). Since
M=\text{rank}(D)=N-2
, by Theorem
5.2
, system (
1.7
) is not exactly null controllable. So, the rank condition (
7.48
) does not hold. Thus, there exists a vector
E\not=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

\widehat{\Lambda}^{T}E=\widehat{\Lambda}E=\mu E\quad\hbox{and}\quad\widehat{D}^{T}E=0.

(7.49)

Noting (
7.47
) and the second formula of (
7.49
), we have both
E
and
w|_{\Gamma}\in\text{Ker}(\widehat{D})
. Since
\text{dim }\text{Ker}(\widehat{D})=1
,
there exists a constant
\alpha
such that
w=\alpha E
on
\Gamma
. Therefore, noting the first formula of (
7.49
), we have

\widehat{\Lambda}w=\widehat{\Lambda}\alpha E=\mu\alpha E=\mu w\quad\text{on }\Gamma.

(7.50)

Thus, (
7.44
) can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w=0&\hbox{in}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}w+\mu w=0&\hbox{on}\quad(T,+\infty)\times\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

(7.51)

Let
z=\widehat{D}^{T}w
. Noting (
7.47
),it follows from (
7.51
) that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}z^{\prime\prime}-\Delta z=0&\hbox{in }(T,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}z=z=0&\hbox{on }(T,+\infty)\times\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

(7.52)

Then, by Holmgren’s uniqueness theorem, we have

t\geqslant T:\quad z=\widehat{D}^{T}w=D_{2}^{T}u\equiv 0\quad\hbox{in }\Omega.

(7.53)

Let
D_{2}^{T}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
. Define the following row vector

c_{3}=\frac{\alpha_{1}e_{1}^{T}}{\|e_{1}\|^{2}}+\frac{\alpha_{2}e_{2}^{T}}{\|e_{2}\|^{2}}.

(7.54)

Noting
(e_{1},e_{2})=0
and (

t\geqslant T:\quad c_{3}U=\alpha_{1}u_{1}+\alpha_{2}u_{2}=D_{2}^{T}u\equiv 0\quad\hbox{in }\Omega.

(7.55)

Let

\widetilde{C}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}C_{2}\\
c_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

(7.56)

We get

t\geq T:\qquad\widetilde{C}_{1}U=0\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega.

(7.57)

Since

and

Since system (

U
to problem (

) of

8
Determination of the exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups

In general, exactly synchronizable states by
p
-groups depend not only on initial data, but also on applied boundary controls. However, when the coupling matrices
A
and
B
satisfy certain algebraic conditions, the exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups can be independent of applied boundary controls. In this section, we first discuss the case when the exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups is independent of applied boundary controls, then we present the estimate on each exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups in general situation.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded domain. Assume that both
A
and
B
satisfy the conditions of
C_{p}
-compatibility (
6.15
). Assume furthermore that
A^{T}
and
B^{T}
possess a common invariant subspace
V
, biorthogonal to Ker
(C_{p})
(see Definition 2.1 in
[
19
]
). Then there exists a boundary control matrix
D
with
M=\hbox{rank}(D)=\hbox{rank}(C_{p}D)=N-p
, such that system (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable by
p
-groups, and the exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups
u=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{p})^{T}
is independent of applied boundary controls.

Proof.

Define the boundary control matrix
D
by

\hbox{Ker}(D^{T})=V.

(8.1)

Since
V
is biorthogonal to Ker
(C_{p})
, by Lemma 2.5 in
[
19
]
, we have

\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})\cap\hbox{Im}(D)=\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})\cap V^{\perp}=\{0\},

(8.2)

then, by Lemma 2.2 in
[
19
]
, we have

\hbox{rank}(C_{p}D)=\hbox{rank}(D)=M=N-p.

(8.3)

Therefore, by Theorem
6.1
, system (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable by
p
-groups. Let
U
be the solution to problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
), which realizes the exact boundary synchronization by
p
-groups at time
T>0
under such
D
and boundary control
H
.

By (
8.2
), noting
\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})=\text{Span}\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{p}\}
, we may write

V=\text{Span}\{E_{1},\cdots,E_{p}\}\quad\text{with}~(e_{r},E_{s})=\delta_{rs}(r,s=1,\cdots,p).

(8.4)

Since
V
is a common invariant subspace of
A^{T}
and
B^{T}
, there exist constants
\alpha_{rs}
and
\beta_{rs}(r,s=1,\cdots,p)
such that

A^{T}E_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s},\qquad B^{T}E_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}E_{s}.

(8.5)

For
r=1,\cdots,p
, let

\phi_{r}=(E_{r},U).

(8.6)

By system (
1.7
) and noting (
8.1
), for
r=1,\cdots,p
we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\phi_{r}^{\prime\prime}-\Delta\phi_{r}+\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}\phi_{s}=0&\hbox{in}\quad(0,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\phi_{r}+\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}\phi_{s}=0&\hbox{on}\quad(0,+\infty)\times\Gamma,\\
t=0:\quad\phi_{r}=(E_{r},\widehat{U}_{0}),\quad\phi_{r}^{\prime}=(E_{r},\widehat{U}_{1})&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(8.7)

On the other hand, for
r=1,\cdots,p
we have

t\geq T:\quad\phi_{r}=(E_{r},U)=\sum_{s=1}^{p}(E_{r},e_{s})u_{s}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\delta_{rs}u_{s}=u_{r}.

(8.8)

Thus, the exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups
u=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{p})^{T}
is entirely determined by the solution to problem (
8.7
), which is independent of applied boundary controls
H
.
∎

The following result gives the counterpart of Theorem
8.1
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded domain (say, with

and

satisfy the conditions of

-compatibility (

) is exactly synchronizable by

of dimension

, such that the projection functions

\phi_{r}=(E_{r},U),\qquad r=1,\cdots,p

(8.9)

are independent of applied boundary controls

, where

is the solution to problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
), which realizes the exact boundary synchronization by

-groups at time

,
then

is a common invariant subspace of
A^{T}
and

,

, and biorthogonal to Ker
(C_{p})

Proof.

Let
(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})=(0,0)
. By Theorem
4.1
, the linear mapping

F:\quad H\rightarrow(U,U^{\prime})

is continuous from
L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M})
to
C^{0}([0,T];(H^{\alpha}(\Omega))^{N}\times(H^{\alpha-1}(\Omega))^{N})
, where
\alpha
is defined by (
2.6
).
Let
F^{\prime}
denote the Fréchet derivative of the application
F
. For any given
\widehat{H}\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M})
, we define

\displaystyle\widehat{U}=F^{\prime}(0)\widehat{H}.

(8.10)

By linearity,
\widehat{U}
satisfies a system similar to that of
U
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\widehat{U}^{\prime\prime}-{\Delta}\widehat{U}+A\widehat{U}=0\quad&\hbox{in }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}\widehat{U}+B\widehat{U}=D\widehat{H}&\hbox{on }(0,+\infty)\times\Gamma\\
t=0:\quad\widehat{U}=\widehat{U}^{\prime}=0&\hbox{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(8.11)

Since the projection functions
\phi_{r}=(E_{r},U)~(r=1,\cdots,p)
are independent of applied boundary controls
H
,
we have

\displaystyle(E_{r},\widehat{U})\equiv 0,\quad\forall~\widehat{H}\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M}),\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.12)

First, we prove that
E_{r}\not\in\hbox{Im}(C_{p}^{T})
for
r=1,\cdots,p
. Otherwise, there exist an
\bar{r}
and a vector
R_{\bar{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-p}
, such that
E_{\bar{r}}=C_{p}^{T}R_{\bar{r}}
, then we have

\displaystyle 0=(E_{\bar{r}},\widehat{U})=(R_{\bar{r}},C_{p}\widehat{U}),\quad\forall\widehat{H}\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M}).

(8.13)

Since
C_{p}\widehat{U}
is the solution to the corresponding reduced problem (
6.20
)–(
6.21
), noting the equivalence between the exact boundary synchronization by
p
-groups for the original system and the exact boundary controllability for the reduced system, from the exact boundary synchronization by
p
-groups for system (
1.7
), we know that the reduced system (
6.20
) is exactly controllable, then the value of
C_{p}\widehat{U}
at the time
T
can be chosen arbitrarily, thus we get

R_{\bar{r}}=0,

which contradicts
E_{\bar{r}}\not=0
. Then, we have
E_{r}\not\in\hbox{Im}(C_{p}^{T})~(r=1,\cdots,p)
.
Thus
V\cap\{\text{Ker}(C_{p})\}^{\perp}=V\cap\text{Im}(C_{p}^{T})=\{0\}
. Hence by Lemma 4.2 and Lemma 4.3 in
[
27
]
,
V
is bi-orthonormal to Ker
(C_{p})
, and
then
(V,C_{p}^{T})
constitutes a set of basis in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Therefore, there exist constant coefficients
\alpha_{rs}~(r,s=1,\cdots,p)
and vectors
P_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-p}~(r=1,\cdots,p)
, such that

\displaystyle A^{T}E_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s}+C_{p}^{T}P_{r},\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.14)

Taking the inner product with
E_{r}
on both sides of the equations in (
8.11
) and noting (
8.12
), we get

0=(A\widehat{U},E_{r})=(\widehat{U},A^{T}E_{r})=(\widehat{U},C_{p}^{T}P_{r})=(C_{p}\widehat{U},P_{r})

(8.15)

for
r=1,\cdots,p.
Similarly, by the exact boundary controllability for the reduced system (
6.20
), we get
P_{r}=0~(r=1,\cdots,p)
, thus we have

\displaystyle A^{T}E_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s},\quad r=1,\cdots,p,

which means that
V
is an invariant subspace of
A^{T}
.

On the other hand, noting (
8.12
) and taking the inner product with
E_{r}
on both sides of the boundary condition on
\Gamma
in (
8.11
), we get

(E_{r},B\widehat{U})=(E_{r},D\widehat{H})\quad\hbox{on }\Gamma,\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.16)

By Theorem
4.1
, for
r=1,\cdots,p
we have

\displaystyle\|(E_{r},D\widehat{H})\|_{H^{2\alpha-1}(\Sigma)}

(8.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(E_{r},B\widehat{U})\|_{H^{2\alpha-1}(\Sigma)}\leqslant c\|\widehat{H}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{M})},

where
\alpha
is given by (
2.6
).

We claim that
D^{T}E_{r}=0
for
r=1,\cdots,p
. Otherwise, for
r=1,\cdots,p
, setting
\widehat{H}=D^{T}E_{r}v
, it follows from (
8.17
) that

\|v\|_{H^{2\alpha-1}(\Sigma)}\leqslant c\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}.

(8.18)

Since
2\alpha-1>0
, it contradicts the compactness of
H^{2\alpha-1}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma)
.
Thus, by (
8.16
) we have

(E_{r},B\widehat{U})=0\quad\hbox{on }(0,T)\times\Gamma,\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.19)

Similarly, there exist constants
\beta_{rs}~(r,s=1,\cdots,p)
and vectors
Q_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-p}~(r=1,\cdots,p)
, such that

\displaystyle B^{T}E_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}E_{s}+C_{p}^{T}Q_{r},\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.20)

Substituting it into (
8.19
) and noting (
8.12
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}(E_{s},\widehat{U})+(C_{p}^{T}Q_{r},\widehat{U})=(Q_{r},C_{p}\widehat{U})=0,\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.21)

By the exact boundary controllability for the reduced system (
6.20
) , we get
Q_{r}=0~(r=1,\cdots,p)
, then we have

B^{T}E_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}E_{s},\quad r=1,\cdots,p,

(8.22)

which indicates that
V
is also an invariant subspace of
B^{T}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 8.1
.

When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a parallelepiped, Theorem
8.2
is still valid with the same proof.

When
A
and
B
do not satisfy all the conditions mentioned in Theorem
8.1
, exactly synchronizable states by
p
-groups may depend on applied boundary controls. We have the following

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded domain. Assume that both
A
and
B
satisfy the conditions of
C_{p}
-compatibility (
6.15
). Then there exists a boundary control matrix
D
such that system (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable by
p
-groups, and each exactly synchronizable state by
p
-groups
u=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{p})^{T}
satisfies the following estimate:

\|(u,u^{\prime})(T)-(\phi,\phi^{\prime})(T)\|_{(H^{\alpha+1}(\Omega))^{p}\times(H^{\alpha}(\Omega))^{p}}\leq c\|C_{p}(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\|_{(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{N-p}\times(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{N-p}},

(8.23)

where
\alpha
is defined by the first formula of (
2.6
),
c
is a positive constant and
\phi=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{p})^{T}
is the solution to the following problem
(1\leq r\leq p)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\phi_{r}^{\prime\prime}-\Delta\phi_{r}+\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}\phi_{s}=0&\hbox{in}\quad(0,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\phi_{r}+\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}\phi_{s}=0&\hbox{on}\quad(0,+\infty)\times\Gamma,\\
t=0:\qquad\phi_{r}=(E_{r},\widehat{U}_{0}),\quad\phi_{r}^{\prime}=(E_{r},\widehat{U}_{1})&\hbox{in}\quad\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(8.24)

in which

Ae_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{sr}e_{s},\quad Be_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{sr}e_{s},\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.25)

Proof.

We first show that there exists a subspace
V
, which is invariant for
B^{T}
and bi-orthonormal to Ker
(C_{p})
.

Let
B=P^{-1}\Lambda P,
where
P
is an invertible matrix, and
\Lambda
be a symmetric matrix. Let
V=\hbox{Span}\{E_{1},\cdots,E_{p}\}
in which

E_{r}=P^{T}Pe_{r},\qquad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.26)

Noting (
6.12
) and the fact that
\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})
is an invariant subspace of
B
, we get

B^{T}E_{r}=P^{T}PBe_{r}\subseteq P^{T}P\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})\subseteq V\qquad r=1,\cdots,p,

(8.27)

then

V
is invariant for
B^{T}
.

We next show that
V^{\perp}\cap\text{Ker}(C_{p})=\{0\}
. Then, noting that dim
(V)
= dim
\text{Ker}(C_{p})=p
, by Lemma 4.2 and Lemma 4.3 in
[
27
]
,
V
is bi-orthonormal to
\text{Ker}(C_{p})
.
For this purpose, let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{p}
be coefficients such that

\sum_{r=1}^{p}a_{r}e_{r}\in V^{\perp}.

(8.28)

Then

(\sum_{r=1}^{p}a_{r}e_{r},E_{s})=(\sum_{r=1}^{p}a_{r}Pe_{r},Pe_{s})=0,\quad s=1,\cdots,p.

(8.29)

It follows that

(\sum_{r=1}^{p}a_{r}Pe_{r},\sum_{s=1}^{p}a_{s}Pe_{s})=0,

(8.30)

then
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{p}=0
, namely,
V^{\perp}\cap\text{Ker}(C_{p})=\{0\}
.

Denoting

\displaystyle Be_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{sr}e_{s},\quad r=1,\cdots,p,

(8.31)

a direct calculation yields that

\displaystyle B^{T}E_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}E_{s},\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.32)

Define the boundary control matrix
D
by

\text{Ker}(D^{T})=V.

(8.33)

Noting (
6.12
), we have

\displaystyle\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})\cap\hbox{Im}(D)

(8.34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})\cap\{\hbox{Ker}(D^{T})\}^{\perp}=\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})\cap V^{\perp}=\{0\},

then, by Lemma 2.2 in
[
19
]
, we have

\hbox{rank}(C_{p}D)=\hbox{rank}(D)=M=N-p.

(8.35)

Therefore, by Theorem
6.1
, system (
1.7
) is exactly synchronizable by
p
-groups. Let
U
be the solution to problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
), which realizes the exact boundary synchronization by
p
-groups at time
T
under such
D
and boundary control
H
.

Denoting
\psi_{r}=(E_{r},U)(r=1,\cdots,p)
, we have

\displaystyle(E_{r},AU)=(A^{T}E_{r},U)

(8.36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s}+A^{T}E_{r}-\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s},U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}(E_{s},U)+(A^{T}E_{r}-\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s},U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}\psi_{s}+(A^{T}E_{r}-\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s},U).

By the assumption that
V
is bi-orthonormal to
\hbox{Ker}(C_{p})
, without loss of generality, we may assume that

\displaystyle(E_{r},e_{s})=\delta_{rs}\quad(r,s=1,\cdots,p).

(8.37)

Then, for any given
k=1,\cdots,p
, by the first formula of (
8.25
), we get

\displaystyle(A^{T}E_{r}-\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s},e_{k})=(E_{r},Ae_{k})-\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}(E_{s},e_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{sk}(E_{r},e_{s})-\alpha_{rk}=\alpha_{rk}-\alpha_{rk}=0,

hence

A^{T}E_{r}-\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s}\in\{\text{Ker}(C_{p})\}^{\bot}=\text{Im}(C_{p}^{T}),\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.38)

Thus, there exist
R_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-p}(r=1,\cdots,p)
, such that

\displaystyle A^{T}E_{r}-\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}E_{s}=C_{p}^{T}R_{r},\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.39)

Taking the inner product on both sides of problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) with
E_{r}
, and noting (
8.32
)–(
8.33
), for
r=1,\cdots,p
we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\psi_{r}^{\prime\prime}-\Delta\psi_{r}+\sum_{s=1}^{p}\alpha_{rs}\psi_{s}=-(R_{r},C_{p}U)&\hbox{in }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\psi_{r}+\sum_{s=1}^{p}\beta_{rs}\psi_{s}=0&\hbox{on}(0,+\infty)\times\Gamma,\\
t=0:\quad\psi_{r}=(E_{r},\widehat{U}_{0}),\ \psi_{r}^{\prime}=(E_{r},\widehat{U}_{1})&\hbox{in }\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(8.40)

Then, by the classic semigroups theory, we have

\displaystyle\|(\psi,\psi^{\prime})(T)-(\phi,\phi^{\prime})(T)\|_{(H^{\alpha+1}(\Omega))^{p}\times(H^{\alpha}(\Omega))^{p}}\leqslant c_{1}\|(R_{r},C_{p}U)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{\alpha}(\Omega))}

(8.41)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{2}\|C_{p}(\widehat{U}_{0},\widehat{U}_{1})\|_{(H^{1}(\Omega))^{N-p}\times(L^{2}(\Omega))^{N-p}},

where
c_{i}
for
i=1,2
are different positive constants,
\alpha
is given by the first formula of (
2.6
), and the second inequality follows from (
5.2
) and Theorem
4.1
since
C_{p}U
is the solution to the reduced problem (
6.20
)–(
6.21
).

On the other hand, noting (
8.37
), it is easy to see that

t\geqslant T:\quad\psi_{r}=(E_{r},U)=\sum_{s=1}^{p}(E_{r},e_{s})u_{s}=u_{r},\quad r=1,\cdots,p.

(8.42)

Substituting it into (
8.41
), we get (
8.23
).
∎

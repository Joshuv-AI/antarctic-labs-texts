Dimensional reduction

Figure S1

(
S2.1

p=10,15,20
dimensions.
Each boxplot shows the distribution across 112 seizures.
Two sets of boxes are shown for the interictal time series: (i) when dimensional reduction was performed independently from reduction of the corresponding seizure data and (ii) when dimensional reduction was performed using the
U
matrix obtained from the seizure data.

Dimensional reduction was performed using the method described in the main text.
Figure
S1
shows the proportion of variance retained in the reduced data sets, as defined by

\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_{j}}{\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_{j}}

(S2.1)

where
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{q}\geq 0
are the eigenvalues of
\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i}S_{i}^{\prime}
.

In addition to the dimensional reduction method described in the main text, we also tested a method that identified sets of channels, rather than linear combinations of channels, thereby potentially giving a more direct interpretation of the reduced data.
The method aimed at minimizing redundancy in the data by bounding eigenvalues of the reduced covariance matrices away from zero.
Given a set of channels
C\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
, let
S^{\prime}_{i}(C)
denote the restriction of the full covariance matrix
S^{\prime}_{i}
to
C
and define

\varphi(C)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\min\sigma\left[S^{\prime}_{i}(C)\right]

(S2.2)

where
\sigma[A]
denotes the set of eigenvalues of
A\in\mathrm{Sym}({p})
.
A greedy algorithm was used to construct sets
C
which maximized
\varphi(C)
for a fixed value of
p
.
This method of dimensional reduction was inferior to the former method: the proportion of variance in the reduced data (see Equation
(
S2.1
)
below) was greater with the former method, but the mean minimum eigenvalue (Equation
(
S2.2
)
) was similar for both methods as shown in Figure
S2
.
Consequently, the former method, detailed in the main text, was chosen for dimensional reduction before further modeling analysis.

S4.
Parameter inference

Assuming
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}
is normally distributed, the likelihood function of our manifold-adapted model is

{}\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{L},B,\Sigma|\boldsymbol{v})&=\prod_{k=L+1}^{n}(2\pi)^{-\frac{m}{2}}|\Sigma|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\times\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}A_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-B\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right)^{T}\Sigma^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{L}A_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-B\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right)\right\}.\end{split}

(S4.1)

The following two subsections give a detailed description of MLE for the scalar and diagonal models.
We then discuss the asymptotic covariance of the estimated parameters.

S4.1.
MLE for the scalar coefficient model

Denote
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{L},\beta)^{T}
and
\Sigma=\sigma^{2}I_{m}
. Taking the logarithm of likelihood function
\log(\mathcal{L})
calculated in Equation (
S4.1
) for the scalar coefficient model, we have

{}\log(\mathcal{L})=-\frac{m(n-L)}{2}\log(2\pi)-m(n-L)\log(\sigma)-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{L}\alpha_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\beta\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right\|^{2},

(S4.2)

where
\|\cdot\|
is the Euclidean norm.

Subsequently, we can calculate first derivatives with respect to
\alpha_{\ell}(\ell=1,\ldots,L)
and
\beta
as

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}}&=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}^{T}(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\beta\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k})=0\\
\frac{\partial\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\beta}&=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}}^{T}(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{L}\alpha_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\beta\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k})=0.\end{split}

Therefore, the MLE parameter vector
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=(\hat{\alpha}_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{L},\hat{\beta})^{T}
is obtained by solving

\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}\|^{2}&\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}&\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}&\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}\|^{2}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}&\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}&\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}&\cdots&\|\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}\|^{2}&\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\\
{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}&{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}&\cdots&{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}&\|\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\|^{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{L}\\
\beta\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{k2}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\\
\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\\
{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\end{bmatrix},

so that
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=(Z^{T}Z)^{-1}Z^{T}Y
, where
Z=\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}\|^{2}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}&\cdots&\|\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\|^{2}\end{bmatrix}
and
Y=\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\\
\vdots\\
{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\end{bmatrix}
for
k=L+1,...,n
.

Additionally,
\hat{\sigma}
is found by computing the first derivative of log-likelihood function
\log(\mathcal{L})
with respect to
\sigma
and letting it be 0, so that

\begin{gathered}\frac{\partial\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma}=-\frac{m(n-L)}{\sigma}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\right\|^{2}\\
\hat{\sigma}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{m(n-L)}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\right\|^{2}},\end{gathered}

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\hat{\beta}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}
is the estimated observation.

S4.2.
MLE for the diagonal model

When
A_{\ell}=\mathrm{diag}\left({a_{\ell 1},\ldots,a_{\ell m}}\right),B=\mathrm{diag}\left({b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}}\right)
, and
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}\left({\sigma_{1}^{2},\ldots,\sigma^{2}_{m}}\right)
are diagonal matrices, we can represent model as

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\begin{bmatrix}v_{k\ell,1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&v_{k\ell,m}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a_{\ell 1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{\ell m}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}v^{*}_{k,1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&v^{*}_{k,m}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}b_{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{m}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon_{k1}\\
\vdots\\
\epsilon_{km}\end{bmatrix}\\
&=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\boldsymbol{u}_{il}\boldsymbol{a}_{\ell}+\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i},\end{split}

where
\boldsymbol{u}
are diagonal matrices of
\boldsymbol{v}
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{u}=\mathrm{diag}\left({v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}}\right)
;
\boldsymbol{a}_{\ell}=(a_{\ell 1},...,a_{\ell m})^{T},\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{1},...,b_{m})^{T}
are vectors from diagonal matrix
A_{\ell},B,\ell=1,\ldots,L
. Furthermore,
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\sim N(0,\Sigma)
and
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}\left({\sigma_{1}^{2},\ldots,\sigma_{m}^{2}}\right)
.

The log-likelihood function is then written as

{}\begin{split}\log(\mathcal{L})&=-\frac{m(n-L)}{2}\log(2\pi)-\frac{n-L}{2}\log(\sigma_{1}^{2}\cdots\sigma_{m}^{2})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\boldsymbol{u}_{k\ell}\boldsymbol{a}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\boldsymbol{b}\right)^{T}\Sigma^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\boldsymbol{u}_{k\ell}\boldsymbol{a}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\boldsymbol{b}\right).\end{split}

(S4.3)

To calculate the maximum likelihoood estimators, the first derivatives of the log-likelihood with respect to coefficients
A_{\ell}
and
B
are calculated and set to 0. Then, the estimated
\hat{\Theta}=(\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1},...,\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{L},\hat{\boldsymbol{b}})^{T}
is found using

\hat{\Theta}=(Z^{T}Z)^{-1}Z^{T}Y,

where
Z=\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{u}_{k1}\boldsymbol{u}_{k1}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{u}_{k1}\boldsymbol{u}_{kL}&\boldsymbol{u}_{k1}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{u}_{kL}\boldsymbol{u}_{k1}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{u}_{kL}\boldsymbol{u}_{kL}&\boldsymbol{u}_{kL}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\\
\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\boldsymbol{u}_{k1}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\boldsymbol{u}_{kL}&\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\\
\end{bmatrix}
and
Y=\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{u}_{k1}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\\
\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{u}_{kL}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\\
\boldsymbol{u}^{*}_{k}\boldsymbol{v}_{k}\end{bmatrix}
.

Similarly,
\hat{\sigma}_{r}
is found setting the derivative of the log-likelihood to zero:

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}}&=-\frac{n-L}{\sigma_{r}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\right)_{r}^{2}=0\\
\hat{\sigma}_{r}&=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\right)_{r}^{2}}{n-L}},\end{split}

where
(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})_{r}
is the
r
th component of residual vector
\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\right)^{2}
,
r=1,...,m
.

S4.3.
Asymptotic covariance matrix of estimators

The asymptotic covariance matrix of maximum likelihood estimator of
\Phi=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{L},\beta,\sigma)^{T}
in the scalar coefficient model (or
\Phi=(\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_{L},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{\sigma})^{T}
in the diagonal model) is used to construct confidence intervals and provides a measurement of the uncertainty associated with the estimated parameters.

S4.3.1.
Fisher information matrix of estimators in the scalar coefficient model.

In model with scalar coefficients, we firstly compute the
Hessian matrix
, which is the second derivative of log-likelihood function defined as

H(\log(\mathcal{L}))=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{1}\partial\alpha_{L}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{1}\partial\beta}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{1}\partial\sigma}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{1}\partial\alpha_{L}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{L}^{2}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{L}\partial\beta}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{L}\partial\sigma}\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{1}\partial\beta}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{L}\partial\beta}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\beta^{2}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\beta\partial\sigma}\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{1}\partial\sigma}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{L}\partial\sigma}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\beta\partial\sigma}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma^{2}}\end{bmatrix}

where the second cross-partial derivative of
\log(\mathcal{L})
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}\partial\alpha_{o}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}\partial\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}^{T}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m(n-L)}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{3}{\sigma^{4}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{i}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\hat{\beta}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}\partial\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}}\left(-\frac{m(n-L)}{\sigma}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\hat{\beta}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\hat{\beta}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sigma^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\hat{\beta}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right)

where
\ell,o=1,\ldots,L
.

Recall the model
\boldsymbol{v}_{k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}+\hat{\beta}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\beta\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{i}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\beta\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=m\sigma^{2}.

Consequently, the negative expectation of the second partial derivative of
\log(\mathcal{L})
are

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}\partial\alpha_{o}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{k\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{ko}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}\partial\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{k\ell}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma^{2}}\right]&=-\frac{m(n-L)}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{3}{\sigma^{4}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\beta\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right\|^{2}\right]\\
&=-\frac{m(n-L)}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{3}{\sigma^{4}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}m\sigma^{2}\\
&=-\frac{m(n-L)}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{3m(n-L)\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}\\
&=\frac{2m(n-L)}{\sigma^{2}}\end{split}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\alpha_{\ell}\partial\sigma}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sigma^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}^{T}]\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{v}_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{k\ell}-\beta\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}\right]\right)=0

Therefore, the
Fisher information matrix
is

\displaystyle I(\Phi)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}[H(\log(\mathcal{L}))]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{k1}\boldsymbol{v}_{k1}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{k1}\boldsymbol{v}_{kL}&\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{k1}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&0\\
{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}}^{T}_{k}\boldsymbol{v}_{i1}&\cdots&{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}}^{T}_{k}\boldsymbol{v}_{iL}&{\boldsymbol{v}^{*}}^{T}_{k}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}_{k}&0\\
0&\cdots&0&0&2m\end{bmatrix}

S4.3.2.
Fisher information matrix of estimation in the diagonal model.

Analogously, we can obtain the Fisher information matrix of maximum likelihood estimator
\Phi=(a_{11},\ldots,a_{1m},a_{21},\ldots,a_{Lm},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})^{T}
from the Hessian matrix. Recalling the log-likelihood function of the manifold-adapted model with diagonal matrix coefficients and representing the diagonal matrix coefficients as vectors, log-likelihood function in (
S4.3
) could be written as

\begin{split}\log(\mathcal{L})=&-\frac{m(n-L)}{2}\log(2\pi)-\frac{n-L}{2}\log(\sigma_{1}^{2}\cdots\sigma_{m}^{2})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}(v_{kr}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}a_{\ell r}v_{k\ell,r}-v^{*}_{k,r}b_{r})^{2}\right)\end{split}

(S4.4)

where
r=1,\ldots,m;\ell=1,\ldots,L;k=L+1,\ldots,n
. Compute the first derivatives of log-likelihood function with respect to all parameters as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{\ell r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v_{k\ell,r}(v_{i,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-b_{r}v^{*}_{k,r})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v^{*}_{k,r}(v_{k,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-b_{r}v^{*}_{k,r})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-L}{\sigma_{r}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(v_{k,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-b_{r}v^{*}_{k,r}\right)^{2},

and their second derivatives are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{\ell r}\partial\alpha_{po}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v_{k\ell,r}v_{kp,o}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{po}\partial a_{\ell r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{o}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v_{k\ell,r}v_{kp,o}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{\ell r}\partial b_{r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v_{k\ell,r}v^{*}_{k,r}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{\ell r}\partial\sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v_{k\ell,r}(v_{k,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-v^{*}_{k,r}b_{r})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{r}\partial a_{\ell r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v^{*}_{k,r}v_{k\ell,r}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{r}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}{v^{*}}_{k,r}^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{o}\partial a_{\ell r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{o}^{2}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v^{*}_{k,o}v_{k\ell,r}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{r}\partial\sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v^{*}_{k,r}(v_{k,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-v^{*}_{k,r}b_{r})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}\partial a_{\ell r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v_{k\ell,r}(v_{k,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-v^{*}_{k,r}b_{r})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}\partial b_{r}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{3}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}v^{*}_{k,r}(v_{k,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-v^{*}_{k,r}b_{r})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-L}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}-\frac{3}{\sigma_{r}^{4}}\sum_{k=L+1}^{n}\left(v_{k,r}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}v_{k\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-v^{*}_{k,r}b_{r}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{o}\partial a_{\ell r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{o}\partial b_{r}}=\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{o}\partial\sigma_{r}}=0,

where
\ell,p=1,\ldots,L;r,o=1,\ldots,m
.

Therefore, we can obtain a large Hessian matrix of dimension (
(L+2)m\times(L+2)m
) as

H(\log(\mathcal{L}))=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{11}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{11}a_{1m}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{11}\partial a_{Lm}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{11}\partial b_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{11}\partial\sigma_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{11}\partial\sigma_{m}}\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{1m}\partial a_{11}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{1m}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{1m}\partial a_{Lm}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{1m}\partial b_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{1m}\partial\sigma_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{1m}\partial\sigma_{m}}\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{Lm}\partial a_{11}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{Lm}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a^{2}_{Lm}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{Lm}\partial b_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{Lm}\partial\sigma_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{Lm}\partial\sigma_{m}}\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{1}\partial a_{11}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{1}a_{Lm}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{1}\partial a_{Lm}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{1}\partial\sigma_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{1}\partial\sigma_{m}}\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{1}\partial a_{11}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{1}a_{1m}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{1}\partial a_{Lm}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{1}\partial b_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{1}\partial\sigma_{m}}\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{m}\partial a_{11}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{m}a_{1m}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{m}\partial a_{Lm}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\ell}{\partial\sigma_{m}\partial b_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{m}\partial\sigma_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma^{2}_{m}}\end{bmatrix}

Now, we compute the expectation of each derivative in the above Fisher information matrix which is defined as the negative expectation of Hessian matrix
H(\log(\mathcal{L}))
, i.e.,
I(\Phi)=-\mathbb{E}[H(\log(\mathcal{L}))]
. According to the model, it is known that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[v_{r}-\sum_{\ell}v_{\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-b_{r}v_{0,r}\right]

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(v_{r}-\sum_{\ell}v_{\ell,r}a_{\ell r}-b_{r}v_{0,r})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sigma_{r}^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial a_{\ell r}\partial\sigma_{r}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial b_{r}\partial\sigma_{r}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}\partial a_{\ell r}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}\partial b_{r}}\right]=0

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\log(\mathcal{L})}{\partial\sigma_{r}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-L}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}+\frac{3(n-L)}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}=\frac{2(n-L)}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}.

The Fisher information matrix is then

I(\Phi)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\sum_{k}v_{k1,1}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{k1,1}v_{k1,m}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{k1,1}v_{kL,m}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{k1,1}v^{*}_{k,1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\frac{\sum_{k}v_{k1,m}v_{k1,1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v^{2}_{k1,m}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{k1,m}v_{kL,m}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{k1,m}v^{*}_{k,1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
\frac{\sum_{k}v_{kL,m}v_{k1,1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{kL,m}v_{k1,m}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{kL,m}^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\frac{\sum_{k}v_{kL,m}v^{*}_{k,1}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
\frac{\sum_{k}v^{*}_{k,1}v_{k1,1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v^{*}_{k,1}v_{k1,m}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\sum_{k}v^{*}_{k,1}v_{kL,m}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\frac{\sum_{k}{v^{*}}_{k,1}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&0&\cdots&0\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&\frac{2(n-L)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}&\cdots&0\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&&&\ddots&&\ddots&\\
0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&\frac{2(n-L)}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\end{bmatrix}.

Asymptotic covariance matrices of maximum likelihood estimators.

We can establish approximate
95\%
confidence intervals for the estimated parameters using:

\hat{\Phi}_{j}\pm 1.96I(\Phi)_{jj}^{-1/2}

where
j=1,\ldots,J
and
J
is the total number of estimated parameters, e.g.
J=L+2
in the scalar coefficient model and
J=(L+2)m
in the diagonal matrix model.

Under the correct specification of the model, the maximum likelihood regularity condition, and additional technical assumptions,
\sqrt{n}(\hat{\Phi}-\Phi)
converges to a multivariate normal distribution with zero mean and covariance matrix
I(\Phi)^{-1}
, i.e.

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\Phi}-\Phi)\rightarrow N(0,I(\Phi)^{-1})

More precisely, for ‘large’
n
, the distribution of the vector
\hat{\Phi}
can be approximated by a multivariate normal distribution with mean
\Phi
and covariance matrix

\frac{1}{n}I(\Phi)^{-1}.

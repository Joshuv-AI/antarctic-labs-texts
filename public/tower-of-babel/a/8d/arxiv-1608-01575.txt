Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the convergence of the Bochner-Riesz means on some Sobolev type spaces including L p L^{p} -Sobolev spaces ( p ≥ 1 ) (p\geq 1) and H q H^{q} -Sobolev spaces ( 0 < q < 1 ) (0<q<1) . The relation between the smoothness imposed on functions and the rate of almost everywhere convergence of the generalized Bochner-Riesz means is given.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
42B99, 41A35.

1.
Introduction

Let
R>0.
We consider the generalized Bochner-Riesz means
{S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}}
on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined via the Fourier
transform by

{(S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f)}^{\wedge}(\xi)=\left(1-\frac{|\xi|^{\gamma}}{R^{\gamma}}\right)_{+}^{\delta}\widehat{f}(\xi),

where
\delta
and
\gamma
are two real numbers satisfying
\delta>-1

and
\gamma>0
. Also, we may initially assume that
f
are functions in
the Schwartz class
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Especially,
S_{R}^{\delta,2}
is the classical Bochner-Riesz means which was studied by many authors
[1–7, 12, 13, 17, 20, 27]. The associated maximal operator of
{S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}}
is
defined by

(S_{\ast}^{\delta,\gamma}f)(x)=\sup_{R>0}|(S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f)(x)|.

The study of convergence of
S_{R}^{\delta,2}
is a long time standing
subject in the classical theory of Fourier analysis. The number
\delta=(n-1)/2
is called the critical index, since
\delta>{(n-1)}/{2},

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}S_{R}^{\delta,2}f(x)=f(x)
, a.e. for any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
while Stein
[
19
]
found an
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function
f
for which
\lim\sup_{R\rightarrow\infty}|S_{R}^{(n-1)/2,2}f(x)|=\infty
a.e. Finding a suitable subspace of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
related to a.e. convergence of
S_{R}^{(n-1)/2,2}(f)(x)

thus is an interesting problem. Please see the related work of Stein
[
20
]
, R. Fefferman
[
9
]
, Lu, Taibleson and Weiss
[
15
]
, Lu and Wang

[
16
]
.

In order to describe our motivation, we mention two theorems related to this paper. Stein, Taibleson and G. Weiss
[
22
]
considered the
boundedness of
S_{R}^{\delta,2}
on the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

as follows:

Theorem A.
Let
0<p<1
and
\delta_{p}=n/p-(n+1)/2
.
If
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:S_{\ast}^{\delta_{p},2}(f)(x)>s\right\}\right|\preceq\left(\frac{\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{s}\right)^{p},

for all
s>0.
Thus,
\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}S_{R}^{\delta_{p},2}f(x)=f(x)
, a.e. for any
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and let
L_{m}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the inhomogeneous Sobolev space of
all functions
f
satisfying
\partial^{\alpha}f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all multi-indicies

\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq m
. Wang
[
29
]
showed
the convergence and approximation for functions in spaces
L_{m}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
Bochner-Riesz means
S_{R}^{\delta,2}
below the critical index.

Theorem B.
Suppose
f\in L_{m}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

m\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
and
{(n-1)}/{2}-m\geq-1
. Then

(S_{R}^{\delta,2}f)(x)-f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}o\left(R^{\frac{n-1}{2}-m-\delta}\right),&\frac{n-1}{2}-m<\delta<\frac{n-1}{2}-m+1,&m\geq 1,\\
o\left(R^{-1}\log R\right),&\delta=\frac{n-1}{2}-m+1,&m\geq 1,\\
o\left(R^{\frac{n-1}{2}-m-\delta}\right),&\frac{n-1}{2}-m+1<\delta<\frac{n-1}{2}-m+2,&m\geq 2,\\
O\left(R^{-2}\log R\right),&\delta=\frac{n-1}{2}-m+2,&m\geq 2,\\
O\left(R^{-2}\right),&\delta>\frac{n-1}{2}-m+2,&m\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

in the sense of almost everywhere convergence.

In our recent work
[
8
]
, we study the convergence of the generalized Bochner-Riesz means

S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}
with
\delta=(n-1)/2
on the
block-Sobolev spaces. The relation between the smoothness imposed on blocks and the rate of almost everywhere convergence of the generalized
Bochner-Riesz means at the critical index is given.
This furnishes us a motivation for studying the generalized Bochner-Riesz
means on some corresponding Sobolev type spaces. We want mention that the study of generalized Bochner-Riesz means
S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}
is
not merely a simple extension of using
\gamma
to replace 2, and it is naturally raised from the approximation theory in order to enhance the saturation of the operator (see Theorem
1.1
). Our aim of this paper is not only to analyze the convergence of the Bochner-Riesz means
S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f
on certain Sobolev type spaces including
L^{p}
-Sobolev spaces and
H^{p}
-Sobolev spaces, but also to obtain the relation
between the smoothness imposed on functions and the rate of almost
everywhere convergence of
S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f
.

Let
I_{\lambda}
denote the Riesz potential operators of
order
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, which may act on functions or tempered
distributions. The Fourier transform of a Schwartz function (or even a
tempered distribution
f
) satisfies
(I_{\lambda}f)^{\wedge}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\lambda}\widehat{f}(\xi)
. If
X
is any function space or a space of
tempered distributions, one can define Sobolev spaces based on
X
using
I_{\lambda}
,
I_{\lambda}(X)
, to be the image of
X
under
I_{\lambda}
. By this definition,
I_{\lambda}(L^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are the
classical homogeneous Sobolev spaces for
p\geq 1
(see
[
10
, p.16]
), and
I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are the Hardy-Sobolev spaces for
0<p\leq 1
(see
[
26
]
). We recall that this
notation
I_{\lambda}(X)
was used by Strichartz in
[
25
,
26
]
. By the
definition (following the terminology in
[
26
]
),
f\in I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
I_{-\lambda}f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It follows from Chapter 3 in
[
21
, p.338]
that
g\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has an atomic
decomposition

g=\sum_{k}c_{k}a_{k},\quad\|g\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\approx\sum_{k}|c_{k}|^{p}<\infty,

where each
a_{k}
is a
(p,2)
-atom. Here, we call a function
a
a
(p,2)
-atom,
0<p\leq 1
, if
a
satisfies:

(i)

a
is supported on a cube
Q
; (support condition)

(ii)

\ \|a\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq|Q|^{1/2-1/p};
(size condition)

(iii)

\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0,

|\alpha|\leq[n(1/p-1)].
(cancellation condition)

We now formulate our main results. They are new even when
\gamma=2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0\leq\lambda\leq\gamma
,
0<p<1
and
\delta_{p}=n/p-(n+1)/2
. If
f\in I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for
0\leq\lambda<\gamma
,

(S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)=o(1/{R^{\lambda}})\ a.e.\ as\ R\rightarrow\infty;

and for
\lambda=\gamma
,

(S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)=O(1/{R^{\gamma}})\ a.e.\ as\ R\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover, this rate is sharp in the sense that

O(1/{R^{\gamma}})
can not be replaced by
o(1/{R^{\gamma}})
.

Let
{M}_{\lambda}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f
be the maximal function defined by

({M}_{\lambda}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)=\sup_{R>0}|R^{\lambda}\{(S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)\}|.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we will need to make use of the following
estimate on
{M}_{\lambda}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0\leq\lambda\leq\gamma
,
0<p<1
and
\delta_{p}=n/p-(n+1)/2
. If
f\in I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:({M}_{\lambda}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)>s\right\}\right|\preceq\left(\frac{\|f\|_{I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{s}\right)^{p},

for all
s>0
.

It is worth pointing out that if
\lambda=0
, one may follow the method of
[
22
]
to yield that
for all
s>0

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:S_{\ast}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(f)(x)>s\right\}\right|\preceq\left(\frac{\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{s}\right)^{p},\ \gamma\geq 0

whenever
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus Theorem
1.2

holds for the case
\lambda=0
on account of Chebyshev’s inequality and the following fact
for
0<p<\infty

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}(e^{-t|x|^{2}}\ast f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For the case
0<\delta<\frac{n-1}{2},
we use a different approach
from
[
29
]
and obtain the following
result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\delta<(n-1)/2
,
1\leq p<\frac{2n}{n-1-2\delta}
and
{(n-1)}/2-\delta<\lambda\leq\gamma
. If
f\in I_{\lambda}(L^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then we have

(S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)=o(1/{R^{\lambda+\delta-\frac{n-1}{2}}})\ a.e.\ as\ R\rightarrow\infty.

Here we make some further comments about Theorem
1.3
.
If
p=1
, then the inhomogeneous space
L_{m}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a proper subspace of the space
I_{m}(L^{1})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
m
is even. So we may compare
Theorem B with Theorem
1.3
(
\gamma=2
) when
m
is even and

0<\frac{n-1}{2}-\delta\leq 2
. If
m+\delta-\frac{n-1}{2}=1
and
f\in L_{m}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
Theorem B gives the rate
o(R^{-1}\log R)
while the rate in Theorem
1.3
is
o(R^{-1})
.
Clearly, this theorem is a substantial extension of Theorem B when
0<\delta<{(n-1)}/2
.

Our idea is mainly inspired from our previous work
[
8
]
. Precisely,
observe that the Fourier transform of
R^{\lambda}\{(S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f)-f\}
is
\mu(\cdot/R)

\widehat{g},
where
g=I_{-\lambda}f
and
the multiplier
\mu
is given by

\mu(\xi)=\frac{(1-\left|\xi\right|^{\gamma})_{+}^{\delta}-1}{\left|\xi\right|^{\lambda}},\ \xi\neq 0\text{ \ and }\mu(0)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{(1-t^{\gamma})_{+}^{\delta}-1}{t^{\lambda}}.

Hence we will decompose the multiplier
\mu
as a sum of
\mu_{0},

\mu_{1},

\mu_{\infty},
centralizing at
0,

1
and near
\infty,

respectively. Then each corresponding kernel will be carefully estimated.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we define some necessary
notations and definitions that will be used throughout this paper. In this section, we also
prove three lemmas which will be used in the next section. In Section 3, we prove
the almost everywhere convergence of the Bochner-Riesz means
S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f
on
H^{p}
-Sobolev spaces, where
0<p<1
. In the last section,
we present the almost everywhere convergence of the Bochner-Riesz means

S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f
on
L^{p}
-Sobolev spaces below the critical index for

1\leq p<\frac{2n}{n-1-2\delta}
.

Throughout this article, we will use the symbol
A\preceq B
to mean that
there exists a constant
C>0
independent of all essential variables such
that
A\leq CB
. We also use the notation
A\simeq B
if
A\preceq B
and
B\preceq A
. The symbol
A\approx B
means that there exists a constant
C
independent of all essential variables such
that
A=CB
.

2.
Some Preliminaries

We introduce the requisite notation. For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the first partial derivative of a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the
j
th variable
x_{j}
is denoted by
\partial_{j}f
while the
m
th partial derivative with respect to the
j
th
variable is denoted by
\partial_{j}^{m}f
. A multi-index
\alpha
is an
ordered
n
-tuple of nonnegative integers. For a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
,
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f
or
\partial^{\alpha}f

denotes the derivative
\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}f
. Let
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}
and
\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{n}!
. The number
|\alpha|
indicates the total order of differentiation of
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
a multi-index, we set
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. Let
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
be a multi-index. The
notation
\alpha\leq\beta
means that
\beta
ranges over all multi-indices
satisfying
0\leq\alpha_{j}\leq\beta_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. For more
details, see
[
10
, p.108]
. Let
[r]
be the greatest integer less than or equal to the real number
r
.

Let
\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{\infty}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be
radial functions satisfying the following conditions:
(i)\ \phi_{0}
is
supported on the set
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|<1/2\}
,
\phi_{1}
is
supported on the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:1/4<|\xi|<2\}
, and
\phi_{\infty}
is supported on the set
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|>3/2\}
;
(ii)\ \phi_{0}(\xi)+\phi_{1}(\xi)+\phi_{\infty}(\xi)=1,

0\leq\phi_{j}(\xi)\leq 1
for
j=0,1,\infty
, and

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&|\xi|\leq{1}/{4},\\
0&|\xi|\geq{1}/{2};\end{array}\right.\quad\phi_{1}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&{1}/{2}\leq|\xi|\leq{3}/{2},\\
0&|\xi|\leq{1}/{4}\ \text{and}\ |\xi|\geq 2;\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\phi_{\infty}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&|\xi|\geq 2,\\
0&|\xi|\leq{3}/{2}.\end{array}\right.

Let
\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)=\phi_{1}(\xi)+\phi_{\infty}(\xi)
. Obviously,
\Psi_{\infty}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is supported on the set
\{\xi:|\xi|>1/4\}
satisfying
0\leq\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)\leq 1
and
\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)=1
on the set
\{\xi:|\xi|\geq 1/2\}
.

Assume that the kernel
B_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}
of
S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}
is defined
by

B_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1-\frac{|\xi|^{\gamma}}{R^{\gamma}}\right)_{+}^{\delta}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi.

We rewrite
S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}
as a convolution operator

S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f=B_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}\ast f.

Let
g=:I_{-\lambda}f
. Then the Fourier transform of the function
R^{\lambda}\{(S_{R}^{\delta,\gamma}f)-f\}
can be written as
m_{\lambda,0}^{\delta,\gamma}(\cdot/R)\widehat{g}+m_{\lambda,1}^{\delta,\gamma}(\cdot/R)\widehat{g}-m_{\lambda,\infty}^{\delta,\gamma}(\cdot/R)\widehat{g}
, where

\displaystyle m_{\lambda,0}^{\delta,\gamma}(\xi)=\frac{\phi_{0}\left({\xi}\right)}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}\left(\left(1-{|\xi|^{\gamma}}\right)_{+}^{\delta}-1\right),m_{\lambda,1}^{\delta,\gamma}(\xi)=\frac{\phi_{1}\left({\xi}\right)}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}\left(1-|\xi|^{\gamma}\right)_{+}^{\delta},\text{ \ \ \ \ \ }

\displaystyle m_{\lambda,\infty}^{\delta,\gamma}(\xi)=\frac{\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}.

Here and in what follows, for simplicity, we define the
operators

(T_{R,\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}g)^{\wedge}(\xi)=m_{\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}(\xi/R)\widehat{g}(\xi),

and denote by
(K_{\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma})_{1/R}(x)=R^{n}K_{\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}(Rx)
the kernel of
T_{R,\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}
, where

K_{\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}(x)={m_{\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}}^{\vee}(x),\ j=0,1,\infty.

Then each operator
T_{R,\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}g
can be written
as a convolution operator:

T_{R,\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma}g(x)=(K_{\lambda,j}^{\delta,\gamma})_{1/R}\ast g(x),

for
j=0,1,\infty
.

In the following three lemmas of this section, we will estimate

\partial_{x}^{\beta}K_{\lambda,j}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)
,
j=0,1,\infty
,
where

K_{\lambda,j}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)={m_{\lambda,j}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}}^{\vee}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{m_{\lambda,j}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi,

here and in what follows, the notation
\delta_{p}
is
\delta_{p}=n/p-(n+1)/2
for
0<p<1
. To get the desired estimates for the case
j=0,\infty
,
we consider cones
E_{k},\ k=1,2,\ldots,n
,
defined by

E_{k}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x_{k}|^{2}\geq|x|^{2}/{2n}\}

and the smaller cones

E_{k}^{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x_{k}|^{2}\geq|x|^{2}/{n}\}.

Then

\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}^{0}=\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
\chi_{E_{k}}
be a nonnegative
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function
supported on
E_{k}
and let
\chi_{E_{k}}(x)=1
for
x\in E_{k}^{0}
and
0\leq\chi_{E_{k}}(x)\leq 1
. Clearly,
we have

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}K_{\lambda,j}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\chi_{E_{k}}(x)\partial_{x}^{\beta}\left({m_{\lambda,j}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}}^{\vee}(x)\right)\right|,\ j=0,\infty.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
0\leq\lambda\leq\gamma
and
0<p<1
, we have

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}K_{\lambda,0}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)|\preceq\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{n+|\beta|+\gamma-\lambda}}.

Proof.

A direct calculation shows that

\partial_{x}^{\beta}K_{\lambda,0}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\phi_{0}\left({\xi}\right)}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}\left(\left(1-{|\xi|^{\gamma}}\right)_{+}^{\delta_{p}}-1\right)(2\pi i\xi)^{\beta}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi.

By Taylor’s expansion we notice that in the support of
\phi_{0}
,

(1-|\xi|^{\gamma})_{+}^{\delta_{p}}-1=\delta_{p}|\xi|^{\gamma}+\omega(\xi),

where
\omega
is a
C^{\infty}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
satisfying
\omega(\xi)=O(|\xi|^{2\gamma})
and
\left(\nabla^{k}\omega\right)(\xi)=O(|\xi|^{2\gamma-k})
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
as
|\xi|\rightarrow 0
.
Thus we only need to work the term

\mathfrak{K}_{0}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{0}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda}\xi^{\beta}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi,

since the other terms are similarly estimated.

To estimate the function
\mathfrak{K}_{0}(x)
, we break up
E_{1}
into the
regions
\{x\in E_{1}:|x|<1\}
and
\{x\in E_{1}:|x|\geq 1\}
. Clearly, we
have

|\mathfrak{K}_{0}(x)|\preceq 1,\ \text{if}\ |x|<1.

We now estimate the second case
|x|>1
. Recalling that

|\mathfrak{K}_{0}(x)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\chi_{E_{k}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{0}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda}\xi^{\beta}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi\right|,

by the symmetry, we only need to work

\chi_{E_{1}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{0}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda}\xi^{\beta}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi.

Denote by
\vec{e}_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
. We integrate
for
n
times on the
\xi_{1}
variable to obtain

\displaystyle\quad\quad\chi_{E_{1}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{0}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda}\xi^{\beta}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=O\left(\frac{\chi_{E_{1}}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{\phi_{0}}(\xi)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda-2k}\xi^{\beta+(2k-n)\vec{e}_{1}}\right)e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi\right),

as
|x|>1,
where, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\widetilde{\phi_{0}}
are different functions in each occurrence satisfying
the following three conditions: (i)
\widetilde{\phi_{0}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),
(ii)
\mathrm{supp}\ \widetilde{\phi_{0}}\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq 1/2\}
, (iii)
\widetilde{\phi_{0}}(\xi)=O(|\xi|^{n})
, as
|\xi|\rightarrow 0
. Now let
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radial function satisfying that
\eta(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
\eta(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
. For
0\leq k\leq n
, write

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\chi_{E_{1}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{\phi_{0}}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda-2k}\xi^{\beta+(2k-n)\vec{e}_{1}}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\chi_{E_{1}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta(|x|\xi)\widetilde{\phi_{0}}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda-2k}\xi^{\beta+(2k-n)\vec{e}_{1}}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\chi_{E_{1}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1-\eta(|x|\xi)\right)\widetilde{\phi_{0}}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda-2k}\xi^{\beta+(2k-n)\vec{e}_{1}}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi.

Then we have

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\left|\frac{\chi_{E_{1}}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{\phi_{0}}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda-2k}\xi^{\beta+(2k-n)\vec{e}_{1}}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\preceq\frac{1}{|x|^{n}}\int_{|\xi|<2/{|x|}}|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda+|\beta|-n}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{\chi_{E_{1}}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1-\eta(|x|\xi)\right)\widetilde{\phi_{0}}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma-\lambda-2k}\xi^{\beta+(2k-n)\vec{e}_{1}}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}d\xi\right|.

The first term

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
and
0<p<1
, we have

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}K_{\lambda,1}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)|\preceq 1,\text{\ if\ }|x|\leq 1,

and

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}K_{\lambda,1}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)|\preceq|x|^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\delta_{p}},\text{\ if\ }|x|>1.

Proof.

Using the formula in
[
23
, p.155]
, we know that

K_{\lambda,1}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{1/{4}}^{1}{\phi_{1}\left({t}\right)}\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{\delta_{p}}t^{n-\lambda-1}V_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(2\pi|x|t)dt,

where

V_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(2\pi|x|t)=\frac{J_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(2\pi|x|t)}{(2\pi|x|t)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}},

J_{v}(t)
denotes the usual Bessel function satisfying that

v
is a complex number
\text{Re}\ v>-1/2
and
t\geq 0
.
Using the formula

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{J_{\gamma}(t)}{t^{\gamma}}\right)=-\frac{J_{\gamma+1}(t)}{t^{\gamma}},

for any multi-index
\beta
and
\lambda\geq 0
, an easy computation shows
that

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\beta}V_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(2\pi|x|t)=(-1)^{|\beta|}(2\pi t)^{2|\beta|}x^{\beta}V_{\frac{n-2}{2}+|\beta|}(2\pi|x|t)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{0<|k|\leq[\frac{|\beta|}{2}]}c_{k}t^{2(|\beta|-|k|)}x^{\beta-2k}V_{\frac{n-2}{2}+|\beta|-|k|}(2\pi|x|t).

Invoking the above estimate (
1
), we only
need to estimate the term

(2)

\mathfrak{K}_{1}(x)=x^{\beta}\int_{1/{4}}^{1}{\phi_{1}\left({t}\right)}\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{\delta_{p}}t^{n+2|\beta|-\lambda-1}V_{\frac{n-2}{2}+|\beta|}(2\pi|x|t)dt.

As a result, the estimate
\partial_{x}^{\beta}K_{\lambda,1}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}(x)
can be obtained because the other terms can be handled in
a similar method.

To estimate (
2
), we need to use the behavior of the Bessel function
J_{v}(t)
. The following estimate and asymptotic formula can be found in
[
10
, p.430]
and
[
21
, p.338]

(3)

|J_{v}(t)|\preceq t^{\text{Re}\ v},\ 0<t\leq 1;

when
v
is fixed, the asymptotic formula for
J_{v}(t)
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, is

\displaystyle J_{v}(t)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle t^{-1/2}e^{it}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}t^{-j}+t^{-1/2}e^{-it}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}d_{j}t^{-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{-1/2}e^{it}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{0}}c_{j}t^{-j}+t^{-1/2}e^{-it}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{0}}d_{j}t^{-j}+O\left(\frac{1}{t^{N_{0}+3/2}}\right)

where
c_{j}
,
d_{j}
are coefficients, and the positive integer
N_{0}

will be determined later.

We now divide the value of
\ |x|
into two cases:
|x|\leq 1
and
|x|>1
.
Using the estimate of (
3
), we have that for
|x|\leq 1
,

|V_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(2\pi|x|t)|\preceq 1,

and

\displaystyle\quad\left|\mathfrak{K}_{1}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|x^{\beta}\int_{1/{4}}^{1}{\phi_{1}\left({t}\right)}\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{\delta_{p}}t^{n+2|\beta|-\lambda-1}V_{\frac{n-2}{2}+|\beta|}(2\pi|x|t)dt\right|

\displaystyle\preceq{|x|}^{|\beta|}\int_{1/4}^{1}(1-t^{\gamma})^{\delta_{p}}dt\preceq 1.

For
|x|>1
, applying the asymptotic formula for
J_{v}(t)
, it is enough to
estimate the function
G_{j}\ (0\leq j\leq N_{0})

G_{j}(x)={|x|}^{-(\frac{n-1}{2}+|\beta|+j)}x^{\beta}\int_{1/{4}}^{1}{\phi_{1}\left({t}\right)}\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{\delta_{p}}t^{\frac{n-1}{2}+|\beta|-\lambda-j}e^{\pm 2\pi i|x|t}dt.

If
\delta_{p}
is an integer, then we take
N_{0}=\delta_{p}
. The estimate

|G_{N_{0}}(x)|\preceq{|x|}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\delta_{p}}
is easy. For
0\leq j<N_{0}
, after integrating by parts
\delta_{p}-j
times, we see
that

G_{j}(x)\approx\frac{x^{\beta}}{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\delta_{p}+|\beta|}}\int_{1/{4}}^{1}\rho_{j,1}(t)\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{j}e^{\pm 2\pi i|x|t}dt,

where
\rho_{j,1}(t)
is a
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function
satisfying
\mathrm{supp}\ \rho_{j,1}(t)\subseteq[1/4,1]
. It is a
trivial case for
j=0
. For
0<j<N_{0}
, using integration by parts again,
we obtain

|G_{j}(x)|\preceq{|x|}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\delta_{p}},\ 0<j<N_{0}.

If
\delta_{p}
is not an integer, then we take
N_{0}=[\delta_{p}]+2
. It is a simple matter to
establish
|G_{N_{0}}(x)|\preceq{|x|}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\delta_{p}}
. For
0\leq j<N_{0}
, using integration by parts
[\delta_{p}]+1-j
times, we get

G_{j}(x)\approx\frac{x^{\beta}}{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}+[\delta_{p}]+|\beta|+1}}\int_{1/{4}}^{1}\rho_{j,2}(t)\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{j-([\delta_{p}]+1-\delta_{p})}e^{\pm 2\pi i|x|t}dt,

where
0<[\delta_{p}]+1-\delta_{p}<1
and
\rho_{j,2}(t)
is a
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function satisfying
\mathrm{supp}\ \rho_{j,2}(t)\subseteq[1/4,1]
. Write

\displaystyle\quad\left|\frac{x^{\beta}}{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}+[\delta_{p}]+|\beta|+1}}\int_{1/{4}}^{1}\rho_{j,2}(t)\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{j-([\delta_{p}]+1-\delta_{p})}e^{\pm 2\pi i|x|t}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{n+1}{2}+[\delta_{p}]}}\left|\int_{1/4}^{1-1/{|x|}}\rho_{j,2}(t)\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{j-([\delta_{p}]+1-\delta_{p})}e^{\pm 2\pi i|x|t}dt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{n+1}{2}+[\delta_{p}]}}\left|\int_{1-1/{|x|}}^{1}\rho_{j,2}(t)\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{j-([\delta_{p}]+1-\delta_{p})}e^{\pm 2\pi i|x|t}dt\right|

\displaystyle:=G_{j,1}(x)+G_{j,2}(x).

For
G_{j,1}(x)
, using one more integration by parts, one has

(4)

G_{j,1}(x)\preceq{|x|}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\delta_{p}}.

For
G_{j,2}(x)
, we have for
|x|>1
,

(5)

\displaystyle\quad G_{j,2}(x)\preceq\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{n+1}{2}+[\delta_{p}]}}\int_{1-1/{|x|}}^{1}\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{j-([\delta_{p}]+1-\delta_{p})}dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\gamma(j+\delta_{p}-[\delta_{p}])}\cdot\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{n+1}{2}+[\delta_{p}]}}\int_{1-1/{|x|}}^{1}t^{1-\gamma}d\left(1-t^{\gamma}\right)^{j+\delta_{p}-[\delta_{p}]}

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{n+1}{2}+[\delta_{p}]}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{|x|}-O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right)^{j+\delta_{p}-[\delta_{p}]}

\displaystyle\preceq{|x|}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\delta_{p}}.

The estimates of (
4
) and (
5
) give

|G_{j}(x)|\preceq{|x|}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\delta_{p}},\ 0\leq j<N_{0}.

Combing all the estimates, we finish the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(K_{\lambda,\infty}^{\delta_{p},\gamma})_{1/R}(x)|\preceq\frac{R^{n+|\beta|}}{|Rx|^{n+|\beta|-\lambda}},\text{\ if\ }R|x|\leq 1,

and

(16)

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(K_{\lambda,\infty}^{\delta_{p},\gamma})_{1/R}(x)|\preceq\frac{R^{n+|\beta|}}{|Rx|^{n+L}},\text{\ if\ }R|x|>1.

Applying Taylor’s theorem and the cancellation condition of

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{0<R\leq 2/l}\left|T_{R,\lambda,\infty}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}b(x)\right|

\displaystyle\preceq\sum_{|\alpha|={|\beta|}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\int_{Q}|b(y)|\cdot|y|^{|\alpha|}\sup_{0<R\leq 2/l}\left(\sup_{|z-x|<|y|}\left|\left(\partial^{\alpha}(K_{\lambda,\infty}^{\delta_{p},\gamma})_{1/R}\right)(z)\right|\right)dy.

In the same manner we break the set
U(x)
into two regions:

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{0<R\leq 2/l}\left(\sup_{z\in U(x),R|z|\leq 1}\left|\left(\partial^{\alpha}(K_{\lambda,\infty}^{\delta_{p},\gamma})_{1/R}\right)(z)\right|\right)\preceq\sup_{0<R\leq 2/l,R|z|\leq 1,z\in U(x)}\frac{R^{n+|\beta|}}{|Rz|^{n+|\beta|-\lambda}}

\displaystyle\preceq{|x|}^{-n/p}\cdot l^{-(n+|\beta|-n/p)}\cdot\sup_{z\in U(x)}{\left(\frac{l}{|z|}\right)}^{n+|\beta|-n/p}

\displaystyle\leq{|x|}^{-n/p}\cdot l^{-(n+|\beta|-n/p)},

where we have used

{3|x|}/2>|z|>{|x|}/2>{\sqrt{n}l}/2

16

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{0<R\leq 2/l}\left(\sup_{z\in U(x),R|z|>1}\left|\left(\partial^{\alpha}(K_{\lambda,\infty}^{\delta_{p},\gamma})_{1/R}\right)(z)\right|\right)\preceq\sup_{0<R\leq 2/l,z\in U(x),R|z|>1}\frac{R^{n+|\beta|}}{|Rz|^{n+L}}

\displaystyle\preceq|x|^{-n/p}\cdot l^{-(n+|\beta|-n/p)}\cdot\sup_{z\in U(x)}{\left(\frac{l}{|z|}\right)}^{n+|\beta|-n/p}

\displaystyle\preceq|x|^{-n/p}\cdot l^{-(n+|\beta|-n/p)}.

Therefore, Hölder’s inequality and the

(p,2)
-atom

\sup_{R>0}\left|T_{R,\lambda,\infty}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}b(x)\right|\preceq|x|^{-n/p}.

Summing all the estimates, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The proof follows a standard
method. We only prove the case
\lambda<\gamma
. Suppose
f\in I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
dense in
I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
for any
\varepsilon>0
, we choose an
h\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\left\|f-h\right\|_{I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\varepsilon.

Since

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left|R^{\lambda}\left((S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}h)(x)-h(x)\right)\right|=0

for all
x,
for any fixed
s>0,
we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lim\sup_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left|R^{\lambda}\left((S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)\right)\right|>s\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lim\sup_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left|R^{\lambda}\left((S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}\left(f-h\right))(x)-\left(f-h\right)(x)\right)\right|>s/2\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\|f-h\|_{I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{s}\right)^{p}<\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{s}\right)^{p}.

To show that

(S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)=O(1/{R^{\gamma}})\ a.e.\ as\ R\rightarrow\infty

is sharp when
\lambda=\gamma
, we pick an
f\in I_{\lambda}(H^{p})(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\left|\xi\right|^{\lambda}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot y}d\xi\neq 0

at some
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Using the continuity, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\left|\xi\right|^{\lambda}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi\neq 0,

in a neighborhood of
y.

Write

\displaystyle R^{\lambda}\left((S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\left(1-\frac{{|\xi|^{\lambda}}}{R^{\lambda}}\right)_{+}^{\delta_{p}}-1\right)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\lambda}\int_{\left|\xi\right|<R/2}\left(\left(1-\frac{{|\xi|^{\lambda}}}{R^{\lambda}}\right)^{\delta_{p}}-1\right)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle+R^{\lambda}\int_{\left|\xi\right|\geq R/2}\left(\left(1-\frac{{|\xi|^{\lambda}}}{R^{\lambda}}\right)_{+}^{\delta_{p}}-1\right)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{R}(x)+II_{R}(x).

Since
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
we have

\left|II_{R}(x)\right|\preceq R^{\lambda}\int_{\left|\xi\right|\geq R/2}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|d\xi\preceq R^{-L},

for any
L>0
. Using the Taylor expansion, we have

I_{R}(x)=-\delta_{p}\int_{\left|\xi\right|<R/2}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi+O(\frac{1}{R^{\lambda}}).

Thus, we obtain that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}R^{\lambda}\left((S_{R}^{\delta_{p},\gamma}f)(x)-f(x)\right)\neq 0

in a set of positive measure. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Ambisonic Encoding of Signals From Spherical Microphone Arrays Find more technical notes at http://www.ta.chalmers.se/education/texts-on-acoustics/
Jens Ahrens

Abstract
This document illustrates how to process the signals from the microphones of a rigid-sphere higher-order ambisonic microphone array so that they are encoded with N3D normalization and ACN channel order and thereby can be used with the standard ambisonic software tools such as SPARTA and the IEM Plugin Suite. A MATLAB script is provided.

Introduction

In the following, we assume that the forward and inverse Fourier transforms over time are defined as

S(\omega)=\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(t)\ \mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}\mathrm{d}t

(1)

and

s(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}S(\omega)\ \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\mathrm{d}\omega\,,

(2)

where
s(t)
is the time-domain representation of our signal under consideration and
S(\omega)
its frequency-domain representation. Note that the algebraic sign of the exponent of the complex exponential is the aspect of importance. Above definitions are compatible with the definition of the discrete Fourier transform in MATLAB

The sound pressure
S^{\text{\;\!int}}(\beta,\alpha,r,\omega)
in an interior domain i.e., the sound pressure inside a spherical domain centered at the coordinate origin that is free of sound sources and free of reflecting boundaries, can be represented in SHs in different ways
(
Gumerov and Duraiswami 2005
)
. To be consistent with the ambisonics formulation, we represent it as
(
Ahrens 2022a
, Sec. 5.5)

S^{\text{\;\!int}}(\beta,\alpha,r,\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\!\!4\pi\mathrm{i}^{n}\,\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)\,\,j_{n}\!\left(\omega\frac{r}{c}\right)\,Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)

(3)

\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)
is the frequency-domain representation of the ambisonic signals
\breve{s}_{n,m}(t)
that we aim at extracting from the microphone signals.
\beta
and
\alpha
are the colatitude and azimuth angles of a spherical coordinate system, respectively.

When
S^{\text{\;\!int}}(\beta,\alpha,r,\omega)
impinges on a rigid sphere of radius
R
that is centered at the coordinate origin (i.e., when
S^{\text{\;\!int}}(\beta,\alpha,r,\omega)
impinges on the surface of our rigid-sphere microphone array), the sound pressure
S(\beta,\alpha,R,\omega)
on the surface of the sphere is consequently given by

S^{(R)}(\beta,\alpha,R,\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\!\!\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)\,\,b_{n}\!\left(\omega\frac{R}{c},R\right)\,Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)\ ,

(4)

with radial term

\displaystyle b_{n}\!\left(\omega\frac{R}{c},R\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \ \,4\pi\mathrm{i}^{n}\left[j_{n}\left(\omega\frac{R}{c}\right)-\frac{j_{n}^{\prime}\left(\omega\frac{R}{c}\right)}{{h_{n}^{(2)}}^{\prime}\left(\omega\frac{R}{c}\right)}h_{n}^{(2)}\left(\omega\frac{R}{c}\right)\right]

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\mathrm{i}^{n}\,\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\left(\omega\frac{R}{c}\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{h_{n}^{\prime\;\!(2)}\!\left(\omega\frac{R}{c}\right)}\ .

(6)

We exploited the Wronskian for spherical Bessel functions
(
Gumerov and Duraiswami 2005
, Eq. (4.2.13))
to convert (
5
) into (
6
).

Pressure microphones on the surface of the spherical baffle sense
S^{(R)}(\beta,\alpha,R,\omega)
, which means that we know all quantities in (
4
) apart from the sought-after ambisonic signals
\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)
. We observe that any square-integrable function
X(\beta,\alpha)
that is defined on the surface of a sphere – like our sound pressure
S^{(R)}(\beta,\alpha,R,\omega)
– can be expanded into SHs coefficients
\mathring{X}_{n,m}
as
(
Williams 1999
, Eq. (6.48))

X(\beta,\alpha)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\!\!\mathring{X}_{n,m}\,Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)\ ,

(7)

and that the coefficients
\mathring{X}_{n,m}(\omega)
of that expansion can be computed via
(
Williams 1999
, Eq. (6.49))

\mathring{X}_{n,m}=\oint_{\Omega\in S^{2}}X(\beta,\alpha)\,Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega

(8)

because of the orthogonality of
Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)
with respect to the mode
(n,m)
.
S^{2}
denotes the unit sphere (apologies for using the symbol ’
S
’ again).

In (
8
), we assume that the spherical harmonics
Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)
are defined as

Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)=(-1)^{m}\,\sqrt{\frac{2n{+}1}{4\pi}\frac{(n{-}|m|)!}{(n{+}|m|)!}}\,P_{n}^{|{m}|}(\cos\beta)\begin{cases}\sqrt{2}\sin{|m|\alpha},\ \forall m<0\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ 1\ \ \ \ \ \ \,,\ \forall m=0\\
\sqrt{2}\cos{|m|\alpha},\ \forall m>0\end{cases}\ ,

(9)

which corresponds to the definition that is used in the N3D ambisonic format
(
Nachbar et al. 2011
)

Associated Legendre functions are used with different normalizations. We assume that
P_{n,m}(\mu)
is defined via the following Rodriguez formula
(
Gumerov and Duraiswami 2005
, Eq. (2.1.20)-(2.1.21))
:

P_{n,m}(\mu)=(-1)^{m}(1-\mu^{2})^{m/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{m}}P_{n}(\mu),\ \ \forall n\geq 0,\ m\geq m\ ,

with

P_{n}(\mu)=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{n}}(\mu^{2}-1),\ \ \forall n\geq 0\ .

This also the definition that MATLAB and SciPy use.

. It is also identical to the definition that is used in Politis’s
getSH.m
MATLAB function when called with the argument
’real’

(
Politis 2015
)
.

Assuming that we have a continuous layer of pressure microphones on the surface of the baffle and comparing (
7
) and (
4
) allows for establishing the equivalent of (
8
) as

\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)=\frac{1}{b_{n}\!\left(\omega\frac{R}{c},R\right)}\oint_{\Omega\in S^{2}}S^{(R)}(\beta,\alpha,R,\omega)\,Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega\ ,

(10)

which extracts the ambisonic signals
\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)
from the microphone signals
S^{(R)}(\beta,\alpha,R,\omega)
. Note that
\frac{1}{b_{n}\!\left(\omega\frac{R}{c},R\right)}
is ill-conditioned at low frequencies and high orders and needs to regularized
(
Moreau et al. 2006
;
Bernschütz 2016
;
Politis and Gamper 2017
)
.
In practice, the integral in (
11
) needs to be discretized because
S^{(R)}(\beta,\alpha,R,\omega)
is measured only at a finite set of discrete microphone positions. This is the cause for the required order truncation as well as spatial aliasing
(
Rafaely 2005
)
.

It also conceivable to implement (
10
) in time domain as

\breve{s}_{n,m}(t)=b_{n}^{(-1)}\!\left(t\frac{R}{c},R\right)\ast_{t}\oint_{\Omega\in S^{2}}s^{(R)}(\beta,\alpha,R,t)\,Y_{n,m}(\beta,\alpha)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega\ ,

(11)

whereby
\ast_{t}
denotes convolution with respect to time, and
b_{n}^{(-1)}\!\left(t\frac{R}{c},R\right)
is the time-domain representation of
\frac{1}{b_{n}\!\left(\omega\frac{R}{c},R\right)}
.

Lastly, we need to arrange the channels of the multichannel signal
\breve{s}_{n,m}(t)
in a standardized order. The
Ambisonic Channel Number
(ACN) is the most popular arrangement. The channel number is given by
n^{2}+n+m
. An example implementation of the above procedure is available in
(
Ahrens 2022b
)
. The resulting signals
\breve{s}_{n,m}(t)
are compatible with the standard ambisonic software tools such as SPARTA

and the IEM Plugin Suite

2
Rendering

For completeness, we complement the processing pipeline here and illustrate how binaural rendering of the ambisonic signals
\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)
and
\breve{s}_{n,m}(t)
, respectively, is performed, i.e., how the left and right ear signals
B^{\text{L,R}}(\omega)
of a virtual listener in the captured sound field are computed.

Taking into account the conventions used in the present report, binaural rendering is performed as
(
Ahrens 2022a
, Tab. 1)

B^{\text{L,R}}(\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\!\breve{S}_{n,m}(\omega)\,\,\mathring{H}_{n,m}^{\text{L,R}}(\omega)\ .

(12)

\mathring{H}_{n,m}^{\text{L,R}}(\omega)
are the SH coefficients of the left and right far-field head-related transfer function
H^{\text{L,R}}(\beta,\alpha,\omega)
, respectively, defined as

H^{\text{L,R}}(\beta,\alpha,\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\!\!\mathring{H}_{n,m}^{\text{L,R}}(\omega)\,Y_{n}^{m}(\beta,\alpha)

(13)

\beta
and
\alpha
are the colatitude and azimuth of the sound incidence direction. The MATLAB scripts provided in
(
Ahrens 2022b
)
also demonstrate how to implement (
12
).

Spinorial higher-spin gauge theory from IKKT model in Euclidean and Minkowski signatures
& Tung Tran

Abstract
We explore the semi-classical relation between the fuzzy 4-hyperboloid H N 4 H_{N}^{4} and non-compact quantized twistor space ℙ N 1 , 2 \mathbb{P}_{N}^{1,2} at large N N . This provides two backgrounds of the IKKT matrix model via two natural stereographic projections, leading to higher-spin gauge theories with Euclidean and Minkowski signature denoted by HS-IKKT. The resulting higher-spin gauge theory can be understood as an uplift of 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 super Yang-Mills to twistor space. The action of HS-IKKT is written using a spinor formalism in both Euclidean and Minkowski signature. We then compute the tree-level amplitudes of the massless sector within the Yang-Mills part of the HS-IKKT model in the flat limit in Euclidean signature. All n n -point tree-level scattering amplitudes for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 of this sector are found to vanish in the flat limit.

The IKKT-matrix model

Some natural backgrounds of the IKKT model are given by covariant fuzzy spaces such as the fuzzy 4-sphere

In this work we will consider two projections of

However, in that case the spinorial description turns out to be rather unconventional, making it hard to study scattering processes in the flat limit.

As an application of the spinorial formulation,

n

To obtain an alternative understanding of this result, we also project all vertices of this sector to the light-cone gauge, and observe that the MHV sector can be removed by a local field redefinition. Due to the unconventional space-like spinor formalism in the Lorentzian case, we compute the tree-level scattering amplitudes of HS-IKKT theory only in Euclidean signature in the present paper, and set the stage to elaborate the Lorentzian case elsewhere.

\displaystyle(\underline{\gamma}^{0})^{A}_{\ B}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&\mbox{1 \kern-5.89996pt{\rm l}}_{2}\\

Semi-classical

\displaystyle y_{4}=R\sinh(\tau)\,,

\displaystyle=+\frac{\eta^{\mu\nu}}{R}y^{4}=\eta^{\mu\nu}\sinh(\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\{t^{\mu},y^{4}\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{y^{\mu}}{R}\,,

(2.34b)

\displaystyle m^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=R^{2}\{t^{\mu},t^{\nu}\}=-\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(\tau)}\Big(\sinh(\tau)(y^{\mu}t^{\nu}-y^{\nu}t^{\mu})+\epsilon^{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}y_{\sigma}t_{\rho}\Big)\,.

(2.34c)

The above suggests that we can identify

\mathcal{M}^{1,3}_{N}

\displaystyle\{t_{\mu},\phi(y)\}=\sinh(\tau)\partial_{\mu}\phi(y)\,.

(2.35)

H^{3}

\displaystyle=R^{2}\sinh(\tau)\cosh^{2}(\tau)\,,

(2.39a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=R\sinh(\tau)^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

(2.39b)

One can show that around

\displaystyle a(t)\sim t^{1/5}\,,

(2.40a)

late time

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle a(t)\sim t\,.

(2.40b)

The above features a FLRW cosmology that is asymptotically coasting at late times, and has a Big Bounce at the initial time

3
Fuzzy twistor geometry and 4-hyperboloid

We now study the spinorial versions of
H_{N}^{4}
in a stereographic projection which naturally admits a smooth flat limit. The spinorial effective vielbein and metric are also derived. We begin with a brief review of twistor geometry following
[
22
]
.

3.1
Twistor space

We define the commutative twistor space
\mathbb{PT}
to be an open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1,2}
(for a review, see e.g.
[
23
]
and
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{PT}=\{Z^{A}=(Z^{1},Z^{2},Z^{3},Z^{4})=(\lambda^{\alpha},\mu^{\dot{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{P}^{1,2}\,|\,\hat{\mathcal{N}}\neq 0\}\,.

(3.1)

Here
Z^{A}\sim r\,Z^{A}\,,\ \forall r\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
are homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{P}^{1,2}
, and
\lambda^{\alpha},\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}

transform in the fundamental of the compact subgroups
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
of
SU(2,2)
. Note that these are
not
Weyl spinors, as any non-compact transformation
(such as boosts in
SO(1,3)
) will mix these two spinors.
The complex conjugation of the twistor
Z^{A}
denoted as
Z_{A}^{\dagger}
:

\displaystyle Z_{A}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{\lambda}}_{\alpha}\\
{\bar{\mu}}_{\dot{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix}\,

(3.2)

transforms in the anti-fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{su}(2,2)
so that the Dirac conjugation of
Z^{A}
reads

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{A}=Z^{\dagger}_{B}(\gamma_{0})^{B}{}_{A}=\binom{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{-\bar{\mu}_{\dot{\alpha}}}\,.

(3.3)

Restricting ourselves to
\mathfrak{so}(1,4)\subset\mathfrak{su}(2,2)
, we
can also use the anti-symmetric matrix
C^{AB}

to define the ‘quaternionic’ conjugate twistor
\hat{Z}^{A}
of
Z^{A}
as

\displaystyle\hat{Z}^{A}

\displaystyle=(\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha},\hat{\mu}^{\dot{\alpha}})=Z_{B}^{\dagger}C^{AB}\,,

(3.4)

which is manifestly compatible with
SO(1,4)
. Note that the hat conjugation acts on spinors with the following rules

\displaystyle\lambda^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1})\mapsto\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}=\overline{\lambda}_{\beta}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}=(\overline{\lambda_{1}},-\overline{\lambda_{0}})\,,

\displaystyle\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=(\mu^{\dot{0}},\mu^{\dot{1}})\mapsto\hat{\mu}^{\dot{\alpha}}=\overline{\mu}_{\dot{\beta}}\varepsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}=(\overline{\mu_{\dot{1}}},-\overline{\mu_{\dot{0}}})\,.

(3.5)

It is useful to check that

\displaystyle\langle\lambda\,\bar{\lambda}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}=-\lambda_{\beta}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta}=\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle\,,

(3.6a)

\displaystyle\hat{\hat{\lambda}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{\alpha}\,,

(3.6b)

\displaystyle\hat{\hat{\mu}}^{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(3.6c)

This means at the level of spinors, we can interchangeably use
(\hat{\lambda},\hat{\mu})
for
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}
). In terms of the hatted spinors, the number operator
\hat{\mathcal{N}}=\bar{Z}_{A}Z^{A}
becomes

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{N}}=\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle-[\mu\,\hat{\mu}]=N\,,

(3.7)

where the angle and square brackets are defined by
\langle u\,v\rangle=u^{\alpha}v_{\alpha}\,,\ [u\,v]=u^{\dot{\alpha}}v_{\dot{\alpha}}
.

The correspondence between
\mathbb{PT}
and
H^{4}
is given by the incidence relations

\displaystyle\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}=\mathtt{x}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{\alpha}\hskip 20.00003pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 20.00003pt\mathtt{x}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}\hat{\mu}^{\dot{\alpha}}-\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}}{\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle}\,,

(3.8)

which state that a point
\mathtt{x}\in H^{4}
corresponds to a holomorphic embedded Riemann sphere. We emphasize that all spinors and the
2\times 2
matrix
\mathtt{x}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
are dimensionless
[
10
]
. Furthermore, we have the following reality condition:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathtt{x}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\mathtt{x}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(3.9)

The Poisson structure (
2.22
) in terms of spinors read:

\displaystyle\{\lambda^{\alpha},\bar{\lambda}_{\beta}\}=\delta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\{\mu^{\dot{\alpha}},\bar{\mu}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=\delta^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{\dot{\beta}}\,,

(3.10)

which leads us to the following gradations:

\displaystyle\{\hat{\mathcal{N}},\lambda^{\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{\alpha}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\{\hat{\mathcal{N}},\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}\}=-\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(3.11a)

\displaystyle\{\hat{\mathcal{N}},\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=+\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\{\hat{\mathcal{N}},\bar{\mu}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}=+\bar{\mu}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(3.11b)

\displaystyle\{\hat{\mathcal{N}},\mathtt{x}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(3.11c)

The algebra of functions (
2.21
) on
\mathbb{P}^{1,2}
in this language becomes:

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{P}^{1,2})=\sum_{n+m=p+q}f_{\alpha(n)\dot{\alpha}(m)}{}^{\beta(p)\dot{\beta}(q)}\lambda^{\alpha(n)}\mu^{\dot{\alpha}(m)}\bar{\lambda}_{\beta(p)}\bar{\mu}_{\dot{\beta}(q)}\,,

(3.12)

which can be reduced further to polynomials in terms of
\lambda,\hat{\lambda}
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{P}^{1,2})=\sum_{n}f_{\alpha(n)}{}^{\beta(n)}(\mathtt{x})\lambda^{\alpha(n)}\bar{\lambda}_{\beta(n)}\,

(3.13)

by using the incident relation (
3.8
). Due to the constraint
\{\hat{\mathcal{N}},f\}=0
where
f\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{P}^{1,2})
, the number of
\lambda
and
\bar{\lambda}
generators must be the same at this point.
Therefore, all higher-spin modes can be viewed as functions on the internal (fuzzy) Riemann sphere
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{N}
.

3.2
The stereographic projection to
H^{4}

Following the lines of
[
10
]
, we can recover the above twistorial construction and the incidence relations from the Hopf map (
2.25
)

\mathbb{P}^{1,2}\to H^{4}
(or Euclidean AdS
4

H^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
Using (
2.25
), we get

\displaystyle y^{\mathtt{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell_{p}}{2}\bar{Z}_{A}(\gamma^{\mathtt{a}})^{A}{}_{B}Z^{B}=-\frac{\ell_{p}}{2}\langle\lambda\,\bar{\lambda}\rangle(\hat{\sigma}^{\mathtt{a}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\mathtt{x}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt\mathtt{a}=1,2,3,4\,,

(3.14a)

\displaystyle y^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell_{p}}{2}\bar{Z}_{A}(\gamma^{0})^{A}{}_{B}Z^{B}=+\frac{\ell_{p}}{2}([\mu\,\bar{\mu}]+\langle\lambda\,\bar{\lambda}\rangle)=R+\ell_{p}[\mu\,\bar{\mu}]\geq R

(3.14b)

where
\hat{\sigma}^{\mathtt{a}}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=(\tilde{\sigma}^{m}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\epsilon_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}})
for
m=1,2,3
. Note that when the spinors
\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}=(0,0)
, we will be at the center
y^{0}=R
of the projection, reflecting the fact that the underlying doubleton irrep
\mathcal{H}_{N}
of
\mathfrak{so}(4,2)
is a lowest-weight representation
[
8
]
. Furthermore,
y^{0}
can be also written as

\displaystyle y^{0}=-R+\ell_{p}\langle\lambda\,\bar{\lambda}\rangle=-R+\ell_{p}\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle\,.

(3.15)

It is then convenient to define

\displaystyle x^{\mathtt{a}}=\frac{R}{\ell_{p}\langle\lambda\,\bar{\lambda}\rangle}y^{\mathtt{a}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pty^{\mathtt{a}}=\Big(1+\frac{y^{0}}{R}\Big)x^{\mathtt{a}}\,

(3.16)

for
\mathtt{a}=1,2,3,4
.
We obtain

\displaystyle y^{0}=\frac{R(x^{2}+R^{2})}{(R^{2}-x^{2})}\,,\hskip 20.00003pty^{\mathtt{a}}=\frac{2R^{2}x^{\mathtt{a}}}{(R^{2}-x^{2})}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptx^{2}=x_{\mathtt{a}}x^{\mathtt{a}}<R^{2}\,.

(3.17)

It is a simple matter to show that

\displaystyle-y_{0}^{2}+y_{\mathtt{a}}y^{\mathtt{a}}=-R^{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathtt{a}=1,2,3,4\,,

(3.18)

so that the above
x^{\mathtt{a}}
define a stereographic projection
H^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
The conformally flat metric corresponding to
H^{4}
can then be obtained by the pullback:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\Big(\frac{\partial y^{a}}{\partial x^{\mathtt{a}}}\frac{\partial y^{b}}{\partial x^{\mathtt{b}}}\eta_{ab}\Big)\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{a}}\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{b}}:=g_{\mathtt{a}\mathtt{b}}\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{a}}\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{b}}=\frac{4R^{4}\eta_{\mathtt{a}\mathtt{b}}\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{a}}\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{b}}}{(R^{2}-x^{2})^{2}}=\Omega^{2}\eta_{\mathtt{a}\mathtt{b}}\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{a}}\mathrm{d}x^{\mathtt{b}}\,

(3.19)

thus defining the conformal factor
\Omega^{2}
,
where
\eta_{\mathtt{a}\mathtt{b}}=\,\mathrm{diag}(+,+,+,+)
.
Although this coordinate system does not give us the desired Lorentzian signature, the metric (
3.19
) can have a smooth flat limit where
R\rightarrow\infty
. Lastly, it is worth noting that (
3.14b
) implies

\displaystyle\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}\Big(1+\frac{y^{0}}{R}\Big)=\frac{NR^{2}}{(R^{2}-x^{2})}=\frac{N}{2}\Omega\,,

(3.20a)

\displaystyle[\mu\,\hat{\mu}]

\displaystyle=\frac{Nx^{2}}{2(R^{2}-x^{2})}\,,

(3.20b)

which allows us to parametrize
\lambda^{\alpha},\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}
fiber coordinates as

\displaystyle\lambda^{\alpha}:=\frac{R}{\sqrt{R^{2}-x^{2}}}\binom{z}{-1}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}:=\frac{R}{\sqrt{R^{2}-x^{2}}}\binom{+1}{\bar{z}}\,,

(3.21)

where
1+|z|^{2}=N
for

Effective vielbein and metric.

Using the explicit form of the gamma matrices we recover the

\displaystyle\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}:=\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle\mathtt{x}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\lambda^{\alpha}\hat{\mu}^{\dot{\alpha}}-\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathtt{y}^{0}:=\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle\,.

It is convenient to work with the projective spinor bundle

has coordinates

and is a trivial bundle
[

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{PS})=\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\times C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\,.

As in
[

]

\{y^{a},-\}

[
8
]

Using (
3.10

[
10
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}|\beta\dot{\beta}}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\mathtt{y}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\}=2(\lambda^{(\alpha}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta)}\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}+\mu^{(\dot{\alpha}}\hat{\mu}^{\dot{\beta})}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta})\,,

(3.24a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{0|\alpha\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathtt{y}^{0},\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\}=\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}+\lambda^{\alpha}\hat{\mu}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(3.24b)

We also find

\displaystyle\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\lambda^{\beta}\}=-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\hat{\lambda}^{\beta}\}=+\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\hat{\mu}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(3.25a)

If
\varphi(\mathtt{y})
is a
\mathfrak{hs}
-valued smooth section of
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{PS})
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\varphi(\mathtt{y}|\lambda,\hat{\lambda})\}:&=\big(\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\mathtt{y}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\}\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}+\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\lambda^{\beta}\}\partial_{\beta}+\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\hat{\lambda}^{\beta}\}\hat{\partial}_{\beta}\big)\varphi\,\\
&=\big(\mathcal{E}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}|\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}+\mathcal{E}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}|\beta}\partial_{\beta}+\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}|\beta}\hat{\partial}_{\beta}\big)\varphi\,,\end{split}

(3.26)

where
\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}:=\partial/\partial\mathtt{y}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
,
\partial_{\alpha}:=\partial/\partial\lambda^{\alpha}
and
\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}:=\partial/\partial\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}
. Similarly, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{\mathtt{y}^{0},\varphi(\mathtt{y}|\lambda,\hat{\lambda})\}:&=\big(\{\mathtt{y}^{0},\mathtt{y}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\}\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}+\{\mathtt{y}^{0},\lambda^{\alpha}\}\partial_{\alpha}+\{\mathtt{y}^{0},\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\}\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}\big)\varphi\,\\
&=\big(\mathcal{E}^{0|\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}+\mathcal{E}^{0|\beta}\partial_{\beta}+\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{0|\beta}\hat{\partial}_{\beta}\big)\varphi\,.\end{split}

(3.27)

Note that all contributions from

and

are subleading in the flat limit as in the case of

(cf.,
[

G^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}\beta\dot{\beta}}
, it is sufficient to consider a scalar field
\vartheta(\mathtt{y})
whose kinetic Lagrangian reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\mathcal{E}^{0|\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\vartheta\,\mathcal{E}_{0|\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\vartheta+\mathcal{E}^{\zeta\dot{\zeta}|\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\vartheta\,\mathcal{E}_{\zeta\dot{\zeta}|\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\vartheta:=G^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\vartheta\,\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\vartheta\,.\end{split}

(3.28)

Proceeding as in
[
10
]

\displaystyle G^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}\beta\dot{\beta}}(\mathtt{y})

Discussion

In this work, we established the connections between the almost-commutative 4-hyperboloid and non-compact semi-classical twistor space
\mathbb{P}^{1,2}_{N}
in the spinor formalism. Using this as a background in the IKKT model, we studied the resulting higher-spin gauge theory for two spaces with distinct signatures:
(i)
a Euclidean 4-hyperboloid;
(ii)
and a FLRW-like cosmological spacetime. We also furnished a simple vectorial description of HS-IKKT in the flat limit of these two cases, which simplifies some of the technical steps in counting and organizing the degrees of freedom of higher-spin modes in HS-IKKT in
[

and
[

Armed with this setup,
we then shown that all tree-level
n
-point on-shell amplitudes (for
n\geq 4
) with an appropriate analytic continuation of the massless sector within the Yang-Mills part of the Euclidean HS-IKKT theory vanish in the flat limit. This result is expected, since HS-IKKT is a ‘parity-violating’ higher-spin theory featuring two-derivative interactions when we express it in terms of chiral representation.
23
23

23

This representation gives the lowest number of derivatives in the interactions, and
unveils an

Indeed, as observed in
[

, massless higher-spin theories constructed from the chiral representation in the flat space with the number of derivatives in the cubic vertices higher than one must have trivial tree-level scattering amplitudes. Thus, the massless sector of HS-IKKT is also in argreement with the result of
[
47
]
.
This can be explained by projecting the cubic vertices of the Yang-Mills sector in the flat limit to the light-cone gauge, we observe that the MHV
3

This allows to identify the massless YM sector with self-dual higher-spin gravity with 2-derivative interactions considered in
[

. In this sense, the massless sector of HS-IKKT theory in the flat limit falls into the class of (quasi-)chiral theories. We conclude that
S=1
at tree-level, at least for the modes under consideration. However, the triviality of the
S

It would be then interesting to study the scattering amplitudes of HS-IKKT or any other higher-spin theories at loop-level, see the discussions in e.g.

While the non-commutative twistor approach produces more contributions than the standard approach in twistor literature, we observe that the non-vanishing contributions in non-commutative twistor approach coincide with the ones using the standard approach of twistor theory in the flat limit.

Finally, let us look at the quartic term:

\begin{split}I_{4}&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathtt{K}\,\{\mathsf{a}^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\gamma}},\mathsf{a}^{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\}\{\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\dot{\sigma}},\mathsf{a}_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\sigma}}\}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathtt{K}\,\mathcal{E}^{\circ\dot{\circ},\bullet\dot{\bullet}}\partial_{\circ\dot{\circ}}\mathsf{a}^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\gamma}}\partial_{\bullet\dot{\bullet}}\mathsf{a}^{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\,\mathcal{E}^{\diamond\dot{\diamond},\blacklozenge\dot{\blacklozenge}}\partial_{\diamond\dot{\diamond}}\mathsf{a}_{\alpha\dot{\sigma}}\partial_{\blacklozenge\dot{\blacklozenge}}\mathsf{a}_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\sigma}}\\

(B.25)

Thanks to the plane-wave solutions (
7.4
) and the specific gauge choice we made in Section
7
, all contributions resulting from the integral over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
vanish on-shell. For this reason, we can either ignore the quartic, or write it as

\displaystyle S_{4}\simeq 2\int d^{4}\mathtt{x}\,\Big(\partial_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}_{1}}\mathbb{A}^{\alpha(2s_{1}-1)\,\dot{\sigma}}\partial_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\gamma}_{1}}\mathbb{A}^{\alpha(2s_{2}-1)}{}_{\dot{\sigma}}\Big)\Big(\partial^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{\alpha(2s_{3}-1)}{}^{\dot{\tau}}\partial^{\alpha\dot{\gamma}_{2}}\mathbb{A}_{\alpha(2s_{4}-1)\,\dot{\tau}}\Big)+\widetilde{S}_{4}\,.

Unlike the construction of higher-spin Yang-Mills

Vertices from the self-dual sector.

To obtain the above result, one can also start from the self-dual sector. This provides a simpler way to contract indices in the interactions. But we first need to know how the higher-spin modes of the
\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}
field talk to each others. Recall that the action of the YM sector in first order formalism has the form cf. Section
6

\displaystyle S^{\text{YM}}=\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\mho\Big(\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}\mathsf{f}^{\alpha\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}\mathsf{b}^{\alpha\alpha}\Big)

(B.27)

Now, we let
\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}
to have the following higher-spin expansion (see the discussion around (
6.19
) for the reality condition of
\mathfrak{hs}
-valued
\mathsf{f}^{\alpha\alpha}
and
\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}
):

\displaystyle\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}=\Big(B_{\beta(2s)\alpha\alpha}-\mathrm{i}\epsilon_{\beta\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\beta(2s-1)\alpha}\Big)\lambda^{\beta(s)}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta(s)}\,.

(B.28)

It can be checked that there is no mixed term between
B
and
\tilde{\mathsf{B}}
. For instance, consider spin-1 case

\displaystyle\int\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}\mathsf{b}^{\alpha\alpha}\big|_{s=1}

\displaystyle=\int B_{\alpha\alpha}B^{\alpha\alpha}+\mathrm{i}B_{\alpha\alpha}\epsilon^{\beta\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}^{\beta\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\hat{\lambda}_{\beta}-\mathrm{i}B_{\alpha\alpha}\epsilon^{\beta\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}^{\beta\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\hat{\lambda}_{\beta}+\lambda^{\beta}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta}\epsilon_{\beta\alpha}\epsilon^{\gamma\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\beta\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}^{\gamma\alpha}\lambda_{\gamma}\hat{\lambda}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int B_{\alpha\alpha}B^{\alpha\alpha}+\lambda^{\beta}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta}\epsilon_{\beta\alpha}\epsilon^{\gamma\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\beta\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}^{\gamma\alpha}\lambda_{\gamma}\hat{\lambda}_{\gamma}\,.

(B.29)

We can shorten the last term in (
B
) to

\displaystyle\int-\frac{1}{4}\langle\lambda\,\hat{\lambda}\rangle^{2}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\alpha\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}^{\alpha\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{\beta}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\beta}{}^{\gamma}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\beta}{}^{\gamma}\lambda_{\gamma}\hat{\lambda}_{\gamma}\sim\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\alpha\alpha}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}^{\alpha\alpha}\,.

(B.30)

Therefore, the
B
and
\tilde{\mathsf{B}}
modes do not couple to each other (other higher-spin cases are analogous). Observe that we can use
\tilde{\mathsf{B}}
as Lagrangian multipliers for the fuzzy Lorenz gauge condition (
6.21
)

\displaystyle\int\mho\,\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{\beta(2s)}\{\mathtt{y}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\mathcal{A}^{\beta(2s)|\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\}

(B.31)

so that only the first modes
B_{\alpha(2s)}
propagate and
\tilde{\mathsf{B}}
becomes non-dynamical. Therefore, it is suggestive to treat
B_{\alpha(2s)}
as negative helicity modes. To proceed, we will look at the kinetic terms

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\int\mho\lambda^{\beta(s_{1})}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta(s_{1})}B_{\beta(2s_{1})\alpha\alpha}\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\alpha}},\mathcal{A}^{\gamma(2s_{2})|\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\}\lambda_{\gamma(s_{2})}\hat{\lambda}_{\gamma(s_{2})}\,.

(B.32)

By decomposing
\mathcal{A}^{\beta(2s)|\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=A^{\beta(2s)\alpha\,\dot{\alpha}}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\mathsf{A}}^{\beta(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}
, we can check that

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\mho\lambda^{\beta(s)}\hat{\lambda}^{\beta(s)}\lambda_{\gamma(s)}\hat{\lambda}_{\gamma(s)}B_{\beta(2s)\alpha\alpha}\Big(\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\alpha}},A^{\gamma(2s)\alpha\,\dot{\alpha}}\}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}\{\mathtt{y}^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\alpha}},\tilde{\mathsf{A}}^{\gamma(2s+1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\}\Big)\,

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}xB_{\alpha(2s)}\partial^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(B.33)

Thus, as always, the kinetic terms decrible the coupling between negative and positive helicity fields. From here, we can express
A
and
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}
in terms of
\mathbb{A}_{\pm}
as

\displaystyle A^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}+\mathbb{A}_{-}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}}{2}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{\mathsf{A}}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}-\mathbb{A}_{-}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}}{2\mathrm{i}}

(B.34)

to study the cubic interactions
\int\mathsf{b}\{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\}
.

In this first-order formalism, the interaction terms have much less structure compared to the second-order case. In particular, after integrating out fiber coordinates, we end up with three main structures

\displaystyle B^{-}_{\alpha(2s_{1})}\partial^{\alpha}{}_{\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{\pm}^{\alpha(2s_{2}-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\partial^{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{\pm}^{\alpha(2s_{3}-1)}{}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(B.35a)

\displaystyle B^{-}_{\alpha(2s_{1})}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{\pm}^{\alpha(2s_{2}-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\partial^{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{\pm}^{\alpha(2s_{3}-1)}{}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(B.35b)

\displaystyle B^{-}_{\alpha(2s_{1})}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{\pm}^{\alpha(2s_{2}-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{\pm}^{\alpha(2s_{3}-1)}{}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(B.35c)

where, as always, all un-dotted indices of the lower level are understood to be contracted in all possible way with the indices of the upper level. Notice that the difference between the above structures are merely the positions of the undotted indices in the partial derivatives.

Besides the polarization tensors (
7.4
) for the
\mathbb{A}_{\pm}
fields, we set

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\alpha(2s)}^{-}=\lambda_{\alpha(2s)}

(B.36)

to the the polarization tensor associated with the
B_{\alpha(2s)}
negative helicity fields
[
35
]
. Note that there is no auxiliary/reference spinors
\epsilon^{-}_{\alpha(2s)}
. Then, upon plugging in the plane-wave solutions, it can be checked that (
B.35c
) is the only structure that survives. It is worth mentioning that there are no combination such as

\displaystyle B_{\alpha(2s_{1})}\{\mathbb{A}^{\alpha(2s_{2}-1-m)\beta(m)\,\dot{\alpha}},\mathbb{A}^{\alpha(2s_{3}-1-m)}{}_{\beta(m)\,\dot{\alpha}}\}

(B.37)

where un-dotted indices of the
\mathbb{A}
fields contracted with each others since it results in trivial amplitudes. In fact, this can also be understood from the light-cone point of view where we set
\mathbb{A}^{\alpha(2s-2)0\,\dot{0}}=0
and choose
\mathbb{A}^{1(2s-1)\,\dot{0}}
to be the components carry physical degrees of the
\mathbb{A}
fields. Then, for any combination such as (
B.37
), it will vanish on the nose in the light-cone gauge.

Further scrutiny shows that non-trivial cubic amplitudes will come from

\displaystyle B^{-}_{\alpha(2s_{1})}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s_{2}-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s_{3}-1)}{}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(B.38)

These are the vertices that give us the
\overline{\text{MHV}}_{3}
amplitudes (

\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}\mathsf{f}^{\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle\int\mho\,\mathsf{b}_{\alpha\alpha}\mathsf{f}^{\alpha\alpha}\sim\int\mathrm{d}^{4}xB^{-}_{\alpha(2s)}F^{\alpha(2s)}

(B.39)

where for convenience we have defined

\displaystyle F_{+}^{\alpha(2s)}:=\partial^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s-1)}{}_{\dot{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m+n=s+2}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s_{2}-1)\,\dot{\sigma}}\partial_{\alpha}{}^{\dot{\gamma}}\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\alpha(2s_{3}-1)}{}_{\dot{\sigma}}\,.

(B.40)

Of course, the above BF action (
B.39
) in flat space is only the self-dual sector which features the
\overline{\text{MHV}}_{3}
amplitudes. To go back to the second-order formalism, we can integrate out the
B^{-}_{\alpha(2s)}
which results in

\displaystyle S_{\text{YM}}=\int F_{\alpha(2s)}F^{\alpha(2s)}

(B.41)

As this stage, one can give the higher-spin fields
\mathbb{A}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}
all possible helicity when we consider cubic and quartic interactions. The reason is that the negative helicity modes
B^{-}_{\alpha(2s)}
need to be replaced by
\mathbb{A}_{-}^{\alpha(2s-1)\,\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle S^{\text{YM}}=\sum_{s}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}\mathtt{x}\,F_{\alpha(2s)}F^{\alpha(2s)}+\widetilde{S}_{3}+\widetilde{S}_{4}\,.

(B.42)

where
\widetilde{S}_{i}
for

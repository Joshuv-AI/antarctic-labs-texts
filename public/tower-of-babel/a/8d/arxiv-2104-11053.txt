Almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds as extensions of 2-dimensional space-forms
Mancho Manev, Veselina Tavkova

Abstract
. Almost paracontact Riemannian manifolds of the lowest dimension are studied, whose paracontact distributions are equipped with an almost paracomplex structure. These manifolds are constructed as a product of a real line and a 2-dimensional Riemannian space-form. Their metric is obtained in two ways: as a cone metric and as a hyperbolic extension of the metric of the underlying paracomplex 2-manifold. The resulting manifolds are studied and characterised in terms of the classification used and their curvature properties.

Introduction

In 1976, I. Sato
[
9
]
introduced the concept of an almost paracontact structure compatible with a Riemannian metric as an analogue of an almost contact Riemannian manifold.
The study of the differential geometry of these manifolds began with
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
by I. Sato, T. Adati and T. Miyazawa.
After that, in
[

-1
of the paracontact endomorphism, respectively.
It also has a simple eigenvalue of 0.

In
[

(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
of type
(n,n)
.
The dimension of
\mathcal{M}
is
2n+1
and the induced almost product structure

on the paracontact distribution
\ker(\eta)
is traceless, i.e.
P
is an almost paracomplex structure.
Because of this, the present authors called them

almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds

[
5
]

and continued their study together with S. Ivanov and H. Manev (e.g.
[
3
,
5
,
6
]

In the present paper, we study the geometry of almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds of the lowest dimension 3. In Section
1
, we recall some necessary facts about the studied manifolds.
In Section
2

3

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds

Let
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\eta,g)
be an
almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifold
(abbr.
apapR manifold
).
This means that
\mathcal{M}
is a
(2n+1)
-dimensional differentiable manifold,

\phi
is a paracontact endomorphism of the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{M}
,
\xi
is a characteristic vector field and
\eta
is its dual 1-form, and
g
is a compatible Riemannian metric, such that:

(1.1)

\begin{array}[]{c}\phi^{2}=\mathcal{I}-\eta\otimes\xi,\quad\eta(\xi)=1,\quad\eta\circ\phi=0,\quad\phi\xi=0,\quad{\rm tr}\phi=0,\\[4.0pt]
g(\phi x,\phi y)=g(x,y)-\eta(x)\eta(y),\end{array}

where
\mathcal{I}
denotes the identity on
T\mathcal{M}

[
9
]
,
[
4
]
.

Here and further
x
,
y
,
z
,
w
will stand for arbitrary
elements of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
of tangent vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
or vectors in the tangent space
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
at
p\in\mathcal{M}
.

The associated metric
\tilde{g}
of
g
on
(\mathcal{M},\allowbreak{}\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)
is determined by

\tilde{g}(x,y)=g(x,\phi y)+\eta(x)\eta(y)
. In
[
4
]
, it is shown that
\tilde{g}
is a compatible metric
with
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta)
as
g
, but
\tilde{g}
is a pseudo-Riemannian metric of signature
(n+1,n)
.

The fundamental tensor
F
of type
(0,3)
on
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is defined by

F(x,y,z)=g\bigl(\left(\nabla_{x}\phi\right)y,z\bigr),

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.
The basic properties of
F
with respect to the structure are the following:

\begin{array}[]{l}F(x,y,z)=F(x,z,y)\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{F(x,y,z)}=-F(x,\phi y,\phi z)+\eta(y)F(x,\xi,z)+\eta(z)F(x,y,\xi).\end{array}

The Lee forms of
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
are the following 1-forms
associated with
F
:

(1.2)

\theta(z)=g^{ij}F(e_{i},e_{j},z),\quad\theta^{*}(z)=g^{ij}F(e_{i},\phi e_{j},z),\quad\omega(z)=F(\xi,\xi,z),

where
g^{ij}
are the components of the inverse
matrix of
g
with respect to a basis

\left\{\xi;e_{i}\right\}

(i=1,2,\dots,2n)
of
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
at an arbitrary point
p\in\mathcal{M}
.

A classification of apapR manifolds is made in
[
4
]
.
It consists of 11 basic
classes
\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\dots
,
\mathcal{F}_{11}
and it is made with respect to

F
.
The components
F_{i}
of
F
corresponding to
\mathcal{F}_{i}
are determined in
[
5
]
.
Namely,
(\mathcal{M},\allowbreak{}\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{i}

(i\in\{1,2,\dots,11\})

if and only if the equality
F=F_{i}
is valid.
Moreover,

(\mathcal{M},\allowbreak{}\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{j}\oplus\cdots
if and only if
the following condition is satisfied
F=F_{i}+F_{j}+\cdots
.

In the present work, we consider the case of the lowest dimension of
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
, i.e.
\dim{\mathcal{M}}=3
.
Let

\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\}
be a
\phi
-basis of
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
, which satisfies the following
conditions:

(1.3)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi e_{0}=0,\quad\phi e_{1}=e_{2},\quad\phi e_{2}=e_{1},&\xi=e_{0},\\[4.0pt]
\eta(e_{0})=1,\quad\eta(e_{1})=\eta(e_{2})=0,&g(e_{i},e_{j})=\delta_{ij},\;i,j\in\{0,1,2\}.\end{array}

The components of
F
,
\theta
,
\theta^{*}
,
\omega
with respect to
\left\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
are denoted by
{F_{ijk}=F(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})}
,
{\theta_{k}=\theta(e_{k})}
,
{\theta^{*}_{k}=\theta^{*}(e_{k})}
and
{\omega_{k}=\omega(e_{k})}
, respectively.
According to
[
5
]
, we have the following:

(1.4)

\begin{array}[]{c}\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{0}=F_{110}+F_{220},&\theta_{1}=F_{111}=-F_{122}=-\theta^{*}_{2},\\[4.0pt]
\theta^{*}_{0}=F_{120}+F_{210},&\theta_{2}=F_{222}=-F_{211}=-\theta^{*}_{1},\\[4.0pt]
\end{array}\\
\begin{array}[]{lll}\omega_{0}=0,&\omega_{1}=F_{001},&\omega_{2}=F_{002}.\end{array}\end{array}

Then, if
F^{s}
,
s\in\{1,2,\dots,11\}
, are the components of
F
in the corresponding basic classes
\mathcal{F}_{s}
and
x=x^{i}e_{i}
,
y=y^{i}e_{i}
,
z=z^{i}e_{i}
are arbitrary vectors in

T_{p}\mathcal{M}
, we have the following:
[
5
]

(1.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}F^{1}(x,y,z)=\left(x^{1}\theta_{1}-x^{2}\theta_{2}\right)\left(y^{1}z^{1}-y^{2}z^{2}\right);\\[4.0pt]
F^{2}(x,y,z)=F^{3}(x,y,z)=0;\\
F^{4}(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}\bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)+x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)\bigr\};\\[4.0pt]
F^{5}(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{*}_{0}\bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)+x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)\bigr\};\\[4.0pt]
F^{6}(x,y,z)=F^{7}(x,y,z)=0;\\[4.0pt]
F^{8}(x,y,z)=\lambda\bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)-x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)\bigr\},\\[4.0pt]
\hskip 38.0pt\lambda=F_{110}=-F_{220};\\[4.0pt]
F^{9}(x,y,z)=\mu\bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)-x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)\bigr\},\\[4.0pt]
\hskip 38.0pt\mu=F_{120}=-F_{210};\\[4.0pt]
F^{10}(x,y,z)=\nu x^{0}\left(y^{1}z^{1}-y^{2}z^{2}\right),\quad\nu=F_{011}=-F_{022};\\[4.0pt]
F^{11}(x,y,z)=x^{0}\bigl\{\omega_{1}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)+\omega_{2}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)\bigr\}.\end{array}

By virtue of the latter equations, the 3-dimensional manifolds of the considered type can belong only to
the basic classes

\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{4}
,
\mathcal{F}_{5}
,
\mathcal{F}_{8}
,
\mathcal{F}_{9}
,
\mathcal{F}_{10}
,
\mathcal{F}_{11}
and their direct sums
[
5
]
.

The curvature
(1,3)
-tensor
R
of
\nabla
is defined as usually by
R=\left[\nabla,\nabla\right]-\nabla_{[\,\ ]}
.
The corresponding
(0,4)
-tensor is denoted by the same letter and it is determined by
R(x,y,z,w)=g(R(x,y)z,w)
.

The Ricci tensor
\rho
and the scalar curvature
\tau
for
R
as well as
their associated quantities are defined respectively by:

(1.6)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho(y,z)=g^{ij}R(e_{i},y,z,e_{j}),&\tau=g^{ij}\rho(e_{i},e_{j}),\\[4.0pt]
\rho^{*}(y,z)=g^{ij}R(e_{i},y,z,\phi e_{j}),&\tau^{*}=g^{ij}\rho^{*}(e_{i},e_{j}).\end{array}

The following tensors are essential curvature-like tensors of type
(1,3)
:

(1.7)

\begin{array}[]{l}\pi_{1}(x,y)z=g(y,z)x-g(x,z)y,\\[4.0pt]
\pi_{2}(x,y)z=g(y,\phi z)\phi x-g(x,\phi z)\phi y.\end{array}

Their corresponding curvature-like
(0,4)
-tensors are determined by
g
as usually,
\pi_{i}(x,y,z,w)=g\left(\pi_{i}(x,y)z,w\right)
,
i=1,2
.

Let
\alpha
be a non-degenerate 2-plane in
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
,
p\in\mathcal{M}
, having a basis
\{x,y\}
.
The sectional curvature
k(\alpha;p)
is determined by

(1.8)

k(\alpha;p)=\frac{R(x,y,y,x)}{\pi_{1}(x,y,y,x)}.

1.2.
Almost paracomplex Riemannian manifold

Consider a differentiable
manifold
\mathcal{N}
of arbitrary dimension.
Let us recall that an
almost product structure

P
on
\mathcal{N}

is an endomorphism in the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{N}
such that

P^{2}
is the identity
I
in
T\mathcal{N}
, but
P
does not coincide with
I
.
Then, such a manifold
(\mathcal{N},P)
is called an
almost product manifold
.
In the particular case when the eigenvalues
+1
and
-1
of
P
have the same
multiplicity
n
, the structure
P
is called an
almost paracomplex structure

and
(\mathcal{N},P)
is known as an
almost paracomplex manifold
of dimension
2n

[
2
]
. In this case
P
is traceless, i.e.
{\rm tr}P=0
.
This kind of manifolds are also known as almost product Riemannian manifolds
with
{\rm tr}P=0
in
[
12
]
.

As it is known, the
2n
-dimensional paracontact distribution

\mathcal{H}=\ker(\eta)
of
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
can be considered as an almost paracomplex manifold
\mathcal{N}

equipped with an almost paracomplex structure

P=\phi|_{\mathcal{H}}
and a metric
h=g|_{\mathcal{H}}
, where
\phi|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
g|_{\mathcal{H}}
are the
restrictions of
\phi
and
g
on
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.

Let
x^{\prime}
,
y^{\prime}
,
z^{\prime}
,
w^{\prime}
denote arbitrary vector fields or vectors on
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{M}
.

Since
g
is a Riemannian metric of
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta)
,
then
h
is the corresponding Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{H}
and due to (
1.1
) it
is compatible with
P
as follows

(1.9)

h(Px^{\prime},Py^{\prime})=h(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

The associated Riemannian metric
\widetilde{h}
of
h
is determined by

\widetilde{h}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=h(x^{\prime},Py^{\prime})

and it is a pseudo-Riemannian of signature
(n,n)
.

Let us note that an
2n
-dimensional manifold
\mathcal{N}
, which is equipped with an almost paracomplex structure
P
and a Riemannian metric
h
satisfying (
1.9
),
is known as an
almost paracomplex Riemannian manifold

(\mathcal{N},P,h)
.

In
[
7
]
, A. M. Naveira gave a classification of almost product Riemannian
manifolds
(\mathcal{N},P,h)
with respect
to the covariant derivative
\nabla^{\prime}P
for the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\prime}
of
h
.

Furthermore, using this classification,
M. Staikova and K. Gribachev present a classification of almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds
(\mathcal{N},P,h)
in
[
12
]
.
The basic classes of the Staikova-Gribachev classification are three,
\mathcal{W}_{1}
,
\mathcal{W}_{2}
and
\mathcal{W}_{3}
.
Their intersection is the class
\mathcal{W}_{0}

determined by the condition
\nabla^{\prime}P=0
.
The manifolds of the latter class are known as

locally product Riemannian manifolds

[
7
]
,
Riemannian
P
-manifolds

[
12
]

or
paraholomorphic paracomplex Riemannian
manifolds

[
3
]
.
In the present paper, we call such manifolds briefly
\mathcal{W}_{0}
-manifolds.

Let us remark that
\mathcal{W}_{1}
contains
the manifolds which are locally conformal equivalent to
\mathcal{W}_{0}
-manifolds.

In the present paper we deal with the case of almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds
(\mathcal{N},P,h)
of the lowest dimension, i.e.
\dim\mathcal{N}=2
.
As it is known such a manifold is a space-form, i.e. the manifold has a curvature tensor of the form
R^{\prime}=k^{\prime}\,\pi^{\prime}_{1}
,
where
k^{\prime}
is its pointwise constant sectional curvature,
and
\pi^{\prime}_{1}
is the essential curvature-like tensor, such as
\pi_{1}
in (
1.7
), but with respect to
h
.
Moreover, each
(\mathcal{N},P,h)
is a
\mathcal{W}_{1}
-manifold determined by

(1.10)

\begin{array}[]{l}F^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\{h(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\theta^{\prime}(z^{\prime})+h(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\theta^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{F^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\{}+h(x^{\prime},Py^{\prime})\theta^{\prime*}(z^{\prime})+h(x^{\prime},Pz^{\prime})\theta^{\prime*}(y^{\prime})\},\end{array}

where
F^{\prime}
is the fundamental tensor of
(\mathcal{N},P,h)
defined by the following equality

F^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=h\bigl(\left(\nabla^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}P\right)y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\bigr)
,
\theta^{\prime}
is the Lee form determined analogously as in (
1.2
) and
\theta^{\prime*}=-\theta^{\prime}\circ P
is valid
[
12
]
.

2.
Cone over a 2-dimensional paracomplex Riemannian space-form

Let us consider
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N})=\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{N}
,
the cone over a 2-dimensional paracomplex space-form
(\mathcal{N},P,h)
,
where
\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the set of positive reals. We introduce a Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N})
defined by

(2.1)

g\left(\left(x^{\prime},a\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right),\left(y^{\prime},b\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)\right)=t^{2}\,h(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+ab,

where
t
is the coordinate on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
a
,
b
are
differentiable functions on
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N})
.

We equip
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N})
with an almost paracontact almost paracomplex structure
(\phi,\xi,\eta)

by the following way

(2.2)

\phi|_{\mathcal{H}}=P,\quad\xi=\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t},\quad\eta=\mathrm{d}t,\quad\phi\xi=0,\quad\eta\circ\phi=0.

Obviously, we establish the truthfulness of the following

Proposition 2.1
.

The manifold
(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N}),\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is a 3-dimensional apapR manifold.

According to (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) and the well known Koszul equality

(2.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}2g(\nabla_{x}y,z)=xg(y,z)+yg(z,x)-zg(x,y)\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{2g(\nabla_{x}y,z)=}+g([x,y],z)+g([z,x],y)+g([z,y],x),\end{array}

we get the following equalities for
the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of
g
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}g\left(\nabla_{x^{\prime}}y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=t^{2}\,h\left(\nabla^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),&g\left(\nabla_{\xi}y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=t\,h\left(y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),\\[4.0pt]
g\left(\nabla_{x^{\prime}}y^{\prime},\xi\right)=-t\,h\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right),&g\left(\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\xi,z^{\prime}\right)=t\,h\left(x^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right).\end{array}

Bearing in mind the latter equalities, we obtain the covariant derivatives with respect to
\nabla
as follows

(2.4)

\nabla_{x^{\prime}}y^{\prime}=\nabla^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}y^{\prime}-\frac{1}{t}g\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)\xi,\quad\nabla_{\xi}y^{\prime}=\frac{1}{t}y^{\prime},\quad\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\xi=\frac{1}{t}x^{\prime}.

By virtue of (
2.4
), we get

\begin{array}[]{l}R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})z^{\prime}=\frac{1}{t^{2}}(k^{\prime}-1)\pi_{1}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})z^{\prime},\\[4.0pt]
R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\xi=R(x^{\prime},\xi)y^{\prime}=R(\xi,x^{\prime})y^{\prime}=R(x^{\prime},\xi)\xi=R(\xi,y^{\prime})\xi=0.\end{array}

Thus, by direct computations, we establish the following

(2.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},w^{\prime})=\frac{1}{t^{2}}(k^{\prime}-1)\pi_{1}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},w^{\prime}),\\[4.0pt]
R(\xi,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=R(x^{\prime},\xi,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},\xi,z^{\prime})=R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},\xi)\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{R(\xi,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})}=R(y^{\prime},\xi,\xi,z^{\prime})=R(\xi,y^{\prime},z^{\prime},\xi)=0.\end{array}

Using (
1.3
) and (
2.1
), we determine the components
h_{ij}=h(e_{i},e_{j})
and
g_{ij}=g(e_{i},e_{j})
with respect to the basis. The non-zero ones of them are the following:

(2.6)

h_{11}=h_{22}=\frac{1}{t^{2}},\quad g_{00}=g_{11}=g_{22}=1.

Taking into account (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), we determine the components of the covariant derivatives of
e_{i}
with respect to
\nabla
:

(2.7)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}=\nabla^{\prime}_{e_{1}}e_{1}-\frac{1}{t}e_{0},&\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}=\nabla^{\prime}_{e_{1}}e_{2},&\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{0}=\frac{1}{t}e_{1},\\[4.0pt]
\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}=\nabla^{\prime}_{e_{2}}e_{1},&\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{2}=\nabla^{\prime}_{e_{2}}e_{2}-\frac{1}{t}e_{0},&\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{0}=\frac{1}{t}e_{2},\\[4.0pt]
\nabla_{e_{0}}e_{1}=\frac{1}{t}e_{1},&\nabla_{e_{0}}e_{2}=\frac{1}{t}e_{2},&\nabla_{e_{0}}e_{0}=0.\end{array}

Bearing in mind (
2.2
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we obtain the components
F_{ijk}

of
F
with respect to the basis
\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\}
. The non-zero ones of them are:

(2.8)

\begin{array}[]{l}F_{111}=-F_{122}=\theta^{\prime}_{1},\qquad F_{222}=-F_{211}=\theta^{\prime}_{2},\\[4.0pt]
F_{120}=F_{102}=F_{210}=F_{201}=-\frac{1}{t},\end{array}

where
\theta^{\prime}_{i}=\theta^{\prime}(e_{i})
for
i=1,2
.

According to (
1.4
), we compute the components of the Lee forms of
(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N}),\phi,\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)
and the non-zero ones of them are:

(2.9)

\theta_{1}=\theta^{\prime}_{1},\quad\theta_{2}=\theta^{\prime}_{2},\quad\theta^{*}_{1}=-\theta^{\prime}_{2},\quad\theta^{*}_{2}=-\theta^{\prime}_{1},\quad\theta^{*}_{0}=-\frac{2}{t}.

By virtue of (
1.5
) and (
2.8
), we establish the following form of the tensor
F
:

F(x,y,z)=(F^{1}+F^{5})(x,y,z).

According to (
1.5
) and (
1.10
), the non-zero components of
F^{1}
and
F^{5}
are the following:

(2.10)

\begin{array}[]{l}F^{1}_{111}=-F^{1}_{122}=\theta_{1},\quad F^{1}_{222}=-F^{1}_{211}=\theta_{2},\\[4.0pt]
F^{5}_{120}=F^{5}_{102}=F^{5}_{210}=F^{5}_{201}=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{*}_{0}.\end{array}

Thus, we obtain the following

Theorem 2.2
.

The 3-dimensional apapR manifold
(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N}),\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
:

(1)

belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{5}
,

(2)

belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{5}
if and only if
(\mathcal{N},P,h)
is a
\mathcal{W}_{0}
-manifold,

(3)

cannot belong to
\mathcal{F}_{1}
.

Proof.

We compare (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10

On steady states for the Vlasov-Schrödinger-Poisson system
Younghun Hong, Sangdon Jin

Abstract
. The Vlasov-Schrödinger-Poisson system is a kinetic-quantum hybrid model describing quasi-lower dimensional electron gases. For this system, we construct a large class of 2D kinetic/1D quantum steady states in a bounded domain as generalized free energy minimizers, and we show their finite subband structure, monotonicity, uniqueness and conditional dynamical stability. Our proof is based on the concentration-compactness principle, but some additional difficulties arise due to lack of compactness originated from the hybrid nature (see Remark 1.9 ). To overcome the difficulties, we introduce a 3-step refinement of a minimizing sequence by rearrangement and partial minimization problems, and the coercivity lemma for the free energy (Lemma 5.3 ) is crucially employed.

1.1.
Model description

The dynamics of a large number of electrons is described by various approximation models depending on the physical scale under consideration. At the microscopic level (atoms, molecules, nanostructures), the Hartree-Fock system provides fairly accurate predictions
[
20
,
14
,
16
,

On the other hand, at the larger mesoscopic level (gases), microscopic models are reduced to kinetic ones such as Vlasov, Boltzmann, Fokker-Planck type equations, and they are employed for computational efficiency. In particular, the Vlasov-Poisson system is rigorously derived as a semi-classical limit from the Hartree-Fock equation
[

,
3
,
25
,
26
,
15

.

In some physical settings, however, gaseous electrons are partially strongly confined in a certain direction
[
18
,
21
]
. In a nanowire, electrons are confined in the diameter direction of order tens of nanometers. Two-dimensional electron gases (2DEGs) are formed at the heterojunction between two semiconductors. In such nanostructures, the kinetic transport and the quantum effects must be taken in account at the same time, because quantized energy levels appear in the confined direction while electrons are transported in the unconfined direction.

In this article, we are concerned with electron gases in a very thin bounded domain, i.e., 2DEGs, whose thickness is comparable to the electron de Broglie length. 2DEGs are constructed in a modulation-doped field-effect transistor (MODFET), a high-electron-mobility-transistor (HEMT) or a graphene where electrons have extremely high mobility
[

1.1
) below). As a consequence, a quantum-kinetic hybrid model, namely a subband model, is derived (see
[

]
).

1.2.
Statement of the main result

The goal of this paper is to construct a large class of stationary states to the Vlasov-Poisson-Schrödinger system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) by minimizing generalized free energies under a mass constraint. For a proper formation of the variational problem, we introduce the notion of an admissible pair.

Definition 1.1
.

(i)

(Kinetic admissible class
\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}
) The kinetic admissible class
\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}
is the collection of kinetic distribution sequences
\mathbf{f}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))
such that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and all
(y,v)\in\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

0\leq f_{j}(y,v)\leq 1\quad\textup{(Pauli exclusion principle)}.

(ii)

(Quantum admissible class
\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
)
The quantum admissible class
\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
is defined as the collection of quantum state sequences
\boldsymbol{\chi}
such that for all
y\in\omega
,
\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1)
and

\langle\chi_{j}(y,\cdot),\chi_{k}(y,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\delta_{jk}\textup{ for all }j,k\in\mathbb{N}\quad\textup{(partial orthonormality)}

(iii)

(Admissible pair) A pair
(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
is called admissible.

Assumption 1.2

(Assumptions on the external potential and Casimir functionals)
.

(i)

V_{\textup{ext}}\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
and
V_{\textup{ext}}\geq 0
.

(ii)

\beta:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
is strictly convex and differentiable, and
\beta(0)=0
.

Now, we fix
M>0
and
T\geq 0
, and we consider the free energy minimization problem under a mass-constraint,

\boxed{\quad\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)=\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M;T,\beta):=\inf\Big\{\mathcal{F}_{T,\beta}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}):(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})\textup{ is admissible and }\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})=M\Big\}.\quad}

(i)
In the literature, the generalized free energy minimization is considered to investigate thermal effects on quantum mixed states

Theorem 1.4

(Minimization of the free energy)

(1)

(Existence) The variational problem

(3)

(Self-consistent equation) The pair

1.3.
Idea of the proof

Our main results consist of construction of free energy minimizers for the subband model, and their uniqueness and stability properties. Here, we describe the main difficulties coming from the hybrid nature, and present the outline of the proof.

First, we note that the variational
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)=\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M;T,\beta)
has a minimizing sequence
\{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
each of which has finite free energy. Indeed,
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)\geq 0
, and an upper bound for
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)
is obtained by a smooth single-band pair
(\tilde{\mathbf{f}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}})
such that
\|\tilde{f}_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}=M
but
\tilde{f}_{j}\equiv 0
for all
j\geq 2
. Thus, one may attempt to construct a minimizer as a (weak subsequential) limit of a minimizing sequence. Then, one would however encounter several non-trivial obstacles described below.

Remark 1.9

(Difficulties in the variational problem
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)
)
.

(i)

(Lack of compactness with respect to the
j
-index) All
j
-summed quantities in the mass and the free energy are invariant under translation in
j
. Hence, the dichotomy and the vanishing (in
j
) scenarios cannot be eliminated in general. This issue may be avoided restricting the number of non-trivial subbands
3
3

3

For
J\in\mathbb{N}
, the admissible class is further restricted to kinetic distributions with
f_{j}\equiv 0
for all
j\geq J+1
, but its minimizer (even if it exists) might be unstable under general perturbations with more non-zero subbands.

(ii)

(Compactness of the quantum state part) More significant challenges arise in the quantum states
\{\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. At first glance, in the free energy (see (
1.9
)), the term

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}f_{j}^{(n)}(y,v)dydv

seems the only useful term for compactness of
\{\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. However, it cannot be directly employed due to the following two reasons.

(a)

A uniform bound on
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
is difficult to obtain, because the weight function
f_{j}^{(n)}(y,v)
also depends on
n
and its uniform lower bound is not expected.

(b)

If
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
is shown to be bounded uniformly in
n
, for each
y\in\omega
, the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem provides convergence of a subsequence
\{\chi_{j_{k;y}}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, where the choice of a sub-index
\{j_{k;y}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
depends on
y\in\omega
. Thus, as functions of
y
, a convergent subsequence of
\{\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
cannot be chosen. This is due to the quantum-kinetic hybrid nature of the problem where no good derivative norm bound is available in the unconfined
y
-variable direction.

We solve the above problems by refining minimizing sequences in three steps.

(Step 1)

The mass and the free energy are invariant under the rearrangements in Section
3.1
. Thus, rearranging each
(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})
with non-decreasing quantum kinetic energies, we can derive a uniform bound on the weighted summation norm for
\mathbf{f}^{(n)}
(see Proposition
3.1
), and it overcomes the obstacle
(i)
.

(Step 2)

We consider the partial free energy minimization “fixing
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}
from Step 1.” By the concentration-compactness principle with the uniform weighted summation norm bound in Step 1, we construct a minimizer
\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{(n)}
solving a self-consistent equation. Then, we refine the sequence replacing
\mathbf{f}^{(n)}
by
\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{(n)}
. Consequently, we deduce from the self-consistent equation that the refined minimizing sequence has only finitely many non-trivial subbands and kinetic distributions are non-increasing in
j
(see Proposition
4.1
).

(Step 3)

We consider another partial free energy minimization “fixing
\mathbf{f}^{(n)}
from Step 2.” Here, we do not employ the concentration-compactness principle. Instead, we take the unique solution
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{(n)}
to the Schrödinger-Poisson equation (
1.6
)-(
1.7
) with
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{f}^{(n)}
, constructed in Ben Abdallah-Méhats
[
7
]
, which exists because
\{f_{j}^{(n)}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is non-increasing in
j
. Then, using coercivity of the free energy functionals (Lemma
5.3
), we show that
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{(n)}
is a desired minimizer. Moreover, replacing
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}
by
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{(n)}
, a minimizing sequence is refined so that each
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}
solves the Schrödinger equation (
1.6
) (see Proposition
5.1
).

By Step 1-3, a minimizing sequence
(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})
is refined so that it has only finitely many non-trivial subbands, distributions in
\mathbf{f}^{(n)}
is non-increasing, and
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}
are eigenfunctions of the quasi-static 1D Schrödinger operator. Then, it follows that the nonlinear potentials,
U^{(n)}=U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}+V_{\textup{ext}}
’s, are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous with respect to
x\in\Omega
and
n\geq 1
, and so are eigenfunctions
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}=\{\chi_{j}^{(n)}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
to
-\frac{\partial_{z}^{2}}{2}+U^{(n)}
. Therefore, finally, we use the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem to take a sub-sequential limit of the refined minimizing sequence.

For uniqueness and stability, the coercivity lemma for the free energy (Lemma
5.3
) is also crucially employed together with the structure of the minimizer from the self-consistent equation.

Basic inequalities for total densities

To begin with, we present elementary inequalities for total densities (see (

Lemma 2.1

(Kinetic interpolation inequality)
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty]
. Then, for an admissible pair
(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})}\|_{L^{\frac{5s-3}{3s-1}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};L^{s}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}^{\frac{2s}{5s-3}}\big\||v|^{2}\mathbf{f}\big\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}^{\frac{2(s-1)}{5s-3}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\cdot\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\big\|\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}f_{j}\big\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\bigg\}^{\frac{s-1}{5s-3}}.

Proof.

The proof can be reduced to the single subband case
f_{j}\equiv 0
for
j\geq 2
, because the inequality (
2.1
) simply follows from the single subband inequality to the right hand side of
\|\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})}\|_{L^{\frac{5s-3}{3s-1}}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\rho_{f_{j}}|\chi_{j}|^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{5s-3}{3s-1}}(\Omega)}
with the Hölder inequality in the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

In the single subband case, applying the 1D Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|\chi\|_{L^{\frac{2(5s-3)}{3s-1}}(0,1)}\lesssim\|\chi\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{2(2s-1)}{5s-3}}\|\partial_{z}\chi\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{s-1}{5s-3}}

with
\|\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=1
and the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho_{f_{1}}|\chi_{1}|^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{5s-3}{3s-1}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\rho_{f_{1}}\|\chi_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{2(5s-3)}{3s-1}}(0,1)}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{5s-3}{3s-1}}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\rho_{f_{1}}^{\frac{2(2s-1)}{5s-3}}\rho_{f_{1}}^{\frac{s-1}{5s-3}}\|\partial_{z}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{2(s-1)}{5s-3}}\big\|_{L^{\frac{5s-3}{3s-1}}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho_{f_{1}}\|_{L^{\frac{2s-1}{s}}(\omega)}^{\frac{2(2s-1)}{5s-3}}\big\|\rho_{f_{1}}\|\partial_{z}\chi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\big\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}^{\frac{s-1}{5s-3}}.

Finally, applying the well-known interpolation inequality

\|\rho_{f}\|_{L^{\frac{2s-1}{s}}(\omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{s}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\frac{s}{2s-1}}\big\||v|^{2}f\big\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\frac{s-1}{2s-1}},

(2.2)

we complete the proof.
∎

For a general admissible pair
(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})
, the inequality (
2.1
) is not good enough to control its total density function, because
\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};L^{s}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}
is not a priori-ly bounded. Nevertheless, we will be concerned only with admissible pairs uniformly bounded in a weighted norm (see Proposition
3.1
). For them, we have a good bound.

Lemma 2.2

(Upgraded total density estimate)
.

Let
T\geq 0
. For
(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
, we have

\|\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})}\|_{L^{\frac{5s-3}{3s-1}}(\Omega)}\lesssim\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{5s-3}}\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})^{\frac{3(s-1)}{5s-3}}\quad\textup{for any }s\in[1,3).

Proof.

It is enough to show that all factors on the right hand sides of (
2.1
) are bounded by
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})
and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}
. Indeed, the second and the last factors are obviously bounded by
\sim\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})
. Hence, we only consider
\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};L^{s}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}
. Note that since
0\leq f_{j}\leq 1
, we have
\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};L^{s}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{s}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1/s}
. Hence, by the Hölder inequality in
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}
with
\frac{2}{s}\cdot\frac{s}{s-1}>1
, we obtain

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};L^{s}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-\frac{2}{s}}\Big(j^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\lesssim\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{s}}.

(2.3)

Thus, combining these, we prove the result.
∎

3.
First refinement of a minimizing sequence: improved summability

In this section, we rearrange a minimizing sequence for better summability.

Proposition 3.1

(Refined minimizing sequence with a better summability)
.

For
M>0
and
T\geq 0
, the variational problem
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)
has a minimizing sequence

\{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}

where
\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
is the subclass of quantum admissible states having non-decreasing kinetic energies, i.e., for each

\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
is non-decreasing in
j\geq 1
. As a consequence (by Lemma
3.5
below), we have

\sup_{n\geq 1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\|f_{j}^{(n)}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right\}\lesssim\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M),

(3.1)

Remark 3.2
.

If
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
, then
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, because
-\partial_{z}^{2}
is unbounded as a quadratic form on any infinite-dimensional subspace in
H_{0}^{1}(0,1)
. Indeed, for all
j\geq 1
, a precise lower bound
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\geq\tfrac{\pi j}{\sqrt{3}}
can be obtained (see (
3.3
)).

Remark 3.3
.

The uniform weighted
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
-bound (
3.1
) resolves the lack of compactness in
j
mentioned in Remark
1.9

(i)
(see the proof of Lemma
4.2
below).

3.1.
Rearrangement of admissible pairs

We note that any permutation-valued function, with the phase space
\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, preserves the mass and the free energy. Precisely, let

\sigma=\sigma(j;y,v):\mathbb{N}\times\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}

be a function such that
\sigma=\sigma(\cdot;y,v):\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is a bijection for each
(y,v)\in\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For an admissible pair
(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})
, we define the
\sigma
-rearranged pair
(\mathbf{f}^{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\sigma})
by

\big(f_{j}^{\sigma}(y,v),\chi_{j}^{\sigma}(x)\big)=\big(f_{\sigma(j;y,v)}(y,v),\chi_{\sigma(j;y,v)}(x)\big).

(3.2)

Then, one can easily see that the rearranged pair is also admissible, and that the mass and the energy are invariant under the rearrangement, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{\sigma})=\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f})\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\sigma})=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}),

because the sums in the mass and the free energy functional
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f_{j}
,
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta(f_{j}),\cdots

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}{f_{j}}
and
\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})}
are invariant under the rearrangement.

3.2.
Improved summability by rearrangement

For Proposition
3.1
, we employ the following rearrangements.

Lemma 3.4

(Rearrangement with non-decreasing quantum kinetic energies)
.

For
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
, there exists
\sigma_{\uparrow}:\mathbb{N}\times\omega\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\sigma_{\uparrow}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
.

Proof.

For
y\in\omega
, we have
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\to\infty
,
because
-\partial_{z}^{2}
is unbounded on any infinite-dimensional subspace in
H_{0}^{1}(0,1)
. Thus, there exists a bijection
{\sigma_{\uparrow}}={\sigma_{\uparrow}}(\cdot;y):\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}^{\sigma_{\uparrow}}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
is non-decreasing in
j
and goes to infinity (see (
3.2
) for the definition of
\chi_{j}^{\sigma_{\uparrow}}
).
∎

If a pair is rearranged by Lemma
3.4
, then it enjoys a better summability in
j
.

Lemma 3.5

(Weighted
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
-bound)
.

If
(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
, then it holds that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\omega}\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\rho_{f_{j}}(y)dy\leq\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}).

Proof.

It suffices to show that for all
j\geq 1
and all
y\in\omega
,

\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\geq\tfrac{\pi j}{\sqrt{3}},

(3.3)

because applying (
3.3
) to the free energy (
1.9
), the lemma follows. Indeed, we assume that there exist
\tilde{y}\in\omega
and
\tilde{j}\geq 1
such that
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{\tilde{j}}(\tilde{y},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}<\frac{\pi\tilde{j}}{\sqrt{3}}
. Then, since
\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(\tilde{y},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
does not decrease, we have
\sum_{j=1}^{\tilde{j}}\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}(\tilde{y},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}<\frac{\pi^{2}(\tilde{j})^{3}}{3}
. On the other hand, the min-max principle
[
24
, Theorem 12.1]
implies

\sum_{j=1}^{\tilde{j}}\big\langle(-\partial_{z}^{2})\chi_{j}(\tilde{y},\cdot),\chi_{j}(\tilde{y},\cdot)\big\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\tilde{j}}(\pi j)^{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}\tilde{j}(\tilde{j}+1)(2\tilde{j}+1)}{6},

which deduces a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1

4.
Second refinement of a minimizing sequence: finite non-increasing band structure

In the previous section, a minimizing sequence
\{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is refined so that the kinetic distributions
\{\mathbf{f}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
has a weak subsequential limit, while the difficulties mentioned in Remark
1.9

(ii)
have not been overcome for the quantum part
\{\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
.

In this section, fixing
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
, we consider the “partial” variational problem

\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)=\inf\Big\{\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\mathbf{f})=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}):\ \mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}\textup{ and }\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f})=M\Big\}.

(4.1)

Then, we further refine the minimizing sequence in Proposition
3.1
by replacing
\mathbf{f}^{(n)}
by a minimizer for the problem
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)};\textup{min}}(M)
. We assert that this secondly refined minimizing sequence has only finitely many non-trivial subbands with preferable monotonicity.

Proposition 4.1

(Refined minimizing sequence with finite subbands)
.

For
M>0
and
T\geq 0
, the variational problem
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)
has a minimizing sequence

\{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}},

where
\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}
is the subclass of non-increasing kinetic admissible distributions, i.e., for a.e.
(y,v)\in\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
f_{j}(y,v)
is non-increasing in
j\geq 1
. Moreover, it satisfies the following.

(i)

(Finite subband structure) There exists
J\geq 1
, independent of
n\geq 1
, such that
f_{j}^{(n)}\equiv 0
for all
j\geq J+1
.

(ii)

(Bounded densities)
\rho_{f_{j}^{(n)}}(y)
are bounded uniformly in
y\in\omega
and
j,n\geq 1
.

4.1.
Free energy minimization for fixed quantum states

First, we show existence of a minimizer for the simpler partial problem.

Lemma 4.2

(Existence of a minimizer for the partial variational problem
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)
)
.

For
T\geq 0
,
M>0
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
, the problem
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)
has a unique minimizer
\mathbf{f}^{*}
.

For the proof, we recall that the weak formulation of the Poisson equation (
1.7
) is stated as

\int_{\Omega}\nabla U\cdot\nabla\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}\rho\varphi dx\quad\textup{for all }\varphi\in H_{\omega}^{1}(\Omega),

(4.2)

where

H_{\omega}^{1}(\Omega):=\Big\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):u|_{\partial\omega\times(0,1)}=0\Big\}.

By the the Lax-Milgram theorem and the Poincaré inequality, a density
\rho\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)
uniquely determines a potential
U_{\rho}\in H_{\omega}^{1}(\Omega)
solving the equation (
4.2
).
Thus, we have the following.

Lemma 4.3

(Potential function)
.

The density-to-potential map
\rho\mapsto U_{\rho}:L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)\to H_{\omega}^{1}(\Omega)
is linear and bounded.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

It suffices to show existence, because the convexity of the functional
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\mathbf{f})
implies uniqueness. Let
\{\mathbf{f}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a minimizing sequence for
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)
. Then, passing to a subsequence, we may assume that for each
j
,
{f}_{j}^{(n)}\rightharpoonup f_{j}^{*}
in
L^{r}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
1<r<\infty
so that
\mathbf{f}^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}
. We will that
\mathbf{f}^{*}
is a minimizer for
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)
.

Note that by
[
17
, Corollary 3.9]
and the positive terms in the series, the following quantities are reduced in the weak limit;

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\||v|^{2}{f}_{j}^{(n)}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\||v|^{2}{f}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}_{\beta}(\mathbf{f}^{(n)})\geq\mathcal{C}_{\beta}(\mathbf{f}^{*})

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle(-\tfrac{\partial_{z}^{2}}{2}+V_{\textup{ext}})\chi_{j},\chi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}{f}_{j}^{(n)}dydv

(4.3)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle(-\tfrac{\partial_{z}^{2}}{2}+V_{\textup{ext}})\chi_{j},\chi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}{f}_{j}^{*}dydv.

In addition, by the uniform weighted norm bound (Lemma
3.5
), we have

\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)\gtrsim\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\|f_{j}^{(n)}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\|f_{j}^{*}\|_{L^{1}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(4.4)

Thus, except the nonlinear potential energy, all terms in the free energy are clearly reduced in the weak limit (see (
1.3
) and (
1.9
)). For the nonlinear potential energy, we claim that as
n\to\infty
,

\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi})}\rightharpoonup\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi})}\textup{ in }L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega).

(4.5)

Indeed, by the density estimate (Lemma
2.2
), it suffices to show that for
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi})}-\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi})})gdy=0.

(4.6)

For small
\epsilon>0
, by the weighted norm bound (Lemma
3.5
), taking large
J_{\epsilon}\geq 1
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi})}-\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi})})gdx=\sum_{j=1}^{J_{\epsilon}}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{f_{j}^{(n)}}-\rho_{f_{j}^{*}})|\chi_{j}|^{2}gdx+O(\epsilon).

On the other hand, we have

\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{f_{j}^{(n)}}-\rho_{f_{j}^{*}})|\chi_{j}|^{2}gdx=\int_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}(f_{j}^{(n)}-f_{j}^{*})\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\leq R}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\chi_{j}|^{2}gdz\right)dx+O(R^{-2})\to 0

taking
n\to\infty
and then
R\to\infty
. Hence, the claim (
4.5
) is proved. Now, we observe from Lemma
4.3
that the functional
\rho\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla U_{\rho}|^{2}dx
is lower semi-continuous on
L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
in the strong topology. Thus, by the weak convergence (
4.5
) and the convexity of the map
\rho\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla U_{\rho}|^{2}dx
, we conclude that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla U_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\|\nabla U_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

holds (see
[
17
, Corollary 3.9]
). Therefore, we conclude that

\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi})\geq\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}).

It remains to show that
\mathbf{f}^{*}=\{f_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is admissible for the problem
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)
. Indeed, for any small
\epsilon>0
, by Lemma
3.5
and (
4.4
), there exist large
J_{\epsilon},R_{\epsilon}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J_{\epsilon}}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\leq R_{\epsilon}}{f}_{j}^{(n)}dydv+O(\epsilon),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J_{\epsilon}}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\leq R_{\epsilon}}{f}_{j}^{*}dydv+O(\epsilon).

Hence, taking
n\to\infty
, it follows from that

M=\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{(n)})\to\sum_{j=1}^{J_{\epsilon}}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\leq R_{\epsilon}}{f}_{j}^{*}dydv+O(\epsilon)=\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{*})+O(\epsilon).

This proves that
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{*})=M
, since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary.
∎

Next, we derive the self-consistent equation for the minimizer
\mathbf{f}^{*}
obtained in Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.4

(Self-consistent equation for the partial minimization problem
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)
)
.

For
T\geq 0
,
M>0
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
, let
\mathbf{f}^{*}
be a minimizer for the variational problem
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)
. Then, for each
j\geq 1
,
f_{j}^{*}
is a solution to the self-consistent equation

f_{j}^{*}(y,v)=\tilde{\beta}\left(\mu-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-h_{j}(y)\right)

(4.7)

for some
\mu\in(0,\tfrac{4}{M}(\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi};\textup{min}}(M)+T\beta^{\prime}(1)M)]
, where

h_{j}(y):=\left\langle\left(-\frac{\partial_{z}^{2}}{2}+(U_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi})}+V_{\textup{ext}})(y,\cdot)\right)\chi_{j}(y,\cdot),\chi_{j}(y,\cdot)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

Proof.

We prove the lemma following the argument in Dolbeault-Felmer-Lewin
[
19
, Lemma 3.6]
. For convenience, we mostly deal with the zero and the positive temperature cases at the same time, because the former case follows simply taking
T=0
in the proof.

Step 1
(Reduction to the auxiliary problem)
Let
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}^{\uparrow}
. For any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}
with
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f})=M
and
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{f})<\infty
(see (
4.8
) below), its free energy is expanded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\mathbf{f})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\mathbf{f}^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+h_{j}+T\beta^{\prime}(f_{j}^{*})\right)(f_{j}-f^{*}_{j})dydv

\displaystyle+T\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\beta(f_{j})-\beta(f_{j}^{*})-\beta^{\prime}(f_{j}^{*})(f_{j}-f_{j}^{*})\right)dydv+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi})}}|^{2}dx,

where the series can be rearranged by Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
3.5
and the assumptions on
\beta
(see Assumption
1.2
(ii)).
Since the last two terms on the right hand side are superlinear in
(f_{j}-f_{j}^{*})
, the second linear term must be non-negative,

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+h_{j}+T\beta^{\prime}(f_{j}^{*})\right)(f_{j}-f^{*}_{j})dydv\geq 0.

Consequently, by the convexity of
\beta
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+h_{j}+T\beta^{\prime}(f_{j}^{*})\right)(f_{j}-f^{*}_{j})dydv

k=1,2,\cdots,J
. Hence, by density argument, we obtain that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\langle\tilde{H}\tilde{\chi}_{j}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}},\tilde{\chi}_{j}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}-\lambda_{j}^{*}\right)\tilde{f}_{j}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}dydv\geq 0.

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\mathbf{f}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}})-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(\tilde{U}-U^{*})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+\lambda_{j}^{*}+\beta^{\prime}\big(f_{j}\big)\right)(\tilde{f}_{j}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}-f_{j})dydv

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\beta(\tilde{f}_{j}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}})-\beta(f_{j})-\beta^{\prime}(f_{j})(\tilde{f}_{j}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}-f_{j})\right)dydv.

Therefore, the lemma follows from the convexity of
\beta
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Fix
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}
having finitely many bands and let
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}=\boldsymbol{\chi}[U^{*}+V_{\textup{ext}}]
. By Lemma
5.3
, for
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\chi})-\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})}}-U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Thus, it follows that
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
is a minimizer for the variational problem
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{f},\min}
. Moreover, if
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*}
is a minimizer for
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{f},\min}
, then
U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}}=U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*})}}
because of the Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial\omega\times(0,1)
(see (
1.7
)).
∎

6.
Construction of a free energy minimizer, and its uniqueness and stability: Proof of the main results (Theorem
1.4
and
1.7
)

6.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Fix
T\geq 0
and
M>0
, and let
\{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a minimizing sequence for the full variational problem
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)=\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M;T,\beta)
in Proposition
4.1
. For each
n
, we replace
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}
by the minimizer of the partial problem
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\min}
(see Proposition
5.1
). In this way, we construct a minimizing sequence
\{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
such that

(i)

There exists
J\geq 1
, independent of
n\geq 1
, such that
f_{j}^{(n)}\equiv 0
for all
j\geq J+1
;

(ii)

\rho_{f_{j}^{(n)}}(y)
are bounded uniformly in
y\in\omega
and
j,n\geq 1
;

(iii)

\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}=\boldsymbol{\chi}[U^{(n)}]
(see the definition (
5.3
)), where
U^{(n)}:=U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}+V_{\textup{ext}}
.

In a sequel, we show that the refined quantum states
\{\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are equicontinuous on
\Omega
. Then, taking the (weak) limit of the minimizing sequence, we prove that the limit is indeed a minimizer for the full variational problem.

6.1.1.
Equicontinuity of quantum states

We claim that for each
\chi_{j}^{(n)}
satisfies

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\chi_{j}^{(n)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega;H_{0}^{1}(0,1))\cap C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)}\lesssim j,

(6.1)

where the implicit constant is independent of
n\geq 1
and

\|u\|_{C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{C(\Omega)}+\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega,\ x\neq x^{\prime}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

The proof of (
6.1
) relies on the well-known fact for the 1D Schrödinger operator discussed in Section
5.1
; the eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenfunctions are stable under potential perturbations (see
[
7
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
30
, Chapter 2]
).

Lemma 6.1

(Stability for 1D Schrödinger operator)
.

If
U,V\in L^{2}(0,1)
, then there exists
C>0
, independent of
U
,
V
and
j
, such that

|\lambda_{j}[U]-\lambda_{j}[V]|+\|\chi_{j}[U]-\chi_{j}[V]\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq Ce^{C(\|U\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|V\|_{L^{2}(0,1)})}\|U-V\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}.

We recall from Lemma
2.1
that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}<\infty.

On the other hand,
U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}
satisfies the Neumann boundary condition on
\omega\times\{0,1\}
, so by the even reflection, we may assume that

U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}\in H^{1}(\omega\times(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}))
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}&=\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}&&\textup{ in }\omega\times(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}),\\
U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}&=0&&\textup{ on }\partial\omega\times(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}).\end{aligned}\right.

Thus, applying elliptic estimates (see
[
22
, Theorem 9.13]
) and the Sobolev embedding, we obtain

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty.

By Lemma
6.1
with
U^{(n)}=U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}+V_{\textup{ext}}
and
\lambda_{j}[0]=(\pi j)^{2}
(see (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) for the notations), we have

\big|\lambda_{j}[U^{(n)}(y,\cdot)]-(\pi j)^{2}\big|\leq Ce^{C\|U^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}}\|U^{(n)}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}\lesssim 1.

Then, by the Sobolev embedding and elliptic estimates (see
[
22
, Theorem 9.13]
), we obtain that

\|\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{2}\lesssim\|\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\leq 1+\lambda_{j}[U^{(n)}(y,\cdot)]\lesssim j^{2}

for all
y\in\omega
. Hence, it follows that

\|U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}\lesssim\|\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\rho_{f_{j}^{(n)}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\|\chi_{j}^{(n)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq J^{3}.

since
f_{j}^{(n)}\equiv 0
for all
j\geq J+1
and
\rho_{f_{j}^{(n)}}(y)
’s are bounded uniformly in
y\in\omega
. Consequently, by Lemma
6.1
again, we prove that

|{\chi}_{j}^{(n)}(y,z)-{\chi}_{j}^{(n)}(y^{\prime},z)|\lesssim\|U^{(n)}(y,\cdot)-U^{(n)}(y^{\prime},\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}\lesssim_{J}|y-y^{\prime}|,

where we used the regularity assumption
V_{\textup{ext}}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, while by the fundamental theorem of calculus,

\displaystyle|{\chi}_{j}^{(n)}(y,z)-{\chi}_{j}^{(n)}(y,z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{z^{\prime}}^{z}(\partial_{s}\chi_{j}^{(n)})(y,s)ds\right|\leq\|\partial_{z}\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim j|z-z^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining the two inequalities, we conclude that
|{\chi}_{j}^{(n)}(x)-{\chi}_{j}^{(n)}(x^{\prime})|\lesssim j|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

6.1.2.
Existence of a minimizer

For the kinetic distribution part
\{\mathbf{f}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}
, taking the weak subsequential limit as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we have
f_{j}^{(n)}\rightharpoonup f_{j}^{*}
in
L^{r}(\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
1<r<\infty
so that
\mathbf{f}^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}
.
For the quantum part
\{\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, by the equicontinuity (
6.1
) and the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, we deduce that up to a subsequence,
\|\chi_{j}^{(n)}-\chi_{j}^{*}\|_{C(\Omega)}\to 0
as well
\chi_{j}^{(n)}(y,\cdot)\rightharpoonup\chi_{j}^{*}(y,\cdot)
in
H^{1}(0,1)
as
n\to\infty
as for each
y\in\omega
.

We claim that
(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})
is a minimizer for the problem
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)
. Indeed, we have

\delta_{jk}=\langle\chi_{j}^{(n)},\chi_{k}^{(n)}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}\to\langle\chi_{j}^{*},\chi_{k}^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

Instead of (
4.3
), by a diagonal argument, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle(-\tfrac{\partial_{z}^{2}}{2}+V_{\textup{ext}})\chi_{j}^{(n)},\chi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}{f}_{j}^{(n)}dydv

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle(-\tfrac{\partial_{z}^{2}}{2}+V_{\textup{ext}})\chi_{j}^{*},\chi_{j}^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}{f}_{j}^{*}dydv.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}-\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\rho_{f_{j}^{(n)}}\big(|\chi_{j}^{(n)}|^{2}-|\chi_{j}^{*}|^{2}\big)+\big(\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}-\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}\big)\rightharpoonup 0\textup{ in }L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega),

because
\|\chi_{j}^{(n)}-\chi_{j}^{*}\|_{C(\Omega)}\to 0
and
\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}\rightharpoonup\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}
in
L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)
(by (
4.5
)). Consequently, it follows that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})}}|^{2}dx\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}}|^{2}dx.

Hence, repeating the proof of Lemma
4.2
, one can show that
(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})
is admissible,
M=\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{(n)})\to\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{*})
and
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{(n)},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(n)})\geq\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})
. Therefore,
(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
is a free energy minimizer.

6.1.3.
Self-consistent equation (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) and uniqueness

Let
(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
be a free energy minimizer for
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{min}}(M)
. To derive the eigenvalue equation (
1.12
), we note that
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
is the unique minimizer for the partial variational problem for fixed
\mathbf{f}^{*}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{f}^{*};\textup{min}}
. Hence, by Proposition
5.1
,
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
satisfies the Schrödinger equation (
1.12
). On the other hand,
\mathbf{f}^{*}
is a minimizer for another partial variational problem for fixed
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})=\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*};\textup{min}}(M)
. Thus, by Lemma
4.4
, we deduce the self-consistent equation (
1.11
).

For uniqueness, we assume that there is another minimizer
(\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*})
with
U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}}\not\equiv U_{\rho_{(\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*})}}
. Here, we may assume that
(\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
because the free energy and the total density
\rho_{(\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\chi})}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\rho_{f_{j}}(y)|\chi_{j}(x)|^{2}
are invariant under the rearrangement in
j
-summed quantities.
Then, by Lemma
5.3
and the estimate in (
4.9
), we have

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*})-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(U_{\rho_{(\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*})}}-U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+\lambda_{j}^{*}+T\beta^{\prime}\big(f_{j}^{*}\big)\right)(\tilde{f}^{*}_{j}-f^{*}_{j})dydv

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(U_{\rho_{(\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{*})}}-U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

which deduces a contradiction.

6.1.4.
Structure

It is known from the spectral theory
[
7
,
9
,
30

that the linear operator

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

Note that
(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})
is a weak solution to the time-dependent Vlasov-Schrödinger-Poisson system, because
\mathbf{f}^{*}
is a function of the microscopic energy
\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-\lambda_{j}(y)
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}=\boldsymbol{\chi}[U_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}+V_{\textup{ext}}]
. For stability, we observe from the assumptions on the initial data and the conservation laws, and the invariant property under the rearrangement that

\displaystyle\left(1+\mu\right)\delta

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}_{0},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{0})-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})-\mu\big(\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}_{0})-M\big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}(t),\boldsymbol{\chi}(t))-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})-\mu\big(\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}(t))-\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{*})\big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}(t),\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}(t))-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})-\mu\big(\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}(t))-\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{f}^{*})\big),

where
(\mathbf{f}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}(t),\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}(t))\in\mathcal{A}_{\textup{c.m.}}^{\downarrow}\times\mathcal{A}_{\textup{q.m.}}
(see Lemma
3.6
for the definition of
\sigma_{\downarrow}
).
Hence, it follows from Lemma
5.3
and the estimate in (
4.9
) that

\displaystyle\left(1+\mu\right)\delta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}(t),\boldsymbol{\chi}(t))}}-U_{\rho_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+\lambda_{j}^{*}+T\beta^{\prime}\big(f_{j}^{*}\big)-\mu\right)(f_{j}^{\sigma_{\downarrow}}(t)-f_{j}^{*})dydv

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(U_{(\mathbf{f}(t),\boldsymbol{\chi}(t))}-U_{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Therefore, taking
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2(1+\mu)}
, we prove the theorem.

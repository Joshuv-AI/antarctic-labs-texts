Abstract
We define canonical lifts of vector fields to the multisymplectic multimomentum bundles of De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian (first-order) field theories and to the appropriate premultisymplectic embedded constraint submanifolds on which singular field theories are studied. These new canonical lifts are used to study the so-called natural Noether symmetries present in both regular and singular Hamiltonian field theories along with their associated conserved quantities obtained from Noether’s theorem. The Klein–Gordon field , the Polyakov bosonic string , and Einstein–Cartan gravity in 3 + 1 3+1 dimensions are analyzed in depth as applications of these concepts; as a peripheral result obtained in the analysis of the bosonic string, we provide a new geometrical interpretation of the well-known Virasoro constraint .

De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian field theories, Covariant phase spaces, Constraint analysis, Canonical lifts, Symmetries, Noether Theorem, Bundles of forms, Multisymplectic forms.

Introduction

Over the years, the most important symmetries exhibited by classical field theories have arguably been
Noether symmetries
.
The vast majority of Noether symmetries in physical field theories are of a specific kind, often referred to in the literature as
natural symmetries
, generated by Lie group actions which are
canonical lifts
from a configuration manifold of the field theory under investigation to some phase space on which the field theory is to be studied.
We wish to discuss such symmetries in this work in a new geometric context, relevant to the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian treatment of field theories
[
11
,
46
]
.

The De Donder–Weyl formulation of classical field theories has been proven to be equivalent to the well-known Lagrangian formulation.
In fact, one of the main characteristics of the De Donder–Weyl formalism is that the equations of motion are given as two sets of first-order partial differential equations, known as the Hamilton–De Donder–Weyl equations, which are equivalent to the Euler–Lagrange equations while maintaining manifest Lorentz covariance for Lorentz invariant field theories.
The relevant Legendre map arises from constructing the
canonical multimomenta
which are obtained from the variation of the Lagrangian function with respect to spacetime derivatives of the fields (sometimes called
multivelocities
),
as opposed to varying with respect to only time derivatives of the fields.
However, the structure of field theories that are singular under this Legendre transform remained unknown for many years.

In more recent years, the Lagrangian and De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian formulations of field theories have been posed in more geometrically formal terms on
jet
and
affine dual jet
bundles respectively
[
3
,
5
,
10
,
12
,
19
,
17
,
20
,
22
,
28
,
33
,
41
,
44
]
.
This formulation begins from constructing a finite dimensional configuration bundle, usually, over space-time in which the fields of the theory are given by local sections of the bundle structure.
Then, the jet bundle on which the Lagrangian formalism takes place is constructed from the projection map of the configuration bundle and is also a fiber bundle over spacetime.
Furthermore, the so-called affine dual jet bundle, which is
also commonly referred to as the

restricted multimomentum bundle
, is where the equivalent De Donder–Weyl formulation takes place (and is also a fiber bundle over spacetime).
It should be noted that in the case of the Polyakov string, the base space of the entire aforementioned fiber bundle structure is instead the string worldsheet.
An important advantage of working on the jet and multimomentum bundles is that they are finite dimensional manifolds.
In the physics literature, the Lagrangian formulation of field theories usually takes place on the space of sections of the relevant jet bundle over spacetime, giving an infinite dimensional phase space on which the variational calculus is carried out.
Similarly, the original construction of the De Donder–Weyl formalism takes place on an infinite dimensional space of sections of the relevant multimomentum bundle.
The variational calculus is then carried out by taking functional variations of an
action functional
that is defined on these infinite dimensional spaces.

However, the variational calculus can instead be carried out on the jet and multimomentum bundles themselves, referred to in this work as the
Lagrangian
and
Hamiltonian multiphase spaces
respectively.
The geometric method for carrying out the variational calculus on the multiphase spaces involves the use of the multisymplectic or premultisymplectic structures
which these multiphase spaces are endowed with

[
3
,
20
,
22
,
28
]
.
The Hamiltonian multiphase space
inherits its (pre)multisymplectic structure
from the
extended multimomentum bundle
, a bundle of differential forms that comes equipped with a canonical exact multisymplectic structure and is a fiber bundle over the restricted multimomentum bundle on which the Hamiltonian variational calculus and geometric constraint analysis is carried out.
On the other hand, the (pre)multisymplectic form on the Lagrangian multiphase space is constructed from the Lagrangian
and is related to the canonical form on the extended multimomentum bundle by an
extended Legendre transform
.
Then,
the degeneracy of the (pre)multisymplectic forms on the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian multiphase spaces depends on the regularity of the theory under investigation, which is specified by a Lagrangian or a De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian function.
Moreover, for singular field theories, the premultisymplectic analysis on multiphase spaces provides us with a method to perform a geometric constraint analysis.
This constraint analysis was introduced in
[
12
,
13
]
and was detailed further and applied to examples of field theories in
[
24
,
25
,
29
]
.
This geometric formalism can be viewed as a multivariable generalization of the presymplectic formulation of time-dependent mechanics;
(see also
[
35
]
for an alternate proposed geometric formalism).

Additionally, the notion of canonical lifts of Lie group actions and their generating vector fields on jet manifolds
has been well understood for many years (see e.g.
[

]
).
Furthermore, symmetries (and more specifically, natural symmetries) on jet bundles have been well understood in terms of (pre)multisymplectic geometry

However, to our knowledge, the notions of canonical lifts and natural symmetries on the multimomentum bundles
of (pre)multisymplectic De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian field theories have not been rigorously studied in previous literature.

In this work, we present a formal mathematical definition of the canonical lift of a configuration bundle diffeomorphism
and of a vector field
on the configuration bundle of a field theory to the extended and restricted multimomentum bundles on which the multisymplectic De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian formulation of field theories takes place.
The notion of natural symmetries in the multisymplectic De Donder–Weyl framework thereby follows, along with a variety of properties and new theorems.
We also give a preliminary definition of canonical lifts for field theories that are singular (or, more specifically,
almost-regular

[
12
,
43
]
) in the De Donder–Weyl sense.
The De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian formulation of almost-regular field theories takes place on an embedded primary constraint submanifold of the restricted multimomentum bundle.
However, in order to arrive at the definition we give
for a canonical lift to embedded submanifolds of a group action depends on the invariance of the primary constraint submanifold under the lifted group action on the restricted multimomentum bundle.
Nevertheless, this requirement could be very restrictive as the last example
analyzed in Section
5.3
shows.

Prior to this work, the usual way to obtain the appropriate symmetries resulting from canonical lifts in the De Donder–Weyl setting was to first work out the full analysis in the Lagrangian setting, including the variational calculus and constraint analysis, and then project the constraint and symmetry structures onto the Hamiltonian setting via the Legendre transform
[
32
]
.
The results presented in this paper allow us to go directly to the De Donder–Weyl formalism for classical field theories without having to first walk through the entire Lagrangian formulation.

This paper is organized as follows:
First, in Section 2, a review of the (pre)multisymplectic De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian formalism for field theories is given; we also give a brief review on canonical lifts of vector fields from spacetime to the configuration bundle of some generic field theory to be studied.
The main results of this work are in Sections 3 and 4.
Section 3 is devoted to defining and characterizing the canonical lifts of vector fields from the configuration bundle to the
extended
and
restricted multimomentum bundles
of a De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian field theory.
We also provide the definition and characterization of the analogous canonical lifts to the
embedded constraint submanifolds of the extended and restricted multimentum bundles on which singular theories are studied
[
12
,
13
]
.
Next, in Section 4 we study symmetries for regular and singular Hamiltonian field theories,
paying special attention to those symmetries generated by vector fields
that are canonical lifts (i.e. natural symmetries);
the statement of Noether´s theorem, involving conserved quantities (multimomentum maps) is given explicitly.
Finally, in Section 5 we study some relevant field theories in theoretical physics to illustrate the new mathematical concepts introduced in this work.
The first example theory is the
Klein–Gordon field
, which is regular.
Next, we study the
Polyakov bosonic string
and
Einstein–Cartan gravity in
3+1
dimensions
, both of which are singular.
The full premultisymplectic constraint analysis is worked out for both singular theories.
Furthermore, we give the appropriate construction of canonical lifts, and the resulting natural symmetries, in both the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalisms for all field theories discussed in this paper.

In the case of the bosonic string, we find from the premultisymplectic constraint analysis that the
Virasoro constraint arises as a
compatibility constraint
.
The specific geometric relationship between our premultisymplectic constraint analysis and the more well-known canonical Dirac Hamiltonian constraint analysis (which is given elegantly for the Polyakov string in
[
6
]
) is still unknown and is left for future work.

In this section, we study the multisymplectic formulation for a real scalar field on a

The configuration manifold

is equipped with projection map
\pi:E\rightarrow M

The local coordinates on

E

(x^{\mu},\varphi)

Recall that the Minkowski metric
\eta_{\mu\nu}
has signature
(-+...+)
and the spacetime partial derivatives are also denoted as
\partial_{\mu}=\partial/\partial x^{\mu}
.

5.1.1
Lagrangian Formalism

The Lagrangian formalism
[
17
,
22
,
44
]
takes place on the first-order jet bundle
J^{1}\pi
which has natural coordinates
(x^{\mu},\varphi,\varphi_{\mu})

The Lagrangian density

\textbf{L}=\mathscr{L}(x^{\mu},\varphi,\varphi_{\mu})\text{d}^{m}x=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\mu}\varphi_{\nu}+m^{2}\varphi^{2}\right)\text{d}^{m}x\ ,

\frac{\partial^{2}\mathscr{L}}{\partial\varphi_{\mu}\partial\varphi_{\nu}}=-\eta^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{\mu\nu}\text{d}\varphi_{\nu}\wedge\left(\text{d}\varphi\wedge\text{d}^{m-1}x_{\mu}-\varphi_{\mu}\text{d}^{m}x\right)\ .

\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}
is
1

The field equations are produced by a class of multivector fields

\bar{\pi}^{1}-

{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}}=\bigwedge^{m-1}_{\mu=0}X_{\mu}=\bigwedge^{m-1}_{\mu=0}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+D_{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}+H_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi_{\nu}}\right)\ .

(36)

The field equations are then given as
\mathop{i}\nolimits({\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}})\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=0
,
from which it follows that

\displaystyle\eta^{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu}-m^{2}\varphi=0\ ,

(37a)

\displaystyle\eta^{\mu\nu}\left(D_{\mu}-\varphi_{\mu}\right)=0\ .

(37b)

Furthermore, the solution to the variational problem on
J^{1}\pi
requires that
X_{\mathscr{L}}
must be
holonomic
multivector fields
(the
sopde
condition).
By definition, this means that they have holonomic integral sections

j^{1}\phi:M\rightarrow M\times\mathds{R}:x^{\mu}\mapsto(x^{\mu},\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi)
,
so
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\varphi
.
Therefore, in the expression (
36
) must be
D_{\mu}=\varphi_{\mu}
,
and this condition shows up in the field equations (
37b
);
this is expected as the
sopde
condition always shows up in the field equations when the Lagrangian is regular.
The final field equations are obtained from (
37a
) on holonomic integral sections, giving:

\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi-m^{2}\varphi=0\ .

(38)

5.1.2
Hamiltonian Formalism

The De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian formulation of Klein–Gordon field theory is constructed on the extended multimomentum bundle
{\cal M}\pi
which has local coordinates
\left(x^{\mu},\varphi,p_{\mu},p\right)
and the restricted multimomentum bundle
J^{1}\pi^{*}

which has natural coordinates
(x^{\mu},\varphi,p_{\mu})
.
The Klein–Gordon field theory is specified by a Hamiltonian section
{\mathrm{h}}=\big(x^{\mu},\varphi,p_{\mu},p=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}\big)
so that the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian is given as

\mathscr{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}\in C^{\infty}(J^{1}\pi^{*})\ .

(39)

Furthermore, this Hamiltonian field theory is related to its Lagrangian version by the
following Legendre map:

\mathscr{FL}^{*}p^{\mu}=\frac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial\varphi_{\mu}}=-\eta^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}\ ,

which is invertible since the Hessian (
35
) is regular
(
\mathscr{FL}
is a local diffeomorphism); it follows that

\left(\mathscr{FL}^{-1}\right)^{*}\varphi_{\mu}=-\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}\ .

(40)

Furthermore, the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian can be produced by the Legendre map as,

\mathscr{H}=p^{\mu}\left(\mathscr{FL}^{-1}\right)^{*}\varphi_{\mu}-\left(\mathscr{FL}^{-1}\right)^{*}\mathscr{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}\ .

(41)

The bundle
J^{1}\pi^{*}
is equipped with the Hamilton–Cartan
m
-form
\Theta_{\mathscr{H}}
and multisymplectic
(m+1)
-form
\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}
given by

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\mu}\text{d}\varphi\wedge\text{d}^{m-1}x_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\text{d}^{m}x\ ,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\text{d}p^{\mu}\wedge\text{d}\varphi\wedge\text{d}^{m-1}x_{\mu}-\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}\text{d}p^{\mu}-m^{2}\varphi\text{d}\varphi\right)\wedge\text{d}^{m}x\ .

The field equations are again produced by a class of multivector fields
\{{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}}\}\subset\mathfrak{X}^{m}(J^{1}\pi^{*})

that are locally decomposable, integrable, and
\bar{\tau}
-transverse
which satisfy
i({\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}})\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}=0
for every
{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}}\in\{{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}}\}
.
As above, we use a representative of
\{{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}}\}
,
expressed as,

{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}}=\bigwedge^{m-1}_{\mu=0}X_{\mu}=\bigwedge^{m-1}_{\mu=0}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+D_{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}+H_{\mu}^{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\rho}}\right)\ ,

(42)

and the field equations
i({\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}})\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}=0
give

\displaystyle H_{\mu}^{\mu}+m^{2}\varphi=0\ ,

\displaystyle D_{\mu}+\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}=0\ .

Recall that the solution to the variational problem in the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian formalism requires that
\psi
are integral sections of
{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{H}}
, hence
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\varphi
and
H_{\mu}^{\rho}=\partial_{\mu}p^{\rho}
,
giving the Hamilton–De Donder–Weyl equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}p^{\mu}+m^{2}\varphi=0\ ,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\varphi+\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}=0\ .

5.1.3
Symmetries

The symmetry group of the Klein–Gordon field theory on Minkowski spacetime consists of Lorentz transformations, written as

x^{\mu}\rightarrow x^{\mu}-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\quad,\quad\varphi_{\mu}(x)\rightarrow\varphi_{\mu}(x)+\Lambda^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\varphi_{\nu}(x)\ ,

where
\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
are the infinitesimal Lorentz matrices which are antisymmetric.
It follows that the vector fields which generate the Lorentz transformations on spacetime
M
and the configuration bundle
E
are,

\xi=-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\quad,\quad\xi_{E}=-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\ ,

respectively.
The canonical lifts
X_{\xi}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi)
,
Z_{\xi}\in\text{\fr X}(\mathcal{M}\pi)
, and
Y_{\xi}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi^{*})
are given by (
28
), (
26
), and (
27
), respectively, and they are

\displaystyle X_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+\Lambda^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\varphi_{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi_{\mu}}\ ,

\displaystyle Z_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}p^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\ ,

\displaystyle Y_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}p^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\ .

Furthermore,
\mathscr{FL}_{*}X_{\xi}=Y_{\xi}
trivially
(see also Proposition
3.13
).

It is straightforward to check that

\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\xi})\textbf{L}=0
,
and hence
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\xi})\Theta_{\mathscr{L}}=0
.
Therefore,
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(Y_{\xi})\Theta_{\mathscr{H}}=0
;
that is, both
X_{\xi}
and
Y_{\xi}
are natural exact Lagrangian and Hamiltonian infinitesimal Noether symmetries, respectively.
The corresponding momentum maps are:

\displaystyle J_{\mathscr{L}}(X_{\xi})

\displaystyle=-i(X_{\xi})\Theta_{\mathscr{L}}=\Lambda^{\rho}_{\ \sigma}x^{\sigma}\eta^{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{\mu}\varphi_{\nu}\text{d}^{m-1}x_{\rho}+\varphi_{\nu}\text{d}\varphi\wedge\text{d}^{m-2}x_{\mu\rho}\right)\in\Omega^{m-1}(J^{1}\pi)\ ,

(43)

\displaystyle J_{\mathscr{H}}(Y_{\xi})

\displaystyle=-i(Y_{\xi})\Theta_{\mathscr{H}}=\Lambda^{\rho}_{\ \sigma}x^{\sigma}\left(p^{\mu}\text{d}\varphi\wedge\text{d}^{m-2}x_{\mu\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\text{d}^{m-1}x_{\rho}\right)\in\Omega^{m-1}(J^{1}\pi^{*})\ .

(44)

5.2
The Polyakov String Action

In the following section, the premultisymplectic treatment of the relativistic string on a Minkowski background spacetime is carried out starting from the famous
Polyakov action
.
The premultisymplectic constraint analysis of this theory has been carried out previously
[
14
]
, using Ehresmann connections to write the field equations intrinsically.
Here, we carry out the premultisymplectic constraint analysis using multivector fields.

5.2.1
Lagrangian Formalism

Consider the string worldsheet
\Sigma
with coordinates
\sigma^{a}

(a=0,1)
and auxiliary metric
g_{ab}
; partial worldsheet derivatives are denoted as
\partial_{a}=\partial/\partial\sigma^{a}
.
The embedding of the string worldsheet in spacetime
M
(which has coordinates
x^{\mu}
) is specified by local maps
\Sigma\rightarrow M
which are denoted as
x^{\mu}=x^{\mu}(\sigma)
.
The variational fields considered here are the embedding maps
x^{\mu}(\sigma)
and the components of the auxiliary inverse metric
g^{ab}(\sigma)
.
It follows that the configuration bundle is specified as
\pi:E\rightarrow\Sigma:(\sigma^{a},x^{\mu},g^{ab})\mapsto\sigma^{a}
, and its first-order jet bundle
J^{1}\pi
has local coordinates
(\sigma^{a},x^{\mu},g^{ab},x^{\mu}_{a},g^{ab}_{c})
.

The
Polyakov Lagrangian
function is written as

\mathscr{L}=-\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}\in C^{\infty}(J^{1}\pi)\ .

(45)

The resulting Lagrangian energy is

E_{\mathscr{L}}=-\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}\in C^{\infty}(J^{1}\pi)\ ,

(46)

and the Poincaré–Cartan forms are:

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathscr{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}x^{\nu}_{b}\text{d}x^{\mu}\wedge\text{d}^{1}\sigma_{a}+\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}\text{d}^{2}\sigma\ ,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}\left(g^{ab}\text{d}x^{\nu}_{b}\wedge\text{d}x^{\mu}+x^{\nu}_{b}\text{d}g^{ab}\wedge\text{d}x^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}g^{ab}x^{\nu}_{b}g_{dc}\text{d}g^{cd}\wedge\text{d}x^{\mu}\right)\wedge\text{d}^{1}\sigma_{a}

\displaystyle-\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}\left(x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}\text{d}g^{ab}+2g^{ab}x^{\mu}_{a}\text{d}x^{\nu}_{b}-\frac{1}{2}g^{ab}x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}g_{dc}\text{d}g^{cd}\right)\wedge\text{d}^{2}\sigma\ .

The multi-Hessian for the Polyakov Lagrangian (
45
) is given by,

\frac{\partial{{}^{2}\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{x^{\mu}_{a}\partial x^{\nu}_{b}}}=-T\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}\quad,\quad\frac{\partial{{}^{2}\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{g^{ab}_{i}\partial g^{cd}_{j}}}=\frac{\partial{{}^{2}\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{x^{\mu}_{a}\partial g^{cd}_{j}}}=0\ ,

(47)

which is clearly singular.

Now, we use a
2
-multivector field,

{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}}=\bigwedge^{1}_{a=0}X_{a}=\bigwedge^{1}_{a=0}\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\sigma^{a}}}+A^{\mu}_{a}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+G^{bc}_{a}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{bc}}}+D^{\nu}_{ab}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\nu}_{b}}}+K^{bc}_{ad}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{bc}_{d}}}\right)\in\text{\fr X}^{2}(J^{1}\pi)\ ,

(48)

to calculate
i({\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}})\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=0
, and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}(x^{\mu}_{a}-A^{\mu}_{a})\ ,\end{split}

(49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}\left(x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}-2x^{\nu}_{b}A^{\mu}_{a}-g^{cd}g_{ab}x^{\nu}_{d}A^{\mu}_{c}-\frac{1}{2}g^{cd}g_{ba}x^{\mu}_{c}x^{\nu}_{d}\right)\ ,\end{split}

(50)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\sqrt{-g\,}\Big(\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}D^{\nu}_{ab}+\eta_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}_{b}G^{ab}_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}g_{cd}x^{\nu}_{b}G^{cd}_{a}\Big)\ .\end{split}

(51)

Equations (
49
) lead to one of the
sopde
conditions,

A^{\mu}_{a}=x^{\mu}_{a}
,
and they need not be imposed.
However, the remaining
sopde
condition,
G^{bc}_{a}=g^{bc}_{a}
, must be imposed
ad hoc
.
It is evident that imposing this last
sopde
condition only affects equation (
51
).
Upon combining equations (
49
) and (
50
) and imposing the aforementioned
sopde
condition,
G^{bc}_{a}=g^{bc}_{a}
, the equations become:

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}\left(x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}-\frac{1}{2}g^{cd}g_{ba}x^{\mu}_{c}x^{\nu}_{d}\right)\ ,\end{split}

(52)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\sqrt{-g\,}\Big(\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}D^{\nu}_{ab}+\eta_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}_{b}g^{ab}_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}g_{cd}x^{\nu}_{b}g^{cd}_{a}\Big)\ .\end{split}

(53)

No
sopde
constraints arise from (
53
).
However, equation (
52
), which arises from combining equations (
49
) and (
50
), is a compatibility constraint.
Similarly to Einstein–Cartan gravity, no stability (tangency) constraints arise from demanding that the Lie derivative of the constraint function (
52
) with respect to the vector fields
X_{a}
of the multivector field
{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}}
vanish on the submanifold defined by (
52
).
It follows that the compatibility constraint submanifold is the final constraint submanifold
S_{f}\hookrightarrow J^{1}\pi^{*}
of this Lagrangian system.
The final field equations on integral sections of
{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}}
are written as,

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}\left(\partial_{a}x^{\mu}\partial_{b}x^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g^{cd}g_{ba}\partial_{c}x^{\mu}\partial_{d}x^{\nu}\right)\ ,\end{split}

(54)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\sqrt{-g\,}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}x^{\nu}+\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_{b}x^{\nu}\partial_{a}g^{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}g_{cd}\partial_{b}x^{\nu}\partial_{a}g^{cd}\right)\ .\end{split}

(55)

Equation (
55
) is the Euler–Lagrange equation for
x^{\mu}

while (
54
) is the Euler–Lagrange equation for
g^{ab}
which is well-known in the physics literature as the
Virasoro constraint
(see e.g.
[
7
]
).

It should be noted that the
Hilbert stress–energy tensor
on
J^{1}\pi
is given as

T_{ab}=-\frac{2}{T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial{\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\left(x^{\mu}_{a}x^{\nu}_{b}-\frac{1}{2}g^{cd}g_{ba}x^{\mu}_{c}x^{\nu}_{d}\right)\ .

(56)

The vanishing of this stress–energy tensor gives the compatibility constraint (
52
).
Notice that
T_{ab}
is traceless everywhere on
J^{1}\pi
and not just on the compatibility constraint as expected.
In conclusion, composing the compatibility constraint (
52
) with sections

gives the well-known Virasoro constraint and composing the

Hilbert stress–energy tensor
(

this is in accordance with the reoccurring theme that we work on the premultisymplectic fiber bundle
J^{1}\pi

while in the physics literature, field theory is carried out on the space of sections of

over the base space (which in this case is the string worldsheet
\Sigma
).

5.2.2
Hamiltonian Formalism

Since the Polyakov Lagrangian is singular, the Hamilton–De Donder–Weyl formalism takes place on a primary constraint submanifold
P_{\circ}\hookrightarrow J^{1}\pi^{*}
.
The local coordinates on
{\cal M}\pi
and
J^{1}\pi^{*}
are
(\sigma^{a},x^{\mu},p^{a}_{\mu},\pi^{c}_{ab},p)
and
(\sigma^{a},x^{\mu},p^{a}_{\mu},\pi^{c}_{ab})
respectively.
The Legendre map gives,

\mathscr{FL}^{*}p^{a}_{\mu}=\frac{\partial{\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{x^{\mu}_{a}}}=-T\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}x^{\nu}_{b}\quad,\quad\mathscr{FL}^{*}\pi^{c}_{ab}=\frac{\partial{\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{g^{ab}_{c}}}=0\ ,

(57)

for the multimomenta.
The first equation above can be inverted to solve for the multivelocities in terms of the multimomenta.
The second equation above specifies the primary constraint submanifold
P_{\circ}\hookrightarrow J^{1}\pi^{*}
.

The De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian
\mathscr{H}_{\circ}\in C^{\infty}(P_{\circ})
which satisfies
E_{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{FL}^{*}_{\circ}\mathscr{H}_{\circ}
is given as,

\mathscr{H}_{\circ}=-\frac{1}{2T\sqrt{-g}}\eta^{\mu\nu}g_{ab}p^{a}_{\mu}p^{b}_{\nu}\ ,

(58)

and the corresponding Hamiltonian section is given as

h_{\circ}=(\sigma^{a},x^{\mu},p^{a}_{\mu},\pi^{c}_{ab},p=-\mathscr{H}_{\circ})
.

The corresponding Hamilton–Cartan forms are,

\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{a}_{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}^{1}\sigma_{a}+\frac{1}{2T\sqrt{-g}}\eta^{\mu\nu}g_{ab}p^{a}_{\mu}p^{b}_{\nu}\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma\ ,

\displaystyle\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{d}p^{a}_{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}^{1}\sigma_{a}-\frac{1}{2T\sqrt{-g}}\eta^{\mu\nu}\left[2g_{ab}p^{b}_{\nu}\mathrm{d}p^{a}_{\mu}+\left(p^{a}_{\mu}p^{b}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}p^{d}_{\mu}p^{c}_{\nu}g_{cd}g^{ba}\right)\mathrm{d}g_{ab}\right]\wedge\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma\ .

Now use a
2
-multivector field,

{\mathbf{X}}^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=\bigwedge^{1}_{a=0}X_{a}=\bigwedge^{1}_{a=0}\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\sigma^{a}}}+A^{\mu}_{a}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+G^{bc}_{a}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{bc}}}+D^{b}_{a\nu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p_{\nu}^{b}}}\right)\in\text{\fr X}^{2}(P_{\circ})\ ,

(59)

to calculate
\mathop{i}\nolimits({\mathbf{X}}^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}})\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=0
; then,

0=A^{\mu}_{a}+\frac{1}{T\sqrt{-g\,}}\eta^{\mu\nu}g_{ab}p^{b}_{\nu}\quad,\quad 0=D^{a}_{a\mu}\quad,\quad 0=\frac{1}{2T\sqrt{-g}}\eta^{\mu\nu}\left(p^{a}_{\mu}p^{b}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{cd}g^{ba}p^{d}_{\mu}p^{c}_{\nu}\right)\ .

The third equation above is the Hamiltonian compatibility constraint which is
the
\mathscr{FL}
projection of the Lagrangian
compatibility constraint (
52
).
As in the Lagrangian formalism, no (tangency) constraints arise since the tangency conditions (i.e., the Lie derivatives of this compatibility constraint function with respect to the vector fields
X_{a}
) provide equations for the coefficients
D^{b}_{a\nu}
.
The third equation thereby defines the final constraint submanifold
P_{f}\hookrightarrow P_{\circ}\hookrightarrow J^{1}\pi^{*}
.
Working on integral sections of the multivector field
{\mathbf{X}}^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}
gives the Hamilton–De Donder–Weyl equations:

0=\partial_{a}x^{\mu}+\frac{1}{T\sqrt{-g\,}}\eta^{\mu\nu}g_{ab}p^{b}_{\nu}\quad,\quad 0=\partial_{a}p^{a}_{\mu}\quad,\quad 0=\frac{1}{2T\sqrt{-g}}\eta^{\mu\nu}\left(p^{a}_{\mu}p^{b}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{cd}g^{ba}p^{d}_{\mu}p^{c}_{\nu}\right)\ .

The first equation gives the expression for the multivelocities written in terms of the multimomenta; this equation is the inverse of the equation obtained from the Legendre transform.
The second equation above is equivalent to the Euler–Lagrange equation (
55
) in the Lagrangian formalism.
And finally, the third equation is the De Donder–Weyl version of the Virasoro constraint (
54
).

5.2.3
Symmetries

The Polyakov string action exhibits three different symmetries:
worldsheet diffeomorphisms, spacetime Poincaré transformations,
and
Weyl transformations
.
Each of these transformations generate exact premultisymplectomorphisms.

Worldsheet diffeomorphisms
:

The worldsheet diffeomorphisms are produced by a general change of coordinates on the string worldsheet as,

\tilde{\sigma}^{a}=\sigma^{a}+\xi^{a}\ \Longrightarrow\ \partial_{b}\tilde{\sigma}^{a}=\delta^{a}_{b}+\partial_{b}\xi^{a}\ .

(60)

The fields
x^{\mu}(\sigma)
and
g^{ab}(\sigma)
transform under the worldsheet diffeomorphisms as,

x^{\mu}\rightarrow x^{\mu}-\xi^{c}\partial_{c}x^{\mu}\quad,\quad g^{ab}\rightarrow g^{ab}-\xi^{c}\partial_{c}g^{ab}+g^{ac}\partial_{c}\xi^{b}+g^{cb}\partial_{c}\xi^{a}\ .

The worldsheet diffeomorphisms are generated by the vector field
\xi_{\Sigma}\in\text{\fr X}(\Sigma)
given by

\xi_{\Sigma}=-\xi^{a}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\sigma^{a}}}\in\text{\fr X}(\Sigma)\ ,

whose canonical lift to the configuration bundle
E
is given by

\xi_{E}=-\xi^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma^{a}}-\left(g^{ac}\partial_{c}\xi^{b}+g^{cb}\partial_{c}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(E)\ .

The canonical lift
X_{\xi}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi)
of
\xi_{E}
to
J^{1}\pi
, given by (
28
), is

X_{\xi}=-\xi^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma^{a}}-\left(g^{ac}\partial_{c}\xi^{b}+g^{cb}\partial_{c}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}+x_{b}^{\mu}\partial_{a}\xi^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{a}^{\mu}}-\left(g^{ac}_{d}\partial_{c}\xi^{b}+g^{cb}_{d}\partial_{c}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}_{d}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi)\ ,

which is tangent to the final Lagrangian constraint submanifold
S_{f}
,
as a simple calculation shows.
The canonical lifts to the multimomentum bundles are given
by (
26
) and (
27
), respectively, and they are

\displaystyle Z_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\xi^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma^{a}}-\left(g^{ac}\partial_{c}\xi^{b}+g^{cb}\partial_{c}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}+\left(p^{a}_{\mu}\partial_{b}\xi^{b}-p^{b}_{\mu}\partial_{b}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{a}_{\mu}}}

\displaystyle+\left(\pi^{c}_{ab}\partial_{d}\xi^{d}-\pi^{d}_{ab}\partial_{d}\xi^{c}+\pi^{c}_{ad}\partial_{b}\xi^{d}+\pi^{c}_{db}\partial_{a}\xi^{d}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\pi^{c}_{ab}}}+p\partial_{a}\xi^{a}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\in\text{\fr X}(\mathcal{M}\pi)\ ,

\displaystyle Y_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\xi^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma^{a}}-\left(g^{ac}\partial_{c}\xi^{b}+g^{cb}\partial_{c}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}+\left(p^{a}_{\mu}\partial_{b}\xi^{b}-p^{b}_{\mu}\partial_{b}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{a}_{\mu}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left(\pi^{c}_{ab}\partial_{d}\xi^{d}-\pi^{d}_{ab}\partial_{d}\xi^{c}+\pi^{c}_{ad}\partial_{b}\xi^{d}+\pi^{c}_{db}\partial_{a}\xi^{d}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\pi^{c}_{ab}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi^{*})\ .

This vector field
Y_{\xi}
is tangent to
P_{\circ}
, since the constraints defining
P_{\circ}
are
\pi^{c}_{ab}=0
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(Y_{\xi})\pi^{c}_{ab}=0

on
P_{\circ}
.
Thus, following Definition
3.15
,
the canonical lift to the Hamiltonian multiphase space
P_{\circ}\subset J^{1}\pi^{*}
is
given as

Y^{\circ}_{\xi}=-\xi^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma^{a}}-\left(g^{ac}\partial_{c}\xi^{b}+g^{cb}\partial_{c}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}+\left(p^{a}_{\mu}\partial_{b}\xi^{b}-p^{b}_{\mu}\partial_{b}\xi^{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{a}_{\mu}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(P_{\circ})\ ,

since
({\jmath}_{\circ})_{*}Y^{\circ}_{\xi}=Y_{\xi}|_{P_{\circ}}
.
It can be shown that
(\mathscr{FL}_{\circ})_{*}X_{\xi}=Y_{\xi}^{\circ}
is satisfied and that
Y_{\xi}^{\circ}
is also tangent to the final Hamiltonian constraint submanifold
P_{f}
.

It is straightforward to show that
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\xi})\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=0
;
then,

0=\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\xi})\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\xi})\big(\mathscr{FL}_{\circ}^{\ *}\,\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}\big)=\mathscr{FL}_{\circ}^{\ *}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits\big((\mathscr{FL}_{\circ})_{*}X_{\xi}\big)\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}\right)=\mathscr{FL}_{\circ}^{\ *}\big(\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(Y^{\circ}_{\xi})\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}\big)\ ,

and, as
\mathscr{FL}_{\circ}
is a surjective submersion, we conclude that

\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(Y^{\circ}_{\xi})\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=0
.
Hence, both
X_{\xi}
and
Y^{\circ}_{\xi}
are natural infinitesimal Noether symmetries.
The multimomentum maps associated with
X_{\xi}
and
Y^{\circ}_{\xi}
are given as,

\displaystyle J_{\mathscr{L}}(X_{\xi})

\displaystyle=-i(X_{\xi})\Theta_{\mathscr{L}}=T\sqrt{-g\,}\xi^{c}\eta_{\mu\nu}g^{ab}x^{\nu}_{b}\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{\mu}_{a}\text{d}^{1}\sigma_{c}-\epsilon_{ac}\text{d}x^{\mu}\right)\in\Omega^{1}(J^{1}\pi)\ ,

\displaystyle J^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}(Y^{\circ}_{\xi})

\displaystyle=-i(Y^{\circ}_{\xi})\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=-\epsilon_{ab}\xi^{b}p^{a}_{\mu}\text{d}x^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2T\sqrt{-g\,}}\eta_{\mu\nu}g_{ab}p^{a}_{\mu}p^{b}_{\nu}\xi^{c}\text{d}^{1}\sigma_{c}\in\Omega^{1}(P_{\circ})\ .\

Poincaré transformations
:

Next, the infinitesimal Poincaré transformations of the background spacetime are given as
x^{\mu}\rightarrow x^{\mu}+\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+{\mathrm{a}}^{\mu}
where both
\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and

are constants and the matrices

\pi

Its canonical lift (

X_{\zeta}=j^{1}\zeta_{E}=-\left(\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+{\mathrm{a}}^{\mu}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}-x^{\nu}_{a}\,\omega^{\mu}_{\nu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}_{a}}}\in\text{\fr X}(J^{1}\pi)\ .

As expected,

S_{f}
.
The canonical lifts to the multimomentum bundles, given respectively
by (
26

27

\displaystyle Z_{\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+{\mathrm{a}}^{\mu}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+p^{a}_{\nu}\,\omega^{\nu}_{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{a}_{\mu}}}\in\text{\fr X}(\mathcal{M}\pi)\ ,

\displaystyle Y_{\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+{\mathrm{a}}^{\mu}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+p^{a}_{\nu}\,\omega^{\nu}_{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{a}_{\mu}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi^{*})\ .

As
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(Y_{\zeta})\pi^{c}_{ab}=0
on
P_{\circ}
, it follows that
Y_{\zeta}
is tangent to
P_{\circ}
and, by Definition
3.15
,
the canonical lift to
P_{\circ}\subset J^{1}\pi^{*}
is

Y^{\circ}_{\zeta}=-\left(\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+{\mathrm{a}}^{\mu}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+p^{a}_{\nu}\,\omega^{\nu}_{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{a}_{\mu}}}\in\text{\fr X}(P_{\circ})\ .

As above,

(\mathscr{FL}_{\circ})_{*}X_{\zeta}=Y_{\zeta}^{\circ}
,
and
Y_{\zeta}^{\circ}
is also tangent to the final Hamiltonian constraint submanifold
P_{f}
as expected.

Again
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\zeta})\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=0
and

\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(Y^{\circ}_{\zeta})\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=0
;

X_{\zeta}
and
Y^{\circ}_{\zeta}
are therefore natural infinitesimal Noether symmetries.
The corresponding multimomentum maps are:

\displaystyle J_{\mathscr{L}}(X_{\zeta})

\displaystyle=-i(X_{\zeta})\Theta_{\mathscr{L}}=-T\sqrt{-g\,}\eta_{\mu\rho}g^{ab}x^{\rho}_{b}\left(\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+{\mathrm{a}}^{\mu}\right)\text{d}^{1}\sigma_{a}\in\Omega^{1}(J^{1}\pi)\ ,

\displaystyle J^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}(Y^{\circ}_{\zeta})

\displaystyle=-i(Y^{\circ}_{\zeta})\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=p^{a}_{\mu}\left(\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+{\mathrm{a}}^{\mu}\right)\text{d}^{1}\sigma_{a}\in\Omega^{1}(P_{\circ})\ .

Weyl transformations
:

Finally, the Weyl gauge transformations are given as:

g^{ab}\rightarrow e^{-\varphi(\sigma)}g^{ab}
.
These transformations are generated on the configuration manifold
E

\varphi_{E}\in\text{\fr X}(E)

\varphi_{E}=e^{-\varphi}g^{ab}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}\ .

The canonical lift (

\varphi_{E}

X_{\varphi}=j^{1}\varphi_{E}=e^{-\varphi}g^{ab}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}+e^{-\varphi}\left(g^{ab}_{c}-g^{ab}\partial_{c}\varphi\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}_{c}}}\in\text{\fr X}(J^{1}\pi)\ ,

S_{f}
.
Furthermore,

) and (
27
) to the multimomentum bundles are

\displaystyle Z_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{-\varphi}g^{ab}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}+e^{-\varphi}\pi^{c}_{ab}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\pi^{c}_{ab}}}-e^{-\varphi}g^{ab}\pi^{c}_{ab}\partial_{c}\varphi\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\in\text{\fr X}(\mathcal{M}\pi)\ ,

\displaystyle Y_{\varphi}

\displaystyle-e^{-\varphi}g^{ab}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{g^{ab}}}+e^{-\varphi}\pi^{c}_{ab}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\pi^{c}_{ab}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi^{*})\ .

5.3
Einstein–Cartan gravity in
3+1
dimensions

In this section, the premultisymplectic treatment of Einstein–Cartan gravity of
(3+1)
-dimensional spacetime
M
is given.
The vierbein
e^{a}_{\mu}
and spin connection
\omega^{ab}_{\mu}
are treated as independent variational fields.
The configuration manifold is the frame bundle augmented by the bundle of connections, given as

\pi:E\rightarrow M:(x^{\mu},e^{a}_{\mu},\omega^{ab}_{\mu})\mapsto x^{\mu}
.
Note that the lowercase Latin indices are in the Lie algebra of the gauge group
SO(3,1)
of local Lorentz transformations.

Alternative treatments of Einstein–Cartan gravity in the multisymplectic setting exist (see e.g.
[
45
]
).
However, such works do not carry out a complete constraint analysis; in this work, we provide the full constraint analysis.
It is also worth noting that a different geometric treatment, sometimes referred to as the
polysymplectic formalism
, exists;
for the treatment of vielbein gravity in this formalism, see e.g.
[
36
]
.

As above, the spacetime partial derivatives are denoted as
\partial_{\mu}=\partial/\partial x^{\mu}
.

5.3.1
Lagrangian Formalism

The
Einstein–Hilbert Lagrangian
is written in terms of the tetrad
e^{a}_{\mu}
and spin connection
\omega^{a}_{\mu c}
as,

\mathscr{L}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\omega^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\right)\in C^{\infty}(J^{1}\pi)\ .

The multi-Hessian given by the second derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to the multivelocities
e^{a}_{\mu\nu}
and
\omega^{ab}_{\mu\nu}
vanishes and is thereby singular.
The Lagrangian energy function
E_{\mathscr{L}}\in C^{\infty}(J^{1}\pi)
is

E_{\mathscr{L}}=-\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\ ,

and the Cartan
4
-form and
5
-form are given as,

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathscr{L}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{3}x_{\rho}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\text{d}^{4}x\right)\ ,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}

\displaystyle=-2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}\left(e^{b}_{\nu}\text{d}e^{a}_{\mu}\wedge\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{3}x_{\rho}+e^{b}_{\nu}\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\text{d}e^{a}_{\mu}\wedge\text{d}^{4}x-e^{a}_{\mu}e^{i}_{\nu}\omega^{b}_{\rho i}\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{4}x\right)\ .

Similarly as above, to compute the field equations we use
4
-multivector fields expressed locally as,

{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}}=\bigwedge^{3}_{\lambda=0}X_{\lambda}=\bigwedge^{3}_{\lambda=0}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\lambda}}+B^{a}_{\lambda\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\mu}}+C^{c}_{\lambda\rho d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{c}_{\rho d}}+D^{a}_{\lambda\sigma\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\sigma\mu}}+H^{c}_{\lambda\sigma\rho d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{c}_{\sigma\rho d}}\right)\in\text{\fr X}^{4}(J^{1}\pi)\ .

(61)

Then
i({\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}})\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=0
gives:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(C^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(B^{a}_{\rho\mu}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}\right)\ .

Upon imposing the
sopde
conditions,
B^{a}_{\rho\mu}=e^{a}_{\rho\mu}
,
C^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}=\omega^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}
,
the field equations become:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\omega^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(e^{a}_{\rho\mu}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}\right)\ .

Both of these equations are
sopde
constraints which specify the
sopde
constraint submanifold.
Imposing the stability (tangency) condition, by demanding that the Lie derivatives of the above constraints with respect to the vector fields
X_{\lambda}
of
{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}}
vanish, yields relations for the
{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathscr{L}}
multivector field coefficients
D^{a}_{\nu\sigma\mu}
and
H^{c}_{\nu\sigma\rho d}
.
It therefore follows that no stability (tangency) constraints arise, and it follows that the
sopde
constraint submanifold is the final constraint submanifold
S_{f}\hookrightarrow J^{1}\pi
.
This is shown component-wise as follows:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\lambda})\left[\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\big(\omega^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\big)\right]\right|_{S_{f}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}\left[e^{b}_{\nu}\big(H^{cd}_{\lambda\rho\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\lambda\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\lambda\sigma}\big)+e^{b}_{\lambda\nu}\big(\omega^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\big)\right]\ ,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\lambda})\left[\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\big(e^{a}_{\rho\mu}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}\big)\right]\right|_{S_{f}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}\left[e^{b}_{\nu}\big(D^{a}_{\lambda\rho\mu}+e^{i}_{\lambda\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\lambda\rho i}\big)+e^{b}_{\lambda\nu}\big(e^{a}_{\rho\mu}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}\big)\right]\ .

Now, working on integral sections of
\textbf{X}_{\mathscr{L}}
,
the final field equations are given as,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\partial_{\rho}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\partial_{\rho}e^{a}_{\mu}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}\right)\ ,

which are the well-known Euler–Lagrange equations for this theory.

5.3.2
Hamiltonian Formalism

Recall from the discussion in the Lagrangian setting that this theory of gravity is singular.
The local coordinates on
{\cal M}\pi
are
(x^{\mu},e^{a}_{\mu},\omega^{ab}_{\mu},p^{\mu\nu}_{a},\pi^{\rho\sigma}_{cd},p)
and

J^{1}\pi^{*}
has local coordinates
(x^{\mu},e^{a}_{\mu},\omega^{ab}_{\mu},p^{\mu\nu}_{a},\pi^{\rho\sigma}_{cd})
.
Furthermore, the primary constraint submanifold
P_{\circ}=\text{Im}\mathscr{F}\mathscr{L}\subset J^{1}\pi^{*}
on which the Hamilton–-De Donder–-Weyl formalism
takes place is given by the following equations:

\mathscr{FL}^{*}p^{\mu\nu}_{a}=\frac{\partial{\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{e^{a}_{\mu\nu}}}=0\quad,\quad\mathscr{FL}^{*}\pi^{\rho\sigma}_{cd}=\frac{\partial{\mathscr{L}}}{\partial{\omega_{\rho\sigma}^{cd}}}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\ .

(62)

The De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian is,

\mathscr{H}_{\circ}=-\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\ ,

and the Hamiltonian section is given as

h_{\circ}=(x^{\mu},e^{a}_{\mu},\omega^{ab}_{\mu},p^{\mu\nu}_{a},\pi^{\rho\sigma}_{cd},p=-\mathscr{H}_{\circ})
.

The Hamilton–Cartan
4
-form and
5
-form are:

\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{3}x_{\rho}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\text{d}^{4}x\right)\ ,

\displaystyle\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=-2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}\left(e^{b}_{\nu}\text{d}e^{a}_{\mu}\wedge\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{3}x_{\rho}+e^{b}_{\nu}\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\text{d}e^{a}_{\mu}\wedge\text{d}^{4}x-e^{a}_{\mu}e^{i}_{\nu}\omega^{b}_{\rho i}\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{4}x\right)\ .

Now using a
4
-multivector field with components,

{\mathbf{X}}^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=\bigwedge^{3}_{\nu=0}X_{\nu}=\bigwedge^{3}_{\mu=0}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\nu}}+B^{a}_{\nu\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\mu}}+C^{c}_{\nu\rho d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{c}_{\rho d}}\right)\in\text{\fr X}^{4}(P_{\circ})\ ,

(63)

the equation
i({\mathbf{X}}^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}})\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=0
gives,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(C^{cd}_{\rho\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(B^{a}_{\rho\mu}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}\right)\ ,

and evidently no compatibility constraints arise.
Note that the
sopde
constraints from the Lagrangian setting are not
\mathscr{FL}
-projectable (see
[
2
]
).
Furthermore, as in the Lagrangian setting, there are no compatibility constraints.
The final field equations are given on integral sections of
{\mathbf{X}}^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}
, and are written as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\partial_{\rho}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}+\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{b}_{\nu}\left(\partial_{\rho}e^{a}_{\mu}+e^{i}_{\mu}\omega^{a}_{\rho i}\right)\ ,

identically to the Lagrangian formulation.

5.3.3
Symmetries

The two kinds of symmetries exhibited by this theory are
spacetime diffeomorphisms
and local
Poincaré transformations
.

Spacetime diffeomorphisms
:

Take spacetime diffeomorphisms to be generated infinitesimally by the vector field,

\xi=-\xi^{\mu}(x)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}\in\text{\fr X}(M)\ ,

(64)

by performing (point-wise) the following coordinate transformation:

x^{\mu}\rightarrow x^{\mu}+\xi^{\mu}(x)
.
It follows that the fields of the theory transform as,

\displaystyle\delta e^{a}_{\mu}(x)=e^{\prime a}_{\mu}(x)-e^{a}_{\mu}(x)=-\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}e^{a}_{\mu}-e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\ ,

\displaystyle\delta\omega^{a}_{\mu b}(x)=\omega^{\prime a}_{\mu b}(x)-\omega^{a}_{\mu b}(x)=-\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\omega^{a}_{\mu b}-\omega^{a}_{\nu b}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\ .

The canonical lift to the configuration bundle
E
is given by,

\xi_{E}=-\xi^{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\mu}}+\omega^{a}_{\nu b}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{a}_{\mu b}}\in\mathfrak{X}(E)\ .

As usual, the canonical lift of
\xi_{E}
to the Lagrangian phase space
J^{1}\pi
is the
1
-jet prolongation of
\xi_{E}
given by (
28
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{\xi}=j^{1}\xi_{E}=&-\xi^{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\mu}}+\omega^{a}_{\nu b}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{a}_{\mu b}}\\
&+\left(e^{a}_{\sigma\rho}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\rho}-e^{a}_{\rho\mu}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\rho}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\sigma\mu}}+\left(\omega^{a}_{\sigma\rho b}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\rho}-\omega^{a}_{\rho\mu b}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\rho}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{a}_{\sigma\mu b}}\in\text{\fr X}(J^{1}\pi)\ .\end{split}

(65)

and, by (
26
) and (
27
),
the canonical lifts to the multimomentum bundles are

\displaystyle Z_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\xi^{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\mu}}+\omega^{a}_{\nu b}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{a}_{\mu b}}

\displaystyle-\left(\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\mu}p^{\lambda\nu}_{a}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}p^{\mu\nu}_{a}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\nu}p^{\mu\lambda}_{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{\mu\nu}_{a}}}

\displaystyle-\left(\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\mu}\pi^{\lambda\nu}_{ab}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}\pi^{\mu\nu}_{ab}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\nu}\pi^{\mu\lambda}_{ab}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\pi^{\mu\nu}_{ab}}}+p\,\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\in\text{\fr X}(\mathcal{M}\pi)\ ,

\displaystyle Y_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\xi^{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\mu}}+\omega^{a}_{\nu b}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{a}_{\mu b}}

\displaystyle-\left(\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\mu}p^{\lambda\nu}_{a}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}p^{\mu\nu}_{a}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\nu}p^{\mu\lambda}_{a}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p^{\mu\nu}_{a}}}

\displaystyle-\left(\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\mu}\pi^{\lambda\nu}_{ab}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}\pi^{\mu\nu}_{ab}-\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\nu}\pi^{\mu\lambda}_{ab}\right)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\pi^{\mu\nu}_{ab}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(J^{1}\pi^{*})\ .

In this theory, it can be shown that

(\mathscr{FL}_{\circ})_{*}X_{\xi}=Y_{\xi}^{\circ}\in\text{\fr X}(P_{\circ})
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y^{\circ}_{\xi}=-\xi^{\mu}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{\mu}}}+e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial e^{a}_{\mu}}+\omega^{a}_{\nu b}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{a}_{\mu b}}\in\text{\fr X}(P_{\circ})\ .\end{split}

(66)

This vector field is not the canonical lift of

to

according to Definition

because the Lie derivatives by

of the primary constraint functions which arise from (

, and hence,

is not tangent to

.

Nevertheless, observe that, as
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(X_{\xi})\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=0
, it follows that
\mathop{\mathrm{L}}\nolimits(Y^{\circ}_{\xi})\Omega^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=0
as in the previous example.

X_{\xi}
is a natural Lagrangian infinitesimal Noether symmetry but

is a Hamiltonian infinitesimal Noether symmetry which is not natural according to our Definition

4.3
,
although it comes from a symmetry in

.
The multimomentum maps associated with
X_{\xi}
and
Y^{\circ}_{\xi}
are:

\displaystyle J_{\mathscr{L}}(X_{\xi})

\displaystyle=-i(X_{\xi})\Theta_{\mathscr{L}}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\xi^{\lambda}\left(\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\text{d}^{3}x_{\lambda}-\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{2}x_{\rho\lambda}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle J^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}(Y^{\circ}_{\xi})

\displaystyle=-i(Y^{\circ}_{\xi})\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathscr{H}}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}\xi^{\lambda}\left(\omega^{c}_{\rho i}\omega^{id}_{\sigma}\text{d}^{3}x_{\lambda}-\text{d}\omega^{cd}_{\sigma}\wedge\text{d}^{2}x_{\rho\lambda}\right)\ .

Local Lorentz transformations
:

The
SO(3,1)
gauge group of local Lorentz transformations acts point-wise on the tangent space coordinates

6
Conclusions and outlook

In this work we have introduced
the intrinsic definition of the canonical lift of diffeomorphisms, and their infinitesimal generating vector fields, of the configuration manifold of a first–order classical field theory
to the extended and restricted multimomentum bundles,

{\cal M}\pi
and
J^{1}\pi^{*}
.
We have also presented the appropriate restricted definition for such canonical lifts relevant to singular (or, more specifically, almost-regular) field theories which are formulated on embedded constraint submanifolds of the multimomentum bundles

{\cal M}\pi
and
J^{1}\pi^{*}
.
In general, the definitions we have given for the aforementioned canonical lifts depend only on the canonical multisymplectic structure of the extended multimomentum bundle
{\cal M}\pi
.
This provides us with a powerful geometric tool to investigate field theories in the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian setting without having to make any reference to a Lagrangian formulation.
It should be noted that, in this work, we have outlined for the reader with the (pre)multisymplectic Lagrangian formulation of classical field theories with the purpose of pointing out the equivalence of the well-known canonical lifts in the Lagrangian setting
[
44
]
to the canonical lifts in the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian setting which we introduce in this work.

It is interesting to observe that the last field theory studied in this paper in Section
5.3
, Einstein–Cartan gravity,
is an example of a case in which we have Hamiltonian infinitesimal Noether symmetries which are not natural in the way that is presented in Definition
4.3
.
This is because the canonical lifts of the corresponding vector fields are not tangent to the primary constraint submanifold
P_{\circ}
, although they really come from infinitesimal symmetries originating from the configuration space
E
.
Interestingly, the symmetries of Enstein–Cartan gravity in the Hamiltonian setting are the Legendre map projection of Noether symmetries which are natural in the Lagrangian setting.
This scenario may arise in other field theories as well and suggests the possibility of establishing a more general definition of canonical lifts for almost-regular Hamiltonian field theories
as a part of a deeper study of symmetries for singular field theories in the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian formalism.

Being able to work directly on the multimomentum bundles
{\cal M}\pi
and
J^{1}\pi^{*}
without making reference to a Lagrangian formulation provides us with the ability to not only investigate field theories which may not have a Lagrangian description, but also search for new physical field theories completely geometrically in a new way.

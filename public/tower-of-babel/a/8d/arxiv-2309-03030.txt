Auxiliary free constructions for explicit embeddings of recursive groups
V. H. Mikaelian

Abstract
. An auxiliary free construction ✻ i = 1 r ​ ( K i , L i , t i ) M \textstyle\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M} based on HNN-extensions and on free product of groups with amalgamated subgroups is suggested, and some of its basic properties are displayed. This construction is a generalization for many of constructions used by Higman in embeddings of recursive groups into finitely presented groups, as well as for constructions we used in research on embeddings of recursive groups recently. Usage of this technical construction simplifies some embedding methods for recursive groups. A few other results on specific subgroups of HNN-extensions of groups and of free product of groups with amalgamated subgroup are obtained.

20F05, 20E06, 20E07.

2.1.
Benign subgroups

A key notion used by Higman to connect group-theoretical concepts with recursion and computability is that of
benign
subgroups:

Definition 2.1
.

A subgroup
H
of a finitely generated group
G
is called a
benign subgroup
in
G
, if
G
can be embedded in a finitely presented group
K
with a finitely generated subgroup
L\leq K
such that
G\cap L=H
.

In fact,
[
5
]
suggests three equivalent definitions for benign subgroups, but we restrict ourselves to this one only.
If we have to work with more than one benign subgroups
H
in
G
, then we may denote the respective groups mentioned in Definition
2.1
via
K=K_{H}
and
L=L_{H}
to stress the correlation with
H
.

An evident example of a benign subgroup is a finitely generated subgroup
H
in a finitely presented group
G
. Then we just have to pick
K_{H}=G
and
L_{H}=H
.
For examples of
infinitely
generated benign subgroups see Section
5
.
Other details on benign subgroups see in Sections 3, 4 in
[
5
]
, check also Section 3 in
[
11
]
.

2.2.
Free constructions, references

Below we use three types of free constructions:
free products of groups with amalgamated subgroups,
HNN-extensions of groups by one or more stable letters, see
[
7
,
3
,
16
,
4
]
, and also the technical
✻
-construction which is a nested combination of both, see Section
4
.

Here is the actual notation we are going to use for free constructions.
If the groups
G
and
H
have subgroups
A
and
B
respectively, and
\varphi:A\to B
is an isomorphism, then we denote by
G*_{\varphi}H
the free product
\langle G,H\mathrel{|}a=a^{\varphi}\text{ for all $a\in A$}\,\rangle
of
G
and
H
with subgroups
A
and
B
amalgamated with respect to
\varphi
. In the simple case, when
G
and
H
two groups intersecting in a subgroup
A
, we may take
B=A
and assume
\varphi
is the identical isomorphism on
A
. Then we prefer to write
G*_{A}H
.

If the group
G
has subgroups
A
and
B
, and
\varphi:A\to B
is an isomorphism, then we denote
by
G*_{\varphi}t
the HNN-extension
\langle G,t\mathrel{|}a^{t}=a^{\varphi}\text{ for all $a\in A$}\,\rangle
of the base group
G

by the stable letter
t

with respect to the isomorphism

\varphi
.
In case when
A=B
and
\varphi
is just the identity function on
A
, we prefer to write
\Gamma=G*_{A}t
. We may also say that the stable letter
fixes

A
in
G
.
The HNN-extensions with more than one stable letters also are used. If for some pairs of subgroups
A_{1},B_{1};\;A_{2},B_{2};\,\ldots
in
G
we have the isomorphisms
\varphi_{1}:A_{1}\to B_{1},\;\varphi_{2}:A_{2}\to B_{2},\ldots
, then we denote the HNN-extension
\langle G,t_{1},t_{2},\ldots\mathrel{|}a_{1}^{t_{1}}=a_{1}^{\varphi_{1}}\!\!,\;a_{2}^{t_{2}}=a_{2}^{\varphi_{2}}\!\!,\,\ldots\;\text{ for all $a_{1}\in A_{1}$, $a_{2}\in A_{2},\ldots$}\,\rangle
by
G*_{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots}(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots)
.
Our notations for the
normal forms
in
free constructions (see Section
3
and Section
4
) is very close to
[
3
]
.

The definition for
✻
-construction (

2.3.
Integer-valued sequences

In
[
5
]
the recursive functions are mostly used to operate on integer-valued sequences interpreted as integer-valued functions.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of all functions
f:{\mdmathbb Z}\to{\mdmathbb Z}
with finite supports
\sup(f)=\big\{i\in{\mdmathbb Z}\mathrel{|}f(i)\neq 0\big\}
.
If for an
f
there is a positive
m
such that

\sup(f)\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
, then
denoting
j_{i}=f(i)
we get a
sequence

(j_{0},\ldots,j_{m-1})
which holds all the non-zero values of
f
. Since having the coordinates
f(i)
of this sequence we can restore the function, we identify them via

f=(j_{0},\ldots,j_{m-1})
.
Say,

f=(0,0,5,-1,0,0,2,2)

means that we can take
m=8
and set

f(2)=5
,
f(3)=-1
,

f(6)=f(7)=2
, and

f(i)=0
for any index
i\in{\mdmathbb Z}\,{\backslash}\,\big\{2,3,6,7\big\}
.
Of course,
m
is
not
determined uniquely, and if needed, we may interpret the function
f
as a
longer
sequence by adding some extra zeros to it. Say, the above function
f
can well be interpreted as the sequence
f=(0,0,5,-1,0,0,2,2,0,0,0,0)
for
m=8+4=12
.
And the constant zero function
f(i)=0
may be interpreted either as
f=(0)
or, say, as
f=(0,0,0)
.
In the current note an application of such sequences can be found in Section
5
.

A significant part of
[
5
]
consists of discussion about
sets
of sequences of the above type with specific restrictions. Also, the
Higman operations

\iota,\upsilon,\rho,\sigma,\tau,\theta,\zeta,\pi,\omega_{m}
are being used to build new sets of sequences from the existing ones. See Section 2 in
[
5
]
or the recent article
[
12
]
where the Higman operations are considered in detail.
However, the Higman operations are not used in this note and, therefore, we limit ourselves to this reference only.

Subgroups in free products with amalgamation and in HNN-extensions

3.1

is a slight variation of Lemma 3.1 given by Higman in
[

]
without a proof as a fact
“obvious from the normal form theorem”
, and

is its analog for HNN-extensions. We accompany both lemmas with corollaries which are going to often be used in
[
11
,
13
]

and elsewhere.

3.1.
Subgroups in free products with amalgamation

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma=G*_{\varphi}\!H
be the free product of the groups
G
and
H
with amalgamated subgroups

A\leq G
and
B\leq H

with respect to the isomorphism

\varphi:A\to B
.
If
G^{\prime},H^{\prime}
respectively are subgroups of
G,H
, such that
for
A^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap A
and
B^{\prime}=H^{\prime}\cap B

we have

\varphi(A^{\prime})=B^{\prime}
, then for the subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle G^{\prime},H^{\prime}\rangle
of
\Gamma
and for the restriction
\varphi^{\prime}
of
\varphi
on
A^{\prime}
we have:

(1)

\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}*_{\varphi^{\prime}}H^{\prime}
,

(2)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap A=A^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap B=B^{\prime}
,

(3)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap G=G^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap H=H^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By definition
\Gamma=(G*H)/N
where

N
is the normal closure of
\{\varphi(a)\,a^{-1}\!\mathrel{|}a\in A\}
.
Any element in
\Gamma^{\prime}
can be presented as

c=c_{0}\,c_{1}\cdots c_{m-1}c_{m}

with each term

c_{i}
picked from the factors
G^{\prime}
or
H^{\prime}
(the case
m=0
is not ruled out). By where necessary merging the neighbour factors, we may suppose any two consecutive terms
c_{i},c_{i+1}
are picked from different factors.
Fix a transversal
T_{A^{\prime}}
to
A^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
, and a transversal
T_{B^{\prime}}
to
B^{\prime}
in
H^{\prime}
, and apply to
c
the “analog” of the procedure of bringing to a normal form. Namely,
for
c_{m}\!\!\in G^{\prime}
, write
c_{m}=u\,l_{n}
where
u\in A^{\prime}
and
l_{n}\in T_{A^{\prime}}
(ignore the value
n
for now).
Since
c_{m-1}\!\in H^{\prime}
, then
c_{m-1}\,u=c_{m-1}v\in H^{\prime}
for
v=\varphi(u)\in B^{\prime}
, and so
c_{m-1}v=w\,l_{n-1}
where
w\in B^{\prime}
and
l_{n-1}\in T_{B^{\prime}}
.
We already have the last two terms for
c=c_{0}\,c_{1}\cdots c_{m-2}\cdot w\,l_{n-1}l_{n}
.
Continuing the process we get:

(3.1)

c=l_{0}\,l_{1}\cdots l_{n-1}l_{n}

where
n\leq m
,

l_{0}\in A^{\prime}
or
l_{0}\in B^{\prime}
, and each of terms

l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}
is a non-trivial element from
T_{A^{\prime}}
or
T_{B^{\prime}}
such that no two consecutive terms are from the same transversal.

To prove point (
1
) it is enough to show that (
3.1
) is
unique
for any
c\in\Gamma^{\prime}
, because unique normal forms are one of the ways to define free products with amalgamation.
Notice that if
l_{i},l_{j}
from
T_{A^{\prime}}
are distinct modulo
A^{\prime}
, they also are distinct modulo
A
because
l_{i},l_{j}
are in
G^{\prime}
, and so
l_{i}^{\vphantom{8}}l_{j}^{-1}\!\in A
would imply

l_{i}^{\vphantom{8}}l_{j}^{-1}\!\in(G^{\prime}\cap A)=A^{\prime}
.
So we can choose a transversal
T_{A}
to
A
in
G
, containing
T_{A^{\prime}}
as its subset.
Similarly, if
l_{i},l_{j}
from
T_{B^{\prime}}
are distinct modulo
B^{\prime}
, they also are distinct modulo
B
, and so we can choose a transversal
T_{B}
to
B
in
H
, containing
T_{B^{\prime}}
.
Finally, we may consider
l_{0}
as an element from
A
or
B
.
Thus, (
3.1
) is nothing but the normal form of
c
in
\Gamma=G*_{\varphi}H
written using
T_{A}
and
T_{B}
.
Since it is unique, point (
1
) is proved.

Points (
2
), (
3
) now follow from point (
1
), and from uniqueness of the normal form.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma=G*_{A}H
,
and let
G^{\prime}\leq G
,
H^{\prime}\leq H
be subgroups for which
G^{\prime}\cap\,A=H^{\prime}\cap\,A
. Then for
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle G^{\prime},H^{\prime}\rangle
and
A^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap\,A
we have:

(1)

\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\!*_{A^{\prime}}H^{\prime}
, in particular, if
A\leq G^{\prime}\!,\,H^{\prime}
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}*_{A}H^{\prime}
;

(2)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,A=A^{\prime}
, in particular, if
A\leq G^{\prime}\!,\,H^{\prime}
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,A=A
;

(3)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,G=G^{\prime}
and

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,H=H^{\prime}
.

In case the subgroup
A^{\prime}
above is
trivial
, then we have much simpler situation:

Corollary 3.3
.

If in the notation of Corollary
3.2
the subgroup
A^{\prime}
is trivial, then:

(1)

\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}*H^{\prime}
is the ordinary free product of
G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
;

(2)

If also

G^{\prime}\cong F_{m}
and
H^{\prime}\cong F_{n}
are isomorphic to free groups of rank
m
and
n
respectively, then
\Gamma^{\prime}\cong F_{m+n}
.

3.2.
Subgroups in HNN-extensions

The facts of previous subsection have analogs for HNN-extensions:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma=G*_{\varphi}t
be the HNN-extension of the base group
G
by the stable letter
t

with respect to the isomorphism

\varphi:A\to B

of the subgroups

A,B\leq G
.
If
G^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
G
such that
for
A^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap\,A
and
B^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap\,B

we have

\varphi(A^{\prime})=B^{\prime}
, then for the subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle G^{\prime},t\rangle
of
\Gamma
and for the restriction
\varphi^{\prime}
of
\varphi
on
A^{\prime}
we have:

(1)

\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}*_{\varphi^{\prime}}t
,

(2)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap G=G^{\prime}
,

(3)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,A=A^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap B=B^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By definition
\Gamma=(G*\langle t\rangle)/N
where

N
is the normal closure of
\{\varphi(a)\,a^{-t}\!\mathrel{|}a\in A\}
.
Any element in
\Gamma^{\prime}
can be presented as

c=c_{0}\,t^{s_{1}}c_{1}\,t^{s_{2}}\cdots\,t^{s_{m-1}}c_{m-1}\,t^{s_{m}}c_{m}\,t^{s_{m+1}}

with each

c_{i}
picked from
G^{\prime}

and
t^{s_{i}}
picked from
\langle t\rangle

(the case
m=0
is not ruled out). By where necessary merging the neighbour factors, we may suppose
no
c_{i}
or
t^{s_{i}}
are trivial except the last one, possibly.
Fix a transversal
T_{A^{\prime}}
to
A^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
, and a transversal
T_{B^{\prime}}
to
B^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
, and apply to
c
the “analog” of the procedure of bringing to a normal form. Namely,
if, say,
s_{m}<0
, then write
t^{s_{m}}c_{m}=t^{s_{m}+1}t^{-1}u\,l_{n}=t^{s_{m}+1}u^{t}t^{-1}l_{n}
where
u\in A^{\prime}
and
l_{n}\in T_{A^{\prime}}
.
Since
u\in A^{\prime}
, then
u^{t}=\varphi(u)
is some element
v\in B^{\prime}
, and so

t^{s_{m}+1}u^{t}t^{-1}l_{n}=t^{s_{m}+1}v\,t^{-1}l_{n}
.

If still
s_{m}\!+1<0
, we can repeat the step to continue to:

t^{s_{m}+1}v\,t^{-1}l_{n}=t^{s_{m}+2}t^{-1}w\,l_{n-1}\,t^{-1}l_{n}=t^{s_{m}+2}\,zt^{-1}l_{n-1}\,t^{-1}l_{n}

with

w\in A^{\prime}
and
z=w^{t}\in B^{\prime}
(the cases
v=1
,
w=1
or
z=1
are not ruled out). After a few such steps our work on
t^{s_{m}}c_{m}
will finished. In case
s_{m}>0
, we would use
B^{\prime}
and
T_{B^{\prime}}
, and write
t^{s_{m}}c_{m}=t^{s_{m}-1}t\,u\,l_{n}=t^{s_{m}-1}u^{t^{-1}}t\,l_{n}
, etc., instead.

Assume a leftover
h
remains after we finish the job with
t^{s_{m}}c_{m}
.
Concatenate
h
to the the next syllable
t^{s_{m-1}}c_{m-1}
, and repeat all the above steps for

t^{s_{m-1}}(c_{m-1}h)
(taking into account if
s_{m-1}
is negative or positive).

Finally, if during our process subwords of type
t^{-1}1\,t
or
t\,1\,t^{-1}
occur, just cancel them out.
At the end of this process we
get:

(3.2)

c=l_{0}\,t^{\varepsilon_{1}}l_{1}\,t^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots\,t^{\varepsilon_{n-1}}l_{n-1}\,t^{\varepsilon_{n}}l_{n}

where
n\leq m
,

\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1
,

l_{0}\in G^{\prime}
, and for
i=1,\ldots,n

if
\varepsilon_{i}=-1
, then
l_{i}\in T_{A^{\prime}}
; while if
\varepsilon_{i}=1
, then
l_{i}\in T_{B^{\prime}}
. The case
l_{i}=1
is
not
ruled out, so subsequences of type
t^{-1}1\,t^{-1}1=t^{-2}
or
t1\,t1=t^{2}
are possible, but subsequences
t^{-1}1\,t
or
t\,1\,t^{-1}
still are impossible in (
3.2
).

(
3.2
) meets all formal requirements on the normal form in HNN-extensions, so to prove point (
1
) it is enough to show that (
3.2
) is
unique
for any
c\in\Gamma^{\prime}
(unique normal forms are one of the ways to define HNN-extensions).
If
l_{i},l_{j}
from
T_{A^{\prime}}
are distinct modulo
A^{\prime}
, they also are distinct modulo
A
(see the proof of Lemma
3.1
).
We can choose a transversal
T_{A}
to
A
in
G
, containing
T_{A^{\prime}}
.
Similarly, we can choose a transversal
T_{B}
to
B
in
H
, containing
T_{B^{\prime}}
.

Thus, (
3.2
) is unique as it is the normal form of
c
in
\Gamma=G*_{\varphi}t
written inside
T_{A}
and
T_{B}
.
Points (
2
), (
3
) now follow from point (
1
).
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma=G*_{A}t
,
and let
G^{\prime}\leq G
be a subgroup. Then for
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle G^{\prime},t\rangle
and
A^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap\,A
we have:

(1)

\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\!*_{A^{\prime}}t
, in particular, if
A\leq G^{\prime}
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}*_{A}t
;

(2)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,A=A^{\prime}
, in particular, if
A\leq G^{\prime}
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,A=A
;

(3)

\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\,G=G^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.6
.

It is easy to adapt the proof of Lemma
3.4
for the case of multiple stable letters
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
. And that adaptation will be especially simple, if all the stable letters
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
just fix the
same
subgroup
A
in
G
. In such a case, say, point (
1
) in Corollary
3.5
will read:

\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\!*_{A^{\prime}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})
for
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle G^{\prime},t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\rangle
and for
A^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap\,A
.

In case the above
A^{\prime}
is
trivial
, we have much simpler situation:

Corollary 3.7
.

If in the notation of Corollary
3.5
the subgroup
A^{\prime}
is trivial, then:

(1)

\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}*\langle t\rangle
is the ordinary free product of
G^{\prime}
and of an infinite cycle
\langle t\rangle
;

(2)

If also

G^{\prime}\cong F_{m}
is isomorphic to free groups rank
m
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}\cong F_{m+1}
.

4.
The
✻
-construction and its subgroups

4.1.
Building the
✻
-construction

Let
G\leq M\leq K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
be an arbitrary system of groups such that
K_{i}\cap K_{j}=M
for any distinct indices
i,j=1,\ldots,r
.
Picking in each
K_{i}
a subgroup
L_{i}

we for technical reasons build a “nested” free construction:

(4.1)

\Big(\cdots\Big(\big((K_{1}*_{L_{1}}t_{1})*_{M}(K_{2}*_{L_{2}}t_{2})\big)*_{M}(K_{3}*_{L_{3}}t_{3})\Big)\cdots\Big)*_{M}(K_{r}*_{L_{r}}t_{r})

in which the HNN-extensions
K_{i}*_{L_{i}}t_{i}
all are amalgamated in their common subgroup
M
.
To avoid the very bulky notation of (
4.1
), let us for the sake of briefness denote the construction by (
1.1
), and call it a
✻
-construction.

We are going to also use this group in some extreme cases, such as,
G=M
or
K_{i}=M
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
.
Denote
G\cap\,L_{i}=A_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
.
When for each
i
we are limited to
K_{i}=G
,
L_{i}=A_{i}
,
M=G
, then

\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(G,A_{i},t_{i})_{G}
is noting but the usual HNN-extension
G*_{A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}}\!(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
.
And when also all the subgroups
A_{i}
are trivial, then this
✻
-construction simply is the free product
G*\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\rangle
where
\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\rangle\cong F_{r}
is a free group of rank
r
.

An evident feature of this construction is:

Lemma 4.1
.

In above notations, if each of
K_{i}
is finitely presented, while
M
and each of
L_{i}
is finitely generated,
i=1,\ldots,r
, then
\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M}
is finitely presented.

We use this feature of
✻
-construction to build finitely presented overgroups of
G
, and Corollary
4.5
is the first usage of it, see also applications of that corollary in
[
11
,
13
]
.

4.2.
Subgroups in
\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M}

It turns out that some HNN-extensions may be identified inside
✻
-constructions.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
G\leq M\leq K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
and
A_{i}=G\cap\,L_{i}
are the groups mentioned above, then in
{\textrm{\footnotesize✻}}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M}

we have:

\langle G,t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\rangle=G*_{A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}}\!(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}).

For any group

Lemma 4.3

\textstyle G\cap G^{t_{1}\cdots\,t_{r}}=I,

\displaystyle g^{t_{1}\cdots\,t_{r}}

\displaystyle=t_{r}^{-1}\cdots t_{1}^{-1}a_{1}l_{1}\,t_{1}\cdots t_{r}

\displaystyle=t_{r}^{-1}\cdots t_{2}^{-1}a_{1}t_{1}^{-1}l_{1}\,t_{1}\cdots t_{r}

\displaystyle=\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle=a_{r}\,t_{r}^{-1}l_{r}\,t_{r-1}^{-1}l_{r-1}\cdots\,l_{3}\,t_{2}^{-1}l_{2}\,t_{1}^{-1}l_{1}\,t_{1}\cdots t_{r}

\textstyle G\cap\big\langle\bigcup_{\,i=1}^{\,r}\,G^{t_{i}}\big\rangle=J.

For simplicity write the proof for the case

By Lemma

4.4.
Free products inside
✻
-constructions

The following corollary allows to detect some free products inside
\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M}
and inside its subgroups, such as
G*_{A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}}\!(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
, as soon as some “narrower” free products are known inside
G
:

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}
be any subgroups in a group
G
such that their join
J
in
G
is isomorphic to their free product

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\,A_{i}
.
Then the join

\big\langle\bigcup_{\,i=1}^{\,r}G^{t_{i}}\big\rangle

is isomorphic to the free product
\prod_{i=1}^{r}G^{t_{i}}

in

, and hence in
\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M}
.

Proof.

Since, for each
i=1,\ldots,r
we have
\langle G,G^{t_{i}}\rangle=G*_{A_{i}}G^{t_{i}}
​, and since all such
subgroups intersect in
G
, and they together generate

G^{*}=\big\langle\!G,\,G^{t_{1}}\!,\dots,G^{t_{r}}\!\big\rangle
, we see that
G^{*}
is the free product of the groups
G*_{A_{1}}G^{t_{1}},\ldots,G*_{A_{r}}G^{t_{r}}
all amalgamated in
G
.

Thus, as generators and defining relations of
G^{*}
we by default can list the following:
any set of generators in
G

plus their copies in each
G^{t_{i}}
;
the relations
R
for
G
, plus their copies
R^{t_{i}}
for each
G^{t_{i}}
​, plus the relations stating that the copies of
G
in each
G*_{A_{i}}G^{t_{i}}
coincide, plus the relations stating that
a_{i}^{t_{i}}=a_{i}
for arbitrary
a_{i}
in each of
A_{i}
.

For each
i
the map
\varphi_{i}:A_{i}\to G^{t_{i}}
given by the rule

\varphi_{i}:a_{i}\to a_{i}^{t_{i}}\in G^{t_{i}}
is an isomorphism from
A_{i}
onto
A_{i}^{t_{i}}
.
Since
J
is the
free
product

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\,A_{i}
, there exists a common continuation
\varphi
(for all
\varphi_{i}
) from
J
to the free product
\prod_{i=1}^{r}G^{t_{i}}
(
onto
its subgroup
\prod_{i=1}^{r}A^{t_{i}}
).
Using this

we can construct the free product
G^{**}=G*_{\varphi}\prod_{i=1}^{r}G^{t_{i}}
with subgroups
J
and
\prod_{i=1}^{r}A^{t_{i}}
amalgamated by
\varphi
.

As generators and defining relations of
G^{**}
we can list the following: the generators earlier chosen for
G
along with the relations
R
;
the copies of those generators in each
G^{t_{i}}
and the copies
R^{t_{i}}
of relations
R
;
plus the relations stating that
\varphi:J\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}A^{t_{i}}
.
The latters can well be replaced by the relations stating that each
A_{i}
in
J
coincides with
A^{t_{i}}
by
\varphi_{i}
.
But this is noting but the list we provided for
G^{*}
earlier, and hence
G^{*}=G^{**}
. Clearly,

contains the free product
\prod_{i=1}^{r}G^{t_{i}}
together with the subgroup
\prod_{i=1}^{r}A^{t_{i}}
of the latter.
∎

5.2.

Using notation of the previous point, for any integer

,

,

M=\langle b,c\rangle
.
The group
K_{J}
clearly is finitely presented, and by Lemma
4.2
it contains the subgroup:

\big\langle\langle b,c\rangle,\,u,v\big\rangle=\langle b,c\rangle*_{\langle b_{m},b_{m+1},\ldots\rangle,\;\langle b_{-1},b_{{-2}},\ldots\rangle}(u,v).

This subgroup may no longer be finitely presented (as
\langle b_{m},b_{m+1},\ldots\rangle
and
\langle b_{-1},b_{{-2}},\ldots\rangle
are not finitely generated). But by Lemma
4.4

inside this subgroup for the join

J=\langle\ldots b_{-2},b_{-1};\;b_{m},b_{m+1},\ldots\rangle

of two subgroup
\langle b_{m},b_{m+1},\ldots\rangle
and
\langle b_{-1},b_{{-2}},\ldots\rangle

we have

\langle b,c\rangle\cap\big\langle\langle b,c\rangle^{u},\langle b,c\rangle^{v}\big\rangle=J
.
Hence
J
is benign, and as the respective finitely generated subgroup
L_{J}
of (
5.6
) one may pick the
4
-generator subgroup
L_{J}=\big\langle\langle b,c\rangle^{u},\langle b,c\rangle^{v}\big\rangle
of
K_{J}
.

And to show that
J
is benign also in
\langle a,b,c\rangle
just use a slightly larger finitely presented overgroup
\langle a\rangle*K_{J}
.

Abstract
We identify a sufficient condition, treewidth-pliability , that gives a polynomial-time algorithm for an arbitrarily good approximation of the optimal value in a large class of Max-2-CSPs parameterised by the class of allowed constraint graphs (with arbitrary constraints on an unbounded alphabet). Our result applies more generally to the maximum homomorphism problem between two rational-valued structures. The condition unifies the two main approaches for designing a polynomial-time approximation scheme. One is Baker’s layering technique, which applies to sparse graphs such as planar or excluded-minor graphs. The other is based on Szemerédi’s regularity lemma and applies to dense graphs. We extend the applicability of both techniques to new classes of Max-CSPs. On the other hand, we prove that the condition cannot be used to find solutions (as opposed to approximating the optimal value) in general. Treewidth-pliability turns out to be a robust notion that can be defined in several equivalent ways, including characterisations via size, treedepth, or the Hadwiger number. We show connections to the notions of fractional-treewidth-fragility from structural graph theory, hyperfiniteness from the area of property testing, and regularity partitions from the theory of dense graph limits. These may be of independent interest. In particular we show that a monotone class of graphs is hyperfinite if and only if it is fractionally-treewidth-fragile and has bounded degree.

Introduction

The problem of finding a
maximum cut

most studied problems from Karp’s original list of 21 NP-complete
problems
[
56
]
. While Max-Cut is NP-hard to solve

there is a trivial
0.5
-approximation algorithm
[
81
]
and

0.878
-approximation algorithm of Goemans and

[
42
]
.
Papadimitriou and Yannakakis established that Max-Cut is
Max-SNP-hard
[
77
]
. By the work of Arora, Lund, Motwani,
Sudan, and Szegedy
[
5
]
this implies that, unless P=NP,
there is no polynomial-time approximation scheme (PTAS) for Max-Cut in general
graphs. However, non-trivial results exist for important special cases. On the
one hand, Max-Cut is solvable exactly in planar graphs, as shown by
Hadlock
[
50
]
, and more generally, Max-Cut admits a PTAS on

Kawarabayashi
[
23
]
. On the other hand, Arora, Karger, and
Karpinski showed a PTAS for Max-Cut in dense graphs
[
4
]
,
where a graph class is dense if every graph in it contains at least a constant fraction of all
possible edges.

Max-Cut is an example of
maximum constraint satisfaction problem

(Max-CSP), although a very special one (the alphabet size is
2
, in particular constant, and every constraint uses the same symmetric predicate “
x\neq y
” of arity 2).
Another well-known example is Max-
r
-SAT, with alphabet size 2 and
r

Motivated by results on planar, excluded-minor, and dense graph classes,
our goal in this paper is to understand the following question:

What structure allows for the existence of a PTAS for Max-CSPs?

We adopt a permissive definition of PTAS here: given a Max-CSP instance and an
arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
, the goal is to find a
(1-\varepsilon)

multiplicative approximation of the value of an optimal solution to the instance
(but, unlike in most papers, we do not require that the algorithm should find a

We focus on two computational problems.
First, we study the general
\mbox{Max-}2\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})
problem parameterised by the class
of underlying constraint graphs (a.k.a.

The input is a graph

The goal is to find an assignment

Similarly, in
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})

The constraints are arbitrary (non-negative) and the alphabets are not fixed, making the problem very expressive.

The question of what structure allows to solve Max-CSPs

A standard dynamic approach works for

Grohe, Schwentick, and Segoufin

Grohe’s theorem

-valued) structures

This was recently extended further to exact optimisation with valued structures by

[

]
.

\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSPs}
do not admit a PTAS in general, since already Max-Cut does not.
On the other hand, the techniques that give PTASes for Max-Cut on sparse and dense graphs

Our main contribution is a unifying condition,

\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})

More formally, for any

Here we consider two structures

and

to be
\varepsilon

\mathrm{opt}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{C})
is
\varepsilon
-close to

\mathrm{opt}(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{C})

(details in Section

While the structure of bounded treewidth is not known and cannot be efficiently computed,
we show that the Sherali-Adams LP relaxation gives a PTAS for

.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a
\tw
-pliable class of structures of bounded arity, then
\mbox{Max-Hom}(\mathcal{A},-)
admits a PTAS.

We emphasise the generality of Theorem
1.1
.

: while an

Firstly, the computational problem (

) captures many fundamental problems,
including graph homomorphisms

, Max-Cut, Max-DiCut, Max-SAT,

.

1.1
Sparse structures: Baker’s technique and fragility

Perhaps the best known technique for solving problems on planar graphs is Lipton and Tarjan’s planar separator theorem
[
63
]
and the divide & conquer approach it enables
[
64
]
.
It can be used to give a PTAS for Max-CSPs with fixed alphabet size
on planar graphs (this extends to excluded-minor graphs
[
2
]
and more
[
35
]
) of bounded degree.

This approach was superseded by Baker’s technique
[
6
]
, which provides better running times and is easily applied to general
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSPs}
on arbitrary planar graphs (see e.g.
[
57
]
).
The idea is very elegant: we partition a planar graph into Breadth-First-Search layers, remove every
\ell
-th layer, and show that the remaining components of
\ell-1
consecutive layers have bounded treewidth (and so can be solved exactly).
By trying different starting layers we can ensure that the removed layers intersect an unknown optimal solution at most
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\ell})
times, giving a
1\pm\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\ell})
approximation.

From planar graphs this was extended to graphs of bounded genus by Eppstein
[
40
]
and later to all graph
classes excluding a fixed minor by Grohe
[
46
]
and Demaine et al.
[
23
]
.
The structural property needed for this approach, originally proved for excluded-minor graphs by DeVos, Ding, Oporowski, Sanders, Reed, Seymour, and Vertigan
[
24
]
, is
\tw
-
fragility
: they can be partitioned into any constant number of parts such that removing any one part leaves a graph of bounded treewidth.
As shown by Hunt, Marathe, and Stearns
[
70
,
54
]
(see also
[
55
]
) as well as Grigoriev and Bodlaender
[
45
]
, the same property applies to some geometrically-defined graph classes that do not exclude any minor. One example is intersection graphs of unit disks whose centers are at least some constant apart (capturing some applications of the closely related shifting technique of Hochbaum and Maass
[
53
]
for geometric packing and covering problems).
Another example is 1-planar graphs, or more generally graphs drawn on a fixed surface with a bounded number of intersections per edge.

An important generalisation,
fractional-
\tw
-fragility
, was introduced by Dvořák
[
29
]
:
it suffices that the parts whose removal results in a graph of bounded treewidth are nearly-disjoint (Definition
4.2
).
This applies to
d
-dimensional variants of the geometric classes mentioned above (for any constant
d
), in particular to
d
-dimensional grids, which are not
\tw
-fragile
[
8
]
; this also includes classes of polynomial growth
[
60
,
31
]
.
Another large family of fractionally-
\tw
-fragile classes are classes of bounded degree with strongly sublinear separators
[
29
]
(equivalently, bounded degree and polynomial expansion
[
35
]
).
For such concrete examples of fragile classes, known proofs show that the nearly-disjoint parts can be computed efficiently.
A PTAS can then easily be designed from the definition
[
29
]
.

We show that the assumption about efficient construction is not needed.
We do this by proving that if
\mathcal{G}
is
any
fractionally-
\tw
-fragile class of graphs
(intuitively, any class where a Baker-like technique is known to work),
then the class
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}
of all possible structures of bounded arity

r
and with Gaifman graph in
\mathcal{G}
is
\tw
-pliable.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a fractionally-
\tw
-fragile class of graphs.
Then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}
is
\tw
-pliable for every
r
.
Consequently,
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})
admits a PTAS.

This captures all graph classes
\mathcal{G}
where a PTAS for
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})
is known.

1.2
Dense structures: the regularity lemma

It is perhaps more surprising that dense structures admit a PTAS.
Here a class is
dense
if a constant factor of all possible constraints is present in every structure in the class, e.g. graphs with
\Omega(n^{2})
edges.
Arora, Karger, and Karpinski
[
4
]
showed that
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSPs}
admit a PTAS in the dense regime if the
alphabet size is constant (in fact Boolean); de la Vega
[
18
]
independently gave a PTAS for dense Max-Cut.
Frieze and Kannan
[
41
]
proved that these results are essentially possible because of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma
[
85
]
: intuitively, every graph can be approximated to within an additive
\pm\varepsilon n^{2}
error by a random graph (with a constant number of parts, depending on
\varepsilon
only, so that the edges between two parts form a uniformly random graph of some density).
For dense graphs, the additive error translates to a relative error, giving a PTAS.
They also showed a variant of the regularity lemma that is still applicable to
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSPs}
with constant alphabet size, yet avoids its infamous tower-type dependency on
\varepsilon
.

Goldreich, Goldwasser, and Ron
[
44
]
connected these results to the area of
property testing
, spawning an entirely new direction of research.
They gave constant-time algorithms estimating the optimum value of some graph Max-CSPs.
In fact, Alon, de la Vega, Kannan, and Karpinski
[
1
]
(see also Andersson and Engebretsen
[
3
]
) showed that
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSPs}
with a fixed alphabet can be approximated with accuracy
\pm\varepsilon n^{r}
by sampling a constant number of vertices (polynomial in
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
) and finding the optimum on the resulting (constant-size) induced substructure.

None of these results apply to any
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})
and
\mbox{Max-Hom}(\mathcal{A},-)
problem, that is, to unbounded alphabets.
We give the first such example: undirected graphs with
\Omega(n^{2})
edges.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
c>0
and let
\mathcal{A}
be a class of graphs with at least
cn^{2}
edges.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is
\tw
-pliable.
Consequently,
\mbox{Max-Hom}(\mathcal{A},-)
admits a PTAS.

(Note here the graphs in
\mathcal{A}
are input structures, not just Gaifman graphs of input structures).
We also show that this cannot be extended to general CSPs:
already for the class of tournaments—that is, orientations of complete graphs—
a PTAS is impossible, (cf. Corollary
8.5
in
Section
8
) and indeed, this class is not
\tw
-pliable (cf.
Remark
7.4
).

1.3
Robustness of pliability

The notion of treewidth-pliability not only unifies the different existing
algorithmic techniques but it is also quite robust: treewidth-pliability captures a
valued analogue of “homomorphic equivalence” (e.g. bipartite graphs, or
3-colourable graphs where each edge is contained in exactly one triangle, cf.
Examples
2.14
and
2.15
in Section
2.5
) as
well as small edits: if
\mathcal{A}
is a pliable class of graphs, say, then the class
of graphs obtained by adding or removing
o(m)
edges from
m
-edge graphs in

\mathcal{A}
is again pliable (Lemma
2.13
in Section
2.5
).
However, this generality comes at a price. First, we show that even for fixed alphabet size,
although the approximate optimum value can be found, an approximate solution cannot be constructed
(unless
\text{P}=\text{NP}
, cf. Example
2.16
in Section
2.5
).
Second, unlike in some of the previous results for more restricted classes, our result does not give an EPTAS (i.e., with the degree of the
polynomial time bound independent of
\varepsilon
) for fixed alphabet size (cf. Question
9.2
).
Finally, the use of strong versions of
the regularity lemma yields tower-type dependencies on the approximation ratio
\varepsilon
in the dense case.

In the definition of treewidth-pliability we approximate structures by
comparing their
\mathrm{opt}(
) values and we ask them to be close to structures where
the problem can be solved exactly. This is a non-constructive and very general
definition. In fact, it is not inconceivable that this captures all tractable cases, i.e., that
\mbox{Max-Hom}(\mathcal{A},-)
has a PTAS if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is
\tw
-pliable.
Nevertheless, we show a variety of equivalent combinatorial definitions,
which allow us to place a fairly tight bound on what pliability is, structurally.

For classes of the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}
, that is, if we only restrict the underlying Gaifman graphs, we show that pliability collapses to fractional fragility.
In this sense we understand the “sparse” setting exactly.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of graphs. The following are equivalent, for any
r\geq 2
:

•

\mathcal{G}
is fractionally-
\tw
-fragile;

•

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}
is
\tw
-pliable.

In general, we can replace treewidth with other parameters of the Gaifman graph:
size (number of vertices), treedepth, denoted by
\td
, Hadwiger number (maximum clique minor size), or maximum connected component size, which we denote by
\cc
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be any class of structures. The following are equivalent:

•

\mathcal{A}
is
\td
-pliable;

•

\mathcal{A}
is
\tw
-pliable;

•

\mathcal{A}
is
Hadwiger
-pliable.

If structures in
\mathcal{A}
have bounded signatures,
then the following are equivalent to the above as well:

•

\mathcal{A}
is
\size
-pliable;

•

\mathcal{A}
is
\cc
-pliable.

Classes of structures with bounded signatures (see Section
2
for precise definitions) correspond to Max-CSP instances with a bounded number of constraint types; e.g. maximum graph homomorphism.
For example, any class of dense graphs as in Theorem
1.3
is in fact
\size
-pliable.
An example of a class with unbounded signatures is any class of the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}
(we do not consider infinite signatures, but there are arbitrarily many symbols in those signatures).
Theorem
1.5
allows us to give concrete and general examples of classes that are
not

\tw
-pliable:
the class of orientations of graphs in
\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathcal{G}
is
any
class of
unbounded average degree (Lemma
2.19
in Section
2.6
), or any
class of 3-regular graphs with unbounded girth (Lemma
2.25
in
Section
2.6
).

Finally, as a side result, we connect
hyperfiniteness
to fragility.
A class of graphs
\mathcal{G}
is called
hyperfinite
if
for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a
k=k(\varepsilon)
such that
in every
G\in\mathcal{G}
one can remove an at-most-
\varepsilon
fraction of edges to obtain a graph with connected components of size at most
k
.
For a monotone class of graphs (closed under taking subgraphs), hyperfiniteness easily implies bounded degree.
It is an important notion in property testing: many results in sparse graphs were generalised by the statement that every property of hyperfinite graphs is testable
[
76
]
.
The idea, originating in the work of Benjamini, Schramm, and Shapira
[
7
]
and Hassidim, Kelner, Nguyen, and Onak
[
51
]
, is that following the approach of Lipton and Tarjan, graphs with sufficiently sublinear separators, such as planar or excluded-minor graphs
[
2
]
, can be recursively partitioned into bounded-size components, which for bounded-degree graphs gives hyperfiniteness (see e.g.
[
14
, Cor. 3.2]
for a slightly stronger property, cf.
[
72
]
).
This allows, analogously as in the dense case, to give a constant-size approximate description of such graphs by sampling constant-radius balls in them
[
76
]
.
See
[
43
]
for a book on property testing and
[
61
]
for a recent improvement for excluded-minor graphs.

We show that a monotone class
\mathcal{G}
is hyperfinite if and only if it is fractionally-
\tw
-fragile and has bounded degree.
In fact, replacing the parameter treewidth by the maximum size of a connected component in a graph, we have:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a monotone class of graphs. The following are equivalent:

•

\mathcal{G}
is hyperfinite;

•

\mathcal{G}
is fractionally-
\tw
-fragile and has bounded degree;

•

\mathcal{G}
is fractionally-
\cc
-fragile;

•

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}
is
\cc
-pliable (for any
r\geq 2
).

The equivalence of the second and third bullet points was shown by Dvořák
[
29
, Observation 15, Corollary 20]
,
while for the third and fourth the proof is established by a generalisation of
Lemma
1.4
, cf. Lemma
4.7
.

Hyperfiniteness originates from the study of amenable groups and graphs limits,
with motivations in geometry and mathematical physics
[
38
]
.
The unexpected connection with fractional fragility already found an application
in that area: Elek
[
39
]

showed that our Theorem
1.6
gives that last missing
implication in proving the equivalence of some properties of infinite,
bounded-degree graphs (in particular “uniform local amenability” and
“Property A”), which answers a question of Brodzki, Niblo, Spakula, Willett
and Wright
[
10
]
.

1.4
Related work

While this paper focuses on
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSPs}
, Baker’s technique and the regularity lemma apply to many more problems.
In fact Khanna and Motwani
[
57
]
argued that most known PTAS algorithms can be derived from three canonical optimisation problems on planar graphs, the first being Max-CSP and the latter two being so-called Max-Ones and Min-Ones CSPs (also solvable with Baker’s technique).
One of the very few results that did not fit their framework was the PTAS for dense Max-Cut.
A follow-up work by Mezei, Wrochna, and Živný
[
71
]
on the
extended abstract of this work
[
80
]
extended some of the results of
the present paper to Min- and Max-CSPs with crisp constraints, which include the
Max-Ones and Min-Ones CSPs mentioned above.

Generic frameworks extending Baker’s technique include
the bidimensionality theory of Demaine, Fomin, Hajiaghayi, and Thilikos
[
21
]
and its application in the design of PTASes by Demaine and Hajiaghayi
[
22
]
(which is however limited to minor-closed graph classes);
monotone FO problems on minor-closed graph classes by Dawar, Grohe, Kreutzer,
and Schweikardt
[
17
]
;
and the idea of Baker games, introduced by Dvořák
[
30
]
(see also
[
34
]
).
The latter gives conditions stronger than fractional-
\tw
-fragility,
but useful for problems beyond Max-CSPs,
and achievable for all examples known to be fractionally fragile.
The work of Dvořák and Lahiri
[
33
]
, which appeared after the
present paper, gives a PTAS
on fractionally-
\tw
-fragile classes of graphs
for problems incomparable with Max-CSPs, namely monotone maximisation
problems expressible in terms of distances.

De la Vega and Karpinski
[
19
,
20
]
extended the dense approach to subdense cases (
\Omega(\frac{n^{2}}{\log n})
edges) for specific problems such as MaxCut and Max-2-SAT.
In contrast, they show that Max-Cut on graphs with
\Omega(n^{2-\delta})
edges is hard to approximate, for any
\delta>0
.

The best known approximation algorithm for general
\mbox{Max-}2\mbox{-CSPs}
is due to Charikar,
Hajiaghayi, and Karloff
[
13
]
and achieves an
approximation factor of
\mathcal{O}((nq)^{1/3})
, where
n

CSPs have also been extensively studied for fixed constraint types, i.e.,

In Section

2.1

A

\sigma

We say that a class of structures has

Note that a class of
\sigma
-structures (that is, structures over a fixed

We denote by

For sets

For

\sigma
-structure with domain

Given a

For

\sigma
-structures

The

3
3

3

While called maximum

Note that when seen as a Max-CSP instance, the domain of the left-hand side structure

.

Let
\sigma=\{f\}

2.2
Overcasts

Before we define pliability formally, it is useful to consider the following relation.
The starting point of all our results is the equivalence of this relation to a more combinatorial notion: the existence of a certain fractional cover, which we shall call an
overcast
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be
\sigma
-structures.
We say that
\mathbb{A}

overcasts

\mathbb{B}
, denoted
\mathbb{A}\mathbin{\succeq}\mathbb{B}
if, for all
\sigma
-structures
\mathbb{C}
,
we have that
\mathrm{opt}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{C})\ \geq\ \mathrm{opt}(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{C})
.

A distribution over a finite set
U
is a function
\pi\colon U\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
such that
\sum_{x\in U}\pi(x)=1
.
We write
\EX_{x\sim\pi}f(x)
for
\sum_{x\in U}\pi(x)\cdot f(x)
and
\Pr_{x\sim\pi}[\phi(x)]
for
\EX_{x\sim\pi}[\phi(x)]
, where
[\phi(x)]
is 1 if
x
satisfies the predicate
\phi
and 0 otherwise.

Given a map
g:A\to B
and a tuple
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in A^{m}
, we write

g(\mathbf{x})
for
(g(x_{1}),\ldots,g(x_{m}))
; i.e., we apply
g
componentwise on

\mathbf{x}
. Hence,
g^{-1}(\mathbf{y})=\{\mathbf{x}\mid g(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{y}\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be
\sigma
-structures.
An
overcast
from
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{B}
is a distribution
\omega
over
B^{A}

such that for each
(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{B})
we have that

\EX_{g\sim\omega}f^{\mathbb{A}}(g^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))\ \geq\ f^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x}).

Here
f^{\mathbb{A}}(g^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))
denotes the sum of
f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{y})
over
\mathbf{y}\in g^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\subseteq A^{\ar(f)}
.

Intuitively, an overcast from
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{B}
is a random function from
A
to
B
such that for each edge (tuple) in
\mathbb{B}
, its preimage has larger expected weight (value). In other words, each edge must be covered by at least its own weight, in expectation.
The following is a consequence of Farkas’ lemma, as shown in Appendix
A
.
5
5

5

The definitions of the
\mathbin{\succeq}
relation and of an overcast are analogous to
the “improvement” relation and “inverse fractional homomorphisms”
from
[
11
]
. Here, however,
\mathrm{opt}(
) is maximising, not
minimising, so inequalities in definitions are swapped. This has consequences
such as the fact that mappings in the support of an overcast are in general
not homomorphisms (mapping non-zero tuples to non-zero tuples), unlike for
inverse fractional homomorphisms. The proof of Proposition
2.5

nevertheless is identical to the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 3.6]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

\mathbb{A}\mathbin{\succeq}\mathbb{B}
if and only if there is an overcast from
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{B}
.

2.3
Pliability and graph parameters

Our definition of pliability involves a notion of distance which may be of independent interest.
It quantifies the
relative
difference between two structures (as measured from the right by weighted multicut densities, in the language of Lovász’s book on graph limits
[
66
, Ch. 12]
).

Definition 2.6
.

The
opt-distance
between two structures with the same signature is defined as:

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}):=\textstyle\sup_{\mathbb{C}}\left|\ln\mathrm{opt}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{C})-\ln\mathrm{opt}(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{C})\right|.

Here
\ln 0=-\infty
and
\left|\ln 0-\ln 0\right|=0
.
Equivalently, we can compare rescaled structures; by definition of
\succeq

and the fact that
\mathrm{opt}(\lambda\mathbb{A},\mathbb{C})=\lambda\mathrm{opt}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{C})
, we have:

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})=\inf\big\{\varepsilon\bigm|\mathbb{A}\,\mathbin{\succeq}\,e^{-\varepsilon}\,\mathbb{B}\ \text{ and }\ \mathbb{B}\,\mathbin{\succeq}\,e^{-\varepsilon}\,\mathbb{A}\,\big\}.

One may think of
e^{\pm\varepsilon}
as close to
1\pm\varepsilon
. Formally

\textstyle 1-\varepsilon\ \leq\ e^{-\varepsilon}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\ =\ 1-\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
for
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Note that the first definition readily implies that opt-distance satisfies the triangle inequality (it defines a pseudometric).

Finally, a class is treewidth-pliable if it is uniformly close to structures of bounded treewidth:

Definition 2.7
.

A class of structures
\mathcal{A}
is
\param
-
pliable
with respect to a graph parameter
\param
if
for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is
k=k(\varepsilon)
such that
for every
\sigma
-structure
\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}
there is a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbb{B}
with
\param(\mathbb{B})\leq k
and
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon
.

Thus to show
\tw
-pliability of various classes, we will construct overcasts from structures
\mathbb{A}
in the class to
(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{B}
, for some
\mathbb{B}
of bounded treewidth, and from
\mathbb{B}
back to
(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{A}
.

Given a graph
G
, we will consider pliability for the following graph parameters:

•

\size(G)=|V(G)|
– the number of vertices of
G
,

•

\cc(G)
– the maximum size of a connected component of
G
,

•

treedepth
\td(G)
, which is a parameter due to Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez
[
74
]
, whose definition we recall below,

•

treewidth
\tw(G)
,
6
6

6

We refer
to
[
25
]
for the standard definitions of treewidth, pathwidth and minors.

•

and finally the Hadwiger number
\text{Hadwiger}(G)
, which is the maximum
k
such that

K_{k}
is a minor of
G
.
We use the Hadwiger number as an example of a broader “sparsity” parameter:
for example, all planar graphs have Hadwiger number at most 4 (yet unbounded treewidth).

Definition 2.8
.

The
treedepth

\td(G)
of a graph
G
is defined recursively as:

•

\ \max\limits_{i}\ \ \td(G_{i})
, if
G
is disconnected with components
G_{i}
;

•

\min\limits_{v\in V(G)}\td(G-v)+1
, if
G
is connected and has more than one vertex;

•

1
, if
G
has one vertex.

An equivalent definition is as follows:
a
treedepth decomposition
of a graph
G
is a rooted forest
T
(a disjoint union of rooted trees) with
V(T)=V(G)
such that for each
uv\in E(G)
,
u
is an ancestor or descendant of
v
in
T
.
In other words,
G
is a subgraph of the transitive closure of a forest
T
directed towards roots.
The treedepth of
G
is equal to the minimum depth among all such decompositions of
G
.
Treedepth is a rather strict parameter: for example, stars have treedepth 2, but paths already have unbounded treedepth.
In fact, a short proof shows that a class of graphs has bounded treedepth if and only if the length of the longest path is bounded
[
74
]
.

Bounded size implies bounded
\cc{}
implies bounded
\td{}
implies bounded pathwidth (
\pw
) implies bounded
\tw
implies bounded Hadwiger number, more precisely:

\text{Hadwiger}(G)-1\leq\tw(G)\leq\pw(G)\leq\td(G)-1\text{ and }\td(G)\leq\cc(G)\leq\size(G).

Moreover, we also have the following inequality (less useful, because of the dependency on
G
):

\td(G)\leq(\tw(G)+1)\cdot\log_{2}|G|.

All these parameters are
monotone
, that is,
\param(H)\leq\param(G)
for a subgraph
H
of a graph
G
.
Their boundedness implies bounded average degree
\frac{2|E(G)|}{|V(G)|}
.
More precisely,
\frac{2|E(G)|}{|V(G)|}\leq 2\tw(G)
(because a graph of treewidth
k
has a vertex of degree at most
k
);
Kostochka
[
59
]
proved
\frac{2|E(G)|}{|V(G)|}\leq\mathcal{O}(h\sqrt{h})
where
h=\text{Hadwiger}(G)
.

The size of a structure
\mathbb{A}
is the number of vertices of its Gaifman
graph:
\size(\mathbb{A})=|V(\Gaifman(\mathbb{A}))|
. The other graph
parameters are also defined in terms of the same parameter
on the Gaifman graph of the structure; e.g., the treewidth of a structure
\mathbb{A}
is the treewidth of its Gaifman
graph:
\tw(\mathbb{A})=\tw(\Gaifman(\mathbb{A}))
.
In particular, since the edges of
\Gaifman(\mathbb{A})
come only from
tuples in
\mathbb{A}
of non-zero weight, a rescaled structure
\lambda\mathbb{A}
, for

\lambda>0
, has
\Gaifman(\lambda\mathbb{A})=\Gaifman(\mathbb{A})
so the
parameters we consider do not change by rescaling; for
\lambda=0
,

\Gaifman(\lambda\mathbb{A})
has no edges.

We will often prove the easy directions in various characterisations via the
following observation that follows from the definition of pliability:

Observation 2.9
.

If two graph parameters
\param
and
\param^{\prime}
satisfy
\param\leq\param^{\prime}
then

\param^{\prime}
-pliability implies
\param
-pliability.

2.4
Opt-distance zero and edit-distance

In this section we define our relative version of edit distance
and prove it upper-bounds opt-distance.
We define the
edit distance

\dedit(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
between two valued
\sigma
-structures
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
to be

\dedit(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}):=\min_{\text{bij. }\phi\colon A\to B}\sum_{f\in\sigma}\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in A^{\ar(f)}}\left|f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x})-f^{\mathbb{B}}(\phi(\mathbf{x}))\right|}{\min(\|\mathbb{A}_{f}\|_{1},\|\mathbb{B}_{f}\|_{1})}.

Here
\mathbb{A}_{f}
denotes the structure
\mathbb{A}
limited to the signature
\{f\}
,
so
\|\mathbb{A}_{f}\|_{1}
denotes
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in A^{\ar(f)}}f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x})
.
The following generalises the notion of “looplessness” in graphs.

Definition 2.10
.

A
\sigma
-structure
\mathbb{A}
is
loopless
if no tuple has a repetition.
That is, for
(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{A})
with
f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x})>0
,

\mathbf{x}
consists of
\ar(f)
different elements of

A
.
7
7

7

Equivalently, the Gaifman graph of
\mathbb{A}
is loopless.

Lemma 2.11
.

For loopless structures, the opt-distance is bounded linearly by the edit
distance:

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}\leq C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit,

where
C_{\sigma}=\max_{f\in\sigma}\ar(f)^{\ar(f)}
.

Proof.

Let
\dedit=\dedit(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
and
let
\phi\colon A\to B
be a bijection minimising the expression in its definition.
We will show that
e^{C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit}\mathbb{A}\succeq(1+C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit)\mathbb{A}\succeq\mathbb{B}
.
Symmetrically,
e^{C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit}\mathbb{B}\succeq\mathbb{A}
,
hence
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}\leq C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit
, which will conclude our claim.

Observe that for
f\in\sigma

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in A^{\ar(f)}}\left|f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x})-f^{\mathbb{B}}(\phi(\mathbf{x}))\right|=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in B^{\ar(f)}}\left|f^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x})-f^{\mathbb{A}}(\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))\right|.

Let
\delta:=\frac{C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit}{1+C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit}
, so
1-\delta=\frac{1}{1+C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit}
.
To show
(1+C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit)\mathbb{A}\succeq\mathbb{B}
,
we construct an overcast
\omega
from
\mathbb{A}
to
(1-\delta)\mathbb{B}
as follows.
With probability
(1-\delta)
we map
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{B}
with
\phi
;
with probability
\delta
we choose a tuple
(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{B})
at random
with probability proportional to its contribution in
d_{1}
, that is,

\frac{|f^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x})-f^{\mathbb{A}}(\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))|}{\min(\|\mathbb{A}_{f}\|_{1},\|\mathbb{B}_{f}\|_{1})}\cdot\frac{1}{d_{1}}
,
and we map all of
\mathbb{A}
uniformly at random to the elements of this tuple. That is, after choosing
(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{B})
, each tuple of
\mathbb{A}_{f}
gets mapped into
\mathbf{x}
with probability
\frac{1}{\ar(f)^{\ar(f)}}
(assuming
\mathbb{A}
is loopless).
Therefore, for each
(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{B})
:

\sum_{g\in B^{A}}\omega(g)\cdot f^{\mathbb{A}}(g^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))\geq(1-\delta)\cdot f^{\mathbb{A}}(\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))+\delta\cdot\frac{|f^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x})-f^{\mathbb{A}}(\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))|}{\min(\|\mathbb{A}_{f}\|_{1},\|\mathbb{B}_{f}\|_{1})}\cdot\frac{1}{d_{1}}\cdot\frac{\|\mathbb{A}_{f}\|_{1}}{\ar(f)^{\ar(f)}}

\geq(1-\delta)f^{\mathbb{A}}(\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))+\frac{\delta}{C_{\sigma}\cdot d_{1}}\cdot|f^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x})-f^{\mathbb{A}}(\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))|\geq(1-\delta)f^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x}),

where the last inequality follows from
\frac{\delta}{C_{\sigma}\dedit}=\frac{1}{1+C_{\sigma}\dedit}=1-\delta
.
This shows that
\omega
is indeed an overcast that certifies
\mathbb{A}\succeq(1-\delta)\mathbb{B}
.
∎

Observation 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\tw
-pliable class.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a class of structures such that
for every
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{B}
there is an
\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}

with
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{A})\leq f(\tw(\mathbb{B}))
,
for some function
f(n)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
.
Then
\mathcal{B}
is
\tw
-pliable.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is
\tw
-pliable, for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a
k=k(\varepsilon)

such that every structure
\mathbb{A}
in
\mathcal{A}
is
\varepsilon
-close
to some structure of
\tw\leq k(\varepsilon)
.
To show that
\mathcal{B}
is
\tw
-pliable, consider any
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
n_{\varepsilon}
be large enough so that
f(n)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for
n\geq n_{\varepsilon}
.
Then for
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{B}
,
either
\tw(\mathbb{B})\leq n_{\varepsilon}

or
\mathbb{B}
is
f(\tw(\mathbb{B}))\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
-close
to some structure
\mathbb{A}\in A
,
which in turn is
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
-close
to some structure of treewidth at most
k(\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
.
In either case
\mathbb{B}
is
\varepsilon
-close to a structure of treewidth
at most
\max(n_{\varepsilon},k(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}))
.
∎

2.5
Pliable examples

We give simple observations and examples: classes that are sufficiently close to
pliable classes (in edit or opt-distance) are themselves pliable.
We first consider consider simple examples with a fixed signature: graphs.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\tw
-pliable class of graphs.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a class of graphs such that
every
H\in\mathcal{B}
can be obtained from some
G\in\mathcal{A}

by adding or removing
f(|E(H)|)
edges,
for some function
f(m)\in o(m)
.
Then
\mathcal{B}
is
\tw
-pliable.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.11
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(H,G)\leq 4\dedit(H,G)\leq\frac{f(|E(H)|)}{|E(H)|-f(|E(H)|)}=f^{\prime}(|E(H)|)

for some function
f^{\prime}(n)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
.
This function can be upper-bounded by a monotonic function
f^{\prime\prime}
decreasing to 0,
say
f^{\prime\prime}(x)\vcentcolon=\sup_{n\geq x}f^{\prime}(n)
.
Since
|E(H)|\geq\tw(H)
, we conclude
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(H,G)\leq f^{\prime\prime}(|E(H)|)\leq f^{\prime\prime}(\tw(H))
.
The claim follows by Observation
2.12
.
∎

Other simple examples arise from considering structures at opt-distance zero. This is a valued analogue of being homomorphically equivalent (see also
valued cores
in
[
11
]
).

Example 2.14
.

For every non-empty bipartite graph
G
,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(G,\lambda K_{2})=0
, for
\lambda=|E(G)|
.
Therefore, since
\{\lambda K_{2}\colon\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\}
is trivially
\tw
-pliable,
every class of bipartite graphs is
\tw
-pliable.

Proof.

A bipartite graph
G
admits a homomorphism
h
to
K_{2}
.
This gives an overcast showing
G\mathbin{\succeq}\lambda K_{2}
: always map everything according to
h
.
Conversely, mapping
\lambda K_{2}
uniformly at random to edges of
G
gives an overcast showing
\lambda K_{2}\mathbin{\succeq}G
.
∎

Example 2.15
.

Let
G
be a 3-colourable graph such that every edge of
G
occurs in exactly one triangle.
Then
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(G,\lambda K_{3})=0
for
\lambda=|E(G)|/3
. Hence the class of all such graphs is
\tw
-pliable.

Proof.

A 3-colouring of
G
corresponds to a homomorphism
h
to
K_{3}
.
Composing
h
with a random rotation of
K_{3}
gives an overcast from
G
to
\lambda K_{3}
.
Conversely, mapping
\lambda K_{3}
to a uniformly random triangle in
G

covers each edge with probability
\frac{1}{\lambda}
,
giving an overcast from
\lambda K_{3}
to
G
.
∎

The above idea also implies that our results cannot be extended to
finding
solutions.
This is analogous to the hardness of finding a 3-colouring of a graph that is homomorphically equivalent to
K_{3}
.

Example 2.16
.

There is a class of weighted graphs
\mathcal{A}
that is
\tw
-pliable, yet for some
\varepsilon>0
, there is no poly-time algorithm that finds a map
h\colon V(G)\to V(K_{3})
with
\val(h)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\mathrm{opt}(G,K_{3})
for
G\in\mathcal{A}
, unless
\text{P}=\text{NP}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the class of weighted graphs
G
satisfying
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(G,\lambda K_{3})=0
for
\lambda=\frac{\|G\|_{1}}{3}
.
Suppose there is an algorithm as above for each
\varepsilon>0
.
There are constants
\varepsilon_{0},d
such that it is NP-hard to distinguish 3-colourable graphs of maximum degree
d
from graphs where any map
h\colon V(G)\to V(K_{3})
miscolours more than an
\varepsilon_{0}
fraction of edges
[
49
]
.
We use our algorithm to solve the problem.
Given an instance
G
, let
G^{\prime}
be the weighted graph obtained by gluing a new triangle to every edge, and then assigning to every edge
e\in E(G^{\prime})
a weight
w(e)
equal to the number of triangles it occurs in.
Note
1\leq w(e)\leq d
.
Observe that if
G
was 3-colourable, then
G^{\prime}
would be as well, hence
\mathrm{opt}(G^{\prime},K_{3})=\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}
.
Moreover,
G^{\prime}
would be in
\mathcal{A}
by an argument similar as in Example
2.15
.
Hence running the algorithm on
G^{\prime}
, we would find a 3-colouring
h
which miscolours at most
\varepsilon\mathrm{opt}(G^{\prime},K_{3})=\varepsilon\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}
of the total weight
\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}
.
Since
w\geq 1
, it miscolours at most
\varepsilon\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}
edges.
Since
w\leq d
, this is at most
\varepsilon d|E(G^{\prime})|\leq 3\varepsilon d|E(G)|
.
Hence running the algorithm for
\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{3d}
would find a colouring of the original graph
G
that miscolours at most
\varepsilon_{0}|E(G)|
edges.
Therefore if we run this procedure for any
G
(regardless of its 3-colourability), then it either outputs a colouring as above, or we can conclude that
G
is not 3-colourable.
∎

2.6
Non-pliable examples

In this section we give examples of non-pliable classes.
In the process we show equivalent definitions of pliability (Lemmas
2.22
and
2.23
).

We will use the following bound (in this section and in the proof of
Lemma
8.3
in Section
8
):

\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda m}\binom{m}{i}\leq 2^{H(\lambda)m},

where
H(\lambda)
is a function which satisfies
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}H(\lambda)=0
; specifically, the binary entropy function
H(\lambda)=\lambda\log_{2}(\frac{1}{\lambda})-(1-\lambda)\log_{2}(1-\lambda)
.

Recall that by Lemma
1.4
, for a class of Gaifman graphs
\mathcal{G}
,
the class
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}
of all structures over
\mathcal{G}
is
\tw
-pliable if and only if

\mathcal{G}
is fractionally-
\tw
-fragile.
So the simplest examples of non-pliable classes are
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}
for some non-fractionally-fragile
\mathcal{G}
.
Fractional-
\tw
-fragility implies bounded expansion (a notion from the theory of sparse graphs introduced by Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez
[
75
]
) and sublinear separators, e.g., 3-regular expander graphs are not fractionally-
\tw
-fragile, see
[
29
]
.
Hence for
\mathcal{G}
the class of all 3-regular graphs,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}
is not
\tw
-pliable.

A somewhat more direct proof is to consider any class of 3-regular graphs of high girth.
Thomassen
[
87
]
showed that such graphs behave much like graphs of high average degree.
We use essentially the same proof below:

Lemma 2.17
.

For
\delta>0
and
g\in\mathbb{N}
,
every graph with average degree
\geq 2+\delta
and girth
\geq 3g

has a minor with average degree
\geq g\delta+2
.

Proof.

Let
G=(V,E)
.
Without loss of generality assume that
G
is connected (otherwise take the component with the largest average degree).
Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
be a partition of
V
into parts of size
|A_{i}|\geq g
that induce connected subgraphs,
with
m
maximum among such partitions (clearly one exists with
m=1
).

We claim that each set
A_{i}
induces a tree.
Indeed, consider any spanning tree
T
of
G[A_{i}]
and let
e
be an edge of
G[A_{i}]
outside of
T
.
Then
T+e
contains a unique cycle, which must have length
\geq 3g\geq 2g
.
Hence one can remove
e
and some other edge from this cycle to split it into two intervals with
\geq g
vertices.
Removing these two edges from
T+e
splits it into two components spanning
A_{i}
with
\geq g
vertices each.
Hence
A_{i}
could be replaced with the vertex sets of these two components, contradicting the choice of
m
.

Similarly, we claim that every two sets
A_{i},A_{j}
are connected by at most one edge.
Otherwise two such edges together with spanning trees of
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
would form a
unicyclic graph, which could be split as above into three connected parts with
\geq g
vertices each.

Let
G^{\prime}=(E^{\prime},V^{\prime})
be the graph resulting by contracting the sets
A_{i}
.
Since we contract sets of
\geq g
vertices,
|V^{\prime}|=m\leq\frac{|V|}{g}
.
Since no two edges get identified and no loop gets created/removed in the process,
the number of contractions is equal to
|E|-|E^{\prime}|
and to
|V|-|V^{\prime}|
.
Hence

|E^{\prime}|=|E|-|V|+|V^{\prime}|\geq(\frac{2+\delta}{2}-1)|V|+|V^{\prime}|\geq(g\frac{\delta}{2}+1)|V^{\prime}|
,
so
G^{\prime}
has average degree
\geq g\delta+2
.
(We note that each
G[A_{i}]
had diameter
<2g-1
, as otherwise it could be split into two parts;
hence the minor we obtain is relatively
shallow
).
∎

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\delta>0
and let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of graphs with unbounded girth
and average degree
\geq 2+\delta
.
Then
\mathcal{G}
is not fractionally-
\tw
-fragile.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is fractionally-
\tw
-fragile. Then, by Lemma
4.4
, for
\varepsilon=\frac{\delta}{2(2+\delta)}
there is a
k=k(\varepsilon)
such that every graph in
\mathcal{G}
has a subset
F\subseteq E(G)
with
|F|\leq\varepsilon|E(G)|
such that
\tw(G-F)\leq k
.
Let
G\in\mathcal{G}
be a graph with girth
\geq\frac{12k}{\delta}
.
Let
F
be as above.
Then
\tw(G-F)\leq k
and
2|E(G-F)|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\cdot 2|E(G)|\geq(1-\varepsilon)(2+\delta)|V(G)|=(2+\frac{\delta}{2})|V(G-F)|
.
Therefore,
G-F
has average degree
\geq(2+\frac{\delta}{2})
and girth
\geq 3\cdot\frac{4k}{\delta}
,
so by Lemma
2.17
it has a minor with average degree
\geq 2+\frac{4k}{\delta}\frac{\delta}{2}>2k
.
But a minor of
G-F
must have treewidth at most
\tw(G-F)\leq k
, so average degree
\leq 2k
, a contradiction.
∎

We now turn to classes of structures with a fixed signature
\sigma
.
We will show that the class of tournaments (orientations of complete graphs) is not
\tw
-pliable (or equivalently,
\size
-pliable, by Theorem
1.5
), in contrast to cliques and dense graphs (Example
7.2
and Theorem
1.3
).

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of graphs of unbounded average degree.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the class of (unweighted) orientations of graphs in
\mathcal{G}
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is not size-pliable.

In order to prove Lemma
2.19
, we will need some definitions (only for this subsection) and alternative characterisations of size-pliability.

Definition 2.20
.

For a graph parameter
\param
,
let
\bar{\param}
be the parameter defined as
\bar{\param}(G):=\max_{i}(\param(G_{i}))
,
where
G_{i}
are the connected components of
G
.

For example, if
\param
is
\size{}
, then
\bar{\param}
is
\cc
(max component size).
All the other parameters we consider (
\cc
,
\td
,
\tw
,
Hadwiger
) satisfy
\bar{\param}=\param
.

Definition 2.21
.

A parameter
\param
is
good
if

\bar{p}
-pliability is the same as size-pliability on classes of structures with bounded signatures.

We use this definition to state the next few lemmas in full generality.
Theorem
1.5
shows that the parameters
\size
,
\cc
,
\td
,
\tw
,
Hadwiger
are good.

For two
\sigma
-structures
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
and a function
g\colon A\to B
,
we define
\Img(g)
to be the
\sigma
-structure on
B

with
f^{\Img(g)}(\mathbf{x}):=\min\left(f^{\mathbb{A}}(g^{-1}(\mathbf{x})),f^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x})\right)
.
Note that
\Img(g)\subseteq\mathbb{B}
(meaning each tuple has value in
\Img(g)
less than or equal its value in
\mathbb{B}
).

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
\param
be a good parameter.
Then a class of
\sigma
-structures
\mathcal{A}
is size-pliable if and only if
\forall_{\varepsilon>0}\exists_{k}\forall\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}
there is an overcast
\omega
from
\mathbb{A}
to
(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{A}
such that every
g\colon A\to A
in its support has
\param(\Img(g))\leq k
.

Proof.

For one direction, suppose that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is an integer
k
such that all
\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}
have an overcast
\omega
from
\mathbb{A}
to
(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{A}
such that every
g:A\to A
in its support has
\param(\Img(g))\leq k
.
Then for these
\varepsilon,k,\mathbb{A}
we can take
\mathbb{B}
to be the disjoint union of rescaled structures
\mathbb{B}_{g}:=\omega(g)\Img(g)
.
We have
\bar{\param}(\mathbb{B})\leq k
.
The overcast
\omega
naturally induces overcasts showing
\mathbb{A}\mathbin{\succeq}\mathbb{B}\mathbin{\succeq}(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{A}
.
Namely, we can define an overcast
\omega^{\prime}
from
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{B}
by letting
\omega^{\prime}(g^{\prime})=\omega(g)
for
g^{\prime}
mapping
A
to
B_{g}\subseteq B
just as
g
maps
A
to
\Img(g)\subseteq\mathbb{A}
.
We can also define an overcast
\omega^{\prime\prime}
from
\mathbb{B}
to
(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{A}
by letting
\omega^{\prime\prime}(g^{\prime\prime})=1
for one function
g^{\prime\prime}
mapping each
\mathbb{B}_{g}\subseteq\mathbb{B}
to
\Img(g)\subseteq\mathbb{A}
.
Hence
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
(recall
1\pm\varepsilon
is close to
e^{\pm\varepsilon}
), which concludes the proof that
\mathcal{A}
is
\bar{\param}
-pliable.
Since we assume that
\param
is a good parameter,
\mathcal{A}
is size-pliable.

In the other direction, suppose
\mathcal{A}
is size-pliable, meaning for every
\varepsilon>0
there is an integer
k
such that all
\mathbb{A}
have a
\mathbb{B}
with
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon
and
|B|\leq k
.
This means there are overcast
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
showing
\mathbb{A}\mathbin{\succeq}e^{-\varepsilon}\mathbb{B}
and
\mathbb{B}\mathbin{\succeq}e^{-\varepsilon}\mathbb{A}
, respectively.
Then composing
\omega
with
\omega^{\prime}
gives an overcast from
\mathbb{A}
to
(1-2\varepsilon)\mathbb{A}
(since
e^{-2\varepsilon}\geq 1-2\varepsilon
), with the property that all images of functions
g
in the support are of size at most
|B|\leq k
, which implies
\param(\Img(g))
is bounded by some function of
k
(namely
\max\param(H)
over all
k
-vertex graphs
H
).
∎

We can now use Farkas’ lemma to deduce another equivalent formulation:

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
\param
be a good parameter.
Then a class of
\sigma
-structures
\mathcal{A}
is
not
size-pliable if
and only if
\exists_{\varepsilon>0}\forall_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
there is a pair of
\sigma
-structures
\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\mathbb{C}
with
C=A
, such that for every
g\colon A\to C
with
\param(\Img(g))\leq k
,
\val(g)<(1-\varepsilon)\val(\id)
. (Here
\id
is the identity map from
A
to
C=A
).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.22
,
\mathbb{A}
is not size-pliable if and only if
\exists_{\varepsilon>0}\forall_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

the following LP over variables
\{\omega(g)\colon g\in V\}
, where
V:=\{g\in A^{A}\colon\param(\Img(g))\leq k\}
, has no non-negative rational solution:

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in V}\omega(g)f^{\mathbb{A}}(g^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))\geq(1-\varepsilon)f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\forall(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{A})

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in V}\omega(g)=1

By applying Lemma
A.2
, this is equivalent to the existence of a non-negative vector
(y(f,\mathbf{x}))_{(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{A})}
such that

\sum_{(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{A})}y(f,\mathbf{x})f^{\mathbb{A}}(g^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))<(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{A})}y(f,\mathbf{x})f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x})\quad\quad\quad\forall g\in V

Let
\mathbb{C}
be the
\sigma
-structure on
C=A
with
f^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbf{x}):=y(f,\mathbf{x})
.
Then the above inequality is restated as follows (interpreting
g\in V
and
\id
as maps from
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{C}
):

\val(g)<(1-\varepsilon)\val(\id)\quad\quad\quad\forall{g\in V}.\qed

Remark 2.24
.

The structures
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{C}
obtained above can be assumed to satisfy
\Gaifman(\mathbb{A})=\Gaifman(\mathbb{C})
without loss of generality, because for any
(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{A})
such that one of
f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x})
or
f^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbf{x})
is zero, decreasing the other to zero will not change
\val(\id)
and can only decrease
\val(g)
.

Proof of Lemma
2.19
.

Let
\varepsilon
be a constant to be chosen later (
\frac{1}{10}
will do).
Given any
k
, let
G\in\mathcal{G}
be a graph with
m\geq 20\cdot\binom{k}{2}
edges,
n
vertices, and average degree
\frac{2m}{n}\geq 100\log_{2}k
.
Let
\mathbb{A}
be a random orientation of
G
(each edge is independently oriented in either direction with probability
\frac{1}{2}
).
We claim that with positive probability
\mathbb{A}
admits no map
g\colon A\to A
to itself with image of size at most
k
such that
\val(g)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\val(\id)
.
This will prove that
\mathcal{A}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
2.23
and hence is not size-pliable.

If a map as above existed, it would imply the existence of an oriented graph
D
(with at most one arc between every two vertices) on at most
k
vertices and a function
g\colon A\to V(D)

with
\val(g)\geq(1-\varepsilon)m
.
Observe that
\val(g)
is the number of arcs of
\mathbb{A}
that are correctly
mapped by
g
(i.e., to an arc of
D
with the same orientation).
Hence there would be a set
F
of at most
\varepsilon m
arcs of
\mathbb{A}
such that

g
maps all arcs of
\mathbb{A}-F
correctly.
Let us bound the probability that there exists such
D,F,g
.
The number of possible
D
is
\leq 3^{\binom{k}{2}}
;
the number of possible
F
is
\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\varepsilon m}\binom{m}{i}\leq 2^{H(\varepsilon)m}
;
the number of possible
g
is
\leq k^{n}
.
Note that
\frac{2m}{n}\geq 100\log_{2}k
and
3^{\binom{k}{2}}\leq 2^{m/10}
by our choice of
G
.
For fixed
D,F,g
, the probability that
g
maps all arcs of
\mathbb{A}-F
correctly to
D

is at most
(\frac{1}{2})^{(1-\varepsilon)m}
.
Hence in total the probability that some such
D,F,g
exist is at most

3^{\binom{k}{2}}\cdot 2^{H(\varepsilon)m}\cdot k^{n}\cdot 2^{-(1-\varepsilon)m}\leq 2^{n\log_{2}k-(1-\varepsilon-H(\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{10})m}\leq 2^{-(1-\varepsilon-H(\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{10}-\frac{1}{50})\cdot m}.

This is less than 1 for
\varepsilon
small enough so that
1-\varepsilon-H(\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{10}-\frac{1}{50}>0
. ∎

Finally, not all classes of bounded degree give pliable classes, even with a fixed signature.

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of graphs with unbounded girth and average degree
\geq 2+\delta
(
\delta>0
).
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the class of (unweighted) orientations of graphs in
\mathcal{G}
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is not size-pliable.

Proof.

We show there exists an
\varepsilon
such that for all
k
, there is an orientation
\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}
of a graph in
\mathcal{G}
such that every function
g\colon\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}
with
\cc(\Img(g))\leq k
has
\val(g)<(1-\varepsilon)\val(\id)
.
We choose
\varepsilon
later depending on
\delta
only.

For any given
k
, let
G\in\mathcal{G}
be a graph of girth
>k
.
Let
m=|E(G)|
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a random orientation of
G
:
every edge is independently oriented in one direction or the other.
We claim that the probability that there exists a
g\colon\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}
with
\cc(\Img(g))\leq k
and
\val(g)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\val(\id)
is strictly less than one
(so there exists an orientation that satisfies our goal).
Note that
\val(\id)=m
and
\val(g)
is the number of arcs in
\mathbb{A}
that are mapped correctly (to an arc in
\mathbb{A}
with the same orientation);
moreover, since the graph underlying
\mathbb{A}
has girth
>k
and
\cc(\Img(g))\leq k
,

g
must map into an oriented forest (disjoint union of trees).
So the event is equivalent to the following: there exists a set
F\subseteq E(G)

with
|F|\leq\varepsilon m
and a function
g\colon\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}
which maps all arcs of
\mathbb{A}-F
correctly into an oriented forest in
\mathbb{A}
.

The probability of this event can be union-bounded by the sum over
F\subseteq E(G)
with
|F|\leq\varepsilon m
of the probability that all of
\mathbb{A}-F
can be mapped correctly into a subdigraph.
The number of such
F
is
\sum_{i=0}^{\varepsilon m}\binom{m}{i}\leq 2^{H(\varepsilon)\cdot m}
;
It remains to bound, for a fixed
F
, the probability that
\mathbb{A}-F
can be mapped correctly.

Consider a fixed
F\subseteq E(G)
with
|F|\leq\varepsilon m
. If
\mathbb{A}-F
can be mapped correctly into an oriented forest in
\mathbb{A}

\param(G-F_{gg^{\prime}})\leq k
.

Let
e\in E(G)
. We claim that
\Pr\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\sim\omega\\
g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}\end{subarray}}[e\in F_{gg^{\prime}}]\leq\varepsilon
.
This holds essentially because the composition of
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
is an overcast from
\mathbb{A}
to
\exp(-\varepsilon)\cdot\mathbb{A}
and because the only edge with non-zero value of
f^{\mathbb{A}}_{e}
is
e
itself.
Formally, since
\omega
is an overcast, we have:

\text{for each }e_{B}\in E(\Gaifman(\mathbb{B}))\quad\quad\EX_{g\sim\omega}f^{\mathbb{A}}_{e}(g^{-1}(e_{B}))\geq\exp(-\textstyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2})f_{e}^{\mathbb{B}}(e_{B}).

Note that by construction of
\mathbb{A}
,
f^{\mathbb{A}}_{e}(g^{-1}(e_{B}))=[g(e)=e_{B}]
.
Hence for each
e_{B}\in E(\Gaifman(\mathbb{B}))
,

\EX_{g\sim\omega}[g(e)=e_{B}]\geq\exp(-\textstyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2})f_{e}^{\mathbb{B}}(e_{B}).

Moreover, since
\omega^{\prime}
is an overcast, we have:

\EX_{g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}}f^{\mathbb{B}}_{e}(g^{\prime-1}(e))\geq\exp(-\textstyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2})f_{e}^{\mathbb{A}}(e).

That is:

\EX_{g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{B}\in E(\Gaifman(\mathbb{B}))\\
g^{\prime}(e_{B})=e\end{subarray}}f^{\mathbb{B}}_{e}(e_{B})\geq\exp(-\textstyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}).

Putting the two together:

\displaystyle\Pr_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\sim\omega\\
g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}\end{subarray}}[e\not\in F_{gg^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\Pr_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\sim\omega\\
g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}\end{subarray}}[g^{\prime}(g(e))=e\text{ and }g(e)\in E(\Gaifman(\mathbb{B}))]

\displaystyle=\EX_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\sim\omega\\
g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{B}\in E(\Gaifman(\mathbb{B}))\\
g^{\prime}(e_{B})=e\end{subarray}}[g(e)=e_{B}]

\displaystyle\geq\EX_{g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{B}\in E(\Gaifman(\mathbb{B}))\\
g^{\prime}(e_{B})=e\end{subarray}}\exp(-\textstyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2})f_{e}^{\mathbb{B}}(e_{B})

\displaystyle\geq\exp(-\varepsilon)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Therefore, we obtained a distribution of edge sets
F_{gg^{\prime}}\subseteq E(G)
such that
\param(G-F_{gg^{\prime}})\leq k

satisfying
\Pr\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\sim\omega\\
g^{\prime}\sim\omega^{\prime}\end{subarray}}[e\in F_{gg^{\prime}}]\leq\varepsilon
.
This is an
\varepsilon
-thin fractional
(\param\leq k)
-edge-modulator.
∎

5
From Hadwiger- to size-pliability: proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
: pliability with respect to different parameters yields equivalent definitions.
The first half of Theorem
1.5
follows easily
from already established results and a simple observation, cf.
Section
5.1
.
The second half of Theorem
1.5
reduces to showing that structures of bounded treedepth with a bounded signature are
\size
-pliable.
The strategy for the proof is similar to a proof of Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez
[
74
, Corollary 3.3]
that relational structures of bounded treedepth have bounded cores.
However the argument is much more intricate due to the fact that we consider valued structures:
the statement that there are only finitely many structures of size at most
C
, for every
C
, is not true anymore.
The main difficulty is proving an approximate version of it.

5.1
Treewidth-, treedepth-, and Hadwiger-pliability

The first half of Theorem
1.5
, that is, the equivalence of

\param
-pliability for
\param\in\{\tw,\td,\text{Hadwiger}\}
, will follow (as
detailed in Corollary
5.2
below) from the equivalence of
fractional-
\param
-fragility for these parameters
(Theorem
4.5
), the fact that fragility implies pliability
(Lemma
4.6
), and transitivity of pliability, in the
following sense.

Observation 5.1

(Transitivity of pliability)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a class of structures with signatures from a set
\Sigma
.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is
\param
-pliable
and for each
k
,
\{\mathbb{A}\colon\param(\mathbb{A})\leq k\}
is
\param^{\prime}
-pliable,
where
\mathbb{A}
runs over all structures with signatures in
\Sigma
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is
\param^{\prime}
-pliable.

Proof.

Intuitively, this hold because
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}
is a pseudometric.
Formally, suppose a class
\mathcal{A}
is
\param
-pliable.
Then every
\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}
is
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
-close (in
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}
distance) to some
\mathbb{B}
with
\param(\mathbb{B})\leq k
(for some
k
depending on
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
).
By assumption, every
\mathbb{B}
with
\param(\mathbb{B})\leq k
is
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
-close to some
\mathbb{C}
with
\param^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})\leq k^{\prime}
(for some
k^{\prime}
depending on
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
k
).
Hence
\mathbb{A}
is
\varepsilon
-close to some structure
\mathbb{C}
with
\param^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})\leq k^{\prime}(\frac{\varepsilon}{2},k(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}))
, which only depends on
\varepsilon
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be any class of structures. The following are equivalent:

•

\mathcal{A}
is
\td
-pliable;

•

\mathcal{A}
is
\tw
-pliable;

•

\mathcal{A}
is
Hadwiger
-pliable.

Proof.

Since
\td(G)\geq\tw(G)+1\geq\text{Hadwiger}(G)
for any graph
G
, each
bullet point implies the next by Observation
2.9
.
It suffices to show that
Hadwiger
-pliability implies
\td
-pliability.
By Observation
5.1
it suffices to show that for every
k
,
the class
\mathcal{A}
of all structures with Hadwiger number at most
k
(and arbitrary signatures) is
\td
-pliable.
These are structures whose Gaifman graphs exclude the clique
K_{k+1}
as a minor.
Their Gaifman graphs are thus fractionally-
\td
-fragile by Theorem
4.5
.
Since their Gaifman graphs do not include cliques
K_{k+1}
the arity of symbols
with non-zero tuples is bounded by
k
.
By Lemma
4.6
, this implies that
\mathcal{A}
is
\td
-pliable
(high-arity symbols with no non-zero tuples can be ignored).
∎

5.2
From cc-pliability to size-pliability

To show the second half of Theorem
1.5
,
i.e., the equivalence of
\td
-pliability,
\cc
-pliability, and
\size
-pliability
(for structures with bounded signatures),
it will be easier to first focus on the latter two.
Since there are only finitely many distinct signatures of bounded size and arity,
we can focus on a single fixed signature (as finite unions of pliable classes are pliable).

Since
\cc\leq\size
,
by Observation
2.9
we have that
\size
-pliability implies

\cc
-pliability. The rest of this section is devoted to proving that

\cc
-pliability implies
\size
-pliability (for a single fixed signature).
This (and in fact equivalence of the two)
would be trivial if there were only a bounded number of distinct values of tuples,
since then there can be only a bounded number of components up to isomorphism,
and isomorphic components can be merged.

Observation 5.3
.

For any structure
\mathbb{A}
and numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
,
the disjoint union
\lambda_{1}\mathbb{A}\uplus\dots\uplus\lambda_{n}\mathbb{A}
is
equivalent (i.e., at
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}
-distance zero) to
\lambda\mathbb{A}
, where
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}
.

Proof.

An overcast in one direction deterministically maps each component
\lambda_{i}\mathbb{A}
to
\lambda\mathbb{A}
identically.
An overcast in the other direction maps
\lambda\mathbb{A}
to the component
\lambda_{i}\mathbb{A}
with probability
\lambda_{i}/\lambda
.
∎

For a structure
\mathbb{A}
with components of bounded size and
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-values,
we can try to change the values slightly to find a structure
\mathbb{B}
at small
edit distance which uses a bounded number of different values (and then proceed
as above). This works if the ratio of the maximum value to the minimum non-zero
value is bounded. If this ratio is large, we could try to change the extremely
small values to zero, hoping the edit distance is small (relative to the
extremely large values). However, this does not always work: consider structures

\mathbb{A}
with few large values and many small values (for example a structure
having
2^{i}
tuples of value
2^{n-i}
, for
i=0\dots n
). So the general case
cannot be reduced to the case of finitely many distinct values just by finding a
structure close in edit distance. Nevertheless, instead of requiring the
modified structure
\mathbb{B}
to have a bounded number of components up to
isomorphism, it suffices to require a bounded number of components up to
rescaling (two structures
\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2}
being the same up to rescaling
if
\mathbb{B}_{1}=\lambda\mathbb{B}_{2}
for some
\lambda>0
). This minor
weakening turns out to be sufficient to fix our problem. We formalise this first
as a statement on sequences of vectors of bounded dimension (which will encode a
sequence of components of bounded size).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N},\varepsilon>0
. There is a
k
such that for every sequence of vectors
v^{(1)},\dots,v^{(n)}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{d}
,
there is a sequence
w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(n)}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{d}
such that for each coordinate
i=1,\dots,d
,
{\sum_{j=1\dots n}|v^{(j)}_{i}-w^{(j)}_{i}|}\leq\varepsilon{\sum_{j=1\dots n}v^{(j)}_{i}}
, and such that up to rescaling, there are only
k
distinct vectors in
w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(n)}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
d
.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N},\varepsilon>0
and consider a sequence
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{d}
.
For
d=1
, the sequence already has only one vector up to rescaling
(or two, if it contains the zero vector), so let
d\geq 2
.

Let
J=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
For a subset
X\subseteq J
, denote
\textrm{mass}_{i}(X):=\sum_{j\in X}v^{(j)}_{i}
.
We focus on the first two coordinates and in particular the ratio of the second to the first.
For
c\in\mathbb{R}
, let
J_{<c}:=\{j\in J\colon v^{(j)}_{2}<c\cdot v^{(j)}_{1}\}
.
Define
J_{\leq c},J_{>c},J_{\geq c}
analogously.

Let
c
be maximum such that
\textrm{mass}_{2}(J_{<c})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\cdot\textrm{mass}_{2}(J)
.
For
j\in J_{<c}
, let
w_{j}
be the vector obtained from
v_{j}
by zeroing the 2nd coordinate.
The resulting difference is

\sum_{j\in J_{<c}}|v^{(j)}_{2}-w^{(j)}_{2}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{2}.

By maximality of
c
we have

\textrm{mass}_{2}(J_{\leq c})>\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\cdot\textrm{mass}_{2}(J).

Observe that the left hand side can be bounded as follows:

\textrm{mass}_{2}(J_{\leq c})\leq c\cdot\textrm{mass}_{1}(J_{\leq c})\leq c\cdot\textrm{mass}_{1}(J)

and similarly the right hand side can be bounded as follows, for
c^{\prime}:=c\cdot\frac{3d}{\varepsilon^{2}}
:

\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\cdot\textrm{mass}_{2}(J)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\cdot\textrm{mass}_{2}(J_{\geq c^{\prime}})\geq c\cdot\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\cdot\textrm{mass}_{1}(J_{\geq c^{\prime}}).

Altogether, this implies

c\cdot\textrm{mass}_{1}(J)>c\cdot\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\cdot\textrm{mass}_{1}(J_{\geq c^{\prime}}),

which after rearranging gives

\textrm{mass}_{1}(J_{\geq c^{\prime}})<\frac{\varepsilon}{d}\cdot\textrm{mass}_{1}(J).

For
j\in J_{\geq c^{\prime}}
, let
w_{j}
be the vector obtained from
v_{j}
by zeroing the 1st coordinate.
The resulting difference is
\sum_{j\in J_{\geq c^{\prime}}}|v^{(j)}_{1}-w^{(j)}_{1}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{d}\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{1}
.

The only remaining vectors, in
J_{mid}:=J\setminus(J_{<c}\cup J_{\geq c^{\prime}})
, satisfy
c\cdot v^{(j)}_{1}\leq v^{(j)}_{2}<c^{\prime}\cdot v^{(j)}_{1}
.
We can round down their 2nd coordinate to
c\cdot v^{(j)}_{1}
times an integer power of
e^{\varepsilon/3}
.
That is, for
j\in J_{mid}
, let
w_{j}
be the vector obtained from
v_{j}
by decreasing the 2nd coordinate to
w^{(j)}_{2}:=c\cdot e^{a\varepsilon/3}\cdot v^{(j)}_{1}
with
a\in\mathbb{N}
maximum such that
w^{(j)}_{2}\leq v^{(j)}_{2}
.
Observe that
a\geq 0
and since
c\cdot e^{a\varepsilon/3}\cdot v^{(j)}_{1}\leq v^{(j)}_{2}\leq c^{\prime}\cdot v^{(j)}_{1}
,
we have
e^{a\varepsilon/3}\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{c}=\frac{3d}{\varepsilon^{2}}
and thus
a\leq\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\cdot\ln(\frac{3d}{\varepsilon^{2}})
.
Note also that
1\geq\frac{w^{(j)}_{2}}{v^{(j)}_{2}}>e^{-\varepsilon/3}
, hence
\frac{|v^{(j)}_{2}-w^{(j)}_{2}|}{v^{(j)}_{2}}\leq 1-e^{-\varepsilon/3}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
, so the resulting difference is
\sum_{j\in J_{mid}}|v^{(j)}_{2}-w^{(j)}_{2}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{2}

To summarise, all vectors
w_{j}
satisfy
w_{j}\leq v_{j}
(coordinate-wise) and when limited to their first two coordinates as
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w^{(j)}_{1}\\
w^{(j)}_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, are either multiples of
(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
0\end{smallmatrix})
(if
j\in J_{<c}
), or multiples of
(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix})
(if
j\in J_{\geq c^{\prime}}
),
or multiples of
(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
c\cdot e^{a\varepsilon/3}\end{smallmatrix})
, for some
a\in\{0,1,\dots,K\}
for
K:=\lfloor\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\cdot\ln(\frac{3d}{\varepsilon^{2}})\rfloor
.
The resulting differences in the first and second coordinate, respectively, are bounded by as

\sum_{j\in J_{\geq c^{\prime}}}|v^{(j)}_{1}-w^{(j)}_{1}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{d}\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{1}

\sum_{j\in J_{<c}}|v^{(j)}_{2}-w^{(j)}_{2}|+\sum_{j\in J_{mid}}|v^{(j)}_{2}-w^{(j)}_{2}|\leq(\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3})\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{2}.

We replace the sequence
v^{(j)}
with the sequence
w^{(j)}
and repeat the same process for the 1st and
i
-th coordinate, for
i=3,\dots,d
.
Since each step only zeroes the 1st coordinate of some vectors and decreases the other coordinates,
the final resulting sequence
w^{(j)}
, when compared to the initial sequence
v^{(j)}
satisfies:

\sum_{j\in J}|v^{(j)}_{1}-w^{(j)}_{1}|\leq(d-1)\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{d}\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{1}

\sum_{j\in J}|v^{(j)}_{i}-w^{(j)}_{i}|\leq(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{i}\quad\mbox{for }i=2,\dots,d.

Each vector
w^{(j)}
either has its 1st coordinate zeroed, or all its other coordinates are determined as
w^{(j)}_{1}
times one of
2+K
possible ratios.
Among vectors with
w^{(j)}_{1}\neq 0
there are thus at most
(2+K)^{d-1}
different vectors, up to rescaling.
The vectors with
w^{(j)}_{1}=0
can be inductively reduced as
(d-1)
-dimensional vectors to
{w^{(j)}}^{\prime}
containing
k(d-1,\frac{\varepsilon}{3})
distinct vectors up to rescaling (where
k(d-1,\frac{\varepsilon}{3})
is the constant
k
obtained by inductive assumption for
d-1
and
\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
) and satisfying

\sum_{j\in J}|w^{(j)}_{i}-{w^{(j)}_{i}}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\sum_{j\in J}w^{(j)}_{i}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}_{i}.

Altogether, the difference is bounded by
\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon
and the number of distinct vectors up to rescaling is bounded by
(2+K)^{d-1}+k(d-1,\frac{\varepsilon}{3})
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For a fixed signature
\sigma
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
the class of
\sigma
-structures with maximum connected component size at most

d
is size-pliable.

Proof.

We simply present each component
\mathbb{A}_{i}
of
\mathbb{A}
as a vector encoding the value of all tuples
(f,\mathbf{x})\in\text{tup}(\mathbb{A}_{i})
.
The dimension of such a vector, for a component of size
d
, is
d^{\prime}:=\sum_{f\in\sigma}d^{\ar(f)}
.
Smaller components can be treated as components of size
d
by adding dummy vertices and tuples.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\varepsilon^{\prime}:=\frac{\varepsilon/C_{\sigma}}{1+\varepsilon/C_{\sigma}}
, where
C_{\sigma}=\max_{f\in\sigma}\ar(f)^{\ar(f)}
.
The previous lemma guarantees the existence of a number
k=k(\varepsilon^{\prime},d^{\prime})
such that for every
\sigma
-structure
\mathbb{A}
with
n
components of size at most
d
, the corresponding vectors
v^{(1)},\dots,v^{(n)}
are approximated by vectors
w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(n)}
such that there are at most
k
distinct vectors up to rescaling and such that, for
i=1\dots d^{\prime}
,

\ {\sum_{j=1\dots n}|v^{(j)}_{i}-w^{(j)}_{i}|}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}{\sum_{j=1\dots n}v^{(j)}_{i}}.

These vectors encode a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbb{B}
with only at most
k
distinct components up to rescaling, all of size at most
d
,
which is hence (by Observation
5.3
) equivalent to a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
bounded in size by
k\cdot d
.
Moreover, the guarantee on
\varepsilon^{\prime}
allows us to bound edit distance as follows:

\sum_{j=1\dots n}|v^{(j)}_{i}-w^{(j)}_{i}|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\sum_{j=1\dots n}v^{(j)}_{i}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\sum_{j=1\dots n}\big(\min(v^{(j)}_{i},w^{(j)}_{i})+|v^{(j)}_{i}-w^{(j)}_{i}|\big)

(for
i=1\dots d^{\prime}
), hence the edit distance (as defined in Section
2.4
) is

\dedit(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\leq\max_{i=1\dots d^{\prime}}\frac{\sum_{j=1\dots n}\left|v^{(j)}_{i}-w^{(j)}_{i}\right|}{\min(\sum_{j=1\dots n}v^{(j)}_{i},\sum_{j=1\dots n}w^{(j)}_{i})}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{\sigma}}.

By Lemma
2.11
,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}^{\prime})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{B}^{\prime})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\leq C_{\sigma}\cdot\dedit(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon
.
(While Lemma
2.11
assumes the structures to be loopless, this can be ensured by replacing tuples with repeated elements like
(f,(x_{1},x_{1},x_{2}))
, say, with
(f^{\prime},(x_{1},x_{2}))
for a new symbol
f^{\prime}
).
∎

By Observation
5.1
(transitivity of pliability), we
conclude that for a fixed signature
\sigma
, if a class of
\sigma
-structures

\mathcal{A}
is cc-pliable then it is also size-pliable.
Thus, we have shown equivalence of size-pliability and cc-pliability
(for structures of bounded signatures).

5.3
From treedepth-pliability to size-pliability

In order to finish the proof of Theorem
1.5
,
it remains to show the equivalence of
\td
- and
\size
-pliability.
We do this by extending the above proof for
\cc
- and
\size
-pliability.

One of the main reasons for which treedepth is useful (and easier to work
with than, say, treewidth) is that the only way for a graph of small treedepth
to be large is to have many repeating parts, like in a large star graph (see
e.g.
[
74
, Theorem 3.1]
). This implies that in a class of graphs of
bounded treedepth, homomorphic cores have bounded size. This does not extend
to weighted graphs or structures in general, but we can approximate the weights or values
as before.

Lemma 5.6
.

For a fixed signature
\sigma
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the class of
\sigma
-structures
\{\mathbb{A}\colon\td(\mathbb{A})\leq d\}
is size-pliable.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
d
that the statement holds for each signature
\sigma
.
It suffices to prove the statement for connected
\sigma
-structures of treedepth at most
d
. Indeed, this implies that disconnected
\sigma
-structures of treedepth at most
d
are

\cc
-pliable, which we already know implies
\size
-pliability.

For
d=1
, each component of the Gaifman graph is a single vertex and we are done.
So let
d>1
and assume that for each signature
\sigma
and each
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
k=k(d-1,\sigma,\varepsilon)
such that every
\sigma
-structure with treedepth
\leq d-1
has a
\sigma
-structure of size
\leq k
at opt-distance at most
\varepsilon
.
Let
\sigma
be a signature and
\mathbb{A}
a
\sigma
-structure of treedepth
d
.
Let
G
be the Gaifman graph of
\mathbb{A}
.
Since it is connected, we can find a vertex
v\in V(G)=A
such that
\td(G-v)=d-1
.

We now define a new signature
\sigma^{\prime}
and a
\sigma^{\prime}
-structure
\mathrm{pack}(\mathbb{A})
whose Gaifman graph will be
G-v
, but will contain all the information about
\mathbb{A}
.
Let
\sigma^{\prime}=\{(f,I)\colon f\in\sigma,I\subseteq\{1,\dots,\ar(f)\}\}
and
\ar((f,I)):=\ar(f)-|I|
, for
(f,I)\in\sigma^{\prime}
.
For
\mathbf{x}\in(A-v)^{\ar((f,I))}
, let
(f,I)^{\mathrm{pack}(\mathbb{A})}(\mathbf{x}):=f^{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{x^{\prime}})
where
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}\in A^{\ar(f)}
is the tuple obtained from
\mathbf{x}
by introducing
v
at positions
I
.
Note that
\sigma^{\prime}
is bounded:
|\sigma^{\prime}|=\sum_{f\in\sigma}2^{\ar(f)}
.

The
\sigma^{\prime}
-structure
\mathrm{pack}(\mathbb{A})
has treedepth
d-1
, so by inductive assumption there is a
\sigma^{\prime}
-structure
\mathbb{B}
at opt-distance at most
\varepsilon
with size at most
k=k(d-1,\sigma^{\prime},\varepsilon)
.
We define
\mathrm{unpack}(\mathbb{B})
to be the
\sigma
-structure with domain
B\cup\{v\}

and
f^{\mathrm{unpack}(\mathbb{B})}(\mathbf{x}):=(f,I)^{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x^{\prime}})
, where
I
is the set of positions in
\mathbf{x}
containing
v
and
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}
is the tuple obtained by removing these positions.
It is straightforward to check that
\mathrm{unpack}(\mathrm{pack}(\mathbb{A}))
is equal to
\mathbb{A}
and that for any
\sigma^{\prime}
-structures
\mathbb{A}^{\prime},\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
we have
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathrm{unpack}(\mathbb{A}^{\prime}),\mathrm{unpack}(\mathbb{B}^{\prime}))\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbb{A}^{\prime},\mathbb{B}^{\prime})
, hence

Theorem

It remains to prove their equivalence with the first bullet point.

Lemma 6.1

Let

\mathcal{G}

\cc

Proof.

Hyperfiniteness of a monotone class

\forall_{\varepsilon>0}\exists_{k}\forall_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\forall_{w\colon E(G)\to\{0,1\}}\exists_{F\subseteq E(G)}\ w(F)\leq\varepsilon w(E(G))\ \text{and}\ \cc(G-F)\leq k.

Hence it is trivially implied by the edge version of fractional-

It remains to show the other direction.

By definition of hyperfiniteness, for every

for all graphs

.
Observe that graphs in

We aim to show that

\forall_{\varepsilon>0}\exists_{k}\forall_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\forall_{w\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}}\exists_{F\subseteq E(G)}\ w(F)\leq\varepsilon w(E(G))\ \text{and}\ \cc(G-F)\leq k.

For

First, the notion can be extended from subsets
W_{1}\subseteq V_{1}
to any
function
f\colon V_{1}\to[0,1]
simply by considering subsets where the function takes at least a given value (here
\|f\|_{1}\vcentcolon=\sum_{x}f(x)
):

Observation 7.8
.

Let
G=(V_{1},V_{2},E)
be
\varepsilon
-homogeneous of density
d
.
Then for every
f\colon V_{1}\to[0,1]
and
g\colon V_{2}\to[0,1]
,

\sum\limits_{x_{1}\in V_{1}}\sum\limits_{x_{2}\in V_{2}}f(x_{1})g(x_{2})w_{G}(x_{1},x_{2})=d\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{1}\ \pm\ \varepsilon|V_{1}||V_{2}|.

Proof.

For
y\in[0,1]
, let
V_{1}^{y}:=\{x\in V_{1}\colon f(x)\geq y\}
and define
V_{2}^{y}
analogously for
g
.
Notice that
f(x)=\int_{0}^{1}[y\leq f(x)]\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}[x\in V_{1}^{y}]\,dy
.
Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1}\in V_{1}}\sum_{x_{2}\in V_{2}}f(x_{1})g(x_{2})w_{G}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{x_{1}\in V_{1}}\sum_{x_{2}\in V_{2}}[x_{1}\in V_{1}^{y_{1}}][x_{2}\in V_{2}^{y_{2}}]w_{G}(x_{1},x_{2})\,dy_{1}\,dy_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}w_{G}(V_{1}^{y_{1}},V_{2}^{y_{2}})\,dy_{1}\,dy_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\big(d|V_{1}^{y_{1}}||V_{2}^{y_{2}}|\pm\varepsilon|V_{1}||V_{2}|\big)\,dy_{1}\,dy_{2}=

\displaystyle d\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|V_{1}^{y_{1}}|\,dy_{1}\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|V_{2}^{y_{2}}|\,dy_{2}\Big)\pm\varepsilon|V_{1}||V_{2}|.

Since
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|V_{1}^{y}|dy=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{x\in V_{1}}[y\leq f(x)]dy=\sum_{x\in V_{1}}f(x)=\|f\|_{1}
and analogously for
g
, the claim follows.
∎

Second, while we cannot say much about any one fixed vertex, we can make similar approximations for most vertices:

Observation 7.9
.

Let
G=(V_{1},V_{2},E)
be
\varepsilon
-homogeneous with density
d
.
For every
g\colon V_{2}\to[0,1]
,
there are at least
(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})|V_{1}|
vertices
x_{1}
in
V_{1}
such that

\sum_{x_{2}}g(x_{2})w_{G}(x_{1},x_{2})=d\|g\|_{1}\ \pm\ \sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{2}|
.

Proof.

Let
W_{1}^{-}
be the set of those
x_{1}
in
V_{1}
for which the sum is too small:

\textstyle\sum_{x_{2}}g(x_{2})w_{G}(x_{1},x_{2})<d\|g\|_{1}-\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{2}|.

Let
f\colon V_{1}\to[0,1]
be the characteristic function of
W_{1}^{-}
.
Then

\textstyle\sum_{x_{1}}\sum_{x_{2}}f(x_{1})g(x_{2})w_{G}(x_{1},x_{2})<\|f\|_{1}\cdot\left(d\|g\|_{1}-\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{2}|\right).

By Observation
7.8
, this implies
\|f\|_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{2}|<\varepsilon|V_{1}||V_{2}|
.
Hence
|W_{1}^{-}|=\|f\|_{1}<\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{1}|
.
We can define and bound
W_{1}^{+}
analogously.
Then
V_{1}\setminus(W_{1}^{-}\cup W_{1}^{+})
is a set of size at least
(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})|V_{1}|
as claimed.
∎

The counting lemma says that the number of
\mathcal{P}
-maps that are homomorphisms from
F
to
G
is close to what one would expect in a purely random graph with the same densities.
Note that the number of all
\mathcal{P}
-maps
g\colon[k]\to V(G)
is exactly
\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|
.

Lemma 7.10

(Counting Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})
be an
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-partition of an
n
-vertex graph
G
.
Let
d_{ij}:=\frac{w_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}
.
For each
F\subseteq\binom{[k]}{2}
,

\sum_{g}\hom_{g}(F,G)=\Big(\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|\Big)\Big(\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\ \pm\ \varepsilon|F|\Big),

where the sum is over all
\mathcal{P}
-maps
g\colon[k]\to V(G)
.

Proof.

Let us write
\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}
as a shorthand for
\sum_{x_{1}\in V_{1}}\dots\sum_{x_{k}\in V_{k}}
.
We wish to approximate

\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}\prod_{ij\in F}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j}).

We do so by replacing each factor
w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})
by its average value
d_{ij}
, one by one.
That is, we prove for all subsets
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
by induction that

\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}\prod_{ij\in F}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})\quad=\quad\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}\prod_{ij\in F-F^{\prime}}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})\prod_{ij\in F^{\prime}}d_{ij}\quad\pm\quad|F^{\prime}|\cdot\varepsilon\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|.

(*)

Clearly this is true initially for
F^{\prime}=\emptyset
and eventually by reaching
F^{\prime}=F
we will have proved that

\begin{gathered}\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}\prod_{ij\in F}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})\quad=\quad\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\quad\pm\quad|F|\cdot\varepsilon\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|;\end{gathered}

which proves the claim, as
\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}1=\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|
.

To prove the induction step, suppose (
*
‣
7
) holds for some
F^{\prime}\subset F
and let
ab\in F-F^{\prime}
.
Let
w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})
denote
w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})
if
ij\not\in F^{\prime}
and
d_{ij}
otherwise.
Then the
left-hand-side
in (
*
‣
7
) is

\begin{gathered}\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}}\prod_{ij\in F}w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})=\\
\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}}h\sum_{x_{a}}\sum_{x_{b}}f(x_{a})g(x_{b})w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{a},x_{b}),\end{gathered}

where for any fixed choice of
(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}
, we let

\begin{gathered}h:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}ij\in F-F^{\prime}\\
i\neq a,j\neq b\end{subarray}}w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{i},x_{j}),\quad\quad f(x_{a}):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}ij\in F-F^{\prime}\\
i=a,j\neq b\end{subarray}}w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{a},x_{j}),\quad\quad g(x_{b}):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}ij\in F-F^{\prime}\\
i\neq a,j=b\end{subarray}}w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{i},x_{b}).\end{gathered}

Since
h,f,g\leq 1
, the claim then follows from Observation
7.8
:
replacing
w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})
with
d_{ab}
adds an error of at most

\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}}h\cdot\varepsilon|V_{a}||V_{b}|\leq\varepsilon|V_{a}||V_{b}|\cdot\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}}1=\varepsilon\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|
.
∎

Lemma 7.11

(Extension Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})
be an
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-partition of an
n
-vertex graph
G
.
Let
d_{ij}:=\frac{w_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}
.
For each
F\subseteq\binom{[k]}{2}
and each
ab\in F
,
all but
2k\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{a}||V_{b}|
edges
x_{a}x_{b}\in V_{a}\times V_{b}
satisfy

\sum_{g}\hom_{g}(F,G)=\Big(\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}|V_{i}|\Big)\cdot\Big(w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})\cdot\prod_{ij\in F-ab}d_{ij}\quad\pm\quad\sqrt{\varepsilon}|F|\Big)

where the sum is over all
\mathcal{P}
-maps
g\colon[k]\to V(G)
such that
g(a)=x_{a}
and
g(b)=x_{b}
.

Proof.

The argument is the same as in the counting lemma, except that edges incident to
a,b
have to be handled differently. First note that for every
c\in[k]-a-b
and every fixed
x_{b}\in V_{b}
, by
Observation
7.9
(with
g(x_{c}):=w_{G}(x_{c},x_{b})
), the
following holds for all but at most
2\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{a}|
vertices
x_{a}
in
V_{a}
:

\sum_{x_{c}}w_{G}(x_{a},x_{c})w_{G}(x_{c},x_{b})=d_{ac}\Big(\sum_{x_{c}}w_{G}(x_{c},x_{b})\Big)\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{c}|.

(**)

For each
c\in[k]-a-b
and each
x_{b}\in V_{b}
, we will ignore those edges going to
x_{a}\in V_{a}
that fail (
**
‣
7
).

Similarly for each
c\in[k]-a-b
, by Observation
7.9
(with
g(x_{c}):=1
)
the following holds for all but at most
2\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{b}|
vertices
x_{b}\in V_{b}
:

\sum_{x_{c}}w_{G}(x_{b},x_{c})=d_{bc}|V_{c}|\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{c}|.

(***)

We ignore all edges
x_{a}x_{b}\in E(V_{a},V_{b})
incident to
x_{b}
for which (
***
‣
7
) fails.
Thus for all but
\leq 2\cdot k\cdot|V_{b}|\cdot 2\sqrt{\varepsilon}|V_{a}|
edges
x_{a}x_{b}\in E(V_{a},V_{b})
, (
**
‣
7
) and (
***
‣
7
) hold for all
c\in[k]-a-b
.

Fix any such
x_{a}\in V_{a},x_{b}\in V_{b}
. We wish to approximate

\begin{gathered}\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}}\prod_{ij\in F}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})=\\
w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})\cdot\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}}\prod_{ij\in F-ab}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})=\dots{}\end{gathered}

Just as in the proof of the counting lemma, we replace factors
w_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})
by
d_{ij}
one by one.
We first do this for pairs in
F_{0}:=\{ij\in F\mid i,j\neq a,b\}
, since the argument works without change, incurring an error of
\pm\varepsilon|F_{0}|\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}|V_{i}|
(we denote this by
\simeq
for simplicity and sum up the errors at the end of the proof).
Since
d_{ij}
does not depend on the choice of
x_{i}\in V_{i},x_{j}\in V_{j}
, we can rearrange:

\dots\simeq w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})\Big(\prod_{ij\in F_{0}}d_{ij}\Big)\cdot\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}}\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}w_{G}(x_{a},x_{i})w_{G}(x_{i},x_{b})=\dots

Then, for each
c\in[k]-a-b
we can replace
ac
by isolating the factors that depend on
x_{c}
and applying (
**
‣
7
) (as before
w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{a},x_{i})
denotes either
w_{G}(x_{a},x_{i})
or
d_{ai}
depending on whether we have already replaced
ai
):

\dots=w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})\Big(\prod_{ij\in F_{0}}d_{ij}\Big)\cdot\sum_{x_{c}}w_{G}(x_{a},x_{c})w_{G}(x_{c},x_{b})\ \cdot\\
\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b-c}}\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b-c}w^{\prime}_{G}(x_{a},x_{i})w_{G}(x_{i},x_{b})\simeq\dots{}

Having thus replaced all edges
ac
for
c\in[k]-a-b
,
the only remaining edges are of the form
ib
for
i\in[k]-a-b
,
so by denoting
F_{1}:=\{ij\in F\mid i,j\neq b\}
the expression becomes:

\begin{gathered}\dots\simeq w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})\Big(\prod_{ij\in F_{1}}d_{ij}\Big)\cdot\sum_{(x_{i})_{i\in[k]-a-b}}\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{b})=\\
w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})\Big(\prod_{ij\in F_{1}}d_{ij}\Big)\cdot\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}\Big(\sum_{x_{i}\in V_{i}}w_{G}(x_{i},x_{b})\Big)\simeq\\
w_{G}(x_{a},x_{b})\Big(\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\Big)\cdot\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}|V_{i}|,\end{gathered}

where the last approximation follows from (
***
‣
7
).
For each of the
|F|
approximations used, the incurred additive error on the whole expression was at most
\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon}\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}|V_{i}|
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
7.7
.
The proof strategy was outlined above: map
G_{/\mathcal{P}}
to a random copy of
F
in
G
, where
F
marks heavy-enough edges of
G_{/\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof of Theorem
7.7
.

Let
G
be a graph with density
c:=\frac{E(G)}{n^{2}}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{0}<1
,
\frac{1}{k}\leq\frac{c}{10}\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}
and
\varepsilon\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{8k^{2}}
,
and suppose
G
has an
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-partition
\mathcal{P}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})
.
We claim that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(G,G_{/\mathcal{P}})\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.
As mentioned above,
G\mathbin{\succeq}G_{/\mathcal{P}}
holds trivially, so it remains to show an overcast from
G_{/\mathcal{P}}
to
e^{-\varepsilon_{0}}G
.

Let
d_{ij}:=\frac{w_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}
for
ij\in[k]^{2}
.
Let
F\subseteq\binom{[k]}{2}
be the set of edges
ij
such that
i\neq j
and
d_{ij}\geq\frac{1}{k}
.
Note that
\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\geq(\frac{1}{k})^{|F|}\geq(\frac{1}{k})^{k^{2}}\geq\varepsilon^{1/8}
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the subgraph of
G
obtained by removing:

•

E(G[V_{i}])
, for
i\in[k]
(the total weight removed in this step is
\leq k\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{2}
)

•

E_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})
, for
ij\not\in F
(their total weight is
\leq k^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{2}
)

•

edges of weight
<\varepsilon^{1/8}
(if
G
is
[0,1]
-weighted; their total weight is
\leq\varepsilon^{1/8}n^{2}
)

•

edges
x_{a}x_{b}\in V_{a}\times V_{b}
for which the Extension Lemma
7.11
does not hold, for each
ab\in F
(their total weight is
\leq|F|\cdot 2k\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{2}
).

The total weight of removed edges is

\|G\|_{1}-\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq n^{2}(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}+\varepsilon^{1/8}+2k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\leq n^{2}\cdot\frac{5}{k}.

By our assumption on
k
,
\frac{5}{ck}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}<1
.
Since
\|G\|_{1}\geq cn^{2}
,
\dedit(G,G^{\prime})\leq\frac{\|G\|_{1}-\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\min(\|G\|_{1},\|G^{\prime}\|_{1})}\leq\frac{n^{2}\cdot\frac{5}{k}}{n^{2}(c-\frac{5}{k})}=\frac{5/ck}{1-5/ck}\leq\varepsilon_{0}/2.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.11
,
G^{\prime}\mathbin{\succeq}e^{-\varepsilon_{0}/2}G
(this
direction requires only
G^{\prime}
to be loopless, which is true because we removed
E(G[V_{i}])
for all
i
).
Thus it remains to show that
G_{/\mathcal{P}}\mathbin{\succeq}e^{-\varepsilon_{0}/2}G^{\prime}
.

We define an overcast
\omega
from
G_{/\mathcal{P}}
to
(1-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon_{0}})G^{\prime}
as follows:
every
\mathcal{P}
-map
g\colon[k]\to V(G)
is taken with probability proportional to
\hom_{g}(F,G)
;
that is,
\omega(g):=\hom_{g}(F,G)/N
where by the Counting Lemma
7.10
, the normalisation factor is (using
\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\geq\varepsilon^{1/8}
):

N:=\sum_{g}\hom_{g}(F,G)=\Big(\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|\Big)\Big(\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\ \pm\ \varepsilon|F|\Big)\leq\Big(\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|\Big)\Big(\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\Big)(1+\varepsilon^{7/8}k^{2}).

To verify that
\omega
is indeed an overcast, we need to check that for each edge
uv
of
G^{\prime}

\EX_{g\sim\omega}w_{G_{/\mathcal{P}}}(g^{-1}(uv))\geq(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})w_{G}(u,v).

Let
a,b\in[k]
be such that
u\in V_{a}
and
v\in V_{b}
.
By the Extension Lemma
7.11
, since we removed from
G^{\prime}
edges
that do not satisfy it and edges with
w_{G}(u,v)<\varepsilon^{1/8}
, we have:

\begin{gathered}N\cdot\EX_{g\sim\omega}w_{G_{/\mathcal{P}}}(g^{-1}(uv))=\sum_{g\colon g(a)=u,g(b)=v}\hom_{g}(F,G)w_{G_{/\mathcal{P}}}(a,b)=\\
w_{G}(V_{a},V_{b})\sum_{g\colon g(a)=u,g(b)=v}\hom_{g}(F,G)=\\
d_{ab}|V_{a}||V_{b}|\Big(\prod_{i\in[k]-a-b}|V_{i}|\Big)\Big(w_{G}(u,v)\prod_{ij\in F-ab}d_{ij}\quad\pm\quad\sqrt{\varepsilon}|F|\Big)\geq\\
\Big(\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|\Big)\Big(w_{G}(u,v)\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\ -\ \sqrt{\varepsilon}k^{2}\Big)\geq\\
\Big(\prod_{i\in[k]}|V_{i}|\Big)w_{G}(u,v)\Big(\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\Big)(1-\varepsilon^{1/4}k^{2}).\end{gathered}

(The last inequality follows from
w_{G}(u,v)\cdot\prod_{ij\in F}d_{ij}\geq\varepsilon^{1/8}\cdot\varepsilon^{1/8}=\varepsilon^{1/4}
).
Dividing by the upper bound on
N
, we conclude:

\EX_{g\sim\omega}w_{G_{/\mathcal{P}}}(g^{-1}(uv))\geq w_{G}(u,v)\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/4}k^{2}}{1+\varepsilon^{7/8}k^{2}}

The ratio here can be bounded quite brutally:

\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/4}k^{2}}{1+\varepsilon^{1/4}k^{2}}\geq 1-2\varepsilon^{1/4}k^{2}\geq 1-\frac{2}{k}\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}\geq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{0}/2}\geq e^{-\varepsilon_{0}/2}.

This concludes the proof that
G_{/\mathcal{P}}\mathbin{\succeq}e^{-\varepsilon_{0}/2}G^{\prime}
and hence
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{opt}}(G_{/\mathcal{P}},G)\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.
∎

8
Hardness of approximation

We show that
\mbox{Max-Hom}(\mathcal{A},-)
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the class of all tournaments (orientations of cliques),
has no PTAS.
This holds under the
Gap Exponential Time Hypothesis
(Gap-ETH)
[
69
,
26
]

which states that no
2^{o(n)}
-time algorithm can distinguish between a satisfiable 3SAT formula and one which is not even
(1-\varepsilon)
-satisfiable for some constant
\varepsilon>0
.

In fact we only require the following weaker conjecture:

Conjecture 8.1
.

There exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
given a
\{0,1\}
-valued Max-2-CSP instance with
k
variables and alphabet size
n

no
f(k)\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time algorithm can distinguish between the following two cases:

•

there is an assignment satisfying every constraint;

•

no assignment satisfies more than
(1-\varepsilon)
constraints.

Gap-ETH implies Conjecture
8.1
: this follows from a proof by Chalermsook et al.
[
12
]
, in fact with a much larger approximation gap, which was further improved by Dinur and Manurangsi
[
28
]
.
Direct proofs for the above simpler version can be found in
[
65
]
and
[
9
, Appendix A]
.
Lokshtanov et al.
[
65
]
moreover propose the Parameterised Inapproximability Hypothesis, stating that the above promise problem is W[1]-hard.

The problem can be rephrased as a minor variation of Densest-
k
-Subgraph (sometimes known as Maximum Colored Subgraph Isomorphism):

Observation 8.2
.

Conjecture
8.1
is equivalent to the following.
There is an
\varepsilon>0
such that no
f(k)\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time algorithm can,
given
k
, a graph
G
on
n
vertices, and a proper
k
-colouring
c
of it,
distinguish between the following two cases:

•

G
contains a
k
-clique
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
(without loss of generality
c(v_{i})=i
);

•

every
k
-tuple
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
with
c(v_{i})=i
induces a subgraph on
<(1-\varepsilon)\binom{k}{2}
edges in
G
.

(Indeed, the
k
variables in the Max-2-CSP correspond to
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
, the set of vertices coloured
i
is the alphabet for variable
v_{i}
, and the edges between two colour sets define a constraint).
As a side note, we remark that an inspection of the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 5.12]
gives that Gap-ETH implies that the above is hard even if the soundness case is strengthened as follows, for
any
constant
\delta>0
:

•

every
k
-tuple
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
(regardless of colours) induces a subgraph on
<\delta\binom{k}{2}
edges in
G
.

The problem in Observation
8.2
is almost a maximum graph homomorphism problem on cliques,
except that, crucially, the mapping
i\mapsto v_{i}
is forced to be injective.
To show that
\mbox{Max-Hom}(\mathcal{A},-)
is hard for the class
\mathcal{A}
of tournaments,
intuitively, we use the fact that a map from a random tournament to itself must map most arcs to themselves (and is hence approximately injective).
This is formalised as follows.

Lemma 8.3
.

For every
\delta>0
, there exists constants
0<\lambda<\delta
and
N\geq 1
such that the following holds.
For every
k\geq N
, there is an orientation
\mathbb{A}
of the clique of size
k
such that every mapping
g:A\to A
of
\mathbb{A}
to itself with
\val(g)\geq(1-\lambda)\binom{k}{2}
must map at least
(1-\delta)\binom{k}{2}
arcs to themselves.

Proof.

For
\delta>0
, denote
m:=\binom{k}{2}
and choose
N\geq 1
such that
k\log_{2}k\leq\frac{\delta}{3}m

Open questions

Dichotomy

Our results, in particular Lemma
1.4
, lead us to believe
that perhaps the next question has a positive answer.

Question 9.1
.

Does
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})
admit a PTAS for every
r
if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is
fractionally-
\tw
-fragile?

A concrete open question concerns cographs, i.e., graphs of clique-width two:
Are cographs pliable? More generally, are graphs of bounded clique-width
pliable? Note that while certain techniques and results transfer from
structurally sparse graphs to dense graphs (clique-width being an example), this
fails for pliability as pliability is not closed under complementation since all
classes of dense graphs are pliable.

Some example cases where it would be important to show hardness of approximation
(or at least integrality gaps for constant levels of the Sherali-Adams
hierarchy) in order to shed light on Question
9.1
are classes of unbounded average degree or classes of 3-regular graphs with unbounded girth. In fact, we do not know of any examples of non-pliable classes of structures
\mathcal{A}
for which
\mbox{Max-Hom}(\mathcal{A},-)
admits a PTAS and thus it is non inconceivable that pliability captures all tractable cases.

Constant-factor approximation

Instead of PTASes one can of course ask about the existence of
some
constant-factor approximation.
For fixed signatures,
Max-Hom
always admits a simple constant-factor approximation: essentially map everything randomly to the densest
r
-tuple, where
r
is the maximum arity.
For the general
\mbox{Max-}r\mbox{-CSP}(\mathcal{G})
problem the situation is more interesting:
in general (when
\mathcal{G}
is the class of all graphs) a constant-factor approximation is impossible;
on the other hand for any monotone class of bounded average degree, there is again a simple solution:
because such classes have bounded degeneracy, the edge set can be partitioned into a constant number of trees, where the problem can be solved exactly.
The results of
[
28
]
imply that if the average degree is too high, the problem is again hard.
Can a dichotomy be shown?

Weak hyperfiniteness

As shown in Theorem
1.6
,
monotone hyperfinite classes are fractionally-
\tw
-fragile and have bounded degree.
The vertex version of hyperfiniteness is called “weakly hyperfinite” in
[
73
]

or “fragmentable” in
[
37
,
36
]
.
Is is strictly weaker: stars satisfy it, despite having unbounded degree.
Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez
[
73
]
proved that for a monotone class of graphs
\mathcal{G}
of bounded average degree,
\mathcal{G}
is weakly hyperfinite if and only if for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{G\in\mathcal{G}\colon\max\deg(G)\leq d\}
is hyperfinite.
This suggests a possible extension to graphs of unbounded degree:
are monotone weakly hyperfinite classes fractionally-
\tw
-fragile?
This would imply a conjecture of Dvořák
[
29
]
, that all graph classes with strongly sublinear separators are fractionally-
\tw
-fragile.
However, it is not even known whether all monotone weakly hyperfinite classes have bounded average degree.

Efficient PTAS

As mentioned in the introduction, both dense graphs and hyperfinite graphs can be approximated by constant-size descriptions, and in fact by constant-size random samples.
Since
\size
-pliability also approximates with constant-size descriptions,
this suggests there may be a general way to sample from such structures
to give constant-time approximations (for an appropriate input model).
In particular, can property-testing results for hyperfinite graphs be extended to fractionally-
\tw
-fragile graphs?
Perhaps this could be a way to obtain EPTASes

Is 3-colouring fixed-parameter tractable when parameterised by the treewidth of the core (the smallest homomorphically equivalent subgraph)?

Farkas’ lemma is the fundamental duality for systems of linear inequalities.

Lemma A.1

(Farkas’ lemma
[
82
, Corollary 7.1d]
)
.

Let
A
be an
m\times n
rational matrix and
\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}
. Then, exactly one of the two holds:

•

A\bar{x}=\bar{b}
for some
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
with
\bar{x}\geq 0
, or

•

A^{T}\bar{y}\geq 0
and
\bar{b}^{T}\bar{y}<0
for some
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}
.

For the duality between the existence of overcasts and the overcast relation
\mathbin{\succeq}

Lemma A.2

(Farkas’ lemma, variant 1)
.

Let
A
be an
m\times n
rational matrix and
\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}
.
Exactly one of the following holds:

•

there are
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
(
i=1,\dots,n
) such that
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i}A_{i,j}x_{i}\geq b_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,m
;

•

there are
y_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
(
j=1,\dots,m
) such that
\sum_{j}A_{i,j}y_{j}<\sum_{j}b_{j}y_{j}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

The first condition is equivalent to the existence of a solution in variables
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
(
i=1,\dots,n)
and
s_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
(
j=1,\dots,m
) of the following system:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}x_{i}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\sum_{i}A_{i,j}x_{i}-s_{j}

\displaystyle=b_{j}\quad\quad\text{(for $j=1,\dots,m$)}.

By Lemma
A.1
, this system has a solution if and only if the following system has no solution in variables
z\in\mathbb{Q}
and
y_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}
for

\displaystyle z+\sum_{j}A_{i,j}y_{j}

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\quad\text{(for $i=1,\dots,n$)}

\displaystyle-y_{j}

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\quad\text{(for $j=1,\dots,m$)}

\displaystyle z+\sum_{j}b_{j}y_{j}

\displaystyle<0

Equivalently, there are no

such that

(for

).
∎

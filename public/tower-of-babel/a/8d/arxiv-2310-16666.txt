Tate duality and transfer for symmetric algebras over complete discrete valuation rings
Markus Linckelmann

Abstract
. We show that dualising transfer maps in Hochschild cohomology of symmetric algebras over complete discrete valuations rings commutes with Tate duality. This is analogous to a similar result for Tate cohomology of symmetric algebras over fields. We interpret both results in the broader context of Calabi-Yau triangulated categories.



1.
Introduction

An algebra
A
over a commutative ring
R
is called
symmetric
if it is
finitely generated projective as an
R
-module and if
A\cong A^{\vee}
as

A
-
A
-bimodules, where
A^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(A,R)
. In that case, the image

s
in
A^{\vee}
of
1_{A}
under such a bimodule isomorphism is called a

symmetrising form for
A
. The form
s
depends on the choice of
the isomorphism
A\cong A^{\vee}
, and is unqiue up to multiplication by
an invertible element in
Z(A)
. There may not be a canonical choice
for
s
. If
G
is a finite group, then
RG
is symmetric and - keeping track of
the image of
G
in
RG
- does have a canonical
symmetrising form, namely the map
s
sending
1_{G}
to
1_{R}
and all nontrivial
group elements to
0
.

For a symmetric algebra
A
over a field, Tate duality
is a duality between the Tate-Ext spaces

\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{A}^{n-1}(U,V)
and
\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{A}^{-n}(V,U)
, for any integer
n
and any
two finite-dimensional
A
-modules
U
,
V
.
In particular, this yields a duality
between Tate-Hochschild cohomology
\widehat{H\!H}^{n-1}(A)
and
\widehat{H\!H}^{-n}(A)

for all integers
n
.
It is shown in
[
22
]
that in that case Tate duality commutes with the
transfer maps introduced in
[
20
]
, extending a well-known
compatibility of Tate duality with restriction and transfer in finite group
cohomology.

If
A
is instead a symmetric algebra over a complete discrete valuation ring

{\mathcal{O}}
with a separable coefficient extension
K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}A
to the field of fractions

K
of
{\mathcal{O}}
, and if
U
,
V
are
{\mathcal{O}}
-free
finitely generated
A
-modules, then Tate duality takes a different form:
there is a non-degenerate bilinear form

\langle-,-\rangle_{A}:\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{A}^{n}(U,V)\times\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{A}^{-n}(V,U)\to K/{\mathcal{O}}

for any integer
n
, which is described explicitly in
[
10
]

and briefly reviewed in
7.3
below.
The purpose of this paper is to show that this duality
commutes with the transfer maps from
[
20
]
.
The proof is quite different from that in
[
22
]
due to the different
description of Tate duality, as given in
[
10
]
, extending the description in
Thévenaz

[
26
, §33]
for finite group algebras. Both this duality as well as
the transfer maps depend on the choices of symmetrising forms. By omitting
choices of symmetrising forms from the statements below we implicitly assert
that these statements hold regardless of these choices.

If
A
,
B
are two symmetric
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebras
and

is an
A

-bimodule
which is finitely generated as a left

-module and as a right

-free

\mathrm{tr}_{M}(U,V):\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{B}^{n}(M^{\vee}\otimes_{A}U,M^{\vee}\otimes_{A}V)\to\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{A}^{n}(U,V),

which we will review in Section
4
.
In degree zero, this is the trace map defined in
[
25
, (57)]
,
[
5
]
or

[
6
, Definition 6.6]
. For transfer induced by biadjoint functors between
more general categories see
[
8
]
, and
for the graded version needed in this paper see
[
21
, §4, §7]
or also

[
22
, §5]
, for instance. For simplicity we will write
\mathrm{tr}_{M}
instead
of
\mathrm{tr}_{M}(U,V)
whenever

,
V
are clear from the context.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
,
B
be symmetric algebras over a complete discrete valuation ring

{\mathcal{O}}
with a field of fractions
K
of characteristic zero.
Suppose that
K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}A
,
K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}B
are semisimple.
Let
M
be an
A
-
B
-bimodule which is finitely generated projective as a left

A
-module and as a right
B
-module. Let
n
be an integer, and let
U
,
V

be finitely generated

{\mathcal{O}}
-free
A
-modules. For any
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}^{n}_{A}(U,V)
and

\beta\in\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{B}^{-n}(M^{\vee}\otimes_{A}U,M^{\vee}\otimes_{A}V)
we have

\langle\alpha,\mathrm{tr}_{M}(\beta)\rangle_{A}=\langle\mathrm{Id}_{M^{\vee}}\otimes\alpha,\beta\rangle_{B},

\langle\mathrm{tr}_{M}(\beta),\alpha\rangle_{A}=\langle\beta,\mathrm{Id}_{M^{\vee}}\otimes\alpha\rangle_{B}

One consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that Tate
duality determines the transfer maps
\mathrm{tr}_{M}(U,V)
.
This comment applies also to the transfer maps in

[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
and Tate duality for symmetric

-algebras.
This has an interpretation in the context of Calabi-Yau triangulated
categories, which we will describe in Section
9
.

Tate-Ext applied to

as a module over the symmetric algebra

yields Tate-Hochschild cohomology
\widehat{H\!H}^{*}(A)
(see

below). Tate duality applied to this situation yields in turn
a non-degenerate bilinear form

\langle-,-\rangle_{A^{e}}:\widehat{H\!H}^{n}(A)\times\widehat{H\!H}^{-n}(A)\to K/{\mathcal{O}};

see for instance
[
10
, Remark 1.5]
. By
[

, Definition 2.9]
or

, §4, §7]

that we will review in Section

The dual

with respect to the base ring is a

finitely generated projective as a left

There is some abuse of notation:

in Tate-Hochschild cohomology is not quite a special

defined previously; their precise

. The compatibility
between transfer and Tate duality for Tate-Hochschild cohomology takes the
following form.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
,
B
be symmetric algebras over a complete discrete valuation ring

{\mathcal{O}}
with a field of fractions
K
of characteristic zero.
Suppose that
K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}A
,
K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}B
are semisimple.
Let
M
be an
A
-
B
-bimodule which is finitely generated projective as a left

For
\zeta\in\widehat{H\!H}^{n}(A)

we have

\langle\zeta,\mathrm{tr}_{M}(\tau)\rangle_{A^{e}}=\langle\mathrm{tr}_{M^{\vee}}(\zeta),\tau\rangle_{B^{e}},

\langle\mathrm{tr}_{M}(\tau),\zeta\rangle_{A^{e}}=\langle\tau,\mathrm{tr}_{M^{\vee}}(\zeta)\rangle_{B^{e}}.

.

In view of the interpretation of Theorem
1.1
in terms of
Calabi-Yau triangulated categories (which we will describe in Section

9
), it is worth noting that
there are finite-dimensional algebras which are selfinjective, not
necessarily symmetric, but
whose stable category is Calabi-Yau of nonnegative dimension. See for
instance
[
11
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
and the references therein.
We further draw attention to the appendix in
[
4
]
by M. Van den Bergh
regarding signs in Serre duality. We largely ignore sign issues in §4 (notably in

) because this will not

Remark 1.4
.

The main motivation for developing this material is to extend results on finite
group cohomology to symmetric algebras, in order to provide techniques to
calculate cohomological invariants, such as the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity,
that might distinguish classes of symmetric algebras from being block algebras
of finite groups (see
[
19
]
for calculations in this context).
One such distinguishing feature (and necessary tool for
calculations of the regularity) is the existence of
a local cohomology spectral sequence for Hochschild cohomology, analogous
to Greenlees’ local cohomology spectral sequence in
[
14
]
.
Benson’s approach to this spectral sequence in
[
1
]

makes use of the compatibility of restriction and transfer in group cohomology
with respect to Tate duality over a field. We expect that this compatibility
at the level of Hochschild cohomology, both in
[
22
]
for algebras over
fields, and the present paper for algebras over complete discrete valuation rings,
will be one of the technical ingredients towards this programme.

Remark 1.5
.

Unlike in the Tate duality for symmetric algebras over fields, there is no degree shift
in the Tate duality for symmetric algebras over a complete discrete valuation
ring
{\mathcal{O}}
with field of fractions
K
. This is due to the fact that we have
replaced duality with respect to the base ring
{\mathcal{O}}
by duality with respect to
the injective syzygy
K/{\mathcal{O}}
of
{\mathcal{O}}
, noting that
we have a short exact sequence
0\to{\mathcal{O}}\to K\to K/{\mathcal{O}}\to 0
in which
both
K
and
K/{\mathcal{O}}
are injective
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules, with
K/{\mathcal{O}}
in degree

1
of this injective resolution of
{\mathcal{O}}
. For Tate cohomology over
more general rings, see Buchweitz
[
7
]
. Further extensions of Tate
cohomology can be found, for instance, in
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We will use without further reference well-known basic material on stable module

[

Assume now that

Then the two classes of relatively

.
For any two integers

.

For

\widehat{H\!H}^{n}(A)=\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}_{A^{e}}^{n}(A,A)=\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{A^{e}}(A,\Sigma^{n}(A)).

3.
Adjunction for symmetric algebras

We briefly review without proofs some formalities on bimodules over symmetric

[

]
,

]
,

[

Let

,
B

be symmetric
R
-algebras, with symmetrising forms
s
and

The functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(-,R)

.
Let

be a perfect

-

-bimodule. Since

-bimodule. We have a

3.1
.

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M,A)\cong M^{\vee}

sending

,

\mathrm{Hom}_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(M,B)\cong M^{\vee}

sending

.
For any

M^{\vee}\otimes_{A}U\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M,A)\otimes_{A}U\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M,U)

Similarly, we have a natural isomorphism

Remarks on transfer for Calabi-Yau triangulated categories

There are two ways to associate transfer maps to a triangle functor

of Calabi-Yau categories: either by making use of a

(if there is such a functor, as described in

To be more precise, let

On products in negative degrees of Tate cohomology

Nonzero products in negative degree in Tate cohomology have implications for the
depth of the nonnegative part. This phenomenon was first observed in
[
2
]

in finite group cohomology, and then generalised in
[
3
]
,
[
22
, §8]
.
This arises over complete discrete valuation rings as well, with essentially the
same arguments used in
[
2
]
.

Let
A
be a symmetric algebra over a complete discrete valuation ring
{\mathcal{O}}
with
a field of fractions
K
of characteristic zero. Let
U
,
V
,
W
be finitely
generated
A
-modules. Let
m
,
n
be integers.
Let
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}^{m}_{A}(U,V)
,
\beta\in\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}^{n}_{A}(V,W)
,
and
\gamma\in

\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}^{-m-n}_{A}(W,U)
. We denote by
\beta\alpha\in

\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}^{m+n}_{A}(U,W)
the Yoneda product; that is,
\beta\alpha
is represented
by
\Sigma^{m}(\beta)\circ\alpha
, where we use the same letters
\alpha
,
\beta

for representatives of their classes in
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(U,\Sigma^{m}(V))
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(V,\Sigma^{n}(W))
.
We have

10.1
.

\langle\beta\alpha,\gamma\rangle_{A}=\langle\alpha,\gamma\beta\rangle_{A}

because both sides are equal to the image in
K/{\mathcal{O}}
of the trace on
KU
of the
endomorphism

z_{A}^{-1}\cdot(\Sigma^{m+n}(\gamma)\circ\Sigma^{m}(\beta)\circ\alpha)
of
KU
,
where as before
we use abusively the same letters
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
for representatives
in
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(U,\Sigma^{m}(V))
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(V,\Sigma^{n}(W))
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(W,\Sigma^{-m-n}(U))

of their classes.
Applied with
U=W
and
\gamma=\mathrm{Id}_{U}
this yields

10.2

\langle\beta\alpha,\mathrm{Id}_{U}\rangle_{A}=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{A}.

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
\zeta
be nonzero element in
\widehat{\mathrm{Ext}}^{n}_{A}(U,V)

∎

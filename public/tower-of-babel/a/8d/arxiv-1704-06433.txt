1.
Introduction

Let
M^{3}
be a three dimensional smooth manifold equipped with a Lorentzian metric. A near-horizon metric on
M^{3}
is a Lorentzian metric of the form

(1)

g_{NH}=2{\rm d}\nu\left({\rm d}r+rh(x){\rm d}x+\frac{r^{2}}{2}F(x){\rm d}\nu\right)+{\rm d}x{\rm d}x,

where
x
,
\nu
and
r
are local coordinates and
h(x)
,
F(x)
are arbitrary functions of
x
. Near-horizon geometries in higher dimensions are studied in relation to the existence of extremal black holes
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
.

The near-horizon metric (
1
) is derived as follows. For a smooth null hypersurface
\Sigma^{2}
in
M^{3}
, there exists an adapted coordinate system called Gaussian null coordinates valid in any neighbourhood of
\Sigma^{2}
. Imposing that the normal vector field
N^{a}
be Killing in
M^{3}
implies that
\Sigma^{2}
is a Killing horizon. We further require that the Killing horizon
\Sigma^{2}
be degenerate. This means that the flow of
N^{a}
is along affinely parameterised null geodesics on
\Sigma^{2}
, or equivalently that the surface gravity of
\Sigma^{2}
is zero. The scaling limit of the degenerate Killing horizon is called a near-horizon geometry, and the metric is of the form (
1
).

In dimensions 4 or more, imposing the vacuum Einstein equations on the near-horizon geometry metric
g_{NH}
give rise to the near-horizon geometry equations on the spatial section of the degenerate Killing horizon. Solutions to this overdetermined system of equations on a compact cross-section are related to the existence of extremal black holes. For further details, see
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
16
]
.

Similarly, we can require that a 3 dimensional near-horizon geometry metric satisfies Einstein’s equations. This has been done in
[
14
]
. In this case, the near-horizon geometry equations reduce to a pair of first order ODEs that can be integrated explicitly. It is also found that global and periodic solutions for
h(x)
exist on a 1-dimensional cross-section
\mathcal{H}
which has the topology of the circle; this is the 1-dimensional analogue of an extremal black hole horizon.

Instead of asking Einstein’s equations to be satisfied, we can ask for a natural generalisation which is to impose the Einstein-Weyl equations instead. This requires an additional structure of a 1-form, to be explained in the next section. Here we investigate whether there exists a 1-form
X
compatible with (
1
) such that
(X,g_{NH})
satisfies the Einstein-Weyl equations.

In the 37th winter school in geometry and physics, held in Srní, Czech Republic in 2017, plenary lectures on non-expanding horizons including near horizon geometries were presented by Jerzy Lewandowski and lectures on Einstein-Weyl geometry and dispersionless integrable systems were presented by Maciej Dunajski. The latter topics were also covered in the lectures by Evgeny Ferapontov. We hope that attendees of the school would find these topics to be interesting and delve into the rich and steadily expanding field that relates integrable PDEs to general relativity and classical differential geometry.

2.
Three dimensional Einstein-Weyl geometries

Einstein-Weyl geometries in 3 dimensions play an important role because of its relationship with the geometry of third order ODEs (see
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
), twistor theory (see
[
12
]
and
[
15
]
) and integrable systems (see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
11
]
).
Let
(M^{3},[g])
be a smooth conformal manifold equipped with a conformal class of metrics
[g]
of (pseudo)-Riemannian signature. In 3 dimensions, this is either Riemannian or Lorentzian. Any 2 representative metric
g
,
\tilde{g}\in[g]
are related via

\tilde{g}=\Omega^{2}g

for some smooth positive function
\Omega
.
A Weyl structure on
(M^{3},[g])
is a torsion-free connection
D
that preserves the conformal class of metrics. Equivalently,

D_{a}g_{bc}=2X_{a}g_{bc}

for
g_{ab}
a representative in the conformal class and
X_{a}
a 1-form that is not necessarily closed.
The Einstein-Weyl equations are the system of equations obtained by requiring that that the symmetric part of the Ricci tensor of the Weyl connection is pure trace, i.e.

R^{D}_{(ab)}=sg_{ab}

for some function
s
. This system of equations is conformally invariant. Writing
R^{D}_{(ab)}
in terms of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
for the metric
g_{ab}
and 1-form
X_{a}
, we get

\nabla_{(a}X_{b)}+X_{a}X_{b}+\mathrm{P}_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab},

where round brackets denote symmetrisation over indices,
\mathrm{P}_{ab}
is the Schouten tensor of
g_{ab}
and the trace term

\Lambda=\frac{1}{3}\left(\nabla_{a}X^{b}+X_{a}X^{b}+\mathrm{P}\right)

is a function (more appropriately, a section of a density valued line bundle and we refer to
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
for more details).

In
[
3
]
, it is shown that a 3-dimensional Lorentzian Einstein-Weyl structure corresponds to a 2 parameter family of totally-geodesic null hypersurfaces in
M^{3}
. A twistor description is developed in
[
12
]
, where it is shown that real-analytic Lorentzian 3-dimensional Einstein-Weyl structures arise precisely from the Kodaira deformation space of rational normal curves with normal bundle
\mathcal{O}(2)
in a 2-dimensional complex manifold.
The 2-form
{\rm d}X
is called the Faraday 2-form of the Weyl structure. If
{\rm d}X=0
, then
X
is locally exact and
g
can be conformally rescaled to a metric of constant curvature in 3 dimensions. In particular, it implies that
g
is locally conformally flat. A computation of the Cotton tensor of the near-horizon metric
g_{NH}
shows that

Proposition 2.1
.

The metric of the form (
1
) is locally conformally flat iff

(2)

F^{\prime}(x)=F(x)h(x).

Proof.

Condition (
2
) implies that the Cotton tensor of
g_{NH}
is zero, and conversely so.
∎

3.
Results

We shall assume that
(M^{3},[g_{NH}])
is smooth.
We consider an ansatz for
X
of the form

X=ch(x){\rm d}x+X_{2}{\rm d}r+X_{3}{\rm d}v,

where
c
is a constant and the functions
X_{2}=X_{2}(x,\nu,r)
,
X_{3}=X_{3}(x,\nu,r)
are to be determined. We also require that
X|_{r=0}=ch(x){\rm d}x
, so that the 1-form
X
is determined up to a constant multiple
c
by its value on the spatial section of the degenerate Killing horizon. In the following analysis, it turns out that there is a degenerate case when
c=-\frac{1}{2}
.
We have the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

A 3 dimensional near-horizon geometry metric (
1
) on
M^{3}
of the form

\displaystyle g_{NH}=

\displaystyle 2{\rm d}\nu\left({\rm d}r+rh(x){\rm d}x+\frac{r^{2}}{2}e^{{\rmintop\nolimits h(x)dx}}\wp\left(\rmintop\nolimits e^{{\frac{1}{2}\rmintop\nolimits h(x)dx}}dx+a;0,b\right){\rm d}\nu\right)

(3)

\displaystyle+{\rm d}x{\rm d}x

where
\wp(z;g_{2},g_{3})
is the Weierstrass elliptic function,
a
,
b
are constants,
and a Weyl connection
X
of the form

X=-\frac{1}{2}h(x){\rm d}x-2re^{{\rmintop\nolimits h(x)dx}}\wp\left(\rmintop\nolimits e^{{\frac{1}{2}\rmintop\nolimits h(x)dx}}dx+a;0,b\right){\rm d}\nu

defines an Einstein-Weyl structure
([g_{NH}],X)
on
M^{3}
. This depends on
1
free function of one variable.

In particular, taking
h(x)
to be globally defined and periodic allows
\mathcal{H}
to have the topology of a circle.
In the case that
h(x)=0
, the metric simplifies to

g=2{\rm d}\nu\left({\rm d}r+\frac{r^{2}}{2}\wp(x+a;0,b){\rm d}\nu\right)+{\rm d}x{\rm d}x

and the 1-form
X
is given by

X=-2r\wp(x+a;0,b){\rm d}\nu.

We recognise that the function

u(x,\nu,r)=-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\wp(x+a;0,b)

satisfies the dispersionless Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (dKP) equation given by

2(u_{\nu}-uu_{r})_{r}=u_{xx}

and the Einstein-Weyl structure corresponds to one of dKP type. For further details about such Einstein-Weyl structures we refer to
[
8
]
.
For generic values of
c
, which also allows for
c=-\frac{1}{2}
, we have

Theorem 3.2
.

A 3 dimensional near-horizon geometry metric (
1
) on
M^{3}
and a Weyl connection
X
of the form

X=ch(x){\rm d}x+r((2c+1)h^{\prime}+c(2c+1)h^{2}-2F(x)){\rm d}v

defines an Einstein-Weyl structure if and only if

F(x)=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}+4chh^{\prime}+2c^{2}h^{3}}{2h}

and
h(x)
satisfies the
4^{\rm th}
order ODE

\displaystyle h^{3}(h^{\prime})^{2}(c-1)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(c-1)^{2}h^{4}h^{\prime\prime}+\frac{9}{4}(c-1)h^{2}h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}

(4)

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}(c-1)h^{3}h^{\prime\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}(h^{\prime})^{2}h^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{2}hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime\prime}+h(h^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\frac{1}{4}h^{2}h^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}=0.

4.
Proof

Proof of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

We start with an ansatz of the form

X=ch(x){\rm d}x+X_{2}{\rm d}r+X_{3}{\rm d}\nu,

where the functions
X_{2}=X_{2}(x,\nu,r)
,
X_{3}=X_{3}(x,\nu,r)
are to be determined, and we require that
X|_{r=0}=ch(x)dx
, so that the 1-form
X
is determined by its value on the spatial section of the degenerate Killing horizon.
We find that substituting this ansatz for
X
into the Einstein-Weyl equations, the
{\rm d}r{\rm d}r
component gives

\partial_{r}X_{2}+X_{2}^{2}=0,

which has solutions

X_{2}=\frac{1}{r+f_{1}(x,\nu)}\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad X_{2}=0.

Since the first solution does not restrict to zero on
\{r=0\}
, we take
X_{2}=0
instead.
The
{\rm d}x{\rm d}x
component gives now

-2F-\partial_{r}X_{3}+c(2c+1)h^{2}+(2c+1)h^{\prime}=0,

from which we obtain

X_{3}=(-2F+c(2c+1)h^{2}+(2c+1)h^{\prime})r+f_{2}(x,\nu).

Once again requiring that
X_{3}|_{r=0}=0
implies
f_{2}(x,\nu)=0
. With this, only the
{\rm d}x{\rm d}\nu
and
{\rm d}\nu{\rm d}\nu
components remain to be solved in the Einstein-Weyl equations.
The
{\rm d}x{\rm d}\nu
component gives

\frac{r}{2}(2c+1)\left(h^{\prime\prime}-2hF+4chh^{\prime}+2c^{2}h^{3}\right)=0.

This vanishes identically when
c=-\frac{1}{2}
. Otherwise, we have

F(x)=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}+4chh^{\prime}+2c^{2}h^{3}}{2h}.

In the first case where
c=-\frac{1}{2}
, the remaining equation in the
{\rm d}\nu{\rm d}\nu
component is

-3Fh^{2}+5hF^{\prime}+2Fh^{\prime}+12F^{2}-2F^{\prime\prime}=0,

which has solutions

F(x)=\wp\left(\rmintop\nolimits\exp\left({\frac{1}{2}\rmintop\nolimits h(x)dx}\right)dx+a,0,b\right)\exp\left({\rmintop\nolimits h(x)dx}\right)

whatever
h(x)
is.
This proves Theorem
3.1
.
For the other case, substituting

F(x)=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}+4chh^{\prime}+2c^{2}h^{3}}{2h}

gives

X_{3}=\frac{ch^{3}+(1-2c)hh^{\prime}-h^{\prime\prime}}{h}r

and the only remaining
{\rm d}\nu{\rm d}\nu
component of the Einstein-Weyl equations gives the
4^{\rm th}
order ODE

\displaystyle h^{3}(h^{\prime})^{2}(c-1)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(c-1)^{2}h^{4}h^{\prime\prime}+\frac{9}{4}(c-1)h^{2}h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}(c-1)h^{3}h^{\prime\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}(h^{\prime})^{2}h^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{2}hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime\prime}+h(h^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\frac{1}{4}h^{2}h^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}=0.

Note that this formula is still well-defined for
c=-\frac{1}{2}
.
This proves Theorem
3.2
.
∎

5.
Explicit solutions

In this section, we find interesting families of solutions to the 4th order ODE (
3.2
). Consider the following second order nonlinear ODE

(5)

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}=\alpha hh^{\prime}+\beta h^{3}

with
\alpha
,
\beta
constant. We find, upon substituting (
5
) and its derivatives into (
3.2
), that we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{h^{3}}{4}\big(2(c-1)^{2}+3\alpha(c-1)+\alpha^{2}-\beta\big)\left(\beta h^{4}+\alpha h^{2}h^{\prime}-2(h^{\prime})^{2}\right)=0.

Thus solutions to (
5

3.2
) provided

(6)

\beta=2(c-1)^{2}+3\alpha(c-1)+\alpha^{2}.

Switching independent and dependent variables
(x,h(x))\mapsto(h,x(h))
, the non-linear second order ODE (
5
) is dual to

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}=-\beta h^{3}(x^{\prime})^{3}-\alpha h(x^{\prime})^{2},

which upon setting
y(h)=h^{2}x^{\prime}(h)
, gives an Abel differential equation of the first kind:

\displaystyle y^{\prime}=\frac{1}{h}\left(-\beta y^{3}-\alpha y^{2}+2y\right).

This has solutions given by

(7)

\displaystyle h=\frac{\gamma\sqrt{y}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{2\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+8\beta}}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{2\beta y+\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+8\beta}}\right)\right)}{(\beta y^{2}+\alpha y-2)^{\frac{1}{4}}},

and consequently

(8)

\displaystyle x(y)=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\rmintop\nolimits\frac{\exp\left(\frac{-\alpha}{2\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+8\beta}}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{2\beta y+\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+8\beta}}\right)\right)}{\sqrt{y}(\beta y^{2}+\alpha y-2)^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\rm d}y.

When
\alpha=0
, we obtain
\beta=2(c-1)^{2}
from (
6
).
In this case, solutions to the nonlinear ODE (
5
)

are given by the Jacobi elliptic function

h(x)=a~{\rm sn}\left(a\left(\frac{\sqrt{-2\beta}}{2}x+b\right),i\right)=a~{\rm sn}\left(a(i(c-1)x+b),i\right),

with

,

the constants of integration. Here the elliptic modulus is given by

.
Alternatively, we obtain

\displaystyle h=\frac{\gamma\sqrt{y}}{(\beta y^{2}-2)^{\frac{1}{4}}}

from (
7
) and

\displaystyle x=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\rmintop\nolimits\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}(\beta y^{2}-2)^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\rm d}y

from (

, this gives the expression in terms of hypergeometric functions

\displaystyle h=\frac{\gamma}{\beta^{\frac{1}{4}}(1-z)^{\frac{1}{4}}}\quad\text{and}\quad x=\frac{\sqrt{2z}}{2\gamma\beta^{\frac{1}{4}}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4};\frac{3}{2};z\right).

When
\beta=0
, we have

(2c-2+\alpha)(c-1+\alpha)=0

from (

\alpha=1-c
or
\alpha=2-2c
.
For
\alpha\neq 0
, which implies

, solutions to (

This is periodic but not globally defined. For

If we do not assume either

(9)

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}=\alpha hh^{\prime}+\alpha^{2}h^{3}.

From (

\displaystyle h=\frac{\gamma\sqrt{y}}{(-1)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\alpha y+2)^{\frac{1}{6}}(1-\alpha y)^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

\displaystyle h^{6}=\frac{\varepsilon y^{3}}{(\alpha y+2)(1-\alpha y)^{2}},

6.
Further remarks and outlook

It is curious that solving for the Einstein-Weyl equations for a near-horizon metric (

as in Theorem

and hyperCR Einstein-Weyl structures for

The metric in Gaussian null coordinates on

with a smooth Killing horizon (see
[
14
]
) is given by

g=2{\rm d}\nu\left({\rm d}r+rh(x,r){\rm d}x+\frac{r}{2}F(x,r){\rm d}\nu\right)+\gamma(x,r)^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}x.

In
[
19
]
, the authors investigated the case where (

) is Einstein with non-zero cosmological constant.
Similarly, we can investigate whether the metric of the form (
14
) admits a compatible 1-form
X_{a}
such that
([g],X)
is Einstein-Weyl.
Some computations have been made but the calculations are considerably more involved than those presented here.

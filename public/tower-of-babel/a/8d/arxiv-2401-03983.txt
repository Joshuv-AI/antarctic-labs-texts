On characteristic properties of the ellipsoid in terms of circumscribed cones of a convex body
E. Morales-Amaya, G. Mondragón and, J. Jerónimo-Castro

Abstract
We strongly believe that in order to prove two important geometrical problems in convexity, namely, the G. Bianchi and P. Gruber’s Conjecture [ 5 ] and the J. A. Barker and D. G. Larman’s Conjecture [ 2 ] , it is necessary obtain new characteristic properties of the ellipsoid, which involves the notions defined in such problems. In this work we present a series of results which intent to be a progress in such direction: Let L , K ⊂ ℝ n L,K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} be convex bodies, n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , and L ⊂ int K L\subset\operatorname*{int}K . Then each of the following conditions i), ii) and iii) implies that L L is an ellipsoid. i) L L is O O -symmetric and, for every x ∈ bd K x\in\operatorname*{bd}K , the support cone S ⁡ ( L , x ) S(L,x) is ellipsoidal. ii) there exists a point p ∈ ℝ n p\in\mathbb{R}^{n} such that for every x ∈ bd K x\in\operatorname*{bd}K , there exists y ∈ bd K y\in\operatorname*{bd}K and hyperplane Π \Pi , passing through p p , such that S ⁡ ( L , x ) ∩ S ⁡ ( L , y ) = Π ∩ bd K . S(L,x)\cap S(L,y)=\Pi\cap\operatorname*{bd}K. iii) K K and L L are O O -symmetric, every x ∈ bd K x\in\operatorname*{bd}K is a pole of L L and Ω x := S ⁡ ( L , x ) ∩ S ⁡ ( L , − x ) \Omega_{x}:=S(L,x)\cap S(L,-x) is contained in int K \operatorname*{int}K . In the case ii), K K is also an ellipsoid and it is concentric with L L . On the other hand, let K ⊂ ℝ n K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} be a O O -symmetric convex body, n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , and let B ⊂ int ℝ n B\subset\operatorname*{int}\mathbb{R}^{n} be a ball with centre at O O . We are going to prove that if B B is small enough and all the sections of K K given by planes tangent to B B are ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -ellipsoids, then K K is an n n -ellipsoid.

2
Characterizations of the ellipsoid in terms of elliptical circumscribed cones of a convex body

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be either a convex body or a convex cone, let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an hyperplane and let
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. The hyperplane is said to be an
affine hyperplane of symmetry of

if every chord of
K
, parallel to
u

Lemma 1
.

Let
C(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex cone with apex at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
C(x)
is elliptical the following properties holds:

i)

All the bounded hyper-sections of
C(x)
are ellipsoids.

ii)

Every hyperplane passing through
x
and with interior points of
C(x)
is an affine hyperplane of symmetry of
K
.

iii)

If
L
is a line passing through
x
and it is contained in
\operatorname*{int}C(x)
, then there exists an hyperplane
\Pi
such that the ellipsoid
\Pi\cap K
has center at
\Pi\cap L
.

Proof.

For the proof of iii), let suppose that
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an affine chart of the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Let
p\in L
,
p\not=x
, and let
H
be a hyperplane such that
p\in H
and
H\cap C(x)
is bounded. By i), the section
H\cap C(x)
is an ellipsoid. Let
L_{p}
be the polar of

with respect to the ellipsoid
H\cap C(x)
. We denote by
\Gamma
the hyper-plane defined by
x
and
L_{p}
. Let
\Pi
be the hyper-plane parallel to
\Gamma
and passing through
p
. We claim that
\Pi
is the hyper-plane that we are looking for. In fact, the
(n-2)
-plane
L_{\infty}:=\Pi\cap\Gamma
is the
(n-2)
-plane at the infinite in
\Pi
whose pole with respect to the ellipsoid
\Pi\cap C(x)
is

. Thus
\Pi\cap C(x)
has center at
p
.
∎

.

Before to present to proof of Theorem

[a,b]

Let

Generalized Euclidean Operator Radius
Mohammad W. Alomari, Mohammad Sababheh, Cristian Conde, and Hamid Reza Moradi

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the f − f- operator radius of Hilbert space operators as a generalization of the Euclidean operator radius and the q − q- operator radius. Properties of the newly defined radius are discussed, emphasizing how it extends some known results in the literature.

Primary 47A12, Secondary 47A30, 26A51

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
denote the
C^{*}-
algebra of all bounded linear operators on a complex Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}.
For
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right),
the operator norm and the numerical radius of
T
are defined, respectively, by

\|T\|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Tx\|\;{\text{ and }}\;\omega(T)=\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\left<Tx,x\right>|.

It is well known that
\omega(\cdot)
defines a norm on
\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
, that is equivalent to the operator norm via the relation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|T\|\leq\omega(T)\leq\|T\|,\;T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right).

It is interesting to find possible bounds of
\omega(\cdot)
in terms of
\|\cdot\|
since the calculations of
\|\cdot\|
are much easier than those of
\omega(\cdot).
We refer the reader to
[
1
,
7
,
15
,
16
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
27
]
as a recent list of references treating numerical radius and operator norm inequalities.

Among the most well-established interesting results in this direction are the following inequalities due to Kittaneh
[
13
,
14
]

\omega(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\;|T|+|T^{*}|\;\|,

(1.2)

\omega^{2}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\;|T|^{2}+|T^{*}|^{2}\|,

and

(1.3)

\omega(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|T\|+\|T^{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

where
T^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
T
and
|T|=(T^{*}T)^{1/2}
.

Extending the numerical radius, the Euclidean operator radius of the operators
T_{1},\cdots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
was defined in
[
18
]
as

{{\omega}_{e}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{{{\left|\left\langle{{T}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|}^{2}}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

This was also generalized in
[
8
]
to

{{\omega}_{q}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{{\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|}^{q}}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{q}}};\text{ }q\geq 1.

We refer the reader to
[
2
,
4
,
9
,

,

,

]
as a list of references treating properties and significance of
\omega_{e}
and
\omega_{q}.

In the literature, it is interesting to introduce and define new related numerical radii or operator radii in a way that extends some well-known concepts. For this particular concern, we refer the reader to
[

]
, where a discussion of other types of numerical radii has been presented.

This paper introduces a generalized form of
\omega_{e}
and
\omega_{q}
that depends on a certain function
f
. It turns out that both
\omega_{e}
and
\omega_{q}
are special cases of this new concept, which we define as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing function with
f(0)=0
. We define the
f-
operator radius of the operators
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
by

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right).

Thus, when
f(t)=t^{2}
,
\omega_{f}=\omega_{e}
, and when
f(t)=t^{q},\omega_{f}=\omega_{q},
for
q\geq 1.

The quantities
\omega_{e},\omega_{q}
were defined in
[
8
,
18
]
as norms on
\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})\times\cdots\times\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H}).
In what follows, we show norm properties of
\omega_{f}
.

It is implicitly understood that
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous increasing function with
f(0)=0
, whenever we write
\omega_{f}.

The Davis-Wielandt radius of
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
is defined as

d\omega\left(T\right)=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,\left\{\sqrt{{{\left|\left\langle Tx,x\right\rangle\right|}^{2}}+{{\left\|Tx\right\|}^{4}}}\right\}.

It is not hard to see that
d\omega\left(T\right)
is unitarily invariant, but it does not define a norm on
\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
.
It is well-known that

\max\left\{\omega\left(T\right),{{\left\|T\right\|}^{2}}\right\}\leq d\omega\left(T\right)\leq\sqrt{{{\omega}^{2}}\left(T\right)+{{\left\|T\right\|}^{4}}}.

Putting
n=2
,
{{T}_{1}}=T
, and
{{T}_{2}}={{T}^{*}}T
, in Definition
1.1
, we deliver

\displaystyle{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{f}^{-1}}\left(f\left(\left|\left\langle Tx,x\right\rangle\right|\right)+f\left(\left|\left\langle{{T}^{*}}Tx,x\right\rangle\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{f}^{-1}}\left(f\left(\left|\left\langle Tx,x\right\rangle\right|\right)+f\left(\left|\left\langle Tx,Tx\right\rangle\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{f}^{-1}}\left(f\left(\left|\left\langle Tx,x\right\rangle\right|\right)+f\left({{\left\|Tx\right\|}^{2}}\right)\right)

which provides an extension of the Davis-Wielandt radius of
T
. Notice that when
f(t)=t^{2}
,
\omega_{f}(T,T^{*}T)=d\omega(T).

We need the following lemmas throughout the subsequent sections. The first lemma has been a helpful tool in studying operator inequalities in the literature.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
6
, (4.24)]
Let
f
be a convex function defined on a real interval
I
and let
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
be a self-adjoint operator with spectrum in
I
. Then
f\left({\left\langle{Tx,x}\right\rangle}\right)\leq\left\langle{f\left(T\right)x,x}\right\rangle
for all unit vectors
x\in\mathscr{H}
.

The second lemma is a useful characterization of numerical radii.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
27
]
Let
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
. Then

\omega(T)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|\mathfrak{R}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}T\right)\right\|,

where
\mathfrak{R}(T)
is the real part of the operator
T
, defined by
\mathfrak{R}T=\frac{T+T^{*}}{2}.

We also need the following lemma, which holds for convex functions with
f(0)\leq 0.

Lemma 1.3
.

If
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a convex function with
f(0)=0
, then
f
is superadditive. That is

f(a+b)\geq f(a)+f(b),

for
a,b\geq 0
. The inequality is reversed when
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is concave, without having
f(0)=0
.

Recall that the Aluthge transform
\widetilde{T}
of
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
is defined by
\widetilde{T}=|T|^{\frac{1}{2}}U|T|^{\frac{1}{2}},
where
U
is the partial isometry appearing in the polar decomposition
T=U|T|
of
T
,
[
3
]
.
Yamazaki showed the following better estimates of (
1.3
) than
[
27
]

(1.4)

\omega\left(T\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|T\right\|+\omega\left(\widetilde{T}\right)\right).

2.
Further discussion of
\omega_{f}

In this section, we discuss the quantity
\omega_{f}
. This includes basic properties and possible relations with the numerical radius
\omega
and the operator norm
\|\cdot\|
. More applications to numerical radius bounds will be discussed too.

We begin with the following basic properties of
\omega_{f}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing function with
f(0)=0
. Then

(i)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)=0\Leftrightarrow{{T}_{1}}=\cdots={{T}_{n}}=0
.

(ii)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left(\alpha{{T}_{1}},\ldots,\alpha{{T}_{n}}\right)=\left|\alpha\right|{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, provided that
f
is multiplicative.

(iii)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}}+T_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}+T_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)+{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},\ldots,T_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)
, provided that
f
is geometrically convex. That is,
f\left(\sqrt{ab}\right)\leq\sqrt{f(a)f(b)}.

(iv)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)={{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T^{*}}_{1}},\ldots,{{T^{*}}_{n}}\right).

(v)

If
{{U}_{1}},\ldots,{{U}_{n}}
are unitary, then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left(U_{1}^{*}{{T}_{1}}{{U}_{1}},\ldots,U_{n}^{*}{{T}_{n}}{{U}_{n}}\right)={{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right).

(vi)

If
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an injective function such that
g(0)=0
, and
f\circ g^{-1}
is convex, then

\omega_{f}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\leq\omega_{g}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}).

Proof.

The first, second, and fourth assertions immediately follow the definition of
\omega_{f}.
For (iii), assume that
f
is an increasing geometrically convex function. Then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle\left({T_{j}}+{T_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle+\left\langle{{T_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle{{T_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)},

where we obtain the last inequality by the triangle inequality and the fact that
f
is increasing.
On the other hand, since
f
is geometrically convex, it follows that
[
17
, Corollary 1.1]

{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle{{T_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)\leq{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)+{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{{T_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right),

which implies,

{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle\left({T_{j}}+{{T_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)\leq{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)+{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{{T_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right).

Consequently,

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}}+{{T_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}+{{T_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right)\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)+{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}},\ldots,{{T_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right).

To prove (v), we have

\displaystyle{{\omega}_{f}}\left(U_{1}^{*}{{T}_{1}}{{U}_{1}},\ldots,U_{n}^{*}{{T}_{n}}{{U}_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle U_{j}^{*}{{T}_{j}}{{U}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|x\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{{T}_{j}}{{U}_{j}}x,{{U}_{j}}x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|y\right\|=1}{\mathop{\sup}}\,{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{{T}_{j}}y,y\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)

\displaystyle={{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right).

Finally, for (vi), we note first that convexity of
f\circ{{g}^{-1}}
, together with the facts that
f(0)=g(0)=0
, implies

fo{{g}^{-1}}\left(a\right)+f\circ{{g}^{-1}}\left(b\right)\leq f\circ{{g}^{-1}}\left(a+b\right);\;a,b\geq 0

thanks to Lemma
1.3
. Since
{{f}^{-1}}
is an increasing function, then

{{f}^{-1}}\left(f\circ{{g}^{-1}}\left(a\right)+f\circ{{g}^{-1}}\left(b\right)\right)\leq{{g}^{-1}}\left(a+b\right).

Now, replacing
a
and
b
by
g\left(a\right)
and
g\left(b\right)
, we get

{{f}^{-1}}\left(f\left(a\right)+f\left(b\right)\right)\leq{{g}^{-1}}\left(g\left(a\right)+g\left(b\right)\right).

The last inequality can be extended to
n
-tuple as follows

{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({{a}_{j}}\right)}\right)\leq{{g}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{g\left({{a}_{j}}\right)}\right);\;a_{j}\geq 0.

Now, let
x\in\mathscr{H}
be a unit vector. Replacing
a_{j}
in the above inequality by
|\left<T_{j}x,x\right>|
, then taking the supremum implies

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq{{\omega}_{g}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we attempt to find a relation between
{{\omega}_{f}}
and
\omega
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing convex function with
f\left(0\right)=0
. Then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)}.

Proof.

Since
f
is convex increasing, it follows that
f^{-1}
is increasing and concave. By Lemma
1.3
, we have

(2.1)

f^{-1}(a+b)\leq f^{-1}(a)+f^{-1}(b);\;a,b\geq 0.

Further, since
f
is convex, superadditivity of
f
implies

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({{a}_{j}}\right)}\leq f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{{a}_{j}}}\right)

for any
{{a}_{j}}\in J
. Monotony of
f^{-1}
then implies

(2.2)

{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({{a}_{j}}\right)}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{{a}_{j}}}.

By replacing
{{a}_{i}}
by
\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|
in (
2.2
), we obtain

{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|},

for all unit vectors
x\in\mathscr{H}.

Now, by taking supremum over unit vectors
x\in\mathscr{H}
, we get

(2.3)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)},

as desired.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

For any
x\in\mathscr{H}
with
\|x\|=1
, it holds

|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle|\leq\omega\left({T_{j}}\right).

If
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is increasing, we get

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)\right)}.

This implies

\omega_{f}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)=\sup_{\|x\|=1}f^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)\right)\leq f^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f\left(\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)\right)\right).

Now, if
f
is convex (and increasing of course),
f^{-1}
is concave (and increasing), hence
f^{-1}
is subadditive. That is

f^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f\left(\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)\right)\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}f^{-1}\left(f\left(\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)\right)\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)}.

Thus, we have shown that if
T_{j}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous increasing convex function then

(2.4)

\omega_{f}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\leq f^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f\left(\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)\right)\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}f^{-1}\left(f\left(\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)\right)\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\omega\left({T_{j}}\right)}.

This indeed provides a considerable refinement of (
2.3
). We notice that the condition
f(0)
is unnecessary here.

In the following theorem, we present the
\omega_{f}
version of the first inequality in (
1.1
). We notice that (
2.3
) provides the
\omega_{f}
version of the second inequality in (
1.1
) because
\omega(T_{j})\leq\|T_{j}\|.
In fact, (
2.4
) provides further details than (
2.3
). However, we need to be cautious here as (
2.4
) is valid for convex functions, while the next is for concave functions.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing concave function with
f\left(0\right)=0
. Then

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{T_{j}}}\right\|\leq\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}\right)\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right).

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathscr{H}
be a unit vector. Since
f
is concave with
f(0)=0
,
f^{-1}
is convex with
f^{-1}(0)=0.
Applying Lemma
1.3
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{f}(T_{1},\cdots,T_{n})

\displaystyle\geq f^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(|\left<T_{j}x,x\right>|)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\left<T_{j}x,x\right>|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left<T_{j}x,x\right>\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left<\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}\right)x,x\right>\right|.

Taking the supremum over unit vectors
x\in\mathscr{H}
, we obtain
\omega_{f}(T_{1},\cdots,T_{n})\geq\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}\right)
. The result follows immediately from (
1.1
).
∎

The following result is concerned with some lower bounds for
\omega_{f}(\cdot).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. Then

(2.5)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\geq\sup_{|\lambda_{j}|\leq 1}\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{n}T_{j}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{|\lambda_{j}|\leq 1}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{n}T_{j}\right\|.

Proof.

By convexity of
f
we have, for any
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda_{j}|\leq 1
and any unit vector
x\in\mathscr{H},

\displaystyle f^{-1}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}f\left(|\big\langle T_{j}x,x\big\rangle|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}|\big\langle T_{j}x,x\big\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\langle\frac{\lambda_{j}}{n}T_{j}x,x\Big\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\Big\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{n}T_{j}x,x\Big\rangle\right|.\

Taking the supremum over
x\in\mathscr{H}
with
\|x\|=1
yields

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\geq\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{n}T_{j}\right),

for any
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
with
|\lambda_{j}|\leq 1
. Therefore,

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\geq\sup_{|\lambda_{j}|\leq 1}\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{n}T_{j}\right).

The second inequality follows quickly from (
1.1
).
∎

On making use of inequality (
2.5
), we find different lower bounds for
{{\omega}_{f}}
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. Then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{n}\max\{\omega(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega(T_{n})\}\geq\frac{1}{2n}\max\{\|T_{1}\|,\ldots,\|T_{n}\|\}.

Proof.

For any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we consider
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\lambda_{i}=1
and
\lambda_{j}=0
if
j\neq i
. Then, by (
2.5
), we have

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{n}\omega(T_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2n}\|T_{j}\|,

for any
1\leq j\leq n
, and this completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. Then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{n}\max\left\{\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pm T_{j}\right)\right\}\geq\frac{1}{2n}\max\left\{\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pm T_{j}\right\|\right\}.

Proof.

It is a simple consequence of (
2.5
) where we consider
\lambda_{j}=\pm 1
for
1\leq j\leq n.

∎

In the previous statement we can consider
\lambda_{j}=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
with
\theta\in[0,2\pi].

Remark 2.2
.

From Corollary
2.2
, we get

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},{{T}_{2}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega\left({{T}_{1}}+{{T}_{2}}\right).

Let
T=B+\mathrm{i}C
be the Cartesian decomposition of the operator
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
. Setting
{{T}_{1}}=B
and
{{T}_{2}}=\mathrm{i}C
, we infer that

{{\omega}_{f}}\left(B,C\right)={{\omega}_{f}}\left(B,\mathrm{i}C\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega\left(B+\mathrm{i}C\right)=\frac{1}{2}\omega\left(T\right).

Remark 2.3
.

Letting
{{T}_{1}}={{T}_{2}}=\cdots={{T}_{n}}=T
. From Theorem
2.1
, we get

(2.6)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,\ldots,T\right)\leq n~\omega\left(T\right).

On the other hand, by Corollary
2.2
, we infer that

(2.7)

{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,\ldots,T\right)\geq\omega\left(T\right).

Combining two inequalities (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we reach to

\omega\left(T\right)\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,\ldots,T\right)\leq n~\omega\left(T\right).

In the following, we present a lower bound for the generalized Davis-Wielandt radius introduced in the introduction.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing concave function with
f\left(0\right)=0
. Then

\left\|\mathfrak{R}T+{{T}^{*}}T\right\|+\frac{\left|\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right)-\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\right|}{2}\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right).

Proof.

From Theorem
2.2
, we have

\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right).

Since
\omega\left(X\right)=\omega\left({{X}^{*}}\right)
for any
X\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
, we get

\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{R}T+{{T}^{*}}T\right\|+\frac{\left|\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right)-\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\right|}{2}

\displaystyle=\omega\left(\mathfrak{R}T+{{T}^{*}}T\right)+\frac{\left|\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right)-\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\right|}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right),\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)}{2}+\frac{\left|\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right)-\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\right|}{2}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right),\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right),

as desired.
∎

We notice that Corollary
2.3
provides some possible relation between
\omega_{f}(T,T^{*}T)
and
\|\mathfrak{R}T+T^{*}T\|
when
f
is a concave function. In contrast, the following corollary presents a possible relation between these quantities when
f
is convex.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. Then

\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{\omega\left(T\right),{{\left\|T\right\|}^{2}}\right\}\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right),

and

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathfrak{R}T+{{T}^{*}}T\right\|+\frac{\left|\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right)-\omega\left({{T}^{*}}+{{T}^{*}}T\right)\right|}{4}\leq{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right).

Proof.

Employing Corollary
2.1
, gives

\displaystyle{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{\omega\left(T\right),\omega\left({{T}^{*}}T\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{\omega\left(T\right),\left\|{{T}^{*}}T\right\|\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{\omega\left(T\right),{{\left\|T\right\|}^{2}}\right\}.

This proves the first inequality. To establish the second inequality, by Corollary
2.2
, we have

{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega\left(T+{{T}^{*}}T\right).

Applying the same arguments as in the proof of Corollary
2.3
indicates the expected result.
∎

3.
More elaborated relations with the numerical radius

In 1994, Furuta
[
11
]
proved an attractive generalization of Kato’s (Cauchy–Schwarz) inequality, for an arbitrary
T\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
, as follows

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{T\left|T\right|^{\alpha+\beta-1}x,y}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}\leq\left\langle{\left|T\right|^{2\alpha}x,x}\right\rangle\left\langle{\left|T^{*}\right|^{2\beta}y,y}\right\rangle

for any
x,y\in\mathscr{H}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\left[0,1\right]
with
\alpha+\beta\geq 1
.

In the following result, we present an upper bound of
\omega_{f}
for operators of the form
T|T|^{\alpha+\beta-1}
appearing in (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing continuous geometrically convex function. If
p,q>1
are such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}}{{\left|{{T}_{1}}\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}{{\left|{{T}_{n}}\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\right)\leq\left\|{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left(\frac{1}{p}{{f}^{\frac{p}{2}}}\left({{\left|{{T}_{j}}\right|}^{2\alpha}}\right)+\frac{1}{q}{{f}^{\frac{q}{2}}}\left({{\left|T_{j}^{*}\right|}^{2\beta}}\right)\right)}\right)\right\|,

for any
\alpha,\beta\in\left[0,1\right]
with
\alpha+\beta\geq 1
.

Proof.

Employing (
3.1
) for the
n
-tuple operators
\left(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\right)
, by setting
y=x
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}\left|T_{j}\right|^{\alpha+\beta-1}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\langle{\left|T_{j}\right|^{2\alpha}x,x}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle{\left|T^{*}_{j}\right|^{2\beta}x,x}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T_{j}\right|^{2\alpha}x,x}\right\rangle\right)f^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T^{*}_{j}\right|^{2\beta}x,x}\right\rangle\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T_{j}\right|^{2\alpha}x,x}\right\rangle\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f^{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T^{*}_{j}\right|^{2\beta}x,x}\right\rangle\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T_{j}\right|^{2\alpha}x,x}\right\rangle\right)}+\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f^{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T^{*}_{j}\right|^{2\beta}x,x}\right\rangle\right)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}\left|T_{j}\right|^{\alpha+\beta-1}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq f^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T_{j}\right|^{2\alpha}x,x}\right\rangle\right)}+\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f^{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\left\langle{\left|T^{*}_{j}\right|^{2\beta}x,x}\right\rangle\right)}\right).

We get the required result by taking the supremum over all unit vector
x\in\mathscr{H}
.
∎

A more straightforward upper bound of
\omega_{f}
can be stated as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. Then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\left\|{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left(\frac{f\left({{\left|{{T}_{j}}\right|}^{2\alpha}}\right)+f\left({{\left|T_{j}^{*}\right|}^{2\left(1-\alpha\right)}}\right)}{2}\right)}\right)\right\|,

for any
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
.

Proof.

For
0\leq\alpha\leq 1,
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, together with the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, implies

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}x,x}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle{\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}x,x}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{\frac{{\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}+\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}}}{2}x,x}\right\rangle,

for the unit vector
x\in\mathscr{H}.

Noting that
f
is increasing, then applying Lemma
1.1
we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\langle{\left({\frac{\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}+\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}}{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle{f\left({\frac{\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}+\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}}{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle{\left({\frac{{f\left({\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}}\right)+f\left({\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}}\right)}}{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left({\frac{{f\left({\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}}\right)+f\left({\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}}\right)}}{2}}\right)x},x}\right\rangle,

which implies

\displaystyle f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq f^{-1}\left(\left\langle{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left({\frac{{f\left({\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}}\right)+f\left({\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}}\right)}}{2}}\right)x},x}\right\rangle\right).

We get the required result by taking the supremum over all unit vectors
x\in\mathscr{H}
, noting that
f^{-1}
is also increasing.
∎

Another bound, similar to that in Theorem
3.3
, can be stated as follows. The proof is very similar to that of Theorem
3.3
, so we do not include it here.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. If
p_{j}>0
so that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}=1
, then

\displaystyle{{\omega}_{f}}\left(p_{1}{{T}_{1}},\ldots,p_{n}{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\left\|f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}\left({\frac{{f\left({\left|{T_{j}}\right|^{2\alpha}}\right)+f\left({\left|{T_{j}^{*}}\right|^{2\left({1-\alpha}\right)}}\right)}}{2}}\right)\right)\right\|,

for any
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
.

In the following result, a super-multiplicative function refers to a function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
f(a)f(b)\leq f(ab)
for all
a,b\in[0,\infty).
We notice that all power functions
f(t)=t^{r},r>0
are such functions.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing, convex and super-multiplicative function. Then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\left\|{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sqrt{n\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\frac{T_{j}^{*}{{T}_{j}}+{{T}_{j}}T_{j}^{*}}{2}\right)}}\right)\right\|.

Proof.

Let
B_{j}+\mathrm{i}C_{j}
be the Cartesian decomposition of the Hilbert space operators
T_{j}
, for
j=1,\cdots,n
. We have

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{B_{j}x,x}\right\rangle^{2}+\left\langle{C_{j}x,x}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{B_{j}^{2}x,x}\right\rangle+\left\langle{C_{j}^{2}x,x}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle,

where we have used Lemma
1.1
to obtain the last inequality, noting that both
B_{j}
and
C_{j}
are self-adjoint and that
f(t)=t^{2}
is convex.
But since
f
is increasing, super-multiplicative and convex, we have

\displaystyle f^{2}\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|}\right)\leq f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}}\right)\leq f\left({\left\langle{\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{f\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left(f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|}\right)\right)^{2}}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({\left\langle{\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle}\right)}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle{f\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle}.

Applying Jensen’s inequality to the function
g(t)=t^{2}
implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|}\right)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left(f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|}\right)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle{f\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle},

and this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|}\right)}\leq\left(n\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle{f\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Also, since
f
is increasing, we get

\displaystyle f^{-1}\left({\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({\left|{\left\langle{T_{j}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}}\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq f^{-1}\left({\left(n\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle{f\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x,x}\right\rangle}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=f^{-1}\left({\sqrt{n}\left\langle{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({B_{j}^{2}+C_{j}^{2}}\right)x},x}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=f^{-1}\left({\sqrt{n}\left\langle{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left({\frac{T_{j}^{*}T_{j}+T_{j}T_{j}^{*}}{2}}\right)x},x}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

We get the required result by taking the supremum over all unit vector
x\in\mathscr{H}
.
∎

In the following remark, we explain the significance of Theorem
3.4
.

Remark 3.1
.

Taking
f\left(t\right)=t^{2}
,
t\geq 0
, Theorem
3.4
implies

(3.2)

\displaystyle{{\omega}_{\rm{e}}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\left\|{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left({T_{j}^{*}T_{j}+T_{j}T_{j}^{*}}\right)^{2}}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

In particular, choosing

and
T_{1}=T
, we get

\displaystyle{{\omega}}\left(T\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left\|{T^{*}T+TT^{*}}\right\|},

or

\displaystyle{{\omega}}^{2}\left(T\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{T^{*}T+TT^{*}}\right\|,

which is an outstanding result of Kittaneh (
1.2
). A more general form of the inequality (
3.2
) could be stated by taking
f\left(t\right)=t^{p}
,
t\geq 0

\left(p\geq 1\right)

\displaystyle{{\omega}_{p}^{p}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2^{\frac{p}{2}}}\left\|{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left({T_{j}^{*}T_{j}+T_{j}T_{j}^{*}}\right)^{p}}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}

holds for all

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
B_{j}+\mathrm{i}C_{j}
be the Cartesian decomposition of the Hilbert space operators
T_{j}\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)

(j=1,\ldots,n)
. Let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function that satisfies
f\left(0\right)=0
. Then

\displaystyle{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq\left\|{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|{{B}_{j}}\right|+\left|{{C}_{j}}\right|\right)}\right)\right\|.

Proof.

Let
B_{j}+\mathrm{i}C_{j}
be the Cartesian decomposition of the Hilbert space operators
T_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. If
x\in\mathscr{H}
is a unit vector, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{{T}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\sqrt{{{\left\langle{{B}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle}^{2}}+{{\left\langle{{C}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle}^{2}}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{{B}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle{{C}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\langle\left(\left|{{B}_{j}}\right|+\left|{{C}_{j}}\right|\right)x,x\right\rangle\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle f\left(\left|{{B}_{j}}\right|+\left|{{C}_{j}}\right|\right)x,x\right\rangle}

where we have used Lemma
1.1
twice to obtain the last two inequalities.
Thus, since
f
is increasing,

\displaystyle{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{{T}_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle f\left(\left|{{B}_{j}}\right|+\left|{{C}_{j}}\right|\right)x,x\right\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle={{f}^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|{{B}_{j}}\right|+\left|{{C}_{j}}\right|\right)}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{f}^{-1}}\left(\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|{{B}_{j}}\right|+\left|{{C}_{j}}\right|\right)}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|{{B}_{j}}\right|+\left|{{C}_{j}}\right|\right)}\right)\right\|,

where we obtain the last equality because
f
is increasing.
∎

Now, extending (
1.4
) to
\omega_{f}
, we have the following.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathscr{H})
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. Then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left({{T}_{1}},\ldots,{{T}_{n}}\right)\leq{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\left(\frac{f\left(\left\|{T_{j}}\right\|\right)+f\left(\omega\left(\widetilde{{T_{j}}}\right)\right)}{2}\right)}\right).

Proof.

For each
T_{j}
, let
T_{j}=U_{j}|T_{j}|
be the polar decomposition of
T_{j}
. By Lemma
1.2
, if
x\in\mathscr{H}
is a unit vector, it follows that
|\left<T_{j}x,x\right>|\leq\mathfrak{R}\left\{e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\left<T_{j}x,x\right>\right\}
, for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for all
\theta
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{R}\left\{{{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+{U_{j}}\right)\left|{T_{j}}\right|\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+U_{j}^{*}\right)x,x\right\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}-{U_{j}}\right)\left|{T_{j}}\right|\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}-U_{j}^{*}\right)x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+{U_{j}}\right)\left|{T_{j}}\right|\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+U_{j}^{*}\right)x,x\right\rangle.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+{U_{j}}\right)\left|{T_{j}}\right|\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+U_{j}^{*}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\langle\left(\frac{\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+{U_{j}}\right)\left|{T_{j}}\right|\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+U_{j}^{*}\right)}{4}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\left\langle f\left(\frac{\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+{U_{j}}\right)\left|{T_{j}}\right|\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+U_{j}^{*}\right)}{4}\right)x,x\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\|\frac{\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+{U_{j}}\right)\left|{T_{j}}\right|\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+U_{j}^{*}\right)}{4}\right\|\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\|\frac{{{\left|{T_{j}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left({{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+U_{j}^{*}\right)\left({{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}+{U_{j}}\right){{\left|{T_{j}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}}{4}\right\|\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\|\frac{2\left|{T_{j}}\right|+{{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}\widetilde{{T_{j}}}+{{e}^{-\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}{{\left(\widetilde{{T_{j}}}\right)}^{*}}}{4}\right\|\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{f\left(\left\|\frac{\left|{T_{j}}\right|+\mathfrak{R}{{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}\widetilde{{T_{j}}}}{2}\right\|\right)}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\left\|f\left(\frac{\left|{T_{j}}\right|+{{\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}e}}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}\widetilde{{T_{j}}}}{2}\right)\right\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\left\|f\left(\left|{T_{j}}\right|\right)+f\left({{\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}e}}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}\widetilde{{T_{j}}}\right)\right\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\left(\left\|f\left(\left|{T_{j}}\right|\right)\right\|+\left\|f\left({{\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}e}}^{\mathrm{i}{{\theta}}}}\widetilde{{T_{j}}}\right)\right\|\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\left(f\left\|~\left|{T_{j}}\right|~\right\|+f\left(\left\|{{\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}e}}^{\mathrm{i}{\theta}}}\widetilde{{T_{j}}}\right\|\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\left(f\left(\left\|{T_{j}}\right\|\right)+f\left(\omega\left(\widetilde{{T_{j}}}\right)\right)\right).}

So,

{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{f\left(\left|\left\langle{T_{j}}x,x\right\rangle\right|\right)}\right)\leq{{f}^{-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\left(\frac{f\left(\left\|{T_{j}}\right\|\right)+f\left(\omega\left(\widetilde{{T_{j}}}\right)\right)}{2}\right)}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

We close this paper by introducing an upper bound for the generalized Davis-Wielandt radius.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{B}\left(\mathscr{H}\right)
with the polar decomposition
T=U|T|
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing convex function. Then

{{\omega}_{f}}\left(T,{{T}^{*}}T\right)\leq{{f}^{-1}}\left(\frac{f\left(\left\|T\right\|\right)+f\left(\omega\left(\widetilde{T}\right)\right)+f\left({{\left\|T\right\|}^{2}}\right)+f\left(\omega\left(\left|T\right|U\left|T\right|\right)\right)}{2}\right).

Abstract

In this paper, we consider quadratic forward-backward SDEs (QFBSDEs), for which the drift in the forward equation does not satisfy the standard globally Lipschitz condition and the driver of the backward system possesses nonlinearity of type
f(|y|)|z|^{2},
where
f
is any locally integrable function. We prove both the Malliavin and classical derivative of the QFBSDE and provide representations of these processes. We study a numerical approximation of this system in the sense of
[
18
]
in which the authors assume that the drift is Lipschitz and the driver of the BSDE is quadratic in the traditional sense (i.e.,
f
is a positive constant). We show that the rate of convergence is the same as in
[
18
]
.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we address the problem of the Malliavin and the classical differentiability of a class of quadratic forward-backward SDEs (FBSDEs) with rough drift. FBSDEs have attracted a lot of interest in the last four decades due to their applications to optimal control, financial/insurance mathematics and the theory of PDE via the non-linear Feynman-Kac formula. Of particular interest is the class of BSDEs whose drivers grow quadratically in the control variable
Z
. Such a BSDE appears for example in exponential utility maximisation or in the Epstein-Zin utility maximisation problems. To the best of our knowledge, the first result on existence and uniqueness of BSDEs with quadratic drivers and bounded terminal value is due to Kobylanski (
[
23
]
). This result was extended to the case of unbounded terminal value in
[
8
,
9
]
and in other different ways in
[
17
,
30
,
12
,
5
]
.

Recently the authors in
[
3
]
studied a new class of unbounded quadratic BSDE for which the generator
g
has the following growth condition
|g(t,y,z)|\leq C(1+|y|+f(y)|z|^{2})
, where
f
is an integrable function. Their approach is based on exponential-type transformation and an Itô-Krylov formula for BSDEs. This result was extended in
[
2
]
to the case of locally integrable function
f
by using the so called
domination method
.

Another question of importance that arises in the study of BSDEs is the characterisation of the control process
Z
. If the coefficients of the FBSDEs are smooth enough,
Z
is given as the ”derivative”, either in the sense of the non linear Feynman-Kac formula or in the Malliavin sense via the Clark-Ocone-Hausmann formula. Moreover,
(Z_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
has a continuous version given by the Malliavin derivative of the backward equation
(D_{t}Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. The later turns out to be a crucial concept when one deals with novel discretisations of BSDEs implemented with deep learning regressions (see for instance
[
31
]
). When the parameters are not smooth, the existence of Malliavin and classical differentiable solutions to FBSDEs remain a challenging question.

Smoothness of solutions of FBSDEs, in both the classical and Malliavin sense when the coefficients are smooth enough were established in
[
33
,
21
]
. In the quadratic case, the first results were derived by the authors in
[
1
]
assuming that the driver is of the form
g(t,x,y,z)=\ell(t,x,y,z)+\alpha|z|^{2}
with
\ell\in C^{1}
and Lipschitz continuous in
(x,y,z)
. This work was then extended to non-linear quadratic generators in
[
19
]
. Their method for establishing the classical differentiability relies on differences of difference quotients together with the completeness of the vector space. In order to prove the Malliavin differentiability, they proposed an approximation procedure via a family of truncated BSDEs and a compactness criterion argument. Using a technique based on Kunita’s method (solving an abstract BSDE with stochastic Lipschitz conditions), the authors in
[
7
]
proved a differentiability result in the classical sense for solutions to quadratic FBSDEs. Other related results include the work
[
20
]
for differentiability of FBSDE driven by continuous martingales with quadratic growth and the work
[
15
]
for classical differentiability of FBSDEs with polynomial growth (see also
[
37
]
).

The results obtained in the aforementioned papers do not cover the case of non uniformly Lipschitz drift coefficients. In
[
34
]
, the authors established a result on Malliavin derivative solutions to coupled FBSDEs with discontinuous coefficients. Their method exploits the regularization effect of the Brownian motion when the diffusion coefficient is a constant and the regularity of the
weak decoupling field
combined with a compactness criterion argument. However, the authors did not provide a representation satisfied by the Malliavin derivatives in terms of BSDEs. This is mainly due to the very mild assumptions that were considered there.

Another motivation of this paper concerns the numerical approximation of the solution to quadratic FBSDEs with rough drift. More precisely, we consider the following Markovian-type BSDE

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{t}=x+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})\mathrm{d}B_{s},\\
Y_{t}=\phi(X_{T})+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}g(s,X_{s},Y_{s},Z_{s})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

The explicit solutions to equation (
1.1
) are generally unknown, thus, developing numerical methods to approximate these solutions becomes a good alternative. Assuming that the coefficients in (
1.1
) are Lipschitz continuous, such problem was studied in
[
11
]
(see also
[
6
,
38
]
). To the best of our knowledge, the case with quadratic drivers was first investigated in
[
19
]
. Their method consists in approximating solution of (
1.1
) by the solution
(Y^{n},Z^{n})
of a family of truncated BSDEs given by:

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{n}=\phi(X_{T})+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}g(s,X_{s},Y_{s}^{n},\rho_{n}(Z_{s}^{n}))\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}B_{s},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(1.2)

where
\rho_{n}
is a smooth real valued function given by (
4.2
). The truncation errors is studied in the natural norms of the solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Its proof uses the facts
Z^{n}_{t}
has a continuous version given by
D_{t}Y_{t}^{n}=(\nabla_{x}Y_{t}^{n})(\nabla_{x}X_{t})^{-1}
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|Z^{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p}}<\infty
.

By assuming that the drift is in addition Hölder continuous in
t
and the diffusion is a differentiable function of time, the author in
[
36
]
improved the results obtained in
[
19
]
by considering a different scheme. More precisely, the author approximates BSDEs with quadratic generators by BSDEs with Lipschitz generators. The above result was generalised in
[
10
]
for time homogeneous drift and diffusion coefficient satisfying a global Lipchitz condition.

In this paper, we assume that the driver
g
is stochastic Lipschitz in
y
and quadratic of the form
f(|y|)|z|^{2}
, where
f
is any increasing and locally integrable function and the drift is either measurable and bounded or bounded and Hölder continuous. We then study both differentiablity in the Malliavin and classical sense of the solution to the quadratic FBSDE. We follow the method developed in
[
1
,
19
]
and work under much weaker conditions. We also provide the representations of both the Malliavin and classical derivatives of the solution to the FBSDE. One of the main obstacles in this work is the non smoothness of the drift.
In the case of
\beta
-Hölder continuous drift coefficients, using the fact that the first variation process is
\beta
-Hölder continuous in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
14
]
), we derive the representation of classical derivative of the BSDE.

In this work we also provide a truncation error of the numerical approximation. Since the driver
g
is only stochastic Lipschitz in
Y
we also have to truncate the driver in
Y
. Another difficulty is to find the bound of the supremum norm of
Z^{n}
, which depends on the supremum norm of the inverse of the first variation process of the forward equation. We tackle this problem by using once more the regularity of the solution to the Kolmogorov equation associated to the forward equation. This allows us to obtain a transformed SDE with bounded and smooth coefficients from which the sought bound can be obtained. We then achieve the same rate of convergence as in
[
35
]
(see Theorem
6.4
). As an independent result, we also obtain a Zhang’s path regularity theorem in our general framework.

The remaining part of the paper is organised as follows: in Section
2
, we provide basic definitions and results on BSDEs. The existence and uniqueness results as well as the main a priori estimates are given in Section
3
. In Section
4
we study the differentiability for an abstract BSDEs with stochastic Lipschitz generators Section
5
is devoted to the smothness of the solution of the FBSDE whereas Section
6
is concerned with rate of convergence of the numerical approximation of the solution to the BSDE.

Below, we briefly introduce the spaces of Malliavin differentiable random variables
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}.
For more information on Malliavin calculus we refer the reader to

32
]
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be the space of random variables
\xi

\xi=F\Big((\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}^{1,i}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{1})_{1\leq i\leq n},\cdots,(\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}^{d,i}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{d})_{1\leq i\leq n}\Big),

For simplicity, we assume that all

we define

and for

\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
be the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\|\xi\|^{p}_{k,p}=\|\xi\|^{p}_{L^{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\||D^{(i)}\xi|\|^{p}_{(\mathcal{H}^{p})^{i}}.

The operator
D^{(k)}
is a closed linear operator on the space
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}.
Observe that if
\xi\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}

\theta\in(t,T].

\mathbb{D}^{k,\infty}=\cap_{p>1}\mathbb{D}^{k,p}.

For

the set of
\mathbb{R}^{m}\text{-valued}

(i)

For Lebesgue a.a.

(ii)

(t,\omega)\rightarrow D_{\theta}^{k}u(t,\omega)\in(L^{2}([0,T]^{1+k}))^{d\times m}

(iii)

\|u\|_{k,p}^{p}=\||u|\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\||D^{(i)}u|\|^{p}_{(\mathcal{H}^{p})^{1+i}}<\infty.

For example if a process
\zeta\in\mathbb{L}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|_{\mathbb{L}_{1,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}\zeta_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}t\Big],

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|_{\mathbb{L}_{2,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\zeta\|_{\mathbb{L}_{1,2}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta_{1}}D_{\theta_{2}}\zeta_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big].

Some preliminary results

Consider the BSDE

(2.1)

Theorem 2.1

(Malliavin differentiability)
.

Suppose
\xi\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
g:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuously differentiable in
(y,z),
with uniformly bounded derivatives. Suppose for each
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
the process
g(\cdot,y,z)
belongs to
\mathbb{L}_{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with Malliavin derivative denoted by
D_{\theta}g(t,y,z).
Let
(Y,Z)
be the solution of the associated BSDE (
2.1
) and suppose in addition

(i)

(g(t,0,0))_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{H}^{4}(\mathbb{R})
and
\xi\in L^{4}(\mathbb{R}),

(ii)

\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|D_{\theta}\xi|^{2}]\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty,\int_{0}^{T}\|(D_{\theta}g)(t,Y,Z)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty\text{ a.s.}
and for
\text{a.a. }\theta\in[0,T]
and any

|D_{\theta}g(t,y^{1},z^{1})-D_{\theta}g(t,y^{2},z^{2})|\leq K_{\theta}(t)(|y^{1}-y^{2}|+|z^{1}-z^{2}|)\text{ a.s.},

with
\{K_{\theta}(t),0\leq\theta,t\leq T\}
a positive real valued adapted process satisfying
\int_{0}^{T}\|K_{\theta}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty.

Then
(Y,Z)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{1,2}\times(\mathbb{D}^{1,2})^{d}).
Furthermore for each
1\leq i\leq d
a version of

is given by

\displaystyle D_{\theta}^{i}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=0,\quad D_{\theta}Z_{t}=0,\qquad 0\leq t<\theta\leq T;

\displaystyle D_{\theta}^{i}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=D_{\theta}^{i}\xi-\int_{t}^{T}D_{\theta}^{i}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}[(D_{\theta}^{i}g)(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})+\langle(\nabla g)(s,Y_{s},Z_{s}),(D_{\theta}^{i}Y_{s},D_{\theta}^{i}Z_{s})\rangle]\mathrm{d}s\qquad\theta\leq t\leq T.

Moreover

3.
Quadratic BSDEs with nonlinearity of type

The main aim of this section is to study the well posedness and derive an a-priori estimates of the BSDE (
2.1

\xi

satisfy the following assumptions:

Assumption 3.1
.

\xi
is an
\mathfrak{F}_{T}\text{-measurable}
uniformly bounded random variable, i.e.,
\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty;

Assumption 3.2
.

The function
g:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
\mathfrak{F}\text{-predictable}
and continuous in its space variables. There exist
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{y},\Lambda_{z}>0
, and a locally bounded function

such that for all
(t,\omega,y,z),(t,\omega,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\alpha\in[0,1)

\|g(t,0,0)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda_{0}

\displaystyle|g(t,\cdot,y,z)-g(t,\cdot,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{y}\Big(1+|z|^{\alpha}+|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\Big)|y-y^{\prime}|+\Lambda_{z}\Big(1+(f(|y|)+f(|y^{\prime}|))(|z|+|z^{\prime}|)\Big)|z-z^{\prime}|\text{ a.s.}

Remark 3.3

It is readily seen that under Assumptions

|g(t,\cdot,y,z)|\leqslant\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{y}|y|+\Lambda_{z}(|z|+f(|y|)|z|^{2})\text{ a.s.}

Indeed,

\displaystyle|g(t,y,z)|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{y}|y|+\Lambda_{z}|z|+2\Lambda_{z}f(|y|)|z|^{2}\text{ a.s.}

Theorem 3.4

(Existence and uniqueness)

Existence:

Let

4.
Differentiability of parametrised quadratic BSDEs

In this section we discuss the both the Malliavin and the classical differentiablility of the solutions to the BSDEs (
2.1
). We provide below, sufficient conditions for these differentiabilities to hold.

4.1.
Malliavin Differentiability

In this subsection we show under some weak conditions on the generator that the solution
(Y,Z)
to the BSDE (
2.1
) is Malliavin differentiable and the Malliavin derivatives
(D_{u}Y_{t},D_{u}Z_{t})_{u,t\in[0,T]}
are given as solution to a BSDE. In addition to Assumptions
3.1
-
3.2
, we will suppose the following additional assumptions

Assumption 4.1
.

(M1)

The random variable
\xi
belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{1,\infty}.
The function
g:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is adapted, measurable and continuously differentiable in
(y,z)
.

(M2)

For each
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
it holds that
(g(t,y,z))_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathbb{L}_{1,2p}(\mathbb{R})
for all
p\geq 1
. Its Malliavin derivative denoted by
(D_{u}g(t,y,z))_{u,t\in[0,T]}
satisfies

\displaystyle|D_{u}g(t,y,z)|\leq K_{u}(t)(1+|y|+f(|y|)|z|^{\alpha})+\tilde{K}_{u}(t)(1+|z|^{\alpha}+f(|y|)|z|)\text{ a.s.}

for any
(u,t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

\alpha\in(0,1)
. Here
(K_{u}(t))_{u,t\in[0,T]}
and
(\tilde{K}_{u}(t))_{u,t\in[0,T]}
are two positive adapted processes such that for all
p\geq 1

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}|K_{u}(t)|^{2p}\mathrm{d}u+\|\tilde{K}_{u}(t)\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{S}^{2p}}<\infty.

Below, we state the main result in this subsection:

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
g
and
\xi
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
–
3.2
, and
4.1
. Then the solution process
(Y,Z)
to the BSDE (
2.1
) is in
\mathbb{L}_{1,2}\times(\mathbb{L}_{1,2})^{d}
and a version of
(D_{u}Y_{t},D_{u}Z_{t})_{u,t\in[0,T]}
is the unique solution to

\displaystyle D_{u}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=0\text{ and }D_{u}Z_{t}=0,\text{ if }t\in[0,u),

\displaystyle D_{u}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=D_{u}\xi-\int_{t}^{T}D_{u}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{T}\left[(D_{u}g)(s,\Theta_{s})+\langle(\nabla g)(s,\Theta_{s}),D_{u}\Theta_{s}\rangle\right]\mathrm{d}s,\text{ if }t\in[u,T].

(4.1)

Moreover,

The strategy to prove the above theorem is also well known and analogous to
[

18

]
. It is performed in two main steps. We first build a family of truncated BSDEs that approximate the BSDE (

4.1.1.
Family of truncated generators

:
Let us consider the family
(\tilde{\rho}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of smooth (continuously differentiable) real valued functions, that truncated the identity on the real line. We use this family of functions to truncate the variables
y
and
z
simultaneously in the driver
g(\cdot,y,z),
for
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If the procedure is well known (see for example
[
1
,
19
,
35
]
), it is worth mentioning that truncating the two variables at the same time is not so common in the literature. This approach is motivated by the form of the driver which is not uniformly Lipschitz in
y
.

The family of functions
(\tilde{\rho}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are such that:

(a)

(\tilde{\rho}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to the identity. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}

\tilde{\rho}_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}n+1,&\quad x>n+2,\\
x,&\quad|x|\leq n,\\
-(n+1),&\quad x<-(n+2).\end{cases}

(4.2)

In addition
|\tilde{\rho}_{n}(x)|\leq|x|
and
|\tilde{\rho}_{n}(x)|\leq n+1
.

(b)

The derivative
\nabla\tilde{\rho}_{n}
is absolutely uniformly bounded by 1, and converges to 1 locally uniformly.

Let
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence defined by

g_{n}(t,y,z):=g(t,\tilde{\rho}_{n}(y),\rho_{n}(z))\text{ for }(\omega,t,y,z)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,n\in\mathbb{N},

(4.3)

where
\rho_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},z\mapsto\rho_{n}(z)=(\tilde{\rho}_{n}(z_{1}),\ldots,\tilde{\rho}_{n}(z_{d})),n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us consider the following sequence
(Y^{n},Z^{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfying the BSDE

Y_{t}^{n}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}g_{n}(s,Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}B_{s},\qquad t\in[0,T],n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.4)

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using (M1), the family
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies: for

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}g_{n}(t,y,z)|+|\nabla_{z}g_{n}(t,y,z)|\leq\Lambda_{y}(1+(n+1)^{\alpha})+\Lambda_{z}(1+(n+1)f(n+1)).

In addition, using the mean value theorem, we obtain for all

y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},
and

|g_{n}(t,y,z)-g_{n}(t,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})|\leq\Lambda_{n}(|y-y^{\prime}|+|z-z^{\prime}|)
. Thus for each
n\in\mathbb{N},
the family of functions
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Lipschitz continuous in its spatial variables.

4.1.2.
Uniform bounds for solutions to BSDE (
4.4

Here, we will prove that the solution
(Y^{n},Z^{n})
to the BSDE (
4.4

. Recall that
\mathbb{L}_{1,2}
stands for the set of progressively measurable processes
(\eta_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
which are Malliavin differentiable, with
(D_{s}\eta_{t})_{s\leq t\leq T}
having a progressively measurable version and such that
\|\eta\|_{1,2}^{2}:=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{t}|\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{s}\eta_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\Big]<+\infty
.

We first establish the next Lemma, which provides the uniform bounds of solutions to the BSDE (
4.4
) in the Banach space
\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Lemma 4.3
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the BSDE (

which is uniformly bounded in

. Furthermore, there exists

independent of

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\mathcal{E}(Z^{n}*B)\|_{L^{r}}<\infty.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of solution to BSDE (
4.4
) follow directly from standard results in the theory of BSDEs, since the generator
g_{n}
satisfies for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
a Lipschitz condition in the space variables.
Let us also remark that the function
g_{n}
satisfies uniformly Assumption
3.2
. Indeed, for all
(t,y,z),(t,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
we obtain by using the properties satisfied by
\bar{\rho}_{n}
and the function

\displaystyle|g_{n}(t,y,z)-g_{n}(t,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=|g(t,\bar{\rho}_{n}(y),\rho_{n}(z))-g(t,\bar{\rho}_{n}(y^{\prime}),\rho_{n}(z^{\prime}))|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{y}\left(1+|\rho_{n}(z)|^{\alpha}+|\rho_{n}(z^{\prime})|^{\alpha}\right)|\bar{\rho}_{n}(y)-\bar{\rho}_{n}(y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\quad+\Lambda_{z}\left(1+(f(|\bar{\rho}_{n}(y)|)+f(|\bar{\rho}_{n}(y^{\prime})|))(|\rho_{n}(z)|+|\rho_{n}(z)|)\right)|\rho_{n}(z)-\rho_{n}(z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{y}(1+|z|^{\alpha}+|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha})|y-y^{\prime}|+\Lambda_{z}(1+(f(|y|)+f(|y^{\prime}|)(|z|+|z^{\prime}|))|z-z^{\prime}|

Moreover, a similar condition to (

\displaystyle|g_{n}(t,y,z)|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{y}|\tilde{\rho}_{n}(y)|+\Lambda_{z}(|\rho_{n}(z)|+f(|\tilde{\rho}_{n}(y)|)|\rho_{n}(z)|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{y}|y|+\Lambda_{z}(|z|+f(|y|)|z|^{2}).

Hence conditions on the existence of a unique solution to the BSDE (

) in Theorem
3.4
are satisfied. Therefore, from (

) we deduce that:
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\mathcal{E}(Z^{n}*B)\|_{BMO}<\infty.
From (P2) in Lemma

we then deduce the existence of such a constant

for which the bound

holds. This concludes the proof.

\Theta^{n}=(Y^{n},Z^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to the BSDE (

) belongs to
\mathbb{L}_{1,2}\times(\mathbb{L}_{1,2})^{d}
. A version of

is given by

\displaystyle D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}=

\displaystyle 0\text{ and }D_{u}Z_{t}^{n}=0,\text{ if }t\in[0,u),

\displaystyle D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}=

\displaystyle D_{u}\xi-\int_{t}^{T}D_{u}Z_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}B_{s}

(4.5)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\left[(D_{u}g_{n})(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})+\langle(\nabla g_{n})(s,\Theta_{s}^{n}),D_{u}\Theta_{s}^{n}\rangle\right]\mathrm{d}s,\text{ if }t\in[u,T].

Moreover
\{D_{t}Y_{t}^{n},0\leq t\leq T\}
defined by the above equation is a version of
\{Z_{t}^{n},0\leq t\leq T\}.

Furthermore for any

the following holds:

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\|D_{u}Y^{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2p}}^{2p}+\|D_{u}Z^{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2p}}^{2p}\Big]\mathrm{d}u<\infty.

(4.6)

Proof.

The proof of the first statement concerning the Malliavin derivatives
(DY^{n},DZ^{n})
of
\Theta^{n}=(Y^{n},Z^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and the representation (
4.5
) follows from Theorem
2.1
under Assumptions
4.1
.

Let us now focus on the proof of the bound (
4.6
). Note that, the generators
F^{n}
in equation (
4.5
) is stochastic Lipschitz in its second and third variables. Indeed, from the properties of the functions
\rho_{,}\tilde{\rho}_{n}
and
f

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}g_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{y}(1+|Z^{n}_{t}|^{\alpha})

\displaystyle|\nabla_{z}g_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{z}(1+f(|Y^{n}_{t}|)|Z^{n}_{t}|).

Then, Lemma
4.3
implies that:
\nabla_{y}g_{n}*B:=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\nabla_{y}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s})\mathrm{d}B_{s}
and
\nabla_{z}g_{n}*B:=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\nabla_{z}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s})\mathrm{d}B_{s}
are both BMO martingales, uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, the subsequent bound follows from Lemma
3.7
: for
p>1
, there exists

such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}|^{2p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|D_{u}Z_{s}^{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big(|D_{u}\xi|^{2pq}+\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}|(D_{u}g_{n})(t,Y_{t}^{n},Z_{t}^{n})|\mathrm{d}t\Big\}^{2pq}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

and
q
only depends on
T,p
and
\|Z^{n}*B\|_{BMO}.
Hence, the Jensen’s inequality for concave functions leads to:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}|^{2p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|D_{u}Z_{s}^{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{p}\Big]\Big\}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[|D_{u}\xi|^{2pq}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|(D_{u}g_{n})(t,Y_{t}^{n},Z_{t}^{n})|\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{2pq}\Big]\mathrm{d}u\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since
\xi\in\mathbb{D}^{1,\infty}
, it follows that the first term on the right side of the above inequality is finite. As for the second term is also finite, set
\gamma=2pq
and recall that the sequence of functions
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is given by equation (
4.3
). From assumption (M2), we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|(D_{u}g_{n})(t,Y_{t}^{n},Z_{t}^{n})|\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}K_{u}(t)(1+|\tilde{\rho}(Y_{t}^{n})|+f(|\tilde{\rho}(Y_{t}^{n})|)|{\rho}(Z_{t}^{n})|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\tilde{K}_{u}(t)|^{2}+\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}(1+|{\rho}(Z_{t}^{n})|^{\alpha}+f(|\tilde{\rho}(Y_{t}^{n})|)|{\rho}(Z_{t}^{n})|)\mathrm{d}t\Big\}^{2}\Big)^{\gamma}

Using the properties of
\tilde{\rho}\text{ and }\rho
, the fact that
f
is increasing, Hölder’s inequality and the inequality
|ab|\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
, we deduce the following:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|(D_{u}g_{n})(t,Y_{t}^{n},Z_{t}^{n})|\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}K_{u}(t)(1+|Y_{t}^{n}|+f(|Y_{t}^{n}|)|Z_{t}^{n}|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\tilde{K}_{u}(t)|^{2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|Z_{t}^{n}|^{\alpha}+f(|Y_{t}^{n}|)|Z_{t}^{n}|)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{2}\Big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|K_{u}(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}(1+|Y_{t}^{n}|+[f(|Y_{t}^{n}|)|Z_{t}^{n}|]^{\alpha})^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\tilde{K}_{u}(t)|^{2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|Z_{t}^{n}|^{\alpha}+f(|Y_{t}^{n}|)|Z_{t}^{n}|)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{2}\Big)^{\gamma}<\infty.

The proof will be complete, if we prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|Y_{t}^{n}|+f(|Y_{t}^{n}|)|Z_{t}^{n}|^{\alpha})^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|Z_{t}^{n}|^{\alpha}+f(|Y_{t}^{n}|)|Z_{t}^{n}|)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{2}\right)^{\gamma}<\infty.

(4.7)

Let us notice that, for any
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have the following elementary inequality:

f(|y|)^{2}|z|^{2\alpha}\leq(1-\alpha)\big(f(|y|)\big)^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}+\alpha|z|^{2}\leq(1-\alpha)\big(\sup_{0\leq y\leq\|y\|}\varphi(y)\big)^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}+\alpha|z|^{2}.

Then the first summand (
4.7
) writes

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|Y_{t}^{n}|+f(|Y_{t}^{n}|)|Z_{t}^{n}|^{\alpha})^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq CT+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}^{n}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{t}^{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq CT+C\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|^{2\gamma q}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|g_{n}(s,0,0)|\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{2\gamma q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

where, we used the Lemma
3.7
again to justify the last inequality. Using almost the same technique as previously, one can derive a uniform bound for the last summand in (
4.7
).
∎

We are now in position to prove the main theorem of this section.

Proof of Theorem

.

Let us define by

4.3
and
4.5
, there exists a subsequence

. Thus, it is readily seen that for almost

, the solution
(Y_{t},Z_{t})

) is Malliavin differentiable and
(D_{u}Y_{t},D_{u}Z_{t})=(U_{u,t},V_{u,t})

in
\Omega\times[0,T]\times[0,T]
. To conclude, we only need to prove that each term in equation (

Using assumption (M1) and the dominated convergence theorem, one can show that
\int_{0}^{T}\langle(\nabla g_{n})(s,\Theta_{s}^{n}),D_{u}\Theta_{s}^{n}\rangle\mathrm{d}s\text{ converges to }\int_{0}^{T}\langle(\nabla g)(s,\Theta_{s}),D_{u}\Theta_{s}\rangle\mathrm{d}s

be any bounded

\displaystyle\leq C\essup|\zeta|\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|D_{u}Y_{s}^{n}|^{2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big(4+|Z_{s}|^{2\alpha}+|Z_{s}^{n}|^{2\alpha}\big)\mathrm{d}s\Big)\Big].

Using Lemma

4.2.
Solvability of the BSDE (
2.1
) with bounded Malliavin differentiable terminal value

In this subsection, we provide a necessary condition under which the unique solution
(Y,Z)
to the BSDE (
2.1
) belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We assume the following:

(M3)

For each
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
it holds that
(g(t,y,z))_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathbb{L}_{1,2p}(\mathbb{R})
for all
p\geq 1
. Its Malliavin derivative denoted by
(D_{u}g(t,y,z))_{u,t\in[0,T]}
satisfies

\displaystyle|D_{u}g(t,y,z)|\leq K_{u}(t)(1+|y|+f(|y|)|z|^{\alpha})\text{ a.s.}

for any
(u,t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

\alpha\in(0,1)
and the process
(K_{u}(t))_{u,t\in[0,T]}
is uniformly bounded.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.2
and (M3) be in force. Assume further that
\xi
has a bounded Malliavin derivative i.e.
\|D\xi\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|D_{t}\xi\|<\infty\,\,\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}
. Then, the BSDE (
2.1
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

We will start by proving the following lemma

Lemma 4.7
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.6
, we have

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\essup_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Z^{n}_{t}(\omega)|<+\infty

where
(Y^{n},Z^{n})
stands for the unique solution to the BSDE (
4.4
).

Proof.

Let us first assume that the drift
g
is continuously differentiable. Then from Lemma
4.5
, the solution
\Theta^{n}=(Y^{n},Z^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to the BSDE (
4.4
) belongs to
\mathbb{L}_{1,2}\times(\mathbb{L}_{1,2})^{d}
and a version of
\{(D_{u}Y^{n}_{t},D_{u}Z_{t}^{n}),0\leq u,t\leq T\}
satisfies for all
t\in[u,T]

\displaystyle D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}=

\displaystyle D_{u}\xi-\int_{t}^{T}D_{u}Z_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}B_{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\left((D_{u}g_{n})(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})+\langle(\nabla g_{n})(s,\Theta_{s}^{n}),D_{u}\Theta_{s}^{n}\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}s.

Since,
(D_{t}Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a continuous modification of
(Z_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, it is sufficient to establish a uniform bound of the process
(D_{t}Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
to obtain the desired result.
Using once more the assumptions of the Lemma, the properties of the functions
\rho_{n},\tilde{\rho}_{n}
and
f
we have

\displaystyle|D_{u}g_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq K_{u}(t)(1+|Y^{n}_{t}|+f(|Y^{n}_{t}|)|Z^{n}_{t}|^{\alpha})\text{ a.s.}

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}g_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{y}(1+|Z^{n}_{t}|^{\alpha})\text{ a.s.}

\displaystyle|\nabla_{z}g_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{z}(1+f(|Y^{n}_{t}|)|Z^{n}_{t}|)\text{ a.s.}.

This implies that:
\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\nabla_{z}g_{n}*B\|_{BMO}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\nabla_{z}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s})\mathrm{d}B_{s}\|_{BMO}<\infty
and the measure
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with density
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{n}:=\mathcal{E}(\nabla_{z}g_{n}*B)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}
defines an equivalent measure to the probability
\mathbb{P}
. By the Girsanov’s theorem the process
(B_{t}^{n})_{t\in[0,T]}
defined by
B_{t}^{n}=B_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{z}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})\mathrm{d}s
is a Brownian motion under the new probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
Thus (
4.5
) can be written is terms of
B^{n}
as follows

where
F^{n}(s,D_{u}Y_{s}^{n},D_{u}Z_{s}^{n}):=(D_{u}g_{n})(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})+(\nabla_{y}g_{n})(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})D_{u}\Theta_{s}^{n}
. By using once more a standard linearisation technique and applying Itô’s formula to the continuous semimartingale
(e_{t}^{n}D_{u}Y_{t}^{n})_{0\leq t\leq T}

e_{t}^{n}D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}=e_{T}^{n}D_{u}\xi-\int_{t}^{T}e_{s}^{n}D_{u}Z_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{n}+\int_{t}^{T}e_{s}^{n}D_{u}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})\mathrm{d}s,

where

belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
for all
p>1
. Multiplying both sides of the above equation by
(e_{t}^{n})^{-1}
and taking the conditional expectation, we have

\displaystyle|D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}|\leq\mathbb{E}^{n}\Big[(e_{t}^{n})^{-1}e_{T}^{n}|D_{u}\xi|+\int_{t}^{T}(e_{t}^{n})^{-1}e_{s}^{n}|D_{u}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})|\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big],

where
\mathbb{E}^{n}
stands for the expectation under
\mathbb{P}^{n}

C
only depending on
\alpha
and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|Z^{n}*B\|_{BMO}
such that

\displaystyle|D_{u}Y_{t}^{n}|\leq C\mathbb{E}^{n}\Big[|D_{u}\xi|^{2}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|D_{u}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})|\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big)^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

From the assumptions of the Lemma, the first term on the right hand side in the above inequality is uniformly bounded. Let us focus on the second term. Applying successively, Girsanov’s theorem, assumption (M3) and Hölder’s inequality we obtain:

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}^{n}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|D_{u}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})|\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\nabla_{z}g_{n}*B)\int_{t}^{T}|D_{u}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})|\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\nabla_{z}g_{n}*B)\Big)^{r}\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big]^{\frac{2}{r}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|D_{u}g_{n}(s,\Theta_{s}^{n})|\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{r^{\prime}}\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big]^{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}(1+|Y_{s}^{n}|+f(|Y_{s}^{n}|)|Z_{s}^{n}|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{r^{\prime}}\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big]^{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{r^{\prime}}\Big|\mathfrak{F}_{t}\Big]^{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}}\leq CT+Cr^{\prime}!\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Z^{n}*B\|_{BMO}^{2}<\infty,

where the last inequality follows from energy inequalities and it is finite thanks to Lemma
4.3
. Thus,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|Z^{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|(D_{t}Y^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}\leq C.

Since, the constant
C
does not depend on the supremum norms of
\nabla_{y}g
and
\nabla_{z}g
respectively, then the uniform bound obtained above remains valid when
g
is not continuously differentiable with respect to its spatial variables by means of standard approximation methods. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.6
:

From Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.7
, we can fix
n^{*}\in\mathbb{N}

4.3.
Classical differentiability

Throughout this section, we consider the following parametrised BSDE

Y_{t}^{x}=\xi(x)+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,\omega,x,Y_{s}^{x},Z_{s}^{x})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{x}\mathrm{d}B_{s},\quad t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(4.8)

We suppose the following assumption:

Assumption 4.8
.

(C1)

Let
m,d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an adapted measurable function, differentiable in the spatial variables with continuous partial derivatives in
y
and
z
. There exist a positive process
(K_{t}(x))_{t\in[0,T]}
depending on
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a locally bounded and non-decreasing function

In the sequel,

Theorem 6.1

(Path regularity)
.

Suppose Assumption
5.5

Proof.

We first assume that the functions
g
and
\phi
are continuously differentiable in

and

For
t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]

\displaystyle Z_{t}-Z_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle=\nabla Y_{t}(\nabla_{x}X_{t})^{-1}-\nabla Y_{t_{i}}(\nabla_{x}X_{t_{i}})^{-1}

Let us first focus on the term

. We first assume that

is a bounded and compactly supported smooth function. Indeed, if

is not differentiable, then, by denseness of the set of compactly supported and differentiable functions in the set of bounded functions, there exists a sequence

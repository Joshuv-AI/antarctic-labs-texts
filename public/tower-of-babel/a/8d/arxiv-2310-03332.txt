TIT/HEP-696 October 2023 Simple-Sum Giant Graviton Expansions for Orbifolds and Orientifolds
Shota Fujiwara, Yosuke Imamura, Tatsuya Mori, Shuichi Murayama, Daisuke Yokoyama

Abstract
We study giant graviton expansions of the superconformal index of 4d orbifold/orientifold theories. In general, a giant graviton expansion is given as a multiple sum over wrapping numbers. It has been known that the expansion can be reduced to a simple sum for the 𝒩 = 4 {\cal N}=4 U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) SYM by choosing appropriate expansion variables. We find such a reduction occurs for a few examples of orbifold and orientifold theories: ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} orbifold and orientifolds with O ​ 3 O3 and O ​ 7 O7 . We also argue that for a quiver gauge theory associated with a toric Calabi-Yau 3 3 -fold the simple-sum expansion works only if the toric diagram is a triangle, that is, the Calabi-Yau is an orbifold of ℂ 3 \mathbb{C}^{3} .

A purpose of this paper is to discuss generalization of the simple-sum expansions to orbifold and orientifold theories.
We will not give comprehensive analysis.
We demonstrate in a few examples that the decoupling occurs and the triple-sum expansion
reduces to the simple-sum expansion.
We use the decoupling criterion above as a main tool to check the decoupling
and we numerically test that the simple-sum expansion actually gives the correct index.

An interesting point of the simple-sum
GG expansion is that not only
the LHS in (

but also the RHS is given in terms of the superconformal index of
four-dimensional theories
labeled by the rank

We can thus consider the large

In fact, the theory also has the holographic dual,
and we can apply the giant graviton expansion to the theory again.
We will show in some examples
that the expansion
of the “dual” theory gives the original theory.
Namely, the relation is mutual
and invertible.
For
{\cal N}=4

U(N)
SYM, this relation
is “self-dual”, but in general two theories may be different.
In the following sections
we demonstrate how we can obtain
the “dual” theory from the original one.

It is natural to ask if the simple-sum expansion works for more general

Projection

We consider the boundary theory obtained

\displaystyle U_{k}=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{k}(R_{x}-R_{y})\right).

The field contents are obtained
from that of the
{\cal N}=4

invariant degrees of freedom

The insertion of

in the trace of the superconformal index

\displaystyle(q,p,x,y,z)\rightarrow(q,p;\omega_{k}x,\omega_{k}^{-1}y,z),\quad\omega_{k}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{k}}.

In addition, the action on the Chan-Paton factor is realized by the following

\mathbb{Z}_{k}

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}\rightarrow\omega_{k}^{h_{a}}\zeta_{a}\quad(a=1,\ldots,N)

(32)

h_{a}
are holonomy variables and each component takes value in
\mathbb{Z}/(k\mathbb{Z})=\{1,\ldots,k-1,k\}
,
and without loosing generality
we can assume
h_{1}\leq h_{2}\leq\cdots\leq h_{N}

(by using the Weyl group).
In other words, holonomy variables
are given by

\displaystyle\{h_{a}\}=\{1^{N_{1}},2^{N_{2}},\ldots,k^{N_{k}}\},

shown in Figure

,
which we denote by
T_{N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{k}}
.

become global baryonic symmetries.
We will put off the discussion of the baryonic charges,
and we here focus on the sector with vanishing baryonic charges.
In the index calculation
this is equivalent to carrying out the gauge fugacity integral
not for
G_{\rm IR}
but for
G_{\rm UV}
including

.

In the following we first consider the case with

(and so

The superconformal index of the non-baryonic sector of

(34)

where the superscript ‘0’ indicates this is the index for the non-baryonic sector
and

denotes the projection associated with the

action
(

) and
(

) on the fugacities:

Note that

k
IR-free

in (

The large

tensor multiplets

2.1.2
Decoupling

As in the
S^{5}
case, the index of the theory on a system of giant gravitons
with wrapping numbers
m_{x}
,
m_{y}
, and
m_{z}
is given by (
8
)
with different
i_{I}[m_{I}]
(and different terms represented by dots).
In the orbifold case
i_{I}[m_{I}]
are given by

\displaystyle i_{I}[m_{I}]=P_{k}(\sigma_{I}f_{\rm vec}\chi^{U(m_{I})}_{\text{adj}}),

(37)

where
P_{k}
is defined by (
35
).
Let us focus on the constant term in the adjoint characters.
For each cycle we obtain

\displaystyle P_{k}[\sigma_{x}f_{\rm vec}]=1-\frac{(1-x^{-1}y^{k-1})(1-q)(1-p)}{(1-y^{k})(1-z)},

\displaystyle P_{k}[\sigma_{y}f_{\rm vec}]=1-\frac{(1-y^{-1}x^{k-1})(1-q)(1-p)}{(1-x^{k})(1-z)},

\displaystyle P_{k}[\sigma_{z}f_{\rm vec}]=1-\frac{(1-z^{-1})(1-x^{k}y^{k})(1-q)(1-p)}{(1-x^{k})(1-y^{k})(1-xy)}.

(38)

Let us first consider the
Z
-expansion defined with the degrees
\deg(q,p,x,y,z)=(1,1,1,1,0)
.
We can easily see that none of three functions in
(
38
) contains negative-degree terms for
k\geq 2
.
This means all three cycles give non-trivial contributions,
and the triple-sum expansion does not reduce to the simple-sum expansion.

Next, let us consider
X
-expansion defined with the degrees
\deg(q,p,x,y,z)=(1,1,0,1,1)
.
In this case, the functions
P_{k}[\sigma_{y}f_{\rm vec}]
and
P_{k}[\sigma_{z}f_{\rm vec}]
contain infinitely many negative-degree terms.
Then, the contributions with
m_{y}+m_{z}\geq 1
become trivial, and we obtain the simple-sum expansion.

As we will discuss in detail in the next subsection,
the gauge group
U(m)
on
m
coincident giant gravitons
is broken down to
\widetilde{G}_{\rm UV}=U(m_{1})\times\cdots\times U(m_{k})
due to
non-trivial holonomies on the giant gravitons.
We denote the theory by
\widetilde{T}_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}}
.
The baryonic charges in
T_{\overline{N},\ldots,\overline{N}}
are related to the ranks
m_{k}
of the unbroken gauge groups,
and vanishing baryonic charges correspond to the gauge groups with equal ranks:
m_{1}=m_{2}=\cdots=m_{k}=:\overline{m}
.
We denote such a theory by
\widetilde{T}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}
.
This means that only the terms with wrapping number
m=k\overline{m}
contribute to the index of non-baryonic sector.
Therefore, the simple-sum expansion takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{I^{0}_{T_{\overline{N},\ldots,\overline{N}}}}{I_{T_{\infty}}}=\sum_{\overline{m}=0}^{\infty}x^{k\overline{m}\overline{N}}\sigma_{x}I^{0}_{{\widetilde{T}}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}},

(39)

where the index appearing on the RHS is that for non-baryonic sector
because the baryonic charges in
\widetilde{T}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}
are related to
the ranks
N_{i}
, and we consider the special case with all
N_{i}
being the same.

2.2.2

Let us consider the triple-sum expansion of

The functions

appearing in the expansion take the form
(
8
) with

\displaystyle i_{I}[m_{I}]=\widetilde{P}_{k}(\sigma_{I}f_{\rm vec}\chi_{\rm adj}^{U(m_{I})}),

(47)

which are similar to
(

) but
P_{k}
is replaced by
\widetilde{P}_{k}
.
Let us focus on the constant terms in the adjoint characters
\chi_{\rm adj}^{U(m_{I})}
.
For each cycle we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{k}[\sigma_{x}f_{\rm vec}]

\displaystyle=1-\frac{(1+x^{-1}q)(1-p)}{(1-y)(1-z)},

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{k}[\sigma_{y}f_{\rm vec}]

\displaystyle=1-\frac{(1-y^{-1})(1-qx^{k-1})(1-p)}{(1-x^{k})(1-z)},

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{k}[\sigma_{z}f_{\rm vec}]

\displaystyle=1-\frac{(1-z^{-1})(1-qx^{k-1})(1-p)}{(1-x^{k})(1-y)}.

appears

is an involution and applying

gives the

\mathbb{Z}_{k}

.

2.3
Baryonic charges

In the analysis in the previous subsections
we focused on the non-baryonic sector,
and obtained (
39
) and (
49
).
Let us generalize the relations by
including states with non-vanishing baryonic charges.

We can introduce baryonic charges on the both sides of the relation.
We use
B_{i}
and
\widetilde{B}_{i}
to denote the charges in
T_{\overline{N},\ldots,\overline{N}}
and those in
\widetilde{T}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}
,
respectively.
As we will explain shortly, baryonic charges on one side are related to the ranks of the
unbroken gauge symmetries on the other side.
Namely,
B_{i}
are identified with
m_{i}
and
\widetilde{B}_{i}
are identified with
N_{i}

up to certain equivalence relations.
In general, if
SU
factors had different ranks, the spectrum of
gauge invariant baryonic operators would be complicated.
Therefore, in the following we turn on only one of
B_{i}
and
\widetilde{B}_{i}

for simplicity.

Let us first discuss the baryonic charges
B_{i}
in
T_{\overline{N},\ldots,\overline{N}}
.
The index of the baryonic sector with charges
B_{i}
can be obtained by the insertion
of the background charges

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\det g_{i}}{\det g_{i+1}}\right)^{B_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\zeta_{i,1}\cdots\zeta_{i,\overline{N}}}{\zeta_{i+1,1}\cdots\zeta_{i+1,\overline{N}}}\right)^{B_{i}}

(50)

in (
34
).
These are defined so that the baryonic operators
\det X_{i+1,i}
and
\det Y_{i,i+1}

carry
B_{i}=+1
and
-1
, respectively.
Because (
50
) is invariant under the shift
B_{i}\rightarrow B_{i}+c

with
i
-independent constant
c
,
the
k
charges
B_{i}
are redundant,
and the baryonic sectors are labeled by the equivalence classes
[B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}]

defined with the equivalence relation

\displaystyle B_{i}\sim B_{i}+c.

(51)

This means that only the differences
B_{i}-B_{j}
are physical quantities.

To extend the GG expansion (
39
),
we should also modify
I^{0}_{\widetilde{T}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}}
on the right hand side.
The ranks
m_{i}
appearing in
\widetilde{T}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}}
are related to the baryonic charges
B_{i}
in
T_{\overline{N},\ldots,\overline{N}}
.
This is because giant gravitons correspond to baryonic operators and baryonic charges correspond
to the numbers of giant gravitons with different holonomy variables on them.
In the single wrapping sector with
m=1
there are
k
contributions with only one of
m_{i}
being
1
.
They correspond to
k
baryonic operators
\det X_{i+1,i}
(
i=1,\ldots,k
).
With this relation we identify
m_{i}
with the baryonic charges
B_{i}

up to the equivalence relation (
51
).

The giant graviton expansion for the baryonic sector is given by

\displaystyle\frac{I_{T_{\overline{N},\ldots,\overline{N}}}^{[B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}]}}{I_{T_{\infty}}}=\sum_{(m)\in[B]}x^{(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k})\overline{N}}\sigma_{x}I_{\widetilde{T}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}}}^{0},

(52)

where
\sum_{(m)\in[B]}
is the summation over
non-negative integers
m_{i}
belonging to the equivalence class
[B_{i}]
.
The large
N
limit appearing in the denominator on the left hand side
in (
52
)
is the same as before.
The inverse expansion of (
52
) is given by

\displaystyle\frac{I^{0}_{\widetilde{T}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}}}}{I_{\widetilde{T}_{\infty}}}=\sum_{\overline{N}=0}^{\infty}x^{\overline{N}(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k})}\sigma_{x}I^{[m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}]}_{T_{\overline{N},\ldots,\overline{N}}}.

(53)

We can also consider the baryonic charges
\widetilde{B}_{i}
in
\widetilde{T}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}
.
Then the corresponding ranks
N_{i}
change.
For arbitrary ranks
m_{i}
the spectrum
of gauge invariant baryonic operators is complicated.
So, we consider the simple case with equal ranks
m_{1}=m_{2}=\cdots=m_{k}=:\overline{m}
.
On the
T_{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}}
side this corresponds to restricting the states
to the non-baryonic sector.
The GG expansion (
49
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{I^{[\widetilde{B}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{B}_{k}]}_{\widetilde{T}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}}}{I_{\widetilde{T}_{\infty}}}=\sum_{(N)\in[\widetilde{B}]}x^{\overline{m}(N_{1}+\cdots+N_{k})}\sigma_{x}I^{0}_{T_{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}}},

(54)

and its inverse expansion is

\displaystyle\frac{I_{T_{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}}}^{0}}{I_{T_{\infty}}}=\sum_{\overline{m}=0}^{\infty}x^{\overline{m}(N_{1}+\cdots+N_{k})}\sigma_{x}I_{\widetilde{T}_{\overline{m},\ldots,\overline{m}}}^{[N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}]}.

(55)

6
Discussion

In this paper we discussed the reduction of the multiple-sum giant graviton expansions
to the simple-sum expansions.
For orbifold and orientifold examples the triple-sum expansion for generic degrees
can be reduced to the simple-sum expansion by assigning appropriate degrees.

All theories studied in this paper are Lagrangian theories,
and we can calculate the superconformal index directly by using the localization formula.
This is in general not the case.
For example,
M2-brane theory (ABJM theory
[
58
]
) and M5-brane theory (six-dimentional
(2,0)
theory)
are related by the simple-sum giant graviton expansions
[
39
]
.
In this case, the calculation of the index of
(2,0)
theory
is only possible with indirect methods
[
59
,
60
,
61
,
62
,
63
]
.
We can use the giant graviton expansion as another convenient method to calculate
the index of
(2,0)
theory by using the ABJM index
[
64
]
.
The orbifold version of the relation between M2-theory and M5-theory is also interesting because
it may enable us to calculate
the index of six-dimensional
(1,0)
theories
by using orbifolds of ABJM theory.

Another interesting class of non-Lagrangian theories
includes Argyres Douglas and Minahan Nemeschanski theories
realized on D3-branes probing
7
-brane backgrounds with constant axiodilation.
They are labeled by the type of the
7
-brane
G=H_{0},H_{1},H_{2},D_{4},E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
and the rank
N
.
The AdS/CFT correspondence of these theories in the large
N
limit was studied in
[
45
,
46
]
,
and it was confirmed in
[
22
]

that the multiple-sum expansion works well at least for the leading giant graviton contributions.
If we can apply the simple-sum expansion
to this class of theories it gives interesting relations
among the indices.
Actually, the
D_{4}[N]
theory studied in Section
4

is a special case of the general theories
G[N]
.
We confirmed that the
Z
-expansion of the
D_{4}[N]
theory works and is self-dual.
If this is also the case for general
G[N]
, the following relation should hold

\displaystyle\frac{I_{G[N]}}{I_{G[\infty]}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{\Delta_{G}mN}\sigma_{z}I_{G[m]},

(173)

with
\Delta_{G}
being the dimension of the Coulomb branch operator of
the
G[1]
theory.
It would be interesting to confirm whether this relation holds,
and if so, to what extent we can bootstrap the index
I_{G[N]}

with the relation (
173
).

In the orbifold and orientifold cases, we can consider three expansions:

X
,
Y
, and
Z
-expansions.
Although not always the decoupling occurs, for some examples
we can perform the expansion in more than one ways,
and we can consider “the web of GG expansions”,
by applying GG expansions repeatedly.
In the case of
{\cal N}=4

U(N)
SYM
all the three expansions work.
In this case the giant graviton expansion is “self-dual” in the sense that
the theory on the giant graviton is also
{\cal N}=4

U(N)
SYM,
and application of the giant graviton expansions gives just different frames.
Each step of giant graviton expansions is specified by one of the variable changes

\sigma_{x}
,
\sigma_{y}
, and
\sigma_{z}
,
and a frame is specified by the composition of the variable changes.
Combining three variable changes
\sigma_{x}
,
\sigma_{y}
,
\sigma_{z}
and
permutations in
(x,y,z)
and
(q,p)

associated with the Weyl groups of
SU(4)_{R}
and
SO(4)_{\text{spin}}
symmetries
we can generate many frames.
All these frames gives
{\cal N}=4

U(N)
SYM.
However, in more general orbifolds and orientifolds,
it may be possible to generate the web including different theories.
By applying a projection
P
, we can obtain an orbifold theory,
and in a frame specified by the variable change
\sigma
the projection is replaced by

\sigma P\sigma^{-1}
, and in general this is different from the original one.
Therefore, the web consists of theories with different orbifold actions.
Although in the orbifold case all theories in the web are Lagrangian theories,
in more general cases (like
G[N]
and orbifolds of M-brane theories) the web may contain
both Lagrangian and non-Lagrangian theories, and then it will be convenient to analyze
theories that are difficult to analyze directly.
This is analogous to the S-duality of type IIB string theory.
The duality takes the type IIB theory to the same theory (with the different coupling constant).
Let us take
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
projection with the worldsheet parity
\Omega
, which gives type I theory.
Then the other side of the duality is also
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
projected theory.
However, this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is not
\Omega
but
(-1)^{F_{R}}
, which gives the
SO(32)
heterotic string.
In this way, by applying a projection, we can generate new duality from the duality for the
theories before the projection.
We can consider a similar situation for the web of giant graviton expansions.

As we have emphasized, the simple-sum expansion is much more easy to calculate
because it does not have issues of integration contours.
This is because the contribution from single cycle is obtained
by a simple variable change from the standard index, for which we can adopt
the standard choice of the contours.
In Section
5
we saw that the giant graviton expansions of the orbifold theory
T_{N,N}
and the Klebanov-Witten theory

{\rm KW}[N]
are related by the RG flow.
In this relation, the simple sum expansion of
T_{N,N}
generates
the double-sum expansion of
{\rm KW}[N]
.
This gives information about rules for pole selection in
{\rm KW}[N]
.
This kind of relation may be useful to find general rules for the pole selection
for general multiple-sum giant graviton expansions.

In all the examples in this work the simple-sum expansions are invertible.
Namely, if expansion of the index of
T_{N}
gives the index of
\widetilde{T}_{m}
,
then the expansion of
\widetilde{T}_{m}
gives
T_{N}
.
It would be interesting if we could analytically prove the invertibility.
Suppose that we have functions
F_{N}
(
N\in\mathbb{Z}
) which vanishes for negative
N
,
and they have giant graviton expansion of the form

\displaystyle F_{N}=F_{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{mN}\sigma_{x}\widetilde{F}_{m}.

(174)

The invertibility means that a similar expansion of
\widetilde{F}_{m}
gives the
original functions
F_{N}
.
Namely, if the functions
F^{\prime}_{N}
appearing in the expansion of
\widetilde{F}_{m}

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{m}=\widetilde{F}_{\infty}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{mN}\sigma_{x}F_{N}^{\prime}

(175)

are the same as
F_{N}
, then we can say the expansion is invertible.
Unfortunately, we have not yet succeeded in proving (or disproving) this fact.
We will only comment on what happen if we naively substitute (
175
) to (
174
).
It gives

\displaystyle F_{N}=(F_{\infty}\sigma_{x}\widetilde{F}_{\infty})\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{m(N-N^{\prime})}F_{N^{\prime}}^{\prime}.

(176)

Let us suppose
x
is a generic phase factor
x=e^{2\pi i\alpha}
with
irrational
\alpha
.
Then with an appropriate regularization we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{m(N-N^{\prime})}={\cal N}\delta_{N,N^{\prime}},

(177)

where
{\cal N}
is a large number depending on the regularization.
Then we obtain

\displaystyle F_{N}={\cal N}(F_{\infty}\sigma_{x}\widetilde{F}_{\infty})F_{N}^{\prime}.

(178)

If the product of
F_{\infty}
and
\sigma_{x}\widetilde{F}_{\infty}
gave

{\cal N}^{-1}
we would obtain expected result
F_{N}=F_{N}^{\prime}
.
Because the large
N
index
F_{\infty}
and
\widetilde{F}_{\infty}

are given as the plethystic exponential of the letter indices

f_{\infty}
and
\widetilde{f}_{\infty}
of
the massless fields in AdS,
this can be formally calculated as follows.

\displaystyle F_{\infty}\sigma_{x}\widetilde{F}_{\infty}=\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(f_{\infty}+\sigma_{x}\widetilde{f}_{\infty}).

(179)

Interestingly, in all examples of pairs of theories
related by the
X
-expansion, we find the relation

\displaystyle f_{\infty}+\sigma_{x}\widetilde{f}_{\infty}=-1

(180)

holds.
Therefore, naively, (
179
) becomes
\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(-1)=0
.
This is nice because we want to obtain
{\cal N}^{-1}
as the result of the regularization.
Of course the above manipulation is so naive and formal that
it does not make sense as it is.
It would be nice if we can improve the derivation.

Appendix A
Characters and Haar measures

In general, the Haar measure is given by the plethystic exponential of the
negative of the adjoint character with constant terms removed.

For
G=U(N)
the adjoint character is given by

\displaystyle\chi^{U(N)}_{\rm adj}(\zeta_{a})=\chi^{U(N)}_{\rm fund}\chi^{U(N)}_{\overline{\rm fund}}=\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}\frac{\zeta_{a}}{\zeta_{b}},

(181)

where
\chi^{U(N)}_{\rm fund}
and
\chi^{U(N)}_{\overline{\rm fund}}

are the characters of the fundamental and the anti-fundamental representations, respectively:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm fund}^{U(N)}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\zeta_{a},\quad\chi_{\overline{\rm fund}}^{U(N)}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}}.

(182)

In the sum in (
181
) each term with
a=b
gives
1
, and in total the constant term in
\chi^{U(N)}_{\rm adj}
is
N
.
The Haar measure is given by

\displaystyle\int_{U(N)}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int\prod_{a=1}^{N}\frac{d\zeta_{a}}{2\pi i\zeta_{a}}\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(-\chi_{\rm adj}^{U(N)}(\zeta_{a})+N)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int\prod_{a=1}^{N}\frac{d\zeta_{a}}{2\pi i\zeta_{a}}\prod_{a\neq b}\left(1-\frac{\zeta_{a}}{\zeta_{b}}\right).

(183)

The overall factor
1/N!
can be fixed by the normalization condition
\int_{G}d\mu=1
.

The characters for the symplectic and orthogonal groups are obtained from
U(N)
characters
by appropriate restrictions and projections.
To obtain
Sp(N)
and
SO(2N)
characters we start from
U(2N)
, and impose
the following constraints on the
2N
fugacities
\zeta_{a}
(
a=1,\ldots,2N
):

\displaystyle\zeta_{a+N}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}}\quad(a=1,\ldots,N).

(184)

Then the fundamental and the anti-fundamental characters in (
182
) (with
N
replaced by
2N
)
become the same.
The adjoint characters for
Sp(N)
and
SO(2N)
are given by
taking the symmetric and anti-symmetric products of two copies of the fundamental character:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm adj}^{Sp(N)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq a\leq b\leq 2N}\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}=\sum_{1\leq a\leq b\leq N}\left(\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}\frac{\zeta_{a}}{\zeta_{b}},

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm adj}^{SO(2N)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq 2N}\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq N}\left(\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}\frac{\zeta_{a}}{\zeta_{b}}.

(185)

Both these characters include constant term
N
, and the Haar measures are given by

\displaystyle\int_{Sp(N)}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!2^{N}}\int\prod_{a=1}^{N}\frac{d\zeta_{a}}{2\pi i\zeta_{a}}\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(-\chi_{\rm adj}^{Sp(N)}(\zeta_{a})+N),

\displaystyle\int_{SO(2N)}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!2^{N-1}}\int\prod_{a=1}^{N}\frac{d\zeta_{a}}{2\pi i\zeta_{a}}\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(-\chi_{\rm adj}^{SO(2N)}(\zeta_{a})+N).

(186)

The adjoint character and the Haar measure of
SO(2N+1)
are obtained in a similar way
starting from
U(2N+1)
and imposing the constraints

\displaystyle\zeta_{a+N}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}}\quad(a=1,\ldots,N),\quad\zeta_{2N+1}=1.

(187)

The adjoint character is

\displaystyle\chi^{SO(2N+1)}_{\rm adj}=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq 2N+1}\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}=\chi_{\rm adj}^{SO(2N)}+\sum_{a=1}^{N}\left(\zeta_{a}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}}\right),

(188)

and the normalized Haar measure is

\displaystyle\int_{SO(2N+1)}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!2^{N}}\int\prod_{a=1}^{N}\frac{d\zeta_{a}}{2\pi i\zeta_{a}}\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(-\chi_{\rm adj}^{SO(2N+1)}(\zeta_{a})+N).

(189)

The orthogonal group
O(2N)
consists of two disconnected components:
the component
G_{+}=SO(2N)\subset O(2N)

containing the identity and the other component which we denote by
G_{-}\subset O(2N)
.
We normalize the Haar measure for each component by
\int_{G_{\pm}}d\mu=1
,
and the normalized measure for
O(2N)
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{O(2N)}d\mu=\frac{1}{2}\int_{G_{+}}d\mu+\frac{1}{2}\int_{G_{-}}d\mu.

(190)

To obtain the adjoint character and the Haar measure for
G_{-}
we
start from
U(2N)
and impose the constraints

\displaystyle\zeta_{a+N}=\frac{1}{\zeta}_{a}\quad(a=1,\ldots,N-1),\quad\zeta_{N}=1,\quad\zeta_{2N}=-1.

(191)

Remark that the value
\zeta_{2N}=-1
should be set after the calculation of the plethystic exponential.
For example,
\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits\zeta_{2N}=1/(1-\zeta_{2N})=1/2
.
To avoid the confusion with fermionic terms with negative coefficients, we introduce an auxiliary variable
\theta

which is set to be
-1
after the calculation of the plethystic exponential.
The adjoint character is

\displaystyle\chi^{G_{-}}_{\rm adj}=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq 2N}\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}=\chi^{SO(2N-1)}_{\rm adj}+\theta\left[\sum_{a=1}^{N}\left(\zeta_{a}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}}\right)+1\right],

(192)

and the normalized Haar measure is

\displaystyle\int_{G_{-}}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(N-1)!2^{N}}\int\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}\frac{d\zeta_{a}}{2\pi i\zeta_{a}}\left.\left[\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(N-1+\theta-\chi_{\rm adj}^{G_{-}}(\zeta_{a}))\right]\right|_{\theta=-1}.

(193)

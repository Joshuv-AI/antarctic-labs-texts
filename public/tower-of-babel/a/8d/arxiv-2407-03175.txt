Low-Rank Toeplitz Matrix Restoration: Descent Cone Analysis and Structured Random Matrix
Song Li

Abstract
This note demonstrates that we can stably recover all symmetric Toeplitz matrices 𝑿 0 ∈ ℝ n × n \boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n} of rank at most r r from a number of rank-one subgaussian measurements on the order of r ​ log 2 ​ n r\log^{2}n with an exponentially decreasing failure probability by employing a nuclear norm minimization program. Our approach utilizes descent cone analysis through Mendelson’s small ball method with the Toeplitz constraint. The key ingredient is to determine the spectral norm of a random matrix with Toeplitz structure, which may be of independent interest. This improves upon earlier analyses and resolves the conjecture in Chen et al. (IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 61(7):4034–4059, 2015).

Introduction

Toeplitz matrices enjoy an important number of applications, including signal processing, numerical analysis, stochastic processes, time series analysis, image processing, and so on
[
4
]
.
Recovery of a matrix that is simultaneously low-rank and Toeplitz, which arises when a random process is generated by a few spectral spikes,
is crucial for many tasks in wireless communications and array signal processing; see, e.g.,
[

Following some ideas in
[

, we aim to reconstruct an unknown symmetric matrix
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
that is simultaneously low-rank and Toeplitz from a small number of rank-one measurements.
In particular, we explore sampling methods of the form

b_{k}=\langle\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}},\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\rangle+e_{k},\quad k=1,\ldots,m,

where
\boldsymbol{b}:=\left\{b_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
denotes the measurements,
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
represents the sensing vector,
\boldsymbol{e}:=\left\{e_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
stands for the noise term, and
m
is the number of measurements.
The rank-one sampling scheme is utilized in a wide spectrum of practical settings,
such as covariance sketching for data streams, noncoherent energy measurements in communications, and phaseless measurements in optical imaging
[
7
,
2
]
.
Furthermore, it admits near-optimal estimation with tractable algorithms and offers computational and storage advantages over other types of measurements.

Let us formally describe our setup and model, in accordance with
[

We focus exclusively on the random sampling model.
Specifically, we assume that the sensing vectors are independent copies of a random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}

\xi

A natural heuristic method is rank minimization to promote low-rank structures.
However, the rank minimization problem is generally considered to be NP-hard.
An alternative scheme is to seek a nuclear norm minimizer over all matrices compatible with the measurements, as well as the Toeplitz constraint:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{minimize}&\quad\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\lVert_{*}\\
\text{subject to}&\quad\left\lVert\mathcal{A}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\boldsymbol{b}\right\lVert_{\ell_{p}}\leq\eta\\

\end{array}

Our proof consists of three ingredients.
The first ingredient is that the descent cone for the nuclear norm has a clear characterization.
The second is that the small ball function in the small ball method, when confined to Toeplitz matrices, is bounded away from zero for rank-one subgaussian measurements.
Finally, we provide an upper bound for the spectral norm of a random matrix with Toeplitz structure.
Our approach relies on embedding Toeplitz matrices into circulant matrices, allowing for the explicit expression of the latter’s eigenvalues,
which diverges from the moment method
[

2.2
Descent Cone Analysis

In this subsection, we present a framework for applying the descent cone analysis method and a detailed examination of the small ball method as it pertains to Toeplitz matrices.

The descent cone of a norm at a point
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the set of all possible directions
\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

such that the norm does not increase.
For the nuclear norm, this leads to the following definition.

Definition 1
.

For any matrix
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, define its descent cone
\mathcal{D}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right)
by

\mathcal{D}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right):=\left\{\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}_{0}+t\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{*}\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right\lVert_{*}\text{for some}\ t>0\right\}.

(8)

The first crucial ingredient for Theorem
1
is the following proposition that characterizes the geometry property of the descent cone.
The proof is inspired by
[
12

Proposition 1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
be of rank at most
r

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{*}\leq\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)\sqrt{r}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F},\ \text{for all}\ \boldsymbol{Z}\in\overline{\mathcal{D}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right)},

(9)

where
\overline{\mathcal{D}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right)}
denotes the topological closure of
\mathcal{D}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right)
.

Proof.

We denote the SVD of
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}
by
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}=\boldsymbol{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{V}^{*}

[
12

\overline{\mathcal{D}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right)}=\left\{\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:-\langle\boldsymbol{U}\boldsymbol{V}^{*},\boldsymbol{Z}\rangle\geq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}_{T_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}^{\perp}}\right\lVert_{*}\right\}.

(10)

Thus, for any
\boldsymbol{Z}\in\overline{\mathcal{D}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right)}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}_{T_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}}\right\lVert_{*}+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}_{T_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}^{\perp}}\right\lVert_{*}

Thus, we need a lower bound on the small ball function
\mathcal{Q}_{2\alpha}\left(\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F};\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)
and an upper bound on the supremum of the empirical process

\mathcal{R}_{m}\left(\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F};\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)
.

The second ingredient is that when confined to the space of the Toeplitz matrices
\mathcal{T}
(it can be seen that
\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathcal{T}
), the small ball function is bounded away from zero for all
\mu\geq 1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\left(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
be a random vector whose entries are i.i.d. copies of a random variable
\xi
satisfying
\mathbb{E}\xi=0
,
\mathbb{E}\xi^{2}=1
,
\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}=\mu\geq 1

and
\left\lVert\xi\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
.
Then it holds that for
0<\alpha<1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F};\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{Z},\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle\right\lvert\geq\alpha\right)

(15)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\min\left\{\frac{4}{K^{8}},\left(\frac{\mu}{1+K^{4}}\right)^{2},1\right\}.

Proof.

We begin by noting two essential facts derived from Lemma 9 and Lemma 8 in
[
11
]
.
Utilizing these two facts, we will demonstrate that for any
\mu\geq 1
, and in particular when
\mu=1
, the small ball function is bounded away from zero.

Fact 1
: The first is that for any
\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}=\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,i}^{2}+2\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,j}^{2}.

(16)

By a direct calculation,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i,j}\xi_{i}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,j}\xi_{j}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i,\tilde{i},j,\tilde{j}}\xi_{i}\xi_{\tilde{i}}\xi_{j}\xi_{\tilde{j}}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,j}\boldsymbol{Z}_{\tilde{i},\tilde{j}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,i}^{2}+2\sum_{i\neq j}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,j}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq\tilde{i}}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,i}\boldsymbol{Z}_{\tilde{i},\tilde{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,i}^{2}+2\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,j}^{2}.

Fact 2
: The second is that for any
\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{4}\lesssim\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{4}+K^{8}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}^{4}.

(17)

By the Hanson-Wright inequality in Lemma
1
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]\right\lvert^{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}4t^{3}\ \mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]\right\lvert>t\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq 8\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{3}\ \exp\left(-c\frac{t^{2}}{K^{4}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert^{2}_{F}}\right)dt+8\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{3}\ \exp\left(-c\frac{t}{K^{2}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{op}}\right)dt

\displaystyle=8K^{8}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}^{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{3}\ \exp\left(-cx^{2}\right)dx+8K^{8}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{op}^{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{3}\ \exp\left(-cx\right)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{8}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}^{4}.

Then the triangle inequality yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{4}+\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]\right\lvert^{4}

\displaystyle=\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{4}+\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]\right\lvert^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{4}+K^{8}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}^{4}.

We now turn to proving (
15
).
For
\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F}
, let the diagonal element of
\boldsymbol{Z}
be
z_{0}
. Since
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}=1
, we have
0\leq\left\lvert z_{0}\right\lvert\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
.
By (
16
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,i}^{2}+2\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,j}^{2}

(18)

\displaystyle=\left(nz_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(\mu-1\right)nz_{0}^{2}+2\left(1-nz_{0}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[n^{2}+\left(\mu-3\right)n\right]z_{0}^{2}+2.

Then it can be checked that
\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}\geq 1=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}^{2}
. Thus, by the Paley-Zygmund inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}\geq\alpha^{2}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert^{2}_{F}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}\geq\alpha^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\frac{\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}\right)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{4}}.

By (
18
) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle f\left(z_{0}\right):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F}\\
Z_{1,1}=z_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{2}\right)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\lvert^{4}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F}\\
\boldsymbol{Z}_{1,1}=z_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left[\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,i}^{2}+2\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\boldsymbol{Z}_{i,j}^{2}\right]^{2}}{\left(\text{Tr}\,\boldsymbol{Z}\right)^{4}+K^{8}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left[\left(n^{2}+\left(\mu-3\right)n\right)z_{0}^{2}+2\right]^{2}}{\left(nz_{0}\right)^{4}+K^{8}}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{\left(n^{2}+\left(\mu-3\right)n\right)z_{0}^{2}+2}{\left(nz_{0}\right)^{2}+K^{4}}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle:=g\left(\left\lvert z_{0}\right\lvert\right)=\left(h\left(z^{2}_{0}\right)\right)^{2}.

By a direct calculation,

\frac{\partial h\left(z^{2}_{0}\right)}{\partial\left(z^{2}_{0}\right)}=\frac{\left(n^{2}+\left(\mu-3\right)n\right)\cdot K^{4}-2n^{2}}{\left(n^{2}z_{0}^{2}+K^{4}\right)^{2}}.

(19)

Since the numerator in (
19
) is constant and the denominator is positive, we have that
h\left(z^{2}_{0}\right)
is monotonic on the interval
z^{2}_{0}\in\left[0,1/n\right]
. Besides, we have that for all
\mu\geq 1
and
z^{2}_{0}\in\left[0,1/n\right]
, the numerator of
h\left(z^{2}_{0}\right)
satisfies
\left(n^{2}+\left(\mu-3\right)n\right)z_{0}^{2}+2\geq 0
.
Thus,
g
is also monotonic on the interval
\left[0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right]
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F};\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\min f\left(z_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\min\left\{g\left(0\right),g\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\min\left\{\frac{4}{K^{8}},\left(\frac{n+\mu-1}{n+K^{4}}\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\min\left\{\frac{4}{K^{8}},\left(\frac{\mu}{1+K^{4}}\right)^{2},1\right\}.

∎

2.3
Structured Random Matrix

We investigate the spectral norm of the structured random matrix
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)
, which constitutes our third ingredient.
Our proof relies on embedding Toeplitz matrices into circulant matrices, which may inspire characterizations of the spectral norms of other structured random matrices.
We provide the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\left(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
be a random vector whose entries are i.i.d. copies of a random variable
\xi
satisfying
\mathbb{E}\xi=0
,
\mathbb{E}\xi^{2}=1
and
\left\lVert\xi\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
.
Assume that
\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
are i.i.d. copies of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
,
then we have

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)-m\mathbb{E}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\lVert_{op}\leq CK^{2}\left(\sqrt{m}\log n+\log^{3/2}n\right),

(20)

where
C>0
is a numerical absolute constant.

Proof.

We divide the proof into four steps.

Step 1: Expectation Calculation.
It can be seen that

\mathbb{E}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)=\mathcal{T}\left(\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)=\boldsymbol{I}_{n}\in\mathcal{T}.

We then set the random matrix

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}:=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)-m\mathbb{E}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}z_{0}&z_{1}&\cdots&z_{n-1}\\
z_{1}&z_{0}&\cdots&z_{n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z_{n-1}&z_{n-2}&\cdots&z_{0}\\
\end{array}\right),

and let

\boldsymbol{z}:=\left(z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
.
Since each entry on a diagonal of the Toeplitz projection is given by the average of the corresponding diagonal, we have that

\displaystyle{z}_{\ell}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\xi^{2}_{k,i}-1\right),\quad\ell=0;\\
&\frac{1}{n-\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n-\ell}\xi_{k,i}\xi_{k,i+\ell},\quad\ell=1,2,\ldots,n-1.\end{aligned}\right.

Apparently, one has
\mathbb{E}{z}_{\ell}=0
for all
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
.

Step 2: Toeplitz Matrix Embedding.
The harmonic structure of Toeplitz matrices motivates us to embed
\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}
into a new circulant matrix
\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\left(2n-1\right)\times\left(2n-1\right)}
such that

\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|ccc}z_{0}&z_{1}&\cdots&z_{n-1}&z_{n-1}&\cdots&z_{1}\\
z_{1}&z_{0}&\cdots&z_{n-2}&z_{n-1}&\cdots&z_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
z_{n-1}&z_{n-2}&\cdots&z_{0}&z_{1}&\cdots&z_{n-1}\\
\hline\cr z_{n-1}&z_{n-1}&\cdots&z_{1}&z_{0}&\cdots&z_{n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z_{1}&z_{2}&\cdots&z_{n-1}&z_{n-2}&\cdots&z_{0}\\
\end{array}\right).

Note that
\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}
is a submatrix of
\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}}
, we only need to give an upper bound for
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}}\right\lVert_{op}
.

Let
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{2n-1}}
be the
\left(2n-1\right)
-th root of unity.
Then the eigenvalues of
\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}}
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle=z_{0}+z_{1}\omega^{j}+z_{2}\omega^{2j}+\cdots+z_{n-1}\omega^{\left(n-1\right)j}+z_{n-1}\omega^{nj}+\cdots+z_{1}\omega^{\left(2n-2\right)j}

\displaystyle=z_{0}+z_{1}\left(\omega^{j}+\omega^{\left(2n-2\right)j}\right)+\cdots+z_{n-1}\left(\omega^{\left(n-1\right)j}+\omega^{nj}\right)

\displaystyle=z_{0}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}z_{\ell}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi j\ell}{2n-1}\right),\quad\text{for all}\ j=0,1,\ldots,2n-2.

It can be seen that
\mathbb{E}\lambda_{j}=0
and

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}\right\lVert_{op}\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}}\right\lVert_{op}=\max_{0\leq j\leq 2n-2}\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\lvert.

Step 3: Eigenvalue Estimation.

Note that
\lambda_{j}
is a centered quadratic form of
\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{m}\right\}
.
Let the symmetric coefficient matrix
\boldsymbol{H}^{j}
be

\boldsymbol{H}_{\alpha,\beta}^{j}:=\frac{1}{n-\left\lvert\ell\right\lvert}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi j\left\lvert\ell\right\lvert}{2n-1}\right),\quad 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n\ \text{and}\ \alpha-\beta=\ell.

(21)

Let

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{\xi}_{m}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\boldsymbol{H}^{j}&&&\\
&\boldsymbol{H}^{j}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\boldsymbol{H}^{j}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{m}\\
\end{array}\right).

Thus,
\lambda_{j}
can be written as

\lambda_{j}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}-\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right].

Then, by the Hanson–Wright inequality in Lemma
1
, we can get

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\lvert\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{K^{4}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert^{2}_{F}},\frac{t}{K^{2}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert_{op}}\right\}\right).

It can be seen that
\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert_{op}=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{H}^{j}\right\lVert_{op}
and
\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert_{F}=\sqrt{m}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{H}^{j}\right\lVert_{F}
.
It therefore suffices to estimate
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{H}^{j}\right\lVert_{F}
, since
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{H}^{j}\right\lVert_{op}\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{H}^{j}\right\lVert_{F}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{H}^{j}
is a symmetric Toeplitz matrix, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{H}^{j}\right\lVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}\left\lvert\boldsymbol{H}_{\alpha,\beta}^{j}\right\lvert^{2}\leq\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n;\alpha-\beta=\ell}\frac{1}{\left(n-\left\lvert\ell\right\lvert\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{n-\left\lvert\ell\right\lvert}+\frac{1}{n}\leq 2\log n+2.

Therefore,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert_{op}&\leq\sqrt{2\log n+2},\\
\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert_{F}&\leq\sqrt{m\left(2\log n+2\right)}.\end{array}\right.

(22)

Step 4: Uniform Argument.
We define the event
\Omega_{j,t}:=\left\{\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\lvert<t\right\}

and define
\Omega_{t}=\bigcap\limits_{0\leq j\leq 2n-2}\Omega_{j,t}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq j\leq 2n-2}\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\lvert\geq t\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{t}^{c}\right)\leq\sum_{0\leq j\leq 2n-2}\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{j,t}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq j\leq 2n-2}2\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{K^{4}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert^{2}_{F}},\frac{t}{K^{2}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{j}\right\lVert_{op}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4n\cdot\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{K^{4}m\left(2\log n+2\right)},\frac{t}{K^{2}\sqrt{2\log n+2}}\right\}\right).

Finally, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}\right\lVert_{op}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{0}}\right\lVert_{op}=\mathbb{E}\max_{0\leq j\leq 2n-2}\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\lvert

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq j\leq 2n-2}\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\lvert\geq t\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int^{C_{1}K^{2}\sqrt{m}\log n}_{0}1dt\ +\int_{C_{1}K^{2}\sqrt{m}\log n}^{\infty}4n\cdot\exp\left(-c\frac{t^{2}}{2K^{4}m\log n}\right)dt

\displaystyle+\int^{C_{2}K^{2}\log^{3/2}n}_{0}1dt+\int_{C_{2}K^{2}\log^{3/2}n}^{\infty}4n\cdot\exp\left(-c\frac{t}{K^{2}\sqrt{2\log n}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}K^{2}\sqrt{m}\log n+\widetilde{C}_{2}K^{2}\log^{3/2}n.

∎

2.4
Proof of Theorem
1

We now put all things together.
By Theorem
2
, the small ball function satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F};\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\geq\mathcal{Q}_{2\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F};\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(1-4\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\min\left\{\frac{4}{K^{8}},\left(\frac{\mu}{1+K^{4}}\right)^{2},1\right\}.

Moreover, for the empirical process term, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\lVert_{op}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\left[\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right]\right\lVert_{op}+\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\lVert_{op}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left[\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right]\right\lVert_{op}+\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\boldsymbol{I}_{n}\right\lVert_{op}

\displaystyle\leq 2CK^{2}\left(\sqrt{m}\log n+\log^{3/2}n\right)+\widetilde{C}\sqrt{m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(K^{2}+1\right)\left(\sqrt{m}\log n+\log^{3/2}n\right).

In the second inequality, we have used the symmetrization principle
[
18
, Lemma 6.4.2]
and
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)=\boldsymbol{I}_{n}
.
In the third inequality, we have used Theorem
3
and the estimate
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\boldsymbol{I}_{n}\right\lVert_{op}=\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\right\lvert\lesssim\sqrt{m}
.
Thus, we can get

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\lVert_{op}\lesssim\left(K^{2}+1\right)\left(\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\log^{3/2}n}{m}\right).

(24)

Now, by Proposition
1
, the supremum of the empirical process satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}\left(\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F};\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F}}\left\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\left\langle\boldsymbol{Z},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle\right\lvert

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F}}\left\lvert\left\langle\boldsymbol{Z},\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\rangle\right\lvert

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)\sqrt{r}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\mathcal{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\lVert_{op}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(K^{2}+1\right)\sqrt{r}\cdot\left(\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\log^{3/2}n}{m}\right).

Finally, we choose
\alpha=1/4
and
t=c_{2}\sqrt{m}
in Lemma
2
.
By (
14
), provided
m\geq Lr\log^{2}n
for a sufficiently large constant
L
, we have

\lambda_{\min}\left(\mathcal{A};\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap\mathbb{S}_{F}\right)\gtrsim\tilde{c}m^{1/p}

with probability exceeding
1-e^{-cm}
.
Here
c_{2},L,c
and
\tilde{c}
are positive constants depending only on
K
and
\mu
.
Thus, by (
13
) we have finished the proof.

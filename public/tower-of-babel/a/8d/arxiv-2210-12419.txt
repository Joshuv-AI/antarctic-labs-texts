The central sheaf of a Grothendieck category
Konstantin Ardakov, Peter Schneider

Abstract
. The center Z ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) Z(\mathcal{A}) of an abelian category 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is the endomorphism ring of the identity functor on that category. A localizing subcategory of a Grothendieck category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is said to be stable if it is stable under essential extensions. The set 𝐋 s ​ t ​ ( 𝒞 ) \mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C}) of stable localizing subcategories of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is partially ordered under reverse inclusion. We show ℒ ↦ Z ⁡ ( 𝒞 / ℒ ) \mathcal{L}\mapsto Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}) defines a sheaf of commutative rings on 𝐋 s ​ t ​ ( 𝒞 ) \mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C}) with respect to finite coverings. When 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is assumed to be locally noetherian, we also show that the sheaf condition holds for arbitrary coverings.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

In his thesis
[
Gab
]
, Gabriel proved that a noetherian scheme
X
can be reconstructed from the category of quasi-coherent sheaves
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
on
X
. This category is an example of a
Grothendieck category
: it is abelian, it has small coproducts (hence small colimits) such that filtered colimits are exact, and it has a generator. It also enjoys two additional properties: it is locally noetherian, and every localizing subcategory is stable.

To prove his reconstruction theorem, Gabriel introduces the injective spectrum
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
of any Grothendieck category
\mathcal{C}
(see
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:StabMain}
below for more details) and shows that for
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
, this spectrum is in a natural bijection with
X
. The open subsets
U
of the scheme
X
, through their images in
\mathbf{Sp}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X))
, give rise to certain localizing subcategories
\mathcal{L}_{U}
of
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
. Then he shows that the ring of sections
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
of the structure sheaf of the scheme
X
over
U
is naturally isomorphic to the center
Z(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)/\mathcal{L}_{U})
of the quotient category
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)/\mathcal{L}_{U}
. Gabriel’s theorem was later generalised to arbitrary quasi-separated schemes (
[
Ros
]
,
[
Bra
]
).

In this paper, motivated by considerations arising from the mod-
p
representation theory of
p
-adic reductive groups, we generalise Gabriel’s approach to the construction of a ‘central’ sheaf for any Grothendieck category
\mathcal{C}
. There exist fairly natural non-trivial examples of Grothendieck categories
\mathcal{C}
where
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
is the empty set, the so-called
continuous spectral categories
, (cf.
[
Pop
]
p. 324). Therefore it seems appropriate to take the viewpoint of ‘pointless topology’ and try to work with the presheaf of centers
\mathcal{L}\mapsto Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})
directly.

1.2.
Stability and main results

Taking cue again from our primary example of interest, namely the category
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
of smooth
k
-linear representations of a connected reductive
p
-adic group
G
with coefficients in a field
k
, we note that although
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
definitely exhibits some serious non-commutative behaviour, this category appears to be nevertheless closer to being ‘commutative’ than, say, the category of modules over a complicated non-commutative ring such as a Weyl algebra. To support this intuition, note that at least when
k=\mathbb{C}
is the field of complex numbers, Bernstein’s famous results about the structure of
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
(cf.
[
Ber
]
) imply that the connected components of this category are Morita equivalent to algebras that are finitely generated modules over their noetherian centers.

For a general non-commutative ring
A
the localizing subcategories (or the hereditary torsion theories,
[
Go2
]
,
[
Ste
]
) of the Grothendieck category
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
can exhibit a rather wild behaviour. The basic outcome of this paper is the observation that, at least as far as the behaviour of centers is concerned, the situation improves considerably if we restrict our attention only to the stable localizing subcategories. We view the set
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
of stable localizing subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
as a category with objects being the elements of
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
and where the morphisms
\mathcal{L}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1}
are given by the inclusions
\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}
. For
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
in
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
we call
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
a
covering
of
\mathcal{L}
if
\mathcal{L}=\bigcap_{i}\mathcal{L}_{i}
. Our first main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any Grothendieck category
\mathcal{C}
,
Z_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{L}):=Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})
is a sheaf on
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
.

This result will be a consequence of the following purely categorical statement, which we prove in Prop.
4.5
. We first recall that Gabriel (cf.
[
Gab
]
p. 439) introduced for any diagram of exact functors between abelian categories

\textstyle{\mathcal{A}_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

the category
\mathcal{A}_{0}\,\underset{\mathcal{A}}{\prod}\,A_{1}
which he calls the
recollement of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}
along
\mathcal{A}
. We apply this with
\mathcal{A}_{i}:=\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}_{i}
and
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{C}/(\mathcal{L}_{0}\vee\mathcal{L}_{1})
and the corresponding quotient functors, where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
are two localizing subcategories of our Grothendieck category
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}\vee\mathcal{L}_{1}
denotes the smallest localizing subcategory which contains
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. It will be easy to see that the corresponding recollement receives a natural functor

\mathcal{C}/(\mathcal{L}_{0}\cap\mathcal{L}_{1})\longrightarrow(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}_{0})\underset{\mathcal{C}/(\mathcal{L}_{0}\vee\mathcal{L}_{1})}{\prod}{}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}_{1})\ .

Our main result is that this functor is an equivalence provided
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
are
stable
.

The restriction to the case of finite coverings in Thm.
1.1
can be removed if we impose a finiteness condition on the Grothendieck category
\mathcal{C}
as follows.

Recall that the injective spectrum
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
of
any
Grothendieck category
\mathcal{C}
is the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable injective objects of
\mathcal{C}
(cf.
[
Pop
]
p. 331). If
\mathcal{L}
is a localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, then an object of
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-torsion-free
if it has no non-zero subobjects in
\mathcal{L}
, and the subset
A(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
is by definition the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable
\mathcal{L}
-torsion-free injective objects. A subset
A\subseteq\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
will be called
stable
if it is of the form
A(\mathcal{L})
for some stable localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Recall that
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
locally noetherian
if it admits a set of noetherian generators. The work of Gabriel
[
Gab
]
implies that when
\mathcal{C}
is locally noetherian, the map
\mathcal{L}\mapsto A(\mathcal{L})
gives a bijection between the set
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
and the set of stable subsets of
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
. It is easy to see that the stable subsets form a topology on
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
that we call the
stable topology
. For any stable subset
A\subseteq\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
, let
\mathcal{L}_{A}
denote the corresponding stable localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
such that
A(\mathcal{L}_{A})=A
. The map
A\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{A}
is inclusion reversing. Hence, for stable subsets
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}
, we have a quotient functor
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}_{A_{2}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}_{A_{1}}
. This means that the rule
A\mapsto\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(A):=Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}_{A})
defines a presheaf
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
on
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
.

Our second main result is then the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a locally noetherian Grothendieck category. Then
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a sheaf on
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
for the stable topology.

Equivalently, the presheaf
Z_{\mathcal{C}}
appearing in Thm.
1.1
satisfies the sheaf condition for arbitrary coverings under the assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is locally noetherian.

In the case where
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Mod}(R)
for some non-commutative noetherian ring
R
, a sheaf of non-commutative rings on
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
in the stable topology is constructed in
[
GM
]
and
[
Lou
]
. Our sheaf
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
is isomorphic to the center of their sheaf. But the techniques in these papers rely heavily on the theory of rings of quotients (
[
Ste
]
) and do not generalise to the case of a general locally noetherian category.

We will show elsewhere that
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Mod}(G)
is a locally noetherian category when
G
is the group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We will also explain how to calculate
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
and give a uniform construction of a large family of stable subsets of
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
. The resulting sheaf
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
is then closely related to the recent work of Dotto-Emerton-Gee
[
DEG
]
.

In fact, in a lecture in Dublin in July 2019 describing the forthcoming work
[
DEG
]
, Emerton talked about the category
\operatorname{Mod}_{\chi}(G)
of mod-
p
smooth
G=GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-representations with a fixed central character
\chi
. He made the very inspiring suggestion that the center of this category is small and uninteresting, but that this only reflects the fact that this center is the ring of global sections of an interesting sheaf formed by the centers of the quotient categories of
\operatorname{Mod}_{\chi}(G)
. In our paper
[
AS
]
we showed that, indeed, the center of the category
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
of mod-
p
smooth
G
-representations of any connected algebraic
p
-adic group
G
only depends on the center of
G
. The present paper now sets up a general formalism of central sheaves.

2.
The setting

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be any abelian category. Its center
Z(\mathfrak{A})
is, by definition, the endomorphism ring of the identity functor on
\mathfrak{A}
. It is a commutative ring, which has the following partial functoriality properties:

–

For any full abelian subcategory
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{A}
restriction induces a ring homomorphism
Z(\mathfrak{A})\rightarrow Z(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime})
.

–

Any quotient functor
\mathfrak{A}\rightarrow\mathfrak{A}/\mathfrak{B}
with respect to a Serre subcategory
\mathfrak{B}
of
\mathfrak{A}
induces a ring homomorphism
Z(\mathfrak{A})\rightarrow Z(\mathfrak{A}/\mathfrak{B})
.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{C}
is a Grothendieck category. This means that
\mathcal{C}
is an abelian category which has small coproducts (hence small colimits) such that filtered colimits are exact and which has a generator. Such a category is locally small (i.e., the subobjects of any given object form a set) and has enough injective objects (hence injective hulls) as well as small products. In this case we may consider the following construction.

Recall that a full subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
localizing
if it is closed under the formation of subobjects, quotient objects, extensions, and arbitrary direct sums. In particular, it is strictly full and abelian.

We note that the collection of all localizing subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
is a set (
[
Pop
]
Note 3 on p. 298), which we will denote by
\mathbf{L}(\mathcal{C})
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Since it is, in particular, a Serre subcategory we may form the quotient category
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
together with the exact quotient functor
T:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
(
[
Gab
]
Prop. 1 on p. 367). The functor
T
has a right adjoint
S:\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
, called the
section functor
, which is left exact and fully faithful (
[
Gab
]
Prop. 8 on p. 377 and Prop. 2 on p. 369 and
[
Pop
]
p. 177). We also have, for any object
Y
in
\mathcal{C}
, a largest subobject
t_{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
of
Y
which is maximal among all subobjects of
Y
which lie in
\mathcal{L}
(
[
Gab
]
Cor. 1 on p. 375).

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
are both Grothendieck categories, and the quotient functor
T:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
commutes with inductive limits.

(2)

T\circ S\cong\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}}
.

(3)

The section functor
S:\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
preserves injective objects as well as essential extensions.

Proof.

(1) is
[
Gab
]
Prop. 9 on p. 378. (2) is
[
Gab
]
Prop. 3(a) on p. 371. (3) The preservation of injective objects follows from the exactness of
T
and the adjointness
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}}(T(-),I)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,S(I))
. For the preservation of essential extensions see
[
Pop
]
Cor. 4.4.7.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be localizing subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
; we have:

i.

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
is a localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
;

ii.

the natural functor
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
induces an equivalence
(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})\big/(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L})\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
;

iii.

The section functor
S:\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
sends
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

That
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
is a Serre subcategory of
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
together with the asserted equivalence is a special case of
[
Pop
]
Ex. 4.3.6. By Lemma
2.1
the functor
T
commutes with arbitrary direct sums. Any family of objects in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
can be lifted to a family in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is closed under the formation of direct sums (in
\mathcal{C}
) we conclude that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
is closed under the formation of direct sums (in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
). It follows (
[
Gab
]
Prop. 8 on p. 377 or
[
Pop
]
Prop. 4.6.3) that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
is localizing in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
.

For iii. let
T(X)
be any object in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
for some object
X
in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, and consider the exact sequence
0\rightarrow t_{\mathcal{L}}(X)\rightarrow X\rightarrow ST(X)\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
. The outer terms
t_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
and
C
lie in
\mathcal{L}
and hence also in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
because
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Applying the exact quotient functor
T^{\prime}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
to this exact sequence shows that
T^{\prime}(ST(X))\cong T^{\prime}(X)=0
. Hence
S(T(X))
lies in
\ker(T^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
as required.
∎

Remark
2.2
says that for localizing subcategories
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
the natural functor
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a quotient functor and hence induces a natural ring homomorphism
Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})\rightarrow Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
between the corresponding centers. Instead of just considering the center
Z(\mathcal{C})
we propose to investigate the “presheaf” of commutative rings
\mathcal{L}\mapsto Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})
on
\mathbf{L}(\mathcal{C})
. This will require the concept of stability of
\mathcal{L}
, which we will discuss in the next section.

Here we only remark that obviously the intersection of any family of localizing subcategories again is localizing. Therefore, for any two localizing subcategories
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
of
\mathcal{C}
the smallest localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}_{1}\vee\mathcal{L}_{2}
of
\mathcal{C}
which contains
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is well defined.

3.
Stability

Throughout the paper we let
\mathcal{C}
denote a Grothendieck category. A localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
stable
if it is closed under the passage to essential extensions. Obviously the intersection of any family of stable localizing subcategories is stable.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any stable localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{C}
the quotient functor
T:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
preserves injective objects.

Proof.

[
Gab
]
Cor. 3 on p. 375.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a stable localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
and let
Y
be an injective object in
\mathcal{C}
. We have:

(1)

ST(Y)
is injective in
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

There is an isomorphism
Y\cong t_{\mathcal{L}}(Y)\oplus ST(Y)
in
\mathcal{C}
.

(3)

t_{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
is injective in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
2.1
(3).

(2) This follows from
[
Gab
]
Cor. 2 on p. 375, using the stability assumption on
\mathcal{L}
.

(3) By (2),
t_{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
is a direct summand of the injective object
Y
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be localizing subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{L}
is stable. Then
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is stable in
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
is stable in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

“
\Longrightarrow
” Let
X\hookrightarrow Y
be an essential extension in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
, where
X
is an object in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
. Then
S(X)\hookrightarrow S(Y)
is an essential extension in
\mathcal{C}
by Lemma
2.1
(3). Since
S(X)
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
by Remark
2.2
.iii and since
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is stable in
\mathcal{C}
by assumption, we see that
S(Y)
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Using Lemma
2.1
(1), we deduce that
Y\cong TS(Y)
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
as required.

“
\Longleftarrow
” Let
X
be a non-zero object in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and let
Y
be an injective hull of
X
in
\mathcal{C}
; we have to show that
Y
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{L}
is stable, using Prop.
3.2
(2) we find an isomorphism
Y\cong W\oplus Y^{\prime}
where
W:=t_{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
and
Y^{\prime}:=ST(Y)
. We will show that
Y^{\prime}
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. We may assume that
Y^{\prime}\neq 0
, as the claim is clear otherwise.

Consider the essential extension
j:X\hookrightarrow W\oplus Y^{\prime}
. Since
Y^{\prime}
is non-zero,
X^{\prime}:=j(X)\cap Y^{\prime}
is essential in
Y^{\prime}
. Since
Y^{\prime}
is
\mathcal{L}
-torsion-free,
T(X^{\prime})
is essential in
T(Y^{\prime})
by
[
Gab
]
Prop. 6 on p. 374. Since
X^{\prime}
is a subobject of
X
,
X^{\prime}
lies in the localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, so
T(X^{\prime})
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
. Since
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
is stable in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
by assumption, the essential extension
T(Y^{\prime})
of
T(X^{\prime})
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}
as well. Hence
ST(Y^{\prime})
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
by Remark
2.2
.iii. However
ST(Y^{\prime})=STST(Y)\cong ST(Y)=Y^{\prime}
, so
Y^{\prime}
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
as claimed.

Since
W
lies in
\mathcal{L}
and since
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, we see that
Y\cong W\oplus Y^{\prime}
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
as required.
∎

Next we will establish that with
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
also
\mathcal{L}_{1}\vee\mathcal{L}_{2}
is stable.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
be two localizing subcatgories of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}=0
. The strictly full subcategory
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
of
\mathcal{C}
whose objects are isomorphic to
X_{1}\oplus X_{2}
with
X_{i}
being an object of
\mathcal{L}_{i}
for
i=1,2
is a stable localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let
X=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}
be an object in
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
, where
X_{i}
lies in
\mathcal{L}_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Let
Y
be a subobject of
X
in
\mathcal{C}
and let
Y_{i}=X_{i}\cap Y
for
i=1,2
. Then
Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2}
is an object in
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
contained in
Y
. We have the monomorphism

Y/Y_{1}=Y/(X_{1}\cap Y)\cong(Y+X_{1})/X_{1}\hookrightarrow X/X_{1}\cong X_{2}\ ,

which shows that
Y/Y_{1}
lies in
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. On the other hand
Y/Y_{1}
admits an epimorphism onto
Y/(Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2})
so that the latter lies in
\mathcal{L}_{2}
as well. The same argument works with the roles of the indices
1
and
2
exchanged. We deduce that
Y/(Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2})
lies in
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}
, and is therefore zero. Hence
Y=Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2}
lies in
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
. By passing to the quotient objects, we see that
X/Y\cong(X_{1}/Y_{1})\oplus(X_{2}/Y_{2})
also lies in
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
.

We have shown that
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
is closed under quotient objects and subobjects. Since the formation of injective hulls commutes with finite direct sums by
[
Gab
]
Lemma 2 on p. 358, and since
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are both stable in
\mathcal{C}
, it is clear that
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
is stable under injective hulls in
\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{3}
is clearly closed under the formation of small coproducts in
\mathcal{C}
, we see that it remains to show that
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
is closed under extensions in
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
0\rightarrow X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow Z\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{C}
, where
X
and
Z
are objects in
\mathcal{L}_{1,2}
. Let
I
and
J
be injective envelopes of
X
and
Z
, respectively. Using (the dual of) the Horseshoe Lemma (
[
Wei
]

Corollary 3.6

5.

We will make precise in which way

is a sheaf.

The set

partially ordered by inclusion together with

is a lattice. By Prop.

the subset
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathbf{L}(\mathcal{C})

Proposition 5.1
.

is distributive.

Proof.

The proof given in
[
Go2
]
Prop. 29.1 in case

for any ring

works in general. For the convenience of the reader we provide one more detail. For avoiding confusion we first point out that Golan defines torsion theories always to be hereditary and therefore to correspond to localizing subcategories. The proof in loc. cit. reduces the assertion to the following claim: Suppose that an object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathcal{L}_{i}
-torsion-free for
i=1,2
; then it is also
\mathcal{L}_{1}\vee\mathcal{L}_{2}
-torsion-free. To see this let
\mathcal{L}(X)
denoted the localizing subcategory cogenerated by the injective hull
E(X)
. Since
X
is
\mathcal{L}_{i}
-torsion-free one has
\mathcal{L}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
. It follows that
\mathcal{L}_{1}\vee\mathcal{L}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
. But by definition
E(X)
is
\mathcal{L}(X)
-torsion-free and hence is a fortiori
\mathcal{L}_{1}\vee\mathcal{L}_{2}
-torsion-free. Therefore
X
must be
\mathcal{L}_{1}\vee\mathcal{L}_{2}
-torsion-free.
∎

We now view
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
as a category with objects being the elements of
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
and where the morphisms
\mathcal{L}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1}
are given by the inclusions
\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}
. For
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
in
\mathbf{L}^{st}(\mathcal{C})
we call

if

.

Lemma 5.2
.

With the above notion of coverings the category

Proof.

Since any diagram in

\textstyle{\mathcal{L}_{1}\vee\mathcal{L}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathcal{L}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{L}}

is a fiber product diagram the category

has fiber products. Obviously coverings compose into coverings. That any base change of a covering again is a covering is immediate from the distributivity in Prop.

.
∎

Obviously
\mathcal{L}\mapsto Z(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})

Proof.

This is now immediate from Prop.
4.6
.
∎

, which is a contradiction. We conclude that

\operatorname{ob}(\mathcal{L})=\{M\in\operatorname{ob}(\mathcal{C}):\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,\oplus_{x\in A(\mathcal{L})}E_{x})=0\}=\operatorname{ob}(\mathcal{L}_{A(\mathcal{L})})\ .

Also recall from Lemma

that
\mathcal{L}_{A}
is stable if and only if

is stable.

For any localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{C}
we let

denote the quotient functor and

its right adjoint section functor. Recall that

is fully faithful.

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a stable localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the quotient functor

restricts to an equivalence of categories between the full subcategory of all injective objects of
\mathcal{C}
which have no non-zero subobject contained in
\mathcal{L}
and the full subcategory of all injective objects of
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

By
[
Gab
]
Lemma 1 on p. 370 an injective object of
\mathcal{C}
has no non-zero subobject contained in
\mathcal{L}
if and only if it is
\mathcal{L}
-closed. We therefore consider the full subcategory
\mathcal{D}
of all
\mathcal{L}
-closed objects of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the restriction of
q_{\mathcal{L}}
to
\mathcal{D}
is an equivalence of categories with quasi-inverse

, by
[
Gab
]
Prop. 3(a) and Cor. on p. 371. So it is enough to show that
q_{\mathcal{L}}
sends the
\mathcal{L}
-closed injectives in
\mathcal{C}
to injectives in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
, and that
s_{\mathcal{L}}
sends injectives in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
to the
\mathcal{L}
-closed injectives in
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
I
be an
\mathcal{L}
-closed injective object in
\mathcal{C}
. Then because
\mathcal{L}
is stable,
q_{\mathcal{L}}(I)
is an injective object in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
by
[
Gab
]
Cor. 3 on p. 375. Conversely, let
I
be an injective object in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
; then
s_{\mathcal{L}}(I)
is an injective object in
\mathcal{C}
since

is an exact left adjoint to

, and furthermore

is
\mathcal{L}
-closed by
[
Gab
]
Lemma 2 on p. 371.
∎

The injective objects
E_{x}
, for
x\in A(\mathcal{L})
, by definition, and also the injective object

, by Remark
6.14
, have no non-zero subobject contained in
\mathcal{L}
. We now assume that
\mathcal{L}
is stable. It follows from
[
Gab
]
last paragraph on p. 383 (using the stability of
\mathcal{L}
) that the

, are precisely the elements of
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})
, so that we choose these

as representatives. The functor

is a left adjoint and therefore commutes with arbitrary direct sums; hence

. The Lemma

then implies that

But the functor

is fully faithful. Therefore we obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 6.16
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a stable localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then
q_{\mathcal{L}}(E_{A(\mathcal{L})})=E_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}}^{\oplus}
, and the functor

induces an isomorphism of rings

\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(E_{A(\mathcal{L})})\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}}(E_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}}^{\oplus})\ .

Note that for the same reason the functor

also induces isomorphisms of rings

(10)

\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(E_{x})\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}}(q_{\mathcal{L}}(E_{x}))\qquad\text{for any $x\in A(\mathcal{L})$}.

At this point we need a generalization of the well-known calculation of the center of a matrix ring

over some associative ring

are necessarily scalar matrices with entries in

.

Lemma 6.17
.

Let
\{X_{i}:i\in I\}
be a collection of objects in
\mathcal{C}
, and let

Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X))=\\
\{(z_{i})_{i}\in\prod_{i\in I}Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i})):z_{j}v=vz_{i}\ \text{for all $i,j\in I$ and $v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},X_{j})$}\}.

Proof.

Recall the injective map
\mu
defined in (
8
). Suppose that
\alpha\in Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X))
. Take some
v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},X_{j})
and take any
k,\ell\in I
. Then
\pi_{k}\alpha(\iota_{j}v\pi_{i})\iota_{\ell}=\pi_{k}(\iota_{j}v\pi_{i})\alpha\iota_{\ell}
; since
\pi_{i}\iota_{\ell}=\delta_{i\ell}\operatorname{id}_{X_{\ell}}
we deduce that

(11)

\delta_{i\ell}\alpha_{jk}v=\delta_{kj}v\alpha_{\ell i}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i,j,k,\ell\in I,v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},X_{j})\ .

Suppose that
j\neq k
. Take
\ell:=i:=j
and
v:=\operatorname{id}_{X_{j}}
in (
11
) to deduce that
\alpha_{jk}=0
whenever
j\neq k
; thus the matrix
\mu(\alpha)
of any
\alpha\in Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X))
is diagonal. Fixing
i,j\in I
and taking
\ell:=i
and
k:=j
in (
11
) shows that
\alpha_{jj}v=v\alpha_{ii}
holds for all
v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},X_{j})
. Taking
i=j
in this last equation shows that
\alpha_{ii}\in Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i}))
for all
i\in I
. This shows that the map

Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X))\rightarrow\{(z_{i})_{i}\in\prod_{i\in I}Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i})):z_{j}v=vz_{i}\ \text{for all $v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},X_{j})$}\}

that sends
\alpha\in Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X))
to the vector
(\alpha_{ii})
is well-defined; it is furthermore injective, because
\mu(\alpha)
is diagonal and because
\mu
is injective.

To show that the map is surjective, take any
(z_{i})_{i}\in\prod_{i\in I}Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i}))
satisfying the given condition and define
\alpha:=\oplus_{i}z_{i}
, an element of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
. We must show that
\alpha
is central. Note that
\pi_{j}\alpha=z_{j}\pi_{j}
and
\alpha\iota_{i}=\iota_{i}z_{i}
for all
i,j\in I
, by the definition of
\alpha
. Now if
\beta\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
and
i,j\in I
, then applying the condition on the
z_{i}
’s with
v:=\pi_{j}\beta\iota_{i}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},X_{j})
, we have

\pi_{j}\alpha\beta\iota_{i}=z_{j}\pi_{j}\beta\iota_{i}=z_{j}v=vz_{i}=\pi_{j}\beta\iota_{i}z_{i}=\pi_{j}\beta\alpha\iota_{i}\ .

Hence
\mu(\alpha\beta)=\mu(\beta\alpha)
for all
\beta\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
. Since
\mu
is injective, we conclude that
\alpha
is central in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
as claimed.
∎

We let
\alpha_{A}:Z(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(E_{A}))\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{F}(A)
be the isomorphism given by the above Lemma

.

7.
Other cases

There are two more cases where
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
can be used for an alternative interpretation of the central sheaf

.

7.1.
The locally finitely presented case

Throughout this section we make the weaker assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is locally finitely presented. This means that the Grothendieck category has a set of finitely presented generators. Then any object of
\mathcal{C}
is a filtered colimit of finitely presented objects.

Recall from Remark
6.1
that in the locally noetherian case all section functors commute with filtered colimits. This is no longer the case for a general locally finitely presented
\mathcal{C}
. Therefore one restricts attention to the localizing subcategories
\mathcal{L}
which are of finite type. By definition this means that the corresponding section functor commutes filtered colimits whose transition maps are monomorphisms.

Remark 7.1
.

For any localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of finite type in
\mathcal{C}
, the quotient category
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L}
is, in general, only locally finitely generated.

Proof.

[
Ga0
]
Thm. 5.8 (compare also Prop. 5.9).
∎

.

i.

\mathcal{C}

subsets of
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\longmapsto A(\mathcal{L})

is an inclusion reversing bijection.

\mathcal{L}_{1}

, and

of

we have:

and

For i. and ii.a) see

7.1.1.
A basic case

An important class of Grothendieck categories where Prop.
7.2
and its corollaries apply is the following. Let
G
be a locally profinite group and
k
be a field. We let
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
denote the category of smooth
G
-representations in
k
-vector spaces. (Recall that a
G
-representation
V
is called smooth if the stabilizer of any vector in
V
is open in
G
.) This obviously is an (AB5) abelian category.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{U}
be a fundamental system of compact open subgroups of
G
. Then the representations
k[G/U]
, for
U\in\mathfrak{U}
, form a set of generators of
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
be a non-zero map and choose a
v\in V
such that
\alpha(v)\neq 0
as well as a
U\in\mathfrak{U}
which fixes
v
. Then the map
\alpha^{U}:V^{U}\rightarrow V^{\prime U}
on
U
-fixed vectors is non-zero. It remains to note that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}(k[G/U],-)=(-)^{U}
.
∎

It follows that
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
is a Grothendieck category. It clearly is locally of finite type.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
U\subseteq G
be a compact open subgroup and
M
be a finite dimensional representation in
\operatorname{Mod}(U)
. Then the compact induction
\operatorname{ind}_{U}^{G}(M)
is finitely presented in
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
.

Proof.

We have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}(\operatorname{ind}_{U}^{G}(M),V)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(U)}(M,V)=(\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(M,k)\otimes_{k}V)^{U}\ .

Now use
[
Pop
]

Thm.
3.5.10 observing that the functor
(-)^{U}
of
U
-invariants commutes with filtered colimits.
∎

Proposition 7.7
.

The category
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
is locally finitely presented.

Proof.

This is immediate from the above two lemmas.
∎

Throughout this section we assume that

Lemma 7.9
.

For any localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{C}
the categories
\mathcal{L}
and

are locally coirreducible as well.

Proof.

[
Pop
]
Prop. 5.3.6.
∎

For the convenience of the reader we first recall the following elementary concept. Let
S
be any set. A
topological closure operator
on
S
is a selfmap
A\mapsto\overline{A}
on the power set
\mathfrak{P}(S)
satisfying:

(1)

\overline{\emptyset}=\emptyset
,

(2)

A\subseteq\overline{A}
(in particular,
\overline{X}=X
),

(3)

\overline{\overline{A}}=\overline{A}
,

(4)

\overline{A\cup B}=\overline{A}\cup\overline{B}
.

Note that
(4)
implies

(5)

A\subseteq B\Longrightarrow\overline{A}\subseteq\overline{B}
.

A subset
A\subset S
is called
closed
if
\overline{A}=A
.

Consider an arbitrary family
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of closed subsets and put
A:=\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}
. Then

A\subseteq\overline{A}\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I}\overline{A_{i}}=\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}=A\ .

It follows that
A
is closed. This shows that there is a unique topology on
S
whose closed subsets are the closed subsets in the above sense.

For any localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{C}
we earlier defined the subset
A(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
. Vice versa, for any subset
A\subseteq\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
, we define

\mathcal{L}_{A}:=\ \text{localizing subcategory of all $Y$ such that $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,E)=0$ for any $[E]\in A$}.

Proposition 7.10
.

i.

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A(\mathcal{L})}
for any localizing subcategory
\mathcal{L}
;

ii.

the map
A\mapsto A(\mathcal{L}_{A})
is a topological closure operator on
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

i. We obviously have

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{A(\mathcal{L})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad A(\mathcal{L})\subseteq A(\mathcal{L}_{A(\mathcal{L})})\subseteq A(\mathcal{L})\ ,

hence

(13)

It follows from
[

]
Cor. 2 on p. 375 that the section functor

induces a bijection
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{L})\xrightarrow{\simeq}A(\mathcal{L})
. (Note that the above even holds for a general Grothendieck category.) Therefore
[
Pop
]
Cor. 5.3.8 says that (

) implies that
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A(\mathcal{L})}
.

ii.
[
Pop
]

∎

We call a subset of
\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}\text{set of all closed subsets of $\mathbf{Sp}(\mathcal{C})$}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\longmapsto A(\mathcal{L})

.

The presheaf

on

has the sheaf property for finite coverings of stable closed subsets by stable closed subsets.

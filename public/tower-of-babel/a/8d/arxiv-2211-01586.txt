Abstract

Our proofs of (1) and (2) rely on the commutativity and spectral theorem for the integrable hierarchy associated to ℒ {\mathcal{L}} , both established by Nazarov–Sklyanin. Finally, we conjecture that the ℒ {\mathcal{L}} -eigenfunctions ψ λ s ∈ F ⁡ [ w ] \psi_{\lambda}^{s}{\in F[w]} with eigenvalue [ s ] [s] and constant term ψ λ s | w = 0 = j λ \psi_{\lambda}^{s}|_{w=0}=j_{\lambda} are polynomials in the rescaled power sum basis V μ ​ w l V_{\mu}w^{l} of F ⁡ [ w ] F[w] with integer coefficients.

1.1
The Nazarov–Sklyanin Lax operator

Consider the graded polynomial ring

F={\mathbb{C}}[V_{1},V_{2},\ldots]

(1.1)

in which
\deg V_{k}=k.
For any
\hbar\in{\mathbb{C}}
, let
V_{-k}
be the differential operator in
F
defined by

V_{-k}=\hbar k\frac{\partial}{\partial V_{k}}.

(1.2)

Since
[V_{-k},V_{k}]=\hbar k
, (
1.1
) is the Fock space representation of the Heisenberg algebra at level
\hbar
. We will identify
F
with the ring of symmetric functions in Section
1.2
. Introduce a new variable
w
with
\deg w=1
and consider the graded polynomial and Laurent polynomial rings

\displaystyle F[w]={\mathbb{C}}[w,V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},\ldots],

(1.3)

\displaystyle F\big[w,w^{-1}\big]={\mathbb{C}}\big[w,w^{-1},V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},\ldots\big].

Define the projection
\pi\colon F\big[w,w^{-1}\big]\rightarrow F[w]
to be the linear extension of

\pi w^{l}=\begin{cases}\,\,w^{l}&\textnormal{if}\,\,l\geq 0,\\
\,\,0&\textnormal{if}\,\,l<0.\end{cases}

(1.4)

We now present the Lax operator from
[
32
]
using the conventions from
[
30
,
36
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

For any
\overline{\varepsilon},\hbar\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\partial_{w}=\frac{\partial}{\partial w}
, the Nazarov–Sklyanin Lax operator
{\mathcal{L}}

[
32
]
is the graded linear operator
{\mathcal{L}}\colon F[w]\rightarrow F[w]
in the graded polynomial ring
F[w]
in (
1.3
) defined by

{\mathcal{L}}=\overline{\varepsilon}w\partial_{w}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}V_{-k}w^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}V_{k}\pi w^{-k}

(1.5)

for
V_{-k}
in (
1.2
),
\pi
in (
1.4
). Below, we may write
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{L}}_{\overline{\varepsilon},\hbar}
to emphasize that
V_{-k}
depends on
\hbar
.

Due to the presence of
\pi
in (
1.5
),
{\mathcal{L}}
is well defined with codomain
F[w]
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{L}}
preserves total degree in
F[w]
since
w\partial_{w}
preserves degree, multiplication by
w^{k}
and
V_{k}
raise degree by
k
, and
V_{-k}
and
\pi w^{-k}
lower degree by
k
. The Lax operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\overline{\varepsilon},\hbar}
is a two-parameter perturbation of a nilpotent linear operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{0,0}
by linear differential operators which act as derivations in
F[w]
.

Since
\big\{w^{l}\big\}_{l=0}^{\infty}
is a basis of
{\mathbb{C}}[w]
indexed by
{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
,
{\mathcal{L}}
in (
1.5
) acts as an
{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
matrix in
F[w]=F\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[w]
with coefficients in
{\textnormal{End}}(F)
: using the definition of
V_{-k}
in (
1.2
), this matrix is

{\mathcal{L}}=\begin{bmatrix}0\overline{\varepsilon}&V_{1}&V_{2}&V_{3}&V_{4}&\cdots\\
V_{-1}&1\overline{\varepsilon}&V_{1}&V_{2}&V_{3}&\ddots\\
V_{-2}&V_{-1}&2\overline{\varepsilon}&V_{1}&V_{2}&\ddots\\
V_{-3}&V_{-2}&V_{-1}&3\overline{\varepsilon}&V_{1}&\ddots\\
V_{-4}&V_{-3}&V_{-2}&V_{-1}&4\overline{\varepsilon}&\ddots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}.

(1.6)

One can see from (
1.6
) that
{\mathcal{L}}_{0,0}
is strictly upper triangular if
\overline{\varepsilon}=\hbar=0
, hence nilpotent in
F[w]
as we already mentioned. If one sets
\overline{\varepsilon}=0
but keeps
\hbar\neq 0
, the diagonal terms in (
1.6
) vanish and the entries of
{\mathcal{L}}_{0,\hbar}
are constant along diagonals. In this special case, one may regard
{\mathcal{L}}_{0,\hbar}
as a block Toeplitz operator with infinite blocks from
{\textnormal{End}}(F)
.

1.2
Jack symmetric functions

Recall that a partition
\lambda
of
n
is a sequence of non-negative integers
\lambda_{i}
which are weakly decreasing
0\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}
and satisfy
\cdots+\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1}=n
. For any
{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, let
j_{\lambda}\in F
be the Jack polynomial in the ring
F
in (
1.1
) as defined in
[
22
,
30
,
36
,
37
,
40
]
. For example, the Jack polynomials in degrees
1\leq|\lambda|\leq 3
are

\displaystyle j_{1}=V_{1},

\displaystyle j_{2}=V_{1}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}V_{2},

\displaystyle j_{1,1}=V_{1}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2}V_{2},

\displaystyle j_{3}=V_{1}^{3}+3{\varepsilon}_{1}V_{1}V_{2}+2{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}V_{3},

\displaystyle j_{1,2}=V_{1}^{3}+({\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2})V_{1}V_{2}+({\varepsilon}_{1}{\varepsilon}_{2})V_{3},

\displaystyle j_{1,1,1}=V_{1}^{3}+3{\varepsilon}_{2}V_{1}V_{2}+2{\varepsilon}_{2}^{2}V_{3}.

For
{\varepsilon}_{2}\neq 0
, these polynomials are equivalent to
J_{\lambda}(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots;\alpha)
, the integral form Jack symmetric function in
[
14
,
23
,
44
]
where
p_{k}
are the power sum symmetric functions and
\alpha
is the Jack parameter. Precisely, for
{\varepsilon}_{2}\neq 0
, if one sets
p_{k}=V_{k}/(-{\varepsilon}_{2})
and
\alpha={\varepsilon}_{1}/(-{\varepsilon}_{2})
, then

j_{\lambda}=(-{\varepsilon}_{2})^{|\lambda|}J_{\lambda}\Bigg(\frac{V_{1}}{(-{\varepsilon}_{2})},\frac{V_{2}}{(-{\varepsilon}_{2})},\ldots;\frac{{\varepsilon}_{1}}{(-{\varepsilon}_{2})}\Bigg).

(1.7)

At
{\varepsilon}_{1}=\alpha
and
{\varepsilon}_{2}=-1
,
j_{\lambda}=J_{\lambda}
.

Addable and removable corners of Young diagrams

\lambda

\lambda=\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\big\{{(c-1,r-1)}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}\colon c\in\{1,2,\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}\big\}

Definition 1.2

For

(1.10)

It is also convenient to define the outer corner set

1.5
Spectral theorem for the Nazarov–Sklyanin Lax operator

Nazarov–Sklyanin proved in
[

that the ingredients in Sections

(1.12)

\displaystyle\hbar=-{\varepsilon}_{1}{\varepsilon}_{2}.

itself.

1.5
). Assume that

parametrizing
\overline{\varepsilon}
,

by the formulas (

) and (

(1)

The degree

component

of the graded ring

-cyclic subspaces

The restrictions of

to the subspaces

Lemma 3.2

Proof.

By the Cauchy interlacing theorem – e.g.,

) and (

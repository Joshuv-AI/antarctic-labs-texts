Smoluchowski coagulation equation with velocity dependence
Franco Flandoli, Ruojun Huang, Andrea Papini

Abstract
. In the present article, we introduce a variant of Smoluchowski’s coagulation equation with both position and velocity variables taking a kinetic viewpoint arising as the scaling limit of a system of second-order (microscopic) coagulating particles. We focus on the rigorous study of the PDE system in the spatially-homogeneous case proving existence and uniqueness under different initial conditions in suitable weighted space, investigating also the regularity of such solutions.



1.
Introduction

Coagulation processes are ubiquitous in nature, from the movement of cells to the atmosphere, and in general complicated to understand experimentally and mathematically. In this work, we are motivated by the question whether a turbulent velocity field in the atmosphere enhances the coagulation of small rain droplets, and therefore favors rain fall. Physics literature on this topic has been vast, at least as early as Saffman-Turner
[
17
]
in the fifties, see also
[
3
,
16
,
18
]
, arguing in favor of such coagulation enhancement. Here and in subsequent works, we give a novel approach to this problem that is fully mathematical. In particular, we take a kinetic viewpoint and study rigorously a variant of Smoluchowski’s coagulation equation with velocity dependence that is akin to Boltzmann equation. It arises as the scaling limit of a system of second-order (microscopic) coagulating particles, modelling the interactions of rain droplets in the clouds, which are subjected to a common noise of transport type. Such a noise, constructed in recent mathematical works
[
7
,
6
]
, possesses several characteristics of real turbulence, such as it enhances diffusion of passive scalars. In the present work, we focus on the existence, uniqueness and regularity of this new

PDE

, after briefly introducing its origin.

Smoluchowski’s classical equation
[
20
]
provides a first model for the time evolution of the probability distribution
\{f_{m}(t,x)\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
of diffusing particles
of different sizes (or masses)
m\in\mathbb{N}
, say in
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{d}
, when they undergo pairwise coagulation with certain coagulation rate
\alpha(m,n)
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{m}(t,x)=\Delta f_{m}(t,x)+

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\alpha(n,m-n)f_{n}(t,x)f_{m-n}(t,x)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha(m,n)f_{m}(t,x)f_{n}(t,x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,m\in\mathbb{N}.

The nonlinearity has two parts, a gain term and a loss term. Such a system of equations has been derived from scaling limits of Brownian particle systems by Hammond-Rezakhanlou
[
11
,
10
]
. To model the influence of a large-scale turbulent flow, it is natural to introduce a common noise. If we adopt a transport noise of the type
in
[
6
]

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{W}}(t,x)=\sum_{k\in K}\sigma_{k}(x)\dot{W}_{t}^{k}

where
\{\sigma_{k}(x)\}_{k\in K}
is a countable collection of divergence-free smooth vector fields and
\{W_{t}^{k}\}_{k\in K}
independent one-dimensional Brownian motions, then we get a stochastic version of Smoluchowski’s equation (an

SPDE

)

(1.1)

\displaystyle df_{m}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\Delta f_{m}(t,x)dt+\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\alpha(n,m-n)f_{n}(t,x)f_{m-n}(t,x)dt

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha(m,n)f_{m}(t,x)f_{n}(t,x)dt-\sum_{k\in K}\nabla f_{m}(t,x)\cdot\sigma_{k}(x)\,dW_{t}^{k}

\displaystyle+\text{div}\left(\mathcal{Q}(x,x)\nabla f_{m}(t,x)\right),\quad m\in\mathbb{N}

where
\mathcal{Q}(x,x):=\sum_{k\in K}\sigma_{k}(x)\otimes\sigma_{k}(x)
coming from the transport-type noise. Under specific choice of
\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k\in K}
, we can have that
\mathcal{Q}(x,x)\equiv\kappa I_{d}
, for an enhanced diffusion coefficient
\kappa>0
, the so-called “eddy diffusion”
[
1
]
. This picture, in its special case of finitely many mass levels
m=1,2,...,M

and unit coagulation rate
\alpha(m,n)\equiv 1
, has been derived from particle systems in
[
4
]
. Another version of the

SPDE

with continuous mass variables
m\in\mathbb{R}
has also been derived from particle systems with mean-field interactions in
[
15
]
.

However, conceptually and mathematically, the most difficult step
in this program
is to verify
that diffusion enhancement leads to
coagulation enhancement, namely, the fast increase of probability densities
f_{m}
for
m\gg 1
(large masses) for large diffusion coefficient case. In fact, the model (
1.1
) turns out to be too crude, and even numerically we cannot verify a coagulation enhancement.

The problem lies in the fact that quick diffusion of masses may not lead to enhanced collision unless the coagulation rate depends on the velocity variable. Otherwise, the masses merely move around.
We introduce a new system with both position and velocity variables. In the atmospheric physics literature, e.g.
[
3
,
16
,
18
,
9
]
, it is also common to consider cloud particles coagulate with a rate that is proportional to (when
d=3
)

\displaystyle\alpha(m_{1},m_{2}):=|v_{1}-v_{2}|(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}

where
v_{i}
,
i=1,2
are the velocities of two colliding rain droplets and
r_{i}:=m_{i}^{1/3}
,
i=1,2
their respective radius. Under certain simplifications, it leads to the following kinetic version of Smoluchowski’s equation (cf. Appendix
A
)

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{m}(t,x,v)=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{m}+c(m)\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}\right)+\kappa c(m)^{2}\Delta_{v}f_{m}+Q_{m}(f,f)\\[5.0pt]
f_{m}|_{t=0}=f^{0}_{m}(x,v),\quad m=1,...,M,\end{cases}

where
(t,x,v)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

(1.3)

\displaystyle Q_{m}(f,f)(t,x,v)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint_{\{nw^{\prime}+(m-n)w=mv\}}s(n,m-n)f_{n}(t,x,w^{\prime})f_{m-n}(t,x,w)|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int s(n,m)f_{m}(t,x,v)f_{n}(t,x,w)|v-w|dw,

where

(1.4)

\displaystyle c(m):=\alpha m^{(1-d)/d},\quad s(n,m):=(n^{1/d}+m^{1/d})^{d-1}.

In Appendix
A
, we sketch the proof of the scaling limit from a coagulating microscopic particle system subjected to a common noise, to an

SPDE

that eventually gives rise to this

PDE

. Although it is not fully rigorous, it should justify the interest of this equation. Here again, turbulence contributes to large
\kappa
versus small
\kappa
when no turbulence. The eddy diffusion occurs now in the velocity variable.

The aim of our research is two-fold. Theoretically, we are interested in proving the well-posedness of the

PDE

and associated

SPDE

cf. (
A.6
), as well as the passage from the one to the other. Then, both theoretically and numerically we aim to demonstrate that the larger the
\kappa
, i.e. the more intense is the turbulence, the faster masses coagulate. See
[
5
]
for a first numerical study in this direction. In the present article, we focus on the

PDE

system (
1.2
) in the spatially-homogeneous case, i.e. by considering the initial conditions
f_{0}^{m}
constant in
x
for every
m
, we can reduce (
1.2
) to

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{m}(t,v)=c(m)\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}(t,v)\right)+\kappa c(m)^{2}\Delta_{v}f_{m}(t,v)+Q_{m}(f,f)(t,v)\\[5.0pt]
f_{m}|_{t=0}=f^{0}_{m}(v),\quad m=1,...,M,\end{cases}

where
(t,v)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Q_{m}(f,f)
is as in (
1.3
) but without the
x
-dependence, and we
prove existence, uniqueness and regularity of the solutions of (
1.5
), for every fixed
\kappa>0
.

Denote a weighted
L^{p}
space

(1.6)

\displaystyle L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ \text{ s.t. }\ f\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\},\quad p\in[1,\infty],\,k\in\mathbb{N},

where

\left\langle v\right\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}

and a weighted Sobolev space

(1.7)

\displaystyle H^{n}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f\in L^{2}_{k}\ \text{ s.t. }\nabla^{\ell}f\in L^{2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;\forall 1\leq\ell\leq n\right\}.

The main result of this article is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix any finite
T
and
\kappa>0
.
Suppose that initial conditions
f_{m}^{0}(v)\in(L^{2}\cap L^{1}_{2})(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and nonnegative, for every
m=1,...,M
, then there exists at least one nonnegative solution in the class
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)^{\otimes M}
.

If the initial conditions
f_{m}^{0}(v)\in(H^{1}_{1}\cap L^{1}_{2})(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and nonnegative, then there exists a unique nonnegative solution, and in this case
f_{m}(t)\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
t>0
.

The most difficult part in our opinion is uniqueness, due mainly to the presence of
|w-w^{\prime}|
in the nonlinear term, and the fact that the velocity variable
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is unbounded, hence in the presence of Laplacian,
f_{m}(t,v)
is never compactly supported even if starting with so. These together with the fact that we have a system rather than just one equation, cause a severe difficulty in closing a Gronwall inequality for uniqueness. As far as we are able, the weighted
L^{1}
space is the only one in which a Gronwall argument can work, even if one is willing to assume that solutions are Schwartz functions. (The problem is related to integrability rather than smoothness.) Indeed, with weighted
L^{1}
we can find certain cancellations that remove those terms with higher weights brought by the kernel, and this seems not achievable with other spaces such as weighted
L^{2}
. Equally essential to this cancellation is considering the sum over the norms of all the densities
f_{m}
,
m=1,..,M
, rather than treating them individually. Indeed, this is already essential to derive various apriori estimates.

On the other hand, existence is proved by constructing a family of approximating equations each corresponding to a truncation of the kernel
|w-w^{\prime}|
in the nonlinearity. These approximating problems are more amenable to study since the difficulty related to the kernel is no longer severe, for each fixed truncation parameter.

There is an unexpected connection to the vast area of Boltzmann equations
[
21
]
. Our (
1.2
) may be viewed as a Boltzmann-type equation with perfectly inelastic collision, rather than the classical elastic collision. Indeed, it is derived from particles undergoing pairwise coagulation, hence two particles merge into one based on the principle of conservation of momentum (and not energy). It is also local in nature in that the nonlinearity acts on the velocity variable, per
(t,x)
. The closest works in the Boltzmann literature seem to be the ones on excited granular media, see
[
8
]
and references therein, and on multi-species Boltzmann equations, see
[
2
]
and references therein. In a sense our equation combines the features of both of them. From a technical point of view, the aforementioned difficulty with uniqueness are also present in
[
14
,
8
]
and some references therein, and we have learned from these sources. On the other hand, there are various differences that set our model apart from these references. Since
M<\infty
, we do not have the conservation of mass and momentum, and we do not expect nontrivial stationary solutions – indeed all
f_{m}(t)
should decay to zero as
t\to\infty
(i.e. eventually all masses are transferred out of the system). In general, our nonliearity
Q_{m}(f,f)
does not enjoy any particular kind of symmetry. Our derivation of the apriori estimates is also quite different, in particular we need not invoke entropy estimates and Povzner-type inequalities, as are standard in the Boltzmann literature.

Without loss of generality and simplifying notations, in the main part of the paper, we set
\kappa=1
,
c(m)=1
,
s(n,m)=1
.

2.
Deterministic Smoluchowski system with Velocity

2.1.
Non-local Smoluchowski Equations

Recall our system of nonlinear integral-differential equations for
\{f_{m}(t,x,v)\}_{m=1}^{M}
defined as:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{m}=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{m}+\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}\right)+\Delta_{v}f_{m}+Q_{m}(f,f)\\[5.0pt]
f_{m}|_{t=0}=f^{0}_{m}\end{cases},\ \quad m=1,...,M

where
(t,x,v)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The nonlinear term
Q_{m}(f,f)
, representing the masses interactions is given by (
1.3
).
Since we expect the system to represent density of particles with different masses we take
f^{0}_{m}\geq 0,\forall\ m=1,...,M
.

We focus on the case when the initial condition is constant in the space variable
x
, and so this property pass to the solution of
(\ref{main-eq})
. We get a new set of equations solved by
f_{m}(t,v),m=1,...,M
, where we omit the subscript
v
in the derivatives in the sequel (since there is no ambiguity)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{m}=\text{div}\left(vf_{m}\right)+\Delta f_{m}+Q_{m}(f,f)\\[5.0pt]
f_{m}|_{t=0}=f^{0}_{m}\end{cases},\quad m=1,...,M

where
(t,v)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f^{0}_{m}\geq 0,\ \forall\ m
.
By a change of variables

w^{\prime}=\varphi(v,w):=\frac{mv-(m-n)w}{n},

the nonlinear term can be written as:

\displaystyle Q_{m}(f,f)(t,v)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint_{\{nw^{\prime}+(m-n)w=mv\}}f_{n}(t,w^{\prime})f_{m-n}(t,w)|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dw

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\int f_{n}\left(t,\varphi(v,w)\right)f_{m-n}(t,w)\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{2}|v-w|dw

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dw.

We will consider the following notion of weak solutions to (
2.2
).

Definition 2.1

(
L^{\infty}_{t}L_{v}^{1}
-weak solution)
.

Fix
T>0
, and
f^{0}_{m}\in L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{2}
. A solution for equation
(\ref{main-eq1})
is a set of functions
f_{m}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
indexed by the masses
m=1,...,M
such that
\forall m,\ Q_{m}(f,f)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and the following holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\langle f_{m}(t),\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\langle f_{m}^{0},\phi\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\langle f_{m}(s),\Delta\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\langle f_{m}(s),v\cdot\nabla\phi\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\langle Q_{m}(f(s),f(s)),\phi\right\rangle ds,

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, and
\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.2.
A priori estimate and conserved quantities for system
(\ref{main-eq1})

We prove for the system with finite number of masses a decay in time of the total mass and the conservation of such quantity in the infinite system.

Our first result is valid even in the system with space variable, hence we state it more generally.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
f_{m}^{0}(x,v)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\{f_{m}(t)\}_{m=1}^{M}
is a nonnegative solution of the equation
(\ref{main-eq})
, such that
f_{m}(t)
and its gradient
\nabla f_{m}(t)
have a fast decay at infinity. Then if
M<\infty
, then the quantity

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(t):=\iint\sum_{m=1}^{M}m\,f_{m}(t,x,v)dxdv

is non-increasing in
t
; if
M=\infty
, then it is constant in
t
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is not clear how to relate the finite- and infinite-level systems. We only work with
M<\infty
. Since
t\mapsto\mathcal{T}(t)
is decreasing in this case, we interpret
\mathcal{T}(0)-\mathcal{T}(t)
as the amount of mass falling out of the system. It can be viewed as an indicator of how efficient the coagulation is (by transferring densities from small masses to large masses, see
[
5
]
).

Proof.

Consider first the nonlinear part. For every fixed
t,x
we have that

\displaystyle\int\sum_{m=1}^{M}m\,Q_{m}(f,f)dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}m\int dv\iint_{\{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}=mv\}}f_{n}(t,x,w)f_{m-n}(t,x,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}m\iint f_{m}(t,x,v)f_{n}(t,x,w)|v-w|dwdv

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}m\iint f_{n}(t,x,w)f_{m-n}(t,x,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}m\iint f_{m}(t,x,v)f_{n}(t,x,w)|v-w|dwdv

Exchanging the order of summations in the first term, and setting
k=m-n
, the above equals

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{M-n}(k+n)\iint f_{n}(t,x,w)f_{k}(t,x,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}m\iint f_{m}(t,x,v)f_{n}(t,x,w)|v-w|dwdv

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{M}(k+n)\iint f_{n}(t,x,w)f_{k}(t,x,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}m\iint f_{m}(t,x,v)f_{n}(t,x,w)|v-w|dwdv=0

where equality is achieved if and only if
M=\infty
. For the linear part, we note that for every
m,t,x
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}+\nabla_{v}f_{m}\right)dv=0

due to integration by parts and
|vf_{m}|+|\nabla_{v}f_{m}|\to 0
as
|v|\to\infty
for every fixed
t,x
. Also, for every
m,t,v
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{m}dx=0

due to integration by parts and
|f_{m}|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
for every fixed
t,v
. Thus we can conclude since
\mathcal{T}(t)
(
2.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{m=1}^{M}m\;\partial_{t}f_{m}(t,x,v)dxdv=-\sum_{m=1}^{M}m\iint v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{m}dxdv

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{M}m\iint\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}+\nabla_{v}f_{m}\right)dvdx+\iint\sum_{m=1}^{M}m\,Q_{m}(f,f)dvdx\leq 0

with equality if and only if
M=\infty
.
∎

We specialize now to consider the spatially-homogeneous case, i.e.
f_{m}(t,x,v)=f_{m}(t,v)
independent of
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Recall the weighted
L^{p}
spaces
L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(
1.6
)
and we will indicate by
L^{p}_{k,M}
the space in which the sequence
\{f_{m}(t)\}_{m=1}^{M}
lives, with norm given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,M,k}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f_{m}\|_{p,k}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\int|f_{m}(v)|^{p}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{pk}dv\right)^{1/p}.

The reason we are going to introduce such spaces is motivated not only to understand the regularity of the solution of the system in study, but also to deal with the terms
|v-w|
in the coagulation kernel since, as we have already noted in the explicit formulation of the nonlinearity
Q_{m}(f,f)
.

There are a few ways to control the norm of such a quantity, and we prove here the main estimates that we apply in all the a priori estimates for the equation.

Proposition 2.4
.

For every
p\in[1,\infty]
and
k\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
p,k,M
and the dimension
d
, such that we have the following bound on the nonlinearity

\|Q_{m}(g,f)\|_{p,k}\leq C\left(\|g\|_{p,M,k+1}\|f\|_{1,M,k+1}\right)

and more general

\|Q(g,f)\|_{p,M,k}\leq MC\left(\|g\|_{p,M,k+1}\|f\|_{1,M,k+1}\right).

Proof.

We prove this with a simple computation, using the duality of the norm for
L^{p}
spaces. For simplicity of notation during the proof we omit whenever is possible the appendix
M
on the norm. Fix
m\in\{1,...,M\}
and consider
Q_{m}(g,f)
for
(g_{n})_{n=1,...,M}
and
(f_{n})_{n=1,...,M}
:

\displaystyle\|Q_{m}(g,f)\|_{p,k}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in L^{p^{\prime}},\ \|\phi\|_{p^{\prime}}=1}\int Q_{m}(g,f)\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}\phi(v)dv,

where
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. We now work directly on each term of the summation in the nonlinearity both in the positive and negative part. For the negative part we have:

\displaystyle\iint g_{m}(v)f_{n}(w)|v-w|\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}\phi(v)dwdv

\displaystyle\leq\iint g_{m}(v)f_{n}(w)\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k+1}\left\langle w\right\rangle\phi(v)dwdv

\displaystyle=\|f_{n}||_{1,1}\int g_{m}(v)\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k+1}\phi(v)dv

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq}_{\|\phi\|_{p^{\prime}}=1}\|g_{m}\|_{p,k+1}\|f_{n}||_{1,1}\leq\|g\|_{p,k+1}\|f\|_{1,k+1}.

And now for the positive:

\displaystyle\iint c_{m}^{n}g_{n}(h(v,w))f_{m-n}(w)|v-w|\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}\phi(v)dwdv

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint g_{n}(h)f_{m-n}(w)\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k+1}\left\langle w\right\rangle\phi(v)dwdv

\displaystyle\underbrace{=}_{h(v,w)=z}\iint g_{n}(z)f_{m-n}(w)\left\langle\tilde{h}(z,w)\right\rangle^{k+1}\left\langle w\right\rangle\phi(\tilde{h})dwdv

\displaystyle\leq\iint g_{n}(z)f_{m-n}(w)(\left\langle z\right\rangle^{k+1}\left\langle w\right\rangle+\left\langle w\right\rangle^{k+1})\phi(\tilde{h})dwdv

\displaystyle=\iint g_{n}(z)f_{m-n}(w)\left\langle z\right\rangle^{k+1}\left\langle w\right\rangle\phi(\tilde{h})dwdv+\iint g_{n}(z)f_{m-n}(w)\left\langle w\right\rangle^{k+1}\phi(\tilde{h})dwdv

\displaystyle=\int f_{m-n}(w)\int g_{n}(z)\left\langle z\right\rangle^{k+1}\phi(\tilde{h})dv\left\langle w\right\rangle dv+\int f_{m-n}(w)\left\langle w\right\rangle^{k+1}\int g_{n}(z)\phi(\tilde{h})dvdw

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq}_{\text{H\"{o}lder\ inequality}}\int f_{m-n}(w)\|\left\langle w\right\rangle\|g_{n}\|_{p,k+1}dv+\int f_{m-n}(w)\left\langle w\right\rangle^{k+1}\|g_{n}\|_{p}dw

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq}_{\text{Translation\ invariance}}2\|g_{n}\|_{p,k+1}\|f_{m-n}||_{1,k+1}\leq 2\|g\|_{p,k+1}\|f\|_{1,k+1}.

Putting everything together we conclude the proof.
∎

We begin now proving the a priori estimates for the standard moments of regular enough solution to the system of coagulation equations.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that
f_{m}^{0}\in L^{1}_{\ell}
and
\{f_{m}(t)\}_{m=1}^{M}
is a nonnegative solution of the equation
(\ref{main-eq1})
, such that
f_{m}(t)
and its gradient
\nabla f_{m}(t)
have a fast decay at infinity. Then, there exists some finite constant
C_{\ell}
depending only on
\ell,d,
and the initial data
\{f_{m}(0)\}_{m=1}^{M}
such that for any
t\geq 0
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{\ell}f_{m}(t,v)dv\leq C_{\ell}.

Proof.

We first consider
\ell=2k
even, and prove by induction on
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The case
k=0
follows from Lemma
2.2
. Now assume
k\geq 1
and assume (
2.4
) is proved for the case
\ell=2(k-1)
. Note that for each
m=1,...,M
, by (
2.2
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)dv=\int|v|^{2k}\partial_{t}f_{m}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int|v|^{2k}\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}\right)dv+\int|v|^{2k}\Delta_{v}f_{m}dv

\displaystyle+\int dv\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint_{\{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}=mv\}}|v|^{2k}f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\int dv\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2k\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}dv+2k(2k+d-2)\int|v|^{2k-2}f_{m}dv

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint\left|\frac{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}}{m}\right|^{2k}f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dwdv.

Above, we assumed that for every
t>0
,

\displaystyle|v|^{2k+1}|f_{m}|\to 0,\quad|v|^{2k}|\nabla_{v}f_{m}|\to 0,\quad\text{ as }|v|\to\infty

such that the boundary terms vanish in the integration by parts. Summing the above in
m=1,...,M
and noticing by Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\left|\frac{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}}{m}\right|^{2k}\leq\left(\frac{n|w|+(m-n)|w^{\prime}|}{m}\right)^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{m}|w|^{2k}+\frac{m-n}{m}|w^{\prime}|^{2k}\leq|w|^{2k}+|w^{\prime}|^{2k},

whenever
1\leq n\leq m-1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle\leq-2k\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)dv+2k(2k+d-2)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k-2}f_{m}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint\left(|w|^{2k}+|w^{\prime}|^{2k}\right)f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dwdv

\displaystyle\leq-2k\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)dv+2k(2k+d-2)C_{2(k-1)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{m=n+1}^{M}\iint\left(|w|^{2k}+|w^{\prime}|^{2k}\right)f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dwdv,

where we used the induction hypothesis that for some finite constant
C_{2(k-1)}
independent of
t
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k-2}f_{m}(t,v)dv\leq C_{2(k-1)}.

Further, setting
\ell=m-n
we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{m=n+1}^{M}\iint\left(|w|^{2k}+|w^{\prime}|^{2k}\right)f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dwdv

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M-n}\iint\left(|w|^{2k}+|w^{\prime}|^{2k}\right)f_{n}(t,w)f_{\ell}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dwdv\;\leq 0.

Thus we conclude that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)dv\leq

\displaystyle-2k\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)dv+2k(2k+d-2)C_{2(k-1)}.

Assuming the initial data is such that

\displaystyle A_{0}(k):=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(0,v)dv

is finite, it follows that for any
t\geq 0
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}(k)e^{-2kt}+(2k+d-2)C_{k-1}(1-e^{-2kt})

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}(k)+(2k+d-2)C_{2(k-1)}=:C_{2k}.

This completes the induction for
n=2k
even. Finally, we turn to the case
n=2k-1
for
k\geq 1
. Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k-1}f_{m}(v)dv

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{|v|\leq 1}|v|^{2k-1}f_{m}(v)dv+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{|v|>1}|v|^{2k-1}f_{m}(v)dv

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f_{m}(v)dv+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(v)dv\leq C_{0}+C_{2k}.

∎

Remark 2.6
.

Call
A_{k}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}A_{k}^{m}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}dv
and consider
\int Q(f,f)\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}dv
. Although we have used other properties, this quantity can be also expanded in the following way:

\displaystyle\int Q(f,f)\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}dv\lesssim-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=M-n+1}^{M}\iint f_{m}(v)f_{n}(w)|v-w|\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}dwdv

Using Proposition
2.4
and the modified triangular inequality:
-2|v-w|\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}\leq-2\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k+1}+2|w|\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\lesssim-2\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=M-n+1}^{M}\iint f_{m}(v)f_{n}(w)|v-w|\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}dwdv\leq 2A_{1}A_{k}-2A_{k+1}C_{k}^{M}

where
C_{k}^{M}:=\inf_{t}\int f_{M}(w)dw\geq 0
. This give us an equation for
A_{k}
of the form:

\frac{d}{dt}A_{k}\leq-2C_{k}^{M}A_{k+1}+C_{k}A_{k}(A_{0}+A_{1})+C_{k,k-1}A_{k-1})

Using the already proved bound on
A_{k}
and the aforementioned equation, integrating in time, is enough to show that moments of order
k\geq 2
are controlled for
t_{0}>0
, however small, even if
f_{0}
has not bounded moments of higher order.

We can prove also an a priori bound in
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{v}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}
of solutions of (
2.2
), under regularity assumption.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
f_{m}^{0}\in L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{2}
and
\{f_{m}(t)\}_{m=1}^{M}
is a nonnegative solution of the equation
(\ref{main-eq1})
, such that
f_{m}(t)
and its gradient
\nabla f_{m}(t)
have a fast decay at infinity. There exists some constant
C>0
depending on the initial data
\{f_{m}(0),\nabla f_{m}(0)\}_{m=1}^{M}
,
T<\infty
,
M<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv+\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla f_{m}(s,v)|^{2}dv\ ds\right)\leq C.

Proof.

Note that for each
m=1,...,M
, by (
2.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv=\int f_{m}(t,v)\partial_{t}f_{m}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle=\int f_{m}\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}\right)dv+\int f_{m}\Delta_{v}f_{m}dv

\displaystyle+\int dv\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint_{\{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}=mv\}}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\int dv\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int f_{m}(t,v)f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dw

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}\int f_{m}^{2}dv-\int|\nabla f_{m}|^{2}dv

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint f^{2}_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dwdv.

Here, we assumed that
|v\nabla_{v}f_{m}|\to 0
, as
|v|\to\infty
for every
t
such that the boundary terms vanish in the integration by parts.

Further, we see that each term on the positive nonlinearity can be written using the Young’s inequality has (same for
v
or
w
variable):

\displaystyle\iint f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)f_{m-n}(t,w)|v|dvdw=

\displaystyle\leq\int\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\int f_{m}\Big(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\Big)f_{n}(t,v)|v|dv\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f_{m-n}(t,w)^{2}\right]dw

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2}\left(\int f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|dv\right)^{2}dw+\frac{1}{2}\|f_{m-n}(t,w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Thus we need to take care of the squared factor and we can do this using Jensen and normalizing respect the measure
(\int f_{n}(t,v)dv)^{-1}f_{n}(t,v)dv
, so that we obtain:

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{2}\left(\int f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|dv\right)^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}\int\int f_{m}^{2}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)|v|^{2}f_{n}(t,v)dvdw

\displaystyle=(A)

We have then, using translation invariance of Lebesgue measure:

\displaystyle(A)=\frac{1}{2}C_{0}\int\left(\int f_{m}^{2}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)dw\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\int|v|^{2}f_{n}(t,v)dv\leq C_{1}\|f_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

where the constant
C_{1}:=C_{1}(M)
depend also on
M
, using the moment bound Lemma
2.5
with
\ell=2
, since we assumed that
f_{m}^{0}\in L^{1}_{2}
.

We can repeat the same argument for the
|w|
part. Thus we conclude that, erasing the negative term of the nonlinearity,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|\nabla f_{m}(t,v)|^{2}dv\leq C(M,0,1,A)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv

where
C(M,0,1,A)>0
, and assuming the initial data is such that

\displaystyle C_{1}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2}f_{m}(0,v)dv,\;C_{0}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f_{m}(0,v)dv,\;C_{l}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(0,v)dv<\infty.

It follows that for any
t\in[0,T]
we pass to integral form

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv+\kappa c(m)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\ \sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|\nabla f_{m}(s,v)|^{2}dv\ ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{l}+C(M,0,1,A)\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(s,v)dvds

\displaystyle\leq C_{l}+C(M,0,1,A)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(s,v)dv+\int_{0}^{s}\ \sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|\nabla f_{m}(r,v)|^{2}dv\ dr\right)ds

From Gronwall’s lemma, it follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv+\int_{0}^{t}\ \sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|\nabla f_{m}(s,v)|^{2}dv\ ds\right)\leq C_{l}\ e^{TC_{M,0,A,1}}\leq C(T)<\infty.

∎

Remark 2.8
.

Consider the constant in the previous lemma on the right hand side of the Gronwall inequality and consider
C_{T}:=\sup_{t\leq T}C_{t}<\infty
. Then from the lemma we can recover the following:

\partial_{t}\left(e^{-C_{T}t}\|f_{t}\|_{2,M}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C_{T}t}\|f_{s}\|_{H^{1},M}^{2}ds\leq 0

using Gronwall’ lemma and using the property of sup and exponential, we have that exists
C:=C_{T}e^{C_{T}T}<\infty
depending only on the initial condition
f_{0}
and not his gradient, such that:

\sup_{t\leq T}\|f_{t}\|_{2,M}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{s}\|_{H^{1},M}^{2}ds\leq C.

∎

Using the already proven a priori estimate and the bound on the nonlinearity we can also show an a priori bound on the solution in the weighted space
L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2}_{k})_{v}\cap L^{2}_{t}(H^{1}_{k})_{v}
, noting also that
|v|^{k}\leq\langle v\rangle^{k}\leq 2^{k-1}(1+|v|^{k})
.

Lemma 2.9
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that
f^{0}_{m}\in L^{1}_{4k+2}\cap L^{2}_{k}
and
\{f_{m}(t)\}_{m=1}^{M}
is a nonnegative solution of the equation
(\ref{main-eq1})
, such that
f_{m}(t)
and its gradient
\nabla f_{m}(t)
have a fast decay at infinity. Then there exists some constant
C>0
depending on the initial data
\{f_{m}(0),\nabla f_{m}(0)\}_{m=1}^{M}
,
T<\infty
,
M<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)|v|^{2k}dv+\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla f_{m}(s,v)|^{2}|v|^{2k}dv\ ds\right)\leq C.

Proof.

We perform induction on
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The case
k=0
is proved in Lemma
2.7
. Suppose that the thesis has been proved for the case
k-1
. Then, for each
m=1,...,M
, by (
2.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv=\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)\partial_{t}f_{m}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle=\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}\text{div}_{v}\left(vf_{m}\right)dv+\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}\Delta_{v}f_{m}dv

\displaystyle+\int dv\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint_{\{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}=mv\}}|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\int dv\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}(t,v)f_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dw

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{d}{2}-k)\int|v|^{2k}f_{m}^{2}dv-\frac{1}{2}\int|v|^{2k}|\nabla f_{m}|^{2}dv+2k^{2}\int|v|^{2k-2}f_{m}^{2}dv

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\iint\left|\frac{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}}{m}\right|^{2k}f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,w)f_{m-n}(t,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}|dwdw^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(t,v)f_{n}(t,w)|v-w|dwdv,

where we used Young’s inequality

\displaystyle-2k\int|v|^{2k-2}v\cdot\nabla f_{m}f_{m}dv

\displaystyle\leq 2k\int|v|^{2k-1}|\nabla f_{m}|f_{m}dv

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int|v|^{2k}|\nabla f_{m}|^{2}dv+2k^{2}\int|v|^{2k-2}f_{m}^{2}dv

where we assumed that
|v|^{2k}|f_{m}\nabla f_{m}|\to 0
,
|v|^{2k+1}|f_{m}|^{2}\to 0
, as
|v|\to\infty
for every
t
such that the boundary terms vanish in the integration by parts.

Further, since

\displaystyle\left|\frac{nw+(m-n)w^{\prime}}{m}\right|^{2k}(|w|+|w^{\prime}|)\leq|w|^{2k+1}+|w^{\prime}|^{2k+1}+|w|^{2k}|w^{\prime}|+|w^{\prime}|^{2k}|w|,

we see that each term on the positive nonlinearity can be written using the Young’s inequality has (same for
v
or
w
variable):

\displaystyle\iint f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)f_{m-n}(t,w)|v|^{2k+1}dvdw

\displaystyle\leq\int\left[\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{2k+1}dv\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{m-n}(t,w)\Big)^{2}\right]dw

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2}\left(\int f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{2k+1}dv\right)^{2}dw+\frac{1}{2}\|f_{m-n}(t,w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Thus we need to take care of the squared factor and we can do this using Jensen and normalizing respect the measure
(\int f_{n}(t,v)dv)^{-1}f_{n}(t,v)dv
, so that we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{2k+1}dv\right)^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}\int\int f_{m}^{2}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)|v|^{4k+2}f_{n}(t,v)dvdw

\displaystyle=(A)

We have then, using translation invariance of Lebesgue measure:

\displaystyle(A)=\frac{1}{2}C_{0}\int\left(\int f_{m}^{2}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)dw\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{4k+2}dv

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|f_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\int|v|^{4k+2}f_{n}(t,v)dv\leq C_{1}\|f_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

where the constant
C_{1}:=C_{1}(M)
depend also on
M
and comes from the momentum bound of
f_{m}
, Lemma
2.5
since we assumed that
f_{m}^{0}\in L^{1}_{4k+2}
.

Similarly, we have another term

\displaystyle\iint f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)f_{m-n}(t,w)|v|^{2k}|w|dw

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{1}{2}\left(\int f_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{2k}dv\right)^{2}dw+\int\frac{1}{2}f^{2}_{m-n}(t,w)|w|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int\frac{1}{2}\int f^{2}_{m}\left(t,\frac{nv+(m-n)w}{m}\right)f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{4k}dvdw+\int\frac{1}{2}f^{2}_{m-n}(t,w)|w|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}||f_{m}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\int f_{n}(t,v)|v|^{4k}dv+\int\frac{1}{2}f^{2}_{m-n}(t,w)|w|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}||f_{m}||_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int\frac{1}{2}f^{2}_{m-n}(t,w)|w|^{2}dw.

Thus we conclude that, erasing the negative term of the nonlinearity and summing over
n=1,...,m-1
, using the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}|\nabla f_{m}(t,v)|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq C(M,0,1,A)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f^{2}_{m}(t,v)|v|^{2k}dv+C_{B},

where
C(M,0,1,A),C_{B}>0
, and assuming the initial data is such that

\displaystyle C_{1}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{4k+2}f_{m}(0,v)dv,\;C_{0}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int f_{m}(0,v)dv,\;C_{l}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(0,v)dv<\infty

It follows that for any
t\in[0,T]
we pass to integral form

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\ \sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}|\nabla f_{m}(s,v)|^{2}dv\ ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{l,B}+C(M,0,1,A)\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(s,v)dvds

\displaystyle\leq C_{l,B}+C(M,0,1,A)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(s,v)dv+\int_{0}^{s}\ \sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}|\nabla f_{m}(r,v)|^{2}dv\ dr\right)ds

From Gronwall’s lemma, it follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}f^{2}_{m}(t,v)dv+\int_{0}^{t}\ \sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|v|^{2k}|\nabla f_{m}(s,v)|^{2}dv\ ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{l,B}\ e^{TC_{M,0,A,1}}\leq C(T)<\infty.

∎

We want to extend the inequality to higher derivatives and higher moments, to this end we consider the following proposition regarding the nonlinearity
Q
:

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
f,g
smooth and rapidly decaying function in
v
at infinity, then

\displaystyle\nabla Q_{m}(f,g)=Q_{m}(c_{m}^{n}\nabla f,g)+Q_{m}(f,c_{m}^{n}\nabla g),

for each
m=1,..,M
.

Proof.

First of all we split the nonlinearity into the positive and negative part and we change variables to get

\displaystyle Q^{+}_{m}(f,g)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\int\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{2}f_{n}(\varphi(v,w))g_{m-n}(w)|v-w|dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\int\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{2}f_{n}(\tilde{\varphi}(v,w))g_{m-n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw

\displaystyle Q^{-}_{m}(f,g)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{M}f_{m}(v)\int g_{n}(w)|v-w|dw=\sum_{n=1}^{M}f_{m}(v)\int g_{n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw,

where we have set

\varphi(v,w):=\frac{mv-(m-n)w}{n},\quad\theta(v,w):=v+w,\quad\tilde{\varphi}(v,w):=v-\frac{m-n}{n}w.

Whit this we can use the differentiation under integral sign and since the dependence on
v
is only on
f
and
g
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\left(\int f_{n}(\tilde{\varphi}(v,w))g_{m-n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw\right)

\displaystyle=\int\left(\nabla_{v}f_{n}(\tilde{\varphi}(v,w))\right)g_{m-n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw+\int f_{n}(\tilde{\varphi}(v,w))\nabla_{v}g_{m-n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw.

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\left(f_{m}(v)\int g_{n}(w)|v-w|dw\right)=\nabla_{v}\left(f_{m}(v)\int g_{n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw\right)=

\displaystyle=\nabla_{v}f_{m}(v)\int g_{n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw+f_{m}(v)\int\nabla_{v}g_{n}(\theta(v,w))|w|dw.

Taking out the constant in the differentiation, depending only on
m
and
n
, and summing all together we conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

As a corollary, iterating Proposition
2.10
, higher-order derivatives of
Q
can be
calculated using the following formula:

\displaystyle\partial^{j}Q(f,g)=\sum_{0\leq l\leq j}\binom{j}{l}Q_{m}\left(c_{m,n}\partial^{j-l}f,c_{m,n}\partial^{l}g\right)

where
j
is a multi-index
|j|=n
such that
j=j_{1}...j_{d}
(with indices possibly being zeroes) and

\partial^{j}:=\partial_{v_{1}}^{j_{1}}...\partial^{j_{d}}_{v_{d}}.

The factor
\binom{j}{l}
is a multinomial coefficients and the sum is intended in the sense of ordering of multi-indices.

With this in mind we are able to state the following a priori estimate on the space
H^{n}_{k}
for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, defined in (
1.7
) equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{H_{k}^{n}}:=\left(\sum_{0\leq|j|\leq n}\|\partial^{j}f\|_{2,k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose that
\{f_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
is a nonnegative solution of the equation
(\ref{main-eq-trunc1})
, such that
f_{m}
is regular enough. For all
n\in\mathbb{N},\ k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some constant
C>0
depending on the initial data
\{f_{m}(0)\}_{m=1}^{M}
,
T<\infty
,
M<\infty
,
n,k
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f_{m}\|^{2}_{H^{n}_{k}}\right)\leq C.

Proof.

We work by induction. We know form previous lemma that
n=0,1
we have already proven the a priori bound. Now we suppose that for every
p<n
it’s true that
f\in H^{p}_{k}
for all
k
, and we prove that
f\in H^{n}_{k}
for all
k
. To this end, we consider a multi index
j,|j|=n
and the quantity
\partial^{j}f_{m}
. Using the equation for
f_{m}
we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\partial^{j}f_{m}\right)=\Delta\left(\partial^{j}f_{m}\right)+\div\left(v\partial^{j}f_{m}\right)+C_{j}\left(\partial^{j}f_{m}\right)+\sum_{0\leq l\leq j}\binom{j}{l}Q_{m}\left(\partial^{j-l}f,\partial^{l}f\right)

where
C_{j}
is either
0
or
1
depending if the multi index has the
i
-th component of the derivative of
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial^{j}\left(\partial_{i}f_{m}v_{i}\right)
. We now consider the quantity

\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv

and derivate in time we have for each
m
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int

\displaystyle\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}(t,v)dv=\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}\partial^{j}f_{m}(t,v)\partial_{t}\partial^{j}f_{m}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle=\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}\partial^{j}f_{m}\text{div}\left(v\partial^{j}f_{m}\right)dv+\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}\partial^{j}f_{m}\Delta(\partial^{j}f_{m})dv+

\displaystyle+\int C_{j}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv+\sum_{0\leq l\leq j}\int Q_{m}(\partial^{j-l}f,\partial^{l}f)\partial^{j}f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{2}\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv-k\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k-2}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv+

\displaystyle+k\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv-k\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k-2}|v||\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv+

\displaystyle-\int|\nabla(\partial^{j}f_{m})|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv+\sum_{0\leq l\leq j}\int Q_{m}(\partial^{j-l}f,\partial^{l}f)\partial^{j}f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{d,k}\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv-\int|\nabla(\partial^{j}f_{m})|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv+

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum_{0\leq l\leq j}\int Q_{m}(\partial^{j-l}f,\partial^{l}f)\partial^{j}f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv}_{A}.

We miss to understand now the nonlinearity. To this end we consider:

\displaystyle\|Q_{m}(f,g)\|_{2}\leq C\left(\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\|f_{m-n}\|_{2,k+1}\|g_{n}\|_{1,k+1}+\|f_{m}\|_{1,k+1}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\|g_{n}\|_{2,k+1}\right).

In particular we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\phi\|_{2}=1}\iint C_{m,n}f_{n}(h(v,w))f_{m-n}(w)|v-w|\phi(v)dvdw

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\phi\|_{2}=1}\iint C^{\prime}_{m,n}f_{n}(z))f_{m-n}(w)|z-w|\phi(h^{\prime}(z,w))dzdw

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}&\sup_{\|\phi\|_{2}=1}\int\int f_{m-n}(w)\phi(h^{\prime}(z,w))\left\langle w\right\rangle dwf_{n}(z))\left\langle z\right\rangle dz\leq C_{n,m}\|f_{n}\|_{2,1}\|f_{m-n}\|_{1,1}\\[5.0pt]
&\sup_{\|\phi\|_{2}=1}\int\int f_{n}(z))\phi(h^{\prime}(z,w))\left\langle z\right\rangle dzf_{m-n}(w)\left\langle w\right\rangle dw\leq C_{n,m}\|f_{m-n}\|_{2,1}\|f_{n}\|_{1,1}.\end{cases}

With this, in particular, we can select where to put the norm and the weight depending on the index of the derivative and we get summation of quantity that depends on lower order in
H^{p}_{k}
with
p<n
and a quantity depending on
\partial^{j}
.

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq l\leq j}\int

\displaystyle Q_{m}(\partial^{j-l}f,\partial^{l}f)\partial^{j}f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv\leq\int Q_{m}(\partial^{j}f,f)\partial^{j}f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv

\displaystyle+\int Q_{m}(f,\partial^{j}f)\partial^{j}f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv+\sum_{1\leq l\leq j-1}\int Q_{m}(\partial^{j-l}f,\partial^{l}f)\partial^{j}f_{m}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv

\displaystyle\leq C_{m,n}(k,M)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|\partial^{j}f_{m}\|^{2}_{2,k+\mu}.

Now we note that:

\displaystyle\|\partial^{j}f_{m}\|^{2}_{2,k+\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\nabla\partial^{j}f_{m}\|^{2}_{2}+c_{\delta}\|\partial^{j-1}f_{m}\|^{2}_{2,2(k+\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\nabla\partial^{j}f_{m}\|^{2}_{2,2k}+c_{\delta}\|\partial^{j-1}f_{m}\|^{2}_{2,2(k+\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\nabla\partial^{j}f_{m}\|^{2}_{2,2k}+C_{\delta,M,n-1}.

We put together all the estimate and we get:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}(t,v)dv-C^{\prime}_{M,d,k}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv\leq

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|\nabla(\partial^{j}f_{m})|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv+\delta C(k,M)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int|\nabla(\partial^{j}f_{m})|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}dv+C_{M}(n-1,k)

Taking
\delta
small enough, up to constant
C_{\delta}(M,k,n-1)
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle-C^{\prime}_{M,d,k}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2k}|\partial^{j}f_{m}|^{2}dv\leq C_{\delta}(M,k,n-1).

Thanks to Gronwall’s lemma we conclude the proof.
∎

With all this a priori estimates follow also estimates on the
L^{2}_{k}
norm of the solution. At the level of the a priori estimate, Lemmas
2.12
and
2.7
guarantee us that we have a bound for every

on the derivatives in

implying that after a arbitrary short time the derivatives

for any

and thus they propagate in time. On the level of a priori estimates the solution results to be immediately infinitely smooth in

and decay faster than any negative power of
|v|
at infinity.

Using the a priori regularity in

and the aforementioned propagation, we note that for every
t>0
and
m=1,...,M
,
f_{m}(t)
is in fact a Schwartz function for
\forall t
and this decay is enough to get that
\partial_{t}f\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

3.2.
Preliminary on semigroups

We want to construct mild solution for our approximated problems. With this in mind, we define the following operator on function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f:=\Delta f+v\cdot\nabla f,

and rewrite our system of equations as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{R}_{m}-\mathcal{L}f_{m}^{R}=df^{R}_{m}+Q^{R}_{m}(f^{R},f^{R})\\
f^{R}_{m}|_{t=0}=f^{0}_{m}\end{cases},\quad m=1,...,M

with the usual definition of space, time and initial condition.

We know,
[
13
]
, that endowed with its maximal domain

D_{p,max}(\mathcal{L}):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ \mathcal{L}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}

the operator
\mathcal{L}
is the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular we can characterize the domain of the operator as follow:

Lemma 3.5

(
[
13
, Theorem 1]
)
.

The domain
D_{p,max}(\mathcal{L})
of the generator of the semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
coincides with:

D_{p}(\mathcal{L}):=\left\{u\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ v\cdot\nabla u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}.

Fur such a semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, one can derive the following properties
[
13
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The operator
(\mathcal{L},D_{p,max}(\mathcal{L}))
generates a semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which satisfies the estimate

\|P_{t}f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}

for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 3.7
.

For every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
T>0
, the function
P(\cdot)f
belongs to
C((0,T],W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{1}((0,T],L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and satisfies the estimates for
t\in(0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|D^{2}P_{t}f\|_{p}\leq\frac{C_{T}}{t}\|f\|_{p},\ \|\nabla P_{t}f\|_{p}\leq\frac{C_{T}}{\sqrt{t}}\|f\|_{p}.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
T>0
and
g\in C([0,T],L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be given, and consider the mild solution

of the
Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{L}u=g\ &in\ [0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\

with

.

Thus, we can define the notion of mild solution for our system. For simplicity of notation we omit the dependence on

.

Definition 3.9

(

-mild solution for approximated problems)
.

We rewrite first our approximated problems as an evolution equation in

space

\displaystyle\dot{f}+\mathcal{L}f=df+Q(f,f),\ t>0\,\ f(0)=f^{0}.

Fix

-mild solution to

\displaystyle f(t)=P_{t}f^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\left(df(s)+Q(f(s),f(s))\right)ds,\ t\in[0,T].

3.3.
Aubin-Lions’ Theorem in full Space

Lemma 3.10

(Aubin-Lions weighted)
.

Consider
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the usual Sobolev space, and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},|v|^{2}dx)
, the weighted Lebesgue space containing functions
f
for which
\int|f|^{2}|v|^{2}dv<\infty
. Then

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},|v|^{2}dv)\subset\joinrel\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and the embedding is compact.
As a consequence for every
q,k\geq 1
we have that

L^{p}\left(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},|v|^{2}dv)\right)\cap W^{1,q}(0,T;H^{-k}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\subset\joinrel\subset L^{p}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

for
p\in[1,\infty]
and the embedding is compact.

Proof.

Call
X:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},|v|^{2}dv)
and consider
Y\subset X
a bounded set.

We claim that
\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists N>0
such that for all
f\in Y
:

\int_{B^{c}_{N}}f^{2}<\varepsilon/2,

Where
B^{c}_{N}
is the complement in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the ball of radius
N
and centered at the origin.
Otherwise, assume this is not the case, then for some
\varepsilon>0
, for every
N>0
there is some
f\in Y
such that
\|f\|_{L^{2}(B^{c}_{N})}\geq\varepsilon
.

This implies that
\|f\|_{X}\geq\|f^{2}|v|^{2}\|_{L^{1}(B^{c}_{N})}\geq N^{2}\varepsilon
so that the set
Y
is not bounded, and this contradicts the hypotheses. So we consider the ball
B_{N}
and we can apply Rellich-Kondrachov theorem to ensure the compact embedding to
L^{2}(B_{N})
.

Now we recall that compactness of a set
A\subset X
means that
\forall\varepsilon>0
we find
f_{1},...,f_{k}\in X
, where
k=k(\varepsilon)
, such that
A\subset\cup_{i=1}^{k}B(f_{i},\varepsilon)
.

This means that
\forall\varepsilon>0
we find functions
f_{1},...,f_{k}\in L^{2}(B_{N}),\ k=k(\varepsilon)
, such that
\forall f\in Y
we have some
f_{j}
such that

\|f-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(B_{N})}<\varepsilon/2

We recall then that
\|f\|_{L^{2}(B^{c}_{N})}<\varepsilon/2
, so we even have

\|f-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\varepsilon
,
which implies the compact embedding
Y\subset\joinrel\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The second part of the theorem now follows from the usual Aubin-Lions Theorem cf.
[
19
, Corollary 5]
, thus concluding the proof.
∎

4.
Existence and Uniqueness for the approximating problems

Theorem 4.1

(Existence and Uniqueness)
.

Consider
d\geq 1
. Given any initial value
f^{0}_{m}\in L^{2},\forall m=1,...,M
, problem
(\ref{main-eq-trunc1})
possesses a unique maximal
L^{2}
-mild solution
f:=f(\cdot;f^{0})
on
[0,T(f^{0}))
. The maximal interval is such that
[0,T(f^{0}))
is an open interval in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. In addition,

f\in L^{\infty}([0,T(f^{0})),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes M})\cap W_{loc}^{1,2}((0,T(f^{0})),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes M}).

In addition: if
T(f^{0})<\infty
, then

\sup_{T(f^{0})/2<t<T(f^{0})}\|f_{t}\|_{2}=\infty.

Proof.

Let
T_{0}>0
be arbitrary and define
X_{T}:=L^{\infty}([0,T],L_{M}^{2})
for
T\in(0,T_{0}]
, where we endowed the space with the usual sup norm in time and
L_{M}^{2}
norm in velocity as
\|f\|_{L^{2}_{M}}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f_{m}\|_{L^{2}}.

Consider
f^{0}\in L_{M}^{2}
then from Proposition
3.7
we know that
P_{t}f^{0}\in X_{T}
. From the same theorem it is implied that exist a constant
\kappa:=\kappa(T_{0})>0
such that
\|P_{t}f^{=}\|\leq e^{\kappa}(T_{0})t
; we show that this is enough to define a map
\Gamma
from
X_{T}\rightarrow X_{T}
, given by

\Gamma(f):=P_{\cdot}f^{0}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}P_{\cdot-s}(df-Q^{R}(f,f))ds,\ f\in X_{T}.

First, thanks to the estimate on the semigroup and the property of the truncated nonlinearity, we have that, as soon as
f\in X_{T}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\|Q^{R}_{m}(f,f)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{R}(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f_{m}\|_{2})^{2}<\infty.\\[5.0pt]
&\int_{0}^{\cdot}P_{\cdot-s}(df-Q^{R}(f,f))ds\in X_{T}.\end{cases}

In particular we obtain the first estimate from the following computation on each of the terms of the nonlinearity

\int\left|\int|v-w|_{R}f_{n}(h(v,w))f_{m-n}(w)dw\right|^{2}dv\leq 2R\int_{B_{R}}\|f_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\|f_{m-n}\|_{2}^{2}dv=2R|B_{R}|\|f_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\|f_{m-n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Now we notice that the function
\Gamma
also satisfies

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(f)-P_{\cdot}f^{0}\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}P_{\cdot-s}(df-Q^{R}(f,f))ds\right\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa T\left(d+C_{R}(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f_{m}\|_{2})\right)(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f_{m}\|_{2}),

for all
f\in X_{T}
. And we can prove the continuity of the map

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g)\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}P_{\cdot-s}(d(f-g)+\left(Q^{R}(g,g)-Q^{R}(f,f)\right)ds\right\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq T\left(d+C_{R}\|f\|_{X_{T}}+C_{R}\|g\|_{X_{T}}\right)\|f-g\|_{X_{T}},

for all
f,g\in X_{T}
.

If we find a way to restrict the mapping
\Gamma
to some closed subset, and prove that it is a contraction then, with a standard fix point argument, we conclude local existence and uniqueness for each approximating problems.

Consider to this extent the norm of the initial condition
\|P_{\cdot}f^{0}\|_{X_{T}}=\gamma
, we choose the ball in
X_{T}
, call it
B_{T}
, centered in
P_{\cdot}f^{0}
with radius
\gamma
. So that every function
f\in B_{T}
as norm
\|f\|_{X_{T}}\leq 2\gamma
. Then selecting

T<\min\left\{\frac{1}{d+4C_{R}\gamma},\frac{1}{\kappa(d+C_{R}2\gamma)}\right\}

The two inequality implies that the map
\Gamma
send function in
B_{T}
to itself and it is a contraction. Therefore there exist a unique
\tilde{f}\in B_{T}
such that
\Gamma(\tilde{f})=\tilde{f}
, that is,

\tilde{f}\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L_{M}^{2})\ \text{ and }\ \tilde{f}=P_{\cdot}f^{0}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}P_{\cdot-s}\left(d\tilde{f}+Q^{R}(\tilde{f},\tilde{f})\right)ds.

and
\tilde{f}
is a mild-
L^{2}
solution to the approximated problems.

Clearly we can extend the solution
\tilde{f}
to a unique maximal solution
\tilde{f}:=\tilde{f}(f^{0})
with maximal interval
[0,T(f^{0}))
that must be open in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Consider
T^{0}:=T(f^{0})<\infty
and suppose there exist an increasing sequence
t_{i}\rightarrow T^{0}
such that
\|f_{t_{i}}\|_{p}\leq r<\infty,\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix now
\overline{T}>T^{0}
and fix
\gamma(\overline{T})
the constant defined in the existence part of the theorem for the ball in which we perform the contraction. We have from the semigroup
P_{t}
in the interval
[0,\overline{T}]
that

\|P_{t}(f_{t_{i}})\|_{X_{\overline{T}}}\lesssim re^{-\overline{T}}.

We choose then
\overline{T}>0
such that it holds
\|P_{t}(f_{t_{i}})\|_{X_{\overline{T}}}\leq\gamma(T^{0})
, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

We can now repeat the same existence scheme and we obtain that
f
exists at least on
[t_{i},t_{i}+\overline{T}],\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}
contradicting the maximal extension of the interval. Thus, for

we must have

\sup_{T(f^{0})-\varepsilon<t<T(f^{0})}\|f_{t}\|_{L^{2}_{M}}=\infty.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Recall that

\displaystyle K(t,w):=\frac{1}{(2\pi(e^{2t}-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2\pi(e^{2t}-1)}},

We show now that a mild solution is indeed a weak solution for our equation and use this to enhance the regularity of the time derivative of the solution.

Lemma 4.3

(

Consider

2.1

Proof.

Fix
T>0

. Notice that

\left\langle f_{t},\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{L}(f_{t})+df_{t},\phi\right\rangle.

Under this definition and observation we consider now
f
mild solution as before

f_{t}=P_{t}f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}f_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}Q_{s}(f,f)ds

ans analyze the well defined quanity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle f_{s},\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{s}f_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr+\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{s}f_{0},\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds}_{A}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds}_{B}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds}_{C}.

Now we analyze, using the regularity result on
f
derived in the previous sections and using
[
13
]
, each of the component on the right hand side.

A:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{s}f_{0},\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{L}P_{s}f_{0},\phi\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{s}f_{0},\phi\right\rangle ds

B:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{L}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\phi\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\phi\right\rangle ds.

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{r}^{t}\mathcal{L}P_{s-r}f_{r}ds,\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{r}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}P_{s-r}f_{r}ds,\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{t-r}f_{r},\phi\right\rangle dr-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle f_{r},\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{L}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\phi\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{r}^{t}\mathcal{L}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)ds,\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{t-r}Q_{r}(f,f)-Q_{r}(f,f),\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\phi\right\rangle ds.

To prove all the following equality we have used the fact that, if

and in that case

Thanks to the reguliraty on

we can apply this result, thus putting all together we get:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle f_{s},\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\left\langle P_{t}f_{0},\phi\right\rangle-\left\langle f_{0},\phi\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{s}f_{0},\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{t-r}f_{r},\phi\right\rangle dr-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle f_{r},\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\phi\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{t-r}Q_{r}(f,f),\phi\right\rangle dr-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle Q_{r}(f,f),\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\phi\right\rangle ds.

For which we derive, using the equality of the mild solution:

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{0},\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle f_{s},\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle Q_{r}(f,f),\phi\right\rangle dr=\underbrace{\left\langle P_{t}f_{0},\phi\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{t-r}f_{r},\phi\right\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{t-r}Q_{r}(f,f),\phi\right\rangle dr}_{:=\left\langle P_{t}f_{o}+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-r}f_{r}dr+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle f_{t},\phi\right\rangle}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle P_{s}f_{0},\phi\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}f_{r}dr,\phi\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{r}(f,f)dr,\phi\right\rangle ds}_{:=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle f_{s},\phi\right\rangle ds}-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle f_{s},\phi\right\rangle ds.

Thus

, concluding the proof.

To conclude we need to extend the regularity on the time derivative and prove that

\partial_{t}f_{m}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Analyzing the equation solved by

, proving that

boils down to prove that the quantity

In order to to this we use the mild formulation of
f
, the explicit formulation of the nonlinearity

and the bound we have previously derived. We reduce again the problem, using Cauchy-Schwartz and the inequality on the weight to prove that

\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla f_{m}|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dvds<\infty.

We consider:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla f_{m}|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dvds

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla P_{t}f_{0}|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dsdv}_{A}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}Q_{m}^{R}(f,f)dr|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dvds}_{B}

we work separately on the two term:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\int K(t,w)f_{0}(e^{t}v-w)dw|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dvds

\displaystyle\leq e^{T}c_{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int\int K(t,w)|\nabla f_{0}(e^{t}v-w)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dwdvds

\displaystyle\leq e^{T}c_{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int K(t,w)\int|\nabla f_{0}(e^{t}v-w)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dvdwds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int K(t,w)\int|\nabla f_{0}(e^{t}v-w)|^{2}(\left\langle e^{t}v\right\rangle^{2}+\left\langle w\right\rangle^{2})dvdwds

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{2,2}+\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{2}.

while for the other terms, we use intensively the form of the nonlinearity
Q^{R}
. In particular we use the bound obtained with
\chi_{R}(v)
and

to control the weight and conclude.

\displaystyle B\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\underbrace{\left(\int|\nabla P_{s-r}Q_{m}^{R}(f,f)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv\right)}_{C}drds

and we compute directly on the term

.

\displaystyle\left(\int|\nabla P_{s-r}Q_{m}^{R}(f,f)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{T}\int|P_{s-r}\nabla Q_{m}^{R}(f,f)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv

\displaystyle=e^{T}\int|P_{s-r}(\left(Q_{m}^{R}(\nabla f,f)+Q_{m}^{R}(f,\nabla f)\right)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\int P_{s-r}|(\left(Q_{m}^{R}(\nabla f,f)+Q_{m}^{R}(f,\nabla f)\right)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv

Since the two addenda of the sum are quite similar we focus only on one of them. In particular we focus on each single term one for the ”positive” part, the negative part being easier.

\displaystyle\int\int K(s-r,w)\left|\int\nabla f_{n}(h(v,w,z)f_{m-n}(z)|z|\chi_{R}(e^{t}v-w)\chi_{R}(z)dz\right|^{2}dw\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq\int\int K(s-r,w)\left|\int\nabla f_{n}(h(v,w,z)f_{m-n}(z)|z|\chi_{R}(e^{t}v-w)\chi_{R}(z)dz\right|^{2}dw\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}^{2}\|f\|_{1}\int\int K(s-r,w)\int|\nabla f_{n}(h(v,w,z)|^{2}f_{m-n}(z)dzdw\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{T}\|\nabla f_{n}(s)\|_{2,2}^{2}\|\|f_{n}\|_{1,2}^{2}\|_{\infty,T}.

Putting this in

we get

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int|\nabla P_{s-r}Q_{m}^{R}(f,f)|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dv\right)drds\leq C(R,M,T,\|f\|_{1},\|f\|_{1,2})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\|\nabla f(s)\|_{2,2}^{2}ds\ dt.

Summing on

\sum_{m}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla f_{m}|^{2}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}dvds\leq C(f_{0})+C(R,M,T,\|f\|_{1},\|f\|_{1,2})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\|\nabla f(s)\|_{2,2}^{2}ds\ dt

Assuming that

f_{m}\in H^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\forall m=1,...,M.

This extended regularity for the truncated solution

Theorem 4.5

Under the hypothesis of regularity of the initial condition, and thanks to Lemma

In this section, we provide our original motivation that gives rise naturally to the

\displaystyle dx^{N}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=v_{i}^{N}(t)dt,\quad i\in\mathcal{N}(t)

\displaystyle m_{i}^{N}(t)dv^{N}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\alpha(m_{i}^{N}(t))^{1/d}\left[\sqrt{2\mu}dB_{i}(t)+\sum_{k\in K}\sigma_{k}(x_{i}^{N}(t))\,\circ\,dW_{t}^{k}-v_{i}^{N}(t)dt\right],

The rule of coagulation between pairs of particles is as follows. Let

One dimensional representations of finite 𝑾 W -algebras, Dirac reduction and the orbit method Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2000 revision ). Primary 17B35, 17B63. Secondary 17B20, 17B10.
Lewis Topley

Abstract
. In this paper we study the variety of one dimensional representations of a finite W W -algebra attached to a classical Lie algebra, giving a precise description of the dimensions of the irreducible components. We apply this to prove a conjecture of Losev describing the image of his orbit method map. In order to do so we first establish new Yangian-type presentations of semiclassical limits of the W W -algebras attached to distinguished nilpotent elements in classical Lie algebras, using Dirac reduction.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Let
G
be a complex connected reductive algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
. Identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by a choice of non-degenerate
G
-invariant trace form on
\mathfrak{g}
. The primitive spectrum
\operatorname{Prim}U(\mathfrak{g})
is the set of all primitive ideals of the enveloping algebra, equipped with the Jacobson topology. These ideals are classically studied via their invariants, and the most important of these are the
associated variety
and
Goldie rank
. The associated variety
\mathcal{V\!A}(I)
is defined to be the vanishing locus in
\mathfrak{g}
of the associated graded ideal
\operatorname{gr}I\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]
with respect to the PBW filtration. A celebrated theorem of Kostant states that
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
is the vanishing locus of the positive degree invariant polynomials
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}_{+}
whilst Joseph’s irreducibility theorem states that
\mathcal{V\!A}(I)
is irreducible (see
[
Ja04
]
for a detailed survey). Together with Dixmier’s lemma, these results show that the associated variety is the closure of a nilpotent orbit. The Goldie rank is defined to be the uniform dimension of the primitive quotient
U(\mathfrak{g})/I
.

It is natural to consider the decomposition
\operatorname{Prim}U(\mathfrak{g})=\bigcup_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Prim}_{\mathcal{O}}U(\mathfrak{g})
, were the union is taken over all nilpotent
G
-orbits
\mathcal{O}
and
\operatorname{Prim}_{\mathcal{O}}U(\mathfrak{g})=\{I\in\operatorname{Prim}U(\mathfrak{g})\mid\mathcal{V\!A}(I)=\overline{\mathcal{O}}\}.

Now fix an orbit
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
and
e\in\mathcal{O}
, and let
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
denote the finite
W
-algebra, first associated to
(\mathfrak{g},e)
by Premet
[
Pr02
]
. The reductive part of the centraliser
G^{e}(0)
acts naturally on
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
by algebra automorphisms, and this induces an action of the component group
\Gamma=G^{e}(0)/G^{e}(0)^{\circ}
on the category of finite dimensional modules. Losev famously gave a new construction of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
via deformation quantization
[
Lo10a
]
and used this to show that
\operatorname{Prim}_{\mathcal{O}}U(\mathfrak{g})
is in bijection with
U(\mathfrak{g},e)\operatorname{-mod}_{\operatorname{f.d.}}/\Gamma

[
Lo11
]
. The one dimensional representations of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
play an especially important role here for two reasons: on one hand the images under Skryabin’s equivalence are all completely prime, and therefore play a key role in Joseph’s theory of Goldie rank polynomials
[
Lo15
]
, and on the other hand they classify quantizations of
G
-equivariant coverings of
\mathcal{O}

[
Lo10b
]
.

1.2.
The above narrative leads us to consider the affine scheme
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e):=\operatorname{Spec}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
associated to the maximal abelian quotient. By Hilbert’s nullstellensatz the closed points classify the one dimensional representations of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
. The work of Losev and Premet
[
Lo10a
,
Pr14
]
shows that
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
is nonempty and in
[
Pr10
,
PT14
]
the first steps were made towards a full description of the variety of closed points.

Recall that the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
are the maximal irreducible subsets consisting of orbits of constant dimension. They are classified via the theory of decomposition classes which, in turn, are classified by the Lusztig–Spaltenstein induction data. One of the main themes of
[
PT14
]
, which we build upon in this paper, is the interplay between the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
and the structure of
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
.

In the case where
\mathfrak{g}
is classical, by which we mean a simple Lie algebra of type
A, B, C
or
D
, we described a combinatorial procedure for enumerating the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
containing a given orbit
\mathcal{O}
, and we named it the
Kempken–Spaltenstein (KS) algorithm
. This algorithm played a key role in
[
PT14
, Theorem 1]
, which states that for
\mathfrak{g}
classical the variety
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
is an affine space if and only if
e
lies in a unique sheet of
\mathfrak{g}
. The first goal of this paper is to elucidate the structure of
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
when
\mathfrak{g}
is classical and
e
is
singular
, which means that it lies in multiple sheets.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},...,\mathcal{S}_{l}
be the set of all sheets containing
\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})/G
. If
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
denotes the Slodowy slice to
\mathcal{O}
at
e
then we define the
Katsylo variety

(1.1)

\displaystyle e+X:=(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{S}_{i}.

In
[
Ka82
]
Katsylo used this variety to construct a geometric quotient of the variety
\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{S}_{i}
. Perhaps the first indication that
e+X
should influence the representation theory of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
appeared in
[
Pr10
]
. Premet used reduction modulo
p
to show that there is a surjective map on the sets of irreducible components

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Comp}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Comp}(e+X)

which restricts to a dimension preserving bijection on some subset of
\operatorname{Comp}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
. The following is our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra of classical type, the map (
1.2
) is a dimension preserving bijection.

Thanks to
[
Pr10
, Corollary 3.2]
the variety
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
is irreducible when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, and so Theorem
1.1
follows in this case, using the properties of (
1.2
) listed above. Hence we focus on types
B
,
C
and
D
in this paper. For these classical types, the dimensions of the irreducible components of
e+X
can be calculated from the KS algorithm, which depends only on the partition associated to
e
; see Proposition
6.2
(2) and Proposition
8.3
. Thus Theorem
1.1
provides an effective method for computing dimensions of all components of
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
. We note that these dimensions were calculated in low ranks in
[
BG18
]
.

1.3.
In
[
Lo22
]
Losev demonstrated that for every conic symplectic singularity the functor of filtered quantizations of Poisson deformations admits an initial object (see
[
ACET20
]
for more detail). We call such an initial object a
universal quantization
. Using this result, he then showed that every coadjoint orbit uniquely gives rise to a quantization of the affinization of a certain cover of a nilpotent orbit, and that each such quantization give rise to a completely prime primitive ideal. Thus we have a map
\mathcal{J}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}/G\to\operatorname{Prim}U(\mathfrak{g})
, which is known to be an embedding whenever
\mathfrak{g}
is classical
[
Lo22
, Theorem 5.3]
. The search for such a map is motivated by the orbit method of Kostant and Kirillov, and we will refer to the map as
Losev’s orbit method map
. An introduction to the orbit method can be found in
[
Vo94
]
, whilst Losev’s construction is surveyed in Section
9.2
of the current paper.

It is important to understand and characterise the primitive ideals appearing in the image of the orbit method map for
\mathfrak{g}
. Losev has conjectured that they are precisely the annihilators of simple Whittaker modules coming from one dimensional representations of
W
-algebras. In the final Section of this paper we deduce his conjecture from Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\mathfrak{g}
classical, the image of
\mathcal{J}
consists of primitive ideals obtained from one dimensional representations of
W
-algebras under Skryabin’s equivalence.

1.4.
It is worth commenting on Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in the context of exceptional Lie algebras: there are 6 rigid orbits for which the finite
W
-algebra admits two 1-dimensional representations (see
[
PT21
, Table 1]
and
[
Pr14
]
for more detail). In these cases
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
has two points and
e+X
consists of a single point. This shows that Theorem
1.1
cannot hold outside classical types. Similarly, for each rigid orbit Losev’s orbit method map attaches a unique primitive ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g})
with associated variety equal to the closure of
G\cdot e
(see Lemma
9.10
) and so these same examples show that Theorem
1.2
also fails outside classical types.

1.5.
There are several new tools involved in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and we now briefly describe the most important ones. One of the basic ideas comes from deformation theory. The finite
W
-algebra is a filtered quantization of the transverse Poisson structure on the Slodowy slice
\mathbb{C}[e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}]
and so there are two natural degenerations associated to
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
. On the one hand, we may degenerate
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
to the classical finite
W
-algebra
S(\mathfrak{g},e)\cong\mathbb{C}[e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}]
and then abelianise, which leads to the spectrum of the maximal Poisson abelian quotient
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{g},e):=\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
. On the other hand we may abelianise and then degenerate, which leads us to the
asymptotic cone of
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
, denoted
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e):=\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}})
.

One of the general results of this paper states that there is a closed immersion of schemes inducing a bijection on closed points, which we prove using reduction to prime characteristic

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{g},e).

Furthermore by considering the rank strata and symplectic leaves of the Poisson structure of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
we see that the reduced subscheme associated to
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{g},e)
is
e+X
(Proposition
6.2
). Therefore the main theorem will follow if we can show that
\operatorname{Comp}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
is no larger than
\operatorname{Comp}\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
. It is an elementary fact from commutative algebra that
\operatorname{Comp}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)\leq\operatorname{Comp}\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
provided
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
is reduced, and so our approach is to show that
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
has no nilpotent elements. By (
1.3
) it suffices to show that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced.

In this paragraph we take
\mathfrak{g}
classical. By passing to the completion of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
at the maximal graded ideal and using the fact that the Slodowy slice is transverse to every point of
e+X
we are able to reduce the problem of showing that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced to the case where
e
is distinguished. To be more precise,
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced if and only if the completion at the maximal graded ideal is so, and we show that this completion is reduced if and only if
(S(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{e})_{x}^{\!\wedge})^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced, where
x
is a point on the Slodowy slice attached to a distinguished element in a larger classical Lie algebra. For the proof we make use of the fact that transverse Poisson manifolds are locally diffeomorphic, which follows from Weinstein’s splitting theorem
[
We83
, Theorem 2.1]
.

1.6.
Now Theorem
1.1
will follow if we can show that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced for distinguished elements
e
. We introduce a new method to attack this problem. If
X
is a complex Poisson scheme of finite type and
H
is a reductive group acting rationally by Poisson automorphisms then the invariant subscheme
X^{H}
can be equipped with a Poisson structure via Dirac reduction. The reduced Poisson algebra will be denoted
R(\mathbb{C}[X],H)
. Now if
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
for
G
reductive and
H\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
is a reductive group fixing an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
then we prove the following isomorphism of Poisson algebras

(1.4)

\displaystyle R(S(\mathfrak{g},e),H)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}S(\mathfrak{g}^{H},e).

Some special cases of (
1.4
) were discovered by Ragoucy
[
Ra01
]
.

We apply this isomorphism in the case where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
H=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by some involution
\tau
. Then
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}=\mathfrak{so}_{n}
or
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{n}
. It follows from the work of Brundan and Kleshchev that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
is a quotient of a (semiclassical) shifted Yangian
y_{n}(\sigma)
depending on
e
. If
e
is distinguished then we can identify an involution
\tau
on
y_{n}(\sigma)
defined so that the Poisson homomorphism
y_{n}(\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g},e)
is
\tau
-equivariant. As a consequence we can apply the Dirac reduction procedure to the shifted Yangian
y_{n}(\sigma)
and thus obtain a presentation of the Poisson structure on
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
. We mention that obtaining presentations of finite
W
-algebras outside of type
A
is one of the key open problems in the field.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
be the maximal graded ideal. Finally we use the presentation of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
to calculate generators and certain relations of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
. We see that the reduced algebra of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is naturally identified with the coordinate ring on the tangent cone
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)]
. Michaël Bulois has recently demonstrated that the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
e
are transversal at
e
(work in preparation
[
Bu
]
), which allows us to calculate the dimensions of the irreducible components of
\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)
, once again they are determined by the KS algorithm. Using a combinatorial argument we then show that the relations mentioned above give a full presentation for
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
. It follows quickly that both
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
are reduced, which allows us to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

1.7.
Since the presentation of the distinguished semiclassical finite
W
-algebras in types
B, C, D
is an important result in its own right we formulate it straight away. For the proof combine Theorem

and Propositions

and

Let

1.8.
To conclude the introduction we describe the structure of the paper, which is divided into two parts. The first part is very algebraic, dealing with Dirac reduction and the presentation of semiclassical
W
-algebras in the distinguished case. The second part is more geometric, studying the degenerations of
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
using Lusztig–Spaltenstein induction and the tangent cone of the Katsylo variety.

Part I:
We begin Section
2
by giving an elementary introduction to the version of Dirac reduction used in this paper. In Subsection
2.3
we prove the isomorphism (
1.4
), which should have independent interest. In Section
3
we describe the semiclassical shifted Yangian
y_{n}(\sigma)
by generators and relations. The main results on the Dirac reduction of
y_{n}(\sigma)
are presented in Subsection
3.4
, including the canonical grading, the loop filtration, the PBW theorem and the presentation by generators and relations. All of the results about
R(y_{n}(\sigma),\tau)
are ultimately deduced from similar results on
y_{n}(\sigma)
. In Section
4
we recall the definition of Brundan–Kleshchev’s isomorphism
y_{n}(\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g},e)
and show by an explicit calculation that this is
\tau
-equivariant for a suitable choice of involution on
y_{n}(\sigma)
. By (
1.4
) this leads to a surjection
R(y_{n}(\sigma),\tau)\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},e)
and in Subsection
4.4
we describe a full set of Poisson generators for the kernel.

Part II:
In Section
5
we gather together some important general facts about degenerations and completions of schemes, as well as reviewing the theory of rank stratification and symplectic leaves of a Poisson scheme. In Section
6
we explain how
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is related to Katsylo variety
e+X
, and use the aforementioned results of Bulois to enumerate the irreducible components of
\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)
and calculate their dimensions. In Section
7
we introduce quantum finite
W
-algebras and prove the existence of the closed immersion (
1.3
). The proof of the latter uses a reduction modulo
p
argument similar to Premet’s construction of the component map (
1.2
) in
[
Pr10
, Theorem 1.2]
, along with the identification of reduced schemes
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{g},e)_{\operatorname{red}}=\mathbb{C}[e+X]
from Section
6
. Finally in Section
8
we describe the Kempken–Spaltenstein algorithm, as well as its relationship with sheets, and then use this to construct an algebraic variety
X_{\lambda}
associated to a distinguished nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}
with partition
\lambda
, which we call the

. In Theorem
8.8
we use the presentation of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
obtained in part I of the paper to demonstrate that

, and we show that these are isomorphisms by comparing the dimensions of the irreducible components. In particular this implies that

is reduced for
e
distinguished. Theorem
8.9
reduces the general case to the distinguished case. Finally we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Subsection
8.8
, making use of deformation techniques gathered in Section
5
.

In Subsection
9.1
we recall the classification of Poisson deformations and their quantizations for conic symplectic singularities, due to Losev and Namikawa. In Subsection
9.2
we recall Losev’s theory of birational induction, describe the orbit method map and formulate a slight refinement of Theorem
1.2
. In Subsection
9.3
we recall some of the key properties of Losev’s dagger functor from
[
Lo11
]
and explain how they treat universal quantizations of affinisations of orbit covers. Finally in Subsection

we relate the orbit method map to

and prove Theorem

.

Notation and conventions

The following notation will be used throughout the paper.
All algebras and vector spaces are defined over
\mathbb{C}
, except in Section
7
where we use reduction modulo a large prime. We use capital letters
G,H,...
for algebraic groups and gothic script
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},...
for their Lie algebras.

If
A
is an algebra and
X\subseteq A
then
(X)
will denote the two-sided ideal of
A
generated by
X
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra then
U(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the enveloping algebra and
S(\mathfrak{g})
the symmetric algebra equipped with its
Lie–Poisson structure
: this is the unique Poisson bracket on
S(\mathfrak{g})
extending the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g})
. The associated graded algebra of an almost commutative, filtered associative algebra is equipped with a Poisson structure in the usual manner. If
A
is a commutative algebra and
I\subseteq A
an ideal, then we write
\operatorname{gr}_{I}A
for the graded algebra with respect to the
I
-adic filtration.

Almost all schemes appearing in this paper will be affine schemes of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
, thus the reader may almost always think of complex affine varieties, except that the coordinate rings will often be non-reduced. In fact, the consideration of nilpotent elements will be vital to our main results. Occasionally we write
\operatorname{Max}(A)
for the variety of closed points in the prime spectrum of a commutative algebra
A
. If
X
is a noetherian scheme then we write
\operatorname{Comp}(X)
for the set of irreducible components.

Part I
Presentations of classical
W
-algebras

2.
Dirac reduction for classical finite
W
-algebras

2.1.
Invariants via Dirac reduction

Throughout this Section
H
will be a reductive algebraic group, although in our later applications
H
will be a cyclic group of order 2. When
H
acts locally finitely and rationally on some vector space
A
we write
A_{+}:=A^{H}
for the invariants and write
A_{-}
for the unique
H
-invariant complement to
A_{+}
in
A
.

Let
X
be an complex affine Poisson variety and suppose
z_{1},...,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[X]
such that the determinant of the matrix
(\{z_{i},z_{j}\})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is a unit. In his seminal paper
[
Dir50
]
Dirac defined a new Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{C}[X]
, such that the
z_{i}
are Casimirs, thus equipping
\mathbb{C}[X]/(z_{1},...,z_{n})
with a Poisson structure. In fact this is a special case of the following procedure: say that
I\subseteq\mathbb{C}[X]
is a
Dirac ideal
if
N_{\mathbb{C}[X]}(I)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}[X]/I
surjects where
N_{\mathbb{C}[X]}(I):=\{f\in\mathbb{C}[X]\mid\{f,I\}\subseteq I\}
denotes the Poisson idealiser. Then the subscheme associated to
I
inherits a Poisson structure from
X
, and we call this induced structure the
Dirac reduction

[
LPV13
, §5.4.3]
.

Now let
H
be a reductive group acting rationally on
X
by Poisson automorphisms. We regard the set of invariants
X^{H}
as a (not necessarily reduced) affine scheme such that the structure sheaf has global sections
\mathbb{C}[X^{H}]:=\mathbb{C}[X]/I_{H}
and
I_{H}:=(h\cdot f-f\mid h\in H,f\in\mathbb{C}[X])
. Although
I_{H}
is usually not a Poisson ideal, it is always a Dirac ideal (this is a corollary of Lemma
2.1
) so that
X^{H}
acquires the structure of a Poisson scheme.

The following suggests an alternative approach of the Poisson structure on
X^{H}
, better-suited to calculations.

Lemma 2.1
.

The map
\mathbb{C}[X]^{H}\to\mathbb{C}[X^{H}]
is surjective and

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X]^{H}/\mathbb{C}[X]^{H}\cap I_{H}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}[X^{H}]

is a Poisson isomorphism.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathbb{C}[X]^{H}
,
g\in\mathbb{C}[X]
and
h\in H
then
\{f,h\cdot g-g\}=h\cdot\{f,g\}-\{f,g\}
and so
\mathbb{C}[X]^{H}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}[X]}(I_{H})
is a Poisson subalgebra. Since
H
is reductive and acts rationally we can decompose
\mathbb{C}[X]=\mathbb{C}[X]_{+}\oplus\,\mathbb{C}[X]_{-}
into its
H
-invariant part, and its complementary
H
-submodule. If
V\subseteq\mathbb{C}[X]_{-}
is an irreducible
H
-submodule then the vector space spanned by
\{h\cdot v-v\mid h\in H,v\in V\}\subseteq V
is
H
-stable and nonzero hence equal to
V
, and it follows that
I_{H}=(\mathbb{C}[X]_{-})
. Therefore the composition
\mathbb{C}[X]^{H}\hookrightarrow N_{\mathbb{C}[X]}(I_{H})\to\mathbb{C}[X^{H}]=\mathbb{C}[X]/I_{H}
is surjective.
∎

Now let
A
be any Poisson algebra with a locally finite, rational action of
H
by Poisson automorphisms. Let
I_{H}
denote the ideal generated by
A_{-}
and define
the Dirac reduction of
A
by
H
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle R(A,H):=A^{H}/A^{H}\cap I_{H}

If
\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)
is a semisimple Poisson automorphism of finite order, then we often abuse notation writing
R(A,\tau)
for
R(A,H)
where
H
is the group generated by
\tau
.

Since
H
is reductive the functor of
H
-invariants is exact on the category of locally finite, rational
H
-modules. This implies that whenever
A
is a Poisson algebra as above and
I\subseteq A
is an
H
-stable Poisson ideal we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle R(A,H)\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow R(A/I,H).

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose the following:

(1)

V
is a direct sum of locally finite
H
-modules with
H
-stable decomposition
V=V^{H}\oplus V_{-}
.

(2)

S(V)
is a Poisson algebra with
H
acting by Poisson automorphisms.

Then the natural map
S(V^{H})\to R(S(V),H)
is an isomorphism of commutative algebras.

Proof.

Since
S(V)=S(V^{H})\oplus(V_{-})
and
S(V^{H})
is
H
-fixed we must have
S(V)_{-}\subseteq(V_{-})
. Combining with
V_{-}\subseteq S(V)_{-}
we deduce that
I_{H}=(V_{-})
which proves the map
S(V^{H})\to R(S(V),H)
is a commutative algebra isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
H
a reductive group of automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
acting locally finitely. In this case the composition
S(\mathfrak{g}^{H})\to S(\mathfrak{g})^{H}\to R(S(\mathfrak{g}),H)
is a Poisson homomorphism and so Lemma
2.2
shows that
S(\mathfrak{g}^{H})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}R(S(\mathfrak{g}),H)
as Poisson algebras.

2.2.
Classical finite
W
-algebras

For the rest of the section we fix a connected reductive algebraic group
G
such that the derived subgroup is simply connected, and write
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
. Let
\kappa:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
be a choice of non-degenerate trace form on
\mathfrak{g}
which is preserved by
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
. Pick a nilpotent element
e\in\mathfrak{g}
and write
\chi:=\kappa(e,\cdot)\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Pick an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}
and write
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}(i)
for the grading by
\operatorname{ad}(h)
-eigenspaces. Throughout the paper we use the notation
\mathfrak{g}(\leq\!i)=\bigoplus_{j\leq i}\mathfrak{g}(j)
and similar for
\mathfrak{g}(\!<\!i)
. Since
e\in\mathfrak{g}(2)
we see that
\chi
restricts to a character on
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
. Make the following notation
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}:=\{x-\chi(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})\}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g})
. The nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
is algebraic and we write
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!0})=\operatorname{Lie}G(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
.

The (classical) finite
W
-algebra associated to
(\mathfrak{g},e)
is a Poisson reduction of
S(\mathfrak{g})

(2.4)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g},e):=(S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})^{G(<0)}.

In more detail, the Poisson normaliser
N=\{f\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mid\{f,\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
is a Poisson subalgebra of
S(\mathfrak{g})
with
N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
embedded as a Poisson ideal, and
(S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})^{\mathfrak{g}(<0)}
is equipped with a Poisson structure via the isomorphism
N/N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\cong(S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})^{\mathfrak{g}(<0)}
.

The
Kazhdan grading
is defined on
S(\mathfrak{g})
by placing
\mathfrak{g}(i)
in Kazhdan degree
i+2
. Notice that
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
generates a homogeneous ideal of
S(\mathfrak{g})
and that
S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
inherits a connected grading in non-negative degrees, with
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
embedded as a graded subalgebra, with Poisson bracket in degree
-2
.

Since the grading is good for
e
the map
\mathfrak{g}^{e}\to S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
is injective.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{0}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
denote the unique maximal graded ideal.

(1)

There exists a Kazhdan graded map
\theta:\mathfrak{g}^{e}\to S(\mathfrak{g},e)
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\theta(x)-x\in\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{2}.

Furthermore
\theta
can be chosen to be equivariant with respect to any reductive group of Poisson automorphisms acting rationally on
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
by graded automorphisms.

(2)

If
\theta
is any map satisfying (
2.5
) then the induced map
S(\mathfrak{g}^{e})\to S(\mathfrak{g},e)
is an isomorphism of commutative algebras.

Proof.

It follows from
[
GG02
, Lemma 2.1]
that the restriction homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\cong\mathbb{C}[\chi+\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})^{\perp}]\to\mathbb{C}[\chi+\mathfrak{g}^{f}]\cong S(\mathfrak{g}^{e})
gives a
G^{e}(0)
-equivariant isomorphism
S(\mathfrak{g},e)\to S(\mathfrak{g}^{e})
of commutative algebras, where
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})^{\perp}:=\{\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\mid\eta(\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1}))=0\}
. Taking the inverse isomorphism restricted to
\mathfrak{g}^{e}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g}^{e})
gives the desired map
\theta
. If
H\subseteq G^{e}(0)
is any reductive group of Poisson isomorphisms then
\theta
can be replaced with an
H
-equivariant map using the standard trick of projecting onto isotypic components of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
for the
H
-action.

Now if
\theta
is any map satisfying (
2.5
) then
S(\mathfrak{g}^{e})\to S(\mathfrak{g},e)
and it suffices to show that this map is surjective. If
x_{1},...,x_{r}\in\mathfrak{g}^{e}
is a homogenous basis then
[
Ja04
, Lemma 7.1]
shows that
\theta(x_{1}),...,\theta(x_{r})
generate a graded radical ideal of finite codimension. The only such ideal is the maximal graded ideal of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
and this implies that
\theta(x_{1}),...,\theta(x_{r})
generate
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
as a commutative algebra.
∎

2.3.
Dirac reduction of classical
W
-algebras

Now let
H\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
a reductive subgroup which fixes our choice of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple pointwise. Make the notation
\mathfrak{g}_{+}:=\mathfrak{g}^{H}
and let
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
denote an
H
-invariant complement to
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Also write
G_{+}\subseteq G
for the connected component of the subgroup consisting of elements
g\in G
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(g)
commutes with the action of
H
.

Our next goal is to relate the Dirac reduction
R(S(\mathfrak{g},e),H)
with the classical
W
-algebra
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e)
. Later in the paper we will only exploit this relationship in the case where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
and
H
is a cyclic group of order 2. For the sake of simplicity, we state and prove our results in a much greater generality.

Lemma 2.5
.

(1)

The restriction of
\kappa
to
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is non-degenerate and
\mathfrak{g}_{+}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

(2)

G_{+}
is reductive and
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\operatorname{Lie}(G_{+})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is spanned by elements
\{h\cdot x-x\mid h\in H,x\in\mathfrak{g}\}
(see Lemma
2.1
) it follows from a short calculation that
\kappa(\mathfrak{g}_{+},\mathfrak{g}_{-})=0
. Since
\kappa
is non-degenerate and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, part (1) follows.

Since
\kappa
is a non-degenerate trace form, we may pick a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
such that
\kappa(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho(x)\rho(y))
. If
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is a nilpotent ideal then by Engel’s theorem there exists
k>0
such that
\rho(\mathfrak{n})^{k}=0
. Hence
(\rho(x)\rho(n))^{k}=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
n\in\mathfrak{n}
, so
\kappa(x,n)=0
. Since
\kappa
is non-degenerate the nilradical of
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is trivial.

If
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}:G\to\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
is the adjoint representation then we consider
\rho_{+}:=\operatorname{Ad}_{\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})}\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{G}:G\to\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g}))
and let
\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}(W)
be any faithful representation admitting
\rho_{+}
as a direct summand. If we identify
G
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}
) with its image in
\operatorname{GL}(W)
(resp.
\mathfrak{gl}(W)
) via
\rho
(resp.
d_{0}\rho
), and identify
H
with a subset of
\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq W
, then
G_{+}
is precisely the subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(W)
fixing
H
, and similar for
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
. Now apply
[
Hum75
, Theorem 13.2]
to see that
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\operatorname{Lie}(G_{+})
. Since the nilradical of
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is trivial,
G_{+}
is reductive thanks to
[
Hum75
, Theorem 13.5]
.
∎

Since
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is a reductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
(e,h,f)
we can consider
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e)
. Furthermore
H
preserves
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
and the induced action on
S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
stabilises the
G(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
-invariants, so that
H
acts by Poisson automorphisms on
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
.

We are now ready to formulate one of our first main theorems, stated in (
1.4
).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of a reductive group. The projection map
\phi
defined in (
2.15
) descends to a Poisson isomorphism

(2.6)

\displaystyle R(S(\mathfrak{g},e),H)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e).

The proof of Theorem
2.6
will be given at the end of the current section. First we prepare for the proof with two Lemmas and a Proposition.

Thanks to Lemma
2.5
(1) we know that
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
with respect to
\kappa
. This implies

(2.7)

\chi(\mathfrak{g}_{-})=0.

Lemma 2.7
.

(1)

S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
;

(2)

S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}=S(\mathfrak{g})(\mathfrak{g}_{-}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})+\mathfrak{g}_{-}\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are graded subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
. In both parts (1) and (2) it is easy to see that the right hand side is contained in the left (for part (2) one should use (
2.7
)). Therefore to prove (1) and (2) it remains to show that we have the reverse inclusion.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
and
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})
, and let
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
and
x=x_{+}+x_{-}
be the decompositions over
S(\mathfrak{g})=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}
respectively. By (
2.7
) the projections of
f(x-\chi(x))
to
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are
f_{+}(x_{+}-\chi(x_{+}))
and
f_{+}x_{-}+f_{-}(x-\chi(x))
respectively. Therefore if
f(x-\chi(x))
lies in
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
we must have
f_{+}x_{-}+f_{-}(x-\chi(x))=0
and
f(x-\chi(x))=f_{+}(x_{+}-\chi(x_{+}))
, proving (1).

As a step towards proving (2) we claim that the projection of
S(\mathfrak{g})
onto
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
across the decomposition
S(\mathfrak{g})=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
preserves
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
. To see this observe that
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
is spanned by elements of the form
f(x-\chi(x))
with
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
. It suffices to show that the projection of such an element lies in
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
. Expanding
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
across
S(\mathfrak{g})=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and writing
x=x_{+}+x_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}
we see that the required projection is
fx_{-}+f_{-}(x_{+}-\chi(x_{+}))\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
thanks to (
2.7
), which proves the claim.

Let
F\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
. Then
F=\sum_{i}f_{i}(x_{i}-\chi(x_{i}))
for some elements
f_{i}\in S(\mathfrak{g})
and
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
. The fact that
F\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
implies that the projection of
F
to
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
is zero. Therefore if we replace each
f_{i}(x_{i}-\chi(x_{i}))
with its projection to
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
then we leave the equality
F=\sum_{i}f_{i}(x_{i}-\chi(x_{i}))
unchanged. It follows that every element of
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
can be written as a sum of elements
f(x-\chi(x))\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
with
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})
,
x\in\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
(here we use the claim from the previous paragraph). This observation reduces (2) to showing that every such element
f(x-\chi(x))\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
lies in
S(\mathfrak{g})(\mathfrak{g}_{-}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})+\mathfrak{g}_{-}\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})
. We let
f(x-\chi(x))
lie in the required space and write
x=x_{+}+x_{-}
where
x_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
. Then
f(x_{-}-\chi(x_{-}))\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
by (
2.7
), whilst
f(x_{+}-\chi(x_{+}))\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
implies that
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
so that
f(x_{+}-\chi(x_{+}))\in S(\mathfrak{g})(\mathfrak{g}_{-}\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})
. This proves (2).
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

The ideal
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
is the direct sum of its intersections with
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. Therefore
S(\mathfrak{g})=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
gives a
G_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
-module decomposition

(2.8)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})/S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}/S(\mathfrak{g})(\mathfrak{g}_{-}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})+\mathfrak{g}_{-}\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}).

Proof.

We begin by proving the claim

(2.9)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}.

The
G_{+}(<0)
-decomposition (
2.8
) is an immediate consequence.

The right hand side of (
2.9
) is clearly contained in the left hand side and so our proof will focus on the reverse inclusion. Let
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})
and
x-\chi(x)\in\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})=\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})\oplus\mathfrak{g}-(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
we can consider two cases: (i) if
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
then by (
2.7
) we have
f(x-\chi(x))\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
; (ii) if
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
then we can write
f=f_{+}+f_{-}\in S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, in which case
f_{+}(x-\chi(x))\in S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
and
f_{-}(x-\chi(x))\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
.
Now the Lemma follows from Lemma
2.7
.
∎

In what follows we will use the notation
S(\mathfrak{g},e)=S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+}\oplus S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{-}
for the decomposition into trivial and non-trivial
H
-modules, generalising our notation for
S(\mathfrak{g})
. Consider the two sets

(2.10)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{f\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mid g\cdot f-f\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\text{ for all }g\in G(\operatorname{\!<\!0})\};

(2.11)

\displaystyle N_{+}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{f\in S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mid g\cdot f-f\in S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\text{ for all }g\in G_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!0})\}.

By differentiating the locally finite actions on
G(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
on
S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
and of
G_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
on
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})/S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
we see that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\{\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1}),N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\}\subseteq N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi};\\
\{\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1}),N_{+}\}\subseteq N_{+}\cap S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}.\end{array}

We consider the projection

(2.15)

\displaystyle\phi:S(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})

across the decomposition
S(\mathfrak{g})=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\oplus S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. Although this is not a Poisson homomorphism the next Proposition shows that
\phi
descends to a Poisson homomorphism
R(S(\mathfrak{g},e),H)\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e)
.

Proposition 2.9
.

(i)

N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g})
and
N_{+}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
are Poisson subalgebras;

(ii)

\phi(N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+})\subseteq N_{+}
and the map
\phi:N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\to N_{+}
is a Poisson homomorphism;

(iii)

The map
\pi:S(\mathfrak{g})\to S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
restricts to a surjective Poisson homomorphism

N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+};

(iv)

The map
\pi_{+}:S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\to S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})/S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
restricts to a surjective Poisson homomorphism

N_{+}\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e);

(v)

The kernel of the map
N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+}
is contained in the kernel of the map
\pi_{+}\circ\phi
. Thus
\phi
induces a Poisson homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+}\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e)
which we also denote by
\phi
;

(vi)

S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+}\cap S(\mathfrak{g},e)S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{-}
is contained in the kernel of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+}\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e)
, inducing a surjective Poisson homomorphism

(2.16)

\displaystyle\phi:R(S(\mathfrak{g},e),H)\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}_{+},e).

Proof.

Since

by Poisson automorphisms the invariant subspace

is a Poisson subalgebra. Therefore the proofs of the two claims in (i) are identical, and we will only prove that

is a Poisson subalgebra. It is evidently closed under multiplication so we only need to show that it is closed under the bracket. Suppose that

, that

and that

. We have
g\cdot\{f_{1},f_{2}\}=\{g\cdot f_{1},g\cdot f_{2}\}=\{f_{1}+h_{1},f_{2}+h_{2}\}
and so (i) will follow if we can show that
\{f_{1},h_{2}\},\{h_{1},f_{2}\},\{h_{1},h_{2}\}\in S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
. Since
\chi
vanishes on

we have

, therefore
\{h_{1},h_{2}\}\in S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
by the Leibniz rule. To complete the proof of (i) we observe that
N_{+}\cap S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
is a Poisson ideal of
N_{+}
, which follows quickly from (
2.3
).

We now address (ii). Let

and write
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
according to the decomposition

. If
g\in G_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
then
g\cdot f_{+}-f_{+}\in S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
by Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
. This shows that
\phi(f)=f_{+}\in N
.

Finally, to see that
\phi:N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\to N_{+}
is a Poisson homomorphism it suffices to show that
N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is a Poisson ideal of
N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\{h\cdot x-x\mid h\in H,x\in\mathfrak{g}\}
and
S(\mathfrak{g})_{-}=\{h\cdot x-x\mid h\in H,x\in S(\mathfrak{g})\}
. If

then

for any

. Lemma
2.2
shows that the ideals generated by

and

coincide, hence
\{S(\mathfrak{g})_{+},\mathfrak{g}_{-}\}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, which shows that
S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is a Poisson ideal of
S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}
. This completes the proof of (ii).

The map
\pi
restricts to a surjection

by definition. Since the latter map is
H
-equivariant we get
N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\to S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+}
, which proves (iii), whilst (iv) is proven similarly.

We move on to (v). The kernel of
N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\to S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{+}
is
N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
. This is mapped to
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\cap S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
by

, thanks to Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
. Finally
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
lies in the kernel of
\pi_{+}
, which proves (v). Now we take
f\in N\cap S(\mathfrak{g})_{+}
such that
\pi(f)\in S(\mathfrak{g},e)S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{-}
. Using Lemma
2.8
again we see that
S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
decomposes as the direct sum of the image of
S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and the image of
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. Therefore
S(\mathfrak{g},e)_{-}
, and the ideal which it generates, are contained in the image of
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. It follows immediately that

\displaystyle f\in S(\mathfrak{g})S(\mathfrak{g})_{-}+S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}

\displaystyle\phi(f)\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}\cap S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})=S(\mathfrak{g}_{+})\mathfrak{g}_{+}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}

Hence

is in the kernel of

, completing (vi). This concludes the proof.

3.
Dirac reduction for shifted Yangians

3.1.
Poisson algebras by generators and relations

Let
X
be a set. The free Lie algebra
L_{X}
on
X
is the initial object in the category of (complex) Lie algebras generated by
X
and can be constructed as the Lie subalgebra of the free algebra
\mathbb{C}\langle X\rangle
generated by the vector space spanned by
X
. When
L
is a Lie algebra generated by
X
we say that
L
has relations
Y\subseteq L_{X}
if
Y
generates the kernel of
L_{X}\twoheadrightarrow L
.

The
free Poisson algebra generated by
X
is the initial object in the category of (complex) Poisson algebras generated by
X
. It can be constructed as the symmetric algebra
S(L_{X})
together with its Poisson structure. If there is a Poisson surjection
S(L_{X})\twoheadrightarrow A
then we say that
A
is Poisson generated by
X
. It is important to distinguish this from
A
being generated by
X
as a commutative algebra, as both notions will occur frequently.

We say that a (complex) Poisson algebra
A
has Poisson generators
X
and relations
Y\subseteq S(L_{X})
if there is a surjective Poisson homomorphism
S(L_{X})\twoheadrightarrow A
and the kernel is the Poisson ideal generated by
Y
.

Let
X
be a set and
Y\subseteq L_{X}\subseteq S(L_{X})
. Write
I
(resp.
J
) for the ideal of
S(L_{X})
(resp.
L_{X}
) generated by
Y
. It is easy to see that the natural map
S(L_{X})\to S(L_{X})/I
induces an isomorphism

(3.1)

\displaystyle S(L_{X}/J)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}S(L_{X})/I.

3.2.
Chevalley–Serre presentations for shifted current Lie–Poisson algebras

Throughout this section we fix an integer
n>0
. Following
[
BK06
,
BG07
]
a
shift matrix
is an
n\times n
array
\sigma=(s_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
of non-negative integers with zero on the diagonal, satisfying

(3.2)

\displaystyle s_{i,k}=s_{i,j}+s_{j,k}

whenever
i\leq j\leq k
or

. A shift matrix is said to be

if it is equal to its transpose. These shift matrices serve two key purposes: they classify certain good gradings for nilpotent elements in general linear Lie algebras

BG07
]
, and they provide one of the ingredients in the definition of
shifted Yangians
which give presentations of finite
W
-algebras in type
A

[
BK06
]
. The symmetric shift matrices correspond to Dynkin gradings.

All examples of shift matrices used in the applications of our results are symmetric, and so for convenience we assume that every shift matrix in this paper is symmetric. Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
do not require this hypothesis.

The
current algebra
is the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{c}_{n}:=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]
. It has a basis consisting of elements
\{e_{i,j}t^{r}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ r\geq 0\}
where we write

for

and

. For any shift matrix
\sigma=(s_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
we define the
shifted current algebra

to be the subalgebra spanned by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\{e_{i,j}t^{r}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ s_{i,j}\leq r\}.

3.3.
The semiclassical shifted Yangian

In this section we fix
n>0
, a shift matrix
\sigma
of size
n
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. The
(semiclassical) shifted Yangian

is the Poisson algebra generated by the set

(3.42)

\begin{array}[]{c}\{d_{i}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\ 0<r\}\cup\{e_{i}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i<n,\ s_{i,i+1}<r\}\\
\cup\,\{f_{i}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i<n,\ s_{i+1,i}<r\}\end{array}

subject to the following relations

(3.43)

\displaystyle\big\{d_{i}^{(r)},d_{j}^{(s)}\big\}=0,

(3.44)

\displaystyle\big\{e_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}\big\}=-\delta_{i,j}\sum_{t=0}^{r+s-1}d_{i+1}^{(r+s-1-t)}\widetilde{d}_{i}^{(t)},

(3.45)

\displaystyle\big\{d_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(s)}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\delta_{i,j}-\delta_{i,j+1})\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}d_{i}^{(t)}e_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)},

(3.46)

\displaystyle\big\{d_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\delta_{i,j+1}-\delta_{i,j})\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}f_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)}d_{i}^{(t)},

(3.47)

\displaystyle\big\{e_{i}^{(r)},e_{i}^{(s)}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=r}^{s-1}e_{i}^{(t)}e_{i}^{(r+s-1-t)}\hskip 31.29802pt\text{if $r<s$},

(3.48)

\displaystyle\big\{f_{i}^{(r)},f_{i}^{(s)}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=s}^{r-1}f_{i}^{(r+s-1-t)}f_{i}^{(t)}\hskip 31.29802pt\text{if $r>s$},

\displaystyle\big\{e_{i}^{(r+1)},e_{i+1}^{(s)}\big\}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\big\{e_{i}^{(r)},e_{i+1}^{(s+1)}\big\}=e_{i}^{(r)}e_{i+1}^{(s)},

(3.50)

\displaystyle\big\{f_{i}^{(r)},f_{i+1}^{(s+1)}\big\}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\big\{f_{i}^{(r+1)},f_{i+1}^{(s)}\big\}=f_{i+1}^{(s)}f_{i}^{(r)},

(3.51)

\displaystyle\big\{e_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(s)}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{ if }|i-j|>1,

(3.52)

\displaystyle\big\{f_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{ if }|i-j|>1,

(3.53)

\displaystyle\Big\{e_{i}^{(r)},\big\{e_{i}^{(s)},e_{j}^{(t)}\big\}\Big\}+\Big\{e_{i}^{(s)},\big\{e_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(t)}\big\}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{ if }|i-j|=1,

(3.54)

\displaystyle\Big\{f_{i}^{(r)},\big\{f_{i}^{(s)},f_{j}^{(t)}\big\}\Big\}+\Big\{f_{i}^{(s)},\big\{f_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(t)}\big\}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{ if
}|i-j|=1.

for all admissible

. In these relations, the notation
d_{i}^{(0)}=\widetilde{d}_{i}^{(0)}:=1
is used, and the elements
\widetilde{d}_{i}^{(r)}

(3.55)

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}_{i}^{(r)}:=-\sum_{t=1}^{r}d_{i}^{(t)}\widetilde{d}_{i}^{(r-t)}

In order to describe the structure of
y_{n}(\sigma)
as a commutative algebra we make the notation

(3.56)

\displaystyle e_{i,i+1}^{(r)}:=e_{i}^{(r)}\text{ for }1\leq i<n,\ s_{i,i+1}<r;

(3.57)

\displaystyle f_{i,i+1}^{(r)}:=f_{i}^{(r)}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n,\ s_{i+1,i}<r,

and inductively define

(3.58)

\displaystyle e_{i,j}^{(r)}:=\{e_{i,j-1}^{(r-s_{j-1,j})},e_{j-1}^{(s_{j-1,j}+1)}\}\text{ for }1\leq i<j\leq n,\ s_{i,j}<r;

(3.59)

\displaystyle f_{i,j}^{(r)}:=\{f_{j-1}^{(s_{j,j-1}+1)},f_{i,j-1}^{(r-s_{j,j-1})}\}\text{ for }1\leq i<j\leq n,\ s_{j,i}<r.

The shifted Yangian admits a Poisson grading
y_{n}(\sigma)=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}y_{n}(\sigma)_{r}
, which we call the
canonical grading
. It places
d_{i}^{(r)},e_{i}^{(r)},f_{i}^{(r)}
in degree
r
and the bracket lies in degree
-1
, meaning

\{\cdot,\cdot\}:y_{n}(\sigma)_{r}\times y_{n}(\sigma)_{s}\to y_{n}(\sigma)_{r+s-1}
.
There is also an important Poisson filtration
y_{n}(\sigma)=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{F}_{i}y_{n}(\sigma)
, called the

, which places
d_{i}^{(r)},e_{i}^{(r)},f_{i}^{(r)}
in degree

. With respect to this filtration, the bracket is in degree
0

\operatorname{F}_{r}y_{n}(\sigma)\times\operatorname{F}_{s}y_{n}(\sigma)\to\operatorname{F}_{r+s}y_{n}(\sigma)
. The associated graded Poisson algebra is denoted
\operatorname{gr}y_{n}(\sigma)
.

The following theorem is a semiclassical analogue of
[
BK06
, Theorem 2.1]
, which we ultimately deduce from the noncommutative setting.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\sigma
be a symmetric shift matrix. There is a Poisson isomorphism
S(\mathfrak{c}_{n}(\sigma))\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{gr}y_{n}(\sigma)
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}e_{i;r-1}\longmapsto e_{i}^{(r)}+\operatorname{F}_{r-2}y_{n}(\sigma)&\text{ for }&1\leq i<n,\ s_{i,i+1}<r;\\
f_{i;r-1}\longmapsto f_{i}^{(r)}+\operatorname{F}_{r-2}y_{n}(\sigma)&\text{ for }&1\leq i<n,\ s_{i+1,i}<r;\\
d_{i;r-1}\longmapsto d_{i}^{(r)}+\operatorname{F}_{r-2}y_{n}(\sigma)&\text{ for }&1\leq i\leq n,\ r<0.\end{array}

As a consequence
y_{n}(\sigma)
is isomorphic to the polynomial algebra on infinitely many variables

(3.64)

\begin{array}[]{c}\{d_{i}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\ 0<r\}\cup\{e_{i,j}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,\ s_{i,j}<r\}\\
\cup\,\{f_{i,j}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,\ s_{j,i}<r\}.\end{array}

Proof.

Comparing the top graded components of the relations (
3.43
)–(
3.54
) with respect to the loop filtration, with relations (
3.12
)–(
3.21
), it is straightforward to see that (
3.4
) gives a surjective Poisson homomorphism. To prove the theorem we demonstrate that the ordered monomials in the elements (
3.64
) are linearly independent.

Consider the set

X:=\{E_{i}^{(r)},F_{i}^{(s)},D_{j}^{(t)}\mid 1\leq i<n,\ 1\leq j\leq n,\ s_{i,i+1}<r,\ s_{i+1,i}<s,\ 0<t\}.

In
[
BK06
, §2]
the shifted Yangian

is defined as a quotient of the free algebra

by the ideal generated by the relations
[

, (2.4)–(2.15)]
. Let

be the free Lie algebra on

and define a grading

by placing

so that

, §5]

is equipped with a Poisson structure in the usual manner. Comparing relations (
3.43
)–(
3.54
) with the top graded components of relations
[

we see that the Poisson surjection

factors through

. As a result there is a surjective Poisson homomorphism

. Following
[

, (2.18), (2.19)]
we introduce elements

of

lying in filtered degree

) we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\pi(e_{i,j}^{(r)})&=&E_{i,j}^{(r)}+\operatorname{F}^{\prime}_{r-1}Y_{n}(\sigma);\\
\pi(f_{i,j}^{(r)})&=&F_{i,j}^{(r)}+\operatorname{F}^{\prime}_{r-1}Y_{n}(\sigma).\\

By

the ordered monomials in

∎

3.4.
The Dirac reduction of the shifted Yangian

In this section we suppose that
\sigma

4.1.
The symmetric shift matrix and the centraliser of a nilpotent element

Choose a partition

We recalled the notion of a shift matrix in Section

]

symmetric shift matrix for

\sigma=(s_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

(4.1)

\displaystyle s_{i,j}:=\frac{|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Introduce symbols
\{b_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\ 1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\}

with the vector space spanned by the

\displaystyle[e_{i_{1},j_{1};k_{1},l_{1}},e_{i_{2},j_{2};k_{2},l_{2}}]=\delta_{k_{1},i_{2}}\delta_{l_{1},j_{2}}e_{i_{1},j_{1};k_{2},l_{2}}-\delta_{k_{2},i_{1}}\delta_{l_{2},j_{1}}e_{i_{2},j_{2};k_{1},l_{1}}

\displaystyle e:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}-1}e_{i,j;i,j+1}

. We refer to the grading

[

]
. It satisfies

\displaystyle\deg(e_{i,j;k,l})=2(l-j)+\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k}.

and, in particular,

.

For

and

\displaystyle c_{i,k}^{(r)}=\sum_{2r=2(l-j)+\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k}}e_{i,j;k,l}

Lemma 4.1

4.2.
Symplectic and orthogonal subalgebras

Consider the matrix

(4.10)

\displaystyle J:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}(-1)^{j}e_{i,j;i,\lambda_{i}+1-j}

This block diagonal matrix can be described as follows. For each index

Now define an involution of

\displaystyle\tau:X\mapsto-J^{-1}X^{\top}J

Lemma 4.2

For all admissible indexes we have:

(ii)

(iii)

\tau(c_{i,k}^{(r)})=(-1)^{r-\frac{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i}}{2}-1}c_{k,i}^{(r)}.

Using (
4.11

4.5
) and (

∎

We decompose

\mathfrak{sp}_{N}&\text{ if }\varepsilon=-1.\par\end{array}\right.

By Lemma

As

5.1.
Conic degenerations of affine schemes

We begin the second Part of the paper by recording two basic results, allowing us to compare the number of irreducible components of certain complex schemes of finite type. The first records an obvious bound on the dimensions of components of subschemes, whilst the second allows us to bound the number of components of a deformation of a reduced conic affine scheme.

We define
\mathcal{P}
be the set of all finite sequences of all non-negative integers of arbitrary length. We order
\mathcal{P}
lexicographically as follows: if
d=(d_{1},...,d_{n})
and
d^{\prime}=(d_{1}^{\prime},...,d_{m}^{\prime})
then we say that
d>d^{\prime}
if there is an index
j\in\{1,...,\min(n,m)\}
such that
d_{i}=d^{\prime}_{i}
for
i=1,...,j
and
d_{j+1}>d^{\prime}_{j+1}
. Here we adopt the convention
d_{m+1}^{\prime}=d_{n+1}=0

If
X
is a complex scheme of finite type then we write
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}X_{i}
for the decomposition into irreducible components, ordered so that
\dim X_{i}\geq\dim X_{j}
for
i<j
. We define
the dimension vector of
X
to be the sequence
d(X):=(\dim X_{1},\dim X_{2},...,\dim X_{l})\in\mathcal{P}
.

The following useful fact will be used in several later arguments.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X,Y
be complex schemes of finite type with a closed embedding
Y\to X
. Then
d(X)\geq d(Y)
with equality if and only if the underlying reduced schemes are isomorphic.

Let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. We say that
A=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{F}_{i}A
is a
standard filtration of
A
if there is a surjection
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]\twoheadrightarrow A
for some
n
, along with integers
m_{1},...,m_{n}\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{F}_{m}A
is spanned by the image of
\{x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}}\mid k_{1}m_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}m_{n}\leq m\}
. If
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is the associated affine scheme the we refer to
\mathbb{C}X=\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}A)
as the
asymptotic cone of
X
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\mathbb{C}X
is reduced then
\hash\operatorname{Comp}(X)\leq\hash\operatorname{Comp}(\mathbb{C}X)
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{gr}A
is reduced then so is
A
. We begin by choosing a presentation for the standard filtration. Let
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}
be a graded polynomial ring with
x_{i}
in degree
m_{i}
. Write
\operatorname{F}_{i}R=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{i}R_{j}
.
We let
\phi:R\to A
be the homomorphism inducing the standard filtration, set
I:=\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
and denote the minimal prime ideals over
I
by
\mathfrak{p}_{1},...,\mathfrak{p}_{m}\subseteq A
, so that
I=\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Using
[
MR01
, Proposition 7.6.13]
, for example, we see that there is a natural isomorphism
\operatorname{gr}A\cong R/\operatorname{gr}I
and we view
X
and
\mathbb{C}X
as subschemes of
\operatorname{Spec}R=\mathbb{A}^{n}
.

For
g\in\operatorname{F}_{i}R
we write
\bar{g}=g+\operatorname{F}_{i-1}R\in\operatorname{gr}R
. For any ideal
J
of
R
we write
V(J)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n}
for the corresponding variety of closed points. We have inclusions
\operatorname{gr}(J)\operatorname{gr}(K)\subseteq\operatorname{gr}(JK)\subseteq\operatorname{gr}(J)\cap\operatorname{gr}(K)
for any ideals
J,K\subseteq R
(see
[
Pr02
, §5.3]
), and so

(5.1)

\displaystyle V(\operatorname{gr}I)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}V(\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{p}_{i}).

We prove the contrapositive of the lemma, so assume that
m=\hash\operatorname{Comp}(X)>\hash\operatorname{Comp}(\mathbb{C}X)
. Then, a fortiori, we must have
V(\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{p}_{j})\subseteq\bigcup_{i\neq j}V(\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{p}_{i})
for some
j
, say
j=1
. Equivalently
\bigcap_{i\neq 1}\sqrt{\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\subseteq\sqrt{\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
, which implies
\bigcap_{i\neq 1}{\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\subseteq\sqrt{\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
. Since
I\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{2}\mathfrak{p}_{3}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{m}
it follows that
\operatorname{gr}I\subsetneq\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{p}_{2}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{m})
and so we may choose
g_{2},...,g_{m}
with
g_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
such that
g:=g_{2}\cdots g_{m}
satisfies
\bar{g}\notin\operatorname{gr}I
. On the other hand, we have
\bar{g}\in\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{p}_{2}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{m})\subseteq\bigcap_{i=2}^{m}\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\sqrt{\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})}=\sqrt{\operatorname{gr}(I)}
, where the last equality follows from (
5.1
). Thus we conclude that
\bar{g}\in\sqrt{\operatorname{gr}(I)}\setminus\operatorname{gr}I
, so that
\mathbb{C}X
is not reduced. This completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Completions and nilpotent elements of graded algebras

If
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)
then, as usual,
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the localisation and
A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\!\wedge}
the completion at the maximal ideal of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The kernel of
A\to A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the set of elements annihilating some element of
A\setminus\mathfrak{p}
, whilst the kernel of
A\to A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\!\wedge}
is
\bigcap_{k>0}\mathfrak{p}^{k}
. When
\mathfrak{p}
is a maximal ideal it follows from Krull’s intersection theorem that these kernels are equal (see the remark following
[
AM69
, Theorem 10.17]
).

Now let
A=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
be a finitely generated, connected graded algebra with unique maximal graded ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
. The connected grading induces a one parameter family of automorphisms of
A
, and a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
\operatorname{Max}(A)
contracting to the unique fixed point
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
. For
a\in A
make the notation

\Xi_{a}:=\Big\{\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(A)\mid a\notin\operatorname{Ker}(A\to A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge})\Big\}.

Lemma 5.3
.

The following hold:

(1)

\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}}\bigcap_{i>0}\mathfrak{m}^{i}=0
where the intersection is taken over all maximal ideals of
A
.

(2)

If
a\in A_{i}
for some
i
then
\Xi_{a}
is closed and conic for the contracting
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action.

Proof.

Since
A
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{C}
the intersection
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(A)}\mathfrak{m}=\operatorname{Rad}(A)
is a graded ideal, however for every
i>0
we have
A_{i}\cap\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{i+1}=0
and so (1) follows.

If
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(A)
, we remarked above that the kernel of
A\to A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
is equal to the kernel of
A\to A_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Writing
\operatorname{Ann}(a)=\{b\in A\mid ab=0\}
, it follows that
\operatorname{Ker}(A\to A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge})=\{a\in A\mid ab=0\text{ for some }b\notin\mathfrak{m}\}=\{a\in A\mid\operatorname{Ann}(a)\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}\}
. We have shown that
\Xi_{a}=\{\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(A)\mid\operatorname{Ann}(a)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\}
, i.e.
\Xi_{a}
is the subvariety of
\operatorname{Max}(A)
cut out by
\operatorname{Ann}(a)
, hence closed. Finally it remains to see that
\Xi_{a}
is stable under the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action, and this follows directly from two facts:
\operatorname{Ann}(a)
is a graded ideal whenever
a
is homogeneous, and
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-stable sets are precisely those defined by graded ideals.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
A=\bigoplus_{i>0}A_{i}
is a finitely generated, connected graded algebra with unique maximal graded ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

A
is reduced.

(ii)

A_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge}
is reduced.

(iii)

A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
is reduced for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)
.

Furthermore if
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}A
is reduced then these equivalent conditions hold.

Proof.

We prove (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) by contraposition. So suppose that
0\neq f\in\operatorname{Rad}(A)
is a nonzero element. Without loss of generality we may assume
f
is homogeneous. By Lemma
5.3
(1) we have
\operatorname{Ker}(A\to\prod_{\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(A)}A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge})=0
, which means that
f
maps to a nonzero element of
A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
for some
\mathfrak{m}
, implying
\mathfrak{m}\in\Xi_{f}
. Using Lemma
5.3
(2) we see
\Xi_{f}
is conic and closed, so it must contain
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
, which implies that
A_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge}
has nilpotent elements. This proves (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).

To see (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) we first of all observe that the property of being reduced is preserved by localisation for any commutative ring, and then apply
[
Ma86
, §32, Remark 1]
. Clearly (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii).

We prove that if
A
is non-reduced then
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}A
is non-reduced. Let
0\neq f\in\operatorname{Rad}(A)
and suppose (without loss of generality) that
f
is homogeneous. There are two cases: either
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{i}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{i+1}
for some
i>0
, or
f\in\bigcap_{i>0}\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{i}
. In the first case
f+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{i+1}
is a nonzero nilpotent element of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}A
, which proves that
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}A
is non-reduced.

We now show that the second case cannot happen. Suppose
f\in\bigcap_{i>0}\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{i}=\operatorname{Ker}(A\to A_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge})
, or equivalently,
\mathfrak{m}_{0}\notin\Xi_{f}
. By Lemma
5.3
(2) we must have
\Xi_{f}=\emptyset
which implies that
f\in\operatorname{Ker}(A\to A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge})
for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
. By Lemma
5.3
(1) we see
f\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}}\bigcap_{i>0}\mathfrak{m}^{i}=0
. This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

5.3.
Commutative quotients of Poisson schemes

If

is a Poisson algebra then the

is the largest Poisson commutative quotient of

. It is constructed by factoring by the ideal generated by

.

When

is a complex manifold it can be stratified into immersed submanifolds known as symplectic leaves (see
[
We83
]
for an introduction). Therefore the closed points of a regular complex Poisson scheme can be decomposed into a disjoint union of symplectic leaves. More generally the closed points of a singular affine Poisson scheme can be decomposed into symplectic leaves by an iterative procedure
[
BG03
, §3.5]
.

The stratification by leaves can be coarsened to a stratification by rank, which is especially transparent in the case of affine schemes: if
A
is finitely generated by elements
x_{1},...,x_{n}
and carries a Poisson structure, then we can form the matrix
\pi=(\{x_{i},x_{j}\})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
and consider the subschemes
X_{0}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X
where
X_{k}
is defined by the ideal of
A
generated by all
(k+1)\times(k+1)
minors of
\pi
.

It follows from
[
BG03
, Proposition 3.6]
that the locally closed set
X_{k}\setminus X_{k-1}
is the union of all symplectic leaves of dimension
k
. In particular
X_{0}
is the union of symplectic leaves of dimension zero. Note that
X_{0}=\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\operatorname{ab}})
by definition. These observations prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

The following reduced subschemes of
X
coincide:

(1)

\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\operatorname{ab}})_{\operatorname{red}}
;

(2)

The union of all zero dimensional symplectic leaves of
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
.
\hfill\qed

If
A
is a Poisson algebra and
I
is a Poisson ideal then the completion
A_{I}^{\!\wedge}
can be equipped with a Poisson structure in a unique way such that
A\to A_{I}^{\!\wedge}
is Poisson: each
A/I^{i}
is a Poisson algebra and
A_{I}^{\!\wedge}
is an inverse limit in the category of Poisson algebras.

The next lemma states that Poisson abelianisation and completion commute.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
A
be finitely generated and Poisson, and pick
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(A^{\operatorname{ab}})
. Then there is a natural isomorphism

(A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\wedge})^{\operatorname{ab}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}(A^{\operatorname{ab}})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\wedge}.

Proof.

According to Lemma
5.5
the point
\mathfrak{m}
is a zero dimensional symplectic leaf, and it follows that
\mathfrak{m}
is a Poisson ideal. Hence
A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
is a Poisson algebra. We let
B
denote the ideal of
A
generated by the Poisson brackets
\{A,A\}
, let
\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}
be the ideal of
A^{\!\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}
generated by
\{A^{\!\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}},A^{\!\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}\}
and let
B_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
be the ideal
A^{\!\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{A}B
, which we identify with an ideal of
A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
using
[
AM69
, Proposition 10.13]
. Considering the exact sequence of
A
-modules
B\to A\to A^{\operatorname{ab}}
, we deduce
(A^{\operatorname{ab}})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}\cong A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}/B_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
from
[
AM69
, Proposition 10.12]
.

We must show that
B_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}=\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. We certainly have an inclusion
B_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}\subseteq\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}
and so the claim will follow from the universal property of
(A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge})^{\operatorname{ab}}
if we can show that
A_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}/B_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
is an abelian Poisson algebra. Since this is isomorphic to
(A^{\operatorname{ab}})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\!\wedge}
, which is a projective limit of abelian Poisson algebras, the claim follows.
∎

6.
Sheets and induction

6.1.
Lusztig–Spaltenstein induction and sheets

Let
G
be a complex connected reductive group and
P\subseteq G
a parabolic subgroup with Levi decomposition
P=LR
, where
R
denotes the unipotent radical, and write
\mathfrak{l}=\operatorname{Lie}(L)
,
\mathfrak{r}=\operatorname{Lie}(R)
. If
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is any quasi-affine subvariety then we write
\mathfrak{v}_{\operatorname{reg}}
for the set of elements
v\in\mathfrak{v}
such that
\dim\operatorname{Ad}(G)v
attains the maximal value.

For any choice of nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
and
z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
it is not hard to see that
\operatorname{Ad}(G)(z+\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r})
contains a dense
G
-orbit. This orbit is
(Lusztig–Spaltenstein) induced from
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0},z)
and is denoted
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l},z}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
. Remarkably the induced orbit depends only on the
G
-conjugacy class of
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0},z)
, not on the choice of
P
admitting
L
as a Levi factor
[
Lo22
, Lemma 4.1]
. We call this
G
-orbit an
induction datum
, and when
z=0
we say that the orbit of
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})
is the induction datum with induced orbit
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
, suppressing
z
.

If an orbit cannot be obtained by induction from a proper Levi subalgebra then it is called
rigid
, otherwise it is called
induced
. Rigid orbits are necessarily nilpotent. We say that an induction datum
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})
is
rigid
if
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is a rigid orbit in
\mathfrak{l}
. When
G
is almost simple of classical type the conjugacy classes of Levi subalgebras and nilpotent orbits can be described combinatorially, and induction can be totally understood in terms of partitions
[
CM93
, §7]
.

The sheets of a Lie algebra are the irreducible components of the rank varieties which consist of points whose orbit has a fixed dimension. These were classified in terms of rigid induction data by Borho
[
Bo81
]
. For each induction datum
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})
define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0}):=\operatorname{Ad}(G)(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{\operatorname{reg}}+\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r})

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0}):=\overline{\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})}_{\operatorname{reg}}.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
Bo81
, Satz 4.3, Satz 5.6]

The sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
are the sets
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})
where
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})
varies over conjugacy classes of rigid data. Furthermore
\dim\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})=\dim\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\dim\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
.

6.2.
Classical finite
W
-algebras and Slodowy slices

Resume the notation
\kappa,\{e,h,f\},\chi
used in Section
2.2
, so
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}(i)
is the
\operatorname{ad}(h)
-grading.

The
Slodowy slice
is the affine variety
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
. Using
\kappa
we can identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as
G
-modules, so that
S(\mathfrak{g})
is identified with the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]
. Then
S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
is identified with the defining ideal of

e+\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\kappa(x-e,\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1}))=0\}=e+\mathfrak{g}(\leq 1).

It follows from
[
GG02
, Lemma 2.1]
that the adjoint action map of
G
restricts to an isomorphism

G(\operatorname{\!<\!0})\times e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}e+\mathfrak{g}(\leq 1).

Via this isomorphism we identify
S(\mathfrak{g},e)=\mathbb{C}[e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}]
, and thus we equip
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
with a Poisson structure.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G
be the cocharacter with differential
d_{1}\gamma(1)=h
. There is a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
\mathfrak{g}
given by
t\mapsto t^{2}\operatorname{Ad}(\gamma(t)^{-1})
which restricts to a contracting
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
. The grading on
\mathbb{C}[e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}]
induced by this action coincides with the Kazhdan grading on
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
.

Thanks to
[
GG02
, §3.2]
we know that the symplectic leaves of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
coincide with the irreducible components of the intersection of adjoint orbits with the slice. Since
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
is Poisson graded in degree
-2
this shows that the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action permutes the symplectic leaves of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
.

6.3.
The Katsylo variety and the tangent cone

The Slodowy slice
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
reflects the local geometry of
\mathfrak{g}
in a neighbourhood of the adjoint orbit of
e
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},...,\mathcal{S}_{l}
be the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
e
. Recall from the introduction that we define the
Katsylo variety
to be

(6.1)

\displaystyle e+X:=(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})\cap\bigcup_{i=1}\mathcal{S}_{i}.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
denote the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{C}[e+X]
corresponding to
e
.
One of the main tools in this paper is the tangent cone
\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)=\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}\mathbb{C}[e+X])
, which we equip with the reduced scheme structure.

Proposition 6.2
.

(1)

\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\operatorname{red}}=e+X
as reduced schemes.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a classical Lie algebra. We have bijections

\operatorname{Comp}(\!\ \bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{S}_{i})\overset{1-1}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Comp}(e+X)\overset{1-1}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Comp}\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X).

The first bijection reduces dimension by
\dim\operatorname{Ad}(G)e
whilst the second is dimension preserving.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
we know that the reduced scheme associated to
\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is the union of zero dimensional symplectic leaves of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
. By
[
GG02
, §3.2]
we know that the symplectic leaves of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
are the irreducible components of the non-empty intersections
\mathcal{O}\cap(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})
where
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is an adjoint orbit. Since
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
intersects orbits transversally it follows that the zero dimensional leaves are precisely the components of
\mathcal{O}\cap(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})
where
\dim\mathcal{O}=\dim\operatorname{Ad}(G)e
. Since the contracting
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action permutes the leaves of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
, these are precisely the orbits lying in the sheets containing
e
. This proves (1).

To prove the first bijection in (2) it suffices to show that for every sheet
\mathcal{S}_{i}
the intersection
\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})
is irreducible. It was proven in
[
Im05
, Theorem 6.2]
that
\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})
can be expressed as the image of a morphism from an irreducible variety, which gives the first bijection. The main result of
loc. cit.
shows that the varieties
\mathcal{S}_{i}
are smooth. Since they are smoothly equivalent to
\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})
the latter are also smooth, and so

\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\operatorname{T}_{e}(\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}).

Now the second bijection will follow if we can show that
\operatorname{T}_{e}(\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})
is never contained in
\operatorname{T}_{e}(\mathcal{S}_{j}\cap e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})
for
i\neq j
which is a consequence of the fact that the sheets
\mathcal{S}_{1},...,\mathcal{S}_{l}
intersect transversally at
e

[
Bu
]
. Finally the claims about dimensions follow from the fact that
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
is transversal to adjoint orbits and the sheets are smooth.
∎

7.
Abelian quotients of finite
W
-algebras

7.1.
The finite
W
-algebra

We now recall the definition of the (quantum) finite
W
-algebra. Once again
G
is a connected complex reductive group with simply connected derived subgroup. A nondegenerate trace form on
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted
\kappa
. We pick an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
in
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
and consider the grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}(i)
given by
\operatorname{ad}(h)
-eigenspaces. Write
\chi:=\kappa(e,\cdot)\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Recall the notation
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
and
G(<0)
.

The generalised Gelfand–Graev module is defined to be

Q:=U(\mathfrak{g})/U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}(<\!-1))}\mathbb{C}_{\chi}

where
\mathbb{C}_{\chi}
is the one dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})
afforded by
\chi
. The finite
W
-algebra is defined to be

U(\mathfrak{g},e):=Q^{G(<0)}.

It inherits a natural algebra structure from
U(\mathfrak{g})
via
\bar{u}_{1}\bar{u}_{2}=\overline{u_{1}u_{2}}
where
\bar{u}
denotes the projection of
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
to
Q
, and
\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2}\in Q^{G(<0)}
.

Remark 7.1
.

The finite
W
-algebra is more commonly defined as a quantization of a Hamiltonian reduction with respect to a certain subgroup of
G
lying between
G(\!<\!\!-1)
and
G(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
. In
[
GG02
, Theorem 4.1]
Gan–Ginzburg show that this definition is equivalent to the one given here: in their notation our definition corresponds to the isotropic space
\ell=0
.

The Kazdan filtration on
U(\mathfrak{g})
is defined by placing
\mathfrak{g}(i)
in degree
i+2
. This descends to a non-negative filtration on
Q
and
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
and we write
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)
for the associated graded algebra. The graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)
naturally identifies with a subalgebra of
\operatorname{gr}Q=S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
, and we have the following fundamental fact (see
[
Pr02
, Proposition 6.3]
,
[
GG02
, Theorem 4.1]
).

Lemma 7.2
.

\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)=S(\mathfrak{g},e)
as Kazhdan graded algebras.

One of the main objects of study in this paper is the maximal abelian quotient
U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
, which is defined to be the quotient by the derived ideal which is generated by all commutators
[u,v]
with
u,v\in U(\mathfrak{g},e)
.

The Kazhdan filtration descends to
U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and we write
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
for the associated Kazhdan graded algebra. Our main results describe the structure of the following affine schemes

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e):=\operatorname{Spec}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e):=\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}).

The closed points of the first of these parameterises the one dimensional representations of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
, whilst the second is the asymptotic cone of the first (cf. Section
5.1
).

7.2.
Bounding the asymptotic cone

The next result is equivalent to (
1.3
), and we view it as a version of Premet’s theorem
[
Pr10
, Theorem 1.2]
for the asymptotic cone of
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)
. Our methods are adapted from his work.

Theorem 7.3
.

If
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
is the Lie algebra of a complex reductive group then there is a surjective homomorphism

S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}

which induces an isomorphism of reduced algebras.

First of all we prove the existence of the surjection

Lemma 7.4
.

There is a surjective homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
.

Proof.

Let
\theta:\mathfrak{g}^{e}\to S(\mathfrak{g},e)
be a PBW map coming from Theorem
2.4
and let
\Theta:\mathfrak{g}^{e}\to U(\mathfrak{g},e)
be a filtered lift of
\theta
. Using the Leibniz rule for the biderivation
[\cdot,\cdot]
we see that the derived ideal
D
of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
is generated by the set
\{[\Theta(u),\Theta(v)]\mid u,v\in\mathfrak{g}^{e}\}
. Similarly the bracket ideal
B
in
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
is generated by
\{\{\theta(u),\theta(v)\}\mid u,v\in\mathfrak{g}^{e}\}
. If
u,v
are homogeneous for the
\operatorname{ad}(h)
-grading on
\mathfrak{g}
then the image of
[\Theta(u),\Theta(v)]
in the graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)=S(\mathfrak{g},e)
is
\{\theta(u),\theta(v)\}
, using the identification of Lemma
7.2
. This shows that
B\subseteq\operatorname{gr}D
. Hence we have
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}=S(\mathfrak{g},e)/\operatorname{gr}D\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g},e)/B=S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
.
∎

In order to show that this surjection gives an isomorphism of reduced algebras we use reduction modulo
p
. This process was pioneered by Premet in the theory of
W
-algebras and used systematically to great effect in subsequent work
[
Pr07b
,
Pr10
,
To17
,
PT21
]
. Rather than repeating the details in full we state the important properties of the modular reduction procedure which we need for our proof.

Let
h
denote the greatest Coxeter number as we vary over simple factors of the derived subgroup
(G,G)
.
Pick a Chevalley
\mathbb{Z}
-form
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
and write
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbb{Z}x_{\alpha}

. When
\mathbbm{k}
is an algebraically closed field we let

denote the reductive algebraic group with Lie algebra

denote the algebraic torus in

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbbm{k}

It follows from the observations of

(1)

The Bala–Carter labels of the adjoint orbits of

(2)

(3)

After canonically identifying to cocharacter lattices

\mathfrak{v}
denote a homogeneous complement to

in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}}
. If

for the complex Katsylo variety (

under the

Lemma 7.5
.

For
\operatorname{char}(\mathbbm{k})\gg 0

\displaystyle\operatorname{Comp}\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)

\displaystyle\overset{1-1}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Comp}\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})

Since

(identifying

Lemma 7.6
.

There is a finite, dominant morphism

\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})\to X_{\mathbbm{k}}.

Proof.

Consider the homomorphism
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})\to U(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})^{\operatorname{ab}}
and denote the kernel by
K
. Write
V(K)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})
for the algebraic subvariety defined by
K
. By definition the points of
V(K)
correspond to the
p
-characters
\eta\in\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+\mathfrak{v}^{\vee}
such that
U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})
admits a one dimensional module. In
[
Pr10
, §2.6]
Premet demonstrated the following relationship between the reduced enveloping algebra and reduced finite
W
-algebra

(7.3)

\displaystyle U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}})\cong\operatorname{Mat}_{p^{d_{\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}}}}U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})

where
d_{\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}}:=\frac{1}{2}\dim\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(G_{\mathbbm{k}})\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}
, under the assumption
p\gg 0
. In
[
GT19a
, Remark 9.4]
the isomorphism (
7.3
) was recovered under weaker hypotheses (
p>h
suffices). It follows that the points of
V(K)
correspond to
p
-characters
\eta\in\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+\mathfrak{v}^{\vee}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{*}
such that
U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}})
admits a module of dimension
p^{d_{\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}}}
.

In
[
Pr95
]
Premet provided his first proof of the second Kac–Weisfeiler conjecture (he later refined his argument using
W
-algebras, see Theorem 2.3(ii) and §2.6 of
[
Pr02
]
). From this, we know that every
U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}})
-module has dimension divisible by
p^{d_{\eta}}
. On the other hand,
[
PT21
, Theorem 1.1]
states that every
U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}})
has a module of dimension
p^{d_{\eta}}
. Therefore

V(K)=\{\eta\in\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+\mathfrak{v}^{\vee}\mid\dim\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(G_{\mathbbm{k}})\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}=\dim\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(G_{\mathbbm{k}})\eta\}.

We claim that this set is equal to
(\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+X^{\vee}_{\mathbbm{k}})^{(1)}
. In the current setting,
\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+\mathfrak{v}^{\vee}
admits a contracting
\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
-action defined in the same manner as the one parameter group of automorphisms appearing in Section
6.2
, using the cocharacter determined by the grading in (3) above. This contracting action preserves the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{*}
and this implies the claim.

Let
R
denote the reduced algebra corresponding to
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g},e)/K
. Since
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g},e)=\mathbbm{k}[(\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+\mathfrak{v}^{\vee})^{(1)}]
as Kazhdan filtered algebras we see that
R=\mathbbm{k}[(\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+X_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\vee})^{(1)}]
is also an identification of Kazhdan filtered algebras. Since
\chi_{\mathbbm{k}}+X_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\vee}
is stable under the contracting
\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
-action inducing the Kazhdan grading, it follows that
R\cong\operatorname{gr}R
. We conclude that there is a finite injective algebra homomorphism
R\hookrightarrow\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.3
.

Recall that finite morphisms are closed, and so a finite dominant morphism is surjective. Now it follows from Lemma
7.6
that there is a surjective map
\operatorname{Comp}\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})\to\operatorname{Comp}X_{\mathbbm{k}}
such that for every
Z\in\operatorname{Comp}X_{\mathbbm{k}}
there exists an element of
\operatorname{Comp}\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbbm{k}},e_{\mathbbm{k}})
mapping to
Z
of the same dimension. Combining with Lemma
7.5
we deduce that there is surjection
\operatorname{Comp}\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Comp}(e+X_{\mathbb{C}})
which respects dimensions in the same manner. In particular we have
d(\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e))\geq d(e+X_{\mathbb{C}})
, where
d
denotes the dimension vector defined in Section
5.1
. Thanks to Lemma
5.1
, Proposition
6.2
(1) and Lemma
7.4
the embedding
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}=e+X_{\mathbb{C}}
is an isomorphism on the underlying reduced schemes.
\hfill\qed

The next result follows immediately from Theorem
7.3
.

Corollary 7.7
.

If
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced then so are
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and
U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
.
\hfill\qed

8.
The abelian quotient of the classical
W
-algebra

In this section we study the finite
W
-algebras associated to classical Lie algebras, and so we refresh the notation once again. Let
N>0
is an integer and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
such that
\varepsilon^{N}=1
. We let
G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{N}
be a classical simple algebraic group and
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
such that

\mathfrak{g}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{rc}\mathfrak{so}_{N}&\text{ if }\varepsilon=1,\\
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}&\text{ if }\varepsilon=-1.\end{array}\right.

8.1.
Partitions for nilpotent orbits in classical Lie algebras

We refer to the
G
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of nilpotent elements as
nilpotent orbits
, and similarly for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
. Nilpotent
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
are classified by partitions
\mathcal{P}(N):=\{\lambda\vdash N\}
, where the parts of a partition
\lambda
correspond to the sizes of the Jordan blocks of elements in the orbit.

For each nilpotent
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
the intersection
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}
is either empty or it is a union of either one or two nilpotent
G
-orbits. Therefore an approximate classification of nilpotent
G
-orbits is achieved by describing the partitions
\lambda\vdash N
for which
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}\neq\emptyset
. The set of such partitions is denoted
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
, and they are characterised as follows.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})\vdash N
. Then
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
if and only if there exists an involution
i\mapsto i^{\prime}
on the set
\{1,...,n\}
such that:

(1)

\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i^{\prime}}
for all
i=1,...,n
;

(2)

i=i^{\prime}
if and only if
\varepsilon(-1)^{\lambda_{i}}=1
;

(3)

i^{\prime}\in\{i-1,i,i+1\}
.
\hfill\qed

Whenever we choose
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
we will assume that a choice of involution has been fixed in accordance with Lemma
8.1
. We also adopt the convention that our partitions are non-decreasing
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}
. For completeness we mention that
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
corresponds to one orbit unless
\varepsilon=1
and all parts of
\lambda
are even, which case there are two
G
-orbits of nilpotent elements of
\mathfrak{g}
with Jordan blocks given by
\lambda
and these are permuted by the outer automorphism group of
\mathfrak{g}
, see
[
CM93
, §5.1]
for example.

8.2.
Rigid, singular and distinguished partitions

We introduce three classes of partition which correspond to important families of nilpotent orbit in classical Lie algebras of type
B, C, D
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
and adopt the convention that
\lambda_{0}=0
and
\lambda_{n+1}=\infty
. A
2-step
is a pair of indexes
(i,i+1)
such that:

•

\varepsilon(-1)^{\lambda_{i}}=\varepsilon(-1)^{\lambda_{i+1}}=-1
;

•

\lambda_{i-1}<\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}<\lambda_{i+2}
.

We note that
\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{n}<\lambda_{n+1}
always hold due to our conventions. We write
\Delta(\lambda)
for the set of 2-steps for
\lambda
. A 2-step
(i,i+1)\in\Delta(\lambda)
is called
bad
if one of the following conditions holds:

•

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}>0
is even;

•

\lambda_{i+2}-\lambda_{i+1}>0
is even.

We note that the second condition never holds for
i=n-1
, whilst if the first condition holds for
i=1
then we necessarily have
\varepsilon=-1
. If the first of these two conditions holds then we refer to
i-1
as the
bad boundary
of the 2-step whilst if the second condition holds then
i+2
is the bad boundary. We say that a partition is
singular
if it admits a bad 2-step, and non-singular otherwise.

We say that a partition is
rigid
if the following two conditions hold:

•

\Delta(\lambda)=\emptyset
;

•

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}<2
for all
i=1,...,n
.

We say that a partition is
distinguished
if the following two conditions hold:

•

\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{i+1}
for all
i=1,...,n
;

•

i=i^{\prime}
for all
i
.

The following result may be gathered from
[
PT14
, Theorem 3]
,
[
Ja04
, Lemma 4.2]
and
[
CM93
, Corollary 7.3.9]
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
e
be a nilpotent element with Jordan block sizes given by the partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
in a classical Lie algebra preserving a non-degenerate form
\Psi
on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that
\Psi(u,v)=\varepsilon\Psi(v,u)
for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. The following hold:

(i)

e
lies in a unique sheet of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\lambda
is non-singular.

(ii)

e
is distinguished in the sense of Bala–Carter theory if and only if
\lambda
is distinguished.

(iii)

e
is rigid in the sense of Lusztig–Spaltenstein induction if and only if
\lambda
is rigid.
\hfill\qed

8.3.
The Kempken–Spaltenstein algorithm

Keep fixed a choice of
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
. We say that
i
is an
admissible index for
\lambda
if Case 1 or Case 2 occurs:

Case 1:

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}>1;

Case 2:

\displaystyle(i-1,i)\in\Delta(\lambda)\text{ and }\lambda_{i-1}=\lambda_{i}.

When
i
is an admissible index we define
\lambda^{(i)}\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(N-2(n-i+1))
as follows:

Case 1:

\displaystyle\lambda^{(i)}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_{i}-2,\lambda_{i+1}-2,...,\lambda_{n}-2);

Case 2:

\displaystyle\lambda^{(i)}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{i-2},\lambda_{i-1}-1,\lambda_{i}-1,\lambda_{i+1}-2,...,\lambda_{n}-2)

Now we extend this definition inductively from indexes to sequences. We say that
(i)
is an admissible sequence for
\lambda
if
i
is an admissible index. We inductively extend these definitions to sequences of indexes by saying that
{\bf i}=(i_{1},...,i_{l})
is an
admissible sequence for
\lambda
and define
\lambda^{\bf i}=(\lambda^{(i_{1},...,i_{l-1})})^{(i_{l})}
if
{\bf i}^{\prime}=(i_{1},...,i_{l-1})
is an admissible sequence for
\lambda
and
i_{l}
is an admissible index for
\lambda^{{\bf i}^{\prime}}
. A sequence
{\bf i}
is called
maximal admissible
if
\lambda^{\bf i}
does not admit any admissible indexes. These definitions first appeared in
[
PT14
]
, where the opposite ordering on the parts of
\lambda
was used.

We define
admissible multisets
to be the multisets taking values in
\{1,...,n\}
obtained from admissible sequences by forgetting the ordering. If
{\bf i}
is an admissible sequence then write
[{\bf i}]
for the corresponding admissible multiset.

For
\varepsilon=\pm 1
let
\mathfrak{g}
be a classical Lie algebra in accordance with Lemma
8.2
, and let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent orbit with partition
\lambda
. According to Proposition 8 and Corollary 8 of
[
PT14
]
, for each addmisible multiset
[{\bf i}]
, there is a procedure for choosing:

(1)

a Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}_{\bf i}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{i_{1}}\times\cdots\mathfrak{gl}_{i_{l}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is a classical Lie algebra of the same Dynkin type as
\mathfrak{g}
and natural representation of dimension
N-2\sum i_{j}
.

(2)

a nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\bf i}=\{0\}\times\cdots\{0\}\times\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{\bf i}}
where
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{\bf i}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{+}
has partition
\lambda^{\bf i}
.

These choices satisfy
\mathcal{O}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{\bf i})
. Furthermore
[{\bf i}]\mapsto(\mathfrak{l}_{\bf i},\mathcal{O}_{\bf i})
sets up a one-to-one correspondence between maximal admissible multisets and rigid induction data for
\mathcal{O}
. Combining with Theorem
6.1
this proves.

Proposition 8.3
.

The sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
e
are in bijection with maximal admissible multisets for
\lambda
. The dimension of sheet corresponding to
{\bf i}
is
|{\bf i}|+\dim(\operatorname{Ad}(G)e)
.
\hfill\qed

8.4.
Distinguished elements and induction

The proof of the first main theorem will be reduced to the distinguished case, and the following result is one of the crucial steps.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent orbit with partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
in a classical Lie algebra of rank
r
. There exists a sequence of indexes
i_{1},...,i_{l}
and a classical Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
of rank
r+\sum_{j}(n-i_{j}+1)
of the same Dynkin type as
\mathfrak{g}
such that:

(1)

\mathfrak{gl}_{n-i_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{gl}_{n-i_{l}+1}\times\mathfrak{g}
embeds as a Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
;

(2)

Identifying
\mathcal{O}
with the as a nilpotent orbit
\{0\}\times\cdots\times\{0\}\times\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
we have that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{O})
is a distinguished orbit in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Furthermore we can assume that the first part of the partition of
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{O})
is arbitrarily large.

Proof.

If
i=2,...,n
is an index such that
i^{\prime}=i-1
then we replace
\lambda
by

(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{i-2},\lambda_{i-1}+1,\lambda_{i}+1,\lambda_{i+1}+2,...,\lambda_{n}+2).

By iterating this procedure we can eventually obtain a partition with
i=i^{\prime}
for all
i
. If
i=1,2,...,n
is an index such that
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i-1}
then we replace our new partition by

(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_{i}+2,\lambda_{i+1}+2,...,\lambda_{n}+2).

Iterating this procedure we eventually replace
\lambda
with a distinguished partition
\tilde{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N+\sum_{i\in{\bf i}}2(n-i+1))
where
{\bf i}\subseteq\{1,...,n\}
is some multiset consisting of indexes which were chosen in the above iterations. By applying the latter procedure at index
i=1
we can make the first part of
\tilde{\lambda}
as large as we choose.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
be the classical Lie algebra of the same type as
\mathfrak{g}
with natural representation of dimension
N+\sum_{j\in{\bf i}}2(n-j+1)
. By Lemma
8.2
every orbit with partition
\tilde{\lambda}
is distinguished. Since
\tilde{\lambda}^{\bf i}=\lambda
the remarks of the previous section show that there is a unique orbit
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
with partition
\lambda
and a unique induction datum
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O})
such that
\mathfrak{l}
and
\mathcal{O}
satisfy the properties described in the current proposition, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{O})
.
∎

8.5.
The combinatorial Katsylo variety

In this section we introduce an affine algebraic variety
X_{\lambda}
, determined by a choice of partition, which we call the
combinatorial Katsylo variety.
In Theorem
8.8
we shall use this variety to relate the
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-adic graded algebra of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
with the tangent cone
\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)
of the Katsylo variety, where
\mathfrak{m}_{0}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g},e)
denotes the maximal Kazhdan graded ideal and the orbit of
e\in\mathfrak{g}
has partition
\lambda
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
such that
i=i^{\prime}
for all
i
, all parts of
\lambda
are distinct, and
\lambda_{1}>1
. In particular
\lambda
is distinguished. This condition ensures that
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}>0
is even for all
i=2,...,n
and that
(i,i+1)\in\Delta(\lambda)
for
i=1,...,n-1
. We set

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lcc}s_{1}:=\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\rfloor;&&\\
s_{i}:=\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}}{2}&\text{ for }&i=2,...,n.\end{array}

Introduce a set of variables

(8.4)

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}:=\left\{x_{i,r}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\ 1\leq r\leq s_{i}\right\}\cup\left\{y_{j}\mid j=1,...,n-1\right\}

and we consider the following collection of quadratic elements
Q_{\lambda}
in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[S_{\lambda}]

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}Q_{\lambda}&:=&\{x_{i,1}y_{i}\mid i=1,...,n-1,\ \lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}>0\text{ is even}\}\\
&&\ \ \cup\ \{x_{i+2,1}y_{i},y_{i}y_{i+1}\mid i=1,...,n-2\}.\end{array}

Finally we define
X_{\lambda}
to be the algebraic variety with coordinate ring

(8.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]:=\mathbb{C}[S_{\lambda}]/(Q_{\lambda}).

The main goal of this section is to describe the irreducible components of
X_{\lambda}
, and calculate their dimensions. For this purpose it suffices to consider a special partition: define
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})
by

\displaystyle\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(2,4,...,2n)&\text{ if }\varepsilon=-1;\\
(3,5,...,2n+1)&\text{ if }\varepsilon=1.\end{array}\right.

It follows directly from the definitions that
\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]\cong\mathbb{C}[X_{\alpha}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[x_{i,r}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\ 1<r\leq s_{i}]
, which implies that

(8.12)

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}\cong X_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{A}^{\lfloor\lambda_{n}/2\rfloor-n}.

It is now sufficient to study
X_{\alpha}
in order to understand the structure of
X_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda
.

Let
P_{n}
be the set of subsets
S\subseteq\{1,...,n-1\}
such that for all
i=1,...,n-2
either
i\in S
or
i+1\in S
or both. For
S\in P_{n}
we let
S^{c}:=\{1,...,n-1\}\setminus S
be the complementary set. We define a map from
P_{n}
to the set of subsets of
\{x_{1,1},...,x_{n,1},y_{1},...,y_{n-1}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[X_{\alpha}]
by

(8.13)

\displaystyle\iota:S\mapsto\{y_{i}\mid i\in S\}\cup\{x_{i}\mid i\in S^{c}\text{ or }i-2\in S^{c}\}.

Lemma 8.5
.

S\mapsto(\iota S)
is a bijection from
P_{n}
to the set of minimal prime ideals of
\mathbb{C}[X_{\alpha}]
and

(8.14)

\displaystyle\dim\mathbb{C}[X_{\alpha}]/(\iota S)=|\{x_{1},...,x_{n},y_{1},...,y_{n-1}\}\setminus\iota S|

Proof.

The torus
T:=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2n-1}
acts on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1,1},...,x_{n,1},y_{1},...,y_{n-1}]
by automorphisms rescaling generators, and this action descends to
\mathbb{C}[X_{\alpha}]
. Since
T
is connected it preserves the irreducible components of
X_{\alpha}
, hence preserves the minimal primes of
\mathbb{C}[X_{\alpha}]
. Since the
T
-weight spaces on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1,1},...,x_{n,1},y_{1},...,y_{n-1}]
are one dimensional, it follows that each minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
is generated by
\mathfrak{p}\cap\{x_{1,1},...,x_{n,1},y_{1},...,y_{n-1}\}
.

By inspection we see that for each
S\in P_{n}
the set
\iota S
contains at least one of the factors of each quadratic relation (
8.5
) in
\mathbb{C}[X_{\alpha}]
and is minimal with respect to this property. This implies that
\iota
maps
P_{n}
to minimal primes.

The injectivity of
S\mapsto(\iota S)
is clear and it remains to show that every minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
is in the image. Suppose that
\mathfrak{p}
is generated by some subset
S\subseteq\{x_{1,1},...,x_{n,1},y_{1},...,y_{n-1}\}
. Using the primality of
\mathfrak{p}
it is straightforward to check that
S^{\prime}:=\{1\leq i\leq n-1\mid y_{i}\in S\}\in P_{n}
and
(\iota S^{\prime})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Now by minimality we have
\mathfrak{p}=(\iota S^{\prime})
.
∎

Proposition 8.6
.

There is a bijection
{\bf i}\mapsto X_{\lambda}^{\bf i}
from maximal admissible multisets for
\lambda
to the set of irreducible components of
X_{\lambda}
. Furthermore
\dim X_{\lambda}^{\bf i}=|{\bf i}|.

Proof.

Applying Case 1 of the Kempken–Spaltenstein algorithm exactly
(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1})/2-1
times at each admissible index
i
we reduce the partition
\lambda
to
\alpha
. This shows that the maximal admissible multisets for
\lambda
are of the form
{\bf i}\cup{\bf j}
where
{\bf i}
is a maximal admissible multiset for
\alpha
and
{\bf j}
is the multiset taking values in
\{1,...,n\}
in which
j
occurs with multiplicity
(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j-1})/2-1
. Combining this with (
8.12
) it suffices to prove the current proposition in the case
\lambda=\alpha
.

By Lemma
8.5
the irreducible components of
X_{\alpha}
are parameterised by
P_{n}
. In order to prove the first claim of the proposition we construct a bijection from
P_{n}
to the set of maximal admissible multisets.

The admissible multisets for
\alpha
have multiplicities
\leq 2
. Thanks to
[
PT14
, Lemma 6]
we know that a maximal admissible multiset is totally determined by the collection of indexes in
\{2,...,n\}
which occur with multiplicity 2: this is the set of indexes such that Case 2 occurs at some point in the KS algorithm. Therefore the collection of indexes of multiplicity 2 is only constrained by the fact that if
i
has multiplicity 2 then neither
i-1
nor
i+1
have multiplicity 2. It follows that if
S\in P_{n}
then there is a unique maximal admissible sequence
{\bf i}
for
\alpha
such that the indexes in
\{2,...,n\}\setminus(S+1)
occur with multiplicity 2. This gives the desired bijection.

It remains to prove the claim regarding dimensions. Let
S\subseteq P_{n}
and enumerate
\{1,...,n-1\}\setminus S=(i_{1},...,i_{l})
. By the remarks of the previous paragraph the maximal admissible sequence associated to
S\in P_{n}
is determined as follows: first we let
{\bf i}^{\prime}=(i_{1}+1,i_{1}+1,i_{2}+1,i_{2}+1,...,i_{l}+1,i_{l}+1)
and then we let
{\bf i}
be the maximal admissible sequence obtained from
{\bf i}^{\prime}
by applying Case 1 of the KS algorithm to
\alpha^{{\bf i}^{\prime}}
as many times as possible. The set of indexes where Case 1 can be applied to
\alpha^{{\bf i}^{\prime}}
are precisely

(8.15)

\displaystyle\{1\leq i\leq n\mid\alpha^{{\bf i}^{\prime}}_{i}-\alpha_{i-1}^{{\bf i}^{\prime}}=2\}=\{1\leq i\leq n\mid i\neq i_{t}\text{ and }i-2\neq i_{t}\text{ for all }t\}

and it follows that the length of the maximal admissible sequence associated to
S
is

(8.16)

\displaystyle n-1-|S|+\hash\{1\leq i\leq n\mid i,i-2\in S\}.

According to (
8.13
) and Lemma
8.5
the dimension of the component of
X_{\alpha}
corresponding to
S
is

(8.17)

\displaystyle 2n-1-|S|-\hash\{1\leq i\leq n\mid i\in S^{c}\text{ or }i-2\in S^{c}\}

It is straightforward to see that (
8.16
) and (
8.17
) are equal.
∎

8.6.
The semiclassical abelianisation I: the distinguished case

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
be a distinguished partition satisfying
\lambda_{1}>1
and let

be a nilpotent element with partition

defined in (

) allow us to construct an

\displaystyle s_{i+1,i}=s_{i,i+1}:=s_{i+1}\text{ for }i=1,...,n-1.

By (

in (

). Next we describe generators of

and certain relations between them.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{0}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
be unique maximal Kazhdan graded ideal. Then
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is generated by elements

(8.19)

\displaystyle\{\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\mid i=1,...,n-1\}\cup\{\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2r)}\mid i=1,...,n,\ r=1,...,s_{i}\}

These elements satisfy the following relations

(8.20)

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\overline{\theta}_{i+1}^{(s_{i+2}+1)}=0

for

\displaystyle i=1,...,n-2;

(8.21)

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}_{1}^{(s_{2}+1)}\eta_{1}^{(2s_{1})}=0

for

\displaystyle\varepsilon=-1;

(8.22)

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}(\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2s_{i})}-\bar{\eta}_{i-1}^{(2s_{i})})=0

for

\displaystyle i=2,...,n-1;

(8.23)

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}(\bar{\eta}_{i+2}^{(2s_{i+2})}-\bar{\eta}_{i+1}^{(2s_{i+2})})=0

\displaystyle i=1,...,n-2

where the elements
\eta_{i}^{(r)}
are taken to be zero if they lie outside the range prescribed by (

).

Proof.

As explained in Section
4.5
there is a surjective Poisson homomorphism
\varphi:R(y_{n}(\sigma),\tau)\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g},e)
. In the following proof we abuse notation and identify
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
with the corresponding quotient of

. This justifies the use of generators and relations appearing in Theorem
3.7

We let
\theta_{i,j}^{(r)}
and
\eta_{i}^{(r)}
be the generators of

described in (
3.81

\overline{\theta}_{i,j}^{(r)}:=\theta_{i,j}^{(r)}+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{2}
and
\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(r)}:=\eta_{i}^{(r)}+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{2}\in\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)

It follows from the proofs of Propositions
4.8
and
4.10
that
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)
is generated as a commutative algebra by elements

(8.24)

\displaystyle\{\overline{\theta}_{i,j}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,\ s_{i,j}<r\leq s_{i,j}+\lambda_{i}\}\cup\{\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2r)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\ 0<r\leq\lambda_{i}\}.

Since natural map
\pi:S(\mathfrak{g},e)\twoheadrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is a Kazhdan graded homomorphism the unique maximal graded ideal of

maps to the unique maximal graded ideal of

contains the left-hand sides of (

)–(
8.23

(8.25)

\displaystyle\{\overline{\theta}_{i,j}^{(r)}\mid j\neq i+1\text{ or }r>s_{i+1}+1\}\cup\{\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2r)}\mid r>s_{i}\}.

Since (
8.24

8.19
) and (

Let
B\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g},e)
be the defining ideal of

, which is generated by the Poisson brackets in

. Then the kernel of

is equal to
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}B
. Corollary

\mathfrak{g}^{e}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g}^{e})
. Furthermore the same Corollary also implies that

. We can express this more informally by saying that the linear terms of Poisson brackets in

are just the Lie brackets in

, which is well-known in general (see
[

, Theorem 2.11]
).

Now
[

implies that the elements (
8.25

, although we warn the reader that the notation there is different. It follows that every element of (
8.25

for some

\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}B=\operatorname{Ker}\pi_{0}

In order to complete the proof we show that the left-hand sides of (

)–(

) lie in
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}B
. Using (

\theta_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\theta_{i+1}^{(s_{i+2}+1)}\in B
and it follows that the left-hand side of (

\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}B
.

Now suppose that
i=1
and that
\varepsilon=-1
, so that
\lambda_{1}
is even. Thanks to Proposition
4.10
we know that
\dot{\theta}_{1}^{(\lambda_{1}+s_{1}+1)}=0
in
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
. Using Example
4.9
and (
3.93

\sum_{t=0}^{\lambda_{1}/2}\eta_{1}^{(2t)}\theta_{1}^{(\lambda_{1}-2t+s_{1,2}+1)}-\sum_{t=0}^{\lambda_{1}/2-1}\eta_{1}^{(2t)}\{\eta_{1}^{(2)},\theta_{1}^{(\lambda_{1}-2t+s_{1,2})}\}=\eta_{1}^{(\lambda_{1})}\theta_{1}^{(s_{2}+1)}\in B.

Note that
\lambda_{1}=2s_{1}
in this case, since
\lambda_{1}
is even. Projecting

into
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)
we see that the left-hand side of (
8.21

\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}B
.

The relations (
8.22
), (
8.23
) can be deduced similarly by considering the quadratic terms appearing in (

), (

) and projecting into

. Since the arguments for these two cases are almost identical we just provide the details for (

Before we proceed we record a single relation which is a special case of (

). For
r>s_{i+1}+1
we have

(8.26)

\displaystyle\{\eta_{i}^{(2)},\theta_{i}^{(r-1)}\}=\theta_{i}^{(r)}

Fix

\big\{\theta_{i+1}^{(s_{i+2}+1)},\{\theta_{i+1}^{(s_{i+2}+1)},\theta_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\}\big\}

This completes the proof of relation (

The proof of (

∎

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a classical Lie algebra and
e\in\mathfrak{g}
a distinguished nilpotent element. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{0}\subseteq S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
be the maximal Kazhdan graded ideal. There is an isomorphism

\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)].

In particular
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced.

Proof.

Let
A
denote the algebra with generators (
8.19
) and relations

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\overline{\theta}_{i+1}^{(s_{i+2}+1)}=0&\text{ for }&i=1,...,n-2;\\
\overline{\theta}_{1}^{(s_{2}+1)}\bar{\eta}_{1}^{(2s_{1})}=0&\text{ for }&\varepsilon=-1;\\
\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2s_{i})}=0&\text{ for }&i=2,...,n-1;\\
\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\bar{\eta}_{i+2}^{(2s_{i+2})}=0&\text{ for }&i=1,...,n-3.\end{array}

Before we proceed, we outline the argument. First of all we will show that
A\cong\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]
where
X_{\lambda}
is the combinatorial Katsylo variety from Section
8.5
. By Lemma
8.7
we have that
A\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and since the reduced algebra of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is
\mathbb{C}[e+X]
by Proposition
6.2
(1), we see that
\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)]
. We then combine deductions of the previous sections to see that the components of
X_{\lambda}
and
\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)
have the same dimensions. Since
X_{\lambda}
is reduced we can apply Lemma
5.1
to deduce that we have isomorphisms
\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]\cong\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)]
, from which the current theorem follows.

Step (i):
Comparing (
8.6
) with (
8.5
) we see there is an isomorphism
A\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]
defined by

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2r)}\longmapsto x_{i,s_{i}+1-r};

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}_{i}^{(s_{i+1}+1)}\longmapsto y_{i}.

Step (ii):
Now we show that
A\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
. We adopt the convention
\bar{\eta}_{i}^{2r}=0
when
r>s_{i}
or
i=0
. The algebra endomorphism of
A
defined by
\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2s_{i})}\mapsto\bar{\eta}_{i}^{(2s_{i})}+\bar{\eta}_{i-1}^{(2s_{i})}
for all
i=1,...,n
and acting identically on the other generators, is a unimodular subsitution. Therefore it induces an automorphism. By slight abuse of notation we denote the images of the generators by the same symbols. With this new set of generators the relations are precisely (
8.20
)-(
8.23
). By Lemma
8.7
and part (i) we have a surjection
A=\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
.

Step (iii):
Recall that we equip
\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)
with the reduced scheme structure. By Corollary
4.11
we have

(8.32)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)]

Applying Proposition
6.2
(2), Proposition
8.3
and Proposition
8.6
we have

d(X_{\lambda})=d(e+X)=d(\operatorname{TC}_{e}(e+X)).

Now by Lemma
5.1
we see that the surjections (
8.32
) are isomorphisms on the underlying varieties of closed points. Since
\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]
is reduced these maps are algebra isomorphisms and
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced. Applying Lemma
5.4
we see that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced, as claimed.
∎

8.7.
The semiclassical abelianisation II: the general case

The following result uses the local geometry of Poisson manifolds to infer reducedness of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
from the case where
e
is distinguished, where we can apply Theorem
8.8
.

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a classical simple Lie algebra and let
e\in\mathfrak{g}
be any nilpotent element. Then
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced.

Proof.

Part (i):
In type
A
the fact that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced can be deduced from
[
Pr10
, Theorem 3.3]
, and so we let
\mathfrak{g}
be simple of type
B
,
C
or
D
throughout the proof. We let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be the adjoint orbit of
e
in
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
be the Lie algebra and distinguished nilpotent orbit introduced in Lemma
8.4
. Pick
\tilde{e}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
and an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(\tilde{e},\tilde{h},\tilde{f})
. Write
\widetilde{G}
for the simply connected, connected complex algebraic group with
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{Lie}(\widetilde{G})
. Recall that
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n-i_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{gl}_{n-i_{l}+1}\times\mathfrak{g}
embeds as a Levi subalgebra of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. If we choose
e\in\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
then
\mathcal{O}
identifies with the adjoint
L
-orbit and we have the following Poisson isomorphism

S(\mathfrak{l},e)\cong S(\mathfrak{gl}_{i_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes S(\mathfrak{gl}_{i_{l}})\otimes S(\mathfrak{g},e)

Therefore
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced if and only if
S(\mathfrak{l},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced. This holds if and only only if
(S(\mathfrak{l},e)_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge})^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced, where
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
is the maximal ideal of
e
, thanks to Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.6
.

Part (ii):
Now pick a regular element
s\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
so that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{s}\cong\mathfrak{l}
. Write
x=s+e\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. We will explain below that
x
can be viewed as a point of
\tilde{e}+\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\tilde{f}}=\operatorname{Spec}S(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{e})
, and by slight abuse of notation we will also write
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
for the maximal ideal of
x
in
S(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{e})
. We claim that
S(\mathfrak{l},e)_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge}\cong S(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{e})_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge}
as Poisson algebras. Note that
(S(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{e})_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge})^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced by Lemma
5.4
and Theorem
8.8
. Thanks to part (i) the current proof will follow from the claim.

Part (iii):
Pick an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
for
e
inside
\mathfrak{l}
, then define
y=s+f\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. By
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-theory the affine variety
x+\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{y}
is a transverse slice to the adjoint orbit
\operatorname{Ad}(\widetilde{G})x
at the point
x
. Also pick an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(\tilde{e},\tilde{h},\tilde{f})
in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. After replacing the latter triple by some conjugate we can actually assume that
x\in\tilde{e}+\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\tilde{f}}
. By construction we have
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{O})=\operatorname{Ad}(\widetilde{G})\tilde{e}
. It follows that
x
lies in a sheet of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
containing
\tilde{e}
, and so we have
x\in\tilde{e}+\widetilde{X}
, where the latter variety is defined in the same way as (
6.1
).

Now we choose small neighbourhoods
U_{x}\subseteq\tilde{e}+\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\tilde{f}}
and
V_{x}\subseteq x+\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{y}
of
x
in the complex topologies. Similarly let
W_{e}\subseteq e+\mathfrak{l}^{f}
be a small neighbourhood of
e
. It is well-known that
U_{x},V_{x},W_{e}
all carry transverse Poisson structures on the ring of analytic functions, see
[
LPV13
, §5.3.3]
for example. Write
\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{an}}(M)
for the ring of analytic functions on a complex manifold
M
. There is a natural restriction map
\mathbb{C}[e+\mathfrak{l}^{f}]\to\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{ab}}(W_{e})
which is a Poisson homomorphism. Thanks to
[
Ser55
, Proposition 3]
this induces an isomorphism
S(\mathfrak{l},e)_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{an}}(W_{e})_{e}^{\!\wedge}
of complete Poisson algebras. Similarly we have a Poisson isomorphism
S(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{e})_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{\!\wedge}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{an}}(U_{x})_{x}^{\!\wedge}
.

Now the claim will follow from the existence of an isomorphism
\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{an}}(U_{x})_{x}^{\!\wedge}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{an}}(W_{e})_{e}^{\!\wedge}
of complete Poisson algebras. First of all we observe that
x\in\tilde{e}+\widetilde{X}
implies that both
\tilde{e}+\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\tilde{f}}
and
x+\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{y}
are transverse slices to
\operatorname{Ad}(\widetilde{G})x
at
x
. Therefore by
[
LPV13
, Proposition 5.29]
we have an isomorphism
U_{x}\to V_{x}
of analytic Poisson manifolds sending
x
to
x
. Furthermore by
[
DSV07
, Proposition 2.1]
we see that there is a similar isomorphism
V_{x}\to W_{e}
sending
x
to
e
. This completes the proof.
∎

8.8.
The abelianisation of the finite
W
-algebra via deformation theory

Here we prove the first main theorem (Theorem
1.1
), which states that Premet’s component map (
1.2
) is a bijection. As we explained after the statement of that theorem, the result is know in type
A
and so we must complete the proof for the other classical types.

First of all we apply Proposition
7.7
and Theorem
8.9
to see that both
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and
U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
are reduced. Now apply Lemma
5.2
, Proposition
6.2
(1) and Theorem

to see that

\hash\operatorname{Comp}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)\leq\hash\operatorname{Comp}\mathbb{C}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{g},e)=\hash\operatorname{Comp}(e+X).

Since Premet’s map (

) is surjective and restricts to a dimension preserving bijection from some subset, the theorem follows.

Remark 8.10
.

Premet asked whether the abelian quotient
U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
of a finite
W
-algebra is reduced
[
Pr10
, Question 3.1]
. Combining Proposition
7.7
with Theorem
8.9
we have given an affirmative answer in the case of classical Lie algebras.

The problem of understanding whether
U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced for exceptional Lie algebra is rather subtle. The methods of this paper will certainly not work in general: in the introduction to
[
Pr14
]
it is explained that there are four orbits in exceptional Lie algebra such that the associated finite
W
-algebra is known to admit precisely two one dimensional representations (these correspond to the first four columns of
[
PT21
, Table 1]
). For these
W
-algebras it is not hard to see that the reduced algebra associated to
U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[x]/(x^{2}-1)
as filtered algebras, with the generator
x
in some positive degree. This ensures that
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is not reduced, and Proposition
7.7
implies that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
admits nilpotent elements.

9.
The orbit method

9.1.
Quantizations and deformations of symplectic singularities

We say that an affine Poisson algebra
A
is
graded of degree
d>0
if
A=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
is a graded commutative algebra such that
A_{0}=\mathbb{C}
and the Poisson bracket is in degree
-d
, meaning
\{A_{i},A_{j}\}\subseteq A_{i+j-d}
. In this case the variety
X:=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is
conical
since the grading induces a contacting
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action with unique fixed point
\bigoplus_{i>0}A_{i}\in X
, and the contracting action rescales the Poisson bivector. The smooth locus of
X
will be denoted
X^{\operatorname{reg}}
. From now on we fix such a Poisson algebra
A
.

A
filtered Poisson deformation
of
A
is a pair
(\mathcal{A},\iota)
consisting of a filtered Poisson algebra
\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{A}_{i}
such that the Poisson bracket satisfies
\{\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{j}\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{i+j-d}
and an isomorphism of graded Poisson algebras
\iota:\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}\to A
.

A
filtered quantization
of
A
is pair
(\mathcal{A},\iota)
where
\mathcal{A}
is a not necessarily commutative, filtered algebra satisfying
[\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{j}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{i+j-d}
such that
\iota:\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}\to A
is a Poisson isomorphism (see
[
ACET20
, 2.2]
for example).

Let
B
be a non-negatively graded connected algebra. By a
graded Poisson
B
-algebra
we shall mean a graded
B
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
with Poisson structure such that the structure map
B\to\mathcal{A}
is graded and factors through the Poisson centre. A
graded Poisson deformation of
A
over base
B
is a pair
(\mathcal{A},\iota)
consisting of a flat graded Poisson
B
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and an isomorphism
\iota:\mathcal{A}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}\to A
.

Now let
B
be a connected graded algebra concentrated in non-negative degrees. We can then equip
B
with the obvious filtration whose
j
th filtered piece is the sum of the first
j
graded pieces, and
\operatorname{gr}B\cong B
as algebras. A
filtered
B
-algebra
is a filtered algebra
\mathcal{A}
equipped with a filtered map
B\to\mathcal{A}
which factors through the centre. We say that
(\mathcal{A},\iota)
is a
filtered quantization (of a Poisson deformation) of
A
over base
B
if
\mathcal{A}
is a flat filtered
B
-algebra and
(\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A},\iota)
is a graded Poisson deformation of
A
over
\operatorname{gr}B=B
, and the map
\iota
is an isomorphism
\iota:\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}B}\mathbb{C}\to A
of Poisson algebras.

For more detail on the above definitions, and a precise description of morphisms, we refer the reader to
[
Lo22
, §2.2, 2.3, 3.1]
or
[
ACET20
, §2]
.

Remark 9.1
.

Filtered Poisson deformations of
A
correspond bijectively to graded Poisson deformations of
A
over base
\mathbb{C}[h]
with
h
in degree 1, by the Rees algebra construction
[
Lo22
, §2.3]
.

Whilst surveying the construction of graded Poisson deformations of Poisson algebras, and their quantizations, below we shall occasionally refer to deformations and quantizations of a Poisson scheme
X
(the distinction between a deformation of an algebra and a scheme is important when
X
is not affine). We refer the reader to
[
Lo22
, §2.2]
for a brief introduction to these notions.

Following Beauville
[
Be00
]
, we say that
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
has
symplectic singularities
if:

•

X
is normal;

•

the Poisson bivector
\omega
on
X
has full rank on
X^{\operatorname{reg}}
, which is therefore a smooth symplectic variety;

•

there exists a projective resolution
\widehat{X}\to X
such that the pullback of
\omega|_{X^{\operatorname{reg}}}
extends to a regular (not necessarily symplectic) bivector on
\widehat{X}
.

From now on we also assume that
A=\mathbb{C}[X]
has symplectic singularities and is graded in degree
d>0
. In this case the variety
X
is known as a
conical symplectic singularity
.

Example 9.2
.

Let
G
be a complex reductive group and
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
. If
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent orbit then the affinisation
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{O}]
is a conical symplectic singularity. It is well-known that the affinisation is isomorphic to the normalisation of the closure (see
[
Ja04
, Proposition 8.3]
). The conical structure on
\mathcal{O}
arises from the vector space structure on
\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
Ja04
, Lemma 2.10]
), whilst the presence of symplectic singularities follows from the work of Panyushev
[
Pa91
]
.

Now suppose that
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathcal{O}
is a
G
-equivariant finite cover of a nilpotent orbit. Then the affinisation
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}]
is also a conical symplectic singularity (see
[
Lo18
, Lemma 2.5]
). We remark that the conical structure comes from lifting the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action, and possibly replacing
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with a cover.

A conical symplectic singularity
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
admits a very nice deformation theory: there is a classification of filtered Poisson deformations and filtered quantizations of
A
, due to Namikawa
[
Na10
,
Na11
]
and Losev
[
Lo22
]
, which we now recall.

Recall that a normal variety
\widetilde{X}
is said to be
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial if every Weil divisor admits a non-zero integer multiple which is Cartier. We refer the reader to
[
Lo22
, Proposition 2.3]
and
[
BCMH00
]
for the defining properties of a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalisation.

Let
\widetilde{X}\to X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalisation and define
\mathfrak{P}:=H^{2}(\widetilde{X}^{\operatorname{reg}},\mathbb{C})
. This is known as the
Namikawa–Cartan space
of
X
. Then according to
[
Lo22
, Proposition 2.6]
there exists a universal graded Poisson deformation of
\widetilde{X}
over the base
\mathfrak{P}
, which should be understood as scheme-theoretic deformation, in the sense of
[
Lo22
, §2.2]
. This means the following: if
\widetilde{X}_{B}
is a graded Poisson deformation of
\widetilde{X}
over
\operatorname{Spec}(B)
there exists a unique
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariant morphism
\operatorname{Spec}(B)\to\mathfrak{P}
and a (non-unique) isomorphism
\operatorname{Spec}(B)\times_{\mathfrak{P}}\widetilde{X}_{\mathfrak{P}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\widetilde{X}_{B}
. In other words every graded Poisson deformation of
\widetilde{X}
can be obtained from
\widetilde{X}_{\mathfrak{P}}
by base change.

A universal graded Poisson deformation of
A
can be produced from
\widetilde{X}_{\mathfrak{P}}
. First of all we describe an important finite group
W_{X}
associated to
X
which controls isomorphisms of deformations. The following facts are explained in more detail in
[
Lo22
, §2.3]
. A result of Kaledin
[
Ka06
]
states that
X
has only finitely many symplectic leaves. Let
\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{k}
be the collection of leaves of codimension 2. Another of the main results of
op. cit.
states that there exists a formal slice
\Sigma_{i}
to each leaf
\mathcal{L}_{i}
. By
[
Be00
, Proposition 1.3]
we know that
X
has rational singularities, and so the formal neigbourhood of
0\in\Sigma_{i}
is a Du Val singularity, i.e. the formal completion of a rational double point of type ADE. Let
\widetilde{W}_{i}
be the Weyl group associated to the Dynkin diagram of the singularity
\Sigma_{i}
. The fundamental group of
\mathcal{L}_{i}
acts on
\widetilde{W}_{i}
by diagram automorphisms and we let
W_{i}
denote the fixed points. The
Namikawa–Weyl group
of
X
is
W_{X}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}W_{i}
.

We are now ready to describe a Poisson deformation of
X
with a remarkable property. Let
X_{\mathfrak{P}}:=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{X}_{\mathfrak{P}}]
and
W=W_{X}
.

Proposition 9.3
.

[
Lo22
, Proposition 2.9, Proposition 2.12, Corollary 2.13]

The algebras
A_{\mathfrak{P}}:=\mathbb{C}[X_{\mathfrak{P}}]^{W}
and
B:=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]^{W}
satisfy the following properties:

(1)

A_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a finitely generated algebra and a free
B
-module.

(2)

A_{\mathfrak{P}}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}=A
where
\mathbb{C}
denotes the unique one dimensional graded
B
-module.

(3)

For every finitely generated positively graded, connected algebra
B^{\prime}
and graded Poisson
B^{\prime}
algebra
A^{\prime}
such that
A^{\prime}\otimes_{B^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}[X]
there exists a unique graded algebra homomorphism
B\to B^{\prime}
and a (not necessarily unique)
B^{\prime}
-linear graded Poisson isomorphism

(9.1)

\displaystyle A\otimes_{B}B^{\prime}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}A^{\prime}

which intertwines the isomorphisms
A\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}[X]\cong A^{\prime}\otimes_{B^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}
.

We call
A_{\mathfrak{P}}
the universal graded Poisson deformation of
A
.

Similar to the situation for Poisson deformations, the smooth locus of the
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalisation
\widetilde{X}
of
X
admits a “universal quantization” (Cf.
[
Lo22
, Proposition 3.1]
). This is a sheaf of filtered, flat
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]
-algebras in the conical topology on
\widetilde{X}
. The pushforward of this sheaf of algebras via
\widetilde{X}^{\operatorname{reg}}\to\widetilde{X}
is denoted
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}}

[
Lo22
, Corollary 3.2]
.

It follows that there is a map from
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]
to the global sections of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}}
. The algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]
is graded whilst
\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}})
is filtered. With respect to the natural filtration induced on
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]
the map
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]\to\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}})
is strictly filtered in the sense of
[
ACET20
, §2.2]
.

Lemma 9.4
.

[
Lo22
, Proposition 3.3]

Write
W=W_{X}
. We have the following:

(1)

\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}})
is a filtered quantization of
\mathbb{C}[X_{\mathfrak{P}}]
and the associated graded homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]\to\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}})
is
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]\to\mathbb{C}[X_{\mathfrak{P}}]
, appearing in Proposition
9.3
.

(2)

W
acts on
\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}})
by filtered automorphisms preserving
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]
. The induced action on
\operatorname{gr}(\mathbb{C}[X_{\mathfrak{P}}])=\mathbb{C}[X_{\mathfrak{P}}]
coincides with the action on
\mathbb{C}[X_{\mathfrak{P}}]
.

Crucially the filtered quantizations of Poisson deformations of
A
are classified by the same space as the graded Poisson deformations of
A
.

Proposition 9.5
.

[
Lo22
, Proposition 3.5]

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}:=\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{P}})^{W}
and
B:=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{P}]^{W}
. Let
B^{\prime}
be any finitely generated, positively graded commutative algebra and let
A^{\prime}
be a graded, flat Poisson
B^{\prime}
-algebra such that
A^{\prime}\otimes_{B^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}=A
. Also let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be a
B^{\prime}
-algebra which is filtered quantization of
A^{\prime}
, such that the associated graded map of
B^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is
B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}
. Then there is a unique filtered algebra homomorphism
B\to B^{\prime}
and a (not necessarily unique) isomorphism

(9.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}\otimes_{B}B^{\prime}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}A^{\prime}

Furthermore the associated graded homomorphism of (
9.2
) is (
9.1
).

We call
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}
the
universal filtered quantization of Poisson deformations of
A
.

For clarity we remark that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is not a filtered quantization of
A
, rather it is a filtered quantization of
\mathbb{C}[X_{\mathfrak{P}}]
. However filtered quantizations of
A
can be obtained by specialising
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}
over points of
\mathfrak{P}
(Cf. Remark
9.1
).

9.2.
Birational induction and the orbit method

Let
G
be a connected complex reductive algebraic group and write
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
. In this section we recap some ideas from
[
Lo22
, §4 & §5]
and explain the construction of the orbit method map.

We identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
in what follows, and so
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]
is equipped with a natural Poisson structure. Furthermore the action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
is Hamiltonian.

Recall from Section
6.1
that an
induction datum
is a triple
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0},z)
consisting of a Levi subalgebra, a nilpotent orbit therein and
z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
. When
z=0
we usually omit this element, and say that
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})
is an induction datum. After fixing a datum we can choose a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{Lie}(P)
admitting
\mathfrak{l}
as a Levi factor, with
\mathfrak{r}=\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{p})
, and construct a homogeneous bundle
G\times^{P}(z+\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{0}+\mathfrak{r})
over
G/P
. This bundle admits a map to
\mathfrak{g}
via the adjoint representation of
G
, which we denote
\pi
. The image of
\pi
is the closure of an adjoint orbit, and this orbit depends only on the induction datum, not on the choice of
P

[
Lo22
, Lemma 4.1]
. This is the
(Lusztig–Spaltenstein) induced orbit
, denoted
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l},z}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
.

•

When
\pi
is generically finite onto its image we say that the orbit datum is birational. We say that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l},z}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
is
birationally induced
from
(z,\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0})

•

Furthermore we say that
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is birationally rigid if it cannot be birationally induced from a proper Levi subalgebra.

•

If
\pi
is birational and
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is birationally rigid then we say that the datum is
birationally minimal
.

One of the key properties of birational induction is the following.

Theorem 9.6
.

[
Lo22
, Theorem 4.4]

For every orbit
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
there is a unique birationally minimal induction datum
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0},z)
such that
\mathcal{O}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l},z}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
.

For background we refer the reader to
[
Am20
]
, where the theory of birational induction was studied in the setting of conjugacy classes in groups.

Now pick an induction datum such that
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
is birationally rigid. Also choose a parabolic subgroup
P
such that
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{Lie}(P)
admits
\mathfrak{l}
as a Levi factor and nilradical
\mathfrak{r}
. Let
X_{0}:=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{O}_{0}]
be the affinisation of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
(this coincides with the normalisation of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, by
[
Ja04
, Proposition 8.3]
) and consider the bundle
G\times^{P}(X_{0}\times\mathfrak{r})
. This contains a dense open orbit, say
G/H
for some subgroup
H
, and this is a finite
G
-equivariant cover of
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
. Thanks to Example
9.2
the affinisation
X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[G/H]
is a conical symplectic singularity. In this setting one can describe the Namikawa–Cartan space, the Namikawa–Weyl group of
X
and the universal graded Poisson deformation of
X
.

Lemma 9.7
.

[
Lo22
, Proposition 2.9 & 4.7]

If
G
is semisimple then:

(1)

\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
;

(2)

W_{X}
is a normal subgroup of
W(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0}):=Z_{N}(\mathcal{O}_{0})/L
where
N=N_{G}(L)
is the normaliser of
L
, and
Z_{N}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
denotes the set of elements of
N
which stabilise the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}
.

(3)

The scheme
\widetilde{X}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is isomorphic to

\check{X}:=G\times^{P}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})\times X_{0}\times\mathfrak{r})
as a Poisson scheme over
\mathfrak{P}
with
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action.

We note that the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
\check{X}
arises from the fact that
\check{X}
is a graded deformation of
G\times^{P}(X_{0}\times\mathfrak{r})
, in the sense of
[
Lo22
, §2.2]
(see the proof of
[
Lo22
, Proposition 4.7]
).

Now pick an orbit
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, and denote the corresponding birational induction datum by
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0},z)
. Let
G/H
be the dense orbit in
G\times^{P}(X_{0}\times\mathfrak{r})
, as above. It follows from
[
Lo22
, Proposition 4.7(2)]
that the affinisation of the dense orbit in
G\times^{P}(\{z\}\times X_{0}\times\mathfrak{r})
is isomorphic to the fibre of the universal graded Poisson deformation of
\mathbb{C}[G/H]
corresponding to the parameter
z\in\mathfrak{P}
(terminology of Proposition
9.3
). We denote the regular functions on the fibre by
\mathbb{C}[X_{z}]
.

Thanks to Proposition
9.5
the above construction has a quantum analogue: let
\mathcal{A}_{z}
be the fibre of the universal filtered quantization of
\mathbb{C}[G/H]
corresponding to parameter
z\in\mathfrak{P}
.

The action of
G
on
G/H
is Hamiltonian and the comoment map
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}[X_{0}]
lifts to a quantum comoment map
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{z}
. This gives rise to an algebra homomorphism
U(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{A}_{z}
and the kernel is denoted
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})
.

Lemma 9.8
.

The ideal
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})
is a completely prime, primitive ideal.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{z}
is a filtered quantization of
\mathbb{C}[G/H]
and the latter is an integral domain it follows that
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})
is completely prime.

Again let
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0},z)
be the birationally minimal orbit datum inducing to
\mathcal{O}
. The comoment map
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]\to\mathbb{C}[G/H]
factors through the comoment map
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]\to\mathbb{C}[\overline{\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})}]
, and since the induced orbit is contained in the nilpotent cone, which is the vanishing locus of the non-constant homogeneous invariant polynomials
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}_{+}
, it follows that the kernel of the map
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}\to\mathbb{C}[G/H]
has codimension 1. The associated graded map of quantum comoment
U(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{A}_{z}
is
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]\to\mathbb{C}[G/H]
, and it follows that the kernel of the map
Z(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{A}_{z}
has codimension 1. This means that the prime ideal
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})
admits a central character. Now by
[
Di96
, §8.5.7]
it follows that
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})
is a primitive ideal.
∎

Hence this construction yields a map

\mathcal{J}:\mathfrak{g}/G\to\operatorname{Prim}U(\mathfrak{g}),

which is injective for classical Lie algebras
[
Lo22
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Recall the notation
\operatorname{Prim}_{\mathcal{O}}U(\mathfrak{g})
from the introduction. The following refinement of Theorem
1.2
uses the notation of Section
7
. It is the main result of this section.

Theorem 9.9
.

Let
e\in\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be an element of a nilpotent orbit in a classical Lie algebra. The following sets coincide:

(1)

The image of
\mathcal{J}
intersected with
\operatorname{Prim}_{\mathcal{O}}U(\mathfrak{g})
.

(2)

The ideals
\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(Q\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},e)}\mathbb{C}_{\eta})
where
\mathbb{C}_{\eta}\in U(\mathfrak{g},e)\operatorname{-mod}
is one dimensional.

The proof of the theorem will occupy the rest of the paper.

The first step is to describe the associated variety of the primitive ideal
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})
. This was stated in
[
Lo22
, §5.4]
, but we provide a proof for completeness. Another proof has appeared recently in
[
LMM21
, Proposition 6.1.2(1)]
.

Lemma 9.10
.

For
\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{g}/G
we let
\mathcal{S}
be a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathcal{O}
. Then

\mathcal{V\!A}(\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O}))=\overline{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})}.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{l},\mathcal{O}_{0},z)
be the birationally minimal orbit datum inducing to
\mathcal{O}
. For
t\in\mathbb{C}
we can consider the orbit
\mathcal{O}(t)
which is dense in the image of the generalised Springer map
G\times^{P}(tz+\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{n})\to\mathfrak{g}
. By definition we have
\mathcal{O}(1)=\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}(0)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
, whilst
\dim\mathcal{O}(t)
is constant. It follows that
\mathcal{O}
and
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
lie in a sheet. Since
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is nilpotent and every sheet contains a unique nilpotent orbit,
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
for every sheet containing
\mathcal{O}
.

Now write
G/H
for the dense orbit in
G\times^{P}(X_{0}\times\mathfrak{n})
. Since
\operatorname{gr}
is exact on strictly filtered vector spaces, the graded ideal
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})
is the kernel of the comoment map
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]\to\mathbb{C}[G/H]
. This factors through the comoment map for
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]\to\mathbb{C}[\overline{\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})}]
, which shows that
\sqrt{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O})}
is the defining ideal of
\overline{\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})}
. This completes the proof.
∎

9.3.
Harish-Chandra bimodules and Losev’s dagger functors

Now we keep fixed
e\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, the adjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}:=\operatorname{Ad}(G)e
, an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}
for
e
and write
G^{e}(0)
for the pointwise stabiliser of the triple. Note that the notation
\mathfrak{g}^{e}(0)=\operatorname{Lie}G^{e}(0)
is consistent with that of Section
2.2
.

In
[
Lo10a
]
Losev constructed a map
\mathcal{I}\mapsto\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
from two-sided ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
to two-sided ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g})
, with remarkable properties. In his subsequent work
[
Lo11
]
this construction was upgraded to a pair of adjoint functors between the categories of Harish–Chadra
U(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodules and
G^{e}(0)
-equivariant Harish–Chandra
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
-bimodules, as we now recall.

The category
\operatorname{HC}U(\mathfrak{g})
of Harish-Chandra
U(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodules consists of
U(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodules, finitely generated on both sides, which are locally finite for
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g})
. This implies that there is a
G
-action which differentiates to
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g})
.
If
\mathcal{O}:=G\cdot e
then we denote by
\operatorname{HC}_{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}U(\mathfrak{g})
the full subcategory of bimodules supported on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, see
[
Lo11
, §1.3]
for the precise definition.

It follows from
[
GG02
]
that
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
admits
G^{e}(0)
-action by filtered automorphisms, and a quantum comoment map

(9.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{e}(0)\hookrightarrow U(\mathfrak{g},e)

This map is injective, thanks to
[
Pr07a
, Lemma 2.4]
. Therefore one can define the category
\operatorname{HC}^{G^{e}(0)}U(\mathfrak{g},e)
of
G^{e}(0)
-equivariant Harish-Chandra
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
-bimodules to be the category of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
-bimodules, finitely generated on both sides, with a compatible
G^{e}(0)
-action which differentiates to the
\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak{g}^{e}(0)
-action (see
[
Lo11
, §1.3]
). We denote the subcategory of finite dimensional objects by
\operatorname{HC}^{G^{e}(0)}_{\text{fin}}U(\mathfrak{g},e)
.

In
[
Lo11
]
Losev constructed a pair
(\bullet_{\dagger},\bullet^{\dagger})
of adjoint functors

\bullet_{\dagger}:\operatorname{HC}_{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}U(\mathfrak{g})\leftrightarrows\operatorname{HC}^{G^{e}(0)}_{\text{fin}}U(\mathfrak{g},e):\bullet^{\dagger}

which behave nicely when applied to quantizations of nilpotent orbit covers (see
[
Lo22
, §5]
). The definition of these functors depends upon the decomposition theorem from
[
Lo10a
]
which we will not describe here. Instead we recall from
[
Lo11
, §3.5]
that there is an isomorphism

(9.4)

\displaystyle M_{\dagger}\cong(M/M\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})^{\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}(<0))}

for any
M\in\operatorname{HC}(\mathfrak{g})
, which is in the spirit of the definitions used throughout this paper. One immediate consequence which we use often is that
U(\mathfrak{g})_{\dagger}\cong U(\mathfrak{g},e)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{l}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})
be an induction datum for
\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is birationally rigid in
\mathfrak{l}_{0}
. This leads to to a finite cover
G/H\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}
discussed in Section
9.2
. Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the universal filtered quantization of
\mathbb{C}[G/H]
described in Proposition
9.5
. We can assume without loss of generality that
(G^{e})^{\circ}\subseteq H\subseteq G^{e}
. By Lemma
9.7
this is a
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{0})]^{W}
-algebra, where
X
denotes the affinisation of
G/H
and
W
is a subgroup of the relative Weyl group
N_{G}(L)/L
.

The image of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{0})]^{W}\to\mathcal{A}
is central and so for every ideal
I\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{0})]^{W}
we see that
\mathcal{A}/I\mathcal{A}\in\operatorname{HC}U(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus there is a natural
G^{e}(0)
-action on
(\mathcal{A}/I\mathcal{A})_{\dagger}
.

The following fact will be used a few times in the sequel.

Lemma 9.11
.

[
Lo22
, Lemma 5.2(1)]

For any maximal ideal
I\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{0})]^{W}
we have
(\mathcal{A}/I\mathcal{A})_{\dagger}\cong\mathbb{C}[G/H]
as
G^{e}(0)
-algebras.

For brevity we write
B=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{0})]^{W}
and for a closed point
z\in\operatorname{Spec}B
we write
I_{z}\subseteq B
for the corresponding maximal ideal, and
\mathbb{C}_{z}=B/I_{z}
for the one dimensional
B
-module, so that
\mathcal{A}_{z}=\mathcal{A}/I_{z}\mathcal{A}
.

Since
B\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is central, the image of the map
B\to\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}
lies in the space of
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!0}))
-invariants, giving an algebra homomorphism
B\to\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
. The multiplication on
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
is induced from that on
\mathcal{A}
it follows that the image of the map is central.

Lemma 9.12
.

\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z}\cong(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z})_{\dagger}
.

Proof.

By (
9.4
) we have

\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z}\cong(\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}))^{\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak{g}(<0)}/(I_{z}\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}))^{\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak{g}(<0)}.

Since
\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!0})
is exact on the category of Whittaker modules
[
GG02
, Theorem 6.1]
it will suffice to show that

(\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})/(I_{z}\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi})\cong(\mathcal{A}/I_{z}\mathcal{A})/(\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}/I_{z}\mathcal{A})

This is easy to see: both sides are isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{g}(\operatorname{\!<\!--1})_{\chi}+I_{z})
.
∎

By the lemma we can (and shall) identify
(\mathcal{A}_{z})_{\dagger}
with
(\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})/I_{z}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
in what follows.

Corollary 9.13
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
is commutative.

Proof.

Pick
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
. Let
\operatorname{ad}(a):\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}\to\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
be the map
b\mapsto[a,b]
. For any closed point
z\in\operatorname{Spec}B
we let
\operatorname{ad}_{z}(a):(\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})_{z}\to(\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})_{z}
be the induced map on the quotient. Since
\mathcal{A}_{z}
is a filtered quantization of
\mathbb{C}[G/H]
it follows from Lemma
9.11
that
(\mathcal{A}_{z})_{\dagger}=(\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})_{z}
is a commutative algebra, and so
\operatorname{ad}_{z}(a)=0
.

We conclude
\operatorname{ad}(a)\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}\subseteq I_{z}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
for all maximal ideals
z\in\operatorname{Spec}B
.

By Proposition
9.3
(1) we see that
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}
is a free
\operatorname{gr}B
-module, and it follows by a standard filtration argument that
\mathcal{A}
is a free
B
-module. We deduce that
\bigcap_{z}(I_{z}\mathcal{A})=0
, and this implies that
\bigcap_{z}(I_{z}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})=(\bigcap_{z}I_{z}\mathcal{A})_{\dagger}=0
by (
9.4
) and Lemma
9.12
. Finally we have
\operatorname{ad}(a)\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}\subseteq\bigcap_{z}(I_{z}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})=0
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 9.14
.

The
G^{e}(0)
-action on
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
factors through the component group
\Gamma:=G^{e}(0)/G^{e}(0)^{\circ}
and

(9.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{\Gamma}:=(\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})^{G^{e}(0)}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{0})]^{W}.

Proof.

As
B
is central in
\mathcal{A}
, it follows that
B
is
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant, hence
G
-invariant. Therefore the image of
B\to\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
is
G^{e}(0)
-invariant. Now consider the homomorphism
\varphi:B\to\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}
. We shall show that this is an isomorphism.

Since
G^{e}(0)
is a reductive group the functor of
G^{e}(0)
-invariants is exact, and so by Lemma
9.12
we have
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z}\cong(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z})_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}
. The latter is nonzero by Lemma
9.11
. If
0\neq b\in\operatorname{Ker}\varphi
then, since
B
is reduced, we can choose a maximal ideal
I_{z}\subseteq B
with
b\notin I_{z}
and we find
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z}=0
. This contradiction shows that
\varphi
is injective.

Now consider the cokernel
C
of
\varphi
in the category of
B
-modules. We claim that
\varphi
is surjective after specialisation at any point
z\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)
. By Lemma
9.11
we have
(\mathcal{A}/I_{z}\mathcal{A})^{G^{e}(0)}=\mathbb{C}
. Therefore the specialisation is just
\mathbb{C}=B/I_{z}\to\mathbb{C}
. If this map is zero then we can choose
b\in B
such that
\mathbb{C}b+I_{z}=B
and
\varphi(b)\subseteq I_{z}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}
. This would force
\varphi(B)\subseteq I_{z}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}
which would contradict the fact that
\varphi(1)=1
. Therefore
\varphi
is surjective after specialisation, as claimed.

Since
(\bullet)\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z}
preserves cokernels, we see that
C/I_{z}C=0
for all
z\in\operatorname{Max}(B)
. We shall show that this implies
C=0
.

It follows from Proposition
9.3
(1), Lemma
9.4
and Proposition
9.5
that
\mathcal{A}
is a free
B
-module, by a standard filtration argument. Hence
\mathcal{A}
is locally finite over
B
. It follows from (
9.4
) that
(\mathcal{A}_{\dagger})^{G^{e}(0)}
is a subquotient of
\mathcal{A}
as a
B
-module, and so it is locally finite over
B
, hence
C
is also a locally finite
B
-module.

Now every finite dimensional
B
-submodule decomposes as a direct sum of its generalised eigenspaces for a set of generators for
B
. Since
C
is the direct limit of its finite dimensional
B
-submodules, the same holds for
C
. To be precise, we let
C_{z}:=\{c\in C\mid I_{z}^{k}c=0\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Then we have

C=\bigoplus_{z\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)}C_{z}.

Now we aim to show that
C_{z}=0
for each
z\in\operatorname{Max}(B)
.

Since
C
is locally finite the same is true for
C_{z}
. Also
I_{z}C=C
implies
I_{z}C_{z^{\prime}}=C_{z^{\prime}}
for all
z,z^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Max}(B)
. In particular,
I_{z}C_{z}=C_{z}
for all such
z
.

The finite dimensional indecomposable modules of
C_{z}
are each of the form
B/I_{z}^{k}
for some
k\geq 0
, thus every finite dimensional submodule of
C_{z}
is a direct sum of modules of this form. It is easy to see that the limit of a directed system of modules of Krull dimension zero has Krull dimension zero. Therefore
C_{z}
is necessarily of the form
\bigoplus_{i\in I}B/I_{z}^{k_{i}}
for some index set
I
and non-negative integers
(k_{i})_{i\in I}
. Finally
I_{z}C_{z}=C_{z}
implies that
k_{i}=0
for all
i
and that
C_{z}=0
as required. We have now shown that
\varphi
is surjective.

It remains to show that the
G^{e}(0)
-action on
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
factors through
\Gamma
. To this end we demonstrate that the differential of the
G^{e}(0)
-action is trivial. For
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{e}(0)
this action coincides with the map
\operatorname{ad}(x):\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}\to\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
given by
a\mapsto xa-ax
, where we identify
x
with its image in
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
under the composition of (
9.3
) with
\mu_{\dagger}
. By Corollary
9.13
this map is zero, and the proof is complete.
∎

9.4.
The commutative quotient of the
W
-algebra and the orbit method

Now we relate
\operatorname{Spec}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
to the orbit method map. Let
(\mathfrak{l}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1}),...,(\mathfrak{l}_{s},\mathcal{O}_{s})
be the collection of induction data for
\mathcal{O}:=G\cdot e
such that
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is birationally rigid in
\mathfrak{l}_{i}
. Let
P_{i}
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
with Levi factor
L_{i}
such that
\operatorname{Lie}(L_{i})=\mathfrak{l}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{r}_{i}:=\operatorname{Rad}\operatorname{Lie}P_{i}
. We let
G/H_{i}
be the dense
G
-orbit in
G\times^{P_{i}}(\mathcal{O}_{i}\times\mathfrak{r}_{i})
. Also write
X_{i}
for the normalisation of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}
.

The universal quantization of
\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]
described in Proposition
9.3
is denoted
\mathcal{A}^{i}
, and let
W_{i}
be the Namikawa–Weyl group of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]
(Cf. Example
9.2
), so that
\mathcal{A}^{i}
is a
B_{i}:=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{i})]^{W_{i}}
-algebra by Lemma
9.7
. Each
\mathcal{A}^{i}
comes equipped with a quantum comoment map
\mu_{i}:U(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{A}^{i}

[
Lo22
, §5.2]
.

Let
B:=\prod_{i=1}^{s}B_{i}
and consider the
B
-algebra
\mathcal{A}:=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{A}_{i}
. There is a homomorphism
\mu:=\prod_{i}\mu_{i}:U(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{A}
which gives

(9.6)

\displaystyle\mu_{\dagger}:U(\mathfrak{g},e)\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}

Lemma 9.15
.

\mu_{\dagger}
factors through
U(\mathfrak{g},e)\to U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and the image lies in
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}
. Furthermore the following sets coincide:

(1)

\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{g}/G)\cap\operatorname{Prim}_{\mathcal{O}}U(\mathfrak{g})
,

(2)

The set of ideals
\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}Q\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},e)}\mathbb{C}_{\eta}\subseteq\operatorname{Prim}U(\mathfrak{g})
where
\mathbb{C}_{\eta}
is a one dimensional
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
-module appearing in the image of the map of maximal spectra

\mu_{\dagger}^{*}:\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}=\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}.

Proof.

The first claim follows directly from Corollary
9.13
.

We now prove that (1)
=
(2). The set (1) is equal to the collection of kernels of the maps
U(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{A}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z}
as we vary over all closed points
z\in\operatorname{Spec}B
. For any fixed
z
(using Lemma
9.12
) the kernel of the map
\mu_{\dagger}:U(\mathfrak{g},e)\to\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z}
is the annihilator of a one dimensional
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
-module, thanks to our previous observations. We denote it
\mathbb{C}_{\eta}
. Our chosen
z
lies in
\operatorname{Spec}B_{i}
for some
i
and we claim that the kernel
K=\operatorname{Ker}(U(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{A}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{C}_{z})
is equal to the annihilator of
Q\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},e)}\mathbb{C}_{\eta}
. This will complete the proof.

As we mentioned above, the functor
\bullet^{\dagger}
on Harish–Chandra bimodules was preceded by Losev’s construction of a map
\mathcal{I}\mapsto\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
from ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
to ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g})

[
Lo10a
]
. Also note that two sided ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g})
can be regarded as Harish–Chadra
U(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodules, and that the map
\mathcal{J}\mapsto\mathcal{J}_{\dagger}
sends ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g})
to ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)

[
Lo11
, Theorem 1.3.1]
.

By
loc. cit.
we know that
K_{\dagger}=\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{g},e)}\mathbb{C}_{\eta}
. By Lemmas
9.8
and
9.10
, we know that
K
is a primitive ideal with associated variety
G\cdot e
, so by
[
Lo10a
, Theorem 1.2.2(viii)]
the ideals
I\subseteq U(\mathfrak{g},e)
which satisfy
I^{\dagger}=K
are precisely minimal primes over
K_{\dagger}
. Since
K_{\dagger}
is primitive it is prime, and in particular
(K_{\dagger})^{\dagger}=K
. Now
[
Lo10a
, Theorem 1.2.2(ii)]
implies that

K=(\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{g},e)}\mathbb{C}_{\eta})^{\dagger}=\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(Q\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},e)}\mathbb{C}_{\eta}).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Finally we prove the main theorem of this section.

Proof of Theorem
9.9
.

Thanks to Lemma
9.15
it suffices to show that
\mu_{\dagger}^{*}:\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}\to\operatorname{Spec}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is surjective. We will show that the map is finite and dominant. Since finite morphisms are closed this will complete the proof. Since
\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced (Corollary
7.7
and Theorem
8.9
), and since the property of being a finite module or an injective homomorphism can both be lifted through the associated graded construction, it is enough to show that the map
\operatorname{gr}\mu_{\dagger}:\mathbb{C}[e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}]^{\operatorname{ab}}\to\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}^{G^{e}(0)}
is injective and gives a finite extension of algebras. The
G^{e}(0)
-action on
\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
factors through a finite group (Proposition
9.14
) and so it will suffice to show that
\mu_{\dagger}:S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}\to\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}_{\dagger}
is injective and finite.

By Lemma
9.7
we know that
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[\check{X}]^{W}
where
\check{X}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}G\times^{P_{i}}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{i})\times X_{i}\times\mathfrak{r}_{i})
and
W=\prod_{i=1}^{s}W_{i}
. It will be convenient to work with a slightly larger algebra: let
\check{\mathcal{A}}
denote the algebra
\prod_{i=1}^{s}\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{i})
where
\mathcal{D}^{i}
is the sheaf associated to the conic symplectic singularity
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]
in Proposition
9.5
. Then we have
\operatorname{gr}\check{\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{C}[\check{X}]
and it will suffice to show that
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}\to\operatorname{gr}(\check{\mathcal{A}}_{\dagger})
is injective and finite, since
\check{\mathcal{A}}^{W}=\mathcal{A}
and
W
is a finite group.

The map
S(\mathfrak{g},e)\to\operatorname{gr}(\check{\mathcal{A}}_{\dagger})
coincides with the pullback to
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]\to\mathbb{C}[\check{X}]
(see
[
Lo22
, Lemma 5.1(4)]
or
[
Lo11
, Lemma 3.3.2(3)]
). This means that if we take the preimage
Y
of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
inside
\check{X}
then
\operatorname{gr}(\check{\mathcal{A}}_{\dagger})=\mathbb{C}[Y]
. The algebra
S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is reduced (Theorem
8.9
) and it follows that the map is injective if and only if the corresponding morphism of spectra is dominant. Now it suffices to show that
Y\to\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is finite and dominant.

The morphism
\check{X}\to\mathfrak{g}
is proper, since it is the collapsing map of a bundle over a projective variety
\prod_{i}G/P_{i}
. Properness is stable under base change and it follows that
Y\to e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
is proper as well.

We now show that
Y\to e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
is quasi-finite. The map
G\times^{P_{i}}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{i})\times X_{i}\times\mathfrak{r}_{i})\to\mathfrak{g}
factors through
\xi:G\times^{P_{i}}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{i})+\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+\mathfrak{r}_{i})\to\mathfrak{g}
and, since
G\times^{P_{i}}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{i})\times X_{i}\times\mathfrak{r}_{i})
is the normalisation of
G\times^{P_{i}}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{i})+\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+\mathfrak{r}_{i})
, it suffices to show that
\xi
map is quasi-finite, when restricted to the preimage of
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
. The image of
\xi
is the closure of the decomposition class
\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
associated to
(\mathfrak{l}_{i},\mathcal{O}_{i})
(see
[
Bo81
, Lemma 2.5]
).
The variety
Y
inherits a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action from the contracting Kazhdan action on
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
described in Section
6.2
. The map
Y\to e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
is
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariant, and so to show that it is quasi-finite it suffices to show that the fibre over
e
is finite. By Lemma
9.7
(3) the fibre over
e
is contained in
G\times^{P_{i}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+\mathfrak{r}_{i})
. The image of the map
G\times^{P_{i}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+\mathfrak{r}_{i})\to\mathfrak{g}
is contained in
\overline{\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}_{i}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{i})}
, and so by dimension comparison we see that
G\times^{P_{i}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+\mathfrak{r}_{i})\to\overline{\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}_{i}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{i})}
is generically finite-to-one. There is an open subset of
\overline{\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}_{i}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{i})}
over which the fibres are finite and, since the collapsing map of the fibre bundle is
G
-equivariant, we see that the fibre is finite over
e\in\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}_{i}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
. Thus
Y\to e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
is quasi-finite.

We have shown that
Y\to e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}
is quasi-finite and proper, hence finite.

It remains to show that
Y\to\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is dominant. Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},...,\mathcal{S}_{k}
be the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathcal{O}
. By Proposition
6.2
(1) we know that
\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}=(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})\cap\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}
. Since the sheets are classified by the rigid induction data (Theorem
6.1
) and the
\{(\mathfrak{l}_{i},\mathcal{O}_{i})\mid i=1,...,s\}
denote all induction data for
G\cdot e
, we might as well assume that
(\mathfrak{l}_{i},\mathcal{O}_{i})
is the induction datum corresponding to
\mathcal{S}_{i}
for
i=1,...,k
. By the remarks of the previous paragraph we know that the image of
G\times^{P_{i}}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}_{i})\times X_{i}\times\mathfrak{r}_{i})\to\mathfrak{g}
is dense in
\mathcal{S}_{i}
and so
Y\to\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is dominant. This completes the proof.
∎

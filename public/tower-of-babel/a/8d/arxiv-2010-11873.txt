2.
Explicit expression of the minimal polynomial of the Kronecker product of matrices

In this section we obtain the minimal polynomial of the Kronecker product of the companion matrices of any finite family of monic polynomials
P_{i}

1\leq i\leq m

\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}L(P_{i})=L(P)

denote the vector space of all sequences over

P

The following definition is from
[
2

Definition 2.1
.

For two positive integers
s
and

s\wedge t
be the maximum value of
i+j+1
such that
\binom{i+j}{i}\neq 0
(in
F
) where
0\leq i\leq s-1
and
0\leq j\leq t-1
.

Let us recall two lemmas and a theorem proved in

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\lambda\in F
and let
\wedge_{\lambda}
be the noncommutative binary operation on
\mathbb{N}
defined by
t\wedge_{\lambda}s=\min(t,s)\delta_{0,\lambda}+t\delta_{0,s}^{c}\delta_{0,\lambda}^{c}

\delta_{e,f}^{c}=1-\delta_{e,f}
and
\delta_{e,f}
is the Kronecker symbol. Then For all

\langle 0\rangle_{t}\langle\lambda\rangle_{s}=\langle 0\rangle_{t\wedge_{\lambda}s}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

,
\lambda,\mu\in F^{\ast}=F-\{0\}
and
\eta\in F
. Put
0\wedge s=s\wedge 0=0

(i)

\dim(\langle\eta\rangle_{i})=i

(ii)

\langle 1\rangle_{i}\langle 1\rangle_{j}=\langle 1\rangle_{i\wedge j}

(iii)

\langle\lambda\rangle_{i}\langle\mu\rangle_{j}=\langle\lambda\mu\rangle_{i\wedge j}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

(1)

If

(2)

If

The following lemma is proved in

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A

Theorem 2.6
.

Let

3.
\mathcal{P}
-canonical form of the exponential of matrices

The following result shows how to deduce the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{H})
of the matrix exponential function
e^{tA}
,
A
is a square matrix with complex elements, from the
\mathcal{P}
-cf of
A
relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{T})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in M_{q}(\mathbb{C})
and let

(3.1)

\boldsymbol{A}=N(A)+\mathcal{A}_{0}\Lambda_{0}+\cdots+\mathcal{A}_{m}\Lambda_{m}

be the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{T})
of
A
. Then for every
t\in\mathbb{C}
, the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{H})
of
{\mathrm{e}}^{tA}
can be easily obtained by transforming the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{T})
of
A
using the following substitutions:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}\Lambda_{i}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\frac{(t\lambda)^{i}\boldsymbol{{\mathrm{e}}^{t\lambda}}\Gamma^{i}}{i!}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{0}_{i}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\frac{t^{i}\Gamma^{i}}{i!}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{A}(X)=X^{t_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}(X-\lambda_{j})^{t_{j}}
(possibly
t_{0}=0
), be the minimal polynomial of
A
and let
t\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\pi_{0},\ldots,\pi_{p}
be the spectral projections of
A
at
0,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p}
respectively. From Proposition
3.1
of
[
4
]
, we have

\boldsymbol{A}=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\boldsymbol{0}_{i}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-i}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}\Lambda_{i}

Then

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{tA}&=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\boldsymbol{0}_{i}\boldsymbol{t}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\boldsymbol{t\lambda}_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-i}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}\Lambda_{i}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\boldsymbol{0}_{i}t^{i}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\boldsymbol{t\lambda}_{j}(t\lambda_{j})^{-i}t^{i}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}\Lambda_{i},\end{split}

where
m=\max\{t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{p}-1\}
. Dividing by
k!
, we obtain

\frac{(tA)^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\delta_{i,k}\frac{t^{i}}{k!}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{1}{k!}(t\lambda_{j})^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}t^{i}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}.

Therefore

\begin{split}{\mathrm{e}}^{tA}&=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{i,k}\frac{t^{i}}{k!}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(t\lambda_{j})^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}t^{i}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\frac{(t\lambda_{j})^{k-i}}{(k-i)!}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\lambda_{j}}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}\end{split}

which is a well known result. But since
({\mathrm{e}}^{tA})^{k}={\mathrm{e}}^{ktA}
for all
k\geq 0
, it follows that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{{\mathrm{e}}^{tA}}&=\sum_{i=0}^{t_{0}-1}\frac{t^{i}\Gamma^{i}}{i!}A^{i}\pi_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\boldsymbol{{\mathrm{e}}^{t\lambda_{j}}}(A-\lambda_{0}I_{q})^{0}\pi_{j}\frac{t^{0}\Gamma^{0}}{0!}+\cdots\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\boldsymbol{{\mathrm{e}}^{t\lambda_{j}}}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{m}\pi_{j}\frac{t^{m}\Gamma^{m}}{m!}.\end{split}

So

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{{\mathrm{e}}^{tA}}&=\sum_{j=1}^{p}(\boldsymbol{{\mathrm{e}}^{t\lambda_{j}}}(A-\lambda_{0}I_{q})^{0}\pi_{j}+A^{0}\pi_{0})\frac{t^{0}}{0!}\Gamma^{0}+\cdots\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{p}(\boldsymbol{{\mathrm{e}}^{t\lambda_{j}}}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{d}\pi_{j}+A^{d}\pi_{0})\frac{t^{d}}{d!}\Gamma^{d},\end{split}

where
d=\max\{m,t_{0}-1\}
, which is the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{H})
of
{\mathrm{e}}^{tA}
.
To conclude, all that remains is to compare equation
3.2
with the
\mathcal{P}
-cf of
A
relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{T})
.

4.
\mathcal{P}
-canonical form of the logarithm of matrices

It is well known that a complex matrix
A
has a logarithm if and only if
A
is nonsingular. The following theorem shows that knowing the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{T})
(or
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{H})
) of a nonsingular matrix
A
, we can derive a logarithm of it.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
be a nonsingular matrix of order
q
over
\mathbb{C}
and let

\lambda_{1}={\mathrm{e}}^{z_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{s}={\mathrm{e}}^{z_{s}}

its eigenvalues. Let us denote by
(A(k))_{k\geq 0}
the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{T})
of
A
, and consider the matrix complex-valued smooth function
A(t)
of a real variable
t
obtained by plugging the variable
t
for
k
in
A(k)
. Then we have

(1)

The derivative
A^{\prime}(0)
of
A(t)
at
t=0
is a matrix logarithm of
A
.

(2)

The
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{T})
of
A^{\prime}(0)
can be easily obtained by transforming the
\mathcal{P}
-cf relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{H})
of
A
using the following substitutions:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\boldsymbol{\lambda_{j}}\Gamma^{i}&\hookrightarrow&i!\boldsymbol{z_{j}}z_{j}^{-i}\Lambda_{i}&\text{if}&z_{j}\neq 0\\
\boldsymbol{\lambda_{j}}\Gamma^{i}&\hookrightarrow&i!\boldsymbol{0}_{i}&\text{if}&z_{j}=0\end{array}

(3)

The eigenvalues of
A^{\prime}(0)
are
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}
. More precisely, if
\chi_{A}=(X-\lambda_{1})^{m_{1}}\,\cdots\,(X-\lambda_{s})^{m_{s}}
is the characteristic polynomial of
A
, then
A^{\prime}(0)
is the unique logarithm of
A
with characteristic polynomial
\chi_{A^{\prime}(0)}=(X-z_{1})^{m_{1}}\,\cdots\,(X-z_{s})^{m_{s}}
.

(4)

If
A
has nonnegative real eigenvalues, then the principal logarithm of
A
is equal to the matrix
A^{\prime}(0)
obtained when we choose the
z_{i}
to be in the strip
\{z\in\mathbb{C}/-\pi<Im(z)<\pi\}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
\binom{t}{i}=\displaystyle\frac{t(t-1)\cdots(t-i+1)}{i!}
be the binomial polynomial of degree
i
. Since, for
i\geq 1
, the coefficient of
t
in that polynomial is
\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{i}
, it follows that the derivative of
\binom{t}{i}
at
0
is
\binom{t}{i}^{\prime}(0)=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{i}
for all integer
i\geq 1
. On the other hand, we have

\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\prime}(0)=z_{1}\pi_{1}+\cdots+z_{s}\pi_{s}.

Hence

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{1}\pi_{1}+\cdots+z_{s}\pi_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{1}\pi_{1}+\cdots+z_{s}\pi_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{i}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_{j}^{-i}(A-\lambda_{j}I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}.

Since the matrix
(\lambda_{j}^{-1}A-I_{q})\pi_{j})^{m+1}=0
, it follows that

C_{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{i}(\lambda_{j}^{-1}A-I_{q})^{i}\pi_{j}

is a logarithm of
I_{q}+(\lambda_{j}^{-1}A-I_{q})\pi_{j}
. Consequently, as the
C_{j}
s are pairwise commuting matrices, we have
C=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s}C_{j}
is a logarithm of

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{s}(I_{q}+(\lambda_{j}^{-1}A-I_{q})\pi_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{q}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}(\lambda_{j}^{-1}A-I_{q})\pi_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\pi_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A{\mathrm{e}}^{-(z_{1}\pi_{1}+\cdots+z_{s}\pi_{s})}.

Hence

A={\mathrm{e}}^{C}{\mathrm{e}}^{-(z_{1}\pi_{1}+\cdots+z_{s}\pi_{s})}.

Finally, since the matrix
C
commutes with
z_{1}\pi_{1}+\cdots+z_{s}\pi_{s}
, it follows that
A^{\prime}(0)
is a logarithm of
A
.

(2)

It is sufficient to use the argument of Proposition
3.1
in reverse direction.

(3)

Follows from property
(2)
above and Corollary (3.6) of
[
4
]
which allows us to deduce the minimal polynomial, and in particular the eigenvalues, of a matrix from its
\mathcal{P}
-cf

(4)

Follows directly from property
(3)
above.

Ergodicity, CLT and asymptotic maximum of the Airy 1 process
Fei Pu

Abstract
We first show that the Airy 1 process is associated using the association property of the solution to the stochastic heat equation and convergence of the KPZ equation to the KPZ fixed point. Then we apply Newman’s inequality to establish the ergodicity and central limit theorem for the Airy 1 process. Combined with the asymptotic behavior of the tail probability, we derive a Poisson limit theorem for the Airy 1 process and give a precise estimate on the asymptotic behavior of the maximum of the Airy 1 process over an interval. Analogous results for the Airy 2 process are also presented.



MSC 2010 subject classification: 60A10, 60F05, 60H15.

Keywords: Airy
1
process, association, CLT, ergodicity.

1
Introduction and main results

The Airy
1
process,
\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
, derived by Sasamoto
[
30
]
in the context of TASEP, is a stationary stochastic process whose finite-dimensional distributions are given in terms of the Fredholm determinant (see
[
34
, Section 4.1.2]
). In particular, the one-point distribution is a scalar multiple of the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution, namely,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq s\}=F_{1}(2s),\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
F_{1}
denotes the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution.
Moreover, it was proven by Quastel and Remenik
[
28
]
that the sample paths are Hölder continuous with exponent
\frac{1}{2}-
.

Recently, Basu et al.
[
5
]
have shown that the covariance of the Airy
1
process
decays super-exponentially by showing that
\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))=\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}+o(1))x^{3}}

as
x\to\infty
. This immediately implies a law of large numbers, i.e., as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\to\mathrm{E}[\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)],\quad\text{ in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

We are aiming to establish the ergodicity and the matching central limit theorem for the Airy
1
process.

Theorem 1.1
.

\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is ergodic, and as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)-\mathrm{E}[\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)])\ \mathrm{d}x\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm N}(0,\sigma^{2}),

(1.1)

where
\sigma^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))\mathrm{d}x\in(0,\infty)
, and “
\xrightarrow{\text{\rm d}\,}
” denotes convergence
in distribution.

Note that Quastel and Remenik
[
28
]
proved that the Airy
1
process fluctuates locally like a Brownian motion (see
[
28
, Theorem 3]
), using a formula for the
n
-dimensional distributions of the Airy
1
process in terms of a Fredholm determinant on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.
We refer to Hägg
[
19
]
for the local central limit theorem for the Airy
2
process and Pimentel
[
25
]
for the local Brownian motion behavior of Airy processes.

The main tool to prove the ergodicity property and CLT (
1.1
) in Theorem
1.1
is the association property of the Airy
1
process.
We recall from Esary et al.
[
17
]
that
a random vector
X:=(X_{1}\,,\ldots,X_{m})
is said to be
associated
if

\Cov[h_{1}(X)\,,h_{2}(X)]\geq 0,

(1.2)

for every pair of functions
h_{1},h_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}

that are nondecreasing in every coordinate and satisfy
h_{1}(X),h_{2}(X)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. A random field
\Phi=\{\Phi(x)\}_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
is

associated
if
(\Phi(x_{1})\,,\ldots,\Phi(x_{m}))
is associated
for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We remark that an associated random vector is also called to satisfy the FKG inequalities; see Newman
[
23
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated.

The association property of the Airy
1
process leads to a fundamental inequality, known as Newman’s inequality (see (
2.1
) below), which plays a crucial role in proving ergodicity and CLT for the Airy
1
process. Furthermore, Newman’s inequality implies an inequality for characteristic functions (see (
2.3
) below), which
together with the asymptotic behavior of the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution,
enables us to derive a Poisson limit theorem for high-level exceedances of the Airy
1
process.

Denote by
\rm T
the inverse function of
s\mapsto 1-F_{1}(s)
. We have the following Poisson approximation for the Airy
1
process.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
\lambda>0
. Let
\{x_{k,N}\}_{N\geq 1,1\leq k\leq N}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\min_{1\leq j\neq k\leq N}|x_{j,N}-x_{k,N}|}{(3\log N)^{1/3}}>1.

(1.3)

Then, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{1}_{\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N})>\frac{1}{2}{\rm T}(\lambda/N)\}}\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm Poisson}(\lambda),

(1.4)

where
{\rm Poisson}(\lambda)
denotes a Poisson random variable with parameter
\lambda
.

The function
\rm T
(inverse of
s\mapsto 1-F_{1}(s)
) is introduced in (
1.4
) so that the expectation of the sum in (
1.4
) is equal to
the constant
\lambda
, as in the classical Poisson approximation theorem for independent Bernoulli random variables.
Theorem
1.3
states that when the points
x_{k,N}
,
k=1,\ldots,N
are far apart (namely, satisfy the distance condition (
1.3
)), then the identically distributed Bernoulli random variables given by the indicator functions in (
1.4
) are approximately independent and hence the sum of them will be approximated by the Poisson distribution.

Conus et al.
[
13
]
studied the spatial asymptotic behavior for the solution to the stochastic heat equation using a localization argument (see Chen
[
12
]
for precise spatial asymptotic behavior for the parabolic Anderson equation).
This localization argument is based on the mild formula for the stochastic heat equation and does not apply to the Airy
1
process. However, the association property provides a way to analyze the independence structure of the Airy
1
process in terms of the asymptotic behavior of the covariance (see the inequality (
6.11
) below).
Combined with the asymptotic behavior of the tail probability of the maximum of the Airy
1
process (see Proposition
6.1
below), we can study the precise asymptotic behavior of
the maximum of the Airy
1
process.

Theorem 1.4
.

Almost surely,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

(1.5)

As for the Airy
2
process
\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
, we have the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Almost surely,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2/3}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2/3}.

(1.6)

The paper is organized as follows. After introducing some preliminaries in Section
2
, we
first prove Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
. Then we establish the ergodicity and CLT in Section
4
. The Poisson approximation (Theorem
1.3
) is established in Section
5
.
Finally, we prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
in Section
6
.

Let us close the introduction with a brief description of the notation of this paper. For a Lipschitz continuous
function
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\text{\rm Lip}(g):=\sup_{a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},a\neq b}\frac{|g(b)-g(a)|}{\|b-a\|}

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and for
j=1,\ldots,m
, we define

\displaystyle\text{\rm Lip}_{j}(g):=\sup_{a_{j}\neq b_{j}}\frac{|g(a)-g(b)|}{|a_{j}-b_{j}|},

where
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
are the
j
-th coordinates of
a
and
b
respectively.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we will first recall some basic facts on associated random variables. We refer to
[
27
]
for more details on
associated sequences.

Recall the definition of association in (
1.2
). Esary et al.

[
17
]
showed that the random vector
X
is associated if and only if
(
1.2
) holds for all bounded and continuous functions
h_{1},h_{2}
that are nondecreasing coordinatewise. Using approximation and dominated convergence theorem, in order to verify that
X
is associated, it suffices to check that (
1.2
) holds for all
h_{1},h_{2}
which are smooth, nondecreasing coordinatewise and have bounded partial derivatives.

Another concept of dependence is positive quadrant dependence. According to
[
21
]
, a pair of random variables
X
,
Y
are said to be positive quadrant dependent if

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{X\leq x,Y\leq y\}-\mathrm{P}\{X\leq x\}\mathrm{P}\{Y\leq y\}\geq 0

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
For an associated random vector
(X,Y)
, it is clear that the random variables
X
and
Y
are positive quadrant dependent. Therefore, according to Newman
[
23
, Lemma 3]
, for an associated random vector
(X,Y)
such that both
X

and
Y
have finite variance, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Cov(f(X)\,,g(Y))\right|\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\Cov(X\,,Y),

(2.1)

for
C^{1}
complex valued functions
f
,
g
on
{\mathbb{R}}
with
f^{\prime}
,
g^{\prime}
bounded, where
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
denotes the sup norm on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Inequality (
2.1
) is known as Newman’s inequality. Bulinski
[
7
]
generalized (
2.1
) to
Lipschitz continuous functions (see also
[
27
, Theorem 6.2.4]
). Moreover, according to Bulinski and Shabanovich
[
8
]
(see also
[
27
, Theorem 6.2.6]
),
letting
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
be an associated random vector such that each component has finite variance, then we have
for Lipschitz continuous functions
f,g:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\Cov(f(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})\,,g(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}))\right|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\text{\rm Lip}_{j}(f)\text{\rm Lip}_{\ell}(g)\Cov(Y_{j}\,,Y_{\ell}),

(2.2)

where
\text{\rm Lip}_{j}(f),\text{\rm Lip}_{\ell}(g)
are defined at the end of Section
1
. Inequality (
2.2
) generalizes
[
23
, (12)]
, which is a
multivariate version of (
2.1
).

Another consequence of Newman’s inequality (
2.1
) is the following inequality for the characteristic functions of associated random vector. Indeed, by
[
23
, Theorem 1]
, for an associated random vector
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
such that each component has finite variance, we have for any real numbers
r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}Y_{j}}\right]-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}r_{j}Y_{j}}\right]\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j,\ell\leq m\\
j\neq\ell\end{subarray}}|r_{j}||r_{\ell}|\Cov(Y_{j}\,,Y_{\ell}).

(2.3)

The study of central limit theorem for associated random sequences essentially relies on the above inequality; see
[
23
, Theorem 2]
. We will see in Section
5
that inequality (
2.3
) also plays a crucial role in the study of Poisson approximation for the Airy
1
process. In order to prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
, we need the following
probability inequalities for associated random vectors.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
be an associated random vector. Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
be real numbers.
For subsets
A,B
of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},j\in A\cup B\}-\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},j\in A\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{k}\leq y_{k},k\in B\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{j\in A}\sum_{k\in B}\left(\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}-\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}\right).

(2.4)

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},1\leq j\leq m\}-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\left(\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}-\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}\right).

(2.5)

Proof.

The proof of (
2.1
) is similar to that of
[
27
, Theorem 1.2.2]
(see also
[
20
, Lemma 1]
). We define
the random variables
Z_{j}=\bm{1}_{\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}}
, for
j=1,\ldots,m
. It is clear that
(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m})

is associated. Then we can follow along the same lines as in the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 1.2.2]
to
conclude (
2.1
).

Clearly, the inequality (
2.1
) holds with
m=2
. Assume that the inequality (
2.1
) is true for
m-1
.
Then for integer
m
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},1\leq j\leq m\}-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},1\leq j\leq m\}-\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},1\leq j\leq m-1\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{m}\leq y_{m}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},1\leq j\leq m-1\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{m}\leq y_{m}\}-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(\mathrm{P}\{Y_{i}\leq y_{j},Y_{m}\leq y_{m}\}-\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{m}\leq y_{m}\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m-1}\left(\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}-\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\left(\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j},Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}-\mathrm{P}\{Y_{j}\leq y_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{Y_{k}\leq y_{k}\}\right),

where in the inequality, we have used (
2.1
) and the assumption for
m-1
. Hence, we prove the inequality
(
2.1
) by induction.
∎

We next introduce some facts on the GOE and GUE Tracy-Widom distributions, denoted by
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
respectively. Let
F_{1}^{\prime}
and
F_{2}^{\prime}
be the derivatives of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
respectively, which correspond to the probability density functions of the GOE and GUE Tracy-Widom distributions. Recall from
[
1
]
(see also
[
31
,
32
]
) that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle F_{1}(s)=F(s)E(s),\qquad F_{2}(s)=F(s)^{2},

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle F(s)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{\infty}R(r)\mathrm{d}r\right),\quad E(s)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{\infty}q(r)\mathrm{d}r\right).

(2.7)

Here the (real) function
q
is the solution to the Painlevé II equation

\displaystyle q^{\prime\prime}=2q^{3}+sq,

that satisfies the boundary condition

\displaystyle q(s)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}s^{1/4}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2}{3}s^{3/2}},\quad s\to+\infty.

(2.8)

The function
R
is defined as

R(s)=\int_{s}^{\infty}(q(r))^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

Taking derivative in (
2.6
) and using the formulas in (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{2}F_{1}(s)\left(R(s)+q(s)\right).

(2.9)

According to the asymptotics of the functions
q,R
and
F_{1}
as
s\to-\infty
(see
[
1
, (11), (12), (18)]
), we derive from (
2.9
) that the probability density function
F^{\prime}_{1}
is continuous and bounded.
Similarly, we have

F_{2}^{\prime}(s)=F_{2}(s)R(s),

and use the asymptotics of the functions
R
and
F_{2}
as
s\to-\infty
(see
[
1
, (12), (16)]
), we deduce that
the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution also has a bounded and continuous probability density function.

Moreover, in light of (
2.8
), we deduce that for all sufficiently large
s
,

\displaystyle R(s)\leq\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}r^{1/4}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2}{3}r^{3/2}}q(r)\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2}{3}s^{3/2}}.

Hence, we combine with (
2.9
) and (
2.8
) to obtain that there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime}(s)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2}{3}s^{3/2}},\quad\text{for all $s\geq C_{1}$}.

(2.10)

Furthermore, we see from
[
15
, Theorem 1]
that for fixed
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
, there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
depending on
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon)s^{3/2}}\leq 1-F_{1}(s)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon)s^{3/2}},\quad\text{for all $s\geq C_{2}$}.

(2.11)

See also
[
1
, (25), (26)]
for the precise upper tail of GOE Tracy-Widom distribution.

Recall that
\rm T
denotes the inverse function of
s\mapsto 1-F_{1}(s)
. The following lemma will be used when we prove Theorem
1.3
in Section
5
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
\lambda>0
and
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
. There exist an integer
N_{0}
and a positive constant
C

both depending on

\lambda
and
\epsilon
such that for all
z\geq N_{0}

\displaystyle{\rm T}(\lambda/z)-{\rm T}(\lambda/(z-1))\geq Cz^{-\frac{2+6\epsilon}{2+3\epsilon}}.

(2.12)

Proof.

Taking derivative on both sides of the following identity

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm T}(\lambda/z)}^{\infty}F^{\prime}_{1}(s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\lambda}{z},\quad z>\lambda,

we observe that for all
z>\lambda

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{\mathrm{d}z}{\rm T}(\lambda/z)=\frac{\lambda}{z^{2}F_{1}^{\prime}({\rm T}(\lambda/z))}.

(2.13)

We can choose a sufficiently large integer
N_{0}
depending on
\lambda>0
and
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
such that

\displaystyle{\rm T}(\lambda/z)>C_{1}\vee C_{2},\quad\text{for all $z\geq N_{0}-1$},

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are the constants in (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) respectively. Hence,
by (
2.13
) and (
2.10
), we derive that for all
z\geq N_{0}-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{\mathrm{d}z}{\rm T}(\lambda/z)\geq\frac{\lambda}{z^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}([{\rm T}(\lambda/z)]^{3/2})}\geq\frac{\lambda}{z^{2}}\left(\frac{z}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{\frac{2}{3}}{\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}}=Cz^{-\frac{2+6\epsilon}{2+3\epsilon}},

where the second inequality holds by the first inequality in (
2.11
).
Finally, the above estimate and the mean-value theorem imply (
2.12
).
∎

3
Association

We prove the association property of the Airy
1
process in this section. We first consider the stochastic heat equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z(t\,,x)=\delta\partial_{x}^{2}Z(t\,,x)+\frac{1}{4}\delta^{1/2}Z(t\,,x)\xi(t\,,x),\quad t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}

(3.1)

subject to flat initial data
Z(0)\equiv 1
or narrow wedge initial data
Z(0)=\delta_{0}
. In the above equation,

\delta
is a positive constant and
\xi
denotes space-time white noise.
Chen et al.
[
11
, Theorem A.4]
have established the association property for stochastic heat equation with general diffusion coefficient and initial data, by showing that the Malliavin derivative of the solution is non-negative almost surely (see
[
11
, (A.4)]
) and then applying the Clark-Ocone formula and Ito’s isometry to show that the covariance

\displaystyle\Cov(h_{1}(Z(t_{1}\,,x_{1}),\ldots,Z(t_{m}\,,x_{m}))\,,h_{2}(Z(t_{1}\,,x_{1}),\ldots,Z(t_{m}\,,x_{m})))

is nonnegative for all
h_{1},h_{2}
which are smooth, nondecreasing coordinatewise and have bounded partial derivatives.
Although the constants (depending on
\delta
) in equation (
3.1
) are different from those in equation (1.1) of
[
11
]
, Theorem A.4 of

[
11
]
ensures that the solution to (
3.1
) is associated.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
11
, Theorem A.4]
)
.

For every fixed
\delta>0
, the solution
\{Z(t\,,x):(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
to (
3.1
)
with flat or narrow wedge initial condition is associated.

Now we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For
\delta>0
, define

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\delta}(t\,,x)=4\delta\log Z(t,x)+\frac{t}{12},\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}},

(3.2)

where
Z(t\,,x)
is the solution to (
3.1
) subject to flat initial condition
Z(0)\equiv 1
. It is clear that for each
\delta>0
, the process

\tilde{h}_{\delta}=\{\tilde{h}_{\delta}(t,x):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated. Let
\mathfrak{h}=\{\mathfrak{h}(t,x;0):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
be the KPZ fixed point (see
[
22
]
) with flat initial data.
According to
[
29
, Theorem 2.2(3)]
,
as
\delta\to 0
, the finite-dimensional distributions of
\tilde{h}_{\delta}
converge to those of
\mathfrak{h}
;
see also
[
33
, Theorem 1]
.
Since convergence in distribution preserves the association property (see (P
5
) of
[
17
]
), we see that
\mathfrak{h}
is associated.
Moreover, because
\{\mathfrak{h}(1,x;0):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
has the same finite-dimensional distributions as
\{2^{1/3}\mathcal{A}_{1}(2^{-2/3}x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}

(see
[
22
, (4.15)]
), we conclude that the Airy
1
process is associated.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

The Airy
2
process
\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
1.2
. Denote by
\{\mathfrak{h}(t,x;\partial_{0}):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
the KPZ fixed point starting from narrow wedge at
0
. Let
\tilde{h}_{\delta}(t\,,x)

be defined as in (
3.2
), where
Z(t\,,x)
is the solution to (
3.1
) subject to narrow wedge initial condition
Z(0)\equiv\delta_{0}
. By Theorem
3.1
, we know that for each
\delta>0
, the process
\{\tilde{h}_{\delta}(t,x):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated. Theorem 2.2(3) of
[
29
]
ensures that
the finite-dimensional distributions of
\{\tilde{h}_{\delta}(t,x):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
converge to those of

\{\mathfrak{h}(t,x;\partial_{0}):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
as
\delta\to 0
. This implies that the process
\{\mathfrak{h}(t,x;\partial_{0}):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated. In particular, the process
\{\mathfrak{h}(1,x;\partial_{0}):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated and hence the process
\{\mathfrak{h}(1,x;\partial_{0})+x^{2}:x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is also associated. Moreover, since the process
\{\mathfrak{h}(1,x;\partial_{0})+x^{2}:x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
has the same finite-dimensional distributions as the Airy
2
process
\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}

(see
[
22
, (4.14)]
), we conclude that the Airy
2
process is associated.
∎

.

The association property of Airy processes can also be derived from last passage percolation by extending the argument in
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
; see also
[
4
, Remark 2]
.

4
Ergodicity and CLT

We prove the ergodicity and central limit theorem for the Airy
1
process in this section. We first prove the ergodicity.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
: ergodicity.

Chen et al.
[
10
, Lemma 7.2]
present a general criterion on the ergodicity for stationary process,
which has been modified to a variant by
Balan and Zheng
[
3
]
. According to Balan and Zheng
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
,
in order to prove that the Airy
1
process is ergodic, we need to verify that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\text{\rm Var}\bigg(\int_{[0,N]}G\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{i}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x+\zeta_{j})\Big)\mathrm{d}x\bigg)=0,

(4.1)

for all integers
k\geq 1
, for every
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
,
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and for
G\in\{x\mapsto\cos x,x\mapsto\sin x\}
.

Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\text{\rm Var}\bigg(\int_{[0,N]}G\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{i}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x+\zeta_{j})\Big)\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\Cov\bigg(G\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x+\zeta_{j})\Big)\,,G\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\mathcal{A}_{1}(y+\zeta_{j})\Big)\bigg).

(4.2)

Since the Airy
1
process is associated, we apply the inequality (
2.2
) with
Y_{j}=\mathcal{A}_{1}(x+\zeta_{j})
for
j=1,\ldots,k
,
Y_{j}=\mathcal{A}_{1}(y+\zeta_{j-k})
for

j=k+1,\ldots,2k
,

f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{2k})=G(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}y_{j})
, and
g(y_{1},\ldots,y_{2k})=G(\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k}b_{j-k}y_{j})
, to see that

\displaystyle\Cov\bigg(G\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{i}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x+\zeta_{j})\Big)\,,G\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{i}\mathcal{A}_{1}(y+\zeta_{j})\Big)\bigg)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|b_{j}||b_{\ell}|\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x+\zeta_{j})\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y+\zeta_{\ell})).

Here, we also use the fact that
\text{\rm Lip}_{j}(f)\leq|b_{j}|
for
1\leq j\leq k
,
\text{\rm Lip}_{j}(f)=0
for
j=k+1,\ldots,2k
and

\text{\rm Lip}_{\ell}(g)=0
for
1\leq\ell\leq k
,
\text{\rm Lip}_{\ell}(g)\leq|b_{\ell-k}|
for
\ell=k+1,\ldots,2k
.
According to
[
5
, Corollary 2.2]
, there exist constants
c,c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))\leq\mathrm{e}^{cx^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}|x|^{3}}\leq K(x):=c_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{2}x^{2}}.

Hence, the left-hand side of (
4
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\frac{\sup_{1\leq j\leq k}b_{j}^{2}}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{[0,N]^{2}}K(x-y+\zeta_{j}-\zeta_{\ell})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=\left[\sup_{1\leq j\leq k}b_{j}^{2}\right]\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(\bm{1}_{N}*\tilde{\bm{1}}_{N}*K\right)(\zeta_{j}-\zeta_{\ell}),

where the function
\bm{1}_{N}
is defined as
\bm{1}_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{1}_{[0,N]}(x)
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\tilde{\bm{1}}_{N}(x)=\bm{1}_{N}(-x)
.
The function
\bm{1}_{N}*\tilde{\bm{1}}_{N}*K
is non-negative definite and hence maximized at the origin. Hence we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\text{\rm Var}\bigg(\int_{[0,N]}g\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{i}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x+\zeta_{j})\Big)\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{1\leq j\leq k}b_{j}^{2}\,k^{2}\left(\bm{1}_{N}*\tilde{\bm{1}}_{N}*K\right)(0)\leq\sup_{1\leq j\leq k}b_{j}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{N}\int_{-N}^{N}K(x)\mathrm{d}x

which converges to
0
as
N\to\infty
. Thus, (
4.1
) is verified and we complete the proof of ergodicity.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We refer to
[
14
,
26
]
for the ergodicity of the Airy
2
process.

We proceed to prove the central limit theorem (
1.1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
: CLT.

We will apply
[
23
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
27
, Theorem 1.2.19]
) to prove the central limit theorem in Theorem
1.1
.
We can assume that the limit in (
1.1
) is taken along integers.
Denote

\displaystyle X_{k}=\int_{k}^{k+1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}x,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since the Airy
1
process is stationary and
\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)
has finite second moment, the process
\{X_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

is stationary and
X_{0}
has
finite second moment. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

\displaystyle X_{k}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{1}(k+\frac{j}{n}),\quad n\geq 1.

Clearly, for each
n\geq 1
, the process
\{X_{k}^{(n)}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is associated.
Moreover, since the Airy
1
process has continuous sample paths (see
[
28
]
), we know that as
n\to\infty
,
the finite-dimensional distributions of

\{X_{k}^{(n)}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
converge to those of
\{X_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Hence, the process
\{X_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is associated.

In order to apply
[
23
, Theorem 2]
, it remains to verify that

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Cov(X_{0}\,,X_{k})

is finite. Indeed, by the stationarity of the Airy
1
process,

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Cov\left(\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}x\,,\int_{k}^{k+1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}y\right)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\int_{k}^{k+1}\mathrm{d}y\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x-y)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))\mathrm{d}x,

which is finite by
[
5
, Corollary 2.2]
.

Therefore, all the conditions in
[
23
, Theorem 2]
are met and we conclude that as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{X_{0}+\ldots+X_{N-1}-N\mathrm{E}[X_{0}]}{\sqrt{N}}\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm N}(0,\sigma^{2}),

which implies (
1.1
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

(1) We can also apply
[
23
, Theorem 2]
to derive a discrete version of (
1.1
), that is, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(1)+\ldots+\mathcal{A}_{1}(N)-N\mathrm{E}[\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)]}{\sqrt{N}}\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm N}(0,\tau^{2})

with
\tau^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(k)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))
.

(2) One can apply the Berry-Esseen theorem for associated sequences (see for instance
[
27
, p.15-16]
) to obtain the rate
of convergence in the central limit theorem for the Airy
1
process.

(3) Recall that
\{\mathfrak{h}(t,x;0):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
denotes the KPZ fixed point (see
[
22
]
) with flat initial data.
Using the scaling invariance property of the KPZ fixed point (see
[
22
, Theorem 4.5(i)]
) and (
1.1
),
we deduce that for fixed
t>0
, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{N}\left(\mathfrak{h}(t,x;0)-\mathrm{E}[\mathfrak{h}(t,x;0)]\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm N}(0,2^{4/3}t^{4/3}\sigma^{2}),

where
\sigma^{2}
is the quantity given in Theorem
1.1
. One may expect that a corresponding
functional central limit theorem in
t
also holds.

Similarly, we can also establish the central limit theorem for the Airy
2
process.

Proposition 4.3
.

As
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{N}(\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)-\mathrm{E}[\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)])\ \mathrm{d}x\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm N}(0,\tilde{\sigma}^{2}),

where
\tilde{\sigma}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{2}(0))\mathrm{d}x\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The proof follows along the same lines as that of (
1.1
), applying
[
23
, Theorem 2]
with the stationarity (see
[
26
]
), association (see Proposition
3.2
) of the Airy
2
process and the fact that the covariance of the Airy
2
process is integrable since the decay rate of

\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{2}(0))
is
x^{-2}
as
x\to\infty
(see
[
35
]
).
∎

We conclude this section by another application of Newman’s inequality, which is used to study the Lebesgue measure of the set
x\in[0,N]
where
\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)
exceeds a high level. The following result is analogous to
[
10
, Corollary 9.5]
, where the Poincaré inequality is used to estimate the covariance of the solution to stochastic partial differential equation. In the context of the Airy
1
process, when it comes to the estimate of covariance, Newman’s inequality plays the role as the Poincaré inequality.

Corollary 4.4
.

For
\alpha\in(0,3/4)
, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log N}\log\left(\int_{0}^{N}\bm{1}_{\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>\frac{1}{2}(\alpha\log N)^{2/3}\}}\mathrm{d}x\right)\to 1-\frac{2}{3}\alpha,\quad\text{in probability}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Choose and fix
\alpha\in(0,3/4)
. For
N\geq 1
, we define
a_{N}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha\log N)^{2/3}
and the two functions

\displaystyle G_{N}(z):=1\wedge(z-a_{N}+1)_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{N}(z)=1\wedge(z-a_{N})_{+},\quad z\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Recall that
\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq s\}=F_{1}(2s)
, where
F_{1}
denotes the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution function.
According to
[
15
, Theorem 1]
,
s^{-3/2}\log\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)>\frac{1}{2}s\}\to-\frac{2}{3}
as
s\to+\infty
. By stationarity of the Airy
1
process,
we see that as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x=N^{1-\frac{2}{3}\alpha+o(1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}G_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x=N^{1-\frac{2}{3}\alpha+o(1)}.

(4.4)

Using Chebychev’s inequality, we have for any fixed
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\left|\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x-\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right|>\epsilon\,\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\text{\rm Var}\left(\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right)\epsilon^{-2}\left|\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right|^{-2}.

(4.5)

Notice that for all
N\geq 1

\displaystyle\text{\rm Var}\left(\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\Cov(g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\,,g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)))

\displaystyle\leq[\text{\rm Lip}(g_{N})]^{2}\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y))

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y))\leq N\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))\mathrm{d}x,

(4.6)

where the first inequality holds by the association property of the Airy
1
process and (
2.2
), and the second equality is because
\text{\rm Lip}(g_{N})=1
.
Hence, we substitute (
4
) into (
4
) to see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\left|\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x-\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right|>\epsilon\,\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\epsilon^{-2}N^{\frac{4}{3}\alpha-1+o(1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\alpha\in(0,3/4)
, we obtain that as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{E}\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x}\to 1\quad\text{in probability}.

Taking logarithm and using (
4.4
), we conclude that as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\int_{0}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right)}{\log N}\to 1-\frac{2}{3}\alpha\quad\text{in probability}.

Similarly, we can prove that
as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\int_{0}^{N}G_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right)}{\log N}\to 1-\frac{2}{3}\alpha\quad\text{in probability}.

Since
g_{N}\leq\bm{1}_{(a_{N},\infty)}\leq G_{N}
, the preceding displays imply (
4.3
) and hence complete the proof.
∎

.

The convergence in (
4.3
) also holds almost surely. This follows from a standard subsequencing
and sandwich type argument (see the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 2.3.9]
). We omit the details here.

5
Poisson approximation

We prove Theorem
1.3
in this section. The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on inequality (
2.3
) and some standard arguments; see
[
24
, Theorem 11]
for a general result on limit theorems for sums of associated variables.

The indicator function in (
1.4
) is not Lipschitz continuous. In order to apply the inequalities (
2.2
) and
(
2.3
) to derive the Poisson limit theorem, we will first approximate the indicator function in (
1.4
) by a Lipschitz continuous function. Fix
\lambda>0
. For
N\geq 2
, we introduce the function

\displaystyle g_{N}(z)=1\wedge\frac{(z-\frac{1}{2}{\rm T}(\lambda/N))_{+}}{[{\rm T}(\lambda/(N+1))-{\rm T}(\lambda/N)]/2},\quad z\in{\mathbb{R}},

(5.1)

where we recall that
\rm T
denotes by the inverse function of
s\mapsto 1-F_{1}(s)
.
It is clear that for all
N\geq 2
,

\displaystyle g_{N}(z)\leq\bm{1}_{(\frac{1}{2}{\rm T}(\lambda/N),\infty)}(z)\leq g_{N-1}(z),\quad\text{for all $z\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

(5.2)

and

\displaystyle\text{\rm Lip}(g_{N})=\frac{2}{{\rm T}(\lambda/(N+1))-{\rm T}(\lambda/N)}.

(5.3)

We first prove the following result on Poisson approximation for the Airy
1
process.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix
\lambda>0
.
Let
g_{N}
be defined as in (
5.1
) for
N\geq 2
. Let
\{x_{k,N}\}_{N\geq 1,1\leq k\leq N}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy the
condition in (
1.3
). Then, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm Poisson}(\lambda).

(5.4)

Proof.

Step 1
.
Let
U_{1,N},\ldots,U_{N,N}
be independent random variables, each having the same distribution
as
g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))
(for this, we can enlarge the probability space if necessary). The first task will be to show
that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N}\xrightarrow{\rm d}{\rm Poisson}(\lambda),\quad\text{as $N\to\infty$}.

(5.5)

Notice that the law of the family of the random variables
\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N},N\geq 1
is tight. This is because that
by the definition of
g_{N}
in (
5.1
),

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 1}\mathrm{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N}\right]=\sup_{N\geq 1}\left(N\mathrm{E}[g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))]\right)\leq\sup_{N\geq 1}\left(N\mathrm{E}\left[\bm{1}_{(\frac{1}{2}{\rm T}(\lambda/N),\infty)}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))\right]\right)=\lambda,

where the first inequality is due to the first inequality in (
5.2
). Thus, in order to prove (
5.5
),
it suffices to prove that the moment generating function of
\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N}
converges to that of
{\rm Poisson}(\lambda)
, as
N\to\infty
.

For
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\theta g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{N}(\theta):=\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\theta\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N}}\right]=\left(\phi_{N}(\theta)\right)^{N}.

Thanks to (
5.2
), for all
\theta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(\theta)\geq\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\theta\bm{1}_{(\frac{1}{2}{\rm T}(\lambda/N),\infty)}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))}\right]=\frac{\lambda}{N}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta}+(1-\frac{\lambda}{N})=1-\frac{\lambda}{N}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\theta}),

and

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(\theta)\leq\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\theta\bm{1}_{(\frac{1}{2}{\rm T}(\lambda/(N+1)),\infty)}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))}\right]=1-\frac{\lambda}{N+1}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\theta}).

This leads to that for all
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{N}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\theta})\right)^{N}\leq\Phi_{N}(\theta)\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{N+1}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\theta})\right)^{N}.

(5.6)

Letting
N\to\infty
, we obtain that for all
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\Phi_{N}(\theta)=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\theta})}.

The above display also holds when
\theta\leq 0
by switching the first and third terms in (
5.6
).
This verifies (
5.5
).

Step 2
. We proceed to compare the characteristic functions of
\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))
and

\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N}
. First, we have for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\theta\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))}\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\theta\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\left|\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\theta g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))}\right]-\prod_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\theta g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))}\right]\right|.

(5.7)

Since the Airy
1
process is associated and
g_{N}
is a nondecreasing function,
the random vector
(g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{1,N})),\ldots,g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{N,N}))
is associated. Clearly, each
component of this random vector has finite variance. Hence, by (
2.3
), we have for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\theta g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))}\right]-\prod_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\theta g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j,k\leq N\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}\theta^{2}\Cov(g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j,N}))\,,g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N})))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}[\text{\rm Lip}(g_{N})]^{2}\theta^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j,k\leq N\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j,N})\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N})),

(5.8)

where the second inequality is due to (
2.2
).
According to
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists a positive constant
c^{\prime}
such that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}

with
|x-y|>1
,

\displaystyle\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y))\leq\mathrm{e}^{c^{\prime}|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}|x-y|^{3}}.

(5.9)

Thus, a combination of (
5
), (
5
) and (
5.9
) yields that for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\theta\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k,N}))}\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\theta\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{k,N}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}[\text{\rm Lip}(g_{N})]^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j,k\leq N\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}\mathrm{e}^{c^{\prime}|x_{j,N}-x_{k,N}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}|x_{j,N}-x_{k,N}|^{3}}

(5.10)

for all sufficiently large
N
.

Under the assumption (
1.3
), there exists
\alpha_{1}>0
such that for all sufficiently large
N

6
Asymptotic behavior of the maximum

In this section, we prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
. We start with the following estimate on the upper tail probability of the maximum of the Airy
1
process.

Proposition 6.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that for all
M\geq 2

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>M\right\}\leq C\,M^{7/4}\mathrm{e}^{(2-\sqrt{2})M+\sqrt{2M}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}},

(6.1)

and it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>M\right\}}{M^{3/2}}=-\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}.

(6.2)

Proof.

Because
\mathrm{P}\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>M\}\geq\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)>M\}
, the equality (
6.2
) follows immediately from the estimate (
6.1
) and the asymptotic behavior of the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution
(see
[
15
, Theorem 1]
). In order to prove (
6.1
), we recall from
[
28
, Theorem 4]
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>M\right\}

\displaystyle=1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq M\right\}=1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq M,\text{for all $x\in[0,1]$}\right\}

\displaystyle=1-\det\left(I-B_{0}+\Lambda_{[0,1]}^{g}\mathrm{e}^{-\Delta}B_{0}\right)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})},

(6.3)

where in the Fredholm determinant, the kernel
\Lambda_{[0,1]}^{g}
has the formula given by (1.15) of
[
28
]
with
g\equiv M
on
[0,1]
and the kernel
B_{0}
is
given in terms of the Airy function (see the equality below (1.5) of
[
28
]
).

We will adopt the method in the proof of
[
28
, Proposition 2.3(a)]
to estimate the above tail probability.
We introduce the operator
U
(depending on
M
) as

\displaystyle Uf(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2M}x}f(x).

Let
\varphi(z)=\sqrt{1+z^{2}}
and write

\displaystyle V(x,z)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{e}^{\Delta}-\Lambda_{[0,1]}^{g}\right)(x,z)\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2M}x}\varphi(z)\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2M}z},

\displaystyle W(z,y)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\Delta}B_{0}\right)(z,y)\varphi(z)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2M}z}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2M}y}.

Then

\displaystyle U\left(B_{0}-\Lambda_{[0,1]}^{g}\mathrm{e}^{-\Delta}B_{0}\right)U^{-1}=VW.

We will estimate the Hilbert-Schmidt norms
\|V\|_{2}
and
\|W\|_{2}
and the use the inequality
\|VW\|_{1}\leq\|V\|_{2}\|W\|_{2}
to give an upper bound on the trace norm

\|VW\|_{1}
.

We first estimate
\|W\|_{2}
. Denote by
{\rm Ai}
the Airy function and
\|\varphi^{-1}\|_{2}
the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-norm of
\varphi^{-1}
. Using (1.9) of
[
28
]
, we write

\displaystyle\|W\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}+2(\sqrt{2M}-1)(x+y)}}{\varphi(x)^{2}}{\rm Ai}(x+y+1)^{2}=\|\varphi^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}+2(\sqrt{2M}-1)y}{\rm Ai}(y+1)^{2}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\|\varphi^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}-2(\sqrt{2M}-1)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{2(\sqrt{2M}-1)y}{\rm Ai}(y)^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

By the estimate (2.19) of
[
28
]
on the Airy function, we have for all
M\geq 2

\displaystyle\|W\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}-2(\sqrt{2M}-1)}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{2(\sqrt{2M}-1)y}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{2(\sqrt{2M}-1)y-\frac{4}{3}y^{3/2}}\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}^{\prime}(\sqrt{2M}-1)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2(\sqrt{2M}-1)}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}(\sqrt{2M}-1)^{3}},

(6.4)

where the second inequality holds by Lemma
A.1
.

We proceed to estimate
\|V\|_{2}
. We use the formula (1.15) of
[
28
]
to write

\displaystyle V(x,y)=\frac{\varphi(y)}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4}-\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}\mathrm{P}_{\hat{b}(0)=x,\hat{b}(1)=y}\left(\hat{b}(t)\geq M\,\,\text{for some $t\in[0,1]$}\right),

where
\hat{b}
denotes a Brownian bridge from
x
at time
0
to
y
at time
1
with diffusion constant
2
. This probability equals
\mathrm{e}^{-(x-M)(y-M)}

if
x\leq M,y\leq M
and
1
otherwise (see line one on page 621 of
[
28
]
or page 69 in
[
6
]
). Hence,

\displaystyle\|V\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{(-\infty,M]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,(1+y^{2})\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4}-\sqrt{2M}(x+y)-(x-M)(y-M)}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,M]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,(1+y^{2})\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4}-\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{(-\infty,M]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)-2(x-M)(y-M)},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{(-\infty,M]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)-2(x-M)(y-M)},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,M]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)},

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,M]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}.

Using change of variable [
x\to x+M,y\to y+M
],

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}}{4\pi}\int_{(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}\leq c_{2}^{\prime}\mathrm{e}^{8M}\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}

(6.5)

for all
M\geq 2
, where the inequality holds by Lemma
A.2
.
Similarly, using the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}}{4\pi}\int_{(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,(y+M)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}

\displaystyle\leq 2M^{2}I_{1}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}}{2\pi}\int_{(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}\leq c_{3}^{\prime}M^{2}\mathrm{e}^{8M}\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}

(6.6)

for all
M\geq 2
, where the second inequality holds by
A.3

We move on to estimate
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
. We use change of variable [
x\to x+M,y\to y+M
] to see that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}\leq c_{4}^{\prime}\sqrt{M}\mathrm{e}^{4M}\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}

(6.7)

for all
M\geq 2
, where the inequality holds by Lemma
A.4
. It remains to estimate
I_{4}
. By change of variable [
x\to x+M,y\to y+M
],

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,(y+M)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}

\displaystyle\leq 2M^{2}I_{3}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\sqrt{2M}(x+y)}\leq c_{5}^{\prime}M^{5/2}\mathrm{e}^{4M}\mathrm{e}^{-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}

(6.8)

for all
M\geq 2
,
where the second inequality holds by (
6.7
) and Lemma
A.5
.

Now we combine the estimates (
6
), (
6.5
), (
6
),(
6.7
) and (
6
) to see that there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
M\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\|V\|_{2}^{2}\|W\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\,M^{7/2}\mathrm{e}^{(8-2\sqrt{2})M}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}(\sqrt{2M}-1)^{3}-4\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}=C^{\prime}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2/3}M^{7/2}\mathrm{e}^{(4-2\sqrt{2})M+2\sqrt{2M}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{8}{3}\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}},

which implies that for all
M\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\|VW\|_{1}\leq\|V\|_{2}\|W\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{C^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-1/3}M^{7/4}\mathrm{e}^{(2-\sqrt{2})M+\sqrt{2M}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}.

(6.9)

Thus,
U(B_{0}-\Lambda_{[0,1]}^{g}\mathrm{e}^{-\Delta}B_{0})U^{-1}
is a trace class operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and we deduce from (
6
) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>M\right\}

\displaystyle=1-\det\left(I-U(B_{0}-\Lambda_{[0,1]}^{g}\mathrm{e}^{-\Delta}B_{0})U^{-1}\right)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle=1-\det\left(I-VW\right)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|VW\|_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\|VW\|_{1}+1},

(6.10)

where the inequality follows from (3.1) of
[
28
]
. Therefore, we conclude from (
6.9
) and (
6
) that there exists
C>0
such that for all
M\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>M\right\}\leq C\,M^{7/4}\mathrm{e}^{(2-\sqrt{2})M+\sqrt{2M}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}M^{3/2}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

We remark that another asymptotics on the probability that the Airy
1
process stays below a given
threshold
c
for a time span of length
L
is investigated in
[
18
]
.

Before we prove Theorem
1.4
, we present a probability inequality for the Airy
1
process: there exists a constant
K>0
such that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq s,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)\leq t\right\}-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq s\right\}\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)\leq t\right\}\right)\leq K\left[\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y))\right]^{1/3}.

(6.11)

The above inequality follows from
[
27
, (6.2.20)]
(see also
[
27
, Theorem 6.2.15]
) because the Airy
1
process is associated and its one-point distribution, i.e., the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution has bounded and continuous density and finite second moment.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We first show that almost surely

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

(6.12)

Let
\beta
be a positive number that is strictly less than
\frac{3}{2}
. Choose and fix
a,\epsilon\in(0,1)

(
a
close to
1
and
\epsilon
close to
0
) such that

\displaystyle\beta<\frac{3a}{2(1+\epsilon)}.

(6.13)

Define
x_{j}=jN/\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor

for
j=1,\ldots,\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor
. Here the notation
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
denotes the largest integer which is less than or equal to
x
. By the stationarity of the Airy
1
process,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\leq\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}-\prod_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}.

Since the Airy
1
process is associated, by (
2.1
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\bigg(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3},\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}.

We apply (
6.11
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\left[\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{j})\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(x_{k}))\right]^{1/3}+\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}.

(6.14)

We can assume that
N
is sufficiently large such that
N/\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor>\max\{1,3c^{\prime}\}
with
c^{\prime}
being the number in
(
5.9
),
\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor>N^{a}/2
and
(\beta\log N)^{2/3}>C_{2}
, where
C_{2}
is the constant in (
2.11
).
Hence,
by (
5.9
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\leq K\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{c^{\prime}}{3}|x_{j}-x_{k}|^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{9}|x_{j}-x_{k}|^{3}}+F_{1}\left((\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq K\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}|x_{j}-x_{k}|^{3}}+\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2}{3}(1+\epsilon)\beta\log N}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq KN^{2a}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}N^{3(1-a)}}+\mathrm{e}^{-N^{-\frac{2}{3}(1+\epsilon)\beta}\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\leq KN^{2a}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}N^{3(1-a)}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}N^{-\frac{2}{3}(1+\epsilon)\beta+a}},

where in the second inequality, we use the first inequality in (
2.11
), and in the third inequality, we use the fact

1-x\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x}
for all
x\geq 0
. Since

by (

), we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}<\infty.

Therefore, Borel-Cantelli’s lemma implies that almost surely,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2/3}.

The above liminf is taken along integers. By the monotonicity of
\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)

in

, we see that the preceding also
holds when the liminf is taken
along real numbers. Finally, by letting
\beta\uparrow\frac{3}{2}
, we obtain (

We turn to proving that almost surely

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

(6.15)

Let
\gamma
be a positive number that is strictly larger than
\frac{3}{4\sqrt{2}}
.
Choose and fix
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\gamma>\frac{1}{\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}-\epsilon}.

(6.16)

For this fixed
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, Proposition
6.1
ensures that there exists constant
C_{1}>0
depending on
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>M\right\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}-\epsilon)M^{3/2}},\quad\text{for all $M\geq C_{1}$}.

(6.17)

Assume for the moment that
N
is a sufficiently large integer such that
(\gamma\log N)^{2/3}\geq C_{1}
with
C_{1}
being the constant in (
6.17
).
By the stationarity of the Airy
1
process,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>(\gamma\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P}\left\{\cup_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\max_{j-1\leq x\leq j}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>(\gamma\log N)^{3/2}\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq N\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>(\gamma\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq N\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}-\epsilon)\gamma\log N}=N^{1-(\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}-\epsilon)\gamma},

where the second inequality follows from (
6.17
). Since
(\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}-\epsilon)\gamma>1
by (
6.16
), letting

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)>(\gamma\log 2^{n})^{2/3}\right\}<\infty,

which implies that almost surely

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)}{(\log 2^{n})^{2/3}}\leq\gamma^{2/3}

by Borel-Cantelli’s lemma.
Since the quantity
\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)
is monotone in
N
, we obtain that almost surely

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}\leq\gamma^{2/3}.

Letting
\gamma\downarrow\frac{3}{4\sqrt{2}}
, we prove (
6.15
).

Theorem
1.4
follows from (
6.12
) and (
6.15
).
∎

We proceed to prove Theorem
1.5
. Recall from
[
15
, Theorem 1]
that for
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}_{2}
depending on
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}+\epsilon)s^{3/2}}\leq\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)\geq s\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}-\epsilon)s^{3/2}},\quad\text{for all $s\geq\tilde{C}_{2}$}.

(6.18)

Analogous to (
6.11
), using the association property of the Airy
2
process and the fact that the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution has bounded continuous
probability density function and finite second moment, we see from
[
27
, (6.2.20)]
(see also
[
27
, Theorem 6.2.15]
) that there exists a constant
K>0
such that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq s,\mathcal{A}_{2}(y)\leq t\right\}-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq s\right\}\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(y)\leq t\right\}\right)\leq K\left[\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{2}(y))\right]^{1/3}.

(6.19)

Now we start the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We first prove the lower bound in (
1.6
). The strategy is similar to that of the proof of (
1.5
), using the
association property of the Airy
2
process, the asymptotic behavior of the covariance of the Airy
2
process
and the tail distribution of the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution.

Let
\beta
be a positive number that is strictly less than
\frac{1}{4}
. Choose and fix
a\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
,

\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\beta<\frac{a}{\frac{4}{3}+\epsilon}.

(6.20)

Define
x_{j}=jN/\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor

for
j=1,\ldots,\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor
. Then by the stationarity of the Airy
2
process,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\leq\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{j})\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{j})\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}-\prod_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{j})\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}.

We can assume that
N
is sufficiently large such that
\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor>N^{a}/2
and
(\beta\log N)^{2/3}>C^{\prime}_{2}
,
where
C^{\prime}_{2}
is the constant in (
6.18
). Since the Airy
2
process is associated (see Proposition
3.2
), by (
2.1
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\bigg(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{j})\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3},\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{k})\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{j})\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{k})\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)>(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\left[\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{j})\,,\mathcal{A}_{2}(x_{k}))\right]^{1/3}+\left(1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)>(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor},

(6.21)

where the second inequality holds by (
6.19
). Moreover, since the decay rate of the covariance

\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{2}(x),

\mathcal{A}_{2}(0))
is
x^{-2}
as
x\to\infty
(see
[
35
]
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq K^{\prime}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\frac{1}{|x_{k}-x_{j}|^{2/3}}+\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}+\epsilon)\beta\log N}\right)^{\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq K^{\prime}N^{\frac{2}{3}(a-1)}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor}\frac{1}{|k-j|^{2/3}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lfloor N^{a}\right\rfloor N^{-(\frac{4}{3}+\epsilon)\beta}},

where we have used (
6.18
) in the first inequality and
1-x\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x}
for all
x\geq 0
in the second inequality.
Because for integer
m\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{(k-j)^{2/3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{j^{2/3}}\leq\sum_{k=2}^{m}\int_{0}^{k-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^{2/3}}=3\sum_{k=2}^{m}(k-1)^{1/3}\leq\frac{9}{4}\int_{0}^{m}x^{1/3}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{9}{4}m^{4/3},

we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq(\beta\log N)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9K^{\prime}}{4}N^{\frac{2}{3}(a-1)+\frac{4}{3}a}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}N^{a-(\frac{4}{3}+\epsilon)\beta}}.

Letting
N=2^{n}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq(\beta\log 2^{n})^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9K^{\prime}}{4}2^{-\frac{2}{3}(1-3a)n}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}2^{(a-(\frac{4}{3}+\epsilon)\beta)n}}.

Since
a<\frac{1}{3}
and
a>(\frac{4}{3}+\epsilon)\beta
(see (
6.20
)), we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leq(\beta\log 2^{n})^{2/3}\right\}<\infty.

Hence, by Borel-Cantelli’s lemma, we have almost surely,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)}{(\log 2^{n})^{2/3}}\geq\beta^{2/3}.

A monotonicity argument yields that almost surely,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)}{(\log N)^{2/3}}\geq\beta^{2/3}.

Letting
\beta\uparrow\frac{1}{4}
, we obtain the lower bound in (
1.6
).

We proceed to prove the upper bound in (
1.6
). By
[
9
, Corollary 1.3]
and stationarity of the Airy
2

process, there exists a positive constant
s_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq y\leq 2}|\mathcal{A}_{2}(x+y)-\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)|\geq s\right)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s^{2}}{16}},\quad\text{for all $s>s_{0}$}.

(6.22)

Let
\gamma
be a positive number that is strictly larger than
\frac{3}{4}
. Choose and fix
\delta\in(0,1)
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle(\frac{4}{3}-\epsilon)\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\gamma>1.

(6.23)

Assume
N
is sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle\delta(\gamma\log N)^{2/3}>\tilde{C}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad(1-\delta)(\gamma\log N)^{2/3}>s_{0},

where the constants
\tilde{C}_{2}
and
s_{0}
are given in (
6.18
) and (
6.22
) respectively. By the
triangle inequality and stationarity of the Airy
2
process,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\max_{0\leq x\leq 2N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\geq(\gamma\log(2N))^{2/3}\right)\leq\mathrm{P}\left(\cup_{j=0}^{N-1}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(2j)\geq\delta(\gamma\log(2N))^{2/3}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathrm{P}\left(\cup_{j=1}^{N}\sup_{y\in[2(j-1),2j]}|\mathcal{A}_{2}(y)-\mathcal{A}_{2}(2(j-1))|\geq(1-\delta)(\gamma\log(2N))^{2/3}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leq N\mathrm{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)\geq\delta(\gamma\log(2N))^{2/3}\right)+N\mathrm{P}\left(\sup_{y\in[0,\,2]}|\mathcal{A}_{2}(y)-\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)|\geq(1-\delta)(\gamma\log(2N))^{2/3}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leq N\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}-\epsilon)\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\gamma\log(2N)}+N\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(1-\delta)^{2}}{16}(\gamma\log(2N))^{4/3}}=c_{0}N^{-(\frac{4}{3}-\epsilon)\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\gamma+1}+N\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(1-\delta)^{2}}{16}(\gamma\log(2N))^{4/3}},

where the last inequality follows from (
6.18
) and (
6.22
).
Letting
N=2^{n}
and because of (
6.23
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}\left(\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n+1}}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\geq(\gamma\log 2^{n+1})^{2/3}\right)<\infty,

which implies that almost surely,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n+1}}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)}{\log 2^{n+1}}\leq\gamma^{2/3},

thanks to Borel-Cantelli’s lemma. Appealing to the monotonicity argument again, we conclude that almost surely,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)}{\log N}\leq\gamma^{2/3}.

Letting
\gamma\downarrow\frac{3}{4}
, we obtain the upper bound in (
1.6
).

The proof is complete.
∎

Appendix

We include in this section a few technical lemmas which are used in the proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a constant
c_{1}>0
such that for all
\theta\geq 1

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{2\theta y-\frac{4}{3}y^{3/2}}\mathrm{d}y\leq c_{1}\theta^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}\theta^{3}}.

(A.1)

Proof.

The estimate follows from a standard calculation in the steepest-descent method (see for instance
[
2
, Appendix B]
). We first use change of variable to see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{2\theta y-\frac{4}{3}y^{3/2}}\mathrm{d}y=\theta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{3}(2y-\frac{4}{3}y^{3/2})}\mathrm{d}y.

Denote
h(y)=2y-\frac{4}{3}y^{3/2}
. It is clear that the function
h
is increasing on
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, maximized at
1
in
[\frac{1}{2},9]
and
h(y)\leq-2y
for all
y\geq 9
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{3}(2y-\frac{3}{4}y^{3/2})}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{3}(2y-\frac{3}{4}y^{3/2})}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{9}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{3}(2y-\frac{3}{4}y^{3/2})}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{9}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2\theta^{3}y}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{h(1/2)\theta^{3}}+\frac{17}{2}\mathrm{e}^{h(1)\theta^{3}}+\frac{1}{2\theta^{3}}\mathrm{e}^{-18\theta^{3}}\leq c_{1}\mathrm{e}^{h(1)\theta^{3}}=c_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}\theta^{3}}.

This proves Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

There exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that for all
\theta\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}\leq c_{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{4\theta^{2}}.

(A.2)

Proof.

Using change of variable,

\displaystyle\int_{(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}=\theta^{2}\int_{[0,\infty)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\theta^{2}\int_{[0,4]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}+2\theta^{2}\int_{0}^{4}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\theta^{2}\int_{[4,\infty)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}.

It is clear that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,4]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}\leq 16\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{4}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}\leq 4\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{y^{2}}{2}+2y)}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{4}{\theta}\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{[4,\infty)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}\leq\left(\int_{4}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{y^{2}}{2}+2y)}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}\leq\theta^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{4\theta^{2}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}.

The preceding estimates prove Lemma
A.2
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

There exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that for all
\theta\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}\leq c_{3}\,\theta^{2}\mathrm{e}^{4\theta^{2}}.

(A.3)

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma A.2. Using change of variable,

\displaystyle\int_{(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}=\theta^{4}\int_{[0,\infty)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\theta^{4}\int_{[0,4]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}+2\theta^{4}\int_{0}^{4}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,(x+y)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\theta^{4}\int_{[4,\infty)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}.

Evidently,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,4]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}\leq 256\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{4}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,(x+y)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}\leq 4\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{y^{2}}{2}+2y)}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{8}{\theta^{3}}\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{32}{\theta}\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}\mathrm{d}y.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{[4,\infty)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x+y))}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+2x)}\mathrm{d}x\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{y^{2}}{2}+2y)}\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{\theta^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{4\theta^{2}}

for some constant
C>0
. The preceding estimates prove Lemma
A.3
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

There exists a constant
c_{4}>0
such that for all
\theta\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}\leq c_{4}\,\theta\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}.

(A.4)

Proof.

Using change of variable,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}

\displaystyle=\theta^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2(x+y))}:=\theta^{2}(J_{1}+2J_{2}),

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2(x+y))},\quad J_{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2(x+y))}.

We perform change of variables
x+y=u,x-y=v
to see that

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-u}^{u}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{v^{2}}{2}-2u)}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}u\mathrm{e}^{-2\theta^{2}u}\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{\theta^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u\mathrm{e}^{-2u}\mathrm{d}u.

Similarly,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2(x-y))}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-u}^{u}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}-2v)}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}u\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}+2u)}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u|\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(u-2)^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u\leq\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u|\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u+2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\left(\theta^{-2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u|\mathrm{e}^{u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u+2\theta^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u\right).

The bounds on
J_{1},J_{2}
prove Lemma
A.4
.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

There exists a constant
c_{5}>0
such that for all
\theta\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}\leq c_{5}\,\theta^{3}\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}.

(A.5)

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma A.4. We write

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2\theta(x+y)}

\displaystyle=\theta^{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(-\infty,0]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2(x+y))}

\displaystyle:=\theta^{4}(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}),

where

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2(x+y))},

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int^{0}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2(x+y))},

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\int^{0}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}-2(x+y))}.

Using change of variables
x+y=u,x-y=v
,

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-u}^{u}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{4}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{v^{2}}{2}-2u)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\theta^{2}u}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta^{2}v^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}v=\frac{1}{2\theta^{5}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2u}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-v^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}v.

Similarly,

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}-2(x-y))}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-u}^{u}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{4}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}-2v)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-u}^{u}\mathrm{d}v\,u^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}-2v)}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{3}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}+2u)}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u+2|^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta^{2}u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u\leq 4\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(|u|^{3}+8)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta^{2}u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=4\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\left(\theta^{-4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u|^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u+8\theta^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u\right),

where in the fourth inequality, we used the inequality
(|a|+|b|)^{3}\leq 4(|a|^{3}+|b^{3}|)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2}+2(x-y))}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-u}^{u}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{4}e^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}+2v)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{-u}^{u}\mathrm{d}v\,u^{2}e^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}+2v)}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{3}\mathrm{e}^{\theta^{2}(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}+2u)}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathrm{e}^{2\theta^{2}}\left(\theta^{-4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u|^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u+8\theta^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u\right),

where the last inequality holds by the same arguments as in the estimate of
T_{2}
. The bounds on
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
prove Lemma
A.5
.
∎

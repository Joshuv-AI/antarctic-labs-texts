M. Archita

1
Introduction

The structure of a group has been studied by examining at the element orders using different invariants. For example, several authors have studied various structural properties of a finite group using the function
\psi(G)=\sum_{a\in G}o(a),
where
o(a)
denotes the order of
a\in G,
for example see
[
HMLM
]
. In 2017, Garonzi, Martino, and Massimiliano Patassini introduced the function
m(G)=\sum_{a\in G}\frac{1}{o(a)},
in
[
GMP
]
. Recently, in 2023, Morteza Baniasad Azad, Behrooz Khosravi and Hamideh Rashidi studied the group theoretical properties such as solvability, supersolvability and nilpotency of a finite group
G,
using the function
m(G),
(cf.
[
BKR
, Theorem
2.7,

2.8,
and
2.9
]
)

In the following theorem, Garonzi, Martino, and Massimiliano Patassini provided an inequality to investigate the cyclic property of a finite group
G.

Theorem 1.1

( cf.
[
GMP
, Theorem
5
]
)
.

For any finite group
G
of order
n
,
m(G)\geq m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
and equality holds if and only if
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{n},
where
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is the cyclic group of order
n.

In this paper, we continue our study of structural properties of a finite abelian group using
m(G).
The following theorem is our main result, which gives an exact bound and strengthens the bound in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a non-cyclic abelian group of order
n,
and
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is the cyclic group of order
n.
Then

m(G)\geq\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),

where
n=2^{t}\cdot l,

t\geq 0
, and
\gcd(2,l)=1.

In the following proposition, it is shown that there exists a finite group of order
n
such that this lower bound is the best possible.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
n=2^{t}l,
where
t\geq 0,
and
l
is an odd integer. Then

m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\frac{2+t}{2}m(\mathbb{Z}_{l})\hskip 22.76219ptm(\mathbb{Z}_{2l}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2^{t-1}})=\frac{3+t}{2}m(\mathbb{Z}_{l})

and hence

m(\mathbb{Z}_{2l}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2^{t-1}})=\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}).

In the view of Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.3
, it is shown that, for each
n=2^{t}\cdot l,
where
t\geq 0,

l
is an odd, there exist a group of order
n
satisfying
m(G)=\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}).

In the next theorem we provides an improvement for non-cyclic finite abelian groups of an odd order.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a non-cyclic finite abelian group of an odd order
n,
and
p
be the largest prime dividing
n.
Then

m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})>\frac{1}{(p-1)}m(G).

2
Preliminaries

In this section we will describe some instrumental results which are already developed for the function
m(G).

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
BKR
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

If
A\leq G
then
m(A)\leq m(G)
and equality holds if and only if
A=G.

Lemma 2.2

(cf.
[
BKR
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

If
A\trianglelefteq G
then
m(G/A)\leq m(G)
and equality holds if and only if
A=1.

Lemma 2.3

(cf.
[
BKR
, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

For any two finite groups
A_{1}
and
A_{2},
we have
m(A_{1}\times A_{2})\geq m(A_{1})\cdot m(A_{2}).
Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\gcd(|A_{1}|,|A_{2}|)=1.

Lemma 2.4

(cf.
[
I
, Theorem 2.20]
)
.

Let
H
be a proper cyclic subgroup of a finite group
G,
and let
M=core_{G}(H).
Then
|H:M|<|G:H|,
and in particular, if
|H|>|G:H|,
then
M>1.

3
Preparatory Bounds

In the following lemmas we establish some bounds for
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
2\leq q=p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{s}=p
be the prime divisors of
n
and the corresponding Sylow subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are
P_{1},P_{2},\cdots P_{s}.
Let the highest power of each
p_{i}
in
P_{i}
i.e., exponent of
p_{i}
in
(P_{i})
are
r_{i}.
Then

m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})<\frac{2+t}{3+t}\sqrt{n},

where
n=2^{t}\cdot l,

t\geq 0
, and
\gcd(2,l)=1.

Proof.

Let
n=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{r_{i}}).
Lemma
2.3
stipulates
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(m(P_{i})),
and for each
i,
we have
m(P_{i})=(\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}r_{i}+1).

So,
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}r_{i}+1)<\prod_{i=1}^{s}(r_{i}+1).
For each natural number
r\geq 1,
we have
r+1\leq 2^{r}=4^{r/2}<(\frac{2+t}{3+t})^{r/2}(p)^{r/2},
for all
p\geq 7.
We will complete the rest of the proof dividing in the following cases.

case (a):
p_{1}=2,

r_{1}\neq 1
or
r_{1}=0,
and
r_{i}\geq 0
for all
2\leq i\leq s

Note that,
(\frac{r}{2}+1)\leq 2^{r/2}
for
r=0,
and for all natural number
r\geq 2.
Also,
(\frac{2}{3}r+1)<3^{r/2}
for all
r\geq 1
, and
(\frac{4}{5}r+1)<5^{r/2}
for all
r\geq 1
. Now,
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=(\frac{r_{1}}{2}+1)(\frac{2r_{2}}{3}+1)(\frac{4r_{3}}{5}+1)\prod_{i=4}^{s}(\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}r_{i}+1)<2^{r_{1}/2}3^{r_{2}/2}5^{r_{3}/2}\prod_{i=4}^{s}(\frac{2+t}{3+t})^{r_{i}/2}(p_{i})^{r_{i}/2}.
Hence,
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})<\frac{2+t}{3+t}\sqrt{n}.

case (b):
p_{1}=2,

r_{1}=1
and
r_{i}\geq 0
for all
2\leq i\leq s

In this case,
n=2\cdot l,
where
l
is an odd integer. So,
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=m(\mathbb{Z}_{l})\cdot m(\mathbb{Z}_{2})<\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{l}(1+\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{l}=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2}}<\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{n}.

Hence, we obtain that
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})<\frac{2+t}{3+t}\sqrt{n},
where
n=2^{t}\cdot l,

t\geq 0
, and
\gcd(2,l)=1.

∎

Next, in the following lemma we calculate some lower bounds for
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
2\leq q=p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{t}=p
be the prime divisors of
n
and the corresponding Sylow subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are
P_{1},P_{2},\cdots P_{t}.
Then

\frac{1}{p}<m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),\hskip 22.76219pt\hbox{and}\hskip 22.76219ptm(\mathbb{Z}_{n})>\frac{\phi(n)}{n},

where
\phi(n)
is the Euler’s phi-function.

Proof.

Let
n=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(p_{i}^{r_{i}}).
Lemma
2.3
gives
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(m(P_{i})),
and for each
i,
we have
m(P_{i})=(\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}r_{i}+1).
Now for each
i,
it is clear that
\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}r_{i}+1>\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}
and
p_{i+1}\geq p_{i}+1.
Hence it follows that
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})>\frac{1}{p}.

For the another inequality note that,
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}r_{i}+1).
The value of Euler’s
\phi
-function is
\phi(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\phi(p_{i}^{r_{i}})=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}})p_{i}^{r_{i}}.
So, we use the fact
\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}<(\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}\cdot r_{i}+1),
for each
i,
and it follows that
\frac{\phi(n)}{n}<m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}).

∎

To obtain another lower bound for
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),
we will obtain a lower bound for the Euler’s phi-function,
\phi(n),
in the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p
be the largest prime divisor and
q
be the smallest prime divisor of an integer
n>1
respectively. Let
2\leq q=p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{t}=p
be the prime divisors of
n.
Then
\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\geq\frac{q-1}{p}.

Proof.

Let
t
be the number of distinct prime divisors of
n.
If
t=1,
we have
n=p^{r},
for some
r\geq 1,
and
\phi(n)=(p-1)p^{r-1}=(p-1)(n/p).
Next assume that
t>1,
and the proof will be done by induction on
t.

Now
n=p^{s}\cdot m,
where,
(p,m)=1.
Then
m
has exactly
t-1
prime divisors. Hence,

\phi(n)=\phi(p^{s})\cdot\phi(m)\geq(p-1)\frac{p^{s}}{p}\cdot\frac{m(q-1)}{p_{t-1}}=\frac{(p-1)(q-1)n}{pp_{t-1}}\geq\frac{n(q-1)}{p}.

The last inequality holds since,
p-1\geq p_{t-1}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p
be the largest prime divisor and
q
be the smallest prime divisor of an integer
n>1
respectively. Then
m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\geq\frac{(q-1)}{p}.

Proof.

Combining the results of Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
, the result follows.
∎

4
Proofs of the main results

We will first proof Theorem
1.2
. Later, we will prove Theorem
1.4
, which specially applies to finite groups of odd order.

Proof.

proof of Theorem
1.2

Equivalent statement of theorem is: If
m(G)<\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
then
G
is a cyclic group. We will proof it by induction on the cardinality of the finite group
G.

If
|G|=1,
then the result is trivial. So move to the case when
|G|>1.
Suppose that
G
contains a non-trivial normal cyclic subgroup
N
such that
(|N|,|G/N|)=1.
Then
m(G/N)<m(G).
Now, Lemma
2.3
, and Theorem
1.1
gives that,

\displaystyle m(N)\cdot m(G/N)=m(N\times G/N)\leq m(G)<\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{|N|})\cdot m(\mathbb{Z}_{|G/N|}).

Since,
N
is a cyclic group,
N\cong\mathbb{Z}_{|N|}.
So we obtain that
m(G/N)<\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{|G/N|}).
This implies
G/N
is a cyclic subgroup of
G.
Hence,
G
is a cyclic group.

So we may assume that
G
has no non-trivial normal cyclic subgroup with
(|N|,|G/N|)=1.
Now using Lemma
3.1
, we have that

m(G)<\frac{3+t}{2+t}\cdot m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})<\sqrt{n}.

So, there exists an element
x\in G
such that
o(x)>\sqrt{n},

and
|\langle x\rangle|>|G:\langle x\rangle|.

Using Lemma
2.4
, one can obtain
N=
core
{}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
is a non-trivial normal cyclic subgroup of
G.

Suppose
(|N|,|G/N|)\neq 1.
Hence,
G
is an abelian group but not a cyclic group. Thus,
G
has no element of of order
n.
The least possible order of a non-identity element in
G
is
q.
Then

m(G)\leq\frac{n-1}{q}+1.

In
\mathbb{Z}_{n},
the maximum possible value of a non-identity, non-generating element is
\frac{n}{q}.
So,

\frac{\phi(n)}{n}+\frac{(n-\phi(n))q}{n}<1+\frac{\phi(n)}{n}+\frac{(n-\phi(n)-1)q}{n}\leq m(\mathbb{Z}_{n}).

Theorem
1.1
and our assumption gives that,

m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\leq m(G)<\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\leq\frac{3+t}{2+t}m(G).

So we obtain that,

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(n)}{n}+\frac{(n-\phi(n))q}{n}<\frac{3+t}{2+t}\cdot\frac{n-1}{q}+\frac{3+t}{2+t}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\cdot(1-q)+q<\frac{3+t}{2+t}\cdot\frac{(n-1+q)}{q}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\cdot(1-q)<\frac{((3+t)n-(3+t)+(3+t)q-(2+t)q^{2})}{(2+t)q}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\cdot\frac{(2+t)q}{((3+t)n-(3+t)+(3+t)q-(2+t)q^{2})}<\frac{1}{1-q}<0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(3+t)n-(3+t)+(3+t)q-(2+t)q^{2}<0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(3+t)n<(3+t)-(3+t)q+(2+t)q^{2}<(3+t)+(3+t)q^{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow n<1+q^{2}.

So it is a contradiction except for
n=q^{2}.
Hence, except for the case
n=q^{2}
we obtain
(|N|,|G/N|)=1,
and
G
is a cyclic group of order
n.
To complete the proof, we need to check the case where
n=q^{2}.
Note that

m(\mathbb{Z}_{q^{2}})=1+\frac{2(q-1)}{q},
and
m(\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})=(1+\frac{q^{2}-1}{q}).
As,
q\geq 2,
it is obvious that
m(\mathbb{Z}_{q^{2}})\leq\frac{2+t}{3+t}m(\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}).
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

We continue with the proof of Theorem
1.4
:

proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose,
\frac{m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})}{m(G)}\leq\frac{1}{p-1}
. Now, Theorem
1.2
exhibits the best upper bound for
\frac{m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})}{m(G)}
is
\frac{2+t}{3+t}.
Hence,
\frac{1}{p-1}\geq\frac{2+t}{3+t}
, which implies that
p\leq 2+\frac{1}{2+t}.
This is a contradiction expect the case
n=p^{2}.

For
n=p^{2}
, clearly one have

\frac{1}{(p-1)}\cdot m(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{p-1}+\frac{(p+1)}{p}\leq m(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}),

as
p\geq 3.
This completes the proof of the result.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
G
be a non-cyclic finite abelian group of order
n,
where
n=2^{t}\cdot l
with
\gcd(2,l)=1,
and
p
is the largest prime dividing
n.
Let
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be the cyclic group of order
n.
Then

\frac{1}{(p-1)}<\frac{m(\mathbb{Z}_{n})}{m(G)}\leq\frac{2+t}{3+t}.

Proof.

This corollary follows from Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
.
∎

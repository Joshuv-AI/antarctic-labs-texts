Sharp Convergence Rates for Darcy’s Law
Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
This paper is concerned with Darcy’s law for an incompressible viscous fluid flowing in a porous medium. We establish the sharp O ⁡ ( ε ) O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}) convergence rate in a periodically perforated and bounded domain in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} for d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , where ε \varepsilon represents the size of solid obstacles. This is achieved by constructing two boundary layer correctors to control the boundary layers created by the incompressibility condition and the discrepancy of boundary values between the solution and the leading term in its asymptotic expansion. One of the correctors deals with the tangential boundary data, while the other handles the normal boundary data. Keywords : Darcy’s Law; Convergence Rate; Stokes Equations. MR (2020) Subject Classification : 35Q35; 35B27; 76D07.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with Darcy’s law for an incompressible viscous fluid in a porous medium.
More precisely, we consider the Dirichlet problem for the steady Stokes equations,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\mu\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}&=f&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\mu>0
is the viscosity constant,

0<\varepsilon<1
, and
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is a periodically perforated and bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
In (
1.1
) we have normalized the velocity vector by a factor
\varepsilon^{2}
,
where
\varepsilon
is the period.
To describe the porous domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,
we let
Y=[0,1]^{d}
be a closed unit cube and
Y_{s}
(solid part) an open subset of
Y
with Lipschitz boundary.
Throughout the paper we shall assume that dist
(\partial Y,\partial Y_{s})>0
and that
Y_{f}=Y\setminus\overline{Y_{s}}
(the fluid part)
is connected.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary.
For
0<\varepsilon<1
, define

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{k}\varepsilon\left(\overline{Y_{s}}+z_{k}\right),

(1.2)

where
z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and the union is taken over those
k
’s for which
\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})\subset\Omega
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be the weak solution of (
1.1
)
with
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0
.
We extend
u_{\varepsilon}
to the whole domain
\Omega
by zero and still denote the extension by
u_{\varepsilon}
.
Let
P_{\varepsilon}
be the extension of
p_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
, defined by (
2.6
).
It has been known since late 1970’s that as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
u_{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

P_{\varepsilon}\to p_{0}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, where
(u_{0},p_{0})
is given by a Darcy law,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{0}=\mu^{-1}K(f-\nabla p_{0})&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&\text{\rm div}(u_{0})=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u_{0}\cdot n=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

with
\int_{\Omega}p_{0}\,dx=0
.
In (
1.3
) the permeability matrix
K=(K_{j}^{i})
is a
d\times d
positive definite and symmetric matrix defined by (
2.3
), and

n
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Furthermore, it was observed in
[
3
]
by G. Allaire that as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)(f-\nabla p_{0})\to 0\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.4)

where
W(y)=(W_{j}^{i}(y))
is an 1-periodic
d\times d
matrix defined by the cell problem (
2.2
)
and
\fint_{Y}W(y)\,dy=K
.
For an excellent exposition on Darcy’s law and closely related topics, we refer the reader to
[
4
]

by G. Allaire and A. Mikelić.

The purpose of this paper is to study the convergence rates for

u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)(f-\nabla p_{0})
and
P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The following is the main result of the paper.
The
O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
rate in (
1.5
) is sharp.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2,\alpha}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
for some
\alpha>0
.
Also assume that
Y_{s}
is an open subset of
Y=[0,1]^{d}
with
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of (
1.1
),
where
f\in C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0
.
Then

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(1.5)

\displaystyle+\|\varepsilon\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}\nabla W(x/\varepsilon)(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\Omega
, and
Y_{s}
.

The first rigorous proof of Darcy’s law by homogenization was given by L. Tartar in an appendix of
[
23
]
, using an energy method.
We refer the reader to
[
4
]
for references on earlier work on the formal derivation of Darcy’s law, using two-scale asymptotic expansions.
In
[
2
,
3
]
, the strong convergence of
(u_{\varepsilon},P_{\varepsilon})
in
L^{2}(\Omega)

was established by the method of two-scale convergence.
Also see related work in
[
15
,
20
,
8
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
16
]
.

Regarding the rate of convergence for
(u_{\varepsilon},P_{\varepsilon})
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
to the best of the author’s knowledge, the only previous result for a bounded domain with
the Dirichlet condition was obtained by E. Marušić-Paloka and A. Mikelić
in
[
17
]
, where a rate
O(\varepsilon^{1/6})
was established for the case
d=2
.
See
[
8
]
for an earlier result for a unbounded domain
\Omega=(0,L)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
We remark that for Laplace’s equation and systems of linear elasticity, quantitative error estimates have been established
in
[
14
,
11
,
22
,
10
,
9
]
.
As pointed out in
[
17
]
, the simple cut-off argument, which seems to work well for standard elliptic
equations and systems, does not yield any convergence rate for the Stokes equations because of the incompressibility condition.
In
[
17
]
, using a stream function from
[
24
]
,
a boundary layer corrector was constructed in the case
d=2
to control the boundary layer
near
\partial\Omega
created by the incompressibility condition.
We mention that
[
17
]
also treated the case of nonlinear stationary Navier-Stokes equations.

We now describe our approach to the problem of convergence rates and error estimates, which is based on energy estimates.
Let

u(x,x/\varepsilon)=\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)\big(f(x)-\nabla p_{0}(x)\big).

(1.6)

To address the discrepancy of boundary values between
u_{\varepsilon}
and
u(x,x/\varepsilon)
as well as the incompressibility condition, we introduce two
boundary layer correctors
(\Psi_{t},q_{t})
and
(\Psi_{n},q_{n})
.
Let
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
.
The tangential boundary layer corrector
(\Psi_{t},q_{t})
is a weak solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\mu\Delta\Psi_{t}+\nabla q_{t}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\text{\rm div}(\Psi_{t})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

with boundary data
\Psi_{t}=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, and

\Psi_{t}=-u(x,x/\varepsilon)+[u(x,x/\varepsilon)\cdot n]n\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

(1.8)

Note that
\Psi_{t}\cdot n=0
on
\partial\Omega
. By the divergence theorem and the Cauchy inequality, this gives,

\|\nabla\Psi_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\|\nabla\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(1.9)

We use a localized Rellich estimate in a Lipschitz domain to show that

\|\nabla\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|\nabla_{\tan}\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right\},

(1.10)

where
\nabla_{\tan}\Psi_{t}
denotes the tangential gradient of
\Psi_{t}
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
The desired
O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
bound for
\varepsilon\|\nabla\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
follows from (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).
See Section
4
for details.

The normal boundary layer corrector
(\Psi_{n},q_{n})
is defined as the solution of the Stokes equations (
1.7
) in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,
with the boundary conditions
\Psi_{n}=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, and

\Psi_{n}=-\big[u(x,x/\varepsilon)\cdot n-\gamma\big]n\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.11)

where

\gamma=\fint_{\partial\Omega}u(x,x/\varepsilon)\cdot n\,d\sigma.

Thanks to (
1.3
), we may write

u(x,x/\varepsilon)\cdot n=\mu^{-1}n_{i}\big[W_{j}^{i}(x/\varepsilon)-K_{j}^{i}\big]\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega

(1.12)

(the repeated indices are summed from
1
to
d
).
Furthermore, there exists a 1-periodic tensor
(\phi_{\ell j}^{i})
such that

\phi_{\ell j}^{i}=-\phi_{ij}^{\ell}\quad\text{ and }\quad W_{j}^{i}(y)-K_{j}^{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{\ell}}\phi_{\ell j}^{i}(y).

(1.13)

It follows from (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) that

u(x,x/\varepsilon)\cdot n=\varepsilon(2\mu)^{-1}\Big(n_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}-n_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big)\Big(\phi_{\ell j}^{i}(x/\varepsilon)\Big)\cdot\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

(1.14)

Since
n_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}-n_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
is a tangential derivative,
the formula (
1.14
) allows us to use an integration by parts on
\partial\Omega
(see (
5.21
)), which generates the needed decay factor
{\varepsilon}
.
In order to carry out this argument, we use an energy estimate to reduce the problem to the
L^{2}
estimate for the Stokes equations
in
\Omega
, whose solutions are then represented by integrals on
\partial\Omega
, using the Poisson kernels.
See Section
5
for details.

We point out that the
C^{1,\alpha}
condition on
Y_{s}
in Theorem
1.1
is used to ensure the boundedness of
\nabla W
,
while the
C^{2,\alpha}
condition on
\Omega
is used for the
C^{2}
estimates for the Stokes equations in
\Omega
.
The
C^{1,1/2}
condition on
f
seems to be more or less optimal for the methods used.
An
O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
estimate with less regularity on
f
would be an interesting and challenging problem.

Let
Y=[0,1]^{d}
and
Y_{s}
(solid part) be an open subset of
Y
with Lipschitz boundary.
We assume that dist
(\partial Y,\partial Y_{s})>0
and that (the fluid part)

Y_{f}=Y\setminus\overline{Y_{s}}

Let

\omega=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big(Y_{f}+z\big)

(2.1)

be the periodic repetition of
Y_{f}
. It is easy to see that the unbounded domain
\omega
is connected, 1-periodic, and that

\partial\omega

For
1\leq j\leq d

(W_{j}(y),\pi_{j}(y))=(W_{j}^{1}(y),\dots,W_{j}^{d}(y),\pi_{j}(y))\in H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\omega)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta W_{j}+\nabla\pi_{j}&=e_{j}&\quad&\text{ in }\omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(W_{j})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\omega,\\
W_{j}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\omega,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

with
\int_{Y_{f}}\pi_{j}\,dy=0
, where
e_{j}=(0,\dots,1,\dots,0)
with
1
in the
j^{th}
place.
We extend
W_{j}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by zero and define

K_{j}^{i}=\int_{Y}W^{i}_{j}(y)\,dy.

(2.3)

Using

K_{j}^{i}=\int_{Y}\nabla W^{\ell}_{j}\cdot\nabla W^{\ell}_{i}\,dy,

it is not hard to show that the
d\times d
constant matrix
(K_{j}^{i})
is symmetric and positive definite.

Thanks to the assumption dist
(\partial Y,\partial Y_{s})>0
, we have

\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
and
dist
(\partial\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon})\geq c\varepsilon
, where

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\partial(\varepsilon\omega).

(2.4)

For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
of the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}&=f&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

with
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0
.
We extend
u_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
by zero and still denote the extension by
u_{\varepsilon}
.
Let
P_{\varepsilon}
be the extension of
p_{\varepsilon}
, defined by

P_{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &p_{\varepsilon}(x)&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&\fint_{\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z_{k})}p_{\varepsilon}&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z_{k})\text{ and }\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})\subset\Omega\text{ for some }z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

(see
[
15

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Let
p_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the weak solution of the Neumann problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}K_{j}^{i}\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
n_{i}K_{j}^{i}\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.7)

with
\int_{\Omega}p_{0}\,dx=0
,
where
n=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Then, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} {u}_{\varepsilon}-W_{j}(x/\varepsilon)\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)&\to 0&\quad&\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}&\to 0&\quad&\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.8)

As indicated in Introduction, a proof of Theorem
2.1
, using the method of two-scale convergence,
may be found in
[

,
3
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Assume that
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
with
u=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. Then

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

(2.9)

Proof.

The case
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

is more or less well known.
See e.g.
[
5
]
.
The proof for the case
u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
with
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is the same.
We sketch a proof here for the reader’s convenience.
Suppose
\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})\subset\Omega
for some
z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Since
u=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, it follows by Poincaré’s inequality that

\int_{\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z_{k})}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z_{k})}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

(2.10)

Similarly,

\int_{B(x_{0},C\varepsilon)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{B(x_{0},C\varepsilon)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

(2.11)

if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
\varepsilon(Y+z)\subset B(x_{0},C\varepsilon)\cap\Omega
for some
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
The estimate (
2.9
) follows from (
2.10
) -(
2.11
) by a covering argument.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
There exists a bounded linear operator

R_{\varepsilon}:H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2.12)

such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &R_{\varepsilon}(u)=0\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ and }\quad R_{\varepsilon}(u)=u\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
&R_{\varepsilon}(u)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{ if }u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
&R_{\varepsilon}(u)=u\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\ \text{ if }\ u=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\
&\text{\rm div}(R_{\varepsilon}(u))=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ if }\ \text{\rm div}(u)=0\text{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.13)

and

\varepsilon\,\|\nabla R_{\varepsilon}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|R_{\varepsilon}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\Big\{\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big\},

(2.14)

where
C
depends only on
\Omega
and
Y_{s}
.
Moreover,

\|\text{\rm div}(R_{\varepsilon}(u))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\,\|\text{\rm div}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(2.15)

Proof.

The proof is the similar to that of a lemma due to Tartar (in an appendix of
[
23
]
, also see Lemma 1.7 in
[
4
]
).
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For each
\varepsilon(Y+z)\subset\Omega
, where
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define
R_{\varepsilon}(u)
on
\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z)
by the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\Delta R_{\varepsilon}(u)+\nabla q&=-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u&\quad&\text{ in }\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z),\\
\text{\rm div}(R_{\varepsilon}(u))&=\text{\rm div}(u)+\frac{1}{|\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z)|}\int_{\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z)}\text{\rm div}(u)\,dx&\quad&\text{ in }\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z),\\
R_{\varepsilon}(u)&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial(\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z)),\\
R_{\varepsilon}(u)&=u&\quad&\text{ on }\partial(\varepsilon(Y+z)).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.16)

If
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
x\notin\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z)
for any
\varepsilon(Y+z)\subset\Omega
,
we let
R_{\varepsilon}(u)(x)=u(x)
.
It is not hard to show that
R_{\varepsilon}(u)\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies the conditions in (
2.13
)-(
2.15
).
∎

We end this section with some observations on the rescaled solutions

(W_{j}(x/\varepsilon),\varepsilon\pi_{j}(x/\varepsilon))
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. It follows from (
2.2
) by rescaling that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\big\{W_{j}(x/\varepsilon)\big\}+\nabla\big\{\varepsilon\pi_{j}(x/\varepsilon)\big\}&=e_{j}&\quad&\text{ in }\varepsilon\omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(W_{j}(x/\varepsilon))&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\varepsilon\omega,\\
W_{j}(x/\varepsilon)&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial(\varepsilon\omega).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.18)

We extend both
W_{j}
and
\pi_{j}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by zero.
Clearly,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \text{\rm div}(W_{j}(x/\varepsilon))&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
W_{j}(x/\varepsilon)&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.19)

Note that in the construction of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, the holes near
\partial\Omega
are not removed.
As a result, the first equation in (
2.18
) needs to be modified for
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. In fact,
a computation using integration by parts shows that

-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\big\{W_{j}(x/\varepsilon)\big\}+\nabla\big\{\varepsilon\pi_{j}(x/\varepsilon)\big\}=e_{j}+\sigma_{\varepsilon,j}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},

(2.20)

where
\sigma_{\varepsilon,j}\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\varepsilon,j},\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(2.21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left\{-\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z_{k})}e_{j}\cdot\psi\,dx-\varepsilon\int_{\overline{\Omega}\cap\partial(\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z_{k}))}(\nabla W_{j}(x/\varepsilon)n-\pi_{j}(x/\varepsilon)n)\cdot\psi d\sigma\right\}.

The sum in (
2.21
) is taken over those
k
’s for which
z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

and
\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
,
and
n
denotes the outward unit normal.
Under the assumption that
\partial Y_{s}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
,
it is known that
|\nabla W_{j}|
and
\pi_{j}
are bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It follows that if
g\in H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle|\langle\sigma_{\varepsilon,j},g\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}|\leq C\sum_{k}\left\{\int_{\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})}|g\psi|\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{\partial(\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z_{k}))}|g\psi|\,d\sigma\right\}

(\psi
is extended to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by zero).
Using the inequality

\int_{\partial(\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z_{k}))}|u|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+C\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})}|u|^{2}\,dx,

(
2.9
) and the Cauchy inequality,
one may prove that

|\langle\sigma_{\varepsilon,j},g\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}|\leq C\varepsilon\big\{\varepsilon\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})}\big\}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(2.22)

for any
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\Sigma_{c\varepsilon}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<c\varepsilon\}
.

3
Energy estimates

In this section we establish the energy estimates for the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}&=f+\varepsilon\,\text{\rm div}(F)&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon})&=g&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=h&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where
(g,h)
satisfies the compatibility condition,

\int_{\Omega}g\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}h\cdot n\,d\sigma.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
with Lipschitz boundary.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of
(
3.1
) with
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0
.
Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
,
where
C
depends only on
\Omega
and
Y_{s}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
By Lemma
2.4
, there exists
v_{\varepsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\text{div}(v_{\varepsilon})=p_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and

\varepsilon\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

(3.4)

By using
v_{\varepsilon}
as a test function we see that

\displaystyle\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\{\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right\},

where we have used (
3.4
) for the last inequality.
This gives

\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\left\{\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right\}.

(3.5)

Step 2. We consider the case
h=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
This allows us to use the test function
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
to obtain

\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have also used the Cauchy inequality.
It follows from the inequality
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
as well as the Cauchy inequality that

\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq C\left\{\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\right\}.

(3.6)

This, together with (
3.5
), yields (
3.3
) by the Cauchy inequality.

Step 3. In the general case, we let
(H,q)\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of

-\Delta H+\nabla q=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{\rm div}(H)=\gamma\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

with boundary data
H=h
on
\partial\Omega
, where

\gamma=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\partial\Omega}h\cdot n\,d\sigma.

Let
w_{\varepsilon}=R_{\varepsilon}(H)
, where
R_{\varepsilon}
is the operator given by Lemma
2.3
.
Note that
w_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
,
w_{\varepsilon}=h
on
\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\varepsilon\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right\},

(3.7)

and

\|\text{\rm div}(w_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C|\gamma|.

(3.8)

Thus,
u_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta(u_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon})+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}=f+\varepsilon\,\text{\rm div}(F)+\varepsilon^{2}\Delta w_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Hence, by Step 2, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+|\gamma|\big\},

where we have used (
3.8
).
It follows from (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+|\gamma|+\varepsilon\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}.

Step 4. To estimate
\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\|H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
we let

\widetilde{H}=H-\gamma d^{-1}(x-x_{0}),

where
x_{0}\in\Omega
is fixed.
Note that

-\Delta\widetilde{H}+\nabla q=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{\rm div}(\widetilde{H})=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

By the energy estimates,

\|\nabla\widetilde{H}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\widetilde{H}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)},

and by the nontangential-maximal-function estimates for the Stokes equations in
[
6
]
,

\|\widetilde{H}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\widetilde{H}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

It follows that

\varepsilon\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big\{\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big\}.

This, together with (
3.9
), completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If we replace the right-hand side
f+\varepsilon\,\text{\rm div}(F)
of the first equation in (
3.1
) by some

\sigma_{\varepsilon}\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies the condition

|\langle\sigma_{\varepsilon},\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}|\leq\varepsilon N\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

for any
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and some
N=N(\sigma_{\varepsilon})>0
, then the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
gives

\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\Big\{N+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

(3.10)

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
, where
C
depends only on
\Omega
and
Y_{s}
.
Note that if
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=f+\varepsilon\,\text{\rm div}(F)
, then
N=C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right\}
.

4
Correctors for tangential boundary data

Consider the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=h&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

with boundary data
h
satisfying the condition

h\cdot n=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

(4.2)

The goal of this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution in
H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
of (
4.1
) with
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0
,
where
h\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies (
4.2
).
Then

\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left\{\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

(4.3)

where
\nabla_{\tan}h
denotes the tangential gradient of
h
on
\partial\Omega
.

Let

\displaystyle D_{r}

\displaystyle=\big\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ |x^{\prime}|<r\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \psi(x^{\prime})<x_{d}<100d(M+1)r\big\},

(4.4)

\displaystyle I_{r}

\displaystyle=\big\{(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ |x^{\prime}|<r\big\},

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function such that

\psi(0)=0
and
\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\leq M
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(v,q)
be a weak solution in
H^{1}(D_{r};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(D_{r})
of the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta v+\nabla q&=0&\quad&\text{ in }D_{r},\\
\text{\rm div}(v)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }D_{r},\\
v&=g&\quad&\text{ on }\partial D_{r},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

where
0<r<\infty
and
g\in H^{1}(\partial D_{r};\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies the condition
\int_{\partial D_{r}}g\cdot n\,d\sigma=0
.
Then

\int_{\partial D_{r}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{\partial D_{r}}|\nabla_{\tan}v|^{2}\,d\sigma,

(4.6)

where
C
depends only on
d
and
M
.

Proof.

By dilation we may assume
r=1
, in which case
the Rellich estimate (
4.6
) was proved in
[
6
, Theorem 4.15]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(v,q)
be a weak solution of (
4.5
) with
r=2
.
Then

\int_{I_{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{I_{2}}|\nabla_{\tan}g|^{2}\,d\sigma+C\int_{D_{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx,

(4.7)

where
C
depends only on
d
and
M
.

Proof.

It follows from (
4.6
) that for
1<r<2
,

\int_{I_{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{\partial D_{r}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{\partial D_{r}}|\nabla_{\tan}v|^{2}\,d\sigma.

Hence,

\int_{I_{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{I_{2}}|\nabla_{\tan}g|^{2}\,d\sigma+C\int_{D_{2}\cap\partial D_{r}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\sigma.

By integrating the inequality above in
r
over the interval
(1,2)
, we obtain (
4.7
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We start with the observation,

\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial n}\cdot u_{\varepsilon}\,d\sigma,

where we have used (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
It follows by the Cauchy inequality that

\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(4.8)

We will show that

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla_{\tan}h|^{2}\,d\sigma+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma_{c\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx,

(4.9)

where
\Sigma_{c\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:\ \text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<c\varepsilon\}\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.
Assume (
4.9
) for a moment.
Then

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla_{\tan}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

This, together with (
4.8
) and the Cauchy inequality, gives

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|\nabla_{\tan}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{3/2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|\nabla_{\tan}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+C\varepsilon\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}+(1/2)\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{e})},

which yields the estimate for
\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
in (
4.3
).
The estimate for
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
follows by (
2.9
), while
the bound for
\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
follows from (
3.5
).

It remains to prove (
4.9
).
To this end, we shall prove in a few lines below that

\int_{B(x_{0},c_{0}\varepsilon)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{B(x_{0},c_{1}\varepsilon)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla_{\tan}h|^{2}\,d\sigma+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\int_{B(x_{0},c_{1}\varepsilon)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx

(4.10)

for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, where
0<c_{0}<c_{1}
are sufficiently small.
The desired estimate (
4.9
) follows from (
4.10
) by covering
\partial\Omega
with a finite number of balls

\{B(x_{k},c_{0}\varepsilon)\}
centered on
\partial\Omega
.

Finally, we note that if
v(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon x)
and
q(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}p_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon x)
,
then
-\Delta v+\nabla q=0
and div
(v)=0
.
As a result, the estimate (
4.10
) follows from (
4.7
) by a translation and rotation of
the coordinate system. We point out that since the constant
C
in (
4.7
) depends only on
d
and
M
,
the constant
C
in (
4.10
) depends only on
d
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.
In particular,
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
4.1
, we are able to construct a tangential boundary layer corrector.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded domain with
C^{2,\alpha}
boundary for some
\alpha>0
.
Also assume that
\partial Y_{s}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
.
Let
(\Psi_{t},q_{t})
be a weak solution in
H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
of the Dirichlet problem
(
4.1
) with
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}q_{t}\,dx=0
, where the boundary data
h
is given by

h=b-W_{j}(x/\varepsilon)\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)+\Big[-b\cdot n+n_{i}W^{i}_{j}(x/\varepsilon)\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)\Big]n,

(4.11)

f\in C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

b\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
\int_{\partial\Omega}b\cdot n\,d\sigma=0
, and
p_{0}
is the solution of the Neumann problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} K_{j}^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
n_{i}K_{j}^{i}\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)&=b\cdot n&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.12)

with
\int_{\Omega}p_{0}\,dx=0
.
Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|q_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Big\{\|f-\nabla p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
.

Proof.

Note that
h\cdot n=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Also,
under the assumption that
\partial Y_{s}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
, we have
W_{j}=W_{j}(y)\in C^{1}(\overline{\omega})
.
It follows that

\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\big\{\|f-\nabla p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big\},

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\tan}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\varepsilon^{-1}\|f-\nabla p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\tan}(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\tan}b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}.

As a result, the estimate (
4.13
) follows readily from (
4.3
).
∎

5
Correctors for normal boundary data

In this section we consider the Dirichlet problem (
4.1
), where the boundary data
h
is given by

h=\big\{n_{i}[W_{j}^{i}(x/\varepsilon)-K_{j}^{i}]g_{j}-\gamma\big\}n,

(5.1)

where
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{d})\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen so that

\int_{\partial\Omega}h\cdot n\,d\sigma=0
, i.e.,

\gamma=\frac{1}{|\partial\Omega|}\int_{\partial\Omega}n_{i}[W_{j}^{i}(x/\varepsilon)-K_{j}^{i}]g_{j}\,d\sigma.

(5.2)

The goal of this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2,\alpha}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
\alpha>0
.
Also assume that
\partial Y_{s}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution in
H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
of
(
4.1
) with
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0
, where
h
is given by (
5.1
).
Then

\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\left\{\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

(5.3)

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
.

We will prove a series of lemmas before we give the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
We begin with an estimate for
\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
h
be given by (
5.1
). Then

\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\big\{\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big\}

(5.4)

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{1/2}\|h\|^{1/2}_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla_{\tan}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

where we have used the Cauchy inequality. It is easy to see that

\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

and

\|\nabla_{\tan}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\varepsilon^{-1}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

where we have used the fact
W_{j}=W_{j}(y)\in C^{1}(\overline{\omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\left\{\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

There exist 1-periodic functions
\phi_{ij}^{\ell}\in H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
1\leq i,j,\ell\leq d
, such that

\frac{\partial\phi_{ij}^{\ell}}{\partial y_{i}}=W_{j}^{\ell}(y)-K_{j}^{\ell}\quad\text{ and }\quad\phi_{ij}^{\ell}=-\phi_{\ell j}^{i},

(5.5)

where the index
i
is summed from
1
to
d
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as Lemma 3.1 in
[
13
]
.
Since

\int_{Y}\left(W_{j}^{\ell}(y)-K_{j}^{\ell}\right)dy=0,

one may solve the periodic boundary value problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta f_{j}^{\ell}=W_{j}^{\ell}-K_{j}^{\ell}\quad\text{ in }Y,\\
&f_{j}^{\ell}\text{ is 1-periodic.}\end{aligned}\right.

Let

\phi_{ij}^{\ell}=\frac{\partial f_{j}^{\ell}}{\partial y_{i}}-\frac{\partial f_{j}^{i}}{\partial y_{\ell}}.

Then
\phi_{ij}^{\ell}=-\phi_{\ell j}^{i}
. Using

we obtain the first equation in (

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Using (

), for
1\leq j\leq d
, we may write

\displaystyle n_{\ell}[W_{j}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)-K_{j}^{\ell}]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon n_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left\{\phi_{ij}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\right\}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big\{n_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-n_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}\Big\}\left\{\phi_{ij}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\right\},

where the skew-symmetric property is used for the last step.
It follows from an integration by parts on

that

\int_{\partial\Omega}n_{\ell}[W_{j}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)-K_{j}^{\ell}]\psi\,d\sigma(x)=-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi_{ij}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\left\{n_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-n_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}\right\}\psi\,d\sigma(x).

(5.7)

This, in particular, implies that

\displaystyle|\gamma|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(5.8)

where
\gamma
is given by (
5.2
), assuming that
(\phi_{ij}^{\ell})
are bounded.

Let

\Sigma_{\rho}=\big\{x\in\Omega:\ \text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\rho\big\}.

(5.9)

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
T
be the operator defined by

T(f)(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{d}}\,d\sigma(y).

Then

\int_{\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|T(f)|^{2}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|^{2}\,d\sigma

(5.10)

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
, and

\int_{\Omega}[\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)]^{\delta}|T(f)|^{2}\,dx\leq C_{\delta}\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|^{2}\,d\sigma

(5.11)

for any
\delta>1

Proof.

It is not hard to see that

\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{d\sigma(y)}{|x-y|^{d}}\leq\frac{C}{\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)}

for any

.
By the Cauchy inequality,

|T(f)(x)|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|f(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}\,d\sigma(y).

The estimate (

A similar argument gives

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(H,q)
be a weak solution in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
of the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta H+\nabla q&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(H)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
H&=h&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.12)

where
h
is given by (
5.1
).
Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\,\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\Big\{\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\},

(5.13)

for any
0<\varepsilon<1
.

Proof.

We first point out that by the standard energy estimates for the Stokes equations,

\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}.

In view of (
5.4
), this gives

\varepsilon\,\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\left\{\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\,\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

Next, we use the nontangential-maximal-function estimate,

\|(H)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(5.14)

to bound
H
on
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
. The estimate (
5.14
) was proved in
[
6
]
for a Lipschitz domain

\Omega
, where the nontangential maximal function

(H)^{*}
is defined by

(H)^{*}(x)=\sup\big\{|H(y)|:\ y\in\Omega\text{ and }|y-x|<C_{0}\,\text{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\big\}

(5.15)

for
x\in\partial\Omega
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\|H\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\|(H)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

(5.16)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

It remains to bound
H
on
\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
To this end, we let
(G(x,y),\Pi(x,y))
denote the matrix of Green functions for the Stokes equation
(
5.12
) in
\Omega
.
That is, for each fixed

x\in\Omega
,
G(x,y)=(G^{ij}(x,y))\in H^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and

\Pi(x,y)=(\Pi^{i}(x,y))\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{y}G^{ij}(x,y)+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\Pi^{i}(x,y)&=\delta_{x}\delta_{ij}&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega\setminus\{x\},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}(G^{ij}(x,y))&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega\setminus\{x\},\\
G^{ij}(x,y)&=0&\quad&\text{ for }y\in\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

in the sense of distribution.
We also require that

\Pi(x,\cdot)\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\Omega}\Pi(x,y)\,dy=0.

Under the assumption that
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2,\alpha}
domain for some
\alpha>0
,
solutions of the Stokes equations (
5.12
)
satisfy the
C^{1,1}
estimate for
H
and
C^{0,1}
estimate for
q
, up to
the boundary.
It follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)|+|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|^{1-d},

(5.17)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)|x-y|^{-d},

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}_{x}G(x,y)|+|\nabla_{y}^{2}G(x,y)|+|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|^{-d},

and that

\displaystyle|\Pi(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|^{1-d},

(5.18)

\displaystyle|\Pi(x,y)-\Pi(x,z)|

\displaystyle\leq\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\{|x-y|^{-d}+|x-z|^{-d}\},

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}\Pi(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|^{-d},

for any
x,y\in\Omega
and
x\neq y
,
x\neq z
.
See e.g.
[
7

21
]
.
This allows us to represent the solution
H(x)
by

H^{i}(x)=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big\{n_{k}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}G^{ij}(x,y)-\big[\Pi^{i}(x,y)-\Pi^{i}(x,z)\big]n_{j}(y)\Big\}h^{j}(y)\,d\sigma(y)

(5.19)

for any

,
where

and
z\neq x
(due to the compatibility condition for

is arbitrary).
Using (
5.6

, where

\displaystyle h^{(1),k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big(n_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-n_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}\Big)\Big\{\phi_{ij}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)g_{j}n_{k}\Big\},

(5.20)

\displaystyle h^{(2),k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\phi_{ij}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\Big(n_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-n_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}\Big)\Big(g_{j}n_{k}\Big)-\gamma n_{k},

for

.
Let

be given by (

Observe that by the divergence theorem,

(5.21)

for

.
It follows that

\displaystyle|H^{(1)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big\{|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|+|\nabla_{y}^{2}G(x,y)|+|\nabla_{y}\Pi(x,y)|+|\Pi(x,y)-\Pi(x,z)|\Big\}|g(y)|\,d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|g(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}}\,d\sigma(y),

where we have used the estimates in (
5.17
) and (

In view of Lemma

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(5.22)

Finally, note that

\displaystyle|H^{(2)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big\{|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|+|\Pi(x,y)-\Pi(x,z)|\Big\}\left(|g(y)|+|\nabla_{\tan}g(y)|\right)d\sigma(y),

Using estimates in (

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{2d\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of (
6.1
) with
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0
.
Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla\big(u_{\varepsilon}-W(x/\varepsilon)(f-\nabla p_{0})\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\Big\{\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{3d\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|f-\nabla p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla[(f-\nabla p_{0})-S_{\varepsilon}(f-\nabla p_{0})\big]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{2d\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{2d\varepsilon})}+\|f-\nabla p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{\tan}(f-\nabla p_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

for
0<\varepsilon<1
.

Proof.

The estimate (
6.11
) follows readily from (
6.9
), (
6.10
),
(
4.13
), and (
5.24
).
∎

To bound the right-hand side of (
6.11
),
we let
p_{0}=p_{0}^{(1)}+p_{0}^{(2)}
, where
p_{0}^{(1)}
and
p_{0}^{(2)}

are solutions of the Neumann problems,

\left\{\begin{aligned} K_{j}^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}^{(1)}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
n_{i}K_{j}^{i}\Big(f_{j}-\frac{\partial p_{0}^{(1)}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.12)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} K_{j}^{i}\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}^{(2)}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
n_{i}K_{j}^{i}\frac{\partial p_{0}^{(2)}}{\partial x_{j}}&=-b\cdot n&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.13)

respectively,
with
\int_{\Omega}p_{0}^{(1)}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}p_{0}^{(2)}\,dx=0
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
p_{0}^{(1)}
be a solution of (
6.12
) for some
f\in C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{0}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega})},

(6.14)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla p^{(1)}_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega})},

and

\|\nabla f-S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla f)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla^{2}p^{(1)}_{0}-S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla^{2}p^{(1)}_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega})}.

(6.15)

Proof.

Since
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2,\alpha}
domain, the first inequality in (
6.14
) follows
from the classical
C^{2}
estimates, up to the boundary, for
second-order elliptic equations with constant coefficients.
Next, note that for
x\in\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla f)(x)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y_{i}}(y/\varepsilon)\nabla f(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y_{i}}(y/\varepsilon)\big[\nabla f(x-y)-\nabla f(x)]\,dy.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1-d}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon/4)}|\nabla\varphi(y/\varepsilon)||y|^{1/2}\,dy\,\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega})}.

By the interior
C^{2,1/2}
estimates,

|\nabla^{2}p^{(1)}_{0}(x-y)-\nabla^{2}p^{(1)}_{0}(y)|\leq C|y|^{1/2}\Big\{\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|p^{(1)}_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}\Big\}

for any
x\in\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
and
|y|\leq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon
.
As in the case of
\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(f)
, this implies that

|\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla p_{0}^{(1)})(x)|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\overline{\Omega})}

for any
x\in\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
Finally, to see (
6.15
), we write

S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla f)(x)-\nabla f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)[\nabla f(x-y)-\nabla f(x)]\,dy

and proceed as in the previous estimates.
∎

.

Let

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{0}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla p_{0}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\cdot n\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(6.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\cdot n\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

(6.17)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(2)}-S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{2\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\cdot n\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

(6.18)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla p_{0}^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{2\varepsilon})}

(6.19)

for

\displaystyle\|(\nabla p_{0}^{(2)})^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)|\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(2)}(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\|(\nabla^{2}p_{0}^{(2)})^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)|\nabla^{3}p_{0}^{(2)}(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\cdot n\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

(6.21)

where

denotes the nontangential maximal function of

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Using Lemmas
6.4
and
6.5
, it is not hard to see that the right-hand side of (
6.11
)
is bounded by

C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big\{\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}({\Omega})}+\|b\cdot n\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big\}.

As a result, we have proved that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla\big(u_{\varepsilon}-W(x/\varepsilon)(f-\nabla p_{0})\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(6.23)

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left\{\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\Omega)}+\|b\cdot n\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

In view of Lemma
2.2
,
it remains to show that

\|P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left\{\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\Omega)}+\|b\cdot n\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

(6.24)

where
P_{\varepsilon}
is an extension of
p_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
, defined by (
2.6
).
To this end, we define

p_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &p_{0}&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&\fint_{\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z_{k})}p_{0}&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\varepsilon(Y_{s}+z_{k})\text{ and }\varepsilon(Y+z_{k})\subset\Omega\text{ for some }z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

(6.25)

i.e., we extend
p_{0}|_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
to
\Omega
in the same manner as we do
p_{\varepsilon}
from
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
.
Then,

\displaystyle\|P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|p_{0}^{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|p_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p_{0}^{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Note that

\|P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|p_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Using Poincaré’s inequality on each cell
\varepsilon(Y_{f}+z_{k})

\displaystyle\|p_{0}^{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\left\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

As a result, we have proved that

\|P_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|p_{\varepsilon}-p_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\varepsilon\left\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

This completes the proof.

Remark 6.6
.

Let
u(x,x/\varepsilon)
be given by (
1.6
).
Due to the discrepancy of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
u(x,x/\varepsilon)
on
\partial\Omega
,
the
O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
rate in Theorem
1.1
is sharp. Indeed,
by applying the following trace inequality to the function

v=v_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-u(x,x/\varepsilon)
,

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})}^{1/2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})}^{1/2}\right\},

(6.26)

we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

where we have used the Cauchy inequality for the first inequality and (
2.9
) for the second.
It follows that the error estimate

\varepsilon\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=o(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,

cannot hold in general.
In fact, if
\Omega
is smooth and uniformly convex, then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\fint_{\partial\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\fint_{\partial\Omega}|W(x/\varepsilon)(f-\nabla p_{0})|^{2}\,d\sigma

(6.27)

\displaystyle=\fint_{\partial\Omega}|K(f-\nabla p_{0})|^{2}\,d\sigma.

See the proof of Lemma 3.2 in
[
1
]
.
Also, note that by Theorem
1.1
,

\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\Omega)}.

This, together with (
6.26
), yields

\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\big(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{{}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\big)^{1/2}\|f\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\right\}.

As a result, it is not possible to have

\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=o(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,

unless
f=\nabla p_{0}
in
\Omega
, in which case,
v_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

Finally, to see (
6.26
), choose a function
\beta\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\beta\cdot n\geq c_{0}>0
on
\partial\Omega
, supp
(\beta)\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq c\varepsilon\}
, and

|\nabla\beta|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}
.
It follows by the divergence theorem that

\displaystyle c_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega}|v|^{2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}|v|^{2}\,\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma\leq\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}\text{div}(\beta)\,dx+2\int_{\Omega}|v||\nabla v||\beta|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\Sigma_{c\varepsilon}}|v|^{2}\,dx+C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{c\varepsilon})},

where we have used the Cauchy inequality for the last step.

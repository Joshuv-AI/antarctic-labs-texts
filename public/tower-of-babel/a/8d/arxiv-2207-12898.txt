High emission rate from a Purcell-enhanced, triggered source of pure single photons in the telecom C-band
Cornelius Nawrath, Raphael Joos, Sascha Kolatschek, Stephanie Bauer, Pascal Pruy, Florian Hornung, Julius Fischer, Jiasheng Huang, Ponraj Vijayan, Robert Sittig, Michael Jetter, Simone Luca Portalupi, Peter Michler

Abstract
Several emission features mark semiconductor quantum dots as promising non-classical light sources for prospective quantum implementations. For long-distance transmission Liao et al. 2017 and Si-based on-chip processing Qiang et al. 2018 ; Bauer et al. 2021 , the possibility to match the telecom C-band Cao et al. 2019 stands out, while source brightness and high single-photon purity are key features in virtually any quantum implementation Takemoto et al. 2015 ; Bozzio et al. 2022 . Here we present an InAs/InGaAs/GaAs quantum dot emitting in the telecom C-band coupled to a circular Bragg grating. The Purcell enhancement of the emission enables a simultaneously high brightness with a fiber-coupled single-photon count rate of 13.9 MHz 13.9\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz} for an excitation repetition rate of 228 MHz 228\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz} (first-lens collection efficiency ∼ \sim 17 % 17\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} for NA = 0.6 \mathrm{NA}=0.6 ), while maintaining a low multi-photon contribution of g ( 2 ) ​ ( 0 ) = 0.0052 g^{(2)}(0)=0.0052 . Moreover, the compatibility with temperatures of up to 40 K 40\text{\,}\mathrm{K} attainable with compact cryo coolers, further underlines the suitability for out-of-the-lab implementations.

In summary, a bright source of single photons emitting in the telecom C-band based on an InAs/InGaAs/GaAs QD under p-shell excitation in a circular Bragg grating is presented. A simultaneously high fiber-coupled single-photon count rate of
13.88\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
for an excitation repetition rate of
228\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
(first-lens collection efficiency
\sim
17\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
for
\mathrm{NA}=0.6
) is demonstrated, while maintaining a low multi-photon contribution of
g^{(2)}_{\mathrm{analyt}}(0)=$5.2\pm 1\text{\times}{10}^{-3}\text{\,}$
due to the Purcell factor of 3. Furthermore, the compatibility with compact cryocoolers is demonstrated, opening routes for real-world implementations. Finally, the degree of indistinguishability is investigated, identifying possible strategies to improve the coherence of the emission.

Methods

The sample is based on the recently reported thin MMB design and corresponding QD growth optimizations
Sittig et al. 2022
. For the device fabrication
Kolatschek et al. 2021
, a
350\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
Al
2
O
3
spacer layer and a
100\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
Au layer are deposited on the sample. Using a flip-chip process, it is transfered to a Si carrier with Su-8 resist as glue. By means of citric acid and hydrofluoric acid, the GaAs substrate and the AlGaAs sacrificial layer are removed. Finally, the CBG rings are fabricated by electron beam lithography with AR-P 6200 resist to produce a SiO
2
hard mask, which is used to transfer the trenches into the InGaAs membrane via ICP-RIE etching.

A continuous-flow helium cryostat is used to cool the sample down to
4\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
. For the experiments at
40\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
, the built-in heating element is employed. A microscope objective (
\mathrm{NA}=0.6
) is used to focus the excitation laser onto the sample and collect the QD signal. The excitation and collection beam path are overlaid using a coated mirror (reflectivity
98.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
). Volume Bragg elements are used for filtering: two notch filters (
\mathrm{FWHM}=$1.2\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}$
) suppress the excitation laser signal in the detection path and a reflective bandpass filter (
\mathrm{FWHM}=$0.55\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}$
) selects the QD transition in question. Since the SNSPDs are polarization sensitive, the two polarization components of the QD signal are split and guided to two different detectors. To this end, a fiber-coupled polarizing beam splitter is employed.

For the optical excitation, ps pulses at repetition rates between
76\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
and
228\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
are employed. Apart from the decay time measurements, where non-resonant excitation at
875\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
is used, the charge carriers are excited to the p-shell of the QD
\sim
1530\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
. Further details on the measurement methods and the setup efficiency are presented in the supplementary material.

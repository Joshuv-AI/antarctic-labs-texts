Contractions, inwardness, tool theorems MSC: 54H25, 54E35, 47H09, 47H10
Lech Pasicki

Abstract
The paper is devoted to the fixed point theory in four aspects: of contractions, nonexpansive mappings, generalized inward mappings, and of the tool theorems. The manuscript was written about ten years ago. At first Nadler’s concept of contraction for multivalued mappings is replaced here by a more general, and yet elegant condition: for some α + ϵ < 1 \alpha+\epsilon<1 , and each x ∈ X x\in X there exists a y ∈ F ⁡ ( x ) y\in F(x) such that d ⁡ ( F ⁡ ( y ) , y ) ≤ α ​ d ​ ( y , x ) ≤ ( α + ϵ ) ​ d ​ ( F ⁡ ( x ) , x ) d(F(y),y)\leq\alpha d(y,x)\leq(\alpha+\epsilon)d(F(x),x) . For “nonexpansive” mappings we apply bead spaces that are more general than uniformly convex spaces, and our requirements on mappings are weaker than nonexpansivity in the sense of the Hausdorff distance. In the last, third section the Caristi theorem is replaced by more specialized “tools”, and we apply them to obtain stronger fixed point theorems on generalized inward mappings. In particular, if for each x ∈ X x\in X a nearest point of F ⁡ ( x ) F(x) belongs to the generalized inward set, then the values of F F need not to be closed.

Lim’s theorem (Theorem

Definition 2.1
(
[

)

A metric space

\begin{split}&\mbox{for every }r>0,\beta>0\mbox{ there exists a }\delta>0\mbox{ such that}\\
&\mbox{for each }x,y\in X\mbox{ with }d(x,y)\geq\beta\mbox{ there exists a }z\in X\\
&\mbox{such that }B(x,r+\delta)\cap B(y,r+\delta)\subset B(z,r-\delta).\end{split}

(8)

In particular, each convex set in a uniformly convex space is a bead space

[
12

with

[
15

A normed space is a bead space if and only if it is uniformly convex
[
15

On the other hand, there exist bead spaces which are not convex sets in uniformly convex spaces

[
14
, Example 3]

lower semicontinuous mapping such that

d(x,g(x))\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(g(x)),\quad x\in X,

then

has a fixed point.

Our first “tool” theorem is the following:

Theorem 3.2

Let
(X,\delta)
be a complete metric space, and
X\subset Y
. Assume that
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is
a lower semicontinuous mapping having a finite lower bound. If
F\colon X\to 2^{Y}
is a mapping satisfying

\begin{split}&\mbox{for each }\mbox{$x\in X\setminus F(x)$}\mbox{ there exists a }\mbox{$z\in X\setminus\{x\}$}\\
&\mbox{such that }\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}-\mbox{$\varphi(z)$},\end{split}

(13)

then
F
has a fixed point.

Proof.
We apply the idea from
[
11
]
. In view of the Teichmüller-Tukey lemma there exists an

x_{0}
belonging to a maximal set
A\subset X
such that all
x,z\in A
satisfy

\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq\mid\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}-\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}\mid.

Let us adopt
\gamma=\inf\{\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}\colon z\in A\}
, and suppose that
\gamma<\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}
,

z\in A
. Then there exists a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
(\varphi(x_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
decreases
to
\gamma
. For each
m<n
we have

\begin{split}&\delta(\mbox{$x_{m}$},\mbox{$x_{n}$})\leq\delta(\mbox{$x_{m}$},x_{m+1})+\cdots+\delta(x_{n-1},\mbox{$x_{n}$})\leq\\
&\varphi(x_{m})-\varphi(x_{m+1})+\cdots+\varphi(x_{n-1})-\mbox{$\varphi(x_{n})$}=\varphi(x_{m})-\mbox{$\varphi(x_{n})$},\end{split}

and therefore
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence converging, say to an
x\in X
(
X
is complete).
For any
z\in A
, and large
n
we have
\delta(x_{n},z)\leq\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}-\mbox{$\varphi(x_{n})$}
,

\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq\delta(x,x_{n})+\delta(x_{n},z)\leq\delta(x,x_{n})+\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}-\mbox{$\varphi(x_{n})$}\leq\delta(x,x_{n})+\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}-\mbox{$\varphi(x)$},

(
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous), and consequently,
\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}-\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}
,
i.e.

x\in A
.
In addition,
\gamma\leq\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}<\mbox{$\varphi(x_{n})$}
means
\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}=\gamma
. Suppose
x\notin F(x)
, and
let
z\neq x
be as in (
13
),
i.e.

\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}\leq\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}-\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}<\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}=\gamma.

On the other hand, for any
y\in A
we have

\delta(y,z)\leq\delta(y,x)+\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq\varphi(y)-\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}+\mbox{$\varphi(x)$}-\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}=\varphi(y)-\mbox{$\varphi(z)$},

i.e.

z\in A
, and therefore,
\gamma\leq\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}
which contradicts
\mbox{$\varphi(z)$}<\gamma
.
\quad\square

Theorem 3.4

Condition (
14
) has much in common with generalized inwardness. Let
(Y,d)
be a metric space.
Then for
x,t\in Y
a metric segment is defined by

[x,t]=\{s\in Y\colon d(x,s)+d(s,t)=d(x,t)\},

and
(x,t]=[x,t]\setminus\{x\}
. For a nonempty set
X\subset Y
a generalized inward set

\tilde{I}_{X}(x)
can be defined by (
cf
.
[

, p. 1209]

\begin{split}&\mbox{$\tilde{I}_{X}(x)$}=\{t\in Y\colon\mbox{ for each }\beta>0\mbox{ there exists an}\\
&s\in(x,t]\mbox{ such that }d(z,s)\leq\beta d(x,s)\}.\end{split}

The inward set
I_{X}(x)
in a normed space is defined by

I_{X}(x)=x+\{\lambda(z-x)\colon z\in X\mbox{, }\lambda\geq 1\},

and the weakly inward set is
\overline{I_{X}(x)}
.

Maciejewski has proved
[

that if

is a normed space, then
\overline{I_{X}(x)}\subset\mbox{$\tilde{I}_{X}(x)$}

Lemma 3.5

Let
(Y,d)
be a metric space,
X,C
nonempty subsets of
Y
, and
x\in X\subset C
. Then for
each
\epsilon>0
,
t\in C\cap\mbox{$\tilde{I}_{X}(x)$}
there exists a
z\in X\setminus\{x\}
such that

(\mbox{$1-\epsilon$})d(x,z)\leq d(x,t)-d(z,t).

If
t
is the nearest point of
C
to
x
, and
t\in\mbox{$\tilde{I}_{X}(x)$}
, then

(\mbox{$1-\epsilon$})d(x,z)\leq d(x,C)-d(z,C).

Proof.
For a
t\in C
let
s\in(x,t]
,
z\in X
be such that
d(z,s)\leq\beta d(x,s)
.
We have

\begin{split}&d(x,z)+d(z,t)-d(x,t)\leq d(x,s)+d(s,z)+d(z,s)+d(s,t)-d(x,t)\leq\\
&2d(z,s)+d(x,s)+d(s,t)-d(x,t)=2d(z,s)\leq 2\beta d(x,s).\end{split}

From

d(x,s)\leq d(x,z)+d(z,s)\leq d(x,z)+\beta d(x,s)

we obtain
d(x,s)\leq d(x,z)/(1-\beta)
for
\beta<1
. Hence it follows that

d(x,z)+d(z,t)-d(x,t)\leq 2\beta d(x,z)/(1-\beta)\leq\epsilon d(x,z),

i.e.

(\mbox{$1-\epsilon$})d(x,z)\leq d(x,t)-d(z,t)

for small
\beta>0
. If
t
is the nearest point to
x
in
C
, then we have

(\mbox{$1-\epsilon$})d(x,z)\leq d(x,C)-d(z,t)\leq d(x,C)-d(z,C).\qquad\square

Theorem 3.8

Let
X
be a set in a complete metric space
(Y,d)
. Assume that a mapping
F\colon X\to 2^{Y}
has
closed graph, and
\delta
is a metric on

\begin{split}&\mbox{for each }\mbox{$x\in X\setminus F(x)$}\mbox{, }t\in F(x)\mbox{ there exist }\mbox{$z\in X\setminus\{x\}$}\mbox{, }v\in F(x)\\
&\mbox{such that }\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq d(x,t)-d(z,v)\mbox{, and }\delta(t,v)\leq k(d(x,t)-d(z,v))\end{split}

(16)

holds for a

Proof.
Assume
\mbox{$x_{0}$}\notin F(\mbox{$x_{0}$})
, and let
A\neq\emptyset
be a maximal set in
G=graph(F)

such that for
(x,t),(z,v)\in A

\gamma=\inf\{d(x,t)\colon(x,t)\in A\}
, and suppose that

\gamma<d(x,t)
,
(x,t)\in A
. Then there exists a sequence
((x_{n},t_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

such that
(d(x_{n},t_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\gamma
. For each
m<n

\delta(\mbox{$x_{m}$},\mbox{$x_{n}$})\leq\delta(\mbox{$x_{m}$},x_{m+1})+\cdots+\delta(x_{n-1},\mbox{$x_{n}$})\leq d(\mbox{$x_{m}$},t_{m})-d(\mbox{$x_{n}$},\mbox{$t_{n}$}),

and therefore,
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence. Similarly,
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also a Cauchy sequence.
Then for
x=\mbox{$\lim_{n\to\infty}$}\mbox{$x_{n}$}
,
t=\mbox{$\lim_{n\to\infty}$}\mbox{$t_{n}$}

(x,t)\in G
holds as

is closed. For any

\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq d(z,v)-d(x,t)
.
In a similar way we state that

(x,t)\in A

\begin{split}&\delta(y,z)\leq\delta(y,x)+\mbox{$\delta(x,z)$}\leq d(y,s)-d(x,t)+d(x,t)-d(z,v)=\\
&d(y,s)-d(z,v)\mbox{, }\qquad\delta(s,v)\leq k(d(y,s)-d(z,v)).\end{split}

Consequently,
(z,v)\in A
holds, and therefore,
\gamma\leq d(z,v)
which contradicts

d(z,v)<\gamma
.

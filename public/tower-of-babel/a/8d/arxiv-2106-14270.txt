Relativistic correction of the Coulomb interaction in the local density approximation for energies and radii in doubly-magic nuclei
Tomoya Naito

Abstract
Effects of the relativistic correction of the Coulomb interaction on doubly-magic nuclei are discussed with Skyrme Hartree–Fock calculations. The relativistic correction is treated by using the local density approximation. It is found that the correction to the total energy is about 2.4 ​ MeV 2.4\,\mathrm{MeV} for Pb 208 {}^{208}\mathrm{Pb} , while proton and neutron radii do not change significantly. This difference is larger than the difference of the Coulomb exchange (Fock) energy calculated with the local density (Hartree–Fock–Slater) approximation and that with the exact treatment and the neutron finite-size effect. Effects of the correction are also compared to the correction due to the vacuum polarization. It is shown that the two contributions to the total energy are comparable in light nuclei, but the latter dominates in heavy nuclei, while the contribution of the relativistic correction to the total energy is non-negligible compared to the target accuracy of the DFT calculation.

Thus, it should be tested to what extent the Breit correction contributes to nuclear binding energies.

The covariant density functional theory, the nuclear DFT in the relativistic scheme,

Then, I will construct the LDA EDF for Eq. (

Referring the result on Eq. (

The Hartree and the exchange EDFs of Eq. (

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Darwin}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{H}}}}}}\left[\rho_{p},\rho_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\int\left\{\frac{C_{pp}}{2}\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}+C_{pn}\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)+\frac{C_{nn}}{2}\left[\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\right\}\,d{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\int\left\{\frac{C_{pp}}{2}\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}+C_{pn}\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right\}\,d{\bm{r}},

(13a)

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Darwin}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x}}}}}}\left[\rho_{p},\rho_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=-\int\left\{\frac{C_{pp}}{4}\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}+\frac{C_{nn}}{4}\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\right\}\,d{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{C_{pp}}{4}\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}},

(13b)

where

C_{\tau_{j}\tau_{k}}=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{2M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\delta_{p\tau_{k}}\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{\tau_{j}}-\delta_{p\tau_{j}}\right)+\delta_{p\tau_{j}}\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{\tau_{k}}-\delta_{p\tau_{k}}\right)\right]

(14)

and accordingly

\displaystyle C_{pp}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{M^{2}c^{2}}\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right),

(15a)

\displaystyle C_{pn}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{M^{2}c^{2}}\tilde{\mu}_{n},

(15b)

\displaystyle C_{nn}

\displaystyle=0.

(15c)

The exchange EDF for Eq. (

) does not include only a contribution from Eq. (

\frac{3\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{2m_{e}^{2}c^{2}}\int\left[\rho\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{m_{e}^{2}c^{2}}\int\left[\rho\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}=\frac{5\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{4m_{e}^{2}c^{2}}\int\left[\rho\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}},

(16)

since the Darwin EDF for electronic systems is

-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{m_{e}c^{2}}\int\left[\rho\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}.

(17)

m_{e}

E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{retar}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x}}}}}}\left[\rho_{p},\rho_{n}\right]=\frac{5\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{4M^{2}c^{2}}\int\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}.

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}\left[\rho_{p},\rho_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\int\left\{\frac{C_{pp}}{2}\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}+C_{pn}\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right\}\,d{\bm{r}}-\int\frac{C_{pp}}{4}\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}+\frac{5\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{4M^{2}c^{2}}\int\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{C_{pp}}{2}-\frac{C_{pp}}{4}+\frac{5\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{4M^{2}c^{2}}\right)\int\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}+C_{pn}\int\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\,d{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\left(3-\tilde{\mu}_{p}\right)\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{2M^{2}c^{2}}\int\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}-\tilde{\mu}_{n}\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{M^{2}c^{2}}\int\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\,d{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{M^{2}c^{2}}\int\left[0.1035\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)+1.913\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\,d{\bm{r}}.

(20)

3
Analytical Estimation

Before performing numerical many-body calculations with the relativistic correction,
I estimate contribution of the correction to the total energy with a simple model
and discuss the systematic behavior.
As in Ref.
[
40
]
,
I assume hard-sphere distributions for both proton and neutron distribution
\rho_{p}
and
\rho_{n}
:

\rho_{\tau}\left(r\right)=\begin{cases}\rho_{0}^{\tau}&r\leq R_{\tau},\\
0&r>R_{\tau},\end{cases}

(21)

where
R_{\tau}
is the proton (
\tau=p
) or neutron (
\tau=n
) radius defined by

R_{\tau}=\left(\frac{3N_{\tau}}{4\pi\rho_{0}^{\tau}}\right)^{1/3}

(22)

with
N_{p}=Z
and
N_{n}=N
.
For simplicity,
the half of the saturation density of atomic nuclei

\rho_{0}^{p}=\rho_{0}^{n}\approx 0.08\,\mathrm{fm}^{-3}
is assumed.
With this density, one can estimate the Coulomb Hartree and exchange energies as
[
40
]

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Coul}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{H}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3e^{2}}{5}\left(\frac{4\pi\rho_{0}^{p}}{3}\right)^{1/3}Z^{5/3}

\displaystyle\simeq 0.60Z^{5/3}\,\mathrm{MeV},

(23a)

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Coul}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3e^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{3\rho_{0}^{p}}{\pi}\right)^{1/3}Z

\displaystyle\simeq-0.46Z\,\mathrm{MeV},

(23b)

respectively.

Since relations

\displaystyle\int\left[\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\right]^{2}\,d{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi R_{p}^{3}}{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho_{0}^{p}Z,

(24a)

\displaystyle\int\rho_{p}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\rho_{n}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\,d{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi R_{<}^{3}}{3}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\rho_{n}^{0}Z&\text{for $R_{p}<R_{n}$},\\
\rho_{p}^{0}N&\text{for $R_{p}>R_{n}$},\end{cases}

(24b)

hold with
R_{<}=R_{p}
for
R_{p}<R_{n}
and
R_{<}=R_{n}
for
R_{p}>R_{n}
,
one, accordingly, obtains

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Darwin}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{H}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{pp}}{2}\frac{4\pi R_{p}^{3}}{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}+C_{pn}\frac{4\pi R_{<}^{3}}{3}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right)R_{p}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}+2\tilde{\mu}_{n}R_{<}^{3}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}\right],

(25a)

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Darwin}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{pp}}{4}\frac{4\pi R_{p}^{3}}{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right)R_{p}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{4M^{2}c^{2}}\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right)\rho_{0}^{p}Z

\displaystyle\simeq 0.018Z\,\mathrm{MeV},

(25b)

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{retar}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{5\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}R_{p}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{5\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{4M^{2}c^{2}}\rho_{0}^{p}Z

\displaystyle\simeq 0.020Z\,\mathrm{MeV},

(25c)

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(3-\tilde{\mu}_{p}\right)R_{p}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}-2\tilde{\mu}_{n}R_{<}^{3}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}\right].

(25d)

In the case of
R_{p}<R_{n}
,
Eqs. (
25a
) and (
25d
) read

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Darwin}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{H}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right)\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}+2\tilde{\mu}_{n}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}\right]R_{p}^{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{2M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right)\rho_{0}^{p}+2\tilde{\mu}_{n}\rho_{n}^{0}\right]Z

\displaystyle\simeq-0.006Z\,\mathrm{MeV},

(26a)

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(3-\tilde{\mu}_{p}\right)R_{p}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}-2\tilde{\mu}_{n}R_{p}^{3}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{2M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(3-\tilde{\mu}_{p}\right)\rho_{0}^{p}-2\tilde{\mu}_{n}\rho_{n}^{0}\right]Z

\displaystyle\simeq 0.032Z\,\mathrm{MeV};

(26b)

in the case of
R_{p}>R_{n}
,
they read

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Darwin}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{H}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right)R_{p}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}+2\tilde{\mu}_{n}R_{n}^{3}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{2M^{2}c^{2}}\rho_{0}^{p}\left[\left(2\tilde{\mu}_{p}-1\right)Z+2\tilde{\mu}_{n}N\right]

\displaystyle\simeq-0.037Z+0.031N\,\mathrm{MeV},

(27a)

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{3M^{2}c^{2}}\left[\left(3-\tilde{\mu}_{p}\right)R_{p}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}^{p}\right)^{2}-2\tilde{\mu}_{n}R_{n}^{3}\rho_{p}^{0}\rho_{n}^{0}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}e^{2}}{2M^{2}c^{2}}\rho_{0}^{p}\left[\left(3-\tilde{\mu}_{p}\right)Z-2\tilde{\mu}_{n}N\right]

\displaystyle\simeq 0.002Z+0.031N\,\mathrm{MeV}.

(27b)

Comparing Eqs. (
23b
) and (
26b
),
one can find that the relativistic correction to the total energy
\left|E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}\right|
is
15
times smaller than
the Coulomb exchange energy
\left|E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Coul}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x}}}}}}\right|
.
This means that although
E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}
is small compared to the other contributions,
one can estimate that
E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}
for
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
may be around
2\,\mathrm{MeV}
,
which is a non-negligible contribution compared to the desired accuracy of
E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{nucl}}}}}}

(
O\left(100\right)\,\mathrm{keV}
).

In atomic physics, the vacuum polarization to the Coulomb potential formed by an atomic nucleus
is the next-leading order to the Breit correction,
i.e., the relativistic correction of electron-electron interaction
[
58
]
.
On the other hand, in nuclear structure,
both the vacuum polarization and the relativistic correction
are corrections of the proton-proton Coulomb interaction.
Thus, it is worthwhile to compare them.
In Ref.
[
40
]
,
the vacuum polarization was taken into account in a nuclear structure calculation
using the Uehling potential
[
79
,
80
]
.
The contribution of the vacuum polarization was estimated as
[
40
]

E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{VP}}}}}}\simeq 0.0047Z^{5/3}\,\mathrm{MeV}.

(28)

Hence, the relativistic correction and the vacuum polarization provide comparable contributions to the total energy in the light nuclei
(e.g., for
Z=20
,

E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}\approx 0.64\,\mathrm{MeV}

and

E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{VP}}}}}}\approx 0.69\,\mathrm{MeV}
)
but
the latter dominates in heavy nuclei
(e.g., for
Z=100
,

E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Rel}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{tot}}}}}}\approx 3.2\,\mathrm{MeV}

and

E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{VP}}}}}}\approx 10\,\mathrm{MeV}
).
In the next section, I shall confirm this by performing numerical calculations.

4
Skyrme Hartree–Fock Calculation

To calculate the ground-state density distribution and energy numerically,
one needs to use a many-body calculation technique.
In this paper, the Skyrme Hartree–Fock method is used.

The EDF for the correction [Eq. (
19
)] is implemented to
the self-consistent Skyrme Hartree–Fock calculation code
skyrme_rpa

[
81
]
.
In this work, doubly-magic nuclei are focused on;
thus, the pairing interaction is not considered,
and the spherical symmetry is assumed.
The calculations are performed with a box of
15\,\mathrm{fm}
with
0.1\,\mathrm{fm}
mesh.
The SAMi EDF
[
82
]

is adopted for the nuclear EDF.

It is worthwhile to mention here that the non-relativistic Coulomb GGA exchange EDF,
more precisely, the modified Coulomb GGA-Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) EDF with
\lambda=1.25

[
39
]
,
reproduces the exact Coulomb Fock energy less than
100\,\mathrm{keV}
error,
which is much less than the relativistic correction, which I will discuss;
thus, in this paper, the exact Coulomb Fock energy is approximated to the Coulomb GGA exchange energy,

E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Coul}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x-GGA}}}}}}\simeq E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Coul}}}}}}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{x-exact}}}}}}
.

First of all, I shall mention effects to nuclear radii.
Neither the proton nor the neutron radii are found to be changed more than
0.002\,\mathrm{fm}
due to the relativistic correction and the vacuum polarization.
Therefore, the effect of the correction on nuclear radii can be safely neglected.

5
Conclusion and Perspectives

In this paper, the relativistic correction of the Coulomb interaction
was introduced in Skyrme Hartree–Fock calculations using the local density approximation (LDA).
The correction contains the Darwin term, the retardation, the spin-orbit interaction, and the spin-magnetic interaction,
while only the first and second terms were considered in this paper
because the remaining terms vanish in the LDA.

It was found that the relativistic correction makes
{}^{40}\mathrm{Ca}
less bound by
0.4\,\mathrm{MeV}

and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
by
2.4\,\mathrm{MeV}
,
which are comparable with or even larger than the difference between the exact Coulomb exchange (exact-Fock),
or its simplified treatment called the generalized gradient approximation (GGA),
and the LDA (Hartree–Fock–Slater approximation) Coulomb exchange energies
[
61
,
62
,
38
,
39
]
,
but with the opposite sign.
Thus,
once both the exact treatment and the relativistic correction are considered at the same time,
even the sign of the total correction is not obvious,
as was pointed out also in the context of atomic physics
[
51
]
.
In addition, since the desired accuracy of the nuclear EDF is
100\,\mathrm{keV}
order,
the relativistic correction is often non-negligible, as well as the correction due to the density gradient.

The correction was also compared with the vacuum polarization.
Since the proton-number dependence of the vacuum polarization is stronger than that of the relativistic correction,
these two contributions to the total energy are comparable in light nuclei,
but the former dominates in heavy nuclei.
This behavior is in contrast to atoms,
in which the vacuum polarization is sub-dominant to the Breit correction
[
58
]
.
Although the contribution of vacuum polarization to the total energy is usually tiny,
it is non-negligible to discuss mirror nuclei mass difference or the isobaric analog energy
[
11
,
7
,
40
]
,
whereas the relativistic correction does not contribute to such properties.

In this calculation, the spin-orbit and spin-magnetic interactions were not able to be considered
since they vanish in the LDA.
The former has been discussed in Refs.
[
7
,
78
,
40
]
,
while the latter has not been considered in the self-consistent calculation.
Consideration of the latter is left for a future task.
The relativistic Coulomb GGA functional is indispensable to achieve more accurate calculation in both atomic and nuclear structure calculations,
which is also left for a future perspective.

The Coulomb correlation energy,
which originates from many-body correlations
[
83
,
84
,
85
,
86
,
87
]
,
is also an important and interesting topic.
In electronic systems, the Coulomb correlation energy can be defined without any ambiguity as the difference between the “exact” energy and the Hartree–Fock energy, which can be calculated by using the Monte Carlo calculation and some other many-body techniques
[
88
,
15
,
16
,
89
,
90
,
91
,
92
,
17
]
.
Reference
[
60
]

had derived the relativistic Coulomb exchange-correlation EDF in the LDA level,
while Ref.
[
51
]

discussed that the relativistic correction of the Coulomb correlation energy is almost negligible.
Meanwhile, in nuclear systems, there exists both the nuclear and Coulomb interaction;
hence, there is still ambiguity in defining the Coulomb correlation energy,
while there have been discussed in several works
[
93
,
94
,
95
,
38
]
.
Thus, it should be discussed whether the relativistic correction of the Coulomb correlation energy in nuclear systems is negligible,
while it is left for a future perspective.

Once all the relevant corrections to the Coulomb energy are taken into account for the nuclear DFT calculation on top of the non-relativistic Coulomb LDA exchange EDF,
the correction may be more than
1\,\mathrm{MeV}
for heavy nuclei, such as
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
.
For instance, the nucleon finite-size effects and the vacuum polarization contribute to the binding energy of
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
, in total, in about
4\,\mathrm{MeV}

[
40
]
,
the relativistic correction discussed in this paper is about
2\,\mathrm{MeV}
,
and
the Coulomb correlation energy may reach an order of
1\,\mathrm{MeV}

[
94
]
.
The current accuracy of nuclear EDFs is about
1
–
5\,\mathrm{MeV}
in binding energies
[
31
,
96
]
,
which is the same order as or even smaller than the Coulomb corrections.
Therefore, in order to achieve accurate calculation of binding energies using nuclear EDF quantitatively,
the nuclear EDF should be, in principle, refitted by considering all the relevant Coulomb corrections.

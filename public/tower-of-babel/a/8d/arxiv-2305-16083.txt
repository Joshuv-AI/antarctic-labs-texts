Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction in strongly spin-orbit-coupled systems: General formula and application to topological and Rashba materials
Yuto Hayakawa, Yusuke Imai, and Hiroshi Kohno

Abstract
We theoretically study the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) mediated by band electrons with strong spin-orbit coupling (SOC). We first derive a general formula for the coefficient 𝑫 i {\bm{D}}_{i} of the DMI in free energy in terms of Green’s functions, and examine its variations in relation to physical quantities. In general, the DMI coefficient can vary depending on physical quantities, i.e., whether one is looking at equilibrium spin structure ( 𝑫 i {\bm{D}}_{i} ) or spin-wave dispersion ( 𝑫 i ( 2 ) {\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)} ), and the obtained formula helps to elucidate their relations. By explicit evaluations for a magnetic topological insulator and a Rashba ferromagnet with perpendicular magnetization, we observe 𝑫 i ( 2 ) ≠ 𝑫 i {\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}\neq{\bm{D}}_{i} in general. In the latter model, or more generally, when the magnetization and the spin-orbit field are mutually orthogonal, 𝑫 i {\bm{D}}_{i} is exactly related to the equilibrium spin current for arbitrary strength of SOC, generalizing the similar relation for systems with weak SOC. Among various systems with strong SOC, magnetic Weyl semimetals are special in that 𝑫 i ( 2 ) = 𝑫 i {\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}={\bm{D}}_{i} , and in fact, the DMI in this system arises as the chiral anomaly.

II
General Formula

In this section, after describing a model, we derive a general formula for the DMI coefficient
in free energy in terms of Green’s functions.
We then study the DMI coefficients in several physical quantities by taking variational derivatives
of the DM free energy.

II.1
Microscopic model

We consider band electrons described by the (nonmagnetic) Hamiltonian
H_{0}

{\bm{M}}

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}{\cal H}^{0}_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}},

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d{\bm{r}}\,c^{\dagger}({\bm{r}})({\bm{M}}({\bm{r}})\cdot{\bm{\sigma}})c({\bm{r}})

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{q}}}c_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}^{\dagger}({\bm{M}}_{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}})c_{\bm{k}}.

In a localized picture of ferromagnetism,

II.4
DMI and physical quantities

Here, we briefly look at how the free energy variations are related to physical quantities.

First, the coefficient
\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}
itself determines the change in (free) energy
when the magnetization is twisted.
This fact is often used in first-principles calculations of the DMI coefficient.
Koretsune2018

For a helical structure,

\displaystyle{\bm{n}}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle={\bm{e}}_{1}\cos({\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}})\pm{\bm{e}}_{2}\sin({\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}),

From the slope of this

In the equilibrium configuration,

28

To determine the magnitude of

III.1
General expression for two-band models

The models studied in this section are (essentially) described by
2\times 2
matrices
in spin space, and the Hamiltonian has the form,

\displaystyle H_{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}+{\bm{M}}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}})\,c_{\bm{k}},

(34)

where
c_{\bm{k}}={}^{t}(c_{\bm{k}\uparrow},c_{\bm{k}\downarrow})

is the two-component electron operator,
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}
is the (scalar) energy,
and
{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{k}}
is the “spin-orbit field”.
The magnetization
\bm{M}=(M_{x},M_{y},M_{z})
is assumed static and uniform,
which is sufficient for the calculation of the coefficient
D_{i}^{\alpha}
based on Eq. (
15
).

The
2\times 2
Green’s function
G_{M}
for
H_{M}
is

\displaystyle G_{M}({\bm{k}},i\varepsilon_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{0}+{\bm{g}}_{M}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}}{D_{M}},

(35)

where

g_{0}=i\varepsilon_{m}+\mu-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}
,

{\bm{g}}_{M}={\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{k}}+{\bm{M}}
,
and
D_{M}=g_{0}^{2}-{\bm{g}}_{M}^{2}
.
The formula (
26

\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}=T\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\partial_{i}g_{0}){\bm{g}}_{M}-g_{0}(\partial_{i}{\bm{g}}_{M})}{D_{M}^{2}}.

(36)

Magnetic topological insulator

As a first example, we consider 2D Dirac fermions on a surface of topological

\displaystyle D_{i}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon_{i\alpha}\,\frac{{\rm sgn}\mu}{4\pi v_{\rm F}}\int_{0}^{M}M^{\prime}dM^{\prime}\,\Theta(|\mu|>|M_{z}^{\prime}|).

By writing

\displaystyle-\varepsilon_{i\alpha}\,\frac{{\rm sgn}\mu}{8\pi v_{\rm F}}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}M^{2}&(|\mu|>|M_{z}|)\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta}&(|\mu|<|M_{z}|)\end{array}\right..

Interestingly, this is finite even when

Though

is typically nonzero even in the gap, it is an odd function of
\mu

at
\mu=0
(i.e., in the ground or equilibrium state).
A finite DMI in the gap, realized at
\mu\neq 0

by, e.g., gating or impurity doping.

The mathematical reason why
{\bm{D}}_{i}
is nonzero (while

at smaller

-integration.
That is, even when
\mu

Physically, it means that twisting the magnetization changes the electronic energy
in a chirality-dependent way even in the insulating state.

The factor

In comparing

IV.3
Relation to equilibrium spin current

Kikuchi et al. pointed out that the DMI coefficient is given by the equilibrium spin-current density.
Kikuchi2016

Their “spin-current formula” holds at the first order in SOC,
thus it is appropriate to systems with weak SOC.
Also, they assumed a parabolic electron dispersion as well as SOC that is linear in
{\bm{k}}
.

In our formulation, such spin-current formula can be obtained if first-order terms in SOC
are retained.
This is demonstrated in Appendix
C.2
, where one can see that the spin-current formula,

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{i}^{\perp}(M)=-{\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}(M),

(61)

holds for arbitrary forms of electron dispersion and SOC.
Note that this is restricted to the perpendicular component of
{\bm{D}}_{i}
.
(The parallel component of the spin current vanishes,
hence
{\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}^{\perp}={\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}
, at the first order in SOC.)

The equality between
{\bm{D}}_{i}^{\perp}
and the equilibrium spin current can be extended
to arbitrary strength of SOC (not just at the lowest order, but to all orders)
for a Rashba ferromagnet with perpendicular magnetization.
In this case, the above relation is generalized to

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{i}^{\perp}(M)=-\left\{{\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}^{\perp}(M)-{\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}^{\perp}(0)\right\},

(62)

in which the spin current at
{\bm{M}}={\bm{0}}
is subtracted.
Therefore, the DMI coefficient is equal to the
change
of the equilibrium spin current induced
by the development of
{\bm{M}}
, rather than the “preexisting” spin current at
{\bm{M}}={\bm{0}}
.
It is known that the equilibrium spin current at
{\bm{M}}={\bm{0}}
is generally nonzero
but starts at third order in SOC.
Rashba2003
;
Tokatly2008
;
Droghetti2022

Thus, Eq. (
62
) reduces to Eq. (
61
)
when only the lowest- (i.e., first-) order terms in SOC are considered.
Finally, we note that Eq. (
62
) holds more generally (beyond the Rashba model)
if the magnetization is perpendicular to the spin-orbit field.
A proof of this is given in Appendix
D
.

V
Summary

In this paper, we have developed a microscopic theory of the DMI mediated by band electrons.
In contrast to Moriya’s original theory, which considered Mott insulators,
the present one applies to metals, semiconductors, semimetals, and even to band insulators;
in fact we have applied it to topological insulator surface states in its insulating state.

We first derived a general formula for the coefficient of the DM free energy in terms of Green’s functions.
We then pointed out that the DMI coefficient generally depends on physical quantities,
i.e., whether we are looking at (equilibrium) spin structure, torque, or spin waves.
This distinction is important in systems with strong SOC, and spectacular examples are given by
the Rashba ferromagnet in the half-metallic state (with one partially-occupied band and one empty band)
and the MTI surface states in the insulating state.
Experimentally, it is expected that the difference between
{\bm{D}}_{i}
and
{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}

can be significant when they deviate from the
\propto M^{2}
behavior.
On the other hand, an exact
\propto M^{2}
behavior, hence the equality

{\bm{D}}_{i}={\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}
, has been found in magnetic Weyl semimetal.
This is probably an exceptional case among systems with strong SOC,
as one might discuss through the analysis of the chiral anomaly.

Also, we have generalized the spin-current formula for the coefficient of the DM free energy,
which was known to hold in systems with weak SOC, to the case of strong SOC.
Under the assumption that the magnetization is perpendicular to the spin-orbit field,
the DMI coefficient is proportional to the equilibrium spin current induced by
the coupling to the magnetization.

Appendix B
Calculation of free energy variations

In this Appendix, we calculate first- and second-order variations of the DM free energy functional,

\displaystyle F_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\,\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}({\bm{M}})\cdot({\bm{M}}\times\partial_{i}{\bm{M}}),

(80)

{\bm{M}}\to{\bm{M}}+\delta{\bm{M}}

Note that
\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}({\bm{M}})

In the following calculation, it is convenient to define

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}({\bm{M}})\times{\bm{M}},

(81)

\displaystyle F_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\,{\bm{B}}_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}{\bm{M}}.

B.1
First-order variation

We start with the first-order variation,

\displaystyle\delta^{(1)}F_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\,(\delta{\bm{B}}_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}{\bm{M}}+{\bm{B}}_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}\delta{\bm{M}}),

(83)

where
\delta{\bm{B}}_{i}=(\partial{\bm{B}}_{i}/\partial M^{\alpha})\delta M^{\alpha}
.

\displaystyle\delta^{(1)}F_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\left(\frac{\partial B_{i}^{\beta}}{\partial M^{\alpha}}-\frac{\partial B_{i}^{\alpha}}{\partial M^{\beta}}\right)\delta M^{\alpha}\partial_{i}M^{\beta}

\displaystyle\equiv\int d{\bm{r}}\,\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(1)}\cdot(\delta{\bm{M}}\times\partial_{i}{\bm{M}}),

(84)

where we defined

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\nabla}}_{M}\times{\bm{B}}_{i}={\bm{\nabla}}_{M}\times(\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}\times{\bm{M}}),

(85)

with
{\bm{\nabla}}_{M}=\partial/\partial{\bm{M}}=(\partial/\partial M_{x},\partial/\partial M_{y},\partial/\partial M_{z})
.

B.2
Second-order variation

The second-order variation is given by
\delta^{(2)}F_{\rm DM}=F_{1}+F_{2}

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\,\delta{\bm{B}}_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}\delta{\bm{M}},

(86)

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\,\delta^{(2)}{\bm{B}}_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}{\bm{M}},

(87)

where
\delta{\bm{B}}_{i}=(\partial{\bm{B}}_{i}/\partial M^{\alpha})\,\delta M^{\alpha}
(as above)

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int d{\bm{r}}\,\frac{\partial B_{i}^{\beta}}{\partial M^{\alpha}}(\partial_{i}\delta M^{\alpha})\cdot\delta M^{\beta}

\displaystyle\quad-\int d{\bm{r}}\,\frac{\partial^{2}B_{i}^{\beta}}{\partial M^{\alpha}\partial M^{\gamma}}(\partial_{i}M^{\gamma})\,\delta M^{\alpha}\delta M^{\beta}.

(88)

Summing Eqs. (

) and (

) leads to

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d{\bm{r}}\,\left(\frac{\partial B_{i}^{\beta}}{\partial M^{\alpha}}-\frac{\partial B_{i}^{\alpha}}{\partial M^{\beta}}\right)\delta M^{\alpha}\,\partial_{i}\delta M^{\beta}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int d{\bm{r}}\,\frac{\partial^{2}B_{i}^{\beta}}{\partial M^{\alpha}\partial M^{\gamma}}(\partial_{i}M^{\gamma})\,\delta M^{\alpha}\delta M^{\beta}.

(89)

We write the first term as

\displaystyle\delta^{(2a)}F_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\,\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}\cdot(\delta{\bm{M}}\times\partial_{i}\delta{\bm{M}}),

(90)

where

\displaystyle 2\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\nabla}}_{M}\times{\bm{B}}_{i}={\bm{\nabla}}_{M}\times(\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}\times{\bm{M}}).

(91)

The remaining terms [
F_{2}
and the second term in Eq. (

)] are combined into

\displaystyle\delta^{(2b)}F_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d{\bm{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial M^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\partial B_{i}^{\gamma}}{\partial M^{\beta}}-\frac{\partial B_{i}^{\beta}}{\partial M^{\gamma}}\right)\delta M^{\alpha}\delta M^{\beta}\,\partial_{i}M^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{r}}\,\frac{\partial\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}}{\partial M^{\alpha}}\delta M^{\alpha}\!\cdot(\delta{\bm{M}}\times\partial_{i}{\bm{M}})

\displaystyle\equiv\int d{\bm{r}}\,\delta\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}\!\cdot(\delta{\bm{M}}\times\partial_{i}{\bm{M}}).

(92)

B.3
General formula for
\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}

As shown in the preceding subsection, for given
{\bm{D}}_{i}
(or
\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}
),
one can calculate

and
\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(1)}=2\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\nabla}}_{\bm{M}}\times(\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}\times{\bm{M}}),

(93)

or

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(1)}=2\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=2\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}+M\frac{\partial\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}}{\partial M}-{\bm{M}}{\rm div}_{M}\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}

where

(96)

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{i}^{(2),\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4i}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,T\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}{\rm tr}\left[\sigma^{\beta}\frac{\partial G_{M}}{\partial k_{i}}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{M}\right],

which does not involve the
M

\zeta

B.4
Proof of
{\rm div}_{M}\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}=0

To prove
{\rm div}_{M}\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}=0
, we use Eq. (
76
) and write

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{i}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\zeta\,d\zeta\,T\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}{\rm tr}\left[\sigma^{\beta}\frac{\partial G_{\zeta}}{\partial k_{i}}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{\zeta}\right].

(99)

Since only the integrand,

\displaystyle d_{i}^{\alpha}({\bm{M}})

\displaystyle\equiv\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\sum_{\bm{k}}{\rm tr}\left[\sigma^{\beta}G_{\zeta}v_{i}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{\zeta}\right],

(100)

depends on
{\bm{M}}
, it is sufficient to prove

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{M}\,{\bm{d}}_{i}({\bm{M}})=0.

(101)

Using
\partial G_{\zeta}/\partial M^{\alpha}=\zeta\,G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\alpha}G_{\zeta}
, one has

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{M}\,{\bm{d}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,\zeta\sum_{\bm{k}}{\rm tr}\left[\sigma^{\beta}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\alpha}G_{\zeta}v_{i}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{\zeta}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21495pt+(\text{2 similar terms}),

(102)

which consists of terms of the form,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\equiv\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\sum_{\bm{k}}{\rm tr}\left[G_{\zeta}v_{i}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\beta}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\alpha}\right].

(103)

Noting
G_{\zeta}v_{i}G_{\zeta}=\partial G_{\zeta}/\partial k_{i}
and integrating by parts, we find

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\sum_{\bm{k}}\biggl\{{\rm tr}\left[G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{\zeta}v_{i}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\beta}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21495pt+{\rm tr}\left[G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\beta}G_{\zeta}v_{i}G_{\zeta}\sigma^{\alpha}\right]\biggr\}

\displaystyle=-2I,

(104)

hence,
I=0
.
This means that the right-hand side of Eq. (
102
) vanishes,
proving Eq. (
101
), thus
{\rm div}_{M}\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}=0
.

B.5

The condition

) and the tensorial structure
(i.e., dependence on

\tilde{D}_{i}^{\alpha}

If

{\bm{M}}

\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}=\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}

where

does not depend on the in-plane components,
M_{x}
and
M_{y}

While the
\tilde{C}_{2}
-term does not contribute to the DM free energy, its presence hinders the extraction
of any information on
\tilde{C}_{1}
from
{\rm div}_{M}\tilde{\bm{D}}_{i}=0
.

C.1

Appendix C
Weak spin-orbit coupling

In this section, we show that at the first order in SOC, the DMI coefficient (in free energy) is given
by the equilibrium spin-current density.
Such relation was found in Ref.
Kikuchi2016
, and here we make a slight generalization.
Since the SOC is treated perturbatively, topological materials are not eligible.

We consider the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=\sum_{\bm{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\gamma_{i}({\bm{k}})\sigma^{\alpha}+\bm{M}\cdot\bm{\sigma})c_{\bm{k}},

(105)

where
c_{\bm{k}}={}^{t}(c_{\bm{k}\uparrow},c_{\bm{k}\downarrow})
.
The second term describes the SOC with strength
\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}
.
A free electron model would assume
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}=k^{2}/2m
and
\gamma_{i}({\bm{k}})=k_{i}
,
as in Ref.
Kikuchi2016
,
but here, we leave
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}
and
\gamma_{i}({\bm{k}})
to be arbitrary functions
of
{\bm{k}}
.
As shown below, even with this setting, the DMI coefficient is still given
by the equilibrium spin current at the first order in
\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}
.

The full Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle G_{M,\lambda}=(g_{0}-\bm{M}\cdot\bm{\sigma}-\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\gamma_{i}({\bm{k}})\sigma^{\alpha})^{-1}.

(106)

with
g_{0}=i\varepsilon_{m}+\mu-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}
.
We expand it up to the first order in
\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}
,

G_{M,\lambda}\simeq G_{M,0}+\lambda_{i}^{\eta}\gamma_{i}({\bm{k}})G_{M,0}\sigma^{\eta}G_{M,0}
,
where

\displaystyle G_{M,0}=\frac{g_{0}+\bm{M}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}{D_{M,0}},

(107)

is
G_{M,\lambda}
at
\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}=0
, with
D_{M,0}=g_{0}^{2}-M^{2}
.

C.1
DMI coefficient

The DMI coefficient (
15
) at the first-order in
\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}
is then

\displaystyle D_{i}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-i\lambda_{j}^{\eta}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\int^{M}_{0}MdM\,T\sum_{n,{\bm{k}}}v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\times{\rm tr}\left[\sigma^{\beta}(G_{M,0})^{2}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{M,0}\sigma^{\eta}G_{M,0}\right],

(108)

where we used
\partial G_{M,0}/\partial k_{i}=v_{i}^{0}(G_{M,0})^{2}

with
v_{i}^{0}=\partial\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}/\partial k_{i}
.
The spin trace is taken as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}{\rm tr}\left[\sigma^{\beta}(G_{M,0})^{2}\sigma^{\gamma}G_{M,0}\sigma^{\eta}G_{M,0}\right]

\displaystyle=4i\frac{(g_{0}^{2}+M^{2})\delta_{\alpha\eta}-2M^{\alpha}M^{\eta}}{D_{M,0}^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2i}{M}\frac{d}{dM}\biggl\{\frac{M^{2}(\delta_{\alpha\eta}-n^{\alpha}n^{\eta})}{D_{M,0}^{2}}+\frac{n^{\alpha}n^{\eta}}{D_{M,0}}\biggr\}.

(109)

Since the
{\bm{M}}
-parallel component of
{\bm{D}}_{i}
does not contribute to the DM free energy,
one may drop the second term (
\sim n^{\alpha}n^{\eta}
) and thereby define
{\bm{D}}_{i}^{\perp}
.
The
M
-integration can be done analytically,

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=2\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\perp}T\sum_{n,{\bm{k}}}v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})\int^{M}_{0}dM\frac{d}{dM}\frac{M^{2}}{D_{M,0}^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\perp}T\sum_{n,{\bm{k}}}v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})\frac{M^{2}}{D_{M,0}^{2}}

(110)

\displaystyle=-\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\perp}T\sum_{n,{\bm{k}}}v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})\left[\frac{g_{0}^{2}-M^{2}}{D_{M,0}^{2}}-\frac{g_{0}^{2}+M^{2}}{D_{M,0}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\perp}T\sum_{n,{\bm{k}}}\left[\frac{v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})}{D_{M,0}}+(\partial_{i}\gamma_{j})\frac{g_{0}}{D_{M,0}}\right],

(111)

where

{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}^{\perp}\equiv{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}-({\bm{\lambda}}_{i}\cdot{\bm{n}})\,{\bm{n}}

is the component perpendicular to
{\bm{M}}
.
In the last equality, we noted

\displaystyle\frac{g_{0}^{2}+M^{2}}{D_{M,0}^{2}}v_{i}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\frac{g_{0}}{D_{M,0}},

(112)

and made an integration by parts.

As to the parallel component of
{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2)}
, Eq. (
109
) leads to

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(2),\parallel}

\displaystyle={\bm{\lambda}}_{j}^{\parallel}M\frac{d}{dM}T\sum_{n,{\bm{k}}}\frac{v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})}{D_{M,0}}

\displaystyle=2{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}^{\parallel}M^{2}T\sum_{n,{\bm{k}}}\frac{v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})}{D_{M,0}^{2}}.

(113)

This is consistent with the analysis summarized in Table I.

C.2
Equilibrium spin-current density

Next, we express the equilibrium spin current in terms of Green’s functions.
The spin current is expressed as

\displaystyle j_{s,i}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\bm{k}}\langle c_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(v_{i}\sigma^{\alpha}+\sigma^{\alpha}v_{i})c_{\bm{k}}\rangle,

(114)

where
v_{i}=v_{i}^{0}+\lambda_{j}^{\beta}(\partial_{i}\gamma_{j})\,\sigma^{\beta}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle j_{s,i}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}T\sum_{n,\bm{k}}v_{i}^{0}\mathrm{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}G_{M,\lambda}\right]+\frac{1}{2}T\sum_{n,\bm{k}}\lambda^{\alpha}_{j}(\partial_{i}\gamma_{j})\mathrm{tr}\left[G_{M,\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{j}^{\beta}T\sum_{n,\bm{k}}v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})\mathrm{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}G_{M,0}\sigma^{\beta}G_{M,0}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}T\sum_{n,\bm{k}}(\partial_{i}\gamma_{j})\mathrm{tr}\left[G_{M,0}\right].

(115)

In the second line, we retained only the first-order terms in
\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}
.
The spin trace is calculated as

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}G_{M,0}\sigma^{\beta}G_{M,0}\right]=\frac{2(g_{0}^{2}-M^{2})\delta_{\alpha\beta}+4M^{\alpha}M^{\beta}}{D_{M,0}^{2}}.

(116)

We first note that the parallel component vanishes,

\displaystyle{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{j}}_{s,i}

\displaystyle=\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\parallel}T\sum_{n,\bm{k}}\left[v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})\frac{g_{0}^{2}+M^{2}}{D_{M,0}^{2}}+(\partial_{i}\gamma_{j})\frac{g_{0}}{D_{M,0}}\right]

\displaystyle=0,

(117)

because of Eq. (
112
).
Thus, the spin current consists purely of the perpendicular component,

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{s,i}

\displaystyle=\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\perp}T\sum_{n,\bm{k}}\left[\frac{v_{i}^{0}\gamma_{j}({\bm{k}})}{D_{M,0}}+(\partial_{i}\gamma_{j})\frac{g_{0}}{D_{M,0}}\right].

(118)

Comparing this with Eq. (
111
), we see that

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{i}^{\perp}=-{\bm{j}}_{s,i},

(119)

confirming that our general formula reduces to the spin-current formula of Ref.
Kikuchi2016

at weak SOC.
We emphasize that this holds even when
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}
and
\gamma_{i}({\bm{k}})

are arbitrary functions of
{\bm{k}}
,
which was not obvious from the derivation in Ref.
Kikuchi2016
.

Appendix D
Generalized spin-current formula

In this Appendix, we show that the spin current formula for the DMI coefficient holds
more generally, irrespective of the strength of SOC,
if the magnetization vector is perpendicular to the spin-orbit field,

{\bm{M}}\perp{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}
.
The derivation is based on the same model as in Appendix C, namely, Eq. (
105
),
but the SOC is fully taken into account.
The spin current operator is given by Eq. (
114
),
and the equilibrium spin current is expressed as

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}

\displaystyle=-T\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\partial_{i}g_{0}){\bm{g}}_{M}-g_{0}(\partial_{i}{\bm{g}}_{M})}{D_{M}}.

(130)

Derivation

We first recall that the DMI coefficient is given by Eq. (

), or its

(131)

Assuming
{\bm{M}}
is orthogonal to
{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}
, we focus on the perpendicular component,

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{i}^{\perp}=\int_{0}^{M}2MdMT\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\partial_{i}g_{0}){\bm{g}}_{\perp}-g_{0}(\partial_{i}{\bm{g}}_{\perp})}{(g_{0}^{2}-{\bm{g}}_{\perp}^{2}-M^{2})^{2}},

(132)

where
{\bm{g}}_{\perp}={\bm{g}}_{M}-{\bm{M}}={\bm{\lambda}}_{i}\gamma_{i}({\bm{k}})
,
and we noted
{\bm{g}}_{M}^{2}={\bm{g}}_{\perp}^{2}+M^{2}

Since
M

and
{\bm{g}}_{\perp}

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{M^{2}}dM^{2}\,T\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\partial_{i}g_{0}){\bm{g}}_{\perp}-g_{0}(\partial_{i}{\bm{g}}_{\perp})}{(g_{0}^{2}-{\bm{g}}_{\perp}^{2}-M^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\partial_{i}g_{0}){\bm{g}}_{\perp}-g_{0}(\partial_{i}{\bm{g}}_{\perp})}{g_{0}^{2}-{\bm{g}}_{\perp}^{2}-M^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-T\sum_{m}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\partial_{i}g_{0}){\bm{g}}_{\perp}-g_{0}(\partial_{i}{\bm{g}}_{\perp})}{g_{0}^{2}-{\bm{g}}_{\perp}^{2}}.

(133)

The resulting quantity is identified as the perpendicular component of the equilibrium spin current

in Eq. (

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=-\{{\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}^{\perp}(M)-{\bm{j}}_{{\rm s},i}^{\perp}(0)\}.

(134)

D.2

Let us consider the Rashba ferromagnet studied in Sec.

This can be calculated analytically as follows,

\displaystyle 1)\

\displaystyle\mu>M:

\displaystyle\qquad j_{{\rm s},i}^{\perp,\alpha}(M)=\varepsilon_{\alpha i}\frac{m^{2}\alpha_{\rm R}^{3}}{6\pi},

(136)

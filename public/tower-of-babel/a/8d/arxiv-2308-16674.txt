A characterization of invariant subspaces for isometric representations of product system over ℕ 0 k \mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
Dimple Saini *, Harsh Trivedi, Shankar Veerabathiran

Abstract
. Using the Wold-von Neumann decomposition for the isometric covariant representations due to Muhly and Solel, we prove an explicit representation of the commutant of a doubly commuting pure isometric representation of the product system over ℕ 0 k . \mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}. As an application we study a complete characterization of invariant subspaces for a doubly commuting pure isometric representation of the product system. This provides us a complete set of isomorphic invariants. Finally, we classify a large class of an isometric covariant representations of the product system.

46L08, 47A15, 47A80, 47B38, 47L55.

1.
Introduction

Beurling’s theorem
[
8
]
, is a classical application of the Wold-von Neumann decomposition
[
24
,
40
]
, says that: Let
\mathcal{K}
be a closed subspace of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
over unit disc
\mathbb{D},
and let
\mathcal{K}
be an invariant subspace for multiplication operator
M_{z}
then
\mathcal{K}=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for some inner function
\theta
in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}).

In Multivariable Operator Theory, the corresponding question is that whether or not we can prove Beurling-type characterization for all invariant subspaces of the shift
(M_{z_{1}},\dots,M_{z_{n}})
on the Hardy space over polydisc
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).
It has been first explained by Rudin
[
31
]
where it is pointed out that the Beurling theorem does not hold in this case. For
n>1,
the parallel problem of the study of invariant subspaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is therefore interesting and there is pioneering work by Agrawal-Clark-Douglas
[
1
]
, Ahern-Clark
[
2
]
, Guo
[
13
]
, Izuchi
[
14
]
and by several other authors mentioned in the references of these papers.

Using the doubly commuting condition to characterize the existence of the wandering subspace for commuting tuple of isometries, Słociński described a Wold-type decomposition for doubly commuting isometries in
[
36
]
. Mandrekar
[
20
]
used the Słociński’s Wold-type decomposition and discussed a Beurling-type characterization for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
under the condition that the tuple
(M_{z_{1}},M_{z_{2}})
is doubly commuting.
Based on a generalization of the Słociński’s decomposition due to Sarkar
[
33
]
,
Sarkar-Sasane-Wick in
[
34
]
worked out the Mandrekar’s result mentioned above for the polydisc case
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
Berger-Coburn-Lebow decomposition
[
7
]
was revisited by Maji-Sarkar-Sankar in
[
19
]
and the explicit decomposition in this paper is based on a representation theorem for commutators of shifts. On the other hand using the explicit representation theorem for commutators, a large class of
n
-tuples of commuting isometries is classified by Maji-Mundayadan-Sarkar-Sankar in
[
17
]
where the following Beurling-Lax-Halmos type theorem is provided:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space,
\mathcal{K}
be a closed subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}(\mathbb{D}),
where
\mathcal{E}_{n}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{E}
and let
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{K}\ominus z\mathcal{K}.
Then
\mathcal{K}
is invariant for
(M_{z},M_{\kappa_{1}},\dots,M_{\kappa_{n}})
if and only if there exist an inner operator
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{B(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{E}_{n})}(\mathbb{D})
and
(M_{\Phi_{1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{n}})
is an
n
-tuple of commuting shifts on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\mathcal{K}=\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D})\quad and\quad\kappa_{i}\Theta=\Theta\Phi_{i},

where
\Phi_{i}(w)=P_{\mathcal{W}}(I_{\mathcal{K}}-wP_{\mathcal{K}}M_{z}^{*})^{-1}M_{\kappa_{i}}|_{\mathcal{W}}
for
i\in I_{n}
and
w\in\mathbb{D}.

Along this direction, Maji-Sankar studied mixed invariant doubly commuting subspaces in the polydisc case in
[
18
]
.

Cuntz
[
9
]
studied a
C^{*}
-algebra, known as Cuntz algebra, generated by row isometries, that is, the tuple
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
of isometries with orthogonal range. Frazho
[
12
]
proposed the Wold decomposition for two isometries with the orthogonal range. Popescu
[
26
]
extended this decomposition to the case of an infinite sequence of isometries with orthogonal final spaces and extended the Beurling-Lax-Halmos theorem in
[
27
]
, where Beurling-Lax-Halmos type multiplier/representer
\Theta
in the noncommutative setup is discussed and it is defined on a Fock space. In this case, the map
\Theta
intertwines corresponding shifts on the respective Fock spaces and it is known as a
multi-analytic
operator. The monograph by Ball-Bolotnikov
[
5
]
discussed transfer-function realization-type formulas for the Beurling-Lax-Halmos representer using Noncommutative Function-theoretic Operator Theory. Popescu considered Operator Theory on noncommutative domains and studied weighted Beurling-Lax-Halmos type representer in monograph
[
28
]
. Recently Ball-Bolotnikov
[
6
]
studied the representer
\Theta
for free noncommutative multivariable setting. In
[
10
]
, Das-Pradhan-Sarkar developed the following theorem for doubly noncommuting shifts which is a generalization of Theorem
1.1
using the Fock space approach:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
{\bf S}=({\bf S}_{n_{1}},\dots,{\bf S}_{n_{k}})
to be the
k
-tuple of tuples of noncommuting pure isometries, where
{\bf S}_{n_{i}}=({\bf S}_{i1},\dots,{\bf S}_{in_{i}})
such that
{\bf S}_{ij}{\bf S}_{pq}={\bf S}_{pq}{\bf S}_{ij}
and
{\bf S}_{ij}^{*}{\bf S}_{pq}={\bf S}_{pq}{\bf S}_{ij}^{*}
for all
1\leq i<p\leq k,j=1,\dots,n_{i}
and
q=1,\dots,n_{p}.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a closed subspace of the Fock
{\bf n}
-module
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\bf n}
for
{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0},

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{M}\ominus\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}{\bf S}_{1j}\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\bf n}=\mathcal{F}^{2}_{n_{2}}\otimes\dots\mathcal{F}^{2}_{n_{k}}.
Then
\mathcal{M}
is submodule of
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\bf n}
if and only if there
exist multi-analytic operators
\Phi_{ij}\in R^{\infty}_{n_{1}}\overline{\otimes}B(\mathcal{E}),

j=1,\dots,n_{i}
and
i=2,\dots,k,
and an inner multi-analytic operator
\Theta\in R^{\infty}_{n_{1}}\overline{\otimes}B(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{\bf n})
such that

\mathcal{M}=\Theta(\mathcal{F}^{2}_{n_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{E})\quad and\quad{\bf S}_{ij}\Theta=\Theta\Phi_{ij},

where the Fourier coefficients of
\Phi_{ij}
are given by
\phi_{ij,\alpha^{t}}=P_{\mathcal{E}}(S^{\alpha^{*}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}_{\bf n}}){\bf S}_{ij}|_{\mathcal{E}}.

Operator theory and its inseparable counterpart, function theory, are well-established disciplines with a long history of overwhelming connections with other subjects. The overarching
field, in keeping with traditional questions, is undergoing continuous development while also
witnessing the generalizations of classical concepts in various settings. One of the generalized
ideas of classical operator and function theory is the notion of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules in terms
of
C^{*}
-correspondences and covariant representation. The paper is along this
line.

Pimsner in
[
25
]
extended the construction of Cuntz-Kreiger algebras using isometric covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences and the construction is in terms of Fock modules. The setup of isometric covariant representations provides a general framework to study row isometries using the tensor product notion of
C^{*}
-correspondences. Muhly-Solel derived an analogue of Popescu’s Wold decomposition for a row isometry known as the Wold decomposition for an isometric covariant representation in
[
22
]
which is based on Fock module approach. In
[
39
]
the Beurling-Lax-Halmos type theorem in the case of the covariant representations of a
C^{*}
-correspondence is discussed where the Beurling-Lax-Halmos type multiplier
\Theta
is defined on the Fock module as a multi-analytic operator.

In
[
3
]
, Arveson investigated the idea of a tensor product system of Hilbert spaces to categorize
E_{0}
-semigroups. Fowler
[
11
]
introduced the notion of Fock modules. Solel
[
37
]
studied the doubly commuting notion of covariant representations of product system of
C^{*}
-correspondences and discussed the theory of regular dilations. The setting of doubly commuting isometric covariant representation is a generalization of
\Lambda
-doubly commuting row isometries considered by Popescu
[
29
]
(see also
[
15
]
for a related doubly noncommuting setup). For the doubly commuting isometric representations, Skalski-Zacharias generalized Słociński’s Wold-type decomposition. Recently, Berger-Coburn-Lebow representation for pure isometric covariant representations of product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
was studied in
[
32
]
and this explicit representation is based on the joint multi-analytic representation theorem for commutators of the induced representation of a product system. As an application of this joint multi-analytic description, the aim of this study is to find a Beurling-Lax-Halmos type theorem for pure doubly commuting isometric representations of a product system and to characterize the invariant subspaces as in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

This paper concerns the issue of the delicate structure of the
invariant subspaces of the Hardy space over the polydisc, but in the setting of correspondences. Here we followed the theme of
[
17
]
and obtained analgous results in
the setting of correspondences. The plan of the paper is as follows: In Section 2, a fundamental commutant theorem for a doubly commuting pure isometric representation of the product system is proved. Section 3, presents a complete characterization of invariant subspaces for a doubly commuting pure isometric representation of a product system, as outlined in
[
17
]
. In Section 4, an application to nested invariant subspaces of Fock module and a complete set of isomorphic invariants of the corresponding invariant subspaces is proved.

1.1.
Preliminaries for Beurling-Lax-Halmos type theorem

In this paper,
E
always a
C^{*}
-correspondence
over a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
with the left module action given by a non zero
*
-homomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}(E)
in the following sense:
b\xi:=\phi(b)\xi,\>b\in\mathcal{B},\xi\in E,

and
\mathcal{L}(E)
denotes the
C^{*}
-algebra of all adjointable operators on
E.
Assume all such
*
-homomorphisms in this article are nondegenerate, that is, the closed linear span of
\phi(\mathcal{B})E
equals
E.
Suppose
F
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{B},
then we have the notion of tensor product
F\otimes_{\phi}E
(cf.
[
16
]
) that satisfies

\langle\eta_{1}\otimes\xi_{1},\eta_{2}\otimes\xi_{2}\rangle=\langle\xi_{1},\phi(\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle)\xi_{2}\rangle,\>\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in F,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in E.

By
B(\mathcal{H})
we denote the algebra of all bounded linear maps acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
V:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a linear map and
\sigma:\mathcal{B}\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a representation.
The pair
(\sigma,V)
is said to be a
completely bounded covariant representation
(simply say,
c.b.c. representation
) (cf.
[
21
]
) of
E
acting on
\mathcal{H}
if
V
is completely bounded as a linear map on
E
(endowed with the usual operator space structure by viewing as a left corner in the linking algebra
\mathcal{L}_{E}
of
E
) and

V(b\xi c)=\sigma(b)V(\xi)\sigma(c)\quad for\quad\xi\in E,b,c\in\mathcal{B}.

Moreover,
(\sigma,V)
is called
isometric
if

V(\xi_{1})^{*}V(\xi_{2})=\sigma(\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle)\quad for\quad\quad\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in E.

Define a bounded linear map
\widetilde{V}:E\otimes\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by
\widetilde{V}(\xi\otimes h)=V(\xi)h,\xi\in E,h\in\mathcal{H},
then
\widetilde{V}
satisfies
\widetilde{V}(\phi(b)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\sigma(b)\widetilde{V}
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
. From
[
21
, Lemma 3.5]
,
(\sigma,V)
is a c.b.c. representation (resp.
isometric representation
) if and only if
\widetilde{V}
is a bounded operator (resp. isometry).

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a closed subspace of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
We say that
\mathcal{N}
is
(\sigma,V)
-
invariant
(resp.
(\sigma,V)
-
reducing
) (cf.
[
35
]
) if it (resp. both
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
) is invariant by every operator
V(\xi)
for all
\xi\in E
and
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
-invariant. The restriction of this representation provides a new representation
(\sigma,V)|_{\mathcal{N}}
of
E
on
\mathcal{N}.

For
k\in\mathbb{N},
define
\widetilde{V}_{k}:E^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by

\widetilde{V}_{k}(\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{k}\otimes h)=V(\xi_{1})\dots V(\xi_{k})h,

for
\xi_{i}\in E,h\in\mathcal{H}.

The
Fock space
of
E
(cf.
[
11
]
),
\mathcal{F}(E)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}E^{\otimes n},
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{B},
with the left module action of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
is defined by

\phi_{\infty}(b)\left(\oplus_{n\geq 0}\xi_{n}\right)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}b\xi_{n},\>\>\xi_{n}\in E^{\otimes n}.

For
\xi\in E,
the
creation operator

V_{\xi}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
is defined by
V_{\xi}(\eta)=\xi\otimes\eta,\>\>\eta\in E^{\otimes n},n\geq 0.
Let
\pi
be a representation of
\mathcal{B}
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
. Define an isometric representation
(\rho,S)
of
E
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
by

\displaystyle\rho(b)=\phi_{\infty}(b)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\quad and\quad S(\xi)=V_{\xi}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}},\>\>\xi\in E,b\in\mathcal{B},

then we say that such a representation
(\rho,S)
is referred to as the
induced representation
(cf.
[
30
]
) induced by
\pi
.

Suppose that
(\sigma,V)
is an isometric representation of
E
acting on
\mathcal{H}.
By Wold-von Neumann decomposition theorem for
(\sigma,V),
due to Muhly and Solel
[
22
]
, there exists a unitary
\Pi_{V}:\mathcal{H}(=\mathcal{H}_{1}{\oplus}\mathcal{H}_{2})\to(\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{W})\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{1}(b)&0\\
0&\sigma_{2}(b)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho(b)&0\\
0&\sigma_{2}(b)\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{V}

and

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}\begin{bmatrix}V_{1}(\xi)&0\\
0&V_{2}(\xi)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}S(\xi)&0\\
0&V_{2}(\xi)\\
\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{V},

where
b\in\mathcal{B},\xi\in E,

\mathcal{H}_{1}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}),

\mathcal{H}_{2}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{W}
is a wandering subspace for
(\sigma,V)
(that is,
\mathcal{W}\perp\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
). Moreover,
(\sigma,V)
is pure (that is, SOT-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{V}_{n}\widetilde{V}^{*}_{n}=0
), then
\mathcal{H}_{2}
=
\{0\}
and we have

\Pi_{V}\sigma(b)=\rho(b)\Pi_{V}\quad and\quad\Pi_{V}V(\xi)=S(\xi)\Pi_{V}.

Then
\Pi_{V}
is called
Wold-von Neumann decomposition
for the pure isometric representation
(\sigma,V)
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a wandering subspace for
(\sigma,V)
of
E
acting on
\mathcal{H}.
We say that
\mathcal{W}
is
generating wandering subspace

if

\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}).

The following Beurling-Lax-Halmos type theorem is from
[
39
]
(also see
[
22
]
):

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(\sigma,V)
be a pure isometric representation of
E
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{M}
be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}.
Then
\mathcal{M}
is invariant for
(\sigma,V)
if and only if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K},
an inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{K}}\to\mathcal{H}
and a representation
\sigma_{1}
of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{K}
such that

\mathcal{M}=M_{\Psi}({\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{K}}),

where
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{K}}
is the generating wandering subspace for an induced representation
(\rho,S)
of
E
induced by
\sigma_{1}.

2.
A fundamental commutant theorem for a doubly commuting pure isometric representation of the product system

In this section, first we recall the important notion of joint multi-analytic completely bounded multiplier which we introduced in
[
32
]
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N},
we denote
I_{k}:=\{1,\dots,k\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.
A product system
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}
is defined by a family of
C^{*}
-correspondence
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{k}\}
, along with unitary isomorphisms
t_{j,i}:E_{j}\otimes E_{i}\to E_{i}\otimes E_{j}
(
j>i
). And define
t_{i,i}={\textrm{id}}_{E_{i}\otimes E_{i}}
,
t_{j,i}=t_{i,j}^{-1}
for
j<i.
Then, we identify the
C^{*}
-correspondence
\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}}),
for all

{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}
with
E_{1}^{\otimes^{n_{1}}}\otimes\dots\otimes E_{k}^{\otimes^{n_{k}}}
(see
[
11
,
22
,
39
,
4
]
).
First, we start with the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

The tuple
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
is called
completely bounded, covariant representation

(
simply say,
c.b.c. representation
)
(cf.
[
21
]
) of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
if each
(\sigma,V^{(i)}),i\in I_{k}
is a c.b.c. representation of
E_{i}
on
\mathcal{H}

and it satisfy the commutative relation

(2.1)

\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})=\widetilde{V}^{(j)}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})(t_{i,j}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{H}}}),\quad\quad i,j\in I_{k}.

Moreover,
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
is
isometric (respectively, fully co-isometric)
if each
(\sigma,V^{(i)}),i\in I_{k}
is isometric (respectively, fully co-isometric).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
be a c.b.c. representation of
{\mathbb{E}}
over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Let
\mathcal{K}
be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}.
We say that
\mathcal{K}
is
invariant
for
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
if it is
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
-invariant for all
i\in I_{k}.

The
Fock module
of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0},

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})=\bigoplus_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}}\mathbb{E}({\bf n}),
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{B},
with the left action
\phi_{\infty}
given by

\phi_{\infty}(b)(\bigoplus_{{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\xi_{\bf{n}})=\bigoplus_{{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}b\xi_{\bf{n}},\>b\in\mathcal{B},\xi_{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{E}(\bf{n}).

For
{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k},

define a map
V_{\bf{n}}:\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}})\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H})
by

V_{\bf{n}}(\xi_{\bf{n}})h=\widetilde{V}_{\bf{n}}(\xi_{\bf{n}}\otimes h)\quad\quad h\in\mathcal{H},\xi_{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}}),

where
\widetilde{V}_{\bf{n}}:\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}})\otimes\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is given by

\widetilde{V}_{\bf{n}}=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}_{n_{1}}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}_{n_{2}})\dots(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes E_{k-1}^{\otimes n_{k-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(k)}_{n_{k}}).

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a Hilbert space and
\pi
be a representation of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{K}.
For
i\in I_{k},
define an isometric representation
(\rho,S^{(i)})
of a
C^{*}
-correspondence
E_{i}
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
by

\displaystyle\rho(b)=\phi_{\infty}(b)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\quad and\quad S^{(i)}(\xi_{i})=V_{{\xi}_{i}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}},\>\>\>b\in\mathcal{B}\>,\xi_{i}\in E_{i},

where
V_{\xi_{i}}
is the
creation operator
determined by
\xi_{i}
on
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E}).

We say that
(\rho,S^{(1)},\dots,S^{(k)})
is an
induced representation
(cf.
[
30
]
) of
\mathbb{E}
induced by
\pi.

Two such representations
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
of
\mathbb{E}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K},
respectively, are
isomorphic
if there exists a unitary
U:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
such that
\psi(b)U=U\sigma(b)
and
T^{(i)}(\xi_{i})U=UV^{(i)}(\xi_{i})
for all
\xi_{i}\in E_{i},b\in\mathcal{B},i\in I_{k}.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a Hilbert space,
\pi
be a representation of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathbb{E}
be a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k+1},k\geq 1
. Let
\Theta:E_{k+1}\to B(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})
be a completely bounded bi-module map, that is,
\Theta
is completely bounded and
\Theta(a\xi b)=\rho(a)\Theta(\xi)\rho(b),
where
\xi\in E_{k+1},a,b\in\mathcal{B}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}
is a product system determined by the
C^{*}
-correspondences
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{k}\}.
Define a bounded linear map
\widetilde{\Theta}:E_{k+1}\otimes\mathcal{K}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
by
\widetilde{\Theta}(\xi\otimes h)=\Theta(\xi)h.

Since
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{S}_{\bf n}(\mathbb{E}({\bf n})\otimes\mathcal{K}),
we define a corresponding completely bounded map
M_{\Theta}:E_{k+1}\longrightarrow B(\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})
by

\displaystyle M_{\Theta}(\xi)(S_{\bf n}(\xi_{\bf n})h)=\widetilde{S}_{\bf n}(I_{\mathbb{E}({\bf n})}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{k+1,{\bf n}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(\xi\otimes\xi_{\bf n}\otimes h),

where
\xi\in E_{k+1},\xi_{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{E}({\bf n})},h\in\mathcal{K},{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k},

(\rho,S^{(1)},\dots,S^{(k)})
is an induced representation of
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}
induced by
\pi,

\displaystyle V(\xi)S_{\bf n}(\xi_{\bf n})h

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{E}

Suppose that

{\mathbb{E}}_{k}
over

on
\mathcal{H}
and
\Pi_{V}
is the Wold-von Neumann decomposition of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
with the wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
(\sigma,V^{(k+1)})
be a c.b.c. representation of
E_{k+1}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Define a c.b.c. representation
(\rho,T)
of
E_{k+1}
on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}
by

T(\xi)=\Pi_{V}V^{(k+1)}(\xi)\Pi_{V}^{*},

where
\pi=\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}},\rho
is defined as above and
\xi\in E_{k+1}.

Then
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
is a c.b.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
if and only if there exists a completely bounded bi-module map
\Theta:E_{k+1}\rightarrow{B}(\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}})
such that

T=M_{\Theta}.

Moreover,
\Theta(\xi)=\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{S}_{\bf n}(I_{\mathbb{E}({\bf n})}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}})\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}^{*}V^{(k+1)}(\xi)|_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}},~\xi\in E_{k+1}
and
P_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}}
is an orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Proof.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
be a c.b.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Since
\Pi_{V}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}=\widetilde{S}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{V}),i\in I_{k}
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}(I_{E_{k+1}}\otimes\widetilde{S}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V}\widetilde{V}^{(k+1)}(I_{E_{k+1}}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V}\widetilde{S}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V}\widetilde{V}^{(k+1)}(I_{E_{k+1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V}))

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(k+1)})(t_{k+1,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{k+1}\otimes E_{i}}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{V})(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(k+1)})(t_{k+1,i}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{T})(t_{k+1,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}}).

(\rho,S^{(1)},\dots,S^{(k)},T)
is a c.b.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}
. By Lemma
2.4
, there exists a completely bounded bi-module map
\Theta:E_{k+1}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{E}}_{k})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}})
such that
T=M_{\Theta}.
Since each
(\sigma,V^{(i)}),i\in I_{k}
is pure and from Equation (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}(I_{\mathbb{E}({\bf n})}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}})\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}(I_{\mathbb{E}({\bf n})}\otimes(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*}))\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}}.

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}V^{(k+1)}(\xi)w

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V}\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}(I_{\mathbb{E}({\bf n})}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}})\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}^{*}V^{(k+1)}(\xi)w

Hence for

\Theta(\xi)=\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{S}_{\bf n}(I_{\mathbb{E}({\bf n})}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}})\widetilde{V}_{\bf n}^{*}V^{(k+1)}(\xi).

Conversely, suppose that

\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.

In this section, we characterize invariant subspaces for doubly commuting pure isometric representation of a product system

(

Now we recall the following definitions and conclusions are from

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
be two c.b.c. representations of
\mathbb{E}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K},
respectively. A bounded linear map
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
is said to be
multi-analytic
if

(3.1)

B\sigma(b)h=\psi(b)Bh\quad and\quad BV^{(i)}(\xi_{i})h=T^{(i)}(\xi_{i})Bh,

for
h\in\mathcal{H},\>\xi_{i}\in E_{i},b\in\mathcal{B}
and
i\in I_{k}.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
are isometric representations of
\mathbb{E}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K},
respectively. Further, assume that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
is pure and doubly commuting, then

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}(E(\mathbf{n})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}),

where
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}
is the generating wandering subspace for
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)}).

Let
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
be a bounded operator that satisfies the intertwining relation (
3.1
), then
B
is determined uniquely by the operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathcal{K}
such that
\Psi\sigma(b)h=\psi(b)\Psi h
for all
b\in\mathcal{B},h\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}},
where
\Psi:=B|_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}}.
This happens because for every
h\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\xi_{\mathbf{n}}\in E(\mathbf{n}),
we have
BV_{\mathbf{n}}(\xi_{\mathbf{n}})h=T_{\mathbf{n}}(\xi_{\mathbf{n}})\Psi h,
where
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}(E(\mathbf{n})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}).

(2)

On the other hand, if we start a bounded operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathcal{K}(=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{T}_{\mathbf{n}}(E(\mathbf{n})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{K}}))
such that
\Psi\sigma(b)h=\psi(b)\Psi h,h\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}.
Since
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}(E(\mathbf{n})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}),
define a bounded operator
M_{\Psi}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
by

M_{\Psi}V_{\mathbf{n}}(\xi_{\mathbf{n}})h=T_{\mathbf{n}}(\xi_{\mathbf{n}})\Psi h,\>\>\>\xi_{\mathbf{n}}\in E(\mathbf{n}),h\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Observe that
M_{\Psi}
is the multi-analytic operator for the representations
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}).
Moreover,

M_{\Psi}\left(\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}}h_{\mathbf{n}}\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}}\widetilde{T}_{\mathbf{n}}(I_{E(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Psi)\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}h_{n},\hskip 28.45274pt\>\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}}h_{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathcal{H}.

(3)

An operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathcal{K}
satisfying
\Psi\sigma(b)h=\psi(b)\Psi h
for all
b\in\mathcal{B},h\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}
is called
inner
if
M_{\Psi}
is an isometry.

(4)

\Psi
is inner if and only if
\Psi
is an isometry and
\Psi(\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}})
is the wandering subspace for
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}),
that means,
\Psi(\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}})\perp\widetilde{T}_{\bf n}(\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}})\otimes\Psi(\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}}))
for all
\bf n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}\setminus\{\bf 0\}.

4.
Applications

In this section, we study some useful results about the structure of invariant subspaces and the characterization of nested invariant subspaces for doubly commuting pure isometric representation of the product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
. In addition, we also verify our important results for the invariant subspaces that are isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}.

First, we discuss the characterization of nested invariant subspaces for pure doubly commuting isometric representation: Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
be a doubly commuting pure isometric representation of
\mathbb{E}
acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
By Theorem
3.3
,
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
is isomorphic to a covariant representation
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
of
{\mathbb{E}}
on
\mathcal{F}({{E}_{1}})\otimes\mathcal{K},
where
(\rho,S)
is the induced representaion of
E_{1}
on
\mathcal{F}({{E}_{1}})\otimes\mathcal{K}.
Therefore, considering such a representation
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}}),
it is sufficient to analyze the nested invariant subspaces.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
be invariant subspaces for
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}}),
by Theorem
3.5
, there exist an inner operators
{\Psi}_{1}:\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\mathcal{K}
(for the representations
(\varrho^{\prime}_{1},S^{\mathcal{W}_{1}},M_{\Phi_{1,1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k,1}})
and
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
) and
{\Psi}_{2}:\mathcal{W}_{2}\to\mathcal{K}
(for the representations
(\varrho^{\prime}_{2},S^{\mathcal{W}_{2}},M_{\Phi_{1,2}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k,2}})
and
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
) such that

\mathcal{M}_{1}=M_{\Psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})\quad and\quad\mathcal{M}_{2}=M_{\Psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}),

where
\Phi_{i,j}(\xi_{i})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}_{j}}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{j}}P_{\mathcal{M}_{j}})\widetilde{S}_{n}^{*}M_{\Theta_{i}}(\xi_{i})|_{\mathcal{W}_{j}},~\xi_{i}\in E_{i+1},i\in I_{k},j\in I_{2}
and
\mathcal{W}_{j}={\mathcal{M}_{j}}\ominus\widetilde{S}(E_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{M}_{j}}).
Define a bounded operator
\Pi:\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}
by

\displaystyle\Pi=M_{\Psi_{2}}^{*}M_{\Psi_{1}}.

From Equations (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) for
M_{\Psi_{1}}
and
M_{\Psi_{2}},
we get

\Pi\varrho^{\prime}_{1}(b)=\varrho^{\prime}_{2}(b)\Pi,\quad\Pi\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}_{1}}=\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}_{2}}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi)

and

\Pi\widetilde{M}_{\Phi_{i,1}}=\widetilde{M}_{\Phi_{i,2}}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes\Pi),\quad\quad i\in I_{k}.

This shows that
\Pi
is multi-analytic for the representations
(\varrho^{\prime}_{1},S^{\mathcal{W}_{1}},M_{\Phi_{1,1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k,1}})
and
(\varrho^{\prime}_{2},S^{\mathcal{W}_{2}},M_{\Phi_{1,2}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k,2}}).

Furthermore, let’s assume that
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{2},
then
\Pi
is an isometry and
M_{\Psi_{1}}=M_{\Psi_{2}}\Pi,
and therefore there exists an inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\mathcal{W}_{2}
such that
\Pi=M_{\Psi}.
Note that
M_{\Psi_{2}}=\sum_{{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\Psi_{2})\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}_{2}^{*}}
and
\widetilde{S}_{n}^{*}|_{\mathcal{K}}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N},
we get
M_{\Psi_{2}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{K}}={\Psi_{2}}^{*}.
Hence

\Psi={M_{\Psi}}|_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}=M_{\Psi_{2}}^{*}M_{\Psi_{1}}|_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}={\Psi_{2}}^{*}{\Psi_{1}}.
On the other hand, suppose that
M_{\Psi_{1}}=M_{\Psi_{2}}M_{\Psi}
and consider the subspaces
\mathcal{M}_{1}=M_{\Psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}=M_{\Psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}),
thus it seems obviously that
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{2}.
This observation leads to the following theorem which characterize the nested invariant subspaces.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
be two invariant subspaces for
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
of
{\mathbb{E}}
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
and
\Psi_{j}:\mathcal{W}_{j}\to\mathcal{K},j\in I_{2}
be an inner operators (for the representations
(\varrho^{\prime}_{j},S^{\mathcal{W}_{j}},M_{\Phi_{1,j}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k,j}})
and
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
) such that
\mathcal{M}_{j}=M_{\Psi}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}),
where
\mathcal{W}_{j}={\mathcal{M}_{j}}\ominus\widetilde{S}(E_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{M}_{j}}).
Then
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{2}
if and only if there exists an inner operator
{\Psi}:\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\mathcal{W}_{2}
(for the representations
(\varrho^{\prime}_{1},S^{\mathcal{W}_{1}},M_{\Phi_{1,1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k,1}})
and
(\varrho^{\prime}_{2},S^{\mathcal{W}_{2}},M_{\Phi_{1,2}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k,2}})
) such that

M_{\Psi_{1}}=M_{\Psi_{2}}M_{\Psi},

where
\Phi_{i,j}(\xi_{i})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}_{j}}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{j}}P_{\mathcal{M}_{j}})\widetilde{S}_{n}^{*}M_{\Theta_{i}}(\xi_{i})|_{\mathcal{W}_{j}},~\xi_{i}\in E_{i+1},i\in I_{k},j\in I_{2}.

Now we discuss the structure of the invariant subspaces for pure doubly commuting isometric representation. We have sufficient data from Theorem
3.3
to consider the representation
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
on
\mathcal{F}({{E}_{1}})\otimes\mathcal{K},
and discuss about the invariant subspces.
First, we start with the following definition:
Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
be c.b.c. representations of
\mathbb{E}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively. Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be invariant subspaces for the representations
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}),
respectively. We say that the subspaces
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
are
isomorphic
if
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})|_{\mathcal{M}}
is isomorphic to
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})|_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}.

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
are doubly commuting pure isometric representations. From Theorem
3.3

(k=1
case
),
there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
and a unitary
U_{\mathcal{H}}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
such that

U_{\mathcal{H}}\sigma(b)=\rho(b)U_{\mathcal{H}},\quad U_{\mathcal{H}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}=\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{H}})

and

U_{\mathcal{H}}\widetilde{V}^{(i+1)}=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{i}}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{H}}),\quad i\in I_{k}.

Similarly, for the representaion
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k+1)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
and a unitary
U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
such that

U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\psi(b)=\rho^{\prime}(b)U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}},\quad U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\widetilde{T}^{(1)}=\widetilde{S}^{\prime}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}})

and

U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\widetilde{T}^{(i+1)}=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{i}^{\prime}}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}),\quad i\in I_{k}.

In fact,
U_{\mathcal{H}}
and
U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
are mutli-analytic.
Suppose that
A:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a multi-analytic operator for the representaions
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}),
then the operator
A^{\prime}:\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
define by
A^{\prime}=U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}AU_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
is also multi-analytic for the representaions
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
and
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}^{\prime}}).

On the other hand, suppose
A^{\prime}:\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
is multi-analytic for the representaions
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
and
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}^{\prime}}).
Then the operator
A
yields a multi-analytic for the representaions
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}),
namely
A=U_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}^{*}A^{\prime}U_{\mathcal{H}}.
Moreover, the construction above shows that
A\in B(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
is a contraction (resp. isometry, unitary) if and only if
A^{\prime}\in B(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
is a contraction (resp. isometry, unitary).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be representations of
\mathcal{B}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime},
respectively. For
i\in I_{k},
let
\Phi_{i}:E_{i}\to B(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W})
and
\Phi^{\prime}_{i}:E_{i}\to B(\mathcal{W}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
be completely bounded bi-module maps such that
(\rho,M_{\Phi_{1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k}})
and
(\rho^{\prime},M_{\Phi_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k}^{\prime}})
are c.b.c representations of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime},
respectively. We say that the pairs
(\rho,{\Phi}_{1},\dots,{\Phi}_{k})
and
(\rho^{\prime},{\Phi}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\Phi}^{\prime}_{k})

coincide
if there exists a unitary
Z:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that

(I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z)\rho(b)=\rho^{\prime}(b)(I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z)

and

(I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z)\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}^{\prime}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes Z),\quad i\in I_{k}.

The following theorem is a complete set of isomorphic invariants of the corresponding invariant subspaces.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k+1)})
be doubly commuting pure isometric representations of
\mathbb{E}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively. Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
be invariant subspaces for the representations
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
and
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}^{\prime}})
of
{\mathbb{E}}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime},
respectively. Then
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
if and only if
\{\varrho^{\prime},{\Phi}_{1},\dots,{\Phi}_{k}\}
is coincide with
\{\varrho^{\prime\prime},{\Phi}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\Phi}^{\prime}_{k}\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a inavriant subspace for
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}}),
by Theorem
3.5
and Remark
3.8
, there exist a representaion of
\mathcal{B}
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{W},
completely bi-module maps
\Phi_{i}:E_{i+1}\to B(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W})
such that
(\varrho^{\prime},S^{\mathcal{W}},M_{\Phi_{1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k}})
is isomorpic to
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})|_{\mathcal{M}}.
Moreover,
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{M}\ominus\widetilde{S}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M})
and
\Phi_{i}(\xi_{i})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}}P_{\mathcal{M}})\widetilde{S}_{n}^{*}M_{\Theta_{i}}(\xi_{i})|_{\mathcal{W}}

\xi_{i}\in E_{i+1},i\in I_{k}.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime},
i.e., the representations
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})|_{\mathcal{M}}
and
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}^{\prime}})|_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
are isomorphic, then using the corresponding covariant representations
(\varrho^{\prime},S^{\mathcal{W}},M_{\Phi_{1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k}})
of
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})|_{\mathcal{M}}
and
(\varrho^{\prime\prime},S^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}},M_{\Phi^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,M_{\Phi^{\prime}_{k}})
of
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}}^{\prime})|_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
from above are isomorphic. This implies that there exists a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle U\varrho^{\prime}(b)=\varrho^{\prime\prime}(b)U,~U\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}}=\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes U),\quad U\widetilde{M}_{\Phi_{i}}=\widetilde{M}_{\Phi^{\prime}_{i}}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes U),

for all
i\in I_{k}.

Note that the unitary operator
U
is multi-analytic, thus
U=M_{\theta}
for some inner operator
\theta:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}^{\prime}.
In fact, the corresponding inner operator
\theta
is onto. Since
U
intertwine the representations
(\varrho^{\prime},S^{\mathcal{W}})
and
(\varrho^{\prime\prime},S^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}})
and by the definition of
M_{\theta}
(see Equation (
2.2
)), we can easily see that
U=I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z,
where
Z=\theta.
Using the above equation, we get

(I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z)\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}^{\prime}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes Z),\quad i\in I_{k}.

Conversely, suppose that
\{\varrho^{\prime},{\Phi}_{1},\dots,{\Phi}_{k}\}
and
\{\varrho^{\prime\prime},{\Phi}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\Phi}^{\prime}_{k}\}
are coincide, there exists a unitary
Z:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that

(I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z)\varrho^{\prime}(b)=\varrho^{\prime\prime}(b)(I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z)\quad and\quad(I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z)\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}^{\prime}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes Z),

for all
i\in I_{k}.
Clearly
U=I_{\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})}\otimes Z
is a unitary from
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}
to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime}.
From Equation (
2.2
), it is easy to verify that
U\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}}=\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes U)
and
U\widetilde{M}_{\Phi_{i}}=\widetilde{M}_{\Phi^{\prime}_{i}}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes U)
for
i\in I_{k}.
Then, by Remark
3.8
,
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}.

∎

Form the above theorem, since
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
are the invariant subspaces for the representations
(\rho,S)
and
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime})
and by Theorem
1.6
, there are Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that

\mathcal{M}=M_{\Psi}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W})\quad and\quad\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=M_{\Psi^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime}),

for some inner operators
\Psi:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{K}
and
\Psi^{\prime}:\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{K}^{\prime}.
Then by Remark
3.7
, the completely bounded bi-module maps
\Phi_{i},\Phi^{\prime}_{i},\>i\in I_{k},
in Theorem
4.3
can be written as

\Phi_{i}(\xi_{i})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}}M_{\Psi})\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{\Theta_{i}}(\xi_{i})|_{\mathcal{W}}

and

\Phi^{\prime}_{i}(\xi_{i})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}M_{\Psi^{\prime}})\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime*}}M_{\Psi^{\prime}}^{*}M_{\Theta^{\prime}_{i}}(\xi_{i})|_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}

for all
\xi_{i}\in E_{i+1},i\in I_{k}.

The following theorem characterize the invariant subspaces for
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
which is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k+1)})
and
(\psi,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k+1)})
be two doubly commuting pure isometric representations of
\mathbb{E}
acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively. Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
be an invariant subspace for
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
of
{\mathbb{E}}
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
if and only if there exists an inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{K}
( for the representations
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}^{\prime}})
and
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
) such that

\mathcal{M}=M_{\Psi}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime},
i.e.,
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})|_{\mathcal{M}}
is isomorphic to
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}^{\prime}}),
then there exists a unitary
U:\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}
such that

U\rho^{\prime}(b)=\rho(b)U,~U\widetilde{S}^{\prime}=\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes U)\quad and\quad U\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{i}^{\prime}}=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{i}}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes U).

Let
i_{\mathcal{M}}
be the inclusion map from
{\mathcal{M}}
to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}.
Define an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
by
\Pi=i_{\mathcal{M}}U,
then
\Pi\Pi^{*}=i_{\mathcal{M}}i_{\mathcal{M}}^{*},

\Pi\rho^{\prime}(b)=\rho(b)\Pi\quad\Pi\widetilde{S}^{\prime}=\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi),\quad and\quad\Pi\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{i}^{\prime}}=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{i}}(I_{E_{i+1}}\otimes\Pi).

Therefore
\Pi
is an isometric multi-analytic operator and thus there exists an inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{K}
such that
\Pi=M_{\Psi}.
This shows that

\mathcal{M}=\Pi(\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime})=M_{\Psi}(\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}).

On the other hand, let
\Psi:\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
be an inner operator such that
\mathcal{M}=M_{\Psi}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K},
then
M_{\Psi}:\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}
is a unitary which intertwines the representations
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime},M_{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}^{\prime}})
and
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})|_{\mathcal{M}}.
Then
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
be a doubly commuting subspace for
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K},
then as an invariant subspace
\mathcal{M},

(\varrho^{\prime},S^{\mathcal{W}},M_{\Phi_{1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k}})
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}
be defined as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
is isomorphic to
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta_{1}},\dots,M_{\Theta_{k}})|_{\mathcal{M}},
that is,
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}.
Therefore, by Theorem
4.4
,
\mathcal{M}=M_{\Psi}(\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}),
for some inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{K}.
Since
\mathcal{M}
is the doubly commuting subspace,
(\varrho^{\prime},S^{\mathcal{W}},M_{\Phi_{1}},\dots,M_{\Phi_{k}})
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}
is pure doubly commuting isometric representation.

Non-adiabatic Ring Polymer Molecular Dynamics in the Phase Space of the S ​ U ​ ( N ) {SU}(N) Lie Group
Duncan Bossion, Sutirtha N. Chowdhury, Pengfei Huo

Abstract
We derive the non-adiabatic ring polymer molecular dynamics (RPMD) approach in the phase space of the S ​ U ​ ( N ) {SU}(N) Lie Group. This method, which we refer to as the spin mapping non-adiabatic RPMD (SM-NRPMD), is based on the spin-mapping formalism for the electronic degrees of freedom (DOFs) and ring polymer path-integral description for the nuclear DOFs. Using the Stratonovich-Weyl transform for the electronic DOFs, and the Wigner transform for the nuclear DOFs, we derived an exact expression of the Kubo-transformed time-correlation function (TCF). We further derive the spin mapping non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics using the Matsubara approximation that removes the high frequency nuclear normal modes in the TCF and derive the SM-NRPMD approach from the non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics by discarding the imaginary part of the Liouvillian. The SM-NRPMD method has numerical advantages compared to the original NRPMD method based on the MMST mapping formalism, due to a more natural mapping using the S ​ U ​ ( N ) {SU}(N) Lie Group that preserves the symmetry of the original system. We numerically compute the Kubo-transformed position auto-correlation function and electronic population correlation function for three-state model systems. The numerical results demonstrate the accuracy of the SM-NRPMD method, which outperforms the original MMST-based NRPMD. We envision that the SM-NRPMD method will be a powerful approach to simulate electronic non-adiabatic dynamics and nuclear quantum effects accurately.

Introduction

Accurately simulating the quantum dynamics of molecular systems in condensed phase remains a challenge in theoretical chemistry, due to the difficulties of accurately describing electronically non-adiabatic dynamics and nuclear quantum effects.
Tully 2012
Such effects are inherent to a lot of key reactions in biochemistry, catalysis, and energy applications that involve electron transfer or proton-coupled electron transfer processes.
Marcus and Sutin 1985
;
Gray and Winkler 1996
;
Reece and Nocera 2009
Despite the recent progress on new theoretical approaches to study those types of reactions, the challenge remains for large systems with many degrees of freedom (DOFs), and the exact quantum simulations remain computationally expensive due to the unfavorable numerical scaling.

Recently emerged state-dependent RPMD approaches provide a unified description of both the electronically non-adiabatic dynamics and nuclear quantum effects, using a trajectory-based description. Ring-polymer molecular dynamics (RPMD)

;
Craig and Manolopoulos 2005a
;
Craig and Manolopoulos 2005b
;
Suleimanov, Allen, and Green 2013
;
Suleimanov et al. 2013

State-dependent RPMD methods are developed based on the RPMD framework, to provide both accurate non-adiabatic dynamics with an explicit description of electronic states, and a reliable treatment of nuclear quantum dynamics through the ring polymer path-integral quantization. These methods include non-adiabatic RPMD (NRPMD),

;
Richardson et al. 2017
;
Chowdhury and Huo 2019

;

Menzeleev, Bell, and Miller 2014
;

Chowdhury and Huo 2017
and ring-polymer surface hopping (RPSH)
Shushkov, Li, and Tully 2012
;
Shakib and Huo 2017
;

Among these various state-dependent RPMD approaches, NRPMD

;
Richardson et al. 2017
;
Chowdhury and Huo 2019
;
Chowdhury and Huo 2021
provides both accurate nuclear quantum dynamics and accurate electronic Rabi oscillations, thus being an ideal approach to investigate quantum dynamics of non-adiabatic systems.
Chowdhury and Huo 2019
;
Chowdhury, Mandal, and Huo 2021
This approach is based on the Meyer-Miller-Stock-Thoss (MMST) mapping formalism
Meyer and Miller 1979
;
Stock and Thoss 1997
;
Thoss and Stock 1999
and has been formally derived using the non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics formalism.
Chowdhury and Huo 2021
This mapping formalism, despite its great success and broad applications,

;

;

;

;
Huo and Coker 2011

has known flaws.

;
Kelly et al. 2012
;
Saller, Kelly, and Richardson 2019
In particular, it maps a
N
-level system onto
N

\otimes_{N}\mathfrak{su}(2)

\mathfrak{su}(2)
(only the ground and first excited states of the oscillators belong to the SEO subspace), whereas the original symmetry of a
N

\mathfrak{su}(N)

Due to this mapping procedure, the MMST mapping operators belong to a larger Hilbert space that contains states outside the SEO subspace, whereas the MMST mapping procedure tries to map the original electronic subspace onto the SEO subspace. It thus requires a projection back to the SEO subspace to obtain accurate results.
Ananth and Miller 2010
;
Kelly et al. 2012
;
Saller, Kelly, and Richardson 2019
As a consequence, the identity operator is not preserved through the MMST mapping and there is an ambiguity on how to evaluate it.
Saller, Kelly, and Richardson 2019
Related to the problem of the non-conserving identity, the non-adiabatic dynamics is sensitive to the separation between the state-dependent and the state-independent Hamiltonian.
Kelly et al. 2012
;
Cotton and Miller 2013

Recently, a new mapping formalism based on the phase space of the
{SU}(N)

;
Runeson and Richardson 2020
This new mapping formalism, referred to as the generalized spin mapping, preserves the original

\mathfrak{su}(N)

In particular, the Stratonovich-Weyl (SW) transform

Várilly and Gracia-Bondía 1989

;
Klimov and Chumakov 2009

;
Nemoto 2000
We have also provided a detailed derivation of the quantum Liouvillian and the linearized Liouvillian based on the spin mapping framework.
Bossion et al. 2022
We have also proposed a spin mapping NRPMD dynamics approach

N=2

IV

V
-

\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle\big[\hat{T}_{R}+U_{0}(\hat{R})\big]\otimes\hat{\mathcal{I}}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{e}}(\hat{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\hat{T}_{R}+U_{0}(\hat{R})\big]\otimes\hat{\mathcal{I}}+\sum_{n,m}V_{nm}(\hat{R})|n\rangle\langle m|,

N

We aim to evaluate the TCF governed by the Hamiltonian in Eq.
1
. In the following sections, we use the
SU(N)

II.1
The Spin Mapping Formalism in the
{SU}(N)
Representation

We briefly review the general expressions of the generators of the
\mathfrak{su}(N)
Lie algebra, which will be used as a matrix basis to represent electronic operators. The commutation (and anti-commutation) relations among these generators are defined in the
\mathfrak{su}(N)

Lie group via the exponential map.
Georgi 2000
;
Hall 2015

The generators, denoted as
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
, are expressed in Appendix
A
. These generators are traceless,
\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\big]=0
, and are orthonormal to each other as
\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{j}\big]=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\delta_{ij}
. The commutation and anti-commutation relations among the generators of the
\mathfrak{su}(N)
Lie algebra are presented as follows

\displaystyle\big[\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{j}\big]

\displaystyle=i\hbar\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}f_{ijk}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k},

(2a)

\displaystyle\big\{\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{j}\big\}_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{N}\delta_{ij}\hat{\mathcal{I}}+\hbar\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}d_{ijk}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k},

(2b)

where
\big\{\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{j}\big\}_{+}
represents the anti-commutator between
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
and
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{j}
, and
f_{ijk}
and
d_{ijk}
are the totally anti-symmetric and totally symmetric
structure constants
, respectively. Using Eqs.
2a
-
2b
, one can express the structure constants as

\displaystyle f_{ijk}=-i\frac{2}{\hbar^{3}}\mathrm{Tr}\Big[\big[\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{j}\big]\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\Big],

(3a)

\displaystyle d_{ijk}=\frac{2}{\hbar^{3}}\mathrm{Tr}\Big[\big\{\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{j}\big\}_{+}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\Big].

(3b)

The generators of an algebra
\{\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\}
can be obtained in different ways, but the most commonly used ones are based on a generalization of the Pauli matrices of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
and of the Gell-Mann matrices
Gell-Mann 1962

\mathfrak{su}(3)
, which is what is used in this work. This representation of the generators is referred to as the Generalized Gell–Mann matrix (GGM) basis.

;
Tilma and Nemoto 2011
Their detailed expressions are provided in Appendix
A
(see Eq.

108
-Eq.
110
). One can also derive an analytic expression (closed formulas) of

and

A

48

Using these generators, the Hamiltonian

(Eq.
1

;

\hat{H}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\hat{R},\hat{P})\cdot\hat{\mathcal{I}}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(\hat{R})\cdot\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k},

(4)

where the elements
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\hat{R},\hat{P})
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(\hat{R})

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}(\hat{R},\hat{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{e}}\big[\hat{H}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{I}}\big]=\hat{T}_{R}+U_{0}(\hat{R})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}V_{nn}(\hat{R}),

(5a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}(\hat{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\hbar}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{H}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\big]=\frac{2}{\hbar}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{V}_{\mathrm{e}}(\hat{R})\cdot\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\big].

(5b)

Note that Eq.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha_{nm}}(\hat{R})={V}_{mn}(\hat{R})+{V}_{nm}(\hat{R}),

(6a)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta_{nm}}(\hat{R})=i\big({V}_{mn}(\hat{R})-{V}_{nm}(\hat{R})\big),

(6b)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma_{n}}(\hat{R})=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n(n-1)}}{V}_{ll}(\hat{R})-\sqrt{\frac{2(n-1)}{n}}{V}_{nn}(\hat{R}),

(6c)

(Eq.
108

\beta_{nm}=n^{2}+2(m-n)

II.2
The Stratonovich-Weyl Transform

The Stratonovich-Weyl (SW) transform evaluates the expectation values of the spin operators under the generalized spin coherent states.
Radcliffe 1971
;
Nemoto 2000
The generalized spin coherent states are expressed as

|\mathbf{\Omega}\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}|n\rangle\langle n|\mathbf{\Omega}\rangle,

(7)

\langle n|\mathbf{\Omega}\rangle

Tilma and Sudarshan 2002

Tilma and Sudarshan 2004

Tilma and Nemoto 2011

where the expression of the differential phase-space volume element

115

N

\hbar\Omega_{k}\equiv\langle\mathbf{\Omega}|\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}|\mathbf{\Omega}\rangle,

(9)

where
\hbar\mathbf{\Omega}
plays the role of the Bloch vector,

;
Bertlmann and Krammer 2008

;
Bertlmann and Krammer 2008

The SW transform of an operator

\big[\hat{A}\big]_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbf{\Omega})=\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{A}\cdot\hat{w}_{\mathrm{s}}\big],

(10)

;
Brif and Mann 1999

Tilma and Nemoto 2011

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbf{\Omega})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\hat{\mathcal{I}}+r_{\mathrm{s}}\cdot\frac{2}{\hbar}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{2}-1}\Omega_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i}.

(11)

The parameter

;
Wangsness and Bloch 1953
;

The SW transform in Eq.

\{{\bm{\Omega}}\}

;
Runeson and Richardson 2020
;

\hat{A}

\hat{A}={\mathcal{A}}_{0}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{I}}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k},

(14)

where
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and
\mathcal{A}_{k}
are expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{e}}\big[\hat{A}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{I}}\big]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}A_{nn},

(15a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\hbar}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{A}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\big],

(15b)

with the following detailed expressions of
\mathcal{A}_{k}
as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha_{nm}}={A}_{mn}+{A}_{nm},

(16a)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta_{nm}}=i\big({A}_{mn}-{A}_{nm}\big),

(16b)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{n}}=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n(n-1)}}{A}_{ll}-\sqrt{\frac{2(n-1)}{n}}{A}_{nn}.

(16c)

Using the expression of the SW kernel in Eq.
11

\hat{A}

\displaystyle\big[\hat{A}\big]_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbf{\Omega})={\mathcal{A}}_{0}+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cdot\Omega_{k}.

One of the important properties of the SW transform is that it can be used to evaluate a quantum mechanical trace in the continuous phase space as follows

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{\Omega}\big[\hat{A}\big]_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbf{\Omega})=\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{A}\big].

\hat{A}

(20)

II.3
The Mixed Stratonovich-Weyl/Wigner Representation

For the nuclear DOF, one often use the following Wigner (W) transform

\big[\hat{O}(\hat{R},\hat{P})\big]_{\mathrm{w}}=\int d\Delta e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}P\Delta}\Big\langle R-\frac{\Delta}{2}\Big|\hat{O}(\hat{R},\hat{P})\Big|R+\frac{\Delta}{2}\Big\rangle,

(23)

which converts an operator
\hat{O}
into a phase space function
\big[\hat{O}(\hat{R},\hat{P})\big]_{\mathrm{w}}
.

For a general operator in the electronic and nuclear Hilbert space, we use the SW representation for the electronic subsystem that exhibits
SU(N)

For an operator

\displaystyle\big[\hat{A}(\hat{R},\hat{P})\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}

\displaystyle=\big[{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\hat{R},\hat{P})\big]_{\mathrm{w}}+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}\big[{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(\hat{R},\hat{P})\big]_{\mathrm{w}}\cdot\Omega_{k},

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}_{0}(R,P)+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(R,P)\cdot\Omega_{k}

(24)

where
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(R,P)
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(R,P)
are Wigner transforms of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\hat{R},\hat{P})
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(\hat{R},\hat{P})
, respectively, with the detailed forms of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and
\mathcal{A}_{k}
defined in Eq.
15

Furthermore, for two operators, the mixed SW/W representation is expressed as

\displaystyle\big[\hat{A}\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{0}e^{-i\frac{\hat{\Lambda}\hbar}{2}}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}+\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{2}-1}{\mathcal{A}}_{i}e^{-i\frac{\hat{\Lambda}\hbar}{2}}{\mathcal{B}}_{i}

(25)

\displaystyle+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{2}-1}\Omega_{i}({\mathcal{A}}_{i}e^{-i\frac{\hat{\Lambda}\hbar}{2}}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}+{\mathcal{A}}_{0}e^{-i\frac{\hat{\Lambda}\hbar}{2}}{\mathcal{B}}_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{r_{\mathrm{s}}}{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N^{2}-1}\Omega_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{j}e^{-i\frac{\hat{\Lambda}\hbar}{2}}{\mathcal{B}}_{k}(d_{ijk}+if_{ijk}),

where

\hat{\Lambda}=\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial P}\frac{\overrightarrow{\partial}}{\partial R}-\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial R}\frac{\overrightarrow{\partial}}{\partial P}

(26)

;
Imre et al. 1967
;

III
The Kubo-transformed time-correlation function

The Kubo-transformed TCF is defined as follows

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{\mathrm{K}}(t)=\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\lambda\mathrm{Tr}\big[e^{-(\beta-\lambda)\hat{H}}\hat{A}e^{-\lambda\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{B}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{N}}-\alpha)\hat{H}}\hat{A}e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\alpha\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{B}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big],

(27)

where
\beta=1/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
,
\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\equiv\beta/\mathcal{N}
, and
\mathrm{Tr}[\cdots]\equiv\mathrm{Tr_{n}Tr_{e}}[\cdots]
is a trace over nuclear and electronic DOFs and
\mathcal{Z}=\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}]
. Note that the index
\alpha
is used to represent a discrete version of the imaginary time integral over
\lambda
, not to be confused with
\alpha_{nm}
, the label of the
\mathfrak{su}(N)
generators used in Eq.
6
. We will later identify that index
\alpha
as the nuclear bead index. In this section, we use the mixed SW/W representation to re-express
C_{AB}^{\mathrm{K}}(t)
, and derive the exact quantum Liouvillian.

III.1
The Kubo-transformed TCF in the mixed SW/W representation

To evaluate the Kubo-transformed TCF, we follow the previous strategy
Chowdhury and Huo 2021
;
Hele et al. 2015a
;
Shi and Geva 2003
;

in Eq.

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{[{\mathcal{N}}]}(t)=\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\mathrm{Tr_{e}Tr_{n}}\Big[\big(e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big)^{{\mathcal{N}}-\alpha-1}

\displaystyle~~~~\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}\hat{A}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big(e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle~~~~\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{B}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\Big].

To evaluate the above trace, we insert the nuclear identity

(where

is the electronic identity) and use the property of the SW transform in Eq.

to compute electronic traces, leading to the following expression

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{[\mathcal{N}]}(t)=\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\int dR_{1}^{\prime}\int d\mathbf{\Omega}^{(1)}\Big[\big\langle R_{1}^{\prime}\big|e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}

\displaystyle~~\times\big(e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big)^{{\mathcal{N}}-\alpha-2}e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}\hat{A}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}

\displaystyle~~\times\big(e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big)^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{B}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}

\displaystyle~~\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}\big|R_{1}^{\prime}\big\rangle\Big]_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)},

(29)

where we denote
[\cdots]_{\mathrm{s}}^{(\alpha)}
as the SW transform of the
\alpha

(30b)

(30c)

We then use the following electronic identities (see Eq.

\displaystyle\hat{\mathds{1}}_{R_{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{\Omega}^{(\gamma)}}=

\displaystyle\int dR_{\gamma}^{\prime}|R_{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle\langle R_{\gamma}^{\prime}|\otimes\int d\mathbf{\Omega}^{(\gamma)}\hat{w}_{\mathrm{s}}^{(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathds{1}}_{R_{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}}=

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{[\mathcal{N}]}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}}\int d\{R_{\alpha}^{\prime}\}\int d\{R_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}\}\int d\{\mathbf{\Omega}^{(\alpha)}\}

(32)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\Big[\big\langle R_{1}^{\prime}\big|e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{\mathds{1}}_{R_{1}^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}\hat{A}\hat{\mathds{1}}_{R_{{\mathcal{N}}-\alpha}^{\prime},\mathbf{\Omega}^{({\mathcal{N}}-\alpha)}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{\mathds{1}}_{R_{{\mathcal{N}}-\alpha}^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}\hat{\mathds{1}}_{R_{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime},\mathbf{\Omega}^{{(\mathcal{N})}}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{B}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{\mathds{1}}_{R_{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{H}}\big|R_{1}^{\prime}\big\rangle\Big]_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)},

where we have introduced the shorthand notation

We can now change the nuclear variables

Poulsen, Nyman, and Rossky 2003

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(R_{\alpha}^{\prime}+R_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}^{\prime}-R_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{[{\mathcal{N}}]}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}(2\pi\hbar)^{\mathcal{N}}}\int d\{R_{\alpha}\}\int d\{P_{\alpha}\}\int d\{\mathbf{\Omega}^{(\alpha)}\}

(36)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times\big[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}\hat{A}\big]_{\mathrm{{w\bar{s}}}}\big[\hat{B}(t)\big]_{\mathrm{ws}},

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\gamma\neq\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}\Big[\big\langle R_{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}D_{\gamma}^{\prime}\big|e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big|R_{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}D_{\gamma}^{\prime}\big\rangle\Big]^{(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\big[\hat{B}(t)\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}.

25

We can formally express the Kubo-transformed TCF in Eq.

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{[{\mathcal{N}}]}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}(2\pi\hbar)^{\mathcal{N}}}\int d\{R_{\alpha}\}\int d\{P_{\alpha}\}\int d\{\mathbf{\Omega}^{(\alpha)}\}

(39)

\displaystyle\times\big[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}\hat{A}\big]_{\mathrm{w\bar{s}}}e^{\mathcal{L}t}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}},

where the time-evolution is governed by the quantum Liouvillian

\big[\hat{B}(t)\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}=e^{{\mathcal{L}}t}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}},

(40)

which will be derived in Sec.
III.2
.

If in addition
\hat{A}
is linear in
\hat{R}

\big[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}\hat{A}\big]_{\mathrm{w\bar{s}}}=\big[\hat{A}\big]_{\mathrm{w}}\big[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}\big]_{\mathrm{w\bar{s}}}=\mathcal{A}_{0}(R)\big[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}\big]_{\mathrm{w\bar{s}}},

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{[{\mathcal{N}}]}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}(2\pi\hbar)^{\mathcal{N}}}\int d\{R_{\alpha}\}\int d\{P_{\alpha}\}\int d\{\mathbf{\Omega}^{(\alpha)}\}

(42)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{A}_{0}(R)\big[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}\big]_{\mathrm{w\bar{s}}}e^{\mathcal{L}t}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}},

The estimator
[\hat{B}]_{\mathrm{ws}}
(in Eq.
38
) can be expressed (using Eq.

) as

\displaystyle\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}

(43)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\big({\mathcal{B}}_{0}^{(\alpha)}+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}{\mathcal{B}}_{k}^{(\alpha)}\cdot\Omega^{(\alpha)}_{k}\big),

with the short notation
\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}\equiv\big[\hat{B}(\hat{R}_{\alpha},\hat{P_{\alpha}})\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}
,
\mathcal{B}_{0}^{(\alpha)}\equiv\mathcal{B}_{0}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})
and
\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(\alpha)}\equiv\mathcal{B}_{k}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})

\hat{B}

\hat{B}=\hat{R}

\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\big[\hat{R}_{\alpha}\big]_{\mathrm{w}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}R_{\alpha}.

(44)

If

48

in Eq.

(46b)

\displaystyle\big[|m\rangle\langle n|\big]^{(\alpha)}_{\mathrm{s}}=r_{\mathrm{s}}\big(\Omega_{\alpha_{nm}}^{(\alpha)}+i\Omega_{\beta_{nm}}^{(\alpha)}\big),

(46c)

III.2
Expression of the Quantum Liouvillian

To obtain the quantum Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}
(in Eq.
40
), we use the time-derivative of
\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}
through the Heisenberg EOMs as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}={\mathcal{L}}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha)}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)},

(47)

leading to an expression of bead specific Liouvillian components,
{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha)}
, evolving each bead. Each of these Liouvillian component is identical to what we have previously derived for a regular TCF.
Bossion et al. 2022
The expression of
{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha)}
is obtained through

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[{\mathcal{B}}_{0}\hat{\mathcal{I}}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N^{2}-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[{\mathcal{B}}_{i}\cdot\frac{1}{\hbar}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[{\mathcal{B}}_{0}\big]_{\mathrm{w}}^{(\alpha)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N^{2}-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\big[{\mathcal{B}}_{i}]_{\mathrm{w}}^{(\alpha)}\cdot r_{\mathrm{s}}\Omega_{i}^{(\alpha)}\Big)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}^{(\alpha)}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}^{(\alpha)}+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{2}-1}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{(\alpha)}\big(\Omega_{i}^{(\alpha)}{\mathcal{B}}_{i}^{(\alpha)}\big).

The state-independent Liouvillian
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{(\alpha)}
is expressed as

{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{(\alpha)}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{(\alpha)}\equiv\frac{2}{\hbar}H_{\mathrm{s}}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})\sin\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\hat{\Lambda}_{\alpha}\right)\mathcal{B}_{0}^{(\alpha)},

(49)

with the SW/W transform of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{s}}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\equiv\big[\hat{H}(\hat{R}_{\alpha},\hat{P}_{\alpha})\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}

(50a)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{0}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}-1}\mathcal{H}_{k}(R_{\alpha})\Omega_{k}^{(\alpha)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{\alpha}^{2}}{2m}+U_{0}(R_{\alpha})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}V_{nn}(R_{\alpha}),

(50b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}({R}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\hbar}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{V}_{\mathrm{e}}({R}_{\alpha})\cdot\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\big],

(50c)

and the negative Poisson operator for the
\alpha_{\mathrm{th}}
bead is

\hat{\Lambda}_{\alpha}=\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial P_{\alpha}}\frac{\overrightarrow{\partial}}{\partial R_{\alpha}}-\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial R_{\alpha}}\frac{\overrightarrow{\partial}}{\partial P_{\alpha}}.

(51)

The state-dependent Liouvillian is split into two terms,
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{(\alpha)}={\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{e}(\alpha)}+{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{n}(\alpha)}
. The first term
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{e}(\alpha)}
evolves the spin mapping variables (electronic DOFs) as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{e}(\alpha)}\big(r_{\mathrm{s}}\Omega_{i}^{(\alpha)}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(\alpha)}\big)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{r_{\mathrm{s}}}{\hbar}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N^{2}-1}f_{ijk}{\mathcal{H}}_{j}(R_{\alpha})\Omega_{k}^{(\alpha)}\cos\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\hat{\Lambda}_{\alpha}\right)\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(\alpha)},

(52)

and the second term
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{n}(\alpha)}
evolves the nuclear DOFs and couples the spin-mapping variables and the nuclear DOFs as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{n}(\alpha)}\big(r_{\mathrm{s}}\Omega_{i}^{(\alpha)}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(\alpha)}\big)\equiv\frac{1}{\hbar}\Bigg[\frac{1}{N}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}(R_{\alpha})+2r_{\mathrm{s}}\Omega_{i}^{(\alpha)}{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N^{2}-1}d_{ijk}{\mathcal{H}}_{j}(R_{\alpha})\Omega_{k}^{(\alpha)}\Bigg]\sin\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\hat{\Lambda}_{\alpha}\right)\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(\alpha)}.

(53)

and note that the
1/N
in Eq.
III.2
comes from the SW transform (where
N
is the number of electronic states, not to be confused with the total number of beads
\mathcal{N}
).

These Liouvillian expressions give rise to the time evolution of each bead,
\alpha
as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha)}\big[\hat{B}\big]_{\mathrm{ws}}^{(\alpha)}

(54)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\Bigg[{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{(\alpha)}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{(\alpha)}+r_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{2}-1}\big({\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{e}(\alpha)}+{\mathcal{L}}_{i}^{\mathrm{n}(\alpha)}\big)\Omega_{i}^{(\alpha)}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(\alpha)}\Bigg].

We emphasize that the Kubo-transformed TCF in Eq.
39
as well as the Liouvillian in Eq.
54
are in principle exact. Directly evaluating these expressions numerically will be as difficult as the exact quantum mechanics.

IV
Non-adiabatic Matsubara Dynamics and SM-NRPMD

In this section, we follow the original work on the Matsubara dynamics in Ref.
50
as well as our recent work on non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics based on the MMST mapping variables to derive the spin mapping based non-adiabatic Matsubara and the spin-mapping non-adiabatic RPMD (SM-NRPMD) approach.

IV.1
Non-adiabatic Matsubara Dynamics

We follow the procedure of our previous work
Chowdhury and Huo 2021
on deriving the non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics. First, a transformation from the bead to the normal mode representation is introduced as follows

{\mathcal{R}}_{l}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\frac{T_{\alpha l}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}R_{\alpha},~~~~~~~~~~{\mathcal{P}}_{l}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\frac{T_{\alpha l}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}P_{\alpha},

(55)

and the corresponding inverse transform is

R_{\alpha}=\sum_{l=-\frac{{\mathcal{N}}-1}{2}}^{\frac{{\mathcal{N}}-1}{2}}T_{\alpha l}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{R}}_{l},~~P_{\alpha}=\sum_{l=-\frac{{\mathcal{N}}-1}{2}}^{\frac{{\mathcal{N}}-1}{2}}T_{\alpha l}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}_{l},

(56)

with the transformation matrix element (with an odd number
{\mathcal{N}}
) defined as

\displaystyle T_{\alpha l}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}},&~~~~~~~~~l=0,\\
\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mathcal{N}}}\sin\frac{2\pi\alpha l}{\mathcal{N}},&~~~~~~~~~1\leq l\leq\frac{{\mathcal{N}}-1}{2},\\
\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mathcal{N}}}\cos\frac{2\pi\alpha l}{\mathcal{N}},&-\frac{{\mathcal{N}}-1}{2}\leq l\leq-1.\end{matrix}\right.

(57)

The new variables
\{\mathcal{R}_{l},\mathcal{P}_{l}\}
are normal modes of the free ring polymer Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rp}}^{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\Big[\frac{P^{2}_{\alpha}}{2m}+\frac{m}{2\beta_{\mathcal{N}}^{2}\hbar^{2}}(R_{\alpha}-R_{\alpha-1})^{2}\Big]

(58)

\displaystyle=

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we derive the SM-NRPMD method for a general
N
-level system. Our formalism is based on the Kubo-transformed time correlation function (TCF). We use the spin-mapping representation and Stratonovich-Weyl transform that map the electronic DOFs onto continuous variables in the phase space of
SU(N)
Lie group, which explicitly addresses the known challenges
Runeson and Richardson 2020
;
Bossion et al. 2022
in the original MMST mapping formalism.
Stock and Thoss 1997
;
Thoss and Stock 1999
We then use the multidimensional Wigner transform to describe the nuclear DOFs in the Kubo-transformed TCF. We further derive the spin mapping non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics using the Matsubara approximation that removes the high frequency nuclear normal modes in the TCF. Further, discarding the imaginary part of the Liouvillian (i.e., the RPMD approximation) from the non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics, we derive the expression of the SM-NRPMD method. The two-state special case of SM-NRPMD was first proposed in Ref.
49
. For the one bead limit, the EOMs of the SM-NRPMD method reduce back to the EOMs used in the spin-LSC method for a regular TCF. Even though the EOMs for SM-NRPMD (Eq.
98
) are formally identical to those of the MMST-based NRPMD,
Richardson and Thoss 2013
;
Chowdhury and Huo 2021
the initial distribution of the mapping variables (see Eq.
101
for SM-NRPMD and Eq.
F
for NRPMD) and the choice of zero-point energy parameters, or rather of Bloch sphere radius (Eq.
97
for SM-NRPMD and
\gamma=1
for NRPMD
Richardson and Thoss 2013
) are indeed different.

We use numerical simulations to demonstrate the accuracy of the SM-NRPMD approach, with a three-state system coupled to one nuclear DOF, with the electronic couplings ranging from adiabatic to the non-adiabatic limit. Using the SM-NRPMD approach, we computed Kubo-transformed nuclear position auto-correlation function, and population time-correlation functions. The results generated from SM-NRPMD are very accurate compared to the numerically exact simulations in all parameter regimes, and provide a significant improvement compared to the NRPMD method. Future applications of SM-NRPMD could be computing rate constants for non-adiabatic systems based on the flux-side correlation function formalism,
Huo, Miller, and Coker 2013
;
Duke and Ananth 2016
;
Menzeleev, Bell, and Miller 2014
;
Lawrence et al. 2019
;
Lawrence and Manolopoulos 2020
where nuclear quantum effects and electronic non-adiabatic effects are both present.

We want to point out that the spin mapping based non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics method (Eq.
70
and Eq.
73
) is a general theoretical framework, which can be used to derive other state-dependent path-integral approaches, such as the non-adiabatic CMD
Liao and Voth 2002
(through a mean field approximation
Hele et al. 2015b
;
Trenins and Althorpe 2018
of the non-adiabatic Matsubara dynamics), or taking advantage of the recent progress in the developments of nuclear quantum dynamics based on various approximations of the Matsubara dynamics.
Hele et al. 2015a
;
M.J.Willatt 2017
;
Jung, Videla, and Batista 2019
;
Jung, Videla, and Batista 2020
;
Cao and Voth 1994
;
Hele et al. 2015b
We hope that our current work provides a framework for developing accurate quantum dynamics approaches.

Appendix E
Non-equilibrium Dynamics

In our previous work, we have justified that NRPMD is also capable of accurately describing the non-equilibrium TCF. Here, we carry the same procedure and show that the SM-NRPMD is also capable to describe the non-equilibrium TCF. For a given photo-induced process, we are often interested in the reduced density matrix dynamics upon an initial excitation of the molecular system. The reduced density matrix element can be expressed as

\rho_{nm}(t)=\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}(0)e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}|n\rangle\langle m|e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}],

(136)

where the initial density operator
\hat{\rho}(0)=|a\rangle\langle a|\otimes\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}}
is a tensor product of the electronic and nuclear DOFs, with
\mathcal{Z}=\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}}]
, and
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}
the ground state Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}=\hat{T}_{R}+U_{g}(\hat{R}),

(137)

with the ground state potential
{U}_{g}(\hat{R})
associated with the ground electronic state
|g\rangle
.

The initial density
\hat{\rho}(0)
evolves under the influence of the total Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
of the system. The reduced density matrix elements can be equivalently expressed as a TCF

\rho_{nm}(t)=C_{AB}(t)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}}\hat{A}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{B}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}],

(138)

where
\hat{A}=|a\rangle\langle a|
is the initially occupied electronic state, and
\hat{B}=|n\rangle\langle m|
. Because
\hat{A}
and
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}
commute, we have

\displaystyle C^{[\mathcal{N}]}_{AB}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\lambda\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-(\beta-\lambda)\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}}\hat{A}e^{-\lambda\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}}\hat{B}(t)]

(139)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\beta}d\lambda}{\beta}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{\mathrm{g}}}\hat{A}\hat{B}(t)]=C_{AB}(t).

Hence, one can rewrite the reduced density matrix elements
\rho_{nm}(t)
into the Kubo-transformed time-correlation function
C^{[\mathcal{N}]}_{AB}(t)
. This Kubo-transformed TCF is
not
an equilibrium correlation function. Nevertheless, the Kubo-transformed structure allows us to express it as the discrete version of the time-correlation function. Following the same derivation outlined in the main text, we can obtain the out-of-equilibrium SM-NRPMD TCF

\displaystyle\rho_{nm}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}(2\pi\hbar)^{\mathcal{N}}}\int\{dR\}\int\{dP\}\int\{d\mathbf{\Omega}\}e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}H_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[\mathcal{N}]}}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\Big[\prod_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}\hat{w}_{\mathrm{\bar{s}}}^{(\alpha)}|a\rangle\langle a|\Big]e^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[\mathcal{N}]}t}\big[|n\rangle\langle m|\big]_{\mathrm{s}},

(140)

where
H_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[\mathcal{N}]}
is expressed as

H_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[\mathcal{N}]}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\Big[{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(R_{\alpha},P_{\alpha})+\frac{m}{2\beta_{\mathcal{N}}^{2}\hbar^{2}}(R_{\alpha}-R_{\alpha-1})^{2}\Big],

(141)

and the bead-averaged estimator
[|n\rangle\langle m|]_{\mathrm{s}}
is expressed in Eq.
106
. The initial electronic phase
\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\prod_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}\hat{w}_{\mathrm{\bar{s}}}^{(\alpha)}|a\rangle\langle a|\big]
is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\Big[\prod_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}\hat{w}_{\mathrm{\bar{s}}}^{(\alpha)}|a\rangle\langle a|\Big]=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\Big[\prod_{\gamma\leq\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}\hat{w}_{\mathrm{\bar{s}}}^{(\gamma)}|a\rangle\langle a|\prod_{\gamma>\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}\hat{w}_{\mathrm{\bar{s}}}^{(\gamma)}\Big].

(142)

Further, the Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[\mathcal{N}]}
corresponds to the EOM in Eq.
98
.

Appendix F
MMST-based NRPMD approach and the Exact Simulation

The original MMST-based NRPMD approach to compute position auto-correlation function was proposed as
Richardson and Thoss 2013

\displaystyle C^{[\mathcal{N}]}_{RR}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{N}}}\int d\{R_{\alpha}\}\int d\{P_{\alpha}\}\int d\{{\bf q}^{(\alpha)}\}\int d\{{\bf p}^{(\alpha)}\}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}H^{[\mathcal{N}]}_{\mathrm{rp}}}\phi e^{-\frac{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{N}}}{\hbar}}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}\big]\bar{R}e^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[N]}t}\bar{R}(t),

(143)

where
d\{{\bf q}^{(\alpha)}\}=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}d{\bf q}^{(\alpha)}
,
d\{{\bf p}^{(\alpha)}\}=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}d{\bf p}^{(\alpha)}
, and
{\bf q}^{(\alpha)}=\{q_{1}^{(\alpha)},\cdots,q_{N}^{(\alpha)}\}
,
{\bf p}^{(\alpha)}=\{p_{1}^{(\alpha)},\cdots,p_{N}^{(\alpha)}\}
. Further,
\phi=\big(\frac{4}{\pi^{N}}\big)^{\mathcal{N}}
,
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{N}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}([\mathbf{q}^{(\alpha)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{q}^{(\alpha)}+[\mathbf{p}^{(\alpha)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{p}^{(\alpha)})
, and
\bar{R}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}R_{\alpha}
is the bead average position. In addition,
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}
is expressed as
Richardson and Thoss 2013

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}}(R_{\alpha}){\bf q}^{(\alpha)}[{\bf q}^{(\alpha)}]^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}}(R_{\alpha}){\bf p}^{(\alpha)}[{\bf p}^{(\alpha)}]^{\mathrm{T}}

(144)

where
\mathcal{M}_{nm}(R_{\alpha})=\langle n|e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\hat{V}_{\mathrm{e}}(R_{\alpha})}|m\rangle
, and
[{\bf q}^{(\alpha)}]^{\mathrm{T}}
represents the transpose of the
{\bf q}^{(\alpha)}
row matrix. The Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[\mathcal{N}]}
(in terms of the MMST mapping variables) is identical to the corresponding one in SM-NRPMD, both corresponding to the EOMs in Eq.
98
.

To compute a population TCF, the NRPMD approach uses
Richardson and Thoss 2013

\displaystyle C^{[\mathcal{N}]}_{mn}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{N}}}\int d\{R_{\alpha}\}\int d\{P_{\alpha}\}\int d\{{\bf q}_{\alpha}\}\int d\{{\bf p}_{\alpha}\}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}H^{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{rp}}}\phi e^{-\frac{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{N}}}{\hbar}}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}\hat{A}\big]e^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rp}}^{[\mathcal{N}]}t}\bar{B}_{n},

(145)

where
\bar{B}_{n}
is the bead average projection operator expressed in Eq.
106

\gamma=1
corresponding to the ZPE parameter in the MMST mapping theory,
Stock and Thoss 1997
;
Thoss and Stock 1999
and
\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}\hat{A}\big]

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\big[\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}\hat{A}\big]=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{Tr_{e}}\Big[\prod_{\gamma\leq\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}{\bm{\mathcal{M}}}(R_{\gamma}){\bf q}^{(\gamma)}[{\bf q}^{(\gamma)}]^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\times{\bm{\mathcal{M}}}(R_{\gamma}){\bf p}^{(\gamma)}[{\bf p}^{(\gamma)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\hat{A}\prod_{\gamma>\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}{\bm{\mathcal{M}}}(R_{\gamma}){\bf q}^{(\gamma)}[{\bf q}^{(\gamma)}]^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\times{\bm{\mathcal{M}}}(R_{\gamma}){\bf p}^{(\gamma)}[{\bf p}^{(\gamma)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\Big].

(146)

Thus, the dynamics between the SM version and the MMST version of NRPMD are identical, and the difference between them comes from the initial distributions of the mapping variables and the choice and justification of the

Note that this is the original version of the MMST-based NRPMD approach,
Richardson and Thoss 2013

Richardson et al. 2017

The exact results are obtained by explicitly computing the Kubo-transformed TCF as follows

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{\mathrm{K}}(t)=\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{N}}-\alpha)\hat{H}}\hat{A}e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\alpha\hat{H}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\hat{B}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}\big],

(147)

where the trace is for both electronic and nuclear DOFs. To evaluate the trace, we explicitly calculate the eigenstate of
\hat{H}
.

The full Hilbert space of the entire system is represented with a basis composed by a tensor product of the electronic subspace
\{|n\rangle\}
and the Fock states
\{|\chi_{a}\rangle\}
of the nuclear DOFs as follows

|n\rangle\otimes|\chi_{a}\rangle\equiv|n,\chi_{a}\rangle,

(148)

where
|n\rangle
is the electronic diabatic states and
|\chi_{a}\rangle
is the eigenstate of
\frac{\hat{P}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega\hat{R}^{2}
. We solve the eigenvalue problem of the total Hamiltonian as follows

\hat{H}|\Psi_{\nu}\rangle=E_{\nu}|\Psi_{\nu}\rangle,

(149)

where
|\Psi_{\nu}\rangle=\sum_{n,a}c^{\nu}_{n,a}|n,\chi_{a}\rangle
is the eigenstate of the
entire
system (including both electronic and nuclear DOFs), and
c^{\nu}_{n,a}=\langle n,\chi_{a}|\Psi_{\nu}\rangle
is the expansion coefficient. Both
E_{\nu}
and
c^{\nu}_{n,a}
can be directly obtained from diagonalizing the matrix of
\hat{H}
with the matrix elements
\langle\chi_{b},m|\hat{H}|n,\chi_{a}\rangle
. Evaluating the
\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{O}]
in Eq.
147
as
\sum_{\nu}\langle\Psi_{\nu}|\hat{O}|\Psi_{\nu}\rangle
, the Kubo-transformed TCF is expressed as

\displaystyle C_{AB}^{\mathrm{K}}(t)=\frac{1}{{\cal{Z}}{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\nu,\mu}

\displaystyle e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{N}}-\alpha)E_{\nu}}[\hat{A}]_{\nu\mu}e^{-\beta_{\mathcal{N}}\alpha E_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\times e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{\mu}t}[\hat{B}]_{\mu\nu}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{\nu}t},

(150)

where the matrix
[\hat{A}]_{\nu\mu}
is

[\hat{A}]_{\nu\mu}=\langle\Psi_{\nu}|\hat{A}|\Psi_{\mu}\rangle=\sum_{n,a}\sum_{m,b}(c^{\nu}_{n,a})^{*}\langle\chi_{a},n|\hat{A}|m,\chi_{b}\rangle c^{\mu}_{m,b},

(151)

and similarly for
[\hat{B}]_{\mu\nu}=\langle\Psi_{\mu}|\hat{B}|\Psi_{\nu}\rangle
. When
\hat{A}=\hat{R}
,
[\hat{A}]_{\nu\mu}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}\sqrt{\frac{a+1}{{2}}}\delta_{m,n+1}
, and when
\hat{A}=|m\rangle\langle m|
,
[\hat{A}]_{\nu\mu}=\delta_{mn}\delta_{ab}
. For the partition function in Eq.
F
, one can evaluate it exactly as

\mathcal{Z}=\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}]=\sum_{\nu}\langle\Psi_{\nu}|e^{-\beta\hat{H}}|\Psi_{\nu}\rangle=\sum_{\nu}e^{-\beta E_{\nu}}.

For the model systems studied here, solving the eigenvalue problem in Eq.
149
requires about 50 Fock states
\{|\chi_{a}\rangle\}
to represent the nuclear operators, and the TCF in Eq.
F
converges with
\mathcal{N}=6
.

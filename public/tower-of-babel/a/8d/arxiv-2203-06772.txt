On a version of a multivariate integration by parts formula for Lebesgue integrals
Jonathan Ansari

Abstract
Multidimensional integration by parts formulas apply under the standard assumption that one of the functions is continuous and the other has bounded Hardy-Krause variation. Motivated by recently developed results in the probabilistic context of price and risk bounds, this paper provides a version of an integration by parts formula for the Lebesgue integral of measure-inducing functions which may both be discontinuous and may have infinite Hardy-Krause variation. To this end, we give a general definition of measure-inducing functions and establish various of their properties, such as a characterization in terms of Δ \Delta -monotone functions. As a consequence of the integration by parts formula, several convergence results are provided, allowing an extension of the Lebesgue integral of a measure-inducing function to the case where one integrates with respect to a continuous semi-copula. The latter class of aggregation functions includes quasi-copulas which serve as bounds for the dependence structure in many applications.

Integration by parts is a standard tool in analysis. In the one-dimensional case, well-known conditions on functions

[

We refer to

In the two-dimensional case, for functions

where
\big[g\big]_{a_{1},a_{2}}^{b_{1},b_{2}}:=g(a_{1},b_{1})-g(a_{1},b_{2})-g(a_{2},b_{1})+g(a_{2},b_{2})\,,

Recent developments in probability motivate a version of an integration by parts formula for the Lebesgue integral on a possibly non-compact domain

In this paper, we fix these inaccuracies and provide a general framework for an integration by parts formula for Lebesgue integrals. The underlying functions are defined on a possibly unbounded domain and are not assumed to be continuous. As motivated above, we assume that the integrator

In Section

In Sections

Measure-inducing functions

We begin with a general definition of a measure-inducing function. Then we provide the necessary notation and outline several properties of measure-inducing functions, which are needed for the integration by parts formulas in (

2.1
Definition of a measure-inducing function

It is well-known that every right-continuous, non-decreasing function
F\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
with
\lim_{x\to-\infty}F(x)=0
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)=1
induces a uniquely determined probability measure
P
on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathbb{R}
such that
P((x,y])=F(y)-F(x)

x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
x\leq y\,.
Dispensing with the assumption of right-continuity,

is still uniquely determined through

\displaystyle P([x,y])=\lim_{y^{\prime}\downarrow y}F(y^{\prime})-\lim_{x^{\prime}\uparrow x}F(x^{\prime})\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }x\leq y\,,

(3)

i.e., the value of each closed interval is approximated from the outside.
If
F
has bounded variation and is no more assumed to be non-decreasing, then, in general,

defined by (
3

However, if
F
has unbounded variation (but is still bounded), it does not necessarily induce a signed measure by (
3

3
Integration by parts for measure-inducing functions

This section presents the main results of integration by parts.
After introducing the expectation operator and the integration by parts operator, we formulate Theorem

In the second part of this section, we give a transformation formula for the integration by parts operator, which corresponds to the change of variables for Lebesgue integrals.

4.2
Non-compact domain

In the case where the underlying space is non-compact, e.g.,
\Xi=\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

\Xi=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\,,
or
\Xi=[0,1)^{d}\,,
the induced signed measures
\{\nu_{f_{I}}\}_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}
are not necessarily finite. So, conditions (
i
) and (

) of Theorem
4.2
, which yield boundedness of

, are not sufficient for the existence of the integrals
\int_{[0,1]^{|I|}}\overline{g}_{I}\mathrm{\,d}\nu_{f_{I}}\,,

I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\,.

The following theorem provides the convergence of the integrals
(\psi_{g_{n}}(f))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
using a simple integrability condition on
f\,.
It extends the integration by parts formula in
[
32
, Proposition 2]
to the multivariate case and to integrators that are approximated by signed measures.

Theorem 4.4

(Convergence on non-compact domain)
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}\,,
let
g_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be right-continuous, grounded, and measure-inducing.
Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be left-continuous with
f_{\emptyset}\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Assume that there are distribution functions
F_{1},\ldots,F_{d}\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]\,,
a function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,,
and
\alpha>0

such that

(i)

g_{n}(x)\to g(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

(ii)

g_{n}^{\emptyset}\to g^{\emptyset}\,,

(iii)

|\overline{g_{n}}(x)|\leq\alpha\,(1-\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\{F_{i}(x_{i})\})
for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\,,
and

(iv)

the Lebesgue integral
\int_{0}^{1}f_{I}\left(F_{i_{1}}^{[-1]}(u),\ldots,F_{i_{k}}^{[-1]}(u)\right)\mathrm{\,d}u
exists for all
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

I\neq\emptyset\,.

Then,
\pi_{f}(\overline{g})
is finite and

\displaystyle\psi_{g_{n}}(f)\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\pi_{f}(\overline{g})\,.

(35)

Remark 4.5
.

By Assumption (
iii
) of Theorem
4.4
,
(\overline{g_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly asymptotically bounded by the survival function
\overline{F_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}}
of a so-called comonotonic random vector
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})=(F_{1}^{[-1]}(U),\ldots,F_{d}^{[-1]}(U))\,,

U
uniformly distributed on
(0,1)\,,
having marginal distribution functions
F_{1},\ldots,F_{d}\,.
Indeed, by (
74

\overline{F_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}}(x)=\overline{M^{d}}(F_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,F_{d}(x_{d}))=1-\max\{F_{i}(x_{i})\}

for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,
where

denotes the upper Fréchet copula.
For
x\to\infty\,,
Assumption (
iii
) yields, in particular, that
(\overline{g_{n}})_{n}
is uniformly bounded by
\alpha\,.

Assumption (
iv

I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

I\neq\emptyset\,,
the expectation of

exists which guarantees, inductively by the integration by parts formula, the
\nu_{f_{I}}
- integrability of
\overline{F_{X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{k}}}}\,.

Hence, Assumption (

is dominated by an integrable function for all

As will be shown below, Theorem
4.4
allows an extension of the Lebesgue integral of a possibly unbounded measure-inducing function

to the case where one integrates with respect to an
L
-Lipschitz semi-copula. Note that semi-copulas describe the dependence structure of capacities which generalize measures by requiring only monotonicity instead of
\sigma

[

Lemma 4.6

For

Corollary 4.7

Applications to stochastic orderings

As an application of the integration by parts formulas and the convergence results established in Sections
3

For

\displaystyle F\leq_{\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ :\,\Longleftrightarrow\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \psi_{F}(f)\leq\psi_{F^{\prime}}(f)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all }f\in\mathcal{F}\text{ such that the expectations exist},

[
24
]

Important classes of functions that generate stochastic orderings by (

of
\Delta
-antitone functions, the class
\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}
of
\Delta
-monotone functions, the class
\mathcal{F}_{sm}
of supermodular functions,

\mathcal{F}_{dcx}

A.10

Several integral stochastic orderings have a smooth generator

Since any smooth function on

Definition 5.1

(Semi-distribution function on

)
.

\displaystyle H(x)=S(F_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,F_{d}(x_{d}))\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all }x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

(39)

A semi-distribution function

is called a

if the semi-copula

in (

) is a quasi-copula.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{+}^{d}

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

Appendix A: Properties of measure-inducing functions

In the sequel, we provide various properties of measure-inducing functions concerning left- and right-continuous versions, transformations, marginal functions, the Hardy-Krause variation, the Jordan decomposition, and survival functions. All proofs are deferred to Appendix B.

A.1
Left- and right-continuity

Denote a multivariate function as left-/right-continuous if it is componentwise left-/right-continuous. By the following result, a left-/right-continuous measure-inducing function has the desirable property that the induced signed measure is generated by half-open intervals and can be expressed by the difference operator
\Delta_{x,y}
in (
6
).

Proposition A.1
.

For
f\in\mathcal{F}_{mi}(\Xi)\,,
the following statements hold true.

(i)

If
f
is left-continuous, then the induced signed measure
\nu_{f}
is generated by

\displaystyle\nu_{f}\left([x,y)\right)=\Delta_{x,y}[f]

(40)

for all
x,y\in\Xi
with
x\leq y\,.

(ii)

If
f
is right-continuous, then the induced signed measure
\nu_{f}
is generated by

\displaystyle\nu_{f}\left((x,y]\right)=\Delta_{x,y}[f]

(41)

for all
x,y\in\Xi
with
x\leq y\,.

(iii)

If
f
is continuous, then the induced signed measure
\nu_{f}
is generated by

\displaystyle\nu_{f}\left(B\right)=\Delta_{x,y}[f]

for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
a\leq x\leq y\leq b\,,
where
B=\bigtimes_{i=1}^{d}B_{i}
with
B_{i}\in\{[x_{i},y_{i}],(x_{i},y_{i}],[x_{i},y_{i}),(x_{i},y_{i})\}\,,

1\leq i\leq d\,.

For a function
f\colon\Xi\to\mathbb{R}\,,
denote by
f_{-}
and
f_{+}
its componentwise left- and right-continuous version, respectively, whenever it exists.
The following lemma states that, under the continuity assumption on
f
at the boundary of the domain, the signed measure induced by
f
coincides with the signed measure induced by
f_{-}
and
f_{+}\,,
respectively. This property is well-known when
F
is a univariate distribution function. Then the corresponding probability measure
P
coincides with the measure induced by the left-continuous version
F_{-}\,,
see (
3
).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f\colon\Xi\to\mathbb{R}
be a function that satisfies the boundary conditions (
11
) and (
12
).
Assume that
f_{-}
and
f_{+}
exist.
If one of the functions
f\,,

f_{-}\,,
and
f_{+}
is measure-inducing, then also the others are measure-inducing and it holds that

\displaystyle\nu_{f}=\nu_{f_{-}}=\nu_{f_{+}}\,,

i.e., the measures induced by
f\,,

f_{-}\,,
and
f_{+}\,,
respectively, coincide.

The following example shows that in the above lemma the continuity assumption at the boundary of
\Xi
cannot be omitted.

Example A.3
.

For
\Xi=[0,1]\,,
let
f\colon\Xi\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

Then
f
does not satisfy condition (

), i.e.,
f
is not continuous at the lower boundary of
\Xi\,.
Denote by
\delta_{\{x\}}
the one-point probability measure with mass

in

Then
\nu_{f}=\delta_{\{0\}}\,.
But
f_{+}(x)=1
for all

and, thus,
\nu_{f_{+}}
is the null measure. However, if the domain of

is extended to

for

then the induced signed measure is the one-point probability measure in

which coincides with the measure induced by the right-continuous version of

i.e.,
\nu_{g}=\nu_{g_{+}}=\delta_{\{0\}}\,,
compare Remark
2.3
(
d
).

For a signed measure

denote by

\displaystyle\nu_{f\circ(\phi_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\phi_{d}^{-1})}=\nu_{f}^{(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{d})}\,.

(42)

A.4
Characterization of measure-inducing functions

As one expects, measure-inducing functions and the concept of bounded variation in the sense of the Hardy and Krause are closely related because the integration by parts formula (
2
) for measure-inducing functions is related to integration by parts for Stieltjes integrals and because the convergence of the Stieltjes integrals on the compact domain
[0,1]^{d}
can be characterized through bounded Hardy-Krause variation, see
[
35
, Proposition 2]
). Since the domain
\Xi
of the functions
f
and
g
in formula (
2
) is not necessarily compact, the functions
f
and
g
may also have infinite Hardy-Krause variation. In the following, we characterize the classes
\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}(\Xi)
of measure-inducing functions with compact domain
\Xi=[0,1]^{d}
and with non-compact domain
\Xi=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
using the concept of Hardy-Krause variation. A characterization for general
\Xi
follows similarly with the transformation in Proposition
A.4
.

For the definition of the Hardy-Krause variation, we consider the domain
\Xi=[a,b]\,.

Then, for
j=1,\ldots,d\,,
let

\displaystyle a_{j}=x_{0}^{(j)}<x_{1}^{(j)}<\cdots<x_{m_{s}}^{(j)}=b_{j}

be a partition of
\Xi\,.
For
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

I\neq\emptyset\,,
denote by
\mathcal{P}^{I}
the partition of
\Xi_{I}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{I}:=\left\{[x_{l_{1}}^{(i_{1})},x_{l_{1}+1}^{(i_{1})}]\times\cdots\times[x_{l_{k}}^{(i_{k})},x_{l_{k}+1}^{(i_{k})}]\,,l_{s}=0,\ldots,m_{s}-1\,,s=1,\ldots,d\right\}\,.

(47)

Then for a function
f\colon\Xi\to\mathbb{R}\,,
the
Vitali variation
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var_{V}}(f):=\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\{1,\ldots,d\}}}\sum_{[x,y]\in\mathcal{P}^{\{1,\ldots,d\}}}\left|\Delta_{x,y}[f]\right|\,,

(48)

where
\Delta_{x,y}
is the difference operator in (
6
). The
Hardy-Krause variation
anchored at
a
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var_{HK}}(f):=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\atop{|I|}=k}\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{I}}\sum_{[x,y]\in\mathcal{P}^{I}}\left|\Delta_{x,y}[f_{I}]\right|

(49)

where the supremum is taken over all partitions
\mathcal{P}^{I}
of
\Xi_{I}
into axis-parallel boxes of the type in (
47
). Hence, the Hardy-Krause variation anchored at
a
is the sum of the Vitali variations of all lower
I
-marginal
f_{I}
of
f\,,

I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

I\neq\emptyset\,.
In the literature, the domain
\Xi
is often chosen as the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
and the Hardy-Krause variation is usually defined w.r.t. the upper
I
-marginals, i.e., anchored at
b=(1,\ldots,1)\,.

For an overview of properties of functions with bounded Hardy-Krause variation, see, e.g.,
[
5
,
28
]
.

The following result characterizes the class
\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}([0,1]^{d})
of measure-inducing functions on
[0,1]^{d}
for which also the lower or upper
I
-marginal functions are measure-inducing. Note that the induced signed measures are all finite because
[0,1]^{d}
is compact, see Remark
2.3
(
a
).

Theorem A.9

(Characterization of
\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}([0,1]^{d})
)
.

Assume that
f\colon[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is right-continuous and fulfils the continuity conditions (
11
) and (
12
). Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

f
has bounded Hardy-Krause variation,

(ii)

f\in\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}([0,1]^{d})\,,

(iii)

f\in\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,u}([0,1]^{d})\,.

In the sequel, we analyse the class of measure-inducing functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\,.

Since the signed measure induced by
f\in\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
is not necessarily finite and
f
may have infinite Hardy-Krause variation, a characterization of this class is more challenging.

As we will see in Theorem
A.12
, every right-continuous function in
\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
can be constructed by a linear combination of
\Delta
-monotone functions which are defined as follows. Recall that
\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{i}
is the difference operator defined by (
5
).

Definition A.10

(
d
-increasing,
\Delta
-monotone,
\Delta
-antitone function)
.

Let

be a function. Then,
f
is said to be

(i)

k
-increasing
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,
if for all
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{i_{k}}f(x)\geq 0

for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\Xi
and
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}>0
such that
x_{i_{j}}+\varepsilon_{j}\in\Xi_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\,,

(ii)

k
-decreasing
if
-f
is
k
-increasing,

(iii)

\Delta
-monotone
if
f
is
k
-increasing for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

(iv)

\Delta
-antitone
if
(-1)^{k}f
is
k
-increasing for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

(v)

if
\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{i}\Delta_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{j}f(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\Xi\,,

\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
1\leq i<j\leq d\,,

(vi)

directionally convex
if
\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{i}\Delta_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{j}f(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\Xi\,,

\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
1\leq i\leq j\leq d\,.

Note that, by definition, a
\Delta
-monotone/-antitone function is componentwise increasing/decreasing and, thus, its componentwise left- and right-hand limits exist. Further, any
\Delta
-antitone, any
\Delta
-monotone, and any directionally convex function is supermodular.

The following result characterizes measure-inducing functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
for which the marginal functions are not necessarily measure-inducing. Since at least one of the positive and negative part of a signed measure is finite, at least one of the
\Delta
-monotone and hence non-decreasing functions

is defined by (

denotes the Hardy-Krause variation defined by (

Proposition A.11

(Characterization of measure-inducing functions on

)
.

For a right-continuous function

(ii)

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

\Delta
-monotone functions

at least one of which is bounded, such that

\displaystyle g(0)

\displaystyle=h(0)=0\,,

(50)

\displaystyle F^{f}(x)

\displaystyle=g(x)-h(x)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var_{HK}}(F^{f}|_{[0,x]})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var_{HK}}(g|_{[0,x]})+\mathrm{Var_{HK}}(h|_{[0,x]})<\infty\penalty\

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\,,

(52)

\displaystyle\Delta_{x,y}[f]

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x,y}[g]-\Delta_{x,y}[h]\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\penalty\ \text{with }x\leq y\,.

(53)

(iv)

Further, the functions

Theorem A.12

(Characterization of the class
\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})

For a function

(ii)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(0)+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\atop I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}}\left[g_{(I)}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}})-h_{(I)}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}})\right]

Remark A.13

(a)

A.5
Survival functions

As the following example shows, care must be taken in the integration by parts formula (
2
), where the lower
I
-marginal of the survival function of
g
has to be determined and not the survival function of the lower
I
-marginal.

Example A.14

(
\overline{f^{I}}\neq\overline{f}^{I}\,,

\overline{f_{I}}\neq\overline{f}_{I}
)
.

Let
f\colon[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
be a bivariate copula, i.e.,
f
is a bivariate distribution function on
[0,1]^{2}
that is grounded and has uniform univariate marginals, so
f(u,0)=0\,,

f(0,v)=0\,,

f(u,1)=u
and
f(1,v)=v
for all
u,v\in[0,1]\,.
It holds that
\overline{f}(u,v)=f(u,v)-u-v+1\,.

(a)

For
I=\{1\}
it follows that
f^{\{1\}}(u)=f(u,1)=u
using the uniform marginal property. This yields
\overline{f^{\{1\}}}(u)=1-u\neq 0=\overline{f}(u,1)=\overline{f}^{\{1\}}(u)
for
u\in[0,1)\,.

(b)

For
I=\{1\}\,,
it follows that
f_{\{1\}}(u)=f(u,0)=0
using that
f
is grounded. This implies for
u\in[0,1)
that
\overline{f_{\{1\}}}(u)=0\neq 1-u=\overline{f}(u,0)=\overline{f}_{\{1\}}(u)\,.

The survival function of a grounded function has the following property, which is applied in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma A.15
.

Let
f\colon\Xi\to\mathbb{R}
be a function that is grounded and fulfils continuity condition (
11
). Then for
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,

I\neq\emptyset\,,
it holds that

\displaystyle\overline{f^{I}}=\overline{f}_{I}\,,

i.e., the survival function of the upper
I
-marginal of
f
coincides with the lower
I
-marginal of the survival function of
f\,.

A.6
Examples

In this section, we give various examples of functions that induce or do not induce a signed measure by (

[0,1]^{d}\,.
Then, we address the case of the non-compact domain
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\,.
Note that a measure-inducing function on
[0,1]^{d}
induces by (

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
may induce a non-finite signed measure, see Remark

(

.

A.6.1
Functions with domain
[0,1]^{d}

Denote by
\mathcal{C}^{n}(\Xi)
the class of functions on
\Xi
for which the (mixed) partial derivatives of order

As a consequence of Theorem

, the following result holds true.

Corollary A.16
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}^{d}([0,1]^{d})\,,
then
f\in\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,l}([0,1]^{d})\cap\mathcal{F}_{mi}^{c,u}([0,1]^{d})\,.

Example A.17

(Copulas, quasi-copulas, and semi-copulas)
.

(a)

Since
d
-variate copulas are
d
-increasing, every copula
C\colon[0,1]^{d}\to[0,1]
induces a probability measure
\nu
in the sense of (
9
). Since copulas are grounded on
[0,1]^{d}
, it holds in particular that
\nu([0,u])=C(u)
for all
u\in[0,1]^{d}
.
Similarly, by Sklar’s Theorem, every
d
-dimensional distribution function
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
induces in the sense of (
9
) a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
through
\mu((-\infty,x])=F(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.
Note that distribution functions and thus copulas are
\Delta
-monotone functions.

(b)

For
d\geq 2\,,
a quasi-copulas is in general not measure-inducing, see
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
for an example for
d=2\,.
As a three-dimensional example, the lower Fréchet bound
W^{3}\colon[0,1]^{3}\to[0,1]\,,

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\mapsto\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_{i}-2,0\right\}
is a proper quasi-copula and does not induce a signed measure, see
[

, Theorem 2.4]
. Hence,

is not the difference of two grounded
\Delta
-monotone functions, where one is bounded; cf. Theorems
A.9
and
A.12
.

The following example shows that a step function on
[0,1]^{2}
with a step on the main diagonal is measure-inducing. However, the reflected function with a step on the anti-diagonal of
[0,1]^{2}
does not induce a signed measure in the sense of Definition

.

Example A.18

(Mass on main diagonal/anti-diagonal)
.

Consider the functions

Then, as a consequence of Theorem
A.9
,

is measure-inducing because it has finite Hardy-Krause variation. But

is not measure-inducing because it has infinite Vitali variation.

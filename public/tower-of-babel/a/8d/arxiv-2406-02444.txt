Noise-adapted qudit codes for amplitude-damping noise
Sourav Dutta, Debjyoti Biswas, Prabha Mandayam

Abstract
Quantum error correction (QEC) plays a critical role in preventing information loss in quantum systems and provides a framework for reliable quantum computation. Identifying quantum codes with nice code parameters for physically motivated noise models remains an interesting challenge. While past work has primarily focused on qubit codes, here we identify a [ 4 , 1 ] [4,1] qudit error correcting code tailored to protect against amplitude-damping noise. We show that this four-qudit code satisfies the error correction conditions for all single-qudit and a few two-qudit damping errors up to the leading order in the damping parameter γ \gamma . We devise a protocol to extract syndromes that unambiguously identify this set of errors, leading to a noise-adapted recovery scheme that achieves a fidelity loss of 𝒪 ⁡ ( γ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2}) . For the d = 2 d=2 case, our QEC scheme is identical to the known example of the 4 4 -qubit code and the associated syndrome-based recovery. We also assess the performance of this code using the Petz recovery map and note some interesting deviations from the qubit case. Finally, we generalize this construction to a family of [ 2 ​ M + 2 , M ] [2M+2,M] qudit codes that can approximately correct all the single-qudit and a few two-qudit amplitude-damping errors.

II
Preliminaries

II.1
Approximate quantum error correction

Quantum states are fragile and susceptible to noise arising due to unwanted interactions with a bath or environment. The effect of noise can be modelled as a completely positive trace preserving (CPTP) map
\mathcal{E}
– commonly referred to as a
quantum channel
– whose action on any state
\rho
is described by a set of
Kraus operators

\{E_{k}\}
, such that
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\sum_{k}E_{k}\rho E_{k}^{\dagger}
. The map
\mathcal{E}
is guaranteed to be trace-preserving so long as the operators
E_{k}
satisfy
\sum_{k}E_{k}^{\dagger}E_{k}=I
.

The central idea in quantum error correction (QEC) is to encode the information contained in the physical Hilbert space into a subspace of a larger Hilbert space, known as the codespace. In order to be able to correct for a noise channel
\mathcal{E}
represented by a set of
N
Kraus operators
\{E_{k}\}_{a=1}^{N}
, the codespace must satisfy specific algebraic conditions known as the Knill-Laflamme conditions
[
39
]
, stated as follows. A codespace
\mathcal{C}
defined as the span of the quantum states
\{|i_{L}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{dim(\mathcal{C})}
corrects the errors associated with operators
{E_{k}}
if and only if,

\langle i_{L}|E_{k}^{\dagger}E_{l}|j_{L}\rangle=c_{kl}\delta_{ij}\qquad\forall~~k,l,

(1)

for every pair of codewords
|i_{L}\rangle,|j_{L}\rangle
. Here,
c_{kl}
are elements of a Hermitian matrix, and are independent of
i,j
. The independence of
c_{kl}
from
i
and
j
ensures that the errors are simply unitary deformations of the codespace, whereas the Kronecker delta in Eq. (
1
) implies that orthogonality of the error subspaces associated with distinct errors
[
40
]
.

More generally, one may consider relaxing the perfect QEC conditions, leading to approximate quantum error correction (AQEC) codes
[
15
,
41
,
17
]
. There are a few variants of AQEC conditions proposed in the literature, depending on how the exact QEC conditions are relaxed and the figure of merit used to benchmark the performace of the codes. The specific form of the AQEC conditions that we use here is due to
[
41
]
, where the Knill-Laflamme conditions in Eq. (
1
) are modified as follows. A quantum code
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

\langle i_{L}|E_{k}^{\dagger}E_{l}|j_{L}\rangle=c_{kl}\delta_{ij}+\langle i_{L}|B_{kl}|j_{L}\rangle\qquad\forall~~k,l,

(2)

for every pair of codewords
|i_{L}\rangle,|j_{L}\rangle
, can correct the errors
\{E_{k}\}
up to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{t})
if and only if the perturbative term in Eq. (
2
) is bounded as

\langle i_{L}|B_{kl}|j_{L}\rangle\leq\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{t+1}).

(3)

Clearly, the code
\mathcal{C}
becomes a
perfect
QEC code for the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
when
\langle i_{L}|B_{kl}|j_{L}\rangle
in (
2
) goes to zero. Indeed, if
\bra{i_L}B_{kl}\ket{j_L}
vanishes for all
i\neq j
, then the orthogonality condition in Eq. (
1
) is satisfied. In that case, there exists a trace non-increasing completely positive map, that can approximately correct the effect of the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
on the code space
\mathcal{C}

[
15
,
42
]
.

II.2
Amplitude-damping noise

The amplitude-damping (AD) noise channel models the phenomenon of energy dissipation in open quantum systems
[
40
]
. It is known to be one of the most commonly occurring and oftentimes dominant noise process for several physical realizations of qubits
[
43
]
.
AD errors arise due to the loss of energy from the system to its surrounding environment, leading to loss of coherence as the probability of finding the qubit in the excited state diminishes.
The strength of AD noise is characterized by
\gamma
, the probability of the qubit getting
damped
, that is, decaying from the ground state from the excited state. Correspondingly,
1-\gamma
is the probability that the qubit remains in the excited state.

At zero temperature, this damping process for qubits is characterized by the following pair of Kraus operators, which describe a single-qubit amplitude-damping channel.

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\end{pmatrix}\qquad A_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{\gamma}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

Going beyond qubits, energy dissipation in qudit systems (
d
-level quantum systems), can be described via a qudit amplitude-damping channel
\mathcal{A}
, with
d
Kraus operators
A_{k}
(
0\leq k\leq d-1
) of the form
[
36
]
,

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r=k}^{d-1}\,\sqrt{r\choose k}\sqrt{(1-\gamma)^{r-k}\gamma^{k}}\ket{r-k}\bra{r},

(5)

where
\gamma
is now the probability for a single damping event to occur. The quantum state
\ket{r}
represents a system in the
r^{\rm th}
excited state. For a
d
-level quantum system, the index
k
runs from
0
to
d-1
.
The operator
A_{k}
thus describes a
k
-level damping event that occurs with probability
\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{k})
. In the rest of the article, we will refer to
A_{k}
as a
k
-damping error with a corresponding damping strength of
\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{k})
.

A schematic representation of the qubit and qutrit amplitude-damping channels is shown in Fig.
1
. In the qutrit case, a single damping event causes the system to decay from the state
\ket{2}
to the state
\ket{1}
, without decaying any further. Thus the probability for this process is
\gamma(1-\gamma)
up to a constant factor. We can thus obtain the Kraus operators for a qutrit amplitude-damping channel by substituting
d=3
in Eq. (
5
), as,

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=\ket{0}\bra{0}+\sqrt{1-\gamma}\ket{1}\bra{1}+(1-\gamma)\ket{2}\bra{2}

(6)

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma}\ket{0}\bra{1}+\sqrt{2\gamma(1-\gamma)}\ket{1}\bra{2}

(7)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma\ket{0}\bra{2},

(8)

where
A_{0}
is the no-damping error,
A_{1}
corresponds to the single-damping error and
A_{2}
is the two-damping error operator.

Figure 1
:
(a) Amplitude-damping process for a qubit system, where only one possible damping from
\ket{1}
to
\ket{0}
takes place with probability
\gamma
. (b) Amplitude-damping process for a qutrit system, where damping from
\ket{2}
to
\ket{0}
takes place with probability
\gamma^{2}
. The probability of damping from
\ket{2}
to
\ket{1}
and
\ket{1}
to
\ket{0}
takes place with probability
2\gamma(1-\gamma)
and
\gamma
respectively.

In this work, we aim to construct quantum codes that can protect qudits from single and multi-level damping errors. It is evident that whenever
\gamma<0.5
, which holds true for most of the realistic cases, the probability of no-damping and single-level damping is higher than multi-level damping. This implies that the single-level damping errors affect the fidelity of a quantum code the most.

II.3
Noise-adapted recovery

Before proceeding to the code construction, we briefly review the known noise-adapted recovery schemes tailored to specific codes and noise processes. In standard QEC, the recovery operation is simply a unitary gate – typically, a Pauli operator – depending on the syndrome bits extracted. On the other hand, noise-adapted QEC requires a non-trivial recovery operation, which could, in general, be a quantum channel. Given a code
\mathcal{C}
and a noise channel
\mathcal{E}
, there are three known approaches to constructing a noise-adapted recovery scheme.

1.

Petz recovery:
This approach to noise-adapted recovery is based on a universal and near-optimal recovery channel, often known as the Petz map
[
16
,
17
]
. The Petz recovery specific to a code
\mathcal{C}
and adapted to a noise channel
\mathcal{E}
with Kraus operators
\{E_{i}\}^{N}_{k=1}
, is the CPTP map
\mathcal{R}_{P}
whose Kraus operators are given by,

\displaystyle R^{(P)}_{k}=PE^{{\dagger}}_{k}\mathcal{E}(P)^{-1/2},

(9)

where
P
is the projector onto the code space
\mathcal{C}
. Recent works
[
44
,
45
]
have shown that it is possible to construct circuit implementations of the Petz recovery channel for arbitrary codes and noise processes.

2.

Leung recovery:
For a quantum code
\mathcal{C}
and a noise channel
\mathcal{E}
with Kraus operators
\{E_{i}\}^{N}_{k=1}
, the recovery map due to Leung
et al..

[
15
]
is constructed using the following set of Kraus operators.

R^{(L)}_{k}=PU_{k}^{{\dagger}},

(10)

where
U_{k}
is the unitary operator obtained via polar decomposition of the operator
E_{k}P
and
P
is the projector onto the codespace. The map
\mathcal{R}_{L}
is trace non-increasing provided the error operators map the codespace to orthogonal subspaces. This means that an additional constraint
\bra{i_L}B_{kl}\ket{j_L}\propto\delta_{kl}
should be imposed on the approximate QEC conditions in Eq. (
2
) for
\mathcal{R}_{L}
to correspond to a physically realisable recovery map. Putting in this additional orthogonality constraint, it is easy to check that the set of operators
\{R^{(L)}_{k}\}
along with the additional operator
P_{E}=I-\sum\limits_{k}R^{(L){\dagger}}_{k}R^{(L)}_{k}
define a CPTP map corresponding to a valid recovery channel.

3.

Cafaro recovery:
Given a code
\mathcal{C}
and a noise channel
\mathcal{E}
with Kraus operators
\{E_{k}\}
, the recovery map due to Cafaro
et al..

[
42
]
is defined by the Kraus operators,

\displaystyle R^{(C)}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i}\frac{\ket{i_L}\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}}{\sqrt{\bra{i_l}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}\ket{i_L}}}.

(11)

This map is trace non-increasing if and only if
\bra{i_L}B_{kl}\ket{j_L}\propto\delta_{kl}\delta_{ij}
in Eq. (
2
). Note that this is a slightly stronger condition than the one required for the Leung recovery, since it has the additional
\delta_{ij}
term. Similar to the Leung case, once the orthogonality condition is satisfied, the full CPTP recovery map is defined as
\{R^{(C)}_{k},I-\sum\limits_{k}R^{(C){\dagger}}_{k}R^{(C)}_{k}\}
.

We can thus establish a hierarchy of noise-adapted recovery maps based on the constraints necessary to ensure that the maps are physical (completely positive and trace-preserving). The Petz recovery map in Eq. (
9
) is a physical map for any noise channel
\mathcal{E}
and code
\mathcal{C}
. On the other hand, the recovery maps defined using the operators in Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
) are physical if and only if the corresponding code and noise channel satisfy certain algebraic conditions. Furthermore, these two recovery maps turn out to be identical for codes and noise channels satisfying a specific form of the approximate QEC condition. This is formally stated in the following Lemma and proved in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 1

Consider a noise channel
\mathcal{E}
and codespace
\mathcal{C}
with codewords
\{|i_{L}\rangle,i=1,2,\ldots,d\}
that satisfy the following form of the AQEC conditions,

\langle i_{L}|E_{k}^{\dagger}E_{l}|j_{L}\rangle=\left(c_{kk}\delta_{ij}+\langle i_{L}|B_{kk}|j_{L}\rangle\right)\delta_{kl},

(12)

for every pair of Kraus operators
E_{k},E_{l}
. Then, the Leung recovery in Eq. (
10
) and the Cafaro recovery in Eq. (
11
) corresponding to this code and noise channel are identical if and only if,

\langle i_{L}|E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}|j_{L}\rangle\propto\delta_{ij},\;\forall,i,j\in[1,d].

(13)

II.4
Fidelity measures

The performance of a QEC protocol is typically quantified by one of the fidelity measures that quantify how close the initial encoded state is to the recovered state. Here, we work with the fidelity function
F
based on the Bures metric, which is defined between a pure state
|\psi\rangle
and a mixed state
\rho
as
[
40
]
,

F(|\psi\rangle,\rho)=\langle\psi|\rho|\psi\rangle.

(14)

Since the fidelity is a function of the initial state, one may quantify the performance of a QEC scheme over the entire codespace is using the
worst-case fidelity
, which is obtained by minimising the fidelity over all possible logical states. The worst-case fidelity for a noise channel
\mathcal{E}
and recovery channel
\mathcal{R}
acting on a codespace
\mathcal{C}
is defined as
[
40
]

\mathcal{F}_{wc}=\min_{\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}}\bra{\psi}\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E}(\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi})\ket{\psi}

(15)

Alternately, one could quantify the performance of a QEC code is by using the entanglement fidelity, defined as
[
40
]
,

\mathcal{F}_{ent}=\bra{\Psi}(\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})\otimes I(\ket{\Psi}\bra{\Psi})\ket{\Psi},

(16)

where
\ket{\Psi}
is a purification of the maximally mixed state on the codespace
\rho_{L}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{i_L}\bra{i_L}
.
By evaluating the right hand side of Eq. (
16
), we get the following useful expression for the entanglement fidelity.

\mathcal{F}_{ent}=\frac{1}{(\textrm{dim}(\mathcal{C}))^{2}}\sum_{jk}\left(|\Tr_{\mathcal{C}}R_{j}E_{k}|^{2}\right).

(17)

Since the state
\ket{\Psi}
is a purification of the maximally mixed state
\rho_{L}
on the codespace, one may view the entanglement fidelity as a measure of the entanglement-preserving capability of a given QEC scheme.

Approximate four-qudit code

We first identify an approximate four-qu
d
it quantum error correcting code for arbitrary values of
d
, which can correct all single-qudit and a few multi-qudit errors. To correct the errors due to the noise models discussed in Sec.
II.2
, the
4
-qudit codespace is constructed as the span of a set of
d
codewords, defined as follows.

\displaystyle\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}\,\ket{i}_{1}\ket{i}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{4}.\

(18)

Here,
m\in\{0,d-1\}
and the subscript
d
indicates addition modulo
d
.

Figure 2
:
Encoding circuit for the approximate four-qudit code. The controlled-
X_{d}
gate is defined in Eq. (
21
) and
\ket{+_d}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}[\ket{0}+\ket{1}+\cdots+\ket{d-1}]

.

The encoding circuit for this qudit code is shown in Fig.
2
. The gate
X_{d}
in the encoding circuit is simply the Pauli
X
operator on a qudit system, given by,

X_{d}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\ket{(k+1)_d}\bra{k}.

(19)

This is simply a shift operator on the discrete set of states
\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle,\ldots,|d-1\rangle\}
. Correspondingly, the phase operator on a qudit system is defined as,

Z_{d}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{d}}\ket{k}\bra{k}.

(20)

The controlled-
X_{d}
gate, which is the
d
-dimensional analogue of the
cnot
gate is then defined as,

\displaystyle CX_{d}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{d}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mathbf{X}_{d}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathbf{X}^{d-1}_{d}\end{bmatrix}

(21)

Also, the state
\ket{+_d}
is simply an equal superposition of the single-qudit basis states in the standard or computational basis.

From Eq. (
18
), it is evident that for
d=2
, this code exactly matches the four-qubit code
[
15
]
, tailored for qubit amplitude-damping noise.

\displaystyle\ket{0_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\ket{0000}+\ket{1111}]

\displaystyle\ket{1_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\ket{0011}+\ket{1100}]

(22)

For
d\geq 3
, this code construction yields code parameters that have not been explicitly studied in the context of the amplitude-damping channel
[
36
]
in previous constructions of stabilizer qudit codes. We note in passing that there are
interesting connections between the
[4,1]_{d}
code and the
distance-two surface codes without periodic boundary conditions. This connection is briefly explained in Appendix
B
. Finally, we note that the code in Eq. (
18
) can be realised as a subcode of the
[[4,2,2]]_{G}
group-qudit code

]
, where the group
G
in our case is
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.

We now show that similar to the
4
-qubit code, this

Specifically, we show that the codewords defined in Eq. (

) satisfy the approximate QEC conditions in Eq. (

) for all single-qudit and some two-qudit errors, with the perturbation terms in Eq. (

) being of

Theorem 1

The four-qudit code defined as the span of the codewords in Eq. (

) up to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\rm corr}

\displaystyle\{\{A_{x000},A_{0x00},A_{00x0},A_{000x}\;x\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\},

\displaystyle A_{0000},A_{1010},A_{0101},A_{0110},A_{1001}\},

comprising

denotes a single-qudit

Proof:

We merely outline the steps of our proof here and leave the details in the Appendix
C
.

To verify that the
4
-qudit code satisfies the Knill-Laflamme conditions up to
\mathcal{O}(\gamma)
, we first explicitly evaluate the action of the single-qudit damping errors on the codewords, as given in Eqs. (
49
)- (
52
). It is then easy to see by inspection that different single-qudit errors map each of the codewords to distinct, orthogonal states. Specifically, we show that the codewords in Eq. (
18
) satisfy,

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A^{\dagger}_{x000}A_{000y}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\delta_{m,n}\delta_{x,0}\delta_{y,0}

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A^{\dagger}_{x000}A_{y000}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{x}(d)\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{x})\delta_{m,n}\delta_{x,y}

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A_{0000}^{{\dagger}}A_{0000}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-2(d-1)\gamma+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2}))\delta_{m,n}.

Furthermore, we show by explicit calculation that the similar orthogonality conditions hold true for the two-qudit errors in the set
\mathcal{A}_{\rm corr}
.

From Eqs. (

Having verified that the
4

Recall that the quantum Singleton bound gives us an upper bound on the number of qudits needed to protect a certain number of qudits from arbitrary errors. The formula that determines the Singleton bound for an
[[n,k,D]]_{d}

\displaystyle{n-k\leq 2(D-1)}.

(39)

We set

and

to be
1
to know the minimum number of qudits required to protect a single logical qudit from any single-qudit physical error.
It follows from the bound that we need a minimum of five qudits to protect one qudit.

Finally, we show that we can extend the
[4,1]_{d}

[(2M+2),M]_{d}

[4,1]_{d}
noise-adapted qudit code discussed in the previous sections. A similar extension of the

[(2M+2),M]_{2}

[

.

The stabilizer generators for the

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}=\langle X_{d}&{}^{\otimes 2M+2},Z_{d}^{d-s}Z_{d}^{s}I_{d}^{\otimes 2M},I_{d}^{\otimes 2}Z_{d}^{d-s}Z_{d}^{s}I_{d}^{\otimes 2M-2},\\
\cdots,&I_{d}^{\otimes 2M-2}Z_{d}^{d-s}Z_{d}^{s}I_{d}^{\otimes 2},I_{d}^{\otimes 2M+2}Z_{d}^{d-s}Z_{d}^{s}\rangle.\end{split}

(40)

Here,
X_{d}
and
Z_{d}
are the qudit Pauli operators defined in Eqs. (
19
) and (
20
).
There is a single
X-
type stabilizer and
M+1
Z-type stabilizers in the stabilizer generators, enabling it to encode
M
qudits.
The value of

in Eq. (

) is chosen such that

is co-prime with

.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ket{m_1,m_2, \cdots, m_M}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{i}_{1}\ket{i}_{2}\ket{i+m_1}_{3}\ket{i+m_1}_{4}\\

To correct all the single-qudit AD errors, we first need to perform a set of measurements leading to the secondary syndromes.

M+1
. The structure of these secondary-syndrome measurement operators depends on the local dimension of the qudit.
When

Z

VII
Summary and Future Directions

In this work, we describe a four-qudit quantum error correcting code tailored to protect against amplitude-damping noise. We show that this code satisfies a special form of the approximate QEC conditions with the deviation term being of
\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2})
where
\gamma
is the probability of a single jump or single-damping event.
Apart from the stabilizer generators, we identify a set of additional measurements that are required to realize a noise-adapted recovery scheme that corrects for all single-qudit and some two-qudit errors, thus achieving the desired order (
\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2})
) of fidelity.

This code includes the well-known
4
-qubit amplitude-damping code
[
15
]
as a special case. Similar to the
4
-qubit case
[
18
,
37
]
, we see that the syndrome-based recovery for the
4
-qutrit and
4
-quqad codes also requires a two-step syndrome extraction procedure with two primary and two secondary syndrome measurements.

However, for
d\geq 5
, we do not need any secondary syndromes to distinguish the single-qudit errors of damping strength up to
\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2})
, so the number of syndrome measurements does not grow with the local dimension of the code.

To benchmark the performance of the syndrome-based recovery, we consider the specific case of the
4
-qutrit code and calculate both the wort-case and entanglement fidelities for this case. We then compare these fidelities with those achieved by the Petz recovery map for the same code and noise channel. Interestingly, in a departure from the qubit case, we find that the syndrome-based recovery method yields higher fidelities than the Petz recovery. We believe that this trend should hold true for higher dimensions as well, since for all
d\geq 3
, this class of codes corrects some subset of two-qudit errors in addition to the single-qudit errors.

Finally, we have generalized the
4
-qudit code to a family of
[(2M+2),M]
qudit codes, which are capable of correcting all single-qudit and a few two-qudit AD errors with similar secondary syndrome measurements as the four-qudit code.

Going forward, this
4
-qudit code and the associated syndrome-based recovery presented here could provide a promising avenue for exploring fault-tolerant quantum computation using qudits that predominantly undergo amplitude-damping noise. We know from the work of Akshaya
et al..

[
37
]
that the
4
-qubit code can be used to achieve fault tolerance against amplitude-damping noise, with a finite pseudothreshold in the noise strength
\gamma
. It would, therefore,
be interesting to study how such a pseudothreshold scales
with the local system dimension
d
, using the codes and the
syndrome-based recovery discussed for the
[4,1]_{d}
code. We
can also study if a similar fault-tolerance threshold exists for
the
[2M+2,M]_{d}
code and how such a threshold may scale
with
M
and
d
.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
1

Given a code with codewords
\{|i_{L}\rangle,i=1,\ldots,d\}
, and a set of Kraus operators
\{E_{k}\}
that satisfy Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
), we first put the two conditions together to get the following form of the AQEC conditions

\langle i_{L}|E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{l}|j_{L}\rangle=\left(c_{kk}+\langle i_{L}|B_{kk}|j_{L}\rangle\right)\delta_{kl}\delta_{ij}.

(44)

Polar decomposition of the Kraus operators on the codespace leads to unitaries
U_{k}
, given by

\displaystyle E_{k}P

\displaystyle=U_{k}\sqrt{PE_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}P}=U_{k}P\sqrt{PE_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}P}

(45)

It follows from the orthogonality in the
(k,l)
indices in Eq. (
44
) that the unitaries
\{U_{k}\}
satisfy,

\displaystyle PU_{k}^{{\dagger}}U_{l}P

\displaystyle\propto\delta_{kl}P

The recovery due to Leung
et al.
is then defined via the operators
R^{(L)}_{k}=PU_{k}^{\dagger}
. This can be rewritten as,

\displaystyle R_{k}^{(L)}

\displaystyle=PU_{k}^{{\dagger}}=\left(\sqrt{PE_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}P}\right)^{-1}PE_{k}^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\ket{i_L}\bra{j_L}\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}\ket{j_L}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}PE_{k}^{{\dagger}}.

(46)

Now, if we also invoke the orthogonality in the codeword indices
(i,j)
, we see that the Leung
et al.
recovery operators can be written as,

R_{k}^{(L)}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{\ket{i_L}\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}}{\sqrt{\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}\ket{i_L}}},

(47)

where we have used
\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}\ket{j_L}=\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}\ket{i_L}\delta_{ij}
. Recalling the definition of the Cafaro
et al.
recovery operators
R_{k}^{(C)}
from Eq. (
11
), we see that the R.H.S. of Eq. (
47
) is indeed the same as
R_{k}^{(C)}
, showing that the two recovery maps are indeed identical.

Conversely, for a set of codewords
\{|i_{L}\rangle\}
and Kraus operators
\{E_{k}\}
for which the two set of recovery operators
\{R_{k}^{(L)}\}
and
\{R_{k}^{(C)}\}
are identical, we must have,

R_{k}^{(L)}=PU_{k}^{\dagger}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{\ket{i_L}\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}}{\sqrt{\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}\ket{i_L}}}.

From the definition of the unitaries
\{U_{k}\}
in Eq. (
46
), we see that this is true only if
\bra{i_L}E_{k}^{{\dagger}}E_{k}\ket{j_L}\propto\delta_{ij}
, thus proving Lemma
1
.

Appendix B
Connection with distance-two qudit surface code

Here, we point out an interesting connection between the
4
-qudit code defined in Eq. (
18
) and a certain class of surface codes. The surface code is a variant of Kitaev’s toric code
[
50
]
and belongs to the class of topological quantum codes defined on a lattice, where the qubits are placed on the edges of the lattice, and the stabilizer generators are defined using plaquette and vertex operators.
Surface codes
[
10
]
are toric codes without periodic boundary conditions and have a similar stabilizer structure.

It is known that the
4
-qubit code that corrects for single amplitude-damping errors can be mapped to one of the distance-
2
qubit surface codes
[
51
]
. We now show that a similar connection exists between the
4
-qudit codes for amplitude-damping and one of the distance-
2
qudit surface codes.

The distance-2 qudit surface code can be defined using four different sets of stabilizer generators, namely,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{S}_{a}=\{X_{d}X_{d}X_{d}X_{d},Z_{d}Z^{-1}I_{d}I_{d},I_{d}I_{d}Z_{d}Z^{-1}\},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{b}=\{X_{d}X^{-1}_{d}X_{d}X^{-1}_{d},Z_{d}Z_{d}I_{d}I_{d},I_{d}I_{d}Z_{d}Z_{d}\},\\
&\mathcal{S}_{c}=\{Z_{d}Z_{d}Z_{d}Z_{d},X_{d}X_{d}^{-1}I_{d}I_{d},I_{d}I_{d}X_{d}X^{-1}_{d}\},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{d}=\{Z_{d}Z^{-1}_{d}Z_{d}Z^{-1}_{d},X_{d}X_{d}I_{d}I_{d},I_{d}I_{d}X_{d}X_{d}\},\end{split}

(48)

each leading to a different set of codewords. All four sets of stabilizers can detect any single-qudit Pauli error. However, when the noise is non-Pauli, the performance differs for different codes. We first note that the
4
-qudit codewords in Eq. (
18
) are stabilized by the generators of form
\mathcal{S}_{a}
. Furthermore, we can explicitly write down the codewords stabilized by
\mathcal{S}_{b}
as,

\displaystyle\ket{m_L}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{i}_{1}\ket{((d-1)i)_d}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{3}\ket{((d-1)(i+m))_d}_{4},

for all
m\in[0,d-1]
. It can be easily checked that the codewords in Eq. (
B
) satisfy the Knill-Laflamme conditions in Eq. (
1
) up to the first order in damping strength
\gamma
, similar to the code in Eq. (
18
). Thus, we see that codes with stabilizer generators
\mathcal{S}_{a}
and
\mathcal{S}_{b}
are indeed amplitude-damping QEC codes, correcting up to first order in noise strength.

A similar exercise shows that the codewords corresponding to the stabilizers in the sets
\mathcal{S}_{c}
and
\mathcal{S}_{d}
do not satisfy the Knill-Laflamme conditions even up to the first order of damping strength for the qudit amplitude-damping channel. Thus, the distance-
2
stabilizer codes associated with the generators in
\mathcal{S}_{c}
and
\mathcal{S}_{d}
do not correspond to noise-adapted codes for amplitude-damping noise.
Finally, one important point to note is that even for the codewords associated with the sets
\mathcal{S}_{a}
and
\mathcal{S}_{b}
, the syndromes measured by these stabilizer generators cannot distinguish all the correctable amplitude-damping errors. Hence, additional measurements are required to determine the exact error that occurred, as described in Sec.
IV
of the main text.

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem
1

The errors on the four-qudit system have the form
A_{ijkl}=A_{i}\otimes A_{j}\otimes A_{k}\otimes A_{l}
where the error
A_{ijkl}
occurs with probability
\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{i+j+k+l})
. Consider the action of the single-qudit errors in the set
\mathcal{A}_{\rm corr}
on the codewords defined in Eq. (
18
).
For all
m,x\in[0,d-1]
, we note that,

\displaystyle A_{x000}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=x}^{d-1}\sqrt{\binom{i}{x}}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x}{2}}\ket{i-x}_{1}\ket{i}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{4}

(49)

\displaystyle A_{0x00}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=x}^{d-1}\sqrt{\binom{i}{x}}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x}{2}}\ket{i}_{1}\ket{i-x}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{4}

(50)

\displaystyle A_{00x0}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{(i+m)_{d}=x}^{d-1}\sqrt{\binom{(i+m)_{d}}{x}}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x}{2}}\ket{i}_{1}\ket{i}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d-x}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{4}

(51)

\displaystyle A_{000x}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{(i+m)_{d}=x}^{d-1}\sqrt{\binom{(i+m)_{d}}{x}}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x}{2}}\ket{i}_{1}\ket{i}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d-x}_{4}.

(52)

We can now prove that the class of
4
-qudit codes studied here satisfy the Knill-Laflamme condition for error correction up to
\mathcal{O}({\gamma})
by taking the inner products of the states in Eqs. (
49
) through (
52
).

We first consider a pair of single-qudit damping errors on different qudit locations.

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A^{\dagger}_{x000}A_{000y}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{i=x,(j+n)_{d}=y}^{d-1}\sqrt{{i\choose x}{(j+n)_{d}\choose y}}(1-\gamma)^{i+j+(i+m)_{d}+(j+n)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{2}}\delta_{i-x,j}\delta_{i,j}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(j+n)_{d}}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(j+n)_{d}-y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{i=x}^{d-1}\sqrt{{i\choose x}{(i+n)_{d}\choose y}}(1-\gamma)^{2i+2(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{2}}\delta_{i-x,i}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(i+n)_{d}}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(i+n)_{d}-y}

(53)

As
\delta_{i-x,i}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(i+n)_{d}}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(i+n)_{d}-y}=\delta_{m,n}\delta_{x,0}\delta_{y,0}
, the R.H.S of the Eq. (
53
) is non-zero if and only if
m=n
and
x=y=0
.
Therefore, single-qudit errors occurring in different locations map the codewords in Eq. (
18
) to mutually orthogonal states.

Next, we consider pairs of single-qudit errors occurring in the same location.

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A_{x000}^{{\dagger}}A_{y000}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=x,j=y}^{d-1}\sqrt{\binom{i}{x}\binom{j}{y}}(1-\gamma)^{i+j+(i+m)_{d}+(j+n)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{2}}\delta_{i-x,j-y}\delta_{i,j}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(j+n)_{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=x}^{d-1}\sqrt{\binom{i}{x}\binom{i}{y}}(1-\gamma)^{2i+(i+m)_{d}+(i+n)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{x+y}{2}}\delta_{i-x,i-y}\delta_{(i+m)_{d},(i+n)_{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=x}^{d-1}\binom{i}{x}(1-\gamma)^{2i+2(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{x}\delta_{x,y}\delta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=x}^{d-1}\gamma^{x}{i\choose x}\left(1-(p-x)\gamma+(\frac{p(p-1)}{2}+\frac{x(x-1)}{2}-xp)\gamma^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})\right)\delta_{x,y}\delta_{m,n}.

(54)

where,
p=2i+2(i+m)_{d}
. The presence of
\delta_{x,y}
and
\delta_{m,n}
in Eq. (
C
) confirms again that different single-qudit errors occurring at the same location also map the codewords in Eq. (
18
) to orthogonal states.

For the cases when
x=y
in Eq. (
C
), we now calculate the coefficients of different orders of
\gamma
, as follows.

•

The no-damping error operator
A_{0000}
(
x=0
)

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A_{0000}^{{\dagger}}A_{0000}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\left(1-p\gamma+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\gamma^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})\right)\delta_{m,n}

(55)

\displaystyle=\left(1-2(d-1)\gamma+\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(i+(i+m)_{d})(2i+2(i+m)_{d}-1)\gamma^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})\right)\delta_{m,n}

•

The single-qudit, single-damping error operator
A_{1000}
(
x=1
)

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A_{1000}^{{\dagger}}A_{10000}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\gamma i\left(1-(p-1)\gamma+(p+\frac{p(p-1)}{2})\gamma^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})\right)\delta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\gamma+\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}i(1-2i-2(i+m)_{d})\gamma^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})\right)\delta_{m,n}

(56)

•

The single-qudit two-damping error operator
A_{2000}
(
x=2
)

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A_{2000}^{{\dagger}}A_{20000}\ket{n_L}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}i(i-1)\gamma^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})\right)\delta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{6}(d-1)(d-2)\gamma^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})\right)\delta_{m,n}

(57)

•

For single-qudit errors with a higher degree of damping, that is,
A_{x000}
with
x\geq 3
, we get,

\bra{m_L}A_{x000}^{{\dagger}}A_{x0000}\ket{n_L}=\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{x})\delta_{m,n}.

Therefore, we see that the coefficient of
\gamma
is independent of
m
, whereas the coefficient of
\gamma^{2}
depends on the value of
m
for
x\leq 2
. This shows that the codewords in Eq. (
18
) satisfy the Knill-Laflamme conditions up to
\mathcal{O}(\gamma)
for all the single-qudit damping errors.

Finally, we consider the two-qudit errors
\{A_{1010},A_{1001},A_{0101},A_{0110}\}
in the set
\mathcal{A}_{\rm corr}
. The action of these errors on the codewords is given by,

\displaystyle A_{1010}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\Theta[(i+m)_{d}-1]i(i+m)_{d}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\ket{i-1}_{1}\ket{i}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d-1}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{4}

(58)

\displaystyle A_{1001}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\Theta[(i+m)_{d}-1]i(i+m)_{d}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\ket{i-1}_{1}\ket{i}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d-1}_{4}

(59)

\displaystyle A_{0101}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\Theta[(i+m)_{d}-1]i(i+m)_{d}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\ket{i}_{1}\ket{i-1}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d-1}_{4}

(60)

\displaystyle A_{0110}\ket{m_L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\Theta[(i+m)_{d}-1]i(i+m)_{d}(1-\gamma)^{i+(i+m)_{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\ket{i}_{1}\ket{i-1}_{2}\ket{(i+m)_d-1}_{3}\ket{(i+m)_d}_{4}

(61)

Here,

It is evident that all the noisy states in Eqs. (

) are orthogonal to each other. Furthermore, it is easy to check that the noisy states in Eqs. (

) are also orthogonal to the noisy states in Eqs. (

Now, we consider the following inner product, where

\displaystyle\bra{m_L}A_{1010}^{{\dagger}}A_{1010}\ket{n_L}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\Theta[(i+m)_{d}-1]i^{2}\gamma^{2}(i+m)_{d}^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2i+2(i+m)_{d}-2}\delta_{m,n}

(62)

We get identical expressions for the other two-qudit errors as well. The

\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2})
, and its coefficient does depend on the codewords.

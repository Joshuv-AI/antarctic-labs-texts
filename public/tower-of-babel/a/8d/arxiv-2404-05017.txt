Abstract
. It is shown that the duals of several categories of topological flavour, like the categories of ordered sets, generalised metric spaces, probabilistic metric spaces, topological spaces, approach spaces, are quasivarieties, presenting a common proof for all such results.

Introduction

It is known since the work of Manes
[
18
]
that the category
\mathsf{CompHaus}
of compact Hausdorff spaces and continuous maps, traditionally belonging to the realm of topology, can be also understood as a variety of algebras (without rank). Approximately at the same time, Duskin
[
10
]
pointed out that also the dual category of
\mathsf{CompHaus}
is a variety: by Urysohn’s Lemma, the unit interval
[0,1]
is a regular injective regular cogenerator of
\mathsf{CompHaus}
; moreover
\mathsf{CompHaus}^{{\rm op}}
is exact (see
[
5
, Corollary 1.11 of Chapter 9]
). On the other hand, the category
\mathsf{Top}
of topological spaces and continuous maps is probably “as far as one can get” from algebra, so it might come “as some surprise … that the situation is quite different when it comes to the dual category”
[
4
]
. In fact, once suspected, it is not hard to see from an abstract point of view that
\mathsf{Top}^{{\rm op}}
is a quasivariety:
\mathsf{Top}
is (co)complete, and the space
3
— an amalgamation of the Sierpinski space and the two-element indiscrete space — is a regular injective regular cogenerator of
\mathsf{Top}
. It is also noted that
\mathsf{Top}^{{\rm op}}
is not exact. In concrete terms,
[
4
]
gives an explicit description of the corresponding algebraic theory. Later, in
[
1
]
, Adámek and Pedicchio described
\mathsf{Top}^{\rm op}
as a regular epireflective subcategory of the variety of topological systems. Later, in
[
20
]
Pedicchio and Wood pointed out that analogous arguments show that the dual of the category
\mathsf{Ord}
of (pre)ordered sets and monotone maps is a quasivariety. Here we use the ideas of Adámek and Pedicchio
[
1
]
, namely the equivalence between the category of topological systems and a special comma category, and results of Barr
[
3
]
, to give a common proof that a variety of categories of topological nature have quasivarieties as duals.

1.
The global construction

We recall that a
varietal category

\mathsf{Set}
, while a

is a regular epireflective (full) subcategory of a variety, or, equivalently, a (full) subcategory closed under subobjects and products. (

.

A functor

is a monadic functor provided that:

(1)

\mathsf{A}
has coequalizers of

(2)

creates limits and every
H
-split pair is a split pair;

(3)

G
creates limits, and coequalizers of

and are preserved by

(4)

F
creates limits and preserves all coequalizers;

(5)

has a left adjoint.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
G\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{Set}
and
H\colon\mathsf{B}\to\mathsf{Set}
be monadic functors and
I\colon\mathsf{B}\to\mathsf{A}
be a right adjoint functor. Then the composite functor

\mathsf{A}\mathrel{\downarrow}I\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptF\hskip 8.19447pt}\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptG\times H\hskip 8.19447pt}\mathsf{Set}\times\mathsf{Set}

is monadic.

Proof.

First note that with
G\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{Set}
and
H\colon\mathsf{B}\to\mathsf{Set}
also
G\times H\colon\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B}\to\mathsf{Set}\times\mathsf{Set}
is monadic. As
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
are cocomplete,
\mathsf{A}\downarrow I
is a cocomplete category. By Lemma
1.1
, the functor
F\colon\mathsf{A}\downarrow I\to\mathsf{A}\times B
has both a left and a right adjoint. Therefore
F
preserves both limits and colimits. Since
I
preserves limits, one easily verifies that
F
creates limits. Therefore the assertion follows from Proposition
1.3
.
∎

Remark 1.5
.

Recall that every concrete functor
I\colon\mathsf{B}\to\mathsf{A}
between varieties has a left adjoint. Hence, in this situation, we can think of
\mathsf{A}\mathrel{\downarrow}I
as a category of two sorted algebras: with sorts
A
and
B
, with the algebraic theory of

at sort
A
and of

at sort

, and an operation of type

together with the equations identifying this operation as a homomorphism.

If

Theorem 1.7
.

If
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}

is monadic over

.

, we obtain immediately that the composite

Theorem 1.8
.

If
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
are varieties admitting constants and

is a right adjoint functor, then
\mathsf{A}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\rotatebox{-90.0}{$\rightarrowtail$}}}}I
is a quasivariety over
\mathsf{Set}
.

In the remaining sections we will show that the results of this section can be used to deduce that several categories – dual to categories of topological nature – are quasivarietal.

2.
Diers’ affine algebraic sets

Motivated by questions in geometry, the notion of affine set was introduced by Diers in
[
8
,
9
]
. Among other results, the author proves that the category of affine sets is topological over
\mathsf{Set}
, and that, moreover, many categories studied in topology are instances of this notion. Using the results of Section
1
, it is easy to deduce that the dual of the category of affine sets is a quasivariety, as we explain next.

Given a variety
\mathsf{A}
and an object
A
of
\mathsf{A}
, an

is a set
X
equipped with a subalgebra
S
of the algebra
A^{X}
. A morphism of affine sets

is a map

such that
A^{f}\colon A^{Y}\to A^{X}
(co)restricts to

.

The category
\mathsf{AfSet}(A)
of affine sets and their morphisms admits a canonical closure operator, called
Zariski-closure
: For an affine set
(X,S)
and
M\subseteq X
, the Zariski-closure of
M
is given by

\overline{M}=\{\mathrm{Eq}(\varphi,\psi)\mid\varphi,\psi\in S,M\subseteq\mathrm{Eq}(\varphi,\psi)\}.

An affine set
(X,S)
is called

whenever the cone
S
is point-separating, that is, for all
x\neq y
in
X
there is some
\varphi\in S
with
\varphi(x)\neq\varphi(y)
, and it is called
algebraic
if it is separated and Zariski-closed in every separated affine set. The full subcategory of
\mathsf{AfSet}(A)
defined by all algebraic affine sets is denoted by
\mathsf{AfAlgSet}(A)
. It is shown in
[
8
,
9
]
that
\mathsf{AfAlgSet}(A)
is dually equivalent to the category of
functional algebras
over
A
,

\mathsf{AfAlgSet}(A)^{{\rm op}}\simeq\mathsf{FcAlg}(A);

here
\mathsf{FcAlg}(A)
is the full subcategory of
\mathsf{A}
defined by those algebras which are subalgebras of powers of
A
. The category
\mathsf{FcAlg}(A)
is a regular-epireflective subcategory of
\mathsf{A}
, hence a quasivariety over
\mathsf{Set}
, which identifies
\mathsf{AfAlgSet}(A)^{{\rm op}}
as a quasivariety.

Using the results of Section
1
it is easy to deduce that also the dual of
\mathsf{AfSet}(A)
is a (two-sorted) quasivariety. Indeed:

(1)

The functor

, which assigns to each

the algebra

and to each map

the morphism

with

, has as left adjoint the hom-functor

.

(2)

Both
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{Set}^{\rm op}
are monadic over
\mathsf{Set}
, with respect to the forgetful functor
\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{Set}
and
\mathsf{Set}(-,2)\colon\mathsf{Set}^{{\rm op}}\to\mathsf{Set}

[
22
]
, respectively.

(3)

\mathsf{AfSet}(A)
is dually equivalent to
\mathsf{A}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\rotatebox{-90.0}{$\rightarrowtail$}}}}I
: each object

can be seen as a monomorphism

, and a morphism

as a morphism in
\mathsf{A}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\rotatebox{-90.0}{$\rightarrowtail$}}}}I
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces IX}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces IY}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I(f)}

Conversely, to every monomorphism

one may assign its image

and to every morphism

as in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}

3.
Examples

3.1.
Quantale-enriched categories

We recall that a complete ordered set is said to be constructively completely distributive (ccd for short) if the monotone map
\bigvee\colon\mathbb{D}X\to X
, where
\mathbb{D}X
is the lattice of downsets of
X
, has a left adjoint
[
11
]
. In the more general context of
V
-categories, for a commutative quantale
V
, a
V
-category
(X,a)
is cocomplete if and only if its Yoneda embedding
y_{X}\colon X\to PX
admits a left adjoint
\bigvee_{X}\colon PX\to X
. As for the case
V=\mathsf{2}
, when
\bigvee_{X}
admits a left adjoint
X
is said to be
constructively completely distributive
, or just ccd. For more information on (completely distributive)
V
-categories we refer to
[
23
,
24
]
. We point out that the notion of complete distributivity in this context was introduced in
[
25
]
under the designation
totally continuous
. The category
V\mbox{-}\mathsf{ccd}
has as objects the ccd
V
-categories and as morphisms the
V
-functors which preserve both weighted limits and weighted colimits. As shown in
[
21
]
,
V\mbox{-}\mathsf{ccd}
is a variety with respect to the canonical forgetful functor
V\mbox{-}\mathsf{ccd}\to\mathsf{Set}
, which in our study above will play the role of the variety
\mathsf{A}
. The role of the algebra
A
is played by the ccd
V
-category
(V,\hom)
. That is, our functor
I\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\rm op}\to V\mbox{-}\mathsf{ccd}
is given by
IX=V^{X}
. So, in order to conclude that
(V\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat})^{\rm op}
is a quasivariety, it remains to show that
V\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat}
is equivalent to
\mathsf{AfSet}(V)
.

.

The category

Proof.

We define functors

-category

,

sends the affine set
(X,S)
to the

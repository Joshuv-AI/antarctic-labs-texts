Evidence of the Schwinger mechanism from lattice QCD
Mauricio Narciso Ferreira

Abstract
In quantum chromodynamics (QCD), gluons acquire a mass scale through the action of the Schwinger mechanism. This mass emerges as a result of the dynamical formation of massless bound-states of gluons which manifest as longitudinally coupled poles in the vertices. In this contribution, we show how the presence of these poles can be determined from lattice QCD results for the propagators and vertices. The crucial observation that allows this determination is that the Schwinger mechanism poles induce modifications, called “displacements”, to the Ward identities (WIs) relating two- and three-point functions. Importantly, the displacement functions correspond precisely to the Bethe-Salpeter amplitudes of the massless bound-states. We apply this idea to the case of the three-gluon vertex in pure Yang-Mills SU(3). Using lattice results in the corresponding WI, we find an unequivocal displacement and show that it is consistent with the prediction based on the Bethe-Salpeter equation.

Overview of the Schwinger mechanism

In the Landau gauge, which will be used throughout this work, the gluon propagator can be written as

Since the gauge symmetry is assumed to be unbroken, the STI of Eq. (
11
) remains valid for the full vertex,
i.e.
,

q^{\mu}{\widetilde{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}_{\mu}(q,r,p)=q^{\mu}{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}(q,r,p)+{\widetilde{C}}(q,r,p)=D^{-1}(p)-D^{-1}(r)\,.

(15)

Then we repeat the procedure of the derivation of the WI, expanding Eq. (
15
) in a Taylor series around
q=0
. At zeroth order, Eq. (
15
) implies

{\widetilde{C}}(0,r,-r)=0\,,

(16)

which is akin to the Eq. (
7
), derived for the three-gluon vertex from Bose symmetry in Section
2
.

Next, at first order we obtain

{\widetilde{\cal A}}(r)=2\left[\frac{\partial D^{-1}(r)}{\partial r^{2}}-{\widetilde{\cal C}}(r)\right]\,,\qquad{\widetilde{\cal C}}(r):=\left.\frac{\partial{\widetilde{C}}(q,r,p)}{\partial p^{2}}\right|_{q=0}\,.

(17)

Comparing Eqs. (
13
) and (
17
), we see that the WI for the form factor
{\widetilde{\cal A}}(r)
gets modified, or “displaced”, by a derivative,
{\widetilde{\cal C}}(r)
, of the pole residue
{\widetilde{C}}(q,r,p)
. Note that the above definition for
{\widetilde{\cal C}}(r)
is completely analogous to the BS amplitude
{\mathbb{C}}(r)
of the three-gluon vertex, defined in Eq. (
8
).

With Eq. (
17
) at hand, if the propagator
D(r)
and the vertex form factor
{\widetilde{\cal A}}(r)
are somehow known, we can compute
{\widetilde{\cal C}}(r)
, thus determining if the vertex has a massless bound-state pole.

The same idea can be applied to the three-gluon vertex. The only fundamental difference is the non-Abelian nature of its STI, which implies that the relevant WI and its displacement have a more complicated form that mixes gluon and ghost sector functions.

Specifically, the STI which relates the three-gluon vertex to the gluon propagator is given by
Marciano and Pagels 1978
;
Ball and Chiu 1980
;
Davydychev et al. 1996
;
von Smekal et al. 1998
;
Binosi and
Papavassiliou 2011

Gracey et al. 2019

q^{\alpha}{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=F(q)\left[\Delta^{-1}(p)P_{\nu}^{\sigma}(p)H_{\sigma\mu}(p,q,r)-\Delta^{-1}(r)P_{\mu}^{\sigma}(r)H_{\sigma\nu}(r,q,p)\right]\,,

(18)

where
F(q)
is the ghost dressing function, defined by
D(q)=F(q)/q^{2}
, and
H_{\nu\mu}(r,p,q)
is the ghost-gluon kernel
Aguilar et al. 2019
, which will be discussed in the next section. We point out that the ghost propagator remains massless, while its dressing function,

Aguilar et al. 2008

;

;

;

;
Cucchieri and Mendes 2008

, as shown in the left panel of Fig.

The WI for the three-gluon vertex is obtained as a special case of the above STI. To derive it, one expands Eq. (
18
) around
q=0
and matches coefficients of equal orders on each side of the resulting equation. Evidently, the zeroth-order expansion leads again to Eq. (
7
). As for the first-order term, after a suitable projection to isolate the classical tensor structure of the three-gluon vertex, one obtains the relation (for detailed derivations see

)

{\mathbb{C}}(r)=\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(r)-F(0)\left\{\frac{{\cal W}(r)}{r^{2}}\Delta^{-1}(r)+\widetilde{Z}_{1}\frac{d\Delta^{-1}(r)}{dr^{2}}\right\}\,.

(19)

In the above equation, the displacement of the WI is
precisely
the BS amplitude
{\mathbb{C}}(r)
of the Schwinger pole of the three-gluon vertex. On the other hand,
\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(r)
is the classical form factor of the three-gluon vertex in the soft gluon limit, defined by
Aguilar et al. 2021a

\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(r)=\left.\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)P_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(q)P_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r)P_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(p){\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)}{\Gamma_{0}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)P_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(q)P_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r)P_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(p)\Gamma_{0}^{\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)}\right|_{q\to 0}\,,

(20)

with
\Gamma_{0}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
denoting the tree-level form of the vertex. By now, this form factor has been extensively studied on the lattice
Athenodorou
et al. 2016
;
Duarte et al. 2016
;
Boucaud et al. 2017
;
Aguilar et al. 2020
;
Aguilar et al. 2021a
;
Pinto-Gómez
et al. 2023

\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(r)
from
Aguilar et al. 2021a
(points), together with a physically motivated fit for it given by Eq. (C12) of
Aguilar et al. 2022a
(blue continuous curve).

Lastly,
{\cal W}(r)
is a particular derivative of the ghost-gluon kernel, namely
Aguilar et al. 2020
;
Aguilar et al. 2022a

{\cal W}(r)=-\frac{1}{3r^{2}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)\left[\frac{\partial H_{\nu\mu}(p,q,r)}{\partial q^{\alpha}}\right]_{q=0}\,,

(21)

while
{\widetilde{Z}}_{1}
is the renormalization constant of the ghost-gluon vertex. The latter is finite in the Landau gauge, by virtue of the well-known Taylor theorem

The Eq. (

4
Ghost-gluon kernel contribution

Now we briefly describe our lattice-driven SDE determination of
{\cal W}(r)
. The starting point of this analysis is the SDE that defines the ghost-gluon kernel, shown diagrammatically in Fig.
7
.

From that equation, the function
{\cal W}(r)
can be isolated through Eq. (
21

{\cal W}(r)
can be written as

{\cal W}(r)={\cal W}_{1}(r)+{\cal W}_{2}(r)\,,

(22)

where the
{\cal W}_{i}(r)
denote the contributions of the diagrams
(h_{i}^{\mu\nu})
in Fig.
7

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\lambda}}\int_{k}\Delta(k)D(k)D(k+r)(r\cdot k)f(k,r)B_{1}(k+r,-k,-r)B_{1}(k,0,-k)\,,

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\lambda}}\int_{k}\Delta(k)\Delta(k+r)D(k+r)B_{1}(k+r,0,-k-r){\cal I}_{{\cal W}}(-r,-k,k+r)\,,

(23)

where
{\widetilde{\lambda}}:=ig^{2}C_{\rm A}{\widetilde{Z}}_{1}/6
,
C_{\mathrm{A}}
is the Casimir eigenvalue of the adjoint representation [
N
for SU
(N)
], and

f(k,r):=1-\frac{(r\cdot k)^{2}}{r^{2}k^{2}}\,.

(24)

In addition to the gluon and ghost propagators,
\Delta(r)
and
D(r)
, respectively, Eq. (
23
) involves quantities that are related to the ghost-gluon and three-gluon vertices, namely
B_{1}(r,p,q)
,
{\widetilde{Z}}_{1}
and
{{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}}(q,r,p)
. Below we explain their meaning in detail.

(
i
)

In Eq. (
23
),
B_{1}(r,p,q)
denotes the classical form factor of the ghost-gluon vertex,
{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\mu}(r,p,q)
, whose most general tensor structure is given by

{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\mu}(r,p,q)=B_{1}(r,p,q)r_{\mu}+B_{2}(r,p,q)q_{\mu}\,.

(25)

Hence, at tree level
B_{1}^{0}=1
and
B_{2}^{0}=0
.

Note that the ghost-gluon vertex and kernel are related by the STI

{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\mu}(r,p,q)=r^{\nu}H_{\nu\mu}(q,r,p)\,.

(26)

Thus, the general kinematics
B_{1}(r,p,q)
can be determined through another projection of the SDE of Fig.
7
.

Such an SDE determination of the general kinematics
B_{1}(r,p,q)
was performed in Refs.
Aguilar et al. 2022b
;
Ferreira and
Papavassiliou 2023
, also using lattice results as inputs for all its ingredients. It is beyond the scope of the present work to describe this analysis in detail. It suffices to mention that the results for
B_{1}(r,p,q)
deviate only moderately from its tree-level value, in agreement with several previous continuum studies
Schleifenbaum
et al. 2005
;
Huber and von Smekal 2013
;
Aguilar et al. 2013
;
Cyrol et al. 2016
;
Mintz et al. 2018
;
Aguilar et al. 2019
;
Huber 2020
;
Aguilar et al. 2019
;
Huber 2020
;
Barrios et al. 2020
, and reproduce the available lattice data from Ref.
Ilgenfritz et al. 2007
;
Sternbeck 2006
. As such, the impact of the precise dressing of
B_{1}(r,p,q)
on the
{\cal W}(r)
computed through Eq. (
22
) is under stringent control.

(
ii
)

As previously mentioned, the ghost-gluon kernel, and hence
{\cal W}(r)
, is finite in Landau gauge
Taylor 1971
. Nevertheless, multiplicative renormalization of the theory leads to the appearance
Aguilar et al. 2020
of the ghost-gluon renormalization constant,
{\widetilde{Z}}_{1}
, in Eq. (
23
). The
finite
value of this constant depends on the renormalization scheme adopted.

To take the most advantage of the lattice data for the propagators and the three-gluon vertex, we adopt the scheme where
\Delta(r)
,
F(r)
and
\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(r)
are most readily renormalized. Namely, the so-called asymmetric MOM scheme
Athenodorou
et al. 2016
;
Boucaud et al. 2017
;
Aguilar et al. 2020
;
Aguilar et al. 2021a
;
Aguilar et al. 2021b
;
Ferreira and
Papavassiliou 2023
. The latter is defined by the prescriptions

\Delta^{-1}(\mu^{2})=\mu^{2}\,,\qquad F(\mu^{2})=1\,,\qquad\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(\mu^{2})=1\,,

(27)

where we choose
\mu=4.3
GeV as renormalization point. The corresponding value for the coupling is
g^{2}=4\pi\alpha_{s}
, with
\alpha_{s}(4.3\text{ GeV})=0.27
, as determined in the lattice study of
Boucaud et al. 2017
. Within this renormalization scheme, the same SDE analysis of Refs.
Aguilar et al. 2022b
;
Ferreira and
Papavassiliou 2023
used to determine
B_{1}(r,p,q)
also yields the value
{\widetilde{Z}}_{1}=0.9333
.

(
iii
)

Finally,
{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}(q,r,p)
is a particular transverse projection of the three-gluon vertex, namely

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}(q-r)^{\nu}{\overline{\Gamma}}^{\alpha}_{\alpha\mu}(q,r,p)\,,

(28)

which encodes the total contribution of
{\overline{\Gamma}}^{\alpha}_{\alpha\mu}(q,r,p)
to the SDE governing
{\cal W}(r^{2})
. Note that the Bose symmetry of
{\overline{\Gamma}}^{\alpha}_{\alpha\mu}(q,r,p)
implies

{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}(q,r,p)={\cal I}_{{\cal W}}(r,q,p)\,.

(29)

In a series of previous works
Aguilar et al. 2020
;
Aguilar et al. 2021
;
Aguilar et al. 2022a
, the ingredient appearing in Eq. (
22
) that represented the largest uncertainty was
{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}(q,r,p)
. Since lattice results for the general kinematics three-gluon vertex were not available then, in those references
{\cal W}(r)
had been approximated by various
Ansätze
based on the Ball-Chiu construction of the three-gluon vertex
Ball and Chiu 1980
;
Aguilar et al. 2019
. Recently, general kinematics lattice data for
{\overline{\Gamma}}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
became available
Pinto-Gómez
et al. 2023
;
Pinto-Gomez and
de Soto 2022
;
Pinto-Gómez
et al. 2023
, prompting a more accurate determination of
{\cal W}(r)
.

Remarkably the general kinematics lattice results of Refs.
Pinto-Gómez
et al. 2023
;
Pinto-Gomez and
de Soto 2022
;
Pinto-Gómez
et al. 2023
;
Aguilar et al. 2022b
, as well as some continuum studies
Eichmann et al. 2014
;
Blum et al. 2014
;
Huber 2016
, revealed that a compact expression provides a rather accurate approximation for the transversely projected three-gluon vertex. Specifically,

{\overline{\Gamma}}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\approx{\overline{\Gamma}}^{0}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(s)\,,\qquad s^{2}:=(q^{2}+r^{2}+p^{2})/2\,,

(30)

where
{\overline{\Gamma}}^{0}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
denotes the tree-level form of
{\overline{\Gamma}}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
.

In Eq. (
30
) the sole dynamical ingredient is the soft gluon form factor,
\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(r)
, of Fig.
6
, which now appears evaluated at the Bose-symmetric combination of momenta given by
s
. Note that general kinematics form factors of the three-gluon vertex are expected to depend on three Lorentz scalars. The fact that in Eq. (
30

s

planar degeneracy

Using the planar degeneracy approximation of Eq. (
30

28

{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}(q,r,p)\approx{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}^{0}(q,r,p)\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(s)\,,

(31)

where
{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}^{0}(q,r,p)
is the tree-level value of
{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}
, given by

{\cal I}_{{\cal W}}^{0}(q,r,p):=\frac{2f(q,r)}{p^{2}}\left[2q^{2}r^{2}-(q^{2}+r^{2})(q\cdot r)-(q\cdot r)^{2}\right]\,.

The Eq. (
31

accurately and expeditiously.

In order to carry out the integrations over the whole momentum space in Eq. (

and

from
Aguilar et al. 2021b

At this point, it is important to quantify the errors introduced in

In order to employ the lattice results for

Since the data points depend on three kinematic variables (the magnitudes of two momenta and the angle between them), it is difficult to come up with a functional form that fits them accurately. A reliable method to interpolate the general kinematics

6
Conclusion

The displacement of the WIs by the formation of massless vertex poles is a distinctive feature of the Schwinger mechanism for gluon mass generation, which allows its verification from lattice QCD results. In the present work, we have used this framework to demonstrate that the three-gluon vertex in Yang-Mills SU(3) has such a pole. Indeed, our analysis of the WI displacement using lattice data unequivocally excludes the null hypothesis of a vanishing BS amplitude,
{\mathbb{C}}=0
. Instead, our results reveal an excellent agreement between the
{\mathbb{C}}(r)
derived from the WI and the BSE prediction, providing outstanding evidence for the occurrence of the Schwinger mechanism in QCD.

It is important to emphasize that while the present analysis was carried out in the simpler setting of pure Yang-Mills SU(3), the same ideas hold in the presence of dynamical quarks. In particular, the WI displacement for the three-gluon vertex retains exactly the same form as in Eq. (
19
) in the unquenched case, for which lattice data for the propagators and vertices also exist
Bowman et al. 2004
;
Kamleh et al. 2007
;
Ayala et al. 2012
;
Cui et al. 2020
;
Aguilar et al. 2020
. Indeed, a study is already underway to investigate the Schwinger mechanism poles in the presence of quarks and should be reported soon.

Finally, in the present work, we have focused entirely on the massless pole content of the three-gluon vertex. However, once the Schwinger mechanism is active, it is expected that massless poles appear in various vertices
Aguilar et al. 2016
, since the different vertices are connected to one another through the SDEs. As such, other important signals of the Schwinger mechanism may be present in functions such as the ghost-gluon kernel, and the quark-gluon and four-gluon vertices, which are currently under investigation as well.

Reply to comment on “Measurability of nonequilibrium thermodynamics in terms of the Hamiltonian of mean force”
Philipp Strasberg 1, Massimiliano Esposito 2

Abstract
We refute the criticism expressed in a comment by P. Talkner and P. Hänggi [Phys. Rev. E 102 , 066101 (2020)] on our paper Phys. Rev. E 101 , 050101(R) (2020). We first make clear that our paper is free of any technical mistakes. We then defend the statements of our manuscript which were claimed to be taken out of context. Finally, we give clear arguments showing that the basic concepts we rely on are meaningful and have a deep rational.



In our recent paper
Strasberg and Esposito 2020
, we were concerned with setting up a nonequilibrium thermodynamic
framework for a system in strong contact with a bath using the concept of the Hamiltonian of mean force (HMF).
A recent comment by Talkner and Hänggi (abbreviated T&H in the following) claims that our results can
“lead to erroneous conclusions” and that we “wrongly conclude” some things
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
.

The first statement was made in the context of a system-bath Hamiltonian of the form
Talkner and Hänggi 2020

H_{\text{tot}}(\mu)=H_{S}(\mu)+H_{SB}(\mu)+H_{B}(\mu).

(1)

Here,
\mu
denotes all externally controllable parameters (such as magnetic or electric fields) appearing either
in the system Hamiltonian
H_{S}(\mu)
, the bath Hamiltonian
H_{B}(\mu)
or their mutual interaction term
H_{SB}(\mu)
.
The setup (
1
) is
not
the one that was considered in our
paper
Strasberg and Esposito 2020
. We explicitly stated that the external driving does not act on the
bath and the system-bath interaction. Concluding in the more general setup of
Eq. (
1
)
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
that
information about the system is not sufficient to characterize the nonequilibrium thermodynamics is obvious, and was
clearly not the statement made in our paper. Thus, within the stated assumptions of our
paper, no evidence is provided against the consistency and correctness of our results.
We also remark that Eq. (

used by T&H in their recent papers

of Ref.
Strasberg and Esposito 2020
. For that purpose, we denote by
p(x,t)
the classical phase space probability
distribution of the open system, where
x
denotes the system phase space coordinates. Now,
in
Talkner and Hänggi 2016
T&H argue that the definition of thermodynamic quantities suffers from ambiguities
since it is possible to add any function
h(x)
of the system’s phase space coordinates to them as long as

\int dxh(x)p(x,t)=0

(2)

holds. Since Eq. (
2

p(x,t)
, which includes in

p(x,t)
(regarded as elements of a Hilbert space), our footnote [60] only states
that this implies
h(x)\equiv 0
, which is a mathematical fact.
Note that this argument continues to hold if
h(x)=h(x,t)
is an arbitrary prescribed function of time.

Talkner and Hänggi 2016

h
also “changes with time in

(3)

We now turn to the claim that our paper contains “two misleading literal citations”
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
.
The first claim refers to the quote “…presents in practice an impossible task” taken from
Ref.
Talkner and Hänggi 2019
, which—in view of T&H—“does not refer to the HMF […] but to the
reconstruction of the total […] Hamiltonian”
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
. What is true is that the subject of
the sentence we quote is the total Hamiltonian and not the HMF. What is also true, however, is that knowledge of the
total Hamiltonian allows to compute (in principle) the HMF and that a preceeding sentence in
Ref.
Talkner and Hänggi 2019
reads “Therefore, the Hamiltonian of mean force cannot be obtained from a purely
system-intrinsic point of view […].” Hence, our quotation is
not
“taken out of [its] original
context”
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
.
The second “misleading literal citation”
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
refers to the fact that we claim that
the assessment of work in the quantum regime by T&H is based on measurements that “need to be
error free”
Strasberg and Esposito 2020
. When looking at Ref.
Talkner and Hänggi 2019
, we can
only find that T&H introduce work in the quantum regime via the two-point measurement scheme
2
2

2

Towards the end
of Sec. IV.A.2 of Ref.
Talkner and Hänggi 2019
, T&H also cite some literature using generalized measurements,
but they still conclude that “the measurements yet need to be error free”
Talkner and Hänggi 2019
.
. It is a
fact that this scheme, also in the way it is reviewed in Ref.
Talkner and Hänggi 2019
, requires perfect
error-free measurements. Therefore, we must conclude that any comment concerning work in the quantum regime made in
Ref.
Talkner and Hänggi 2019
refers to this particular measurement scheme.

Finally, we turn to two general claims of T&H. First, it is claimed that the use of the HMF outside equilibrium
considerations is “without a deeper rational”
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
. We believe that the 70 references given
in our paper can be seen as convincing counterevidence. In fact, the HMF was successfully used in even more
nonequilibrium contexts without explicitly noting it (which might be related to the
fact that this terminology is non-standard in physics). Also, as we have demonstrated in an earlier
paper
Strasberg and Esposito 2017
, the HMF framework emerges naturally whenever a system is strongly coupled to
a
fast
environment. We started our investigation in Ref.
Strasberg and Esposito 2017
based on a Markov
process obeying local detailed balance, but the argument can be generalized by using projection operator
techniques.

Second, T&H claim that the fluctuating probability density function used in stochastic thermodynamics, e.g., to
compute a stochastic entropy “does not stay normalized in general” (supplement of Ref.
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
)
and “has no obvious probabilitic meaning”
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
. We disagree. The construction of this
fluctuating probability density function is well defined, although it experimentally requires
pre
processing of
many recorded stochastic trajectories. This makes its application not straighforward, but the theory of classical
stochastic processes—by virtue of the Kolmogorov consistency condition
Kolmogorov 2018
—guarantees
that this probability density function is normalized and non-negative. Thus, also any functional defined it terms of
it and evaluated along stochastic trajectories gives rise to a well defined stochastic process.

In conclusion,
we made clear that our paper
Strasberg and Esposito 2020
does not contain technically or
contextually wrong statements. Moreover, we feel that a significant part of the criticism
Talkner and Hänggi 2020

was not targeted at our paper, but also applies to weak coupling thermodynamics or, more generally, any attempt to
formulate thermodynamics out of equilibrium. We clearly refute this criticism (which has also been already debated in
other places): the foundations of stochastic thermodynamics are sound. Finally, we acknowledge that many open
questions remain outside the traditional scope of stochastic thermodynamics, but we remain confident that they can be
also addressed in a constructive way.

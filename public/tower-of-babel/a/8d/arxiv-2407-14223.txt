Partial mass dynamics of the defocussing nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Jiaqi Liu, Xixi Xu

Abstract
. We study the long time dynamics of the defocussing NLS equation. Compared with previous literature, we revisit the direct and inverse scattering map to obtain asymptotics in some weighted energy space that requires less restrictive decay and regularity assumptions. The main result is derived from an application of uniform resolvent bound and an approximation argument in the spirit of Riemann-Lebesgue lemma. As a consequence, our result depicts the long time dynamics of the zeros of the solution to the defocussing NLS equation.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we apply the inverse scattering transform (IST) to study the long time dynamics of the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation on the real line with finite density initial data:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}iq_{t}+q_{xx}-2\left(|q|^{2}-1\right)q=0,\\
q(x,0)=q_{0}(x),\\
\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}q_{0}(x)=\pm 1.\end{array}\right.

Main results.

2.
Direct and Inverse scattering

2.1.
The direct scattering

Following the formalism in
[
7
]
, the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation (
1.1
) is the compatibility condition for the following Lax pair:

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{x}=\mathcal{L}\psi\\
i\psi_{t}=\mathcal{B}\psi\end{array}

in the sense that

(2.2)

i(i\mathcal{L}_{t}-\mathcal{B}_{x}+[\mathcal{L},\mathcal{B}])=0.

The
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{B}
operators are given by

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=i\sigma_{3}(Q-\lambda(z)),

(2.3b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=-2i\lambda(z)\mathcal{L}-(Q^{2}-I)\sigma_{3}+iQ_{x}

where

Q=Q(x,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\overline{q(x,t)}\\
q(x,t)&0\end{array}\right),\qquad\lambda(z)=\frac{1}{2}(z+z^{-1})

and
\sigma_{3}
is the third
Pauli
matrix:

(2.4)

\sigma_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{0}&{1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{array}\right),\qquad\sigma_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{0}&{-i}\\
{i}&{0}\end{array}\right),\qquad\sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{1}&{0}\\
{0}&{-1}\end{array}\right).

We now define the simultaneous solutions (
2\times 2
matrix-valued
Jost functions
)

\psi^{(\pm)}=\left[\psi_{1}^{(\pm)},\psi_{2}^{(\pm)}\right]

to the Lax pair (
2.1
) in the column-wise form and prescribe the boundary conditions at
\pm\infty

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{(-)}(x;z)

\displaystyle\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{l}1\\
-z^{-1}\end{array}\right)e^{-ix\zeta(z)},\quad\psi_{2}^{(-)}(x;z)\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{l}-z^{-1}\\
1\end{array}\right)e^{ix\zeta(z)}\quad\text{as }x\rightarrow-\infty,

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{(+)}(x;z)

\displaystyle\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{l}1\\
z^{-1}\end{array}\right)e^{-ix\zeta(z)},\quad\psi_{2}^{(+)}(x;z)\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{l}z^{-1}\\
1\end{array}\right)e^{ix\zeta(z)}\quad\text{as }x\rightarrow+\infty,

where

(2.6)

\zeta(z)=\frac{1}{2}(z-z^{-1}).

We further normalize the

at

(2.7a)

\displaystyle m_{1}^{(\pm)}(x;z)

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}^{(\pm)}(x;z)e^{ix\zeta(z)},

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{\theta}^{\pm}\restriction_{\mathbb{R}}={w}_{\theta}^{\pm},\quad{w}_{\theta}^{\pm}\restriction_{\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{*}}=\mathbf{0}.

It is important to notice that since
r(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{0}
and
(V(z)+V(z)^{\dagger})\restriction_{\mathbb{R}}
is positive definite, thus
\mathbf{1}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}}
is
Fredholm
by a rational approximation argument and is of index zero by
[
22
, Lemma 9.3]
, thus
(\mathbf{1}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}})^{-1}
exists. Then by
second resolvent identity
, we have that

(2.117)

\left(\mathbf{1}-C_{{w}_{\theta}}\right)^{-1}\left[\mathbf{1}-\left(C_{{w}_{\theta}}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}}\right)(\mathbf{1}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}})^{-1}\right]=(\mathbf{1}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}})^{-1}.

Notice that
C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}}\equiv\mathbf{0}
.
We also partition the set
\{1,...,N\}
into the pair of sets

(2.118)

\mathcal{D}^{-}=\{k:\pi/2<\arg(z_{k})<\pi\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{D}^{+}=\{k:0<\arg(z_{k})<\pi/2\}.

Notice that if
k\in\mathcal{D}^{-}
for
k=1,2...N
, then
{w}_{\theta}^{\pm}\restriction_{\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{*}}
decay exponentially as
t\to+\infty
. Thus

(2.119)

\left\|{\left(C_{{w}_{\theta}}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}}\right)(\mathbf{1}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}})^{-1}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-t}\left\|{\left(\mathbf{1}-C_{\widetilde{w}_{\theta}}\right)^{-1}}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus we can construct
\left(\mathbf{1}-C_{{w}_{\theta}}\right)^{-1}
through
Neumann
series. If for some
k=1,2...N
, we have that
1,2,...,k\in\mathcal{D}^{+}
, then we construct
\widetilde{M}_{\pm}=T_{k}(\infty)^{-\sigma_{3}}M_{\pm}T_{k}^{\sigma_{3}}(z)
with

(2.120)

T_{k}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{z-z_{i}}{zz_{i}-1}\right)

to reverse the triangularity of
w_{\theta}^{\pm}
and obtain exponential decay in
t
.
∎

The potential
q(x,t)
is recovered by the reconstruction formula

(2.121)

q(x,t)=\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}z\left(M(z;x,t)-I\right)_{21}

and the partial mass is obtained through:

(2.122)

\int_{x}^{+\infty}\left(|q(y,t)|^{2}-1\right)dy=-i\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}z\left(M(z;x,t)-I\right)_{22}.

We end this section with a proposition about the uniform resolvent bound.

Proposition 2.20
.

Given
r(z)\in H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\left\|{r}\right\|_{H^{1,1}}<\eta
uniformly bounded, if
(1-C_{w_{\theta}})^{-1}
exists, then for some
C_{\eta}>0
we have

(2.123)

\sup_{r\in H^{1,1}}\left(\sup_{x,t\in[a,\infty)}\left\|\left(I-C_{w_{\theta}}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)<C_{\eta}.

Proof.

Recall that the embedding
i:H^{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow H^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
is compact for
\alpha>\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta>\beta^{\prime}
. Thus we can construct operator
C_{\breve{w}_{\theta}}
with
\breve{r}\in H^{1/2+\varepsilon,1/2+\varepsilon}
for some
0<\varepsilon<1/2
and obtain
(\mathbf{1}-C_{\breve{w}_{\theta}})^{-1}
a
Fredholm
operator with zero index. The rest of the proof follows from
[
15
, Appendix B]
.
∎

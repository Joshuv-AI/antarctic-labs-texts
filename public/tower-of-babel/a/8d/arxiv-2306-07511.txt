On the uniqueness of energy-minimizing curves in constrained spaces
Ki-Ahm Lee, Taehun Lee

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate energy-minimizing curves with fixed endpoints p p and q q in a constrained space. We prove that when one of the endpoints, say p p , is fixed, the set of points q q for which the energy-minimizing curve is not unique has no interior points.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded convex domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
, and let
p,q
be two points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}}
. In this paper, we are concerned with energy minimizing curves joining two points

and

in

. More precisely, we investigate minimizers of the energy

(1.1)

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{u})=\int_{I}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\text{d}t

in the admissible set

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})|\,\,\mathbf{u}(0)=p,\,\mathbf{u}(1)=q,\,\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathcal{O}^{c}\text{ for all }t\in I\right\},

where

is the unit interval and

is the Sobolev space of vector-valued functions.
The set
\mathcal{O}
is the so-called

\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{O}

To avoid trivial cases, we assume throughout that the line segment

joining

to

\mathcal{O}

The first result of this paper shows existence of a minimizer and the optimal regularity of minimizers. A simple example where the minimizer is not

is

. This scenario will be discussed in the final section,

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a bounded convex domain in

with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
, and let

be two points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}}

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a solution
\mathbf{u}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{c}
minimizing energy (
1.1
) over the admissible set
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}
is bounded, any two points outside
\mathcal{O}
are connected by a smooth curve in
\mathcal{O}^{c}
, which induces that
\mathcal{A}
is not empty. We now take a minimizing sequence
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}E(\mathbf{u}_{k})=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{A}}E(\mathbf{v}).

Let
c:I\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{c}
be a smooth curve connecting two points
p
and
q
. Since
c\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{A}}E(\mathbf{v})\leq E(c)<\infty
, and thus
E(\mathbf{u}_{k})
is uniformly bounded. Using the Poincarè inequality,
\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}
is also uniformly bounded. Then there exists a function
\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that a subsequence of
\mathbf{u}_{k}
converges weakly to
\mathbf{u}
in
H^{1}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and converges (strongly) to
\mathbf{u}
almost everywhere in
I
. We still denote by
\mathbf{u}_{k}
the converging subsequence.

Clearly,
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}
since
\mathbf{u}_{k}(0)=p
,
\mathbf{u}_{k}(1)=q
, and
\mathbf{u}_{k}(I)
is contained in the closed set
\mathcal{O}^{c}
. Moreover, since
E
is weakly lower semicontinuous on
H^{1}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{u})\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}E(\mathbf{u}_{k})=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{A}}E(\mathbf{v})\leq E(\mathbf{u})

which conclude that
\mathbf{u}
is a minimizer of

on
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

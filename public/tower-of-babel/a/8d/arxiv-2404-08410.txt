On Weak Inverse Mean Curvature Flow and Minkowski-type Inequalities in Hyperbolic Space
Brian Harvie

Abstract
. We prove that a proper weak solution { Ω t } 0 ≤ t < ∞ \{\Omega_{t}\}_{0\leq t<\infty} to inverse mean curvature flow in hyperbolic space ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , 3 ≤ n ≤ 7 3\leq n\leq 7 , is eventually smooth and star-shaped for an arbitrary initial domain Ω 0 \Omega_{0} . In fact, this happens by the time T = ( n − 1 ) ​ log ⁡ ( sinh ​ ( r + ) sinh ​ ( r − ) ) , T=(n-1)\log\left(\frac{\text{sinh}\left(r_{+}\right)}{\text{sinh}\left(r_{-}\right)}\right), where r + r_{+} and r − r_{-} are the geodesic out-radius and in-radius of Ω 0 \Omega_{0} . The approach is based on an Alexandrov reflection method for extrinsic curvature flows originally introduced by Chow-Gulliver [ 8 ] . In addition, our methods characterize expanding spheres as proper weak IMCF on ℍ n ∖ { 0 } \mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\} for arbitrary n n , thereby implying a result for ancient smooth solutions. As applications of the regularity theorem, we derive optimal Minkowski inequalities for arbitrary smooth domains of ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , 3 ≤ n ≤ 7 3\leq n\leq 7 , extending those of Brendle-Hung-Wang [ 4 ] and De Lima-Girao [ 11 ] . From this, we also extend the Riemannian Penrose inequality from [ 11 ] to balanced asymptotically graphs over the exteriors of outer-minimizing domains in ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} .



1.
Introduction

Hyperbolic space is the Riemannian manifold of constant negative curvature given by

(\mathbb{H}^{n},g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})=(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times(0,\infty),dr\otimes dr+\sinh^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}),

(1.1)

where
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
is the unit sphere metric. This paper considers the inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF) of hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space. Given a closed smooth manifold
\Sigma^{n-1}
, a one-parameter family of
C^{\infty}
embeddings
X:\Sigma^{n-1}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}
solves IMCF if

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=\frac{1}{H}\nu(x,t),\hskip 56.9055pt(x,t)\in\Sigma^{n-1}\times[0,T),

(1.2)

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal and
H>0
the mean curvature of the hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}=X_{t}(\Sigma^{n-1})\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
. IMCF enjoys a wide variety of geometric applications, but a key obstacle to these is the formation of singularities which terminate the flow in finite time. In
[
19
]
, Huisken and Ilmanen introduced a notion of weak solutions of IMCF which flow beyond singularities. Given a bounded domain
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
with connected
C^{\infty}
boundary
\partial\Omega_{0}=\Sigma_{0}
, a proper weak solution to IMCF with initial condition
\Omega_{0}
corresponds to a proper locally Lipschitz function
u:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which solves the degenerate-elliptic exterior Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla u|\hskip 28.45274pt\text{in}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{0}},

(1.3)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\hskip 39.83368pt\text{on}\hskip 14.22636pt\Sigma_{0}

in a variational sense, with weak flow surfaces
\Sigma_{t}
defined by
\Sigma_{t}=\partial\{u<t\}
.

For a smooth solution

to (
1.3
),
\Sigma_{t}
are smooth, strictly mean-convex hypersurfaces and form a solution to (
1.2
), but for a general weak solution
\Sigma_{t}
are only

with a possible singular set in higher dimensions. Furthermore, a phenomenon may occur where
\Sigma_{t}
“jumps” outward at certain times– we discuss this more thoroughly in Section 2. Understanding the long-time behavior of proper weak IMCF is crucial to its geometric applications. For weak IMCF in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a blow-down lemma due to Huisken and Ilmanen (Lemma 7.1 in
[
19
]
) ensures that the weak flow surfaces
\Sigma_{t}
converge to a round sphere in a

sense after scaling as

\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and an explicit example from
[
22
]
shows that even smooth IMCF in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
may not become round as
t\rightarrow\infty

[
28

\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the asymptotic profile of
\Sigma_{t}
, including even the eventual topology, is unclear a priori.

In this paper, we show that
\Sigma_{t}
are star-shaped after an explicit time

determined by the in-radius and out-radius of the initial data. In dimensions less than
8
, this implies that
\Sigma_{t}
form a classical solution to (
1.2

t\in(T,\infty)
, meaning in particular that the flow is smooth and free of jumps beyond time

\mathbb{R}^{n}
by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
20
]
and in asymptotically hyperbolic

[
30

A key application of IMCF is to Minkowski inequalities, which are fundamental geometric inequalities in Riemannian geometry. Recall that the classical Minkowski inequality in Euclidean space for a convex body
\Omega_{0}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with boundary
\Sigma_{0}=\partial\Omega_{0}

\frac{1}{(n-1)w_{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}Hd\sigma\geq\left(\frac{|\Sigma_{0}|}{w_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

In their breakthrough paper
[
4

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-1)w_{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}fHd\sigma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\Sigma_{0}|}{w_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}+\frac{n}{w_{n-1}}\int_{\Omega_{0}}fd\Omega,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-1)w_{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}fHd\sigma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\Sigma_{0}|}{w_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}+\left(\frac{|\Sigma_{0}|}{w_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}},

(1.7)

where

is the potential function

(1.8)

2.
Preliminaries

To introduce weak solutions of IMCF, we consider the functional

J_{u}(F)=|\partial^{*}F|-\int_{F}|\nabla u|d\Omega,

(2.1)

, where

is a locally Lipschitz function. Given a proper function

\Omega_{t}=\{u<t\}

for each

solves the Dirichlet problem (

are a solution to classical IMCF (

Let

This formulation is inspired by the level-set formulation for mean curvature flow due to Evans-Spruck

and Chen-Goto-Giga

]

\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\bigg\rvert_{\epsilon=0}|X_{\epsilon}(\Sigma_{0})|=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}HV^{\perp}d\sigma

3.
An Alexandrov Reflection Principle in Hyperbolic Space

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we will use the Poincare ball model for hyperbolic space. This consists of the unit ball
B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the metric

g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}=\frac{4}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

where
\rho
is the radial coordinate of
B_{1}(0)
. This model is related to (
1.1
) by the change of variable

\displaystyle r(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\right).

(3.1)

Throughout this section, we will denote the position vector of a point
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\mathbf{x}
.

In
[
16
]
and following earlier work by Chow-Gulliver in
[
8
]
, I studied weak solutions of IMCF in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
using a parabolic analogue to the moving plane method of Alexandrov
[
1
]
for constant mean curvature hypersurfaces. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, composition of a weak solution of IMCF with reflection about a fixed hyperplane produces a new weak solution. The key in
[
16
]
is a comparison principle within the lower half-plane between the original solution and the reflected one. For a survey of the Euclidean version of this method, see
[
9
]
.

Here we consider sphere inversions in the Poincare ball, which are the analogues of reflections. For every constant
\lambda\in(0,1)
and point
\theta\in\partial B_{1}(0)
, we consider the ball
B_{R_{\lambda}}(x_{\lambda,\theta})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius

R_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{-1}-\lambda)

(3.2)

and centered at the point

\mathbf{x}_{\lambda,\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{-1}+\lambda)\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}(0).

(3.3)

B_{R_{\lambda}}(x_{\lambda,\theta})
lies a distance
\lambda
from
0\in B_{1}(0)
, is centered on the line containing
0
and
\theta
, and intersects
\partial B_{1}(0)
orthogonally. Given these facts, one may verify that inversion
F_{\lambda,\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x_{\lambda,\theta}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x_{\lambda,\theta}\}
about
B_{R_{\lambda,\theta}}(x_{\lambda,\theta})
defined by

\mathbf{F}_{\lambda,\theta}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{R_{\lambda}^{2}}{||\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x_{\lambda,\theta}}||^{2}}\left(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\lambda,\theta}\right)+\mathbf{x}_{\lambda,\theta}

(3.4)

restricts to an isometry over
(B_{1}(0),g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})
. For a domain
\Omega_{0}\subset B_{1}(0)
we denote

\Omega^{\lambda,\theta}_{0}=F_{\lambda,\theta}(\Omega_{0}),

(3.5)

and consider the “lower half-space” region
H_{\lambda,\theta}\subset B_{1}(0)
given by

H_{\lambda,\theta}=B_{1}(0)\cap B_{R_{\lambda}}(x_{\lambda,\theta}).

(3.6)

Given a proper weak solution
u:(B_{1}(0),g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of IMCF, we define another weak solution
u_{\lambda,\theta}:(B_{1}(0),g_{\mathbb{H}}^{n+1})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

u_{\lambda,\theta}(x)=u\circ F_{\lambda,\theta}(x).

(3.7)

First, we derive a comparison principle between
u
and
u_{\lambda,\theta}
. Geometrically, the following proposition gives that if
\Omega_{0}^{\lambda,\theta}\setminus\overline{H_{\lambda,\theta}}\subset\Omega_{0}
, then the inverted image
\Omega_{t}^{\lambda,\theta}\setminus\overline{H_{\lambda,\theta}}
gets trapped in the evolving domain
\Omega_{t}
for every

Proposition 3.1

(Comparison Principle for Inverted Weak Solutions)
.

\Omega_{0}

for every

Proof.

For any

is a variational solution to IMCF on

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh(r_{+})\sinh(r_{t})}{\left(\sinh^{2}(r_{t})-\sinh^{2}(r_{+})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

To briefly address Theorem

be a proper weak IMCF on

\Sigma_{t}
is star-shaped and

from the above estimate, thus proving the assertion.

The Heintze-Karcher Inequality for Weak Mean Curvature

In

Theorem 4.1

Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}

Theorem 4.2

Let

\Sigma_{0}

1

Proposition 4.3

Let

For each

under (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}H+\left(|h|^{2}+\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\right)H

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}H+\left(|h|^{2}-(n-1)\right)H,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\psi_{k}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{\left(e^{(n-1)\epsilon}H+k^{-1}\right)^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\left(e^{(n-1)\epsilon}H\right)-\frac{1}{e^{(n-1)\epsilon}H+k^{-1}}H^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\frac{2e^{2(n-1)\epsilon}}{\left(e^{(n-1)\epsilon}H+k^{-1}\right)^{3}}|\nabla H|^{2}d\sigma\leq 0,

(4.5)

Theorem 4.4

Let

be a bounded domain. Suppose that

where

is the weak mean curvature of

5.
The Volumetric Minkowski Inequality

In this section, we prove the Minkowski inequality (
1.9

[

[
14
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and later Wei

33

in Schwarzschild space. Throughout this section, we will use the relation

\nabla^{2}f=fg_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}

\mathbb{H}^{n}

support function

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f,\nu(x)\rangle.

(5.2)

(

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Q(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2-n}{n-1}|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}fHd\sigma-n(n-1)\int_{\Omega_{t}}fd\Omega\right)

\displaystyle+|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f\right)H+f\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H\right)+fHd\sigma-n(n-1)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{f}{H}d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\frac{2-n}{n-1}|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}fHd\sigma-n(n-1)\int_{\Omega_{t}}fd\Omega\right)

\displaystyle+|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\phi-f\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{H}-\frac{|h|^{2}}{H}f+(n-1)\frac{f}{H}+fHd\sigma-n(n-1)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{f}{H}d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\frac{2-n}{n-1}|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}fHd\sigma-n(n-1)\int_{\Omega_{t}}fd\Omega\right)

\displaystyle+|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}2\phi-(H\phi+\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}f)\frac{1}{H}+\frac{n-2}{n-1}fH-\frac{|\mathring{h}|^{2}}{H}f-(n-1)^{2}\frac{f}{H}d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{|\mathring{h}|^{2}}{H}fd\sigma+n\left(-(n-1)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{f}{H}d\sigma+n\int_{\Omega_{t}}fd\Omega\right)\right)

\displaystyle-|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{|\mathring{h}|^{2}}{H}fd\sigma\leq 0.

Proposition 5.1

(Growth of the Bulk Term)
.

Let
u\in C^{0,1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})

\Omega_{0}

Given that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t_{2}}}fd\Omega-\int_{\Omega_{t_{1}}}fd\Omega\geq\int_{\Omega_{t_{2}}\setminus\Omega_{t_{1}}}h|\nabla u|d\Omega=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}hd\sigma ds=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\frac{f}{H}d\sigma ds.

Lemma 5.2

Let

-\int_{\Omega_{t}}fH\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}d\Omega=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\psi[2H\phi_{s}+f(H^{2}-|h|^{2})]d\Omega,

\Sigma_{s}=\{u=s\}

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{s}}-f|\nabla u|^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}d\sigma

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}fHd\sigma-nw_{n-1}^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}|\Sigma_{t}|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)

, II.

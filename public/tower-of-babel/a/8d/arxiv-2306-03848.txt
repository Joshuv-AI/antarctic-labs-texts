On the Minkowski inequality near the sphere
Otis Chodosh, Michael Eichmair, Thomas Koerber

Abstract
. We construct a sequence { Σ ℓ } ℓ = 1 ∞ \{\Sigma_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty} of closed, axially symmetric surfaces Σ ℓ ⊂ ℝ 3 \Sigma_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3} that converges to the unit sphere in W 2 , p ∩ C 1 W^{2,p}\cap C^{1} for every p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) p\in[1,\infty) and such that, for every ℓ \ell , ∫ Σ ℓ H Σ ℓ − 16 ​ π ​ | Σ ℓ | < 0 \int_{\Sigma_{\ell}}H_{\Sigma_{\ell}}-\sqrt{16\,\pi\,|\Sigma_{\ell}|}<0 where H Σ ℓ H_{\Sigma_{\ell}} is the mean curvature of Σ ℓ \Sigma_{\ell} . This shows that the Minkowski inequality with optimal constant fails even for perturbations of a round sphere that are small in W 2 , p ∩ C 1 W^{2,p}\cap C^{1} unless additional convexity assumptions are imposed.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a closed surface and

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}H-\sqrt{16\,\pi\,|\Sigma|}

where
|\Sigma|
is the area of
\Sigma
and
H\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
is the mean curvature computed with respect to the outward unit normal.

Let
S=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x|=1\}
be the unit sphere centered at the origin. Given
u\in C^{\infty}(S)
, we denote by

\Sigma(u)=\{(1+u(x))\,x:x\in S\}

the normal graph of
u
above
S
.

Note that
\mathcal{M}(S)=0
. A classical result of H. Minkowski
[
14
, §7]
asserts that, provided that
\Sigma
is convex,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)\geq 0\text{ with equality if and only if }\Sigma\text{ is a round sphere.}

P. Guan and J. Li
[
12
, Theorem 2]
have proven that (
1
) holds provided that
\Sigma
is star-shaped and mean-convex. G. Huisken has shown that (
1
) holds for outward-minimizing surfaces; see
[
12
, Theorem 6]
. J. Dalphin, A. Henrot, S. Masnou, and T. Takahashi
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
have established (
1
) in the case where
\Sigma
is axially symmetric and such that
\Sigma\cap\Pi
is connected for every affine plane
\Pi
orthogonal to the axis of symmetry.

By contrast, there are closed surfaces
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)<0
. The following examples of such surfaces have been constructed in
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 1
.

There is a sequence
\{\Sigma_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
of closed surfaces
\Sigma_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\Sigma_{\ell}|=4\,\pi
and
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\ell})\to-\infty
. There is a sequence
\{\tilde{\Sigma}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
of closed axially symmetric surfaces
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\tilde{\Sigma}_{\ell}|=4\,\pi
and
\mathcal{M}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{\ell})\to-8\,\pi
.

It is natural to ask whether the Minkowski inequality (
1
) holds for surfaces in a neighborhood of
S
. Since every
C^{2}
-perturbation of the round sphere is strictly convex, we consider lower regularity perturbations. In this direction, F. Glaudo
[
11
, Appendix A]
has recently proved the following result.

Theorem 2
.

There is a sequence
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
of functions
u_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(S)
with the following properties.

\circ

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma(u_{\ell}))\to-\infty

\circ

|u_{\ell}|_{C^{1}(S)}\to 0

The goal of this paper is to establish the following related result.

Theorem 3
.

There is a sequence
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
of axially symmetric functions
u_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(S)
with the following properties.

\displaystyle\circ\qquad|u_{\ell}|_{C^{1}(S)}\to 0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\circ\qquad|u_{\ell}|_{W^{2,p}(S)}\to 0\text{ for every }p\in[1,\infty)

\displaystyle\circ\qquad\int_{\Sigma(u_{\ell})}|\mathring{h}|^{2}\to 0

\displaystyle\circ\qquad\mathcal{M}(\Sigma(u_{\ell}))<0\text{ for every }\ell

Our interest in pursuing Theorem
3
stems from the following result due to the first-named and the second-named author; see
[
4
, Proposition E.4]
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions
u_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(S)
with
|u_{\ell}|_{C^{1}(S)}\to 0
. Then, as
\ell\to\infty
,

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma(u_{\ell}))\geq-o(1)\int_{\Sigma(u_{\ell})}|\mathring{h}|^{2}.

C. De Lellis and S. Müller
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
have proven the following quantitative Schur estimate.

Theorem 5
.

There exists a constant
c\geq 2
such that, for every closed surface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(H-|\Sigma|^{-1}\,\int_{\Sigma}H\right)^{2}\leq c\,\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{h}|^{2}.

Note that, if
\Sigma
has genus zero, then

\int_{\Sigma}\left(H-|\Sigma|^{-1}\,\int_{\Sigma}H\right)^{2}=2\,\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{h}|^{2}-\sqrt{64\,\pi}\,|\Sigma|^{-1/2}\,\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)-|\Sigma|^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)^{2}

by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem. Consequently, given
\varepsilon>0
, Theorem
4
shows that (
2
) holds with
c=2+\varepsilon
provided that
\Sigma
is sufficiently close to
S
in
W^{2,2}\cap C^{1}
. By contrast, in view of Theorem
3
, (
2
) is false with
c=2
, even for closed surfaces
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that are
W^{2,p}\cap C^{1}
-close to
S
where
p\in[1,\infty)
. See also
[
15
, Chapter 3]
and
[
11
]
for other, related considerations.

Remark 6
.

Theorem
4
implies that the surfaces
\Sigma(u_{\ell})
constructed by F. Glaudo as described in Theorem
2
satisfy

\int_{\Sigma(u_{\ell})}|\mathring{h}|^{2}\to\infty.

Theorem
4
has played a pivotal role in the classification of large stable constant mean curvature surfaces in initial data sets; see
[
4
, Lemma 4.8]
and also
[
9
, Lemma 22]
. This has led the authors to hope that there might exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that (
1
) holds provided that

\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{h}|^{2}<\varepsilon_{0}.

Theorem
3
dashes this hope.

By contrast, S. Brendle
[
3
]
has recently proved the Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
with optimal constant; see also the alternative proof of S. Brendle and the second-named author
[
2
]
. This result implies the following Minkowski inequality with non-optimal constant for mean-convex surfaces.

Theorem 7

(
[
3
, Corollary 2]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a closed surface with non-negative mean curvature. There holds

\int_{\Sigma}H\geq\sqrt{4\,\pi\,|\Sigma|}.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a closed surface with non-negative mean curvature such that
|\Sigma|=4\,\pi
. Theorem
7
contrasts Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
in the sense that
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)\geq-4\,\pi
. Moreover,
[
12
, Theorem 2]
contrasts Theorem
3
in the sense that
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)\geq 0
provided that
\Sigma
is
C^{1}
-close to
S_{1}(0)
. These reasons support the following conjecture; see also
[
6
, p. 2732]
.

Conjecture 8
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a closed surface with non-negative mean curvature. There holds
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere.

Remark 9
.

By
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, Conjecture
8
holds if
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is assumed to be closed, axially symmetric, and with non-negative mean curvature. Indeed, by the maximum principle,
\Sigma\cap\Pi
is connected for every affine plane
\Pi
orthogonal to the axis of symmetry.

Remark 10
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a closed surface with non-negative mean curvature and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
the compact domain bounded by
\Sigma
. V. Agostiniani, M. Fogagnolo, and L. Mazzieri
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
have shown that

(3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H\geq(384\,\pi^{2}\,|\Omega|)^{1/3}

with equality if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere. Note that (
3
) would follow from Conjecture
8
and the isoperimetric inequality.

F. Glaudo’s proof of Theorem
2
is based on adding dents with very negative mean curvature to the sphere. In view of Theorem
4
, the traceless second fundamental form of such dents is large in
L^{2}
. The construction of the examples in Theorem
3
is necessarily more subtle.

We now outline the proof of Theorem
3
. Let
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions
u_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
with zero mean such that
|u_{\ell}|_{C^{1}(S)}\to 0
. As in
[
11
, Appendix A]
, our starting point is a Taylor expansion of the functional
\mathcal{M}
off the unit sphere
S
. Indeed, we have

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\Sigma(u_{\ell}))=-\int_{S}|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}\,\Delta u_{\ell}+O(1)\,|u_{\ell}|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(S)}+O(1)\,|u_{\ell}|^{2}_{C^{1}(S)}\,|u_{\ell}|_{W^{1,2}(S)}\,|u_{\ell}|_{W^{2,2}(S)};

see Lemma
12
. For ease of exposition, we focus on the first two terms on the right-hand side of (
4
). The key idea is to exploit the cubic nature of the first of those terms. Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we consider

u_{\ell}=-\ell^{-2-\alpha}\,\sum_{i=0}^{2^{-5}\,\ell}v_{\ell_{i}}

where
v_{\ell_{i}}\in C^{\infty}(S)
are spherical harmonics whose frequency is comparable to
\ell
. A straightforward computation shows that
|u_{\ell}|_{W^{2,2}(S)}\to 0
. What is more, given
p\in[1,\infty)
, we show that
|u_{\ell}|_{W^{2,p}(S)}\to 0
provided that
\alpha>1-2/p
; see Lemma
15
. Next, we observe that

\int_{S}|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}\,\Delta u_{\ell}\sim\ell^{-2-3\,\alpha}\sum_{i_{1},\,i_{2},\,i_{3}=1}^{2^{-5}\,\ell}\int_{S}v_{\ell_{i_{1}}}\,v_{\ell_{i_{2}}}\,v_{\ell_{i_{3}}}\qquad\text{while}\qquad\int_{S}|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}\sim\ell^{-2-2\,\alpha}.

Choosing the spherical harmonics
v_{\ell_{i}}
carefully, we obtain that

\inf_{\ell,\,i_{1},\,i_{2},\,i_{3}}\ell^{2}\,\int_{S}v_{\ell_{i_{1}}}\,v_{\ell_{i_{2}}}\,v_{\ell_{i_{3}}}>0;

see Lemma
16
. We note that these estimates build on precise quantitative estimates for E. Wigner’s
3j
-symbol; see Appendix
B
.

2.
Graphs over Euclidean spheres

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(S)
and recall that
\Sigma(u)
is the normal graph of
u
over
S
. Let
g(u)
be the first fundamental form,
\nu(u)
the outward unit normal,
h(u)
the second fundamental form, and
H(u)
the mean curvature of
\Sigma(u)
. Let
g
be the first fundamental form of
S
and
f(u)=\sqrt{(1+u)^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}}
.

Lemma 11
.

The following identities hold.

\displaystyle\circ\qquad g(u)=(1+u)^{2}\,g+du\otimes du\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\circ\qquad g(u)^{-1}=(1+u)^{-2}\,\left(g^{-1}-f(u)^{-2}\,\nabla u\otimes\nabla u\right)

\displaystyle\circ\qquad\nu(u)=f(u)^{-1}\,((1+u)\,x-\nabla u)

\displaystyle\circ\qquad h(u)=f(u)^{-1}\,\big[(1+u)^{2}\,g+2\,du\otimes du-(1+u)\,\nabla du\big]

Lemma 12
.

Let
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions
u_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(S)
with
|u_{\ell}|_{C^{1}(S)}\to 0
and

\displaystyle\int_{S}u_{\ell}=0

for all
\ell
. Then

|\Sigma(u_{\ell})|=4\,\pi+O(1)\,|u_{\ell}|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(S)}

and

\int_{\Sigma(u_{\ell})}H=8\,\pi+\int_{S}\nabla du_{\ell}(\nabla u_{\ell},\nabla u_{\ell})+O(1)\,|u_{\ell}|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(S)}+O(1)\,|u_{\ell}|^{2}_{C^{1}(S)}\,|u_{\ell}|_{W^{1,2}(S)}\,|u_{\ell}|_{W^{2,2}(S)}.

Proof.

Note that

\sqrt{\det g(u_{\ell})}=(1+u_{\ell})\,f(u_{\ell})

and

\displaystyle H(u_{\ell})

\displaystyle=2\,f(u_{\ell})^{-1}+f(u_{\ell})^{-3}\,|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-(1+u_{\ell})^{-1}\,f(u_{\ell})^{-1}\,\Delta u_{\ell}+(1+u_{\ell})^{-1}\,f(u_{\ell})^{-2}\,\nabla du_{\ell}(\nabla u_{\ell},\nabla u_{\ell}).

Using Taylor’s theorem, we obtain

\sqrt{\det g(u_{\ell})}=1+2\,u_{\ell}+O(1)\,(|u_{\ell}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2})

and

\displaystyle H(u_{\ell})\,\sqrt{\det g(u_{\ell})}

\displaystyle=2\,(1+u_{\ell})+(1+u_{\ell})\,f(u_{\ell})^{-2}\,|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}-\Delta u_{\ell}+f(u_{\ell})^{-1}\,\nabla du_{\ell}(\nabla u_{\ell},\nabla u_{\ell})

\displaystyle=2\,(1+u_{\ell})-\Delta u_{\ell}+\nabla du_{\ell}(\nabla u_{\ell},\nabla u_{\ell})

\displaystyle\qquad+O(1)\,|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}+O(1)\,(|u_{\ell}|+|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2})\,|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}\,|\nabla^{2}u_{\ell}|.

The assertion follows from these estimates, using that

\int_{S}\Delta u_{\ell}=0

and

\displaystyle\int_{S}(|u_{\ell}|+|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2})\,|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{2}\,|\nabla^{2}u_{\ell}|

\displaystyle=O(1)\,|u_{\ell}|^{2}_{C^{1}(S)}\,\int_{S}|\nabla u_{\ell}|\,|\nabla^{2}u_{\ell}|

\displaystyle=O(1)\,|u_{\ell}|^{2}_{C^{1}(S)}\,|u_{\ell}|_{W^{1,2}(S)}\,|u_{\ell}|_{W^{2,2}(S)}.

∎

Appendix A
Spherical harmonics and Legendre polynomials

Recall that the eigenvalues of the operator

-\Delta:H^{2}(S)\to L^{2}(S)

are given by

\ell\,(\ell+1)
where
\ell\geq 0
is an integer. We let
\Lambda_{\ell}
denote the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
\ell\,(\ell+1)
and recall that
\dim\,\Lambda_{\ell}=2\,\ell+1
.

The Legendre polynomials
P_{\ell}
may be defined via a generating function as follows. Given
s\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,1)
, there holds

(5)

\displaystyle(1-2\,s\,t+t^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}P_{\ell}(s)\,t^{\ell}.

We define
v_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(S)
by

v_{\ell}(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})=P_{\ell}(-x^{3}).

Recall from, e.g.,
[
5
, §8]
that
v_{\ell}\in\Lambda_{\ell}
, namely,
-\Delta v_{\ell}=\ell\,(\ell+1)\,v_{\ell}
.

Lemma 17

(
[
5
, §8]
)
.

Let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\geq 0
be integers. The following identities holds.

\displaystyle\circ\qquad\frac{1}{4\,\pi}\,\int_{S}v_{\ell_{1}}\,v_{\ell_{2}}=\frac{1}{2\,\ell_{1}+1}\,\delta_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\circ\qquad\frac{1}{4\,\pi}\,\int_{S}\nabla v_{\ell_{1}}\cdot\nabla v_{\ell_{2}}=\frac{\ell_{1}\,(\ell_{1}+1)}{2\,\ell_{1}+1}\,\delta_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\circ\qquad\frac{1}{4\,\pi}\,\int_{S}\Delta v_{\ell_{1}}\,\Delta v_{\ell_{2}}=\frac{\ell_{1}^{2}\,(\ell_{1}+1)^{2}}{2\,\ell_{1}+1}\,\delta_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}

Lemma 18
.

Let
\ell_{1},\,\ell_{2},\,\ell_{3}\geq 0
be integers. There holds

\int_{S}\Delta v_{\ell_{1}}\,\nabla v_{\ell_{2}}\cdot\nabla v_{\ell_{3}}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\ell_{1}\,(\ell_{1}+1)\,\big[\ell_{2}\,(\ell_{2}+1)+\ell_{3}\,(\ell_{3}+1)-\ell_{1}\,(\ell_{1}+1)\big]\int_{S}v_{\ell_{1}}\,v_{\ell_{2}}\,v_{\ell_{3}}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\,(v_{\ell_{1}}\,v_{\ell_{2}}\,\nabla v_{\ell_{3}}+v_{\ell_{1}}\,v_{\ell_{3}}\,\nabla v_{\ell_{2}}-v_{\ell_{2}}\,v_{\ell_{3}}\,\nabla v_{\ell_{1}})

\displaystyle\qquad=2\,v_{\ell_{1}}\,\nabla v_{\ell_{2}}\cdot\nabla v_{\ell_{3}}+v_{\ell_{1}}\,v_{\ell_{2}}\,\Delta v_{\ell_{3}}+v_{\ell_{1}}\,v_{\ell_{3}}\,\Delta v_{\ell_{2}}-v_{\ell_{2}}\,v_{\ell_{3}}\,\Delta v_{\ell_{1}}.

Integrating and using that
v_{\ell_{i}}\in\Lambda_{i},\,i=1,\,2,\,3
, the assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 19
.

There holds, as
\ell\to\infty
,

|v_{\ell}|_{C^{0}(S)}=O(1).

Proof.

Given
x\in S
, consider the linear map
\Lambda_{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f\mapsto f(x)
. By the Riesz representation theorem, for every
x\in S
, there is a unique
F_{x}\in\Lambda_{\ell}
so that

f(x)=\int_{S}f\,F_{x}.

In particular,

|f(x)|\leq|f|_{L^{2}(S)}\,|F_{x}|_{L^{2}(S)}.

Since
SO(3)
acts transitively on
S
, it follows that
|F_{x}|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}
is independent of
x
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}
be an orthonormal basis of
\Lambda_{\ell}
, we write

F_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}\left(\int_{S}f_{i}\,F_{x}\right)\,f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}f_{i}(x)\,f_{i}

so that

|F_{x}|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}f_{i}(x)^{2}.

Integrating with respect to
x
, we obtain

4\,\pi\,|F_{x}|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}=2\,\ell+1.

It follows that

|f|_{C^{0}(S)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\,\ell+1}{4\,\pi}}\,|f|_{L^{2}(S)}=O(1)\,\ell^{1/2}\,|f|_{L^{2}(S)}

for all
f\in\Lambda_{\ell}
.

Using also Lemma
17
, the assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 20
.

There holds, as
\ell\to\infty
,

|\nabla v_{\ell}|_{C^{0}(S)}=O(1)\,\ell.

Proof.

Given
x\in S
, consider the linear map
\Lambda_{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
given by
f\mapsto(\nabla f)(x)
. By the Riesz representation theorem, there is a unique

\vec{F}_{x}=(F_{x}^{1},\,F_{x}^{2},\,F_{x}^{3})\in\Lambda_{\ell}\times\Lambda_{\ell}\times\Lambda_{\ell}

such that, for all
f\in\Lambda_{\ell}
,

\nabla f(x)=\int_{S}f\,\vec{F}_{x}

As in the proof of Lemma
19
, we may argue by symmetry that
|\vec{F}_{x}|_{L^{2}(S)}
is independent of
x
.

Given an orthonormal basis
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}
of
\Lambda_{\ell},
we write

\vec{F}_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}\left(\int_{S}f_{i}\,\vec{F}_{x}\right)\,f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}(\nabla f_{i})(x)\,f_{i}.

Note that

|\vec{F}_{x}|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}|\nabla f_{i}(x)|^{2}

Integrating with respect to
x
, we obtain that

4\,\pi\,|\vec{F}_{x}|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2\,\ell+1}|\nabla f_{i}|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}=\ell\,(\ell+1)(2\,\ell+1).

Thus,

|\nabla f|_{C^{0}(S)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\ell\,(\ell+1)\,(2\,\ell+1)}{4\,\pi}}\,|f|_{L^{2}(S)}=O(1)\,\ell^{3/2}\,|f|_{L^{2}(S)}

for every
f\in\Lambda_{\ell}
.

In conjunction with Lemma
17
, the assertion follows.
∎

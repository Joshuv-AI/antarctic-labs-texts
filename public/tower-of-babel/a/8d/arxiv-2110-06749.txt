”Infinite” properties of certain local cohomology modules of determinantal rings
Peter Schenzel

Abstract
. For given integers m , n ≥ 2 m,n\geq 2 there are examples of ideals I I of complete determinantal local rings ( R , 𝔪 ) , dim R = m + n − 1 , grade ⁡ I = n − 1 , (R,\mathfrak{m}),\dim R=m+n-1,\operatorname{grade}I=n-1, with the canonical module ω R \omega_{R} and the property that the socle dimensions of H I m + n − 2 ​ ( ω R ) H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R}) and H 𝔪 m ​ ( H I n − 1 ​ ( ω R ) ) H^{m}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R})) are not finite. In the case of m = n m=n , i.e. a Gorenstein ring, the socle dimensions provide further information about the τ \tau -numbers as studied in [ 10 ] . Moreover, the endomorphism ring of H I n − 1 ​ ( ω R ) H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R}) is studied and shown to be an R R -algebra of finite type but not finitely generated as R R -module generalizing an example of [ 15 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
I
denote an ideal of a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
with

\Bbbk=R/\mathfrak{m}
its residue field. Let
M

be a finitely generated
R
-module, and let
H^{i}_{I}(M),i\in\mathbb{Z},

denote the local cohomology modules of
M
with respect to
I
(see

[
3
]
or
[
1
]
for definitions). By
\operatorname{grade}(I,M)
we denote the length
of the largest regular sequence contained in
I
. In the case of
M=R
,
an equicharacteristic complete regular local ring, the following is known:

(a)

The Bass numbers
\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\Bbbk,H^{i}_{I}(R))
are finite
(see
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
).

(b)

The natural homomorphism
R\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{c}_{I}(R),H^{c}_{I}(R)),c=\operatorname{grade}I,

is an isomorphism if and only if
H^{i}_{I}(R)=0
for
i=d-1,d,
and
c<d-1,d=\dim R

(see
[
15
]
).

The socle
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{i}_{I}(M))
is in general not finite dimensional as has been
shown at first by R. Hartshorne (see
[
5
]
) by disproving a question
by A. Grothendieck about cofiniteness of local cohomology, i.e. the finiteness of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/I,H^{i}_{I}(R))
.
In their paper Huneke and Koh
(see
[
7
]
) studied cofiniteness of various ideals. Moreover they showed that for a field
\Bbbk
of
characteristic zero and a polynomial ring
R=\Bbbk[x_{i,j},1\leq 2,1\leq j\leq 3]
the local cohomology

H^{3}_{I}(R)
is not
I
-cofinite for
I
the ideal generated by the maximal minors of the

2\times 3
-matrix
(x_{i,j})
. A large class of
local cohomology modules with infinite socle has been constructed by
T. Marley and C. Vassilev (see
[
11
]
).

The main topic of the present note is to discuss and relate the properties (a) and (b)
above in the non-smooth situation of certain determinantal rings. More precisely:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
denote the completion of the coordinate ring of the vanishing ideal of the

2\times 2
minors of an
m\times n
-matrix of variables and therefore
\dim R=m+n-1
. For the ideal
I
generated by the first
n-1
columns we have
\dim R/I=m
and

\operatorname{grade}I=n-1
. Let
\omega_{R}
denote the canonical module of
R
.

(a)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R}))
and

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m+n-2}_{I}(R))
are not finite dimensional, that is
I

is not cofinite and
H^{i}_{I}(\omega_{R})=0
for
i\not=n-1,m+n-2
.

(b)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R}),H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R}))
is an
R
-algebra of finite type, not finitely generated over
R
.

(c)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R})))

and
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R})))
are
not finite dimensional.

In the case of
m=n
the ring
R
is a Gorenstein ring and
\omega_{R}\cong R
. For
m=n=2
we recover R. Harsthorne’s example (see
[
5
]
) by completely different arguments. For an ideal
I
the cohomological dimension is defined by
\operatorname{cd}I=\sup\{i\in\mathbb{N}|H^{i}_{I}(R)\not=0\}
(introduced by R. Hartshorne (see
[
4
]
)).
Whence,
\operatorname{cd}I=m+n-2
for the examples above. That is, possible non-cofinite
ideals could occur in the highest non-vanishing cohomological level. In the case of a
d
-dimensional
Gorenstein ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
the socle dimension
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{d-j}_{I}(R)))

are called
\tau
-numbers of type
(i,j)
of
I
(see
[
10
]
). In the case of a regular local ring containing
a field they coincide with the Lyubeznik numbers introduced by Lyubeznik in
[
9
]
(see
[
10
, 3.5]
for the
details). Therefore, the above results yield
further information about these
\tau
-numbers.

In Section 2 we prove the essentials about local cohomology for our purposes. Theorem

2.2
is the needed technical result for our constructions. Furthermore, we
introduce the basics for the construction of our examples, based about some results
of Segre varieties. In Section 3 we prove the statements of the examples
introduced in Section 2. For the needed results about Commutative Algebra we refer to Matsumura’s
book
[
12
]
. For a few homological arguments resp. some facts about determinantal ideals we refer to
[
16
]
resp. to
[
2
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and constructions

At the beginning let us recall a few basics from local duality.

Notations and Recalls 2.1
.

(A) Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
denote a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring
with
E=E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})
the injective hull of its residue field. Suppose that

R
possesses a normalized dualizing complex
D
in the sense of

[
16
, 11.4.6]
. Then
R
admits a canonical module
\omega_{R}
(see

[
13
]
). Moreover, for an arbitrary
R
-module
X
there is the following
Local Duality Theorem

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d-i}(X,\omega_{R}),E)

for all
i\geq 0
(see e.g.
[
16
, 10.4.3]
). For further properties of
\omega_{R}

we refer to
[
16
]
and
[
13
]
.

(B) With the notation of (A) let
M
denote a finitely generated
R
-module. Then

\omega_{R}(M)
, the canonical module of
M
(in the sense of
[
13
]
), exists
and there is an isomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(M,\omega_{R})\cong\omega_{R}(M)
, where

c=\dim R-\dim_{R}M
(see also

[
13
]
for more details).

(C) With the notation of (A) it follows that
\omega_{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}D
, a minimal injective
resolution of
\omega_{R}
, is a normalized dualizing complex in the sense of

[
16
, 11.4.6]
. Let
M
denote a finitely generated
R
-module. Then there
is a natural morphism

M\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,D),D)

that is an isomorphism in cohomology. Let
c=d-\dim_{R}M
, then it induces a
natural homomorphism

M\to\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(M,\omega_{R}),\omega_{R})=\omega_{R}(\omega_{R}(M)).

(D) It is known that for an
R
-module
M
the module
\omega_{R}(\omega_{R}(M))
is the

S_{2}
-fication of
M
(see
[
13
]
). Moreover, the natural homomorphism

M\to\omega_{R}(\omega_{R}(M))
induces a short exact sequence

0\to M/u(M)\to\omega_{R}(\omega_{R}(M))\to C\to 0,

where
u(M)
denotes the intersection of those primary components of
M
that are
of highest dimension
\dim_{R}M
and
\dim_{R}C\leq\dim_{R}M-2
for
C
the cokernel.
Let
I\subset R
an ideal of grade
c
in the Cohen-Macaulay ring
R
. Then we get
an inverse system of homomorphisms
R/I^{\alpha}\to\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(R/I^{\alpha},\omega_{R}),\omega_{R})
. By passing to the inverse limit
there is a homomorphism

\hat{R}^{I}\to B:=\varprojlim\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(R/I^{\alpha},\omega_{R}),\omega_{R})\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}),\omega_{R}),

where
\hat{R}^{I}
denotes the

I
-adic completion of
I
. Note that the inverse maps are induced by

R/I^{\alpha+1}\to R/I^{\alpha}
. By a slight modification
of the arguments of
[
15
, Section 3]
it follows that
B
is a commutative ring.

(E) For an arbitrary
R
-module
X
there is a natural homomorphism
R\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(X,X),r\mapsto\phi(r)
, the multiplication by
r\in R
on
X
. Its kernel is

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}X
. In general the endomorphism ring
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(X,X)
is not commutative.

For the property of the ring
B
in
2.1
(D) we need a few more
intrinsic properties.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},\Bbbk)
be a
d
-dimensional complete local Cohen-Macaulay
ring. Let
\omega_{R}
denote its canonical module. For an ideal
I\subset R
with

c=\operatorname{grade}I
we have:

(a)

There are isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}),H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}))\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}),\omega_{R})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R})),E).

(b)

The endomorphism ring
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}),H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}))

is a finitely generated
R
-module if and only if
\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R})))
is finite.

(c)

The natural ring homomorphism
\rho:R\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}),H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}))
is onto if and only if

\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R})))=1

Proof.

(a): In order to prove the first isomorphism we refer to
[
14
, 2.2 (d)]
. In order to proof the
second one we have that

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}),\omega_{R})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R})),E)

as follows by the Local Duality for the complete local Cohen-Macaulay ring
R

(see
2.1
(A)).

(b): By virtue of (a) we have isomorphisms

B/\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha},H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R})),E)

that form an inverse system. By passing to the inverse limit there are isomorphisms

\hat{B}^{\mathfrak{m}}=\varprojlim\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha},H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R})),E)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}))),E)\cong B

because of
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}H^{d-c}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}))\subseteq V(\mathfrak{m})
.
That is,
B
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adic complete. Then
B
is a finitely generated

R
-module if and only if
\dim_{\Bbbk}B/\mathfrak{m}B<\infty
(see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 8.4]
).

(c): Since
H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R})\not=0

it follows that the natural homomorphism

\rho\otimes 1_{\Bbbk}:R\otimes_{R}\Bbbk\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}),H^{c}_{I}(\omega_{R}))\otimes_{R}\Bbbk

is not zero. Then the statement follows by (b).
∎

Construction 2.3
.

(A) Let
\Bbbk
denote a field. Let
m,n\geq 2
denote integers and let

\mathcal{X}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{11}&\ldots&x_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{m1}&\ldots&x_{mn}\end{pmatrix}

denote a
m\times n
matrix of

mn
variables over
\Bbbk
. Let
P=\Bbbk[[\mathcal{X}]]

denote the formal powers series ring in the
mn
variables of
\mathcal{X}
.
We define
R=P/I_{2}
, where

I_{2}=I_{2}(\mathcal{X})
denotes the ideal generated by the
2\times 2

minors of
\mathcal{X}
. Then
R
is a local Cohen-Macaulay domain of dimension
m+n-1

(see
[
6
]
).

(B) We put
\underline{x}_{i}=x_{1i},\ldots,x_{mi},i=1,\ldots,n,
the elements of the

i
-th column of
\mathcal{X}
and
\underline{x}=\underline{x}_{n}
the elements of the last column.
We define
I=(\underline{x}_{1},\ldots,\underline{x}_{n-1})R
the ideal generated by the elements
of the first
n-1
columns of
\mathcal{X}
. Then
\dim R/I=m
and

0ptI=\operatorname{grade}I=n-1
.
In the following we recall the form ring
G_{I}(R)
of
I\subset R
. First we define
a
m\times n
-matrix

\mathcal{Y}=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}&\ldots&T_{1,n-1}&x_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
T_{m1}&\ldots&T_{m,n-1}&x_{mn}\end{pmatrix}

where
T_{ij},1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n-1,
are variables over
R
.
Let
J=I_{2}(\mathcal{Y})
the ideal generated by the
2\times 2
-minors of
\mathcal{Y}
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
denote the set of all variables
T_{i,j},1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n-1
of degree 1 and let
\mathcal{G}=(R/I)[\mathcal{T}]
. Then consider the
induced homogeneous map

\Theta:\mathcal{G}/J\to Gr_{I}(R),\;T_{ij}+J\mapsto x_{ij}+I/I^{2},\;1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n-1.

Note that it is well defined and surjective. Clearly,
\mathcal{G}/J

is a Cohen-Macaulay determinantal domain with
\dim\mathcal{G}/J=m+n-1
, i.e.

J
is a prime ideal. Moreover

Gr_{I}(R)
is a ring with
\dim Gr_{I}(R)=m+n-1
. Let
\mathcal{J}
be the preimage of
\ker\Theta
in
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{G}
denote the preimage of a highest dimensional prime ideal in
Gr_{I}(R)
.
Then
J\subseteq\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
and
\dim\mathcal{G}/J=\dim\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{P}
and therefore
J=\ker\Theta
. That is,
\Theta
is an isomorphism and
Gr_{I}(R)
is a domain.

(C) Next we introduce the ring
S=\Bbbk[[\underline{x},\underline{a}]]
, where
\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
with
x_{i}=x_{in},i=1,\ldots,m
and
\underline{a}=a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}
are sequences of
variables over
\Bbbk
. Then
S
is a regular local ring with
\underline{a}=a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}
a regular sequence. Therefore
Gr_{(\underline{a})}(S)\cong(S/\underline{a})[\underline{T}]\cong\Bbbk[[\underline{x}]][\underline{T}]
with

\underline{T}=T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1}
variables of degree
1
. We define a ring homomorphism

\phi:R\to S,\;x_{ij}\mapsto a_{i}x_{j},\;i=1,\ldots,n-1,j=1,\ldots,m.

Note that
S
is a domain and
\dim R=\dim S=m+n+1
. Therefore

\phi
is injective. The homomorphism
\phi
extends to a homomorphism

\psi:Gr_{I}(R)\to Gr_{(\underline{a})}(S),\;T_{ij}+J\mapsto x_{j}T_{i},\;i=1,\ldots,m,j=1,\ldots,n-1.

Since both
Gr_{I}(R)
and
Gr_{(\underline{a})}(S)
are domains of the same dimension
\psi

is injective.

3.
Examples and remarks

In the following we denote by
\omega_{R}
the canonical module of a Cohen-Macaulay
ring
R
. For basic definitions and properties of
\omega_{R}
we refer to
[
2
, 3.3]
,
the generalization in
[
13
]
and the summary in
2.1
. For a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})

we denote by
E=E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})
the injective hull of the residue field.

Example 3.1
.

We fix the notation of
2.3
, that is,
R

is the determinantal Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
m+n-1

with
I=(\underline{x}_{1},\ldots,\underline{x}_{n-1})R
the ideal generated by the elements
of the first
n-1
columns of
\mathcal{X}
.
Then there are the following results:

(a)

H^{i}_{I}(\omega_{R})\not=0,i=n-1,m+n-2,
that is,
\operatorname{cd}(I)=m+n-2
.

(b)

\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R}))
and

\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m+n-2}_{I}(R))
are not finite, that is
I

is not cofinite.

(c)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R}),H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R}))
is a commutative
Noetherian ring, not finitely generated over
R
.

(d)

\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R})))

and
\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R})))
are
not finite.

Proof.

The ring homomorphism
\phi
of
2.3
(C) induces ring homomorphisms

\phi^{\alpha}:R/I^{\alpha}\to S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S\;\mbox{ for all }\alpha\geq 1,

which are well-defined.
We claim that
\phi^{\alpha}
is injective for all
\alpha\geq 1
. The
restriction
\psi^{\alpha}
of the injection
\psi
of
2.3
(C) to degree
\alpha
yields injective homomorphisms

{\psi}^{\alpha}:I^{\alpha}/I^{\alpha+1}\to(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha+1}S\;\mbox{ for all }\alpha\geq 0.

Let
D_{\alpha}:=\operatorname{Coker}\psi^{\alpha}
and let
\underline{x}\cdot\underline{a}
be the sequence of elements

\{x_{i}a_{j}:i=1,\ldots,n-1,j=1,\ldots,m\}
. We define
C_{\alpha}=\operatorname{Coker}\phi^{\alpha}=S/((\underline{a})^{\alpha}S+S[[\underline{x}\cdot\underline{a}]])

because of
\operatorname{Im}\phi=S[[\underline{x}\cdot\underline{a}]]
. Now it follows that

D_{\alpha}\cong((\underline{a})^{\alpha}S+S[[\underline{x}\cdot\underline{a}]])/((\underline{a})^{\alpha+1}S+S[[\underline{x}\cdot\underline{a}]]).

as a consequence of the following commutative diagram with exact
rows and the snake lemma

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\to&I^{\alpha}/I^{\alpha+1}&\to&R/I^{\alpha+1}&\to&R/I^{\alpha}&\to&0\\
&&\downarrow\psi^{\alpha}&&\downarrow\phi^{\alpha+1}&&\downarrow\phi^{\alpha}&&\\
0&\to&(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha+1}S&\to&S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha+1}S&\to&S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S&\to&0.\end{array}

Since
\psi^{\alpha}
is injective for all
\alpha\geq 0
as the restriction of
\phi^{\alpha}
it
follows by induction that
\phi^{\alpha}
is injective too.
Therefore
D_{\alpha}
is spanned by
(\underline{x})^{i}(\underline{a})^{\alpha}
with
0\leq i<\alpha

over
\Bbbk
. As an
R
-module it is finitely generated. For the radical
of the annihilator it follows that
\operatorname{Rad}_{S}\operatorname{Ann}_{S}D_{\alpha}=(\underline{x},\underline{a})
.
So that
C_{\alpha},\alpha\geq 1,
is an
R
-module of finite length.
There is the short exact sequence

0\to R/I^{\alpha}\to S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S\to C_{\alpha}\to 0\;\mbox{ for all }\alpha\geq 1.

Because of
C_{\alpha}\cong\oplus_{\beta=1}^{\alpha-1}D_{\beta}
it follows that
it is an
R
-module of finite length. Since
S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S
is a finitely generated
Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module it implies that
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I^{\alpha})\cong C_{\alpha}
and
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I^{\alpha})=0
for all
i\not=1,m
. The
above short exact sequences form an inverse system of short exact sequences.
By passing to the inverse limit we get a short exact sequence

0\to R\to S\to\varprojlim H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I^{\alpha})\to 0.

Note that the first family is given by surjective maps. By virtue of the Local Duality
Theorem for a Cohen-Macaulay ring (see
2.1
(A)) there are isomorphisms

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I^{\alpha})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{m+n-1-i}(R/I^{\alpha},\omega_{R}),E)

for all
\alpha\geq 1
and by passing to the inverse limit
\varprojlim H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I^{\alpha})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{m+n-1-i}_{I}(\omega_{R}),E)
for all
i
.
Therefore
H^{i}_{I}(\omega_{R})=0
for
i\not=n-1,m+n-2
and

\varprojlim H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I^{\alpha})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R}),E)\not=0.

Because
S
is not a finitely generated
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R}),E)
is not finitely generated too. By the isomorphism

\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{n}_{I}(\omega_{R}),E)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R})),E)

and by Matlis Duality
\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R}))

is not finite.
Moreover,
H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R})\cong H^{m+n-2}_{I}(R)\otimes_{R}\omega_{R}
so that

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{m+n-2}_{I}(\omega_{R}),E)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega_{R},\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{m+n-2}_{I}(R),E))
. Because
\omega_{R}
is a finitely generated
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{m+n-2}_{I}(R),E)

is not finitely generated and – as above –
\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Bbbk,H^{m+n-2}_{I}(R))
is not finite.
That is, (a) and (b) of
3.1
are proved.

Now recall that
S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S

is an
m
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring, finitely generated over
R
and
\dim_{R}C_{\alpha}=0
. Therefore,

S/(\underline{a})^{\alpha}S\cong\omega_{R}(\omega_{R}(R/I^{\alpha}))\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{n-1}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{n-1}(R/I^{\alpha},\omega_{R}),\omega_{R})

(by view
of
2.1
(D)). By passing to the inverse limit of the corresponding inverse and by Theorem
2.2
(a) it yields that
S\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R}),H^{n-1}_{I}(\omega_{R}))
, whence
(c) is shown.
The first claim in (d) follows by
2.2
(b) since
S
is not finitely generated
over
R
. The second one is clear by (b) since

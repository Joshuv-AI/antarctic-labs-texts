Twisted recurrence for dynamical systems with exponential decay of correlations


Abstract
We study the set of points returning infinitely often to a sequence of targets dependent on the starting points. With an assumption of decay of correlations for L 1 L^{1} against bounded variations, we prove a generalized quantitative recurrence result under Lipschitz twists.



Jiajie Zheng

1
Introduction

Let
(X,d)
be a separable and compact metric space, and let
(X,\mu,T)
be a Borel probability measure-preserving system. The Poincaré Recurrence Theorem, see e.g.
[
EW
]
states that almost all points in measurable dynamical systems return close to themselves under a measure-preserving map; i.e.,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}d(T^{n}x,x)=0\quad\text{for almost every }x\in X.

Boshernitzan quantified the speed of recurrence in
[
Bo
]
. Namely, if the
\alpha
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
X
is
\sigma
-finite for some
\alpha>0
, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{1/\alpha}d(T^{n}x,x)<\infty\quad\text{for almost every }x\in X.

A natural generalization of the recurrence speed is to consider the following set

R(\psi):=\{x\in X:d(T^{n}x,x)<\psi(n)\text{ for infinitely many }n\}

given a function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
. Much has been done on the quantitative recurrence theory since then; for example, see
[
BF
,
CWW
,
DFL
,
HLSW
,
KZ
,
KKP
,
Pe
]
.

A topic closely related to recurrence theory is the so-called shrinking target problem, which is concerned with determining the speed at which the orbit of a
\mu
-generic point accumulates near a fixed point
y\in X
. More precisely, for
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
and a given point
y\in X
, one can define the set

A(\psi,y):=\{x\in X:d(T^{n}x,y)<\psi(n)\text{ for infinitely many }n\}.

There have been plenty of results concerning the zero-one laws for
\mu(A(\varphi,y))
in specific systems; for example, see
[
CK
,
FMP
,
HNPV
,
KM
,
LLVZ
,
Ph
]
.

A more general setting called twisted recurrence, which can specialize into shrinking target problem and quantitative recurrence, was introduced in
[
KZ
,
LWW
]
. For
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
and a Borel measurable function
f:X\to X
, one can consider the set

R(\psi,f):=\{x\in X:d(T^{n}x,f(x))<\psi(n)\text{ for infinitely many }n\}.

Clearly
R(\psi,f)=R(\psi)
if
f
is the identity function and
R(\psi,f)=A(\psi,y)
if
f
is the constant function with value
y
. When
f
is Lipschitz, the zero-one laws for
\mu(R(\psi,f))
were proved for some special systems in
[
KZ
,
LWW
]
, including classical dynamical systems like
\beta
-transformations, Gauss transformations and left shift on Cantor sets. Specifically, in these cases

\mu(R(\psi,f))=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(n,x)^{\delta}<\infty\\
1&\text{if }\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(n,x)^{\delta}=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\delta
is the Hausdorff dimension of the support of
\mu
.

In this paper, we prove quantitative Lipschitz recurrent properties of dynamical systems with exponential decay of correlations. To state the main result of the paper, we need to adapt and modify the settings and assumptions from
[
KKP
]
. For the rest of the paper, let
X=[0,1]
and
d
be the standard metric, and assume for any sequence of positive real number
\{M_{n}\}_{n}
contained in
(0,1)
, there exists a sequence of functions
\{r_{n}:X\to(0,1)\}_{n}
such that
r_{n}(x)=\inf\{r:\mu(B(x,r))=M_{n}\}
for all
x\in X
. For a function
f:X\to X
, the
f
-twisted recurrence set we are interested is defined by

R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f):=\{x\in X:T^{n}x\in B(f(x),r_{n}(x))\text{ for infinitely many }n\}.

We say that
\mu
is
Ahlfors regular
if there exist constants
c,\delta>0
such that

\frac{1}{c}r^{s}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq cr^{s}\quad\forall x\in\text{Supp}X\text{ and balls }B(x,r)\subset X

and that
\mu
is
upper Ahlfors regular
if
there exist constants
c,\delta>0
such that

\mu(B(x,r))\leq cr^{s}\quad\forall x\in\text{Supp}X\text{ and balls }B(x,r)\subset X.

(1)

If
\mu
is Ahlfors regular, then
R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f)=R(\psi,f)
where
\psi(n)=r_{n}(x)
. In general, these definitions are different.
Before stating our main theorems, we now specify the class of functions
f
which we deal with by our technique.
f:X\to X
is said to be
Lipschitz
if

\sup_{x,y\in X,x\neq y}\frac{d(f(x),f(y))}{d(x,y)}<\infty.

(2)

Definition 1
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be a measure-preserving system and
p:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a sequence. We say that the
correlations for the system decay as
p
for
L^{1}
against bounded variation (BV)
, if

\left|\int(f\circ T^{n})\cdot g\,d\mu-\int f\,d\mu\int g\,d\mu\right|\leq||f||_{L^{1}}\cdot||g||_{\text{BV}}\cdot p(n)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all functions
f
with
||f||_{1}:=\int|f|\,d\mu<\infty
and
g
with
||g||_{\text{BV}}:=\sup_{x_{i}}\sum|g(x_{i+1})-g(x_{i})|+\sup|g|<\infty
.

Remark 1
.

Definition
1
is weaker than the uniform mixing condition found in
[
KZ
]
, as for any balls
E,F\subset X
, we can take
f=\chi_{E}
and
g=\chi_{F}
and we will get

\left|\mu(T^{-n}E\cap F)-\mu(E)\mu(F)\right|\leq 3\mu(E)p(n).

Remark 2
.

For a non-increasing function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we know that there exists some

\alpha\in\mathcal{W}(\psi):=\{\alpha\in[0,1]:|q\alpha-p|<\psi(q)\text{ for infinitely many natural numbers }p,q\}.

Then consider the system

T:[0,1]\to[0,1],\quad x\mapsto x+\alpha\,(\bmod{1})

together with the Lebesgue measure. Then
R(\psi,\text{Id})=[0,1]
, where
\text{Id}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is the identity function. The convergence case of Theorem
1
would fail; hence some mixing condition is need for a zero-one law for
R(\psi,f)
to hold. For more details, see
[
KZ
, §2]
.

We first state the sufficient condition for
R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f)=0
, which depends on the convergence of
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}
. If there exists a countable partition of subinterval
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
so that
f|_{X_{i}}
is Lipschitz for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, then we say
f
is
piecewise Lipschitz
. If there exists a countable partition of subinterval
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
so that
f|_{X_{i}}
is monotone for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, then we say
f
is
piecewise monotone
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a function and assume the correlations for
(X,\mu,T)
decay as
p
for
L^{1}
against BV with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(n)<\infty
. Let
\{M_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence contained in
(0,1)
. Suppose
f:X\to X
is piecewise Lipschitz and piecewise monotone. If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}<\infty
, then
\mu(R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f))=0
.

For the full measure part,

Theorem 2
.

Let
p:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
(X,\mu,T)
,
\{M_{n}\}_{n}
and
f:X\to X
be as in Theorem
1
. Additionally, suppose that

•

There exist
C>0
and
0<\gamma<1
such that
p(n)=C\gamma^{n}
.

•

\mu
is upper Ahlfors regular.

•

For any
q>0
,

\limsup_{N}\sum_{n=\lfloor q\log N\rfloor}^{N}M_{n}=\infty

(3)

Then
\mu(R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f))=1
.

In
[
KKP
]
, the authors proved Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
for
f
being the identity map. We will apply some of the ideas and techniques used in the proof in
[
KKP
]
, but the proof is different due to the new setup.

We get an immediate corollary for Ahlfors regular systems.

Corollary 3
.

Let
p(n)=C\gamma^{n}
for some
C>0
and
0<\gamma<1
and suppose the correlations for
(X,\mu,T)
decays as
p
for
L^{1}
against BV. Suppose
\mu
is
\delta
-Ahlfors regular. Assume
f
is piecewise Lipschitz and piecewise monotone. Then

1.

If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(n)^{\delta}<\infty
, then
R(\psi,f)
is null.

2.

If for any
q>0
,

\limsup_{N}\sum_{n=\lfloor q\log N\rfloor}^{N}M_{n}=\infty,

(4)

then
R(\psi,f)
is full.

We shall remark that Corollary
3
is a generalization of the convergence part of
[
KZ
, Theorem 1.2]
in the case
X=[0,1]
. Most notably, the expanding, bounded distortion and conformality assumptions are omitted. Corollary
3
partially generalizes the divergence case of
[
KZ
, Theorem 1.2]
, with a stronger summability assumption of the measures of the targets (
4
).

The structure of the paper is as follows. In §2, we reduce the proofs of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
to the case where
f:X\to X
is Lipschitz and it only changes monotonicity at finitely many points. In §3 we construct a sequence of measurable sets whose limsup set is
R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f)
, and we estimate the measure of each set and conclude Theorem
1
. In §4, we study quasi-independence properties of this sequence and prove Theorem
2
.

2
Piecewise Lipschitz and piecewise monotone twists

By the properties of
R(\psi,f)
, to prove Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
, it suffices to show the statements for
f
Lipschitz and monotone.

Lemma 4
.

Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
hold for
f
Lipschitz and monotone.

We will prove Lemma
4
in §§3-4. Here we first conclude Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
from this lemma.

Proofs of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
.

Suppose there exists a countable collection of disjoint open intervals
\{X_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
so that
\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}X_{i}
is full and
f
is Lipschitz and monotone on each
X_{i}
. Then for each
i\in\mathcal{I}
,
f
is bounded on
X_{i}
and hence we can define

f_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if }x\in(a_{i},b_{i})\\
\lim_{x\to a_{i}^{-}}f(x)&\text{if }x\leq a_{i}\\
\lim_{x\to b_{i}^{+}}f(x)&\text{if }x\geq b_{i}.\end{cases}

Then
f_{i}
is Lipschitz and monotone. By Lemma
4
, Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
hold for
f_{i}
for each
i\in\mathcal{I}
. When
\mu(R(\psi,f_{i}))=0
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
,

\mu(R(\psi,f))=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mu(R(\psi,f)\cap X_{i})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mu(R(\psi,f_{i})\cap X_{i})=0;

when
\mu(R(\psi,f_{i}))=1
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
,

\mu(R(\psi,f))=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mu(R(\psi,f)\cap X_{i})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mu(R(\psi,f_{i})\cap X_{i})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mu(X_{i})=1,

so the theorems are proved once we prove Lemma
4
in the following sections.
∎

3
The convergence part

In this section we prove the convergence part of Lemma
4
, thereby fixing
p:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
(X,\mu,T)
,
\{M_{n}\}_{n}
and
f:X\to X
to be Lipschitz and monotone. Let us define

R_{n}(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f):=\{x\in X:T^{n}x\in B(f(x),r_{n}(x))\}.

Without ambiguity, we shall denote
R_{n}(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f)
simply by
R_{n}
. Clearly
R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}R_{n}
.

We first prove a fact about the functions
r_{n}
.

Lemma 5
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
r_{n}
is
1
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in[0,1]
. Without the loss of generality, suppose
r_{n}(x)\leq r_{n}(y)
. Then

\displaystyle B(x,r_{n}(x))\subset B(y,r_{n}(x)+d(x,y)),

so
\mu(B(y,r_{n}(x)+d(x,y)))\geq M_{n}
and hence

\displaystyle r_{n}(y)\leq r_{n}(x)+d(x,y),

i.e.,
|r_{n}(y)-r_{n}(x)|\leq|x-y|
.
∎

For each
n
, we define
Y_{n}
to be a subset of
[0,1]^{2}
such that

Y_{n}=\{(x,y):y\in B(f(x),r_{n}(f(x)))\}.

Then we have

Lemma 6
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
Y_{n}
is an open subset of
[0,1]^{2}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We prove that
Y_{n}
has closed complement in
[0,1]^{2}
. Let
\{(x_{m},y_{m})\}_{m}
be a Cauchy sequence in the complement of
Y_{n}
and we denote its limit in
[0,1]^{2}
by
(x,y)
. We show that
(x,y)\not\in Y_{n}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
f
is continuous, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
so that

\displaystyle|f(x_{k})-f(x)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{L}\quad\text{and}\quad|y_{k}-y|<\varepsilon.

Then

\displaystyle|f(x)-y|\geq

\displaystyle|f(x_{k})-y_{k}|-|f(x_{k})-f(x)|-|y_{k}-y|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r_{n}(f(x_{k}))-2\varepsilon

\displaystyle\underset{\text{Lemma }\ref{lemma3}}{\geq}

\displaystyle r_{n}(f(x))-3\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is chosen arbitrarily, we must have
|f(x)-y|\geq r_{n}(f(x))
as desired.
∎

Now we are ready to estimate the measure of
R_{n}
for each
n
.

Lemma 7
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

|\mu(R_{n})-M_{n}|\leq 3p(n).

Proof.

Define
F_{n}:[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
to be the characteristic function of
Y_{n}
. Since
Y_{n}
is open, we can approximate
F_{n}
by the following a sequence of uniformly continuous functions
\{F_{n,k}\}_{k}
, where

\displaystyle F_{n,k}(x,y):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }(x,y)\not\in Y_{n}\\
\min\{1,kd((x,y),\partial Y_{n})&\text{if }(x,y)\in Y_{n}\end{cases}

and
\partial Y_{n}
denotes the boundary of
Y_{n}
. Note that
\{F_{n,k}\}_{k}
is increasing in
k
and it converges pointwise to
F_{n}
, so by the monotone convergence theorem, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
k
so that

\left|\int F_{n}(x,T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)-F_{n,k}(x,T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)\right|<\varepsilon

and

\left|\int F_{n}-\int F_{n,k}\right|<\varepsilon.

Since
F_{n,k}
is
2k
-Lipschitz, we can choose a partition by intervals
\{I_{h}\}_{h=0}^{m-1}
of
[0,1]
so that

\left|F_{n,k}(x,y)-\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\chi_{I_{h}}(y)\right|<\varepsilon

for all
x,y\in[0,1]
, where
y_{h}
is the middle point of
I_{h}
. Then consider the integral

\displaystyle\int\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\chi_{I_{h}}(T^{n}x)

(5)

For each summand in (
5
), apply the decay of correlations to get

\displaystyle\left|\int F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\chi_{I_{h}}(T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)-\int F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\,d\mu(x)\int\chi_{I_{h}}(x)\,d\mu(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mu(I_{h})||F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})||_{BV}p(n)

Note that for each
y_{h}
,

F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }d(f(x),y_{h})<r_{n}(f(x))\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}=\chi_{\{x:d(f(x),y_{h})<r_{n}(f(x))\}},

so
||F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})||_{BV}\leq 3
. Then summing over
h
we get

\left|\int\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\chi_{I_{h}}(T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)-\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}\int F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\,d\mu(x)\cdot\mu(I_{h})\right|\leq 3p(n).

Hence

\displaystyle\left|\mu(R_{n})-M_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int F_{n}(x,T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)-\int F_{n}\,d\mu\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int F_{n}(x,T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)-\int F_{n,k}(x,T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int F_{n,k}(x,T^{n}x)\,d\mu(x)-\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}\int F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\,d\mu(x)\cdot\mu(I_{h})\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}\int F_{n,k}(x,y_{h})\,d\mu(x)\cdot\mu(I_{h})-\int F_{n,k}\,d\mu\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int F_{n,k}\,d\mu\,d\mu-\int F_{n}\,d\mu\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon+(\varepsilon+3p(n))+\varepsilon+\varepsilon.

As we let
k\to\infty
, the lemma is proved.
∎

The convergence/zero measure case will follow immediately from Lemma
7
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By Lemma
7
,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(R_{n})\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(M_{n}+3p(n))<\infty
, so
\mu(R(\{M_{n}\}_{n},f))=0
by the Borel-Cantelli lemma.
∎

4
The divergence part

In this section, we prove the divergence part of Lemma
, so throughout the section we will assume that
(X,\mu,T)
,
\{M_{n}\}_{n}
and
f:X\to X
satisfy the conditions stated in Theorem
2
and that
\lim_{n\to\infty}p(n)=0
. The key ingredients for the proof of the divergence case are the estimate of the measure of each
R_{n}
, which was proved in Lemma
7
, and a quasi-independence property of
\{R_{n}\}_{n}
, which we will prove next.

Lemma 8
.

There exist positive constants
K_{1},K_{2}
and
K_{3}
such that for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mu(R_{n}\cap E_{R+m})\leq M_{n}M_{n+m}(1+K_{1}\sqrt{p(n)})+K_{2}(M_{n}p(n)^{s/2}+M_{n+m}(p(n)^{s/2}+p(m)))+K_{3}p(n)^{s}

for some positive constants
K_{1},K_{2}
and
K_{3}
.

Proof.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Define

E_{n,m}(x,y,z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }y\in B(f(x),r_{n}(f(x))),z\in B(f(x),r_{n+m}(f(x)))\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Note that

\mu(R_{n}\cap R_{n+m})=\int F_{n,m}(x,T^{n}x,T^{n+m}x)\,d\mu(x)

We will approximate
E_{n,m}
by a sum of products of characteristic functions of intervals. For each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we first partition
X
evenly into
\ell
subintervals
\{I_{h}\}_{h=0}^{\ell-1}
and denote the middle point of
I_{h}
by
x_{h}
for each
h=0,\ldots,\ell-1
. Define

F_{n,m,\ell}(x,y,z):=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell-1}\chi_{I_{k}}(x)\cdot\chi_{B(f(x_{h}),r_{n}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell})}(y)\cdot\chi_{B(f(x_{h}),r_{n+m}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell})}(z)

Note that for each
x\in I_{h}
and
w\in B(f(x),r_{n}(f(x)))
,
|x-x_{h}|<\frac{1}{2\ell}
,
|f(x)-f(x_{h})|<\frac{L}{2\ell}
and
|r_{n}(f(x))-r_{n}(f(x_{h}))|<\frac{L}{2\ell}
, so
w\in B(f(x_{h}),r_{n}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell})
and hence
F_{n,m,\ell}\geq F_{n,m}
.
Then

\displaystyle\int F_{n,m}(x,T^{n}x,T^{n+m}x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int F_{n,m,\ell}(x,T^{n}x,T^{n+m}x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^{\ell-1}\int\chi_{I_{k}}(x)\cdot\chi_{B(f(x_{h}),r_{n}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell})}(T^{n}x)\cdot\chi_{B(f(x_{h}),r_{n+m}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell})}(T^{n+m}x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\underset{\text{BV}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^{\ell-1}\int\chi_{B(f(x_{h}),r_{n}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell})}(x)\cdot\chi_{B(f(x_{h}),r_{n+m}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell})}(T^{m}x)\,d\mu(x)\cdot\left(\mu(I_{h})+3p(n)\right)

\displaystyle\underset{\text{BV}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\mu\left(B\left(f(x_{h}),r_{n}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell}\right)\right)+3p(m)\right)\left(\mu\left(B\left(f(x_{h}),r_{n+m}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cdot(\mu(I_{k})+3p(n))

By upper Ahlfors regularity (
),

\mu\left(B\left(f(x_{h}),r_{n}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell}\right)\right)\leq M_{n}+2c\left(\frac{L}{\ell}\right)^{s}

and

\mu\left(B\left(f(x_{h}),r_{n+m}(f(x_{h}))+\frac{L}{\ell}\right)\right)\leq M_{n+m}+2c\left(\frac{L}{\ell}\right)^{s}.

Now we pick
\ell
so that
\sqrt{p(n)}/2<\frac{L}{\ell}<\sqrt{p(n)}
. Then

\displaystyle\int F_{n,m}(x,T^{n}x,T^{n+m}x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(M_{n}+2cp(n)^{s/2}+3p(m))(M_{n+m}+2cp(n)^{s/2})\cdot(1+6L\sqrt{p(n)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{n}M_{n+m}(1+K_{1}\sqrt{p(n)})+K_{2}(M_{n}p(n)^{s/2}+M_{n+m}(p(n)^{s/2}+p(m)))+K_{3}p(n)^{s}

where
K_{1}=6L
,
K_{2}=(1+6L\sqrt{\sup_{n}p(n)})(2c+3)
and
K_{3}=4c^{2}(1+6L\sqrt{\sup_{n}p(n)})
.
∎

To prove the divergence case, we will need the Chung-Erdös inequality.

Lemma 9
.

In a probability space, for measurable sets

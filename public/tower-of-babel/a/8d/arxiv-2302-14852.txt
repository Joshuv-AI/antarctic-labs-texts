1.
The Navier-Stokes equation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

The Navier-Stokes equation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
subjected to no gravitational forces are provided as:

(1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{u}+\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}=-\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}(p)

(2)

\displaystyle\textbf{Div}(\textbf{u})=0

(3)

\displaystyle\textbf{u}(x,0)=\textbf{u}^{0}(x).

Here
\rho>0
is a scalar which represents the fluids density and
\nu>0
is a scalar which denotes the fluids kinetic viscosity. These equations are to be solved for the velocity field
\textbf{u}(x,t)
and scalar pressure function
p(x,t)
as time progresses. For physical reasonable solutions the initial condition is restricted to be smooth (infinitely differentiable) and decay

|\textbf{u}^{0}|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\infty

so that
\textbf{u}(x,t)
does not grow large as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
. According to Fefferman’s official problem description
[
2
]
we say a solution
u
and
p
is reasonable if

(4)

p,\textbf{u}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\infty))\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{|\textbf{u}|}<\epsilon,\quad\forall t>0.

That is
u
and
p
are infinitely differentiable and
u
has finite energy.

Theorem 1
.

If there is a smooth solution to Eq.(
1
)-Eq.(
3
) that satisfy the conditions of Eq.(
4
) such that
\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}
vanishes as fast as
\dfrac{1}{x}
is twice differentiable, and is of bounded support then the velocity must take the form

\textbf{u}(x,t)-\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\textbf{Curl}\Biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{Curl}(\textbf{u}(x^{\prime},t))}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}\Biggr)=-\int_{0}^{t}{\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}\big(\Gamma(x,s)\big)}ds,\indent t>0

with

\Gamma(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\alpha(x-y,\dfrac{t}{\nu})(p-\rho\phi)\circ(x,0)}dy

and
\alpha(x,t)
the kernel to the non-homogenous heat equation with
\phi(x,0)
denoting the irrotational part of the non-linear term
\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}
at time
t=0
.

Proof.

Assume for simplicity that there is a smooth solution to Eq.(
1
)-Eq.(
3
) that satisfy the conditions of Eq.(
4
). If the vector field
\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}
vanishes as fast as
\dfrac{1}{x}
as
x\rightarrow\infty
, is twice differentiable, and is of bounded support then according to Helmholtz decomposition the vector field
\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}
can be written as

(5)

\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}=\textbf{Grad}(\phi)+\textbf{Curl}(\Phi)

where
\phi
and
\Phi
are provided explicitly by the formula

\phi(x)=\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{Div}\bigl(\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}\bigr)(x^{\prime})}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}

and

\Phi(x)=\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{Curl}\bigl(\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}\bigr)(x^{\prime})}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}

respectively (see
[
3
]
). Substituting Eq.(
5
) into Eq.(
1
) yields a new expression for the velocity

(6)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{u}+\textbf{Curl}(\Phi)=-\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}(p-\rho\phi).

Computing the divergence of Eq.(
6
) one finds

(7)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{Div}(\textbf{u})+\textbf{Div}\big(\textbf{Curl}(\Phi)\big)=-\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Div}\big(\textbf{Grad}(p-\rho\phi)\big).

According to Eq.(
2
) and the fact that
\textbf{Div}\big(\textbf{Curl}(\textbf{f})\big)=0
for any vector field
f
Eq.(
7
) then reduces to

(8)

\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Div}\big(\textbf{Grad}(p-\rho\phi)\big)=\dfrac{1}{\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}(p-\rho\phi)}=0.

Conveniently, Eq.(
8
) is simply the non-homogeneous heat equation in terms of the variable
(p-\rho\phi)
which then yields the analytic result

(9)

(p-\rho\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\alpha(x-y,\dfrac{t}{\nu})(p-\rho\phi)\circ(x,0)}dy:=\Gamma(x,t)

with
\alpha(x,t)
the kernel to the non-homogeneous heat equation

\alpha(x,t):=\dfrac{1}{(4\pi t)^{3/2}}~\exp\biggl(\dfrac{|x|^{2}}{4t}\biggr),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},~~t>0.

according to Evan’s book on PDE’s
[
1
, p. 48]
as the heat equations solution is unique. Next using Eq.(
9
) we may rewrite Eq.(
6
) in terms of the mapping
\Gamma
as followed

(10)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{u}+\textbf{Curl}(\Phi)=-\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}\big(\Gamma(x,t)\big).

Recall
\Gamma(x,t)
is known and the vector field
\Phi
is not because it is a function of the velocity
\textbf{u}(x,t)
. Substituting the definition of
\Phi
into Eq.(
10
) reveals

(11)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{u}+\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\textbf{Curl}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{Curl}\bigl((\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}\bigr)(x^{\prime},t)}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}\right)\\
&\qquad=-\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}\big(\Gamma(x,t)).\end{split}

The exceptional part in this formula is that it does not involve any pressure even as difficult as it may seem. A simple reduction can be done by comparing to the curl of Eq.(
1
) and noticing the curl of the vector field
\textbf{u}(x,t)
is equivalent to the curl of its non-linear part minus the Laplacian term

(12)

-\dfrac{d}{dt}\textbf{Curl}(\textbf{u})=\textbf{Curl}\bigl(\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}\bigr).

Substituting Eq.(
12
) into Eq.(
11
) gives the result

(13)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{u}(x,t)-\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\textbf{Curl}\Biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{Curl}(\frac{d}{dt}\textbf{u}(x^{\prime},t))}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}\Biggr)=-\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}\big(\Gamma(x,t)\big).

We can pull out the time derivative (as the volume integral is independent of the time variable
t
) and we should actually get the following

(14)

\textbf{u}(x,t)-\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\textbf{Curl}\Biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{Curl}(\textbf{u}(x^{\prime},t))}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}\Biggr)=-\int_{0}^{t}{\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}\big(\Gamma(x,s)\big)}ds.

which proves the result.

∎

Lemma 1
.

If
\textbf{curl}(\textbf{u}(x,t^{\prime}))=\textbf{0}
then
\textbf{curl}(\textbf{u}(x,t))=\textbf{0}
for all
t\geq t^{\prime}>0
.

Proof.

The proof is simple and instructive. Note that if
\textbf{u}(x,t)
is smooth and satisfied Eq.(
1
) then
\textbf{v}(x,t):=\textbf{curl}(\textbf{u}(x,t))
satisfies

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{v}+\textbf{curl}\biggl(\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}\biggr)=\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{v}.

Next assume
\textbf{v}(x,t^{\prime})=\textbf{0}
for some
t^{\prime}>0
then it follows that

\begin{split}\textbf{curl}\biggl(\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}\biggr)(x,t^{\prime})&=\biggl((\textbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{u}-(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{v}\biggr)(x,t^{\prime})=0\textbf{0},\end{split}

and
\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{v}(x,t^{\prime})=\textbf{0}
which implies
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{v}(x,t^{\prime})=\textbf{0}
. However, both
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{v}(x,t^{\prime})=\textbf{0}
and
\textbf{v}(x,t^{\prime})=\textbf{0}
imply
\textbf{v}(x,t)=\textbf{0}
for all
t\geq t^{\prime}>0
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

If there is a smooth solution to Eq.(
1
)- Eq.(
4
) such that the non-linear component
\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}-\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{u}
vanishes as fast as

, is twice differentiable, has bounded support, and there exist a value

then the velocity

can be written as

\textbf{u}(x,t)=\textbf{H}^{k+1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},t)-\int_{0}^{t}{\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}\big(\Gamma(x,s)\big)}ds

for

with

\xi_{i}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\alpha(x-y,\dfrac{t}{\nu})v^{k}_{i}(x,0)}dy,\indent v^{k}_{i}(x,0)=\biggl(\textbf{curl}^{k}(\textbf{u}(x,0))\biggr)_{i},\indent 1\leq i\leq 3

and
\Gamma
defined explicitly in Eq.(
9
),
\textbf{v}^{k}(x,t)
the

application of the curl operator

\textbf{v}^{k}(x,t)=\textbf{curl}^{k}(\textbf{u}(x,t))=\underbrace{\textbf{curl}(\textbf{curl}\cdots\cdots\textbf{curl})(\textbf{u}(x,t))}_{k-applications},

and
\textbf{H}^{k}(\textbf{v})
defined as the kth composition of Helmholtz operator

\textbf{H}^{k}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,t),\indent\textbf{H}^{k}(\textbf{v}):=\underbrace{\biggl(\textbf{H}\circ\textbf{H}\circ\cdots\circ\textbf{H}\biggr)\circ(\textbf{v})}_{k-applications}\indent\textbf{H}^{1}(\textbf{v})=\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{curl}(\textbf{v}(x^{\prime},t))}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}.

Proof.

Assume the conditions of the hypothesis. According to Eq.(
1
) the

(15)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{v}^{k}+\textbf{curl}^{k}\biggl(\left(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\right)\textbf{u}\biggr)=\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{v}^{k}.

for all

then immediately

(16)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\textbf{v}_{i}^{k}=\nu\bigtriangleup\textbf{v}_{i}^{k}\indent 1\leq i\leq 3,\indent t\geq t^{\prime}>0.

and so

admits a solution to the non-homogeneous heat equation

(17)

v_{i}^{k}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\alpha(x-y,\dfrac{t}{\nu})v^{k}_{i}(x,0)}dy:=\xi_{i}(x,t),\indent v^{k}_{i}(x,0)=\biggl(\textbf{curl}^{k}(\textbf{u}(x,0))\biggr)_{i},\indent 1\leq i\leq 3.

By Helmholtz theorem as long as

\begin{split}\textbf{v}(x,t)&=\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\dfrac{\textbf{curl}(\textbf{v}(x^{\prime},t))}{|x-x^{\prime}|}}dV^{\prime}.\\
\\
&=\textbf{H}(\textbf{curl}(\textbf{v}(x,t)))\\
&=\underbrace{\biggl(\textbf{H}\circ\textbf{H}\circ\cdots\circ\textbf{H}\biggr)}_{l-times}\circ(\textbf{curl}^{l}(\textbf{v}(x,t),t),\indent L\leq k.\\

This is simply a iteration of Helmholtz theorem on each

\textbf{u}(x,t)=\textbf{H}^{k+1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},t)-\int_{0}^{t}{\dfrac{1}{\rho}\textbf{Grad}\big(\Gamma(x,s)\big)}ds,\indent t\geq t^{\prime}.

∎

2.
Relative Boundaries on the Vorticity Equation

In the previous section we have shown that a solution to the Navier-stokes equation depends entirely on the vorticity whenever a smooth solution to the vorticity exist (that decays quickly) as we can write the velocity using Helmholtz decomposition. This is why we seek an analytical solution for the vorticity formula. As turbulence and vorticity is difficult to study (at least analytically) we seek another approach to examine the behavior. One probable approach is to bound the vorticity with some PDE we can solve. If we cannot bound the vorticity with a solvable PDE then it might actually contain a blow up. The intuition here is that we should be able to find a vector field that has similar speed to the vorticity but is simpler to solve with the same initial condition. We beg the following question: For each
1\leq i\leq 3
is there a smooth mapping
F
such that the PDE

(20)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi_{i}+F(\textbf{curl}(\Phi),\Phi,\nabla\phi_{i},\nabla(\textbf{curl}(\Phi)_{i}))=\nu\bigtriangleup\phi_{i}

has a solution
\Phi=[\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3}]
(which doesn’t need to be unique) with

(21)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi_{i},\indent\phi_{i}(x,0)=v_{i}(x,0),\indent\phi_{i}(x,t)\leq M\indent 1\leq i\leq 3.

That is the initial condition of
\phi_{i}
matches
v_{i}
and
\phi_{i}
is bounded for each
i
. If no such
F
exist then we might speculate that a blow up occurs for some initial condition. The first attempt one should take is to bound the no-linear term inside the vorticity equation.

Theorem 3
.

If there is a solution to Eq.(
1
)- Eq.(
3
) such that
\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\geq 0
then

\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}+|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}.

for all points in time
t>0
.

Proof.

Define
\alpha_{i}
as the angle between
v
and
\nabla u_{i}
, and
\beta_{i}
the angle between the vectors
u
and
\nabla v_{i}
. Using the dot product identity the vorticity equation can be written as

(22)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}&=\biggl[|\textbf{v}|~|\nabla u_{i}|\cos(\beta_{i})-|\textbf{u}|~|\nabla v_{i}|\cos(\alpha_{i})\biggr]+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}.\\
\end{split}

Notice that if
\bigtriangleup v_{i}\geq 0
for
1\leq i\leq 3
then

(23)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}&=\biggl[|\textbf{v}|~|\nabla u_{i}|\cos(\beta_{i})-|\textbf{u}|~|\nabla v_{i}|\cos(\alpha_{i})\biggr]+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
&\leq\biggl[|\textbf{v}|~|\nabla u_{i}|+|\textbf{u}|~|\nabla v_{i}|\biggr]+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
&\leq\max(|\textbf{v}|,|\nabla u_{i}|)^{2}+\max(|\textbf{u}|,|\nabla v_{i}|)^{2}+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
&\leq\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\biggl(|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
&\leq 2\max\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2},|\textbf{u}|^{2}\biggr)+2\max\biggl(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2},|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\end{split}

Thus, whenever
\bigtriangleup v_{i}\geq 0
at least one of the four cases must hold at each point in time

(24)

\begin{split}(i)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
\\
(ii)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
\\
(iii)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
\\
(iv)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}.\\
\end{split}

In any situation we may add (i)-(iv) to find

(25)

\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}+|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}

∎

Theorem 4
.

If there is a solution to Eq.(
1
)- Eq.(
3
) such that
\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}<0
then

\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}+|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}.

for all points in time
t>0
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as Theorem
3
. Note that if
\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}<0
then

(26)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}&=\biggl[|\textbf{v}|~|\nabla u_{i}|\cos(\beta_{i})-|\textbf{u}|~|\nabla v_{i}|\cos(\alpha_{i})\biggr]+\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\\
&\leq\biggl[|\textbf{v}|~|\nabla u_{i}|+|\textbf{u}|~|\nabla v_{i}|\biggr]\\
&\leq\max(|\textbf{v}|,|\nabla u_{i}|)^{2}+\max(|\textbf{u}|,|\nabla v_{i}|)^{2}\\
&\leq\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)+\biggl(|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\biggr)\\
&\leq 2\max\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2},|\textbf{u}|^{2}\biggr)+2\max\biggl(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2},|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)\end{split}

Thus, whenever
\bigtriangleup v_{i}<0
at least one of the four cases must hold at each point in time

(27)

\begin{split}(i)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\biggr)\\
\\
(ii)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)\\
\\
(iii)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\biggr)\\
\\
(iv)~~~~\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq 2\biggl(|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\biggr).\\
\end{split}

In any situation we may add (i)-(iv) to find

(28)

\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq|\textbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}+|\textbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}.

which completes the proof.

∎

Note
: Theorem
3
and
4
illustrate that the time derivative of the vorticity is bounded by norm of four distinct vector fields squared ( in particular
|\textbf{v}|^{2}
,
|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}
,
|\textbf{u}|^{2}
, and
|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}
). As long as the Laplacian is non zero
\bigtriangleup v_{i}\neq 0
there should exist non zero mappings
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
,
g_{3}
, and
g_{4}
such that

(29)

\begin{split}|\textbf{v}|^{2}\leq g_{1}\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i},\indent|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\leq g_{2}\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i},\indent|\textbf{u}|^{2}\leq g_{3}\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i},\indent|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\leq g_{4}\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}\end{split}

since the four norms
|\textbf{v}|^{2}
,
|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}
,
|\textbf{u}|^{2}
, and
|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}
are all real numbers in which case according to Eq.(
25
) we have

(30)

\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{i}\leq\biggl(g_{1}+g_{2}+g_{3}+g_{4}+1\biggr)\nu\bigtriangleup v_{i}.

Thus hinting at a possible formulation for
F
leading us to the following PDE

(31)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\lambda_{i}=\delta(x,t)\bigtriangleup\lambda_{i}

with initial condition

\lambda_{i}(x,0)=v_{i}(x,0)=(\textbf{curl}(\textbf{u}(x,0)))_{i}.

We wish to study the possibility of a blow up relative to
\delta
. In order to discover a possible boundary here we need to choose the correct delta so that all of this computation makes since. That is we must choose

\delta=(g_{1}+g_{2}+g_{3}+g_{4}+1)

for any
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
and
g_{4}

Summary

We take a second to reflect some of the results we found thus far. In the first section we have shown that under certain decay assumptions if the
k^{th}
application of the curl operator of the non-linear term ever vanishes then the solution of the Selondial part of
\textbf{u}(x,t)

\textbf{curl}^{k}((\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{u})

It is still probable that a solution exist that has a blow up where one can compute the curl of the non-linear term consecutively.

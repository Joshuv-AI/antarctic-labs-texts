Virtual Crystals and Nakajima Monomials
Ben SALISBURY † and Travis SCRIMSHAW ‡

Abstract
An explicit description of the virtualization map for the (modified) Nakajima monomial model for crystals is given. We give an explicit description of the Lusztig data for modified Nakajima monomials in type A n A_{n} .

Introduction

Diagram folding is an effective technique used to study a non-simply-laced Kac–Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by embedding
\mathfrak{g}
inside of a simply-laced Kac–Moody algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. On the level of crystals, Kashiwara
[
28
]
introduced a method for embedding highest weight
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-crystals inside highest weight
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
-crystals, provided the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
can be obtained from the Dynkin diagram of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
using a diagram folding. The embedding is called a virtualization map and resulting image with an induced crystal structure is called a virtual crystal.

It is known that simply-laced crystals have a nice local characterization
[
72
]
, a description which is lacking in the non-simply-laced types (see
[
6
,
73
]
for partial results in this direction). One can develop the theory of crystals using a combination of the Stembridge axioms and the theory of virtual crystals, which is the approach taken in the recent text of Bump and Schilling
[
4
]
. Therefore, developing an explicit virtualization map on different models for highest weight crystals and
B(\infty)
, the crystal of the lower half of the quantum group
U_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
, for the correct model could result in an extension of the Stembridge axioms to all symmetrizable types.

[

, which only is valid for classical types,

2
Crystals and Nakajima monomials

Since we will only be working with one particular model for crystals, we explicitly describe the properties of that model and leave the generalities for the interested reader to pursue. (See, for example,
[
4
,
13
]
.)

Let
C=(C_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
be a generalized Cartan matrix for a symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with index set
I
, weight lattice
P
, fundamental weights
\{\Lambda_{i}\colon i\in I\}
, and simple roots
\{\alpha_{i}\colon i\in I\}
. Let
P^{\vee}
denote the coroot lattice. For
\lambda\in P
and
h\in P^{\vee}
, let
\langle h,\lambda\rangle=\lambda(h)
be the canonical pairing. Recall that
\Lambda_{j}(h_{i})=\delta_{ij}
(Kronecker delta) and
\alpha_{j}(h_{i})=C_{ij}
, where
\{h_{i}\colon i\in I\}
is the set of simple coroots in
P^{\vee}
. We identity simple roots in the weight space
\mathbf{Q}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}P
via the Cartan matrix; that is, in
\mathbf{Q}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}P
, we have
\alpha_{i}=\sum\limits_{j\in I}C_{ji}\Lambda_{i}
. We write
i\sim j
if
i\neq j
and
C_{ij}\neq 0
; i.e., if
i
and
j
are adjacent in the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{Y_{i,k}\colon i\in I,\,k\in\mathbf{Z}\}\cup\{\bm{1}\}
be a set of commuting variables and in which
\bm{1}
denotes the multiplicative identity. Choose an array
{\bm{c}}=(c_{ij}\colon i,j\in I,\,i\neq j)
of integers which satisfy the condition
c_{ij}+c_{ji}=1
. Define
\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{c}}}
as
\mathcal{M}
with a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-crystal structure as follows. For a monomial
M=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\prod\limits_{k\in\mathbf{Z}}Y_{i,k}^{y_{i}(k)}
, define

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(M)=\sum_{i\in I}\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}}y_{i}(k)\bigg)\Lambda_{i},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(M)=\max\bigg\{\sum_{j\leq k}y_{i}(j)\colon k\in\mathbf{Z}\bigg\},

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}(M)=\varphi_{i}(M)-\langle h_{i},\mathrm{wt}(M)\rangle.

(2.1c)

\displaystyle f_{i}M=\begin{cases}MA^{-1}_{i,k_{i}^{f}}&\text{if }\varphi_{i}(M)>0,\\
0&\text{if }\varphi_{i}(M)=0,\end{cases}

(2.1d)

\displaystyle e_{i}M=\begin{cases}MA_{i,k_{i}^{e}}&\text{if }\varepsilon_{i}(M)>0,\\
0&\text{if }\varepsilon_{i}(M)=0,\end{cases}

(2.1e)

where

\displaystyle A_{i,k}=Y_{i,k}Y_{i,k+1}\prod_{j\neq i}Y_{j,k+c_{j,i}}^{C_{ji}},

\displaystyle k_{i}^{f}=k_{i}^{f}(M)=\min\bigg\{k\colon\varphi_{i}(M)=\sum_{j\leq k}y_{i}(j)\bigg\},

\displaystyle k_{i}^{e}=k_{i}^{e}(M)=\max\bigg\{k\colon\varphi_{i}(M)=\sum_{j\leq k}y_{i}(j)\bigg\}.

Remark 2.1
.

We can also define
\varepsilon_{i}
by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}(M)=\max\bigg\{{-}\sum_{j>k}y_{i}(j)\colon k\in\mathbf{Z}\bigg\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}(M)_{{\bm{c}}}
denote the closure of a monomial
M
under the crystal operators given above, and let
B(\lambda)
denote the
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-crystal associated to the irreducible highest weight representation
V(\lambda)
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
with highest weight
\lambda\in P
.

Theorem 2.2

(Kashiwara
[
31
]
)
.

Let
M
be a monomial such that
e_{i}M=0
for all
i\in I
. Then
\mathcal{M}(M)_{{\bm{c}}}\cong B\bigl({\rm wt}(M)\bigr)
.

In particular, denote
\mathcal{M}(\lambda)_{{\bm{c}}}:=\mathcal{M}(Y_{\lambda})_{{\bm{c}}}
, where

\displaystyle Y_{\lambda}=\prod_{i\in I}Y_{i,0}^{\langle h_{i},\lambda\rangle}.

This is referred to as the
B(\lambda)
model using
Nakajima monomials
.

Next, suppose
c_{ij}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
for all
i
,
j
. We define
\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{{\bm{c}}}
as the closure of
\bm{1}
under the modified Kashiwara operators

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{i}M=MA_{i,\overline{k}_{i}^{f}}^{-1},

where

\displaystyle\overline{k}_{i}^{f}=\min\bigg\{k\geq 0\colon\varphi_{i}(M)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}y_{i}(j)\bigg\},

with the remaining crystal structure being the same as for
\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{c}}}
. Note if
M\in\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{\bm{c}}
, then
M
has the form

\displaystyle M=\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{k\geq 0}Y_{i,k}^{y_{i}(k)}.

(2.2)

When there is no danger of confusion, we will simply write the modified Kashiwara operator as
f_{i}
. The crystal
\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{{\bm{c}}}
is called the crystal of
modified Nakajima monomials
.

Theorem 2.3

(Kang–Kim–Shin
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be of symmetrizable type. Then
\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{\bm{c}}\cong B(\infty)
.

Let
\lambda\in P
be a dominant integral weight. Define
R_{\lambda}=\{r_{\lambda}\}
to be the one-element abstract
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-crystal whose operations, for all
i\in I
, are defined as

\displaystyle e_{i}r_{\lambda}=f_{i}r_{\lambda}=0,\qquad\varepsilon_{i}(r_{\lambda})=-\langle h_{i},\lambda\rangle,\qquad\varphi_{i}(r_{\lambda})=0,\qquad{\rm wt}(r_{\lambda})=\lambda.

By
[
26
]
, there is a strict crystal embedding
B(\lambda)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow B(\infty)\otimes R_{\lambda}
, where
\otimes
denotes the crystal tensor product defined in
[
25
]
. We will not require the general definition of tensor product of crystals, but note that the connected component containing
u_{\infty}\otimes r_{\lambda}
, where
u_{\infty}
is the highest weight element of
B(\infty)
, is isomorphic to
B(\lambda)
. The crystal operators of the tensor product
M\otimes r_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{{\bm{c}}}\otimes R_{\lambda}
precisely corresponds to taking
Y_{\lambda}M
for any
M\in\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{{\bm{c}}}
and using the (unmodified) crystal structure of (
2.1
). Going in the opposite direction, we can construct
\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{{\bm{c}}}
as the direct limit

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{{\bm{c}}}=\varinjlim_{\lambda\to\infty}Y_{\lambda}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(Y_{\lambda}),

where we consider the partial order
\lambda\leq\mu
if and only if
\langle h_{i},\lambda\rangle\leq\langle h_{i},\mu\rangle
for all
i\in I
. We refer the reader to
[
29
]
for a precise definition.

3
Virtual crystals

A
diagram folding
is a surjective map
\phi\colon\widehat{I}\longrightarrow I
between index sets of Kac–Moody algebras and a set
(\gamma_{i}\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}\colon i\in I)
of
scaling factors
. One may induce a map from
\phi
on the corresponding weight lattices
\widetilde{\phi}\colon P\longrightarrow\widehat{P}
by asserting

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}\mapsto\gamma_{i}\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)}\widehat{\Lambda}_{i^{\prime}}.

(3.1)

If there exists an embedding
\iota\colon\mathfrak{g}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
of symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebras that induces
\widetilde{\phi}
, then
\iota
induces an injection
v\colon B(\lambda)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow B(\widehat{\lambda})
as sets, where
\widehat{\lambda}:=\widetilde{\phi}(\lambda)
. Suppose the crystal structure on
B(\lambda)
is denoted by
(e_{i},f_{i},\varepsilon_{i},\varphi_{i},{\rm wt})
and the crystal structure on
B(\widehat{\lambda})
is denoted by
(\widehat{e}_{i},\widehat{f}_{i},\widehat{\varepsilon}_{i},\widehat{\varphi}_{i},\widehat{{\rm wt}})
. Then there is additional structure on the image under
v
as a
virtual crystal
, where the action
e_{i}
and
f_{i}
are defined on the image as

\displaystyle e^{v}_{i}=\prod_{i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)}\widehat{e}_{i^{\prime}}^{\,\gamma_{i}}\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad f^{v}_{i}=\prod_{i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)}\widehat{f}_{i^{\prime}}^{\,\gamma_{i}},

(3.2)

respectively, and commute with
v

[
1
,
57
,
58
]
; i.e.,
e_{i}^{v}\circ v=v\circ e_{i}
for all
i\in I
, etc. These are known as the
virtual Kashiwara (crystal) operators
. It is shown in
[
28
]
that for any
i\in I
and
i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)
we have
\widehat{e}_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\widehat{e}_{i_{2}^{\prime}}=\widehat{e}_{i_{2}^{\prime}}\widehat{e}_{i_{1}^{\prime}}
and
\widehat{f}_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\widehat{f}_{i_{2}^{\prime}}=\widehat{f}_{i_{2}^{\prime}}\widehat{f}_{i_{1}^{\prime}}
as operators (recall that the nodes indexed by
i_{1}^{\prime}
and
i_{2}^{\prime}
in the Dynkin diagram of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
are not connected), so both
e^{v}_{i}
and
f^{v}_{i}
are well-defined. The inclusion map
v
also satisfies the relation
\widetilde{\phi}\circ{\rm wt}=\widehat{{\rm wt}}\circ v
.
In
[
1
]
, it was shown that this defines a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-crystal structure on the image of
v
. More generally, we define a virtual crystal as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Consider any symmetrizable types
\mathfrak{g}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
with index sets
I
and
\widehat{I}
, respectively. Let
\phi\colon\widehat{I}\longrightarrow I
be a surjection such that
\widehat{C}_{i_{1}^{\prime}i_{2}^{\prime}}=0
for all
i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)
and
i\in I
. Let
\widehat{B}
be a
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
-crystal and
V\subseteq\widehat{B}
. Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{i}\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}\colon i\in I)
be the scaling factors. A
virtual crystal
is the quadruple
(V,\widehat{B},\phi,\gamma)
such that
V
has an abstract
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-crystal structure defined using the Kashiwara operators
e_{i}^{v}
and
f_{i}^{v}
from (
3.2
) above,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{v}(x):=\frac{\widehat{\varepsilon}_{i^{\prime}}(x)}{\gamma_{i}},\qquad\varphi_{i}^{v}(x):=\frac{\widehat{\varphi}_{i^{\prime}}(x)}{\gamma_{i}},\qquad\text{for all}\quad i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)\quad\text{and}\quad x\in V,

and
{\rm wt}^{v}:=\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}\circ\widehat{{\rm wt}}
.

We say
B

virtualizes
in
\widehat{B}
if there exists a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-crystal isomorphism
v\colon B\longrightarrow V
. The resulting isomorphism is called the
virtualization map
. We denote the quadruple
(V,\widehat{B},\phi,\gamma)
simply by
V
when there is no risk of confusion.

4
The virtualization map

Assume the notations of the previous sections. We will say
\widehat{{\bm{c}}}=(\widehat{c}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}\neq j^{\prime}}
is
compatible
with
{\bm{c}}
when
\widehat{c}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=c_{ij}
for all
i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)
and
j^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(j)
satisfying
i^{\prime}\sim j^{\prime}
and
i\sim j
. For this section assume that
\widehat{{\bm{c}}}
is compatible with
{\bm{c}}
. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\widehat{{\bm{c}}}}
be the set of modified Nakajima monomials of type
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
in the variables
\{\widehat{Y}_{i,k}\colon i\in\widehat{I},\,k\in\mathbf{Z}\}
and identity
\widehat{\bm{1}}
equipped with
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
-crystal structure denoted by
\widehat{e}_{i}
,
\widehat{f}_{i}
,
\widehat{\varepsilon}_{i}
,
\widehat{\varphi}_{i}
, and
\widehat{{\rm wt}}
. With these definitions, define a map

\displaystyle v\colon\ \mathcal{M}_{\bm{c}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\widehat{{\bm{c}}}}\qquad\text{by}\qquad Y_{i,k}\mapsto\prod_{i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)}\widehat{Y}_{i^{\prime},k}^{\gamma_{i}}

and extend multiplicatively. By abuse of notation, we also consider
v\colon\mathcal{M}(\infty)_{{\bm{c}}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(\infty)_{\widehat{{\bm{c}}}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(\alpha_{i})=\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)}\gamma_{i}\widehat{\alpha}_{i^{\prime}}

for all
i\in I
. Then, for all
i\in I
and
k\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\displaystyle v(A_{i,k})=\prod_{i^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(i)}\widehat{A}_{i^{\prime},k}^{\gamma_{i}}.

Example 4.2
.

Let

\displaystyle C=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2\\
-1&2\end{pmatrix},\!\!\!\qquad{\bm{c}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bullet&0\\
1&\bullet\end{pmatrix},\!\!\!\qquad\text{and}\!\!\!\qquad\widehat{C}=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\
-1&2&-1\\
0&-1&2\end{pmatrix},\!\!\!\qquad\widehat{{\bm{c}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bullet&0&0\\
1&\bullet&1\\
1&0&\bullet\end{pmatrix}.

Here,
C
is a Cartan matrix of type
C_{2}
and
\widehat{C}
is a Cartan matrix of type
A_{3}
. For arbitrary scaling factors
(\gamma_{i}\colon i\in I)
, the condition
\widetilde{\phi}(\alpha_{i})=\sum\limits_{j\in\phi^{-1}(i)}\gamma_{i}\widehat{\alpha}_{j}
imposes a linear dependence relation amongst the
\gamma_{i}
. In this example, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(\alpha_{1})=\widetilde{\phi}(2\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2})=2\widetilde{\phi}(\Lambda_{1})-\widetilde{\phi}(\Lambda_{2})=2\gamma_{1}(\widehat{\Lambda}_{1}+\widehat{\Lambda}_{3})-\gamma_{2}\widehat{\Lambda}_{2},

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(\widehat{\alpha}_{1}+\widehat{\alpha}_{3})=\gamma_{1}(2\widehat{\Lambda}_{1}-\widehat{\Lambda}_{2}-\widehat{\Lambda}_{2}+2\widehat{\Lambda}_{3})=2\gamma_{1}(\widehat{\Lambda}_{1}-\widehat{\Lambda}_{2}+\widehat{\Lambda}_{3}),

so the relation
2\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
is required to ensure
\widetilde{\phi}(\alpha_{1})=\gamma_{1}(\widehat{\alpha}_{1}+\widehat{\alpha}_{3})
. Similarly, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle v(A_{1,k})=v(Y_{1,k})v(Y_{1,k+1})\prod_{j\neq i}v(Y_{j,k+c_{j1}})^{C_{j1}}=\widehat{Y}_{1,k}^{\gamma_{1}}\widehat{Y}_{3,k}^{\gamma_{1}}\widehat{Y}_{1,k+1}^{\gamma_{1}}\widehat{Y}_{3,k+1}^{\gamma_{1}}\widehat{Y}_{2,k+1}^{-\gamma_{2}},

Proof of Theorem
6.5
.

Let
i\in I=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. For notational brevity, once and for all in this proof, denote

\displaystyle M=\prod_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}\left(\prod_{p=j}^{k}A_{p,k-p}^{-1}\right)^{\ell_{j,k}},\qquad

\displaystyle f_{i}M=\prod_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}\left(\prod_{p=j}^{k}A_{p,k-p}^{-1}\right)^{\ell_{j,k}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\bm{\alpha}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}\ell_{j,k}(\alpha_{j,k}),\qquad

\displaystyle f_{i}\bm{\alpha}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}\ell_{j,k}^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{j,k}).

Recall

\displaystyle S_{i}(\bm{\alpha})=\underbrace{)\cdots)}_{\ell_{{i,n}}}\ \underbrace{(\cdots(}_{\ell_{{i+1,n}}}\ \underbrace{)\cdots)}_{\ell_{{i,n-1}}}\ \underbrace{(\cdots(}_{\ell_{{i+1,n-1}}}\ \ \ \cdots\ \ \ \underbrace{)\cdots)}_{\ell_{{i,i+1}}}\ \underbrace{(\cdots(}_{\ell_{{i+1,i+1}}}\ \underbrace{)\cdots)}_{\ell_{{i,i}}}.

Suppose the leftmost uncanceled ‘(’ in
S_{i}(\bm{\alpha})
occurs in the position corresponding to the root
\alpha_{i+1,k_{0}}
, for some
i<k_{0}\leq n
. Then
f_{i}\bm{\alpha}=\bm{\alpha}-(\alpha_{i+1,k_{0}})+(\alpha_{i,k_{0}})
. Hence

\displaystyle\ell_{j,k}^{\prime\prime}=\begin{cases}\ell_{i,k_{0}}+1&\text{if }(j,k)=(i,k_{0}),\\
\ell_{i,k_{0}}-1&\text{if }(j,k)=(i+1,k_{0}),\\
\ell_{j,k}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If there is no uncanceled ‘(’ in
S_{i}(\bm{\alpha})
, then set
k_{0}=i
; therefore,
f_{i}\bm{\alpha}=\bm{\alpha}+(\alpha_{i,i})
and

\displaystyle\ell_{j,k}^{\prime\prime}=\begin{cases}\ell_{i,i}+1&\text{if }(j,k)=(i,i),\\
\ell_{j,k}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We intend to show
k_{i}^{f}(M)=k_{0}-i
; in this case, a direct calculation shows that
\ell_{j,k}^{\prime}=\ell_{j,k}^{\prime\prime}
for all
1\leq j\leq k\leq n
.

Expanding the given expression for
M
, we have

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}\left(\prod_{p=j}^{k}A_{p,k-p}^{-1}\right)^{\ell_{j,k}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}\left(\prod_{p=j}^{k}Y_{p,k-p}^{-1}Y_{p,k-p+1}^{-1}Y_{p-1,k-p+1}Y_{p+1,k-p}\right)^{\ell_{j,k}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}\big(Y_{j-1,k-j+1}Y_{j,k-j+1}^{-1}Y_{k,0}^{-1}Y_{k+1,0}\big)^{\ell_{j,k}}.

If we express this same

using the form
\prod\limits_{p\in I}\prod\limits_{q\geq 0}Y_{p,q}^{y_{p}(q)}
, then, for
i\in I
,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{s}y_{i}(r)=-\ell_{i,i}+\sum_{t=1}^{i}(\ell_{t,i-1}-\ell_{t,i})+\sum_{u=0}^{s-1}(\ell_{i+1,i+1+u}-\ell_{i,i+u}).

If there is no uncanceled ‘(’ in
S_{i}(\bm{\alpha})
, then each term in the third summand is nonpositive. Therefore

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(M)=\max\left\{\sum_{r=0}^{s}y_{i}(r)\colon s\geq 0\right\}=0

and

\displaystyle k_{i}^{f}(M)=\min\left\{s\geq 0\colon\varphi_{i}(M)=\sum_{r=0}^{s}y_{i}(r)\right\}=0=k_{0}-i.

On the other hand, if there is an uncanceled ‘(’ in

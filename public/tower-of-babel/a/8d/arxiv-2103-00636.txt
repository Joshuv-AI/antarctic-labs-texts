Counting Representations of Quivers Respecting Nilpotent Relations over Finite Fields
Bangming Deng, Jiuzhao Hua

Abstract
This paper presents analogous results of Hua [ 7 ] [ 8 ] on numbers of representations of quivers over finite fields which respect nilpotent relations under certain assumptions. A closed formula which counts isomorphism classes of absolutely indecomposable representations with given dimension vectors is given and a q q -deformation of Weyl-Kac denominator identity is established. In principle, if the numbers of representations are known, then the numbers of isomorphism classes of absolutely indecomposable representations are known.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. A monomial in the following form is called a
simple relation
for
\Gamma
:

X_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{(k_{1})}X_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{(k_{2})}X_{i_{2}i_{3}}^{(k_{3})}\cdots X_{i_{s-1}i_{s}}^{(k_{s})},

where
i_{0}
is called the
starting point
of the relation and
i_{s}
is called the
ending point
. It is necessary that
a_{i_{0}i_{1}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}}a_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots a_{i_{s-1}i_{s}}\neq 0
and
1\leq k_{m}\leq a_{i_{m-1}i_{m}}
(
1\leq m\leq s
). It is evident that every simple relation is induced by a path in
\Gamma
. If
i_{0}=i_{s}
then the relation is called
cyclic
. So simple cyclic relations are induced by oriented cycles in
\Gamma
. A
relation
for
\Gamma
is a linear combination over
\mathbb{F}
of simple relations that share the same starting point and the same ending point. A relation is called
cyclic
if all of its summands are cyclic. For example, the following is a cyclic relation for the quiver mentioned above:

X_{11}^{(1)}X_{12}^{(1)}X_{21}^{(1)}-X_{12}^{(1)}X_{21}^{(1)}X_{11}^{(1)}.

In this paper, we are only interested in cyclic relations.

Given
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, a
representation of quiver

\Gamma
of dimension
\alpha
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is a function

\sigma:\Omega\mapsto\{\textit{Matrices over }\mathbb{F}\}
such that
\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})
has order
\alpha_{i}\times\alpha_{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
and

1\leq k\leq a_{ij}
. Note that matrices with
0
rows or
0
columns are permitted.
\alpha
is called the
dimension vector
of
\sigma
and denoted by
\dim\sigma
. The following diagram defines a representation of the quiver mentioned above with
dimension vector
(1,2,2
):

Given two representations
\sigma
and
\tau
with dimension vectors

\alpha
and
\beta
respectively, an
n
-tuple
(H_{1},\cdots,H_{n})
of
matrices over
\mathbb{F}
is called a
homomorphism
from
\sigma
to
\tau
if
H_{i}
has order
\alpha_{i}\times\beta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and

\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})H_{j}=H_{i}\tau(X_{ij}^{(k)})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq a_{ij}
. If
\dim\sigma=\dim\tau
and
H_{1},\cdots,H_{n}
are all nonsingular, then
(H_{1},\cdots,H_{n})
is called an
isomorphism
. The
direct sum
of
\sigma
and
\tau
, denoted by
\sigma\oplus\tau
, is defined by

(\sigma\oplus\tau)(X_{ij}^{(k)})=\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})\oplus\tau(X_{ij}^{(k)})
(
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq a_{ij}
).

A representation is called

decomposable
if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of two representations with non-zero dimension vectors. An indecomposable representation of
\Gamma
over
\mathbb{F}

is called
absolutely indecomposable
if it is still indecomposable when it is considered as a representation of
\Gamma
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
, the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}
.

Let
\text{Mat}(m\times n,\mathbb{F})
be the set of all
m\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}
for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
let
\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})
be the set of all representations of
\Gamma
of dimension
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}
. Thus
\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})
is naturally identified with the following affine variety:

\bigoplus_{X_{ij}^{(k)}\in\Omega}\text{Mat}(\alpha_{i}\!\times\!\alpha_{j},\mathbb{F}).

Let
\text{GL}(m,\mathbb{F})
be the General Linear Group of order
m
over
\mathbb{F}
. Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
let
\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\!\text{GL}(\alpha_{i},\mathbb{F})
,
then the linear algebraic group
\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})
acts on
\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})
as follows:

\displaystyle\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})\times\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})

\displaystyle\to\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})

\displaystyle(g,\sigma)

\displaystyle\mapsto g\sigma,

where
g\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})=g_{i}^{-1}\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})g_{j}
for
g=(g_{1},\cdots,g_{n})\in\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})
. It is obvious that two representations from

\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F})
are isomorphic if and only if they are in the same orbit.

Given a representation
\sigma
of
\Gamma
and a simple relation
R
for
\Gamma
, where

R=X_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{(k_{1})}X_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{(k_{2})}X_{i_{2}i_{3}}^{(k_{3})}\cdots X_{i_{s-1}i_{s}}^{(k_{s})},

\sigma
acts on
R
naturally, i.e.,

\sigma(R)=\sigma\!\left(X_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{(k_{1})}\right)\sigma\!\left(X_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{(k_{2})}\right)\sigma\!\left(X_{i_{2}i_{3}}^{(k_{3})}\right)\cdots\sigma\!\left(X_{i_{s-1}i_{s}}^{(k_{s})}\right).

This action is naturally extended to any relation for
\Gamma
.

\sigma
is said to be
respecting

R
if
\sigma(R)=0
;
\sigma
is said to be
respecting

R

nilpotently
if
\sigma(R)
is a nilpotent matrix, i.e.,

\sigma(R)^{m}=0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

From now on, let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the finite field with
q
elements where
q
is a prime power,
\mathcal{R}
a set of cyclic relations for
\Gamma
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, let
\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}
be the set
all representations of
\Gamma
of dimension
\alpha

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that respect all relations in
\mathcal{R}
nilpotently, i.e.,

\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}=\{\sigma\in\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q}):\sigma(R)\textit{ is nilpotent for all }R\in\mathcal{R}\}.

It is evident that
\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}
is closed under the action of
\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
In what follows, we assume that
|\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}|
is a polynomial in

with rational coefficients for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, i.e.,
there exists
r(\alpha,q)\in\mathbb{Q}[q]
such that

for

.
Let
M(\alpha,q)
(
I(\alpha,q)
,
A(\alpha,q)
) be the number of isomorphism classes of representations (indecomposable representations, absolutely indecomposable representations respectively) of
\Gamma
of dimension
\alpha

.

Counting formulae for

,

exist in Hua

In case

2
Numbers of Stabilizers

This paper uses the same methodology as Hua
[
7
]
. A key step is to determine the number of representations that are stabilized by conjugacy classes of
\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
, which are parametrized by
n
-tuples of partitions and monic irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

A
partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{s})
is a finite sequence of positive integers such that
\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq s-1
.
The unique partition of
0
is
(0)
.
|\lambda|:=\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}
is called the
weight
of
\lambda
.
Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of partitions of all non-negative integers.
Let
f(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+\dots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+x^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
c(f)
be its
companion matrix
, i.e.,

c(f)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&1&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1\\
-a_{0}&-a_{1}&-a_{2}&\dots&-a_{n-1}\end{array}\right].

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
, let
J_{m}(f)
be the
Jordan block matrix
of order
m
with
c(f)
on the main diagonal, i.e.,

J_{m}(f)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}c(f)&I&0&\dots&0\\
0&c(f)&I&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&I\\
0&0&0&\dots&c(f)\end{array}\right]_{m\times m},

where
I
is the identity matrix of order
\deg(f)
.
For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{s})\in\mathcal{P}
, let
J_{\lambda}(f)
be the

direct sum
of
J_{\lambda_{i}}(f)

(i=1,\dots,s)
, i.e.,

J_{\lambda}(f)=J_{\lambda_{1}}(f)\oplus J_{\lambda_{2}}(f)\oplus\dots\oplus J_{\lambda_{s}}(f),

which stands for

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}J_{\lambda_{1}}(f)&0&\dots&0\\
0&J_{\lambda_{2}}(f)&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&J_{\lambda_{s}}(f)\end{array}\right].

Definition 2.1
.

For any matrix of order
m\times n
, the arm length of index
(i,j)
is one plus the number of minimal moves from
(i,j)
to
(1,n)
, where diagonal moves are not permitted. Thus the arm length distribution is as follows:

\left[\begin{array}[]{llllll}n&n-1&\dots&3&2&1\\
n+1&n&\dots&4&3&2\\
n+2&n+1&\dots&5&4&3\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
m+n&m+n-1&\dots&m+2&m+1&m\end{array}\right]_{m\times n}.

The arm rank of a matrix
M=[a_{ij}]
of order
m\times n
, denoted by
ar(M)
, is the largest arm length of indexes of non-zero elements of
M
, i.e.,

ar(M)=\max\left\{\textit{arm length of }(i,j)\,|\,a_{ij}\neq 0\textit{ where }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n\right\}.

Definition 2.2
.

A matrix
M=[a_{ij}]
of order
m\times n
is of type-U if it satisfies the following conditions:

•

a_{ij}=a_{st}
if
(i,j)
and
(s,t)
have the same arm length,

•

the arm rank of
M
is at most
\min\{m,n\}
.

Thus a type-U matrix of order
m\times n
has either the following form when
m\geq n
:

\left[\begin{array}[]{lllll}a_{1}&a_{2}&\dots&a_{n-1}&a_{n}\\
0&a_{1}&\dots&a_{n-2}&a_{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&a_{1}&a_{2}\\
0&0&\dots&0&a_{1}\\
\cline{1-5}\cr 0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\end{array}\right]_{m\times n},

or the following form when
m\leq n
:

\left[\begin{array}[]{lllllllll}0&\dots&0&&a_{1}&a_{2}&\dots&a_{m-1}&a_{m}\\
0&\dots&0&&0&a_{1}&\dots&a_{m-2}&a_{m-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0&&0&0&\dots&a_{1}&a_{2}\\
0&\dots&0&&0&0&\dots&0&a_{1}\end{array}\right]_{m\times n}.

Theorem 2.1

(Turnbull & Aitken
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{s})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{t})
be two partitions and
f(x)=x-a_{0}
with

, then any matrix
U
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that satisfies

J_{\lambda}(f)U=UJ_{\mu}(f)

block matrix in the following form:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}U_{11}&U_{12}&\dots&U_{1t}\\
U_{21}&U_{22}&\dots&U_{2t}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
U_{s1}&U_{s2}&\dots&U_{st}\end{array}\right],

where each submatrix
U_{ij}
is a type-U matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of order
\lambda_{i}\times\mu_{j}
for all
(i,j)
where
1\leq i\leq s
and
1\leq j\leq t
.

As an example, let
\lambda=(3,2,2),\mu=(3,3,2)
and
f(x)=x-t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, then

J_{\lambda}(f)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}t&1&0&&0&0&&0&0\\
0&t&1&&0&0&&0&0\\
0&0&t&&0&0&&0&0\\
\cline{1-9}\cr 0&0&0&&t&1&&0&0\\
0&0&0&&0&t&&0&0\\
\cline{1-9}\cr 0&0&0&&0&0&&t&1\\

0&t&1&&0&0&0&&0&0\\
0&0&t&&0&0&0&&0&0\\
\cline{1-10}\cr 0&0&0&&t&1&0&&0&0\\
0&0&0&&0&t&1&&0&0\\
0&0&0&&0&0&t&&0&0\\
\cline{1-10}\cr 0&0&0&&0&0&0&&t&1\\
0&0&0&&0&0&0&&0&t\end{array}\right].

Every matrix
U
which satisfies
J_{\lambda}(f)\,U=UJ_{\mu}(f)
can be written as a block matrix in the following form:

U=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}a&b&c&&h&i&j&&m&n\\
0&a&b&&0&h&i&&0&m\\
0&0&a&&0&0&h&&0&0\\
\cline{1-10}\cr 0&p&q&&0&d&e&&k&l\\
0&0&p&&0&0&d&&0&k\\
\cline{1-10}\cr 0&u&v&&0&r&s&&f&g\\
0&0&u&&0&0&r&&0&f\end{array}\right].

For a partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, let
m_{\lambda}^{\underline{i}}
be the
multiplicity
of
i
, i.e.,
m_{\lambda}^{\underline{i}}
is the number of parts equal to
i
in
\lambda
, and
\lambda
can be written in its “
exponential form
”
(1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}3^{m_{3}}\cdots)
, where
m_{i}=m_{\lambda}^{\underline{i}}
.
Let
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\lambda_{3}^{\prime},\dots)
be the
conjugate partition
of
\lambda
, which means that
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
is the number of parts in
\lambda
that are greater than or equal to
i
for all
i\geq 1
.
Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be two partitions,
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\lambda_{3}^{\prime},\dots)
and
\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime},\mu_{3}^{\prime},\dots)
be their conjugate partitions, we define two types of “
inner product
” of
\lambda
and
\mu
as follows:

\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle=\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\mu_{i}^{\prime}\textit{ and }(|\lambda,\mu|)=\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle-\sum_{s\geq 1}m_{\lambda}^{\underline{s}}m_{\mu}^{\underline{s}}.

(1)

\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle
can also be expressed in the following forms:

\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\min(i,j)m_{\lambda}^{\underline{i}}m_{\mu}^{\underline{j}}=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\min(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}).

(2)

For an
n
-tuple of partitions
\pi=(\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
and
s\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
, we define the
multiplicity vector
of order
s
by
d_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}}:=(m_{\pi_{1}}^{\underline{s}},\cdots,m_{\pi_{n}}^{\underline{s}})
. Thus,
(|\pi_{1}|,\cdots,|\pi_{n}|)=\sum_{s\geq 1}sd_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}}
.

.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}
be two partitions and
f(x)=x-a_{0}
with

, then the number of matrices
U
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that satisfy
J_{\lambda}(f)U=UJ_{\mu}(f)
is equal to
q^{\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle}
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.1
and identity (
2
). An alternative proof is given in Hua
[

]
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Given
\pi=(\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
and
f(x)=x-a_{0}
, let
\alpha=(|\pi_{1}|,\cdots,|\pi_{n}|)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and

\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
is defined as:

X_{g}=\left\{\sigma\in\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q}):g\sigma=\sigma\right\}.

There holds:

|X_{g}|=q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{ij}\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{j}\rangle}.

Proof.

For
\sigma\in\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
\sigma\in X_{g}
if and only if
J_{\pi_{i}}\!(f)\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})=\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})J_{\pi_{j}}\!(f)

for all
X_{ij}^{(k)}\in\Omega
,.
Thus

\displaystyle|X_{g}|

\displaystyle=\prod_{X_{ij}^{(k)}\in\Omega}|\left\{U\in\text{Mat}(|\pi_{i}|\!\times\!|\pi_{j}|,\mathbb{F}_{q}):J_{\pi_{i}}\!(f)U=UJ_{\pi_{j}}\!(f)\right\}|

Corollary
2.1
implies that

|X_{g}|=\prod_{X_{ij}^{(k)}\in\Omega}q^{\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{j}\rangle}=\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}q^{a_{ij}\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{j}\rangle}=q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{ij}\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{j}\rangle}.

∎

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U=[u_{ij}]
be a type-U matrix of order
m\times n
, the core of
U
, denoted by
U_{0}
, is defined as follows:

\displaystyle U_{0}=\begin{cases}\quad 0&\!\!\!\text{matrix of order $m\times n$ if $m\neq n$},\\
u_{11}I,&\!\!\!\text{where $I$ is the identity matrix of order $n$ if $m=n$}.\end{cases}

Obviously, the core of a type-U matrix is also a type-U matrix.
If
U=[U_{ij}]
is a block matrix of type-U matrices, then the core of
U
, denoted by
U_{0}
, is the block matrix
[(U_{ij})_{0}]
.

The following is an example of a

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be an
m\times n
type-U matrix and
N
an
n\times k
type-U matrix, then MN is an
m\times k
type-U matrix and
(MN)_{0}=M_{0}N_{0}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M=[M_{ij}]
be an
m\times n
block matrix of type-U matrices and
N=[N_{ij}]
an
n\times k
block matrix of type-U matrices such that
M
and
N
have compatible
multiplication orders, i.e., the number of columns in
M_{is}
is equal to
the number of rows in
N_{sj}
for
1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq k
and
1\leq s\leq n
. Then MN is an
m\times k
block matrix of type-U matrices and
(MN)_{0}=M_{0}N_{0}
.

The details of the proofs of the above two lemmas are left to the reader.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
,
f(x)=x-a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
U
a block matrix of type-U matrices satisfying
J_{\lambda}(f)U=UJ_{\lambda}(f)
.
Then
U
is nilpotent if and only if
U_{0}
is nilpotent.

Proof.

It can be shown by induction on the number of distinct parts in
\lambda
that
U-U_{0}
is always nilpotent. Suppose that
U
is nilpotent, then
U^{m}=0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus Lemma
2.2
implies that

(U_{0})^{m}=(U^{m})_{0}=0
. Thus
U_{0}
is nilpotent. Conversely, suppose that
U_{0}
is nilpotent, then
(U_{0})^{m}=0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Lemma
2.2
implies that
(U^{m})_{0}=(U_{0})^{m}=0
.
Since
U^{m}-(U^{m})_{0}
is always nilpotent,
U^{m}
is nilpotent, and hence
U
is nilpotent.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Given
\pi=(\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
and
f(x)=x-a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, let
\alpha=(|\pi_{1}|,\cdots,|\pi_{n}|)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
g=(J_{\pi_{1}}\!(f),\cdots,J_{\pi_{n}}\!(f))\in\textup{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and

X_{g}=\left\{\sigma\in\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q}):g\sigma=\sigma\right\}
the stabilizer of
g
in
\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
There holds:

|X_{g}\cap\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}|=q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{ij}(|\pi_{i},\pi_{j}|)}\prod_{s\geq 1}\!r(d_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}},q),

where
d_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}}=(m_{\pi_{1}}^{\underline{s}},\cdots,m_{\pi_{n}}^{\underline{s}})
is the multiplicity vector of order
s
induced by
\pi
and
r(d_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}},q)=|\textup{Rep}(d_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}},\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}|
for
s\geq 1
.

Proof.

For any
\sigma\in X_{g}
, Theorem
2.1
implies that
\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})
is a block matrix of type-U matrices for all

X_{ij}^{(k)}\in\Omega
. The
core
of
\sigma
denoted by
\sigma_{0}
, is the representation of
\Gamma
defined by:

\sigma_{0}(X_{ij}^{(k)})=\sigma(X_{ij}^{(k)})_{0}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq a_{ij}
. Obviously,
\sigma_{0}\in X_{g}
.

Let
R
be a simple cyclic relation for
\Gamma
and assume that the starting point (also the ending point) of
R
is
e
. Then
\sigma(R)
is a block matrix of type-U matrices
and it satisfies
J_{\pi_{e}}\!(f)\sigma(R)=\sigma(R)J_{\pi_{e}}\!(f)

2.2
implies that
\sigma(R)_{0}=\sigma_{0}(R)

2.3

\sigma(R)

\sigma_{0}(R)
is nilpotent. This equivalence still holds when
R
is a relation for
\Gamma
. Thus we have

\sigma\in X_{g}\cap\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}\textit{ if and only if }\sigma_{0}\in X_{g}\cap\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}.

Let
\pi^{(s)}=(\pi_{1}^{(s)},\cdots,\pi_{n}^{(s)}):=(s^{m_{\pi_{1}}^{\underline{s}}},\cdots,s^{m_{\pi_{n}}^{\underline{s}}})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}

(

\alpha^{(s)}=(sm_{\pi_{1}}^{\underline{s}},\cdots,sm_{\pi_{n}}^{\underline{s}})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

\sigma_{0}

\sigma_{0}\cong\oplus_{s\geq 1}\tau^{(s)},

and
\tau^{(s)}\in X_{g^{(s)}}

Theorem 2.3
.

Given
\pi=(\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
an
n
-tuple of partitions and
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
d
,
let
\alpha=d(|\pi_{1}|,\cdots,|\pi_{n}|)
,
g=(J_{\pi_{1}}\!(f),\cdots,J_{\pi_{n}}\!(f))\in\textup{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
and

X_{g}=\left\{\sigma\in\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q}):g\sigma=\sigma\right\}
the stabilizer of
g
in
\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
There holds:

|X_{g}\cap\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}|=q^{d\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{ij}(|\pi_{i},\pi_{j}|)}\prod_{s\geq 1}\!r(d_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}},q^{d}).

Proof.

Suppose that
d>1
as the case for
d=1
has been proved in Theorem
2.2
. Let
c(f)
be the companion matrix for
f
and
\langle c(f)\rangle
be the subalgebra of
\textup{Mat}(d\times d,\mathbb{F}_{q})
generated by
c(f)
. Since
f
is the characteristic equation of
c(f)
,
c(f)
satisfies the polynomial
f
, i.e.,
f(c(f))=0
. Since
f
is irreducible,
f
is the minimal polynomial satisfied by
c(f)
. This implies that

I,c(f),c(f)^{2},\cdots,c(f)^{d-1}
form a basis for
\langle c(f)\rangle
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.,

\langle c(f)\rangle=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}a_{i}c(f)^{i}\,|\,a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},0\leq i\leq d-1\right\}.

Thus
\langle c(f)\rangle
is a commutative subalgebra of
\textup{Mat}(d\times d,\mathbb{F}_{q})
and the following map is an isomorphism:

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(f(x))

\displaystyle\to\langle c(f)\rangle

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto c(f).

Since
f
is irreducible,
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(f(x))
is isomorphic to the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
, and hence
\langle c(f)\rangle
is a finite field with
q^{d}
elements.

When
\deg(f)>1
, Theorem
2.1
still holds as long as all submatrices
U_{ij}
take values from the finite field
\langle c(f)\rangle
. All arguments in the proof of Theorem
2.2
still work with
\mathbb{F}_{q}
being replaced by
\langle c(f)\rangle
. Thus Theorem
2.2
implies the desired results.
∎

3
Counting Formulae

Let
\varphi_{r}(q)=(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{r})
for
r\geq 1
and
\varphi_{0}(q)=1
. For
\lambda=(1^{n_{1}}2^{n_{2}}3^{n_{3}}\cdots)\in\mathcal{P}
in its “
exponential form
”, we define
b_{\lambda}(q)=\prod_{i\geq 1}\varphi_{n_{i}}(q)
.
Let
\phi_{n}(q)
be the number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree
n
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
x
excluded. It is known that for any positive integer
n
,

\phi_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\,|\,n}\mu(d)(q^{\frac{n}{d}}-1),

(3)

where the sum runs over all divisors of
n
and
\mu
is the Möbius function.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\pi=(\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
, let
X^{|\pi|}=X_{1}^{|\pi_{1}|}\cdots X_{n}^{|\pi_{n}|}
and
\mathbb{Q}(q)
the field of rational functions in
q
over
the rational field
\mathbb{Q}
. We define a formal power series in
\mathbb{Q}(q)[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}]]
as follows:

P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},q)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}^{n}}\!\frac{q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\!a_{ij}(|\pi_{i},\pi_{j}|)}\!\prod_{s\geq 1}\!r(d_{\pi}^{\,\underline{s}},q)}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\!q^{\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{i}\rangle}b_{\pi_{i}}\!(q^{-1})}X^{|\pi|}.

Note that
((0),\cdots,(0))\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
gives rise to a term equal to
1
in the sum above.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, let
X^{\alpha}=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. There holds:

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}^{\infty}M(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha}=\prod_{d=1}^{\infty}\left(P(X_{1}^{d},\cdots,X_{n}^{d},q^{d})\right)^{\phi_{d}(q)}.

Proof.

The method applied in Theorem 4.3 from Hua
[
7
]
still works here.
In current context, the Burnside orbit counting formula is applied to
\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}
and the number of stabilizers for
X_{g}
is given by
Theorem
2.3
. Repeating the arguments there yields the desired result.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, let
\bar{\alpha}=\gcd(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
.
Define rational functions
H(\alpha,q)
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
as follows:

\log\left(P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},q)\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}^{\infty}\!H(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha},

where
\log
is the formal logarithm, i.e.,
\log(1+x)=\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}x^{i}/i
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The following identity holds for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
:

A(\alpha,q)=(q-1)\sum_{d\,|\,\bar{\alpha}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}H\Big(\frac{\alpha}{d},q^{d}\Big),

where the sum runs over all divisors of
\bar{\alpha}
.

Proof.

This is the counterpart of Theorem 4.6 from Hua
[
7
]
with slight adjustment on the definition of
H(\alpha,q)
, same arguments apply.
∎

Analogues of Theorem 4.6 of Hua
[
7
]
have been proved by Bozec, Schiffmann & Vasserot
[
1
]
for Lusztig nilpotent varieties and their variants using techniques from Algebraic Geometry. Their definition of
nilpotency is stronger than the one used here. In the language of
\lambda
-ring and Adams operator, Theorem
3.2
is equivalent to the following identities in the ring of formal power series

\mathbb{Q}(q)[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}]]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\!A(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\,\,(q-1)\text{Log}\left(P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},q)\right),

\displaystyle P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},q)=

\displaystyle\,\,\text{Exp}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\!A(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha}\right).

For the definitions of operator
Log
and
Exp
, we refer to the Appendix in Mozgovoy
[
10
]
.

Under the assumption that
r(\alpha,q)
is a polynomial in
q
with rational coefficients for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

H(\alpha,q)
’s must be rational functions in
q
, so are
A(\alpha,q)
’s. As
A(\alpha,q)
’s take integer values for all prime powers
q
,
A(\alpha,q)
’s must be polynomials in
q
with rational coefficients. It follows from Lemma 2.9 of Bozec, Schiffmann & Vasserot
[
1
]
that

. Kac
[
9
]
implies that the degree of
A(\alpha,q)

is at most
1-\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle
where
\langle-,-\rangle
is the Euler form defined by quiver
\Gamma
.

Theorem
3.2
implies that if

’s are known for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
then
A(\alpha,q)
’s are known.

I(\alpha,q)
and
M(\alpha,q)
can be calculated by the following identities:

I(\alpha,q)=\sum_{d\,|\,\bar{\alpha}}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{r\,|\,d}\mu\Big(\frac{d}{r}\Big)A\Big(\frac{\alpha}{d},q^{r}\Big),

(4)

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}M(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha}=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}^{\infty}(1-X^{\alpha})^{-I(\alpha,q)}.

(5)

Identity (
4
) is the counterpart of the first identity of Theorem 4.1 from Hua
[
7
]
and identity (
5
) is a consequence of the Krull–Schmidt Theorem from representation theory. It follows that

and
M(\alpha,q)
are polynomials in
q
with rational coefficients for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Delta^{+}=\{\alpha:A(\alpha,q)\neq 0,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}

The following identity holds in

\displaystyle P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},q)=\!\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\!\!\prod_{s=0}^{1-\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-q^{s+i}X^{\alpha})^{t_{\alpha,s}}.

This is the counterpart of Theorem 4.9 from Hua
[

, same arguments apply.
∎

Kac conjecture now a theorem confirms that Theorem 4.9 of Hua

is a

In view of Lemma 2.9 of Bozec, Schiffmann & Vasserot

Conjecture 3.1

Case 1.
Let

and

. Thus,

\displaystyle P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}^{n}}\!\frac{q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\!a_{ij}(|\pi_{i},\pi_{j}|)}\prod_{s\geq 1}\!\!\left(q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{ij}m_{\pi_{i}}^{\underline{s}}m_{\pi_{j}}^{\underline{s}}}\right)}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\!q^{\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{i}\rangle}b_{\pi_{i}}\!(q^{-1})}X^{|\pi|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}^{n}}\!\frac{q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\!\left(a_{ij}(|\pi_{i},\pi_{j}|)+\sum_{s\geq 1}a_{ij}m_{\pi_{i}}^{\underline{s}}m_{\pi_{j}}^{\underline{s}}\right)}}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\!q^{\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{i}\rangle}b_{\pi_{i}}\!(q^{-1})}X^{|\pi|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}^{n}}\!\frac{q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\!a_{ij}\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{j}\rangle}}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\!q^{\langle\pi_{i},\pi_{i}\rangle}b_{\pi_{i}}\!(q^{-1})}X^{|\pi|}.

Thus Theorem

is equivalent to Theorem 4.9 of Hua
[

.

Case 2.
Let

, and

. Thus,

\displaystyle P(X,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}}\!\frac{q^{g(|\pi,\pi|)}\prod_{s\geq 1}\!q^{g\left((m_{\pi}^{\underline{s}})^{2}-m_{\pi}^{\underline{s}}\right)}}{q^{\langle\pi,\pi\rangle}b_{\pi}(q^{-1})}X^{|\pi|}

. Thus Theorem

is equivalent to Theorem 4.1 of Hua
[

]
.

Case 3.
Let
\Gamma
be the quiver defined by the following matrix, where

is an integer greater than 0:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
g&0\end{array}\right],

and

.

Let
[n]_{q}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(q^{n}-q^{i})
for

. Thus
|\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})|=[n]_{q}
.
Given a dimension vector

, let

be the

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right],

where

is the identity matrix of order

.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{(m,n,r)}
be the centralizer of

in
\text{GL}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q})
, i.e.,

\mathcal{C}_{(m,n,r)}=\left\{(M,N)\in\text{GL}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q}):M^{-1}D_{(m,n,r)}N=D_{(m,n,r)}\right\},

and hence the number of
m\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which have rank

is equal to
{|\text{GL}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q})|}/{|\mathcal{C}_{(m,n,r)}|}
.
For any
(M,N)\in\text{GL}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
M
and

can be written as block matrices as follows:

C_{(m-r)\times r}&D_{(m-r)\times(m-r)}\end{array}\right],N=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}E_{r\times r}&F_{r\times(n-r)}\\
G_{(n-r)\times r}&H_{(n-r)\times(n-r)}\end{array}\right],

where the orders of the submatrices are indicated by their subscripts. Since

M^{-1}D_{(m,n,r)}N=D_{(m,n,r)}\text{ if and only if $A=E$, $C=0$ and $F=0$},

it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}_{(m,n,r)}\right|=\,

\displaystyle[r]_{q}[m-r]_{q}q^{r(m-r)}[n-r]_{q}q^{(n-r)r}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle[r]_{q}[m-r]_{q}[n-r]_{q}q^{r(m+n)-2r^{2}}.

Let

\mathcal{N}_{(m,n,r)}=\left\{N\in\textup{Mat}(n\times m,\mathbb{F}_{q}):D_{(m,n,r)}N\textit{ is nilpotent }\right\}.

Any

can be written as a block matrix as follows:

\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{r\times r}&B_{r\times(m-r)}\\
C_{(n-r)\times r}&D_{(n-r)\times(m-r)}\end{array}\right],

where the orders of the submatrices are indicated by their subscripts.
D_{(m,n,r)}N
is nilpotent if and only if

is nilpotent, therefore

|\mathcal{N}_{(m,n,r)}|=q^{r^{2}-r}q^{mn-r^{2}}=q^{mn-r}.

Let

\mathcal{E}_{(m,n,r)}=\{\sigma\in\text{Rep}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}:\sigma(X_{12}^{(1)})\textit{ has rank }r\}.

Since the number of
m\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that have rank

is equal to
{|\text{GL}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q})|}/{|\mathcal{C}_{(m,n,r)}|}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{(m,n,r)}|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\text{GL}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q})|}{|\mathcal{C}_{(m,n,r)}|}|\mathcal{N}_{(m,n,r)}|^{g}=\frac{[m]_{q}[n]_{q}q^{g(mn-r)}}{[r]_{q}[m-r]_{q}[n-r]_{q}q^{r(m+n)-2r^{2}}}.

Since
\text{Rep}((m,n),\mathbb{F}_{q})_{\mathcal{R}}
is a disjoint union of
\mathcal{E}_{(m,n,r)}
where
0\leq r\leq\min(m,n)
,

r((m,n),q)=\sum_{r=0}^{\min(m,n)}\frac{[m]_{q}[n]_{q}q^{g(mn-r)}}{[r]_{q}[m-r]_{q}[n-r]_{q}q^{r(m+n)-2r^{2}}}.

(6)

It follows that

is a polynomial in

with integral coefficients and hence

can be calculated by Theorem
3.2
.

When

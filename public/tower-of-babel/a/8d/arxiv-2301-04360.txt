Modulation theory for the sine-Gordon equation
A. M. Kamchatnov

Abstract
We give the solution of the Whitham modulation equations for envelopes of pulses evolving according to the sine-Gordon equation. The Whitham equations are interpreted as the equations of relativistic hydrodynamics and their solving is reduced by the hodograph method to solving a linear partial differential equation. We describe the class of solutions of this equation with separation of variables and illustrate the theory by an example of the nonlinear wave packet evolution accompanied by its shrinking and decrease of the number of oscillations in the Whitham nonlinear region.

I
Introduction

The phenomenon of modulation instability was discovered independently in several
physical contexts: as self-focusing of intensive and wide light beams propagating
through a nonlinear medium
askaryan-62
;
askaryan-73
;
cgt-64
, as formation of blobs
in a gas of Langmuir plasmons
vr-64
;
zakharov-72
, as self-contraction of wave
packets in optics
ostrovsky-66
and on deep water
bf-67
;
zakharov-68
.
If the initial distributions of physical parameters of a wave are modulated by
smooth enough functions, then dynamics of modulations is governed in the main
approximation by hydrodynamic type equations which can be solved by the methods of
the compressible fluid dynamics. First examples of such an approach were given in
the theory of self-focusing where the evolution of light beams is described by the
focusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation so that the modulation parameters
are the light intensity and the transverse wave number of the light wave. They obey
the geometric optics equations equivalent to the hydrodynamic equations for
“inverted shallow water” waves. In this case, V. I. Talanov found the solution
for evolution of a light beam with a parabolic initial intensity profile

talanov-65
, and S. A. Akhmanov, A. P. Sukhorukov, and R. V. Khokhlov
ask-66

for beams with initial profiles of the type
\cosh^{-2}(x)
. In the papers

lighthill-1965
;
hayes-73
a similar approach was formulated without derivation
of the NLS equation, however also in the approximation of the moderate wave intensity,
and the resulting modulation hydrodynamic system was reduced by the hodograph method
to the linear equation of elliptic type. Some examples of its solutions for
self-focusing of beams and self-contraction of pulses were given in Ref.
gs-70

and many other examples can be found in the book
zt-91
.

Naturally, these solutions assuming smooth modulation of a wave are only correct up to
the self-focusing moment. Besides that, they are unstable with respect to small
perturbations violating smoothness of the wave profile. Already in
Refs.
karpman-67
;
kk-68
it was noticed that a localized initial perturbation of a
uniform plane leads in the NLS equation theory to formation of an expanding strongly
modulated wave structure. Application to its evolution of the Whitham modulation
theory
whitham-65
;
whitham-77
;
fl-86
;
pavlov-87
showed
kamch-92
;
ehkk-93
;
bk-94
,
that the front of instability propagates with the minimal group velocity corresponding
to the real branch of the dispersion relation and this result was confirmed in
Ref.
bm-16
by the inverse scattering transform method. Generalization
ks-21

of this theory to non-uniform evolving distributions permitted one to find the laws of
propagation of the boundaries of the instability region for arbitrary initial profiles
of the unstable pulse.

The derivation of the results described above is based on the assumption that in the main
approximation the wave is linear and the nonlinearity introduces only a small
intensity-dependent correction into the dispersion relation. In geometrical optics
approximation this is equivalent to the dependence of the refraction index on the intensity,
and then the geometrical optics equations are equivalent to the hydrodynamic equations for
the “inverted shallow water” which simplicity allows one to use the well-developed
methods of gas dynamics. However, the situation is different in case of the well-known
nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation

\varphi_{tt}-\varphi_{xx}+U^{\prime}(\varphi)=0,\quad U^{\prime}=\frac{dU}{d\varphi},\quad U^{\prime}(0)=0,

(1)

which has numerous applications to various physical problems, especially in a particular
case of the sine-Gordon equation with
U^{\prime}(\varphi)=\sin\varphi
(see, e.g.,
scott-07
;
cmkw

and references therein). This equation has traveling wave solutions
\varphi=\varphi(A,kx-\omega t)

in which the dependence of the frequency
\omega
on the wave amplitude
A
cannon be considered
as small anymore. In a modulated wave its amplitude
A
and phase velocity
V=\omega/k
become
slow functions of the space coordinate
x
and time
t
which change little in one wave length
and one period. The corresponding modulation equations for dynamics of
A
and
V
were obtained
by Whitham
whitham-65
;
whitham-77
. In Ref.
maslov-69
similar equations for the
quasi-classical asymptotic solutions of Eq. (
1
) were derived by the Bogoliubov-Mitropolsky
method and it was indicated that these equations are equivalent to equations of relativistic
hydrodynamics (see also the review article
dm-80
). It is essential that in the most
important case of the sine-Gordon equation this dynamics is modulationally unstable, however,
because of complexity of the equation of state related with the dispersion relation

\omega=\omega(k,A)
for waves in the “effective relativistic matter” and of complexity of the
equations of the relativistic hydrodynamics, this theory has not been applied yet to any
concrete problems about evolution of wave packets.

The aim of this paper is to develop the method of solving the Whitham modulation equations for
one-phase traveling waves whose evolution obeys the sine-Gordon equation. Earlier the equations
of relativistic hydrodynamics were studied in the theory of multiple particle creation in
ultra-relativistic collisions of nuclei and nucleons
landau-53
;
khal-54
;
nk-76
;
kamch-19
.
We will show that the methods used in these papers in case of extremely simple equation of state

p=e/3
(
p
is pressure,
e
is energy density) can be modified to become applicable
to much more complicated equation of state corresponding to the sine-Gordon equation. As a result,
we shall derive a linear partial differential equation whose solutions define solutions of the
relativistic hydrodynamics equations in the hodograph method. We shall describe a class of solutions
of this equation with separation of variables and illustrate the theory by an example of the
solution for the self-contracting wave packet when its evolution is accompanied by decrease of
the number of waves in the region of nonlinear oscillations.

II
Whitham equations

First, we reproduce here the main relations of the Whitham modulation theory

whitham-65
;
whitham-77
for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation (
1
). It is easy to
see that this equation has traveling wave solutions
\varphi=\varphi(\xi)
,
\xi=x-Vt
, where
\varphi(\xi)

is defined implicitly by the equation

\xi-\xi_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{V^{2}-1}{2}}\int_{\varphi_{0}}^{\varphi}\frac{d\varphi}{\sqrt{A-U(\varphi)}},

(2)

so that
V
and the integration constant
A
are parameters,
\varphi(\xi_{0})=\varphi_{0}
, and the variable

\varphi
oscillates between two roots of the equation
A-U(\varphi)=0
in the positivity interval of
this expression. Following Whitham
whitham-65
;
whitham-77
, we define the function

\begin{split}W(V,A)&=\sqrt{2(V^{2}-1)}\oint\sqrt{A-U(\varphi)}\,d\varphi\\
&\equiv\sqrt{V^{2}-1}\cdot G(A),\end{split}

(3)

where the integration is taken along a contour around this positivity interval. Then the wavelength
of the above solution is given by the expression

L=\frac{\partial W}{\partial A}=\sqrt{V^{2}-1}\cdot G^{\prime}(A).

(4)

We define the wave number as
k=1/L
, so that

k^{2}(V^{2}-1)=(G^{\prime})^{-2}
, and obtain for the frequency
\omega=kV
the dispersion relation

\omega^{2}=k^{2}+(G^{\prime}(A))^{-2},

(5)

which depends essentially on the amplitude
A
. The group velocity is defined as

v=\left(\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k}\right)_{A}=\frac{k}{\omega}=\frac{1}{V}.

(6)

In a modulated wave the parameters
V
and
A
become slow functions of
x
and
t
, and their evolution
obeys the Whitham modulation equations
whitham-65
;
whitham-77
which in our notation can be
written in the form

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{kV}{V^{2}-1}+A\right)_{t}+\left(\frac{kVW}{V^{2}-1}\right)_{x}=0,\\
&\left(\frac{kVW}{V^{2}-1}\right)_{t}+\left(\frac{kV^{2}W}{V^{2}-1}-A\right)_{x}=0.\end{split}

(7)

For relativistic interpretation of these equations it is convenient to exclude
W
and
V
with
the use of Eqs. (
3
), (
6
) and then we get

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{G/G^{\prime}}{1-v^{2}}+A-\frac{G}{G^{\prime}}\right)_{t}+\left(\frac{(G/G^{\prime})v}{1-v^{2}}\right)_{x}=0,\\
&\left(\frac{(G/G^{\prime})v}{1-v^{2}}\right)_{t}+\left(\frac{(G/G^{\prime})v^{2}}{1-v^{2}}-A+\frac{G}{G^{\prime}}\right)_{x}=0.\end{split}

(8)

Linearization of these equations with respect to small deviations from constant values

A
and
v
yields the characteristic velocities

v_{\pm}=\frac{v\pm c}{1\pm vc},

(9)

where
c
is defined by the expression

c^{2}=-\frac{GG^{\prime\prime}}{(G^{\prime})^{2}}.

(10)

If its right-hand side is positive, then formulas (
9
) have simple physical sense: they
give velocities of propagation of a sound signal with the speed
c
upstream or downstream the
flow of a “fluid” moving with velocity
v
, so that in the laboratory reference frame the
velocity of the signal is equal to the relativistic sum of these two velocities.

Equations (
8
) can be transformed to the diagonal form

\frac{\partial r_{\pm}}{\partial t}+v_{\pm}\frac{\partial r_{\pm}}{\partial x}=0

(11)

by introduction of the Riemann invariants

r_{\pm}=\int^{v}\frac{dv}{1-v^{2}}\pm\int^{A}\frac{cG^{\prime}}{G}\,dA,

(12)

and this simplifies essentially solving concrete problems in case of real sound velocity

c
. However, we are interested in the opposite case of imaginary “sound velocity”
c
,
so let us discuss in some detail the properties of relativistic hydrodynamics for the
sine-Gordon model.

III
Relativistic hydrodynamics for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation

Already Whitham in his fundamental paper
whitham-65
remarked that his averaging procedure
of conservation laws over fast oscillations is analogous to transition from microscopic
description of medium’s motion in statistical mechanics to the averaged hydrodynamic description
which is correct under condition of smallness of gradients of physical variables.
In our case these parameters are
A
and
V
, Eq. (
1
) is relativistically invariant,
so it is natural to expect that after averaging the conservation laws we must arrive at the
equations of relativistic hydrodynamics. In framework of the asymptotic WKB method this was
shown in Ref.
maslov-69
, but for transition from Eqs. (
8
) to equations of
relativistic hydrodynamics it is convenient to start from the equations of the energy-momentum
conservation law in a relativistic flow (see
LL6
),

\frac{\partial T^{00}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial T^{10}}{\partial x}=0,\quad\frac{\partial T^{10}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial T^{11}}{\partial x}=0,

(13)

where

T^{ij}=wu^{i}u^{j}-pg^{ij},\quad i,j=1,2,

(14)

is the energy-momentum tensor in two-dimensional Minkowski space with the metric tensor

g^{ij}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right),

(15)

w=e+p
is the enthalpy density,
e
is the energy density,
p
is the pressure, and

u^{i}
is a two-dimensional vector of “4-velocity”. To identify the Whitham equations (
8
)
with Eqs. (
13
), we introduce in a usual way the 4-vector
u^{i}
,

u^{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}},\qquad u^{1}=\frac{v}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}},

(16)

and then it is easy to see that equations (
8
) and (
13
) coincide with each other if

\begin{split}&T^{00}=\frac{w}{1-v^{2}}-p=\frac{G/(2G^{\prime})}{1-v^{2}}+\frac{A}{2}-\frac{G}{2G^{\prime}},\\
&T^{10}=T^{01}=\frac{wv}{1-v^{2}}=\frac{(G/(2G^{\prime}))v}{1-v^{2}},\\
&T^{11}=\frac{wv^{2}}{1-v^{2}}+p=\frac{G/(2G^{\prime})}{1-v^{2}}-\frac{A}{2}+\frac{G}{2G^{\prime}},\end{split}

(17)

where we divided Eqs. (
8
) by 2 for further convenience. Consequently we get the
relationships of the wave amplitude
A
with thermodynamical functions of effective matter whose
dynamics obeys equations (
13
),

e=\frac{A}{2},\quad p=\frac{G}{2G^{\prime}}-\frac{A}{2},\quad w=e+p=\frac{G}{2G^{\prime}}.

(18)

It is important that the pressure
p
only depends on the energy density
e
. This means that the
mass of particles in the effective matter is negligibly small.
The expression (
10
) for the sound velocity can be written in the standard form

c^{2}=\frac{dp}{de}.

(19)

The temperature
T
and the entropy density
\sigma
of the effective matter can be defined in
the following way. The chemical potential of a gas of massless particles equals to zero, so
the enthalpy is given by the formula
w=T\sigma
, consequently the relationship

dw=Td\sigma+dp=d(T\sigma)
gives
dp=\sigma dT

(see
LL5
). Hence, the squared sound velocity can be written in two forms: from (
10
)
and (
18
) we get

c^{2}=-w\frac{d^{2}G/de^{2}}{dG/de},

whereas Eq. (
19
) with account of
dp=\sigma dT=(w/T)dT
yields

c^{2}=\frac{w}{T}\frac{dT}{de}.

Comparison of these two expressions gives the formulas

T=\gamma\cdot\left(\frac{dG}{de}\right)^{-1},\qquad\sigma=\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot G(e)

(20)

with the same numerical factor
\gamma
in both formulas. Then we obtain the relation

d\sigma=\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{dG}{de}de=\frac{de}{T},

(21)

which agrees with the standard thermodynamical definition of the entropy.

The hydrodynamic equations get especially simple form in the variables
T,\sigma,u^{i}
.
We notice that the formulas for the wave vector
k=1/L
and the frequency
\omega=kV=k/v

are transformed to

\begin{split}&k=2u^{1}\left(\frac{dG}{de}\right)^{-1}=\frac{2}{\gamma}u^{1}T,\\
&\omega=k\frac{u^{0}}{u^{1}}=\frac{2}{\gamma}u^{0}T.\end{split}

(22)

Consequently the conservation of the number of waves law, which follows from Eqs. (
7
)
(see
whitham-65
;
whitham-77
)

k_{t}+\omega_{x}=0,

(23)

transforms to

(u^{1}T)_{t}+(u^{0}T)_{x}=0.

(24)

It is worth noticing that this relation was obtained by I. M. Khalatnikov
khal-54

from Eqs. (
13
) for any one-dimensional relativistic flow what proves that it is
potential. In Whitham’s theory Eq. (
23
) follows from definition of the wave vector
and the frequency as the phase derivatives,
k=\theta_{x},\omega=-\theta_{t}
. We see that both
pictures, Whitham’s modulational and relativistic hydrodynamical ones, agree mathematically
and differ only in notation and physical meaning of variables.

One more equation we obtain from the formula

\frac{\partial(wu^{i})}{\partial x^{i}}-u^{i}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x^{i}}=0,

(25)

which is a consequence of Eqs. (
13
), (
14
) (see Eq. (134.5) and problem 2
in Ref.
LL6
, §134). Substitution of
w=T\sigma
,
dp=\sigma dT
gives at once
the equation

\frac{\partial(\sigma u^{0})}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial(\sigma u^{1})}{\partial x}=0,

(26)

which means conservation of entropy, i.e. the flow is adiabatical.

Let us specify these relation for the sine-Gordon equation when we have in Eq. (
1
)

U^{\prime}(\varphi)=\sin\varphi,\qquad U(\varphi)=1-\cos\varphi.

(27)

Then the integral in Eq. (
2
) reduces to the elliptical one of the 1st kind and its
inversion yields the periodic solution in explicit form

\begin{split}\varphi&=2\arcsin\left[\sqrt{e}\,\,\mathrm{sn}\left(\frac{\xi-\xi_{0}}{\sqrt{V^{2}-1}},e\right)\right]=\\
&=2\arcsin\left[\sqrt{e}\,\,\mathrm{sn}\left(\frac{v(x-x_{0})-t}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}},e\right)\right].\end{split}

(28)

In the limit
e\to 1
this solution converts into the known kink solution of the sine-Gordon equation

\varphi=2\arcsin\left[\sqrt{e}\,\,\tanh\left(\frac{v(x-x_{0})-t}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}},e\right)\right],

(29)

so that
\varphi=-\pi
as
x\to-\infty
and
\varphi=\pi
as
x\to+\infty
. The integral in
Eq. (
3
) can be calculated with the help of substitution

\sin(\varphi/2)=\sqrt{e}\sin\psi
,

\begin{split}G(e)&=2\sqrt{2}\int_{-\varphi_{0}}^{\varphi_{0}}\sqrt{2e-1+\cos\varphi}\,d\varphi=\\
&=16\{E(e)-(1-e)K(e)\},\end{split}

(30)

where
K(e),E(e)
are the complete elliptic integrals of the 1st and 2nd kind, respectively,
defined here according to the handbook
as-79
(
\pm\varphi_{0}
are the roots of the integrand
function). We choose for the factor
\gamma
in Eqs. (
20
) the value
\gamma=8
and then the formulas

\frac{dK}{de}=\frac{E-(1-e)K}{2e(1-e)},\qquad\frac{dE}{de}=\frac{E-K}{2e}

(31)

for derivatives of the elliptic integrals yield the expressions for the thermodynamic functions

T=\frac{1}{K},\quad\sigma=4e(1-e)\frac{dK}{de}=2[E-(1-e)K].

(32)

Equation of state
p=p(e)
has the form

p=2\left(\frac{E(e)}{K(e)}-1\right)+e.

(33)

The squared sound velocity equals to

c^{2}=-4e(1-e)\left(\frac{1}{K}\frac{dK}{de}\right)^{2}

(34)

and it is negative in the region
0\leq e<1
of existence of periodic solutions, that is they are
modulationally unstable. The corresponding Riemann invariants (
12
) are complex:

r_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+v}{1-v}\pm i\arcsin\sqrt{e}.

(35)

In spite of that, as in the NLS equation theory, evolution of the modulation parameters obeys
the hydrodynamic equations (
24
), (
26
) and their solution can be obtained by means
of the hodograph transform.

IV
Hodograph transform

To perform the hodograph transform, we introduce ‘rapidity’
y
instead of velocity
v

according to the relations

u^{0}=\cosh y,\qquad u^{1}=\sinh y,\qquad v=\tanh y,

(36)

and pass to the ‘light-cone’ variables

x_{-}=t-x,\qquad x_{+}=t+x.

(37)

Then Eq. (
24
) takes the form

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{-}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-y}}{K(e)}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{+}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{y}}{K(e)}\right)=0,

(38)

and it is satisfied if

\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-y}}{K(e)}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{-}},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{e}^{y}}{K(e)}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{+}}

(39)

for some potential
\phi=\phi(x_{-},x_{+})
. Now, following Khalatnikov
khal-54
, we make the
Legendre transform to the potential
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}(e,y)
,

\mathcal{W}=\phi-K^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{y}x_{-}-K^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-y}x_{+},

(40)

so that

\begin{split}d\mathcal{W}=&-\frac{dK^{-1}}{de}(\mathrm{e}^{y}x_{-}+\mathrm{e}^{-y}x_{+})de-\\
&-\frac{1}{K}(\mathrm{e}^{y}x_{-}-\mathrm{e}^{-y}x_{+})dy,\end{split}

(41)

and, hence,

\begin{split}&x_{-}=-\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-y}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{dK^{-1}/de}\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial e}+K\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial y}\right),\\
&x_{+}=-\frac{\mathrm{e}^{y}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{dK^{-1}/de}\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial e}-K\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial y}\right).\end{split}

(42)

These formulas correspond to the hodograph transform: if the function
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}(e,y)

is known, then they give the dependence of
e
and
y
on the variables (
37
) and,
consequently, on
x
and
t
.

Equation (
26
) after substitutions of (
36
), (
37
) transforms to

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial(\sigma\mathrm{e}^{-y})}{\partial x_{-}}+\frac{\partial(\sigma\mathrm{e}^{y})}{\partial x_{+}}=\\
&=\frac{\partial(\sigma\mathrm{e}^{-y},x_{+})}{\partial(x_{-},x_{+})}+\frac{\partial(x_{-},\sigma\mathrm{e}^{y})}{\partial(x_{-},x_{+})}=0.\end{split}

(43)

Multiplying it by the Jacobian
\partial(x_{-},x_{+})/\partial(e,y)
, we obtain after simple transformations
with account of Eqs. (
32
) and
d\sigma/de=1/T=K(e)
the equation for
\mathcal{W}
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial e}\left[e(1-e)K^{2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial e}\right]+\frac{K^{2}}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{W}}{\partial y^{2}}=0

(44)

or

e(1-e)\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{W}}{\partial e^{2}}+\left(\frac{E(e)}{K(e)}-e\right)\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial e}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{W}}{\partial y^{2}}=0.

(45)

This equation is of elliptic type and it replaces the Euler-Poisson equation appearing in
applications of the hodograph transform to the gas dynamics equations (see, e.g.,
LL6
).
In modulationally unstable dispersionless dynamics of the NLS equation theory the
analogous equation is the two-dimensional Laplace equation written in polar coordinates
(see, e.g.,
lighthill-1965
;
hayes-73
;
gs-70
). Equation (
44
) transforms to this
Laplace equation in the limit of small
e
.

An important class of solutions of Eq. (
45
) is obtained after separation of variables,

\mathcal{W}(e)=Z(e)Y(y),

(46)

so that

\frac{d^{2}Y}{dy^{2}}=\lambda^{2}Y,\qquad Y(y)=\mathrm{e}^{\pm\lambda y},

(47)

and
Z=Z(e)
satisfies the equation

e(1-e)\frac{d^{2}Z}{de^{2}}+\left(\frac{E(e)}{K(e)}-e\right)\frac{dZ}{de}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}Z=0.

(48)

One can check with the use of formulas (
31
) that for
\lambda^{2}=1
its particular solution
is given by
Z=1/K(e)
. Therefore we make in Eq. (
48
) the substitution

Z=\frac{F(e)}{K(e)}

(49)

and obtain for
F(e)
the hypergeometric equation

e(1-e)\frac{d^{2}F}{de^{2}}+(1-2e)\frac{dF}{de}+\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{4}F=0.

(50)

Its solution is the hypergeometric function
F=F((1+\lambda)/2,(1-\lambda)/2,1;e)

(see, e.g.,
as-79
;
ww-63
). The substitution
e=(1-z)/2
casts it to the Legendre equation

(1-z^{2})\frac{d^{2}F}{dz^{2}}-2z\frac{dF}{dz}+n(n+1)F=0,\quad n=\frac{\lambda-1}{2},

(51)

which for integer
n=0,1,2,\ldots
has solutions in the form of Legendre polynomials

P_{n}(z)=P_{n}(1-2e)
and Legendre functions of 2nd kind
Q_{n}(z)=Q_{n}(1-2e)
. It is clear that any
linear combination of solutions (
46
) is also a solution of Eq. (
45
).

.

VI
Conclusion

As is known, the gas dynamics methods can be used for description of evolution of unstable systems
(see, e.g.,
zt-91
). Creation of the Whitham modulation theory
whitham-65
;
whitham-77
,
discovery of the inverse scattering transform method
zmnp-80
and development of the general
theory of hydrodynamic type systems
dn-89
permitted one to extend these methods to the vast
area of dispersive shock waves theory (see, e.g., review articles
kamch-21a
;
eh-16
). Though
these methods are applicable to modulationally unstable systems as well (see, e.g., the papers

akns-73
;
takhtajan-74
;
kk-76
;
gn-03
on the sine-Gordon equation theory),
their practical applications to one-phase solutions in unstable systems was
limited so far only to step-like situations
kamch-92
;
ehkk-93
;
bk-94
(see also
kamch-97

and references therein). Evolution of the instability region was discussed in Ref.
ks-21

also only for the small-amplitude edge and evolution of modulation parameters in the whole
region of nonlinear oscillations was not considered. In this paper, we have given the exact
solution of the Whitham modulation equations in the sine-Gordon model. Its application to a
concrete example demonstrates quite nontrivial evolution of a nonlinear wave packet when its
contraction is accompanied by going out of waves from the region of nonlinear oscillations.
When the number of oscillations becomes of the order of unity, the Whitham theory becomes
inapplicable and some other methods should be used for description of the wave evolution.

One may hope that this approach will be effective for other versions of the nonlinear
Klein-Gordon equation which describe, in particular, excitation of water waves by wind
(see
kl-95
) or propagation of electromagnetic waves in nonlinear media
(see, e.g.,
su-06
;
sazonov-14
;
su-18
).

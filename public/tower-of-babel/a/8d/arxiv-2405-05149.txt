Local Theory of Yang-Mills-Higgs-Schrödinger Flow
Zonglin Jia

Abstract
In this article, we study two Hamiltonian type flows: Yang-Mills-Higgs-Schrödinger flow and A A -Schrödinger flow. For the first one, we only obtain local existence. However, the uniqueness follows from classical tricks for the second one. Keywords: Geometric energy; Method of viscous disappearance; DeTurck’s trick. Subclass: 58E15, 35J50, 53C80.

1.1
Two flows

The Yang-Mills-Higgs-Schrödinger(YMHS) flow is defined as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &J(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi=\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}\phi+\mu(\phi)\nabla\mu(\phi)\\
&j\partial_{t}A=D_{A}^{*}F_{A}+\phi^{*}\nabla_{A}\phi\\
&\phi(0,\cdot):=\phi_{0},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,A(0,\cdot):=A_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
\phi
is a section on
P\times_{G}K

A
is a connection on
P

is a

\left(\phi^{*}\nabla_{A}\phi,B\right):=\left(\nabla_{A}\phi,B\phi\right).

The above geometric concepts can be referred to
[

The

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &J(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi=\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}\phi+\mu(\phi)\nabla\mu(\phi)\\

1.2
Background

Schrödinger flow

Schrödinger flow origins from two intersting physical models: Landau-Lifshitz system and homogeneous Schrödinger equation. The former describes the evolution of ferromagnets(
[
13
]
); The latter is the fundamental equation of quantum mechanics telling us the states of particles without potentials.

Yang-Mills-Higgs flow

The Yang-Mills-Higgs (YMH) functional consists of the Yang-Mills functional, the kinetic energy functional and the Higgs potential energy functional. Since this functional appears naturally in the classical gauge theory, it has generated a lot of interests among both physicists and mathematicians during the past decades. For example, the Ginzburg-Landau equation in the superconductivity theory coincides with the variational equation of YMH functional. The critical points of the YMH functional are known as Yang-Mills-Higgs fields and the YMH functional is an appropriate Morse function to study the underlying spaces (
[
1
,
16
,
21
,
22
,
27
]
). On the other hand, it is also well-known that the minimal YMH fields are the so-called symplectic vortices and their moduli space can be used to define invariants on symplectic manifolds with Hamiltonian actions (
[
4
,
18
]
).

Chern-Simons-Schrödinger equation

The cousin of the Yang-Mills-Higgs-Schrödinger equation is the Chern-Simons-Schrödinger equation, which is often used to describe the behavior of particles moving in an electromagnetic field without energy dissipation. This equation is related to the quantum Hall theory, and details can be found in
[
11
,
12
]
, etc.

1.6
The methods and tricks

Method of viscous disappearance

The YMHS flows is a kind of Hamiltonian geometric flow, which is closely related to an energy functional
E(u)
and its general definition is as following

J(u)\partial_{t}u=\frac{\delta E}{\delta u}(u)

which is equivalent to

\partial_{t}u=-J(u)\frac{\delta E}{\delta u}(u).

The conception of Hamiltonian geometric flow will be brought in later. Noting that the variation is generally a non-degenerative and positive operator, we approximate this flow by another system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=-(\epsilon\mbox{Id}+J(u))\frac{\delta E}{\delta u}(u).

(1.4)

The advantage is that we transform a degenerative gradient flow into a negative one in order to use the classical theory of second order parabolic equations.

DeTurck’s trick

In our present article, The Yang-Mills-Higgs functional is gauge invariant, which means
(\ref{AS})
is also degenerative. To solve
(\ref{AS})
, we add a term to make the new system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=-(\epsilon\mbox{Id}+J(u))\frac{\delta E}{\delta u}(u)-\mathcal{E}u

(1.5)

be parabolic, where
\mathcal{E}
is an operator of second order. As soon as we get a solution
v
to
(\ref{AS2})
, it is natural to define a new map

u(t,\cdot):=S(t)^{*}v(t,\cdot)

for an operator
\mathcal{D}
of first order and a gauge transformation
S
dependent of time which satisfy

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\frac{dS}{dt}=S\circ\mathcal{D}v,\\
&S(0):=\mbox{Id}\end{array}\right.

such that
u
is a solution to
(\ref{AS})
.

Geometric energy

After obtaining the unique smooth solution
(\phi_{\epsilon},A_{\epsilon})
to the approximation system, We define an energy functional

E_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}\left(||\nabla_{A_{\epsilon}}^{l}F_{A_{\epsilon}}||_{L^{2}}^{2}+||\nabla_{A_{\epsilon}}^{l+1}\phi_{\epsilon}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

since of two reasons. The first one is that the evolutionary equation with respect to
\nabla_{A_{\epsilon}}^{l}F_{A_{\epsilon}}
admits parabolic structure. Meanwhile the second one is that

\frac{d}{dt}||\nabla^{k}_{A_{\epsilon}}a_{\epsilon}||^{2}_{L^{2}}

can be bounded by
E_{k+1}
as soon as we get the uniform estimations with respect to
E_{k+1}
, where
a_{\epsilon}
is the difference between
A_{\epsilon}
and the reference connection
C
, i.e.
a_{\epsilon}:=A_{\epsilon}-C
.

Uniform estimations

The Gronwall’s inequalities with respect to
E_{k}
are divided into two cases: when
k\leq n+1
,

dE_{n+1}/dt\leq C(n+1)(1+E_{n+1})^{\lambda(n)};

and when
k\geq n+2
,

dE_{k}/dt\leq C(k)(1+E_{k})(1+E_{k-1})^{\lambda(k)},

where
\lambda(k)
are positive integers and
C(k)
does not depend upon
\epsilon
. Such format ensures a uniform existing time
T
for all the positive integers
k
.

2.7
Yang-Mills-Higgs functional and the Hamiltonian geometric flow

In order to illustrate the idea of YMHS flow, we firstly bring in the conception of Hamiltonian geometric flow. Suppose
u
is a map taking values in a Kähler manifold with a complex structure
J
and
E(u)
is a kind of energy functional. Moreover,
\delta E/\delta u
is the variation with respect to
E
. The Hamiltonian geometric flow is the following kind of partial differential equation

J(u)\partial_{t}u=\frac{\delta E}{\delta u}(u).

Secondly, we define the following Yang-Mills-Higgs energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{YMH}(\phi,A):=\int_{M}|\nabla_{A}\phi|^{2}+|F_{A}|^{2}+|\mu(\phi)|^{2}.

The critical point of
\mathcal{YMH}
satisfies exactly the Yang-Mills-Higgs equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}\phi+\mu(\phi)\nabla\mu(\phi)=0\\
&D_{A}^{*}F_{A}+\phi^{*}\nabla_{A}\phi=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Theorem 3.1

For any

Proof.

To get existence, we consider two systems. The first one is as following

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}\psi+(\epsilon\mbox{Id}+J(\psi))\nabla_{B}^{*}\nabla_{B}\psi=-(\epsilon\mbox{Id}+J(\psi))\mu(\psi)\nabla\mu(\psi)+(\epsilon D_{B}^{*}b)\psi\\

&\psi(0,\cdot):=\phi_{0},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,b(0,\cdot):=0\end{array}\right.

The second one is with respect to a family of gauge transformations

&S(0):=\mbox{Id}.\end{array}\right.

At last, Let

For the uniqueness we reverse the above process. Suppose

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}S=-\epsilon D_{A}^{*}D_{A}S-D_{A}^{*}\circ S\{\epsilon(A-A_{0})\}\\
&S(0):=\mbox{Id}.\end{array}\right.

It is easy to check that if we define

, the next identity holds true

S\circ\{\epsilon D_{B}^{*}(B-A_{0})\}=\epsilon D_{A}^{*}D_{A}S+D_{A}^{*}\circ S\{\epsilon(A-A_{0})\}

and

||F_{A_{0}}||_{W^{n+1,2}(\nabla_{A_{0}})}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,||\nabla_{A_{0}}\phi_{0}||_{W^{n+1,2}(\nabla_{A_{0}})}.

4.6
Controlling
||A-C||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}
and
||\nabla_{C}\phi||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}

Recall
a=A-C
. Given any
Y\in T^{*}M^{\otimes r}\otimes\phi^{*}V(P\times_{G}K)
, it is not difficult to verify

\displaystyle\nabla_{C}^{k}Y=\nabla_{A}^{k}Y+\mathcal{Q}^{k}(Y,a)

(4.3)

where
\mathcal{Q}^{k}
are lower order terms depending on derivatives of
Y
and
a
up to order
k-1
and satisfy

|\mathcal{Q}^{k}(Y,a)|\leq C_{k}\sum|\nabla_{A}^{i_{1}}Y|\cdots|\nabla_{A}^{i_{r}}Y||\nabla_{A}^{i_{r+1}}a|\cdots|\nabla_{A}^{i_{r+s}}a|

with
0\leq i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r}+i_{r+1}+\cdots+i_{r+s}\leq k-1
,
r,s\geq 1
and
r+s\leq k+1
for some constant
C_{k}
depending upon
P\times_{G}K
.

Direct calculation gives

\partial_{t}\nabla_{A}^{k}a=\nabla_{A}^{k}\partial_{t}a+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}\nabla_{A}^{i}\partial_{t}a\#\nabla_{A}^{k-i}a

and

\nabla_{A}^{l}\partial_{t}a=-(\epsilon\mbox{Id}+j)\left(\nabla_{A}^{l}D_{A}^{*}F_{A}+\phi^{*}\nabla_{A}^{l}\phi+\sum\limits_{p=1}^{l}\nabla_{A}^{p}\phi\#\nabla_{A}^{l-p+1}\phi\right),

which means

\displaystyle||\nabla_{A}^{l}\partial_{t}a||_{L^{r}}\leq C_{15}\left(||\nabla_{A}^{l+1}F_{A}||_{L^{r}}+||\nabla_{A}^{l}\phi||_{L^{r}}+\sum\limits_{p=1}^{l}||\nabla_{A}^{p}\phi||_{L^{s}}||\nabla_{A}^{l-p+1}\phi||_{L^{t}}\right)

for all
r,s,t\in[1,\infty]
with
1/r=1/s+1/t
.

In order to estimate
||\nabla_{A}^{k}a||_{L^{2}}
, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}||\nabla_{A}^{k}a||^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C_{16}||\nabla^{k}_{A}a||_{L^{2}}\left(||\nabla^{k}_{A}\partial_{t}a||_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}||\nabla^{i}_{A}\partial_{t}a||_{L^{p_{i}}}||\nabla_{A}^{k-i}a||_{L^{q_{i}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{17}||\nabla^{k}_{A}a||_{L^{2}}\left(||\nabla_{A}^{k+1}F_{A}||_{L^{2}}+||\nabla_{A}^{k}\phi||_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{p=1}^{k}||\nabla_{A}^{p}\phi||_{L^{s}}||\nabla_{A}^{k-p+1}\phi||_{L^{t}}\right)

\displaystyle+C_{17}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}||\nabla^{k}_{A}a||_{L^{2}}||\nabla_{A}^{k-i}a||_{L^{q_{i}}}\left(||\nabla_{A}^{i+1}F_{A}||_{L^{p_{i}}}+||\nabla_{A}^{i}\phi||_{L^{p_{i}}}+\sum\limits_{p=1}^{i}||\nabla_{A}^{p}\phi||_{L^{s_{i}}}||\nabla_{A}^{i-p+1}\phi||_{L^{t_{i}}}\right)

with
s,t,p_{i},q_{i},s_{i},t_{i}\in[2,\infty]
to be determined such that
1/2=1/s+1/t
,
1/p_{i}+1/q_{i}=1/2
and
1/p_{i}=1/s_{i}+1/t_{i}
. Theorem
2.6
tells us that we can find indices meeting the above conditions to ensure the following inequalities

||\nabla_{A}^{p}\phi||_{L^{s}}\leq C_{18}||\nabla_{A}\phi||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,||\nabla_{A}^{k-p+1}\phi||_{L^{t}}\leq C_{18}||\nabla_{A}\phi||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}

||\nabla_{A}^{k-i}a||_{L^{q_{i}}}\leq C_{18}||a||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,||\nabla_{A}^{i+1}F_{A}||_{L^{p_{i}}}\leq C_{18}||F_{A}||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}

||\nabla_{A}^{i}\phi||_{L^{p_{i}}}\leq C_{18}||\nabla_{A}\phi||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,||\nabla_{A}^{p}\phi||_{L^{s_{i}}}\leq C_{18}||\nabla_{A}\phi||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}

and

||\nabla_{A}^{i-p+1}\phi||_{L^{t_{i}}}\leq C_{18}||\nabla_{A}\phi||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}.

To sum it up, it follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}||a||^{2}_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}\leq C_{19}(1+E_{k+1}^{2})(1+||a||^{2}_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}).

Gronwall’s Inequality tells us that there exists a smooth increasing function
f_{k}
such that

||a(t)||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A})}\leq f_{k}\left(t,E_{k+1},||A_{0}-C||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A_{0}})}\right)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

At last of this subsection, we shall estimate
||a(t)||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}
and
||\nabla_{C}\phi||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}
. From
(\ref{formula})
it follows

\displaystyle||\nabla_{C}^{k}a||_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\nabla_{A}^{k}a||_{L^{2}}+C_{k}\sum\Big|\Big||\nabla_{A}^{i_{1}}a|\cdots|\nabla_{A}^{i_{t}}a|\Big|\Big|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\nabla_{A}^{k}a||_{L^{2}}+C_{k}\sum||\nabla_{A}^{i_{1}}a||_{L^{p_{i_{1}}}}\cdots||\nabla_{A}^{i_{t}}a||_{L^{p_{i_{t}}}}

with
0\leq i_{1}+\cdots+i_{t}\leq k-1
and
1\leq t\leq k+1
, where
p_{i_{1}},\cdots,p_{i_{t}}
are positive numbers to be determined such that

\frac{1}{p_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{i_{t}}}=\frac{1}{2}.

Theorem
2.6
tells us that we can pick indices
p_{i_{1}},\cdots,p_{i_{t}}
such that

||\nabla_{A}^{i_{r}}a||_{L^{p_{i_{r}}}}\leq C_{k}||a||_{W^{k-1,2}(\nabla_{A})}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{for $1\leq r\leq t$.}

To sum it up, we are able to obtain an increasing function
\beta_{k}
such that

||a(t)||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}\leq\beta_{k}\left(t,E_{k+1},||A_{0}-C||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A_{0}})}\right)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Similar approaches are applied to

\nabla_{C}^{k+1}\phi=\nabla_{A}^{k+1}\phi+\mathcal{Q}^{k}(\nabla_{A}\phi,a)\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\nabla_{C}^{k}F_{A}=\nabla_{A}^{k}F_{A}+\mathcal{Q}^{k}(F_{A},a).

Hence we can get two increasing functions
\alpha_{k}
and
\tau_{k}
such that

||\nabla_{C}\phi(t)||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}\leq\alpha_{k}\left(t,E_{k},||A_{0}-C||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A_{0}})}\right)

and

||F_{A}(t)||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}\leq\tau_{k}\left(t,E_{k},||A_{0}-C||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A_{0}})}\right)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We only list the sketch since the trick to get local smooth or regular solution is the same as that of Theorem 1.1 in
[
8
]
.

Step 1 of smoothness:

Theorem
2.9
tells us that we have already obtained

||F_{A_{\epsilon}}(t)||_{W^{k+1,2}(\nabla_{C})}+||D_{C}\phi_{\epsilon}(t)||_{W^{k+1,2}(D_{C})}+||a_{\epsilon}(t)||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C})}\leq\gamma_{k}

for some constant

\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{k}\left(K_{k+1},||A_{0}-C||_{W^{k,2}(\nabla_{A_{0}})}\right)

and all
t\in[0,T]
.

Step 2 of smoothness:

Fixing
k
, we get
(\phi_{k},A_{k})\in C^{k}
by sending
\epsilon
to
0
and Sobolev Embedding Theorem.

Step 3 of smoothness:

Diagonal method implies that we are able to find
(\phi,A)\in C^{\infty}
such that for any
k
,
(\phi_{\epsilon},A_{\epsilon})
converges to
(\phi,A)
in
C^{k}
. Hence,
(\phi,A)
is a smooth solution to
(\ref{YMHS})
with smooth initial data.

Step 1 of regularity:

If the initial data
(\phi_{0},A_{0})
lies in
W^{k+2,2}(M,K;D_{C})\times\mathscr{A}^{k+2,2}(M,P\times_{ad}\mathfrak{g})
, we choose a family of smooth pair
(\phi^{i}_{0},A^{i}_{0})
to approximate
(\phi_{0},A_{0})
in
(W^{k+2,2}(D_{C}),W^{k+2,2}(\nabla_{C}))
-norm. Let
(\phi_{i},A_{i})
be the smooth solution to
(\ref{YMHS})
taking
(\phi^{i}_{0},A^{i}_{0})
as its initial data and existing for
[0,T_{i}]
.

Step 2 of regularity:

Repeating the process of Section
4
, we get a uniform lower bound
\hat{T}
of
T_{i}
, i.e.
T_{i}\geq\hat{T}>0
for large
i
and uniform upper bounds of
(W^{k+2,2}(D_{C}),W^{k,2}(\nabla_{C}),W^{k+1,2}(\nabla_{C}))
-norms with respect to
(\phi_{i},A_{i},F_{A_{i}})
.

Step 3 of regularity:

Sending
i
to
\infty
gives a weak limit
(\phi,A,F_{A})
. Classical trick implies that it is also a strong solution.

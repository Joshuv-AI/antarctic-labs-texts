Superalgebras with homogeneous structures of Lie type
Sami Mabrouk and Othmen Ncib

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we extend the concept of Lie superalgebras to a more generalized framework called Super-Lie superalgebras. In addition, they seem to be exploring various super-generalizations of other algebraic structures, such as Super-associative, left (right) Super-Leibniz, and Super-left(right)-symmetric superalgebras, then we give some examples and related fundamental results. The notion of Rota-Baxter operators with any parity on the Super-Lie superalgebras is given. Moreover, we study a representations of Super-Lie superalgebras and its associate dual representations. The notion of derivations of Super-Lie superalgebras is introduced thus we show that the converse of a bijective derivation defines a Rota-Baxter operator. Finally, we give a generalization of the Super-Lie superalgebras and some other structures in the ternary case which we supported this with some examples and interesting results.

Keywords:
Lie superalgebra, Super-Lie superalgebra, (Super)-left-symmetric superalgebra, Rota-Baxter operator, representation, derivation,
3
-super-Lie superalgebra.

1.
Introduction

Graded Lie algebras are a topic of interest in physics in the context of ”supersymmetries”, relating particles of differing statistics. In mathematics, graded Lie algebras have been known fore same time in the context of deformation theory.
A superalgebra is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
equipped with an even bilinear map (i.e. if
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},\;y\in\mathfrak{g}_{j};\;i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\overline{0},\overline{1}\}
, then
x\cdot y\in\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}
). A Lie superalgebra is a superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
where

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{ij}[y,x],\;\;\text{for}\;x\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},\;y\in\mathfrak{g}_{j},

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]]

\displaystyle=[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{ij}[y,[x,z]],\;\;\text{for}\;x\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},\;y\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}.

In
[
13
]
, the authors studies the theory of Lie superalgebras as Lie algebras of certain generalized groups,
nowadays called Lie supergroups, whose function algebras are algebras with commuting and anticommuting variables. Recently, a satisfactory theory, similar to Lie’s theory, has been developed on the connection between Lie supergroups and Lie superalgebras
[
14
]
.

Another class of non-associative superalgebras called
Lie antialgebras is introduced by V. Ovsienko in
[
32
]
. The axioms were established after encountering two “unusual”
algebraic structures in a context of symplectic geometry: the algebras, named
\text{asl}_{2}

and the conformal antialgebra
\mathcal{AK}(1)
. Ovsienko found that both of them are related
to an odd bivector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{2|1}

invariant under the action of the orthosymplectic
Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
. The algebra
\mathcal{AK}(1)
is also related to the famous NeveuSchwartz Lie superalgebra (see
[
28
,
29
]
for more details).

The study of Rota-
Baxter algebra appeared for the first time in the work of Baxter
[
11
]
in
1960

and were then intensively studied by Atkinson
[
6
]
, Miller
[
30
]
, Rota
[
34
]
, Cartier

[
17
]
, and more recently, they reappeared in the work of Guo
[
25
]
and Ebrahimi-Fard

[
23
]
. A few years later, the Rota-Baxter operators were studied in the super case of some algebraic structures. We are interested in the Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
on Lie superalgebras which is introduced in
[
1
]
and defined as an even linear map
\mathfrak{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying for any homogeneous elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, the identity

(1.1)

[\mathfrak{R}(x),\mathfrak{R}(y)]=\mathfrak{R}\Big([\mathfrak{R}(x),y]+[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)]+\lambda[x,y]\Big).

Pre-Lie algebras (called also left-symmetric algebras, Vinberg algebras, quasi-associative
algebras) are a class of a natural algebraic systems appearing in many
fields in mathematics and mathematical physics. They were first mentioned by Cayley
in
1890

[
18
]
. They play an important
role in the study of symplectic and complex structures on Lie groups and Lie algebras

[
5

19
,
20
,
21
,
27
]
, phases spaces of Lie algebras
[
9
,
26
]
,
classical and quantum Yang–Baxter equations
[
22
]
etc. Recently, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded version of pre-Lie algebras called pre-Lie superalgebras are defined as a linear superspace
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with an even bilinear map
"\circ"
satisfying for any homogeneous elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, the identity

(1.2)

\mathfrak{ass}(x,y,z)=(-1)^{|x||y|}\mathfrak{ass}(y,x,z),

where
\mathfrak{ass}(x,y,z)=x\circ(y\circ z)-(x\circ y)\circ z
, also appeared in many others fields (see
[
15

24

35
]
for more details). They were first introduced by Gerstenhaber
in
1963
to study the cohomology structure of associative algebras
[
24
]

When we look for the relationships between Lie superalgebras and pre-Lie superalgebras structures we find that we can construct pre-Lie superalgebras from Lie superalgebras uses Rota–Baxter operators. Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, then, the product

(1.3)

x\circ y=[\mathfrak{R}(x),y],\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}),

defines a pre-Lie superalgebra.
The reader notices that the Rota-Baxter operator is always considered as an even linear map and he has the right to ask the following question: why is the operator
\mathfrak{R}
not considered odd? In
[
10
]
, the authors answer this question and define the homogeneous Rota-Baxter operators with parity as the data in the following:

A Rota-Baxter operator on Lie-superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is a linear map
\mathfrak{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
defined by

(1.4)

[\mathfrak{R}(x),\mathfrak{R}(y)]=\mathfrak{R}\Big((-1)^{|\mathfrak{R}|(|x|+|\mathfrak{R}|)}[\mathfrak{R}(x),y]+[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)]\Big),\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Motiveted by this works, we introduce the notion of superalgebra with homogenous structures of Lie type. We study this phenomenon for other algebraic structures (associative superalgebras, pre-Lie superalgebras etc) and their applications and associated operators such as representations, derivations and Rota-Baxter operators. The ternary extending superalgebra with homogenous structures of Lie type is studied.

1.2.
Conventions and notations

(1)

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{K}
denotes an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and all vector spaces are over
\mathbb{K}
and finite-dimensional.

(2)

A vector space
V
is said to be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded if we are given a family
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
of vector subspaces of
V
such that
V=V_{\overline{0}}\oplus V_{\overline{1}}.

(3)

The symbol
\overline{x}
always implies that
x
is a

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous element and it is their
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-degree. We denote by
\mathcal{H}(V)
the set of all homogeneous elements of
V
and
\mathcal{H}(V^{n})
refers to the set of tuples with homogeneous elements.

(4)

We refer by
\overline{F}
to the parity of a multilinear map

F:V^{1}\times\dots\times V^{n}\rightarrow W
by

F(V_{i_{1}}^{1},\dots,V_{i_{n}}^{1})\subset W_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}+\overline{F}}.

(5)

Let
End(V)
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space of endomorphisms of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
V.
The graded binary commutator
[f,g]=f\circ g-(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{g}}g\circ f
induces a structure of Lie superalgebra in
End(V)
.

2.
Super-Lie superalgebras and Admissibilty

In this section, we introduce the Super-generalization of some algebraic structures such as Super-associative superalgebras, left(right) Super-Leibniz superalgebras, Super-Lie superalgebras, Super-left(right)-symmetric superalgebras and we give some examples and related results of these structures. All structures studied throughout this work are considered homogeneous.

2.1.
Definitions and Examples

Definition 2.1
.

A
Super-associative superalgebra
is a couple
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
consisting of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}
and a bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(2.1)

\mathfrak{ass}_{\mu}(x,y,z)=0,\;\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},\;(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\text{-graded associativity condition})

where
\mathfrak{ass}_{\mu}(x,y,z)=\mu(\mu(x,y),z)-\mu(x,\mu(y,z))
. If, in addition
\mu(x,y)=(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\mu})(\overline{y}+\overline{\mu})}\mu(y,x),\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
, the super-associative superalgebra is called supercommutative.

Remark 2.1
.

If
\overline{\mu}=0
, we recover the classical associative superalgebras structures also called even associative superalgebras. If
\overline{\mu}=1
, we call it odd associative superalgebras.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
where
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}=<e_{1}>
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}=<e_{2}>
be a two dimentional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Let use define the bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
on the elements of the basis of
\mathfrak{g}
by

\mu(e_{1},e_{2})=-\mu(e_{2},e_{1})=e_{1},\;\;\mu(e_{2},e_{2})=e_{2}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
is a super-noncommutative odd associative superalgebra.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=<e_{1},e_{2}\;|\;e_{3}>
be a three dimentional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Then, the odd bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
defined by

\mu(e_{1},e_{2})=-\mu(e_{2},e_{1})=e_{3},

defines on
\mathfrak{g}
a supercommutative odd associative superalgebra structure.

Definition 2.2
.

A
left Super-Leibniz superalgebra
is a couple
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
where,
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and
[\cdot,\cdot]
be a bilinear map satisfying the following identity

(2.2)

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[y,[x,z]],

for any homogeneous elements
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The identity (
2.2
) is called
left Super-Leibniz identity
.

Let us fixed an element
x\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
. Define the linear map
ad^{L}_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

(2.3)

ad^{L}_{x}(y)=[x,y],\;\forall y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Then,
\overline{ad^{L}_{x}}=\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}
and the left Super-Leibniz identity (
2.2
) can be written as follow

(2.4)

ad^{L}_{x}[y,z]=[ad^{L}_{x}(y),z]+(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[y,ad^{L}_{x}(z)].

Definition 2.3
.

A
right Super-Leibniz superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space equipped with a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]
such that, for any homogeneous elements
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following identity hold

(2.5)

[[x,y],z]=[x,[y,z]]+(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[[x,z],y].

The identity (
2.5
) is called
right Super-Leibniz identity
.

For any fixed homogeneous element
x
of
\mathfrak{g}
, we define the linear map
ad^{R}_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

(2.6)

ad^{R}_{x}(y)=[y,x],\;\forall y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Then,
\overline{ad^{R}_{x}}=\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}
and the right Super-Leibniz identity (
2.5
) can be written as follow

(2.7)

ad^{R}_{z}[x,y]=[x,ad^{R}_{z}(y)]+(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[ad^{R}_{z}(x),y].

Definition 2.4
.

A
Super-Lie superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a bilinear product
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(2.8)

\displaystyle[x,y]=-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(|y|+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[y,x]\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\text{-graded super-skewsymmetry}),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[,\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{z}+\overline{[,\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,[y,z]]=0\;\;(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\text{-graded super-Jacobi identity})

for any
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
. Such Super-Lie superalgebra is denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}=0
, we recover the (even) Lie superalgebra structure and if
\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}=1
, we recall it an odd Lie superalgebra.
A Super-Lie superalgebra is just a left Super-Leibniz (resp. right Super-Leibniz) superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
in which, the product
[\cdot,\cdot]
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded super-skewsymmetric.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=<e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}>
be a three dimentional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space where
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}=<e_{1}>
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}=<e_{2},e_{3}>
. Define the bilinear product
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

[e_{2},e_{3}]=e_{2},

and the other products are zeros. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is an odd Lie superalgebra.

2.2.
Super-Lie admissible superalgebras

Definition 2.5
.

A
Super-Lie admissible superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that, the bracket defined for all homogeneous elements

by

(2.10)

[x,y]=\mu(x,y)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\mu})(\overline{y}+\overline{\mu})}\mu(y,x),

satisfies the

-graded super-Jacobi identity (

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Then,
\mathfrak{g}
is called:

(1)

Super-left-symmetric superalgebra
if it is equipped with a product
\vartriangleright

\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleright}(x,y,z)=(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\vartriangleright})(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleright})}\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleright}(y,x,z),\;\forall x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

(2)

Super-right-symmetric superalgebra
if it is equipped with a product
\vartriangleleft
satisfying

(2.12)

\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleleft}(x,y,z)=(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})(\overline{z}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})}\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleleft}(x,z,y),\;\forall x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Remark 2.3
.

Any Super-associative superalgebra is a Super-left-symmetric (resp. Super-right-symmetric) superalgebra.

3.
Rota-Baxter of Super-Lie superalgebras

In this section, we give an extension of the notion of Rota-Baxter operators on the Super-Lie Superalgebras, for this we are obliged to introduce the notions of the left Rota-Baxter operators, and the right Rota-Baxter operators on Super-Lie superalgebras wich is a generalization of what given in
[
10
]
. Thus, we give the relation between Super-Lie superalgebras and Super-left(right)-symmetric superalgebras from the left (right) Rota-Baxter operators on Super-Lie superalgebras. We also enriched it with some examples.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Super-Lie superalgebra. A
left Rota-Baxter operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
is an homogeneous linear map
\mathfrak{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(3.1)

[\mathfrak{R}(x),\mathfrak{R}(y)]=\mathfrak{R}\Big([\mathfrak{R}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(\overline{y}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)]\Big),\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be left Rota-Baxter operator on a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
. Then, the binary operation defined by

(3.2)

x\vartriangleright y=[\mathfrak{R}(x),y],\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}),

defines on
\mathfrak{g}
a Super-left-symmetric superalgebra structure.

Proof.

It is easy to see that Eq. (
3.2
) gives,
\overline{\vartriangleright}=\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}
.
Let
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then, by using Eqs. (
2.2
) and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleright}(x,y,z)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\vartriangleright})(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleright})}\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleright}(y,x,z)

\displaystyle=(x\vartriangleright y)\vartriangleright z-x\vartriangleright(y\vartriangleright z)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\vartriangleright})(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleright})}(y\vartriangleright x)\vartriangleright z

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\vartriangleright})(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleright})}y\vartriangleright(x\vartriangleright z)

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{R}[\mathfrak{R}(x),y],z]-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{R}[\mathfrak{R}(y),x],z]

\displaystyle-[\mathfrak{R}(x),[\mathfrak{R}(y),z]]+(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{R}(y),[\mathfrak{R}(x),z]]

\displaystyle=\Big[\Big(\mathfrak{R}[\mathfrak{R}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(\overline{y}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\mathfrak{R}[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)]\Big),z\Big]

\displaystyle-[[\mathfrak{R}(x),\mathfrak{R}(y)],z]

\displaystyle=[[\mathfrak{R}(x),\mathfrak{R}(y)],z]-[[\mathfrak{R}(x),\mathfrak{R}(y)],z]

\displaystyle=0.

Then,
(\mathfrak{g},\vartriangleright)
is a Super-left-symmetric superalgebra.
∎

Example 3.1
.

Let us consider the orthosymplectic superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)=\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)_{\overline{1}}
by the respective basis
\{Y,F,G\}
of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)_{\overline{0}}
,
\{E_{+},E_{-}\}
of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)_{\overline{1}}
and the even bilinear product define on the basis of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
by

[Y,F]=F,\;\;[Y,G]=-G,\;\;[F,G]=2Y,

[Y,E_{\pm}]=\pm\frac{1}{2}E_{\pm},\;\;[F,E_{+}]=[G,E_{-}]=0,

[F,E_{-}]=-E_{+},\;\;\;[G,E_{+}]=-E_{-},

[E_{+},E_{+}]=F,\;\;[E_{-},E_{-}]=-G,\;\;[E_{+},E_{-}]=Y.

Then
(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2),[\cdot,\cdot])
is an even Lie superalgebra (See
[
33
]
for more details).

Now, let us define the odd linear map
\mathfrak{R}_{L}:\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)\to\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
on the elements of basis of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
by

\mathfrak{R}_{L}(F)=E_{-},\;\;\;\;\mathfrak{R}_{L}(E_{+})=-\frac{1}{2}G,

and the other terms are zeros. Then
\mathfrak{R}
is a left Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
. By using Theorem (
3.1
), the bilinear map
\vartriangleright:\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)\times\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)\to\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
defined by

F\vartriangleright Y=[\mathfrak{R}_{L}(F),Y]=\frac{1}{2}E_{-},\;\;F\vartriangleright F=[\mathfrak{R}_{L}(F),F]=E_{+},

F\vartriangleright E_{+}=[\mathfrak{R}_{L}(F),E_{+}]=Y,\;\;F\vartriangleright E_{-}=[\mathfrak{R}_{L}(F),E_{-}]=-G,

E_{+}\vartriangleright Y=[\mathfrak{R}_{L}(E_{+}),Y]=\frac{1}{2}G,\;\;E_{+}\vartriangleright F=[\mathfrak{R}_{L}(E_{+}),F]=-Y,

E_{+}\vartriangleright E_{+}=[\mathfrak{R}_{L}(E_{+}),E_{+}]=\frac{1}{2}E_{-},

defines an odd Super-left-symmetric superalgebra structure on
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Super-Lie superalgebra. A
right Rota-Baxter operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
is an homogeneous linear map
\mathfrak{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satifying

(3.3)

[\mathfrak{R}(x),\mathfrak{R}(y)]=\mathfrak{R}\Big((-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(\overline{x}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{R}(x),y]+[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)]\Big),\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a right Rota-Baxter operator on a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},\vartriangleleft)
is a Super-right-symmetric superalgebra, where the bilinear map
"\vartriangleleft"
is defined by

(3.4)

x\vartriangleleft y=[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)],\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
. By using Eqs. (
2.5
) and (
3.3
), a direct computation gives that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleleft}(x,y,z)-(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})(\overline{z}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})}\mathfrak{ass}_{\vartriangleleft}(x,z,y)

\displaystyle=(x\vartriangleleft y)\vartriangleleft z-x\vartriangleleft(y\vartriangleleft z)-(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})(\overline{z}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})}(x\vartriangleleft z)\vartriangleleft y

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})(\overline{z}+\overline{\vartriangleleft})}x\vartriangleleft(z\vartriangleleft y)

\displaystyle=[[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)],\mathfrak{R}(z)]-[x,\mathfrak{R}[y,\mathfrak{R}(z)]]

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}})}[[x,\mathfrak{R}(z)],\mathfrak{R}(y)]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{R})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}})}[x,\mathfrak{R}[z,\mathfrak{R}(y)]]

\displaystyle=-\Big([x,\mathfrak{R}[y,\mathfrak{R}(z)]]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}})}[x,\mathfrak{R}[\mathfrak{R}(y),z]]\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big([[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)],\mathfrak{R}(z)]-(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}})}\Big)[[x,\mathfrak{R}(z)],\mathfrak{R}(y)]

\displaystyle=-[x,[\mathfrak{R}(y),\mathfrak{R}(z)]]+[[x,\mathfrak{R}(y)],\mathfrak{R}(z)]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}+\overline{\mathfrak{R}})(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{R}(y),[x,\mathfrak{R}(z)]]

\displaystyle=-[x,[\mathfrak{R}(y),\mathfrak{R}(z)]]+[x,[\mathfrak{R}(y),\mathfrak{R}(z)]]

\displaystyle=0.

Then,
(\mathfrak{g},\vartriangleleft)
is a Super-right-symmetric superalgebra.
∎

Example 3.2
.

Let us consider the even Lie Superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
given in the Example
3.1
. Define the odd linear map
\mathfrak{R}_{R}:\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)\to\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
by

\mathfrak{R}_{R}(F)=E_{-},\quad\mathfrak{R}_{R}(E_{+})=\frac{1}{2}G,

and the other terms are zeros. Then
\mathfrak{R}_{R}
is a right Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
. So, by using Theorem
3.2
, the bilinear map
\vartriangleleft:\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)\times\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)\to\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
defined by

Y\vartriangleleft F=[Y,\mathfrak{R}_{R}(F)]=-\frac{1}{2}E_{-},\;\;F\vartriangleleft F=[F,\mathfrak{R}_{R}(F)]=-E_{+},

F\vartriangleleft E_{+}=[F,\mathfrak{R}_{R}(E_{+})]=Y,\;\;E_{-}\vartriangleleft F=[E_{-},\mathfrak{R}_{R}(F)]=-G,

Y\vartriangleleft E_{+}=[Y,\mathfrak{R}_{R}(E_{+})]=-\frac{1}{2}G,\;\;E_{+}\vartriangleleft F=[E_{+},\mathfrak{R}_{R}(F)]=Y,

E_{+}\vartriangleleft E_{+}=[E_{+},\mathfrak{R}_{R}(E_{+})]=\frac{1}{2}E_{-},

defines an odd Super-right-symmetric superalgebra structure on
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
.

4.
Represenations and derivations of Super-Lie superlagebras

In this section, we define the notion of representation with parity of Super-Lie superalgebras and to construct its
associated dual representation. A characterisation of representations on a Super-Lie superalgebra structure via semi-direct product has been studied. In addition, we are required to introduce the notions of right and left derivations of Super-Lie superalgebras in which, we show that, the converse of an invertible left (right) derivation define a right (left) Rota-Baxter operator. Still we have not forgotten to give some examples and associated fundamental results.

4.1.
Represenations of Super-Lie superalgebras

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Super-Lie superalgebra and
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space.

Definition 4.1
.

A linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to End(V)
is called representation of
\mathfrak{g}
if
\overline{\rho}=\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}
and it satisfies

(4.1)

\rho([x,y])=\rho(x)\rho(y)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(y)\rho(x),\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

We also say that
V
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. This representation is denoted by
(V,\rho)
.

Example 4.1
.

For any
x\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
, the linear map
ad:\mathfrak{g}\to End(\mathfrak{g});\;x\mapsto ad(x)
(or
ad_{x}
) defined by

ad_{x}(y)=[x,y],\;\forall y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}),

defines a representation on
\mathfrak{g}
called adjoint representation. Indeed, for any
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
, by using the identity (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle ad_{[x,y]}(z)

\displaystyle=[[x,y],z]=[x,[y,z]]-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[y,[x,z]]

\displaystyle=ad_{x}\circ ad_{y}(z)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}ad_{y}\circ ad_{x}(z).

Then,
ad[x,y]=ad_{x}\circ ad_{y}-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}ad_{y}\circ ad_{x},\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
, which gives that, the map ”
ad
” is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to End_{\mathbb{K}}(V)
be a linear map. Then
(V,\rho)
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if,
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V:=(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V})
is a Super-Lie superalgebra, where the bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}
is defined by

(4.2)

[x+u,y+v]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}=[x,y]+\rho(x)(v)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(y)(u),

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
and
u,v\in\mathcal{H}(V)
. We call
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V
the semi-direct product of the Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
and
V
. Note that
\overline{x+u}=\overline{x}=\overline{u}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
and
u,v\in\mathcal{H}(V)
, we have

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[x,y]+\rho(x)(v)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(y)(u)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[y,x]-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(y)(u)+\rho(x)(v)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}([y,x]+\rho(y)(u)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(x)(v))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[y+v,x+u]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}.

Then, the product
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded super-antisymmetric.
It remains to show that, the identity (
2.2
) is satisfied. Let
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
and
u,v,w\in\mathcal{H}(V)
, then by a direct computation, we have:

\displaystyle[x+u,[y+v,z+w]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[x,[y,z]]+\rho(x)\Big(\rho(y)w-(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(z)v\Big)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{z})}\rho([y,z])u,

\displaystyle[[x+u,y+v]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V},z+w]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[[x,y],z]+\rho([x,y])w

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{y})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(z)\Big(\rho(x)v-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(y)u\Big),

and

\displaystyle[y+v,[x+u,z+w]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[y,[x,z]]+\rho(y)\Big(\rho(x)w-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{z}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(z)u\Big)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{x}+\overline{z})}\rho([x,z])v.

Then,
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V:=(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V})
is a Super-Lie superalgebra if and only if

\displaystyle[x+u,[y+v,z+w]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[[x+u,y+v]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V},z+w]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[y+v,[x+u,z+w]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V}]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus V},

for any,
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
and
u,v,w\in\mathcal{H}(V)
. Then, by applying the fact that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Super-Lie superalgebra and we take
u=v=0
, we get

\rho[x,y]w=\rho(x)\rho(y)w-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\rho(y)\rho(x)w

for any homogeneous element
w\in V
, which gives the result.
∎

Let
(V,\rho)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Define
\rho^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\to End(V^{*})
by

(4.3)

\rho^{*}(x)(\xi)=-(-1)^{\overline{\xi}(\overline{x}+\overline{\rho})}\xi\circ\rho(x),\quad\forall\quad x\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})\quad\text{and }\quad\xi\in\mathcal{H}(V^{*}).

Proposition 4.2
.

With the above notations,
\rho^{*}
is a represenation of
\mathfrak{g}
on the dual space
V^{*}
, which is called the dual representation of
\rho
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}(V^{*})
. Then, by using Eq. (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\rho^{*}([x,y])(\xi)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{\overline{\xi}(\overline{x}+\overline{y}+\overline{\rho}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\xi\rho[x,y]

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{\overline{\xi}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}\xi\big(\rho(x)\rho(y)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\rho})(\overline{y}+\overline{\rho})}\rho(y)\rho(x)\big)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\rho})(\overline{y}+\overline{\rho})}\rho^{*}(y)\rho^{*}(x)\xi+\rho^{*}(x)\rho^{*}(y)\xi.

Then,
\rho^{*}([x,y])=\rho^{*}(x)\rho^{*}(y)-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{\rho})(\overline{y}+\overline{\rho})}\rho^{*}(y)\rho^{*}(x),\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus,
\rho^{*}
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V^{*}
. The proof is finished.
∎

4.2.
Derivations of Super-Lie superalgebras

Definition 4.2
.

A left derivation of a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is an homogeneous linear map
\mathfrak{D}^{L}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the following relation

(4.4)

\mathfrak{D}^{L}[x,y]=[\mathfrak{D}^{L}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{D}^{L}(y)]

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
. We denoted by
\mathfrak{D}er^{L}(\mathfrak{g})
, the set of all left derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{D}er^{L}(\mathfrak{g})
with commutator define a Lie superalgebra.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1},\;\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}er^{L}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{D}=[\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1},\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}]
. Then, for any
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}[x,y]

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1},\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}][x,y]

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1}(\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}[x,y])-(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1}}\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}}}\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}(\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1}[x,y])

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1}([\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}(y)])

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1}}\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}}}\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}([\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(y)])

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}(\overline{x}+\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}(x),\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(y)]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(x),\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}(y)]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}+\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}})(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}(y)]-(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{1}}\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}}}[\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(x),y]-(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(x),\mathfrak{D}^{L}_{2}(y)]

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}(\overline{x}+\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}(x),\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(y)]-(-1)(-1)^{(\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}+\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}})(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}(y)]

\displaystyle=[(\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}-(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}}}\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L})(x),y]+(-1)^{(\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}+\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}})(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,(\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}-(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L}}\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}}}\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L})(y)]

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{D}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{D}(y)].

Then,
\mathfrak{D}=[\mathfrak{D}_{1}^{L},\mathfrak{D}_{2}^{L}]\in\mathfrak{D}er^{L}(\mathfrak{g})
, which gives that,
\mathfrak{D}er^{L}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Lie sub-superalgebra of
End(\mathfrak{g})
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{D}er^{L}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Lie superalgebra.
∎

Example 4.2
.

For any fixed homogeneous element
x
of a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
,

the map
ad^{L}_{x}
defined by Eq. (
5.5
) define a left derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
, with the consideration that

\overline{ad_{x}^{L}}=\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]}
as an immediate relation by the definition of
ad^{L}_{x}
.

Definition 4.3
.

A right derivation of a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is an homogeneous linear map
\mathfrak{D}^{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that, the following condition hold

(4.5)

\mathfrak{D}^{R}[x,y]=[x,\mathfrak{D}^{R}(y)]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{R}}(\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{D}^{R}(x),y]

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
. The set of all right derivations of
g
is generally denoted by
\mathfrak{D}er^{R}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Example 4.3
.

For any fixed homogeneous element
x
of a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, the map
ad^{R}_{x}
defined by Eq. (
2.6
) define a right derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Super-Lie superalgebra, then
(\mathfrak{D}er^{R}(\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie superalgebra.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be an homogeneous invertible linear map of a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
. Then:

(1)

\mathfrak{D}
is a left drivation of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\mathfrak{D}^{-1}
is a right Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}.

(2)

\mathfrak{D}
is a right drivation of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\mathfrak{D}^{-1}
is a left Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

(1)

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a left derivation of a Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, then, for any two homogeneous elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\mathfrak{D}[x,y]=[\mathfrak{D}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[x,\mathfrak{D}(y)].

Then
\mathfrak{D}
is invertible if and only if

[x,y]=\mathfrak{D}^{-1}[\mathfrak{D}(x),y]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}(\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}\mathfrak{D}^{-1}[x,\mathfrak{D}(y)].

Suppose that
X=\mathfrak{D}(x)
and
Y=\mathfrak{D}(y)
, we obtain

[\mathfrak{D}^{-1}(X),\mathfrak{D}^{-1}(Y)]=\mathfrak{D}^{-1}([X,\mathfrak{D}^{-1}(Y)]+(-1)^{\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{-1}}(\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{-1}}+\overline{X}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{D}^{-1}(X),Y]),

since the fact that
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}=\overline{\mathfrak{D}^{-1}}
. Then
\mathfrak{D}^{-1}
is a right Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
.

(2)

This is a direct computation by a similar way given in
(1)
.

∎

5.
3
-super-Lie superalgebras

In this section we introduce the case of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
3
-Lie superalgebras which called
3
-Super-Lie superalgebras in which we give some properties, examples and related interesting results.

Definition 5.1
.

A
left ternary Super-Leibniz superalgebra
is a couple
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
where,
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
be an homogeneous trilinear map with degree
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
satisfying for any homogeneous elements
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
the following identity

(5.1)

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2},[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]]

\displaystyle=[[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}],y_{2},y_{3}]+(-1)^{(\overline{x_{1}}+\overline{x_{2}}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{y_{1}}+\mathfrak{q})}[y_{1},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}],y_{3}]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{x_{1}}+\overline{x_{2}}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{y_{1}}+\overline{y_{2}})}[y_{1},y_{2},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{3}]].

The identity (
5.1
) is called
left ternary Super-Leibniz identity
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}
be two fixed homogeneous elements of
\mathfrak{g}
. Define the linear map
ad^{L}_{x_{1},x_{2}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

(5.2)

ad^{L}_{x_{1},x_{2}}(y)=[x_{1},x_{2},y],\;\forall y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Then,
\overline{ad^{L}_{x_{1},x_{2}}}=\overline{x_{1}}+\overline{x_{2}}+\mathfrak{q}
and the left ternary Super-Leibniz identity (
5.1
) can be written as follow

(5.3)

\displaystyle ad^{L}_{x_{1},x_{2}}[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]

\displaystyle=[ad^{L}_{x_{1},x_{2}}(y_{1}),y_{2},y_{3}]+(-1)^{(\overline{x_{1}}+\overline{x_{2}}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{y_{1}}+\mathfrak{q})}[y_{1},ad^{L}_{x_{1},x_{2}}(y_{2}),y_{3}]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{x_{1}}+\overline{x_{2}}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{y_{1}}+\overline{y_{2}})}[y_{1},y_{2},ad^{L}_{x_{1},x_{2}}(y_{3})].

Definition 5.2
.

A
right ternary Super-Leibniz superalgebra
is a couple
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
consisting of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}
and an homogeneous trilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
on
\mathfrak{g}
with degree
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
satisfying, for any homogeneous elements
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following identity

(5.4)

\displaystyle[[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}],y_{2},y_{3}]

\displaystyle=[x_{1},x_{2},[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]]+(-1)^{(\overline{y_{2}}+\overline{y_{3}}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{y_{1}}+\mathfrak{q})}[x_{1},[x_{2},y_{2},y_{3}],y_{1}]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{x_{2}}+\overline{y_{1}})(\overline{y_{2}}+\overline{y_{3}}+\mathfrak{q})}[[x_{1},y_{2},y_{3}],x_{2},y_{1}].

The identity (
5.4
) is called
right ternary Super-Leibniz identity
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}
be two fixed homogeneous elements of
\mathfrak{g}
. Define the linear map
ad^{R}_{x_{1},x_{2}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

(5.5)

ad^{R}_{x_{1},x_{2}}(y)=[y,x_{1},x_{2}],\;\forall y\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}).

Then,
\overline{ad^{R}_{x_{1},x_{2}}}=\overline{x_{1}}+\overline{x_{2}}+\mathfrak{q}
and the left ternary Super-Leibniz identity (
5.1
) can be written as follow

(5.6)

\displaystyle ad^{R}_{y_{2},y_{3}}[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}]

\displaystyle=[x_{1},x_{2},ad^{R}_{y_{2},y_{3}}(y_{1})]+(-1)^{(\overline{y_{2}}+\overline{y_{3}}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{y_{1}}+\mathfrak{q})}[x_{1},ad^{R}_{y_{2},y_{3}}(x_{2}),y_{1}]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{x_{2}}+\overline{y_{1}})(\overline{y_{2}}+\overline{y_{3}}+\mathfrak{q})}[ad^{R}_{y_{2},y_{3}}(x_{1}),x_{2},y_{1}].

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. A trilinear mapping
(x,y,z)\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\mapsto[x,y,z]\in\mathfrak{g}
is said to be an homogeneous ternary Super-Lie bracket with degree
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
\overline{[x,y,z]}=\overline{x}+\overline{y}+\overline{z}+\mathfrak{q}
, it is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded super-skewsymmetric, i.e.,

(5.7)

[x,y,z]=-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{y}+\mathfrak{q})}[y,x,z]=-(-1)^{(\overline{y}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{z}+\mathfrak{q})}[x,z,y],

and satisfies the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Filippov-Jacobi identity

(5.8)

\displaystyle[x,y,[z,t,u]]

\displaystyle=[[x,y,z],t,u]+(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{y}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{z}+\mathfrak{q})}[z,[x,y,t],u]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{x}+\overline{y}+\mathfrak{q})(\overline{z}+\overline{t})}[z,t,[x,y,u]]

A vector superspace
\mathfrak{g}
, together with an homogeneous ternary Super-Lie bracket defined on it, is called a

3
-Super-Lie superalgebra.

Remark 5.1
.

If
\mathfrak{q}=0
, we recover the (even)
3
-Lie superalgebra structure (see
[
16
]
for more details) and if
\mathfrak{q}=1
, which is called odd
3
-Lie superalgebra.

Example 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=<e_{0},e_{1}>
be a two dimentional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space where
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}=<e_{0}>
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}=<e_{1}>
. Define the trilinear product
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

[e_{1},e_{1},e_{1}]=e_{0},

and the other products are zeros. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
defines an odd
3
-Lie superalgebra.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Super-Lie superalgebra. An homogeneous linear map
\mathfrak{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is called
Rota-Baxter operator
(called also
left Rota-Baxter operator
) with degree
\mathfrak{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, if it satisfies the following rule

(5.9)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{R}x,\mathfrak{R}y,\mathfrak{R}z]

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}\Big([\mathfrak{R}x,\mathfrak{R}y,z]+(-1)^{\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{r}+\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{R}x,y,\mathfrak{R}z]+(-1)^{\mathfrak{r}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}[x,\mathfrak{R}y,\mathfrak{R}z]\Big)

for any homogeneous elements
x,y,z
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

By a straightforward computation, we obtain the following results

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a Rota-Baxter operator on a
3
-Super-Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{R}})
is a
3
-Super-Lie superalgebra, where

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{R}}

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{R}x,\mathfrak{R}y,z]+(-1)^{\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{r}+\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]})}[\mathfrak{R}x,y,\mathfrak{R}z]+(-1)^{\mathfrak{r}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}[x,\mathfrak{R}y,\mathfrak{R}z]

for any homogeneous elements
x,y,z
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover,
\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{R}}}=\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]}
and
{\mathfrak{R}}
is
3
-super-Lie superalgeras morphism from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{R}})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Super-Lie superalgebra and
a\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\overline{a}=\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]}=\mathfrak{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{a}=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{a}=[a,\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie superalgebra. Morover
a\in\mathcal{Z}({\mathfrak{g_{a}}})=\{x\in\mathfrak{g};\;[x,y]_{a}=0,\;\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}\}
.

Proof.

It’s obvious to show that
[\cdot,\cdot]_{a}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded super-skewsymmetric and
[a,x,a]=[a,a,x]=0,\;\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Let
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g})
, then, by using (
5.8
), we have

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{a}]_{a}=

\displaystyle[a,x,[a,y,z]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[a,x,a],y,z]+(-1)^{(\overline{a}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{a}+\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]})}[a,[a,x,y],z]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(\overline{a}+\overline{x}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]})(\overline{a}+\overline{y}+\overline{[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]})}[a,y,[a,x,z]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{a},z]_{a}+(-1)^{\overline{x}\overline{y}}[y,[x,z]].

This completed the proof.
∎

Example 5.2

6.
Further discussion

In this paper, we introduce a generalization of Lie superalgebras, when the bracket is not necessary even.
We study some related algebraic structure called Super-Lie admissible superalgebras constructed by Rota-Baxter operators. Their representations are studied and characterized by semi-direct product on the sum direct of superalgebra and super-module. Here we collect some further questions regarding this new algebraic structure:

Deformations and Cohomolgy
: In Section
4
, we study representations of the new algebraic structures called Super-Lie superalgebras as a generalization of Lie superalgebras. In
[
4
]
the authors study deformations and cohomlogy of Lie superalgebra with a given representation. Motivated by this work, will this allow us to generalize the studies of cohomology and deformations to the Super-Lie superalgebras?

Maurer-Cartan characterization
: In
[
4
]
, the authors give Maurer-Cartan characterizations as well as a cohomology theory
of Lie superalgebras. Explicitly, they introduce the notion of a bidifferential graded
Lie algebra and thus give Maurer-Cartan characterizations of Lie superalgebras. One may now ask the following question: Can the graded Lie algebra introduced by the authors characterize the cohomolgy of Super-Lie superalgebra when the bracket is odd?

\mathcal{O}
-operators
: An
\mathcal{O}
-operator is a relative generalization of Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie superalgebra associated to a given representation (see
[
10
]
). A homogeneous linear map
T:V\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of a Lie superalgebra

({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot])
associated to the representation
(V,\rho)
if it satisfies

(6.1)

[T(v),T(w)]=T\Big((-1)^{(|T|+|v|)|T|}\rho(T(v))w-(-1)^{|v|(|T|+|w|)}\rho(T(w))v\Big),\;\;\forall v,w\in\mathcal{H}(V).

In Section
3
, we introduce a generalization of Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie superalgebra to Super-Lie superalgebra case. It is interesting to generalize this notion for the relative case and study their cohomolgies and deformations.

Classification of odd Rota-Baxter operators
: In
[
1
,
3
]
, the authors give a classification of the even homogeneous Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie superalgebras and construct pre-Lie superalgebras from the homogeneous Rota-Baxter operators and their subadjacent Lie superalgebras. We can also classify the odd homogeneous Rota-Baxter operators on Lie superalgebras like Witt superalgebras and Witt superalgebras of Block type. We construct their associated subadjacent odd Lie superalgebras.

Induced
3
-super-Lie superalgebras
: In
[
7
]
, the structure of
3
-Lie algebras induced by Lie algebras have been
investigated. The super case of this construction using an even trace function is studied by V. Abramov in
[
2
]
(see more
[
12
,
31
]
). By this works, can we induce an odd
3
-Lie superalgebra using an odd trace function on a Lie superalgebra?

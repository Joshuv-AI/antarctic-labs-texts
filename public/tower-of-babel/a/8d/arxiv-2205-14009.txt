Universality of minimal length
Ahmed Farag Ali ∇ △, Ibrahim Elmashad △, Jonas Mureika □,○

Abstract
We present an argument reinterpreting the generalized uncertainty principle (GUP) and its associated minimal length as an effective variation of Planck constant ( ℏ \hbar ), complementing Dirac’s large number hypothesis of varying G G . We argue that the charge radii ( i.e. the minimal length of a scattering process) of hadrons/nuclei along with their corresponding masses support an existence of an effective variation of ℏ \hbar . This suggests a universality of a minimal length in measurement of scattering process. Varying ℏ \hbar and G G explains the necessity of Von Neumann entropy correction in Bekenstein-Hawking entropy-area law. Lastly, we suggest that the effective value of ℏ \hbar derived from various elements may be related to the epoch of their creation via nucleosynthesis. “Change is the only constant” Heraclitus

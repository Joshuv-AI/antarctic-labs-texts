Introduction

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on the interval
[0,1)
. The Hankel matrix

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}=(\mu_{n,k})_{n,k\geq 0}
with entries
\mu_{n,k}=\mu_{n+k}
, where

\mu_{n}=\int_{[0,1)}t^{n}d\mu(t)
, For analytic functions
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{n}
,
the generalized Hilbert operator define as

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a_{k})z^{n}.

whenever the right hand side makes sense and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
.

In recent decades, the generalized Hilbert operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
which induced by the
Hankel matrix
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
have been studied extensively. For example, Galanopoulos and
Peláez
[
7
]
characterized the Borel measure
\mu
for which the Hankel operator

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is a bounded (resp.,compact) operator on
H^{1}
. Then Chatzifountas, Girela
and Peláez
[
1
]
extended this work the all Hardy spaces
H^{p}
with
0<p<\infty
. In

[
9
]
, Girela and Merchán studied the operator acting on certain conformally invariant
spaces of analytic functions on the disk.

In 2021, Ye and Zhou
[
16
]
firstly used the Hankel matrix defined the Derivative-Hilbert
operator
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a_{k})(n+1)z^{n}.

(1.1)

Another generalized Hilbert-integral operator related to
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
denoted by

\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{\alpha}}(\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
) is defined by

\mathcal{I}\mu_{\alpha}(f)(z)=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{{(1-tz)^{\alpha}}}d\mu(t).

whenever the right hand side makes sense and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
.
We can easily see that the case
\alpha=1
is the integral representation of the generalized
Hilbert operator. Ye and Zhou characterized the measure
\mu
for which
\mathcal{I}\mu_{2}

and
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is bounded (resp.,compact) on Bloch space
[
16
]
and Bergman spaces

[
17
]
.

In this paper, we consider the operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{DH}_{\mu},\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}:H^{p}\rightarrow H^{q},\quad 0<p<\infty,\quad q\geq p\;and\;q\geq 1.

\displaystyle\mathcal{DH}_{\mu},\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}:H^{p}\rightarrow B_{q},\quad 0<p\leq 1,\quad 0<q<1.

The aim is to study the boundedness (resp.,compactness) of
\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}
and

\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
.

In this article we characterize the positive Borel measures
\mu
for which the operator
which
\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}
and
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is well defined in the Hardy spaces

H^{p}
. Then we give the necessary and sufficient conditions such that operator

\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is bounded (resp.,compact) from the Hardy space
H^{p}(0<p<\infty)
into
the space
H^{q}(q\geq p
and
q\geq 1)
, or from
H^{p}(0<p\leq 1)
into
B_{q}(0<q<1)
.

2
Notation and Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disk of the complex plane, and let
H(\mathbb{D})
denote the set of all analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

If
0<r<1
and
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, we set

\displaystyle M_{p}(r,f)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad 0<p<\infty.

\displaystyle M_{\infty}(r,f)=\sup_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, the Hardy space
H^{p}
consists of those
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

||f||_{H^{p}}\overset{def}{=}\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(r,f)<\infty.

We refer to
[
5
]
for the notation and results regarding Hardy spaces.

For
0<q<1
, we let
B_{q}

[
15
]
denote the space consisting of those
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
for which

||f||_{B_{q}}=\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{1/q-2}M_{1}(r,f)dr<\infty.

The Banach space
B_{q}
is the “containing Banach space” of
H^{q}
, that is,
H^{q}
is a dense subspace of
B_{q}
, and the two spaces have the same continuous linear functionals. (We mention
[
15
]
as general references for the
B_{q}
spaces.)

The space
BMOA
consists of those function
f\in H^{1}
whose boundary values have bounded mean oscillation on
\partial\mathbb{D}
as defined by John and Niirenberg. There are many characterizations of
BMOA
functions. Let us mention the following.

Let
\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}
be a M
\ddot{o}
bius transformations. If
f
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f\in BMOA
if and only if

||f||_{BMOA}\overset{def}{=}|f(0)|+||f||_{*}<\infty.

where

||f||_{*}\overset{def}{=}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}||f\circ\varphi_{a}-f(a)||_{H^{2}}.

It is clear that the seminorm
||\;||_{*}
is conformally invariant. If

\lim_{|a|\rightarrow 1}||f\circ\varphi_{a}-f(a)||_{H^{2}}=0,

then we say that
f
belongs to the space VMOA (analytic functions of vanishing mean oscillation). We refer to
[
8
]
for the theory of
BMOA
functions.

Finally, we recall that a function
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
is said to be a Bloch function if

||f||_{\mathcal{B}}\overset{def}{=}|f(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

The space of all Bloch functions is denoted by
\mathcal{B}
. A classical reference for the theory of Bloch functions is
[
10
,
14
]
. The relation between these spaces we introduced above is well known,

H^{\infty}\varsubsetneq BMOA\varsubsetneq\mathcal{B},\quad BMOA\varsubsetneq\bigcap_{0<p<\infty}H^{p}.

Let us recall the knowledge of Carleson measure, which is a very useful tool in the study of Banach spaces of analytic functions. For
0<s<\infty
, a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
will be called an
s
-Carleson measure if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\mu(S(I))\leq C|I|^{s}.

The Carleson square
S(I)
is defined as

S(I)=\{z=re^{i\theta}:e^{i\theta}\in I;1-\frac{|I|}{2\pi}\leq r\leq 1\}.

where
I
is an interval of
\partial\mathbb{D}
,
|I|
denotes the length of
I
. If
\mu
satisfies
\lim_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|^{s}}=0
, we call
\mu
is a vanishing
s
-Carleson measure.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. For
0\leq\alpha<\infty
and
0<s<\infty
we say that
\mu
is
\alpha
-logarithmic
s
-Carleson measure,if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\frac{\mu(S(I))(\log\frac{2\pi}{|I|})^{\alpha}}{|I|^{s}}\leq C\quad\quad I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}.

If
\mu(S(I))(\log\frac{2\pi}{|I|})^{\alpha}=o(|I|^{s})
, as
|I|\rightarrow 0
, we say that
\mu
is vanishing
\alpha
-logarithmic
s
-Carleson measure
[
13
,
18
]
.

Suppose
\mu
is a
s
-Carleson measure on
\mathbb{D}
, we see that the identity mapping
i
is well defined from
H^{p}
into
L^{q}(\mathbb{D},\mu).
Let
\mathcal{N}(\mu)
be the norm of
i
. For
0<r<1
, let

d\mu_{r}(z)=\chi_{r<|z|<1}(t)d\mu(t).

Then
\mu
is a vanishing
s
-Carleson measure if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mu_{r})\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad r\rightarrow 1^{-}.

(2.1)

A positive Borel measure on [0, 1) also can be seen as a Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
by identifying it with the measure
\mu
defined by

\tilde{\mu}(E)=\mu(E\bigcap[0,1)).

for any Borel subset E of
\mathbb{D}
. Then a positive Borel measure
\mu
on [0,1) can be seen as an
s
-Carleson measure on
\mathbb{D}
, if

\mu([t,1))\leq C(1-t)^{s},\quad 0\leq t<1.

Also, we have similar statements for vanishing
s
-Carleson measures,
\alpha
-logarithmic

s
-Carleson and vanishing
\alpha
-logarithmic
s
-Carleson measures.

As usual, throughout this paper,
C
denotes a positive constant which depends only on the displayed parameters but not necessarily the same from one occurrence to the next. For any given
p>1
,
p^{\prime}
will denote the conjugate index of p, that is,
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
.

3
Conditions such that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is well defined on Hardy spaces

In this section, we find the sufficient condition such that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
are well defined
in
H^{p}(0<p<\infty)
and obtain that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f)=\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}(f)
, for all
f\in H^{p}
, with the certain condition.

We first recall two results about the coefficients of functions in Hardy spaces.

Lemma 3.1

[
5
, p.98]
If

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H^{p},\quad 0<p\leq 1,

then

a_{n}=o(n^{1/p-1}),

and

|a_{n}|\leq Cn^{1/p-1}||f||_{H^{p}}.

Lemma 3.2

[
5
, p.95]
If

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H^{p},\quad 0<p\leq 2,

then
\sum n^{p-2}|a_{n}|^{p}<\infty
and

\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{p-2}|a_{n}|^{p}\right\}^{1/p}\leq C||f||^{p}_{H^{p}}.

Theorem 3.1

Suppose
0<p<\infty
and let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
. Then the power series in (
1.1
) defines a well defined analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
for every
f\in H^{p}
in any of the two following cases.

(i) The measure
\mu
is a 1/p-Carleson measure, if
0<p\leq 1.

(ii) The measure
\mu
is a 1-Carleson measure, if
1<p<\infty.

Furthermore, in such cases we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f)(z)=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{{(1-tz)^{2}}}d\mu(t)=\mathcal{I}\mu_{2}(f)(z).

(3.1)

First recall a well known result of Hastings
[
11
]
: For
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\mu
is a
q/p
-Carleson measure if and only if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{[0,1)}|f(t)|^{q}d\mu(t)\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C||f||_{H^{p}},\quad for\;all\;f\in H^{p}.

(3.2)

(i) Suppose that
0<p\leq 1
and
\mu
is a
1/p
-Carleson measure. Then (
3.2
) gives

\int_{[0,1)}|f(t)|d\mu(t)\leq C||f||_{H^{p}},\quad for\;all\;f\in H^{p}.

Fix
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in H^{p}
and
z
with
|z|<r
,
0<r<1
. It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)}\frac{|f(t)|}{{|1-tz|^{2}}}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-r)^{2}}\int_{[0,1)}|f(t)|d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{(1-r)^{2}}||f||_{H^{p}}.

This implies that the integral
\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{{(1-tz)^{2}}}d\mu(t)
uniformly converges and that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\mu_{2}(f)(z)=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{{(1-tz)^{2}}}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\left(\int_{[0,1)}t^{n}f(t)d\mu(t)\right)z^{n}.

(3.3)

Take
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in H^{p}
. Since
\mu
is
1/p
-Carleson measure, by
[
1
, Proposition 1]
and Lemma
3.1
, we have that there exists
C>0
such that

|\mu_{n,k}|=|\mu_{n+k}|\leq\frac{C}{(k+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}},

|a_{k}|\leq C(k+1)^{(1-p)/p}\quad for\;all\;n,k.

Then it follows that, for every
n
,

\displaystyle(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\mu_{n,k}||a_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq C(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(k+1)^{1/p}}=C(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{k}|^{p}|a_{k}|^{1-p}}{(k+1)^{1/p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{k}|^{p}(k+1)^{(1-p)^{2}/p}}{(k+1)^{1/p}}

\displaystyle=C(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^{p-2}|a_{k}|^{p},

and then by Lemma
3.2
, we deduce that

(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\mu_{n,k}||a_{k}|\leq C(n+1)||f||_{H^{p}}^{p}.

This implies that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a well defined for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and that

\displaystyle\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f)(z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a_{k})z^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\int_{[0,1)}t^{n}f(t)d\mu(t)z^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{{(1-tz)^{2}}}d\mu(t).

(3.4)

This give that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f)=\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}(f).

(ii) When
1<p<\infty
, since
\mu
is a 1-Carleson measure, (
3.2
) holds, then the argument used in the proof of (i) give that, for every
f\in H^{p}
,
I_{\mu_{2}}
is well defined analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
and we have (
3
).

And since
\mu
is
1
-Carleson measure by
[
1
, Theorm 3]
, we know

(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a_{k}=(n+1)\int_{[0,1)}t^{n}f(t)d\mu(t),

which implies that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a well defined for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f)=\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}(f).

4
Bounededness of
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
acting on Hardy spaces

In this section, we mainly characterize those measures
\mu
for which
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a bounded (resp., compact) operator from
H^{p}
into
H^{q}
for some
p
and
q
.

Theorem 4.1

Suppose
0<p\leq 1
and let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
which satisfies the condition in Theorem
3.1
.

(i) If
q>1
, then
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a bounded operator from
H^{p}
into
H^{q}
if and only if
\mu
is a
(1/p+1/q^{\prime}+1)
-Carleson measure.

(ii) If
q=1
, then
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a bounded operator from
H^{p}
into
H^{1}
if and only if
\mu
is a
(1/p+1)
-Carleson measure.

(iii) If
0<q<1
, then
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a bounded operator from
H^{p}
into
B_{q}
if and only if
\mu
is a
(1/p+1)
-Carleson measure.

Suppose
0<p\leq 1
. Since
\mu
satisfies the condition in Theorem
3.1
, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain that

\int_{[0,1)}|f(z)|d\mu(t)<\infty,\quad for\;all\;f\in H^{p}.

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{[0,1)}\left|\frac{f(t)g(e^{i\theta})}{(1-re^{i\theta}t)^{2}}\right|d\mu(t)d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-r)^{2}}\int_{[0,1)}|f(t)|d\mu(t)\int_{0}^{2\pi}|g(e^{i\theta})|d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C||g||_{H^{1}}}{(1-r)^{2}}<\infty\quad 0\leq r<1,\;f\in H^{p},g\in H^{1}.

(4.1)

Using Theorem
3.1
, (
4
) and Fubini’s theorem, and Cauchy’s integral representation of of
H^{1}

[
5
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f)(re^{i\theta})}g(e^{i\theta})d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\int_{[0,1)}\frac{\overline{f(t)}d\mu(t)}{(1-re^{-i\theta}t)^{2}}\right)g(e^{i\theta})d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f(t)}\int_{|e^{i\theta}|=1}\frac{g(e^{i\theta})e^{i\theta}}{(e^{i\theta}-rt)^{2}}de^{i\theta}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f(t)}(g(rt)rt)^{\prime}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f(t)}(g(rt)+rtg^{\prime}(rt))d\mu(t),\quad 0\leq r<1,\;f\in H^{p},g\in H^{1}.

(4.2)

(i)First consider
q>1
. Using (
4
) and the duality theorem
[
5
]
for
H^{q}
which says that
(H^{p})^{*}\cong H^{p^{\prime}}
and
(H^{p^{\prime}})^{*}\cong H^{p}(p>1)
, under the Cauchy pairing

\displaystyle<f,g>=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{f(e^{i\theta})}g(e^{i\theta})d\theta,\quad f\in H^{p},g\in H^{p^{\prime}}.

(4.3)

We obtain that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a bounded operator from
H^{p}
into
H^{q}
if and only if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f(t)}(g(t)+tg^{\prime}(t))d\mu(t)\right|\leq C||f||_{H^{p}}||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}},\;f\in H^{p},g\in H^{q^{\prime}}.

(4.4)

Assume that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a bounded operator from
H^{p}
into
H^{q}
. Take the families of text functions

f_{a}(z)=\left(\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-az)^{2}}\right)^{1/p},\quad g_{a}(z)=\left(\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-az)^{2}}\right)^{1/{q^{\prime}}},\quad 0<a<1.

A calculation shows that
\{f_{a}\}\subset H^{p}
,
\{g_{a}\}\subset H^{q^{\prime}}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in[0,1)}||f||_{H^{p}}<\infty\quad and\quad\sup_{a\in[0,1)}||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}}<\infty,

It follows that

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle>C\;\sup_{a\in[0,1)}||f||_{H^{p}}\sup_{a\in[0,1)}||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq\left|\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f_{a}(t)}(g_{a}(t)+tg^{\prime}_{a}(t))d\mu(t)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{[a,1)}\left(\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-at)^{2}}\right)^{1/p}\left(\left(\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-at)^{2}}\right)^{1/{q^{\prime}}}+\frac{2t^{2}}{q^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-at)^{2+{q^{\prime}}}}\right)^{1/{q^{\prime}}}\right)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(1-a^{2})^{1/p+1/{q^{\prime}}+1}}\mu([a,1)).

(4.5)

This is equivalent to saying that
\mu
is a
(1/p+1/q^{\prime}+1)
-Carleson measure.

On the other hand, suppose
\mu
is a
(1/p+1/q^{\prime}+1)
-Carleson measure,It is well known that any function

[
5
]
has the property

\displaystyle|g(z)|\leq C\frac{||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}}}{(1-|z|)^{1/q^{\prime}}}.

(4.6)

By Cauchy formula, we can obtain that

\displaystyle|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq C\frac{||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}}}{(1-|z|)^{1/q^{\prime}+1}}.

(4.7)

Let
d\nu(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{1/q^{\prime}+1}}d\mu(t)
. Using Lemma 3.2 of
[

]
, we obtain that

is a

-Carleson.
This together with (

) and (

) we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f(t)}(g(t)+tg^{\prime}(t))d\mu(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}}\int_{[0,1)}|f(t)|\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{1/q^{\prime}}}+\frac{t}{(1-t)^{1/q^{\prime}+1}}\right)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq C||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}}\int_{[0,1)}|f(t)|d\nu(t),

\displaystyle\leq C||g||_{H^{q^{\prime}}}||f||_{H^{p}},\quad g\in H^{q^{\prime}}\;f\in H^{p}.

(4.8)

Hence (

) holds and then
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a bounded operator from
H^{p}
into
H^{q}
.

(ii)We shall use Fefferman’s duality theorem, which says that
(H^{1})^{*}\cong BMOA
and
(VMOA)^{*}\cong H^{1}
, under the Cauchy pairing

\displaystyle<f,g>=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{f(re^{i\theta})}g(e^{i\theta})d\theta,\quad f\in H^{1},g\in BMOA(resp.VMOA).

Theorem 5.3

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
which
satisfies the condition in Theorem
3.1
. Then
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a compact operator in
H^{p}
if and only if
\mu
is a vanishing 2-Carleson measure.

Firstly, let
p=1
, we know that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a compact operator in
H^{1}
if and only if
\mu
is a vanishing 2-Carleson measure by Theorem 5.1.

Next, let
p=2
, by Theorem 5.2, We only need to prove
if
\mu
is a vanishing 2-Carleson measure then
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a compact operator in
H^{2}
.

Assume that
\mu
is a vanishing 2-Carleson measure and let
\{f_{j}\}
be a sequence of functions in
H^{2}
with
||f_{j}||_{H^{2}}\leq 1
, for all
j
, and such that
f_{j}\rightarrow 0
, uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.
Since
\mu
is a vanishing 2-Carleson measure then
\mu_{n+k}=o(\frac{1}{(n+k+1)^{2}})
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Say

\mu_{n,k}=\mu_{n+k}=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{(n+k+1)^{2}},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

Then
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}\rightarrow 0
. Say that, for every
j
,

f_{j}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{(j)}z^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

By using the classical Hilbert inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{(j)}_{k}}{n+k+1}\right|^{2}\leq\pi^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|{a^{(j)}_{k}}|^{2}\leq\pi^{2}.

(5.5)

Take
\varepsilon>0
and next take a natural number
N
such that

n\geq N\quad\Rightarrow\quad\varepsilon_{n}^{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi^{2}}.

We have

\displaystyle||\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f_{j})||_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a^{(j)}_{k}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a^{(j)}_{k}\right|^{2}+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a^{(j)}_{k}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a^{(j)}_{k}\right|^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}a^{(j)}_{k}}{(n+k+1)^{2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a^{(j)}_{k}\right|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{(j)}_{k}}{n+k+1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a^{(j)}_{k}\right|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(5.6)

Now, since
f_{j}\rightarrow 0
, uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, it follows that

\sum_{n=0}^{N}(n+1)^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}a^{(j)}_{k}\right|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad as\;j\rightarrow\infty.

Then it follows that that there exist
j_{0}\in N
such that
||\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f_{j})||_{H^{2}}^{2}<\varepsilon
for all
j\geq j_{0}
. So, we have proved that
||\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}(f_{j})||_{H^{2}}^{2}\rightarrow 0
. The compactness of
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
on
H^{2}
follows.

Since we have prove that when
p=1
, the compactness of
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
on
H^{1}
. To deal with the cases
1<p<2
, we use again complex interpolation. Let
1<p<2
and
\mu
be a vanishing 2-Carleson measure. Recall that

\displaystyle H^{p}=(H^{2},H^{1})_{\theta},\quad if\;1<p<2\;and\;\theta=\frac{2}{p}-1.

We have also that if
2<s<\infty
then

\displaystyle H^{2}=(H^{s},H^{1})_{\alpha}.

for a certain
\alpha\in(0,1)
, namely,
\alpha=(1/2-1/s)/(1-1/s)
. Since
H^{2}
is reflexive, and
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is compact from
H^{2}
into itself and from
H^{1}
into itself, Theorem 10 of
[
3
]
gives that
\mathcal{DH}_{\mu}
is a compact operator in
H^{p}(1\leq p\leq 2)
.

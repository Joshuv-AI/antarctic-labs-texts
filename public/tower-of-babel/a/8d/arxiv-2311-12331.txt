1.
Introduction

Let
1<q<\infty
and
-1+N/q\leq s<1/q
, where
N
is the space dimension.
In this paper, we use the
L_{1}
–
B^{s+1}_{q,1}\times B^{s}_{q,1}
maximal regularity framework to show
the local well-posedness of the Navier-Stokes
equations describing the isotropic motion of the compressible viscous fluid flows
in the half-space. Let

{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mid x_{N}>0\},\quad\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mid x_{N}=0\}.

The equations considered in this paper read as

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho_{t}+{\rm div}\,(\rho{\mathbb{v}})=0&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\times(0,T)$},\\
\rho({\mathbb{v}}_{t}+{\mathbb{v}}\cdot\nabla{\mathbb{v}})-\alpha\Delta{\mathbb{v}}-\beta\nabla{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{v}}+\nabla P(\rho)=0&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\times(0,T)$},\\
{\mathbb{v}}|_{\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}=0,\quad(\rho,{\mathbb{v}})=(\rho_{0},{\mathbb{v}}_{0})&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,

\alpha
and
\beta
denote respective
the viscosity coefficients and the second viscosity
coefficients satisfying the conditions

(1.2)

\alpha>0,\quad\alpha+\beta>0,

and

P(\rho)
is a smooth function defined on
(0,\infty)
satisfying
P^{\prime}(\rho)>0
,
that is, the barotropic fluid is considered.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<q<\infty
and
-1+1/q<s<1/q
. Moreover,
we assume that

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -1+\frac{N}{q}\leq&\,s<\frac{1}{q}&\quad&\text{ for $N-1<q<2N$},\\
-\frac{N}{q}<&\,s<\frac{1}{q}&\quad&\text{ for $q\geq 2N$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\eta_{0}
be a function satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

For some positive constants
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
, it holds

(1.4)

\rho_{1}<\eta_{0}(x)<\rho_{2},\quad\rho_{1}<P^{\prime}(\eta_{0}(x))<\rho_{2}\quad(x\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}).

(2)

There exist a positive constant
\rho_{*}
and a function
\tilde{\eta}_{0}\in B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
such that
\eta_{0}=\gamma_{*}+\tilde{\eta}_{0}
.

Here and in the sequel,
\rho_{*}
is a positive constant describing the mass density of the
reference domain
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
, and
B^{\mu}_{q,p}
denotes the standard Besov space.

Then,
there exist small numbers
T>0
and
\sigma>0
such that
for any initial data

\rho_{0}=\gamma_{*}+\tilde{\rho}_{0}
with
\tilde{\rho}_{0}\in B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
and
{\mathbb{v}}_{0}\in B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
,
problem (
1.1
) admits unique solutions
\rho
and

satisfying the regularity conditions:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\rho-\rho_{0}

\displaystyle\in L_{1}((0,T),B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}))\cap W^{1}_{1}((0,T),B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{v}}

\displaystyle\in L_{1}((0,T),B^{s+2}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})^{N})\cap W^{1}_{1}((0,T),B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})^{N})

.

.

If
q\geq 2N
, then we see that
-1+N/q\leq-N/q
. Thus,
-1+N/q\leq s
. And, what

,
and the main assumption is that the fluid domain is bounded.
To obtain the

so called Stokes equations in the compressible fluid flow case, in

]

they used their extended version of

[

, which was a first result concerning

which seems to be necessary to obtain the linear theory for Lamé equations cf.
[

in their argument

The final goal of our study is
to solve equations (
1.1
) if the fluid domain is a general

If the fluid domain is the whole space, a number of results have been estabilished

[

,

,

,

]
and references given therein.
Thus, our interest is in the initial boundary value problem case.
As a first step of our study, in this paper we consider equations in the half-space,
namely the model problem for the initial boundary value problem.
To explain our approach, let
A
be Stokes operator,
{\mathbb{I}}
the identity operator,
and
\Sigma_{\mu}
a parabolic sector defined by

(1.6)

\Sigma_{\mu}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\mid|\arg\lambda|\leq\pi-\mu\}.

for
\mu\in(0,\pi/2)
. Let
X=B^{s+1}_{q,1}\times B^{s}_{q,1}
be a underlying space of
the operator
A
, and we may prove that there exists a large number
\gamma>0
such that
the resolvent
(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}+A)^{-1}
exists as a surjective operator on
X

for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mu}+\gamma
.
Notice that
B^{s+1}_{q,1}
is the underlying space of the mass density and

B^{s}_{q,1}
the underlying space of the velocity field.
Thus,
A
generates a
C_{0}
analytic semigroup
\{e^{-At}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
But, to prove that this is
L_{1,{\rm loc}}
in tim, we prove that
(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}+A)^{-1}
is
divided as
(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}+A)^{-1}={\mathcal{A}}_{1}(\lambda)+{\mathcal{A}}_{2}(\lambda)
, where
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}(\lambda)

(
i=1,2
) satisfy the following estimates:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \|\lambda{\mathcal{A}}_{1}(\lambda)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,X_{\sigma})}&\leq C|\lambda|^{-\sigma/2},\\
\|\lambda\partial_{\lambda}{\mathcal{A}}_{1}(\lambda)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,X_{-\sigma})}&\leq C|\lambda|^{-(1-\sigma/2)};\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \|\lambda{\mathcal{A}}_{2}(\lambda)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,X)}&\leq C|\lambda|^{-\sigma/2},\\
\|\lambda\partial_{\lambda}{\mathcal{A}}_{2}(\lambda)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,X)}&\leq C|\lambda|^{-(1-\sigma/2)}\end{aligned}\right.

for every
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mu}+\gamma
.
Here,
{\mathcal{L}}(E,F)
denotes the set of all bounded linear operators from
E
into
F
,

X_{\pm\sigma}=B^{s+1\pm\sigma}_{q,1}\times B^{s\pm\sigma}_{q,1}
, and

\sigma
is a very small positive number such that
-1/q<s-\sigma<s<s+\sigma<1/q
.
As is known in theory of continuous analytic semigroup
[
36
]
,
e^{-At}
can be represented as

e^{-At}F=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma+\gamma}(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}+A)^{-1}F\,\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

where
\Gamma=\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}
and
\Gamma_{\pm}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid\lambda=re^{\pm i(\pi-\mu)},r\in[0,\infty)\}
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(t)
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(t)
be semigroups generated defined by

{\mathcal{E}}_{i}(t)F=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma+\gamma}{\mathcal{A}}_{i}(\lambda)F\,\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Obvisously,
e^{-At}={\mathcal{E}}_{1}(t)+{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(t)
. Moreover, using (
1.7
), we see that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(t)F\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\|F\|_{X_{\sigma}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(t)F\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\|F\|_{X_{-\sigma}}.

Thus, using real interpolation method, we have

\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(t)F\|_{X}\,dt\leq C\|F\|_{(X_{\sigma},X_{-\sigma})_{1/2,1}}.

And also, using (
1.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(t)F\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\|F\|_{X},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(t)F\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\|F\|_{X}.

Thus, using real interpolation method, we have

\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(t)F\|_{X}\,dt\leq C\|F\|_{(X,X)_{1/2,1}}=C\|F\|_{X}.

Since
(X_{\sigma},X_{-\sigma})_{1/2,1}=X
and
e^{-At}F={\mathcal{E}}_{1}(t)F+{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(t)F
, we have

\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\partial_{t}e^{-At}F\|_{X}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\|F\|_{X},

which is our
L_{1}
maximal regularity.

After reformulating equations (
1.1
) by using
Lagrange transformation to eliminate the convection term
{\mathbb{v}}\cdot\nabla\rho
,
we apply the Banach fixed point theorem to the resultant nonlinear problem based on
our
L_{1}
-
X
maximal regularity to prove the local well-posedness in Lagrange coordinates.
This is a rough idea of our proof of Theorem
1
.

1.1.
Problem Reformulation

To prove Theorem
1
, it is advantageous to transfer
equations (
1.1
) to equations in Lagrange coordinates.
In fact,
the convection term
{\mathbb{v}}\cdot\nabla\rho
in the material derivative disappears
in the equations of Lagrange coordinates.

Let
{\mathbb{u}}(x,t)
be the velocity field in Lagrange coordinates:

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
and we consider Lagrange transformation:

y=X_{\mathbb{u}}(x,t):=x+\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbb{u}}(x,\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

where equations (
1.1
) are written in Euler coordinates:
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})
. We assume that

(1.9)

\Bigl\|\int^{T}_{0}\nabla{\mathbb{u}}(\cdot,\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}\leq c_{0}

with some small constant
c_{0}>0
, and then for each
t\in(0,T)
,
the map:
X_{\mathbb{u}}(x,t)=y
is a

C^{1}
diffeomorphism from
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
onto
\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
under the assumption that

{\mathbb{u}}\in L_{1}((0,T),B^{s+2}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})^{N})

with
-1+N/q\leq s<1/q
(cf. Danchin et al
[
7
]
). Moreover,
using an argument due to Ströhmer
[
30
]
, we have
\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})={\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
,
and so as a conclusion,
\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism from

{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
.

We shall drive equations in Lagrange coordinates. Let

{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}
is the Jacobi matrix of transformation:
y=X_{\mathbb{u}}
, that is

{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}=\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}=(\frac{\partial y}{\partial x})^{-1}=\Bigl({\mathbb{I}}+\int^{t}_{0}\nabla{\mathbb{u}}(x,\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int^{t}_{0}\nabla{\mathbb{u}}(x,\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr)^{j},

which is well-defined under the smallness assumption (
1.9
),
where
{\mathbb{I}}
denotes the
N\times N
identity matrix.
We have the following
well-known formulas:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}^{\top}\nabla_{x},\quad{\rm div}\,_{y}(\,\cdot\,)={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}^{\top}\colon\nabla_{x}(\,\cdot\,)={\rm div}\,_{x}({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}(\,\cdot\,)),

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}{\rm div}\,_{y}(\,\cdot\,)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}^{\top}\nabla_{x}(({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}^{\top}-{\mathbb{I}})\colon\nabla_{x}(\,\cdot\,))+{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}^{\top}\nabla_{x}{\rm div}\,_{x}(\,\cdot\,),

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}(\,\cdot\,)

\displaystyle={\rm div}\,_{y}\nabla_{y}(\,\cdot\,)={\rm div}\,_{x}({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}^{\top}\nabla_{x}(\,\cdot\,))={\rm div}\,_{x}(({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{u}}}^{\top}-{\mathbb{I}})\nabla_{x}(\cdot))+\Delta_{x}(\,\cdot\,).

Transformation law (
1.10
)
transforms the system of equations (
1.1
) into the following system of equations:

(1.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho+\rho{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}=F(\rho,{\mathbb{u}})&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\times(0,T)$},\\
\rho\partial_{t}{\mathbb{u}}-\alpha\Delta{\mathbb{u}}-\beta\nabla{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}+\nabla P(\rho)={\mathbb{G}}(\rho,{\mathbb{u}})&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\times(0,T)$},\\
{\mathbb{u}}|_{\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}=0,\quad(\rho,{\mathbb{u}})|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},{\mathbb{u}}_{0})&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here, we have set

(1.12)

\displaystyle F(\rho,{\mathbb{u}})

\displaystyle=\rho(({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}):\nabla{\mathbb{u}})

\displaystyle{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,{\mathbb{u}})

\displaystyle=({\mathbb{I}}-({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}^{\top})^{-1})(\rho\partial_{t}{\mathbb{u}}-\alpha\Delta{\mathbb{u}})+\alpha({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}^{\top})^{-1}{\rm div}\,(({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}^{\top}-{\mathbb{I}}):\nabla{\mathbb{u}})

\displaystyle+\beta\nabla(({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{u}}^{\top}-{\mathbb{I}}):\nabla{\mathbb{u}}).

\gamma_{*}
,

\|\tilde{\eta}_{0}\|_{B^{N/q}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}
and
\|\tilde{\eta}^{\epsilon}_{0}\|_{B^{N/q}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}
,
and

is a constant depending on
\gamma_{*}
, and

\|\tilde{\eta}_{0}\|_{B^{N/q}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}
.

To show the estimate of time derivatives, we use the relations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho

\displaystyle=-\eta_{0}^{\epsilon}{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}+F,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mathbb{u}}

\displaystyle=(\eta^{\epsilon}_{0})^{-1}(\alpha\Delta{\mathbb{u}}+\beta\nabla{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}-\nabla(P(\eta_{0}^{\epsilon})\rho)+{\mathbb{G}}),

and then,

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}(\|\partial_{t}\rho(\cdot,t)\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}+\|\partial_{t}{\mathbb{u}}(\cdot,t)\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})})\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma_{*},\|\tilde{\eta}_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{q,1}})e^{\gamma T}(\|(f,{\mathbb{g}})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|(F,{\mathbb{G}})\|_{L_{1}((0,T),{\mathcal{H}})}).

Noting that

, we see that

depends on
\gamma_{*}
and

\|\tilde{\eta}_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}
. Thus, we have obtained Corollary

.

4.
A proof of Theorem

In this section, we shall prove Theorem

.
In what follows, we assume that
\theta_{0}\in B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})

and
{\mathbb{u}}_{0}\in B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})^{N}

We divide equations (

. Moreover, we write

The resultan equations read

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\theta+\eta^{\epsilon}_{0}{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}=(\eta^{\epsilon}_{0}-\theta_{0}-\theta){\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}+F(\theta+\theta_{0},{\mathbb{u}})&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\times(0,T)$},\\

To prove Theorem

, we use the Banach contraction

\displaystyle(\eta,{\mathbb{w}})=\|(\eta,\partial_{t}\eta)\|_{L_{1}((0,T),B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}))}+\|{\mathbb{w}}\|_{L_{1}((0,T),B^{s+2}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}))}+\|\partial_{t}{\mathbb{w}}\|_{L_{1}((0,T),B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}))},

,

In view of (

\displaystyle\|({\mathbb{G}}(\theta+\theta_{0},{\mathbb{u}})\|_{L_{1}((0,T),B^{s}_{q,1})}

)

\eta^{\epsilon}_{0}+\ell(\theta_{0}-\eta^{\epsilon}_{0})=\eta_{0}+(1-\ell)(\eta^{\epsilon}_{0}-\eta_{0})+\ell(\theta_{0}-\eta_{0})

(4.21)

\|\eta^{\epsilon}_{0}-\eta_{0}+\ell(\theta_{0}-\eta^{\epsilon}_{0})\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq C((1-\ell)\|\eta^{\epsilon}_{0}-\eta_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{q,1}}+\ell\|\theta_{0}-\eta_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{q,1}})

for any
\ell\in(0,1)
, In view of (
4.12
), we choose

\rho_{1}/2-\gamma_{*}<\tilde{\eta}^{\epsilon}_{0}+\ell(\theta_{0}-\eta^{\epsilon}_{0})<2\rho_{2}-\gamma_{*}

Singular sets on spaces with integral curvature bounds and diffeomorphism finiteness for manifolds
Xin Qian

Abstract
In this paper, we are concerned with noncollapsed Riemannian manifolds ( M n , g ) (M^{n},g) with integral curvature bounds, as well as their Gromov-Hausdorff limits ( M i n , g i ) → G ​ H ( X , d ) (M^{n}_{i},g_{i})\xrightarrow{GH}(X,d) . Our main result generalizes Cheeger’s Hausdorff dimension estimate for the singular set in [ 7 ] and improve it into Minkowski dimension estimate in the spirit of Cheeger-Naber [ 12 ] . We also prove a difffeomorphism finiteness theorem for manifolds in the critical case, using Cheeger-Naber’s methods in [ 13 ] .



1
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold. For each
x\in M^{n}
, we denote
h(x)
as the smallest eigenvalue for the Ricci tensor
Ric:T_{x}M\to T_{x}M
and let
Ric_{-}(x)=\max\left\{0,-h(x)\right\}
. In this paper, we consider manifolds
(M^{n},g)
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Ric_{-}|^{p}\leqslant\lambda,

(1.1)

\displaystyle vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n},\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n}\ \text{and}\ r\leqslant 1,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{q}\leqslant\Lambda,

(1.3)

where
p>\frac{n}{2}
and
1\leqslant q\leqslant\frac{n}{2}
. We will be particularly concerned with pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limts

\displaystyle(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(X,d,x)

of sequences of such manifolds. Sometimes we suppose in place of (
1.1
) that

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\lambda.

(1.4)

The geometry of manifolds with bounded integral Ricci curvature has been studied extensively. In particular, Petersen-Wei derived a relative volume comparison theorem in
[
21
]
. Afterward, under the non-collapsing condition (
1.2
), Petersen-Wei
[
22
]
showed some almost rigidity results and Tian-Zhang
[
23
]
developed a regularity theory for manifolds with bounded integral Ricci curvature. Also, the almost rigidity structures in collapsing case was discussed by Chen in
[
14
]
. Their results were directly derived from similar arguments as Cheeger-Colding
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
for manifolds with lower Ricci curvature bound.

The main purpose of this note is to generalize Cheeger’s Hausdorff dimension estimate for the singular set in
[
7
]
. Our main theorem is stated following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
p>\frac{n}{2}
,
1\leqslant q\leqslant\frac{n}{2}
and positive constants
v,\lambda
and
\Lambda
, let
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(X,d,x)
be a Gromov-Hausdorff limit of manifolds satisfying (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Then the singular set
\mathcal{S}
of
X
satisfies

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{S}\leqslant n-2q.

(1.5)

Furthermore, the Hausdorff dimension can be improved to be the Minkowski dimension if we assume (
1.4
) instead of (
1.1
).

Note that if
q>\frac{n}{2}
, then we can get a uniform lower bound on the harmonic radius (see Theorem
2.14
) and hence, the singular set
\mathcal{S}
must be empty. Indeed, the limit space
X
is a
C^{\alpha}
-Riemannian manifold, for any
0<\alpha<2-\frac{n}{q}
. Thus, it is reasonable to consider
1\leqslant q\leqslant\frac{n}{2}
.

We mention that we use Cheeger-Naber’s methods in
[
12
]
to get volume estimates on the quantitative stratification and then derive the Minkowski dimension estimate for the singular set.

In the critical case
q=\frac{n}{2}
, we know from Theorem
1.1
that the singular set
\mathcal{S}
consists of isolated points. Inspired by the bubble tree construction in
[
4
]
and
[
13
]
, we prove the following diffeomorphism finiteness theorem, which generalizes Anderson-Cheeger’s finiteness theorem in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
p>\frac{n}{2}
and positive constants
v,D,\lambda
and
\Lambda
, there exists
C=C(n,p,v,D,\lambda,\Lambda)
such that the collection of closed Riemannian n-manifolds
M^{n}
satisfying (
1.2
), (
1.4
),

\displaystyle diam(M^{n})\leqslant D,

(1.6)

and

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda,

(1.7)

contains at most C diffeomorphism types.

Note that the all scale non-collapsing condition (
1.2
) is essential and cannot be replaced by a definite scale non-collapsing condition such as
vol(B(x,1))\geqslant v
. Indeed, due to an example of Yang
[
24
]
, for any
p\geqslant\frac{n}{2}
, there exists a sequence of n-manifolds
M_{k}
such that

\left\|Rm\right\|_{p}\leqslant\lambda,\ diam\leqslant D,\ vol\geqslant v
, but
b_{2}(M_{k})\to\infty
, as
k\to\infty
. In fact,
vol(B(x_{k},r))=o(r^{n})
for some
x_{k}\in M_{k}
. In
[
24
,
25
]
, Yang also proved an orbifold type limit theorem (see Theorem
2.17
), which was analogous to Anderson’s result in
[
2
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we supply some known results about manifolds with integral curvature bounds. In Section
3
, we give an
\epsilon
-regularity theorem and prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
, we prove the finite diffeomorphism statement of Theorem
1.2
by using the methods in
[
13
]
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some notions and properties we need for manifolds with integral curvature bounds.

2.1
Relative volume comparison

Consider an
n
-manifold
M
. For each
x\in M
, let
h(x)
denote the smallest eigenvalue for the Ricci tensor
Ric:T_{x}M\to T_{x}M
and
Ric_{-}(x)=\max\left\{0,-h(x)\right\}
. Then Petersen-Wei proved the following relative volume comparison theorem in
[
21
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
p>\frac{n}{2}
, there exists
C(n,p)
such that for any
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{vol(B(x,r_{2}))}{r_{2}^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}-\left(\dfrac{vol(B(x,r_{1}))}{r_{1}^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leqslant C(n,p)\left(r_{2}^{2p-n}\int_{B(x,r_{2})}|Ric_{-}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

(2.1)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{vol(B(x,r_{1}))}{r_{1}^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\geqslant\left(\dfrac{vol(B(x,r_{2}))}{r_{2}^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\left(1-C(n,p)r_{2}\left(\fint_{B(x,r_{2})}|Ric_{-}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\right).

(2.2)

Remark 2.2
.

By inequality (
2.1
), if we assume
k(p,r_{2}):=\sup\limits_{x\in M}r_{2}^{2}\left(\fint_{B(x,r_{2})}|Ric_{-}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
is sufficiently small, then the volume doubling property can be easily derived (see
[
22
]
). Many works on integral Ricci curvature assume this smallness (see
[
14
,
16
,
22
]
for example) and as pointed out in
[
16
]
, this smallnes is necessary in order to get
L^{p}
version of Cheeger-Colding’s theory, which will be reviewed later. Also, under our assumption (
1.1
) and (
1.2
),
k(p,r)\leqslant Cr^{2-\frac{n}{p}}\left\|Ric_{-}\right\|_{p}
, which is always small when the radius
r
in consideration is small. This was used by Tian-Zhang in
[
23
]
to study almost rigidity and regularity under integral Ricci curvature bounds.

Letting
r_{1}\to 0
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

Under the asssumption (
1.1
), we have the volume bound

\displaystyle vol(B(x,r))\leqslant C(n,p)(r^{n}+\lambda r^{2p}),\ \ \forall r>0.

(2.3)

If we further assume (
1.2
), then we get the volume doubling property

\displaystyle\frac{vol(B(x,r_{2}))}{vol(B(x,r_{1}))}\leqslant C(n,p,v,R,\lambda)\dfrac{r_{2}^{n}}{r_{1}^{n}},\ \ \forall r_{1}<r_{2}\leqslant R,\ r_{1}\leqslant 1.

(2.4)

\forall\epsilon>0

Finally, recall that an

\forall\epsilon>0

2.3
Harmonic radius and diffeomorphisms

We first recall the notion of the harmonic radius.

Definition 2.8
.

For
x\in(M^{n},g)
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we define the
C^{\alpha}
harmonic radius
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)
to be the largest
r>0
such that there exists a coordinate map
\mathcal{X}=(x^{1},...,x^{n}):B(x,r)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

(1)

\Delta_{g}x^{i}=0
.

(2)

g_{ij}=g(\nabla x_{i},\nabla x_{j})
on
B(x,r)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right\|_{C^{0}}+r^{\alpha}[g_{ij}]_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant 10^{-6}.

(2.6)

Remark 2.9
.

(1) We call the above coordinate map
\mathcal{X}:B(x,r)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
a
C^{\alpha}
harmonic coordinate system. Analogously, one can define the
W^{2,p}
harmonic coordinate system and harmonic radius by requiring a scaling invariant
W^{2,p}
bound

\displaystyle\left\|g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right\|_{C^{0}}+\sum_{k=1}^{2}r^{k-\frac{n}{p}}\left\|\partial^{k}g_{ij}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leqslant 10^{-6},

(2.7)

instead of (
2.6
). We will denote
r_{h}^{2,p}(x)
to be the
W^{2,p}
harmonic radius at
x
. When
p>\frac{n}{2}
and
0<\alpha\leqslant 2-\frac{n}{p}
,

harmonic coordinates are also
C^{\alpha}
harmonic coordinates.

(2) One may replace the bound
10^{-6}
in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) by any constant
Q>0
and denote the
C^{\alpha}
and
W^{2,p}
harmonic radius by
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x,Q)
and
r_{h}^{2,p}(x,Q)
respectively. For manifolds satisfying (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), we have
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x,CQ)\geqslant r_{h}^{2,p}(x,Q)
for some constant
C=C(n,p,v,\lambda,\alpha)
when

and
0<\alpha\leqslant 2-\frac{n}{p}
. However, we will omit the constants
C,Q
and just write
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)\geqslant r_{h}^{2,p}(x)
, since this will not affect our argument.

(3) Harmonic coordinates have many good properties when it comes to regularity issues. We refer to
[
3
,
20
,
19
]
for a nice introduction. In particular, the
L^{p}
bound of the Ricci curvature (
p>\frac{n}{2}
) gives
a priori

C^{\alpha}\cap W^{2,p}
bounds on the metric tensor
g_{ij}
in harmonic coordinates for any
\alpha\in(0,2-\frac{n}{p}]
.

such that

\displaystyle V^{2\tilde{q}}\fint_{B(x,\frac{3}{4})}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2q-2\tilde{q}}+V^{2q}\sum_{j=q-\tilde{q}+1}^{q}\fint_{B(x,1)}|Rm|^{j}\geqslant\epsilon(q)\fint_{B(x,\frac{1}{2})}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2q},

(2.13)

where

.

Remark 2.20
.

Recall that if we assume

satisfies (

) and (

), then up to a rescaling on metric,

can be estimated in terms of

by gradient estimates derived in
[

]
.

Remark 2.21
.

In (

), the constant

goes to zero as

. For

, under the condition (

) and (

), the estimate (

) can be used to obtain

estimates for

. See Section 1 in Cheeger’s paper
[

]
for more details.

3.1

We first recall some basic facts. As before, we always assume

Consider a manifold

) and (

\displaystyle d_{GH}(B(x,\delta^{-1}),B((0^{n-k},y^{*}),\delta^{-1}))<\delta.

Then by Theorem

\psi(\delta|n,p,v)

\displaystyle b=(b_{1},...,b_{n-k}):B(x,3)\to\mathbb{R}^{n-k},

(3.2)

where we denote
\psi(c_{1},...,c_{s}|n_{1},...,n_{t})

\displaystyle b_{j}=z_{j}\circ F.

Following lines of Cheeger-Colding-Tian

, we may obtain some additional facts. There exists

\displaystyle u=r\circ F,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Delta u^{2}=2k,

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,3)}||\nabla u^{2}|-2u|^{2}+\sum_{j}|\langle\nabla u^{2},\nabla b_{j}\rangle|<\psi(\delta|n,p,v),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,3)}|\nabla^{2}u^{2}-2(g-\sum_{i,j}db_{i}\otimes db_{j})|^{2}<\psi(\delta|n,p,v),

\displaystyle\phi=(b,u),

(3.6)

and let

(3.7)

Similarly, there exists
D_{\delta}\subset B(0^{n-k},1)\times[0,1]

(z,r)\in D_{\delta}

\displaystyle\left\lvert vol_{k-1}(\phi^{-1}((z,r)))-r^{k-1}vol(Y)\right\rvert\leqslant\psi.

(3.8)

Before stating the
\epsilon
-regularity theorem, we first prove a lemma which is analogous to Lemma 4.14 in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M^{n}
satisfy (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Let
f:B(m,1)\to\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
be a Lipschitz function with
|\textnormal{Lip}f|\leqslant l
and
h:B(m,3)\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Denote

S_{\eta}=f\left(\{x\in B(m,1):|h(x)|\geqslant\eta\}\right).

Then for all
\mu,\eta>0
, there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,p,v,\lambda,l,\mu,\eta,q,k)
such that if
2q>k
and

\displaystyle\fint_{B(m,3)}|\nabla h|^{2q}\leqslant\mu,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\fint_{B(m,3)}|h|\leqslant\epsilon,

(3.10)

then
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(S_{\eta})\leqslant\eta
.

Proof.

Consider the collection

\mathcal{B}=\{B(x,r):x\in B(m,2),r\leqslant 1,\fint_{B(x,r)}|\nabla h|^{2q}\geqslant Ar^{-k}\},

where
A
is a constant to be determined later. By the 5-times covering lemma, there is a disjoint subcollection
\{B(m_{t},\frac{r_{t}}{5})\}
, such that
\bigcup_{t}B(m_{t},r_{t})\supseteq\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}B
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{t}\frac{vol(B(m_{t},\frac{r_{t}}{5}))}{vol(B(m,3))}\frac{A}{\mu}\left(\frac{r_{t}}{5}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{t}\frac{vol(B(m_{t},\frac{r_{t}}{5}))}{vol(B(m,3))}\frac{1}{\mu}\fint_{B(m_{t},\frac{r_{t}}{5})}|\nabla h|^{2q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\mu}\fint_{B(m,3)}|\nabla h|^{2q}\leqslant 1.

Thus, by the volume doubling property (
2.4
),

\sum_{t}r_{t}^{-k}vol(B(m_{t},r_{t}))\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda)\frac{\mu}{A}vol(B(m,3)).

By (
1.2
) and (
2.3
),

\sum_{t}r_{t}^{n-k}\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda)\frac{\mu}{A}.

We can now choose
A=A(n,p,v,\lambda,l,\mu,\eta,k)
such that

C(n,p,v,\lambda)\frac{\mu}{A}\leqslant\frac{\eta}{\omega_{n-k}l^{n-k}}.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(S_{\eta})\leqslant l^{n-k}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\left(\{x\in B(m,1):|h(x)|\geqslant\eta\}\right)
, it suffices to show that there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,p,v,\lambda,l,\mu,\eta,q,k)
, such that if
x\in B(m,1)
and
|h(x)|\geqslant\eta
, then
B(x,r)\in\mathcal{B}
for some
r\leqslant 1
.

Fix
x\in B(m,1)
and denote
h_{x,r}=\fint_{B(x,r)}h
. If
B(x,r)\notin\mathcal{B}
for any
r\leqslant 1
, then for some sufficiently small
r=r(n,p,v,\lambda,l,\mu,\eta,q,k)
,

\displaystyle|h_{x,r}-h_{x,\frac{r}{2}}|\leqslant\fint_{B(x,\frac{r}{2})}|h-h_{x,r}|

\displaystyle\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda)\fint_{B(x,r)}|h-h_{x,r}|

\displaystyle\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,q)r\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|\nabla h|^{2q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2q}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,q)Ar^{1-\frac{k}{2q}}.

and

\displaystyle|h(x)|\leqslant|h|_{x,r}+C(n,p,v,\lambda,l,\mu,\eta,q,k)r^{1-\frac{k}{2q}}<|h|_{x,r}+\frac{\eta}{2}.

(3.12)

In (
3.11
), we use the
(1,2q)
-type Neumann-Poincaré inequality, which can be obtained from the work of Dai-Wei-Zhang
[
16
]
.

Finally, by (
3.10
) and volume doubling property (
2.4
), we can choose
\epsilon
such that
|h|_{x,r}<\frac{\eta}{2}
and hence,
|h(x)|<\eta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now we can proceed to prove the
\epsilon
-regularity theorem, following the arguments of Cheeger
[
7
]
and Cheeger-Colding-Tian
[
11
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any
\eta>0
, there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,p,v,\lambda,\eta,q)
and
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\lambda,\eta,q)
, such that if
(M^{n},g)
satisfies (
1.1
), (
1.2
),

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2q}\fint_{B(x,3\epsilon)}|Rm|^{q}\leqslant\delta,

(3.13)

and for
(0^{n-k},y^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times C(Y)
,

\displaystyle d_{GH}(B(x,\delta^{-1}\epsilon),B((0^{n-k},y^{*}),\delta^{-1}\epsilon))<\delta\epsilon,

(3.14)

where

2q>\begin{cases}k,&\ k\ \text{even},\\
k-1,&\ k\ \text{odd},\end{cases}

then we have

d_{GH}(B(x,\epsilon),B(0^{n},\epsilon))\leqslant\eta\epsilon.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let
\lambda=1
. Rescale the metric by a factor
\epsilon^{-2}
, we may also assume

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Ric_{-}|^{p}\leqslant\epsilon^{2p-n},

(3.15)

\displaystyle vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n}\ \text{and}\ r\leqslant\epsilon^{-1}.

(3.16)

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,3)}|Rm|^{q}\leqslant\delta,

(3.17)

\displaystyle d_{GH}(B(x,\delta^{-1}),B((0^{n-k},y^{*}),\delta^{-1}))<\delta.

(3.18)

We only need to show the theorem for
q>1
. Consider first the case in which
q>\frac{3}{2}
and
k
is even. Let
b,u,\phi
be the maps in (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.6
). By (
2.5
), (
2.13
) and (
3.17
), it follows that

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,2)}|\nabla^{2}b_{j}|^{2q}+\sum_{i,j}|\langle\nabla b_{i},\nabla b_{j}\rangle-\delta_{ij}|^{2}\leqslant\psi(\epsilon,\delta|n,p,v,q).

(3.19)

For the function
u:B(x,3)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we can get an
L^{2q}
bound of
\nabla^{2}u^{2}
from (
2.13
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
). Then we choose
q_{1}
satisfying
2q-1<2q_{1}<2q
. For any
t\in(0,\infty)
, we have the elementary inequality

\displaystyle\int f^{2q_{1}}\leqslant t^{2q_{1}-2}\int f^{2}+t^{2q_{1}-2q}\int f^{2q}.

Then we get

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,3)}|\nabla^{2}u^{2}-2(g-\sum_{i,j}db_{i}\otimes db_{j})|^{2q_{1}}<\psi.

For the map
\phi=(b,u):B(x,3)\to\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we denote the level set by

\displaystyle U_{r}=\phi^{-1}(B(0^{n-k},1)\times[0,r]).

By (
2.5
) and the coarea formula, there exists
(z,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
1-\psi\leqslant r\leqslant 1
,
z
a regular value of
b
and
(z,r)
a regular value of
\phi
, such that (
3.7
) holds and

\displaystyle\fint_{b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r}}|Rm|^{q}\leqslant\psi,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\fint_{b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r}}|\nabla^{2}b_{j}|^{2q}\leqslant\psi.

(3.21)

From (
3.19
) and Lemma
3.1
(where we need
2q>k
), with
f=b
and
h=\langle\nabla b_{i},\nabla b_{j}\rangle-\delta_{ij}
, we can also assume

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r}}|\langle\nabla b_{i},\nabla b_{j}\rangle-\delta_{ij}|\leqslant\psi.

(3.22)

Similarly, we may assume (
3.8
) holds and

\displaystyle\fint_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}|Rm|^{q}\leqslant\psi,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\fint_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}|\nabla^{2}b_{j}|^{2q}\leqslant\psi,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}|\langle\nabla b_{i},\nabla b_{j}\rangle-\delta_{ij}|\leqslant\psi,

(3.25)

\displaystyle\fint_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}|\nabla^{2}u^{2}-2(g-\sum_{i,j}db_{i}\otimes db_{j})|^{2q_{1}}\leqslant\psi,

(3.26)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}\left(||\nabla u^{2}|-2u|+|\langle\nabla u^{2},\nabla b_{j}\rangle|\right)\leqslant\psi.

(3.27)

Applying Gram-Schmidt process to
\nabla b_{1},...,\nabla b_{n-k},\nabla u
, we get the normal vector fields
e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{n-k},N
. By (
3.21
)-(
3.27
), we obtain estimates on the second fundamental form of

and
\phi^{-1}(z,r)

\displaystyle\fint_{b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r}}|\nabla e_{j}|^{2q}\leqslant\psi,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\fint_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}|\nabla e_{j}|^{2q}\leqslant\psi,

(3.29)

\displaystyle\fint_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}|\nabla N-r^{-1}g_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}|^{2q_{1}}\leqslant\psi,

(3.30)

where
g_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}
is the induced metric on
\phi^{-1}(z,r)
.

Note that

is a
k
-dimensional submanifold with boundary

and
2q>k
,
2q_{1}>k-1
. The Gauss equation and Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula gives

\displaystyle\chi(b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r})

\displaystyle=\int_{b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r}}P_{\chi}+\int_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}TP_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\pm\psi+\frac{vol(Y)}{vol(S^{k-1})}

Since
\chi(b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r})
is an integer and

, it follows that
\chi(b^{-1}(z)\cap U_{r})=1
and

. This implies

, which completes the proof for

and
k
is even.

For
q>\frac{3}{2}
and
k

3.1
(where we need

) with

and
h=\langle\nabla b_{i},\nabla b_{j}\rangle-\delta_{ij}
. We still get estimates on
\mathrm{II}_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}
. The Gauss equation and Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula gives

\displaystyle\chi(\phi^{-1}(z,r))

\displaystyle=\int_{\phi^{-1}(z,r)}P_{\chi}

\displaystyle=2\frac{vol(Y)}{vol(S^{k-1})}\pm\psi.

Since
\dim\phi^{-1}(z,r)=k-1
is even and
\phi^{-1}(z,r)
is a boundary,
\chi(\phi^{-1}(z,r))
is even. Thus
\chi(\phi^{-1}(z,r))=2
and

. As above, we complete the proof for

.

When

and

or 3, we do not have the elliptic estimate (
2.13
) and hence, we cannot apply Lemma
3.1
. But we already have the corresponding
L^{2}
-estimates for

and

-estimates for

∎

.

The above theorem actually holds in the case

)-(

]

3.2
The Hausdorff dimension estimate

In this subsection, we will prove the first part of Theorem
1.1
, namely
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{S}\leqslant n-2q
. The proof follows Cheeger’s arguments in
[
7
]
.

We first recall some basic facts. The
\epsilon
-regular set
\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}=\left\{x\in X:\text{for all sufficiently small }r,\ d_{GH}(B(x,r),B(0^{n},r))\leqslant\epsilon r\right\}.

Let
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}
denote the interior of
\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, such that
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\subset\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}=\cap_{\epsilon}\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}
.

Let
(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(X,d,x)
satisfy (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Then up to a subsequence, we may assume
(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},|Rm_{g_{i}}|^{q}vol_{g_{i}},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pmGH}(X,d,m_{q},x)
in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff sense, where
m_{q}
is a Borel measure on
X
with
m_{q}(X)\leqslant\Lambda
(see
[
9
,
17
]
). Also, from Theorem
2.16
(a1), we know that

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x_{i},r)}|Rm|^{q}dvol_{g_{i}}\to\frac{m_{q}(B(x,r))}{\mathcal{H}^{n}(B(x,r))}.

(3.31)

Theorem 3.4
.

Given
p>\frac{n}{2}
,
1\leqslant q\leqslant\frac{n}{2}
and positive constants
v,\lambda
and
\Lambda
, let
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(X,d,x)
be a GH limit of manifolds satisfying (
1.1
)-(
1.3
).

(1)

If
q
is an integer, then
\mathcal{H}^{n-2q}(\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{n-2q})=0
.

(2)

If
q
is not an integer, then
\mathcal{H}^{n-2q}(\mathcal{S})=0
.

In particular,
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{S}\leqslant n-2q
, for all
q
.

Proof.

\forall a\geqslant 0,\ \epsilon>0
, let
D_{a,\epsilon}(q)
denote the set of points such that
y\in X\setminus\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}r^{2q}\frac{m_{q}(B(y,r))}{\mathcal{H}^{n}(B(y,r))}\leqslant a,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 0}r^{2q}\frac{m_{q}(B(y,r)\cap\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon})}{\mathcal{H}^{n}(B(y,r))}=0.

(3.33)

Put
D_{a}(q)=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}D_{a,\epsilon}(q)
and
D(q)=\bigcup_{a=1}^{\infty}D_{a}(q)
. Then by a standard covering argument and
m_{q}(X)\leqslant\Lambda
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-2q}(\mathcal{S}\setminus D(q))=0,

(3.34)

\displaystyle m_{q}\llcorner D(q)\ll\mathcal{H}^{n-2q}\llcorner D(q).

(3.35)

Denote
m_{i,s}:=|Rm_{g_{i}}|^{s}vol_{g_{i}}
, for any
1\leqslant s\leqslant q
. It is clear that
m_{i,s}
is finite on bounded sets and
m_{i,s}\ll\mathcal{H}^{n}
on
M_{i}^{n}
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{H}^{n})
be a measure space and
f
be a nonnegative function with
\int_{M}f^{q}\leqslant\Lambda
. Then for any
q_{1}<q
and
U\subset M
,

\displaystyle\int_{U}f^{q_{1}}\leqslant(\mathcal{H}^{n}(U))^{1-\frac{q_{1}}{q}}\left(\int_{U}f^{q}\right)^{\frac{q_{1}}{q}}\leqslant\Lambda^{\frac{q_{1}}{q}}(\mathcal{H}^{n}(U))^{1-\frac{q_{1}}{q}}.

(3.36)

Therefore, the
L^{q_{1}}
-norm of
f
cannot concentrate on a set
U
of small measure.

By passing to a subsequence in
(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},m_{i,q},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pmGH}(X,d,m_{q},x)
, we may assume
(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},m_{i,q_{1}},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pmGH}(X,d,m_{q_{1}},x)
. This implies that there exists a metric space
(W,d_{W})
and isometric embeddings
\varphi_{i}:M_{i}^{n}\to W
and
\varphi:X\to W
, such that
\varphi_{i}(x_{i})\to\varphi(x)
in
W
and
(\varphi_{i})_{\#}m_{i,q_{1}}\to(\varphi)_{\#}m_{q_{1}}
in the weak* topology on
C_{bs}(W)^{*}
(see
[
18
]
). It is obvious that
(\varphi_{i})_{\#}m_{i,q_{1}}\ll\mathcal{H}^{n}
on
W
.

Then by (
3.36
), we know that the limit measure
(\varphi)_{\#}m_{q_{1}}\ll\mathcal{H}^{n}
on
W
and hence,
m_{q_{1}}\ll\mathcal{H}^{n}
on
X
. It follows that

\displaystyle m_{q_{1}}(B(y,r)\cap\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon})=m_{q_{1}}(B(y,r)),\ \forall y\in X,

\displaystyle D_{0}(q_{1})=D(q_{1})=\{y\in\mathcal{S}:\lim\limits_{r\to 0}r^{2q_{1}}\frac{m_{q_{1}}(B(y,r))}{\mathcal{H}^{n}(B(y,r))}=0\}.

Notice that for any open subset
U_{i}\subset M^{n}_{i}
, we have

r^{2q_{1}}\fint_{U_{i}}|Rm|^{q_{1}}\leqslant\left(r^{2q}\fint_{U_{i}}|Rm|^{q}\right)^{\frac{q_{1}}{q}}.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle D(q)\subset D_{0}(q_{1}),

(3.37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-2q}(\mathcal{S}\setminus D_{0}(q_{1}))=0.

(3.38)

Now we go back and prove the theorem. If
q
is an integer, then we can choose
q_{1}
such that
2q_{1}\in(2q-1,2q)
and by Theorem
3.2
,
\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{n-2q}\subset\mathcal{S}\setminus D_{0}(q_{1})
. Hence, we complete the proof for (1).

If
q
is not an integer, then we can choose
q_{1}
such that
2q_{1}\in(2[q],2q)
and choose
k=2[q]+1
in Theorem
3.2
. Thus,
\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{n-k-1}\subset\mathcal{S}\setminus D_{0}(q_{1})
. Note that
k+1>2q
and hence,
\mathcal{H}^{n-2q}(\mathcal{S}^{n-k-1})=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary can be derived directly from Theorem
3.4
and the results in
[
9
]
.

Corollary 3.5
.

let
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},x_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(X,d,x)
be a GH limit of manifolds satisfying (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with
q=\frac{n}{2}
. If
n
is odd, then the singular set
\mathcal{S}
is empty and
(X,d)
is bi-Hölder equivalent to a Riemannian manifold.

3.3
The Minkowski dimension estimate

In this subsection, we will prove the remaining part of Theorem
1.1
. The proof relies on the Minkowski type estimates on the quantitative stratification of the singular set, introduced by Cheeger-Naber in
[
12
]
.

Definition 3.6
.

A metric space
Y
is called
k
-symmetric if
Y
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times C(Z)
for some compact metric space
Z
.

Definition 3.7
.

Given a metric space
Y
with
y\in Y
,
r>0
and
\epsilon>0
, we say that
y
is
(k,\epsilon,r)
-symmetric if there is a
k
-symmetric metric space
X
such that
d_{GH}(B(y,r),B(x,r))<\epsilon r
, where
x\in X
is a vertex.

Definition 3.8
.

For any
r\in(0,1)
and
\epsilon>0
, we define the quantitative
k
-stratum of a metric space
X
by

\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon,r}(X):=\{x\in X:\text{for no }r\leqslant s\leqslant 1\ \text{is }x\ \text{a }(k+1,\epsilon,s)\text{-symmetric point}\}.

The first main result in
[
12
]
is to show a Minkowski type estimate on
\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon,r}
for noncollapsed manifolds with
Ric\geqslant-(n-1)
. The proof requires the cone-splitting principle and the monotonicity of the volume ratio
\frac{vol(B(x,r))}{vol_{-1}(B(r))}
. However, under our assumption (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), the formula (
2.2
) tells us that the volume ratio
\frac{vol(B(x,r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}
is almost monotone whenever the radius
r
is sufficiently small. Then by a slight modification of Cheeger-Naber’s arguments in
[
12
]
, one may generalize their result into the following form.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
satisfy (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with
x\in M^{n}
. Then there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,p,v,\lambda)>0
, such that for any
\epsilon,\eta>0
and
r\leqslant r_{0}
,

vol\left(T_{r}(\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon,r}(M^{n})\cap B(x,1))\right)\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,\epsilon,\eta)r^{n-k-\eta}.

The Minkowski estimates require a more rigid statement of
\epsilon
-regularity theorem. Namely, assuming (
1.4
) in place of (
1.1
), we can combine Theorem
2.10
and Theorem
3.2
together to obtain the following.

Theorem 3.10
.

There exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,p,v,\lambda,q)
and
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\lambda,q)
, such that if
(M^{n},g)
satisfies (
1.2
), (
1.4
),

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2q}\fint_{B(x,3\epsilon)}|Rm|^{q}\leqslant\delta,

(3.39)

and for
(0^{n-k},y^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times C(Y)
,

\displaystyle d_{GH}(B(x,\delta^{-1}\epsilon),B((0^{n-k},y^{*}),\delta^{-1}\epsilon))<\delta\epsilon,

where

2q>\begin{cases}k,&\ k\ \text{even},\\
k-1,&\ k\ \text{odd},\end{cases}

then for any
\alpha\in(0,2-\frac{n}{p})
, we have

r^{\alpha}_{h}(x)>\frac{1}{2}\epsilon.

From the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we may fix
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\lambda,q)<1
with the property that there exists
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,p,v,\lambda,q)
such that the above theorem holds for every
0<\epsilon\leqslant\epsilon_{0}
. Without loss of generality, we may also assume
\delta^{-1}\epsilon_{0}<r_{0}
, where
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,p,v,\lambda)
is the constant in Theorem
3.9
.

Now we combine Theorem
3.9
and Theorem
3.10
to prove the following theorem, which implies the Minkowski dimension estimate of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
satisfy (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) with
x\in M^{n}
. Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,p,v,\lambda,q)>0
, such that for any
\eta>0
,
\alpha\in(0,2-\frac{n}{p})
and
\epsilon\leqslant\epsilon_{0}
,

\displaystyle vol\left(T_{\epsilon}(\{y\in B(x,1):r^{\alpha}_{h}(y)\leqslant\frac{\epsilon}{2}\})\right)\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,\Lambda,q,\eta)\epsilon^{2q-\eta}.

(3.40)

Proof.

As noted above, let us fix
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\lambda,q)<1
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,p,v,\lambda,q)
. Consider first the case in which
q
is an integer.

For arbitrary
\epsilon\leqslant\epsilon_{0}
, the above
\epsilon
-regularity theorem (Theorem
3.10
) gives the following inclusion

\left\{y\in B(x,1):r^{\alpha}_{h}(y)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\right\}\subseteq\left(\mathcal{S}^{n-2q}_{\delta^{2},\delta^{-1}\epsilon}\cup\{y:\epsilon^{2q}\fint_{B(y,3\epsilon)}|Rm|^{q}>\delta\}\right)\cap B(x,1).

By Theorem
3.9
, we know that

\displaystyle vol\left(T_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}^{n-2q}_{\delta^{2},\delta^{-1}\epsilon}\cap B(x,1))\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant vol\left(T_{\delta^{-1}\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}^{n-2q}_{\delta^{2},\delta^{-1}\epsilon}\cap B(x,1))\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,\delta,\eta)\epsilon^{2q-\eta}

(3.41)

\displaystyle\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,q,\eta)\epsilon^{2q-\eta}.

Then we denote
A=\{y\in B(x,1):\epsilon^{2q}\fint_{B(y,3\epsilon)}|Rm|^{q}>\delta\}
.

Now, our goal is to get volume estimate of the
\epsilon
-tubular neighborhood
T_{\epsilon}(A)
. Consider the collection

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\left\{B(y,3\epsilon):y\in A\right\}.

By the 5-times covering lemma, there exists a pairwise disjoint subcollection
\{B(y_{i},3\epsilon)\}
such that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}B\subset\bigcup_{i}B(y_{i},15\epsilon).

(3.42)

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i}vol(B(y_{i},3\epsilon))<\frac{\epsilon^{2q}}{\delta}\sum_{i}\int_{B(y_{i},3\epsilon)}|Rm|^{q}\leqslant C(\delta,\Lambda)\epsilon^{2q},

(3.43)

and by volume doubling property (
2.4
),

\displaystyle vol(T_{\epsilon}(A))\leqslant\sum_{i}vol(B(y_{i},15\epsilon))\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,\Lambda,q)\epsilon^{2q}.

(3.44)

Combining (
3.41
) and (
3.44
), we obtain the desired estimate (
3.40
).

Now, we consider the case in which
q
is not an integer. Similarly, by Theorem
3.10
, we have the inclusion

\left\{y\in B(x,1):r^{\alpha}_{h}(y)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\right\}\subseteq\left(\mathcal{S}^{n-2[q]-2}_{\delta^{2},\delta^{-1}\epsilon}\cup\{y:\epsilon^{2q}\fint_{B(y,3\epsilon)}|Rm|^{q}>\delta\}\right)\cap B(x,1).

Following the same argument as above, we can obtain a stronger estimate:

\displaystyle vol\left(T_{\epsilon}(\{y\in B(x,1):r^{\alpha}_{h}(y)\leqslant\frac{\epsilon}{2}\})\right)\leqslant C(n,p,v,\lambda,\Lambda,q)\epsilon^{2q}.

Thus, we complete the proof.
∎

The Minkowski dimension estimate of Theorem
1.1
can be viewed as an easy corollary of Theorem
3.11
(see similar arguments in Section 7 of
[
13
]
). Thus, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4
Diffeomorphism finiteness

In this section, we will prove our diffeomorphism finiteness theorem (Theorem
1.2
). The idea is following Cheeger-Naber’s bubble tree construction in Section 8 of
[
13
]
.

4.1
Neck regions

In this subsection, we prove some technical results to handle the neck regions in the bubble tree structure.

From formula (
2.2
), under the assumption (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), the volume ratio
\frac{vol(B(x,r))}{r^{n}}
will be almost monotone whenever the radius
r
is sufficiently small. For convenience in writing, we use the notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{r}(x):=-\ln\left(\frac{vol(B(x,r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\right)

(4.1)

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(q)
denote the geodesic annulus centered at some
q\in M^{n}
, i.e.,

A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(q):=B(q,r_{2})\setminus\overline{B(q,r_{1})}.

We begin with the following annulus estimate. As before, we always assume
n\geqslant 4,\ p>\frac{n}{2}
and
\alpha\in(0,2-\frac{n}{p})
. In fact, one may set
\alpha
as a fixed value, such as
1-\frac{n}{2p}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exists
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon)
such that if a manifold
(M^{n},g)
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\delta^{2p-n},

(4.3)

\displaystyle vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n}\ \text{and}\ r\leqslant\delta^{-1},

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}_{4}(q)-\mathcal{V}_{2}(q)|\leqslant\delta,

(4.5)

then there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,v)
and a discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq O(n)
with
|\Gamma|<C(n,v)
such that the following hold:

(1)

for any
x\in A_{\epsilon,2}(q)
,
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)>r_{0}\epsilon
;

(2)

there exists a subset
A_{\epsilon,2}(q)\subseteq U\subseteq A_{\frac{1}{2}\epsilon,2+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon}(q)
and a diffeomorphism
\phi:A_{\epsilon,2}(0)\to U
where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
, such that

\left\|\phi^{*}g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right\|_{C^{0}}+[\phi^{*}g_{ij}]_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant\epsilon.

Proof.

Argue by contradiction. We assume that there exists a sequence of manifolds
(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},q_{i})
with
\int_{M_{i}^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda,\ \int_{M_{i}^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\delta_{i}^{2p-n}\to 0,\ vol(B(x_{i},r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x_{i}\in M_{i}^{n},r\leqslant\delta_{i}^{-1}
and
|\mathcal{V}_{4}(q_{i})-\mathcal{V}_{2}(q_{i})|\leqslant\delta_{i}\to 0
, but the conclusions fail. After passing to a subsequence, we get

(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},q_{i})\xrightarrow{GH}(X,d,q).

By Theorem
2.6
, we know that
B(q,4)=B(z^{*},4)
where
z^{*}
is the cone vertex of a metric cone
C(Z)
with
diam(Z)\leqslant\pi
. Choose a sequence
r_{i}\to 0
and let
\tilde{g}_{i}=r_{i}^{-2}g_{i}
. Then passing to a subsequence, we may assume

(M^{n}_{i},\tilde{g}_{i},q_{i})\xrightarrow{GH}(C(Z),d_{C},z^{*}).

Using Theorem
1.1
and the scaling invariance of metric cone, we know that
z^{*}
is the only possible singularity of
C(Z)
(otherwise, there is a ray full of singularities) and by Theorem
2.16
(b2),
\tilde{g}_{i}
converges to
g_{C}
in the
C^{\alpha}
and weak
W^{2,p}
topology away from the vertex
z^{*}
. Considering the equation for Ricci curvature in harmonic coordinates and using
\left\|Ric_{\tilde{g}_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\to 0
, it follows that
(C(Z),g_{C})
is smooth and Ricci flat away from the vertex
z^{*}
. Also,
\tilde{g}_{i}
converges to
g_{C}
in the strong
W^{2,p}
topology away from
z^{*}
. Since
\int_{M_{i}^{n}}|Rm_{\tilde{g}_{i}}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{C(Z)\setminus\{z^{*}\}}|Rm_{g_{C}}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda.

(4.6)

However, by scaling invariance of the metric cone
C(Z)
and the curvature energy
\left\|Rm\right\|_{L^{\frac{n}{2}}}
, it follows that for any
r>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{A_{r,2r}(z^{*})}|Rm_{g_{C}}|^{\frac{n}{2}}=\int_{A_{2r,4r}(z^{*})}|Rm_{g_{C}}|^{\frac{n}{2}}.

(4.7)

Combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
),
\int_{C(Z)\setminus\{z^{*}\}}|Rm_{g_{C}}|^{\frac{n}{2}}=0
and
C(Z)
is a flat cone. Thus
Z=S^{n-1}/\Gamma
has constant sectional curvature +1. In addition, we know from Theorem
2.16
(a1) that
|\Gamma|<C(n,v)
.

Now we come back to the original converging sequence
(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},q_{i})\xrightarrow{GH}(X,d,q)
. We have already known that
B(q,4)=B(0,4)
where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
. It follows that there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,v)
such that for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
with
|z|=1
, we have
B(z,2r_{0})=B(0^{n},2r_{0})
where
0^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, for sufficiently large
i
, we obtain from Theorem
2.10
that
r^{\alpha}_{h}(x)\geqslant r^{2,p}_{h}(x)>r_{0}\epsilon
, for all
x\in A_{\epsilon,2}(q_{i})
and
\alpha\in(0,2-\frac{n}{p})
. Thus, (1) is true and (2) must fail to hold.

However, by (1) and Theorem
2.11
, we have that for sufficiently large
i
, there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi_{i}:A_{\epsilon,2}(0)\to M_{i}^{n}
where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
, such that the pullback metric
\phi_{i}^{*}g_{i}
is
\epsilon
-close to
\delta_{ij}
in
C^{\alpha}
norm. This implies (2); a contradiction.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that since we have taken scaling in (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we do not need to consider small radius
r
as in Theorem
2.6
and Theorem
2.10
.

Motivated by Proposition
4.1
, we introduce the following definition and lemma similar to those in
[
13
]
before stating our key neck theorem.

Definition 4.3
.

Consider the scales
r_{k}=2^{-k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
x\in M^{n}
, we define

T_{k}^{\delta}(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }|\mathcal{V}_{4r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{2r_{k}}(x)|>\delta,\\
0&\text{if }|\mathcal{V}_{4r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{2r_{k}}(x)|\leqslant\delta.\end{cases}

We also denote by
|T^{\delta}|(x)=\sum_{k}T_{k}^{\delta}(x)
the number of bad scales at
x
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists
\delta=\delta(n,p,v)
satisfying the following property. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a manifold with
\int_{M^{n}}|Ric_{-}|^{p}\leqslant\delta^{2p-n}
and
vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n}\ \text{and}\ r\leqslant\delta^{-1}
. Then for any
\delta^{\prime}>0
and
x\in M^{n}
, there exists at most
N=N(n,p,v,\delta^{\prime})
scales
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T_{k}^{\delta^{\prime}}(x)=1
.

Proof.

For any
x\in M^{n}
,
\mathcal{V}_{1}(x)\leqslant-\ln(\frac{v}{\omega_{n}})=C(n,v)
. And the almost monotonicity formula (
2.2
) gives:
\forall r<R\leqslant 1
,

\mathcal{V}_{R}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{r}(x)\geqslant 2p\ln(1-C(n,p,v)(\delta R)^{1-\frac{n}{2p}})\geqslant 2p\ln(1-\frac{1}{2}R^{1-\frac{n}{2p}}).

Here, we have chosen
\delta=(2C(n,p,v))^{\frac{2p}{n-2p}}
. Then

\displaystyle C(n,v)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathcal{V}_{1}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k+1}}(x))

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{k}|\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k+1}}(x)|+4p\sum_{k}\ln(1-\frac{1}{2}r_{k}^{1-\frac{n}{2p}})

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{k}|\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k+1}}(x)|-8p\sum_{k}2^{-k(1-\frac{n}{2p})}

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{k}|\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k+1}}(x)|-C(n,p).

Then
\sum|\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k+1}}(x)|\leqslant C(n,p,v)
. In particular, there are at most
N=C(n,p,v)(\delta^{\prime})^{-1}
scales
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(x)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k+1}}(x)|>\delta^{\prime}
.
∎

Note that for any good scale
k\in\mathbb{N}
at
q
, i.e.,
T_{k}^{\delta}(q)=0
, we can denote by
\Gamma_{k}\subseteq O(n)
the subgroup arising from Proposition
4.1
. The following is our key neck theorem, which is similar to Proposition
4.1
while
\delta
is independent of the ratio
r_{1}/r_{2}
of the annulus
A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(q)
. Generally, we are aimed to construct a bubble tree decomposition to prove the diffeomorphism finiteness and in the decomposition, we will encounter two different types of regions: body regions and neck regions. The neck regions will be diffeomorphic to cylinders
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma
and connect different body regions.

Theorem 4.5

(Neck Theorem)
.

For any
0<\epsilon\leqslant\epsilon(n,v)
, there exists
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon)
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g)
satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda,

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\delta^{2p-n},

\displaystyle vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n}\ \text{and}\ r\leqslant\delta^{-1},

and assume
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies
T_{k_{1}}^{\delta}(q)=0
with
\Gamma_{k_{1}}
the corresponding group. If
k_{2}\geqslant k_{1}
satisfies
\mathcal{V}_{r_{k_{2}}}(q)\geqslant\ln|\Gamma_{k_{1}}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
, then there exists a subset
A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(q)\subseteq U\subseteq A_{\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon)r_{k_{2}},2(1+\epsilon)r_{k_{1}}}(q)
and a diffeomorphism
\phi:A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0)\to U
, where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma_{k_{1}}
, such that

\left\|\phi^{*}g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right\|_{C^{0}(A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0))}+r_{k_{2}}^{\alpha}[\phi^{*}g_{ij}]_{C^{\alpha}(A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0))}\leqslant\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon_{1}>0
be arbitrary and
\delta(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon_{1})
be the corresponding number from Proposition
4.1
. This number
\delta
will be fixed later to be the number we need. If
T_{k_{1}}^{\delta}(q)=0
, then there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi_{k_{1}}:A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{1}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0)\to U_{k_{1}}
, where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma_{k_{1}}
and
A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{1}},2r_{k_{1}}}(q)\subseteq U_{k_{1}}\subseteq A_{\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})r_{k_{1}},2(1+\epsilon_{1})r_{k_{1}}}(q)
, such that

\left\|\phi_{k_{1}}^{*}g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right\|_{C^{0}}+r_{k_{1}}^{\alpha}[\phi_{k_{1}}^{*}g_{ij}]_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant\epsilon_{1}.

Moreover, let
\epsilon>0
be fixed and
4\delta_{1}(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon)
be the corresponding number from Proposition
4.1
. Then we can choose
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta_{1})=\epsilon_{1}(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon)
with
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon_{1})=\delta(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon)
sufficiently small such that
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}\leqslant\delta_{1}
and

\mathcal{V}_{2r_{k_{1}}}(q)<\ln|\Gamma_{k_{1}}|+\delta_{1}.

Thus, if
k_{2}\geqslant k_{1}
satisfies
\mathcal{V}_{r_{k_{2}}}(q)\geqslant\ln|\Gamma_{k_{1}}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}\geqslant\ln|\Gamma_{k_{1}}|-\delta_{1}
, then by the almost monotonicity formula (
2.2
), we have
T_{k}^{4\delta_{1}}(q)=0
for all scales
k\in[k_{1},k_{2}]\cap\mathbb{N}
.

By Proposition
4.1
, for each
k\in[k_{1},k_{2}]
, there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi_{k}:A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k},2r_{k}}(0)\to U_{k}
, where

and
A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k},2r_{k}}(q)\subseteq U_{k}\subseteq A_{\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon)r_{k},2(1+\epsilon)r_{k}}(q)
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{k}^{*}g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right\|_{C^{0}(A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k},2r_{k}}(0))}+r_{k}^{\alpha}[\phi_{k}^{*}g_{ij}]_{C^{\alpha}(A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k},2r_{k}}(0))}\leqslant\epsilon.

(4.8)

In particular, this implies that
\Gamma_{k}=\Gamma
is independent of
k
.

Then we consider the inverse maps
\phi_{k}^{-1}:U_{k}\to A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k},2r_{k}}(0)
. Observe that after composing
\phi_{k}
with a rotation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
, we can assume that

\displaystyle|\phi_{k}^{-1}(x)-\phi_{l}^{-1}(x)|\leqslant\epsilon r_{k},\ \forall x\in U_{k}\cap U_{l}.

(4.9)

Now we can choose a sufficiently small
\epsilon(n,v)
, such that for any
0<\epsilon\leqslant\epsilon(n,v)
, if

, then

where

.

For each
k
, let
\eta_{k}:U_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth cutoff function with

\eta_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in A_{\frac{5}{8}r_{k},\frac{15}{8}r_{k}}(q),\\
0&\text{if }x\notin A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k},2r_{k}}(q)\end{cases}

and
|\nabla\eta_{k}|<10r_{k}^{-1}
. If we let
\eta(x)=\sum_{k}\eta_{k}(x)
, then

\psi_{k}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{\eta}:U_{k}\to\mathbb{R}

satisfies
\sum_{k}\psi_{k}(x)=1
and
|\nabla\psi_{k}|<200r_{k}^{-1}
. Now we define the map

\phi^{-1}:U=\bigcup_{k}U_{k}\to A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0)

by
\phi^{-1}(x)=\sum_{k}\psi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}^{-1}(x)
. Notice that all the
\phi_{k}^{-1}(x)
live in a Euclidean ball, so the convex combination is well defined. On each domain
U_{k}
,
\phi^{-1}
and
\phi_{k}^{-1}
are
C^{1}
-close by (
4.8
) and (
4.9
). Thus,
\phi^{-1}:U\to A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0)
is a diffeomorphism. Again by (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we can derive the desired estimate:

\left\|\phi^{*}g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right\|_{C^{0}(A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0))}+r_{k_{2}}^{\alpha}[\phi^{*}g_{ij}]_{C^{\alpha}(A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{k_{2}},2r_{k_{1}}}(0))}\leqslant 400\epsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

To end this section, we prove the following lemma which will be used to control the number of the neck regions and body regions.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any sufficiently small
\delta\leqslant\delta(n,p,v)
, there exists a scale
k=k(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
with the following property. Let
M^{n}
be a manifold with
\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda
,
\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\delta^{2p-n}
,
vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n},\ r\leqslant\delta^{-1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{1}(q)<\ln N-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
for some integer
N\geqslant 2
. Then

\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(q)<\ln(N-1)+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}.

Proof.

Let
\delta\leqslant\delta(n,p,v)
satisfy Lemma
4.4
. By Proposition
4.1
, we can choose
\delta^{\prime}(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
such that if
\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda
,
\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant(\delta^{\prime})^{2p-n}
,
vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n},r\leqslant(\delta^{\prime})^{-1}
and
T_{1}^{\delta^{\prime}}(q)=0
, then

|\mathcal{V}_{1}(q)-\ln|\Gamma||<\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}.

By rescaling, we see that if
\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda
,
\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\delta^{2p-n}
,
vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n},r\leqslant\delta^{-1}
and
|\mathcal{V}_{2r_{k}}(q)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(q)|\leqslant\delta^{\prime}
with
k\geqslant k(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
, then

|\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(q)-\ln|\Gamma||<\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}.

Here, the scale
k\geqslant k(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
satisfies
\frac{\delta}{\delta^{\prime}}r_{k}\leqslant 1
.

Now, we can apply Lemma
4.4
to see that the scale
k\geqslant k(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
such that
|\mathcal{V}_{2r_{k}}(q)-\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(q)|\leqslant\delta^{\prime}
does exist. Hence, in the context of the lemma, we get

|\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(q)-\ln|\Gamma||<\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}.

However, if
\mathcal{V}_{1}(q)<\ln N-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
, then by the almost monotonicity formula (
2.2
),
\mathcal{V}_{r_{k}}(q)<\ln N-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}+C(n,p,v)\delta^{1-\frac{n}{2p}}<\ln N-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
.

Thus,
\ln|\Gamma|<\ln N
and
|\Gamma|\leqslant N-1
, which completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Bubble tree structure

In this subsection, we will construct the bubble tree structure and prove our diffeomorphism finiteness Theorem
1.2
.

We have built the neck regions of our bubble tree in Theorem
4.5
. What is left is to construct the various body regions. The body regions are designed to have uniform lower bound on harmonic radius. By the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we see that if the corresponding group
\Gamma
is a trivial group, then we do not need to consider the annulus (neck)
A_{\epsilon r,2r}(q)
. Indeed, the ball
B(q,2r)
is GH-close to
B(0^{n},2r)
and serves as a body region. So, we will first assume
|\Gamma|\geqslant 2
and try to apply Lemma
4.6
. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any sufficiently small
\delta\leqslant\delta(n,p,v)
, there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
and
N=N(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
with the following property. Let
M^{n}
be a manifold with
\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda
,
\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\delta^{2p-n}
and
vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n}\ \text{and}\ r\leqslant\delta^{-1}
. Then there exist points
\{x_{j}\}_{1}^{m}\subset B(q,1)
with
m\leqslant N
, and scales
\{k_{j}\}_{1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{N}
with
r_{j}:=r_{k_{j}}>r_{0}
such that

(1)

T_{k_{j}}^{\delta}(x_{j})=0
;

(2)

if
s_{j}
denotes the largest integer such that
\mathcal{V}_{r_{s_{j}}}(x_{j})\geqslant\ln|\Gamma_{j}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
, then for any
x\in B(x_{j},2r_{s_{j}})
, we have
\mathcal{V}_{\frac{1}{2}r_{s_{j}}}(x)<\ln|\Gamma_{j}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
;

(3)

if
x\in B(q,1)\setminus\bigcup_{j}B(x_{j},r_{j})
, then
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)>r_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta\leqslant\delta(n,p,v)
satisfy Lemma
4.4
with
\eta=\eta(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
to be chosen later. For any
x\in M^{n}
, there exists a scale
k_{x}\in[k(n,p,v,\eta),4k(n,p,v,\eta)]
so that
T_{k_{x}}^{\eta}(x)=0
. Pick a covering
\{B(x,r_{k_{x}})\}
of
B(q,1)
and choose a finite subcovering
\{B(y_{j},r_{j})\}_{1}^{m}
, where
r_{j}=r_{k_{j}}
,
k_{j}=k_{y_{j}}
and all the balls
\{B(y_{j},\frac{1}{10}r_{j})\}_{1}^{m}
are disjoint. Thus,
m\leqslant N(n,p,v,\eta)
by volume doubling property (
2.4
).

By Proposition
4.1
and the proof of Lemma
4.6
, for any sufficiently small
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,p,v,\delta)
, we can choose
\eta(n,p,v,\Lambda,\epsilon,\delta)
so that for each
x\in A_{\epsilon r_{j},2r_{j}}(y_{j})
, we have
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)\geqslant r(n,v)\epsilon r_{j}>r_{0}(n,p,v,\epsilon,\eta)
and
|\mathcal{V}_{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\epsilon}r_{j}}(y_{j})-\ln|\Gamma_{j}||<\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
. Also,
\epsilon
and
\eta
can be chosen to satisfy
T_{k_{j}}^{\delta}(x)=0
for all
x\in B(y_{j},4\sqrt{\epsilon}r_{j})
.

For each
x\in B(y_{j},4\sqrt{\epsilon}r_{j})
, let
s_{j}(x)
denote the largest integer such that
\mathcal{V}_{r_{s_{j}(x)}}(x)\geqslant\ln|\Gamma_{j}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
. Here, we need
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}\ll\ln 2
. Consider

W_{j}:=\{x\in B(y_{j},4\sqrt{\epsilon}r_{j}):B(x,4r_{s_{j}(x)})\subset B(y_{j},4\sqrt{\epsilon}r_{j})\}.

Note that
r_{s_{j}(y_{j})}\leqslant\sqrt{\epsilon}r_{j}
, so
y_{j}\in W_{j}
.

Now let
s_{j}:=\max\{s_{j}(x):x\in W_{j}\}
with
x_{j}
the corresponding point, i.e.,
s_{j}(x_{j})=s_{j}
. Since
B(x_{j},4r_{s_{j}})\subset B(y_{j},4\sqrt{\epsilon}r_{j})
, we know that for any
x\in B(x_{j},2r_{s_{j}})
,
\mathcal{V}_{r_{s_{j}+1}}(x)=\mathcal{V}_{\frac{1}{2}r_{s_{j}}}(x)<\ln|\Gamma_{j}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
.

Consider the collection of balls
\{B(x_{j},r_{j})\}_{1}^{m}
. Conditions (1) and (2) are satisfied by construction. If
x\in B(q,1)\setminus\bigcup_{j}B(x_{j},r_{j})
, then since
B(x_{j},\frac{3}{2}r_{j})\supseteq B(y_{j},r_{j})
and
\{B(y_{j},r_{j})\}_{1}^{m}
cover
B(q,1)
, we have
x\in A_{r_{j},\frac{3}{2}r_{j}}(x_{j})\subseteq A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{j},2r_{j}}(y_{j})
, which implies
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)\geqslant r_{0}(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)
. This completes the proof.
∎

By Lemma
4.7
and Theorem
2.12
, the open set
B(q,1)\setminus\overline{\bigcup_{j}B(x_{j},r_{j})}
, which will be referred as a body region, has finite diffeomorphism types.

We are now in the position to prove the following bubble tree decomposition result, which is stronger than Theorem
1.2
. The proof is the same as the one in Cheeger-Naber’s paper
[
13
]
, since we have established all the tools they used in constructing the bubble tree.

Theorem 4.8
.

Given
p>\frac{n}{2}
and positive constants
v,D,\lambda
and
\Lambda
, if
(M^{n},g)
is a closed Riemannian manifold satisfying (
1.2
), (
1.4
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), then there exists a decomposition of
M^{n}

\displaystyle M^{n}=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{0}\cup\bigcup_{i_{1}=1}^{N_{1}}\mathcal{N}_{i_{1}}^{1}\cup\bigcup_{i_{1}=1}^{N_{1}}\mathcal{B}_{i_{1}}^{1}\cup\cdots\cup\bigcup_{i_{m}=1}^{N_{m}}\mathcal{N}_{i_{m}}^{m}\cup\bigcup_{i_{m}=1}^{N_{m}}\mathcal{B}_{i_{m}}^{m}

(4.10)

into open sets with the following properties:

(1)

if
x\in\mathcal{B}_{i}^{l}
, then
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)\geqslant r_{0}(n,p,v,\lambda,\Lambda,D)diam(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{l})
;

(2)

each neck
\mathcal{N}_{i}^{l}
is diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma_{i}^{l}
, for some
\Gamma_{i}^{l}\subset O(n)
with
|\Gamma_{i}^{l}|\leqslant C(n,v)
;

(3)

\mathcal{N}_{i}^{l}\cap\mathcal{B}_{i}^{l}
is diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma_{i}^{l}
;

(4)

\mathcal{B}_{j}^{l-1}\cap\mathcal{N}_{i}^{l}
are either empty or diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma_{i}^{l}
;

(5)

N_{l}\leqslant N(n,p,v,\lambda,\Lambda,D)
and
m\leqslant m(n,v)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
\lambda=1
. Fix
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,v)
from Theorem
4.5
with
\delta=\delta(n,p,v,\Lambda,D,\epsilon)=\delta(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)
sufficiently small to satisfy Proposition
4.1
, Theorem
4.5
, Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7
. Since we want to apply Lemma
4.7
on the whole manifold
M^{n}
instead of
B(q,1)
, we need
\delta
to be dependent on
D
. After rescaling, we may suppose that
(M^{n},g)
is a closed manifold with
diam(M^{n})\leqslant D\delta^{-1}
,
\int_{M^{n}}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}\leqslant\Lambda
,
\int_{M^{n}}|Ric|^{p}\leqslant\delta^{2p-n}
,
vol(B(x,r))\geqslant vr^{n}\ \text{for all}\ x\in M^{n}
and
r\leqslant\delta^{-1}
.

To begin with, we apply Lemma
4.7
to produce a collection of balls
\{B(x_{i}^{1},r_{i}^{1})\}_{1}^{N_{1}}
such that
r_{i}^{1}=r_{k_{i}^{1}}>r_{0}(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)D
,
N_{1}\leqslant N(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)
,
T_{k_{i}^{1}}^{\delta}(x_{i}^{1})=0
and if
x\in M^{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i}^{1},r_{i}^{1})
, then
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)>r_{0}(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)D
. Moreover, if we denote by
\Gamma_{i}^{1}
the group associated to
B(x_{i}^{1},r_{1}^{1})
, then if
s_{i}^{1}
denotes the largest integer such that
\mathcal{V}_{r_{s_{i}^{1}}}(x_{i}^{1})\geqslant\ln|\Gamma_{i}^{1}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
, then for any
x\in B(x_{i}^{1},2r_{s_{i}^{1}})
, we have

\mathcal{V}_{\frac{1}{2}r_{s_{i}^{1}}}(x)<\ln|\Gamma_{i}^{1}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}.

Define
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{0}=M^{n}\setminus\overline{\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i}^{1},r_{i}^{1})}
as the first body region. Then we can write

\displaystyle M^{n}=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{0}\cup\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i}^{1},2r_{i}^{1}),

(4.11)

and
B(x_{i}^{1},2r_{i}^{1})\cap\mathcal{B}_{1}^{0}
is diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma_{i}^{1}
by Proposition
4.1
.

Now, let us inductively construct a decomposition of
M^{n}
,

\displaystyle M^{n}=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{0}\cup\bigcup_{i_{1}=1}^{N_{1}}\mathcal{N}_{i_{1}}^{1}\cup\bigcup_{i_{1}=1}^{N_{1}}\mathcal{B}_{i_{1}}^{1}\cup\cdots\cup\bigcup_{i_{t}=1}^{N_{t}}\mathcal{N}_{i_{t}}^{t}\cup\bigcup_{i_{t}=1}^{N_{t}}\mathcal{B}_{i_{t}}^{t}\cup\bigcup_{a=1}^{N_{t+1}}B(x_{a},2r_{a}^{t+1})

(4.12)

with the following properties:

(1)

if
x\in\mathcal{B}_{i}^{l}
, then
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)\geqslant r_{0}(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)diam(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{l})
;

(2)

each neck
\mathcal{N}_{i}^{l}
is diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma_{i}^{l}
, for some
\Gamma_{i}^{l}\subset O(n)
with
|\Gamma_{i}^{l}|\leqslant C(n,v)
;

(3)

\mathcal{N}_{i}^{l}\cap\mathcal{B}_{i}^{l}
is diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma_{i}^{l}
;

(4)

\mathcal{B}_{j}^{l-1}\cap\mathcal{N}_{i}^{l}
are either empty or diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma_{i}^{l}
;

(5)

N_{l}\leqslant N(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)
;

(6)

if
\mathcal{B}_{j}^{l-1}\cap\mathcal{N}_{i}^{l}\neq\emptyset
, then
|\Gamma_{i}^{l}|\leqslant|\Gamma_{j}^{l-1}|-1
;

(7)

r_{a}^{t+1}=r_{k_{a}^{t+1}}
with
T_{k_{a}^{t+1}}^{\delta}(x_{a})=0
, and
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{t}\cap B(x_{a},2r_{a}^{t+1})\subseteq A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{a}^{t+1},2r_{a}^{t+1}}(x_{a})
;

(8)

if
s_{a}^{t+1}
is the largest integer such that
\mathcal{V}_{r_{s_{a}^{t+1}}}(x_{a})\geqslant\ln|\Gamma_{a}^{t+1}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
, then for any
x\in B(x_{a},2r_{s_{a}^{t+1}})
, we have
\mathcal{V}_{\frac{1}{2}r_{s_{a}^{t+1}}}(x)<\ln|\Gamma_{a}^{t+1}|-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
.

Note that (
4.11
) provides the case
t=0
. So let us suppose that the decomposition (
4.12
) has been built for
t\geqslant 0
, and we are going to construct for
t+1
.

We first use Theorem
4.5
to see that there exists an open set

A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{s_{a}^{t+1}},2r_{k_{a}^{t+1}}}(x_{a})\subset\mathcal{N}_{a}^{t+1}\subset A_{\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon)r_{s_{a}^{t+1}},2(1+\epsilon)r_{k_{a}^{t+1}}}(x_{a})

and a diffeomorphism
\phi_{a}^{t+1}:\mathcal{N}_{a}^{t+1}\to A_{\frac{1}{2}r_{s_{a}^{t+1}},2r_{k_{a}^{t+1}}}(0)
where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma_{a}^{t+1}
. By Lemma
4.6
and condition (8), there exists a radius
r_{a}=r(n,p,v,\Lambda,\delta)r_{s_{a}^{t+1}}=r(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)r_{s_{a}^{t+1}}<1
such that for any
x\in B(x_{a},2r_{s_{a}^{t+1}})
,

\mathcal{V}_{r_{a}}(x)<\ln(|\Gamma_{a}^{t+1}|-1)+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}.

Now we apply Lemma
4.7
to each ball
B(x_{a},r_{s_{a}^{t+1}})
to construct a collection of balls
\{B(x_{aj},r_{aj}^{t+2})\}
with
r_{aj}^{t+2}=r_{k_{aj}^{t+2}}
and
T_{k_{aj}^{t+2}}^{\delta}(x_{aj})=0
. There are at most
N(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)
such balls in total and for any
x\in B(x_{a},r_{s_{a}^{t+1}})\setminus\bigcup_{j}B(x_{aj},r_{aj}^{t+2})
,
r_{h}^{\alpha}(x)>r_{0}(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)r_{s_{a}^{t+1}}
. Also, from the proof of Lemma
4.7
, we can assume that
r_{aj}^{t+2}<r(n,p,v,\Lambda,D)r_{s_{a}^{t+1}}=r_{a}
and
|\mathcal{V}_{r_{aj}^{t+2}}(x_{aj})-\ln|\Gamma_{aj}^{t+2}||<\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})}
where
\Gamma_{aj}^{t+2}
is the corresponding group of
B(x_{aj},r_{aj}^{t+2})
.

On the other hand, by the almost monotonicity formula (
2.2
), we have

\mathcal{V}_{r_{aj}^{t+2}}(x_{aj})<\mathcal{V}_{r_{a}}(x_{aj})+C(n,p,v)\delta^{1-\frac{n}{2p}}<\ln(|\Gamma_{a}^{t+1}|-1)+2\delta^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{n}{2p})},

which implies
|\Gamma_{aj}^{t+2}|\leqslant|\Gamma_{a}^{t+1}|-1
. If we put

\mathcal{B}_{a}^{t+1}=B(x_{a},r_{s_{a}^{t+1}})\setminus\overline{\bigcup_{j}B(x_{aj},r_{aj}^{t+2})},

and write

M^{n}=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{0}\cup\cdots\cup\bigcup_{i_{t}=1}^{N_{t}}\mathcal{N}_{i_{t}}^{t}\cup\bigcup_{i_{t}=1}^{N_{t}}\mathcal{B}_{i_{t}}^{t}\cup\bigcup_{a=1}^{N_{t+1}}\mathcal{N}_{a}^{t+1}\cup\bigcup_{a=1}^{N_{t+1}}\mathcal{B}_{a}^{t+1}\cup\bigcup B(x_{aj},2r_{aj}^{t+2}),

then one can check the inductive conditions below (
4.12
) easily by shrinking neck regions if necessary.

Once we have the inductive decomposition, we could finish the proof immediately. In fact, by condition (6) and the upper bound
|\Gamma_{i}^{l}|\leqslant C(n,v)
, we see that the decomposition will stop within
m
steps where
m\leqslant m(n,v)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Once we have the bubble tree decomposition, our diffeomorphism finiteness statement of Theorem
1.2
can be proved directly. Notice that all body regions have finite diffeomorphism types by Theorem
2.12
and any neck region is diffeomorphic to
(0,1)\times S^{n-1}/\Gamma
for some
\Gamma\subseteq O(n)
with
|\Gamma|<C(n,v)
. See
[
13
]
for more details. Hence, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

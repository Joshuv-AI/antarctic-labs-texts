1.
Statement of the results and motivation

1.1.
Main results

Consider a subshift of finite type
(\mathbb{X},T)
endowed with a Gibbs measure
\nu
and
let
f
be a real-valued Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}

(the precise definitions are given in Section
2
).
Define the Birkhoff sums

\displaystyle S_{n}f=f+f\circ T+\ldots+f\circ T^{n-1},\quad n\geqslant 1.

A fundamental result of the theory of dynamic systems is the celebrated Birkhoff ergodic theorem which asserts that
\nu
-almost surely,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}f}{n}=\int_{\mathbb{X}}f(x)\nu(dx)=:\nu(f).

Much effort was made to establish another important property – the central limit theorem for
S_{n}f
.
To formulate the corresponding statement, we first note that the following limit exists:

\displaystyle\sigma_{f}^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{X}}(S_{n}f-n\nu(f))^{2}d\nu.

It is known that
\sigma_{f}^{2}=0
if and only if
f
is a coboundary w.r.t. to
T
, which means that
there exists a Hölder continuous function
g
on
\mathbb{X}
such that
f(x)=g(Tx)-g(x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
.
In the case when
\sigma_{f}>0
(or equivalently when
f
is not a coboundary) the following central limit theorem holds:
for any bounded continuous function
F:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}F\left(\frac{S_{n}f(x)-n\nu(f)}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu(dx)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(t)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}dt.

All these statements, which can be found in the excellent book of Parry and Pollicott
[
22
]
,
are consequences of many successive works such as Sinai
[
25
,
26
]
, Ratner
[
23
]
, Ruelle
[
24
]
and
Denker-Phillip
[
9
]
, to cite only a few.
The goal of this paper is to complement the central limit theorem (
1.1
) by proving limit theorems for the Birkhoff sum
t+S_{n}f
under the condition that the trajectory

(t+S_{k}f)_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}
stays positive, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
is a starting point.
A brief historical foray into the subject of conditioned limit theorems and our motivation are presented
in Section
1.2
.

To state our results assume that
\nu(f)=0
and that
f
is not a coboundary.
For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the following exit time is finite for
\nu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{X}
:

\displaystyle\tau_{t}^{f}(x):=\inf\left\{k\geqslant 1,\,t+S_{k}f(x)<0\right\}.

Thus, by definition,
the set
\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\}
is the set
where the trajectory
(t+S_{k}f)_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}
stays non-negative.

Our first theorem states the existence of a special Radon measure which will play a central role in the paper and
will be used in the formulations of the subsequent results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\nu(f)=0
and
f
is not a coboundary.
Then, there exists a unique Radon measure
\mu^{f}
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)S_{n}f(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt=\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\mu^{f}(dx,dt).

Moreover, the Radon measure
\mu^{f}
satisfies the following quasi-invariance property: for every continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\mu^{f}(dx,dt)=\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi\left(T^{-1}x,t-f(T^{-1}x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\{t\geqslant 0\}}\mu^{f}(dx,dt).

The limit (
1.2
) takes a simpler form when the function
\varphi
does not depend on the first argument.
Indeed, we will show in Section
3.3
that the marginal of
\mu^{f}
on
\mathbb{R}

is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
Its density function is a non-decreasing function on
\mathbb{R}
that will be denoted by
V^{f}
.
In particular, by standard arguments, the asymptotic (
1.2
) is valid for functions
\varphi

of the form
\varphi(x,t)=\mathds{1}_{[a,b]}(t)
for
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
This leads to the following:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\nu(f)=0
and
f
is not a coboundary.
Then, for any real numbers
-\infty<a<b<\infty
, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\mathbb{X}}S_{n}f(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt=\mu^{f}(\mathbb{X}\times[a,b])=\int_{a}^{b}V^{f}(t)dt.

Note that (
1.2
) and (
1.4
)
are stated in integral forms with respect to
t
.
It is an open question whether it is possible to give an asymptotic of the integral

\int_{\mathbb{X}}S_{n}f(x)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\}}\nu(dx)

for a fixed value of
t
.

The Radon measure
\mu^{f}
appearing in Theorem
1.1

will be called the
harmonic measure
associated to the dynamical system
(\mathbb{X},T,\nu)

with the observable
f
.
The reason for this is that the measure
\mu^{f}
is related to the harmonicity property
that appears in the study of killed random walks on the half line.
We refer to Section
3.3

for precise statements.

The following results describe the limit behavior of the Birkhoff sum
t+S_{n}f

under the condition that the trajectory
(t+S_{k}f)_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}
stays non-negative. We start by giving the equivalent of the probability that the trajectory
(t+S_{k}f)_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}
stays non-negative.
Denote by
\check{\mu}^{f}
the harmonic measure related to the reversed dynamical system
(\mathbb{X},T^{-1},\nu)

with the observable
f\circ T^{-1}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
f
is not a coboundary and
\nu(f)=0
.
Then, for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\}}\nu(dx)dt\sim\frac{2}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{2\pi n}}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\mu^{f}(dx,dt)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(T^{n}x,t+S_{n}f(x))

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\}}\nu(dx)dt\sim\frac{2}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{2\pi n}}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{(-f)}(dx,dt).

In particular, as the measure
\mu^{f}
has absolutely continuous marginal on
\mathbb{R}
,
Theorem
1.3
also applies to the function
\varphi(x,t)=\mathds{1}_{[a,b]}(t)

for
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, this gives the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
f
is not a coboundary and
\nu(f)=0
.
Then, for any real numbers
-\infty<a<b<\infty
, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right)dt\sim\frac{2}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{2\pi n}}\int_{a}^{b}V^{f}(t)dt.

Now we give a conditioned central limit theorem for the Birkhoff sum
S_{n}f
,
which states that the law of
S_{n}f
conditioned to stay positive converges weakly to the Rayleigh law.
Let
\phi^{+}
be the Rayleigh density function:

\displaystyle\phi^{+}(u)=ue^{-u^{2}/2}\mathds{1}_{\{u\geqslant 0\}},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
f
is not a coboundary and
\nu(f)=0
.
Then, for any continuous compactly supported function
F
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
,
we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}F

\displaystyle\left(x,T^{n}x,t,\frac{S_{n}f(x)}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\sim\frac{2}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{2\pi n}}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}F(x,x^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\phi^{+}(t^{\prime})\nu(dx^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\mu^{f}(dx,dt).

As above, we can actually apply Theorem
1.5

to the function
F(x,x^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\mathds{1}_{[a,b]}(t)\mathds{1}_{[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]}(t^{\prime})
for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}
and
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, this implies the following corollary:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
f
is not a coboundary and
\nu(f)=0
.
Then, for any real numbers
-\infty<a<b<\infty
and
-\infty<a^{\prime}<b^{\prime}<\infty
, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:\frac{S_{n}f(x)}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{n}}\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}],\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right)dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\sim\frac{2}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{2\pi n}}\int_{a}^{b}V^{f}(t)dt\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}\phi^{+}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

Next we formulate a conditioned local limit theorem for
S_{n}f
,
which is a refinement of the previous result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\nu(f)=0
. Assume that for any
p\neq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
pf+q
is not cohomologous to a function with values in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for any continuous compactly supported function
F
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
,
we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}F

\displaystyle(x,T^{n}x,t,t+S_{n}f(x))\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n-1\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{f}^{3}n^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}F(x,x^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\mu^{f}(dx,dt)\check{\mu}^{(-f)}(dx^{\prime},dt^{\prime}).

In Theorem
1.7
, we assumed the function
f
to satisfy a non-arithmeticity condition.
When this is not the case but
f
is still not cohomologous to
0
,
we could still get an analogue of this result by the same method.

In the particular case when the function
F
has the form

F(x,x^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\mathds{1}_{[a,b]}(t)\mathds{1}_{[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]}(t^{\prime})
for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}
and
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
from the previous theorem we obtain the following:

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\nu(f)=0
.
Assume that for any
p\neq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
pf+q
is not cohomologous to a function with values in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for any real numbers
-\infty<a<b<\infty
and
-\infty<a^{\prime}<b^{\prime}<\infty
, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}

\displaystyle\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:t+S_{n}f(x)\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}],\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n-1\right)dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{f}^{3}n^{3/2}}\int_{a}^{b}V^{f}(t)dt\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}\check{V}^{(-f)}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

In this corollary we have denoted by
\check{V}^{(-f)}
the density function with respect to the Lebesgue measure
of the marginal on
\mathbb{R}
of the Radon measure
\check{\mu}^{(-f)}
.

As
f
is bounded, by taking the interval
[a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
to be
[-c,0)
for
c>0
large enough,
we get from Corollary
1.8
the following:

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\nu(f)=0
.
Assume that for any
p\neq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
pf+q
is not cohomologous to a function with values in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for any real numbers
-\infty<a<b<\infty
, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:\tau_{t}^{f}(x)=n\right)dt\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{f}^{3}n^{3/2}}\int_{a}^{b}V^{f}(t)dt\int_{-\infty}^{0}\check{V}^{(-f)}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

Our Corollary
1.9
could be extended
without difficulties to the case when one only assumes that
f
is not cohomologous to
0
.
This assertion could be deduced from a version of Theorem
1.7

for functions
f
that are cohomologous to functions with values
in a set of the form
\alpha\mathbb{Z}+\beta
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Again, similarly to the comment after Corollary
1.2
,
Theorems
1.3
,
1.5
and
1.7
are stated in integral forms with respect to
t
.
It is an open problem to obtain asymptotics for a fixed value of
t\in\mathbb{R}
of the following probabilities:

\displaystyle\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right),\quad\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:\frac{S_{n}f(x)}{\sigma_{f}\sqrt{n}}\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}],\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right),

\displaystyle\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:S_{n}f(x)\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}],\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right).

Due to the theory of Markov partitions (see Appendix III of
[
22
]
and Chapter 18.7 of
[
18
]
),
Theorems
1.1
,
1.3
,
1.5

and
1.7
can be applied without any changes to hyperbolic dynamical systems.
Finally, using the approach of this paper, one can obtain analogous results for hyperbolic flows.
The latter is out of the scope of this article and will be done in another work.

1.2.
Previous work and motivation

The first examples of conditioned limit theorems for
sums of independent random variables are due to the pioneering work of Spitzer
[
27
]
and Feller
[
12
]
.
Since then integral and local limit theorems for random walks conditioned to stay positive attracted
a lot of attention.
Very many authors contributed to this subject, among them
Borovkov
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
,
Bolthausen
[
2
]
, Iglehart
[
20
]
, Eppel
[
11
]
, Bertoin and Doney
[
1
]
,
Caravenna
[
6
]
, Vatutin and Wachtel
[
28
]
, Doney
[
10
]
, Kersting and Vatutin
[
21
]
.
Most of this work is based on the Wiener-Hopf factorization and
various factorization identities. Recently Denisov and Wachtel
[
7
,
8
]
have studied the setting of random walks in cones and have developed a new approach
based on the construction of the harmonic function thus avoiding
the use of the Wiener-Hopf factorization.
Following this method, in the case of dependent random variables very recent
progress was made in
[
15
,
13
]
,
where conditioned integral limit theorems for products of random matrices and
for Markov chains satisfying spectral gap properties have been obtained.
In
[
14
]
a conditioned local limit theorem for a Markov chain with finite state space was considered.

The question of establishing
conditioned integral and local limit theorems for Birkhoff sums remained for a very long time
a challenging problem mainly because the Wiener-Hopf factorisation
does not work in the dynamical system framework.
The emergence of new approaches for proving the integral and local limit theorems for Markov chains

[
7
,
15
,
13
,
14
]

opens an opportunity to investigate these problems for dynamical systems.
However, there are two main difficulties which have motivated our study.

The first difficulty is that the
alternative approaches described above are based on the existence of the harmonic function.
For Markov chains its existence is proved in
[
15
,
13
]
.
Or a subshift of finite type exhibits a dependence which in general is not of
Markovian type and therefore the
existence of the harmonic function is not granted, except in some very special cases.
Indeed, one of the major findings of the paper is that in the case for the subshift of finite type
(\mathbb{X},T,\nu)

with a general Hölder continuous observable
f

a more general object – the harmonic measure
\mu^{f}
– has to be introduced.
The conditioned integral and local limit theorems in this case are stated in terms of the harmonic measures

\mu^{f}
and
\check{\mu}^{-f}
(the second corresponding to the reversed subshift
(\mathbb{X},T^{-1},\nu)

with observable
f\circ T^{-1}
),
which therefore cannot be avoided and constitute essential characteristics of the model.
The construction of
\mu^{f}
is performed first for a Birkhoff sum
t+S_{n}f
, where the observable

f
depends only on the future coordinates
(in the reversed setting it corresponds to studying a Markov chain).
Then it is extended gradually to a function
f
depending on the whole set of coordinates using some smoothing
techniques and a vague convergence argument, see Section
3
.

The second difficulty resides in adapting the approaches
from the Markov chains
[
13
,
14
]
to the case of dynamical systems.
Our approach is close to that of the recent paper
[
16
]
, where random walks with independent increments
are considered.
We shall first establish the corresponding theorems for the Birkhoff sum
t+S_{n}f
with an observable

f
depending only on the future coordinates (which corresponds to dealing with some Markov chain).
Then using the technique similar to that in the previous point we extend them to the general case.
The main difficulty here consists in the construction of the dual Markov chain,
which is an important step in establishing a conditioned local limit theorem.
Fortunately, in the case of the subshift of finite type the dual Markov chain
corresponds again to a dynamical system
– the reversed subshift
(\mathbb{X},T^{-1},\nu)
with observable
f\circ T^{-1}
.

To put it a perspective, the approach developed in our paper can be applied to Birkhoff sums with drift, via a Cramér Type change of measure,
see Parry and Pollicott
[
22
]
and Waddington
[
29
]
.
It is also possible to apply the developed approach for
studying local limit theorems for products of random matrices and more generally for Markov chains
with values in general state spaces in contrast to
[
14
]
, where the chain with finite state spaces have been considered.
This will be the subject of a forthcoming paper.

2.
Background and auxiliary statements

2.1.
Subshift of finite type and Gibbs measure

We start by precisely introducing the subshift of finite type.
Let
k\geqslant 2
be an integer and
A=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.
Let
M
be a transition matrix on
A
, that is to say,

M=(M(i,j))_{i,j\in A}
is a matrix with coefficients in
\{0,1\}
.
We assume that the transition matrix
M
is irreducible in the sense that
there exists an integer
p\geqslant 1
such that all the coefficients of the matrix
M^{p}
are strictly positive.
Consider the associated subshift of finite type

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}=\left\{x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in A^{\mathbb{Z}}:M(x_{n},x_{n+1})=1,n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\subset A^{\mathbb{Z}},

equipped with the shift map
T
defined by
(Tx)_{n}=x_{n+1}
for
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The set
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}

is equipped with the discrete topology,
so the space
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is compact with the corresponding Tychonov product topology.
We equip
\mathbb{X}
with the induced topology, which is also compact.
For any
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{X}
and
y=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{X}
, define

\displaystyle\omega(x,y)=\min\{k\geqslant 0:x_{k}\neq y_{k}\ \mbox{or}\ x_{-k}\neq y_{-k}\}.

Note that for any constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the function
(x,y)\mapsto\alpha^{\omega(x,y)}
is a distance on
\mathbb{X}

which induces the natural product topology.

The space of real-valued continuous functions
f:\mathbb{X}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X})
.
For any function
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X})
, we say that
f
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{X}

if there exist constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{X}
,

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leqslant C\alpha^{\omega(x,y)}.

Denote by
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
the space of all real-valued
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
\mathbb{X}
,
equipped with the following norm

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{X}}|f(x)|+\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{X}:x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\alpha^{\omega(x,y)}}.

In the sequel we shall say that a real-valued function on
\mathbb{X}
is Hölder continuous if there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)

such that
f
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous.
Let
\mathscr{B}=\cup_{0<\alpha<1}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
be the set of all real-valued Hölder continuous functions on
\mathbb{X}
.

For any
f\in\mathscr{B}
, we consider the Birkhoff sum process
(S_{n}f)_{n\geqslant 0}
by setting
S_{0}f=0
and

\displaystyle S_{n}f=f+\ldots+f\circ T^{n-1},\quad n\geqslant 1.

Let us denote by
\mathbb{X}^{+}\subset A^{\mathbb{N}}
the set

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}^{+}=\left\{x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in A^{\mathbb{N}}:M(x_{n},x_{n+1})=1,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subset A^{\mathbb{N}}.

The set of continuous functions on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})
.
By abuse of notation, the one-sided shift map
\mathbb{X}^{+}\mapsto\mathbb{X}^{+}
will still be denoted by
T
.

The Ruelle operator
\mathcal{L}_{f}:\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})\mapsto\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})
related to
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})

is defined as follows:
for any
g\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}g(x)=\sum_{y:\,Ty=x}e^{-f(y)}g(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}.

One can easily see that
\mathcal{L}_{f}
is a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})
.
From (
2.2
), by iteration, it follows that for any
n\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}^{n}g(x)=\sum_{y:\,T^{n}y=x}e^{-S_{n}f(y)}g(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}.

Also, if
h\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})
, we have the conjugacy relation

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f+h\circ T-h}g=e^{-h}\mathcal{L}_{f}\left(e^{h}g\right),

which tells us that the spectral properties of the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{f}
only depend on the cohomology class of
f
.
We say that a real valued and Hölder continuous function
\psi
on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
is normalized if
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}\mathds{1}=\mathds{1}
.
By
[
22
, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.2]
, there exist a real valued Hölder continuous function
h
and a real number
\lambda

such that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}e^{h}=e^{\lambda+h}
.
From the conjugacy relation (
2.3
), this tells us that the function
\psi-h\circ T+h+\lambda
is also normalized.
Therefore, all over the paper, we will assume that
\psi
is normalized.
In this case, it is well known (e.g.
[
22
]
) that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}
admits a unique invariant probability measure

\nu^{+}
.
The measure
\nu^{+}
is called the Gibbs measure related to the potential
\psi
.
Since
\psi
is normalized, the measure
\nu^{+}
is
T
-invariant, that is, for any
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nu^{+}(f\circ T)=\nu^{+}(f),

see
[
22
, Chapter 2]
.

Note that
\nu^{+}
is also
T
-ergodic, meaning that any
T
-invariant Borel subset
B

of
\mathbb{X}^{+}
has
\nu^{+}
measure
0
or
1
:

\displaystyle T^{-1}B=B\quad\Rightarrow\quad\nu^{+}(B)\in\{0,1\}.

Thanks to the following lemma, the measure
\nu^{+}
allows to define a
T
-invariant measure on
\mathbb{X}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\nu^{+}
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
which is
T
-invariant.
Then there exists a unique
T
-invariant Borel probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{X}

such that the image of
\nu
under the natural projection map
\mathbb{X}\mapsto\mathbb{X}^{+}
is equal to
\nu^{+}
.

The proof of this lemma is just a consequence of Kolmogorov’s extension theorem.
We will actually give an explicit formula for the measure
\nu
below.

2.2.
Conditional measures on the past

For any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
, we shall construct a measure
\nu_{z}^{-}
, which is the conditional measure of
\nu
with respect to the map

x\in\mathbb{X}\mapsto x_{+}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
To this end, for any
a\in A
, let

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a}=\left\{y\in A^{-\mathbb{N}^{*}}:M(y_{-1},a)=1,M(y_{-n-1},y_{-n})=1,\forall n\geqslant 1\right\},

where
M
is the transition matrix on the set
A

which was used to define the finite type subshift
\mathbb{X}\subset A^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
, we set
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}=\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z_{0}}
, where
z_{0}
is the first coordinate of
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
We have the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}\times\{z\}.

The point
z
may be thought of as the future of the trajectory whereas the elements of
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
describe
the pasts which are compatible with this future.
Let us introduce some notation related to this decomposition.
For any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
, we denote
y\cdot z=(y,z)\in\mathbb{X}
.
For
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
k\geqslant 1
, we set

\displaystyle A^{k}_{z}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(y_{-k},\ldots,y_{-1})\in A^{\{-k,\ldots,-1\}}:

\displaystyle\qquad M(y_{-1},z_{0})=1,M(y_{-n-1},y_{-n})=1,\forall 1\leqslant n\leqslant k-1\Big\}.

For
(y_{-k},\ldots,y_{-1})\in A^{k}_{z}
, we set

y_{-k}\ldots y_{-1}\cdot z

to be the element
w\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
defined by

w_{n}=\begin{cases}y_{n-k}&\mbox{if}\ 0\leqslant n\leqslant k-1\\
z_{n-k}&\mbox{if}\ n\geqslant k.\end{cases}

For
a\in A^{k}_{z}
, let

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{a,z}=\{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:y_{-k}=a_{-k},\ldots,y_{-1}=a_{-1}\}

be the associated cylinder of length
k
in
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.

Recall that the two-sided shift map

T:\mathbb{X}\mapsto\mathbb{X}
and its inverse
T^{-1}
are defined by

(Tx)_{n}=x_{n+1}
and
(T^{-1}x)_{n}=x_{n-1}
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By abuse of notation, the one-sided forward shift map will be denoted by
T:\mathbb{X}^{+}\mapsto\mathbb{X}^{+}

and is defined by
T(x)=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots),

for any
x=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
Let us define conditional measures on the past of trajectories.
For
k\geqslant 0
, define
\nu^{k}_{z}
as a function on cylinders of length
k
in
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}

by the formula

(2.6)

\displaystyle\nu^{k}_{z}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z})=\exp(-S_{k}\psi(a\cdot z)),

for
a\in A_{z}^{k}
.
Since
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}\mathds{1}=1
,
we have that for any
a\in A^{k}_{z}
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\nu^{k}_{z}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in A\\
M(b,a_{-k})=1\end{subarray}}\nu^{k+1}_{z}(\mathbb{C}_{b\cdot a,z}).

By Kolmogorov’s extension theorem, from
equation (
2.7
) it follows that
there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\nu^{-}_{z}
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}

such that for any
k\geqslant 0
,

\nu^{k}_{z}
is the restriction of
\nu^{-}_{z}
to cylinders of length
k
.

We can now give an explicit formula for the measure
\nu
in terms of the measures
\nu^{+}
and
\nu_{z}^{-}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C(\mathbb{X})}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\nu(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y\cdot z)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)\nu^{+}(dz).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to prove that the measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{X}

defined by the above equation is
T
-invariant.
This property is a direct consequence of the definition of the measures
\nu_{z}^{-}
,
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,
and of the fact that
\nu^{+}
is
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}
-invariant.
∎

We shall use the fact that the measures
\nu^{-}_{z}
and
\nu^{-}_{z^{\prime}}
are equivalent.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a real-valued continuous function
\theta
on the set

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{3}:=\left\{(y,z,z^{\prime})\in A^{-\mathbb{N}^{*}}\times\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{X}^{+}:z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime},\,y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}=\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}}\right\}

such that for any
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and any continuous function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y)\nu^{-}_{z^{\prime}}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y)e^{\theta(y,z,z^{\prime})}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

Besides, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
(y,z,z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{X}_{3}
,

\displaystyle|\theta(y,z,z^{\prime})|\leqslant c\alpha^{\omega(z,z^{\prime})}.

Proof.

Indeed, it suffices to set

\displaystyle\theta(y,z,z^{\prime})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\psi(T^{-k}(y\cdot z))-\psi(T^{-k}(y\cdot z^{\prime}))\right).

∎

2.3.
General properties of exit times

From the following lemma it follows that the function
x\mapsto\tau_{t}^{f}(x)
is finite
\nu
-almost surely.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{B}
with
\nu(f)=0
. Assume that
f
is not a coboundary.
Then for
\nu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{X}
, one has

\displaystyle\inf_{n\geqslant 1}S_{n}f(x)=-\infty.

Proof.

Consider the Borel set

\displaystyle A=\left\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\inf_{n\geqslant 1}S_{n}f(x)>-\infty\right\}.

It is clear that the set
A
is
T
-invariant. Therefore
\nu(A)=0
or
\nu(A)=1
.
Assume that
\nu(A)=1
, then let us show that
f
is a coboundary.
Indeed, for any
x\in A
, we have that
h(x):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}S_{n}f(x)>-\infty
.
Since
\nu(f)=0
, it is well known that
S_{n}f(x)
admits finite limit points for
\nu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{X}
,
so that
h(x)<\infty
.
Now by definition, we have
h(Tx)=h(x)-f(x)
, hence
f
is a coboundary as a measurable function on
\mathbb{X}
. Therefore, by
[
22
, Proposition 6.2]
, we get that
f
is a coboundary as a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
.
∎

For notational reasons, it is more convenient to study objects defined by the reverse shift
T^{-1}
.
Note that the two studies are equivalent.

Indeed, let us define the flip map
\iota:A^{\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto A^{\mathbb{Z}}
by the following relation: for any

x=(\ldots,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\in A^{\mathbb{Z}}
it holds
\iota(x)=(\ldots,x_{1},x_{0},x_{-1},\ldots)\in A^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
that is
(\iota x)_{n}=x_{-n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The following lemma is classical (see
[
22
, Chapter 2]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

The set
\iota\mathbb{X}
is a subshift of finite type
and the measure
\iota_{*}\nu
is a Gibbs measure on
\iota\mathbb{X}
.

For
f\in\mathscr{B}
, consider the reversed Birkhoff sum process
(\check{S}_{n}f)_{n\geqslant 1}

which is defined as follows: for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
,

\displaystyle\check{S}_{n}f(x)=f(T^{-1}x)+f(T^{-2}x)+\ldots+f(T^{-n}x)=S_{n}f(T^{-n}x),\quad n\geqslant 1.

In the same way, denote by
\check{\tau}_{t}^{f}(x)
the first time when
t+\check{S}_{n}f(x)
becomes negative:
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\check{\tau}_{t}^{f}(x):=\inf\left\{k\geqslant 1,\,t+\check{S}_{k}f(x)<0\right\}.

Then the relation between the exit times
\tau_{t}^{f}
and
\check{\tau}_{t}^{f\circ\iota}
is given by

\displaystyle\tau_{t}^{f}(Tx)=\check{\tau}_{t}^{f\circ\iota}(\iota x),\quad x\in\mathbb{X}.

In the present paper we deal with the measure

(2.9)

\displaystyle\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right)

which, by the discussion above, is equivalent to studying the measure

(2.10)

\displaystyle\nu\left(x\in\mathbb{X}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right).

In turn, Lemma
2.2
shows that in order to study (
2.10
),
it suffices to investigate

(2.11)

\displaystyle\nu^{-}_{z}\big(y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{f}(y\cdot z)>n\big),

for
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
We will do it by using tools from the theory of Markov chains
[
13
]
.
In particular, we will make use of the martingale approximation for the process
(\check{S}_{n}f)_{n\geqslant 1}
.

2.4.
Martingale approximation

Recall that
\mathscr{B}=\cup_{0<\alpha<1}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
is
the space of real-valued
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
\mathbb{X}
endowed with the norm (
2.1
).
In the same way, we denote by
\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}

the space of real-valued
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}|f(x)|+\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{X}^{+}:x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\alpha^{\omega(x,y)}}.

Let
\mathscr{B}^{+}=\cup_{0<\alpha<1}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}^{+}
.
Note that every Hölder continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
can be extended to a
Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
defined by

\displaystyle x=(\ldots,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\in\mathbb{X}\mapsto f(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots),

so we can identify
\mathscr{B}^{+}
with a subspace of
\mathscr{B}
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{B}
.
Define the cohomology class of
f
as the following set of Hölder continuous functions:

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(f)=\{f_{0}\in\mathscr{B}\ |\ f_{0}=f-h\circ T+h,\ h\in\mathscr{B}\}.

The following proposition tells us that there exists a natural choice in
\mathscr{C}(f)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{B}
be such that
\nu(f)=0
.
Then there exists a unique function
f_{0}\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}f_{0}=0

and its extension on
\mathbb{X}
belongs to
\mathscr{C}(f)
.

Proof.

First we prove the existence of
f_{0}
.
By Proposition 1.2 in
[
22
]
, there exists a Hölder continuous function
g
on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
, whose extension to
\mathbb{X}
is cohomologous to
f
.
As
\nu(f)=0
, we have
\nu^{+}(g)=0
.
Now we choose
\alpha\in(0,1)
close enough to
1
so that

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}
is bounded on
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
and
g\in\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
.
By the spectral gap property for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}
(see Theorem 2.2 of
[
22
]
),
there exists a Hölder continuous function
h\in\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
such that

(2.12)

\displaystyle h-\mathcal{L}_{\psi}h=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}g.

Since
h=h\mathcal{L}_{\psi}1=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(h\circ T)
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(g-h\circ T+h)=0.

Hence there exists a function
f_{0}:=g-h\circ T+h\in\mathscr{C}(f)
satisfying
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}f_{0}=0
.

Now we prove the uniqueness of
f_{0}
.
Suppose that there exist
f_{0},f_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}(f)
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}f_{0}=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}f_{0}^{\prime}=0
.
Then
f_{0}-f_{0}^{\prime}
is a coboundary, namely, there exists
h_{1}\in\mathscr{B}
such that

f_{0}-f_{0}^{\prime}=h_{1}\circ T-h_{1}
.
As
f_{0}
and
f_{0}^{\prime}
depend only on the future coordinates,
it is well known that
h_{1}
depends only on the future coordinates.
It follows that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(h_{1}\circ T-h_{1})=0
and hence
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}h_{1}=h_{1}
.
This implies that
h_{1}
is a constant and therefore
f_{0}^{\prime}=f_{0}
.
∎

For any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
, we have defined a probability measure
\nu^{-}_{z}
on the set
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}\subset A^{-\mathbb{N}^{*}}
of past sequences
which are compatible with
z
.
For
n\geqslant 1
, we let
\mathscr{F}_{n}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
A^{-\mathbb{N}^{*}}
generated by the coordinate maps

y\mapsto(y_{-1},\ldots,y_{-n})
.
By convention, we also define
\mathscr{F}_{0}
as the trivial
\sigma
-algebra.
And we let
\mathscr{F}_{n}^{z}
be the
\sigma
-algebra induced on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.
The following proposition is a classical result from
[
22
]
:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}^{+})
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}f_{0}=0
if and only if for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,
the sequence of random variables

\displaystyle y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}\mapsto\check{S}_{n}f_{0}(y\cdot z),\ \ n\geqslant 0

is a martingale on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
equipped with the probability measure
\nu^{-}_{z}

w.r.t. the filtration
(\mathscr{F}_{n}^{z})_{n\geqslant 0}
.

Proof.

Denote by
g_{n}^{z}:\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
the function
y\mapsto\check{S}_{n}f_{0}(y\cdot z)
.
Then for
y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
and
n\geqslant 1
, we have by the definition of the measure
\nu^{-}_{z}
,

\displaystyle\nu^{-}_{z}\left(g_{n}^{z}\big|\mathscr{F}_{n-1}^{z}\right)(y)=g_{n-1}^{z}(y)+\mathcal{L}_{\psi}f_{0}(T^{-n}(y\cdot z)).

From this identity, the assertion follows.
∎

The following result shows that the difference
\check{S}_{n}f-\check{S}_{n}g
is bounded,
for
f
and
g
in the same cohomology class.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{B}
and
g\in\mathscr{C}(f)
. Let
h\in\mathscr{B}
be such that
f-g=h\circ T-h
.
Then, for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
and any
n\geqslant 1

we have

\displaystyle\left|\check{S}_{n}f(x)-\check{S}_{n}g(x)\right|\leqslant c=2\|h\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Indeed, we have
S_{n}f-S_{n}g=h\circ T^{n}-h.

Since
\check{S}_{n}f=(S_{n}f)\circ T^{-n}
, we get

\check{S}_{n}f-\check{S}_{n}g=h-h\circ T^{-n}
, which proves the assertion.
∎

2.5.
The Hölder continuity and approximation

We establish several technical results which will be used in the proofs of the main results of the paper.
In particular, they will allow us to prove that several convergences hold uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

For any
g\in\mathscr{B}
,
there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for any
n\geqslant 1
,
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}

with
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}
and
y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}(=\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}})
, one has

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left|\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)-\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right|\leqslant c_{0}\alpha^{\omega(z,z^{\prime})}.

In particular, for any
g\in\mathscr{B}
,
there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for any
n\geqslant 1
,

z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}

with
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}
and
y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}(=\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}})
, it holds

\displaystyle\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\leqslant\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})+c_{0}.

Proof.

Since
g\in\mathscr{B}
, there exists a constant
L_{g}
such that for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}
,

\displaystyle|g(x)-g(x^{\prime})|\leqslant L_{g}\alpha^{\omega(x,x^{\prime})},

where
0<\alpha<1
.
Hence for any
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}

with
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}
and
y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
, and
n\geqslant 1
, one has

\displaystyle\left|\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)-\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L_{g}\alpha^{n-k+\omega(z,z^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{g}\frac{\alpha^{1+\omega(z,z^{\prime})}}{1-\alpha}=:c_{0}\alpha^{w(z,z^{\prime})}.

The desired result follows.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

For any
g\in\mathscr{B}
,
there exist constants
c_{0}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any
n\geqslant 1
,
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}

with
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}
and
y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}(=\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}})
, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left|\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)-\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right|\leqslant c_{0}\alpha^{w(z,z^{\prime})}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
, there exist constants
c_{0}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any
n\geqslant j\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\leqslant\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\leqslant\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})+c_{0}\alpha^{w(z,z^{\prime})}.

Taking the minimum over
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
on the right hand side, we get

(2.15)

\displaystyle\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\leqslant\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})+c_{0}\alpha^{w(z,z^{\prime})}.

In the same way, again by Lemma
2.9
, there exist constants
c_{0}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any
n\geqslant j\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\geqslant\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})-c_{0}\alpha^{w(z,z^{\prime})}\geqslant\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})-c_{0}\alpha^{w(z,z^{\prime})}.

Taking the minimum over
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
on the left hand side, we get

(2.16)

\displaystyle\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\geqslant\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})-c_{0}\alpha^{w(z,z^{\prime})}.

Combining (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), we conclude the proof of (
2.14
).
∎

We will also need the following technical lemma that allows us to approximate the function
g\in\mathscr{B}

by a function
x\mapsto g_{m}(x)
on
\mathbb{X}
which only depends on the coordinates
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geqslant-m}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
. Then there exist constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
c_{1}>0

and a sequence of Hölder continuous functions
(g_{m})_{m\geqslant 0}
on
\mathbb{X}

which only depend on the coordinates
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geqslant-m}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}g_{0}=0
and for any
m\geqslant 0
,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\left\|\check{S}_{n}g_{m}-\check{S}_{n}g\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant c_{1}\alpha^{m}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.6
, there exist
g_{0}\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
and
h\in\mathscr{B}
with
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}g_{0}=0

and

(2.18)

\displaystyle g_{0}=g-h\circ T+h.

Since
h\in\mathscr{B}
, there is
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
h\in\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
.
Then, for any
m\geqslant 0
, there exists
a Hölder continuous function
h_{m}
on
\mathbb{X}
which only depends on the coordinates
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geqslant-m}
such that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|h-h_{m}\|_{\infty}\leqslant c_{1}\alpha^{m},

where
c_{1}>0
is a fixed constant not depending on
p
,
and by convention
h_{0}=0
.
We define for any
m\geqslant 0
,

(2.20)

\displaystyle g_{m}=g_{0}+h_{m}\circ T-h_{m}.

From (
2.18
), (
2.19
) and (
2.20
), we get (
2.17
).
∎

2.6.
Duality

The next duality property will be crucial in the proof of the main results.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
.
For any
n\geqslant 1
and any non-negative measurable function

F:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}F\left(x,t,T^{-n}x,t+\check{S}_{n}g(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n-1\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}F\big(T^{n}x,u-S_{n}g(x),x,u\big)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{u}^{-g}(x)>n-1\right\}}\nu(dx)du.

Proof.

By a change of variable
t=u-\check{S}_{n}g(x)
, it follows that

\displaystyle I:

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}F\left(x,t,T^{-n}x,t+\check{S}_{n}g(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{t+\check{S}_{n-1}g(x)\geqslant 0,\ldots,t+\check{S}_{1}g(x)\geqslant 0\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}F\left(x,u-\check{S}_{n}g(x),T^{-n}x,u\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\mathds{1}_{\left\{u-g(T^{-n}x)\geqslant 0,\ldots,u-g(T^{-n}x)-\ldots-g(T^{-2}x)\geqslant 0\right\}}\nu(dx)du.

Since the measure
\nu
is
T^{-1}
-invariant, we obtain

\displaystyle I=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}F\big(T^{n}x,u-S_{n}g(x),x,u\big)\mathds{1}_{\left\{u-S_{1}g(x)\geqslant 0,\ldots,u-S_{n-1}g(x)\geqslant 0\right\}}\nu(dx)du,

which ends the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Harmonicity for dynamical system

3.1.
Existence of the harmonic function

The aim of this section is to prove the existence of a function
V^{f}
on the state space
\mathbb{R}

which we call the harmonic function of
f
by analogy with the theory developed for Markov chains in
[
13
]
.
Our main result is the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
f
is not a coboundary and
\nu(f)=0
.
Then there exists a unique non decreasing and right continuous function
V^{f}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{X}}S_{n}f(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{t}^{f}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)V^{f}(t)dt.

Besides, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\max\{t-c,0\}\leqslant V^{f}(t)\leqslant\max\{t,0\}+c.

Note that, the bound (
3.2
) implies that
V^{f}(t)/t\to 1
as
t\to\infty
.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
will be given at the end of this section.
At this point we start by giving an explicit formula for the harmonic function in the case where the observable only depends on future coordinates.
Let
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
with
\nu(g)=0
and assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Let
g_{0}
be the unique element of
\mathscr{B}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}g_{0}=0
and
g_{0}
is cohomologous to
g
,
as in Proposition
2.6
.
For
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

(3.3)

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g}(z,t)=-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

This integral makes sense. Indeed, first, by Lemma
3.3
,
the stopping time
y\mapsto\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)
is finite
\nu^{-}_{z}
-almost everywhere.
Second, the Birkhoff sum
t+\check{S}_{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)}g(y\cdot z)

takes values in the interval
[-\|g\|_{\infty},0]

when
t
is non-negative, and in the interval
[t-\|g\|_{\infty},0]
when
t
is negative.
Third, by Lemma
2.8
, the difference of the Birkhoff sums for
g
and
g_{0}
is uniformly bounded.

The function
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
plays a crucial role in proving conditioned limit theorems for
products of random matrices and more generally for Markov chains, see
[
15
]
and
[
13
]
.
From the results of
[
13
]
it follows that
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
has the following harmonicity property.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g
be in
\mathscr{B}^{+}
such that
\nu^{+}(g)=0
and
g
is not a coboundary.
Then for any
(z,t)\in\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g}(z,t)=\sum_{z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}:T(z^{\prime})=z}e^{-\psi(z^{\prime})}\mathds{1}_{\{t+g(z^{\prime})\geqslant 0\}}\check{V}^{g}(z^{\prime},t+g(z^{\prime})).

The proof of the existence of the harmonic function
\check{V}^{g}
given in
[
13
]
is rather difficult.
In the case of the subshift of finite type (since the jumps are bounded) it is possible to give a much shorter direct proof, which is not included because of the space limitations.

We shall extend the definition of
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
to the case of any function
g\in\mathscr{B}
,
that is, the case of a function
g
that depends on both the past and the future coordinates.
We will use the following technical assertion:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
such that
\nu(g)=0
and
g
is not a coboundary with respect to T.
Then, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu^{-}_{z}\left(y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right)=0.

Proof.

Let
c_{0}>0
be as in Lemma
2.9
.
By Lemma
2.4
and Fubini’s theorem,
for any
a\in A
,
there exists
z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
such that
z^{\prime}_{0}=a
and the function

y\mapsto\tau_{t+c_{0}}^{g}(y\cdot z^{\prime})
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}}
is finite
\nu^{-}_{z^{\prime}}
-almost everywhere.
Then for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
with
z_{0}=a
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}\subseteq\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}}:\check{\tau}_{t+c_{0}}^{g}(y\cdot z^{\prime})>n\right\}.

From Lemma
2.3
, we get that for some constant
c>0
,

\displaystyle\nu^{-}_{z}\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}\leqslant c\nu^{-}_{z^{\prime}}\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}}:\check{\tau}_{t+c_{0}}^{g}(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right\}.

Thus, the lemma follows from the fact that

\nu^{-}_{z^{\prime}}\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}}:\check{\tau}_{t+c_{0}}^{g}(y\cdot z^{\prime})>n\right\}

converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Now we give an alternative definition of the function
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
for
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
,
where the key point is that in this case, the function
y\mapsto\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)
is a stopping time
with respect to the filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{k}^{z}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
with
\nu(g)=0
and assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Let
g_{0}
be the unique element of
\mathscr{B}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(g_{0})=0
and
g_{0}
is cohomologous to
g
.
Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

In addition, there is a constant
c>0
such that, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)\leqslant\max\{t,0\}+c,

for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle t-c\leqslant\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\leqslant t+c,

and for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t<-c
, it holds that
\check{V}^{g_{0}}(z,t)=0
.

Moreover, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
, the function
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
is non decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.8
and
3.3
,
the limits in (
3.4
) coincide since the difference of the Birkhoff sums for
g
and
g_{0}
is uniformly bounded and

\nu^{-}_{z}(y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
By the optional stopping theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)\leqslant n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)\leqslant n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

By Lemma
3.3
, we have
\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)<+\infty
for
\nu^{-}_{z}
-almost every
y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.
Using the Lebesgue convergence theorem,
we obtain (
3.4
) as well as (
3.6
).

Since for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
the function
y\mapsto t+\check{S}_{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)}g_{0}(y\cdot z)
takes values in
[-\|g\|_{\infty}-c,c]
,
where
c>0
is from Lemma
2.8
,
we get
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\in[t-c,t+\|g\|_{\infty}+c]
.
Besides, if
t<-\|g\|_{\infty}
, we get
\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)=1
everywhere, for all
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,
thus by (
2.6
) and (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g}(z,t)=\int_{A^{1}_{z}}\left(g_{0}(y_{-1}\cdot z)\right)\nu^{1}_{z}(dy_{-1})=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(g_{0})(z)=0.

As
\check{\tau}_{t_{1}}^{g}\leqslant\check{\tau}_{t_{2}}^{g}
for any
t_{1}\leqslant t_{2}
,
and
t_{2}+\check{S}_{n}g\geqslant 0
on the set
\{\check{\tau}_{t_{2}}^{g}>n\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t_{1}+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t_{1}}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t_{2}+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t_{2}}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

Letting
n\to\infty
yields that the function
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
is non decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

By using Lemma
3.4
, we can now give a definition of
\check{V}^{g}

for a function
g
only depending on finitely many negative coordinates.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
such that
\nu(g)=0
and
g
is not a coboundary.
Assume that
g
only depends on
m
negative coordinates for some
m\geqslant 0
.
In other words, the function
h=g\circ T^{m}\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
.
Then, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}^{m}\left(\check{V}^{h}(\cdot,t)\right)(z).

Let
g
and
h
be as in Lemma
3.5
. We set for
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g}(z,t)=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}^{m}\left(\check{V}^{h}(\cdot,t)\right)(z).

Lemma
3.5
implies that this notation is coherent with that introduced in (
3.3
).

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

By conditioning over the
m
first coordinates of
y
, we get for
n\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{z}^{m}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\check{S}_{n}g((y\cdot a)\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}((y\cdot a)\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{z}^{m}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\check{S}_{n}h(T^{-m}(y\cdot a)\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{h\circ T^{-m}}((y\cdot a)\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{z}^{m}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\check{S}_{n}h(y\cdot(a\cdot z))\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{h}(y\cdot(a\cdot z))>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy),

where we have used the relations
(y\cdot a)\cdot z=T^{m}(y\cdot(a\cdot z))

and
\check{\tau}_{t}^{h\circ T^{-m}}=\check{\tau}_{t}^{h}\circ T^{-m}
.
The conclusion now follows from Lemma
3.4
and the definition of the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}^{m}.

∎

We will prove that the convergence in Lemma
3.5

holds in a weak sense for every function
g\in\mathscr{B}
.
The key step to prove Theorem
3.1
is the following technical lemma which shows that
the convergence of Lemma
3.5
holds for all functions
g\in\mathscr{B}
in a weak sense.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
g\in\mathscr{B}
is not a coboundary w.r.t. to
T
and
\nu(g)=0
.
Then, for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,
uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
, the following limit exists and is finite:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt.

Proof.

Assume that
g\in\mathscr{B}
. Let
(g_{m})_{m\geqslant 0}
,
c_{1}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
be as in Lemma
2.11
.
Set

\displaystyle W_{n}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

and

\displaystyle W_{n,m}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g_{m}(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{m}}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

By (
2.17
), we have the inclusions

\displaystyle\left\{\check{\tau}_{t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}^{g_{m}}>n\right\}\subseteq\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}>n\right\}\subseteq\left\{\check{\tau}_{t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}^{g_{m}}>n\right\},

which imply that

(3.8)

\displaystyle W_{n,m}(z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\leqslant W_{n}(z,t)\leqslant W_{n,m}(z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}).

In the same way, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle W_{n,m}(z,t)\leqslant W_{n,0}(z,t+2c_{1})\leqslant c_{2}+2\max\{t,0\},

where the last bound follows from (
3.6
).

By lemma
3.5
, for fixed
m\geqslant 0
, as
n\to\infty
,
the function
W_{n,m}(z,t)
converges to
\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t)
, uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
From (
3.8
) we get

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}W_{n}(z,t)\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}W_{n}(z,t)\leqslant\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}).

Now we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\left[\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m})-\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\varphi(t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})-\varphi(t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\right]\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t)dt.

Using (
3.9
) and Lemma
3.5
, we have that
\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t)\leqslant c_{2}+\max\{t,0\}
.
As
\varphi
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support,
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get that uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\left[\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m})-\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\right]dt=0.

This tells us that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)W_{n}(z,t)dt
has a uniform limit as
n\to\infty
.
∎

We will use the previous lemma to build a function
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)
.
The existence of this function will be deduced from the following elementary fact from the theory of distributions.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(V_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of non decreasing functions on
\mathbb{R}
.
Assume that for every continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,
the sequence
\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{n}(t)\varphi(t)dt
admits a finite limit.
Then there exists a unique right continuous and non decreasing function
V
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{n}(t)\varphi(t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(t)\varphi(t)dt.

Now we construct the function
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)
for any
g\in\mathscr{B}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume that
g\in\mathscr{B}
is not a coboundary w.r.t. to
T
and
\nu(g)=0
.
Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,
there exists a unique non decreasing and right continuous function
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

1. For any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,
uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt.

2. For any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,
the mapping
z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt
is continuous on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
.

3. There exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle t-c\leqslant\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)\leqslant t+c.

In addition, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\leqslant-c
, we have
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)=0
.

By Lemma
3.4
, in the case
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
, the notation
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)

is coherent with the one in (
3.3
).

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.6
, the following limit exists:
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set

(3.13)

\displaystyle\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

Then the function
\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,\cdot)
is non decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
.
By Lemma
3.7
, there exists a unique non decreasing and right continuous function
\check{V}^{g}(z,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}

such that for any continuous function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)\varphi(t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\varphi(t)dt.

Note that for
t<-\|g\|_{\infty}
,
we have
\check{\tau}^{g}_{t}=1
everywhere. Hence
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)=0
for
t\leqslant-c
.

We now prove (
3.11
).
By Proposition
2.6
, there exists
g_{0}\in\mathscr{B}^{+}

such that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(g_{0})=0
and
g
is cohomologous to
g_{0}
. By Lemma
2.11
, we can choose a constant
c>0
large enough such that for any
n\geqslant 1
,
it holds that
\|\check{S}_{n}g-\check{S}_{n}g_{0}\|_{\infty}\leqslant c
.
By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.6
, we have,
for any continuous non negative function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+c+\check{S}_{n}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\varphi(t)dt.

Note that from Lemma
3.3
, we have
\nu^{-}_{z}(y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
As we have the following inclusion: for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left\{\check{\tau}_{t-c}^{g_{0}}>n\right\}\subset\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}>n\right\}\subset\left\{\check{\tau}_{t+c}^{g_{0}}>n\right\},

and as
t+c+\check{S}_{n}g_{0}\geqslant 0
on the set
\{\check{\tau}_{t+c}^{g_{0}}>n\}
,
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g_{0}}(z,t-c)\varphi(t)dt\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\varphi(t)dt\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g_{0}}(z,t+c)\varphi(t)dt.

Since this holds for any continuous non-negative test function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,
we obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g_{0}}(z,t-c)\leqslant\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\leqslant\check{V}^{g_{0}}(z,t+c).

This, together with Lemma
3.7
, concludes the proof of (
3.11
).

We now want to prove the continuity in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
of the function
z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt
.
To this aim, we establish a uniform bound for the quantity
\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)
defined in (
3.13
).
Indeed, as usual, we have
\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)\leqslant\check{V}_{n}^{g_{0}}(z,t+c)
.
Now the optional stopping theorem gives

\displaystyle\check{V}_{n}^{g_{0}}(z,t)

\displaystyle=t\nu^{-}_{z}\left(y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{0}}(y\cdot z)>n\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{0}}(y\cdot z)}g_{0}(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{0}}(y\cdot z)\leqslant n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant|t|+|t|+\|g_{0}\|_{\infty}=2|t|+\|g_{0}\|_{\infty}.

From (
3.14
) we get

(3.15)

\displaystyle\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)\leqslant 2|t|+2c+\|g_{0}\|_{\infty}.

It remains to prove that for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,
the mapping
z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt
is continuous on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
It suffices to prove that for any
n\geqslant 1
,
the mapping
z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)dt
is continuous on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
.

A priori, for fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
z\mapsto\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)
is not continuous.
Nevertheless, we claim that it satisfies the following weak continuity property:
for
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
with
w(z,z^{\prime})\geqslant k

we have

\displaystyle\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t-\varepsilon)\leqslant\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z^{\prime},t)\leqslant\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t+\varepsilon).

Indeed, this follows from the inequality (
2.13
) in Lemma
2.9
.
This, together with the bound (
3.15
) and the uniform continuity of the function
\varphi
, implies that
the mapping
z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}_{n}^{g}(z,t)dt
is continuous on
\mathbb{X}^{+}
.
∎

The previous statements can be summarized as follows:

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
g
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\nu(g)=0
and
g
is not a coboundary.
Then there exists a unique non decreasing and right continuous function
\check{V}^{g}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
with the following properties:

1. For any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{X}}\check{S}_{n}g(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g}(t)dt.

2. There exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

(3.17)

\displaystyle\max\{t-c,0\}\leqslant\check{V}^{g}(t)\leqslant\max\{t,0\}+c.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\nu^{+}(dz).

Then the points 1 and 2 of Theorem
3.9

follow from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) in Lemma
3.8
, respectively.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

It is easy to see that Theorem
3.1
is equivalent to Theorem
3.9

for the reversed dynamics, i.e. by replacing
f
with
g=f\circ T^{-1}\circ\iota=f\circ\iota\circ T
, and
\nu
with
\iota_{*}\nu
.
∎

3.2.
Properties of the harmonic function

The goal of this section is to give some additional properties of the harmonic function
\check{V}^{g}

which will be necessary for the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
We start with a continuity result on the cohomology class of the function
g
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
with
\nu(g)=0
. Assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
(h_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be a sequence of element of
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
that converges to
0

with respect to the Hölder norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
.
For
n\geqslant 0
, set
g_{n}=g+h_{n}\circ T-h_{n}
.
Then, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
n\geqslant 0
,
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

(3.18)

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g_{n}}(z,t)\leqslant\max\{t,0\}+c.

Moreover, for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
, we have,
uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g_{n}}(z,t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt.

Proof.

The bound (
3.18
) follows from (
3.11
) and the relation
g_{n}=g+h_{n}\circ T-h_{n}
.
The construction of the function
\check{V}^{g}
in (
3.19
)
can be performed in the same way as in Lemmata
3.6
and
3.8
.
∎

We can also describe how the function
\check{V}^{g}
behaves when the function
g
is shifted by the dynamics.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
with
\nu(g)=0
. Assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g\circ T^{-1}}(z,t)=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}\left(V^{g}(\cdot,t)\right)(z).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.8
, for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\check{V}^{g\circ T^{-1}}(z,t)dt=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(T^{-1}(y\cdot z))\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g\circ T^{-1}}(y\cdot z)>n\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt.

By conditioning on the coordinate
y_{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(T^{-1}(y\cdot z))\mathds{1}_{\big\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g\circ T^{-1}}(y\cdot z)>n\big\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{A^{1}_{z}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(w\cdot(y_{-1}\cdot z))\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(w\cdot(y_{-1}\cdot z))>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)\nu^{1}_{z}(dy_{-1}).

Again by Lemma
3.8
, we obtain

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g\circ T^{-1}}(z,t)=\int_{A^{1}_{z}}\check{V}^{g}(y_{-1}\cdot z,t)\nu^{1}_{z}(dy_{-1})=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}\left(V^{g}(\cdot,t)\right)(z),

as desired.
∎

3.3.
The harmonic measure and the proof of Theorem
1.1

In the case when
g
depends only on the future (
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
),
the function
\check{V}^{g}
satisfies the harmonicity equation (
3.4
).
In general when
g
depends also on the past, this property may not hold.
It turns out that equation (
3.4
)
can be reinterpreted as a kind of invariance property of a certain Radon measure, which we will introduce at the end of this section.
Indeed, we have:

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
g
be in
\mathscr{B}^{+}
and let
V
be a locally integrable non-negative function on
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

For
\nu^{+}\otimes dt
almost every
(z,t)
in
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\displaystyle V(z,t)=\sum_{z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}:T(z^{\prime})=z}e^{-\psi(z^{\prime})}\mathds{1}_{\{t+g(z^{\prime})\geqslant 0\}}V(z^{\prime},t+g(z^{\prime})).

(2)

For any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,
we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(z,t)V(z,t)\nu^{+}(dz)dt=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(Tz,t-g(z))V(z,t)\nu^{+}(dz)dt.

Proof.

The proof is a direct computation. Indeed, for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,
by a change of variable, the right hand side of (
3.20
)
can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\varphi(Tz,t)\mathds{1}_{\{t+g(z)\geqslant 0\}}V(z,t+g(z))\nu^{+}(dz)dt.

As
\nu^{+}
is
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}
invariant,
by using (
2.4
), we get for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\varphi(Tz,t)\mathds{1}_{\{t+g(z)\geqslant 0\}}V(z,t+g(z))\nu^{+}(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\varphi(z,t)\left(\sum_{Tz^{\prime}=z}e^{-\psi(z^{\prime})}\mathds{1}_{\{t+g(z^{\prime})\geqslant 0\}}V(z^{\prime},t+g(z^{\prime}))\right)\nu^{+}(dz).

This proves the lemma.
∎

We will now show that the functions
\check{V}^{g}
and
V^{g}
can be seen as the densities with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}

of the projections on
\mathbb{R}

of certain natural Radon measures
\check{\mu}^{g}
and
\mu^{g}
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
,
which satisfy an invariance property similar to (
3.20
).
Those measures will play a key role in the statement of the conditioned local limit theorem.
The purpose of this subsection is to build them. This construction will follow the same lines as the one of the harmonic functions.
We will first use Markov chain arguments to define these objects when
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
and then use approximation arguments
to extend the definition to the general case.

We first assume that
g
is in
\mathscr{B}^{+}
. In that case, for
(z,t)\in\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
with
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)>0
,
let us introduce a Borel probability measure
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.
To do this, for
n\geqslant 1
, let
A^{n}_{z}
be as in the definition (
2.6
).
For
a\in A^{n}_{z}
, let us write
a\cdot z
for the element
\mathbb{X}^{+}

whose
n
first coordinates are
a_{-n},\ldots,a_{-1}
and whose
k
-th coordinate is
z_{k-n}
for
k\geqslant n
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
g
be in
\mathscr{B}^{+}
such that
\nu^{+}(g)=0
and
g
is not cohomologous to
0
.
Let
(z,t)
be in
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
with
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)>0
.
Then, there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}

such that for any
n\geqslant 0
and any
a\in A^{n}_{z}

we have

\displaystyle\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(\{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:y_{-n}=a_{-n},\ldots,y_{-1}=a_{-1}\})

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}\exp(-S_{n}\psi(a\cdot z))\check{V}^{g}(a\cdot z,t+S_{n}g(a\cdot z)),

as soon as
t+S_{k}g(T^{k}(a\cdot z))\geqslant 0
for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
.

Proof.

The proof is a translation of the general construction of the Markov measures
on the set of trajectories of a Markov chain.

Recall that, for
a\in A^{n}_{z}
, we denoted by
\mathbb{C}_{a,z}

(see (
2.5
))
the associated cylinder of length
n
in
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.
For
n\geqslant 0
, define
\check{\mu}^{g,n}_{z,t}
as a function on cylinders of length
n
in
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}

by the formula

\displaystyle\check{\mu}^{g,n}_{z,t}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z})=\frac{1}{\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}\exp(-S_{n}\psi(a\cdot z))\check{V}^{g}(a\cdot z,t+S_{n}g(a\cdot z)),

if
t+S_{k}g(T^{k}(a\cdot z))\geqslant 0
for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
;
if not, we set
\check{\mu}^{g,n}_{z,t}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z})=0
,
(compare with (
2.6
)).
We claim that for any
a\in A^{n}_{z}
, we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle\check{\mu}^{g,n}_{z,t}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in A\\
M(b,a_{-n})=1\end{subarray}}\check{\mu}^{g,n+1}_{z,t}(\mathbb{C}_{b\cdot a,z}),

(compare with (
2.7
)).
Indeed, this follows from the harmonicity property of the function
\check{V}^{g}
established in Lemma
3.2
.
By Kolmogorov’s extension theorem, equation (
3.22
) implies that
there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}

such that for any
n\geqslant 0
,

\check{\mu}^{g,n}_{z,t}
is the restriction of
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}
to cylinders of length
n
.
The lemma follows.
∎

In the same way as for the function
\check{V}^{g}
, we can
give an alternative definition of the measures
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}
, which relies on
a convergence property.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
with
\nu(g)=0
and assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Let
(z,t)
be in
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}

and
\varphi
be a continuous function on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.
Then, we have

(3.23)

\displaystyle\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(\varphi)\check{V}^{g}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\varphi(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, the limit in equation (
3.23
) is the same as the limit of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\varphi(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

The latter quantity is non-negative whenever
\varphi
is non-negative.
Besides, if
\varphi=1
, the convergence follows from Lemma
3.4
.
Therefore, it suffices to check the convergence when
\varphi
is the indicator function of a cylinder set.
Thus, let
m\geqslant 0
be an integer. Pick
a\in A^{m}_{z}
and let
\mathbb{C}_{a,z}
be the associated cylinder in
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.
If
S_{k}g(T^{k}(a\cdot z))<0
for some
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
, we have for
n\geqslant m
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{C}_{a,z}}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)=0.

If not, we have for
n\geqslant m
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{C}_{a,z}}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)=\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n-m}g(y\cdot a\cdot z)+S_{m}g(a\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t+S_{m}g(a\cdot z)}^{g}(y\cdot a\cdot z)>n-m\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy).

By Lemma
3.4
, as
n\to\infty
, this converges to

\displaystyle\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\check{V}^{g}(a\cdot z,t+S_{m}g(a\cdot z)),

which, by the definition of
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}
in Lemma
3.13
,
is equal to
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z})\check{V}^{g}(z,t)
.
∎

Using Lemma
3.14
, we can now give a definition of
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}

for a function
g
only depending on finitely many negative coordinates.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
such that
\nu(g)=0
and
g
is not a coboundary.
Assume that
g
only depends on
m
negative coordinates for some
m\geqslant 0
.
In other words, the function
h=g\circ T^{m}\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
.
Let
(z,t)
be in
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\varphi
be a continuous function on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
.
For
a\in A^{m}_{z}
, set
\varphi_{a}
to be the function
y\mapsto\varphi(y\cdot a)
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\varphi(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A^{m}_{z}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\check{V}^{h}(a\cdot z,t)\check{\mu}^{h,-}_{a\cdot z,t}(\varphi_{a}).

Before proving this lemma, we recall some useful facts.
Let
g
and
h
be as in Lemma
3.15
.
For
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\check{V}^{g}(z,t)=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}^{m}\left(\check{V}^{h}(\cdot,t)\right)(z)=\sum_{a\in A^{m}_{z}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\check{V}^{h}(a\cdot z,t).

If
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)>0
and
\varphi
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
, we set

(3.24)

\displaystyle\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}\sum_{a\in A^{m}_{z}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\check{V}^{h}(a\cdot z,t)\check{\mu}^{h,-}_{a\cdot z,t}(\varphi_{a}).

Lemma
3.15
implies that
the notation (
3.24
)
is coherent with that introduced in Lemma
3.13
.

Proof of Lemma
3.15
.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.5
, by conditioning over the
m
first coordinates of
y
, we get for
n\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\varphi(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{z}^{m}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\check{S}_{n}g((y\cdot a)\cdot z)\varphi(y\cdot a)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}((y\cdot a)\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{z}^{m}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\check{S}_{n}h(T^{-m}(y\cdot a)\cdot z)\varphi_{a}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{h\circ T^{-m}}((y\cdot a)\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{z}^{m}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\check{S}_{n}h(y\cdot(a\cdot z))\varphi_{a}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{h}(y\cdot(a\cdot z))>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy),

where we have used the relations
(y\cdot a)\cdot z=T^{m}(y\cdot(a\cdot z))

and
\check{\tau}_{t}^{h\circ T^{-m}}=\check{\tau}_{t}^{h}\circ T^{-m}
.
The conclusion now follows from Lemma
3.14
.
∎

Now we prove that the convergence in Lemma
3.15

holds in a weak sense for every function
g\in\mathscr{B}
.

Lemma 3.16
.

Assume that
g\in\mathscr{B}
is not a coboundary w.r.t. to
T
and
\nu(g)=0
.
Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}\times\mathbb{R}
,
the following limit exists and is finite:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\varphi(y,t)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt.

Proof.

First let us assume that
\varphi
is of the form
(y,t)\mapsto\varphi_{1}(y)\varphi_{2}(t)
,
where
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are non-negative continuous functions on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
and
\mathbb{R}
,
and
\varphi_{2}
is compactly supported.
In that case, let
(g_{m})_{m\geqslant 0}
,
c_{1}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
be as in Lemma
2.11
.
Set

\displaystyle W_{n}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\varphi_{1}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

and

\displaystyle W_{n,m}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g_{m}(y\cdot z)\right)\varphi_{1}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{m}}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

By (
2.17
), we have the inclusions

\displaystyle\left\{\check{\tau}_{t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}^{g_{m}}>n\right\}\subseteq\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}>n\right\}\subseteq\left\{\check{\tau}_{t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}^{g_{m}}>n\right\},

which imply that

(3.25)

\displaystyle W_{n,m}(z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\leqslant W_{n}(z,t)\leqslant W_{n,m}(z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}).

By lemma
3.15
, for fixed
m\geqslant 0
, as
n\to\infty
,
the function
W_{n,m}(z,t)
converges to
\check{\mu}^{g_{m},-}_{z,t}(\varphi_{1})\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t)
.
From (
3.25
) we get

\displaystyle\check{\mu}^{g_{m},-}_{z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}(\varphi_{1})\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}W_{n}(z,t)\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}W_{n}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\check{\mu}^{g_{m},-}_{z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}(\varphi_{1})\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}).

Now we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{2}(t)\left[\check{\mu}^{g_{m},-}_{z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}(\varphi_{1})\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m})-\check{\mu}^{g_{m},-}_{z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m}}(\varphi_{1})\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\right]dt

(3.26)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\varphi_{2}(t-2c_{1}\alpha^{m})-\varphi_{2}(t+2c_{1}\alpha^{m})\right]\check{\mu}^{g_{m},-}_{z,t\alpha^{m}}(\varphi_{1})\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t)dt.

Using (
3.9
) and Lemma
3.5
, we have that
\check{V}^{g_{m}}(z,t)\leqslant c_{2}+\max\{t,0\}
.
As
\varphi_{2}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support,
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get
that the left hand side of (
3.3
) converges to
0
as
m\to\infty
.
This tells us that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{2}(t)W_{n}(z,t)dt
has a limit as
n\to\infty
.
In other words, the lemma holds for the function
\varphi(y,t)=\varphi_{1}(y)\varphi_{2}(t)
.
This is also true when
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are not necessarily non-negative.

The general case follows from a standard but tedious approximation argument.
Indeed, we can find a continuous compactly supported function
\theta
on
\mathbb{R}
with support
K

such that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist an integer
p\geqslant 0
and
continuous functions
\varphi_{i,1}
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}

and continuous compactly supported functions
\varphi_{i,2}
on
\mathbb{R}
with support included in
K
,

1\leqslant i\leqslant p
, with

(3.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left|\varphi(y,t)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,t)\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\theta(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\varphi_{i,1}(y)\varphi_{i,2}(t)
.
We set
t_{0}=\sup_{t\in K}|t|
. By Lemma
3.3
, we need to show that

\displaystyle U_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t_{0}+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\varphi(y,t)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt

has a limit as
n\to\infty
.
By the first case, we know that

\displaystyle U_{n,\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t_{0}+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,t)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt

has a limit
U_{\varepsilon}
as
n\to\infty
.
Besides, by Lemma
3.6
, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left(t_{0}+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\theta(t)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt

converges to
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\theta(t)dt
.
By (
3.27
), we have

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\theta(t)dt\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}U_{n}\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}\leqslant U_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\theta(t)dt,

which gives

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}-\liminf_{n\to\infty}U_{n}\leqslant 2\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\theta(t)dt.

Hence the proof of Lemma
3.16
is complete.
∎

Now we use the previous lemma to build a Radon measure
\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}

on
\mathbb{X}_{z}^{-}\times\mathbb{R}
for any
g\in\mathscr{B}
.

Lemma 3.17
.

Assume that
g\in\mathscr{B}
is not a coboundary w.r.t. to
T
and
\nu(g)=0
.
Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,
there exists a unique Radon measure
\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}
on
\mathbb{X}_{z}^{-}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}_{z}^{-}\times\mathbb{R}
,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y,t)\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y,t)\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}(dy,dt).

Besides, the marginal measure of
\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}
on
\mathbb{R}
under the natural projection map
is the absolutely continuous measure
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.16
, the limit on the left hand side of (
3.28
) exists.
By Lemma
3.3
, the limit is the same as the one of

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y,t)\left(t_{0}+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt,

where
t_{0}>0
is arbitrarily large.
In particular, this limit is non-negative.
By Riesz representation theorem, it may be written as
\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}(\varphi)
,
where
\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}
is a Radon measure on
\mathbb{X}_{z}^{-}\times\mathbb{R}
.
By Lemma
3.8
, the marginal measure of
\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}
on
\mathbb{R}

under the natural projection map
is the absolutely continuous measure
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt
.
∎

We define the Radon measure
\check{\mu}^{g}
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
by setting,
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\check{\mu}^{g}(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y\cdot z,t)\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}(dy,dt)\nu^{+}(dz).

The main result of this section is stated as follows.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
g
be a Hölder continuous function on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\nu(g)=0
and
g
is not a coboundary.
Then, for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
, we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{S}_{n}g(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt=\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

Moreover, the following harmonicity property holds:

(3.30)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt)=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(Tx,t-g(x))\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

Proof.

We can assume that
\varphi
is non-negative.
By Lemma
3.17
, for every
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y\cdot z,t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y\cdot z,t)\check{\mu}_{z}^{g,-}(dy,dt).

Thanks to the dominated convergence theorem, this will imply (
3.29
).
Indeed, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set
\theta(t)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\varphi(x,t)
, so that

\theta
is a continuous compactly supported function on
\mathbb{R}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y\cdot z,t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt.

By Lemma
3.8
, we have, uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(t)\check{V}^{g}(z,t)dt.

By the dominated convergence theorem, we get (
3.29
).

Now we prove (
3.30
).
By (
3.29
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{S}_{n}g(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt.

As
\nu
is
T
-invariant, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{S}_{n}g(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(Tx,t)\left(\check{S}_{n-1}g(x)+g(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t+g(x)}^{g}(x)>n-1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{t+g(x)\geqslant 0\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(Tx,t-g(x))\left(\check{S}_{n-1}g(x)+g(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n-1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{t\geqslant 0\}}\nu(dx)dt.

By Lemma
3.3
, the latter has the same limit, as
n\to\infty
, as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(Tx,t-g(x))\check{S}_{n-1}g(x)\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n-1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{t\geqslant 0\}}\nu(dx)dt.

We will prove below that we can apply (
3.29
)
to the function
(x,t)\mapsto\varphi(Tx,t-g(x))\mathds{1}_{\{t\geqslant 0\}}

to get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(Tx,t-g(x))\check{S}_{n-1}g(x)\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n-1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{t\geqslant 0\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(Tx,t-g(x))\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt),

which proves (
3.30
).

To finish the proof, we need to show that (
3.29
) implies
that for any continuous compact supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
,
as
n\to\infty
, the quantity

\displaystyle I_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(x,t)\check{S}_{n}g(x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

converges to

(3.31)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

This is a standard argument by an approximation.
Indeed, for
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set
\chi_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t)=0
if
t<0
;
\chi_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t)=\frac{t}{\varepsilon}
if
0\leqslant t\leqslant\varepsilon

and
\chi_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t)=1
if
t>\varepsilon
.
Define also
\chi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(t)=\chi_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t+\varepsilon)
.
Then, for any
n\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\chi_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t)\varphi(x,t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle\leqslant I_{n}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\chi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(t)\varphi(x,t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt.

By (
3.30
) and Lemma
3.3
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\chi_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t)\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt)

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_{n}\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{n}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\chi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(t)\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

We claim that the left and right hand sides of the latter inequality converge to the integral in (
3.31
).
Indeed, for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
, we have that
|\chi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(t)\varphi(x,t)|
and
|\chi_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t)\varphi(x,t)|

are dominated by
|\varphi(x,t)|
.
The conclusion now follows from the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

As for the function
\check{V}^{g}
, the measure
\check{\mu}^{g}
enjoys the following
continuity property on cohomology classes.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
with
\nu(g)=0
. Assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
(h_{m})_{m\geqslant 0}
be a sequence of element of
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
that converges to
0

with respect to the Hölder norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
.
For
m\geqslant 0
, set
g_{m}=g+h_{m}\circ T-h_{m}
.
Then, for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
, we have,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g_{m}}(dx,dt)=\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

Proof.

We can assume that
\varphi
is non-negative.
By Theorem
3.18
, for
m\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\mu^{g_{m}}(dx,dt)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g_{m}(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{m}}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt.

For any
n\geqslant 0
, we have

S_{n}g_{m}\leqslant S_{n}g+2\|h_{m}\|_{\infty}
. Hence, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have
\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{m}}\leqslant\check{\tau}_{t+2\|h_{m}\|_{\infty}}^{g}
.
We get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g_{m}(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g_{m}}(x)>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\left(t+\check{S}_{n}g_{m}(x)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t+2\|h_{m}\|_{\infty}}^{g}>n\right\}}\nu(dx)dt.

Again by Theorem
3.18
, as
n\to\infty
, the latter quantity converges to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t-2\|h_{m}\|_{\infty})\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g_{m}}(dx,dt)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t-2\|h_{m}\|_{\infty})\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

In the same way, one also has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)\check{\mu}^{g_{m}}(dx,dt)\geqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t+2\|h_{m}\|_{\infty})\check{\mu}^{g}(dx,dt).

As
\varphi
is continuous, the conclusion follows from the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

So far we have proved Theorem
3.18

which is an analogue of Theorem
1.1

for the reversed dynamical system
(\mathbb{X},T^{-1},\nu)
.
By Lemma
2.5
, this dynamical system is isomorphic to a subshift of finite type equipped with a Gibbs measure.
Therefore, Theorem
1.1
is actually equivalent to Theorem
3.18
.
Formally, the former can be obtained from the latter
by replacing
f
with
g=f\circ T^{-1}\circ\iota=f\circ\iota\circ T
, and
\nu
with
\iota_{*}\nu
.
∎

The reader may notice that (
3.20
) is a particular case of (
1.3
),
which is the reason to call the Radon measure
\mu^{f}
harmonic.

4.
Conditioned limit theorems

In this section we prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

As in the construction of the harmonic function
\check{V}^{g}
and the harmonic measure
\check{\mu}^{g}
,
we will prove Theorem
1.3
in several steps.
The first step is to deal with the case of functions
g
depending only on the future. The following result follows from the general result
for Markov chains established in
[
13
, Theorem 2.3]
.
The assumptions of this statement can be checked to hold thanks to the spectral gap properties of the Ruelle
operator formulated in Section
5.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
with
\nu(g)=0
and assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Then, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have, uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{g}\sqrt{2\pi n}\,\nu^{-}_{z}\left(y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right)=2\check{V}^{g}(z,t).

We have to strengthen Lemma
4.1
by proving the following integral form:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
with
\nu(g)=0
and assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Then, for any
(z,t)\in\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
and
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{g}\sqrt{2\pi n}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)=2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y)\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(dy).

Proof.

It suffices to prove this result when
\varphi
is the indicator function of a cylinder set in
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
,
since the general case follows by a standard approximation argument.
Thus, let
m\geqslant 0
and
a\in A_{z}^{m}
and, as before, denote by
\mathbb{C}_{a,z}

the associated cylinder in
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
(see (
2.5
)).

If
t+S_{k}g(T^{m-k}(a\cdot z))\geqslant 0
for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{g}\sqrt{2\pi n}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{C}_{a,z}}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{g}\sqrt{2\pi n}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t+S_{m}g(a\cdot z)}^{g}(y\cdot(a\cdot z))>n-m\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy).

By Lemma
4.1
, as
n\to\infty
, the latter quantity converges to

\displaystyle 2\check{V}^{g}(a\cdot z,t+S_{m}g(a\cdot z))\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z)),

which, by definition, is equal to
2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z}).

If there exists
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
with
t+S_{k}g(T^{m-k}(a\cdot z))<0
,
we have
\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(\mathbb{C}_{a,z})=0

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{C}_{a,z}}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)=0,

for
n>k
.
The conclusion follows.
∎

From Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
,
we deduce the analogous result for functions which depend only on finitely many negative coordinates.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
g\in\mathscr{B}
be such that
\nu(g)=0
and there exists
m\geqslant 0
with
g\circ T^{m}\in\mathscr{B}^{+}
.
Assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Then, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have, uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{g}\sqrt{2\pi n}\,\nu^{-}_{z}\left(y\in\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}:\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right)=2\check{V}^{g}(z,t).

Moreover, for any
(z,t)\in\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
and
for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{g}\sqrt{2\pi n}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)=2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y)\check{\mu}^{g,-}_{z,t}(dy).

Proof.

As in Lemma
3.15
,
for
a\in A_{z}^{m}
, let
\varphi_{a}
be the continuous function
y\mapsto\varphi(a\cdot y)
on
\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}
.
We have, by setting
h=g\circ T^{m}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\varphi(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A^{m}_{z}}\exp(-S_{m}\psi(a\cdot z))\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{a\cdot z}}\varphi_{a}(y)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{h}(y\cdot(a\cdot z))>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{a\cdot z}(dy).

The conclusion now follows from Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
and (
3.24
).
∎

Now we use the same approximation argument as before to deduce from Lemma
4.3

a slightly weaker statement that works for every function
g
in
\mathscr{B}
.
This is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

5.
Effective local limit theorems

So far we have adapted some results from the theory of Markov chains to the case of hyperbolic dynamical systems
by constructing the analogs of the harmonic functions
V^{g}
and
\check{V}^{g}

and building the harmonic measures
\mu^{g}
and
\check{\mu}^{g}
.
In the remaining part of the paper we shall use these objects to establish conditioned limit theorems,
by adapting the strategy from the case of sums of independent random variables
[
16
]
.
We start with formulating an effective version of the ordinary local limit theorem which is adapted to our needs.

5.1.
Spectral gap theory

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\psi\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
, where
\psi
is the potential function used for the construction of the Gibbs measure
\nu
(see Section
2.1
).
Denote by
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha},\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha})

the set of all bounded linear operators from
\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
to
\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}

equipped with the standard operator norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}\to\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}
.
From the general construction of the Ruelle operator,
every
f\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
gives rise to a family of perturbed operators

(\mathcal{L}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf})
defined as follows:
for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}\varphi(z)=\sum_{z^{\prime}:\,Tz^{\prime}=z}e^{-\psi(z^{\prime})-\mathbf{i}tf(z^{\prime})}\varphi(z^{\prime}),\quad z\in\mathbb{X}^{+},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

By iteration, it follows that for any
\psi,f\in\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}\varphi(z)=\sum_{z^{\prime}:\,T^{n}z^{\prime}=z}e^{-S_{n}(\psi+\mathbf{i}tf)(z^{\prime})}\varphi(z^{\prime}),\quad z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}.

The following result (see
[
22
]
) provides the spectral gap properties for the perturbed operator
\mathcal{L}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}
.
For similar statements in the case of Markov chains we refer to
[
19
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
f\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
is not a coboundary and that
\nu(f)=0
.
Then, there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that for any
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}=\lambda^{n}_{t}\Pi_{t}+N^{n}_{t},\quad n\geqslant 1,

where the mappings
t\mapsto\Pi_{t}:(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha},\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha})

and
z\mapsto N_{t}:(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha},\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha})
are analytic
in the operator norm topology,

\Pi_{t}
is a rank-one projection with

\Pi_{0}(\varphi)(z)=\nu^{+}(\varphi)
for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
and
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\Pi_{t}N_{t}=N_{t}\Pi_{t}=0
.
Moreover, there exist
n_{0}\geqslant 1
and
q\in(0,1)
such that for any
t\in(-\delta,\delta)

the
\|N_{t}^{n_{0}}\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}\to\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant q
.

The eigenvalue
\lambda_{t}
has the asymptotic expansion: as
t\to 0
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}=1-\frac{\sigma_{f}^{2}}{2}t^{2}+O(|t|^{3}).

Note that since
f
is not a coboundary w.r.t. to
T
,
the asymptotic variance
\sigma_{f}^{2}
appearing in (
5.3
) is strictly positive.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
and
t\neq 0
.
Assume that for any
p\neq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
pf+q
is not cohomologous to a function with values in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Then,
for any
t\neq 0
, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\psi+{\mathbf{i}}tf}
has spectral radius strictly less than
1

in
\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
.
More precisely, for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
there exist constants
c_{K},c_{K}^{\prime}>0
such that for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
and
n\geqslant 1
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in K}\|\mathcal{L}_{\psi+{\mathbf{i}}tf}^{n}\varphi\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant c_{K}^{\prime}e^{-c_{K}n}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}.

Proof.

The proof of the first assertion can be found in
[
22
, Theorem 4.5]
.
Now we prove (
5.4
).
For every
t\in K
, there exist
n_{0}(t)\geqslant 1
and
\alpha(t)\in(0,1)
such that

\|\mathcal{L}_{\psi+{\mathbf{i}}tf}^{n_{0}(t)}\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}\to\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}<\alpha(t)
.
As the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\psi+{\mathbf{i}}tf}
depends continuously on
t
for the operator norm topology,
there exists
\delta=\delta(t)
such that for any
s\in(t-\delta(t),t+\delta(t))
, we still have

\|\mathcal{L}_{\psi+{\mathbf{i}}sf}^{n_{0}(t)}\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}\to\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}<1
.
In particular, for every
n\geqslant 0
we have

\|\mathcal{L}_{\psi+{\mathbf{i}}sf}^{n}\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}\to\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant c(t)\alpha(t)^{n/n_{0}(t)}
, for some
c(t)>0
.
By compactness, we can find
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in K
such that
K\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(t_{i}-\delta(t_{i}),t_{i}+\delta(t_{i}))
.
In particular, by setting
c=\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}c(t_{i})
,
\alpha=\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}\alpha(t_{i})

and
n_{0}=\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}n_{0}(t_{i})
, we get for any
s\in K
and
n\geqslant 0
,

\|\mathcal{L}_{\psi+{\mathbf{i}}sf}^{n}\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}\to\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant c\alpha^{n/n_{0}}
.
∎

5.2.
Local limit theorem for smooth target functions

In the following we establish a local limit theorem for Markov chains with a precise estimation of the remainder term.
Let
F
be a measurable non-negative bounded target function on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The probability we are interested in can be written as follows: for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}F\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

The main difficulty is to give a local limit theorem with the explicit dependence of the remainder terms on
F
.

We first describe the kind of target functions that we will use.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space.
Let
F
be a real-valued function on
X\times\mathbb{R}
such that

1.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
z\mapsto F(z,t)
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
X
.

2.

For any
z\in X
, the function
t\mapsto F(z,t)
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}
.

Then, the function
(z,t)\mapsto F(z,t)
is measurable on
X\times\mathbb{R}
and
the function
t\mapsto\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{\alpha}
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}
,
where the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
is the usual norm on the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
X
.
Moreover, if the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{\alpha}dt
is finite,
we define the partial Fourier transform
\widehat{F}
of
F
by setting for any
z\in X
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

\widehat{F}(z,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-itu}F(z,t)dt.

This is a continuous function on
X\times\mathbb{R}
.
In addition, for every
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
z\mapsto\widehat{F}(z,u)
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
and
\|\widehat{F}(\cdot,u)\|_{\alpha}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{\alpha}dt
.

Proof.

Since the space
X
is separable and the function
z\mapsto F(z,t)
is continuous on
X
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
the supremum
\sup_{z\in X}|F(z,t)|

can be taken over a countable dense subset, so that

t\mapsto\sup_{z\in X}|F(z,t)|
is measurable.
In the same way,
since the function
z\mapsto F(z,t)
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
X
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
one can also verify that
\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in X}\frac{|F\left(z,t\right)-F\left(z^{\prime},t\right)|}{\alpha^{\omega(z,z^{\prime})}}

is a measurable function in

.

In case the integral

is finite,
the partial Fourier transform

is well defined and continuous by the dominated convergence theorem.
The norm domination is obvious.
∎

We denote by
\mathscr{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
the set of real-valued functions on

such that conditions (1) and (2) of Lemma

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Assume that
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
such that
\nu^{+}(g)=0

and for any
p\neq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{R}

the function
pg+q

\mathbb{Z}

Let

,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{g}}\phi\left(\frac{u}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}\right)F\left(z^{\prime},u\right)du\nu^{+}(dz^{\prime})\bigg|\leqslant\frac{c_{K}}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}dt.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\sigma_{g}=1
.
By the Fourier inversion formula, the Fubini theorem and a change of variable
t

\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}
,
we get

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}F\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}\times\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mathbf{i}t\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)}\widehat{F}((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},t)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)dt

Denote

\displaystyle J(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}e^{-\frac{\mathbf{i}t}{\sqrt{n}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)}\widehat{F}\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\leqslant\delta\sqrt{n}}J(t)dt,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\delta\sqrt{n}<|t|}\left[\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}e^{-\frac{\mathbf{i}t}{\sqrt{n}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)}\widehat{F}\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)\right]dt,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\leqslant\delta\sqrt{n}}\left[e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\widehat{F}\left(z^{\prime},\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{+}(dz^{\prime})\right]dt.

with Hölder norm at most

, for any fixed

.
Applying (

\displaystyle J(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\frac{\mathbf{i}t}{\sqrt{n}}g}\widehat{F}\left(\cdot,\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)(z)-e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\widehat{F}\left(z^{\prime},\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{+}(dz^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\left(\Pi_{\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}}-\Pi_{0}\right)\widehat{F}\left(\cdot,\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)(z)+N^{n}_{\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}}\widehat{F}\left(\cdot,\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)(z)

\displaystyle=:J_{1}(t)+J_{2}(t)+J_{3}(t).

\displaystyle\left|J_{1}(t)\right|\leqslant c\left|\lambda^{n}_{\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}}-e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\right|\sup_{|t^{\prime}|\leqslant\delta}\left\|\widehat{F}\left(\cdot,t^{\prime}\right)\right\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,u)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}du.

For the second and third terms, using again Lemma

\displaystyle\left|J_{2}(t)\right|+\left|J_{3}(t)\right|\leqslant C\left(\frac{|t|}{\sqrt{n}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}+e^{-cn}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,u)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}du.

Therefore, we obtain the following upper bound for

(5.6)

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leqslant\left(\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}+Ce^{-cn}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,u)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}du\leqslant\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,u)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}du.

Estimate of
I_{2}.

Since the function

is compactly supported on

is a constant not depending on

,
we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\left[\int_{\delta\sqrt{n}<|t|\leqslant C_{1}\sqrt{n}}e^{-\frac{\mathbf{i}t}{\sqrt{n}}\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)}\widehat{F}\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)dt\right]\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2\pi}\int_{\delta<|t|\leqslant C_{1}}\left[\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}\widehat{F}\left(\cdot,t\right)(z)\right]dt.

Note that, for any

satisfying

,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}\left|\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}\widehat{F}\left(\cdot,t\right)(z)\right|\leqslant\left\|\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}\widehat{F}\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant\left\|\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}\right\|_{\mathcal{L(\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha},\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha})}}\|\widehat{F}(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}.

Then, by Lemma
5.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\delta<|t|\leqslant C_{1}}\sqrt{n}\left\|\mathcal{L}^{n}_{\psi+\mathbf{i}tf}\right\|_{\mathcal{L(\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha},\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha})}}dt\sup_{|t^{\prime}|\in[\delta,C_{1}]}\|\widehat{F}(\cdot,t^{\prime})\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{K}^{\prime}\sqrt{n}e^{-c_{K}n}\sup_{|t^{\prime}|\in[\varepsilon,C_{1}]}\|\widehat{F}(\cdot,t^{\prime})\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant c_{K}^{\prime}e^{-c_{K}n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}dt.

Estimate of

Notice that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\widehat{F}\left(z,\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{+}(dz)\right]dt

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|>\delta\sqrt{n}}\left[e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\widehat{F}\left(z,\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{+}(dz)\right]dt.

For the first term, by the Fourier inversion formula,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\widehat{F}\left(z,\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{+}(dz)dt=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2n}}F\left(z,t\right)dt\nu^{+}(dz).

For the second term, using the fact that

,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|>\delta\sqrt{n}}\left[e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\widehat{F}\left(z,\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\nu^{+}(dz)\right]dt

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|>\delta\sqrt{n}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}dt\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}}|F(z,u)|du\nu^{+}(dz)\leqslant ce^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,u)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}du.

Combining (

), (

), (

) and (
5.2
),
and taking into account that

5.3.
Local limit theorem for
\varepsilon
-dominated target functions

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
f,g
be functions on
\mathbb{R}
.
We say that the function
g

\varepsilon
-dominates the function
f

(or
f

\varepsilon
-minorates
g
) if for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(t)\leqslant g(t+v),\quad\forall\ |v|\leqslant\varepsilon.

In this case we write
f\leqslant_{\varepsilon}g
or
g\geqslant_{\varepsilon}f
.
For any functions
F
and
G
on
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, we say that
F\leqslant_{\varepsilon}G
if
F(z,\cdot)\leqslant_{\varepsilon}G(z,\cdot)

for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
.

In the proofs we make use of the following assertion.
Denote by
\rho
the non-negative density function on
\mathbb{R}
,
which is the Fourier transform of the function
(1-|t|)\mathds{1}_{|t|\leqslant 1}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Set
\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho(\frac{u}{\varepsilon})
for
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

be integrable functions and that
f\leqslant_{\varepsilon}g
.
Then for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle f(u)\leqslant\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}g*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u),\quad g(u)\geqslant f*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)-\int_{|v|>\varepsilon}f\left(u-v\right)\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(v)dv.

Remark 5.6
.

The domination property
\leqslant_{\varepsilon}
implies, in particular, that if
f\leqslant_{\varepsilon}g
and the function
g
is integrable, then
f
is bounded and

\lim_{u\to\infty}f(u)=0
,
\lim_{u\to-\infty}f(u)=0
.
Indeed, since
f\leqslant_{\varepsilon}g
and
g
is an integrable function,
by Lemma
5.5
we have
f\leqslant\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}g*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}
.
Since the Fourier transform of
g*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}
is compactly supported on
[-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}},\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}]
,
by the Fourier inversion formula,

\displaystyle|g*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(x)|=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{itx}\widehat{g}(t)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)dt\right|\leqslant c.

Therefore,
g*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}
, so that
f
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}
.

Below, for any function
F\in\mathscr{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
, we use the notation

\displaystyle F*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(z,t-v)\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(v)dv,\quad z\in\mathbb{X}^{+},\ t\in\mathbb{R},

and

\displaystyle\|F\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(\cdot,u)\|_{\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}}du,\qquad\|F\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|F(z,u)|du\nu^{+}(dz).

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

and

6.
Effective conditioned local limit theorems

6.1.
Formulation of the result

We will prove the following conditioned local limit theorem for Markov chains which provides a rate of order
n^{-1}
.
This result will serve as an intermediate step between the conditioned central limit Theorem
1.5

and the conditioned local limit Theorem
1.7
.
The interest of this result lies in the fact that it is uniform in the function
F
.
In particular, the theorem is effective when the support of the function
F
moves to infinity with the rate
\sqrt{n}
.
This strategy is inspired by
[
7
]
for random walks in cones of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
see also
[
14
]
for finite Markov chains
and
[
16
]
for random walks on
\mathbb{R}
.
For a different approach based on the Wiener-Hopf factorisation we refer to
[
6
,
10
,
28
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
be such that
\nu^{+}(g)=0
.
Assume that for any
p\neq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
pg+q
is not cohomologous to a function with values in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then, there exist a constant
c>0
and a sequence
(r_{n})
of positive numbers satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}=0

with the following properties.
For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{8})
,
there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for any
n\geqslant 1
,
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\leqslant t_{0}
,

1.

For any non-negative function
F

and any function
G\in\mathscr{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
satisfying
F\leqslant_{\varepsilon}G
,

\displaystyle n\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}F\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}{\sigma_{g}^{2}\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z^{\prime},u^{\prime}\right)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{u^{\prime}}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}\right)du^{\prime}\nu^{+}(dz^{\prime})

(6.1)

\displaystyle\quad+c\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}+\frac{r_{n}}{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\right)\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}+\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{n}}\|G\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}.

2.

For any non-negative function
F
and non-negative functions
G,H\in\mathscr{H}_{\alpha}^{+}

satisfying
H\leqslant_{\varepsilon}F\leqslant_{\varepsilon}G
,

\displaystyle n\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}F\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}{\sigma_{g}^{2}\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H(z^{\prime},u^{\prime})\phi^{+}\left(\frac{u^{\prime}}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}\right)du^{\prime}\nu^{+}(dz^{\prime})

(6.2)

\displaystyle\qquad-c\left(\varepsilon^{1/12}+\frac{r_{n}}{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\right)\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}-\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\left\|G\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}+\left\|H\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}\right).

6.2.
Auxiliary statements

The normal density of variance
v>0
is denoted by

\displaystyle\phi_{v}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2v}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

and the Rayleigh density with scale parameter
\sqrt{v}
is denoted by

\displaystyle\phi^{+}_{v}(x)=\frac{x}{v}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2v}}\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

The standard normal density is denoted by
\phi(x)=\phi_{1}(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
The following lemma from
[
16
]
shows that when
v
is small the convolution
\phi_{v}*\phi^{+}_{1-v}

behaves like the Rayleigh density.

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
v\in(0,1/2]
and
x\geqslant 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-v}\phi^{+}(x)\leqslant\phi_{v}*\phi^{+}_{1-v}(x)\leqslant\sqrt{1-v}\phi^{+}(x)+\sqrt{v}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2v}}.

We need the following inequality of Haeusler
[
17
, Lemma 1]
,
which is a generalisation of Fuk’s inequality for martingales.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
be a martingale difference sequence with respect to the non-decreasing

\sigma
-fields
\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathscr{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{F}_{n}
.
Then, for all
u,v,w>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{i}\right|\geqslant u\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left(|\xi_{i}|>v\right)+2\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\xi_{i}^{2}|\mathscr{F}_{i-1}\right)>w\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2\exp\left\{\frac{u}{v}\left(1-\log\frac{uv}{w}\right)\right\}.

In order to control certain natural quantities appearing in the proof,
we shall need the following definitions.
For
\varepsilon>0
,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\chi_{\varepsilon}(u)=0\ \mbox{for}\ u\leqslant-\varepsilon,\ \chi_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{u+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\ \mbox{for}\ u\in(-\varepsilon,0),\ \chi_{\varepsilon}(u)=1\ \mbox{for}\ u\geqslant 0.

Denote
\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}(u)=1-\chi_{\varepsilon}(u)
and note that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\chi_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon\right)\leqslant\mathds{1}_{(0,\infty)}\left(t\right)\leqslant\chi_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right),\quad\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\leqslant\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]}\left(t\right)\leqslant\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon\right).

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
g\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{\alpha}
be such that
\nu^{+}(g)=0
.
Assume that
g
is not a coboundary.
Let
\kappa
be a smooth compactly supported function on
\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
G\in\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}

and any
m\geqslant 1
,
the function
A_{m}
defined on
\mathbb{X}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle A_{m}(z,t):=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}G*\kappa\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z)\right)

\displaystyle\times\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon+\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy),

belongs to
\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|A_{m}\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\kappa(t)|dt\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb},\qquad\|A_{m}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}\leqslant\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\|G\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}.

Proof.

For the first inequality, we write

\displaystyle|A_{m}(z,t)|\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}|G*\kappa|\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z)\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy),

which gives

\displaystyle\|A_{m}\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}|G*\kappa|\left((T^{-m}x)_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(x)\right)\nu(dx)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}|G*\kappa|\left(x_{+},t\right)\nu(dx)dt\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\kappa(t)|dt\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}.

This finishes the proof of the first inequality.

For the second inequality,
recall that

\displaystyle\|A_{m}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}|A_{m}\left(z,t\right)|dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}\frac{|A_{m}\left(z,t\right)-A_{m}\left(z^{\prime},t\right)|}{\alpha^{\omega(z,z^{\prime})}}dt.

We pick
c_{0}>0
as in Lemma
2.9

and for
t\in\mathbb{R}
we set
\kappa_{1}(t)=\sup_{|s|\leqslant c_{0}}|\kappa(t+s)|

and
H(t)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}|G(z,t)|
.
We get for
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
with
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|A_{m}(z,t)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}H*\kappa_{1}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

By Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle|A_{m}(z,t)|\leqslant c\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime}}}H*\kappa_{1}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)\nu^{-}_{z^{\prime}}(dy),

for some constant
c
.
By integrating over
z^{\prime}
, we get

\displaystyle|A_{m}(z,t)|\leqslant c^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{X}}H*\kappa_{1}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(x)\right)\nu(dx).

By integrating over
t
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}|A_{m}(z,t)|dt\leqslant c^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H*\kappa_{1}\left(t\right)dt=c^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\kappa_{1}(t)|dt\int_{\mathbb{R}}H(t)dt.

Now we dominate the second term in the norm
\|A_{m}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}
.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set
\kappa_{2}(t)=\sup_{|s|\leqslant c_{0}}|\kappa^{\prime}(t+s)|
.
We get for
|t-t^{\prime}|\leqslant c_{0}
and
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

\displaystyle|G*\kappa(z,t)-G*\kappa(z,t^{\prime})|\leqslant|t-t^{\prime}|H*\kappa_{2}(t).

Hence for
z,z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
with
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}=z_{0}^{\prime\prime}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle I_{1}(z,z^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=:\bigg|A_{m}(z,t)-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}G*\kappa\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon+\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant c\alpha^{\omega(z,z^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}H*\kappa_{2}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime\prime})\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}\alpha^{\omega(z,z^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z^{\prime\prime}}}H*\kappa_{2}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime\prime})\right)\nu^{-}_{z^{\prime\prime}}(dy),

where we have used Lemma
2.3
.
Again by integrating over
z^{\prime\prime}
, we get that

(6.5)

\displaystyle I_{1}(z,z^{\prime},t)\leqslant c_{2}\alpha^{\omega(z,z^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{X}}H*\kappa_{2}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(x)\right)\nu(dx).

Besides, as
G
is in
\mathscr{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
, the function
L(t)=\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}}\alpha^{-\omega(z.z^{\prime})}|G(z,t)-G(z^{\prime},t)|

is integrable on
\mathbb{R}
and for
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
with
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}(z,z^{\prime},t):

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\bigg[G*\kappa\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-G*\kappa\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z^{\prime})_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon+\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha^{\omega(z.z^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}L*\kappa\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leqslant c\alpha^{\omega(z.z^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{X}}L*\kappa_{1}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(x)\right)\nu(dx),

where we have again used Lemmas
2.3
and
2.9
.

As
\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}
is
1/\varepsilon
-Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}
,
by reasoning in the same way and using Corollary
2.10
, we get

\displaystyle I_{3}(z,z^{\prime},t)=:\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}G*\kappa\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z^{\prime})_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon+\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z)\right)-\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon+\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)\bigg]\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)\bigg|

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\alpha^{\omega(z.z^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{X}}H*\kappa_{1}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(x)\right)\nu(dx).

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle I_{4}(z,z^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=:\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}G*\kappa\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z^{\prime})_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\overline{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t-\varepsilon+\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\check{S}_{j}g(y\cdot z^{\prime})\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)-A_{m}(z^{\prime},t)\bigg|

(6.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant c\alpha^{\omega(z.z^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{X}}H*\kappa_{1}\left(t+\check{S}_{m}g(x)\right)\nu(dx).

Putting (
6.5
), (
6.2
), (
6.2
) and (
6.2
) together,
and integrating over
t\in\mathbb{R}
, yields the required domination.
∎

6.3.
Proof of the upper bound

We prove the inequality (
1.
) in Theorem
6.1
.
It is enough to prove (
1.
) only for sufficiently large
n>n_{0}(\varepsilon)
, where
n_{0}(\varepsilon)
depends on
\varepsilon
,
otherwise the bound becomes trivial.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\sigma_{g}=1
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{8})
. With
\delta=\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, set

m=\left[\delta n\right]
and
k=n-m.

Note that
\frac{1}{2}\delta\leqslant\frac{m}{k}\leqslant\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}
for
n\geqslant\frac{2}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
. Denote, for
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}F\left((T^{-n}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{n}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>n\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

By the Markov property, we have that for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(6.9)

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(z,t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}\Psi_{m}\left((T^{-k}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{k}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>k\right\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy).

By bounding the indicator function by
1
in the definition of
\Psi_{m}
, we get

(6.10)

\displaystyle\Psi_{m}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}^{-}_{z}}F\left((T^{-m}y\cdot z)_{+},t+\check{S}_{m}g(y\cdot z)\right)\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)=:J_{m}(z,t).

By the local limit theorem (cf. Theorem
5.8
),
there exist constants
c,c_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
for any
m\geqslant 1
,
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(6.11)

\displaystyle J_{m}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant H_{m}(t)+\frac{c\varepsilon}{\sqrt{m}}\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}+\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{m}\|G\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}},

where for brevity we set

(6.12)

\displaystyle H_{m}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,u\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\phi\left(\frac{u-t}{\sqrt{m}}\right)du\nu^{+}(dz).

Using (
6.9
), (
6.10
) and (
6.11
),
and Lemma
4.1
,
we get that for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{X}_{z}^{-}}H_{m}\left(t+\check{S}_{k}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>k\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

(6.13)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c\varepsilon}{\sqrt{mk}}\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}+\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{m\sqrt{k}}\|G\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}.

Now we deal with the first term on the right hand side of (
6.3
).
Denote
L_{m}(s)=H_{m}(\sqrt{k}s)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
. We have

(6.14)

\displaystyle L_{m}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,\sqrt{k}u\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{m/k}}\phi\left(\frac{s-u}{\sqrt{m/k}}\right)du\nu^{+}(dz).

Since the function
s\mapsto L_{m}(s)
is differentiable on
\mathbb{R}
and vanishes as
s\to+\infty
,
using integration by parts, we have, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle H_{m,k}(z,t):

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}_{z}^{-}}H_{m}\left(t+\check{S}_{k}g(y\cdot z)\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>k\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}_{z}^{-}}L_{m}\left(\frac{t+\check{S}_{k}g(y\cdot z)}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>k\}}\nu^{-}_{z}(dy)

(6.15)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}L^{\prime}_{m}(s)\nu^{-}_{z}\left(\frac{t+\check{S}_{k}g(y\cdot z)}{\sqrt{k}}>s,\check{\tau}_{t}^{g}(y\cdot z)>k\right)ds.

Applying the conditioned central limit theorem
(see Lemma
4.6
),
we have

(6.16)

\displaystyle H_{m,k}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}{\sqrt{2\pi k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}L^{\prime}_{m}(s)\left(1-\Phi_{+}(s)\right)ds+\frac{r_{k}}{k^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|L^{\prime}_{m}(s)|ds,

where
r_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
By (
6.14
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|L^{\prime}_{m}(s)|ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,\sqrt{m}\frac{u}{\sqrt{m/k}}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s-u}{\sqrt{m/k}}\right)\frac{du}{\sqrt{m/k}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{m/k}}\nu^{+}(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,\sqrt{m}u\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(s-u\right)dsdu\nu^{+}(dz)

(6.17)

\displaystyle\leqslant c\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,\sqrt{m}u\right)du\nu^{+}(dz)=\frac{c}{\sqrt{m}}\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}.

By integration by parts and a change of variable, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}L^{\prime}_{m}(s)\left(1-\Phi^{+}(s)\right)ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}H_{m}(s)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\frac{ds}{\sqrt{k}}.

Hence, from (
6.3
), (
6.16
) and (
6.3
), we obtain

(6.18)

\displaystyle H_{m,k}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}{\sqrt{2\pi k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}H_{m}(s)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\frac{ds}{\sqrt{k}}+\frac{r_{k}}{\sqrt{km}}\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}.

Implementing this bound into (
6.3
)
we get, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
,

(6.19)

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}I_{m,k}+\frac{c\varepsilon+r_{k}}{\sqrt{km}}\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}+\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{m\sqrt{k}}\|G\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}},

where

\displaystyle I_{m,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}H_{m}(s)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\frac{ds}{\sqrt{k}}.

By the definition of
H_{m}
(cf. (
6.12
)) and Fubini’s theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{m,k}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{\sqrt{m}}(u-s)G\left(z,u\right)du\nu^{+}(dz)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\frac{ds}{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,u\right)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\phi_{\sqrt{m}}(u-s)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\frac{ds}{k}\right]du\nu^{+}(dz).

Denote
\delta_{n}=\frac{m}{n}=\frac{[\delta n]}{n}
.
By a change of variable, we have

\displaystyle I_{m,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,\sqrt{n}u\right)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\phi_{\delta_{n}}(u-s)\phi^{+}_{1-\delta_{n}}\left(s\right)ds\right]du\nu^{+}(dz)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,\sqrt{n}u\right)\phi_{\delta_{n}}*\phi_{1-\delta_{n}}^{+}(u)du\nu^{+}(dz)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,u\right)\phi_{\delta_{n}}*\phi_{1-\delta_{n}}^{+}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{n}}\right)du\nu^{+}(dz).

Using Lemma
6.2
and the fact that
\sqrt{1-\delta_{n}}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{m,k}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{k}}{n^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,u\right)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{n}}\right)du\nu^{+}(dz)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sqrt{m}}{n^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,u\right)e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2m}}du\nu^{+}(dz)

(6.20)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{k}}{n^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,u\right)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{n}}\right)du\nu^{+}(dz)+\frac{\sqrt{m}}{n^{3/2}}\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}.

Substituting this into (
6.19
), and using the fact that
\check{V}^{g}(z,t)\leqslant t+c
gives

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(z,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\check{V}^{g}(z,t)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{k}}{n^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(z,u\right)\phi^{+}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{n}}\right)du\nu^{+}(dz)

\displaystyle\quad+c\left(\frac{\sqrt{m}}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{c\varepsilon+r_{k}}{\sqrt{mk}}\right)\|G\|_{\nu^{+}\otimes\Leb}+\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{m\sqrt{k}}\|G\|_{\mathscr{H}^{+}_{\alpha}}.

Since
\varepsilon^{1/2}n\geqslant m\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{1/2}n
and
n>k\geqslant\frac{1}{2}n

Abstract




Abstract
Let
A
be an expansive dilation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a variable exponent function satisfying the globally log-Hölder continuous condition. Let
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the variable anisotropic Hardy space defined via the non-tangential grand
maximal function. In this paper, the authors establish
its molecular decomposition, which is still new even in the classical isotropic setting (in the case
A:=2\mathrm{I}_{n\times n}
).
As applications, the authors obtain
the boundedness of anisotropic Calderón-Zygmund operators
from
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
or from
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to itself.

1
Introduction

As a good substitute of the Lebesgue space
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
when
p\in(0,1]
,
Hardy space
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
plays an important role in various fields of analysis and partial differential equations; see, for examples,
[
12
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
25
]
.
On the other hand, variable exponent function spaces have their applications in fluid dynamics
[
2
]
, image processing
[
5
]
, partial differential equations and variational calculus
[
11
,
23
]
.

Let
p(\cdot):{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a variable exponent function satisfying the globally log-Hölder continuous condition (see Section
2
below for its definition). Recently, Nakai and Sawano
[
18
]
introduced
the variable Hardy space
H^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, via the radial grand maximal function, and then obtained some real-variable characterizations of the space, such as the characterizations in terms of the atomic and the molecular decompositions. Moreover, they obtained the boundedness of
\delta
-type Calderón-Zygmund operators from
H^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
or from
H^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to itself. Then Sawano
[
19
]
, Yang et al.
[
24
]
and Zhuo et al.
[
26
]
further contributed to the theory.

Very recently, Liu et al.
[
14
]

introduced the variable anisotropic Hardy space
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
associated with a general expansive matrix
A
, via the non-tangential grand maximal function, and then established its various real-variable characterizations of
H_{A}^{p(\cdot)}
, respectively, in terms of the atomic characterization and the Littlewood-Paley characterization. Nevertheless, the molecular decomposition of
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
has
not been established till now. Once its molecular decomposition is established,
it can be conveniently used to prove the boundedness of many important operators on the space

H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, for example, one of the most famous operator in harmonic analysis, Calderón-Zygmund operators.
To complete the theory of the variable anisotropic Hardy space
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, in this article, we establish the molecular decomposition of
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then, as applications, we further obtain
the boundedness of anisotropic Calderón-Zygmund operators
from
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and from
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to itself.

Precisely, this article is organized as follows.

2
Molecular Characterization of
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}

In this section, we first recall the notion of variable anisotropic Hardy space
H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
, via the non-tangential grand maximal function
M_{N}(f)
, and then given its molecular characterization.

We begin with recalling the notion of expansive dilations
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
; see
[
3
, p. 5]
. A real
n\times n
matrix
A
is called an
expansive dilation
, shortly a
dilation
, if

\min_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}|\lambda|>1
, where
\sigma(A)
denotes the set of
all
eigenvalues
of
A
. Let
\lambda_{-}
and
\lambda_{+}
be two
positive numbers
such that

1<\lambda_{-}<\min\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}\leq\max\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}<\lambda_{+}.

In the case when
A
is diagonalizable over

\mathbb{C}
, we can even take
\lambda_{-}:=\min\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}
and

\lambda_{+}:=\max\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}
.
Otherwise, we need to choose them sufficiently close to these
equalities according to what we need in our arguments.

It was proved in
[
3
, p. 5, Lemma 2.2]
that, for a given dilation
A
,
there exist a number
r\in(1,\,\infty)
and a set
\Delta:=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\,|Px|<1\}
, where
P
is some non-degenerate
n\times n
matrix, such that
\Delta\subset r\Delta\subset A\Delta,
and one can additionally
assume that
|\Delta|=1
, where
|\Delta|
denotes the

n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the set
\Delta
. Let

B_{k}:=A^{k}\Delta
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.
Then
B_{k}
is open,
B_{k}\subset rB_{k}\subset B_{k+1}
and
|B_{k}|=b^{k}
, here and hereafter,
b:=|\det A|
.
An ellipsoid
x+B_{k}
for some
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
dilated ball
.
Denote by
\mathfrak{B}
the set of all such dilated balls, namely,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=\{x+B_{k}:\ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Throughout the whole paper, let
\sigma
be the
smallest integer
such that
2B_{0}\subset A^{\sigma}B_{0}

and, for any subset
E
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let
E^{\complement}:={{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus E
. Then,
for all
k,\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
k\leq j
, it holds true that

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{k}+B_{j}\subset B_{j+\sigma},

(2.3)

\displaystyle B_{k}+(B_{k+\sigma})^{\complement}\subset(B_{k})^{\complement},

where
E+F
denotes the
algebraic sum

\{x+y:\ x\in E,\,y\in F\}

of sets
E,\,F\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
quasi-norm
, associated with
dilation
A
, is a Borel measurable mapping

\rho_{A}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, for simplicity, denoted by

\rho
, satisfying

(i)

\rho(x)>0
for all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus\{\vec{0}_{n}\}
,
here and hereafter,
\vec{0}_{n}
denotes the origin of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
;

(ii)

\rho(Ax)=b\rho(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where, as above,
b:=|\det A|
;

(iii)

\rho(x+y)\leq H\left[\rho(x)+\rho(y)\right]
for
all
x,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
H\in[1,\,\infty)
is a constant independent of
x
and
y
.

In the standard dyadic case
A:=2{{\rm I}_{n\times n}}
,
\rho(x):=|x|^{n}
for
all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is
an example of homogeneous quasi-norms associated with
A
, here and hereafter,
{\rm I}_{n\times n}
denotes the
n\times n

unit matrix
,

|\cdot|
always denotes the
Euclidean norm
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

It was proved, in
[
3
, p. 6, Lemma 2.4]
,
that all homogeneous quasi-norms associated with a given dilation

A
are equivalent. Therefore, for a
given dilation
A
, in what follows, for simplicity, we
always use the
step homogeneous quasi-norm

\rho
defined by setting, for all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\rho(x):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b^{k}\chi_{B_{k+1}\setminus B_{k}}(x)\ {\rm if}\ x\neq\vec{0}_{n},\hskip 8.5359pt{\mathrm{or\ else}\hskip 8.5359pt}\rho(\vec{0}_{n}):=0.

By (
2.2
), we know that, for all
x,\,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\rho(x+y)\leq b^{\sigma}\left(\max\left\{\rho(x),\,\rho(y)\right\}\right)\leq b^{\sigma}[\rho(x)+\rho(y)];

see

[
3
, p. 8]
. Moreover,
({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,\rho,\,dx)
is a space of
homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
6
,
7
]
,
where
dx
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
.

Now we recall that a measurable function
p(\cdot):{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
is called a
variable exponent
. For any variable exponent
p(\cdot)
, let

(2.4)

\displaystyle p_{-}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,inf\,}_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}p(x)\quad\mathrm{and}\ \ p_{+}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}p(x).

Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}
the set of all variable exponents
p(\cdot)
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
.

Let
f
be a measurable function on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
. Then the
modular function
(or, for simplicity, the
modular
)
\varrho_{p(\cdot)}
, associated with
p(\cdot)
, is defined by setting

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f):=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx

and the
Luxemburg
(also called
Luxemburg-Nakano
)
quasi-norm

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
by

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\,\infty):\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\}.

Moreover, the
variable Lebesgue space

L^{p(\cdot)}
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f

satisfying that
\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)<\infty
, equipped with the quasi-norm
\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

The following remark comes from
[
16
, Remark 2.3]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

(i)

Obviously, for any

r\in(0,\,\infty)
and
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}
,

\left\|{|f|^{r}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\left\|f\right\|^{r}_{L^{rp(\cdot)}}.

Moreover, for any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
f,g\in L^{p(\cdot)}
,

\left\|\mu f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=|\mu|\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
and

\left\|f+g\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\leq\left\|f\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}+\left\|g\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}},

here and hereafter,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\underline{p}:=\min\{p_{-},\,1\}

with
p_{-}
as in (
2.4
). In particular, when
p_{-}\in[1,\,\infty]
,
L^{p(\cdot)}
is a Banach space
(see
[
10
, Theorem 3.2.7]
).

(ii)

It was proved in
[
8
, Proposition 2.21]
that, for any function
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}

with
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}>0
,

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f/{\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}})=1

and, in
[
8
, Corollary 2.22]
that, if
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\leq 1
, then

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

A function
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
is said to satisfy the
globally log-Hölder continuous condition
, denoted by

p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
, if there exist two positive constants
C_{\log}(p)
and
C_{\infty}
, and
p_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}

such that, for any
x,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C_{\log}(p)}{\log(e+1/\rho(x-y))}

and

\displaystyle|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+\rho(x))}.

A
C^{\infty}
function
\varphi
is said to belong to the Schwartz class
{\mathcal{S}}
if,
for every integer
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and multi-index
\alpha
,

\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\ell}:=\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}[\rho(x)]^{\ell}|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|<\infty
.
The dual space of
{\mathcal{S}}
, namely, the space of all tempered distributions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
equipped with the weak-
\ast

topology, is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{N}:=\left\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}:\ \|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\ell}\leq 1,\ |\alpha|\leq N,\ \ \ell\leq N\right\}.

In what follows, for
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let
\varphi_{k}(x):=b^{-k}\varphi\left(A^{-k}x\right)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. The
non-tangential maximal function

M_{\varphi}(f)
with respect to
\varphi
is defined by setting,
for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}(f)(x):=\sup_{y\in x+B_{k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f*\varphi_{k}(y)|.

Moreover, for any given
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
non-tangential grand maximal function

M_{N}(f)
of
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
is defined by setting,
for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle M_{N}(f)(x):=\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{N}}M_{\varphi}(f)(x).

H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
was introduced in

Definition 2.4
.

Let

,

Definition 2.6
.

Let

,

,

(2.6)

\displaystyle s\in\left[\left\lfloor\left(\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1\right)\frac{\ln b}{\ln\lambda_{-}}\right\rfloor,\,\infty\right)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

and
\varepsilon\in(0,\,\infty)

Remark 2.7
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
.

(i)

When the exponent function

is reduced to the constant exponent

(ii)

When it comes back to the isotropic setting, i.e.,

, and

, we claim that the definition of the molecule in

Recall that, in Sawano et al.
[

as follows.
Let

and

A function

-molecule centered at the ball

If

To prove our claim, let

-molecule as above. We only need to show that

satisfies the above (ii) of Definition

\displaystyle\|M\|_{L^{q}(U_{j}(x_{0},\,r))}

Let us begin with the notion of anisotropic Calderón-Zygmund operators

Definition 4.1

A locally integrable function

We call that

into

that extends to a bounded linear

Tf(x):=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}K(x,\,y)f(y)dy.

Theorem 4.2

Let

extends to a bounded linear operator on

Proof.

By
[

Definition 4.3

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.4
).
Suppose that
T
is an anisotropic Calderón-Zygmund operator of order
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

T^{*}(x^{\alpha})=0

for all
|\alpha|\leq s
with
s<N\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln\lambda_{+}
.
Then, for any
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom
a
supported on some
x_{0}+B_{j_{0}}

with
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
j_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
T(a)
is a harmless constant multiple
of a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,\varepsilon)
-molecule associated with
x_{0}+B_{j_{0}}
, where

\varepsilon:=N\ln{\lambda_{-}}/\ln b+1/q^{\prime}
.

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

First, we show that (
4.4
) holds true for any
f\in H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}\cap L^{r}
with
r\in(1,\,\infty]\cap(p_{+},\,\infty]
. For any
f\in H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}\cap L^{r}
, by Lemma
4.9
,
we know that there exist
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and a sequence of

(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atoms,
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported, respectively,
on
\{{B^{(i)}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
, where
B^{(i)}:=x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}
with
x_{i}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \mathrm{almost}\ \ \mathrm{everywhere},

and

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A}}\thicksim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}.

It is easy to see that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}\cap[\lfloor({1/\underline{p}}-1)/\ln\lambda_{-}\rfloor+2,\,\infty)
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left\|M_{N}(T(f))\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|M_{N}\left(T\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{A^{\omega}B^{(i)}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}+\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{({A^{\omega}B^{(i)}})^{\complement}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{A^{\omega}B^{(i)}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}+\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{({A^{\omega}B^{(i)}})^{\complement}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\mathrm{K_{1}}+\mathrm{K_{2}},

where
A^{\omega}B^{(i)}
is the
A^{\omega}
concentric expanse on
B^{(i)}
, that is,
A^{\omega}B^{(i)}:=x_{i}+A^{\omega}B_{\ell_{i}}
,

\omega:=u+v+2\sigma
with
u
and
v
as in Lemma
4.10
and
\underline{p}
as in (
2.5
).

For
{\mathrm{K_{1}}}
, from the fact that
M_{N}
and
T
are bounded on
L^{q}
for all
q\in(1,\,\infty)
(see Theorem
4.2
),
we know that

\left\|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{A^{\omega}B^{(i)}}\right\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|a_{i}\chi_{A^{\omega}B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\frac{|B^{(i)}|^{1/q}}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}.

From this, Lemma
4.12
and (
4.8
), we further deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{K_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}\thicksim\|f\|_{H^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A}}^{\underline{p}}.

For
{\mathrm{K_{2}}}
, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
x\in({A^{\omega}B^{(i)}})^{\complement}
, by Lemma
4.16
, for any
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom
a_{i}(x)
supported on a ball
B^{(i)}
, we see that
T(a_{i})
is a harmless constant multiple
of a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,\varepsilon)
-molecule associated with
B^{(i)}
, where

\varepsilon:=N\log_{b}(\lambda_{-})+1/q^{\prime}
. From this and an argument similar to that used in the proof of (
3.5
), we know that

(4.10)

\displaystyle M_{N}(T(a_{i}))(x)\lesssim\left\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\left[M_{HL}(\chi_{B^{(i)}})(x)\right]^{\beta},

where, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x_{i}
denotes the centre of the dilated ball
B^{(i)}
and

\beta
as in (
3.6
).
By (
4.10
) and an argument same as that used in the proof of (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{K_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\underline{p}}\thicksim\|f\|_{H^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A}}^{\underline{p}}.

Combining (
4.9
) and the estimates of
\mathrm{K_{1}}
and

, we further conclude that

\|T(f)\|_{H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A}}\thicksim\|f\|_{H^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}.

From Lemma
4.15
, Remark
2.2
(i) and a similar proof of Theorem

(

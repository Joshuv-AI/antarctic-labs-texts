Sections of Hamiltonian Systems
Konstantinos Kourliouros

Abstract
. A section of a Hamiltonian system is a hypersurface in the phase space of the system, usually representing a set of one-sided constraints (e.g. a boundary, an obstacle or a set of admissible states). In this paper we give local classification results for all typical singularities of sections of regular (non-singular) Hamiltonian systems, a problem equivalent to the classification of typical singularities of Hamiltonian systems with one-sided constraints. In particular we give a complete list of exact normal forms with functional invariants, and we show how these are related/obtained by the symplectic classification of mappings with prescribed (Whitney-type) singularities, naturally defined on the reduced phase space of the Hamiltonian system. Keywords: Hamiltonian Systems, Constraints, Singularities, Normal Forms, Functional Moduli. MSC2020: 34C20, 37J06, 57R45, 70H15, 70H45.

[

,

[

(X,\omega,f,H)

In this paper we deal with the problem of local classification of typical singularities of sections

of regular (i.e. non-singular) Hamiltonian systems
(X,\omega,f)
by diffeomorphisms preserving the system itself, i.e. by symplectomorphisms of

In the author’s knowledge, this problem was first posed by R. B. Melrose in
[

One may also consider it as a problem of classification of families of systems of solutions (Lagrangian manifolds-singularities c.f.
[

In

Arnol’d’s discoveries led in turn to a series of many interesting results in symplectic and Riemannian geometry, in variational calculus and optimal control, in the theory of Lagrangian and Legendrian singularities (caustics and wave fronts), and many others, most of which are now well known and collected in a series of papers and books (c.f.

Surprisingly enough, the problem of symplectic classification of Hamiltonian systems with one-sided constraints

We describe here the hierarchy of singularities (partially presented also in

[

]

H\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n+2}

(\omega,f)
,

H=\{h=0\}

\Phi^{*}h^{\prime}=uh.

By the symplectic rectification theorem we may always fix the standard normal form of the pair

(2)

Definition 2.1

We say that a section

\{f,h\}(0)\neq 0.

The condition implies that the hypersurface

is transversal to the Hamiltonian vector field

Z_{f}\lrcorner\omega=df\Longleftrightarrow Z_{f}=\{f,\cdot\}.

Classification of non-singular sections is an easy exercise (using for example the Darboux-Givental theorem or any of its variations):

Any non-singular section

H=\{x=0\}.

2.1.
Hierarchy of Singularities

For economy in the exposition we will use, whenever is necessary, the following:

NOTATION: For a pair of functions
(f,h)
we denote by
\{f,h\}_{i}
,
i\geq 0
, (resp.
\{h,f\}_{i}
) the
i
-times iterated Poisson brackets of
f
with
\{f,h\}
(and of
h
with
\{h,f\}=-\{f,h\}
respectively), i.e. under the following rule:

\{f,h\}_{i}:=Z_{f}^{i+1}(h),\quad\{h,f\}_{i}:=Z_{h}^{i+1}(f),\quad i\geq 0,

so that:

\{f,h\}_{0}:=Z_{f}(h):=\{f,h\},\quad\{f,h\}_{1}:=Z_{f}^{2}(h):=\{f,\{f,h\}\},\quad\cdots,

(\{h,f\}_{0}:=Z_{h}(f):=\{h,f\},\quad\{h,f\}_{1}:=Z_{h}^{2}(f):=\{h,\{h,f\}\},\quad\cdots,\quad resp.)

Definition 2.2
.

A section
H
of a Hamiltonian system
(\omega,f)
is said to have a singularity of type
S_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, if the following conditions hold:

(4)

df\wedge dh(0)\neq 0,

and

(5)

\{f,h\}_{i}(0)=0,\quad\forall i=0,\cdots,k-1,\quad\{f,h\}_{k}(0)\neq 0.

In the definition above condition (
4
) means that the hypersurface
X_{0}
is transversal to
H
. Conditions (
5
) imply that the Hamiltonian vector field
Z_{f}
of
f
has exactly
k
-order tangency with the hypersurface
H
. Thus, the singularities
S_{k}
are of the standard Whitney type: the first occurring singularity class
S_{1}
(fold singularities) is defined by
\{f,h\}(0)=0
,
\{f,\{f,h\}\}(0)\neq 0
, the next singularity class
S_{2}
(cusp singularities) by
\{f,h\}(0)=0
,
\{f,\{f,h\}\}(0)=0
,
\{f,\{f,\{f,h\}\}\}(0)\neq 0
and so on.

Remark 2.1
.

It is obvious from the definition that the set of points where a section
H
has singularity
S_{k}
forms a subset of codimension
k
in
H
. Thus, the only typical singularity classes
S_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n+2}
are those that appear in the range
1\leq k\leq 2n+1
.

Each of the singularity classes
S_{k}
is naturally stratified further to singularity subclasses defined by the relative positions of the function
f
(of the hypersurface
X_{0}
) with respect to the characteristic line field of the hypersurface
H
, i.e. the one obtained by the 1-dimensional field of kernels of the restriction
\omega_{H}
of the symplectic form
\omega
on
H
. In that way a double index series of singularities arises:

Definition 2.3
.

A section
H\in S_{k}
has a singularity of type
S_{k,l}
,
l\geq 1
, if the following further conditions hold:

(6)

\{h,f\}_{i}(0)=0,\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,l-1,\quad\{h,f\}_{l}(0)\neq 0.

As before, the conditions (
6
) are equivalent to the conditions that the Hamiltonian vector field
Z_{H}=Z_{h}|_{H}
spanning the characteristic line field of
H
, has exactly
l
-order tangency with the hypersurface
X_{0}
. Thus, the singularities
S_{k,l}
are again of the standard Whitney-Whitney type: the first occurring singularity
S_{1,1}
(fold-fold) is defined by:

\{f,h\}(0)=0,\quad\{f,\{f,h\}\}(0)\neq 0,\quad\{h,\{h,f\}\}(0)\neq 0,

whereas adjacent to it are the singularities
S_{1,2}
(fold-cusp):

\{f,h\}(0)=\{h,\{h,f\}\}(0)=0,\quad\{h,\{h,\{h,f\}\}\}(0)\neq 0,\quad\{f,\{f,h\}\}(0)\neq 0,

as well as the singularities
S_{2,1}
(cusp-fold):

\{f,h\}(0)=\{f,\{f,h\}\}(0)=0,\quad\{f,\{f,\{f,h\}\}\}(0)\neq 0,\quad\{h,\{h,f\}\}(0)\neq 0,

and so on.

Remark 2.2
.

As it is obvious again from the definition, the set of points where a section
H
has singularity of type
S_{k,l}
forms a subset of codimension
k+l-1
in
H
. Thus, the typical singularity classes
S_{k,l}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n+2}
are only those that appear in the range
1\leq k+l-1\leq 2n+1
.

Away from the singularites
S_{k,l}
described above, there is one more typical singularity class, adjacent to the first occurring singularities
S_{1,1}
, which is defined by the loss of transversality of
f
(equivalently, of
X_{0}
) with
H
, i.e. such that condition (
4
) in Definition
2.2
ceases to hold.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a section
H
has a singularity of type
A_{1}
if the restriction
f|_{H}
of
f
on
H
has a non-degenerate critical point at the origin (i.e. it is a Morse function):

(7)

df|_{H}(0)=0,\quad d^{2}f|_{H}(0)\neq 0,

and moreover the following conditions hold:

(8)

\{f,\{f,h\}\}(0)\neq 0,\quad\{h,\{h,f\}\}(0)\neq 0.

Remark 2.3
.

The condition that the restriction
f|_{H}
is a Morse function is obviously equivalent to the condition that the restriction
h|_{X_{0}}
of the function
h
defining
H=\{h=0\}
on the
0
-level set
X_{0}=\{f=0\}
of
f
, is also a Morse function, i.e. with a non-degenerate critical point at the origin:

dh|_{X_{0}}(0)=0,\quad d^{2}h|_{X_{0}}(0)\neq 0.

This, along with conditions (
8
), gives an equivalent definition for
A_{1}
-singularities, more adequate for our purposes (see Theorem
2.7
below).

2.2.
Normal Forms and Functional Moduli

Here we state the corresponding theorems on exact normal forms with functional invariants for all the typical singularities of sections
S_{k.l}
and
A_{1}
defined above. We give first a preliminary normal form (i.e. containing non-equivalent germs) which is in a sense the “simplest” normal form, serving for the whole singularity class
S_{k}
, for each
k\geq 1
(as well as for
A_{1}
). As it will become apparent in the text, having such a normal form is a decisive step in order to obtain further classification results for all singularity subclasses
S_{k,l}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Any section
H\in S_{k}
of the Hamiltonian system
(\omega,f)=(\ref{nfHS})
is equivalent to the normal form:

(9)

H=\{x^{k+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}R_{i}(y,p,q)x^{i}=0\},

for some functions
R_{i}(y,p,q)
,
i=0,\cdots,k-1
, such that
R_{i}(0)=0
,
i=0,\cdots,k-1
, and:

(10)

dy\wedge dR_{0}(0)\neq 0,

(11)

H\in S_{1,1}:\quad\partial_{y}R_{0}(0)\neq 0,

H\in S_{1,l\geq 2}:\quad\partial_{y}R_{0}(0)=\{R_{0},\partial_{y}R_{0}\}_{i}(0)=0,\quad i=0,\cdots,l-3,

(12)

\{R_{0},\partial_{y}R_{0}\}_{l-2}(0)\neq 0

(13)

H\in S_{k\geq 2,l}:\quad\{R_{0},R_{1}\}_{i}(0)=0,\quad i=0,\cdots,l-2,\quad\{R_{0},R_{1}\}_{l-1}(0)\neq 0.

Remark 2.4
.

Condition (
10
) on the function
R_{0}
is just the transversality condition (
4
) in Definition
2.2
, in terms of the normal form (
9
). Condition (
11
) follows by definition
\{h,\{h,f\}(0)\neq 0
. Condition (
12
) follows by condition (
6
) in Definition
2.3
and a simple calculation:

\{h,f\}_{1}(0)=\partial_{y}R_{0}(0),\quad\{h,f\}_{i}(0)=\{R_{0},\partial_{y}R_{0}\}_{i-2}(0),\quad\forall i\geq 2,

whereas condition (
13
) follows also by condition (
6
) in Definition
2.3
where now, as one may easily verify:

\{h,f\}_{i}(0)=\{R_{0},R_{1}\}_{i-1}(0),\quad\forall i\geq 1.

Theorem
2.2
above allows us to reduce the classification problem of sections
H\in S_{k,l}
to the problem of classification of the associated coefficient functions (the coefficient mapping)
R(y,p,q)=(R_{0}(y,p,q),\cdots,R_{k-1}(y,p,q))
in normal form (
9

)-(

), under (quasi-)symplectomorphisms of the pair

, i.e. symplectomorphisms of the form:

(y,p,q)\mapsto(y,\Phi(p,q)),\quad\Phi^{*}(dp\wedge dq)=dp\wedge dq.

Indeed, any symplectomorphism of the pair
(\omega,f)=(\ref{nfHS})
which also preserves the ”

C_{k}:=\{\{f,h\}_{k-1}=0\}=\{x=0\},

is necessarily of the form:

(x,y,p,q)\mapsto(x,y,\Phi(p,q)),\quad\Phi^{*}(dp\wedge dq)=dp\wedge dq.

In order to obtain exact normal forms for such mappings, notice that conditions (

)-(

) in Theorem

define conditions only on the first pair of functions
(R_{0},R_{1})
, and in fact, on their restrictions
(r_{0}(p,q),r_{1}(p,q)):=(R_{0}(0,p,q),R_{1}(0,p,q))
on the reduced symplectic space
(\mathbb{R}^{2n}_{(p,q)},dp\wedge dq)
of the

of

, whereas the rest of the functions

, are arbitrary. Thus they can be supposed to satisfy some generic condition with respect to the initial pair
(R_{0},R_{1})
, in the sense that the corresponding jet of the coefficient map

belongs in an open set in the appropriate space of jets of mappings in quasi-symplectic space
(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}_{(y,p,q)},dp\wedge dq)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
, satisfying conditions (

)-(

Of course, the choice is not unique and different choices lead to different exact normal forms with functional moduli, given possibly by a different number of functions in a different number of variables. Below we present the corresponding results under certain natural generic conditions on the Taylor expansions of the functions

,

R_{i}(y,p,q)=\sum_{j\geq 0}r_{i,j}(p,q)y^{j},\quad i=0,\cdots,k-1.

,

the singular locus

(the vanishing of the Jacobian determinant of
r
) is given by the hypersurface:

\Sigma(r)=\{\{r_{0},r_{1}\}=0\}.

Indeed, condition (a) then implies that the Hamiltonian vector field
Z_{r_{0}}:=\{r_{0},\cdot\}
of the function
r_{0}
spans the
1
-dimensional kernel field of the differential of the map
r
over its singular locus
\Sigma(r)
:

\ker dr=\text{span}\{r_{0},\cdot\},

and has exactly
(s-1)
-order of tangency with it at the origin. By condition (b) we obtain then that the mapping
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
is indeed a Whitney map in the ordinary sense, i.e.
r\in S_{s}
(c.f. again
[
4
]
,
[
15
]
). Notice thought that the singularity class
S_{2n}
is generic for ordinary Whitney mappings whereas, due to (a), it does not appear typically for Whitney-type mappings in symplectic space
S_{2n+1}(\widetilde{\omega})=\emptyset
.

Remark 3.2
.

Notice also that conditions (a)-(c) define an open set in the space
s
-jets of all mappings
(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\widetilde{\omega})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
satisfying conditions:

\{r_{0},r_{1}\}_{i}(0)=0,\quad i=0,\cdots,s-1,\quad\{r_{0},r_{1}\}_{s}(0)\neq 0,

whereas condition (d) of being Whitney in the ordinary sense, is non-generic in the space of all mappings satisfying (a)-(c).

The
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\omega})
-classification of mappings
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
with respect to a fixed symplectic structure
\widetilde{\omega}
in the source, is naturally a sub-problem of the
\mathcal{R}
-classification of mappings
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
, i.e. under ordinary (non-symplectic) diffeomorphisms of the source space (recall that no changes of coordinates in the target are allowed), a problem which already contains functional moduli for all
s\geq 1
(for
s=0
it is trivial that any diffeomorphism
r
is
\mathcal{R}
-equivalent to the identity). Indeed:

Proposition 3.1
.

Any mapping
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
,
1\leq s\leq 2n
, is
\mathcal{R}
-equivalent to the normal form:

(41)

r(p,q)=(p_{1},q_{1}^{s+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})q_{1}^{j},p_{2}+\widetilde{r}_{2}(p,q),\cdots,q_{n}+\widetilde{r}_{2n-1}(p,q)),

where the functions
\widetilde{r}_{i}(p,q)
belong in the ideal
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}:=<q_{1}>
,
\widetilde{r}_{i}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
,
i=2,\cdots,2n-1
, and the functions
r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})
,
r_{1,j}(0)=0
,
j=0,\cdots,s-1
, are differentially independent at the origin:

(42)

dr_{1,0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dr_{1,s-1}(0)\neq 0.

The functions
\{r_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\}_{i=2}^{2n-2},\{r_{1,j}\}_{j=0}^{s-1}
, are functional moduli for mappings
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
under
\mathcal{R}
-equivalence.

Proof.

Since by condition (a) the function
r_{0}
is non-singular,
dr_{0}(0)\neq 0
, we can rectify its corresponding Hamiltonian vector field
Z_{r_{0}}
in some coordinates
(p,q)
(not necessarily Darboux), such that
Z_{r_{0}}:=\{r_{0},\cdot\}=\partial_{q_{1}}
. In these coordinates we obtain from condition (a) the following conditions:

\partial_{q_{1}}^{i}r_{1}(0)=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,s,\quad\partial_{q_{1}}^{s+1}r_{1}(0)\neq 0.

We may reduce now the function
r_{1}(p,q)
by a diffeomorphism preserving the foliation
\text{span}\{\partial_{q_{1}}\}
of the Hamiltonian vector field
Z_{r_{0}}=\partial_{q_{1}}
, to the classical preliminary normal form of a versal unfolding of an
A_{s}
-singularity
q_{1}^{s+1}
(c.f.
[
2
]
):

(43)

r_{1}(p,q)=q_{1}^{s+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})q_{1}^{j},

for some functions
r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})
,
r_{1,j}(0)\neq 0
,
j=0,\cdots,s-1
, which, from condition (b), are differentially independent at the origin, i.e. satisfy (
42
).
From conditions (c) and (a) we obtain also:

dr_{0}\wedge d(\partial_{q_{1}}^{s}r_{1})\wedge dr_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dr_{2n-1}(0),

from which we can suppose (up to renumeration) that the following conditions are satisfied:

\partial_{p_{1}}r_{0}(0)\neq 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\partial_{p_{i+1}}r_{2i}(0)\neq 0,\quad\partial_{q_{i+1}}r_{2i+1}(0)\neq 0,\quad i=1,\cdots,n-1.

Using now a diffeomorphism of the source preserving the coordinate
q_{1}
, as well as the foliation
\text{span}\{\partial_{q_{1}}\}
, i.e. of the form:

(p,q)\mapsto(P_{1}(\widehat{q}_{1}),q_{1},P_{2}(\widehat{q}_{1}),\cdots,Q_{n}(\widehat{q}_{1})),

we may reduce each of the functions
r_{i}(p,q)
,
i\neq 1
, to:

r_{0}(p,q)=p_{1},\quad r_{2i}(p,q)=p_{i+1}+\widetilde{r}_{2i}(p,q),\quad r_{2i+1}(p,q)=q_{i+1}+\widetilde{r}_{2i+1}(p,q),\quad i=1,\cdots,n-1,

for some functions
\widetilde{r}_{i}(p,q)
belonging in the ideal
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}:=<q_{1}>
,
\widetilde{r}_{i}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
,
i=2,\cdots,2n-1
. These, along with the (new) functions
r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})
,
j=0,\cdots,s-1
, in normal form (
43
) for
r_{1}(p,q)
, are obvious functional invariants by construction, which proves the proposition.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The functions
r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})
,
j=0,\cdots,s-1
, in the normal form (
43
) of
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
, are already invariants for the
\mathcal{R}
-classification of Whitney mappings in the ordinary sense. Indeed, in this case we may reduce the mapping
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
by a (non-symplectic) diffeomorphism in the source to:

r(p,q)=(p_{1},r_{1}(p,q),p_{2},\cdots,q_{n}),

and then from condition (d) we obtain:

\Sigma(r)=\{\{r_{0},r_{1}\}=0\}=\{\partial_{q_{1}}r_{1}=0\}.

From conditions (a) and (b) we may reduce now the function
r_{1}(p,q)
by some diffeomorphism
q_{1}\mapsto Q_{1}(p,q)
fixing all other coordinates, to the standard normal form (
43
), and consequently the mapping
r\in S_{s}
to the exact normal form:

r(p,q)=(p_{1},q_{1}^{s+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})q_{1}^{i},p_{2},\cdots,q_{n}),

with the functional invariants
r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})
,
j=0,\cdots,s-1
, being functionally independent at the origin (by (b)).

Fix now a symplectic structure
\widetilde{\omega}
in the source
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, given say by the Darboux normal form
\widetilde{\omega}=(\ref{dnft})
. Then the corresponding
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\omega})
-classification of Whitney-type mappings
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
,
s\geq 0
, has of course more functional moduli than the
\mathcal{R}
-classification. Below we show that these extra moduli can be naturally represented by functions in the whole nested sequence of invariant ideals
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,2n-1
, parametrising the space of all symplectic structures
\Omega^{2}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. The case of diffeomorphisms
s=0
has been treated extensively in
[
19
]
. The same method of proof can be applied to the more general cases,
s\geq 1
as well:

Theorem 3.2
.

Any
S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
-singularity
r:(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\widetilde{\omega})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
s\geq 0
, is equivalent to the normal form:

r(p,q)=(p_{1},r_{1}(p,q),\cdots,r_{2n-1}(p,q)),

where:

(44)

r_{2m+1}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{2m+1},\quad\partial_{q_{m+1}}r_{2m+1}(0)\neq 0,\quad m=1,\cdots,n-1,

(45)

r_{2m}(p,q)=p_{m+1}+\widetilde{r}_{2m}(p,q),\quad\widetilde{r}_{2m}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{2m},\quad m=1,\cdots,n-1,

and:

(46)

r_{1}(p,q)=\psi(p,q)q_{1}^{s+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}r_{1,j}(\widehat{q}_{1})q_{1}^{j},

where
\psi(0)\neq 0
,
r_{1,j}(0)=0
,
j=0,\cdots,s-1
, and the
s
-functions
r_{1,j}
are differentially independent at the origin:

(47)

dr_{1,0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dr_{1,s-1}(0)\neq 0.

The set of functions:

(48)

\text{m}_{s}(r)=\big\{\psi,\{r_{1,j}\}_{j=0}^{s-1},\{r_{2m+1}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{2m+1}\}_{m=1}^{n-1},\{\widetilde{r}_{2m}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{2m}\}_{m=1}^{n-1}\big\}

is a complete set of functional moduli for the classification of Whitney-type mappings in
S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
: two mappings
r
,
r^{\prime}
in
S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
are
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\omega})
-equivalent if and only if
\text{m}_{s}(r)\equiv\text{m}_{s}(r^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We denote by
r(p,q)=(r_{0}(p,q),\cdots,r_{2n-1}(p,q))
the coordinate functions of our initial map
r\in S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega})
. Since
dr_{0}(0)\neq 0
, we may write
r_{0}(p,q)=p_{1}
by a symplectomorphism of
\widetilde{\omega}=dp\wedge dq
, and then the corresponding Hamiltonian vector field of
r_{0}
is also rectified
Z_{p_{1}}=\partial_{q_{1}}
. Denote now by:

\Sigma_{s}(r_{1}):=\{\{r_{0},r_{1}\}_{s-1}=0\}=\{\partial^{s}_{q_{1}}r_{1}=0\},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for}\hskip 5.69046pts\geq 1,

(\text{or}\hskip 5.69046pt\Sigma_{0}(r_{1})=\{r_{1}=0\},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for}\hskip 5.69046pts=0,)

the “
s
-singular locus” of the function
r_{1}
. It is a smooth hypersurface which, by Theorem
2.1
on non-singular sections, can be put into normal form:

\Sigma_{s}(r_{1})=\{q_{1}=0\},

by a symplectomorphism of the pair
(\widetilde{\omega},p_{1})
. We can continue now the normalisation of the rest of the functions
(r_{2},\cdots,r_{2n-1})
by symplectomorphisms preserving the triple
(\widetilde{\omega},p_{1},\Sigma_{s}(r_{1})=\{q_{1}=0\})
(or what is equivalent, the triple
(\widetilde{\omega},p_{1},\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}=<q_{1}>)
). These, as is easily verified, are necessarily mappings of the form:

(p,q)\mapsto(p_{1},q_{1},\Phi(\widehat{q}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1})),\quad\Phi^{*}(\omega|_{p_{1}=q_{1}=0})=\omega|_{p_{1}=q_{1}=0},

where
\Phi(\widehat{q}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1})
is a symplectomorphism of the restriction
\widetilde{\omega}|_{p_{1}=q_{1}=0}=\sum_{i=2}^{n}dp_{i}\wedge dq_{i}
of
\widetilde{\omega}
on the symplectic subspace
p_{1}=q_{1}=0
. With such a symplectomorphism we can reduce the second pair of functions
(r_{2},r_{3})
to the preliminary normal form:

r_{2}(p,q)=p_{2}+\widetilde{r}_{2}(p,q),\hskip 5.69046pt\text{where}\hskip 5.69046pt\widetilde{r}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}=<q_{1},p_{1}>,

r_{3}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{3}=<q_{1},p_{1},q_{2}>,\quad\partial_{q_{2}}r_{3}(0)\neq 0,

by taking restriction on the
q_{1}=p_{1}=0
symplectic subspace and using again Theorem
2.1
on non-singular sections. Continuing in the same way, i.e. by taking successive restrictions of the rest of the pairs
(r_{2i},r_{2i+1})
,
i=2,\cdots,n-1
, on the symplectic subspaces
p_{1}=q_{1}=\cdots=p_{i}=q_{i}=0
, and normalising, using Theorem
2.1
, with respect to the induced symplectic forms, we obtain the required result. In particular, by the normal form
\Sigma_{s}(r_{1})=\{q_{1}=0\}
of the
s
-singular locus, we deduce that the function
r_{1}
is also reduced to the exact normal form:

r_{1}(p,q)=\psi(p,q)q_{1}^{s+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}r_{1j}(\widehat{q}_{1})q_{1}^{i},

with the functional invariants
\psi(p,q)
,
\psi(0)\neq 0
, and
r_{1j}(\widehat{q}_{1})
,
r_{1j}(0)=0
,
j=0,\cdots,s-1
. The latter satisfy also (
47
) by condition (b) in Definition
3.1
.
∎

From this, and Remark
3.3
, we immediately obtain the following description of the corresponding moduli space
\mathcal{M}(S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega}))
of Whitney-type mappings under
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\omega})
-equivalence:

Corollary 3.1
.

The exists an isomorphism:

\mathcal{M}(S_{s}(\widetilde{\omega}))\cong\Omega^{2}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\times\mathcal{M}(S_{s})\cong C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})^{n(2n-1)}\times C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n-1})^{s},

where
\mathcal{M}(S_{s})
is the moduli space of Whitney mappings
S_{s}
under ordinary
\mathcal{R}
-equivalence.

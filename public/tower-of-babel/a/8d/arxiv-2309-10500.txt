A scattering result around a non-localised equilibria for the quintic Hartree equation for random fields
Cyril Malézé

Abstract
. We consider a quintic Hartree equation for a random field, which describes the temporal evolution of a infinitely many fermions, considering a three body interaction. We show a scattering result around a non-localised equilibria of the equation, for high dimensions d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 . The Hartree equation for random variables was introduced by de Suzzoni [ 12 ] but only for a two body interaction, that leads to a cubic Hartree equation for random variables. Scattering results for the cubic Hartree equation have been shown in [ 10 , 11 ] , and we extend those results to the quintic Hartree equation. As in [ 11 ] we consider a large range of potentials that includes the Dirac delta



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we prove the stability of a non-localised equilibrium for an equation for random fields, which represents the dynamics of an infinite number of fermions, considering a three body interaction. Precisely, we prove a scattering result concerning the following dispersive equation:

(1)

i\partial_{t}X=-\Delta X+w\circledast(\rho,\rho)X,

where
X:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is a time dependent random field over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},d\omega)
is the underlying probability space, where we denote
\rho:=\mathbb{E}[\lvert X\rvert^{2}]
the density function and:

w\circledast(\rho,\rho):=\int w(x_{1}-\cdot,x_{2}-\cdot)\rho(x_{1})\rho(x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}.

The expectation is with respect to the probability space:
\mathbb{E}(\lvert X\rvert^{2})(t,x):=\int_{\Omega}\lvert X\rvert^{2}(t,x,\omega)d\omega
.

In this work, we extend a result proved by Collot and de Suzzoni in
[
10
]
, in which the authors prove a result of scattering for a similar equation than (
1
), but for a two body interaction.

Lewin and Sabin were the first to investigate the problem of stability of equilibria for the Hartree equation in a density operator framework
[
24
,
25
]
. The authors proved a scattering results around Fourier multipliers, that are translation invariant equilibria of the Hartree equation. Important tools in their works are dispersion estimates for orthonormal systems
[
13
,
14
]
. The work of Lewin and Sabin was continued by Chen, Hong and Pavlović who proved global well-posedness in dimensions
d=2,\ 3
for an equilibrium solution at zero temperature
[
7
]
and scattering results in the case of dimension 3 and higher
[
8
]
, left undone in
[
24
]
.
Solutions of the Hartree equation for the density operators with an infinite number of particles were also studied in previous works such as
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. More recently, Lewin and Sabin studied the semi-classical limit of the Hartree equation
[
26
]
, and Pusateri and Sigal described the long-time behaviour of the time-dependent Kohn-Sham equation
[
29
]
, which is closely related to the Hartree equation.

The Hartree equation for random field was introduced in the work of de Suzzoni
[
12
]
, and studied by Collot and de Suzzoni in
[
10
,
11
]
. In
[
12
]
, the author links the equilibrium studied in
[
10
]
and in
[
11
]
for the pairwise potential equation for random fields, to the translation invariant stationary states for the analogous equation in density operators framework, studied in
[
25
,
24
]
. The stability of this non-localised equilibrium was showed through the proof of a scattering result, first in dimension higher than
4

[
10
]
and then in dimension
2
and
3

[
11
]
. Many thermodynamical equilibrias are included in the result of Collot and de Suzzoni (for instance for bosonic gazes at positive temperature), but not the Fermi gas at zero temperature. In
[
23
]
, Hadama proved the stability of steady states in a wide class, which includes Fermi gas at zero temperature in dimension greater than
3
, with strong condition for the potential function.

1.1.
Derivation of the equation

Before introducing our result, we explain formally the derivation of the equation, inspired by the work of de Suzzoni in
[
12
]
. The dynamics of fermionic systems was also studied in
[
1
,
2
]
. We used the same method than de Suzzoni to obtain our model with a three body interaction: the particles are interacting through the potential
w
, a distribution of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}
of the form:

w(u,v)=\tilde{w}(u,v)+\tilde{w}(v,u+v)+\tilde{w}(u+v,u),

where
\tilde{w}
is a distribution of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}
which is even for each variables and symmetric. That is to say we considered a system of
N
particles represented by the function
\psi:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
with the energy:

E_{N}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}}dx\ \bar{\psi}\Delta_{i}\psi+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j,i\neq k,j\neq k}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}}dx\ w(x_{i}-x_{j},x_{j}-x_{k})\bar{\psi}\psi.

The first term of the energy is a term of kinetic energy of the system and the second one is the interaction part.

We take
\psi
of the form of a Slater determinant:

\psi(x_{1},...,x_{N})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\sum_{\sigma}\varepsilon(\sigma)\prod_{i}u_{\sigma(i)}(x_{i}),

where
(u_{i})
is an orthonormal family of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\varepsilon(\sigma)
is the signature of the permutation
\sigma
. In this case the system satisfies the Pauli principle for fermions.

Under some mean field assumptions, we get the standard system of
N
coupled Schrödinger equations on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d
is the space dimension:

(2)

\forall j,\ i\partial_{t}u_{j}=-\Delta u_{j}+\bigg(\sum_{k,l=1,...,N}\int w(x_{1}-\cdot,x_{2}-\cdot)\lvert u_{k}\rvert^{2}(x_{1})\lvert u_{l}\rvert^{2}(x_{2})dx_{1},dx_{2}\bigg)u_{j}.

If we take
X:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
a time dependent random field over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},d\omega)
is the underlying probability space, of the form
X:=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum u_{i}g_{i}
where
(g_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
is an orthonormal family of
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then
X
is a solution of (
1
). In the rest of the paper, we do not consider only random fields of the previous form
X:=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum u_{i}g_{i}
, solutions of (
1
), but time dependent random fields
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\Omega,H^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

1.2.
Main result

We prove the stability of a non-localised equilibrium, through the proof of a scatterig result. Before we give the main result, we define the equilibrium we consider, given by Wiener integrals:

(3)

Y_{f}(t,x,\omega):=\int_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\xi)e^{i\xi x-it(m+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})}dW(\xi),

for a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
and where we set:

m=\tilde{m}^{2}\int w(u,v)dudv=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert f\rvert^{2}\bigg)^{2}\int w(u,v)dudv.

Above,
dW(\xi)
denotes infinitesimal complex Gaussian characterised by:

\mathbb{E}(\overline{dW(\eta)}dW(\xi))=\delta(\eta-\xi)d\eta d\xi,

where
\delta
is the Dirac delta function. The function
Y_{f}
is a solution of (
1
) (see section 3), but it is not stationary. However, its law is invariant under time and space translations, and in particular
Y_{f}
is not localised.

In this present work we prove the stability of the equilibria (
3
) for (
1
), via the proof of scattering of perturbations to linear waves in their vicinity, as in
[
10
]
.

We set the following conditions for a spherically symmetric function
f
in
L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
s_{c}=\frac{d-1}{2}
:

(
A_{1}
):

\langle\xi\rangle^{\lceil s_{c}\rceil}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(
A_{2}
):

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert\xi\rvert^{1-d}\lvert f\nabla f\rvert<\infty
,

(
A_{3}
):

writing
r=\lvert\xi\rvert
the radial variable,
\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
,
\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}<0
for
r>0
, and
r^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(r)\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
,

(
A_{4}
):

writing
h
the inverse Fourier transform of
\lvert f\rvert^{2}
,
\langle x\rangle^{2}\partial^{\alpha}h\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq 3\lceil s_{c}\rceil
,

(
A_{5}
):

\lvert x\rvert^{2-d}h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Finally, we denote:
\underline{w}(u):=\int w(y,u)dy
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Stability of equilibria for Equation (
1
)). Let
d\geq 4
and
s_{c}=\frac{d-1}{2}
. Let f be a spherically symmetric function in
L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that
f
satisfies assumptions
(A_{1})
to
(A_{6})
.

Then there exists
C(f)
such that for all finite measure
w
of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}
even for each variables and symmetric, that satisfies:
\lvert\lvert(\hat{\underline{w}})_{-}\rvert\rvert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(f)
; there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(w,f)
such that for any
Z_{0}\in H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}\cap L_{x}^{\frac{d}{d+2}},L_{\omega}^{2}
with:

\lvert\lvert Z_{0}\rvert\rvert_{L_{\omega}^{2}H^{s_{c}}\cap L_{x}^{\frac{d}{d+2}}L_{\omega}^{2}}\leq\varepsilon,

there is a solution of (
1
) with initial data
X_{0}=Y_{f}(t=0)+Z_{0}
which is global in both time directions. Moreover, it scatters to a linear solution in the sense that there exists
Z_{-},Z_{+}\in L_{\omega}^{2}H^{s}
such that:

X(t)=Y_{f}(t)+e^{it(\Delta-m)}Z_{\pm}+o_{L_{\omega}^{2}H^{s}}(1)\ at\ t\to\pm\infty.

Remark 1.1
.

The conditions on
f
are satisfied by thermodynamical equilibria for bosonic or fermionic gazes at a positive temperature
T
:

\lvert f(\xi)\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{e^{\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\mu}{T}}-1},\ \ \mu<0,

and:

\lvert f(\xi)\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{e^{\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\mu}{T}}+1},\ \ \mu\in\mathbb{R},

respectively.

Note that we have more assumptions on the function
f
compared to the result for a two body interaction given by Collot and de Suzzoni, see Theorem 1 in
[
10
]
. But those assumptions are natural, they are due to the relaxation of the hypothesis on
w
compared to
[
10
]
. However they are similar to the ones in
[
11
]
where the hypothesis on
w
are similar to what we have.

Remark 1.2
.

Notice that the assumptions on the smallness of
\lvert\lvert(\hat{\underline{w}})_{-}\rvert\rvert_{L^{\infty}}
already appears in the seminal work
[
24
]
and were used also in
[
8
,
10
]
, but in these aforementioned work there is also an hypothesis on
\hat{\underline{w}}(0)_{+}
. Those hypothesis are needed to invert the linear part (see Section 5), but due to new arguments we do not need the hypothesis of smallness of
\hat{\underline{w}}(0)_{+}
(the defocusing part of the equation).

Remark 1.3
.

In this paper we take
w
a finite measure which implies that
\underline{w}
is also (at most) a finite measure, this is to be compared with
[
24
]
(Corollary 1), in which the authors use a potential in
L^{1}
and to
[
11
]
.

We can also note that we take a potential with no regularity, on the contrary in
[
10
]
the potential is in a
H^{s,1}
space, in order to treat the nonlinear part of their equation. However, in
[
11
]
, Collot and de Suzzoni aready gives method to take a finite measure as a potential. This latest result is in dimension
2
and
3
, and we generalize the method to higher dimensions.

Remark 1.4
.

We highlight that we work with the critical regularity for the quintic Schrödinger equation:
s_{c}=\frac{d-1}{2}
.

1.3.
Ideas of proof

The methods used in this paper were in part inspired by
[
19
]
, by noticing similarities between the study of the stability of equilibria (
3
) for (
1
) and the stability of the trivial solutions for the Gross-Pitaevskii equation
i\partial_{t}\psi=-\Delta\psi+(\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}-1)\psi
. In the two problems, the linearised dynamics has distinct dispersive properties at low and high frequencies, and different regularities for low and high frequencies need to be introduced, this is why in Section 2 we introduce inhomogeneous Besov spaces. We also refer to the work of Gustafson, Nakanishi and Tsai
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
for more information on scattering result for the Gross-Pitaevskii equation.

The first intuition in
[
10
]
was for the authors to consider a Banach fixed point problem on a pair of variables: the perturbation
Z:=X-Y_{f}
and another variable
V=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]+2Re\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{f}Z)
which corresponds to the perturbation of the one particle density fonction. The authors then noticed that, for their framework, even if
Re\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{f}Z)
is linear in
Z
, it has good properties regarding dispersion. It has similar integrability properties than
\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]
that is quadratic in
Z
, which leads to satisfying dispersive properties, sufficient to obtain the scattering result. However, this is not sufficient for our framework because we have less regularity on our potential compared to
[
10
]
.

In this paper, we need to exploit regularity in high frequencies on
V
. We split the variable
V
into two terms, a linear one with respect to
Z
:
V_{1}:=2Re\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{f}Z)
and a quadratic one:
V_{2}:=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]
. We notice that, in low frequencies,
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
have indeed similar integrability. However,
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
have different integrability at high frequencies. In particular,
V_{2}
displays better decay at infinity for high frequencies. We use this better decay to optimize the hypothesis on the initial condition. Performing a contraction argument on
V=V_{1}+V_{2}
would not allow to observe this better decay at infinity of
V_{2}
. And the lesser decay of
V_{1}
would allow to perform a contraction argument but only if the initial condition is very regular (strictly more than the critical regularity). This is why we divide
V
into
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
and perform a contraction argument on
(Z,V_{1},V_{2})
, see Remark
7.1
.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we give some notations and known results useful for the rest of the paper. In Section 3, we state the fixed point problem we need to solve in order to prove the theorem. In Section 4, we show some direct embeddings and Strichartz estimates. In Section 5, we study the linear terms, using previous works. In section 6, we identify a condition sufficient to treat all of the nonlinear terms, and we verifiy that this condition is verified in our framework. Estimates for the initial perturbation are showed in Section 7. Theorem
1.1
is proven in section 8.

2.1.
Besov spaces

Here we take

Notation 2.1
.

For any

\Delta_{j}
is the Fourier multiplier by
\eta_{j}

We have

and we call this the Littlewood-Paley decomposition of

In the following definition, we define the inhomogeneous Besov spaces where the low and high frequencies are treated differently, in the following sense:

Definition 2.1

(Inhomogeneous Besov spaces)
.

Let

and

, we define
B_{p}^{s,t}
by the space induced by the norm:

\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{B_{p}^{s,t}}=\bigg(\sum_{j<0}2^{2js}\lvert\lvert u_{j}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p}}^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2jt}\lvert\lvert u_{j}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Remark 2.1
.

B_{p}^{s,t}
is a Banach space when
s\leq\frac{d}{p}
.

3.
Strategy of the proof

We want to investigate the stability of the solution
Y_{f}
of (
1
). In the following we use the notation
Y
instead of
Y_{f}
to lighten the notations.

We begin by showing that
Y
is indeed a solution.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
,
Y
is a solution of equation
(\ref{eqprinc})
.

Proof.

We verify that:

Y(t)=e^{it\Delta}Y_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}[w\circledast(\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}],\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}])Y(\tau)d\tau].

First we have that:

\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}]=\int\lvert f\rvert^{2}=\tilde{m},

and we get:

w\circledast(\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}],\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}])=\tilde{m}^{2}\int w(x_{1}-x,x_{2}-x)dx_{1}dx_{2}=m.

We then compute that:

e^{it\Delta}Y_{0}=e^{itm}Y(t),

and:

e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}Y(\tau)=e^{i(t-\tau)m}Y(t).

Combining the equalities above, we get:

e^{it\Delta}Y_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}[w\circledast(\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}],\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}])Y(\tau)d\tau]=Y(t)[e^{itm}-im\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)m}d\tau]=Y(t).

∎

We consider a perturbation of the equilibrium
X=Z+Y
that is a solution of (
1
). We have formally:

i\partial_{t}(Y+Z)=-\Delta(Y+Z)+\int dx_{1}dx_{2}w(x_{1}-\cdot,x_{2}-\cdot)\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y+Z\rvert^{2}](x_{1})\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y+Z\rvert^{2}](x_{2})(Y+Z).

Using that
\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y\rvert^{2}]=\tilde{m}
and introducing
V:=2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}Z]+\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]
, we obtain the following system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}(Y+Z)Z=-\Delta Z+\int dx_{1}dx_{2}w(x_{1}-\cdot,x_{2}-\cdot)(\tilde{m}+V(x_{1}))(\tilde{m}+V(x_{2}))(Y+Z)-\Delta Y\\
V=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]+2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}Z].\end{array}\right.

Using that
Y
is a solution of (
1
) and because
w
is invariant by permutation we obtain:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}Z=(m-\Delta)Z+\int dx_{1}dx_{2}w(x_{1}-\cdot,x_{2}-\cdot)(2\tilde{m}V(x_{2})+V(x_{1})V(x_{2}))(Y+Z)\\
V=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]+2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}Z].\end{array}\right.

Then we use that
\int dx_{1}w(x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3})=\underline{w}(x_{2}-x_{3})
and we get:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}Z=(m-\Delta)Z+2\tilde{m}(\underline{w}*V)(Y+Z)+(w\circledast(V,V))(Y+Z)\\
V=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]+2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}Z],\end{array}\right.

recalling that:

(10)

w\circledast(V,V)=\int w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2}-\cdot)V(x_{1})V(x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Thus, giving an initial perturbation
Z_{0}
, we want to solve the following Cauchy problem:

(11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}Z=(m-\Delta)Z+2\tilde{m}(\underline{w}*V)(Y+Z)+(w\circledast(V,V))(Y+Z)\\
V=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]+2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}Z]\\
Z_{|t=0}=Z_{0}.\end{array}\right.

We use Duhamel’s formula on
Z
and we inject it on the last term of

, thus we get that:

(12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}Z=S(t)Z_{0}+2\tilde{m}W_{V}(Y)+2\tilde{m}W_{V}(Z)+\tilde{W}_{V,V}(Y)+\tilde{W}_{V,V}(Z)\\
V=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]+2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}(S(t)Z_{0}+2\tilde{m}W_{V}(Y)+2\tilde{m}W_{V}(Z)+\tilde{W}_{V,V}(Y)+\tilde{W}_{V,V}(Z))],\end{array}\right.

with the respectively bilinear and trilinear operators:

(13)

\begin{array}[]{l}W_{V}(Z)(t):=-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds,\\
\tilde{W}_{V,V}(Z)(t):=-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)(w\circledast(V,V)(s))Z(s)ds.\end{array}

We split the variable
V
as two other variables

and
V_{2}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}Z=S(t)Z_{0}+2\tilde{m}(W_{V_{1}+V_{2}}(Y+Z)+\tilde{W}_{V_{1}+V_{2},V_{1}+V_{2}}(Z+Y)\\
V_{1}=2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}(S(t)Z_{0}+2\tilde{m}(W_{V_{1}+V_{2}}(Y+Z)+\tilde{W}_{V_{1}+V_{2},V_{1}+V_{2}}(Z+Y)]\\
V_{2}=\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}].\end{array}\right.

Then
(Z,V_{1},V_{2})^{T}
verifies:

(14)

\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}=C_{Z_{0}}+L\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}+R\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where the constant term is:

(15)

C_{Z_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}S(t)Z_{0}\\
2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}S(t)Z_{0}]\\
0\end{pmatrix},

the linearised operator is:

(16)

L\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

V_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}2\tilde{m}W_{V_{1}}(Y)+2\tilde{m}W_{V_{2}}(Y)\\

and the nonlinear remainder term is:

(17)

R\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}2\tilde{m}W_{V_{1}+V_{2}}(Z)\tilde{W}_{V_{1}+V_{2},V_{1}+V_{2}}(Z+Y)\\

\mathbb{E}[\lvert Z\rvert^{2}]\end{pmatrix}

To solve the Cauchy problem (

To do this we need to find the right function spaces to consider. We set the following spaces:

In this section we present linear and bilinear inequalities on the spaces and operators we introduced in section 3.

4.1.
A Bilinear inequality

Before we present a bilinear inequality, we give an interpolated Leibniz rule lemma, useful in all of the article, which is a consequence of a result proved by Gulisashvili and Kon
[
18
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
18
]
Theorem 1.4)
Let
s>0
and
r,p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2}
such that for
i=1,2
:

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{1}{q_{i}}.

Then for
u\in L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}
and
v\in H^{s,q_{1}}\cap L^{q_{2}}
, we have that

with:

\lvert\lvert uv\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{1}}}.

We now give the embedding result.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have for all
u,v\in\Theta_{Z}
:

(19)

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}(uv)\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{V_{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}},

in other terms the application
\gamma:(u,v)\mapsto uv
is continous from
\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{Z}
into
\Theta_{V_{2}}

Proof.

Step 0:
We have that
\gamma
is continuous from
L_{t,x}^{2(d+2)}\times L_{t,x}^{2(d+2)}
into
L_{t,x}^{d+2}
by the Hölder inequality.

Step 1:
We now show that
\gamma
is continuous from
\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{Z}
into
L_{t}^{2}B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}
. For this we treat the high and low frequencies separately. Let
(u,v)\in\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{Z}
.

We have by definition of the Besov spaces:

(20)

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\lvert\lvert\bigg(\sum_{j<0}2^{-j}\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]_{j}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2}}+\lvert\lvert\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2j(s_{c}-\frac{1}{2})}\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]_{j}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2}}.

We first look at the first term, the low frequencies. We have:

\lvert\lvert\bigg(\sum_{j<0}2^{-j}\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]_{j}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2}}\leq\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}.

We then use Sobolev embeddings to get:

\lvert\lvert\bigg(\sum_{j<0}2^{-j}\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]_{j}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}}.

Finally, by the Hölder inequality:

(21)

\lvert\lvert\bigg(\sum_{j<0}2^{-j}\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]_{j}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{4},L_{x}^{\frac{4d}{d+1}}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{4},L_{x}^{\frac{4d}{d+1}}}\leq\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}},

because
H^{s_{c},\frac{2d}{d-1}}
is embedded in
L^{\frac{4d}{d+1}}
.

We now look at high frequencies. We first write that by the Littlewood-Paley theorem:

\lvert\lvert\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2j(s_{c}-\frac{1}{2})}\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]_{j}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2}}\leq\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{0,s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},H^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

So we need to estimate
\lvert\lvert\langle\nabla\rangle^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2}}
.

We use Lemma
4.1
and the Hölder inequality to get:

\lvert\lvert\langle\nabla\rangle^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{4},L_{x}^{2d},L_{\omega}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4},H_{x}^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2},\frac{2d}{d-1}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{4},L_{x}^{2d},L_{\omega}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4},H_{x}^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2},\frac{2d}{d-1}},L_{\omega}^{2}},

because:
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2d}+\frac{d-1}{2d}
.

Thus, we get:

(22)

\lvert\lvert\langle\nabla\rangle^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}.

Combining (
20
), (
21
) and (

) gives:

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}.

Step 3:
We are left to prove that
\gamma
is continuous from
\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{Z}
into
L_{t}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+1}},H_{x}^{s_{c},{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+1}}}
.

We use the Lemma
4.1
we have:

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+1}},H_{x}^{s_{c},\frac{2(d+2)}{d+1}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}},L_{\omega}^{2}\cap L_{t,x}^{2(d+2)},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}},L_{\omega}^{2}\cap L_{t,x}^{2(d+2)},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

So, we conclude that:

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[uv]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+1}},H_{x}^{s_{c},\frac{2(d+2)}{d+1}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert u\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}.

∎

j_{1}
:

\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-\tau)\nabla^{j_{1}}Y(\tau)\nabla^{\frac{1}{2}}U(\tau)d\tau\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{p_{*}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert\nabla^{\frac{1}{2}}U\rvert\rvert_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t,x}}=\lvert\lvert U\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

And by the Hölder inequality:

\lvert\mathbb{E}[\langle\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-\tau)\nabla^{j_{1}}Y(\tau)\nabla^{\frac{1}{2}}U(\tau)d\tau,\nabla^{j_{2}}W\rangle]\rvert\leq\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-\tau)\nabla^{j_{1}}Y(\tau)\nabla^{\frac{1}{2}}U(\tau)d\tau\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{p_{*}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lvert\lvert\nabla^{j_{2}}W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

Combining the two inequalities above gives:

\lvert\mathbb{E}[\langle\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-\tau)\nabla^{j_{1}}Y(\tau)\nabla^{\frac{1}{2}}U(\tau)d\tau,\nabla^{j_{2}}W\rangle\rvert\lesssim\lvert\lvert U\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lvert\lvert\nabla^{j_{2}}W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}

So we get:

\lvert\langle U,\nabla^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rangle\rvert\lesssim\lvert\lvert U\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s-1,p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

Then by duality:

(25)

\lvert\lvert\nabla^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s-1,p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
W\in L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}\cap L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}
then
\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\in\Theta_{V_{1}}
and:

\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\lvert\lvert_{\Theta_{V_{1}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}+\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

Proof.

First we notice that
\langle\nabla\rangle^{j}Y\in L_{t,x}^{\infty},L_{\omega}^{2}
for any
0\leq j\leq\lceil s_{c}\rceil
by hypothesis on
f
.

Thus by the Hölder inequality and definition of
\Theta_{Z}
:

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2(d+2)}\cap L_{t}^{p_{*}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}.

We conclude by using Proposition
4.3
that:

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{2(d+2)}\cap L_{t}^{p_{*}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

We are left to show the following estimate:

(26)

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}+\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

To do this we are going to look to both high and low frequencies, using:

(27)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}&\leq&\lvert\lvert\sum_{j<0}\bigg(\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\bigg)_{j}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\\
&&+\lvert\lvert\sum_{j\geq 0}\bigg(\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\bigg)_{j}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}.\end{array}

Low frequencies:
in this subpart, we want to control the
L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}
norm by duality, but only for the low frequency term in (
27
). So we take
U\in L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}-s_{c}}
of the form:

U=\sum_{j\leq 0}U_{j}.

We write:

\langle U,\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-\tau)Y(\tau)U(\tau)d\tau,W\rangle.

We use the Hölder inequality and inequality (
9
) with
(p,q,\sigma_{1},\sigma)=(\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2},\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2},1,0)
to get:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\lvert\langle U,\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]]\rangle\rvert&\leq\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-\tau)Y(\tau)U(\tau)d\tau\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\lvert\lvert U\rvert\rvert_{B_{2}^{1-\frac{1}{2},1-\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}},\end{array}

Because:
\frac{2(d-2)}{2(d+2)}+\frac{d(d-2)}{2(d+2)}=\frac{d}{2}-1
and:
\frac{d-2}{2(d+2)}+\frac{d+6}{2(d+2)}=1
.

And by considering that
U
contains only low frequency we obtain:

\lvert\langle U,\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]]\rangle\rvert\lesssim\lvert\lvert U\rvert\rvert_{B_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}-s_{c}}}\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

Thus by duality:

(28)

\lvert\lvert\sum_{j<0}\bigg(\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\bigg)_{j}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},0}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

High frequencies:
to control the high frequency term in (
27
), we use Proposition
4.4
to get:

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s_{c}-1,p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

And:

\lvert\lvert\sum_{j\geq 0}\bigg(\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\bigg)_{j}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{0,s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Thus:

(29)

\lvert\lvert\sum_{j\geq 0}\bigg(\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\bigg)_{j}\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{0,s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

Combining (
28
) and (
29
) gives:

\lvert\lvert\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)W(\tau)d\tau]\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{p_{*}^{\prime}},H_{x}^{s_{c},p_{*}^{\prime}},L_{\omega}^{2}}+\lvert\lvert W\rvert\rvert_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+6}},L_{\omega}^{2}}.

And the proof is complete.

∎

Proposition 5.1
.

The operator

L_{2}:V\mapsto-W_{V}(Y)

is continuous from
L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}
into
\Theta_{Z}

\Theta_{Z}

Proof.

Let

2.3

) to
(p_{1},q_{1},\sigma_{1},\sigma)

\ (4,\frac{2d}{d-1},s_{c},0),\text{ or }(\infty,2,0,s_{c}),

\lvert\lvert W_{V}(Y)\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\leq C\lvert\lvert\underline{w}*V\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

\lvert\lvert W_{V}(Y)\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\leq C\rvert\rvert V\rvert\rvert_{L_{t}^{2},B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Proposition 5.2
.

(Lewin-Sabin
[
24
]
, see also Collot-de Suzzoni
[
10
]
)
Let
d\geq 4
and
s=\frac{d}{2}-1
. Let
f
be a radial map in
L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that:

•

\langle\xi\rangle^{\lceil s_{c}\rceil}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

•

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert\xi\rvert^{1-d}\lvert f\nabla f\rvert<\infty
,

•

writing
r=\lvert\xi\rvert
the radial variable,
\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
,
\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}<0
for
r>0
, and
r^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(r)\in L^{1}
,

•

\lvert\xi\rvert^{2-d}h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

If
\underline{w}
is an even finite measure such that:

\lvert\lvert(\hat{\underline{w}})_{-}\rvert\rvert_{L^{\infty}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\lvert h\rvert}{\lvert x\rvert^{d-2}}dx\bigg)<2\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert,

then there holds

(32)

\underset{(\omega,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}{inf}\lvert\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0,

and
1+L_{1}
is continous, invertible and of continuous inverse on
L^{2}_{t,x}
and
L_{t}^{2}B_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2},s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

See
[
24
]
where the authors show an analogous result but with more hypotheses. The following new arguments follows from a private conversation with Daniel Han Kwan.

In
[
24
]
, the authors show the condition (
32
) and claim that it suffices to invert the operator. However, in order to invert the operator we need actually a Penrose stability condition, see for instance
[
27
,
28
]
. We explain below this new condition.

The Fourier multiplier
m_{f}
we consider is defined by:

m_{f}(\omega,k)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\omega}sin(\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}t)h(2\xi t)dt.

We introduce for
\gamma\geq 0
the quantity
m_{h}^{\gamma}
:

m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(\gamma+i\omega)}sin(\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}t)h(2\xi t)dt.

We will denote:
m_{h}=m_{h}^{0}=m_{f}
.

In order to invert the operator, the condition needed is:

(33)

\underset{\gamma\geq 0,\ (\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}{inf}\lvert\underline{\hat{w}}(k)m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0.

Still we begin by showing that the condition (
32
) holds, and implies the new condition (
33
).

Step 1: Reduction to the vincinity of the origin

We recall briefly the arguments from
[
24
]
, that are still valid under our hypotheses, showing that for any
V
neighborhood of
(0,0)
:

\underset{(\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V}{inf}\lvert 1+\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)\rvert>0.

Indeed,
m_{f}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
and there is a compact set
A
of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that on the complement of this set:

\lvert\hat{\underline{w}}m_{f}+1\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2}.

In this paper, we use that the potential is a finite measure and not necessarily in
L^{1}
. The hypothesis of a potential in
L^{1}
is used in the proof of Lewin and Sabin
[
24
]
to have that:

\underline{\hat{w}}(\xi)m_{f}(\tau,\xi)\to 0,

when
\lvert\xi\rvert\to\infty
uniformly on
\tau
. But
m_{f}
verifies that

m_{f}(\tau,\xi)\to 0
when
\lvert\xi\rvert\to\infty
uniformly on
\tau
. Then we only need that
\underline{\hat{w}}\in L^{\infty}
, which is true when
\underline{w}
is a finite measure.

To show the continuity and most of the following results we use the Lindhard function in dimension
d
for the particular case
\lvert f\rvert^{2}=\textbf{1}(\lvert k\rvert^{2}\leq\mu)
denoted by
m_{d}^{F}
, then:

(34)

\begin{array}[]{rl}m_{1}^{F}(\mu,\omega,k)=&\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\lvert k\rvert}\Bigg[\frac{1}{4}log\bigg\lvert\frac{(\lvert k\rvert^{2}+2\lvert k\rvert\mu)^{2}-\omega^{2}}{(\lvert k\rvert^{2}-2\lvert k\rvert\mu)^{2}-\omega^{2}}\bigg\rvert\\
&+i\frac{\pi}{4}\bigg(\mathbf{1}(\lvert\omega+\lvert k\rvert^{2}\rvert\leq 2\sqrt{\mu}\lvert k\rvert)-\mathbf{1}(\lvert\omega-\lvert k\rvert^{2}\rvert\leq 2\sqrt{\mu}\lvert k\rvert)\bigg)\Bigg]\end{array}

for
d=1
and for
d\geq 2
:

(35)

m_{d}^{F}(\mu,\omega,k)=\frac{\lvert\mathbb{S}\rvert^{d-2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\mu^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}m_{1}^{F}(\mu(1-r^{2}),\omega,k)r^{d-2}dr.

We note that:

(36)

m_{f}(\omega,k)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}m_{d}^{F}(s,\omega,k)\partial_{s}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(s)ds.

We first treat the space
\{\omega=0\}
where by hypothesis:

\lvert m_{f}(0,k)\hat{\underline{w}}(k)+1\rvert\geq 1-\lvert\lvert\hat{\underline{w}}_{-}\rvert\rvert_{L^{\infty}}\frac{1}{2\lvert\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rvert}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\lvert h(x)\rvert}{\lvert x\rvert^{d-2}}dx\Bigg)>0,

and in
\{k=0\}
:
m_{f}(\omega,0)=0
.

Then, the imaginary part of
m_{f}
does not vanish outside of
\{k=0\}\cup\{\omega=0\}
, by using that for
\omega>0
and
k\neq 0
, in dimension
1
(for higher dimensions it is similar, we detail the computation for higher dimensions in step 2):

(37)

Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)\leq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{2}\lvert k\rvert}\int_{\frac{(\omega-\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}}}^{\frac{(\omega+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}}}\partial_{\tau}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(\tau)d\tau<0,

and that
m_{f}(\omega,k)=-m_{f}(-\omega,k)
. We can conclude that for any
V
neighborhood of
(0,0)
:

\underset{(\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V}{inf}\lvert 1+\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)\rvert>0.

Step 2: Study near the origin

We are left to study
\lvert\hat{w}m_{f}+1\rvert
on a neighborhood of
(0,0)
and we have to show:

\exists V\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}((0,0)),\exists\varepsilon_{0}>0,\forall(\omega,k)\in V,\lvert 1+\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)\rvert\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

We begin at the points where
Re(m_{f})>0
. In this case we use the hypothesis on
(\hat{\underline{w}})_{-}
and the fact that
m_{f}
is uniformly bounded by:

\frac{1}{2\lvert\mathbb{S}\rvert^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\lvert h\rvert}{\lvert x\rvert^{d-2}}dx,

we get:

1+\hat{\underline{w}}Re(m_{f})\geq 1-(\hat{\underline{w}})_{-}Re(m_{f})\geq\frac{1}{2}.

For the other points, we use a remark in
[
24
]
:

(38)

Re(m_{f}(\omega,k))\underset{k,\omega\to 0}{\sim}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}th(t)cos\bigg(\frac{\omega}{2\lvert k\rvert}t\bigg)dt.

But we have that:

\int_{0}^{\infty}th(t)cos(at)dt\underset{a\to 0}{\to}\int_{0}^{\infty}th(t)dt>0,

and we are in a case previously treated (
\mbox{Re}(m_{f}(\omega,k))>0
). Moreover:

\int_{0}^{\infty}th(t)cos(at)dt\underset{a\to\infty}{\to}0,

thus when
\frac{\omega}{\lvert k\rvert}\to 0
or when
\frac{\omega}{\lvert k\rvert}\to\infty
, the real part of
\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)
stays far from
-1
.

Then we only have to look at what happens when
\frac{\omega}{\lvert k\rvert}\in[c,R]
, for
c>0
and
R<\infty
. In this case, without the assumptions introduced by Lewin and Sabin, we cannot be sure that the real part of
\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)
stays far from
1
.

We have two cases, either
\hat{\underline{w}}(0)=0
and in for
k
small enough
\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)
stays far from
-1
, either
\hat{\underline{w}}(0)\neq 0
and we show the imaginary part of
\hat{\underline{w}}(k)m_{f}(\omega,k)
is far from
0
.

We use (
36
) and (
35
) to write that there is a constant
C_{d}>0
such that:

Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)=-C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\partial_{s}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(s)Im\Big(m_{1}^{F}(s(1-r^{2}),\omega,k)\Big)r^{d-2}drds.

And because
Im\bigg(m_{1}^{F}(\cdot,\omega,k)\bigg)\in L^{\infty}
and
r^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(r)\in L^{1}
, by Fubini’s theorem:

Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)=-C_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\partial_{s}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(s)Im\Big(m_{1}^{F}(s(1-r^{2}),\omega,k)\Big)r^{d-2}dsdr.

Using the case in dimension
1
(inequality (
37
)) we get, for
\omega>0
:

\displaystyle Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{d}\frac{1}{\lvert k\rvert}\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-2}\int_{\frac{(\omega-\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}(1-r^{2})}}^{\frac{(\omega+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}(1-r^{2})}}\partial_{s}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(s)s^{\frac{d-1}{2}}dsdr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{d}\frac{1}{\lvert k\rvert}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{d-2}\int_{\frac{(\omega-\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}(1-r^{2})}}^{\frac{(\omega+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}(1-r^{2})}}\partial_{s}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(s)s^{\frac{d-1}{2}}dsdr,

because
\partial_{\tau}\lvert f\rvert^{2}(\tau)<0
.

Then we use the fact that
\frac{\omega}{\lvert k\rvert}\in[c,R]
, and the continuity of
\partial_{r}\lvert f\rvert^{2}
, to get for
\lvert k\rvert
small enough (ie
\lvert k\rvert<c)
that there exist a constant
C_{f}>0
such that:

\displaystyle Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{d}\frac{1}{\lvert k\rvert}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{d-2}\int_{\frac{(\omega-\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}(1-r^{2})}}^{\frac{(\omega+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\lvert k\rvert^{2}(1-r^{2})}}C_{f}s^{\frac{d-1}{2}}dsdr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{d}C_{f}\frac{1}{\lvert k\rvert}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{d-2}\bigg(\frac{(c+\lvert k\rvert)^{2}}{4(1-r^{2})}\bigg)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{(\frac{\omega}{\lvert k\rvert}+\lvert k\rvert)^{2}}{4(1-r^{2})}-\frac{({\lvert k\rvert}-\lvert k\rvert)^{2}}{4(1-r^{2})}\bigg)dr.

Thus there is a constant
C_{d}^{\prime}
such that:

\displaystyle Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{d}^{\prime}C_{f}\frac{\omega}{\lvert k\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{d}^{\prime}C_{f}c.

Then, there exist a constant
C_{1}>0
(depending on
f
) such that for
\lvert k\rvert
small enough and for any
(\omega,k)
such that
\frac{\omega}{\lvert k\rvert}\in[c,R]
and
\omega>0
, we have:

Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)\leq-C_{1}<0.

If
\omega<0
we use:
Im\Big(m_{f}(\omega,k)\Big)=-Im\Big(m_{f}(-\omega,k)\Big)
.

Thus, if we take a neighborhood of
(0,0)
small enough, we can find a
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that on this neighborhood:

\lvert 1+\hat{\underline{w}}m_{F}\rvert\geq\epsilon_{0},

and this concludes the proof of (
32
) by combining step 1 and 2.

Conclusion:
We now show that (
33
) holds.

Firstly, the quantity
m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k)
is continuous on
\gamma
, thus, as (
32
) holds, there exist
\lambda>0
such that the condition becomes:

\underset{\gamma\geq\lambda,\ (\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}{inf}\lvert\underline{\hat{w}}(k)m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0.

Then, as in the case
\gamma=0
, we consider the case where
\lvert f\rvert^{2}=\mathbf{1}(\lvert k\rvert^{2}\leq\mu)
denoted by
m_{d}^{F,\gamma}
. And we have that:

Re(m_{1}^{F,\gamma}(\mu,\omega,k))=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4\sqrt{\pi}\lvert k\rvert}log\bigg(\frac{(\gamma^{2}+(\lvert k\rvert^{2}+2\sqrt{\mu}\lvert k\rvert-\omega)^{2})(\gamma^{2}+(\lvert k\rvert^{2}+2\sqrt{\mu}\lvert k\rvert+\omega)^{2})}{(\gamma^{2}+(\lvert k\rvert^{2}-2\sqrt{\mu}\lvert k\rvert-\omega)^{2})(\gamma^{2}+(\lvert k\rvert^{2}+2\sqrt{\mu}\lvert k\rvert+\omega)^{2})}\bigg).

Thus, using relations (
35
) and (
36
) we have that:

Re(m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k))\underset{(\omega,k)\to 0}{\to}0,

uniformly in
\gamma\geq\lambda
. Then, there exists
V_{0}
a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the condition (
33
) becomes:

\underset{\gamma\geq\lambda,\ (\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V_{0}}{inf}\lvert\underline{\hat{w}}(k)m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0.

Then, using that:

m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k)\underset{\gamma\to\infty}{\to}0,

uniformly in
(\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V_{0}
. Then we need to show that for a certain
\gamma_{0}>0
and
\gamma_{1}>0
:

\underset{\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma\leq\gamma_{1},\ (\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V_{0}}{inf}\lvert\underline{\hat{w}}(k)m_{h}^{\gamma}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0.

Finally, we do a change of variable
\gamma^{\prime}\lvert k\rvert=\gamma
and we are going to prove that for some
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}>0
:

\underset{\gamma\leq\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\ (\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V_{0}}{inf}\lvert\underline{\hat{w}}(k)m_{h}^{\gamma\lvert k\rvert}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0.

We use the continuity of the function and show that for any
\gamma\leq\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\ (\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V_{0}
we have:

\lvert\underline{\hat{w}}(k)m_{h}^{\gamma\lvert k\rvert}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0.

We write that:
m_{h}^{\gamma\lvert k\rvert}=m_{\tilde{h}}
, where
\tilde{h}(x)=e^{-\gamma\lvert x\rvert/2}h(x)
. Then the
\tilde{f}
function associated such that
m_{\tilde{h}}=m_{\tilde{f}}
, is characterized by:

\lvert\tilde{f}\rvert^{2}=\lvert f\rvert^{2}*\mathcal{F}(e^{\frac{-\gamma\lvert\cdot\rvert}{2}}).

So,
\lvert\tilde{f}\rvert^{2}

\lvert f\rvert^{2}

L^{1}
function. Thus
\tilde{f}
verifies the same condition as
f
and we can apply (
32
) with
\tilde{f}
instead of
f
.

Thus, for any
\gamma\leq\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\ (\omega,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash V_{0}
we have:

\lvert\underline{\hat{w}}(k)m_{h}^{\gamma\lvert k\rvert}(\omega,k)+1\rvert>0.

And we conclude that (
33
) holds.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
r\geq 1
. For any
p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\in[1,\infty]
such that:

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}

and for
(V_{1},V_{2})\in L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}\times L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{1}}
we have the following estimate:

\lvert\lvert w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2})\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert V_{1}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}}\lvert\lvert V_{2}\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{1}}}.

Proof.

We use an argument of duality by working on the norm
\lvert\lvert\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}(w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2}))\rvert\rvert_{L^{r}}
. Let’s take
h\in L^{r^{\prime}}
.

We have by a change of variables:

w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2})(\tau)=\int w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})V_{1}(x_{1}+\tau)V_{2}(x_{2}+\tau)dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Then:

(43)

\lvert\int\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}(w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2}))(\tau)h(\tau)d\tau\rvert\leq\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}(V_{1}(x_{1}+\tau)V_{2}(x_{2}+\tau))h(\tau)\rvert d\tau dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Using the Hölder inequality on the integral over
\tau
gives:

(44)

\lvert\int w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2})(\tau)h(\tau)d\tau\rvert\leq\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\rvert\lvert\lvert x\mapsto\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}(V_{1}(x_{1}+x)V_{2}(x_{2}+x))\rvert\rvert_{L^{r}}dx_{1}dx_{2}\lvert\lvert h\rvert\rvert_{L^{r^{\prime}}}.

By duality, we already have that:

\lvert\lvert w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2})\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r}}\leq\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\rvert\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{1}(x_{1}+x)V_{2}(x_{2}+x)\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r}}dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Using Lemma
4.1
gives:

\lvert\lvert w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2})\rvert\rvert_{L^{r}}\leq\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\rvert\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{1}(x_{1}+x)\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}}\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{2}(x_{2}+x)\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{1}}}dx_{1}dx_{2}.

And we get:

\lvert\lvert w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2})\rvert\rvert_{L^{r}}\leq\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\rvert dx_{1}dx_{2}\lvert\lvert V_{1}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}}\lvert\lvert V_{2}\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{1}}}.

The lemma is then proved.

Cubic terms

We are left with the formally cubic terms of (

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
r\geq 1
. Let
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 1
and
p_{i},q_{i}\geq 1
,
i=1,2,3
, be such that, if
r_{3}=r_{2}
:

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{i}}+\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{1}{q_{i}}.

Let
V_{1}\in H^{s,r_{1}}\cap L^{r_{2}}
,
V_{2}\in L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}\cap L^{p_{3}}
and
Z\in L^{q_{1}}\cap L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{3}}
. Then
(w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2}))Z\in H^{s,r}
and:

\lvert\lvert(w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2}))Z\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert V_{1}\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r_{1}}\cap L^{r_{2}}}\lvert\lvert V_{2}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}\cap L^{p_{3}}}\lvert\lvert Z\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{1}}\cap L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{3}}}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
6.1
we get by duality that:

N:=\lvert\lvert(w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2}))Z\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r}}\leq\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\rvert\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{1}(x_{1}+x)V_{2}(x_{2}+x)Z(x)\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r}}dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Using Lemma
4.1
gives:

N\leq\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\rvert\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{1}(x_{1}+x)\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r_{1}}\cap L^{r_{2}}}\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{2}(x_{2}+x)Z(x)\rvert\rvert_{L^{t_{1}}\cap H^{s,t_{2}}}dx_{1}dx_{2},

where:

\frac{1}{t_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}},

and :

\frac{1}{t_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{3}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}}.

By Hölder’s inequality:

\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{2}(x_{2}+x)Z(x)\rvert\rvert_{L^{t_{1}}}\leq\lvert\lvert V_{2}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}}\lvert\lvert Z\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{1}}}.

By Lemma
4.1
:

\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{2}(x_{2}+x)Z(x)\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,t_{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert V_{2}\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,p_{2}}\cap L^{p_{3}}}\lvert\lvert Z\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{3}}}.

Then we get:

\lvert\lvert x\mapsto V_{2}(x_{2}+x)Z(x)\rvert\rvert_{L^{t_{1}}\cap H^{s,t_{2}}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert V_{2}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}\cap L^{p_{3}}}\lvert\lvert Z\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{1}}\cap L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{3}}}.

And finally we have:

\lvert\lvert(w\circledast(V_{1},V_{2}))Z\rvert\rvert_{L^{r}}\lesssim\int\lvert w(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{2})\rvert dx_{1}dx_{2}\lvert\lvert V_{1}\rvert\rvert_{H^{s,r_{1}}\cap L^{r_{2}}}\lvert\lvert V_{2}\rvert\rvert_{L^{p_{1}}\cap H^{s,p_{2}}\cap L^{p_{3}}}\lvert\lvert Z\rvert\rvert_{L^{q_{1}}\cap L^{q_{2}}\cap H^{s,q_{3}}}.

The lemma is then proved.
∎

\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

We use the classical method to solve this Banach fixed point problem. First we define the mapping:

\Phi[Z_{0}]:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{V_{1}}\times\Theta_{V_{2}}&\to&\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{V_{1}}\times\Theta_{V_{2}}\\
\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

V_{2}\end{pmatrix}&\mapsto&(Id-L)^{-1}\bigg(C_{Z_{0}}+\mathcal{R}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}\bigg)\end{array}\right..

Let us denote
\Theta_{0}:=H^{s_{c}},L^{2}_{\omega}\cap L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}},L_{\omega}^{2}
the space for the initial datum. We are going to show that for
Z_{0}
small enough in
\Theta_{0}
, the mapping
\Phi[Z_{0}]
is a contraction on
B(0,R\lvert\lvert Z_{0}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{0}})
, for some

. For simplification we denote the norm
\lvert\lvert\cdot\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{V_{1}}\times\Theta_{V_{2}}}
by
\lvert\lvert\cdot\rvert\rvert
.

By Proposition
5.5
, we already have that
(Id-L)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\Theta_{Z}\times\Theta_{V_{1}}\times\Theta_{V_{2}})
, so:

\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\Phi[Z_{0}]\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}\bigg\rvert\bigg\rvert\lesssim\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert C_{Z_{0}}+\mathcal{R}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}\bigg\rvert\bigg\rvert.

And by the estimate (
49
) we have:

\lvert\lvert C_{Z_{0}}\rvert\rvert\lesssim\lvert\lvert Z_{0}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{0}}.

To study the operator
\mathcal{R}
we will write
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Q}_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{2}
where
\mathcal{Q}_{1}=\mathcal{Q}_{1,1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1,2}
is the quadratic part with:

\mathcal{Q}_{1,1}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

V_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\

4\tilde{m}Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}(W_{V_{1}}(Z)+W_{V_{2}}(Z))]\\
0\end{pmatrix},

and:

\mathcal{Q}_{1,2}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{W}_{V_{1},V_{1}}(Y)+2\tilde{W}_{V_{1},V_{2}}(Y)+\tilde{W}_{V_{2},V_{2}}(Y)\\
2Re\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}(\tilde{W}_{V_{1},V_{1}}(Y)+2\tilde{W}_{V_{1},V_{2}}(Y)+\tilde{W}_{V_{2},V_{2}}(Y))]\\
0\end{pmatrix},

and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
the cubic part:

\mathcal{Q}_{2}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{W}_{V_{1},V_{1}}(Z)+2\tilde{W}_{V_{1},V_{2}}(Z)+\tilde{W}_{V_{2},V_{2}}(Z)\\

because

is symmetric.

First by Propositions
4.1
and
6.1
:

\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\mathcal{Q}_{1,1}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

V_{2}\end{pmatrix}\bigg\rvert\bigg\rvert\lesssim\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}\bigg\rvert\bigg\rvert^{2}\leq R^{2}\lvert\lvert Z_{0}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{0}}^{2}.

And we have by bilinearity of
(u,v)\mapsto W_{u}(v)
:

\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\mathcal{Q}_{1,1}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{pmatrix}-\mathcal{Q}_{1,1}\begin{pmatrix}Z^{\prime}\\
V_{1}^{\prime}\\
V_{2}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\bigg\rvert\bigg\rvert\lesssim\bigg(\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

thus:

\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\mathcal{Q}_{1,1}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

V_{2}\end{pmatrix}-\mathcal{Q}_{1,1}\begin{pmatrix}Z^{\prime}\\

we get the estimate:

And by bilinearity of

we get:

\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\mathcal{Q}_{1,2}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

thus:

\bigg\lvert\bigg\lvert\mathcal{Q}_{1,2}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\

8.2.
Scattering result

To get the scattering result we write that:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}Z(t)&=&S(t)\bigg(Z_{0}-i\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds-i\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Y(s)ds\\
&&-i\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(w\circledast(V,V))(s)Z(s)ds-i\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(w\circledast(V,V))(s)Y(s)ds\bigg)\\
&&+i\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds+i\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Y(s)ds\\
&&+i\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(w\circledast(V,V))(s)Z(s)ds+i\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(w\circledast(V,V))(s)Y(s)ds,\end{array}

with
V=V_{1}+V_{2}
.

By Proposition
6.1
, one obtains that
\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds\in H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}
and that:

\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds\rvert\rvert_{H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert Z(s)\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert V(s)\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{V}}\to 0

as
t\to+\infty
. Indeed by Proposition
6.1
applied to
Z
and
(V_{1}+V_{2})\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}
for
t>0
we have:

\underset{\tau>0}{sup}\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\tau}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds\rvert\rvert_{H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert Z(s)\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert V(s)\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{V}}.

Thus for
\tau>t>0
we have that:

\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\tau}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds\rvert\rvert_{H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert Z(s)\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert V(s)\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{V}}\to 0,

When
t\to+\infty
.We use Cauchy criteria on complete metric space to conclude that
\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds\in H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}
and that:

\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Z(s)ds\rvert\rvert_{H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\to 0

as
t\to+\infty
.

Similarly, by Proposition
5.1
, one obtains that
\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Y(s)ds\in H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}
and that:

\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(\underline{w}*V(s))Y(s)ds\rvert\rvert_{H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert V(s)\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{V}}\to 0

as
t\to+\infty
.

Moreover, by Proposition
6.3
one obtains that
\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(w\circledast(V,V)(s))Z(s)ds\in H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}
and that:

\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(w\circledast(V,V)(s))Z(s)ds\rvert\rvert_{H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert Z(s)\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{Z}}\lvert\lvert V(s)\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{V}}^{2}\to 0

as

Finally, by Proposition

one obtains that
\int_{0}^{\infty}S(-s)(w\circledast(V,V)(s))Y(s)ds\in H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}

\lvert\lvert\int_{t}^{\infty}S(t-s)(w\circledast(V,V)(s))Y(s)ds\rvert\rvert_{H^{s_{c}},L_{\omega}^{2}}\lesssim\lvert\lvert V(s)\mathbf{1}_{s\geq t}\rvert\rvert_{\Theta_{V}}^{2}\to 0

. Therefore, there exists indeed

.

The generalized 3-connectivity of the folded hypercube
F

Abstract




Abstract
The generalized
k
-connectivity of a graph
G
, denoted by
\kappa_{k}(G)
, is a generalization of the traditional connectivity. It is well known that the generalized
k
-connectivity is an important indicator for measuring the fault tolerance and reliability of interconnection networks. The
n
-dimensional folded hypercube
FQ_{n}
is obtained from the
n
-dimensional hypercube
Q_{n}
by adding an edge between any pair of vertices with complementary addresses. In this paper, we show that
\kappa_{3}(FQ_{n})=n
for
n\geq 2
, that is, for any three vertices in
FQ_{n}
, there exist
n
internally disjoint trees connecting them.

Keywords
generalized
k
-connectivity, hypercube, folded hypercube, tree

MR(2000) Subject Classification
05C40, 05C05

Introduction

It is well known that the underlying topology of an interconnection network can be modelled by a connected graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
, where
V(G)
is the set of processors and
E(G)
is the set of communication links in the network. The
n
-dimensional hypercube
, denoted by
Q_{n}
, is the graph in which each vertex is corresponding to a distinct
n
-digit binary string
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
on the set
\{0,1\}
, and edges exist between any pair of vertices differing by a single digit in their binary representation. Since
Q_{n}
posses many attractive properties, such as recursive structure, regularity, symmetry, small diameter
[
1
,
2
]
, the hypercube structure is a widely used interconnection model.

The graph
FQ_{2}

Figure 2:
The graph
FQ_{3}

As a variant of the hypercube, the
n
-dimensional folded hypercube

FQ_{n}
, proposed first by EL-Amawy and Latifi
[
4
]
, is a graph obtained from the hypercube
Q_{n}
by adding an edge between any two vertices
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
and
\overline{x_{1}}~\overline{x_{2}}\cdots\overline{x_{n}}
, where
\overline{x_{i}}=1-x_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
. The graphs shown in Figure
2
and Figure
2
are
FQ_{2}
and
FQ_{3}
, respectively. It is shown that
FQ_{n}
is
(n+1)
-regular
(n+1)
-connected. Moreover, like
Q_{n}
,
FQ_{n}
is a Cayley graph and thus it is vertex-transitive. In
[
4
]
, EL-Amawy and Latifi proved that
FQ_{n}
has diameter
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, about a half of the diameter of
Q_{n}
. Therefore,
FQ_{n}
is superior to
Q_{n}
in some properties.

In the design and analysis of interconnection network, the traditional connectivity of the corresponding graph is an essential parameter measuring reliability and fault tolerance of the network. For a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
, the
connectivity

\kappa(G)
of
G
is the minimum cardinality of a set
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
such that
G-V^{\prime}
is disconnected or trivial. A famous theorem of Whitney
[
5
]
provides an equivalent definition of the connectivity. For each 2-subsets
S=\{X,Y\}
of
V(G)
, let
\kappa_{G}(S)
denote the maximum number of internally disjoint
(X,Y)
-paths in
G
. Then

\kappa(G)=\min\{\kappa_{G}(S):~S\subseteq V(G)~{\rm and}~|S|=2\}.

As a generalization of the traditional connectivity, Chartrand et al.
[
3
]
introduced the concept of the generalized
k
-connectivity. Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a connected graph and let
k
be an integer with
2\leq k\leq|V(G)|
. For a set
S
of
k
vertices of
G
, a tree
T
in
G
is called an
S
-tree, if
S\subseteq V(T)
. A collection of
\{T_{1},T_{2},\cdots,T_{r}\}
of
S
-trees in
G
is called internally disjoint if
E(T_{i})\cap E(T_{j})=\emptyset
and
V(T_{i})\cap V(T_{j})=S
for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq r
. Let
\kappa_{G}(S)
denote the maximum number of internally disjoint
S
-trees in
G
. The
generalized
k
-connectivity
, denoted by
\kappa_{k}(G)
, is defined to be

\kappa_{k}(G)=\min\{\kappa_{G}(S):S\subseteq V(G)~{\rm and}~|S|=k\}.

Clearly, the generalized 2-connectivity
\kappa_{2}(G)
is exactly the traditional connectivity
\kappa(G)
.

Over the past few years, the generalized
k
-connectivity has received meaningful progress. Li et al.
[
6
]
derived that it is NP-complete for a general graph
G
to decide whether there are
l
internally disjoint trees connecting
S
, where
l
is a fixed integer and
S\subseteq V(G)
. Authors in
[
7
,
8
]
investigated the bounds of
\kappa_{k}(G)
and the relationship between
\kappa(G)
and
\kappa_{k}(G)
for a general graph
G
. Many authors studied exact values of the generalized connectivity of some important classes of graphs, such as, complete graphs
[
9
]
, complete bipartite graphs
[
10
]
, Cartesian product graphs
[
11
,
12
]
, star graphs and bubble-sort graphs
[
13
]
, bubble-sort star graphs
[
14
]
,
(n,k)
-bubble-sort graphs
[
15
]
, Cayley graphs generated by trees and cycles
[
16
]
, hypercubes
[
11
,
17
,
18
]
, dual cubes
[
19
]
, and so on.

In this paper, we focus on determining the generalized 3-connectivity of the folded hypercube
FQ_{n}
, and get the main result as follows.

Theorem 1.1

\kappa_{3}(FQ_{n})=n
for any
n\geq 2
.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we consider a simple, connected graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
with
V(G)
be its vertex set and
E(G)
be its edge set. For a vertex
X\in V(G)
, the
degree
of
X
in
G
, denoted by
{\rm deg}_{G}(X)
, is the number of edges of
G
incident with
X
. Let
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
, denote by
G-V^{\prime}
the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all the vertices in
V^{\prime}
together with their incident edges. Let
P=X_{0}X_{1}\cdots X_{m}
be a path connecting two vertices
X_{0}
and
X_{m}
in
G
, then
P
is called an
(X_{0},X_{m})
-
path
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
d
be an integer that
1\leq d\leq n
. Denote
Q_{n}^{d}[0]
(resp.
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
) be the graph with vertex set
V(Q_{n}^{d}[0])=\{x_{1}\cdots x_{d-1}0x_{d+1}\cdots x_{n}:x_{i}\in\{0,1\},i\in[n]\backslash\{d\}\}
(resp.
V(Q_{n}^{d}[1])=\{x_{1}\cdots x_{d-1}1x_{d+1}\cdots x_{n}:x_{i}\in\{0,1\},i\in[n]\backslash\{d\}\}
), and two vertices in
Q_{n}^{d}[0]
(resp.
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
) are adjacent if and only if they differ in exactly one coordinate. It is easily seen that Property
2.1
holds.

Property 2.1

(1) Both
Q_{n}^{d}[0]
and
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
are induced subgraphs of the hypercube
Q_{n}
;

(2) Both
Q_{n}^{d}[0]
and
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
are isomorphic to
Q_{n-1}
.

Let
X=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
and
Y=y_{1}y_{2}\cdots y_{n}
be two vertices in the hypercube
Q_{n}
. If
X
and
Y
exactly differ by the
d
th digit (
1\leq d\leq n
), that is
|x_{d}-y_{d}|=1
and
x_{i}=y_{i}
for all
i\in[n]\backslash\{d\}
, then
XY
is named a
d
-dimensional edge
. Based on these notations, we can divide
Q_{n}
along the
d
th dimension into
Q_{n}^{d}[0]
and
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
by deleting all the
d
-dimensional edges from
Q_{n}
.

The folded hypercube
FQ_{n}
(
n\geq 2
) can be obtained from
Q_{n}^{d}[0]
and
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
by adding
2\times 2^{n-1}
edges as follows: a vertex
X=x_{1}\cdots x_{d-1}0x_{d+1}\cdots x_{n}\in V(Q_{n}^{d}[0])
is joined to a vertex
Y=y_{1}\cdots y_{d-1}1y_{d+1}\cdots y_{n}\in V(Q_{n}^{d}[1])
if for every
i\in[n]\backslash\{d\}
, either

(i)

x_{i}=y_{i}
; in this case,
XY
is called a
hypercube edge
, or

(ii)

x_{i}=\overline{y_{i}}
; in this case,
XY
is called a
complement edge
.

For any integer
d\in[n]
, the construction of
FQ_{n}
can be symbolically written as
FQ_{n}=Q_{n}^{d}[0]\bigotimes Q_{n}^{d}[1]
. It follows from the definition that
X
differs with
Y
by
d
th digit when
XY
is a hypercube edge, in this case, denote
Y=N_{d}(X)
. Furthermore,
X
differs with
Y
by each digit when
XY
is a complement edge, in this case, denote
Y=\overline{X}
.

Let
X
be an arbitrary vertex in
V(FQ_{n})\cap V(Q_{n}^{d}[0])
, it has two neighbours in
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
, one is
N_{d}(X)
, which is called a
hypercube neighbour
of
X
, the other is
\overline{X}
, which is called a
complement neighbour
of
X
. Both
N_{d}(X)
and
\overline{X}
are named
out neighbours
of
X
in
Q_{n}^{d}[1]
. Similarly, for any vertex
Y
in
V(FQ_{n})\cap V(Q_{n}^{d}[1])
, it has a hypercube neighbour
N_{d}(Y)
and a complement neighbour
\overline{Y}
in
Q_{n}^{d}[0]
.

The authors in
[
20
,
11
]
studied the connectivity and the generalized 3-connectivity of the hypercube

, respectively.

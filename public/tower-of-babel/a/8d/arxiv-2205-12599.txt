On the Impact of Hardware Impairments on RIS-aided Localization
Cüneyd Öztürk 1, Musa Furkan Keskin 2, Henk Wymeersch 2, Sinan Gezici 1

Abstract
We investigate a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-aided near-field localization system with single-antenna user equipment (UE) and base station (BS) under hardware impairments by considering a practical phase-dependent RIS amplitude variations model. To analyze the localization performance under the mismatch between the practical model and the ideal model with unit-amplitude RIS elements, we employ the misspecified Cramér-Rao bound (MCRB). Based on the MCRB derivation, the lower bound (LB) on the mean-squared error for estimation of UE position is evaluated and shown to converge to the MCRB at low signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs). Simulation results indicate more severe performance degradation due to the model misspecification with increasing SNR. In addition, the mismatched maximum likelihood (MML) estimator is derived and found to be tight to the LB in the high SNR regime. Finally, we observe that the model mismatch can lead to an order-of-magnitude localization performance loss at high SNRs.

MCRB Definition

We first introduce the pseudo-true parameter

where

VI
Concluding Remarks

We have considered the problem of RIS-aided near-field localization in the presence of model misspecification accounting for mismatch between ideal and realistic RIS amplitude models. In particular, we have focused on a scenario in which the exact model for the amplitudes of the RIS elements is unknown and the amplitudes are assumed to be constant (unity). Based on the realistic amplitude model given in
[
22
]
, we have first derived the theoretical performance bounds for estimating the UE position when the belief and the reality of the RIS amplitude models do not match. We have observed that the amplitude knowledge becomes crucial in the high SNR regime. Moreover, when the true model is known, the value of
\beta_{\text{min}}
does not affect the theoretical bounds notably. Lastly, we have implemented the MML estimator for the considered problem, and observed that the MML estimator can achieve the LB in the high SNR regime, as expected.

Proof of Lemma 1

Based on the definition of the KL divergence and the system model in Section
II
, (
8
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int p(\boldsymbol{y}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\ln\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{y}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})}{\tilde{p}(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{\eta})}\right)\,\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}

(25)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{5}}-\int p(\boldsymbol{y}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\ln\tilde{p}(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{\eta})\,\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}

(26)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int p(\boldsymbol{y}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left\lVert\boldsymbol{y}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\rVert^{2}\,\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}

(27)

where the second equality is due to the independence of
p(\boldsymbol{y}\mid\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})
from
\boldsymbol{\eta}
, and the last equality is obtained from (
5
). Then, it can be shown that the following equations hold:

\displaystyle\int p(\boldsymbol{y}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left\lVert\boldsymbol{y}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\rVert^{2}\,d\boldsymbol{y}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\int p(\boldsymbol{y}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left|y_{t}-\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}\,\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underbrace{\left(\prod_{t^{\prime}\neq t}\int p(y_{t^{\prime}}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,\text{d}y_{t^{\prime}}\right)}_{=1}\left(\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left|y_{t}-\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}\,\text{d}y_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left|y_{t}-\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}\,\text{d}y_{t}.

(28)

We now introduce
\epsilon_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=\mu_{t}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})-\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
, so that
\left|y_{t}-\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}=\left|y_{t}-\mu_{t}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})+\epsilon_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}
, and the integral expression in
(
28
) can be manipulated as follows:

\displaystyle\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left|y_{t}-\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}\,\text{d}y_{t}

\displaystyle=\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left|y_{t}-\mu_{t}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\right|^{2}\,\text{d}y_{t}+\left|\epsilon_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,\text{d}y_{t}

\displaystyle+2\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\Re\left((y_{t}-\mu_{t}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}}))^{*}\epsilon_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right)\,\text{d}y_{t}.

(29)

Since
y_{t}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mu_{t}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}}),N_{0})
and
\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,\text{d}y_{t}=1
, the expression in (

) can be simplified as

\int p(y_{t}|\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\left|y_{t}-\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}\,\text{d}y_{t}=N_{0}+\left|\epsilon_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}.

(30)

By combining (
27
), (
28
) and (

), we finally obtain that

\displaystyle\bar{\boldsymbol{\eta}}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{5}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(N_{0}+\left|\epsilon_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2}\right)=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{5}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left|\epsilon_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|^{2},

which completes the proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}
be given by
\boldsymbol{\eta}=[\alpha_{r}\,\alpha_{i}\,\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{z}]^{\intercal}
. Also, define
\boldsymbol{p}\triangleq[\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{z}]^{\intercal}
,
b_{m}\triangleq[(\boldsymbol{p})]_{m}
, and
\alpha\triangleq\alpha_{r}+j\alpha_{i}
. We also introduce
\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{RIS}}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{RIS}}\right\rVert}
and for any
1\leq m\leq M

\boldsymbol{u}_{m}=\frac{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{m}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{m}\right\rVert}

\boldsymbol{u}=[u_{x}\,u_{y}\,u_{z}]^{\intercal}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{m}=[u_{m,x}\,u_{m,y}\,u_{m,z}]^{\intercal}
.Then, the first and second derivatives of
\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
with respect to
\boldsymbol{\eta}
are given as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\alpha_{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t},\,\frac{\partial\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\alpha_{i}}=j\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t}.

For
\nu\in\{\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{z}\}
, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\nu}

\displaystyle=-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\left(u_{m,\nu}-u_{x}\right)\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\alpha_{r}\partial\nu}

\displaystyle=-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\left(u_{m,\nu}-u_{\nu}\right)\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\alpha_{i}\partial\nu}

\displaystyle=j\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\alpha_{r}\partial\nu},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\nu\partial\nu}=-\alpha\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\left(u_{m,\nu}-u_{\nu}\right)^{2}\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t}

\displaystyle-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\left(\frac{1-u^{2}_{m,\nu}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{m}\right\rVert}-\frac{1-u^{2}_{\nu}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{RIS}}\right\rVert}\right)\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t}.

\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\{\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{z}\}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{{\mu}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\partial\nu_{1}\partial\nu_{2}}=-\alpha\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\left(u_{m,\nu_{1}}-u_{\nu_{1}}\right)\left(u_{m,\nu_{2}}-u_{\nu_{2}}\right)\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t}

\displaystyle+j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{m}\left(\frac{u_{m,\nu_{1}}u_{m,\nu_{2}}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{m}\right\rVert}-\frac{u_{\nu_{1}}u_{\nu_{2}}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{RIS}}\right\rVert}\right)\tilde{w}_{t,m}s_{t}.

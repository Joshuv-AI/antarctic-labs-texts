Abstract

Introduction and summary

The AdS/CFT correspondence
Maldacena_1997
relates a string theory on AdS background to a conformal field theory (CFT). In particular, the energy spectrum of a string model coincides with the spectrum of scaling dimensions of the dual CFT. There are several string sigma models which are integrable on the classical and (conjecturally) quantum level, and one hopes to be able to determine their exact energy spectra.

The Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz (TBA) approach is a powerful method to find the ground state energy (GSE) of integrable field theories
Zamolodchikov:1989cf
. It relates the GSE of a model on a cylinder of circumference
L
to the free energy of a so-called mirror model with temperature
T=1/L
. The mirror model is obtained by a double Wick rotation, and for non-relativistic models which appear in the AdS/CFT context it differs from the original one
Arutyunov:2007tc
. The mirror TBA equations can then be used to find the spectrum of excited states via the contour deformation trick
Bazhanov:1996aq
;
Dorey:1996re
;
Arutyunov:2009ax
.

The TBA approach has been successfully applied to the
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}

Arutyunov:2009zu
;
Arutyunov:2009ur
;
Bombardelli:2009ns
;
Gromov:2009bc
and
AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3}
superstrings
Bombardelli:2009xz
;
Gromov:2009at
, see
Arutyunov:2009ga
;
Beisert:2010jr
for a review. The next interesting example of an integrable string sigma model is provided by
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times T^{4}
superstring
Cagnazzo:2012se
. The superstring background can be supported by both Ramond-Ramond (RR) and Neveu- Schwarz-Neveu-Schwarz (NSNS) fluxes
Larsen:1999uk
;
OhlssonSax:2018hgc
.
The properties of the general model are poorly understood, and its dual CFT is unknown.
In the case of pure NSNS background the superstring theory in the conformal gauge is a level-
k
supersymmetric Wess-Zumino-Novikov-Witten (WZNW) model, and its spectrum can be found by using CFT methods
Maldacena:2000hw
. The pure NSNS superstring is believed to be dual to symmetric product orbifold CFTs
Giribet:2018ada
;
Eberhardt:2018ouy
;
Eberhardt:2021vsx

Massive.

The massive bosonic fields are periodic while the fermionic fields satisfy the twisted boundary conditions

TBA GSE: Small Twist

In this section we use the

\mu\rightarrow 0

Ground State Energy.

We can now obtain the ground state energy by expanding (

\displaystyle E(h,L,\mu)

\displaystyle\approx h^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}q\,q\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p\,\left(\sqrt{\frac{\left(p^{2}+1\right)q^{2}}{4}+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\left(p^{2}+1\right)q^{2}}{4}}\right)^{-2L}\,.

The integrals can be easily taken by making the substitution

\displaystyle q={2\sinh x\over\sqrt{p^{2}+1}}\,,

(72)

and we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(h\gg L,L)

\displaystyle\approx h^{2}{\pi\over L^{2}-1}\,,

(73)

and

\displaystyle E(h\gg L,L)\approx-\frac{\mu^{2}}{\pi}\left(N_{0}\frac{4hL}{4L^{2}-1}+{h^{2}\pi\over L^{2}-1}\right)\,.

(74)

In this case massless modes provide a subleading contribution.

Computing a similar integral for the
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
case, one gets

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{AdS_{5}}(h\gg L,L)

\displaystyle\approx h^{4}\frac{3\pi}{L^{4}-5L^{2}+4}\,,

(75)

\displaystyle E_{AdS_{5}}(h\gg L,L)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\mu^{2}}{\pi}\frac{6h^{4}\pi}{L^{4}-5L^{2}+4}\,.

We see again that there is a substantial difference between the
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times T^{4}
and
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
superstrings.

Twisted Generalised Lüscher formula.

We have found that both small

The massless contribution does not vanish exponentially for large

\displaystyle E(h,L)

\displaystyle\approx-\sum_{\dot{\alpha}=1}^{N_{0}}\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\widetilde{p}}{2\pi}e^{-L\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{0}}\mathfrak{F}_{0}^{(\dot{\alpha})}-\sum_{Q=1}^{+\infty}\,\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\widetilde{p}}{2\pi}e^{-L\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{Q}}(\mathfrak{F}_{Q}+\bar{\mathfrak{F}}_{Q})\,,

(82)

where

(83)

and
\text{Tr}_{X}

plays the role of a fermion number operator of the corresponding four-dimensional representation where
F^{\prime}
,

are fermion number operators of the left/right two-dimensional representations.

Calculating the trace, one gets for all the representations

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{X}e^{i(\pi+\mu)F}

\displaystyle=\left(\text{Tr}_{X^{\prime}}e^{i(\pi+\mu)F^{\prime}}\right)\left(\text{Tr}_{X^{\prime\prime}}e^{-i(\pi+\mu)F^{\prime\prime}}\right)

(84)

\displaystyle=\left(1-e^{i\mu}\right)\left(1-e^{-i\mu}\right)=4\sin^{2}(\tfrac{\mu}{2})\,.

Clearly, the twisted generalised Lüscher expression (

Twisted mixed-flux

In this section we first generalise the semiclassical consideration in section

In fact, as far as the semiclassical computation is concerned, we can consider the even more general one-parameter family of

backgrounds (where the parameter is the relative radius of the two spheres). It reduces to the mixed-flux

Baggio:2017kza

Lightcone
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times S^{3}\times S^{1}
string GSE

The quadratic action of the lightcone
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times S^{3}\times S^{1}
string derived in
Dei:2018yth
takes the form

\displaystyle S=\int\dd\tau\int_{0}^{\cal J}\dd\sigma\,\mathcal{L}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^{b}+\mathcal{L}^{f}\,,

(85)

where
\mathcal{L}^{b}
and
\mathcal{L}^{f}
are the bosonic and fermionic quadratic Lagrangians, respectively

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{4}{1\over 2}\left[\left|\partial_{k}z_{j}\right|^{2}-m_{b,j}^{2}\left|z_{j}\right|^{2}+iqm_{b,j}(\bar{z}_{j}z_{j}^{\prime}-\bar{z}_{j}^{\prime}z_{j})\right]\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{f}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\Big[i\bar{\theta}_{1j}\left(\dot{\theta}_{1j}-i\hat{q}\theta_{2j}^{\prime}+q\theta_{1j}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle+i\bar{\theta}_{2j}\left(\dot{\theta}_{2j}+i\hat{q}\theta_{1j}^{\prime}-q\theta_{2j}^{\prime}\right)-m_{f,j}(\bar{\theta}_{1j}\theta_{1j}-\bar{\theta}_{2j}\theta_{2j})\Big]\,.

Here
\hat{q}=\sqrt{1-q^{2}}
, and the masses are given by

\displaystyle m_{b,1}=1\,,\quad m_{b,2}=\cos\varphi\cos{\omega}\,,\quad m_{b,3}=\sin\varphi\sin{\omega}\,,\quad m_{b,4}=0\,,

(87)

for the bosons, while for the fermions

\displaystyle m_{f,1}

\displaystyle={1+\cos(\varphi-{\omega})\over 2}\,,\quad m_{f,2}={1+\cos(\varphi+{\omega})\over 2}\,,\quad

(88)

\displaystyle m_{f,3}

\displaystyle={1-\cos(\varphi+{\omega})\over 2}\,,\quad m_{f,4}={1-\cos(\varphi-{\omega})\over 2}\,,

where
\varphi
parametrises the
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times S^{3}\times S^{1}
background and is related to the commonly used parameter
{\alpha}
as
{\alpha}=\cos^{2}\varphi
, and
{\omega}
parametrises the one-parameter family of choice of gauge-fixing that determines the lightcone string sigma models. The models are supersymmetric only for
{\omega}=\pm\varphi
. Then, the
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times T^{4}
model is recovered at
{\omega}=\varphi=0
. For any values of
\varphi,{\omega}
the masses satisfy the following important relation

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{4}m_{b,j}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{4}m_{f,j}^{2}\,,

(89)

which as we will see in a moment allows one to introduce a regularisation leading to a finite ground state energy of the lightcone model.

We impose the most general twisted boundary conditions

\displaystyle z_{j}(\sigma+{\cal J},\tau)

\displaystyle=e^{i\mu_{j}^{b}}z_{j}(\sigma,\tau)\,,\quad\theta_{a,j}(\sigma+{\cal J},\tau)

\displaystyle=e^{i\mu_{j}^{f}}\theta_{a,j}(\sigma,\tau)\,,\quad a=1,2\,,

(90)

and find the bosonic and fermionic frequencies

\displaystyle{\omega}_{n,j}^{b}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left({2\pi\over{\cal J}}(n+\hat{\mu}_{j}^{b})+qm_{b,j}\right)^{2}+\hat{q}^{2}m_{b,j}^{2}}\,,

(91)

\displaystyle{\omega}_{n,j}^{f}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left({2\pi\over{\cal J}}(n+\hat{\mu}_{j}^{f})+qm_{f,j}\right)^{2}+\hat{q}^{2}m_{f,j}^{2}}\,.

Then, the GSE is given by

\displaystyle E=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{\omega}_{n,j}^{b}-\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{\omega}_{n,j}^{f}\,.

(92)

We use the following regularisation

\displaystyle E=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{\omega}_{n,j}^{b}\,\exp\left({-{{\epsilon}\over\hat{q}m_{b,j}}{\omega}_{n,j}^{b}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{\omega}_{n,j}^{f}\,\exp\left({-{{\epsilon}\over\hat{q}m_{f,j}}{\omega}_{n,j}^{f}}\right)\,.

(93)

which is a generalisation of (
37
). We also assume that all the masses are nonzero.

By using again the Poisson summation formula, we get

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{w=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(F_{w,j}^{b}-F_{w,j}^{f}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left(F_{0,j}^{b}-F_{0,j}^{f}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{w\neq 0}\left(F_{w,j}^{b}-F_{w,j}^{f}\right)\,.

(94)

Here

\displaystyle F_{w,j}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dn\,{\omega}_{n,j}^{\star}\,\exp\left({-{{\epsilon}\over\hat{q}m_{\star,j}}{\omega}_{n,j}^{\star}}\right)e^{-i2\pi wn}

(95)

\displaystyle=e^{iw(\mu_{j}^{\star}+qm_{\star,j}{\cal J})}\frac{\mathcal{J}\hat{q}^{2}m_{\star,j}^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,\sqrt{x^{2}+1}e^{-\epsilon\,\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}e^{-i\hat{q}m_{\star,j}{\cal J}wx}\,,

where
\star=b,f
.

Taking into account the relation (
89
), we see that
\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left(F_{0,j}^{b}-F_{0,j}^{f}\right)=0
, and therefore we only need to calculate
F_{w}^{\star}
for
w\neq 0
. This has been done in section
2
, and by using the results we find the GSE

\displaystyle E=-2\hat{q}\,\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\left(m_{b,j}\cos\left(w(\mu_{j}^{b}+qm_{b,j}{\cal J})\right)\frac{K_{1}(\hat{q}m_{b,j}{\cal J}w)}{\pi w}\right.

(96)

\displaystyle\left.-m_{f,j}\cos\left(w(\mu_{j}^{f}+qm_{f,j}{\cal J})\right)\frac{K_{1}(\hat{q}m_{f,j}{\cal J}w)}{\pi w}\right)\,.

If some of the masses are equal to 0 then we can either use the zeta-function regularisation, or take the limit
m\to 0
in the formula. Assuming that
-\pi<\mu<\pi
, we get for the massless contribution

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=-2\hat{q}\,\lim_{m\to 0}\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}(m\cos\left(w(\mu+qm{\cal J})\right)\frac{K_{1}(\hat{q}m{\cal J}w)}{\pi w}

(97)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\cos(\mu w)}{\pi{\cal J}w^{2}}=-\frac{\pi}{3{\cal J}}+\frac{|\mu|}{{\cal J}}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\pi{\cal J}}\,,

which agrees with the result obtained by using the zeta-function regularisation.

Thus, assuming that we have
N_{0}^{b}
and
N_{0}^{f}
complex massless bosons and fermions, and
N_{\rm m}^{b}
and
N_{\rm m}^{f}
complex massive bosons and fermions, we can write (

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi(N_{0}^{b}-N_{0}^{f})}{3{\cal J}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}^{b}}\left(\frac{|\mu_{0j}^{b}|}{{\cal J}}-\frac{(\mu_{0j}^{b})^{2}}{2\pi{\cal J}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}^{f}}\left(\frac{|\mu_{0j}^{f}|}{{\cal J}}-\frac{(\mu_{0j}^{f})^{2}}{2\pi{\cal J}}\right)

(98)

\displaystyle-2\hat{q}\,\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\rm m}^{b}}\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}m_{b,j}\cos\left(w(\mu_{j}^{b}+qm_{b,j}{\cal J})\right)\frac{K_{1}(\hat{q}m_{b,j}{\cal J}w)}{\pi w}

\displaystyle+2\hat{q}\,\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\rm m}^{f}}\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}m_{f,j}\cos\left(w(\mu_{j}^{f}+qm_{f,j}{\cal J})\right)\frac{K_{1}(\hat{q}m_{f,j}{\cal J}w)}{\pi w})\,.

In the supersymmetric case
N_{0}^{b}=N_{0}^{f}=N_{0}
,
N_{\rm m}^{b}=N_{\rm m}^{f}=N_{\rm m}
,
m_{b,j}=m_{f,j}=m_{j}
, and (

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}}\left(\frac{|\mu_{0j}^{b}|-|\mu_{0j}^{f}|}{{\cal J}}-\frac{(\mu_{0j}^{b})^{2}-(\mu_{0j}^{f})^{2}}{2\pi{\cal J}}\right)

(99)

\displaystyle-4\hat{q}\,\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\rm m}}\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}m_{j}\sin\left(w{\mu_{j}^{b}+\mu_{j}^{f}+2qm_{j}{\cal J}\over 2}\right)\sin\left(w{\mu_{j}^{f}-\mu_{j}^{b}\over 2}\right)\frac{K_{1}(\hat{q}m_{j}{\cal J}w)}{\pi w}\,.

In the large
{\cal J}
limit the main contribution comes from
w=1
, and one gets

\displaystyle

\displaystyle E({\cal J}\gg 1)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}}\left(\frac{|\mu_{0j}^{b}|-|\mu_{0j}^{f}|}{{\cal J}}-\frac{(\mu_{0j}^{b})^{2}-(\mu_{0j}^{f})^{2}}{2\pi{\cal J}}\right)

(100)

\displaystyle-4\hat{q}\,\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\rm m}}m_{j}\sin\left({\mu_{j}^{b}+\mu_{j}^{f}+2qm_{j}{\cal J}\over 2}\right)\sin\left({\mu_{j}^{f}-\mu_{j}^{b}\over 2}\right)\frac{e^{-\hat{q}m_{j}{\cal J}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\hat{q}m_{j}{\cal J}}}\,.

We can now apply (

\displaystyle\mu_{01}^{b}

\displaystyle=\mu+\mu^{\prime}\,,\quad\mu_{02}^{b}=\mu-\mu^{\prime}\,,\quad\mu_{01}^{f}=\mu^{\prime}\,,\quad\mu_{02}^{f}=-\mu^{\prime}\,,\quad

(101)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{b}

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad\mu_{2}^{b}=0\,,\quad\mu_{1}^{f}=\mu\,,\quad\mu_{2}^{f}=-\mu\,,\quad m_{1}=m_{2}=1\,,

and therefore

\displaystyle E_{\rm T^{4}}({\cal J}\gg 1)=

GSE from TBA

The mirror theory of the mixed-flux
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times T^{4}
string is nonunitary, and it is unclear whether one can justify using it to derive the mixed-flux TBA equations.
5
5

5

The derivation of the TBA for such non-unitary mirror models was considered, at least formally, in
Dei:2018mfl
;
Dei:2018jyj
for the
q=1

Still, one can try to engineer a set of integral equations which would determine the ground state energy of the string theory.
Since the Bethe equations and the bound states of the mixed-flux

Y_{Q}
-functions are small then one can expect that they are given by

\displaystyle Y_{Q}\approx\mu^{2}e^{-L\widetilde{\cal E}_{Q}}\,,\quad\overline{Y}_{Q}\approx\mu^{2}e^{-L\widetilde{\cal E}_{Q}^{*}}\,,

(103)

where
\widetilde{\cal E}_{Q}^{*}
should be complex conjugate to
\widetilde{\cal E}_{Q}
to ensure the reality of the GSE.

For finite

However, in the semi-classical limit
h\to\infty
,
L\to\infty
,
{\cal J}=L/h
fixed they take the following simple form

\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal E}_{Q}={1\over h}\left(\sqrt{{\widetilde{p}}^{2}+\hat{q}^{2}Q^{2}}+iqQ\right)\,.

(104)

Then, the GSE is given by

\displaystyle E({\cal J},\mu,q)

\displaystyle\approx-\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\widetilde{p}\,N_{0}\,Y_{0}-\sum_{Q=1}^{+\infty}\,\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\widetilde{p}\,(Y_{Q}+\overline{Y}_{Q})

(105)

\displaystyle=-N_{0}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\pi{\cal J}}-\mu^{2}\mathcal{I}_{q}({\cal J})\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{q}({\cal J})

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q=1}^{+\infty}\,{1\over\pi}\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\widetilde{p}\,\cos(q{\cal J}Q)\,e^{-{\cal J}\sqrt{{\widetilde{p}}^{2}+\hat{q}^{2}Q^{2}}}

(106)

\displaystyle={1\over\pi}\sum_{Q=1}^{+\infty}\,\hat{q}Q\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{p}\,\cos(q{\cal J}Q)\,e^{-\hat{q}Q{\cal J}\sqrt{p^{2}+1}}\,,

At large
{\cal J}
the main contribution comes from the

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{q}({\cal J}\gg 1)=\hat{q}\cos\left(q{\cal J}\right)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\frac{e^{-\hat{q}{\cal J}}}{\sqrt{\hat{q}{\cal J}}}\,.

(107)

This agrees with the semi-classical calculation in the previous section.

6
Conclusion and remarks

In the present work we have calculated the leading wrapping contribution of massless and massive particles to the ground state energy of the lightcone pure RR
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times T^{4}
superstring with fields subject to twisted boundary conditions by using first the semi-classical string consideration and then the
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times T^{4}
TBA equations with any number
N_{0}
of massless
Y_{0}^{(\dot{\alpha})}
functions. The comparison of the two calculations has shown that the agreement requires
N_{0}=1
contrary to the conjecture made in
Frolov:2021bwp
where
N_{0}
was chosen to be equal to 2.

The
\mu
dependence of the massless contribution in (
81
), however, disagrees with the semi-classical string result (
45
) even if
\mu^{\prime}=\mu
. A reason for the disagreement might be in the order-of-limits problem. In the semi-classical calculation we first take
h,L\to\infty
with
L/h
fixed while in the TBA consideration we take
L\to\infty
keeping
h
fixed. It would be important to analyse the TBA equations in the limit
h,L\to\infty
with
L/h
fixed. If the disagreement would be resolved then it would strongly support the TBA equations with
N_{0}=1
. If the disagreement persists it would be an indication that the
su(2)_{\circ}
invariant S-matrix for massless particles introduced in
Borsato:2014hja
, and assumed to be trivial in
Sundin:2014ema
on the basis of perturbative calculations, is in fact nontrivial nonperturbatively. This might be also necessary in order to introduce the second twist
\mu^{\prime}
in the TBA equations.

The massless contribution has been shown to be proportional to
hL/(4L^{2}-1)
, and the contribution of massive particles appears to be subleading in all cases where
h\ll L
. We have also checked that the generalised Lüscher formula
Luscher:1985dn
;
Janik:2007wt
;
Bajnok:2008bm
;
Hatsuda:2008na
leads to the same leading order results as the TBA.

It would be interesting to analyse the TBA equations at the next-to-leading order where additional nontrivial contributions start to arise due to dressing phase dependent kernels. The GSE at this order can be also found by means of the next-to-leading order Lüscher formula
Ahn:2011xq
;
Bombardelli:2013yka
. Comparison of the results obtained by using the TBA and the Lüscher formula may clarify a reason for the mismatch between semiclassical calculations and the Lüscher formula found in
Abbott:2015pps
.

It would also be important to generalise the recent proposal for the Quantum Spectral Curve (QSC) of pure RR
AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times T^{4}
superstring
Ekhammar:2021pys
;
Cavaglia:2021eqr
to the twisted superstring, and to use the QSC to calculate the GSE. This would be a definite test on whether the QSC describes massless modes and how many of them. The results of
Cavaglia:2022xld
do not answer these questions.

A very important if not ultimate test of the TBA system is to use the contour deformation trick to derive the excited states equations and to solve them numerically for large
h
keeping

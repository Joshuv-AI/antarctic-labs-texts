Abstract.
The octonion offset linear canonical transform (
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT) can be defined as a time-shifted and frequency-modulated version of the octonion linear canonical
transform (
\mathbb{O}-
LCT), a
more general framework of most existing signal
processing tools. In this paper, we first define the (
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT) and provide its closed-form representation. Based on this fact, we study some fundamental properties of proposed transform including inversion formula, norm split and energy conservation. The crux of the paper lies in the generalization of several well known uncertainty relations for the (
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT) that include Pitt’s inequality, logarithmic uncertainty inequality, Hausdorff-Young inequality
and local uncertainty inequalities.

Keywords: Quaternion offset linear canonical transform(QOLCT); Octonion; Octonion Fourier transform
(\mathbb{O}-
OFT); Octonion offset linear canonical transform(
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT); Uncertainty principle.

Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic facts on the octonion algebra and the offset linear canonical transform(OLCT), which will be needed throughout the paper.

2.1.
Octonion algebra

The octonion algebra denoted by
\mathbb{O},

[
32
]
is generated by the eighth-order Cayley-Dickson construction. According to His
construction, a hypercomplex number
o\in\mathbb{O}
is an ordered pair of quaternions
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle o

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{0},q_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((z_{0},z_{1}),(z_{2},z_{3}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{0}+q_{1}.e_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z_{0}+z_{1}.e_{2})+(z_{2}+z_{3}.e_{2}).e_{4}

which has equivalent form

(2.2)

o=s_{o}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}s_{i}e_{i}=s_{0}+s_{1}e_{1}+s_{2}e_{2}+s_{3}e_{3}+s_{4}e_{4}+s_{5}e_{5}+s_{6}e_{6}+s_{7}e_{7}

that is
o
is a hypercomplex number defined by eight real numbers
s_{i},i=0,1,\dots,7
and seven imaginary units
e_{i}
where
i=1,2,\dots,7.
The octonion algebra is non-commutative and non-associative algebra. The multiplication
of imaginary units in the Cayley-Dickson algebra of octonions are presented in Table I or in diagram called Fano scheme, shown in Figure 1.

Table I

Multiplication Rules in Octonion Algebra.

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\cdot&1&e_{1}&e_{2}&e_{3}&e_{4}&e_{5}&e_{6}&e_{7}\\
\hline\cr 1&1&e_{1}&e_{2}&e_{3}&e_{4}&e_{5}&e_{6}&e_{7}\\
\hline\cr e_{1}&e_{1}&-1&e_{3}&-e_{2}&e_{5}&-e_{4}&-e_{7}&e_{6}\\
\hline\cr e_{2}&e_{2}&-e_{3}&-1&e_{1}&e_{6}&e_{7}&-e_{4}&-e_{5}\\
\hline\cr e_{3}&e_{3}&e_{2}&-e_{1}&-1&e_{7}&-e_{6}&e_{5}&-e_{4}\\
\hline\cr e_{4}&e_{4}&-e_{5}&-e_{6}&-e_{7}&-1&e_{1}&e_{2}&e_{3}\\
\hline\cr e_{5}&e_{5}&e_{4}&-e_{7}&e_{6}&-e_{1}&-1&-e_{3}&e_{2}\\
\hline\cr e_{6}&e_{6}&e_{7}&e_{4}&-e_{5}&-e_{2}&e_{3}&-1&-e_{1}\\
\hline\cr e_{7}&e_{7}&-e_{6}&e_{5}&e_{4}&-e_{3}&-e_{2}&e_{1}&-1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

The conjugate of an octonion is defined as

(2.3)

\overline{o}=s_{0}-s_{1}e_{1}-s_{2}e_{2}-s_{3}e_{3}-s_{4}e_{4}-s_{5}e_{5}-s_{6}e_{6}-s_{7}e_{7}

Therefore norm is defined by
|o|=\sqrt{o\overline{o}}
and
|o|^{2}=\sum_{i=o}^{7}s_{i}.
Also
|o_{1}o_{2}|=|o_{1}||o_{2}|,\forall o_{1},o_{2}\in\mathbb{O}.

From (
2.1
) it is evident that every
o\in\mathbb{O}
can be represented in quaternion form as

(2.4)

o=a+be_{4}

where
a=s_{0}+s_{1}e_{1}+s_{2}e_{2}+s_{3}e_{3}
and
b=s_{4}+s_{5}e_{1}+s_{6}e_{2}+s_{7}e_{3}
are both quaternions. By direct verification we have following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{H},
then

(1)
e_{4}a=\overline{a}e_{4};
(2)
e_{4}(ae_{4})=-\overline{a};

(3)\quad(ae_{4})e_{4}=-a;

(4)
a(be_{4})=(ba)e_{4}
;  (5)
(ae_{4})b=(a\overline{b})e_{4};
(6)
(ae_{4})(be_{4})=-\overline{b}a.

It is clear from above Lemma that, for an octonion
a+be_{4},a,b\in\mathbb{H},
we have

(2.5)

\overline{a+be_{4}}=\overline{a}-be_{4}

and

(2.6)

|a+be_{4}|^{2}=|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}.

An octonion-valued function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O}
has following explicit form

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}+f_{1}(x)e_{1}+f_{2}(x)e_{2}+f_{3}(x)e_{3}+f_{4}(x)e_{4}+f_{5}(x)e_{5}+f_{6}(x)e_{6}+f_{7}(x)e_{7}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(x)+h(x)e_{4}

where each
f_{i}(x)
is a real vaiued functions,
g,h\in\mathbb{H}
and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

For each octonion-valued function
f(x)
over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1\leq p<\infty,
the
L^{p}-
norm of
f
is defined by

(2.8)

\|f\|^{p}_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(x)|^{p}dx.

And for
p=\infty
, then the
L^{\infty}
-norm is defined by

(2.9)

\|f\|_{\infty}=esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(x)|.

2.2.
Offset linear canonical transform

The offset linear canonical transform(OLCT)
[
33
]
of any function

with respect to the matrix parameter
A=(a,b,c,d,e,\tau,\eta)
is defined as

(2.10)

\mathcal{O}_{A}\big[f(x)\big]w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)K_{A}^{i}(x,w)dx.

with

(2.11)

K_{A}(x,w)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}e^{\frac{{i}}{2b}\big[ax^{2}-2x(w-\tau)-2w(d\tau-b\eta)+d(w^{2}+\tau^{2})-\frac{\pi}{2}\big]},b\neq 0\,

where
e^{\frac{-i\pi}{4}}
is the polar form of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}.

And the Offset linear canonical transform in quaternion
[
34
]
setting is given as:

Let

A_{s}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{s}&b_{s}|&\tau_{s}\\
c_{s}&d_{s}|&\eta_{s}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a matrix parameter satisfying
{\rm det}(A_{s})=1
, for
s=1,2
. Then QLCT of signal
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
is defined by

(2.12)

\mathcal{O}_{i,j}^{A_{1},A_{2}}[f]({w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f({x})K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},w_{1})K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},w_{2})\rm{d}{x},

where
{w}=(w_{1},w_{2}),x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and the kernel signals
K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},w_{1})
,
K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},w_{2})
are respectively given by

(2.13)

K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},w_{1})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{1}{i}}}\,e^{\frac{{i}}{2b_{1}}\big[a_{1}x_{1}^{2}-2x_{1}(w_{1}-\tau_{1})-2w_{1}(d_{1}\tau_{1}-b_{1}\eta_{1})+d_{1}(w_{1}^{2}+\tau_{1}^{2})\big]},b_{1}\neq 0\,

(2.14)

K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},w_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{2}{j}}}\,e^{\frac{{j}}{2b_{2}}\big[a_{2}x_{2}^{2}-2x_{2}(w_{2}-\tau_{2})-2w_{2}(d_{2}\tau_{2}-b_{2}\eta_{2})+d_{2}(w_{2}^{2}+\tau_{2}^{2})\big]},\,b_{2}\neq 0\,

Octonion Fourier transform

Let

\mathbb{O}-

[

(3.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-\mu_{4}2\pi xw}dx,

with inversion

(3.2)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{4}}^{-1}\{\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{4}}\{f\}\}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)e^{\mu_{4}2\pi xw}dx,

And for octonion valued function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O})
, the three dimensional
\mathbb{O}-
FT
[
20
,
24
]
is defined as

(3.3)

\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)e^{-\mu_{1}2\pi x_{1}w_{1}}e^{-\mu_{2}2\pi x_{2}w_{2}}e^{-\mu_{4}2\pi x_{3}w_{3}}dx,

with inversion

(3.4)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}\}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)e^{-\mu_{1}2\pi x_{1}w_{1}}e^{-\mu_{2}2\pi x_{2}w_{2}}e^{-\mu_{4}2\pi x_{3}w_{3}}dw,

where
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

The multiplication in the above integrals is done from left to right as the octonion is non-
associative. And the order of imaginary units in (

[
36
]

Let

\mathbb{O}-

b\neq 0.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||g_{e}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||g_{o}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||h_{e}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||h_{o}(x)|^{2}dx.

By logarithmic uncertainty principle for the QOLCT given in lemma
4.2
, we get

(4.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln\left|\frac{w}{b}\right|\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{g_{e}\}(w)\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||g_{e}(x)|^{2}dx\geq D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|g_{e}(x)|^{2}dx.

Similarly ,

(4.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln\left|\frac{w}{b}\right|\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{g_{e}\}(w)\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||g_{e}(x)|^{2}dx\geq D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|g_{o}(x)|^{2}dx,

(4.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln\left|\frac{w}{b}\right|\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{g_{e}\}(w)\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||g_{e}(x)|^{2}dx\geq D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|h_{e}(x)|^{2}dx.

(4.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln\left|\frac{w}{b}\right|\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{g_{e}\}(w)\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x||g_{e}(x)|^{2}dx\geq D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|h_{o}(x)|^{2}dx.

Collecting all equations (
4.9
,
4.10
,
4.11
,
4.12
) and making use of (
4.1
,
4.1
), we obtain the desired result

(4.13)

2\pi|b_{3}|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln\left|\frac{w}{b}\right|\left|{\mathcal{O}}^{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}}\{f\}(w)\right|^{2}dw+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x||f(x)|^{2}dx\geq D\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|f(x)|^{2}dx.

Which completes the proof
∎

4.2.
Hausdorff-Young inequality for
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT

In this subsection we will establish Hausdorff-Young inequality which is very important in signal processing. This inequality will be helpful for researchers in establishing Shannon’s entropy uncertainty relation.

Lemma 4.3

(Hausdorff-Young inequality for QOLCT)
.

[
26
]
For
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,
we have

(4.14)

\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},}\{f\}(w)\|_{q}\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}|b_{1}b_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f(x)\|_{p}.

Theorem 4.3

(Hausdorff-Young inequality for
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT)
.

For
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,
we have

(4.15)

\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)\|_{q}\leq{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2q}-\frac{1}{p}}}|b_{1}b_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}|b_{3}|^{-\frac{1}{2q}}\|f(x)\|_{p}.

Proof.

From (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)\|_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi|b_{3}|)^{\frac{1}{2q}}}\left(\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{g_{e}\}(w)\|_{q}+\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{g_{o}\}(w)\|_{q}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{h_{e}\}(w)\|_{q}+\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{h_{o}\}(w)\|_{q}\right)

Now applying lemma
4.3
to the R.H.S of above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi|b_{3}|)^{\frac{1}{2q}}}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}|b_{1}b_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|g_{e}(x)\|_{p}+\|g_{o}(x)\|_{p}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|h_{e}(x)\|_{p}+\|h_{o}(x)\|_{p}\right).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2q}-\frac{1}{p}}}|b_{1}b_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}|b_{3}|^{-\frac{1}{2q}}\|f(x)\|_{p}

where last equality follows from (
3.2
).

Which completes the proof
∎

4.3.
Local uncertainty principle for
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT

Local uncertainty principle states that if
f
is highly localized, then the octonion Fourier transform can not
be concentrated in a small neighborhood of two or more separated points. We shall establish Local uncertainty principle for
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT in this subsection.

Lemma 4.4

(Local uncertainty principle for QOLCT)
.

[
26
]

(1) For
0<\alpha<1
and for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}),
there is a constant
M_{\alpha}
and all measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that holds

(4.16)

\int_{bE}|\mathcal{O}^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\{f\}(w)|^{2}dw\leq M_{\alpha}|E|^{\alpha}\||x|^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{2}.

(2) If
\alpha>1
,and for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}),
there is a constant
M_{\alpha}
and all measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that holds

(4.17)

\int_{bE}|\mathcal{O}^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\{f\}(w)|^{2}dw\leq M_{\alpha}|b_{1}b_{2}|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{\alpha}}|E|^{\alpha}\|f\|^{2-2\alpha}_{2}\||x|^{\alpha}f\|^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}_{2},

(4.18)

M_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\dfrac{(1+\alpha^{2})}{\alpha^{2\alpha}}(2-2\alpha)^{\alpha-2},&0<\alpha<1,\\
\\
\dfrac{\pi}{\alpha\Gamma(1/2)}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)(\alpha-1)^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{-1},&\alpha>1,\\
\\
\end{array}\right.

Theorem 4.4

(Local uncertainty principle for
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT)
.

(1) For
0<\alpha<1
and for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O}),
there is a constant
M_{\alpha}
and all measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that holds

(4.19)

\int_{bE}|\mathcal{O}^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\{f\}(w)|^{2}dw\leq\frac{1}{2\pi|b_{3}|}M_{\alpha}|E|^{\alpha}\||x|^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{2}.

(2) If
\alpha>1
,and for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{O}),
there is a constant
M_{\alpha}
and all measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that holds

(4.20)

\int_{bE}|\mathcal{O}^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\{f\}(w)|^{2}dw\leq\frac{1}{2\pi|b_{3}|}M_{\alpha}|b_{1}b_{2}|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{\alpha}}|E|^{\alpha}\|f\|^{2-2\alpha}_{2}\||x|^{\alpha}f\|^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}_{2},

(4.21)

M_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\dfrac{(1+\alpha^{2})}{\alpha^{2\alpha}}(2-2\alpha)^{\alpha-2},&0<\alpha<1,\\
\\
\dfrac{\pi}{\alpha\Gamma(1/2)}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)(\alpha-1)^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{-1},&\alpha>1,\\
\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By the splitting of
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT in (
3.3
),we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b_{3}|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g_{e}(x)K_{A_{1}}^{\mu_{1}}(x_{1},w_{1})K_{A_{2}}^{\mu_{2}}(x_{2},w_{2})c_{3}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b_{3}|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}h_{o}(x)K_{A_{1}}^{-\mu_{1}}(x_{1},w_{1})K_{A_{2}}^{-\mu_{2}}(x_{2},w_{2})s_{3}dx

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b_{3}|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}h_{e}(x)K_{A_{1}}^{-\mu_{1}}(x_{1},w_{1})K_{A_{2}}^{-\mu_{2}}(x_{2},w_{2})c_{3}dx\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b_{3}|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g_{o}(x)K_{A_{1}}^{\mu_{1}}(x_{1},w_{1})K_{A_{2}}^{\mu_{2}}(x_{2},w_{2})s_{3}dx\right)\mu_{4}.

Setting
f_{m}(x)=g_{e}(x_{+++})+h_{o}(x_{+-+})e_{2}
and
f_{n}(x)x=h_{e}(x_{+++})-g_{o}(x_{+-+})e_{2}
. Then
\mathbb{O}-
OLCT can be written as the combination of two QOLCTs as

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi|b_{3}|}\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{f_{m}\}(w)\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{f_{n}\}(w)\|^{2}

Now, by lemma
4.4
we have

(4.23)

\int_{bE}|\mathcal{O}^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\{f_{j}\}(w)|^{2}dw\leq M_{\alpha}|E|^{\alpha}\||x|^{\alpha}f_{j}\|^{2}_{2},\quad j=m,n

Therefore from (
4.3
),(
4.23

2.1
,we have

\displaystyle\int_{bE}\|O^{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{4}}\{f\}(w)\|^{2}dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi|b_{3}|}\int_{bE}\left(\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{f_{m}\}(w)\|^{2}dw+\|\mathcal{O}^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\{f_{n}\}(w)\|^{2}dw\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi|b_{3}|}M_{\alpha}|E|^{\alpha}\left(\||x|^{\alpha}f_{m}\|^{2}+\||x|^{\alpha}f_{n}\|^{2}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi|b_{3}|}M_{\alpha}|E|^{\alpha}\||x|^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{2}

which completes the proof.

Similarly we can easily prove (

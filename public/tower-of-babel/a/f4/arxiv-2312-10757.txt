More characterizations of morphic words
Golnaz Badkobeh, Pascal Ochem

Abstract
An interesting phenomenon in combinatorics on words is when every recurrent word satisfying some avoidance constraints has the same factor set as a morphic word. An early example is the Hall-Thue word, fixed point of the morphism 0 → 012 \texttt{0}\to\texttt{012} , 1 → 02 \texttt{1}\to\texttt{02} , 2 → 1 \texttt{2}\to\texttt{1} , which is essentially the only ternary word avoiding squares and the words 010 and 212 . We provide some examples of this phenomenon from various contexts.

Introduction

Let
\Sigma_{k}
denote the
k
-letter alphabet
\left\{\texttt{0,1},\dots,\texttt{k-1}\right\}
.
Let
\varepsilon
denote the empty word.
A finite word is

in an infinite word

if it appears as a factor of
w
infinitely many times.
An infinite word

is

if all its finite factors are recurrent in
w
.
If a morphism

is such that

starts with

, then the

of

is the unique word
w=f^{\omega}(\mathtt{0})
starting with

and satisfying

.
An infinite word is

if it is the fixed point of a morphism.
An infinite word is
morphic
if it is the image
g(f^{\omega}(\texttt{0}))
by a morphism
g
of a pure morphic word
f^{\omega}(\texttt{0})
.
A pattern
P
is a finite word of variables over the alphabet
\left\{A,B,\dots\right\}
.
We say that a variable is
isolated
if it appears exactly once in the pattern.
Following Cassaigne
[
6
]
, we associate to a pattern

the

f

obtained by replacing every isolated variable in
p
by a dot.
For example, the formula associated to the pattern
ABBACABADAA
is
ABBA.ABA.AA
.
The factors between the dots are called

.
An

of a formula
f
in a word
w
is a non-erasing morphism
h:\Delta^{*}\to\Sigma^{*}

such that the
h
-image of every fragment of
f
is a factor of
w
.
A word
w
(finite or infinite)

a formula

if it contains no occurrence of
f
.

A

is a factor of the form
r=u^{n}v
, where

is non-empty and

is a prefix of
u
.
Then
|u|
is the

of the repetition

and its

is
|r|/|u|
.
A

is a repetition of exponent 2. Equivalently, it is an occurrence of the pattern
AA
.
An

is a repetition with exponent strictly greater than 2.
We use the notation
SQ_{t}
for the pattern corresponding to squares with period at least
t
,
that is,
SQ_{1}=AA
,
SQ_{2}=ABAB
,
SQ_{3}=ABCABC
, and so on.
A morphism
m
is given in the format
m(\texttt{0})/m(\texttt{1})/...

A popular theme in combinatorics on words is to construct or at least prove the existence of
an infinite word over
\Sigma_{k}
that satisfies some given avoidance constraints, such as
limiting or forbidding repetitions and/or patterns.
A step further is to describe the avoiding words over
\Sigma_{k}
, and the following situations can occur.

1.

There are exponentially many avoiding words (with respect to their length).
Classical examples are square free words over
\Sigma_{3}

and words over
\Sigma_{2}
that contain only 3 distinct squares
[
22
,
15
]
.

2.

Every recurrent avoiding word has the same factor set as one morphic word.
For example, Thue
[
23
,
24
]
proved that every bi-infinite word
avoiding squares and the words
010
and
212
has the same factor set as
the fixed point of the morphism
\texttt{0}\to\texttt{012}
,
\texttt{1}\to\texttt{02}
,
\texttt{2}\to\texttt{1}
.

3.

There is a finite set
S
of morphic words such that every recurrent avoiding word has the same factor set as one word in
S
. For example, every bi-infinite binary word avoiding the formula
AA.ABA.ABBA

has the same factor set as one of 4 specific binary morphic words
[
19
]
.

4.

There are polynomially many avoiding words, but there are infinitely many recurrent avoiding words
such that no two of them have the same factor set. The only known examples of this case are the binary words
avoiding
ABACA.ABCA
and the binary words avoiding
ABAC.BACA.ABCA

[
20
]
.

In this paper, we identify quite natural examples of the second situation, with a unique morphic word.
Let

be a morphic word over an alphabet of exactly
k

be a set of avoidance constraints.
We say that
\mathcal{C}

characterizes

if every recurrent word over
\Sigma_{k}
avoiding

has the same factor set as

Every morphic word can be written as

in many different ways.
In particular,

can be chosen to be a coding (i.e.,

For our morphic words that admit a characterization, the defining morphisms are found
such that the fixed point

is a famous pure morphic word

2.3

A

r_{2}=g_{12}(r_{3})
for some bi-infinite ternary word

.
Now,
g_{12}(r_{3})
and

have the same set of factors of length 120.
So
r_{3}

have the same set of factors of length

In particular,

Then
r_{3}
also avoids squares with period at least 21, since otherwise

w_{3}
.
By taking the

∎

3.2
Few occurrences of
ABBA

A pattern

is

if every variable appears at least twice in

contains no isolated variable.
It is known that doubled patterns are 3-avoidable
[
17
]
, that many doubled patterns are 2-avoidable
[
18
]
,
and it is conjectured that only finitely many doubled patterns are not 2-avoidable.
Let

be the minimum number of distinct occurrences of
P
in an infinite word over
\Sigma_{k}
.
So
oc_{k}(P)=0
if
P
is
k
-avoidable and we leave as an exercise the proof that
oc_{k}(P)=\infty

if
P
is not doubled and is not
k
-avoidable.
The many known constructions of infinite binary words with only three distinct squares (see
[
13
]
for a survey)
imply that

and that there are exponentially many such words.
We have checked

.

Now, we show by contradiction that

Because of the square

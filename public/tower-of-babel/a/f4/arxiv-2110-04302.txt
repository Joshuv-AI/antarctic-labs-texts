Now, we consider the probability that a given universal primorial is prime. Formally, the probability that a number is prime is either zero or one, but the Prime Number Theorem (PNT) maintains that

The probability that

Definition 1.5

A Lemma on Universal Primorials

A universal primorial is never divisible by any prime

p_{n}

x_{0}

x_{0}

2.1
shows that

and is proved next. Note that instead of leaving

to minimize

Lemma 2.1

Let

(2.1)

p_{n}\#<\prod_{p\leqslant\sqrt{x}}p,\hskip 14.22636ptx>106.

Proof.

Suppose that

. It follows that

where

is the subsequent prime to

. Substitute

because

Brun
[
4
]

Theorem 5.1

(
Brun).

For
x\geqslant 3
,

(5.1)

\pi_{2}(x)=O\bigg(\frac{x(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log^{2}(x)}\bigg).

Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
1.11
together supply an alternative proof to Brun’s Theorem, the former being provided and proved quickly below.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider
x=Kp_{n}\#+g
and suppose
\alpha(x,k;p_{n})\sim 1
. We have

\frac{\log p_{n}}{\log x}=\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}(1+o(1)).

Proof.

Since
\alpha(x,k;p_{n})\approxeq 1

, we have

\frac{\log\log x-\log(1+\epsilon_{0}(p_{n}))}{\log x}<\frac{\log p_{n}}{\log x}<\frac{\log\log x-\log(1-\epsilon_{0}(p_{n}))}{\log x},

for

. Take the bounds given by Dusart in Eq. (

) so that

and

, yielding

Lemma
5.2
follows immediately.

The derivation of Brun’s Theorem is shown now. Let

and suppose that
\alpha(x,k;p_{n})\sim 1

\pi_{2}(x)\ll B_{0}\sum_{j=2}^{x}\left(\frac{\log\log j}{\log j}\right)^{2},

\begin{split}\pi_{2}(x)&\ll B_{0}\left(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\right)^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{x}\left(\frac{\log x(\log\log j)}{(\log\log x)\log j}\right)^{2}=O\bigg(\frac{x(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log^{2}(x)}\bigg).\end{split}

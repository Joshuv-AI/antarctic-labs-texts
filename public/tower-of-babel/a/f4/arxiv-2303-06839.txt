A closed-form expression for the variance of truncated distribution and its uses
Roberto Vila, Narayanaswamy Balakrishnan, Raul Matsushita

Abstract
This work sheds some light on the relationship between a distribution’s standard deviation and its range, a topic that has been discussed extensively in the literature. While many previous studies have proposed inequalities or relationships that depend on the shape of the population distribution, the approach here is built on a family of bounded probability distributions based on skewing functions. We offer closed-form expressions for its moments and the asymptotic behavior as the support’s semi-range tends to zero and ∞ \infty . We also establish an inequality in which the well-known Popoviciu’s one is a special case. Finally, we provide an example using US dollar prices in four different currencies traded on foreign exchange markets to illustrate the results developed here.

Introduction

Relating the standard deviation (
\sigma
) to the range is a well-studied topic
(
Tippett, 1925
;
Popoviciu, 1935
;
Shone, 1949
;
David et al., 1954
,
see
)
. However, most of the results found in the literature in this regard propose inequalities or relationships that depend on the shape of the population distribution.
Matsushita et al., 2020
;
Matsushita et al., 2023
recently suggested a power law between
\sigma
and the semi-range
\ell
without knowing the population distribution, but assuming symmetric truncated forms restricted to
\ell\ll 1
. They argued that truncation is a phenomenon naturally generated by the sampling process. In their approach, conditional distribution properties can link the usual unbounded distribution for describing unobserved data.

Here, we establish a closed-form expression for the variance of truncated distributions valid for all
\ell>0
. We start with the general case (Section
2.1
), by considering a truncated variable
X
over the interval
[a,b]
based on a cumulative distribution function
G
with unbounded support as a skewing function. For
c\in(a,b)
and
p>0
, we present a general form for
\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]
as well as its asymptotic behavior as the support’s semi-range tends to zero and
\infty
. We also present some inequalities that included Popoviciu’s inequality as a special case.

Section
2.2
presents some properties and examples regarding the symmetrically truncated distribution as a particular case. Importantly, we deduce the form of the ratio
\sigma/\ell
as a function of
G
and
\ell
. For illustrative purposes, we have presented an example using actual financial data (Section
3
). They consist of 16 million tick-by-tick returns of four currencies against the US dollar transacted on foreign exchange markets. Finally, Section
4
makes some brief concluding remarks.

2
Main results

2.1
General case

Suppose we have a random variable with cumulative distribution function (CDF)
G(x)
and with infinite support. Based on
G
, given two real numbers
a
and
b
, such that
a<b
, we have

\displaystyle F_{X}(x)={G(x)-G(a)\over G(b)-G(a)},\quad a<x<b,

(2.1)

to be the truncated CDF of a random variable
X
with support
(a,b)
. Then, we have the following result concerning its moments.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
c
and
p
be real numbers such that
a<c<b
and
p>0
.
Then, the
p
-th moment about
c
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]=\dfrac{1}{G(b)-G(a)}\,\big[(b-c)^{p}G(b)-(a-c)^{p}G(a)-pI_{G}(c;a-c,b-c,p)\big],

where
I_{G}(c;s,t,p)
is defined as

\displaystyle I_{G}(c;s,t,p)=\int_{s}^{t}y^{p-1}G(y+c)\,{\rm d}y,\quad s<t.

Proof.

From (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]

\displaystyle={1\over G(b)-G(a)}\int_{a}^{b}(x-c)^{p}\,{\rm d}G(x)

\displaystyle={p\over G(b)-G(a)}\left\{\int_{a}^{c}\left[\,\int_{0}^{x-c}y^{p-1}\,{\rm d}y\right]{\rm d}G(x)+\int_{c}^{b}\left[\,\int_{0}^{x-c}y^{p-1}\,{\rm d}y\right]{\rm d}G(x)\right\}.

Change the order of integration,
the above expression can be written as

\displaystyle={p\over G(b)-G(a)}\left\{\int_{0}^{a-c}y^{p-1}\left[\,\int_{a}^{y+c}{\rm d}G(x)\right]{\rm d}y+\int_{0}^{b-c}y^{p-1}\left[\,\int_{y+c}^{b}{\rm d}G(x)\right]{\rm d}y\right\}

\displaystyle={1\over G(b)-G(a)}\left[{(b-c)^{p}G(b)}-{(a-c)^{p}G(a)}\right]

\displaystyle-{p\over G(b)-G(a)}\int_{a-c}^{0}y^{p-1}G(y+c)\,{\rm d}y-{p\over G(b)-G(a)}\int_{0}^{b-c}y^{p-1}G(y+c)\,{\rm d}y,

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2.1
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\leqslant\begin{cases}(b-c)^{p}[1-F_{X}(c)]+(a-c)^{p}F_{X}(c),&\text{if}\ p\ \text{is even},\\[5.69046pt]
(b-c)^{p}[1-F_{X}(c)],&\text{if}\ p\ \text{is odd},\end{cases}

where
F_{X}
is as given in (
2.1
).

Proof.

It is evident that

\displaystyle I_{G}(c;a-c,b-c,p)=I_{G}(c;a-c,0,p)+I_{G}(c;0,b-c,p).

(2.2)

Assuming
p
to be even, for
a-c<y<0
, we have
y^{p-1}G(c)<y^{p-1}G(y+c)<y^{p-1}G(a)
, and for
0<y<b-c
, we have
y^{p-1}G(c)<y^{p-1}G(y+c)<y^{p-1}G(b)
. Consequently, we get

\displaystyle I_{G}(c;a-c,0,p)\geqslant-{G(c)(c-a)^{p}\over p}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{G}(c;0,b-c,p)\geqslant{G(c)(b-c)^{p}\over p}.

Now, upon using these inequalities in (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{G}(c;a-c,b-c,p)\geqslant{G(c)\over p}\,\big[(b-c)^{p}-(c-a)^{p}\big].

Using the above inequality in the expression in Theorem
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\leqslant\dfrac{1}{G(b)-G(a)}\,\big\{(b-c)^{p}G(b)-(a-c)^{p}G(a)-G(c)\big[(b-c)^{p}-(c-a)^{p}\big]\big\}.

From (
2.1
), the right-hand side of the above inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle=(b-c)^{p}[1-F_{X}(c)]+(a-c)^{p}F_{X}(c).

This proves the inequality for the case when
p
is even.

The inequality for the case when
p
is odd can be established in an analogous manner.
∎

When
p
is even, a lower bound for
\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]
can be established as below.

Proposition 2.3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2.1
,
for
p
even, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\geqslant(t-c)^{p}\left[F_{X}(b)-F_{X}(t)\right],\quad\text{if}\ c<t<b.

Proof.

Suppose
p
is even. Then, as

(X-c)^{p}\geqslant 0
and
(X-c)^{p}\geqslant(t-c)^{p}
for
X>t>c
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\geqslant\mathbb{E}\big[(X-c)^{p}\mathds{1}_{\{X>t\}}\big]\geqslant(t-c)^{p}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{X>t\}}\big],

which yields the required result.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2.1
,
we have

\displaystyle\min_{a<c<b}\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\leqslant\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{b-a}{2}\right)^{p},&\text{if}\ p\ \text{is even},\\[5.69046pt]
0,&\text{if}\ p\geqslant 1\ \text{is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Suppose
p
is even. In this case, from Corollary
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\leqslant(b-c)^{p}[1-F_{X}(c)]+(a-c)^{p}F_{X}(c)\leqslant S(F(c))T(c),

(2.3)

where
S(F(c))=\max\left\{1-F_{X}(c),F_{X}(c)\right\}
and
T(c)=(b-c)^{p}+(a-c)^{p}
. As
F_{X}(c)=1/2
is a minimum point of
M(F(c))
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{0<F(c)<1}S(F(c))={1\over 2}.

Taking the minimum over
0<F(c)<1
in (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\leqslant{1\over 2}\,T(c).

Now, taking the minimum over
a<c<b
in the above inequality and using the fact that the function
T(c)
reaches a minimum value at the point
c=(a+b)/2
, we get

\displaystyle\min_{a<c<b}\mathbb{E}[(X-c)^{p}]\leqslant{1\over 2}\,T\left({a+b\over 2}\right)=\left(\dfrac{b-a}{2}\right)^{p}.

This proves the inequality for the case when
p
is even. Further,
the inequality for the case when
p
is odd trivially follows from Corollary
2.2
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The variance
\sigma^{2}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}={\rm Var}(X)=\dfrac{1}{G(b)-G(a)}\,\big[(b-\mu)^{2}G(b)-(a-\mu)^{2}G(a)-2I_{G}(\mu;a-\mu,b-\mu)\big],

where
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)
and

\displaystyle I_{G}(\mu;s,t)=\int_{s}^{t}yG(y+\mu)\,{\rm d}y,\quad s<t.

Proof.

By taking
c=\mu
and
p=2
in Theorem
2.1
,
we obtain the required result.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

The Popoviciu inequality
(
Popoviciu, 1935
)
on variances given by

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\leqslant\left(\dfrac{b-a}{2}\right)^{2},

follows from Proposition
2.4
.

Proof.

The proof follows immediately by setting
p=2
in Proposition
2.4
.
∎

A reverse form of Popoviciu’s inequality can be obtained upon taking
p=2,c=\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)
and
t=(\mu+b)/2
in Proposition
2.3
.

Proposition 2.7
.

We have

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\geqslant\left(\dfrac{b-\mu}{2}\right)^{2}\left[F_{X}(b)-F_{X}\left({\mu+b\over 2}\right)\right].

Asymptotic behavior

In the following theorem, we establish the asymptotic behaviour of the
p
-th moment.

Theorem 2.8
.

If
X
is distributed as in (
2.1
), then
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\dfrac{X-a}{b-a}\right)^{p}\,\right]={1\over p+1},\quad p>-1,

and

\displaystyle\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\dfrac{X-a}{b-a}\right)^{p}\,\right]={1\over 2^{p}}.

Proof.

From (
2.1
), we find

\displaystyle F_{\frac{X-a}{b-a}}(z)={G\big(z(b-a)+a\big)-G(a)\over G(b)-G(a)}=\dfrac{G\big({b-a\over 2}\,(2z-1)+{a+b\over 2}\big)-G\big({a+b\over 2}-{b-a\over 2}\big)}{G\big({b-a\over 2}+{a+b\over 2}\big)-G\big({a+b\over 2}-{b-a\over 2}\big)},\ 0<z<1.

Now, it is a simple task to verify that

\displaystyle\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow 0}F_{\frac{X-a}{b-a}}(z)=F_{U}(z),\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R},

and

\displaystyle\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow\infty}F_{\frac{X-a}{b-a}}(z)=F_{Y}(z),\quad\forall z\neq{1\over 2},

where
U\sim U(0,1)
and
Y
is a discrete random variable such that
\mathbb{P}(Y=1/2)=1
.

Moreover, we note that
[(X-a)/(b-a)]^{p}
is uniformly integrable because
0<(X-a)/(b-a)<1
. As convergence in distribution along with uniform integrability imply convergence in mean
(cf.
Billingsley, 1968
, Theorem 5.4)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\dfrac{X-a}{b-a}\right)^{p}\,\right]=\mathbb{E}(U^{p}),\quad p>-1,

and

\displaystyle\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\dfrac{X-a}{b-a}\right)^{p}\,\right]=\mathbb{E}(Y^{p}),

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

We further have

\displaystyle\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow 0^{+}}{\sigma^{2}+(\mu-a)^{2}\over(b-a)^{2}}={1\over 3}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{{b-a\over 2}\longrightarrow\infty}{\sigma^{2}\over(b-a)^{2}}=0.

where
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)
and
\sigma^{2}={\rm Var}(X)
.

Proof.

By taking
p=2
in Theorem
2.8
, the above results follow readily.
∎

2.2
Symmetric case

In this section, we assume that
G
is a skewing function, i.e., it is such that
G(x)\geqslant 0
and
G(-x)=1-G(x)
, and
X
distributed as in (
2.1
) with
a=-\ell
and
b=\ell>0
. That is,
X
has its CDF as

\displaystyle F_{X}(x)={G(x)+G(\ell)-1\over 2G(\ell)-1},\quad-\ell<x<\ell.

(2.4)

Proposition 2.10
.

The variance
\sigma^{2}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}=\ell^{2}H(\ell),

where

\displaystyle H(\ell)=1-\dfrac{2C(\ell)-1}{2G(\ell)-1},

with
C(\ell)=C(\ell,G)
defined as

\displaystyle C(\ell)={2\over\ell^{2}}\int_{0}^{\ell}yG(y)\,{\rm d}y.

Moreover,
1/2\leqslant C(\ell)\leqslant G(\ell)
.

Proof.

As
G
is a skewing function, we have
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)=0
. Moreover,
I_{G}(\mu;a-\mu,b-\mu)
in Proposition
2.5
satisfies the identity

\displaystyle I_{G}(\mu;a-\mu,b-\mu)=\ell^{2}\left[C(\ell)-{1\over 2}\right].

Upon substituting this in Proposition
2.5
and carrying out some simple algebraic steps, the required result follows.
∎

3
Illustration with financial data

We illustrate the results developed here with intraday spot exchange rate data of four currencies against the US dollar transacted on the foreign exchange (Forex) market. There are 16 million tick-by-tick returns of bid prices provided by Tick Data, LLC (Table
2
). Following the discussion regarding the truncated nature of the past
(
Matsushita et al., 2020
;
Matsushita et al., 2023
)
, we consider the symmetric case here.

For each currency, let
\{X_{ij}\}
be the
j
th return observed on day
i=1,\ldots,d
(Table
2
). Taking the daily sample standard deviation
s_{i}
and the maximum daily absolute return
\ell_{i}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n_{i}}\{X_{ij}\}
, where
n_{i}
denotes the sample size on day
i
with
n=\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}
, Figure
1
depicts the daily sample ratios
\{s_{i}/\ell_{i}\}
in the form of dots.

Now, consider the general sequence ignoring days as
\{X_{t}\}
, where
t=1,\ldots,n
. Letting

\ell^{*}=\max_{1\leq t\leq n}\{X_{t}\}
, we generated a grid of 1,000 truncation points,

\{\ell:\ell=m\ell^{*}/1000,\text{where }m=1,\ldots,1000\}
. For each
\ell
over this grid, we obtained the sample standard deviation of the conditional (truncated) data
\{X_{t}||X_{t}|\leq\ell\}
.
In this way, we empirically find the form of the ratio
\sigma/\ell
for the returns of each currency (Figure
1
). Then, Proposition
2.10
provides a feasible and practical way of describing the relationship between the variance and the cutoff
\ell
. For small
\ell
,
Matsushita et al., 2020
;
Matsushita et al., 2023
proposed the power law
\sigma^{\beta}/\ell\approx\zeta
from a second-order approximation, where
\beta>0
and
\zeta
are real constants. So, we may approximate
\sigma/\ell
as

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{\ell}\approx\zeta^{1/\beta}\ell^{-1+1/\beta}.

Figure
2
depicts the log-log plots of this approximated result, and shows the validity of such a power law approximation for
\sigma/\ell
.

4
Concluding remarks

In this work, we have presented a general approach for understanding the relationship between the variance and the range of a general family of truncated distributions based on skewing functions. We have established a closed-form expression for its moments and their asymptotic behavior as the support’s semi-range tends to zero and
\infty
.

As discussed previously by
Matsushita et al., 2020
;
Matsushita et al., 2023
, if the truncated nature arises naturally from the past, the function relating truncation length and standard deviation may assist in connecting the bounded past and unbounded future data. For this reason, we expect our results to be useful in many practical situations.

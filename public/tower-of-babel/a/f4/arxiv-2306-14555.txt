Application and analysis of MUSIC algorithm for anomaly detection in microwave imaging without a switching device
Won-Kwang Park

Abstract
Although the MUltiple SIgnal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm has demonstrated suitability as a microwave imaging technique for detecting anomalies, there is a fundamental limit that it requires a switching device to be used which permits an antenna to transmit and receive signals simultaneously. In this paper, we design a MUSIC-type imaging function using scattering parameter data to find small anomaly and explore its mathematical structure. Considering the investigated structure, we confirm that the imaging performance is highly dependent on the antenna configurations and suggest an arrangement of antennas to enhance imaging performance. Simulation results with synthetic data are displayed to support theoretical result.

Let us emphasize that for a successful application of MUSIC in microwave imaging, the generated scattering matrix whose elements are scattering parameters must be symmetric. If the matrix is non-symmetric, it is impossible to apply the MUSIC directly. Due to this reason, most of researches has been performed with the strong assumption that the antenna can transmit and receive signals, i.e., switching devices can be used. However, the issue of microwave imaging without a switching device may also exist as a result of limited system configurations.

In this paper, we consider the application of MUSIC algorithm for identifying small anomaly from measured scattering parameter data at a fixed frequency when the switching device is not available. To this end, we assume that the scattering matrix is non-symmetric, design a new imaging function of MUSIC based on the structure of left- and right-singular vectors associated with nonzero singular values of the scattering matrix, and show that it can be represented by an infinite series of Bessel functions and antenna arrangements. This is based on the application of the Born approximation and integral equation formula for the scattering parameter in the existence of a small anomaly. Based on the theoretical result, we confirm that the imaging performance of proposed MUSIC is significantly dependent on the total number and location of transmitting and receiving antennas. To support the theoretical result, various simulation results with synthetic and experimental data are exhibited. It is worth to mention that if the total number of transmitting and receiving antennas is sufficiently large, it is possible to obtain a good result via MUSIC. However, in real-world microwave imaging applications, increasing total number of antennas somehow difficult. Instead of increasing total number of antennas, we investigate a method of arranging the antennas for obtaining a better result based on the elimination of unnecessary extra terms that disturb the detection in the imaging function.

\displaystyle e^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r}}-Ce^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\\
\vdots\\
\displaystyle e^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{r}}-Ce^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{r}}-Ce^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\\
\displaystyle e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r}}-Ce^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\\
\vdots\\
\displaystyle e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{r}}-Ce^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\end{bmatrix}\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big(1-\Phi(\mathbf{r})-\overline{\Phi(\mathbf{r})}+\Psi(\mathbf{r})\overline{\Psi(\mathbf{r})}\Big),\end{aligned}

(11)

where

\Phi(\mathbf{r})=Ce^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N}e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n^{\prime}}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi(\mathbf{r})=-Ce^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n^{\prime}}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}.

Since
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|\cos(\theta_{m}-\phi)
and the following Jacobi-Anger expansion formula holds uniformly

e^{ix\cos\theta}=J_{0}(x)+\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(x)e^{ip\theta},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Phi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{N}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\right)=\frac{C}{N}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{ik|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|\cos(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{N}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigg(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\bigg)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=CN\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\right|^{2}

and consequently

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big(\Phi(\mathbf{r})+\overline{\Phi(\mathbf{r})}\Big)=2CN\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\right|^{2}.

(12)

Moreover, since

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{r})\overline{\Psi(\mathbf{r})}

\displaystyle=\left(-Ce^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n^{\prime}}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\right)\left(-Ce^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N}e^{-ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n^{\prime}}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star})}\right)

\displaystyle=C^{2}\left|\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N}e^{ik|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|\cos(\theta_{n^{\prime}}-\phi)}\right|^{2}=C^{2}N^{2}\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n^{\prime}}-\phi)}\right|^{2},

we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Psi(\mathbf{r})\overline{\Psi(\mathbf{r})}&=\frac{C^{2}N^{2}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n^{\prime}}-\phi)}\right|^{2}\\
&=C^{2}N^{2}\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\right|^{2}.\end{aligned}

(13)

Hence, by combining (
11
), (
12
), and (
13
), we have

|\mathbb{F}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))|^{2}=1-(2CN-C^{2}N^{2})\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\right|^{2}.

Since
|\mathbb{F}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}_{\star}))|=0
,
J_{0}(0)=1
, and
J_{p}(0)=0
for any nonzero integer
p
,

|\mathbb{F}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}_{\star}))|=1-2CN-C^{2}N^{2}=(1-CN)^{2}=0\quad\text{implies}\quad CN=1.

Therefore,

|\mathbb{F}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))|^{2}=1-\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\right|^{2}.

(14)

Next, since
|\tau_{1}|^{2}\mathbf{V}_{1}\mathbf{V}_{1}^{*}=\mathbb{K^{*}K}
, we can evaluate

\mathbf{V}_{1}\mathbf{V}_{1}^{*}=\frac{1}{|\tau_{1}|^{2}}\mathbb{K^{*}K}=\left|\frac{\alpha^{2}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}_{\star})k}{32R\omega\mu_{\mathrm{b}}\tau_{1}}\right|^{2}N\begin{bmatrix}e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}&e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}&\cdots&e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{M})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\\
e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}&e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}&\cdots&e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{M})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{M}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}&e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{M}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}&\cdots&e^{-ik(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{M}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{M})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{\star}}\end{bmatrix}.

Then, throughout a similar process, we can also obtain

|\mathbb{G}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))|^{2}=1-\left|J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{q=-\infty,q\neq 0}^{\infty}(-i)^{q}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{-iq(\vartheta_{m}-\phi)}\right|^{2}.

(15)

Since
|z|^{2}=|\overline{z}|^{2}
for any

, based on the (

Based on the explored structure of the designed imaging function (
9
), we can examine some properties.

Remark 3.1

(Decomposition of the Imaging Function)
.

The imaging function is decomposed by two factors; the factor
J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)
, which is independent on the antenna arrangement, contributes to the identification because
J_{0}(0)=1
, and the factors
\mathcal{E}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{n})
and
\mathcal{E}_{\tx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{b}_{m})
that are significantly dependent on the antenna arrangement disturb the identification by generating several artifacts because
J_{p}(0)=0
for any nonzero integer
p
.

Remark 3.2

(Applicability and Limitation)
.

Based on the Remark
3.1
, the location
\mathbf{r}_{\star}
can be identified through the map of
\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{r})
if
M
(total number of transmitting antennas) and
N
(total number of receiving antennas) are sufficiently large. However, if
M
or
N
is not large enough, it will be very difficult to recognize the location
\mathbf{r}_{\star}
because
\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{r})
will be dominated by the factors
\mathcal{E}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{n})
or
\mathcal{E}_{\tx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{b}_{m})
. Let us emphasize that if one of
M
or
N
is large enough then the location
\mathbf{r}_{\star}
can be identified through the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r})
or
\mathfrak{F}_{\tx}(\mathbf{r})
because the terms
\mathcal{E}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{n})/N
or
\mathcal{E}_{\tx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{b}_{m})/M
will be dominated by the factor
J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)
.

Remark 3.3

(Optimal Antenna Arrangement)
.

Based on the (
9
), it will be possible to obtain a good result if
\mathcal{E}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{n})=0
and
\mathcal{E}_{\tx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{b}_{m})=0
simultaneously. On e possible method is to increase
M
and
N
but this is difficult due to the simulation configuration. Note that based on the asymptotic form of the Bessel function

J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)\approx\sqrt{\frac{2}{k\pi|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|}}\cos\left(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|-\frac{p\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\quad\text{implies}\quad\lim_{\omega\to+\infty}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)=0.

Hence, by applying extreme frequency
\omega\longrightarrow+\infty
, we can eliminate
\mathcal{E}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{n})
and
\mathcal{E}_{\tx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{b}_{m})
and correspondingly it is expected that good imaging results can be retrieved. However, this is an ideal condition. Notice that since

\mathcal{E}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{n})=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}=\sum_{p=-\infty,p\neq 0}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}|)\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)},

the factor
\mathcal{E}_{\rx}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{n})
can be eliminated if
\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{ip(\theta_{n}-\phi)}=0
. Based on recent contribution
[
37
, Observation 4.4]
, if
M
and
N
are even numbers, antennas are located uniformly on a circular array, and positions of two antennas are symmetric about the origin, then it can be expected that we can obtain a good result whose imaging quality is almost same the one obtained with the switching device. We refer to Example
4.3
for a detailed discussion.

Based on the above discussions, we can conclude the following important result of unique determination.

Corollary 3.2

(Unique Identification)
.

Assume that total number of antennas for receiving and transmitting signals is sufficiently large or the antenna arrangement satisfies the condition in Remark
3.3
. Then, the location of anomaly can be identified
uniquely through the map of
\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{r})
.

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we designed an imaging function of MUSIC for identifying small anomalies from the scattering matrix without a switching device. Based on the analyzed structure of the designed imaging function, we confirmed that the imaging performance of the MUSIC significantly depends on the total number of transmitters and receivers. Additionally, the analyzed structure also allows us to find the optimal antenna arrangement for obtaining better imaging results via MUSIC. To support the derived theoretical result, we presented various numerical simulation results from synthetic and experimental data.

Here, we considered the identification of small targets. Extension to the identification of arbitrary shapes targets such as large anomaly, crack-like defects, etc., will be the forthcoming work. Moreover, to obtain better imaging results, development of proposed MUSIC algorithm will be an interesting topic for future research.

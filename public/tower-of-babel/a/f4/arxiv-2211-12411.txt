Time-reversibility and integrability of p : − q p:-q resonant vector fields
Jaume Giné 1 , Valery G. Romanovski 2,3,4 , Joan Torregrosa 5 1 Departament de Matemàtica, Universitat de Lleida, Av. Jaume II, 69, 25001 Lleida, Catalonia, Spain 2 Faculty of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, University of Maribor, Koroška cesta 46, SI-2000 Maribor, Slovenia 3 Center for Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics, Mladinska 3, SI-2000 Maribor, Slovenia 4 Faculty of Natural Science and Mathematics, University of Maribor, Koroška cesta 160, SI-2000 Maribor, Slovenia 5 Departament de Matemátiques, Universitat Autónoma de Barcelona, 08193 Bellaterra, Barcelona, Catalonia, Spain

Abstract
We study local analytical integrability in a neighborhood of p : − q p:-q resonant singular point of a two-dimensional vector field and its connection to time-reversibility with respect to the non-smooth involution φ ⁡ ( x , y ) = ( y p / q , x q / p ) . \varphi(x,y)=(y^{p/q},x^{q/p}). Some generalizations of the theory developed by K. S. Sibirsky for 1 : − 1 1:-1 resonant case to the p : − q p:-q resonant case are presented.

Introduction

Consider an
n
-dimensional system of ordinary differential equations

\dot{x}=F(x)

(1.1)

where
F(x)
is an
n
-dimensional vector functions
defined on some domain
D
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
It is said (see e.g.
[
3
,
16
]
)
that system (
1.3
)
is
time-reversible
on
D
if there exists an
involution
\psi
defined on
D

such that

D_{\psi}^{-1}\cdot F\circ\psi=-F.

(1.2)

We say that system (
1.3
) is
completely analytically integrable
on
D
if it admits
n-1
functionally independent analytic first integrals on
D
.

A time-reversal symmetry is one of the fundamental
symmetries that appears in nature, in particular, important both
for classical and quantum mechanics. Various properties of systems exhibiting such symmetries
have been studying by many authors, see e.g.

[
2
,
3
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
28
,
30
]
and the references therein.

Our paper is devoted to the investigation of the interconnection of
time-reversibility and local integrability in a neighborhood
of a singular point of systems of the form

\dot{\bf x}=A{\bf x}+X({\bf x}),

(1.3)

where
A
is an
n\times n
matrix with entries in
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}

{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
X({\bf x})
is a vector-function without
constant and linear terms defined on some domain
D
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

One of the first results in such studies is due to Poincaré.
It follows from his results that if in the two-dimensional
case the eigenvalue of
A

are pure imaginary and the system has an axis of symmetry
passing through the origin, then it admits an analytic first integral
in a neighborhood of the origin.

A generalization of this result is presented in
[
4
]
, where it is shown that if system (
1.3
) is time-reversibile with
respect to a certain linear involution and two eigenvalues of the matrix
A

are pure imaginary, then under some assumptions the
system has at least one analytic first integral in a neighborhood of the origin.

A detail study of the interconnection of time-reversibility and local integrability
for systems (
1.3
) was presented in
[
18
]
.
In
[
18
]
and
[
29
]
the notion of time reversibility was generalized to the
case when on the right hand side of (
1.2
) ”
-1
” is replaced by a primitive
root of unity.

In this paper we limit our consideration to the two-dimensional systems
(
1.3
) with non-degenerate matrix
A
.
In the case when a 2-dim system (
1.3
) is real and the
eigenvalues of
A
are pure imaginary, and the vector field is symmetric
with respect to a curve passing through the origin, the origin of (
1.3
)
is a center, and, therefore, has an analytic local integral in a neighborhood
of the origin. This geometric argument was used in
[
31
]
in order to
find some integrable systems in the family of real cubic systems
(see also
[
3
]
for recent developments in this direction).
The symmetry axis is, in the general case, an analytic curve passing through the origin. However, from the work of Montgomery and Zippin
[
19
]
, any analytic involution
\psi
associated with a time-reversal
symmetry can be linearized in such a way that the symmetry axis becomes a
straight line. From this result in
[
2
]
the normal form theory is used to establish an algorithm to determine if
a two-dimensional systems (
1.3
) is orbitally reversible.

A detail study of real polynomial systems which are
time-reversible under reflection with respect to a line was performed
by Sibirsky
[
25
,
26
]
. In particular, he showed that in the polynomial
case the set of such systems in the space of parameters
is the variety of a binomial ideal defined by invariants
of the rotation group of the system (some similar results were obtained also in
[
6
,
17
]
). Later on the results obtained
by Sibirsky were generalized to the case of complex systems (
1.3
)
with
1:-1
resonant singular point at the origin in
[
15
,
20
,
21
]
.

In this paper we consider system (
1.3
)
having a
p:-q
resonant singular point at the origin, which we write
in the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}px-\sum_{j+k\geq 1,j\geq-1}^{\infty}a_{jk}x^{j+1}y^{k}=px(1-\sum_{j+k\geq 1,j\geq-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p}a_{jk}x^{j}y^{k})

(1.4)

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=-qy+\sum_{j+k\geq 1,j\geq-1}^{\infty}b_{kj}x^{k}{y}^{j+1}=-qy(1-\sum_{j+k\geq 1,j\geq-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q}b_{kj}x^{k}{y}^{j}),

where
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
GCD(p,q)=1
. Both vector field (
1.4
)
and the associated differential operator are denoted by
\mathcal{X}
.

Unless
p=q=1
this system is not time-reversible under a linear transformation.
Our study deals with the time-reversibility of system (
1.4
) with respect to
the involution

\varphi(x,y)=(y^{p/q},x^{q/p}).

(1.5)

We will prove that if a system (
1.4
) is time-reversible with respect to (
1.5
), then it admits an analytic first integral on a neighborhood of the origin.
We will also give an extension of the results of
[
25
,
26
]
and their
generalizations obtained in
[
15
,
20
,
21
]
presenting an algorithm for
finding subsets of polynomial systems (
1.4
) which are time-reversible with respect to (
1.5
) and generalizing the notion of Sibirsky ideal to
polynomial systems (
1.4
). It will be shown that, in fact,
the theory developed for
1:-1
resonant case can be extended to systems
(
1.4
), however not to the whole family, but only to a certain subfamily of (
1.4
).

2
Integrability of time-reversible systems

For system (
1.4
) it is always possible to find
a series of the form (
2.1

\Psi(x,y)=x^{q}y^{p}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k>p+q\\
j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}v_{j-q,k-p}x^{j}y^{k}

\mathcal{X}\Psi=g_{q,p}(x^{q}y^{p})^{2}+g_{2q,2p}(x^{q}y^{p})^{3}+g_{3q,3p}(x^{q}y^{p})^{4}+\cdots\,,

a_{jk},b_{kj}

1.4

quantities of system (
1.4

System (
1.4

a neighborhood of the origin if and only if
g_{kq,kp}(a^{*},b^{*})=0

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}

(see e.g.
[
21
,
24
]

The following theorem shows that if system (
1.4

to (
1.5

(
2.1
). Observe, that if
p=q=1
then the map (
1.5

of the variables, so the statement presents a generalization of known results of

[
4
,
18
,
20
]

p:-q

Theorem 1
.

Assume that system (
1.4
) is time-reversible
with respect to the involution (
1.5

that is,

D_{\varphi}^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{X}\circ\varphi=-\mathcal{X}.

Then it admits an analytic first integral of the form
(

To prove the theorem we will need the following results.

Lemma 1
.

System (
1.4

Proof.

Using involution (
1.5
), that is, performing
the substitution

x_{1}=y^{p/q},\qquad y_{1}=x^{q/p},

after straightforward calculations we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=\phantom{}-px_{1}(1-\sum_{j_{1}+k_{1}\geq 1,j_{1}\geq-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q}b_{k_{1}j_{1}}x_{1}^{\frac{qj_{1}}{p}}y_{1}^{\frac{pk_{1}}{q}})

(2.6)

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{1}

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}qy_{1}(1-\sum_{j_{1}+k_{1}\geq 1,j_{\geq}-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p}a_{j_{1}k_{1}}x_{1}^{\frac{qk_{1}}{p}}{y_{1}}^{\frac{pj_{1}}{q}}).

In view of (
2.3
) it should hold

\frac{1}{p}a_{jk}x^{j}y^{k}=\frac{1}{q}b_{k_{1}j_{1}}x^{\frac{qj_{1}}{p}}y^{\frac{pk_{1}}{q}}

where the exponents on the right hand side should be non-negative integers

However the latter equality is impossible unless

Thus, (

, where

∎

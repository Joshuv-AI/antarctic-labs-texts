1.
Introduction

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the standard dot product on
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
and
|\cdot|
, the corresponding norm.

For Schwartz class functions, the ray transform (also called the X-ray transform) is defined by

(1.1)

If(x,\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x+t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}t

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
|\xi|=1
and
\langle x,\xi\rangle=0.

For a Schwartz class symmetric
m
-tensor field

f=(f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})
, the ray transform is defined by

(1.2)

\begin{split}I_{m}f(x,\xi)&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle f(x+t\xi),\xi^{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

We use the Einstein summation rule to sum from
1
to
n
over every repeated index in lower and upper positions in a monomial.
In particular, when
m=0
, the definition (
1.2
) coincides with (
1.1
) and when
m=1
, (
1.2
)
represents the ray transform of vector fields which is also called the Doppler transform.

In the case of
m=0
, the ray transform
If
uniquely determines a function
f
and there is an explicit inversion formula. However, if
m\geq 1
, the ray transform
I_{m}
has a nontrivial kernel. In particular,
I(\sigma\nabla h)=0
whenever
h
is a smooth
symmetric
(m-1)
-tensor field on
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
decaying at infinity,
\nabla
is the total covariant derivative, and
\sigma
denotes the symmetrization of a tensor.
A symmetric
m
-tensor field
f
sufficiently fast decaying at infinity can be uniquely decomposed

f=f^{s}+\sigma\nabla h,\quad h(x)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ |x|\to\infty

to the solenoidal (= divergence-free) part
f^{s}
and potential part
\sigma\nabla h
; see
[
Sha94
, Theorem 2.6.2]
and
[
PSU23
, Theorem 6.4.7]
for the detailed explanation in the Euclidean case as well as in the case of Riemannian manifolds. The solenoidal part of a symmetric
m
-tensor field
f
can be uniquely determined from
I_{m}f
and there is an explicit inversion formula
[
Sha94
, Theorem 2.12.2]
.

It is natural to ask: what additional information required along with
I_{m}f
so that one could recover the entire tensor field
f
. This leads to the notion of the momentum ray transform
I_{m}^{k}
introduced in
[
Sha94
, Section 2.17]
by

\displaystyle I_{m}^{k}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{k}f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{k}\langle f(x+t\xi),\xi^{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\quad(k=0,1,2.\dots)

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
|\xi|=1
and
\langle x,\xi\rangle=0
.
In particular
I_{m}^{0}=I_{m}
.

A rank
m
symmetric tensor field
f
is uniquely determined by the data
(I_{m}^{0}f,\dots,I_{m}^{m}f)
.
This was proved in
[
Sha94
, Theorem 2.17.2]
.
Later this result was extended to a Helgason type support theorem for tensor fields on a simple real analytic Riemannian manifold
[
AM19
]
.
An algorithm for recovering
f
from the data
\left(I_{m}^{0}f,\dots,I_{m}^{m}f\right)
is presented in
[
KMSS19
]

as well as a stability estimate in (generalized) Sobolev norms. A range characterization for the operator
f\mapsto(I_{m}^{0}f,\dots,I_{m}^{m}f)
on the Schwartz space was established in
[
KMSS20
]
.

Let us introduce the normal operator
N_{m}^{k}=\left(I_{m}^{k}\right)^{*}I_{m}^{k}
, where
\left(I_{m}^{k}\right)^{*}
is the
L^{2}
-adjoint of the momentum ray transform
I_{m}^{k}
.
Since
N_{m}^{k}
is an averaging operator, the data
N_{m}^{k}f
could represent a better measurement model rather than
I^{k}_{m}f
.

We derive the inversion formula

f=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}D_{m,n}^{k}(N_{m}^{k}f)

recovering a rank
m
tensor field
f
from the data
(N_{m}^{0}f,\dots,N_{m}^{m}f)
.
Here
D_{m,n}^{k}
are linear differential operators, on the space of rank
m
symmetric tensor fields, defined by an explicit formula.

The ray transform has several important applications that include X-ray computerized tomography (CT) in medical imaging when
m=0.
In the case of
m=1
, the ray transform is used in Doppler tomography to analyze vector fields. In cases where
m=2
or
m=4
, the ray transform and its variants are applied to tomography problems in anisotropic media regarding the elasticity and Maxwell systems, see
[
Sha94
]
and
[
LS09
,
SW12
]
. Recently, the momentum ray transform has been adopted as a solution tool for the classical Calderón problem for the bi-Laplace model and other higher-order operators
[
BKS23
,
SS23
,
BK24
]
. The unique continuation principle for
I_{m}
and
I^{k}_{m}
is proved in
[
AKS22
]
. See also
[
IKS23
]
for a related work involving a fractional momentum operator.

2.
Basic definitions

First of all, mostly following
[
Sha94
, Chapter 2]
, we introduce some notation and definitions concerning tensor algebra and analysis which will be used throughout the article.

2.1.
Tensor algebra over
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}

Let
T^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
n^{m}
-dimensional complex vector space of
m
-tensors on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular,
T^{0}\mathbb{R}^{n}={{\mathbb{C}}}
and

T^{1}\mathbb{R}^{n}={{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
. We need complex tensors since we are going to use the Fourier transform.
Assuming
n
to be fixed, the notation
T^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be mostly abbreviated to
T^{m}
.
For a fixed orthonormal basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by
u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=u^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=u(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{m}})
we denote
coordinates
(=
components
) of a tensor
u\in T^{m}
with respect to the basis. There is no difference between covariant and contravariant tensors since we use orthonormal bases only. Given
u\in T^{m}
and
v\in T^{k}
,
the tensor product

u\otimes v\in T^{m+k}
is defined by

(u\otimes v)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m+k}}=u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}v_{i_{m+1}\dots i_{m+k}}.

The standard dot product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
extends to
T^{m}
by

\langle u,v\rangle=u^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}\overline{v_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}}.

Throughout the article, the Einstein summation convention is used.

Let
S^{m}=S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
\binom{n+m-1}{m}
-dimensional subspace of
T^{m}
consisting of symmetric tensors.

The partial symmetrization

\sigma(i_{1}\dots i_{m}):T^{m+k}\to T^{m+k}
in the indices
(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})
is defined by

\sigma(i_{1}\dots i_{m})u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{k}}=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{m}}u_{i_{\pi(1)},\dots,i_{\pi(m)}j_{1}\dots j_{k}},

where the summation is performed over the group
\Pi_{m}
of all substitutions of the set
\{1,\dots,m\}
. In particular,

\sigma:T^{m}\rightarrow S^{m}
is the symmetrization in all indices.
Given
u\in S^{m}
and
v\in S^{k}
,
the symmetric product

uv\in S^{m+k}
is defined by
uv=\sigma(u\otimes v)
. Being equipped with the symmetric product,
S^{*}{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}=\bigoplus\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}S^{m}{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
becomes a commutative graded algebra that is called
the algebra of symmetric tensors over

{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
. The algebra
S^{*}{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
is canonically isomorphic to the algebra of polynomials on
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
. Every statement on symmetric tensors can be translated to the langauge of polynomials, and vice versa.

Given
u\in S^{m}
, let
i_{u}:S^{k}\to S^{m+k}
be the operator of symmetric multiplication by
u
and let
j_{u}:S^{m+k}\to S^{k}
be the adjoint of
i_{u}
. These operators are written in coordinates as

\displaystyle\left(i_{u}v\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m+k}}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(i_{1}\ldots i_{m+k}\right)u_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}v_{i_{m+1}\ldots i_{m+k}}

\displaystyle\left(j_{u}v\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}

\displaystyle=v_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m+k}}u^{i_{k+1}\ldots i_{m+k}}.

The tensor
j_{u}v
will be also denoted by
v/u
. For the Kronecker tensor
\delta
, the notations
i_{\delta}
and
j_{\delta}
will be abbreviated to
i
and
j
respectively.

2.2.
Tensor fields

Recall that the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is the topological vector space consisting of
C^{\infty}
-smooth complex-valued functions on
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
fast decaying at infinity together with all derivatives, equipped with the standard topology.
Let
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)=\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\otimes S^{m}
be the topological vector space of smooth fast decaying symmetric
m
-tensor fields, defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whose components belong to the Schwartz space. In Cartesian coordinates, such a tensor field is written as
f=(f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})
with coordinates (= components)
f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=f^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
symmetric in all indices. We again emphasize that there is no difference between covariant and contravariant coordinates since we use Cartesian coordinates only.

We use
the Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

f\mapsto\widehat{f}

in the form (hereafter
i
is the imaginary unit)

\mathcal{F}{f}(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\textsl{i}\langle y,x\rangle}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
,
f\mapsto\widehat{f}
of symmetric tensor fields is defined component-wise:

\widehat{f}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}=\widehat{f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}}
.

Besides
\mathcal{S}\left({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m}\right)
, we use some other spaces of tensor fields. In particular,

C^{\infty}\left(U;T^{m}\right)
is the space of smooth
m
-tensor fields on an open set
U\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
.
See details in

[
Sha94
, Section 2.1]
.

The
L^{2}
-product on
C_{0}^{\infty}\left({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};T^{m}\right)
is defined by

(2.1)

(f,g)_{L^{2}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};T^{m})}=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\langle f(x),g(x)\rangle\,dx.

2.3.
Inner derivative and divergence

The first order differential operator

d:C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})\to C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m+1})

defined by

(df)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m+1}}=\sigma(i_{1}\dots i_{m+1})\frac{\partial f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}}{\partial x^{i_{m+1}}}=\frac{1}{m+1}\Big(\frac{\partial f_{i_{2}\dots i_{m+1}}}{\partial x^{i_{1}}}+\dots+\frac{\partial f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}}{\partial x^{i_{m+1}}}\Big)

is called
the inner derivative
.

The divergence

\mbox{div}:C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m+1})\to C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})

is defined by

(\mbox{div}\,f)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=\delta^{jk}\frac{\partial f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j}}{\partial x^{k}}.

The operators
d
and
-\mbox{div}
are formally adjoint to each other with respect to the
L^{2}
-product (
2.1
). The divergence is denoted by
\delta
in
[
Sha94
]
. But we will always use the notation
div
since some our formulas involve the divergence and Kronecker tensor simultaneously.

2.4.
The space
{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})

The Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}(E)
is well defined for a smooth vector bundle
E\to M
over a compact manifold using a finite atlas and a partition of unity subordinate to the atlas.
In particular, the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
is well defined for the tangent bundle

T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\xi\rangle=0\}\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\quad(x,\xi)\mapsto\xi

of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\{x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}:|x|=1\}
.

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{S}\left(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\left(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right),\varphi\mapsto\widehat{\varphi}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}{\varphi}(y,\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{(n-1)/2}}\int_{\xi^{\perp}}e^{-\textsl{i}\langle y,x\rangle}\varphi(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\mathrm{d}x
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on the hyperplane
\xi^{\perp}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\right.

\langle\xi,x\rangle=0\}
. Notice that it is the standard Fourier transform in the
(n-1)
-dimensional variable
x
while
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is considered as a parameter.

The
L^{2}
-product on
{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
is defined by

(2.2)

(\varphi,\psi)_{L^{2}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}\varphi(x,\xi)\overline{\psi(x,\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
\mathrm{d}\xi
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Euclidean volume form on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

2.5.
Ray transforms

It is convenient to parameterize the family of oriented lines in
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
by points of the manifold
T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Namely, a point

(x,\xi)\in T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
determines the line
\{x+t\xi:t\in{{\mathbb{R}}}\}
through
x
in the direction
\xi
.

The ray transform

I_{m}:\mathcal{S}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})\to\mathcal{S}\left(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)

is the linear continuous operator defined by

I_{m}f(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\mathrm{~d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle f(x+t\xi),\xi^{m}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

The ray transform is related to the Fourier transform by the important formula
[
Sha94
, formula 2.1.15]

(2.3)

\widehat{I_{m}f}(y,\xi)=(2\pi)^{1/2}\langle\widehat{f}(y),\xi^{m}\rangle\quad\big((y,\xi)\in T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\big),

where
\widehat{I_{m}f}
is the Fourier transform of the function
I_{m}f\in\mathcal{S}\left(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)
.

For
0\leq k\leq m
,
the momentum ray transform

I_{m}^{k}:\mathcal{S}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})\to\mathcal{S}\left(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)

is the linear continuous operator defined by

(2.4)

I_{m}^{k}f(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\mathrm{~d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}\left\langle f(x+t\xi),\xi^{m}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

The formula (
2.3
) is generalized as follows
[
KMSS19
, formula (2.9)]
:

\widehat{I_{m}^{k}f}(y,\xi)=(2\pi)^{1/2}{\textsl{i}}^{k}\left\langle d^{k}\widehat{f}(y),\xi^{m+k}\right\rangle\quad\big((y,\xi)\in T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\big).

As we will see later,
I_{m}^{k}
should be considered together with lower degree operators
I_{m}^{0},\dots,I_{m}^{k-1}
, i.e., the collection

(I_{m}^{0}f,\dots,I_{m}^{k}f)
represents more convenient information about
f
than
I_{m}^{k}f
alone.

2.6.
Normal operators

The formal adjoint of the ray transform
I_{m}
with respect to
L^{2}
-products (
2.1
) and (
2.2
)

I_{m}^{*}:\mathcal{S}\left(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)\rightarrow C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)

is expressed by

\left(I_{m}^{*}\varphi\right)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\varphi\big(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi,\xi\big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

We emphasize that, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, the tensor field
I_{m}^{*}\varphi
does not need to decay fast at infinity.

Similarly, the formal
L^{2}
-adjoint of the momentum ray transform

I_{m}^{k}

\left(I_{m}^{k}\right)^{*}:\mathcal{S}\left(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)\rightarrow C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)

is expressed by

(2.5)

\big((I_{m}^{k})^{*}\varphi\big)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\varphi\big(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi,\xi\big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Let

N_{m}=I_{m}^{*}I_{m}:\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)\rightarrow C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)

be the normal operator for the ray transform
I_{m}
.
Similarly, let

N_{m}^{k}=(I_{m}^{k})^{*}I_{m}^{k}:\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)\rightarrow C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)

be the normal operator for the momentum ray transform
I_{m}^{k}
.

Given
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
, the tensor field
N_{m}^{k}f
does not grow too fast at infinity, i.e., the estimate

|N_{m}^{k}f(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{N}

holds with some constants
C
and
N
. In particular,
N_{m}^{k}f
can be considered as a tempered tensor field-distribution, i.e.,

N_{m}^{k}f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
. Hence the Fourier transform

\widehat{N_{m}^{k}f}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
is well defined at least in the distribution sense. We will show that, for
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
, the restriction of
\widehat{N_{m}^{k}f}
to
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
belongs to

C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m}\right)
.

The operator
N_{m}
was computed in
[
Sha94
, formula 2.11.3]
where the notation
\mu^{m}
was used instead of
I_{m}^{*}
.
In Section 4, we will derive a similar formula for
N_{m}^{k}
. In
[
AKS22
]
, a similar expression for the normal operator is considered to study the unique continuation principle for momentum ray transforms.

3.
The inversion formula

Let
\Gamma
be Euler’s Gamma function and let the operator
(-\Delta)^{1/2}
be defined defined via Fourier transform by
|y|{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}(-\Delta)^{1/2}
.
We use the definition

(2l+1)!!=1\cdot 3\cdots(2l+1),\quad(-1)!!=1.

Theorem 3.1
.

Given integers
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 2
,
a tensor field
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
is recovered from the data
(N_{m}^{0}f,\dots,N_{m}^{m}f)

by the inversion formula

(3.1)

f(x)=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}D_{m,n}^{k}(N_{m}^{k}f)(x),

where the linear differential operator of order
m+k

D_{m,n}^{k}:C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})\to C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})

is defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}D_{m,n}^{k}&=c_{m,n}^{k}\sum\limits_{p=k}^{m}(n\!+\!2m\!-\!2p\!-\!3)!!\!\!\!\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\min(p,m-p,p-k)}\!\!\!\!\frac{(-1)^{q}}{2^{q}q!(m\!-\!p\!-\!q)!(p\!-\!k\!-\!q)!}\,d^{p-q}\,i^{q}\,j^{q}\,j_{x}^{p-k-q}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{k}\end{split}

with the coefficient

(3.3)

c_{m,n}^{k}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\,\frac{2^{m-2}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{2m+n-1}{2}\big)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}\,(n+2m-3)!!}

and the operators
i
,
j
, and
j_{x}
are defined in Section
2.1
.

The appearance of
(-\Delta)^{1/2}
in the inversion formula (
3.1
) needs some justification. Indeed, according to the definition

(-\Delta)^{1/2}u={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(|y|\widehat{u})
, the tensor field
(-\Delta)^{1/2}u
is well defined if
|y|\widehat{u}(y)
belongs to the domain of the Fourier transform. We will show in the next section that the tensor field
u=\sum_{k}D_{m,n}^{k}(N_{m}^{k}f)
is such that
|y|\widehat{u}
can be interpreted as a tempered distribution. Therefore the right-hand side of (
3.1
) is well defined.

Let us write down the inversion formula (
3.1
) in the cases of
m=1
and
m=2
.

For a vector field
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}\right)\ (n\geq 2)
,

f(x)=\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{n+1}{2}\big)}{2\pi^{(n+1)/2}}(-\Delta)^{1/2}\Big(N_{1}^{0}f+\frac{1}{n\!-\!1}\,d\,j_{x}\,(N_{1}^{0}f)-\frac{1}{n\!-\!1}\,\,d\,\mbox{\rm div}\,(N_{1}^{1}f)\Big).

For a second rank tensor field
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{2})
,

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{n+3}{2}\big)}{2\pi^{(n+1)/2}}\,(-\Delta)^{1/2}\bigg[

\displaystyle N_{2}^{0}f-\frac{1}{n+1}\,ij\,N_{2}^{0}f

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n+1}\,d\Big(j_{x}\,N_{2}^{0}f-\mbox{div}\,N_{2}^{1}f\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(n\!-\!1)(n\!+\!1)}\,d^{2}\Big(j_{x}^{2}N_{2}^{0}f-2\,j_{x}\,\mbox{div}\,N_{2}^{1}f+\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{div}^{2}\,N_{2}^{2}f\Big)\bigg].

The rest of the article is mostly devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Now, we discuss the scheme of the proof.

We introduce the tensor fields

(3.4)

A^{(m,k)}\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{2m-k})\quad(0\leq k\leq m)

by

(3.5)

A^{(m,k)}(y)=d^{2m-k}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

These tensor fields play an important role in all our arguments.

In the next section, we compute the normal operators
N_{m}^{k}\ (0\leq k\leq m)
and show that a tensor field
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})
satisfies
A^{(m,0)}/(d^{k}\widehat{f})=F^{(m,k)}
, where the tensor field

F^{(m,k)}\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m-k})
is defined by

(3.6)

F^{(m,k)}(y)=\frac{2^{m-2}(2m-1)!!\,\Gamma\big(\frac{2m\!+\!n\!-\!1}{2}\big)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}\,k!}\,j_{y}^{k}\,\widehat{N_{m}^{k}f}(y).

To avoid proliferation of the
\widehat{\cdot}
symbol, we denote
g(y)=\widehat{f}(y)

and write the equation
A^{(m,0)}/(d^{k}\widehat{f})=F^{(m,k)}
in the form

(3.7)

A^{(m,0)}/(d^{k}g)=F^{(m,k)}\quad(0\leq k\leq m).

Given the data
(F^{(m,0)},\dots,F^{(m,m)})
, we consider (
3.7
) as a system of linear equations for the unknown tensor field
g
.

The first equation of the system (
3.7
)

A^{(m,0)}(y)/g(y)=F^{(m,0)}(y)\quad(y\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})

is a pure algebraic equation. More precisely, being written in coordinates, it constitutes a system of linear algebraic equations in the components of the tensor
g(y)
with coefficients depending on
y
. The system was considered in
[
Sha94
, Theorem 2.12.1]

where the tensor field

\varepsilon^{m}(y)=\frac{|y|}{((2m-1)!!)^{2}}\,d^{2m}|y|^{2m-1}
was used instead of
A^{(m,0)}
. It allows to determine the
tangential part
of the tensor field
g
which corresponds to the solenoidal part of
f={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g
(see
[
Sha94
, Section 2.6]
for the definition of the tangential part).

The second equation of the system (
3.7
),
A^{(m,1)}/(dg)=F^{(m,1)}
, constitutes a system of linear first order PDEs in components of the tensor field
g
, the third equation constitutes a system of linear second order PDEs, and so on.

The system (
3.7
) can be reduced to the purely algebraic system

(3.8)

A^{(m,k)}/g=H^{(m,k)}\quad(0\leq k\leq m)

with the right-hand side defined by

(3.9)

H^{(m,k)}=\frac{(2m-2k-1)!!(n+2m-2k-3)!!}{(2m-1)!!(n+2m-3)!!}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\text{div}^{k-p}\,F^{(m,p)}.

The reduction is presented in Section 5. The precise sense of the reduction is expressed by Proposition
5.3
below, see also the paragraph after Proposition
5.3
.

Some consistency conditions should be imposed on right-hand side
H^{(m,k)}
for solvability of the system (
3.9
). In the case of a general
m
, it is not easy to write down the consistency conditions explicitly. Fortunately, we do not need to know the consistency conditions; in our setting, the system (
3.9
) has a solution by Propositions
4.3
and
5.3
presented below. If the system (
3.9
) has a solution, then the solution is unique and is expressed by (
3.5
) with
\widehat{f}=g
. This fact is proved in Section 6.

4.
Normal operator

We start with the proof of (
2.5
). For
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})
and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle(I_{m}^{k}f,\varphi)_{L^{2}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}(I_{m}^{k}f)(x,\xi)\,\overline{\varphi(x,\xi)}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{k}\langle f(x^{\prime}+t\xi),\xi^{m}\rangle\,\overline{\varphi(x^{\prime},\xi)}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi.

We transform the inner integral by the change
x=x^{\prime}+t\xi
of integration variables

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{k}\langle f(x^{\prime}+t\xi),\xi^{m}\rangle\,\overline{\varphi(x^{\prime},\xi)}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{d}}t\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\prime}=\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}\langle f(x),\xi^{m}\rangle\,\overline{\varphi(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi,\xi)}\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}f^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x)\,\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\,\overline{\varphi(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi,\xi)}\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

Substituting this expression into (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle(I_{m}^{k}f,\varphi)_{L^{2}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}f^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x)\,\overline{\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\,\varphi(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi,\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi}\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=(f,(I_{m}^{k})^{*}\varphi)_{L^{2}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})}.

This proves (
2.5
).

Recall that
N_{m}^{k}=(I_{m}^{k})^{*}I_{m}^{k}
is the normal operator for the momentum ray transform.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
0\leq k\leq m
and
n\geq 2
.
For a tensor field
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
,

(4.2)

(N_{m}^{k}f)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x)=2\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(x^{k+l}f)^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}\ast\frac{(x^{2m+k+l})_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}}{|x|^{2m+2l+n-1}},

where
\ast
denotes the convolution.

The right-hand side of (
4.2
) needs the following comment. For
x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
, according to our definition of the symmetric product,

x^{k+l}\in S^{k+l}
with coordinates
(x^{k+l})^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}=x^{p_{1}}\dots x^{p_{k+l}}
. Therefore, for
f\in S^{m}
,

(x^{k+l}f)^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}=\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l})(x^{p_{1}}\dots x^{p_{k+l}}f^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}).

Before proving Proposition
4.1
, we observe that it implies some regularity of the tensor field
N_{m}^{k}f
. Indeed, the first factor

(x^{k+l}f)^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}
on the right-hand side of (
4.2
) belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n})
. The second factor is a function locally summable over
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
and bounded for
|x|\geq 1
. Hence the second factor can be considered as an element of the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n})
of tempered distributions. As well known
[
Vla79
]
, for
u\in{\mathcal{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n})
and

v\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n})
, the convolution
u\ast v
is defined and belongs to the space of smooth functions whose every derivative increases at most as a polynomial at infinity. In this case the standard formula is valid:
\widehat{u\ast v}=(2\pi)^{n/2}\,\widehat{u}\widehat{v}
. Thus, we can state that

N_{m}^{k}:\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)\to C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)

is a continuous operator.

To prove Proposition
4.1
we need the following

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
k\geq 0
be an integer,
0\neq a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(a^{2}+b)^{2k-l}}{a^{2k-2l}}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{b^{k+l}}{a^{2l}}.

Proof.

By the binomial formula,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(a^{2}+b)^{2k-l}}{a^{2k-2l}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a^{2k}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-a^{2})^{l}(a^{2}+b)^{2k-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a^{2k}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-a^{2}+b-b)^{l}(a^{2}+b)^{2k-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a^{2}+b)^{k}}{a^{2k}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-a^{2}+b-b)^{l}(a^{2}+b)^{k-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{k}\left(a^{2}+b\right)^{k}}{a^{2k}}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{b^{k+l}}{a^{2l}}.\qed

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Using (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we first compute

\displaystyle\left(N_{m}^{k}f\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(I_{m}^{k}\right)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}^{*}I_{m}^{k}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}\xi_{i_{1}}\ldots\xi_{i_{m}}(I_{m}^{k}f)(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}t^{k}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}f^{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi+t\xi)\xi_{j_{1}}\dots\xi_{j_{m}}\xi_{i_{1}}\ldots\xi_{i_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}f^{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}(x-\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi+t\xi)(\xi^{2m})_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\xi.

Replacing
t-\langle x,\xi\rangle
by
t
in the last integral, we have

\displaystyle\left(N_{m}^{k}f\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t+\langle x,\xi\rangle\big)^{k}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{k}f^{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}(x+t\xi)(\xi^{2m})_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}{\xi}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{l}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{2k-l}f^{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}(x+t\xi)(\xi^{2m})_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}{\xi}.

Changing integration variables by

x+t\xi=z,\quad t=|z-x|,\quad\xi=\frac{z-x}{|z-x|},\quad\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\xi=|z-x|^{1-n}\mathrm{d}z,

we obtain

\left(N_{m}^{k}f\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)=2\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle x,z\!-\!x\rangle^{2k-l}\big((z\!-\!x)^{2m}\big)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}\frac{f^{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}(z)}{|z-x|^{2m+2k-2l+n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Let us write this in the form

\displaystyle\left(N_{m}^{k}f\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg[\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}\frac{\langle x,x-z\rangle^{2k-l}}{|x-z|^{2k-2l}}\bigg]\big((z\!-\!x)^{2m}\big)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}\frac{f^{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}(z)}{|z-x|^{2m+n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}z.

By Lemma
4.2
with
a=|x-z|
and
b=\langle z,x-z\rangle
,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}\frac{\langle x,x-z\rangle^{2k-l}}{|x-z|^{2k-2l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}\frac{\big(|x-z|^{2}+\langle z,x-z\rangle\big)^{2k-l}}{|x-z|^{2k-2l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{\langle z,x-z\rangle^{k+l}}{|x-z|^{2l}}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{\langle z,x-z\rangle^{k+l}}{|z-x|^{2l}}.

Substituting this expression into the previous formula, we obtain

\left(N_{m}^{k}f\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)=2\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle z,x-z\rangle^{k+l}\big((x\!-\!z)^{2m}\big)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}\frac{f^{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}(z)}{|x-z|^{2m+2l+n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Then we represent the first factor of the integrand as follows

\langle z,x-z\rangle^{k+l}=(z^{k+l})^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}\big((x-z)^{k+l}\big)_{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}.

Substituting this expression into the previous formula, we write the result in the form

\displaystyle\left(N_{m}^{k}f\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(z^{k+l}\otimes f(z)\big)^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}\frac{\big((x\!-\!z)^{2m+k+l}\big)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}j_{1}\ldots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}}{|x-z|^{2m+2l+n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}z.

We can replace
\big(z^{k+l}\otimes f(z)\big)^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}
with

\big(z^{k+l}f(z)\big)^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}
since the second factor in the integrand is symmetric in all indices. Hence

\displaystyle\left(N_{m}^{k}f\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(z^{k+l}f(z)\big)^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}\frac{\big((x\!-\!z)^{2m+k+l}\big)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}j_{1}\ldots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}}{|x-z|^{2m+2l+n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Every integral on the right-hand side is the convolution of
\big(x^{k+l}f\big)^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}
with

\frac{(x^{2m+k+l})_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}j_{1}\ldots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}}{|x|^{2m+2l+n-1}}
. We thus arrive at (
4.2
).
∎

We use the abbreviated notation
\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}=\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial y^{i_{1}}\dots\partial y^{i_{k}}}
for partial derivatives.
Recall that indices can be written either in lower position or in upper position. In particular,
\partial^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}=\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}
.
Recall that
j_{y}:S^{m}\to S^{m-1}
is the operator of contraction with
y

j_{u}

0\leq k\leq m
, tensor fields
A^{(m,k)}\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{2m-k})
are defined by (

) or in coordinates

A^{(m,k)}_{i_{1}\dots i_{2m-k}}=\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{2m-k}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

Proposition
4.1
can be equivalently written in terms of Fourier transforms
\widehat{f}
and

.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
0\leq k\leq m
and
n\geq 2
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
, the equation (
3.7
) holds with
g=\hat{f}
and
F^{(m,k)}
defined by (
3.6
).

Proof.

Applying the Fourier transform to the equality (
4.2
), we obtain

\widehat{N_{m}^{k}f}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=2(2\pi)^{n/2}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\big(\widehat{x^{k+l}f}\big)^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\mathcal{F}}\Big[\frac{(x^{2m+k+l})_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}}{|x|^{2m+2l+n-1}}\Big].

Using the standard properties of the Fourier transform
[
Hör83
, Lemma 7.1.2]

\widehat{x_{j}f}=\textsl{i}\,\partial_{j}\widehat{f},\quad\widehat{\partial_{j}f}=\textsl{i}\,y_{j}\widehat{f},

we transform our formula to the form

\displaystyle\widehat{N_{m}^{k}f}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=2(2\pi)^{n/2}(-1)^{m\!+\!k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}

\displaystyle\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l})\big(\partial^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\widehat{f}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}\big)

\displaystyle\times\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\mathcal{F}}\Big[|x|^{-2m-2l-n+1}\Big].

Here we can omit the symmetrization
\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l})
since the second factor

\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\mathcal{F}}\Big[|x|^{-2m-2l-n+1}\Big]
is symmetric in all indices. Thus,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{N_{m}^{k}f}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}

\displaystyle=2(2\pi)^{n/2}(-1)^{m\!+\!k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}

\displaystyle\times\big(\partial^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\widehat{f}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}\big)\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\mathcal{F}}\Big[|x|^{-2m-2l-n+1}\Big].

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n})
be the space of tempered distributions.
Recall that
\lambda\mapsto|x|^{\lambda}
is the meromorphic

{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n})
-valued function of
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
with simple poles at points
-n,-n-2,-n-4,\dots
. For a detailed discussion,
[
Hel11
, Chapter VII, Lemmas 6.1 and 6.2]
.
The Fourier transform of
|x|^{\lambda}

is expressed by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}[|x|^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\lambda+n/2}\Gamma\Big(\frac{\lambda+n}{2}\Big)}{\Gamma(-\lambda/2)}|y|^{-\lambda-n}\quad(\lambda,-\lambda-n\notin 2{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{+}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}[|x|^{2k}]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n/2}(-\Delta)^{k}\delta\quad(k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{+}),

where
\delta
is the Dirac function. As compared with formulas (42) and (43) on p. 238 of
[
Hel11
]
, there is the additional factor
(2\pi)^{-n/2}
on right-hand side of these formulas. The factor appears since our definition of the Fourier transform is slightly different from the one in Helgason’s book
[
Hel11
]
.

In particular,

{\mathcal{F}}\Big[|x|^{-2m-2l-n+1}\Big]=\frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{1-2m-2l}{2}\Big)}{2^{2m+2l+n/2-1}\Gamma\Big(\frac{2m+2l+n-1}{2}\Big)}\,|y|^{2m+2l-1}.

Substituting this value into (
4.3
), we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{N_{m}^{k}f}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)=&\frac{(-1)^{m\!+\!k}(2\pi)^{n/2}}{2^{2m\!+\!n/2\!-\!2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(-1)^{l}\Gamma\Big(\frac{1\!-\!2m\!-\!2l}{2}\Big)}{2^{2l}\Gamma\Big(\frac{2m\!+\!2l\!+\!n\!-\!1}{2}\Big)}\\
&\qquad\times\Big(\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}|y|^{2m\!+\!2l\!-\!1}\Big)\partial^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\widehat{f}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y).\end{split}

Let us contract the equation (
4.4
) with
y^{i_{1}}\dots y^{i_{k}}
, i.e., multiply the equation by
y^{i_{1}}\dots y^{i_{k}}
and perform the summation over indices
i_{1}\dots i_{k}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\big(j_{y}^{k}

\displaystyle\widehat{N^{k}_{m}f}\big)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}(y)=\frac{(-1)^{m\!+\!k}(2\pi)^{n/2}}{2^{2m\!+\!n/2\!-\!2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(-1)^{l}\Gamma\Big(\frac{1\!-\!2m\!-\!2l}{2}\Big)}{2^{2l}\Gamma\Big(\frac{2m\!+\!2l\!+\!n\!-\!1}{2}\Big)}

\displaystyle\times\bigg[y^{i_{1}}\dots y^{i_{k}}\partial_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}\Big(\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}|y|^{2m\!+\!2l\!-\!1}\Big)\bigg]\partial^{p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}{\widehat{f}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y).

On the right-hand side, all summands corresponding to
l>0
are equal to zero. Indeed,

\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}|y|^{2m\!+\!2l\!-\!1}
is the positively homogeneous function of degree
l-1
. By the Euler equation for homogeneous functions,

\displaystyle y^{i_{1}}\dots y^{i_{k}}\partial_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}\Big(

\displaystyle\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}|y|^{2m\!+\!2l\!-\!1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{k}k!\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k+l}}|y|^{2m\!+\!2l\!-\!1}&\mbox{if}\ l=0,\\
0&\mbox{if}\ l>0.\end{array}\right.

The formula (
4.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\big(j_{y}^{k}\,\widehat{N^{k}_{m}f}\big)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{(-1)^{m}(2\pi)^{n/2}k!\Gamma\big(\frac{1\!-\!2m}{2}\big)}{2^{2m\!+\!n/2\!-\!2}\Gamma\big(\frac{2m\!+\!n\!-\!1}{2}\big)}\Big(\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k}}|y|^{2m\!-\!1}\Big)\partial^{p_{1}\dots p_{k}}{\widehat{f}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{\pi^{(n+1)/2}\,k!}{2^{m-2}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{2m+n-1}{2}\big)}\big(A^{m,0}/(d^{k}\widehat{f})\big)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}.

This is equivalent to (
3.7
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
0\leq k\leq m
and
n\geq 2
. Then
j_{y}^{k+1}\,\widehat{N^{k}_{m}f}(y)=0

for any tensor field
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\right)
and for any
y\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
.

Proof.

The statement trivially holds in the case of
k=m
. In the case of
k<m
we apply the operator
j_{y}
to the first line of (
4.6
)

\displaystyle\big(j_{y}^{k+1}\,\widehat{N^{k}_{m}f}\big)_{i_{k+2}\dots i_{m}}(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=C_{m}^{k}\Big[\big(y^{i_{k+1}}\partial_{i_{k+1}}\big)\Big(\partial_{i_{k+2}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k}}|y|^{2m\!-\!1}\Big)\Big]\partial^{p_{1}\dots p_{k}}{\widehat{f}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y),

where
C_{m}^{k}=(-1)^{m}(2\pi)^{n/2}k!\Gamma\big(\frac{1\!-\!2m}{2}\big)/\Big(2^{2m\!+\!n/2\!-\!2}\Gamma\big(\frac{2m\!+\!n\!-\!1}{2}\big)\Big)
.
The expression in brackets is equal to zero since
\partial_{i_{k+2}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}p_{1}\dots p_{k}}|y|^{2m\!-\!1}
is a positively homogeneous function of zero degree.
∎

From now on we can forget the momentum ray transform. The rest of the article is devoted to investigation of the system (
3.7
).

Lemma
4.4
implies that right-hand sides of equations (
3.7
) satisfy

(4.7)

j_{y}\,F^{(m,k)}(y)=0\quad(0\leq k\leq m).

Thus, equalities (
4.7
) constitute necessary conditions for existence of a solution
g\in{\mathcal{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n};S^{m})
to the system (
3.7
).
Equalities (
4.7
) appear to be necessary and sufficient consistency conditions for the system (
3.7
), although this fact has not been formally proven.

5.
Reduction of the system (
3.7
) to an algebraic system

Tensor fields
A^{(m,k)}\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{2m-k})\ (0\leq k\leq m)
are defined by (
3.5
).
There exist two important relations between these tensor fields.

Lemma 5.1
.

The following equalities are valid:

(5.1)

j_{y}A^{(m,k)}=-kA^{(m-1,k-1)}\quad(0\leq k\leq m),

(5.2)

\mbox{\rm div}\,A^{(m,k)}=(2m-2k-1)(n+2m-2k-3)A^{(m,k+1)}\quad(0\leq k\leq m-1).

Proof.

Applying the operator
j_{y}
to the equality (
3.5
), we have

j_{y}A^{(m,k)}=j_{y}d^{2m-k}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

Using the operator
\langle y,\partial\rangle=y^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}
, the latter formula can be written as

(5.3)

j_{y}A^{(m,k)}=\langle y,\partial\rangle\,d^{2m-k-1}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

The tensor field
d^{2m-k-1}|y|^{2m-2k-1}
is positively homogeneous of degree
-k
. By the Euler equation for homogeneous functions,

\langle y,\partial\rangle\,d^{2m-k-1}|y|^{2m-2k-1}=-k\,d^{2m-k-1}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

Substituting this expression into (
5.3
), we obtain

j_{y}A^{(m,k)}=-k\,d^{2m-k-1}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

By (
3.5
), the right-hand side of this formula is equal to
-kA^{(m-1,k-1)}
. This proves (
5.1
).

Let us write (
3.5
) in the coordinate form

A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{2m}}=\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{2m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

Differentiate this equality

\frac{\partial A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{2m}}}{\partial y^{j}}=\partial_{ji_{k+1}\dots i_{2m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}.

From this

\displaystyle(\mbox{\rm div}\,A^{(m,k)})_{i_{k+2}\dots i_{2m}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{jl}\,\frac{\partial A^{(m,k)}_{li_{k+2}\dots i_{2m}}}{\partial y^{j}}=\delta^{jl}\,\partial_{jli_{k+2}\dots i_{2m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i_{k+2}\dots i_{2m}}(\Delta|y|^{2m-2k-1}).

This can be written in the coordinate-free form

(5.4)

\mbox{\rm div}\,A^{(m,k)}=d^{2m-k-1}(\Delta|y|^{2m-2k-1}).

Using the well-known formula

\Delta|y|^{\alpha}=\alpha(\alpha+n-2)|y|^{\alpha-2},

we obtain

\Delta|y|^{2m-2k-1}=(2m-2k-1)(n+2m-2k-3)|y|^{2m-2k-3}.

Substituting this expression into (
5.4
), we have

\mbox{\rm div}\,A^{(m,k)}=(2m-2k-1)(n+2m-2k-3)\,d^{2m-k-1}|y|^{2m-2k-3}.

By (
3.5
),

d^{2m-k-1}|y|^{2m-2k-3}=A^{(m,k+1)}.

The two last formulas imply (
5.2
).
∎

From (
5.2
), one can easily prove by induction on
k

(5.5)

\mbox{\rm div}^{k}\,A^{(m,0)}=\frac{(2m-1)!!(n+2m-3)!!}{(2m-2k-1)!!(n+2m-2k-3)!!}\,A^{(m,k)}\quad(0\leq k\leq m).

We reproduce the system (
3.7
)

(5.6)

A^{(m,0)}/(d^{l}g)=F^{(m,l)}\quad(l=0,1,\dots,m).

Here
F^{(m,l)}\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m-l})\ (0\leq l\leq m)
are arbitrary tensor fields belonging to the kernel of
j_{y}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If a tensor field
g\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m})
satisfies (
5.6
), then

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\mbox{\rm div}^{k}A^{(m,0)})/(d^{l}g)\\
&\qquad\qquad=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p}\,F^{(m,k+l-p)}\quad(0\leq k\leq m,0\leq l\leq m-k).\end{split}

Proof.

We prove (
5.7
) by induction on
k
. For
k=0
, (
5.7
) coincides with (
5.6
). Assume (
5.7
) to be valid for some
k
. Apply the operator
div
to the equation (
5.7
)

(5.8)

\mbox{\rm div}\Big((\mbox{\rm div}^{k}A^{(m,0)})/(d^{l}g)\Big)=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{p}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p+1}\,F^{(m,k+l-p)}.

We assume binomial coefficients to be defined for all integers
k
and
p
with the convention:

(5.9)

\binom{k}{p}=0\quad\mbox{if either}\ k<0\ \mbox{or}\ p<0\ \mbox{or}\ k<p.

With this convention, we can assume the summation to be performed over all integers
p
in (
5.8
) and the formulas below.

The equality

\mbox{\rm div}(u/v)=(\mbox{\rm div}\,u)/v+u/(dv)

is valid for any two tensor fields
u
and
v
. This can be readily proved based on the definitions of the operators
d
and
\rm div
. Using this equality, we write (
5.8
) in the form

(\mbox{\rm div}^{k+1}A^{(m,0)})/(d^{l}g)+(\mbox{\rm div}^{k}A^{(m,0)})/(d^{l+1}g)=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{p}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p+1}\,F^{(m,k+l-p)}.

By the induction hypothesis,

(\mbox{\rm div}^{k}A^{(m,0)})/(d^{l+1}g)=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{p}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p}\,F^{(m,k+l-p+1)}.

Substituting this expression into the previous formula, we write the result in the form

\displaystyle(\mbox{\rm div}^{k+1}A^{(m,0)})/(d^{l}g)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{p}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p+1}\,F^{(m,k+l-p)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}\sum\limits_{p}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p}\,F^{(m,k+l-p+1)}.

Changing the summation variable of the first sum as
p:=p-1
, we obtain

(\text{div}^{k+1}A^{(m,0)})/(d^{l}g)=(-1)^{k+1}\sum\limits_{p}(-1)^{p}\bigg[\binom{k}{p-1}+\binom{k}{p}\bigg]\text{div}^{p}\,F^{(m,k+l-p+1)}.

By the Pascal triangle equality,
\binom{k}{p-1}+\binom{k}{p}=\binom{k+1}{p}
. Substituting this expression into the last formula, we arrive at (
5.7
) for
k:=k+1
.
∎

Setting
l=0
in (
5.7
), we obtain

(\mbox{\rm div}^{k}A^{(m,0)})/g=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p}\,F^{(m,k-p)}\quad(0\leq k\leq m).

Substituting the value (
5.5
) of
\mbox{\rm div}^{k}A^{(m,0)}
, we arrive at the equation

A^{(m,k)}/g=(-1)^{k}\frac{(2m-2k-1)!!(n+2m-2k-3)!!}{(2m-1)!!(n+2m-3)!!}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}\,\mbox{\rm div}^{p}\,F^{(m,k-p)}.

We have thus proved

Proposition 5.3
.

If a tensor field
g\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m})
solves the system (
3.7
), then it also solves the system (
3.8
) with right-hand sides defined by (
3.9
).

We emphasize that (
3.8
) is a system of linear algebraic equations in coordinates of the unknown tensor field
g
. Of course the system (
3.8
) is not equivalent to (
3.7
). Proposition
5.3
states that (
3.7
) implies (
3.8
) but not vice versa. Nevertheless, we will see that
g
can be uniquely recovered from (
3.8
). In this sense the system (
3.7
) is reduced to the algebraic system (
3.8
).

6.
Solution of the system (
3.8
)

The following statement actually contains the most important part of the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

If the system (
3.8
) is solvable, then the solution is unique and is expressed by the formula

(6.1)

g(y)=\frac{|y|}{m!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2m\!-\!2k\!-\!1)!!}\binom{m}{k}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\min(k,m-k)}\!\!\frac{(-1)^{p}}{2^{p}}\binom{m-k}{p}\,i^{p}i_{y}^{k-p}j_{y}^{p}H^{(m,k)}(y).

The proof of Proposition
6.1
involves several steps. The main part of the proof is contained in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 6.2
.

Given a tensor field
g\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m+1})
, let us fix a Cartesian coordinate system on
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
, fix a value of the index
i_{m+1}
and introduce the tensor field

\tilde{g}\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m})
by

(6.2)

\tilde{g}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=g_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}i_{m+1}}.

Let us also introduce the vector field
\tilde{\delta}
by

\tilde{\delta}_{i}=\delta_{ii_{m+1}}.

Then, for every
0\leq k\leq m
,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(A^{(m,k)}/\tilde{g})_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}=\frac{1}{m\!+\!1}\Big[\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}\,(A^{(m+1,k)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}}\\
&\qquad\qquad-y_{i_{m+1}}\,(A^{(m+1,k+1)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}-(m\!-\!k)\,\Big(\tilde{\delta}\,(A^{(m,k)}/g)\Big)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}\Big].\end{split}

Proof.

The identity

\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}(|y|^{\alpha}y_{k})=y_{k}\,\partial_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}|y|^{\alpha}+p\,\sigma(i_{1}\dots i_{p})(\delta_{i_{1}k}\,\partial_{i_{2}\dots i_{p}}|y|^{\alpha})

holds for any integer
p\geq 0
, any real
\alpha
and any
1\leq k\leq n
. It is easily proved by induction on
p
.
Using this identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k+1}=(2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1)\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}(|y|^{2m-2k-1}y_{i_{m+1}})

\displaystyle=(2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1)\bigg[y_{i_{m+1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle+(2m\!-\!k\!+\!1)\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}\Big)\bigg].

Expanding the symmetrization
\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1})
with respect to the index
j_{m+1}

(see
[
Sha94
, Lemma 2.4.1]
), we write this in the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}

\displaystyle\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k+1}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle+\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle+(2m\!-\!k)\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}\Big).

Since the tensor
\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}
is symmetric in

i_{k+1},\dots,i_{m},j_{1},\dots,j_{m}
, the formula can be written as follows:

(6.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k+1}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle+\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle+(2m\!-\!k)\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}\Big).

By (
3.5
),

\displaystyle\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle=A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}},

\displaystyle\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle=A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}},

\displaystyle\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k-1}

\displaystyle=A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}.

Substituting these expressions into (
6.4
)

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k+1}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}

\displaystyle+(2m\!-\!k)\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big).

The equality

(6.6)

\displaystyle\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m\!-\!k}\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big[(m\!-\!k)\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+m\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big]

can be easily proved based on the fact that the tensor
A^{(m,k)}
is symmetric. Formally speaking, the first term

(m\!-\!k)\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}
in brackets makes sense for
k\leq m-2
only. Nevertheless, the formula (
6.6
) holds for
k=m-1
if we assume that

A^{(m,k)}_{i_{m}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}=A^{(m,k)}_{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}
.
In the case of
k=m
, the first term in brackets is equal to zero because of the factor
(m-k)
. Thus, the formula (
6.6
) holds for all
0\leq k\leq m
.

Using (
6.6
), the formula (
6.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k+1}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}

\displaystyle+(m\!-\!k)\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+m\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big).

In the last line, the symmetrization
\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})
is unnecessary, as the tensor

A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}
is already symmetric in these indices. The formula simplifies to the following one:

(6.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}\,\partial_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}|y|^{2m-2k+1}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}

\displaystyle+(m\!-\!k)\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+m\,\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big).

The formulas (
6.4
) and (
6.7
) imply

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}\,(A^{(m+1,k)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}}=\bigg[y_{i_{m+1}}\,A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}

\displaystyle+(m\!-\!k)\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+m\,\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)\bigg]g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}.

The symmetrization
\sigma(j_{1}\dots j_{m})
can be omitted after expanding the expression, as the tensor
g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}
is symmetric in these indices. We thus obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}

\displaystyle(A^{(m+1,k)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+(m\!-\!k)\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+m\,\delta_{i_{m+1}j_{1}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{2}\dots j_{m+1}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}.

Implementing the contraction with the Kronecker tensor in second and last lines, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}

\displaystyle(A^{(m+1,k)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}g_{i_{m+1}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}

\displaystyle+(m\!-\!k)\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+m\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}g_{i_{m+1}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}.

In the last line, we have replaced the summation indices
j_{2},\dots,j_{m+1}
with
j_{1},\dots,j_{m}
. We see now that second and last lines contain similar terms. Grouping these terms, we write the formula as follows:

(6.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}

\displaystyle(A^{(m+1,k)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,A^{(m+1,k+1)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}

\displaystyle+(m\!-\!k)\,\sigma(i_{k+1}\dots i_{m})\Big(\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m}}\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m-1}j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+(m+1)\,A^{(m,k)}_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}g_{i_{m+1}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}.

Recall that the value of the index
i_{m+1}
is fixed and
\tilde{g}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}=g_{i_{m+1}}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}
.
The formula (
6.8
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m\!-\!2k\!+\!1}

\displaystyle(A^{(m+1,k)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m+1}}=y_{i_{m+1}}\,(A^{(m+1,k+1)}/g)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}

\displaystyle+(m\!-\!k)\,\Big(\tilde{\delta}\,(A^{(m,k)}/g)\Big)_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}+(m+1)\,(A^{(m,k)}/\tilde{g})_{i_{k+1}\dots i_{m}}.

This is equivalent to (
6.3
).
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

For a tensor field
g\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m})
, the following identity holds:

(6.9)

7.
Commutator formula for
d
and
i_{y}

The operators
i_{u}
of symmetric multiplication by a tensor
u
and
j_{u}
of contraction with
u
were defined in Section 2.1. For arbitrary tensors
u
and
v
,

i_{u}i_{v}=i_{v}i_{u}=i_{uv}.

These equalities hold in virtue of the fact: the symmetric product of tensors is associative and commutative. Passing to adjoints, we obtain the less obvious statement

j_{u}j_{v}=j_{v}j_{u}=j_{uv}.

Recall that
i=i_{\delta},j=j_{\delta}
where
\delta
is the Kronecker tensor;
d
is the inner derivative. The operators
d
and
i
commute, this fact is proved by a direct calculation in coordinates. But
d
and
j
do not commute.

The operator
i_{y}
of symmetric multiplication by the vector
y\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
plays an important role in our arguments.

Proposition 7.1
.

For integers
k\geq 0
and
l\geq 0
,

(7.1)

d^{k}i_{y}^{l}=\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l)}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{l!}{(p-k+l)!}\,i_{y}^{p-k+l}i^{k-p}d^{p}.

Proof.

The equality (
7.1
) trivially holds in the case of
k=0
and in the case of
l=0
. In the case
k=l=1
, (
7.1
) looks as follows:

(7.2)

di_{y}=i+i_{y}d.

This formula is easily proved by a direct calculation in coordinates. In the case
k=1
and of arbitrary
l
, (
7.1
) looks as follows:

(7.3)

di_{y}^{l}=li_{y}^{l-1}i+i_{y}^{l}d.

This formula is easily proved on the base of (
7.2
) by induction in
l
.

Now we prove (
7.1
) by induction in
k
. Assuming the validity of (
7.1
) for some
k
, we apply the operator
d
to this equality

d^{k+1}i_{y}^{l}=\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l)}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{l!}{(p-k+l)!}\,(di_{y}^{p-k+l})i^{k-p}d^{p}.

By (
7.3
),

di_{y}^{p-k+l}=(p-k+l)i_{y}^{p-k+l-1}i+i_{y}^{p-k+l}d
. Substituting this expression into the previous formula, we obtain

\displaystyle d^{k+1}i_{y}^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l)}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{l!(p-k+l)}{(p-k+l)!}\,i_{y}^{p-k+l-1}i^{k-p+1}d^{p}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{p^{\prime}=\max(0,k-l)}^{k}\binom{k}{p^{\prime}}\frac{l!}{(l-k+p^{\prime})!}\,i_{y}^{p^{\prime}-k+l}i^{k-p^{\prime}}d^{p^{\prime}+1}.

Changing the summation variable in the second sum as
p^{\prime}=p-1
, we write this in the form

(7.4)

\displaystyle d^{k+1}i_{y}^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l)}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{l!(p-k+l)}{(p-k+l)!}\,i_{y}^{p-k+l-1}i^{k-p+1}d^{p}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l)+1}^{k+1}\binom{k}{p-1}\frac{l!}{(l-k+p-1)!}\,i_{y}^{p-k+l-1}i^{k-p+1}d^{p}.

We assume the binomial coefficients to be defined for all integers with the convention (
5.9
). With the convention, the summation limits of the first sum on (
7.4
) can be extended to

\max(0,k-l)\leq p\leq k+1.

Let us demonstrate that the summation limits of the first sum on (
7.4
) can be changed to

\max(0,k-l+1)\leq p\leq k+1.

Indeed,
\max(0,k-l)=\max(0,k-l+1)
if
k-l<0
. If
k-l\geq 0
, then
\max(0,k-l)=k-l
and
\max(0,k-l+1)=k-l+1
. In the latter case, the term corresponding to
p=k-l
is equal to zero because of the presence of the factor
p-k+l
.

Similar arguments show that the summation limits of the second sum on (
7.4
) can be changed to

\max(0,k-l+1)\leq p\leq k+1
. The formula (
7.4
) takes the form

\displaystyle d^{k+1}i_{y}^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l+1)}^{k+1}\binom{k}{p}\frac{l!}{(p-k+l-1)!}\,i_{y}^{p-k+l-1}i^{k-p+1}d^{p}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l+1)}^{k+1}\binom{k}{p-1}\frac{l!}{(l-k+p-1)!}\,i_{y}^{p-k+l-1}i^{k-p+1}d^{p}.

We can now unite two sums

d^{k+1}i_{y}^{l}=\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k-l+1)}^{k+1}\bigg[\binom{k}{p}+\binom{k}{p-1}\bigg]\frac{l!}{(p-k+l-1)!}\,i_{y}^{p-k+l-1}i^{k-p+1}d^{p}.

By the Pascal triangle equality,
\binom{k}{p}+\binom{k}{p-1}=\binom{k+1}{p}
and our formula becomes

d^{k+1}i_{y}^{l}=\sum\limits_{p=\max(0,k+1-l)}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{p}\frac{l!}{(p-(k+1)+l)!}\,i_{y}^{p-(k+1)+l}i^{k+1-p}d^{p}.

This finishes the induction step.
∎

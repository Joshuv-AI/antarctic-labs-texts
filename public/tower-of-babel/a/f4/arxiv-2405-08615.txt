Introduction

In this paper,
\mathcal{A}
will represent an unital complex Banach algebra with the unit
1
. An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
, is said to be Drazin invertible
[
3
]
with the Drazin inverse
b\in\mathcal{A}

(\text{denoted by }a^{d})
if the following holds

ab=ba,\text{ }bab=b\text{ and }\left(a(1-ab)\right)^{n}=0\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1)

The smallest
n
that satisfy (
1
) is defined to be the Drazin index of
a
, which denoted by
i(a)
and the collection of all Drazin invertible elements in
\mathcal{A}
will be represented by
\mathcal{A}^{d}
. The Drazin inverse is a fundamental concept widely used across various domains of matrix analysis. It serves as a valuable tool for solving singular linear differential equations and difference equations
[
4
,
9
]
and holds significant importance in areas such as Markov chains
[
5
]
, cryptography
[
6
]
and other areas
[
8
,
7
]
. Koliha
[
2
]
introduces a generalized version of Drazin inverse, known as generalized Drazin (g-Drazin) inverse by using quasinilpotent
[
10
]
elements (elements with spectral radius
0
) in (
1
). An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be g-Drazin invertible with the g-Drazin inverse
b\in\mathcal{A}
(denoted by
a^{D}
) if the following holds

ab=ba,\text{ }bab=b\text{ and }a(1-ab)\in\mathcal{A}^{\text{qnil}},

where
\mathcal{A}^{\text{qnil}}
represent the collection of all quasinilpotent elements in
\mathcal{A}.

The collection of all g-Drazin invertible elements in
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{D}.

One of the most interesting problems in Drazin inverse theory is to study the Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) invertibility of
x_{1}+x_{2}
when
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{d}\text{ }(\text{resp. }\mathcal{A}^{D}).
In 1958, Drazin
[
3
]
proved that if

where
x_{1}x_{2}=x_{2}x_{1}=0
, then
x_{1}+x_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{d}
with
(x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}=x_{1}^{d}+x_{2}^{d};
and the same result is proved for g-Drazin inverse by Koliha
[
2
]
. In the case of matrices, just under the assumption
x_{1}x_{2}=0
, Hartwig et al.
[
11
]
derived the representation for
(x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}
using
x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{d}
and
x_{2}^{d}.
This result is further generalized for g-Drazin inverse in
[
12
]
, within a Banach algebra under a weaker assumption. Over the last two decades, many researchers have worked on this additive problem of Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) inverse under different assumptions, some of which can be found in
[
15
,
13
,

,

,

In the context of additive problems, if the choices of two elements from
\mathcal{A}
are restricted to idempotents only, then investigating the Drazin invertibility of their sum reveals numerous intriguing findings. For two idempotents
p,q\in\mathcal{A},
Koliha et al.
[
1
]
establish that the Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) invertibility of

where

The proof of this result depends on the following equation satisfied by the idempotents

and

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-\lambda_{1}(1-p)\right)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-(\lambda_{1}p+\gamma_{1}q)\right)\left(\lambda-\gamma_{1}(1-q)\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left((\lambda_{1}-\lambda)(\gamma_{1}-\lambda)-\lambda_{1}\gamma_{1}pq\right),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.
Recently, Benabdi and Barraa
[
18
]
proved the equivalence between the Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) invertibility of
\lambda_{1}p+\gamma_{1}q-\lambda_{2}pq
and

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-\lambda_{1}(1-p)\right)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-(\lambda_{1}p+\gamma_{1}q-\lambda_{2}pq)\right)\left(\lambda-\gamma_{1}(1-q)\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}-\lambda(\lambda_{1}+\gamma_{1})+\lambda_{1}\gamma_{1}+(\lambda_{2}\lambda-\lambda_{1}\gamma_{1})pq\right),

where
\lambda_{1},\gamma_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}.
Motivated by these findings, in this paper, we present new additive results regarding the Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) invertibility, which are determined by the behaviour of the product

(\lambda-x_{1})(\lambda-x_{2})(\lambda-x_{3})

(2)

under some assumptions on

where

and

Section

and

are Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) invertible then the remaining one is also Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) invertible. Next, in Section
3
, these results are utilized to prove new additive results regarding Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) invertibility of combinations of two idempotents. In Section
4

by considering the product

Moreover, here we establish a correlation between the representation of the Drazin (resp. g-Drazin) inverse of

and

whenever they exist. This recovers the scenario

]
, and also confirms the converse aspect of their result.

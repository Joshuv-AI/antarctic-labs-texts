On Picard Value Problem of Some Difference Polynomials
Zinelâabidine

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we study the value distribution
of zeros of certain nonlinear difference polynomials of entire functions of
finite order.

2010
Mathematics Subject Classification
:30D35, 39A05.

Key words
: Entire functions, Non-linear difference
polynomials, Nevanlinna theory, Small function.

1
Introduction and Results

Throughout this paper, we assume that the reader is familiar with
the fundamental results and the standard notations of the Nevanlinna’s value
distribution theory
(

\left[10\right],

\left[13\right])
. In
addition, we will use
\rho\left(f\right)
to denote the order of growth
of
f
, we say that a meromorphic function
a\left(z\right)
is a small
function of
f\left(z\right)
if
T\left(r,a\right)=S\left(r,f\right),

where
S\left(r,f\right)=o\left(T\left(r,f\right)\right),
as
r\rightarrow+\infty
outside of a possible exceptional set of finite
logarithmic measure, we use
S\left(f\right)
to denote the family of all
small functions with respect to
f\left(z\right)
. For a meromorphic
function
f\left(z\right),
we define its shift by
f_{c}\left(z\right)=f\left(z+c\right).

In 1959, Hayman proved in
\left[11\right]
that if
f

is a transcendental entire function, then
f^{n}f^{\prime}
assume every
nonzero complex number infinitely many times, provided that
n\geq 3.

Later, Hayman
\left[12\right]
conjectured that this result remains to be
valid when
n=1
and
n=2
. Then Mues
\left[18\right]
confirmed the case
when
n=2
and Bergweiler-Eremenko
\left[2\right]
and Chen-Fang
\left[3\right]
confirmed the case when
n=1
, independently. Since then, there
are many research publications
\left(\text{see }\left[17\right]\right)

regarding this type of Picard-value problem
.
In 1997, Bergweiler obtained
the following result.

Theorem A.
\left(\left[1\right]\right)

If
f
is a transcendental meromorphic function of finite
order and
q
is a not identically zero polynomial, then
ff^{\prime}-

q
has infinitely many zeros.

In 2007, Laine and Yang studied the difference analogue of
Hayman’s theorem and proved the following result.

Theorem B.
\left(\left[14\right]\right)

Let
f\left(z\right)
be a transcendental entire
function of finite order, and
c
be a nonzero complex constant.
Then for
n\geq 2,

f^{n}\left(z\right)f\left(z+c\right)
assume every non-zero value
a\in\mathbb{C}
infinitely often.

In the same paper, Laine and Yang showed that Theorem B
does not remain valid for the case
n=1.
Indeed, take
f\left(z\right)=e^{z}+1.
Then

f\left(z\right)f\left(z+\pi i\right)-1=\left(1+e^{z}\right)\left(1-e^{z}\right)-1=-e^{2z}.

After their, a stream of studies on the value distribution of nonlinear
difference polynomials in
f
has been launched and many related results
have been obtained, see e.g.
\left[5,14,15,16\right].
For example, Liu
and Yang improved the previous result and obtained the following.

Theorem C.
\left(\left[15\right]\right)

Let
f\left(z\right)
be a transcendental entire
function of finite order, and
c
be a nonzero complex constant.
Then for
n\geq 2,

f^{n}\left(z\right)f\left(z+c\right)-p\left(z\right)
has infinitely many zeros, where
p\left(z\right)\not\equiv 0
is a polynomial in
z.

Hence, it is natural to ask:
What can be said about the
value distribution of
f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c\right)-q\left(z\right),
when
f
is a transcendental meromorphic
function and
q
be a not identically zero small function of
f
?
In this paper, as an attempt in resolving this question, we
obtain the following results.

Theorem 1.1

Let
f
be a transcendental
entire function of finite order, let
c_{1},

c_{2}
be
two nonzero complex numbers such that
f\left(z+c_{1}\right)\not\equiv f\left(z+c_{2}\right)
and
q
be not identically zero
polynomial
.
Then
f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{1}\right)-q\left(z\right)
and
f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{2}\right)-q\left(z\right)
at least one of them has infinitely many zeros.

The following corollary arises directly from Theorem 1.1 and
Theorem C.

Corollary 1.1
Let
n\geq 1

be an
integer and let
c_{1},

c_{2}

\left(c_{1}c_{2}\neq 0\right)

be two distinct complex numbers. Let
\alpha,\beta,

p_{1},

p_{2}

and
q

\left(\not\equiv 0\right)

be nonconstant
polynomials. If
f

is a finite order transcendental entire
solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f^{n}\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{1}\right)-q\left(z\right)=p_{1}\left(z\right)e^{\alpha\left(z\right)}\\
f^{n}\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{2}\right)-q\left(z\right)=p_{2}\left(z\right)e^{\beta\left(z\right)}\end{array}\right.,

then,
n=1
and
f
must be a periodic function
of period
c_{1}-c_{2}.

2
Some lemmas

The following lemma is an extension of the difference
analogue of the Clunie lemma obtained by Halburd and Korhonen
\left[8\right]
.

Lemma 2.1

\left[4\right]

Let
f\left(z\right)
be a non-constant, finite order meromorphic solution of

f^{n}P\left(z,f\right)=Q\left(z,f\right),

where
P\left(z,f\right),

Q\left(z,f\right)
are difference polynomials in
f\left(z\right)
with
meromorphic coefficients
a_{j}\left(z\right)

\left(j=1,\cdots,s\right),
and let
\delta<1.
If the
degree of
Q\left(z,f\right)

as a polynomial in
f\left(z\right)

and its shifts is at most
n,

then

m\left(r,P\left(z,f\right)\right)=o\left(\frac{T\left(r+\left|c\right|,f\right)}{r^{\delta}}\right)+o\left(T\left(r,f\right)\right)+O\left(\overset{s}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}m\left(r,a_{j}\right)\right).

for all
r
outside an exceptional set of finite
logarithmic measure
.

Lemma 2.2
\left[6\right]

Let
f\left(z\right)
be a non-constant, finite order meromorphic function and
let
c\neq 0

be an arbitrary complex number. Then

T\left(r,f\left(z+c\right)\right)=T\left(r,f\left(z\right)\right)+S\left(r,f\right).

Lemma 2.3

\left[7\right]

Let
f\left(z\right)
be a transcendental meromorphic function of finite order

\rho,
and let
\varepsilon>0
be a given constant.
Then, there exists a set
E_{0}\subset\left(1,+\infty\right)
that has finite logarithmic measure, such that for all
z
satisfying
\left|z\right|\notin E_{0}\cup\left[0,1\right],
and for all
k,j,

0\leq j<k,
we have

\left|\frac{f^{\left(k\right)}\left(z\right)}{f^{\left(j\right)}\left(z\right)}\right|\leq\left|z\right|^{\left(k-j\right)\left(\rho-1+\varepsilon\right)}.

The following lemma is the lemma of the logarithmic
derivative.

Lemma 2.4
\left[10\right]

Let
f

be
a meromorphic function and let
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

m\left(r,\frac{f^{\left(k\right)}}{f}\right)=S\left(r,f\right),

where
S\left(r,f\right)=O\left(\log T\left(r,f\right)+\log r\right),

possibly outside a set
E_{1}\subset\left[0,+\infty\right)

of a finite linear measure
.

If
f

is
of finite order of growth, then

m\left(r,\frac{f^{\left(k\right)}}{f}\right)=O\left(\log r\right).

The following lemma is a difference analogue of the lemma
of the logarithmic derivative for finite order meromorphic functions.

Lemma 2.5
\left[6,8,9\right]

Let
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}

be two arbitrary complex numbers such that
\eta_{1}\neq\eta_{2}

and let
f\left(z\right)

be a finite
order meromorphic function. Let
\sigma

be the order of
f\left(z\right)
.
Then for each
\varepsilon>0,

we have

m\left(r,\frac{f\left(z+\eta_{1}\right)}{f\left(z+\eta_{2}\right)}\right)=O\left(r^{\sigma-1+\varepsilon}\right).

Lemma 2.6
Let
f\left(z\right)
be a
transcendental meromorphic solution of the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{1}\right)-q\left(z\right)=p_{1}\left(z\right)e^{\alpha\left(z\right)},\\
f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{2}\right)-q\left(z\right)=p_{2}\left(z\right)e^{\beta\left(z\right)},\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

where
\alpha,\beta
are polynomials and
p_{1},

p_{2},

q

are not identically zero rational functions. If
N\left(r,f\right)=S\left(r,f\right),

then

\deg\alpha=\deg\beta=\deg\left(\alpha+\beta\right)=\rho\left(f\right)>0.

Proof
.
First,

we prove that
\deg\alpha=\rho\left(f\right)
and by the same we can deduce that
\deg\beta=\rho\left(f\right).
It’s clear from
\left(2.1\right)
that
\deg\alpha\leq\rho\left(f\right).
Suppose that
\deg\alpha<\rho\left(f\right),
this means that

f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{1}\right):=F=q\left(z\right)+p_{1}\left(z\right)e^{\alpha\left(z\right)}\in S\left(f\right).

(2.2)

Applying Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2 into
\left(2.2\right),
we obtain
T\left(r,f_{c}\right)=T\left(r,f\right)=S\left(r,f\right)
which is a
contradiction. Assume now that
\deg\left(\alpha+\beta\right)<\rho\left(f\right),
this leads to
p_{1}p_{2}e^{\alpha+\beta}\in S\left(f\right).
From this and
\left(2.1\right)
we have

f^{2}P\left(z,f\right)=p_{1}p_{2}e^{\alpha+\beta}+q^{2},

where

P\left(z,f\right)=a\left(z\right)f^{2}-b\left(z\right)

and

a=\frac{f_{c_{1}}}{f}\frac{f_{c_{2}}}{f},\text{ }b=q\left(\frac{f_{c_{1}}}{f}+\frac{f_{c_{2}}}{f}\right).

It’s clear that
P\left(z,f\right)\not\equiv 0,
and by using Lemma 2.1,
we get

m\left(r,P\left(z,f\right)\right)=S\left(r,f\right)

which leads to

2T\left(r,f\right)=m\left(r,\frac{b\left(z\right)+P\left(z,f\right)}{a\left(z\right)}\right)=S\left(r,f\right)

which is a contradiction. Hence,
\deg\left(\alpha+\beta\right)=\deg\alpha=\deg\beta.
Finally, by using Lemma 2.1, it’s easy to see that
both of
\alpha
and
\beta
are nonconstant polynomials.

3
Proof of Theorem 1.1

We shall prove this theorem by contradiction. Suppose contrary to
our assertion that both of
f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{1}\right)-q\left(z\right)
and
f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{2}\right)-q\left(z\right)

have finitely many zeros. Then, there exist four polynomials
\alpha
,
\beta,

p_{1}
and
p_{2}
such that

f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{1}\right)-q\left(z\right)=p_{1}\left(z\right)e^{\alpha\left(z\right)}

(3.1)

and

f\left(z\right)f\left(z+c_{2}\right)-q\left(z\right)=p_{2}\left(z\right)e^{\beta\left(z\right)}.

(3.2)

By differentiating
\left(3.1\right)
and eliminating
e^{\alpha},
we get

A_{1}ff_{c_{1}}-f^{\prime}f_{c_{1}}-ff_{c_{1}}^{\prime}=B_{1},

(3.3)

where
A_{1}=\frac{p_{1}^{\prime}}{p_{1}}+\alpha^{\prime},

B_{1}=\left(\frac{p_{1}^{\prime}}{p_{1}}+\alpha^{\prime}\right)q-q^{\prime}.
By
Lemma 2.6 we have

\deg\alpha=\deg\beta=\deg\left(\alpha+\beta\right)=\rho\left(f\right)>0.

Now, we prove that
A_{1}\not\equiv 0.
To show this, we suppose the
contrary. Then, there exists a constant
A
such that
A=p_{1}\left(z\right)e^{\alpha},
which implies the contradiction
\deg\alpha=\rho\left(f\right)=0.
By the same, we can prove that
B_{1}\not\equiv 0.
By
the same arguments as above,
\left(3.2\right)
gives

A_{2}ff_{c_{2}}-f^{\prime}f_{c_{2}}-ff_{c_{2}}^{\prime}=B_{2},

(3.4)

where
A_{2}=\frac{p_{2}^{\prime}}{p_{2}}+\beta^{\prime}
and
B_{2}=\left(\frac{p_{2}^{\prime}}{p_{2}}+\beta^{\prime}\right)q-q^{\prime}.
Obviously,
A_{2}\not\equiv 0
and
B_{2}\not\equiv 0.

Dividing both sides of
\left(3.3\right)
and
\left(3.4\right)
by
f^{2},
we get for each
\varepsilon>0

2m\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)\leq m\left(r,\frac{f_{c_{i}}}{f}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\frac{f_{c_{i}}}{f}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f_{c_{i}}^{\prime}}{f_{c_{i}}}\frac{f_{c_{i}}}{f}\right)+O\left(\log r\right)

=O\left(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\log r\right)=S\left(r,f\right).

So, by the first fundamental theorem, we deduce that

T\left(r,f\right)=N\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)+O\left(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\log r\right).

(3.5)

It’s clear from
\left(3.3\right)
and
\left(3.4\right)
that any
multiple zero of
f
is a zero of
B_{i}

\left(i=1,2\right).
Hence

N_{(2}\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)\leq N\left(r,\frac{1}{B_{i}}\right)=O\left(\log r\right),

where
N_{(2}\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)
denotes the counting function of
zeros of
f
whose multiplicities are not less than 2. It follows by this
and
\left(3.5\right)
that

T\left(r,f\right)=N_{1)}\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)+O\left(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\log r\right),

(3.6)

where
N_{1)}\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)
is the counting function of
zeros, where only the simple zeros are considered. From
\left(3.3\right)

and
\left(3.4\right),
for every zero
z_{0}
such that
f^{\prime}\left(z_{0}\right)\neq 0
which is not zero or pole of
B_{1}
and
B_{2},
we have

\left(f^{\prime}f_{c_{1}}+B_{1}\right)\left(z_{0}\right)=0

(3.7)

and

\left(f^{\prime}f_{c_{2}}+B_{2}\right)\left(z_{0}\right)=0.

(3.8)

By
\left(3.7\right)
and
\left(3.8\right),
we obtain

\left(B_{2}f_{c_{1}}-B_{1}f_{c_{2}}\right)\left(z_{0}\right)=0

(3.9)

which means that the function
\frac{B_{2}f_{c_{1}}-B_{1}f_{c_{2}}}{f}
has
at most a finite number of simple poles. We consider two cases:

Case 1.

B_{2}f_{c_{1}}-B_{1}f_{c_{2}}\not\equiv 0.
Set

h\left(z\right)=\frac{B_{2}f_{c_{1}}-B_{1}f_{c_{2}}}{f\left(z\right)}.

(3.10)

Then, from the lemma of logarithmic differences, we have
m\left(r,h\right)=O\left(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\log r\right).
On the
other hand

N\left(r,h\right)=N\left(r,\frac{B_{2}f_{c_{1}}-B_{1}f_{c_{2}}}{f}\right)=N_{1)}\left(r,\frac{B_{2}f_{c_{1}}-B_{1}f_{c_{2}}}{f}\right)

+O\left(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\log r\right)=S\left(r,f\right).

Thus,
T\left(r,h\right)=O\left(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\log r\right)=S\left(r,f\right).
From the equation
\left(3.10\right),

we have

f_{c_{1}}\left(z\right)=\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}f_{c_{2}}\left(z\right)+\frac{h}{B_{2}}f\left(z\right).

(3.11)

By differentiating
\left(3.11\right),
we get

f_{c_{1}}^{\prime}\left(z\right)=\left(\frac{h}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}f\left(z\right)+\frac{h}{B_{2}}f^{\prime}\left(z\right)+\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}f_{c_{2}}\left(z\right)+\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}f_{c_{2}}^{\prime}\left(z\right).

(3.12)

Substituting
\left(3.11\right)
and
\left(3.12\right)
into
\left(3.3\right)

\left[\frac{A_{1}h}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{h}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}\right]f^{2}+\left[-\frac{2h}{B_{2}}\right]ff^{\prime}

+\left[\frac{A_{1}B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}\right]ff_{c_{2}}-\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}f^{\prime}f_{c_{2}}-\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}ff_{c_{2}}^{\prime}=B_{1}.

(3.13)

Equation
\left(3.4\right),
can be rewritten as

-\frac{B_{1}A_{2}}{B_{2}}ff_{c_{2}}+\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}f^{\prime}f_{c_{2}}+\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}ff_{c_{2}}^{\prime}=-B_{1}.

By adding this to
\left(3.13\right)
, we get

\left[\frac{A_{1}h}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{h}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}\right]f+\left[-\frac{2h}{B_{2}}\right]f^{\prime}+\left[\frac{A_{1}B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{B_{1}A_{2}}{B_{2}}\right]f_{c_{2}}=0.

(3.14)

Its clear that
-\frac{2h}{B_{2}}\not\equiv 0
. In order to complete the
proof of our theorem, we need to prove

\frac{A_{1}h}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{h}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\text{ and }\frac{A_{1}B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{B_{1}A_{2}}{B_{2}}\not\equiv 0.

Suppose contrary to our assertion that
\frac{A_{1}h}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{h}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}\equiv 0.
Then, by the definition of
A_{1}
and by
simple integration, we get

p_{1}e^{\alpha}=C_{1}\frac{h}{B_{2}},

where
C_{1}
is a nonzero constant. This implies that
\deg\alpha=\rho\left(f\right)-1,
which is a contradiction. Hence,
\frac{A_{1}h}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{h}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}\not\equiv 0.
Next, we shall prove

\frac{A_{1}B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{B_{1}A_{2}}{B_{2}}\not\equiv 0.
Suppose that
\frac{A_{1}B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{B_{1}A_{2}}{B_{2}}\equiv 0.
Then we obtain

\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}e^{\alpha-\beta}=C_{2}\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}:=\gamma,

where
C_{2}
is a nonzero constant and
\gamma
is a small function of
f.

From
\left(3.1\right)
and
\left(3.2\right)
we get

f\left(f_{c_{1}}-\gamma f_{c_{2}}\right)=\left(1-\gamma\right)q.

(3.15)

If
\gamma\not\equiv 1,
then by applying Clunie’s lemma to
\left(3.15\right),
we obtain

m\left(r,f_{c_{1}}-\gamma f_{c_{2}}\right)=T\left(r,f_{c_{1}}-\gamma f_{c_{2}}\right)=S\left(r,f\right).

By this and
\left(3.15\right),
we have

T\left(r,f\right)=T\left(r,\frac{\left(1-\gamma\right)q}{f_{c_{1}}-\gamma f_{c_{2}}}\right)=S\left(r,f\right)

which is a contradiction. If
\gamma\equiv 1,
then we obtain the
contradiction
f_{c_{1}}\left(z\right)\equiv f_{c_{2}}\left(z\right).

Thus,
\frac{A_{1}B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{B_{1}A_{2}}{B_{2}}\not\equiv 0.
From the above discussion and
\left(3.14\right),
we have

f_{c_{2}}\left(z\right)=M\left(z\right)f\left(z\right)+N\left(z\right)f^{\prime}\left(z\right)

(3.16)

and

f_{c_{1}}\left(z\right)=\varphi\left(z\right)f\left(z\right)+\psi\left(z\right)f^{\prime}\left(z\right),

(3.17)

where

M=\frac{\left(\frac{h}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}-A_{1}\frac{h}{B_{2}}}{\left(A_{1}-A_{2}\right)\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}},\text{ }N=\frac{\frac{2h}{B_{2}}}{\left(A_{1}-A_{2}\right)\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}-\left(\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right)^{\prime}}

and

\varphi\left(z\right)=\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}M+\frac{h}{B_{2}},\text{ }\psi=\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}N.

Differentiation of
\left(3.16\right),
gives

f_{c_{2}}^{\prime}=M^{\prime}f+\left(M+N^{\prime}\right)f^{\prime}+Nf^{\prime\prime}.

(3.18)

Substituting
\left(3.16\right)
and
\left(3.18\right)
into
\left(3.4\right),
we get

\left[M^{\prime}-A_{2}M\right]f^{2}+\left[N^{\prime}-A_{2}N+2M\right]f^{\prime}f+N\left(\left(f^{\prime}\right)^{2}+ff^{\prime\prime}\right)=-B_{2}.

(3.19)

Differentiating
\left(3.19\right),
we get

\left[M^{\prime}-A_{2}M\right]^{\prime}f^{2}+\left(2\left[M^{\prime}-A_{2}M\right]+\left[N^{\prime}-A_{2}N+2M\right]^{\prime}\right)f^{\prime}f

+\left(2N^{\prime}-A_{2}N+2M\right)\left(\left(f^{\prime}\right)^{2}+ff^{\prime\prime}\right)+N\left(3f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}+ff^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)=-B_{2}^{\prime}.

(3.20)

Suppose
z_{0}
is a simple zero of
f
and not a zero or pole of
B_{2}.

Then from
\left(3.19\right)
and
\left(3.20\right),
we have

\left(Nf^{\prime}+\frac{B_{2}}{f^{\prime}}\right)\left(z_{0}\right)=0,

\left[\left(2N^{\prime}-A_{2}N+2M\right)f^{\prime}+3Nf^{\prime\prime}+\frac{B_{2}^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right]\left(z_{0}\right)=0.

It follows that
z_{0}
is a zero of
\left[B_{2}\left(2N^{\prime}-A_{2}N+2M\right)-B_{2}^{\prime}N\right]f^{\prime}+3B_{2}Nf^{\prime\prime}.
Therefore the function

H=\frac{\left[2B_{2}N^{\prime}-B_{2}A_{2}N+2B_{2}M-B_{2}^{\prime}N\right]f^{\prime}+3B_{2}Nf^{\prime\prime}}{f}

satisfies
T\left(r,H\right)=S\left(r,f\right)
and

f^{\prime\prime}=\frac{H}{3B_{2}N}f+\frac{\left[-2B_{2}N^{\prime}+B_{2}A_{2}N-2B_{2}M+B_{2}^{\prime}N\right]}{3B_{2}N}f^{\prime}.

(3.21)

Substituting
\left(3.21\right)
into
\left(3.19\right),

Differentiating

satisfies

On the other hand, since

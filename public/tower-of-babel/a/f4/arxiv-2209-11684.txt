Relative entropy decay and complete positivity mixing time
Li Gao, Marius Junge, Nicholas LaRacuente, Haojian Li

Abstract
. We prove that the complete modified logarithmic Sobolev constant of a quantum Markov semigroup is bounded by the inverse of its complete positivity mixing time. For classical Markov semigroups, this implies that every sub-Laplacian given by a Hörmander system on a compact manifold satisfies a uniform modified log-Sobolev inequality for matrix-valued functions. For quantum Markov semigroups, we obtain that the complete modified logarithmic Sobolev constant is comparable to spectral gap up to a constant as logarithm of dimension constant. This estimate is asymptotically tight for a quantum birth-death process. Our results and the consequence of concentration inequalities apply to GNS-symmetric semigroups on general von Neumann algebras.



1.
Introduction

Over the past decades, functional inequalities have stimulated fruitful exchanges between analysis, geometry, and probability. Central to these interactions is the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, which connects the curvature conditions with the analytical properties like Sobolev inequality and spectral gap. Furthermore, it leads to probabilistic consequences such as the concentration phenomenon. In recent years, logarithmic Sobolev inequalities of quantum systems are studied for applications in quantum information theory and quantum many-body systems. In this work, we introduce a novel information-theoretic approach to modified logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and offer some new insights within both classical and quantum regimes.

Since the seminal works of Gross
[
38
,
37
]
, logarithmic Sobolev inequalities have been widely studied on manifolds, graphs, as well as noncommutative spaces (see the surveys
[
51
,
39
]
). A natural framework of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities is given by the theory of Markov semigroups, i.e. semigroups of positive unital maps on functions of some probability space
(\Omega,\mu)
. Recall that a Markov semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}:L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
satisfies the
logarithmic Sobolev inequality
(
\LSI
) if there is a positive constant
\alpha>0
such that,

(
\LSI
)

\displaystyle\int f^{2}\ln f^{2}d\mu-\left(\int f^{2}d\mu\right)\ln\left(\int f^{2}d\mu\right)\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}\int(Lf)fd\mu\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\hskip 2.84544ptf\in\text{dom}(L)\cap L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)

where
\text{dom}(L)
is the domain of the generator
L
of the semigroup
P_{t}=e^{-tL}
. LSI was first introduced by Gross
[
38
]
as a reformulation of Hypercontractivity (HC)
[
62
,
63
]
(see also
[
13
,
14
,
72
,
74
]
for earlier related results). That is, (

) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\parallel\!P_{t}:L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)\!\parallel\leq 1\hskip 2.84544pt\text{ if }\hskip 2.84544ptp\leq 1+e^{2\alpha t}\hskip 2.84544pt.

LSI is often called
L_{2}
-logarithmic Sobolev inequality, and it admits a
L_{1}
-variant called

(

), which states that for any positive

(
MLSI

\displaystyle\int g\ln gd\mu-\left(\int gd\mu\right)\ln\left(\int gd\mu\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int\left(Lg\right)\ln gd\mu.

These two forms of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities are closely related and in general (

LSI

) implies (
MLSI
).
(
MLSI
) is equivalent to the exponential entropy decay that for any probability density
g
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle H(P_{t}g)\leq e^{-2\alpha t}H(g)\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
H(g)=\int g\ln g\hskip 2.84544ptd\mu
is the entropy functional. The exponential entropy decay (
1.1

-mixing time) of the semigroup evolution. Conversely, Diaconis and Saloff-Coste in
[
26

is lower bounded by the inverse of

-mixing time. Later, Otto and Villani
[

in deriving his concentration inequality. These implications later have also been extended to finite Markov chains on discrete spaces in
[

,
30

. To summarize, one has the following chain of functional inequalities:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\boxed{L_{\infty}\text{-mixing time}}\Longrightarrow\boxed{\text{HC}\Leftrightarrow\text{LSI}}\Longrightarrow\boxed{\text{MLSI}}\Longrightarrow\boxed{\text{Concentration}}

In a series of works
[

47
,

,

,
23
,
18
,
33
,
85
]
, the above chain of functional inequalities has been extended for
ergodic

primitive
in mathematical physics literature.
While the study of classical Markov chains often focuses on the ergodic cases, there are structural reasons to study non-ergodic semigroup on quantum systems. From physics perspective, the invariant states of a general quantum decoherence process may forms a non-trivial classical system, hence non-ergodic. Mathematically, one major motivation is the
tensorization property
: given two (classical) Markov semigroups
(P_{t})
and
(Q_{t})
, the tensor product semigroup satisfies

(1.3)

\displaystyle\alpha(P_{t}\otimes Q_{t})=\min\{\alpha(P_{t}),\alpha(Q_{t})\}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
\alpha(P_{t})
can be optimal constant
\alpha
in either (

LSI

) or (

). However,
while being crucial in the study of many-body systems, the tensorization property is false for MLSI constant of QMS
[
15
]
, and in general open for LSI constant (see
[
9
]
for some limited examples).
The tensorization property motivates the following definition of the
complete modified logarithmic Sobolev constant
(in short,

\displaystyle\alpha_{c}(P_{t}):=\inf_{n}\alpha_{1}(P_{t}\otimes\id_{\mathbb{M}_{n}})\hskip 2.84544pt,

where the infimum is over the optimal MLSI constant
\alpha_{1}(P_{t}\otimes\id_{\mathbb{M}_{n}})
of the semigroup
P_{t}
tensoring with the identity map on arbitrary matrix algebra
\mathbb{M}_{n}

1

. This notion of ‘‘complete” MLSI is motivated from the completely bounded (CB) norm in operator space theory, and the study of it inevitably encounters non-ergodic semigroups, as
P_{t}\otimes\id_{\mathbb{M}_{n}}
is always invariant on the amplified part

Using various methods, the positivity of CMLSI constant

(1.4)

\displaystyle\boxed{\text{CB mixing time}}\Longrightarrow\boxed{\text{CMLSI}}\Longrightarrow\boxed{\text{Concentration inequality}}

Here, we left out the LSI and HC because both fail for any non-ergodic QMS on matrix algebras

1.1.
CMLSI via complete positivity

Quantum Markov semigroups are noncommutative generalizations of Markov semigroups, where the underlying probability spaces are replaced by matrix algebras or operator algebras. We start with the relatively simple case of tracial von Neumann algebras. Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal faithful tracial state
\tau
. A quantum Markov semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is a continuous semigroup of completely positive (CP) unital maps,
(T_{t})
is called symmetric if

\tau(T_{t}(x)y)=\tau(yT_{t}(x)),\quad\forall t\geq 0\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptx,y\in{\mathcal{M}}.

Under the symmetric assumption, the fixed point space

{\mathcal{N}}=\{x\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544ptT_{t}(x)=x,t\geq 0\}

forms a subalgebra, which admits a unique trace preserving conditional expectation
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
onto
{\mathcal{N}}
. Our main theorem is the following CMLSI via a CP mixing time. For two maps
\Phi
and
\Psi
, we write
\Phi\leq_{cp}\Psi
if
\Psi-\Phi
is completely positive.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T_{t}=e^{-tL}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a symmetric QMS with the generator
L
and
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
be the conditional expectation onto its fixed point subalgebra
{\mathcal{N}}
. Define

t_{cb}(\varepsilon):=\inf\{t>0\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt(1-\varepsilon)E\leq_{cp}T_{t}\leq_{cp}(1+\varepsilon)E\}.

Then the modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality

(1.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt\tau(\rho\ln\rho-E(\rho)\ln E(\rho))\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\tau(L(\rho)\ln\rho)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\rho\geq 0,\rho\in\dom(L)\hskip 2.84544pt,

is satisfied for

\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2t_{cb}(0.1)}.

Moreover, the same inequality holds for the semigroup
T_{t}\otimes\id_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
with any finite von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Denote
\alpha_{1}
as the optimal (largest) constant satisfying (
1.5
), and
\alpha_{c}
as the optimal CMLSI constant such that
L\otimes\id_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
satisfying (
1.5
) for all
{\mathcal{Q}}
. The above theorem states that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{c}\geq\frac{1}{2t_{cb}(0.1)}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Here,
t_{cb}
is the mixing time in terms of complete positivity, which was first introduced in
[
33
]
under the name
CB return time
, where the terminology was motivated comes from an equivalent definition via CB norm.

The form (
1.5
) of MLSI is equivalent to the exponential decay of relative entropy that for any density operator
\rho
(positive and trace 1)

D(T_{t}(\rho)\|E(\rho))\leq e^{-2\alpha t}D(\rho||E(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt.

where
D(\rho||\sigma)=\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\rho\ln\sigma)
denotes the quantum relative entropy. The quantum relative entropy is a fundamental quantity that measures how well one can distinguish the state
\rho
from
\sigma
(see the survey
[
83
]
for the ubiquitous role of relative entropy in quantum information).
The key property behind the wide applications of relative entropy is the data processing inequality that for any quantum channel
\Phi
(completely positive and trace preserving map)

\displaystyle D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi(\sigma))\leq D(\rho||\sigma)\hskip 2.84544pt.

From this perspective, MLSI is an improved data processing inequality that the relative entropy
D(\rho||E(\rho))
from a state
\rho
to its equilibrium
E(\rho)
is not only non-increasing, but actually decays exponentially over time. Indeed, the proof of Theorem
1.1
can be reduced from the following entropy contraction of symmetric quantum Markov maps.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Phi
be a symmetric completely positive unital map and let
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
be the conditional expectation onto the multiplicative domain of
\Phi
. Define

k_{cb}(\varepsilon):=\{\hskip 2.84544ptk\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt(1-\varepsilon)E\leq_{cp}\Phi^{2k}\leq_{cp}(1+\varepsilon)E\}.

Then for any quantum state
\rho
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi\circ E(\rho))\leq(1-\frac{1}{2k_{cb}(0.1)})D(\rho||E(\rho))

Furthermore, the same inequality holds for
\Phi\otimes\id_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
for any finite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{Q}
.

In the ergodic cases (
{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbb{C}1
), such relative entropy contraction was considered in both classical
[
25
]
and quantum
[
43
,
61
]
settings. They are closely related to the strong data processing inequality studied in
[
69
,
28
,
52
,
42
]
. In particular, the existence of strict contraction in finite dimensions was obtained in
[
35
]
. The above theorem gives the first explicit estimate of contraction coefficient for non-ergodic cases as well as the complete version (as the assertion extends to
\Phi\otimes\id_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
). This theorem is the discrete time analog of Theorem
1.1
, which follows easily from applying (
1.7
) to a semigroup map
T_{t}
and taking the continuous-time limit. It is interesting that the proof of Theorem
1.2
is purely information-theoretical and use only entropic quantities.

1.2.
CMLSI for matrix valued functions

We first present the consequence of Theorem
1.1
in classical cases. For a classical Markov semigroup
P_{t}:L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
, the notion of CMLSI is simply a uniform MLSI for all positive matrix-valued functions
g:\Omega\to{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
and
n\geq 1
,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mu\circ{\text{tr}}(g\ln g-E_{\mu}(g)\ln E_{\mu}(g))\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mu\circ{\text{tr}}((Lg)\ln g)

where
\mu(f)=\int fd\mu
is the scalar valued mean,
E_{\mu}(g)=\int gd\mu\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
is the matrix valued mean, and
tr
is the standard matrix trace. In this setting, the CB return time
t_{cb}
coincides with the
L_{\infty}
-mixing time

t_{b}(\varepsilon)=\{t>0|\parallel\!T_{t}-E_{\mu}:L_{1}(\Omega)\to L_{\infty}(\Omega)\!\parallel\leq\varepsilon\}\hskip 2.84544pt,

which is accessible by standard kernel estimates. Indeed,
t_{b}
is always finite if
P_{t}
satisfies both ultra-contractivity and Poincaré inequality (spectral gap).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
P_{t}=e^{-Lt}:L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
be an ergodic Markov semigroup symmetric to the probability measure
\mu
. Suppose

i)

P_{t}
satisfies
\lambda
-Poincaré inequality for some
\lambda>0
: for
f\in\dom(L^{1/2})
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\lambda\mu(|f-E_{\mu}(f)|^{2})\leq\int f(Lf)d\mu\hskip 2.84544pt.

ii)

There exists
t_{0}>0
such that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\parallel\!P_{t_{0}}:L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\!\parallel\leq C_{0}

Then

(1.11)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{c}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2(\lambda t_{0}+\ln C_{0}+2)}\hskip 2.84544pt.

For finite Markov chains, a similar bound for LSI constant was proved by Diaconis and Saloff-Coste
[
26
]
that
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2(\lambda t_{0}+\ln C_{0}+\ln 10)}
.
In particular, this implies
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\frac{2}{t_{b}(e^{-2})}
, which can be compared to our bound (
1.6
).
We remark that such
\Omega(t_{b}^{-1})
lower bounds are asymptotically tight for
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{c}
of graph Laplacians on the odd cycles. It is quite interesting that Diaconis and Saloff-Coste’s argument relies on the assumption i) and ii) above (which always holds for finite Markov chains), whereas our information-theoretic approach does not need such assumptions and applies to infinite dimensional, noncommutative systems. Combining these two results, we have the following comparison between
\alpha_{c}
and
\alpha_{2}
for ergodic finite Markov chains (Corollary
3.8
),

\alpha_{c}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{2}}{3(4+\ln\ln\parallel\!\mu^{-1}\!\parallel_{\infty})}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Beyond finite state spaces, an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
is the CMLSI for sub-Laplacian generators of Hörmander systems.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact connected Riemannian manifold without boundary and let
\omega d\operatorname{vol}
be a probability measure with a smooth density
\omega
with respect to the volume form
d\operatorname{vol}
. Suppose
H=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\subset TM
is a family of vectors fields satisfies the

that at every point

T_{x}M=\operatorname{span}\{[X_{i_{1}},[X_{i_{2}},\cdots,[X_{i_{n-1}},X_{i_{n}}]]]\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt1\leqslant i_{1},i_{2}\cdots i_{n}\leqslant k,n\geq 1\}.

Then the horizontal heat semigroup
P_{t}=e^{-\Delta_{H}t}
generated by the sub-Laplacian

\Delta_{H}=\sum_{i}X_{i}^{*}X_{i}

has CMLSI constant
\alpha_{c}(\Delta_{H})>0
. Here
X_{i}^{*}
is the adjoint operator with respect to
L_{2}(M,\omega d\operatorname{vol})
.

Before this work, the only known example of sub-Laplacian satisfying CMLSI is the canonical sub-Laplacian on
SU(2)
. The difficulty of CMLSI for sub-Laplacians was the lack of curvature. Indeed, the CMLSI for Heat semigroup was obtained using a non-commutative Bakry-Émery curvature dimension condition
[
53
,
16
]
. Nevertheless, in the sub-elliptic case the Ricci curvature in the degenerate direction of the vector field
H=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
can be interpreted as
-\infty
. For

, the CMLSI of sub-Laplacian was obtained in
[
32
]
using certain gradient estimate as a quasi-curvature condition. Such gradient estimate was first introduced by Driver and Melcher
[
27
]
for Heisenberg group, later obtained for nilpotent Lie
groups
[
58
]
and

[
8
]
. Our Corollary

asserts CMLSI for all sub-Laplacian of Hörmander systems, without any curvature condition.

For scalar-valued functions, the positivity of
\alpha_{2}(\Delta_{H})
was proved by Ługiewicz and Zegarliński
[

, using a similar hyper-contractive argument from
[

. In particular, both

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mu(f^{2}\ln f^{2})\leq\mu(\hat{f}^{2}\ln\hat{f}^{2})+\parallel\!\hat{f}\!\parallel_{2}^{2}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
\hat{f}=f-E_{\mu}(f)
is the mean zero part of

. We remark that both approaches fail for matrix valued functions.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
P_{t}=e^{-tL}:L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
be an ergodic symmetric Markov semigroup. Suppose either of the following inequalities holds for all

(LSI)

\displaystyle\alpha\hskip 2.84544pt\mu\circ\emph{{\text{tr}}}\Big(|f|^{2}\ln|f|^{2}-E_{\mu}(|f|^{2})\ln E_{\mu}(|f|^{2})\Big)\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pt\mu\circ\emph{{\text{tr}}}(f^{*}(L\otimes\id_{{\mathbb{M}}_{2}})f)\hskip 2.84544pt,

\displaystyle\parallel\!(P_{t}\otimes\id_{{\mathbb{M}}_{2}})f\!\parallel_{L_{2}({\mathbb{M}}_{2},L_{p(t)}(\Omega))}\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pt\parallel\!f\!\parallel_{L_{2}({\mathbb{M}}_{2},L_{2}(\Omega))}\emph{\text{ for }}\hskip 2.84544ptp(t)=1+e^{2\alpha t}\hskip 2.84544pt,

1.3.
GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup

Although the trace symmetric case is broad enough to include all classic Markov chains under detail balance condition,
most physical models of quantum systems have the equilibrium as a non-tracial state. For instance, the quantum Gibbs state at finite temperature is never a trace. The tracial state corresponds to the infinite temperature only in the theoretical limit.
From the mathematical perspective, a general von Neumann algebra may not even admit a non-trivial trace, called Type III von Neumann algebra. These von Neumann algebras models the system in quantum field theory and the only symmetric condition we can hope there is for a state.

It turns out that relative entropy decay formulation of MLSI seamlessly extends to Type III setting under a state symmetric condition. We note that such difference between trace symmetric and state symmetric does not exist for classic systems as every measure gives a trace.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal faithful state
\phi
. Suppose
T_{t}=e^{-tL}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is a quantum Markov semigroup that is GNS-symmetric to
\phi
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle\phi(xT_{t}(y))=\phi(T_{t}(x)y)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall x,y\in{\mathcal{M}},\ t\geq 0\hskip 2.84544pt.

Denote
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
as the
\phi
-preserving conditional expectation onto the fixed point subalgebra
{\mathcal{N}}
and the CB return time
t_{cb}:=t_{cb}(0.1)
as before. Then for any state
\rho
and
t\geq 0
,

(1.14)

\displaystyle D(T_{t,*}(\rho)||E_{*}(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pte^{-\frac{t}{t_{cb}}}D(\rho||E_{*}(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt.

The same inequality holds for
T_{t}\otimes\id_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
for any
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{Q}}
.

The exponential decay of relative entropy (
1.14
) can be viewed as an equivalent definition of MLSI in Type III cases
2

2

The equivalent logarithmic Sobolev form in Type III setting is studied in a paper under preparation by Vernooij, Wirth and Zhang.
. The technical tool to extend Theorem
1.1
to Theorem

In finite dimensions, the positivity of CMLSI constant was proved

(1.15)

\displaystyle\alpha_{c}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2C_{cb}(E)}

where

C(E)=\inf\{c>0\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544ptx\leq c\>E(x)\hskip 2.84544pt,\forall\hskip 2.84544ptx\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}\}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptC_{cb}(E):=\sup_{{\mathcal{Q}}}C(E\otimes\id_{{\mathcal{Q}}})\hskip 2.84544pt.

As an application of Theorem

Corollary 1.7

For GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{c}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2\ln(10C_{cb}(E))}.

1.4.
Concentration inequalities

One important application of MLSI is to derive concentration inequalities.
Such implication is discovered by Otto and Villani
[
65
]
for classical Markov semigroups (see also the work of Erbar and Maas
[
30
]
for the discrete case), and recently extended to the noncommutative setting in
[
71
,
33
,
18
]
. As an application of our MLSI for GNS-symmetric semigroups, we obtain concentration inequalities for a general invariant state
\phi
. Recall that the Lipschitz semi-norm

\|x\|_{\text{Lip}}\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt:\max\{\parallel\!\Gamma_{L}(x,x)\!\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\parallel\!\Gamma_{L}(x^{*},x^{*})\!\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}\}

is defined through the

(or Carré du Champ operator)

\Gamma_{L}(x,y)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{1}{2}\Big(L(x^{*})y+x^{*}L(y)-L(x^{*}y)\Big)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall x,y\in\text{dom}(L)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra and

be a GNS-
\phi
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup. Suppose

satisfies MLSI with parameter

\alpha\|x-E(x)\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)}\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544ptc\sqrt{p}\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{\emph{Lip}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Moreover, for any
t>0
, there exist a projection
e
such that

\|e(x-E(x))e\|_{\infty}\leq t\quad\mbox{and}\quad\phi(1-e)\leq 2\exp(-\frac{\alpha^{2}t^{2}}{16ec^{2}\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{\emph{Lip}}^{2}})\hskip 2.84544pt.

One example is the following matrix concentration inequality that can be compared to the work of Tropp
[
80
]
.

Example 1.9
.

Let
S_{1},\cdots,S_{n}
be an independent sequence of random
d\times d
-matrices
S_{1},\cdots,S_{n}
such that

\parallel\!S_{i}-\mathbb{E}S_{i}\!\parallel_{\infty}\leq M\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pta.e.
. We have the matrix Bernstein inequality that for the sum

{\mathbb{E}}\parallel\!Z-{\mathbb{E}}Z\!\parallel_{\infty}\leq 2ce^{-1/2}\sqrt{(v(Z)+M^{2})\ln d}

and the matrix Chernoff bound

P(|Z-\mathbb{E}Z|>t)\leq 2d\exp\Big(-\frac{t^{2}}{64ec^{2}(v(Z)+M^{2})}\Big)\hskip 2.84544pt.

where

Entropy Contraction of Symmetric Markov maps

2.1.
States, channels and entropies

We briefly review some basic information-theoretic concepts in the noncommutative setting.
Recall that a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
is a unital
*
-subalgebra of
B(H)
closed under weak
∗
-topology. A linear functional
\phi:{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a state if it is positive
\phi(x^{*}x)\geq 0
for any
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
and in addition
\phi(1)=1
.
We say
\phi
is normal if
\phi
is weak
∗
-continuous. Throughout the paper, we will only consider normal states and denote
S({\mathcal{M}})
as the normal state space of
{\mathcal{M}}
. We write
s(\phi)
as the support projection of a state
\phi
, which is the minimal projection
e
such that
\phi(x)=\phi(exe)\hskip 2.84544pt,\forall\hskip 2.84544ptx\in{\mathcal{M}}
. A normal state
\phi
is faithful if
s(\phi)=1
. For two normal states
\rho
and
\sigma
, the relative entropy
is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle D(\rho||\sigma)=\begin{cases}\langle\xi_{\rho}|\ln\Delta(\rho/\sigma)|\xi_{\rho}\rangle\hskip 2.84544pt,&\mbox{if }s(\rho)\leq s(\sigma)\\
+\infty,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
\xi_{\rho}
is a vector implementing the state
\rho
and
\Delta(\rho/\sigma)
is the relative modular operator. This form of definition (
2.1
) was introduced by Araki
[
2
]
for general von Neumann algebras.

In this section, we will focus on the case that
{\mathcal{M}}
is a finite von Neumann algebra. Namely,
{\mathcal{M}}
is equipped with a normal faithful tracial state
\tau
. The tracial noncommutative
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is defined as the completion of
{\mathcal{M}}
with respect to the
p
-norm
\parallel\!a\!\parallel_{p}=\tau(|a|^{p})^{1/p}
. We identify
L_{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})\cong{\mathcal{M}}
, and also
L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\cong{\mathcal{M}}_{*}
via the trace duality

\displaystyle d_{\phi}\in L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\longleftrightarrow\phi\in{\mathcal{M}}_{*},\hskip 2.84544pt\phi(x)=\tau(d_{\phi}x)\hskip 2.84544pt.

We say
\rho\in L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
is a density operator if
\rho\geq 0
and
\tau(\rho)=1
, which corresponds to a normal state in the above identification. For example, the identity element
1
is the density operator corresponding to the trace
\tau
itself.
We will often identify normal states with their density operators if no ambiguity. Via this identification, relative entropy reduces to the original definition of Umegaki
[
81
]
,

D(\rho||\sigma)=\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\rho\ln\sigma)

provided this trace is well-defined. For example, for
\rho
and
\sigma
in the bounded state space

\displaystyle S_{b}({\mathcal{M}})=\{\rho\in S({\mathcal{M}})\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{1}1\leq\rho\leq\mu_{2}1\text{ for some }\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0\}.

the Umegaki’s formula is always well-defined and finite. For this reason, we will mostly work with bounded states from
S_{b}({\mathcal{M}})
and derive results for general case
S({\mathcal{M}})
by approximation.
When the second state
\sigma=1
, this gives the entropy functional

H(\rho):=D(\rho||1)=\tau(\rho\ln\rho)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Note that the standard convention of von Neumann entropy in quantum information literature is often with an additional negative sign .

We say a linear map
T:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is a quantum Markov map if
T
is normal, unital and completely positive. The pre-adjoint map
T_{*}:{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{*}
is called a quantum channel, which sends normal states to normal states. In the tracial setting,
T_{*}:L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
given by

\tau(T_{*}(\rho)y)=\tau(\rho T(y))\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\hskip 2.84544pty\in{\mathcal{M}},\rho\in L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})

is completely positive and trace preserving (in short, CPTP). One fundamental inequality about quantum channel is the data processing inequality (also called monotonicity of relative entropy)

(2.2)

\displaystyle D(\rho||\sigma)\geq D(T_{*}(\rho)||T_{*}(\sigma))\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\rho,\sigma\in S({\mathcal{M}})\hskip 2.84544pt.

The data processing inequality states that two quantum states cannot become more distinguishable under a quantum channel. We note that the above inequality remains valid for
T
being positive but not necessarily completely positive
[
60
,
31
]
. The main technical result of this work is an improved data processing inequality for quantum channels under symmetric conditions.

2.2.
Entropy contraction for unital quantum channels

We start our discussion on entropy contraction of unital quantum channels. A quantum channel
\Phi:L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
is unital if
\Phi(1)=1
. The restriction of
\Phi
on
{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded and normal, thus
\Phi
can be viewed as the
L_{1}
-norm extension of its restriction
\Phi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
. By duality, its adjoint
\Phi^{*}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}

is a trace preserving quantum Markov map hence also extends to a unital quantum channel.

For a state
\rho
with
H(\rho)<\infty
, we define the entropy difference of
\Phi
,

D_{\Phi}(\rho):=H(\rho)-H(\Phi(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt.

Note that
D_{\Phi}(\rho)\geq 0
because of data processing inequality (
2.2
) and
\Phi(1)=1
,

H(\rho)=D(\rho||1)\geq D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi(1))=H(\Phi(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt.

We start with a simple but key lemma in our argument.

Lemma 2.1

(Entropy difference lemma)
.

Let
\Phi:L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
be a unital quantum channel and
\Phi^{*}
be its adjoint. Then for two bounded states
\rho,\omega\in{S_{B}}({\mathcal{M}})
,

D(\rho\|\Phi^{*}\Phi(\omega))\leq D_{\Phi}(\rho)+D(\rho\|\omega)\leq\tau((\id-\Phi^{*}\Phi)(\rho)\ln\rho)+D(\rho\|\omega)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Proof.

By duality,
\Phi^{*}
is also completely positive unital.
Then,

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\Phi^{*}\Phi(\omega))=

\displaystyle\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\rho\ln\Phi^{*}\Phi(\omega))

\displaystyle=\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\Phi(\rho)\ln\Phi(\rho))+\tau(\Phi(\rho)\ln\Phi(\rho)-\rho\ln\Phi^{*}\Phi(\omega))

\displaystyle=\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\rho)+\tau\big(\Phi(\rho)\ln\Phi(\rho)-\rho\ln\Phi^{*}\Phi(\omega)\big)

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\leq}\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\rho)+\tau\Big(\Phi(\rho)\ln\Phi(\rho)-\rho\Phi^{*}\big(\ln\Phi(\omega)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\rho)+\tau\Big(\Phi(\rho)\ln\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\rho)\ln\Phi(\omega)\Big)

\displaystyle=\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\rho)+D(\Phi(\rho)\|\Phi(\omega))

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{\leq}\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\rho)+D(\rho\|\omega)\hskip 2.84544pt,

where (2) follows from the monotonicity of relative entropy.
The inequality (1) uses the operator concavity
[
20
]
of logarithm function
t\mapsto\ln t
that for any positive operator
x\geq 0
,

\Phi^{*}(\ln x)\leq\ln\Phi^{*}(x)\hskip 2.84544pt.

This proves the first inequality in the assertion. For the second part, it suffices to note that

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\Phi(\rho)\ln\Phi(\rho))\leq\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\Phi(\rho)\Phi(\ln\rho))=\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\Phi^{*}\Phi(\rho)\ln\rho),

where we use the operator concavity
\Phi(\ln x)\leq\ln\Phi(x)
again.
∎

Our idea is to iterate the above lemma as follows,

\displaystyle D(\rho||(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{n}(\rho))\leq\hskip 2.84544ptD_{\Phi}(\rho)+D(\rho||(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{n-1}(\rho))\leq\hskip 2.84544ptnD_{\Phi}(\rho)+D(\rho||\rho)=nD_{\Phi}(\rho)

Then a relevant question is what would be the limit of
(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{n}(\rho)
as
n\to\infty
. This leads to the multiplicative domain of
\Phi
. Recall that the multiplicative domain of a unital completely positive map
\Phi
is

{\mathcal{N}}_{\Phi}=\{x\in{\mathcal{M}}\ |\ \Phi(y)\Phi(x)=\Phi(yx)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\Phi(x)\Phi(y)=\Phi(xy),\forall y\in\mathcal{M}\}\hskip 2.84544pt.

When
\Phi
is normal,
{\mathcal{N}}_{\Phi}\subset\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann subalgebra. A linear map
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is called a conditional expectation if
E
is a unital completely positive map and idempotent
E\circ E=E
. When
{\mathcal{M}}
is a finite von Neumann algebra, for any subalgebra
{\mathcal{N}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
, there always exists a (unique) trace preserving conditional expectation
E
onto
{\mathcal{N}}
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\tau(xy)=\tau(xE(y))\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptx\in{\mathcal{N}},y\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

In particular,
E
is also a unital quantum channel.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Phi:L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
be a unital quantum channel and let
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
be the trace preserving conditional expectation onto the multiplicative domain
{\mathcal{N}}:={\mathcal{N}}_{\Phi}
. Then

i)

\Phi:{\mathcal{N}}\to\Phi({\mathcal{N}})
is a
*
-isomorphism with inverse
\Phi^{*}:\Phi({\mathcal{N}})\to{\mathcal{N}}
. Moreover,
\Phi({\mathcal{N}})
is the multiplicative domain for
\Phi^{*}
, and

(2.4)

\displaystyle(\Phi^{*}\Phi)\circ E=E\circ(\Phi^{*}\Phi)=E\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptE_{0}\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ E\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
E_{0}:{\mathcal{M}}\to\Phi({\mathcal{N}})
is the trace preserving conditional expectation onto
\Phi({\mathcal{N}})
.

ii)

\Phi
is an isometry on
L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})
. If in addition
\parallel\!\Phi(\id-E):L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\!\parallel_{2}<1
, then
E=\lim_{n}(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{n}
as a map from
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
to
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\Phi
is a
*
-homomorphism on
{\mathcal{N}}
.
For any
x,y\in L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})\subset L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
,

\tau(y(\Phi^{*}\circ\Phi)(x))=\tau(\Phi(y)\Phi(x))=\tau(\Phi(xy))=\tau(xy)

Thus
\Phi^{*}\circ\Phi|_{{\mathcal{N}}}=\id_{\mathcal{N}}
is the identity map. This verifies
(\Phi^{*}\Phi)\circ E=E
. Since
E^{*}=E
,
E\circ(\Phi^{*}\Phi)=E
follows from taking adjoint. Thus
\Phi:{\mathcal{N}}\to\Phi({\mathcal{N}})
is a
*
-isomorphism with inverse
\Phi^{*}
. Denote
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
as the multiplicative domain for
\Phi^{*}
, we have
\Phi({\mathcal{N}})\subset{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
. Conversely, we also have
\Phi^{*}(N_{0})\subset{\mathcal{N}}
by
switching the role of
\Phi=(\Phi^{*})_{*}
. Then
\Phi({\mathcal{N}})={\mathcal{N}}_{0}
since
\Phi
is bijective on
{\mathcal{N}}
.
For ii), we note that by (
2.4
)

\displaystyle(\id-E)\Phi^{*}\Phi(\id-E)=(\id-E)(\Phi^{*}\Phi-E)=\Phi^{*}\Phi-E,\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt(\Phi^{*}\Phi-E)^{n}=\Phi^{*}\Phi^{n}-E.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\parallel\!\Phi^{*}\Phi-E:L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\!\parallel=\parallel\!\Phi(\id-E)\!\parallel_{2}^{2}<1,

\displaystyle\parallel\!(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{n}-E:L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\!\parallel=\parallel\!(\Phi^{*}\Phi-E)^{n}:L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\!\parallel=\parallel\!\Phi(\id-E)\!\parallel_{2}^{2n},

which goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

In order to estimate entropic quantities, we will use the approximation in terms of complete positivity.
Recall that for a density operator
\sigma\in S({\mathcal{M}})
with full support, the Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori (BKM) metric for
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\sigma}(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau(X^{*}(\sigma+s)^{-1}X(\sigma+s)^{-1})ds\hskip 2.84544pt.

The BKM metric is a Riemannian metric on the state space
S({\mathcal{M}})
that is monotone under any quantum channel
\Psi
,

\gamma_{\Psi(\sigma)}(\Psi(X))\leq\gamma_{\sigma}(X)\hskip 2.84544pt,\forall X\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

It connects to the relative entropy as follows

(2.5)

\displaystyle D(\rho||\sigma)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\gamma_{\rho_{t}}(\rho-\sigma)dt\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
\rho_{t}=t\rho+(1-t)\sigma
for
t\in[0,1]
.
It is proved in
[
35
, Lemma 2.1 & 2.2]
that if
\rho\leq c\sigma
,

\displaystyle c\gamma_{\rho}(X)\leq\gamma_{\sigma}(X)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall X\in{\mathcal{M}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle k(c)\gamma_{\sigma}(\rho-\sigma)\leq D(\rho||\sigma)\leq\gamma_{\sigma}(\rho-\sigma)

where
k(c)=\frac{c\ln c-c+1}{(c-1)^{2}}
. The above discussion remains valid if
s(\rho)\leq s(\sigma)
and
X\in s(\sigma){\mathcal{M}}s(\sigma)
. For two positive maps
\Psi
and
\Phi
, we write
\Phi\leq\Psi
if
\Psi-\Phi
is positive.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E
be a conditional expectation (not necessarily trace preserving) and
\Psi
be a quantum Markov map such that

(1-\varepsilon)E\leq\Psi\leq(1+\varepsilon)E\hskip 2.84544pt.

Assume that
E\circ\Psi=E
. Then for any
\rho\in S({\mathcal{M}})
,

D(\rho||\Psi_{*}(\rho))\geq\Big(\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)k(2)}\Big)D(\rho||E_{*}(\rho))

In particular, for
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{10}
,

D(\rho||\Psi_{*}(\rho))\geq\frac{1}{2}D(\rho||E_{*}(\rho))

Proof.

By assumption,
\Psi_{*}=(1-\varepsilon)E_{*}+\varepsilon\Psi_{0}
for some unital positive map
\Psi_{0}\leq 2E_{*}
. We denote
\sigma=E_{*}(\rho),\tilde{\sigma}=\Phi_{*}(\rho)
and
\omega=\Psi_{0}(\rho)
. Then
\tilde{\sigma}=(1-\varepsilon)\sigma+\varepsilon\omega
.
Note that for any bounded state
\sigma\in S_{B}({\mathcal{M}})
,
X\mapsto\sqrt{\gamma_{\sigma}(X)}
is a Hilbert space norm. Then by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma(\rho-\tilde{\sigma})}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma(\rho-(1-\varepsilon)\sigma-\varepsilon\omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma((\rho-\sigma)+\varepsilon(\sigma-\omega))}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma(\rho-\sigma)}-\varepsilon\sqrt{\gamma(\sigma-\omega)}

where
\gamma
can be
\gamma_{\phi}
for any bounded state
\phi\in S_{B}({\mathcal{M}})
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho-\tilde{\sigma})\geq

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho-\sigma)-2\varepsilon\sqrt{\gamma(\rho-\sigma)}\sqrt{\gamma(\sigma-\omega)}+\varepsilon^{2}\gamma(\sigma-\omega)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho-\sigma)-2\varepsilon\sqrt{\gamma(\rho-\sigma)}\sqrt{\gamma(\sigma-\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho-\sigma)-\varepsilon\gamma(\rho-\sigma)-\varepsilon\gamma(\sigma-\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\gamma(\rho-\sigma)-\varepsilon\gamma(\sigma-\omega)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Now take
\rho_{t}=t\rho+(1-t)\sigma
and
\tilde{\rho}_{t}=t\rho+(1-t)\tilde{\sigma}
,

\displaystyle D(\rho||\tilde{\sigma})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\gamma_{\tilde{\rho}_{t}}(\rho-\tilde{\sigma})dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\gamma_{\tilde{\rho}_{t}}(\rho-\sigma)dt-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\gamma_{\tilde{\rho}_{t}}(\sigma-\omega)dt\hskip 2.84544pt.

For the first term, because
\tilde{\rho}_{t}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{t}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\gamma_{\tilde{\rho}_{t}}(\rho-\sigma)dt\geq(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\gamma_{\rho_{t}}(\rho-\sigma)dtds=(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}D(\rho||\sigma)\hskip 2.84544pt.

For the second term, consider that
\tilde{\rho}_{t}\geq(1-\varepsilon)(1-t)\sigma

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\gamma_{\tilde{\rho}_{t}}(\sigma-\omega)dt\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)}\int_{0}^{1}\gamma_{\sigma}(\omega-\sigma)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)}\gamma_{\sigma}(\omega-\sigma)

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)k(2)}D(\omega||\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)k(2)}D(\Psi_{*}(\rho)||\sigma)\overset{(2)}{\leq}\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)k(2)}D(\rho||\sigma)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Here, the inequality (1) above uses
\omega\leq 2\sigma
and (
2.6
). The inequality (2) above follows from the monotonicity of relative entropy and the fact
\Psi_{*}(\sigma)=\sigma
.
Combined the estimated above, we obtained

D(\rho||\tilde{\sigma})\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}D(\rho||\sigma)-\varepsilon k(2)^{-1}D(\rho||\sigma)=\Big(\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)k(2)}\Big)D(\rho||\sigma)\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
k(2)=2\ln 2-1
. The above inequality is non-trivial for
\varepsilon
such that

\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)k(2)}>0\hskip 2.84544pt.

Taking
\varepsilon=0.1
, the above expression is approximately
0.53>\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

This lemma is related to
[

, Corollary 2.15]
and is a variant of

\Phi
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle D(\rho||(\Phi_{*})^{2}(\rho))\geq(1-\varepsilon^{2}k(2)^{-1})D(\rho||E_{*}(\rho)).

The above lemma improves (

Putting the above lemmas together, we obtain Theorem
1.2
in the following slightly more general form.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Phi
be a unital quantum channel and let
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
be the trace preserving conditional expectation onto the multiplicative domain
\mathcal{N}
of
\Phi
. Define the CB return time

(2.8)

\displaystyle k_{cb}(\Phi):=\inf\{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt0.9E\leq_{cp}(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{k}\leq_{cp}1.1E\}\ .

Then for any state
\rho\in S({\mathcal{M}})
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi\circ E(\rho))\leq\Big(1-\frac{1}{2k_{cb}(\Phi)}\Big)D(\rho||E(\rho))\ .

Furthermore, for any finite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{Q}
and state
\rho\in S({\mathcal{M}}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{Q})

(2.10)

\displaystyle D(\Phi\otimes\id(\rho)||(\Phi\circ E)\otimes\id(\rho))\leq\Big(1-\frac{1}{2k_{cb}(\Phi)}\Big)D(\rho||E\otimes\id(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt.

Proof.

It suffices to consider a bounded state
\rho\in S_{B}({\mathcal{M}})
. Note that by the conditional expectation property
(
2.3
),

\displaystyle D(\rho||E(\rho))=\tau(\rho\ln\rho-\rho\ln E(\rho))=\tau(\rho\ln\rho)-\tau(E(\rho)\ln E(\rho))=H(\rho)-H(E(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt,

\displaystyle D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi\circ E(\rho))=D(\Phi(\rho)||E_{0}\circ\Phi(\rho))=H(\Phi(\rho))-H(E_{0}\circ\Phi(\rho))=H(\Phi(\rho))-H(\Phi\circ E(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt,

where we used the property
\Phi\circ E=E_{0}\circ\Phi
from Proposition
2.2
. Moreover,
H(E(\rho))=H(\Phi\circ E(\rho))
as
\Phi
is a trace preserving
*
-isomorphism on
{\mathcal{N}}
. Thus we have

D_{\Phi}(\rho)=H(\rho)-H(\Phi(\rho))=D(\rho||E(\rho))-D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi\circ E(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt.

Iterating the entropy difference Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle D(\rho\|(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{k}(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\rho)+D(\rho\|(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{k-1}(\rho))

\displaystyle\leq\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle kD_{\Phi}(\rho)+D(\rho\|\rho)

\displaystyle=\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle k(D(\rho||E(\rho))-D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi\circ E(\rho))

Now using Lemma
2.3
, for
k=k_{cb}(\Phi)
,

D(\rho||E(\rho))\leq 2D(\rho\|(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{k}\rho))\leq 2k\big(D(\rho||E(\rho))-D(\Phi(\rho)||\Phi\circ E(\rho))\big)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Rearranging the terms gives the assertion. The general case
\rho\in S({\mathcal{M}})
can be obtained via approximation
\rho_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon 1
as
[
16
, Lemma A.2]
. The same argument applies to

, because the CB return time

of

is same as of
\Phi
by the definition.
∎

The above theorem is an improved data processing inequality for the relative entropy between a state
\rho
and its conditional expectation
E(\rho)
. Here
{\mathcal{N}}
is the “decoherence free” subalgebra. Indeed,
for any two states
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in{\mathcal{N}}

D(\sigma_{1}||\sigma_{2})\geq D(\Phi(\sigma_{1})||\Phi(\sigma_{2}))\geq D(\Phi^{*}\Phi(\sigma_{1})||\Phi^{*}\Phi(\sigma_{2}))=D(\sigma_{1}||\sigma_{2})

does not decay.
Outside the “decoherence free” subalgebra
{\mathcal{N}}
, the relative entropy from a state
\rho
to its projection
E(\rho)
on
{\mathcal{N}}
is strictly contractive under every use of the channel
\Phi
.

For
\Phi
being a symmetric quantum Markov map, we have
\Phi=\Phi_{*}
and this gives Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, the Proposition
2.2
reduces to

\Phi\circ E=E\circ\Phi\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\Phi^{2}\circ E=E\circ\Phi^{2}=E\hskip 2.84544pt,

and one can iterate entropy contraction to obtain the discrete time entropy decay,

\displaystyle D(\Phi^{2n}(\rho)||E(\rho))\leq(1-\frac{1}{2k_{cb}(\Phi)})^{2n}D(\rho||E(\rho)).

3.1.
Functional inequalities

In this section, we discuss a continuous time relative entropy decay for symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. We first review some basics of quantum Markov semigroups.
A quantum Markov semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is a family of maps satisfying

i)

for each

T_{t}
is a quantum Markov map (i.e. normal, completely positive, and unital)

ii)

T_{0}=\id_{\mathcal{M}}
and
T_{s}\circ T_{t}=T_{s+t}
for any

iii)

for

is weak
∗

The generator of the semigroup is defined as

\hskip 2.84544ptLx=w^{*}\text{-}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}(x-T_{t}(x))\hskip 2.84544pt

on the domain of
L
that the limit exists. In this section we still consider

as a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal faithful tracial state
\tau

(T_{t})
is
symmetric
(or more specifically,

\tau(x^{*}T_{t}(y))=\tau(T_{t}(x)^{*}y)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall x,y\in{\mathcal{M}},t\geq 0,

the generator

is a positive, symmetric operator, densely defined on

. Its kernel is
the fixed-point subspace

is an
{\mathcal{N}}
-bimodule map

In particular, we have

T_{t}\circ E=E\circ T_{t}=E\hskip 2.84544pt.

where

is the trace preserving conditional expectation onto the fixpoint algebra
{\mathcal{N}}

Note that in the mathematical physics literature it is common to use

In this case, the semigroup admits a unique invariant state, namely the trace
\tau

Recall that a semigroup is equivalently determined by its

the set of bounded density operators from

Definition 3.1

Let

\displaystyle\lambda\parallel\!x-E(x)\!\parallel_{2}^{2}\leq{\mathcal{E}}(x,x)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{E}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

\alpha

3.4.
Hörmander system

We now discuss the application to Markov semigroups on smooth manifolds generated by sub-Laplacians.
Let
(M,g)
be a
d
-dimensional compact connected Riemannian manifold without boundary and let
d\mu=\omega d\operatorname{vol}
be a probability measure with smooth density
\omega
w.r.t to the volume form
d\operatorname{vol}
. A family of vectors fields
H=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\subset TM
with
k\leqslant d
is called a
Hörmander system
if at every point
x\in M
, the tangent space at
x
can be spanned by the iterated Lie brackets of
X_{i}
’s

T_{x}M=\operatorname{span}\{[X_{i_{1}},[X_{i_{2}},\cdots,[X_{i_{n-1}},X_{i_{n}}]]]\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt1\leqslant i_{1},i_{2}\cdots i_{n}\leqslant k\}.

By compactness we can assume there is a global constant
l_{H}
such that for every point
x\in M
, we need at most
l_{H}
th iterated Lie bracket in above expression (also called strong Hörmander condition). Denote
\nabla=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k})
and by
X_{i}^{*}
the adjoint of
X_{i}
on
L^{2}(M,d\mu)
. Under the Hörmander condition, the sub-Laplacian

\Delta_{H}=\nabla^{*}\nabla=\sum_{i}X_{i}^{*}X_{i}=\sum_{i}X_{i}^{2}+\text{div}_{\mu}(X_{i})X_{i}

is a symmetric operator on
L^{2}(M,\mu)
which generates an ergodic Markov semigroup
P_{t}=e^{-\Delta_{H}t}
, often called the horizontal heat semigroup. Here
\operatorname{div}_{\mu}(X)
is the divergence of
X
w.r.t to
\mu
. When
H=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
forms an orthonormal frame to the Riemannian metric,
\Delta_{H}=\Delta
recovers the (weighted) Laplace-Beltrami operator and
P_{t}=e^{-\Delta t}
is the (weighted) heat semigroup on
M
.

The
gradient form
(Carré du Champ operator) of
\Delta_{H}
is given by

\Gamma(f,g):=\frac{1}{2}(f\Delta_{H}(g)+\Delta_{H}(f)g-\Delta_{H}(fg))=\sum_{i}\langle X_{i}f,X_{i}g\rangle\hskip 2.84544pt.

It follows from the product rule of derivatives that
\Gamma
is diffusive, i.e.
\Gamma(fg,h)=f\Gamma(g,h)+g\Gamma(f,h)
. For diffusion semigroups , it is known
[
4
, Theorem 5.2.1]
that the MLSI constant
\alpha_{1}
and the LSI constant
\alpha_{2}
coincides, i.e.
\alpha:=\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
. The positivity

\alpha(\Delta_{H})>0\hskip 2.84544pt.

for any Hörmander’s system
H=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
on a compact connected Riemannian manifold without boundary was proved in
[
56
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Our Corollary
1.4
improve this to
\alpha_{c}(\Delta_{H})>0
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Recall the following Sobolev-type inequality (see e.g.
[
56
, Lemma 2.1]
)

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q}\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544ptC\big(\langle\Delta_{H}f,f\rangle+\parallel\!f\!\parallel_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
q=\frac{2dl_{H}}{dl_{H}-2}>2
and
l_{H}
is globoal Lie bracket length needed in the strong Hörmander condition. By Varopoulos’ Theorem (see
[
82
, Chapter 2]
) on the dimension of semigroups, this implies the following ultra-contractive estimate

(3.15)

\displaystyle\parallel\!e^{-\Delta_{H}t}:L_{1}(M,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(M,\mu)\!\parallel\leqslant C^{\prime}t^{-m/2}\text{ for }0\leq t\leq 1\text{ and some }C^{\prime}>0\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
m=dl_{H}
. Also, it was proved in
[
56
, Theorem 2.3]
that
\Delta_{H}
satisfies Poincaré inequality
\lambda(\Delta_{H})>0
. Combining these with our Theorem
1.3
yields the assertion.
∎

The Sobolev-type inequality (
3.14
) is also used in
[
56
]
by Lugiewicz and Zegarlínski to prove that
\alpha_{2}(\Delta_{H})>0
. Their proof relies on the Rothaus lemma, so does the discrete case by Diaconis and Saloff-Coste
[
26
]
. However, we will see in Section

that this approach is out of scope for showing the CMLSI constant
\alpha_{c}(\Delta_{H})>0
.

3.6.
Failure of matrix valued log-Sobolev inequality

As mentioned above, a standard analysis approach to MLSI through Hypercontractivity or LSI relies the Rothaus Lemma (see e.g.
[
70
,
3
]
)

H(|f|^{2})\leq H(|f-E_{\mu}(f)|^{2})+\parallel\!f-E_{\mu}(f)\!\parallel_{2}^{2}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Here we show that Rothaus Lemma, LSI and Hypercontractivity all fail for matrix valued functions for any classical Markov semigroups. This is a strong indicator that the approach by Diaconis and Saloff-Coste’s hypercontractive
[
26
]
estimate (also used in
[
56
]
) can not be used in proving lower bounds for the CMLSI constants.

The following lemma calculates the derivatives of the entropy functional
H(\rho)=\tau(\rho\ln\rho)
. Recall the BKM metric of a operator
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
at a base state
\rho
is

\gamma_{\rho}(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau(X^{*}(\rho+s)^{-1}X(\rho+s)^{-1})\hskip 2.84544pt.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
t\mapsto\rho_{t}\in S_{B}({\mathcal{M}}),t\in(a,b)
be a smooth family of bounded density operator.
Define the function
F(t)=H(\rho_{t})=\tau(\rho_{t}\ln\rho_{t})
. Then

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=\tau(\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\ln\rho_{t}+1))\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptF^{\prime\prime}(t)=\tau(\rho_{t}^{\prime\prime}(\ln\rho_{t}+1))+\gamma_{\rho_{t}}(\rho^{\prime}_{t})

where
\rho_{t}^{\prime}
and
\rho_{t}^{\prime\prime}
are the first and second order derivative of
\rho_{t}
.

Proof.

The formula for
F^{\prime}
follows from
[
85
, Lemma 5.8]
. For the second derivative, recall the noncommutative chain rule

\frac{d}{dt}(\ln\rho_{t})=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\rho_{t}+s)^{-1}\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\rho_{t}+s)^{-1}ds\hskip 2.84544pt.

By calculating the second derivative, we obtain the second assertion

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\tau(\rho_{t}^{\prime\prime}(\ln\rho_{t}+1))+\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau(\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\rho_{t}+s)\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\rho_{t}+s))ds\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\tau(\rho_{t}^{\prime\prime}(\ln\rho_{t}+1))+\gamma_{\rho_{t}}(\rho^{\prime}_{t})\qed

We now restate and prove Proposition
1.5

Proposition 3.14

Let

*

\pi_{\phi}:{\mathcal{M}}\to H_{\phi}
with a cyclic and separating vector
\eta_{\phi}
such that

\phi(x)=\langle\eta_{\phi},\pi_{\phi}(x)\eta_{\phi}\rangle\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptx\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

By identifying
{\mathcal{M}}\cong\pi_{\phi}({\mathcal{M}})
, the modular automorphism group
\alpha^{\phi}_{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\alpha^{\phi}_{t}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha^{\phi}_{t}(x)=\Delta^{it}x\Delta^{-it}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptx\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
\Delta
is the modular operator of
\phi
, defined as follows

\Delta=S^{*}\bar{S}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptS(\pi_{\phi}(x)\eta_{\phi})=\pi_{\phi}(x^{*})\eta_{\phi}\hskip 2.84544pt.

We consider the following two symmetric conditions with respect to a state
\phi
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say a quantum Markov map
\Phi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is GNS-symmetric with respect to
\phi
(in short, GNS-
\phi
-symmetric) if

\phi(\Phi(x)y)=\phi(x\Phi(y)),\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\hskip 2.84544ptx,y\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt;

ii) We say
\Phi
is KMS-symmetric with respect to
\phi
(in short, KMS-
\phi
-symmetric) if

\langle\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}x\eta_{\phi},\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}\Phi(y)\eta_{\phi}\rangle=\langle\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}\Phi(x)\eta_{\phi},\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}y\eta_{\phi}\rangle,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\hskip 2.84544ptx,y\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Correspondingly, we call the pre-adjoint
\Phi_{*}:{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{*}
a GNS- or KMS-
\phi
-symmetric quantum channel.

Both definitions are generalizations of the detailed balance condition for classical Markov chains. It is proven in
[
86
]
that GNS-
\phi
-symmetric quantum Markov map is equivalent to KMS-
\phi
-symmetric plus that
\Phi
commutes with the modular group

\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\Phi\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\Phi\circ\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptt\in{\mathbb{R}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Both KMS and GNS implies
\phi=\phi\circ\Phi=\Phi_{*}(\phi)
is an invariant state of
\Phi
.

For simplicity, we will consider a semifinite von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
equipped with a normal faithful semi-finite trace
\tau
, but our discussion applies to general von Neumann algebras with proper interpretation of notations. In the tracial setting, we can write
\phi(x)=\tau(d_{\phi}x)
using the density operator
d_{\phi}
of
\phi
. Then the modular automorphism group is given by

\alpha_{t}^{\phi}(x)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544ptd_{\phi}^{-it}xd_{\phi}^{it}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptx\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt,t\in\mathbb{R}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Let
\Phi_{*}:L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
be the pre-adjoint quantum channel via trace duality. The KMS-
\phi
-symmetry is equivalent to

(4.1)

\Phi_{*}(d_{\phi}^{1/2}xd_{\phi}^{1/2})\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544ptd_{\phi}^{1/2}\Phi(x)d_{\phi}^{1/2}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall x\in{\mathcal{M}}

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the weighted
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
is the completion of
{\mathcal{M}}
under the norm

\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{p,\phi}=\parallel\!d_{\phi}^{1/2p}xd_{\phi}^{1/2p}\!\parallel_{p}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
\parallel\!y\!\parallel_{p}=\tau(|y|^{p})^{1/p}
is the tracial
p
-norm.
For
p=2
,
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
is a Hilbert space with KMS-inner product
\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{2,\phi}^{2}=\langle\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}x\eta_{\phi},\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}x\eta_{\phi}\rangle
. By equation (
4.1
),
\Phi
is also a contraction on
L_{1}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
, and hence a contraction on
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
by complex interpolation.

The lemma below is an analog of Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Phi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a GNS-
\phi
-symmetric quantum Markov map for a normal faithful state
\phi
. Denote
{\mathcal{N}}

as the multiplicative domain of
\Phi
. Then

i)

{\mathcal{N}}
is invariant under
\alpha_{t}^{\phi}
. Hence there exists a
\phi
-preserving normal conditional expectation
E:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
.

ii)

\Phi|_{\mathcal{N}}
is an involutive
*
-automorphism satisfying

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Phi^{2}\circ E=E\circ\Phi^{2}=E\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptE\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ E\hskip 2.84544pt.

Moreover,
\Phi^{2}
is a
{\mathcal{N}}
-bimodule map satisfying
\Phi^{2}(axb)=a\Phi^{2}(x)b
for any
a,b\in{\mathcal{N}}
and
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

iii)

\Phi
is an isometry on
L_{2}({\mathcal{N}},\phi)
. If in addition,

{\parallel\!\Phi(\id-E):L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)\!\parallel_{2}<1}\hskip 2.84544pt,

then
E=\lim_{n}\Phi^{2n}
as a map from
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
to
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
.

Proof.

It suffices to explain i). The rest follows similar as Proposition
2.2
(see also
[
35
, Lemma 2.5]
for the finite dimensional case). Indeed, since
\Phi
commutes with
\alpha_{t}^{\phi}
, for
x\in{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle\Phi\big(\alpha_{t}^{\phi}(x)y\big)=

\displaystyle\Phi\big(\alpha_{t}^{\phi}(x\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\alpha_{-t}^{\phi}(y))\big)=\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\Phi\big(x\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\alpha_{-t}^{\phi}(y)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\Big(\Phi(x)\Phi(\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\alpha_{-t}^{\phi}(y))\Big)=\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\Phi(x)\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\Phi\circ\alpha_{-t}^{\phi}(y)=\Phi(\alpha_{t}^{\phi}(x))\Phi(y)\hskip 2.84544pt.

The other side multiplicativity is similar and this implies
\alpha_{t}^{\phi}(x)\in{\mathcal{N}}
. By Takesaki’s theorem
[
76
]
,
there exists
\phi
-preserving conditional expectation satisfying the defining property

\phi(xy)=\phi(xE(y))\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\hskip 2.84544ptx\in{\mathcal{N}},y\in{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt,

from which the GNS-
\phi
-symmetricness follows.
∎

4.2.
Haagerup’s reduction

A von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
is called type III if it does not admit a non-trivial semifinite trace.
We briefly review the basics of Haagerup’s construction and refer to
[
40
]
for more details. The key idea is to consider the additive subgroup
G=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}
of the automorphism group.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset B(H)
be a von Neumann algebra and
\phi
be a normal faithful state.
One can define the crossed product by the action
\alpha^{\phi}:G\curvearrowright{\mathcal{M}}

\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt{\mathcal{M}}\rtimes_{\alpha^{\phi}}G\hskip 2.84544pt,

\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
can be considered as the von Neumann subalgebra
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}=\{\pi({\mathcal{M}}),\lambda(G)\}^{\prime\prime}\subset{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(G))
generated by the embeddings

\displaystyle\pi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\rtimes_{\alpha^{\phi}}G\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\pi(a)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\sum_{g}\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a)\otimes|{g}\rangle\langle{g}|

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lambda:G\to{\mathcal{M}}\rtimes_{\alpha^{\phi}}G,\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\lambda(g)(|{x}\rangle\otimes|{h}\rangle)=|{x}\rangle\otimes|{gh}\rangle\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\hskip 2.84544pt|{x}\rangle\in H\hskip 2.84544pt,|{h}\rangle\in\ell_{2}(G)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Basically,
\pi
is the transference homomorphism
{\mathcal{M}}\to\ell_{\infty}(G,{\mathcal{M}})
, and
\lambda
is the left regular representation on
\ell_{2}(G)
.
The set of finite sums

\{\sum_{g}a_{g}\lambda(g)\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pta_{g}\in{\mathcal{M}}\}\subset\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
forms a dense
w^{*}
-subalgebra of
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
.
In the following, we identify
{\mathcal{M}}
with
\pi({\mathcal{M}})\subset\hat{M}
(resp.
a
with
\pi(a)
) and view
{\mathcal{M}}\subset\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
as a subalgebra.
The state
\phi
admits a natural extension as a normal faithful state on
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}

\hat{\phi}(\sum_{g}a_{g}\lambda(g))\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\phi(a_{0})\hskip 2.84544pt.

Moreover,

E_{\mathcal{M}}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptE_{{\mathcal{M}}}(\sum_{g}a_{g}\lambda(g))=a_{0}

is the canonical normal conditional expectation such that
\phi\circ E_{\mathcal{M}}\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\hat{\phi}
.

The main object in Haagerup’s construction is an increasing family of subalgebras

{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\psi_{n}}:=\{x\in\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha_{t}^{\psi_{n}}(x)=x\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall\hskip 2.84544ptt\in{\mathbb{R}}\}\hskip 2.84544pt,

given by the centralizer algebra
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\psi_{n}}
for a suitable family of states
\psi_{n}
so that
\bigcup_{n}{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is
w^{*}
-dense in
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
. The state
\psi_{n}
is defined via a Radon-Nikodym density w.r.t to
\hat{\phi}

\psi_{n}(x)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\hat{\phi}(e^{-a_{n}}x)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pta_{n}\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt-i2^{n}\text{Log}(\lambda(2^{-n}))\hskip 2.84544pt.

Here
Log
is the principal branch of the logarithmic function with
0\leq\text{Log}(z)<2\pi
.
Each subalgebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
contains
\lambda(G)
and there exists normal conditional expectation
E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. Indeed, by the definition of
\psi_{n}
, the modular group
\alpha_{t}^{\psi_{n}}
is
2^{-n}
periodic. The explicit form (see
[
40
, Lemma 2.3]
) is given by

E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=2^{n}\int_{0}^{2^{-n}}\alpha_{t}^{\psi_{n}}dt\hskip 2.84544pt.

The normalized state
\tau_{n}=\frac{\psi_{n}}{\psi_{n}(1)}
is a normalized trace on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.
The key properties of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
are summarized in
[
40
, Theorem 2.1 & Lemma 2.7]
, which we state below.

Theorem 4.3
.

With above notations,
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is an increasing family of von Neumann subalgebras satisfying the following properties

(1)

Each
({\mathcal{M}}_{n},\tau_{n})
is a finite von Neumann algebra.

(2)

\bigcup_{n\geq 1}{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is weak
∗
-dense in
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
.

(3)

There exists a
\hat{\phi}
-preserving normal faithful conditional expectation
E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
such that

\hat{\phi}\circ E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=\hat{\phi}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha_{t}^{\hat{\phi}}\circ E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}\circ\alpha_{t}^{\hat{\phi}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Moreover,
E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}(x)\to x
in
\sigma
-strong topology for any
x\in\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
.

We now look at the Haagerup reduction on the states. For a state
\rho\in S({\mathcal{M}})
,
\hat{\rho}=\rho\circ E_{\mathcal{M}}
is the canonical extension on
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
. We denote
\rho_{n}:=\hat{\rho}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n,*}
as the restriction state of
\hat{\rho}
on the subalgebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\subset\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
. Note that the predual
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,*}
can be viewed as a subspace of
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{*}
via the embedding

\iota_{n,*}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{n,*}\to\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{*}\hskip 2.84544pt,\iota_{n,*}(\omega)=\omega\circ E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Via this identification,
\rho_{n}=\hat{\rho}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}\circ E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=\hat{\rho}\circ E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n},*}(\hat{\rho})\in\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{*}
. Moreover, by the weak
∗
-density of the family
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
,
\rho_{n}\to\hat{\rho}
converges in the weak topology. An immediate consequence is the following approximation of relative entropy.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be two normal states of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Then

D(\rho||\sigma)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544ptD(\hat{\rho}||\hat{\sigma})=\lim_{n\to\infty}D(\rho_{n}||\sigma_{n})\hskip 2.84544pt.

Proof.

Let
\iota:{\mathcal{M}}\subset\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
be the inclusion map. Because
\hat{\rho}=\rho\circ E_{\mathcal{M}}
is an extension of
\rho
,
\iota_{*}(\hat{\rho})=\hat{\rho}|_{\mathcal{M}}=\rho
and similarly for
\sigma
. Both
\iota:{\mathcal{M}}\to\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
and
E_{\mathcal{M}}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
are quantum Markov maps. Then by the data processing inequality,

D(\rho||\sigma)=D(\iota_{*}(\hat{\rho})||\iota_{*}(\hat{\sigma}))\leq D(\hat{\rho}||\hat{\sigma})=D(E_{{\mathcal{M}},*}(\rho)||E_{{\mathcal{M}},*}(\sigma))\leq D(\rho||\sigma).

Thus
D(\rho||\sigma)=D(\hat{\rho}||\hat{\sigma})
.
As for the limit, we have

\displaystyle D(\hat{\rho}\|\hat{\sigma})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}D(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n}D(E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n},*}(\rho_{n})||E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n},*}(\sigma_{n,*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(\hat{\rho}||\hat{\sigma})\hskip 2.84544pt,

where the equality follows from the lower semi-continuity of relative entropy (see e.g.
[
41
, Theorem 2.7]
). The second inequality is another use the data processing inequality.
∎

We shall also apply the Haagerup’s reduction on GNS-symmetric maps. Let
\Phi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a GNS-

-symmetric quantum Markov map. Its canonical extension map

\hat{\Phi}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\to\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\hat{\Phi}(\sum_{g}a_{g}\lambda(g))\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\sum_{g}\Phi(a_{g})\lambda(g)\hskip 2.84544pt

4.3.
Entropy contraction

We shall now discuss the entropy contraction of GNS-
\phi
-symmetric channels. The first step is to extend the entropy difference Lemma
2.1
. Define the state space that are bounded with respect to
\phi
,

S_{b}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)=\{\rho\in S({\mathcal{M}})\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544ptc^{-1}\phi\leq\rho\leq c\phi\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for some }c>0\}\hskip 2.84544pt.

For all
\rho\in S_{b}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
,
D(\rho||\phi)<\infty
is finite. Such
S_{b}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
is a dense subset of
S({\mathcal{M}})
because for any
\rho
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\rho_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon\phi\in S_{b}({\mathcal{M}})
.
For
\rho\in S_{b}({\mathcal{M}})
, we define the entropy difference for a GNS-
\phi
-symmetric quantum channel
\Phi_{*}
as

D_{\Phi_{*}}(\rho):=D(\rho||\phi)-D(\Phi_{*}(\rho)||\phi)\hskip 2.84544pt.

By data processing inequality and
\Phi_{*}(\phi)=\phi
,
D_{\Phi_{*}}(\rho)\geq 0
. In the trace symmetric case,
D_{\Phi_{*}}(\rho)=D(\rho||1)-D(\Phi_{*}(\rho)||1)=H(\rho)-H(\Phi_{*}(\rho))
as in Section
2.2
. Let
E
be the conditional expectation onto the multiplicative domain of
\Phi
.
By the chain rule
[
66
, Theorem 2]
that for any
E

invariant state
\psi\circ E=\psi
,

D(\rho||\psi)=D(\rho||E_{*}(\rho))+D(E_{*}(\rho)||\psi)\hskip 2.84544pt,

we have the alternative expressions
D_{\Phi_{*}}(\rho)=D(\rho||E_{*}(\rho))-D(\Phi_{*}(\rho)||\Phi_{*}E_{*}(\rho)),
where we used the property
\Phi_{*}E_{*}=E_{*}\Phi_{*}
in Proposition
4.2
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\Phi_{*}
be a GNS-
\phi
-symmetric quantum channel.
For any state
\rho,\omega\in S_{b}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)
,

D(\rho||\Phi_{*}^{2}(\omega))\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544ptD_{\Phi_{*}}(\rho)+D(\rho||\omega)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Proof.

Recall that we use
\rho_{n}=\hat{\rho}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n},*}(\hat{\rho})
and
\omega_{n}=\hat{\omega}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n},*}(\hat{\omega})

as the restriction states on finite von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\subset\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
obtained from the Haagerup reduction. By Lemma
4.5
, we know that
\Phi_{n}=\hat{\Phi}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
is a quantum Markov map symmetric with respect to the tracial state
\tau_{n}
. Thus by Lemma
2.1
in the tracial case,

D(\rho_{n}||\Phi_{n}^{2}(\omega_{n}))\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544ptD_{\Phi_{n}}(\rho_{n})+D(\rho_{n}||\omega_{n})\hskip 2.84544pt,

where we identify
\Phi_{n}=\Phi_{n,*}
by trace symmetry. Here, since
\Phi_{n}=\Phi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
is GNS-symmetric to
\phi_{n}=\phi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}

5.1.

In this section, we discuss the implications of our results on contraction coefficients studied in

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Phi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a quantum Markov map GNS-
\phi
-symmetric to a normal faithful state
\phi

be the
\phi
-preserving conditional expectation onto the multiplicative domain of

We define

i)

the

-contraction coefficient:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lambda(\Phi):=\parallel\!\Phi(\id-E):L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)\!\parallel.

ii)

the entropy contraction coefficient:

\alpha(\Phi):=\sup_{\rho}\frac{D(\Phi_{*}(\rho)||\Phi_{*}\circ E_{*}(\rho))}{D(\rho||E_{*}(\rho))}\hskip 2.84544pt.

iii)

the complete entropy contraction coefficient
\alpha_{c}(\Phi):=\sup_{{\mathcal{Q}}}\alpha(\id_{\mathcal{Q}}\otimes\Phi)
where the supremum is over all
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebras
{\mathcal{Q}}
.

The condition
\lambda(\Phi)<1
can be viewed as a Poincaré inequality for a quantum channel
\Phi
, which implies the exponential convergence in
L_{2}

\parallel\!\Phi^{n}(X)-E(X)\!\parallel_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)}\leq\lambda(\Phi)^{n}\parallel\!X-E(X)\!\parallel_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}},\phi)}\to 0\hskip 2.84544pt.

Similarly, the entropy contraction coefficient gives the convergence in relative entropy

D(\Phi^{n}(\rho)||\Phi^{n}\circ E(\rho))\leq\alpha(\Phi)^{n}D(\rho||E(\rho))\hskip 2.84544pt,

The complete constant
\alpha_{c}(\Phi)
controls not only the entropy contraction of
\Phi
but also
\id_{\mathcal{Q}}\otimes\Phi
with any environment system
\mathcal{Q}
. This leads to the tensorization property of
\alpha_{c}
that for two GNS-symmetric quantum channels
[
35
]

(5.2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{c}(\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2})=\max\{\alpha_{c}(\Phi_{1}),\alpha_{c}(\Phi_{2})\}

For classical Markov maps, the tensorization property (
5.2
) is known to also hold for the non-complete constant
\alpha
. Nevertheless, for quantum Markov map (channel), this is not the case and
\alpha(\Phi)
in general can be strictly less than
\alpha_{c}(\Phi)
(see
[
16
, Section 4.4]
).

In finite dimensions, the existence of strictly contractive constant
\alpha_{c}(\Phi)<1
was obtained in
[
35
, Theorem 4.1]
. Our results gives an explicit estimate for
\alpha_{c}(\Phi)
.

\Phi
be a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov map,

Proof.

The estimate follows from Theorem
4.8
and a discrete time analog of Proposition
4.12
.
∎

Remark 5.3

In the ergodic trace symmetric case

and

,
we have the trace map

\displaystyle\lambda(\Phi)\leq\alpha(\Phi)\leq\alpha_{c}(\Phi)\leq(1-\frac{-\ln\lambda(\Phi)}{\ln(10d^{2})})

This can be compared to

[
61
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
47
, Corollary 27]
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\alpha(\Phi)\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{2}(\id-\Phi^{*}\Phi)\leq 1-\frac{(1-\lambda(\Phi)^{2})^{2}(1-\frac{2}{d})}{\ln(d-1)}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
\alpha_{2}(\id-\Phi^{2})
is the LSI constant of
\id-\Phi^{2}
as a generator of quantum Markov semigroup. The two upper bounds in (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) are comparable, as both are asymptotically
\Theta(\frac{-\ln\lambda(\Phi)}{\ln d})
. The strength of our results is that (
5.3
) also bounds the complete constant

which has tensorization property.

Remark 5.4
.

Our Lemma
2.1
implies

1-\alpha_{1}(\id-\Phi^{2})\leq\alpha(\Phi),

where
\alpha_{1}
is MLSI constant of the semigroup generator

.
For classical Markov map, it was proved by Del Moral, Ledoux and Miclo

0<c<1
such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle 1-\alpha_{1}(\id-\Phi^{*}\Phi)\leq\alpha(\Phi)\leq 1-c\alpha_{1}(\id-\Phi^{*}\Phi)\hskip 2.84544pt.

5.2.
Graph random walks

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite undirected graph with
|V|=d
and the edge set
E\subset V\times V
. The discrete time random walk on
G
is a finite Markov chain given by the stochastic matrix

K_{G}(u,v)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d(u)},&\mbox{if }(u,v)\in E\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Here
d(u)
is the degree of vertex
u\in V
. Then
K_{G}:l_{\infty}(V)\to l_{\infty}(V)
is a Markov map.
If
G
is not bipartite,
K_{G}
admits a unique station distribution
\pi(u)=\frac{d(u)}{2m}
, where
|E|=m
. It is clear that
K_{G}
is symmetric to the measure
\pi
, also called reversible. Hence
K_{G}
is an ergodic unital channel on
L_{\infty}(V,\pi)
as
\pi(K_{G}(f))=\pi(f)
. The expectation map is
E_{\pi}(f)=\pi(f)1
whose index is

C_{cb}(E_{\pi})=\parallel\!\pi^{-1}\!\parallel_{\infty}.

Both
K_{G}
is connected and
E_{\pi}
are symmetric operator on
L_{2}(V,\pi)
and

\lambda(K_{G})=\parallel\!K_{G}-E_{\pi}:L_{2}(V,\pi)\to L_{2}(V,\pi)\!\parallel<1

if
K_{G}
not bipartite (in the bipartite case
K_{G}
has eigenvalue
-1
). Then our results implies

(5.6)

\displaystyle\alpha(K_{G})\leq\alpha_{c}(K_{G})\leq(1-\frac{1}{2k_{cb}(K_{G})})\leq(1-\frac{-\ln\lambda(K_{G})}{\ln(10\parallel\!\pi^{-1}\!\parallel_{\infty})})\hskip 2.84544pt.

Example 5.5

(Cyclic graphs)
.

Let us consider the cyclic graph
C_{d}=(V,E)
with
d\geq 4
where
V=\{1,\cdots,d\}
and
E=\{(j,j+1)|j=1,\cdots,d\}
. Here the addition is understood in the sense of “mod d”.
Then

K_{C_{d}}(i,j)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{if }|i-j|=1\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As
C_{d}
is 2-regular,
K_{C_{d}}
is symmetric to the uniform distribution
\pi(i)=1/d
. It is known that
K_{C_{d}}
has spectrum

\lambda_{j}=\cos(\frac{2\pi j}{d})\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptj=0,\cdots,d-1\hskip 2.84544pt.

The associated eigenvector is
e_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}(1,\omega^{j},\omega^{2j},\cdots,\omega^{(d-1)j})
where
\omega=\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{d})
.
When
d=2m+1
is odd,
\pi
is the unique stationary measure and
E_{\pi}
is the projection onto the vector
e_{0}
. We have

\displaystyle K_{G}^{k}-E_{\pi}=(K_{G}-E_{\pi})^{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}\lambda_{j}^{k}|{e_{j}}\rangle\langle{e_{j}}|

By triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\parallel\!K_{G}^{k}-E_{\pi}:L_{1}(V,\pi)\to L_{\infty}(V,\pi)\!\parallel

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{2m}|\lambda_{j}|^{k}=2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\cos(\frac{\pi j}{d})^{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{d}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{k}(x)dx=2\frac{d}{\pi}W_{k}\leq 2Cd\sqrt{\frac{1}{2k\pi}}

where
C>0
is some absolute constant by fact that the Wallis integrals
W_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{k}(x)dx\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2k}}
. Thus

k_{cb}(K_{C_{d}})\leq\frac{(10Cd)^{2}}{\pi}\sim\mathcal{O}(d^{2}),

and (
5.6
) implies

\alpha(K_{C_{d}})\geq\alpha_{c}(K_{C_{d}})\geq 1-\mathcal{O}(d^{-2})

By Miclo’s result (
5.5
), this is asymptotically tight because the MLSI constant
\alpha_{1}(I-K_{G}^{2})\sim\mathcal{O}(d^{-2})
(see Example
5.6
below for detials).
The similar asymptotic estimate also holds for even circle
d=2m
.

For the continuous time random walk, we consider
w:E\to(0,\infty)
be a positive weighted function on the edge set
E
. The (weighted) graph Laplacian is given by the matrix

\displaystyle L_{G}(u,v)=\begin{cases}\sum_{e=(u,u^{\prime})\in E}w_{e},&\mbox{if }u=v\\
-w_{e},&\mbox{if }(u,v)\in E\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

L_{G}
generates the continuous time random walk
T_{t}=e^{-L_{G}t}
as a Markov semigroup and is symmetric with to the uniform distribution
\pi
on
V
.
T_{t}
is ergodic if and only if
G=(V,E)
is connected.
The expectation map
E_{\pi}(f)=\pi(f){\bf 1}
has index
C_{cb}(E_{\pi})=d
. Then Corollary
1.7
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(L_{G})}{2(\ln d+\ln 10)}\leq\alpha_{c}(L_{G})\leq\alpha(L_{G})\leq\lambda(L_{G})\hskip 2.84544pt.

This lower bound of
\alpha_{c}(L_{G})
has better dependence on the dimension

than
[
53
, Lemma 5.2]
.

5.3.
A noncommutative Birth-Death process

Let us illustrate our estimate with a noncommutative Birth-Death process. This example is a generalization of graph Laplacians on matrix algebras (see
[
53
,
45
]
for similar constructions). To fix the notation, let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph with
n=|V|
vertices and edge set
E
. For each edge
(r,s)\in E
, we introduce the edge Lindbladian on
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
,

\displaystyle L_{rs}(x)=

\displaystyle e^{\beta_{rs}/2}L_{e_{rs}}(x)+e^{-\beta_{rs}}L_{e_{sr}}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{\beta_{rs}/2}(e_{ss}x+xe_{ss}-2e_{sr}xe_{rs})+e^{-\beta_{rs}/2}(e_{rr}x+xe_{rr}-2e_{rs}xe_{sr})\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
e_{rs}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
is the matrix unit with
1
at the
(r,s)
position. The total Lindbladian is a weighted sum over the edge set
E
,

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\sum_{(r,s)\in E}w(r,s)L_{rs}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt2\sum_{s\in V}\left(\sum_{(r,s)\in E}w(r,s)e^{\beta_{rs}/2}\right)(e_{ss}x+xe_{ss})-4\sum_{(r,s)\in E}w(r,s)e^{\beta_{rs}/2}e_{sr}xe_{rs}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where we assume
\beta_{rs}=-\beta_{sr}
and
w(r,s)=w(s,r)>0
for the GNS-symmetry condition.
Note that for
j\neq k
,

\displaystyle L(e_{jk})=2(\sum_{(r,k)\in E}w(r,k)e^{\beta_{rk}/2}+\sum_{(r,j)\in E}w(r,j)e^{\beta_{r,j}/2})e_{jk}\hskip 2.84544pt,

\displaystyle L(e_{jj})=4\sum_{(r,j)\in E}w(r,j)(e^{\beta_{r,j}/2}e_{jj}-e^{-\beta_{r,j}/2}e_{rr})\hskip 2.84544pt.

Let us collect some relevant facts of such a Lindbladian
L
as noncommutative extension of graph Laplacian.

i)

Denote
\ell_{\infty}(V)\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
as the diagonal subalgebra.

L(\ell_{\infty}(V))\subset\ell_{\infty}(V)
, and
L|_{\ell_{\infty}(V)}
is a weighted graph Laplacian;

ii)

For
r\neq s
, the matrix unit
e_{rs}
is an eigenvector of
L

L(e_{rs})\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\gamma_{rs}e_{rs},

where
\gamma_{rs}=2(\sum_{(r,j)\in E}w(r,j)e^{\beta_{rj}/2}+\sum_{(k,s)\in E}w(k,s)e^{-\beta_{ks}/2})
.

iii)

\ker(L)\subset\ell_{\infty}(V)
, and
\ker(L)=\mathbb{C}1
if
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is connected.

iv)

Let
\mu=(\mu_{k})\in\ell_{\infty}(V)
be a density operator in the diagonal subalgebra. Then
L
is GNS-
\mu
-symmetric if
e^{\beta_{rs}}=\mu_{s}/\mu_{r}
for any
s\neq r
.

Assume
L=\sum_{(s,r)\in E}L_{sr}
is an ergodic graph Lindbladian satisfying GNS-
\mu
-symmetric condition for a diagonal density operator
\mu
. Denote
E_{d}
as the projection onto diagonal subalgebra. We can decompose the semigroup
T_{t}=e^{-tL}
on the diagonal part and off diagonal part.

(5.8)

\displaystyle T_{t}=T_{t}E_{d}+T_{t}(\id-E_{d}):=T_{t}^{diag}+T_{t}^{off}\hskip 2.84544pt.

It is clear from i) and ii) that
T_{t}E_{d}
is a classical graph random walk and
T_{t}(\id-E_{d})
is a Schur multiplier on
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
. Using this decomposition, we consider the CB-return time of the semigroup

t_{cb}(\varepsilon):=\inf\{t>0\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\parallel\!T_{t}-E_{\mu}:L_{1}({\mathbb{M}}_{n},\mu)\to{\mathbb{M}}_{n}\!\parallel_{cb}\leq\epsilon\}

satisfying

t_{cb}(2\varepsilon)\leq t^{diag}_{cb}(\varepsilon)+t^{off}_{cb}(\varepsilon)\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
t^{diag}_{cb}
and
t^{off}_{cb}
are the CB-return time for the diagonal part
T_{t}E_{d}
and off diagonal part
T_{t}(\id-E_{d})
respectively.
where

\displaystyle t_{cb}^{diag}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=:\inf\{t>0\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\|T_{t}E_{d}-E_{\mu}:L_{1}(v,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(V)\|_{cb}\leq\epsilon\}

\displaystyle t_{cb}^{off}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=:\inf\{t>0\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\|T_{t}(\id-E_{d}):L_{1}(\mathbb{M}_{n},\mu)\to\mathbb{M}_{n}\|_{cb}\leq\varepsilon\}

For the diagonal part,
t_{cb}^{diag}(\epsilon)
is a classical
L_{\infty}
mixing time, i.e. the smallest
t
such that

\parallel\!T_{t}E_{d}-E_{\mu}:L_{1}(V,\mu)\to L_{\infty}(V)\!\parallel\leq\varepsilon\hskip 2.84544pt.

For the off-diagonal term, we have by Effros-Ruan isomorphism that a Schur multiplier map

\displaystyle\parallel\!T_{t}(\id-E_{d}):L_{1}({\mathbb{M}}_{n},\mu)\to{\mathbb{M}}_{n}\!\parallel_{cb}=

\displaystyle\parallel\!\sum_{r\neq s}\mu_{r}^{-1/2}e^{-\gamma_{rs}t}\mu_{s}^{-1/2}e_{rs}\otimes e_{rs}\!\parallel_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\parallel\!\sum_{r\neq s}\mu_{r}^{-1/2}e^{-\gamma_{rs}t}\mu_{s}^{-1/2}e_{rs}\!\parallel_{\infty}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Note that for each
t
,

A_{t}=\sum_{r\neq s}\mu_{r}^{-1/2}e^{-\gamma_{rs}t}\mu_{s}^{-1/2}e_{rs}

is a symmetric matrix with positive entry. A standard application of Schur’s lemma for matrices with positive entries implies

\displaystyle\parallel\!A_{t}\!\parallel_{\infty}\leq\sup_{r}\Big(\sum_{s}\mu_{r}^{-1/2}e^{-\gamma_{rs}t}\mu_{s}^{-1/2}\Big)\hskip 2.84544pt,

which gives us an estimate for the off diagonal term
t_{cb}^{off}(\epsilon)
.

Now we consider the birth-death process on a finite state space
V=\{1,\cdots,n\}
, which we denote as
L^{BD}_{n}
. The corresponding edge
E
set consists of only successive vertices
E=\{(j,j+1)|1\leq j\leq n-1\}
. The simplest case chooses the uniform weight
w(r,s)=1
for
(r,s)\in E
and allows only one Bohr frequency
e^{-\beta}=\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{j+1}}
, and the resulting stationary measure is the well-studied thermal state

\mu=Z_{\beta}^{-1}(e^{-\beta j})_{j=1}^{n}\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
Z_{\beta}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{-\beta j}
is the normalization constant. In this case,
\gamma_{rs}=8(\cosh\beta)t
, and the off diagonal CB norm can be estimated by

\displaystyle\parallel\!A_{t}\!\parallel_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{r}\Big(\sum_{s=1}^{n}e^{\beta r/2}e^{\beta s/2}\Big)Z_{\beta}e^{-8(\cosh\beta)t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\beta\frac{n-2}{2}}\frac{1-e^{n\beta/2}}{1-e^{\beta/2}}\frac{1-e^{-n\beta}}{1-e^{-\beta}}e^{-8(\cosh\beta)t}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Thus
t_{cb}^{off}(\varepsilon)\leq C_{1}(\beta)n
for some constant
C_{1}(\beta)
depending on
\beta
. For the classic part, We refer to
[
59
]
and
[
19
]
for the fact that the spectral gap is of order
O(1)
, i.e.

c(\beta)\leq\lambda(L^{diag}_{n})\leq C_{2}(\beta)

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For the commutative system on the diagonal part this implies (see also
[
26
]
)

t^{diag}_{cb}(\varepsilon)\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pt2c(\beta)^{-1}(2+|\ln\mu_{n}|)\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544ptC_{2}(\beta)n\hskip 2.84544pt,

(for
\varepsilon=e^{-2}
, but here the actual value of
\varepsilon
does not change the asymptotic estimate).
On the other hand, we have based on
[
59
]
that

t^{diag}_{cb}(0.1)\geq\alpha_{1}(L^{diag}_{n})^{-1}\hskip 2.84544pt\geq\hskip 2.84544ptc(\beta)n\hskip 2.84544pt.

Combining the diagonal and off diagonal part, we know
t_{cb}(L^{BD}_{n})\sim n
. It turns out CMLSI constant has asymptotic
\alpha_{c}(L^{BD}_{n})\sim\frac{1}{n}
, which indicates our estimate
\alpha_{c}\geq\frac{1}{2t_{cb}}
is asymptotically tight for this example.

Theorem 5.7
.

For
\beta>0
, there exist constants
c(\beta),C(\beta)>0
such that the CMLSI constant of noncommutative birth-death process
L_{n}^{BD}
satisfies

\frac{c(\beta)}{n}\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha_{c}(L_{n}^{BD})\leq\alpha_{1}(L^{BD}_{n})\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{C(\beta)}{n}\hskip 2.84544pt.

The same
\Theta(\frac{1}{n})
asymptotic holds for
t_{cb}(L^{BD}_{n})^{-1}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\alpha_{c}(L^{BD}_{n})\leq\alpha_{1}(L^{BD}_{n})\leq\frac{C(\beta)}{n}\hskip 2.84544pt.

For this, we consider the function in the commutative system on the diagonal

f(k)=\frac{Z(\beta)}{n}e^{\beta k}\text{ and }\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(k)\mu(k)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{Z(\beta)}{n}e^{\beta k}\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}e^{-\beta k}=1

so that
\rho:=f\mu
represents a probability density. The relative entropy term satisfies

D(\rho||\mu)=D(f\mu||\mu)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\sum_{k}\frac{e^{-\beta k}}{Z(\beta)}f(k)(\beta k+\ln Z(\beta)-\ln n)\ \hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\ln Z(\beta)-\ln n+\beta\frac{n+1}{2}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Our density is
\rho\equiv(\frac{1}{n})
and the reference density is
\mu(k)=\frac{e^{-\beta k}}{Z(\beta)}
.

Denote
a_{k}=|k\rangle\langle k+1|
. On the diagonal, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}L_{*}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}e^{\beta/2}(a_{k}a_{k}^{*}f-a_{k}^{*}fa_{k})+e^{-\beta/2}(a_{k}^{*}a_{k}f-a_{k}fa_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}e^{\beta/2}(e_{k}f(k)-f(k)e_{k+1})+e^{-\beta/2}(f(k+1)e_{k+1}-f(k+1)e_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z(\beta)n}(e^{\beta/2}(e_{0}-e_{n})+e^{-\beta/2}(e_{n}-e_{0}))\hskip 2.84544pt.

We have

\displaystyle L_{n,*}^{BD}(f)(k)=\begin{cases}4(e^{\beta/2}-e^{-\beta/2}),&\quad\text{if}\quad k=1;\\
0,&\quad\text{if}\quad k=2,n-1;\\
4(e^{-\beta/2}-e^{\beta/2}),&\quad\text{if}\quad k=n.\\
\end{cases}

Note that

\ln\rho-\ln\mu\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\ln f\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\big(\beta k-\ln(Z(\beta)n)\big)_{k=1}^{n}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Then we have the entropy production

I_{L_{n}^{BD}}(\rho)=\tau(L_{n,*}^{BD}(f)\ln f)\sim c(\beta)\hskip 2.84544pt

for some constant
c(\beta)
only depending on
\beta
. This holds for
n\hskip 2.84544pt\geq\hskip 2.84544ptn_{0}
large enough.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

When
\beta>0
,
\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{-\beta k}=O(1)
as a geometric series. In the case that
\beta=0
, the above birth-death process reduces to a “broken” version of the cyclic graph (linear graph) as in Example

with

.

5.4.
Noncommutative concentration inequality

In this section we show that
CMLSI
of a GNS-
\phi
-symmetric semigroup implies concentration inequalities for the state
\phi
. The key quantity in the discussion is the Lipschitz semi-norm

\|x\|_{\text{Lip}}^{2}\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\max\{\parallel\!\Gamma_{L}(x,x)\!\parallel\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\parallel\!\Gamma_{L}(x^{*},x^{*})\!\parallel\}

where the
gradient form
(or Carré du Champ operator) is

\Gamma_{L}(x,y)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{1}{2}\Big(L(x^{*})y+x^{*}L(y)-L(x^{*}y)\Big)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall x,y\in\text{dom}(L)\hskip 2.84544pt.

Note that
\|\cdot\|_{\text{Lip}}
is a semi-norm (satisfying triangle inequality) because
\Gamma_{L}
is completely positive bilinear form.
Our first lemma is to show that
\|x\|_{\text{Lip}}
can be approximated by Haagerup reduction.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
. Then for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\|E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}(x)\|_{\text{Lip}}\hskip 2.84544pt\leq\hskip 2.84544pt\|x\|_{\text{Lip}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Proof.

Recall the conditional expectation
E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is given by

E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}(x)=2^{n}\int_{0}^{2^{-n}}\alpha_{t}^{\psi_{n}}(x)dt

Note that
\alpha_{t}^{\psi_{n}}
is an inner automorphism on
{\mathcal{M}}\rtimes_{\alpha}2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}\cong L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T},{\mathcal{M}})
. We note that for a modular automorphism
\alpha_{t}
such that
L\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{t}L
,

\Gamma_{L}(\alpha_{t}(x),\alpha_{t}(y))\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha_{t}(\Gamma_{L}(x,y))\hskip 2.84544pt,

which implies
\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{\text{Lip}}=\parallel\!\alpha_{t}(x)\!\parallel_{\text{Lip}}
.
Here both
\alpha_{t}^{\hat{\phi}}
and
\alpha_{t}^{\psi_{n}}
commutes with
\hat{L}=\id_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes L
by the GNS-symmetricness of
\hat{L}
.
Then by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\parallel\!E_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}(x)\!\parallel_{\text{Lip}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|2^{n}\int_{0}^{2^{-n}}\alpha_{t}^{\psi_{n}}(x)dt\Big\|_{\text{Lip}}\leq 2^{n}\int_{0}^{2^{-n}}\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{\text{Lip}}dt=\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{\text{Lip}}\hskip 2.84544pt.\quad\qed

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{0},{\mathcal{N}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}

\phi

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{N}}

satisfy the commuting square condition
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{N}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544pt,}
E
E_{0}
E_{0}
E

where
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{N}}
is a subalgebra.
Then for any
p\in[1,\infty]
and any

,

\parallel\!E_{0}(x)\!\parallel_{L_{\infty}^{p}({\mathcal{N}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{0},\phi)}=\parallel\!E_{0}(x)\!\parallel_{L_{\infty}^{p}({\mathcal{N}}\subset{\mathcal{M}},\phi)}\leq\parallel\!x\!\parallel_{L_{\infty}^{p}({\mathcal{N}}\subset{\mathcal{M}},\phi)}

as a

We can assume

in the Remark

2. GNS and KMS symmetry.
Both GNS-symmetry and KMS-symmetry are noncommutative generalizations for the detailed balance condition of classical Markov chains. As observed in
[
17
]
, GNS-symmetry is the strongest generalization of detail balance condition and KMS is the weakest, which means the assumption of GNS-symmetry is the most restrictive. It is natural to ask whether our main results (c.f. Theorem
1.6
&
4.8
) can be obtained for KMS-symmetric channels or semigroups.

Problem 6.3
.

Do entropy decay results Theorem
1.6
&
4.8
or the entropy difference Lemma
4.6

The key property of a GNS-symmetric map
\Phi
is the commutation with modular group
\Phi\circ\alpha_{t}^{\phi}=\alpha_{t}^{\phi}\circ\Phi
. This has been used to ensure the compatibility of Haagerup reduction with channel and semigroups (see Lemma 4.5). It is interesting to see whether the same commuting diagram Figure

can be obtained for KMS Markov maps. That will allow us to use Haagerup reduction to obtain the entropy difference Lemma (

) for KMS-symmetric channels. Another approach is, again, to avoid using Haagerup reduction and prove the KMS-case directly. At the moment of writing, this is not unclear to us even on finite dimensional matrix algebras.

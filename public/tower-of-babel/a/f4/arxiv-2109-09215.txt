Towards characterizing the > ω 2 >\omega^{2} -fickle recursively enumerable Turing degrees Journal: Annals of Pure and Applied Logic
Liling Ko

Abstract
Given a finite lattice L L that can be embedded in the recursively enumerable (r.e.) Turing degrees ⟨ ℛ T , ≤ T ⟩ \langle\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{T}},\leq_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle , it is not known how one can characterize the degrees 𝐝 ∈ ℛ T \mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{T}} below which L L can be bounded. Two important characterizations are of the L 7 L_{7} and M 3 M_{3} lattices, where the lattices are bounded below 𝐝 \mathbf{d} if and only if 𝐝 \mathbf{d} contains sets of “ fickleness ” > ω >\omega and ≥ ω ω \geq\omega^{\omega} respectively. We work towards finding a lattice that characterizes the levels above ω 2 \omega^{2} , the first non-trivial level after ω \omega . We considered lattices that are as “short” and “narrow” as L 7 L_{7} and M 3 M_{3} , but the lattices characterize also the > ω >\omega or ≥ ω ω \geq\omega^{\omega} levels, if the lattices are not already embeddable below all non-zero r.e. degrees. We also considered upper semilattices (USLs) by removing the bottom meet(s) of some previously considered lattices, but the removals did not change the levels characterized. This leads us to conjecture that a USL characterizes the same r.e. degrees as the lattice it is based on. We discovered three lattices besides M 3 M_{3} that also characterize the ≥ ω ω \geq\omega^{\omega} -levels. Our search for a > ω 2 >\omega^{2} -candidate therefore involves the lattice-theoretic problem of finding lattices that do not contain any of the four ≥ ω ω \geq\omega^{\omega} -lattices as sublattices.

\tau
is satisfied and inflicts no injury.

Proof.

\tau
never sets restraints or enumerates elements, and so cannot inflict injury. If the outcome of
\tau
is
fin
, then
\tau
is vacuously satisfied. So, assume there are infinitely many
\tau
-expansionary stages. Each follower
e
of
\tau
is assigned to at most one child node
\tau_{\Pi}
, which by the
\tau_{\Pi}
-strategy acts only finitely often until cancellation. Uses
\lambda_{A}(e)
,
\lambda_{B}(e)
, and

always exist at
\tau
-expansionary stages, and when

is enumerated, those uses are also enumerated. Therefore, the uses are lifted finitely often, and

.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

If
\lambda=\tau_{\Pi}
, then
\tau_{\Pi}
is satisfied and inflicts finite injury.

Proof.

Wait for
\tau_{\Pi}
to stop being initialized. Followers that are realized never become un-realized because of the restraints. If some follower was never realized, the lemma holds trivially. So assume all followers eventually become realized. Each follower
e
requests
\leq\omega^{|\tau_{\Pi}|+1}
many permissions. Some
e
must eventually receive enough permissions to satisfy
\tau_{\Pi}
, otherwise we can approximate
\Delta(X)
by a
\leq\omega^{|\tau_{\Pi}|+2}
-fickle function, contradicting the
\geq\omega^{\omega}
-fickleness of
\Delta(X)
. Even though
\omega^{|\tau_{\Pi}|+1}\geq\omega
, in reality
e
only enumerates finitely many elements, except these elements may not be indexed canonically by a natural number. Therefore, the injury by
\tau_{\Pi}
is finite.
∎

Lemma 5.6

Let
\lambda=\eta

be the first

can determine stage

\eta,m

believes its computation

1.

The subrequirement can only be injured by followers that existed at stage

2.

After stage

\Phi_{0}(m)

If
\eta^{\frown}\texttt{fin}\prec g

\eta

\eta
will also be satisfied from the above two claims.

Claim 1 follows from the tree framework: Let

be the first element whose enumeration contradicts the claim, and let

be node to which

must have existed at stage

y
be too large to inflict injury.

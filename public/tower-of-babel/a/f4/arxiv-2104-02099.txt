1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the class of analytic functions
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
such that
|f(z)|<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and let
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{f\in\mathcal{B}:f(0)=0\}
. In
1914
, Bohr
[
11
]
proved that if
f\in\mathcal{B}
is of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then the majorant series
M_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}
of
f
satisfies

(1.1)

M_{f_{0}}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1-|a_{0}|=d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
|z|=r\leq 1/3
, where
f_{0}(z)=f(z)-f(0)
. The interesting inequality (
1.1
) is known as the Bohr inequality. Bohr actually obtained the inequality (
1.1
) for
|z|\leq 1/6
. Later, Wiener, Riesz and Schur, independently established the inequaliy (
1.1
) for
|z|\leq 1/3
and hence proved that
1/3
is the best possible. The radius
1/3
is called as Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, for

\phi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-az},\;\;a\in[0,1),

it follows easily that
M_{\phi_{a}}(r)>1
if, and only if,
r>1/(1+2a)
, which for
a\rightarrow 1
shows that
1/3
is optimal.

The Bohr inequality has also emerged as an active area of research for operator algebraists after Dixon
[
13
]
used it to settle in the negative a conjecture that a Banach algebra satisfying a non-unital von Neumann inequality is necessarily an operator algebra. Subsequently, Paulson and Singh
[
28
]
, and Blasco
[
9
]
have extended the Bohr inequality in the context of Banach algebra.

The main aim of this paper is to establish various Bohr inequalities for the harmonic functions in a suitable fashion. A harmonic mapping in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is a complex-valued function
f=u+iv
of
z=x+iy
in
\mathbb{D}
, which satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\bar{z}}=0
, where
f_{z}=(f_{x}-if_{y})/2
and
f_{\bar{z}}=(f_{x}+if_{y})/2
and
u
and
v
are real-valued harmonic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. It follows that the function
f
admits the canonical representation
f=h+\bar{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. The Jacobian
J_{f}
of
f=h+\overline{g}
is given by
J_{f}=|h^{\prime}|^{2}-|g^{\prime}|^{2}
. We say that
f
is sense-preserving in
\mathbb{D}
if
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
. Consequently,
f
is locally univalent and sense-preserving in
\mathbb{D}
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
; or equivalently if
h^{\prime}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{D}
and the dilation
\omega_{f}:=\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
has the property that
|\omega(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
.

The interests in the Bohr phenomenon was revived in the
1990
s due to the extensions to holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and to abstract setting
[
4
]
. For example, Boas and Khavinshon
[
10
]
found bounds for Bohr’s radius in any complete Reinhard domains and proved that the Bohr radius decreses to zero as the dimension of the domain incresses. In the recent years, studying Bohr inequalities and Bohr radius is become an active area of research in univalent function theory. The Bohr’s phenomenon for the complex-valued harmonic mappings have been studied extensively by many authors (see
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
16
,
17
]
). In
2016
, Ali
et al.

[
5
]
studied Bohr radius for the stralike log-harmonic mappings and obtain several interesting results. In
2021
, Liu and Ponnusamy
[
27
]
established a version of multidimensional analogue of the refined Bohr inequality as well as improved Bohr inequality with initial coefficient being zero. Bohr-type inequalities for the class of harmonic
p
-symmetric mappings and also for harmonic mappings with a multiple zero at the origin have been discussed by Huang
et al.

[
18
]
. The Bohr radius for various classes of functions, for example, locally univalent harmonic mappings,
k
-quasiconformal mappings, bounded harmonic functions, lacunary series have been studied extensively in
[
19
,
21
,
24
]
. For more intriguing aspects of Bohr phenomenon we refer to
[
3
,
6
,
8
,
15
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
and the references therein.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of all complex-valued harmonic functions
f=h+\bar{g}
defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic
\mathbb{D}
with the normalization
h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)-1=0
and
g(0)=0
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be defined by
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{H}:g^{\prime}(0)=0\}.
Then each
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
has the following form

(1.2)

h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

In
1977
, Chichra
[
12
]
first introdcued the class
\mathcal{W}(\alpha)
consisting normalized analytic functions
h
, satisfying the condition
{\rm Re}\left(h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)>0\right)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Moreover, Chichra
[
12
]
has shown that functions in the class
\mathcal{W}(\alpha)
constitute a subclass of close-to-convex functions in
\mathbb{D}
. In
2014
, Nagpal and Ravichandran
[
25
]
studied the following class

\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}=\{f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{H}:{\rm Re}\left(h^{\prime}(z)+zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>|g^{\prime}(z)+zg^{\prime\prime}(z)|\;\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}

and obtained the coefficient bounds for the functions in the class
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
. In
2019
, Ghosh and Vasudevarao studied the class
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
, where

\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)=\{f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{H}:{\rm Re}\left(h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>|g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)|\;\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}.

From the following results, it is easy to see that functions in the class
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
are univalent for
\alpha\geq 0
, and they are closely related to functions in
\mathcal{W}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
17
]

The harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
belongs to
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
if, and only if, the analytic function
F=h+\epsilon g
belongs to
\mathcal{W}(\alpha)
for each
|\epsilon|=1.

The coefficient bounds and the sharp growth estimates for functions in the class
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
have been studied in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 1.4
.

[
17
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\geq 0
and be of the form (
1.2
). Then for any
n\geq 2
,

(i)

|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\leq\displaystyle\frac{2}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}
;

(ii)

||a_{n}|-|b_{n}||\leq\displaystyle\frac{2}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}
;

(iii)

|a_{n}|\leq\displaystyle\frac{2}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}
.

All these inequalities are sharp for the function
f=f_{\alpha}
given by

(1.5)

f_{\alpha}(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2z^{n}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}.

Lemma 1.6
.

[
17
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
and be of the form (
1.2
) with
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Then

(1.7)

|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}|z|^{n}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}\leq|f(z)|\leq|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2|z|^{n}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}.

Both the inequalities are sharp for the function
f=f_{\alpha}
given by (
1.5
).

Lemma 1.8
.

[
17
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\geq 0
and be of the form (
1.2
). Then for
n\geq 2
,

|b_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}.

The inequality is sharp for the function
f=f^{*}_{\alpha}
given by

(1.9)

f^{*}_{\alpha}(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{z^{n}}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}.

In
2020
, Vasudevarao and Halder
[
7
]
proved the following sharp Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
.

Theorem 1.10
.

[
7
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\geq 0
be of the form (
1.2
). Then

|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)|z|^{n}\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

for
|z|=r\leq r_{f}
, where

is the unique root of

r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2r^{n}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

. The radius

is the best possible.

In
2020
, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
23
]
obtained the following improved versions of Bohr inequality for analytic functions.

Theorem 1.11
.

[
23
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
,
|f(z)|\leq 1
and
S_{r}
denotes the image of the subdisk
|z|<r
under the mapping
f
. Then

B_{1}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3},

and the number
1/3
and
16/9
cannot be improved. Moreover,

B_{2}(r):=|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{8}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{2},

and the number
1/2
and
8/9
cannot be improved.

In analogy to Bohr radius, Bohr-Rogosinski radius has also been defined (see
[
29
]
) which is describes as follows: If
f\in\mathcal{B}
, then for
N\geq 1
, we have
|S_{N}(z)|<1
in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{1/2}
and this radius is sharp, where
S_{N}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}z^{n}
denotes the partial sum of
f
. There is a relavent quantity, which we call the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R^{f}_{N}(z)
of
f
defined by

(1.12)

R^{f}_{N}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n},\;\;|z|=r.

It is important to note that for
N=1
, the quantity (
1.12
) is reduces to the classical Bohr sum in which
f(0)
is replaced by
|f(z)|
. In
2017
, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
20
]
proved the following interesting result on Bohr-Rogosinski radius for the analytic functions.

Theorem 1.13
.

[
20
]

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq R_{N},

where
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r^{2})=0
. The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible. Moreover,

|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq R^{\prime}_{N},

where
R^{\prime}_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r^{2})=0
. The radius
R^{\prime}_{N}
is the best possible.

The main aim of this paper is to study the Bohr phenomenon for the class
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\geq 0
. We find the improved Bohr radius, refined Bohr radius as well as Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for functions in the class
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
. In Section 2, we state the main results of the paper, and in Section 3, we prove all the main results.

2.
Main results

Before stating the main results of this paper, we recall the definiton of dilogarithm. The dilogarithm function
{\rm Li}_{2}(z)
is defined by the power series

{\rm Li}_{2}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{2}}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|<1.

The definition and the name, of course, come from the analogy with the Taylor series of the ordinary logarithm around
1
,

-\log(1-z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|<1,

which leads similarly to the defintion of the polylogarithm

{\rm Li}_{m}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{m}}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|<1,\;\;m=1,2,3,\ldots.

Figure 1.
Graph of
{\rm Li}_{2}(r)
for
r\in(-0.5,1)
.

In addition, the relation

\frac{d}{dz}\left({\rm Li}_{m}(z)\right)=\frac{1}{z}{\rm Li}_{m-1}(z)\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;m\geq 2

is obvious and leads by induction to the extension of the domain of definition of
{\rm Li}_{m}
to the cut plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,\infty)
. In particular, the analytic continuation of the dilogarithm is given by

{\rm Li}_{2}(z)=-\int_{0}^{z}\log(1-u)\frac{du}{u}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,\infty).

Proof of Theorem

Let

(3.1)

Let

be defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle J_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2r^{n}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2r^{n}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}\right)^{p}-1

Clearly, the function

is continuous on
[0,1]
and differentiable on
(0,1)
. Since,

\bigg|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{2}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;n\geq 2.

it is easy to see that

J_{1}(0)=-1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}<0.

On the other hand, since

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}\geq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;n\geq 2

a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle J_{1}(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}+2^{p}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n)^{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{p}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n)^{p}}>0.

Therefore,
J_{1}(0)J_{1}(1)<0
, and hence by the Intermediate value theorem, we conclude that
J_{1}(r)
has roots in
(0,1)
. In order to show that
J_{1}(r)
has exactly one root in
(0,1)
, it is sufficient to show that
J_{1}
is strictly monotic function in
(0,1)
. A simple computation shows that

J_{1}^{\prime}(r)=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2nr^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{np2^{p}r^{np-1}}{(\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n)^{p}}>0

for all
r\in(0,1)
, which shows that

is strictly increasing function. Therefore,
J_{1}(r)
has the unique root in
(0,1)

r_{p}(\alpha)
. Therefore, we have

and hence from (

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}.

r_{p}(\alpha)
is the best possible, we consider the function
f=f_{\alpha}
defined by (
1.5
). It is easy to see that, the function

and for
f=f_{\alpha}

(3.4)

It is easy to see that

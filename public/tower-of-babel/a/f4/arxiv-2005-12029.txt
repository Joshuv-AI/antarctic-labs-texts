Quantum Holonomy Fields
Nicolas Gilliers

Abstract
We investigate lattice and continuous quantum gauge theories on the Euclidean plane with a structure group that is replaced by a H H -algebra. H H -algebras are non-commutative analogues of groups and contain the class of Voiculescu’s dual groups. We are interested in non-commutative analogues of random gauge fields, which we describe through the random Holonomy that they induce. We propose a general definition of a Holonomy Field with symmetries displaying the structure of a H H -algebra and construct such a field starting from a quantum Lévy process on a H H -algebra in the category of probability spaces. We call them Quantum Holonomy Fields. We also consider the more abstract case of an H H -algebra in a given algebraic category. This yields the notion of a Categorical Holonomy Field. As an application, we define higher dimensional generalizations of the so-called Master Field on the plane.

together with morphisms

\iota_{d^{\prime}}\circ F(f)=\iota_{d},\penalty\ d,d^{\prime},\penalty\ f:d\to d^{\prime},

such that:

1.

2.

and

When

3.1
Classical lattice Holonomy Fields

Before giving the main definitions of this paper, namely the definition of a Quantum Holonomy Field on a

In this paper, all the paths that we will consider will be piecewise affine continuous paths on the Euclidean plane
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

{\sf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
the set of all these paths. Each path

\underline{c}

c_{1}

c_{2}

c_{1}
and
c_{2}
is defined in the most natural way and denoted by
c_{1}c_{2}
. Reversing the orientation of a path

results in a new path denoted by
c^{-1}
. A path which finishes at its starting point is called a
loop
. A loop which is as injective as possible, that is, a loop which visits twice its starting point and once every other point of its image, is called a
simple loop
. A path of the form
c\ell c^{-1}

c
is a path and
\ell
is a simple loop, is called a
lasso
(see Fig.
22

Figure 22:

A lasso drawn on the plane.

Let us call

a path that is either an injective path (thus with distinct endpoints) or a simple loop. By a
graph
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\mathbb{E}
of edges with the following properties:

1.

for all edge
e

\mathbb{E}

also belongs to
\mathbb{E}
,

2.

\mathbb{E}
which are not each other’s inverse meet, if at all, at some of their endpoints,

3.

the union of the ranges of the elements of
\mathbb{E}
is a connected subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

From the set

\mathbb{E}
, and the set
\mathbb{F}

of the graph, which are the bounded connected components of the complement of the union of the range of the edges of the graph. Although it is entirely determined by

that we regard as the graph.

From the graph
\mathbb{G}

{\sf L}_{v}(\mathbb{G})
of all loops based at

that can be obtained by concatenating edges of
\mathbb{G}

a monoid, with the constant loop as the unit element. In order to make a group out of this monoid, one introduces the backtracking equivalence of loops and the notion of reduced loops. A loop is
reduced
if in its expression as the concatenation of a sequence of edges (which is unique) one does not find any two consecutive edges of the form
ee^{-1}
. We denote by
{\sf RL}_{v}(\mathbb{G})
the subset of
{\sf L}_{v}(\mathbb{G})
formed by reduced loops. It is however not a submonoid of
{\sf L}_{v}(\mathbb{G})
, for the concatenation of two reduced loops needs not to be reduced. The appropriate operation on
{\sf RL}_{v}(\mathbb{G})
is that of opposite concatenation followed by reduction where, as the name indicates, one concatenates two loops, the second one preceding the first one, and then erases sub-loops of the form
ee^{-1}
until no such loops remain. It is true, although perhaps not entirely obvious, that this operation is well defined, in the sense that the order in which one erases backtracking sub-loops does not affect the final reduced loop.

From the graph
\mathbb{G}
and the vertex

. This group is in fact isomorphic in a very natural way to the fundamental group based at

of the subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
formed by the union of the edges of
\mathbb{G}
. An important property of the group

A classical lattice gauge field on

We choose a monoidal structure

1.

With

1.

is the stochastic process which is the strong solution of the following classical stochastic differential equation:

\begin{split}&\textrm{d}\textrm{{{U}}}_{N}^{\mathbb{K}}(t)=\textrm{d}{\sf W}_{N}^{\mathbb{K}}(t)\textrm{{{U}}}_{N}^{\mathbb{K}}(t)+\frac{1}{2}c^{\mathbb{K}}_{N}\textrm{{{U}}}_{N}^{\mathbb{K}}(t)\textrm{dt}\\
&\textrm{{{U}}}_{N}^{\mathbb{K}}(0)=I_{N},\end{split}

{\sf W}_{N}^{\mathbb{K}}
a standard Brownian motion
\mathfrak{u}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
with respect to the scalar product
\langle-,-\rangle_{N}

. The entries of

are, up to symmetries, independent Brownian motions whose variance scales as the inverse of the dimension

Lemma 29

The matricial stochastic process

exists for any time

\mathbb{U}(N,(\mathbb{K})
and is gauge-invariant;

The second and third points concern the initial object
k
. There exists an unique morphism
\eta_{k\mathrel{\sqcup}H}:k\to k\mathrel{\sqcup}H
and we prove that
(k,\eta_{k\mathrel{\sqcup}H})
is an object of
{\rm rCoMod}\mathcal{C}(H)
. The two morphisms
({\rm id}_{H}\mathrel{\sqcup}\eta_{k\mathrel{\sqcup}H})\circ\eta_{k\mathrel{\sqcup}H}
and
(\Delta\mathrel{\sqcup}{\rm id}_{H})\circ\eta_{k\mathrel{\sqcup}H}
are equal because there is a unique morphism from
k
to
k\mathrel{\sqcup}H\mathrel{\sqcup}H
. For the same reason,
(\varepsilon\mathrel{\sqcup}{\rm id}_{k})\circ\eta_{k\mathrel{\sqcup}H}={\rm id}_{k}
. The third point, that the unique map from
k
to any comodule-algebra
M
is equivariant, also follows from the same argument. The proof is complete.

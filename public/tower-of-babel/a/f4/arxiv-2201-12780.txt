1.1.

-gerbes

Let

Definition 1.1

A

We record the following results on

Proposition 1.2

There is a natural bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of

-gerbes on

We simply call
\alpha\in H^{2}(C,\mu_{n})

\mu_{n}
-gerbe under the above correspondence.

Definition 1.3

A
\mu_{n}
-gerbe

Definition 1.4

Let

C

-gerbe.

We recall the following facts on essentially trivial

-gerbs from (
[

The Kummer sequence

1\to\mu_{n}\to\mathbf{G}_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\to}\limits^{n}}\mathbf{G}_{m}\to 1

yields an exact sequence

\Pic C\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\to}\limits^{n}}\Pic C\to H^{2}(C,\mu_{n})\to H^{2}(C,\mathbf{G}_{m}).

Every essentially trivial
\mu_{n}
-gerbe

is isomorphic to
[L]^{\frac{1}{n}}

where

is an invertible line bundle on

There is an invertible
\alpha

\mathscr{L}_{\alpha}^{n}\simeq L

Given an

1.2.
Twisted sheaves and Stability

We recall the definition of stability and semistability for locally free sheaves on
C

For a vector bundle

\frac{deg(E)}{rank(E)}

The vector bundle

Let

For an invertible

We define deg

Definition 1.5
.

The slope

of a locally free

-twisted

is
\frac{deg(\mathscr{F})}{rank(\mathscr{F})}

An

\alpha
-twisted subbundle

\mu(\mathscr{G})\leq\mu(\mathscr{F})\,\,\,({resp.}\mu(\mathscr{G})<\mu(\mathscr{F})).

Let

-twisted invertible sheaf

over

The stability /semistability of an
\alpha
-twisted locally free sheaf

2.

In this section, we recall the Hecke correspondence, introduced by
Narasimhan and Ramanan (
[
22
]
]). This was further investigated by
Beauville-Laszlo-Sorger (
[
1
]
). We shall use the terminology and notations from (

We recall the definition of the stability index
s(E)
of a vector bundle
E

on
C

Definition 2.1
.

The stability index
s(E)
is defined to be the minimum of

\mu(E^{\prime\prime})-\mu(E^{\prime})
as
(E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime})
vary over pairs of locally free sheaves on

which fit into an exact sequence

0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0.

The bundle

is stable if and only if
s(E)>0
and semistable if and only if
s(E)\geq 0
.

Let
C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus

of characteristic not 2. We assume that

For any line bundle

be the moduli space of stable rank
r

locally free sheaves of determinant

L
and
M^{ss}_{C}(r,L)
the moduli space of semistable rank
r

locally free sheaves of determinant

L
. Let
P
be a closed point of
C(k)
. For

, let

\mathscr{E}\to C\times M_{C}(r,\mathscr{O}_{C}(P))

be the Poincaré bundle (
[
36
]
,
[
26
]
). Let
\mathscr{E}_{P}
be the restriction of
\mathscr{E}
to
P\times M_{C}(r,\mathscr{O}_{C}(P))
.
For
0<h<r
, let
\mathscr{P}=Gr(h,\mathscr{E}_{P})
be the Grassmannian bundle parametrizing rank

locally free quotients of
\mathscr{E}_{P}
. A closed point of
\mathscr{P}
is a pair
(E,F)
of vector bundles on
C
with

E\in M_{C}(r,\mathscr{O}_{C}(P))
and
E(-P)\subset F\subset E
and dim
(E_{P}/F_{P})=h
(
[

).

We have a morphism

given by

. This map is a
Grassmann bundle. We recall the following Lemma from (
[

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(E,F)\in\mathscr{P}
. If

and
E

, then

is semistable with
determinant
\mathscr{O}_{C}((1-h)P)

We thus have the Hecke correspondence

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathscr{P}&\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt-~-\longrightarrow}\limits^{s^{\prime}}}&M_{C}^{ss}(r,\mathscr{O}_{C}((1-h)P))\\
s\Big\downarrow&&&\hskip 56.9055pt(\star)\\
M_{C}(r,\mathscr{O}_{C}(P)).\end{array}

where the rational map

is defined by
s^{\prime}(E,F)=F
if

Then

In the above Hecke correspondence we may replace

by any line bundle L of degree 1 and get a Hecke correspondence

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathscr{P}&\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt-~-\longrightarrow}\limits^{s^{\prime}}}&M_{C}^{ss}(r,L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C}((-h)P))\\
s\Big\downarrow&&&\hskip 56.9055pt(\star)\\
M_{C}(r,L).\end{array}

By tensoring with a suitable power of
\mathscr{O}_{C}(P)
, one also gets a Hecke correspondence

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathscr{P}&\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt-~-\longrightarrow}\limits^{s^{\prime}}}&M_{C}^{ss}(r,L^{\prime}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C}((-h)P))\\
s\Big\downarrow&&&\hskip 56.9055pt(\star)\\
M_{C}(r,L^{\prime}).\end{array}

for any line bundle
L^{\prime}
of odd degree.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g=2

L

1

Proof.

Since the stability index of a rank 2 vector bundle is an integer,

Let

line bundles on

In this section we give a proof of a local global principle for rational points on certain conic

Theorem 4.1
.

(Suresh)

Let

.
Let
\alpha

Let
\tilde{\beta}\in\Br_{nr}(K(C)/k)

is a conic and
\Br_{nr}(K(C)/k)\subseteq\Br_{nr}(K(C)/K)

\tilde{\beta}

\Br(K)
(
[
8
, Proposition 6.2.1]
). Let
\beta\in\Br(K)
which maps to
\tilde{\beta}

Let

Let

Let

Then there exists a discrete valuation

is the unique quadratic extension of

Suppose that

by a symmetric matrix

\mu_{2n+2}

\sigma\in\Gamma_{k}
.

.
The map

is a 1-cocycle with values in

\tilde{Q}_{1}
and
\tilde{Q}_{2}

of the quadratic forms
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
with
det
(\tilde{Q}_{1})=
det
(Q_{1})
and det
(\tilde{Q}_{2})=
det
(Q_{2})
. Let
\tilde{Q}=t\tilde{Q}_{1}-\tilde{Q}_{2}
.

There is an isomorphism

\psi:Q_{k^{s}}\to\tilde{Q}_{k^{s}}

such that the associated cocycle

(\delta\sigma(\delta))^{-1}g(\sigma)^{-1}\alpha_{\sigma}
. Here
\sigma
stands for the

Q_{k^{s}}
as well
\tilde{Q}_{k^{s}}
.

the
twist
of
tQ_{1}-Q_{2}
with respect to the cocycle
\alpha
.

Case 2:
Suppose
[\epsilon_{\alpha}]\in H^{2}(k,\mu_{2n+2})

The map
\Gamma_{k}\to\mathbf{PSO}(Q_{1})

\sigma\mapsto
int
(\alpha_{\sigma})

\alpha_{\sigma}\in\mathbf{PGL}_{2n+2}(k^{s})

\phi:(M_{2n+2}(k^{s}),\tau_{Q_{1}})_{k^{s}}\to(A,\tau_{1})_{k^{s}}

(\alpha_{\sigma})=\phi^{-1}\sigma\phi\sigma^{-1}

Appendix A
A note on hyperelliptic curves and pencils of quadrics

S. Ramanan

A.1.
Introduction

There is a deep relationship between a hyper-elliptic curve of genus
g>0
,
and a pencil of quadrics in
\mathbb{C}^{2g+2}
. This goes back to Gauthier (
[
12
]
).
The space of
(g+1)
-dimensional (maximal) isotropic subspaces for
a non-singular quadric has two connected components. If the quadric
has rank 1 less, maximal isotropic subspaces are still
(g+1)
-dimensional but
the space of such subspaces is connected. Thus pairs consisting
of a quadric of the pencil (assuming it is generic) and a component of the space of maximal
isotropic subspaces in that quadric, constitute a 2-sheeted covering over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or what is the
same, a
hyper-elliptic curve. Hence it is not surprising that many constructions
over the hyper-elliptic curve have corresponding geometric interpretations
in terms of the pencil.

In particular, initially the Jacobian of the curve was described in terms of the
variety of
g
-dimensional isotropic spaces for all quadrics of the pencil.
Narasimhan and I in the case
g=2
, and Desale and I (
[
10
]
) in the general case, gave a similar
description for the moduli varieties of vector bundles of rank
2
over the curve, in terms of the geometry
of the pencil. Still later, I gave such a meaning (
[
27
]
) to the variety of isotropic subspaces of other dimensions
in terms of suitable moduli on the curve.

More recently, Manjul Bhargava
[
3
]
) considered similar properties over a number field.
I am giving below a proof of the results mentioned above in such a way that they
are valid for curves over any field of characteristic
\neq 2
.
This requires an assumption that the curve itself admits a rational point.
I have given full details of the construction in proving the isomorphism of the Jacobian with
the variety of
g
-dimensional isotropic subspaces for the pencil and have sketched how
similar considerations in the case of vector bundles of rank 2 are also valid over such fields.

Parimala Raman and Jaya Iyer have been studying Selmer groups and Hasse
principle etc. and wished for such a proof. I am thankful to Parimala for
encouraging me to re-visit what I had done half a century ago.

All considerations below are over any field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2
.

be a 2-dimensional vector space and

A.4.
Properties of a generic pencil

We wish to study properties of generic pencils of quadrics.
We recall first some well-known properties of a quadric defined by a quadratic form
Q
. If
V
is a vector space of dimension
n
,
and
Q
is a quadratic form on

, often we will denote the corresponding quadric in

also by

as a symmetric linear map

. The kernel of the above map is called the

of

A subspace

of

is

for

if the restriction of the quadratic form to

is zero. If

and

is non-degenerate or has nul-space of dimension 1, then the maximal isotropic subspaces have dimension

.
The subvariety of the Grassmannian of
(g+1)
-dimensional subspaces of

, consisting of isotropic subspaces,
has two irreducible components if

is non-degenerate and is itself irreducible if the nullspace of

is of dimension 1.
Orthogonal transformations of

act on the space of isotropic subspaces of fixed dimension

Q
is non-degenerate,
and

, an orthogonal transformation preserves each component if and only if its determinant is 1.

Let

be a projective line and

be a family of quadrics parametrised by

.
We call it a

of quadrics in

. We identify

with the field

Its determinant

(called the

) is a section of

that

is non-zero, which is the same as saying that the generic quadric of the pencil is non-degenerate.
If

has

zeros (in the algebraic closure) with multiplicity

.
The divisor in

defined by

will be denoted by

Those quadrics which correspond to the zeros of

are then singular quadrics with

of dimension 1 each, and all other quadrics are smooth.

If

and

are two singular quadrics in a generic pencil with nullspaces

and

respectively,
then

and

are orthogonal for both

and

and hence for all quadrics of the pencil.
Thus

is the direct sum of the nullspaces

of singular quadrics of
the pencil and therefore has a basis which is orthogonal for all the quadrics of the pencil.

We will now recall some properties of a generic pencil over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic

in a vector space

of even dimension, say

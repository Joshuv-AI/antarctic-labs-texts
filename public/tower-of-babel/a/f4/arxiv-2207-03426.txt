Generalized Minimizing Movements for the varifold Canham-Helfrich flow
Katharina Brazda, Martin Kružík, Ulisse Stefanelli

Abstract
. The gradient flow of the Canham-Helfrich functional is tackled via the Generalized Minimizing Movements approach. We prove the existence of solutions in Wasserstein spaces of varifolds, as well as upper and lower diameter bounds. In the more regular setting of multiply covered C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} surfaces, we provide a Li-Yau-type estimate for the Canham-Helfrich energy and prove the conservation of multiplicity along the evolution.

1.
Introduction

Minimizers of the
Canham-Helfrich energy

E_{\mathrm{CH}}(M)=\int_{M}\Big(\frac{\beta}{2}(H-H_{0})^{2}+\gamma K\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}

(1.1)

can be seen as models for the equilibrium shapes of single-phase biological membranes

[
14
,
29
]
.
The membrane is represented by the closed, orientable
C^{2}
surface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
H
and
K
respectively denoting its mean and Gauss curvature. The material properties of the membrane material are encoded in the bending rigidities
\beta>0
,
\gamma<0
and in the spontaneous curvature
H_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and minimization is usually performed under area and enclosed-volume constraint.

We are interested in studying the
gradient flow dynamics
associated to the Canham-Helfrich energy
E_{\mathrm{CH}}
. This can be expected to schematically describe the dissipative evolution of single-phase biomembranes in a viscous environment, possibly up to convergence to equilibrium over time. Our aim is to tackle such evolution in the weak setting of varifolds, building on the recent varifold approach to the minimization of the Canham-Helfrich energy in
[
11
]
.
Compared with stronger approaches, working with varifolds allows us to overcome the bottleneck represented by possible singularity formation and to obtain a global existence theory. The price to pay for this is the weakness of the evolution notion, as we resort in considering variational solutions in
De Giorgi’s

Generalized Minimizing Movements
(GMMs) sense
[
2
,
3
,
18
]
.

The case of axisymmetric surfaces is considered by

[

15

[

]

In the weak ambient approach of oriented varifolds one has to record the result by

[

]

[

]

[

In the evolutionary case, local existence results for the classical

The focus of this work is on the Generalized-Minimizing-Movements approach to the Canham-Helfrich functional (
1.1
) in the varifold setting, where it is reformulated as
G_{\mathrm{CH}}
, see
[
11
]
. The very weak varifold setting is instrumental for proving the existence of equilibria without a priori structural assumptions on the minimizers. In particular, it is well-suited to handle the possible onset of singularities while evolving far from stationary points.

The GMM evolution notion rests on a limit passage in time-discrete approximations
V^{n}
, which are obtained from some given initial state
V^{0}
and time step
\tau>0
via the successive in
n\in\mathbb{N}
minimization of the incremental functional

V\mapsto G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)+\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{p}^{2}(V,V^{n-1}),

see (
4.3
). The GMMs
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto V(t)
are then pointwise limits of subsequences of (piecewise constant in time interpolants) of time-discrete approximations
V^{n}
as
\tau\to 0
, see Definition
4.1
. Note that the incremental functional features the interplay of energy minimization and distance-control from the previous discrete state
V^{n-1}
. In particular, we consider here the Wasserstein distance
W_{p}
with
p\in[1,\infty)
between the two varifolds
V
and
V^{n-1}
, seen as Radon measures with equal mass. This point distinguishes our analysis from previous contributions, where
L^{2}
gradient flows on different parametrization settings are considered instead.

The plan of the paper is as follows.
After providing preliminaries in Section
2
and recalling some material on the stationary case in Section
3
, we prove global existence of GMMs for
G_{\mathrm{CH}}
in Section
4
. Contrary to previous results on Canham-Helfrich flow, no smallness condition on the energy of the initial state is required (Theorem
4.2
). GMMs are absolutely continuous curves with respect to the metric
W_{p}
and the Canham-Helfrich energy does not increase along the evolution. This entails that GMMs satisfy lower and upper diameter bounds (Proposition
4.4
).
Moreover, we prove that the flow can be constrained to fulfill additional properties, and still admit the existence of a GMM (Corollary
4.3
). This allows us to consider an enclosed-volume constraint or to reduce to reflection- or axisymmetric varifolds. In the same spirit, evolution can be constrained to multiply covered surfaces as well, as long as uniform
C^{1,1}
regularity is enforced as in
[
25
]
, see also
[
34
]
. We discuss this more regular case in Section
5
, where we prove that GMMs exist for any given genus and that the multiplicity of the initial surface is conserved along the flow (Theorem
5.6
). The minimality (hence stationarity) of multiply covered spheres is analyzed in Subsection
5.2
. Moreover, we present an a-priori bound on the multiplicity in terms of the Canham-Helfrich energy (Proposition
5.4
). Such Li-Yau-type bound can be compared with the recent one from
[
52
]
where however no Gauss term is considered, i.e.,
\gamma=0
. Eventually, we discuss in Subsection
5.6
the possibility of weakening the metric
W_{p}
and consider instead the Wasserstein distance between the surfaces, here seen as restricted two-dimensional Hausdorff
\mathcal{H}^{2}
measures in space. This choice still allows for existence of GMMs of multiply covered surfaces, as long as the multiplicity is conserved. We conclude by briefly addressing more general curvature functionals than the Canham-Helfrich energy.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

2.1.
Measures and perimeter

Let
X
be a locally compact and separable metric space and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The dual space
\mathcal{M}^{N}(X):=C_{c}(X;\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
of compactly supported vector functions on
X
is the space of vector-valued
Radon measures
on
X
having
N
components. It is normed by the total variation
\|\cdot\|_{{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}}(X)
and equipped with the
weak-
\ast
topology
:

\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}\mu
(
n\to\infty
) if
\int_{X}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\to\int_{X}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for all
\varphi\in C_{c}(X;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
The
support
of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{N}(X)
, denoted by
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
, is the closed set of points
x\in X
such that
\|\mu\|_{{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}}(U)>0
for all neighborhoods
U
of
x
. The
restriction
of
\mu
to
B\subset X
is the measure
\mu\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}B
given by
(\mu\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}B)(A):=\mu(A\cap B)
for all Borel sets
A\subset X
. For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{N}(X)
and a
\mu
-measurable map
f\colon X\to Y
, where
Y
is another locally compact and separable metric space, the
pushforward measure
of
\mu
by
f
is defined by
(f_{\sharp}\mu)(A):=\mu(f^{-1}(A))
for all Borel sets
A\subset Y
. If
f
is continuous and such that
f^{-1}(B)
is compact for all compact
B\subset Y
, then
f_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{N}(Y)
. In this case,
\mathrm{supp}(f_{\sharp}\mu)=f(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
and
\int_{Y}g(y)\,\mathrm{d}(f_{\sharp}\mu)(y)=\int_{X}(g\circ f)(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
hold for all measurable
g\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We omit
N
from the notation if
N=1
. The scalar Radon measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
is called
positive
if
\mu(A)\geq 0
for every Borel set
A\subset X
. In this case,
\mu=\|\mu\|_{{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
,
L^{p}_{\mu}(X;\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the Lebesgue space of all
\mu
-measurable functions
v\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\|v\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}(X)}:=(\int_{X}|v(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x))^{1/p}<\infty
. If
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu
is the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, we simply write
\,\mathrm{d}x
instead of
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Lebesgue measurable set. We say that
E
is of
finite perimeter

{\rm Per}(E)
, if there exists a Radon measure
\mu^{E}\in\mathcal{M}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\int_{E}(\!\,\mathrm{div}\,\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{E}
for every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
{\rm Per}(E):=\|\mu^{E}\|_{{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})<\infty
. The
relative perimeter
of
E
in a Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
{\rm Per}(E;A):=\|\mu^{E}\|_{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}(A)
.
By definition,
\mu^{E}=-\mathrm{D}1_{E}
, where
\mathrm{D}
stands for the weak derivative and
1_{E}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\{0,1\}
is the characteristic function of
E
.
With
B_{r}(x)
we indicate the open ball
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\>|x-y|<r\}
centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and of radius
r>0
. The set of all points
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu^{E})
such that
\nu(x):=\lim_{r\to 0}\mu^{E}(B_{r}(x))/|\mu^{E}(B_{r}(x))|
exists and satisfies
\nu(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is called the
reduced boundary
of
E
, denoted by
\partial^{*}E
. The field
\nu
defined in this way is called the (measure-theoretic outer unit)
normal
to
E
.

2.2.
Varifolds and currents

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
0\leq m\leq d
(note however that we will only need the case
d=3
and
m=2
later on). The
Grassmannian

G_{m,d}
is the set of all
m
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Elements of
G_{m,d}
are identified with the corresponding orthogonal projections
P\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
on the
m
-dimensional subspaces. The
oriented Grassmannian
,
G_{m,d}^{o}
, is the set of all oriented
m
-dimensional linear subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Its elements are represented by
m
-vectors
\xi
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

An
m
-varifold
(later
varifold
, for short) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a positive Radon measure

V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}).

Similarly, an
oriented
m
-varifold
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined on the oriented Grassmannian, i.e.,
V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}^{o})
. The
mass
of the varifold
V
(either oriented or not) is the positive Radon measure
\mu_{V}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by

\mu_{V}(\Omega):=V(\Omega\times G_{m,d})

for all Borel sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We introduce the

two-fold covering map

q\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}^{o}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}
given by
q(x,\pm\xi)=(x,P)
, where
P
is the projection map onto the linear subspace spanned by
\pm\xi
.
Then, to any oriented varifold
V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}^{o})
, we can associate a varifold
q_{\sharp}V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d})
given by the push-forward of
V
under
q
, i.e., for all
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d})
,

(q_{\sharp}V)(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}}\varphi(x,P)\,\mathrm{d}(q_{\sharp}V)(x,P)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}^{o}}\varphi(q(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\xi).

In addition,
V
gives rise to the
m
-current

T_{V}\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined by

T_{V}(\omega):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}^{o}}\langle\omega(x),\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\xi)

for all
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the smooth, compactly supported
m
-forms in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Recall that a
general
m
-current
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted by
T\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is an element of the dual space to
\mathcal{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Its
mass
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by the dual norm

\mathbb{M}_{T}(\Omega):=\sup\{T(\omega):\,\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\mathrm{supp}(\omega)\subset\Omega,\,\|\omega\|_{\infty}\leq 1\},

where
\|\omega\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\omega(x)|
. If
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
we simply write
\mathbb{M}_{T}
.
The
boundary

\partial T
of
T\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the
(m-1)
-current given by
\partial T(\eta):=T(\!\,\mathrm{d}\eta)
for all
\eta\in\mathcal{D}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

A set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
0\leq m\leq d
) is called
m
-rectifiable
(see, e.g.,
[
53
]
) if its
m
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{m}(M)
is finite and if, up to a
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-zero set
M_{0}
, it is contained in a countable union of disjoint images
M_{i}
of Lipschitz maps from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,

\textstyle{M\subset\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}M_{i}\right)\cup M_{0}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{m}(M_{0})=0.

If
M
is
m
-rectifiable, then its cone of
approximate tangent vectors

[
49
, p. 7]

is an
m
-dimensional plane for
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-almost all
x\in M
, which we will denote by
T_{x}M
as in the smooth setting. An
orientation

\xi^{M}
of an
m
-rectifiable set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable choice of orientation for each tangent space
T_{x}M
. If
T_{x}M
is spanned by
(\tau_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{m}
with
\tau_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then, for a.e.
x\in M
, one has the two choices

\pm\xi^{M}(x):=\pm\tau_{1}(x)\wedge\cdots\wedge\tau_{m}(x)=(T_{x}M)^{\pm}\in G_{m,d}^{o}
.
If
m=d-1
, we can identify the orientation
\xi^{M}(x)
with the unit normal vector
\nu^{M}(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. In particular, in the case
m=2
and
d=3
, which is the relevant one in our case, we will apply the covering map in the form
q\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times G_{2,3}
,
q(x,\pm\nu)=(x,P)
, where
P\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is the projection map onto the plane orthogonal to
\pm\nu
.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
m
-rectifiable with orientation
\xi^{M}
. A
rectifiable
m
-current
T\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with integer multiplicity

\theta\in L^{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{Z})
is defined as

T(\omega)=\int_{M}\langle\omega(x),\xi^{M}(x)\rangle\,\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)

for all
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This current will be denoted by
T=T[M,\xi^{M},\theta]
to emphasize that it is determined by the triple
M,\xi^{M},\theta
. Its mass corresponds to

\mathbb{M}_{T}=\int_{M}|\theta(x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)
.
A rectifiable current
T
with
\mathbb{M}_{T}<\infty
is called an
integral current
, if in addition
\mathbb{M}_{\partial T}<\infty
. If an integral current is supported on a smooth submanifold and its boundary is supported on the boundary of this submanifold, then the current must have constant multiplicity. This fact is known as
Federer’s Constancy Theorem
,
which actually also holds in the more general case of Lipschitz submanifolds

[
49
, Cor. 1.54]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Constancy,
[
27
, Sec. 4.1.31]
)
.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an
m
-dimensional, connected
C^{1}
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with boundary and assume that
M
is oriented by
\xi^{M}\in C(M;G_{m,d}^{o})
. If an integral current
T\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

\mathrm{supp}(T)\subset M
and
\mathrm{supp}(\partial T)\subset\partial M
,
then
T
has a constant multiplicity
\theta=c\in\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.,

T(\omega)=c\int_{M}\langle\omega,\xi^{M}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}

for all
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
m
-rectifiable and
\theta\in L^{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{N})
. An
integral varifold

V=V[M,\theta]
is a varifold defined for all
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d})
by the formula

V(\varphi)=\int_{M}\varphi(x,T_{x}M)\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}(x).

Equivalently, we will also write

\,\mathrm{d}V(x,P)=\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)\otimes\delta_{T_{x}M}(P)
.
The set of all integral
m
-varifolds on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
IV_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
The mass of
V=V[M,\theta]
corresponds to

\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\int_{M}\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)
.
If
\xi^{M}
is an orientation of
M
and
\theta^{\pm}\in L^{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{N}_{0})
, then an oriented varifold
V
defined for all
\psi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}^{o})
by the formula

V(\psi)=\int_{M}\left(\theta^{+}(x)\psi(x,\xi^{M}(x))+\theta^{-}(x)\psi(x,-\xi^{M}(x))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)

is called an
oriented integral varifold
and is denoted by
V=V[M,\xi^{M},\theta^{+},\theta^{-}]\in IV_{m}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We also write

\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\xi)=\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M)(x)\otimes(\theta^{+}(x)\delta_{\xi^{M}(x)}(\xi)+\theta^{-}(x)\delta_{-\xi^{M}(x)}(\xi))
.
The mass of
V
corresponds to

\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\int_{M}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)
.
If
V=V[M,\xi^{M},\theta^{+},\theta^{-}]\in IV_{m}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

q_{\sharp}V=q_{\sharp}V[M,\theta^{+}+\theta^{-}]\in IV_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{V}=T_{V}[M,\xi^{M},\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}]\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We say that
V\in IV_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
curvature varifold with boundary
and write
V\in AV_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if there exists
A^{V}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc},V}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})
and
\partial V\in\mathcal{M}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(P_{ij}\partial_{j}\varphi(x,P)+\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\partial_{P_{jk}}\varphi(x,P))\,A_{ijk}^{V}(x,P)+A_{jij}^{V}(x,P)\,\varphi(x,P)\right)\,\mathrm{d}V(x,P)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d}}\varphi(x,P)\,\mathrm{d}(\partial V)_{i}(x,P)

(2.1)

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d})
and
1\leq i\leq d
. The
curvature functions

A^{V}_{ijk}
(
1\leq i,j,k\leq d
) give rise to the
generalized second fundamental form

I\hskip-1.99997ptI^{V}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc},V}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times G_{m,d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})
via

(I\hskip-1.99997ptI^{V}(\cdot,P))_{ij}^{k}:=\sum_{l=1}^{d}A_{ikl}^{V}(\cdot,P)P_{lj}

and the vector-valued Radon measure
\partial V
is called the
boundary measure
. The set of
oriented curvature varifolds
,
AV_{m}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is given by all
V\in IV_{m}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
q_{\sharp}V\in AV_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.3.
Compactness

Let us recall the
compactness theorems
for oriented integral varifolds by Hutchinson
[
30
]
and for curvature varifolds with boundary by Mantegazza
[
43
]
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. The classes of varifolds defined on
\Omega
are denoted by
IV_{m}(\Omega)
,
IV_{m}^{o}(\Omega)
, and
AV_{m}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 2.2

(Compactness,

[
30
, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

The set

\left\{V\in IV^{o}_{m}(\Omega):\>\mu_{V}(\Omega)+\|\delta(q_{\sharp}V)\|_{{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}}(\Omega)+\mathbb{M}_{\partial T_{V}}(\Omega)<\infty\right\}

is compact in the weak-
\ast
topology of
\mathcal{M}(\Omega\times G_{m,d}^{o})
.

Here,
\delta(q_{\sharp}V)\colon C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
stands for the first variation
[
1
]
of the varifold
q_{\sharp}V
, which can be estimated in terms of mass, curvature, and boundary
[
11
, Lemma
2.3]
,

\|\delta(q_{\sharp}V)\|_{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}(\Omega)\leq\sqrt{d\,\mu_{V}(\Omega)}\,\|A^{q_{\sharp}V}\|_{L^{2}_{q_{\sharp}V}(\Omega\times G_{m,d})}+\|\partial(q_{\sharp}V)\|_{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}(\Omega\times G_{m,d}).

The
curvature varifold convergence

V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
in
AV_{m}(\Omega)
(
n\to\infty
) is defined as the
measure-function pairs convergence

(V_{n},A^{V_{n}})\rightharpoonup^{\ast}(V,A^{V})
:
V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega\times G_{m,d})
and
A^{V_{n}}V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}A^{V}V
in
\mathcal{M}^{d\times d\times d}(\Omega\times G_{m,d})
, cf.
[
30
]
. In particular, if
V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
in
AV_{m}(\Omega)
then, by definition (
2.1
),
\partial V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}\partial V
in
\mathcal{M}^{d}(\Omega\times G_{m,d})
.

Theorem 2.3

(Compactness,

[
43
, Thm. 6.1]
)
.

The set

\left\{V\in AV_{m}(\Omega):\>\mu_{V}(\Omega)+\|A^{V}\|^{2}_{L_{V}^{2}(\Omega\times G_{m,d})}+\|\partial V\|_{{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}}(\Omega\times G_{m,d})<\infty\right\}

is compact with respect to curvature varifold convergence.

2.4.
Wasserstein space

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a complete separable metric space and

\mathcal{P}(X):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X):\mu(X)=m_{0}\}

indicate the set of positive Radon measures with fixed mass
m_{0}>0
.
For
p\in[1,\infty)
, let

\mathcal{P}_{p}(X):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X):\>\int_{X}d_{X}^{p}(x,x_{0})\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty\}

for some
x_{0}\in X
denote its subspace of measures with
finite
p
-th moment
and recall that
\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)=\mathcal{P}(X)
for all
p
if
X
is compact. The
p
-
Wasserstein distance
between
\mu,\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
is defined by

W_{p}(\mu,\tilde{\mu}):=\left(\min_{\lambda\in\Pi(\mu,\tilde{\mu})}\int_{X\times X}d_{X}^{p}(x,\tilde{x})\,\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x,\tilde{x})\right)^{1/p},

where

\Pi(\mu,\tilde{\mu}):=\left\{\lambda\in\mathcal{M}(X\times X):\>(\pi_{1})_{\sharp}\lambda=\mu,\>(\pi_{2})_{\sharp}\lambda=\tilde{\mu}\right\}

is the set of all couplings with marginals
\mu
and
\tilde{\mu}
. The metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X),W_{p})
is called the
p
-
Wasserstein space
on
(X,d_{X})
.
If
\mu,\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(X)
and have bounded support, then by
[
10
, Thm. 8.10.45]
and
[
3
, (7.1.2)]
,

W_{1}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})=\sup\left\{{\int_{X}f\,\,\mathrm{d}(\mu-\tilde{\mu}):\>f\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X)}\right\},

which is known as the
Kantorovich-Rubinshtein distance
(or
modified bounded Lipschitz distance
). Here,
\mathrm{Lip}_{1}
stands for Lipschitz continuous functions with unit Lipschitz constant. This representation renders the
W_{1}
distance particularly useful for applications, see
[
12
,
13
]
.

The metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X),W_{p})
is complete and separable
[
3
, Prop. 7.1.5]
. Moreover,
W_{p}
generates the weak-
\ast
topology on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
: If
(\mu_{n})\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
, and
n\to\infty
, then

W_{p}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0\>\>\Longleftrightarrow\>\>\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}\mu\>\>\text{and}\>\>\text{$\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu\>\>$ for $f\in C(X)$ with $p$-growth,}

i.e.,
|f|\leq c(1+d_{X}^{p}(\cdot,x_{0}))
for some
x_{0}\in X
and
c>0
.

3.
Canham-Helfrich functional

We devote this section to recalling some results for the stationary case. In particular, by restricting to
d=3
and
m=2
we follow
[
11
]
and introduce the generalization of the classical Canham-Helfrich energy (
1.1
) to oriented curvature two-varifolds

F_{\mathrm{CH}}\colon AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}f_{\mathrm{CH}}\big(\nu,A^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,\nu))\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\nu),

(3.1)

where the integrand
f_{\mathrm{CH}}\colon\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3\times 3}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

{f_{\mathrm{CH}}(\nu,A):=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{jij}-\nu_{i}H_{0}\Big)^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{jij}\Big)^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{4}\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}A_{ijk}^{2}\right).}

The form of the integrand in (
3.1
) can be deduced from the identity
(H-H_{0})^{2}=|\overline{H}-\nu H_{0}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\overline{H}_{i}-\nu_{i}H_{0})^{2}
by replacing the mean curvature vector
\overline{H}=H\nu
by its
varifold analogue

\textstyle{\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{jij}^{q_{\sharp}V}\right)_{i=1}^{3}}.

Note that the squared norm of the second fundamental form of a curvature varifold is given by

|I\hskip-1.99997ptI^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|A^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}(A_{ijk}^{q_{\sharp}V})^{2},

which allows us to define the
generalized Gauss curvature

K^{q_{\sharp}V}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}-|I\hskip-1.99997ptI^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|A^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}.

(3.2)

Since
q(x,\nu)=(x,P)=q(x,-\nu)
, it follows that
(x,\nu)\mapsto A^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,\nu))
does not depend on the sign of
\nu
and reversing orientation has the sole effect of changing the sign in front of
H_{0}
.

We identify the
area
of an oriented varifold
V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
with its mass, namely,
\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=V(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. For
m_{0}>0
fixed, at the stationary level we are interested in varifolds
V\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
minimizing
F_{\mathrm{CH}}
under the mass constraint

\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=m_{0}.

(3.3)

3.1.
Varifold minimizers

Existence of varifold minimizers follows by the Direct Method, exploiting compactness results for oriented and curvature varifolds (see Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
) together with the lower semicontinuity
[
11
, Thm. 3.2]
,

F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V_{n})\quad\text{for}\quad V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V\>\>(n\to\infty)\quad\text{in}\quad AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

where
V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
is the curvature varifold convergence, namely,
V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
(q_{\sharp}V_{n},A^{q_{\sharp}V_{n}})\rightharpoonup^{\ast}(q_{\sharp}V,A^{q_{\sharp}V})
as measure-function pairs. A key ingredient in the mentioned lower-semicontinuity proof is the convexity of the integrand
f_{\mathrm{CH}}(\nu,.)
, which holds under the following condition on the parameters:

-{\frac{6}{5}}\,\beta<\gamma<0.

(3.4)

In fact, such convexity gives rise to the
curvature bound

[
11
, Prop. 3.1]

\|A^{q_{\sharp}V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{q_{\sharp}V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times G_{2,3})}\leq c_{1}\left(F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)+c_{2}\,\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right)

(3.5)

for all
V\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, with constants
c_{1}>0
,
c_{2}\geq 0
depending on data (in particular,
c_{2}=0
if
H_{0}=0
). An early consequence of (
3.5
) is that the Canham-Helfrich energy
F_{\mathrm{CH}}
is bounded from below in terms of the mass.

3.2.
Lower bound in terms of the Willmore energy

A special instance of the classical Canham-Helfrich energy is the
Willmore energy

\mathcal{W}(M):=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2},

corresponding indeed to the choice
H_{0}=0
,
\beta=1/2
, and
\gamma=0
in (
1.1
). If
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an immersed closed surface one has
\mathcal{W}(M)\geq 4\pi
with equality only for the sphere
M=\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}
of any radius
R>0

[
57
]
.

Without changing notation, one can define the Willmore energy of an (oriented) curvature varifold
V\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
as

\mathcal{W}(V):=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times G_{2,3}}|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}(x,P)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}(q_{\sharp}V)(x,P)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,\nu))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\nu),

which for
V=V[M,\nu^{M},\theta^{+},\theta^{-}]
reduces to

\mathcal{W}(V)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{V}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}.

(3.6)

The latter controls the multiplicity of the varifold
q_{\sharp}V=q_{\sharp}V[M,\theta^{+}+\theta^{-}]
via the
Li-Yau inequality

[
35
]
(see
[
39
]
for its generalization to integral varifolds),

\mathcal{W}(V)\geq 4\pi(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-}).

(3.7)

The algebraic estimate

|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}A^{q_{\sharp}V}_{jij}\right)^{2}\leq 3\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}\left(A^{q_{\sharp}V}_{ijk}\right)^{2}=3|A^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}
,
cf.
[
11
, Lemma 2.3]
, entails
4\mathcal{W}(V)\leq 3\|A^{q_{\sharp}V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{q_{\sharp}V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times G_{2,3})}
. From this, under assumption (
3.4
), the curvature bounds (
3.5
) imply the control

4\mathcal{W}(V)\leq 3\,c_{1}\left(F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)+c_{2}\,\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right),

(3.8)

which, in combination with (
3.7
), in turn gives a multiplicity bound in terms of the Canham-Helfrich energy, i.e., a Li-Yau-type inequality.
Let us recall again that Li-Yau inequalities for the Canham-Helfrich functional have been recently obtained in
[
52
]
, where, however, the Gauss term is not considered.

In the following, we derive an alternative estimate in terms of the Willmore energy with explicit constants.
Recall
that
V=V[M,\nu^{M},\theta^{+},\theta^{-}]
is given by

\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\nu)=\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{H}^{2}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M)(x)\otimes(\theta^{+}(x)\delta_{\nu^{M}(x)}(\nu)+\theta^{-}(x)\delta_{-\nu^{M}(x)}(\nu)),

(3.9)

with an
\mathcal{H}^{2}
-measurable, countably rectifiable
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with orientation
\pm\nu^{M}(x)
(corresponding to
(T_{x}M)^{\pm}
) and locally integrable multiplicities
\theta^{\pm}(x)\in\mathbb{N}
for
(\mathcal{H}^{2}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M)
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Moreover, if
V
is an (oriented) curvature varifold, i.e.,
V\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
, then the energy (
3.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\left|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,\nu))-\nu H_{0}\right|^{2}+\gamma\,K^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,\nu))\right)\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\nu)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Bigg(\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\left|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,\nu^{M}(x)))-\nu^{M}(x)H_{0}\right|^{2}+\gamma\,K^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,\nu^{M}(x)))\right)\theta^{+}(x)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\left|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,-\nu^{M}(x)))+\nu^{M}(x)H_{0}\right|^{2}+\gamma\,K^{q_{\sharp}V}(q(x,-\nu^{M}(x)))\right)\theta^{-}(x)\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}(x).

Taking into account that
q(x,-\nu^{M})=q(x,\nu^{M})
,

|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}\mp\nu^{M}H_{0}|^{2}=|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}\mp 2\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}\cdot\nu^{M}H_{0}+|\nu^{M}|^{2}H_{0}^{2}
,
and
|\nu^{M}|^{2}=1
, this reduces to

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\left|\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}\right|^{2}+H_{0}^{2}\right)+\gamma\,K^{q_{\sharp}V}\right)(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\beta\,H_{0}\int_{M}\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}\cdot\nu^{M}(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2},

(3.10)

where the argument
q(\cdot,\nu^{M}(\cdot))
of
\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}
and
K^{q_{\sharp}V}
has been omitted for conciseness. This representation of the Canham-Helfrich energy allows deducing
another lower bound of
F_{\mathrm{CH}}
in terms of the Willmore energy.

Lemma 3.1

(Lower bound for the energy)
.

Let
\beta>0
and
\gamma
,
H_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for varifolds
V=V[M,\nu^{M},\theta^{+},\theta^{-}]\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with mass
\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=m_{0}>0
,

F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)\geq 2\beta\Big({\textstyle{\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{W}(V)}{m_{0}}}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}}}\Big)^{2}m_{0}+\gamma\int_{M}K^{q_{\sharp}V}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}.

(3.11)

Proof.

Along the proof, we use the short-hand notation
H=\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}\cdot\nu^{M}
and omit everywhere the superscript
(\cdot)^{q_{\sharp}V}
for brevity. Moving from (
3.10
) we get

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\left(H^{2}+H_{0}^{2}\right)+\gamma K\right)(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}-\beta H_{0}\int_{M}H(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\beta\left(\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}H^{2}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}-\frac{H_{0}}{2}\int_{M}H(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{4}m_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\int_{M}K(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2},

where we used the mass constraint
\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\int_{M}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=m_{0}
(
3.3
) for the second equality. As
\mathcal{W}(V)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}H^{2}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}
from (
3.6
) and
\theta^{+},\theta^{-}\geq 0
, the second term in the right-hand side can be estimated as

\displaystyle\frac{H_{0}}{2}\int_{M}H(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq|H_{0}|\int_{M}\frac{|H|}{2}|\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq|H_{0}|\int_{M}\frac{|H|}{2}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq|H_{0}|\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}H^{2}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}}\sqrt{\int_{M}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}}=|H_{0}|\sqrt{\mathcal{W}(V)}\sqrt{m_{0}}\,.

This gives the lower bound

F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)\geq 2\beta\left(\mathcal{W}(V)-|H_{0}|\sqrt{\mathcal{W}(V)}\sqrt{m_{0}}+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{4}m_{0}\right)+\>\gamma\int_{M}K(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2},

where the first term can be rewritten as

\textstyle{\mathcal{W}(V)-|H_{0}|\sqrt{\mathcal{W}(V)}\sqrt{m_{0}}+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{4}m_{0}=\Big(\sqrt{\mathcal{W}(V)}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}\sqrt{m_{0}}\Big)^{2}=\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{W}(V)}{m_{0}}}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}\Big)^{2}m_{0}}\,,

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Varifold setting

We now turn to the evolutionary case and consider the gradient flow of the Canham-Helfrich energy in the setting of curvature varifolds as in
[
11
]
. In Subsection
4.1
, we show global existence of a solution using the GMM approach. We discuss restricted flows in Subsection
4.2
and, finally, provide diameter bounds in Subsection
4.3
.

To ease notations, the evolution will first be constrained to take place in a given compact set
Q\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
By fixing the mass
m_{0}>0
we let

\mathcal{V}:=\left\{V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}):\>\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=V(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})=m_{0},\>{\rm\mathrm{supp}}(V)\subset Q\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\right\},

(4.1)

where we recall that
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
indicates the set of Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We prescribe the class
\mathcal{A}
of
admissible varifolds
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\left\{V\in\mathcal{V}:\>V\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\>\partial(q_{\sharp}V)=0,\>\partial T_{V}=0\right\}.

(4.2)

The class
\mathcal{A}
is closed in
AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with respect to curvature varifold convergence. Indeed, let
V_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
and
V\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be such that
V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
in
AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
n\to\infty
. One readily gets that
\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=m_{0}
,
{\rm supp}(\mu_{V})\subset Q
. Moreover, curvature varifold convergence implies that
\partial(q_{\sharp}V_{n})\rightharpoonup^{\ast}\partial(q_{\sharp}V)
in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times G_{2,3})
, which with
\partial(q_{\sharp}V_{n})=0
gives the condition
\partial(q_{\sharp}V)=0
. As shown in
[
11
, Lemma 4.1; Eq. (2.4)]
, the limit
V
also
satisfies
\partial T_{V}=0
.

Let us note that the requirement
\partial T_{V}=0
in
\mathcal{A}
could be relaxed to
\mathbb{M}_{\partial T_{V}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\leq m^{c}
for some fixed
m^{c}\geq 0
, not affecting the analysis. We restrict ourselves to the choice
m^{c}=0
, however, for it is well-adapted to the modeling of single-phase membranes without boundary. Moreover, it simplifies the notation and it will be useful in the case of multiply covered smooth surfaces (Section
5
), where we can take advantage of Federer’s Constancy Theorem

2.1
.

In order to enforce the evolution in
\mathcal{A}
, we additionally constrain the energy by defining

{G_{\mathrm{CH}}}\colon\mathcal{V}\to(-\infty,\infty],\quad{G_{\mathrm{CH}}}(V):=\begin{cases}F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V),\quad V\in{\mathcal{A}}\\
\infty,\qquad\quad\>\>\text{else.}\end{cases}

We now endow
\mathcal{V}
with the Wasserstein metric
W_{p}
. To this aim, we fix
p\in[1,\infty)
and follow the construction explained in Subsection
2.4
with
(X,d_{X})=(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},d)
where
d
is the metric defined as

d((x,\nu),(\tilde{x},\tilde{\nu})):=|x-\tilde{x}|+|\nu-\tilde{\nu}|

(other equivalent metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
could be considered as well). Note that
W_{p}(V,\tilde{V})<\infty
for all
V,\,\tilde{V}\in\mathcal{V}
, for the supports of
\mu_{V}
and
\mu_{\tilde{V}}
are contained in the bounded set
Q
. We have that

W_{p}^{p}(V,\tilde{V})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\min_{\lambda\in\Pi(V,\tilde{V})}\displaystyle\int_{(Q\times\mathbb{S}^{2})^{2}}(|x-\tilde{x}|+|\nu-\tilde{\nu}|)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda((x,\nu),(\tilde{x},\tilde{\nu}))&\quad\text{for}\ p>1,\\[17.07164pt]
\sup\left\{{\displaystyle\int_{Q\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}f\,\,\mathrm{d}(V-\tilde{V}):\>f\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(Q\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right\}&\quad\text{for}\ p=1.\end{array}\right.

The actual choice of
p
is irrelevant for the analysis and can be possibly adjusted to different modeling or computational needs.

Note that the Wasserstein metric
W_{p}(V,\tilde{V})
controls the distance of the varifolds
V
and
\tilde{V}
both in space position and in orientation. Such strong control is necessary, for two varifolds sharing the same support in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
could still differ in their orientation. In the more regular case of multiply covered smooth surfaces, we will be able to consider a weaker metric as well, featuring just the Wasserstein distance of the supports of
\mu_{V}
and
\mu_{\tilde{V}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, see Section
5
.

4.1.
Generalized Minimizing Movements

We are now ready to introduce our concept of evolution. Given an
initial state

V^{0}\in\mathcal{V}
with
G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})<\infty
and a time step
\tau>0
, we recursively define
discrete minimizers

(V^{n}_{\tau})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by

V^{0}_{\tau}:=V^{0}
and
V^{n}_{\tau}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathcal{V}}\,G_{\mathrm{CH},\tau}(\cdot;V^{n-1}_{\tau})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
G_{\mathrm{CH},\tau}(\cdot;V^{n-1}_{\tau})\colon\mathcal{V}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is the
incremental functional

G_{\mathrm{CH},\tau}(V;V^{n-1}_{\tau}):=G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)+\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{p}^{2}(V,V^{n-1}_{\tau}).

(4.3)

Define
\overline{V}_{\tau}\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{V}
to be the piecewise constant interpolant given by
\overline{V}_{\tau}(0):=V^{0}
and
\overline{V}_{\tau}(t):=V_{\tau}^{n}
for
t\in((n-1)\tau,n\tau]
. The choice of the power
2
in the Wasserstein term above corresponds to the case of
gradient flows
. Replacing
W_{p}^{2}(V,V^{n-1}_{\tau})
by
W_{p}^{p}(V,V^{n-1}_{\tau})
would require just minor notational adaptations and would correspond to the case of
doubly nonlinear flows
instead
[
3
]
.

We define our evolution notion as follows, see
[
3
, Def. 2.0.6]
.

Definition 4.1

(Generalized Minimizing Movements)
.

A curve
V\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{V}
is called a Generalized Minimizing Movement (GMM) for the varifold Canham-Helfrich flow if there exists a sequence
\tau_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, such that
\overline{V}_{\tau_{m}}(t)\to V(t)
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
.

A curve
V\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{V}
is said to be
locally
L^{2}
-absolutely continuous
if there exists
w\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty)
such that

W_{p}(V(t_{1}),V(t_{2}))\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}w(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

(4.4)

for all
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\infty
. In this case, we write
V\in AC^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathcal{V})
. Given a locally
L^{2}
-absolutely continuous curve
V
, the
metric derivative

|V^{\prime}|(t):=\lim_{s\to t}\frac{W_{p}(V(s),V(t))}{|s-t|}

can be proved to exists for almost every
t\geq 0

[
3
, Ch. 1]
. The function
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto|V^{\prime}|(t)
belongs to
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty)
and is minimal among the functions
w
fulfilling (
4.4
).

We start by recording a first existence result.

Theorem 4.2

(Existence of GMMs)
.

Assume (
3.4
) and let
V^{0}\in\mathcal{A}
with
G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})<\infty
. Then, there exists a Generalized Minimizing Movement
V\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}([0,\infty);\mathcal{V})
associated to
G_{\mathrm{CH}}
starting from
V(0)=V^{0}
. Moreover,
G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V(t))\leq G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})
and
V(t)\in\mathcal{A}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

The functional
G_{\mathrm{CH}}\colon\mathcal{V}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is defined on the complete metric space
(\mathcal{V},W_{p})
and has domain
\mathcal{A}
. By assuming (
3.4
), namely
-6\beta/5<\gamma<0
, the Canham-Helfrich integrand
f_{\mathrm{CH}}(\nu,\cdot)
is strictly convex for all
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and the curvature bound (
3.5
)

\|A^{q_{\sharp}V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{q_{\sharp}V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times G_{2,3})}\leq c_{1}\left(G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)+c_{2}\,\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right)

holds. Owing to the fact that the
W_{p}
-metric topology and the weak-
\ast
topology of varifolds are equivalent, we deduce from
[
11
, Thm. 3.2]
that
G_{\mathrm{CH}}
is lower semicontinuous in
(\mathcal{V},W_{p})
. On the other hand, Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
ensure that the sublevel sets of
G_{\mathrm{CH}}
are compact. We are hence in the setting of
[
3
, Prop. 2.2.3]
and the existence of a GMM
V\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}([0,\infty);\mathcal{V})
ensues for all
V^{0}\in\mathcal{A}
with
G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})<\infty
.
Moreover, the solution
V
satisfies the energy inequality

G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V(t))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|V^{\prime}|^{2}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})

for all
t\geq 0
.
In particular, we have
G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V(t))\leq G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})<\infty
, which in turn entails that
V(t)\in\mathcal{A}
.
∎

By their definition, GMMs starting from a minimizer
V^{0}
of the Canham-Helfrich functional are stationary, i.e.,
V(t)=V^{0}
for all
t\geq 0
whenever
V^{0}\in\text{argmin}_{\mathcal{V}}\,G_{\mathrm{CH}}
.

4.2.
Restricted flows

In the following, we will be interested in considering GMMs in more restrictive settings. This can be easily accommodated within the above theory by simply constraining the functional
G_{\mathrm{CH}}
to a closed subset of the admissible class
\mathcal{A}
. More precisely, we have the following corollary to Theorem
4.2
.

Corollary 4.3

(Existence of restricted GMMs)
.

Assume (
3.4
) and let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\subset\mathcal{A}
be closed with respect to the weak-
\ast
topology. Then, there exists a GMM associated to

\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{CH}}(V):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)&\quad\text{if}\ V\in\tilde{A},\\
\infty&\quad\text{else}\end{array}\right.

starting form
V^{0}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
with
V(t)\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

The main application of Corollary
4.3
is that of ensuring the existence of GMMs in the regular setting of multiply covered smooth surfaces in Section
5
. In the remainder of this subsection, we apply the corollary to demonstrate the possibility of including a volume constraint or enforcing symmetry.

4.2.1.
Volume constraint

The modeling of biomembranes usually requires
to constrain the minimization of the Canham-Helfrich energy to surfaces with fixed enclosed volume. This can be accomplished in the varifold setting as well.

We introduce the
enclosed volume
of an oriented varifold
V\in IV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

\,\mathrm{encvol}(V):=\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}x\cdot\nu\,\,\mathrm{d}V(x,\nu).

(4.5)

This definition is inspired by the smooth setting: if
V=V[M,\nu^{M},1,0]
such that
M=\partial E
for a
C^{1}
domain
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with outer unit normal
\nu^{M}
, as
\,\mathrm{div}(x)=3
the divergence theorem yields

\,\mathrm{encvol}(V)=\frac{1}{3}\int_{M}x\cdot\nu^{M}(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}(x)=\frac{1}{3}\int_{E}\,\mathrm{div}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{E}\,\mathrm{d}x=|E|=:{\rm vol}(M).

To include the volume constraint we restrict the varifold evolution to the set

\tilde{A}:=\{V\in\mathcal{A}:\>\,\mathrm{encvol}(V)=v_{0}\}

for some fixed volume
v_{0}>0
. In order not to rule out classical smooth solutions by violating the isoperimetric constraint, we require
36\pi v_{0}^{2}\leq m_{0}^{3}
. As the functional
V\mapsto\,\mathrm{encvol}(V)
from (
4.5
) is linear, the set
\tilde{A}
above is closed with respect to the weak-
\ast
convergence. Corollary
4.3
thus gives existence of restricted GMMs respecting a volume constraint.

4.2.2.
Symmetry

Starting form a symmetric initial configuration
V^{0}
, it is unclear if GMMs conserve symmetry. The lack of uniqueness of GMMs and the fact that minimizers of the Canham-Helfrich energy are not necessarily spheres suggest that symmetry conservation may not hold.

On the other hand, one can use Corollary
4.3
to enforce additionally symmetry by means of a constraint. Let us firstly discuss the case of
reflection symmetry
. Without loss of generality, we assume that we reflect about a plane
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
through the origin
and that the compact
Q\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
in (
4.1
) is symmetric with respect to
P
.
The associated reflection mapping

\psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
is defined by

\psi(x,\nu)=(Sx,S\nu)\qquad\text{with}\qquad S\in O(3),\quad\det S=-1.

The reflection of
V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
is the pushforward measure
\psi_{\sharp}V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. As reflection is linear, the class of reflection-symmetric admissible varifolds,

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:=\{V\in\mathcal{A}:\>\psi_{\sharp}V=V\},

is weakly-
\ast
closed in
\mathcal{A}
. One can hence apply Corollary
4.3
and obtain that, starting from a reflection-symmetric configuration
V_{0}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
, there exists a reflection-symmetric GMM
V(t)\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

The case of
rotational symmetry
around a fixed axis
(x_{a},\nu_{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
can be treated analogously. Without loss of generality, we choose
x_{a}
to be the
x_{3}
-axis in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\nu_{a}
to be the north pole in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, i.e.,
\nu_{a}=(0,0,1)
. Moreover, we assume that
Q
is rotationally symmetric around
x_{a}
. Let
\psi^{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
given by

\psi^{\alpha}(x,\nu)=(R^{\alpha}x,R^{\alpha}\nu)\qquad\text{with}\qquad R^{\alpha}\in SO(3)

denote the anticlockwise rotations about an angle
\alpha\in[0,2\pi)
around the axes
x_{a}
,
\nu_{a}
respectively. A measure
V\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
is rotationally symmetric if for all
\alpha\in[0,2\pi)
there exists a rotation
\psi^{\alpha}
such that
\psi_{\sharp}^{\alpha}V=V
.
Any rotation
R^{\alpha}\in SO(3)
can be represented as composition of two reflections, namely
R^{\alpha}=S_{1}\circ S_{2}
for
S_{i}\in O(3)
with
\det S_{i}=-1
(
i=1,2
), so that
\psi^{\alpha}=\psi_{1}\circ\psi_{2}
for
\psi_{i}(x,\nu):=(S_{i}x,S_{i}\nu)
, and hence the class of rotationally symmetric varifolds,

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:=\{V\in\mathcal{A}:\>\psi_{\sharp}^{\alpha}V=V\>\text{for all}\>\alpha\in[0,2\pi)\},

can be readily checked to be weakly-
\ast
closed as well. Therefore, Corollary
4.3
applies, showing existence of rotationally symmetric GMMs, starting from a rotationally symmetric
V_{0}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
.

4.3.
Diameter bounds

Although our notion of Canham-Helfrich flow is rather weak, we can show that solutions do not degenerate, for the GMMs of Definition
4.1
do not shrink to points nor expand indefinitely.

Proposition 4.4

(Diameter bounds)
.

Assume (
3.4
) and let
t\mapsto V(t)
be a GMM. Then, there exist constants
c_{0},c^{0}>0
depending only on the parameters
(V^{0},m_{0},\beta,\gamma,H_{0})
, such that

c_{0}\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V(t)}))\leq c^{0}\quad\forall\>t\geq 0.

Proof.

We start from the lower bound. By
[
11
, Lemma 4.12]
, an oriented curvature varifold
V\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies

\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V}))\geq\frac{\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\sqrt{\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,\mathcal{W}(V)}+\|\partial(q_{\sharp}V)\|_{{{}_{\mathrm{TV}}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times G_{2,3})}.

As
V\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=m_{0}
and
\partial(q_{\sharp}V)=0
, so that the right-hand-side is bounded below by

\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}}{\mathcal{W}(V(t))}}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{W}(V(t))\leq\frac{3}{4}\,c_{1}\left(F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V(t))+c_{2}\,m_{0}\right)
by (
3.8
). As a GMM satisfies
V(t)\in\mathcal{A}
and
F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V(t))=G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V(t))\leq G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})
for all
t\geq 0
by the energy-decreasing property, this implies

\mathcal{W}(V(t))\leq\frac{3}{4}\,c_{1}\left(G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})+c_{2}\,m_{0}\right)
. Consequently we obtain the lower bound

\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V(t)}))\geq\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}}{\frac{3}{4}\,c_{1}\left(G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})+c_{2}\,m_{0}\right)}}:=c_{0}>0.

The starting point for the upper bound is the generalization to curvature varifolds without boundary of the estimate

\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V}))\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,\mathcal{W}(V)}
,
[
54
,
56
]
.
Then, similar arguments as for the lower bound show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V(t)}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{3\,m_{0}\,c_{1}\left(G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})+c_{2}\,m_{0}\right)}=:c^{0}>0

for all
t\geq 0
, proving the claim.
∎

Note in particular that the upper bound
c^{0}
is independent of the choice of the bounding compact
Q
. This proves that restricting to
Q
is in fact not necessary. This allows us to omit
Q
in the sequel, i.e., we replace
\mathcal{V}
introduced in (
4.1
) by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
, see Subsection
2.4
.

5.
Regular setting

Let us now turn our attention to the case of multiply covered
surfaces, which amounts to constrain the evolution to the class of varifolds with smooth supports in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Such more regular setting allows for finer results. In fact, one can prove that the multiplicity is preserved along the evolution. On this basis, one has the possibility of considering a weaker setting where the Wasserstein distance between varifolds, which is rather strong, is replaced by the more natural Wasserstein distance between supports
in space.

We start by introducing some notation in Subsection
5.1
. Then, we discuss the reference case of multiply covered spheres in Subsection
5.2
. In particular, we assess the minimality of multiply covered spheres in dependence of the given mass constraint
m_{0}
and spontaneous curvature
H_{0}
. We then present a Li-Yau-like bound on the multiplicity in terms of the Canham-Helfrich energy for general multiply covered surfaces in Subsection
5.3
. In order to possibly consider a restricted flow in the spirit of Corollary
4.3
for multiply covered surfaces, we verify closedness
of the corresponding classes in Subsection
5.4
. An argument for multiplicity conservation during the flow is presented in Subsection
5.5
. Eventually, we discuss the case of the weaker evolution setting given by the Wasserstein distance between the supports in Subsection
5.6
and we comment on some generalizations in Subsection
5.7
.

5.1.
Multiply covered surfaces

In the remainder of the paper, we restrict to varifolds
V=V[M,\nu,\theta^{+},\theta^{-}]
that are supported on closed (i.e., compact without boundary), connected

C^{1,1}
surfaces
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, that is, embedded
C^{1}
-surfaces
M
with Lipschitz continuous unit normal vector
\nu:=\nu^{M}\colon M\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In this setting, by Rademacher’s theorem,
\overline{H}^{q_{\sharp}V}
,
|A^{q_{\sharp}V}|^{2}
, and
K^{q_{\sharp}V}
are defined almost everywhere on
M
.

By the admissibility condition on the zero boundary current in the Definition (
4.2
) of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
\partial T_{V}=0
, Federer’s constancy theorem for smoothly supported currents (Theorem
2.1
) entails that the density
\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}
of
T_{V}
is constant. This gives
\overline{\theta}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(\mathcal{H}^{2}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M)
-a.e.,

\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}=\overline{\theta}_{0}.

In this setting, the Canham-Helfrich functional (
3.10
) reduces to

F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\left(H^{2}+H_{0}^{2}\right)+\gamma K\right)(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}-\beta\,\overline{\theta}_{0}\,H_{0}\int_{M}H\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}.

It follows that
F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)
will be small for large
\overline{\theta}_{0}
, provided that
H_{0}\int_{M}H\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}>0
. This favorable case occurs for example if
H
does not change sign (i.e.,
M
is mean convex) and
H_{0}H>0
.
Otherwise, if
H_{0}\int_{M}H\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}<0
, a negative multiplicity
\overline{\theta}_{0}
is favored, corresponding to an orientation flip.

This suggests to restrict with no loss of generality to multiplicities of the form

\theta^{+}=\overline{\theta}_{0}=k\in\mathbb{N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta^{-}=0.

In order to ensure the closure of this more regular class of configurations, we follow
[
25
]
and further restrict to surfaces of
uniform

C^{1,1}
regularity with given genus
g\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. More precisely, fix
L>0
and a smooth connected closed surface
\Sigma^{g}
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and with genus
g\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the subclass of admissible varifolds given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}:=\Big\{

\displaystyle V=V[M,\nu,k,0]\in\mathcal{A}:\>\text{$M=\psi(\Sigma^{g})$ for an embedding $\psi\in C^{1,1}(\Sigma^{g};\mathbb{R}^{3})$ }

\displaystyle\text{with $\|\psi\|_{C^{1,1}(\Sigma^{g})}\leq L$, $\>\nu$ is the outer unit normal vector to $M$}\Big\}.

(5.1)

We will also employ the notation

\mathcal{A}^{g}:=\{V\in\mathcal{A}:\,\exists\>k\in\mathbb{N}\>\>\text{such that}\>V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}\}.

(5.2)

to indicate multiply covered surfaces of genus
g
. Recall that a differentiable map
\psi\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an embedding if it is injective and
\nabla\psi
has rank two. Moreover,

\|\psi\|_{C^{1,1}(\Sigma)}:=\sup_{x\in\Sigma}\left(|\psi(x)|+|\nabla\psi(x)|+\sup_{x^{\prime}\neq x\in\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right).

The constraint
\|\psi\|_{C^{1,1}(\Sigma^{g})}\leq L
entails that elements
V=V[M,\nu,k,0]\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
have a uniform curvature bound of their spatial supports
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Equivalently, the sets
M
satisfy a uniform ball condition or are sets of positive reach
[
26
,
25
]
.

For
V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
we have that

\displaystyle\hskip-10.00002ptG_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)=F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)

\displaystyle=k\,E_{\mathrm{CH}}(M),

with

E_{\mathrm{CH}}(M)=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}(H-H_{0})^{2}+\gamma K\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{M}(H-H_{0})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}+4\pi\gamma(1-g)

by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem.
Note that the existence of smooth closed surfaces
M
of fixed
genus
g\in\mathbb{N}
minimizing
E_{\mathrm{CH}}
can be proved in some parameter
range
[
15
,
25
,
45
,
52
]
.

5.2.
Stationarity of spheres

Before moving on, let us collect some remarks on the case where
M
is a sphere. We thus restrict to the class
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{0}
in the following. As GMMs starting from a Canham-Helfrich energy minimizer are stationary, we investigate
under which conditions we have minimality of multiply covered spheres.

By
\mathbb{S}_{R}^{2}
we denote a sphere with radius
R>0
, centered at some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and equipped with its outer unit normal
\nu\colon\mathbb{S}_{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In particular,
\mathbb{S}_{R}^{2}
has the constant mean curvature
H=-2/{R}
and the constant Gauss curvature
K=1/{R^{2}}
.
A
multiply covered sphere
is an oriented varifold of the form

V_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}}:=V[\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R},\nu,\theta_{0},0]
with multiplicity
\theta_{0}\colon\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}\to\mathbb{N}
. Its mass is given by
{\mu_{V_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}}\theta_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=m_{0}>0}
and
its energy (
3.1
) takes the form

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{2}\left(-\frac{2}{R}-H_{0}\right)^{2}m_{0}+\frac{\gamma}{R^{2}}m_{0}=2\beta m_{0}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{H_{0}}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{R^{2}}m_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\beta+\gamma}{R^{2}}+\frac{2\beta H_{0}}{R}+\frac{\beta H_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)m_{0}=2\beta m_{0}\left(\frac{1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta}}{R^{2}}+\frac{H_{0}}{R}+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{4}\right).

(5.3)

In case of a constant multiplicity
\theta_{0}(x)=k\in\mathbb{N}
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}
, the mass of
V_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}}
reads

\mu_{V_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=4\pi kR^{2}=m_{0},

which relates the radius and the multiplicity. Consequently, for fixed mass, the only multiply covered sphere (up to isometries) with constant multiplicity
k\in\mathbb{N}
is the
k
-covered sphere

S_{k}:=V_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R_{k}}}=V[\mathbb{S}^{2}_{R_{k}},\nu,k,0]\qquad\text{with}\qquad R_{k}:=\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}}{4\pi k}}=\frac{R_{1}}{\sqrt{k}},\qquad R_{1}:=\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}}{4\pi}}.

Since
\mathbb{S}_{R_{k}}^{2}
is just a scaling of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we have

S_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{0},

at least for
k
small with respect to the uniform curvature bound
L
in (
5.1
). The energy of
S_{k}
is a function of the multiplicity
k
alone,

F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{k})=2\beta\,m_{0}\left(\frac{1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta}}{R_{1}^{2}}k+\frac{H_{0}}{R_{1}}\sqrt{k}+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{4}\right).

(5.4)

For
\beta=1/2
,
H_{0}=0
, and
\gamma=0
, this reduces to the Willmore energy of a
k
-covered sphere,

\textstyle{\mathcal{W}(S_{k})=\frac{1}{4}\big(\frac{2}{R_{k}}\big)^{2}m_{0}=4\pi k}
, which corresponds to equality in the Li-Yau estimate
(
3.7
).

The following lemma shows that the
k
-covered sphere is the only Canham-Helfrich minimizer among multiply covered surfaces of genus zero, provided that
|H_{0}|
is small enough.

Proposition 5.1

(Spheres are unique minimizers for fixed multiplicity)
.

Let
\beta>0
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{0}
. If
H_{0}\leq 0
and satisfies

|H_{0}|\leq 2/R_{k}=\sqrt{16\pi k/m_{0}},

then
S_{k}
uniquely minimizes
F_{\mathrm{CH}}
on
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{0}
. The statement is also true if
H_{0}>0
, if
S_{k}
is defined with the inner normal. In case
H_{0}=0
,
S_{k}
is a minimizer independently of its orientation.

Proof.

Let
V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{0}
be arbitrary. Starting from the lower bound (
3.11
) given in Lemma
3.1
,
we aim at deducing a new lower bound that is independent of
V
.
Since
\theta^{+}+\theta^{-}=k\in\mathbb{N}
and
M
is a closed
C^{1,1}
surface of genus
g=0
, the Gauss-Bonnet theorem applies:

\gamma\int_{M}K^{q_{\sharp}V}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=\gamma\,k\int_{M}K^{q_{\sharp}V}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=4\pi\,\gamma\,k(1-g)=4\pi\,\gamma\,k.

(5.5)

Combining the assumption

\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}\leq\frac{1}{R_{k}}=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi k}{m_{0}}}
with
\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{W}(V)}{m_{0}}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{4\pi k}{m_{0}}}
, from the Li-Yau estimate (
3.7
), shows that

\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{W}(V)}{m_{0}}}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}\geq\sqrt{\frac{4\pi k}{m_{0}}}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}\geq 0
.
With (
3.11
) and (
5.5
), this gives

F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)\geq 2\beta m_{0}\Big({\textstyle{\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{W}(V)}{m_{0}}}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}}}\Big)^{2}+\gamma\int_{M}K^{q_{\sharp}V}(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\geq 2\beta m_{0}\Big({\textstyle{\sqrt{\frac{4\pi k}{m_{0}}}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}}}\Big)^{2}+4\pi\,\gamma\,k.

This lower bound is uniquely attained at
S_{k}
. In fact, by (
5.3
) and
H_{0}\leq 0
we get

\textstyle{F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{k})=2\beta m_{0}\left(\frac{1}{R_{k}}+\frac{H_{0}}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{R_{k}^{2}}m_{0}=2\beta m_{0}\left(\sqrt{\frac{4\pi k}{m_{0}}}-\frac{|H_{0}|}{2}\right)^{2}+4\pi\,\gamma\,k.}

This proves that
S_{k}
is the unique minimizer in
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{0}
, because equality in the Li-Yau estimate (
3.7
) holds only for spheres. Choosing
\nu
as the inner normal just reverses the sign in front of
H_{0}
and the assertion follows as above. Finally, we note that for
H_{0}=0
, the assumption
|H_{0}|/2\leq 1/R_{k}
is trivially satisfied.
∎

Proposition
5.1
is an extension of an argument by
Dalphin

[
24
, Chapter 5]
to the multilayered setting. There, also counterexamples to sphere minimality are explicitly constructed in classes of axisymmetric surfaces (cigars or stomatocytes), if the bound on
H_{0}
is violated. Of course, the same counterexamples apply to our multiply covered case.

Let us now drop the prescription of the multiplicity while still asking
0\leq-H_{0}\leq\sqrt{16\pi/m_{0}}
, so that any minimizer of
F_{\mathrm{CH}}
in
\mathcal{A}^{0}
(
5.2
) is necessarily a sphere. Our aim is then to determine which sphere is actually the absolute minimizer across multiplicities. We emphasize that due to the possibly nontrivial multiplicity, the Gauss-term (
5.5
)
is variable as well and, unless
\gamma=0
, can not be neglected in the optimization process. We have the following.

Proposition 5.2

(Optimal spheres)
.

Let
-2\beta<\gamma\leq 0
,
0\leq-H_{0}\leq\sqrt{16\pi/m_{0}}
, and define

\displaystyle k_{\ast}

\displaystyle:=\frac{m_{0}H_{0}^{2}}{16\pi}\,\frac{1}{(1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta})^{2}}\,,

(5.6)

\displaystyle Y_{\ast}

\displaystyle:=(\sqrt{\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor}-\sqrt{\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil}\,)\,H_{0}-\textstyle{\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{m_{0}}}\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta}\right)}.

(5.7)

If
k_{\ast}\in\mathbb{N}
, then the unique minimizer of
F_{\mathrm{CH}}
on
\mathcal{A}^{0}
is given by
S_{k_{\ast}}
. If
k_{\ast}\in[0,1]
then the unique minimizer is
S_{1}
. If
k_{\ast}\in(1,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
, then the unique minimizer is
S_{\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor}
for
Y_{\ast}<0
and
S_{\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil}
for
Y_{\ast}>0
. In case
k_{\ast}\in(1,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
and
Y_{\ast}=0
, the two spheres
S_{\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor}
and
S_{\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil}
are the sole minimizers.

Before giving the proof of the proposition, let us note that the assumption
-2\beta<\gamma\leq 0
implies
0<1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta}\leq 1
. Moreover, under the smallness condition on
|H_{0}|
of the statement, one has that
k_{*}\leq(1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta})^{-2}
. In particular, the multiplicity of the optimal sphere is bounded above in terms of the parameters
\beta
and
\gamma
only. Yet, the determination of the actual minimizing sphere
S_{k}
via the sign of
Y_{\ast}
does depend on the parameters
m_{0}
and
H_{0}
as well.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Looking back to
(
5.4
),
we have that
k\mapsto F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{k})
is the restriction to
\mathbb{N}
of the smooth, strictly convex, and unbounded function
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

f(r):=2\beta m_{0}\left(\frac{1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta}}{R_{1}^{2}}r+\frac{H_{0}}{R_{1}}\sqrt{r}+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{4}\right).

As
f
is uniquely minimized at
r=k_{\ast}
, one has that
\mathbb{N}\ni k\mapsto F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{k})
is minimized either in
k=k_{\ast}
if
k_{\ast}\in\mathbb{N}
, in
k=1
if
0\leq k_{\ast}\leq 1
, and otherwise in
k=\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor
, in
k=\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil
, or in both of the latter. These assertions follow directly by comparing
F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor})
and
F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil})
via the sign of

Y_{\ast}=\frac{R_{1}}{2\beta m_{0}}\left(F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor})-F_{\mathrm{CH}}(S_{\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil})\right)=\frac{R_{1}}{2\beta m_{0}}\left(f(\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor)-f(\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil)\right)
, which by
R_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}}{4\pi}}
and
\lfloor k_{\ast}\rfloor-\lceil k_{\ast}\rceil=-1
takes the form (
5.7
).
∎

Let us close this subsection by discussing the specific case of the

Corollary 5.3

(Optimality of the single-covered sphere)
.

Let
-2\beta<\gamma\leq 0
and

\textstyle{0\leq-H_{0}<\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{m_{0}}}\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta}\right)(1+\sqrt{2}).}

Then,
S_{1}
is the unique minimizer of
F_{\mathrm{CH}}
on
\mathcal{A}^{0}
.

Indeed, under the condition on

one readily checks that
-H_{0}<\sqrt{16\pi/m_{0}}
as well as that
k_{\ast}
from (
5.6
) and

from (

) fulfill

and

, respectively. Hence, the assertion of Corollary

follows from Proposition
5.2
.

5.3.
Li-Yau estimate

We now derive a multiplicity bound in terms of the Canham-Helfrich energy, reminiscent of the Li-Yau inequality

4\pi k\leq\mathcal{W}(V)

(
3.7
)
for the Willmore energy of an integral varifold
V=V[M,k]
. The estimate below can be compared with the recent
[
52
]
, where some alternative Li-Yau inequality is derived for the case
\gamma=0
.

We restrict to varifolds
V
in the class
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
(
5.1
), for we make a crucial use of the Gauss-Bonnet theorem. We have the following.

Proposition 5.4

(Multiplicity bound)
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
,
H_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta>0
,
\gamma<0
, and
-2\beta<\gamma(1-g)
. Then, there exists a nondecreasing function
\mathbb{R}\ni F\mapsto\overline{k}(F)\in\mathbb{N}
depending on
\beta
,
\gamma
, and
H_{0}
, but independent of the genus
g
, such that

k\leq\overline{k}({F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)})
.

The proposition ensures that the multiplicity of any
V\in\mathcal{A}^{g}
can be bounded in terms of its Canham-Helfrich energy, as long as
0<-2\beta<\gamma(1-g)
. Note that the latter holds for any genus
g\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
if
-2\beta<\gamma<0
or (
3.4
) holds. In fact, with (
3.4
), i.e.,
-\frac{6}{5}\beta<\gamma<0
, we also have
-2\beta<\gamma<0
, because
-2\beta=-\frac{5}{3}\frac{6}{5}\beta<\frac{5}{3}\gamma<\gamma<0
. This immediately gives
-2\beta<\gamma(1-g)
in the cases
g=0
and
g=1
, namely
-2\beta<\gamma
and
-2\beta<0
respectively. If
g>1
then
1-g<0
, so that
\gamma<\gamma(1-g)
for
\gamma<0
. Therefore
-2\beta<\gamma<0
implies
-2\beta<\gamma(1-g)
in this case as well.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

By Lemma
3.1
, any oriented curvature varifold
V=V[M,\nu,\theta^{+},\theta^{-}]\in AV_{2}^{o}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with mass
\mu_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=m_{0}
satisfies

F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)\geq 2\beta\left(\mathcal{W}(V)-|H_{0}|\sqrt{\mathcal{W}(V)}\sqrt{m_{0}}+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{4}m_{0}\right)+\>\gamma\int_{M}K(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2},

where the Gauss curvature
K=K^{q_{\sharp}V}
is given by (
3.2
). For
V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
, we have

\theta^{+}=k
,
\theta^{-}=0
, and
M
is a
C^{1,1}
surface, for which Gauss-Bonnet applies:

\int_{M}K(\theta^{+}+\theta^{-})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=k\int_{M}K\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=4\pi(1-g)k
. By Young’s inequality,

|H_{0}|\sqrt{\mathcal{W}(V)}\sqrt{m_{0}}\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{W}(V)+\frac{H_{0}^{2}m_{0}}{4\varepsilon}

with
\varepsilon>0
. By employing the classical Li-Yau inequality
\mathcal{W}(V)\geq 4\pi k
, the estimate then gives

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)

\displaystyle\geq 2\beta\left((1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{W}(V)+\left(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{H_{0}^{2}m_{0}}{4}\right)+\>4\pi\gamma(1-g)k

\displaystyle\geq 4\pi\left(2\beta(1-\varepsilon)+\gamma(1-g)\right)k+\beta\left(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{H_{0}^{2}m_{0}}{2}.

Alternatively, one can deduce this lower bound from
F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)=kE_{\mathrm{CH}}(M)
and
k\mathcal{H}^{2}(M)=m_{0}
, starting form the analogue of Lemma
3.1
in the classical case.

Thanks to the assumptions
\beta>0
,
\gamma<0
, and
2\beta+\gamma(1-g)>0
, we can choose

0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2\beta}(2\beta+\gamma(1-g))=1+\frac{\gamma(1-g)}{2\beta}\begin{cases}=1+\frac{\gamma}{2\beta},&g=0,\\
=1,&g=1,\\
>1,&g\geq 2,\end{cases}

such that
2\beta\varepsilon<2\beta+\gamma(1-g)
. Then

2\beta(1-\varepsilon)+\gamma(1-g)=2\beta+\gamma(1-g)-2\beta\varepsilon>0
and we obtain the multiplicity bound

k\leq\frac{F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)-\beta\left(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{H_{0}^{2}m_{0}}{2}}{4\pi\left(2\beta(1-\varepsilon)+\gamma(1-g)\right)}.

With the choice
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{4\beta}(2\beta+\gamma(1-g))
for
g=0
or
1
and
\varepsilon=1
for
g\geq 2

we get

k\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{2}{2\beta+\gamma}F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)+\beta\frac{2\beta-\gamma}{(2\beta+\gamma)^{2}}H_{0}^{2}m_{0}\right)=:\overline{k}_{0},&g=0,\\
\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)+\frac{H_{0}^{2}m_{0}}{2}\right)=:\overline{k}_{1},&g=1,\\
\frac{F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)}{4\pi\,\gamma(1-g)}\leq-\frac{F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)}{4\pi\,\gamma}=:\overline{k}_{2},&g\geq 2.\\
\end{cases}

The inequality in case
g\geq 2
follows from
\gamma<0
, by which
\gamma(1-g)\geq-\gamma
, and the fact that
F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)\geq 0
in this case. Thus, the next largest integer of the maximum of these values gives the desired upper multiplicity bound that is increasing in
F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)
,

\overline{k}({F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)}):=\lceil\max\{\overline{k}_{0},\overline{k}_{1},\overline{k}_{2}\}\rceil.

We note that
\overline{k}({F_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)})
depends on
\beta
,
\gamma
, and
H_{0}
but not on the genus
g
.
∎

5.4.
Closedness of regular classes

We now check that Corollary
4.3
can be applied to the subclass of admissible varifolds corresponding to multiply covered smooth surfaces. In particular, this amounts to proving that the classes
\mathcal{A}^{g}
(
5.2
) and
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
(
5.1
) are closed in
\mathcal{A}
(
4.2
). We have the following.

Lemma 5.5

(Closedness)
.

Let
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}
and
V\in\mathcal{A}
with
V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
as
n\to\infty
.

(i)

If
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}^{g}
then
V\in\mathcal{A}^{g}
.

(ii)

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
then
V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
.

Proof.

We first prove (ii). Let
V_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
and
V\in\mathcal{A}
be such that
V_{n}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}V
as varifolds. By definition,
V_{n}=V_{n}[M_{n},\nu^{M_{n}},k,0]
and by compactness of the uniform
C^{1,1}
surfaces
M_{n}

[
26
,
25
]
, there exists a uniform
C^{1,1}
surface
M
such that (a not relabeled subsequence satisfies)
M_{n}\to M
(
n\to\infty
) in the Hausdorff distance. This gives rise to the varifold

\tilde{V}:=\tilde{V}[M,\nu^{M},k,0]\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}.

By uniqueness of varifold limits
\tilde{V}
coincides with
V
(which also implies that the whole sequence
M_{n}
converges). This shows that
V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
, completing the proof of (ii).

In order to show (i), we observe that by (
5.2
), elements
V_{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{g}
are of the form
V_{n}=V_{n}[M_{n},\nu^{M_{n}},j_{n},0]
for some
j_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
. The mass constraint implies that

j_{n}=\frac{m_{0}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{n})}.

As before,
M_{n}\to M
(
n\to\infty
) in the Hausdorff sense. The uniform
C^{1,1}
regularity implies that oscillations are controlled, and
\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{2}(M)
. Therefore, we have

j_{n}=\frac{m_{0}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{n})}\to\frac{m_{0}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}(M)}=:j.

This gives rise to the varifold
\tilde{V}=\tilde{V}[M,\nu^{M},j,0]
, which must coincide with
V
, as in the proof of (ii), so
V\in\mathcal{A}^{g}
.
∎

5.5.
Conservation of multiplicity

Corollary
4.3
and Proposition
5.5
ensure that, starting from an initial state
V^{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
, there exists a restricted GMM for the varifold Canham-Helfrich flow in
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
.
In this section, we prove a stronger fact, namely, that multiplicity is conserved by GMMs in
\mathcal{A}^{g}
, without the need to be constrained.

Theorem 5.6

(Conservation of multiplicity)
.

Assume (
3.4
) and
V^{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
with
G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})<\infty
. Let
V
be a GMM restricted to
\mathcal{A}^{g}
. Then
V(t)\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
for all
t\geq 0
, i.e., the flow conserves multiplicity.

Proof.

Let us start by remarking that, thanks to Proposition
5.4
, any
V\in\mathcal{A}^{g}
actually satisfies
V\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
for some
k\leq\overline{k}({G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V)})
. Let
V
be a restricted GMM in
\mathcal{A}^{g}
with
V(0)=V^{0}
. We know by the closedness of
\mathcal{A}^{g}
from Lemma
5.5
(i) and by Corollary
4.3
that such GMMs exist. Let
(V^{n}_{m})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}^{g}
be a corresponding sequence of discrete minimizers with time step
\tau_{m}>0
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
\tau_{m}\to 0
. Then
G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})\geq G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{n}_{m})
and, since the function
\overline{k}
from Proposition
5.4
is nondecreasing, it follows that

\overline{k}({G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{n}_{m})})\leq\overline{k}({G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})})=:\overline{k}_{0}.

Next, we show that
V^{n}_{m}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
V^{n-1}_{m}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
and let by contradiction
V^{n}_{m}\in\mathcal{A}^{g}_{j}
with
j\neq k
. Lemma
5.7
below ensures that

W_{p}(V^{n}_{m},V^{n-1}_{m})\geq\alpha(j,k)>0.

(5.8)

By defining
\overline{\alpha}:=\min_{1\leq j\neq k\leq\overline{k}_{0}}\alpha(j,k)>0
and
f_{j}:=\inf_{\mathcal{A}^{g}_{j}}G_{\mathrm{CH}}
we hence have

G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})\geq G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{n-1}_{m})\geq G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{n}_{m})+\frac{1}{2\tau_{m}}W_{p}(V^{n}_{m},V^{n-1}_{m})\geq f_{j}+\frac{\overline{\alpha}^{2}}{2\tau_{m}}.

This leads to a contradiction as soon as the time step
\tau_{m}
is
small enough, namely, if

\tau_{m}<\frac{\overline{\alpha}^{2}}{2(G_{\mathrm{CH}}(V^{0})-f_{j})}.

Therefore, all discrete minimizers must satisfy
V^{n}_{m}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
for
\tau_{m}
small enough. Of course, the same holds true for the piecewise constant interpolants
\overline{V}_{\tau_{m}}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. Finally, the closedness of
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
from Proposition
5.5
(ii) shows that multiplicity
k
is preserved when taking the limit
\tau_{m}\to 0
as well, i.e.,
\overline{V}_{\tau_{m}}(t)\to V(t)\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
for all
t\geq 0
, which
completes the proof.
∎

We are hence left with checking the following technical Lemma, which is used in the argument of Theorem
5.6
for formula (
5.8
). In particular, we show a uniform control from below of the Wasserstein distance between two multiply covered surfaces with different multiplicities.

Lemma 5.7

(Distance threshold)
.

For all
j,\,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
j\not=k
there exists
\alpha=\alpha(j,k)>0
such that

W_{p}(V_{j},V_{k})\geq\alpha

for all
V_{j}\in\mathcal{A}^{g}_{j}
and
V_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
.

Illustration of the argument of Lemma
5.7
for
j>k
. The surface
M_{j}
(blue) must leave the tubular neighbourhood (dashed line) around the surface
M_{k}
(grey).

Proof.

As the statement is symmetric in
j
and
k
, with no loss of generality we may assume that
j>k
. We proceed by contradiction and assume that for all
\delta>0
there exist
V_{j}\in\mathcal{A}^{g}_{j}
and
V_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
with

W_{p}(V_{j},V_{k})<\delta^{2}.

(5.9)

We show that condition (
5.9
) leads to a contradiction if
\delta
is chosen to be small enough. More precisely, we prove that one can find
0<\delta_{*}<1
depending only on
k
and
j
, as well as on the data
m_{0}
,
L
, and
p
, such that for any
0<\delta<\delta_{*}
no varifolds
V_{j}\in\mathcal{A}^{g}_{j}
and
V_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
exist such that (
5.9
) holds.

Along the proof, we use the notation
V_{j}=V[M_{j},\tilde{\nu},j,0]
and
V_{k}=V[M_{k},\nu,k,0]
for any varifold in
\mathcal{A}^{g}_{j}
and
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
, respectively. Moreover, we indicate by
C_{i}>0
,
i=0,\dots,7
, some constants depending solely on the data, in particular possibly on
L
. To start with, let us note that there exists
C_{0}>0
such that any
V\in\mathcal{A}^{g}
is such that the corresponding spatial support
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
fulfills the lower bound

{\rm vol}(M)\geq C_{0},

where
{\rm vol}(M)
indicates the volume of the region which is enclosed by
M
, cf. Subsection
4.2.1
.

We subdivide the proof into three steps. At first, we show that any surface which is completely contained in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
M_{k}
must have at least the area of
M_{k}
, up to a small error. Since

\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{j})<\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{k})

by
j>k
and by the mass constraint, this implies
that
M_{j}
cannot be completely contained in such a small neighborhood of
M_{k}
, see Figure

.
In the second step, we give a lower bound on the area of
M_{j}
which is not contained in the small neighborhood of
M_{k}
. Eventually, in Step 3 we show that such a lower bound contradicts condition (
5.9
).

Step 1: A lower bound on the area.

We start by checking that one can find
\delta_{0}>0
and
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,0<\delta<\delta_{0},\ \forall\,V_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g},\ \forall\,E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\ \text{of finite perimeter with}\,\ |E|\geq C_{0}:

\displaystyle\quad\partial^{*}E\subset M_{k}^{\delta}:=M_{k}+B_{\delta}(0)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial^{*}E)\geq\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{k})-C_{1}\delta,

(5.10)

where we recall the notation for the open ball
B_{\delta}(0)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\>|x|<\delta\}
.

Let us start by choosing
\delta_{1}>0
so small that the projection
\pi_{M_{k}}
onto
M_{k}
is well-defined on
M_{k}^{\delta_{1}}
, for any
V_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
. As
M_{k}
is a set of positive reach, such a

exists and depends on
L
only. Then, one can find
C_{2}>0
such that

{\rm vol}(M_{k}^{\delta})\leq C_{2}\delta\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{k})\quad\forall\,0<\delta<\delta_{1}.

(5.11)

Note that
C_{2}
depends on
L
and that the latter inequality cannot be guaranteed if the uniform
C^{1,1}
regularity of
M_{k}
is dropped.
Next we choose
0<\delta_{0}<\min\{C_{0}k/(C_{2}m_{0}),\delta_{1}\}

\partial^{*}E\subset M_{k}^{\delta_{0}}\ \Rightarrow\ \pi_{M_{k}}(\partial^{*}E)\equiv M_{k}.

Indeed, if
\pi_{M_{k}}(\partial^{*}E)\not=M_{k}
, the fact that
\partial^{*}E\subset M_{k}^{\delta_{0}}
would imply that the whole
E
is contained in
M_{k}^{\delta_{0}}
. In combination with inequality (

) this would require that

C_{0}\leq|E|\leq{\rm vol}(M_{k}^{\delta_{0}})\leq C_{2}{\delta_{0}}\mathcal{H}^{2}(M_{k})=C_{2}{\delta_{0}}\frac{m_{0}}{k}<C_{0},

which is a contradiction.

Let
\xi\colon U\to\Sigma^{g}
be a fixed smooth a.e. injective parametrization of
\Sigma^{g}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
U=[0,1]^{2}
. We can cover

5.6.
Existence of GMMs under a weaker metric

In the regular case, one can prove the existence of GMMs for the Canham-Helfrich functional under the weaker metric given by the Wasserstein distance of the spatial supports.
Indeed, let
V^{0},V^{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
and set
\mu^{0}:=\mu_{V^{0}},\mu^{1}:=\mu_{V^{1}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, one can prove that the varifold Wasserstein distance,
W_{p}(V^{1},V^{0})
, controls the Wasserstein distance between spatial Radon measures,
\overline{W}_{p}(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})
, in the following sense

W_{p}(V^{1},V^{0})\geq\overline{W}_{p}(\mu^{1},\mu^{0}).

Indeed, with
\lambda\in\Pi(V^{1},V^{0})
realizing the minimum in the definition of
W_{p}(V^{1},V^{0})
, we get

\displaystyle W_{p}^{p}(V^{1},V^{0})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})^{2}}(|x-\tilde{x}|+|\nu-\tilde{\nu}|)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda((x,\nu),(\tilde{x},\tilde{\nu}))

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})^{2}}|x-\tilde{x}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda((x,\nu),(\tilde{x},\tilde{\nu}))+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{p}\binom{p}{k}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})^{2}}|x-\tilde{x}|^{p-k}|\nu-\tilde{\nu}|^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda((x,\nu),(\tilde{x},\tilde{\nu}))}_{\geq\>0}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x-\tilde{x}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}(\pi_{\sharp}^{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\lambda)(x,\tilde{x})\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}(\pi_{\sharp}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\lambda)(\nu,\tilde{\nu})}_{=\>1}\geq\overline{W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{1},\mu^{0}),

as the
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
-marginals
V^{0}
and
V^{1}
of
\lambda
are oriented varifolds of the form (
3.9
) and the
(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}
-marginal
\pi_{\sharp}^{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\lambda\in\mathcal{M}((\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2})
is a possible choice in
\Pi(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})
.

On the other hand, as soon as multiplicity is fixed to some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and we restrict to the regular varifolds
V^{1},V^{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
(
5.1
), which have with fixed orientation, we have that

\overline{W}_{p}(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})=0\>\Longrightarrow\>W_{p}(V^{1},V^{0})=0,

(5.21)

for
\overline{W}_{p}(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})=0
in particular implies that
M^{0}=M^{1}=:M
. The equality
V^{1}=V^{0}
then follows from
V^{0}=V[M,\nu^{M},k,0]=V^{1}
with
\mu_{V^{0}}=k(\mathcal{H}^{2}\,\text{\Large{\reflectbox{$\lrcorner$}}}M)
.

The nondegeneracy condition (
5.21
) is instrumental in proving the existence of GMMs, because it qualifies
\overline{W}_{p}
as a distance on
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{g}
. Correspondingly, the existence result from
[
3
, Prop. 2.2.3]
can still be applied.
However, note that condition (
5.21
) hinges on the conservation of multiplicity and that the proof of Theorem
5.6
, in particular the argument of Lemma
5.7
, requires the control of the stronger distance
W_{p}(V^{1},V^{0})
. This amounts to say that, effectively, we can resort to the weaker metric
\overline{W}_{p}(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})
just in the frame of GMMs restricted a priori
to
\mathcal{A}^{g}
, namely, multiply covered uniformly regular
C^{1,1}
surfaces.

5.7.
More general curvature functionals

Most results of this section can also be established
for a larger class of geometric functionals. In particular, we could prove the existence of GMMs for more general curvature energies of the form

D(M):=\int_{M}f(x,\nu(x),H(x),K(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}(x).

Here,
M
is a uniformly regular
C^{1,1}
surface in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
, as in (
5.1
), and the integrand
f
is continuous and convex in its last two arguments. This class of curvature functionals has already been considered in
[
25
]
concerning equilibrium problems. In particular, it has been verified there that
D
admits minimizers in
\mathcal{A}^{g}_{1}
for any fixed genus
g\in\mathbb{N}
, also under a volume constraint.

The analysis in
[
25
]
hinges upon the compactness of
\mathcal{A}^{g}_{1}
and on the lower semicontinuity of
D
. These same tools ensure the existence of a GMM for
D
with
respect to the Wasserstein metric
W_{p}
.
The adjustments required are mainly notational. The only point deserving some attention is the a priori multiplicity bound in terms of the functional, for a Li-Yau inequality may fail in degenerate cases. Still, a bound on the
multiplicity can be directly deduced from the uniform
C^{1,1}
regularity, i.e., depending on the constant
L
in the definition (
5.1
) of
\mathcal{A}^{g}_{k}
.
By assuming such a priori bound on the multiplicity, the argument of Theorem
5.6
still holds. In particular, multiplicity is conserved along the evolution.
Moreover, in the weaker metric setting given by the Wasserstein distance
\overline{W}_{p}
on the spatial supports, one can still prove existence of a
restricted GMM to
\mathcal{A}^{g}_{k}
by constraining the multiplicity.

Dispersion Relations of Nambu-Goldstone Modes
Tohru Koma

Abstract




Abstract:
We study the energy-momentum relations of the Nambu-Goldstone modes in
quantum antiferromagnetic Heisenberg models on a hypercubic lattice.
This work is a sequel to the previous series about the models.
We prove that the Nambu-Goldstone modes show a linear dispersion relation for the small momenta,
i.e., the low energies of the Nambu-Goldstone excitations are proportional to the momentum of the spin wave
above the infinite-volume ground states with symmetry breaking.
Our method relies on the upper bounds for the susceptibilities which are derived from
the reflection positivity of the quantum Heisenberg antiferromagnets.
The method is also applicable to other systems when the corresponding upper bounds
for the susceptibilities are justified.

1
Introduction

In contrast to the quantum ferromagnetic Heisenberg models,
the ground state of the quantum antiferromagnets is mysterious.
In fact, although the ground state of the quantum antiferromagnets for a finite volume is unique
[
1
,
2
]
,
an infinite-volume ground state of the same model exhibits a spontaneous staggered magnetization
[
3
]
.
As is well known, the classical Néel state exhibits a staggered magnetization
but is not a ground state of the quantum antiferromagnets. In other words, the unique finite-volume ground state retains
the symmetry of the model for any finite volume, but the infinite-volume ground state breaks the symmetry.
This phenomenon can be explained by the quasi-degeneracy of the finite-volume ground state
[
4
,
5
]
.
Namely, there appear many low-lying eigenstates whose excitation energy is
very close to the energy of the symmetric ground state of the finite-volume Hamiltonian,
and these low-lying eigenstates yield a set of symmetry-breaking ground states in the infinite-volume limit
by forming linear combinations of the low-lying eigenstates and the symmetric ground state.

This idea of the quasi-degeneracy explains well the emergence of the infinite-volume ground states
with symmetry breaking. However, there arises another question as follows:
How do we obtain the low-energy excitations above an infinite-volume ground state?
In other words, how do we distinguish the low-energy excitations from the low-lying eigenstates
which yield a set of the infinite-volume ground states?
Clearly, the low energy limit of a low-energy excitation above an infinite-volume ground state
is degenerate to the infinite-volume ground state.
The excitation spectrum for low energy states above the ground state has been often computed
by using trial wavefunctions within Bijl-Feynman single-mode approximation
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Such a trial state is not guaranteed to be orthogonal to the sector of the infinite-volume ground states.

In order to avoid this difficulty, we can use a spectral projection which is defined by the time evolution of
local operators
[
11
,
12
]
for the purpose of obtaining the spectrum of the low-energy excitations above the infinite-volume
ground state with symmetry breaking. By the spectral projection, the excitation energies of a trial state
are restricted to strictly positive energies in the infinite-volume limit.

In this paper, we study the energy-momentum relations of the Nambu-Goldstone modes
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
in
quantum antiferromagnetic Heisenberg models on a
d
-dimensional hypercubic lattice with
d\geq 2
.
As a result, we prove that the Nambu-Goldstone modes show a linear dispersion relation for the small momenta,
i.e., the low energies of the Nambu-Goldstone excitations are proportional to the momentum of the spin wave
above the infinite-volume ground states with symmetry breaking.
Our method relies on the upper bounds for the susceptibilities which are derived from
the reflection positivity
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
of the quantum Heisenberg antiferromagnets.
The method is also applicable to other systems when the corresponding upper bounds
for the susceptibilities are justified.
The present work is a sequel to the previous series
[
3
,
4
,
20
,
21
]

about the quantum Heisenberg antiferromagnets.

The present paper is organized as follows: In the next Section
2
, the precise definition of
the quantum antiferromagnetic Heisenberg models and the main Theorem
2.1
are given.
Section
3
is devoted to a preliminary for giving the proof of the main Theorem
2.1
.
In Sec.
4
, we recall the standard thermodynamic formalism
[
11
,
12
]

to deal with excitation energies above infinite-volume ground states.
If a reader is familiar with the formalism or is not interested in the mathematical justification,
then one can skip Sec.
4
. The proof of the main Theorem
2.1
is given in Sec.
5
.
In Sec.
6
, we present a different type Nambu-Goldstone mode from that in the main Theorem
2.1
.
The set of these two types of the excitations is an analogue of the des Cloizeaux-Pearson mode
[
22
]
in
the one-dimensional quantum Heisenberg antiferromagnet.
Appendices
A
and
B
are devoted to technical estimates.

2
Model and result

We consider quantum Heisenberg antiferromagnets which have reflection positivity
[
17
,
19
]
.
The extension of our method to anisotropic antiferromagnets is relatively straightforward
[
23
,
24
]
.
More precisely, we can treat the Hamiltonian
H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
of (
2.2
) below with an additional Ising term.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite subset of the
d
-dimensional hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, i.e.,
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
with
d\geq 1
. For each site
x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},\ldots,x^{(d)})\in\Gamma
,
we associate three component quantum spin operator
{\bf S}_{x}=(S_{x}^{(1)},S_{x}^{(2)},S_{x}^{(3)})

with magnitude of spin,
S=1/2,1,3/2,2,\ldots
. More precisely, the spin operators,
S_{x}^{(1)},S_{x}^{(2)},S_{x}^{(3)}
,
are
(2S+1)\times(2S+1)
matrices at the site
x
. They satisfy the commutation relations,

[S_{x}^{(1)},S_{x}^{(2)}]=iS_{x}^{(3)},\quad[S_{x}^{(2)},S_{x}^{(3)}]=iS_{x}^{(1)},\quad\mbox{and}\quad[S_{x}^{(3)},S_{x}^{(1)}]=iS_{x}^{(2)},

and
(S_{x}^{(1)})^{2}+(S_{x}^{(2)})^{2}+(S_{x}^{(3)})^{2}=S(S+1)
for
x\in\Gamma
.
For the finite lattice
\Gamma
, the whole Hilbert space is given by

\mathfrak{H}_{\Gamma}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}^{2S+1}.

More generally, the algebra of observables on
\mathfrak{H}_{\Gamma}
is given by

\mathfrak{A}_{\Gamma}:=\bigotimes_{x\in\Gamma}M_{2S+1}(\mathbb{C}),

where
M_{2S+1}(\mathbb{C})
is the algebra of
(2S+1)\times(2S+1)
complex matrices.
When two finite lattices,
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
, satisfy
\Gamma_{1}\subset\Gamma_{2}
,
the algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\Gamma_{1}}
is embedded in
\mathfrak{A}_{\Gamma_{2}}
by
the tensor product
\mathfrak{A}_{\Gamma_{1}}\otimes I_{\Gamma_{2}\backslash\Gamma_{1}}\subset\mathfrak{A}_{\Gamma_{2}}
with the identity
I_{\Gamma_{2}\backslash\Gamma_{1}}
.
The local algebra is given by

\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}=\bigcup_{\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\Gamma|<\infty}\mathfrak{A}_{\Gamma},

where
|\Gamma|
is the number of the sites in the finite lattice
\Gamma
.
The quasi-local algebra is defined by the completion of the local algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}

in the sense of the operator-norm topology.

Consider a
d
-dimensional finite hypercubic lattice,

\Lambda:=\{-L+1,-L+2,\ldots,-1,0,1,\ldots,L-1,L\}^{d}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d},

(2.1)

with a large positive integer
L
and
d\geq 2
.
The Hamiltonian of the quantum antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model on the lattice
\Lambda
is given by

H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}:=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda:|x-y|=1}[S_{x}^{(1)}S_{y}^{(1)}+S_{x}^{(2)}S_{y}^{(2)}+S_{x}^{(3)}S_{y}^{(3)}]-B\sum_{x\in\Lambda}(-1)^{x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}+\cdots+x^{(d)}}S_{x}^{(1)},

(2.2)

where we impose the periodic boundary condition so that the system has the translational invariance in
all the directions with the period
2
, and
B\geq 0
. Further, in the following, we assume
d\geq 2

because the phase transitions do not occur in the one-dimensional system
[
25
,
26
]
.

To begin with, we show that the ground state of the Hamiltonian
H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
is unique for
B>0
.
Following
[
27
]
, we introduce a unitary transformation,

U^{(\Lambda)}:=\prod_{x\;:\;x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}+\cdots+x^{(d)}=\mbox{\footnotesize odd}}\exp[i\pi S_{x}^{(3)}].

(2.3)

One obtains

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle[U^{(\Lambda)}]^{\ast}H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}U^{(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda:|x-y|=1}\left\{-\frac{1}{2}[S_{x}^{(+)}S_{y}^{(-)}+S_{x}^{(-)}S_{y}^{(+)}]+S_{x}^{(3)}S_{y}^{(3)}]\right\}-\frac{B}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}[S_{x}^{(+)}+S_{x}^{(-)}],

where
S_{x}^{(\pm)}:=S_{x}^{(1)}\pm iS_{x}^{(2)}
. Clearly, this Hamiltonian has the translational invariance with the period
1

in contrast to
H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
.
Therefore, the ground state
\tilde{\Phi}_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
of
this Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
is unique with the momentum zero for
B>0

by the Perron-Frobenius theorem (See, e.g., Appendix A.4.1 in the book
[
28
]
).
We write
\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
for the ground-state vector of the Hamiltonian
H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
of (
2.2
)
with the normalization
\|\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\|=1
. Then, one has

\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}=U^{(\Lambda)}\tilde{\Phi}_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}.

(2.5)

The infinite-volume ground state is given by

\omega_{0}(\cdots):=\mbox{weak}^{\ast}\mbox{-}\lim_{B\searrow 0}\mbox{weak}^{\ast}\mbox{-}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},(\cdots)\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\right\rangle

(2.6)

with the infinitesimally weak field
B
for the symmetry breaking.

Consider an observable
a^{(n)}
which is given by

a^{(n)}=\sum_{x}f^{(n)}(x)S_{x}^{(2)},

(2.7)

where the complex-valued function
f^{(n)}(x)
has a compact support, and satisfies

f^{(n)}(x)\rightarrow f(x)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for a function,

f(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}dk^{(1)}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}dk^{(2)}\cdots\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}dk^{(d)}\hat{f}(k)e^{ikx},

(2.8)

with a complex-valued function
\hat{f}(k)
of the momentum
k=(k^{(1)},k^{(2)},\ldots,k^{(d)})
.
Here,
kx
denotes the inner product
kx=k^{(1)}x^{(1)}+k^{(2)}x^{(2)}+\cdots+k^{(d)}x^{(d)}
.
We take the function
\hat{f}(k)
to be infinitely differentiable so that the function
f(x)
in the infinite-volume limit
rapidly decays with distance from the origin
x=0
. More specifically, we choose the function
\hat{f}
such that

{\rm supp}\;\hat{f}\subset\{k\;|\;{|p|}/{2}\leq|k|\leq|p|\}

(2.9)

with a momentum
p
satisfying
0<|p|<\kappa
with a small
\kappa>0
. Namely, the wavepacket has
the small momenta whose strengths are all nearly equal to
|p|
.

Next, in order to introduce an energy cutoff, we consider the time evolution
[
29
]
of local
operator
a^{(n)}
,

\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)}):=\exp[iH_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}t]a^{(n)}\exp[-iH_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}t],

and

\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)}):=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)}),

where the function
g
is the Fourier transform of an infinitely differentiable function
\hat{g}

with a compact support, i.e.,
\hat{g}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
We choose the real-valued function
\hat{g}

to satisfy
{\rm supp}\;\hat{g}\subseteq(\epsilon,\gamma)
with two positive parameters,
\epsilon
and
\gamma
,
which satisfy
0<\epsilon<\gamma
. Write
[
29
]

\tau_{\ast g,0}(a):=\lim_{n\nearrow\infty}\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)}),

where
a=\lim_{n\nearrow\infty}a^{(n)}
and the sequences in the double limit about
\Lambda
and
B

are taken to be the same as those in the ground state
\omega_{0}
of (
2.6
).
However, this converges to the desired value
\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)
, irrespective of the subsequences
[
30
]
.
In passing, we note the following: The observable
a
is given by

a=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x)S_{x}^{(2)},

(2.10)

from (
2.7
). Since the function
f(x)
of (
2.8
) has the momentum distribution (
2.9
),
the observable
a
is interpreted as a wavepacket operator of the spin wave with small momenta
whose strengths are nearly equal to
|p|
.

Our aim is to estimate the energy of the excitation above the infinite-volume ground state which is given by

\Delta E:=\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)]^{\ast}[H_{0,0}^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)])}{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,0}(a))}\geq\epsilon,

(2.11)

where
H_{0,0}^{(\Lambda)}
is given by
H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
with
B=0
.
The lower bound in the right-hand side is obtained from the definition of the function
\hat{g}

which gives a projection onto the subspace with the energy interval
[\epsilon,\gamma]
.
More specifically, we choose the real-valued function
\hat{g}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that it satisfies the following conditions:

0\leq\hat{g}(E)\leq 1\ \mbox{for}\ \mbox{all}\ E\in\mathbb{R},\quad\hat{g}(E)=1\ \mbox{for}\ 2\epsilon\leq E\leq\gamma-\Delta\gamma,

(2.12)

and

\hat{g}(E)=0\ \mbox{for}\ E\leq\epsilon\ \mbox{or}\ E\geq\gamma

(2.13)

with a constant
\Delta\gamma>0
satisfying
2\epsilon<\gamma-\Delta\gamma
.

The staggered magnetization is given by

m_{B}^{(\Lambda)}:=\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}(-1)^{x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}+\cdots+x^{(d)}}\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},S_{x}^{(1)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle.

(2.14)

The spontaneous magnetization
m_{\rm s}
in the infinite-volume limit is given by

m_{\rm s}:=\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}m_{B}^{(\Lambda)}.

(2.15)

We write

\omega_{\rm wp}(\cdots):=\frac{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)]^{\ast}(\cdots)\tau_{\ast g,0}(a))}{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,0}(a))}

for short.

Theorem 2.1

Suppose that the spontaneous magnetization
m_{\rm s}
is non-vanishing, i.e.,
m_{\rm s}>0
.
Then, there exist positive constants,
v_{\rm min}
and
v_{\rm max}
, such that
the excitation energy
\Delta E
of the wavepacket
\omega_{\rm wp}
above the infinite-volume ground state
\omega_{0}
satisfies

v_{\rm min}|p|\leq\Delta E\leq v_{\rm max}|p|

(2.16)

with the strength
|p|
of the momentum
p
which is the approximate momentum of the wavepacket with a compact,
narrow distribution (
2.9
) of
\hat{f}
in the momentum space.

Remark:
(i) The excitation in Theorem
2.1
has the momenta around the zero momentum
p=0
.
We can also construct a low energy excitation around the momentum
Q=(\pi,\pi,\ldots,\pi)
,
as will show in Sec.
6
.
The set of these two types of the excitations is an analogue of the des Cloizeaux-Pearson mode
[
22
]
in
the one-dimensional quantum Heisenberg antiferromagnet.

(ii) Our method relies on the upper bounds for the susceptibilities which are derived from
the reflection positivity
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. For general models, some kind of boundedness about susceptibilities is needed
for the existence of a gapless mode. In fact, the following model is very instructive:
Let us consider two spins,
{\bf S}_{1}
and
{\bf S}_{2}
, with
S=1/2
. The Hamiltonian is given by

H_{B}=-BS_{1}^{(3)}-S_{2}^{(3)},

where
B
is the symmetry breaking field.
When
B=0
, the spin
{\bf S}_{1}
is free, and the ground state is two-fold degenerate.
The ground state exhibits symmetry breaking for the continuous symmetry of the Hamiltonian
H_{B=0}
in the limit
B\searrow 0
.
However, there exists the non-zero spectral gap above the ground state.
Of course, some of the susceptibilities are divergent.

3
Preliminary

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we prepare some tools.

From (
2.8
), one has

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x)S_{x}^{(i)}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}dk^{(1)}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}dk^{(2)}\cdots\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}dk^{(d)}\hat{f}(k)e^{ikx}S_{x}^{(i)}

(3.1)

for
i=1,2,3
. In particular,

a=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x)S_{x}^{(2)}

(3.2)

These are nothing but the spin-wave operators with the weight
\hat{f}(k)
for the momentum
k
.
We write

\hat{S}_{k}^{(i)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{x}e^{ikx}S_{x}^{(i)}

for
i=1,2,3
, and

a_{\Lambda}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{k}\hat{f}(k)\hat{S}_{k}^{(2)}.

(3.3)

From the symmetry of the Hamiltonian
H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
, one has

\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{k}^{(i)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle=0\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ i=2,3.

Therefore, the reflection positivity
[
17
]
yields the following bounds in the zero temperature limit
[
18
]
:

\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{-k}^{(i)}\frac{1-P_{0}}{H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}}\hat{S}_{k}^{(i)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2\mathcal{E}_{k+Q}}

(3.4)

for
i=2,3
, where
P_{0}
is the projection onto the ground state
\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}

with the energy eigenvalue
E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}
,
the momentum
Q
is given by
Q=(\pi,\pi,\ldots,\pi)
, and

\mathcal{E}_{k}=d-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos k^{(i)}.

In the following, we will often use the inequality,

\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},A^{\ast}[H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}]A\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle\leq\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[A^{\ast},[H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},A]]\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle,

(3.5)

for any operator
A
. Further, the following formula is useful:

\displaystyle\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(A)\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(A)\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\exp[iH_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}t]A\exp[-iH_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}t]\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\exp[i(H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)})t]A\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{g}(H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)})A\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}

for any local observable
A
.

4
Excitation energies in the infinite-volume limit

In this section, we recall the standard thermodynamic formalism
[
11
,
12
]
to deal with excitation energies above
infinite-volume ground states. If a reader is familiar with the formalism or is not interested in
the mathematical justification, then one can skip this section.

Since the function
\hat{f}(k)
in (
2.8
) is infinitely differentiable,
the function
f(x)
in (
3.1
) decays by power law with distance and with a desired large power.
Therefore, we can approximate the function
f(x)
by a function
f^{(n)}(x)
with a compact support
such that
f^{(n)}(x)\rightarrow f(x)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Then, the Fourier transform
\hat{f}^{(n)}(k)
of
f^{(n)}(x)
converges to
\hat{f}(k)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
In the following, we assume
{\rm supp}\;f^{(n)}\subset\Lambda
.
Namely, we take the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
after taking the limit
\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Our aim of this section is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 4.1

The following relation is valid:

\lim_{\Gamma\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)]^{\ast}[H_{0,0}^{(\Gamma)},\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)])}{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a)]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,0}(a))}=\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})]^{\ast}[H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})]\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle},

(4.1)

where the observable
a_{\Lambda}
for the finite lattice
\Lambda
is given by

a_{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{k}\hat{f}(k)\hat{S}_{k}^{(2)}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\hat{S}_{k}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}e^{ikx}S_{x}^{(2)}.

(4.2)

Here, if necessary, the limits are taken to be a subsequence.

In passing, we stress the following: Write

f_{\Lambda}(x):=\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{k}\hat{f}(k)e^{ikx}.

Then, this function
f_{\Lambda}(x)
is not necessarily equal to
f(x)
which is given by (
2.8
).
Of course, one has
f_{\Lambda}(x)\rightarrow f(x)
as
\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

We first prove the following proposition:

Proposition 4.2

The following relation holds:

\displaystyle\lim_{\Gamma\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a^{(n)})]^{\ast}[H_{0,0}^{(\Gamma)},\tau_{\ast g,0}(a^{(n)})])}{\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a^{(n)})]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,0}(a^{(n)}))}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})]^{\ast}[H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})]\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle}.

After proving this proposition, we will take the limit
n\nearrow\infty
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
:

The observable
a^{(n)}
is given by (
2.7
), i.e.,

a^{(n)}=\sum_{x}f^{(n)}(x)S_{x}^{(2)}

(4.4)

with the above approximate function
f^{(n)}
with a compact support. In the following, we require

\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle>0

(4.5)

for a sufficiently large
n
. In the next Sec.
5
, we will show

\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle>0.

Therefore, we will show

\displaystyle\lim_{n\nearrow\infty}\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a^{(n)})\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{B\searrow 0}\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},[\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})]^{\ast}\tau_{\ast g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{\Lambda})\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle

in the proof of Theorem
4.1
below.

Since the right-hand side of (
4.4
) is a finite sum,
it is enough to consider the quantity,
\omega_{0}([\tau_{\ast g,0}(a_{y}^{(n)})]^{\ast}[H_{0,0}^{(\Gamma)},\tau_{\ast g,0}(a_{x}^{(n)})])
,
for
x,y\in{\rm supp}f^{(n)}
, where we have written
a_{x}^{(n)}:=f^{(n)}(x)S_{x}^{(2)}
.

\displaystyle\sqrt{\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{Q-k}^{(3)}\frac{1}{H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}}\hat{S}_{Q+k}^{(3)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle}

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{k+Q}\mathcal{E}_{k}}}

When the corresponding observable

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{a}_{\Lambda}^{\ast}[H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}][\hat{g}(H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)})]^{2}\tilde{a}_{\Lambda}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle

\displaystyle\sqrt{\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{-k+Q}^{(2)}P(2\epsilon,\infty)\hat{S}_{k+Q}^{(2)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sqrt{\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{-k}^{(3)}P(2\epsilon,\infty)\hat{S}_{k}^{(3)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle}.

Further, similarly,

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{-k+Q}^{(2)}P(2\epsilon,\infty)\hat{S}_{k+Q}^{(2)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{-k+Q}^{(2)}[\hat{g}(H_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}-E_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)})]^{2}\hat{S}_{k+Q}^{(2)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{-k+Q}^{(2)}P[\gamma-\Delta\gamma,\infty)\hat{S}_{k+Q}^{(2)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)},\hat{S}_{-k}^{(3)}P(2\epsilon,\infty)\hat{S}_{k}^{(3)}\Phi_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}\rangle

Appendix A
Quasi-locality of the time evolution
\tau_{*g,B}

Following
[
32
]
, we decompose the time evolution
\tau_{*g,B}(\cdots)
into a sum of local operators.

The present system satisfies the following Lieb-Robinson bound
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
for any operator
b
:

\left\|\left[\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}),b\right]\right\|\leq K_{1}\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|\|b\|\sum_{y\in{\rm supp}\;b}e^{v|t|}e^{-\alpha|x-y|},

(A.1)

where
K_{1},v
and
\alpha
are some positive constants. We write
\Pi_{X}
for
the partial trace over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{X^{c}}
on the complement
X^{c}
of the sublattice
X
, i.e.,

\Pi_{X}(b):=\frac{1}{{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{X^{c}}}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{X^{c}}}b

(A.2)

for any operator
b
. Then, the Lieb-Robinson bound (
A.1
) yields
[
31
,
32
,
33
]

\left\|\Pi_{X}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right\|\leq 2K_{1}\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|\sum_{y\in X^{c}}e^{v|t|}e^{-\alpha|x-y|}.

(A.3)

Following
[
32
]
, we set

X_{m}(x):=\{y\;|\;|x-y|\leq m\}\quad\mbox{for}\ \ m=0,1,2,\ldots,

(A.4)

and

\Delta_{0}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}):=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\cdot\Pi_{X_{0}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})),

(A.5)

and

\Delta_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}):=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\left[\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\Pi_{X_{m-1}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]

(A.6)

for
m\geq 1
. Then, one has

\tau_{*g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\cdot\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}).

(A.7)

The summand satisfies

\left\|\Delta_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right\|\leq K_{2}\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|\frac{1}{(m+1)^{\beta}},

(A.8)

where
K_{2}
and
\beta
are positive constants.
Since the positive exponent
\beta
is determined by the decay of the function
g(t)
for large
|t|
,
we can take
\beta
to be sufficiently large compared to the dimension
d
of the lattice
\Lambda
.
In addition, one has
{\rm supp}\;\Delta_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\subset X_{m}(x)
by definition.
Therefore, the quantity
\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}[H_{0,0}^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{*g,0}(a_{x}^{(n)})]
exists
and the norm is bounded because of the existence of
\tau_{*g,B}(\cdots)
and of the continuity

\tau_{*g,0}(\cdots)=\lim_{B\searrow 0}\tau_{*g,B}(\cdots)
(See Appendix
B
).

Let us give the proof of the bound (
A.8
). Note that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\left[\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\leq T}dt\;g(t)\left[\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{|t|>T}dt\;g(t)\left[\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]

for any
T>0
. By using the bound (
A.3
), the first term in the right-hand side can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{|t|\leq T}dt\;g(t)\left[\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\leq T}dt\;|g(t)|\left\|\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2K_{1}\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|\int_{|t|\leq T}dt\;|g(t)|\sum_{y\in X_{m}(x)^{c}}e^{vT}e^{-\alpha|x-y|}\leq K_{3}\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|e^{vT}e^{-\alpha m},

where
K_{3}
is a positive constant. As to the second term, one has

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{|t|>T}dt\;g(t)\left[\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|t|>T}dt\;|g(t)|\cdot 2\|a_{x}^{(n)})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{4}\|a_{x}^{(n)})\|\frac{1}{T^{\beta}},

where
K_{4}
and
\beta
are positive constants, and we have used that the function
g(t)
decays by power law for a large
|t|
.
By choosing
T=\alpha(m+1)/(2v)
, we have

\left\|\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\left[\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]\right\|\leq{\rm Const.}\|a_{x}^{(n)})\|\frac{1}{(m+1)^{\beta}}.

(A.9)

From this bound, one can obtain the desired bound (
A.8
).

Appendix B
Continuity of the time evolution
\tau_{*g,B}

In this appendix, we prove the inequality (
B.8
) below.

Note that

\displaystyle\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})-\tau_{t,0}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{iH_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}t}a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,B}^{(\Lambda)}t}-e^{iH_{0,0}^{(\Lambda)}t}a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,0}^{(\Lambda)}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}du\frac{d}{du}e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}du\left\{\left[\frac{d}{du}e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}\right]a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}+e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}a_{x}^{(n)}\frac{d}{du}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}\right\}.

The derivative in the right-hand side is calculated as follows:

\frac{d}{du}\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}=it\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}O^{(\Lambda)}e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t(1-w)},

where we have written

O^{(\Lambda)}:=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}(-1)^{x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}+\cdots+x^{(d)}}S_{x}^{(1)}.

Using this formula, the above integrand is computed as

\displaystyle\left[\frac{d}{du}e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}\right]a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}+e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}a_{x}^{(n)}\frac{d}{du}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle it\int_{0}^{1}dw\left[e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}O^{(\Lambda)}e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t(1-w)}a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}-e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t}a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t(1-w)}O^{(\Lambda)}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle it\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t(1-w)}a_{x}^{(n)}e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}t(1-w)}\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle it\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}

From these observations, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau_{*g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})-\tau_{*g,0}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})

(B.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;g(t)\left[\tau_{t,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})-\tau_{t,0}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}.

In order to estimate the right-hand side, we set

\tilde{\Delta}_{0}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})):=\Pi_{X_{0}(x)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})),

and

\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})):=\Pi_{X_{m}(x)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))-\Pi_{X_{m-1}(x)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))

for
m\geq 1
. Then, one has

\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})).

Substituting this into the right-hand side of above (
B.1
), we have

\displaystyle i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}.

This summand for
m\geq 1
is written

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}

(B.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\leq T}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{|t|>T}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}.

Since the support of the operator
\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))
is contained in
the set
X_{m}(x)
by definition, we have

\left\|\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]\right\|\leq\tilde{K}_{1}m^{d}\left\|\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right\|,

(B.4)

where
\tilde{K}_{1}
is a positive constant which depends on the dimension
d
of the lattice
\Lambda
.

Consider first the first term in the right-hand side of (
B.3
).
Similarly to the preceding Appendix
A
, one has

\left\|\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right\|\leq\tilde{K}_{2}\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|e^{vT}e^{-\alpha m}

(B.5)

with a positive constant
\tilde{K}_{2}
for
|t|\leq T
. Combining this with the above bound (
B.4
),
the first term in the right-hand side of (
B.3
) can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{|t|\leq T}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\right\|

(B.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{3}|B|\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|m^{d}e^{vT}e^{-\alpha m}

with a positive constant
\tilde{K}_{3}
.

For the second term in the right-hand side of (
B.3
), we use the inequality,

\int_{|t|>T}dt\;|t||g(t)|\leq{\rm Const.}\frac{1}{T^{\tilde{\beta}}},

with a positive constant
\tilde{\beta}
. This holds, and we can take a sufficiently large
\tilde{\beta}

because of the assumption of the infinite differentiability of the function
\hat{g}
.
Combining this inequality with the above bound (
B.4
), one has

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{|t|>T}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{4}\frac{1}{T^{\tilde{\beta}}}|B|m^{d}\left\|\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right\|\leq 2\tilde{K}_{4}|B|\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|\frac{m^{d}}{T^{\tilde{\beta}}}

with a positive constant
\tilde{K}_{4}
. By choosing
T=\alpha(m+1)/(2v)
, we consequently obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tilde{\Delta}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)}))\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{5}|B|\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|\frac{1}{(m+1)^{\tilde{\beta}-d}}

with a positive constant
\tilde{K}_{5}
. Substituting this into (
B
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\;tg(t)\int_{0}^{B}du\;\int_{0}^{1}dw\;e^{iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\left[O^{(\Lambda)},\tau_{t(1-w),u}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right]e^{-iH_{0,u}^{(\Lambda)}tw}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{5}|B|\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(m+1)^{\tilde{\beta}-d}}\leq\tilde{K}_{6}|B|\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|

with a positive constant
\tilde{K}_{6}
, where we have chosen
\tilde{\beta}
so that

\tilde{\beta}-d>1
. Therefore, from (
B.1
), we obtain

\left\|\tau_{*g,B}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})-\tau_{*g,0}^{(\Lambda)}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right\|\leq\tilde{K}_{6}|B|\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|.

(B.7)

This is the desired continuity at
B=0
with respect to
B
. Since the constant
\tilde{K}_{6}
does not depend on
the lattice
\Lambda
, one has

\left\|\tau_{*g,B}(a_{x}^{(n)})-\tau_{*g,0}(a_{x}^{(n)})\right\|\leq\tilde{K}_{6}|B|\|a_{x}^{(n)}\|.

(B.8)

in the infinite-volume limit.

Chapter 2
Conventional Optimization

To begin with, imaging we observe a corrupted set of measurements
\bm{y}
of an image
\bm{x}
under a linear measurement operator
\bm{\Phi}
with some noise
{\bm{e}}
according to

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{e},

(2.1)

Suppose we have a known regularization function
R
that could be applied to an image x. Then we could compute an image estimate
\hat{x}
by solving the optimization problem

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{x}}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\mathbf{x}}\frac{1}{2}||\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\mathbf{x}).

(2.2)

2.1
Gradient Descent

If
R
is differentiable, this can be accomplished via gradient descent. We could get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{x}}[\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{x})]\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\cdot\bm{\Phi}^{T}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x})+\frac{\partial{R(\bm{x})}}{\partial\bm{x}}\\
=&-\bm{\Phi}^{T}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x})+\bigtriangledown R(\bm{x}).\end{aligned}

(2.3)

That is, we start with an initial estimate
\bm{x}^{(0)}
such as
\bm{x}^{(0)}=\bm{\Phi}^{T}\bm{y}
and choose a step size
\alpha>0
, such that for iteration
k=1,2,3...
, we set

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{x}^{(k+1)}&=\bm{x}^{(k)}-\alpha[-\bm{\Phi}^{T}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x})+\bigtriangledown R(\bm{x})]\\
&=\bm{x}^{(k)}+\alpha\bm{\Phi}^{T}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x})-\alpha\bigtriangledown R(\bm{x}).\end{aligned}

(2.4)

2.2
ADMM: Alternating Directions Method of Multipliers

By introducing an auxiliary parameter
\bm{v}
, the unconstrained optimization in Eq.
2.2
can be converted into

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\hat{\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{v}})=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x},\bm{v}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v}),\\
s.t.\ \bm{x}=\bm{v}.\end{aligned}

(2.5)

2.2.1
Augmented Lagrangian Method

Using augmented Lagrangian method, we introduce an parameter
{\bm{u}}
to be updated and another
\rho
to be manually set, and get the augmented Lagrangian function of Eq.
2.5
as

\displaystyle L_{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{v},{\bm{u}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+{\bm{u}}^{T}(\bm{x}-\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}||_{2}^{2}

(2.6)

To facilitate updates of
\bm{x}
, we rewrite Eq.
2.6
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} L_{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{v},{\bm{u}})&=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{x}-{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{v}+\frac{\rho}{2}(\bm{x}^{T}\bm{x}-2\cdot\bm{x}^{T}\bm{v}+\bm{v}^{T}\bm{v})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}[\bm{x}^{T}\bm{x}-2(\bm{v}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})\bm{x}+(\bm{v}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})^{2}-(\bm{v}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})^{2}]-{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{v}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-(\bm{v}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}(\bm{v}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})^{2}-{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{v}.\end{aligned}

(2.7)

Similarly, to facilitate updates of
\bm{v}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} L_{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{v},{\bm{u}})&=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{x}-{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{v}+\frac{\rho}{2}(\bm{v}^{T}\bm{v}-2\cdot\bm{x}^{T}\bm{v}+\bm{x}^{T}\bm{x})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}[\bm{v}^{T}\bm{v}-2(\bm{x}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})\bm{v}+(\bm{x}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})^{2}-(\bm{x}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})^{2}]+{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{x}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\Phi\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{v}-(\bm{x}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}(\bm{x}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}})^{2}+{\bm{u}}^{T}\bm{x}.\end{aligned}

(2.8)

Then ADMM solves it by the following sequence of sub-problems:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{x}^{(k+1)}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x}}L_{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{v}^{k},{\bm{u}}^{k})\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v}^{k})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-(\bm{v}^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{k})||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}(\bm{v}^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{k})^{2}-{{\bm{u}}^{k}}^{T}\bm{v}^{k}\quad(Eq.\ref{eq:lag1})\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-(\bm{v}^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{k})||_{2}^{2}\quad(delete\ terms\ not\ related\ to\ \bm{x}),\end{aligned}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{v}^{(k+1)}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{v}}L_{\rho}(\bm{x}^{k},\bm{v},{\bm{u}}^{k})\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{v}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{v}-(\bm{x}^{k}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{k})||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}(\bm{x}^{k}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{k})^{2}+{{\bm{u}}^{k}}^{T}\bm{x}\quad(Eq.\ref{eq:lag2})\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{v}}R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{v}-(\bm{x}^{k}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{k})||_{2}^{2}\quad(delete\ terms\ not\ related\ to\ \bm{v}),\end{aligned}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\bm{u}}^{(k+1)}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{{\bm{u}}}L_{\rho}(\bm{x}^{k},\bm{v}^{k},{\bm{u}})\\
&={\bm{u}}^{k}+\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{u}}}L_{\rho}(\bm{x}^{(k+1)},\bm{v}^{(k+1)},{\bm{u}})\quad(gradient\ descent)\\
&={\bm{u}}^{k}+\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{u}}}(\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}^{(k+1)}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v}^{(k+1)})+{\bm{u}}^{T}(\bm{x}^{(k+1)}-\bm{v}^{(k+1)})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}^{(k+1)}-\bm{v}^{(k+1)}||_{2}^{2})\\
&={\bm{u}}^{k}+\alpha(\bm{x}^{(k+1)}-\bm{v}^{(k+1)})\\
&={\bm{u}}^{k}+\rho(\bm{x}^{(k+1)}-\bm{v}^{(k+1)})\quad(set\ \alpha=\rho).\end{aligned}

(2.11)

2.2.2
Scaled Form

ADMM can be written in a slightly different form, which is often more convenient, by combining the linear and quadratic terms in the augmented Lagrangian and scaling the dual variable.
Defining the residual
{\bm{r}}=\bm{x}-\bm{v}
and recall Eq.
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\bm{u}}^{T}(\bm{x}-\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}||_{2}^{2}&={\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{r}}+\frac{\rho}{2}||{\bm{r}}||_{2}^{2}\\
&=\frac{\rho}{2}{\bm{r}}^{T}{\bm{r}}+{\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{r}}\\
&=\frac{\rho}{2}({\bm{r}}^{T}{\bm{r}}+2\cdot\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}{\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{u}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}{\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{u}})\\
&=\frac{\rho}{2}({\bm{r}}^{T}{\bm{r}}+2\cdot\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{u}})-\frac{1}{2\rho}{\bm{u}}^{T}{\bm{u}}\\
&=\frac{\rho}{2}||{\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}||_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\rho}||{\bm{u}}||_{2}^{2}\\
&=\frac{\rho}{2}||{\bm{r}}+\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}\quad(\bm{w}=\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}),\end{aligned}

(2.12)

where
\bm{w}=\frac{1}{\rho}{\bm{u}}
is the scaled dual variable. Using the scaled dual variable, we can rewrite Eq.
2.6
as

\displaystyle L_{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{v},\bm{w})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}+\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}

(2.13)

Then we can express ADMM as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{x}^{(k+1)}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x}}L_{\rho}(\bm{x},\bm{v}^{k},\bm{w}^{k})\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}+\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}+\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}\quad(delete\ terms\ not\ related\ to\ \bm{x}),\end{aligned}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{v}^{(k+1)}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{v}}L_{\rho}(\bm{x}^{k},\bm{v},\bm{w}^{k})\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{v}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}||_{2}^{2}+R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}+\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}\\
&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{v}}R(\bm{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}+\bm{w}||_{2}^{2}\quad(delete\ terms\ not\ related\ to\ \bm{v}),\end{aligned}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{w}^{(k+1)}&=\bm{w}^{k}+\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{w}}L_{\rho}(\bm{x}^{(k+1)},\bm{v}^{(k+1)},\bm{w})\quad(gradient\ descent)\\
&=\bm{w}^{k}+\alpha[\rho(\bm{x}^{(k+1)}-\bm{v}^{(k+1)}+\bm{w})-\rho\bm{w}]\\
&=\bm{w}^{k}+\alpha\rho(\bm{x}^{(k+1)}-\bm{v}^{(k+1)})\\
&=\bm{w}^{k}+\bm{x}^{(k+1)}-\bm{v}^{(k+1)}\quad(set\ \alpha\rho=1).\end{aligned}

(2.16)

2.3
GAP: Generalized Alternating
Projection

Generalized alternating projection (GAP) can be recognized as a special case of ADMM, which works as a lower computational workload algorithm. Recall Eq.
2.5
, GAP updates
\bm{x}^{k}
and
\bm{v}^{k}
as follows:

•

Updating
\bm{x}:\bm{x}^{k}
is updated via a Euclidean projection of
\bm{v}^{k}
on the linear manifold
{{\bf M}}:\bm{y}=\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}
. That is,

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(k+1)}=\bm{v}^{k}+{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top})^{-1}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{v}^{k}).

(2.17)

•

Updating
\bm{v}
: After the projection, the goal of the next step is to bring
\bm{x}^{(k+1)}
closer to the desired signal domain. This could be achieved by employing an appropriate trained denoiser
\mathcal{D}_{k+1}
and letting

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{D}_{k+1}(\bm{x}^{(k+1)}).

(2.18)

Derivation of Eq.
2.17
: given
\bm{v}^{k}
, how to obtain the Euclidean projection of
\bm{v}^{k}
on the linear manifold? Since the Euclidean projection is essentially finding the shortest distance between
{{\bf M}}:\bm{y}=\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}
and
\bm{v}
, this problem can be modeled as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{x}}\frac{1}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}||_{2}^{2},&\quad(minimize\ the\ distance\ between\ \bm{x}\ and\ \bm{v})\\
s.t.\ \bm{\Phi}\bm{x}=\bm{y}.&\quad(ensure\ finding\ the\ point\ \bm{x}\ on\ {{\bf M}}\ nearest\ to\ \bm{v})\end{aligned}

(2.19)

Easily we could get the Lagrangian function of Eq.
2.19
by introducing an parameters
\lambda
,

\displaystyle L(\bm{x},\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{x}-\bm{v}||_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}-\bm{y}).

(2.20)

Then the optimal conditions of Eq.
2.20
are:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{x}}L(\bm{x},\lambda)=(\bm{x}-\bm{v})+\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\lambda=0,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}L(\bm{x},\lambda)=\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}-\bm{y}=0.

(2.22)

According to Eq.
2.21
, we have
\bm{x}=\bm{v}-\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\lambda
, combined with
\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}=\bm{y}
, we could get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bm{\Phi}(\bm{v}-\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\lambda)=\bm{y}\\
\Rightarrow\quad&\bm{\Phi}\bm{v}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\lambda=\bm{y}\\
\Rightarrow\quad&-\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\lambda=\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{v}\\
\Rightarrow\quad&\lambda=-(\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top})^{-1}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{v}).\end{aligned}

(2.23)

Combining
\lambda=-(\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top})^{-1}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{v})
with
\bm{x}=\bm{v}-\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\lambda
, we could get

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(k+1)}=\bm{v}^{k}+\bm{\Phi}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top})^{-1}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{v}^{k}).

(2.24)

Chapter 3
Deep Equilibrium Models

3.1
Review of Deep Learning based Algorithms

Given the masks and measurements, plenty of algorithms including conventional optimization
Liu et al., 2018
;
Yang et al., 2015
;
Yang et al., 2014
;
Yuan, 2016
, end-to-end deep learning
Qiao et al., 2020
;
Zheng et al., 2021
;
Wang et al., 2022
;
Cheng et al., 2022
;
Meng and Yuan, 2021
, deep unfolding
Meng et al., 2020
;
Wu et al., 2021
and plug-and-play
Yuan et al., 2020
;
Yuan et al., 2021
;
Wu et al., 2022
;
Yang and Zhao, 2022
are proposed for reconstruction.

To accommodate the state-of-the-art SCI architectures and to enable
low-memory stable
reconstruction, this chapter sets about utilizing deep equilibrium models (DEQ)
Bai et al., 2019
for solving the inverse problem of video SCI. Specifically, we applied DEQ to two existing models for video SCI reconstruction: recurrent neural networks (RNN) and plug-and-play framework (PnP).

Given measurement
\bm{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with compression rate
B
and sensing matrix
\bm{\Phi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times nB}
as input, we consider an optimization iteration or neural network as:

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(k+1)}=f_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{(k)};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi}),\quad k=0,1,\dots,\infty,

(3.1)

where
\theta
denotes the weights of embedded neural networks;
\bm{x}^{(k)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
is the output of the
k^{th}
iterative step or hidden layer, and
\bm{x}^{(0)}=\mathbf{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}\bm{y}
;
f_{\theta}(\cdot\ ;\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
is an iteration map
{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
towards a stable equilibrium:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\bm{x}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}f_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{(k)};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{\bm{x}}=f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi}),

(3.2)

where
\hat{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
denotes the fixed point and reconstruction result.

In following sections, we design different
f_{\theta}
for SCI, in terms of the implicit infinite-depth RNN architecture and infinitely iterative PnP framework. Following
Zhao et al., 2022
;
Gilton et al., 2021
, we utilize Anderson acceleration
Walker and Ni, 2011
to compute the fixed point of
f_{\theta}
efficiently in Sec.
3.2
. Following
Zhao et al., 2022
;
Gilton et al., 2021
, for gradient calculation, we optimize the network wights
\theta
by approximating the inverse Jacobian, described in Sec.
3.3
. Convergence of this scheme for specific
f_{\theta}
designs is discussed in Sec.
3.4
.

3.2
Forward Pass

Unlike the conventional optimization method where the terminal step number is manually chosen or a network where the output is the activation from the limited layers, the result of DEQ is the equilibrium point itself. Therefore, the forward evaluation could be any procedure
that solves for this equilibrium point. Considering SCI reconstruction, we design novel iterative models that converge to equilibrium.

3.2.1
Recurrent Neural Networks

To achieve integration of DEQ and RNN for video SCI, we have:

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(k+1)}={\rm RNN}_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{(k)},\bm{y},\bm{\Phi}),

(3.3)

where
{\rm RNN}(\cdot\ )
is a trainable RNN network learning to iteratively reconstruct effective and stable data. As shown in Fig
3.1
, the corresponding iteration map is:

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})={\rm RNN}_{\theta}(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{\Phi}).

(3.4)

i.e.
, DE-RNN.

3.2.2
Generalized Alternating Projection

Regarding the optimization iterations in the GAP method, represented in Eq. (
2.17
)-(
2.18
), we iteratively update
\bm{x}
by:

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(k+1)}=\textstyle\mathcal{D}^{(k+1)}_{\theta}\left[\bm{x}^{(k)}+{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top})^{-1}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}^{(k)})\right].

(3.5)

Therefore, as illustrated in Fig.
3.2
, the iteration map is:

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})=\textstyle\mathcal{D}_{\theta}(\bm{x}+{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top})^{-1}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{x})).

(3.6)

Figure 3.2:
Illustration of our proposed DEQ for SCI using generalized alternating projection (GAP),
i.e.
, DE-GAP.

3.2.3
Anderson Acceleration

To enforce fixed-point iterations converge more quickly, we make full use of the ability to accelerate inference with standard fixed-point accelerators,
e.g.
, Anderson accelerator. Anderson acceleration utilizes previous iterations to seek promising directions to move forward. Under the setting of Anderson accelerator, we identify a vector
\bm{\alpha}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
, for
\delta>0
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{x}^{(k+1)}=&\textstyle(1-\delta)\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}^{(k)}\bm{x}^{(k-i)}\\
&+\textstyle\delta\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}^{(k)}f_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{(k-i)};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi}),\end{aligned}

(3.7)

where the vector
\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}^{(k)}
is the solution to the optimization problem:

\displaystyle\textstyle\argmin_{\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}}||{\bf A}\bm{\alpha}||_{2}^{2},\quad s.t.\quad\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bm{\alpha}=1,

(3.8)

where
{\bf A}
is a matrix whose
i
-th column is the vectorized residual
f_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{(k-i)};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})-\bm{x}^{(k-i)}
, with
i=0,\dots,s-1
. When
s
is small (
e.g.
,
s=3
), the optimization problem in Eq. (
3.8
) introduces trivial computation.

3.3
Backward Pass

While previous work often utilizes Newton’s method to achieve the equilibrium and then backpropagate through all the Newton iterations, following
Zhao et al., 2022
;
Gilton et al., 2021
, we alternatively adopt another method with high efficiency and constant memory requirement.

3.3.1
Loss Function

To optimize network parameters
\theta
, stochastic gradient descent is used to minimize a loss function as follows:

\displaystyle\textstyle\theta^{*}=\argmin_{\theta}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell(f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}}_{i};\bm{y}_{i},\bm{\Phi}_{i}),\bm{x}^{\star}_{i}),

(3.9)

where
m
is the number of training samples;
\ell(\cdot,\cdot)
is a given loss function,
\bm{x}^{\star}_{i}
is the ground truth 3D data of the
i
-th training sample,
\bm{y}_{i}
is the paired measurement,
\bm{\Phi}_{i}
denotes the sensing matrix, and
f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}}_{i};\bm{y}_{i},\bm{\Phi}_{i})
denotes the reconstruction result given as the fixed point
\hat{\bm{x}}
of the iteration map
f_{\theta}(\cdot\ ;\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
, as derived from Eq. (
3.2
).
The mean-squared error (MSE) loss is used for our video SCI reconstruction:

\displaystyle\textstyle\ell(\hat{\bm{x}},\bm{x}^{\star})=\frac{1}{2}||\hat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}^{\star}||_{2}^{2}.

(3.10)

Since the reconstruction result is a fixed point of the iteration map
f_{\theta}(\cdot\ ;\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
, gradient calculation of this loss term could be designed to avoid large memory demand. Following
Gilton et al., 2021
, we calculate the gradient of the loss term, which takes the network parameters
\theta
into consideration.

3.3.2
Gradient Calculation

Following
Zhao et al., 2022
;
Gilton et al., 2021
, we calculate the loss gradient. Let
\ell
be an abbreviation of
\ell(\hat{\bm{x}},\bm{x}^{\star})
in Eq. (
3.10
), then the loss gradient is:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \textstyle\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\theta}&={\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}{\partial\theta}\right)}^{\top}\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}&={\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}{\partial\theta}\right)}^{\top}{\left(\hat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}^{\star}\right)},\end{aligned}

(3.11)

where
\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}{\partial\theta}
is the Jacobian of
\hat{\bm{x}}
evaluated at
\theta
, and
\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}
is the gradient of
\ell
evaluated at
\bm{x}^{\star}
.

Then to compute the Jacobian
\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}{\partial\theta}
, we recall the fixed point equation
\hat{\bm{x}}=f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
in Eq. (
3.2
). By implicitly differentiating both sides of this fixed point equation, the Jacobian
\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}{\partial\theta}
is solved as:

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{x}}}{\partial\theta}=\left[\bm{I}-\left.\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\bm{x}}\right|_{\bm{x}=\hat{\bm{x}}}\right]^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\theta},

(3.12)

which could be plugged into Eq. (
3.11
) and thus get:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \textstyle\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\theta}=\left[\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\theta}\right]^{\top}\!{\left[\bm{I}-\left.\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\bm{x}}\right|_{\bm{x}=\hat{\bm{x}}}\right]^{-\top}}{(\hat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}^{\star})},\end{aligned}

(3.13)

where
-⊤
denotes the inversion followed by transpose.
As this method converted gradient calculation to the problem of calculating an inverse Jacobian-vector product, it avoids the backpropagation through many iterations of
f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
. To approximate the inverse Jacobian-vector product, we define the vector
\bm{a}^{(\infty)}
as:

\displaystyle\textstyle\bm{a}^{(\infty)}={\left[\bm{I}-\left.\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\bm{x}}\right|_{\bm{x}=\hat{\bm{x}}}\right]^{-\top}}\!{(\hat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}^{\star})}.

(3.14)

Following
Gilton et al., 2021
, it is noted that
\bm{a}^{(\infty)}
is a fixed point of the equation:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{a}^{(k+1)}=\textstyle{\left[\left.\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\bm{x}}\right|_{\bm{x}=\hat{\bm{x}}}\right]^{-\top}}\bm{a}^{(k)}+{(\hat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}^{\star})},\\
\forall k=0,1,\dots,\infty.\end{aligned}

(3.15)

Therefore, the same algorithm used to calculate the fixed point
\hat{\bm{x}}
could also be used to calculate
\bm{a}^{(\infty)}
. The limit of fixed-point iterations for solving Eq. (
3.15
) with initial iterate
\bm{a}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}
is denoted equivalently to the Neumann series:

\displaystyle\bm{a}^{(\infty)}=\textstyle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\left\{{\left[\left.\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\bm{x}}\right|_{\bm{x}=\hat{\bm{x}}}\right]^{\top}}\right\}^{p}\!{(\hat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}^{\star})}.

(3.16)

To quickly calculate the vector-Jacobian products in Eq. (
3.15
) and
Eq. (
3.16
), a lot of auto-differentiation tools (
e.g.
, autograd packages in Pytorch
Paszke et al., 2019
) could be utilized. After the accurate approximation of
\bm{a}^{(\infty)}
is calculated, the gradient in Eq. (
3.11
) is given by:

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\theta}={\left(\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{x}};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})}{\partial\theta}\right)}^{\top}\bm{a}^{(\infty)}.

(3.17)

3.4
Convergence Theory

Given the iteration map
f_{\theta}(\cdot\ ;\bm{y},\bm{\Phi}):{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
, in this section, we discuss conditions that guarantee the convergence of the proposed deep equilibrium models
\bm{x}^{(k+1)}=f_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{(k)};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
to a fixed-point
\hat{\bm{x}}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

{assumption}

(Convergence of DE-RNN)
.
For all
\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
, if there exists a constant
0\leq c<1
satisfies that:

\displaystyle\|{\rm RNN}_{\theta}(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})-{\rm RNN}_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})\|\leq c\|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}\|,

(3.18)

then the DE-RNN iteration map
f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
is contractive.

{assumption}

(Convergence of DE-GAP)
.
For all
\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
, if there exists a
\varepsilon>0
such that the denoiser
\mathcal{D_{\theta}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
satisfies:

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{D}_{\theta}-{\bf I})(\bm{x})-(\mathcal{D}_{\theta}-{\bf I})(\bm{x}^{\prime})\|\leq\varepsilon||\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}||,

(3.19)

where
(\mathcal{D}_{\theta}-{\bf I})(\bm{x}):=\mathcal{D}_{\theta}(\bm{x})-\bm{x}
, that is, we assume the map
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}-{\bf I}
is
\varepsilon
-Lipschitz,
then the DE-GAP iteration map
f_{\theta}(\cdot;\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
defined in Eq. (
3.6
) satisfies:

\displaystyle\textstyle\|f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})-f_{\theta}(\bm{x}^{\prime};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})\|\leq\eta\|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}\|

(3.20)

for all
\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
. The coefficient
\eta
is less than 1, in which case the DE-GAP iteration map
f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
is contractive.

Following
Gilton et al., 2021
, to prove
f_{\theta}(\cdot;\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
is contractive it suffices to show
||\partial_{\bm{x}}f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})||<1
for all
\bm{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
, where
||\cdot||
denotes the spectral norm,
\partial_{\bm{x}}f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
is the Jacobian of
f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})
with respect to
\bm{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
given by:

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{x}}f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})({\bf I}-{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top})^{-1}\bm{\Phi}),

(3.21)

where
\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB\times nB}
is the Jacobian of
\mathcal{D_{\theta}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
with respect to
\bm{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{nB}
.

Finally, we derive (details can be found in
Zhao, 2022
or supplementary material):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} ||\partial_{\bm{x}}f_{\theta}(\bm{x};\bm{y},\bm{\Phi})||=&\ ||\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})({\bf I}-\bm{\Psi})||,\\
=&\ ||\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})-\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})\bm{\Psi}||\\
=&\ ||\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})-{\bf I}+{\bf I}-\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})\bm{\Psi}||\\
\leq&\ ||\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})-{\bf I}||+||{\bf I}-\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})\bm{\Psi}||\\
\leq&\ \varepsilon+1+||(\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})-{\bf I}+{\bf I})\bm{\Psi}||\\
=&\ 1+\varepsilon+||(\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})-{\bf I})\bm{\Psi}+\bm{\Psi}||\\
\leq&\ 1+\varepsilon+||(\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})-{\bf I})\bm{\Psi}||+||\bm{\Psi}||\\
\leq&\ 1+\varepsilon+\varepsilon||\bm{\Psi}||+||\bm{\Psi}||\\
=&\ (1+\varepsilon)(1+||\bm{\Psi}||)\\
\leq&\textstyle(1+\varepsilon)\max_{i}|1-\lambda_{i}|,\end{aligned}

(3.22)

where
\lambda_{i}
are eigenvalues of
{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\top})^{-1}\bm{\Phi}
; and the inequality Eq. (
3.22
) is based on the assumption that the map
(\mathcal{D}_{\theta}-{\bf I})(\bm{x}):=\mathcal{D}_{\theta}(\bm{x})-\bm{x}
is
\varepsilon
-Lipschitz.
Therefore the spectral norm of its Jacobian
\partial_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D_{\theta}}(\bm{x})-{\bf I}
is bounded by
\eta
, which demonstrates
f_{\theta}
is
\eta
-Lipschitz with
\eta=(1+\varepsilon)\max_{i}|1-\lambda_{i}|
.

It is worth noting that convergence is not yet guaranteed in our calculation above since
\eta
is larger than 1. In SCI cases, it is challenging to provide a theoretical guarantee. However, we observe our models converge well in the experiments.

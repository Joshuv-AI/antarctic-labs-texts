Radiative Meson and Glueball Decays in the Witten-Sakai-Sugimoto Model
Florian Hechenberger, Josef Leutgeb, Anton Rebhan

Abstract
We calculate radiative decay rates of mesons and glueballs in the top-down holographic Witten-Sakai-Sugimoto model with finite quark masses. After assessing to what extent this model agrees or disagrees with experimental data, we present its predictions for so far undetermined decay channels. Contrary to widespread expectations, we obtain sizeable two-photon widths of scalar, tensor, and pseudoscalar glueballs, suggesting in particular that the observed two-photon rate of the glueball candidate f 0 ​ ( 1710 ) f_{0}(1710) is not too large to permit a glueball interpretation, but could be even much higher. We also discuss the so-called exotic scalar glueball, which in the Witten-Sakai-Sugimoto model is too broad to match either of the main glueball candidates f 0 ​ ( 1500 ) f_{0}(1500) and f 0 ​ ( 1710 ) f_{0}(1710) , but might be of interest with regard to the alternative scenario of the so-called fragmented scalar glueball. Employing the exotic scalar glueball for the latter, much smaller two-photon rates are predicted for the ground-state glueball despite a larger total width; relatively large two-photon rates would then apply to the excited scalar glueball described by the predominantly dilatonic scalar glueball. In either case, the resulting contributions to the muon g − 2 g-2 from hadronic light-by-light scattering involving glueball exchanges are small compared to other single meson exchanges, of the order of ≲ 10 − 12 \lesssim 10^{-12} .

Because of their sizeable two-photon coupling in the WSS model, we consider also the effect the lightest three glueballs may have as single-meson contributions to hadronic light-by-light scattering, which is an important ingredient of the Standard Model prediction of the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon

III.1
Vector meson dominance

According to the holographic principle, non-normalizable modes are interpreted as external sources.
This permits to study electromagnetic interactions to leading order by setting asymptotic values of the gauge field
A_{\mu}
on the D8 branes according to
Sakai:2005yt

\lim_{z\to\pm\infty}A_{\mu}(x,z)=A_{L,R\mu}(x)=eQA_{\mu}^{\text{em}}(x),

(24)

where
e
is the electromagnetic coupling constant and
Q
is the
electric charge matrix, given as

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2\\
&-1\\
&&-1\end{array}\right)

for the
N_{f}=3
case.
The ansatz (
8
) changes to

\begin{split}A_{\mu}(x^{\mu},z)=&A_{L\mu}(x^{\mu})\psi_{+}(z)+A_{R\mu}(x^{\mu})\psi_{-}(z)\\
&+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}v_{\mu}^{n}(x^{\mu})\psi_{n}(z),\end{split}

(29)

with the functions
\psi_{\pm}(z)
defined as

\psi_{\pm}(z)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\psi_{0}(z)\right),\qquad\psi_{0}(z)\equiv\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan z,

(30)

They satisfy (
11
) as non-normalizable zero modes,
because
\partial_{z}\psi_{\pm}(z)\propto\phi_{0}(z)\propto 1/K(z)
.

To distinguish between vector and axial-vector fields we introduce the
notation

\begin{split}&\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{L\mu}+A_{R\mu}\right),\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{L\mu}-A_{R\mu}\right),\\

so that

\begin{split}A_{\mu}(x^{\mu},z)=&\mathcal{V}_{\mu}(x^{\mu})+\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x^{\mu})\psi_{0}(z)\\

(32)

The first term in (

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2}\int\text{d}z

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!K^{-1/3}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{\mathcal{V}\mathcal{V}}}{2}\text{tr}\left(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{V}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{a_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}}}{2}\text{tr}\left(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\left(\partial_{\mu}v_{\nu}^{n}-\partial_{\nu}v_{\mu}^{n}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\left(\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu}^{n}-\partial_{\nu}a_{\mu}^{n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+a_{\mathcal{A}a^{n}}\left(\left(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\right)\left(\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu}^{n}-\partial_{\nu}a_{\mu}^{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\text{interaction terms}\right),

with coupling constants

mixing the photon field
\mathcal{V}

The photon field
\mathcal{V}

this model and can only couple via the mixing (
34

realizing VMD. Alternatively, it is possible to
perform a field redefinition to diagonalize the action and to get rid of the

IV
Glueballs in the Witten-Sakai-Sugimoto model

Glueballs are realized in the WSS model as fluctuations of the background
in which the probe D8 branes are placed, where certain superselection rules are applied. In particular states with odd parity in the extra circle along
\tau
are discarded, as well as Kaluza-Klein modes of the compact
S^{1}
and
S^{4}
subspaces. The resulting glueball spectrum was
discussed in
Brower:2000rp
, where the lift of (
1
)
to 11-dimensional supergravity is used.
In the following we shall consider
scalar, tensor, and pseudoscalar glueballs,
for which hadronic decays have been worked out
in the WSS model in
Hashimoto:2007ze
;
Brunner:2015oqa
;
Brunner:2015yha
;
Brunner:2015oga
;
Leutgeb:2019lqu
and which we review and update in Appendix
A
in some detail for the scalar and tensor glueballs.

The lift of a type IIA string-frame metric to 11-dimensional supergravity
is given by the relation

\begin{split}\text{d}s^{2}&=G_{MN}\text{d}x^{M}\text{d}x^{N}\\
&=e^{-2\phi/3}g_{AB}\text{d}x^{A}\text{d}x^{B}+e^{4\phi/3}\left(\text{d}x^{11}+A_{B}\text{d}x^{B}\right)^{2},\end{split}

(65)

with
M,N=0,\dots 10
and
A,B=0,\dots 9
, omitting the 11th index. By
introducing the radial coordinate
r
related to
U
by
U=\frac{r^{2}}{2L}
,
we get the lifted metric

\text{d}s^{2}=\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\left[f(r)\text{d}x_{4}^{2}+\eta_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}+\text{d}x_{11}^{2}\right]+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{\text{d}r^{2}}{f\left(r\right)}+\frac{L^{2}}{4}\text{d}\Omega_{4}^{2},

(66)

and the field strength
F_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\frac{6}{L}\sqrt{g_{S^{4}}}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
,
which are solutions to the equations of motion following from the
unique supergravity action

2\kappa_{11}^{2}S_{11}=\int\mathrm{d}^{11}x\sqrt{-G}\left(R-\frac{1}{2}|F_{4}|^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{3!}\int A_{3}\wedge F_{4}\wedge F_{4}.

(67)

Scalar and tensor glueball modes appear as normalizable modes of metric fluctuations
\delta G
, which translate to perturbations
of the type-IIA string metric and dilaton through

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{3}}{L^{3}}\left[\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{2r^{2}}\delta G_{11,11}\right)\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\delta G_{\mu\nu}\right]

\displaystyle g_{44}=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{3}f}{L^{3}}\left[1+\frac{L^{2}}{2r^{2}}\delta G_{11,11}+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}f}\delta G_{44}\right]

\displaystyle g_{rr}=

\displaystyle\frac{L}{rf}\left[1+\frac{L^{2}}{2r^{2}}\delta G_{11,11}+\frac{r^{2}f}{L^{2}}\delta G_{rr}\right]

\displaystyle g_{r\mu}=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{L}\delta G_{r\mu}

\displaystyle g_{\Omega\Omega}=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{L}\left(\frac{L}{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{2r^{2}}\delta G_{11,11}\right)

\displaystyle e^{4\phi/3}=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\delta G_{11,11}\right).

(68)

Inducing these metric fluctuations to the world volume of the D8-brane
system described by the action (
5
),
Hashimoto:2007ze
calculated interaction vertices of the lightest scalar glueball with mesons,
which was revisited and extended in
Brunner:2015oqa
.

Pseudoscalar, vector, and pseudovector glueballs appear as fluctuations of
the type-IIA form fields; glueballs with higher spin
would need a stringy description beyond the supergravity approximation
Sonnenschein:2018fph
.

IV.1
Exotic and dilatonic scalar glueballs

Superficially, the emerging glueball spectrum resembles the one found in lattice calculations (see Fig. 1 in
Brunner:2015oga
),
containing a lightest scalar glueball with a mass below that of the tensor glueball,
whereas most other holographic models have the scalar glueball degenerate with the tensor. This is achieved by an “exotic” polarization of the bulk metric involving the extra compact dimension
(\tau)
separating the D8-branes,

\displaystyle\delta G_{\tau\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{r^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{E}\,L^{2}}f(r)S_{4}(r)G_{E}(x^{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\delta G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{E}\,L^{2}}S_{4}(r)\left[\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\mu\nu}-\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{3r_{\text{KK}}^{6}}{5r^{6}-2r_{\text{KK}}^{6}}\right)\frac{\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{M^{2}}\right]G_{E}(x^{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\delta G_{11,11}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{E}\,4L^{2}}S_{4}(r)G_{E}(x^{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\delta G_{rr}

\displaystyle=-\frac{L^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{E}\,r^{2}f(r)}\frac{3r_{\text{KK}}^{6}}{5r^{6}-2r_{\text{KK}}^{6}}S_{4}(r)G_{E}(x^{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\delta G_{r\mu}=\delta G_{\mu r}

IV.2
Extrapolations to realistic glueball masses

In the WSS model, the masses of glueballs are given by pure numbers times
M_{\mathrm{KK}}
, which is also the case for the (axial) vector mesons.
However, when
M_{\mathrm{KK}}
has been fixed by the mass of the
\rho
meson,
the glueball masses appear to be too small compared to lattice QCD results.

In order to predict decay rates for different glueball candidates we
manually change the masses of glueball modes in amplitudes and
phase space integrals, which could be viewed as assuming a different
scale
M_{\mathrm{KK}}
for the glueball sector.
The coupling constants involving glueballs
are all inversely proportional to
M_{\mathrm{KK}}
and we interpret
this appearance of
M_{\mathrm{KK}}
to be tied to the mass scale of glueballs,
which shows up also in their normalization factors
\mathcal{N}
, whereas
explicit appearances of
M_{\mathrm{KK}}
in the DBI action of the D branes
are considered as being fixed like the mass of the
\rho
meson.
When upscaling glueball masses, we have therefore
correspondingly reduced the dimensionful
glueball-meson/photon coupling constants.
[Without such a rescaling, the results for all glueball decay rates and the glueball contributions to
a_{\upmu}
presented in Sect.
V
extrapolated to some mass
M_{G}
would be simply larger by a factor
(M_{G}/M_{G}^{\mathrm{WSS}})^{2}
.]

We consider this rescaling plausible in that the overlap
integrals of glueball and meson holographic profiles should
become smaller when glueball and meson modes are separated further in energy. It may well be, however, that this reduction is
only insufficiently accounted for by the overall change
of the mass scale in the glueball coupling constants;
thus our numerical results should be considered as somewhat rough estimates.

A.1
Dilatonic scalar glueball

The scalar glueball fluctuation which in
Brunner:2015oqa

is referred to as (predominantly) dilatonic scalar glueball,
reads

\displaystyle\delta G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{D}\,L^{2}}T_{4}(r)\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{\Box}\right)G_{D}(x^{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\delta G_{11,11}

\displaystyle=-3\frac{r^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{D}\,L^{2}}T_{4}(r)G_{D}(x^{\sigma}),

(108)

with an undetermined normalization parameter
\mathcal{N}_{D}
. To
be a solution of the Einstein equations, the radial function
T_{4}(r)

has to satisfy the differential equation

\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}r}\left(r^{7}-r\,r_{KK}^{6}\right)\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}r}T_{4}(r)+L^{4}M_{D}^{2}r^{3}T_{4}(r)=0,

(109)

with boundary conditions
T_{4}(r_{\text{KK}})=1
and
T_{4}^{\prime}(r_{\text{KK}})=0
,
and therefore is normalizable for a discrete set of mass eigenvalues

M_{D}
. In the following, we will only consider the lightest mode
with
M_{D}=1.567M_{\mathrm{KK}}=1487\,\text{MeV}
.

The kinetic and mass term for
G_{D}
reads

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{L}_{4}\right|_{G_{D}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\int\text{d}r\frac{3r^{3}T_{4}(r)^{2}}{L^{3}\mathcal{N}_{D}^{2}}G_{D}\left(\Box-M_{D}^{2}\right)G_{D}

(110)

with the constant

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{L}{2}\right)^{4}\Omega_{4}\frac{1}{2\kappa_{11}^{2}}\left(2\pi\right)^{2}R_{4}R_{11}.

(111)

The radial integration for the lightest mode yields the constant

\displaystyle\int\text{d}r\frac{r^{3}T_{4}(r)^{2}}{L^{3}}=0.22547\left[T_{4}(r_{\text{KK}})\right]^{2}\frac{r_{\text{KK}}^{4}}{L^{3}}.

(112)

To get a canonically normalized kinetic term

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{L}_{4}\right|_{G_{D}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}G_{D}\left(\Box-M_{D}^{2}\right)G_{D},

(113)

we have to set

\mathcal{N}_{D}=0.0335879\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{C}M_{\text{KK}}.

(114)

Inducing the fluctuation (
108
) in the D8 brane action
(
5
) we obtain the derivative coupling of two pseudoscalar mesons to

\mathcal{L}_{G_{D}\Pi\Pi}=d_{1}\,\text{tr}\,\partial_{\mu}\Pi\partial_{\nu}\Pi\left(\eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}}{\Box}\right)G_{D}

(115)

where

d_{1}=\frac{17.2261}{\sqrt{\lambda}M_{\text{KK}}N_{c}}

(116)

(see

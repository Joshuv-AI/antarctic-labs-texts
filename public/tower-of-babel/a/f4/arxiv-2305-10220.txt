Dear Reader,

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring of postive dimension and let

M
be a non-zero module of finite length and finite projective dimension. In part 1 of this paper we showed that the Lowey
length
\ell\ell(M)\geq\operatorname{ord}(A)
. This generalizes a result of Avramov, Buchweitz, Iyengar and Miller,
[
2
, 1.1]
.
Thus the singularity of

imposes a lower bound on the Lowey length of modules of finite projective dimension.

In this paper we investigate upper bounds of Lowey lengths of finite length modules of finite projective dimension.
An obvious
upper bound is

\ell\ell(M)\leq\lambda(M)
.
Note that if
A
is regular then
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
.
has finite projective dimension and

\ell\ell(k)=\lambda(k)=1
. Our first result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
. Suppose there exists an

A
-module
M
of non-zero finite length module of finite projective dimension such that

\ell\ell(M)=\lambda(M)
. Then
A
is at worst a hypersurface singularity, i.e., the completion
\widehat{A}=Q/(f)
where

(Q,\mathfrak{n})
is a complete regular local ring and
f\in\mathfrak{n}
.

Note that we do not require

A
to be Gorenstein in Theorem
1.1
.

We complement Theorem
1.1
by proving

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be an abstract hypersurface ring of positive dimension. Then there exists a
finite length
A
- module
M
of finite projective dimension with
\ell\ell(M)=\lambda(M)
.

A natural question is what happens if
\lambda(M)-\ell\ell(M)=1
?
Our next result implies that this still forces strong conditions on
A
.
Set
\operatorname{embdim}(A)
to be the embedding dimension of
A
, i.e, the number of minimal generators of
\mathfrak{m}
.
We show

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d\geq 1

and let
M
be a non-zero finite length module of finite projective dimension. If
\lambda(M)-\ell\ell(M)=1
then

\operatorname{embdim}(A)-d\leq 2
(in particular
A
is a complete intersection of codimension
\leq 2
). Furthermore we have

(1)

Assume
\operatorname{embdim}(A)-d=1
(the hypersurface ring case). If
\widehat{A}=Q/(f)
where
(Q,\mathfrak{n})
is regular local then
f\in\mathfrak{n}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{3}
.

(2)

Assume
\operatorname{embdim}(A)-d=2
. If
\widehat{A}=Q/(f,g)
where
(Q,\mathfrak{n})
is regular local then
f,g\in\mathfrak{n}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{3}
. Furthermore the images of
f,g
in
\mathfrak{n}^{2}/\mathfrak{n}^{3}
is linearly independent over
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
.

(3)

If
M
is not cyclic then necessarily

is regular local.

We complement Theorem
1.3
by giving examples

which shows that all the cases above occur.

To prove our results we use some elementary techniques which arise in the study of associated graded rings of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals
in Cohen-Macaulay rings. It is suprising to us that a single module

imposes such restrictions on the ring

.

2.1

reduction to the case when

The first two assertions are well-known, cf

(3) Suppose

By same argument we get

(4) This is well-known.

Proof of Theorem

.

We may assume that

Step-1:

Let

.

Step-2:

Let

be the Matlis dual of

\ell\ell(M^{v})=\ell\ell(M)=\lambda(M)=\lambda(M^{\vee}).

Set

is utmost one.
So

. We may also assume that

is not regular.

By induction on

We first consider the case when

Notice

. Thus
\mathfrak{m}=(a,t)

As

Now assume

We now make the following:

Claim 1:
I\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.

Claim 2:
I\nsubseteq P
for any
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(A)

We finish the proof by assuming Claims1,2 which we prove later.

By prime avoidance there exists
x\in I
such that
x
is
A
-regular and
x\notin\mathfrak{m}^{2}
. Note that

is also a

-module. By a result of Nagata (cf.,

By

we get that

is a polynomial. In particular

is finite.
By our induction hypothesis it follows that

is a hypersurface ring. As

and

is
A
-regular it follows
that

is also a hypersurface ring.

Proof of Claim 1: Suppose if possible

. Then by Nakayama Lemma we get

. In particular

which is a contradiction.

Proof of Claim 2: If

then

, a contradiction.
∎

We now give

Proof of Proposition

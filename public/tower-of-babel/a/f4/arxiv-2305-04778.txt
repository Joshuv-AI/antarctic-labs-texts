On irreducibility of prefixed algebraic sets in moduli spaces of prime degree polynomials
Niladri Patra School of Mathematics, Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Mumbai, India.

Abstract
. Consider the moduli space, ℳ d \mathcal{M}_{d} , of degree d > 2 d>2 polynomials over ℂ {\mathbbm{C}} , with a marked critical point. Given k ≥ 0 , p k\geq 0,\;p an odd prime, we show that the set Σ k , 1 , p \Sigma_{k,1,p} of conjugacy classes of degree p p polynomials, for which the marked critical point is strictly ( k , 1 ) (k,1) -preperiodic, is an irreducible quasi-affine variety. Irreducibility of these sets was conjectured by Milnor, and has been proved for p = 3 p=3 by Buff, Epstein and Koch. We prove that the subspaces of Σ k , 1 , p \Sigma_{k,1,p} , that arise by varying the ramification index of the marked critical point all the way up to the unicritical case, are all irreducible subvarieties. Finally, using the irreducibility of Σ k , 1 , p \Sigma_{k,1,p} we give a new and short proof of the fact that the set of all unicritical points of Σ k , 1 , p \Sigma_{k,1,p} form one Galois orbit under the action of absolute Galois group of ℚ {\mathbbm{Q}} .

1.
Introduction

Complex dynamics studies the behaviour of iterates of rational maps on the Riemann sphere. Since the time of Fatou and Julia, it has been known that the behaviour of critical points of a map, under iterations, dictates the behaviour of the dynamical system generated by that map. Thurston (
[
DH93
]
) showed that fixing the number of distinct images of critical points of a polynomial map, under the self-iterates of the map, determines the conjugacy class of the dynamical system, up to finitely many choices.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{d}
be the space of all degree
d
polynomials over
{\mathbbm{C}}
with a marked critical point, modulo affine conjugation. The space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
can be seen as
(d-1)
-dimensional affine variety. Thurston’s work shows that by fixing preperiodicities for
all
of the critical points of a general degree
d
polynomial map, one gets finitely many points in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Instead of
all
the critical points, fixing the behaviour of
some
of the critical points results in dynamically interesting subfamilies of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. These subfamilies have been studied since the 1990s (
[
BH88
]
,
[
M90
]
,
[
BDK91
]
,
[
BH92
]
,
[
MP92
]
,
[
R06
]
,
[
LQ07
]
,
[
Mil09
]
,
[
BKM10
]
,
[
BEK18
]
,
[
AK20
]
). A long-standing conjecture of Milnor (
[
Mil09
]
) states that:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Fix
k\geq 0,n>0,d>2
. The family
\Sigma_{k,n,d}
of conjugacy classes of degree
d
polynomials for which the marked critical point is strictly
(k,n)
-preperiodic is an irreducible subset of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
, under the Zariski topology.

Fixing the ramification index of the marked critical point to be equal to
d
, we arrive at the unicritical case. In other words, polynomials with a unique critical point in
{\mathbbm{C}}
are called
unicritical polynomials
. Any unicritical polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
is conjugate to a polynomial of the form
f_{c}(X)=X^{d}+c,c\in{\mathbbm{C}}
(
0
is the unique finite critical point of
f_{c}
with ramification index

). In the article
[

]
, Milnor studied arithmetic properties of unicritical polynomials and made the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Fix
k\geq 0,n>0,d\geq 2
. The values of

(k,n)
-preperiodic under

{\mathbbm{Q}}
.

Conjecture
1.2

[

]
,
[

]

Remark 1.3

In this article, we go the other way around. We study Conjecture

1.1.
Mixed critical cases:

In Conjecture
1.1
, the ramification index of the marked critical point has been allowed to vary freely between
2
and
d
. Fixing a lower bound
j

[

, Roesch studied the topology of the connectedness locus of a particular mixed critical family of maps. Here, we consider algebraic properties of mixed critical subfamilies of
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
and show that these are irreducible under Zariski topology on

.

Theorem 1.6
.

Fix
k\geq 0,p\text{ prime },p>3,\;2<j<p
. The family of conjugacy classes of degree
p
polynomials for which the marked critical point is strictly
(k,1)
-preperiodic and has ramification index
\geq j

\mathcal{M}_{p}

1.2.
Sketch of Proof.

1.2.1.
Normal form.

To study the families mentioned in Theorem
1.4
, we first require a concrete description of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
as an affine variety. By Thurston’s work, the dynamical behaviour of any polynomial is dictated by the dynamical behaviour of its critical points. This suggests representing polynomials by their finite critical points and one finite critical value, modulo affine conjugation. We introduce a normal form for monic, reduced degree
d\geq 2
polynomials, which is a modification of
Branner-Hubbard normal form
appearing in (
[
BH88
]
). This normal form also generalizes the unicritical normal form
f(X)=X^{d}+c
used in (
[
Mil14
]
,
[
HT15
]
,
[
Gok20
]
,
[
G20
]
,
[
BGok22
]
,
[
BG23
]
) and each of the various other normal forms considered in (
[
BH88
]
,
[
Mil09
]
,
[
LQ07
]
,
[
FG15
]
).

This has two applications. First, we use this normal form to express
\mathcal{M}_{d}
explicitly as an affine variety,
(d-1)
-dimensional affine space quotiented by the action of the group of
(d-1)
th roots of unity, and a permutation group. Secondly, we use the normal form to construct polynomials
h_{k,n,d},~k\geq 0,n>0,d>2,
such that the families
\Sigma_{k,n,d}
appearing in Conjecture
1.1
are Zariski dense in the algebraic sets of these polynomials in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
.

1.2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
: Irreducibility of
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
.

Let
k\geq 0,p
be an odd prime. To show the irreducibility of
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
, it suffices to show that the polynomials
h_{k,1,p}
are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
.

The proof of irreducibility uses arithmetical methods. We first observe that the coefficients of these polynomials lie in the ring
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
, the localization of the rational integers
{\mathbbm{Z}}
at the prime
p
. We show that the polynomials
h_{k,1,p}
are generalised Eisenstein-type polynomials with respect to the prime
p
. For the resultant condition of the generalised Eisenstein criterion, we need a weaker version of Thurston’s rigidity, of which an elementary algebraic proof is given in Subsection
4.2
. This shows that
h_{k,1,p}
polynomials are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
.

To prove irreducibility over the complex numbers, we observe that the lowest degree homogeneous part of
h_{k,1,p}
factorizes into linear factors over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, for any
k\geq 2,\;p
an odd prime. As
h_{k,1,p}
polynomials are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, this forces their irreducibility over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, for any
k\geq 2
. The case
k=0
is a trivial case, as
h_{0,1,p}
is linear. For
k=1
, a little modification of the above proof shows that
h_{1,1,p}
is also irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
.

1.2.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.6
: Mixed critical case.

The variables of the polynomials
h_{k,1,p}
stand for the critical points and one critical value. The polynomials defining the mixed critical locus arise by identifying some of the variables (critical points). The resulting polynomials are once again generalised Eisenstein polynomials, and hence are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. As long as we do not identify all the critical points, the lowest degree homogeneous part of the resulting polynomials mentioned above always has at least one simple linear factor defined over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. This enforces their irreducibility over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, completing the proof of irreducibility of all the mixed critical loci.

1.2.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
: Unicritical case.

Identifying all the finite critical points to
0
, we reach at the unicritical case. The resulting polynomial we get from
h_{k,1,p}
is a univariate polynomial, which is an Eisenstein-type polynomial multiplied with some power of the variable
\beta
(critical value). Throwing away this power of
\beta
, we see that the roots of these Eisentstein-type polynomials are the values of
c
mentioned in Conjecture
1.2
. This allows us to complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
f
be a polynomial over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Let
f^{m},m\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, denote the iteration
f^{m}:=f\circ f^{m-1}
, and
f^{0}
is defined to be the identity map. For any point
x\in{\mathbbm{C}}
, the
forward orbit
of
x
is defined to be the set,
\{x,f(x),f^{2}(x),\cdot\cdot\cdot,f^{m}(x),\cdot\cdot\cdot\}=\{f^{m}(x)|m\in{\mathbbm{N}}\cup\{0\}\}
. Recall that the roots of the derivative
f^{\prime}
of
f
, are called the
finite critical points
or
ramified points
of
f
. Since the time of Fatou and Julia, it has been known that behaviour of a complex dynamical system (which means forward orbits of
x
, where
x
varies over
{\mathbbm{C}}
) is largely governed by the forward orbits of the critical points of the map. Hence, in order to understand the dynamical system generated by
f
, one needs to study the forward orbits of critical points of
f
.

2.1.
Periodic and preperiodic points

Let
g
be a polynomial defined over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Let
k\geq 0,n>0
be integers. A point
x\in{\mathbbm{C}}
is called a
periodic point of period
n
(or,
n
-periodic point
) if and only if
g^{n}(x)=x
, and called
strictly
n
-periodic point
(or,
periodic point of exact period

n
) if and only if
n
is the smallest natural number such that
g^{n}(x)=x
.

A point
x\in{\mathbbm{C}}
is called a
(k,n)
-
preperiodic point
if and only if
g^{k+n}(x)=g^{k}(x)
, and
strictly
(k,n)
-preperiodic point
if and only if
g^{k+n}(x)=g^{k}(x)
and for all
0\leq l\leq k,\;0\leq m\leq n
,
(l,m)\neq(k,n)\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{2}
, we have
g^{l+m}(x)\neq g^{l}(x)
.

2.2.
Affine conjugation

Consider
Aut({\mathbbm{C}})=\{h\in{\mathbbm{C}}[z]~|~h(z)=az+b,a\in{\mathbbm{C}}\backslash\{0\},b\in{\mathbbm{C}}\}
, the group of holomorphic automorphisms of
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Two maps
g,\tilde{g}\in{\mathbbm{C}}[z]
are called
affine conjugate
to each other if and only if there exists a map
h\in Aut({\mathbbm{C}})
, such that
\tilde{g}=h\circ g\circ h^{-1}
.

2.3.
Normal form and moduli spaces of polynomials with a marked critical point.

For an arbitrary but fixed integer
d>2
, let
S_{d}
be the set of all polynomials of degree
d
with a marked critical point. Consider the
moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{d}
, which is defined to be the quotient space of
S_{d}
, obtained by identifying polynomials that are affine conjugate to each other and the affine conjugation map sends the marked critical point of the first to the marked critical point of the latter. We say a polynomial is
reduced
if the sum of its roots is zero, which is also equivalent to saying that the coefficient of the second highest degree term of the polynomial is zero. Any polynomial over
{\mathbbm{C}}
is affine conjugate to a monic reduced polynomial over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Thus, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is the set of equivalence classes of monic reduced polynomials of degree
d
over
{\mathbbm{C}}
with a marked critical point, where the equivalence relation is given by affine conjugation.

Let
f
be a monic, reduced, degree
d
polynomial. We define
normal form
of
f
to be a form of the polynomial
f
in which each coefficient of
f
is expressed as polynomials in its finite critical points and one finite critical value. Note that, any polynomial of degree
d
has
(d-1)
number of finite critical points, counted with multiplicity.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f
be a monic and reduced element of
{\mathbbm{C}}[z]
of degree
d
. Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-1}
be the finite critical points of
f
, not necessarily distinct. Let
f(\alpha_{1})=\beta,\;\bar{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-1})
and

denote the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in
\alpha_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-1}
. Then, the polynomial
f(z)
can be written in a normal form as,

z^{d}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d}{d-i}\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})\cdot z^{d-i}-\alpha_{1}^{d}-\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d}{d-i}\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})\cdot\alpha_{1}^{d-i}+\beta,

with
\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}=0
.

Proof.

The derivative of
f
can be written as,

f^{\prime}(z)=c(z-\alpha_{1})(z-\alpha_{2})\cdot\cdot\cdot(z-\alpha_{d-1}),

where
c\in{\mathbbm{C}},c
constant.
Since
f
is reduced,
f^{\prime}
is also reduced. Hence,
\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}=0
. So,

f^{\prime}(z)=c\left(z^{d-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})z^{d-i-1}\right),

(2.1)

where
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-1})
and
s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})
is the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-1}
. By integration,

f(z)=c\left(\frac{z^{d}}{d}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})}{d-i}z^{d-i}\right)+c^{\prime},

where
c^{\prime}\in{\mathbbm{C}}
,
c^{\prime}
constant. The polynomial

. Hence,

f(z)=z^{d}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d}{d-i}\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})\cdot z^{d-i}+c^{\prime}.

Equating
f(\alpha_{1})
with
\beta
,

\beta=f(\alpha_{1})=\alpha_{1}^{d}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d}{d-i}\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})\cdot\alpha_{1}^{d-i}+c^{\prime},

\implies c^{\prime}=-\alpha_{1}^{d}-\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d}{d-i}\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})\cdot\alpha_{1}^{d-i}+\beta.

Hence,

f(z)=z^{d}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d}{d-i}\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})\cdot z^{d-i}-\alpha_{1}^{d}-\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d}{d-i}\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})\cdot\alpha_{1}^{d-i}+\beta.

(2.2)

∎

\mathcal{M}_{d}
by a monic reduced polynomial written in the normal form as in Equation (

2.4.
Moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is an affine variety

As
f
varies over all monic reduced polynomials over
{\mathbbm{C}}
of degree
d
,
\alpha_{i},1\leq i\leq p-1,
and
\beta
vary over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}=0
. So, we can parametrize the set of all monic, reduced polynomials over
{\mathbbm{C}}
of degree
d
with a marked critical point, using
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2}
as
(d-2)
finite critical points with
\alpha_{1}
as its marked critical point and
\beta
such that
f(\alpha_{1})=\beta
. From here on in this article, we will consider
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},\beta
as variables over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, unless otherwise mentioned. For the rest of this subsection, we will denote the polynomial
f
in Equation (
2.2
) as
f_{\bar{\alpha},\beta}
, where
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-1})
.

Following the previous subsection and the above paragraph,
\mathcal{M}_{d}
can be considered as a quotient space of
{\mathbbm{C}}^{d-1}
, quotiented by affine conjugation relation, where the coordinates of
{\mathbbm{C}}^{d-1}
are
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},\beta
. Next, we will show that
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is an affine variety.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
d\in{\mathbbm{N}},\;d>2
, the space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is an affine variety.

Proof.

Consider the affine space
{\mathbbm{C}}^{d}
with
\alpha_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-1},\beta
as coordinates. Consider the hyperplane
V
which is the zero set of
\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}
in
{\mathbbm{C}}^{d}
. We have an affine isomorphism,

{\mathbbm{C}}^{d-1}\rightarrow V,\;\;\;(\alpha_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},\beta)\mapsto(\alpha_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},-\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}\alpha_{i},\beta).

(2.3)

As we have seen above, we have a surjective map,

V\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{d},\;\;\;\;(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},\alpha_{d-1},\beta)\mapsto f_{\bar{\alpha},\beta}.

(2.4)

Now, two points
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},\alpha_{d-1},\beta)
and
(\alpha^{\prime}_{1},\alpha^{\prime}_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha^{\prime}_{d-2},\alpha^{\prime}_{d-1},\beta^{\prime})
in
V
maps to the same point in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
iff there is an affine conjugation map
h
such that
h\circ f_{\bar{\alpha},\beta}\circ h^{-1}=f_{\bar{\alpha^{\prime}},\beta^{\prime}}
and
h(\alpha_{1})=\alpha^{\prime}_{1}
.

If
h
is the identity map, then
f_{\bar{\alpha},\beta}=f_{\bar{\alpha^{\prime}},\beta^{\prime}},\alpha_{1}=\alpha^{\prime}_{1}
and
\beta=\beta^{\prime}
. Hence, the ordered set
\{\alpha^{\prime}_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha^{\prime}_{d-2},\alpha^{\prime}_{d-1}\}
is a permutation of
\{\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},\alpha_{d-1}\}
, and every such permutation will map to the same point in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
.

Let
h
be a non-identity map. As
f_{\bar{\alpha},\beta},f_{\bar{\alpha^{\prime}},\beta^{\prime}}
are both reduced, monic and of degree
d
, the affine conjugation map between them has to be
h(z)=\zeta\cdot z
, for some
(d-1)
th root of unity
\zeta
. A brief computation shows that
h^{-1}\circ f_{\bar{\alpha},\beta}\circ h=f_{\zeta^{-1}\cdot\bar{\alpha},\zeta^{-1}\cdot\beta}
.

Consider the following two finite subgroups of
GL_{d}({\mathbbm{C}})
,

\mu_{d-1}:=\{\zeta I\;|\;I\text{ is the identity matrix},\zeta\text{ is a }(d-1)\text{th root of unity}\}.

P:=\text{Group of all }d\times d\text{ permutation matrices which fix the first and last coordinate.}

The hyperplane
V

4.1.

Let

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

such that
l\leq k
and

, the polynomial

divides

{\mathbbm{Q}}[d-1]

{\mathbbm{Q}}[d-2]
.

Proof.

To prove the lemma, it is enough to show that
f_{k-1,n,d}
divides
f_{k,n,d}
and
f_{k,m,d}
divides
f_{k,n,d}
in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[d-1]
, for any
k,n,m\in{\mathbbm{N}},
such that
m|n
.

Consider the polynomial
f
in Equation (
2.2
) as a polynomial in a single variable,
z
, over the integral domain
{\mathbbm{Q}}[d-1]
. Observe that,
(z-w)
divides

. Putting

, one gets that
f_{k-1,n,d}
divides
f_{k,n,d}
in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[d-1]
.

Hence, for any non-negative integers

such that
l\leq k,n>0
, the polynomial
f_{l,n,d}
divides
f_{k,n,d}
in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[d-1]
. Let

. Then,

\displaystyle f_{k,n,d}

\displaystyle=f_{k,mt,d}

\displaystyle=f_{k+m(t-1),m,d}+f_{k+m(t-2),m,d}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+f_{k,m,d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}f_{k+mi,m,d}.

As
f_{k,m,d}
divides
f_{k+mi,m,d},
for

, the polynomial

divides
f_{k,n,d}
, whenever

divides

4.2.
A weak version of Thurston’s rigidity theorem for
\mathcal{M}_{p^{e}}

Thurston’s rigidity theorem studies transversality of the intersection of equations for two different critical points to be preperiodic, in moduli spaces of rational maps (see
[
DH93
]
). In this article, we need a much weaker version of Thurston’s rigidity for moduli spaces of polynomials with a marked critical point. While the proof of the general Thurston’s rigidity theorem requires deep tools, the version we need here (Theorem
4.4
) can be proved using elementary algebraic methods. Let
f
be the polynomial as defined in Equation (
4.1
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Fix
i\in\{2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,p^{e}-1\}
. Fix
k_{1},k_{i}\in{\mathbbm{N}}\cup\{0\}
, and
n_{1},n_{i}\in{\mathbbm{N}}
. Then, the polynomials

f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})\text{ and }f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})

are coprime in
{\mathbbm{C}}[p^{e}-1]:={\mathbbm{C}}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-1},\beta]
. Moreover, the polynomials

\hat{f}^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})-\hat{f}^{k_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})\text{ and }\hat{f}^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})-\hat{f}^{k_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})

are coprime in
{\mathbbm{C}}[p^{e}-2]
.

Proof.

As all the polynomials in the statement of the theorem are defined over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, it is enough to show that they are coprime over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
.

More precisely, all the polynomials mentioned above are defined over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
. Let
l\in{\mathbbm{N}}
. Consider
f^{l}(\alpha_{j})
, where
j\in\{1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,p^{e}-1\}
. The term in
f^{l}(\alpha_{j})
containing the highest power of
\beta
is
\beta^{p^{(l-1)e}}
. As
n_{1},n_{i}\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, we have
k_{1}+n_{1}>k_{1}\geq 0
and
k_{i}+n_{i}>k_{i}\geq 0
. Hence,
f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})
and
f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
are both monic polynomials in
\beta
over the integral domain
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-1}]
. Hence, to show that
f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})
and
f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
are coprime over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, it is enough to show that they are coprime over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
.

Observe that
\;\hat{}\;
being a ring homomorphism,

\displaystyle\hat{f}^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})-\hat{f}^{k_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})

\displaystyle=\widehat{f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad\hat{f}^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})-\hat{f}^{k_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})

\displaystyle=\widehat{f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})}.

As
\hat{\beta}=\beta
, we get that
\hat{f}^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})-\hat{f}^{k_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})
and
\hat{f}^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})-\hat{f}^{k_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})
are both monic polynomials in
\beta
over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-2}]
. Similarly as above, to show that
\hat{f}^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})-\hat{f}^{k_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})
and
\hat{f}^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})-\hat{f}^{k_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})
are coprime over
Q
, it is enough to show that they are coprime over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
.

Now, we will show that
f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})
and
f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
are coprime over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
. As they are both monic polynomials in
\beta
over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-1}]
, their g.c.d. must be monic polynomial in
\beta
over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-1}]
too, upto associates. Hence,
f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})
and
f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
are coprime over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
if they are coprime modulo
p{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
. Observe that,

f(z)\equiv z^{p^{e}}-\alpha_{1}^{p^{e}}+\beta\;(\text{mod }p).

So,
f^{l}(\alpha_{1})=\alpha_{1}\;(\text{or, }\beta)
if
l=0\;(\text{or, }1)
, and for
l>1
,

\displaystyle f^{l}(\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\equiv(\beta^{p^{(l-1)e}}-\alpha_{1}^{p^{(l-1)e}})+\cdot\cdot\cdot+(\beta^{p^{e}}-\alpha_{1}^{p^{e}})+\beta

\displaystyle\equiv\beta+\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p^{je}}\;(\text{mod }p).

Hence,

f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})\equiv\sum_{j=k_{1}}^{k_{1}+n_{1}-1}(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p^{je}}\;(\text{mod }p).

Similarly,
f^{0}(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i}
, and for
l\in{\mathbbm{N}}

f^{l}(\alpha_{i})\equiv(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{1})^{p^{le}}+(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p^{(l-1)e}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p^{e}}+\beta\;(\text{mod }p).

So modulo
p
,

f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})=\left((\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{1})^{p^{(k_{i}+n_{i})e}}-(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{1})^{p^{k_{i}e}}\right)+\left(\sum_{j=k_{i}}^{k_{i}+n_{i}-1}(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p^{je}}\right).

Modulo
p
, the highest degree homogeneous part of
f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})
is

(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p^{(k_{1}+n_{1}-1)e}}

and the highest degree homogeneous part of
f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
is

(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{1})^{p^{(k_{i}+n_{i})e}}.

The highest degree homogeneous part of g.c.d. of two polynomials must divide the highest degree homogeneous part of each of them. As
(\beta-\alpha_{1})
and
(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{1})
are two distinct irreducible polynomials over
{\mathbbm{F}}_{p}
, the polynomials
f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})
and
f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
are coprime modulo
p{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
. Hence, They are coprime over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
too.

In the last paragraph, replacing
f^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})-f^{k_{1}}(\alpha_{1})
and
f^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\alpha_{i})-f^{k_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
with
\hat{f}^{k_{1}+n_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})-\hat{f}^{k_{1}}(\hat{\alpha_{1}})
and
\hat{f}^{k_{i}+n_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})-\hat{f}^{k_{i}}(\hat{\alpha_{i}})
, respectively, every argument follows verbatim and one obtains the second part of the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

We will require the above theorem in later sections. Whenever we mention “Thurston’s rigidity” from here on, we will mean the above theorem (Theorem
4.4
).

4.3.
Generalised Eisenstein Irreducibility criterion

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
p,e\in{\mathbbm{N}},p
odd prime. Let
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}
be the localisation of
{\mathbbm{Z}}
at the prime
p
. Let
g,h
be non-constant elements of
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[p^{e}-2]
, both monic as elements of
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-2}][\beta]
. Let
\mbox{Res}(g,h)
denote the resultant of
g,h
both considered as polynomials in
\beta
over the integral domain
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-2}]
. Suppose the following conditions hold,

1)
g\equiv h^{n}\;(\text{mod }p)
, for some
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
.

2)
h\;(\text{mod }p)
is irreducible in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{p}[p^{e}-2]
.

3)
\mbox{Res}(g,h)\not\equiv 0\;(\text{mod }p^{2\cdot deg(h)}{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)})
, where
deg(h)
is the degree of
h
as a polynomial in
\beta
over
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-2}]
.

Then,
g
is irreducible in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[p^{e}-2]
.

Proof.

We will prove by contradiction. Let us assume that
g
is reducible in

{\mathbbm{Q}}[p^{e}-2]
. As
g
is monic in
\beta
, by Gauss lemma, let
g=g_{1}\cdot g_{2},
such that
g_{1},g_{2}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[p^{e}-2]
both monic as elements of
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p^{e}-2}][\beta]
and none of them is a unit. By conditions
1
and
2
of the theorem,
g_{1}\equiv h^{n_{1}}\;(\textnormal{mod }p)
, and
g_{2}\equiv h^{n_{2}}\;(\textnormal{mod }p)
, such that
n_{1},n_{2}\in{\mathbbm{N}},n_{1}+n_{2}=n
. So, there exist polynomials
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[p^{e}-2]
, such that
g_{1}=h^{n_{1}}+p\cdot x_{1}
and
g_{2}=h^{n_{2}}+p\cdot x_{2}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mbox{Res}(g,h)

\displaystyle=\mbox{Res}(g_{1},h)\cdot\mbox{Res}(g_{2},h)

\displaystyle=\mbox{Res}(p\cdot x_{1},h)\cdot\mbox{Res}(p\cdot x_{2},h)

\displaystyle=p^{2\cdot deg(h)}\cdot\mbox{Res}(x_{1},h)\cdot\mbox{Res}(x_{2},h).

But by the third assumption of the theorem,
p^{2\cdot deg(h)}
does not divide the
\mbox{Res}(g,h)
. Hence, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Let the degree of the polynomial

Lemma 5.3

For any natural number

\hat{f}_{k,1,p}

Next, we need to figure out what are the irreducible polynomials in

Let

2.5
, it is enough to show that
h_{k,1,p}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
.

For
k=0
, this is trivial.

Let
k=1
. Recall that the derivative of
f
is of the form,

f^{\prime}(z)=p\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}(z-\alpha_{i}).

From Equation (
5.6
), we get

h_{1,1,p}\equiv\frac{\widehat{f^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{1})}}{2}\equiv\frac{p}{2}\prod_{i=2}^{p-1}(\alpha_{1}-\hat{\alpha_{i}})\;(\text{mod }\beta-\alpha_{1}).

So,

h_{1,1,p}=\frac{p}{2}\prod_{i=2}^{p-1}(\beta-\hat{\alpha_{i}})+(\beta-\alpha_{1})t_{p},

for some polynomial
t_{p}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{(p)}[p-2]
. Let us assume that
h_{1,1,p}
is reducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Up to a rearrangement of
\alpha_{i}
’s,
2\leq i\leq p-1
, there exists an irreducible factor
g
of
h_{1,1,p}
of the form

g=\left(\prod_{i=2}^{l}(\beta-\hat{\alpha_{i}})\right)+(\beta-\alpha_{1})t,

for some
l\in{\mathbbm{N}},2\leq l\leq p-1,
and
t\in{\mathbbm{C}}[p-2]
.

Consider
G_{{\mathbbm{Q}}}
, the absolute Galois group of
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. For any
\sigma\in G_{{\mathbbm{Q}}}
, we have
\sigma(g)
divides
h_{1,1,p}
. Observe that the first summand in the expression of
g
above is defined over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. Hence, if
\sigma(g)
is a constant multiple of
g
then
\sigma(g)=g
. Otherwise, if
\sigma(g)\neq g
, then
g
being irreducible,
g\cdot\sigma(g)
divides
h_{1,1,p}
. But that is not possible as the first summand in the expression of
g
has all simple factors and they are all defined over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. So,
\sigma(g)=g
. As
\sigma\in G_{{\mathbbm{Q}}}
was arbitrarily chosen, we get that
g\in{\mathbbm{Q}}[p-2]
. But,
h_{1,1,p}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, by Theorem
5.8
. We arrive at a contradiction. Hence,
h_{1,1,p}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
.

For
k\geq 2
, from Lemma
5.3
, we have that the lowest degree homogeneous part of
h_{k,1,p}
is

p\prod_{i=2}^{p-1}(\beta-\hat{\alpha_{j}}).

Observe that the lowest degree homogeneous part of
h_{k,1,p}
has all simple factors and they are all defined over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. Similarly as the
k=1
case, one sees that any irreducible factor of
h_{k,1,p}
must lie in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[p-2]
. As
h_{k,1,p}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
by Theorem
5.8
, it is also irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
.
∎

6.
mixed critical cases: The cases in between the unicritical and separably critical case

Let
k\geq 0,p>3,p
prime. For a degree
p
polynomial
f
with marked critical point
\alpha_{1}
,
we define
ramification index
of the pair
(f,\alpha_{1})
to be the ramification index of
\alpha_{1}
under the map
f
, which is the multiplicity of
\alpha_{1}
as a root of the equation
f(z)-f(\alpha_{1})
. As
\alpha_{1}
is a critical point of a degree
p
polynomial
f
, ramification index of the pair
(f,\alpha_{1})
varies between
2
and
p
. By definition,
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is the space of affine conjugacy classes of pairs
(f,\alpha_{1})
, where
f
is a degree
p
polynomial and
\alpha_{1}
is one of its finite critical points. Ramification index stays invariant under affine conjugation of polynomials with a marked critical point. Hence, for any point in
\mathcal{M}_{p}
, its
ramification index
is well-defined and it lies between
2
and
p
.

For any point
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p-2},\beta)\in\mathcal{M}_{p}
, we call it
separably critical
if its finite critical points
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p-2},\alpha_{p-1}:=-(\sum_{i=1}^{p-2}\alpha_{i})
are all distinct and
unicritical
if
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\cdot\cdot\cdot.=\alpha_{p-1}=0
. Every finite critical point of a separably critical polynomial in
\mathcal{M}_{p}
has ramification index
2
, but the converse is not true. A polynomial in
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is unicritical iff it has a unique finite critical point of ramification index
p
.

Observe that the set of all separably critical points of
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
is non-empty and open in
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
. As
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
is irreducible and quasi-affine, the set of all separably critical points in
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
is also an irreducible quasi-affine variety. In this section, we will study similar irreducibility questions for mixed critical cases. A point in
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is called
mixed critical
iff its ramification index
e
satisfies
2<e<p
.

Fix
j,\;2\leq j\leq p-2
. Let
\Sigma_{j}:=\Sigma_{k,1,p}\cap V_{j},\text{ where }V_{j}:=\{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p-2},\beta)\in\mathcal{M}_{p}\;|\;\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\cdot\cdot\cdot=\alpha_{j}\}
. The set
\Sigma_{j}
is the set of all points of
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
for which the ramification index is
\geq j+1
. This is a quasi-affine subset of the affine variety
V_{j}
. Here, we will show that
\Sigma_{j}
is irreducible, for every
j,\;2\leq j\leq p-2
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
k,p,j\in{\mathbbm{N}},p\text{ prime },p>3,2\leq j\leq p-2
. The set
\Sigma_{j}:=\Sigma_{k,1,p}\cap V_{j}
is an irreducible quasi-affine subvariety of
\mathcal{M}_{p}
.

Proof.

Similar to the separably critical case, to prove irreducibility of
\Sigma_{j}
it is enough to show that the ideal

(h_{k,1,p},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}),

is prime in
{\mathbbm{C}}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p-2},\beta]
.
For that, it is enough to show that the polynomial

h_{k,1,p}^{j}:=h_{k,1,p}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{j+1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p-2},\beta),

is irreducible in
{\mathbbm{C}}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{j+1},\alpha_{j+2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p-2},\beta]
.

Now,
h_{k,1,p}
being
p
-Eisenstein with respect to the polynomial
\beta-\alpha_{1}
, the polynomial
h_{k,1,p}^{j}
is so too. Hence,
h_{k,1,p}^{j}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. As
j<p-1
, Lemma
5.3
shows that the lowest degree homogeneous part of
h_{k,1,p}^{j}
has at least one simple linear factor defined over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, for example:

l:=(\beta+j\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{j+1}+\alpha_{j+2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\alpha_{p-2}).

We argue similarly as in the proof of Theorem
5.9
. By irreducibility of
h_{k,1,p}^{j}
over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, the lowest degree homogeneous part of any factor of
h_{k,1,p}^{j}
over
{\mathbbm{C}}
must have
l
as one of its factor. As
l
is a simple factor of the lowest degree homogeneous part of
h_{k,1,p}^{j}
, the polynomial
h_{k,1,p}^{j}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
.
∎

Theorem
6.1
is equivalent to Theorem
1.6
, mentioned in the introduction. Fix
k\geq 0,p>3,p
prime. Theorem
6.1
provides us with a chain of quasi-affine subvarities of
\mathcal{M}_{p}
of strictly decreasing dimensions.

\mathcal{M}_{p}\supset\Sigma_{k,1,p}\supset\Sigma_{2}\supset\Sigma_{3}\supset\cdot\cdot\cdot\supset\Sigma_{p-2}\supset\text{ \lx@text@lbrace unicritical points\lx@text@rbrace }.

(6.1)

As
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is a
(p-1)
-dimensional affine variety, dimension decreases by exactly
1
in each stage of the above chain. Let
2\leq j\leq p-2
. Similar to the separably critical case, as
\Sigma_{j}
is irreducible, the set of all points of
\Sigma_{k,1,p}
for which ramification index is exactly
j+1
is open and dense in
\Sigma_{j}
. This shows that,

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
k\geq 0,p
be an odd prime and
2\leq j\leq p-1
. The set of all points of
\mathcal{M}_{p}
for which the marked critical point is strictly
(k,1)
-preperiodic and has ramification index
j
, is an irreducible quasi-affine variety of dimension
p-j
.

Theorem
6.1
provides evidence for extending Milnor’s conjecture to the mixed critical case. More precisely,

Conjecture 6.3
.

Fix
k\geq 0,\;n>0,\;d>2,\;2<j<d
. The family of conjugacy classes of degree
d
polynomials for which the marked critical point is strictly
(k,n)
-preperiodic, and has ramification index
\geq j
, is an irreducible quasi-affine subvariety of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
.

Remark 6.4
.

Observe that, the method above can be generalised for any
\mathcal{M}_{d}
,
d\in{\mathbbm{N}},d>2
. Let
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}\cup\{0\},n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
. Let us assume that
h_{k,n,d}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Then, one can prove irreducibility in the mixed critical cases, by showing the irreducibility of
h_{k,n,d}(\alpha_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{j+1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d-2},\beta)
over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, for
1<j<d-1
.

7.
The unicritical case

Putting
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\cdot\cdot\cdot=\alpha_{d-1}=0
in Equation (
2.2
), we obtain a normal form for degree
d\geq 2
unicritical polynomials,

f(z)=z^{d}+\beta.

(7.1)

Let
k,n\in{\mathbbm{Z}},k\geq 0,n>0
. Let
R_{k,n,d}
be the polynomial in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[\beta]
, whose roots are exactly the values of
\beta
for which
0
is strictly
(k,n)
-preperiodic under
f(z)=z^{d}+\beta
.
Milnor’s conjecture
in the unicritical case (Conjecture
1.2
), is equivalent to
R_{k,n,d}
polynomials being either constant or irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, for every
k\geq 0,n>0.

Putting
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\cdot\cdot\cdot=\alpha_{d-1}=0
in Equation (
2.10
), we get that
R_{k,n,d}
divides
h_{k,n,d}(0,0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,0,\beta)
in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[\beta]
. In this section, we will prove the irreducibility of
R_{k,1,p}
over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, where
k\geq 0,p
an odd prime.

Theorem 7.1
.

For any
k,p\in{\mathbbm{Z}},k\geq 0,p
odd prime, The polynomial
R_{k,1,p}
is either constant or irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
.

Proof.

The polynomial
R_{0,1,p}
is
\beta
, hence irreducible. For any
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, if
0
is
(1,n)
-preperiodic under
f
as in Equation (
7.1
), then it is also
n
-periodic. Hence, the polynomial
R_{1,1,p}
is constant.

Let
k\geq 2
. From section
5
, the polynomial
h_{k,1,p}
is
p
-Eisenstein with respect to the polynomial
\beta-\alpha_{1}
, and its lowest degree homogeneous part is

p(\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{p-2}\alpha_{i})\prod_{i=2}^{p-2}(\beta-\alpha_{i}).

Hence, the lowest degree term of
h_{k,1,p}(0,0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,0,\beta)
is
p\beta^{p-2}
, and the polynomial

\dfrac{h_{k,1,p}(0,0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,0,\beta)}{\beta^{p-2}},

is
p
-Eisenstein with respect to
\beta
. By definition of
R_{k,1,p}
,
\;\beta
divides
R_{k,1,p}
iff
k=0
. Hence, for any
k\geq 2
, if
R_{k,1,p}
is non-constant polynomial, then it is
p
-Eisenstein with respect to
\beta
, for every
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
. Therefore
R_{k,1,p}
is either constant or irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, for any
k\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
∎

8.
A study of
\Sigma_{k,2,p}

For this section,
f
is as defined in Equation (
5.1
).

f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{p\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})}{p-i}z^{p-i}-\alpha_{1}^{p}-\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{p\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})}{p-i}\alpha_{1}^{p-i}+\beta.

Lemma 8.1
.

The polynomial
h_{0,2,p}(\text{mod }p)
is reducible in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{p}[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{p-2},\beta]
, for all prime
p>3
.

Proof.

We have,

\displaystyle f_{0,2,p}

\displaystyle=f^{2}(\alpha_{1})-\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=(\beta^{p}-\alpha_{1}^{p})+\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{p\cdot s_{i}(\bar{\alpha})}{p-i}(\beta^{p-i}-\alpha_{1}^{p-i})+(\beta-\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\equiv(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p}+(\beta-\alpha_{1})\;(\textnormal{mod }p).

As
f_{0,1,p}=\beta-\alpha_{1}
, we have

h_{0,2,p}\equiv(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{p-1}+1\;(\textnormal{mod }p).

For
p\equiv 1(\text{mod }4)
,
-1
being a square modulo
p
,
h_{0,2,p}
is reducible over
{\mathbbm{F}}_{p}
. For
p\equiv 3(\text{mod }4),p\neq 3
, the integer
p-1
has at least one odd prime factor, say
q
. Then
(\beta-\alpha_{1})^{q}+1
divides
h_{0,2,p}
modulo
p
.
So,
h_{0,2,p}\;(\text{mod }p)
is reducible for any prime
p>3
.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

Lemma
8.1
shows that modulo
p
, the polynomial
h_{0,2,p}
is reducible, for any prime
p>3
. So, one can not extend the proof for
(k,1,p)
case directly to this case.

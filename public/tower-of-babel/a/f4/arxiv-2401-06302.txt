Mixed-permutation channel with its application to estimate quantum coherence
Lin Zhang and, Ming-Jing Zhao

Abstract
Quantum channel, as the information transmitter, is an indispensable tool in quantum information theory. In this paper, we study a class of special quantum channels named the mixed-permutation channels . The properties of these channels are characterized. The mixed-permutation channels can be applied to give a lower bound of quantum coherence with respect to any coherence measure. In particular, the analytical lower bounds for l 1 l_{1} -norm coherence and the relative entropy of coherence are shown respectively. The extension to bipartite systems is presented for the actions of the mixed-permutation channels.

2
Mixed-permutation channel and its application in estimating quantum coherence

In this section, we consider the
d
-dimensional quantum system with
its Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and fix the reference basis as

\{|i\rangle\}_{i=1}^{d}
. The so-called quantum state is represented
by the density matrices/operators acting on the Hilbert space

\mathbb{C}^{d}
. We denote the set of all density matrices by

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)=\left\{\rho=\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\rho_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|:\rho\geqslant 0,\trace\left(\rho\right)=1\right\}.

Any quantum channel
\Lambda
acting on the density
matrices/operators in
\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
can be expressed with
Kraus representation
\Lambda(\cdot)=\sum_{\mu}K_{\mu}(\cdot)K_{\mu}^{\dagger}
where
\sum_{\mu}K_{\mu}^{\dagger}K_{\mu}=\mathbb{1}_{d}
, the
identity operator on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Next we shall introduce the
mixed-permutation channels and characterize it both analytically and
geometrically. Then we explain its application in estimating quantum
coherence.

2.1
Mixed-permutation channels

Definition 1
.

For any matrix
\boldsymbol{O}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we define the

mixed-permutation channel

\Delta
as

\displaystyle\Delta(\boldsymbol{O}):=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{O}\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi},

(1)

where
S_{d}
is the symmetric group of permutations of
d
elements,
and the permutation matrix
\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}
is

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}|\pi(i)\rangle\langle i|.

(2)

induced by
\pi\in S_{d}
.

By this definition we see for any matrix
\boldsymbol{O}
acting on

\mathbb{C}^{d}
, the action of the mixed-permutation channel is the
average action over all possible permutation matrices. It is easily
seen that
\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi\sigma}
and

\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi^{-1}}
for any
\pi
and

\sigma
in
S_{d}
. Moreover,
\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{O}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}
. The properties of the
mixed-permutation channel
\Delta
can be listed below:

(i)

\Delta\circ\Delta=\Delta
, that is
\Delta\circ\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})=\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})
for any matrix
\boldsymbol{O}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

(ii)

\Delta
is unital, that is
\Delta(\mathbb{1}_{d})=\mathbb{1}_{d}
, where
\mathbb{1}_{d}
is the identity matrix on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

(iii)

\Delta
is self-adjoint with respect to Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, i.e.,
\Delta=\Delta^{\dagger}
in the sense of
\left\langle\Delta^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{X}),\boldsymbol{Y}\right\rangle=\left\langle\boldsymbol{X},\Delta(\boldsymbol{Y})\right\rangle
for all matrices
\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{Y}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, that is,
\Delta^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{O})=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{O}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}=\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})
.

(iv)

[\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}=\Delta(\boldsymbol{O}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}})
, that is
[\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}=(\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{O}\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi})^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{O}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}=\Delta(\boldsymbol{O}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}})
.

(v)

\Delta
is invariant under any permutation matrices, that is,
\boldsymbol{P}_{\tau}\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\tau}=\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})
for any permutation matrix
\boldsymbol{P}_{\tau}
, induced by the permutation
\tau\in S_{d}
.

Furthermore, the explicit form of the mixed-permutation channel on the

\mathbb{C}^{d}
can be obtained analytically.

Theorem 1
.

For any
d\times d
matrix
\boldsymbol{O}=(o_{ij})
, the mixed-permutation
channel can be characterized as

\displaystyle\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})=\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d}}{d}+\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}(\boldsymbol{E}-\mathbb{1}_{d})\right)\frac{(\boldsymbol{E}-\mathbb{1}_{d})}{d(d-1)},

(3)

where
\boldsymbol{E}:=|\boldsymbol{e}\rangle\!\langle\boldsymbol{e}|
for
d
-dimensional vector

\boldsymbol{e}=(1,1,\ldots,1)^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
with all entries being one.

Proof.

In fact, this result will be established by linearity once we show
it holds for any Hermitian matrix
\boldsymbol{O}
because every complex
square matrix
\boldsymbol{M}
can be represented as a complex linear
combination of two Hermitian matrices, i.e.,

\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{H}+\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{K}
, where
\boldsymbol{H}:=\frac{\boldsymbol{M}+\boldsymbol{M}^{\dagger}}{2}

and
\boldsymbol{K}=\frac{\boldsymbol{M}-\boldsymbol{M}^{\dagger}}{2\mathrm{i}}
. In what follows,
it suffices to consider Hermitian case.

Let us assume that
\boldsymbol{O}
is
Hermitian. If
i\neq j
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle i\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|j\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\left\langle i\left|\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{O}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\right|j\right\rangle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\left\langle\pi(i)\left|\boldsymbol{O}\right|\pi(j)\right\rangle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}o_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}.

Now there exists a permutation
\tau_{0}\in S_{d}
such that

i=\tau_{0}(1)
and
j=\tau_{0}(2)
due to the assumption
i\neq j
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}o_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}=\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}o_{\pi\tau_{0}(1)\pi\tau_{0}(2)}=\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}o_{\pi(1)\pi(2)},

implying that, whenever
i\neq j
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle i\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|j\right\rangle=\left\langle 1\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|2\right\rangle.

Similarly, if
i=j
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle i\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|i\right\rangle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}o_{\pi(i)\pi(i)}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}o_{\pi(1)\pi(1)}=\left\langle 1\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|1\right\rangle.

Note that
\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)=\trace\left(\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right)=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\left\langle i\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|i\right\rangle=d\left\langle 1\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|1\right\rangle
,
which means that

\displaystyle\left\langle i\left|\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\right|i\right\rangle=\frac{\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d}.

From the above discussion, let
\boldsymbol{E}:=|\boldsymbol{e}\rangle\!\langle\boldsymbol{e}|
, where

\boldsymbol{e}=(1,\ldots,1)^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
. We see that

\displaystyle\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})=\frac{\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d}\mathbb{1}_{d}+\zeta(\boldsymbol{E}-\mathbb{1}_{d}).

Then, via
\boldsymbol{E}^{2}=d\cdot\boldsymbol{E}
,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\boldsymbol{E}=\frac{\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d}\boldsymbol{E}^{2}+\zeta(\boldsymbol{E}^{3}-\boldsymbol{E}^{2})=[\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)+\zeta d(d-1)]\boldsymbol{E}.

Because
\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\boldsymbol{E}=\boldsymbol{E}
, we get that
\boldsymbol{E}\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})\boldsymbol{E}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{O}\boldsymbol{E}=\left\langle\boldsymbol{e}\left|\boldsymbol{O}\right|\boldsymbol{e}\right\rangle\boldsymbol{E}
. From these, we see that

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{e}\left|\boldsymbol{O}\right|\boldsymbol{e}\right\rangle=\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)+\zeta d(d-1),

from which we get

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{\left\langle\boldsymbol{e}\left|\boldsymbol{O}\right|\boldsymbol{e}\right\rangle-\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d(d-1)}=\frac{2\mathrm{Re}(o_{ij})}{d(d-1)}=\binom{d}{2}^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(o_{ij}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\Delta(\boldsymbol{O})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d}\mathbb{1}_{d}+\zeta(\boldsymbol{E}-\mathbb{1}_{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d}\mathbb{1}_{d}+\frac{\left\langle\boldsymbol{e}\left|\boldsymbol{O}\right|\boldsymbol{e}\right\rangle-\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d(d-1)}(\boldsymbol{E}-\mathbb{1}_{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d}\mathbb{1}_{d}+\frac{\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\boldsymbol{E}\right)-\trace\left(\boldsymbol{O}\right)}{d(d-1)}(\boldsymbol{E}-\mathbb{1}_{d}).

After simplifying it then we get the desired expression. This
completes the proof.
∎

In Theorem
1
, when
\boldsymbol{O}
is taken as a quantum state

\rho\in\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
, i.e., if a quantum state
\rho
goes
through the mixed-permutation channel
\Delta
, then the output

\Delta(\rho)
can be characterized analytically.

Corollary 1
.

For any quantum state
\rho=\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\rho_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\in\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
, the corresponding output state

\rho_{\star}:=\Delta(\rho)
from the mixed-permutation channel is
given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\star}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}d^{-1}&\mathfrak{a}&\cdots&\mathfrak{a}\\
\mathfrak{a}&d^{-1}&\cdots&\mathfrak{a}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathfrak{a}&\mathfrak{a}&\cdots&d^{-1}\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}:=\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\rho_{ij}

(9)

for the given reference basis
\{|i\rangle:1,\ldots,d\}
.

Remark 1
.

We remark here that
\mathfrak{a}\in\left[-\frac{1}{d(d-1)},\frac{1}{d}\right]
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}\rho_{ij}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\rho_{ij}-1=\trace\left(\rho\boldsymbol{E}\right)-1=\left\langle\boldsymbol{e}\left|\rho\right|\boldsymbol{e}\right\rangle-1.

Therefore, combining

d\lambda_{\min}(\rho)\leqslant\left\langle\boldsymbol{e}\left|\rho\right|\boldsymbol{e}\right\rangle\leqslant d\lambda_{\max}(\rho)
, where
\lambda_{\min}(\rho)
and
\lambda_{\max}(\rho)
are the minimum and maximum eigenvalues of the quantum state
\rho
respectively, we have,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\leqslant\frac{d\lambda_{\min}(\rho)-1}{d(d-1)}\leqslant\mathfrak{a}=\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\rho_{ij}\leqslant\frac{d\lambda_{\max}(\rho)-1}{d(d-1)}\leqslant\frac{1}{d}.

(10)

In order to see clearly what is the image states
\Delta(\rho)
of
the channel
\Delta
when input state is
\rho
, let us focus on
qubit systems. In fact, any qubit state can be represented as

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{1}_{2}+\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+r_{3}&r_{1}-\mathrm{i}r_{2}\\
r_{1}+\mathrm{i}r_{2}&1-r_{3}\end{array}\right),

where
r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
r:=\left\lvert\mspace{1mu}\boldsymbol{r}\mspace{1mu}\right\rvert=\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+r_{3}^{2}}\leqslant 1
, and

\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}:=\sum^{3}_{k=1}r_{k}\sigma_{k}
,

\boldsymbol{\sigma}:=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
is the vector
of the Pauli matrices, given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\mathrm{i}\\
\mathrm{i}&0\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right).

It is easily seen that the action of
\Delta
is

\displaystyle\Delta(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{1}_{2}+\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&r_{1}\\
r_{1}&1\end{array}\right).

So the action of the mixed-permutation channel on the set of quantum
states can be simulated by the transformation on the Bloch ball,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\rightarrow\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}=(r_{1},0,0).

(24)

From Figure

2.2
Applying the mixed-permutation channel to estimate quantum coherence

In order to estimate quantum coherence using the mixed-permutation
channel, we need recall some notions about quantum coherence. Given
the reference basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=1}^{d}
, a diagonal quantum
state
\varrho=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}|i\rangle\!\langle i|
is called the
incoherent state. The set of incoherent states is denoted as
\mathcal{I}
.
The so-called incoherent operation is a completely positive linear
mapping
\Phi
such that its Kraus decomposition

\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{\nu}K_{\nu}\rho K_{\nu}^{\dagger}
with
\sum_{\nu}K_{\nu}^{\dagger}K_{\nu}=\mathbb{1}
fulfills
K_{\nu}\delta K_{\nu}^{\dagger}/\trace\left(K_{\nu}\delta K_{\nu}^{\dagger}\right)\in\mathcal{I}
for all

\delta\in\mathcal{I}
and for all
\nu
.

The quantum coherence is quantified by a nonnegative function named
the coherence measure. A coherence measure is required to satisfy
the following conditions
[
22
]
:

(A1)

Nonnegativity:
C(\rho)\geqslant 0
for
\rho\in\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
, and moreover
C(\rho)=0
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{I}
;

(A2)

Monotonicity:

C(\Phi(\rho))\leqslant C(\rho)
for any incoherent operation

\Phi
;

(A3)

Strong monotonicity:
\sum_{\nu}p_{\nu}C(K_{\nu}\rho K_{\nu}^{\dagger}/p_{\nu})\leqslant C(\rho)
for any incoherent
operation
\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{\nu}K_{\nu}\rho K_{\nu}^{\dagger}
with

p_{\nu}=\trace\left(K_{\nu}\rho K_{\nu}^{\dagger}\right)
and

\rho_{\nu}=K_{\nu}\rho K_{\nu}^{\dagger}/p_{\nu}
;

(A4)

Convexity:
C(\rho)\leqslant\sum_{k}p_{k}C(\rho_{k})
for
any quantum state
\rho=\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho_{k}
with
p_{k}\geqslant 0
and
\sum_{k}p_{k}=1
.

Two commonly used coherence measures are the
l_{1}
-norm coherence
and the relative entropy of coherence
[
22
]
. The

l_{1}
-norm coherence of the quantum state
\rho=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|
is the sum of the magnitudes of all the
off-diagonal entries

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(\rho):=\sum_{i\neq j}\left\lvert\mspace{1mu}\rho_{ij}\mspace{1mu}\right\rvert.

(25)

The relative entropy of coherence is the difference of von Neumann entropy between the density matrix and the diagonal matrix given by its diagonal entries,

\displaystyle C_{r}(\rho):=S(\Pi(\rho))-S(\rho),

(26)

where
\Pi(\rho)=\mathrm{diag}(\rho_{11},\rho_{22},\cdots,\rho_{dd})
is
the diagonal matrix obtained by the diagonal entries of
\rho
and

S(\rho):=-\trace\left(\rho\ln\rho\right)
is the von Neumann entropy of the state

\rho
.

Now we come back to the mixed-permutation channel. It is obvious
that
\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}
is incoherent for any
incoherent state
\rho
. So the mixed-permutation channel is an
incoherent operation. By the monotonicity of the coherence measures,
we know the coherence of input state
\rho
is nonincreasing under
this channel. Since the output states of the mixed-permutation
channel have an analytical form
\rho_{\star}
in
Eq. (
1
), we can utilize it to estimate the coherence
of the input state
\rho
.

Theorem 2
.

For any coherence measure
C
and any quantum state

\rho\in\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
, the quantum coherence of
\rho
is
bounded from below by the quantum coherence of
\rho_{\star}
,
namely,

\displaystyle C(\rho)\geqslant C(\rho_{\star}).

(27)

Proof.

First, since any permutation matrices
\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}
and

\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi^{-1}}
are incoherent operations, so

C(\rho)=C[\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi})\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}]\leqslant C(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi})\leqslant C(\rho).

Therefore
C(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi})=C(\rho)
for any
permutation matrcix
\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}
. Then by the convexity of coherence
measure, we have further that

\displaystyle C(\rho_{\star})=C(\Delta(\rho))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}C\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}C(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\rho),

which completes the proof.
∎

From the proof above, we see that Theorem
2
is universal in
the sense that Eq. (

) is independent of the
coherence measures. So Theorem
2
can be applied to all
coherence measures. Additionally, the output states
\rho_{\star}

is of one parameter, so its coherence is much easy to calculate

In fact, for any quantum state

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}:=\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{i\neq j}\rho_{ij}\in\left[-\frac{1}{d-1},1\right]

(29)

and
|\Phi_{d}\rangle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum^{d}_{i=1}|i\rangle
is the
maximally coherent state
[

]
. This state
\rho_{\star}

is also called the
maximally coherent mixed state
in

[

]
. By this decomposition in Eq. (

), we get

and

with

. In view of this, we get the following result:

Corollary 2
.

For any quantum state
\rho\in\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle d(d-1)\left\lvert\mspace{1mu}\mathfrak{a}\mspace{1mu}\right\rvert,

(30)

\displaystyle C_{r}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)(1-\mathfrak{p})\ln(1-\mathfrak{p})+\frac{1}{d}[(d-1)\mathfrak{p}+1]\ln[(d-1)\mathfrak{p}+1],

(31)

respectively, where
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{p}
are depended
by
\rho
as Eqs. (
9
) and (
29
).

For
l_{1}
norm coherence, the lower bound in Eq. (

) is tight for

quantum states, that is,

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(\rho)=C_{l_{1}}(\rho_{\star})

(32)

for any
real

\rho

From another point of view, it is
interesting to estimate quantum coherence from above.
Let us turn to another quantity called the
coherence of
assistance
, induced by any coherence measure
C
, which is defined
in the form of concave bottom extension,

\displaystyle C_{a}(\rho)=\max\sum_{k}p_{k}C(|\psi_{k}\rangle),

(33)

where the maximization is taken over all pure state decompositions
of
\rho=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|
. Coherence of assistance
quantifies the coherence that can be extracted assisted by another
party under local measurements and classical communication

[
31
]
. Suppose Alice holds a state

\rho^{A}=\rho=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|
with coherence
C(\rho)
. Bob
holds another part of the purified state of
\rho
. The joint state
between Alice and Bob is
\sum_{k}p_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle_{A}\otimes|k\rangle_{B}
.
Bob performs local projective measurements
\left\{|k\rangle\!\langle k|\right\}
along
the given basis and informs Alice the measurement outcomes by
classical communication. Alice’s system will be in a pure state
ensemble
\left\{p_{k},|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|\right\}
with average coherence
\sum_{k}p_{k}C(|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|)
. The process is called assisted coherence
distillation. The maximum average coherence is called the coherence
of assistance which quantifies the one-way coherence distillation
rate
[
31
]
.

The coherence of assistance of any quantum state

\rho
can be estimated by the output state

\rho_{\star}=\Delta(\rho)
.

Theorem 3
.

For any coherence measure
C
and any quantum state

\rho\in\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
, the coherence of assistance of
\rho

is bounded from above by the coherence of assistance of

\rho_{\star}=\Delta(\rho)
,

\displaystyle C_{a}(\rho)\leqslant C_{a}(\rho_{\star}).

(34)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle C_{a}(\rho_{\star})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{a}(\Delta(\rho))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{a}\left(\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}C_{a}(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{a}(\rho).

Here the inequality is the concavity of the coherence of assistance.
The last equality is due to the equality
C_{a}(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\rho\boldsymbol{P}^{\dagger}_{\pi})=C_{a}(\rho)
for any permutation
\pi\in S_{d}
.
∎

Mixed-permutation channel on bipartite systems

In this section we study the mixed-permutation channel on bipartite
systems
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
. Without loss of
generality, we suppose
d_{A}\leqslant d_{B}
. Suppose that

\{|i\rangle\}^{d_{A}}_{i=1}
and
\{|j\rangle\}^{d_{B}}_{j=1}
are
rothonormal bases of the subsystems
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}
and

\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, respectively, we denote by

\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}\right)=\left\{\rho_{AB}=\sum^{d_{A}}_{i,i^{\prime}=1}\sum^{d_{B}}_{j,j^{\prime}=1}\rho_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime},ij}|i^{\prime}j^{\prime}\rangle\langle ij|:\rho_{AB}\geqslant 0,\trace\left(\rho_{AB}\right)=1\right\}

the set of density operators acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
. The identical channel on operator spaces are
denoted by
\mathrm{id}
. By Theorem
1
, we
derive the explicit form of the mixed-permutation channel on bipartite systems
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}

Theorem 4
.

For any bipartite operator
\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, denote
\boldsymbol{E}_{A(B)}=|\boldsymbol{e}_{A(B)}\rangle\!\langle\boldsymbol{e}_{A(B)}|
where

\boldsymbol{e}_{A(B)}
is a
d_{A(B)}
-dimensional column vector with all
entries being one, we have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B})(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})=\frac{\mathbb{1}_{A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\trace_{A}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}\right)+\frac{\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{A}}{d_{A}(d_{A}-1)}\otimes\trace_{A}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{A})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)

(35)

and thus

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\otimes\Delta_{B})(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})\mathbb{1}_{AB}+c_{1}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})\mathbb{1}_{A}\otimes(\boldsymbol{E}_{B}-\mathbb{1}_{B})+c_{2}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{A})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}

(36)

\displaystyle+c_{3}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{A})\otimes(\boldsymbol{E}_{B}-\mathbb{1}_{B}).

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}c_{0}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})=\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}\trace\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}\right),\\
c_{1}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})=\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}(d_{B}-1)}\trace\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{A})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right),\\
c_{2}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})=\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}(d_{A}-1)}\trace\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}\mathbb{1}_{A}\otimes(\boldsymbol{E}_{B}-\mathbb{1}_{B})\right),\\
c_{3}(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB})=\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}(d_{A}-1)(d_{B}-1)}\trace\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{A})\otimes(\boldsymbol{E}_{B}-\mathbb{1}_{B})\right).\end{cases}

(37)

Proof.

The result can be also established by linearity once we show it
holds for any Hermitian matrix
\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}
. So it suffices to consider
the Hermitian case.

(i)

In fact, for
\boldsymbol{X}_{AB}=|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{AB}|
where

|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, we suppose its Schmidt
decomposition is

|\Psi_{AB}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle_{A}\otimes|\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\rangle_{B},

with
\{|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle_{A}\}_{i=1}^{d_{A}}
and
\{|\boldsymbol{b}_{j}\rangle_{B}\}_{j=1}^{d_{B}}
the orthnormal bases of the subsystems
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}
and
\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
respectively,
then the density operator of
|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{AB}|
can be expressed as

|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{AB}|=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j}|_{A}\otimes|\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{b}_{j}|_{B}.

In the following reasoning, we omit the subindexes
A
and
B
when
no confusing arises. The action of the mixed-permutation channel on
the first subsystem is then

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B})(|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{AB}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\Delta_{A}(|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j}|)\otimes|\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{b}_{j}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\left[\trace\left(|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j}|\right)\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}}+\trace\left(|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j}|(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})\right)\frac{(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})}{d_{A}(d_{A}-1)}\right]\otimes|\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{b}_{j}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}}\otimes\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\trace\left(|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j}|\right)|\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{b}_{j}|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})}{d_{A}(d_{A}-1)}\otimes\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\trace\left(|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j}|(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})\right)|\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{b}_{j}|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}}\otimes\trace_{A}(|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{AB}|)+\frac{(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})}{d_{A}(d_{A}-1)}\otimes\trace_{A}(|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{AB}|(\boldsymbol{E}_{A}-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}}).

By the linearity of both sides, this equality holds for any positive
semi-definite bipartite operator, and thus for a general bipartite
operator.

If we focus on the bipartite quantum states, then we get an output
state of the one-sided and two-sided mixed-permutation channels as

Corollary 3
.

For any quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{D}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}\right)
, if the first subsystem goes through the mixed-permutation channel, then the output state is

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B})(\rho_{AB})=\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}}\otimes\rho_{B}+\frac{d_{A}|\Phi_{d_{A}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{A}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}(d_{A}-1)}\otimes\trace_{A}\left(\rho_{AB}(d_{A}|\Phi_{d_{A}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{A}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}}\right),

(38)

where
\rho_{B}=\trace_{A}(\rho_{AB})
. If both subsystems go through the
separate mixed-permutation channels, the output state is

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\otimes\Delta_{B})(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}}\otimes\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}}}{d_{B}}+\gamma_{1}(\rho_{AB})\frac{\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}}\otimes\frac{d_{B}|\Phi_{d_{B}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{B}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}}}{d_{B}(d_{B}-1)}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{2}(\rho_{AB})\frac{d_{A}|\Phi_{d_{A}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{A}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}}{d_{A}(d_{A}-1)}\otimes\frac{\mathbb{1}_{B}}{d_{B}}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{3}(\rho_{AB})\frac{d_{A}|\Phi_{d_{A}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{A}}|-\mathbb{1}_{A}}{d_{A}(d_{A}-1)}\otimes\frac{d_{B}|\Phi_{d_{B}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{B}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}}}{d_{B}(d_{B}-1)}.

Here
|\Phi_{d_{A}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}|\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\rangle
and
|\Phi_{d_{B}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{B}}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{B}}|\boldsymbol{b}_{j}\rangle
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\trace\left(\rho_{AB}(d_{A}|\Phi_{d_{A}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{A}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}}\right),

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\trace\left(\rho_{AB}\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}}\otimes(d_{B}|\Phi_{d_{B}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{B}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}})\right),

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\trace\left(\rho_{AB}(d_{A}|\Phi_{d_{A}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{A}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{A}})\otimes(d_{B}|\Phi_{d_{B}}\rangle\!\langle\Phi_{d_{B}}|-\mathbb{1}_{d_{B}})\right).

By the form of the output states
(\Delta_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B})(\rho_{AB})
and
(\Delta_{A}\otimes\Delta_{B})(\rho_{AB})
in the above

3

(\Delta_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B})(\rho_{AB})
and

are positive
under partial transpositions (PPT) for all states
\rho_{AB}

PPT states in qubit-qubit systems and qubit-qutrit systems are all
separable, so the action of the local mixed-permutation channel(s)
on two-qubit systems will completely erase the entanglement between
two subsystems.

For any quantum state
\rho_{AB}

4
Conclusions and discussions

In summary, we studied one kind of special channels, i.e., the
mixed-permutation channels. The properties of this channel was
characterized. The mixed-permutation channel can be used in bounding
quantum coherence with respect to all coherence measures. The action
of the mixed-permutation channel on bipartite systems is also
discussed and can be generalized to multipartite systems. Beyond
that, there are some interesting problems which can be further
considered in the future:

•

The first question is what is the average collective action of
the permutations on the bipartite quantum states,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\otimes\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi})\boldsymbol{X}(\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi}\otimes\boldsymbol{P}_{\pi})^{\dagger}=?.

We are expecting a closed-form formula of the above expression. With
this potential formula, we can extend symmetrized variance for pure
states in
[
28
]
to mixed states.

•

The second question
is what is the correlation quantifiers
[
37
]
or coherence
measure
[
38
]
induced by the mixed-permutation channel.

In light of the special properties and the closed form of the output
state of the mixed-permutation channel, we expect the corresponding
correlation measure and coherence measure could characterize the
quantum features of quantum states from a permutation-invariant way.

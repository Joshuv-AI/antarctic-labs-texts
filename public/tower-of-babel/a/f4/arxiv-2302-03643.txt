Top-degree components of Grothendieck and Lascoux polynomials
Jianping Pan, Tianyi Yu

Abstract
. The Castelnuovo–Mumford polynomial 𝔊 ^ w \widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w} with w ∈ S n w\in S_{n} is the highest homogeneous component of the Grothendieck polynomial 𝔊 w \mathfrak{G}_{w} . Pechenik, Speyer and Weigandt define a statistic 𝗋𝖺𝗃𝖼𝗈𝖽𝖾 ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \mathsf{rajcode}(\cdot) on S n S_{n} that gives the leading monomial of 𝔊 ^ w \widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w} . We introduce a statistic 𝗋𝖺𝗃𝖼𝗈𝖽𝖾 ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \mathsf{rajcode}(\cdot) on any diagram D D through a combinatorial construction “snow diagram” that augments and decorates D D . When D D is the Rothe diagram of a permutation w w , 𝗋𝖺𝗃𝖼𝗈𝖽𝖾 ⁡ ( D ) \mathsf{rajcode}(D) agrees with the aforementioned 𝗋𝖺𝗃𝖼𝗈𝖽𝖾 ⁡ ( w ) \mathsf{rajcode}(w) . When D D is the key diagram of a weak composition α \alpha , 𝗋𝖺𝗃𝖼𝗈𝖽𝖾 ⁡ ( D ) \mathsf{rajcode}(D) yields the leading monomial of 𝔏 ^ α \widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha} , the highest homogeneous component of the Lascoux polynomials 𝔏 α \mathfrak{L}_{\alpha} . We use 𝔏 ^ α \widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha} to construct a basis of V ^ n \widehat{V}_{n} , the span of 𝔊 ^ w \widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w} with w ∈ S n w\in S_{n} . Then we show V ^ n \widehat{V}_{n} gives a natural algebraic interpretation of a classical q q -analogue of Bell numbers.

Introduction

Introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
LS82a
]
,
the Grothendieck polynomial
\mathfrak{G}_{w}

is a polynomial representative of the

K
-class of structure sheaves of
Schubert varieties of flag varieties.
It is the inhomogeneous analogue
of the Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
:
The lowest-degree component
of
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
forms
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
.
Pechenik, Speyer and Weigandt
[
PSW21
]
introduce the
Castelnuovo–Mumford polynomial
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
1

1

Pechenik, Speyer and Weigandt
[
PSW21
]
denote it as
\mathfrak{CM}_{w}
.
,
the top-degree component of
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
.
They describe the leading monomial of
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}

with respect to the tail lexicographic order
by defining a new statistic

\mathsf{rajcode}(\cdot)
on
S_{n}
.
We summarize some of their results on
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
PSW21
]
)
.

Let
w,u
be permutations in
S_{n}
.

(A)

The polynomial
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
has leading monomial
x^{\mathsf{rajcode}(w)}
.

(B)

We have

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
is a scalar multiple of
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{u}

if and only if
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)=\mathsf{rajcode}(u)
.

(C)

If
w
is inverse fireworks
(see §
5
),
then
x^{\mathsf{rajcode}(w)}
has
coefficient 1 in
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
.
Moreover, there exists
exactly one
u^{\prime}\in S_{n}
that is inverse fireworks
such that
\mathsf{rajcode}(u)=\mathsf{rajcode}(u^{\prime})
.

Dreyer, Mészáros and St. Dizier
[
DMS22
]

provide an alternative proof of (A) via the
climbing chain model for Grothendieck polynomials
introduced by Lenart, Robinson, and Sottile
[
LRS06
]
.
Hafner
[
Haf22
]
provides an alternative proof of (A)
for vexillary permutations via bumpless pipedreams.

Schubert polynomials are related
to key polynomials
\kappa_{\alpha}

which are indexed by weak compositions.
The key polynomials are the characters of
Demazure modules
[
Dem74
]
.
Both Schubert and key polynomials
can be defined recursively
via the divided difference operators
(see §
2
).
In addition, Schubert polynomials
expand positively into key polynomials
[
RS95
]
.
The key polynomials also have
inhomogeneous analogues called Lascoux polynomials
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}

[
Las03
]
.
Grothendieck polynomials and Lascoux polynomials are related:
An expansion of Grothendieck polynomials into Lascoux polynomials
was conjectured by Reiner and Yong
[
RY21
]
and
proven by Shimozono and Yu
[
SY23
]
.

Due to the connection between
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
, one would expect the top Lascoux polynomial
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
, the top-degree component of
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
, to parallel
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
.
We define a statistic
\mathsf{rajcode}(\cdot)
on weak compositions and show in §
4
that
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
enjoy properties analogous to the properties of
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
listed in Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha
and
\gamma
be two weak compositions.

(a)

The polynomial
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
has leading monomial
x^{\mathsf{rajcode}(\alpha)}
.

(b)

We have

\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
is a scalar multiple of
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\gamma}

if and only if
\mathsf{rajcode}(\alpha)=\mathsf{rajcode}(\gamma)
.

(c)

We say
\alpha
is snowy if its
positive entries are distinct.
If
\alpha
is snowy, then
x^{\mathsf{rajcode}(\alpha)}
has coefficient 1 in
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
.
Moreover,
there exists exactly one snowy weak composition
\gamma^{\prime}

such that
\mathsf{rajcode}(\gamma)=\mathsf{rajcode}(\gamma^{\prime})
.

Our definition of
\mathsf{rajcode}(\cdot)

on weak compositions is diagrammatic.
Given a diagram
D
,
we define a combinatorial
construction called the snow diagram
that augments and decorates
D
.
Let
\mathsf{rajcode}(D)

be the weight of the snow diagram.
Every weak composition
\alpha
is naturally
associated with a diagram called
the key diagram
D(\alpha)

(see Subsection
2.2
).
Then we define
\mathsf{rajcode}(\alpha):=\mathsf{rajcode}(D(\alpha))
.

Snow diagrams unify the computation of leading monomials in
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
.
Each permutation
w
is also associated
with a diagram called the Rothe diagram

RD(w)
.
In §
5
,
we show
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)=\mathsf{rajcode}(RD(w))
.
In other words, we give a diagrammatic way to compute
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)
.

Finally, let
\widehat{V}_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}:w\in S_{n}\}
and
\widehat{V}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\widehat{V}_{n}
.
In Proposition
2.7
,
we show
\widehat{V}

is a filtered algebra.
Theorem
1.1
can be used to construct a basis of
\widehat{V}_{n}

and
\widehat{V}

consisting of
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
.
In particular,
the dimension of
\widehat{V}_{n}

is
B_{n}
, the
n\textsuperscript{th}
Bell number.
In §
6
,
we use Theorem
1.2
to construct another basis consisting of
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
.
This basis allows us to compute the Hilbert series of
\widehat{V}_{n}

and
\widehat{V}

involving a
q
-analogue of
B_{n}
.

2.
Background

2.1.
Polynomials

We provide necessary background
for Grothendieck polynomials and Lascoux polynomials.
Then we introduce

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}

which span the spaces
\widehat{V}_{n}
and
\widehat{V}
.

The
Grothendieck polynomials

\mathfrak{G}_{w}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots][\beta]

were recursively defined by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
LS82a
]
.
Let
\partial_{i}(\cdot)
be the divided difference operators acting
on the polynomial ring.
For each
i
,
define
\partial_{i}(f):=\dfrac{f-s_{i}f}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}
,
where
s_{i}
is the operator that swaps
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
.
Then for
w\in S_{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{w}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{n-2}\cdots x_{n-1}&\text{if $w$ is $[n,n-1,\dots,1]$ in one-line notation,}\\
\partial_{i}((1+\beta x_{i+1})\mathfrak{G}_{ws_{i}})&\text{if $w(i)<w(i+1)$.}\end{cases}

Let
S_{+}
be the set of permutations
of
\{1,2,\dots\}

such that only finitely many numbers are permuted.
Take
w\in S_{+}
and assume
w
only permutes numbers in
[n]
.
Let
w^{\prime}\in S_{n}
be the restriction of
w
to
[n]

and define
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
as
\mathfrak{G}_{w^{\prime}}
.
It is shown in
[
LS82a
]
that
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
is well-defined.

A
weak composition

is an infinite sequence of non-negative integers with finitely many positive entries.
Let
C_{+}
be the set of weak compositions.
For
\alpha\in C_{+}
,
we use
\alpha_{i}
to denote its
i
th
entry,
and write
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})

where
\alpha_{n}
is the last positive entry.
We use
x^{\alpha}
to denote the monomial

x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
and

|\alpha|=\sum_{i\geqslant 1}^{n}\alpha_{i}
.
The
Lascoux polynomials

\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
, indexed by weak compositions,
are in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots][\beta]
.
By
[
Las03
]
, they are defined recursively

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}x^{\alpha}&\text{if $\alpha$ is weakly decreasing,}\\
\pi_{i}((1+\beta x_{i+1})\mathfrak{L}_{s_{i}\alpha})&\text{if $\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{i+1}$,}\end{cases}

where
\pi_{i}
is the operator
\pi_{i}(f):=\partial_{i}(x_{i}f)
.

We say a pair
(i,j)
is an
inversion

of
w\in S_{n}
if
i<j
and
w(i)>w(j)
.
Let
\mathsf{Inv}(w)
be the set of all inversions in
w

and let
\mathsf{inv}(w)=|\mathsf{Inv}(w)|
.
Then we may view
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
as a polynomial in
\beta
, where

[\beta^{d}]\mathfrak{G}_{w}:=\textrm{coefficient of $\beta^{d}$ in }\mathfrak{G}_{w}

is a homogeneous polynomial in the
x
-variables with degree

\mathsf{inv}(w)+d
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
.
The
Schubert polynomial

\mathfrak{S}_{w}:=[\beta^{0}]\mathfrak{G}_{w}
.
Similarly, viewing
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
as a polynomial of
\beta
,

[\beta^{d}]\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
is a homogeneous polynomial with degree

|\alpha|+d
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
.
The
key polynomial

\kappa_{\alpha}:=[\beta^{0}]\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
.
The representation theoretic, geometric and combinatorial perspectives of Schubert polynomials and key polynomials
are well-studied
[
Dem74
,
LS88
,
BJS93
]
.

Define
V_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\mathfrak{S}_{w}:w\in S_{n}\}

and
V:=\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\mathfrak{S}_{w}:w\in S_{+}\}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}V_{n}
.
In fact,
V=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
.
By the increasing sequence

V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset\cdots\subset V
,

V
has the structure of a filtered algebra.

In this paper, we are interested in the top-degree
components of
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
.
For a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots][\beta]
,
let
\widehat{f}=[\beta^{d}](f)
where
d
is the largest such that

[\beta^{d}](f)\neq 0
.
The
Castelnuovo–Mumford polynomial
of
w\in S_{+}

is defined as
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
.
The
top Lascoux polynomial
of
\alpha\in C_{+}

is defined as
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
. In appendix §
8
, we list some Grothendieck polynomials and Lascoux polynomials.
Pechenik, Speyer and Weigandt
[
PSW21
]
first study
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
.
To the best of the authors knowledge,

\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
has not been studied previously.

Now consider the
tail lexicographic order

on monomials in the
x
-variables.
We say a monomial
x^{\alpha}
is larger than
x^{\gamma}

if there exists
k
such that
\alpha_{k}>\gamma_{k}
and

\alpha_{j}=\gamma_{j}
for all
j>k
.
The
leading monomial
of
f\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]

is the largest monomial in
f
.
Among the four homogeneous
polynomials above,
three of them have combinatorial rules for their leading terms:

(1)

[
BJS93
]
The leading monomial of
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
with
w\in S_{n}

is
x^{\mathsf{invcode}(w)}
,
where

\mathsf{invcode}(w)_{i}=|\{j:(i,j)\in\mathsf{Inv}(w)\}|.

(2)

[
LS89
]
The leading monomial of
\kappa_{\alpha}
is
x^{\alpha}
.

(3)

[
PSW21
]
The leading monomial of
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}

is
x^{\mathsf{rajcode}(w)}
defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

[
PSW21
]

Let
\textup{LIS}^{w}(q)
be the
length of the longest increasing subsequence
of
w\in S_{n}
that starts with
q
.
The
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)
for
w\in S_{n}
is a weak composition
where

\mathsf{rajcode}(w)_{r}:=n+1-r-\textup{LIS}^{w}(w(r))
for
r\in[n]

and
0
if
r>n
.
Then
\mathsf{raj}(w):=|\mathsf{rajcode}(w)|
.

Example 2.2
.

Consider
w=3721564\in S_{7}
. We have
\textup{LIS}^{w}(2)=3
,
so
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)_{3}=7+1-3-3=2
.
All together,
we get
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)=(4,5,2,1,1,1)
and
\mathsf{raj}(w)=14
.

We will define
\mathsf{rajcode}(\cdot)
on
C_{+}

and show the leading monomial of
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
is
x^{\mathsf{rajcode}(\alpha)}

in §
4
.

A connection between
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}

is established by Shimozono and Yu
[
SY23
]
.
To describe this connection,
we need the following notion.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f,f_{1},f_{2},\dots
be polynomials in

\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
.
We say
f

expands positively

into
\{f_{1},f_{2},\dots\}
if there exist

c_{1},c_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

such that
f=\sum_{i}c_{i}f_{i}
.

Now assume
f,f_{1},f_{2},\dots
are polynomials
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[\beta][x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
.
We say
f

expands positively

into
\{f_{1},f_{2},\dots\}
if there exist

g_{1},g_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[\beta]

such that
f=\sum_{i}g_{i}f_{i}
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
SY23
]
)
.

For
w\in S_{+}
,
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
expands positively
into
\{\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{+}\}
.

This result implies

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
also expands
positively into

\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
by the following lemma whose proof
is sufficiently elementary.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f,f_{1},f_{2},\dots

in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[\beta][x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
.
If
f
expands positively
into
\{f_{1},f_{2},\dots\}
,
then
\widehat{f}
expands positively into
\widehat{f_{1}},\widehat{f_{2}},\dots
.

Corollary 2.6
.

For
w\in S_{+}
,

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
expands positively into
\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{+}\}
.

Define
\widehat{V}_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}:w\in S_{n}\}
and
\widehat{V}:=\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}:w\in S_{+}\}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\widehat{V}_{n}
.
By work of Lascoux, Schützenberger
[
LS82b
]
and Brion
[
Bri02
]
,
the product
\mathfrak{G}_{u}\mathfrak{G}_{v}
with
u\in S_{m}
and
v\in S_{n}

expands positively into
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
with
w\in S_{m+n}
.
By Lemma
2.5
,

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{u}\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{v}
with
u\in S_{m}
and
v\in S_{n}
expands positively into
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}

with
w\in S_{m+n}
.
Finally, we conclude the following.

Proposition 2.7
.

The space
\widehat{V}
is a filtered
algebra with respect to the filtration

\widehat{V}_{1}\subset\widehat{V}_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\widehat{V}
.

2.2.
Diagrams

A
diagram
is a finite subset
of
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
We represent a diagram by putting a cell at row
r

and column
c
for each
(r,c)
in the diagram.
The leftmost column (resp. topmost row) is called column
1
(resp. row
1
).
The
weight
of a diagram
D
,
denoted as
\mathsf{wt}(D)

is a weak composition
whose
i
th
entry is the number of boxes
in its row
i
.
We recall two classical families of diagrams.

Each weak composition
\alpha

is associated with a diagram called
the
key diagram
,
denoted as
D(\alpha)
.
It is the unique left-justified diagram
with weight
\alpha
.
One important key diagram we will use later is

\mathsf{Stair}_{n}:=D((n-1,n-2,\cdots,1))
.

5.1.
The Schensted Insertion

If a diagram is top-justified and left-justified,
we say it is a
Young diagram
.
A filling of a Young diagram with positive integers
is called a
tableau
.
A tableau is called
partial

if it contains distinct numbers and
each row (resp. column) is decreasing from left to right
(resp. top to bottom).
Notice that usually in literature, columns and rows
are increasing.
We reverse the convention to make our results easier to state.

The Schensted insertion
[
Sch61
]

is an algorithm defined on a partial tableau
T
and
a positive number
x
that is not in
T
.
It finds the largest
x^{\prime}

in the first row of
T

such that
x>x^{\prime}
.

•

If such
x^{\prime}
does not exist,
it appends
x
at the end of row one
and terminates.

•

Otherwise, it replaces
x^{\prime}
by
x

and insert
x^{\prime}
to the next row
in the same way.

When the algorithm terminates,
the resulting partial tableau is the output.

For
w\in S_{n}
, we insert
w(n),w(n-1),\dots,w(1)
to the empty tableau via the Schensted insertion and denote the result by
P(w)
.

5.3.
Dark Clouds of the Rothe Diagram via Viennot’s geometric construction

In 1977, Xavier Gérard Viennot gave a diagrammatic construction of the RSK correspondence
in terms of shadow lines (
[
Vie77
]
). It is also known as the matrix-ball construction. We will show that the dark clouds in the snow diagram of a permutation can be obtained via Viennot’s geometric construction.
We denote
{\mathsf{{Row}}}_{1}(P(w))
to be the first row of the tableau obtained by Schensted insertion on
w
.

For two cells
(i,j),(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
,

(m,n)
lies in the shadow of
(i,j)
if and only if
m\leq i
and
n\leq j
. This can be visualized by imagining shedding light from the Southeast.

2
2

2

The usual convention can be thought of shedding light from the Northwest, which corresponds to the usual Schensted insertion. We reverse the direction to match our decreasing Schensted insertion convention.

To obtain the
shadow diagram
of
w\in S_{n}
, consider the points
(1,w(1)),\dots,(n,w(n))
. Let
\left(i^{(1)}_{1},w(i^{(1)}_{1})\right),\dots,\left(i^{(1)}_{\ell_{1}},w(i^{(1)}_{\ell_{1}})\right)
be the points that are not in the shadow of any other point for some
\ell_{1}\geqslant 1
and

. Then the first
shadow line

is the boundary of the combined shadows of the points
\left(i^{(1)}_{1},w(i^{(1)}_{1})\right),\dots,\left(i^{(1)}_{\ell_{1}},w(i^{(1)}_{\ell_{1}})\right)

L_{j}(w)

is the boundary of the shadow of the remaining points of the points left, which we label as

\left(i^{(j)}_{1},w(i^{(j)}_{1})\right),\dots\left(i^{(j)}_{\ell_{j}},w(i^{(j)}_{\ell_{j}})\right),

\ell_{j}\geqslant 1

i^{(j)}_{1}>i^{(j)}_{2}>\dots>i^{(j)}_{\ell_{j}}

the rightmost cell in row
r

(resp.
r^{\prime}
)
is at
(r,w(r)-1)

(resp.
(r^{\prime},w(r^{\prime})-1)
).
Clearly,
w(r)-1\neq w(r^{\prime})-1
,
so we have our second statement.

Now we assume
w
is not inverse fireworks.
We can find a number
r
in
w^{-1}

such that
r
is the initial element in
its decreasing run,
but
r
is less than
r^{\prime}
,
the initial element of the
previous decreasing run.
Let
c^{\prime}=w(r^{\prime})
and
c=w(r)
.
Since
(c^{\prime},c)\in\mathsf{Inv}(w^{-1})
,

(r,c^{\prime})\in RD(w)
.
Thus, row
r
of
RD(w)
is not empty.
Let
(r,i)
be the rightmost cell
in row
r
.
In other words,
i
is the largest
such that
(i,c)\in\mathsf{Inv}(w^{-1})
.
We have
c^{\prime}\leqslant i<c-1
.
Consider the decreasing run before
w^{-1}(c)
:

w^{-1}(c^{\prime})>w^{-1}(c^{\prime}+1)>\cdots>w^{-1}(c-1)
.
We see
(i,i+1)
is also in
\mathsf{Inv}(w^{-1})
.
In row
w^{-1}(i+1)
,
the cell
(w^{-1}(i+1),i)
is the
rightmost cell of its row.
Thus, the second statement does not hold,
and the proof is finished.
∎

With the above proposition, we can compute
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)
easily
if
w
is inverse fireworks.
The following rule is similar to Lemma
4.18
(
2
).

Proposition 5.15
.

Assume
w\in S_{n}
is inverse fireworks.
For each
r\in[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{rajcode}(w)_{r}=|\{r^{\prime}>r:

\displaystyle(r,r^{\prime})\in\mathsf{Inv}(w)\textrm{ or }

\displaystyle w(r^{\prime})>w(r)\textrm{ and }(r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime})\in\mathsf{Inv}(w)\textrm{ for some }r^{\prime\prime}\}|.

Proof.

First, we know
\mathsf{rajcode}(w)_{r}=\mathsf{rajcode}(RD(w))_{r}

is the number of cells in the
r
th
row of
\mathsf{snow}(RD(w))
.
The number of non-snowflake cells
on this row is given by
|\{r^{\prime}:(r,r^{\prime})\in\mathsf{Inv}(w)\}|
.

Now we count the number of snowflakes
in row
r
of
\mathsf{snow}(RD(w))
.
It is the number of
r^{\prime}>r

such that row
r^{\prime}
of
\mathsf{snow}(RD(w))
has a dark cloud
on the right of the column
w(r)
.
By Lemma

,
row
r^{\prime}
has a dark cloud
at column

if

.
Thus, the number of snowflakes
in row

of

is the number of

such that

and
(r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime})\in\mathsf{Inv}(w)

for some

.
∎

6.
Vector space spanned by
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}

We now study the vector spaces

\widehat{V}_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}:w\in S_{n}\}

and
\widehat{V}:=\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}:w\in S_{+}\}
.
By Theorem
1.1
,
they have bases

\{\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}:w\in S_{n}\textrm{ is inverse fireworks}\}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\{\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}:w\in S_{+}\textrm{ is inverse fireworks}\}

respectively.
By
[
Cla01
]
,
the number of inverse fireworks permutations in
S_{n}

is
B_{n}
, the
n
th
Bell number.
Thus,
\widehat{V}_{n}
has dimension
B_{n}
.

We introduce another basis of
\widehat{V}_{n}
and
\widehat{V}

consisting of
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
, the top-degree components of Lascoux polynomials.
One application of
the top Lascoux basis
is to compute the Hilbert series of
\widehat{V}_{n}

and
\widehat{V}
.
For a vector space
V\subseteq\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots]
,
the
Hilbert series
of
V
is

\mathrm{Hilb}(V;q):=\sum_{d\geq 0}\,m_{d}q^{d},

where
m_{d}
is the number of polynomials
with degree
d
in a homogeneous
basis of
V
.

In Subsection
6.1
,
we recall the definition of
B_{n}
and its
q
-analogue
B_{n}(q)
.
In Subsection
6.2
, we compute
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)

using
B_{n}(q)
and rook-theoretic results.
In Subsection
6.3
,
we compute
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)
.

6.1.
Stirling numbers, Bell numbers
and their
q
-analogues

Let
n,k
be non-negative integers
throughout this subsection.
Let
S_{n,k}
be the
Stirling number
of the second kind
,
defined by the recurrence relation

S_{n+1,k}=S_{n,k-1}+kS_{n,k},

together with
S_{0,0}=1

and
S_{0,k}=0
if
k>0
.
Let
B_{n}:=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{n,j}

be the Bell number
which satisfies the following recurrence relation

B_{n+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}}B_{j}.

Let
\mathsf{Rook}_{n}
be the set
of non-attacking rook diagrams contained
in
\mathsf{Stair}_{n}
.
It is an exercise to show

B_{n}=|\mathsf{Rook}_{n}|
.
In
[
BCHR10
]
,
Butler, Can, Haglund, and Remmel
built an explicit bijection between

\mathsf{Rook}_{n}
and set partitions of
[n]
.

Now consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Q}[q]
.
Define
[n]_{q}:=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}
.
Define a
q
-analogue of
S_{n,k}
recursively by:

S_{n+1,k}(q)=q^{k-1}S_{n,k-1}(q)+[k]_{q}S_{n,k}(q),

with base cases
S_{0,k}(q)=S_{0,k}
.
Similarly, define a
q
-analogue of
B_{n}
by
B_{n}(q):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{n,j}(q)
.
The coefficients in
B_{n}(q)

are given in OEIS A126347.
By
[
Wag04
]
,

B_{n}(q)
satisfies the recurrence relation

B_{n+1}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}q^{j}{\binom{n}{j}}B_{j}(q).

Milne
[
Mil82
]
first gave a combinatorial
model for
S_{n,k}(q)
using set partitions.
We use the combinatorial model
developed by Garsia and Remmel
[
GR86
]
.
They defined a statistic on
\mathsf{Rook}_{n}

called “inversion”.
We rename it as
\mathsf{GR}_{n}
to distinguish it
from the inversion on permutations.

Definition 6.1

(
[
GR86
]
)
.

Assume
R\in\mathsf{Rook}_{n}
.
For each
(r,c)\in R
,
mark all cells
(r^{\prime},c)
with
r^{\prime}\in[r]
in
\mathsf{Stair}_{n}
.
Also, mark all cells
(r,c^{\prime})
with
c^{\prime}\in[c]
in
\mathsf{Stair}_{n}
.
The number
\mathsf{GR}_{n}(R)
counts cells in
\mathsf{Stair}_{n}

that are not marked.

Garsia and Remmel prove that

(41)

S_{n,k}(q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathsf{Rook}_{n}\\
|D|=n-k\end{subarray}}q^{\mathsf{GR}_{n}(D)},

which implies

(42)

B_{n}(q)=\sum_{D\in\mathsf{Rook}_{n}}q^{\mathsf{GR}_{n}(D)}.

From this formula,
B_{n}(q)
has degree
{\binom{n}{2}}

since
\mathsf{GR}_{n}(\emptyset)={\binom{n}{2}}
.

6.2.
Computing
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)

Define

C_{n}:=\{\alpha\in C_{+}:\mathsf{supp}(\alpha)\subseteq[n-1],\alpha_{i}\leq n-i\textrm{ for all $i\in[n-1]$}\}.

Then we can refine
Theorem
2.4
.

Corollary 6.2
.

For
w\in S_{n}
,

\mathfrak{G}_{w}
expands positively into
\{\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
,
we can expand
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
into a sum of Lascoux polynomials.
We just need to make sure for each
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
appearing
in the expansion with a nonzero coefficient,
we have
\alpha\in C_{n}
.

We know the monomial
x^{\alpha}
is the leading monomial of

\kappa_{\alpha}
, so
x^{\alpha}
appears in
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
.
Since all coefficients in the sum are positive,
we know
x^{\alpha}\beta^{m}
appears in
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
for
some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
By the monomial expansion of
\mathfrak{G}_{w}

given by Fomin and Kirillov
[
FK94
]
,
we have
\alpha\in C_{n}
.
∎

By this corollary and Lemma
2.5
,
we have the following.

Corollary 6.3
.

For
w\in S_{n}
,

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}_{w}
expands positively
into
\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\}
.

Now we are ready to give another basis of
\widehat{V}_{n}
:

Proposition 6.4
.

The space
\widehat{V}_{n}
is also
\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\}
.
It has a basis

\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\textrm{ is snowy}\}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
6.3
,

\widehat{V}_{n}
is a subspace of

\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\}
.
By Lemma
,
for any
\alpha\in C_{n}
,
we can find a snowy
\gamma\in C_{n}

such that
\gamma\sim\alpha
.
Then by Theorem
1.2
,

\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
is a scalar
multiple of
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\gamma}
.
Thus,

\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\}
is a subspace of

\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\textrm{ is snowy}\}
.
Notice that
\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{n}\textrm{ is snowy}\}

is linear independent since its polynomials
have distinct leading terms
by Theorem
1.2
.

By
[
PSW21
, Theorem 1.4]

\widehat{V}_{n}
has dimension
B_{n}
.
It remains to check the number of snowy weak compositions
in
C_{n}
is also
B_{n}
.
In Lemma
4.20
,
we show
\mathsf{dark}(\cdot)
a bijection from
snowy weak compositions in
C_{+}
to
\mathsf{Rook}_{+}
.
Clearly it restricts to a bijection from

C_{n}
to
\mathsf{Rook}_{n}
,
which has size
B_{n}
.
∎

We use the top Lascoux basis
to derive
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)
.
Let us translate
the statistic
\mathsf{raj}(\cdot)

on snowy weak compositions
to non-attacking rook diagrams.

Definition 6.5
.

Take
R\in\mathsf{Rook}_{+}
.
Define the
Northwest number
of
R
,
denoted as
\mathsf{NW}(R):=\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)
,
where
\alpha
is any weak composition
with
\mathsf{dark}(\alpha)=R
.

Equivalently, we may compute
\mathsf{NW}(R)
as follows:
For each
(r,c)\in R
, we mark all cells
weakly above it and to its left.
By Lemma
4.14
,
these marked cells agree with the underlying diagram of
\mathsf{snow}(D(\alpha))
for any
\alpha
with
\mathsf{dark}(\alpha)=R
.
Then
\mathsf{NW}(R)
is just the number of marked cells.
Comparing this statistic with
\mathsf{GR}_{n}(\cdot)

defined in Subsection
6.1
,
we have the following connection.

Remark 6.6
.

Take
R\in\mathsf{Rook}_{n}
.
Then
\mathsf{GR}_{n}(R)=|\mathsf{Stair}_{n}|-\mathsf{NW}(R)={\binom{n}{2}}-\mathsf{NW}(R)
.

Finally, we can derive an expression
for the degree generating function of
\widehat{V}_{n}
.

Proposition 6.7
.

We have

\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)=q^{{\binom{n}{2}}}B_{n}(q^{-1})=\mathsf{rev}(B_{n}(q)),

where
\mathsf{rev}(\cdot)
is the operator that
reverse the coefficients of a polynomial.
In other words, it sends a polynomial
f(q)

of degree
d
to
q^{d}f(q^{-1})
.

Proof.

By Prop
6.4
,

\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)=\quad\sum_{\alpha}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}

where the sum is over snowy
\alpha\in C_{n}
.
Apply the bijection
\mathsf{dark}(\cdot)

to
\alpha
in the summation, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Rook}_{n}}q^{\mathsf{NW}(R)}=\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Rook}_{n}}q^{{\binom{n}{2}}-\mathsf{GR}_{n}(R)}

\displaystyle=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Rook}_{n}}q^{-\mathsf{GR}_{n}(R)}=q^{{\binom{n}{2}}}B_{n}(q^{-1})\,,

where the second equality is by
Remark
6.6

and the last equality is by (
42
).
Since
B_{n}(q)
has degree
{\binom{n}{2}}
, we have
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)=\mathsf{rev}(B_{n}(q))
.
∎

6.3.
Computing
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)

First, we show the top Lascoux polynomials also span the space
\widehat{V}
.

Proposition 6.8
.

We have
\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in C_{+}\}=\widehat{V}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.6
,

\widehat{V}
is in the
\mathbb{Q}\textrm{-span}
of

\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{+}\}
.
Now consider
\alpha\in C_{+}
.
There exists
n
large enough such that

\alpha\in C_{n}
.
Then
\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}\in\widehat{V}_{n}\subset\widehat{V}
.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

The space
\widehat{V}

has a basis

\{\widehat{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in C_{+}\textrm{ is snowy}\}.

With the top Lascoux basis, we have

\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)\quad=\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\>\in\>C_{+},\\
\alpha\textrm{ is snowy}\end{subarray}}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}\quad=\quad\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Rook}_{+}}q^{\mathsf{NW}(R)},

where the second equality is obtained by
applying
\mathsf{dark}(\cdot)
on
\alpha
in the second expression.
On the other hand, since
\widehat{V}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\widehat{V}_{n}
,

\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)
is the limit of
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V}_{n};q)

as
n
goes to infinity.
According to OEIS,
coefficients in
B_{n}(q)
are in
A126347
and
the coefficients of
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)
are in
A126348
.
A formula for
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)
in OEIS is given by Jovovic:

\prod_{m>0}(1+\frac{q^{m}}{1-q})
.
For completeness, we check this rule using our formula
of
\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)
involving snowy weak compositions.

Proposition 6.10
.

We have

\mathrm{Hilb}(\widehat{V};q)=\sum_{\alpha\text{ is snowy}}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}=\prod_{m>0}\left(1+\frac{q^{m}}{1-q}\right)

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{snowy}(M)
be the set of all snowy weak compositions
with the largest entry being at most
M
.
It suffices to show

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M)}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}=\prod_{m>0}^{M}\left(1+\frac{q^{m}}{1-q}\right).

We prove it by induction on
M
. The claim is immediate when
M=0
as both sides are
1
.

Now assume the claim above holds for some
M\geq 0
.
Let
\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{i}
be the set of all snowy weak compositions

\alpha
such that its largest entry is
\alpha_{i}=M
.
With this notation, we can express
\mathsf{snowy}(M)
recursively:

\mathsf{snowy}(M)=\mathsf{snowy}(M-1)\>\bigsqcup\>\left(\bigsqcup_{i\geq 1}\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{i}\right).

Next, we define a map

\displaystyle\phi:\mathsf{snowy}(M-1)

\displaystyle\to\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{1}

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle\mapsto(M,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots)

It is straightforward to see that
\phi
is a bijection.
Furthermore, we have
\mathsf{raj}(\phi(\alpha))=\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)+M
.
To get
\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{i}
for
i>1
, notice that
the operator
s_{i}
on the set of weak compositions is a
bijection between
\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{i}

and
\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{i+1}
.
For
\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{i}
, we have

\mathsf{raj}(s_{i}(\alpha))=\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)+1

by Corollary
4.19
.
Inductively, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M)_{i}}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}=q^{M+i-1}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M-1)}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M)}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M-1)}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}\>+\>\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}q^{M+i-1}\right)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M-1)}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{i\geq 1}q^{M+i-1}\right)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{snowy}(M-1)}q^{\mathsf{raj}(\alpha)}=\left(1+\frac{q^{M}}{1-q}\right)\prod_{m>0}^{M-1}\left(1+\frac{q^{m}}{1-q}\right).

∎

We conclude with several open problems for future study.

.

Find further connections between Viennot’s geometric construction of Schensted insertion
and
\mathsf{snow}(RD(w))
.

Problem 7.2
.

Find further connections between Schensted insertion
and
\mathsf{snow}(RD(w))
.

The Grothendieck to Lascoux expansion, proven in
[
SY23
]
,
involves finding certain tableaux and computing their right keys.

Problem 7.3
.

Find a combinatorial formula for the expansion of Castelnuovo–Mumford polynomials
into top Lascoux polynomials indexed by snowy weak compositions.

Finding a combinatorial formula for the
structure constants

c^{w}_{u,v}

for Grothendieck polynomials, defined as

\mathfrak{G}_{u}\mathfrak{G}_{v}=\sum_{w}c^{w}_{u,v}\mathfrak{G}_{w}\,,

has been a long-standing open problem.
These coefficients have a geometric interpretation: They are the intersection
numbers for the Schubert classes in the connective
K
-theory.
If we consider only the top-degree terms on both sides, we get the structure
constants for Castelnuovo–Mumford polynomials, which we denote as
\widehat{c^{w}_{uv}}
,
which are still non-negative integers.

Problem 7.4
.

Find a combinatorial formula for
\widehat{c^{w}_{uv}}
.

The Grothendieck polynomials
\mathfrak{G}_{w}(\bm{x})
are a specialization of the double
Grothendieck polynomials
\mathfrak{G}_{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
by setting
y_{1}=y_{2}=\dots=0
.
In
[
KM01
]
, Knutson and Miller introduced pipe dream rules for both

\mathfrak{G}_{w}(\bm{x})
and
\mathfrak{G}_{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
.
For Castelnuovo–Mumford polynomials
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}_{w}}(\bm{x})
, we can think they
correspond to a subset of pipe dreams for
\mathfrak{G}_{w}(\bm{x})
.
In
[
PSW21
]
, the authors proved a factorization of
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}_{w}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})

into a
\bm{x}
-polynomial and a
\bm{y}
-polynomial, and they showed the
the leading term is in fact,

\bm{x}^{\mathsf{rajcode}(w)}\bm{y}^{\mathsf{rajcode}(w^{-1})},

with coefficient
1
by constructing a pipe dream associated with it iteratively.

Problem 7.5
.

Use the snow diagrams to give an explicit construction of pipe dreams for the leading term
in
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}_{w}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
.

In general, one can define a
K
-Kohnert polynomial for any diagram
D
:

\kappa_{D}(\bm{x};\beta):=\sum_{D^{\prime}\in\mathsf{KKD}(D)}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{wt}(D^{\prime})}\beta^{\mathsf{ex}(D^{\prime})}.

Problem 7.6
.

Find characterizations of diagram
D
such that the leading monomial of
\widehat{\kappa}_{D}
is given by
\mathsf{rajcode}(D)
.

On the global regularity for minimizers of variational integrals: splitting-type problems in 2D and extensions to the general anisotropic setting
Michael Bildhauer & Martin Fuchs

Abstract
We mainly discuss superquadratic minimization problems for splitting-type variational integrals on a bounded Lipschitz domain Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} and prove higher integrability of the gradient up to the boundary by incorporating an appropriate weight-function measuring the distance of the solution to the boundary data. As a corollary, the local Hölder coefficient with respect to some improved Hölder continuity is quantified in terms of the function dist ⁡ ( ⋅ , ∂ Ω ) \operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega) . The results are extended to anisotropic problems without splitting structure under natural growth and ellipticity conditions. In both cases we argue with variants of Caccioppoli’s inequality involving small weights. 1 1 1 AMS subject classification: 49N60, 49Q20, 49J45 Keywords: global higher integrability, splitting-type energies, anisotropic growth

\displaystyle c\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla|\eta|^{\kappa},\nabla|\eta|^{\kappa}\big)|\nabla u|^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\frac{q-2}{2}}|\eta|^{2\kappa-2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\Gamma^{1+\alpha}\,{\rm d}x=c\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha}|\nabla\eta|^{2}|\eta|^{2\kappa-2}\,{\rm d}x\,.

From (
5.6
) - (
5.8
) we conclude

\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{s}|\eta|^{2\kappa}\,{\rm d}x\leq c\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\frac{q}{2}-\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}|\eta|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x+cT_{2}^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}

(5.9)

with constants
c
being independent of
\eta
and
it remains to discuss
T_{2}
in (
5.9
): we have for
\hat{\mu}=2/\gamma
,
\mu=2/(2-\gamma)
,

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg[\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\frac{s\gamma}{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{\gamma}|\eta|^{(\kappa-1)\gamma}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}=\Bigg[\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\frac{p}{2\mu}}\Gamma^{-\frac{p}{2\mu}}\Gamma^{\frac{s\gamma}{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{\gamma}|\eta|^{(\kappa-1)\gamma}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\Bigg[\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\frac{p}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{2}{n}}\cdot\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{-\frac{p}{n}+s}|\nabla\eta|^{2}|\eta|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x\,.

With (
5.5
), (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) the proof of Lemma
5.1
is completed by choosing
\tilde{\varepsilon}\ll\varepsilon
. ∎

Now we come to the proof of the theorem: we choose
\eta_{m}=(u-u_{0})\varphi_{m}
with
\varphi_{m}
defined after (
2.2
).
Then Lemma
5.1
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\overline{s}_{\alpha}}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{(\frac{q}{2}+\alpha)}\big[|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{0}|\big]^{2}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x

(5.11)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{-\frac{p}{n}+s}\big[|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{0}|\big]^{2}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{(\frac{q}{2}+\alpha)}|\nabla\varphi_{m}|^{2}|u-u_{0}|^{2}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{-\frac{p}{n}+s}|\nabla\varphi_{m}|^{2}|u-u_{0}|^{2}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x+c\,.

Here the first two integrals on the right-hand side can be absorbed in the left-hand side provided that we have (recall that
u_{0}
is Lipschitz)

\max\Big\{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha,-\frac{p}{n}+s\Big\}<\overline{s}_{\alpha}-1\,.

(5.12)

Also on account of (
5.12
) we can handle the remaining integrals on the right-hand side of (
5.11
) with the
help of Young’s inequality. We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{(\frac{q}{2}+\alpha)}|\nabla\varphi_{m}|^{2}|u-u_{0}|^{2}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\overline{s}_{\alpha}}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x+c\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\varphi_{m}}|\nabla\varphi_{m}|^{2\beta_{1}^{*}}|u-u_{0}|^{2\beta_{1}^{*}}|\eta_{m}|^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x\,.

as well as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{(-\frac{p}{n}+s)}|\nabla\varphi_{m}|^{2}|u-u_{0}|^{2}\eta_{m}^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{\overline{s}_{\alpha}}\eta_{m}^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x+c\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\varphi_{m}}|\nabla\varphi_{m}|^{2\beta_{2}^{*}}|u-u_{0}|^{2\beta_{2}^{*}}\eta_{m}^{2(\kappa-1)}\,{\rm d}x\,.

In (
5.13
) we choose (
\alpha
sufficiently close to
-1/(2n)
)
\beta_{1}
such that

1<\beta_{1}<\overline{s}_{\alpha}\Big[\frac{q}{2}-\frac{1}{2n}\Big]^{-1}=\Bigg[p\frac{\kappa(n+2)-2}{\kappa}-\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa}\Bigg]\frac{1}{qn-1}

with conjugate exponent (recall
q-p<2p/n
on account of (
1.18
))

\beta_{1}^{*}>\frac{\kappa\big[p(n+2)-1\big]-2p+1}{\kappa\big[2p-(q-p)n\big]-2p+1}\,.

(5.15)

In (
5.14
) we define
\beta_{2}
according to

1<\beta_{2}:=\overline{s}_{\alpha}\Big[-\frac{p}{n}+s\Big]^{-1}=\Bigg[p\frac{\kappa(n+2)-2}{2\kappa}-\frac{\kappa-1}{2\kappa}\Bigg]\frac{1}{sn-p}

with conjugate exponent

\beta_{2}^{*}>\frac{\kappa\big[pn+4p-1-2sn\big]-2p+1}{\kappa\big[2p-(q-p)n\big]-2p+1}\,.

(5.16)

Returning to (
5.13
) and (
5.14
), respectively, the first integral again is absorbed in the left-hand side of (
5.11
).
The remaining integrals stay bounded if we have for
i=1
,
2

m^{-1}m^{\beta_{i}^{*}\big[2-2(1-n/p)\big]-2(\kappa-1)(1-n/p)}\leq c\,,

Thus we require the condition

\beta_{i}^{*}<(\kappa-1)\frac{p-n}{n}+\frac{p}{2n}\,,\qquad i=1,\,2\,,

(5.17)

which on account of
p>n
is satisfied for
\kappa
sufficiently large.

It remains to arrange (
5.12
) together with the inequality on the right-hand side of (
5.2
). Here we first observe

\displaystyle\frac{q}{2}+\alpha<p\frac{\kappa(n+2)-2}{2n\kappa}+\alpha\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa}-1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle q<p\frac{\kappa(n+2)-2}{n\kappa}-2-2\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}

(5.18)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle q-p<\frac{2}{n}(p-n)-\frac{2p+2n\alpha}{n\kappa}

For
\alpha
sufficiently close to

, (

) is a consequence of (
1.19
).
We finally have to discuss

\displaystyle-\frac{p}{n}+s\leq\overline{s}_{\alpha}-1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle-\frac{p}{n}+\overline{s}_{\alpha}\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-1}-\frac{p}{2(\kappa-1)}<\overline{s}_{\alpha}-1

(5.19)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\overline{s}_{\alpha}<(\kappa-1)\frac{p-n}{n}+\frac{p}{2}\,.

Assumption (
1.21
) implies (
5.19
). Hence, (
5.11
), (

) and (

) prove

5
passing to the limit

. ∎

6
Appendix. Caccioppoli-type inequalities

We prove two Caccioppoli-type inequalities with small weights (i.e. involving powers of
\Gamma=1+|\nabla u|^{2}
or of

\Gamma_{i}=1+|\partial_{i}u|^{2}
,
i=1
, …,
n
, with a certain range of negative exponents), where
the first one is the appropriate version in the splitting context.

There is no need to restrict the following considerations to the case
n=2
. Thus,
throughout this appendix, we suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and that

f
:
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{2}
satisfying
D^{2}f(Z)(Y,Y)>0
for all
Z
,
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Moreover we suppose that
u\in W^{2,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
solves the differentiated Euler equation

0=\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\psi\big)\,{\rm d}x\qquad\mbox{for all}\;\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)

(6.1)

and for any
1\leq i\leq n
fixed.

Proposition 6.1
.

Fix
l\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
.
Then the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\eta^{2l}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x

(6.2)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)(\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta)\eta^{2l-2}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\,,\quad\Gamma_{i}:=1+|\partial_{i}u|^{2}\,,

holds for any
\alpha>-1/2
and for any fixed
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.
Suppose that
-1/2<\alpha
and fix
1\leq i\leq n
(no summation with respect to
i
).
Using approximation arguments we may insert

\psi:=\eta^{2l}\partial_{i}u\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}

in the equation (
6.1
) with the result

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\eta^{2l}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\big)\partial_{i}u\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x

(6.3)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla(\eta^{2l})\big)\partial_{i}u\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle S_{1}+S_{2}\,.

In (
6.3
) we have

S_{1}=-2\alpha\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)|\partial_{i}u|^{2}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha-1}\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x

which gives
S_{1}\leq 0
if
\alpha\geq 0
. In this case we will just neglect
S_{1}
in the following. In the case
-1/2<\alpha<0
we estimate

\displaystyle|S_{1}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|\alpha|\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)|\partial_{i}u|^{2}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha-1}\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|\alpha|\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x\,.

Since we have
2|\alpha|<1
we may absorb
|S_{1}|
in the left-hand side of (
6.3
), hence

\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\eta^{2l}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x\leq c|S_{2}|\,.

For
0<\varepsilon
sufficiently small we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to discuss
S_{2}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\eta)\eta^{2l-1}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\partial_{i}u\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u)\eta^{2l}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+c(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)(\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta)\eta^{2l-2}\Gamma_{i}^{\alpha}|\partial_{i}u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,.

After absorbing the first term in the right-hand side of (
6.3
) we have established our claim (
6.2
). ∎

Instead of the quantities
\Gamma_{i}
our second inequality (compare
[
20
]
for the discussion in two dimensions)
involves the full derivative, i.e. we incorporate certain negative powers of
\Gamma=1+|\nabla u|^{2}
. As a consequence we do not obtain the range

-1/2<\alpha
and have to replace this condition by the requirement
-1/(2n)<\alpha
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}\big(\Omega\big)
and fix some real number
\alpha
such that
-1/(2n)<\alpha
.
Then we have (summation with respect to
i=1
, …,
n
)

\displaystyle\big[1+2\alpha n\big]\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\Gamma^{\alpha}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(6.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\Bigg[\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\eta}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\Gamma^{\alpha}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Bigg[\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\eta}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta\big)\big|\nabla u\big|^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

where the constant
c
is not depending on
\eta
.
In particular it holds

\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u)\Gamma^{\alpha}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq c\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\eta}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta\big)\big|\nabla u\big|^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x\,.

(6.5)

Proof.
For
i=1
, …,
n
and any
\eta
as above we deduce from (
6.1
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\Gamma^{\alpha}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(6.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\partial_{i}u\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}\big)\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\eta\big)\partial_{i}u\Gamma^{\alpha}\eta\,{\rm d}x=:I+II\,,

where at this stage no summation with respect to the index
i
is performed.
Following
[
20
]
we denote the bilinear form
D^{2}f(\cdot,\cdot)
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The first observation
is the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial_{j}u)^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

(6.7)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle(\partial_{i}u)^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}\,.

For handling the integrand of the term
I
from (
6.6
) we use the identity

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\nabla\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial_{j}u)^{2}\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

(6.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle-2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\sum_{j\not=i}\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}\,.

The last term on the right-hand side of (
6.8
) is estimated as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\sum_{j\not=i}\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigg[\sum_{j\not=i}\frac{1}{2}\Big[\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u,\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Big]\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigg[(n-1)\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle+\sum_{j\not=i}\Big\langle\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u,\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Big]\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigg[(n-2)\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u,\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Big]\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[(n-2)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle+n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u,\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Big]\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\,.

This, together with (
6.8
) gives (recall
-2\alpha>0
)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

(6.9)

\displaystyle-2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\sum_{j\not=i}\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle-2\alpha(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\alpha n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\partial_{i}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma^{\alpha-1}\,.

Combining (
6.9
) and (
6.7
) we get

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}u\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}\big)\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(6.10)

\displaystyle\leq-2\alpha n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\partial_{i}\nabla u,\partial_{i}\nabla u\big)\Gamma^{\alpha}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,.

Returning to (
6.6
) and using (
6.10
) we get (from now on summation with respect to
i
)

\displaystyle\big[1+2\alpha n\big]\int_{\Omega}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{i}u,\nabla\partial_{i}u\big)\Gamma^{\alpha}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq-2\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\eta}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\eta\nabla\partial_{i}u,\partial_{i}u\nabla\eta\big)\Gamma^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}x\,.

This finishes the proof by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. ∎

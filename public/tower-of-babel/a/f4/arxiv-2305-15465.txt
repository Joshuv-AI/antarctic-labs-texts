The QCD axion sum rule Preprint: IFT-UAM/CSIC-23-58
Belén Gavela, Pablo Quílez, Maria Ramos

Abstract
We demonstrate that the true QCD axion that solves the strong CP problem can be found in all generality outside the customary standard QCD band, with QCD being the sole source of Peccei-Quinn breaking. The essential reason is that the basis of axion-gluon interactions does not need to coincide with the mass basis. Specifically, we consider the case in which the QCD axion field is not the only singlet scalar in Nature but it mixes with other singlet scalars (besides the η ′ \eta^{\prime} ). We determine the exact mathematical condition for an arbitrary N N -scalar potential to be Peccei-Quinn invariant. Such potentials provide extra sources of mass for the customary axion without enlarging the Standard Model gauge symmetry. The contribution to the axion mass stemming from the QCD topological susceptibility is shown to be shared then among the N N axion eigenstates through a precise sum rule. Their location can only be displaced to the right of the standard QCD band. We demonstrate that the axion closest to this band can be displaced from it by a factor of N \sqrt{N} at most, and this corresponds to the case in which all axion signals are maximally deviated. Conversely, if one axion is found on the standard QCD band, the other eigenstates will be out of experimental reach. Our results imply that any ALP experiment which finds a signal to the right of the standard QCD axion band can be solving the strong CP problem within QCD, with the associated N − 1 N-1 excitations to be found in an area of parameter space that we determine. We illustrate the results and phenomenology in some particular cases.

Introduction

Axions and axion-like particles (ALPs) are the subject of intense exploration at present, with both novel experimental proposals and theoretical work constantly being put forward.
Being pseudo-Goldstone bosons (pGBs), their search constitutes an epic and generic quest to uncover symmetries hidden in Nature and awaiting discovery.

{\mathbf{M}}^{2}
in Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\det{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}{\det{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}=\left(b_{11}-\mathbf{X}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{-2}\mathbf{X}\right)=\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{F^{2}}\,,

(28)

i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{\det{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}{\det{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}=\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}^{2}}{{F}^{2}}\frac{m_{u}m_{d}}{\left(m_{u}+m_{d}\right)^{2}}\,,

(29)

plus the mixing condition stated in
Footnote

5
.
For a given mass eigenstate
|a_{i}\rangle
with eigenvalue
m_{i}
, let

denote the physical strength of its coupling to gluons,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{\alpha_{s}}{8\pi}\frac{a_{i}}{f_{i}}G\widetilde{G}\,.

(30)

Denoting by
v_{ij}
the elements of the rotation matrix that diagonalizes
{\mathbf{M}}^{2}
(which define the eigenvectors of the system), the projection of
|a_{i}\rangle
onto the unit vector which couples to gluons reads

\displaystyle v_{i1}=\langle{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}|a_{i}\rangle\,.

(31)

It then follows that
m_{i}
can then be expressed as

\displaystyle\quad m_{i}^{2}=\left|v_{i1}\right|^{2}\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{F^{2}}+\langle a_{i}|{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}|a_{i}\rangle\,,

(32)

while

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{i}}=\frac{1}{F}\times|v_{i1}|\,\implies\frac{1}{F^{2}}=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{f_{i}^{2}}\,,

(33)

that is, the physical coupling to gluons of a given axion eigenstate can be seen as the projection of the coupling of
{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}
into that eigenstate.

III.1
Distance from the standard axion band

The physical, measurable, quantities are
m_{i}
and
f_{i}
.
How much can their value depart from the standard
\left\{m_{a},1/f_{a}\right\}
QCD band, defined by Eq. (
1
)?

The factor of
g_{i}
defined in
Eq.

2
precisely describes how much the mass-scale relation for an eigenstate
a_{i}
deviates from that for the single QCD axion. In light of Eq. (
33
) the
g_{i}
factors can be expressed in the preferred basis as

\displaystyle g_{i}=\frac{m_{i}^{2}\,F^{2}}{\left|v_{i1}\right|^{2}\chi_{\text{QCD}}}\,,

(34)

which in turn implies in terms of
\beta_{i}=1/g_{i}
:

\displaystyle\quad m_{i}^{2}=\beta_{i}\,m_{i}^{2}+\langle a_{i}|{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}|a_{i}\rangle\,,

(35)

which shows that
\beta_{i}
is the fraction of a given mass eigenvalue
m_{i}
due to QCD, its “
axionness
”.

A sum rule.

It is fruitful to apply now the eigenvalue-eigenvector identity
JacobiDeBQ
;
Denton:2019pka
(see
Eq.

85
). In one of its versions it reads

\frac{\text{det}\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{N-1}-M_{j}\right)}{\text{det}\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{N}-A\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{ij}|^{2}}{\lambda-\lambda_{i}}\,,

(36)

where
A
denotes any Hermitian matrix with eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}(A)\}
and associated eigenvectors
v_{ij}
, and where
M_{j}
denotes the minor matrix formed by removing the
j^{\text{th }}
row and column of
A
. Applying this identity to the mass matrix in the preferred basis in
Section

III
,
A={\mathbf{M}}^{2}
, it implies for
j=1
and
\lambda=0
that

\displaystyle\frac{\det{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}{\det{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\left|v_{i1}\right|^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}=\frac{F^{2}}{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{g_{i}}.

(37)

By comparison with the requirement that the potential has a
true PQ symmetry
,
Eq.

29
, the following constraint on the possible values of the
g_{i}
factors results:

\displaystyle\exists\,\,U(1)_{PQ}\implies\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{g_{i}}=1\,,

(38)

or equivalently

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{i}=1\,.

(39)

An alternative derivation of this QCD axion sum rule can be found in App.
B
, which provides an intuitive understanding of
axionness
.

This sum rule links the multiple QCD axion signals and allows a direct comprehension of the decoupling limits in which only one single QCD axion is reachable. In order to obtain a different phenomenology with respect to this standard case, at least one scale in the mixing potential is required to be of the order of the QCD induced mass scale, a fact we demonstrate in App.
C
.

Furthermore, a number of properties of the multiple axion eigenstates follow from our sum rule, as we will rigorously prove next.

1.

All
g_{i}
-factors are larger or equal than one,

g_{i}\geq 1\,

(i.e.
\beta_{i}\leq 1
), as a consequence of the positivity of the extra potential. i.e. of
{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}
being positive semi-definite. For a
true PQ symmetry,
this implies that the QCD axion line in the
\left\{m_{i},1/f_{i}\right\}
parameter space of any of
these multiple axions can only be displaced to the right
of that of the single axion case, by a factor
\sqrt{g_{i}}
.

Proof
.
Combining the expression for the mass eigenvalues in the rotated basis, Eq. (
32
), with Eq. (
78
), it results that

\displaystyle g_{i}=1+\frac{F^{2}}{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}\frac{\langle a_{i}|{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}|a_{i}\rangle}{\left|\langle a_{i}|{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\rangle\right|^{2}}\geq 1

(40)

since
{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}
is semi-positive definite
8
8

8

The mass matrix of a system of axions is defined as the second derivative in the minimum of the potential and thus it is semi-positive definite. However, if one splits the mass matrix in two contributions, nothing guarantees that both matrices are still semi-positive definite. We argue, nevertheless, that this is indeed the case for our matrices. Due to the PQ symmetry,
{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}
has vanishing determinant and thus there are more axions than constraints in the extra potential in order to find the minimum.

. It follows that
\beta_{i}\leq 1\,,\forall i
.

2.

If a given axion
a_{i}
has a factor
g_{i}\neq 1
, the sum rule sets an upper limit on the
g
-factor of any other axion,

\displaystyle g_{j}\geq\frac{1}{1-1/g_{i}}\,\quad\forall\,j\neq i\,,

(41)

or, equivalently

\displaystyle\beta_{j}\leq 1-\beta_{i}\,\quad\forall\,j\neq i\,.

(42)

Proof
. This follows directly from the inequality

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{g_{i}}=\sum_{j\neq i}^{N}\frac{1}{g_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{g_{j}}\,.

(43)

This bound has interesting experimental consequences as we will discuss in
Section

V
.

3.

As a particular case of the previous property, if one axion is detected with
g_{i}=1
, then no other axion eigenstate can couple to gluons and thus no other axion signal is to be expected. Conversely, it is enough to detect one axion signal with
g_{i}\neq 1
to imply that at least another axion signal awaits discovery.

4.

Maximal distance of the axion closest to standard: Maxions.

The maximum value that the smallest of all
g_{i}
’s can take is
N
, the number of physical axions, and it corresponds to the case where all of them are equal,

\displaystyle\qquad\max\left\{\min_{i}\{g_{i}\}\right\}=N\quad\implies g_{i}=N,\,\,\forall\,i\,.

(44)

This follows directly from the sum-rule in Eq. (
38
), that shows that if any
g_{i}
deviates from the point where all are equal, another one has to deviate contrarily following a see-saw pattern. In the scenario in Eq. (
44
), all the
N
axion signals will appear aligned over just one QCD line parallel to that for the single QCD axion, and displaced to its right by a factor
\sqrt{N}
.
We will denominate this type of solutions as
QCD maxions
(maximally deviated QCD axions).

For maxions, the coefficients
c^{\mathbf{M}}_{k}
of the characteristic polynomial of
{\mathbf{M}}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{N}c^{\mathbf{M}}_{k}\lambda^{k}\,,

(45)

and the analogous ones for the minor
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}
,
c^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}}_{k}
,
fulfill the relation

\displaystyle c^{\mathbf{M}}_{k}=-N\,\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{F^{2}(N-k)}\,c^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}}_{k}\,.

(46)

These coefficients can be expressed in all generality in terms of the complete exponential Bell polynomials,

\qquad c_{n-k}^{\mathbf{A}}=\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k!}\mathcal{B}_{k}\big(\operatorname{tr}A,-1!\operatorname{tr}A^{2},2!\operatorname{tr}A^{3},\\
\ldots,(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!\operatorname{tr}A^{k}\big)

(47)

and then the relation in Eq. (
46
) reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{N-k}^{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}=N\,\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{{F}^{2}}\,\mathcal{B}_{N-k-1}^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}\,.

(48)

This constitutes a set of
N

QCD maxion conditions
necessary to solve the strong CP problem via maxions. For the particular case in which all decay constants in Eq. (
14
) are equal,
{\hat{f}}_{k}={\hat{f}}
, it follows that
F=\hat{f}/\sqrt{N}
and these relations simplify to

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{N-k}^{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}=N^{2}\,\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{{\hat{f}}^{2}}\,\mathcal{B}^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}_{N-1-k}\,.

(49)

A particular relation of interest is that for
{k=N-1}
, which implies the following constraint the sum of all square maxion masses:

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}=\text{ tr}\,{\mathbf{M}^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}^{2}=N\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{F^{2}}\,.

(50)

Furthermore, for
k=0
the maxion condition boils down to the PQ constraint on the determinants of a generic potential,
Eq.

29
.
The proof of the relations in Eq. (
49
) is somewhat lengthy, and we have thus deferred it to App.
D
.

Interestingly, the maxion conditions are automatically fulfilled by Laguerre polynomials, as explained in

Extension to the coupling to photons

It is not completely straightforward to extend the constraints studied above for the axion-gluon coupling to the interactions to photons. The reason is that the axion-photon coupling has a component that depends on the UV axion model through the ratio
E/{\mathcal{N}}
, where
E
denotes the model-dependent electromagnetic anomaly and
{\mathcal{N}}
the color anomaly.

A simple case is that in which
E/{\mathcal{N}}
is assumed to be equal for all axion fields
\hat{a}_{k}
in Eq. (
14
),
{{E_{k}}/{{\mathcal{N}}_{k}}={E}/{{\mathcal{N}}}}
,
\forall k
. The model-dependent axion-photon interactions read then

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{N}g^{0}_{{\hat{a}}_{k}\gamma\gamma}\,{{\hat{a}}_{k}}F\widetilde{F}\,\equiv\frac{\alpha_{em}}{8\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{E}{{\mathcal{N}}}\frac{{\hat{a}}_{k}}{{\hat{f}}_{k}}F\widetilde{F}\,,

(51)

which in terms of the preferred rotated basis becomes

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{L}}=\,\frac{\alpha_{em}}{8\pi}\frac{E}{{\mathcal{N}}}\frac{a_{G\tilde{G}}}{F}F\widetilde{F}\,,

(52)

i.e. the axion-photon coupling is aligned with the gluonic one, see Eq. (
19
). This already suggests that the fate of the standard band for the QCD axion-photon coupling will follow a very similar pattern to that
determined for the coupling to gluons.
The physical coupling to photons will receive as usual a model-independent QCD contribution stemming from the axion-
\eta^{\prime}
-pion mixing. This component can be easily computed by performing an axion-dependent rotation on the quark fields
Georgi:1986df
;
diCortona:2015ldu
,

\displaystyle q=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u\\
d\end{array}\right)\rightarrow e^{i\gamma_{5}{a_{G\tilde{G}}}/({2F})Q_{a}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
d\end{array}\right),

where the matrix
Q_{a}
is chosen so that the tree-level axion-pion mixing is avoided,
{Q_{a}={M_{q}^{-1}}/{\text{tr}\,(M_{q}^{-1})}}
, where
{M_{q}=\text{diag}(m_{u},\,m_{d})}
denotes the quark mass matrix. In other words, it is chosen so that the mass matrix for
\{a_{G\tilde{G}},\pi^{0}\}
is automatically diagonal. This anomalous rotation modifies the coupling to photons as customary with a shift on
E/{\mathcal{N}}
of
-6\operatorname{tr}\left(Q_{a}Q^{2}\right)
-where
Q
is the electric quark charge matrix- leading to
diCortona:2015ldu
,

\displaystyle\frac{E}{{\mathcal{N}}}\longrightarrow\frac{E}{{\mathcal{N}}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{4m_{d}+m_{u}}{m_{d}+m_{u}}\xrightarrow{\text{NLO}}\frac{E}{{\mathcal{N}}}-1.92\,,

(57)

where in the last step next-to-leading corrections in the chiral expansion have been incorporated. Finally, in terms of the mass eigenvalues
m_{i}
and physical scale
f_{i}
of the eigenstates
a_{i}
, the axion-photon interactions, for this universal
E/{\mathcal{N}}
case, are given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}\supset\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}\left[\frac{E}{{\mathcal{N}}}-1.92\right]\sum_{i}\frac{a_{i}}{f_{i}}\,F\widetilde{F}\,.

(58)

This implies that the single QCD axion band in the coupling to photons will be shifted by the same factor of
g_{i}
that has been determined for the gluon-axion coupling in the previous section,

\displaystyle\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{g_{a_{i}\gamma\gamma}^{2}}=\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}}\Bigg|_{\text{single QCD axion}}\times g_{i}\,.

(59)

A sum rule for the coupling to photon of the
N
axion excitations follows (analogous to that for the PQ condition in Eq. (
38
)),

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{2}\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{\alpha_{em}^{2}}\left[\frac{E}{{\mathcal{N}}}-1.92\right]^{-2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{g_{a_{i}\gamma\gamma}^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}=1\,.

(60)

Two plausible scenarios in which
E/{\mathcal{N}}
would be universal for all axions -and thus the results above hold directly- are: i) the UV models in which no exotic fermions are electromagnetically charged i.e.
E/{\mathcal{N}}=0
, and ii) when the SM is embedded in
a Grand Unified Theory (GUT), which can fix
E/{\mathcal{N}}=8/3

Srednicki:1985xd
;
Agrawal:2022lsp
.
In fact, the authors of Ref.
Agrawal:2022lsp
already noted that, in the presence of multiple axions in a GUT theory, their photon-couplings were always located to the right of the standard axion-photon band.
They did not explore the fundamental axion-gluon coupling, though, neither determined the maximal distances nor the general proofs and sum-rules obtained here.

The more general case in which
E/{\mathcal{N}}
differs for each of the axion fields can be worked out from the results above.

IV
Maxion masses

Even though the maxion conditions require the presence of mass scales in the extra potential which are of the same order as the QCD-induced mass, and restrict the shape of the matrices, there are still infinitely many maxion matrices. Indeed, the class of matrices that verify these QCD Maxion conditions generate a
m
-parameter family of matrices with
m=N(N+1)/2
.
Fixing all
\hat{f}_{i}=\hat{f}
, the number of families reduces to
m=N(N-1)/2+1
. Consequently, in the latter case all
N=2\,(3)
maxion potentials are functions of
1\,(3)
free parameter(s), plus the overall scale.

In a scenario with 2 axion fields produced with the same decay constant (
\hat{f}_{1}=\hat{f}_{2}=\hat{f}
), the only family of maxions is characterized by the following mass matrix:

\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}=\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{\hat{f}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}3-r&1+\sqrt{r(2-r)}\\
1+\sqrt{r(2-r)}&1+r\end{pmatrix}\,,

(61)

where
r\in[0,1)\cup(1,2]
. For
r=2
, the toy example presented in Sec.
II
is recovered, while
r=1/5
reproduces the example in App.
A
.

The eigenvalues obtained from this matrix lead to
{\Delta\equiv|m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}|/(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2})}
ranging from
\sim 0.7-1
. In the limiting case in which
\Delta=1
, corresponding to
r=1
, the system is not PQ-invariant, as the massless eigenstate has no mixing with
a_{G\widetilde{G}}
; see
Footnote

5
. Nonetheless, values of
\Delta=1+\epsilon
(with
\epsilon\ll 1
) are possible, corresponding to an almost massless maxion and another one with

m_{2}^{2}\approx 2\chi_{\rm QCD}/F^{2}
.
This shows that
highly hierarchical masses are possible
. Assuming a different hierarchy for
\hat{f}_{1}
and
\hat{f}_{2}
, values of
\Delta\sim 0
can be attained in addition.

In scenarios with 3 axions, the maxion families are instead characterized by 3 free parameters, assuming equal decay constants for all fields. Identifying
\hat{a}_{1}
with the PQ combination, an illustrative example is given by the mass matrix

\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}=\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{\hat{f}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&4-\sqrt{3+r-r^{2}}&1+r\\
1&1+r&4+\sqrt{3+r-r^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(62)

with
r\in\left[\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{13}),\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{13})\right]
. The eigenvalues sourced by this mass matrix are shown in Fig.

If it was furthermore assumed that the extra source of interactions
V_{B}
is diagonal in the scalar fields, it would follow that:

\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}_{p=0}=\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{\hat{f}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\

(63)

V
Discussion and outlook

The main experimental impact of our results is that multiple axion signals –instead of a single one– are expected from the generic
QCD axion solution to the strong CP problem. The customary solution with a single axion QCD corresponds instead to a certain limit of the general parameter space. These multiple solutions can only be displaced towards the right of the customary QCD axion band.

In case of a positive ALP signal to the right of that band (or even within the single QCD band given its error bars), experiments are encouraged to look for similar signals within their frequency range. In addition, signals widely separated and thus accessed by different experiments are possible. This establishes a beautiful synergy between experiments sensitive to vastly different masses and scales, as the complete reconstruction of the solution to the strong CP problem may require this complementary search.

An illustration of this experimental impact on the
\{m_{a},1/f_{a}\}
parameter space of axion-gluon couplings (which are directly tackled by axion-nEDM experiments) is provided in

In practice, for signals close to the standard QCD band the possible displacement of a given signal will be obscured by the incertitude on the width of the band itself.

Furthermore, most of the experiments with best reach rely on the assumption that axions account for the dark matter (DM) of the universe. Haloscopes are sensitive to
\sqrt{\rho_{{\rm DM},local}}\times g_{aXX}
, where

;

The quest to unravel the fundamental symmetries of the visible and dark sectors of the Universe is inspiring. We have proven that the PQ solution to the strong CP problem within pure QCD leads in all generality to multiple axion signals with well delimitated properties. This stems from lifting the requirement that the basis of axion-gluon interactions and the axion mass basis are simultaneously diagonal.
In this perspective, the usual single QCD axion is just one particular case of the landscape of solutions, corresponding to zero mixing. The results open novel experimental territory
and
blur the distinction between the search for the QCD axion and that for ALPs
in a huge region of the parameter space.

Appendix A
UV completion

We consider a double version of the KSVZ model
Kim:1979if
;
Shifman:1979if
, with two vector-like fermions
\Psi_{1,2}\sim(3,1,0)
and two singlet complex scalars
{S_{1,2}\sim(1,1,0)}
, i.e.

\mathcal{L}_{\rm UV}=|\partial_{\mu}S_{1}|^{2}+|\partial_{\mu}S_{2}|^{2}+\overline{\Psi}_{1}\text{i}\not{D}\Psi_{1}+\overline{\Psi}_{2}\text{i}\not{D}\Psi_{2}-\left[y_{1}\overline{\Psi}_{1}\Psi_{1}S_{1}+y_{2}\overline{\Psi}_{2}\Psi_{2}S_{2}+\text{h.c.}\right]-V(S_{1,2})\,.

(65)

In the absence of
V(S_{1,2})
, the Lagrangian is invariant under two independent global (and QCD anomalous)
U(1)
axial symmetries. If the potential
V(S_{1,2})
breaks both spontaneously, the
S_{i}
fields can be decomposed
in terms of two radial and two angular modes,

S_{1,2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{f}_{1,2}+\rho_{1,2}\right)e^{\text{i}\hat{a}_{1,2}/\hat{f}_{1,2}}\,.

(66)

Let us assume for simplicity
\hat{f}_{1}=\hat{f}_{2}=\hat{f}
. The corresponding eigenstates are then
{a_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}_{2}\right)}
and
a_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}_{2}\right)
. The first plays the role of the canonical QCD axion, with
m_{1}^{2}f_{1}^{2}=\chi_{\rm QCD}
, while the second is massless and decouples from the QCD sector.

Nevertheless, the potential may mix both fields, breaking explicitly the two
U(1)
global symmetries down to just one PQ symmetry. A simple example is given by

V(S_{1,2})=\lambda S_{1}^{3}S_{2}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(67)

which leads at low energies to an effective potential which, expanded to second order, reads:

\displaystyle V_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\chi_{\rm QCD}\left(\frac{\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}_{2}}{\hat{f}}-\bar{\theta}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\hat{f}^{4}\left(\frac{3\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}_{2}}{\hat{f}}\right)^{2}\,.

(68)

Defining
r\equiv\lambda\hat{f}^{4}/(2\chi_{\rm QCD})
and considering the preferred basis of Eq. (
III
),
the corresponding mass matrix is

\mathbf{M}^{2}=\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{{\hat{f}}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}2+8r&-4r\\
-4r&2r\end{pmatrix}\,,

(69)

which indeed satisfies the condition for PQ invariant potentials in
Eq.

28
, with
1/F^{2}=2/\hat{f}^{2}
. The system thus reabsorbs
\bar{\theta}
at the minimum, solving the strong CP problem.

Among the family of solutions in Eq. (
69
), it is possible to find maxion ones. Indeed, the maxion condition in Eq. (
50
) is satisfied in this example
for
r=1/5
: two maxions are then obtained with
g_{1}=g_{2}=2
.

Note that many other explicit
V(S_{1,2})
potentials can break explicitly the two
U(1)
symmetries to one
PQ
symmetry. For instance,
V(S_{1,2})\sim S_{2}^{4}
would lead precisely to the effective potential in the toy model of
Section

II
.

Appendix B
A physical interpretation of the sum rule

In
Section

III.1
we provided a rather mathematical proof of the sum rule.
In order to obtain a more physical intuition for the meaning of the
axionness
,
{\beta_{i}\equiv}1/g_{i}
, and its constraint, we provide here an alternative derivation. The main idea is to impose that the modification of the Lagrangian under a PQ transformation coincides
in any starting basis and in the mass basis. This “PQ matching” constrains the possible values of the couplings to gluons and masses of the axion mass eigenstates leading to our sum rule,
\sum_{i}{\beta_{i}}=1
.

On top of the mass eigenstates
\{a_{i}\}
, there are two linear combinations of particular importance for the QCD axion system. One of them is the combination that couples to the topological term,
{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}
, and the other is the one that transforms under the PQ symmetry,
a_{\text{PQ}}
. The latter is the combination whose shift
a_{\text{PQ}}\to a_{\text{PQ}}+\alpha f_{\text{PQ}}
is only broken by the QCD anomaly (an equivalent definition is that it corresponds to the eigenvector of
{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}
with vanishing eigenvalue). We will now show how the
axionness
of an axion will depend on the projections of a given mass eigenstate
a_{i}
onto both of these linear combinations,
{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}
and
a_{\text{PQ}}
.

Let us consider a basis where the mass matrix stemming from the extra potential is diagonal,
\left\{a_{\text{PQ}},\tilde{a}_{l}\right\}
for
{l=1,\ldots N-1}
. Since the only term that breaks
U(1)_{{PQ}}
is the
a_{\text{PQ}}\,G\tilde{G}
term, the extra potential does not contain the
a_{\text{PQ}}
field,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{8\pi}{\underbrace{\left(\frac{a_{\text{PQ}}}{f_{\text{PQ}}}+\sum_{l=1}^{N-1}\frac{\tilde{a}_{l}}{\hat{f}_{l}}\right)}_{{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}/F}G\tilde{G}-V_{B}\left(\tilde{a}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{a}_{N-1}\right)}\,.

(Note that
f_{\rm PQ}
, as defined above, does not need to coincide with the vacuum expectation value of the PQ scalar.)

The mass Lagrangian of the axion system
reads,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}={-}\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{2}\left(\frac{{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}}{F}\right)^{2}{-}V_{B}\left(\tilde{a}_{1,}\ldots\tilde{a}_{N-1}\right)={-}\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{2}\left(\frac{a_{\text{PQ}}}{f_{\text{PQ}}}+\sum^{N-1}\frac{\tilde{a}_{l}}{\tilde{f}_{l}}\right)^{2}{-}V_{B}\left(\tilde{a}_{1,}\ldots\tilde{a}_{N-1}\right)\,,

(70)

which under a
U(1)_{\text{PQ}}
shift transforms as
10
10

10

Recall that, by definition, the relevant projections take the following values,
\left\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle={F}/{f_{\text{PQ}}}
and
{\left\langle a_{i}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle={F}/{f_{i}}}
.

\displaystyle U(1)_{\text{PQ}}:\quad

\displaystyle a_{\text{PQ}}\longrightarrow a_{\text{PQ}}+\alpha f_{\text{PQ}}\,;

\displaystyle{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\longrightarrow{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}+\alpha f_{\text{PQ}}\left\langle{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle={a}_{G\widetilde{G}}+\alpha F\,;

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}^{\prime}\simeq-\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{2}\left(\frac{{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}}{F}+\alpha\right)^{2}\simeq-\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{2}\frac{{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}^{2}}{F^{2}}-\alpha\chi_{\text{QCD}}\frac{{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}}{F}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\chi_{\text{QCD}}\,.

Now let us consider the diagonalized mass Lagrangian,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}={-}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{N}m_{i}^{2}a_{i}^{2}\,,

(71)

and perform the same
U(1)_{\text{PQ}}
transformation:

\displaystyle U(1)_{\text{PQ}}:\quad

\displaystyle a_{\text{PQ}}\longrightarrow a_{\text{PQ}}+\alpha f_{\text{PQ}}

(72)

\displaystyle a_{i}\quad\longrightarrow a_{i}+\alpha f_{\text{PQ}}\left\langle a_{i}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle\,;

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{N}m_{i}^{2}\left(a_{i}+\left\langle a_{i}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle\alpha f_{\text{PQ}}\right)^{2}\,;

(73)

\displaystyle\,\qquad\qquad\qquad=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{N}m_{i}^{2}a_{i}^{2}-\alpha f_{\text{PQ}}\sum_{i}^{N}m_{i}^{2}\left\langle a_{i}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle a_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}f_{\text{PQ}}^{2}\sum_{i}^{N}m_{i}^{2}\left\langle a_{i}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle^{2}\,.

By imposing the “PQ matching” order by order, we find:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha):\qquad\chi_{\mathrm{QCD}}\frac{{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}}{F}=f_{\text{PQ}}\sum_{i}^{N}m_{i}^{2}\left\langle a_{i}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle a_{i}\,,

(74)

and since
{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}=\sum\left\langle{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\mid a_{i}\right\rangle a_{i}=F\sum a_{i}/f_{i}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\langle a_{i}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle=\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{f_{i}f_{\text{PQ}}m_{i}^{2}}\,.

(75)

Finally, we reproduce our relation by noting that

\displaystyle 1=\frac{\left\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle}{\left\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle}=\sum_{i}^{{N}}\frac{\left\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid a_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle a_{i}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle}{\left\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle}=\sum_{i}^{N}\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{f_{i}f_{\text{PQ}}m_{i}^{2}}\frac{F}{f_{i}}\frac{f_{\text{PQ}}}{F}=\sum_{i}^{N}\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{m_{i}^{2}f_{i}^{2}}=\sum_{i}^{N}\frac{1}{g_{i}}\,,

that is,

\displaystyle\beta_{i}=\frac{1}{g_{i}}=\frac{\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid a_{i}\rangle\left\langle a_{i}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle}{\left\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\right\rangle}\,.

(76)

This shows that for an axion mass eigenstate to coincide with the traditional single QCD axion,
\beta_{i}=1
, the overlap with both
a_{\text{PQ}}
and
{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}
needs to be maximal. Conversely, for a given physical axion to decouple from the sum rule,
\beta_{i}=0,
it is necessary to either have a vanishing projection on the anomalous axion linear combination,
\langle a_{i}\mid{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}\rangle=0
, or on the combination implementing the PQ symmetry,
\left\langle a_{\text{PQ}}\mid a_{i}\right\rangle=0
. Indeed, it is these two vanishing projections that explain the decoupling of one axion from the sum rule in the
N=2
example of
Section

II
, in the limits
r\to 0
and
r\to\infty
.

Besides proving the sum rule in Eq. (
39
) without appealing to the eigenvalue-eigenvector theorem, this derivation directly suggests further relations such as

\displaystyle 1=\sum_{i}^{N}\left\langle a_{i}\mid a_{\text{PQ}}\right\rangle=\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{f_{\text{PQ}}}\sum_{i}^{N}\frac{1}{f_{i}\,m_{i}^{2}}\,,

(77)

which may have a more limited phenomenological impact as compared to the previous sum rule, though, because we do not have direct experimental access to
f_{\text{PQ}}
. In an eventual multi-axion detection, it could help to reconstruct the whole axion system.

Appendix C
Decoupling from the sum rule and mass scales in the extra potential

As we explicitly derived for the toy model in
Section

II
, in the limits where the extra potential is either much smaller or much larger than the QCD contribution, one of the axions decouples from the sum rule. Two pertinent questions arise: how does this behaviour generalize for arbitrary
N
? Is the contribution from the extra potential required to be of the same order of magnitude as the QCD contribution,
in order for all axions to play a relevant role in the sum rule?

Let us consider the basis where the extra mass contribution is diagonal
{\mathbf{M}}_{B}^{2}=\text{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\lambda}_{N})
, which corresponds to the states
\left\{a_{\text{PQ}},\tilde{a}_{1},\dots,\tilde{a}_{N-1}\right\}
. This is the same basis used in the demonstration in App.
B
.

Using
Appendix

B
, the
g_{i}
-factors can be expressed as

\displaystyle g_{i}=\frac{m_{i}^{2}\,F^{2}}{\left|\langle{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}|a_{i}\rangle\right|^{2}\chi_{\text{QCD}}}=\frac{m_{i}^{2}\,}{\left|\langle a_{\text{PQ}}|a_{i}\rangle/f_{\text{PQ}}+\sum_{j}^{N-1}\langle\tilde{a}_{j}|a_{i}\rangle/\tilde{f}_{j}\right|^{2}\chi_{\text{QCD}}}\,.

(78)

For
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\gg\chi_{\rm QCD}/F^{2}
, one of the mass eigenstates of the full matrix corresponds to
a_{j}\simeq\tilde{a}_{j}
, with mass
m_{j}^{2}\simeq\tilde{\lambda}_{j}
, and it is orthogonal to all the other
\tilde{a}_{k\neq j}
. Thus, its
axionness
reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g_{j}}\simeq\frac{\left|\langle{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}|\tilde{a}_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\,F^{2}\,}=\frac{(F/\tilde{f}_{j})^{2}\,\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\,F^{2}}\leq\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\,F^{2}}\longrightarrow 0\,,

(79)

showing that this state effectively decouples from the sum rule.

In the opposite limit,
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\ll\chi_{\rm QCD}/F^{2}
, or equivalently
\varepsilon\equiv\tilde{\lambda}_{j}F^{2}/\chi_{\rm QCD}\to 0
, one can show that the linear combination,

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}=\frac{a_{\text{PQ}}}{\tilde{f}_{j}}-\frac{\tilde{a}_{j}}{f_{\text{PQ}}}+{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon)

(80)

corresponds to a mass eigenstate of the full matrix
{\mathbf{M}}^{2}
with eigenvalue
m_{\epsilon}^{2}\sim\tilde{\lambda}_{j}=\varepsilon\,\chi_{\rm QCD}/F^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g_{j}}\simeq\frac{\left|\langle{a}_{G\widetilde{G}}|\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\right|^{2}\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\,F^{2}}\sim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow 0\,,

(81)

showing that this state also decouples from the sum rule.

Overall, we have demonstrated that whenever one eigenvalue of
M_{B}^{2}
,
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}
, is either much larger or much smaller than the QCD induced mass scale,
\chi_{\rm QCD}/F^{2}
, one axion eigenstate decouples from the sum rule and the phenomenology is well described by the
N-1
axion system.

This statement depends however on the experimental precision.
For example, the matrix

\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}=\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{\hat{f}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&1+\tilde{\lambda}_{1}&1\\
1&1&1+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(82)

with
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}=10^{-3}\tilde{\lambda}_{2}=0.5
, generates a multiple axion system with
(g_{1},\,g_{2},\,g_{3})\approx(1.2,\,7.3,\,497)
. Measuring
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
with sufficiently high precision could allow us to infer the existence of the third axion (or more) even though
1/g_{3}\ll 1
.

Appendix D
Proof of the QCD maxion conditions

We will show that the necessary and sufficient condition to generate QCD maxions is

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n-k}^{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}=N\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{F^{2}}\,\mathcal{B}^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}_{n-1-k}\,,

(83)

determining the characteristic polynomials of
{\mathbf{M}}^{2}
and the minor
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N-k}}{(n-k)!}\,\mathcal{B}_{n-k}^{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\,\lambda^{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1-k}}{(n-1-k)!}\mathcal{B}^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}_{n-1-k}\,\lambda^{k}\,.

(84)

In order to prove this result, we will make use of the eigenvector-eigenvalue identity
JacobiDeBQ
;
Denton:2019pka
stated below.

Theorem
. If
A
is an
N\times N
hermitian matrix with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(A),\ldots,\lambda_{N}(A)
and
i,j=1,\ldots,N
, then the
j^{\text{th }}
component
v_{ij}
of a unit eigenvector
v_{i}
associated to
\lambda_{i}(A)
is related to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\left(M_{j}\right),\ldots,\lambda_{N-1}\left(M_{j}\right)
of the minor
M_{j}
, obtained by removing the
j^{\text{th }}
row and column of A, by the formula

\displaystyle\left|v_{ij}\right|^{2}\prod_{k=1;k\neq i}^{N}\left(\lambda_{i}(A)-\lambda_{k}(A)\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\left(\lambda_{i}(A)-\lambda_{k}\left(M_{j}\right)\right)\,.

(85)

We will start by proving that
g_{i}=N,\,\forall i
implies
Eq.

83
.
Let us first rewrite the
g
-factors in terms of the characteristic polynomials defined above,

\displaystyle g_{i}=\frac{F^{2}}{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}\frac{\lambda_{i}\prod_{i\neq k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})}{\,\prod_{i\neq k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k}(M_{1}))}=\frac{F^{2}}{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{i}}\frac{\lambda\,\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}(\lambda)}{(\lambda-\lambda_{i})\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}}(\lambda)}\,.

(86)

Since
\lambda_{i}
is a zero of
{p_{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\,(\lambda)
, it follows that
{p_{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\left(\lambda_{i}\right)=0
and thus the
g
-factor can be expressed in terms of the derivative of the characteristic polynomial evaluated in
\lambda_{i}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda i}\frac{\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}(\lambda)}{\lambda-\lambda_{i}}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{i}}\frac{\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}(\lambda)-\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}\left(\lambda_{i}\right)}{\lambda-\lambda_{i}}=\left.\frac{d\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}(\lambda)}{d\lambda}\right|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}\quad\implies\quad g_{i}=\frac{F^{2}}{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}}(\lambda_{i})}\left.\frac{d\mbox{\large$p$}_{{\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}}(\lambda)}{d\lambda}\right|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}\,.

(87)

Differentiating the characteristic polynomial in
Eq.

84
and assuming the condition
g_{i}=N,\,\forall i
we find:

\displaystyle N\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{F^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-k-1}}{(n-k-1)!}\mathcal{B}_{n-k-1}^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}\lambda_{i}^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-k-1}}{(n-k-1)!}\mathcal{B}_{n-k}^{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\lambda_{i}^{k}

(88)

\displaystyle{\Leftrightarrow}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-k-1}}{(n-k-1)!}\left[N\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{F^{2}}\mathcal{B}_{n-k-1}^{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}-\mathcal{B}_{n-k}^{{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\right]\lambda_{i}^{k}{\,\equiv\,}A_{ik}C_{k}=0\,,

(89)

where
A_{ik}\equiv\lambda_{i}^{k}
is the Vandermonde matrix of dimension
N-1
, whose determinant is

\displaystyle\operatorname{det}(A)=\prod_{0\leq i<j\leq n}\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}\right)\,.

(90)

Provided none of the eigenvalues are degenerate,
C_{k}=0
, proving the claim.

The proof in the other direction, that is showing that the relations (
83
) imply
g_{i}=N\,\,\forall i
, can be trivially obtained by inserting these relations in
Eq.

87
.

Appendix E

Comparison with clockwork scenario

In general, clockwork matrices
Kim:2004rp
;
Choi:2014rja
;
Kaplan:2015fuy
;
Giudice:2016yja
do not generate maxions (one exception being the model comprising only 2 scalars). The reason being that the next neighbor interactions of clockwork scenarios are engineered to generate exponentially small mixings whereas the maxion solutions require sizable mixings. To see a concrete example, we focus on a scenario with three scalars, where the typical clockwork mass matrix (including the QCD contribution) reads:

\hat{\mathbf{M}}^{2}=\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{\hat{f}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}+r\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{\hat{f}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-q&0\\
-q&1+q^{2}&-q\\
0&-q&q^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(91)

with
q=3
and assuming that only one field in the 3rd-site develops couplings to gluons.

One can easily check that the PQ condition
{\det{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}/{\det{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}}={\chi_{\text{QCD}}}/{{F}^{2}}
is satisfied by this matrix, as
F^{2}=\hat{f}^{2}
.
To prove that this model does not generate maxions, it suffices to show that the remaining two maxions conditions spanned by
Eq.

83
cannot be satisfied for the same
r
. Indeed,

\displaystyle{\text{Tr}\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}=N\,\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r=\frac{1}{10}\,,

(92)

\displaystyle{\text{Tr}^{2}\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}-{\text{Tr}\,{\mathbf{M}}^{2}\cdot{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}=N\,\frac{\chi_{\text{QCD}}}{{F}^{2}}\text{Tr}\,{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r=0\lor r=\frac{11}{182}\,.

(93)

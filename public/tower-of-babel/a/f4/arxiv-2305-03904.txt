Singularity formation for full Ericksen-Leslie system of nematic liquid crystal flows in dimension two
Geng Chen, Tao Huang, Xiang Xu

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the singularity formation for Poiseuille laminar flow of full Ericksen-Leslie system modeling nematic liquid crystal flows in dimension two. The singularity is due to the geometric effect at the origin.



1
Introduction

Liquid crystal materials have a degree of crystal structures but also exhibit many hydrodynamic features so they are capable to flow.
Nematic liquid crystals are composed of rod-like molecules characterized by average alignment of the long axes of neighboring molecules, which have the simplest structure among liquid crystals and have been widely studied analytically and experimentally that lead to fruitful applications
[
17
,
9
,
18
,
33
]
.

The dynamic theory of nematic liquid crystals was first proposed by Ericksen
[
19
]
and Leslie
[
32
]
in the 1960s, (see, e.g.
[
33
,
35
]
).
More precisely, if the orientation order parameters of molecules are treated as unit vector
{\mathbf{d}(x)}
, the classic static theory relies on the following Oseen-Frank free energy density

2W(d,\nabla\mathbf{d})=k_{1}(\mbox{div\,}\mathbf{d})^{2}+k_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\mbox{curl\,}\mathbf{d})^{2}+k_{3}|\mathbf{d}\times\mbox{curl\,}\mathbf{d}|^{2}+(k_{2}+k_{4})[\mbox{tr}(\nabla\mathbf{d})^{2}-(\mbox{div\,}\mathbf{d})^{2}]

where
k_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,3
are the positive constants representing splay, twist, and bend affects respectively, with

k_{2}\geq|k_{4}|,\quad 2k_{1}\geq k_{2}+k_{4}.

The full Ericksen-Leslie system is given as follows

\begin{cases}\dot{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla P=\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\nabla\mathbf{d}}\otimes\nabla\mathbf{d}\right)\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\\
\nu\ddot{\mathbf{d}}=\gamma\mathbf{d}-g-\frac{\partial W}{\partial\mathbf{d}}+\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\nabla\mathbf{d}}\right),\\
|\mathbf{d}|=1\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{u}
is the velocity field of underlying incompressible fluid,

\dot{f}=f_{t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla f
is the material derivative,

\gamma
is Lagrangian multiplier of the constraint
|\mathbf{d}|=1
, and the constant
\nu>0
.
Let

D=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{T}),\quad\omega=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}-(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{T})=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}\mathbf{u}^{i}-\partial_{i}\mathbf{u}^{j}),\quad N=\dot{\mathbf{d}}-\omega\mathbf{d},

represent the rate of strain tensor, skew-symmetric part of the strain rate, and the
rigid rotation part of director changing rate by fluid vorticity, respectively. The kinematic transport
g
is given by

g=\lambda_{1}N+\lambda_{2}Dd

which represents the effect of the macroscopic flow field on the microscopic structure. The material coefficients
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
reflect the molecular shape and the slippery part between fluid and particles. The first term of
g
represents the rigid rotation of molecules, while the second term stands for the stretching of molecules by the flow.
The viscous (Leslie) stress tensor
\sigma
has the following form

\sigma=\mu_{1}(\mathbf{d}^{T}D\mathbf{d})\mathbf{d}\otimes\mathbf{d}+\mu_{2}N\otimes\mathbf{d}+\mu_{3}\mathbf{d}\otimes N+\mu_{4}D+\mu_{5}(D\mathbf{d})\otimes\mathbf{d}+\mu_{6}\mathbf{d}\otimes(D\mathbf{d}).

These coefficients
\mu_{i}(1\leq i\leq 6)
, depending on material and temperature, are called Leslie coefficients, and are related to certain local correlations in the fluid. The following relations are assumed in the literature.

\lambda_{1}=\mu_{3}-\mu_{2},\quad\lambda_{2}=\mu_{6}-\mu_{5},\quad\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=\mu_{6}-\mu_{5}.

The first two relations are compatibility conditions, while the third relation is called Parodi’s relation, derived from Onsager reciprocal relations expressing the equality of certain relations between flows and forces in thermodynamic systems out of equilibrium. They also satisfy the following empirical relations (p.13,
[
33
]
)

\displaystyle\mu_{4}>0,\quad 2\mu_{1}+3\mu_{4}+2\mu_{5}+2\mu_{6}>0,\quad\gamma_{1}=\mu_{3}-\mu_{2}>0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle 2\mu_{4}+\mu_{5}+\mu_{6}>0,\quad 4\gamma_{1}(2\mu_{4}+\mu_{5}+\mu_{6})>(\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}+\gamma_{2})^{2}.

Note that the 4th relation is implied by the 3rd together with the last relation.

The full Ericksen-Leslie system (
1.1
) is a coupled system of forced Navier-Stokes equations and the wave map equations. Basic questions about existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions are not completely understood. Several wellposedness results have been established in
[
7
,
26
,
29
]
, where local-in-time existence and uniqueness for initial data with finite energy, and global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions with small initial data have been established for various choices of coefficients.

When
\nu=0
, the Ericksen-Leslie system (
1.1
) becomes a parabolic system (also called Ericksen-Leslie system in literature). For the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie system in dimension two, the existence and uniqueness of global solution have been studied in
[
51
,
25
,
24
,
34
,
50
]
. In dimension three, under some simplified assumptions, the authors of
[
51
]
established global existence of solutions for small initial data and provided a characterization of the maximal existence time for general initial data. We also refer the reader to the pioneer paper by Fanghua Lin
[
35
]
.

However, the full Ericksen-Leslie system (
1.1
), which is a coupled system of forced Navier-Stokes equations and the wave map equations, is still poorly understood. In general, finite time singularity might form for solutions of (
1.1
) even in one space dimension with smooth initial data. This new phenomenon on formation of cusp singularity due to the quasi-linearity in the wave equation, was first found by the authors in
[
11
]
for the one dimensional Poiseuille laminar flow. On the other hand, the global existence for Hölder continuous solution beyond cusp singularity was established in
[
11
]
. Also see
[
12
]
. To obtain these results, we use earlier frameworks for the variational wave equation: formation of cusp singularity
[
21
,
10
,
13
]
; existence
[
6
,
23
,
13
,
4
,
52
]
. Also see uniqueness
[
3
]
, and Lipschitz continuous dependence results in
[
1
,
5
,
2
]
.

Currently, the study of the solution in multiple space dimension is widely open. In this paper, we investigate the axisymmetric solutions to the full Ericksen-Leslie system (
1.1
) with the special case of Oseen-Frank energy density

2W(\mathbf{d},\nabla\mathbf{d})=|\nabla\mathbf{d}|^{2},

and a simple case of the coefficients

\nu=1,\quad\mu_{1}=\mu_{5}=\mu_{6}=0,\quad\mu_{2}=-1,\quad\mu_{3}=1,\quad\mu_{4}=1,\quad\lambda_{1}=\mu_{3}-\mu_{2}=2.

(1.3)

By the Onsager-Parodi relation,

\lambda_{2}=\mu_{6}-\mu_{5}=\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=0.

The system (
1.1
) becomes

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla P=\nabla\cdot\big(\mathbf{d}\otimes N-N\otimes\mathbf{d}+D\big)-\nabla\cdot\left(\nabla\mathbf{d}\odot\nabla\mathbf{d}\right)\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\\
\ddot{\mathbf{d}}+2\dot{\mathbf{d}}-2\omega\mathbf{d}=\Delta\mathbf{d}+\big(|\nabla\mathbf{d}|^{2}-|\dot{\mathbf{d}}|^{2}\big)\mathbf{d}.\\
|\mathbf{d}|=1.\end{cases}

(1.4)

The goal of this paper is to study the large data solution for (
1.4
). Since the
{\bf d}
equation has a “semi-linear” structure instead of a quasilinear one, we guess the finite time cusp singularity found in
[
11
]
will not form. However, in this paper, we prove that the systems will have another type of finite time blowup, similar as the one for wave map equations, such as the
O(3)
-
\sigma
model in dimension two.

More precisely, we consider the solution for the Poiseuille laminar flow via a tube for (
1.4
) (or (
1.1
) with
k_{1}=k_{2}
). To describe the Poiseuille flow, let
(r,\theta,z)
denote the cylindrical
coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We consider a special form of solutions, so called Poiseuille type solutions. For
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
, consider

\mathbf{u}(x,y,t)=(0,0,v(r,t)),\quad P(x,y,t)=P(r,t)

\mathbf{d}(x,y,t)=\big(\sin\phi(r,t)\cos k\theta,\sin\phi(r,t)\sin k\theta,\cos\phi(r,t)\big).

Then the system (
1.4
) becomes

\begin{cases}\displaystyle v_{t}=\frac{1}{r}\Big(rv_{r}+r\phi_{t}\Big)_{r},\\
\\
\displaystyle\phi_{tt}+2\phi_{t}=\frac{1}{r}\big(r\phi_{r}\big)_{r}-k^{2}\frac{\sin(2\phi)}{2r^{2}}-v_{r}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

We consider the following initial and boundary values

v(r,0)=v_{0},\quad\phi(r,0)=\phi_{0},\quad\partial_{t}\phi(r,0)=\phi_{1},

(1.6)

v(0,t)=0,\quad\phi(0,t)=0,\quad\phi(\infty,t)=\pi.

(1.7)

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

There exist initial values
v_{0}
,
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
, such that the local smooth solutions to the initial-boundary value problem (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) blow up at finite time.

Remark 1.2

The detail of initial values and description of singularity will be given in Theorem
3.1
.

1.5
) includes a damped

-
\sigma

-
\sigma

\phi_{tt}=\frac{1}{r}\big(r\phi_{r}\big)_{r}-k^{2}\frac{\sin(2\phi)}{2r^{2}},\qquad k\geq 1,

which can be derived from the wave map equation under a symmetry in dimension two. See

-
\sigma

In

When

[

]

, and
[

for all other cases.

For solutions satisfying

the global existence and scattering have been proved by a sequence of seminal works: Côte-Kenig-Merle
[

]
,
Côte-Kenig-Lawrie-Schlag
[

[

There are many other fundamental works on the existence and behavior of solutions for (

Our main analysis builds on the framework by
[

]
for (
1.8

. One major difficulty in studying the singularity formation of (
1.5
) is that there is lack of an

The remaining of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove several a priori energy estimates for the system (

Lemma 2.1

Any smooth solution to the system (

) with initial and boundary values (

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|v|^{2}+|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{r}|^{2}+k^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{r^{2}}\right)r\,dr\\

Remark 2.2

For later reference, we denote the energy decay by

E(t):=E[\phi,v](t)=\pi\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}[(\phi_{t})^{2}+(\phi_{r})^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\sin^{2}(\phi)+v^{2}]rdr\leq E[\phi,v](0)=:E(0).

(2.2)

Proof.
Multiplying the first equation of (
1.5
) by

(0,\infty)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{r}|^{2}+k^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{r^{2}}\right)r\,dr=-2\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{t}|^{2}r\,dr-\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{t}v_{r}r\,dr.\end{split}

Adding above two equations together, we conclude

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|v|^{2}+|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{r}|^{2}+k^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{r^{2}}\right)r\,dr=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(2|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|v_{r}|^{2}\right)r\,dr-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{t}v_{r}r\,dr.

which completes the proof of (

We also need some higher order estimates of velocity, which will play a crucial part in our construction of singularity formations. The idea is inspired by arguments in
[
11
]
for one dimensional problem. The main observation is that the scales of

and

are different in heat and wave equations. More precisely, we introduce a new variable

h(r,t)=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}v(R,t)\,R\,dR.

(2.4)

To obtain the equation of

, multiplying the first equation of (
1.5
) by
r

(0,r)
and using the fact

\left(\int_{0}^{r}v(R,t)\,R\,dR\right)_{t}=rv_{r}+r\phi_{t}.

Direct computation implies that

(rh)_{r}=rv,\quad v_{r}=\left(\frac{(rh)_{r}}{r}\right)_{r}=h_{rr}+\frac{h_{r}}{r}-\frac{h}{r^{2}}.

The equation of

is as follows

h_{t}=v_{r}+\phi_{t}=\frac{1}{r}(rh_{r})_{r}-\frac{h}{r^{2}}+\phi_{t}.

(2.5)

By the definition, the boundary condition is

.
The second equation in (
1.5

\phi_{tt}+\phi_{t}=\frac{1}{r}\big(r\phi_{r}\big)_{r}-k^{2}\frac{\sin(2\phi)}{2r^{2}}-h_{t}.

(2.6)

Lemma 2.3

Any smooth solution
(h,\phi)

\leq&\xi\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{tr}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr+C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|h_{tr}|^{2}+|h_{t}|^{2}\right]r\,dr,\end{split}

for some constant
\xi>0
, which will be determined later.
Similarly, we can estimate the third term of right hand side of (
2.14
) as follows

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{t}h_{t}r^{2+\delta}\,dr\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|h_{t}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr,\end{split}

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}(r\phi_{r})_{r}h_{t}r^{1+\delta}\,dr\right|\\
=&\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\phi_{r}h_{tr}r^{2+\delta}+(1+\delta)\phi_{r}h_{t}r^{1+\delta}\right]\,dr\right|\\
\leq&\xi\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{tr}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr+\xi\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{t}|^{2}r^{\delta}\,dr+C\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{r}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\phi)}{2r^{2}}h_{t}r^{2+\delta}\,dr\right|\\
=&\left|\int_{0}^{1}\sin(2\phi)h_{t}r^{\delta}\,dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\sin(2\phi)h_{t}r^{\delta}\,dr\right|\\
\leq&C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{r^{2}}+|h_{t}|^{2}\right]r\,dr+C\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{t}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr.\end{split}

Combining all above estimates together with (
2.14
), we have

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{t}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr+(1-2\xi)\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{tr}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr+(1-\xi)\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{t}|^{2}r^{\delta}\,dr\\
\leq&C+C\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{tr}|^{2}r\,dr+C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|h_{t}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr.\end{split}

(2.15)

To estimate the last term related to
\phi
, we need to use the equation (
2.6
). Multiplying the equation (
2.6
) by
\phi_{t}r^{2+\delta}
and integrating over
(0,\infty)
with respect to
r
, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{t}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{t}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr\\
=&\int_{0}^{\infty}(r\phi_{r})_{r}\phi_{t}r^{1+\delta}\,dr-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}\sin(2\phi)}{2}\phi_{t}r^{\delta}\,dr-\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{t}\phi_{t}r^{2+\delta}\,dr.\end{split}

(2.16)

Similar as the above arguments, we can estimate the first term of right hand side as follows

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}(r\phi_{r})_{r}\phi_{t}r^{1+\delta}\,dr=-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{r}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr-(1+\delta)\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{r}\phi_{t}r^{1+\delta}\,dr.\end{split}

The last term can be estimated as follows

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{r}\phi_{t}r^{1+\delta}\,dr\right|\\
=&\left|\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{r}\phi_{t}r^{1+\delta}\,dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\phi_{r}\phi_{t}r^{1+\delta}\,dr\right|\\
\leq&C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|\phi_{r}|^{2}+|\phi_{t}|^{2}\right]r\,dr+C\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{t}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr.\end{split}

For the other terms on right hand side of (
2.16
), we have

\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\phi)}{2}\phi_{t}r^{\delta}\,dr\right|\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{r^{2}}+|\phi_{t}|^{2}\right]r\,dr+C\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{t}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,dr,

and

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{t}h_{t}r^{2+\delta}\,dr\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|h_{t}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr.\end{split}

Combining all above estimates together with (
2.16
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{r}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr\leq C+C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|h_{t}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr.

(2.17)

Therefore, combining estimates (
2.15
) and (
2.17
), choosing
\xi
small enough and using (
2.2
) and (
2.7
), we finally obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|h_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{r}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr\leq C+C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|h_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{r}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr,\end{split}

then standard Gronwall arguments imply that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[|h_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{r}|^{2}\right]r^{2+\delta}\,dr\leq C,

for any
t\in[0,1]
, where we use the initial assumption (
2.11
). Then using (
2.15
), (
2.12
) and (
2.11
), we can easily prove that

\int_{0}^{t}\int|h_{tr}|^{2}r^{2+\delta}\,drds\leq C,

for any
t\in[0,1]
, which completes the proof of lemma.

\Box

Basic setup and main results of singularity formation

In the rest of this paper, we will establish a singularity formation for system (

) which is also equivalent to the following system

\begin{split}h_{t}&=\frac{1}{r}(rh_{r})_{r}-\frac{h}{r^{2}}+\phi_{t},\\
\phi_{tt}+\phi_{t}&=\frac{1}{r}(r\phi_{r})_{r}-k^{2}\frac{\sin(2\phi)}{2r^{2}}-h_{t},\end{split}

(3.1)

where

First notice that we can rewrite the energy functional as follows

\begin{split}E[\phi,v]&=\displaystyle\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}[(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+(\partial_{r}\phi)^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\sin^{2}(\phi)+v^{2}]rdr\\
&=\displaystyle\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}[(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+(\partial_{r}\phi-\frac{k}{r}\sin(\phi))^{2}+v^{2}]rdr+2\pi\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}[k\sin(\phi)\partial_{r}\phi]dr\\

(3.2)

Let
I(r)
and

be the stationary solution to the energy functional (

) with boundary data (
1.7
). Then

satisfies

(3.3)

It is not hard to solve

(3.4)

For a scaling trajectory
\lambda(t)
which will be defined later,
we denote that

I_{\lambda}(r,t)=I(\lambda(t)r)=2\arctan(\lambda r)^{k},\quad J_{\lambda}(r,t)=k\sin(I_{\lambda})=r\partial_{r}I_{\lambda}=r\partial_{r}I(\lambda r).

(3.5)

For convenience, we denote

\dot{I}_{\lambda}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(I_{\lambda}),\qquad\ddot{I}_{\lambda}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{t^{2}}}(I_{\lambda}),

so does
\dot{J}_{\lambda}
and
\ddot{J}_{\lambda}
.
It is easy to check that

\dot{I}_{\lambda}=\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}J_{\lambda},\quad\dot{J}_{\lambda}=\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}(r\partial_{r}J)_{\lambda}.

(3.6)

As in
[
44
]
, the linearized Hamiltonian

is given as follows

H_{\lambda}=-\partial_{rr}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\cos(2I_{\lambda})=A_{\lambda}^{*}A_{\lambda},

where

A_{\lambda}=-\partial_{r}+\frac{k}{r}\cos(I_{\lambda}),\quad A_{\lambda}^{*}=\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}+\frac{k}{r}\cos(I_{\lambda}).

It is not hard to see that
J_{\lambda}
solves the first order linearized Bogomol’nyi equation

A_{\lambda}J_{\lambda}=0.

(3.8)

The conjugate operator of
H_{\lambda}
is defined as follows

\tilde{H}_{\lambda}=A_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}^{*}=-\partial_{rr}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+V_{\lambda}(r),

(3.9)

where

V_{\lambda}(r)=\frac{k^{2}+1}{r^{2}}+\frac{2k}{r^{2}}\cos(I_{\lambda}).

The following relation holds for
H_{\lambda}
and
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}

A_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}=\tilde{H}_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}.

(3.10)

The following positive, space-repulsive and time-repulsive properties hold for

(cf. (51)-(53) in
[
44
]

V_{\lambda}\geq\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{r^{2}},\quad-\partial_{r}V_{\lambda}\geq 2\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{r^{3}},\quad-\partial_{t}V_{\lambda}=\frac{2k^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}\sin^{2}(I_{\lambda}).

(3.11)

Denote

which is equivalent to the following decomposition of

\phi(t,r)=I_{\lambda}+u.

By (
3.1
) and direct computation, we obtain the equation of

Furthermore, we may decompose

[

]

(3.14)

where

with

,

By the definition of

\langle w_{0},J_{\lambda}\rangle=0,\quad A_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}(w_{0})=\tilde{H}_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}(w_{0})=-A_{\lambda}(\ddot{I}_{\lambda}+2\dot{I}_{\lambda}).

(3.16)

In fact, direct computation implies the orthogonality. To show the second equation, first notice that

-A_{\lambda}\dot{I}_{\lambda}=-\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}A_{\lambda}(r\partial_{r}I_{\lambda})=-\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}A_{\lambda}J_{\lambda}=0,

A_{\lambda}\ddot{I}_{\lambda}=\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}A_{\lambda}\partial_{t}(r\partial_{r}I_{\lambda})=\frac{\dot{\lambda^{2}}}{\lambda^{2}}A_{\lambda}(r\partial_{r}J_{\lambda})=\frac{\dot{\lambda}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}A_{\lambda}(aJ+r\partial_{r}J)_{\lambda}.

Combining these with the definition of

Following the notation in

Lemma 4.1

Let

be defined as in (

\phi
be sufficiently smooth. Suppose that the orthogonality

{}\langle u,J_{\lambda}\rangle=0,

(4.1)

is valid for any time

satisfies the following equation

\dot{\lambda}\left(2\langle I,J\rangle+\left\langle\phi\left(\frac{r}{\lambda}\right),r\partial_{r}J\right\rangle\right)=-\langle\phi_{t},J_{\lambda}\rangle\lambda^{3}.

(4.2)

Reversely, if
\lambda(t)
satisfies the equation (
4.2
) and the orthogonality is valid at initial time, then
u
is orthogonal to
J_{\lambda}
for any time
t>0

Proof.
Taking derivative with respect to time
t
on equation (
4.1
), we obtain

0=\frac{d}{dt}\langle u,J_{\lambda}\rangle=\langle u_{t},J_{\lambda}\rangle+\langle u,\partial_{t}J_{\lambda}\rangle\\
=\langle u_{t},J_{\lambda}\rangle+\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}\langle u,(r\partial_{r}J)_{\lambda}\rangle,

(4.3)

where we use that (
3.6
).
Direct computation implies

\begin{split}\langle u_{t},J_{\lambda}\rangle&=\langle\phi_{t}-(I_{\lambda})_{t},J_{\lambda}\rangle=\langle\phi_{t},J_{\lambda}\rangle-\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}\langle r\partial_{r}I_{\lambda},J_{\lambda}\rangle=\langle\phi_{t},J_{\lambda}\rangle-\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda^{3}}\langle J,J\rangle\end{split}

where we have used the definition of
J_{\lambda}
and the fact

\langle J_{\lambda},J_{\lambda}\rangle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\langle J,J\rangle
in last step.
Similarly, it holds

\begin{split}&\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}\langle u,(r\partial_{r}J)_{\lambda}\rangle\\
=&\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\phi(r\partial_{r}J)_{\lambda}rdr-\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}I_{\lambda}(r\partial_{r}J)_{\lambda}rdr\\
=&\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\phi(r\partial_{r}J)_{\lambda}\lambda rd\lambda r-\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}I(r\partial_{r}J)rdr\\
=&\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda^{3}}\left\langle\phi\left(\frac{r}{\lambda}\right),r\partial_{r}J\right\rangle+\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\lambda^{3}}\big(2\langle I,J\rangle+\langle J,J\rangle\big)\end{split}

where we have used integration by parts in the last step.
Combining above equations with (
4.3
) implies (
4.2
).
The section part of this lemma is trivial. This completes the proof of lemma.

\Box

\begin{split}\epsilon^{2}\geq&E[\phi,v]-4k\pi=E[\phi,v]-E[I_{\lambda},0]\\

where

is smooth, the energy

(4.13)

By the definition of

and

which gives

|\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}(u)|\leq C\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\left(\partial_{r}u+\frac{u}{r}\right)^{2}\,rdr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\frac{u^{4}}{r^{2}}rdr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq C\big(E_{0}(u,v)\big)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

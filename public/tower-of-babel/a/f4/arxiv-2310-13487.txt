Two-stage weighted least squares estimator of multivariate discrete-valued observation-driven models
Mirko Armillotta

Abstract
In this work a general semi-parametric multivariate model where the first two conditional moments are assumed to be multivariate time series is introduced. The focus of the estimation is the conditional mean parameter vector for discrete-valued distributions. Quasi-Maximum Likelihood Estimators (QMLEs) based on the linear exponential family are typically employed for such estimation problems when the true multivariate conditional probability distribution is unknown or too complex. Although QMLEs provide consistent estimates they may be inefficient. In this paper novel two-stage Multivariate Weighted Least Square Estimators (MWLSEs) are introduced which enjoy the same consistency property as the QMLEs but can provide improved efficiency with suitable choice of the covariance matrix of the observations. The proposed method allows for a more accurate estimation of model parameters in particular for count and categorical data when maximum likelihood estimation is unfeasible. Moreover, consistency and asymptotic normality of MWLSEs are derived. The estimation performance of QMLEs and MWLSEs is compared through simulation experiments and a real data application, showing superior accuracy of the proposed methodology.



Keywords:
Categorical data, Multivariate binary time series, Multivariate Integer-valued GARCH, Multivariate Poisson, Quasi-likelihood.

1
Introduction

A large variety of statistical models have been proposed in the literature to model the conditional mean of time series data. The specification of the model strongly depends on the nature of the time series variables of the analysis. An example of models typically employed when the time series variables are continuous and take values on
\mathbb{R}
are the well-known Autoregressive Moving Average (ARMA) models
(
Box et al., 1970
)
. Instead, Autoregressive Conditional Duration (ACD) models
Engle and Russell, 1998
are tailored for modeling non-negative continuous processes and Beta autoregressive models
Rocha and Cribari-Neto, 2009
are employed for modeling time series data lying in a specified interval. Coming to discrete variables, Integer-valued Autoregressive (INAR) models
Al-Osh and Alzaid, 1987
;
McKenzie, 1988
and Integer-valued GARCH models (INGARCH)
Heinen, 2003
;
Ferland et al., 2006
have been proposed to model the discrete non-negative count processes.
Qaqish, 2003
,

Russell and Engle, 2005
and
Moysiadis and Fokianos, 2014
introduced analogous autoregressive models for binary and categorical time series. Description of further modelling approaches for discrete-valued time series can be found in
Armillotta et al., 2022b
and
Armillotta et al., 2023c
. Some of the models for discrete-valued data have also been extended to the multivariate framework.
Fokianos et al., 2020
introduced the multivariate INGARCH for vector count time series. A few models for multivariate binary and categorical time series have also been proposed
(
Raftery, 1985
)
and multivariate logistic regressions following the line of
Azzalini, 1994
. This review of modelling approaches is far from being comprehensive. See
Joe, 1997
and
MacDonald and Zucchini, 1997
for the description of further modelling techniques for discrete data.

The estimation of the mentioned models can be performed by appealing the Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE), which constitutes a natural approach for unknown parameters’ estimation in both univariate and multivariate parametric models. However, the MLE lies on parametric assumptions regarding the whole joint distribution of the time series. This constitutes a limiting feature in cases where the interest is only on modeling the conditional means rather than the whole distribution. Moreover, the likelihood function can sometimes present a complex form and the implementation of the MLE can become cumbersome or unfeasible. For instance, exact likelihood inference of INAR models is well-known to be numerically difficult
Bu et al., 2008
;
Drost et al., 2009
;
Pedeli et al., 2015
. Finally, such problems can become even more severe when multivariate models are taken into account, especially for discrete-valued data due to complex form of multivariate discrete distributions; see
Dai et al., 2013
and
Fokianos, 2022
, among others. In such situations, the use of quasi-likelihood methods becomes attractive.

The Quasi-MLE (QMLE), proposed by
Wedderburn, 1974
, follows the same philosophy of the MLE but where the object of the optimization is a quasi-likelihood, not necessarily the true distribution of the data. Quasi-likelihoods are typically a member of the one-parameter exponential family in univariate models whereas in multivariate models the marginal quasi-likelihoods including only the marginal distributions of the data are most often employed. The QMLE has been shown to be consistent for the true unknown parameters of the model
(
Gourieroux et al., 1984
)
but it can lead to inefficient estimates for two main reasons: i) given a parametric function for the conditional mean of the time series, the conditional variance is implicitly constrained to be a function of the mean according to the selected exponential family of distributions; ii) in multivariate models the marginal QMLE does not account for cross-sectional dependence between the observations.
So as to tackle the source of inefficiency i) for the estimation of the parameters of the conditional mean in univariate time series models,
Aknouche and Francq, 2021
introduce a two-stage Weighted Least Squares Estimator (WLSE) where in the first step the conditional variance of the process is estimated and it is then used in the second step as weighting sequence for the solution of the weighted least squares problem. WLSEs lead to improved efficiency with respect to QMLEs if the variance function is correctly specified. Se also
Armillotta and Gorgi, 2023
for an alternative improved efficiency estimator leveraging simultaneous estimation of conditional mean and variance. Nevertheless, two-stage estimation has been showed to reduce inefficiency only for univariate time series models and it does not take into account problem ii) arising when only marginal distributions are included in multivariate estimation problems.

In this paper, we propose a novel class of two-stage Multivariate Weighted Least Squares Estimator (MWLSEs) for the estimation of the conditional mean of semi-parametric time series models. In the first step estimation a weighted least squares solution is obtained for the mean parameters if a preliminary weighting sequence is available, otherwise a least squares solution is employed. The first-step estimates of the unknown parameters are then employed as a basis to compute a working conditional covariance matrix for the process.
This is then used in the second step as weighting sequence of matrices for the solution of the multivariate weighted least squares problem. MWLSEs are able to overcome problem i) since the conditional variances of the process can take a general form and are not required to be correctly specified. Moreover, they can depend on additional parameters. The proposed family of estimators also allows to avoid problem ii) by taking into account cross-sectional correlations between the observations. Analogously to the specification of the conditional covariances, the correlation matrix is allowed to be misspecified and to depend on extra parameters. The described features show that MWLSEs provide a quite flexible specification of the second moment that can ultimately enhance the efficiency of the estimates with respect to previous QMLE-based approaches. An example of superior performances of MWLSE is provided through extensive numerical results for multivariate integer-valued time series models which yields evidence of improved estimator efficiency where a moderate or high degree of cross-sectional dependence occurs in the observations.

Moreover, the MWLSE only requires parametric assumptions on the conditional expectation as the conditional covariance matrix does not need to be correctly specified and does not require the specification of the entire conditional probability distribution of the process. In this way the estimation is robust against possible misspecification of the conditional distribution and simplifies the inference when the full MLE is undoable or too complex. Strong consistency and asymptotic normality of the MWLSEs are established under very general conditions.

Finally, the practical usefulness of the MWLSE approach is illustrated by means of a real data application concerning binary time series. In financial time series analysis expected values of stock returns are typically considered to be unpredictable. However, the direction of price change (positive or negative) can be predicted. Therefore I propose a multivariate binary time series model for the probability of a positive sign of returns and show that the two-stage MWLSE improves the estimation capacity of the model with respect to Bernoulli QMLE. The proposed approach can be easily extended to more general multivariate categorical time series.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
discusses the QMLE inefficiency problem in semi-parametric time series models with particular attention to discrete-valued data. Section
3
introduces the general framework of MWLSEs and the way the first-step working conditional covariance matrix is computed. Asymptotic theory of first-step and two-stage MWLSEs are also established in this section.
Section
4
presents an extensive numerical analysis for integer-valued data with the comparison between the MWLSE and alternative quasi-likelihood methods showing the improved efficiency performances of MWLSEs. Finally, Section
5
presents an empirical application on IT companies stock price directions involving binary and categorical data.

2
Inefficiency of QMLE

Consider a multivariate stationary and ergodic sequence of real-valued processes
\{\mathbf{Y}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
defined in a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with first conditional moment given by

\mathrm{E}(\mathbf{Y}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t-1})=\lambda(\mathbf{Y}_{t-1},\mathbf{Y}_{t-2},\dots;\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})=\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}

(1)

where
\mathbb{Z}
is the set of integers,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
denotes the
\sigma
-field generated by
\{\mathbf{Y}_{s}\,,\,\,s\leq t\}
,
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\times\Theta\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a known
measurable function, with
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
unknown parameter vector belonging to some compact parameter space
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Model (
1
) is sometimes called an observation-driven model
(
Cox, 1981
)
since the conditional mean parameter purely depends only on past observations.
Sometimes the specification of model (
1
) can be completed by setting a function of the data
\mathbf{Y}_{t}=f(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t};\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})
where
\{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an iid sequence. It is the case of the Vector Autoregressive Moving-Average models (VARMA), see
Lütkepohl, 2005
, or also the Multivariate Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity model (MGARCH); see
Francq and Zakoian, 2019
. Although the MLE for model (
1
) is sometimes readily computable when the joint conditional distribution function (cdf) can be specified (as in the case of VARMA and MGARCH models) this may not always be the case. For example, when count time series models are analyzed, for models encompassed in eq. (
1
), such as the Multivariate Integer-valued GARCH (MINGARCH)
(
Fokianos et al., 2020
)
, a Poisson distribution can be specified for the marginal probability mass functions (pmf) of the univariate process
Y_{i,t}
, for
i=1,\dots,d
. However, to obtain a complete joint cdf a particular joint pmf needs to be specified as well. The functional form of such joint pmf can be unknown or too complex to be employed for the MLE.
Indeed, the development of a multivariate count time series model would be based on specification of a joint pmf distribution, so that the standard likelihood inference and testing procedures can be developed.
The choice of a suitable multivariate version of the Poisson pmf is a challenging problem. In fact, multivariate Poisson-type pmfs have usually complicated closed form and the associated likelihood inference is theoretically and computationally cumbersome. Furthermore, in many cases, the available multivariate Poisson-type pmfs implicitly imply restricted models, which are of limited use in applications (e.g. covariances always positive, constant pairwise covariances). See
Inouye et al., 2017
and
Fokianos, 2022
for a discussion on the choice of multivariate count distributions and several alternatives. Other approaches try to avoid the problem of specifying a multivariate Poisson distribution, like in
Fokianos et al., 2020
, where the joint distribution of the vector
\left\{\mathbf{Y}_{t}\right\}
is constructed by following a copula approach where all marginal distributions of
Y_{i,t}
are univariate Poisson, conditionally to the past, with mean process
\lambda_{i,t}
, for
i=1,\dots,d
. However, this approach does not allow to perform a maximum likelihood estimation but requires appealing the class of marginal QMLEs based on the linear exponential family that assumes independence between the univariate processes
Y_{i,t}
, for
i=1,\dots,d
. Analogous limitations appear when trying to model binary and categorical time series. For example, the Mixture
Transition Distribution (MTD) model of
Raftery, 1985
still require the application of some form of marginal QMLE which assumes independence between observations. Similar limitations apply to multivariate logistic regressions. For further details the interested reader can see the related discussion in
Weiß, 2018
.

The marginal QMLE for multivariate time series can be generally written as the log-likelihood on marginal pdfs
Y_{i,t}|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\sim q(\lambda_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}))
coming from the exponential family of distributions
(
Wedderburn, 1974
)

\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{Q}=\argmax_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Theta}L^{Q}_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\,,\quad\quad L^{Q}_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{d}q(\lambda_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta}))\,.

(2)

If the conditional mean vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})
is correctly specified then (
2
) is consistent and asymptotically normal for the unknown parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}

(
Gourieroux et al., 1984
)
. Moreover, the QML estimator
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{Q}
can also be seen as the vector which solves the first order conditions that typically take the form

\mathbf{S}^{Q}_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime}}\mathbf{D}_{t}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\Big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\Big)=\mathbf{0}\,,

(3)

where
\mathbf{S}^{Q}_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
is the score of the QMLE,
\mathbf{D}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
is the
d\times d
diagonal matrix with diagonal elements equal to
\nu_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
for
i=1,\dots,d
and
\nu_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
is the single element of the conditional pseudo-variance vector of the process

\nu(\mathbf{Y}_{t-1},\mathbf{Y}_{t-2},\dots;\boldsymbol{\theta})=\boldsymbol{\nu}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}

(4)

with
\nu:\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\times\Theta\to\left[0,+\infty\right)^{d}
being a measurable function. I refer to (
4
) as a pseudo-variance vector as it is not necessarily correctly specified.
From the property of the linear exponential family the pseudo-variance model depends on the mean model, i.e.
\nu_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=g(\lambda_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta}))
where
g(\cdot)
is some measurable function. The possible value of
\nu_{i,t}
is however restricted by the fact that it must match the conditional variance of the selected distribution in the exponential family. For example, in the QMLE employed in
Fokianos et al., 2020
all marginal distributions of
Y_{i,t}
are univariate Poisson, conditionally to the past, so
\nu_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\lambda_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
, for
i=1,\dots,d
. In practice, one can easily
conceive that the true conditional variance may have other forms. Obviously, the choice of inadequate marginal pmf, or probability density function (pdf), may affect the efficiency of the QMLEs. Moreover, it is clear that the score of the QMLE described in (
3
) does not directly include any joint dependence between the univariate processes because is computed upon a quasi-likelihood which assumes contemporaneous independence among
Y_{i,t}
, for
i=1,\dots,d
. Therefore, the QMLE suffer from two main sources of inefficiency: constrained variance specification and exclusion of joint dependence parameters. Then, the aim of the present paper is two-fold: improving the efficiency of QMLEs by reducing the impact of these two sources of inefficiency and retaining the same amount of assumptions on the model i.e. a semi-parametric approach assuming only the conditional mean
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})
to be correctly specified.

3
Multivariate Weighted Least Squares Estimators

In order to mitigate the inefficiency of QMLEs the present contribution proposes a novel class of two-stage Multivariate Weighted Least Square Estimators (MWLSEs) of the mean parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
. Given a matrix weight function
\mathbf{W}_{t}=\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{Y}_{t-1},\mathbf{Y}_{t-2},\dots)
, where
\mathbf{W}
is a measurable function from
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, a first-step MWLSE is defined by

\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{1}=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Theta}L_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{W})

(5)

where

L_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{W})=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{W}_{t})\,,\quad\quad l_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{W}_{t})=\Big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\Big)^{\prime}\mathbf{W}_{t}\Big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\Big)\,.

(6)

For example, when
\mathbf{W}_{t}=\mathbf{I}
where
\mathbf{I}
is the identity matrix of suitable dimension, (
6
) corresponds to the multivariate version of the conditional Least Squares Estimator (LSE) of
Klimko and Nelson, 1978
. See also
Armillotta et al., 2023a
. It is well-known that the optimal choice of the weights
\mathbf{W}_{t}
in weighted least squares is the inverse of the true conditional covariance matrix of the process, say
(\tilde{\boldsymbol{V}}_{t}^{-1})_{t\geq 1}
. In practice,
\tilde{\boldsymbol{V}}_{t}
is generally unknown. Therefore, I specify a conditional pseudo-covariance matrix with a parametric specification of the form
\boldsymbol{V}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0})=V(\mathbf{Y}_{t-1},\mathbf{Y}_{t-2},\dots;\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0})
, where
V
is a measurable function defined in
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, which is not necessarily the true form of the covariance matrix. It depends on a pseudo-true parameter vector
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0}
containing the true mean parameter vector
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
, or some of its elements, and possibly some extra parameters
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{0}
. Define
\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{1}^{\prime},\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\prime})^{\prime}
the estimator of
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0}
where
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{1}
is the first-step estimator of
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
is some previously obtained estimator of
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{0}
. Then, the optimal sequence of weights is estimated by

\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{t}=\boldsymbol{V}_{t}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})^{-1}=\mathbf{D}_{t}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})^{-1/2}\mathbf{P}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{t}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})^{-1/2}

(7)

where, analogously to Section
2
,
\mathbf{D}_{t}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is the
d\times d
diagonal matrix with diagonal elements equal to
\nu_{i,t}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
for
i=1,\dots,d
and
\nu_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
is defined as

\nu(\mathbf{Y}_{t-1},\mathbf{Y}_{t-2},\dots;\boldsymbol{\tau})=\boldsymbol{\nu}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\tau})\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(8)

Differently from (
4
) the pseudo-variance is not constrained to be some pre-specified function of the mean and can depend on extra parameters. Moreover,
\mathbf{P}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is a
d\times d
working correlation matrix whose structure can be defined by the researcher and whose single element has a functional form of the type

r_{i,j}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=r(\mathbf{Y}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{Y}_{T};\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,,\quad\text{for}\quad i,j=1,\dots,d

(9)

where
r:\mathbb{R}^{T}\to(-1,1)
.
Then, the two-stage MWLSE for the unknown mean parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
is given by

\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Theta}L_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\hat{\mathbf{W}})\,,\quad\quad L_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\hat{\mathbf{W}})=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{t})

(10)

where
l_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{t})
is defined analogously to (
6
) and
\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{t}
has the form (
7
). Hence, the two-stage MWLSE defined in (
10
) is the solution of the first order condition system

\mathbf{S}_{T}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\hat{\mathbf{W}})=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{V}_{t}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})^{-1}\Big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\Big)=\boldsymbol{0}\,.

(11)

The first order conditions (
3
) and (
11
) allow for a direct and intuitive comparison between QMLEs and MWLSEs. The first main difference is that in (
11
) there is no particular constraint on the shape of the univariate conditional pseudo-variances,
\nu_{i,t}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
whereas in (
3
) the form of the pseudo-variances are constrained by the univariate conditional distribution specified in the quasi-likelihood. Moreover, the MWLSE allows the function
\nu(\cdot)
to depend on extra parameters apart from the mean vector parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Consequently, the MWLSE adds more flexibility in the choice of the variances form and this can lead to an improved efficiency estimator. A second difference lies in the possibility of including correlation effects in the estimation system (
11
) whereas the QMLE simply implies no correlation in the score (
3
), i.e.
\mathbf{P}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}})^{-1}=\mathbf{I}
. For these additional features the MWLSE is expected to explain a larger part of the variability connected to the process of study compared to QMLE.
In Section
4
I will show with a simulated experiment that when a moderate or high degree of contemporaneous dependence is indeed present among the variables
Y_{i,t}
for
i=1,\dots,d
the two-stage MWLSE entails a relevant gain in efficiency with respect to the QMLE.

The MWLSEs do require only the correct specification of the first conditional moment of the process instead of entirely specifying its conditional distribution. In this sense, they are semi-parametric estimators because, except for the first moment, they are totally agnostic about the distribution of the observations. In this way the estimation is robust against possible misspecification of the conditional distribution and simplifies the inference when the full MLE is undoable or too complex. Finally, MWLSEs present the extra advantage of being quite immediate to estimate since they require the call of two simple least squares estimations.

Modeling IT companies stock return directions

Binary time series are of utmost importance in financial applications. Indeed, in financial time series analysis, it is well known that the expected values of the stock returns are quite difficult to predict, as they typically behave like white noise sequences. However, the volatility of the returns can generally be forecasted; this implies that also the sign of the returns can be predicted, see for instance

Christoffersen and Diebold, 2006
and
Christoffersen et al., 2007

Breen et al., 1989

Moysiadis and Fokianos, 2014

i

Remark 1
.

(Categorical data) Estimation of model (
17
) can be immediately extended to more general categorical data with
M
categories. Consider the categorical multivariate random variable
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}=\left\{1,2,\dots,M\right\}^{d}
for
t=1,\dots,n
. The
t
-th observation of the categorical
time series can be expressed by the
dq
-dimensional vector
\mathbf{Y}_{t}=(\mathbf{Y}^{\prime}_{(1)t},\dots,\mathbf{Y}^{\prime}_{(q)t})^{\prime}
where
q=M-1
and, for
j=1,\dots,q
,
\mathbf{Y}_{(j)t}
, are
d
-dimensional binary multivariate time series whose single elements take value
Y_{(j)i,t}=1
if category
j
occurs for observation
i
at time
t
and
Y_{(j)i,t}=0
otherwise, for
i=1,\dots,d
. The last category is then defined as
\mathbf{Y}_{(M)t}=1-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\mathbf{Y}_{(j)t}
. Analogously, the estimation of (
17
) can be carried out on the aggregated probability vector
\boldsymbol{p}_{t}=(\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}_{(1)t},\dots,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}_{(q)t})^{\prime}
where
\boldsymbol{p}_{(j)t}
is the multivariate
d
-dimensional vector of probabilities associated to category
j
whose single element is defined by
p_{(j)i,t}=\mathrm{P}(Y_{(j)i,t}=1|\mathcal{F}_{t-1})
. To reduce parameters proliferation a parsimonious block diagonal structure specification may be considered for parameter matrices
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
with
d\times d
block diagonal elements
\mathbf{A}_{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{A}_{(q)}
and
\mathbf{B}_{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{B}_{(q)}
. Finally, the simple constraint
{\left|\kern-0.86108pt\left|\kern-0.86108pt\left|\sum_{j=1}^{q}(\mathbf{C}_{(j)}+\mathbf{A}_{(j)}+\mathbf{B}_{(j)})\right|\kern-0.86108pt\right|\kern-0.86108pt\right|}_{\infty}<1
where
\mathbf{C}_{(j)}=\text{diag}(\mathbf{c}_{(j)})
allows to retain the summability condition
\sum_{j=1}^{q}p_{(j)i,t}<1
for any
i
and
t
and clearly
\boldsymbol{p}_{(M)t}=1-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\boldsymbol{p}_{(j)t}
. Therefore, the two-stage MWLSE discussed in this work directly applies also to categorical data.

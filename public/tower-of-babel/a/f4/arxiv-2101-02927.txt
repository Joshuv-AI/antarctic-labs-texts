Global solution to the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations with uniform energy bounds
Shijie Dong

Abstract
We are interested in the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations in ℝ 1 + 3 \mathbb{R}^{1+3} , and we aim to show that the energy for the global solution to the equations is uniformly bounded, and we do not require the compactness assumption on the initial data. To achieve these goals, the key is to apply Alinhac’s ghost weight energy estimates adapted to the Klein-Gordon equations.



Keywords.
Klein-Gordon-Zakharov model; small global solution; uniform energy bounds.

Introduction

1.1
Model problem and main result

We are interested in the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov model in
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
, which describes the nonlinear interaction between Langmuir waves and ion-acoustic waves. The equations read

\displaystyle-\Box E+E=-nE,

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\Box n=\Delta|E|^{2},

and the initial data are prescribed on the slice
t=t_{0}=0

\displaystyle\big(E,\partial_{t}E\big)(t_{0},\cdot)=\big(E_{0},E_{1}\big),\qquad\big(n,\partial_{t}n\big)(t_{0},\cdot)=\big(n_{0},n_{1}\big).

(1.2)

The unknowns include
E=(E^{1},E^{2},E^{3})
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
n
taking values in
\mathbb{R}
, which can be regarded as a Klein-Gordon component and a wave component respectively.
As usual, the wave operator is denoted by
\Box=\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}=-\partial_{t}\partial_{t}+\Delta
, with the Laplace operator
\Delta=\partial_{a}\partial^{a}
.
We take the signature
(-,+,+,+)
in the spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
, and the indices are raised or lowered by the Minkowski metric
m=\text{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
. The Greek letters
\alpha,\beta,\cdots\in\{0,1,2,3\}
are used to denote the spacetime indices, and Latin letters
a,b,\cdots\in\{1,2,3\}
are used to represent the space indices, and we adopt the Einstein summation convention. The notation
\lesssim
will be frequently used, and we write
A\lesssim B
to indicate
A\leq CB
with
C
a universal constant. The Japanese bracket
\langle A\rangle=\sqrt{1+|A|^{2}}
is used in the usual way, and we use the abbreviation
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
to denote the
L^{2}
norm.

In addition to showing the small data global existence result to the model problem (
1.1
), and to deriving the pointwise asymptotic behavior of the solution, it is also interesting to verify whether the energy for the solution is uniformly bounded or blows up at infinity. We now provide the statement of the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations in (
1.1
), and let
N\geq 15
be an integer. There exits
\epsilon_{0}>0
, such that for all
\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
, and all initial data satisfying the smallness condition

\sum_{|I|\leq N+2}\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|+1}\partial^{I}E_{0}\|+\sum_{|I|\leq N+1}\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|+2}\partial^{I}E_{1}\|+\sum_{|I|\leq N+1}\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|}n_{0}\|+\sum_{|I|\leq N}\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|+1}n_{1}\|\leq\epsilon,

(1.3)

the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) admits a global-in-time solution
(E,n)
,
which satisfies the following sharp pointwise decay results

|E(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-3/2},\quad|n(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-1/2}.

(1.4)

With
\Gamma\in\{\partial_{\alpha},L_{a},\Omega_{ab}\}
, the following uniform energy estimates are also valid

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{I}E(t)\|+\|\Gamma^{I}E(t)\|\lesssim 1,\qquad|I|\leq N+1,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\Gamma^{I}n(t)\|\lesssim 1,\qquad|I|\leq N+1.

In terms of small data global existence result for the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations and the asymptotic behavior of the solution, there exist a few proofs, see for instance
[

,

,
18
,

]
.
So the main contribution in Theorem

is that the energy for the solution to the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations is shown to be uniformly bounded.

In order to prove the energy for
E,n
is uniformly bounded, the key is to apply Alinhac’s ghost weight method adapted to Klein-Gordon equations, which was used in
[
6
]
by the author when dealing with a coupled wave and Klein-Gordon system in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
. Originally, the ghost weight method was applied to wave equations with null nonlinearities
[

]
, and allows one to benefit from the

acting on the wave components. But we find in

that the ghost weight energy estimates on Klein-Gordon equations provide us with some new and strong result, i.e. we can benefit from the

The analysis in the proof of Theorem

1.3
Reformulation of the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations

In the original formulation of the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations, there appears wave component without derivatives in the nonlinearities. This will somewhat cause difficulties because the wave component without derivatives cannot be controlled by its nature energy. So we reformulate the equations as

\displaystyle-\Box E+E=-n^{0}E-\Delta n^{\Delta}E,

(1.6)

\displaystyle-\Box n^{0}=0,\qquad-\Box n^{\Delta}=|E|^{2},

with initial data on
t=t_{0}

\big(E,\partial_{t}E,n^{0},\partial_{t}n^{0},n^{\Delta},\partial_{t}n^{\Delta}\big)(t_{0})=(E_{0},E_{1},n_{0},n_{1},0,0).

(1.7)

The following relation can be used to estimate the original unknowns
(E,n)
by the new unknowns
(E,n^{0},n^{\Delta})

n=n^{0}+\Delta n^{\Delta}.

(1.8)

This kind of reformulation was used by Katayama
[
18
]
for wave equations with nonlinearities of divergence form, which provides one with better estimates for the wave components without derivatives.
In the new formulation (
1.6
), we note that the wave component
n^{0}
is a free wave, while the wave component
n^{\Delta}
is with derivatives in the nonlinearities, and thus we expect that the reformulated system of equations is easier to handle.

1.4
Some unsolved problems

Besides of the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov model, there exist many other physical models which can be described by coupled wave and Klein-Gordon equations, including the Dirac-Klein-Gordon model, the Maxwell-Klein-Gordon model, the Dirac-Proca model, the Einstein-Klein-Gordon model, etc.. The existence of small global solutions and pointwise decay of the solutions for the aforementioned models in
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
have already been obtained. But whether the energy for the solutions is uniformly bounded is still unknown, except the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov model.

We take the (simplified) Einstein-Klein-Gordon equations as an example, and we recall that there appear terms of type
u\partial\partial v
in the nonlinearities, with
u
a wave component, and
v
a Klein-Gordon component. But in that case, it seems that the best decay rate we can get for the wave component is
|u|\lesssim t^{-1}
, which does not have any extra
t-r
decay. This observation unfortunately indicates that there is little chance for one to obtain the uniform energy bounds for the Einstein-Klein-Gordon equations by applying either the ghost weight method or the hyperboloidal foliation method.

-\Box\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}=\partial\Gamma^{I}|\Psi|^{2}.

We then apply the conformal energy estimates in (
2.6
) to find (recall
N\geq 15
)

\displaystyle E_{con}(t,\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{con}(t_{0},\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta})^{1/2}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\langle t^{\prime}+|x|\rangle\partial\Gamma^{I}\big(|\Psi|^{2}\big)\big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon+\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq N-7,|I_{2}|\leq N+1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\langle t^{\prime}+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I_{1}}\Psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\partial\Gamma^{I_{2}}\Psi\big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq N-7,|I_{2}|\leq N+1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\langle t^{\prime}+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I_{1}}\Psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\Psi\big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\langle t\rangle^{1/2}.

The proof is done.
∎

As a consequence, we are able to obtain good pointwise estimates for
\widetilde{\phi}
away from the light cone by the aid of the scaling vector field, and this strategy of taking advantage of the scaling vector field in the coupled wave and Klein-Gordon equations was first used in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\Psi,\phi)\in X
, and denote
\big(\widetilde{\Psi},\widetilde{\phi}\big)=T(\Psi,\phi)
, then we have

\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1/2}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1},\qquad|I|\leq N-2.

(4.10)

Proof.

The conformal energy estimates in (
4.9
) give

\big\|L_{0}\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big\|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t\rangle^{1/2},\qquad|I|\leq N+1,

which implies

\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq 3,|I_{2}|\leq N-2}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{1}}L_{0}\partial\Gamma^{I_{2}}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big\|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t\rangle^{1/2}.

The application of the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality (
2.8
) deduces

\big|L_{0}\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1/2},\qquad|I|\leq N-2.

Similarly, we have

\big|\Gamma\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1/2},\qquad|I|\leq N-2.

Then, we recall the simple relation

|\partial w|\lesssim\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1}\big(|L_{0}w|+|\Gamma w|\big),

which allows us to have

\big|\partial\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1/2}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1},\qquad|I|\leq N-2.

Finally, the relation
\widetilde{\phi}=\widetilde{\phi}^{0}+\Delta\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}
implies

\displaystyle\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}\big|\lesssim\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{0}\big|+\big|\Gamma^{I}\Delta\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1/2}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1},\qquad|I|\leq N-2.

The proof is complete.
∎

As a complement of Lemma
4.6
, we have the following result, which gives us good pointwise decay of
\widetilde{\phi}
away from the origin.

Lemma 4.7
.

It holds

\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle|x|\rangle^{-1}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1/2},\qquad|I|\leq N-9.

(4.11)

Proof.

Consider the
\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}
equation in (
4.8
), and note that we have

\sum_{|I|\leq N-9,|J_{1}|+|J_{2}|\leq 1}\big|\partial^{J_{1}}\Omega^{J_{2}}\big(\partial\Gamma^{I}|\Psi|^{2}\big)\big|\lesssim(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-3}.

Then we apply Lemma
2.6
, with
\rho=0,\kappa=1,\mu=1/2
, to obtain

\big|\partial\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big|\lesssim(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\langle|x|\rangle^{-1}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1},\qquad|I|\leq N-9.

Finally, we recall that

\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}\big|\lesssim\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{0}\big|+\big|\Gamma^{I}\Delta\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle|x|\rangle^{-1}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1/2},\qquad|I|\leq N-9.

∎

A combination of Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7
gives us the following pointwise decay result of
\widetilde{\phi}
component.

Proposition 4.8
.

We have

\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1/2},\qquad|I|\leq N-9.

(4.12)

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(\Psi,\phi)\in X
, and denote
\big(\widetilde{\Psi},\widetilde{\phi}\big)=T(\Psi,\phi)
, then we have

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\widetilde{\Psi},\widetilde{\phi}\big)\big\|_{X}\lesssim\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{3/2}.

(4.13)

Proof.

Recall the definition of the
X
-norm in (
4.1
), we need to show that each of terms has the desired bound. We act the vector fields
\Gamma^{I},\Gamma^{J},\partial\Gamma^{I}
, with
|I|\leq N+1,|J|\leq N
, to the reformulated equations to get

\displaystyle-\Box\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\Phi}+\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\Phi}=-\Gamma^{I}\big(\phi\Psi\big),

\displaystyle-\Box\Gamma^{J}\widetilde{\phi}^{0}=0,\qquad-\Box\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}=\partial\Gamma^{I}|\Psi|^{2},

\displaystyle\big(\widetilde{\Phi},\partial_{t}\widetilde{\Phi},\widetilde{\phi}^{0},\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}^{0},\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta},\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big)(t_{0})=(E_{0},E_{1},n_{0},n_{1},0,0),

with the relation
\widetilde{\phi}=\widetilde{\phi}^{0}+\Delta\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}
.

Step 1.

First, we show the bound for ghost energy for the
\widetilde{\Psi}
component.
By the energy estimates (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{gst,1}(t,\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\Psi})

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{gst,1}(t_{0},\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\Psi})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\partial_{t}\Gamma^{I}\Psi\Gamma^{I}\big(\phi\Psi\big)\big\|_{L^{1}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\partial_{t}\Gamma^{I}\Psi\big\|\big\|\Gamma^{I}\big(\phi\Psi\big)\big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+C_{1}\epsilon\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\Gamma^{I}\big(\phi\Psi\big)\big\|\,dt^{\prime}.

We proceed to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{gst,1}(t,\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\Psi})

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+C_{1}\epsilon\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\Gamma^{I}\big(\phi\Psi\big)\big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+C_{1}\epsilon\sum_{|I_{1}|+|I_{2}|\leq N+1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\Psi\big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+C_{1}\epsilon\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq N-9,|I_{2}|\leq N+1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\langle t^{\prime}-|x|\rangle^{1/2+\delta}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big\|{\Gamma^{I_{2}}\Psi\over\langle t^{\prime}-|x|\rangle^{1/2+\delta}}\Big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\epsilon\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq N+1,|I_{2}|\leq N-3}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|\big\|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\Psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{3}\Big(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2+2\delta}\,dt^{\prime}\Big)^{1/2}+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{3}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-3/2+\delta}\,dt^{\prime}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{3}.

Step 2.
We now show the pointwise estimates for the
\widetilde{\Psi}
component.

On one hand, for
|I|\leq N+1
we have

\displaystyle\big\|\langle t+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I}(\phi\Psi)\big\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq N-3,|I_{2}|\leq N+1}\big\|\langle t+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I_{1}}\Psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\big\|+\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq N+1,|I_{2}|\leq N-2}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{1}}\Psi\big\|\big\|\langle t+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}.

Then the application of the Sobolev inequality in Proposition
2.5
deduces that

\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\Psi}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-3/2+\delta},\qquad|I|\leq N-3.

With
|I|\leq N-3
, we note that

\displaystyle\big\|\langle t+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I}(\phi\Psi)\big\|\lesssim\sum_{|I_{1}|,|I_{2}|\leq N-3}\big\|\langle t+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I_{1}}\Psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\big\|\lesssim(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\langle t\rangle^{-\delta}.

Thus the Sobolev inequality in Proposition
2.5
implies that

\big|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\Psi}\big|\lesssim\big(\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\big)\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-3/2},\qquad|I|\leq N-7.

Step 3.
We turn to derive the bounds for the
L^{2}
norms of
\widetilde{\phi}
component.

First, for the
\widetilde{\phi}^{0}
part, we easily get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t,\Gamma^{J}\widetilde{\phi}^{0})^{1/2}+\mathcal{E}_{con}(t,\Gamma^{J}\widetilde{\phi}^{0})^{1/2}\leq\mathcal{E}(t_{0},\Gamma^{J}\widetilde{\phi}^{0})^{1/2}+\mathcal{E}_{con}(t_{0},\Gamma^{J}\widetilde{\phi}^{0})^{1/2}\lesssim\epsilon,\qquad|J|\leq N,

which gives

\big\|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{0}\big\|\lesssim\epsilon,\qquad|I|\leq N+1.

Next, we treat the
\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}
part, and the energy estimates imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t,\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}(t_{0},\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta})^{1/2}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\|\partial\Gamma^{I}|\Psi|^{2}\big\|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-3/2}\,dt^{\prime}\lesssim\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2},\qquad|I|\leq N+1,

which means

\big\|\partial\partial\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}^{\Delta}\big\|\lesssim\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2},\qquad|I|\leq N+1.

Gathering the above estimates yields

\big\|\Gamma^{I}\widetilde{\phi}\big\|\lesssim\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{2},\qquad|I|\leq N+1.

Step 4.

Finally, by recalling the estimates in Proposition
4.8
, we find

\big\|\big(\widetilde{\Psi},\widetilde{\phi}\big)\big\|_{X}\lesssim\epsilon+(C_{1}\epsilon)^{3/2}.

Thus we finish the proof.
∎

In the same way as the proofs for Proposition
4.8
and Proposition
4.9
, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.10
.

For two elements
(\Psi,\phi),(\Psi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\in X
, we denote
\big(\widetilde{\Psi},\widetilde{\phi}\big)=T(\Psi,\phi),\big(\widetilde{\Psi^{\prime}},\widetilde{\phi^{\prime}}\big)=T(\Psi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
, then we have

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\widetilde{\Psi}-\widetilde{\Psi^{\prime}},\widetilde{\phi}-\widetilde{\phi^{\prime}}\big)\big\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim(C_{1}\epsilon)^{1/2}\big\|(\Psi-\Psi^{\prime},\phi-\phi^{\prime})\big\|_{X}.

(4.14)

Thus we are now able to provide the proof for Proposition
4.3
.

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

We choose
C_{1}\gg 1
very large, and
\epsilon\ll 1
sufficiently small, so that the estimates in Proposition
4.9
and Proposition
4.10
lead us to

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\widetilde{\Psi},\widetilde{\phi}\big)\big\|_{X}\leq{1\over 2}C_{1}\epsilon,

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\widetilde{\Psi}-\widetilde{\Psi^{\prime}},\widetilde{\phi}-\widetilde{\phi^{\prime}}\big)\big\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq{1\over 2}\big\|(\Psi-\Psi^{\prime},\phi-\phi^{\prime})\big\|_{X}.

The proof is done.
∎

Finally, we provide the proof for the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the fixed point theorem, we know there exists a unique element
(E,n)\in X
, such that

(E,n)=T(E,n).

This means that
(E,n)
is the solution to the original Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations in (
1.1
). According to the definition of the solution space
X
, we know that the sharp pointwise decay results (
1.4
) are valid, and the energy for
(E,n)
is uniformly bounded.

∎

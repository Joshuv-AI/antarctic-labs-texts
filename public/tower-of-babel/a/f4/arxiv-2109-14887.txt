A local-global principle for torsors under geometric prosolvable fundamental groups II
Mohamed Saïdi

Abstract
We prove a local-global principle for torsors under the prosolvable geometric fundamental group of an affine curve over a number field .



§0. Introduction

Let
k
be a characteristic
0
field and
X\to\operatorname{Spec}k
a separated, smooth, and geometrically connected

curve
over
k
. Let
\eta
be a geometric point of
X
with values in its generic point.
Thus,
\eta
determines an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of
k
, and a geometric point
\overline{\eta}

of
X_{\bar{k}}\overset\text{def}\to{=}X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}\bar{k}
.
There exists a canonical exact sequence of profinite groups (cf. [Grothendieck], Exposé IX, Théorème 6.1)

1\to\pi_{1}(X_{\bar{k}},\overline{\eta})\to\pi_{1}(X,\eta)@>>>G_{k}\to 1.

Here,
\pi_{1}(X,\eta)
denotes the arithmetic étale fundamental group of
X
with base
point
\eta
,
\pi_{1}(X_{\bar{k}},\overline{\eta})
denotes the étale fundamental group of
X_{\bar{k}}
with base
point
\overline{\eta}
, and
G_{k}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
denotes the absolute Galois group of
k
.
Write

\Delta\overset\text{def}\to{=}\pi_{1}(X_{\bar{k}},\overline{\eta})\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \Pi\overset\text{def}\to{=}\pi_{1}(X,\eta).

We have the above natural exact sequence

1\to\Delta\to\Pi\to G_{k}\to 1.

[formula]

Suppose that the sequence
(0.1)
splits
[for example assume that
X(k)\neq\emptyset
]. Let

s:G_{k}\to\Pi

be a section of the projection
\Pi\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
. We view
\Delta
as a
G_{k}
-group
via the conjugation action of
s(G_{k})
.

Assume that
k
is a
number field
; i.e.,
k
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
v
be a prime of
k
,
k_{v}
the completion of
k
at
v
, and
G_{k_{v}}\subset G_{k}
a decomposition group associated to
v
.
Thus,
G_{k_{v}}
is only defined up to conjugation. We view
\Delta
as a
G_{k_{v}}
-group
via the conjugation action of
s(G_{k_{v}})
. For each prime
v
of
k
we have a natural restriction map (of pointed non-abelian cohomology sets)

\operatorname{Res}_{v}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta)\to H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta),

and a natural map

\prod_{\operatorname{all}v}\operatorname{Res}_{v}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta)\to\prod_{\operatorname{all}v}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta),

where the product is over all primes
v
of
k
. The main problem we are concerned with in this paper is the following.

Question A

Is the map

\prod_{\operatorname{all}v}\operatorname{Res}_{v}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta)\to\prod_{\operatorname{all}v}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta)

injective
?

As explained in [Saïdi]
\lx@sectionsign 0
, the above question is related to the Grothendieck anabelian section conjecture.
Let
\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}}
be the maximal
prosolvable
quotient of
\Delta
, which is a characteristic quotient.
The above
G_{k}
(resp.
G_{k_{v}}
)-group structure on
\Delta
induces naturally a
G_{k}
(resp.
G_{k_{v}}
)-group structure on

\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}}
. Let
\mathfrak{Primes}_{k}
be the set of primes of
k
and
S\subseteq\mathfrak{Primes}_{k}
a non-empty subset, we have as above a natural restriction map

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{\operatorname{sol}}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}})\to\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}}).

In [Saïdi] we proved the following result.

Theorem A

Assume
k
is a
number field
,
X
is
proper
,
and
S\subseteq\mathfrak{Primes}_{k}
is a set of primes of
k
of
density
1
.
Then the map

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{\operatorname{sol}}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}})\to\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}})

is
injective
.

In this note we generalise Theorem A by removing the assumption therein that
X
is proper. Our main result is the following.

Theorem B

Assume
k
is a
number field
,
X
is
affine
, and
S\subseteq\mathfrak{Primes}_{k}
is a set of primes of
k
of
density
1
.
Then the map

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{\operatorname{sol}}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}})\to\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}})

is
injective
.

Our proof of Theorem B relies on a devissage argument, and a careful analysis of the structure of the geometric prosolvable
(resp. geometrically prosolvable arithmetic) (tame) fundamental group of an affine curve which is established in
\lx@sectionsign 1
(resp.
\lx@sectionsign 2
). The results established in
\lx@sectionsign 1
and
\lx@sectionsign 2

may be of interest independently of the question discussed in this paper.
The abelian analog of Theorem B is a consequence of results of Serre (cf. proof of Proposition 3.2). Theorem B is proved in
\lx@sectionsign 3
.

Let

be an
algebraically closed

V\to\operatorname{Spec}\ell
a separated, smooth, and connected

\ell
-
curve
,
Z
the smooth compactification of
V
, and
Z\setminus V
the complement of
V
in
Z
which is either empty
if
Z=V
or otherwise
V
is affine and
Z\setminus V
consists of finitely many closed points of
Z
.
Let
r\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Card}(Z\setminus V)
be the cardinality of
Z\setminus V
, thus
r=0
if
Z=V
.
Let
\xi
be a geometric point of
V
with values in its generic point, and

\Delta\overset\text{def}\to{=}\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{tame}}(V,\xi)

the
tame fundamental group
of
V
with base point
\xi
.
The geometric point
\xi
determines a separable closure

of the function field

of

.
Thus,
\Delta\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{tame}}/K)
where

is the maximal sub-extension of
K^{\operatorname{sep}}/K
which is
étale above
V
and tamely ramified over the (discrete) valuations of

corresponding to closed points of
Z\setminus V
.

Consider the
derived series
of
\Delta

.....\subseteq\Delta(i+1)\subseteq\Delta(i)\subseteq......\subseteq\Delta(1)\subseteq\Delta(0)=\Delta

[formula]

where

\Delta(i+1)=\overline{[\Delta(i),\Delta(i)]},

for
i\geq 0
, is the
i+1
-th derived subgroup which is a characteristic subgroup of
\Delta
. Write

\Delta_{i}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\Delta/\Delta(i).

Thus,
\Delta_{i}
is the
i
-th step solvable
quotient of
\Delta
, and
\Delta_{1}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\Delta^{\operatorname{ab}}
is the maximal
abelian
quotient of
\Delta
.
Note that there exists a natural exact sequence

1\to\Delta^{i+1}\to\Delta_{i+1}\to\Delta_{i}\to 1

[formula]

where
\Delta^{i+1}
is the subgroup
\Delta(i)/\Delta(i+1)
of
\Delta_{i+1}
. In particular,
\Delta^{i+1}
is
abelian
.

Lemma 1.1

We have a natural identification
\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{i\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}\ \Delta_{i}
.
In particular,
\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta\twoheadrightarrow\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}})=\bigcap_{i\geq 1}\Delta(i)
,
and
\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}}
is a characteristic quotient of
\Delta
.

Proof

Follows from the various definitions.
∎

Let
i\geq 1
be an integer. The profinite group
\Delta_{i}
is finitely generated as follows from the well-known
finite generation of
\Delta
which projects onto
\Delta_{i}
(cf. [Grothendieck], Exposé XIII, Corollaire 2.12).
Let
\{\widehat{\Delta}_{i}[n]\}_{n\geq 1}
be a countable system of
characteristic open
subgroups of
\Delta_{i}
such that

\widehat{\Delta}_{i}[n+1]\subseteq\widehat{\Delta}_{i}[n],\ \ \ \ \widehat{\Delta}_{i}[1]\overset\text{def}\to{=}\Delta_{i},\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \bigcap_{n\geq 1}\widehat{\Delta}_{i}[n]=\{1\}.

Write
{\Delta_{i}}[n]
for the inverse image of
\widehat{\Delta}_{i}[n]
in
\Delta
via the map
\Delta\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_{i}
. Thus,
\Delta_{i}[n]\subseteq\Delta
is an open subgroup corresponding to an étale Galois cover
V_{i,n}\to V
which extends to a tamely ramified Galois cover
Z_{i,n}\to Z
between proper, smooth, and connected

\ell
-curves (the cover
Z_{i,n}\to Z
is étale if
r=0
, i.e., if
V=Z
, or if
i=r=1
). Note that since the étale cover
V_{i,n}\to V

is defined via an open subgroup of
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{tame}}(V,\xi)
it is a pointed étale cover, and
V_{i,n}

(hence also
Z_{i,n}
) is naturally endowed with a geometric point
\xi_{i,n}
above
\xi
.

If
i=r=1
, let

\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]\overset\text{def}\to{=}{\Delta}_{i}[n]=\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{tame}}(V_{i,n},\xi_{i,n})

be the tame fundamental group of
V_{i,n}
with base point
\xi_{i,n}
.

If
(i,r)\neq(1,1)
, let

\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]\overset\text{def}\to{=}\pi_{1}(Z_{i,n},\xi_{i,n})

be the étale fundamental group of

Z_{i,n}
with geometric point
\xi_{i,n}
, which is a quotient of
{\Delta_{i}}[n]=\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{tame}}(V_{i,n},\xi_{i,n})
.

Let
i\geq 1
. We have,
\forall n\geq 1
, finite morphisms
V_{i,n+1}\to V_{i,n}
, and

Z_{i,n+1}\to Z_{i,n}
, which induce continuous homomorphisms

\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n+1]\to\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]

and

\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n+1]^{\operatorname{ab}}\to\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}}
. Thus, we have a projective system
\{\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}}\}_{n\geq 1}
.

Our main result in this section is the following description of the structure of
\Delta^{i+1}
; this description in the case

(i,r)\neq(1,1)
is in terms of the Tate modules
of the jacobians of the
\{Z_{i,n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. Proposition 1.2
plays a key role in the proof of Theorem B and may be of interest independently of the question discussed in this paper.

Proposition 1.2

Assume
i\geq 1
. We have a natural identification

\Delta^{i+1}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{n\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}\ \widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}}.

Proof

If
r=0
, i.e.,
V=Z
is proper, or if
i=r=1
, the assertion follows easily from the various Definitions. Observe that in the case
i=r=1
the natural projection

\Delta_{1}=\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{tame}}(V,\xi)^{\operatorname{ab}}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(Z,\xi)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is an isomorphism.

Next, we assume
V=Z\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
is affine and
V
is the complement in
Z

of a finite set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
of closed points with
\max(i,r)>1
.
Let
G
be a finite quotient of
\Delta_{i+1}
which inserts in
the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>\Delta^{i+1}@>{}>{}>\Delta_{i+1}@>{}>{}>\Delta_{i}@>{}>{}>1\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
1@>{}>{}>A@>{}>{}>G@>{}>{}>G^{\prime}@>{}>{}>1\\
\end{CD}

where the vertical maps are surjective.
The quotient
G
corresponds to a finite étale Galois cover
V_{1}\to V
which extends to a tamely ramified
finite Galois cover
Z_{1}\to Z
with Galois group
G
. The cover
Z_{1}\to Z
factorizes as

Z_{1}\to Z_{1}^{\prime}\to Z
, where
Z_{1}^{\prime}\to Z
is the sub-cover with Galois group
G^{\prime}
, and
Z_{1}\to Z_{1}^{\prime}
is a Galois abelian cover with group
A
.
For
s\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, let
I_{x_{s}}\subset G
be an inertia subgroup associated to
x_{s}
. Thus,
I_{x_{s}}
is only defined up to conjugation.
Moreover,
I_{x_{s}}
is cyclic of order
e_{s}\geq 1
with
\text{gcd}(e_{s},p)=1
as the ramification is tame.
The following claim follows immediately from the well-known structure of
\Delta
(cf. [Grothendieck], Exposé XIII, Corollaire 2.12).

Claim 1.2.2

There exists a finite quotient
\Delta_{i}\twoheadrightarrow P
of
\Delta_{i}

corresponding to a finite tamely ramified Galois cover
Z_{2}\to Z
which is étale above
V

such that the followings hold. For
s\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, if
I^{\prime}_{x_{s}}\subseteq P
is an inertia subgroup associated to
x_{s}
,
then
I^{\prime}_{x_{s}}
is
cyclic
of order
f_{s}=e_{s}h_{s}
a multiple of
e_{s}
with
\text{gcd}(f_{s},p)=1
.

Note that Claim 1.2.2, which holds under the assumption
\max(i,r)>1
, is false if
i=r=1
. Indeed, a finite abelian tamely ramified cover
C^{\prime}\to C

between proper and connected smooth
\ell
-curves which is étale above
C\setminus\{x\}
, where
x\in C
is a closed point, is necessarily étale above
C
.

Next, let
K_{1}\overset\text{def}\to{=}K_{Z_{1}}
(resp.
K_{2}\overset\text{def}\to{=}K_{Z_{2}}
) be the function field of
Z_{1}
(resp.
Z_{2}
),

L\overset\text{def}\to{=}K_{1}.K_{2}
the compositum of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
in
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
, and
\widetilde{Z}
the normalisation of
Z
in
L
. Thus,
\widetilde{Z}\to Z
is a
tamely ramified Galois cover with Galois group
H\subseteq G\times P
which is étale above
V
.

Lemma 1.2.3

The quotient
\Delta\twoheadrightarrow H
factorizes as
\Delta\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_{i+1}\twoheadrightarrow H
.

Proof of Lemma 1.2.3

This follows from the facts that
\widetilde{Z}\to Z
is étale above
V
,
tamely ramified,
H\subseteq G\times P
, and
G\times P
is
(i+1)
-th step solvable.
∎

Let
H^{\prime}
be the image of
H
in
G^{\prime}\times P
. We have a commutative diagram of exact sequences where the vertical maps are natural inclusions.

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>A_{H}\overset\text{def}\to{=}H\cap\lgroup A\times\{1\}\rgroup @>{}>{}>H@>{}>{}>H^{\prime}@>{}>{}>1\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
1@>{}>{}>A\times\{1\}@>{}>{}>G\times P@>{}>{}>G^{\prime}\times P@>{}>{}>1\\
\end{CD}

Lemma 1.2.4

The group
H^{\prime}
is a quotient of
\Delta_{i}
. Moreover, the cover
\widetilde{Z}\to Z
factorizes as
\widetilde{Z}\to\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}\to Z

where
\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}\to Z
is étale above
V
, tamely ramified, and Galois with Galois group
H^{\prime}
, and
\widetilde{Z}\to\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}
is an
abelian étale
cover with Galois group
A_{H}
.

Proof of Lemma 1.2.4

The first assertion, as well as the assertion concerning the cover
\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}\to Z
, follow from the various definitions.
We prove the last assertion regarding the cover
\widetilde{Z}\to\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}
. Recall the factorisation

Z_{1}\to Z_{1}^{\prime}\to Z
where
Z_{1}\to Z_{1}^{\prime}
is Galois with abelian Galois group
A
and

Z_{1}^{\prime}\to Z
is Galois with group
G^{\prime}
. Let
\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}
be the normalisation of
Z
in the compositum of the function fields of
Z_{1}^{\prime}
and
Z_{2}
(in
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
).
Thus,
\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}\to Z
is a Galois cover with Galois group
H^{\prime}
and
we have the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\widetilde{Z}@>{}>{}>Z_{1}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}@>{}>{}>Z_{1}^{\prime}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
Z_{2}@>{}>{}>Z\\
\end{CD}

of finite Galois covers.
The ramification index in the Galois cover
Z_{2}\to Z

above a branched (closed) point
x_{s}\in Z
is divisible by the ramification index above
x_{s}
in the Galois cover
Z_{1}\to Z
(cf. the condition in Claim 1.2.2
that

is divisible by

). The fact that the cover
\widetilde{Z}\to Z_{2}
, and a fortiori
\widetilde{Z}\to\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}
which is abelian with Galois group

, is étale follows from Abhyankar’s Lemma (cf. [Grothendieck], Exposé X, Lemma 3.6).
∎

Going back to the proof of Proposition 1.2, the above discussion shows that the finite quotients
\Delta_{i+1}\twoheadrightarrow H
as in Lemma 1.2.3 form a cofinal system
of finite quotients of
\Delta_{i+1}
. Thus,

\Delta_{i+1}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{H}\to{\varprojlim}\ H
. Proposition 1.2 follows from the facts that the various
H
above fit in an exact sequence

1\to A_{H}\to H\to H^{\prime}\to 1
,
\Delta_{i}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{H^{\prime}}\to{\varprojlim}\ H^{\prime}
, and the above Galois covers
\widetilde{Z}\to\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}

with group

are étale abelian (cf. Lemma 1.2.4).

This finishes the proof of Proposition 1.2.
∎

Lemma 2.1

We have a natural identification
\Pi^{(\operatorname{sol})}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{i\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}\ \Pi_{i}

Follows from Lemma 1.1 and the various definitions.

If
v\in\mathfrak{Primes}_{k}
is a prime of
k
of residue characteristic
\neq p
, and
\text{Fr}_{v}\in G
is a Frobenius at
v
, then its eigenvalues on
I_{X}(p)
have complex absolute value
Nv
;
where
Nv
is the cardinality of the residue field
\kappa(v)
at
v
, and thus satisfy the condition in Lemme 2 of [Serre2].
Hence the Lie algebra
G
of
G
satisfies the hypothesis of Théorème 1 in [Serre2] and we have
H^{1}(G,I_{X}(p)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p})=0
. This implies that

H^{1}(G,I_{X}(p))
is finite. Indeed,
H^{1}(G,I_{X}(p))
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module and
H^{1}(G,I_{X}(p))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
injects in

H^{1}(G,I_{X}(p)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p})=0
(cf. [Serre2] Corollaire to Lemme 3). Moreover, since
(I_{X}(p)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p})^{\text{Fr}_{v}}=0
we have

H^{1}_{*}(G,I_{X}(p))=0
by [Serre1] Théorème 1. Now
\text{Kernel}[H^{1}(G_{k},I_{X}(p))\to\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},I_{X}(p))]
is contained in

H^{1}_{*}(G,I_{X}(p))
(cf. Propositions 7 and 8 in [Serre1]), thus is trivial as required. Note that in [Serre1] and [Serre2] the set
S
is the complement of a finite set
of primes but the same proof works in the case where
S
has density
1
(see also [Jannsen], the proof of Theorem 3, and the references therein).
∎

Proof of Proposition 3.1

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition 2.1 in [Saïdi] using Proposition 3.2 and Proposition 2.2.
Fix an integer
i\geq 1
. Consider the following commutative diagram of maps of pointed cohomology sets

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{i+1})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i+1})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}}V{}V@V{{\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i}}}V{}V\\
1@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{i+1})@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta_{i+1})@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta_{i})\\
\end{CD}

where the horizontal sequences are exact (cf. Lemma 2.5) and the vertical maps are the natural restriction maps.
We assume by induction hypothesis that the right vertical map

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i})\to\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta_{i})

is
injective
. We will show that the middle vertical map

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}:H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i+1})\to\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta_{i+1})

is
injective
.
Let

[\rho],[\tau]\in H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i+1})

be two cohomology classes such that

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}([\tau])=\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}([\rho]).

Write
s_{j}:G_{k}\to\Pi_{j}
for the section of the projection
\Pi_{j}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
induced by the section
s:G_{k}\to\Pi
, for
j\geq 1
.
We will show
[\tau]=[\rho]
. We can (without loss of generality), and will, assume that
[\tau]=[s_{i+1}]=1
is the distinguished element of
H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i+1})
.
The classes
[\rho]
and
[\tau]
map to the classes
[\rho_{1}]
and
[\tau_{1}]=[s_{i}]=1
in
H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i})
, respectively.
In particular, we have the equality

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i}([\tau_{1}])=\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i}([\rho_{1}]),

hence
[\tau_{1}]=[\rho_{1}]=1
since
\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i}
is injective by the induction hypothesis.
Thus, there exist classes
[\tilde{\tau}]=1
and
[\tilde{\rho}]
in
H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{i+1})
which map to
[\tau]
and
[\rho]
, respectively
in
H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i+1})
(cf. exactness of horizontal sequences in the above diagram).

Next, and in order to show that
[\rho]=[\tau]
in
H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta_{i+1})

it suffices to show
[\tilde{\rho}]=[\tilde{\tau}]
in
H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{i+1})
.
Note that the assumption
\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}([\tau])=\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}([\rho])

implies that
\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}([\tilde{\tau}])=\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}([\tilde{\rho}])
in
H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{i+1})
, for each place
v\in S
,
as follows from the injectivity of the maps
H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{i+1})\to H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta_{i+1})

(cf. Lemma 2.5).
We have a commutative diagram of group homomorphisms

\begin{CD}H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{i+1})@>{}>{}>\underset{n\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}H^{1}(G_{k},\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}})\\
@V{\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{i+1}}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{i+1})@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in S}\lgroup\underset{n\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}})\rgroup\\
\end{CD}

where the horizontal maps are induced by the identification

H_{i}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{n\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}\ \widetilde{\Pi}_{i}[n]^{(\operatorname{ab})}
(cf. Proposition 2.2) and the vertical maps are restriction maps.
The identification
H_{i}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{n\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}\ \widetilde{\Pi}_{i}[n]^{(\operatorname{ab})}

induces isomorphisms

H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{i+1})\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{n\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}H^{1}(G_{k},\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}})
,
and
H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{i+1})\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\underset{n\geq 1}\to{\varprojlim}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}})

for each
v\in S
, where the transition homomorphisms in the projective limit are induced by the natural

G_{k}
(resp.
G_{k_{v}}
)-homomorphisms
\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n+1]^{\operatorname{ab}}\to\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}}
(cf. [Neukirch-Schmidt-Wingberg], Chapter II, (2.3.5)Corollary).
The right vertical map in the above diagram is injective in the case
(i,r)\neq(1,1)
by Proposition 2.2 in [Saïdi]
[recall in this case that the
G_{k}
-module
\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}}
is isomorphic to the Tate module of the jacobian of the curve
Y_{i,n}
defined in
\lx@sectionsign 2
].
In case
(i,r)=(1,1)
the injectivity of the right vertical map in the above diagram follows from Proposition 3.2 [recall; with the notations in
\lx@sectionsign 2
, that in this case the
G_{k}
-module

\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}[n]^{\operatorname{ab}}
is
\pi_{1}((X_{i,n})_{\bar{k}},\overline{\eta}_{i,n})^{\operatorname{ab}}
].

Thus, the left vertical map

H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{i+1})\to\prod_{v\in S}H^{1}(G_{k_{v}},\Delta^{i+1})

is
injective
. In particular,
[\tilde{\rho}]=[\tilde{\tau}]
and
[\rho]=[\tau]
.
This finishes the proof of Proposition 3.1.
∎

Finally, going back to the proof of Theorem B, let

[\alpha],[\beta]\in H^{1}(G_{k},\Delta^{\operatorname{sol}})

be two cohomology classes such that

\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{\operatorname{sol}}([\alpha])=\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Res}_{v}^{\operatorname{sol}}([\beta]).

One shows
[\alpha]=[\beta]
by similar arguments to the ones used to conclude the proof of Theorem B in [Saïdi] (cf. loc. cit. discussion after the proof of Lemma 2.1),
using Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 2.3 in loc. cit..

This finishes the Proof of Theorem B.
∎

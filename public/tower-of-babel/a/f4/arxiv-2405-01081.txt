Muckenhoupt-type Weights and Quantitative Weighted Estimates in the Bessel Setting
Ji Li, Chong-Wei Liang, Chun-Yen Shen, Brett D. Wick

Abstract




Abstract:

Part of the intrinsic structure of singular integrals in the Bessel setting is captured by Muckenhoupt-type weights. Anderson–Kerman showed that the Bessel Riesz transform is bounded on weighted
L^{p}_{w}
if and only if
w
is in the class
A_{p,\lambda}
. We introduce a new class of Muckenhoupt-type weights
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
in the Bessel setting, which is different from
A_{p,\lambda}
but characterizes the weighted boundedness for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operators. We also establish the weighted
L^{p}
boundedness and compactness, as well as the endpoint weak type boundedness of Riesz commutators. The quantitative weighted bound is also established.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background

In this paper, we develop new Muckenhoupt-type weights and establish the weighted boundedness characterization in the Bessel setting,
along the line of Muckenhoupt and Stein
[
MS65
]
, Anderson–Kerman
[
AK81
]
, and our recent result
[
Li+24
]
.

Consider the Bessel operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
defined by Muckenhoupt and Stein in
[
MS65
]

{\triangle_{\lambda}}:=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda}{x}\frac{d}{dx},\quad\lambda>0

with measure
d\mu(x):=x^{2\lambda}\,dx
and the Bessel Riesz transform
R_{\lambda}:=\frac{d}{dx}(\triangle_{\lambda})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

As noted in
[
Li+24
]
, if we just simply treat
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,d\mu)
as a space of homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
CW77
]
, then it does not fully reflect the intrinsic structure in the Bessel setting.
For example, Anderson and Kerman
[
AK81
]
first introduced a Muckenhoupt-type weights
A_{p,\lambda}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure (but not
dm_{\lambda}
), i.e., for
w\in A_{p,\lambda}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wdx)}:=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\bigg)^{1\over p},

(1.1)

and proved:
“
R_{\lambda}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
(in the sense of (
1.1
))
if and only if

w
is in
A_{p,\lambda}
”.

In
[
Li+24
]
we studied the natural question: does
A_{p,\lambda}
also characterize the weighted boundedness of Hardy–Littlewood maximal function in the Bessel setting
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,d\mu)
? The answer is no! The Hardy–Littlewood maximal function is linked to another Muckenhoupt-type weight introduced in
[
Li+24
]
:
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
(given in Definition
1.2
).

The aim of this paper is to study the weighted estimates with respect to
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
, including

1. quantitative weighted norm for
R_{\lambda}
;

2. the weighted
L^{p}
boundedness, compactness of the commutator
[b,R_{\lambda}]
in terms of BMO, and VMO spaces, respectively;

3. the weighted endpoint estimate for
[b,R_{\lambda}]
with respect to
\widetilde{A}_{1,\lambda}
.

Before stating our main results, it is natural to ask, is there any difference between these weighted estimates and those obtained before in the classical
A_{p}(\mu)
setting on
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,d\mu)
?

Recall that the weighted
L^{p}
for the classical
A_{p}(\mu)
setting on
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,d\mu)
is given by

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wd\mu)}:=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)\bigg)^{1\over p}.

We first point out that
A_{p}(\mu)
and
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
are different. As classes of weights, they have a non-trivial intersection, but are distinct in that one does not contain the other, which is reflected by the indices of the power weight.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
w(t):=t^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then we have

(1)
w\in A_{p}(\mu)\Longleftrightarrow-1-2\lambda<\alpha<p-1+2\lambda(p-1)
,

(2)
w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow-1<\alpha<p-1+(2\lambda+1)p.

1.2
Statement of main results

We first recall the definition of new Muckenhoupt weight class
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
in
[
Li+24
]
(throughout this paper, we denote

d\nu_{\lambda}(x):=x^{2\lambda+1}dx
).

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
. For a non-negative measurable function
w
,
w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}w(t)dt\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\int_{B}t^{(2\lambda+1)p^{\prime}}w(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\bigg)^{p-1}<C.

For
p=1
,

w\in\widetilde{A}_{1,\lambda}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\frac{w(B)}{\nu_{\lambda}(B)}\leq C\frac{w(x)}{\nu_{\lambda}(x)},

for almost all
x\in B
. For
p=\infty
, we define the borderline class
\widetilde{A}_{\infty,\lambda}
to be

\widetilde{A}_{\infty,\lambda}:=\underset{p\geq 1}{\bigcup}\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}.

As noted in
[
Li+24
]
,
\widetilde{A}_{1,\lambda}
is different from
A_{1,\lambda}
.
A_{1,\lambda}
is not the limiting class of
A_{p,\lambda}
(as showed in
[
AK81
]
), while
\widetilde{A}_{1,\lambda}
is the limiting class of
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}
.

Definition 1.5

(Sparse Operator associated to the BMO function)

Let

The second main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then for

\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},b}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wdx)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wdx)},\ \ \|\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathcal{S},b}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wdx)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wdx)}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}[w]^{2\max\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\}}_{\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}}.

(Weighted

Theorem 1.11
.

Let

2.2
A System of Dyadic Cubes

In the space of homogeneous type
(X,d,\mu)
, a
countable family

\mathcal{D}:=\underset{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\bigcup}{D}_{k},\ \mathcal{D}:=\{Q^{k}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{k}\},

•

X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{k}}Q^{k}_{\alpha}\quad\text{(disjoint union) for all}~k\in\mathbb{Z}
;

•

\text{if }\ell\geq k\text{, then either }Q^{\ell}_{\beta}\subseteq Q^{k}_{\alpha}\text{ or }Q^{k}_{\alpha}\cap Q^{\ell}_{\beta}=\emptyset
;

•

\text{for each }(k,\alpha)\text{ and each }\ell\leq k,\text{ there exists a unique }\beta\text{ such that }Q^{k}_{\alpha}\subseteq Q^{\ell}_{\beta}

•

for each
(k,\alpha)

•

\text{if }\ell\geq k\text{ and }Q^{\ell}_{\beta}\subseteq Q^{k}_{\alpha}\text{, then }B(Q^{\ell}_{\beta})\subseteq B(Q^{k}_{\alpha})

3
Relation: Proof of Proposition
1.1

Recall that for
w(t):=t^{\alpha}
, we have

w\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda}\Longleftrightarrow-1<\alpha<p-1+(2\lambda+1)p.

The class that we focus on is
\widetilde{A}_{p,\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}
, and hence
\alpha
must satisfy

-1<\alpha<p-1+2\lambda p.

Now, we investigate the range of
\alpha
such that
w\in A_{p}(\mu)
. Given a ball
B=B_{R}(x_{0})
, where
x_{0}
is the center and
R
is the radius, we consider the following two cases:

•

Case (1)
:
|x_{0}|\geq 3R
.

In the first case, for those
|x-x_{0}|<R
, we have
|x|\sim|x_{0}|
, hence

\sup_{B}\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}d\mu\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|x|^{{-\alpha}\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}d\mu\bigg)^{p-1}\sim|x_{0}|^{\alpha}|x_{0}|^{-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}(p-1)}\sim 1.

•

Case (2)
:
|x_{0}|<3R
.

In the second case, one has

B_{R}(x_{0})\subset B_{4R}(0)\subset B_{7R}(x_{0})

and by the doubling property of
\mu
, we may replace
B_{R}(x_{0})
by
B_{4R}(0)
and hence compute the following

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{R^{2\lambda+1}}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{\alpha}d\mu\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{R^{2\lambda+1}}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}d\mu\bigg)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{R^{2\lambda+1}}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{\alpha+2\lambda}dx\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{R^{2\lambda+1}}\int_{-R}^{R}x^{-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+2\lambda}dx\bigg)^{p-1}.

If we want the above term to be finite uniformly in
R
, we only need to check both terms are integrable near
0
. Hence this gives the following conditions for
\alpha
:

\alpha+2\lambda>-1,\quad-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+2\lambda>-1,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle-1-2\lambda<\alpha<p-1+2\lambda(p-1).

The proof is complete.

Define

\sigma_{*}(t):=t^{2\lambda p^{\prime}}w(t)^{1-p^{\prime}}

\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wdx)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}wdx)}=\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot\sigma)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\sigma dx)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},wdx)}.

•

Case
(1)
:
p\geq 2

For all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\sigma dx)

A
to be with

A^{-1}(x):=\frac{C^{-1}(x)}{B^{-1}(x)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{w}{\mu}\chi_{F_{K}}\right\|_{L\log L,Q}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\chi_{F_{K}}\right\|_{A,Q}\cdot\left\|\frac{w}{\mu}\right\|_{B,Q}=\frac{2}{A^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(Q)}{\mu(F_{k})}\right)}\cdot\left\|\frac{w}{\mu}\right\|_{B,Q}.

Moreover, by John–Nirenberg Inequality, we have

\mu(F_{k})\leq\mu(Q)\cdot\min\{1,e^{1+\frac{-(\frac{3}{2})^{k}}{c\cdot e}}\}.

Therefore, if we define
\alpha_{k}:=\min\{1,e^{1+\frac{-(\frac{3}{2})^{k}}{c\cdot e}}\}
, then

\displaystyle w(E_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\sum_{Q\in S_{k}}\int_{E_{Q}}|f|d\mu\cdot\frac{2}{A^{-1}(\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}})}\cdot\left\|\frac{w}{\mu}\right\|_{B,Q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{2}{A^{-1}(\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}})}\int|f|\cdot M_{\Phi\circ\phi}\left(\frac{w}{\mu}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\phi}\cdot\int|f|\cdot M_{\Phi\circ\phi}\left(\frac{w}{\mu}\right)d\mu.

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 8.6
.

Let
b\in\rm BMO_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\phi
be a Young function that satisfies

C_{\phi}:=\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{\phi^{-1}(t)}{t^{2}\log(e+t)}dt<\infty.

Then for all
t>0
,

w(\{|[b,R_{\lambda}]f|>t\})\lesssim C_{\phi}\cdot\int\Phi\bigg(\|b\|_{\rm BMO_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\cdot\frac{|f(x)|}{t}\bigg)M_{\Phi\circ\phi}\left(\frac{w}{\mu}\right)(x)d\mu(x),

where
\Phi(x):=x\log(e+x).

Proof.

By Lemma
8.2
, for all
t>0
,

w(\{|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},L\log L}(f)|>t\})\lesssim K_{\phi}\int\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{t}\bigg)M_{\phi}\left(\frac{w}{\mu}\right)(x)d\mu(x).

Hence, by lemma
8.4
, we have

w(\{|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},L\log L}(f)|>t\})\lesssim C_{\phi}\int\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{t}\bigg)M_{\Phi\circ\phi}\left(\frac{w}{\mu}\right)(x)d\mu(x),

therefore combining all the lemmas we obtain that

\displaystyle w(\{|[b,R_{\lambda}]f|>t\})

\displaystyle\leq w(\{|\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathcal{S},b}(f)|>\frac{t}{2}\})+w(\{|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},b}(f)|>\frac{t}{2}\})

\displaystyle\lesssim w(\{|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},L\log L}(f)|>\frac{t}{2\|b\|_{\rm BMO_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}}\})+C_{\phi}\frac{\|b\|_{\rm BMO_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}}{t}\int|f(x)|\cdot M_{\Phi\circ\phi}\left(\frac{w}{\mu}\right)(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\phi}\cdot\int\Phi\bigg(\|b\|_{\rm BMO_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\cdot\frac{|f(x)|}{t}\bigg)M_{\Phi\circ\phi}\left(\frac{w}{\mu}\right)(x)d\mu(x).

The proof is complete.
∎

1+(1+\varepsilon)\alpha>1

Suppose that

\forall t>0,\quad w(\{|[b,R_{\lambda}]f|>t\})\underset{w,\Phi}{\lesssim}\int\Phi\bigg(\hat{A}\frac{|f(x)|}{t}\bigg)w(x)dx.

We will show that

\exists A>0\quad s.t.\quad\forall B\implies w(\{x\in B:|b(x)-C_{B}|>A\})\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot w(B).

This will induce

b\in\rm BMO_{0,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w).

Since there exist positive constants
3\leq A_{1}\leq A_{2}
such that for any interval
B:=B(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, there exists another interval
\widetilde{B}:=B(y_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

such that
A_{1}r\leq|x_{0}-y_{0}|\leq A_{2}r
, and for all
(x,y)\in(B\times\widetilde{B})
,
K(x,y)
does not change sign and

|K(x,y)|\underset{A_{1},A_{2}}{\gtrsim}\frac{1}{\mu(\widetilde{B})}.

Let
\alpha_{b}(\widetilde{B})
be a median value of
b
on the ball
\widetilde{B}
with respect to
\mu
. Namely,

\alpha_{b}(\widetilde{B})
is a real number so that the two sets below have measures at least
\frac{1}{2}\mu(\tilde{B})
.

\displaystyle F_{+}\subset\{y\in\tilde{B}:b(y)\geq\alpha_{b}(\tilde{B})\},\quad F_{-}\subset\{y\in\tilde{B}:b(y)\leq\alpha_{b}(\tilde{B})\}.

Next, we define

E_{+}=\{x\in B:b(x)\geq\alpha_{b}(\tilde{B})\}
and
E_{-}=\{x\in B:b(x)<\alpha_{b}(\tilde{B})\}.
Then
B=E_{+}\cup E_{-}
and
E_{+}\cap E_{-}=\emptyset
.
And it is clear that

\begin{split}b(x)-b(y)&\geq 0,\quad(x,y)\in E_{+}\times F_{-},\\
b(x)-b(y)&<0,\quad(x,y)\in E_{-}\times F_{+}.\end{split}

Let
B
be an interval and take
C_{B}=\alpha_{b}(\widetilde{B})
, then

\displaystyle|b(x)-\alpha_{b}(\widetilde{B})|\chi_{B}(x)

\displaystyle=(b(x)-\alpha_{b}(\widetilde{B}))\chi_{E_{+}}(x)+(\alpha_{b}(\widetilde{B})-b(x))\chi_{E_{-}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq(b(x)-b(y))\chi_{E_{+}}(x)+(b(z)-b(x))\chi_{E_{-}}(x),

for all
y\in F_{-}
and
z\in F_{+},

and hence

\displaystyle|b(x)-\alpha_{b}(\widetilde{B})|\chi_{B}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(F_{-})}\int_{F_{-}}(b(x)-b(y))\chi_{E_{+}}(x)d\mu(y)+\frac{1}{\mu(F_{+})}\int_{F_{+}}(b(z)-b(x))\chi_{E_{-}}(x)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(\widetilde{B})}\int_{F_{-}}(b(x)-b(y))\chi_{E_{+}}(x)d\mu(y)+\frac{1}{\mu(\widetilde{B})}\int_{F_{+}}(b(z)-b(x))\chi_{E_{-}}(x)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{F_{-}}(b(x)-b(y))|K(x,y)|\chi_{E_{+}}(x)d\mu(y)+\int_{F_{+}}(b(z)-b(x))|K(x,y)|\chi_{E_{-}}(x)d\mu(z)

Therefore,

\displaystyle w(\{x\in B:|b(x)-C_{B}|>A\})

\displaystyle\leq w\left(\left\{x\in B:\chi_{E_{+}}(x)\cdot|[b,R_{\lambda}](\chi_{F_{-}})|(x)|>\frac{A}{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+w\left(\left\{x\in B:\chi_{E_{-}}(x)\cdot|[b,R_{\lambda}](\chi_{F_{+}})|(x)>\frac{A}{2}\right\}\right)

and take

w(\{x\in B:|b(x)-C_{B}|>A\})\leq\frac{1}{2}w(B).

Note that
\mu\in\widetilde{A}_{1,\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}
and that the kernel of the Riesz operator has the following expression

R_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(x,y)=-\frac{2\lambda}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{(x-y\cos\theta)(\sin\theta)^{2\lambda-1}}{(x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1}}d\theta.

First observe the following fact.

Proposition 10.1
.

\log x^{2\lambda}\in\rm BMO_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Proof.

Let
B:=(a,b)
be an interval in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}\int^{b}_{a}\left|\log x^{2\lambda}-\log b^{2\lambda}\right|\cdot x^{2\lambda}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}\int^{b}_{a}2\lambda\left|\log(\frac{x}{b})\right|\cdot x^{2\lambda}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\lambda}{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}\int^{b}_{a}\log\frac{x}{b}\cdot x^{2\lambda}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\lambda}{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}\bigg(\log\frac{x}{b}\cdot\frac{x^{2\lambda+1}}{2\lambda+1}\bigg|^{b}_{a}-\int^{b}_{a}\frac{1}{x}\frac{x^{2\lambda+1}}{2\lambda+1}dx\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\lambda}{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}\bigg(-\log\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{a^{2\lambda+1}}{2\lambda+1}-\frac{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}{(2\lambda+1)^{2}}\bigg),

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}\int^{b}_{a}\left|\log x^{2\lambda}-\log b^{2\lambda}\right|\cdot x^{2\lambda}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda}{b^{2\lambda+1}-a^{2\lambda+1}}\cdot\log\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{a^{2\lambda+1}}{2\lambda+1}+\frac{2\lambda}{(2\lambda+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\underset{\lambda}{\lesssim}\frac{\log\frac{a}{b}}{(\frac{b}{a})^{2\lambda+1}-1}+1.

Since

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{2\lambda+1}\log t=0,

then we have

\log x^{2\lambda}\in{\rm BMO_{\triangle_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

The proof is complete.

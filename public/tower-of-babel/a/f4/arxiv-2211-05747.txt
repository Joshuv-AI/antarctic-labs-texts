Lower Bounds for Rankin-Selberg L L -functions on the Edge of the Critical Strip
Qiao Zhang

Abstract
. Let F F be a number field, and let π 1 \pi_{1} and π 2 \pi_{2} be distinct unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of GL n 1 ⁡ ( 𝔸 F ) \operatorname{GL}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{A}_{F}) and GL n 2 ⁡ ( 𝔸 F ) \operatorname{GL}_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{F}) respectively. In this paper, we derive new lower bounds for the Rankin-Selberg L L -function L ⁡ ( s , π 1 × π ~ 2 ) L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2}) along the edge ℜ ⁡ s = 1 \Re s=1 of the critical strip in the t t -aspect. The corresponding zero-free region for L ⁡ ( s , π 1 × π ~ 2 ) L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2}) is also determined.

1.
Introduction

The value distributions of
L
-functions on the edge of the critical strip are important in number theory. In particular, the lower bounds for the
L
-values along the edge
\Re s=1
are closely related to the determination of zero-free regions of the corresponding
L
-function.

In this paper, we are interested in the lower bounds of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions along the edge of the critical strip. More precisely, let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field of degree
n_{F}
, and let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
\operatorname{GL}_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
respectively. Assume that the central characters of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are unitary and normalized so that they are trivial on the diaginally embedded copy of the positive reals. Note that, with this normalization, the Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{1})
and
L(s,\pi_{2}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
both have simple poles at
s=1
. The main subject of this paper is the lower bound for the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
, which in turn yields a new zero-free region for
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
.

In 1980, Shahidi
[
10
]
showed that
L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\neq 0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since then, there has been substantial progress on the determination of lower bounds and zero-free regions for these Rankin Selberg
L
-functions, especially in the following two scenarios. On the one hand, if either
\pi_{1}
or
\pi_{2}
is self-dual, then it is known, by the work of Moreno
[
8
]
in 1985 and Sarnak
[
9
]
in 2004, that
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
has the classical zero-free region of the de la Vallée-Poussin type, namely

\sigma>1-\frac{c}{\log\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})(|t|+2)\Big)}

where
\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})
and
\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})
are analytic conductors of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
respectively. On the other hand, the special case with
\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}
has also attracted much attention. In 2018, Goldfeld and Li
[
4
]
obtained the lower bound

L(1+it,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})\gg\frac{1}{(\log(|t|+2))^{3}}

where
\pi
is an irreducible cuspidal unramified representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with
n\geq 2
, tempered almost everywhere. This lower bound immediately leads to the zero-free region for
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})

\sigma>1-\frac{c_{\pi}}{(\log(|t|+2))^{5}}\qquad|t|\geq 1

These results were improved by Humphries and Brumley
[
5
]
in 2019 and generalized to an arbitrary number field
F
, with the lower bound

L(1+it,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})\gg_{\pi}\frac{1}{\log(|t|+2)}

and the corresponding zero-free region for
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})

(1)

\sigma>1-\frac{c_{\pi}}{\log(|t|+2)}\qquad|t|\geq 1

Recently in 2022, Humphries and Thorner
[
6
]
made explicit the dependence of the constant
c_{\pi}
upon
\pi
, and gave a more precise form for (
1
) given by

\sigma>1-\frac{c}{\log\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi)^{n}(|t|+e)^{n^{2}n_{F}}\Big)}

for some effective absolute constant
c>0
.

The study of general Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions without the self-duality assumption is much more difficult, and our knowledge is rather limited. A lower bound

L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\gg\frac{1}{\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})(|t|+2)\Big)^{N_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}}}

and the corresponding zero-free region

\sigma>1-\frac{c}{\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})(|t|+2)\Big)^{N_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}}}

were obtained in 2006 by Brumley
[
1
]
in connection with the strong multiplicity one theorem, and by Gelbert and Lapid
[
3
]
for a broad family of
L
-functions. In 2013, Brumley
[
2
]
gave an explicit value for
N_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}
. More precisely, if
\pi_{1}\neq\pi_{2}
, then he showed in
[
2
, Theorem A.1]
that

(2)

L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\gg_{F,n_{1},n_{2},\varepsilon}\mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}})-\varepsilon}

where (see (
6
))

\mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})=\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2},1+it)\ \mathfrak{c}(\widetilde{\pi}_{1}\times\pi_{2},1-it)

In particular, by the estimate (
7
)

\mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})\leq\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{2n_{1}+2n_{2}}(|t|+2)^{2n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}}

the lower bound (
2
) can be re-written as

(3)

L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\gg\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{-n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}})-\varepsilon}

Further, Brumley
[
2
]
also derived the corresponding zero-free regions for
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
; in fact, it is shown that these lower bounds still hold in these zero-free regions.

In this paper, we seek to improve upon the above results of Brumley in the
t
-aspect, in particular the lower bound (
3
), and obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field of degree
n_{F}
, and let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
\operatorname{GL}_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
respectively. Assume that
\pi_{1}\neq\pi_{2}
, and that the central characters of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are unitary and normalized so that they are trivial on the diagonally embedded copy of the positive reals. Then we have

L(\sigma+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\gg\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{-\frac{n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}}\right)-\varepsilon}

where the
\gg
-constant depends upon
F
,
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
and
\varepsilon
only.

Remark 1.2
.

Brumley
[
2
]
used the notation
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
to denote the complete Rankin-Selberg
L
-function. Hence our (finite)
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
is what he denoted by
L^{\infty}(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
.

Remark 1.3
.

The work of Brumley
[
2
]
also considered the case
\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}
, and similar results were obtained for the lower bounds and zero-free regions. As it is clear from Section 3, our arguments can also be applied to derive the corresponding improvements of these results in the
t
-aspect. However, these results have now been superseded by the above mentioned works of Goldfeld and Li
[
4
]
, of Humphries and Brumley
[
5
]
, and of Humphries and Thorne
[
6
]
. Hence we skip the relevant discussions in this paper.

Applying the convexity bound of Li
[
7
]
, we can also derive from the above lower bound the corresponding zero-free region. Since the arguments are essentially the same as the first part of the proof to
[
2
, Theorem A.1]
, we omit the details here and only present our result in the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem 1, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
c=c(F,n_{1},n_{2},\varepsilon)>0
such that

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\gg_{F,n_{1},n_{2},\varepsilon}\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{-\frac{dn_{1}n_{2}}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}}\right)-\varepsilon}

in the region

\left\{s=\sigma+it\mathrel{\left|\vphantom{s=\sigma+it\sum\sigma\geq 1-\frac{c}{\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)+\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{\frac{dn_{1}n_{2}}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}}\right)+\varepsilon}}}\right.}\sigma\geq 1-\frac{c}{\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)+\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{\frac{dn_{1}n_{2}}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}}\right)+\varepsilon}}\right\}

2.
Preliminary

Let
F
be a number field of degree
n_{F}
, and let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
\operatorname{GL}_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
respectively. Assume that the central characters of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are unitary and normalized so that they are trivial on the diagonally embedded copy of the positive reals.

Since the Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{1})
and
L(s,\pi_{2}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})
both have a simple pole at
s=1
, we may write their Laurent expansions at
s=1
as

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{1})=\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}A_{k}(s-1)^{k}

L(s,\pi_{2}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})=\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}B_{k}(s-1)^{k}

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{1}^{\prime}=C_{1}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

|A_{-1}|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})^{\varepsilon}\qquad|A_{0}|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})^{\varepsilon}

|B_{-1}|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})^{\varepsilon}\qquad|B_{0}|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})^{\varepsilon}

For simplicity, write

(4)

C_{1}=C_{1}^{\prime}\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})^{\varepsilon}\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})^{\varepsilon}

then we have

|A_{-1}|\leq C_{1}\qquad|A_{0}|\leq C_{1}\qquad|B_{-1}|\leq C_{1}\qquad|B_{0}|\leq C_{1}

Proof.

The lemma follows from Li’s convexity bounds
[
7
]
; see also
[
2
]
.∎

Further, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
consider the isobaric representation

\Pi_{t}=(\pi_{1}\otimes|\det|^{it/2})\boxplus(\pi_{2}\otimes|\det|^{-it/2})

then
we have the factorization of
L
-functions

(5)

L(s,\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{1})\ L(s,\pi_{2}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\ L(s+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\ L(s-it,\widetilde{\pi}_{1}\times\pi_{2})

and so
the corresponding factorization of analytic conductors

(6)

\mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})=\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2},1+it)\ \mathfrak{c}(\widetilde{\pi}_{1}\times\pi_{2},1-it)

In particular, by the bound
[
1
, (8)]
we have

(7)

\mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})\leq\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{2n_{1}+2n_{2}}(|t|+2)^{2n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}}

By the factorization (
5
), since
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are assumed distinct, the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})
has a double pole at
s=1
and is holomorphic everywhere else. Accordingly, we write the Laurent series representation of
L(s,\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})
at
s=1
as

L(s,\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})=\sum_{k=-2}^{\infty}r_{k}\ (s-1)^{k}

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{2}^{\prime}=C_{2}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle|r_{-1}|+|r_{-2}|\geq

\displaystyle C_{2}^{\prime}\ \mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}})-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{2}^{\prime}\ \Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{-n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}})-\varepsilon}

For simplicity, write

(8)

C_{2}=C_{2}^{\prime}\ \Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}

then we have

|r_{-1}|+|r_{-2}|\geq C_{2}\ (|t|+2)^{-n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}})-\varepsilon}

Proof.

This follows immediately from
[
1
, Theorem 3]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
|t|\geq 1
. Then we have

|r_{-1}|+|r_{-2}|\\
\leq 3C_{1}^{2}\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}+C_{1}^{2}\ \left|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg(\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}\Bigg)\right|

where
C_{1}
is as given in (
4
).

Proof.

By the factorization (
5
), we have

r_{-2}=A_{-1}\ B_{-1}\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}

so by Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle|r_{-2}|=

\displaystyle|A_{-1}|\ |B_{-1}|\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}^{2}\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}

Also, by the factorization (
5
) we have

\displaystyle r_{-1}=

\displaystyle(A_{-1}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{-1})\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+2A_{-1}\ B_{-1}\ \Re\Big(L^{\prime}(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\cdot\overline{L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{-1}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{-1})\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+A_{-1}\ B_{-1}\ \frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg(\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}\Bigg)

so again by Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle|r_{-1}|\leq

\displaystyle(|A_{-1}|\ |B_{0}|+|A_{0}|\ |B_{-1}|)\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+|A_{-1}|\ |B_{-1}|\ \left|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg(\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}\Bigg)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C_{1}^{2}\ \Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}+C_{1}^{2}\ \left|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg(\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}\Bigg)\right|

Hence our lemma follows as we combine all the above estimates.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem 1

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem 1, and will freely use the notations introduced in Section 2.

The theorem is well known if
n_{1}=n_{2}=1
, so henceforth we assume that either
n_{1}\geq 2
or
n_{2}\geq 2
. For simplicity, henceforth we write

\theta=\frac{n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By
[
2
, Theorem A.1]
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}>0
such that

\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|\geq C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\ \mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}+n_{2}})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{F}n_{1}n_{2})}}

Recall the bound (
7
)

\mathfrak{c}(\Pi_{t}\times\widetilde{\Pi}_{t})\leq\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{2n_{1}+2n_{2}}(|t|+2)^{2n_{F}n_{1}n_{2}}

then over the interval
[-1,1]
the above lower bound gives

\displaystyle\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|\geq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\ \Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}

(9)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{3^{\theta}}\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{-\theta-\varepsilon}~

Now we claim that

(10)

\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|\geq C_{\varepsilon}(|t|+2)^{-\theta-\varepsilon}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R})

where

C_{\varepsilon}=\min\left\{\frac{C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{3^{\theta}}\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon},\ \sqrt{\frac{C_{2}}{6C_{1}^{2}\ (2\theta+2\varepsilon)}}\right\}

and
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are the constants defined in (
4
) and (
8
) respectively. Note in particular that we have

C_{\varepsilon}\gg\Big(\mathfrak{c}(\pi_{1})\ \mathfrak{c}(\pi_{2})\Big)^{-(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)-\varepsilon}

where the
\gg
-constant depends upon
F
,
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
and
\varepsilon
only.

By the construction of
C_{\varepsilon}
and the estimate (
9
), the claim (
10
) is obviously valid over the interval
[-1,1]
. Now assume that there indeed exists some point
|t_{0}|>1
such that

(11)

\Big|L(1+it_{0},\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|<C_{\varepsilon}\ (|t_{0}|+2)^{-\theta-\varepsilon}

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
t_{0}>1
. For simplicity, henceforth we write

g(t)=\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|^{2}

then our claim (
10
) becomes

g(t)\geq C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (|t|+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R})

and the assumption (
11
) becomes

(12)

g(t_{0})<C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{0}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}

Define

\mathcal{S}_{1}=\left\{0\leq t<t_{0}\mathrel{\left|\vphantom{0\leq t<t_{0}\sum g(t)\geq C_{\varepsilon}^{2}(t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}}\right.}g(t)\geq C_{\varepsilon}^{2}(t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}\right\}

Since
[0,1]\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{1}\neq\emptyset
, so the supremum
t_{1}=\sup(\mathcal{S}_{1})
exists and is finite, and we have

t_{1}\geq 1\qquad g(t_{1})=C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{1}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}

Also, we define

\mathcal{S}_{2}=\left\{t>t_{0}\mathrel{\left|\vphantom{t>t_{0}\sum g(t)\geq C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}}\right.}g(t)\geq C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}\right\}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}\qquad t_{2}=\inf(\mathcal{S}_{2})

so
t_{0}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
and we have

(13)

g(t)<C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}\qquad(t_{1}<t<t_{2})

Further, if
t_{2}<\infty
, then we also have

g(t_{2})=C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{2}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}

Combining Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, for
t\geq 1
we have

C_{2}(t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\leq|r_{-1}|+|r_{-2}|\leq 3C_{1}^{2}\ g(t)+C_{1}^{2}\ |g^{\prime}(t)|

Hence we have

(14)

|g^{\prime}(t)|\geq\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}-3g(t)

In particular, over the interval
(t_{1},t_{2})
, by (
13
) we have

g(t)<C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (|t|+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{6C_{1}^{2}}\ (|t|+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}

so the estimate (
14
) gives

\displaystyle|g^{\prime}(t)|\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}-3g(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}-3\cdot\frac{C_{2}}{6C_{1}^{2}}(t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\qquad\qquad\qquad(t_{1}<t<t_{2})

In particular, we have
g^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
over
(t_{1},t_{2})
, so the derivative
g^{\prime}(t)
must keep the same sign in this interval. If
g^{\prime}(t)>0
over
(t_{1},t_{2})
, then
g(t)
is a strictly increasing function, and in particular

g(t_{0})>g(t_{1})=C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{1}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}>C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{0}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}

in contradiction to the assumption (
12
). Hence we have
g^{\prime}(t)<0
over
(t_{1},t_{2})
, and so

(15)

-g^{\prime}(t)=|g^{\prime}(t)|\geq\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\qquad(t_{1}<t<t_{2})

If
t_{2}<\infty
, then by the choices of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\Big(-g^{\prime}(t)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t=g(t_{1})-g(t_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{1}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}-C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{2}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (2\theta+2\varepsilon)(t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

which is simply

(16)

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{2\theta+2\varepsilon}{(t+2)^{1+\varepsilon}}(t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}(t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t

On the other hand, over the interval
(t_{1},t_{2})
, by the construction of the constant
C_{\varepsilon}
we have

C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{2\theta+2\varepsilon}{(t+2)^{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{6C_{1}^{2}(2\theta+2\varepsilon)}\cdot\frac{2\theta+2\varepsilon}{1+2}<\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}

so we should have

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ \frac{2\theta+2\varepsilon}{(t+2)^{1+\varepsilon}}(t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t<\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t

a contradiction to (
16
). Hence we must have
t_{2}=\infty
. In particular, this implies that we can take
t_{0}
to be any value in the infinite interval
(t_{1},\infty)
.

In summary, combining (
13
) and (
15
) gives

g(t)<C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}\qquad-g^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\qquad(t>t_{1})

so for every
t_{0}>t_{1}
we have

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ (t_{1}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle g(t_{1})\geq g(t_{1})-g(t_{0})=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}\Big(-g^{\prime}(t)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ (t+2)^{-2\theta-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\left.\frac{(t+2)^{1-2\theta-\varepsilon}}{1-2\theta-\varepsilon}\right|_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ \frac{(t_{1}+2)^{1-2\theta-\varepsilon}-(t_{0}+2)^{1-2\theta-\varepsilon}}{2\theta+\varepsilon-1}

Since
\theta\geq 1/2
, we have
1-2\theta-\varepsilon>0
, so letting
t_{0}\to\infty
gives

C_{\varepsilon}^{2}(t_{1}+2)^{-2\theta-2\varepsilon}\geq\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ \frac{(t_{1}+2)^{1-2\theta-\varepsilon}}{2\theta+\varepsilon-1}

This implies that

C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\geq\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}^{2}}\ \frac{(t_{1}+2)^{1+\varepsilon}}{2\theta+\varepsilon-1}

and so

\displaystyle(t_{1}+2)^{1+\varepsilon}\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\cdot\frac{2C_{1}^{2}}{C_{2}}\cdot(2\theta+\varepsilon-1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{6C_{1}^{2}(2\theta+2\varepsilon)}\cdot\frac{2C_{1}^{2}}{C_{2}}\cdot(2\theta+\varepsilon-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\theta+\varepsilon-1}{3(\theta+\varepsilon)}<1

which is impossible. Hence there cannot exist a point
|t_{0}|>1
satisfying (
12
), and so the claim (
10
) is valid for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

\Big|L(1+it,\pi_{1}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{2})\Big|\geq C_{\varepsilon}\ (|t|+2)^{-\theta-\varepsilon}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R})

This completes our proof of Theorem 1.

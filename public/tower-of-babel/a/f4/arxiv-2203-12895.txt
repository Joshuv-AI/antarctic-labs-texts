Binomial Approximation to Locally Dependent CDO
Amit N. Kumar, P. Vellaisamy

Abstract
In this paper, we develop Stein’s method for binomial approximation using the stop-loss metric that allows one to obtain a bound on the error term between the expectation of call functions. We obtain the results for a locally dependent collateralized debt obligation (CDO), under certain conditions on moments. The results are also exemplified for an independent CDO. Finally, it is shown that our bounds are sharper than the existing bounds.



Keywords:
Binomial distribution; error bounds; Stein’s method; CDO.

MSC 2020 Subject Classifications:
Primary: 62E17, 62E20; Secondary: 60F05, 60E05.

Introduction

The collateralized debt obligation (CDO) is a financial tool of structured asset-backed security. It is used to repackage assets into a product and sold to investors in the secondary market. These packages are in terms of debt such as auto loans, credit card debt, mortgages, and corporate debt. The assets are sliced into tranches, a set of repayment ties with different payment priorities and interest rates. The elementary tranches are senior (low risk and low return), mezzanine and equity (high risk and high return). Investors can choose a tranche of their interest to invest. For more details, see Jiao and Karoui
[
11
]
, Jiao
et al.

[
12
]
, Hull and White
[
13
]
, Kumar
[
14
,
15
]
, Neammanee and Yonghint
[
18
]
, Yonghint
et al.

[
23
]
, and the reference therein.

Condider the tranche pricing with
n
portfolios, where each portfolio has a constant recovery rate
R>0
. Then, the percentage loss at time
T
can be defined as (see Neammanee and Yonghint
[
18
, p. 2]
)

\displaystyle L(T)=\frac{(1-R)}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_{i}\leq T\}},

(1.1)

where
\tau_{i}
is the default time of the
i
-th portfolio and
{\bf 1}_{A}
denotes the indicator function of the set
A
. For a detachment point or an attachment point, say
z^{*}
, the main problem of CDO pricing is to evaluate the value of the mean of percentage total loss for each tranche given by (see Yonghint
et al.

[
23
, p. 2]
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(L(T)-z^{*})^{+}]=\frac{(1-R)}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(W_{n}-z\right)^{+}\right],

where
h(x)=x^{+}=\max\{x,0\}
is the call function,
W_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_{i}\leq T\}}
and
z=({nz^{*}}/{(1-R)})>0
. For additional details, see, for example, Kumar
[
14
,
15
]
, Neammanee and Yonghint
[
18
]
, Yonghint
et al.

[
23
]
.

In general, the mean of percentage total loss is difficult to compute when
{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_{i}\leq T\}}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, are locally dependent random variables. Therefore, it is of interest to find a suitable approximating distribution which is close to
W_{n}
. The proximity between
\mathbb{E}[(W_{n}-z)^{+}]
and
\mathbb{E}[(\text{P}_{\lambda}-z)^{+}]
is studied by Yonghint
et al.

[
23
]
, where
\text{P}_{\lambda}
denotes the Poisson random variable with parameter
\lambda
. Observe that
W_{n}
is the sum of dependent Bernoulli random variables and so the binomial distribution also could a good choice to approximate
W_{n}
. See Vellaisamy and Punnen
[
21
]
, where it is shown that the binomial distribution may arise as the distribution of the sum of a certain dependent Bernoulli random variables. Therefore, we choose the binomial distribution as the target distribution.

Throughout this paper, let
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
follow the binomial distribution with probability mass function

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}=k)=\binom{\alpha}{k}p^{k}q^{\alpha-k},\quad\text{for }k=0,1,\ldots,\alpha,

(1.2)

where
\alpha
is a positive integer and
0<p=1-q<1
. We derive the result using the stop-loss metric defined by

\displaystyle d_{sl}(X,Y)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{E}[(X-z)^{+}]-\mathbb{E}[(Y-z)^{+}]\right|,

(1.3)

where
\mathbb{R}
denotes the set of real numbers. For more details, see Bautsikas and Veggelatou
[
4
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we develop Stein’s method for binomial distribution, under the stop-loss metric. In particular, we discuss uniform and non-uniform bounds for the solution of the Stein equation. In Section
3
, we derive the error in approximation of a locally dependent CDO to a suitable binomial distribution. As a special case, we demonstrate the results for the independent CDO. Also, we give some numerical comparisons between our results and the existing results. It is shown that our bounds improve significantly over the existing bounds.

Stein’s Method

Stein
[
20
]
proposed an elegant method to find the error in approximating
the sums of real-valued random variables to a normal distribution. Then, Chen
[
9
]
adapted the technique for approximating the sums of discrete random variables to a
suitable Poisson distribution. Later, numerous authors adapted or used
Stein’s method for several distributions and under various distance metrics. To mention a few relevant ones, Stein’s method for negative binomial distribution has been studied by Brown and Phillips
[
5
]
under the total variation distance and for the Poisson distribution has been studied by Neammanee and Yonghint
[
18
]
, under the stop-loss metric. For recent developments, see Barbour
et al.

[
1
]
, Brown and Xia
[
6
]
, Eichelsbacher and Reinert
[
10
]
and Kumar
[
15
]
.

In this section, we develop the Stein’s method for the binomial distribution, under the stop-loss metric defined in (
1.3
). Our work is mainly focused on finding the upper bound for
|\Delta g_{z}(k)|=|\ g_{z}(k+1)-g_{z}(k)|
, where
g_{z}(k)
is the solution of the Stein equation given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}g_{z}(k)=(k-z)^{+}-\mathbb{E}(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}.

(2.1)

Here,
\mathscr{A}
denotes the Stein operator of
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}g_{z}(k)=\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{q}g_{z}(k+1)-kg_{z}(k),\quad\text{for }k=0,1,\ldots,\alpha.

(2.2)

Note that
\mathbb{E}(\mathscr{A}g_{z}(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}))=0
. Using (
2.2
), the Stein equation (
2.1
) leads to

\displaystyle\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{q}g_{z}(k+1)-kg_{z}(k)=(k-z)^{+}-\mathbb{E}(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}.

From Section 2 of Kumar
et al.

[
16
, p. 4]
with appropriate changes, it can easily be verified that the solution of the above equation is

\displaystyle g_{z}(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{if $k=0$};\\
\displaystyle{-\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}[(j-z)^{+}-\mathbb{E}(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}]},&\text{if $1\leq k\leq\alpha$.}\end{array}\right.

The bounds for
|\Delta g_{z}(k)|
may be essentially uniform for all
k
and
z
. However, the bound depends on
k
may be useful in practice to get a sharper bound for the stop-loss metric. So, we obtain the non-uniform upper bound for
|\Delta g_{z}(k)|
.

The following lemma gives the non-uniform upper bound for
|\Delta g_{z}(k)|
, for all
z\geq 0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
z\geq 0
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle|\Delta g_{z}(k)|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2q^{1-\alpha}-q,&\text{for}\penalty\ k=0;\\
2q^{k-\alpha},&\text{for}\penalty\ 1\leq k\leq\alpha.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It can be easily verified that
\mathbb{E}[(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}]\leq\alpha p
. For
k=0
, we have

\displaystyle|\Delta g_{z}(0)|=|g_{z}(1)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-1)!}{(\alpha-j)!j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-1}[(j-z)^{+}+\mathbb{E}(B_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha-1}{j-1}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-1}+p\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-1}

\displaystyle=2q^{1-\alpha}-q.

For
1\leq k\leq\alpha-1
, let

\displaystyle h_{1}(k)=\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}(j-z)^{+}

and

\displaystyle h_{2}(k)=\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\mathbb{E}(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}.

Then,

\displaystyle\Delta g_{z}(k)

\displaystyle=g_{z}(k+1)-g_{z}(k)=[h_{1}(k)-h_{1}(k+1)]-[h_{2}(k)-h_{2}(k+1)].

(2.5)

First, consider

\displaystyle 0<h_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}(j-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{(j-1)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{k(k+1)\ldots(j-1)}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha-k}{j-k}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha-k}{j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=q^{k-\alpha}.

(2.6)

Similarly,
0<h_{1}(k+1)\leq q^{k+1-\alpha}\leq q^{k-\alpha}
. Now, consider

\displaystyle 0<h_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\mathbb{E}(\text{B}_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha p}{k}\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{k!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha p}{k}\left(1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{k!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p}{k}\left(1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{(k+1)\ldots j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha p}{k}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha-k+1}{j-k+1}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p}{k}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha-k+1}{j+1}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p}{k}\left(1+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-(\alpha-k)p-1}{(\alpha-k+1)p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha p}{k}\left(1+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-(\alpha-k+1)p}{(\alpha-k+1)p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha pq^{k-\alpha}}{(\alpha-k+1)kp}

\displaystyle\leq q^{k-\alpha}.

(2.7)

Similarly, it follows that
0<h_{2}(k+1)\leq q^{k+1-\alpha}\leq q^{k-\alpha}
. Therefore, from (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), the result follows for
1\leq k\leq\alpha-1
.

Finally, for
k=\alpha
, it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle|\Delta g_{z}(\alpha)|=|g_{z}(\alpha)|=\frac{(\alpha-z)^{+}}{\alpha}+\frac{\mathbb{E}(B_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}}{\alpha}\leq 2.

This proves the result.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

(i)

Observe that the uniform bound can be taken as

\displaystyle|\Delta g_{z}(k)|\leq 2q^{1-\alpha},\quad\text{for all $z\geq 0$ and $0\leq k\leq\alpha$.}

(2.8)

(ii)

From (
2
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), it can be easily seen that

\displaystyle|g_{z}(k)|\leq 2q^{k-n},\quad\text{for all $z\geq 0$.}

(2.9)

Also, the uniform bound for
|g_{z}(k)|
is the same as given in (
2.8
).

Next, using the similar technique of the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the following lemma which gives the non-uniform upper bound for
|\Delta g_{z}(k)|
for all
z>1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
z>1
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle|\Delta g_{z}(k)|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle{2\left(1+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{qz}\right)},&\text{for $k\geq z$;}\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle{\frac{3\left(q^{k-\alpha}-1\right)}{pz}},&\text{for $2\leq k<z$;}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{2(\alpha-1)pq^{1-\alpha}}{z}},&\text{for $1=k<z$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
k\geq z
. First, consider

\displaystyle 0<h_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}(j-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{(j-1)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{kq}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=k+2}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{(k+1)\ldots(j-1)}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{kq}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=2}^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha-k}{j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{kq}+\frac{1}{k}\left(q^{k-\alpha}-\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{q}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{z}.

(2.10)

Next, for
k\geq z
and
k\geq\alpha p
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{k!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{(k+1)q}+\sum_{j=k+2}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{k!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{(k+1)q}+\frac{1}{(k+1)}\sum_{j=k+2}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k+1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{(k+1)q}+\frac{1}{(k+1)}\sum_{j=k+2}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{(k+2)\ldots j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{(k+1)q}+\frac{1}{(k+1)}\sum_{j=k+2}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!(j-k)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{(k+1)q}+\frac{1}{(k+1)}\sum_{j=2}^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha-k}{j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{(k+1)q}+\frac{1}{(k+1)}\left(q^{k-\alpha}-\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{q}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{z}\right).

(2.11)

Also, for
k\geq z
and
k\leq\alpha p
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k+1)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\left(\frac{\alpha-k+1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k+1)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{k!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\left(\frac{\alpha-k+1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k+1)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{(k+1)\ldots j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\left(\frac{\alpha-k+1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k+1)!}{(\alpha-j)!(j-k+1)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\left(\frac{\alpha-k+1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha-k+1}{j+1}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\left(\frac{\alpha-k+1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{p}-(\alpha-k)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-k+1}\left(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{kp}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\alpha-k+1)k}+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{(\alpha-k)zp}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{\alpha pqz}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha p}\left(1+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{qz}\right).

(2.12)

Therefore, form (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), for
k\geq z
, we have

\displaystyle 0<h_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\mathbb{E}(B_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{qz}.

(2.13)

Hence, from (
2.5
), (
2.10
) and (
2.13
), the result follows for
k\geq z
.

Next, let
2\leq k<z
. Note that

\displaystyle 0<h_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}(j-z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{z}\sum_{j=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{(j-1)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}(j-z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{z}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{(j-1)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}(j-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{z}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{(j-2)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{q}+\sum_{j=k+2}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{k(k+1)\ldots(j-2)}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{q}+\sum_{j=k+2}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{2.3\ldots(j-k)}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{(\alpha-k)p}{q}+\sum_{j=2}^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha-k}{j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{k-\alpha}-1}{z}.

(2.14)

Next, consider

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{k!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\left(1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{1}{(k+1)\ldots j}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\left(1+2\sum_{j=k+1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!(j-k+2)!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{(\alpha-k)(\alpha-k+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha-k+2}{j+2}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{(\alpha-k)(\alpha-k+1)}\left(\frac{2q^{k-\alpha}-2-2(\alpha-k)p-(\alpha-k)(\alpha-k+1)p^{2}}{2p^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(q^{k-\alpha}-1)}{(\alpha-k)(\alpha-k+1)kp^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(q^{k-\alpha}-1)}{\alpha(\alpha-z)p^{2}}.

Note that, for
k<z\leq\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(B_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\alpha}(m-z)\binom{\alpha}{m}p^{m}q^{\alpha-m}

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha-z)\sum_{m=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\alpha}\frac{\alpha!}{(\alpha-m!)m!}p^{m}q^{\alpha-m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha(\alpha-z)}{z}\sum_{m=1}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha-1}{m-1}p^{m}q^{\alpha-m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-z)p}{z}

Therefore,

\displaystyle 0<h_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-k)!}{(\alpha-j)!}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-k}\mathbb{E}(B_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(q^{k-\alpha}-1)}{pz}.

(2.15)

Hence, from (
2.5
), (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), the result follows for
2\leq k<z\leq\alpha
.

Next, for
1=k<z
, we have

\displaystyle 0<h_{1}(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-1)!}{(\alpha-j)!j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-1}(j-z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{z}\sum_{j=2}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha-2}{j-2}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha-1)pq^{1-\alpha}}{z}.

and

\displaystyle 0<h_{2}(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha-1)!}{(\alpha-j)!j!}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{j-1}\mathbb{E}(B_{\alpha,p}-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q^{1-\alpha}-q}{\alpha p}\right)\left(\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)p^{2}}{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha-1)pq^{1-\alpha}}{z}.

This proves the result.
∎

3
Bounds for Binomial Approximation

In this section, we obtain the error bounds for binomial approximation to locally dependent CDO. We derive the results for the stop-loss metric under certain conditions on moments. Moreover, we demonstrate the results under an independent setup. It is shown that binomial distribution is more suitable for a CDO using the numerical comparison between our bounds and the existing bound given by Neammanee and Yonghint
[
18
]
.

In Yonghint
et al.

[
23
]
, it is shown that the locally dependent CDO is useful in real-life applications in various aspects. So, we consider a similar locally dependent structure that can also be used for independent setup. Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
be a collection of random variables such that
X_{i}
is independent of
X_{A_{i}^{c}}
, while
X_{A_{i}}
is independent of
X_{B_{i}^{c}}
, where
i\in A_{i}\subseteq B_{i}\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Here,
X_{A}
denotes the collection of random variables
\{X_{i},i\in A\}
, and
A^{c}
denotes the complement of the set
A
. Note that if
A_{i}=B_{i}=\{i\}
, then
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
become independent random variables. See Kumar
[
14
,
15
]
, Röllin
[
19
]
and Čekanavičius and Vellaisamy
[
7
,
8
]
for a similar locally dependent setup.

Let
\tau_{i}
is the default time of the
i
-th portfolio and
X_{i}
henceforth denote the random variable
{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_{i}\leq T\}}
with
\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=1)=p_{i}=1-q_{i}=1-\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=0)
. Consider

\displaystyle W_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},

(3.1)

which is a key factor of percentage loss up to time
T
defined in (
1.1
). Our aim is to approximate
W_{n}
by a suitable binomial random variable.

Throughout this section, let
g_{z}=g
,

\displaystyle W_{i}=W_{n}-X_{A_{i}}=\sum_{j\notin A_{i}}X_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{i}^{*}=W_{n}-X_{B_{i}}=\sum_{j\notin B_{i}}X_{i}.

(3.2)

Note that
X_{i}
is independent of
W_{i}
. Also,
X_{i}
and
X_{A_{i}}
are independent of
W_{i}^{*}
.

First, we choose one parameter of binomial distribution of our choice, and accordingly, another parameter can be obtained. Since
\alpha
should be a positive integer (number of identical Bernoulli trials) for the binomial distribution, we choose
\alpha=n
and let

\displaystyle p=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}.

(3.3)

The following theorem gives the error in the approximation between
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
and
W_{n}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
and
W_{n}
be defined as in (
1.2
) and (
3.1
), respectively, and satisfy (
3.3
). Then

\displaystyle d_{sl}(W_{n},\text{B}_{n,p})\leq\frac{2}{pq^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\mathbb{E}\left((X_{i}+p)q^{W_{i}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left((p_{i}+qX_{i})q^{W_{n}}\right)\right].

(3.4)

where
d_{sl}
denote the stop-loss distance defined in (
1.3
).

Proof.

Multiplying the Stein operator (
2.2
) by
q
and taking expectation with respect to
W_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=\alpha p\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1))-p\mathbb{E}(W_{n}g(W_{n}+1))-q\mathbb{E}(W_{n}g(W_{n})).

(3.5)

Using (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1))-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}g(W_{n}+1))+q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}\Delta g(W_{n}))

From (
3.2
), note that
X_{i}
and
W_{i}
are independent. Therefore, the above expression can be written as

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1)-g(W_{i}+1))+q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}\Delta g(W_{n}))

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}(g(W_{n}+1)-g(W_{i}+1)))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(g(W_{i}+X_{A_{i}}+1)-g(W_{i}+1))+q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}\Delta g(W_{n}))

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}(g(W_{i}+X_{A_{i}}+1)-g(W_{i}+1)))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\Delta g(W_{i}+j)\right)+q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}\Delta g(W_{n}))

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\Delta g(W_{i}+j)\right).

(3.6)

Using Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{pq^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\mathbb{E}\left(q^{W_{i}}(1-q^{X_{A_{i}}})\right)+\frac{2}{q^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}q^{W_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\frac{2}{pq^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}q^{W_{i}}\left(1-q^{X_{A_{i}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{pq^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\mathbb{E}\left((X_{i}+p)q^{W_{i}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left((p_{i}+qX_{i})q^{W_{n}}\right)\right].

This proves the result.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
and
W_{n}
be defined as in (
1.2
) and (
3.1
), respectively, and satisfy (
3.3
). Assume
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent random variables then

\displaystyle d_{sl}(W_{n},\text{B}_{n,p})\leq\frac{2}{q^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|p-p_{i}|p_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}(1-pp_{j}).

(3.7)

Proof.

Substituting
A_{i}=\{i\}
in (
3.6
), it can be easily verified that

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}(\Delta g(W_{i}+1))+q\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\mathbb{E}(\Delta g(W_{i}+1))-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\mathbb{E}(\Delta g(W_{i}+1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_{i}-p)p_{i}\mathbb{E}(\Delta g(W_{i}+1)).

Hence, using Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{q^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|p-p_{i}|p_{i}\mathbb{E}\left(q^{W_{i}}\right)=\frac{2}{q^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|p-p_{i}|p_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}(1-pp_{j}).

This proves the result.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

(i)

Note that if
p_{i}=p
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, in (
3.7
) then
d_{sl}(W_{n},\text{B}_{n,p})=0
, as expected.

(ii)

In Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.1
, the bounds become shaper for sufficiently small values of
p_{i}
.

(iii)

If we choose the parameter
p
of our choice, then
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})
. In this situation, the parameter
\alpha
may not be an integer. So, we can take

\displaystyle\alpha=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\right\rfloor,

where
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
is the integer part of
x>0
, and use (
2.9
) and (
3.12
) to get

\displaystyle d_{sl}(W_{n},\text{B}_{\alpha,p})\leq\frac{2}{pq^{\alpha}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\mathbb{E}\left((X_{i}+p)q^{W_{i}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left((p_{i}+qX_{i}-\delta p^{2})q^{W_{n}}\right)\right].

(3.8)

Therefore, it is suggested to consider the minimum of the bounds given in (
3.4
) and (
3.8
).

If we choose
\alpha p=\mathbb{E}(W_{n})
and
\alpha pq=\mathrm{Var}(W_{n})
(the first two moments matching condition), then the choice of
\alpha
may not be a positive integer. So, we choose

\displaystyle\alpha=\left\lfloor\frac{(\mathbb{E}(W_{n}))^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(W_{n})-\mathrm{Var}(W_{n})}\right\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad p=\frac{\mathbb{E}(W_{n})-\mathrm{Var}(W_{n})}{\mathbb{E}(W_{n})}.

(3.9)

Also, define
D(Z):=2d_{TV}(Z,Z+1)
and

\displaystyle\delta:=\frac{(\mathbb{E}(W_{n}))^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(W_{n})-\mathrm{Var}(W_{n})}-\alpha.

Observe that
0\leq\delta<1
,

\displaystyle\alpha p=\mathbb{E}(W_{n})-\delta p\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha pq=\mathrm{Var}(W_{n})-\delta pq

(3.10)

The next result gives the error in approximation between
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
and
W_{n}
satisfying the above conditions.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
and
W_{n}
be as defined in (
1.2
) and (
3.1
), respectively, and satisfy (
3.9
). Then

\displaystyle d_{sl}(W_{n},\text{B}_{\alpha,p})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p^{2}q^{\alpha}}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\left(pX_{A_{i}}+q^{X_{B_{i}}}\left(1-q^{-X_{A_{i}}}\right)\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{A_{i}},X_{B_{i}})\right)\right.

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\left(pX_{A_{i}}+q^{X_{B_{i}}}\left(1-q^{-X_{A_{i}}}\right)\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{i},X_{A_{i}},X_{B_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +p\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\left(q^{B_{i}}-q\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{B_{i}})\right)+\frac{\delta p^{3}}{q}\mathbb{E}(q^{W_{n}})

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \left.+\frac{p}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(X_{A_{i}})-\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{A_{i}})+q\mathbb{E}(X_{i})|\mathbb{E}\left(\left(q^{B_{i}}-q\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{B_{i}})\right)\right\}.

(3.11)

Proof.

From (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=\alpha p\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1))-p\mathbb{E}(W_{n}g(W_{n}+1))-q\mathbb{E}(W_{n}g(W_{n})).

Using (
3.10
) and following the steps similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\Delta g(W_{i}+j)\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\Delta g(W_{i}+j)\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}\Delta g(W_{n}))-\delta p\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1)).

(3.12)

Using (
3.2
) and (
3.10
), the above expression leads to

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}(\Delta g(W_{i}+j)-\Delta g(W_{i}^{*}+1))\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}(\Delta g(W_{i}+j)-\Delta g(W_{i}^{*}+1))\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}(\Delta g(W_{n})-\Delta g(W_{i}^{*}+1)))-\delta p\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1))

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(X_{A_{i}})-\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{A_{i}})+q\mathbb{E}(X_{i})]\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1)-g(W_{i}^{*}+1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}\backslash A_{i}}+j-1}\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}\backslash A_{i}}+j-1}\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}}-1}\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)\right)-\delta p\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1))

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(X_{A_{i}})-\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{A_{i}})+q\mathbb{E}(X_{i})]\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}}-1}\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}\backslash A_{i}}+j-1}\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)|X_{A_{i}},X_{B_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{X_{A_{i}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}\backslash A_{i}}+j-1}\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)|X_{i},X_{A_{i}},X_{B_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}}-1}\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)|X_{B_{i}})\right)-\delta p\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1))

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(X_{A_{i}})-\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{A_{i}})+q\mathbb{E}(X_{i})]\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{X_{B_{i}}-1}\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)|X_{B_{i}})\right)

Note that
\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}g(W_{i}^{*}+\ell)|\cdot)\leq 2q^{\ell-\alpha}D(W_{i}^{*}|\cdot)
. Hence, using (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p^{2}q^{\alpha}}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\left(pX_{A_{i}}+q^{X_{B_{i}}}\left(1-q^{-X_{A_{i}}}\right)\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{A_{i}},X_{B_{i}})\right)\right.

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\left(pX_{A_{i}}+q^{X_{B_{i}}}\left(1-q^{-X_{A_{i}}}\right)\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{i},X_{A_{i}},X_{B_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +p\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\left(q^{B_{i}}-q\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{B_{i}})\right)+\frac{\delta p^{3}}{q}\mathbb{E}(q^{W_{n}})

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \left.+\frac{p}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(X_{A_{i}})-\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{A_{i}})+q\mathbb{E}(X_{i})|\mathbb{E}\left(\left(q^{B_{i}}-q\right)D(W_{i}^{*}|X_{B_{i}})\right)\right\}.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\text{B}_{\alpha,p}
and

be defined as in (

) and (

\displaystyle d_{sl}(W_{n},\text{B}_{\alpha,p})\leq\frac{2}{q^{\alpha}}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}-\gamma^{*}\right)^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|p-p_{i}|p_{i}^{2}+\delta p\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-pp_{i})\right\},

\displaystyle q\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(W_{n})]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p-p_{i})p_{i}\mathbb{E}(\Delta g(W_{n}+1)-\Delta g(W_{i}+1))-\delta p\mathbb{E}(g(W_{n}+1))

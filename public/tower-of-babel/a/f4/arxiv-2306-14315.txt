The growth order of the optimal constants in Turán-Erőd type inequalities in L q ​ ( K , μ ) L^{q}(K,\mu)
P. Yu. Glazyrina, Yu. S. Goryacheva, Sz. Gy. Révész

Abstract
In 1939 Turán raised the question about lower estimations of the maximum norm of the derivatives of a polynomial p p of maximum norm 1 1 on the compact set K K of the complex plain under the normalization condition that the zeroes of p p in question all lie in K K . Turán studied the problem for the interval I = [ − 1 , 1 ] I=[-1,1] and the unit disk D D and found that with n := deg ⁡ p n:=\deg p tending to infinity, the precise growth order of the minimal possible derivative norm (oscillation order) is n \sqrt{n} for I I and n n for D D . Erőd continued the work of Turán considering other domains. Finally, in 2006, Halász and Révész proved that the growth of the minimal possible maximal norm of the derivative is of order n n for all compact convex domains. Although Turán himself gave comments about the above oscillation question in L q L^{q} norms, till recently results were known only for D D and I I . Recently, we have found order n n lower estimations for several general classes of compact convex domains, and proved that in L q L^{q} norm the oscillation order is at least n / log ⁡ n n/\log n for all compact convex domains. In the present paper we prove that the oscillation order is not greater than n n for all compact (not necessarily convex) domains K K and L q L^{q} -norm with respect to any measure supported on more than two points on K K .

J^{\prime}_{K_{B}}=\frac{1}{n^{q}}\int_{K_{B}}|p^{\prime}(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)\leqslant\frac{|p(B+2w+iw)|^{q}}{(1-\delta)^{q}(1-5/m)^{q}}\mu(K_{B}).

(36)

Adding (
35
) to (
36
) we get

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{K\setminus Q}

\displaystyle=J^{\prime}_{K_{A}}+J^{\prime}_{K_{B}}\leqslant\frac{|p(A-2w+iw)|^{q}}{(1-\delta)^{q}(1-2/m)^{q}}\mu(K_{A})+\frac{|p(B+2w+iw)|^{q}}{(1-\delta)^{q}(1-5/m)^{q}}\mu(K_{B})\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\mu(K)}{(1-\delta)^{q}(1-5/m)^{q}}\max\{|p(A-2w+iw)|,\,|p(B+2w+iw)|\}^{q}.

Combining this with (
33
) and (
34
) we deduce

\frac{J^{\prime}_{K\setminus Q}}{J_{Q}}\leqslant\frac{2w^{q}}{\theta(1-\delta)^{q}(1-5/m)^{q}}\max\left\{\frac{|p(A-2w+iw)|}{w^{1+1/q}p(A-w)},\,\frac{|p(B+2w+iw)|}{w^{1+1/q}p(B+w)}\right\}^{q}.

(37)

2.) Now our goal is to find conditions on the degree
n
of the polynomial
p
under which

\dfrac{|p(A-2w+iw)|}{w^{1+1/q}p(A-w)}\leqslant 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dfrac{|p(B+2w+iw)|}{w^{1+1/q}p(B+w)}\leqslant 1

(38)

or equivalently

\ln\frac{|p(A-2w+iw)|}{p(A-w)}<(1+1/q)\ln w

(39)

and

\ln\frac{|p(B+2w+iw)|}{p(B+w)}<(1+1/q)\ln w.

(40)

We first deal with inequality (
39
).
To simplify notation we set
A^{\prime}=A-w
,

a:=\frac{(1+A^{\prime}-w)^{2}+w^{2}}{(1+A^{\prime})^{2}},\quad b:=\frac{(1-(A^{\prime}-w))^{2}+w^{2}}{(1-A^{\prime})^{2}}=\frac{(1-A^{\prime}+w)^{2}+w^{2}}{(1-A^{\prime})^{2}}.

Then the left side of (
39
) can be written as

\displaystyle\ln\frac{|p(A-2w+iw)|}{p(A-w)}=\ln\frac{|p(A^{\prime}-w+iw)|}{p(A^{\prime})}=\frac{1}{2}\ln a^{n-k}b^{k}=\frac{n}{4}\left((1+M)\ln a+(1-M)\ln b\right).

Since
\ln x\leqslant x-1
(
x>0
), we have
\ln a\leqslant a-1
and

\ln b\leqslant b-1
.
We proceed

\begin{gathered}(1+M)\ln a+(1-M)\ln b\leqslant(1+M)(a-1)+(1-M)(b-1)\\
=(1+M)\frac{-2(1+A^{\prime})w+2w^{2}}{(1+A^{\prime})^{2}}+(1-M)\frac{2(1-A^{\prime})w+2w^{2}}{(1-A^{\prime})^{2}}\\
=\frac{4w}{1-A^{\prime 2}}\left(-(M-A^{\prime})+w-\frac{2A^{\prime}(M-A^{\prime})w}{1-A^{\prime 2}}\right)\\
<\frac{4w}{1-A^{\prime 2}}\left(-(M-A^{\prime})+w\right)=\frac{4w}{1-A^{\prime 2}}\left(-(M-A)\right)\leqslant 4w\left(-(M-A)\right)\leqslant-4w^{2},\end{gathered}

(41)

the last inequality following from (
19
).
Therefore, inequality (
39
) will be satisfied once we get

\dfrac{n}{4}(-4w^{2})\leqslant(1+1/q)\ln w.

This is equivalent to the following restriction on
n
:

n\geqslant\frac{(1+1/q)\ln 1/w}{w^{2}}.

(42)

We now turn to inequality (
40
).
With the notation
B^{\prime}:=B+w
,

a_{1}:=\frac{(1+B^{\prime}+w)^{2}+w^{2}}{(1+B^{\prime})^{2}},\quad b_{1}:=\frac{(1-(B^{\prime}+w))^{2}+w^{2}}{(1-B^{\prime})^{2}}=\frac{(1-B^{\prime}-w)^{2}+w^{2}}{(1-B^{\prime})^{2}},

the left side of (
40
) can be written as

\displaystyle\ln\frac{|p(B+2w+iw)|}{p(B+w)}=\ln\frac{|p(B^{\prime}+w+iw)|}{p(B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln a_{1}^{n-k}b_{1}^{k}=\frac{n}{4}\left((1+M)\ln a_{1}+(1-M)\ln b_{1}\right).

Replacing in (
41
)
A^{\prime}
by
B^{\prime}=B+w
and
w
by
-w
,
and successively applying inequality (
29
) to estimate
1-B^{\prime}
from below
and inequality (
31
) to estimate
B^{\prime}-M
from below, we get

\displaystyle(1+M)\ln a_{1}+(1-M)\ln b_{1}\leqslant\frac{-4w}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\left(-(M-B^{\prime})-w-\frac{2B^{\prime}(M-B^{\prime})(-w)}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4w}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\left((M-B^{\prime})+w+\frac{2B^{\prime}(B^{\prime}-M)w}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4w}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\left(-(B^{\prime}-M)+w+\frac{(B^{\prime}-M)w}{1-B^{\prime}}\right)\leqslant\frac{4w}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\left(-(B^{\prime}-M)+\frac{B^{\prime}-M}{m-4}+w\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-4w^{2}}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\left(\dfrac{B^{\prime}-M}{w}\left(1-\frac{1}{m-4}\right)-1\right)\leqslant\frac{-4w^{2}}{1-B^{\prime 2}}\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{m-4}\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant-2w^{2}\left(3-\dfrac{5}{m-4}\right).

The inequality (
40
) will be satisfied once we get

\dfrac{n}{4}(-2w^{2})\left(3-\dfrac{5}{m-4}\right)\leqslant(1+1/q)\ln w.

This is equivalent to

n\geqslant\dfrac{2}{\left(3-5/(m-4)\right)}\frac{(1+1/q)\ln 1/w}{w^{2}}.

(43)

So, we have established (
40
) under restriction (
43
).

Therefore, if both (
42
) and (
43
) are satisfied, and, moreover, we also have (
18
), then both (
39
) and (
40
) follows, whence (
38
) obtains, too.
Combining (
37
) and (
38
) then yields

J^{\prime}_{K\setminus Q}\leqslant\frac{2}{\theta(1-\delta)^{q}(1-5/m)^{q}}w^{q}J_{Q}

(44)

for
n
satisfying (
42
), (
43
) and (
18
).

4.4
Estimate of
J^{\prime}_{K}

Suppose
0<q<\infty.
Estimates (
32
) and (
44
) give us the numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
from (
25
).
Substituting them into (
26
)
we obtain

\displaystyle C_{q}(\delta,\theta)

\displaystyle\leqslant 4(\alpha+\beta)^{1/q}=4\left(\dfrac{17^{q/2}}{(1-\delta)^{q}(1-5/m)^{q}}+\frac{2}{\theta(1-\delta)^{q}(1-5/m)^{q}}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4\cdot 17^{1/2}}{(1-5/m)}\dfrac{1}{(1-\delta)}\left(1+\frac{2}{\theta}\right)^{1/q}

for
n
satisfying (
42
), (
43
) and (
18
).

Let us compare these three conditions.
Observe that
7<m<8
, which entails

1<\dfrac{2}{3-5/4}<\dfrac{2}{3-5/(m-4)}<\dfrac{2}{3-5/3}=1.5.

Thus we can replace the restriction (
43
) by

n>\frac{1.5(1+1/q)\ln 1/w}{w^{2}}.

(45)

which is stronger than (
42
) and (
9
) in Theorem
1
.
From
w\leqslant(1-\delta)/m\leqslant 1/7
and monotonicity of
x\ln x
for
x\geqslant 7
we get that
(1/w)\ln(1/w)\geqslant 7\ln 7\geqslant 7
. Thus it follows that

\dfrac{2}{\left(3-5/(m-4)\right)}\frac{(1+1/q)\ln 1/w}{w^{2}}\geqslant\frac{\ln 1/w}{w^{2}}\geqslant\dfrac{7}{w}.

Thus (
43
) is stronger than (
18
) for all
q
, and in fact even without the term containing
q
.

Finally taking into account that
4\cdot 17^{1/2}/(1-5/m)\leqslant 4\cdot 5/(1-5/7)=70<121
we obtain the assertion of Theorem
1
for
0<q<\infty
and all
n
satisfying (
45
).

We are left with the case
q=\infty.

Observe that the constructed polynomial
p
is independent of
q
,
and that any
n\geqslant\dfrac{2\ln 1/w}{w^{2}}
meets (
45
) for sufficiently large
q
.
Therefore we can pass to the limit (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Problem 4.7.44]
) for all
n\geqslant\dfrac{2\ln 1/w}{w^{2}}

and obtain

M_{n,\infty}(K,\mu)\leqslant\dfrac{\|p^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}{\|p\|_{\infty}}=\lim_{q\to\infty}\dfrac{\|p^{\prime}\|_{q}}{\|p\|_{q}}\leqslant\limsup_{q\to\infty}C_{q}(\delta,\theta)\dfrac{w}{d^{2}}n\leqslant\dfrac{121}{1-\delta}\dfrac{w}{d^{2}}n.

(46)

This proves Theorem
1
for
q=\infty.

5
The case of a large width

We now turn to the case
(1-\delta)/m<w\leqslant 1
. Recall that there exist
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that (
8
) holds.
Then at least one of the “halves”

K_{\ell}=K_{\delta}\cap([-\delta,0]\times[-iw,iw])
or

K_{r}=K_{\delta}\cap([0,\delta]\times[-iw,iw])
has measure greater than
\dfrac{\theta}{2}\mu(K).
Without loss of generality we assume that
\mu(K_{\ell})\geqslant\dfrac{\theta}{2}\mu(K).

Let us take the polynomial
p(z)=(1-z)^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2.

As in Section
4
we first deal with
0<q<\infty
.
We divide
K
into two subsets

K_{1}=\{x+iy\in K\ :\ x\in[-1,3/4]\},\quad\mbox{and}\quad K_{2}=\{x+iy\in K\ :\ x\in[3/4,1]\}.

The estimate
|1-z|\geqslant|1-x|\geqslant 1/4
for
z\in K_{1}
yields

\left\|p^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(K_{1},\mu)}^{q}=n\left\|(1-z)^{n-1}\right\|_{L^{q}(K_{1},\mu)}^{q}\leqslant n^{q}\max_{z\in K_{1}}\frac{1}{|1-z|^{q}}\|p\|_{L^{q}(K_{1},\mu)}^{q}\leqslant(4n)^{q}\|p\|_{q}^{q}.

(47)

We proceed to estimate
\left\|p^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(K_{2})}.

Since the diameter
d(K)=2
and
-1,1\in K
, the set
K
is bounded by
the circle of radius
2
and centered at the point
-1
, thus
y^{2}\leqslant 4-(1+x)^{2}
for any
z=x+iy\in K
.
This gives

|1-z|^{2}=(1-x)^{2}+y^{2}\leqslant(1-x)^{2}+4-(1+x)^{2}=4-4x\leqslant 1,\quad z\in K_{2},

hence

\left\|p^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(K_{2},\mu)}^{q}=n^{q}\left\|(1-z)^{n-1}\right\|_{L^{q}(K_{2},\mu)}\leqslant n^{q}\max_{z\in K_{2}}|1-z|^{(n-1)q}\mu(K_{2})\leqslant n^{q}\mu(K).

(48)

To estimate
\mu(K)
we note that

\displaystyle\|p\|_{q}^{q}\geqslant\|p\|_{L^{q}(K_{\ell})}^{q}\geqslant\min_{z\in K_{\ell}}|1-z|^{nq}\mu(K_{\ell})\geqslant\dfrac{\theta}{2}\mu(K),

thus
\mu(K)\leqslant\dfrac{2}{\theta}\|p\|_{q}^{q}.

Substituting this into (
48
) yields

\left\|p^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(K_{2},\mu)}^{q}\leqslant n^{q}\frac{2}{\theta}\|p\|_{q}^{q}.

(49)

Adding (
47
) to (
49
) we deduce that

\|p^{\prime}\|_{q}^{q}\leqslant\left((4n)^{q}+n^{q}\frac{2}{\theta}\right)\|p\|_{q}^{q}\leqslant(4n)^{q}\left(1+\frac{2}{\theta}\right)\|p\|_{q}^{q},

and finally that

\dfrac{\|p^{\prime}\|_{q}}{\|p\|_{q}}\leqslant 4n\left(1+\frac{2}{\theta}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant 4n\frac{m}{1-\delta}w\dfrac{4}{d^{2}}\left(1+\frac{2}{\theta}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{16m}{1-\delta}\left(1+\frac{2}{\theta}\right)^{1/q}\frac{w}{d^{2}}n.

It remains to observe that
16m\leqslant 121.

A passage to the limit similar to (
46
) proves the case
q=\infty
.

6
Proof of the Corollaries

Suppose first that
\mu
is the linear Lebesgue measure on the boundary of
K
. We want to derive Corollary
1
from Theorem
1
.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

It is plausible that to any
0<\delta<1
there is some appropriate
\theta
with which Theorem
1
can be applied. To obtain an effective estimate we first look for a concrete admissible value of
\theta
corresponding to an arbitrary value of
\delta\in(0,1)
.

By Lemma
1
,

K
is contained in the rectangle
[-1,1]\times[-iw,iw]
.
By properties of convex curves
[
3
, p. 52, Property 5]

the arc length of
\partial K
is not greater than the perimeter of the rectangle, i.e.
\mu(K)\leqslant 4(1+w)\leqslant 8
, while
the arc length of the part of
\partial K
belonging to
K_{\delta}
is trivially at least
4\delta
, i.e.
\mu(K_{\delta})\geqslant 4\delta
.
This provides us with the admissible value
\theta=\delta/2.

Therefore, an application of Theorem
1
leads to

M_{n,q}(K)\leqslant C_{q}(\delta)\frac{w^{2}}{d}n,\quad\textrm{where}\quad C_{q}(\delta):=C_{q}\left(\delta,\frac{\delta}{2}\right)=\frac{121}{1-\delta}\left(1+\frac{4}{\delta}\right)^{1/q}\qquad(0<\delta<1).

(50)

It remains to optimize on the choice of
\delta
in function of
q
. So now we are looking for
\displaystyle\min_{0<\delta<1}\dfrac{1}{121}C_{q}(\delta)=\min_{0<\delta<1}\dfrac{(1+4/\delta)^{1/q}}{1-\delta}
. Denote

\varphi(\delta):=\log\dfrac{(1+4/\delta)^{1/q}}{1-\delta}=\dfrac{1}{q}\log(1+4/\delta)-\log(1-\delta).

It is easy to see that on
(0,1)
this function is strictly convex, with
\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)=+\infty
determining a
U
-shape form. We are looking for its unique minimum point. Differentiation results in

\varphi^{\prime}(\delta)=\dfrac{-4}{q}\dfrac{1}{\delta^{2}+4\delta}+\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}
, and the unique root of this in
(0,1)
can be obtained from the quadratic equation
\delta^{2}+4\delta=\dfrac{4}{q}(1-\delta)
or
\delta^{2}+(4+4/q)\delta-4/q=0
. This equation has two roots, one in the negative semiaxis and one in
(0,1)
: the latter is
\delta_{1}:=\frac{2}{q}\left(Q-(q+1)\right)
, where
Q:=\sqrt{q^{2}+3q+1}
. This latter point is the unique minimum point for
\varphi(\delta)
, so that we get

\displaystyle\min_{0<\delta<1}\frac{1}{121}C_{q}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\min_{0<\delta<1}\exp(\varphi(\delta))=\exp(\varphi(\delta_{1}))=\frac{\{1+4/(\frac{2}{q}(Q-(q+1)))\}^{1/q}}{1-\frac{2}{q}(Q-(q+1))}

\displaystyle=q\frac{\{1+2q\frac{Q+q+1}{Q^{2}-(q+1)^{2}}\}^{1/q}}{3q+2-2Q}=q(3q+2+2Q)\frac{\{1+2q\frac{Q+q+1}{q}\}^{1/q}}{(3q+2)^{2}-4Q^{2}}

\displaystyle=q(3q+2+2Q)\frac{\{1+2(Q+q+1)\}^{1/q}}{5q^{2}}=\frac{3q+2+2Q}{5q}(3+2q+2Q)^{1/q}.

Substituting back into (
50
) yields the stated inequality (
11
).
∎

Next, we consider the case when
\mu
is the (restriction to
K
) of the 2-dimensional Lebesgue measure (area).

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Again by Lemma
1
,
\mu(K)\leqslant\mu([-1,1]\times[-iw,iw])=4w.

Taking into account the definition of the minimal width, we observe that there exist

\alpha\in[0,1]
and points
x^{+}
,
x^{-}\in[-1,1]
such that the points
z^{+}=x^{+}+i\alpha w
and
z^{-}=x^{-}-i(1-\alpha)w
belong to
K
(as otherwise the width of
K
in the vertical direction would be less than
w
).
Write
K^{+}_{\delta}=\{x+iy\in K_{\delta}\colon y\geqslant 0\},

K^{-}_{\delta}=\{x+iy\in K_{\delta}\colon y\leqslant 0\}.

If
x^{+}\in[-\delta,\delta]
, then by convexity of
K
, the value of
\mu(K_{\delta}^{+})
is at least the area of the triangle with the vertices
-\delta
,
z^{+}
,
\delta
, which is equal to
\alpha w\delta
.
If
x^{+}\in[\delta,1]
, then
\mu(K_{\delta}^{+})
is not less then the area of the trapezium bounded by the real axis, the straight lines
x=\pm\delta
, and the straight line passing through the points
-1
and
1+i\alpha w.
The area of this trapezium is also
\alpha w\delta
. Similarly, if
x^{+}\in[-1,-\delta]
, then
\mu(K_{\delta}^{+})
is not less then the area of the trapezium bounded by the real axis, the straight lines
x=\pm\delta
, and the straight line passing through the points
-1+i\alpha w
and
1
.
Hence, in all cases,
\mu(K_{\delta}^{+})\geqslant\alpha w\delta.

Analogously,
\mu(K_{\delta}^{-})\geqslant(1-\alpha)w\delta.

Finally,
\mu(K_{\delta})\geqslant w\delta
. Therefore,
\mu(K_{\delta})/\mu(K)\geqslant\delta/4
.

Thus for any
0<\delta<1
we can set
\theta:=\delta/4
as an admissible value for an application of Theorem
1
. The theorem provides

M_{n,q}(K,\lambda)\leqslant C_{q}^{*}(\delta)\frac{w^{2}}{d}n,\quad\textrm{where}\quad C_{q}^{*}(\delta):=C_{q}\left(\delta,\frac{\delta}{4}\right)=\frac{121}{1-\delta}\left(1+\frac{8}{\delta}\right)^{1/q}.

(51)

Now we consider
\varphi(\delta):=\log\frac{1}{121}C_{q}^{*}(\delta)
, which is again a strictly convex,
U
-shaped function with endpoint values
\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)=+\infty
. To find the minimum, we compute the unique critical point satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}(\delta)=0
. This equation can be written as

\frac{1}{q}\frac{1}{1+8/\delta}\cdot(-\frac{8}{\delta^{2}})-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\cdot(-1)=0\quad\text{or}\quad q\delta^{2}+(8q+8)\delta-8=0.

The quadratic equation has two roots, one in the negative semiaxes and another one in
(0,1)
: this latter one is
\delta_{1}=\frac{2}{q}\left(Q-2q-2\right)
, where
Q:=\sqrt{4q^{2}+10q+4}
. This is the minimum point of
\varphi(\delta)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\min_{0<\delta<1}\frac{1}{121}C_{q}^{*}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\min_{0<\delta<1}\exp(\varphi(\delta))=\exp(\varphi(\delta_{1}))=\frac{\{1+8/(\frac{2}{q}(Q-2q-2))\}^{1/q}}{1-\frac{2}{q}(Q-2q-2))}

\displaystyle=q\frac{\left(1+\frac{4q}{Q-2q-2}\right)^{1/q}}{q-2Q+4q+4}=\frac{q(5q+4+2Q)}{(5q+4)^{2}-4Q^{2}}\left(1+\frac{4q(Q+2q+2)}{Q^{2}-(2q+2)^{2}}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\frac{q(5q+4+2Q)}{9q^{2}}\left(1+\frac{4q(Q+2q+2)}{2q}\right)^{1/q}=\frac{5q+4+2Q}{9q}\left(4q+5+2Q\right)^{1/q}.

Substitution in (
51
) results in the stated assertion (
12
).
∎

Our present work has dealt with the case of complex
domains
with positive width
w>0
.
The partition of the set
K
and the calculus depended on
w
and are valid only for
n>d^{2}/w^{2}
, a fatal condition if
w=0
.

As we mention in the introduction, in all known cases the
lower estimates
for
w=0
have
\sqrt{n}
order. However, we are not aware of general
upper estimations
which would ensure that the order is indeed
\sqrt{n}
. The most general we are aware of is the remark of Xiao and Zhou following
[
20
, p. 198, Theorem 1, Corollary 1]
and pointing out that their estimate is of the right order for Jacobi weights. It seems likely that
O(\sqrt{n})
upper estimates for
M_{n,q}(I,\mu)
remain valid even for more general weights and measures.

Vertex-transitive Neumaier graphs
Mojtaba Jazaeri

Abstract
. A graph Γ \Gamma is called edge-regular whenever it is regular and for any two adjacent vertices, the number of their common neighbors is independent of the choice of vertices. A clique C C in Γ \Gamma is called regular whenever for any vertex out of C C , the number of its neighbors in C C is independent of the vertex. A Neumaier graph is a non-complete edge-regular graph with a regular clique. In this paper, we study vertex-transitive Neumaier graphs. We give a necessary and sufficient condition under which a vertex-transitive Neumaier graph is strongly regular. We also identify Neumaier Cayley graphs with small valency at most 10 10 among vertex-transitive Neumaier graphs.

Introduction

Neumaier
[

]
studied regular cliques in an edge-regular graph and stated the question that is every edge-regular graph with a regular clique strongly regular? Recently, a non-complete edge-regular graph with a regular clique is called a Neumaier graph and it has attracted a great deal of attention among authors, see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
. It is known that a Neumaier graph has diameter
2
or
3
. Soicher
[
16
]
studied cliques in an edge-regular graph. Greaves and Koolen
[
14
]
answered the Neumaier question by constructing an infinite number of examples of Neumaier graphs that are not strongly regular. Evans
[
11
]
studied regular induced subgraphs in an edge-regular graph. Abiad et al.
[
4
]
studied Neumaier graphs with few eigenvalues and proved that there is no Neumaier graph with exactly four distinct eigenvalues. Abiad et al.
[

[

]
.

A vertex-transitive Neumaier graph has diameter

[

]
,
[

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper, all graphs are connected, undirected and simple, i.e., there are no loops or multiple edges. From now on, let
\Gamma
denote a graph with vertex set
V
. We denote the complement of
\Gamma
by
\overline{\Gamma}
. Recall that a subset
C\subseteq V
is called a clique in
\Gamma
whenever the induced subgraph on
C
is complete. The clique
C
is called a
regular clique with nexus
a
whenever for any vertex out of
C
, the number of its neighbors in
C
is
a
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
4
, p. 13]
)
Let
\Gamma
be a non-complete
k
-regular graph containing a regular clique of size
c
with nexus
a
. Then
k
and
c-a-1
are two nonnegative distinct eigenvalues of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
C
be a regular clique of size
c
with nexus
a
in
\Gamma
. Then
C
gives rise to an equitable partition for
\Gamma
with the following quotient matrix.

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c-1&k-c+1\\
a&k-a\\
\end{array}\right)

It follows that
k
and
c-a-1
are two eigenvalues of
\Gamma
(cf.
[
7
, Lemma 2.3.1]
). Moreover,
a<c<k+1
because
\Gamma
is not a complete graph. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

(cf.
[
12
, Lemma 2.5(i)]
)
Let
\Gamma
be a
k
-regular graph containing a regular clique of size
c
with nexus
a
. Then
c(k-c+1)=(n-c)a
.

Proof.

Let
C
be a regular clique of size
c
with nexus
a
in
\Gamma
. Then the result follows by the double counting method for the number of edges between the regular clique
C
and out of
C
.
∎

A graph
\Gamma
with
n
vertices is called
edge-regular
with parameters
(n,k,\lambda)
whenever it is
k
-regular and for any two adjacent vertices the number of their common neighbors is
\lambda
. A non-complete edge-regular graph with a regular clique is called a
Neumaier graph
. A Neumaier graph that is not strongly regular is called a
strictly Neumaier graph
(cf.
[
12
]
). It is known that if
\Gamma
is a Neumaier graph, then the size and nexus of any regular clique in
\Gamma
is independent of the choice of the regular clique (cf.
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
). A Neumaier graph
\Gamma
is said to have parameters
(n,k,\lambda;a,c)
whenever
\Gamma
is edge-regular with parameters
(n,k,\lambda)
containing a regular clique of size
c
with nexus
a
. We note that a Neumaier graph has diameter
2
or
3
because it has a regular clique with nexus
a\geq 1
.

A
strongly regular graph
with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
is a non-complete connected
k
-regular graph with
n
vertices such that two vertices have
\lambda
and
\mu
common neighbors depending on whether these two vertices are adjacent or non-adjacent, respectively. A strongly regular graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
is called nontrivial whenever
0<\mu<k
(cf.
[
6
, § 1.1]
). Recall that a strongly regular graph has diameter
2
and exactly three distinct eigenvalues (cf.
[
6
, § 1.3]
). Let
\Gamma
be a strongly regular graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
. Assume that
\Gamma
is a Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda;a,c)
. Then we say that
\Gamma
is a strongly regular Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu;a,c)
.

Let
G
be a (finite) group and
S
be an inverse-closed subset of
G
without the identity element. Then the
Cayley graph

Cay(G,S)
is a graph whose vertex set is
G
and two vertices
a,b
are adjacent whenever
ab^{-1}\in S
. We call the subset
S
of
G
, the connection set of
Cay(G,S)
. From now on, let
Cay(G,S)
denote a Cayley graph over the group
G
with the connection set
S
, and
e
denote the identity element of
G
. We note that
Cay(G,S)
is connected if and only if the subgroup generated by
S
(which is denoted by
\langle S\rangle
) equals
G
(cf.
[
13
, Lemma 3.7.4]
). We call a Neumaier graph that is Cayley a
Neumaier Cayley graph
. We denote the dihedral group of order
n
by
D_{n}
, the symmetric group on
n
letters by
S_{n}
, the alternating group on
n
letters by
A_{n}
, and the cyclic additive group modulo
n
by
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We note that a circulant graph with
n
vertices is a Cayley graph over the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

A
vertex-transitive
graph is a graph whose automorphism group acts transitively on its vertex set. We denote the automorphism of a graph
\Gamma
by
Aut(\Gamma)
. Recall that a Cayley graph is vertex-transitive (cf.
[
13
, § 3.7]
).

3.
Neumaier graphs

Recall that a Neumaier graph has diameter
2
or
3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda;a,c)
. If
\lambda=0
, then
c=2
,
a=1
, and
\Gamma
is the complete bipartite graph
K_{k,k}
. Moreover, if
c=2
, then
a=1
,
\lambda=0
, and
\Gamma
is the complete bipartite graph
K_{k,k}
.

Proof.

By the definition of a Neumaier graph.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Neumaier graph with diameter
2
. Then
\Gamma
has at most
\max\{1+k+k(k-2),2k\}
vertices.

Proof.

If
\lambda=0
, then
\Gamma
has
2k
vertices by Lemma
3.1
. If
\lambda>1
, then
\Gamma
has at most
1+k+k(k-2)
vertices by
[
6
, Proposition 1.4.1]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Neumaier graph with vertex set
V
and parameters
(n,k,\lambda;a,c)
. Assume that
C\subset V
is a regular clique with nexus
a
and
e\in C
is an arbitrary vertex. Let
S
denote the set of neighbors of
e
in
\Gamma
. Then the following items hold.

(i)

The induced subgraph on
S\setminus C
is regular. Moreover, if the number of neighbors of any vertex in
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
to the vertices in
S\setminus C
is independent of the choice of vertex, then
\{\{e\},C\setminus\{e\},S\setminus C,V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})\}
is an equitable partition for
\Gamma
.

(ii)

Let
\Gamma
be strongly regular. Then the number of neighbors of any vertex in
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
to the vertices in
S\setminus C
is independent of the vertex.

(iii)

Let
\Gamma
be vertex-transitive. Then
\Gamma
has diameter
2
. Moreover,
\Gamma
is strongly regular if and only if the number of neighbors of any vertex in
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
to the vertices in
S\setminus C
is independent of the vertex.

Proof.

(
i
): We note that
C\setminus\{e\}\subset S
. This implies that every vertex in
C\setminus\{e\}
has
c-2
neighbors in
C\setminus\{e\}
,
\lambda-c+2
neighbors in
S\setminus C
and therefore
k-\lambda-1
neighbors in
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
. Moreover, every vertex in
S\setminus C
has
a-1
neighbors in
C\setminus\{e\}
,
\lambda-a+1
neighbors in
S\setminus C
, and therefore
k-\lambda-1
neighbors in
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
. Furthermore, every vertex in
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
has the same number
a
neighbors in
C
because
C
is a regular clique with nexus
a
. Therefore the result follows by the hypothesis and noting that
\Gamma
is
k
-regular (see Figure
1
below).

(
ii
): By (
i
), noting that
e
is non-adjacent to any vertex in
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
, and the definition of a strongly regular graph.

(
iii
): The distance between
e
and an arbitrary vertex in
V
is at most
2
by (
i
) and noting that
a>0
. Let
x,y\in V
. Then there is an automorphism
\sigma\in Aut(\Gamma)
such that
\sigma(x)=e
since
\Gamma
is vertex-transitive. Let
\sigma(y)=y^{\prime}
. Then the distance between
x,y
is the same as
e,y^{\prime}
. Assume that the distance between
x,y
is
2
. Then
x,y
have the same number of common neighbors as
e,y^{\prime}
. Therefore the result follows by (
i
), (
ii
).
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda;a,c)
. Then
\Gamma
has at least the following number of edges:

k(k-\lambda)+(k-c+1)(a-1)+\frac{(k-c+1)(\lambda-a+1)}{2}+\frac{(c-1)(c-2)}{2}.

Proof.

We count the number of edges of
\Gamma
by Theorem
3.3
(
i
). The number of edges from
e
to
S
and
S
to
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
is
k
and
k(k-\lambda-1)
, respectively. Moreover, the number of edges in
S
equals

(k-c+1)(a-1)+\frac{(k-c+1)(\lambda-a+1)}{2}+\frac{(c-1)(c-2)}{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that a strongly regular graph has exactly three distinct eigenvalues. We have the following result about the eigenvalues of a strongly regular Neumaier graph.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a strongly regular Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu;a,c)
. Then
\Gamma
has exactly three distinct eigenvalues
k
,
c-a-1
, and
-\frac{\mu}{a}
. Moreover,
-\frac{\mu}{a}
is an integer.

Proof.

Let
-m
be the smallest eigenvalue of
\Gamma
. Then the size of every regular clique admits the Hoffman bound, namely
c=1+\frac{k}{m}
, and
a=\frac{\mu}{m}
by
[
6
, Proposition 1.3.2(ii)]
. This implies that
k
,
c-a-1
, and
-m=-\frac{\mu}{a}
are all three distinct eigenvalues of
\Gamma
by Lemma
2.1
. Moreover,
-\frac{\mu}{a}
is a rational and algebraic integer. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a strongly regular Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu;a,c)
. Then

(1)

(k-c+1)(k-\lambda-1)=(n-k-1)(\mu-a),

(2)

\mu(c-a-1)=a(k-\mu).

Proof.

We have (
1
) by Theorem
3.3
(
ii
) and the double counting method for the number of edges between
S\setminus C
and
V\setminus(S\cup\{e\})
in Theorem
3.3
(
i
). We have (
2
) by evaluating the eigenvalues of
\Gamma
in Proposition
3.7
and
[
6
, Theorem 1.3.1(iii)]
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

(cf.
[
12
, Theorem 3.3]
)
Let
\Gamma
be a Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda;1,c)
. Then
\lambda=c-2
and
k-2c+3>0
.

Proof.

We have
\lambda=c-2
by Lemma
3.5
. Moreover, the regular induced subgraph on
S\setminus C
has valency
c-2
by Theorem
3.3
(
i
) since
\lambda=c-2
and
a=1
. This implies that
k-c+1>c-2
because the number of vertices in
S\setminus C
is
k-c+1
. Therefore
k-2c+3>0
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda;a,c)
. If
c=k
, then
\Gamma
is the cycle graph
C_{4}
.

Proof.

We have
k=(n-k)a
by Lemma
2.2
, and
(k-1)(k-\lambda-1)=(n-k-1)a
by Lemma
3.6
. This implies that
(k-1)(k-\lambda-1)=k-a
. It follows that
a=1
since
k-1\geq k-a
. Therefore
k<3
by Lemma
3.9
. This implies that
n=4
,
k=2
, and therefore
\Gamma
is the cycle graph
C_{4}
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

There is no nontrivial strongly regular Neumaier circulant graph.

Proof.

The Paley graph with a prime number of vertices is the only nontrivial strongly regular circulant graph (cf.
[
6
, Corollary 2.10.6]
). This completes the proof by Proposition
3.7
because it has non-integer eigenvalues.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

A conference graph is a strongly regular graph with parameters
(4\mu+1,2\mu,\mu-1,\mu)
. A conference graph has exactly three distinct eigenvalues
\{2\mu,\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1+4\mu}}{2}\}
(cf.
[
6
, Theorem 1.3.1(ii)]
). The Paley graph is an example of a conference graph (see
[
6
, § 1.3]
).

4.
Vertex-transitive Neumaier graphs with small valency

In this section, we study vertex-transitive Neumaier graphs with small valency at most
10
. Recall that a vertex-transitive Neumaier graph has diameter
2
(cf. Theorem
3.3
(
iii
)).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Neumaier graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda;a,c)
. If
k\leq 10
, then
n\leq 36
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have
c(k-c+1)\geq n-c
. The maximum value of
n
, which is
36
, is attained when
k=10
and
c=6
. This completes the proof.
∎

Example 4.2
.

The complete multipartite graph
K_{\underbrace{m,m,\ldots,m}_{n\,times}}
is a strongly regular Neumaier graph with parameters
(nm,(n-1)m,(n-2)m,(n-1)m;n-1,n)
. It is also a Cayley graph over the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{m}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
with the connection set
S=G\setminus H
, where
H
is a subgroup of the group
G
of order
m
(cf.
[
2
, Prop. 2.6]
).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
Cay(G,S)
be a Cayley graph. Assume that
C\subset G
is a regular clique with nexus
a
. If
C
is a subgroup of
G
, then

|S|=([G:C]-1)a+|C|-1.

Proof.

We count the number of vertices adjacent to
e
, which equals
|S|
. Since
C
is a clique, the number of adjacent vertices to
e
in
C
is
|C|-1
. Note that every right coset of
C
in
G
is a regular clique with nexus
a
. Therefore, excluding
C
,
e
has exactly
a
adjacent vertices in each of the remaining right cosets of
C
. This completes the proof.
∎

Example 4.4
.

The lattice graph
L_{2}(n,n)
is the line graph of the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
. It is a strongly regular Neumaier graph with parameters
(n^{2},2(n-1),n-2,2;1,n)
. It is also a Cayley graph over the group

with the connection set

S=\{(0,1),(0,2),\ldots,(0,n-1),(1,0),(2,0),\ldots,(n-1,0)\}

(cf.
[

\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),\ldots,(0,n-1)\},

and

\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),\ldots,(n-1,0)\}

are two subgroups of

that are regular cliques with nexus

(see Proposition

). The complement of

is also a strongly regular Neumaier Cayley graph with parameters

Example 4.7

The triangular graph
T(n)
is the line graph of the complete graph

Lemma 4.8
.

The complement of
T(n)
is a Neumaier graph if and only if
n
is even. In this case, if the Neumaier graph
\overline{T(n)}
has parameters
(\binom{n}{2},k,\lambda,\mu;a,c)
, then
n=2a+4
and
c=a+2
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{T(n)}
be a Neumaier graph with parameters
(\binom{n}{2},k,\lambda,\mu;a,c)
. Then
-\frac{\mu}{a}
is the negative eigenvalue of
\overline{T(n)}
by Proposition
3.7
. This implies that
-\frac{\mu}{a}=3-n
, where
\mu=\frac{n(n-1)-6(n-2)}{2}
(cf.
[
8
, § 1.1.2, § 1.1.7]
). It follows that
2a(n-3)=n(n-1)-6(n-2)
and therefore
n=2a+4
. This implies that
c=a+2
that is the number of mutually disjoint edges in the complete graph
K_{n}
. The other way around by noting that the set of
a+2
mutually disjoint edges in the complete graph
K_{n}
, where
n=2a+4
, is a regular clique with nexus
a
in
\overline{T(n)}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a strongly regular graph with valency at most
10
. Then
\Gamma
is a (vertex-transitive) Neumaier graph if and only if
\Gamma
is isomorphic to one of the following graphs:

•

The complete multipartite graph;

•

The lattice graph;

•

The complement of the lattice graph;

•

The triangular graph;

•

The complement of the triangular graph
T(6)
;

•

The complement of the Shrikhande graph;

•

The complement of the Schläfli graph.

Proof.

The feasible parameters for strongly regular graphs with at most
512
vertices are available in
[
8

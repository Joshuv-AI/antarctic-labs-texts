Optimal Polynomial Approximants in H p H^{p}
Raymond Centner, Raymond Cheng, Christopher Felder

Abstract
. This work studies optimal polynomial approximants (OPAs) in the classical Hardy spaces on the unit disk, H p H^{p} ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ). In particular, we uncover some estimates concerning the OPAs of degree zero and one. It is also shown that if f ∈ H p f\in H^{p} is an inner function, or if p > 2 p>2 is an even integer, then the roots of the nontrivial OPA for 1 / f 1/f are bounded from the origin by a distance depending only on p p . For p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 , these results are made possible by the novel use of a family of inequalities which are derived from a Banach space analogue of the Pythagorean theorem.

1.
Introduction

For
1<p<\infty
, we let
H^{p}
denote the classical Hardy space of holomorphic functions on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
,

H^{p}:=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\ d\theta<\infty\right\}.

As is standard, for
f\in H^{p}
, we adopt the norm notation

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\ d\theta\right)^{1/p}.

Given a non-negative integer
n
, the
n
-th optimal polynomial approximant
to
1/f
is the polynomial solving the minimization problem

\min_{q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\|1-qf\|_{p},

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the set of complex polynomials of degree less than or equal to
n
.
For brevity, we shall use the acronym
OPA
to mean optimal polynomial approximant.
Notationally, for fixed
n
and
f\in H^{p}
, we denote this polynomial by
q_{n,p}[f]
.
It is important to note that when
1<p<\infty
, the space
H^{p}
is
uniformly convex
. Consequently,
H^{p}
possesses the
unique nearest point property
: if
\mathcal{V}\subseteq H^{p}
is a closed linear subspace and
h\in H^{p}
, then there is a unique vector
u\in\mathcal{V}
such that
u
solves the minimization problem

\min_{\mu\in\mathcal{V}}\|\mu-h\|_{p}.

The function
u
is referred to as the
metric projection
of
h
onto
\mathcal{V}
. When
p=2
, this projection is the usual orthogonal projection; when
p\neq 2
, the metric projection is generally nonlinear. Consequently, in the latter case, the metric projection does not come equipped with the explicit computational benefits enjoyed in the Hilbert space case.

In terms of OPAs, we see that
q_{n,p}[f]
is simply the metric projection of the unit constant function onto the subspace of
H^{p}
spanned by
\{f,zf,z^{2}f,\ldots,

z^{n}f\}
. Heuristically, we see that if a polynomial
Q
looks like
1/f
, then
Qf-1
looks like zero; thus,
q_{n,p}[f]
is indeed an approximation to
1/f
. As
n
is exhausted ad infinitum,
\|q_{n,p}[f]f-1\|_{p}
decreases
optimally
.

The genesis of OPAs is in the setting of
H^{2}
and first appeared in engineering literature with respect to the study of digital filters in signal processing. More recently, OPAs have attracted interest among mathematicians working in various settings (see, for example, the surveys by Bénéteau and the first named author
[
3
]
, and Seco
[
21
]
). This revival has been primarily informed by the hunt for the classification of cyclic functions. In a space of functions where the forward shift is bounded and the polynomials are dense, it is elementary to see that the linear span of orbit of the function 1 under the shift is dense in the space itself. A function with this property is referred to as
cyclic
. In
H^{2}
, it is well known that such vectors have an explicit function theoretic form, known as
outer
. However, there is little known outside of
H^{2}
.

Conceptually, OPAs extend directly to any normed function space that is uniformly convex.
They have been explored in Dirichlet type spaces,

[
4
,
6
,
7
]
;
more general reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces

[
17
]
;
the spaces
\ell^{p}_{A}

[
15
,
22
]
;

L^{p}
of the unit circle
[
12
]
;
Hilbert spaces of analytic
functions on the bidisk

[
5
,
8
]
and the unit ball

[
19
,
20
]
.
They have been studied in the context of free polynomials as well

[
2
]
.

A characterization for cyclic functions in
H^{p}
is well-known (as a consequence of the classical Beurling Theorem), so the motivation of cyclicity does not directly carry over to the exploration here. However, the Banach space geometry makes the study of these types of approximation problems much more challenging; the analysis becomes nonlinear when
p\neq 2
. Further, as we shall see, these problems require novel techniques.

The objective of this paper is to more closely study some of the characteristics of OPAs in
H^{p}
. This study originated in
[
12
]
, where OPAs in
L^{p}
(of the unit circle) and
H^{p}
were first considered. Since OPAs were originally created to be implemented in the design of digital filters, it was natural to consider how a change to the
H^{p}
setting would affect the properties of the filter. We continue to explore OPAs in
H^{p}
here,
1<p<\infty
. We note that this exploration is distinctly different from the
p=2
case; in that setting, much is known– thanks in part to the Hilbert space geometry. In
H^{2}
, the orthogonal projection provides a direct connection to many developed areas of analysis, including the theory of reproducing kernels and orthogonal polynomials. We point to
[
9
]
for results in this arena, including lower bounds on the moduli of the root of an OPA (there, it is shown that an OPA cannot vanish inside the closed unit disk).

The outline of the paper is as follows:
in Section
2
, we provide some background and notation, introducing extensions of the Pythagorean inequality, which will later be used in lieu of linearity when
p\neq 2
.
Sections
3
and
4
establish results concerning the coefficients of the constant and linear OPAs associated to a function in
H^{p}
, respectively.
We end this work in Section
5
, with results about the location of OPA roots in two cases—when
f
is an inner function and when
p>2
an even integer.

2.
Notation, Orthogonality, and Pythagorean Inequalities

In this section we establish some notation, review a notion of orthogonality for normed spaces, and present an associated version of the Pythagorean theorem that applies to the
L^{p}
spaces. This extension of the Pythagorean theorem takes the form of a family of inequalities. They will play a central role in our study of OPAs.

Let
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
be vectors belonging to a normed linear space
\mathscr{X}
. We say that
\mathbf{x}
is
orthogonal
to
\mathbf{y}
in the Birkhoff-James sense
[
1
,
18
]
if

\|\mathbf{x}+\beta\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathscr{X}}\geqslant\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathscr{X}}

for all scalars
\beta
.
In this situation we write
\mathbf{x}\perp_{\mathscr{X}}\mathbf{y}
.

There are other ways to generalize orthogonality from an inner product space to a normed space, but this approach is particularly useful since it is based on an extremal condition.

If
\mathscr{X}
is an inner product space, then the relation
\perp_{\mathscr{X}}
coincides with the usual orthogonality relation. In more general spaces, however, the relation
\perp_{\mathscr{X}}
is neither symmetric nor linear. In case
\mathscr{X}=L^{p}(\mu)
, for any measure
\mu
, let us write
\perp_{p}
instead of
\perp_{L^{p}}
, and similarly for
\mathscr{X}=H^{p}
.

There is a practical criterion for
\perp_{p}
when
1<p<\infty
.

Theorem 2.1

(James
[
18
]
)
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
. Then for
f
and
g
belonging to
L^{p}
we have

(2.2)

{f}\perp_{p}{g}\iff\int|f|^{p-2}\overline{f}g\,d\mu=0,

where any occurrence of “
|0|^{p-2}\bar{0}
” in the integrand is interpreted as zero.

In light of (
2.2
) we define, for a complex number
\alpha=re^{i\theta}
, and any
s>0
, the quantity

\alpha^{\langle s\rangle}=(re^{i\theta})^{\langle s\rangle}:=r^{s}e^{-i\theta}.

It is straightforward to verify that for any complex numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
, exponent
s>0
, and integer
n\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha\beta)^{\langle s\rangle}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\langle s\rangle}\beta^{\langle s\rangle}

\displaystyle|\alpha^{\langle s\rangle}|

\displaystyle=|\alpha|^{s}

\displaystyle\alpha^{\langle s\rangle}\alpha

\displaystyle=|\alpha|^{s+1}

\displaystyle(\alpha^{\langle s\rangle})^{n}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{n})^{\langle s\rangle}

\displaystyle(\alpha^{\langle p-1\rangle})^{\langle p^{\prime}-1\rangle}

\displaystyle=\alpha.

By comparison with the case
p=2
, we can think of taking the
{\langle s\rangle}
power as generalizing complex conjugation.

Throughout this paper, the parameter
p
satisfies
1<p<\infty
, and we use
p^{\prime}
to denote the Hölder conjugate of
p
, that is,

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

If
f\in L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, then
f^{\langle p-1\rangle}\in L^{p^{\prime}}
.
Thus, from (
2.2
) we get

f\perp_{p}g\iff\langle g,f^{\langle p-1\rangle}\rangle=0.

Consequently, the relation
\perp_{p}
is linear in its second argument, and it then makes sense to speak of a vector being orthogonal to a subspace of
L^{p}
. In particular, if
f\perp_{p}g
for all
g
belonging to a subspace
\mathscr{M}
of
L^{p}
, then

\|f+g\|_{p}\geqslant\|f\|_{p}

for all
g\in\mathscr{M}
. That is,
f
relates to a nearest-point problem with respect to the subspace
\mathscr{M}
.

Direct calculation will also confirm that

\langle f,f^{\langle p-1\rangle}\rangle=\|f\|^{p}_{p},

and hence
f^{\langle p-1\rangle}/\|f\|^{p-1}_{p}\in L^{p^{\prime}}
is the norming functional of
f\in L^{p}\setminus\{0\}
(smoothness ensures that the norming functional is unique).

In connection with Birkhoff-James Orthogonality, there is a version of the Pythagorean theorem for
L^{p}
.
It takes the form of a family of inequalities relating the lengths of orthogonal vectors with that of their sum.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
x\perp_{p}y
in
L^{p}
.
If
p\in(1,2]
, then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|x\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{1}{2^{p-1}-1}\|y\|^{p}_{p}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}_{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|x\|^{2}_{p}+(p-1)\|y\|^{2}_{p}.

If
p\in[2,\infty)
, then

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|x\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{1}{2^{p-1}-1}\|y\|^{p}_{p}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|x\|^{2}_{p}+(p-1)\|y\|^{2}_{p}.

These inequalities originate from
[
10
,
11
,
13
]
; see
[
14
, Corollary 3.4]
for a unified treatment with broader classes of spaces.

It will sometimes be expedient to refer to (
2.4
) and (
2.7
) as the upper Pythagorean inequalities, and (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) as the lower Pythagorean inequalities, so that the two cases depending on
p
can be handled together. The specific values of the positive multiplicative constants, i.e.,
p-1
and
1/(2^{p-1}-1)
, are generally unimportant, and thus they will usually be denoted simply by
K
, possibly with a subscript.

When
p=2
, these Pythagorean inequalities reduce to the familiar Pythagorean theorem for the Hilbert space
L^{2}
. More generally, these Pythagorean inequalities enable the application of some Hilbert space methods and techniques to smooth Banach spaces satisfying the weak parallelogram laws; see, for example,
[
16
, Proposition 4.8.1 and Proposition 4.8.3; Theorem 8.8.1]
.

Before moving onto results, we recall the notation for the
n
-th optimal polynomial approximant.

Definition 2.8

(OPA)
.

Given a non-negative integer
n
and a function
f\in H^{p}
, the
n
-th optimal polynomial apporoximant
to
1/f
in
H^{p}
is the polynomial solving the minimization problem

\min_{q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\|1-qf\|_{p},

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the set of complex polynomials of degree less than or equal to
n
. This polynomial exists, is unique, will be denoted by

q_{n,p}[f].

3.
The Degree Zero OPA

In this section, we use the Pythagorean inequalities to obtain upper and lower bounds for
q_{0,p}[f]
, the constant OPA. When
p=2
, we can explicitly calculate OPAs with routine Hilbert space (linear algebra) methods. When
p\neq 2
, the Pythagorean formula manifests itself in the form of inequalities. In turn, we trade direct calculation for estimates. We use this updated Pythagorean toolbox to culminate the results in the present section.

From
[
12
, Proposition 5.2]
, we know that
f(0)=0
if and only if
q_{0,p}[f]
vanishes identically. Thus, to avoid the trivial case, we assume that
f(0)\neq 0
.

Fix
1<p<\infty
. For
f\in H^{p}
, let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
be the constant OPA
q_{0,p}[f]
, that is,

\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}=\inf_{c\in\mathbb{C}}\|cf-1\|_{p}.

Let us adopt the following notation for the Pythagorean inequalities: when
f\perp_{p}g
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{p}^{r}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|f\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|g\|_{p}^{r}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{p}^{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|g\|_{p}^{s}.

Remark
: If
g
is small compared to
f
, then (
3.2
) is sharper than the triangle inequality.

With that, here is an upper bound for
|q_{0,p}[f]|
, in terms of
f
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and suppose that
f\in H^{p}
satisfies
f(0)\neq 0
. If
\lambda=q_{0,p}[f]
, then

|\lambda|\leqslant\frac{1}{K_{1}^{1/r}\big[\|f-f(0)\|_{p}^{r}+\|f\|_{p}^{r}\big]^{1/r}},

where
r
and
K_{1}
are the lower Pythagorean parameters.

Proof.

First note that

1\perp_{p}z^{k}

for all
k\geqslant 1
, due to
\int|1|^{p-2}\bar{1}e^{ik\theta}\,dm=0
. Thus for any
f\in H^{p}
,

\lambda f(0)-1\ \ \perp_{p}\ \ \lambda(f-f(0)).

The lower Pythagorean inequality, (
3.1
), gives

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{r}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|\lambda f(0)-1\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}

Next, by the extremal property of
\lambda
, we also have

\lambda f-1\ \perp_{p}\ \lambda f.

This yields

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|1\|_{p}^{r}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{r}

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) takes us to

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geqslant\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{r}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\geqslant\|\lambda f(0)-1\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{r}.

Drop the first term on the right, and isolate
|\lambda|
, resulting in the following bound:

|\lambda|\leqslant\frac{1}{K_{1}^{1/r}\big[\|f-f(0)\|_{p}^{r}+\|f\|_{p}^{r}\big]^{1/r}}.

∎

We may extract a sharper bound by estimating the first term of (
3.6
), rather than dropping it.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and suppose that
f\in H^{p}
satisfies
f(0)\neq 0
. If
\lambda=q_{0,p}[f]
, then

(3.8)

|\lambda|^{r-1}\leqslant\frac{r|f(0)|}{K_{1}\|(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|f\|_{p}^{r}},

where
r
and
K_{1}
are the lower Pythagorean parameters.

Proof.

Starting from (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geqslant\|\lambda f(0)-1\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{r}

\displaystyle 1-\|\lambda f(0)-1\|_{p}^{r}

\displaystyle\geqslant+K_{1}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{r}

\displaystyle r\cdot 1^{r-1}(1-\|\lambda f(0)-1\|_{p})

\displaystyle\geqslant+K_{1}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{r}

\displaystyle r|\lambda f(0)|

\displaystyle\geqslant+K_{1}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{r},

in which we have employed the elementary inequality

\frac{b^{r}-a^{r}}{b-a}=rc^{r-1}\leqslant rb^{r-1},

for
a<b
and some
c\in(a,b)
, arising from the mean value theorem. We thus obtain

|\lambda|^{r-1}\leqslant\frac{r|f(0)|}{K_{1}\|(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|f\|_{p}^{r}}.

∎

The other Pythagorean inequality enables a lower bound for
|q_{0,p}[f]|
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and suppose that
f\in H^{p}
satisfies
f(0)\neq 0
. If
\lambda=q_{0,p}[f]
, then
for any constant
c
we have

|\lambda|^{s}\geqslant\frac{1-\|cf-1\|_{p}^{s}}{K_{2}\big(\|f-f(0)\|_{p}^{s}+\|f\|_{p}^{s}\big)},

where
s
and
K_{2}
are the upper Pythagorean parameters.

Proof.

The upper Pythagorean inequality (
3.2
) provides that

\displaystyle\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\lambda f(0)-1\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{s}

\displaystyle\|1\|_{p}^{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{s},

where
s
and
K_{2}
are the upper Pythagorean parameters.

Combining these yields that

1\leqslant\|\lambda f(0)-1\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{s}.

Apply (
3.4
) to get

1\leqslant\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{s}.

Replacing
\lambda
with
c
makes the first term at least as large, so we obtain

1\leqslant\|cf-1\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda(f-f(0))\|_{p}^{s}+K_{2}\|\lambda f\|_{p}^{s},

from which the claim follows.
∎

In particular, we could choose
c=1/f(0)
, and then this says

|\lambda|^{s}\geqslant\frac{1-\|(f-f(0))/f(0)\|_{p}^{s}}{K_{2}\big(\|f-f(0)\|_{p}^{s}+\|f\|_{p}^{s}\big)}.

Evidently this result has value only when
f
has relatively little mass outside of its constant term.

4.
The Degree One OPA

As seen in
[
12
]
, it can be quite difficult to calculate OPAs for
p\neq 2
. However, if one were to discover an efficient way to compute the coefficients, then the
H^{p}
setting could have use in the design of digital filters. The reason for this is because the zeros would have a different configuration, and thus the filter would amplify/attenuate different frequencies compared to the
p=2
case. Of course, the utility of the filter would rest on whether or not it is stable (i.e., zero-free in
\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. Therefore, it seems sensible to start by providing estimates for the zeros. Again, we note that by using the Pythagorean inequalities, we swap direct calculation for estimates. By doing so, we obtain expressions and bounds for the linear OPA root and leading coefficient. The reason for considering the linear case is due to the following reduction.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
w
is the root of some OPA in
H^{p}
if and only if
w
is the root of some optimal linear approximant in
H^{p}
.

Proof.

Let
Q
be the OPA of degree up to
n
for some
1/f
, with
f\in H^{p}
and
Q(w)=0
. Then
Q(z)=(a+bz)Q_{0}(z)
for some
Q_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{n-1}
, where
-a/b=w
. Then

\displaystyle Qf-1\ \

\displaystyle\perp_{H^{p}}\ \ \mathscr{P}_{n}f\ \ \ \implies

\displaystyle(a+bz)Q_{0}(z)f(z)-1\ \

\displaystyle\perp_{H^{p}}\ \ \mathscr{P}_{1}Q_{0}f\ \ \ \implies

a+bz
is the optimal linear approximant to
1/(Q_{0}f)
.
The converse is trivial.
∎

If we are looking for bounds for the roots of OPAs, we may therefore restrict our attention to the linear ones.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
, and
w\in\mathbb{D}
.
Let
D_{w}
be the difference-quotient operator

(D_{w}f)(z):=\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{z-w},

for all
f\in H^{p}
.

Recall that

D_{w}=B(I-wB)^{-1},

and consequently

\|D_{w}\|\leqslant\frac{1}{1-|w|}.

Here is a bound for the root of a linear polynomial, based on boundedness of the difference-quotient.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
f\in H^{p}
, and suppose

Q(z)=Q_{0}\Big(1-\frac{z}{z_{0}}\Big)

for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
and
Q_{0}\neq 0
. Then

|z_{0}|\geqslant\frac{\|Q_{0}f\|_{p}}{\|Q_{0}f\|_{p}+\|Qf-1\|_{p}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\big(D_{z_{0}}(Qf-1)\big)(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{[Q(z)f(z)-1]-[Q(z_{0})f(z_{0})-1]}{z-z_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q(z)f(z)}{z-z_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{0}(z_{0}-z)f(z)}{z_{0}(z-z_{0})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Q_{0}f(z)}{z_{0}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\big(D_{z_{0}}(Qf-1)\big)(z)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|D_{z_{0}}\|\|Qf-1\|_{p}

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{Q_{0}f(z)}{z_{0}}\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-|z_{0}|}\|Qf-1\|_{p},

from which the claim follows.
∎

Note that the statement and proof of Proposition
4.2
make no use of the extremal nature of
q_{1,p}[f]
, and hence the conclusion holds for any linear polynomial
Q
.

Next we apply the Pythagorean inequalities to derive upper and lower bounds for the OPA root.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and suppose that
f\in H^{p}
with
f(0)\neq 0
. If
Q(z)=a(z-w)=q_{1,p}[f]
, then

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{2}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}}{K_{1}\|af\|_{p}^{2}}\Bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geqslant|w|\geqslant\Bigg(\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{p}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{p}}{K_{2}\|af\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigg)^{1/p},\ \,1<p<2

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{2}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}}{K_{2}\|af\|_{p}^{2}}\Bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant|w|\leqslant\Bigg(\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{p}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{p}}{K_{1}\|af\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigg)^{1/p},\ \,2<p<\infty,

where
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are respectively the lower and upper Pythagorean constants.

Proof.

If
2<p<\infty
, then the Pythagorean inequalities are

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|x\|^{p}_{p}+K_{1}\|y\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|x\|^{2}_{p}+K_{2}\|y\|^{2}_{p}.

The condition

Qf-1=a(z-w)f-1\ \ \perp_{p}\ \ f

then enables us to write

\displaystyle azf-1

\displaystyle=azf-awf-1+awf

\displaystyle\|azf-1\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|Qf-1\|^{p}_{p}+K_{1}\|waf\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle|w|^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{p}-\|Qf-1\|^{p}_{p}}{K_{1}\|af\|_{p}^{p}},

and

\displaystyle\|azf-1\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|Qf-1\|^{2}_{p}+K_{2}\|awf\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle|w|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{2}-\|Qf-1\|^{2}_{p}}{K_{2}\|af\|_{p}^{2}}.

Hence we have

\Bigg(\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{2}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}}{K_{2}\|af\|_{p}^{2}}\Bigg)^{1/2}\leqslant|w|\leqslant\Bigg(\frac{\|azf-1\|_{p}^{p}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{p}}{K_{1}\|af\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigg)^{1/p}.

The steps are similar when
1<p<2
.
∎

These bounds are limited by the fact that they depend on the OPA itself. Nonetheless, we will see that such results have their uses.

Here follows a bound for the coefficient
a
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and suppose that
f\in H^{p}
with
f(0)\neq 0
. If
q_{1,p}[f]=a(z-w)
,
then

|a|^{r-1}\leqslant\frac{r|wf(0)|}{K_{1}\|(z-w)f(z)+wf(0)\|_{p}^{r}+K_{1}\|(z-w)f(z)\|_{p}^{r}},

where
r
and
K_{1}
are the lower Pythagorean parameters.

Proof.

Notice that
a=q_{0,p}[(z-w)f]
. Thus the claim follows from (
3.8
), being that

(z-w)f(z)
takes the value
-wf(0)
when
z=0
.
∎

We close this section with some formulas for the OPA root
w
and coefficient
a
, made possible by the extremal property of the OPA, and the associated orthogonality relations. These will be useful when we seek bounds for
w
in the concluding section.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and suppose that
f\in H^{p}
satisfies
\|f\|_{p}=1
and
f(0)\neq 0
. Let
Q(z)=q_{1,p}[f](z)=a(z-w)
, and define

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\int|Qf-1|^{p-2}\bar{f}\,dm

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int|Qf-1|^{p-2}\overline{zf}\,dm

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\int|Qf-1|^{p-2}|f|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\int|Qf-1|^{p-2}\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm,

provided that the integrals exist.

Then we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\frac{B\overline{D}-AC}{BC-AD}

(4.7)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\frac{a\bar{D}-A}{aC}

(4.8)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\frac{aC-B}{aD}

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{B-wA}{2C-w\bar{D}-\bar{w}D}

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{AD-BC}{|D|^{2}-C^{2}}.

Note: These formulas always hold if
2<p<\infty
, and hold for
1<p<2
as well if
\|Qf\|_{\infty}<1
.

Proof.

Use the orthogonality properties of
Q
, along with Theorem
2.1
.

Suppose that
q_{1,p}[f]=a(z-w)
. The associated orthogonality equations tell us that

\displaystyle\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}(1-Qf)\overline{f}\,dm

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}(1-Qf)\overline{zf}\,dm

\displaystyle=0.

Providing that the separate integrals exit, we can rearrange these to get

\displaystyle\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}f\,dm

\displaystyle=-\overline{aw}\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}|f|^{2}\,dm+\bar{a}\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}zf\,dm

\displaystyle=-\overline{aw}\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}z|f|^{2}\,dm+\bar{a}\int|1-Qf|^{p-2}|f|^{2}\,dm.

This linear system can be solved for
a
and
w
. The result is

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-aw\\
a\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C&\bar{D}\\
D&C\end{array}\right]^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}A\\
B\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|C|^{2}+|D|^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{rr}C&-\bar{D}\\
-D&C\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}A\\
B\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|C|^{2}+|D|^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}AC-B\bar{D}\\
BC-AD\end{array}\right].

The formula (
4.6
) then derives from

w=-\frac{-aw}{a}=-\frac{(AC-B\bar{D})/(|C|^{2}+|D|^{2})}{(BC-AD)/(|C|^{2}+|D|^{2})},

and the rest is similar.
∎

5.
OPA Roots Are Bounded From Zero

When
p=2
, the root of a non-trivial linear OPA must lie outside the unit disk
[
9
]
. When
p\neq 2
, we are able to show that in two special cases the root of a linear OPA must be bounded from the origin. We suspect that this is true in general, but this seems beyond the reach of our present methods. We start with the following observation.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that
f
is a cyclic function in
H^{p}
with
f(0)\neq 0
, and
\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of non-negative integers. If
\{z_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of numbers in
\mathbb{D}
such that
q_{n_{k},p}[f](z_{n_{k}})=0
, then
|z_{n_{k}}|\rightarrow 1
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

From
[
12
, Proposition 5.1]
, we have that

\sqrt{1-\|q_{n,k}[f]f-1\|_{p}^{p}}\leqslant|z_{n_{k}}|\leqslant 1

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, since
f
is cyclic in
H^{p}
, it follows that
\|q_{n_{k},p}[f]f-1\|_{p}^{p}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, the result follows.
∎

Next, we list the two main results.

Theorem
5.13
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
.
Let
f\in H^{p}
be an inner function such that
f(0)\neq 0
. There exists a constant
\rho>0
, depending only on
p
, such that if
q_{1,p}[f](z)=a(z-w)
, then
|w|\geqslant\rho
.

Theorem
5.16
.

Suppose that
p>2
is an even integer. Let
f\in H^{p}
satisfy
f(0)\neq 0
. There exists
\rho>0
, depending only on
p
, such that if
q_{1,p}[f](z)=a(z-w)
, then
|w|\geqslant\rho
.

Both of the above theorems will be established via proofs by contradiction. We will therefore assume, for the sake of argument, that there is a sequence of functions
f_{k}\in H^{p}
such that
\|f_{k}\|_{p}=1
and
f_{k}(0)\neq 0
for each

, for which the respective linear OPAs
q_{1,p}[f_{k}]=a_{k}(z-w_{k})
satisfy

. We will show, in a set of lemmas, that the corresponding parameters
\lambda_{k}=q_{0,p}[f_{k}]
,

, and

must also tend toward zero at certain rates. Those rates will turn out to be incommensurate. This contradiction will show that the OPA roots

cannot be made arbitrarily close to zero.

For ease of reading we will suppress the index

in the above structures, and instead bear in mind throughout that
f(0)
,
\lambda
,

, and

are dependent on
f
.
Since the OPA root is independent from rescaling

, there is no harm in assuming that
\|f\|_{p}=1
.

In what follows, we will also employ the binomial approximation in the forms

\displaystyle(1+|x|)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1+(\alpha/2)|x|

\displaystyle(1-|x|)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1-(\alpha/2)|x|

\displaystyle(1-|x|)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-(2\alpha)|x|

for
|x|
sufficiently small. The factors of 2 are intended to absorb the error, which is on the order of
|\alpha x|^{2}
.

The first lemma arises from boundedness of the difference-quotient operator on

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and let
f\in H^{p}
with
f(0)\neq 0
. If
0<|w|<1
, and
Q(z)=a(z-w)
, then

\|Qf\|_{p}\geqslant(1-|w|)|a|\|f\|_{p}.

Proof.

Consider again the difference-quotient operator
D_{w}
in
H^{p}
.

\displaystyle D_{w}(Qf)

\displaystyle=\frac{a(z-w)f-a(w-w)f(w)}{z-w}

\displaystyle=\frac{a(z-w)f-0}{z-w}

\displaystyle=af

\displaystyle\|D_{w}\|\|Qf\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|af\|_{p}

\displaystyle\|Qf\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant(1-|w|)\|af\|_{p}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=(1-|w|)|a|\|f\|_{p}.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
p\neq 2
, and let
f\in H^{p}
with
f(0)\neq 0
. If
Q(z)=q_{1,p}[f](z)=a(z-w)
, then

\displaystyle|w|^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant(pK_{1}/K_{2})|a|^{2-p}\ \,\ 1<p<2

\displaystyle|w|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant(pK_{1}/4K_{2})|a|^{p-2},\ \,\ 2<p<\infty

for
|w|
sufficiently small.

Proof.

Let us proceed with the case
2<p<\infty
, the other case being similar.
From the relation
Qf-1\perp_{p}Qf
, the lower Pythagorean inequality yields

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=-(Qf-1)+Qf

(5.5)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geqslant\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{p}+K_{1}\|Qf\|_{p}^{p}.

Next, from
[
12
, Proposition 5.1]
, we know that
|w|^{2}\geqslant 1-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{p}
. Combining this with (

) results in the bound

\displaystyle|w|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-(1-K_{1}\|Qf\|_{p}^{p})^{2/p}.

By the hypothesis that

can be made small, this forces

to be correspondingly small, so now the binomial approximation allows for

\displaystyle|w|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{K_{1}}{p}\|Qf\|_{p}^{p}.

We extend this further by utilizing Lemma

|w|^{2}\geqslant(K_{1}/p)\|Qf\|_{p}^{p}\geqslant(K_{1}/p)(1-|w|)^{p}|a|^{p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

This shows that

Finally, start with the upper Pythagorean inequality again, then use the Lower Pythagorean inequality, the binomial approximation, and (

) to get

\displaystyle\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}+K_{2}\|awf\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|azf+1\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle|aw|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\|azf+1\|_{p}^{2}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}}{K_{2}\|f\|_{p}^{2}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{(1+K_{1}\|azf\|_{p}^{p})^{2/p}-1}{K_{2}\|f\|_{p}^{2}}

(5.7)

\displaystyle|aw|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{K_{1}}{pK_{2}\|f\|_{p}^{2}}|a|^{p},

for

sufficiently close to zero. Notice that to reach (

1\perp_{p}azf.

Dividing through (
5.7
) by
|a|^{2}
yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose

,

,

,

\displaystyle(4K_{2}/p)|a|^{p}(1+|w|)^{p}

\displaystyle pK_{2}|a|^{2}(1+|w|)^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}|\lambda|^{p},\ \,2<p<\infty.

Proof.

Once again, let us treat the case

in detail. The upper Pythagorean inequality gives

\displaystyle Qf-1

\displaystyle=azf-awf-1

\displaystyle awf+1

\displaystyle=-(Qf-1)\oplus_{p}(azf)

\displaystyle\|awf+1\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}+K_{2}\|azf\|_{p}^{2}.

Note

\displaystyle K_{2}|a|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|awf+1\|_{p}^{2}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{2}-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant 0.

If we assume, for the sake of argument, that

goes to zero.

Next,

\displaystyle\lambda f-1

\displaystyle\perp_{p}\lambda f

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=-(\lambda f-1)\oplus_{p}\lambda f

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geqslant\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{p}+K_{1}|\lambda|^{p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle 1-\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}|\lambda|^{p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle 1-\|Qf-1\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}|\lambda|^{p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle 1-(1-K_{2}\|Qf\|_{p}^{2})^{p/2}

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}|\lambda|^{p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle(pK_{2})\|Qf\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}|\lambda|^{p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle(pK_{2})|a|^{2}(1+|w|)^{2}\|f\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}|\lambda|^{p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle(pK_{2})|a|^{2}(1+|w|)^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}|\lambda|^{p}

when

is small.

The case

is handled analogously.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Suppose

\displaystyle\leqslant C|\lambda|,\ \,2<p<\infty

.

Proof.

Yet again let us take

.

. Then use

\displaystyle\|\lambda f-1\|_{p}\leqslant\|cf-1\|_{p}

.

∎

Lemma 5.11

Suppose

For the second inequality, start with

Theorem 5.13
.

Suppose that

.
Let

be an inner function such that

. There exists a constant
\rho>0
, depending only on

, such that if

, then

Proof.

The proof will be by contradiction. Accordingly, we assume that by choice of

In the notation of Theorem

To evaluate this formula for

and

The condition

\displaystyle\Big|\int(Qf)^{j}(\overline{Qf})^{k}z^{m}f^{n}\,dm\Big|

(5.14)

\displaystyle|Qf-1|^{p-2}

\displaystyle=1-razf+rawf-r\overline{azf}+r\overline{awf}+O(|a|^{2})

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=-ra+O(|a|^{2})

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=1+rawf(0)+r\overline{awf(0)}+O(|a|^{2})

\displaystyle=-r{af(0)}+r{aw\hat{f}_{1}}+O(|a|^{2}),

Remark 5.17
.

In the following proof, we present the case

is an even integer.

Proof.

As we proceed, there is no harm in assuming that

.
The proof will be by contradiction. To that end, suppose that we have a sequence of selections for

satisfying the hypotheses, for which the corresponding values of

tend toward zero. For visual clarity we suppress the index. We have previously established that the associated values of

and

Write

Again, let us use the integral expressions

,

,

and

and associated formulas from Theorem

By taking integrals against

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\overline{f(0)}-\int\bar{f}Qf\,dm-\int\overline{f^{2}Q}\,dm+\int\bar{f}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle=\int\overline{zf}\,dm-\int\overline{zf}Qf\,dm-\int\overline{zf^{2}Q}\,dm+\int\overline{zf}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle=0-\int\overline{zf}Qf\,dm-0+\int\overline{zf}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle=-a\int|f|^{2}\,dm+aw\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm+\int\overline{zf}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\int|f|^{2}\,dm-\int Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-\int\overline{Qf}|f|^{2}\,dm+\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-\int\bar{z}Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-\int\overline{zQf}|f|^{2}\,dm+\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm.

Notice that in the final expression for

, one of the integrals was broken out into two terms, namely

\int\overline{zf}Qf\,dm=a\int|f|^{2}\,dm-aw\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}.

Next, we substitute the resulting expressions into the formula

from (

),
with the result

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{a\int|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{Qf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\qquad-\bigg[-a\int|f|^{2}\,dm+aw\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm+\int\overline{zf}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg\{a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\bar{z}Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{zQf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{2a\int|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{Qf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\qquad-aw\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-\int\overline{zf}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg\{a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\bar{z}Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{zQf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg\}.

Next, we bound
|w|
from below using the triangle inequalities for sums and integrals. This yields

\displaystyle|w|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|2a\int|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{Qf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\qquad-aw\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-\int\overline{zf}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg|a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\bar{z}Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{zQf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg|

\displaystyle\geqslant\Bigg\{2|a|\int|f|^{2}\,dm-\bigg[|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\qquad+|aw|\int|f|^{2}\,dm+\int|f||Qf|^{2}\,dm\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg\{|a|\int|f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg\}

Apply the following estimates

\displaystyle|Q(z)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|a|(1+|w|)\leqslant 2|a|

\displaystyle\int|f|^{3}\,dm

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\int|f|^{2}\,dm\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int|f|^{4}\,dm\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\|f\|_{2}\|f\|_{4}^{2}=\|f\|_{2},

with occurrences of
\int|f|^{2}\,dm
recorded as
\|f\|_{2}^{2}
. The bounds continue with

\displaystyle|w|

\displaystyle\geqslant\Bigg\{2|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}-\bigg[2|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+2|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}+|aw|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+4|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg\{|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+2|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+2|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{2|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}-\bigg[8|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}+|aw|\|f\|_{2}^{2}\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg\{|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+4|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}\Bigg\}.

This implies

\displaystyle|aw|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+4|a|^{2}|w|\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}|w|+8|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}+|aw|\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant 2|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}

(5.18)

\displaystyle 2|a|^{2}(2+|w|)\|f\|_{2}+2|a|^{3}(1+|w|)

\displaystyle\geqslant|a|(1-|w|)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

By solving the last inequality we find that

\|f\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{2|a|^{2}(2+|w|)+\sqrt{4|a|^{4}(2+|w|)^{2}+8|a|^{4}(1-|w|^{2})}}{2|a|(1-|w|)}.

In particular, this must be true in the limit as
|w|
tends toward zero (through which the right hand side decreases), so we get

(5.19)

\|f\|_{2}\leqslant C|a|,

where we can take
C=(2+\sqrt{6})
.

Let us set (
5.19
) aside for now, and perform the analogous calculation starting with the formula

w=\frac{a\bar{D}-A}{aC},

also from Theorem
4.5
.

The result is

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\bar{z}Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{zQf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\qquad-\bigg[\overline{f(0)}-\int\bar{f}Qf\,dm-\int\overline{f^{2}Q}\,dm+\int\bar{f}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg\{a\int|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{Qf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg\}

\displaystyle|w|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\bar{z}Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{zQf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int\bar{z}|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\qquad-\bigg[\overline{f(0)}-\int\bar{f}Qf\,dm-\int\overline{f^{2}Q}\,dm+\int\bar{f}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\bigg]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg|a\int|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int Qf|f|^{2}\,dm-a\int\overline{Qf}|f|^{2}\,dm+a\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg|

\displaystyle\geqslant\Bigg\{|{f(0)}|-\bigg[|a|\int|f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\qquad+\int|f||Qf|\,dm+\int|f^{2}Q|\,dm+\int|f||Qf|^{2}\,dm\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\div\Bigg\{|a|\int|f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|Qf||f|^{2}\,dm+|a|\int|f|^{2}|Qf|^{2}\,dm\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\Bigg\{|{f(0)}|-\bigg[5|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+8|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}\bigg]\Bigg\}\div\Bigg\{|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+4|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}\Bigg\}.

This yields

(5.20)

|aw|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+4|a|^{2}|w|\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}|w|+5|a|\|f\|_{2}^{2}+8|a|^{2}\|f\|_{2}+4|a|^{3}\geqslant|f(0)|

Now apply (
5.19
), to replace the left side with a larger bound:

\displaystyle|aw|C^{2}|a|^{2}+4|a|^{2}|w|C|a|+4|a|^{3}|w|+5|a|C^{2}|a|^{2}+8|a|^{2}C|a|+4|a|^{3}\geqslant|f(0)|.

The conclusion is that

(5.21)

|f(0)|\leqslant C^{\prime}|a|^{3},

where, with
|w|\leqslant 1
, we can take

Finally, combine (
5.21
) with Lemma

, to the effect that

C^{\prime}|a^{3}w|\geqslant|wf(0)|\geqslant C_{1}|a|^{3}.

for some

.

But then, this implies that

|w|\geqslant\frac{C_{1}}{C^{\prime}},

contradicting the assumption that

can be made arbitrarily small.
∎

This proves Theorem

when

.
Our aim now is to extend this argument to the general case, in which
p
is any even integer exceeding four. Before doing so, we need two lemmas. The key observation for this extension is contained in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.22
.

Let
2<p<\infty
, and suppose that
2\leqslant k<p
. Then for any
f\in H^{p}
with
\|f\|_{p}=1
, we have

\int|f|^{k}\,dm\leqslant\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-k},

where
\varphi=|f|^{2/(p-2)}
.

Proof.

For
k=2
the claim follows from

\int|f|^{2}\,dm=\int\varphi^{p-2}\,dm=\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-2}.

Next, let
2<k<p
. From

\frac{p-k}{p-2}+\frac{k-2}{p-2}=1

we see that
(p-2)/(p-k)
and
(p-2)/(k-2)
are Hölder conjugates to each other.

Notice also that

\bigg[k-\frac{2(p-k)}{p-2}\bigg]\frac{p-2}{k-2}=\bigg[\frac{pk-2k-2p+2k}{p-2}\bigg]\frac{p-2}{k-2}=p.

We now apply Hölder’s inequality, resulting in

\displaystyle\int|f|^{k}\,dm

\displaystyle=\int|f|^{2(p-k)/(p-2)}|f|^{k-2(p-k)/(p-2)}\,dm

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\int|f|^{[2(p-k)/(p-2)]\cdot[(p-2)/(p-k)]}\,dm\Big)^{(p-k)/(p-2)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\int|f|^{[k-2(p-k)/(p-2)]\cdot[(p-2)/(k-2)]}\,dm\Big)^{(k-2)/(p-2)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg\{\Big(\int|f|^{[2/(p-2)]\cdot(p-2)}\,dm\Big)^{1/(p-2)}\bigg\}^{p-k}\Big(\int|f|^{p}\,dm\Big)^{(k-2)/(p-2)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg\{\Big(\int|\varphi|^{p-2}\,dm\Big)^{1/(p-2)}\bigg\}^{p-k}\bigg\{\Big(\int|f|^{p}\,dm\Big)^{1/p}\bigg\}^{p(k-2)/(p-2)}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-k}\|f\|_{p}^{p(k-2)/(p-2)}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-k}.

∎

In addition to the lemma above, we’ll also need the following elementary fact.

Lemma 5.23
.

Suppose that
n
is an integer greater than 2.
Let
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k}\}
be positive sequences, and suppose that for some
a_{0}>0
and nonnegative constants
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
we have

a_{0}x_{k}^{n}+a_{1}x_{k}^{n-1}y_{k}+a_{2}x_{k}^{n-2}y_{k}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n-1}x_{k}y_{k}^{n-1}\geqslant y_{k}^{n},\ \,\forall k.

Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

Cx_{k}\geqslant y_{k},\ \,\forall k.

Proof.

First, by selecting possibly larger values of the constants
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
, we can find
C^{\prime}>0
such that

C^{\prime}x_{k}(x_{k}^{n-1}+y_{k}^{n-1})\geqslant y_{k}^{n},\ \,\forall k.

For those indices
k
satisfying
x_{k}\geqslant y_{k}
, we have

2C^{\prime}x_{k}^{n}\geqslant C^{\prime}x_{k}(x_{k}^{n-1}+y_{k}^{n-1})\geqslant y_{k}^{n}.

This implies

(2C^{\prime})^{1/n}x_{k}\geqslant y_{k}

for such
k
.

For the remaining indices
k
, in which
x_{k}<y_{k}
, we have

2C^{\prime}x_{k}y_{k}^{n-1}\geqslant C^{\prime}x_{k}(x_{k}^{n-1}+y_{k}^{n-1})\geqslant y_{k}^{n}.

This implies

2C^{\prime}x_{k}\geqslant y_{k}

for such
k
.

Thus, the claim holds, with
C=(2C^{\prime})^{1/n}+2C^{\prime}
.
∎

We are now equipped to complete the full proof of Theorem
5.16
.

Proof.

(Theorem
5.16
,
p>4
an even integer).

We begin by following the calculations from the
p=4
case, using estimates of the form

\int|f|^{j}|Qf|^{k}\,dm\leqslant|2a|^{k}\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-j-k},

arising from Lemma
5.22
.

During these calculations, the quantity
\|\varphi\|_{p-2}
takes over the role of
\|f\|_{2}
from the
p=4
case. (In fact, when
p=4
, we have

\|\varphi\|_{p-2}=\Big(\int|\varphi|^{p-2}\,dm\Big)^{1/(p-2)}=\Big(\int|f|^{2}\,dm\Big)^{1/2}=\|f\|_{2}.)

The outcome analogous to (

) is that

b_{0}|a|^{p-1}+b_{1}|a|^{p-2}\|\varphi\|_{p-2}+b_{2}|a|^{p-3}\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{2}+\cdots+b_{p-2}|a|\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-2}\geqslant\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-1},

for some positive constants

,
b_{1}
,…,

.

As a result of Lemma

, there is a positive constant
C
such that

\|\varphi\|_{p-2}\leqslant C|a|.

Analogous to (

), we have

B_{1}(1+|w|)|a|\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-2}+B_{2}(1+|w|)|a|^{2}\|\varphi\|_{p-2}^{p-3}+\cdots+B_{p-1}(1+|w|)|a|^{p-1}\geqslant|f(0)|,

for some positive constants

. As a consequence,

Once again, we invoke Lemma

. The end result is

which implies

contradicting the assumption that

∎

On Continuous Full-Order Integral-Terminal Sliding Mode Control with Unknown Apriori Bound on Uncertainty
Jit Koley a , Dinesh Patra b and Binoy Krishna Roy c

Abstract
This study aims at providing a solution to the problem of designing a continuous and finite-time control for a class of nonlinear systems in the presence of matched uncertainty with an unknown apriori bound. First, we propose a Full-Order Integral-Terminal Sliding Manifold (FOITSM) with a conventional (discontinuous) sliding mode to show that it provides the combined attributes of the nonsingular terminal and integral sliding mode algorithms. Secondly, an Adaptive Disturbance Observer (ADO) has been designed to alleviate the effect of the uncertainty acting on the system. On application of the ADO-based Full-Order Integral-Terminal Sliding Mode Control (FOITSMC), the chattering phenomenon in control input has been reduced substantially in the presence of conditionally known matched disturbances. Moreover, the adaptive gains of ADO are updated non-monotonically without over-bounding the acting disturbance, yet sustain the global boundedness of state trajectories within a specific bound.

Integral sliding mode control, Terminal sliding mode control, Adaptive disturbance observer, Chattering-free control

1
Introduction

1.1
Literature survey

Initiation of control amidst uncertainties has been one of the main topics of concern for the past few decades. Sliding Mode Control (SMC)
(
Utkin 1977
;
Edwards and Spurgeon 1998
;
Utkin et al. 1999
;
Bloch and Drakunov 1996
;
Kwan and Yeung 1993
;
Bartolini and Zolezzi 1993
)
is one of the control strategies that converges the state trajectories to a predetermined manifold in finite time and thereby constrained to evolve in that manifold. As a consequence, the time evolution of state trajectories becomes insensitive to external disturbances converge to the origin asymptotically (Conventional sliding mode
(
Shtessel et al. 2014
)
) or in a finite time (Terminal sliding mode
(
Venkataraman and Gulati 1993
;
Feng et al. 2002
;
Chen and Lin 2010
;
Feng et al. 2013
;
Yang et al. 2013
;
Kamal et al. 2016
)
). Manifestation of such control algorithms requires a high frequency switching leading to an undesired phenomenon called ”chattering”. An SMC generally composed of two phases; the
reaching phase
, the state trajectories are forced to reach the manifold from its initial condition, and the
sliding phase
, the state trajectories are compelled to remain invariant to that manifold.

In contrast to a conventional linear sliding mode (LSM), terminal sliding mode control (TSMC) algorithm converges states on finite time and the integral sliding mode (ISM) (
(
Utkin and Shi 1996
;
Pan et al. 2017
;
Rubagotti et al. 2011
;
Chalanga et al. 2013
;
Cui et al. 2017
;
Xiong et al. 2019
)
) is devoid of any reaching phase because it ensures the invariance of state trajectories from the initial instant, and hence, guarantees robustness from its inception. In all these algorithms, some partial knowledge about the acting external disturbances, preferably the upper bound of the disturbances, needs to be known. The apriori knowledge of the matched disturbances is anonymous in several practical applications or bound to differ with time. In pursuit to alleviate this barrier, several adaptive sliding mode control (ASMC) algorithms have been proposed. Among many possibilities of adaptation, monotonically increasing the gain
(
Su and Stepanenko 1994
;
Chang 2009
;
Nasiri et al. 2014
;
Negrete-Chávez and Moreno 2016
)
associated with the signum structure of the control algorithm may result in high gain
(
Plestan et al. 2010
)
. In a recent development
(
Roy et al. 2020
)
, a novel ASMC technique is proposed which doesn’t require a known apriori bound on the disturbance and ensures uniformly ultimate boundedness of state trajectories.

Several approaches are available in the literature regarding continuous adaptive SMC algorithms, preferably adaptive super-twisting control (STC) algorithms (
Moreno and Osorio 2012
;
Levant 2003
). All these approaches can broadly be classified into two main categories. The first category
(
Plestan et al. 2010
;
Taleb et al. 2013
;
Alwi and Edwards 2013
;
Bartolini et al. 2013
)
involves increasing the gain of the STC until the second-order sliding (2SM) is obtained; once 2SM has been obtained, adaptive gains stop evolving and thereby, remain constant. The second category
Utkin and Poznyak 2013
;
Edwards and Shtessel 2014
;
Edwards and Shtessel 2016
comprises the development of adaptive algorithms with minimum gain required to achieve 2SM and thereby, ensuring that there is no unnecessary overestimation of adaptive gains while ensuring sliding. This category has some advantages over the previous one- specifically from a chattering mitigation viewpoint. To the best of authors’ knowledge, the number of research articles from a gain minimization perspective is still considerably less and our study aims in this direction using a Lyapunov based analysis of closed-loop stability to global uniformly ultimately boundedness (GUUB) of state trajectories and estimation errors.

Contributions

In this manuscript, our main contributions are as follows.

6
Conclusion and outlook

This paper provides an algorithm for GUUB of state trajectories in presence of disturbances with unknown bound. Further, a sliding manifold is designed which retains the properties of both ITSMC and nonsingular TSMC. Lyapunov-based stability analysis of the closed-loop system for the proposed approach is provided considering the ADO dynamics into account. As a result, the monotonically increasing nature of adaptive gain has been eliminated.

One of the directions for future work would be designing an observer-based controller for the same and maybe to determine a more stringent bound on states and errors.

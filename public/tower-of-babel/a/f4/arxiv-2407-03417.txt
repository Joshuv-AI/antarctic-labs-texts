Unifying Floquet theory of longitudinal and dispersive readout
A. Chessari, E. A. Rodríguez-Mena, J. C. Abadillo-Uriel, V. Champain, S. Zihlmann, R. Maurand, Y.-M. Niquet, M. Filippone

Abstract
We devise a Floquet theory of longitudinal and dispersive readout in circuit QED. By studying qubits coupled to cavity photons and driven at the resonance frequency of the cavity ω r \omega_{\rm r} , we establish a universal connection between the qubit AC Stark shift and the longitudinal and dispersive coupling to photons. We find that the longitudinal coupling g ∥ g_{\parallel} is controlled by the slope of the AC Stark shift as function of the driving strength A q A_{\rm q} , while the dispersive shift χ \chi depends on its curvature. The two quantities become proportional to each other in the weak drive limit ( A q → 0 A_{\rm q}\rightarrow 0 ). Our approach unifies the adiabatic limit ( ω r → 0 \omega_{\rm r}\rightarrow 0 ) – where g ∥ g_{\parallel} is generated by the static spectrum curvature (or quantum capacitance) – with the diabatic one, where the static spectrum plays no role. We derive analytical results supported by exact numerical simulations. We apply them to superconducting and spin-hybrid cQED systems, showcasing the flexibility of faster-than-dispersive longitudinal readout.

In this work, we devise a unifying Floquet formalism to establish a universal connection between AC Stark shift, longitudinal and dispersive readout in cQED, see Fig.

Derivation –
To establish the relation between AC Stark shift, longitudinal and dispersive readout, we start by considering the simplest case of a (charge) qubit of Larmor frequency
\omega_{\rm q}
transversally coupled to a resonator of frequency
\omega_{\rm r}
, and transversally driven at the same frequency, see Fig.
1
. This scenario is described by the time-periodic Rabi Hamiltonian (we assume
\hbar=1
)

\mathcal{H}=\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\,\sigma_{z}+\omega_{\rm r}\,a^{\dagger}a+g_{\perp}(a+a^{\dagger})\sigma_{x}+A_{\rm q}\cos(\omega_{\rm r}t)\sigma_{x}\,,

(2)

where the drive amplitude
A_{\rm q}
acts as a static detuning in the adiabatic
\omega_{\rm r}\rightarrow 0
limit. In the frame rotating at frequency
\omega_{\rm r}
, the cavity field operators
a^{(\dagger)}
renormalize the drive amplitude
A_{\rm q}
according to the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}e^{-i\omega_{\rm r}t}+\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}e^{i\omega_{\rm r}t}\right)\sigma_{x}\,,

(3)

with
\mathcal{A}=A_{\rm q}+2g_{\perp}a
. For simplicity, we initially assume that, when the resonator loses photons at rate
\kappa
, the driven qubit steers the cavity towards a coherent state
|\alpha\rangle
, such that
a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle
. In this case, we can treat
\mathcal{A}
as a number and simply derive the effective qubit-photon coupling within the Floquet formalism
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
, which we review in the Supplemental Material (SM)
[
39
]
.

In analogy to space-periodic systems, Floquet’s theorem states that the solutions of the Schrödinger equation corresponding to the time-periodic Hamiltonian (
3
) read
\ket{\Psi_{j}(t)}=e^{-i\varepsilon_{j}t}\ket{u_{j}(t)}
, with periodic
\ket{u_{j}(t+2\pi/\omega_{\rm r})}=\ket{u_{j}(t)}
. The quasi-energies
\varepsilon_{j}
are defined up to multiple integers of
\omega_{\rm r}
, since
\ket{u_{j}(t)}
and
e^{in\omega_{\rm r}t}\ket{u_{j}(t)}
are equivalent solutions for any integer
n
. Thus, it is natural to build an extended space of replicas of the
\ket{\pm}
states, each one associated to a replica of the quasi-energy. For
\mathcal{A}=0
, the Floquet spectrum consists of the replicated qubit energies
\pm\omega_{\rm q}/2+n\omega_{\rm r}
, see Fig.
1
b. For
\mathcal{A}\neq 0
, the time-periodic term in Eq. (
3
) couples every
\ket{\pm}
state of one replica to the
\ket{\mp}
state of the closest one
[
40
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
.
Similarly to a charge qubit detuning,
|\mathcal{A}|
controls the repulsion between replicas, leading to the AC Stark shift of the qubit states. Thus, the quasi-energies acquire
curvature
as function of
|\mathcal{A}|
. As sketched in Fig.
1
b, the sign of the detuning
\delta
between qubit and resonator controls the sign of this curvature. The Floquet Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}
associated to Eq. (
3
) is the time-independent Hamiltonian whose eigenvalues are the quasi-energies
\varepsilon_{j}
. It can be cast in the diagonal form

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\varepsilon\big(\omega_{\rm q},\omega_{\rm r},|\mathcal{A}|\big)\,\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\,,

(4)

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{z}
is the
z
Pauli matrix, acting on replica
n=0
.

For sufficiently weak occupations of the cavity (
g_{\perp}|\alpha|\ll A_{\rm q}
), we can expand the quasi-energy
\varepsilon
close to
\mathcal{A}=A_{\rm q}
, up to second order in
g_{\perp}\alpha
. Neglecting all Kerr terms (
\propto\alpha^{2},\alpha^{*2}
), we find

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\left[\varepsilon(|A_{\rm q}|)+g_{\parallel}(\alpha+\alpha^{*})+\chi|\alpha|^{2}\right]\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\,,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\frac{g_{\parallel}}{g_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial\mbox{Re}\mathcal{A}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}},

\displaystyle\frac{\chi}{g^{2}_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|}\right]_{A_{\rm q}}\,.

(6)

These equations are the main result of our work. They establish a relation between the AC Stark shift,
controlled by the driving amplitude
A_{\rm q}
, the longitudinal coupling
g_{\parallel}
and the dispersive shift
\chi
. The longitudinal coupling
g_{\parallel}
is proportional to the
slope
of the Floquet quasi-energies, while the dispersive coupling
\chi
includes a contribution from their
curvature
with respect to the drive strength.

Equation (
6
) generalizes the expectation that both
g_{\parallel}
and
\chi
are governed by the curvature of the static spectrum
\partial^{2}\varepsilon/\partial|\mathcal{A}|^{2}|_{A_{\rm q},\omega_{\rm r}\rightarrow 0}

[
8
,
9
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
or, equivalently, the quantum capacitance
[
33
]
. Indeed, the static regime is directly obtained by considering Eq. (
6
) in the
\omega_{\rm r}\rightarrow 0
limit, where the drive in Eq. (
2
) acts as a static detuning.

If we instead consider the small drive limit
A_{\rm q}\rightarrow 0
, but for arbitrary
\omega_{\rm r}
, we find a direct connection between the longitudinal and the dispersive coupling

\displaystyle g_{\parallel}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi^{(0)}}{2g_{\perp}}A_{\rm q}\,,

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}

\displaystyle=2g_{\perp}^{2}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}=0}\,.

(7)

In this limit, both couplings become proportional to the Floquet (AC Stark) curvature
\partial^{2}\varepsilon/\partial|\mathcal{A}|^{2}|_{A_{\rm q}=0}
, and, in the specific case of a charge qubit,
\chi^{(0)}=2g_{\perp}^{2}\omega_{\rm q}/(\omega_{\rm q}^{2}-\omega_{\rm r}^{2})
. Equation (
7
) showcases the parametric dependence of the longitudinal coupling
g_{\parallel}
on the drive
A_{\rm q}
. The relation between
g^{(0)}_{\parallel}
and
\chi^{(0)}
was first found for transmons
[
13
]
. We show here that this relation holds in general and that it is connected to Floquet spectral properties. Notice that both
g^{(0)}_{\parallel}
and
\chi^{(0)}
are proportional to

\omega_{q}^{-1}
in the adiabatic limit (
\omega_{\rm r}\rightarrow 0
)
[
16
,
30
]
, corresponding to the static curvature of a charge qubit at zero detuning
[
33
,
30
]
(see also SM
[
39
]
), but change sign when
\omega_{\rm r}>\omega_{\rm q}
.

In the SM
[
39
]
, we derive Eqs. (
5
-
6
) in the operator formalism. We apply a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation
[

]
in Floquet space and derive the Hamiltonian (

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\left[\varepsilon(|A_{\rm q}|)+\frac{\chi}{2}+g_{\parallel}(a+a^{\dagger})+\chi a^{\dagger}a\right]\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\,.

(8)

Derivation of Eqs. (4-7) based on the Floquet formalism

We provide here a short review of the Floquet formalism in relation to the solution of the time-dependent Hamiltonian (3) in the main text and then derive Eqs. (4-7).

I.1
Floquet formalism

Consider the time-periodic Hamiltonian (3) in the main text

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}e^{-i\omega_{\rm r}t}+\mathcal{A}^{*}e^{i\omega_{\rm r}t}\right)\sigma_{x}\,,

(S1)

where we have already made the assumption that
\mathcal{A}
is a complex number. Floquet’s theorem
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
states that the solutions of the periodic time-dependent Hamiltonian (
S1
) have the form

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{j}\left(t\right)}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\varepsilon_{j}t}\ket{u_{j}\left(t\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\ket{u_{j}(t)}=\ket{u_{j}(t+2\pi/\omega_{\rm r})}\,.

(S2)

We refer to the states
\ket{u_{j}(t)}
as Floquet modes. In strict analogy to Bloch’s states in space-periodic systems, the Floquet modes
\ket{u_{j}(t)}
share the time-periodicity of the Hamiltonian (
S1
). The Schrödinger equation for the
j
-th Floquet mode reads

(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}-i\partial_{t})\ket{u_{j}\left(t\right)}=\varepsilon_{j}\ket{u_{j}\left(t\right)}.

(S3)

In our case, each Floquet mode decomposes as
\ket{u_{j}(t)}=\sum_{\sigma}u_{j,\sigma}(t)\ket{\sigma}
, where
\sigma=\pm
and the coefficients obey the periodicity condition
u_{j,\sigma}(t+2\pi/\omega_{\rm r})=u_{j,\sigma}(t)
. Thus, each Floquet mode can be decomposed in Fourier harmonics
u_{j,\sigma}(t)=\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-ip\omega_{\rm r}t}u_{j,\sigma,p}
. Introducing the compact notation for the Fourier coefficients
\ket{u_{j,p}}=\sum_{\sigma}u_{j,\sigma,p}\ket{\sigma}
, Eq. (
S3
) can be recast as

\left(\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\sigma_{z}-p\,\omega_{\rm r}\right)\ket{u_{j,p}}+\frac{\mathcal{A}}{2}\sigma_{x}\ket{u_{j,p-1}}+\frac{\mathcal{A}^{*}}{2}\sigma_{x}\ket{u_{j,p+1}}=\varepsilon_{j}\ket{u_{j,p}}\,,

(S4)

which is a time-independent problem. To understand the structure of the solutions of Eq. (
S4
), it is important to stress that if
\ket{u_{j}(t)}
, in Eq. (
S2
), solves the time-dependent Schrödinger equation with quasi-energy
\varepsilon_{j}
, then also
\ket{u_{j}^{(n)}(t)}=e^{in\omega t}\ket{u_{j}(t)}
with
\varepsilon_{j}^{(n)}=\varepsilon_{j}+n\omega_{\rm r}
does. This is immediately demonstrated by the equality

\ket{\psi_{j}\left(t\right)}=e^{-i\varepsilon_{j}t}\ket{u_{j}\left(t\right)}=e^{-i\varepsilon^{(n)}_{j}t}\ket{u^{(n)}_{j}\left(t\right)}\,,

(S5)

which implies, by making the Fourier decomposition
\ket{u^{(n)}_{j}(t)}=\sum_{p}e^{-ip\omega_{\rm r}t}\ket{u^{(n)}_{j,p}}
, that

\ket{u^{(n)}_{j,p}}=\ket{u_{j,p+n}}\,.

(S6)

As a consequence, Eq. (
S4
) admits an infinite amount of equivalent solutions, called
Floquet replicas
. They are shifted in Fourier space according to Eq. (
S6
) and are associated to evenly spaced quasi-energies
\varepsilon_{j}^{(n)}
. We label the replicas by the index
n
, and we will take the convention that the
n=0
replica is the coinciding with
\varepsilon_{\pm}=\pm\omega_{\rm q}/2
for
\mathcal{A}=0
. To account for the replicated structure (
S6
), it is useful to introduce an extended Hilbert space stacking up all the Fourier coefficients of a given replica
n

\displaystyle\ket{\left.u_{j},n\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\ldots\\
\ket{u^{(n)}_{j,-1}}\\
\ket{u^{(n)}_{j,0}}\\
\ket{u^{(n)}_{j,+1}}\\
\ldots\\
\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{p}|p)\otimes\ket{u^{(n)}_{j,-p}}=\sum_{p}|p)\otimes\ket{u^{(0)}_{j,n-p}}=\begin{pmatrix}\ldots\\
\ket{u^{(0)}_{j,n-1}}\\
\ket{u^{(0)}_{j,n}}\\
\ket{u^{(0)}_{j,n+1}}\\
\ldots\\
\end{pmatrix}\,,

(S7)

where we have introduced a canonical basis for the extended replica space

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\left|-1\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\vdots\\
0\\
0\\
1\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle\left|0\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\vdots\\
0\\
1\\
0\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle\left|1\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\vdots\\
1\\
0\\
0\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle\ldots

(S8)

To stress the consistency of this notation, notice that the vectors
|u_{j},n\rangle\rangle
are orthonormal

\delta_{jk}\delta_{nm}=\langle\langle u_{j},n|u_{k},m\rangle\rangle=\sum_{p}\braket{u^{(0)}_{j,n+p}|u^{(0)}_{k,m+p}}=\sum_{p}\braket{u_{j,p}|u_{k,p+m-n}}\,,

(S9)

from which we deduce that the modes
\ket{u_{j}(t)}
are orthonormal at any instant
t

\displaystyle\braket{u_{j}(t)|u_{k}(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq}e^{i(p-q)\omega_{\rm r}t}\braket{u^{(0)}_{j,p}|u^{(0)}_{k,q}}=\sum_{p}\braket{u^{(0)}_{j,p}|u^{(0)}_{k,p}}+\sum_{d\neq 0}e^{id\omega_{\rm r}t}\sum_{p}\braket{u^{(0)}_{j,p}|u^{(0)}_{k,p+d}}=\delta_{jk}\,,

(S10)

as it should be. Thus, one can exploit the relation
(p|u_{j},n\rangle\rangle=\ket{u_{j,-p}^{(n)}}
to cast Eq. (
S4
) as an eigenvalue problem

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}\ket{\left.u_{j},n\right\rangle}=\varepsilon_{j}^{(n)}\ket{\left.u_{j},n\right\rangle}\,,

(S11)

where we have defined the Floquet Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathbf{p}\,\omega_{\rm r}+\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\,f_{-}+\mathcal{A}^{*}\,f_{+}\right)\sigma_{x}\,,

(S12)

and introduced diagonal jump operators in the extended space

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}p\left|p\right)\left(p\right|\,,

\displaystyle f_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}|p-1)(p|\,.

\displaystyle f_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}|p+1)(p|\,.

(S13)

\mathcal{A}=A_{\rm q}+2g_{\perp}\alpha

\ket{\alpha}

\mathcal{A}=A_{\rm q}

\varepsilon(|A|)=\varepsilon(|A_{\rm q}|)+2g_{\perp}\alpha\left.\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}}+2g_{\perp}\alpha^{*}\left.\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}}+2g_{\perp}^{2}\left[\alpha^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}^{2}}+2|\alpha|^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}+\alpha^{*2}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}^{*2}}\right]_{A_{\rm q}}\,.

(S17)

A more compact expression is derived by expressing the derivatives along the real and imaginary part of
\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\text{Re}\mathcal{A}}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\text{Im}\mathcal{A}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\text{Re}\mathcal{A}}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial\text{Im}\mathcal{A}}\right)\,.

\displaystyle 4g_{\perp}^{2}|\alpha|^{2}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}}

\displaystyle i\mathcal{S}

with

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{U}\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\right.\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{U}\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}}{\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}\right.=-\gamma_{1}^{\dagger}\,.

(S33)

In these notations, we can write also
\mathcal{H}_{2}=-2g_{\perp}[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}]a+\mbox{h.c.}
.
Plugging Eq. (
S32
) into Eq. (
S31
) one sees that the second-order corrections generated by
\mathcal{S}
read

i\left[\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}_{1}\right]+\frac{i}{2}[\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}_{2}]=4g_{\perp}^{2}\left(\left[\gamma_{2}a^{\dagger},\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}a\right]-\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{2}a^{\dagger},\left[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}\right]a\right]+\mbox{h.c.}\right)\,,

(S34)

where we have neglected all the Kerr terms proportional to
a^{2},\,a^{\dagger 2}
, which contribute at higher orders. According to Eq. (
S21
), our aim is to make apparent the presence of the second derivatives of the spectrum in the dispersive term. Thus, we first establish the connection between such derivatives and the
\gamma_{1,2}
operator factors introduced in Eq. (
S33
). From the first derivative

\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}=[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}]+\mathcal{U}\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\,,

(S35)

we construct the second derivative as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{D}}{\partial\mathcal{A}\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}\left[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{2},\mathcal{U}\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}\left[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{2},\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{2},\left[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}\right]\right]\,.

(S36)

Then, making use of the identities
[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[B,C]A
and
AB=\{B,A\}/2-[B,A]/2
, we rearrange the terms in Eq. (
S34
) as

\displaystyle i\left[\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}_{1}\right]+\frac{i}{2}[\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}_{2}]

\displaystyle=\chi\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sigma_{z}+\mathcal{V}\,,

\displaystyle\chi=4g_{\perp}^{2}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}\partial\mathcal{A}^{*}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}}=g^{2}_{\perp}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|}\right]_{A_{\rm q}}\,,

(S37)

where we have introduced the term leading to Eq. (8) in the main text plus an additional one

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=2g_{\perp}^{2}\left(\left[\frac{\partial\gamma_{2}}{\partial\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{D}\right]aa^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{2},\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\right]-\left\{\gamma_{2},\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\gamma_{2},\left[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}\right]\right\}+\text{h.c.}\right)\,.

(S38)

We show now that
\mathcal{V}
is either off-diagonal in the qubit and replica space, either is proportional to the identity operator in both the qubit and the replica space. Since all the off-diagonal terms can be eliminated at higher orders, they can be here neglected when projecting into the diagonal subspace.

The first two terms in
\mathcal{V}
are off-diagonal, since they are commutators with a diagonal operator. To discuss the other terms, we inspect the general structure of the operators
\gamma_{1,2}
. Using Eq. (
S7
), we write explicitly the operator
\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}
, whose columns are the eigenvectors of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}=\sum_{jn}\ket{\left.u_{j},n\right\rangle}\bra{j}\left(n\right|=\sum_{jn}f^{-n}\ket{u_{j,n}}\bra{j}\,,

(S39)

such that
\gamma_{1}
reads

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{U}\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}=-\sum_{jj^{\prime}\alpha}\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}^{\alpha}f^{\alpha}\ket{j}\bra{j^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}^{\alpha}=\sum_{n}\bra{u_{j,n}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\ket{u_{j^{\prime},n-\alpha}}\right)\,,

(S40)

where we have introduced the notation
f^{\alpha}\equiv f^{|\alpha|}_{\text{sgn}\alpha}
. The coefficients of
\gamma_{2}=-\gamma_{1}^{\dagger}
are related to the ones of
\gamma_{1}
through the important property
\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}^{\alpha*}=-\Gamma_{1-j,1-j^{\prime}}^{\alpha}
, that we will prove below.
Considering the symmetry transformation

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}|-n)(n|\sigma_{x}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{W}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\,,

(S41)

where
*
takes the complex conjugation of the coefficients buried in the operator (
\mathcal{A}\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{*}
), given one of the two eigenvectors in the zero-th replica (or in any other replica subspace) we can construct the other one as
|u_{1-j},0\rangle\rangle=\mathcal{W}|u_{j},0\rangle\rangle^{*}
. Indeed

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{W}|u_{j},0\rangle\rangle^{*}=(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\mathcal{W}|u_{j},0\rangle\rangle)^{*}=-\varepsilon_{j}\mathcal{W}|u_{j},0\rangle\rangle^{*}\,,

(S42)

which, since
\varepsilon_{1-j}=-\varepsilon_{j}
, proofs that
\mathcal{W}|u_{j},0\rangle\rangle^{*}=|u_{1-j},0\rangle\rangle
. We remark that
*
only takes the complex conjugate of the coefficients, and thus the meaning of
\mathcal{W}|u_{j},0\rangle\rangle^{*}=|u_{1-j},0\rangle\rangle
is

u_{j,n,\sigma}=(-1)^{n}u_{1-j,-n,1-\sigma}^{*}\,.

(S43)

Finally, we have
(\partial_{\mathcal{A}}\ket{u_{j,n}})^{*}=\mathcal{W}\partial_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\ket{u_{1-j,n}}
, hence
\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}^{\alpha*}=-\Gamma_{1-j,1-j^{\prime}}^{\alpha}
.
We consider now the first anti-commutator in Eq. (
S38
)

-\left\{\gamma_{2},\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\right\}+\text{h.c.}=-\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\sum_{\alpha}f^{\alpha}(\Gamma_{11}^{\alpha*}\ket{1}\bra{1}-\Gamma_{00}^{\alpha*}\ket{0}\bra{0})+\text{h.c.}\,.

(S44)

Since the derivatives are taken on the real axis, we have
\partial_{\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon=\partial_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\varepsilon=\partial_{\text{Re}\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon/2
(see Eqs. (
S18
), (
S19
)), and the only non off-diagonal term is
(\partial_{\text{Re}\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon/2)(\Gamma_{00}^{0}-\Gamma_{11}^{0})\mathds{1}
, that is proportional to the identity operator. For the second anti-commutators, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\gamma_{2}\left[\gamma_{1},\mathcal{D}\right]\right\}+\text{h.c.}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{jj^{\prime}k\alpha\beta}f^{\alpha-\beta}\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\left(\Gamma_{jk}^{\beta*}\ket{k}\bra{j^{\prime}}+\Gamma_{kj^{\prime}}\ket{j}\bra{k}\right)(\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{j}-\alpha\omega_{\rm r})+\text{h.c.}\,,

(S45)

whose only non off-diagonal terms are

\displaystyle-\sum_{jj^{\prime}\alpha}\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}^{\alpha*}\left(\ket{j^{\prime}}\bra{j^{\prime}}+\ket{j}\bra{j}\right)(\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{j}-\alpha\omega_{\rm r})+\text{h.c.}=-4\sum_{\alpha}\alpha\omega_{\rm r}(\Gamma_{01}^{\alpha}\Gamma_{10}^{\alpha}+\Gamma_{00}^{\alpha}\Gamma_{11}^{\alpha})\mathds{1}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(S46)

that are again only proportional to the identity operator.
We have shown that all the terms in Eq. (
S38
) are either off-diagonal in the replica or qubit space, or proportional to the identity operator, and thus they can be neglected in the Hamiltonian after projection on the diagonal subspace.

So, projecting to the diagonal subspace up to order
g_{\perp}^{2}
, we find the effective Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}=e^{i\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{U}\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{U^{\dagger}}e^{-i\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{D}+g_{\parallel}(a+a^{\dagger})\sigma_{z}+\chi\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sigma_{z}\,.

(S47)

Inverting the rotation, we can write
\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{F}}
as

\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{F}}=\tilde{\mathbf{n}}\omega_{r}+\varepsilon(\omega_{\rm q},\omega_{\rm r},|\mathcal{A}|)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+g_{\parallel}(\tilde{a}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger})\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\chi\left(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\,,

(S48)

where we switched to the operators
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}e^{-i\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{O}e^{i\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{U}
, which are diagonal in the basis of the eigenvectors of the system. As shown in Eqs. (
S30
) and (
S37
), the longitudinal and dispersive couplings,
g_{\parallel}
and
\chi
, coincide with those given by Eq. (6) in the main text

\displaystyle g_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=g_{\perp}\left.\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial\mbox{Re}\mathcal{A}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}},

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=g^{2}_{\perp}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|}\right]_{A_{\rm q}}\,.

(S49)

The projection of the Floquet Hamiltonian into the zero-th replica space gives
Eq. (8) in the main text, where we omitted the
\sim
symbol on the
a,\,a^{\dagger}
operators for simplicity.

III
Extensions: additional drive to the cavity, dispersive readout and Purcell effect

The study of realistic multi-level systems and the comparison to dispersive readout requires the extension of the previous results to the case in which both the qubit and the resonator are driven at a generic frequency
\omega_{\rm d}
and the cavity drive is phase-shifted by a phase
\varphi
. We thus consider an Hamiltonian of the form

\mathcal{H}=\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+g_{\perp}(a+a^{\dagger})\sigma_{x}+\omega_{\rm r}a^{\dagger}a+A_{\rm q}\cos(\omega_{\rm d}t)\sigma_{x}+A_{\rm r}\cos(\omega_{\rm d}t+\varphi)(a+a^{\dagger})\,.

(S50)

In the rotating frame defined by
\mathcal{W}(t)=e^{i\omega_{\rm d}ta^{\dagger}a}
, the rotated Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\mathcal{W}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{W}^{\dagger}+i(\partial_{t}\mathcal{W})\mathcal{W}^{\dagger}
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}e^{-i\omega_{\rm d}t}+\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}e^{i\omega_{\rm d}t}\right)\sigma_{x}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}\left(ae^{\varphi}+a^{\dagger}e^{-i\varphi}\right)+(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})a^{\dagger}a\,,

(S51)

where we neglect the fast terms rotating at
\pm 2\omega_{\rm d}t
. Following the same procedure used in Section
II
, one finds the extended version of Eq. (
S48
), namely

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbf{n}}\,\omega_{\rm d}+\varepsilon(\omega_{\rm q},\omega_{\rm d},|\mathcal{A}|)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+g_{\parallel}(\tilde{a}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger})+\chi\left(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}\left(\tilde{a}e^{i\varphi}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}e^{-i\varphi}\right)+(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}\,.

(S52)

Standard dispersive readout is achieved by sending

A_{\rm q}=0
and
\omega_{\rm d}=\omega_{\rm r}
in the above expression. Choosing
\varphi=3\pi/2
to ease comparison with the lonigitudinal readout protocol in the main text, the effective Hamiltonian reads
[
2
]

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\varepsilon(\omega_{\rm q},\omega_{\rm r},|\mathcal{A}=0|)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\left.\chi\right|_{\mathcal{A}=0}\left(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2i}\left(\tilde{a}-\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\right)\,,

(S53)

with
\left.\chi\right|_{\mathcal{A}=0}=\chi^{(0)}
given in Eq. (
S25
).

We also give an alternative derivation of the Purcell effect within our formalism, which is equivalent to standard approaches
[
2
]
. We rely on a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation
[
45
]
to obtain a perturbative expression of the unitary
U
at small values of
\mathcal{A}
to diagonalize
\mathcal{D}
in Eq. (
S29
). Similarly to the Schrieffer-Wolff procedure described in Section
II
we split the Floquet Hamiltonian in a diagonal and off-diagonal term

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\mathcal{V}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{n}\omega_{\rm r}+\frac{\omega_{\rm q}}{2}\sigma_{z}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}f_{-}+\mathcal{A}^{*}f_{+}\right)\sigma_{x}\,,

(S54)

and look for an operator
\mathcal{T}
such that
\mathcal{U}=e^{i\mathcal{T}}
and satisfying

i[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{H}_{0}]=-\mathcal{V}\,.

(S55)

This equation is solved by

\displaystyle i\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\omega_{\rm q}-\omega_{\rm r}}f_{-}+\frac{\mathcal{A}^{*}}{\omega_{\rm q}+\omega_{\rm r}}f_{+}\right)\sigma_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\omega_{\rm q}+\omega_{\rm r}}f_{-}+\frac{\mathcal{A}^{*}}{\omega_{\rm q}-\omega_{\rm r}}f_{+}\right)\sigma_{-}\,.

(S56)

The Purcell effect is a qubit relaxation mediated by emission of photons in the resonator. To estimate this relaxation rate, we consider first the fact that photons in the cavity decay at rate
\kappa
. This decay is described by a Lindblad equation, which describes the evolution of the density matrix
\rho
of the system

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-i[\mathcal{H},\rho]+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{a}[\rho]\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{a}\left[\rho\right]

\displaystyle=a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{a^{\dagger}a,\rho\right\}\,.

(S57)

However, in the rotated frame derived in Section
II
, where the unitary dynamics is diagonal, the dissipative term becomes
\kappa\mathcal{D}(a)=\kappa\mathcal{D}(e^{i\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{U}\tilde{a}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}e^{-i\mathcal{S}})
. To do so, we compute
\gamma_{1}
up to zero-th order in
\mathcal{A}
through Eq. (
S56
)

\gamma_{1}=-\mathcal{U}\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}=i\frac{\partial\mathcal{T}}{\partial\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\omega_{\rm q}-\omega_{\rm r}}f_{-}\sigma_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\omega_{\rm q}+\omega_{\rm r}}f_{-}\sigma_{-}\,.

(S58)

Up to linear order in
g_{\perp}
and zero-th order in
\mathcal{A}
we have

\displaystyle a\simeq\tilde{a}+2g_{\perp}\gamma_{1}^{\dagger}=\tilde{a}+\frac{g_{\perp}}{\omega_{\rm q}-\omega_{\rm r}}f_{+}\sigma_{-}-\frac{g_{\perp}}{\omega_{\rm q}+\omega_{\rm r}}f_{+}\sigma_{+}\,.\begin{comment}

(S59)

Considering
\sigma_{-}\simeq\tilde{\sigma}_{-}
and neglecting sub-dominant terms proportional to
g_{\perp}^{2}/(\omega_{\rm q}+\omega_{\rm r})^{2}
, we obtain
\kappa\mathcal{D}(a)\approx\kappa\mathcal{D}(\tilde{a})+\gamma\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\sigma}_{-})
, describing an effective decay for the qubit at rate
[
2
]

\gamma=\frac{\kappa g_{\perp}^{2}}{(\omega_{\rm q}-\omega_{\rm r})^{2}}\,.

(S60)

IV
Derivation of the effective Hamiltonian (9) for multi-level systems

In this section we give a generalization of the Floquet approach of Section
I.2
to multi-level systems, leading to Eq. (9) in the main text. We consider systems described by a general Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}[Q]
. The Hamiltonian depends on a generic control parameter
Q
, that corresponds to an excess of charge on a capacitor. Charge fluctuations caused by the classical drive and by the resonator lead to
Q\rightarrow Q_{0}+\delta Q
, with
\delta Q=A_{\rm q}\cos(\omega_{\rm d}t)+g_{\perp}(a+a^{\dagger})
. The coupling
g_{\perp}
, depends on the coupling scheme whereas
\omega_{\rm d}
is the strength of the drive. To apply the compensation tones described in the main text, we also consider an additional drive to the cavity as in Eq. (
S50
). Expanding the Hamiltonian till linear order in
\delta Q
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}[Q_{0}]+\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}}{\partial Q}\right|_{Q_{0}}\delta Q+\omega_{\rm r}a^{\dagger}a+A_{\rm r}\cos(\omega_{\rm d}t+\varphi)(a+a^{\dagger})\,.

(S61)

Following the same procedures detailed in Sections
I
and
III
, we build the Floquet Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}[Q_{0}]\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}f_{-}+\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}f_{+}\right)\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}}{\partial Q}\right|_{Q_{0}}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}\left(\tilde{a}e^{i\varphi}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}e^{-i\varphi}\right)+(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}\,,

(S62)

that, projected into the coherent states
\ket{\alpha}
and in the basis set
\{|p)\}
of the extended replica space, reads

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\begin{pmatrix}\ldots\\
&\omega_{\rm r}\mathds{1}+\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}&\left.\frac{\mathcal{A}^{*}}{2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}}{\partial Q}\right|_{Q_{0}}&0\\
&\frac{\mathcal{A}}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}}{\partial Q}\right|_{Q_{0}}&\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}&\frac{\mathcal{A}^{*}}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}}{\partial Q}\right|_{Q_{0}}\\
&0&\frac{\mathcal{A}}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}}{\partial Q}\right|_{Q_{0}}&-\omega_{\rm r}\mathds{1}+\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}\\
&&&&\ldots\end{pmatrix}\,,

(S63)

where
\mathds{1}
is the identity acting on the Hilbert space spanned by the system Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\rm sys}
.
The problem can be in principle diagonalized leading to the quasi-energies
\varepsilon_{j}
. We restrain to the zero replica. Assuming that the Schrieffer-Wolff trasnformation discussed in Section
II
can be extended to this case, the Taylor expansion of the quasi-energies around
\mathcal{A}=A_{\rm q}
leads to the effective Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{j}\left[\varepsilon_{j}(\omega_{\rm q},\omega_{\rm d},|A_{\rm q}|)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{j}\right]\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{j}+g_{\parallel,j}(\tilde{a}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger})\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{j}+\chi_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{j}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}\left(\tilde{a}e^{i\varphi}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}e^{-i\varphi}\right)+(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}\,,

(S64)

where we have introduced the projectors over the
j
-th Floquet eigenmodes
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{j}=\ket{u_{j}(0)}\bra{u_{j}(0)}
. Notice the presence of the term
\chi_{j}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{j}/2
, which accounts for quantum effects in the cavity. Each Floquet mode thus couples longitudinally and dispersively to the cavity independently. The expressions of
g_{\parallel,j}
and
\chi_{j}
are given by

\displaystyle g_{\parallel,j}

\displaystyle=g_{\perp}\left.\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{j}}{\partial\text{Re}\mathcal{A}}\right|_{A_{\rm q}},

\displaystyle\chi_{j}

\displaystyle=g^{2}_{\perp}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{j}}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{j}}{\partial|\mathcal{A}|}\right]_{A_{\rm q}}\,.

(S65)

A projection of Eq. (
S64
) onto the manifold spanned by the two lowest levels
\ket{0}
and
\ket{1}
leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\left(\varepsilon(A_{\rm q})+\frac{\chi}{2}\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+g_{\parallel}(\tilde{a}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger})\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\chi\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\bar{g}_{\parallel}(\tilde{a}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger})+\bar{\chi}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}\left(\tilde{a}e^{i\varphi}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}e^{-i\varphi}\right)+(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}\,,

(S66)

where we have mapped the system onto a qubit Hamiltonian whose energy is given by

\varepsilon(A_{\rm q})=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\varepsilon_{1}(A_{\rm q})-\varepsilon_{0}(A_{\rm q})\Big]\,,

(S67)

and with the resulting couplings

\displaystyle g_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{\parallel,1}-g_{\parallel,0}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{\parallel,1}+g_{\parallel,0}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\chi_{1}-\chi_{0})\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\chi_{1}+\chi_{0})\,.

(S68)

As we will discuss in detail in Section
V.1
, the terms proportional to
\bar{\chi}
and
\bar{g}_{\parallel}
in Eq. (
S66
) give rise to a qubit-independent dynamics. In order to compensate these terms we choose
A_{\rm r}=-2\bar{g}_{\parallel}
,
\varphi=0
and
\omega_{\rm d}-\omega_{\rm r}=\bar{\chi}
, such that Eq. (
S66
) reproduces the simple form given by Eq. (9) in the main text, the closest leading to a pure longitudinal splitting of the cavity dynamics.

V
Cavity dynamics calculations. Analytics and numerics.

In this Section we give details about the analytical and numerical calculations to derive the cavity dynamics and their comparison. Analytics are possible as they are performed starting from the effective model (
S66
), while numerics simulate exactly the dynamics of the parent model (
S61
).

V.1
Analytical integration of the equations of motion of the cavity field expectation value

Considering the Hamiltonian (
S66
) and the dual expression of the Lindblad operator, the equations of motion for the expectation value of an operator
\mathcal{O}
read

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle

\displaystyle=i\langle[\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{O}]\rangle+\kappa\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{\tilde{a}}[\mathcal{O}]+\gamma\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{\tilde{\sigma}_{-}}[\mathcal{O}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{X}\left[\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\langle X^{\dagger}\mathcal{O}X\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle\left\{X^{\dagger}X,\mathcal{O}\right\}\rangle\,,

(S69)

where
\kappa
is the cavity photon decay rate and
\gamma
is the Purcell decay rate of the spin given by Eq. (
S60
).
Applying equation (
S69
) to the evolution of
\langle\tilde{a}\rangle
, one finds that the following set of coupled equations needs to be solved

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{a}

\displaystyle=-i\chi\tilde{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{z}-a\left[i\bar{\chi}+\frac{\kappa}{2}+i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})\right]-ig_{\parallel}\tilde{\sigma}_{z}-i\left(\bar{g}_{\parallel}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right)\,,

(S70)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[\tilde{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\right]

\displaystyle=-\left[i\bar{\chi}+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\gamma+i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})\right]\tilde{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{z}-\tilde{a}(i\chi+\gamma)-ig_{\parallel}-i\left(\bar{g}_{\parallel}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\sigma}_{z}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\left(1+\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\right)\,.

The dynamics of
\tilde{\sigma}_{z}
is immediately obtained as

\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\left(t\right)=-1+\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\left(0\right)+1\right)e^{-\gamma t}\,.

(S71)

To solve the system of differential equations we introduce the normal modes

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\tilde{a}-\frac{i\chi}{\gamma+i\chi}\tilde{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\,,

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=a+a\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\,.

(S72)

The system in Eq. (
S70
) can now be decoupled and reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\alpha

\displaystyle=-\left[i(\bar{\chi}-\chi)+i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})+\frac{\kappa}{2}\right]\alpha-i\left[g_{\parallel}-\frac{i\chi}{\gamma+i\chi}\left(\bar{g}_{\parallel}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right)\right]\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+1\right)

(S73)

\displaystyle+i\frac{\gamma+2i\chi}{\gamma+i\chi}\left(g_{\parallel}-\bar{g}_{\parallel}-\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\beta

\displaystyle=-\left[i(\chi+\bar{\chi})+i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\gamma\right]\beta-i\left[g_{\parallel}+\bar{g}_{\parallel}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right](\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+1).

Considering an empty cavity at
t=0
, the solutions of Eq. (
S73
) are

\displaystyle\alpha\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=-i\left[\left(\bar{g}_{\parallel}-g_{\parallel}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right)\left(\frac{\gamma+2i\chi}{\gamma+i\chi}\right)\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}t-i(\bar{\chi}-\chi)t-i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})t}}{i(\bar{\chi}-\chi)+i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})+\kappa/2}\right.

(S74)

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\left(0\right)+1\right)\left(g_{\parallel}-\frac{i\chi}{\gamma+i\chi}\left(\bar{g}_{\parallel}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right)\right)\frac{e^{-\gamma t}-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}t-i(\bar{\chi}-\chi)t-i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})t}}{i(\bar{\chi}-\chi)+i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})+\kappa/2-\gamma}\right],

\displaystyle\beta\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=-i\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{z}\left(0\right)+1\right)\left(\bar{g}_{\parallel}+g_{\parallel}+\frac{A_{\rm r}}{2}e^{-i\varphi}\right)\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}t-i(\bar{\chi}+\chi)t-i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})t}}{i(\bar{\chi}+\chi)+i(\omega_{\rm r}-\omega_{\rm d})+\kappa/2}e^{-\gamma t}\,,

(S75)

and the evolution of
\langle a\rangle(t)
is obtained through

\langle a(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{\gamma+2i\chi}\big[\left(\gamma+i\chi\right)\alpha\left(t\right)+i\chi\beta\left(t\right)\big]\,.

(S76)

Based on this analytical formula, we compute the trajectories in Figs. 2, 3, 4 in the main text. In particular, the coupling constants
g_{\parallel}
,
\bar{g}_{\parallel}
,
\chi
,
\bar{\chi}
are computed by taking numerical derivatives of the Floquet spectrum, see Section
I
. To account for the effect of the classical drive on the qubit, we compute
\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{z}(0)\rangle
through
\tilde{\sigma}_{z}=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\sigma_{z}\mathcal{U}
, neglecting the effect of the resonator. We obtain
\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}
through Eq. (
S39
), where the eigenvectors are computed numerically solving the spectrum of Eq. (
S14
). We give below the simpler expressions which describe dispersive and longitudinal readout for an ideal charge qubit. We then discuss the effects of a finite
\bar{\chi}
and
\bar{g}_{\parallel}
on longitudinal readout and show how they can be compensated by applying a fine-tuned tone to the cavity.

V.1.1
Dispersive and longitudinal readout for ideal charge qubit and compensation tones

Dispersive readout –
We consider here the simplest case of an ideal charge qubit where
\bar{\chi}=\bar{g}_{\parallel}=0
and neglect Purcell effect (
\gamma=0
). In this case, the standard analytic formula for the evolution of the cavity
\langle a\rangle
with dispersive readout is obtained by driving only the resonator at the frequency
\omega_{\rm d}=\omega_{\rm r}
. We thus set
A_{\rm q}=0
and
\varphi=3\pi/2
to ease comparison with longitudinal readout in Fig. 2 in the main text. Equation (
S76
) simplifies to

\langle a(t)\rangle_{\rm disp.}=\frac{A_{\rm r}}{4}\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\sigma\left[\big(\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{z}(0)\rangle+\sigma\big)\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}t-i\sigma\chi t}}{i\sigma\chi+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\right]\,.

(S77)

In the stationary
t\rightarrow\infty
limit, assuming
\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{z}(0)\rangle\simeq\sigma
, one finds that
\langle a(t=\infty)\rangle_{\rm disp.}=A_{\rm r}/2(i\sigma\chi+\kappa/2)
, while, at short times, the expansion of the exponential gives
\langle a(t\rightarrow 0)\rangle_{\rm disp.}\approx A_{\rm r}t/2
, that is qubit state independent. Both these asymptotic expressions are given in the main text.

Longitudinal readout –
An analog expression is derived for the longitudinal driving protocol

\langle a(t)\rangle_{\rm long.}=-\frac{ig_{\parallel}}{2}\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\left[(\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{z}(0)\rangle+\sigma)\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}t-i\sigma\chi t}}{i\sigma\chi+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\right]\,,

(S78)

which, if
\chi\approx 0
, can be further simplified to

\langle a(t)\rangle_{\rm long.}=-ig_{\parallel}\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{z}(0)\rangle\left(\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}t}}{\kappa/2}\right)\,,

(S79)

which describes the
180^{\circ}
splitting of the trajectories sketched in Fig. 1 in the main text. Notice that such
180^{\circ}
splitting is also reproduced by Eq. (
S78
) at short times
\langle a(t\rightarrow 0)\rangle_{\rm long.}=-ig_{\parallel}\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle t
, as discussed in the main text.

Compensation tones –
It is obvious from Eqs. (
S74
) and (
S75
), that these results are corrected for finite values of
\bar{\chi}
and
\bar{g}_{\parallel}
. As it will become apparent in the sections below, such contributions always when considering realistic systems outside of an idealized charge qubit. To get the feeling of how such terms on the dynamics, we can still consider
\chi=0
and Eq. (
S79
) becomes

\langle a(t)\rangle_{\rm long.}=-i\left(\bar{g}_{\parallel}+g_{\parallel}\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{z}(0)\rangle\right)\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}t-i\bar{\chi}}}{i\bar{\chi}+\kappa/2}\,.

(S80)

This expression shows that the cavity dynamics is controlled by the
\bar{g}_{\parallel}
term which is not sensitive to the value of
\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{z}(0)\rangle
. However, inspection Eqs. (
S74
) and (
S75
) indicates that this spurious effects can be compensated choosing by adding an additional driving tone to the resonator tuned to
A_{\rm r}=-2\bar{g}_{\parallel}
,
\varphi=0
and
\omega_{\rm d}-\omega_{\rm r}=\bar{\chi}
, as we show in Figs. 3 and 4 in the main text.

Charged Lepton EDM with Extra Yukawa Couplings
Wei-Shu Hou, Girish Kumar and Sven Teunissen

Abstract
In a two Higgs doublet model with extra Yukawa couplings, we assess the new physics contributions to charged lepton electric dipole moment. We focus especially on muon (and tau) EDM where in the coming decade several experiments — Muon g-2, J-PARC and PSI (and Belle II) — will push sensitivities down by several orders of magnitude. With the working assumption that extra Yukawa couplings are analogous to SM ones in strength and taking exotic scalar masses in sub-TeV range, we find that μ \mu and τ \tau EDM can be enhanced to values larger than new physics scenarios that scale with lepton mass. The main effect comes from the flavor-conserving extra top coupling ρ t ​ t \rho_{tt} via well-known two-loop diagrams. Deviating from our working assumption, if the muon g − 2 g-2 anomaly arises from the one-loop diagram, driven by singly enhanced lepton flavor violating ρ τ ​ μ \rho_{\tau\mu} coupling, it can also induce rather large muon EDM, accessible at upcoming experiments.



I
Introduction

All charged-parity violation (CPV) observed in the laboratories
so far are in the quark sector.
The Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM)
matrix
Cabibbo:1963yz
;
Kobayashi:1973fv

that govern the weak interactions of the Standard Model (SM)
has been impressively successful in accounting for all such effects.
But, as is known, the KM phase does not
provide enough CPV to satisfy
the Sakharov condition
Sakharov:1967dj
for
the disappearance of antimatter in the Universe.
Various new physics (NP) models attempt to make up for
this by supplying new complex phases to hopefully explain
the baryon asymmetry of the Universe (BAU).

The general two Higgs doublet model
(g2HDM)
Branco:2011iw
;
Davidson:2005cw
, also known as
2HDM Type-III
Hou:1991un
, is attractive in this context.
In g2HDM, one does not introduce a
Z_{2}
symmetry to
enforce Natural Flavor Conservation
Glashow:1976nt
.
Thus, the second Higgs doublet brings in extra Yukawa coupling matrices,
which are in general
complex
. The new phases,
especially those associated with extra top quark couplings,

\rho_{tt}
and
\rho_{tc}
, which are likely the stronger in strength,
can drive electroweak baryogengesis (EWBG)
and explain BAU
Fuyuto:2017ewj
;
Fuyuto:2019svr
.
Complex extra Yukawa couplings can also contribute to
CPV observables accessible in the laboratory,
such as charged lepton electric dipole moment (
\ell
EDM),
which we denote as
d_{\ell}
.

Lepton EDMs are powerful probes of NP CPV.
The SM predictions for
\ell
EDM are extremely tiny
(e.g.
|d_{e}|\sim 10^{-38}\;e

cm
Pospelov:2005pr
;
Pospelov:2013sca
;
Smith:2017dtz
)
because the sole complex phase, the KM phase,
survives only at the four-loop level.
With essentially no SM background, detection of
\ell
EDM
will establish the presence of NP of CPV nature.

Recently, there has been very good progress in the search for
e
EDM.
The ACME experiment exploits the large internal electric field
of ThO molecules to measure
e
EDM.
Already in 2013, ACME provided the best
upper limit on
e
EDM,

|d_{e}|<8.7\times 10^{-28}

e
cm
ACME:2013pal
.
Impressively, the bound was further improved to
ACME:2018yjb

\displaystyle|d_{e}|<1.1\times 10^{-29}\;e\,{\rm cm},~~\;({\rm ACME\,2018})

(1)

which is the best upper limit on any EDM.
The bound of Eq. (
1
) puts stringent constraint
on any BSM model that seek to address the BAU problem.

For g2HDM, consistency with Eq. (
1
) has
helped shed light on the structure of extra Yukawa couplings.
As noted in Ref.
Fuyuto:2019svr
,
one can accommodate EWBG in g2HDM
with flavor conserving coupling
|\rho_{tt}|\gtrsim 0.1

(see Ref.
Fuyuto:2017ewj
for flavor violating coupling

\rho_{tc}
which is immune to
e
EDM constraint),
but parameter space with single coupling scenario
runs into trouble with the ACME 2018 bound.
This can be avoided by introducing extra electron Yukawa coupling

\rho_{ee}
, which helps salvage parameter space favorable for EWBG.
Interestingly, the organization of these couplings follow the pattern
Fuyuto:2019svr
,

\displaystyle|\rho_{ee}/\rho_{tt}|\propto\lambda_{e}/\lambda_{t},

(2)

suggesting that the extra
\rho
couplings of g2HDM have
similar hierarchical structure as Yukawa couplings in SM.
Eq. (
2
) provides not only an elegant way of
complying with the ACME 2018 bound, it connects
EWBG with tiny CPV effects in the laboratories,
such as
e
EDM.

Seeing that
e
EDM is highly sensitive to extra Yukawa couplings,
in this paper we wish to explore the detailed contributions of
extra Yukawa couplings to
\mu
and
\tau
EDM.
Such an investigation is well-timed for the muon,
where the Fermilab Muon g-2 experiment
Muong-2:2021ojo

has affirmed recently the significant deviation from SM expectation
in a related observable — the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon.
Any NP in muon
g-2
certainly can have important implications
for
\mu
EDM, as we will show later.

The current bound on
\mu
EDM is by the Muon g-2 experiment
at Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL)
Muong-2:2008ebm
,

\displaystyle|d_{\mu}|<1.8\times 10^{-19}\;e\,{\rm cm},~~\;({\rm BNL\,2008})

(3)

while an indirect limit, extracted from
e
EDM bound of ACME 2018,
is claimed

In the coming years, several experiments will search for

II
Preliminaries

Before we proceed to the specifics,
let us lay out the framework of the model.
The Yukawa interactions in g2HDM
are given by
Davidson:2005cw
;
Hou:2017hiw
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{f=u,d,\ell}\bar{f}_{i}\Big[\big(\lambda^{f}_{i}\delta_{ij}s_{\gamma}+\rho^{f}_{ij}c_{\gamma}\big)h

\displaystyle+\big(\lambda^{f}_{i}\delta_{ij}c_{\gamma}-\rho^{f}_{ij}s_{\gamma}\big)H-i\,{\rm sgn}(Q_{f})\rho^{f}_{ij}A\Big]R\,f_{j}

\displaystyle-\bar{u}_{i}\left[(V\rho^{d})_{ij}R-(\rho^{u\dagger}V)_{ij}L\right]d_{j}H^{+}

\displaystyle-\bar{\nu}_{i}\rho^{\ell}_{ij}R\,\ell_{j}H^{+}+{h.c.},

(4)

where
L\,(R)=(1\mp\gamma_{5})/2
are projection operators,

Q_{f}
is the charge of fermion
f
in units of

,
and

is the CKM matrix.
The shorthand
c_{\gamma}\equiv\cos\gamma

and

represent
mixing between two CP even scalars,
h
and
H
,
which is known to be very small.

Although
\rho_{ij}
couplings are generic and do not possess
information about SM Yukawa couplings
\lambda_{i}
,
it is intriguing that data suggest the
\rho
matrices
imitate closely the hierarchical structure of
SM Yukawa matrices (see Eq. (
2
)),
as already mentioned in the Introduction.
The
\Delta F=2

and

constrain off-diagonal entries of down-type
\rho^{d}
to very small values

,
which indicates that
\rho^{d}
is nearly diagonal.
On the other hand, the extra top Yukawa coupling
\rho_{tt}
,
important for our study,
is allowed to be significant in strength

.
For the lepton sector, the
\rho_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
coupling
related to third and second generation are tightly
constrained from LFV decays

and
\tau\to\mu\gamma
,
but with
\rho_{tt}\sim 1

\rho_{\tau\mu}=\rho_{\mu\tau}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{\tau})

.

With these arguments in mind,
we assume the following strengths for

\rho_{ij}
related to

- and
\ell
-type matrices

,

\displaystyle\rho_{ii}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{i});\;\;\rho_{1i}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{1});\;\;\rho_{3j}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{3})\;(j\neq 1),

(5)

where the first two conditions can also be applied to

\rho^{d}

Down-type couplings, however, do not play

5

But we note that deviation from Eq. (
5
) is possible.
For example, NP in muon

5
) for interpretation
in 2HDM

.
We will discuss in Sec.
V

It is worth pointing out that with

our assumptions

and
\rho_{\tau\tau}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{\tau})

\mu

The EDM,

, of lepton

effective Lagrangian,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\rm EDM}=-\frac{i}{2}d_{\ell}\,\bar{\ell}\,\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{5}\ell\,F^{\alpha\beta},

where

In g2HDM, the first finite contribution to

A sample one-loop diagram for
\mu

in g2HDM is given in Fig.
1

The dipole operator in Eq. (
8

so an additional mass insertion
is required on the fermion line to obtain the correct
chiral structure. This means that analogous diagrams with
lighter leptons in the loop are chirally suppressed,
and we neglect them.
Fig.
1
gives

,

\displaystyle d_{\mu}|_{\rm 1-loop}\simeq

\displaystyle-e\frac{m_{\tau}}{32\pi^{2}}\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{\mu\tau})\left[c_{\gamma}^{2}\,\frac{\log x_{h\tau}-3/2}{m_{h}^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.s^{2}_{\gamma}\,\frac{\log x_{H\tau}-3/2}{m_{H}^{2}}-\frac{\log x_{A\tau}-3/2}{m_{A}^{2}}\right],

(9)

where

x_{ij}=m_{i}^{2}/m_{j}^{2}
.

We note that the contribution of the
h
boson is negligible
in Eq. (
9

c_{\gamma}^{2}\ll 1
,
while the contributions of CP even and odd scalars
have a relative sign between them, indicating that
in the
H
–

degeneracy limit,

For example, taking
c_{\gamma}=0.1

|d_{\mu}|\simeq 4\times 10^{-22}|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{\mu\tau})|\;e

With

, Eq. (
5

we obtain

With

and
m_{H}

For example, with
m_{H}=300

Thus, we find that our working assumption of

For

For both red and black lines,

always lie far lower than
|d^{\phi\gamma}_{\mu}|

except near cancellation regions of

On the other hand, complex extra Yukawa couplings of

to

\displaystyle(d_{\mu}^{H^{+}W^{+}})_{t/b}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3e\alpha|V_{tb}|^{2}\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{tt}\rho_{\mu\mu})}{128\pi^{3}m_{t}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\times\Bigl[Q_{t}F_{t}(x_{\phi t},x_{Wt})+Q_{b}F_{b}(x_{\phi t},x_{Wt})\Bigr],

Adapting to our case, we find,

\displaystyle(d_{\mu}^{H^{+}W^{+}})_{W}

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{e\,\alpha s_{\gamma}c_{\gamma}\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\mu\mu}}{128\sqrt{2}\pi^{3}\,v\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\times\Bigl[{\cal I}_{4}(m_{h},m_{H^{+}})-{\cal I}_{4}(m_{H},m_{H^{+}})\Bigr],

(16)

where

we give

Some comments are in order. There are additional subdominant diagrams related to

\displaystyle d_{\tau}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{e\,m_{\tau}}{32\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\,\times\left\{\left[s_{2\gamma}\lambda_{\tau}\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\tau\tau}+c_{\gamma}^{2}\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\tau\tau}^{2}\right]\frac{\log x_{h\tau}-3/2}{m_{h}^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\,+\left[-s_{2\gamma}\lambda_{\tau}\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\tau\tau}+s_{\gamma}^{2}\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\tau\tau}^{2}\right]\frac{\log x_{H\tau}-3/2}{m_{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 91.6179pt-\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\tau\tau}^{2}\left.\frac{\log x_{A\tau}-3/2}{m_{A}^{2}}\right\}.

(17)

V
Discussion

With our working assumption for
\rho_{ij}
given in Eq. (
5
)
and sub-TeV exotic scalars, we find that
\mu
and
\tau
EDM
can be enhanced in g2HDM, but are not large enough to be probed in the coming years.
However, recent progress in muon
g-2
may suggest
good prospects for
\mu
EDM.

The Fermilab Muon g-2 experiment
Muong-2:2021ojo

reported recently their first measurement of the muon anomalous
magnetic moment,
a_{\mu}\equiv(g-2)_{\mu}
, confirming
the old BNL result
Muong-2:2006rrc
.
The combined value of

a_{\mu}^{\rm Exp}=116592061(41)\times 10^{-11}

disagrees with the SM prediction,

a_{\mu}^{\rm SM}=116591810(43)\times 10^{-11}

Aoyama:2020ynm
,
by more than
4\sigma
,

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{\rm Exp}-a_{\mu}^{\rm SM}=(251\pm 59)\times 10^{-11}.

(19)

One of the simplest
(and easiest, thanks to the chiral enhancement factor
of
m_{\tau}/m_{\mu}\simeq 17
)
NP explanation of Eq. (
19
) is provided by g2HDM
via the same diagram as in Fig.
1
.
But it would require significant departure from Eq. (
5
) for some couplings.
As shown in Ref.
Hou:2021sfl
,
to explain Eq. (
19
) at
1\sigma
, one needs

|\rho_{\tau\mu}|=|\rho_{\mu\tau}|\simeq{\cal O}(20)\lambda_{\tau}
,
with
m_{H}
and
m_{A}
sub-TeV but nondegenerate.

Several implications, relevant for the present paper, follow.
First, one has to adopt
Hou:2021sfl
near-perfect alignment, i.e.
c_{\gamma}\to 0
.
Though odd, it is needed to satisfy the CMS limit of

{\cal B}(h\to\tau\mu)<0.15\%

CMS:2021rsq
,
which constrains
|\rho_{\tau\mu}c_{\gamma}|<0.1\lambda_{\tau}
for
\rho_{\tau\mu}=\rho_{\mu\tau}
.
This bound implies
c_{\gamma}<0.005
for
1\sigma
solution of muon
g-2
.
Another important implication is a rather small
\rho_{tt}
.
The decay
\tau\to\mu\gamma
arises from Fig.
2

with initial
\mu
replaced by
\tau
,
which constrains the product
\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{tt}
.
To comply with the Belle bound of

{\cal B}(\tau\to\mu\gamma)<4.2\times 10^{-8}

Belle:2021ysv
,
large
\rho_{\tau\mu}
means small
\rho_{tt}
.
Even more surprising
Hou:2021sfl
is that the collider search for

gg\to H,A\to\tau\mu
provides a much better constraint.
Combining both flavor and LHC constraints,

\rho_{tt}
seems limited to small values
\rho_{tt}\lesssim 0.1
,
which is small compared with
\lambda_{t}\cong 1
.

Small
\rho_{tt}
and
c_{\gamma}
will suppress the two-loop contributions to
\ell
EDM:
the top-loop diagrams by small
\rho_{tt}
,
and
W
-loop diagrams by
c_{\gamma}\to 0
.
But large
\rho_{\tau\mu}(\rho_{\mu\tau})
values can
give rather large
\mu
EDM via the one-loop diagram.

Defining
\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{\mu\tau}=|\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{\mu\tau}|\exp(\phi_{CP})
,
we illustrate in Fig.
5
the range of
d_{\mu}
accessible in
the
|\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{\tau\mu}|
-
\phi_{CP}
plane
while simultaneously accounting for muon
g-2
, Eq. (
19
).
The left plot is for
m_{H}=
300 GeV and
m_{A}=
340 GeV,
which are not far from degenerate, while the right plot is
for the same
m_{H}
but a much heavier
m_{A}=
500 GeV.
We see from both plots that, given the complexity of
\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{\mu\tau}
,
the solution space for muon
g-2
projects large
d_{\mu}

values that are well within PSI sensitivity!
In the more optimistic scenario where
m_{H}
and
m_{A}
are farther apart,
the parameter space in the right plot may even appear to be
within reach of Fermilab and J-PARC muon g-2 experiments,
namely
\sim 10^{-21}\;e
cm.
We caution, however, that this
would demand
very
large
|\rho_{\tau\mu}|\sim|\rho_{\mu\tau}|
values.

On the other hand, one does not expect such extraordinary enhancements for
d_{\tau}
.
In fact, if muon
g-2
arises from g2HDM as described,
then
d_{\tau}
will be much smaller than our estimates in Sec.
IV
.
This is because the two-loop contribution to
d_{\tau}
in Eq. (
18
)
becomes suppressed by small
\rho_{tt}
and
c_{\gamma}
, as argued previously.
Furthermore, one-loop enhancement similar to
\mu
EDM is not possible
because simultaneously large
\rho_{\tau\tau}
and
\rho_{\tau\mu}(\rho_{\mu\tau})

would make
\tau\to\mu\gamma
too large at one-loop and violate the Belle bound,
as we showed recently in Ref.
Hou:2021qmf
.

What about
e
EDM?
The leading one-loop results (
\tau
in the loop) for
e
EDM are
given by Eq. (
9
), replacing
``\mu"\to``e"
.
Therefore, at one loop,

d_{e}
is proportional to
\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{\tau e}\rho_{e\tau})
.
The
\mu\to e\gamma
process constrains
\rho_{\tau e}\,(\rho_{\tau e})
severely.
For values of
\rho_{\tau\mu}
consistent with muon
g-2
solution,
the current MEG
MEG:2016leq
bound of

{\cal B}(\mu\to e\gamma)<4.2\times 10^{-13}

gives
|\rho_{\tau e}|=|\rho_{e\tau}|\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{e})

Hou:2021qmf
,
which is in line with our working assumption in Eq. (
5
).
Taking
|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{\tau e}\rho_{e\tau})|\simeq\lambda_{e}^{2}
,

c_{\gamma}=0
, and
m_{H}=
300 GeV and
m_{A}=
500 GeV,
we find
|d_{e}|\lesssim 5\times 10^{-32}\;e
cm,
which is more than two orders of magnitude below
the current ACME bound,
|d_{e}|<1.1\times 10^{-29}\;e
cm.
For
m_{H}
closer to
m_{A}
,
d_{e}
will be further suppressed.
The two-loop results for
d_{e}
can be obtained by
replacing
``\mu"\to``e"
in the corresponding expressions for

\mu
EDM in Sec.
III
. If one assumes
\rho_{ee}\sim 0
,
then two-loop diagrams for

EDM is essentially given by the
second term of Eq. (
12
) (after obvious change of lepton indices),
which depends on
|s_{2\gamma}\operatorname{Im}\rho_{tt}|
and
m_{H}
.
For
m_{H}=
300 GeV, we find

|d_{e}|\simeq 2\times 10^{-27}|s_{2\gamma}\operatorname{Im}\rho_{tt}|\;e
cm.
With both parameters constrained to be small,
c_{\gamma}<0.005
and
|\rho_{tt}|\lesssim 0.1
,
it is rather easy to satisfy the current ACME bound,
without resort to the cancellation mechanism
Fuyuto:2019svr

to evade the experimental bound.
It remains to be seen whether parameter space exists
where simultaneously small
\rho_{tt}
and
c_{\gamma}

can still provide solutions to BAU in g2HDM.

VI
Conclusion

We study one- and two-loop mechanisms for charged lepton EDM in g2HDM,
the two Higgs doublet model that possesses extra Yukawa couplings.
We find both
d_{\mu}
and
d_{\tau}
can be enhanced to considerably larger values
— thanks to the extra top Yukawa coupling
\rho_{tt}\sim{\cal O}(1)
.

The question for g2HDM is how it has managed to hide so well
from scrutiny, if the exotics Higgs bosons are sub-TeV in mass.
This we have elucidated in a previous work
Hou:2020itz
,
where we developed Eq. (
5
) as
our working assumption for the strength of extra
\rho^{f}_{ij}
couplings,
namely
\rho_{ii}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{i})
,

\rho_{1i}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{1})
, and

\rho_{3j}\lesssim{\cal O}(\lambda_{3})
for
j\neq 1
.
It reflects the mass-mixing hierarchies observed in the SM sector
Hou:1991un
,
and finds further support from the cancellation mechanism
Fuyuto:2019svr
,
or interplay between
\rho_{ee}
and
\rho_{tt}

to satisfy the recent ACME 2018 bound on
d_{e}
,
while
\rho_{tt}
still drives baryogenesis.
Flavor changing couplings of
h(125)
are further suppressed
by the recent emergent phenomenon of alignment, i.e. small
h
–
H
mixing.

With our working assumption as above and taking central values,
we showed that
d_{\mu}
lies in the ballpark range of
10^{-24}

e
cm,
while
d_{\tau}
is in the
10^{-23}

e
cm range.
These numbers could go up by another order of magnitude
as allowed by Eq. (
5
).
But though they are much larger than typical expectations in MFV-like scenarios,
with future sensitivities as stated in Table 1, it is unlikely
that
d_{\mu}
and
d_{\tau}
would be observed in the near future.
Thus, Eq. (
5
) continues to “protect” g2HDM from
revealing itself, and we have advocated elsewhere for
LHC direct search
Hou:2020chc
,
as well as several flavor probes
Hou:2020itz
.

The recent experimental affirmation of the muon
g-2
“anomaly”,
however, brings in some hope, but at a cost.
In g2HDM, the one-loop mechanism can quite simply account for the discrepancy,

if

|\rho_{\tau\mu}|\simeq|\rho_{\mu\tau}|={\cal O}(20\lambda_{\tau})\sim 0.2
.
This would grossly violate our working assumption of Eq. (
5
);
but
Nature
herself is the judge and the jury, and maybe this is what
she has been telling us through the “anomaly”.
The point is, the same one-loop diagram can generate
d_{\mu}

through the complexity of
\rho_{\tau\mu}\rho_{\mu\tau}
,
which
can
be tested with projected experimental sensitivities.
But this single-enhanced coupling scenario would then imply
quite small
\rho_{tt}\lesssim 0.1
, while alignment must be near-perfect,
which by themselves are rather peculiar.
These in turn suggest that
d_{\tau}
will be rather suppressed,
and
d_{e}
can be much below ACME bound
without resort to cancellation mechanism.

1.
Introduction

The distribution of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line is conjecturally related to random matrices, a fact discovered by Montgomery
[
19
]
for local statistics of the zeros.
It was extended in many directions including to distributions for families of
L
-functions
[
16
]
and their moments
[
17
]
.
Fyodorov, Hiary & Keating
[
13
]
and Fyodorov & Keating
[
14
]
proposed to further expand the scope of this analogy at the level of extreme values. Based on a similar conjecture for random unitary matrices, they
put forward the very precise asymptotics

\frac{1}{T}\cdot\text{\rm meas}\Big\{T\leq t\leq 2T:\max_{|h|\leq 1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i}h)|>e^{y}\cdot\frac{\log T}{(\log\log T)^{3/4}}\Big\}\rightarrow F(y),

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where the limiting distribution function
F
satisfies
F(y)\sim Cye^{-2y}
for large
y
.
While the explicit form of
F
is not expected to be universal, the exponent
3/4
and the tail asymptotics
ye^{-2y}
characterize the universality class of logarithmically correlated fields.

In the first part of this series
[
4
]
, we showed the upper bound of this conjecture,
F(y)\ll ye^{-2y}
.
The main goal of this paper is to complete this work and establish tightness in the Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating conjecture,
by showing
F(y)\to 1
as
y\to-\infty
. The following is the main result.

Theorem 1
.

There exists
c>0
such that for any
T\geq 100
and
0\leq y\leq(\log\log T)^{1/10}
we have

\frac{1}{T}\cdot\text{\rm meas}\Big\{T\leq t\leq 2T:\max_{|h|\leq 1}|\zeta(1/2+\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i}h)|<e^{-y}\frac{\log T}{(\log\log T)^{3/4}}\Big\}\leq c^{-1}y^{-c}.

A direct consequence of the above result and
[
4
, Theorem 1]
is the expected tightness of maxima on short intervals, and existence of subsequential limits.

Corollary 1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C>0
such that for any
T\geq 100
, for
t\in[T,2T]
in a set of measure larger than
(1-\varepsilon)T
we have

\left|\max_{|h|\leq 1}\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i}h)|-(\log\log T-\frac{3}{4}\log\log\log T)\right|\leq C.

In particular, there exists a subsequence
T_{\ell}\to\infty
and a distribution function
F
such that

\frac{1}{T_{\ell}}\cdot\text{\rm meas}\Big\{t\in[T_{\ell},2T_{\ell}]:\max_{|h|\leq 1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i}h)|>e^{y}\cdot\frac{\log T_{\ell}}{(\log\log T_{\ell})^{3/4}}\Big\}\rightarrow F(y),

uniformly in
y\in\mathbb{R}
outside of a countable set.

Previous results in the direction of Theorem
1
were limited to the first order
\log T
, conditionally on the Riemann Hypothesis by Najnudel
[
20
]
and unconditionally by the authors with Belius and Soundararajan
[
1
]
. This contrasts with the developments on the upper bound, starting with the first order
\log T
proved in
[
20
,
1
]
, then the second order by Harper
[
15
]
, and finally the optimal upper bound with the tail distribution
[
4
]
. In fact, the present work builds on many techniques developed for the upper bound in
[
4
]
, as well as new inputs as we now explain.

Progress towards the Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating conjecture has relied on the observation that the maxima of
|\zeta|
on a short interval are related to extremes of branching processes. Indeed,
the emergence of large values of
|\zeta|
follows a scenario first identified by Bramson
[
7
]
in the setting of branching Brownian motion. As explained in the introduction of
[
4
]
,
the explicit branching structure behind
\zeta
comes from the Dirichlet polynomials
(S_{k}(h),k\geq 1)
,
|h|\leq 1
, defined in (
3
).
These polynomials behave similarly to correlated random walks, the time index
k
corresponding to primes in the loglog scale.
Bramson’s scenario translates into the ballistic behavior of

(S_{k}(h),k\leq n_{\mathcal{L}})
conditioned not to cross an upper barrier.
Estimating the maximum of
\zeta
with a precision of order one is a delicate task because the final index
n_{\mathcal{L}}
needs to be
y
-dependent and very large, i.e., the sum must include primes very close to
T
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is decomposed into two parts. First, it is shown that large values of
S_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}
indeed imply large values of
\log|\zeta|
, cf. Proposition
1
. Second, we prove that large values of
S_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}
of the claimed size are achieved, see Proposition
2
.
Proposition
2
builds on two techniques from
[
4
]
, namely the introduction of a lower barrier ensuring that large deviations of the increments of
S_{k}
can be obtained even for large primes, and the precise encoding through Dirichlet sums of the event that
S
remains in the corridor defined by an upper barrier and lower barrier.

To justify that large values of
S_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}
imply large values of
\log|\zeta|
, the first order asymptotics from
[
1
]
relied on working on the right of the critical line. However, implementing this method for the much finer tightness would be considerably more involved. Instead, Proposition
1
uses a new, simpler argument allowing to work directly on the critical line, through an integral approximation of
\zeta
by a finite Euler product (Lemma
5
), and a control of the regularity in
h
of
S_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}
on high points (Proposition
4
).

With these methods developed for Theorem
1
, we can also complement the upper bound
F(y)\ll ye^{-2y}
from
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, and show that
F(y)\asymp ye^{-2y}
for positive
y
.

Theorem 2
.

For any
C>0
there exists
c>0
such that
for any
10\leq y\leq C\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}
, we have

(1)

\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{|h|\leq 1}|\zeta(1/2+\mathrm{i}\tau+\mathrm{i}h)|>e^{y}\frac{\log T}{(\log\log T)^{3/4}}\Big)\geq c\,ye^{-2y}e^{-y^{2}/\log\log T}.

This proves the matching lower bound of the upper tail not only in the in the exponential regime
y\leq\sqrt{\log\log T}
but also in the Gaussian regime
\sqrt{\log\log T}\leq y\leq C\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}
, because the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 1]
implies the Gaussian decay in this range.

The estimate (
1
) essentially
matches the range
y={\rm o}(t)
proved by Bramson
[
8
]
for the branching Brownian motion up to time
t
.
(The time
t
corresponds to
\log\log T
in our problem.)
It is weaker by a logarithmic factor as it would corresponds to
y\leq C\,t/\log t
in the branching Brownian motion case.
We are not aware of other examples of log-correlated processes where the order of the right tail of the maximum is known to this level of precision. In fact, any form of decay has only been proved for a few models in this universality class.
Notably
for the branching random walk, the best known range is
y={\rm O}(\sqrt{t})

[
5
]
, which matches the known precision for the two dimensional discrete Gaussian free field on the
N\times N
square grid,
y={\rm O}(\sqrt{\log N})

[
12
,
11
,
6
]
.
A finer control of the contributions from small primes in the random walk would improve this range of
y
in Theorem
2
to match Bramson’s.

The distributional limit obtained in Corollary
1
is presumably unique but we believe this is out of reach with current number theory techniques. Moreover, no explicit formula for
F
was conjectured.
Indeed, denoting
U_{n}
a Haar-distributed
n\times n
unitary matrix,
[
13
]
proposed a very precise limiting distribution for

(2)

\sup_{|z|=1}\big(\log|{\rm det}(z-U_{n})|-\log n+\frac{3}{4}\log\log n\big),

but as explained in
[
14
]
this limit is not expected to coincide with
F
: It
primarily suggested
the characteristic exponent
3/4
and the tail distribution
ye^{-2y}
for
\zeta
, which are the prominent signatures of extremal statistics in log-correlated fields
[
9
]
.
Progress on a limit for (
2
) culminated in the breakthrough proofs of
tightness
[
10
]
and uniqueness
[
22
]
of a limiting distribution for the more general circular beta ensembles, after initial steps verifying the first
[
2
]
and second order terms
[
21
]
.

The exact form of the limiting distribution of (
2
), and universality of its right tail, remain open.

Proof of Theorem

Let

n_{0}:=\lfloor y\rfloor\text{ and }n:=\lfloor\log\log T\rfloor\text{ and }n_{\mathcal{L}}:=n-n_{0}.

For

(3)

S_{k}(h)=\sum_{n_{0}<\log\log p\leq k}{\rm Re}\Big(p^{-(1/2+\mathrm{i}\tau+\mathrm{i}h)}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot p^{-2(1/2+\mathrm{i}\tau+\mathrm{i}h)}\Big).

Essentially one can think of

as an approximation to

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\tau+\mathrm{i}h+\mathrm{i}x)|f(e^{k}x)\ e^{k}{\rm d}x.

for some choice of smoothing with

compactly supported.

We will show that with high probability the local maxima of
S_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}(h)
arise at those
h
at which the partial sums
S_{k}(h)
evolve in a predictable manner as
k
runs from
n_{0}
to
n_{\mathcal{L}}
. More precisely, the partial sums
S_{k}(h)
of maximizing
h

and

(defined below) for all

. Once

reaches

there are only

well-spaced (i.e,

spaced) values of

that can satisfy all those constraints, thus identifying the maximum almost uniquely.

In order to define
L_{k}
and
U_{k}
we introduce the

,

(4)

\alpha=1-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\log n}{n},

Furthermore given a function

, we define a symmetrized version,

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}(f)(k):=\begin{cases}f(k-n_{0})&\text{ for }n_{0}<k\leq\frac{n}{2},\\
f(n_{\mathcal{L}}-k)&\text{ for }\frac{n}{2}<k<n_{\mathcal{L}},\\
0&\text{ for }k\geq n_{\mathcal{L}}\text{ or }k\leq n_{0}.\end{cases}

Then, the so-called

(i.e., values
L_{k}
and
U_{k}
) are defined as

(5)

\displaystyle U_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{y}{10}+\alpha(k-n_{0})-10\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}(x\mapsto\log(x))(k),

(6)

\displaystyle L_{k}

\displaystyle=-10y+\alpha(k-n_{0})-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}(x\mapsto x^{3/4})(k).

We now introduce the set of
good points

G_{\mathcal{L}}
, defined more generally for

n_{0}\leq\ell\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}
as

(7)

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]\cap e^{-(n_{\mathcal{L}}-n_{0})}\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle G_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\{h\in G_{0}:S_{k}(h)\in[L_{k},U_{k}]\text{ for all }k\leq\ell\}.

We will show that with high probability the local maximum belongs to
G_{\mathcal{L}}
.

We first comment on the above choices of barriers and discrete sets.
The interval
[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]
defining
G_{0}
needs to be strictly included in the original interval
[-1,1]
, as it will be apparent in the proof of Proposition
3
. Moreover, the discretization step

will be convenient for the proof of Proposition

as it corresponds to the number of steps of the random walk (

would work. However, contrary to
[
4
]
, it is essential that the upper barrier is convex and not concave, as we will see in the proof of Proposition

.

The proof of the main theorem reduces now to two main propositions. In the first proposition, we show how the local maxima of the zeta function arise from the good points
h\in G_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Proposition 1
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that uniformly in
T\geq 100
and
0\leq y\leq(\log\log T)^{1/10}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{|h|\leq 1}

\displaystyle\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\tau+\mathrm{i}h)|\geq n-\frac{3}{4}\log n-100y-C\Big)\geq\mathbb{P}\Big(\exists h\in G_{\mathcal{L}}\Big)+{\rm O}(e^{-y}).

In the second proposition, we then show that good points exist with high probability.

Proposition 2

There exists
c>0
such that uniformly in
T\geq 100
and
0\leq y\leq(\log\log T)^{1/10}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\exists h\in G_{\mathcal{L}}\Big)=1+{\rm O}(y^{-c}).

and Proposition

.

There exists an absolute constant

We have

\displaystyle G^{-}_{\Delta,A}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{1}(x\in[\Delta^{-A/2},\Delta^{-1}-\Delta^{-A/2}])\cdot(1-Ce^{-\Delta^{A-1}}).

Proof.

Lemma 5

\displaystyle\ll e^{n_{\mathcal{L}}-n_{0}-k+10((k-n_{0})\wedge(n_{\mathcal{L}}-k))^{3/4}}\cdot e^{-v(n_{\mathcal{L}}-k)}\cdot v!\cdot e^{\tilde{C}v}\cdot(n_{\mathcal{L}}-k)^{v}\cdot e^{n_{\mathcal{L}}-k},

Proof of Proposition
6

We first need a lemma which precisely captures the coupling/decoupling of the Gaussian walks
\mathcal{G}_{k}(h)
defined in (
9
) as a function of the distance
|h-h^{\prime}|
. For this, the following elementary lemma will be key in the decoupling regime
|h-h^{\prime}|>e^{-j}
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
|\rho|<\mathfrak{s}^{2}
.
Consider the following Gaussian vectors and their covariance matrices:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{s}^{2}&\rho\\
\rho&\mathfrak{s}^{2}\end{matrix}\right),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{N}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{2}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{s}^{2}+|\rho|&0\\
0&\mathfrak{s}^{2}+|\rho|\end{matrix}\right).

Then for any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have

\mathbb{P}((\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1})\in A)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{s}^{2}+|\rho|}{\mathfrak{s}^{2}-|\rho|}}\cdot\mathbb{P}((\mathcal{N}_{2},\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{2})\in A).

Proof.

The proof is simply by expanding the density of
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1})
, which is

(70)

\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\mathfrak{s}^{4}-\rho^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\mathfrak{s}^{2}w^{2}+\mathfrak{s}^{2}z^{2}-2\rho wz}{2(\mathfrak{s}^{4}-\rho^{2})}\right).

If
\rho\geq 0
then for any
w,z\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\mathfrak{s}^{2}w^{2}+\mathfrak{s}^{2}z^{2}-2\rho wz\geq(\mathfrak{s}^{2}-\rho)(w^{2}+z^{2})
so that

\frac{\mathfrak{s}^{2}w^{2}+\mathfrak{s}^{2}z^{2}-2\rho wz}{2(\mathfrak{s}^{4}-\rho^{2})}\geq\frac{w^{2}+z^{2}}{2(\mathfrak{s}^{2}+\rho)},

and the conclusion follows.
If
\rho\leq 0
then from the previous case for any
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathbb{P}((\mathcal{N}_{1},-\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1})\in B)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{s}^{2}-\rho}{\mathfrak{s}^{2}+\rho}}\cdot\mathbb{P}((\mathcal{N}_{2},-\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{2})\in B),

which concludes the proof by choosing
B=\{(x,-y):(x,y)\in A\}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6
.

We have

\mathbb{E}[(\#\mathfrak{G}^{+}_{\mathcal{L}})^{2}]=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{S}(h)\cap\mathfrak{S}(h^{\prime})\Big)

where

(71)

\mathfrak{S}(h)=\{\mathcal{G}_{k}(h)\in[L_{k}-1,U_{k}+1],\forall n_{0}<k\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}\},\quad h\in[-1,1].

In what follows, we fix
h
,
h^{\prime}
and simply write
(\mathcal{G}_{k},\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime})
for
(\mathcal{G}_{k}(h),\mathcal{G}_{k}(h^{\prime}))
.
We divide the above sum over pairs in three ranges of
|h-h^{\prime}|
; this is necessary to achieve the precision
1+{\rm o}(1)
required by Proposition
6
.

6.1.
Case
|h-h^{\prime}|>e^{-n_{0}/2}

This is the dominant term.
We can express the events
\mathfrak{S}(h)
in terms of the increments using
28
, and then in terms of independent increments using Lemma
7
.
Under the product over
j
, the multiplicative error from Lemma
7
is

(72)

\prod_{n_{0}<j\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{s}_{j}^{2}+|\rho_{j}|}{\mathfrak{s}_{j}^{2}-|\rho_{j}|}}=\exp\Big({\rm O}(\sum_{n_{0}\leq j\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}}\rho_{j})\Big)=\exp\Big({\rm O}(\sum_{n_{0}\leq j\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}}\frac{1}{e^{j}|h-h^{\prime}|}\Big)\leq 1+{\rm O}(e^{-n_{0}/2}),

therefore we obtain

\sum_{|h-h^{\prime}|>e^{-n_{0}/2}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{S}(h)\cap\mathfrak{S}(h^{\prime})\Big)\leq(1+{\rm O}(n_{0}^{-10}))\cdot\Big(\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{j}\in[L_{j}-1,U_{j}+2],n_{0}<j\leq n_{\mathcal{L}})\Big)^{2},

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{j}=\sum_{i\leq j}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{j}
and the independent Gaussian centered
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{j}
’s have variance
\mathfrak{s}_{j}^{2}+|\rho_{j}|
. Moreover the change from the original interval
[L_{j}-1,U_{j}+1]
to
[L_{j}-1,U_{j}+2]
is due to (
27
) when transferring the constraint on increments back to the random walk itself.
From the Ballot theorem in Proposition
14
the barrier can be changed into
[L_{j}+1,U_{j}-1]
, and the

can be replaced by
{\mathcal{G}}_{j}

, so that in particular

\sum_{|h-h^{\prime}|>e^{-n_{0}/2}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{S}(h)\cap\mathfrak{S}(h^{\prime})\Big)\leq(1+{\rm O}(y^{-c}))(\mathbb{E}[\#\mathfrak{G}_{\mathcal{L}}^{-}])^{2}.

All the other cases will be much smaller than
(\mathbb{E}[\#\mathfrak{G}_{\mathcal{L}}^{-}])^{2}
.

6.2.
Case
e^{-n_{0}}<|h-h^{\prime}|\leq e^{-n_{0}/2}

The same reasoning as above applies in this case. The multiplicative error term analogue to (
72

{\rm O}(1)

\ll e^{2(n_{\mathcal{L}}-n_{0})}e^{-n_{0}/2}

to consider. Therefore, we obtain

\sum_{e^{-n_{0}}<|h-h^{\prime}|\leq e^{-n_{0}/2}}\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{S}(h)\cap\mathfrak{S}(h^{\prime}))\ll e^{-\frac{n_{0}}{2}}(\mathbb{E}[\#\mathfrak{G}_{\mathcal{L}}^{-}])^{2}.

6.3.
Case
e^{-(n_{\mathcal{L}}-n_{0})}\leq|h-h^{\prime}|\leq e^{-n_{0}}

We start by writing,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{G}^{+}_{\mathcal{L}}\\
e^{-(n_{\mathcal{L}}-n_{0})}<|h-h^{\prime}|\leq e^{-n_{0}}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{S}(h)\cap\mathfrak{S}(h^{\prime})\Big)=\sum_{n_{0}\leq j^{\star}\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{G}^{+}_{\mathcal{L}}\\

In order to evaluate
\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{S}(h)\cap\mathfrak{S}(h^{\prime}))
, we apply the Gaussian decorrelation Lemma
7
for the increments
j\geq j^{*}
.
For the increments before
j^{*}
, it will be useful to consider the

(73)

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{j}=\frac{{\mathcal{G}}_{j}+\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j}}{2},\qquad\mathcal{G}^{\perp}_{j}=\frac{{\mathcal{G}}_{j}-\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j}}{2},\quad n_{0}<j\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}.

Note that
(\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{j})_{j}

(\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\perp})_{j}
are independent and
\mathcal{G}_{j}=\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{j}+\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\perp}
,
\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\prime}=\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{j}-\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\perp}.

As before we can express the events
\mathfrak{S}(h)

28

7
, up to a multiplicative error equal to

\prod_{j^{*}<j\leq n_{\mathcal{L}}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{s}_{j}^{2}+|\rho_{j}|}{\mathfrak{s}_{j}^{2}-|\rho_{j}|}}={\rm O}(1).

For

6.3.1.
The Correlated term

) and (

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\perp}_{j}\in[q,q+1))\ll e^{-cq^{2}},\quad\text{for some $c>0$.}

(75)

\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{S}(h)\cap\mathfrak{S}(h^{\prime})\Big)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{j^{\star}}-1\leq v_{1}\leq U_{j^{\star}}+1\\

Conclusion.

When

7.
Proof of Theorem
2

The proof of the theorem follows the same structure as the one of Theorem 1. The parameters need to be picked differently.
We take for the times

n_{0}=\lfloor y/100\rfloor,\qquad n_{\mathcal{L}}=\log\log(T^{1/100})=n-\log 100.

The partial sums on primes are now starting from
p=2
and not
\exp e^{n_{0}}

(78)

S_{j}(h)=\sum_{\log\log p\leq j}{\rm Re}\Big(p^{-(1/2+\mathrm{i}\tau+\mathrm{i}h)}+\frac{1}{2}p^{-2(1/2+\mathrm{i}\tau+\mathrm{i}h)}\Big),\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

The set of good points are

where the barriers are now for

(79)

\displaystyle U_{j}

\displaystyle=y+\alpha j-10\log(j\wedge(n-j)),

\displaystyle L_{j}

\displaystyle=-10+(\alpha+\frac{y}{n_{\mathcal{L}}})j-(j\wedge(n-j))^{3/4}.

The slope
\alpha
is
1-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\log n}{n}
as before. Both barriers are convex, which is crucial. Note that the final interval for
S_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}
is
[L_{n_{\mathcal{L}}},U_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}]

U_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}=n-\frac{3}{4}\log n+y\qquad L_{n_{\mathcal{L}}}=n-\frac{3}{4}\log n+y-10.

The reason for the slightly larger slope in

(\alpha+\frac{y}{n_{\mathcal{L}}})
instead of
\alpha
, is to ensure that the width of the final interval is order one. The factor

will not affect the proof.

It is necessary to take

, as this is the origin of the factor
y
in front of the exponential decay in Theorem
2
.
For

of order one, it would be possible to take

. However, for larger

, the spread

could be quite large for small

For this reason, the barrier starts at
n_{0}
, a multiple of

. For these times, the spread is proportional to variance and the Gaussian comparison goes through.

Unlike the left tail, we do need to include the small primes in the partial sums. Dropping the first
\exp e^{n_{0}}
primes would give a lower bound
ye^{-2y}e^{-n_{0}}e^{-y^{2}/n}
, which is suboptimal for
n_{0}\asymp y
.
A more involved analysis of the small primes would probably allow to improve the result range of Theorem
2
to
y={\rm o}(n)
, matching the branching Brownian motion estimate.

We denote

\mathbb{P}_{x}=\mathbb{P}_{(0,x)}

Lemma 15
.

Let
x,y>0
. Then

\mathbb{P}_{(0,x)}^{(t,y)}\left(m_{t}\geq 0\right)=1-e^{-\frac{2xy}{t}}.

Proof.

From the reflection principle, for any measurable
A\subset(0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(m_{t}\geq 0,B_{t}\in A\right)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(B_{t}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(B_{t}\in-A\right).

This implies

\mathbb{P}_{(0,x)}^{(t,y)}\left(m_{t}\geq 0\right)=1-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(B_{t}\in-[y,y+\varepsilon]\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(B_{t}\in[y,y+\varepsilon]\right)}=1-e^{-\frac{2xy}{t}},

concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 16
.

Let
a,c>0
and
A\subset[-c,a]
be measurable. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\leq a,m_{t}\geq-c,B_{t}\in A\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(m_{t}\geq-c,B_{t}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(B_{t}\in A-2a\right).

Proof.

The above left-hand side is

\mathbb{P}\left(m_{t}\geq-c,B_{t}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq a,m_{t}\geq-c,B_{t}\in A\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(m_{t}\geq-c,B_{t}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq a,B_{t}\in A\right).

From the reflection principle, this last probability is
\mathbb{P}\left(B_{t}\in 2a-A\right)
.
∎

Lemma 17
.

Let
\delta>1/2
,
v_{s}\geq y+\min(s,t-s)^{\delta}
. Let
c\in(0,2-\frac{1}{\delta})
. Then uniformly in

t\geq 0
,
2\leq y\leq t^{1/10}
,
a,b\in[1,y-1]
we have

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{0\leq B_{s}\leq v_{s}\}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(m_{t}\geq 0\right)\cdot\left(1+{\rm O}(e^{-\min(|y-a|,|y-b|)^{c}})\right).

Proof.

In this proof we abbreviate
B\geq 0
for
m_{t}\geq 0
and start with

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0,\exists s:B_{s}>v_{s}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t/2}\left(\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0,\exists s\in[k,k+1]:B_{s}>v_{k}\right)+\mathbb{P}_{(0,b)}^{(t,a)}\left(B\geq 0,\exists s\in[k,k+1]:B_{s}>v_{k}\right).\right)

The first probability above is smaller than

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0,\exists s\in[0,k+1]:B_{s}>v_{k}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0\right)-\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0,\max_{[0,k+1]}B<v_{k}\right).

We write

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0,\max_{[0,k+1]}B<v_{k}\right)\\
=\int_{0}^{v_{k}}\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(k+1,x)}\left(B\geq 0,\max_{[0,k+1]}B<v_{k}\right)\mathbb{P}_{(k+1,x)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0\right)\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}(B_{k+1}\in{\rm d}x).

The first probability in this integral is estimated with Lemma
16
:

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(k+1,x)}\left(B\geq 0,\max_{[0,k+1]}B<v_{k}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(k+1,x)}\left(B\geq 0\right)-e^{-\frac{2(v_{k}-a)(v_{k}-x)}{k+1}}.

This gives

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0,\exists s\in[k,k+1]:B_{s}>v_{k}\right)\\
\leq\int_{0}^{v_{k}}e^{-\frac{2(v_{k}-a)(v_{k}-x)}{k+1}}\mathbb{P}_{(k+1,x)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0\right)\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}(B_{k+1}\in{\rm d}x).

From Lemma
15
, we have
\mathbb{P}_{(k+1,x)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0\right)\leq 5\frac{xb}{t}
.
Moreover,
\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}(B_{s}\in{\rm d}x)=\mathbb{P}_{(0,0)}^{(t,0)}(B_{s}\in{\rm d}x+x_{s})=\frac{e^{-(x-x_{s})^{2}/(2w_{s})}}{\sqrt{2\pi w_{s}}}{\rm d}x
where
w_{s}=s(t-s)/t
,
x_{s}=(1-\frac{s}{t})a+\frac{s}{t}b
.
This gives

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(B\geq 0,\exists s\in[k,k+1]:B_{s}>v_{k}\right)\leq C\frac{b}{t}\int_{0}^{v_{k}}e^{-\frac{2(v_{k}-a)(v_{k}-x)}{k+1}}x\frac{e^{-\frac{(x-x_{k+1})^{2}}{2w_{k+1}}}}{\sqrt{w_{k+1}}}{\rm d}x.

In the above integral, the contribution from
|x-x_{k+1}|>v_{k}/3
is bounded with

(88)

\int_{|x-x_{k+1}|>v_{k}/3}(x_{k+1}+|x-x_{k+1}|)\frac{e^{-\frac{(x-x_{k+1})^{2}}{2w_{k+1}}}}{\sqrt{w_{k+1}}}{\rm d}x\leq Cx_{k+1}e^{-\frac{v_{k}^{2}}{100w_{k+1}}}.

The regime
|x-x_{k+1}|<v_{k}/3
gives the error

(89)

\int_{|x-x_{k+1}|<v_{k}/3}e^{-\frac{(k^{\delta}+|y-a|)k^{\delta}}{k+1}}x\frac{e^{-\frac{(x-x_{k+1})^{2}}{2w_{k+1}}}}{\sqrt{w_{k+1}}}{\rm d}x\leq Cx_{k+1}e^{-\frac{(k^{\delta}+|y-a|)k^{\delta}}{k+1}}.

We first bound the sum of the error terms from (
89

0\leq k\leq t/2
.
For

, from the hypothesis

we have

\sum_{k^{\delta}<|y-a|}x_{k+1}e^{-\frac{(k^{\delta}+|y-a|)k^{\delta}}{k+1}}\leq 2a\sum_{k^{\delta}<|y-a|}e^{-\frac{|y-a|k^{\delta}}{k+1}}\leq 2a|y-a|^{1/\delta}e^{-|y-a|^{2-\frac{1}{\delta}}}=a\,{\rm O}(e^{-|y-a|^{c}}).

For

\sum_{k\geq|y-a|^{1/\delta}}x_{k}e^{-k^{2\delta-1}}\leq\sum_{k\geq|y-a|^{1/\delta}}(a+\frac{kb}{t})e^{-k^{2\delta-1}}\leq(a+1)\sum_{k\geq|y-a|^{1/\delta}}ke^{-k^{2\delta-1}}\\
\leq C_{\delta}(a+1)\int_{v>|y-a|^{2-\frac{1}{\delta}}}v^{\frac{3-2\delta}{2\delta-1}}e^{-v}{\rm d}v=a\,{\rm O}(e^{-|y-a|^{c}}).

The same estimate can be obtained for the sum over
k
from (
88

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{0\leq B_{s}\leq v_{s}\}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(m_{t}\geq 0\right)+{\rm O}\left(\frac{ab}{t}e^{-\min(|y-a|,|y-b|)^{c}}\right).

The result follows from the above estimate and Lemma

Lemma 18
.

Let
\delta>1/2>\alpha>0
. Then there exists
c=c(\alpha,\delta)
, such that, uniformly in

t\geq 0
,
y\geq 10
,
a,b\in[1,y-1]
, and uniformly in the functions
v_{s}\geq y+\min(s,t-s)^{\delta}
,

, we have

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{u_{s}\leq B_{s}\leq v_{s}\}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(m_{t}\geq 0\right)\cdot\left(1+{\rm O}(d^{-c})\right),

where
d=\min(|y-a|,|y-b|,|a|,|b|)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
\alpha+\delta<1
. We also pick
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and write
d_{0}=d^{1-\varepsilon}
. Let
s_{1},s_{2}
be the solutions of
s_{1}^{\alpha}=d_{0}
,
(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}=d_{0}
.
Let

(90)

\bar{u}_{s}=(d_{0}+(s-s_{1})\alpha s_{1}^{\alpha-1})\mathbf{1}_{s^{\alpha}<d_{0}}+(d_{0}-(s-s_{2})\alpha(t-s_{2})^{\alpha-1})\mathbf{1}_{(t-s)^{\alpha}<d_{0}}+\min(s,t-s)^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{s_{1}<s<s_{2}}.

In other words,
\bar{u}
is the function coinciding with
\min(s,t-s)^{\alpha}
on
(s_{0},s_{1})
and linearly expanded on the complement, with continuous derivative. Note that
\bar{u}_{0}=\bar{u}_{t}=(1-\alpha)d_{0}
. We also denote

\bar{v}_{s}=(1-\frac{s}{t})a+\frac{s}{t}b+\min(s,t-s)^{\delta}+d
. We have

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{u_{s}\leq B_{s}\leq v_{s}\}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{\bar{u}_{s}\leq B_{s}\leq\bar{v}_{s}\}\right).

The Cameron-Martin formula gives

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{\bar{u}_{s}\leq B_{s}\leq\bar{v}_{s}\}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\bar{u}}_{s}{\rm d}B_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\bar{u}}_{s}^{2}{\rm d}s}\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{d^{1-\varepsilon}\leq B_{s}\leq\bar{v}_{s}-\int_{0}^{u}\dot{\bar{u}}_{u}{\rm d}u\}}\right],

where we denote the derivative in
s
of
f
by
\dot{f}
.
We now bound both terms in the measure bias, deterministically. First,

\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\bar{u}}_{s}^{2}{\rm d}s\leq C\int_{s>s_{1}}s^{2\alpha-2}{\rm d}s+C\int_{s<s_{1}}s_{1}^{2\alpha-2}{\rm d}s\leq Cd^{-(\frac{1}{\alpha}-2)(1-\varepsilon)}.

Moreover, by integration by parts we have (using the fact that
\bar{u}
has continuous derivative)

-\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\bar{u}}_{s}{\rm d}B_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}B_{s}\ddot{\bar{u}}_{s}{\rm d}s-B_{t}\dot{\bar{u}}_{t}+B_{0}\dot{\bar{u}}_{0}=\int_{0}^{t}\big(B_{s}-((1-\frac{s}{t})B_{0}+\frac{s}{t}B_{t})\big)\ddot{\bar{u}}_{s}{\rm d}s.

On the set
\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{B_{s}\leq{\bar{v}}_{s}\}
, we therefore have the deterministic bound

-\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\bar{u}}_{s}{\rm d}B_{s}\geq-\int_{s^{\alpha}>d^{1-\varepsilon}}(d^{1-\varepsilon}+s^{\delta})s^{\alpha-2}{\rm d}s\geq-C(d^{-(\frac{1}{\alpha}-2)(1-\varepsilon)}+d^{(\frac{\delta}{\alpha}+1-\frac{1}{\alpha})(1-\varepsilon)}).

We have therefore proved

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{\bar{u}_{s}\leq B_{s}\leq\bar{v}_{s}\}\right)\\
\geq\exp\left({\rm O}(d^{-(\frac{1}{\alpha}-2)(1-\varepsilon)}+d^{-\frac{1-\alpha-\delta}{\alpha}(1-\varepsilon)})\right)\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{d^{1-\varepsilon}\leq B_{s}\leq v_{s}-\int_{0}^{s}\dot{\bar{u}}_{u}{\rm d}u\}\right).

The desired lower bound follows by using Lemma
17
, noting that
\frac{(a-d^{1-\varepsilon})(b-d^{1-\varepsilon})}{t}=\frac{ab}{t}(1+{\rm O}(d^{-\varepsilon}))
.
∎

For the upper bound, we only need to bound

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-\bar{u}_{k}\leq S_{k}\}\right)
,
where
\bar{u}
is defined in (
90
).
By the change of variables
S_{k}=\tilde{S}_{k}-\sum_{0\leq i\leq k-1}(\bar{u}_{i+1}-\bar{u}_{i})
, and denoting
d_{1}=(1-\alpha)d^{1-\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-\bar{u}_{k}\leq S_{k}\}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\bar{u}_{k+1}-\bar{u}_{k}))^{2}+\sum_{k}(S_{k+1}-S_{k})(\bar{u}_{k+1}-\bar{u}_{k})}\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-d_{1}\leq S_{k}\}}\right]\\
\leq\mathbb{E}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left[e^{\sum_{k}(S_{k+1}-S_{k})(\bar{u}_{k+1}-\bar{u}_{k})}\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-d_{1}\leq S_{k}\}}\right].

Let
\bar{S}_{s}=S_{s}-(a\frac{t-s}{t}+b\frac{s}{t})
. We have

\sum_{k}(S_{k+1}-S_{k})(\bar{u}_{k+1}-\bar{u}_{k})=\sum_{k}(\bar{S}_{k+1}-\bar{S}_{k})(\bar{u}_{k+1}-\bar{u}_{k})=\sum_{k}a_{k}\bar{S}_{k},

where
the constants
a_{k}
satisfy
0\leq a_{k}\leq 10\min(k,t-k+1)^{\alpha-2}
and vanish outside
[d_{2},t-d_{2}]
where we define
d_{2}=d^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha}}
.
As
ab\leq\max(a^{2},b^{2})
, we have obtained

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-\bar{u}_{k}\leq S_{k}\}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left[e^{2\sum_{k\leq t/2}a_{k}\bar{S}_{k}}\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-d_{1}\leq S_{k}\}}\right].

Let
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\alpha)
and define
\kappa=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha-\varepsilon_{0})
. Note that for any integer
v\geq 1
,
\sum_{k\leq t/2}a_{k}\bar{S}_{k}>vd^{-\kappa}
implies that there exists
k
such that
d_{2}\leq k\leq t/2
such that

\bar{S}_{k}>vk^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{0}}
. This observation together with the union bound gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left[e^{2\sum_{k\leq t/2}a_{k}\bar{S}_{k}}\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-d_{1}\leq S_{k}\}}\right]-\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-d_{1}\leq S_{k}\}\right)(1+d^{-\kappa})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{v\geq 1,d_{0}\leq k\leq t/2,w\geq vk^{1/2+\varepsilon_{0}}}e^{vd^{-\kappa}}\,\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\{\bar{S}_{k}\in[w,w+1]\}\cap_{j\leq k}\{S_{j}\geq-d_{1})\}\right)

(91)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{v\geq 1,d_{0}\leq k\leq t/2,w\geq vk^{1/2+\varepsilon_{0}}}e^{vd^{-\kappa}}\,\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\bar{S}_{k}\in[w,w+1]\right)\times

(92)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\Big(\sup_{c\in[w,w+1]+a\frac{t-k}{t}+b\frac{k}{t}}\mathbb{P}_{(0,c)}^{(t-k,b)}\left(\cap_{1\leq j\leq t-k}\{S_{j}\geq-d_{1}\}\right)\Big)

where we used the Markov property for the second inequality. To bound the first probability above, note that under
\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}
, the random variable
\bar{S}_{k}
is centered, Gaussian with variance
k-\frac{k^{2}}{t}\asymp k
. For the second probability,
from Lemma
20
, we have uniformly in all parameters

(93)

\mathbb{P}_{(0,c)}^{(t-k,b)}\left(\cap_{1\leq j\leq t-k}\{S_{j}\geq-d_{1}\}\right)\ll\frac{(w+a\frac{t-k}{t}+b\frac{k}{t})b}{t}.

This allows to bound the left-hand side of (
92
) with

\sum_{v\geq 1,d_{0}\leq k\leq t/2,w\geq vk^{1/2+\varepsilon_{0}}}e^{vd^{-\kappa}-c\frac{w^{2}}{k}}\cdot\frac{(w+a\frac{t-k}{t}+b\frac{k}{t})b}{t}

for some absolute
c>0
.
The above sum over
w
and then
v
is
\ll e^{-c^{\prime}k^{2\varepsilon_{0}}}
for some absolute
c^{\prime}>0
.
We conclude that uniformly in our parameters, the left-hand side of (
92
) is bounded with

\frac{b}{t}\sum_{d_{0}\leq k\leq t/2}e^{-c^{\prime}k^{2\varepsilon_{0}}}(1+a\frac{t-k}{t}+b\frac{k}{t})\ll\frac{ab}{t}e^{-c^{\prime}d_{0}^{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\ll d^{-\kappa}\frac{ab}{t}.

Finally, Lemma
21
yields

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{-d_{1}\leq S_{k}\}\right)=2\frac{ab}{\sigma}(1+{\rm O}(d^{-c})).

This concludes the proof.

Lemma 19
.

There exists
C=C(\kappa)>0
such that for any
t\geq 10
,
a\geq 1
, we have

(94)

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{S_{k}\geq 0\}\right)\leq C\frac{a}{\sqrt{t}}.

Proof.

By monotonicity in
a
, we can consider
a\in\mathbb{N}
without loss of generality . Moreover, we have

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{S_{k}\geq 0\}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq\frac{t}{a^{2}}}\{\frac{S_{ka^{2}}}{a}\geq 0\}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{(0,1)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq\frac{t}{a^{2}}}\{\tilde{S}_{k}\geq 0\}\right),

where
\tilde{S}_{k}:=S_{ka^{2}}/a
has independent Gaussian increments with variance in
[\kappa,\kappa^{-1}]
, like
S
. This proves that (
94
) only needs to be proved for
a=1
.

Consider
T=\min(t,\min\{k\leq t\mid S_{k}\leq 0\})
. By the stopping time theorem,

\mathbb{E}[S_{T}]=1,

so that

\mathbb{E}[|S_{t}|\mathbf{1}_{T\geq t}]=1+\mathbb{E}[-S_{T}\mathbf{1}_{T<t}].

Moreover, we have the correlation inequality

(95)

\mathbb{E}[|S_{t}|\mathbf{1}_{T>t}]\geq\mathbb{E}[\max(0,S_{t})]\mathbb{P}[T>t],

which is a simple consequence of the Harris inequality. Indeed, consider the random walk
Z^{(n)}_{k}=1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq kn}\varepsilon_{j}
with independent Bernoulli random variables
\varepsilon_{k}
, and
I^{(n)}=\{\lfloor n{\rm Var}S_{k}\rfloor,k\geq 1\}
; denote
U^{(n)}=\min\{k\in I^{(n)}:Z^{(n)}_{k}\leq 0\}
. Then the functions

\mathbf{1}_{U^{(n)}>t}
and
\max(0,Z^{(n)}_{t})
are non-decreasing functions of
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\leq nt}
, so that
\mathbb{E}[\max(0,Z^{(n)}_{t})\mathbf{1}_{U^{(n)}>t}]\geq\mathbb{E}[\max(0,Z^{(n)}_{t})]\cdot\mathbb{P}[U^{(n)}>t]
. This implies (
95
)
by taking
n\to\infty
.

We have obtained

(96)

\mathbb{P}[T>t]\leq C\frac{1+\mathbb{E}[-S_{T}\mathbf{1}_{T<t}]}{\sqrt{t}},

and we will now prove that

(97)

\mathbb{E}[-S_{T}\mathbf{1}_{T<t}]\leq C_{1},

for some
C_{1}>0
uniform in
t
, which together with (
96
) will conclude the proof of (
94
).

To prove (
97
), first note that
\mathbb{E}[-S_{T}\mathbf{1}_{T<t}]\leq\mathbb{E}[-S_{T_{0}}]
where
T_{0}=\min\{k\geq 0\mid S_{k}\leq 0\}
. We now consider

Z_{n}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}\in[n,n+1),k<T_{0}},\ n\geq 0,

the time spent by
S
in
[n,n+1)
before it hits
0
. Define
U_{0}=0
and
U_{i+1}=\min\{u\geq U_{i}+n^{2}:S_{u}\in[n,n+1)\}
. For any
\lambda\geq n^{2}
we have the inclusion

\{Z_{n}\geq\lambda\}\subset\cap_{i\leq\lambda/n^{2}}\left\{S_{U_{i}+n^{2}}-S_{U_{i}}\geq-(n+1)\right\}.

By the strong Markov property the events on the right-hand side are independent, and there exists
\alpha=\alpha(\kappa)
such that each such event has probability at most
1-\alpha
, uniformly in
n
.
This implies

\mathbb{E}(Z_{n})\leq Cn^{2}

for some
C=C(\kappa)
, a key estimate in the last inequality below: for any
\ell\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{P}[|S_{T_{0}}|\geq\ell]\leq\sum_{k,n\geq 0}\mathbb{P}[S_{k}\in[n,n+1),|S_{k+1}-S_{k}|\geq\ell+(n+1),k<T_{0}]\\
=\sum_{k,n\geq 0}\mathbb{P}[S_{k}\in[n,n+1),k<T_{0}]\cdot\mathbb{P}[|S_{k+1}-S_{k}|\geq\ell+(n+1)]\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-c(\ell+n)^{2}}\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]\leq Ce^{-c\ell^{2}},

which immediately implies
\mathbb{E}[-S_{T_{0}}]\leq C_{1}
and concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 20
.

With the same notations as Proposition
14
, there exists
C=C(\kappa)>0
such that for any
t\geq 10
,
a,b\geq 1
we have

(98)

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{S_{k}\geq 0\}\right)\leq C\frac{ab}{t}.

Proof.

We first assume that
a,b\leq\sqrt{t}
. Let
n_{1}=\lfloor t/3\rfloor
and
n_{2}=\lfloor 2t/3\rfloor
, and
p_{t}(x)=e^{-x^{2}/(2t)}/\sqrt{2\pi t}
, and abbreviate
\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}=\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}(S>0)
. Then
\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}(S>0)
is equal to

\frac{1}{p_{t}(a-b)}\iint_{x_{1},x_{2}>0}p_{n_{1}}(x_{1}-a)\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(n_{1},x_{1})}p_{n_{2}-n_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})\mathbb{P}_{(n_{1},x_{1})}^{(n_{2},x_{2})}p_{t-n_{2}}(b-x_{2})\mathbb{P}_{(n_{2},x_{2})}^{(t,b)}{\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2}\\
\leq C\int_{x_{1}}p_{n_{1}}(x_{1}-a)\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(n_{1},x_{1})}{\rm d}x_{1}\int_{x_{2}>0}p_{t-n_{2}}(b-x_{2})\mathbb{P}_{(n_{2},x_{2})}^{(t,b)}{\rm d}x_{2}\leq C\frac{ab}{t},

where we have used the trivial bounds
\mathbb{P}_{(n_{1},x_{1})}^{(n_{2},x_{2})}\leq 1
,
p_{n_{2}-n_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})\leq Ct^{-1/2}
, the estimate (valid for
a,b\leq\sqrt{t}
)
(p_{t}(a-b))^{-1}\leq C\sqrt{t}
, and Lemma
19
.

For the general case, we can assume
a<\sqrt{t}<b
and
ab<t
. Let
B
be a Brownian bridge from
a
(
s=0
) to
b
(
s=\sigma
). There exists
s_{1}<\dots<s_{t}=\sigma
such that
(S_{k})_{k\leq t}
and
(B_{s_{k}})_{k\leq t}
have the same distribution.
Moreover, from
[
8
, pages 21, 22]
, by a simple coupling argument the function

(99)

b\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(b,t)}(B_{s}>0,0\leq s\leq\sigma\mid B_{s_{i}}>0,0\leq i\leq t)\ \mbox{ is non-decreasing}.

This implies

\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(b,t)}(B_{s_{i}}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq t)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(b,t)}(B_{s_{i}}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq t)}{\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(b,t)}(B_{s}\geq 0,0\leq s\leq\sigma)}\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(b,t)}(B_{s}\geq 0,0\leq s\leq\sigma)\\
\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(\sqrt{t},t)}(B_{s_{i}}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq t)}{\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(\sqrt{t},t)}(B_{s}\geq 0,0\leq s\leq\sigma)}\mathbb{P}_{(a,0)}^{(b,t)}(B_{s}\geq 0,0\leq s\leq\sigma).

From Lemma
15
, the denominator is lower-bounded with
c\frac{a\sqrt{t}}{t}
and the last probability is upper-bounded with
\frac{ab}{t}
. And from the previously discussed case, the numerator is at most
\frac{a\sqrt{t}}{t}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 21
.

With the same notations as Proposition
14
, there exists
C=C(\kappa)>0
such that for any
t\geq 10
,
y\leq t^{1/10}
,
1\leq a,b\leq y-1
we have

\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{S_{k}\geq 0\}\right)=\frac{2ab}{\sigma}\cdot\left(1+{\rm O}_{\alpha,\delta,\kappa}(d^{-c})\right).

Proof.

The lower bound follows directly from Lemma
15
.

For the upper bound, consider first the case
a=b=d
.
Note that for any
k\leq t-1
and
u,v>0
, under
\mathbb{P}_{(k,u)}^{(k+1,v)}
we can decompose

B_{s}=(k+1-s)v+(s-k)u+\tilde{B}_{s-k}-(s-k)\tilde{B}_{1},

where
\tilde{B}
is a standard Brownian motion.
Therefore, if there exists
s\in[k,k+1]
such that
B_{s}<0
, we have
\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}|\tilde{B}_{u}|+|\tilde{B}_{1}|>\min(u,v)
.
As
|\tilde{B}_{1}|+\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}|\tilde{B}_{u}|
is clearly dominated by
|\mathcal{N}|
with
\mathcal{N}
a Gaussian random variable with variance
{\rm O}(1)
,
by a union bound, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{(0,d)}^{(t,d)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{S_{k}\geq 0\}\right)-\mathbb{P}_{(0,d)}^{(t,d)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq t}\{B_{s}\geq 0\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{u.v\geq 0,k\leq t-1}\max_{x\in[u,u+1]}\mathbb{P}_{(0,d)}^{(k,x)}(S_{i}>0,1\leq i\leq k)\cdot\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{N}|>\min(u,v))

\displaystyle\cdot\max_{y\in[v,v+1]}\mathbb{P}_{(k+1,y)}^{(t,d)}(S_{i}>0,k+1\leq i\leq t)\cdot\mathbb{P}(B_{k}\in[u,u+1],B_{k+1}\in[v,v+1]).

All terms above can be bounded, giving the estimate

\sum_{0\leq k\leq t/2,u,v\geq 0}\frac{du}{k+1}\frac{dv}{t-k}e^{-c\min(u,v)^{2}}\frac{e^{-c\frac{(u-d)^{2}}{k+1}}}{\sqrt{k+1}}e^{-c(v-u)^{2}}\\
\ll\frac{d^{2}}{t}\sum_{0\leq k\leq t/2,u\geq 0}\frac{u^{2}}{(k+1)^{3/2}}e^{-cu^{2}-c\frac{(u-d)^{2}}{k+1}}\\
\ll\frac{d^{2}}{t}\sum_{0\leq k\leq t/2}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{3/2}}e^{-c\frac{d^{2}}{k+1}}\leq C\frac{d^{2}}{t}d^{-1/4+\varepsilon},

for any arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, concluding the proof in the case
a=b=d
.

For the general case, from (
99
) assuming
b>a
without loss of generality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{S_{k}\geq 0\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,b)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq\sigma}\{B_{s}\geq 0\}\right)\cdot\frac{\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,a)}\left(\cap_{0\leq k\leq t}\{S_{k}\geq 0\}\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{(0,a)}^{(t,a)}\left(\cap_{0\leq s\leq\sigma}\{B_{s}\geq 0\}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2ab}{\sigma}(1+{\rm O}(d^{-c}))

from the previous discussion, concluding the proof.
∎

Yan Ge, Yuan Xu

Abstract
. We prove several new families of Bernstein inequalities of two types on the simplex. The first type consists of inequalities in L 2 L^{2} norm for the Jacobi weight, some of which are sharp, and they are established via the spectral operator that has orthogonal polynomials as eigenfunctions. The second type consists of inequalities in L p L^{p} norm for doubling weight on the simplex. The first type is not necessarily a special case of the second type when d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 .

1.
Introduction

We revisit Bernstein inequalities on the simplex in order to understand a new family of Bernstein
inequalities on the triangle that appeared recently in
[
14
]
. Let
\triangle^{d}
be the simplex defined by

\triangle^{d}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,x_{d}\geq 0,\,|x|\leq 1\right\},\quad|x|:=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}.

For
{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=({\kappa}_{1},\ldots,{\kappa}_{d+1})
, let
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
be the Jacobi weight function on the simplex,

W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x):=x_{1}^{{\kappa}_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{{\kappa}_{d}}(1-|x|)^{{\kappa}_{d+1}},\qquad{\kappa}_{i}>-1,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,d+1.

Denote by
\partial_{j}
the
j
-th partial derivative. If
f
is a polynomials of degree at most
n
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
then we have the Bernstein inequality
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
: for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
1\leq j\leq d
,

(1.1)

\|\psi_{j}\partial_{j}f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},p}\leq c\,n\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},p}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\psi_{j}(x)=\sqrt{x_{j}(1-|x|)},

where
\|\cdot\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},p}
denotes the
L^{p}(\triangle^{d},W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
norm for
1\leq p<\infty
and uniform norm
if
p=\infty
, which agrees with the classical Bernstein inequality on
[0,1]
when
d=1
. The factor
\psi_{j}

in front of
\partial_{j}
is a trade mark of Bernstein inequalities; see, for example,
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
14
]
.
Recently, a new set of inequalities on the triangle,
\triangle^{d}
when
d=2
, appeared in

[
14
]
, which shows in particular that, for
i,j=1,2
and
i\neq j
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x_{j}}}\psi_{i}\partial_{i}f\right\|_{p,w}\leq c\,n\|f\|_{p,w},

which is evidently stronger than that of (
1.1
). The inequality (
1.2
) came out as
a byproduct in
[
14
]
, derived from the Bernstein inequalities on the conic domains. It raises several
questions.

One obvious question is a possible extension of (
1.2
) to the simplex
\triangle^{d}
. The answer
is positive but it turns out, somewhat surprisingly, that there are several families of extensions. Another question
is a possible geometric interpretation of the new factor in front of the derivative. In a recent paper, Kroó

[
11
]
establishes the Bernstein inequality on polyhedra, including the simplex as a special case, with
\psi_{j}

bing the Euclidean distance from
x
to the boundary of the domain. As we shall show later, however, our
\psi_{j}

has a geometric interpretation but it is not via Euclidean distance. One more question arises from the link
between the Bernstein inequality in
L^{2}
norm and the spectral operator, see
[
10
,
14
]
, where the latter
is a second-order linear differential operator on the domain that has orthogonal polynomials as eigenfunctions.
For the simplex, the spectral operator is given by
[
7
, section 5.3]

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}(1-x_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}x_{i}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}x_{j}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big({\kappa}_{i}+1-(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d+1)x_{i}\big)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

and the Bernstein inequality (
1.1
) in the
L^{2}
norm can be derived from the self-adjoint integration
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
, in which
\phi_{j}\partial_{j}
appears (see (
2.1
) below). One immediate question is if
and how the new inequality (
1.2
) is related to the spectral operator.

The answer to the last question is somewhat surprising: it turns out that
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
can be written in several other
symmetric forms that lead to self-adjoint
integrations of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
. Each of the new symmetric forms contains fractions such as the one in
front of
\partial_{j}
in (
1.2
) and each yields a family of new Bernstein inequalities
in
L^{2}
norm on the simplex, of which some are sharp in the sense that equality holds for some
polynomials. For the first two questions, we are able to establish new Bernstein inequalities for all
derivatives in
L^{p}
norm that extend (
1.2
) to
\triangle^{d}
. They agree with those
in
L^{2}
norm derived from the spectral operator when
d=2
but, surprisingly, not for
d>2
.
Moreover, these
L^{p}
inequalities are established for all doubling weight on the simplex, not just
for
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
. The proof follows the approach in
[
14
]
that relies on a framework based on highly
localized kernels developed in
[
13
]
for localizable space of homogeneous type. As a byproduct,
our proof shows that
\triangle^{d}
with
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
and its intrinsic distance is such a space, which is
to be expected but not verified before.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we derive Bernstein inequalities in
L^{2}
norm
from the spectral operator, which requires only a basic definition of orthogonality, and discuss their
properties. The Bernstein inequalities in
L^{p}
norm will be stated and discussed in the first
subsection of Section 3 and proved in later subsections.

Throughout this paper, we adopt the convention that the letter
c
denotes a constant that depends
on fixed parameters, such as
d
,
p
and
{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
but independent of the degree
n
of polynomials.

2.
Spectral operator and
L^{2}
Bernstein inequality

In this section, we prove the Bernstein inequality in the
L^{2}
norm via the Jacobi weight function
on the simplex. The triangle
\triangle^{d}
is symmetric under permutations of
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},x_{d+1})
.
We often write
x_{d+1}=1-|x|
, so that the Jacobi weight function can be written as

W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{{\kappa}_{i}}(1-|x|)^{{\kappa}_{d+1}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}x_{i}^{{\kappa}_{i}}.

The classical orthogonal polynomials on the simplex are orthogonal with respect to the inner product

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}=b_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{\triangle^{d}}f(x)g(x)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathrm{d}x,\qquad b_{\kappa}=\frac{\Gamma(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d+1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}\Gamma({\kappa}_{i}+1)},

where
b_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is the normalized constant of
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
, so that
{\langle}1,1{\rangle}_{\kappa}=1
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})
be
the space of orthogonal polynomials of degree
n
with respect to
W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
. It is well-known that

\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})=\binom{n+d-1}{n}.

An orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
can be given in terms of the standard Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}

[
7
, Section 5.3]
. Let
\operatorname{proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f:L^{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
be the orthogonal projection operator.
The Fourier orthogonal series of
f\in L^{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
and its Parseval identity are given by

f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\|\operatorname{proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2},\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}),

where
\|\cdot\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}=\sqrt{{\langle}f,f{\rangle}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
denotes the norm for
L^{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
.

2.1.
Spectral operator and Bernstein inequality

Orthogonal polynomials in the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
are eigenfunctions of the spectral operator

{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
define in (
1.3
) with an eigenvalue depending on
n
; more precisely

[
7
, Section 5.3]
,

{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}u=-n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)u,\qquad u\in{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}).

For convenience, we denote by
\partial_{j}
the
j
-th partial derivative and we further define

\partial_{i,j}:=\partial_{i}-\partial_{j},\qquad 1\leq i\neq j\leq d.

The operator
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
can be written more symmetrically as

(2.1)

{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=\frac{1}{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\big(x_{i}(1-|x|)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\partial_{i}\big)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\partial_{i,j}\big(x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\partial_{i,j}\big)\right],

where
\partial_{i,j}=\partial_{i}-\partial_{j}
. The weight function
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)
is invariant under the simultaneous
permutation of
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},1-|x|)
and
({\kappa}_{1},{\kappa}_{2},\ldots,{\kappa}_{d+1})
. This symmetry is preserved in the
decomposition of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
since the right-hand side of (
2.1
) is invariant under the simultaneous
permutation, as can be easily verified.

Using integration by parts, the decomposition in (
2.1
) leads immediately to

(2.2)

\displaystyle-\int_{\triangle^{d}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f(x)g(x)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\triangle^{d}}x_{i}(1-|x|)\partial_{i}f(x)\partial_{i}g(x)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\int_{\triangle^{d}}x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{i,j}f(x)\partial_{i,j}g(x)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathrm{d}x,

which implies, in particular, that
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is self-adjoint. As an application of the identity (
2.2
), we
can obtain a sharp Bernstein inequality in the
L^{2}
norm. Throughout this paper, we call an inequality sharp if
equality holds for some polynomials.

Let
\Pi_{n}^{d}
be the space of polynomials of degree at most
n
in
d
variables.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
n=0,1,2,\ldots
and
f\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
. Then

(2.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\sqrt{x_{i}(1-|x|)}\partial_{i}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left\|\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}\partial_{i,j}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}\leq n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}

and the equality holds if and only if
f\in{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
.
1
1

1

We thank Andras Kroó for pointing out this
characterization for equality.

Furthermore, the following two inequalities are also sharp,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\sqrt{x_{i}(1-|x|)}\partial_{i}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}\leq n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2},

and, for
1\leq\ell\leq d
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{x_{\ell}(1-|x|)}\partial_{\ell}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{d}\left\|\sqrt{x_{\ell}x_{j}}\partial_{\ell,j}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}\leq n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
{\lambda}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)
. Since
f
is a polynomial of degree
n
, we have

f=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\operatorname{proj}_{j}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{\lambda}_{j}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\operatorname{proj}_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}f.

In particular, by the orthogonality and the Parseval identity, we obtain

\left\|{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f(x)\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}({\lambda}_{j}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})^{2}\left\|\operatorname{proj}_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}\leq({\lambda}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left\|\operatorname{proj}_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}=({\lambda}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})^{2}\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}.

Consequently, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we deduce

(2.6)

\left|c_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{\triangle^{d}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f(x)\cdot g(x)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\left\|{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f(x)\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}\cdot\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}\leq{\lambda}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}.

Setting
g=f
in (
2.2
) and applying the above inequality, we have proved (
2.3
), whereas
(
2.4
) and (
2.5
) are immediate consequences of (
2.3
). To see that (
2.3
)
is sharp, we notice that it becomes equality for any orthogonal polynomial in
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
by the spectral
property
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f={\lambda}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f
and, moreover, the above proof also shows that the reversed inequality (
2.3
)
holds if
f\in{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
. Furthermore, we consider the Jacobi polynomial

(2.7)

P_{e_{1}}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)=P_{n}^{(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-{\kappa}_{1}+d-1,{\kappa}_{1})}(2x_{1}-1),

which is the orthogonal polynomial of degree
n
in
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})
by setting
{\alpha}_{1}=n
and
{\alpha}_{2}\ldots={\alpha}_{d}=0

for the orthogonal polynomial
P_{{\alpha}}
given in
[
7
, Prop. 5.3.1]
. Choosing
f=g=P_{e_{1}}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)
in (
2.2
)
shows that (
2.5
) is sharp for
\ell=1
. The case of
\ell\neq 1
follows from the simultaneous permutation
of variables and parameters. Furthermore, making a change of variables
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\mapsto(1-|x|,x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-1})
and simultaneously for
{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
, we see that the polynomial

(2.8)

R_{e_{1}}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)=P_{e_{1}}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(1-|x|,x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})=P_{n}^{(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-{\kappa}_{d+1}+d-1,{\kappa}_{d+1})}(1-2|x|)

is also an element in
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
. Setting
f=g=R_{n}
in (
2.2
) shows that equality holds in (
2.5
).
Moreover, we evidently have
\partial_{i,j}R_{n}(x)=0
. Hence, the identity for (
2.4
) holds as well for
R_{e_{1}}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of the theorem, we obtain the Bernstein inequalities

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{x_{i}(1-|x|)}\partial_{i}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)}\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}\partial_{i,j}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)}\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq d,

for any polynomial
f
of degree at most
n
. These inequalities are classical and, apart from the explicit constant
on the right-hand side, hold for the weighted
L^{p}
norm (
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
).

2.2.
New decomposition of spectral operator and Bernstein inequality

In this subsection, we present another type of decomposition of the spectral operator
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
, which is characteristically
different from (
2.1
) and is somewhat surprising. It leads to a number of new Bernstein inequalities, including
those on the triangle mentioned in the introduction.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
d\geq 2
, the spectral operator
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
on
\triangle^{d}
satisfies

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=\frac{1}{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\left(|x|^{d-1}(1-|x|)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\left.+\frac{1}{|x|}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\partial_{i,j}\left(x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\partial_{i,j}\right)\right].

Proof.

Let
e=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
. We start with the identity

\displaystyle L:=

\displaystyle{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\partial_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)x_{j}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle|x|\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)x_{j})\partial_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left[\partial_{j}\big(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)x_{j}\partial_{j}\big)-\partial_{i}\big(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)x_{j}\partial_{j}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle|x|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\big(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)x_{i}\partial_{i}\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\partial_{i}-\partial_{j})\big(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)x_{j}\partial_{j}\big):=L_{1}+L_{2}.

Using
x_{k}\partial_{k}f(x)=\partial_{k}x_{k}f(x)-f(x)
, the second term
L_{2}
on the right-hand side can be written as

\displaystyle L_{2}\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{d}(\partial_{i}-\partial_{j})\big(x_{i}x_{j}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)\partial_{j}\big)-(d-1)W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)x_{j}\partial_{j}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\partial_{i,j}\big(x_{i}x_{j}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)\big)\partial_{i,j}-(d-1)|x|W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle},

where the second step, using
\partial_{i,j}=\partial_{i}-\partial_{j}
, follows from the identity

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{d}A_{i,j}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}(A_{i,j}+A_{j,i}).

Hence, using
W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)=(1-|x|)W_{\kappa}(x)
and
\partial_{i,j}\phi(|x|)=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle L={\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\,=

\displaystyle|x|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\big(x_{i}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)\big)\partial_{i}-(d-1)|x|W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}

\displaystyle-(1-|x|)\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\partial_{i,j}\big(x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\big)\partial_{i,j}.

Rearranging the terms, the above identity yields

\displaystyle|x|{\mathcal{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\,

\displaystyle=|x|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\big(x_{i}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x)\big)\partial_{i}+|x|\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\partial_{i,j}\big(x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\big)\partial_{i,j}

\displaystyle={\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\big(W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\big)+(d-1)W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\partial_{i,j}\big(x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\big)\partial_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{d-1}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\big(|x|^{d-1}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\big)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\partial_{i,j}\big(x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\big)\partial_{i,j},

where the second equality follows from applying the identity

{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\big(|x|^{d-1}G(x)\big)=|x|^{d-1}\big[{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}G(x)+(d-1)G(x)\big]

with
G(x)=W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}
. This completes the proof.
∎

It is worth mentioning that while the right-hand side of (
2.1
) is invariant under the simultaneous permutation of

{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
and
(x,1-|x|)
, the right-hand side of (
2.11
) is not. Indeed, we have the following:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\ell
be an integer
1\leq\ell\leq d
. Then the spectral operator
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)}\left[\frac{1}{(1-x_{\ell})^{d}}({\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}-\partial_{\ell})\left(x_{\ell}(1-x_{\ell})^{d-1}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)({\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}-\partial_{\ell})\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{1-x_{\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
i\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\partial_{i}\big(x_{i}(1-|x|)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\partial_{i}\big)+\frac{1}{1-x_{\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq d\\
i\neq\ell,j\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\partial_{i,j}\left(x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\partial_{i,j}\right)\bigg].

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case when
\ell=1
. We make a simultaneous change of variables

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=(y_{2},y_{3},\ldots,y_{d},1-|y|)
and
{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\mapsto({\kappa}_{2},\ldots,{\kappa}_{d+1},{\kappa}_{1})
. Then

W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)
is unchanged and
\partial_{x_{j}}=\partial_{y_{j+1}}-\partial_{y_{1}}
for
1\leq j\leq d-1
and

\partial_{x_{d}}=-\partial_{y_{1}}
. Hence, it follows readily that
{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}={\langle}y,\nabla_{y}{\rangle}-\partial_{y_{1}}
;
moreover,
\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x_{j}}=\partial_{y_{i+1}}-\partial_{y_{j+1}}
for
1\leq i<j\leq d-1
and

\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x_{d}}=\partial_{y_{i+1}}
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
. It follows that the right-hand side
of (
2.11
) becomes the stated new decomposition in
y
variables for
\ell=1
.
∎

As an application of the new decomposition of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}

).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f
and
g
be functions in

. Then

\displaystyle-\int_{\triangle^{d}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f(x)\cdot g(x)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\,=

\displaystyle\int_{\triangle^{d}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}f(x)\cdot{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}g(x)(1-|x|)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\int_{\triangle^{d}}\partial_{i,j}f(x)\partial_{i,j}g(x)x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|}.

Moreover, for
1\leq\ell\leq d
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle-\int_{\triangle^{d}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f(x)

\displaystyle\cdot g(x)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{d}\int_{\triangle^{d}}\partial_{i}f(x)\cdot\partial_{i}g(x)x_{i}(1-|x|)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{1-x_{\ell}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq d\\
i\neq\ell,\,j\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\int_{\triangle^{d}}\partial_{i,j}f(x)\partial_{i,j}g(x)x_{i}x_{j}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{1-x_{\ell}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\triangle^{d}}({\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}-\partial_{\ell})f(x)\cdot({\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}g(x)-\partial_{\ell})g(x)x_{\ell}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{1-x_{\ell}}.

Proof.

We prove (
2.12
) via integration by parts of the expression of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
in (
2.11
).
For the first term in the right-hand side of (
2.11
), we use the following identity

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\left[\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{d}}g(x)\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}-\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{d+1}}\right)g(x)+\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}g(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}g(x),

which implies immediately that the integral by parts gives

\displaystyle-\int_{\triangle^{d}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}\big(|x|^{d-1}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}f(x)\big)\cdot g(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\triangle^{d}}|x|^{d-1}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}f(x)\partial_{i}\left[\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{d}}g(x)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\triangle^{d}}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e}(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}f(x){\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}g(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|}.

For the second term in the right-hand side of (
2.11
), the factor
\frac{1}{|x|}
can be put inside the derivatives,
using
\partial_{i,j}\phi(|x|)=0
, so that the integration by parts can be carried out straightforwardly.
The proof of (
2.12
) follows likewise via integration by parts of the expression of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
in the
Corollary
2.3
.
∎

.

Let

,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sqrt{1-|x|}}{\sqrt{|x|}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left\|\frac{\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}}{\sqrt{|x|}}\partial_{i,j}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}\leq n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sqrt{1-|x|}}{\sqrt{|x|}}{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}\leq\sqrt{n(n+|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d)}\|f\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}.

Proof.

These inequalities follow from the Parseval identity and (

in (

The above corollary is derived using (

Corollary 2.6

Let

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sqrt{x_{\ell}}}{\sqrt{1-x_{\ell}}}({\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}-\partial_{\ell})f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\

3.
L^{p}
Bernstein inequalities for doubling weight

We prove Bernstein inequalities in the
L^{p}
norm with respect to a doubling weight. The main result will
be stated and discussed in the first subsection. The proof follows the approach in
[
14
]
, which allows us
to handle the doubling weight. In the second subsection, we verify that
(\triangle^{d},W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}},{\mathsf{d}})
is
indeed a space of localized homogenous type. In the third subsection, we estimate the derivatives of the
localized kernel. The proof of Bernstein’s inequality is given in the fouth subsection.

3.1.
Bernstein inequalities

We first recall the definition of a doubling weight, which we restrict on the simplex. The distance function
{\mathsf{d}}
on

\triangle^{d}
is defined by

{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)=\arccos\left(\sqrt{x_{1}}\sqrt{y_{1}}+\cdots\sqrt{x_{d}}\sqrt{y_{d}}+\sqrt{1-|x|}\sqrt{1-|y|}\right).

For
x\in\triangle^{d}
and
r>0
, we denote a ball centered at
x
with radius
r
in
\triangle^{d}
by
{\mathsf{B}}(x,r)
and
denote its measure with respect to a weight function
{\mathsf{w}}
by
{\mathsf{w}}(B(x,r))
; then

{\mathsf{B}}(x,r):=\left\{y\in\triangle^{d}:{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)\leq r\right\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathsf{w}}({\mathsf{B}}(x,r)):=b_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathsf{B}}(x,r)}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

A weight function
{\mathsf{w}}
is called a doubling weight on
\triangle^{d}
if its satisfies the doubling condition:
there exists a constant
c_{\mathsf{w}}>0
such that

{\mathsf{w}}({\mathsf{B}}(x,2r))\leq c_{\mathsf{w}}\,{\mathsf{w}}({\mathsf{B}}(x,r)),\qquad\forall x\in\triangle^{d},\,\,0<r\leq r_{0},

where
r_{0}
is the largest positive number such that
{\mathsf{B}}(x,r)\subset\triangle^{d}
. For a doubling weight
{\mathsf{w}}
, we
denote the smallest
c_{\mathsf{w}}
by
c({\mathsf{w}})
and call it the
doubling constant
. We also call the positive number

{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})
that satisfies

\sup_{{\mathsf{B}}(x,r)\subset\triangle^{d}}\frac{{\mathsf{w}}({\mathsf{B}}(x,2^{m}r))}{{\mathsf{w}}({\mathsf{B}}(x,r))}\leq c_{w}2^{{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})}m,\quad m=0,1,2,\ldots,

the doubling index. Iteration shows that
{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})\leq\log_{2}c({\mathsf{w}})
.

To state our Bernstein inequalities on the simplex, we introduce the following functions: for

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\triangle^{d}
and
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

\phi_{i,j}(x):=\frac{\sqrt{x_{i}}\sqrt{x_{j}}}{\sqrt{x_{i}+x_{j}}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\phi_{i}(x):=\frac{\sqrt{x_{i}}\sqrt{1-|x|}}{\sqrt{x_{i}+1-|x|}}.

Moreover, for a doubling weight
{\mathsf{w}}
, we let
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}
denote the norm of
L^{p}(\triangle^{d},{\mathsf{w}})
for

1\leq p\leq\infty
, where we adopt the convention that the norm becomes
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
when
p=\infty

and the space becomes
C(\triangle^{d})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight on
\triangle^{d}
. For
n,r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

(3.1)

\left\|\phi_{i}^{r}\partial_{i}^{r}f\right\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}\leq c_{p}n^{r}\|f\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,

and

(3.2)

\left\|\phi_{i,j}^{r}\partial_{i,j}^{r}f\right\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}\leq c_{p}n^{r}\|f\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p},\quad 1\leq i<j\leq d,

where
c_{p}
is a constant depending only
{\mathsf{w}}
,
d
and
p
.

These inequalities are evidently stronger than the
L^{p}
version of (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) in
the literature
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
. For a geometric perspective, let us consider the distance function
{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(x,y)

on
[0,1]
, defined by

{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(x,y)=\arccos\left(\sqrt{x}\sqrt{y}+\sqrt{1-x}\sqrt{1-y}\right),\qquad x,y\in[0,1].

For
x\in[0,1]
, it is well-known that the distance from
x
to the boundary of
[0,1]
is proportional to
\sqrt{x(1-x)}
.
Indeed, using
1-\cos{\theta}=2\sin^{2}\frac{{\theta}}{2}\sim{\theta}^{2}
, we have
{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(x,0)\sim\sqrt{x}
and

{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(x,1)\sim\sqrt{1-x}
; thus

\sqrt{x(1-x)}\sim\min\{{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(x,0),{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(x,1)\}.

For
x\in\triangle^{d}
, we evidently have
0\leq\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}+x_{j}}\leq 1
, so that

\phi_{i,j}(x)=\min\left\{{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}+x_{j}},0\right),{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}+x_{j}},1\right)\right\}.

Similarly, since
0\leq x_{i}\leq 1-|x|+x_{i}
, we also have

\phi_{i}(x)\sim\min\left\{{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{1-|x|+x_{i}},0\right),{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{1-|x|+x_{i}},1\right)\right\}.

Note that neither
\phi_{i,j}
or
\phi_{i}
is proportional to the Euclidean distance of
x
to the boundary of
\triangle^{d}
.

As another interesting aspect of the new inequalities in Theorem
3.1
, let us compare them when
p=2
and
r=1

with the inequalities
derived from the spectral operator in Subsection
2.2
. It is easy to verify that the inequalities in (
3.1
)
and (
3.2
) agree with those in (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) when
d=2
, but they differ when

d>2
. Moreover, for
d\geq 3
,
1\leq\ell\leq d
and
x\in\triangle^{d}
,

1-x_{\ell}\geq\sum_{j=1,j\neq\ell}^{d}x_{j}\geq x_{i}+x_{j},\qquad i\neq\ell,\,j\neq\ell,

and
1-x_{\ell}\geq x_{i}+1-|x|
for
\ell\neq i
, which shows, for example,

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell\leq d\\
\ell\neq i\end{subarray}}\left\|\frac{\sqrt{x_{i}(1-|x|)}}{\sqrt{1-x_{\ell}}}\partial_{i}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}\leq\left\|\frac{\sqrt{x_{i}(1-|x|)}}{\sqrt{1-|x|+x_{i}}}\partial_{i}f\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},2}.

Consequently, if we disregard the constant
c_{p}
in the right-hand side, then the inequalities (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)
are in fact stronger than those in (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) for
d\geq 3
, as the above inequality shows,
which is somewhat unexpected.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
will be given in the last subsection, after we establish the necessary
estimates for highly localized kernels on the simplex. For the weight function
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
, there could be other
proofs based on changing variables and making use of results in one variable. Our proof has the advantage
that it works for all doubling weight functions and, as a byproduct, it shows that
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
and the simplex

\triangle^{d}
fits into the general scheme developed in
[
13
]
for localizable space of homogeneous type.

3.2.
Highly localized kernels

The projection operator
\operatorname{proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}:L^{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},\triangle^{d})\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
is an integral operator

\operatorname{proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f(x)=b_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{\Delta^{d}}P^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}_{n}(x,y)f(x,y)W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(y)\mathrm{d}y,

where
P_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
, which ensures
\operatorname{proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f=f

for all
f\in{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
. For
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
,
1\leq i\leq d+1
, the reproducing kernel satisfies a closed-form formula

[
7
, Theorem 5.2.4]

(3.1)

P_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)=a_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{-1}^{1}Z_{n}^{(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\left(2\xi(x,y;t)^{2}-1\right)\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

where
Z_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
is a constant multiple of the Jacobi polynomial

Z_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)=\frac{P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(1)P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)}{h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}}

and
\xi(x,y,t)
is a function defined by

\xi(x,y;t)=\sqrt{x_{1}y_{1}}t_{1}+\cdots+\sqrt{x_{d}y_{d}}t_{d}+\sqrt{x_{d+1}y_{d+1}}t_{d+1},

and
a_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is the normalization constant that ensures
P_{0}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}};x,y)=1
, given by

a_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=a_{{\kappa}_{1}}\ldots a_{{\kappa}_{d+1}}\quad\hbox{with}\quad a_{\kappa}=\frac{\Gamma({\kappa}+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}.

The formula (
3.1
) holds if one or more
{\kappa}_{i}
is zero under the limit.

Let
\widehat{a}
be an admissible cut-off function, defined as a
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
that has support

[0,2]
and satisfies
\widehat{a}(t)=1
for
0\leq t\leq 1
. We consider localized kernels defined by

L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\widehat{a}\Big(\frac{j}{n}\Big)P_{j}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y).

Using the closed-form formula of the reproducing kernel, this kernel can be written as

(3.2)

L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)=a_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}L_{n}^{(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\left(2\xi(x,y;t)^{2}-1\right)\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

where
L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
denotes the localized kernel for the Jacobi polynomials

L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\widehat{a}\Big(\frac{j}{n}\Big)Z_{j}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t),\qquad t\in[-1,1],

which is highly localized and satisfies the lemma
[
4
, Theorem 2.6.7]
below.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\ell
be a positive integer and let
\eta
be a function that satisfy,
\eta\in C^{3\ell-1}({\mathbb{R}})
,

\mathrm{supp}\,\eta\subset[0,2]
and
\eta^{(j)}(0)=0
for
j=1,2,\ldots,3\ell-2
. Then,
for
{\alpha}\geq{\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
t\in[-1,1]
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.3)

\left|\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)\right|\leq c_{\ell,m,{\alpha}}\left\|\eta^{(3\ell-1)}\right\|_{\infty}\frac{n^{2{\alpha}+2m+2}}{(1+n\sqrt{1-t})^{\ell}},\quad m=0,1,2,\ldots.

Let
B(x,r)
be ball centered at
x
and radius
r
in
\triangle^{d}
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it is not difficult to see
that, for
n=1,2,3,\ldots
and
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
,
1\leq i\leq d+1
,

W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(B(x,r))=b_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{B(x,r)}W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(y)\mathrm{d}y\sim r^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(\sqrt{x_{i}}+r)^{2{\kappa}_{i}+1},\qquad x\in\triangle^{d};

which follows, for example, from
[
4
, Lemma 11.3.6]
by making a change of variables
x_{j}\mapsto\sqrt{x_{j}}
.
For convenience, we introduce

W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;x):=\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(x_{i}+n^{-2})^{{\kappa}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad x\in\triangle^{d},

so that
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(B(x,n^{-1}))\sim n^{-d}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;x)
.

In
[
13
]
, highly localized kernels are considered on a space of homogeneous type
(\Omega,{\mathsf{w}},{\mathsf{d}})
,
where
\Omega
is a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
{\mathsf{w}}
is a doubling weight defined on
\Omega
in terms of the distance
function
{\mathsf{d}}
on
\Omega
. They are defined as kernels that satisfy three assertions, the first two describe
localization properties of the kernel and the third one is about the weight function. For the space

(\triangle^{d},W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle})
, the third property states

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\triangle^{d}}\frac{n^{d}W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(y)dy}{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}}}\leq c_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},

which will be a consequence of Lemma
3.8
below. The first localization property is the
following proposition in
[
8
, Theorem 7.1]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\widehat{a}
be an admissible cutoff function. Then for any
{\gamma}>0
there exists a constant

c_{\gamma}
depending on
{\gamma}
,
{\mathbf{k}}
, and
d
such that

(3.5)

|L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)|\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d}}{\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;y)}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}(x,y)\big)^{{\gamma}}},\quad x,y\in\triangle^{d}.

The proof of this proposition relies on the estimate of (
3.3
) with
m=0
and two lower bounds of

1-\xi(x,y;t)
(cf.
[
8
, p. 384]
),

1-\xi(x,y;t)\geq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)^{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad 1-\xi(x,y;t)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\sqrt{x_{i}y_{i}}(1-t_{i}).

The key step in the proof of (
3.5
) is the following lemma, which we shall need later, and its proof is the
central piece of the proof of Theorem 7.2 in
[
8
]
; see the estimate of
J
in the proof there.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
and
{\gamma}\geq 2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|
,

\displaystyle\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)}]^{\gamma}}\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-d-1}}{\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|}}.

The second localization property of the kernel on the simplex is the inequality below.

Proposition 3.5
.

For
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
with some
\delta_{0}<1
and
z\in B(x,\frac{\delta}{n})
,

(3.6)

\left|L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)-L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(z,y)\right|\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d+1}{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)}{\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;y)}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)\big)^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-2}}.

Proof.

Denote the left-hand side of (
3.6
) by
K
. Let
\partial L(u)=L^{\prime}(u)
and let
I_{t}
denote the interval
that has the endpoints
\xi(x,y;t)
and
\xi(z,y;t)
. Then, by (
3.2
),

(3.7)

\displaystyle K\leq 2a_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}L_{n}((x,y),(z,y);t)\left|\xi(x,y;t)^{2}-\xi(z,y;t)^{2}\right|\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

where

\displaystyle L_{n}((x,y),(z,y);t):=\big\|\partial L_{n}^{(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\big(2(\cdot)^{2}-1\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{t})}.

Since
\max_{r\in I_{v}}|1+n\sqrt{1-r}|^{-{\gamma}}
is attained at one of the endpoints of the interval, it follows from
(
3.3
) with
m=1
that

\displaystyle L_{n}((x,y),(z,y);t)\leq c\left[\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)^{2}}\big)^{{\gamma}}}+\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(z,y;t)^{2}}\big)^{{\gamma}}}\right].

Using
|\xi(x,y;t)|\leq 1
, we obtain
|\xi(x,y;t)^{2}-\xi(z,y;t)^{2}|\leq 2|\xi(x,y;t)-\xi(z,y;t)|
. Since
\xi(x,y;1)=\cos{\mathsf{d}}(x,y)
, we
can write

\displaystyle\xi(x,y;t)-\xi(z,y;t)\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\sqrt{x_{i}y_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\sqrt{z_{i}y_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\sqrt{x_{i}y_{i}}(1-t_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\sqrt{z_{i}y_{i}}(1-t_{i})

\displaystyle=\cos{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)-\cos{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(z,y)+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\left(\sqrt{x_{i}}-\sqrt{z_{i}}\right)\sqrt{y_{i}}(1-t_{i}).

From the elementary identity

\displaystyle\cos{\theta}-\cos\phi=2\sin\frac{{\theta}-\phi}{2}\sin\frac{{\theta}+\phi}{2}=2\sin\frac{{\theta}-\phi}{2}\left(2\sin\frac{{\theta}}{2}+\sin\frac{|{\theta}-\phi|}{2}\right),

it follows readily that
|\cos{\theta}-\cos\phi|\leq|{\theta}-\phi|\left(|{\theta}|+\tfrac{1}{2}|{\theta}-\phi|\right)
. Hence, using the triangle
inequality for the distance
{\mathsf{d}}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain

|\cos{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)-\cos{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(z,y)|\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)\left({\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)+{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(v,y)\right)\quad\hbox{with $v=x$ or $v=z$}.

For
1\leq i\leq d+1
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that (
[
8
, (7.5)]
)

\left|\sqrt{x_{j}}-\sqrt{y_{j}}\right|\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y),\quad x,y\in\triangle^{d}.

Consequently, we conclude that

|\xi(x,y;t)-\xi(z,y;t)|\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)\left[{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)+{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(v,y)+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\sqrt{y_{i}}(1-t_{i})\right].

Using these estimates on the right-hand side of (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle K\leq c\,{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)^{2}}\big)^{{\gamma}}}+\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(z,y;t)^{2}}\big)^{{\gamma}}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left({\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(v,y)+{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)+\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\sqrt{y_{j}}(1-t_{j})\right)\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t.

We write the righthand side as a sum according to the last bracket, so that

K\leq c\,{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)\left(K_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}K_{j}\right).

For
z\in B(x,\frac{\delta}{n})
, we have
{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,z)+{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(v,y)\leq cn^{-1}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(v,y))
. Hence, applying
Lemma
3.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{0}\,

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\left[\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+2}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)^{2}}\big)^{{\gamma}}}\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left.\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+2}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(z,y))}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(z,y;t)^{2}}\big)^{{\gamma}}}\right]\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d+1}}{\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-1}}.

The estimate of
K_{j}
also follows from Lemma
3.4
with
(1-t_{j}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t_{j}
replaced by

(1-t_{j}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t_{j}
; hence, let
e_{j}
denotes the
j
-th coordinator vector of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{j}\,

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d}\sqrt{y_{j}}}{\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e_{j}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e_{j}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-2}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d+1}}{\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-2}},

where we have used
n^{-1}\sqrt{y_{j}}\leq(\sqrt{x_{j}}+n^{-1})(\sqrt{y_{j}}+n^{-1})
in the second step. Putting these estimates
together proves (
3.6
).
∎

With these localization properties established, we have verified that the kernels
L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(\cdot,\cdot)
are highly
localized on the simplex. Thus, the space
(\triangle^{d},W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle})
is a localizable space of
homogeneous type as defined in
[
13
]
. From the general framework for such spaces, developed in
[
13
]
,
a number of results on the simplex follow readily, some of which will be used in the proof of Bernstein
inequalities in the last subsection.

3.3.
Derivative of highly localized kernels

Our proof of the Bernstein inequality requires the estimate of the derivatives of the highly localized kernel.
Recall that
\partial_{i,j}:=\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x_{j}}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
,
1\leq i\leq d+1
and
{\gamma}\geq 2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2
. For
1\leq i<j\leq d+1
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\partial_{i,j}L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)|\leq c_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d+1}(\sqrt{x_{i}+x_{j}}+n^{-1})}{\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-2}},

where
\partial_{i,j}=\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x_{j}}
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
and
\partial_{i,d+1}=\partial_{x_{i}}
,
and
x_{d+1}=1-|x|
.

Proof.

Taking derivative in the
x
-variable of the identity for
L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(\cdot,\cdot)
in (
3.2
) gives

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{i,j}L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)=a_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\partial L_{n}^{(|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+d-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\left(2\xi(x,y,t)^{2}-1\right)

\displaystyle\times 4\xi(x,y,t)\cdot\partial_{i,j}\xi(x,y,t)\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

where
\partial f(z)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}f(z)
. Let
{\gamma}
be a positive integer. Applying (
3.3
) with
m=1
gives

\displaystyle\left|\partial L_{n}^{(|{\kappa}|+d-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}(2\xi(x,y,t)^{2}-1)\right|\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)}\big)^{{\gamma}}}.

Moreover, by the definition of
\xi(x,y;t)
, taking derivative and writing
t_{i}=1-(1-t_{i})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{i,j}\xi(x,y,t)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{y_{i}}{x_{i}}}t_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}}t_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{y_{i}x_{j}}-\sqrt{x_{i}y_{j}}}{\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{y_{i}}{x_{i}}}(1-t_{i})-\sqrt{\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}}(1-t_{j})\Big).

Together these estimates give an upper bound for the right-hand side of (
3.9
),

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{i,j}L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)\right|\leq c\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)}\big)^{{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\left[\left|\frac{\sqrt{y_{i}x_{j}}-\sqrt{x_{i}y_{j}}}{\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}}\right|+\Big(\sqrt{\frac{y_{i}}{x_{i}}}(1-t_{i})-\sqrt{\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}}(1-t_{j})\Big)\right]\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

which we rearrange as

\displaystyle\left|2\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}\partial_{i,j}L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)\right|\leq c\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)}\big)^{{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\big[\left|\sqrt{y_{i}}\sqrt{x_{j}}-\sqrt{x_{i}}\sqrt{y_{j}}\right|+\sqrt{{y_{i}}{x_{j}}}(1-t_{i})+\sqrt{{y_{j}}{x_{i}}}(1-t_{j})\big]\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad=:c\,(J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}),

where the sum is split according to the sum of three terms in the square bracket of the integrant. Thus, we need to prove that
each
J_{i}
is bounded by the right-hand side of (
3.8
) multiplied by
\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}
. For

J_{1}=\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)}\big)^{{\gamma}}}\left|\sqrt{y_{i}}\sqrt{x_{j}}-\sqrt{x_{i}}\sqrt{y_{j}}\right|\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

we further write

\displaystyle 2(\sqrt{y_{i}}\sqrt{x_{j}}-\sqrt{x_{i}}\sqrt{y_{j}})=(\sqrt{y_{i}}-\sqrt{x_{i}})(\sqrt{y_{j}}+\sqrt{x_{j}})-(\sqrt{y_{j}}-\sqrt{x_{j}})(\sqrt{y_{i}}+\sqrt{x_{i}})

and apply the inequality
[
8
, (7.5)]

\left|\sqrt{y_{i}}-\sqrt{x_{i}}\right|\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y),\quad x,y\in\triangle^{d},\quad 1\leq i\leq d+1,

as well as

|\sqrt{y_{i}}+\sqrt{x_{i}}|=|\sqrt{y_{i}}-\sqrt{x_{i}}+2\sqrt{x_{i}}|\leq|\sqrt{y_{i}}-\sqrt{x_{i}}|+2\sqrt{x_{i}}\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)+2\sqrt{x_{i}}

to obtain

\displaystyle 2|(\sqrt{y_{i}}\sqrt{x_{j}}-\sqrt{x_{i}}\sqrt{y_{j}})|

\displaystyle\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)({\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)+2\sqrt{x_{j}})+{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)({\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)+2\sqrt{x_{i}})

\displaystyle=2{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)({\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)+\sqrt{x_{i}}+\sqrt{x_{j}})

and use this inequality to derive an upper bound

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)({\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)+\sqrt{x_{i}}+\sqrt{x_{j}})}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)}\big)^{{\gamma}}}\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-t_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d+1}({\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)+\sqrt{x_{i}}+\sqrt{x_{j}})}{\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-1}},

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
3.4
. Applying the inequality

{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)+A\leq n^{-1}[1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)]+A\leq(n^{-1}+A)[1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)]

with
A=\sqrt{x_{i}}+\sqrt{x_{j}}
and reducing the power of
1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)
in the denominator by
1
, we
see that
J_{1}
has the desired upper bound.

For the estimates of
J_{2}
and
J_{3}
, we use Lemma
3.4
with
(1-t_{j}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t_{j}
replaced by

(1-t_{j}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t_{j}
. Let
e_{i}
denote the
i
-th coordinator vector of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
. Then

\displaystyle J_{2}\,

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\frac{n^{2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|+2d+3}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\xi(x,y;t)}\big)^{{\gamma}}}\sqrt{{y_{i}}{x_{j}}}(1-t_{i})\prod_{k=l}^{d+1}(1-t_{l}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{l}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d}\sqrt{y_{i}}\sqrt{x_{j}}}{\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e_{i}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+e_{i}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-2}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\frac{n^{d+1}\sqrt{x_{j}+x_{i}}}{\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-2}},

where we have used
n^{-1}\sqrt{y_{i}}\leq(\sqrt{x_{i}}+n^{-1})(\sqrt{y_{i}}+n^{-1})
in the second step.
The proof for
J_{3}
follows similarly, just exchanging the indexes
i
and
j
. Putting these estimates together
proves (
3.8
). ∎

3.4.
Proof of the Bernstein inequality in Theorem
3.1

We will need the maximal function
f^{*}_{{\beta},n}
defined by

\displaystyle f^{*}_{{\beta},n}(x)=\max_{y\in\triangle^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{\beta}}.

The highly localized kernel guarantees, by
[
13
, Corollary 2.11]
, the following property, where we recall
that
c({\mathsf{w}})
and
{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})
are doubling constant and doubling index, respectively.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight on
\triangle^{d}
. If
0<p\leq\infty
,
f\in\Pi(\triangle^{d})
and
{\beta}>\frac{{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})}{p}
,
then

\|f\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}\leq\|f^{*}_{{\beta},n}\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}\leq c\|f\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}

where
c
depends on
c({\mathsf{w}})
and on
{\beta}
when
{\beta}
is either large or close to
{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})/p
.

We will need two more lemmas. The case
p=2
of the first lemma verifies (
3.4
), the third assertion
for highly localized kernels.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
d\geq 2
. For
0<p<\infty
, assume
{\gamma}
is large enough. Then for
x\in\triangle^{d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\triangle^{d}}\frac{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(y)dy}{({W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)})^{\frac{p}{2}}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}p}}\leq c\,n^{-d}(W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;x))^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
13
, Lemma 4.14]
. Let
J_{p}(x)
denote the left-hand side of the first inequality.
Using the doubling property of
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
, it is sufficient to consider the case
p=2
. Thus, it suffices to show,
for
x,y\in\triangle^{d}
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle J_{2}(x):=\int_{\triangle^{d}}\frac{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(y)}{{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{2{\gamma}}}\mathrm{d}y\leq c\,n^{-d}.

From the definition of
W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(y)
and
W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;y)
, it follows readily that

\displaystyle\frac{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(y)}{W_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(n;y)}\leq\frac{(1-|y|)^{{\kappa}_{d+1}}}{(1-|y|+n^{-2})^{{\kappa}_{d+1}+\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{y_{1}\cdots y_{d}}}

Using this inequality in
J_{2}
and changing variables
y_{i}=u_{i}^{2}
for
1\leq i\leq d
in the resulted integral, so that

\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{y_{1}\cdots y_{d}}}=\mathrm{d}u
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}_{+}}\frac{(1-\|u\|^{2})^{{\kappa}_{d+1}}}{{({1-\|u\|^{2}}+n^{-2})^{{\kappa}_{d+1}+\frac{1}{2}}(1+n\mathrm{d}_{\triangle}(x,u^{2}))^{2{\gamma}}}}\mathrm{d}u,

where
{\mathbb{B}}_{+}^{d}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{i}\geq 0,\|x\|\leq 1\}
is the positive quadrant of the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
.
Let
{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathbb{B}}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the distance function on
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
. For
x\in\triangle^{d}
, let

x^{2}=(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{d}^{2})\in{\mathbb{B}}_{+}^{d}
and also let
\sqrt{x}=(\sqrt{x_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt{x_{d}})
.
Then
{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x^{2},y^{2})={\mathsf{d}}_{\mathbb{B}}(x,y)
; see
[
4
, p. 403 and 404]
. Hence, the last
displayed inequality can be written as

J_{2}(x)\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}_{+}}\frac{(1-\|u\|^{2})^{{\kappa}_{d+1}}}{{(\sqrt{1-\|u\|^{2}}+n^{-1})^{2{\kappa}_{d+1}+1}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathbb{B}}(\sqrt{x},u))^{2{\gamma}}}}\mathrm{d}u.

Enlarging the integral domain to the entire ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
shows that
J_{2}(x)
is bounded by
an integral on the unit ball, which has appeared and estimated in
[
12
, Lemma 4.6]
, from which
the inequality (
3.10
) follows readily.
∎

The second lemma relies on the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality, which requires a definition.
Let
{\varepsilon}>0
. A finite subset
\Xi\subset\triangle^{d}
is called
{\varepsilon}
-separated if

{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y)\geq{\varepsilon}
for every pair of distinct points
x,y\in\Xi
, and such a set is called
maximal if, in addition,
\displaystyle\triangle^{d}=\cup_{y\in\Xi}{\mathsf{B}}(y,{\varepsilon})
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight function on
\triangle^{d}
. For
\delta>0
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}},
let

\triangle_{n,\delta}^{d}=\{x\in\triangle^{d}:\tfrac{\delta}{n}<x_{i}\leq 1-\tfrac{\delta}{n},1\leq i\leq d+1\},

where
x_{d+1}=1-|x|
. Then, for
f\in\Pi_{n}(\triangle^{d})
,
1\leq p<\infty
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\triangle^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}{\mathsf{w}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{\delta}\int_{\triangle_{n,\delta}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}{\mathsf{w}}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, let
\chi_{n,d}
be the characteristic function of
\triangle_{n,\delta}^{d}
; then for
p=\infty
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}\leq c\|f\chi_{n,\delta}(x)\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

We recall the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality on the simplex. Let
\Xi
be a maximal

{\varepsilon}
-separated set. For
{\varepsilon}=\frac{\delta}{n}
with
\delta>0
, the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality
states
[
13
, Theorem 2.15]
that, for
1\leq p<\infty
, there is a
\delta>0
such that for
f\in\Pi_{n}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}^{p}=\int_{\triangle^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}{\mathsf{w}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq c\sum_{y\in\Xi}{\mathsf{w}}\left({\mathsf{B}}(y,\tfrac{\delta}{n})\right)\min_{x\in{\mathsf{B}}(y,\frac{\delta}{n})}|f(x)|^{p}.

Hence, for a fixed
\delta>0
, by choosing
n
sufficient large, we see that
f(x)=f(x)\chi_{n,\delta}

for all
x\in{\mathsf{B}}(y,\frac{\delta}{2n})
and
y\in\Xi
. Consequently, for all
x\in{\mathsf{B}}(y,\frac{\delta}{2n})
,

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\mathsf{B}}(y,\frac{\delta}{n})}|f(x)|^{p}\leq\min_{x\in{\mathsf{B}}(y,\frac{\delta}{2n})}|f(x)|^{p}\leq|f(x)|^{p}\chi_{n,\delta}

Using this inequality and the doubling property of
{\mathsf{w}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{y\in\Xi}\int_{{\mathsf{B}}(y,\frac{\delta}{2n})}|f(x)\chi_{n,\delta}(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{\triangle^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}\chi_{n,\delta}(x){\mathsf{w}}(x)\mathrm{d}x

for all
f\in\Pi_{n}
, which is the desired inequality (
3.11
). The inequality (
3.12
)
follows immediately from
[
13
, (2.36)]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.
Let
\partial_{i,d+1}=\partial_{i}
. By the definition of
L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)
, every
f\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
satisfies

\displaystyle f(x)=\int_{\triangle^{d}}f(y)L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)W_{\kappa}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

Taking
\partial_{i,j}
derivative and using the maximal function
f^{*}_{{\beta},n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{i,j}f(x)\right|\leq cf^{*}_{{\beta},n}(x)\int_{\triangle^{d}}\frac{|\partial_{i,j}L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)|}{(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{\beta}}W_{\kappa}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

For
x\in\triangle^{d}_{n,\delta}
, we have
\frac{\delta}{n}\leq\sqrt{x_{i}+x_{j}}
, so that (
3.8
) becomes

\left|\partial_{i,j}L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)\right|\leq c\frac{n^{d+1}\sqrt{x_{i}+x_{j}}}{\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;x)}\sqrt{W_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(n;y)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{{\gamma}-2|{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}|-2}}.

Consequently, choosing
{\gamma}
sufficiently large, it follows from Lemma
3.8
with
p=1
that

\phi_{i,j}(x)\int_{\triangle^{d}}\frac{|\partial_{i,j}L_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x,y)|}{(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{\triangle}(x,y))^{\beta}}W_{\kappa}(y)\mathrm{d}y\leq c\,n^{d+1}n^{-d}=c\,n,

which implies, in particular, that for
x\in\triangle_{n,\delta}
,

\left|\phi_{i,j}\partial_{i,j}f(x)\right|\leq c\,nf^{*}_{{\beta},n}(x).

Since
\phi_{i,j}(x)\leq 1
, it is easy to see that
\phi_{i,j}{\mathsf{w}}
is also a
doubling weight. Hence, recall
\chi_{n,\delta}(x)=\chi_{\triangle_{n,\delta}}(x)
and choose
\delta>0

as in Lemma
3.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{i,j}\partial_{i,j}f\right\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}\leq c_{\delta}\|\phi_{i,j}\partial_{i,j}f\chi_{n,\delta}\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}\leq c\,n\|f^{*}_{{\beta},n}\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}\leq c\,n\|f\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p},

where the last step follows from Proposition
3.7
. This proves Theorem
3.1
for
r=1
.
The proof for
r>1
follows from iteration. Indeed, for fixed
i,j
and
p
, denote by
{\mathsf{w}}^{*}
the
doubling weight
{\mathsf{w}}^{*}(x)=\phi_{i,j}^{(r-1)p}{\mathsf{w}}(x)
. Then

\left\|\phi_{i,j}^{r}\partial_{i,j}^{r}f\right\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p}=\left\|\phi_{i,j}\partial_{i,j}(\partial_{i,j}^{r-1}f)\right\|_{{\mathsf{w}}^{*},p}\leq c\,n\left\|\partial_{i,j}^{r-1}f\right\|_{{\mathsf{w}}^{*},p}=c\,n\left\|\phi_{i,j}^{r-1}\partial_{i,j}^{r-1}f\right\|_{{\mathsf{w}},p},

which can be further iterated to complete the proof.
∎

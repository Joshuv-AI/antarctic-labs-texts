Abstract
. In an earlier paper, we described bordered algebras for knot Floer homology. In this paper, we introduce closely related differential graded algebra, the pong algebra , and compute the A ∞ A_{\infty} structure on its homology.



1.
Introduction

Knot Floer homology
[
13
,
21
]
is a knot invariant
defined using methods from symplectic geometry. This invariant has
many variants, the most general being a module over
\mathbb{Z}[u,v]
. Its

u=v=0
specialization,
\widehat{HFK}
, is a categorification of the
Alexander polynomial.

In
[
14
,
18
]
we developed an algebraic invariant for knots,
inspired by Floer homology. This invariant is the homology of a chain
complex associated to a knot projection. The homology is a module over
the ring
\mathbb{Z}[u,v]/uv=0
. In
[
17
]
, we identified these
constructions with analogues in knot Floer homology with coefficients mod
2
;
specifically, the
u=v=0
specialization computes
\widehat{HFK}
with

{\mathbb{Z}}/{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
coefficients while the
v=0
specialization computes

{HFK}^{-}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}/{2}{\mathbb{Z}}[u]
.

The construction of the bordered knot invariants associates an
algebra to a
generic horizontal slice of the knot diagram and modules
over this algebra to the upper and lower diagrams. Specifically,
versions of the bordered algebras are indexed by pairs of integers
integers
m
and
k
with
k<m
. These
bordered algebras

\mathcal{C}(m,k)
have idempotents
{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}
corresponding to
idempotent states

\mathbf{x}
, which are increasing sequences of integers
1\leq x_{1}<\dots<x_{k}\leq m-1
.
These idempotents generate a subalgebra
{\mathcal{I}}(m,k)\subset\mathcal{C}(m,k)
.
The algebra also contains distinguished
elements
U_{1},\dots,U_{m}
,
L_{2},\dots,L_{m-1}
, and
R_{2},\dots,R_{m-1}

satisfying various relations, including
R_{i}R_{i+1}=0
and
L_{i}L_{i-1}=0
.
(See Section
3
.)

Our goal here is to enhance the
\mathcal{C}(m,k)
with an eye towards
constructing a knot invariant over
\mathbb{Z}[u,v]
. We introduce
here a differential graded algebra, the
pong algebra
. Like the
bordered algebras, the pong algebras are indexed by pairs of integers

m
and
k
; we denote them
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
. The algebra
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
is
an algebra over the polynomial algebra over
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{Z}}/{2}{\mathbb{Z}}

in
m
variables

v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
. The definitions of these algebras is combinatorial,
and closely connected to the strands algebras which have appeared in
bordered Floer homology
[
9
]
. These algebras are defined in
Section
4
. By construction, the pong algebra

{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
is a differential graded algebra, with a preferred

\mathbb{Z}
-grading (which drops by one under the boundary operator), and an
additional
({1\over 2}\mathbb{Z})^{m}
-grading, which is preserved by the differential.

The bordered algebra

\mathcal{C}(m,k)
also has
a
weight grading
with values in
({1\over 2}\mathbb{Z})^{m}
, whose

i^{th}
component
\mathfrak{w}_{i}
is specified by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{i}(L_{j})=\mathfrak{w}_{i}(R_{j})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}&{\text{if $i=j$}}\\
0&{\text{otherwise}}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{i}(U_{j})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&{\text{if $i=j$}}\\
0&{\text{otherwise}}\end{array}\right.

We extend this to a grading on
\mathcal{C}(m,k)[t]

with the convention that

\mathfrak{w}_{i}(t)=1
for
i=1,\dots,m
.
We also introduce a further integer-valued grading
\mathrm{gr}

on
\mathcal{C}(m,k)[t]
so that

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal{C}(m,k))

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}(t)

\displaystyle=2m-2k-2.

The main result here identifies the homology of
the pong algebra with the bordered algebras, and computes the
induced
A_{\infty}
structure on the homology, as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

When
m>1
and
0<k<m-1
,
the homology of
{\mathcal{P}}(m,m-k-1)
is isomorphic, as an algebra, to

\mathcal{C}(m,k)[t]
, equipped with the above gradings.
The homology of
{\mathcal{P}}(m,m-k-1)
, with its induced
A_{\infty}
structure,
is
uniquely characterized up to isomorphism by the following properties:

•

The underlying algebra is
\mathcal{C}(m,k)[t]
.

•

The actions respect the above gradings, in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{i}(\mu_{d}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathfrak{w}_{i}(a_{j})

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}(\mu_{d}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d}))

\displaystyle=d-2+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{gr}(a_{j})

•

the
\mu_{d}
are
\mathbb{F}[t]
-multilinear; i.e.

\mu_{d}(a_{1},\dots,a_{j-1},t\cdot a_{j},a_{j+1},\cdots,a_{d})=t\cdot\mu_{d}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d}).

•

There is a non-zero operation
\mu_{2m-2k}
.

See Section
12.1
for a few remarks on the extreme
cases when
k=0
or
m-1
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
involves a blend
of algebraic and holomorphic techniques. In particular, the proof
relies on the construction of modules associated to Heegaard diagrams
whose boundary is marked in a particular manner, the
pong-bordered boundary

In this article, we first describe the pong algebra. Next, we compute
its homology as a

-bimodule, in

.
The unique characterization of the

The Hochschild cohomology computation is facilitated by a certain

[
10
]
.)

Let

Q(m,k)=\frac{{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)}{v_{1}=\dots=v_{m}=0}.

Theorem 1.2

Given

The above Koszul duality is proved as follows. First, we construct a

.
Next, we construct a bordered diagram whose boundary decomposes as a

in
[

]
. Adapting methods of bordered Floer

, we associate to this diagram a type

Motivation for the constructions.

We pause to explain some of the geometric content of these algebras.
As explained in
[
17
]
, the bordered algebras
\mathcal{C}(m,k)
can
be thought of as generated by
k
-tuples of Reeb chords, with products
in the algebra recording the collisions that occur in certain ends of
moduli spaces. In Section
8
, we also interpret the pong
algebra (and especially its
v_{1}=\dots=v_{m}=0
specialization) as an
algebra of Reeb chords. (Compare the “strands algebras”
of
[
9
]
; see
also
[
4
,
3
,
20
]
.)

Both algebras can be thought of as part of a more general construction
which simultaneously generalizes both the pointed matched circles
of
[
9
]
and the configuration of boundary circles
from
[
17
]
.
The Koszul duality result from Theorem
1.2
can be
thought of as an analogue of the duality result
from
[
7
]
. That paper sets up a Koszul duality between
the algebra associated to a pointed matched circle (i.e. a handle
decomposition of a surface with a single
0
-handle and
2
-handle)
and its dual pointed matched circle. One could envision a more
general, where one associates an algebra to a (more general) handle
decomposition of a surface, which is Koszul dual to the algebra
associated to its dual handle decomposition.

Figure 1.

Dual cell decompositions of
S^{2}
.

The linear arrangement corresponds to
\mathcal{C}
and its dual wedge of circles
correpsponds to the pong algebra.

In this interpretation,
\mathcal{C}(m,k)
corresponds to the handle
decomposition of the sphere with
m
zero-cells,
m-1
one-cells, and
one
2
-cell. The zero-cells are arranged in a linear change,
connected by the
m-1
one-cells. The parameter
k
specifies the
multiplicity of the symmetric product we are considering. The algebra

Q(m,\ell)
corresponds to the dual handle decomposition, with
one
0
-cell and
m-1
one-cells arranged in a bouquet, and
m

2
-cells. See Figure
1
. One expects these algebras
to be Koszul dual, when
k+\ell=m-1
. This Koszul duality is further
explored in
[
16
]
.

Following Auroux
[
2
]
, one can think of the generators of
pong algebra as morphisms in a wrapped Fukaya category, the
(specialized)
v_{1}=\dots=v_{m}=0
product as product on the partially
wrapped Fukaya category of
[
1
]
, and the
unspecialized algebra actions as products in a relative Fukaya
category. This latter identification is explored further
in
[
19
]
.

On an algebraic level, the algebras we consider here are closely
related to the algebras associated to the extended affine symmetric
groups of Manion and Rouquier
[
12
]
; see also
Remark
4.4
below.

Constructing a signed lift of the pong algebra (to
\mathbb{Z}
coefficients)
is a straightforward task in the spirit of
[
15
, Chapter 15]
. Lifting
the holomorphic aspects would require more work.

If

3.1.
The algebras
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{B}}

First, we recall the construction of
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(m,k)
, associated to a
pair of integers
(m,k)
with
0\leq k\leq m-1
. (The

{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(m,k)

The base ring of

Given idempotents states

To specify the product, we proceed as follows. Each idempotent state

has a

The multiplication is specified by

3.2.

Having defined

In Definition

Given

The aim of this section is to introduce the

4.1.
Lifted partial permutations

Let

be the reflection

by

and

denote this quotient map.
Explicitly,

is the integer

so that

or

.

Note that

also acts on the set
\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}

\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
by

is naturally the
m

. We think of these points as being in one-to-one
correspondence with the underlying variables in the pong algebra,
where the point

of

•

is injective.

\langle{\widetilde{f}}(i),{\widetilde{f}}(j)\rangle\not\in\mathrm{Cross}(g)

\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(\widetilde{g}\circ f)

•

\langle i,j\rangle\not\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)

denotes
the map induced from

, then the map from

with the automorphism of

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(S,f)
be a lifted partial permutation,
and
\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)
be a crossing.
Then,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{w}(f)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{cross}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{cross}(f)-1.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{w}(f)-\mathfrak{w}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\subset(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})^{m}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\langle k,\ell\rangle
with

\{[k],[\ell]\}\cap\{[i],[j]\}=\emptyset
,

\langle k,\ell\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)
if and only if

\langle k,\ell\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})
.
Moreover, it is
straightforward to verify the following:

•

If

|\{\langle i,k\rangle,\langle j,k\rangle\}\cap\mathrm{Cross}(f)|=0

then

|\{\langle i,k\rangle,\langle j,k\rangle\}\cap\mathrm{Cross}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})|=0
.

•

If

|\{\langle i,k\rangle,\langle j,k\rangle\}\cap\mathrm{Cross}(f)|=1
,
then

|\{\langle i,k\rangle,\langle j,k\rangle\}\cap\mathrm{Cross}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})|=1
.

•

If

|\{\langle i,k\rangle,\langle j,k\rangle\}\cap\mathrm{Cross}(f)|=2
,
then

|\{\langle i,k\rangle,\langle j,k\rangle\}\cap\mathrm{Cross}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})|=0
.

Obviously,
\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)
and
\langle i,j\rangle\not\in\mathrm{Cross}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})
.
Thus,

\mathrm{cross}(f)-\mathrm{cross}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})=1+2\#\{k\big|\langle i,k\rangle~\text{and}~\langle j,k\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)\rangle\},

and Inequality (
4.5
) follows.

Fix
t\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
, and
let
X_{t}(f)
be as in Equation (
4.3
).
Given
\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)
, define a map

T\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
by

T(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k&{\text{if $[k]\not\in\{[i],[j]\}$}}\\
g\cdot j&{\text{if $k=g\cdot i$ for some $g\in G_{m}$}}\\
g\cdot i&{\text{if $k=g\cdot j$ for some $g\in G_{m}$}}.\end{array}\right.

Let

A=\{k\in X_{t}(f)\big|T(k)\in X_{t}(f),k<t<T(k)\text{~or~}T(k)<t<k\}

and
B=X_{t}(f)\setminus A
.
It is elementary to see that
B=X_{t}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})
.
To see this, note that
k\in B
if
t
lies between
k
and
f(k)
, and one of the following three holds:

•

[k]\not\in\{[i],[j]\}
, in which case
f(k)=f_{\langle i,j\rangle}(k)
.

•

k=g\cdot i
for some
g\in G_{m}
, and
t
does not lie between
g\cdot i
and
g\cdot j
.
In this case,
t
lies between
g\cdot j
and
g\cdot f(i)
, i.e.
t\in X_{t}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})
.

•

k=g\cdot j
for some
g\in G_{m}
, and
t
does not lie between
g\cdot i
and
g\cdot i
.
In this case,
t
lies between
g\cdot i
and
g\cdot f(j)
, so once again,

.

Moreover,

\mathfrak{w}_{t}(f)-\mathfrak{w}_{t}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})=\frac{1}{2}|A|.

The map
T
is a fixed point free involution on

, showing that

\mathfrak{w}_{[t]}(f)-\mathfrak{w}_{t}(f_{\langle i,j\rangle})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, as claimed.

Lemma 4.12
.

Given any
x\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)
, there is an inclusion

\Phi_{x}\colon\mathrm{Cross}(f_{x})\to\mathrm{Cross}(f)

with the property that if
y=\Phi_{x}(y^{\prime})
, then for some

x^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Cross}(f_{y})
,
x=\Phi_{y}(x^{\prime})
; and

(f_{x})_{y^{\prime}}=(f_{y})_{x^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Write
x=\langle i,j\rangle
and
\langle k,\ell\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)\setminus\{x\}
.

If
\{[i],[j]\}\cap\{[k],[\ell]\}=\emptyset
, let

\Phi_{x}(\langle k,\ell\rangle)=\langle k,\ell\rangle
.

Otherwise, we can assume without loss of generality that
[j]=[\ell]
, i.e.

\ell=g\cdot j
. Replacing
k
by
g\cdot i
, we have a triple of integers

(i,j,k)
so that
\langle i,j\rangle=x
and
\langle j,k\rangle=y
.
In this case, let

\Phi_{x}(\langle i,j\rangle)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle i,k\rangle&{\text{if $k$ is between $i$ and $j$.}}\\
\langle j,k\rangle&{\text{otherwise.}}\end{array}\right.

It is straightforward to check that
\Phi_{x}
is a one-to-one
correspondence, and that
(f_{x})_{y^{\prime}}=(f_{y})_{x^{\prime}}

4.2.
The pong algebra

The pong algebra

\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]

Definition 4.13

is as in Equation (

).

Given a partial permutation

\mu_{1}(S,f)=\sum_{\{c\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)\big|\mathrm{cross}(f_{c})=\mathrm{cross}(f)-1\}}v(f,c)\cdot f_{c},

as in Equation (

).

Proposition 4.14
.

The set
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
equipped with
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
is a differential graded algebra.

Proof.

Associativity of
\mu_{2}
follows from the associativity of
the composition of lifted partial permutations.

The property that
\mu_{1}\circ\mu_{1}=0
is an easy consequence of
Lemma
4.12
.

A crossing
\langle i,j\rangle
in

is a

\langle{\widetilde{g}}(i),{\widetilde{g}}(j)\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(f)

or
{\widetilde{g}}(i)>{\widetilde{g}}(j)

\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}(g)

In the first case,

({\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}})_{\langle i,j\rangle}={\widetilde{f}}_{\langle{\widetilde{g}}(i),{\widetilde{g}}(j)\rangle}\circ{\widetilde{g}};

while in the second case,

({\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}})_{\langle i,j\rangle}={\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}}_{\langle i,j\rangle}.

The Leibniz rule,

follows readily.

We abbreviate

For example, we have

4.3.
Alexander and Maslov gradings

Idempotents in the pong algebra correspond to lifted partial
permutations which send
S\subset\mathbb{Z}
to itself via the identity map.
In terms of their pong diagrams, these algebra elements are
collections of
k
vertical segments. These
\binom{m-1}{k}
elements
generate the idempotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
of the pong algebra.

In Definition
4.6
,
the weight
\mathfrak{w}
of a lifted partial permutation
f

was defined as
a map from
(\frac{1}{2}+Z)/G_{m}
to
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. We prefer to think of it
henceforth as a vector
\vec{\mathfrak{w}}
, with components indexed by
integers
i\in(1,\dots,m)
. With this normalization,
\mathfrak{w}_{i}
corresponds
to the value of the function
\mathfrak{w}
at
i-1/2
.

This vector
\vec{\mathfrak{w}}
induces
a grading on
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
with values in
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, with the
understanding that the element
v_{i}
has grading
e_{i}
(the
i^{th}

basis vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
). This grading is additive under
multiplication; and differentiation preserves this grading.

Similarly, the function
\mathrm{cross}(f)
on lifted partial permutations
induces a grading on
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
with values in
\mathbb{Z}
, with the
understanding that
v_{i}
has grading
0
. The grading is additive
under multiplication, and differential drops it by
1
. In the
language of Definition
2.2
,
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
is a
Maslov/Alexander bigraded algebra.

Sometimes, we find it convenient to work with the following
renormalized
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mathbf{N}
on
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
, specified by

(4.9)

\mathbf{N}(f)=\mathrm{cross}(f)-\left(2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathfrak{w}_{i}(f)\right).

Definition 4.15
.

By analogy with
\mathcal{C}
, each element in
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
corresponding to
a lifted partial permutation is called a pure algebra
element. Thus, the pong algebra is the free

\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]
-module generated by the pure algebra elements.

Definition 5.1

An atomic element in

The terminology is justified by the following:

Proposition 5.2
.

Every pure algebra element in
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
(i.e. those elements
corresponding to lifted partial permutations) can be written as a
product of idempotents and atomic generators.

Proof.

We find it convenient to construct factorizations into
{\mathcal{X}}_{i,j}
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{i,j}
, and
{\mathcal{L}}_{j,i}
. This
suffices, since for any idempotent

, the element

{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathcal{X}}_{i,j}
can be written as a product of

{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}
and elements of the form

,
R_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
and

L_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
. Similarly,
{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathcal{R}}_{i,j}
can be
written as a product of
{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}
and elements of the
form
R_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
; and
{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathcal{L}}_{j,i}
can be
written as a product of
{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}
and elements of the form

.

Suppose that

\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}

a
is
an idempotent. For the inductive step, there are 3 cases.

Suppose that for some
x_{i}\in\mathbf{x}
, the strand in
a

x_{i}
moves to the right. Choose
i

We divide this into two further

Suppose that there is some strand

x_{j}
moves to the

j

x_{i}

x_{i}

If there is no strand

moves to the left,
then there is a factorization

or a factorization of

.
The result now holds by induction on the total weight.

Case 2.
All strands in

are stationary. This is clearly an idempotent.

Case 3.
There is some strand in
a
that starts out moving to the left,
but none that starts moving to the right. Take the minimal

so that there

starting at

, then we can factor

We have the following characterization of the atomic generators:

Proposition 5.3
.

For any non-zero homogeneous, non-idempotent element

2\mathfrak{w}(a)-\mathrm{cr}(a)\geq 1
, with equality precisely when

is atomic.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{gr}(a)=2\mathfrak{w}(a)-\mathrm{cr}(a)
. Clearly,
\mathfrak{w}
is additive
under multiplication, in the sense that if

and
b
are pure
algebra elements with
a\cdot b\neq 0
, then

\mathfrak{w}(a\cdot b)=\mathfrak{w}(a)+\mathfrak{w}(b)
. By construction,
\mathrm{cr}
is
also additive under multiplication. It is easy to see that for all

,
\mathrm{gr}(a)=1
. Thus, from
Proposition

, it follows that that
\mathrm{gr}(a)\geq 1

for all pure algebra elements, with

if and only if

is
atomic. Having proved the proposition for pure algebra elements, the

for any homogenous element
a
and

.

\mathrm{gr}(v_{i}\cdot a)=2+\mathrm{gr}(a)
.

We can describe the differential on the pong algebra in terms of its action on
atomic generators. Specifically:

Lemma 5.4
.

The differentials of atomic generators are specified as follows.
Given
i<j
, we have that

(5.1)

\displaystyle dR_{{i},{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<\ell<j}R_{{\ell},{j}}\cdot R_{{i},{\ell}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle dL_{{j},{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<\ell<j}L_{{\ell},{i}}\cdot L_{{j},{\ell}}

, then

while

, then

Proof.

This follows immediately from the definition of the boundary map.

.

\Omega

for

This is straightforward.

.

to

.

A quotient

As a stepping-stone, we will find it convenient to consider the quotient

Lemma 6.7

12.1.
Degenerate cases

The reader may wonder to what extent Theorem
1.1

holds when
k=0
or
k=m-1
.

When
k=m-1
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(m,m-1)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]/(v_{1}\cdots v_{m})

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}(m,0)=H({\mathcal{P}}(m,0))

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]

Thus,

H_{*}({\mathcal{P}}(m,0))\cong\mathcal{C}(m,m-1)[t]
,
provided we introduce to
\mathcal{C}(m,m-1)[t]
new
\mu_{2}
operations, such as

\mu_{2}(v_{1}\cdots v_{i},v_{i+1}\cdots v_{m})=t
for all
1<i<m
.

When
k=0
we have
\mathcal{C}(m,0)\cong\mathbb{F}
, while
H_{*}({\mathcal{P}}(m,m-1))\cong\mathbb{F}[\Omega]
.
Thus, Theorem
1.1
holds on
additively (i.e. Theorem
6.2
holds), but there
cannot be a non-trivial
\mu_{2m}
-action.

Note that Theorem
1.2
applies when
k=m-1
.
When
k=0

Q(m,0)\cong\mathbb{F}
,
\mathcal{C}(m,k)=0
, so
\mathrm{Cobar}(\mathcal{C}(m,k))\cong\mathbb{F}

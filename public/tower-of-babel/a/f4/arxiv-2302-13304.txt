Time evolution of the local gravitational parameters and gravitational wave polarizations in a relativistic MOND theory
Shuxun Tian, Shaoqi Hou, Shuo Cao, Zong-Hong Zhu

Abstract
The recently proposed Skordis-Złośnik theory is the first relativistic MOND theory that can recover the success of the standard Λ \Lambda CDM model at matching observations of the cosmic microwave background. This paper aims to revisit the Newtonian and MOND approximations and the gravitational wave analysis of the theory. For the local gravitational parameters, we show that one could obtain both time-varying effective Newtonian gravitational constant G n G_{\textsc{n}} and time-varying characteristic MOND acceleration scale a mond a_{\textsc{mond}} , by relaxing the static assumption extensively adopted in the literature. Specially, we successfully demonstrate how to reproduce the redshift dependence of a mond a_{\textsc{mond}} observed in the Magneticum cold dark matter simulations. For the gravitational waves, we show that there are only two tensor polarizations, and reconfirm that its speed is equal to the speed of light.



I
Introduction

Modified Newtonian dynamics (MOND) is an alternative to the dark matter paradigm, through the modification of Newton’s law of universal gravitation or Newton’s second law of motion
Milgrom 1983a
;
Milgrom 1983b
;
Milgrom 1983c
. The former belongs to the traditional modified gravity, and construction of its relativistic counterpart has been extensively discussed.
Bekenstein and Milgrom 1984
proposed the first one. However, there are two major problems: the acausal problem
Bekenstein and Milgrom 1984
and the gravitational lensing problem
Bekenstein and Sanders 1994
. Further modifications of the theory have been proposed to address these issues, such as the phase coupling
Bekenstein 1988
and disformal transformations
Bekenstein 1992
;
Bekenstein 1993
;
Sanders 1997
;
Bekenstein 2004
. These attempts made great progress in shaping the relativistic MOND theory and explaining the local gravitational phenomena
Famaey and McGaugh 2012
. However, for the cosmological linear perturbations, no such theory has been shown to successfully fit all the current data about the cosmic microwave background anisotropies and matter power spectra
Skordis et al. 2006
;
Dodelson and Liguori 2006
;
Zuntz et al. 2010
;
Xu et al. 2015
;
Tan and Woodard 2018
. Recently,
Skordis and Złośnik 2021
proposed a new MOND theory to address this observational fitting problem. Analysis of this theory is the topic of this paper. In addition, we note that modification of Newton’s second law still requires further development to arrive at a complete and observationally accepted theory
Milgrom 1994
;
Milgrom 1999
;
Petersen and Lelli 2020
;
Milgrom 2022
.

MOND theories generally predict an universal radial acceleration relation (RAR) — correlation between the observed radial acceleration and that predicted by baryons with Newtonian gravity.
McGaugh et al. 2016
first observed the RAR in the SPARC database, and further data confirmed the conclusion
Lelli et al. 2017
;
Tian et al. 2020
. This may be regarded as an observational evidence supporting MOND. However, after
McGaugh et al. 2016
, the same relation was also observed in the
N
-body simulations of cold dark matter (CDM)
Dai and Lu 2017
;
Keller and Wadsley 2017
;
Garaldi et al. 2018
;
Dutton et al. 2019
. The mass discrepancy acceleration relation, which is similar to RAR, was also predicted by MOND, and observed in both observations
Durazo et al. 2017
and CDM simulations
Navarro et al. 2017
;
Ludlow et al. 2017
. In particular,
Keller and Wadsley 2017
found that the CDM simulated RAR depends on the cosmological redshift. This result implies that, in the framework of CDM, rotating galaxies still satisfy the
universal
RAR at high redshifts. However, the parameter characterizing the acceleration scale in RAR is redshift-dependent. Recently,
Mayer et al. 2023
presented an explicit redshift evolution of this characteristic MOND acceleration scale
a_{\textsc{mond}}
in the
Magneticum
CDM simulations.

In the relativistic MOND theories,
a_{\textsc{mond}}
is a parameter and could be time-varying.
Milgrom 1983a
conjectured that
a_{\textsc{mond}}\propto cH
based on the numerical coincidence of their values at today. In the framework of T
e
V
e
S theory (a relativistic MOND theory)
Bekenstein 2004
,
Bekenstein and Sagi 2008
analyzed this issue after considering the cosmological background evolution of the relevant fields. They found that
a_{\textsc{mond}}
changes on time scales much longer than the Hubble timescale. In this paper, we present the first analysis of the possible time evolution of the local Newtonian and MOND parameters in the Skordis-Złośnik theory
Skordis and Złośnik 2021
. The method is principally the same as that in
Bekenstein and Sagi 2008
. Our result demonstrates how to reproduce the
Magneticum
redshift dependence
Mayer et al. 2023
in such relativistic MOND theory.

The first direct detection of the gravitational wave signal GW150914 has marked the new era of gravitational wave astronomy
Abbott et al. 2016
. In general relativity, there exist two well-known gravitational wave polarizations (plus and cross), traveling at the speed of
light. GW170814 and GW170817 observations confirmed these predictions
Abbott et al. 2017a
;
Takeda et al. 2021
;
Abbott et al. 2017b
. In this paper, we present a gauge-invariant gravitational wave analysis for the Skordis-Złośnik theory, in which the polarization content and the propagation speed are determined.

II
The theory and cosmic evolutions

The Skordis-Złośnik MOND theory is constructed based on a scalar field
\phi
and a vector field
A_{\mu}

Skordis and Złośnik 2021

S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R+\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{mond}}\right]/2\kappa+S_{\rm m}

\kappa=8\pi\tilde{G}/c^{4}

\tilde{G}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{mond}}=-\frac{K_{\textsc{b}}}{2}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}+2(2-K_{\textsc{b}})J^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi

\displaystyle\quad-(2-K_{\textsc{b}})\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Q})-\lambda(A^{\mu}A_{\mu}+1),

(1)

where
F_{\mu\nu}=2\nabla_{[\mu}A_{\nu]}

\mathcal{Y}=q^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi

q^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}+A^{\mu}A^{\nu}
,
\mathcal{Q}=A^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi
,
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Q})
is an arbitrary function,
\lambda
is the Lagrange multiplier (a scalar),
K_{\textsc{b}}
is a dimensionless constant. In our conventions, the dimension of

[A^{\mu}A_{\mu}]=1

\phi
is dimensionless, and
[\mathcal{Y}]=[\mathcal{Q}^{2}]=[\mathcal{F}]=[\lambda]={\rm length}^{-2}

The field equations can be derived from the variational principle. Variation of the action with respect to the metric gives the gravitational field equations

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}-K_{\textsc{b}}F_{\mu}^{\ \alpha}F_{\nu\alpha}+(2-K_{\textsc{b}})\left\{2J_{(\mu}\nabla_{\nu)}\phi-A_{\mu}A_{\nu}\Box\phi\right.

\displaystyle\ +\left.2[A_{(\mu}\nabla_{\nu)}A_{\alpha}-A_{(\mu}\nabla_{|\alpha|}A_{\nu)}]\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\right\}-\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}}A_{(\mu}\nabla_{\nu)}\phi

\displaystyle\ -(2-K_{\textsc{b}}+\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Y}})[\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi+2\mathcal{Q}A_{(\mu}\nabla_{\nu)}\phi]

\displaystyle\ -\lambda A_{\mu}A_{\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{mond}}/2=\kappa T_{\mu\nu},

(2a)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Y}}=\partial\mathcal{F}/\partial\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}}=\partial\mathcal{F}/\partial\mathcal{Q}

\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{I}^{\mu}=0,

(2b)

where
\mathcal{I}^{\mu}=(2-K_{\textsc{b}})J^{\mu}-(2-K_{\textsc{b}}+\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Y}})q^{\alpha\mu}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi-\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}}A^{\mu}/2
. Variation of the action with respect to
A_{\mu}
gives the vector field equations

\displaystyle K_{\textsc{b}}\nabla_{\nu}F^{\nu\mu}+(2-K_{\textsc{b}})[(\nabla^{\mu}A_{\nu})\nabla^{\nu}\phi-\nabla_{\nu}(A^{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\phi)]

\displaystyle\quad-\left[(2-K_{\textsc{b}}+\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Y}})\mathcal{Q}+\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}}/2\right]\nabla^{\mu}\phi-\lambda A^{\mu}=0.

(2c)

Variation of the action with respect to
\lambda
gives a constraint equation for the vector field

A^{\mu}A_{\mu}+1=0.

(2d)

Energy and momentum conservation
\nabla_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
can be directly derived from Eq. (
2

As we discussed in Sec.
I
, one goal of this paper is to study the time evolution of the local gravitational parameters in the Skordis-Złośnik MOND theory. For example, the effective Newtonian gravitational

Damour et al. 1990

,

2d

2

\displaystyle H^{2}+2\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-\frac{8\pi\tilde{G}}{c^{2}}p_{\rm m}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}c^{2},

III
Newtonian and MOND analysis

In the Skordis-Złośnik theory, the form of
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Q})
determines the local gravitational behaviors.

pointed out two key properties. For physically acceptable scenarios, in the strong field region
Note2
,

\mathcal{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
or
\mathcal{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}^{p}

\mathcal{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}^{3/2}

\mathcal{Q}
appearing in
\mathcal{J}
reaches its cosmological minimum
\mathcal{Q}_{c}

\mathcal{J}
(see Fig.
1

;
Damour et al. 1990
;
Babichev et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Burrage and Dombrowski 2020

Considering the great success of the
\Lambda
CDM model in both theories and observations

;
Frieman et al. 2008
;
Bertone and Hooper 2018

Magneticum

\Lambda
CDM simulations

\Lambda

IV
Gravitational wave analysis

Gravitational wave properties in the Skordis-Złośnik theory can be determined by solving the linearized equations of motion about the Minkowski spacetime defined by
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}
,
\bar{A}^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{0}
, and
\bar{\phi}={\rm const.}
. This background solution requires that
\bar{\mathcal{F}}=\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu},\bar{A}^{\mu},\bar{\phi})=0
. The perturbed solutions are
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}
,
A^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{0}+a^{\mu}
, and
\phi=\bar{\phi}+\varphi
. Since the linearized equations of motion are very complicated and coupled together, it is easier to use the gauge-invariant formalism to decouple the equations
Flanagan and Hughes 2005
;
Gong et al. 2018
. Following
Gong et al. 2018
, one can decompose the components of
h_{\mu\nu}
and
a^{\mu}
as

\displaystyle h_{tt}=2\phi,

(14a)

\displaystyle h_{tj}=\beta_{j}+\partial_{j}\gamma,

(14b)

\displaystyle h_{jk}=h_{jk}^{\mathrm{TT}}+\frac{1}{3}H\delta_{jk}+\partial_{(j}\epsilon_{k)}+\left(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{jk}\nabla^{2}\right)\rho,

(14c)

\displaystyle a^{t}=\frac{1}{2}h_{tt}=\phi,

(14d)

\displaystyle a^{j}=\mu^{j}+\partial^{j}\omega.

(14e)

Here,
\partial^{k}h_{jk}^{\mathrm{TT}}=0
,
\eta^{jk}h_{jk}^{\mathrm{TT}}=0
, and
\partial_{j}\beta^{j}=\partial_{j}\epsilon^{j}=\partial_{j}\mu^{j}=0
.
Under the infinitesimal coordinate transformation parameterized by
\xi^{\mu}=(\xi^{t},\xi^{j})=(A,B^{j}+\partial^{j}C)
with
\partial_{j}B^{j}=0
, one knows that

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu},

(15a)

\displaystyle a^{\mu}\rightarrow a^{\mu}+\bar{A}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\mu},

(15b)

\displaystyle\varphi\rightarrow\varphi.

(15c)

Therefore, one determines the following gauge-invariant variables,

\displaystyle\varphi,\quad h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{jk},

(16a)

\displaystyle\Phi=-\phi+\dot{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\ddot{\rho},

(16b)

\displaystyle\Theta=\frac{1}{3}(H-\nabla^{2}\rho),

(16c)

\displaystyle\Xi_{j}=\beta_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\epsilon}_{j},

(16d)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{j}=\beta_{j}+\mu_{j},

(16e)

\displaystyle\Omega=\omega+\frac{1}{2}\dot{\rho}.

(16f)

Then, one can try to reexpress the linearized equations of motion to conclude that

\displaystyle\ddot{h}_{jk}^{\mathrm{TT}}-\nabla^{2}h_{jk}^{\mathrm{TT}}=0,

(17a)

\displaystyle\ddot{\Sigma}_{j}-\nabla^{2}\Sigma_{j}=0,

(17b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial\mathcal{Q}^{2}}\ddot{\varphi}+2\left[\frac{2(2-K_{\textsc{b}})}{K_{\textsc{b}}}+\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial\mathcal{Y}}\right]\nabla^{2}\varphi=0,

(17c)

\displaystyle\Xi_{j}=0,

(17d)

\displaystyle\Theta=\Phi=0,

(17e)

\displaystyle\dot{\Omega}=\frac{K_{\textsc{b}}-2}{K_{\textsc{b}}}\varphi,

(17f)

assuming
\partial\bar{\mathcal{F}}/\partial\mathcal{Q}=0
, where barred quantities are to be evaluated at the flat spacetime background. The above equations show that the tensor and vector modes are propagating at the speed of light, while
\varphi
generally travels at a different speed, which is smaller than the speed of light for the parameter values adopted in Sec.
II
. This result reconfirmed the conclusion presented in
Skordis and Złośnik 2021
.

Provided that the ordinary matter couples with the metric minimally, one can calculate the geodesic deviation equation,
\ddot{x}^{j}=-R_{tjtk}x^{k}
, to determine the polarizations of gravitational waves
Misner et al. 1973
. It turns of that

R_{tjtk}=-\frac{1}{2}h_{jk}^{\mathrm{TT}}.

(18)

Therefore, there are only two polarizations (plus and cross), like in general relativity.

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we discuss the Newtonian, MOND and gravitational wave analyses for the Skordis-Złośnik theory
Skordis and Złośnik 2021
. In the first two cases, after abandoning the static assumption adopted in
Skordis and Złośnik 2021
, we find that whether
G_{\textsc{n}}
and
a_{\textsc{mond}}
are time-varying depends on the specific form of the
\mathcal{J}
function of the theory. Screening the scalar field in strong field region is a sufficient condition to give a constant
G_{\textsc{n}}
, and this scenario may be a preferred choice in both theory and observations. For the
a_{\textsc{mond}}
, we highlight that the theory with Eq. (
11
) could reproduce the
a_{\textsc{mond}}
–
z
dependence observed in the
Magneticum
simulations
Mayer et al. 2023
. Minor extension of the original Skordis-Złośnik theory with Eq. (
12
) gives
a_{\textsc{mond}}\propto cH
. For the gravitational wave analysis, we show that there are only two tensor polarizations, which is preferred by the GW170814 observations
Abbott et al. 2017a
;
Takeda et al. 2021
.

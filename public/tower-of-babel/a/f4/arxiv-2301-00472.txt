Bosonization of the interacting Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model
Tony Jin, Paola Ruggiero, Thierry Giamarchi

Abstract
We derive the bosonization of the interacting fermionic Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) with open boundaries. We use the classical Euler-Lagrange equations of motions of the bosonized theory to compute the density profile of the Majorana edge mode and observe excellent agreement with numerical results, notably the localization of the mode near the boundaries. Remarkably, we find that repulsive or attractive interactions do not systematically localize or delocalize the edge mode but their effects depend on the value of the staggering parameter. We provide quantitative predictions of these effects on the localization length of the edge mode.



Topological concepts have become a central part of contemporary condensed
matter physics
Haldane 2017
. The understanding of the geometrical and topological
objects underpinning band theory
Altland and Zirnbauer 1997
;
Chiu et al. 2016
;
Nakahara 2018
;
Cayssol and Fuchs 2021
has fostered intense activities in diverse areas of condensed matter physics such
as the study of the quantum Hall effect
Klitzing et al. 1980
;
von Klitzing et al. 2020
, spin-orbit induced topological
band insulators
Fu and Kane 2008
;
Kane and Mele 2005
;
Bernevig et al. 2006
, topological quantum computing
Nayak et al. 2008
and so on and so forth.

One current limitation of topological band theory is its
restriction to non-interacting systems. Interactions
will in general spoil the band structure, rendering usual classification
schemes inoperative. There, novel phenomena may be expected, the most
famous example being the fractional quantum Hall effect
Laughlin 1983
.

One of the simplest model capturing the key features of topological
insulators is the Su-Shrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model
Su et al. 1979
. The fermionic
SSH model consists of a 1D tight-binding model with alternating bond
value. Depending on whether the first bond is weak (strong) the model
is either in the topological (trivial) phase. For open boundaries,
one possible characterization of the topological phase is the presence
of two-fold, quasi-degenerate, zero energy, Majorana edge modes that are exponentially
localized at the boundaries.
Remarkably, these edge modes have been observed and characterized experimentally in one-dimensional optical lattices
Atala et al. 2013
and artificial spin chains simulated with Rydberg atoms
de Léséleuc et al. 2019
in ultracold atoms setup.

Although the SSH is now considered a textbook model for topological
insulators, the inclusion of interactions in this model remains to
this day an open question. On the other hand, a powerful technique
that was developped in the previous decades to treat interacting fermionic
systems in 1D is
bosonization

Haldane 1981
;
Giamarchi 2003
.
One trademark prowess of bosonization is to
map interacting spinless fermions in 1D to a
free
bosonic theory. Remarkably this technique has been successfully applied to study the effects of interactions on Majorana modes in superconducting wires
Gangadharaiah et al. 2011
;
Lobos et al. 2012
;
Chua et al. 2020
but so far, to the best of our knowledge, the SSH model has escaped from a similar treatment. In particular, the spatial localization of the edge modes has not been described within the bosonization language.

In this paper, we fill this gap by deriving
the bosonized theory of the interacting SSH model with open boundaries. We use the classical Euler-Lagrange (EL) equations of motion of the bosonized theory to compute the density profile of the Majorana edge mode and observe excellent agreement with numerical
results, notably the localization of the mode near the boundaries. Remarkably, we find that repulsive or attractive interactions do not systematically localize or delocalize the edge mode but their effects depend on the value of the staggering parameter. We provide quantitative predictions of these effects on the localization length. Our results pave the way to a generalization to other interacting topological models.

We begin by discussing the bosonization procedure in the absence of

The Fourier transform for open boundaries is given by

The size of the sytem is taken to be

The continuous fermionic field is

Following the usual bosonization procedure

\displaystyle\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{{\rm R}}(x)+\Psi_{{\rm L}}(x),

\displaystyle\Psi_{{\rm R}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}e^{ik_{F}x}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{\pi}\tilde{c}_{k+k_{F}}e^{ikx},

\displaystyle\Psi_{{\rm L}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\Psi_{{\rm R}}(-x).

For convenience, we also define the “slow” fields

Importantly, because of open boundaries, the left and right movers

In the continuum,

\displaystyle{\rm R}(x)

\displaystyle=F_{{\rm R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha}}e^{i\phi^{R}(x)},

(6)

\displaystyle\phi^{{\rm R}}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\pi x}{L}N_{{\rm R}}+\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(a_{n}e^{i\frac{n\pi x}{L}}+a_{n}^{\dagger}e^{-i\frac{n\pi x}{L}})e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}}.

(7)

where

is the Klein factor associated to the right-mover

F_{{\rm L}}=-F_{{\rm R}}
,
\phi^{{\rm L}}(x)=\phi^{{\rm R}}(-x)
.
The conjugated fields
\phi

\theta
are customarily defined as

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{-\phi_{R}(x)+\phi_{L}(x)}{2},

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi x}{L}N_{R}-i\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(a_{n}-a_{n}^{\dagger})\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}},

(8)

\displaystyle\theta(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\phi_{R}(x)+\phi_{L}(x)}{2},

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(a_{n}+a_{n}^{\dagger})\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}}.

(9)

The particle density operator
\rho
, which counts the number of
particle above the Fermi sea, is deduced from
\phi(x)
through the
relation

\rho(x)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\partial_{x}\phi(x).

(10)

In the remaining, as we want to characterize the
0
energy modes,
we will work at half-filling. It is important to notice that the definition
of the half-filling depends on the total number of sites. Let
N_{F}\in\mathbb{N}
label the last occupied state of the Fermi sea. For
N

even, we have
N_{F}\equiv\frac{N}{2}
. The corresponding momentum is

k_{F}=\frac{\pi N/2}{a(N+1)}\approx\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{2aN}\approx\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{2L}

to first order in
1/L
. For
N
odd, the two possible definitions
of the half-filled state are
N_{F}=\frac{N\pm 1}{2}
with corresponding
Fermi momenta
k_{F}=\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{2L}(1\mp 1)
. In the remaining of the paper, we will chose the convention
N_{F}=\frac{N-1}{2}
for odd number of sites. The Fermi
sea state with all modes filled up to
N_{F}
and empty above will
be referred to as the

state.

We show in the SM
SM
that
H
can be expressed in terms of the
bosonic fields as

\displaystyle H=\int_{0}^{L}dx\bigg(\frac{v_{F}}{2\pi}\left(:(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}:+:(\partial_{x}\theta)^{2}:\right)

(11)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta}{L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}:\cos\left(2\phi(x)+\left(\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L}\right)x\right):\bigg)

where :: denotes normal-ordering of the fermionic modes with respect
to the vacuum and
v_{F}\equiv 2ta\sin(k_{F}a)
the Fermi velocity.
Formula (
11
) constitutes one of the main results of this
paper. We see that the SSH in the bosonized language is almost equivalent
to a sine-Gordon Hamiltonian except for the spatial dependence of the prefactor in front of the sinus. To derive (
11
),
we discarded constant terms and fast-varying modes
\propto e^{2ik_{F}x}
, so the bosonic
field describes modes with slow spatial variation with respect to the
lattice spacing. Since bosonization is a theory describing low energy
excitations, we also expect this expression to be valid for
\frac{\delta}{t}\ll 1
.

We now turn to the computation of
\rho
using the classical Euler-Lagrange (EL) equations of motion. Let
Z\equiv{\rm tr}(e^{-\beta H})

and
\Pi
the conjugated field to
\phi
,
\Pi\equiv\frac{1}{\pi}\partial_{x}\theta.

In the imaginary time formalism, we have

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int{\cal D}\phi{\cal D}\Pi e^{\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{L}d\tau dx{\cal L}},

(12)

with
{\cal L}
the Lagrangian density :
\int dx{\cal L}=\int dxi\Pi\partial_{\tau}\phi-H
.

The EL equations
\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\phi}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\partial_{\tau}\phi}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\partial_{x}\phi}

yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v_{F}}\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi+v_{F}\partial_{x}^{2}\phi

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi\delta}{L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\sin\left(2\phi(x)+\left(\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L}\right)x\right).

(13)

In the
0
temperature limit, all the weight of the probability measure
will be contained in the stationary solution
\partial_{\tau}\phi=0
. Introducing the natural rescaling

y\equiv x/L
,
\varphi(y)\equiv\phi(yL)
, we get the
L
-independent equation

\displaystyle\partial_{y}^{2}\varphi(y)

\displaystyle=-\Delta\frac{\sin(2\varphi(y)+\epsilon\pi y)}{\sin(\pi y)}.

(14)

where we introduced
\Delta=\frac{2\pi\delta L}{v_{F}}\approx\frac{\pi\delta(N+1)}{t}

and
\epsilon=0
, for an
even
number of sites where
k_{F}=\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{2L}

and
\epsilon=1
for an
odd
number of sites using the convention

k_{F}=\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{L}
. The boundary conditions for
\varphi
are
read from Eq.(
8
): At
y=0
we have
\varphi=0

and at
y=1
we have
\varphi=-\pi n_{{\rm R}}
with
n_{{\rm R}}

the number of particles created on top of the vacuum.

Let us make some remarks here. First, note that
\Delta
is an adimensioned
parameter that fully characterizes the solution of the EL equations.
Note that
\Delta
scales linearly in
N
and
\frac{\delta}{t}
,
so increasing the system size has exactly the same effect as increasing
the ratio
\frac{\delta}{t}
. Interestingly, going from the odd to
the even case is equivalent to shift
\varphi
by
\frac{\pi}{2}y

which can be interpreted as substracting
half
a particle to the system.

To the best of our knowledge, (
14
)
has no known analytical solution and we have to resort to numerics. To assert the validity of our approach, we compare numerical
solutions of (
14

(ED) results performed on the discrete Hamiltonian (
1
) in the zero temperature ground state.
The ED results show fast oscillations on the scale of the lattice

\propto 1/k_{F}
that we do not see from the solutions of

over the fast oscillations gives a smoothly varying density profile

.
We also checked that the agreement holds both for
\delta
positive
or negative, for an even or an odd number of sites and for different

For the even case and

To match the boundary condition, the field

The stiffness of the jumps of

For the free SSH, it is known -see e.g

We now turn to interactions. We consider a nearest neighbor interacting
term of the form

H_{{\rm I}}:=V\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(n_{j}-1/2\right)\left(n_{j+1}-1/2\right),

(17)

with

,

\displaystyle H=

Conclusion
- In this paper, we derived the bosonized theory of the interacting SSH model with open boundaries. Importantly, our study offers quantitative arguments to determine the effects of interactions on the edge mode and pave the way to study other interacting topological models such as the spin 1 antiferromagnetic Heisenberg chain
Haldane 1983a
;
Haldane 1983b
, the AKLT model
Affleck et al. 1987
or the Kitaev fermionic chain
Kitaev 2001
. One of our remarkable results for the SSH model is that attractive (repulsive) interactions do not systematically delocalize or localize the edge mode, but this behavior is strongly dependent on the value of the staggering parameter
\delta
.

We focused on the mean density profile of the edge mode but it would be interesting as a future direction to understand the interplay between interactions and quantum correlations of the edges. Another notable point is that we systematically discarded the Umklapp term in our study. Nevertheless, in the strongly repulsive regime, it is expected to play a role, leading to possibly interesting new phenomena.

(29)

\displaystyle\phi^{{\rm L}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi x}{L}N_{{\rm R}}+\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(a_{n}e^{-i\frac{n\pi x}{L}}+a_{n}^{\dagger}e^{i\frac{n\pi x}{L}})e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}}.

(30)

and the canonical fields
\phi
and
\theta
are defined as

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{-\phi_{\rm R}(x)+\phi_{\rm L}(x)}{2},

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi x}{L}N_{{\rm R}}-i\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(a_{n}-a_{n}^{\dagger})\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}},

(31)

\displaystyle\theta(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\phi_{\rm R}(x)+\phi_{\rm L}(x)}{2},

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(a_{n}+a_{n}^{\dagger})\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}}.

(32)

It will turn out to be useful to divide the
\phi^{{\rm R}}
operator
into an annihilation part
\varphi^{{\rm R}}
and a creation part
with respect to the vacuum state :

\displaystyle\phi^{{\rm R}}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{{\rm R}}+\varphi^{{\rm R}\dagger},

(33)

\displaystyle\varphi^{{\rm R}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi x}{L}N_{R}+\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}a_{n}e^{i\frac{n\pi x}{L}}e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}},

(34)

\displaystyle\varphi^{{\rm R}\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}a_{n}^{\dagger}e^{-i\frac{2n\pi x}{2L}}e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi n}{2L}}.

(35)

These operators have commutation relation

\displaystyle[\varphi^{R},\varphi^{R\dagger}]

\displaystyle=-\log(1-e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi}{L}}).

(36)

Using Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff relation
e^{A+B}=e^{A}e^{B}e^{-\frac{1}{2}[A,B]}
when
[[A,B],B]=[[A,B],A]=0

and
e^{-\frac{1}{2}[\varphi^{R},\varphi^{R\dagger}]}=\sqrt{1-e^{-\alpha\frac{\pi}{L}}}\approx\sqrt{\frac{\alpha\pi}{L}}
,
we can express
\mathrm{R}(x)
in terms of normal-ordered operators
(Normal-ordering denoted by
::
here refers to the normal order
of bosons with respect to the Fermi sea vacuum state denoted
\left|\Omega\right\rangle
)
:

{\rm R}(x)=F_{\mathrm{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2L}}:e^{i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)}:.

(37)

The normal ordered version of the left field is

{\rm L}(x)=-F_{\mathrm{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2L}}:e^{i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(-x)}:.

(38)

A useful relation allowing us to deal with product of normal ordered
field is

:e^{ia\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)}::e^{ib\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(y)}:=e^{-ab\left\langle\Omega\right|\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(y)\left|\Omega\right\rangle}:e^{ia\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)+ib\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(y)}:

(39)

and an explicit calculation gets us

e^{-ab\left\langle\Omega\right|\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(y)\left|\Omega\right\rangle}=(1-e^{-\frac{\pi}{L}(\alpha+i(y-x))})^{ab}.

(40)

Similarly, for the field
\phi
:

:e^{ia\phi(x)}::e^{ib\phi(y)}:=:e^{ia\phi(x)+ib\phi(y)}:\left(\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(x+y)}{2L}\right)}{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(x-y)}{2L}\right)}\right)^{-ab/4}.

(41)

Finally, another useful relation for normal-ordering is :

\displaystyle e^{ib\phi(x)}

\displaystyle=:e^{ib\phi(x)}:e^{-\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left\langle\Omega\right|\phi^{2}(x)\left|\Omega\right\rangle},

(42)

\displaystyle=:e^{ib\phi(x)}:\left(\frac{2L}{\pi\alpha}\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)\right)^{-\frac{b^{2}}{4}}.

(43)

Appendix B
Derivation of the bosonized form of the SSH Hamiltonian

In this part, we show how to derive the bosonized Hamiltonians (11)
and (18) of the main text.

The discrete Hamiltonian of the fermionic interacting SSH with open
boundaries was given by

H=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(-t-\delta(-1)^{j}\right)\left(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+{\rm h.c}\right)+V\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right).

(44)

We split it in three parts that we will treat separately.
H=H_{0}+H_{1}+H_{{\rm I}}

with
H_{0}
the tight-binding term,
H_{1}
the staggered part
and
H_{{\rm I}}
the interacting part

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=-t\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+{\rm h.c}.\right),

(45)

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=-\delta(-1)^{j}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+{\rm h.c}.\right),

(46)

\displaystyle H_{{\rm I}}

\displaystyle=V\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right).

(47)

B.1
Bosonization of the tight-binding part
H_{0}

Although the bosonization of the tight-binding chain is a standard
textbook derivation, we redo it here because we are dealing with open
boundaries. Because of this, the left and right movers are not independent
fields and this may give rise to differences with the standard infinite
system case.

Let us write
H_{0}
in terms of the continuous fermionic field
:

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=-t\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+{\rm h.c}.\right)

(48)

\displaystyle=-t\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(\Psi^{\dagger}(x)\Psi(x+a)+{\rm h.c}\right),

(49)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{t}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dx\left(e^{ik_{F}a}\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)+e^{-ik_{F}a}\mathrm{L}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{L}(x+a)+{\rm h.c}\right)

(50)

where going from the second line to the third we “unfolded” the
fields on
[-L,L]
by making use of
{\rm R}(x)=-{\rm L}(-x)
and
discarded fast oscillating terms proportional to
e^{\pm i2k_{F}x}
,

\Psi_{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\mathrm{L}}
and
\Psi_{\mathrm{L}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\mathrm{R}}
.
As usual in continuous field theory, the
a\to 0
limit must be regularized.
To this end, we denote by
::\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)::

the
point-splitting
procedure which consists in substracting
the infinite vacuum contribution to our operator

\displaystyle::\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)::

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 0}[\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)-\left\langle\Omega\right|\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)\left|\Omega\right\rangle].

(51)

Using Eq.(
39
) we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}:e^{-i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)+i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x+a)}:(1-e^{-\frac{\pi}{L}(\alpha+ia)})^{-1},

(52)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Omega\right|\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)\left|\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}(1-e^{-\frac{\pi}{L}(\alpha+ia)})^{-1}.

(53)

\displaystyle::\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)::

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i\pi a}:\left(ia\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\left(a\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)\right)^{2}+i\frac{a^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)\right):

(54)

Similarly, for the left moving field, we have

\displaystyle::\mathrm{L}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{L}(x+a)::

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i\pi a}:\left(ia\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(-x)+\frac{1}{2}\left(a\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(-x)\right)^{2}-i\frac{a^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(-x)\right):

(55)

When integrating these fields over
[-L,L]
, using the periodicity
of
\phi^{\mathrm{R}}
on that interval, the terms
\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}

and
\partial_{x}^{2}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}
vanish. Now combining with
the h.c. we get

\displaystyle-\frac{t}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dx\left(e^{ik_{F}a}::\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{R}(x+a)::+{\rm h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle=-i\frac{ta}{8\pi}\int_{-L}^{L}dx\left(e^{ik_{F}a}:\left(\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)\right)^{2}:-e^{-ik_{F}a}:\left(\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)\right)^{2}:\right),

(56)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{2ta\sin(k_{F}a)}_{=v_{F}}\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{-L}^{L}dx:\left(\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)\right)^{2}:.

(57)

And similarly for the left field

\displaystyle-\frac{t}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dx\left(e^{-ik_{F}a}::\mathrm{L}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{L}(x+a)::+{\rm h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{F}}{8\pi}\int_{-L}^{L}dx:\left(\partial_{x}\phi^{{\rm L}}(x)\right)^{2}:.

Using
\phi^{{\rm R}}(x)=\phi^{{\rm L}}(-x)
we can fold back everything
on the interval
[0,L]
. Using additionally that
(\partial_{x}\phi^{{\rm R}})^{2}+(\partial_{x}\phi^{{\rm L}})^{2}=2((\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}+(\partial_{x}\theta)^{2})
,
we end up with

H_{0}=\int_{0}^{L}dx\frac{v_{F}}{2\pi}\left(:(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}:+:(\partial_{x}\theta)^{2}:\right).

(58)

B.2
Bosonization of the staggered part
H_{1}
.

We now turn to the bosonization of the staggered part
H_{1}
.
The philosophy is essentially the same as before except that, because
of the
(-1)^{j}
prefactor, the slow-varying terms are going to
be the cross terms
\Psi_{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\mathrm{L}}

and
\Psi_{\mathrm{L}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\mathrm{R}}
and the fast varying
ones, that we will discard,
\Psi_{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\mathrm{R}}

and
\Psi_{\mathrm{L}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\mathrm{L}}
.

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=-\delta(-1)^{j}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+{\rm h.c}.\right),

(59)

\displaystyle=-\delta\int_{0}^{L}dxe^{i\frac{\pi x}{a}}\left(\Psi^{\dagger}(x)\Psi(x+a)+{\rm h.c}\right),

(60)

\displaystyle\approx-\delta\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(e^{i\frac{\pi x}{a}}e^{-i2k_{F}x}e^{-ik_{F}a}{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)+e^{-i\frac{\pi x}{a}}e^{2ik_{F}x}e^{ik_{F}a}{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x+a)+{\rm h.c}\right)

(61)

where going from the second to the third line, we ignored the fast
oscillating terms and made use of the fact that
(-1)^{j}=e^{i\frac{\pi x}{a}}=e^{-i\frac{\pi x}{a}}

for
x=ja
. Since we are half-filling
2k_{F}x\approx\frac{\pi x}{a}

which will cancel the
(-1)^{j}
contribution. However, as is discussed
in the main text, the expression of
k_{F}
has subleading correction
in
1/L
depending on the parity of the number of sites. For
N

even,
k_{F}\approx\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{2L}
to first order
in
1/L
. For
N
odd, the two possible Fermi momenta are
k_{F}\approx\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{2L}(1\mp 1)

depending on the convention. We will use the convention
k_{F}=\frac{\pi}{2a}

for odd sites in the following. It is important to keep track of
1/L

term since
x
itself ranges from
0
to
L
. Thus, terms proportional to
x/L

are in general not negligible in the thermodynamic limit.

To regularize this expression, we again need to consider the point-splitting
of
H_{1}
. We focus on the first term :

\displaystyle::{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)::

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 0}[{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)-\langle{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)\rangle],

(62)

\displaystyle{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2L}:e^{-i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)}::e^{i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(-x-a)}:.

(63)

Using Eq.(
39
) we get

\displaystyle{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2L}:e^{-i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(x)+i\phi^{\mathrm{R}}(-x-a)}:(1-e^{-\frac{i\pi(-2x-a)}{L}})^{-1},

(64)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{4L\sin\frac{\pi(x+a/2)}{L}}e^{-\frac{i\pi(x+a/2)}{L}}:e^{2i\phi(x)+ia\partial_{x}\phi^{\mathrm{L}}(x)}:

(65)

which in the limit
a\to 0
becomes

\displaystyle{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)

\displaystyle=-\frac{ie^{-\frac{i\pi x}{L}}}{4L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}:e^{2i\phi(x)}:

(66)

and

::{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x+a)::=-\frac{ie^{-\frac{i\pi x}{L}}}{4L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}(:e^{2i\phi(x)}:-1).

(67)

Similarly

\displaystyle::{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x+a)::

\displaystyle=\frac{ie^{\frac{i\pi x}{L}}}{4L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}(:e^{-2i\phi(x)}:-1)

(68)

which leads to

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=-\delta\int_{0}^{L}dx\frac{1}{4iL\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\left(e^{i\left(-k_{F}a+x(\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L})\right)}(:e^{2i\phi(x)}:-1)-e^{-i\left(-k_{F}a+x(\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L})\right)}(:e^{-2i\phi(x)}:-1)+{\rm h.c}\right),

(69)

\displaystyle=-\delta\int_{0}^{L}dx\frac{1}{L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\left(:\sin\left(2\phi(x)+(\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L})x-k_{F}a\right):-\sin\left((\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L})x-k_{F}a\right)\right),

(70)

\displaystyle=\delta\int_{0}^{L}dx\frac{1}{L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\left(:\cos\left(2\phi(x)+(\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L})x\right):-\cos\left((\frac{\pi}{a}-2k_{F}-\frac{\pi}{L})x\right)\right).

(71)

In the last line, we simplified the expression by making the approximation

k_{F}a\approx\frac{\pi}{2}
. Note that such approximation cannot
be done for the
k_{F}x
term. The part independent from
\phi(x)

is precisely the contribution of the vacuum energy.
For the rest of the derivation, we will focus on the case of an even
number of sites for which
k_{F}=\frac{\pi}{2a}-\frac{\pi}{2L}
so
that

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\delta\int_{0}^{L}dx\frac{1}{L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\left(:\cos\left(2\phi(x)\right):-1\right).

(72)

B.3
Bosonization of the interacting part
H_{{\rm I}}
.

We now derive the bosonization of the interacting term

H_{{\rm I}}\equiv V\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)

(73)

with
n_{j}
the particle number operator
c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}
.
Recall

\displaystyle c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}

\displaystyle=a({\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x)+{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x)+{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x)e^{-2ik_{F}x}+{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x)e^{2ik_{F}x}).

The point-split expressions we previously found were

\displaystyle::{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x)::=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(:\partial_{x}\phi^{{\rm R}}:\right),

(74)

\displaystyle::{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x)::=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(:\partial_{x}\phi^{{\rm L}}:\right),

(75)

\displaystyle::{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x)::=-\frac{i}{4L}\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi x}{L}}}{\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\left(:e^{2i\phi}:-1\right),

(76)

\displaystyle::{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x)::=\frac{i}{4L}\frac{e^{i\frac{\pi x}{L}}}{\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\left(:e^{-2i\phi}:-1\right).

(77)

So that

\displaystyle::{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x)::+::{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x)::

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\partial_{x}\phi,

(78)

\displaystyle::{\rm R}^{\dagger}(x){\rm L}(x)e^{-2ik_{F}x}::+::{\rm L}^{\dagger}(x){\rm R}(x)e^{2ik_{F}x}::

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{4L}\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi x}{a}}}{\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}\left(:e^{2i\phi}:-:e^{-2i\phi}:\right),

(79)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi x}{a}}}{\pi\alpha}\left(\sin(2\phi)\right).

(80)

Where we used the formula
e^{ib\phi(x)}=:e^{ib\phi(x)}:\left(\frac{2L}{\pi\alpha}\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)\right)^{-\frac{b^{2}}{4}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle H_{{\rm I}}=

\displaystyle aV\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(-\frac{1}{\pi}\partial_{x}\phi(x)-\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi x}{a}}}{\pi\alpha}\left(\sin(2\phi(x))\right)\right)\left(-\frac{1}{\pi}\partial_{x}\phi(x+a)-\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi(x+a)}{a}}}{\pi\alpha}\left(\sin(2\phi(x+a))\right)\right).

(81)

Terms proportional to
e^{\pm ik_{F}x}
in this product will be discarded
as they are oscillating rapidly. We will also discard any constant term
whenever they arise and systematically take the limit
a\to 0

when it is unambiguous. Note that, because we are at half-filling,
we have to keep the Umklapp terms proportional to
e^{\pm i4k_{F}x}
.

\displaystyle H_{{\rm I}}=

\displaystyle aV\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(\partial_{x}\phi\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{(\pi\alpha)^{2}}\left(\sin(2\phi(x))\sin(2\phi(x+a))\right)\right),

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aV\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(\partial_{x}\phi\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2(\pi\alpha)^{2}}\left(\cos(4\phi(x))-\cos(2a\partial_{x}\phi)\right)\right),

(83)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle aV\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\partial_{x}\phi\right)^{2}+\frac{\cos(4\phi(x))}{2(\pi a)^{2}}\right)

(84)

where in the last line we identified the cut-off
\alpha
and the
lattice spacing
a
. The normal-ordered version is

H_{{\rm I}}=\int_{0}^{x}dx\left(\frac{2aV}{\pi^{2}}:(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}:+\frac{V\pi^{2}a^{3}}{2^{5}\left(L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)^{4}}:\cos(4\phi):\right).

(85)

B.4
Putting everything together and normal-ordering with the new vacuum

Putting everything together, we finally arrive for the total Hamiltonian
to

H=\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{u}{K}:(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}:+uK:\left(\partial_{x}\theta\right)^{2}:\right)+\frac{\delta}{L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}}:\cos(2\phi):+\frac{V\pi^{2}a^{3}}{2^{5}\left(L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)^{4}}:\cos(4\phi):\right),

(86)

with
uK\equiv v_{F}
and
\frac{u}{K}=v_{F}+\frac{4Va}{\pi}
. The
non-normal ordered version is

H=\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{u}{K}(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}+uK\left(\partial_{x}\theta\right)^{2}\right)+\frac{2\delta}{a\pi}\cos(2\phi)+\frac{V}{2a\pi^{2}}\cos(4\phi)\right).

(87)

With the interactions, the free part of the Hamiltonian has been renormalized.
We have to redefine the normal-ordering with respect to this new free
Hamiltonian in order to get the correct path integral formulation.
Let’s call the new normal order
::_{{\rm I}}
. We introduce
the rescaled fields
\tilde{\phi}=\sqrt{K}\phi
and
\tilde{\theta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\theta
.
This transformation preserves the commutation relation between the
field operators. The free part of the Hamiltonian in terms of these
fields is expressed as

\int_{0}^{L}\frac{dx}{2\pi}u\left((\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi})^{2}+(\partial_{x}\tilde{\theta})^{2}\right).

(88)

We see that it has the same form than a genuine free Hamiltonian whose
Fermi velocity would be given by
u
. In particular, let
\left|\Omega_{{\rm I}}\right\rangle

be the new vacuum. We have :

\left\langle\Omega_{{\rm I}}\right|\tilde{\phi}^{2}\left|\Omega_{{\rm I}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\Omega\right|\phi^{2}\left|\Omega\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{2L}{\pi\alpha}\sin(\frac{\pi x}{L})\right),

(89)

so that

\displaystyle e^{i\zeta\phi}

\displaystyle=:e^{i\zeta\phi}:_{{\rm I}}e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{2}K\left\langle\Omega_{{\rm I}}\right|\tilde{\phi}^{2}\left|\Omega_{{\rm I}}\right\rangle},

(90)

\displaystyle=:e^{i\zeta\phi}:_{{\rm I}}\left(\frac{2L}{\pi\alpha}\sin(\frac{\pi x}{L})\right)^{-\frac{K\zeta^{2}}{4}}.

(91)

The normal-ordered Hamiltonian thus becomes :

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}dx\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{u}{K}:(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}:_{{\rm I}}+uK:(\partial_{x}\theta)^{2}:_{{\rm I}}\right)+\frac{2\delta}{a\pi}\left(\frac{\pi a}{2}\right)^{K}\frac{1}{\left(L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)^{K}}:\cos(2\phi):_{{\rm I}}

(92)

\displaystyle+\frac{V}{2a\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(\frac{2L}{\pi\alpha}\sin(\frac{\pi x}{L})\right)^{4K}}:\cos(4\phi):_{{\rm I}}\Bigg).

(93)

Note that in the main text, we neglected the Umklapp contribution which is the
\cos(4\phi)
term.

Appendix C
Bessel functions

In this section, we show that the differential equation

\partial_{x}^{2}h=-a\frac{h}{x^{b}}

(94)

with
a,b
real and positive admits as solution functions of the
form

h(x)\equiv\sqrt{x}f_{(|2-b|)^{-1}}\left(\sqrt{a}\frac{2}{|2-b|}x^{\frac{2-b}{2}}\right)

(95)

where
f_{\alpha}
is a Bessel function of the first or second kind,
i.e a function satisfying the following relation :

y^{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(y)+yf^{\prime}_{\alpha}(y)+(y^{2}-\alpha^{2})f_{\alpha}(y)=0.

(96)

Let
y=\mu x^{\nu}
and
h(x)=\sqrt{x}f\left(y=\mu x^{\nu}\right)

with
\mu\geq 0
and
\nu
free variables for now. The previous equation
translates for
h
into the relation

\displaystyle h(x)x^{-1/2}\left(\frac{1}{4\nu^{2}}-\alpha^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}h^{\prime\prime}(x)x^{3/2}+\mu^{2}x^{2\nu-1/2}h(x)

\displaystyle=0.

(97)

Fixing
\alpha=\frac{1}{|2\nu|}
, we end up with

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\nu^{2}\mu^{2}x^{2(\nu-1)}h(x).

(98)

For
h
to be solution of (
94
), we must have

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{2-b}{2},

(99)

\displaystyle|\mu|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{a}\frac{2}{|2-b|}

(100)

which ends our proof.

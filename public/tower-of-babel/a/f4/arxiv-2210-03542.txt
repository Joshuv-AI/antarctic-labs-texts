Ferromagnetic Heisenberg model with the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction
E. Albayrak

Abstract
The spin-1/2 Heisenberg model is formulated in terms of a mean-field approximation (MFA) by using the matrix forms of spin operators S ^ x , S ^ y \hat{S}_{x},\hat{S}_{y} and S ^ z \hat{S}_{z} in three-dimensions. The considered Hamiltonian consists of bilinear exchange interaction parameters ( J x , J y , J z ) (J_{x},J_{y},J_{z}) , Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions ( Δ x , Δ y , Δ z ) (\Delta_{x},\Delta_{y},\Delta_{z}) and external magnetic field components ( H x , H y , H z ) (H_{x},H_{y},H_{z}) . The magnetization and its components are obtained in the MFA with the general anisotropic case with J x ≠ J y ≠ J z J_{x}\neq J_{y}\neq J_{z} for various values of coordination numbers q q . Then, the thermal variations of magnetizations are investigated in detail to obtain the phase diagrams of the model for the isotropic case with J x = J y = J z > 0 J_{x}=J_{y}=J_{z}>0 . It is found that the model exhibits ferromagnetic, paramagnetic, random phase regions and an extra ferromagnetic phase at which the components of magnetizations present branching. Key words: spin 1/2, ferromagnetic, Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya, XYZ model, magnetization, phase diagrams

2
The formulation

The anisotropic spin-1/2 XYZ Heisenberg Hamiltonian in terms of the bilinear exchange interaction parameters and the DM interactions between the nearest-neighbor (NN) spins and the external magnetic field components acting on each spin site is given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-J_{x}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}S_{i}^{x}S_{j}^{x}-J_{y}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}S_{i}^{y}S_{j}^{y}-J_{z}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}S_{i}^{z}S_{j}^{z}-\Delta_{x}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}(S_{i}^{y}S_{j}^{z}-S_{i}^{z}S_{j}^{y})-\Delta_{y}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}(S_{i}^{z}S_{j}^{x}-S_{i}^{x}S_{j}^{z})

(2.1)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Delta_{z}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}(S_{i}^{x}S_{j}^{y}-S_{i}^{y}S_{j}^{x})-H_{x}\sum_{i}S_{i}^{x}-H_{y}\sum_{i}S_{i}^{y}-H_{z}\sum_{i}S_{i}^{z},

where
\langle i,j\rangle
refers to the summation over the NN spins.
\hat{S}_{i}^{x},\hat{S}_{i}^{y}
and
\hat{S}_{i}^{z}
are the components of spin-1/2 operator at site
i
which are given in the matrix forms as

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{i}^{x}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right),\hskip 1.42271pt\hat{S}_{i}^{y}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-i\\
i&0\\
\end{array}\right)\hskip 1.42271pt,\hskip 1.42271pt\hat{S}_{i}^{z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right).

In the MFA, the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
in equation (
2.1
) can be written in the MF form as

\displaystyle-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\rm{MFA}}=-\beta\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}_{\rm{MFA}}^{(i)},

(2.9)

in which

\displaystyle-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\rm{MFA}}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta q(J_{x}M_{x}S_{i}^{x}+J_{y}M_{y}S_{i}^{y}+J_{z}M_{z}S_{i}^{z})+\beta(H_{x}S_{i}^{x}+H_{y}S_{i}^{y}+H_{z}S_{i}^{z})

(2.10)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}(S_{i}^{y}M_{z}-S_{i}^{z}M_{y})+\beta\Delta_{y}(S_{i}^{z}M_{x}-S_{i}^{x}M_{z})+\beta\Delta_{z}(S_{i}^{x}M_{y}-S_{i}^{y}M_{x}),

where
M_{\mu}=\langle S_{j}^{\mu}\rangle
are the magnetization components with
{\mu}=x,y,z
and
\beta=1/({\rm k}T)
with
{\rm k}
being the Boltzmann constant set equal to 1 for convenience.

The matrix representation of
-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\rm{MFA}}^{(i)}
is obtained by using the spin operators, i.e., equation (
2
), and is found as

\displaystyle-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\rm{MFA}}^{(i)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}H_{11}&H_{12}\\
H_{21}&H_{22}\\
\end{array}\right),

where the matix elements are given as

\displaystyle H_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\beta}/{2}[q(\Delta_{y}M_{x}-\Delta_{x}M_{y}+J_{z}M_{z})+H_{z}],

\displaystyle H_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\beta}/{2}[q(J_{x}M_{x}+\Delta_{z}(\mathrm{i}M_{x}+M_{y})-\mathrm{i}J_{y}M_{y}-\mathrm{i}\Delta_{x}M_{z}-\Delta_{y}M_{z})+(H_{x}-\mathrm{i}H_{y})],

\displaystyle H_{21}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\beta}/{2}[q(J_{x}M_{x}+\Delta_{z}(-\mathrm{i}M_{x}+M_{y})+\mathrm{i}J_{y}M_{y}+\mathrm{i}\Delta_{x}M_{z}-\Delta_{y}M_{z})+(H_{x}+\mathrm{i}H_{y}),

\displaystyle H_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\beta}/{2}[q(-\Delta_{y}M_{x}+\Delta_{x}M_{y}-J_{z}M_{z})-H_{z}].

It is clear that
H_{12}=H_{21}^{*}
, which ensures that the eigenvalues of this matrix are real. Thus, the eigenvalues are given as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm{\beta}/{2}\left[\left(H_{x}^{2}+H_{y}^{2}+H_{z}^{2}\right)+2q\left(-\Delta_{z}H_{y}M_{x}+\Delta_{y}H_{z}M_{x}+H_{x}J_{x}M_{x}+\Delta_{z}H_{x}M_{y}\right.\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\Delta_{x}H_{z}M_{y}+H_{y}J_{y}M_{y}-\Delta_{y}H_{x}M_{z}+\Delta_{x}H_{y}M_{z}+H_{z}J_{z}M_{z}\right)+q^{2}\left(\Delta_{y}^{2}M_{x}^{2}+\Delta_{z}^{2}M_{x}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle J_{x}^{2}M_{x}^{2}-2\Delta_{x}\Delta_{y}M_{x}M_{y}+2\Delta_{z}J_{x}M_{x}M_{y}-2\Delta_{z}J_{y}M_{x}M_{y}+\Delta_{x}^{2}M_{y}^{2}+\Delta_{z}^{2}M_{y}^{2}+J_{y}^{2}M_{y}^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\Delta_{x}\Delta_{z}M_{x}M_{z}-2\Delta_{y}J_{x}M_{x}M_{z}+2\Delta_{y}J_{z}M_{x}M_{z}-2\Delta_{y}\Delta_{z}M_{y}M_{z}+2\Delta_{x}J_{y}M_{y}M_{z}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\left.2\Delta_{x}J_{z}M_{y}M_{z}+\Delta_{x}^{2}M_{z}^{2}+\Delta_{y}^{2}M_{z}^{2}+J_{z}^{2}M_{z}^{2}\right)\right]^{1/2}.

Having obtained the eigenvalues (
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
), we are now ready to obtain the partition function which is given as

\displaystyle Z_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{(}Tr)_{(i)}\exp[-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\rm{MFA}}^{(i)}]

(2.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{n}}=\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}+\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{2}}=2\cosh(\Lambda).

since
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}=-\Lambda_{2}
. The free energy of the model is found from the well-known definition by using the partition function as

\displaystyle f=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln Z_{i}

(2.15)

which will be utilized to find the formulations of the order-parameters.
The dipolar moments or magnetization components,
M_{\mu}=\langle S_{j}^{\mu}\rangle
with
{\mu}=x,y,z
are found from

\displaystyle M_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle S_{i}^{\mu}\rangle=-\frac{\partial f}{\partial H_{\mu}}=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{i}}{\partial H_{\mu}}=\frac{\mathrm{(}Tr)_{(i)}\left\{S_{i}^{\mu}\exp\left[-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\rm{MFA}}^{(i)}\right]\right\}}{Z_{i}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\left[\frac{({\partial\Lambda_{1}}/{\partial H_{\mu}})\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}+({\partial\Lambda_{2}}/{\partial H_{\mu}})\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{2}}}{\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}+\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\left[\frac{({\partial\Lambda_{1}}/{\partial h_{\mu}})\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}+({\partial\Lambda_{2}}/{\partial h_{\mu}})\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{2}}}{\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{1}}+\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda_{2}}}\right],

where
\beta^{\prime}=\beta J_{z}
and
h_{\mu}=H_{\mu}/J_{z}
which are the reduced temperature and external magnetic field components. The explicit forms of the magnetization components are too long to be given explicitly. The magnitude of the magnetization vector,
\vec{M}_{T}
=
M_{x}\hat{i}+M_{y}\hat{j}+M_{z}\hat{k}
, is obtained by using the magnetization components given in equation (
2.16
) as

\displaystyle M_{T}=\sqrt{\sum_{\mu=x,y,z}M_{\mu}^{2}}=\sqrt{M_{x}^{2}+M_{y}^{2}+M_{z}^{2}}.

(2.17)

Having obtained the formulations for the magnetization components and magnetization in the MFA for the general case, we are now ready to study their thermal variations for the given values of
\Delta_{\mu}
,

,
H_{\mu}
and the coordination number

J=J_{x}=J_{y}=J_{z}>0
corresponding to the isotropic case, as seen in the next section.

5
Summary and conclusions

The spin-1/2 Heisenberg Hamiltonian for the FM case with
J=J_{x}=J_{y}=J_{z}>0
is considered to study the effects of DM interactions with
\Delta_{m}=\Delta_{x}=\Delta_{y}=\Delta_{z}
on the thermal variations of the magnetization components and magnetization when the external magnetic field components
H=H_{x}=H_{y}=H_{z}
are turned off and on. The phase diagrams of the model are calculated on the (
\Delta_{m}/J,T/J
) planes for
q=3,4
and 6. Three different FM phase regions are observed with
M_{x}=M_{y}=M_{z}
,
M_{x}=M_{z}=-M_{y}
and the one with
M_{x}=M_{z}\neq M_{y}
exhibits branching in addition to the PM and random phase regions. It should be noted that this model has not been studied before, so the comparison is not possible. As a last word, the anisotropic case of this model is going to be considered as a continuation of this work.

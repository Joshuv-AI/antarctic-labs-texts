Floquet Condition for Quantum Adiabaticity
Jie Gu, X.-G. Zhang

Abstract
Quantum adiabaticity is characterized by the evolution of a quantum system that remains close to an instantaneous eigenstate of a time-varying Hamiltonian, precluding transitions between eigenstates. Employing Floquet formalism, we rigorously establish two sufficient conditions that ensure quantum adiabaticity in periodically driven systems, applicable for time evolution of arbitrary length. Distinct from traditional conditions, the Floquet-based conditions are tight for certain specific parameter regimes, dispense with supplementary constraints, and suggest the possibility of adiabaticity at high frequencies. We provide three illustrative examples that compare and contrast the Floquet conditions with traditional ones.



1
Introduction

Quantum adiabatic theorem guides most of our understanding of quantum dynamics near equilibrium
[
1
,
2
]
, underpinning a broad spectrum of theories and applications ranging from
Gell-Mann and Low theorem
[
3
]
to adiabatic quantum computation
[
4
]
.
Adiabaticity is considered possible when the dynamics is sufficiently slow, i.e., when a system is “quasi-static”. This understanding would preclude all periodically driven systems with fast periods.
Periodically driven quantum systems, also known as Floquet systems, are ubiquitous in applications and continue to attract attentions theoretically and experimentally (see
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
for reviews).
Given that the dynamics of Floquet systems can be effectively described by a time-independent
Floquet Hamiltonian, it is natural to ask whether quantum adiabatic theorem can also be generalized to Floquet systems.

The definition of quantum adiabaticity (QA) can be stated as follows
[
9
,
10
]
:
For
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
the state
|\Psi(t)\rangle
evolving from an eigenstate

|E_{n}(t_{0})\rangle
remains all the time close to this eigenstate in the sense that the the modulus of their overlap
|\langle E_{n}(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle|

remains close to unity.
Using static quantum systems as the reference point, most studies quantify adiabaticity using the time derivative
of the quantum state
d|E_{n}(t)\rangle/dt\equiv|\dot{E}_{n}(t)\rangle
.
Therefore,
the condition (
\hbar=1
)

\max_{m(\neq n)}\bigl|{\langle E_{m}(t)|\dot{E}_{n}(t)\rangle}\bigr|\ll\min_{m(\neq n)}\left|{E_{m}(t)-E_{n}(t)}\right|

(1)

was long believed to be sufficient for QA, where the maximum and minimum functions are defined over
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
for given
n
and all
m\neq n
.
However, Eq. (
1
) has been demonstrated to be neither sufficient nor necessary
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
10
]
.

Almost all the counterexamples constructed to demonstrate the insufficiency of the traditional condition pertain to periodically driven systems, where the “resonance” or “oscillation” is identified as the reason behind the breakdown of adiabaticity
[
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
9
,
17
]
.
While several resolutions have been proposed
[
18
,
19
,
9
,
20
]
, none have explicitly taken advantage of the inherent time-periodicity of these systems.
Although these are indeed sufficient conditions, they are overly restrictive and, with the exception of Ref.
[
20
]
, they preclude the possibility of adiabaticity in high-frequency driven systems.
Additionally, these conditions, derived for finite evolution times, do not address the fundamental question: For a given system with known energy levels, what type of time-periodic driving (e.g., microwave) will
never
induce excitation?
The “frequency condition”
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
, given by,

\omega\ll\min_{m\neq n}|E_{m}-E_{n}|,

(2)

where
\omega
is the angular frequency of driving, appears to address the question above.
This condition is intuitive since the Hamiltonian is generally considered to be varying slowly as long as the period is much longer than the system’s characteristic time scale, thus becoming a folk theorem without being rigorously proved.
However, as will be shown in an example we construct, the frequency condition is also neither sufficient nor necessary for QA.
Furthermore, it was suggested that the traditional condition should be supplemented by a “driving frequency far away from any quasienergy degeneracy” to guarantee adiabaticity
[
27
]
, but how to incorporate a quantitative measure of this “farness”
into Eq. (
1
) has not been proposed.

A fundamental distinction between generic and periodically-driven systems lies in the accumulation of approximation errors. While errors typically accumulate rapidly in generic systems, they may not do so in time-periodic systems. This characteristic enables the derivation of adiabatic conditions valid for evolution times of arbitrary length.
In this paper, we present two rigorous sufficient conditions for quantum adiabaticity (QA) by leveraging the time-periodicity within the Floquet formalism. These Floquet conditions are given by:

\max_{m\neq n}|{\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle}|\\
\ll\frac{\omega}{\pi\sqrt{2(N-1)}}\min_{m\neq n}\left|\sin\left[\frac{\pi(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})}{\omega}\right]\right|

(3)

and

\max_{m}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\sqrt{\langle\dot{E}_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}}\text{d}t\\
\ll\sqrt{2}\min_{n\neq m}\left|\sin\left[\frac{\pi(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})}{\omega}\right]\right|,

(4)

where
|E_{m}(t)\rangle
is the instantaneous energy eigenstate of the
N
-dimensional system,

\omega=2\pi/\mathcal{T}
is the angular frequency and
\epsilon_{i}
denotes the
Floquet quasienergy.
These equations effectively modify the traditional condition by introducing a quantitative measure of the “farness” of the driving frequency from quasienergy degeneracy
[
27
]
.
Our Floquet conditions are applicable to periodic systems across any frequency range, facilitating the prediction of high-frequency QA.
We demonstrate that these Floquet conditions are tighter than traditional conditions and each stands as a sufficient condition without the need for additional constraints.
Through several examples, we illustrate the utility of these conditions, showing that they address scenarios where the traditional condition fails.

2
Main results

Consider a Hamiltonian
H(t)
, which is time-dependent and acts on a Hilbert space of dimension
N
.
The state
|\Psi(t)\rangle
can be expanded in the instantaneous energy eigenbasis

\{|E_{n}(t)\rangle\}
of the Hamiltonian
H(t)
as

|\Psi(t)\rangle=\sum_{n}d_{n}(t)e^{i\theta_{n}(t)+i\gamma_{n}(t)}|E_{n}(t)\rangle,

(5)

where the dynamical and geometric phases are, respectively,

\displaystyle\theta_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}E_{n}(s)\text{d}s,

(6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}\langle E_{n}(s)|\dot{E}_{n}(s)\rangle\text{d}s.

The time derivative of the expansion coefficients are,

\dot{d}_{m}=-\sum_{n(\neq m)}d_{n}\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle e^{i(\theta_{n}-\theta_{m}+\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{m})},

(7)

where the argument
t
has been dropped to simplify the notation.
The absolute value of the time derivative is bounded by,

\left|\dot{d}_{m}\right|\leq\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|\sum_{n\neq m}|d_{n}|,

(8)

where
\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|
is the maximum value of all functions
|\langle E_{m}(t)|\dot{E}_{n}(t)\rangle|
with
m\neq n
on the interval
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
.
From

\sum_{n\neq m;n^{\prime}\neq m}(|d_{n}|-|d_{n^{\prime}}|)^{2}\geq 0.

(9)

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq m}|d_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(N-1)\sum_{n\neq m}|d_{n}|^{2}}

(10)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(N-1)(1-|d_{m}|^{2})},

where the equality is from the normalization of the wave function,
\sum_{n}|d_{n}(t)|^{2}=1
.
From Eq. (
8
) we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|d_{m}|^{2}}}\frac{\text{d}|d_{m}|}{\text{d}t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left|\dot{d}_{m}\right|}{\sqrt{1-|d_{m}|^{2}}}

(11)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{N-1}\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|,

Integrating both sides over time from
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+\tau
leads to

\displaystyle\arcsin|d_{m}(t_{0})|-

\displaystyle\arcsin|d_{m}(t_{0}+\tau)|

(12)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{N-1}\tau\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|.

If
d_{m}(t_{0})=1
, and if we assume

\sqrt{N-1}\tau\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|\leq\frac{\pi}{2},

(13)

which we will justify later, then Eq. (
12
) implies,

|d_{m}(t_{0}+\tau)|\geq\cos\left[\sqrt{N-1}\tau\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|\right],

(14)

or

\displaystyle 1-|d_{m}(t_{0}

\displaystyle+\tau)|

(15)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sin^{2}\left[\frac{\sqrt{N-1}}{2}\tau\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|\right].

This inequality will serve as the foundation for the subsequent derivation of the adiabatic conditions.

One can find a tighter inequality than Eq. (
15
) by starting from Eq. (
7
) again.
The absolute value of the time derivative is alternatively bounded by,

\displaystyle\left|\dot{d}_{m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n(\neq m)}|d_{n}||\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|

(16)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{n(\neq m)}|d_{n}|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{n(\neq m)}|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-|d_{m}|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{n(\neq m)}|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|^{2}},

where the second inequality arises from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
{\bf a}\cdot{\bf b}\leq|{\bf a}||{\bf b}|
for two
N-1
dimensional vectors, and for the last equality we used the normalization condition of the wave function,
\sum_{n}|d_{n}(t)|^{2}=1
.
The last factor can be simplified with,

\displaystyle\sum_{n(\neq m)}|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|^{2}-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}|\langle\dot{E}_{m}|E_{n}\rangle|^{2}-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\dot{E}_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}.

From Eq. (
16
) we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|d_{m}|^{2}}}\left|\frac{\text{d}|d_{m}|}{\text{d}t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left|\dot{d}_{m}\right|}{\sqrt{1-|d_{m}|^{2}}}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\langle\dot{E}_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}},

Integrating both sides over time from
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+\tau
, we obtain,

\left|\arcsin|d_{m}(t_{0})|-\arcsin|d_{m}(t_{0}+\tau)|\right|\leq\mathcal{I}_{m}(\tau),

(19)

where

\mathcal{I}_{m}(\tau)\equiv\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+\tau}\sqrt{\langle\dot{E}_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}}\text{d}t.

(20)

Clearly,
\mathcal{I}_{m}(t)
is a monotonically increasing function of time. If
d_{m}(t_{0})=1
, then,

|d_{m}(t_{0}+\tau)|\geq\cos\mathcal{I}_{m}(\tau),

(21)

or

1-|d_{m}(t_{0}+\tau)|\leq 2\sin^{2}\left[\frac{\mathcal{I}_{m}(\tau)}{2}\right],

(22)

where
\mathcal{I}_{m}
is defined in Eq. (
20
).

Based upon Eq. (
15
) and (
22
),
two sets of adiabatic conditions can be derived.

2.1
Adiabatic condition for generic systems

The right-hand side of Eq. (
15
) is a monotonically increasing function of
\tau
, so the adiabaticity is guaranteed as long as the right-hand side is close to zero.
Mathematically, the condition is,

\frac{\sqrt{N-1}}{2}\tau\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|\ll\frac{\pi}{4}.

(23)

This justifies the validity of Eq. (
13
) and also the small-angle approximation
|\sin x|\approx|x|
.
Thus, Eq. (
15
) offers a sufficient condition for adiabaticity,

\frac{N-1}{2}\tau^{2}\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|^{2}\ll 1.

(24)

Equivalently, it sets a bound on the time for adiabaticity:

\tau\ll\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{N-1}\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|}.

(25)

This condition is similar to condition (C) in Ref.
[
18
]
. But unlike the latter, Eq. (
25
) is a standalone sufficient condition that does not require Eq. (
1
).

Similarly, Eq. (
22
) offers a sufficient condition for adiabaticity,

\frac{\mathcal{I}_{m}(\tau)^{2}}{2}\ll 1.

(26)

Writing this out explicitly gives

\max_{m}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+\tau}\sqrt{\langle\dot{E}_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}}\text{d}t\ll\frac{\pi}{2}.

(27)

2.2
Adiabatic condition for Floquet systems

Now we consider periodically driven systems, whose Hamiltonian is time-periodic,
H(t)=H(t+\mathcal{T})
.
The instantaneous eigenstate

|E_{m}(t)\rangle
can be expanded in the basis of Floquet modes
|\phi_{i}(t)\rangle
,

|E_{m}(t)\rangle=\sum_{i}a_{i}(t)|\phi_{i}(t)\rangle.

(28)

where
a_{i}(t)
are also periodic.

Based on the Floquet theorem,
|\Psi(t)\rangle
with

|\Psi(t_{0})\rangle=|E_{m}(t_{0})\rangle
as the initial state can be expanded in the basis of Floquet states with coefficients
\{a_{i}(t_{0})\}
,

|\Psi(t)\rangle=\sum_{i}a_{i}(t_{0})|\phi_{i}(t)\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{i}(t-t_{0})}.

(29)

We define
c_{i}(t)=|a_{i}(t)|^{2}
, with
\sum_{i}c_{i}(t)=1
.
Then the fidelity (defined as the square of the overlap)
at
t
is

\displaystyle|d_{m}(t)|^{2}=

\displaystyle|\langle E_{m}(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}a_{i}(t)a_{i}^{*}(t_{0})a_{j}^{*}(t)a_{j}(t_{0})e^{i(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})(t-t_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{i,j\neq i}c_{i}(t)c_{j}(t_{0})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j\neq i}a_{i}(t)a_{i}^{*}(t_{0})a_{j}^{*}(t)a_{j}(t_{0})e^{i(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})(t-t_{0})}

This simplifies for
t=t_{0}+\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle|d_{m}

\displaystyle(t_{0}+\mathcal{T})|^{2}

(31)

\displaystyle=1-2\sum_{i,j\neq i}c_{i}(t_{0})c_{j}(t_{0})\sin^{2}\left[\frac{(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})\mathcal{T}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\biggl\{1-\sum_{i,j\neq i}c_{i}(t_{0})c_{j}(t_{0})\sin^{2}\left[\frac{(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})\mathcal{T}}{2}\right]\biggr\}^{2},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle|d_{m}

\displaystyle(t_{0}+\mathcal{T})|

(32)

\displaystyle\leq 1-2\sum_{i,j<i}c_{i}(t_{0})c_{j}(t_{0})\sin^{2}\left[\frac{(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})\mathcal{T}}{2}\right]

Making use of Eqs. (
15
) for
\tau=\mathcal{T}
and (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\min\left\{\sin^{2}\left[\frac{(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})\mathcal{T}}{2}\right]\right\}\sum_{i,j<i}c_{i}(t_{0})c_{j}(t_{0})

(33)

\displaystyle\leq\sin^{2}\left[\frac{\sqrt{N-1}}{2}\mathcal{T}\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|\right],

where the minimum function is defined over all states (
i\neq j
).
Thus,

\sum_{i,j<i}c_{i}(t_{0})c_{j}(t_{0})\leq\delta,

(34)

where

\delta\equiv\frac{\sin^{2}\big[\sqrt{N-1}\mathcal{T}\max|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|/2\big]}{\min\{\sin^{2}[(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})\mathcal{T}/2]\}}.

(35)

Note that
\delta
is time-independent, and since the initial time
t_{0}
is arbitrary, Eq. (
34
) must be a universal bound for
\{c_{i}(t)\}
at an arbitrary instant
t
.
For any
i
, we have

c_{i}(t)(1-c_{i}(t))=c_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}c_{j}\leq\sum_{i,j<i}c_{i}c_{j}\leq\delta.

(36)

Because
\sum c_{i}(t)=1
, if
\delta\ll 1
(a constraint we will impose), at any instant there must be a
c_{i}(t)
that is close to
1
. Due to the continuity of function
c_{i}(t)
, without loss of generality, we denote
the largest coefficient by
c_{1}(t)
, so Eq. (
36
) implies
c_{1}\geq 1-\delta
and
\sum_{i>1}c_{i}\leq\delta
.

For the second and third terms in Eq. (
30
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\neq i}c_{i}(t)c_{j}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{i}c_{i}(t)c_{i}(t_{0})

(37)

\displaystyle\leq 1-c_{1}(t)c_{1}(t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\neq i}a_{i}(t)

\displaystyle a_{i}^{*}(t_{0})a_{j}^{*}(t)a_{j}(t_{0})e^{i(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})(t-t_{0})}

(38)

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{i,j\neq i}\sqrt{c_{i}(t)c_{j}(t)c_{i}(t_{0})c_{j}(t_{0})}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\neq i}\left[c_{i}(t)c_{j}(t)+c_{i}(t_{0})c_{j}(t_{0})\right]

\displaystyle\geq-2\delta,

where the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality has been used.

Combining these two terms into Eq. (
30
),
the lower bound on the modulus of overlap at
t
is obtained as,

|d_{m}(t)|\geq\sqrt{1-4\delta}=1-2\delta+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}).

(39)

This is an exact result.
It yields a concise sufficient condition for adiabaticity,

2\delta\ll 1
, or explicitly,

\displaystyle 2\sin^{2}\left[\frac{\pi\sqrt{N-1}}{\omega}\max_{m\neq n}|{\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle}|\right]

(40)

\displaystyle\ll\min_{m\neq n}\left|\sin^{2}\left[\frac{\pi(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})}{\omega}\right]\right|,

Eq. (
40
) also implies

\left|\sin\left[\frac{\pi\sqrt{N-1}}{\omega}\max_{m\neq n}|{\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle}|\right]\right|\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},

(41)

which allows us to apply the small-angle approximation and obtain the Floquet condition, Eq. (
3
).
Correspondingly, by making use of Eqs. (
22
) for
\tau=\mathcal{T}
and (
32
) and following the same reasoning, one can obtain Eq. (
4
).

2.3
Remarks

The derivation of the Floquet conditions is rigorous: it does not require perturbation expansion or other uncontrollable approximations.
An upper bound of
2\delta
is imposed by Eq. (
39
)
on the deviation from the instantaneous eigenstate at all
time.
Therefore, satisfying the Floquet condition guarantees
QA
indefinitely
.

Now we retype the two fundamental Floquet conditions herein for ease of reference, juxtaposing them for comparative analysis:

\max_{m\neq n}|{\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle}|\\
\ll\frac{\omega}{\pi\sqrt{2(N-1)}}\min_{m\neq n}\left|\sin\left[\frac{\pi(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})}{\omega}\right]\right|

(42)

and

\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\sqrt{\langle\dot{E}_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle-|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{m}\rangle|^{2}}\text{d}t\\
\ll\sqrt{2}\min_{n\neq m}\left|\sin\left[\frac{\pi(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})}{\omega}\right]\right|.

(43)

The right-hand side of both conditions features a term

\min_{n\neq m}\left|\sin\left[\frac{\pi(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})}{\omega}\right]\right|,

which quantitatively delineates the criterion for the driving frequency to be sufficiently distant from any quasienergy degeneracy for ensuring quantum adiabaticity, previously introduced in a qualitative manner in Ref.
[
27
]
.

The former condition is more concise and bears a strong resemblance to the traditional condition, Eq. (
1
). They exhibit a common left-hand side, characterized by
\max_{m\neq n}\left|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle\right|
.
The distinction lies in the right-hand side, where the energy level spacing is fundamentally supplanted by the spacing of Floquet quasienergies.
A scaling issue emerges within this condition, which intensifies with an increasing
N
and ultimately becomes inapplicable as
N
approaches infinity.
To our knowledge, this issue has been a long standing problem without a satisfactory resolution and is a commonality among all previously established criteria for quantum adiabaticity, as exemplified by Eq. (16) in Ref.
[
18
]
, Eq. (25) in Ref.
[
16
]
, Eq. (19) in Ref.
[
19
]
, and Eq. (16) in Ref.
[
20
]
.

The aforementioned scaling issue is effectively addressed by the alternative condition presented in Eq. (
4
).
The distinction arises from the inequality regarding
|\dot{d}_{m}|
[c.f. Eqs. (
8
) and (
16
)].
This latter condition, which forgoes the maximization, is generally tighter and, crucially, allows the expression
\sum_{n(\neq m)}|\langle E_{m}|\dot{E}_{n}\rangle|^{2}
to be formulated as a functional of a single eigenenergy
E_{m}(t)
, leveraging the orthonormality of the energy eigenbasis.
Furthermore, despite its more intricate appearance, the latter condition does not necessitate additional information beyond what is required by the former.
For the specific case of
N=2
, it is evident that the two conditions are identical, rendering them indistinguishable for this scenario.

3
Examples

Let us demonstrate these adiabatic conditions using three illustrative examples and establish connections with previous findings in the literature.

3.1
Schwinger-Rabi model

Consider a two-level system driven by an oscillatory force
[
28
,
29
]
,

H(t)=\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta e^{-i\omega t}\\
\sin\theta e^{i\omega t}&-\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},

(44)

which is exactly solvable and extensively studied in the
literature on adiabatic conditions

[
12
,
9
,
30
]
.
Here,
\omega_{0},\theta
and
\omega
are constants, and

\theta\in[0,\pi)
.
The instantaneous energy eigenvalues and eigenstates are
E_{0,1}=\mp\omega_{0}/2
and

|E_{0}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\omega t/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
-e^{i\omega t/2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,|E_{1}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\omega t/2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\\
e^{i\omega t/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\end{pmatrix},

(45)

respectively.
Suppose the system is in the ground state at
t=0
, then the overlap between the state and the ground state is given by

|d_{0}(t)|=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\omega\sin\theta}{\Omega}\sin\frac{\Omega t}{2}\right)^{2}}

(46)

where

\Omega=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}+\omega^{2}-2\omega\omega_{0}\cos\theta}
.

For arbitrarily long evolution time, the sufficient and necessary condition
for QA for this example is
1-\min|d_{0}|\ll 1
, i.e.,

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\omega\sin\theta}{\Omega}\right)^{2}\ll 1.

(47)

To satisfy Eq. (
47
), there are three cases:

(1)

\sin\theta\ll 1,\theta>\pi/2
, and
\omega
can be arbitrary;

(2)

\omega\ll\omega_{0}
, and
\theta
can be arbitrary,

(3)

\sin\theta\ll 1,\theta<\pi/2
, and
\omega
need to be off-resonance.

The traditional condition, as expressed in Eq. (1), can be written as:

\frac{1}{2}\omega\sin\theta\ll\omega_{0}
.
However, it is not hard to construct two counterexamples that demonstrate its limitations:

Counterexample 1: when
\sin\theta\ll 1,\theta<\pi/2
, even if

\omega=\omega_{0}
satisfies the traditional condition, it does not satisfy Eq. (
47
).
Thus, the traditional condition is not sufficient.

Counterexample 2:
when
\sin\theta\ll 1,\theta>\pi/2
and
\omega\gg\omega_{0}
, it satisfies
Eq. (
47
) but not the traditional condition, so the traditional condition is not necessary.

Now let us verify the Floquet condition. The Floquet quasienergies for this system are

\epsilon_{0,1}=(\omega\pm\Omega)/2
,
so the Floquet condition Eq. (
3
) gives

\frac{1}{2}\omega\sin\theta\ll\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\sin\Big(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\omega}\Big).

(48)

Applying the inequality
|\sin x|\leq|x|
yields Eq. (
47
) immediately. This
verifies that Eq. (
3
) is a sufficient condition for QA.
Since the Floquet condition implies
|\sin\theta|\ll 1
, it is a subset of Cases (1) and (3).

3.3
Bloch-Siegert model

We construct a model in which the frequency condition is not sufficient. It is inspired by the well-known two-level system
[
34
]
, which was also considered in Ref.
[
16
]
. The Hamiltonian is given by

H(t)=-\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}\sigma_{z}-\left[V\cos\omega t+V^{\prime}\cos(\frac{\omega t}{\mathcal{N}})\right]\sigma_{x},

(53)

where
V^{\prime}\ll V\ll\omega_{0}
, and
\cal N
is an integer. The angular frequency of the system is
\omega/\mathcal{N}
. Below we consider the regime of near resonance,
\omega\approx\omega_{0}
, and zeroth order of
V^{\prime}/V
, i.e., the term proportional to
V^{\prime}
is neglected.

Near resonance, rotating-wave approximation can be invoked, and we
obtain the probability

|d_{1}(t)|^{2}=\left(\frac{V}{\Omega^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Omega^{\prime}t}{2}\right),

(54)

where
\Omega^{\prime}=\sqrt{(\Delta\omega)^{2}+V^{2}},\Delta\omega=\omega-\omega_{0}
.
It implies that the sufficient condition for QA is
V\ll|\Delta\omega|
, and also the well-known result that the breakdown of adiabaticity occurs near resonance. Since
\mathcal{N}
can always be tuned such that the frequency condition is satisfied, the sufficiency of the frequency condition is invalidated. This result is not surprising, because even though the driving frequency
\omega/\mathcal{N}
of the system is low, the Hamiltonian actually explicitly contains a high frequency
\omega
and does not vary slowly at all. Therefore,
satisfying the frequency condition alone is not sufficient.

The Floquet condition, using
the instantaneous eigenenergies

E_{0,1}=\mp\Omega/2
, eigenstates
|E_{0,1}\rangle=\left(\sqrt{(\Omega\pm\omega_{0})/2\Omega},\pm\sqrt{(\Omega\mp\omega_{0})/2\Omega}\right)^{T}
,
with
\Omega=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}+4V^{2}\sin^{2}\omega t}
,
and the quasienergies
[
5
]

\epsilon_{\pm}=\pm(\omega+\Omega_{R})/2
,
with
\Omega_{R}=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}+4V^{2}}
,
leads to,

\frac{\omega V}{\Omega_{R}}\ll\frac{\omega/\mathcal{N}}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sin\Big(\frac{\pi\Omega_{R}}{\omega/\mathcal{N}}\Big).

(55)

Because
\text{R.H.S.}\leq\left|\Omega_{R}-\omega\right|/\sqrt{2}
, the inequality above implies
V\ll|\Delta\omega|
and QA.

4
Discussion and conclusion

The term “adiabaticity” has traditionally been equated with “slow” or “low-frequency” processes. Contrary to this notion, our examples demonstrate that the definition of quantum adiabaticity (QA), based solely on wave function overlap
[
13
,
10
,
35
,
36
]
, permits adiabatic evolution even at high frequencies.
Moreover,
since any aperiodic Hamiltonian over a finite-time can be extended to be time-periodic, the Floquet approach can be generalized to a wide array of rapidly varying systems.
It is noteworthy that adiabatic conditions in thermodynamics are not confined to slow processes; even those occurring on timescales shorter than relaxation can be deemed adiabatic. The revelation of fast yet adiabatic processes holds the potential to substantially enhance the efficiency of quantum heat engines by minimizing cycle durations.
In this context, we anticipate that our formulation of the Floquet condition for quantum dynamics will also illuminate the investigation of adiabaticity in stochastic thermodynamics
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
and quantum thermodynamics
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
, where the quantum adiabatic approximation is frequently invoked
[
43
]
.

One should note the difference between our approach and extensions of adiabaticity to quasi-periodically driven systems to describe processes such as rapid adiabatic passage and stimulated Raman adiabatic passage
[
44
]
. In such cases, a pulsed laser is used to transfer occupation from one quantum level to another. Theoretical descriptions of such processes utilize a basis rotation to transform the time-dependent Hamiltonian into an effective Hamiltonian in which the perturbation is slowly varying in time. Then an adiabatic condition is applied. The original system is not adiabatic, otherwise there cannot be any change in populations. The adiabatic description is enabled by the basis rotation.
A similar basis rotation may be applied to analyse clock transition magnetic molecular qubits operating under a microwave field
[
45
]
.

While this study primarily concentrates on adiabaticity, achieved by deliberately avoiding Floquet resonances, the converse condition of nonadiabacity holds even wider implications. Nonadiabatic effects, encompassing state transitions
[
46
,
47
,
48
]
, out-of-equilibrium phase transitions
[
49
]
, and enhanced precision in quantum sensing
[
50
]
induced by Floquet resonances, find diverse and significant applications.
Reversing the Floquet conditions establishes quantitative necessary criteria for realizing these nonadiabatic phenomena.

In conclusion, the Floquet conditions, Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), offer sufficient conditions for quantum adiabaticity that remain valid throughout arbitrarily long evolution times without constraining the system to be slowly varying. The observed tightness of the Floquet condition within specific parameter regimes, as depicted in
1
and
2
, is an encouraging indication that further exploration of the Floquet approach holds great promise.

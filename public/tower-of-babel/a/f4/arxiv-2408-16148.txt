Pan-Xu conjecture and reduction formulas for polylogarithms
Marian Genčev

Abstract
The objective of the paper is the study of Mneimneh-like sums with a parametric variant of the multiple harmonic-star values. We generalize and resolve the Pan-Xu conjecture on generalized Mneimneh-like sums and present their transformation. As an application, we deduce new reduction formulas for specific multiple polylogarithms enabling lowering their depth, and provide additional findings on arithmetic means of multiple harmonic-star values, resulting in new representations of arbitrary multiple zeta-star values.



∎

Introduction

*
In 2023, Mneimneh
mneimneh
published a paper devoted to applications of specific binomial-harmonic sums, and proved the simply-looking identity

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot p^{k}\cdot(1-p)^{n-k}\cdot H_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1-(1-p)^{k}}{k},

(1)

with
0\leq p\leq 1
and
H_{k}:=1+1/2+\cdots+1/k
denoting the harmonic number. Short after Mneimneh’s paper
mneimneh
appeared, Campbell
campbell
published two additional proofs of (
1
); first, by employing an integral representation of
H_{k}
, and second, by a modification of the Wilf-Zeilberger method. Even if Campbell mentioned that further generalizations of (
1
) are possible, especially with higher powers of harmonic numbers
H_{k}^{m}
, only a few generalizations of (
1
) has been investigated recently. One of the recent modifications of (
1
) was examined by Komatsu and Wang
komatsu
who focused on the case with hyperharmonic numbers.

Independent of the analyses in
campbell
;
komatsu
;
mneimneh
, we proposed in
gencev.dm
another generalization of Mneimneh’s binomial sums in the form

M_{n}^{(s)}(a,p):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot p^{k}\cdot(1-p)^{n-k}\cdot H_{k}^{(s)}(a),

(2)

where
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
a,p\in\mathbb{R}
, and
H_{k}^{(s)}(a):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a^{j}/j^{s}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, denotes the generalized harmonic number of order
s
. As a result, we deduced the transformation identity, see
(
gencev.dm
, Theorem 2.1 and Eq. (6))
,

M_{n}^{(s)}(a,p)=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s}\geq 1}\frac{(1-p)^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{s}}\cdot\left(\left(1+\frac{ap}{1-p}\right)^{n_{s}}-1\right)

(3)

with the right-hand side in the form of partial sums of specific nested series. The corresponding infinite series are called
multiple polylogarithms
and defined by

\Li^{\star}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{d}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}):=\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{x_{i}^{n_{i}}}{n_{i}^{s_{i}}},\qquad s_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\quad x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.

(4)

Remark 1

We note that the one-dimensional polylogarithmic function, corresponding to
d=1
in (
4
), is called the polylogarithm function. In this simple case, the star symbol is usually dropped, i.e.
\Li_{s}(x)=\Li^{\star}_{s}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x^{n}/n^{s}
. Furthermore, throughout this paper, we often employ the polylogarithmic values with the arguments
x_{i}=1
,
i=1,\dots,d
, whose standardized notation is
\zeta^{\star}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
, i.e.

\Li^{\star}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{d}}(1,\dots,1)=:\zeta^{\star}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})

with
s_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
s_{1}>1
, to ensure the convergence. The values
\zeta^{\star}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
are called multiple zeta-star values and are intensively studied due to their interesting mathematical properties, see Zhao
zhao
and Eie
eie.book
for an overview, and applications in physics, see Smirnov
smirnov
and Weinzierl
weinzierl
. The definition of these sums goes back to Hoffman
hoffman.pjm
and Zagier
zagier
who invented these values independently around 1992.

One of our applications deduced in
gencev.dm
led to the following new relation.

Theorem 1.1
(
(
gencev.dm
, Theorem 2.2)
)

Assume that
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\geq 2
, and
a,p\in\mathbb{R}
with
p\neq 1
. Then

\Li_{s}(a)=\Li^{\star}_{\{1\}_{s}}\bigl(1-p,\{1\}_{s-2},1+\tfrac{ap}{1-p}\bigr)-\Li^{\star}_{\{1\}_{s}}\bigl(1-p,\{1\}_{s-1}\bigr),

(5)

where
|a|\leq\min\bigl(1,2/p-1\bigr)
, and
\{1\}_{r}
stands for
1
repeated
r
times.

The identity in (
5
), coming from the limiting case of (
3
) for
n\to\infty
, is appealing from two aspects. First, by temporarily suppressing the discrete parameter
s
, its right-hand side involves two real variables
a,p
, whereas its left-hand side involves only one of them, the
a
. Second, the computational complexity of both sides is essentially different. While the left-hand side of (
5
) is represented by a one-dimensional polylogarithm, the right-hand side is in the form of a difference of
s
-dimensional polylogarithms. As a by-product, we also obtained in
(
gencev.dm
, Corollary 2.3)
the reductions of the multiple polylogarithms on the right-hand side of (
5
) to one-dimensional polylogarithms in the form

\displaystyle\Li^{\star}_{\{1\}_{s}}\bigl(1-p,\{1\}_{s-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\Li_{s}\bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\bigr),

\displaystyle\Li^{\star}_{\{1\}_{s}}\bigl(1-p,\{1\}_{s-2},1+\tfrac{ap}{1-p}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Li_{s}(a)-\Li_{s}\bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\bigr),

where
a,p\in\mathbb{R}
with
|a|\leq 1
,
1/2\leq p<1
, and
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s\geq 2
. Hence, the last two relations allow us to calculate high-precision approximations of the multiple polylogarithms on the left-hand sides with the help of their one-dimensional ‘equivalents’.

1.1
Conjecture of Pan and Xu

The subject of Mneimneh’s sums and their various modifications have dramatically developed in recent times due to the essential popularity of harmonic sums. Just recently, Pan and Xu
pan.xu
stated a conjecture related to specific Mneimneh-like sums, where the standard harmonic numbers
H_{k}
are replaced by much more general objects
\zeta_{k}^{\star}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k})
defined by

\zeta_{k}^{\star}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d}):=\sum_{k\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}

and called
multiple harmonic-star values
. Here,
s_{i}
are assumed to be positive integers.

Conjecture (Pan, Xu
pan.xu
)

Assume that
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\neq-y
. Furthermore, let
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{r+1})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r+1}
,
\bm{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
, and set

|\bm{u}|_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{j}u_{i},\qquad\qquad|\bm{m}|_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{j}m_{i}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}\cdot x^{k}y^{n-k}\cdot\zeta^{\star}_{k}\left(\{1\}_{u_{1}},m_{1}+2,\dots,\{1\}_{u_{r}},m_{r}+2,\{1\}_{u_{r+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=(x+y)^{n}\cdot\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{u}|_{r+1}+|\bm{m}|_{r}+r-1}\geq 1}\frac{\bigl(\frac{y}{x+y}\bigr)^{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\bigl(n_{|\bm{u}|_{j}+|\bm{m}|_{j-1}+j-1}-n_{|\bm{u}|_{j}+|\bm{m}|_{j}+j}\bigr)}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{u}|_{r+1}+|\bm{m}|_{r}+r-1}}\times

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\times\left(1-\left(\frac{y}{x+y}\right)^{n_{|\bm{u}|_{r+1}+|\bm{m}|_{r}+r-1}}\right).

Although complex, the above formula can be simplified if divided by
(x+y)^{n}
. After that, one can introduce the variable
p:=x/(x+y)
, and consequently, the left-hand side of the conjectured formula becomes
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot p^{k}\cdot(1-p)^{n-k}\cdot\zeta^{\star}_{k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
for suitable arguments
s_{i}
according to the left-hand side of the conjectured formula. Similarly as in the formula (
3
), we observe that the above conjecture is related to partial sums of multiple polylogarithms and, therefore, of a hypothetical significance.

1.2
Objectives and paper outline

The base for the research conducted in this work consists of sums that are slightly more general than those introduced by Pan and Xu. In fact, we focus on the properties of the sums

M_{n}^{(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})}(a,p):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot p^{k}\cdot(1-p)^{n-k}\cdot\zeta_{k}^{\star}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d};a),

(6)

where
s_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
a,p\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\zeta_{k}^{\star}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d};a):=\sum_{k\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\frac{a^{n_{d}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}

denotes the
generalized multiple harmonic-star value
.

In the following paragraphs, we outline three particular goals of this paper.

Goal 1 – Transformation of Mneimneh-like sums

Our first goal is to establish a generalized variant of identity (
3
) relating to the ‘higher’ Mneimneh-like sums
M_{n}^{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})}(a,p)
. Our corresponding result presented in Theorem
2.1
encompasses the conjecture of Pan and Xu and extends its statement due to the introduced parameter
a
. Even if stating Theorem
2.1
can be perceived as a pure mathematical vagary consisting of writing one sum to a new one (even more complicated, see the right-hand side of (
3
) as well as the conjecture of Pan and Xu), our main impetus for studying these sums lies in finding a generalization of Theorem
1.1
providing a link to multiple polylogarithms. Therefore, Theorem
2.1
is one of our main results.

Goal 2 – Polylogarithm relations

As just anticipated, our second goal is to provide applications of Theorem
2.1
to multiple polylogarithms. We show in Sect.
3
that the transformed form of
M_{n}^{(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})}(a,p)
, see (
7
), is still suitable for studying properties of specific multiple polylogarithms whose understanding is currently considered as highly incomplete. We present the complex Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
aiming to show that the value
\Li_{\bm{s}}^{\star}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a)
, where
\|\bm{s}\|:=\dim\bm{s}
, can be written as a difference of another multiple polylogarithms containing one more additional parameter
p
, not involved in
\Li_{\bm{s}}^{\star}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a)
. To present a very simple instance, Example
3
(based on Theorem
3.1
with
a=1
) implies the somewhat strange formula

\Li^{\star}_{1,1,1,1}\bigl(1-p,\tfrac{1}{1-p},1-p,\tfrac{1}{1-p}\bigr)-\Li^{\star}_{1,1,1,1}\bigl(1-p,\tfrac{1}{1-p},1-p,1\bigr)=\tfrac{7}{360}\,\pi^{4},

where
p\in(0,1)
is arbitrary. Nevertheless, our investigations of Mneimneh’s generalized sums allow us to uncover new general properties of multiple polylogarithms. For example, we present the reduction formula, see Corollary
3
,

\Li^{\star}_{\{1\}_{|\bm{s}|}}\left(\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{d-1}\bigl\{1-p,\{1\}_{m_{i}},\frac{1}{1-p},\{1\}_{u_{i}}\bigr\},1-p,\{1\}_{m_{d}+1}\right)=-\Li^{\star}_{\bm{s}}\left(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},1-\tfrac{1}{p}\right),

where
\sqcup
stands for concatenation of strings,
|\bm{s}|=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}
denotes the sum of all components of
\bm{s}
, and
\|\bm{s}\|=\dim\bm{s}
. We refer readers to Sect.
3
for more general scenarios and precise assumptions.

Goal 3 – Arithmetic means of multiple harmonic numbers

The third goal extends the described theory in a different sense. Our general result presented in Theorem
4.1
provides a transformation of the arithmetic mean of the elements
\zeta_{k}^{\star}(\bm{s};a)
,
k=0,1,\dots,n
. The corollaries of Theorem
4.1
include new representations of arbitrary zeta-star values
\zeta^{\star}(\bm{s})
or, more generally, of arbitrary convergent values
\Li_{\bm{s}}^{\star}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a)
, see Corollaries
6
and
7
.

2
Transformation of Mneimneh-like sums

2.1
Statement and examples

In this section, we state the main transformation theorem allowing us to rewrite the Mneimneh-like sum
M_{n}^{(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})}(a,p)
to a partial sum of a specific multiple polylogarithm. The following statement forms the technical base for our applications, which are described in the rest of this paper.

Theorem 2.1

Assume that
\bm{s}:=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
a,p\in\mathbb{R}
, and
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\frac{\prod_{r=1}^{d}(1-p)^{n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}-n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}\cdot\Bigl[(1-p+ap)^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}-(1-p)^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}\Bigr],

(7)

where
|\bm{s}|_{r}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}
and
|\bm{s}|:=|\bm{s}|_{d}
.

Before approaching the proof of Theorem
2.1
, which is postponed to Sect.
2.2
, we present several more straightforward consequences to familiarize the statement.

Example 1

We have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{i^{2}}}{j^{3}}=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{5}\geq 1}\frac{2^{n-n_{1}+n_{3}-n_{4}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{5}}.

Proof
(of Example
1
)

The sum on the left-hand side in the example agrees with
M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)
with
\bm{s}=(3,2)
,
a=-1
, and
p=1/2
. Therefore,
|\bm{s}|_{1}=s_{1}=3
and
|\bm{s}|_{2}=s_{1}+s_{2}=5
. Consequently,
\prod_{r=1}^{2}(1-p)^{n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}-n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}}=(1-p)^{n_{1}-n_{3}}\cdot(1-p)^{n_{4}-n_{5}}
. By setting all the given parameters into (
7
), we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{k}\cdot\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-k}\cdot\zeta^{\star}_{k}(3,2;-1)=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{5}\geq 1}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n_{1}-n_{3}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n_{4}-n_{5}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{5}}\cdot\left[0-\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n_{5}}\right].

Simplifying the last relation concludes the proof.∎

Example 2

Assume that
a,p\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\neq 1
, and
d,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle M_{n}^{(\{m\}_{d})}(a,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{md}\geq 1}\frac{\prod_{r=1}^{d}(1-p)^{n_{mr-m+1}-n_{mr}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{md}}\cdot\bigl[(1-p+ap)^{n_{md}}-(1-p)^{n_{md}}\bigr],

where
\{m\}_{d}
denotes the argument
m
repeated
d
times.

Proof
(of Example
2
)

Set
\bm{s}=(\{m\}_{d})
in Theorem
2.1
. Then
|\bm{s}|=md
and
|\bm{s}|_{r}=mr
for every
r=0,\dots,d
. Therefore,
\prod_{r=1}^{d}(1-p)^{n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}-n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}}=\prod_{r=1}^{d}(1-p)^{n_{mr-m+1}-n_{mr}}
. Substituting this form into (
7
) concludes the proof.
∎

The formula in Example
2
simplifies with
m=1
. In this particular instance, we deduce

M_{n}^{(\{1\}_{d})}(a,p)=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\frac{(1-p+ap)^{n_{d}}-(1-p)^{n_{d}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{d}}.

(8)

We also point out that by setting
a=d=1
in (
8
), the identity reduces to Mneimneh’s original result (
1
). Additionally, by dividing (
8
) by
(1-p)^{n}
with
p\to\infty
, we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot(-1)^{k}\cdot\zeta^{\star}_{k}(\{1\}_{d};a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1\\[1.63885pt]
n_{d}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{(1-a)^{n_{d}}-1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{d}}=\frac{(1-a)^{n}-1}{n^{d}}.

(9)

For example, the last identity with
a=2
implies the somewhat curious formula

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot(-1)^{k-1}\cdot\zeta^{\star}_{k}(\{1\}_{d};2)=\begin{cases}0&\text{$n$ even},\\
\frac{2}{n^{d}}&\text{$n$ odd},\end{cases}

(10)

which enables us to arrive at the following corollary.

Corollary 1

For every
d,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k-1}\cdot\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{d}}=\frac{\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{1\}_{d};2)}{2}.

Proof

Recall the binomial inverse relation, i.e. if
a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot(-1)^{k}\cdot b_{k}
, then
b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot(-1)^{k}\cdot a_{k}
, see e.g. Riordan
(
riordan
, p. 43)
. Applying this principle to (
10
) with
b_{k}=\zeta^{\star}_{k}(\{1\}_{d};2)
and
a_{n}
the right-hand side of (
10
), we immediately deduce the sought identity.
∎

The above relation is connected with the so-called Dilcher’s formula (see Dilcher
(
dilcher
, p. 93)
or Nica
(
nica
, Eq. (3))
) stating that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot(-1)^{k-1}/k^{d}=\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{d}).

Corollary
1
provides its odd counterpart. The even counterpart with
\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}/(2k)^{d}
can then easily be obtained as a complement. Furthermore, setting
a=1
in (
9
) and applying the principle of the binomial inversion lead to Dilcher’s identity directly.

We finish our initial considerations by the following statement, enabling us to exclude uninteresting cases from the rest of the paper.

Lemma 1

For
p=0
and
p=1
, the summation transformation (
7
) is trivial.

Proof

For
p=0
, the statement immediately follows from the definition of
M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)
and (
7
). For
p=1
, the situation requires more attention. First of all, the definition of
M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)
in (

) implies that
M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,1)=\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\bm{s};a)
. Next, we concentrate on the right-hand side of (

\bm{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
. Since

(1-p)^{n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}-n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}}\Bigr|_{p=1}=\begin{cases}0&\text{for $n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}>n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}$},\\[5.0pt]
1&\text{for $n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}=n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}$},\end{cases}

this and (

M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,1)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1\\[1.63885pt]
n_{1}=n_{|\bm{s}|_{1}}\\[1.63885pt]
n_{|\bm{s}|_{1}+1}=n_{|\bm{s}|_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
n_{|\bm{s}|_{d-1}+1}=n_{|\bm{s}|}\end{subarray}}\frac{a^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}=\sum_{n\geq j_{1}\geq\cdots\geq j_{d}\geq 1}\frac{a^{j_{d}}}{j_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots j_{d}^{s_{d}}}=\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\bm{s};a),

where we set
j_{r}:=n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}=\cdots=n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}
,
r=1,\dots,d
.
∎

2.2
Proof of Theorem
2.1

We precede the proof of Theorem
2.1
by the following auxiliary lemma, which will be applied several times within this section.

Lemma 2

Assume that
a,x\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
are arbitrary. Then

\int_{0}^{a}\frac{(1+Ax)^{n}-1}{A}\,\mathrm{d}A=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(1+ax)^{j}-1}{j}.

(11)

Proof

The statement is clear for
x=0
. Therefore, we further assume that
x\neq 0
. Under this condition, we can write

\int_{0}^{a}\frac{(1+Ax)^{n}-1}{A}\,\mathrm{d}A=x\cdot\int_{0}^{a}\frac{(1+Ax)^{n}-1}{(1+Ax)-1}\,\mathrm{d}A=x\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{a}(1+Ax)^{j-1}\,\mathrm{d}A.

Finally, performing the integration concludes the proof.∎

Based on Lemma
2
, we observe that the similar identity

\int_{1-a}^{1}\frac{(1+Ax)^{n}-1}{A}\,\mathrm{d}A=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(1+x)^{j}-(1+x-ax)^{j}}{j}

(12)

holds under identical assumptions as in Lemma
2
. Of course, for the proof of (

), we apply (

) twice. First, with
a=1
, and second, with
1-a
instead of

. This and
\int_{1-a}^{1}=\int_{0}^{1}-\int_{0}^{1-a}
then imply (

2.1
.

\displaystyle\int_{1-A}^{1}

\displaystyle\Bigl(M_{n}^{(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})}(1,p)-M_{n}^{(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})}(1-a,p)\Bigr)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}a}{a}

(13)

\bm{s}=\left(\sqcup_{i=1}^{d}\{m_{i}+2,\{1\}_{u_{i}}\}\right)
. If
p\neq 1
and
|a|\leq\min(1,2/p-1)
then

\displaystyle\Li^{\star}_{\bm{s}}\left(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a\right)

\displaystyle=\Li^{\star}_{\{1\}_{|\bm{s}|}}\left(\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{d}\bigl\{1-p,\{1\}_{m_{i}},\frac{1}{1-p},\{1\}_{u_{i}-\delta_{i,d}}\bigr\},1-p+ap\right)

\displaystyle{}\,-\Li^{\star}_{\{1\}_{|\bm{s}|}}\left(\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{d-1}\bigl\{1-p,\{1\}_{m_{i}},\frac{1}{1-p},\{1\}_{u_{i}-\delta_{i,d}}\bigr\},1-p\right),

(23)

where
\delta_{i,j}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

Remark 4

We omit the proof of Theorem

4
Arithmetic means of multiple harmonic-star numbers

With the help of the findings established in the previous sections, we develop additional results concerning arithmetic means of the generalized multiple harmonic-star numbers
\zeta^{\star}_{k}(\bm{s};a)
,
k=0,\dots,n
, including the limiting case
n\to\infty
. Our main result in this section is the following general statement, which forms the basis for further applications.

Theorem 4.1

Assume that
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\bm{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and define

Q(\bm{s}):=\sum_{r=1}^{d}\left(n_{|\bm{s}|_{r-1}+1}-n_{|\bm{s}|_{r}}\right).

(24)

Then

\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta^{\star}_{k}(\bm{s};a)=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|+1}\geq 1}\frac{\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{\binom{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}\cdot\frac{a^{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}.

(25)

Even if the theorem is rather complex, we show that its particular cases are interesting as the limiting forms of (
25
) for
n\to\infty
imply new identities. Before we approach these applications, we first prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof

The idea of the proof is straightforward; we perform the integration
\int_{0}^{1}M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)\,\mathrm{d}p
in two different ways. The first integration consists in considering the defining expression of the sum
M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)
in (
6
), the second one in considering the transformed variant from Theorem
2.1
. Let us realize these steps below.

Integrating the defining expression given in (
6
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot\zeta_{k}^{\star}(\bm{s};a)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}p^{k}\cdot(1-p)^{n-k}\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta_{k}^{\star}(\bm{s};a),

(26)

where we used the beta-type integral evaluation
\int_{0}^{1}p^{k}\cdot(1-p)^{n-k}\,\mathrm{d}p=1/\bigl((n+1)\cdot\binom{n}{k}\bigr)
.

In the second step, we employ the formula from Theorem
2.1
. Considering the definition of the
Q(\bm{s})
in (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle M_{n}^{(\bm{s})}(a,p)\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\frac{(1-p)^{Q(\bm{s})}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}\cdot\Bigl[(1-p+ap)^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}-(1-p)^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}\Bigr]\mathrm{d}p

(27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-p)^{Q(\bm{s})}\cdot(1-p+ap)^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}-(1-p)^{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{j}\cdot(1-p)^{Q(\bm{s})+j}\cdot(ap)^{n_{|\bm{s}|}-j}-(1-p)^{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{j}\cdot a^{n_{|\bm{s}|}-j}\cdot\frac{1}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot\binom{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|}-j}}-\frac{1}{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}\frac{\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{j}}{\binom{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|}-j}}\cdot a^{n_{|\bm{s}|}-j}-1}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|+1}\geq 1}\frac{\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{\binom{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}\cdot\frac{a^{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}},

(28)

where we used thy symmetry of binomial coefficients
\binom{n}{j}=\binom{n}{n-j}
in the last step. Comparing the expressions in (
26
) and (
28
) concludes the proof.
∎

Recall that the expression
Q(\bm{s})
can essentially be simplified if the components of
\bm{s}
are equal to
1
. We thus present the form of Theorem
4.1
for
\bm{s}=(\{1\}_{d})
in the following example.

Example 5

Assume that
d,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta^{\star}_{k}(\{1\}_{d})=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{d-1}\cdot(n_{d}+1)},

where the product
n_{1}\cdots n_{d-1}
is considered as empty for
d=1
.

Notice that for
d=1
, Example
5
implies the identity
\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}=(n+1)\cdot(H_{n+1}-1)
, which is well known.

Proof
(of Example
5
)

Setting
\bm{s}=(\{1\}_{d})
in Theorem
4.1
implies
Q(\{1\}_{d})=0
following from the definition of
Q(\bm{s})
in (
24
). Therefore, from
|\bm{s}|=d
and by (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta^{\star}_{k}(\{1\}_{d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d+1}\geq 1}\frac{\binom{n_{d}}{n_{d+1}}}{\binom{n_{d}}{n_{d+1}}}\cdot\frac{1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{d}\cdot(n_{d}+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{d}\cdot(n_{d}+1)}\sum_{n_{d+1}=1}^{n_{d}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{d-1}\cdot(n_{d}+1)},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Let us now study particular cases of Theorem
4.1
for
n\to\infty
. We present two corollaries offering new series representations of
\Li^{\star}_{\bm{s}}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a)
and
\zeta^{\star}(\bm{s})
.

Corollary 6

Assume that
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
|a|\leq 1
,
\bm{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and let
Q(\bm{s})
be defined in (
24
). Then

\Li^{\star}_{\bm{s}}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a)=\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|+1}\geq 1}\frac{\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{\binom{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}\cdot\frac{a^{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}

(29)

whenever the polylogarithm on the left-hand side converges.

Proof

The proof is straightforward; it suffices to calculate the limits of both sides of (
25
) for
n\to\infty
. Of course, the limit of the left-hand side of (
25
) can be calculated with the help of Remark
3
. Consequently, we recognize the value of this limit as
\Li^{\star}_{\bm{s}}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a)
assuming its convergence. Taking into account the simpler limit of the right-hand side of (
25
) completes the proof since the existence of this limit is uniquely determined by the convergence of
\Li^{\star}_{\bm{s}}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},a)
on the left, which was assumed above.
∎

Corollary 7

Assume that
\bm{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
s_{1}>1
, and let
Q(\bm{s})
be defined in (
24
). Then

\zeta^{\star}(\bm{s})=\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\frac{1}{(Q(\bm{s})+1)\cdot(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|-1}}.

(30)

Proof

Setting
a=1
into the right-hand side of (
29
), we obtain
\Li^{\star}_{\bm{s}}(\{1\}_{\|\bm{s}\|-1},1)=\zeta^{\star}(\bm{s})
representing a convergent value due
s_{1}>1
. On the contrary, the right-hand side of (
29
) reads for
a=1

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|+1}\geq 1}

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{\binom{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}\cdot\frac{1}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\frac{1}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}\cdot\sum_{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}=1}^{n_{|\bm{s}|}}\frac{\binom{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}{\binom{Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}}{n_{|\bm{s}|+1}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{|\bm{s}|}\geq 1}\frac{1}{(Q(\bm{s})+n_{|\bm{s}|}+1)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{|\bm{s}|}}\cdot\frac{n_{|\bm{s}|}}{Q(\bm{s})+1},

where we used the identity
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}/\binom{n}{k}=\frac{m}{n+1-m}
. After canceling the variable
n_{|\bm{s}|}
, the proof is completed.
∎

Example 6

Assume that
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{2d}\geq 1}\frac{1}{\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{2d}(-1)^{i-1}\cdot n_{i}\right)\cdot\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{2d-1}(-1)^{i-1}\cdot n_{i}\right)\cdot n_{1}\cdots n_{2d-1}}=\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}_{d}).

(31)

It is worth mentioning that the last formula for
d=1
is well known. In this case, the identity in (
31
) reduces to

\sum_{n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1+n_{1}-n_{2})\cdot(1+n_{1})\cdot n_{1}}=\zeta(2)

whose left-hand side can be written as
\sum_{n_{1}\geq 1}H_{n_{1}}/(n_{1}\cdot(1+n_{1}))
, where
H_{n_{1}}
denotes the harmonic number.

Proof
(of Example
6
)

It is not hard to see that for
\bm{s}=(\{2\}_{d})
, the definition of
Q(\bm{s})
in (
24
) implies

Q(\bm{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(n_{2i-1}-n_{2i})=\sum_{i=1}^{2d}(-1)^{i-1}\cdot n_{i}.

Applying this formula to (
30
) and performing simplifications, we immediately obtain (
31
).
∎

\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}_{d})=\bigl(2-4^{1-d}\bigr)\cdot\zeta(2d)

31

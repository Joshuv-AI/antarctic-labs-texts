Shortest Distance in Modular Hyperbola and Least Quadratic Nonresidue
Tsz Ho Chan

Abstract
In this paper, we study how small a box contains at least two points from a modular hyperbola x ​ y ≡ c ( mod p ) xy\equiv c\pmod{p} . There are two such points in a square of side length p 1 / 4 + ϵ p^{1/4+\epsilon} . Furthermore, it turns out that either there are two such points in a square of side length p 1 / 6 + ϵ p^{1/6+\epsilon} or the least quadratic nonresidue is less than p 1 / ( 6 ​ e ) + ϵ p^{1/(6\sqrt{e})+\epsilon} .



1
Introduction and Main results

Let
p>2
be a prime and
(c,p)=1
. We consider the modular hyperbola

H_{c}:=\{(x,y):xy\equiv c\pmod{p}\}.

We are interested in the shortest distance between two points in
H_{c}
. Rather than distances, we consider how small a box

B(X,Y;H):=\{(x,y):X+1\leq x\leq X+H\pmod{p},Y+1\leq y\leq Y+H\pmod{p}\}

contains two points in
H_{c}
where
X
and
Y
run over
0
,
1
, …,
p-1
.

By Hölder’s inequality and Weil’s bound on character sum, we have

Theorem 1

For all
(c,p)=1
, there exist some
0\leq X,Y\leq p-1
so that

|H_{c}\cap B(X,Y;H)|\geq 2

if
H\gg_{\epsilon}p^{1/4+\epsilon}
.

Now let us switch to the subject of the least quadratic nonresidue modulo
p
. Many people have been interested in the upper bound for
n_{p}
. By Polya-Vinogradov bound on character sums, we have

n_{p}\ll p^{1/2}\log p.

Vinogradov
[
5
]
applied a trick and got

n_{p}\ll_{\epsilon}p^{1/(2\sqrt{e})+\epsilon}.

Burgess
[
1
]
proved a new bound on short character sum which together with Vinogradov’s trick yielded

n_{p}\ll_{\epsilon}p^{1/(4\sqrt{e})+\epsilon}.

Recall a recent result of Heath-Brown
[
2
]
and Shao
[
3
]
on mean-value estimates of character sums:

Theorem 2

Given
H\leq p
, a positive integer and any
\epsilon>0
. Suppose that
0\leq N_{1}<N_{2}<...<N_{J}<p
are integers satisfying
N_{j+1}-N_{j}\geq H
for
1\leq j<J
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\max_{h\leq H}|S(N_{j};h)|^{2r}\ll_{\epsilon,r}H^{2r-2}p^{1/2+1/(2r)+\epsilon}

where

S(N;H):=\sum_{N<n\leq N+H}\chi(n).

and
\chi
is any non-principal character modulo
p
.

Applying the above theorem, we can show that

Theorem 3

For any
\epsilon>0
, we have either

n_{p}\ll_{\epsilon}p^{1/(6\sqrt{e})+\epsilon}

or, for any
(c,p)=1
and
H\gg_{\epsilon}p^{1/6+\epsilon}
,

|H_{c}\cap B(X,Y;H)|\geq 2

for some
0\leq X,Y\leq p-1
.

It is probably the case that the above two statements are true simultaneously. The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we give a basic argument transforming the existence of two close points in the modular hyperbola to a certain equality in Legendre symbol. Then we prove Theorem
1
in section 3 and Theorem
3
in section 4.

Some Notations
Throughout the paper,
p
stands for a prime. The symbol
|S|
denotes the number of elements in the set
S
. We also use the Legendre symbol
(\frac{\cdot}{p})
. The notation
f(x)=o(g(x))
means that the ratio
f(x)/g(x)
is going to zero as
x,p\rightarrow\infty
. The notations
f(x)\ll g(x)
,
g(x)\gg f(x)
and
f(x)=O(g(x))
are equivalent to
|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for some constant
C>0
. Finally,
f(x)\ll_{\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k}}g(x)
,
g(x)\gg_{\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k}}f(x)
and
f(x)=O_{\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k}}(g(x))
mean that the implicit constant
C
may depend on
\lambda_{1}
, …,
\lambda_{k}
.

The Basic Argument

For
(c,p)=1
, suppose
|H_{c}\cap B(X,Y;H)|\geq 2
for some
0\leq X,Y\leq p-1
. This means that

xy\equiv c\pmod{p},\text{ and }(x+a)(y+b)\equiv c\pmod{p}

(1)

for some
1\leq x,y\leq p-1
and
1\leq a,b\leq H
. After some algebra, one can show that (
1
) is equivalent to

bx+ac\overline{x}+ab\equiv 0\pmod{p}

where
\overline{x}
stands for the multiplicative inverse of
x
modulo
p
(i.e.
x\overline{x}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. This, in turn, is equivalent to

(2bx+ab)^{2}\equiv(ab)^{2}-4abc\pmod{p}.

Therefore
|H_{c}\cap B(X,Y;H)|\geq 2
if and only if

\Bigl(\frac{ab}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{ab-4c}{p}\Bigr)=1

for some
1\leq a,b\leq H
. We are going to restrict our attention to even
a=2a^{\prime}
’s and
b=2b^{\prime}
’s. So we want

\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)=1\text{ for some }1\leq a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\leq H/2.

(2)

3
Proof of Theorem
1

Throughout this section, we assume that
H\gg_{\epsilon}p^{1/4+\epsilon}
. We want to show that

\sum_{a^{\prime}\leq H/2}\sum_{b^{\prime}\leq H/2}\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)=o(H^{2}).

Then either we have two pairs with
(\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}b_{1}^{\prime}-c}{p})=0=(\frac{a_{2}^{\prime}b_{2}^{\prime}-c}{p})
which gives Theorem
1
automatically; or at most one such pair equal to
0
which would imply that
(\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}b_{1}^{\prime}}{p})(\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}b_{1}^{\prime}-c}{p})=1
and
(\frac{a_{2}^{\prime}b_{2}^{\prime}}{p})(\frac{a_{2}^{\prime}b_{2}^{\prime}-c}{p})=-1
for some
1\leq a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime}\leq H/2
. However, we are going to restrict
a^{\prime}
to a special form, namely
a^{\prime}=uv
with
1\leq u\leq U
,
1\leq v\leq V
and
UV=H/2
. So let us consider

S:=\sum_{u\leq U}\sum_{v\leq V}\sum_{b^{\prime}\leq H/2}\Bigl(\frac{uvb^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{uvb^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr).

Then

S\leq\sum_{v\leq V}\sum_{b^{\prime}\leq H}\Big|\sum_{u\leq U}\Bigl(\frac{u}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{uvb^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)\Big|\ll_{\epsilon}p^{\epsilon}\sum_{n\leq VH}\Big|\sum_{u\leq U}\Bigl(\frac{u}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{un-c}{p}\Bigr)\Big|

as the number of divisors of
n
is
O_{\epsilon}(n^{\epsilon})
. Now apply Hölder’s inequality and get

\displaystyle S\ll_{\epsilon,r}

\displaystyle p^{\epsilon/(2r)}\Bigl(\sum_{n\leq VH}1\Bigr)^{(2r-1)/(2r)}\Bigl(\sum_{n\leq p-1}\Big|\sum_{u\leq U}\Bigl(\frac{u}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{un-4c}{p}\Bigr)\Big|^{2r}\Bigr)^{1/(2r)}

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon,r}

\displaystyle p^{\epsilon/(2r)}(VH)^{1-1/(2r)}(U^{r}p+U^{2r}p^{1/2})^{1/(2r)}

by Lemma 4 in
[
4
]
which follows from Weil’s bound on multiplicative character sums. Now we take
U=p^{1/(2r)}
and
V=H/U
with
1/r<\epsilon
. Then one can verify that
S=o(H^{2})
which implies that there is some
uv\leq H/2
and
b^{\prime}\leq H/2
such that
\Bigl(\frac{uvb^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{uvb^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)=1
. This together with the argument in section
2
gives Theorem
1
.

4
Proof of Theorem
3

Throughout this section, we assume that
H\gg_{\epsilon}p^{1/6+\epsilon}
. Suppose, for all
(c,p)=1
,

\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)=1

for some
1\leq a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\leq H/2
. This together with section
2
implies that

|H_{c}\cap B(X,Y;H)|\geq 2

for any
(c,p)=1
and
H\gg_{\epsilon}p^{1/6+\epsilon}
.

Now, suppose this is not the case. Then, for some
(c,p)=1
,

\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)=0\text{ or }-1

for all
1\leq a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\leq H/2
. Suppose two such pairs give

\Bigl(\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}b_{1}^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)=0=\Bigl(\frac{a_{2}^{\prime}b_{2}^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr).

This implies
|H_{c}\cap B(X,Y;H)|\geq 2
automatically by section
2
. Subsequently, we assume that

\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}-c}{p}\Bigr)=-1

for all but at most one pair of
1\leq a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\leq H/2
.
This implies that

\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}-c\overline{b^{\prime}}}{p}\Bigr)=-\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)

for all but at most one pair of
1\leq a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\leq H/2
. Consequently,

\sum_{b^{\prime}\leq H/2}\Big|\sum_{a^{\prime}\leq H/2}\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}-c\overline{b^{\prime}}}{p}\Bigr)\Big|^{2r}\geq([H/2]-1)\Big|\sum_{a^{\prime}\leq H}\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)\Big|^{2r}=:([H/2]-1)|\Sigma|^{2r}.

Now we apply Theorem
2
with
N_{b^{\prime}}=-c\overline{b^{\prime}}
. First we claim that
c\overline{b_{1}^{\prime}}-c\overline{b_{2}^{\prime}}
cannot be congruent to some
l\leq H
modulo
p
for
1\leq b_{1}^{\prime}<b_{2}^{\prime}\leq H/2
. For otherwise

c\overline{b_{1}^{\prime}}-c\overline{b_{2}^{\prime}}\equiv l\pmod{p}

for some
1\leq l\leq H
. Let
a_{1}^{\prime}\equiv c\overline{b_{1}^{\prime}}\pmod{p}
and
a_{2}^{\prime}\equiv c\overline{b_{2}^{\prime}}\pmod{p}
. Then
(a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime})
and
(a_{2}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime})
would be two points of the modular hyperbola
H_{c}
lying in a square of side length
H
which contradicts our assumption that no such square contains two such points. Therefore, we can apply Theorem
2
and get

([H/2]-1)|\Sigma|^{2r}=\sum_{b^{\prime}\leq H/2}\Big|\sum_{a^{\prime}\leq H/2}\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}-c\overline{b^{\prime}}}{p}\Bigr)\Big|^{2r}\ll_{\epsilon,r}H^{2r-2}p^{1/2+1/(2r)+\epsilon}.

This implies that

\Sigma\ll_{\epsilon,r}H^{1-3/(2r)}p^{(r+1)/(4r^{2})+\epsilon/(2r)}=o(H)

if
r
is sufficiently large. Hence we have that the character sum

\sum_{a^{\prime}\leq H/2}\Bigl(\frac{a^{\prime}}{p}\Bigr)=o(H).

Feeding this character sum estimate into the standard Vinogradov’s trick in obtaining upper bound for the least quadratic nonresidue, we have the first half of Theorem
3
.

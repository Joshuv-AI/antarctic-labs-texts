Mean reflected McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation Journal: Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications
Shaopeng Hong, Sheng Xiao

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a class of mean reflected McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation (MR-MVSDE), which extends the equation proposed by [ 1 ] by allowing the solution’s distribution to not only constrain its behavior but also affect the diffusion and drift coefficients. We establish the existence and uniqueness results of MR-MVSDE, investigate the propagation of chaos, and examine the stability properties with respect to the initial condition, coefficients, and driving process. Moreover, we provide rigorous proof of a Freidlin-Wentzell type LDP using the weak convergence method. We also demonstrate the existence of an invariant measure and employ the coupling by change of measure method to prove log-Harnack inequality and shift Harnack inequality.

2.1
The Wasserstein space

Let

For

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|x-y|^{p}d\pi(x,y)\right]^{1/p}\,,

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\left(\inf_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X-Y\right|^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X-Y\right|^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

In the following result, we mainly use

[
63
]
and references therein.

Then, we introduce the Lions’ derivative (L-derivative) on

For any

\displaystyle T_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle:=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R};\mu\right)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R};\phi\text{ is measurable with }\mu\left(|\phi|^{2}\right):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi(x)|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty\right\},

e_{n}=4\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|b(X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})|d|A^{n}_{s}-A_{s}|\right)^{2}\right]+4\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma(X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})|d|M^{n}_{s}-M_{s}|\right)^{2}\right]\,.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\left|K^{n}_{t}-K_{t}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{2}}{m^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left|U^{n}_{r}-U_{r}\right|^{2}\right]

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{2}}{m^{2}}C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|X^{n}_{s}-X_{s}|^{2}\right]+\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}\right)^{2}ds+Ce_{n}

Combining (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}|^{2}\right]\left(dA^{n}_{s}+d\left\langle M^{n}\right\rangle_{s}\right)+Ce_{n}\,.

Since

is an increasing process, then according to the Stochastic Gronwall lemma, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq Ce_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[A^{n}_{T}+\left\langle M^{n}\right\rangle_{T}\right]<\infty,

using the assumptions of

Therefore,

∎

. Moreover,

\forall t\geq 0,\quad K_{t}=\sup_{s\leq t}\inf\left\{x\geq 0:\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(x+U_{s}\right)\right]\geq 0\right\}=\sup_{s\leq t}G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{U_{s}}\right),

(6.8)

where
\left(U_{t}\right)_{0\leq t\leq T}
is the process defined by :

\displaystyle U_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})dA_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})dM_{s}.

(6.9)

With these notations, denoting by
\mathcal{L}_{U_{s}}
the family of marginal laws of
\left(U_{t}\right)_{0\leq t\leq T}
we have

K_{t}=\sup_{s\leq t}G_{0}(\mathcal{L}_{U_{s}}).

Proof.

Let us consider the set
\mathcal{C}^{2}=\left\{X\text{ is }\mathcal{F}-\text{adapted continuous process},\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|^{2}<\infty\right)\right\}
. And let
\widetilde{X}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
be a given process. We set

\tilde{U}_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}b\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)dA_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)dM_{s},\quad\operatorname{Law}\left(\tilde{U}_{s}\right)=:\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{s}}\,,

(6.10)

and define the function
K
by setting

{K_{t}=\sup_{s\leq t}\inf\left\{x\geq 0:\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(x+\tilde{U}_{s}\right)\right]\geq 0\right\}=G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{s}}\right).}

The function
K
being given, let us define the process
X
by the formula

\displaystyle X_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}b\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)dA_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)dM_{s}+K_{t}.

(6.11)

Based on the definition of
K
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[h(X_{t})\right]\geq 0
,
K_{t}=G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{t}}\right),dK_{t}-a.e.
and
G_{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{t}})>0,dK_{t}-a.e.
Then we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(X_{s}\right)\right]dK_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(\tilde{U}_{s}+K_{s}\right)\right]dK_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(\tilde{U}_{s}+G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{s}}\right)\right)\right]dK_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(\tilde{U}_{s}+G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{s}}\right)\right)\right]\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{s}}\right)>0\right\}}dK_{s}.

Moreover, since
h
is continuous, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(\tilde{U}_{s}+G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{s}}\right)\right)\right]=0
as soon as
G_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{U}_{s}}\right)>0
, so that

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(X_{s}\right)\right]dK_{s}=0\,.

Let
\tilde{X},\tilde{X}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
and define
\tilde{U}
,
K
and
\tilde{U}^{\prime}
,
K^{\prime}
as above, using the some Brownian motion. We have from the Lipschitz assumption of
b
and
\sigma
, B-D-G inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|\tilde{X}_{t}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{\prime}|^{2}\right]

(6.12)

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\left\{\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(b\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)-b\left(\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{s}}\right)\right)dA_{s}\right|^{2}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)-\sigma\left(\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{s}}\right)\right)dM_{s}\right|^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\qquad+\left|K_{t}-K^{\prime}_{t}\right|^{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left[\left.T\cdot\sup_{t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\left(b\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)-b\left(\tilde{X}_{s}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}^{\prime}}\right)\right)dA_{s}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+3\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma\left(\tilde{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}}\right)-\sigma\left(\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{s}}\right)\right)dM_{s}\right|^{2}\right]+3\sup_{t\leq T}\left|K_{t}-K^{\prime}_{t}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|\tilde{X}_{t}-\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{t}|^{2}\right]d(A_{s}+\left\langle M\right\rangle_{s})+3\sup_{t\leq T}\left|K_{t}-K^{\prime}_{t}\right|^{2}\,.

From the representation of
K
and Lemma
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}|K_{t}-K^{\prime}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\leq T}\left|\sup_{s\leq t}G_{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{t}})-\sup_{s\leq t}G_{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{t}^{\prime}})\right|\leq\sup_{t\leq T}\left|G_{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{t}})-G_{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{X}_{t}^{\prime}})\right|

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\left|\tilde{U}_{t}-\tilde{U}^{\prime}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\left|\tilde{X}_{t}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{\prime}\right|^{2}\right]\,.

Plugging (
6.13
) into (
6.12
), and use the stochastic Gronwall lemma, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|\tilde{X}_{t}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{\prime}|^{2}\right]\leq C(1+T)T\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\left|\tilde{X}_{t}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{\prime}\right|^{2}\right].

Hence, there exists a positive
\tilde{T}
, depending on
b,\sigma
and
h
only, such that for all
T<\tilde{T}
, the map
\Phi
is a contraction. We first deduce the existence and uniqueness of the solution on
[0,\tilde{T}]
and then on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
by iterating the construction.

∎

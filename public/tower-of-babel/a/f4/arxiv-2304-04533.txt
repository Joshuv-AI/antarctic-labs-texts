Impurity effect on Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces: Analysis based on iron-based superconductors
Tatsuya Miki, Hiroaki Ikeda, Shintaro Hoshino

Abstract
The effect of impurities on a superconductor with Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces (BFSs) is studied using a realistic tight-binding model. Based on the band structure composed of d d -orbitals in tetragonal FeSe \mathrm{FeSe} , whose S-doped sample is a potential material for BFS, we construct the superconducting state by introducing a time-reversal broken pair potential in terms of the band index. We further consider the effect of impurities on the BFS, where the impurity potential is defined as a local potential for the original d d -orbitals. The self-energy is calculated using the (self-consistent) Born approximation, which shows an enhancement of the single-particle spectral weight on the Fermi surface. This is consistent with the previous phenomenological theory and is justified by the present more detailed calculation based on the FeSe \mathrm{FeSe} -based material.



I
Introduction

The phenomenon of superconductivity is induced by Cooper-pair condensation near the Fermi surface, which typically results in the formation of a superconducting gap at the Fermi level.
This gap structure is usually classified into three categories: full-gap, point-node, and line-node
Sigrist91
.
However, it has been suggested that some superconductors exhibit a fourth type of gap structure known as Bogoliubov Fermi surface (BFS) or ultra-nodal pair, in which the Fermi surface persists even in the superconducting state.
This type of superconductor was first proposed in the context of multi-band superconductors
Volovik89-1
;
Volovik89-2
, superfluid helium
Liu03
;
Gubankova05
;
Autti20
.
More recently, specific models with broken time-reversal symmetry and preserved inversion symmetry have also been proposed as candidate systems
Agterberg17
;
Brydon18
.
In these models, BFS is topologically protected and remains stable against small perturbations.
The characteristic features of BFS have been the subject of theoretical studies
Volovik93
;
Yuan18
;
Sumita19
;
Suh20
;
Setty20
;
Setty20-prb
;
Lapp20
;
Oh20
;
Tamura20
;
Timm21
;
Timm21_2
;
Jiang21
;
Miki21
;
Hoshino22
;
Menke19
;
Link20
;
Link20-2
;
Herbut20
;
Dutta21
;
Kim21
;
Kobayashi22
;
Kitamura22
.

In addition to these theoretical studies, the possibility of BFS has been experimentally implied.
It has been pointed out in some materials with unconventional superconductivity that there exists a residual zero-energy density of state (DOS) in the superconducting state
Schuberth92
;
Zieve04
;
Kittaka18
;
Shibauchi20
.
Especially in
\mathrm{Fe(Se,S)}
, the zero-energy DOS and the presence of low-energy carriers have been observed through the tunnel conductance of STS
Hanaguri18
, the heat capacity, the thermal conductivity
Sato18
;
Mizukami21
, and the laser ARPES
Nagashima22
.
As for the theoretical description of BFS in
\mathrm{Fe(Se,S)}
, the inter-band pairing with broken time reversal symmetry is suggested to play an important role for the system having the BFS
Setty20
.
This model has succeeded in qualitatively reproducing the behavior of the DOS and the heat capacity.

Given the fact that the actual materials may have BFS, it is interesting to ask if there exists characteristic physics specific to superconductors with BFS.
The electronic states near BFS is composed of Bogoliubov quasi-particles (bogolons), which describe the low energy excitation of the superconducting state.
It is expected that the low-energy properties are governed by the bogolon’s nature.
In our previous work, the authors pointed out that the characteristic feature of bogolon enters through the impurity scattering and interaction
Miki21
.
We studied physical properties of the Bogoliubov Fermi liquid state near BFS for a system with preserved inversion and broken time-reversal symmetries, and found that the pair amplitude (anomalous Green’s function) of bogolons becomes finite.
Interestingly, the pair amplitude has a purely odd-function with respect to the relative time of two bogolons, which is called the odd-frequency pairing.
The concept of odd-frequency pairing has been previously examined in relation to electrons and
3
He
Berezinskii74
;
Kirkpatrick91
;
Balatsky92
;
Emery92
;
Coleman93
;
Tanaka12
;
Linder19
, but in the present context the Cooper pair is composed of bogolons.
Since the impurity effect gives a dominant contribution at low energies, we analyzed it in detail and found that the odd-frequency pair induces the zero-energy peak in the single particle DOS in bulk.
While this analysis can capture a qualitative feature of BFS, the origin of the impurity potential on bogolons is not clear, which should be derived from the scattering potential defined in terms of original electrons.

II
Overview: odd-frequency pairing of bogolon

Before we go into the details of the tight-binding model study,
we here overview the concept of our work by comparing the two cases: the electrons in the conventional (
s
-wave) superconductor and the low-energy bogolons near BFS discussed in Refs.
Miki21
;
Hoshino22
.
We assume that the system with BFS has the inversion symmetry and does not have time reversal symmetry.
Although the pure odd-frequency pair of bogolon is generally induced by non-ideality, i.e., disorder or interaction effects
Miki21
, we below limit ourselves to the impurity effect which becomes dominant at low energies.

As schematically shown in Fig.
1
, we consider the two superconducting systems with impurity potential.
In both cases, the total Hamiltonian is given in the form
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{0}+\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{imp}}
, where
\mathscr{H}_{0}
is a clean-limit part and
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{imp}}
is an impurity potential part.
The clean-limit part is explicitly given by
\mathscr{H}_{0}=\sum_{\bm{k}\sigma}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\sigma}c_{\bm{k}\sigma}+\Delta c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\uparrow}c_{-\bm{k},\downarrow}^{\dagger}+{\rm H.c.})
for electrons with the single-particle energy
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}
, the annihilation operator
c_{\bm{k}\sigma}
, and the
s
-wave pair potential
\Delta
(left column).
As for the low-energy effective model of bogolons, the Hamiltonian is
\mathscr{H}_{0}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}
where
\alpha_{\bm{k}}
is the annihilation operator of bogolon (right column).
We note that the latter bogolon model describes the degrees of freedom near the BFS and does not have spin index and off-diagonal part since we assume the broken time-reversal symmetry and preserved inversion symmetry
Miki21
.
Namely, we have the identity
\sum_{\bm{k}}\Delta_{\bm{k}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\alpha_{-\bm{k}}^{\dagger}=-\sum_{\bm{k}}\Delta_{-\bm{k}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\alpha_{-\bm{k}}^{\dagger}=0
for
\Delta_{\bm{k}}=\Delta_{-\bm{k}}
.
This point is summarized in the the second row (labeled as ‘Clean’) of Fig.
1
.

In the presence of impurity potentials, the Green’s functions
\hat{G}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}
is written in the form of
2\times 2
matrix by using Nambu spinor
(c_{\bm{k}\uparrow},c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger})^{\mathrm{T}}
for electrons with
s
-wave superconductivity and
(\alpha_{\bm{k}},\alpha_{-\bm{k}}^{\dagger})^{\mathrm{T}}
for bogolons near the BFS, respectively.
The Green’s functions for each system satisfy the Dyson equation, which is written by

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\hat{G}_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\hat{G}_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\sum_{\bm{k}_{1}}\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}_{1}}\hat{G}_{\bm{k}_{1}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}),

(1)

where
\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}
is a impurity scattering matrix, which is to be averaged.

\omega_{n}
is a fermionic Matsubara frequency.

One may wonder if the odd-frequency pair amplitude might not arise from the static potential
\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}_{1}}
.
The appearance of the dynamical pair amplitude and pair potential can be understood by considering the lowest-order perturbation term.
First-order term vanishes after the random average, and hence we consider the second-order self-energy of the impurity potential:

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\sum_{\bm{k}_{1}}\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}_{1}}\hat{G}_{\bm{k}_{1}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\hat{u}_{\bm{k}_{1}\bm{k}^{\prime}}.

(2)

This self-energy includes both the even- and odd-frequency parts originating from the Green’s function
G^{0}
, and breaks the inversion and translational symmetry.
This situation is summarized in the third row (labeled as ‘Dirty’) of Fig.
1
.
After taking a impurity average, the inversion and translational symmetry are recovered (See, the fourth row of Fig.
1
.).

The above Green-function structures are same between electrons in
s
-wave superconductor and bogolons near the BFS.
Below, we clarify the difference between the two superconducting systems by focusing on the detailed structures of the Green’s functions.

(a) Electrons in
s
-wave superconductor

First, we consider the case of the
s
-wave superconductor with inversion symmetry in clean limit.
The unperturbed Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}G_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})&F_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\\
F_{\bm{k}}^{0\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})&\bar{G}_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

The off-diagonal part is present already in the clean limit.
Since the impurity scattering potential is gauge invariant in terms of electrons,
\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}
has only the diagonal part:

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}u(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})&0\\
0&-u(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

Inserting Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
) into Eq. (
2
), the off-diagonal self-energy, the pair potential
[\hat{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})]_{12}
, can be written by

\displaystyle[\hat{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})]_{12}=\sum_{\bm{k}_{1}}u(\bm{k},\bm{k}_{1})F_{\bm{k}_{1}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})u(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(5)

The frequency dependence enters through
F_{\bm{k}_{1}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})
which is the even function of frequency.
Correspondingly, the pair potential also has the even frequency functional form after the impurity average [See the fourth row (labeled as ‘Impurity averaged’) of Fig.
1
.].

(b) Low-energy bogolons near Bogoliubov Fermi surface

We next consider the case of bogolon.
In the clean limit, the Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}G_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})&0\\
0&\bar{G}_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where the inversion symmetry prohibits the off-diagonal terms.
Since the gauge symmetry is broken, the impurity potential of bogolon generally has both diagonal (
\alpha^{\dagger}\alpha
) and off-diagonal (
\alpha^{\dagger}\alpha^{\dagger}
) terms effectively, which are denoted by
u_{1}(\bm{k},\bm{k})
and
u_{2}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})
, respectively.
We write the concrete form of
\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})&u_{2}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\\
u_{2}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})^{\ast}&-u_{1}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}.

(7)

The pair potential (=anomalous self-energy) then becomes

\displaystyle[\hat{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})]_{12}=\sum_{\bm{k}_{1}}[u_{1}(\bm{k},\bm{k}_{1})G^{0}_{\bm{k}_{1}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})u_{2}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+u_{2}(\bm{k},\bm{k}_{1})G^{0}_{\bm{k}_{1}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})u_{1}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}^{\prime})].

(8)

We note that the diagonal Green’s function
G_{\bm{k}_{1}}^{0}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})
is composed of mixed even and odd functions of frequency.
Since the pair potential obeys the Fermi-Dirac statistics,
[\hat{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}]_{12}
becomes the odd function of frequency in inversion-symmetric system after the impurity average (the fourth row of Fig.
1
).
The pure odd-frequency pair of bogolon is thus realized.
We note that the interaction effect also induces the self-energies and the odd-frequency pairing amplitude, although the impurity effect is dominant at low-frequency and low-temperature limit
Miki21
.

In the following sections, in order to clarify the microscopic origin of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
and their physical consequences, we investigate the impurity effect on BFS based on the realistic tight-binding model, where the impurity potential is defined in the real-space representation in terms of the original electrons.

Then, we obtain the diagonalized

III.2
Pair potential part

Here, we consider the pair potential part phenomenologically, following the procedure given in Ref.

.
It is important to consider the inter-band pairing with time reversal symmetry breaking for BFS.
Below, for convenience of explanation, we assume that the bands with the indices

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}}c_{\bm{k}\lambda\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\lambda>\lambda^{\prime}}c_{\bm{k}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\lambda\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Bigg)+\mathrm{H.c.}

(13)

where

III.3
Phase of wave-function at each
\bm{k}

Since the pair potential is defined in the band basis (
\lambda
), the phase of the eigenfunctions must be determined with careful thoughts.
Even after ordering
\lambda
in each
\bm{k}
point using the eigenvectors Eq. (
12

Namely, the transformation
\bm{v}(\bm{k})_{\lambda}\to\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\lambda}(\bm{k})}\bm{v}(\bm{k})_{\lambda}
does not alter the energy-eigenvalues Eq. (
10

This degrees of freedom are usually not reflected in physical quantity.
However the spectral functions in the superconducting states is in general not invariant under this transformation, because our pair potential part
\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}
is given in band basis, while the normal part
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{N}}
is given in the basis of the Wannier function.

As will be discussed in Sec.
V
, we will concentrate on the low-energy contribution near the BFSs, and hence it is necessary to construct a smooth function for the paths along BFSs
C_{\Gamma},C_{\mathrm{M}1},C_{\mathrm{M}2}
[shown in Fig.
3
(a) and (b)].
We also choose the phase so as to preserve the inversion symmetry with which the theoretical results are consistent with the experiment
Nagashima22
.
More specifically, we consider a (non-twisted) parallel-transport gauge for
C_{\mathrm{M}1},C_{\mathrm{M}2}
and a twisted parallel-transport gauge with inversion symmetry for
C_{\Gamma}

Vanderbilt_book
.
Let us write the eigenvector with the parallel-transport gauge as
\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k})
and the numerically obtained one as
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{k})
.
We fix the phase at the beginning point
\bm{k}_{0}
for each path as
\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}_{0})_{\lambda}=\bm{v}(\bm{k}_{0})_{\lambda}
[indicated by the black dot in Fig.
3
(b)].
The phase of the next
\bm{k}
-point is chosen as it is parallel to previous point.
For this purpose, we first introduce the relative phase between
\bm{k}
and
\bm{k}+\Delta\bm{k}
as

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(\bm{k})=-\mathrm{Im}\,\ln\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k})_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{k}+\Delta\bm{k})_{\lambda}.

(20)

This quantity corresponds to the Berry connection in a continuous limit.
Then we define the eigenvector with parallel-transport gauge at
\bm{k}+\Delta\bm{k}
as

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}+\Delta\bm{k})_{\lambda}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\lambda}(\bm{k})}\bm{v}_{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{k}+\Delta\bm{k})_{\lambda}.

(21)

In this gauge,
\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k})_{\lambda}
and
\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}+\Delta\bm{k})_{\lambda}
become parallel:

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Im}\,\ln\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k})_{\lambda}\cdot\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}+\Delta\bm{k})_{\lambda}=0.

(22)

The Berry phase
\phi_{C}
can be calculated numerically by the summation of the left hand side of the above equation taken over the the closed paths
C=C_{\mathrm{M}1},C_{\mathrm{M}2}
.
We obtain

because of the inversion symmetry and time reversal symmetry of the normal state.
For their counter parts (
k_{y}>0

57
).

For unclosed paths
C_{\Gamma}
[See, Fig.
3
(b)], we choose the twisted parallel transport gauge
\tilde{\bm{v}}(\bm{k})
form
\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k})
.
In numerical calculation, we calculate the

on the path
C_{\Gamma}
, and determine
\bar{\bm{v}}(-\bm{k}_{0})_{\lambda}
by using inversion symmetric operation for
\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}_{0})_{\lambda}
[See Eq. (
57
) in Sec.
VI

Then, we calculate the relative phase between
\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}_{N_{\Gamma}-1})_{\lambda}
and
\bar{\bm{v}}(-\bm{k}_{0})_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}=-\mathrm{Im}\,\ln\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}_{N_{\Gamma}-1})_{\lambda}\cdot\bar{\bm{v}}(-\bm{k}_{0})_{\lambda}.

(23)

Finally, we define the twisted parallel transport for
n
-th point (
n=0,\cdots,N_{\Gamma}-1

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}_{n})_{\lambda}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}n/N_{\Gamma}}\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{k}_{n})_{\lambda}.

(24)

This choice of gauge results in smooth and inversion-symmetry-preserved eigenvector.

IV
Impurity effects and Green’s functions

In this section, we proceed to a concrete analysis of the impurity effects.
We use the Green’s function method, which is appropriate for the analysis of the impurity effect on the superconducting system
Abrikosov59
;
Abrikosov61
.
We calculate the spectral function which is an experimentally observable quantity.

IV.1
Definition of Green’s functions and self-energies

First, we define the Green’s function as

\displaystyle\check{G}_{\bm{k}}(\tau)=-\langle\mathcal{T}\vec{\Psi}_{\bm{k}}(\tau)\vec{\Psi}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}G_{\bm{k}}(\tau)&F_{\bm{k}}(\tau)\\
F^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}(\tau)&\bar{G}_{\bm{k}}(\tau)\end{pmatrix},

(25)

where
\mathcal{T}
represents imaginary time ordering,
\langle\cdots\rangle
is a statistical average, and
A(\tau)=\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathscr{H}}A\mathrm{e}^{-\tau\mathscr{H}}
is the Heisenberg representation with imaginary time.

G_{\bm{k}},F_{\bm{k}}
correspond to the normal and anomalous Green’s functions, respectively, and their conjugate quantities are
\bar{G}_{\bm{k}},F_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}
.
The Fourier transformation from imaginary time to Matsubara frequency is defined by

\displaystyle\check{G}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\int_{0}^{1/T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\check{G}_{\bm{k}}(\tau)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}\tau},

(26)

where
T
is a temperature.
Using the clean limit Hamiltonian
\check{H}_{0}(\bm{k})
in Eq. (
14
), the self-energy is also introduced by

\displaystyle\check{G}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})^{-1}=\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}\check{1}-\check{H}_{0}(\bm{k})-\check{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}).

(27)

Each component of the self-energy is expressed as

\displaystyle\check{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{\bm{k}}&S_{\bm{k}}\\
S_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}&\bar{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}}\end{pmatrix}
.

IV.2
Impurity potential

We now explain the impurity potential part.
It is convenient to deal with real space representation for the disorder potential.
We start from the expression written by the field operator
\psi,\psi^{\dagger}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{imp}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{r}\,\psi_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})U_{\mathrm{imp}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})\psi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r}),

(28)

where
U_{\mathrm{imp}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})
is an impurity potential with spin
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
, and
\bm{r}_{i}
is a scattering center.
We can move to the Wannier function basis from Eq. (
28
) by expanding the field operator, which is written by

\displaystyle\psi_{\sigma}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{n}}\sum_{a\gamma}w_{\gamma}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}}-\bm{d}_{a})c_{\gamma\sigma}(\bm{R}_{\bm{n}}+\bm{d}_{a}).

(29)

Inserting Eq. (
29
) into Eq. (
28
), we obtain the impurity potential part of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{imp}}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{aa^{\prime}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\rho_{\bm{q}}U_{\mathrm{imp}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle\times f_{a\gamma,a^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}}^{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{q})[\hat{V}(\bm{k})^{\dagger}]_{\lambda,a\gamma}[\hat{V}(\bm{k}^{\prime})]_{a^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}c_{\bm{k}\lambda\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}},

(30)

where the form factor
f_{a\gamma,a^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}}^{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{q})
can be written as

\displaystyle f_{a\gamma,a^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}}^{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{r}w_{\gamma}^{\ast}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}}-\bm{d}_{a})

\displaystyle\times w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{m}}-\bm{d}_{a^{\prime}})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\bm{n}}+\mathrm{i}\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\bm{m}}},

(31)

and
\rho_{\bm{q}}=\sum_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}_{i}}
is a structure factor for the impurity configuration
\bm{r}_{i}
.
Note that
\bm{q}
is defined in an infinite range, while
\bm{k}
is defined in the Brillouin zone.

Now we apply the random average for impurity configuration

\displaystyle\overline{\rho_{\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{q}^{\prime}}}=Vn_{\mathrm{imp}}\delta_{\bm{q},-\bm{q}^{\prime}},

(32)

where
n_{\mathrm{imp}}=V^{-1}\sum_{i}1
(
V
is a system volume).
We consider the second-order self-energy with respect to the impurity potential as follows

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}\lambda\sigma,\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}_{1}\in{\rm HBZ}}\sum_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[\mathscr{U}_{\lambda\sigma,\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k})G_{\bm{k}_{1}\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})

\displaystyle-\mathscr{U}_{\lambda\sigma,\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k},-\bm{k}_{1},-\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k})\bar{G}_{\bm{k}_{1}\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1}}(-\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\Bigg],

(33)

\displaystyle S_{\bm{k}\lambda\sigma,\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}_{1}\in{\rm HBZ}}\sum_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[\mathscr{U}_{\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda\sigma,\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}_{1},-\bm{k},-\bm{k}_{1})F_{\bm{k}_{1}\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})

\displaystyle-\mathscr{U}_{\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda\sigma,\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{k},-\bm{k}_{1},-\bm{k},\bm{k}_{1})F_{\bm{k}_{1}\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1}}(-\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\Bigg],

(34)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda_{3}\sigma_{3},\lambda_{4}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})

\displaystyle=n_{\mathrm{imp}}N\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{4}}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{4}}U_{\mathrm{imp}}^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{q})U_{\mathrm{imp}}^{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(-\bm{q})

\displaystyle\times[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{1})^{\dagger}]_{\lambda_{1},a_{1}\gamma_{1}}[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{2})]_{a_{2}\gamma_{2},\lambda_{2}}[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{3})^{\dagger}]_{\lambda_{3},a_{3}\gamma_{3}}[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{4})]_{a_{4}\gamma_{4},\lambda_{4}}

\displaystyle\times f_{a_{1}\gamma_{1},a_{2}\gamma_{2}}^{\bm{k}_{1}\bm{k}_{2}}(\bm{q})f_{a_{3}\gamma_{3},a_{4}\gamma_{4}}^{\bm{k}_{3}\bm{k}_{4}}(-\bm{q})\delta_{\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{4}}.

(35)

We also obtain
\bar{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}\lambda\sigma,\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})
and
S^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\lambda\sigma,\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})
in a similar manner to the above expressions.
We note that
\delta_{\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{4}}
in (
35
) originates from the recovered-translational symmetry after the random average.
If we regard
G,F,\bar{G},F^{\dagger}
as unperturbed Green’s functions, we obtain the self-energy of Born approximation.
On the other hand, if we regard them as dressed Green’s functions, we obtain the self-energy of self-consistent Born approximation.

IV.3
Evaluation of self-energies

In order to evaluate the self-energies, we need to define the specific form of the impurity potential
U_{\mathrm{imp}}(\bm{q})
.
First, we consider the case of non-magnetic impurity (later we will also discuss the case of magnetic impurity).
The impurity potential is given by

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{imp}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=U_{\mathrm{imp}}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},

(36)

which is
\bm{q}
-independent and is frequently used for an electron gas model.

U_{\mathrm{imp}}
is a magnitude of the potential.
Although this is not a realistic impurity potential,
we can further analyze the model in a simple form and make a semi-quantitative estimate of the effect of impurities.

The concrete form of the form factor in Eq. (
35
) is written using the Wannier functions as

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,f_{a_{1}\gamma_{1},a_{2}\gamma_{2}}^{\bm{k}_{1}\bm{k}_{2}}(\bm{q})f_{a_{3}\gamma_{3},a_{4}\gamma_{4}}^{\bm{k}_{3}\bm{k}_{4}}(-\bm{q})\delta_{\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\bm{n}_{1},\cdots,\bm{n}_{4}}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{r}\,w_{\gamma_{1}}^{\ast}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{1}}-\bm{d}_{a_{1}})

\displaystyle\times w_{\gamma_{2}}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{2}}-\bm{d}_{a_{2}})w_{\gamma_{3}}^{\ast}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{3}}-\bm{d}_{a_{3}})

\displaystyle\times w_{\gamma_{4}}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{4}}-\bm{d}_{a_{4}})

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}_{1}\cdot\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{1}}+\mathrm{i}\bm{k}_{2}\cdot\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{2}}-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}_{3}\cdot\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{3}}+\mathrm{i}\bm{k}_{4}\cdot\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{4}}}\delta_{\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{4}},

(37)

where we have performed
\bm{q}
integration.
To proceed the calculation further, we use the two approximations.
First, we observe that the above quantity is expected to become largest when the locality condition
\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{1}}=\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{2}}=\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{3}}=\bm{R}_{\bm{n}_{4}},a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}
is satisfied.
Hence, we assume that the integration of
\bm{r}
takes finite value only if it satisfies this condition.
Second, we replace the Wannier function
w_{\gamma}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}}-\bm{d}_{a})
with atomic orbital function
\phi_{\gamma}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{\bm{n}}-\bm{d}_{a})
for simplicity.
We write the atomic orbital function as
\phi_{\gamma}(\bm{r})=R(r)\Theta_{\gamma}(\theta,\varphi)
, where
R(r)
is a radial wave function and
\Theta(\theta,\varphi)
is a cubic harmonics for the
d
-orbital, whose specific form is shown in Appendix
C
.
Then we can evaluate the integral with respect to
\theta,\varphi
by using Eq. (
64
), and obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda_{3}\sigma_{3},\lambda_{4}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})

\displaystyle=\Gamma\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda_{3}\sigma_{3},\lambda_{4}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4}),

(38)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{5n_{\mathrm{imp}}U_{\mathrm{imp}}^{2}}{28\pi}\int\mathrm{d}r\,r^{2}|R(r)|^{4}

(39)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1},\lambda_{2}\sigma_{2},\lambda_{3}\sigma_{3},\lambda_{4}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{4}}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{4}}[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{1})^{\dagger}]_{\lambda_{1},a_{1}\gamma_{1}}[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{2})]_{a_{2}\gamma_{2},\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\times[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{3})^{\dagger}]_{\lambda_{3},a_{3}\gamma_{3}}[\hat{V}(\bm{k}_{4})]_{a_{4}\gamma_{4},\lambda_{4}}

\displaystyle\times(\delta_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\delta_{\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}}+\delta_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3}}\delta_{\gamma_{2},\gamma_{4}}+\delta_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{4}}\delta_{\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}})

\displaystyle\times\delta_{a_{1},a_{2}}\delta_{a_{2},a_{3}}\delta_{a_{3},a_{4}}\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\delta_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}.

(40)

Once this form factor is obtained, we can immediately evaluate the self-energies by using Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
).

In this paper, we consider the two types of magnetic impurity (Heisenberg type, Ising type) in addition to non-magnetic impurity.
In the case of the isotropic magnetic impurity (Heisenberg type), we replace
the form factor of the spins in Eq. (
40
) as

\displaystyle\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\delta_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}\to\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}=2\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{4}}\delta_{\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}}-\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\delta_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}

(41)

for spin
S=1/2
.
We can also consider the magnetic impurity with anisotropy in

-direction (Ising type):

\displaystyle\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\delta_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}\to\sigma^{z}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\sigma^{z}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}.

(42)

Thus, the parameters that control the impurity effect is the scattering strength
\Gamma
and the type of the impurity potential (non-magnetic or Ising type/Heisenberg type).

the parameter

dependence of single particle spectra.

Numerical Results for single-particle spectra

In the above, we have formulated the theory of disordered BFS.

V.1
Single particle spectra near Fermi level

From the Green’s function, we can calculate the DOS.
Using the retarded Green’s function
\check{G}_{\bm{k}}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})

\displaystyle D(\omega)=-\frac{1}{N\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathrm{Im}\,\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{HBZ}}\check{G}_{\bm{k}}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+}).

Similar to the Fermi liquid theory of electrons, the low energy contributions are extracted in order to see the detailed structure near the BFSs.
The concrete calculation procedure is shown in Appendix

We write down the final result:

\displaystyle D(\omega)=D_{0}\sum_{C}\int_{C}\mathrm{d}\bm{k}A_{\bm{k}}(\omega),

\displaystyle A_{\bm{k}}(\omega)=\sum_{b\in\mathrm{BFS}}\frac{V_{\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{2}|v_{b}(\bm{k})|D_{0}}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Re}\,\Bigg[\frac{\mathrm{sgn}\,\mathrm{Im}\,\varepsilon_{\bm{k}+}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})-\mathrm{sgn}\,\mathrm{Im}\,\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})}{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}+}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})}

\displaystyle\times(2\omega-[\check{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})]_{11}-[\check{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})]_{22})\Bigg]

(45)

is the wave-vector-resolved spectral function, and

\displaystyle D_{0}=\sum_{C}\int_{C}\mathrm{d}\bm{k}\frac{V_{\mathrm{c}}}{2\pi^{2}|v_{b}(\bm{k})|}

(46)

is a zero-energy DOS in the clean limit.

\int_{C}\mathrm{d}\bm{k}
is the path integral along the BFS, which is taken over the specific path
C=C_{\Gamma},C_{\mathrm{M}1},C_{\mathrm{M}2}
[See, Fig.
3
(b)].

\check{\Sigma}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}
is the self-energy of bogolon defined by Eq. (
68
) in Appendix

′

\varepsilon_{\bm{k}\pm}

In the clean limit, it reduces to

\displaystyle A^{0}_{\bm{k}}=\frac{V_{\mathrm{c}}}{\pi^{2}D_{0}|v_{b}(\bm{k})|}

(47)

whose values are plotted in Fig.

Since the

VI
Summary

In this paper, we have analyzed the impurity effect on Bogoliubov Fermi surface using a realistic model for iron-based materials combined with the Born approximation. We have investigated the detailed structure for the single-particle spectra at low energies. Based on the tight-binding parameters obtained from first-principles calculations, we have calculated the wave-vector-dependent dynamical self-energy focusing on the low-energy regime near the Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces.

We have found that the zero-energy peak appears in the density of states as induced by the off-diagonal self-energy for bogolons. Furthermore, we have estimated an order of magnitude of the peak.
The peak height is about one percent of the density of states in the clean limit and is independent of the impurity density and the magnitude of the potential in the Born approximation.
On the other hand, the peak height varies with the type of non-magnetic or magnetic impurities.
These features are unique to Bogoliubov Fermi surface.
Hence, a systematic study of impurity effects may provide experimental confirmation of the presence of Bogoliubov Fermi surface in
\mathrm{Fe(Se,S)}
.
Although we have assumed a specific pair potential in this paper, the present method can be applied to other superconducting states.

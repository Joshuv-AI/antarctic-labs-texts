Adiabatic Lindbladian Evolution with Small Dissipators
Alain Joye

Abstract




Abstract
: We consider a time-dependent small quantum system weakly coupled to an environment, whose effective dynamics we address by means of a Lindblad equation. We assume the Hamiltonian part of the Lindbladian is slowly varying in time and the dissipator part has small amplitude. We study the properties of the evolved state of the small system as the adiabatic parameter and coupling constant both go to zero, in various asymptotic regimes. In particular, we analyse the deviations of the transition probabilities of the small system between the instantaneous eigenspaces of the Hamiltonian with respect to their values in the purely Hamiltonian adiabatic setup, as a function of both parameters.

Keywords
: Adiabatic approximation, Lindblad generators, Quantum dynamics.

Introduction

The adiabatic approximation of quantum mechanics, designed to address the time-dependent Schrödinger equation in its Hamiltonian formulation, has been introduced very soon after the discovery of quantum mechanics
[
BF
,
La
,
Z
]
. It has since been developed in order to accommodate more general Hamiltonians and to improve its accuracy; see
[
K1
,
N1
,
ASY
,
Te
,
AE
,
N2
,
JKP
,
JP1
,
J1
,
JP2
,
Sc
,
BDF
]
for examples along these lines. The adiabatic approximation being instrumental in the analysis of time-dependent phenomena, this mathematical method was extended and applied to a variety of evolution equations in more general contexts, like discrete time evolutions,
[
DKS
,
HJPR1
,
HJPR2
]
, non-linear setups
[
CFK1
,
CFK2
,
GG
,
S
,
LLFY
,
F-KJ
]
, or contracting evolutions in Banach spaces
[
Kr
,
NR
,
J2
,
AFGG1
]
for example.

Specifically, adiabatic approaches were successfully adapted to address the evolution of open quantum systems consisting of a time-dependent small system of interest coupled to an environment, be it from a global Hamiltonian perspective encompassing a modeling of the environment, or from an effective point of view through a Lindblad evolution equation. In particular, asymptotic expressions for quantum states solution to an evolution equation driven by time-dependent Lindblad generators in the adiabatic limit are provided in
[
AFGG1
,
AFGG2
,
FH
]
, together with detailed analyses of the special case of dephasing Lindbladians. See
[
DS
,
TW
,
JMS
]
for results along the same lines, including a Hamiltonian description of an environment at positive and zero temperature, while
[
A-SF
,
HJPR1
,
HJPR2
,
BFJP
]
focus on entropy production issues in the adiabatic regime of such systems.

From the point of view of applications to quantum engineering and quantum control, these adiabatic approaches are suited to describe the evolution of a small system that can be monitored in a time dependent fashion by external agents, and which is weakly coupled to its environment, due to imperfect isolation. In the adiabatic limit, and for a regime of small coupling, one expects to get a description of the evolution of the small system in terms of the characteristics of the Hamiltonian, with quantitative information of the perturbations induced by the effect of the environment. While these questions have been addressed in the physics literature in various setups, see
e.g.
the discussions and references in
[
AFGG2
,
FH
]
, the mathematical approaches of those questions are less numerous and often concern specific cases.

Detailed information about the evolution of the small system is available for dephasing Lindbladians only
[
AFGG1
,
AFGG2
,
FH
,
H
]
, in which by definition, the dissipator is a function of the Hamiltonian. Hence, a time dependence in the Hamiltonian implies a similar time dependence in the dissipator as well. In the Hamiltonian model addressed in
[
JMS
]
, the coupling between the small system and the (bosonic) environment is assumed to be energy conserving at all times, which makes it dependent on the Hamiltonian as well. This is arguably a shortcoming of the approach since the dissipator models the effect of the coupling of the small system to the environment that, in general, is likely to be independent of the way the system is monitored and is possibly time-independent. In the model considered in
[
DS
]
, the coupling of the small system to the (fermionic) environment is time independent, however the coupling constant is determined by the adiabatic parameter.

In case the Lindblad generator is time independent, the dynamics of states can be inferred from the spectral properties of the Lindbladian. Therefore, a host of perturbative methods have been designed to identify and approximate the asymptotic state, as well as to analyse more precise properties of the dynamics,
e.g.
unravelling. See for example the recent papers
[
ABFJ
,
BCF+
,
MGLG
,
HJ
,
BBC+
]
, and the references therein for works along these lines, in various setups.

The present contribution is devoted to the study of the effective dynamics of a small quantum system weakly coupled to an environment, assuming a Lindbladian description that consists in a slowly varying time-dependent Hamiltonian drive and an arbitrary dissipator. We analyse the evolved state of the small quantum system as both the adiabatic parameter
\varepsilon>0
and the coupling constant
g>0
vanish, in an independent way. Actually we can, and will, consider time-dependent dissipators, which will allow for comparisons with some of the results mentioned above.

Under suitable assumptions, we provide leading order approximations of the density matrix of the small system in the
perturbative regime
,
g\ll\varepsilon
, where the coupling constant is much smaller than the adiabatic parameter,
in the
slow drive regime
,
\varepsilon\ll g
, where the adiabatic parameter is much smaller than the coupling constant,
as well as in a
transition regime
,
g\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, bridging the gap between the two previous regimes. The transition regime is addressed by means of a
reduced dynamics
defined on the kernel of the Hamiltonian part of the Lindbladian, that depends on the single parameter
\varepsilon/g
that determines the regime we are in. We show that the reduced dynamics approximates the asymptotic Lindbladian evolution in the transition regime, that covers the perturbative regime and, partially, the slow drive regime.

As a consequence, we also derive the asymptotics of the transition probabilities between instantaneous eigenspaces of the Hamiltonian in these regimes. In the perturbative regime, the leading order of these transition probabilities is shown to be given by the familiar expression of order
\varepsilon^{2}
depending only on the Hamiltonian if
g\ll\varepsilon^{3}
,
and by an explicit integral expression of order
g/\varepsilon
that depends on the dissipator if
\varepsilon^{3}\ll g\ll\varepsilon
. This is in keeping with
[
JMS
]
where a similar transition in the asymptotics of the transition probability was observed for the Hamiltonian model considered. In case
g=\varepsilon
, we are in the transition regime and the reduced dynamics is independent of
\varepsilon
to leading order. This regime corresponds, in spirit, to the regime addressed in their Hamiltonian model by
[
DS
]
, Section 3. For the slow drive regime,
\varepsilon\ll g
, we get that the transition probability is independent of
\varepsilon
and
g
to leading order, and is characterised by the kernel of the dissipator.

These features are illustrated for a two-level system with a dissipator displaying a certain symmetry. This allows for the explicit computation of the reduced dynamics which interpolates between these regimes.

2
Setup and main results

The separable Hilbert space of the small system is denoted by
{\cal H}
and
t\mapsto H(t)
is its time dependent Hamiltonian on
{\cal H}
. The dissipator of the Lindbladian is constructed by means of a finite sum (for simplicity) of bounded operators on
{\cal H}
, called jump operators,
t\mapsto\Gamma_{l}(t)
,
l\in I
, a set of indices. We will assume the following regularity hypotheses:

Reg

\bullet

H:[0,1]\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H})
is self-adjoint valued and
C^{\infty}
in norm (with right and left derivatives at
\{0,1\}
).

\bullet

\partial_{t}^{k}H(t)|_{t=0}=0
, for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
.

\bullet
For each
j\in I
,
I
a finite set of indices,
\Gamma_{j}:[0,1]\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H})
is
C^{\infty}
in norm (with right and left derivatives at
\{0,1\}
).

Note that while the leading order results stated in the present section do not require all derivatives of the Hamiltonian at zero to vanish, the arbitrary high order generalisations of Section
7
require this property. This assumption ensures that high order adiabatic approximations of the Heisenberg unitary evolution of the spectral projectors of the Hamiltonian coincide with the spectral projectors of the Hamiltonian at time zero.

For
g\geq 0
, and each
t\in[0,1]
, the time-dependent Lindblad operator
{\cal L}_{t}^{[g]}(\cdot)\in{\cal B}({\cal B}({\cal H}))
reads

{\cal L}_{t}^{[g]}(\cdot)={\cal L}_{t}^{0}(\cdot)+g{\cal L}^{1}_{t}(\cdot)=-{\rm i}[H(t),\cdot]+g\sum_{l\in I}\Big(\Gamma_{l}(t)\cdot\Gamma_{l}^{*}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\big\{\Gamma_{l}^{*}(t)\Gamma_{l}(t),\cdot\big\}\Big),

(2.1)

where the Hamiltonian part
{\cal L}_{t}^{0}(\cdot)
is time-dependent, while the dissipator
g{\cal L}^{1}_{t}(\cdot)
is possibly constant.
The Lindbladian
{\cal L}_{t}^{[g]}
acts in particular on the Banach space
{\cal T}({\cal H})
, the set of trace class operators on
{\cal H}
with norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{1}
.
Further specialising, the Lindbladian acts on the set of density matrices or states,
i.e.
positive trace class operators of trace one, in the Schrödinger picture we adopt here.

A special case of interest for which the dissipator depends on time is that of
dephasing Lindbladians
characterized by
\Gamma_{l}(t)=F_{l}(H(t))
, where
F_{l}:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is some smooth function, for each
l\in I
, see
[
AFGG1
]
. Among other things, dephasing Lindbladians enjoy the following properties for each
t
fixed:

\displaystyle{\,\rm Ker\,}{\cal L}_{t}^{[g]}(\cdot)={\,\rm Ker\,}[H(t),\cdot]\ \ \mbox{in }\ {\cal T}({\cal H}),

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{j}(t),P(t)]=0,\ \ \forall\ \mbox{spectral projector}\ P(t)\ \mbox{of}\ H(t).

(2.2)

We shall work on
{\cal T}({\cal H})
, unless stated otherwise, and the corresponding operator norm of
{\cal A}\in{\cal B}({\cal T}({\cal H}))
will be denoted by
\|{\cal A}\|_{\tau}
. In particular, for any
A\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
, the maps on
{\cal T}({\cal H})\ni\rho
given by
{\cal A}_{l}:\rho\mapsto A\rho
and
{\cal A}_{r}:\rho\mapsto\rho A
belong to
{\cal B}({\cal T}({\cal H}))
and have norms satisfying
\|{\cal A}_{\#}\|_{\tau}\leq\|A\|
,
\#\in\{l,r\}
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm on
{\cal B}({\cal H})
. For
{\cal A},{\cal B}
two operators in
{\cal B}({\cal B}({\cal H}))
, we will denote their composition by
{\cal A}\circ{\cal B}
, or simply
{\cal A}{\cal B}
if no risk of confusion arises.

For
\varepsilon>0
,
g\geq 0
, we consider the Lindblad equation

\left\{\begin{matrix}\varepsilon\dot{\rho}=({\cal L}_{t}^{0}+g{\cal L}^{1}_{t})(\rho),&t\in[0,1],\cr\rho(0)=\rho_{0},\hfill&\phantom{x}\rho\in{\cal T}({\cal H}),\end{matrix}\right.

(2.3)

in the adiabatic and small coupling regimes, characterised by
(\varepsilon,g)\rightarrow(0,0)
.

We recall here the main properties of the solutions to (
2.3
). As is well known, see
[
D1
,
Li
]
and is recalled in
[
AFGG1
,
H
]
for example, for each fixed
t\in[0,1]
, the one-parameter family
({\rm e}^{s{\cal L}^{[g]}_{t}})_{s\geq 0}
considered on
{\cal T}({\cal H})
forms a norm continuous semigroup of completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP) applications, which are contraction operators.
Consequently, denoting by
({\cal U}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq 1}
the two-parameter propagator associated to (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\varepsilon\partial_{t}{\cal U}(t,s)=({\cal L}_{t}^{0}+g{\cal L}^{1}_{t})({\cal U}(t,s)),\cr{\cal U}(s,s)={\mathbb{I}},\ \ 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1,\hfill\end{matrix}\right.

(2.4)

it follows from Thm X.70 in
[
RS
]
for example that the propagator is a contraction:

\displaystyle\|{\cal U}(t,s)\rho\|_{1}\leq\|\rho\|_{1},\phantom{2}\ \

\displaystyle\forall\rho\in{\cal T}({\cal H}),\ \forall 1\geq t\geq s\geq 0.

(2.5)

In particular we have
\|{\cal U}(t,s)\|_{\tau}=1
, since
{\cal U}(t,s)
is trace preserving.

While we shall stick to the bounded case, note that unbounded Hamiltonians and/or dissipators could also be accommodated,
[
D1
,
J2
,
FFFS
]
, for example.

We suppose that the spectrum of the Hamiltonian is separated into several disjoint subsets, which corresponds to the familiar gap assumption of the adiabatic theory.

Spec

For
2\leq d<\infty
, there exists
G>0
such that for all
t\in[0,1]
, the spectrum of
H(t)
,
\sigma(H(t))
, satisfies

\sigma(H(t))=\cup_{1\leq j\leq d}\,\sigma_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \inf_{t\in[0,1],1\leq j\neq k\leq d}{\rm dist}(\sigma_{j}(t),\sigma_{k}(t))\geq G>0.

(2.6)

Accordingly, we introduce the corresponding self-adjoint spectral projectors on
{\cal H}
for
1\leq j\leq d

P_{j}(t)=-\frac{1}{2{\rm i}\pi}\oint_{\gamma_{j}}(H(t)-z)^{-1}dz,

(2.7)

where
\gamma_{j}\in\rho(H(t))
is a positively oriented simple loop encircling
\sigma_{j}(t)
which contains no element of
\sigma(H(t))\setminus\sigma_{j}(t)
in its interior,
i.e.

{\rm int}\,\gamma_{j}\cap\sigma(H(t))=\sigma_{j}(t)
.

Moreover,
P_{j}:t\mapsto P_{j}(t)
is
C^{\infty}
since
H
is, and

\displaystyle P_{j}(t)P_{k}(t)=\delta_{jk}P_{j}(t),\ \ \ \sum_{1\leq j\leq d}P_{j}(t)={\mathbb{I}}.

(2.8)

Note that for all
t\in[0,1]
and
1\leq j\leq d
,
P_{j}(t)
belongs to
{\,\rm Ker\,}{\cal L}_{t}^{0}
,
i.e.

{\cal L}_{t}^{0}(P_{j}(t))\equiv 0
.
Following
[
K1
]
, we introduce the operator on
{\cal H}

K(t)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}P^{\prime}_{j}(t)P_{j}(t)=-\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}P_{j}(t)P^{\prime}_{j}(t),

(2.9)

and the corresponding
parallel transport, or Kato,
operator on
{\cal H}
solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\partial_{t}W(t,s)=K(t)W(t,s),\hfill\\
W(s,s)=\mathbb{I},\ \ 0\leq s,t\leq 1.\end{matrix}\right.

(2.10)

It is unitary and satisfies the well known intertwining relation

W(t,s)P_{j}(s)=P_{j}(t)W(t,s),

(2.11)

whose proof is based on the fact that for any smooth projector
P(t)=P^{2}(t)
,
P(t)P^{\prime}(t)P(t)\equiv 0
, see
[
K1
,
K2
,
Kr
]
.
Note that the propagator
(W(t,s))_{0\leq s,t\leq 1}
is actually well defined and invertible for any set of projectors that satisfy (
2.8
) in a Banach space framework, and it satisfies the intertwining relation (
2.11
) for all
1\leq j\leq d
.

Proposition 5.2

6
Example

We consider here a two-level system or Qubit, for which the reduced dynamics
\tilde{\Psi}_{\delta}(t,s)
can be computed explicitly under some symmetry of the Lindblad operators. Beyond its intrinsic interest, this example allows us to illustrate the different regimes we encountered in the general case.

We assume
Reg
and
Gen
and with the notations introduced so far, for
{\cal H}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, we consider the two-level Hamiltonian

H(t)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq 2}e_{j}(t)P_{j}(t),\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ P_{j}(t)=|\varphi_{j}(t)\rangle\langle\varphi_{j}(t)|,

(6.1)

and
\varphi_{j}(t)=W(t,0)\varphi_{j}(0)
,
W(t,0)
being the unitary Kato operator.
We assume the dissipator

{\cal L}_{t}^{1}(\cdot)=\sum_{l\in I}\Big(\Gamma_{l}(t)\cdot\Gamma_{l}^{*}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\big\{\Gamma_{l}^{*}(t)\Gamma_{l}(t),\cdot\big\}\Big),

(6.2)

has jump operators in
{\cal B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
satisfying the symmetry condition

\sum_{l\in I}|\langle\varphi_{1}(t)|\Gamma_{l}(t)\varphi_{2}(t)\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{l\in I}|\langle\varphi_{2}(t)|\Gamma_{l}(t)\varphi_{1}(t)\rangle|^{2}.

(6.3)

This is the case in particular if all jump operators are self-adjoint. Note that condition (
6.3
) is independent of the normalised basis of eigenvectors of
H(t)
used to express it.

Then we have:

Proposition 6.1

Let
{\cal H}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, and assume
Reg
and
Gen
for
d=2
. Further suppose the dissipator satisfies the symmetry condition (
6.3
) and set
\gamma(t)=\sum_{l\in I}|\langle\varphi_{1}(t)|\Gamma_{l}(t)\varphi_{2}(t)\rangle|^{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Then, for any
\delta>0
, the reduced dynamics takes the explicit form in the ordered basis
\{P_{1}(0),P_{2}(0)\}
of
{\cal P}_{0}(0)
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}_{\delta}(t,s)|_{{\,\rm Span}\{P_{1}(0),P_{2}(0)\}}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\cr 1&1\cr\end{pmatrix}+{\rm e}^{-\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{s}^{t}\gamma(u)du}\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\cr-1&1\cr\end{pmatrix}.

(6.4)

Hence, in the regime
g\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ll 1
, for any initial state
\rho_{0}=\rho_{1}(0)P_{1}(0)+\rho_{2}(0)P_{2}(0)
, any
t\in[0,1],

\displaystyle{\cal U}(t,0)(\rho_{0})=\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)P_{1}(t)+\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)P_{2}(t)+{\cal O}(\varepsilon+g+g^{2}/\varepsilon),

(6.5)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+{\rm e}^{-2\frac{g}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds}(\rho_{1}(0)-\rho_{2}(0))\Big),

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+{\rm e}^{-2\frac{g}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds}(\rho_{2}(0)-\rho_{1}(0))\Big).

(6.6)

In particular, the transition probabilities read in the same regime

\displaystyle{\,\rm Tr}(P_{2}(t){\cal U}(t,0)(P_{1}(0)))={\,\rm Tr}(P_{1}(t){\cal U}(t,0)(P_{2}(0)))=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-2\frac{g}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds}\Big)+{\cal O}(\varepsilon+g+g^{2}/\varepsilon).

(6.7)

Remark 6.2

i) For
0<t\leq 1
fixed such that
\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds>0
, if
\varepsilon\ll g\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ll 1
,

{\cal U}(t,0)(\rho_{0})=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{I}}+{\cal O}(g^{2}/\varepsilon+(\varepsilon/g)^{\infty}),

(6.8)

which corresponds to Theorem
2.7
. Note that
{\mathbb{I}}
spans
{\,\rm Ker\,}\tilde{\cal L}_{t}^{1}|_{{\cal P}_{0}(t){\cal H}}
.

ii) If
g\ll\varepsilon\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ll 1
,

\displaystyle{\cal U}(t,0)(\rho_{0})=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(0)P_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}(0)P_{2}(t)

\displaystyle-\frac{g}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds\,(\rho_{1}(0)-\rho_{2}(0))(P_{1}(t)-P_{2}(t))+{\cal O}(\varepsilon+g^{2}/\varepsilon^{2})

(6.9)

which corresponds to Corollary
5.9
and Theorem
2.5
.

iii) The state
{\cal U}(t,0)(\rho_{0})
is determined by the asymptotics of the scalar factor
{\rm e}^{-2\frac{g}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds}
. In case
g\simeq\varepsilon
, i.e.
g=\alpha\varepsilon
, for some fixed
\alpha>0
, the leading order of
{\cal U}(t,0)(\rho_{0})
takes the form of any diagonal state with respect to the eigenbasis of
H(t)
, depending on the value of
{\rm e}^{-2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds}
.

iv) The Markov process interpretation of Lemma
5.5
remains in force here, with
\tilde{\Psi}_{\delta}(t,0)|_{{\,\rm Span}\{{\cal P}_{0}(0){\cal B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})\}}
being bistochastic.

Proof:

The arguments leading to Lemma
5.5
show that the generator of the reduced dynamics
{\cal W}_{0}(0,t)\tilde{\cal L}_{t}^{1}{\cal W}_{0}(t,0)
has the following matrix form in the basis
\{P_{1}(0),P_{2}(0)\}

(dropping the variable
t
from the notation)

\displaystyle\tilde{L}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in I}\begin{pmatrix}|\langle\varphi_{1}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{1}\rangle|^{2}-\|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{1}\|^{2}&|\langle\varphi_{1}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{2}\rangle|^{2}\cr|\langle\varphi_{2}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{1}\rangle|^{2}&|\langle\varphi_{2}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{2}\rangle|^{2}-\|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{2}\|^{2}\cr\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{l\in I}\begin{pmatrix}-|\langle\varphi_{2}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{1}\rangle|^{2}&|\langle\varphi_{1}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{2}\rangle|^{2}\cr|\langle\varphi_{2}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{1}\rangle|^{2}&-|\langle\varphi_{1}|\Gamma_{l}\varphi_{2}\rangle|^{2}\cr\end{pmatrix},

(6.10)

thanks to property (
4.16
). The assumed symmetry (
6.3
) allows us to get (restoring the time variable)

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in I}|\langle\varphi_{2}(t)|\Gamma_{l}(t)\varphi_{1}(t)\rangle|^{2}\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\cr 1&-1\cr\end{pmatrix}=\gamma(t)\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\cr 1&-1\cr\end{pmatrix}.

(6.11)

Therefore, in the same basis, the reduced dynamics
solution to (
5.15
) with
\delta>0
reads

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}_{\delta}(t,s)|_{{\,\rm Span}\{P_{1}(0),P_{2}(0)\}}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\cr 1&1\cr\end{pmatrix}+{\rm e}^{-\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{s}^{t}\gamma(u)du}\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\cr-1&1\cr\end{pmatrix}.

(6.12)

Then, Corollary
5.4
together with Remark
3.6
i), yield (
6.5
) and (
6.7
).
\Box

7
Generalisation

We present here a generalisation of the results concerning the perturbative regime to arbitrary high order in the adiabatic parameter. This is made possible by the use of a systematic improvement of the adiabatic approximations of the Schrödinger propagator
U(t,s)
(
3.2
), allowed by our general setup, see
e.g.

[
ASY
,
N2
,
JP2
]
. We briefly present here the approach of
[
JP2
]
based on a hierarchy labelled by
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
of smooth hamiltonians in
{\cal B}({\cal H})
, before spelling out the improvement it brings to the leading order results of Section
3
.

7.1
Higher order adiabatics

Set

\displaystyle H^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(t)

(7.1)

\displaystyle P_{j}^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{j}(t),\ \forall 1\leq j\leq d

(7.2)

\displaystyle K^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K(t).

(7.3)

and define the self-adjoint operator

H^{1}(t)=H(t)-{\rm i}\varepsilon K^{0}(t).

(7.4)

For
\varepsilon
small enough, the gap hypothesis
Spec
holds for all
t\in[0,1]
,
and we set for all
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,
\varepsilon
small enough, with

denoting the time derivative,

\displaystyle P_{j}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{j}}(H^{1}(t)-z)^{-1}\,dz,

(7.5)

\displaystyle K^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}{P_{j}^{1}}^{\prime}(t)P_{j}^{1}(t).

(7.6)

Note that
H^{1}
,
P^{1}
, and
K^{1}
are
\varepsilon
-dependent and smooth on
[0,1]
.
We define inductively, for
\varepsilon
small enough, the following hierarchy of operators
for
q\geq 1
, and all
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}

\displaystyle H^{q}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(t)-{\rm i}\varepsilon K^{q-1}(t)

(7.7)

\displaystyle P_{j}^{q}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{j}}(H^{q}(t)-z)^{-1}\,dz,

(7.8)

\displaystyle K^{q}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}{P_{j}^{q}}^{\prime}(t)P_{j}^{q}(t).

(7.9)

It is proven in
[
JP2
]
, see also
[
JP3
]
, that in our
C^{\infty}
framework, the
following holds:

Proposition 7.1

For any
q\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, there exists
\varepsilon_{q}>0
and
C_{q},\kappa_{q}<\infty
, such that for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{q}
,
t\in[0,1]
,
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,

are well defined and smooth for all

. Moreover, these operators and all their derivatives admit an asymptotic expansion in powers of
\varepsilon

and the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\|K^{q}(t)-K^{q-1}(t)\|\leq C_{q}\varepsilon^{q}

(7.10)

\displaystyle\|K^{q}(t)\|\leq\kappa_{q}.

(7.11)

Remark 7.2

i) The hierarchy above was actually designed to reach exponential accuracy in the adiabatic approximation, in an analytic framework, in which case it provides an estimate on the behaviour in
q
of the constant

.

ii) At

, the second point of assumption

ensures that for all
q\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

and

.

iii) For any time
t
,
q\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,
1\leq j\leq d
,
P_{j}^{q}(t)=P_{j}(t)+{\cal O}(\varepsilon)
, by perturbation theory.

Let
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{q}
and consider the unitary propagator
V_{q}(t,s)_{0\leq s,t\leq 1}
, defined as the solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}{\rm i}\varepsilon\partial_{t}V_{q}(t,s)=(H^{q}(t)+{\rm i}\varepsilon K^{q}(t))V_{q}(t,s),\\
V_{q}(s,s)=\mathbb{I},\ \ 0\leq s,t\leq 1.\hfill\end{matrix}\right.

(7.12)

As is well known, see
[
K2
,
Kr
]
,

also satisfies

V_{q}(t,s)P_{j}^{q}(s)=P_{j}^{q}(t)V_{q}(t,s),\ \ 0\leq s,t\leq 1.

Note that since

, we get that

is a smooth perturbation of

. Thus, the difference between

and

(7.15)

This identity and the previous proposition immediately yield

\|U(t,s)-V_{q}(t,s)\|\leq C_{q}|t-s|\varepsilon^{q}.

(7.16)

We improve the error term to
{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{q+1})
by performing an integration by parts on (

) (see the Appendix), at the cost of slightly altering the definition of
V_{q}
:
Let
\hat{V}_{q}(t,s)
be the unitary solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}{\rm i}\varepsilon\partial_{t}\hat{V}_{q}(t,s)=(H^{q}(t)+{\rm i}\varepsilon(K^{q}(t))+{\cal D}_{q}K^{q-1}(t))\hat{V}_{q}(t,s),\\

(7.17)

where

{\cal D}_{q}K^{q-1}(t)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}P^{q}_{j}(t)K^{q-1}(t)P^{q}_{j}(t),

(7.18)

and, by convention,

to recover

.
This allows us to get the following generalisations of (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), see
[
JP3
]

Proposition 7.3

Under assumptions
Reg
and
Spec
, for all
q\in{\mathbb{N}}

\varepsilon_{q}>0
and

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{q}(t,s)P_{j}^{q}(s)=P_{j}^{q}(t)\hat{V}_{q}(t,s)

\displaystyle\|U(t,s)-\hat{V}_{q}(t,s)\|\leq c_{q}\varepsilon^{q+1}

(7.19)

Remark 7.4

i) As a consequence, the quantum evolution follows the instantaneous subspace

Appendix: Integration by Parts

We present here a reformulation of the integration by parts argument used in
[
ASY
]
to prove the adiabatic theorem of quantum mechanics, suited to our setup.

Let
{\cal Z}
be a Banach space and assume
{\cal G}:[0,1]\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal Z})
,
{\cal K}:[0,1]\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal Z})
are bounded operator valued
C^{\infty}
functions on
[0,1]
, in the norm sense.
Let
\varepsilon>0
, and consider the two-parameter propagators
({\cal X}(t,s))_{1\leq s\leq t\leq 1}
and
({\cal Y}(t,s))_{1\leq s\leq t\leq 1}
, solution to the equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\varepsilon\partial_{t}{\cal X}(t,s)={\cal G}(t){\cal X}(t,s),\hfill\cr{\cal X}(s,s)={\mathbb{I}},\ \ 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1,\hfill\end{matrix}\right.

(8.1)

and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\varepsilon\partial_{t}{\cal Y}(t,s)=({\cal G}(t)+\varepsilon{\cal K}(t)){\cal Y}(t,s),\cr{\cal Y}(s,s)={\mathbb{I}},\ \ 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1.\hfill\end{matrix}\right.

(8.2)

The smooth propagators
{\cal X}(t,s)
and
{\cal Y}(t,s)
are determined by the corresponding Dyson series, both depend on
\varepsilon>0
with norms that diverge as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,
a priori
.
Moreover, they satisfy the integral relation

{\cal X}(t,r)={\cal Y}(t,r)-\int_{r}^{t}{\cal Y}(t,s){\cal K}(s){\cal X}(s,r)ds,\ \ \forall 1\geq t\geq r\geq 0.

(8.3)

Assume the existence of gaps in the spectrum of
{\cal G}(t)
, uniformly in
t\in[0,1]
. For
d\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

\sigma({\cal G}(t))=\cup_{1\leq j\leq d}\,\sigma_{j}(t)\subset{\mathbb{C}},\ \ \inf_{t\in[0,1],1\leq j\neq k\leq d}{\rm dist}(\sigma_{j}(t),\sigma_{k}(t))\geq G>0.

(8.4)

Consider the corresponding spectral projector

{\cal P}_{j}(t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\gamma_{j}}({\cal G}(s)-z)^{-1}dz,

(8.5)

where
\gamma_{j}
is a simple loop in
\rho({\cal G}(t))
, the resolvent set of
{\cal G}(t)
, encircling
\sigma_{j}(t)
and such that for all
k\neq j
,
\mbox{int}\gamma_{j}\cap\sigma_{k}(t)=\emptyset
.
For
{\cal B}:[0,1]\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal Z})
, a smooth bounded operator valued function, define for any
t\in[0,1]

{\cal R}_{j}({\cal B})(t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\oint_{\gamma_{j}}({\cal G}(t)-z)^{-1}{\cal B}(t)({\cal G}(t)-z)^{-1}dz,

(8.6)

with the same loop
\gamma_{j}
as in (
8.5
). This operator is smooth as well, and satisfies the identity

[{\cal G}(t),{\cal R}_{j}({\cal B})(t)]=[{\cal B}(t),{\cal P}_{j}(t)].

(8.7)

Remark 8.1

If
\sigma_{k}(t)=\{g_{k}(t)\}
for all
1\leq k\leq d
,
({\cal G}(t)-z)^{-1}=\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}{\cal P}_{k}(t)/(g_{k}(t)-z)
, and

{\cal R}_{j}({\cal B})(t)=\sum_{1\leq k\leq d\atop k\neq j}\frac{{\cal P}_{j}(t){\cal B}(t){\cal P}_{k}(t)+{\cal P}_{k}(t){\cal B}(t){\cal P}_{j}(t)}{g_{k}(t)-g_{j}(t)}.

(8.8)

Lemma 8.2

Suppose
{\cal K}(t)
is off-diagonal for all
t\in[0,1]
, i.e. s.t.
{\cal P}_{j}(t){\cal K}(t){\cal P}_{j}(t)\equiv 0

\forall 1\leq j\leq d

\displaystyle{\cal X}(t,r)

\displaystyle-{\cal Y}(t,r)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}\varepsilon\Big({\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(t){\cal X}(t,r)-{\cal Y}(t,r){\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(r)\Big)

(8.9)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}\varepsilon\int_{r}^{t}\Big\{{\cal Y}(t,s){\cal K}(s)[{\cal G}(s),{\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(s)]{\cal X}(s,r)-{\cal Y}(t,s)(\partial_{s}{\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(s)){\cal X}(s,r)\Big\}ds.

Proof:

The operator
{\cal K}
being off-diagonal and (
8.7
) give

{\cal K}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}\big[[{\cal K}(t),{\cal P}_{j}(t)\big],{\cal P}_{j}(t)]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}\big[{\cal G}(t),{\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(t)\big].

(8.10)

Hence, using (
8.3
), (

) and (

\displaystyle{\cal X}(t,r)-{\cal Y}(t,r)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}\int_{r}^{t}{\cal Y}(t,s)\big[{\cal G}(s),{\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(s)\big]{\cal X}(s,r)ds

(8.11)

where, for each integral in the summand

\displaystyle{\cal G}(s),{\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(s)]{\cal X}(s,r)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{r}^{t}\Big\{(\partial_{s}Y(t,s)){\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(s){\cal X}(s,r)+{\cal Y}(t,s){\cal K}(s)\big[{\cal G}(s),{\cal R}_{j}([{\cal K},{\cal P}_{j}])(s)\big]{\cal X}(s,r)

Thanks to the smoothness of all operators in the integrand, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}{\cal Y}(t,s))

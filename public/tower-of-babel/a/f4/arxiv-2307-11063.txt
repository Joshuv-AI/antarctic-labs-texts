Self-Dual Fields on Self-Dual Backgrounds
and the Double Copy

Abstract




QMUL-PH-23-21

We explore the double copy for self-dual gauge and gravitational fields on self-dual background spacetimes. We consider backgrounds associated to solutions of the second Plebanski equation and describe results with different gauge-fixing conditions. Finally we discuss the kinematic and
w
-algebras and the double copy, identifying modified Poisson structures and kinematic structure constants in the presence of the self-dual background. The self-dual plane wave and Eguchi-Hanson spacetimes are studied as examples and their respective
w
-algebras derived.

2
Flat background

We start by setting notation and briefly recalling the standard results for self-dual Yang-Mills (YM) and self-dual gravity in a flat background. In this section, we will generally follow the discussion in
[
34
]
.
The spacetime coordinates are taken to be
(u,v,X,Y)
, with the metric

ds^{2}=2du\,dv-2dXdY.

(2.1)

For real coordinates, this implies we are using
(2,2)
signature. The coordinates
(u,v,X,Y)
are related to the usual
(t,x,y,z)
as follows

\begin{split}u=\frac{t+z}{\sqrt{2}},\quad v=\frac{t-z}{\sqrt{2}},\quad X=\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}},\quad Y=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}}\end{split}

(2.2)

and in terms of the coordinates
(t,x,y,z)
the metric signature is
(+,-,+,-)
.

2.1
Self-dual Yang-Mills

A gauge field
A_{\mu}=(A_{u},A_{v},A_{X},A_{Y})
on flat space with metric (
2.1
)
is self-dual if its field strength
2

2

In our conventions
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}+[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]

satisfies

F_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\sqrt{g}}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\,.

(2.3)

where

is the determinant of the metric (
2.1
).
Imposing the gauge-fixing condition
A_{v}=0
, the self-duality condition above can be satisfied by setting
A_{X}=0
and

\begin{split}A_{u}&=\phi_{X},\\
A_{Y}&=\phi_{v},\end{split}

(2.4)

for a (Lie algebra valued) function
\phi(u,v,X,Y)
satisfying the self-dual Yang-Mills equation

\Box\phi-2[\phi_{v},\phi_{X}]=0,

(2.5)

where the scalar Laplacian is
\Box=2(\partial_{u}\partial_{v}-\partial_{X}\partial_{Y})
.
In what follows, we will be using a notation where subscripts on scalar fields such as

\phi_{v}=\partial_{v}\phi,\phi_{Xv}=\partial_{X}\partial_{v}\phi

If we introduce the Poisson bracket

\{f,g\}=\partial_{v}f\partial_{X}g-\partial_{X}f\partial_{v}g,

(2.6)

then the self-dual Yang-Mills equation becomes

\Box\phi-[\{\phi,\phi\}]=0,

(2.7)

where we have used a notation suggestive of colour-kinematics duality, as used in

]

[\{f,g\}]:=[f_{v},g_{X}]-[f_{X},g_{v}]\,.

(2.8)

For the covector
k_{\mu}=(k_{u},k_{v},k_{X},k_{Y})
and coordinate vector
x^{\mu}=(u,v,X,Y)
, with
k\cdot x:=k_{\mu}x^{\mu}
, the plane wave
e^{ik\cdot x}
satisfies

\Box\,e^{ik\cdot x}=0

(2.9)

if
k_{\mu}
is a null vector.
In momentum space, the cubic coupling arising from the self-dual YM equation (
2.7
) involves the kinematic structure constants

X(k_{1},k_{2})=k_{1X}k_{2v}-k_{1v}k_{2X},

(2.10)

along with the Lie algebra constants
f^{abc}
.
Explicitly, the bracket (
2.8
) of two plane waves and Lie algebra generators satisfies
[
34
]

[\{T^{a}e^{ik_{1}\cdot x},T^{b}e^{ik_{2}\cdot x}\}]=X(k_{1},k_{2})f^{abc}\,T^{c}e^{i(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}.

(2.11)

There is also an alternative gauge-fixing condition
A_{u}=0
, for which the self-duality condition can be satisfied by setting
A_{Y}=0
and

\begin{split}A_{v}&=\phi_{Y},\\
A_{X}&=\phi_{u},\end{split}

(2.12)

for a function
\phi(u,v,X,Y)
satisfying the self-dual YM equation (
2.5
) but with the coordinates
u\leftrightarrow v
and
Y\leftrightarrow X
exchanged, that is

\Box\phi-2[\phi_{u},\phi_{Y}]=0.

(2.13)

The flat metric is invariant under this exchange and so results obtained in this new gauge are trivially related to the previous gauge by a simple interchange of coordinates. This is not the case when we consider self-dual backgrounds in the sections below, since these backgrounds have no such symmetry, and we will describe the two different gauges separately.

2.2
Self-dual gravity

Now we recall the analogous construction for self-dual gravity, where the metric is taken to be the following variation from the flat metric:

ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}(\Psi)dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=2dudv-2dXdY+\Psi_{XX}du^{2}+\Psi_{vv}dY^{2}+2\Psi_{Xv}dudY,

(2.14)

with some function
\Psi(u,v,X,Y)
.
Define the expression

{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Psi):=2(\Psi_{uv}-\Psi_{XY})-\Psi_{XX}\Psi_{vv}+(\Psi_{vX})^{2},

(2.15)

where the subscript
0
indicates the flat background, and define the operator
\Delta_{0}(\Psi)
by the variation of this expression as

\Delta_{0}(\Psi)(\delta\Psi):=\delta({\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Psi)).

(2.16)

Explicitly

\Delta_{0}(\Psi)=2(\partial_{uv}-\partial_{XY})-\Psi_{XX}\partial_{vv}+2\Psi_{vX}\partial_{vX}-\Psi_{vv}\partial_{XX}.

(2.17)

Then the anti-self-dual part of the Weyl tensor is zero except for the component
C^{-}_{uYuY}
(and components related to this by the symmetries of the tensor), and we find

C^{-}_{uYuY}=-\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{0}(\Psi){\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Psi).

(2.18)

The non-vanishing components of the Ricci tensor are given by

R_{ab}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\bar{a}}\partial_{\bar{b}}{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Psi).

(2.19)

where
a,b=(u,Y)
and
\bar{u}=X,\bar{Y}=v
. Thus the metric
g_{\mu\nu}(\Psi)
given by (
2.14
) is Ricci-flat and has self-dual Weyl tensor if the scalar field
\Psi
satisfies the gravitational Plebanski equation

{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Psi)=2(\Psi_{uv}-\Psi_{XY})-\Psi_{XX}\Psi_{vv}+(\Psi_{vX})^{2}=0.

(2.20)

Defining the following gravitational bracket
\{\{\cdot,\cdot\}\}
using (
2.6
)

\{\{f,g\}\}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\{\partial_{v}f,\partial_{X}g\}-\{\partial_{X}f,\partial_{v}g\}\Big)\,,

(2.21)

the Plebanski equation (
2.20
) can be written

\Box\Psi-\{\{\Psi,\Psi\}\}=0,

(2.22)

revealing the double copy relation
[
34
]

\phi\rightarrow\Psi,

[\{\cdot,\cdot\}]\rightarrow\{\{\cdot,\cdot\}\}
compared with (
2.7
). Furthermore, we can consider the gravitational bracket acting on a pair of plane wave solutions in flat space and we find the following double copy structure

\{\{e^{ik_{1}\cdot x},e^{ik_{2}\cdot x}\}\}=\frac{1}{2}e^{i(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}X(k_{1},k_{2})^{2}\,,

(2.23)

where the colour structure constants in (
2.11
) have been replaced by additional kinematic ones. Alternatively, from (
2.11

\{e^{ik_{1}\cdot x},e^{ik_{2}\cdot x}\}=e^{i(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}X(k_{1},k_{2})\,.

(2.24)

As explained in
[
57
]

X(k_{1},k_{2})
in both YM and gravity implies the chirality of the operator product expansion in both cases. The second ‘right structure constants’ ,
f^{abc}
in the YM case and the second copy of

X(k_{1},k_{2})
in the gravity case, correspond to the structure constants of the OPEs. The soft expansion of the latter may be explored by noting that
the null vector condition
k^{2}=2(k_{u}k_{v}-k_{X}k_{Y})=0
implies that
we may set

\frac{k_{u}}{k_{X}}=\frac{k_{Y}}{k_{v}}=\rho

(2.25)

for some
\rho

k\cdot x=(\rho Y+v)k_{v}+(\rho u+X)k_{X}\,.

(2.26)

The soft limit of the momentum

(k_{v},k_{x})\rightarrow 0

e^{ik\cdot x}=\sum_{a,b=0}^{\infty}\frac{(ik_{v})^{a}(ik_{X})^{b}}{a!b!}e_{ab},

(2.27)

where the ‘soft mode generators’ are given by
e_{ab}=(\rho Y+v)^{a}(\rho u+X)^{b}

k_{1},

k_{2}
appearing in the algebra (
2.24

(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})\rightarrow 0

and
k_{2}
collinear since

k_{1}\cdot k_{2}=(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})X(k_{1},k_{2})\,.

(2.28)

At leading order in the holomorphic collinear limit (corresponding to the first term in the OPE expansion in the celestial holography context) we may set
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=\rho
and substitute the expansion (
2.27

2.24

\{e_{a,b},e_{c,d}\}=(ad-bc)e_{a+c-1,b+d-1}.

(2.29)

Defining the conventional generators

\{w_{m}^{p},w_{n}^{q}\}=\Big(m(q-1)-n(p-1)\Big)w_{m+n}^{p+q-2}.

(2.30)

The conditions that

As in the YM case, there is another gauge-fixing condition related to the above by interchanging

Our first exploration of self-dual perturbations of self-dual backgrounds starts with YM backgrounds in flat space. To begin with we consider a background self-dual gauge field

which is simply the scalar Laplacian in the background gauge field
A(\chi)
. The covariant derivative is given by

3

3

This is consistent with our conventions for the field strength since

\psi
.

D_{\chi}^{\mu}\coloneqq\partial^{\mu}+[A^{\mu}(\chi),\cdot]\,,

(3.5)

when acting on an adjoint valued field.

General self-dual background spacetimes

We now turn to generalising the above results in section

The hatted momenta are those in the tangent space - if we write the vierbein

Self-dual Yang-Mills

With a gauge field
A_{\mu}=(A_{u},A_{v},A_{X},A_{Y})

A_{v}=0.

\Box_{\Phi}\phi-2[\phi_{v},\phi_{X}]=0,

The above conditions imply that the flat Laplacian acting on

and as before replacing the unhatted
k
’s in this expression by coordinate derivatives with respect to the two functions in the Poisson bracket, i.e.

\{f,g\}_{\Phi}=\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi)(f)\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi)(g)-\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi)(f)\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi)(g)\,,

(4.15)

or

\begin{split}\{u,v\}_{\Phi}&=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{Xv},\quad\{u,X\}_{\Phi}=\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{vv},\quad\{u,Y\}_{\Phi}=1,\\
\{v,X\}_{\Phi}&=\frac{1}{4}\Big(\Phi_{Xv}^{2}-\Phi_{XX}\Phi_{vv}\Big),\quad\{v,Y\}_{\Phi}=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{XX},\quad\{X,Y\}_{\Phi}=\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{Xv}.\end{split}

(4.16)

The Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket
\{\,,\,\}_{\Phi}
is satisfied since the self-dual background
\Phi
satisfies the Plebanski equation
{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi)=0
. Furthermore, since the Kerr-Schild condition
\Phi_{Xv}^{2}-\Phi_{XX}\Phi_{vv}=0
is satisfied the bracket
\{v,X\}
vanishes. The symplectic form connected with the Poisson bracket (
4.15
) is

\omega=\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{Xv}(du\wedge dv-dX\wedge dY)-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{vv}\,dv\wedge dY+\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{XX}du\wedge dX+du\wedge dY\,.

(4.17)

We observe that
\omega^{2}=0
and is closed,
d\omega=0
(c.f.
[
88
]
) when the Kerr-Schild condition and background Plebanski equation are satisfied.

Using the notation

[\{f,g\}]_{\Phi}:=\Big[\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi)(f),\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi)(g)\Big]-\Big[\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi)(f),\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi)(g)\Big],

(4.18)

the condition (
4.14
) on the field
\phi
may then be written

\Box_{\Phi}\phi-[\{\phi,\phi\}]_{\Phi}=0.

(4.19)

4.2
Self-dual gravity

We now consider self-dual gravity perturbations on the background metric in (
4.1
). That is we simply consider the shifted metric
g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi+\Psi)
given by (
4.1
) with
\Phi
replaced by
\Phi+\Psi
. We take the metric
g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi)
to be the background self-dual spacetime, with
{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi)=0
. This setup corresponds to the so called ‘matched gauge’ for the gravity perturbation.

We can then define the gravitational Plebanski function in the background metric
g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi)
by

{\rm Pleb}_{\Phi}(\Psi)\coloneqq\Box_{\Phi}\Psi+\Psi_{Xv}^{2}-\Psi_{XX}\Psi_{vv},

(4.20)

with
\Box_{\Phi}
the scalar Laplacian in the background metric. Once again this can be written in terms of the double bracket notation (
2.21
)

{\rm Pleb}_{\Phi}(\Psi)=\Box_{\Phi}\Psi-\{\{\Psi,\Psi\}\}\,,

(4.21)

illustrating the double copy structure compared with eqn. (
4.9
). Alternatively, (
4.21
) can be viewed as the double copy of (
3
), where we double copy both the YM background and the perturbation.
Now one can check that the Plebanski equation satisfies the following identity

{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi+\Psi)={\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi)+{\rm Pleb}_{\Phi}(\Psi).

(4.22)

This immediately gives the gravitational Plebanski equation in the background metric as simply

{\rm Pleb}_{\Phi}(\Psi)=0.

(4.23)

This follows since the identity (
4.22
) shows that if
\Phi
leads to a self-dual metric then
\Phi+\Psi
does as well if the Plebanski equation for
\Psi
in a
\Phi
metric background (
4.23
) is satisfied. The above conclusions can be confirmed explicitly. The relevant non-trivial component of the anti-self-dual part of the Weyl tensor for the metric
g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi+\Psi)
is given by

\begin{split}C^{-}_{uYuY}(g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi+\Psi))&=-\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{0}(\Phi+\Psi){\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi+\Psi))\\
&=-\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{0}(\Phi+\Psi)({\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi)+{\rm Pleb}_{\Phi}(\Psi))\\
&=-\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{0}(\Phi+\Psi)({\rm Pleb}_{\Phi}(\Psi))\\
&=0\end{split}

(4.24)

where we have used the self-duality of the background metric, with
{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi)=0
, and imposed the condition (
4.23
).
It is also immediate that the variations of the Plebanski function (
4.20
) are related to variations of the flat Plebanski function - if we define the variation

\Delta_{\Phi}(\Psi)(\delta\Psi):=\delta_{\Psi}({\rm Pleb}_{\Phi}(\Psi))\,,

(4.25)

then as differential operators

\Delta_{\Phi}(\Psi)=\Delta_{0}(\Phi+\Psi)\,,

(4.26)

as expected. A similar argument, based on (
2.19
), shows Ricci-flatness of the shifted metric.

5
The self-dual plane wave spacetime

Plane wave backgrounds have been the object of some interest recently in the area of amplitudes, kinematic algebras and the double copy (see, for example,
[
58
,
59
,
83
,
84
,
89
,
90
,
91
]

and references therein).
Here we study the self-dual plane wave metric

ds^{2}_{PW}=2dudv-2dXdY+2F(v)dY^{2},

(5.1)

where
F(v)
is a function related to the wave profile.

This metric is an example of the general form (
4.1
) considered earlier and is also Kerr-Schild, we simply set
\Phi=\Phi(v)
with
\Phi_{vv}=2F(v)
.
The self-dual plane wave metric is Ricci-flat and has self-dual Weyl tensor; the only non-vanishing components of the self-dual part of the Weyl tensor being
C_{vYvY}=-2F^{\prime\prime}[v]
and those related to this by the symmetries of this tensor.

5.1
Self-dual Yang-Mills

A self-dual gauge field in the ‘matched gauge’ on this spacetime is given by

A_{\mu}=(A_{u},A_{v},A_{X},A_{Y})=(\phi_{X},0,0,\phi_{v}),

(5.2)

where, in order to solve the self-duality conditions, the scalar field
\phi(u,v,X,Y)
must satisfy the plane wave background Plebanski equation

\Box_{PW}\phi-2[\phi_{v},\phi_{X}]=0,

(5.3)

with
\Box_{PW}
the Laplacian in the metric (
5.1
).
Using the Poisson bracket
\{f,g\}=f_{v}g_{X}-f_{X}g_{v}
which is the same as the flat space case, we can write (
5.3
) as

\Box_{PW}\phi-[\{\phi,\phi\}]=0,

(5.4)

where the double bracket notation (
2.8
) is defined as usual.

There is also the ‘flipped’ self-dual gauge field solution in this background, from (
4.13
) which can be used to elucidate the algebraic structure of self-dual perturbations on the self-dual plane wave background. We find

A_{\mu}=\big(0,\phi_{Y}+F(v)\phi_{X},\phi_{u},-F(v)\phi_{u}\big),

(5.5)

where the field
\phi
satisfies

\Box_{PW}\phi-2[\phi_{u},\phi_{Y}+F(v)\phi_{X}]=0.

(5.6)

This leads us to the modified Poisson bracket in the plane wave background

\{f,g\}_{PW}=f_{u}\big(g_{Y}+F(v)g_{X}\big)-\big(f_{Y}+F(v)f_{X}\big)g_{u},

(5.7)

and the re-writing of (
5.6
) as

\Box_{PW}\phi-[\{\phi,\phi\}]_{PW}=0.

(5.8)

We are now tasked with finding the analogue of plane wave solutions to the wave equation in flat space, but for solutions to the wave equation in the background (
5.1
). Such solutions then act as generators of our kinematic Poisson algebra. We begin by constructing a null vector in flat space
k_{\mu}
satisfying
k_{u}k_{v}-k_{X}k_{Y}=0
so that (as before)
k_{u}=\rho k_{X},k_{Y}=\rho k_{v}
for some
\rho
. Then for the function
G(v)
given by the indefinite integral of
F(v)
, ie
G^{\prime}=F
, we may define the quantity

\begin{split}Q_{k}(u,v,X,Y)&\coloneqq(\rho Y+v)k_{v}+(\rho u+X)k_{X}+\frac{1}{\rho}G(v)k_{X}\\
&=k\cdot x+\frac{1}{\rho}G(v)k_{X}\,.\end{split}

(5.9)

Then one can show that the vector
K_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}Q_{k}
is null,
K^{\mu}K_{\mu}=0
, divergence free,
\nabla^{\mu}K_{\mu}=0
(which is just the wave equation on
Q_{k}
), and geodesic,
K^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}K_{\mu}=0
, where
\nabla_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative in the plane wave metric. One consequence is that any function of
Q_{k}
is annihilated by the Laplacian, in particular

\Box_{PW}e^{iQ_{k}(u,v,X,Y)}=0.

(5.10)

Whence the function
e^{iQ_{k}(u,v,X,Y)}
satisfies the wave equation in the plane wave background and furthermore reduces to the usual plane wave
e^{ik\cdot x}
in the flat space limit.
The Poisson bracket of two of these solutions is

\begin{split}\{e^{iQ_{1}},e^{iQ_{2}}\}&=e^{i(Q_{1}+Q_{2})}(k_{1X}k_{2v}-k_{1v}k_{2X}+k_{1X}k_{2X}\frac{(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})}{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}F(v))\\
&=:e^{i(Q_{1}+Q_{2})}X_{PW}(k_{1},k_{2}),\end{split}

(5.11)

leading to a modification of the structure constants defining the kinematic algebra compared to the flat space case. This modification is however sub-leading in the holomorphic collinear limit so we expect it to not alter the
w
-algebra, which we confirm in the next section.

This result, and hence also the
w
-algebra in (
5.20
), also holds if one uses the flipped gauge Poisson bracket (
5.7
), although in that case it is more natural to write the function (
5.9
) in terms of
k_{u}
and
k_{Y}
as follows

Q_{k}(u,v,X,Y)=(Y+\tilde{\rho}v)k_{Y}+(u+\tilde{\rho}X)k_{u}+\tilde{\rho}^{2}G(v)k_{u}

(5.12)

where
\tilde{\rho}\coloneqq 1/\rho
. The flipped Poisson bracket of two plane waves is then

\begin{split}\{e^{iQ_{1}},e^{iQ_{2}}\}_{PW}&=e^{i(Q_{1}+Q_{2})}(k_{1Y}k_{2u}-k_{1u}k_{2Y}+k_{1u}k_{2u}(\tilde{\rho}_{1}-\tilde{\rho}_{2})F(v))\\
&=e^{i(Q_{1}+Q_{2})}(-\rho_{1}\rho_{2})X_{PW}(k_{1},k_{2})\,.\end{split}

(5.13)

5.2
Self-dual gravity

The gravitational analogue of the discussion above in the ‘matched gauge’ is based on the metric

ds^{2}_{PWG}=2dudv-2dXdY+2F(v)dY^{2}+\Psi_{vv}dY^{2}+\Psi_{XX}du^{2}+2\Psi_{vX}dudY.

(5.14)

This metric has vanishing Ricci tensor and self-dual Weyl tensor if the following Plebanski equation is satisfied:

\Box_{PW}\Psi+\Psi_{vX}^{2}-\Psi_{vv}\Psi_{XX}=0,

(5.15)

where
\Box_{PW}
is the Laplacian in the self-dual background.
Employing the double bracket notation (
2.21
) as usual we have

\Box_{PW}\Psi-\{\{\Psi,\Psi\}\}=0,

(5.16)

revealing the double copy structure compared with (
5.3
).

If we apply the double bracket to two of the ‘plane waves’ (
5.9
) we find

\{\{e^{iQ_{1}},e^{iQ_{2}}\}\}=e^{i(Q_{1}+Q_{2})}\frac{1}{2}\bigg(X_{PW}(k_{1},k_{2})^{2}-\frac{ik_{1X}k_{2X}(k_{1X}\rho_{1}+k_{2X}\rho_{2})F^{\prime}(v)}{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}\bigg)

(5.17)

which is not just the simple square of the relation (
5.11
). Despite this, we can still derive a
w-
algebra as follows. Similarly to the flat space case, we may expand the above solutions to the wave equation in powers of soft momenta variables
k_{v},k_{X}
to find

e^{iQ_{k}(u,v,X,Y)}=\sum_{a,b=0}^{\infty}\frac{(ik_{v})^{a}(ik_{X})^{b}}{a!b!}e_{ab}\,,

(5.18)

where we have defined
e_{ab}=(\rho Y+v)^{a}(\rho u+X+\frac{1}{\rho}G(v))^{b}
in the self-dual plane wave background. Defining the modified
w
generators

w^{p}_{m}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}e_{p-1+m,p-1-m}=\frac{1}{2}(\rho Y+v)^{p-1+m}(\rho u+X+\frac{1}{\rho}G(v))^{p-1-m}\,,

(5.19)

in analogy with the flat space case. We recover the standard
w_{1+\infty}
-algebra for these modified generators, working to leading order in the holomorphic collinear limit

\{w_{m}^{p},w_{n}^{q}\}=\Big(m(q-1)-n(p-1)\Big)w_{m+n}^{p+q-2}\,.

(5.20)

We may also consider the ‘flipped gauge’ with its modified Poisson bracket (
5.7
) which satisfies a double copy relation analogous to (
2.23
) acting on two solutions
e^{iQ_{k}(u,v,X,Y)}
. First we define a modified double bracket

\{\{f,g\}\}_{PW}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\{f_{u},g_{Y}+F(v)g_{X}\}_{PW}-\{f_{Y}+F(v)f_{X},g_{u}\}_{PW}\Big)\,,

(5.21)

then we find the expected double copy of (
5.13
), that is

\{\{e^{iQ_{1}},e^{iQ_{2}}\}\}_{PW}=\frac{1}{2}e^{i(Q_{1}+Q_{2})}(-\rho_{1}\rho_{2}X_{PW}(k_{1},k_{2}))^{2}.

(5.22)

Interestingly, in contrast to the matched double bracket (
5.17
) of plane waves, the above does exhibit a simple squaring relation when compared to the single bracket (
5.13
). As mentioned before, we can also define analogous soft generators
\tilde{w}_{m}^{p}
in the flipped gauge, now as coefficients of
k_{u}^{a}
and
k_{Y}^{b}
. One can then show that these generators also satisfy the
w_{1+\infty}
algebra (
5.20
), but now with the bracket (
5.7
).

6
The Eguchi-Hanson spacetime

We now move on to consider a more complicated example, the Eguchi-Hanson space-time. This is self-dual, and in the form (
4.11
) has the scalar function

\Phi_{EH}=\frac{mv^{2}}{2Y^{2}(uv-XY)},

(6.1)

with
m
a constant, satisfying the Plebanski equation in flat space

{\rm Pleb}_{0}(\Phi_{EH})=0.

(6.2)

The full metric is then

\begin{split}ds^{2}_{EH}&=g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi_{EH})dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}\\
&=2dudv-2dXdY+\frac{mv^{2}}{(uv-XY)^{3}}du^{2}+\frac{mX^{2}}{(uv-XY)^{3}}dY^{2}-\frac{2mvX}{(uv-XY)^{3}}dudY\end{split}

(6.3)

and satisfies the Kerr-Schild condition.

We now repeat the methods laid out for the general case and the plane wave example but now with the function
\Phi_{EH}
. We will encounter a much richer algebraic structure than was found in the self-dual plane wave background, reproducing in spacetime some of the results recently described via twistor space in
[
85
]
.

6.1
Self-dual Yang-Mills

Consider firstly self-dual Yang-Mills in an Eguchi-Hanson background. From the results earlier, a gauge field
A_{\mu}
in the ‘matched gauge’
A_{v}=0
has self-dual field strength if in addition
A_{X}=0,A_{u}=\phi_{X}
and

A_{Y}=\phi_{v}
, with
\phi
satisfying the Plebanski equation in the EH background

\Box_{EH}\phi-2[\phi_{v},\phi_{X}]=0,

(6.4)

where
\Box_{EH}
is the Laplacian in the metric (
6.3
). In the case at hand, the EH Plebanski equation can be written in terms of the flat space Poisson bracket (
2.6
) as

\Box_{EH}\phi-[\{\phi,\phi\}]=0\,.

(6.5)

The alternative ‘flipped gauge’ (
4.13
) in the Eguchi-Hanson case comes from the null vector
m^{\mu}=(1,0,0,v/X)
and gauge-fixing condition
m^{\mu}A_{\mu}=0
and sets

\begin{split}A_{u}&=\frac{mv}{2(uv-XY)^{3}}\Big(v\phi_{Y}+X\phi_{u}\Big),\\
A_{v}&=\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi_{EH})(\phi)=\phi_{Y}+\frac{mX}{2(uv-XY)^{3}}\Big(X\phi_{X}+v\phi_{v}\Big),\\
A_{X}&=\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi_{EH})(\phi)=\phi_{u}-\frac{mv}{2(uv-XY)^{3}}\Big(X\phi_{X}+v\phi_{v}\Big),\\
A_{Y}&=-\frac{mX}{2(uv-XY)^{3}}\Big(v\phi_{Y}+X\phi_{u}\Big).\end{split}

(6.6)

This gauge field has self-dual field strength if the scalar field
\phi
satisfies

\Box_{EH}\phi-2\Big[\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi_{EH})(\phi)\,,\,\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi_{EH})(\phi)\Big]=0.

(6.7)

Using the notation

[\{f,g\}]_{EH}:=\Big[f_{u}-\frac{mv}{2(uv-XY)^{3}}\big(Xf_{X}+vf_{v}\big)\,,\,g_{Y}+\frac{mX}{2(uv-XY)^{3}}\big(Xg_{X}+vg_{v}\big)\Big]+(f\leftrightarrow g),

(6.9)

\begin{split}\{f,g\}_{EH}&=\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi_{EH})(f)\,\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi_{EH})(g)-\hat{k}_{Y}(\Phi_{EH})(f)\,\hat{k}_{u}(\Phi_{EH})(g)\,,\\
\end{split}

(6.10)

and we note that the terms quadratic in

To find the equivalent of plane wave solutions in the EH background we introduce a null co-vector
k_{\mu}=(k_{u},k_{v},k_{X},k_{Y})
whose components satisfy
k_{u}k_{v}=k_{X}k_{Y}
so that as before we may write

\frac{k_{u}}{k_{X}}=\frac{k_{Y}}{k_{v}}=\rho

(6.11)

for some parameter
\rho

e^{ik\cdot x}
plane wave in the flat space limit
m\rightarrow 0

Following

\begin{split}R_{k}(u,v,X,Y)&\coloneqq(k\cdot x)^{2}-\frac{m(vk_{v}+Xk_{X})^{2}}{2(uv-XY)^{2}}\\
&=((\rho Y+v)k_{v}+(\rho u+X)k_{X})^{2}-\frac{m(vk_{v}+Xk_{X})^{2}}{2(uv-XY)^{2}}.\end{split}

(6.12)

Then the vector

is null,
K^{\mu}K_{\mu}=0
, divergence free,
\nabla^{\mu}K_{\mu}=0
(which is just the wave equation on
R_{k}

K^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}K_{\mu}=0
, where
\nabla_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative in the EH metric.
One consequence is that any function of

is annihilated by the Laplacian, in particular

\Box_{\Phi}e^{i\sqrt{R_{k}(u,v,X,Y)}}=0,

(6.13)

e^{i\sqrt{R_{k}(u,v,X,Y)}}
gives the standard plane wave
e^{ik\cdot x}
in the flat space limit
m\rightarrow 0

R_{k}

k_{\mu}
, versus

6.2
Self-dual gravity

For the case of self-dual gravity, a perturbation of the EH metric in the matched gauge is given by
g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi_{EH}+\Psi)=g_{\mu\nu}(\Phi_{EH})+g_{\mu\nu}(\Psi)
has vanishing anti-self-dual components of the Weyl tensor except for

C^{EH-}_{uYuY}=-\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\Phi_{EH}}(\Psi)({\rm Pleb}_{\Phi_{EH}}(\Psi)).

(6.16)

Thus, the perturbed EH metric has self-dual Weyl tensor if the EH Plebanski equation is satisfied. The EH Plebanski equation for self-dual gravity in this case is given by

{\rm Pleb_{\Phi_{EH}}}(\Psi)=\Box_{EH}\Psi-\Psi_{XX}\Psi_{vv}+(\Psi_{vX})^{2}=\Box_{EH}\Psi-\{\{\Psi,\Psi\}\}=0\,,

(6.17)

using the double bracket (

Similarly for the Ricci tensor one finds that its components vanish except for

with

and for these components

R^{EH}_{ab}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\bar{a}}\partial_{\bar{b}}{\rm Pleb}_{\Phi_{EH}}(\Psi),

(6.18)

where
\bar{u}=X,\bar{Y}=v
.

The non-trivial form of the single bracket (
6.14
) suggests that the double copy, realised by using a double bracket, may involve more than just the square of
X(k_{1},k_{2})

\{\{e^{i\sqrt{R_{1}}},e^{i\sqrt{R_{2}}}\}\}=\frac{1}{2}e^{i(\sqrt{R_{1}}+\sqrt{R_{2}})}X_{EH}(k_{1},k_{2})^{2}+\dots,

7
Conclusions

We have studied the self-duality of gauge and gravitational fields on the self-dual background spacetimes defined by solutions of Plebanski’s second equation. In light-cone gauges we showed that the conditions for self-duality could be reduced to second order scalar equations generalising the flat space equations. We found two classes of general solutions.
One, which we called a ‘matched’ gauge, was a direct generalisation of the flat space solutions to the curved self-dual backgrounds under consideration. The other involves a Kerr-Schild condition on the gravitational background, which we called the ‘flipped’ gauge, and can be seen as the curved space versions of ‘flipped’ flat space solutions. We discussed the double copy and kinematic algebra in these two cases. Finally, we studied two examples in more detail - the self-dual plane wave spacetime
and the Eguchi-Hanson (EH) metric - connecting with some recent results from
[
84
]
and
[
85
]
, and noting that in the EH background the kinematic algebra squaring relations are modified by curvature terms.

There are a number of avenues of research which follow from this. It would be interesting to explore more examples in detail, and investigate perturbative solutions to the equations
where direct solutions prove difficult. Gravitational analogues of the ‘flipped’
gauge self-dual YM solution, eqn. (
4.13
) could be studied further, in general and in particular examples. Plebanski-type conditions of the generic form
\Box\phi-\{\{\phi,\phi\}\}=0
for the different double brackets given above would be expected to feature. In radiative spacetimes this should connect with the very recent analysis of self-dual deformations in
[
84
]
, which relates these to twistor sigma models and MHV generating functionals.
It would also be interesting to explore applications to known deformations of the Plebanski equations such as those involving Moyal brackets (c.f.

[
12
,
85
]
and references therein). The application of the formalism used recently for self-dual YM in
[
92
]
could also be explored in self-dual backgrounds.

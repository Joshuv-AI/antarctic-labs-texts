Phase diagram of the Hubbard model on a square lattice: A cluster slave-spin Study
Ming-Huan Zeng, Tianxing Ma, Y.-J. Wang

Abstract
The cluster slave-spin method is employed to investigate systematically the ground state properties of the Hubbard model on a square lattice with doping δ \delta and coupling strength U U being its parameters. At half-filling, a relation between the staggered magnetization M M and the antiferromagnetic (AFM) gap Δ AFM \Delta_{\text{AFM}} is established in the small U U limit to compare with that from the Hartree-Fock theory, and a first-order metal-insulator Mott transition in the paramagnetic state is substantiated, which is characterized by discontinuities and hystereses at U Mott = 10 ​ t U_{\text{Mott}}=10t . The interaction U c U_{c} for the crossover in the AFM state, separating the weak- and strong-coupling regimes, is found to remain almost unchanged with large dopings, and smaller than U Mott U_{\text{Mott}} at half-filling because of long range AFM correlations. Finally, an overall phase diagram in the U U - δ \delta plane is presented, which is composed of four regimes: the AFM insulator at half-filling, the AFM metal with the compressibility κ > 0 \kappa>0 or κ < 0 \kappa<0 , and the paramagnetic metal, as well as three phase transitions: (i) From the AFM metal to the paramagnetic metal, (ii) between the AFM metal phases with positive and negative κ \kappa , and (iii) separating the AFM insulating phase at δ = 0 \delta=0 from the AFM metal phase for δ > 0 \delta>0 .

IV
CONCLUSION

In summary, the cluster slave-spin method has been employed to investigate systematically the ground state properties
of the single-band 2D Hubbard model on a square lattice in the parameter space of
U
and
\delta
. We substantiated that the system presents
a broad crossover between the weak- and strong-coupling regimes: (i) The
\Delta_{\text{AFM}}

increases monotonically with
U
in the weak-coupling regime, while decreases for large couplings
Lee and Lee 2017
.
(ii) The AFM in the weak-coupling regime is stabilized by the interaction potential gain, while that for large
U
is supported by
the kinetic energy gain
Qin et al. 2021
;
Tocchio et al. 2016
;
Fratino et al. 2017a
. In Fig.
15
,
the
U_{c}
marked by the red dashed line which is close to
U_{\text{Mott}}=10t
, signifying that the underlying
Mott transition in the half-filled PM state dominates the properties of the AFM states by changing into a
crossover
Tocchio et al. 2016
;
Fratino et al. 2017a
;
Fratino et al. 2017b
between the weak- and strong-coupling regimes.
It is worthy mentioning that
U_{c}
at half-filling is smaller than
U_{\text{Mott}}
because of
long range AFM correlations
Park et al. 2008
.

For the half-filled system, we analytically calculated in Eq. (
24
) the relation between
M
and

\Delta_{\text{AFM}}
in the small
U
limit, implying that the one from the HF method needs to be
improved to include quantum fluctuations. After fitting the AFM gap of the cluster slave-spin data using the
formula from the QMC simulation
Hirsch 1985
, and calculating the dependence of
M
upon
U
by
Eq. (
24
), we find the result is in a good agreement with the numerical data
as shown in Fig.
8
.

The
U
-
\delta
phase diagram was discussed in Sec.
III.3
, from which we extracted four regimes: AFM insulator
at
\delta=0
, AFM metal with
\kappa>0
and
\kappa<0
, and the PM metal. The second-order transition occurs when the
system transits from the AFM metal with
\kappa<0
to the PM metal phase, whereas the existence of the transition from
the AFM metal phase with
\kappa>0
to that with
\kappa<0
needs to be proven further by taking the inhomogeneous
states into account. Moreover, as shown in Figs.
12
and
15
, the crossover,
separating the weak- and strong- coupling regimes in the AFM state, always locates around
U_{\text{Mott}}
,
and whether this property is specifically associated with the geometry of the square lattice is worthy of
subsequent investigations.

According to Figs.
2
and
9
, the difference between the two- and four-site
cluster results is rather quantitative than qualitative. We believe that the four-site
cluster approximation is sufficient to capture the physics because its geometry obeys the same lattice
symmetry as the original square lattice, which is very important to extract the reasonable solutions of the system. This
conclusion can be reached in our ongoing studies on the honeycomb lattice.

Appendix A
RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE HOLON-DOUBLON CORRELATOR AND THE MOTT TRANSITION

The holon-doublon correlator
Lee and Lee 2017
is defined as

C_{ij}=\frac{\langle N_{i}D_{j}\rangle-\langle N_{i}\rangle\langle D_{j}\rangle}{\langle N_{i}\rangle\langle D_{j}\rangle}\;,

(30)

where

N_{i}=(1-c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma})(1-c^{\dagger}_{i\bar{\sigma}}c_{i\bar{\sigma}}),\hskip 5.0ptD_{j}=c^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}c_{j\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{j\bar{\sigma}}c_{j\bar{\sigma}}\;,

(31)

which can be approximately factorized as

\langle c^{\dagger}_{1}c_{2}c^{\dagger}_{3}c_{4}\rangle\approx\langle c^{\dagger}_{1}c_{2}\rangle\langle c^{\dagger}_{3}c_{4}\rangle+\langle c^{\dagger}_{1}c_{4}\rangle\langle c_{2}c^{\dagger}_{3}\rangle\;,

(32)

leading to

\displaystyle\langle D_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\langle n_{i\uparrow}\rangle\langle n_{i\downarrow}\rangle=n_{i\uparrow}n_{i\downarrow}

(33)

\displaystyle\langle N_{i}D_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle n_{j\sigma}n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\big(\delta+n_{i\sigma}n_{i\bar{\sigma}}\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma}|\langle c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}\rangle|^{2}\big[\tfrac{1}{2}|\langle C^{\dagger}_{i\bar{\sigma}}c_{j\bar{\sigma}}\rangle|^{2}+n_{j\bar{\sigma}}(1-n_{i\bar{\sigma}})\big]\;,

Noticing that there is a simple relation between
\langle N_{i}\rangle
and
\langle D_{i}\rangle
:

\displaystyle\langle N_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(1-c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma})(1-c^{\dagger}_{i\bar{\sigma}}c_{i\bar{\sigma}})\rangle

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{\sigma}\langle c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma}\rangle+\langle c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{i\bar{\sigma}}c_{i\bar{\sigma}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta+\langle D_{i}\rangle\;,

the holon-doublon correlator
C_{ij}
can thus be rewritten as:

C_{ij}\approx\frac{\sum\limits_{\sigma}|\langle c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}\rangle|^{2}\big[\frac{1}{2}|\langle c^{\dagger}_{i\bar{\sigma}}c_{j\bar{\sigma}}\rangle|^{2}+n_{j\bar{\sigma}}(1-n_{i\bar{\sigma}})\big]}{(\delta+\langle D_{i}\rangle)\langle D_{j}\rangle}\;.

(36)

We find from Eq. (
36
) that when
U\sim U_{\text{Mott}}
, the decrease of the double occupancy will enhance

C_{ij}
dramatically. In the case of
U\gg U_{\text{Mott}}
, the double occupancy decreases at the same rate as
the nearest neighbor hopping probability and more mildly than the next nearest one. Thus, the nearest neighbor
holon-doublon correlator will saturate eventually and the next nearest one decrease after reaching its maximum.

Appendix B
ELECTRON-MOMENTUM DISTRIBUTION

Within the cluster slave-spin scheme, the electron momentum distribution
n_{d}^{(2/4)}(\bm{k})=\langle d^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\sigma}d_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle
with respect to 2/4-site cluster approximation are as follows.
For the former,

\displaystyle n_{d}^{(2)}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}\langle f_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{j\sigma}\rangle

(37)

\displaystyle+\frac{\langle S_{j+\delta\sigma}^{+}\rangle\langle S_{j\sigma}^{-}\rangle}{N}\sum_{j,\delta}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\delta}}\langle f_{j+\delta\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{j\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\delta}{2}-\frac{4Z\epsilon}{t}\gamma_{\bm{k}}\;,

and for the latter,

\displaystyle n_{d}^{(4)}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}\langle f_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{j\sigma}\rangle

(38)

\displaystyle+\frac{\langle S_{j+\delta\sigma}^{+}\rangle\langle S_{j\sigma}^{-}\rangle}{N}\sum_{j,\delta}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\delta}}\langle f_{j+\delta\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{j\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\langle S_{j+\eta\sigma}^{+}\rangle\langle S_{j\sigma}^{-}\rangle}{N}\sum_{j,\eta}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\eta}}\langle f_{j+\eta\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{j\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\delta}{2}-\frac{4Z\epsilon}{t}\gamma_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\gamma^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}}{N}\sum_{\bm{k^{\prime}}\in\text{RBZ}}\gamma^{\prime}_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}\bigg\{Z_{\text{ave}}[\theta(-E_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}^{+})+\theta(-E_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}^{-})]

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt+\frac{Z_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{(4tZ\gamma_{\bm{k^{\prime}}})^{2}+\Delta^{2}}}[\theta(-E_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}^{+})-\theta(-E_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}^{-})]\bigg\}\;,

where

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}=\cos k_{x}\cos k_{y}\;,\;\;\;\;E_{\bm{k}}^{\pm}=-\mu^{\text{eff}}\pm\sqrt{(4tZ\gamma_{\bm{k}})^{2}+\Delta_{\sigma}^{2}}\;,

\displaystyle Z_{\text{ave}}=\frac{|\langle\tilde{z}_{A\sigma}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle\tilde{z}_{B\sigma}\rangle|^{2}}{2}\;,\;\;\;\;Z_{\Delta}=\frac{|\langle\tilde{z}_{A\sigma}\rangle|^{2}-|\langle\tilde{z}_{B\sigma}\rangle|^{2}}{2}\;.

To understand our present results concerning the negative and beyond unity part of this quantity around
M

and
\Gamma
point, we refer to the previous work on slave-particle formalism
Feng et al. 1993
,
where a “no-go” theorem has been shown to prevent to obtain a complete correct answer for the electron
momentum distribution based merely on a simple decoupling procedure for different degrees of freedom.
In the work, the electron momentum distribution is expressed as:

n_{c}(\bm{k},\sigma)=\frac{1-\delta}{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{\bm{q}}n_{f\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})n_{b}(\bm{q})\;.

(39)

The common feature between Eqs. (
37
), (
38
), and (
39
) is
that all of them are corrected from an average occupancy
n_{\sigma}=\frac{1-\delta}{2}
, and it is easy to
check that our results ensure the sum-rule of
N^{-1}\sum_{\bm{k}}n_{d}(\bm{k})=\frac{1-\delta}{2}
.

Appendix C
U(1) SLAVE-SPIN FORMALISM IN THE NONINTERACTING LIMIT

In this Appendix, we prove analytically that in the
U(1)
slave-spin theory
Yu and Si 2012

and within the single-site approximation, the condition
Z=1
at
U=0
recovers the correct noninteracting spinon dispersion.

Starting from the approximate Hamiltonian of a multiorbital system in the single-site approximation
[Eqs. (15) and (16) in Yu and Si’s work
Yu and Si 2012

\displaystyle H_{f}^{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\alpha\beta}[\epsilon_{k}^{\alpha\beta}\langle\tilde{z}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\tilde{z}_{\beta}\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\delta_{\alpha\beta}(\Delta_{\alpha}+\tilde{\mu}_{\alpha}-\lambda_{\alpha}-\mu)]f_{k\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{k\beta}\;,

\displaystyle H_{S}^{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\beta}\Big[\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\left(\langle\tilde{z}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rangle\tilde{z}_{\beta}+\langle\tilde{z}_{\beta}\rangle\tilde{z}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\right)+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{2}(\hat{n}_{\alpha}^{a}-\hat{n}_{\alpha}^{b})\Big]

(41)

\displaystyle+H_{\text{int}}^{S}\;,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle a_{\alpha}^{\dagger}b_{\alpha}\langle P_{\alpha}^{-}\rangle\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\langle P_{\alpha}^{\pm}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1/2\pm(n_{\alpha}^{f}-\frac{1}{2})}}\;,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{ij\sigma}t^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\langle f^{\dagger}_{i\alpha\sigma}f_{j\beta\sigma}\rangle\;,\hskip 11.99998pt\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}_{\bm{k}}=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{ij}t^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j})}\;,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\overline{\epsilon}_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}\;,\hskip 72.00008pt\eta_{\alpha}=\frac{2n_{\alpha}^{f}-1}{4n_{\alpha}^{f}(1-n_{\alpha}^{f})}\;,

\displaystyle\overline{\epsilon}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta}(\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\langle\tilde{z}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\tilde{z}_{\beta}\rangle+\text{c.c.})\;.

(42)

Eq. (
41
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle H_{S}^{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}[k_{\alpha}\tilde{z}_{\alpha}+k_{\alpha}^{*}\tilde{z}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{2}(\hat{n}_{\alpha}^{a}-\hat{n}_{\alpha}^{b})]

(43)

\displaystyle+H_{\text{int}}^{S}\;,

where
k_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta}\epsilon^{\beta\alpha}\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}_{\beta}\rangle
. In the
noninteracting limit, because of the decoupling of the orbits, we can keep only one degree of freedom in
Eq. (
43
). After dropping the crystal-field splitting
\Delta_{\alpha}
and the
interaction term, the above Hamiltonians become

\displaystyle H_{f}^{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}[\epsilon_{k}\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\tilde{z}\rangle+(\lambda+\tilde{\mu}-\mu)]f_{k}^{\dagger}f_{k}\;,

(44)

\displaystyle H_{S}^{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\rangle\tilde{z}+\langle\tilde{z}\rangle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\hat{n}^{a}-\hat{n}^{b})\;,

(45)

where
\overline{\epsilon}=2\epsilon\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\tilde{z}\rangle
and

\tilde{\mu}=4\epsilon\eta\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\tilde{z}\rangle
.

Under the hard-core boson constraint:
a^{\dagger}a+b^{\dagger}b=1
, the restricted Hillbert space is spanned by

\{|a=1,b=0\rangle,|a=0,b=1\rangle\}
, in which
the slave-spin Hamiltonian (
45
) has the form

\displaystyle H_{S}^{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\rangle\tilde{z}+\langle\tilde{z}\rangle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\hat{n}^{a}-\hat{n}^{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\frac{\lambda}{2}&\epsilon R\langle\tilde{z}\rangle\\
\epsilon R\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\rangle&-\frac{\lambda}{2}\end{array}\right)

with
R=\langle P_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle\langle P_{\alpha}^{-}\rangle
, which has the ground state and energy as

\displaystyle|\Psi_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\tfrac{-\epsilon R\langle\tilde{z}\rangle}{N}\;,\tfrac{\lambda/2+R_{0}}{N}\Big)\;,

(49)

\displaystyle E_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2}+\epsilon^{2}R^{2}\lvert\langle\tilde{z}^{\dagger}\rangle\rvert^{2}}\;,

(50)

where
R_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}+\epsilon^{2}R^{2}},\;N=\sqrt{2R_{0}(\frac{\lambda}{2}+R_{0})}
.
Accordingly, the expectation value of
\hat{n}_{a}-\hat{n}_{b}\;\text{and}\;\tilde{z}
can be calculated as

\langle\hat{n}_{a}-\hat{n}_{b}\rangle=-\frac{\lambda}{2R_{0}},\;\;\;\;\langle\tilde{z}\rangle=-\frac{\epsilon R^{2}\langle\tilde{z}\rangle}{2R_{0}}\;.

(51)

Because of the constraints
n_{a}-n_{b}=2n^{f}-1
and
|\langle\tilde{z}\rangle|^{2}=1
in the noninteracting case, we have

|\langle\tilde{z}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{\epsilon^{2}R^{4}}{4R_{0}^{2}}=1

(52)

with
R=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^{f}(1-n^{f})}}
. Then, we finally arrive at

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2R_{0}(2n^{f}-1)=\epsilon\frac{2n^{f}-1}{n^{f}(1-n^{f})}\;,

(53)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\epsilon\eta=\frac{4\epsilon(2n^{f}-1)}{4n^{f}(1-n^{f})}=\epsilon\frac{(2n^{f}-1)}{n^{f}(1-n^{f})}\;,

(54)

where
\epsilon
is negative. Thus,
\lambda=\tilde{\mu}
in the noninteracting case
(Z=1)
for the single orbital
system, and it is straightforward to generalize the conclusion to the multiorbital systems.

Introduction

1.1.
The Picard group of a Hopf algebra

Given a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, its
Picard group
is defined as the set of invertible isomorphism classes in
\mathcal{C}
, equipped with the multiplication induced by the tensor product.

A notable example is the
(stable) Picard group

\operatorname{Pic}(A)
of a
finite-dimensional cocommutative Hopf algebra

A
over a field
k
.
That is defined as the Picard group of the
stable module category

\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right)
.
In
\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right)
, objects are finitely generated left
A
-modules, and morphisms are
A
-module homomorphisms modulo those factoring through projective
A
-modules.
The symmetric monoidal structure is given by the
smash product
.
For an
A
-module
M
, its
k
-dual
M^{\vee}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(M,k)
naturally becomes a comodule over the dual coalgebra
A^{\vee}
of
A
.
Furthermore, the Hopf algebra structure of
A
ensures that any
A^{\vee}
-comodule is equipped with an
A
-module structure.
Consequently, dualization provides us a contravariant auto-equivalence on
\operatorname{\mathbf{mod}}\left(A\right)
, which passes to the stable module category

D\colon\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right)^{\rm op}\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right).

Therefore,
\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right)
is a
rigid category
allowing us to determine whether an object in
\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right)
is invertible by checking if it is
endo-trivial
.
Some elements of
\operatorname{Pic}(A)
are readily identified: such as the possible grading shiftings of the tensor unit.
Additionally, the category
\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right)
is triangulated, and the algebraic loop functor also contributes to
\operatorname{Pic}(A)
.
However, these elements may not account for the entire Picard group.
The cokernel of the inclusion is thus the interesting part of investigating.

1.3.
The
\mathbb{R}
-motivic situation

In both classical and
\mathbb{C}
-motivic cases, the Steenrod subalgebra being considered is a Hopf algebra. One major difference is, the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Steenrod subalgebra

\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}(1)^{\mathbb{C}}
is free of finite rank over
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
, the coefficient ring of the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic cohomology theory
(cf.
), rather than over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Unlike its
\mathbb{C}
-motivic counterpart, the
\mathbb{R}
-motivic Steenrod subalgebra

\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}(1)^{\mathbb{R}}
is not a Hopf algebra.
However, it remains free of finite rank over the coefficient ring
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Its
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}
-dual, turns out to be a
Hopf algebroid
with base
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}
in the sense of
[
ravenel2023complex
]
*A1 and
[
hovey2003homotopy
]
.

Over a Hopf algebroid, the dualization functor is not an endo functor on
\operatorname{\mathbf{stab}}\left(A\right)
. However, many results concerning finite-dimensional cocommutative Hopf algebras can be extended to the Hopf algebroid setting. In this paper, we investigate the notions of the
stable (co)module category
and the
Picard group
and constructed a functor behaves analogously as the dualization functor. Even better, since
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a polynomial ring, the stable module category of
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}(1)^{\mathbb{R}}
comes from a
Frobenius exact category
(cf.
) and is thus
rigid
.
Then, following standard arguments on a rigid symmetric monoidal category, we are able to determine the part of the Picard group coming from grading shifting of the unit and the algebraic loop functor.

The computation of the rest part can be achieved through a reduction technique utilizing a base change functor from
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. This allows us to return to the more familiar realm of finite-dimensional cocommutative Hopf algebras over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
The key observation is that the base change functor between the relevant exact categories behaves very well, enabling us to pull back information regarding the Picard group.

For
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}(1)^{\mathbb{R}}
, its base change to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{2}[D_{8}]
of the
dihedral group

D_{8}
of order
8
, which is well-understood.
Our computation shows that the Picard group of
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}(1)^{\mathbb{R}}
is isomorphic to that of
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}(1)^{\mathbb{C}}
and of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[D_{8}]
.

However, the assumption that the base ring of the Hopf algebroid is a polynomial ring needs not to be satisfied.
One notable example is the
C_{2}
-equivariant cohomology over a point
.
Consequently, the results presented in this paper may not be directly applicable to its
C_{2}
-equivariant counterpart.

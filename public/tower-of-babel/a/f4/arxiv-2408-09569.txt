Forcing Minimal Interval Patterns as Interval Exchange Transformations
Sourav Bhattacharya

Abstract
. We prove that any over-twist pattern is conjugate to an interval exchange transformation with bounded number of segments of isometry , restricted on one of its cycles . The bound is independent of the period and over-rotation number of the over-twist pattern and depends only on its modality .

1.
INTRODUCTION

A classical problem in
one-dimensional dynamical systems
is to classify
cycles
(or
periodic orbits
) of a
continuous
interval map. Any such
classification
relies on
properties
of
cycles
that are
invariant
under
topological conjugacy
. Considering
period
of these
cycles
as the
property
under study, we get an elegant
classification
of
cycles
given by the renowned Sharkovsky Theorem (
[
15
]
). It elucidates how one
period
of a
cycle

forces
the
existence
of other
periods
, translating the
problem
of
classification
of
cycles
to one of
forcing among cycles
. However, Sharkovsky Theorem is inapplicable for
classification
if two
cycles
have the same
period
. In such situations, we need to search for other
criterion
.

Now, one can consider the
finest
possible
criterion
:
group

cycles
into
equivalence classes
, called
patterns
based upon associated
cyclic permutation
and ascertain a rule of
forcing
among these
groups
. However, the
forcing relation
among
patterns
is a
partial ordering
(
[
2
]
); the
results
in the case are much
more

complicated
than the one obtained for
period
and a coherant description of the structure of the set of
patterns

exhibited
by
periodic orbits
of a given
continuous interval
map is not possible (see
[
2
]
).

This motivated researchers to look for other middle-of-the-road ways of describing
cycles
in between the theories based upon
periods
and
permutations
. One such prominent work is that of Blokh and Misiurewicz (
[
9
]
). They introduced a new
invariant
of
topological conjugacy
for
cycles
:
over-rotation numbers
, by extending
rotation theory
to
interval maps
. The
classification rule
induced by
over-rotation numbers
, is on one hand much
finer
than the
period-based classification
; on the other hand, the
forcing relation
on the
collection
of all
cycles
induced by the
theory
is
linear
, like the case of
periods
and
allows
for a transparent description.

In
over-rotation theory
for
interval maps
, one of the
most important
objects of study are, the
forcing minimal cycles
with a given
over-rotation number
, called
over-twist cycles
. These are the
simplest cycles
with a given
over-rotation number
: a
cycle

P
with
over-rotation number

\rho
is
over-twist
if there exists an interval map
f_{P}
such that
P
is the
unique cycle
of
f_{P}
with
over-rotation number

\rho
. A
necessary
and
sufficient condition
for a given
cycle
to be an
over-twist cycle
was established in
[
10
]
. Further, description of the
dynamics
of all possible
over-twist cycles
of
modality
less than or equal to 2 were obtained in
[
11
]
and
[
3
]
.

The goal of this
paper
is to present a new perspective of looking at these
over-twist cycles
by connecting them with
interval exchange transformations
. An
interval exchange transformation
(IET) is a
right continuous bijective map
from an
interval
to
itself
such that the
interval
can be
subdivided
into a
finite
number of
pieces
, called
segments of isometry
, on each of which the map acts as a
translation
. Since Keane’s seminal paper
[
13
]
, the field of IETs has witnessed a remarkable surge in interest. These maps have fascinated researchers not only from a theoretical standpoint, as evidenced by the substantial body of work in this area (see
[
16
]
for a comprehensive review), but also due to their numerous applications. Notably, IETs play a crucial role in the study of
polygonal billiards
(
[
14
,
4
]
) and
surface flows

[
12
]
, among others.

Our
results
seeks to offer a unified framework to study problems in
over-rotation theory
, IETs, and the various disciplines related to IETs. The main
results
of this paper are:

(1)

We show that any
over-twist cycle
of
modality

m
is
conjugate
to an IET with
atmost

(m+1)(m+2)

segments of isometry
,
restricted
on one of its
cycles
.

(2)

In particular, we show:

(a)

any
unimodal

over-twist cycle
is
conjugate
to an IET with
3

segments of isometry
,
restricted
on one of its
cycles
.

(b)

any
bimodal

over-twist cycle
is
conjugate
to an IET with
4

segments of isometry
,
restricted
on one of its
cycles
.

This
paper
contains 4
sections
:

(1)

Section 1
briefly
outlines
the
paper
.

(2)

In Section 2
, we present all essential
definitions
,
lemmas
, and
theorems
necessary to describe the results.

(3)

In Section 3 we
prove
our
results
.

2.1.
Modality

Let
U

be given

and

be a given

. If there exists a subset

of
U
such that the

of every element of

is a

is

on
S
. Further, if

is the

of

is a

of

. If

,

is

on

, either

, or

. In the

, we say

on

; in the

, we say

or

on

We call a

,

into

of

, such that

is

on each of these

. We call the smallest number of such

, the

of

. If the

is
2
, we call

,
unimodal
. If the
lap count
is
3
, we call

,

. In this paper, we will consider only

.

(
[

]
)

The ordering induced by the forcing relation

(

]
)
.

The necessary and sufficient condition for a pattern

2.4.
Over-rotation numbers

Consider a
continuous
map
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
. We assume
f
has a
cycle
of
period
strictly greater than
1
; we make this assumption because otherwise for any
x\in[0,1]
, the
sequence

\{f^{n}(x):n\in\mathbb{N}\}

converges
to some
fixed point
of
f
and hence the dynamics is not interesting. Given a
cycle

P
of

of
period

q
strictly greater than
1

count
the
number
of
points

x\in P
such that
f(x)-x
and
f^{2}(x)-f(x)
have different
signs
. If there are
m
such
points
, the
pair

(\frac{m}{2},q)
and the
number

\frac{m}{2q}
are called

and
over-rotation number
of the
cycle

P
and denoted by
orp(P)
and
orn(P)
respectively.

In an
over-rotation pair

(s,t)
,
s
and
t
are
integers
and
0<\frac{s}{t}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
. Suppose
f
has a unique
fixed point

a
,
f(x)>x
if
x<a
and
f(x)<x
if
x>a
. In this case,
s
in the
over-rotation pair

(s,t)
is equal to the
number
of
points
which are
located
to the
right
of the
fixed point

a
but mapped to the
left
of
a
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose
p,q,r,s
are integers which satisfies one of the following two conditions:

(1)

\frac{p}{q}<\frac{r}{s}
.

(2)

\frac{p}{q}=\frac{r}{s}=\frac{k}{\ell}
where
k
and
\ell
are coprime integers and
\frac{p}{k}\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;\frac{r}{k}

\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;
denotes Sharkovsky ordering.

Then, any continuous interval map
f
having a cycle of over-rotation pair
(p,q)
must have a cycle of over-rotation pair
(r,s)
.

By Theorem
2.1
, any
continuous interval map
having a

of

of
period

2
. Thus, the following result thus follows from Theorem

Corollary 2.5
.

The closure of the set of over-rotation numbers of periodic points of a continuous interval map

forms an interval of the form
[r_{f},\frac{1}{2}]
where

By definition, all

which exhibit the same

over-rotation number
and same
over-rotation pair
. We define, the
over-rotation number
and
over-rotation pair
of a
pattern

\pi

over-rotation number
of any

which
exhibits

\pi
. If

is a

which exhibits
\pi

of the
P
-

Noncommutative Residue and Canonical Trace on Noncommutative Tori. Uniqueness Results This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Noncommutative Manifolds and their Symmetries in honour of Giovanni Landi. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Landi.html
Raphaël PONGE

Abstract
In this paper we establish uniqueness theorems for the noncommutative residue and the canonical trace on pseudodifferential operators on noncommutative tori of arbitrary dimension. The former is the unique trace up to constant multiple on integer order pseudodifferential operators.The latter is the unique trace up to constant multiple on non-integer order pseudodifferential operators. This improves previous uniqueness results by Fathizadeh–Khalkhali, Fathizadeh–Wong, and Lévy–Neira–Paycha.

Introduction

The noncommutative residue trace of Guillemin
[
25
]
and Wodzicki
[
56
,
57
]
is a trace on the algebra of integer order pseudodifferential operators (
\Psi
DOs) on a closed manifold. Its importance in noncommutative geometry mostly stems from its relationships with the noncommutative integral, the local index formula in noncommutative geometry, and the spectral action principle (see
[
8
,
10
,
11
]
). The noncommutative residue appears as the residual functional induced by the analytic extension of the ordinary trace to non-integer
\Psi
DOs. This analytic extension gives rise to a trace, which is called the canonical trace
[
25
,
31
]
. The noncommutative residue and canonical trace provide us with a neat approach to the meromorphic extension of spectral functions attached to elliptic operators such as zeta and eta functions.

A striking feature shared by the noncommutative residue and canonical trace is uniqueness. Namely, by a well known result of Wodzicki
[
56
]
on any connected closed manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
the noncommutative residue is the unique trace up to constant multiple on integer order
\Psi
DOs (see also
[
35
,
38
,
39
,
48
]
). This result was extended to Fourier integral operators by Guillemin
[
27
]
and to
\Psi
DOs with log-polyhomogeneous symbols by Lesch
[
35
]
. It was also extended to Heisenberg
\Psi
DOs by the author
[
47
]
. The uniqueness of the canonical trace was established by Maniccia–Seiler–Schrohe
[
44
]
(see also
[
38
,
48
]
).

The noncommutative residue and the canonical trace have been extended to numerous classes of
\Psi
DOs in various geometric settings. This paper focuses on their versions on noncommutative tori. The noncommutative tori are arguably the most well known examples of noncommutative spaces. In particular, 2-dimensional noncommutative tori naturally arise from actions of
\mathbb{Z}
on the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
by irrational rotations. More generally, an
n
-dimensional noncommutative tori
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
is generated by unitaries
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
subject to the relations,

\displaystyle U_{l}U_{j}={\rm e}^{2{\rm i}\pi\theta_{jl}}U_{j}U_{l},\qquad j,l=1,\ldots,n,

where
\theta=(\theta_{jl})
is a given real anti-symmetric matrix. The pseudodifferential calculus on NC tori of Connes
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
28
,
29
]
has been receiving a great deal of attention recently thanks to its role in the spectral theoretic approach to curvature on noncommutative tori following the seminal work of Connes–Tretkoff
[
13
]
and Connes–Moscovici
[
12
]
(see, e.g.,
[
14
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
30
,
36
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
49
,
53
]
;
see also
[
9
,
20
,
37
]
for recent surveys).

The noncommutative residue for NC tori was defined in dimension
n=2
and
n=4
by Fathizadeh–Wong
[
21
]
and Fathizadeh–Khalkhali
[
19
]
, respectively. It was extended to any dimension
n\geq 2
by Lévy–Neira–Paycha
[
40
]
who also constructed the canonical trace. We refer to
[
16
,
49
]
for the relationship between noncommutative residue and scalar curvature on NC tori.

Uniqueness results for the noncommutative residue and the canonical trace are established under some conditions. It is shown in
[
21
]
(resp.,
[
19
]
) that the noncommutative residue is the unique
exotic
trace up to constant multiple in dimension
n\geq 2
(resp.,
n=4
), where by an exotic trace it is meant a trace that vanishes on
\Psi
DOs of sufficiently negative order. This result was subsequently improved in
[
40
]
, where it is shown that the noncommutative residue is the unique
singular
trace up to constant multiple in any dimension
n\geq 2
. In the terminology of
[
40
]
a functional on
\Psi
DOs is singular when it vanishes on smoothing operators (and so any exotic trace is singular).

As for the canonical trace, in
[
40
]
it is also shown to be the unique
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-continuous
trace up to constant multiple on non-integer order
\Psi
DOs.
A functional on
\Psi
DOs is
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-continuous when its restriction to
\Psi
DOs of order
<-n
is continuous with respect to the topology induced by the trace-class norm.

The aim is of this paper is to remove all the above restrictions on the trace classes in the aforementioned uniqueness theorems. Our first uniqueness result states that the noncommutative residue is the unique trace on integer order
\Psi
DOs on NC tori of any dimension
n\geq 2
(Theorem
7.3
). This implies that the first Hochschild cohomology group has dimension 1 (Corollary
7.5
). Our second uniqueness result asserts that
the canonical trace is the unique trace on non-integer order
\Psi
DOs on NC tori of any dimension
n\geq 2
(Theorem
7.8
).
As a by-product the canonical trace is the unique trace on non-integer order
\Psi
DOs that agrees with the ordinary trace on smoothing operators (Corollary
7.10
).

The approach of this paper is similar to the approaches used in
[
35
,
47
,
48
]
. In particular, we show that the noncommutative residue (resp., the canonical trace) is the unique obstruction to being a sum of integer order (resp., non-integer order)
\Psi
DO commutators (see Propositions
7.1
and
7.6
for the precise statements). These results are obtained in two main steps. In the first step we show that a
\Psi
DO is a sum of commutators modulo smoothing operators if and only if either it has non-integer order, or it has integer order and zero noncommutative residue (Proposition
5.8
). This step is enough to recover the uniqueness results of
[
19
,
21
,
40
]
for the noncommutative residue (see Corollary
5.9
).

The second step is twofold. First, we apply a result of Guillemin
[
27
]
to show that any smoothing operator with zero trace is the sum of two commutators of smoothing operators (Proposition
6.2
). Combining this with the first step gives the uniqueness of the canonical trace. Second, we show that any smoothing operator is a sum of commutators of integer order
\Psi
DOs (Proposition
6.5
). Combining this with the first step establishes the uniqueness of the noncommutative residue.

The noncommutative residue of
[
19
,
21
]
is defined in dimension
n=2
and
n=4
only. In
[
40
]
the noncommutative residue and the canonical trace are defined in any dimension. However, the authors actually consider toroidal
\Psi
DOs.
We obtain traces on our class of
\Psi
DOs by using the equivalence between these classes established in
[
28
]
.
Furthermore, the arguments of
[
40
]
involves considerations on extension maps that are not needed for our purpose.
For sake of simplicity we present a direct construction of the noncommutative residue and canonical trace for our class of
\Psi
DOs in Section
4
.

In addition, the proof of the trace property of the canonical trace in
[
40
]
is not complete (see Remark
4.27
on this point). For sake of completeness we provide a proof of this result by another approach at the end of Section
4
. The approach is similar to the classical approach of
[
25
,
31
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the main definitions and properties of noncommutative tori.
In Section
3
, we recall the main definitions and properties of pseudodifferential operators on noncommutative tori.
In Section
4
, we review the constructions of the noncommutative residue on integer-order
\Psi
DOs and of the canonical trace on non-integer-order
\Psi
DOs on NC tori.
In Section
5
, we derive the decomposition of
\Psi
DOs as sums of
\Psi
DO commutators modulo smoothing operators.
In Section
6
, we study the commutator structure of smoothing operators.
In Section
7
, we prove our main uniqueness results for the noncommutative residue and the canonical trace.
The last two sections, Section
8
and Section
9
, are devoted to the proofs of Lemmas
5.6
and
6.3
, respectively.

It would be interesting to try to extend the results of this paper in two directions. The first direction concerns traces on log-polyhomogeenous symbols on NC tori. We should expect to have analogues for NC tori of the results of Lesch
[
35
]
. The other direction concerns pre-traces and hyper-traces on
\Psi
DOs of a fixed given order (see
[
38
]
for the corresponding results on ordinary manifolds). There are results in this direction in
[
40
]
, but they are established under some continuity conditions that it would be desirable to remove.

2
Noncommutative tori

In this section, we review the main definitions and properties of noncommutative
n
-tori,
n\geq 2
. We refer to
[
8
,
28
,
50
,
51
]
, and the references therein, for a more comprehensive account.

Throughout this paper, we let
\theta=(\theta_{jk})
be a real anti-symmetric
(n\times n)
-matrix. Denote by
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
its column vectors. We also let
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be the Hilbert space of
L^{2}
-functions on the ordinary torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\penalty 2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{n}
equipped with the inner product,

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi|\eta\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\xi(x)\overline{\eta(x)}{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}x,\qquad\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

(2.1)

For
j=1,\ldots,n
, let
U_{j}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be the unitary operator defined by

\displaystyle(U_{j}\xi)(x)={\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}\xi(x+\pi\theta_{j}),\qquad\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

We then have the relations,

\displaystyle U_{k}U_{j}={\rm e}^{2{\rm i}\pi\theta_{jk}}U_{j}U_{k},\qquad j,k=1,\ldots,n.

(2.2)

The
noncommutative torus

A_{\theta}=C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the unitary operators
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
. For
\theta=0
we obtain the
C^{*}
-algebra
C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of continuous functions on the ordinary
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Note that (
2.2
) implies that
A_{\theta}
is the closure in
\mathscr{L}\big(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\big)
of the linear span of the unitary operators,

\displaystyle U^{k}:=U_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots U_{n}^{k_{n}},\qquad k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Let
\tau\colon\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the state defined by the constant function
1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tau(T)=\left\langle T1|1\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(T1)(x){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}x,\qquad T\in\mathscr{L}\big(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\big).

This induces a continuous tracial state on the
C^{*}
-algebra
A_{\theta}
such that
\tau(1)=1
and
\tau\big(U^{k}\big)=0
for
k\neq 0
.
The GNS construction (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
) then allows us to associate with
\tau
a
*
-representation of
A_{\theta}
as follows.

Let
\left\langle\cdot|\cdot\right\rangle
be the sesquilinear form on
A_{\theta}
defined by

\displaystyle\left\langle u|v\right\rangle=\tau\left(uv^{*}\right),\qquad u,v\in A_{\theta}.

(2.3)

Note that the family
\big\{U^{k};\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\big\}
is orthonormal with respect to this sesquilinear form. We let
\mathscr{H}_{\theta}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
the Hilbert space arising from the completion of
A_{\theta}
with respect to the pre-inner product (
2.3
). The action of
A_{\theta}
on itself by left-multiplication uniquely extends to a
*
-representation of
A_{\theta}
in
\mathscr{H}_{\theta}
. When
\theta=0
we recover the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with the inner product (
2.1
) and the representation of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
by bounded multipliers. In addition, as
(U^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathscr{H}_{\theta}
, every
u\in\mathscr{H}_{\theta}
can be uniquely written as

\displaystyle u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k},\qquad u_{k}:=\big\langle u\,|\,U^{k}\big\rangle,

(2.4)

where the series converges in
\mathscr{H}_{\theta}
. When
\theta=0
we recover the Fourier series decomposition in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

The natural action of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
by translation gives rise to an action on
\mathscr{L}\big(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\big)
. This induces a
*
-action
(s,u)\rightarrow\alpha_{s}(u)
on
A_{\theta}
given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}(U^{k})={\rm e}^{{\rm i}s\cdot k}U^{k},\qquad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}$ and $s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

This action is strongly continuous, and so we obtain a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
\big(A_{\theta},\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha\big)
. We are especially interested in the subalgebra
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
of smooth elements of this
C^{*}
-dynamical system (a.k.a.
smooth noncommutative torus
). Namely,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\theta}:=\big\{u\in A_{\theta};\,\alpha_{s}(u)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};A_{\theta}\big)\big\}.

The unitaries
U^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, are contained in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
, and so
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is a dense subalgebra of
A_{\theta}
. Denote by
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\big)
the space of rapid-decay sequences with complex entries. In terms of the Fourier series decomposition (
2.4
) we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\theta}=\bigg\{u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k};\,(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\big)\bigg\}.

When
\theta=0
we recover the algebra
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of smooth functions on the ordinary torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and the Fourier-series description of this algebra.

For
j=1,\ldots,n
, let
\delta_{j}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
be the derivation defined by

\displaystyle\delta_{j}(u)=D_{s_{j}}\alpha_{s}(u)|_{s=0},\qquad u\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta},

where we have set
D_{s_{j}}=\frac{1}{i}\partial_{s_{j}}
. When
\theta=0
the derivation
\delta_{j}
is just the derivation
D_{x_{j}}=\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. In general, for
j,l=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{j}(U_{l})=\begin{cases}U_{j}&\text{if $l=j$},\\
0&\text{if $l\neq j$}.\end{cases}

More generally, given any multi-order
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, define

\displaystyle\delta^{\beta}(u)=D_{s}^{\beta}\alpha_{s}(u)|_{s=0}=\delta_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\delta_{n}^{\beta_{n}}(u),\qquad u\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}.

We endow
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
with the locally convex topology defined by the semi-norms,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\ni u\longrightarrow\big\|\delta^{\beta}(u)\big\|,\qquad\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

With the involution inherited from
A_{\theta}
this turns
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
into a (unital) Fréchet
*
-algebra. The Fourier series (
2.4
) of every
u\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
converges in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
with respect to this topology. In addition, it can be shown that
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is closed under holomorphic functional calculus (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
28
]
).

3
Pseudodifferential operators on noncommutative tori

In this section, we recall the main definitions and properties of pseudodifferential operators on noncommutative tori
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
28
,
29
]
. The exposition closely follows
[
28
,
29
]
(see also
[
54
]
).

3.1
Symbols on noncommutative tori

There are various classes of symbols on noncommutative tori.

Definition 3.1

(standard symbols; see
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
)
.

\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, consists of maps
\rho(\xi)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
such that, for all multi-orders
\alpha
and
\beta
, there exists
C_{\alpha\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\rho(\xi)\big\|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\beta|}\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Definition 3.2
.

\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
consists of maps
\rho(\xi)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
such that, for all
N\geq 0
and multi-orders
\alpha
,
\beta
, there exists
C_{N\alpha\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\rho(\xi)\big\|\leq C_{N\alpha\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{-N}\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 3.3
.

\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)=\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
.

Definition 3.4

(homogeneous symbols)
.

S_{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
, consists of maps

\rho(\xi)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 0;\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)

that are homogeneous of degree
q
, i.e.,

\rho(t\xi)=t^{q}\rho(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 0
and
t>0
.

Remark 3.5
.

If
\rho(\xi)\in S_{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
and
\chi(\xi)\in C^{\infty}_{c}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
is such that
\chi(\xi)=1
near
\xi=0
, then
(1-\chi(\xi))\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{\Re q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
.

Definition 3.6

(classical symbols; see
[
3
,
4
]
)
.

S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
, consists of maps
\rho(\xi)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
that admit an asymptotic expansion,

\displaystyle\rho(\xi)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}\rho_{q-j}(\xi),\qquad\rho_{q-j}\in S_{q-j}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big),

where
\sim
means that, for all
N\geq 0
and multi-orders
\alpha
,
\beta
, there exists
C_{N\alpha\beta}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\bigg(\rho-\sum_{j<N}\rho_{q-j}\bigg)(\xi)\bigg\|\leq C_{N\alpha\beta}|\xi|^{\Re{q}-N-|\beta|}.

Remark 3.7
.

S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)\subset\mathbb{S}^{\Re{q}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
.

Example 3.8
.

Any polynomial map
\rho(\xi)=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
,
a_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is in
S^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
.

Remark 3.9
.

It is also convenient to consider scalar-valued symbols. We shall denote by
\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
,
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
,
S_{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
and
S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
the corresponding classes of scalar-valued symbols. We will consider them as sub-classes of the
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
-valued symbol classes via the natural embedding of
\mathbb{C}
into
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
.

3.2
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
DOs on noncommutative tori

Given
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we let
P_{\rho}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
be the operator defined by

\displaystyle P_{\rho}u=\iint{\rm e}^{{\rm i}s\cdot\xi}\rho(\xi)\alpha_{-s}(u){\rm d}s{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}\xi,\qquad u\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}.

The above integral is meant as an oscillating integral (see
[
28
]
). Equivalently, for all
u=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k}
in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle P_{\rho}u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\rho(k)U^{k}.

(3.1)

In any case, this defines a continuous linear operator
P_{\rho}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
.

Definition 3.10
.

\Psi^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
, consists of all linear operators
P_{\rho}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
with
\rho(\xi)
in
S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
.

Remark 3.11
.

If
P=P_{\rho}
with
\rho(\xi)
in
S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
\rho(\xi)\sim\sum\rho_{q-j}(\xi)
, then
\rho(\xi)
is called a
symbol
for
P
. This symbol is not unique, but its restriction to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and its class modulo
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
are unique (see Remark
3.19
). As a result, the homogeneous symbols
\rho_{q-j}(\xi)
are uniquely determined by
P
. The leading symbol
\rho_{q}(\xi)
is called the
principal symbol
of
P
.

Example 3.12
.

A differential operator on
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is of the form
P=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}\delta^{\alpha}
,
a_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
(see
[
5
,
8
]
). This is a
\Psi
DO of order
m
with symbol
\rho(\xi)=\sum a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
(see
[
28
]
).

3.3
Composition of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
DOs

Suppose we are given symbols
\rho_{1}(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m_{1}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
m_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\rho_{2}(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m_{2}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. As
P_{\rho_{1}}
and
P_{\rho_{2}}
are linear operators on
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
, the composition
P_{\rho_{1}}P_{\rho_{2}}
makes sense as such an operator.
In addition, we define the map
\rho_{1}\sharp\rho_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
by

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\sharp\rho_{2}(\xi)=\iint{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t\cdot\eta}\rho_{1}(\xi+\eta)\alpha_{-t}[\rho_{2}(\xi)]{\rm d}t{\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}\eta,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

3.4
Toroidal symbols and

In this section, we recall the relationship between standard

\Psi

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
denote the space of sequences with values in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
that are indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. In addition, let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
, the (forward) difference operator
\Delta_{i}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}u_{k}=u_{k+e_{i}}-u_{k},\qquad(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}.

The operators
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}
pairwise commute. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, we set
\Delta^{\alpha}=\Delta_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. We similarly define backward difference operators
\overline{\Delta}=\overline{\Delta}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\overline{\Delta}_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
, where
\overline{\Delta}_{i}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\!\allowbreak\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\Delta}_{i}u_{k}=u_{k}-u_{k-e_{i}},\qquad(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}.

We will also denote by
\Delta^{\alpha}
and
\overline{\Delta}^{\alpha}
the extensions of these operators to operators on
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, where
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the space of maps from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
. Namely, we set

\Delta^{\alpha}=\Delta_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
and
\overline{\Delta}=\overline{\Delta}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\overline{\Delta}_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
, where
\Delta_{j}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
\overline{\Delta}_{j}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
are given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\rho(x)=\rho(\xi+e_{j})-\rho(\xi),\qquad\overline{\Delta}_{j}\rho(x)=\rho(\xi)-\rho(\xi-e_{j}),\qquad\rho(\xi)\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

(3.2)

Definition 3.15

(

,

such that, for all multi-orders
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

\displaystyle\big\|\delta^{\alpha}\Delta^{\beta}\rho_{k}\big\|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}(1+|k|)^{m-|\beta|}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Definition 3.16
.

\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
consists of sequences
(\rho_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\subset\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
such that, for all

and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, there is
C_{N\alpha}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\delta^{\alpha}\rho_{k}\|\leq C_{N\alpha}(1+|k|)^{-N}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Remark 3.17
.

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta})=\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)

4
Noncommutative residue and canonical trace

In this section, we review the constructions of the noncommutative residue on integer-order
\Psi
DOs and of the canonical trace on non-integer-order
\Psi
DOs on NC tori. We focus more especially on their trace properties. In what follows, given any subset
X\subset\mathbb{C}
, we set

\displaystyle\Psi^{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}):=\bigcup_{q\in X}\Psi^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\qquad S^{X}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big):=\bigcup_{q\in Z}S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big).

For instance,
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
consists of all classical
\Psi
DOs on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
.

4.1
Noncommutative residue

The noncommutative residue is defined on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
. This is a sub-algebra of
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. Thus, by a trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
we shall mean any linear form that is annihilated by the commutator subspace,

\displaystyle\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]:=\operatorname{Span}\big\{[P_{1},P_{2}];\,P_{j}\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big\}.

(4.1)

Definition 4.1
.

The
noncommutative residue
is the linear functional
\operatorname{Res}\colon\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}(P)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau[\rho_{-n}(\xi)]{\rm d}^{n-1}\xi,\qquad P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta}),

where
\rho_{-n}(\xi)
is the symbol of degree
-n
of
P
.

Remark 4.2
.

If
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
, then the homogeneous symbol
\rho_{-n}(\xi)
is uniquely determined by
P
(cf. Remark
3.11
). We make the convention that
\rho_{-n}(\xi)=0
when
P
has order
<-n
.

Remark 4.3
.

It is immediate from the above definition that
\operatorname{Res}(P)=0
when its symbol of degree
-n
is zero. In particular, the noncommutative residue vanishes on the following classes of operators:

(i)

\Psi
DOs of order
<-n
, including smoothing operators.

(ii)

Differential operators (since for such operators the symbols are polynomials without any homogeneous component of negative degree).

Remark 4.4
.

In dimension
n=2
(resp.,
n=4
) we recover the noncommutative residues of
[
21
]
(resp.,
[
19
]
).

Remark 4.5
.

Under the equivalence between our classes of
\Psi
DOs and the classes of toroidal
\Psi
DOs (cf. Proposition
3.22
) the above noncommutative residues agrees with the noncommutative residue for toroidal
\Psi
DOs introduced in
[
40
]
.

Proposition 4.6

(
[
19
,
21
,
40
]
)
.

Let
P\in\Psi^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
Q\in\Psi^{q^{\prime}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
be such that
q+q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}(PQ)=\operatorname{Res}(QP).

In particular, the noncommutative residue
\operatorname{Res}
is a trace on the algebra
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
.

Remark 4.7
.

An alternative proof of Proposition
4.6
is provided at the end of this section.

4.2
Canonical trace

The canonical trace is defined on
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Note that
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
is not a subspace of
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
and is not closed under the composition of operators. Therefore, we need to explain what is meant by a trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
.

Definition 4.8
.

We say that a functional
T\colon\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
linear
when

\displaystyle T(\lambda P)=\lambda T(P)\qquad\forall\,P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})\ \forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

(4.2)

\displaystyle T(P_{1}+P_{2})=T(P_{1})+T(P_{2})\qquad\forall\,P_{i}\in\Psi^{q_{i}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta}),\ q_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z},\ q_{1}-q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.

(4.3)

We say this is a
trace
when it is linear and we have

\displaystyle T(P_{1}P_{2})=T(P_{2}P_{1})\qquad\forall\,P_{i}\in\Psi^{q_{i}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta}),\ q_{1}+q_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Z}.

Remark 4.9
.

A functional
T\colon\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is linear if and only if it induces a linear form on each space
\Psi^{q+\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. It is trace when we further require it to be annihilated by the commutator subspaces,

\displaystyle\big[\Psi^{q_{1}+\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{q_{2}+\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]:=\operatorname{Span}\big\{[P_{1},P_{2}];\,P_{j}\in\Psi^{q_{j}+\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big\},\qquad q_{1}+q_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}.

Linear traces on non-integer order
\Psi
DOs naturally appear in the analytic extension of the ordinary trace to non-integer order
\Psi
DOs (see
[
26
,
31
,
57
]
). Incidentally, this is a key ingredient in the analytic extension of the zeta functions of elliptic operators and other related spectral functions.

In the setting of noncommutative tori this leads us to the canonical trace
\operatorname{TR}\colon\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of
[
40
]
. Strictly speaking, in
[
40
]
the functional is defined on classical toroidal
\Psi
DOs, but we obtain a functional on
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
by using the equality of the class of toroidal
\Psi
DOs with our class of
\Psi
DOs. Furthermore, the construction in
[
40
]
involves the use of extension maps from toroidal symbols to classical symbols. We shall now explain how to define the canonical trace directly on our class of classical
\Psi
DOs.

In what follows we denote by
\Psi^{<-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
(resp.,
S^{<-n}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
) the class of classical
\Psi
DOs (resp., symbols) in (
4.1
) associated with the half-space
X=\{\Re q<-n\}
. By Proposition
3.29
every operator
P\in\Psi^{<-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is trace-class, and we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(P)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau\big[\rho_{\!{}_{P}}(\xi)\big]{\rm d}\xi=L[\tau\circ\rho_{\!{}_{P}}],

(4.4)

where the symbol
\rho_{\!{}_{P}}(\xi)
is defined in (
3.4
) and
L\colon S^{<-n}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is defined by

\displaystyle L(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho(\xi){\rm d}\xi.

(4.5)

Definition 4.10
.

Given any open
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, we shall say that a family of symbols
(\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega}
in
S^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
is
holomorphic
when the following properties hold:

(i)

\rho(z)\in S^{w(z)}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
, where
w(z)
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega
.

(ii)

The map
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(z,\xi)\rightarrow\rho(z)(\xi)\in\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is holomorphic with respect to
z
and
C^{\infty}
with respect to
\xi
.

(iii)

We have an asymptotic expansion,

\displaystyle\rho(z)(\xi)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}\rho_{w(z)-j}(z)(\xi),\qquad\rho_{w(z)-j}(z)\in S_{w(z)-j}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big),

(4.6)

in the sense that, for all integers
N\geq 0
, for compact sets
K\subset\Omega
, and multi-orders
\alpha
,
\beta
, there is
C_{NK\alpha\beta}>0
such that, for all
z\in K
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|\xi|\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\bigg[\rho(z)-\sum_{j<N}\rho_{w(z)-j}(z)\bigg](\xi)\bigg\|\leq C_{NK\alpha\beta}|\xi|^{\Re w(z)-N-|\beta|}.

Remark 4.11
.

Suppose there is
m\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Re w(z)<m
on
\Omega
. Then the condition (ii) and the estimate (
4.6
) for
N=0
imply that
(\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega}
is a holomorphic family in the Fréchet space
\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. This can be seen by means of some Cauchy formula argument.

Remark 4.12
.

For every symbol
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
we can find a holomorphic family
(\rho(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
in
S^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
such that
\rho(0)=\rho
and
{\operatorname{ord}}\rho(z)=z+q
. For instance, we may take

\displaystyle\rho(z)(\xi):=(1-\chi(\xi))|\xi|^{z}\rho(\xi)+\chi(\xi)\rho(\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ z\in\mathbb{C}.

Such a family is called a
holomorphic gauging
of
\rho(\xi)
.

In what follows, we shall say that a functional
\varphi\colon S^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
analytic
when, for every holomorphic family
(\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega}
in
S^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
, the function
z\rightarrow\varphi[\rho(z)]
is analytic on
\Omega
.

Remark 4.13

(analytic continuation principle)
.

An analytic functional
\varphi\colon S^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is uniquely determined by its restriction to symbols of order
<m
for any given
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, suppose that
\varphi
vanishes on such symbols. Then it must vanish on any holomorphic gauging in the sense of Remark
4.12
by the analytic continuation principle. As explained in Remark
4.12
, any symbol can be embedded into such a family. It then follows that
\varphi
is identically zero.

The key step in the construction of the canonical trace on
\Psi
DOs on (closed) manifolds is the following lemma.

Lemma 4.14

(
[
26
]
; see also
[
11
,
31
,
35
,
46
]
)
.

The following holds.

(i)

The functional
L\colon S^{<-n}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by (

) has a unique analytic extension
\tilde{L}\colon

S^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. For every holomorphic gauging
(\rho(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
for
\rho
, the composition
\tilde{L}[\rho(z)]
has at worst a simple pole singularity near

, and we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\tilde{L}\big[\rho(z)\big]=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{-n}(\xi){\rm d}^{n-1}\xi,

where
\rho_{-n}(\xi)
is the symbol of degree
-n
of
\rho(\xi)
.

Remark 4.15
.

The linearity of
L
and the analytic continuation principle mentioned in Remark

ensure us that its analytic extension

is linear in the sense of (

)–(
4.3
).

Definition 4.16
.

Given any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, a family
(P(z))_{z\in\Omega}\subset\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
is
holomorphic
when there is a holomorphic family of symbols
(\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega}
such that
P(z)=P_{\rho(z)}
for all

.

Remark 4.17
.

Given
P\in\Psi^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
be such that
P=P_{\rho}
and let
(\rho(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\subset S^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
be a holomorphic gauging of
\rho
. Such a family exists by Remark
4.12
. Set
P(z)=P_{\rho(z)}
,

. Then
(P(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
is a holomorphic family in
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
such that
P(0)=P_{\rho(0)}=P_{\rho}=P
and
{\operatorname{ord}}P(z)={\operatorname{ord}}\rho(z)=z+q
. Such a family is called a
holomorphic gauging
of

.

Lemma 4.18
.

Given any family
(P(z))_{z\in\Omega}\subset\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

(P(z))_{z\in\Omega}
is a holomorphic family in
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
.

(ii)

The family of symbols
(\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}})_{z\in\Omega}\subset S^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
is holomorphic.

Proof.

It is immediate that (ii) implies (i), so we only have to establish the converse. Let
(P(z))_{z\in\Omega}
be a holomorphic family in
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. Put
P(z)=P_{\rho(z)}
, where
(\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega}
is a holomorphic family in
S^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. The order
w(z)
of
\rho(z)
is an analytic function on
\Omega
. Thus, for any open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\!\subset\Omega
, there is
m\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Re w(z)<m
. As explained in Remark
4.11
this implies that
(\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega^{\prime}}
is a holomorphic family in
\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
.

Given any symbol
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
denote by
\tilde{\rho}(\xi)
the symbol (
3.3
) associated with
\rho_{k}=\rho(k)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
By
[
33
, Lemma 5.21]
this gives rise to a continuous linear map
\rho(\xi)\rightarrow\tilde{\rho}(\xi)-\rho(\xi)
from
\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
to
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. It then follows that
(\tilde{\rho}(z)-\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega^{\prime}}
is a holomorphic family in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. As mentioned in §
3.4
we have
\tilde{\rho}(z)(\xi)=\rho_{\!{}_{P_{\rho(z)}}}(\xi)=\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}}(\xi)
. Therefore, we see that
(\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}})_{z\in\Omega^{\prime}}
and
(\rho(z))_{z\in\Omega^{\prime}}
differ by a holomorphic family in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. We then deduce that
(\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}})_{z\in\Omega^{\prime}}
is a holomorphic family in
S^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
over every set open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\!\subset\Omega
, and so this is a holomorphic family over all
\Omega
. This shows that (i) implies (ii). The proof is complete.
∎

Definition 4.19
.

A functional
T\colon\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
analytic
when, for every holomorphic family
(P(z))_{z\in\Omega}\subset\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, the composition

is an analytic function on

.

Remark 4.20

(analytic continuation principle)
.

As every
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
can be embedded in a holomorphic gauging, in the same way as in Remark
4.13
we see that any analytic functional on
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
is uniquely determined by its restriction to operators of order
<m
for any given
m\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 4.21
.

The
canonical trace

\operatorname{TR}\colon\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{TR}(P)=\tilde{L}[\tau\circ\rho_{\!{}_{P}}],\qquad P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),

(4.7)

where the symbol
\rho_{\!{}_{P}}(\xi)
is defined as in (
3.4
).

Remark 4.22
.

The linearity of
\tilde{L}
(cf. Remark
4.15
) and the linearity of the symbol map
P\rightarrow\rho_{\!{}_{P}}
ensure us that

\operatorname{TR}
satisfies the linearity properties (
4.2
)–(
4.3
). We will see later that the canonical trace is indeed a trace (see Proposition
4.26
below).

Proposition 4.23
.

The following holds.

1.

The functional
\operatorname{TR}\colon\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the unique analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
of the ordinary trace.

2.

Let
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. For every holomorphic gauging
(P(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
of
P
, the composition
\operatorname{TR}[P(z)]\!
has at worst a simple pole singularity near
z=0
, and we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\operatorname{TR}[P(z)]=-\operatorname{Res}(P).

(4.8)

Proof.

If
P\in\Psi^{<-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\cap\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
\rho_{\!{}_{P}}(\xi)\in S^{<-n}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
, and so by Lemma
4.14
and the integral formula (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{TR}(P)=\tilde{L}[\tau\circ\rho_{\!{}_{P}}]=L[\tau\circ\rho_{\!{}_{P}}]=\operatorname{Tr}(P).

Moreover, if
(P(z))_{z\in\Omega}
is a holomorphic family in
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
(\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}})_{z\in\Omega}
is a holomorphic family in
S^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. As
\tau
is a continuous linear form on
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
the composition
(\tau\circ\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}})_{z\in\Omega}
is a holomorphic family in
S^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. The first part of Lemma
4.14
then ensures us that
\operatorname{TR}[P(z)]=\tilde{L}[\tau\circ\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}}]
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. This shows that
\operatorname{TR}
is an analytic extension of the ordinary trace to
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. The unique continuation principle mentioned in Remark
4.20
then ensures us that this is the unique such functional on
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

Finally, given
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, let
(P(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
be a holomorphic gauging of
P
. It follows from Lemma
4.18
and the continuity of
\tau
that
(\tau[\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}}])_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
is a holomorphic gauging of
\tau[\rho_{\!{}_{P}}(\xi)]
in
S^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. Therefore, by the 2nd part of Lemma
4.14
the function
\operatorname{TR}[P(z)]
has at worst a simple pole singularity near
z=0
, and we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\operatorname{TR}[P(z)]=\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\tilde{L}[\tau\circ\rho_{\!{}_{P(z)}}]=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau[\rho_{-n}(\xi)]=-\operatorname{Res}(P),

where
\rho_{-n}(\xi)
is the symbol of degree
-n
of
P
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.24
.

The right-hand side of (

) involves the symbol map

whose definition (

)

depends on the choice of the function

. It follows from the uniqueness contents of the 1st part of Proposition

that the canonical trace is independent of the choice of

.

Remark 4.25
.

In
[
15
]
the authors attempted to define a canonical trace on NC 2-tori (see
[

, Definition 2]
). This definition is ambiguous, since it depends on the choice of symbol, which is not unique. Moreover, on operators of order

this does not agree with the ordinary trace. Namely, for operators

the definition gives

, which need not agree with the trace of

in general (cf. Remark

Proposition 4.26
.

The functional
\operatorname{TR}
is a trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

Remark 4.27

Proposition

is mentioned in

. However, some further arguments are required to complete the proof. It is a bit lengthly to complete the proof by the same approach

4.3
Proofs of Propositions

and

The approach relies on the following result.

Lemma 4.28

(
[
32
,
34
]
)
.

Let
(P(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
and
(Q(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
be holomorphic families in
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Then the composition
(P(z)Q(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
is a holomorphic family in
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
as well.

We first prove Proposition

since the result will be used to prove Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition
4.26
.

Let
P_{1}\in\Psi^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
P_{2}\in\Psi^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with
q_{1}+q_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. Let
(P_{1}(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
and
(P_{2}(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
be holomorphic gaugings of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, respectively. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
set
R(z):=[P_{1}(z/2),P_{2}(z/2)]
. It follows from Lemma
4.28
that
(R(z))_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
is a holomorphic gauging of the commutator
R(0)=[P_{1},P_{2}]
, and so
\operatorname{TR}(R(z))
is an analytic function on
\Omega:=\mathbb{C}\setminus(-q_{1}-q_{2}+\mathbb{Z})
.

Without any loss of generality we may assume
\Re q_{1}\geq\Re q_{2}
. Let
z\in\Omega
be such that
\Re(z+q_{1})\leq 0
and
\Re(z+q_{2})<-2n
. This ensures us that
P_{1}(z/2)
has non-positive order, and hence is bounded, while
P_{2}(z)
and
R(z)
have order
<-n
, and hence are trace-class. Thus,

\displaystyle\operatorname{TR}(R(z))=\operatorname{Tr}(R(z))=\operatorname{Tr}([P_{1}(z/2),P_{2}(z/2)])=0.

The unique analytic continuation principle then implies that
\operatorname{TR}(R(z))=0
for all
z\in\Omega
. In particular, for
z=0
we get
\operatorname{TR}([P_{1},P_{2}])=\operatorname{TR}(R(0))=0
. This proves Proposition
4.26
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.6
.

Let
P_{1}\in\Psi^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
P_{2}\in\Psi^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with

5
Pseudodifferential commutators and derivatives

In this section, we derive a decomposition of
\Psi
DOs as sums of
\Psi
DO commutators modulo smoothing operators.
As with classical
\Psi
DOs on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see, e.g.,
[
22
,
26
,
35
]
) this is done by looking at the relationships between derivatives and commutators with elementary differential operators.

5.1
Sums of
\boldsymbol{\delta_{j}}
-derivatives

We have the following relationships between
\delta_{j}
-derivatives and commutators.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
j=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\displaystyle[\delta_{j},P_{\rho}]=P_{\delta_{j}\rho}.

Proof.

Given
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let
u=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k}
be in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
. As
\delta_{j}u=\sum u_{k}\delta_{j}\big(U^{k}\big)=\sum k_{j}u_{k}U^{k}
, we have

P_{\rho}(\delta_{j}u)=\sum u_{k}k_{j}\rho(k)U^{k}
. We also have

\displaystyle\delta_{j}(P_{\rho}u)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\delta_{j}\big(\rho(k)U^{k}\big)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}(\delta_{j}\rho(k))U^{k}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\rho(k)k_{j}U^{k}.

Thus,

\displaystyle P_{\rho}(\delta_{j}u)-\delta_{j}(P_{\rho}u)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}(\delta_{j}\rho(k))U^{k}=P_{\delta_{j}\rho}u.

This shows that
[\delta_{j},P_{\rho}]=P_{\delta_{j}\rho}
. The proof is complete.
∎

The above lemma implies that if the symbol of a
\Psi
DO is a sum of
\delta_{j}
-derivatives, then the operator is a sum of
\Psi
DO commutators. In order to exhibit symbols that are sums of
\delta_{j}
-derivatives we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Phi\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
be a continuous linear map. Then the following holds.

(i)

\Phi
induces a continuous linear map from
\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
to itself for every
m\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

\Phi
induces a linear map from
S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
to itself for every
q\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

As
\Phi\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is a continuous linear map, for every open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we get a (continuous) linear map

C^{\infty}(U;\mathscr{A}_{\theta})\ni\rho(\xi)\rightarrow\Phi[\rho(\xi)]\in C^{\infty}(U;\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. By arguing along similar lines as that of the proof of
[
28
, Lemma 4.16(i)]
it can be shown that this induces a continuous linear map from
\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
to itself for every
m\in\mathbb{R}
. This gives (i).

It remains to prove (ii). Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
. We have
\rho(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\rho_{q-j}(\xi)
with
\rho_{q-j}(\xi)\in S_{q-j}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. Set
m=\Re q
, and let
\chi(\xi)\in C^{\infty}_{c}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
be such that
\chi(\xi)=1
near
\xi=0
. Then
\rho(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}(1-\chi(\xi))\rho_{q-j}(\xi)
, and so
\rho(\xi)-\sum\limits_{j<N}(1-\chi(\xi))\rho_{q-j}(\xi)
is contained in
\mathbb{S}^{m-N}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
for every
N\geq 1
. Applying the linear map
\Phi
and using (i) we then deduce that

\displaystyle\Phi\big[\rho(\xi)\big]-\sum_{j<N}(1-\chi(\xi))\Phi[\rho_{q-j}(\xi)]\in\mathbb{S}^{m-N}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)\qquad\forall\,N\geq 1.

(5.1)

Note that
\Phi[\rho_{q-j}(\xi)]
is in
C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0;\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
and is homogeneous of degree
q-j
, and so this is a symbol in
S_{q-j}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. Together with (
5.1
) this implies that
\Phi[\rho(\xi)]\sim\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\Phi[\rho_{q-j}(\xi)]
, and hence
\Phi[\rho(\xi)]
is a symbol in
S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. This proves (ii) and completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
. Then there are symbols
\rho_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\rho_{n}(\xi)
in
S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
such that

\displaystyle\rho(\xi)=\tau[\rho(\xi)]+\delta_{1}\rho_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\delta_{n}\rho_{n}(\xi).

(5.2)

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\delta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\delta_{n}^{2}
be the flat Laplacian on
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
. This is a selfadjoint unbounded operator on
\mathscr{H}_{\theta}
with domain
\mathscr{H}_{\theta}^{(2)}=\big\{u=\sum u_{k}U^{k}\in\mathscr{H}_{\theta};\,\sum|k|^{4}|u_{k}|^{2}<\infty\big\}
. The unitaries
U^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, forms an orthonormal eigenbasis for
\Delta
, since we have

\displaystyle\Delta\big(U^{k}\big)=|k|^{2}U^{k}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Let
\Delta^{-1}\colon\mathscr{H}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}_{\theta}
be the partial inverse of
\Delta
. Namely, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{-1}\big(U^{k}\big)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $k=0$},\\
|k|^{-2}U^{k}&\text{if $k\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Note that
\Delta\Delta^{-1}(1)=0
and
\Delta\Delta^{-1}\big(U^{k}\big)=|k|^{-2}\Delta\big(U^{k}\big)=U^{k}
if
k\neq 0
. Thus, for all
u=\sum u_{k}U^{k}
in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta\Delta^{-1}u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\Delta\Delta^{-1}\big(U^{k}\big)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus 0}u_{k}U^{k}=u-u_{0}=u-\tau(u).

(5.4)

We also observe that (
5.3
) implies that
\Delta^{-1}=P_{\rho}
with
\rho(\xi)=(1-\chi(\xi))|\xi|^{-2}
, where
\chi(\xi)
is any function in
C^{\infty}_{c}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that
\chi(\xi)=1
near
\xi=0
and
\chi(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\geq 1
. This shows that
\Delta^{-1}
is a
\Psi
DO of order
-2
, and so it induces a continuous linear map from
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
to itself.

Bearing all this in mind, let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
. For
j=1,\ldots,n
, set

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(\xi)=\delta_{j}\Delta^{-1}\big(\rho(\xi)\big),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

As
\delta_{j}\Delta^{-1}\colon\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is a continuous linear map, it follows from Lemma
5.2
that
\rho_{j}(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. Moreover, by using (
5.4
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\delta_{j}\rho_{j}(\xi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\delta_{j}^{2}\Delta^{-1}(\rho(\xi))=\Delta\Delta^{-1}(\rho(\xi))=\rho(\xi)-\tau[\rho(\xi)].

That is,
\rho(\xi)=\tau[\rho(\xi)]+\delta_{1}\rho_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\delta_{n}\rho_{n}(\xi)
.
The proof is complete.
∎

5.2
Sums of finite differences

The decomposition (
5.2
) reduces the decomposition of
\Psi
DOs as sums of commutators to the case of
\Psi
DOs with scalar symbols.
For classical symbols on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
it is natural to attempt to get a decomposition as sums of
\partial_{\xi_{j}}
-derivatives, since this amounts to taking commutators with coordinates functions
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.

In the noncommutative setting, however, coordinate functions do not exist. Furthermore, even on ordinary tori the coordinate functions
x_{j}
do not acts on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
since they are not periodic. Nevertheless, as the following lemma shows, there is a simple relationship between finite differences of symbols and commutators with the unitaries
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
j=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\displaystyle P_{\Delta_{j}\rho}=\big[P_{U_{j}^{-1}\rho},U_{j}\big].

Proof.

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Note that
U_{j}^{-1}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. Moreover, for all
u=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k}
in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle P_{U_{j}^{-1}\rho}u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U_{j}^{-1}\rho(k)U^{k}=U_{j}\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\rho(k)U^{k}\bigg)=U_{j}^{-1}P_{\rho}u.

This shows that
P_{U_{j}^{-1}\rho}=U_{j}^{-1}P_{\rho}
.

Given any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
there is
c_{j,k}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
U_{j}U^{k}={\rm e}^{-2{\rm i}\pi c_{j,k}}U^{k+e_{j}}
(see, e.g.,
[
28
, equation (2.4)]
). Thus,

\displaystyle P_{\rho}\big(U_{j}U^{k}\big)={\rm e}^{-2{\rm i}\pi c_{j,k}}P_{\rho}\big(U^{k+e_{j}}\big)={\rm e}^{-2{\rm i}\pi c_{j,k}}\rho(k+e_{j})U^{k+e_{j}}=\rho(k+e_{j})U_{j}U^{k}.

This implies that
U_{j}^{-1}P_{\rho}\big(U_{j}U^{k}\big)=\rho(k+e_{j})U^{k}
. It then follows that, for all
u=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k}
in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle U_{j}^{-1}\big(P_{\rho}U_{j}\big)u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U_{j}^{-1}P_{\rho}\big(U-jU^{k}\big)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\rho(k+e_{j})U^{k}=P_{\rho(\cdot+e_{j})}u.

This shows that
U_{j}^{-1}P_{\rho}U_{j}=P_{\rho(\cdot+e_{j})}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\big[P_{U_{j}^{-1}\rho},U_{j}\big]=\big[U_{j}^{-1}P_{\rho},U_{j}\big]=U_{j}^{-1}P_{\rho}U_{j}-P_{\rho}=P_{\rho(\cdot+e_{j})}-P_{\rho}=P_{\Delta_{j}\rho}.

The result is proved.
∎

The above lemma suggests to seek for a decomposition of symbols as sums of finite differences. The strategy is to use the decomposition as sums of
\partial_{\xi_{j}}
-derivatives and re-express the
\partial_{\xi_{j}}
-derivatives as finite differences.

Lemma 5.5

(
[
22
,
25
]
)
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that one of the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

q
is not an integer
\geq-n
.

(ii)

q
is an integer
\geq-n
and
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{-n}(\xi){\rm d}^{n-1}\xi=0
.

Then there are symbols
\rho_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\rho_{n}(\xi)
in
S^{q+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that

\displaystyle\rho(\xi)=\partial_{\xi_{1}}\rho(\xi)+\cdots+\partial_{\xi_{n}}\rho(\xi)\quad\bmod\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

The following lemma provides us with a relationship between partial differential derivatives and finite differences for classical symbols. Its proof is postponed to Section
8
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, for
j=1,\ldots,n
, there is a symbol
\rho_{j}(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that

\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)=\Delta_{j}\rho_{j}(\xi)\bmod\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
.

By combining Lemmas
5.5
and
5.6
we arrive at the following description of sums of finite differences modulo
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
, satisfy one of the conditions
(i)
or
(ii)
of Lemma
5.5
. Then there are symbols

\rho_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\rho_{n}(\xi)
in
S^{q+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that

\displaystyle\rho(\xi)=\Delta_{1}\rho_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\Delta_{n}\rho_{n}(\xi)\quad\bmod\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

5.3
Sums of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
DOs commutators

We are in a position to prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
P\in\Psi^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
. Assume that either
q\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, or
q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\operatorname{Res}(P)=0
. Then there are operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{q+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and operators
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
in
\Psi^{q}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
such that

\displaystyle P=[P_{1},U_{1}]+\cdots+[P_{n},U_{n}]+[\delta_{1},Q_{1}]+\cdots+[\delta_{n},Q_{n}]\quad\bmod\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

(5.5)

Proof.

Let us write
P=P_{\rho}
with
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. By Lemma
5.3
there are symbols
\sigma_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\sigma_{n}(\xi)
in

S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
such that

\displaystyle\rho(\xi)=\tau[\rho(\xi)]+\delta_{1}\sigma_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\delta_{n}\sigma_{n}(\xi).

Here
\tau[\rho(\xi)]\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. Moreover, by assumption either
q\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, or
q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau[\rho_{-n}(\xi)]{\rm d}^{n-1}\xi\allowbreak=\operatorname{Res}(P)=0
. This implies that

\tau[\rho(\xi)]
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
5.7
. Therefore, there are symbols
\rho_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\rho_{n}(\xi)
in
S^{q+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that

\displaystyle\tau[\rho(\xi)]=\Delta_{1}\rho_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\Delta_{n}\rho_{n}(\xi)\quad\bmod\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle\rho(\xi)=\Delta_{1}\rho_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\Delta_{n}\rho_{n}(\xi)+\delta_{1}\sigma_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\delta_{n}\sigma_{n}(\xi)\quad\bmod\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

Combining this with Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.4
we then obtain

\displaystyle P=P_{\rho}=[P_{1},U_{1}]+\cdots+[P_{n},U_{n}]+[\delta_{1},Q_{1}]+\cdots+[\delta_{n},Q_{n}]\quad\bmod\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),

where we have set
P_{j}=P_{U_{j}\rho}\in\Psi^{q+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
Q_{j}=P_{\sigma_{j}}\in\Psi^{q}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. The proof is complete.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
5.8
we can easily recover the uniqueness result of
[
40
]
for the noncommutative residue. Recall that a trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is called
singular
when it is annihilated by smoothing operators.

Corollary 5.9

(
[
40
]
)
.

The noncommutative residue is the unique singular trace up to constant multiple on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

Proof.

The noncommutative residue is a singular trace. Conversely, let
\varphi
be a singular trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Let
P_{0}\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
be such that
\operatorname{Res}(P_{0})=1
. Let
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and set
\tilde{P}=P-\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}
. Then
\operatorname{Res}(\tilde{P})=\operatorname{Res}(P)-\operatorname{Res}(P)\operatorname{Res}(P_{0})=0
, and so it follows from
Proposition
5.8
that
P=\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}
modulo
\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]+\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. As
\varphi
is annihilated by
\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]+\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we deduce that
\varphi(P)=\operatorname{Res}(P)\varphi(P_{0})
. That is,
\varphi=c\operatorname{Res}
, with
c:=\varphi(P_{0})
. Thus, every singular trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a scalar multiple of the noncommutative residue. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

It was shown in
[
19
,
21
]
that in dimension
n=2
and
n=4
the noncommutative residue is the unique exotic trace up to constant multiple on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. In the terminology of
[
40
]
a trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is called
exotic
when it vanishes on
\Psi
DOs of order
\leq m
for some given
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

6
Commutator structure of smoothing operators

The decomposition (
5.5
) holds modulo smoothing operators. In this section, we study the commutator structure of smoothing operators.

It is well known that on a closed manifold a smoothing operator is a sum of commutators of smoothing operators if and only if its trace vanishes (see
[
27
,
56
]
). We shall now establish the analogue of this result for smoothing operators on
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n}
. To reach this end we need the following characterization of smoothing operators.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
R\in\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{H}_{\theta})
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

R
is a smoothing operator.

(ii)

For every
N\geq 0
, there is
C_{N}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle R\big(U^{k}\big)|U^{\ell}\big\rangle\big|\leq C_{N}(1+|k|+|\ell|)^{-N}\qquad\forall\,k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(6.1)

Proof.

The proof relies on the following claim.

Claim
.

A sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\subset\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is contained in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
if and only if, for every
N\geq 0
, there is
C_{N}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle a_{k}|U^{\ell}\big\rangle\big|\leq C_{N}(1+|k|+|\ell|)^{-N}\qquad\forall\,k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(6.2)

Proof of the claim.

The Fourier series decomposition
u=\sum u_{k}U^{k}
provides us with a topological isomorphism between
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
and
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\big)
(see
[
7
,
28
]
). This means that the topology of
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is generated by the semi-norms,

\displaystyle u\longrightarrow\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|\ell|)^{-M}\big|\big\langle u|U^{\ell}\big\rangle\big|,\qquad M\geq 0.

Therefore, a sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\subset\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
is contained in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
if and only if, for all
M,N\geq 0
there is
C_{MN}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|\ell|)^{-M}\big|\big\langle u|U^{\ell}\big\rangle\big|\leq C_{MN}(1+|k|)^{-N}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

That is,

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle u|U^{\ell}\big\rangle\big|\leq C_{MN}(1+|k|)^{-N}(1+|\ell|)^{-M}\qquad\forall\,k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(6.3)

The proof of the claim is then completed by observing that the estimates (
6.3
) are equivalent to the estimates (
6.2
) thanks to the inequalities

(1+|k|)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\ell|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(1+|k|+|\ell|)\leq(1+|k|)(1+|\ell|).

Suppose that
R
is a smoothing operator. By Proposition
3.26
this is the toroidal
\Psi
DO associated with a symbol
(\rho_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
in

\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}\big)
. We then have
R\big(U^{k}\big)=\rho_{k}U^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Note that if a sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
, then

\big(a_{k}U^{k}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}\big)
as well (see
[
28
, Lemma 3.29]
). Therefore, we see that
\big(R\big(U^{k}\big)\big)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. The claim above then ensures that
R
satisfies the estimates (
6.1
).

Conversely, suppose that the estimates (
6.1
) are satisfied. The above claim implies that the sequence
\big(R\big(U^{k}\big)\big)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. Set
\rho_{k}=R\big(U^{k}\big)\big(U^{k}\big)^{-1}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then
(\rho_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is in
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
as well.
Let
R^{\prime}
be the associated toroidal
\Psi
DO. This is a smoothing operator by Proposition
3.26
.
Moreover, by using the continuity of
R
, for all
u=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k}
in
\mathscr{A}_{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle Ru=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}R\big(U^{k}\big)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\rho_{k}U^{k}=R^{\prime}u.

Thus,
R^{\prime}=R
, and hence
R
is a smoothing operator. The proof is complete.
∎

In the terminology of
[
27
, Appendix A]
, the estimates (
6.1
) mean that the operator
R
is smoothing with respect to the orthonormal basis
\big\{U^{k}\big\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
. Therefore, by using
[
27
, Theorem A.1]
we arrive at the following statement.

Proposition 6.2
.

For every
R\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that
\operatorname{Tr}(R)=0
there are operators
R_{1},\ldots,R_{4}
in
\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that
R=[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}]
.

Thanks to the above lemma we see that in order to show that every smoothing operators is a sum of
\Psi
DO commutators we only need to exhibit one such operator that has a non-zero trace. For smoothing operators in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
this is done in
[
48
]
by using the Green function of the Laplacian to construct a smoothing operator whose symbol is a sum of derivatives. We shall proceed somewhat similarly by exhibiting a smoothing operator with non-zero trace whose symbol is a sum of finite differences.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\chi(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
be such that
\check{\chi}(x)=1
near
x=0
and
\operatorname{supp}\check{\chi}\subset(-2\pi,2\pi)^{n}
. Then there are symbols
\rho_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\rho_{n}(\xi)
in
S^{-n+2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that
\chi=\Delta_{1}\rho_{1}+\cdots+\Delta_{n}\rho_{n}
.

The proof of Lemma
6.3
is postponed to Section
9
. Assuming this lemma we shall prove the following result.

Lemma 6.4
.

There exists
R_{0}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with
\operatorname{Tr}(R_{0})=1
for which there are
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{-n+1}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
such that

R_{0}=[P_{1},U_{1}]+\cdots+[P_{n},U_{n}]
.

Proof.

Let
\chi(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
be such that
\check{\chi}(x)=1
near
x=0
and
\operatorname{supp}\check{\chi}\subset(-2\pi,2\pi)^{n}
. Set
R_{0}=(2\pi)^{-n}P_{\chi}
. Combining the trace formula (
3.5
) with Poisson’s summation formula gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(R_{0})=(2\pi)^{-n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\chi(k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\check{\chi}(2\pi k)=\check{\chi}(0)=1.

Moreover, by Lemma
6.3
there are symbols
\rho_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\rho_{n}(\xi)
in
S^{-n+2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that
\chi=\Delta_{1}\rho_{1}+\cdots+\Delta_{n}\rho_{n}
.
Combining this with Lemma
5.4
we then get

\displaystyle R_{0}=(2\pi)^{-n}P_{\chi}=[U_{1},P_{1}]+\cdots+[U_{n},P_{n}],

where we have set
P_{j}=(2\pi)^{-n}P_{U_{j}^{-1}\rho_{j}}\in\Psi^{-n+1}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
. The result is proved.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 6.5
.

For every
R\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, there are operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{-n+1}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
and operators
R_{1},\ldots,R_{4}
in
\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that

\displaystyle R=[P_{1},U_{1}]+\cdots+[P_{n},U_{n}]+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}].

In particular, every smoothing operator is contained in
\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]
.

Proof.

Let
R_{0}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
be as in Lemma
6.4
. Let
R\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and set
\tilde{R}=R-\operatorname{Tr}(R)R_{0}
. Then
\tilde{R}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and, as
\operatorname{Tr}(R_{0})=1
, we have
\operatorname{Tr}(\tilde{R})=0
. Therefore, by Lemma
6.2
there are operators
R_{1},\ldots,R_{4}
in
\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that
\tilde{R}=[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}]
. Thus,

\displaystyle R=\operatorname{Tr}(R)R_{0}+\tilde{R}=\operatorname{Tr}(R)R_{0}+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}].

We also know by Lemma
6.4
that there are operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{-n+1}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
such that
R_{0}=\sum[P_{j},U_{j}]
. It then follows that we have

\displaystyle R=\operatorname{Tr}(R)R_{0}+\tilde{R}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[\tilde{P}_{j},U_{j}]+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}],

where we have set
\tilde{P}_{j}=\operatorname{Tr}(R)P_{j}\in\Psi^{-n+1}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. The proof is complete.
∎

7
Uniqueness theorems

In this section, we prove our main uniqueness results for the noncommutative residue and the canonical trace on noncommutative tori.

7.1
Integer-order
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
DOs

For integer-order
\Psi
DOs we have the following decomposition modulo sums of
\Psi
DO commutators.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
P_{0}\in\Psi^{-n}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
be such that
\operatorname{Res}(P_{0})=1
. Then, for every
P\in\Psi^{m}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{m^{\prime}+1}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
with
m^{\prime}\!=\!\max(m,-n)
, operators
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
in
\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and smoothing operators
R_{1},\ldots,R_{4}
such that

\displaystyle P=\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[P_{j},U_{j}]+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[\delta_{j},Q_{j}]+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}].

In particular, for all
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle P=\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}\quad\bmod\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big].

(7.1)

Proof.

Set
\tilde{P}=P-\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}
. We have
\operatorname{Res}\big(\tilde{P}\big)=\operatorname{Res}(P)-\operatorname{Res}(P)\operatorname{Res}(P_{0})=0
. Moreover, if
m\geq-n
, then
\tilde{P}\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. If
m<-n
, then
\tilde{P}
is in
\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, since
\operatorname{Res}(P)=0
, and so
\tilde{P}=P\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. In any case, it follows from Proposition
5.8
that there are operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, operators
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
in
\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and an operator
R\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{P}=[P_{1},U_{1}]+\cdots+[P_{n},U_{n}]+[\delta_{1},Q_{1}]+\cdots+[\delta_{n},Q_{n}]+R.

Moreover, by Proposition
6.5
there are operators
P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{n}^{\prime}
in
\Psi^{-n+1}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
and operators
R_{1},\ldots,\allowbreak R_{4}
in
\Psi^{-\infty}
such that

\displaystyle R=[P_{1},U_{1}]+\cdots+[P_{n},U_{n}]+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}].

As
P=\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}+\tilde{P}
we then deduce that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[\tilde{P}_{j},U_{j}]+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[\delta_{j},Q_{j}]+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}],

where we have set
\tilde{P}_{j}=P_{j}+P_{j}^{\prime}
. Note that
\tilde{P}_{j}\in\Psi^{m^{\prime}+1}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
with
m^{\prime}=\max(m,-n)
. The proof is complete.
∎

It follows from (
7.1
) that if
\operatorname{Res}(P)=0
, then
P\in\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]
. Conversely, we know from Proposition
4.6
that the noncommutative residues is annihilated by the commutator space
\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]
. Therefore, we arrive at the following statement.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. Then
P\in\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]
if and only if
\operatorname{Res}(P)=0
.

We are now in a position to prove our first uniqueness result.

Theorem 7.3
.

Every trace on the algebra
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
is a constant multiple of the noncommutative residue.

Proof.

We know by Proposition
4.6
that
\operatorname{Res}
is a trace on the algebra
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
. Let
\varphi
be another trace. Let
P_{0}\in\Psi^{-n}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
be such that
\operatorname{Res}(P_{0})=1
. As
\varphi
vanishes on the commutator space
\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big]
, by using (
7.1
) we see that, for all
P\in\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(P)=\varphi[\operatorname{Res}(P)P_{0}]=\varphi(P_{0})\operatorname{Res}(P).

This shows that
\varphi=\varphi(P_{0})\operatorname{Res}
, i.e.,
\varphi
is a scalar multiple of
\operatorname{Res}
. The proof is complete.
∎

The first uniqueness result for the noncommutative residue was established by Wodzicki

7.2
Non-integer order
\boldsymbol{\Psi}

We have the following version of Proposition
7.1
for non-integer order
\Psi
DOs.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
R_{0}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
be such that
\operatorname{Tr}(R_{0})=1
. For every
P\in\Psi^{q}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
,
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, there are operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{q+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, operators
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
in
\Psi^{q}(\mathscr{A}_{\theta})
, and smoothing operators
R_{1},\ldots,R_{4}
such that

\displaystyle P=\operatorname{TR}(P)R_{0}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[P_{j},U_{j}]+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[\delta_{j},Q_{j}]+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}].

(7.2)

In particular, we have

\displaystyle P=\operatorname{TR}(P)R_{0}\quad\bmod\big[\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\Psi^{q+\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big].

(7.3)

Proof.

Let
P\in\Psi^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. By Proposition
5.8
there are operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
in
\Psi^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
, operators
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
in
\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and an operator
R\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that

\displaystyle P=[P_{1},U_{1}]+\cdots+[P_{n},U_{n}]+[\delta_{1},Q_{1}]+\cdots+[\delta_{n},Q_{n}]+R.

Set
\tilde{R}=R-\operatorname{Tr}(R)R_{0}
. Then
\tilde{R}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
\operatorname{Tr}\big(\tilde{R}\big)=\operatorname{Tr}(R)-\operatorname{Tr}(R)\operatorname{Tr}(R_{0})=0
. Therefore, by Proposition
6.2
there are operators

in
\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that

, i.e.,

\displaystyle P=\operatorname{Tr}(R)R_{0}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[P_{j},U_{j}]+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}[\delta_{j},Q_{j}]+[R_{1},R_{2}]+[R_{3},R_{4}].

(7.4)

As

is a trace functional on

that agrees with the ordinary trace on smoothing operators, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{TR}(P)=\operatorname{Tr}(R)\operatorname{TR}(R_{0})=\operatorname{Tr}(R)\operatorname{Tr}(R_{0})=\operatorname{Tr}(R).

Combining this with (
7.4
) gives the decomposition (
7.2
). The proof is complete.

It follows from (

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
P\in\Psi^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,

. Then

We are now in a position to prove our second uniqueness result.

Theorem 7.8
.

Every trace on
\Psi^{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a scalar multiple of the canonical trace
\operatorname{TR}
.

Proof.

We know from Proposition
4.26
that
\operatorname{TR}
is a trace on

T
be another trace functional. In addition, let

,

\displaystyle T(P)=T[\operatorname{TR}(P)R_{0}]=T(R_{0})\operatorname{TR}(P).

8
Proof of Lemma
5.6

In this section, we prove Lemma
5.6
. The proof is a consequence of a few intermediate results.

We record the following version of Peetre’s inequality.

Lemma 8.1

(see, e.g.,
[
1
, Lemma I.8.2]
)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\big(1+|\xi+\eta|\big)^{m}\leq\big(1+|\xi|\big)^{m}\big(1+|\eta|\big)^{|m|}.

(8.1)

The Taylor formula allows us to approximate finite differences
\Delta_{j}f(\xi)=f(\xi+e_{j})-f(\xi)
by Taylor polynomials
\sum\limits_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}f(\xi)
. Conversely, partial derivatives
\partial_{\xi_{j}}f(\xi)
can be approximated by finite differences. In particular, for one-variable functions we have the following result.

Lemma 8.2

(
[
52
, Theorem 3.3.39]
)
.

Let
N
be an integer
\geq 2
. Then there is
C_{N}>0
such that, for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\varphi^{\prime}(t)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\Delta_{1}^{\ell}\varphi(t)\bigg|\leq C_{N}\sup_{s\in[0,N-1]}\big|\varphi^{(N)}(t+s)\big|.

By
[
28
, Lemma 6.10]
if
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
, then
\Delta^{\alpha}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m-|\alpha|}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
for every multi-order
\alpha
. Keeping this in mind, we have the following result.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
j=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)\sim\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell+1}\Delta_{j}^{\ell+1}\rho(\xi).

Proof.

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
N\geq 2
. Given any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by applying Lemma
8.2
to
\varphi(t)=\rho(\xi+te_{j})
we get

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\bigg|\leq C_{N}\sup_{0\leq t\leq N-1}\big|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N}\rho(\xi+te_{j})\big|.

(8.2)

As
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
there is
C>0
such that
|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N}\rho(\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{m-N}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Together with Peetre’s inequality (
8.1
) this implies that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,N-1]
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N}\rho(\xi+te_{j})\big|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\xi+t_{j}|)^{m-N}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{m-N}(1+t)^{|m-N|}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{|m-N|}C(1+|\xi|)^{m-N}.

Combining this with (
8.2
) we see that there is
C_{N0}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\bigg|\leq C_{N0}C(1+|\xi|)^{m-N}.

Likewise, given any multi-order
\alpha
, as
\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m-|\alpha|}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
, there is
C_{N\alpha}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\bigg(\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho\bigg)(\xi)\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\rho(\xi)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C_{N0}C(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\alpha|-N}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m-N}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\qquad\forall\,N\geq 2.

It then follows that
\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)
. This proves the result.
∎

As mentioned above, if
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
, then
\Delta^{\alpha}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m-|\alpha|}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
for every multi-order
\alpha
. For classical symbols we further have the following result.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
\Delta^{\alpha}\rho(\xi)\in S^{q-|\alpha|}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the result when
|\alpha|=1
. Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
N\geq 1
. Then by Taylor’s formula for differential maps with values in locally convex spaces (see, e.g.,
[
28
, Proposition C.15]
), for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)

\displaystyle=\rho(x+e_{j})-\sum_{0\leq l\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{N}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N+1}\rho(\xi+te_{j}){\rm d}t.

Thus,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{N}\big\|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N+1}\rho(\xi+te_{j})\big\|{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)!}\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\big\|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N+1}\rho(\xi+te_{j})\big\|.

(8.3)

Set
m=\Re q
. As
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. There is
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N+1}\rho(\xi)\big\|\leq C\big(1+|\xi|)^{m-N-1}\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Together with Peetre’s inequality (
8.1
) this implies that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{N+1}\rho(\xi+te_{j})\big\|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+|\xi+te_{j}|)^{m-N-1}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{m-N-1}(1+t)^{|m-N-1|}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{|m-N-1|}C(1+|\xi|)^{m-N-1}.

Combining this with (
8.3
) then shows there is
C_{N}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\bigg\|\leq C_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{m-N-1}.

Likewise, given any multi-orders
\alpha
and
\beta
, as
\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\rho(\xi)\in S^{q-|\beta|}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
, there is
C_{N\alpha\beta}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\bigg(\Delta_{j}\rho-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho\bigg)(\xi)\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\Delta_{j}\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\rho(\xi)\bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{N\alpha\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\beta|-N-1}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m-N-1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)\qquad\forall\,N\geq 1.

It then follows that
\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)
. As
\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\in S^{q-\ell}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
for every
\ell\geq 1
, we then deduce that
\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)\in S^{q-1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big)
. This proves the result when
|\alpha|=1
. The proof is complete.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Lemma
5.6
.

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
,
q\in\mathbb{C}
, and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We know by Lemma
8.3
that
\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)\!\sim\!\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\!\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell+1}\!\Delta_{j}^{\ell+1}\rho(\xi).\!
Moreover, it follows from Lemma
8.4
that
\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\!\in\!S^{q-\ell}\!\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathscr{A}_{\theta}\big).\!
By the Borel lemma for scalar-valued symbols (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Proposition I.2.3]
) there is
\rho_{j}(\xi)\in S^{q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that
\rho_{j}(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell+1}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)
.

Set
m=\Re q
. The fact that
\rho_{j}(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\Delta_{j}^{\ell+1}\rho(\xi)
means that, for every
N\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(\xi)-\sum_{\ell<N}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell+1}\Delta_{j}^{\ell}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m-N}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

As
\Delta_{j}
maps
\mathbb{S}^{m-N}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
to
\mathbb{S}^{m-N-1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\rho_{j}(\xi)-\sum_{\ell<N}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell+1}\Delta_{j}^{\ell+1}\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m-N-1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\qquad\forall\,N\geq 1.

This means that
\Delta_{j}\rho_{j}(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell+1}\Delta_{j}^{\ell+1}\rho(\xi)
. As
\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell+1}\Delta_{j}^{\ell+1}\rho(\xi)
, we then deduce that
\partial_{\xi_{j}}\rho(\xi)-\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. This proves the result.
∎

9
Proof of Lemma
6.3

In this section, we prove Lemma
6.3
. The proof is based on the characterization of the Fourier transforms of symbols on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In what follows we say that a continuous function
K(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
has
bounded support
when its support is a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
. Equivalently, there is
R>0
such that
K(x)=0
for
|x|>R
. In particular, if
K(x)
is a such a function and is integrable near
x=0
, then
K(x)
defines a compactly supported distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and so it has a smooth Fourier transform
\hat{K}(\xi)
.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
K(x)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0\big)
have bounded support. Assume that near
x=0
there are analytic functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
such that

\displaystyle K(x)=\frac{f(x)}{g(x)},\qquad f(x)=\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{a}\big),\qquad g(x)\sim|x|^{2b},

(9.1)

where
a
and
b
are non-negative integers such that
a-2b>-n
. Then
\hat{K}(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-m+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
, with
m=n+a-2b
.

Proof.

Set
m=n+a-2b\in\mathbb{N}
. The analyticity of
f(x)
and
g(x)
and (
9.1
) ensure us that, for every multi-order
\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}K(x)=\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{-n+m-|\alpha|}\big)\qquad\text{near $x=0$}.

(9.2)

In particular, for
\alpha=0
we see that
K(x)
is integrable near
x=0
. As
K(x)
is smooth outside the origin and has bounded support, we see that
K(x)\in L^{1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
, and so the Fourier transform
\hat{K}(\xi)
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, the boundedness of the support of
K
implies that
\hat{K}(\xi)
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

More generally, the asymptotic (
9.2
) ensures us that, for every multi-order
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq m-1
, the partial derivative
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}K(x)
is integrable, and hence has a bounded Fourier transform. Thus, there is
C_{\alpha}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\xi^{\alpha}\hat{K}(\xi)\big|=\big|\big(\widehat{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}K}\big)(\xi)\big|\leq C_{\alpha}.

As
(1+|\xi|+\cdots+|\xi_{n}|)^{m-1}
is a universal linear combination of monomials
|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots|\xi_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}=|\xi^{\alpha}|
with
|\alpha|\leq m-1
we deduce there is
C>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\hat{K}(\xi)\big|\leq C\big(1+|\xi_{1}|+\cdots+|\xi_{n}|\big)^{-m+1}\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{-m+1}.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
. We have
D_{\xi}^{\beta}\hat{K}(\xi)=\big(\widehat{x^{\beta}K}\big)(\xi)
. Note that
x^{\beta}K(x)
is a
C^{\infty}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
with bounded support.
Moreover, the asymptotic (
9.2
) for
K(x)
implies that
x^{\beta}K(x)
satisfies the corresponding asymptotic for
m+|\beta|
. Therefore, by the first part of the proof there is
C_{\beta}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\big|D_{\xi}^{\beta}\hat{K}(\xi)|=\big|\big(\widehat{x^{\beta}K}\big)(\xi)\big|\leq C_{\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{-m-|\beta|+1}.

This shows that
\hat{K}(\xi)
is contained in
\mathbb{S}^{-m+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
K(x)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0\big)
be such that there is
f(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
such that

\displaystyle K(x)=|x|^{-2b}f(x),\qquad f(x)=\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{a}\big)\quad\text{near $x=0$},

where
a
and
b
are non-negative integers such that
a-2b>-n
. Then
\hat{K}(\xi)\in S^{-n-a+2b}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
.

Proof.

As
f(x)
is
C^{\infty}
and is
\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{a}\big)
near
x=0
, by Taylor’s formula, for every
N>a
, we have

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{a\leq|\alpha|<N}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|<}x^{\alpha}r_{N\alpha}(x),

where
a_{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{\alpha!}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(0)
and
r_{N\alpha}(x)\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. Thus, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
, we have

\displaystyle K(x)=|x|^{-2b}f(x)=\sum_{a\leq|\alpha|<N}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}|x|^{-2b}+\sum_{|\alpha|<}x^{\alpha}|x|^{-2b}r_{N\alpha}(x).

Note that if
N>2b
and
|\alpha|=N
, then
x^{\alpha}|x|^{-2b}r_{N\alpha}(x)
is
C^{N-2b-1}
. Thus,

\displaystyle K(x)-\sum_{a\leq|\alpha|<N}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}|x|^{-2b}\in C^{N-2b-1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\qquad\forall\,N>2b.

Here
K(x)
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
and has bounded support. Moreover,
x^{\alpha}|x|^{-2b}
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
and homogeneous of degree
|\alpha|-2b\geq a-2b>-n
for
|\alpha|\geq a
. Therefore, it follows from the characterization of the Fourier transforms of classical symbols of negative integer order (see
[
55
, Proposition 7.3.2]
) that
\hat{K}(\xi)\in S^{-(n+a-2b)}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. The proof is complete.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Lemma
6.3

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. For
j=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{j}\rho)^{\vee}(x)

\displaystyle=\int{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}(\rho(\xi+e_{j})-\rho(\xi)){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}\xi,

\displaystyle=\int{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x\cdot(\xi-e_{j})}\rho(\xi){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}\xi-\int{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}\rho(\xi){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}\xi

\displaystyle=\big({\rm e}^{-x_{j}}-1\big)\int{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}\rho(\xi){\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mu{\rm d}}}\xi,

\displaystyle=\big({\rm e}^{-x_{j}}-1\big)\check{\rho}(x).

That is, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\rho(\xi)=\big[\big({\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\check{\rho}\big]^{\wedge}(\xi).

(9.3)

This formula extends by continuity to all tempered distributions
\rho(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
, where the extension of the operator
\Delta_{j}
to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta_{j}\rho,u\right\rangle=-\left\langle\rho,\overline{\Delta}_{j}u\right\rangle,\qquad\rho\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big),\ u\in\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

Here
\overline{\Delta}_{j}
is the
j
-th backward finite difference (
3.2
) on
\mathscr{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
.

Let
\nu\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

\displaystyle\nu(x)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\big|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big|^{2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\nu(x)=0\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\big|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big|^{2}=0\Longleftrightarrow{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{1}}=\cdots={\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{n}}=1\Longleftrightarrow x\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

We also have

\displaystyle\nu(x)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\big({\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}2(1-\cos(x_{j})).

Thus,
\nu(x)
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, as
2(1-\cos(t))=t^{2}+\operatorname{O}\big(t^{4}\big)
near
t=0
, we see that, near
x=0
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(x)=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}+\operatorname{O}\big(x_{1}^{4}+\cdots+x_{n}^{4}\big)=|x|^{2}+\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{4}\big).

(9.4)

In particular, this shows that
\nu(x)\sim|x|^{2}
near
x=0
.

Bearing this in mind, let
\chi(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
be such that
\check{\chi}(x)=1
near
x=0
and
\operatorname{supp}\check{\chi}\subset(-2\pi,2\pi)^{n}
. For
j=1,\ldots,n
, define

\displaystyle K_{j}(x)=\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1}{\nu(x)}\check{\chi}(x),\qquad x\neq 0.

As
\operatorname{supp}\check{\chi}\subset(-2\pi,2\pi)^{n}
and the only zero of
\nu(x)
on
(-2\pi,2\pi)^{n}
is the origin, this defines a smooth function with bounded support on

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
. Moreover, as
\check{\chi}(x)=1
near
x=0
, we see that
K_{j}(x)=\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\nu(x)^{-1}
near
x=0
. Here
{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x_{j}}-1
is analytic and is
\operatorname{O}(|x|)
near
x=0
. As mentioned above,
\nu(x)
is analytic function such that
\nu(x)\sim|x|^{2}
near
x=0
. Therefore, we see that
K_{j}(x)
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
9.1
with
a=b=1
, and so
\rho_{j}(\xi):=\widehat{K}_{j}(\xi)
is a symbol in
\mathbb{S}^{-n+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Moreover, by using (
9.3
) we see that
(\Delta_{j}\rho_{j})^{\vee}(x)
is equal to

\displaystyle\big({\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\check{\rho}_{j}(x)=\big({\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\frac{\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)}{\nu(x)}\check{\chi}(x)=\big|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big|^{2}\nu(x)^{-1}\check{\chi}(x).

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\big(\Delta_{j}\rho_{j}\big)^{\vee}(x)=\check{\chi}(x)\nu(x)^{-1}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\big|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big|^{2}=\check{\chi}(x)\nu(x)^{-1}\nu(x)=\check{\chi}(x).

Taking Fourier transforms then shows that
\chi(\xi)=\Delta_{1}\rho_{1}(\xi)+\cdots+\Delta_{n}\rho_{n}(\xi)
.

In order to complete the proof it remains to show that
\rho_{j}(\xi)\in S^{-n+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Set
\tilde{\nu}(x)=\nu(x)-|x|^{2}
. Then
\tilde{\nu}(x)
is analytic function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By (
9.4
) it is
\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{4}\big)
near
x=0
. Given any
N\geq 1
and
x\in(-2\pi,2\pi)^{n}\setminus 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu(x)}=\frac{|x|^{2}}{1-|x|^{-2}\tilde{\nu}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell<N}|x|^{-2\ell-2}\tilde{\nu}(x)^{\ell}+\frac{|x|^{-2N-2}\tilde{\nu}(x)^{N}}{1-|x|^{-2}\tilde{\nu}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell<N}\frac{\tilde{\nu}(x)^{\ell}}{|x|^{2\ell+2}}+\frac{\tilde{\nu}(x)^{N}}{|x|^{2N}\nu(x)}.

Thus, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
, we have

\displaystyle K_{j}(x)=\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1}{\nu(x)}\check{\chi}(x)=\sum_{\ell<N}K_{j,\ell}(x)+r_{j,N}(x),

(9.5)

where we have set

\displaystyle K_{j,\ell}(x):=\frac{\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\tilde{\nu}(x)^{\ell}}{|x|^{2\ell+2}}\check{\chi}(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{j,N}(x):=\frac{\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\tilde{\nu}(x)^{N}}{|x|^{2N}\nu(x)}\check{\chi}(x).

By definition
K_{j,\ell}(x)
and
r_{j,N}(x)
are functions in
C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0\big)
with bounded support, and, near
x=0
we have

\displaystyle K_{j,\ell}(x)=\frac{\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\tilde{\nu}(x)^{\ell}}{|x|^{2\ell+2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{j,N}(x)=\frac{\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\tilde{\nu}(x)^{N}}{|x|^{2N}\nu(x)}.

Recall that
\tilde{\nu}(x)
is analytic and
\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{4}\big)
near
x=0
. Thus,
\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\tilde{\nu}(x)^{\ell}
is smooth (and even analytic), and it is

\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{4\ell+1}\big)
near
x=0
. It then follows from Lemma
9.2
that
\rho_{j,\ell}(\xi):=\widehat{K_{j,\ell}}(\xi)
is a classical symbol on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of order

-n-(4\ell+1)+2(\ell+1)=-n+1-2\ell
.

As mentioned above
\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{j}}-1\big)\tilde{\nu}(x)^{N}
is analytic and
\operatorname{O}\big(|x|^{4N+1}\big)
near
x=0
. In addition,
|x|^{2N}\nu(x)
is analytic and is equivalent to

|x|^{2N+2}
near
x=0
. It then follows from Lemma
9.1
that the Fourier transform
\widehat{r}_{j,N}(\xi)
is a standard symbol on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of order

-n-(4N+1)+(2N+2)+1=-n+2-2N
. Combining this with (
9.5
) we see that, for all
N\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(\xi)-\sum_{\ell<N}\rho_{j,\ell}(\xi)=\bigg(K-\sum_{\ell<N}K_{j,\ell}\bigg)^{\wedge}\!(\xi)=\widehat{r_{j,N}}(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-n+2-2N}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

This shows that
\rho_{j}(\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\rho_{j,\ell}(\xi)
. As
\rho_{j,\ell}S^{-n+1+\ell}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
for
\ell\geq 0
, it then follows that
\rho_{j}(\xi)
is a symbol in
S^{-n+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. This completes the proof of Lemma
6.3
.
∎

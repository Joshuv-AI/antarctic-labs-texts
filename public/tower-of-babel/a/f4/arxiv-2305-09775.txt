Fast-reaction limits for predator–prey reaction–diffusion systems: improved convergence
Cinzia Soresina, Bao Quoc Tang, Bao-Ngoc Tran

Abstract
The fast-reaction limit for reaction–diffusion systems modelling predator–prey interactions is investigated. In the considered model, predators exist in two possible states, namely searching and handling. The switching rate between these two states happens on a much faster time scale than other processes, leading to the consideration of the fast-reaction limit for the corresponding systems. The rigorous convergence of the solution to the fast-reaction system to the ones of the limiting cross-diffusion system has been recently studied in [Conforto, Desvillettes, Soresina, NoDEA, 25(3):24, 2018]. In this paper, we extend these results by proving improved convergence of solutions and slow manifolds. In particular, we prove that the slow manifold converges strongly in all dimensions without additional assumptions, thanks to use of a modified energy function. This consists in a unified approach since it is applicable to both types of fast-reaction systems, namely with the Lotka–Volterra and the Holling-type II terms. Keywords: Fast-reaction limit; Predator–prey models; Reaction–diffusion systems; Energy method; Slow manifold.

Introduction

An interesting modelling issue in several contexts is the derivation of cross-diffusion terms via Quasi-Steady-State Approximation (QSSA) from a fast-reaction model involving different time scales and standard diffusion. In fact, the justification of cross-diffusion terms from semilinear reaction–didffusion systems including fast-reactions and linear diffusion is fundamental to the understanding of the hidden processes that cross-diffusion terms can describe. It is extremely interesting to identify such fast processes at the
microscopic level
(in terms of time scales) and to obtain the
macroscopic
limiting system in which these fast processes are naturally incorporated either in the reaction or in the nonlinear diffusion.
In
[
IMN06
]
, this approach has been exploited to justify the well-known cross-diffusion SKT model for competing species, by considering an instantaneous (fast) conversion between two subpopulations or states. Recently, the fast dichotomy dynamics has been applied to predator–prey interactions
[
CDS18
,
DS19
,
IIK
]
, aggregation phenomena in biology
[
EIMT22
,
FIMU12
]
, hunter–gatherers and farmers in Neolithic transition
[
EHMM21
]
, evolution of dietary diversity and starvation effects
[
BCDK21
]
, autotoxicity effects in plant growth dynamics
[
GIS
]
, and behavioural epidemiology
[
BS
]
.

Althought the QSSA can be performed at a formal level, a proper analysis of the convergence of solutions to the fast-reaction system to the limiting cross-diffusion one is challenging and needs sophisticated techniques
[
CD14
,
DT15
,
CDS18
,
KS23
,
TT23
]

This paper is devoted to study the fast-reaction limit of the following predator–prey model incorporating competition among predators, proposed in
[
CDS18
]
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, e.g.

is of class
C^{2+\kappa}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{clllll}\partial_{t}N^{\varepsilon}-d_{1}\Delta N^{\varepsilon}&=&r_{0}(1-\eta N^{\varepsilon})N^{\varepsilon}-\dfrac{\alpha p_{s}^{\varepsilon}}{\xi p_{s}^{\varepsilon}+1}N^{\varepsilon},&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\
\partial_{t}p_{s}^{\varepsilon}-d_{2}\Delta p_{s}^{\varepsilon}&=&\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\gamma p_{h}^{\varepsilon}-\dfrac{\alpha p_{s}^{\varepsilon}}{\xi p_{s}^{\varepsilon}+1}N^{\varepsilon}\right)-\mu p_{s}^{\varepsilon}+\Gamma p_{h}^{\varepsilon},&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\

p_{s}^{\varepsilon}\geq 0

(4)

\nabla N^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu=\nabla p_{s}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu=\nabla p_{h}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>0,

where

N^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=N_{in}(x),\quad p_{h}^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=p_{h,in}(x),\quad p_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=p_{s,in}(x),\quad x\in\Omega.

Here, parameters

are the diffusion coefficients of prey, searching, and handling predators, respectively. We assume that handling predators move less far than searching predators, yielding the biologically meaningful constraint on the diffusion coefficients
d_{3}<d_{2}
. In the prey equation, the prey-growth is modelled by a logistic term with maximum growth rate
r_{0}

\alpha

\xi

\mu

\Gamma

1/\gamma
. Furthermore, we assume
d_{1},\,d_{2},\,d_{3},\,r_{0},\,\eta,\,\alpha,\,\gamma,\,\Gamma,\,\mu>0
and
\xi\geq 0
. Note that, for
\xi=0
, we have no competition effect for prey among predators and the predation and switching terms reduce to the Lotka–Volterra type.
Under the assumption that the searching–handling switch happens on a much faster time scale than the reproduction and mortality processes, we have that
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.

As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, in some suitable sense, we formally expect that the switching dynamics reaches a
quasi-steady-state
, namely

(6)

\displaystyle\gamma p_{h}^{\varepsilon}-\dfrac{\alpha p_{s}^{\varepsilon}}{\xi p_{s}^{\varepsilon}+1}N^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0,

,

\displaystyle\gamma\xi p_{h}^{2}-(\alpha N+\gamma+\gamma\xi P)p_{h}+\alpha NP=0.

We treat the case

\xi>0

For the case
\xi=0

By adding up the second and third equations of system (

(9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}N-d_{1}\Delta N=r_{0}(1-\eta N)N-\frac{\gamma\alpha NP}{\alpha N+\gamma},&&x\in\Omega,t>0\\
&\partial_{t}P-d_{2}\Delta P=(d_{3}-d_{2})\Delta\left(\frac{\alpha NP}{\alpha N+\gamma}\right)-\mu P+\Gamma\frac{\alpha NP}{\alpha N+\gamma},&&x\in\Omega,t>0,\end{aligned}\right.

subject to homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

(10)

\nabla N\cdot\nu=\nabla P\cdot\nu=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\;t>0,

For the case

subject boundary conditions (

it satisfies the initial condition

and for all

\partial_{n}\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
,

-

if
\xi>0
, it satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{T}}(\partial_{t}N\phi+d_{1}\nabla N\cdot\nabla\phi)=\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{T}}(r_{0}(1-\eta N)N-\gamma\varphi(N,P))\phi,\\
\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{T}}(\partial_{t}P\psi+d_{2}\nabla P\cdot\nabla\psi)=\displaystyle(d_{3}-d_{2})\iint_{Q_{T}}\varphi(N,P)\Delta\psi+\iint_{Q_{T}}(\Gamma\varphi(N,P)\psi-\mu P\psi),\end{array}

Lemma 5

It holds

\displaystyle\|\alpha p_{s}^{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}-\gamma p_{h}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{4/3}_{x,t}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/6}.

The energy estimate in Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\iint_{Q_{T}}

\displaystyle\Big(\alpha p_{s}^{\varepsilon}(N^{\varepsilon}+\beta(\varepsilon))-\gamma p_{h}^{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big((\alpha p_{s}^{\varepsilon}(N^{\varepsilon}+\beta(\varepsilon)))^{p-1}-(\gamma p_{h}^{\varepsilon})^{p-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\iint_{Q_{T}}\Big((\beta(\varepsilon))^{p-2}(p_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{p}|\partial_{t}N^{\varepsilon}|+(p_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{p-1}p_{h}^{\varepsilon}+(\beta(\varepsilon))^{p-3}(p_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{p}|\nabla N^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim H(0)+\frac{\beta(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\iint_{Q_{T}}(p_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+\iint_{Q_{T}}\Big((\beta(\varepsilon))^{p-2}(p_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{p}|\partial_{t}N^{\varepsilon}|+(p_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{p-1}p_{h}^{\varepsilon}+(\beta(\varepsilon))^{p-3}(p_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{p}|\nabla N^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle N^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow N\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(Q_{T}).

A note on extreme points of the unit ball of Hardy-Lorentz spaces
Javier Carrillo-Alanís, Guillermo P. Curbera

Abstract
. We show that inner functions are extreme points of the unit ball of the Hardy-Lorentz space H ⁡ ( Λ ⁡ ( φ ) ) H(\Lambda(\varphi)) , for Λ ⁡ ( φ ) \Lambda(\varphi) a Lorentz space with φ \varphi strictly increasing and strictly concave.



1.
Introduction

In 1958 De Leeuw and Rudin characterized the extreme points of the unit ball
of the Hardy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
,
[
4
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 1

(De Leeuw-Rudin)
.

A function in
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
is an extreme point of the unit ball of
{H^{1}(\mathbb{D})}
if
and only if has norm one and is an outer function.

In 1974 Bryskin and Sedaev proved a partial extension of the result
by De Leeuw and Rudin,
[
2
, Theorem 1]
.

Let
E
be a symmetric (rearrangement invariant) space on
\mathbb{T}
satisfying
the Fatou property.
The space
H(E)
is the set of all analytic functions
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\|f\|_{H(E)}:=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\|f_{r}\|_{E}<\infty,

where
f_{r}(e^{i\theta}):=f(re^{i\theta})
, for
0\leq r<1
and

\theta\in[0,2\pi]
. Since
H(E)\subseteq H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
, functions in
f\in H(E)
have a.e. boundary
values, which we still denote by
f
. Thus, for
f\in H(E)
, the function
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}\mapsto f(e^{i\theta})
is defined a.e. and belongs to
E
.

The space
E
has

strictly monotone norm
if
\|f\|_{E}<\|g\|_{E}
whenever

f,g\in E
satisfy
|f(e^{i\theta})|\leq|g(e^{i\theta})|
a.e. in

\mathbb{T}
and

m(\{e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}:|f(e^{i\theta})|<|g(e^{i\theta})|\})>0.

Theorem 2

(Bryskin-Sedaev)
.

If
E
has strictly monotone norm, then every outer function
in
H(E)
with norm one is an extreme point of the unit ball of
{H(E)}
.

For a function
\varphi\colon[0,2\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
, increasing and concave with

\varphi(0)=0
, the
Lorentz
space
\Lambda(\varphi)
is the set of all measurable functions
f\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}:=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f^{*}(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt<\infty,

where
f^{*}\colon[0,2\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
is the decreasing rearrangement of
f
.
The space
\Lambda(\varphi)
is symmetric. The Hardy-Lorentz space is
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
.
Since
\varphi
being strictly monotone implies that the norm in
\Lambda(\varphi)
is strictly monotone, the following results holds,

[
2
, Corollary]
.

Theorem 3

(Bryskin-Sedaev)
.

Let
\varphi
be a strictly increasing, concave function on
[0,2\pi]

with
\varphi(0)=0
.
Then every outer function
in
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
with norm one is an extreme point of the unit ball of
{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}
.

A further result of Bryskin and Sedaev shows that not all norm one functions in

H(\Lambda(\varphi))
are extreme points of the unit ball of
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
,

[
2
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 4

(Bryskin-Sedaev)
.

Let
f
be a norm
one function in the space
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
with
f(\alpha)=0
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
.
Set
\mu(\theta):=|f(e^{i\theta})|
, for
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
. Suppose
that
\mu
is continuously differentiable on

[0,2\pi]
and for some
\delta>0
the
inequality
\mu^{\prime}(\theta)\leq-\delta
holds for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
.
Then
f
is not an extreme point of the unit ball in
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
.

In 1978, E. M. Semenov stated the problem of describing the set of extreme
points of the unit ball of
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
,
[
10
]
.
The problem was part of a collection of 99 problems in linear and complex analysis
from the Leningrad branch of the Steklov Mathematical Institute,
[
6
, 5.1 pp. 23–24]
,
and appeared in the subsequent list of problems in 1984,

[
7
, 1.6 pp. 22–23]
, and in 1994,

[
8
, 1.5 p. 12]
.

For matters related to symmetric (rearrangement invariant) spaces we refer
the reader to
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
. For matters
related to Hardy spaces we refer the reader to
[
5
]
.

2.
A result

It could be expected that the extreme points of the unit ball of

H(\Lambda(\varphi))
would behave as for
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
, so that,
following Theorem
1
, every extreme point
of the unit ball of
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
is an outer function. We show
that is not necessarily the case.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\varphi
be an increasing, concave function on
[0,2\pi]
with

\varphi(0)=0
. Suppose that
if
f,g\in\Lambda(\varphi)
satisfy

\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}+\|g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}=\|f+g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)},

then

f^{*}+g^{*}=(f+g)^{*}.

Then every inner function is an extreme point of the unit ball of
{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}
.

Proof.

We assume that the norm in
\Lambda(\varphi)

is normalized so that
\|\chi_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}=1
. Let
f
be an inner function.
Since
|f(e^{i\theta})|=1
, a.e. in
\mathbb{T}
, we have
f^{*}=\chi_{[0,2\pi]}
, so

f\in H(\Lambda(\varphi))
and
\|f\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=1
.

In order to check that
f
is an extreme point
of the unit ball of
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
we need to show that if for some
h\in H(\Lambda(\varphi))
we have

(1)

\|f+h\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=\|f-h\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=\|f\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=1,

then necessarily
h=0
.

From (
1
) it follows that

\|f+h\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}+\|f-h\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=2=\|2f\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}.

It follows, from the assumption, that

(f+h)^{*}+(f-h)^{*}=2f^{*}=2\chi_{[0,2\pi]}=2.

Since
(f+g)^{*}
and
(f-g)^{*}
are decreasing functions and their sum is a
constant function, both functions
(f+g)^{*}
and
(f-g)^{*}
must be constant.
Hence,
(f+h)^{*}=c_{1}
and
(f-h)^{*}=c_{2}
. This,
together with
\|f+h\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=\|f-h\|_{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=1
,
implies that
c_{1}=c_{2}=1
.
Thus,

|f(e^{i\theta})+h(e^{i\theta})|=1,\quad|f(e^{i\theta})-h(e^{i\theta})|=1,\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\;e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}.

Thus, we have

|f(e^{i\theta})+h(e^{i\theta})|=|f(e^{i\theta})-h(e^{i\theta})|,\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\;e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}.

This is only possible if
h(e^{i\theta})=0
a.e. Thus,
h=0
on
\mathbb{D}
, and so
f

is an extreme point of the unit ball of
H(\Lambda(\varphi))
.
∎

The following result gives a simple and explicit condition on the function

\varphi
for the space

\Lambda(\varphi)
satisfying the requirement of Theorem
5
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\varphi
be a strictly increasing and strictly concave
function on
[0,2\pi]
with

\varphi(0)=0
. Suppose that
f,g\in\Lambda(\varphi)
satisfy

\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}+\|g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}=\|f+g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)},

then

f^{*}+g^{*}=(f+g)^{*}.

Proof.

We use the following alternative expression for the norm
\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}

of
f
in
\Lambda(\varphi)
:

(2)

\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(t)d(-\varphi^{\prime}(t))+\varphi^{\prime}(2\pi)\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})},

where

F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)ds,\quad t\in[0,2\pi],

which follows, via integration-by-parts, from the definition of the norm in
\Lambda(\varphi)
;
see, for example,
[
3
]
.

From

\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}+\|g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}=\|f+g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}\leq\||f|+|g|\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}\leq\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}+\|g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)},

it follows that

\|f+g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}=\||f|+|g|\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}.

Since
\varphi
is strictly increasing, the norm in
\Lambda(\varphi)
is strictly monotone.
It follows that
|f+g|=|f|+|g|
a.e. in
\mathbb{T}
. This implies (is equivalent to, in fact) that

f\cdot g\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{T}
and
\|f+g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}
.

Consider, for
t\in[0,2\pi]
, the functions

F_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}(f+g)^{*}(s)\,ds,\qquad F_{2}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\left(f^{*}(s)+g^{*}(s)\right)\,ds.

We will prove that
F_{1}=F_{2}
. Since
\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}=\|f+g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}
, we have, via (
2
), that

\begin{split}0&=\|f\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}+\|g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}-\|f+g\|_{\Lambda(\varphi)}\\
&=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(F_{2}(t)-F_{1}(t))\,d(-\varphi^{\prime}(t))\\
&\qquad+\varphi^{\prime}(2\pi)\Big(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}-\|f+g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)\\
&=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(F_{2}(t)-F_{1}(t))\,d(-\varphi^{\prime}(t)).\end{split}

The function
\varphi
being strictly concave implies that

-\varphi^{\prime}
is strictly increasing, so that
d(-\varphi^{\prime}(t))>0
.
Since
F_{2}\geq F_{1}
, it follows that
F_{1}=F_{2}
.
We deduce that
(f+g)^{*}=f^{*}+g^{*}
.
∎

Theorem
5
together with Lemma
6
yield the following result.

Corollary 7
.

Let
\varphi
be a strictly increasing and strictly concave function on
[0,2\pi]
with

\varphi(0)=0
. Then every inner function is an extreme point of the unit ball of
{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}
.

Remark 8
.

(i) For the Lorentz space
L^{p,1}
, with
1<p<\infty
,
Carothers and Turett have shown that if
f,g\in L^{p,1}
satisfy

\|f\|_{p,1}+\|g\|_{p,1}=\|f+g\|_{p,1}
, then
f^{*}+g^{*}=(f+g)^{*}
,

[
3
, Lemma 1]
. Accordingly, from Theorem
5
it follows that
every inner function is an extreme point of the unit ball of
H(L^{p,1})
.

(ii) The requirement of
\varphi(t)
being strictly concave can not be dropped
in Corollary
7
.
To see this, consider
\varphi(t)=t
, in which case
\Lambda(\varphi)=L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
so

{H(\Lambda(\varphi))}=H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
, where inner functions are not extreme points.

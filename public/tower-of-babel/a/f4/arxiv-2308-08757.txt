On the Order of P-Strict Promotion on V × [ ℓ ] V\times[\ell]
Ben Adenbaum

Abstract
Denote by 𝖵 \mathsf{V} the poset consisting of the elements { A , B , C } \{A,B,C\} with cover relations { A ⋖ B , A ⋖ C } \{A\lessdot B,A\lessdot C\} . We show that P P -strict promotion, as defined by Bernstein, Striker, and Vorland, on P P -strict labelings of 𝖵 × [ ℓ ] \mathsf{V}\times[\ell] with labels in the set [ q ] [q] has order 2 ​ q 2q for every ℓ ≥ 1 \ell\geq 1 and q ≥ 3 q\geq 3 . The idea of proof is to suitably generalize the methods of Hopkins and Rubey which they used to prove that Promotion on the linear extensions of 𝖵 × [ n ] \mathsf{V}\times[n] has order 6 ​ n 6n . As a consequence of results of Bernstein, Striker, and Vorland, this result proves that piecewise-linear promotion, and thus picewise-linear rowmotion, on 𝖵 × [ k ] \mathsf{V}\times[k] has order 2 ​ ( k + 2 ) 2(k+2) for all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , as conjectured by Hopkins.

Introduction

Promotion is an action on the linear extensions of a finite poset, see section
2.1
for definitions. Throughout we will be concerned with a generalization of promotion, due to Bernstein, Striker, and Vorland, named
P
-strict promotion
; see Section
2.1
for the definition.

Similarly, rowmotion is an operation defined on the order ideals of a finite poset; see
Section
2.3
for definitions.
Historically, rowmotion was first described by Brouwer and Schrijver
[
BS74
]
, and then again by Cameron and Fon-der-Flaass
[
CFDF95
]
as a composition of certain involutions called toggles. The name of rowmotion comes from the work of Striker and Williams
[
SW12
]
where, for certain posets, rowmotion is described as a composition of toggles along the rows, and it is shown to be related to promotion. In particular, the toggle definition was extended to an action referred to as piecewise-linear rowmotion on the order polytope of
P
in
[
EP21
]
.

The family of posets of the form
\mathsf{V}\times[k]
have been the focus of study, because of their “good” dynamical behavior, first conjectured by Hopkins in
[
Hop20
]
, especially with respect to both Schützenberger promotion of linear extensions and rowmotion. In particular it has been shown that the orders of these actions for a fixed
k
are
6k

[
HR22
]
and
2(k+2)

[
Pla22
]
and
[
PR24
]
respectively. Furthermore it has been conjectured
[
Hop20
]
that the order of piecewise-linear rowmotion on the order polytope of
\mathsf{V}\times[k]
has the same order as rowmotion on the order ideals of
\mathsf{V}\times[k]
. The first goal of this work is to resolve this conjecture by proving an equivalent conjecture of Bernstein, Striker, and Vorland concerning the order of
P
-strict promotion on
P
-strict labelings of
\mathsf{V}\times[\ell]
with entries in
[q]
for all
\ell\geq 1,q\geq 3
. The formal statement is given in Theorem
4
. These two conjectures are equivalent as the action of
P
-strict promotion on
\mathsf{V}\times[\ell]
with entries bounded by
q
for all
\ell\geq 1
for a fixed
q\geq 3
is in equivariant bijection with piecewise-linear rowmotion on
\ell
-bounded
P
-partitions of
\mathsf{V}\times[q-2]
. By rescaling these are the rational points of the order polytope of
\mathsf{V}\times[q-2]
whose denominators are divisible by
\ell
. The method of proof was suggested as a possible attack for this problem by Bernstein, Striker, and Vorland in
[
BSV24
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section

[

Rowmotion

We now review rowmotion on the order polytope of

Definition 5

(

For a poset

\mathcal{O}(P)=\{f:\hat{P}\to[0,1]|\text{ if }p\leq_{\hat{P}}p^{\prime}\text{ then }f(p)\leq f(p^{\prime})\text{ and }f(\hat{0})=0,f(\hat{1})=1\}.

We now define rowmotion on

and on

[

]

[

]

Definition 6

that is matched in the shortest arc of
A_{D}
, in which case no double arc would be formed. Then the
B
and
C
which will form a double arc must be matched to different

which follow

. But this would then force the shortest arc of

to cross one of the arcs connecting to these

and
C
, as it must be connected to a

or
C
which strictly precedes the two. Therefore there can be no new double arcs.
∎

For a double arc

in
\mathcal{D}_{w}
, we say the

of
D
is the set of arcs of
\mathcal{D}_{w}
connected to an

with both arcs nested beneath the double arc. Similarly the

consists of all other arcs. Importantly, no

can have arcs in both the interior and exterior of a double arc, as it would induce a crossing in one of the matchings. We now have everything needed to show that the removal of double arcs does not impact
\Pro
, formalized in the following Proposition.

Proposition 21
.

Suppose that
w
is an
(\ell,q)
-partial multi Kreweras word where the associated arc diagram
\mathcal{D}_{w}
has a double arc
D
of the form
(k,j)
. Let
w_{D}
be the
(\ell-1,q)
-partial multi Kreweras word obtained from
w
by deleting
D
. Then the resulting word obtained by deleting the double arc corresponding to
\Pro(D)
in
\Pro(w)
is
\Pro(w_{D})
.

Proof.

Note that
W(w)
and
W(w_{D})
have the same noncrossing layer decomposition aside from the layer corresponding to
D
. Let
\Int_{D}(w)
and
\Ext_{D}(w)
denote the
P
-strict labelings corresponding to the labels of the interior and exterior of
D
respectively. Since there is no overlap between these two collection of arcs, the noncrossing layer decomposition of
W(w)
is their union together with the layer corresponding to
D
. So by Proposition
17
,
\Pro(w)
is obtained by applying
\Pro
to layers corresponding to
D
,
\Int_{D}(w)
, and
\Ext_{D}(w)
and then combining. Following the same reasoning,
\Pro(w_{D})
is obtained by applying
\Pro
to the layers corresponding to
\Int_{D}(w)
and
\Ext_{D}(w)
and then combining. The only difference in these layer decompositions is the layer corresponding to
\Pro(L^{\prime}_{D})
, where
L^{\prime}_{D}
is the layer corresponding to
D
. By Lemma
18
the layer corresponding to
L^{\prime}_{D}
is just
\Pro(L^{\prime}_{D})
, so deleting this layer before or after applying
\Pro
will make no difference in the corresponding word.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{w}
contains some number of double arcs and consider
\Pro^{q}(w)
. By Lemmas
18
and
20
, it follows that the double arcs of
\mathcal{D}_{\Pro^{q}(w)}
are the same as in
\mathcal{D}_{w}
, since the endpoints of each double arc were just shifted by
q\mod q
. So by Proposition
21
we can reduce to the case where
w
has no double arcs.

If
\mathcal{D}_{w}
has no double arcs, consider
\Pro^{q}(w)
. Note that by Proposition
17
the multiset of values for the labels will be the same. Additionally, one can notice that through the
q

1’s in the multisets of labels, as the number of instances of each label cyclically rotates. By Lemma

\std(\Pro^{q}(w))=\Pro^{3\ell}(\std(w))
, which by

is the reflection of the labels. Then since the standardization is invertible if the multiset of values is known,

is just swapping all instances of

. Thus

.
∎

3.4
Periodicity of Piecewise-Linear Rowmotion

In this section, we examine the impact of Theorem
4
on
Row
on
\mathcal{O}(P)
beyond just implying the finite periodicity. First, we will utilize the equivariance of
\Pro
acting on
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{V}\times[\ell]}(R^{q})
and
Row
acting on
\mathcal{PP}^{\ell}(\mathsf{V}\times[q-2])
to show that
\text{Row}^{q}
is also just the reflection of the labels.
We will utilize the fact that this reflection, denoted Flip, is an automorphism of
\mathsf{V}
.

In a more general setting, if
P
is a graded poset of rank
n
and
\psi
is an automorphism of
P
, we have an action of
\psi
on
P\times[k]
for every
k
where
\psi((p,i))=(\psi(p),i)
for all
(p,i)\in P\times[k]
. This induces an action on
\mathcal{O}(P\times[k])
by
\psi(f((p,i)))=f((\psi(p),i))
.

Before stating the technical lemma that will be key to the proof that
\text{Row}^{q}
acts by reflecting the labels on
\mathcal{O}(P)
, we state the intermediate bijection used in the proof of Proposition
7
, known as
toggle-promotion
, defined more generally in
[
BSV21
]
, between
\Pro
and
Row
. As before we assume
P
is graded.

Definition 23

(
[
BSV24
]
)
.

Toggle-promotion
on
\mathcal{PP}^{\ell}(P\times[q-n-1])
is defined as the toggle composition
\text{TogPro}:=\cdots\circ\tau_{3}\circ\tau_{2}\circ\tau_{1}
, where
\tau_{k}
denotes the composition of all the
\tau_{(p,k)}
over all
p\in P
, such that
(p,i)\in P\times[q-n-1]
and
i=q-n+\text{rk}(p)-k
.

Lemma 24
.

For
P
a graded poset with
\psi
an automorphism of
P
, the action of
\psi
commutes with the equivariant bijection between
TogPro
and
Row
on
\mathcal{O}(P\times[q-(n+1)])
.

Proof.

To begin suppose that
L
is an arbitrary ordering of
P\times[k]
. Then consider the realization of
\mathcal{O}(P\times[k])
where the coordinate for
(p,i)
is
L((p,i))
. Let
L^{\prime}=L((\psi(p),i)).
Note that for any
f\in\mathcal{O}(P\times[k])
,
\psi(f)
is the same as if we instead chose the realization given by
L^{\prime}
. As TogPro,
Row
, and
\varphi
are all defined independently of the choice of coordinates,
\psi
will commute with all of them, as they all commute with a change of coordinates.
∎

Proposition 25
.

The action of
\text{Row}^{q}
on
\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{V}\times[q-2])
is equal to the action of Flip.

Proof.

As a consequence of Theorem
4
we know that
\text{Flip}\circ\text{TogPro}^{q}
is the identity on the rational points of
\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{V}\times[q-2])
and thus on
\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{V}\times[q-2])
. Then consider
\text{Flip}\circ\text{Row}^{q}=\text{Flip}\circ\varphi\circ\text{TogPro}^{q}\circ\varphi^{-1}
. By Lemma
24
,
\text{Flip}\circ\varphi\circ\text{TogPro}^{q}\varphi^{-1}=\varphi\circ\text{Flip}\circ\text{TogPro}^{q}\circ\varphi^{-1}
which is the identity. So
\text{Flip}=\text{Row}^{-q}=\text{Row}^{q}
as
\text{Row}^{q}
is an involution.
∎

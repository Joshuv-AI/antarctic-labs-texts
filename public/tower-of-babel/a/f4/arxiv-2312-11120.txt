Classification of gradient Yamabe soliton hypersurfaces of space forms
Willian Isao Tokura, Marcelo Bezerra Barboza

Abstract
In this paper we investigate gradient Yamabe solitons, either shrinking or steady, that can be isometrically immersed into space forms as hypersurfaces that admit an upper bound on the norm of their second fundamental form. Those solitons satisfying an additional condition, that could be constant mean curvature or the number of critical points of the potential function being at most one, are fully classified. Our argument is based on the weak Omori-Yau principle for the drifted Laplacian on Riemannian manifolds.



1
Introduction

Given an
n
-dimensional differentiable manifold
M
, let’s write

\mathcal{D}
for the group of diffeomorphisms of
M
and

\mathcal{M}
for the space of Riemannian metrics on
M
. Recall the
right action of
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathcal{M}
by pullbacks

\mathcal{M}\times{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{M}},\quad(g,\phi)\mapsto{\phi_{\ast}(g)}.

The Yamabe flow
[
15
]
starting from a given Riemannian
manifold
(M,g)
is a
1
-parameter family

\mathopen{[}0,T\mathclose{]}\ni{t}\mapsto{g^{t}}\in{\mathcal{M}}
of Riemannian
metrics such that

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dfrac{\partial{g^{t}}}{\partial{t}}&=&-\textup{R}^{t}\cdot{g^{t}},\\[8.5359pt]
g^{0}&=&g,\end{array}\right.

where
\textup{R}^{t}
is the scalar curvature of
g^{t}
. Yamabe
solitons are self-similar solutions of (
1
), meaning that

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}g^{t}&=&\alpha^{t}\cdot\psi^{t}_{\ast}{\left(g^{0}\right)},\\
\alpha^{0}&=&1,\\
\psi^{0}&=&\textup{id}_{M},\end{array}\right.

for certain smooth function

\mathopen{[}0,T\mathclose{]}\ni{t}\mapsto{\alpha^{t}}\in{\mathopen{(}0,\infty\mathclose{)}}
and

1
-parameter family

\mathopen{[}0,T\mathclose{]}\ni{t}\mapsto{\psi^{t}}\in{\mathcal{D}}
of
diffeomorphisms of
M
.

By differentiating the first equation of (
2
) with
respect to
t
and evaluating the expression then obtained at
t=0
we find
yet another way of describing a Yamabe soliton.

Definition 1
.

A Yamabe soliton is a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
together with a
vector field
X\in{\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(M\right)}}
and a real number

\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

(3)

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}{g}=(\textup{R}-\lambda)g,

where
\mathcal{L}_{X}{g}
is the Lie derivative of
g
with respect to

X
and
R
is the scalar curvature of
g
. It’s called
shrinking if
\lambda>0
, steady if
\lambda=0
or expanding if

\lambda<0
. If
\mathcal{L}_{X}{g}=0
, it’s called trivial. We write
the Yamabe soliton given by (
3
) like

(M^{n},g,X,\lambda)
in order to emphasize all of its components.

The next definition deals with those Yamabe solitons
(M,g,X,\lambda)
in
which the vector field
X
is the gradient field of some scalar function

f\in{C^{\infty}{\left(M\right)}}
.

Definition 2
.

A gradient Yamabe soliton is a Yamabe soliton
(M^{n},g,X,\lambda)
in
which
X=\textup{grad}_{g}\left(f\right)
is the gradient field of some function

f\in{C^{\infty}{\left(M\right)}}
, called potential function. In this case,
equation (
3
) becomes

(4)

\textup{Hess}_{g}\left(f\right)=(\textup{R}-\lambda)g.

The classification of Yamabe solitons according to the sign of
\lambda

applies also to gradient Yamabe solitons. A gradient Yamabe soliton is
trivial in case
\textup{Hess}_{g}\left(f\right)=0
. Finally, we write a gradient Yamabe
soliton like
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda)
.

Many efforts have been made in order to classify the geometry of Yamabe solitons
that can be isometrically immersed into space forms. For instance, Chen and
Deshmukh
[
7
]
studied Yamabe solitons whose soliton field is
the tangent component of the position vector on Euclidean space, classifying
such objects.

Seko and Maeta
[
25
]
showed that if the soliton vector
field of a Yamabe soliton hypersurface of an Euclidean space is concurrent then
the soliton must be contained in either a hyperplane or a sphere. Fujii and
Maeta
[
13
]
extended Seko and Maeta’s classification
for other gradient Yamabe solitons.

Suh
[
26
]
completely classifies both Yamabe solitons and gradient
Yamabe solitons on real hypersurfaces of complex the hyperbolic space

\mathbb{C}H^{n}
. Tokura et al.
[
27
]
investigates
gradient almost Yamabe solitons immersed into warped products of the kind

\mathbb{R}\times_{h}M^{n}
such that the potential function is the height
function with respect to some given direction. As a result, they were able to
classify rotationally symmetric gradient almost Yamabe soliton hypersurfaces of

\mathbb{R}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

These works tells us just how deeply the geometry of a Yamabe soliton is
governed by its vector field. Nevertheless, it’s desireable to work with
gradient Yamabe soliton hypersurfaces without extra conditions on the soliton
field. This is precisely what we propose with this paper, to study gradient
Yamabe solitons
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda)
isometrically immersed into space forms
with no further assumptions about the gradient field
\textup{grad}\left(f\right)
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents some
examples of gradient Yamabe solitons and establishes the notation to be used
through out the paper. Section
3
provides the weak Omori-Yau maximum
principle, proving it to hold true also for gradient Yamabe soliton
hypersurfaces. We use it to determine the sign of the scalar curvature of
gradient Yamabe solitons, both shrinking and steady. Finally,
section
4
classifies the gradient Yamabe soliton
hypersurfaces of space forms such that its mean curvature is constant or else
the potential function has at most one critical point.

2
Preliminaries and examples

Let
\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
be a simply connected Riemannian

(n+1)
-manifold of constant sectional curvature
c
, where

c\in{\left\{-1,0,1\right\}}
. In order to make it precise, let

\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{\nu}
be the
(n+2)
-dimensional real vector space

\mathbb{R}^{n+2}=\left\{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+2}):u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+2}\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\},

endowed with either the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+2}u_{i}v_{i},

if
\nu=0
, or else with the non-degenerate symmetric
bilinear form

\langle u,v\rangle=-u_{1}v_{1}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n+2}u_{i}v_{i},

if
\nu=1
, where
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+2})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}}
and

v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+2})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}}
. Then, we take

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mathbb{M}_{-1}^{n+1}&=&\mathbb{H}^{n+1}&\equiv&\left\{u\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}}:\langle u,u\rangle=-1,\,\langle u,e_{1}\rangle>0\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{1}}\\
\mathbb{M}_{0}^{n+1}&=&\mathbb{R}^{n+1}&\equiv&\left\{u\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}}:\langle u,e_{n+2}\rangle=0\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{0}}\\
\mathbb{M}_{1}^{n+1}&=&\mathbb{S}^{n+1}&\equiv&\left\{u\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}}:\langle u,u\rangle=1\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{0}}\end{array}

all three of them munished with the induced metric from the surrounding space,
where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+2}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+2}}
stand for the canonical
vectors as usual.

Let
\phi:\textup{M}^{n}\to{\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}}
be an isometric immersion
of a connected and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
\textup{M}^{n}
into

\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
. For all local formulas and computations we may
consider
\phi
as an imbedding and thus identify
x\in{\textup{M}^{n}}
with

\phi(x)\in{\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}}
. The tangent space

\textup{T}_{x}\textup{M}^{n}
is identified with a subspace of the tangent
space
\textup{T}_{x}\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
and the normal space

\textup{T}^{\perp}_{x}\textup{M}^{n}
is the subspace of

\textup{T}_{x}\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
consisting of all

X\in{\textup{T}_{x}\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}}
which are orthogornal to

\textup{T}_{x}\textup{M}^{n}
with respect to the Riemannian metric of

\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
.

The second fundamental form
h
and the shape operator

A
are related to the covariant differentiations

\bar{\nabla}
of
\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
and
\nabla
of

\textup{M}^{n}
by the following formulas:

(5)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}{Y}=\nabla_{X}{Y}+\textup{h}\left(X,Y\right)\quad\left(\forall{X,Y\in{\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\textup{M}^{n}\right)}}}\right),

(6)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}{\textup{N}}=-\textup{A}{X}\quad\left(\forall{X\in{\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\textup{M}^{n}\right)}}}\right),

where
\textup{N}\in{\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}\right)}}
is a
field of unit vectors normal to
\textup{M}^{n}
. The Gauss equation is given
by

(7)

\textup{Rm}\left(X,Y\right)Z=c(X\wedge{Y})Z-(\textup{A}{X}\wedge{\textup{A}{Y}})Z,\quad\left(\forall{X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\textup{M}^{n}\right)}}}\right),

where
X\wedge{Y}
is the skew-symmetric endomorphism of

\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\textup{M}^{n}\right)}
given by

\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\textup{M}^{n}\right)}\to{\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\textup{M}^{n}\right)}},\quad Z\mapsto{(X\wedge{Y})Z=\langle X,Z\rangle Y-\langle Y,Z\rangle X}.

The mean curvature of the immersion is

\textup{H}=\frac{1}{n}\textup{tr}{\left(\textup{A}\right)}
and the Ricci curvature of

\textup{M}^{n}
at a given direction then is

(8)

\textup{Rc}\left(X,X\right)=c(n-1)\lVert{X}\rVert^{2}+n\textup{H}\langle\textup{A}{X},X\rangle-\lVert{\textup{A}{X}}\rVert^{2}\quad\left(\forall{X\in{\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}{\left(\textup{M}^{n}\right)}}}\right),

with what we get that the scalar curvature of
\textup{M}^{n}
must be given by

(9)

\textup{R}=n(n-1)c+n^{2}\textup{H}^{2}-\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}.

As a final note we would to recall an interesting inequality regarding the
quantities
\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert
and
H
. If

\Phi=\textup{A}-\textup{H}\cdot{\textup{id}}

denotes the traceless part of the shape operator, then

\lVert{\Phi}\rVert^{2}=\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}-n\,\textup{H}^{2}\geqslant{0}\implies\sqrt{n}\,\lvert\textup{H}\rvert\leqslant{\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert},

with the equality taking place if, and only if,
\textup{M}^{n}
is a totally
umbilical submanifold of
\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
. We end up this section with
some examples.

Example 1

(Hyperplanes)
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an isometric
immersion. Then, because this is a totally geodesic submanifold with null
scalar curvature, we get that

(\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle,\rangle,f,\alpha)
is a gradient Yamabe
soliton for

f(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert{x}\rVert^{2}+\langle x,\beta\rangle+\gamma,

for any constants
\alpha,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

\beta\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. When
\beta=\gamma=0
this is known as
the Gaussian soliton.

Example 2

(Horospheres)
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be an
isometric immersion of a horosphere into
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then,
because
\textup{A}=\textup{id}
we also get that

(\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle,\rangle,f,\alpha)
is a gradient Yamabe
soliton for

f(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert{x}\rVert^{2}+\langle x,\beta\rangle+\gamma,

for any constants
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and

\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Example 3

(Cylinders)
.

Let

\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}(r)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

be an isometric immerion of a cylinder of radius
r>0
into flat Euclidean

(n+1)
-space. Then, because this cylinder has principal curvatures

\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{k}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa_{k+1}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}=\frac{1}{r},

we get that

\textup{H}=\frac{n-k}{nr}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}=\frac{n-k}{r^{2}}.

Therefore, the tuple

(\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}(r),\langle,\rangle,f,\lambda)

is a trivial gradient Yamabe soliton for

f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}(r)\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad(x,y)\mapsto{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}+\beta,

for whatever constants
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}^{k}}
and

\beta\in\mathbb{R}
that we take, as long as we set

\lambda=\textup{R}=n^{2}\textup{H}^{2}-\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}
.

Example 4

(Products of spheres)
.

If

\mathbb{S}^{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}(r)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

is an isometric immersion of a Riemannian product of spheres into flat
Euclidean
(n+1)
-space, where
r\in\mathopen{(}0,1\mathclose{)}
, we then get that

\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{k}=\frac{r}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa_{k+1}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}=-\frac{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}{r},

are the principal curvatures for this immersion. Therefore, we conclude that

\textup{H}=\frac{nr^{2}-(n-k)}{nr\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}=\frac{kr^{4}+(n-k)(1-r^{2})^{2}}{r^{2}(1-r^{2})}.

Since compact gradient Yamabe solitons of dimension
n\geqslant{3}
must be
trivial (see
[
18
]
), the tuple

(\mathbb{S}^{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(r),\langle,\rangle,f,\lambda)

satisfies equation (
3
) for a constant
f
, with

\lambda=\textup{R}=n^{2}\textup{H}^{2}-\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}
.

Example 5

(Products of hyperbolic spaces and circles)
.

Let
r>0
. If

\mathbb{H}^{k}(-\sqrt{1+r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

is an isometric immersion of the Riemannian product of a hyperbolic

k
-space and an
(n-k)
-sphere into the hyperbolic
(n+1)
-space, then
we get

\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{k}=\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa_{k+1}=\cdots\kappa_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}{r}.

for the principal curvatures of this immersion, and it then follows that

\textup{H}=\frac{nr^{2}+(n-k)}{nr\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}=\frac{kr^{4}+(n-k)(1+r^{2})^{2}}{r^{2}(1+r^{2})}.

Therefore, for a constant
f
, the tuple

(\mathbb{H}^{k}(-\sqrt{1+r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k},\langle,\rangle,f,\lambda)

is a trivial gradient Yamabe soliton with

\lambda=\textup{R}=-n(n-1)+n^{2}\textup{H}^{2}-\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert^{2}
.
This soliton is steady if
(n,k)=(2,1)
and expanding if
(n,k)=(n,n-1)

where
n>2
. At this point we would like to propose the following question:
could we have made a gradient Yamabe soliton out of

\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(-\sqrt{1+r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

in any other way? For example, could we have chosen
f
in some class of
smooth functions other than the constant ones? It follows from Lemma
3.3

that, for
n>2
, the answer is negative for gradient Yamabe solitons of the
steady and shrinking kinds.

3
The weak Omori-Yau maximum principle on gradient Yamabe solitons hypersurfaces

We utilize the Omori-Yau maximum principle to prove our results. Following the
terminology introduced by Pigola, Rigoli and Setti in
[
24
]
,
and also by Alías, Mastrolia and Rigoli in
[
4
]
, the weak
Omori-Yau maximum principle holds for the drifted Laplacian

\Delta_{w}=e^{w}\textup{div}(e^{-w}\nabla)
on a Riemannian manifold

(M^{n},g)
if, for any function
u\in{C^{2}(M)}
with

u^{\ast}=\sup_{M}u<+\infty
, there exists a sequence of points

\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
M
such that

\Delta_{w}{u}(x_{k})<\frac{1}{k}\quad\textup{and}\quad u(x_{k})>u_{\ast}-\frac{1}{k},

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The weak Omori-Yau maximum has been shown to be true
(see Corollary 8.3 of
[
4
]
) on every complete

n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold satisfying

\textup{Rc}\left(\textup{grad}\left(r\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right)\right)+\textup{Hess}\left(w\right)\left(\textup{grad}\left(r\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right)\right)\geqslant-(n-1)G(r),

as well as
\lVert{\textup{grad}\left(w\right)}\rVert^{2}\leqslant{F(r)}
, where

r:M\to\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}
is the Riemannian distance from a given point,
and
F,G
are real
C^{1}
-functions on
\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}
with
F

nondecreasing and

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}}\dfrac{G^{\prime}}{G^{3/2}}>-\infty,\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{F}+\sqrt{G}}\notin{L^{1}(\mathopen{(}0,\infty\mathclose{)})},

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F}+\sqrt{G}\right)^{\prime}(t)\geqslant{-B(\log{t+1})},

for all large enough
t
and some constant
B\in\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}
.

It has been brought to our attention by the work
[
1
]
of
Albanese, Alías and Rigoli that the validity of the weak Omori–Yau maximum
principle on
(M^{n},g)
does not depend on curvature bounds as much as one
would expect. For instance, the weak Omori-Yau maximum principle for

\Delta_{w}
remains true on every complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian
manifold admitting a non-negative
C^{2}
-function
r
on
M
satisfying
the following requirements:

a)

\lim\limits_{x\to{\infty}}r(x)=\infty
;

b)

\Delta_{w}r(x)\leqslant G(r(x))
outside of a compact subset of
M
,

where
G
is a non-negative
C^{1}
-function on
\mathopen{(}0,\infty\mathclose{)}

satisfying

\dfrac{1}{G}\notin L^{1}\quad\textup{and}\quad G^{\prime}(t)\geqslant-B(\log t+1),

for all large enough
t
and some constant
B\in\mathopen{[}0,\infty\mathclose{)}
. The
next result states that the weak Omori-Yau maximum principle remains true for

\Delta_{f/[2(n-1)]}
on
(M^{n},g)
provided that
f
is the potential
function of a gradient Yamabe soliton
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda)
, either shrinking
or steady.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda)
be a complete gradient Yamabe soliton immersed
into a space form
\mathbb{M}_{c}^{n+1}
. Then, the weak Omori-Yau maximum
principle holds true for
\Delta_{\frac{f}{2(n-1)}}
provided that:

a)

n=2
;

b)

i.

n>2

ii.

\sup\limits_{M^{n}}\lVert{\textup{A}}\rVert<\infty
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let
n>2
. Take some
x_{0}\in{M^{n}}
and let
r(x)=d(x,x_{0})
be the
Riemannian distance from
x
to
x_{0}
. Since
M^{n}
is complete,
there exists a minimizing geodesic
\alpha:\mathopen{[}0,r(x)\mathclose{]}\to{M^{n}}

from
x_{0}
to
x
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
r

is smooth at
x
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be an
orthonormal basis at
x_{0}
with
\alpha^{\prime}(0)=e_{1}
and then take

e_{1}(s),\ldots,e_{n}(s)
to be the parallel displacement of the

e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
from
x_{0}
up to
\alpha(s)
. For each

j\in{\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}
, let
X_{j}(s)
be the Jacobian field
along
\alpha(s)
which is given by:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}X_{j}(0)&=&0,\\
X_{j}(r(x))&=&e_{j}(r(x)).\end{array}\right.

Then, we get that

\Delta{r}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{r(x)}\left(\lVert{X_{j}^{\prime}(s)}\rVert^{2}-R(\alpha^{\prime}(s),X_{j}(s),\alpha^{\prime}(s),X_{j}(s))\right)ds.

Let’s take some
r_{0}\in{\mathopen{(}0,r(x)\mathclose{)}}
such that

\lVert{\textup{Rc}}\rVert\leqslant{(n-1)\,r_{0}^{-2}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\lVert{\textup{grad}\left(f\right)}\rVert(x_{0})\leqslant{(n-1)\,r_{0}^{-1}},

on
B(x_{0},r_{0})\subset{M^{n}}
and define vector fields

Y_{1}(s)=X_{1}(s)\quad\textup{and}\quad Y_{j}(s)=c(s)e_{j}(s)\quad(j\geqslant{2}),

along
\alpha:\mathopen{[}0,r(x)\mathclose{]}\to{M}
, where

c:\mathopen{[}0,r(x)\mathclose{]}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

c(s)=\begin{cases}r_{0}^{-1}s,&\text{if }s\in\mathopen{[}0,r_{0}\mathclose{]},\\
1,&\text{if }s\in\mathopen{[}r_{0},r(x)\mathclose{]}.\end{cases}

It follows from the standard index comparison theorem that

\begin{split}\Delta{r}(x)&\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{r(x)}\left(\lVert{Y_{j}^{\prime}(s)}\rVert^{2}-R(\alpha^{\prime}(s),Y_{j}(s),\alpha^{\prime}(s),Y_{j}(s))\right)ds\\
&\leqslant\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\left[\frac{n-1}{r_{0}^{2}}+\left(1-\frac{s^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}\right)\textup{Rc}\left(\alpha^{\prime}(s),\alpha^{\prime}(s)\right)\right]ds\\
&\hskip 73.97733pt-\int_{0}^{r(x)}\textup{Rc}\left(\alpha^{\prime}(s),\alpha^{\prime}(s)\right)ds\\
&\leqslant\frac{n-1}{r_{0}}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{r_{0}}-\int_{0}^{r(x)}\textup{Rc}\left(\alpha^{\prime}(s),\alpha^{\prime}(s)\right)ds.\end{split}

Also, we get from the Yamabe soliton equation (
2
) that

\begin{split}g(\textup{grad}\left(f\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right))(x)&=\int_{0}^{r(x)}\frac{d}{ds}g(\textup{grad}\left(f\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right))ds\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+g(\textup{grad}\left(f\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right))(x_{0})\\
&=\int_{0}^{r(x)}\textup{Hess}\left(f\right)\left(\alpha^{\prime}(s),\alpha^{\prime}(s)\right)ds\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+g(\textup{grad}\left(f\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right))(x_{0})\\
&\leqslant\int_{0}^{r(x)}\textup{R}ds+\lVert{\textup{grad}\left(f\right)}\rVert(x_{0})-\lambda{r(x)}.\end{split}

Then, we get that

(10)

\begin{split}&2(n-1)\Delta{r}(x)+g(\textup{grad}\left(f\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right))(x)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\leqslant\frac{C_{n}}{r_{0}}-2(n-1)\int_{0}^{r(x)}\textup{Rc}\left(\alpha^{\prime}(s),\alpha^{\prime}(s)\right)ds+\int_{0}^{r(x)}\textup{R}ds-\lambda{r(x)},\end{split}

for every
x\in{M^{n}\setminus{B(x_{0},r_{0})}}
, where
C_{n}
is a
constant that depends on
n
.

On the other hand, it follows from (
8
)
and (
9
) that

\begin{split}&2(n-1)\Delta{r}(x)+g(\textup{grad}\left(f\right),\textup{grad}\left(r\right))(x)\\

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a complete, shrinking or steady, gradient

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Take both
-u=\textup{R}
and

. Then, we get from

By applying Theorem 3.2 of
[
1
]
with

we get that

. Also, by

, there exists a sequence

Thus, we get that

\frac{\lambda{u}^{\ast}}{n-1}+\frac{(u^{\ast})^{2}}{n-1}\leqslant{0},

and because

we have that

with

. Therefore,

\textup{R}\geqslant{0}
on
M^{n}
. Now, suppose that we have

\textup{R}(x_{0})=-u(x_{0})=0
for some

.

4
Classification of gradient Yamabe solitons hypersurfaces

The study of constant mean curvature hypersurfaces in space forms is one of the
oldest subjects in differential geometry. A well known result of Klotz and
Osserman
[
19
]
states that the standard spheres and the
circular cylinders are the only complete surfaces in the Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{3}
with nonzero constant mean curvature whose Gaussian
curvature does not change sign. Hoffman
[
17
]
and
Tribuzy
[
28
]
gave an extension of that result to the case of
surfaces with constant mean curvature in the Euclidean 3-sphere

\mathbb{S}^{3}
and in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
,
respectively. Summarizing the results of those authors in a single statement,
we obtain the following result.

Proposition 1

(
[
2
,
3
]
)
.

Let
M^{2}
be a complete surface isometrically immersed into a

3
-dimensional space form
\mathbb{M}_{c}^{3}
with constant mean
curvature
H
. If its Gaussian curvature
K
does not change
sign, then
M^{2}
is either a totally umbilical surface or else
K=0
and
also:

a)

M^{2}
is the cylinder

\mathbb{R}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

with
r>0
if
c=0
.

b)

M^{2}
is the flat torus

\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(r)\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}

with
0<r<1
if
c=1
.

c)

M^{2}
is the hyperbolic cylinder

\mathbb{H}^{1}(-\sqrt{1+r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(r)\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}

with
r>0
if
c=-1
.

As a consequence of Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
1
, we deduce the
following classification of gradient Yamabe soliton surfaces, either shrinking
or steady, isometrically immersed into
3
-dimensional space forms.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M^{2},g,f,\lambda)
be a complete
2
-dimensional gradient Yamabe
soliton, shrinking or steady, isometrically immersed with constant mean
curvature into a
3
-dimensional space form
\mathbb{M}_{c}^{3}
. Then

M^{2}
is either totally umbilical, or

a)

M^{2}
is the cylinder

\mathbb{R}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

with
r>0
.

b)

M^{2}
is the flat torus

\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(r)\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}

where
0<r<1
. In this case, a trivial soliton since
M^{2}
is
compact.

c)

M^{2}
is the hyperbolic cylinder

\mathbb{H}^{1}(-\sqrt{1+r^{2}})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(r)\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}

with
r>0
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

It follows from the Gauss equation in conjunction with Lemma
3.3
that

0\leqslant\inf_{M^{2}}K\leqslant K=c+k_{1}k_{2}\leqslant{c+\textup{H}^{2}},

with the equality

\inf\left\{K(x):x\in{M^{2}}\right\}=c+\textup{H}^{2}
taking place if
and only if
M^{2}
is totally umbilical. If
c+\textup{H}^{2}=0
,
then
c+\textup{H}^{2}=\inf\left\{K(x):x\in{M^{2}}\right\}=0
and

M^{2}
is totally umbilical. If
c+\textup{H}^{2}>0
, then

\inf\left\{K(x):x\in{M^{2}}\right\}\leqslant{0}
because otherwise we
would get
K\geqslant{\inf\left\{K(x):x\in{M^{2}}\right\}}>0
, thus
contradicting Proposition
1
. Now,

\inf\left\{K(x):x\in{M^{2}}\right\}=0
and so
K
does not change its
sign on
M^{2}
and the proof follows once again from
Proposition
1
.
∎

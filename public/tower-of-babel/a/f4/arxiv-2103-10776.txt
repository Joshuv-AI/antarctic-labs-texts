The square lattice Ising model on the rectangle III: Hankel and Toeplitz determinants
Alfred Hucht

Abstract
Based on the results obtained in [ Hucht, J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 50, 065201 (2017) ], we show that the partition function of the anisotropic square lattice Ising model on the L × M L\times M rectangle, with open boundary conditions in both directions, is given by the determinant of a M / 2 × M / 2 \nicefrac{{M}}{{2}}\times\nicefrac{{M}}{{2}} Hankel matrix, that equivalently can be written as the Pfaffian of a skew-symmetric M × M M\times M Toeplitz matrix. The M − 1 M-1 independent matrix elements of both matrices are Fourier coefficients of a certain symbol function, which is given by the ratio of two characteristic polynomials. These polynomials are associated to the different directions of the system, encode the respective boundary conditions, and are directly related through the symmetry of the considered Ising model under exchange of the two directions. The results can be generalized to other boundary conditions and are well suited for the analysis of finite-size scaling functions in the critical scaling limit using Szegő’s theorem.

\hat{\bm{\mathrm{H}}}_{\Delta}\equiv\left[\sum_{\mu=1}^{M}\frac{t^{*}}{z_{-}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\Delta(L\gamma_{\mu}-\theta_{\mu}+\psi_{\mu})}}{P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{\prime}(\chi_{\mu})}\,\chi_{\mu}^{i+j}\right]_{i,j=0}^{\frac{M}{2}-1}.

(3.21)

Note that (
3.20
) is block lower anti-triangular
similar to
\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{x}}
in (
3.19
),
as
\hat{\bm{\mathrm{H}}}_{0}=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
. The important consequence of this result
is the generalized determinant identity

\det(\bm{\mathrm{W}}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{D}}\bm{\mathrm{W}})={\det}^{2}\,\bm{\mathrm{W}}\det\bm{\mathrm{D}}={\det}^{2}\,\hat{\bm{\mathrm{H}}}_{1},

(3.22)

which leads to the surprising result that the Ising partition function
can be mapped to a Hankel determinant. Inserting the simplifications
from above, and defining
\bm{\mathrm{H}}\equiv 2\mathrm{i}z_{-}\hat{\bm{\mathrm{H}}}_{1},

we can draw the square root in (
3.14
) and get the compact
exact expression for the partition function of the square lattice
Ising model on the rectangle,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=Z_{1}\det\bm{\mathrm{H}},

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv t^{-\frac{L}{2}}z^{\frac{M}{2}}\left(-\frac{2}{z_{-}}\right)^{\negmedspace\frac{LM}{2}},

(3.23a)

where the
\nicefrac{{M}}{{2}}\times\nicefrac{{M}}{{2}}
Hankel matrix
\bm{\mathrm{H}}=\left[\vphantom{A_{2}^{2}}h_{i+j+1}\right]_{i,j=0}^{M/2-1}

has the matrix elements

h_{n}\equiv\sum_{\mu=1}^{M}\frac{2\mathrm{i}t^{*}\,\mathrm{e}^{L\gamma_{\mu}-\theta_{\mu}+\psi_{\mu}}}{P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{\prime}(\chi_{\mu})}\,\chi_{\mu}^{n-1},

(3.23b)

with
n=1,\ldots,M-1
. This expression represents a significant
simplification with respect to the result from
(
Hucht16a
)
.
However, in the next section we will proceed further by rewriting
the sum over
\mu
as a complex contour integral, inserting the known
formula for
P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\chi)
from (
3.5
).

3.4
Contour integral representation

The matrix elements
h_{n}
of the Hankel matrix
\bm{\mathrm{H}}
(
3.23b
)
can be evaluated using complex contour integration, and the characteristic
polynomial
P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\chi)
plays a crucial role in this calculation.
In principle we use Cauchy’s residual theorem and calculate the sum
over
\mu
in
h_{n}
as a contour integral over a suitable contour

C
around the points
u_{\mu}
in the complex

-plane,

\displaystyle h_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{C}\frac{2\mathrm{i}t^{*}\,\mathrm{e}^{L\gamma-\theta+\psi}}{P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{\prime}(\chi)}\,\chi^{n-1}\,\frac{\partial\log P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\chi)}{\partial\chi}\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial u}\,\mathrm{d}u

(3.24a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{C}\frac{2\mathrm{i}t^{*}\,\mathrm{e}^{L\gamma-\theta+\psi}}{P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(\chi)}\,\chi^{n-1}\,\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial u}\,\mathrm{d}u,

where, most importantly, the derivative
P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{\prime}(\chi)
cancels

While the CP

has two sets of zeroes

and

\lambda_{\mu}
and

\lambda_{\mu}^{-1}

We can easily remove the additional zeroes

As the first (second) bracket vanishes at

(

In the next step we use (

model on the rectangle as the determinant of a Hankel matrix

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=Z_{1}\det\bm{\mathrm{H}},

\displaystyle h_{n}

3.5
From Hankel to Toeplitz

The Hankel matrix (
3.27
) can be further transformed using
the identities derived by Basor & Ehrhardt
(
BasorEhrhardt02
)
.
Using theorem 2.3, the symbols
a
and
b
, as well as the notation

T_{M}(a)
,
H_{M}(a)
and
H_{M}[b]
from
(
BasorEhrhardt02
)
,
the determinant of
\bm{\mathrm{H}}
, with elements (
3.27
), corresponds
to the Hankel moment determinant
\det H_{M}[b]
and can be transformed
to the symmetric Toeplitz plus Hankel determinant
\det(T_{M}(a)+H_{M}(a))
,
with the symbol

a(\zeta)=\mathrm{e}^{-\psi}b(\zeta_{+})=\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{L\gamma-\theta}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(M\varphi-\omega)}}\,\frac{\partial\gamma}{\mathrm{i}\partial\varphi},

(3.29)

and with the Fourier components
(
BasorEhrhardt02
, eq. (3))

\displaystyle a_{n}=a_{-n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{C}a(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi})\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\varphi}\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial u}\,\mathrm{d}u

(3.30a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{C}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{L\gamma-\theta}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(M\varphi-\omega)}}\zeta^{-n}\,\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial u}\,\mathrm{d}u.

(3.30b)

Note that (
3.29
) transforms to its reciprocal,

\mathcal{S}[a(\zeta)]=1/a(\zeta)
, under exchange of the two directions.
The advantage of this representation is the occurrence of the simpler
Fourier base
\zeta^{-n}
, which eliminates the poles in the integrand
stemming from
\zeta\to\infty
at the points
u_{\infty,\infty}=\eta

and
u_{0,\infty}=\mathrm{i}K^{\prime}-\eta
, provided
L<M
(as
n\geq 0
). We
can therefore simplify the integration contour to a curve around the
two remaining poles at
u_{\infty,0}=-\eta
and
u_{0,0}=-\mathrm{i}K^{\prime}+\eta
.
However, for arbitrary
L
and
M
the pole order of the integrand
(
3.30b
) at the four points (
2.18
)
is
\{-n+2-M,-n+L-M,n+L,n\}
and can be positive in all four cases,
such that all four points must be enclosed by the contour.

Finally, from chapter 3 of
(
BasorEhrhardt02
)
we borrow a clever
transformation from the symmetric Toeplitz plus Hankel representation
to a skew-symmetric
M\times M
Toeplitz matrix
\hat{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}
,
with the result

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{\mu=1}^{M}\frac{2\mathrm{i}t^{*}\,\mathrm{e}^{L\gamma_{\mu}-\theta_{\mu}}}{P_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{\prime}(\chi_{\mu})\sin\varphi_{\mu}}\cot(\tfrac{1}{2}\varphi_{\mu})\sin\!\big[(i-j)\varphi_{\mu}\big]\right]_{i,j=0}^{M-1}

(3.31a)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{C}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{L\gamma-\theta}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(M\varphi-\omega)}}\cot(\tfrac{1}{2}\varphi)\sin\!\big[(i-j)\varphi\big]\,\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial u}\,\mathrm{d}u\right]_{i,j=0}^{M-1},

(3.31b)

such that the Pfaffian of

fulfills
\Pf\hat{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}=\det\bm{\mathrm{H}}
,
and therefore

(3.31c)

As before, the integration contour
C
can be freely deformed
as long as it enclosed all zeroes
u_{\mu}
or, alternatively, the
four points (
2.18
).

4
Discussion

In this work, we showed that the partition function of the anisotropic
square lattice Ising model on the
L\times M
rectangle with open
boundary conditions in both directions is given by the determinant
of a
\nicefrac{{M}}{{2}}\times\nicefrac{{M}}{{2}}
Hankel matrix
\bm{\mathrm{H}}
,
which equivalently can be written as the Pfaffian of a skew-symmetric

M\times M
Toeplitz matrix
\hat{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}
. The
M-1
independent
matrix elements of
\bm{\mathrm{H}}
or
\hat{\bm{\mathrm{T}}}
are Fourier coefficients
of a symbol function (
3.29
), which is given by the
ratio of two characteristic polynomials. These polynomials are associated
to the two directions
(\leftrightarrow,\updownarrow)
of the system, encode the respective
boundary conditions, and are directly related through the symmetry
of the square lattice Ising model under exchange of the two directions.

In the framework of the square lattice Ising model, Toeplitz matrices
and determinants are well known in the context of bulk spatial correlation
functions
\langle\sigma_{0,0}\sigma_{\ell,m}\rangle

(
FisherHartwig68
;
McCoyWu73
;
DeiftItsKrasovsky13
)
,
eventually leading to the spontaneous magnetization for
\ell^{2}+m^{2}\text{$\to\infty$}
.
Surprisingly, they now also appear in the exact expressions for the
partition function
Z
of finite systems.

The considered anisotropic Ising model with open BCs in both directions
is invariant under exchange of the two directions
\leftrightarrow
and
\updownarrow
,
such that both the system dimensions and the coupling constants are
exchanged according to
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\leftrightarrow}}M

and
\mathcal{K}^{\leftrightarrow}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\leftrightarrow}}\mathcal{K}^{\updownarrow}
, see table
1
.
This
swap transformation

\mathcal{S}
(
1.5
)
leads to the mapping of
z
and
t
to their respective duals,
(z,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\mapsto}}(t^{*},z^{*})
,
as well as to the exchange of the eigenvalues and angles according
to
\lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\leftrightarrow}}\zeta
,
\gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\leftrightarrow}}\mathrm{i}\varphi

and
\theta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\leftrightarrow}}\mathrm{i}\omega
. In the
complex
u
-plane, it corresponds to a point reflection
(u,\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\mapsto}}(\tilde{u},\tilde{\eta})

of the complex torus at the point
\eta_{\mathrm{iso}}={\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}\mathrm{i}K^{\prime}

from (
2.15
). The elliptic modulus
k
, however, is invariant
under this transformation. This symmetry must not be confused with
the related duality transformation
\mathcal{D}:(z,t^{*})\mapsto\mathcal{D}[(z,t^{*})]=(t,z^{*})

of the bulk system, which maps a low-temperature system with spins

\sigma_{\ell,m}
at
k>1
to a high-temperature system with the
plaquette spins
\tilde{\sigma}_{\ell,m}
at
k\mapsto\tilde{k}=k^{-1}<1
,
see figure
1
. Note that in systems with boundaries,
this transform is known to change the boundary conditions, e. g.,
from open to fixed.

During the present calculation, it was tried to find a representation
of the partition function
Z
that is formally symmetric under the
exchange of the two directions, mediated by the transformation
\mathcal{S}
.
While the symbol function
a(\zeta)
from (
3.29
)
already fulfills
\mathcal{S}[a(\zeta)]=1/a(\zeta)
, the transformation

\mathcal{S}
exchanges the system dimensions
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\leftrightarrow}}M

and therefore changes the dimensions of the involved matrices, such
that, e. g., a representation using
(M{+}L)\times(M{+}L)
dimensional
matrices might be necessary for a unified description.

The obtained results might possibly be rewritten using elliptic product
identities, as done by Baxter
(
Baxter16
)
in the thermodynamic
limit. The ultimate goal would be to use elliptic determinant evaluations
as done successfully by Iorgov & Lisovyy
(
IorgovLisovyy11
)
,
which might lead to a closed product representation of the partition
function.

We expect that our results can be extended to other boundary conditions
by using the corresponding characteristic polynomials, these generalizations
are left for future work.

From the Toeplitz determinant representation of the partition function,
it is rather straightforward to derive the (anisotropic) scaling limit

L,M\to\infty
,
T\to T_{\mathrm{c}}
at fixed scaling variables
x_{\updownarrow}\equiv(T/T_{\mathrm{c}}-1)(M/\xi_{0}^{\updownarrow})^{1/\nu}

and
\rho\equiv(L/\xi_{0}^{\leftrightarrow})/(M/\xi_{0}^{\updownarrow})
using Szegő’s
theorem
(
Szego15
;
Szego20
;
DeiftItsKrasovsky13
)
. This task will
be addressed in a forthcoming work.

Appendix B
A block Hankel matrix identity

Let
\bm{\mathrm{\mathcal{V}}}_{\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{x}}
be the generalized

1\times B
block Vandermonde matrix with
M\times N
blocks

\bm{\mathrm{\mathcal{V}}}_{\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{x}}\equiv\left[\vphantom{A_{2}^{2}}g_{\mu}^{b}\,x_{\mu}^{n}\right]_{b=0;\,\,\mu=1,\,n=0}^{B-1\,M\hskip 6.02777ptN-1}=\left[\vphantom{A_{2}^{2}}\bm{\mathrm{G}}^{b}\,\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\!\boldsymbol{x}}\right]_{b=0}^{B-1},

(B.1)

with

\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\!\boldsymbol{x}}\equiv\left[\vphantom{A_{2}^{2}}x_{\mu}^{n}\right]_{\mu=1,\,n=0}^{M\hskip 6.02777ptN-1},\qquad\bm{\mathrm{G}}\equiv\left[\vphantom{A_{2}^{2}}\delta_{\mu\nu}\,g_{\mu}\right]_{\mu,\nu=1}^{M}.

(B.2)

As an example, for
M=4
,
B=3
and
N=3
we have

\bm{\mathrm{\mathcal{V}}}_{\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{x}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc|ccc}1&x_{1}&x_{1}^{2}&g_{1}&g_{1}x_{1}&g_{1}x_{1}^{2}&g_{1}^{2}&g_{1}^{2}x_{1}&g_{1}^{2}x_{1}^{2}\\
1&x_{2}&x_{2}^{2}&g_{2}&g_{2}x_{2}&g_{2}x_{2}^{2}&g_{2}^{2}&g_{2}^{2}x_{2}&g_{2}^{2}x_{2}^{2}\\
1&x_{3}&x_{3}^{2}&g_{3}&g_{3}x_{3}&g_{3}x_{3}^{2}&g_{3}^{2}&g_{3}^{2}x_{3}&g_{3}^{2}x_{3}^{2}\\
1&x_{4}&x_{4}^{2}&g_{4}&g_{4}x_{4}&g_{4}x_{4}^{2}&g_{4}^{2}&g_{4}^{2}x_{4}&g_{4}^{2}x_{4}^{2}\end{array}\right].

(B.3)

Furthermore, let
\bm{\mathrm{D}}
be an arbitrary
M\times M

diagonal matrix. Then, the
B\times B
block Hankel matrix with
N\times N

blocks

\bm{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}_{\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{x}}\equiv\left[\sum_{\mu=1}^{M}d_{\mu}g_{\mu}^{a+b}\,x_{\mu}^{m+n}\right]_{a,b=0;\,m,n=0}^{B-1\;\;\;N-1}=\left[\bm{\mathrm{V}}^{\top}_{\!\boldsymbol{x}}\,\bm{\mathrm{D}}\,\bm{\mathrm{G}}^{a+b}\,\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\!\boldsymbol{x}}\right]_{a,b=0}^{B-1}

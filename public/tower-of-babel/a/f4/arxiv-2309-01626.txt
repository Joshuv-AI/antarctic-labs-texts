Order and chain polytopes of maximal ranked posets
Ibrahim Ahmad and Ghislain Fourier and Michael Joswig

Abstract
. The order and chain polytopes, introduced by Richard P. Stanley (1986), form a pair of Ehrhart equivalent polytopes associated to a given finite poset. A conjecture by Takayuki Hibi and Nan Li from 2016 states that the f f -vector of the chain polytope dominates the f f -vector of the order polytope. In this paper we prove a stronger form of that conjecture for a special class of posets. More precisely, we show that the f f -vectors increase monotonically over an admissible family of chain-order polytopes for such posets.



1.
Introduction

For a given finite poset
P
with
n
elements, Stanley
[
Sta86
]
introduced the
order polytope

(1)

\mathcal{O}(P)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\mid x_{p}\leq 1\text{ and }x_{p}\leq x_{q}\text{ if }p\lessdot q\}

and the
chain polytope

(2)

\mathcal{C}(P)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\mid x_{p_{1}}+\dots+x_{p_{s}}\leq 1\text{ for all }p_{1}\lessdot p_{2}\lessdot\dots\lessdot p_{s}\}\kern 5.0pt,

where
\lessdot
denotes the covering relation in
P
.
Both polytopes have full dimension
n
.
The primary result of
[
Sta86
]
establishes that these two polytopes are Ehrhart-equivalent, meaning they share identical Ehrhart polynomials.
Specifically, the lattice point counts of
\mathcal{O}(P)
and
\mathcal{C}(P)
are equal.
In this work, we focus on exploring the combinatorial properties of order and chain polytopes.

For an
n
-dimensional polytope, the
f
-vector

(f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n-1})
counts the faces per dimension.
Stanley
[
Sta86
, Theorem 3.2]
showed that
f_{0}(\mathcal{O}(P))=f_{0}(\mathcal{C}(P))
.
Hibi and Li
[
HL16
]
proved
f_{n-1}(\mathcal{O}(P))\leq f_{n-1}(\mathcal{C}(P))
.
Moreover, they proved that
\mathcal{O}(P)
and
\mathcal{C}(P)
are affinely isomorphic if and only if the poset
P
does not contain five pairwise distinct elements
a,b,c,d,e
such that
d,e\lessdot c\lessdot a,b
.
Subsequently, Hibi et al.
[
Hib+17
]
established
f_{1}(\mathcal{O}(P))\leq f_{1}(\mathcal{C}(P))
.
These results lead to the natural conjecture (the
Hibi–Li Conjecture
) that the
f
-vector of the chain polytopes always dominates the
f
-vector of the order polytope
[
HL16
, Conjecture 2.4]
.
Recently, this conjecture was proved by Freij-Hollanti and Lundström in a special case
[
FHL24
]
.

A particularly promising approach to addressing the Hibi–Li Conjecture arises from the following construction.
Given a poset
P
, we consider a partition
P=C\sqcup O
with
\hat{0}\in C
and
\hat{1}\in O
.
This partition defines the
chain-order polytope

\mathcal{O}_{C,O}
, introduced in
[
FF16
]
and later generalized in
[
Fan+20
,
FFP20
]
.
When the partition of
P
into
C
and
O
is sufficiently well-structured, the chain-order polytopes
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(P)
remain Ehrhart-equivalent to
\mathcal{O}(P)
and
\mathcal{C}(P)

[
FF16
, Theorem 3.1]
.
By appropriately varying the partition, one obtains an entire family of polytopes that
“interpolate”
between the order and chain polytopes of the poset.
A stronger version of the Hibi–Li Conjecture posits that the
f
-vectors of these polytopes increase monotonically throughout this family
[
FF16
]
.

Our main result (Theorem
4.1
) confirms the strong Hibi–Li Conjecture for a certain class of posets.
A poset is
ranked
if each maximal chain has the same length.
For a vector
\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{\ell})
of positive integers, we define the ranked poset
P_{\tau}
which has
\tau_{i}
elements in rank
i
, and any two elements in distinct ranks are comparable.
All ranked posets with
\tau_{i}
elements in rank
i
are subposets of
P_{\tau}
.
Therefore, we call
P_{\tau}
a
maximal ranked poset
.
Fixing a parameter
k
with
0\leq k\leq\ell
, we define
C
as the set of elements of rank at most
k
and
O
as the elements of larger rank.
Then
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(P_{\tau})=\mathcal{O}(P_{\tau})
for
k=0
, and
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(P_{\tau})=\mathcal{C}(P_{\tau})
for
k=\ell
.
We show that, for any maximal ranked poset
P_{\tau}
, the
f
-vectors of the chain-order polytopes of
P_{\tau}
monotonically increase with
k
.
In particular, we obtain an independent new proof of the Hibi–Li conjecture for maximal ranked posets.
The latter form a subclass of the posets studied by Freij-Hollanti and Lundström
[
FHL24
]
.
Our proof exploits combinatorial properties of posets to arrive at normal forms for faces of the chain-order polytopes.
In
[
Sta86
, p. 12]
, Stanley pointed out that the faces of the chain polytopes are difficult to describe, whereas face structure of the order polytopes is known.
We hope that our proof serves as an inspiration for understanding the face structure of more general classes of chain polytopes.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce the objects of interest.
Section
3
is devoted to a normal form for the faces of chain-order polytopes.
The proof of the main result is contained in Section
4
.
Algorithmic aspects and computational experiments are the focus of Section
5
.
Here combinatorial optimization enters the picture.
In fact, computing maximal cliques in the comparability graph of a poset yields double descriptions of order and chain polytopes.

2.
Preliminaries

We briefly recall known facts on the order and chain polytopes.
Throughout the entire article, we solely consider finite posets.
Let
P
be such a poset (with
n
elements).
The face lattice of
\mathcal{O}(P)
can be described in terms of partitions.
So consider a partition

\pi=\{T_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq s\}

of
P
.
We say that
\pi
is
P
-compatible
if the relation
\leq
on
\pi
defined as the transitive closure of

B\leq C\,\text{ if and only if }p\leq q\,\text{ for some $p\in B$ and $q\in C$}

is antisymmetric.
In this case,
\leq
is a partial order on
\pi
.
Further,
\pi
is
connected
if its blocks are connected as induced subposets.
A
P
-compatible partition
\pi
of
P
gives rise to a
quotient poset

(P/\pi,\leq)
where
(P/\pi,\leq)
is the poset of blocks in
\pi
.
Furthermore, the quotient map

q:(P,\leq)\rightarrow(P/\pi,\leq)\,,\ x\mapsto B\text{, for $x\in B$}

is order-preserving; i.e., whenever
x\leq y
in
P
, we have
q(x)\leq q(y)
in
P/\pi
.

Equipped with these definitions, we can now state the combinatorial description of the order polytope.
To keep the notation terse, we extend
P
by a minimal and a maximal element
\hat{P}=P\cup\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
.
The first result in this direction goes back to Geissinger
[
Gei81
]
and Stanley
[
Sta86
]
; see also
[
Peg18
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
P
be a poset.
Then every non-empty face
F\subseteq\mathcal{O}(P)
can be identified with a partition
\pi_{F}
of
\hat{P}
that is connected,
\hat{P}
-compatible such that
\hat{0}
and
\hat{1}
are in different blocks.
Further, the map

\mathcal{O}(\hat{P}/\pi_{F})\rightarrow F\,,\ (x_{B})_{B\in(\hat{P}/\pi_{F})}\mapsto(x_{q(p)})_{p\in\hat{P}}

is an affine isomorphism.
In particular, we have
\dim(F)=|\pi_{F}|-2
.

Next we recall the chain-order polytopes, first introduced in
[
FF16
]
and then generalized in
[
Fan+20
,
FFP20
]
.
They provide an Ehrhart-equivalent family of polytopes that interpolate between the order and chain polytope of a given poset.
This family will be crucial for our main result.
For a poset
P
consider a partition
P=C\sqcup O
such that
\hat{0}\in C
and
\hat{1}\in O
.
We are going to refer to the elements of
C
as the
chain elements
and and the elements of
O
as
order elements
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
chain-order polytope

\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(P)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

a.

x_{\hat{0}}=0
and
x_{\hat{1}}=1
,

b.

for each covering relation
p\lessdot q\in O
an inequality
x_{p}\leq x_{q}
,

c.

for each
p\in C
an inequality
0\leq x_{p}
,

d.

for each maximal chain
p_{1}\lessdot p_{2}\lessdot\dots\lessdot p_{r}\lessdot a
with
p_{i}\in C
,
a\in O
and
r\geq 0
an inequality

x_{p_{1}}+\dots+x_{p_{r}}\leq x_{a}\kern 5.0pt.

It is known that the inequalities (
1
) and (
2
) of the order and chain polytopes, respectively, are facet defining.
In contrast the facet description of the chain-order polytopes are unknown in general; see
[
Fan+20
, Conjecture 8.2]
.
However, in our scenario, where
P
is ranked, the facets are known.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Fan+20
, Proposition 8.6]
)
.

Let
P
a ranked poset with partition
P=C\sqcup O
.
Then the inequality description for
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(P)
in Definition
2.2
is facet defining.

The comparability graph of a poset
P
is the undirected graph
{\operatorname*{Comp}}(P)=(P,E)
with
\{p,q\}\in E
if and only if
p
and
q
are comparable.
It follows from the description (
2
) that the chain polytope
\mathcal{C}(P)
only depends on the graph
{\operatorname*{Comp}}(P)
.

3.
Face normal form for chain-order polytope

Each
k
-dimensional face of a
d
-dimensional polytope can be written as the intersection of
d-k
facets.
However, in general, the choice of facets is not unique.
The purpose of a face normal form is to make such a selection.

From now on, we consider a maximal ranked poset
P_{\tau}
where
\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{\ell})
is a sequence of positive integers; here
\tau_{i}
dictates the number of elements in rank
i
.
We abbreviate the notation for the order and chain polytopes by
\mathcal{O}(\tau)
and
\mathcal{C}(\tau)
, respectively.
To describe the intermediate chain-order polytopes, we introduce the following concept.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\ell}
be a tuple and
0\leq k\leq\ell
.
Then we define
k
-decomposition
of
P_{\tau}
as the decomposition
P_{\tau}=C\sqcup O
with

C=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{k}Y^{{i}},\hskip 10.00002ptO=\bigcup\limits_{i=k+1}^{\ell+1}Y^{{i}},

where
Y^{i}
refers to the elements of rank
i
and
Y^{0}=\{\hat{0}\}
and
Y^{\ell+1}=\{\hat{1}\}
.
The decompositions for
k=0
and
k=\ell
are called
trivial
, and the other
k
-decompositions are
non-trivial
.
Throughout, we abbreviate
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(P_{\tau})
as
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
.

To describe the face lattice of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
, we rephrase the facet description from Definition
2.2
for our class of posets
P_{\tau}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\ell}
and let
P_{\tau}=C\sqcup O
be a
k
-decomposition.
Then the chain-order polytope
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
is defined by the following facet-defining inequalities:

a.

x_{\hat{0}}=0
and
x_{\hat{1}}=1
;

b.

for each
p\in C
the inequality
0\leq x_{p}
;

c.

for each
a,b\in O
with
a\lessdot b
the
order inequality

x_{a}\leq x_{b}
;

d.

for any
p_{i}\in Y^{i}
and
q_{k+1}\in Y^{k+1}
the
chain inequality

x_{p_{1}}+\dots+x_{p_{k}}\leq x_{q_{k+1}}
.

Proof.

The first two types are the same as in Definition
2.2
.
The last two types of inequalities in the lemma together form the third class of inequalities in the original definition.
The order inequalities arise when
r=0
, whereas the chain inequalities appear when
r>0
. The latter occurs only if the minimum of the corresponding chain is exactly
\hat{0}
.
Proposition
2.3
confirms that these inequalities are facet-defining.
∎

We will construct a normal form for the faces of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
in several steps:
Proposition
3.3
will deal with faces contained in order facets but not in chain facets.
In Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
, we will consider faces that are contained in chain facets but not in order facets.
The remaining case, where order and chain facets may intersect nontrivially, will be discussed in Theorem
3.8
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Consider a
k
-decomposition of
P_{\tau}
.
Let
F\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
be a face defined only by order inequalities.
Then
F
is given by a face partition of
O
.

Proof.

We are going to project downwards onto the order polytope
\mathcal{O}(\tilde{P})
, where
\tilde{P}
is the induced subposet

\tilde{P}=\{\hat{0}\}\cup O.

The latter is the poset restricted to the order part.
Now consider the projection

\phi:\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\tilde{P})\,,\ (x_{p})_{p\in P_{\tau}}\mapsto(x_{p})_{p\in O}\kern 5.0pt.

The image
\phi(F)
is again a face of
\mathcal{O}(\tilde{P})
, and the equations satisfied are precisely the same as those for
F
.
As
F
does not satisfy any chain inequality, it follows that is does not satisfy
x_{q}=0
for any
q\in O
.
This implies that
\phi(F)
only satisfies equalities of the form
x_{p}=x_{q}
with
p,q\in O
for
p\leq q
, or
x_{p}=1
for some
p\in Y^{{n}}
.
Theorem
2.1
implies that
\phi(F)
is given by a face partition of
\tilde{P}
.
However, since not all
x\in\phi(F)
satisfy
x_{p}=0
for any
p\in O
, the element
\hat{0}
is in a singleton block.
Thus, the face
F
defined only by order inequalities, is entirely determined by a face partition of
O
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Consider a
k
-decomposition of
P_{\tau}
and a face
F\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
given only by order inequalities and let
\varphi
be the face partition of
O
and let
\pi
be the extended partition from
\varphi
by adding singletons.
Then we have

\mathcal{O}_{C,\varphi}(P_{\tau}/\pi)\cong F\kern 5.0pt.

In particular, the codimension of
F
is exactly
|O|-|\varphi|
.

Here
“
\cong
”
refers to affine isomorphy.

Proof.

Checking that
\pi
is a face partition will be left out as it is a straight-forward verification.
Then according to Theorem
2.1
, using the projection onto the blocks
q:P_{\tau}\rightarrow P_{\tau}/\pi
, we get the affine isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{C,\varphi}(P_{\tau}/\pi)\rightarrow F\,,\ (x_{p^{\prime}})_{p^{\prime}\in(P_{\tau}/\pi)}\mapsto(x_{q(p)})_{p\in P_{\tau}}\kern 5.0pt.

The dimension of
F
is exactly
|\pi|-2
, where we account for the blocks containing
\hat{0}
and
\hat{1}
.
However,
\hat{0}
and all other elements of
C
are in singleton blocks.
Hence

|\pi|-2=|C|+|\varphi|-2

where we account for the block in
\varphi
containing
\hat{1}
and
\hat{0}\in C
.
Since the chain-order polytope is of dimension
|O|+|C|-2
, the codimension equals

|O|+|C|-2-(|C|+|\varphi|-2)=|O|-|\varphi|\kern 5.0pt.\qed

Finally, we need to understand what the quotient poset
P_{\tau}/\pi
looks like.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix a
k
-decomposition of
P_{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\ell}
. Let
F\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
be a face given only by order inequalities, and let
\pi
be the extended partition on
P_{\tau}
.
Then the quotient poset
P_{\tau}/\pi
is of the form
P_{\tau^{\prime}}
for some other tuple
\tau^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Instead of inspecting the entire partition, we will only consider what happens when turning an edge of the Hasse diagram into an equality.
The proof then follows by induction.
Consider the edge
\{a,b\}
for
a\in Y^{{i}}
,
b\in Y^{{i+1}}
and
i<\ell
. Then for all
x\in Y^{{t}}
with
t<i+1
and
x\neq a
we have
x<b
.
Similarly, for all
y\in Y^{{s}}
with
s>i
and
y\neq b
, we get
y>a
.
Thus, all blocks satisfy the relations of
P_{\tau^{\prime}}
with

\tau^{\prime}=(\tau,\dots,\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i}-1,1,\tau_{i+1}-1,\dots,\tau_{\ell})

and thus the quotient poset is just
P_{\tau^{\prime}}
.
If
i=\ell
, the same arguments yield that the quotient poset is isomorphic to
P_{\tau^{\prime}}
with

\tau^{\prime}=(\tau,\dots,\tau_{\ell}-1)

Now, if a zero entry should arise in the tuples above, then we just skip it.
∎

Now we will inspect the chain inequalities, where
C\neq\{\hat{0}\}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
be a face given by
x_{b}=0
for some
b\in C\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
from a
k
-decomposition
P_{\tau}=C\sqcup O
.
For
b\in Y^{i}
we have
F\cong\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C},O}(\tilde{\tau})

where

\tilde{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i}-1,\tau_{i+1},\dots,\tau_{\ell})

and
\tilde{C}=C\setminus\{b\}
.
Again, zero entries in
\tilde{\tau}
are omitted.

Proof.

The chain-inequalities using
b
are of the form
\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{p_{j}}\leq x_{a}
with
b=p_{i}
.
For
x_{b}=0
such an inequality simplifies to

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}x_{p_{i}}\leq x_{a}\kern 5.0pt.

If there exists
c\in Y^{{i}}\setminus\{b\}
, then these inequalities are redundant in view of

x_{c}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}x_{p_{i}}\leq x_{a}\kern 5.0pt.

Either way, the defining inequalities of the face are exactly the defining inequalities of
\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}O}(\tilde{\tau})
.
Thus, the linear projection

F\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C},O}(\tilde{\tau})\,,\ (x_{p})_{p\in P_{\tau}}\mapsto(x_{p})_{p\in P_{\tilde{\tau}}}

is an affine isomorphism.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
be the face for a
k
-decomposition given by the equalities from the maximal chains

\mathbf{p}=\{\hat{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{k},a\},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{p^{\prime}}=\{\hat{0},p^{\prime}_{1},\dots,p^{\prime}_{k},a^{\prime}\}

where
a
and
a^{\prime}
belong to the minimal antichain of
O
.
We define
p_{k+1}:=a
and
p^{\prime}_{k+1}:=a^{\prime}
.
Let
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,k+1\}
be the set of indices, where we have
p_{i}\neq p^{\prime}_{i}
for
i\in I
.
By projecting the coordinates of
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\setminus\mathbf{p}
away, we get the affine isomorphism

\nu:F\rightarrow F^{\prime}\,,\ (x_{p})_{p\in P_{\tau}}\mapsto(x_{p^{\prime}})_{p^{\prime}\in P_{\tau^{\prime}}}\kern 5.0pt,

where
\tau^{\prime}=(\tau^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\tau^{\prime}_{\ell})
with

\tau^{\prime}_{j}=\begin{cases}\tau_{j}-1&\text{ if $j\in I$}\\
\tau_{j}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

and the face
F^{\prime}
is given by the equation of
\mathbf{p}
. Each coordinate that gets projected away yields us exactly one additional codimension.

Proof.

Fix an
i\in I
with
i<k+1
. Then we can form the two maximal chains

\mathbf{q}=(\mathbf{p}\setminus\{p_{i}\})\cup\{p^{\prime}_{i}\},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{q}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\setminus\{p^{\prime}_{i}\})\cup\{p_{i}\}

Then, we get the inequalities

x_{a}+x_{p^{\prime}_{i}}-x_{p_{i}}=\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbf{q}\cap C}x_{q}\leq x_{a}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{a^{\prime}}+x_{p_{i}}-x_{p^{\prime}_{i}}=\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\cap C}x_{q}\leq x_{a^{\prime}}\kern 5.0pt.

In particular, we get
x_{p^{\prime}_{i}}\leq x_{p_{i}}
as well as
x_{p_{i}}\leq x_{p^{\prime}_{i}}
.
That is, we have
x_{p_{i}}=x_{p^{\prime}_{i}}
.
If we also have
a\neq a^{\prime}
, then using the chains

\mathbf{r}=(\mathbf{p}\setminus\{a\})\cup\{a^{\prime}\},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{r}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\setminus\{a^{\prime}\})\cup\{a\}

we obtain

x_{a}=\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbf{p}\cap C}x_{q}=\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbf{r}\cap C}x_{q}\leq x_{a^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{a^{\prime}}=\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\cap C}x_{q}=\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\cap C}x_{q}\leq x_{a}\kern 5.0pt.

We infer
x_{a}=x_{a^{\prime}}
.
So the face
F
satisfies the equation given by
\mathbf{p}
and the equations

(3)

x_{p_{i}}=x_{p^{\prime}_{i}}\text{ for all $i\in I$}\kern 5.0pt.

Further, the subset of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
given by the equation by
\mathbf{p}
and the equalities (
3
) satisfies the equation given by
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
, i.e. the equations of (
3
) and
\mathbf{p}
satisfy
F
.
We can thus project the coordinates from
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\setminus\mathbf{p}
away and get the claimed affine isomorphism.
∎

We can now establish a normal form for the faces of the chain-order polytope.

Theorem 3.8
.

Consider a
k
-decomposition
P_{\tau}=C\sqcup O
.
Any face
F\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
is uniquely given by:

a.

a face partition of
O
, which we call
\pi
;

b.

sets
F_{0,i}\subseteq Y^{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
(indicating those variables which are zero for all points in
F
);

c.

sets
F_{\mathrm{eq},i}
for
1\leq i\leq k+1
(indicating the tight chain inequalities) satisfying

(i)

F_{\mathrm{eq},i}\subseteq Y^{i}\setminus F_{0,i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq k
,

(ii)

if
Y^{{i}}\setminus F_{0,i}\neq\emptyset
, then
\emptyset\neq F_{\mathrm{eq},i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq k
,

(iii)

F_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}\subseteq Y^{k+1}
,

(iv)

F_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}
only contains elements of
Y^{k+1}
that belong to singleton blocks of the face partition
\pi
.

Proof.

The face partition results from Proposition

.

We now fix the set of variables equal to

.
Recall that the variables correspond to the elements of the poset

If we now want to add chains, then each chain has to contain an element

.

Then, all chains have to end in the minimal elements of

(rigorously speaking, we must put the remaining elements in singleton blocks to form the quotient).
These minimal elements are the singletons blocks of

if they exist.
If all elements of

are in blocks containing elements greater than them, and thus in the same block, there is also no choice for us to make.
∎

With the notation from Theorem

From the special case

In this section we use Theorem

to obtain our main result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\ell}
and let
0\leq k\leq n-1
.
We consider the
k
-decomposition
P_{\tau}=C\sqcup O
and the
(k+1)
-decomposition
P_{\tau}=C^{\prime}\sqcup O^{\prime}
.

Then, there exists an injective map from the set of faces of codimension
i
of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
to the set of faces of codimension
i
of
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime},O^{\prime}}(\tau)
for all
2\leq i\leq|\tau|
.

The crucial insight is that the normal forms of the
k
- and
(k+1)
-decompositions differ only on the set
Y^{k+1}\cup Y^{k+2}

Thus, we construct the proof based on the case distinction of the face partition of the
k
-decomposition.
Proving the above result establishes the Hibi–Li inequality.

Corollary 4.2
.

For
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\ell}

f_{i}(\mathcal{O}(\tau))\leq f_{i}(\mathcal{C}(\tau))\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq|\tau|\kern 5.0pt.

Proof.

The claim follows from Theorem
4.1
since
\mathcal{O}(\tau)
is a chain-order polytope for the
0

\mathcal{C}(\tau)
to that of the
n
-partition.
∎

4.1.
Construction: Preliminaries

We now define a map,
\psi
, which sends faces of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
to faces of
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime},O^{\prime}}(\tau)
.
We first introduce some technical details that will be used in the construction, then we will define
\psi
by distinguishing several cases.
Finally, in Section
4.4
we will see that
\psi
is injective.
Along the way, we exploit that the normal forms differ only locally.
Let us fix a face
F
of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
with
\codim F=t
. We construct a corresponding face
F^{\prime}=\psi(F)
of
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime},O^{\prime}}(\tau)
of the same codimension.

4.1.1.
Face partition:

The idea is to
“cut off”
the elements
Y^{k+1}
from all blocks
\pi
of the face partition of
O\cup\{\hat{1}\}
.
We look at the partition

\pi^{\prime\prime}:=\{K\cap O^{\prime}\mid K\in\pi,K\not\subseteq Y^{{k+1}}\cup Y^{{k+2}}\}

and define the extended partition
\pi^{\prime}
by putting the remaining elements of
O^{\prime}
(those which are not contained in any block of
\pi^{\prime\prime}
) into singletons.
The following result shows that
\pi^{\prime}
is a face partition.

Lemma 4.3
.

The partition
\pi^{\prime\prime}
is connected, and thus also
\pi^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
K\cap O^{\prime}=K
for
K\in\pi
, then this block is, of course, connected.
If
K\cap O^{\prime}\neq K
, then
K
contains elements from
Y^{{k+1}}
.
However, by construction, we require that
K
also contains elements greater than
Y^{{k+2}}
and thus
K
contains
Y^{{k+2}}
(
K
is convex
[
Peg18
, Remark 3.8]
), which allows it to be connected by forming the fence
a>y<b
for
a,b\in K\cap O^{\prime}
and some
y\in Y^{k}
.
Thus
K\cap O^{\prime}
is connected.
Hence, we have that
\pi^{\prime}
is connected, and of course, it is again compatible and a face partition.
∎

One might ask why we cannot just intersect with
O^{\prime}
and leave out the condition on
K
.
The reason is that a block
K\in\pi
of height
1
would result in a disconnected block if we intersect it with
O^{\prime}
.
Hence, we must leave out the minimal blocks of height
1
.
Therefore, we need to put the restriction on the block
K
.

4.1.2.
Zero elements:

Here, we just copy the zero elements. We set

F^{\prime}_{0,i}=F_{0,i}

for all
1\leq i\leq k
.
The variables for
i=k+1
will be considered in the individual cases.

4.1.3.
Chain elements:

Similarly, as before, we simply copy the chain elements.
If
F
already uses chains, then we set

F^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eq},i}=F_{\mathrm{eq},i}

for all
1\leq i\leq k+1
.
The chain variables for
i=k+2
will be considered in the individual cases.

4.2.1.

\pi

Here, we do not cut off any block. Thus, we simply continue the chains and define

The codimension also match as we did not alter the non-singleton blocks in

4.2.2.
Not all elements of

4.4.
Injectivity of the Map
\psi

We now consider a function
\psi
which maps faces of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
to faces of
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime},O^{\prime}}(\tau)
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be a face of
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime},O^{\prime}}(\tau)
with codimension
t
, and let
G
be a face of
\mathcal{O}_{C,O}(\tau)
such that
\psi(G)=G^{\prime}
.
To show that
\psi
is injective, we need to prove that
G
is unique.

Let
\pi^{\prime}
be the face partition of
G^{\prime}
, and let
\pi
be the face partition of
G
.
Then, we can immediately reconstruct all blocks of
G
contained in
O^{\prime}
.
Next, we distinguish the set of variables set to
0
.

4.4.1.
The case
G^{\prime}_{0,k+1}=\emptyset

There are two subcases.

4.4.1.1
Assume
G^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eq},k+2}=\{y_{r^{\prime}}^{k+2}\}
or
G^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eq},k+2}=\emptyset
.

The condition
G^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eq},k+2}=\emptyset
also includes the case when no chain inequality is tight.
Here,
y_{r^{\prime}}^{{k+2}}
has the minimal index
r^{\prime}
while in a singleton block in
\pi
.
Then,
G
’ came from the Cases
4.2.1
or
4.3.1
.
Thus, we can reconstruct
\pi
, the sets
G_{0,i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and if we use chains, we can fully reconstruct them as well.

4.4.1.2
Assume
G^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eq},k+2}\neq\emptyset
and
G^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eq},k+2}\neq\{y_{r^{\prime}}^{k+2}\}
.

Then we are in the case
4.3.2.2.1
. Hence, we know that
G
uses no chain and has a block

.
We can reconstruct
K
via

and thus the whole partition
\pi
.
Furthermore, we also have that we can reconstruct
G_{0,i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

4.4.2.
The case
G^{\prime}_{0,k+1}\neq\emptyset

We have
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{l}\}\subseteq Y^{{k+1}}
contained in a block

containing elements greater than them.
There are three subcases.

4.4.2.1
All elements of
Y^{{k+2}}
are contained in the same block in
\pi^{\prime}

Then we know that
K
has height at least
2
. Hence, we are in the Cases
4.2.2
or
4.3.2.1
.
In this instance,
G^{\prime}_{0,k+1}
provides us exactly with the minimal elements of

. Thus, we can reconstruct
K
via

K=T\cup G^{\prime}_{0,k+1}\kern 5.0pt,

where

is the block containing
Y^{k+2}
. In this way, we can recover the entire partition
\pi
.
Similarly, we can reconstruct all chain elements and also all variables set to
0
.

4.4.2.2
Not all elements of
Y^{{k+2}}
are contained in the same block in
\pi^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
uses a chain.

Then we know that
K
is of height exactly
1
,
G
already uses a chain and we are in the Case
4.2.2
.
In this instance, we can reconstruct

and thus, again, the whole partition
\pi
.
We recover the chains and the remaining variables set to

.

Not all elements of

K

.

if

.

\pi
, and so we can reconstruct all variables set to

.

5.
Computations

Here we discuss methods of computing the face numbers of the order and chain polytopes of an arbitrary finite poset and their implementation.
Order and chain polytopes of posets are unique in that both their vertex and facet descriptions can be derived from the poset through combinatorial means.
Such a pair of a point set and a set of linear inequalities is also known as the
double description
of a polytope.
This means that convex hull computations, which can be tedious or even forbiddingly expensive, are unnecessary; see
[
Sei18
]
for a survey and
[
ABS97
,
Ass+17
]
for computational experiments.
The incidences between vertices and facets are directly available from a double description.
Since the face lattice of a polytope is both atomic and coatomic
[
Zie95
, §2.2]
, those vertex-facet incidences determine the entire face lattice.
Kaibel and Pfetsch found an output-sensitive algorithm for computing the entire Hasse diagram of the face lattice from the vertex-facet incidences of a polytope
[
KP02
]
.
This algorithm is implemented in
polymake

[
Ass+17
]
, which we used for our experiments.
Kliem and Stump recently described a slightly faster method to compute the faces without the covering relations, i.e., the nodes of the Hasse diagram but not the edges
[
KS22
]
.
Yet, for our purposes, we found the Kaibel–Pfetsch algorithm to be sufficiently fast.

This leaves the question of how to obtain the vertices and the facets of the order and chain polytopes in the first place.
Each step in the procedure are standard graph algorithms.
The initialization phase is the same for the order and the chain polytope.
The input consists of the Hasse diagram of the extended poset
\hat{P}
, represented as a directed graph with arcs oriented upward.
Starting at the bottom element
\hat{0}
, we perform a depth-first search to obtain all maximal chains.
This gives the comparability graph
{\operatorname*{Comp}}(P)
, which is undirected, by enumerating all pairs of nodes in each maximal chain.
The most interesting subtask is to enumerate the maximal antichains.
These are precisely the maximal independent sets of
{\operatorname*{Comp}}(P)
, which in turn is the same as the set of maximal cliques in the complement of
{\operatorname*{Comp}}(P)
.
Computing maximal independent sets or, equivalently, maximal cliques is a key algorithmic problem in combinatorial optimization.
The decision problem CLIQUE, which asks if a given graph has a maximal clique (or independent set) of size at least
k
, is known to NP-complete
[
GJ79
, GT19]
.
This means that there is no hope for a fast algorithm.
Makino and Uno gave an algorithm for CLIQUE which performs well in practice
[
MU04
]
, and this is implemented in
polymake
.
Equipped with this information, formulating double descriptions of
\mathcal{O}(P)
and
\mathcal{C}(P)
becomes straightforward.
The details are spelled out in Algorithms
1
and
2
.

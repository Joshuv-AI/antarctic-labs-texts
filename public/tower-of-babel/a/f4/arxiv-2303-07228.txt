Estimate distillable entanglement and quantum capacity by squeezing useless entanglement
Chengkai Zhu, Chenghong Zhu, Xin Wang

Abstract
Quantum Internet relies on quantum entanglement as a fundamental resource for secure and efficient quantum communication, reshaping data transmission. In this context, entanglement distillation emerges as a crucial process that plays a pivotal role in realizing the full potential of the quantum internet. Nevertheless, it remains challenging to accurately estimate the distillable entanglement and its closely related essential quantity, the quantum capacity. In this work, we consider a general resource measure known as the reverse divergence of resources which quantifies the minimum divergence between a target state and the set of free states. Leveraging this measure, we propose efficiently computable upper bounds for both quantities based on the idea that the useless entanglement within a state or a quantum channel does not contribute to the distillable entanglement or the quantum capacity, respectively. Our bounds can be computed via semidefinite programming and have practical applications for purifying maximally entangled states under practical noises, such as depolarizing and amplitude damping noises, leading to improvements in estimating the one-way distillable entanglement. Furthermore, we provide valuable benchmarks for evaluating the quantum capacities of qubit quantum channels, including the Pauli channels and the random mixed unitary channels, which are of great interest for the development of a quantum internet.

Main contributions

This work considers a family of resource measures called

II
Reverse divergence of resources

II-A

Preliminaries

Let
{\cal H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, and
{\cal L}({\cal H})
be the set of linear operators acting on it. We consider two parties Alice and Bob with Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B}
, whose dimensions are
d_{A},d_{B}
, respectively. A linear operator
\rho\in{\cal L}({\cal H})
is called a density operator if it is Hermitian and positive semidefinite with trace one. We denote the trace norm of
\rho
as
\|\rho\|_{1}=\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\rho^{\dagger}\rho}
and let
{\cal D}({\cal H})
denote the set of density operators. We call a linear map CPTP if it is both completely positive and trace-preserving. A CPTP map that transforms linear operators in
{\cal L}({\cal H}_{A})
to linear operators in
{\cal L}({\cal H}_{B})
is also called a quantum channel, denoted as
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
. For a quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, its Choi-Jamiołkowski state is given by
J_{AB}^{{\cal N}}\equiv\sum_{i,j=0}^{d_{A}-1}|i\rangle\!\langle j|\otimes\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}(|i\rangle\!\langle j|)
, where
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d_{A}-1}
is an orthonormal basis in
{\cal H}_{A}
. The von Neumann entropy of a state
\rho_{A}
is
S(A)_{\rho}:=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A})
and the coherent information of a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
is defined by
I_{c}(A\rangle B)_{\rho}:=S(B)_{\rho}-S(AB)_{\rho}
. The entanglement of formation of a state
\rho_{AB}
is given by

E_{F}(\rho_{AB})=\min_{\{p_{i},|\phi_{i}\rangle\}}\sum_{i}p_{i}S(A)_{\phi_{i}},

(1)

where
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\phi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{i}|_{AB}
and the minimization ranges over all pure state decomposition of
\rho_{AB}
. We introduce the generalized divergence
\bm{D}(\rho_{A}\|\sigma_{A})
as a map
\bm{D}:{\cal D}({\cal H}_{A})\times{\cal D}({\cal H}_{A})\mapsto\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
that obeys:

1.

Faithfulness:
\bm{D}(\rho_{A}\|\sigma_{A})=0
iff
\rho_{A}=\sigma_{A}
.

2.

Data processing inequality:
\bm{D}(\rho_{A}\|\sigma_{A})\geq\bm{D}[\mathcal{N}_{A\to A^{\prime}}(\rho_{A})\|\mathcal{N}_{A\to A^{\prime}}(\sigma_{A})]
, where
\mathcal{N}_{A\to A^{\prime}}
is an arbitrary quantum channel.

The generalized divergence is intuitively some measure of distinguishability of the two states, e.g., Bures metric, quantum relative entropy. Another example of interest is the
sandwiched Rényi relative entropy

[
36
,
37
]
of
\rho,\sigma
that is defined by

D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}\right],

(2)

if
\operatorname{supp}(\rho)\subset\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)
and it is equal to
+\infty
otherwise, where
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. In the case that
\alpha\to\infty
, one can find the
max-relative entropy

[
38
]
of
\rho
with respect to
\sigma
by

D_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)=\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\rho\leq 2^{\lambda}\sigma\}.

(3)

II-B

Reverse divergence of resources

In the usual framework of quantum resource theories
[
39
]
, there are two main notions: i) subset
{\cal F}
of free states, i.e., the states that do not possess the given resource; ii) subset
{\cal O}
of free operations, i.e., the quantum channels that are unable to generate the resource. Meanwhile, two axioms for a quantity being a resource measure
{\cal R}(\cdot)
are essential:

1).

Vanishing for free states:
\rho\in{\cal F}\Rightarrow{\cal R}(\rho)=0
.

2).

Monotonicity:
{\cal R}({\cal O}(\rho))\leq{\cal R}(\rho)
for any free operation
{\cal O}
.
{\cal R}(\cdot)
is called a
resource monotone
.

Let us define a family of resource measures called
reverse divergence of resources
:

{\cal R}_{{\cal F}}(\rho):=\min_{\tau\in{\cal F}}\bm{D}(\tau||\rho),

(4)

where
\mathcal{F}
is some set of free states. By the definition of the reverse divergence of resources in Eq. (
4
), one can easily check it satisfies condition 1). Whenever the free state set
{\cal F}
is closed under the free operations, by the data-processing inequality of
\bm{D}(\cdot\|\cdot)
, condition 2) will be satisfied. Thus
{\cal R}_{\mathcal{F}}(\cdot)
is a resource measure. Specifically, in the resource theory of entanglement, some representative free state sets are the separable states (SEP) and the states having a positive partial transpose (PPT). Examples of free sets of operations are LOCC and PPT. We note that the ”reverse” here means minimizing the divergence over a free state set in the first coordinate, rather than the second one which has helped one define the
relative entropy of entanglement

[
40
,
41
]
and the
max-relative entropy of entanglement

[
38
]
. For some divergence of particular interest, e.g., the quantum relative entropy
D(\cdot||\cdot)
, relevant discussion of the coordinate choices can be traced back to work in
[
41
]
. In
[
42
]
, the authors further studied properties of the quantity
\min_{\tau\in{\cal F}}D(\tau||\rho)
. Here, we try to further investigate meaningful applications of some reverse divergence of resources.

In the following, we consider the generalized divergence as the max-relative entropy and study a measure called
reverse max-relative entropy of resources
,

{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho):=\min_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}}D_{\max}(\tau||\rho),

(5)

where
\mathcal{F}
is some set of free states. If there is no such state
\tau\in{\cal F}
that satisfies
\tau\leq 2^{\lambda}\rho
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho)
is set to be 0.
This measure bears many nice properties. First, it can be efficiently computed via semidefinite programming (SDP) in many cases. Second, Eq. (
5
) gives the closest free state
\tau\in{\cal F}
to
\rho
, w.r.t. the max-relative entropy. Third,
{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\cdot)
is subadditive w.r.t the tensor product of states. In fact,
{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho)
is closely related to the
weight of resource

W(\rho)

[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
]
and the
free component

\Gamma(\rho)

[
47
]
, both of which have fruitful properties and applications
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
, as follows

2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho)}=1-W(\rho)=\Gamma(\rho).

(6)

We note that each part of Eq. (
6
) quantifies the largest weight that a free state can take in a convex decomposition of
\rho
. When moving on to operational tasks that the free state can be ignored, what is left in a convex decomposition becomes our main concern. Optimization of the weight in the decomposition can be visualized as
squeezing out
all free parts of the given state. Thus, we further introduce the
{\cal F}
-squeezed state
of
\rho
as follows.

Definition 1

For a quantum state
\rho
and a free state set
{\cal F}
, if
{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho)
is non-zero, the
{\cal F}
-squeezed state of
\rho
is defined by

\omega=\frac{\rho-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho)}\cdot\tau}{1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho)}},

(7)

where
\tau
is the closest free state to
\rho
in terms of the max-relative entropy, i.e., the optimal solution in Eq. (
5
). If
{\cal R}_{\max,\mathcal{F}}(\rho)=0
, the
{\cal F}
-squeezed state of
\rho
is itself.

In the following sections, we will illustrate the applications of the reverse max-relative entropy of resources as well as the squeezing idea on example tasks. One is giving upper bounds on the distillable entanglement of arbitrary quantum states. The other is to derive upper bounds on the quantum capacity of channels.

III
Applications on distillable entanglement

In this section, we investigate the information-theoretic application of the reverse max-relative entropy of resources in deriving efficiently computable upper bounds on the distillable entanglement. To showcase the advantage of our bounds, we compare the results with different continuity bounds and the Rains bound on the maximally entangled states with practical noises.

III-A

Upper bound on the one-way distillable entanglement

Recall that the one-way distillable entanglement has a regularized formula
[
9
]
:

\displaystyle E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}E^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}),

(8)

where

E^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB}):=\max_{T}I_{\textup{\tiny C}}(A^{\prime}\rangle MB)_{T(\rho_{AB})}
,
and the maximization ranges over all quantum instruments

T:A\to A^{\prime}M
on Alice’s system. The regularization in Eq. (
8
) for
E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})
is intractable to compute in most cases. However, there are some categories of states whose
E_{D,\to}
can be reduced to single-letter formulae. Two important classes are called
degradable states
and
anti-degradable states
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state with purification
|\phi\rangle_{ABE}
.
\rho_{AB}
is called degradable if there exists a CPTP map
\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow E}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow E}(\rho_{AB})=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(\phi_{ABE})
, and is called anti-degradable if there exists a CPTP map
\mathcal{M}_{E\rightarrow B}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{E\rightarrow B}(\rho_{AE})=\operatorname{Tr}_{E}(\phi_{ABE})
. Equivalently, a state is anti-degradable if and only if it has a symmetric extension
[
51
]
, thus is also called a
two-extendible state
. For the degradable states, it is shown that
[
17
]

\displaystyle E^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=nE^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})=nI_{\textup{\tiny C}}(A\rangle B)_{\rho},\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

(9)

resulting in
E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})=I_{\textup{\tiny C}}(A\rangle B)_{\rho}
. For the anti-degradable states, consisting of a compact and convex set, it always holds

E^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})=E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})=0.

(10)

Moreover, Leditzky, Datta, and Smith
[
17
]
showed that
E_{D,\to}(\cdot)
is convex on decomposing a state into degradable and anti-degradable parts, i.e.,

E^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})\leq s\cdot E^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\omega_{AB})+(1-s)\cdot E^{(1)}_{D,\to}(\tau_{AB}),

(11)

where
\rho_{AB}=s\omega_{AB}+(1-s)\tau_{AB}
,
\omega_{AB}
is degradable, and
\tau_{AB}
is anti-degradable.

To better exploit this convexity, we introduce the
reverse max-relative entropy of unextendible entanglement
to help identify the anti-degradable (two-extendible) part of a given bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
:

{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB}):=\min_{\tau\in{\rm ADG}}D_{\max}(\tau_{AB}||\rho_{AB}),

(12)

where
{\rm ADG}
is the set of all anti-degradable states. In this resource theory, the resource we consider is the extendible entanglement, and the free states are bipartite states that are possibly shareable between
A
and a third party
E
, where
E
is isomorphic to
B
. Notably, the extendibility of entanglement is a key property in entanglement theory with many existing applications
[
18
,
52
]
. Here, combined with the idea of entanglement of formation,
{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})
can be applied to derive an upper bound on the one-way distillable entanglement of an arbitrary state
\rho_{AB}
as shown in Theorem
1
.

Fig. 1:
Illustration of the
{\cal F}
-squeezed state of
\rho_{AB}
and Theorem
1
. The brown region corresponds to the sub-state
\tau_{AB}
belonging to the free state set
{\cal F}
, which is squeezed out via a convex decomposition of
\rho_{AB}
.
\omega_{AB}
is the
{\cal F}
-squeezed state.

Theorem 1

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
,

\displaystyle E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle\leq E_{\rm rev}^{u}(\rho_{AB})

(13)

\displaystyle:=[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}]\cdot E_{F}(\omega_{AB}),

where
\omega_{AB}
is the
{\rm ADG}
-squeezed state of
\rho_{AB}
,
{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\cdot)
is the reverse max-relative entropy of unextendible entanglement, and
E_{F}(\cdot)
is the entanglement of formation.

Proof.

Suppose the ADG-squeezed state of
\rho_{AB}
is
\omega_{AB}
and the optimal solution in Eq. (
5
) for
\rho_{AB}
is
\tau_{AB}
. It follows
\rho_{AB}=[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}]\omega_{AB}+2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}\tau_{AB}
,
where
\tau_{AB}
is anti-degradable. Since any pure state is degradable, we can decompose
\rho_{AB}
into degradable and anti-degradable parts as

\rho_{AB}=[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}]\sum_{i}p_{i}|\omega_{i}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{i}|_{AB}+2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}\tau_{AB}
, where
\omega_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\omega_{i}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{i}|_{AB}
is any pure state decomposition of
\omega_{AB}
. Based on the essential convexity of
E_{D,\to}(\cdot)
on decomposing a state into degradable and anti-degradable parts
[
17
]
(cf. Eq. (
11
)), we have

\displaystyle E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle\leq[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}]\cdot\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{D,\to}(\omega_{i})

\displaystyle+2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}E_{D,\to}(\tau_{AB})

\displaystyle\leq[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}]\cdot\sum_{i}p_{i}I_{c}(A\rangle B)_{\omega_{i}},

where the second inequality is due to the properties in Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
10
) for degradable states and anti-degradable states, respectively. After taking the minimization over all possible decomposition of
\omega_{AB}
, we arrived at
E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})\leq[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}]\cdot E_{F}(\omega_{AB})
.
∎

Remark 1
The bound
E_{\rm rev}^{u}(\rho_{AB})
has a cartoon illustration as shown in Fig.
1
. The main insight of it is to squeeze out as much of the free or useless part, the anti-degradable state here, as possible. We point out that squeezing
all
useless parts out does not necessarily give the tightest upper bound in terms of the one-way distillable entanglement, e.g., isotropic states
[
17
]
. Instead of squeezing out all the useless parts, there may be room for exploring more appropriate decompositions when we try to decompose a specific quantum state. However, the approach we present in Theorem
1
is an invaluable method for general states as shown in subsection
III-C
and can be seen as a variant of the continuity bound in terms of the anti-degradability of the state.

Corollary 2

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
,

\displaystyle E_{D,\to}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{u}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle:=[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}]\cdot\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}S(B)_{\psi_{i}},

where
\omega_{AB}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|
is the spectral decomposition of the ADG-squeezed state
\omega_{AB}
of
\rho_{AB}
.

Corollary
2
is followed by the fact that
E_{F}(\omega_{AB})
has a trivial upper bound as
E_{F}(\omega_{AB})\leq\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}S(B)_{\psi_{i}}
. We note that any other good upper bounds on the entanglement of formation can also be applied to Theorem
1
. In particular, the bound
\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{u}(\rho_{AB})
is efficiently computable since
{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})
and
2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}
can be efficiently computed via an SDP.
By Slater’s condition
[
53
]
, the primal and dual problems satisfy strong duality, and both evaluate to
1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}(\rho_{AB})}
. We remain the derivation of the dual program in Appendix
A
.

\displaystyle\underline{\textbf{Primal Program}}

\displaystyle\min_{\omega_{AB},\tau_{AB},\tau_{ABE}}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[\omega_{AB}],

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\omega_{AB}+\tau_{AB},

\displaystyle\omega_{AB}\geq 0,\tau_{AB}\geq 0,\tau_{ABE}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{E}[\tau_{ABE}]=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[\tau_{ABE}]=\tau_{AB}.

It is worth mentioning that this new bound is related to the bound

proposed in
[
17
]
utilizing the convexity of

on decomposing a state into degradable and anti-degradable parts. Remarkably, constructing such a decomposition is challenging due to the non-convex nature of the degradable state set. Thus, it is difficult to compute
E_{\rm DA}(\cdot)
in practice due to the hardness of tracking all possible decompositions. For example, when considering maximally entangled states affected by general noises, we currently lack strategies to compute the exact value of
E_{\rm DA}(\cdot)
. In contrast,
\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{u}(\cdot)
overcomes this difficulty and can be efficiently computed for general states, serving as a valuable approximation of

. It outperforms the known computable upper bounds for many maximally entangled states under practical noises in a high-noise region, as shown in subsection
III-C
. Moreover, our method in particular offers flexibility in selecting other decomposition strategies, i.e., the object functions in the SDP, beyond simply maximizing the proportion of antidegradable states. Such a method provides a new algorithmic route to fully exploit the bound given in
[
17
]
.

III-B

Continuity bounds of the one-way distillable entanglement

Note that the insight of the bound above is considering the distance of a given state between the anti-degradable states set. With different distance measures, the authors in
[

derived continuity bounds on quantum capacity in terms of the (anti)degradability of the channel. Thus for self-contained, we introduce some distance measures between a state and the set
{\rm ADG}

Definition 2

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite quantum state, the anti-degradable set distance is defined by

where the minimization ranges over all anti-degradable states on system AB.

Similarly, one also has the anti-degradable map distance as follows.

Definition 3

(​​
[

]
)

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite quantum state with purification
\phi_{ABE}
, the anti-degradable map distance is defined by

d_{\rm map}(\rho_{AB}):=\min_{\mathcal{D}:E\rightarrow B}\frac{1}{2}\|\rho_{AB}-\mathcal{D}(\rho_{AE})\|_{1}\\
,

(15)

where

CPTP

\mathcal{D}

For any bipartite state

III-D

Extending the method to the two-way distillable entanglement

Similar to the
reverse max-relative entropy of unextendible entanglement
, for a given bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, we introduce the
reverse max-relative entropy of NPT entanglement
as

{\cal R}_{\max,{\text{\rm PPT}}}(\rho_{AB}):=\min_{\tau\in{\text{\rm PPT}}}D_{\max}(\tau_{AB}||\rho_{AB}),

(19)

where the minimization ranges over all
PPT
states. The term NPT refers to states whose partial transpose has negative eigenvalues. Based on the convexity of
E_{D,\leftrightarrow}(\cdot)
on decomposing a state into maximally correlated (MC) states and PPT states
[
17
]
(see Appendix
B
for more details), we can utilize the reverse max-relative entropy of NPT entanglement to establish an upper bound on the two-way distillable entanglement, as outlined in Theorem
5
.

Theorem 5

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, it satisfies

E_{D,\leftrightarrow}(\rho_{AB})\leq E_{\rm rev}^{npt}(\rho_{AB}):=[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\text{\rm PPT}}}(\rho_{AB})}]\cdot E_{F}(\omega_{AB}),

where
\omega_{AB}
is the PPT-squeezed state of
\rho_{AB}
and
{\cal R}_{\max,{\text{\rm PPT}}}(\cdot)
is the reverse max-relative entropy of NPT entanglement.

It also follows an efficiently computable relaxation as
\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{npt}(\rho_{AB})=[1-2^{-{\cal R}_{\max,{\text{\rm PPT}}}(\rho_{AB})}]\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}S(B)_{\psi_{i}}
, where
\omega_{AB}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|
is the spectral decomposition of the PPT-squeezed state
\omega_{AB}
of
\rho_{AB}
.

In fact,
\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{npt}(\cdot)
can be interpreted as an easily computable version of the bound
E_{\rm MP}(\cdot)
in
[
17
]
, utilizing the convexity of
E_{D,\leftrightarrow}(\rho_{AB})
on the convex decomposition of
\rho_{AB}
into MC states and PPT states. Since the set of all MC states is not convex, tracking all possible decompositions to compute
E_{\rm MP}(\cdot)
is generally hard. However,
\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{npt}(\cdot)
is efficiently computable by SDP and provides a remarkably tighter approximation of
E_{\rm MP}(\cdot)
for the example states presented in
[
17
]
. The comparison in detail can be found in Appendix
B
. We note that
R(\rho_{AB})\leq E_{\rm MP}(\rho_{AB})\leq\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{npt}(\rho_{AB})
where
R(\cdot)
is the Rains bound for the two-way distillable entanglement. Nevertheless,
\hat{E}_{\rm rev}^{npt}(\cdot)
connects the reverse max-relative entropy of NPT entanglement with the entanglement of formation, and we believe such connection would shed light on the study of other quantum resource theories as well.

IV
Applications on quantum channel capacity

For a general quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
, its quantum capacity has a regularized formula proved by Lloyd, Shor, and Devetak
[
56
,
57
,
58
]
:

Q({\cal N})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}Q^{(1)}({\cal N}^{\otimes n}),

(20)

where
Q^{(1)}({\cal N}):=\max_{|\phi\rangle_{A^{\prime}A}}I_{c}(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{({\cal I}\otimes{\cal N})(\phi)}
is the channel coherent information. Similar to the one-way distillable entanglement of a state, the regularization in Eq. (
20
) makes the quantum capacity of a channel intractable to compute generally. Substantial efforts have been made to establish upper bounds. One routine is inspired by the Rains bound in entanglement theory
[
14
]
. Tomamichel et al. introduced Rains information
[
59
]
for a quantum channel as an upper bound on the quantum capacity. Then some efficiently computable relaxations or estimations are given in
[
28
,
31
]
. Another routine is to consider the (anti)degradability of the quantum channels and to construct flag extensions, which gives the currently tightest upper bound for quantum channels with symmetry or known structures
[
25
,
19
,
23
,
21
]
.

A channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
is called
degradable
if there exists a CPTP map
{\cal D}_{B\rightarrow E}
such that
{\cal N}^{c}={\cal D}\circ{\cal N}
, and is called
anti-degradable
if there exists a CPTP map
{\cal A}_{E\rightarrow B}
such that
{\cal N}={\cal A}\circ{\cal N}^{c}
. It is known that
{\cal N}
is (anti)degradable if and only if its Choi state
J_{{\cal N}}
is (anti)degradable. The quantum capacity of an anti-degradable channel is zero and the coherent information of a degradable channel is additive which leads to
Q({\cal N})=Q^{(1)}({\cal N})
. Concerning the (anti)degradability of a channel, the authors in
[
25
]
called a channel
\varepsilon
-degradable channel
if there is a CPTP map
{\cal D}_{B\rightarrow E}
such that
||{\cal N}^{c}-{\cal D}\circ{\cal N}||_{\diamond}\leq 2\varepsilon
. A channel is called
\varepsilon
-anti-degradable channel
if there is a CPTP map
{\cal A}_{E\rightarrow B}
such that
||{\cal N}-{\cal A}\circ{\cal N}^{c}||_{\diamond}\leq 2\varepsilon
. Based on these, one has continuity bounds of the quantum capacity as follows.

Theorem 6

(​​
[
25
]
)

Given a quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, if it is
\varepsilon
-degradable, then it satisfies
Q({\cal N})\leq Q^{(1)}({\cal N})+\varepsilon\log(d_{E}-1)+h(\varepsilon)+2\varepsilon\log d_{E}+g(\varepsilon)
. If
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
is
\varepsilon
-anti-degradable, it satisfies
Q({\cal N})\leq\varepsilon\log(|B|-1)+2\varepsilon\log|B|+h(\varepsilon)+g(\varepsilon)
.

With a similar spirit of the reverse max-relative entropy of unextendible entanglement in Eq. (
12
), we define the
reverse max-relative entropy of anti-degradability of the channel
as

\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N}_{A\to B}):=\min_{{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}\in{\cal C}_{{\rm ADG}}}D_{\max}({\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}||{\cal N}_{A\to B}),

(21)

where
{\cal C}_{{\rm ADG}}
is the set of all anti-degradable channels and the max-relative entropy of
{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}
with respect to
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
is defined by

D_{\max}({\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}||{\cal N}_{A\to B}):=\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:J_{AB}^{{\cal N}^{\prime}}\leq 2^{\lambda}J_{AB}^{{\cal N}}\}.

If there is no such a channel
{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}\in{\cal C}_{{\rm ADG}}
that satisfies
J_{AB}^{{\cal N}^{\prime}}\leq 2^{\lambda}J_{AB}^{{\cal N}}
,
\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N}_{A\to B})
is set to be 0. Similar to the state case,
\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N}_{A\to B})
has a geometric implication analogous to the distance between
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
to the set of all anti-degradable channels. We can introduce the
{\rm ADG}
-squeezed channel
of
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
as follows.

Definition 4

For a quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
and the anti-degradable channel set
{\cal C}_{{\rm ADG}}
, if
\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})
is non-zero, the
{\rm ADG}
-squeezed channel of
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
is defined by

{\cal S}_{A\to B}=\frac{{\cal N}_{A\to B}-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}\cdot{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}}{1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}}

(22)

where
{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}
is the closest anti-degradable channel to
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
in terms of the max-relative entropy, i.e., the optimal solution in Eq. (
21
). If
\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})
is zero, the
{\rm ADG}
-squeezed channel of
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
is itself.

Notably,
\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})
can be efficiently computed via SDP shown in Appendix
A
. The conceptual idea we used here is similar to that for the state case in Eq. (
5
) and Definition
1
, which is to squeeze or isolate out as much part of anti-degradable channel as possible via a convex decomposition of the original channel. The insight here is that one can ignore the contribution from the anti-degradable part for the quantum capacity, and the quantum capacity admits convexity on the decomposition into degradable and anti-degradable parts. In this way, the following Theorem
7
gives an upper bound
Q_{\rm sqz}({\cal N})
for the quantum capacity of
{\cal N}
.

Theorem 7

Given a quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, if it has an ADG-squeezed channel
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
, we denote

\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}
as an extended channel of
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
such that
\operatorname{Tr}_{B^{\prime}}[\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}(\rho_{A})]={\cal S}_{A\to B}(\rho_{A}),\forall\rho_{A}\in{\cal D}({\cal H}_{A})
. Then it satisfies

\displaystyle Q({\cal N})\leq

\displaystyle Q_{\rm sqz}({\cal N}):=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]

(23)

\displaystyle\cdot\min\left\{Q^{(1)}(\widehat{{\cal S}})|\;\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}\mbox{\rm\; is degradable}\right\},

where the minimization is over all possible extended channels of
S_{A\to B}
. If there is no such a degradable
\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}
exists, the value of this bound is set to be infinity.

Proof.

By the definition of the
{\rm ADG}
-squeezed channel of
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, we have

{\cal N}_{A\to B}=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]\cdot{\cal S}_{A\to B}+2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}\cdot{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}

where
{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}
is anti-degradable. We write an extended channel of
{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}
as
\widehat{{\cal N}^{\prime}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}(\rho_{A})={\cal N}_{A\to B}(\rho_{A})\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{B^{\prime}}
, which is obviously anti-degradable. Then we can construct a quantum channel
\widehat{{\cal N}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}
as
\widehat{{\cal N}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]\cdot\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}+2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}\cdot\widehat{{\cal N}^{\prime}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}},
such that
\operatorname{Tr}_{B^{\prime}}[\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}(\rho_{A})]={\cal S}_{A\to B}(\rho_{A})
for any state
\rho_{A}
and
\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}
is degradable. This means after discarding the partial environment
B^{\prime}
, the receiver can obtain the original quantum information sent through
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
. In this case,
\widehat{{\cal N}}
can certainly convey more quantum information than the
{\cal N}
, i.e.,
Q({\cal N})\leq Q(\widehat{{\cal N}})
. Note that the quantum capacity admits convexity on the decomposition into degradable parts and anti-degradable parts
[
22
]
. We conclude that

\displaystyle Q({\cal N})\leq Q(\widehat{{\cal N}})

\displaystyle\leq[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]\cdot Q(\widehat{{\cal S}})

(24)

\displaystyle+2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}\cdot Q(\widehat{{\cal N}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]\cdot Q^{(1)}(\widehat{{\cal S}}),

where the equality is followed by the quantum capacity is additive on degradable channels and is zero for anti-degradable channels. Considering the freedom of the choice of
\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{\rm sqz}({\cal N})

\displaystyle:=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]

(25)

\displaystyle\cdot\min\left\{Q^{(1)}(\widehat{{\cal S}})|\;\widehat{{\cal S}}_{A\to BB^{\prime}}\mbox{\rm\; is degradable}\right\}

as an upper bound on
Q({\cal N})
.
∎

Remark 2

IV-A

Quantum capacity of qubit channels

For a quantum channel with dimension two in both input and output systems, we prove that the ADG-squeezed channel is always degradable. Thus, we give an efficiently computable upper bound on the quantum capacity using the idea of the reverse max-relative entropy of anti-degradability.

Proposition 8

For any qubit channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, it is either anti-degradable or satisfies

\displaystyle Q({\cal N})\leq

\displaystyle[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]

(26)

\displaystyle\cdot\max_{p\in[0,1]}I_{\mathrm{c}}\Big(p|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+(1-p)|1\rangle\!\langle 1|,{\cal S}_{A\to B}\Big),

where
I_{\mathrm{c}}(\rho,\mathcal{N})\equiv H(\mathcal{N}(\rho))-H\left(\mathcal{N}^{c}(\rho)\right)
and
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
is the ADG-squeezed channel of
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
.

Proof.

By the definition of the
{\rm ADG}
-squeezed channel of
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, we have

{\cal N}_{A\to B}=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]\cdot{\cal S}_{A\to B}+2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}\cdot{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}

where
{\cal N}^{\prime}_{A\to B}
is anti-degradable and
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
is not anti-degradable. If
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
is also not degradable, we can further decompose
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
into
{\cal S}=c{\cal S}_{0}+(1-c)S_{1}
such that
{\cal S}_{0}
is degradable and
{\cal S}_{1}
is anti-degradable since the extreme points of the set of all qubit channels have been shown to be degradable or anti-degradable channels
[
60
,
25
]
. This conflicts with the definition of
2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}
, which implies
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
is degradable. Thus,

\displaystyle Q({\cal N})

\displaystyle\leq[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]\cdot Q({\cal S})+2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}\cdot Q({\cal N}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]\cdot Q^{(1)}({\cal S})

\displaystyle=[1-2^{-\widetilde{{\cal R}}_{\max,{\rm ADG}}({\cal N})}]

\displaystyle\cdot\max_{p\in[0,1]}I_{\mathrm{c}}\Big(p|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+(1-p)|1\rangle\!\langle 1|,{\cal S}_{A\to B}\Big).

Note that the last equality is because
{\cal S}_{A\to B}
is degradable, and diagonal input states outperform non-diagonal states during the optimization of the channel coherent information
[
22
]
.

Concluding remarks

The design and implementation of a quantum internet
[

involve a unique set of challenges and require advancements in the realms of entanglement generation, manipulation, and distribution, as well as the development of robust quantum communication protocols. By addressing the challenges associated with entanglement distillation and reliable quantum communication, our work contributes to the advancement of the field of quantum internet.

We have introduced the reverse max-relative entropy of entanglement which is related to the ”weight of resource” in general resource theory. From a conceptual and technical side, the concept helps us to quantify how much useless entanglement we can squeeze out from a state or a channel, which is meaningful for the distillable entanglement and the quantum capacity, respectively. Importantly, these quantities can be efficiently computed via SDP, providing upper bounds on the distillable entanglement and quantum capacity that can be easily computed.

To further investigate entanglement distillation, we have derived various continuity bounds on the one-way distillable entanglement using the anti-degradability of the state. In particular, our bound derived from the reverse max-relative entropy of unextendible entanglement outperforms both continuity bounds and the Rains bound in estimating the one-way distillable entanglement for maximally entangled states under certain relevant noises. These bounds are also the only known computable ones for general states. We further introduced the reverse max-relative entropy of NPT entanglement and established connections to prior results on the two-way distillable entanglement. For the quantum capacity of noisy channels, our bounds based on the reverse max-relative entropy of anti-degradability deliver improved results for random mixed unitary qubit channels. Also, the analytical bound obtained from our method recovers the no-cloning bound on Pauli channels

These results open a novel way to connect valuable quantum resource measures with quantum communication tasks. Except for the existing applications of the reverse max-relative entropy of resources

The primal SDP for calculating

\displaystyle\min_{\omega_{AB},\tau_{AB},\tau_{ABE}}

\displaystyle\;\rho_{AB}=\omega_{AB}+\tau_{AB},

(A.1b)

\displaystyle\;\omega_{AB}\geq 0,\tau_{AB}\geq 0,\tau_{ABE}\geq 0,

(A.1c)

\displaystyle\;\operatorname{Tr}_{E}[\tau_{ABE}]=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[\tau_{ABE}]=\tau_{AB},

(A.1d)

\tau_{AB}
. The Lagrange function of the primal problem is

\displaystyle L(\omega_{AB},\tau_{AB},\tau_{ABE},M,N,K)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}[\omega_{AB}]+\langle M,\rho_{AB}-\omega_{AB}-\tau_{AB}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle N,\operatorname{Tr}_{E}[\tau_{ABE}]-\tau_{AB}\rangle+\langle K,\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[\tau_{ABE}]-\tau_{AB}\rangle

B
and
E

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[M_{AB}\rho_{AB}],

\displaystyle M_{AB}\leq I_{AB},

\displaystyle M_{AB}+N_{AB}+K_{AB}\leq 0,

\displaystyle N_{AB}\otimes I_{E}+P_{BE}(K_{AB}\otimes I_{E})P_{BE}^{\dagger}\geq 0.

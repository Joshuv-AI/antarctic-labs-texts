Calculation of magnon drag force induced by an electric current in ferromagnetic metals
Hiroshi Funaki, Gen Tatara

Abstract
Magnon drag effect induced by an applied electric field in ferromagnetic metals is theoretically studied by a microscopic calculation of the force on magnons arising from magnon emission/absorption and scattering due to driven electrons. It is shown that magnon scattering contribution dominates over the emission/absorption one in a wide temperature regime in good metals with long elastic lifetime τ \tau , as the latter has a relative suppression factor of ( Δ ​ τ ) − 2 (\Delta\tau)^{-2} due to the electron spin flip by the magnon, where Δ \Delta is the s ​ d sd exchange interaction energy. Spin-transfer efficiency is discussed including the magnon drag effect.



I
Introduction

Magnon or spin wave is an elementary excitation in magnetic systems, which are expected to be useful in spintronics for information transport and processing different from the conventional electronics
Chumak et al. 2015
.
An obvious advantage of using magnons is the fact that they exist even in insulators, where conduction electrons are absent.
Spin-transfer effect due to magnon current was theoretically argued in the context of domain wall motion in Ref.
Yan et al. 2011
.
Magnons are, however, not easy to control and to detect, as it does not have direct coupling to an electric field.
Thermal driving by applying a temperature gradient is therefore a common method for inducing magnon flow like in the spin Seebeck effect
Uchida et al. 2010
.
Unlike conduction electrons, magnon effects are generally temperature-dependent owing to a Bose distribution function for magnon excitation, but the effects are not easy to separate from other bosonic origins such as phonons with similar character.
Responses to an external magnetic field were used to identify the magnon contribution
Grannemann and Berger 1976
.
Magnon drag effect was experimentally identified by use of a thermopile structure to cancel nonmagnetic origins in Ref.
Costache et al. 2012
.

In ferromagnetic metals, conduction electron spin is polarized due to strong
sd
exchange interaction to the magnetization, as suggested by large magnetoresistance in magnetic multilayers and high efficiency of the spin-transfer effect for domain walls.
Strong
sd
exchange interaction indicates that the system is a strongly correlated fluid of magnon and electron with spin up and down, where a significant magnon drag effect by the electron flow and vice versa are expected.
In 1976, magnon-drag Peltier effect, i.e., the energy current due to magnon flow when an electric field is applied, was phenomenologically argued and magnon drift velocity was found from experimental data to be

v_{\rm m}=\mu_{\rm m/e}v_{\rm e}
with a constant
\mu_{\rm m/e}=2-3
, where
v_{\rm e}
is the electron drift velocity
Grannemann and Berger 1976
.
It is striking that the magnons are driven so efficiently by an electric field even without a direct coupling.
Magnon-drag effect was studied by use of a microscopic diagrammatic method in Ref.
Miura and Sakuma 2012
.
The magnon velocity was calculated taking account of the magnon emission and absorption due to the sd exchange interaction and an applied electric field, and an expression
\mu_{\rm m/e}\propto P\eta/(\alpha_{\rm G}\Delta)
was obtained for the velocity ratio, where
P
denotes the spin polarization of conduction electron,
\eta
is the inverse electron elastic lifetime,
\Delta
is the electron spin polarization energy and
\alpha_{\rm G}
is the (Gilbert) damping constant for magnon.
The electric current induced by the magnon current driven by a temperature gradient was calculated and argued in the context of magnonic spin-motive force in Ref.
Yamaguchi et al. 2019
.

The mechanism considered in Refs.
Grannemann and Berger 1976
;
Miura and Sakuma 2012
, the magnon emission and absorption, is the following process.
When the conduction electron has a drift velocity opposite to the applied electric field, a forward emission of a magnon and an absorption of a backward propagating magnon occurs
(Fig.
1
). The transferred momentum from or to the magnon contributes to a drag force.
Magnon carries a spin of
-1
, and thus an absorption and emission flips the electron spin.
The absorption/emission event therefore results in a high energy excitation of the order of
2\Delta
for the electron at the Fermi energy, unless the magnon wave vector matches the difference of the Fermi wave vectors,
k_{F+}-k_{F-}
for spin
\pm
.
The electron amplitude for the force has therefore a suppression factor of
(\Delta\tau)^{-n}
(
n\geq 1
), where
n
turns out to be 1 (Eq. (
26
)),
\tau
being the electron elastic lifetime.
Being a single magnon process, the magnon amplitude for the emission/absorption turns out to depend on the temperature
T
as
T^{\frac{5}{2}}
.
Besides the emission and absorption, electron drift causes a magnon scattering.
The scattering conserves the electron spin, and the electron stays near the Fermi energy, and thus there is no electron suppression factor for the scattering process.
The scattering is a two magnon process, whose amplitude turns out to be
\propto T^{4}
and is weaker at low temperatures.
The relative strength of the emission/absorption and scattering contribution to the force is given by

\Gamma_{{\rm ea}/{\rm sc}}\propto S^{-1}(\Delta\tau)^{-2}({k_{B}}T/\tilde{J})^{-\frac{3}{2}}

(Eq. (
42
)), where
\tilde{J}
is the ferromagnetic exchange energy,
S
is the magnitude of localized spin and
{k_{B}}
is the Boltzmann constant. The factor of
S
is due to the fact that emission/absorption and absorption processes corresponds to the first and the second order of the
1/S
-expansion for the magnons.
The emission/absorption process therefore dominates for

{k_{B}}T\lesssim S^{-\frac{2}{3}}(\Delta\tau)^{-\frac{4}{3}}\tilde{J}
, which correspond to a very low temperature for good metals with a long elastic lifetime.

Figure 1:
Diagrammatic representation of the magnon emission, absorption and scattering.
Solid line is the electron with spin labeled by

\sigma=\pm
and wavy line is the magnon.

Besides the Peltier effect, magnon drag effect contributes to other transport effects.
In fact, magnon velocity contributes to a temperature-dependent spin-transfer effect, and magnon drag force is directly detected as a contribution to electric conductivity.
Close analysis of experimental data on various transport effects would make possible the separation of the magnon drag effect from other effects.
The aim of the paper is to provide a comprehensive theory of magnon drag effects on spin-transfer effect and resistivity by calculating the drag force microscopically using the approach of Ref.
Funaki and Tatara 2021
;
Funaki et al. 2021
taking account of the magnon scattering effect.
It turns out that the magnon scattering effect for the magnon drag force and spin-transfer effect is larger than the emission/absorption effect in the wide temperature range as we argued above.

Our result for the magnon emission/absorption contribution is
\mu_{\rm m/e}\propto\eta/(\alpha_{\rm G}\Delta)
(the first term of Eq. (
53
)), and is different from the result of Ref.
Miura and Sakuma 2012
, which is proportional to
P
.
Comparing the analyses of Ref.
Miura and Sakuma 2012
and ours, the difference appears to arose from perhaps an insufficient treatment in Ref.
Miura and Sakuma 2012
of electron (lesser) and hole (greater) contributions of the vertex function
\Lambda
(Eq. (
98
)).
Physically, the absence of
P
in the magnon velocity appears natural as driven electrons with both spin contribute to the forward motion of magnons. In fact, up spin electron, which can only absorb a magnon, transfers positive momentum to magnons by absorbing magnons moving backward relative to the electron, while down spin electron pushes magnons forward by emitting magnons forward.
The transferred momentum and force in both cases are positive and would not change sign by a reversal of spin polarization of the electrons.

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\pi S^{2}m}\sum_{{\bm{p}}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}(p-p^{\prime})_{i}k_{j}{\rm tr}\biggl[{\rm Im}(\Pi^{(2),k+p-p^{\prime},p,p^{\prime},{\rm a}}_{k,\sigma,\omega=0})|g_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}^{\rm a}|^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\pi S^{2}m}\sum_{{\bm{p}}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}(p-p^{\prime})_{i}k_{j}{\rm Im}[d_{p\nu}^{\rm a}]{\rm Im}[d_{p^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{\rm a}]n_{\nu}(1+n_{\nu^{\prime}})f_{\nu^{\prime}-\nu}{\rm Im}[g_{k+p-p^{\prime}}^{\rm a}]|g_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}^{\rm a}|^{2}

The scattering force has other contributions of higher-order in the

\displaystyle\Pi_{k+p-p^{\prime}}^{(2+),(k,p,p^{\prime})}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\Delta}{2}\int_{C}dt_{1}\Pi_{k+p}^{(+),(k,p)}(t,t_{1})g_{k+p,-}(t_{1},t^{\prime})d_{p^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle\Pi_{k+p-p^{\prime}}^{(2-),(k,p,p^{\prime})}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\Delta}{2}\int_{C}dt_{1}\Pi_{k-p^{\prime}}^{(-),(k,p^{\prime})}(t,t_{1})g_{k-p^{\prime},+}(t_{1},t^{\prime})d_{p}(t,t^{\prime})

where factors are included to have the same normalization as
\Pi^{(2)}

The electron spin (
\pm

Considering a strong

2\Delta

The electron propagators with flipped spin inside the composite propagator of Eq. (

\displaystyle\Pi_{k+p-p^{\prime}}^{(2+),(k,p,p^{\prime})}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Pi_{k+p-p^{\prime}}^{(2-),(k,p,p^{\prime})}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{i}{4}\Pi_{k-p^{\prime},-}^{(-),(k,p^{\prime})}(t,t^{\prime})d_{p}(t,t^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{4}\Pi^{(2),k,p,p^{\prime}}_{k+p-p^{\prime},-}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{(2{\rm b})}_{ij}

\displaystyle=i\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\pi S^{2}m}\sum_{{\bm{p}}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}(p-p^{\prime})_{i}k_{j}\frac{\sigma}{4}{\rm tr}\biggl[{\rm Im}(\Pi^{(2),k+p-p^{\prime},p,p^{\prime},{\rm a}}_{k,\sigma,\omega=0})|g_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}^{\rm a}|^{2}\biggr]

Using Eq. (

and low frequency magnons, we obtain the sum of the two contributions as

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{(2)}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv{\cal F}^{(2{\rm a})}_{ij}+{\cal F}^{(2{\rm b})}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\overline{{\cal F}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal F}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{3S^{2}m^{2}}\sum_{{\bm{p}}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}({\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime})^{2}n_{\omega_{p}}(1+n_{\omega_{p^{\prime}}})f_{\omega_{p^{\prime}}-\omega_{p}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}k^{2}\left(1+\frac{\sigma}{4}\right)|g_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}^{\rm a}|^{2}{\rm Im}[(g_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}^{\rm a})^{2}]

The summation over

and
\sigma

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{3S^{2}ma^{2}}{(\Delta\tau)}^{2}\overline{{\nu_{\rm e}}}\left(1+\frac{P_{\nu}}{4}\right)W^{(2)}(T)

\displaystyle W^{(2)}(T)\equiv

\displaystyle a^{2}\sum_{{\bm{p}}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}({\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime})^{2}n_{\omega_{p}}(1+n_{\omega_{p^{\prime}}})f_{\omega_{p^{\prime}}-\omega_{p}}

III.3
Force on electron

The recoil force on the electron with spin
\sigma

arising from the magnon emission/absorption due to the
sd
exchange interaction is (in the field operator form)

\displaystyle{F}^{(1)}_{{\rm e}+,i}({\bm{q}}=0)

\displaystyle=i\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{2S}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{p}}}p_{i}b_{{\bm{p}}}(c_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{-}c_{{\bm{k}}})_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}={\bm{k}}+{\bm{p}}}

\displaystyle{F}^{(1)}_{{\rm e}-,i}({\bm{q}}=0)

\displaystyle=i\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{2S}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{p}}}p_{i}b_{-{\bm{p}}}^{\dagger}(c_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{+}c_{{\bm{k}}})_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}={\bm{k}}+{\bm{p}}}

(38)

whose sum is opposite to the force on magnons;
\sum_{\sigma}{F}^{(1)}_{{\rm e}\sigma,i}=-{F}^{(1)}_{{\rm m},i}
.
It turns out that the force is spin-independent, i.e.,

\displaystyle{F}^{(1)}_{{\rm e}+{i}}={F}^{(1)}_{{\rm e}-{i}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}{F}^{(1)}_{i}

(39)

This result indicates that magnon emission and absorption induced by an electric field does not acts as spin motive force but drives only charge sector.

The force on electron spin arising from magnon scattering is calculated using

\displaystyle{F}^{(2)}_{{\rm e}\sigma,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta}{S}[\nabla(b^{\dagger}b)]\sigma c^{\dagger}_{\sigma}c_{\sigma}

(40)

and its coefficient (
{F}^{(2)}_{{\rm e}\sigma}\equiv eE{\cal F}^{(2)}_{{\rm e}\sigma}
)
is (from Eq. (
34
)),

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{(2)}_{{\rm e}\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\Delta}^{2}}{6S^{2}m^{2}}\sum_{{\bm{p}}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}({\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime})^{2}n_{\omega_{p}}(1+n_{\omega_{p^{\prime}}})f_{\omega_{p^{\prime}}-\omega_{p}}\left(1+\frac{\sigma}{4}\right)\sum_{{\bm{k}}}k^{2}|g_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}^{\rm a}|^{2}{\rm Im}[(g_{{\bm{k}}\sigma}^{\rm a})^{2}]

(41)

This force is generally spin-dependent.

Those forces on the electron are different from the driven-magnon contribution to the motive force due to smooth magnetization structures discussed in Refs.
Lucassen et al. 2011
;
Yamaguchi et al. 2019
.
In fact, the motive force in the adiabatic (slowly varying) limit are proportional to the magnon energy current linear in the magnon momentum, while the force argued in the present analysis are the second order of
p
and
p^{\prime}
, corresponding to nonadiabatic contributions.

From Eqs. (

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\rm m}=

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}W^{(1)}(T)+\gamma_{2}(\Delta\tau)^{2}W^{(2)}(T)

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{3}{8S}\gamma_{f}\overline{n_{\rm e}}a^{3}

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\pi}{3S^{2}}\gamma_{f}{\epsilon_{F}}\overline{{\nu_{\rm e}}}(1+\tfrac{P_{\nu}}{4})

\displaystyle\gamma_{f}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{ma^{2}{\epsilon_{F}}}

(43)

where the magnon weight factors are

\displaystyle W^{(1)}(T)=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{5}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dpp^{4}\frac{1}{e^{2\beta\omega_{p}}-1}

\displaystyle W^{(2)}(T)=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{8}}{(2\pi^{2})^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime}p^{2}(p^{\prime})^{2}(p^{2}+(p^{\prime})^{2})\frac{1}{e^{\beta\omega_{p}}-1}\frac{1}{1-e^{-\beta\omega_{p^{\prime}}}}\frac{1}{e^{\beta(\omega_{p^{\prime}}-\omega_{p})}+1}

Considering strong spin polarization in 3

(\Delta\tau)^{2}

Considering temperatures higher than the magnon gap (
{k_{B}}T\gg KS
)
the weight factors in three dimensions are
(
x\equiv\beta\omega_{p}
)

\displaystyle W^{(1)}(T)=

\displaystyle I_{f}^{(1)}\left(\frac{{k_{B}}T}{\tilde{J}}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}

\displaystyle W^{(2)}(T)=

\displaystyle I_{f}^{(2)}\left(\frac{{k_{B}}T}{\tilde{J}}\right)^{4}

where

and

\displaystyle I_{f}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{e^{2x}-1}=7.985\times 10^{-3}

\displaystyle I_{f}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{(4\pi^{2})^{2}}\int\int_{0}^{\infty}dxdx^{\prime}\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}(x^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}(x+x^{\prime})}{(e^{x}-1)(1-e^{-x^{\prime}})(e^{x^{\prime}-x}+1)}=1.069\times 10^{-2}

(46)

The total force coefficient

The temperature is normalized by

The temperature regime in Fig.

This is confirmed from the plot of the magnon number per cite (Fig.

\displaystyle I_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{\sqrt{x}}{e^{x}-1}=0.05864

IV.1
Magnon velocity

When a force
{F}_{\rm m}
acts on a magnon, the magnon is driven at a velocity

{F}_{\rm m}\frac{\tau_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm m}}
, where
\tau_{\rm m}
and
m_{\rm m}
are the magnon lifetime and mass. The lifetime is written in terms of the Gilbert damping parameter
\alpha_{\rm G}
and the frequency of the magnon
\omega
as

1/\tau_{\rm m}=\alpha_{\rm G}\omega
and magnon mass in the present case is

m_{\rm m}=(2JS)^{-1}
.
The correctness of the above argument is supported by a direct linear response calculation of the magnon velocity (Appendix
C
).
From our results of the forces, Eq. (
42
), the magnon current induced by each force reads
\bm{j}_{\rm m}^{(1)}=\sigma_{\rm m}^{(1)}e{\bm{E}}
and
\bm{j}_{\rm m}^{(2)}=\sigma_{\rm m}^{(2)}e{\bm{E}}
, where

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm m}^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\alpha_{\rm G}a}\left(\frac{{k_{B}}T}{\tilde{J}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}I_{v}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm m}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\gamma_{2}}{\alpha_{\rm G}a}(\Delta\tau)^{2}\left(\frac{{k_{B}}T}{\tilde{J}}\right)^{3}I_{v}^{(2)}

(51)

correspond to conductivity for magnons (without the factor of
e^{2}
),
with magnon integrals

\displaystyle I_{v}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{e^{2x}-1}=2.073\times 10^{-2}

\displaystyle I_{v}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{(4\pi^{2})^{2}}\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}dx\int_{0}^{\infty}dx^{\prime}\frac{x^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}(x+x^{\prime})}{(e^{x}-1)(1-e^{-x^{\prime}})(e^{x^{\prime}-x}+1)}

(52)

.
The expressions for
I_{v}^{(2)}
diverges at low energy due to insufficient phenomenological treatment of magnon lifetime in terms of the Gilbert damping in the low frequency limit.
Here we avoid the problem by introducing a low energy cutoff
\omega_{0}
(
x_{0}=\omega_{0}^{2}/({k_{B}}T)
).

I_{v}^{(2)}
is of the order of 0.01 for
x_{0}\gtrsim 0.02
and logarithmically diverges as
x_{0}\rightarrow 0
.
The electric conductivity in the same approximation
(
1/(2ma^{2})\sim{\epsilon_{F}}
,
{k_{F}}a\sim 1
,
\overline{{\nu_{\rm e}}}\sim 1/{\epsilon_{F}}
) is

\sigma_{\rm e}/e^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{a}{\epsilon_{F}}\tau
.
The ratio of the magnon conductivity to the electron one thus is

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm m/e}=\frac{\sigma_{\rm m}}{\sigma_{\rm e}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2}{\alpha_{\rm G}{\epsilon_{F}}\tau}\left[\gamma_{1}I_{v}^{(1)}\tilde{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\gamma_{2}I_{v}^{(2)}({\Delta\tau})^{2}\tilde{T}^{{3}}\right]

(53)

where
\tilde{T}\equiv\frac{{k_{B}}T}{\tilde{J}}
.
As seen from Fig.
7
, in good metals with large
\Delta\tau
, magnon conductivity is larger than the electric conductivity even for a low temperature (e.g.,
\tilde{T}\gtrsim 0.4
for
{\epsilon_{F}}\tau=20
).
The result is qualitatively consistent with seminal work of Grannemann and Berger
Grannemann and Berger 1976
, where it was argued that average drift velocity of magnon is 2-3 times larger than that of electron in Ni
66
Cu
34
and Ni
69
Fe
31
.
Our result for
\sigma_{\rm m}^{(1)}
is consistent with previous analysis
Miura and Sakuma 2012
, where energy current driven by an electric field was microscopically calculated taking account of the magnon emission/absorption (
f^{(1)}
) .
Our result, however, indicates that more efficient magnon-drag effect occurs due to the magnon scattering (
f^{(2)}
) in strongly spin-polarized good metals.
The reason is that the scattering contribution is a response of the electron charge sector (spin summed), while the emission/absorption is a spin response containing electron propagators with opposite spins, resulting in a relative suppression factor of

(\Delta\tau)^{-2}
.
As the electron elastic lifetime is long,
{\epsilon_{F}}\tau\gg 1
, except for extremely dirty metals, the enhancement factor of
(\Delta\tau)^{2}
for scattering contribution makes the contribution larger even for temperatures of

{k_{B}}T\gtrsim(\Delta\tau)^{-\frac{4}{3}}\tilde{J}

(See Sec.
IV.3
).

The expression for the emission/absorption,
\sigma_{\rm m}^{(1)}
, of Eq. (
51
) is consistent with previous analysis indicating

\sigma_{\rm m}^{(1)}\propto\frac{1}{\alpha_{\rm G}\tau}T^{\frac{3}{2}}

Grannemann and Berger 1976
;
Miura and Sakuma 2012
, although
the result of Ref.
Miura and Sakuma 2012
is proportional to electron spin polarization,

\sigma_{\rm m}^{(1)}\propto\frac{P}{\alpha_{\rm G}\tau}T^{\frac{3}{2}}
, probably due to an insufficient treatment of magnon and hole contributions in Ref.
Miura and Sakuma 2012
.

The result for

\sigma_{\rm m}^{(2)}
suggests that the magnon damping effect for low energy magnons are critical for estimation of the magnon conductivity.
Further theoretical and experimental investigations are expected in this direction.

IV.2
Magnon spin-transfer effect

The magnon current can be estimated by observing magnetization dynamics induced by the magnon spin-transfer effect.
In the case of electron spin-transfer effect, the flow of magnetization structures is at the velocity of

\displaystyle v_{\rm st,e}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{3}P}{2eS}j

(54)

in the direction of spin current
Pj
(
P=\frac{n_{\uparrow}-n_{\downarrow}}{n_{\uparrow}+n_{\downarrow}}
)
in the adiabatic limit
Tatara et al. 2008
.
The current is written in terms of the current in the low temperature limit (i.e., without magnons)
j^{(0)}
as

j=j^{(0)}(1-{\cal F}_{\rm m})
, where
{\cal F}_{\rm m}
represents the resistivity effect due to magnons.
Assuming the adiabatic limit for magnons, the magnon spin transfer effect drives magnetization structures at the velocity of

\displaystyle v_{\rm st,m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{a^{3}}{S}j_{\rm m}

(55)

in the opposite direction to the magnon current.
Considering the two mechanisms for magnon current, the velocity of the magnetization structure for an applied electric current
\bm{j}
is

\displaystyle v_{\rm st}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{3}}{2eS}P_{\rm eff}j^{(0)}

(56)

where

\displaystyle P_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\equiv P\left[1-{\cal F}_{\rm m}\right]-\frac{4}{\alpha_{\rm G}{\epsilon_{F}}\tau}\left[\gamma_{1}I_{v}^{(1)}\tilde{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\gamma_{2}I_{v}^{(2)}({\Delta\tau})^{2}\tilde{T}^{3}\right]

(57)

represents the effective spin-transfer efficiency including the magnon effects, plotted in Fig.
8
.
In the weak damping regime
\alpha_{\rm G}\lesssim({\epsilon_{F}}\tau)^{-1}
, the contribution of
{\cal F}_{\rm m}
(magnon resistivity) is negligible compared to the conductivity correction (the last square bracket) in the temperature regime in Fig.
8
.
Crossover from electron-dominated to the magnon-dominated regime occurs at

{k_{B}}T/\tilde{J}\lesssim 0.2
in the present case, with a significant negative enhancement in the high temperature regime.
The magnon spin-transfer effect is correlated with the behavior of magnon contribution to the resistance, that is proportional to the force plotted in Fig.
6
. Identification of magnon drag effects would be carried out by careful analyses of temperature dependence of the experimental data.

IV.3
Crossover temperatures

Let us look into the crossover temperatures based on the results Eqs. (
42
) (
57
).
We consider the case
\gamma_{1}\sim\gamma_{2}\sim 1
,
\Delta/{\epsilon_{F}}\sim 1
and neglect the contribution from

{\cal F}_{\rm m}
in
P_{\rm eff}
for simplicity.
As for the magnons, emission/absorption effect is dominant at low temperature, and the crossover to the scattering-dominated temperature is read from Eq. (
42
) as

\displaystyle T_{{\rm ea-sc}}\simeq(\Delta\tau)^{-\frac{4}{3}}\tilde{J}/{k_{B}}

(58)

The crossover would be seen in the magnon resistivity, assuming that drag force is not directly observable.

The spin-transfer efficiency,
P_{\rm eff}
, at zero temperature reduces to the electron origin,
P
.
The magnon emission/absorption contribution becomes larger than the electron contribution
above

\displaystyle T_{{\rm e-ea}}\simeq(12\times\alpha_{\rm G})^{\frac{2}{3}}(\Delta\tau)^{\frac{2}{3}}\tilde{J}/{k_{B}}

(59)

which corresponds to high temperature
T_{{\rm e-ea}}\gtrsim\tilde{J}/{k_{B}}
unless in an extremely low damping materials with
\alpha_{\rm G}\ll 0.08\times(\Delta\tau)^{-1}
.
In contrast, magnon scattering effect overcomes the electron contribution at lower temperature of

\displaystyle T_{{\rm e-sc}}\simeq(25\times\alpha_{\rm G})^{\frac{1}{3}}(\Delta\tau)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\tilde{J}/{k_{B}}

(60)

for
I_{v}^{(2)}=0.01
, meaning that the magnon emission/absorption regime emerges only in very dirty metals with
\Delta\tau\sim O(1)
(Fig.
8
).

V
Summary

We have calculated the force between the magnon and conduction electron when an electric field is applied to a ferromagnetic metal based on a microscopic approach.
The force due to magnon emission/absorption and scattering were considered and the latter turned out to dominate in a wide temperature regime in good metals with long elastic mean free path.
The magnon contribution to the resistivity and total spin-transfer efficiency were discussed.

d_{p\nu}^{\rm K}=-(1+2n_{\nu})(d_{p\nu}^{{\rm a}}-d_{p\nu}^{{\rm r}})
,

\displaystyle d_{p\nu}^{\rm r}d_{p\nu}^{\rm K}[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega,+}]^{\rm r}

\displaystyle+d_{p\nu}^{\rm K}d_{p\nu}^{{\rm a}}[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega,+}]^{\rm a}

\displaystyle=-(1+2n_{\nu})[d_{p\nu}^{\rm r}(d_{p\nu}^{{\rm a}}-d_{p\nu}^{{\rm r}})[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega,+}]^{\rm r}+(d_{p\nu}^{{\rm a}}-d_{p\nu}^{{\rm r}})d_{p\nu}^{{\rm a}}[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega,+}]^{\rm a}]

\displaystyle\simeq(1+2n_{\nu})d_{p\nu}^{\rm r}d_{p\nu}^{{\rm a}}\left[[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega,+}]^{\rm a}-[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega,+}]^{\rm r}\right]

(96)

where contributions
(d_{p\nu}^{\rm r})^{2}
and
(d_{p\nu}^{\rm a})^{2}
are neglected as they are smaller compared to
d_{p\nu}^{\rm r}d_{p\nu}^{{\rm a}}
after
\nu
summation.
The magnon current in this approximation reads

\displaystyle j_{{\rm m},i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2m}J\Delta^{2}\sum_{p\nu}\sum_{k\omega}\sum_{\Omega}p_{i}k_{j}A_{j}(\Omega)d_{p\nu}^{\rm r}d_{p\nu}^{\rm a}\left[[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k+,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{\rm K}+(1+2n_{\nu})\left[[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k+,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{{\rm a}}-[\Lambda^{k+p,\omega+\nu,-}_{k+,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{{\rm r}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+[\Lambda^{k-p,\omega-\nu,+}_{k-,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{\rm K}-(1+2n_{\nu})\left[[\Lambda^{k-p,\omega-\nu,+}_{k-,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{{\rm a}}-[\Lambda^{k-p,\omega-\nu,+}_{k-,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{{\rm r}}\right]\right]

(97)

The Keldysh, advanced and retarded components of
\Lambda
are (in the suppressed notation)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\rm K}

\displaystyle=g^{<}(gg)^{>}+g^{>}(gg)^{<}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\rm a}

\displaystyle=g^{{\rm a}}(gg)^{<}+g^{<}(gg)^{\rm r}=g^{{\rm a}}(gg)^{>}+g^{>}(gg)^{\rm r}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\rm r}

\displaystyle=g^{{\rm r}}(gg)^{<}+g^{<}(gg)^{\rm a}=g^{{\rm r}}(gg)^{>}+g^{>}(gg)^{\rm a}

(98)

Focusing on the Fermi surface (excitation) contribution, we neglect terms containing only the retarded or the advanced Green’s functions, to obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\rm K}

\displaystyle\simeq(f_{\omega}-f_{\omega^{\prime}})(2f_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}-1)(g^{\rm a}-g^{\rm r})g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}

\displaystyle-[f_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}(f_{\omega^{\prime}}-1)+f_{\omega^{\prime}}(f_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}-1)]g^{\rm r}(gg)^{\rm a}-[f_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}(f_{\omega}-1)+f_{\omega}(f_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}-1)]g^{\rm a}(gg)^{\rm r}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{{\rm a}}

\displaystyle\simeq f_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}g^{\rm a}(gg)^{\rm r}+g^{\rm a}\left[(f_{\omega}-f_{\omega^{\prime}})g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}-f_{\omega}(gg)^{\rm r}\right]

\displaystyle\Lambda^{{\rm r}}

\displaystyle\simeq-f_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}g^{\rm r}(gg)^{\rm a}+g^{\rm r}\left[(f_{\omega}-f_{\omega^{\prime}})g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}+f_{\omega^{\prime}}(gg)^{\rm a}\right]

(99)

where
\Lambda
of Eq. (
94
) is simply denoted by
g_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}g_{\omega^{\prime}}g_{\omega}
(with
\omega^{\prime\prime}=\omega\pm\nu
,
\omega^{\prime}-\omega+\Omega
).
Using the Fermi surface approximation for the two Greens function,
(gg)^{<}\simeq(f_{\omega}-f_{\omega^{\prime}})g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\rm K}

\displaystyle\simeq(2f_{\pm\nu}-1)(\Lambda^{{\rm a}}-\Lambda^{{\rm r}})

\displaystyle\Lambda^{{\rm a}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\Omega f^{\prime}(\omega)g^{\rm a}g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{{\rm r}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\Omega f^{\prime}(\omega)g^{\rm r}g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}

(100)

Considering low temperature,
f^{\prime}(\omega)\simeq-\delta(\omega)
, we obtain (using
2f_{-\nu}-1=1-2f_{\nu}
)

\displaystyle j_{{\rm m},i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{e}{2\pi m}J\Delta^{2}\sum_{p\nu}\sum_{k\omega}\sum_{\Omega}p_{i}k_{j}\Omega A_{j}(\Omega)|d_{p\nu}^{\rm a}|^{2}\sum_{\pm}(\pm)(f_{\nu}+n_{\nu})\left[[\Lambda^{k\pm p,\pm\nu,\mp}_{k,\pm,0,0}]^{{\rm a}}-[\Lambda^{k\pm p,\pm\nu,\mp}_{k,\pm,0,0}]^{{\rm r}}\right]

(101)

Using
f_{\nu}+n_{\nu}=2n_{\nu}(1-f_{\nu})
, and
i\Omega A=E
, we obtain the final result of

\displaystyle j_{{\rm m},i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{2e}{\pi m}{J\Delta^{2}}{\tau_{\rm m}}\sum_{p\nu}\sum_{k\omega}\sum_{\Omega}p_{i}k_{j}E_{j}{\rm Im}[d_{p\nu}^{\rm a}]\sum_{\pm}(\pm)n_{\nu}(1-f_{\nu}){\rm Im}[g_{k\pm p,\pm\nu,\mp}^{\rm a}]|g_{k,\pm}^{{\rm a}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sigma_{{\rm m},i,j}^{(1)}E_{j}

(102)

We therefore confirm the correct relation between the force and velocity of magnon,

\sigma_{{\rm m},ij}^{(1)}={\cal F}_{ij}^{(1)}\frac{\tau_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm m}}
, where
m_{\rm m}=(2JS)^{-1}
is the effective magnon mass.

\displaystyle\times{\rm tr}[\sigma_{z}\Pi^{(+)}({{\bm{r}}_{1},t_{1}},{\bm{r}}_{2},t_{2})\sigma_{z}g({\bm{r}}_{2},t_{2},{\bm{r}}_{3},t_{3})\hat{p}_{j}g({\bm{r}}_{3},t_{3},{\bm{r}}_{1},t_{1})

where

) (suffixes are suppressed in Eq. (

As the expression of Eq. (
103
) has the same structure as Eq. (

))

\displaystyle j_{{\rm m},i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2mS}J\Delta^{2}\sum_{p\nu}\sum_{k\omega\Omega}p_{i}k_{j}A_{j}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\times\left[\left\{d_{p\nu}^{\rm r}d_{p\nu}^{\rm a}[\Lambda^{(2+),k+p,\omega+\nu}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{\rm K}+(rKr)+(Kaa)\right\}+\left\{d_{p\nu}^{\rm r}d_{p\nu}^{\rm a}[\Lambda^{(2-),k-p,\omega-\nu}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega}]^{\rm K}+(rKa)+(Kar)\right\}\right]

(104)

where ((
{\bm{p}}^{\prime}
is the wave vector of the magnon in the pair propagators)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2+),k+p,\omega+\nu}_{k,\omega+\Omega,\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\Pi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{p}},\omega+\nu}^{(+),({\bm{k}}+{\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\sigma,{\bm{p}}^{\prime})}g_{{\bm{k}},\omega+\Omega,\sigma}g_{{\bm{k}},\omega,\sigma}

(105)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2){\rm K}}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2){\rm a}}

\displaystyle\simeq(f_{\omega}-f_{\omega^{\prime}})\Pi^{\rm a}g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}\simeq-\Omega f^{\prime}(\omega)\Pi^{\rm a}g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}

\displaystyle\simeq-\Omega f^{\prime}(\omega)\Pi^{\rm r}g^{\rm r}g^{\rm a}

Here the magnon-electron pair propagator has an external frequency of

\displaystyle j_{{\rm m},i}^{(2)}

confirming the relation between the corresponding force density and current density,

j_{{\rm m},i}^{(2)}={\cal F}^{(2)}\frac{\tau_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm m}}

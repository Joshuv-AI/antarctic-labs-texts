The summation of infinite partial fraction decomposition I: some formulae related to the Hurwitz zeta function
Xiaowei Wang

Abstract
In this paper we establish a new summation method by expanding ∏ k ( 1 − z a k ) − 1 \prod_{k}(1-\frac{z}{a_{k}})^{-1} with two approaches: the Taylor expansion and the infinite partial fraction decomposition. Here we focus on the case when a k a_{k} is arithmetic sequence. By this summation we obtain many equalities involve Hurwitz zeta function and Gammma function.

1
Introduction

Let
S
be the series of rational functions
S=\sum\frac{P(n)}{Q(n)}
with
\deg(Q)\geq\deg(P)+2
. By decomposing
\frac{P}{Q}
into partial fractions,
S
can be rewritten as finite linear combination of Hurwitz zeta function
\zeta(m,a)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(a+k)^{m}}
with positive interger
m
. In this paper, we construct another series representation of
\zeta(m,a)
by discuss two kinds of expansions of the infinite product
\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-(\frac{z}{a+k})^{m})
.

In fact, the correspondence is natural. In general, there is some relation between
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{k}^{m}}
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(a_{k})}\frac{1}{a_{k}^{m+1}}
, where
F(z)
is the product
\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-(\frac{z}{a_{k}})^{m})
. Basically, the relation is derived by the infinite partial fraction decomposition of
\frac{1}{F(z)}
. And the correspondence is not only valid for infinite series, but also valid for finite sum. Therefore the convergence can be discussed easily. In this we paper we study this relation and focus on the case when
a_{k}=a+k
, namely, the problem involves the Hurwitz zeta function.

Throughout the paper we denote the coefficient of
z^{n}
in the Laurent expansion of
f(z)
around
0
by
[f(z)]_{n}
. The notation
F^{\prime}(a_{n})
refers to
\lim_{z\rightarrow a_{n}}F^{\prime}(z)
.

Prelimilary

Lemma 1
.

(Homogeneous partial fraction decomposition)

Let
a_{1},...,a_{n}
be distinct complex numbers,
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{a_{1},...,a_{n}\}
, then there exist
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
such that following identity is true,

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x-a_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{i}}{x-a_{i}}

(1)

where
\mu_{i}=\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i}-a_{j}}
. Further, The relation
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}=0
holds.

Proof.

The proof is easy, one can consider the Lagrange’s interpolation formula. For more details, See author’s paper
[
1
]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

(One point lifting)

Suppose that
N,L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
a_{n}
are complex numbers that differ from each other, then the following identity holds

\frac{1}{x^{L}}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x-a_{n}}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{-\mu_{n}}{a_{n}^{L-j+1}}\frac{1}{x^{j}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\mu_{n}}{a_{n}^{L}}\frac{1}{x-a_{n}}

where

\mu_{n}=\prod_{s=1,s\neq n}^{N}\frac{1}{a_{n}-a_{s}}

Proof.

Firstly one can see the case
L=0
is exactly Lemma
1
, hence the the identity is true for
\ell=0
. Following we assume that
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Suppose that
z_{0},z_{1},...,z_{N}
be complex variables that take distinct values. Let
H(z_{0},z_{1},...,z_{n})=\prod_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{x-z_{n}}
. Then by Lemma 1 we have the identity

H(z_{0},z_{1},...,z_{n})=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x-z_{n}}

Where
\lambda_{n}=\prod_{s=0,s\neq n}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{n}-z_{s}}
. Now we taking partial derivatives of
H
with respect to
z_{0}
. There are two ways to carry out. On the one hand,

\frac{\partial^{l}H}{\partial z_{0}^{l}}=\frac{l!}{(x-z_{0})^{l+1}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x-z_{n}}

(2)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{l}H}{\partial z_{0}^{l}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{l}}{\partial z_{0}^{l}}(\lambda_{0}\frac{1}{x-z_{0}})+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x-z_{n}}\frac{\partial^{l}\lambda_{n}}{\partial z_{0}^{l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\binom{l}{j}\frac{\partial^{l-j}\lambda_{0}}{\partial z_{0}^{l-j}}\frac{j!}{(x-z_{0})^{j+1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x-z_{n}}\frac{\partial^{l}\lambda_{n}}{\partial z_{0}^{l}}

Recall that
\lambda_{0}=\prod_{s=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{s}}
. reapplying Lemma
1

\lambda_{0}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{M_{n}}{z_{0}-z_{n}}

where
M_{n}=\prod_{s=1,s\neq n}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{n}-z_{s}}
. Therefore

\frac{\partial^{l-j}\lambda_{0}}{\partial z_{0}^{l-j}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}M_{n}\frac{(-1)^{l-j}(l-j)!}{(z_{0}-z_{n})^{l-j+1}}

Further, for
n=1,...,N

\frac{\partial^{l}\lambda_{n}}{\partial z_{0}^{l}}=\frac{l!M_{n}}{(z_{n}-z_{0})^{l+1}}

Now compare to (
2
) we obtain

\frac{1}{(x-z_{0})^{l+1}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x-z_{n}}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{M_{n}(-1)^{l-j}(l-j)!}{(z_{0}-z_{n})^{l-j+1}}\frac{1}{(x-z_{0})^{j+1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x-z_{n}}\frac{M_{n}}{(z_{n}-z_{0})^{l+1}}

Lastly let
L=l+1
,
z_{0}=0
, and
z_{i}=a_{i}
,
\mu_{n}=M_{n}|_{z_{i}=a_{i}}
for
i=1,...,N
respectively, we obtain what required.
∎

Main Results

In the last section we introduce some lemmas about partial fraction decomposition. In follow we discuss the partial fraction expansion of the infinite product

Theorem 1
.

(
Partial Fraction Summation
)

Let
(a_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and satisfy that: 1,
a_{n}
differ from each other for all
n
; 2,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}}
absolutely converges. Define

F(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-\frac{z}{a_{n}})

then following identity holds for
k=0,1,2,...
.

[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{k+1}}

On the other hand, for
L=0,1,2,...
,
\frac{1}{F(z)}
has the following partial fraction decompositions

\frac{1}{F(z)}=[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{0}+[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{1}z+...+[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{L-1}z^{L-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{L}}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})a_{n}^{L}(z-a_{n})}

For given
L
, we call
L
the order of this expansion
.

Proof.

Let
M=\inf(|a_{n}|)
, in the proof we always assume that
|z|<M
. Consider the expansion of
\frac{1}{F(z)}
, namely,

\frac{1}{F(z)}=\exp(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log(1-\frac{z}{a_{n}}))=\exp(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k})

Denote
[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{k}
by
c_{k}
, it can be computed by expanding the right hand side in the above formula. Then

\frac{1}{z^{L}F(z)}=\frac{1}{z^{L}}(c_{0}+c_{1}z+c_{2}z^{2}+...)

(3)

On the other hand, consider the another expansion via Lemma
2
and let
N\rightarrow\infty
, that is

\frac{1}{z^{L}F(z)}=\frac{1}{z^{L}}(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}})+...+\frac{1}{z^{2}}(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L-1}})+\frac{1}{z}(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L}})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L}(z-a_{n})}

(4)

Where
\lambda_{n}=-a_{n}\prod_{s=1,s\neq n}^{\infty}\frac{a_{s}}{a_{s}-a_{n}}
. Further, we can reformulate
\lambda_{n}
as following

\lambda_{n}=-a_{n}\lim_{z-a_{n}\rightarrow 0}\frac{a_{n}-z}{a_{n}}\prod_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{s}}{a_{s}-z}=\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})}

Now consider the expansion of
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L}(z-a_{n})}
, due to the absolute convergence of
\sum\frac{1}{a_{n}}
, for
|z|<M
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L}(z-a_{n})}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L+1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L+2}}z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L+3}}z^{2}+...

Then equation (
4
) becomes

\frac{1}{z^{L}F(z)}=\frac{1}{z^{L}}(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{2}}z+...+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{a_{n}^{L+1}}z^{L}+...)

(5)

Compare to (
3
) we have for
k=0,1,2,3,...
, the equalities hold

c_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{k+1}}

∎

Theorem 2
.

(
Partial fraction summation for symmetric products
)

Let
(a_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}

a_{n}
differ from each other for all
n
; 2,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}

[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{2k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-2}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2k+1}}

On the other hand, for
L=0,2,4,...
,
\frac{1}{F(z)}
has the following partial fraction decompositions

\frac{1}{F(z)}=[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{0}+[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{2}z^{2}+...+[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{L-2}z^{L-2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2z^{L}}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})a_{n}^{L-1}(z^{2}-a_{n}^{2})}

1

The remaining partial fraction decomposition formulae (

Theorem 3
.

(
Partial fraction summation for cyclotomic product
)

Let
(a_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and satisfy that:

I,
a_{n}
differ from each other for all
n
;

II,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{m}}
absolutely converges.

Define

F(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-(\frac{z}{a_{n}})^{m})

then following identity holds for
J=0,1,2,...
.

[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{J}=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }\mod(J,m)\neq 0\\
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-m}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{J+1}},\text{ if }\mod(J,m)=0\\
\end{cases}

(12)

Proof.

The proof is quite similar to Theorem
2
. For
k=1,2,..
, let
k=m(n-1)+r
and

b_{k}=b_{m(n-1)+r}=a_{n}\omega_{m}^{r-1},r=1,2,...,m

where
\omega_{m}=e^{\frac{2i\pi}{m}}
is the
m-
th root of unity. Then

F(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-\frac{z}{b_{k}})

On the othe hand, let

f_{r,N}(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}(1-\frac{z}{a_{n}\omega_{m}^{r-1}}),r=1,2,...,m

Then
f_{r,N}(z)=f_{1,N}(z/\omega_{m}^{r-1})
, we denote
f_{1,N}(.)
directly by
f_{N}(.)
. Then
f_{r,N}(a_{n}\omega_{m}^{r-1})=f_{N}(a_{n})=0
for
n\leq N
.
To compute
F^{\prime}(b_{k})=F^{\prime}(b_{m(n-1)+r})
:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(b_{m(n-1)+r})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}(f_{1,N}(z)f_{2,N}(z)...f_{m,N}(z))^{\prime}|_{z=a_{n}\omega_{m}^{r-1}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}f_{N}(z)f_{N}(\frac{z}{\omega_{m}})...f_{N}(\frac{z}{\omega_{m}^{m-1}})(\frac{f_{N}^{\prime}(z)}{f_{N}(z)}+\frac{1}{\omega_{m}}\frac{f_{N}^{\prime}(z/\omega_{m})}{f_{N}(z/\omega_{m})}+...+\frac{1}{\omega_{m}^{m-1}}\frac{f_{N}^{\prime}(z/\omega_{m}^{m-1})}{f_{N}(z/\omega_{m}^{m-1})})|_{z=a_{n}\omega_{m}^{r-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{m}^{r-1}}f^{\prime}(a_{n})f(a_{n}\omega_{m})...f(a_{n}\omega_{m}^{m-1})

One can implies that for all
n=1,2,3,...
and
r=1,2,...,m
,

F^{\prime}(b_{m(n-1)+r})=\frac{1}{\omega_{m}^{r-1}}F^{\prime}(b_{m(n-1)+1})

According to Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{J}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{F^{\prime}(b_{k})}\frac{1}{b_{k}^{J+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{F^{\prime}(b_{m(n-1)+r})}\frac{1}{(a_{n}\omega_{m}^{r-1})^{J+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\omega_{m}^{J(r-1)}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{F^{\prime}(b_{m(n-1)+1})}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{J+1}}

Therefore

[\frac{1}{F(z)}]_{J}=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }\mod(J,m)\neq 0\\
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-m}{F^{\prime}(a_{n})}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{J+1}},\text{ if }\mod(J,m)=0\\
\end{cases}

Corollary 3
.

(
The PFS representation of
\zeta(m,a)
)

For
m=2,3,4,...
, following identity holds,

\zeta(m,a)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{m(-1)^{k}\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}\Gamma(a-\omega_{m}^{r}(a+k))}{\Gamma(a)^{m}k!(a+k)^{m+1}}

(13)

Proof.

In the Theorem
3
for convenience let
k=n-1
, let
a_{n}=a-1+n=a+k
, then
\frac{1}{F(z)}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-(\frac{z}{a+k})^{m})^{-1}

Example 5
.

Let

It turns out that

Corollary 5
.

Denote

\displaystyle\zeta_{A}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{s}}

\displaystyle\zeta_{A,H}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}H_{k}}{k^{s}}

Proof.

Let

\frac{\pi z}{\sin(\pi z)}(\psi(1+z)+\psi(1-z))=-2\gamma+S_{1}(1,z)-S_{2}(1,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4(-1)^{k}\psi(2+k)}{(1+k)^{2n}}z^{2n}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4(-1)^{k}}{(1+k)^{2n}}z^{2n}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4(-1)^{k}H_{1+k}}{(1+k)^{2n}}z^{2n}

Suppose that the sequence

2

Equivalently, the relation can be reformulated as

[\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(z)}{F(z)}]_{J}=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }J\text{ odd}\\
-6\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{k}2},\text{ if }J=0\\

Proof.

Note that
H=(\log F(z))^{\prime}

(14)

On the other hand, by (

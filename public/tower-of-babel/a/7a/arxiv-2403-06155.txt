A Parisi Formula for Quantum Spin Glasses
Chokri Manai and Simone Warzel

Abstract




\minisec

Abstract
We establish three equivalent versions of a Parisi formula for the free energy of mean-field spin glasses in a transversal magnetic field.
These results are derived from available results for classical vector spin glasses by an approximation method using the functional integral representation of the partition function. In this approach, the order parameter is a non-decreasing function with values in the non-negative, real hermitian Hilbert-Schmidt operators.
For the quantum Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model, we also show that under the assumption of self-averaging of the self-overlap, the optimising Parisi order parameter is found within a two-dimensional subspace spanned by the self-overlap and the fully stationary overlap.

Setting the stage

Transcending their initial roots in physics
[
34
]
, classical mean-field spin glasses, as the Sherrington Kirkpatrick model
[
43
]
, have found interest in mathematics and computer science
[
7
,
33
,
47
,
48
,
15
]
. Their widespread appeal is rooted in the universal applicability of the tools and concepts associated with replica symmetry breaking. At their core, these models exhibit a phase transition into a glass phase, which is marked by the breaking of replica symmetry, and described through Parisi’s functional order parameter
[
40
,
41
]
.
Part of the charm of these seemingly simple mean-field models is that Parisi’s replica approach enables the derivation of a variational formula for the free energy
[
34
,
46
,
47
,
48
,
15
,
37
]
.

Soon after Parisi’s discovery
[
40
,
41
]
, physicists have started searching for an extension of Parisi’s replica approach to quantum models
[
44
,
19
]
. More recently, the interest in quantum glasses have been renewed due to questions related to quantum information
[
49
,
10
]
, but also since disordered mean-field models still serve as paradigms in quantum matter or, as the SYK model, even in high-energy physics
[
21
,
53
,
3
]
.

The quantum nature of the spin degrees of freedom may enter such models in various degrees of difficulty. In case the non-commutativity is present in the interaction terms, such as in SYK or a Heisenberg spin glass, an extension of Parisi’s formalism is wide open, and very little is known rigorously (see e.g.
[
35
,
15
,
18
]
). In case the non-commutativity only enters in the form of a transversal field, the resulting model may be analysed using a functional integral. Based on this, replica symmetry breaking approximations to the free energy have been found early on
[
19
,
52
,
50
]
(see also
[
9
,
45
]
and references therein). However, aside from the case of hierarchical transversal models of generalised random energy type
[
22
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
, a closed-form variational formula for the free energy has been absent. The purpose of this paper is to present a first version of such a variational formula of Parisi type for a large class of transversal field models, which includes the quantum Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (QSK) model. Using the functional integral representation of the partition function, we derive our three equivalent versions of such a formula from available formulae for vector-spin glasses
[
38
,
13
,
12
]
. For the QSK, we thus complete an earlier approach
[
1
]
, in which a related variational expression has been obtained. The latter, however, suffers from the crucial drawback of an additional limit in its formulation. Our variational principle extends
the variational principle for the free energy of the QSK in the high-temperature phase from
[
28
]
to the full parameter range.

The variational principle will identify the quantum analogue of Parisi’s functional order parameter with monotone functions taking values in a subset of the non-negative Hilbert-Schmidt operators. That subset matches the support of the functional integral.
For the QSK, we also show how, under a widely believed assumption of the self-averaging of the self-overlap, our variational formula simplifies in the sense that the optimising Hilbert-Schmidt operators are from a two-parameter subspace spanned by the self-overlap and the stationary kernel.
This simplification of the order parameter is akin to similar results for other glasses with a symmetry in the underlying a-priori measure such as in case of balanced Potts glasses
[
4
,
17
]
.

2
, we first need to set the stage by introducing the models, the functional integral and the quantum Parisi functional, which involves several correlation functionals. This is the purpose of the remainder of this section.

1.1
Models and assumptions

We consider systems of spin-
\frac{1}{2}
quantum degrees of freedom. For each spin, the observable algebra on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is generated by the identity
\mathbbm{1}
and the Pauli matrices:

S^{x}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad S^{y}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&i\\
-i&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad S^{z}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{matrix}\right).

The Hilbert space of
N
spin-
\frac{1}{2}
is the
N
-fold tensor product
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The Pauli matrices are lifted to the tensor product in the canonical way, i.e. for the
j
th spin the non-trivial factor is inserted in the
j
th tensor component,
S_{j}^{x}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}\otimes\dots\otimes S^{x}\otimes\dots\mathbbm{1}
and similarly for
y,z
.

The quantum spin glasses studied in this paper are transversal field models, whose Hamiltonians on
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{C}^{2}
are of the form

H_{N}\coloneqq U\left(S_{1}^{z},\dots S_{N}^{z}\right)+\beta h\sum_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}^{z}-\beta b\sum_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}^{x}.

(1.1)

The last term represents a constant transversal magnetic field in the (negative)
x
-direction. The second term is a constant longitudinal field in the
z
-direction, which agrees with the axis along which the interaction among the spins is mediated. The operator
U\left(S_{1}^{z},\dots,S_{N}^{z}\right)
is defined via the functional calculus of the jointly diagonalisable
z
-components of the spins in terms of a random function
U:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, stemming from a classical, mean-field Ising spin glass like the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model.
More generally, we assume
U
to be a realisation of a Gaussian random process with mean zero, i.e.
\mathbb{E}\left[U(\bm{\sigma})\right]=0
for all
\bm{\sigma}\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, and covariance given in terms of the classical overlap of two Ising configurations
\bm{\sigma}^{(1)},\bm{\sigma}^{(2)}\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
:

\mathbb{E}\left[U\big(\bm{\sigma}^{(1)}\big)\ U\big(\bm{\sigma}^{(1)}\big)\right]=N\ \widehat{\zeta}\left(N^{-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(1)}\cdot\bm{\sigma}^{(2)}\right),\qquad\bm{\sigma}^{(1)}\cdot\bm{\sigma}^{(2)}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{(1)}\sigma_{j}^{(2)}.

(1.2)

Throughout this paper, the function
\widehat{\zeta}
, which determines the covariance, will be tacitly assumed to satisfy:

Assumption 1.1
.

The function
\widehat{\zeta}:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
admits an absolutely convergent series representation of the form

\widehat{\zeta}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\beta_{2p}^{2}x^{2p},\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(2p)^{2}\beta_{2p}^{2}<\infty.

(1.3)

This covers all classical, mixed, even
p
-spin interactions and, in particular, the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model, for which
\beta_{2p}=0
unless
p=1
. More precisely, each of the terms in (
1.3
) represents via (
1.2
)
the covariance of a
p
-spin interaction on
\bm{\sigma}\coloneqq(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N})
of the form

U(\bm{\sigma})=\frac{1}{N^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}=1}^{N}g_{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{j_{p}}

(1.4)

with
\left(g_{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}}\right)
independent and identically distributed, standard Gaussian random variables. Note that such potentials do include self-interactions, which however do not contribute to the free energy in the thermodynamic limit. We restrict to even
p
-spin interactions due to issues with positivity and convexity.

The central quantity of interest is the quantum partition function
{\operatorname{Tr}\,}e^{-H_{N}}
corresponding to (
1.1
), and its free energy. We choose to include
the inverse temperature
\beta\in[0,\infty)
in the Hamiltonian. The interaction term incorporates the temperature through the factors
\beta_{2p}
in (
1.3
).

1.2
Functional integral

As in other works on transversal field models, our analysis of the quantum partition function is based on a functional integral representation
[
11
,
1
,
28
]
.
For its formulation,
we consider a Poisson process
\omega_{1}
on
[0,1)
with intensity
\beta b\geq 0
and conditioned to have an even number of points in
[0,1)
. Setting
\eta_{1}(t,\omega_{1}):=\#\{\mbox{Poisson points of $\omega_{1}$ in $[0,t)$}\}
and picking initial conditions
\sigma_{1}\in\{-1,1\}
independently with probability
1/2
, we
let
\mu_{1}
stand for the induced probability measure on the path

\xi_{1}:[0,1)\to\{-1,1\},\quad\xi_{1}(t):=\sigma_{1}\ (-1)^{\eta_{1}(t)}.

Any such path is continuous from the right with a limit from the left (’càdlàg’). Moreover, since the number of jumps is even, by identifying
\xi_{1}(1):=\xi_{1}(0)
, it can be uniquely extended to a càdlàg path on the unit torus. The measure
\mu_{1}
is hence a well-defined probability measure on paths
\xi_{1}
on the unit torus endowed with the Borel sigma-algebra
\mathcal{B}
corresponding to the Skohorod topology.

Since our Hamiltonian
H_{N}
also incorporates a constant longitudinal magnetic field,
\beta h\in\mathbb{R}
, we modify the apriori probability measure
\mu_{1}
by setting for any
A\in\mathcal{B}
:

\nu_{1}(A):=\frac{\cosh(\beta b)}{\cosh(\beta\sqrt{b^{2}+h^{2}})}\int_{A}\exp\left(-\beta h\int_{0}^{1}\xi_{1}(s)ds\right)\mu_{1}(d\xi_{1}).

(1.5)

The prefactor ensures that
\nu_{1}
is again a probability measure. We now set
\nu_{N}\coloneqq\nu_{1}^{\otimes N}
the product measure of
N
identical copies of
\nu_{1}
, which govern the collection of paths
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})
. The partition function of
H_{N}
,

{\operatorname{Tr}\,}e^{-H_{N}}=\left(2\cosh(\beta\sqrt{b^{2}+h^{2}})\right)^{N}Z_{N},

is known
[
28
]
to be given by the functional integral

Z_{N}\coloneqq\int\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds\right)\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi}).

The main purpose of this paper is to derive a quantum Parisi formula for
Z_{N}
. Loosely speaking, this will be done by identifying
Z_{N}
as the partition function of a mixed
p
-spin model with continuous ’vector’ spins
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})
. The action
\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds
in the functional integral serves as its random energy. By (
1.2
) the latter is a Gaussian process with mean zero and covariance

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{(1)}(s)\right)ds\ \int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{(2)}(t)\right)dt\right]=N\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\zeta}\left(N^{-1}\bm{\xi}^{(1)}(s)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{(2)}(t)\right)dsdt.

(1.6)

for two replicas
\bm{\xi}^{(1)},\bm{\xi}^{(2)}
of the paths.

1.3
Space of asymptotic overlap operators

Given two replicas of paths
\bm{\xi}^{(1)},\bm{\xi}^{(2)}
, the covariance (
1.6
) only depends on the
path-overlap

\widehat{r}_{N}(\bm{\xi}^{(1)},\bm{\xi}^{(2)})(s,t)\coloneqq N^{-1}\bm{\xi}^{(1)}(s)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{(2)}(t).

Through its kernel the latter defines
a rank-
N
overlap operator
r_{N}(\bm{\xi}^{(1)},\bm{\xi}^{(2)})
on the real Hilbert space

L^{2}(0,1):=\left\{f:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}\ \big|\ \int_{0}^{1}f(t)^{2}dt<\infty\right\}.

The real scalar product on this Hilbert space is denoted by
\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{0}^{1}f(s)g(s)ds
.
Recall that an integral operator

\varrho
on
L^{2}(0,1)
with kernel
\widehat{\varrho}:[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
acts as

(\varrho f)(s):=\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)f(t)dt,\quad f\in L^{2}(0,1).

(1.7)

We will mostly be concerned with hermitian integral operators, whose kernels are real and symmetric by definition, i.e.
\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)=\widehat{\varrho}(t,s)
, and amongst those with Hilbert-Schmidt operators. They form the real Hilbert space
\mathcal{S}_{2}
with the scalar product

\langle\varrho,\varrho^{\prime}\rangle:={\operatorname{Tr}\,}\varrho\varrho^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(t,s)\widehat{\varrho}^{\prime}(s,t)\ dsdt.

Any real symmetric function
\widehat{\varrho}\in L^{2}(0,1)^{2}
uniquely defines though (
1.7
)
an operator
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
on
L^{2}(0,1)
and vice versa. Any such
\varrho
then also belongs to the real Banach space of hermitian Schatten-
p
operators
\mathcal{S}_{p}
with
p\geq 2
.
Generally,
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
is not necessarily in the trace class
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and it is generally rather difficult to decide whether
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
or not, solely based on the integral kernel
\widehat{\varrho}
.
However, if
\varrho
belongs to the convex cone
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}\coloneqq\{\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\ |\ \varrho\geq 0\}
of non-negative, hermitian Hilbert-Schmidt operators,
a result of Weidmann
[
51
]
ensures that if the kernel is essentially bounded,
\|\widehat{\varrho}\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\operatorname{esssup}_{s,t\in(0,1)^{2}}|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)|<\infty
, then
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
More precisely, in this situation the function defined for almost every (a.e.)
\tau\in(0,1)
by

\widehat{\varrho}(\tau,\tau)\coloneqq\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{(2r)^{2}}\int_{-r}^{r}\int_{-r}^{r}\widehat{\varrho}(s+\tau,t+\tau)dsdt

(1.8)

is non-negative and bounded with
{\operatorname{Tr}\,}\varrho=\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(\tau,\tau)\ d\tau
and
|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)|\leq\sqrt{\widehat{\varrho}(s,s)\ \widehat{\varrho}(t,t)}

for a.e.
(s,t)\in(0,1)^{2}
, cf.
[
51
,
8
]
and Proposition
A.4
in Appendix
A
.
Subsequently, we will tacitly assume that the diagonal of the bounded integral kernel of
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
is given by (
1.8
).

In our spin glass model the self-overlap
r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})
is an example of a non-negative integral operator with a bounded kernel. Its kernel agrees with
N^{-1}\bm{\xi}(s)\cdot\bm{\xi}(t)
, and the diagonal is obtained by setting
s=t
by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem.
The self-overlap hence belongs to the set

\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\left\{\varrho\geq 0\ \mbox{is an integral operator on $L^{2}(0,1)$ with some kernel $\widehat{\varrho}:(0,1)^{2}\to[-1,1]$}\right\}.

This subset of
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
contains the support,
{\operatorname{supp}\,}\nu_{1}
, of the functional integral. It is convex and closed, i.e.
\mathcal{D}=\overline{\mathcal{D}}
, where here and in the following, we equip
\mathcal{D}
with the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.
This set will serve as the domain of the quantum Parisi functional, which we construct in the next two subsections.

1.4
Correlation functional

As is apparent from (
1.6
) the correlation functional
\zeta:\mathcal{D}\to[0,\infty)
,

\zeta(\varrho)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\zeta}\left(\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)\right)dsdt

(1.9)

plays an important role.
Since
\widehat{\zeta}\geq 0
, the integral in (
1.9
) is well defined, potentially infinite, for any measurable kernel
\widehat{\varrho}
. It thus extends to the Hilbert space
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
By the representation (
1.3
) of
\widehat{\zeta}
, we may represent
the correlation functional as a series involving the even Hadamard products of
\varrho
:

\zeta(\varrho)\coloneqq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\beta_{2p}^{2}\ \langle 1,\varrho^{\odot 2p}1\rangle,\quad\mbox{with}\quad\langle 1,\varrho^{\odot 2p}1\rangle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)^{2p}dsdt.

(1.10)

The abbreviation
\langle 1,\varrho^{\odot 2p}1\rangle
alludes to the fact that in case
\widehat{\varrho}
is bounded, this term agrees with the matrix element involving the constant function one,

1\in L^{2}(0,1)
, of the
2p
-fold Hadamard product
\varrho^{\odot 2p}
of
\varrho
(see Appendix
A
, which compiles basic properties of this product). We summarize some properties of
\zeta
, which will be crucial for the definition of the Parisi functional, in the following lemma.

Lemma 1.2
.

1.

\zeta:\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}\to[0,\infty]
is convex, weakly lower semicontinuous and proper, i.e. with non-empty domain
\dom\zeta\coloneqq\left\{\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}\ |\ \zeta(\varrho)<\infty\right\}\supset\mathcal{D}
. For any
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
, we have
\zeta(\varrho)\leq\widehat{\zeta}(1)
.

2.

The functional
\zeta
is monotone, i.e. if
0\leq\pi\leq\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
, then
0\leq\zeta(\pi)\leq\zeta(\varrho)
.

3.

At every
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
the functional

\zeta
is Gateaux differentiable with derivative represented by the non-negative operator

d\zeta(\varrho)\coloneqq\sum_{p\geq 1}2p\beta_{2p}^{2}\ \varrho^{\odot 2p-1}

(1.11)

on
L^{2}(0,1)
. The latter is uniformly bounded,
\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{D}}\|d\zeta(\varrho)\|\leq N_{\zeta}:=\sum_{p\geq 1}2p\beta_{2p}^{2}<\infty
.
As a function of
\varrho
, this operator is monotone increasing, i.e. if
0\leq\pi\leq\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
, then
0\leq d\zeta(\pi)\leq d\zeta(\varrho)
, and Lipschitz continouus,

.\left\|d\zeta(\varrho)-d\zeta(\pi)\right\|\leq L_{\zeta}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)-\widehat{\pi}(s,t)\right|dsdt\leq L_{\zeta}\left\|\varrho-\pi\right\|_{2}

(1.12)

for any
\varrho,\pi\in\mathcal{D}
with
L_{\zeta}\coloneqq\sum_{p\geq 1}2p(2p-1)\beta_{2p}^{2}
.

Proof.

1. The convexity immediately follows from the convexity of the functions
[0,\infty)\ni x\mapsto x^{2p}
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
The weak lower semicontinuity follows from Proposition
A.5
.
The claimed bound on
\zeta
for
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
is immediate from the boundedness of the kernel
|\widehat{\varrho}|\leq 1
.

2. This follows straightforwardly from Lemma
A.3
.

3. The Gateaux differentiability and its representation in terms of the operator (
1.11
) is immediate. The boundedness with the explicit norm bounds of the operator
d\zeta(\varrho)

are also straightforward from its definition as a Hadamard-Schur product. So is its monotonicity by Lemma
A.3
.
The proof of (
1.12
) follows from the same elementary inequality as (
A.5
).
∎

Employing the positive part
\varrho_{+}=\varrho\ 1_{[0,\infty)}(\varrho)
of the hermitian
Hilbert-Schmidt operator
\varrho=\varrho_{+}-\varrho_{-}
, one extends

\zeta:\mathcal{S}_{2}\to[0,\infty)
,
\zeta(\varrho)\coloneqq\zeta(\varrho_{+})
. This functional remains weakly lower semicontinuous and convex, since
(a+b)_{+}\leq a_{+}+b_{+}
for any
a,b\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Its Legendre transform

\zeta^{*}:\mathcal{S}_{2}\to[0,\infty],\quad\zeta^{*}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\big(\langle x,\varrho\rangle-\zeta(\varrho)\big).

(1.13)

again defines a proper, convex, weakly lower semicontinuous functional, whose Legendre transform agrees with
\zeta=\left(\zeta^{*}\right)^{*}
by the Fenchel-Moreau theorem
[
5
]
.
The domain of this one-sided Legendre transform
\zeta^{*}
is
\dom\zeta^{*}=\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
.
If
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
, it is easy to see that the supremum in (
1.13
) coincides with the supremum restricted to
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
.

The quantum Parisi functional also involves the functional
\theta:\mathcal{D}\to[0,\infty)
,

\theta(\varrho):=\langle d\zeta(\varrho),\varrho\rangle-\zeta(\varrho)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(2p-1)\beta_{2p}^{2}\ \varrho(s,t)^{2p}\ dsdt=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(2p-1)\beta_{2p}^{2}\ \langle 1,\varrho^{\odot 2p}1\rangle.

(1.14)

Its properties are listed in the following

Lemma 1.3
.

1.

\theta
is convex and uniformly bounded,
\displaystyle\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{D}}\theta(\varrho)\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(2p-1)\beta_{2p}^{2}\leq N_{\zeta}<\infty
.

2.

\theta
is monotone increasing, i.e. if
0\leq\pi\leq\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
, then
0\leq\theta(\pi)\leq\theta(\varrho)
.

3.

\theta
is Lipschitz continuous when
\mathcal{D}
is equipped with the Hilbert-Schmidt norm:

\left|\theta(\varrho)-\theta(\pi)\right|\leq L_{\zeta}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)-\widehat{\pi}(s,t)\right|dsdt\leq L_{\zeta}\ \|\varrho-\pi\|_{2}.

(1.15)

Proof.

1. The convexity again follows from the convexity of the functions
[0,\infty)\ni x\mapsto x^{2p}
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
. The uniform boundedness is immediate from the boundedness of the kernel
|\widehat{\varrho}|\leq 1
.

2. The representation (
1.14
) as a non-negative series involving the Hadamard product such that Proposition
A.2
yields the claim.

3. The proof of the Lipschitz continuity parallels the proof of (
1.12
).
∎

1.5
Quantum Parisi functional

In a first step, the space of order parameters of the quantum glass will be identified with monotone increasing, piecewise constant, and right-continuous with left limits (càdlàg) functions with values in
\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle\Pi:=\ \left\{\pi:[0,1]\to\mathcal{D}\ \big|\ 0\leq\pi(s)\leq\pi(t)\;\mbox{for all $s\leq t$ with }\;\pi(0)=0,\;\mbox{and constant on}\right.\quad

some partition
0=:m_{0}<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}<m_{r+1}:=1
:

\displaystyle\left.\quad\pi=\sum_{k=1}^{r+1}\operatorname{1}_{[m_{k-1},m_{k})}\pi(m_{k-1})+\pi(m_{r+1})\operatorname{1}_{\{1\}}\right\}.

Note that
\pi(m)\in\mathcal{D}
for any
m\in[0,1]
once we require
\pi(1)\in\mathcal{D}
.

To a function
\pi\in\Pi
with
r
-steps, i.e. a partition
0=:m_{0}<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}<m_{r+1}:=1
with ordered values
0=\pi(m_{0})\leq\pi(m_{1})\dots\leq\pi(m_{r})\leq\pi(1)\in\mathcal{D}
, we associate
j=1,\dots,r+1
independent Gaussian random vectors in
w_{j}\in L^{2}(0,1)
. They are characterised by their zero mean and their covariance functional

\mathbb{E}\left[\langle f,w_{j}\rangle\langle g,w_{j}\rangle\right]=\langle f,\left(d\zeta(\pi(m_{j}))-d\zeta(\pi(m_{j-1}))\right)g\rangle,\quad f,g\in L^{2}(0,1).

(1.16)

Note that Lemma
1.2
in particular safeguards that
d\zeta(\pi(m_{j}))-d\zeta(\pi(m_{j-1}))\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
is a legitimate covariance operator of a Gaussian process in
L^{2}(0,1)
, cf.
[
25
]
. To any hermitian Hilbert-Schmidt operator
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we then associate the random variable

X_{r}(\pi,x):=\ln\int\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\langle\xi,w_{j}\rangle+\langle\xi,\left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta(\pi(1))\right)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi).

(1.17)

Note that
\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\langle\xi,w_{j}\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\xi,d\zeta(\pi(1))\xi\rangle\right)
is an exponential martingale with respect to the Gaussian average.
For
1\leq j\leq r+1
, we proceed inductively and set

X_{j-1}(\pi,x):=\frac{1}{m_{j}}\ln\mathbb{E}_{j}\left[e^{m_{j}X_{j}(\pi,x)}\right],

(1.18)

where
\mathbb{E}_{j}
denotes the average with respect to
w_{j}
.

The quantum Parisi functional
\mathcal{P}:\Pi\times\mathcal{S}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is then defined as the non-random end-point of this iterative procedure plus an integral:

\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)\coloneqq X_{0}(\pi,x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\theta(\pi(m))dm.

(1.19)

Remarks 1.4
.

1.

Since the last random variable
w_{r+1}
comes in (

) with weight
m_{r+1}=1
, it may be integrated out. This results in a random variable
X_{r}(\pi,x)
, which is of the same form as
X_{r+1}(\pi,x)
with
\pi(1)
replaced by
\pi(m_{r})
. One may hence always assume

without loss of generality.

2.

To analyse the Parisi functional, we also rely on a well-known equivalent representation using Ruelle probability cascades
[
42
,
37
]
. For the above partition with
r
steps associated to
\pi\in\Pi
, let

denote the weights of the corresponding Ruelle probability cascade
[
37
]
. One then defines a family of
L^{2}
-valued, centred Gaussian processes
(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}
with covariance

\mathbb{E}\left[\langle f,z_{\alpha}\rangle\langle g,z_{\alpha^{\prime}}\rangle\right]=\langle f,d\zeta\left(\pi(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}})\right)g\rangle,\quad f,g\in L^{2}(0,1).

(1.20)

where
\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}\coloneqq\min\left\{0\leq j\leq r\ \big|\ \alpha_{j}=\alpha^{\prime}_{j}\;\mbox{and}\;\alpha_{j+1}\neq\alpha^{\prime}_{j+1}\right\}
and by convention
\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}=0
in case
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha^{\prime}_{1}
(see.
[
37
, Sec. 2]
for an explicit construction of these processes).
Then the iterative construction of
X_{0}(\pi,x)
for
\pi\in\Pi
with
r
steps is known to be equivalent to the following expectation value

X_{0}(\pi,x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}\int\exp\left(\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}\rangle+\langle\xi,\left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta(\pi(1)\right)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)\right].

(1.21)

Before we proceed, we collect some further properties of
\mathcal{P}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

There is some
L\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
and
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
:

\left|\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\mathcal{P}\left(\pi^{\prime},x^{\prime}\right)\right|\leq L\left(\|x-x^{\prime}\|+\int_{0}^{1}\|\pi(s)-\pi^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}\ ds\right).

(1.22)

The proof, which is by standard Gaussian interpolation
[
47
,
37
]
, is found in Appendix
B
. For fixed
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, one can hence continuously extend
\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)
to the set

\left\{\pi:[0,1]\to\mathcal{D}\;\mbox{c\`{a}dl\`{a}g}\ \big|\ 0\leq\pi(s)\leq\pi(t)\leq\pi(1)\;\mbox{for all}\;s\leq t\;\mbox{with}\;\pi(0)=0\right\}.

For future purpose, we also record some elementary bounds as well as smoothness and related properties of the Parisi functional. For its formulation, we recall that
\|x\|
stands for the operator norm on
L^{2}(0,1)
, and

\left\|\widehat{x}\right\|_{q}\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{x}(s,t)\right|^{q}dsdt\right)^{1/q}
,
q\in[1,\infty)
,
is the norm of the integral kernel
\widehat{x}
corresponding to
x
.

Proposition 1.6
.

1.

For any
\pi\in\Pi
, the functional
\mathcal{S}_{2}\ni x\mapsto\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)
is convex and bounded. If
x=x_{+}-x_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, then:

\displaystyle-\frac{N_{\zeta}}{2}-\min\left\{\|x_{-}\|,\|\widehat{x}_{-}\|_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq X_{0}(\pi,x)\leq\min\left\{\|x_{+}\|,\|\widehat{x}_{+}\|_{1}\right\}.

The functional is Gateaux differentiable at every
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
with bounded derivative,
\left\|d\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)\right\|_{2}\leq 1
. The second derivative is non-negative and also uniformly bounded, i.e. for any

:

0\leq\frac{d^{2}}{d\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{P}(\pi,x+\lambda y)\Big|_{\lambda=0}\leq 2\left\|y\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(1.23)

2.

\displaystyle\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)\leq\!\mathcal{P}\left(0,x\right)
and

\displaystyle-\frac{N_{\zeta}}{2}\leq\!\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{*}(2x)\right]\leq\!\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\mathcal{P}\left(0,x\right)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{*}(2x)\right]\leq\frac{\widehat{\zeta}(1)}{2}
.

The proof, which is found in Appendix
B
, is based on extensions of techniques, which are rather standard in spin glass theory.

2
Main results

2.1
Quantum Parisi formula

Our main result are three equivalent versions of a Parisi formula for the quantum glasses (
1.1
). We start with the two versions inspired and based on the corresponding formulae for general vector-spin glasses established by Chen
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under Assumption
1.1
:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\ln Z_{N}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{*}(2x)\right]

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{D}}\left[\inf_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\langle x,\varrho\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\zeta(\varrho)\right].

(2.2)

As will be explained in the proof, which is found in Section
4
, the second line (
2.2
) is just an application of the Toland-Singer theorem to the supremum of the difference of two convex functionals.
In this aspect of our proof, we deviate from available results in
[
13
]
. In contrast,
[
13
]
establishes the equality of the right sides via the Hopf-Lax representation of the solution of an underlying Hamilton-Jacobi equation (see also
[
20
,
36
,
14
]
).

The outer supremum in (
2.1
) is over non-negative, real hermitian Hilbert-Schmidt operators. As in
[
13
]
, the role of
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
is that of a Lagrange multiplier fixing the self-overlap in the functional integral
Z_{N}
.
For fixed
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
, the inner infimum arises from a Parisi formula for a self-overlap corrected functional integral spelled in Theorem
2.3
below.
Both the infimum as well as the supremum are finite as seen from Proposition
1.6
.

We may also cast our Parisi formula in Theorem
2.1
in a form analogous to Panchenko’s version
[
38
]
of the Parisi formula for vector-spin glasses.
This version involves order parameters from the set of paths

\Pi_{\varrho}\coloneqq\left\{\pi:[0,1]\to\mathcal{D}\;\mbox{c\`{a}dl\`{a}g}\ \big|\ 0\leq\pi(s)\leq\pi(t)\;\mbox{for all $s\leq t$ with }\;\pi(0)=0\;\mbox{and}\;\pi(1)=\varrho\right\},

(2.3)

terminating at a fixed
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
.
In the present context, the analogue of
[
38
, Thm. 1]
or
[
12
, Prop.3.1]
reads as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under Assumption
1.1
:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\ln Z_{N}\right]=\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{D}}\left[\inf_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\langle x,\varrho\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\zeta(\varrho)\right].

(2.4)

The proof can be found in Section
4
.
Comparing with (
2.2
), the optimal
\pi
there is seen to end at
\varrho
, which turns our to have the meaning of the self-overlap in the model (cf. Subsection
2.3
below). Up to a limit and minor details in the variational sets, the functional and variational form in the right side of (
2.4
) agrees with the one in
[
1
]
.

We follow up with some further comments
on special cases and implications of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.

Classical case
b=0
:
The version (
2.4
) of the Parisi fromula is closest to the classical Parisi formula without transversal field. In case
b=0
, where the Poisson measure
\nu_{1}
has no jumps,
\xi(t)=\xi(0)
for all
t\in[0,1)
. The weight
\langle\xi,x\xi\rangle=\langle 1,x1\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{x}(s,t)dsdt
in the Parisi functional is hence a real number,
\langle\xi,d\zeta(\pi(1))\xi\rangle=\langle 1,d\zeta(\varrho)1\rangle
and
\langle w_{\alpha},\xi\rangle=\langle w_{\alpha},1\rangle\xi(0)
. Consequently, the infimum in (
2.4
) over
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
forces
\varrho
to coincide with the rank-one projection
|1\rangle\langle 1|
onto the constant function
1\in L^{2}(0,1)
, because otherwise the infimum is minus infinity. Since

\zeta(|1\rangle\langle 1|)-\langle 1,d\zeta(|1\rangle\langle 1|)1\rangle=\theta(|1\rangle\langle 1|)=\widehat{\zeta}^{\prime}(1)-\widehat{\zeta}(1)\eqqcolon\widehat{\theta}(1)
,
the right side in (
2.4
) therefore reduces at
b=0
to

\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{|1\rangle\langle 1|}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{N}^{r}}\frac{\mu_{\alpha}^{\pi}}{2\cosh(\beta h)}\sum_{\zeta(0)\in\{-1,1\}}\exp\left[\left(\langle w_{\alpha},1\rangle-\beta h\right)\xi(0)\right]\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\theta(\pi(m))dm-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\theta}(1),

with
\mathbb{E}\left[\langle w_{\alpha},1\rangle\langle w_{\alpha^{\prime}},1\rangle\right]=\langle 1,d\zeta(\pi(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}}))1\rangle
. To any given
\pi\in\Pi_{|1\rangle\langle 1|}
, we may hence associate a classical order parameter
\pi_{c}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
ending at
\pi_{c}(1)=1
by setting

\widehat{\zeta}^{\prime}\left(\pi_{c}(m)\right)\coloneqq\langle 1,d\zeta(\pi(m)))1\rangle.

Convexity of
\zeta
implies
\theta(\varrho_{1})\geq\langle\varrho_{2},d\zeta(\varrho_{1})\rangle-\zeta(\varrho_{2})
for any
\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}\in\mathcal{D}
. The choice
\varrho_{1}=\pi(m)
and
\varrho_{2}=\pi_{c}(m)|1\rangle\langle 1|
leads to the lower bound for any

:

\theta(\pi(m))\geq\pi_{c}(m)\langle 1,d\zeta(\pi(m)))1\rangle-\widehat{\zeta}(\pi_{c}(m))=\pi_{c}(m)\widehat{\zeta}^{\prime}\left(\pi_{c}(m)\right)-\widehat{\zeta}(\pi_{c}(m))\eqqcolon\widehat{\theta}(\pi_{c}(m)).

(2.5)

Therefore, the above infimum over quantum order parameters
\pi\in\Pi_{|1\rangle\langle 1|}
can be reduced to an infimum over classical order parameters,

\Pi_{c}\coloneqq\left\{\pi:[0,1]\to[0,1]\;\mbox{c\`{a}dl\`{a}g}\ \big|\ 0=\pi(0)\leq\pi(s)\leq\pi(t)\leq\pi(1)=1\;\mbox{for all}\;s\leq t\right\},

and one arrives at the familiar form
[
34
,
48
,
37
]
of the Parisi variational expression in the classical case:

\inf_{\pi_{c}\in\Pi_{c}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\frac{\mu_{\alpha}^{\pi_{c}}}{2\cosh(\beta h)}\sum_{\zeta(0)\in\{-1,1\}}\exp\left[\left(\widehat{w}_{\alpha}-\beta h\right)\xi(0)\right]\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\theta}(\pi_{c}(m))dm-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\theta}(1)

(2.6)

with centred Gaussian random variables
\widehat{w}_{\alpha}
with covariance
\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{w}_{\alpha}\widehat{w}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right]=\widehat{\zeta}^{\prime}(\pi_{c}(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}}))
.

Annealed bound:
The choice
\pi=0
in (

) results in the annealed functional,

\mathcal{P}\left(0,x\right)=\ln\int\exp\left(\langle\xi,x\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi).

As a corollary of Theorem

, it enters an upper bound on the true quantum free energy:

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\ln Z_{N}\right]\leq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\mathcal{P}\left(0,x\right)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{*}(2x)\right]=\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{D}}\left[\inf_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\mathcal{P}\left(0,x\right)-\langle x,\varrho\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\zeta(\varrho)\right].

QSK:
For the quantum Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model, for which

\zeta(\varrho)=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\|\varrho\|_{2}^{2},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\zeta^{*}(x)=\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}}\|x\|_{2}^{2},

the free energy in the high-temperature regime

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\ln Z_{N}\right]=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\mathcal{P}\left(0,x\right)-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left\|x\right\|^{2}_{2}\right],\qquad\mbox{for all $\beta<1$, $b\in\mathbb{R}$ at $h=0$.}

(2.7)

The result

for

persists in the low temperature regime

The argument in

2.2
Self-overlap corrected partition function

Behind Chen’s results
[
13
]
as well as our main result, Theorem
2.1
, is the proof of a Parisi formula for a self-overlap corrected partition function of the form

W_{N}(x)\coloneqq\int\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds-\frac{N}{2}\zeta\left(r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})\right)\right)e^{N\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}),x\rangle}\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi}),

(2.9)

where
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
is arbitrary. The first exponential is an exponential martingale. The second exponential involving
N\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}),x\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\xi_{j},x\xi_{j}\rangle
, may be incorporated in the apriori measure of the path integral.
We extend Chen’s results
[
13
]
is the following way.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\ln W_{N}(x)\right]=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)\eqqcolon\mathcal{F}(x).

(2.10)

This theorems harbours a variety of useful consequences for the functional
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{S}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by the right side. Among it is its convexity and Gateaux differentiability.
Note that the prelimit

G_{N}(x)\coloneqq{N}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\ln W_{N}(x)\right]

is evidently convex and differentiable at every
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Its derivative
dG_{N}(x)
is the averaged self-overlap under the Gibbs expectation
\langle\cdot\rangle_{x,N}
corresponding to
W_{N}(x)
in (
2.9
),

\langle dG_{N}(x),y\rangle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}),y\rangle\rangle_{x,N}\right],\qquad\mbox{for any $y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}$.}

Clearly, the right side defines a non-negative, rank-
N
operator
dG_{N}(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
.
Identical arguments as in the case of vector-spin models
[
12
, Prop. 2.3.]
allow to establish the differentiability of
\mathcal{F}
. On top of that, we identify properties of its Legendre transform, which will be needed for establishing and exploiting the Toland-Singer identity (
2.2
).

Corollary 2.4
.

The functional
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{S}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)
is
1
-Lipschitz continuous and convex with domain
\dom\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

1.

Its Legendre transform
\mathcal{F}^{*}:\mathcal{S}_{2}\to[0,\infty)
,

\mathcal{F}^{*}(\varrho)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\left(\langle x,\varrho\rangle-\mathcal{F}(x)\right)

(2.11)

is a proper, lower semicontinuous and convex functional with
\dom\mathcal{F}^{*}\subset\mathcal{D}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}=\left(\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)^{*}
.

2.

\mathcal{F}
is Gateaux differentiable at every
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
with derivative
d\mathcal{F}(x)
given by

\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle dG_{N}(x),y\rangle=\langle d\mathcal{F}(x),y\rangle,\qquad\mbox{for any $y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}$.}

(2.12)

Under the averaged Gibbs measure
\langle\cdot\rangle_{x,N}
, the self-overlap weakly concentrates in the sense that for all
y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\langle\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}),y\rangle\rangle_{x,N}-\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}),y\rangle\rangle_{x.N}\right]\right|\right]=0.

(2.13)

Although a large part of the proof of 2. closely follows the arguments in
[
12
]
, we spell it in Subsection
4.2
with an eye on the pitfalls of the infinite-dimensional situation in the quantum set-up. Part of the argument relies on the self-averaging property of
\ln W_{N}(x)
. Since this property is fundamental to the free energy of quantum spin glasses, we recall it from
[
16
]
. Note that in the present case of Gaussian processes
U
, which are linear combinations of
p
-spin interactions (
1.4
), the following proposition is a straightforward consequence of Gaussian concentration for Lipschitz functions.

Proposition 2.5

(cf.
[
16
]
)
.

There are constants
c,C\in(0,\infty)
such that for any of the mixed-
p
-spin models described in Assumption
1.1
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
and
t>0
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\ln W_{N}(x)-\mathbb{E}\left[\ln W_{N}(x)\right]\right|\geq\sqrt{N\ \widehat{\zeta}(1)}t\right)\leq C\exp\left(-ct^{2}\right),

and
\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\ln Z_{N}-\mathbb{E}\left[\ln Z_{N}\right]\right|\geq\sqrt{N\ \widehat{\zeta}(1)}t\right)\leq C\exp\left(-ct^{2}\right)
.

2.3
Structure of the order parameter and conjectures

Similarly as in (
2.13
) for the self-overlap corrected Gibbs measure, it is reasonable to believe that the self-overlap
r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})
asymptotically as
N\to\infty
concentrates with respect to the Gibbs measure corresponding to
Z_{N}
. In the classical case
b=0
, this is evident since the self-overlap is identically one,
\widehat{r}_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})(s,t)=1
for all
s,t\in[0,1)
. If this conjecture is true also in the quantum case, then the self-overlap coincides with its Gibbs average
\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})\rangle
, which is clearly invariant under time shifts in the path integral. More precisely, the integral kernel of the self-overlap,
\widehat{r}_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})(s,t)
, would coincide with its
time-averaged version. For a general Hilbert-Schmidt operator with kernel
\widehat{x}
, the time-averaged Hilbert-Schmidt operator
\overline{x}
is defined through its kernel

\widehat{\overline{x}}(s,t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{x}(s+\tau,t+\tau)\ d\tau.

(2.14)

Here and in the following, addition is understood on the unit torus, i.e. modulo one.
Any such operator is diagonal in the orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(0,1)
composed of sine and cosine functions, of which the constant
1\in L^{2}(0,1)
is a member.

The self-averaging of the self-overlap would have far reaching consequences. The outer supremum in (
2.2
) could be omitted by setting
\varrho=\overline{\varrho}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})\rangle\right]
. The infimum over
x
in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) could also be omitted by setting
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
the dual parameter, i.e.
x=\overline{x}
with
\langle\overline{x},\overline{\varrho}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\zeta(\overline{\varrho})+\zeta^{*}(2\overline{x})\right)
.

We furthermore conjecture that the remaining infimum over the Parisi order parameter can then be restricted. For the QSK, our final result supports this conjecture. In this case, the infimum can be restricted to the two-parameter family which is spanned by
\overline{\varrho}
and the rank-one projection onto the constant function:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}^{c}\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}\ \big|\ \mbox{there is $\kappa,\lambda:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ such that}\ \pi(m)=\kappa(m)\overline{\varrho}+\lambda(m)|1\rangle\langle 1|\;\right.\qquad

with
\kappa(0)=\lambda(0)=\lambda(1)=0
and
\kappa(1)=1

\displaystyle\left.\right\}.

This is along the lines of simplifications found in early replica-calculations
[
19
,
52
,
50
,
9
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

For the quantum Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model, for which
\zeta(\varrho)=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\|\varrho\|_{2}^{2}
:

\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,\overline{x}\right)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}^{c}}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,\overline{x}\right).

(2.15)

Using the spectral decomposition, we may rewrite
\overline{\varrho}=\overline{\varrho}^{\perp}+\langle 1,\overline{\varrho}\ 1\rangle\ |1\rangle\langle 1|
with the first term on the right collecting all eigenprojections orthogonal to the constant eigenfunction
1\in L^{2}(0,1)
. The function
\pi\in\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}^{c}
may thus be rewritten as

\pi(m)=\kappa(m)\ \overline{\varrho}^{\perp}+\widetilde{\lambda}(m)\ |1\rangle\langle 1|

with
\kappa,\widetilde{\lambda}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
monotone non-decreasing with
\kappa(0)=\widetilde{\lambda}(0)=0
and
\kappa(1)=1
and
\widetilde{\lambda}(1)=\langle 1,\overline{\varrho}\ 1\rangle
.
Theorem
2.6
is therefore similar in spirit to the analogous observation in the balanced Potts glass, where the Parisi order parameter also reflects the symmetry of the species
[
17
]
. This was recently proven
[
4
]
under the assumption of the self-averaging of the self-overlap. Our short proof of Theorem
2.6
is simpler than the one in
[
4
]
. As it stands, it however also crucially relies on the fact that
d\zeta(\varrho)=\beta^{2}\varrho
is linear in
\varrho
.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

The inclusion
\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}^{c}\subset\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}
, implies the ’
\leq
’ part of (
2.15
). For the reverse inequality ’
\geq
’, we

and set

\kappa(m)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\kappa\in[0,1]\ |\ \kappa\ \overline{\varrho}\leq\pi(m)\right\}.

This quantity is well defined for any
m\in[0,1]
, monotone-nondecreasing in
m
, and we have
\kappa(0)=0
since
\pi(0)=0
, and
\kappa(1)=1
since
\pi(1)=\overline{\varrho}
. We then define two independent
L^{2}
-valued, centred Gaussian processes
u_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}
indexed by the Ruelle probability cascade
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
corresponding to
\pi
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[u_{\alpha}u_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right]=d\zeta\left(\pi(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}})\right)-d\zeta\left(\kappa(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}})\overline{\varrho}\right),\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[v_{\alpha}v_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right]=d\zeta\left(\kappa(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}})\overline{\varrho}\right).

(2.16)

Thanks to the monotonicity of
d\zeta
established in Lemma

The above processes give rise to the decomposition
z_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha}+u_{\alpha}
of the orginal
L^{2}
-valued, centred Gaussian process corresponding to
\pi
and defined in (
1.20
).
Since the measure
\nu_{1}
is invariant under the shifts
\xi\mapsto\xi(\cdot+\tau)
for any
\tau\in(0,1)
, we may rewrite

\displaystyle X_{0}(\pi,\overline{x})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu_{\alpha}^{\pi}\int\exp\left(\langle u_{\alpha}(\cdot-\tau)+v_{\alpha}(\cdot-\tau),\xi\rangle+\langle\xi,\big(\overline{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta\left(\overline{\varrho}\right)\big)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)\ \right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu_{\alpha}^{\pi}\int\exp\left(\langle u_{\alpha}(\cdot-\tau),\xi\rangle+\langle v_{\alpha},\xi\rangle+\langle\xi,\big(\overline{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta\left(\overline{\varrho}\right)\big)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)\ \right].

(2.17)

Note that the translation invariance of
\overline{x}
and
\overline{\varrho}
imply that the quadratic terms in
\xi
in the exponential is unaffected by the shift of
\xi
.
This also implies that the distribution of the process
v_{\alpha}
equals that of
v_{\alpha}(\cdot-\tau)
for any
\tau\in(0,1)
, which results in the second equality.

We may now average
X_{0}(\pi,\overline{x})
over
\tau\in[0,1]

p\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu_{\alpha}^{\pi}\int\exp\left(p\langle u_{\alpha}(\cdot-\tau),\xi\rangle+(1-p)\langle u_{\alpha}(\cdot-\tau^{\prime}),\xi\rangle+\langle v_{\alpha},\xi\rangle+\langle\xi,\big(\overline{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta\left(\overline{\varrho}\right)\big)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)\ \right]

is convex for

\displaystyle X_{0}(\pi,\overline{x})\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu_{\alpha}^{\pi}\int\exp\left(\langle\overline{u}_{\alpha}+v_{\alpha},\xi\rangle+\langle\xi,\big(\overline{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta\left(\overline{\varrho}\right)\big)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)\right],\quad\overline{u}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}u_{\alpha}(\cdot-\tau)d\tau.

which is independent of

\pi_{c}(m)\coloneqq\kappa(m)\overline{\varrho}+\lambda(m)|1\rangle\langle 1|,\quad\mbox{with}\;\lambda(m)\coloneqq\langle 1,\pi(m)1\rangle-\kappa(m)\langle 1,\overline{\varrho}1\rangle.

Then
\pi_{c}\in\Pi_{\overline{\varrho}}^{c}

is Gaussian with
\mathbb{E}\left[w_{\alpha}w_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right]=d\zeta\left(\pi_{c}(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}})\right)
by the linearity of
d\zeta(\varrho)=\beta^{2}\varrho
.

Convexity of
\theta
implies

\theta(\pi(m))\geq\theta(\overline{\pi(m)})
for every

3
Approximation results

Our proofs of the Parisi formulae in Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
will proceed through approximating the quantum partition functions
Z_{N}
and
W_{N}(x)
by partition functions of classical vector-spin glasses. A key observation, already contained in
[
1
]
, is that the functional integral concentrates on paths with a limited number of jumps. This enables the comparison of
Z_{N}
to the partition function of suitably defined vector-spin glasses. In contrast to the Fourier basis used in
[
1
]
, we base the approximations of paths on square-wave pulses. This approximation has good monotonicity properties with regard to the correlation functionals. Moreover, it is closer to the philosophy used in the physics literature by which the single-spin process in the functional integral is related via the Trotter formula to a strongly coupled Ising system
[
45
]
.

In this section, we proceed to study the resulting approximation properties of the various main players in the Parisi formula: the partition function, the Parisi functional and its ingredient, the correlation functional.

3.1
Paths space and functional integral

In order to discretize paths, we use the a dyadic decomposition
t_{k}:=\frac{k}{2^{D}}
with
k=0,1,\dots,2^{D}
of the unit interval
(0,1]
into equidistant intervals
(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
of lengths
2^{-D}
with
D\in\mathbb{N}
the approximation parameter.
The square-wave pulses

e_{k}:=2^{D/2}1_{(t_{k-1},t_{k}]},\quad k=1,\dots,2^{D},

form an orthonormal set in
L^{2}(0,1)
. They span an increasing sequence of nested, finite-dimensional subspaces

\mathcal{V}_{D}:=\operatorname{span}\left\{e_{k}\ |\ k=1,\dots,2^{D}\right\}\subset\mathcal{V}_{D+1}\subset L^{2}(0,1).

Let
Q_{D}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{2^{D}}|e_{k}\rangle\langle e_{k}|
stand for the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{V}_{D}
. The projection of any
f\in L^{2}(0,1)
onto
\mathcal{V}_{N}
corresponds to the approximation of
f
by

f^{D}(s):=\left(Q_{D}f\right)(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{D}1_{(t_{k-1},t_{k}]}(s)\ f(k),\quad\mbox{with}\;f(k)\coloneqq 2^{D}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\!f(t)dt.

This approximation has the following elementary properties:

1.

If
f\in B_{\infty}^{2}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}(0,1)\ |\ \|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}
, then
f^{D}\in B_{\infty}^{2}
for any
D
. This in particular applies to the paths
\xi_{j}:[0,1]\to\{-1,1\}
.

2.

For any
f\in L^{2}(0,1)
:

\lim_{D\to\infty}\|\left(1-Q_{D}\right)f\|_{2}=0.

(3.1)

For continuous
f
this is evident by uniform continuity. The general case follows by the density of continuous functions in
L^{2}(0,1)
. By the boundedness of paths
\xi_{j}:[0,1]\to\{-1,1\}
for any
j
, this implies the convergence

\lim_{D\to\infty}\|\left(1-Q_{D}\right)\xi_{j}\|_{q}=0

(3.2)

in any
L^{q}
-norm with
q\in[1,\infty)
.

For a path
\xi_{j}:[0,1]\to\{-1,1\}
, the approximation
\xi_{j}^{D}
is encoded in the averages

\sigma_{j}(k):=2^{D}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\!\xi_{j}(t)dt\in[-1,1],\qquad\mbox{with $k=1,\dots,2^{D}$.}

(3.3)

Under the discrete averaging (
3.3
), the probability measure
\nu_{1}
on paths gets pushed forward to a probability measure
\nu_{1}^{D}
on the Borel measure space over
[-1,1]^{2^{D}}
.
The next crucial lemma shows that the measure
\nu_{1}
is in fact heavily concentrated on the projected paths.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
:

\nu_{1}\left(\left\{\|(1-Q_{D})\xi\|_{2}\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right)\leq\exp\left(-2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta be}\right)\right).

Proof.

An elementary computation shows

\|(1-Q_{D})\xi\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{D}\left[\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\!\xi(s)^{2}ds-2^{D}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\!\xi(s)ds\right)^{2}\right].

In case
\xi:[0,1]\to\{-1,1\}
is constant on
(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
, the term in the square-brackets vanishes. Otherwise this term is bounded from above by
2^{-D}
. Therefore the sum is estimated by

\|(1-Q_{D})\xi\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2^{-D}\eta_{1}(1,\omega_{1}).

in terms of the total number
\eta_{1}(1,\omega_{1})
of Poisson points on
[0,1)
. An exponential Chebychev-Markov estimate thus yields

\displaystyle\nu_{1}\left(\left\{\|(1-Q_{D})\xi\|_{2}\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}}\int\exp\left(\lambda 2^{-D}\eta_{1}(1,\omega_{1})\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{\cosh(\beta|h|)}{\cosh(\beta\sqrt{b^{2}+h^{2}})}\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\beta b)^{2k}}{(2k)!}\exp\left(\lambda 2^{-D}2k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}}\cosh\left(\beta be^{\lambda 2^{-D}}\right)\leq\exp\left(-2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta be}\right)\right).

This concludes the proof.
∎

3.2
Partition functions

The main goal in this subsection is to show that the partition function
Z_{N}
as well as its self-overlap corrected version
W_{N}(x)
is approximated by

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{D}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{D}(s)\right)ds\right)\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi}),

\displaystyle W_{N}^{D}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{D}(s)\right)ds-\frac{N}{2}\zeta\left(r_{N}(\bm{\xi}^{D},\bm{\xi}^{D})\right)+N\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi}^{D},\bm{\xi}^{D}),x\rangle\right)\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi}),

in which we projected the paths in the action functionals to the square-wave pulses
\bm{\xi}^{D}\coloneqq(Q_{D}\xi_{1},\dots,Q_{D}\xi_{N})
.

By construction,
Z_{N}^{D}
coincides with the partition function of a vector-spin glass with apriori measure
\nu_{1}^{D}
, i.e. the push-forward measure of
\nu_{1}
, on the
2^{D}
-component vectors
\left(\sigma_{1}(k)\right)_{k=1,\dots,2^{D}}\in[-1,1]^{2^{D}}
, and
W_{N}^{D}(x)
is a self-overlap corrected analogue.
The (classical) random energy on
N
such vector-spins is the Gaussian process with mean zero and covariance given by

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{(1,D)}(s)\right)ds\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{(2,D)}(t)\right)dt\right]\\
=N\zeta\left(r_{N}\big(\bm{\xi}^{(1,D)},\bm{\xi}^{(2,D)}\big)\right)=\frac{N}{2^{2D}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{D}}\widehat{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{(1)}(k)\sigma_{j}^{(2)}(k^{\prime})\right)

(3.4)

for two copies
\bm{\sigma}^{(1)},\bm{\sigma}^{(2)}
of
N
vector-spins with
2^{D}
components.
Panchenko’s proof
[
38
]
and, in particular, Chen’s simplifications
[
13
]
on the Parisi formula for the free energy of mixed
p
-spin models with vector spins hence become applicable. Similarly as in
[
1
]
, our proof is based on this observation and the following approximation result.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{D\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\ln Z_{N}-\ln Z_{N}^{D}\right]\right|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{D\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\ln W_{N}(x)-\ln W_{N}^{D}(x)\right]\right|=0.

Proof.

Corollary
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
below immediately yield the claim.
∎

To establish the ingredients of the proof, Corollary
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
, we largely follows the strategy in
[
1
]
with the Fourier bases exchanged by the square-wave pulses.
We first restrict the path measure
\nu_{N}
to paths
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N}):[0,1]\to\{-1,1\}^{N}
, whose empirical self-overlap is well approximated by the square-wave pulses

S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon):=\left\{\bm{\xi}\ \Bigg|\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\xi_{j},(1-Q_{D})\xi_{j}\rangle<\varepsilon\right\}.

(3.5)

The measure
\nu_{N}
heavily concentrate on this set.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
D,N\in\mathbb{N}
the complement of the set in (
3.5
) has exponentially small measure:

\nu_{N}\left(S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)^{c}\right)\leq\exp\left(-N\varphi_{D,x}^{*}(\varepsilon)\right),

(3.6)

with

\varphi_{D}^{*}(\varepsilon):=\sup_{t\geq 0}\left(t\varepsilon-\varphi_{D}(t)\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi_{D}(t):=\ln\int\exp\left(t\langle\xi|(1-Q_{D})\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi).

For any
\varepsilon,L>0
there is
D(\varepsilon,L)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi_{D}^{*}(\varepsilon)\geq 2L
for all
D\geq D(\varepsilon,L)
.

Proof.

The first assertion (
3.6
) is a standard concentration estimate based on the product nature of the measure. For an estimate of the Legendre transform
\varphi_{D}^{*}(\varepsilon)
, we use the convexity estimate
\varphi_{D}^{*}(\varepsilon)\geq t(\varepsilon-\varphi^{\prime}_{D}(t))
, which is valid for any
t\geq 0
, at
t_{0}=4L/\varepsilon
. By the dominated convergence theorem and (
3.1
), the derivative

\varphi^{\prime}_{D}(t)=\int\exp\left(t\langle\xi|(1-Q_{D})\xi\rangle\right)\langle\xi|(1-Q_{D})\xi\rangle\nu_{1}(d\xi)\Big/\int\exp\left(t\langle\xi|(1-Q_{D})\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)

converges pointwise to zero as
D\to\infty
. Consequently, for any
\varepsilon>0
and fixed
t_{0}\geq 0
there is
D(\varepsilon,t_{0})\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}_{D}(t_{0})<\varepsilon/2
for all
D\geq D(\varepsilon,t_{0})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The restricted partition functions

\displaystyle Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds\right)\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi}),

\displaystyle W_{N}(D,\varepsilon,x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds-\frac{N}{2}\zeta\left(r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})\right)\right)e^{N\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}),x\rangle}\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi})

are evidently a lower bound on
Z_{N}
respectively
W_{N}(x)
. The concentration, Lemma
3.3
, implies that this bound is exponentially sharp under a quenched average. The same applies to the respective partition functions

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{D}(s)\right)ds\right)\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi})\leq Z_{N}^{D},

\displaystyle W_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon,x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{D}(s)\right)ds-\frac{N}{2}\zeta\left(r_{N}\big(\bm{\xi}^{D},\bm{\xi}^{D}\big)\right)\right)e^{N\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi}^{D},\bm{\xi}^{D}),x\rangle}\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi})\leq W_{N}^{D}(x),

for the approximands.

Corollary 3.4
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is some
D(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
D\geq D(\varepsilon)
and all
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\ln Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon)-\ln Z_{N}\right|\right]\leq\frac{e^{-4N\widehat{\zeta}(1)}}{1-e^{-8N\widehat{\zeta}(1)}}.

(3.7)

The same bound applies to
Z_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon)
and
Z_{N}^{D}
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, there is some
D(\varepsilon,x)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
D\geq D(\varepsilon,x)
and all
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\ln W_{N}(D,\varepsilon,x)-\ln W_{N}(x)\right|\right]\leq\frac{e^{-3N\widehat{\zeta}(1)}}{1-e^{-8N\widehat{\zeta}(1)}}.

(3.8)

The same bound applies to
W_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon,x)
and
W_{N}^{D}(x)
.

Proof.

The left side in (
3.8
) is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\frac{Z_{N}}{Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon)^{-1}\int_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)^{c}}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds\right)\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{N}\left(S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)\right)}\int_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)^{c}}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\int\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}(s)\right)ds\ \widehat{\nu}_{N}^{D,\varepsilon}(d\widetilde{\bm{\xi}})-\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds\right)\right]\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle\leq e^{4N\widehat{\zeta}(1)}\ \frac{\nu_{N}\left(S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)^{c}\right)}{\nu_{N}\left(S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)\right)},

(3.9)

where
\widehat{\nu}_{N}^{D,\varepsilon}
abbreviates the conditional probability measure
\nu_{N}((\cdot)\ 1_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)})/\nu_{N}\left(S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)\right)
. In the above bound, the first inequality results from the elementary estimate
\ln(1+x)\leq x
, with
x=(Z_{N}-Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon))/Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon)
. The second inequality is obtained from lower bounding
Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon)
using Jensen’s inequality with respect to
\nu_{N}
.
Since the exponent in the second line is a Gaussian random variable with mean zero and covariance bounded by
4N\widehat{\zeta}(1)
, the third inequality follows. Lemma
3.3
, in which we choose
L=4\widehat{\zeta}(1)
, thus concludes the proof.

The same strategy yields the same upper bound for
Z_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon)
and
Z_{N}^{D}
.

For a bound on
\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\frac{W_{N}(x)}{W_{N}(D,\varepsilon,x)}\right]
, we proceed as in (
3.2
). In the second line, the exponent then also contains the term

\int\frac{N}{2}\zeta\left(r_{N}\big(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}},\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}\big)\right)\widehat{\nu}_{N}^{D,\varepsilon}(d\widetilde{\bm{\xi}})-\frac{N}{2}\zeta\left(r_{N}\big(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}\big)\right)\leq\frac{N}{2}\widehat{\zeta}(1),

and

N\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi}^{D},\bm{\xi}^{D}),x\rangle-\int N\langle r_{N}(\bm{\xi}^{D},\bm{\xi}^{D}),x\rangle\ \widehat{\nu}_{N}^{D,\varepsilon}(d\widetilde{\bm{\xi}})\leq 2N\|x\|_{2}\ \varepsilon.

The claim then again follows from Lemma
3.3
with
L=4\widehat{\zeta}(1)+2\|x\|_{2}\varepsilon
.

The bound on
\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\frac{W_{N}^{D}(x)}{W_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon,x)}\right]
follows similarly.
∎

The following final input for the proof of Proposition
3.2
is a Gaussian interpolation bound.

Proposition 3.5
.

There is some constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,
D,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\ln Z_{N}(D,\varepsilon)-\ln Z_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon)\right]\right|\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ N,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\ln W_{N}(D,\varepsilon,x)-\ln W_{N}^{D}(D,\varepsilon,x)\right]\right|\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ N,

Proof.

The proof is a standard Gaussian interpolation argument. For the first claim we control the derivative of

f(r)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\ln\int_{S_{N}^{D}(\varepsilon)}\exp\left(-\sqrt{r}\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}(s)\right)ds-\sqrt{1-r}\int_{0}^{1}U\left(\bm{\xi}^{D}(s)\right)ds\right)\nu_{N}(d\bm{\xi})\right]

(3.10)

Let
\langle\cdot\rangle_{r}
stand for the corresponding Gibbs expectation value of the interpolated action under the restricted path measure
\widehat{\nu}_{N}^{D,\varepsilon}
, and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{r}^{\otimes 2}
for the Gibbs expectation value on a duplicated system.
A straightforward differentiation followed by Gaussian integration by parts yields (cf.
[
37
, Ch. 1.2]
)

f^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\langle\zeta\left(r_{N}(1,1)\right)\rangle_{r}-\langle\zeta\left(r_{N}^{D}(1,1)\right)\rangle_{r}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\langle\zeta\left(r_{N}(1,2)\right)\rangle_{r}^{\otimes 2}-\langle\zeta\left(r_{N}^{D}(1,2)\right)\rangle_{r}^{\otimes 2}\right],

(3.11)

where we abbreviated the Hilbert-Schmidt integral operators
r_{N}(1,1)
,
r_{N}^{D}(1,1)
and
r_{N}(1,2)
,
r_{N}^{D}(1,2)
, which are defined through their kernels

\displaystyle\widehat{r}_{N}^{(D)}(1,1)(s,t)

\displaystyle:=N^{-1}\bm{\xi}^{(1)(D)}(s)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{(1)(D)}(t)

\displaystyle\widehat{r}_{N}^{(D)}(1,2)(s,t)

\displaystyle:=N^{-1}\bm{\xi}^{(1)(D)}(s)\cdot{\bm{\xi}}^{(2)(D)}(t),

and
\bm{\xi}^{(1)}
respectively
\bm{\xi}^{(2)}
refer to the paths in the first respectively second copy of the system. Since
\widehat{\zeta}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
was assumed to be Lipschitz continuous with constant
L<\infty
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\zeta\left(r_{N}(1,2)\right)-\zeta\left(r_{N}^{D}(1,2)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq L\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|N^{-1}\bm{\xi}^{(1)}(s)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{(2)}(t)-N^{-1}\bm{\xi}^{(1)D}(s)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{(2)D}(t)\right|dsdt

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|r_{N}(1,2)-Q_{D}r_{N}(1,2)Q_{D}\right\|_{2}\leq 2L\left\|(1-Q_{D})r_{N}(1,2)\right\|_{2},

where the last step is the triangle inequality for the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.
These quantities are bounded using

\displaystyle\left\|(1-Q_{D})r_{N}(1,2)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\left|\langle\xi_{j}^{(1)}|(1-Q_{D})\xi_{k}^{(2)}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\xi_{j}^{(1)}|(1-Q_{D})\xi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle\xi_{k}^{(2)}|(1-Q_{D})\xi_{k}^{(2)}\rangle}\leq\varepsilon,

where the second inequality is a combination of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Jensen’s inequality, and the last inequality is valid due to the restriction of the support of the measures to the set in (
3.5
).
The difference
\left|\zeta\left(r_{N}(1,1)\right)-\zeta\left(r_{N}^{D}(1,1)\right)\right|
is estimated similarly. Therefore, the derivative
|f^{\prime}(r)|
is bounded by a constant, which is independent of
D
, times
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. This completes the proof of the first assertion.

For the second assertion we proceed analogously. Modifying the exponentials in (
3.10
) by the respective self-overlap correction, results in the cancellation of the first two terms in (
3.11
) of the respective derivative. Proceeding analogously as above for the remaining terms yields the claim.
∎

3.3
Correlation functional

Our approximation argument will also rely on approximations of the quantum Parisi functional in terms of their projections onto the subspace of square-wave pulses. In this section, we will set the stage and consider the space

\mathcal{D}_{D}:=\left\{\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}=\mathbb{R}^{2^{D}\times 2^{D}}\,\big|\,\varrho\geq 0,\;\left|\varrho_{k,k^{\prime}}\right|\leq 1\right\}

of non-negative
2^{D}\times 2^{D}
matrices
\mathcal{S}_{2,D}^{+}
, whose elements are bounded by one. It will be identified with the projection of
\mathcal{D}
onto the subspace of square-wave pulses, i.e.
Q_{D}\varrho\ Q_{D}
for
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
. This is accomplished by the embedding

\displaystyle I_{D}:\

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2,D}\to\mathcal{S}_{2},\qquad I_{D}(\varrho)(s,s^{\prime})=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{D}}1_{(t_{k-1},t_{k}]}(s)1_{(t_{k^{\prime}-1},t_{k^{\prime}}]}(s^{\prime})\,\varrho_{k,k^{\prime}},

(3.12)

which has the following elementary properties, which follow by a computation directly from the definition:

1.

I_{D}(\varrho)=Q_{D}I_{D}(\varrho)Q_{D}
for any
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}

2.

Equipping
\mathcal{S}_{2,D}
with the scalar product

\langle x,\varrho\rangle_{D}:=\frac{1}{2^{2D}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{D}}x_{k,k^{\prime}}\varrho_{k^{\prime},k},

the embedding preserves the scalar product, i.e.
\langle x,\varrho\rangle_{D}=\langle I_{D}(x),I_{D}(\varrho)\rangle
for all
x,\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}
.

3.

\displaystyle\|\widehat{(I_{D}x)}\|_{q}^{q}=\frac{1}{2^{2D}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{D}}|x_{k,k^{\prime}}|^{q}
for any
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}
and
q>0
.

We will also use the inverse of
I_{D}
on its range, which coincides with the projection

\displaystyle J_{D}:\

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}\to\mathcal{S}_{2,D},\qquad(J_{D}\varrho)_{k,k^{\prime}}\coloneqq 2^{2D}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}-1}}^{t_{k^{\prime}}}\varrho(s,s^{\prime})\ dsds^{\prime},\quad k,k^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,2^{D}\},

(3.13)

for which:

1.

J_{D}\circ I_{D}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{S}_{2,D}}
,

2.

J_{D}(\varrho)=J_{D}(Q_{D}\varrho Q_{D})
for all
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

The restriction of the functional
\zeta:\mathcal{D}\to[0,\infty)
to
\mathcal{D}_{D}
is

\zeta_{D}(\varrho)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{2D}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{2D}}\widehat{\zeta}(\varrho_{k,k^{\prime}}).

(3.14)

It trivially extends from
\mathcal{D}
to
\mathcal{S}_{2,D}^{+}
. Its Legendre transform is defined on
2^{D}\times 2^{D}
matrices
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}^{+}
:

\zeta_{D}^{*}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}^{+}}\left(\langle x,\varrho\rangle_{D}-\zeta_{D}(\varrho)\right).

(3.15)

The following lemma summarises the relation of these functionals to the correlation functional
\zeta
and its Legendre transform
\zeta^{*}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

1.

For any
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
and
D\in\mathbb{N}
:

\zeta(Q_{D}\varrho Q_{D})\leq\zeta(Q_{D+1}\varrho Q_{D+1})\leq\zeta(\varrho).

(3.16)

For any
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
:
\zeta(Q_{D}\varrho\ Q_{D})=\zeta_{D}(J_{D}(\varrho))
.

2.

For any
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
and any
D\in\mathbb{N}
:
\zeta^{*}(x)\geq\zeta_{D}^{*}(J_{D}(x))
and:

\zeta^{*}\left(Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)=\zeta_{D}^{*}(J_{D}(x)).

(3.17)

Moreover:
\zeta^{*}\left(Q_{D+1}xQ_{D+1}\right)\geq\zeta^{*}\left(Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)
.

3.

For any
\varrho=Q_{D}\varrho Q_{D}\in\mathcal{D}
and
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
:
\langle d\zeta(\varrho),x\rangle=\langle d\zeta(\varrho),Q_{D}xQ_{D}\rangle
.

For any
\varrho_{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{D}
and
x_{D}\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}
:

\langle d\zeta_{D}(\varrho_{D}),x_{D}\rangle_{D}=\langle d\zeta\left(I_{D}(\varrho_{D})\right),I_{D}(x_{D})\rangle.

Proof.

1. The monotonicity (
3.16
) follows from the convexity of
\zeta
and the fact that
Q_{D}\varrho\ Q_{D}
results from
Q_{D+1}\varrho\ Q_{D+1}
by averaging. The second claim is by straightforward computation using (
3.12
).

2. For any
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
and
\varrho=Q_{D}\varrho\ Q_{D}
, we have
\langle x,\varrho\rangle=\langle Q_{D}xQ_{D},Q_{D}\varrho Q_{D}\rangle=\langle J_{D}(x),J_{D}(\varrho)\rangle_{D}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\zeta^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}\\
\varrho=Q_{D}\varrho Q_{D}\end{subarray}}\left(\langle x,\varrho\rangle-\zeta(\varrho)\right)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}\\
\varrho=Q_{D}\varrho\ Q_{D}\end{subarray}}\left(\langle J_{D}(x),J_{D}(\varrho)\rangle_{D}-\zeta(\varrho)\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varrho_{D}\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D}^{+}}\left(\langle J_{D}(x),\varrho_{D}\rangle_{D}-\zeta_{D}(\varrho_{D})\right)=\zeta_{D}^{*}(J_{D}(x)),

where we used the first part of the lemma in the last line. This finishes the proof of the first assertion. The second assertion follows from
\zeta(\varrho)\geq\zeta(Q_{D}\varrho\ Q_{D})
established in 1., which leads to

\zeta_{D}^{*}\left(J_{D}(x)\right)=\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\langle Q_{D}\varrho\ Q_{D},x\rangle-\zeta(Q_{D}\varrho Q_{D})\right]\geq\sup_{\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\langle\varrho,Q_{D}xQ_{D}\rangle-\zeta(\varrho)\right]=\zeta^{*}\left(Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right),

which, together with the first part, concludes the proof of (
3.17
). The last assertion follows from the above using
Q_{D}Q_{D+1}=Q_{D}
.

3. The operator
d\zeta(\varrho)
representing the Gateaux derivative satisfies
d\zeta(\varrho)=Q_{D}d\zeta(\varrho)Q_{D}
for
\varrho=Q_{D}\varrho Q_{D}
.
Therefore

\langle d\zeta(\varrho),x\rangle=\langle Q_{D}d\zeta(\varrho)Q_{D},x\rangle=\langle d\zeta(\varrho),Q_{D}xQ_{D}\rangle.

The Gateaux derivative of
\zeta_{D}
is given by
d\zeta_{D}(\varrho_{D})=\sum_{p\geq 1}\beta_{2p}^{2}\varrho_{D}^{\odot 2p-1}
in terms of the (ordinary) Hadamard products of the matrix
\varrho_{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{D}
.
Consequently,

\langle d\zeta_{D}(\varrho_{D}),x_{D}\rangle_{D}=\sum_{p\geq 1}\beta_{2p}^{2}\langle\varrho_{D}^{\odot 2p-1},x_{D}\rangle_{D}=\sum_{p\geq 1}\beta_{2p}^{2}\langle I_{D}\big(\varrho_{D}^{\odot 2p-1}\big),I_{D}(x_{D})\rangle=\langle d\zeta\left(I_{D}(\varrho_{D})\right),I_{D}(x_{D})\rangle,

where the last step used
I_{D}\big(\varrho_{D}^{\odot 2p-1}\big)=I_{D}\big(\varrho_{D}\big)^{\odot 2p-1}
.
∎

The above lemma in particular guarantees that the functional
\theta_{D}:\mathcal{S}_{2,D}^{+}\to[0,\infty)
defines by

\theta_{D}(\varrho_{D})\coloneqq\langle d\zeta_{D}(\varrho_{D}),\varrho_{D}\rangle_{D}-\zeta_{D}(\varrho_{D})=\theta\left(I_{D}(\varrho_{D})\right)

(3.19)

agrees with the restriction of
\theta
to the projected values.

3.4
Quantum Parisi functional

A crucial step in the proof of our main results is the approximation of the quantum Parisi functional in terms of the Parisi functional of the vector-spin model, which corresponds to the projection onto square-wave pulses. The Lipschitz continuity, Proposition
1.5
, is a basic tool for this. It immediately implies the following lemma, which will be used in the proofs of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\lim_{D\to\infty}\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}xQ_{D})=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(\pi,x).

(3.20)

For any
\varrho\in\mathcal{D}
:

\liminf_{D\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}xQ_{D})-\langle Q_{D}xQ_{D},\varrho\rangle\geq\inf_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}}\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)-\langle x,\varrho\rangle.

(3.21)

Proof.

By the Lipschitz continuity, Proposition
1.5
,

\left|\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}xQ_{D})-\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},x)\right|\leq\|Q_{D}xQ_{D}-x\|_{2}.

From (
3.1
) we know that for every
y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\lim_{D\to\infty}\left\|Q_{D}yQ_{D}-y\right\|_{2}=0.

(3.22)

Enlarging the set over which the infimum is taken by dropping the projections, we conclude

\displaystyle\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},x)\geq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(\pi,x).

For a complementary upper bound, we recall from Proposition
1.6
that the infimum is finite. For
\varepsilon>0
we may thus pick
\pi_{\varepsilon}\in\Pi
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)+\varepsilon\geq\mathcal{P}(\pi_{\varepsilon},x)\geq\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi_{\varepsilon}Q_{D},x)-L\int_{0}^{1}\|Q_{D}\pi_{\varepsilon}(m)Q_{D}-\pi_{\varepsilon}(m)\|_{2}dm,

where the last inequality is by Proposition
1.5
.
The last integral converges to zero as
D\to\infty
by (
3.22
) and the dominated convergence theorem.
Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this finishes the proof of (
3.20
).

The proof of (
3.21
) proceeds analogously to the first step above. The Lipschitz continuity in
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, Proposition
1.5
, controls

\left|\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}xQ_{D})-\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},x)+\langle x-Q_{D}xQ_{D},\varrho\rangle\right|\leq 2\|Q_{D}xQ_{D}-x\|_{2}.

Elarging the set, over which the infimum over
\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}
is taken, yields the estimate
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}}\mathcal{P}(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},x)\geq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\varrho}}\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)
, which completes the proof of (
3.21
).
∎

Due to the additional presence of the supremum over
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
, the proof of Theorem
2.1
will rely on a more subtle approximation analysis. To prepare this, we again restrict the path measure
\nu_{1}
to the set

S^{D}(\varepsilon):=\left\{\|(1-Q_{D})\xi\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

For
\pi\in\Pi
and
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we consider the restricted functionals

X_{0}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}\int_{S^{D}(\varepsilon)}\exp\left(\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}\rangle+\langle\xi,\left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta(\pi(1)\right)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)\right],

(3.23)

as well as
\mathcal{P}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon):=X_{0}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\theta(\pi(m))dm
.

The analogue of Proposition
3.2
on the level of the quantum Parisi functional is the following.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
with integral kernel
\widehat{x}
, any
\varepsilon>0
and all
D\in\mathbb{N}
:

\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}\left|\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)-\mathcal{P}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon)\right|\leq\exp\left(4N_{\zeta}+\|\widehat{x}\|_{1}-2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta be}\right)\right),

(3.24)

where we recall
N_{\zeta}=\sum_{p\geq 1}2p\beta_{2p}^{2}
.

Proof.

Since the proof proceeds analogously as that of Proposition
3.2
, we will only highlight the differences.
We abbreviate the random action

\mathcal{A}(\alpha,\xi;\pi,x):=\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}\rangle+\langle\xi,\left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta(\pi(1)\right)\xi\rangle,

and estimate the left side in (
3.24
) for any
\pi\in\Pi
with
r
steps by

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}\int_{S^{D}(\varepsilon)^{c}}\exp\left(\mathcal{A}(\alpha,\xi;\pi,x)\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)}{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}\int_{S^{D}(\varepsilon)}\exp\left(\mathcal{A}(\alpha,\xi;\pi,x)\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)}\right]\\
\leq\nu_{1}\left(S^{D}(\varepsilon)\right)^{-1}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}\int_{S^{D}(\varepsilon)^{c}}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\mathcal{A}(\alpha,\xi;\pi,x)-\langle\mathcal{A}(\cdot;\pi,x)\rangle_{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\ \nu_{1}(d\xi)\qquad

(3.25)

where we used the normalization
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}=1
and Jensen’s inequality for the probability measure

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}\int_{S^{D}(\varepsilon)}(\cdot)\ \overline{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}(d\xi)=:\langle(\cdot)\rangle_{\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\overline{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\cdot):=\nu_{1}\left(S^{D}(\varepsilon)\right)^{-1}\nu_{1}(\cdot).

The non-random terms in the exponential in the right side of (
3.25
) involving
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
and
\pi(1)
are bounded from above by
0\leq\langle\xi,x\xi\rangle\leq\|\widehat{x}\|_{1}
and
0\leq\langle\langle\xi,d\xi(\pi(1))\xi\rangle\rangle_{\varepsilon}\leq N_{\zeta}
by Lemma
1.2
. The random terms in the exponential in the right side of (
3.25
) is the Gaussian random variable
\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}\rangle-\langle\langle\cdot,z_{\cdot}\rangle\rangle_{\varepsilon}
. The latter is centered and has a covariance, which is bounded as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}\rangle-\langle\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}\rangle\rangle_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right]\leq 2\ \mathbb{E}\left[\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\right]+2\ \mathbb{E}\left[\langle\langle\cdot,z_{\cdot}\rangle\rangle_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\ \langle\xi,d\zeta(\pi(1))\xi\rangle+2\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha}\mu^{\pi}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{S^{D}(\varepsilon)}\int_{S^{D}(\varepsilon)}\langle\xi,d\zeta(\pi(\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}))\xi^{\prime}\rangle\ \overline{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}(d\xi)\overline{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}(d\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq 2\ N_{\zeta}+2\left\|d\zeta(\pi(1))\right\|\leq 4\ N_{\zeta}.

The estimate in the last line is again from Lemma
1.2
.
The proof is concluded by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Proposition
3.8
is key in the following lemma, which yields an alternative control over differences compared to just the Lipschitz continuity, Proposition
1.5
. The benefit here is the uniformity of the approximation
D\to\infty
as long as a norm
\|\widehat{x}\|_{q}
with
q>1
of the integral kernel of
x
can be controlled.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
with integral kernel
\widehat{x}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, any
D\in\mathbb{N}
, all
p\geq 2
and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
:

\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}\left|\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)-\mathcal{P}(\pi,Q_{D}xQ_{D})\right|\leq 2\exp\left(4N_{\zeta}+\|\widehat{x}\|_{1}-2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{2^{D}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta be}\right)\right)+4^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}\left\|\widehat{x}\right\|_{q}.

Proof.

We pick
\varepsilon>0
and use the triangle inequality to estimate

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)-\mathcal{P}(\pi,Q_{D}xQ_{D})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathcal{P}(\pi,x)-\mathcal{P}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon)\right|+\left|\mathcal{P}(\pi,Q_{D}xQ_{D})-\mathcal{P}(\pi,Q_{D}xQ_{D};D,\varepsilon)\right|+\left|\mathcal{P}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon)-\mathcal{P}(\pi,Q_{D}xQ_{D};D,\varepsilon)\right|.

The first and second term on the right side is estimated using Proposition
3.8
and the bound

\|\widehat{(Q_{D}xQ_{D})}\|_{1}\leq\|\widehat{x}\|_{1}

on the
L^{1}
-norm integral of the kernel of the projection, see also (
3.27
) below. To bound the last term, we note that for
\xi\in S^{D}(\varepsilon)
by the triangle inequality and a subsequent application of Hölder’s inequality with
p\geq 2
on
L^{2}\left((0,1)^{2}\right)
:

\displaystyle\left|\langle\xi,x\xi\rangle-\langle\xi,Q_{D}xQ_{D}\xi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle(1-Q_{D})\xi,x\xi\rangle\right|+\left|\langle Q_{D}\xi,x(1-Q_{D})\xi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\xi\|_{p}+\|Q_{D}\xi\|_{p}\right)\|(1-Q_{D})\xi\|_{p}\|\widehat{x}\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2\ 2^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\ \|(1-Q_{D})\xi\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{p}}\|\widehat{x}\|_{q}\leq 4^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}\|\widehat{x}\|_{q}.

This implies

\left|\mathcal{P}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon)-\mathcal{P}(\pi,Q_{D}xQ_{D};D,\varepsilon)\right|=\left|X_{0}(\pi,Q_{D}xQ_{D};D,\varepsilon)-X_{0}(\pi,x;D,\varepsilon)\right|\leq 4^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}\|\widehat{x}\|_{q},

which completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma shows that when restricting the outer supremum to a subset of the form

\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}\ |\ \left\|\widehat{x}\right\|_{q}\leq R\right\}
,
where
\widehat{x}
is the integral kernel of
x
, one may effectively pull the limit
D\to\infty
and obtain a convergence result similar to Lemma
3.7
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
R>0
and
q\in(1,2]
be arbitrary. Every sequence
D\to\infty
has a subsequence
D_{n}\to\infty
such that

\limsup_{D_{n}\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)}\left[\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D_{n}}\pi Q_{D_{n}},Q_{D_{n}}xQ_{D_{n}}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2Q_{D_{n}}xQ_{D_{n}}\right)\right]\leq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2x\right)\right]

Moreover:

\quad\displaystyle\liminf_{D\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)\right]\geq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2x\right)\right]
.

Proof.

In this proof, we abbreviate the functional

\mathcal{K}(x):=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,x\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2x\right),\quad x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}.

Lower bound:
We let
\varepsilon>0
, and pick
x_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
such that

\mathcal{K}\left(x_{\varepsilon}\right)\geq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\mathcal{K}(x)-\varepsilon
.
This is possible since the supremum in the right side is finite by Proposition
1.6
. The pre-limit in the right side of the assertion is trivially lower bounded for any
D
:

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\left[\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}x_{\varepsilon}Q_{D}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2Q_{D}x_{\varepsilon}Q_{D}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}x_{\varepsilon}Q_{D}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2x_{\varepsilon}\right),

where the last step follows from Lemma
3.6
, which guarantees
\xi^{*}\left(2Q_{D}x_{\varepsilon}Q_{D}\right)\leq\xi^{*}\left(2x_{\varepsilon}\right)
.
We now use Lemma
3.7
to conclude

\liminf_{D\to\infty}\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}x_{\varepsilon}Q_{D}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2x_{\varepsilon}\right)=\mathcal{K}\left(x_{\varepsilon}\right),

(3.26)

and hence the claim, since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary.

Upper bound:
We first fix
D_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, and suppose without loss of generality that
D\geq D_{0}
. We employ Lemma
3.9
and the fact that for any
p\in[1,\infty)

\|\widehat{(Q_{D}xQ_{D})}\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{j,k=1}^{2^{D}}2^{-2D}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\widehat{x}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})ds^{\prime}dt^{\prime}\ \operatorname{1}_{(t_{j-1},t_{j}]}(s)\operatorname{1}_{(t_{k-1},t_{k}]}(t)\Big|^{p}dsdt\leq\|\widehat{x}\|_{p}^{p}

(3.27)

by Jensen’s inequality. This guarantees that for any
D\geq D_{0}
:

\displaystyle\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},Q_{D_{0}}xQ_{D_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\exp\left(4N_{\zeta}+\|\widehat{x}\|_{1}-2^{D_{0}}\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{2^{D_{0}}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta be}\right)\right)+4^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}\left\|\widehat{x}\right\|_{q}.

(3.28)

Since
x\in\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)
and hence
\|\widehat{x}\|_{1}\leq\|\widehat{x}\|_{q}\leq R
, the two terms in the last line are arbitrarily small if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough and
D_{0}>0
is large enough.
Since for
D\geq D_{0}
, we also have
\xi^{*}\left(2Q_{D}xQ_{D}\right)\geq\xi^{*}\left(2Q_{D_{0}}xQ_{D_{0}}\right)
by Lemma
3.6
. It hence remains to bound the sequence of functionals

\mathcal{K}_{D}(x^{D_{0}})\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi Q_{D},x^{D_{0}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2x^{D_{0}}\right)\quad\mbox{defined on $x^{D_{0}}:=Q_{D_{0}}xQ_{D_{0}}$.}

By the finiteness of the supremum, for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
D\geq D_{0}
, there is
x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\in\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)}\mathcal{K}_{D}(x^{D_{0}})\leq\mathcal{K}_{D}(x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D))+\varepsilon.

Via
J_{D_{0}}
in (
3.13
) and
I_{D_{0}}
in (
3.12
), the sequence
\left(x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\right)_{D\geq D_{0}}
is bijectively identified with the sequence
\left(\big(x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\big)_{k,k^{\prime}}\right)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2^{D_{0}}\times 2^{D_{0}}}
. Moreover, since
x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\in\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)
, by (
3.27
) we have

\frac{1}{2^{2D_{0}}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{D_{0}}}\left|\big(x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\big)_{k,k^{\prime}}\right|^{q}=\left\|\widehat{(Q_{D_{0}}x_{\varepsilon}(D)(Q_{D_{0}})}\right\|_{q}^{q}\leq\left\|\widehat{x_{\varepsilon}(D)}\right\|_{q}^{q}\leq R^{q}.

By compactness and the equivalence of norms in finite-dimensional vector spaces, there is some
\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}\in\mathcal{S}_{2,D_{0}}^{+}
and a subsequence
D_{n}\to\infty
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D_{n})-\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}\|_{2}=0.

We now pick
\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}})\in\Pi
such that

\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}\left(\pi,\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}\right)\geq\mathcal{P}\left(\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}),\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}\right)-\varepsilon.

We have thus established the bound

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)}\mathcal{K}_{D}(x^{D_{0}})\leq\mathcal{K}_{D}(x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D))+\varepsilon\leq\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}})Q_{D},x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{*}\left(2x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\right)+\varepsilon,

with
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary, where in the second inequality the infimum is estimated by the choice
\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}})
.
We now use the Lipschitz continuity, Proposition
1.5
, of the Parisi functional to further estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\left(Q_{D}\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}})Q_{D},x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{P}\left(\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}),\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\ +L\left(\|x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D)-\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{1}\left\|Q_{D}\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}})Q_{D}-\pi_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}})\right\|_{2}dm\right).

Along the subsequence
D_{n}\to\infty
, the last terms on the right vanish. By the lower semicontinuity of
\zeta^{*}
, we also have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\xi^{*}\left(2x_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}(D_{n})\right)\geq\xi^{*}\left(2\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}}\right).

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we thus arrive at the following bound on the sub-sequential upper limit:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{q}^{+}(R)}\mathcal{K}_{D_{n}}(x^{D_{0}})\leq\mathcal{K}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}^{D_{0}})\leq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}}\mathcal{K}(x).

The proof of the upper bound is completed by taking first
D_{0}\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
in the terms on the second line of (
3.4
).
∎

Appendix A
Hadamard-Schur product of certain integral operators

In this appendix, we extend the well known properties of the Hadamard-Schur product of matrices
[
6
,
24
]
to certain integral operators.
We consider integral operators
\varrho
on the complex Hilbert space
L^{2}(0,1)
with a bounded, measurable kernel
\widehat{\varrho}:(0,1)^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
. Such integral operators are automatically Hilbert-Schmidt and hence bounded.
Their Hadamard-Schur product
\varrho\odot\pi
is the bounded integral operator on
f\in L^{2}(0,1)
:

\left(\varrho\odot\pi\ f\right)(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(t,s)\ \widehat{\pi}(t,s)\ f(s)ds.

(A.1)

A.1
Positivity and monotonicity

We start by recording the analogue of the positivity of the Hadamard-Schur product of matrices.

Proposition A.1
.

If
\varrho,\pi\geq 0
are non-negative integral operators on
L^{2}(0,1)
with bounded kernels, so is their Hadamard-Schur product
\varrho\odot\pi\geq 0
.

Proof.

The Hadamard-Schur product is related to the tensor product
\varrho\otimes\pi
defined on
L^{2}(0,1)\otimes L^{2}(0,1)
. More specifically, by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem

\langle f,\varrho\odot\pi\ f\rangle=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\ \langle f_{r}^{(2)},\varrho\otimes\pi f_{r}^{(2)}\rangle.

(A.2)

where, for future purpose, we define for
k\geq 2
arbitrary:

f^{k}_{r}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2r)^{k-1}}\prod_{l=2}^{k}1[|t_{1}-t_{l}|<r]\ \prod_{j=1}^{k}f(t_{j})^{1/k}.

(A.3)

This function is in the
k
-fold tensor product space
L^{2}(0,1)^{\otimes k}
.
The non-negativity thus immediately follows from that of the tensor product
\varrho\otimes\pi
and (
A.2
).
∎

Iterating (
A.1
), one defines the
k
-fold Hadamard-Schur product
\varrho^{\odot k}
on
L^{2}(0,1)
for arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
\varrho\geq 0
, then
\varrho^{\odot k}\geq 0
by the above proposition.
We also need the monotonicity of this operation, which is again well known in the matrix case (cf.
[
6
,
24
]
).

Proposition A.2
.

If
\varrho\geq\pi\geq 0
are non-negative integral operators on
L^{2}(0,1)
with bounded kernels, then
\varrho^{\odot k}\geq\pi^{\odot k}\geq 0
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

A straightforward generalization of (
A.2
) yields the following relation to the
k
-fold tensor product
\varrho^{\otimes k}
:

\langle f,\varrho^{\odot k}f\rangle=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\ \langle f_{r}^{(f)},\varrho^{\otimes k}f_{r}^{(k)}\rangle.

with
f_{r}^{(k)}
from (
A.3
). The claim follows from the fact that
\varrho\geq\pi\geq 0
implies
\varrho^{\otimes k}\geq\pi^{\otimes k}\geq 0
.
∎

A.2
Properties of correlation functionals

In our context, we mostly encounter the expectation value

\zeta_{k}(\varrho)\coloneqq\langle 1,\varrho^{\odot k}1\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(t,s)^{k}dtds

(A.4)

of the
k
-fold Hadamard-Schur product of a non-negative Hilbert Schmidt operator
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
in the normalized vector
1\in L^{2}(0,1)
.
Note that although initially only defined in case of bounded integral kernels
\widehat{\varrho}
, the right side in (
A.4
) does make sense for any
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
with
\widehat{\varrho}\in L^{2}\left((0,1)^{2}\right)\cap L^{k}\left((0,1)^{2}\right)
.

We start recording elementary properties of the above functional.

Lemma A.3
.

On the space of non-negative Hilbert-Schmidt operators with a bounded integral kernel the functional
\zeta_{k}
is for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

1.

non-negative and monotone, i.e.
0\ \leq\zeta_{k}(\pi)\leq\zeta_{k}(\varrho)
for any
0\leq\pi\leq\varrho
,

2.

continuous, i.e. for any
\varrho,\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
with bounded integral kernels
\widehat{\varrho},\widehat{\pi}
with
R\coloneqq\max\left\{\|\widehat{\varrho}\|_{\infty},\|\widehat{\pi}\|_{\infty}\right\}
:

\left|\zeta_{k}(\varrho)-\zeta_{k}(\pi)\right|\leq kR^{k-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)-\widehat{\pi}(s,t)\right|dsdt\leq kR^{k-1}\left\|\varrho-\pi\right\|_{2}.

(A.5)

Proof.

The monotonicity is by Proposition
A.2
. The continuity
follows from the elementary inequality
\left|x^{k}-y^{k}\right|\leq k\max\{|x|,|y|\}^{k-1}|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

To further analyze the functionals
\zeta_{k}(\varrho)
, it will be useful to recall the spectral representation of the intergral kernel,

\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\widehat{\varrho}_{N}(s,t),\quad\widehat{\varrho}_{N}(s,t)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}(s)\overline{\varphi_{j}(t)},

(A.6)

in terms of the eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j})\subset\mathbb{R}
and the corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions
(\varphi_{j})\subset L^{2}(0,1)
of
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
The convergence in (
A.6
) is in
L^{2}\left((0,1)^{2}\right)
, and hence pointwise for a.e.
(s,t)\in(0,1)^{2}
along a subsequence.
As was already alluded to in the introduction, more is true in case
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
with a bounded kernel.

Proposition A.4
.

For any
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{+}
with a bounded kernel
\widehat{\varrho}\in L^{\infty}\left((0,1)^{2}\right)
, the integral kernel admits a bounded extension
\widehat{\varrho}(t,t)
to the diagonal given by (
1.8
) for which

1.

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a.e.
t\in(0,1)
:
\displaystyle\widehat{\varrho}_{N}(t,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}|\varphi_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\widehat{\varrho}(t,t)
.

Consequently, the eigenfunctions
(\varphi_{j})
corresponding to non-zero eigenvalues are bounded.

2.

for a.e.
(s,t)\in(0,1)^{2}
:
\displaystyle|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)|\leq\sqrt{\widehat{\varrho}(s,s)\ \widehat{\varrho}(t,t)}
,  and
{\operatorname{Tr}\,}\varrho=\int\widehat{\varrho}(t,t)dt
.

3.

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
:
\displaystyle\zeta_{k}(\varrho)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(t,s)^{k}\ dtds=\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{j_{k}}\left|\langle 1,\varphi_{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{j_{k}}\rangle\right|^{2}
.

Proof.

The proofs of 1. and 2. can be found in
[
51
]
. Note that the bound in 1. indeed guarantees
that
\lambda_{j}|\varphi_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\widehat{\varrho}(t,t)
for a.e.
t\in(0,1)
and all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, the scalar products

\langle 1,\varphi_{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{j_{k}=1}\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{j_{1}}(t)\cdots\varphi_{j_{k}}(t)dt

are all finite.
We now approximate
\varrho
by truncating its spectral representation at
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\varrho_{N}
be the finite-rank operator with integral kernel given by (
A.6
).
By construction
0\leq\varrho_{N}\leq\varrho
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\varrho_{N}-\varrho\|_{2}=0
. Consequently, using (
A.5
) and the bound in 2. on the kernels,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\zeta_{k}(\varrho_{N})=\zeta_{k}(\varrho),\qquad 0\leq\zeta_{k}(\varrho_{N})\leq\zeta_{k}(\varrho),

where the last inequality is by Proposition
A.2
.
By the finiteness of the scalar products, an elementary computation based on an application of Fubini’s theorem yields for any
N\in\mathbb{N}

\zeta_{k}(\varrho_{N})=\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=1}^{N}\lambda_{j_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{j_{k}}\left|\langle 1,\varphi_{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{j_{k}}\rangle\right|^{2}.

(A.7)

By monotone convergence, the right side converges to the right side in the assertion as
N\to\infty
. This completes the proof.
∎

Our final result concerns the extension of the functional to
\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\widetilde{\zeta}_{2p}:\mathcal{S}_{2}\to[0,\infty],\quad\widetilde{\zeta}_{2p}(\varrho)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)^{2p}dsdt.

Note that the integral is well-defined, potentially infinite, for all
\widehat{\varrho}\in L^{2}\left((0,1)^{2}\right)
.

Proposition A.5
.

The functional
\widetilde{\zeta}_{2p}:\mathcal{S}_{2}\to[0,\infty]
is weakly lower semicontinuous for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We pick
\varrho_{n},\varrho\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle x,\varrho_{n}\rangle=\langle x,\varrho\rangle
for all
x\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Let
R>0
be arbitrary, and pick
x_{R}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
with integral kernel

\widehat{x}_{R}(s,t)\coloneqq e^{i\arg\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)}\left|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)\right|^{2(p-1)}\operatorname{1}[\left|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)\right|\leq R].

From weak convergence we conclude

I_{R}(2p)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)\right|^{2p}\operatorname{1}[\left|\widehat{\varrho}(s,t)\right|\leq R]dsdt=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle x_{R},\varrho_{n}\rangle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\widehat{\varrho}_{n}\|_{2p}\ I_{R}(2p)^{1-\frac{1}{2p}},

where the last step is Hölder’s inequality. Hence,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\widehat{\varrho}_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}\geq I_{R}(2p)
for any
p\geq 1
and
R>0
. Combined with Fatou’s lemma, this yields:

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\zeta}_{2p}(\varrho_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\widehat{\varrho}_{n}\|_{2p}^{2p}\geq I_{R}(2p).

As
R\to\infty
, the right side converges to
\zeta(\varrho)
by monotone convergence. This finishes the proof of the weak lower semicontinuity.
∎

In this appendix, we collect the proofs of properties of the quantum Parisi functional listed in Subsection

B.1
Continuity properties

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

The claim (

) follows by establishing separately the Lipschitz continuity of the functional

and
\int_{0}^{1}\theta(\pi(s))ds
. The latter immediately follows from (
1.15
). It thus remains to establish the Lipschitz continuity of

. For its proof we follow closely
[
1
]
, which in turn is based on the Gaussian interpolation argument in
[
39
]
.

Consider two
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
, and suppose without loss of generality that they correspond to a common (refined) partition
0=:m_{0}<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}<m_{r+1}:=1
. Let
(\mu^{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}
stand for the corresponding weights and consider two independent families of

-valued, centred Gaussian processes
(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}
and
(z^{\prime}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}
with covariances given by (

) with values
\pi(m_{j})
and
\pi^{\prime}(m_{j})
respectively. The interpolated Gaussian process

z_{\alpha}(r):=\sqrt{r}z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{1-r}z^{\prime}_{\alpha},\quad r\in[0,1],

then has the covariance

\mathbb{E}\left[\langle f,z_{\alpha}(r)\rangle\langle g,z_{\alpha^{\prime}}(r)\rangle\right]=r\langle f,d\zeta\left(\pi(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}})\right)g\rangle+(1-r)\langle f,d\zeta\left(\pi^{\prime}(m_{\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime}})\right)g\rangle,\quad f,g\in L^{2}(0,1).

The main idea is to control the derivative of

\displaystyle f(r):=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mu^{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}_{\alpha}\!\int\exp\left(\langle\xi,z_{\alpha}(r)\rangle+r\langle\xi,\left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta(\pi(1)\right)\xi\rangle+(1-r)\langle\xi,\left(x^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}d\zeta(\pi^{\prime}(1)\right)\xi\rangle\right)\nu_{1}(d\xi)\right],

which coincides with
X_{0}(\pi,x)
at
r=1
and with
X_{0}(\pi^{\prime},x^{\prime})
at
r=0
.
Let
\langle(\cdot)\rangle_{r}
denote the (random) Gibbs expectation value associated to partition function defined by the logarithm, and
\langle(\cdot)\rangle_{r}^{\otimes 2}
the Gibbs expectation value of the corresponding duplicated system.
Then straightforward differentiation and Gaussian integration by parts, yields

f^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\langle\xi,d\zeta\left(\pi^{\prime}(m_{\alpha\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}})\right)\widetilde{\xi}\rangle\right\rangle_{r}^{\otimes 2}-\left\langle\langle\xi,d\zeta\left(\pi(m_{\alpha\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}})\right)\widetilde{\xi}\rangle\right\rangle_{r}^{\otimes 2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\langle\xi,(x-x^{\prime})\xi\rangle\right\rangle_{r}\right],

(B.1)

where the variables with and without the tilde refer to the first and second system in the duplication.
Note that two terms
\frac{1}{2}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\langle\xi,d\zeta\left(\pi(1)\right)\xi\rangle\right\rangle_{r}\right]
and
\frac{1}{2}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\langle\xi,d\zeta\left(\pi^{\prime}(1)\right)\xi\rangle\right\rangle_{r}\right]
, which appear in the Gaussian integration by parts cancels due to the presence of this term in the exponent. This implies that

\left|f^{\prime}(r)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|d\zeta\left(\pi(m_{\alpha\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}})\right)-d\zeta\left(\pi^{\prime}(m_{\alpha\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}})\right)\right\|\right\rangle_{r}\right]+\|x-x^{\prime}\|.

The weights of the Ruelle probability cascade have the property
[
37

Comparison results for Poisson equation with mixed boundary condition on manifolds Thanks: The research of the third author is supported by NSFC No.11601359
Haiqing Cheng, Tengfei Ma, Kui Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we establish a L 1 L^{1} estimate for solutions to Poisson equation with mixed boundary condition, on complete noncompact manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and compact manifolds with positive Ricci curvature respectively. On Riemann surfaces we obtain a Talenti-type comparison. Our results generalize main theorems in [ 2 ] to Riemannian setting, and Chen-Li’s result [ 8 ] to the case of variable Robin parameter.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with nonempty smooth boundary,
\Omega^{\sharp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a round ball with the same volume as
\Omega
,
f(x)
be a nonnegative function on
\Omega
and
f^{\sharp}
be the Schwarz decreasing rearrangement of
f
(see Definition
2.1
). Assume
u(x)
and
v(x)
are solutions to

-\Delta u(x)=f(x),\qquad x\in\Omega,

and

-\Delta v(x)=f^{\sharp}(x),\qquad x\in\Omega^{\sharp}

with Dirichlet boundary, respectively. Talenti
[
19
]
proved that

(1.1)

\displaystyle u^{\sharp}(x)\leq v(x),\qquad x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

Moreover the equality occurs if and only if
f(x)
is a radial function and
\Omega
is a round ball, see
[
16
]
. The key tools of the proof are Schwarz symmetrization and isoperimetric inequalities on manifolds.
Talenti’s comparison (
1.1
) plays important roles in both partial differential equations and geometry problems. There provide
L^{\infty}
estimates for solutions to PDEs and Faber-Krahn type inequality for the first Dirichlet eigenvalue, see
[
8
,
14
,
16
]
. Talenti’s comparison was generalized to nonlinear elliptic and parabolic equations with Dirichlet boundary condition (cf.
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
20
]
and references therein), to compact Riemannian manifolds with positive Ricci curvature
[
11
]
, and to noncompact manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and positive asymptotic volume ratio
[
8
]
. We also refer the reader to the excellent books
[
15
,
17
]
for related topics.

Recently, Alvino, Nitsch and Trombetti
[
3
]
studied the Poisson equation with Robin boundary condition when Robin parameter is a positive constant. They proved estimate (
1.1
) on planer domains, and a sharp
L^{1}
estimate for higher dimensions.
These results were generalized to Riemannian manifolds
[
9
]
, to Robin boundary with variable Robin parameter
[
2
]
, and to the
torsion problem for the Hermite operator
[
10
,
12
]
.

The purpose of the present paper is to study Talenti’s comparison and related estimates for solutions to Poisson equation with mixed boundary condition on manifolds. In particular, we generalize Alvino-Chiacchio-Nitsch-Trombetti’s result
[
2
]
to compact manifolds with positive Ricci curvature, and to noncompact manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature.

To state our results, we give some notations. Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold, which is either compact with
\operatorname{Ric}\geq(n-1)\kappa
for
\kappa>0
, or noncompact with nonnegative Ricci curvature and positive asymptotic volume ratio. Denote by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\theta=\begin{cases}\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|B(r)|}{\omega_{n}r^{n}},&\quad\kappa=0,\\
\frac{|M|}{|M_{\kappa}|},&\quad\kappa>0,\end{cases}

where
B(r)
is a round geodesic ball of radius
r
in
M
,
M_{\kappa}
is the
n
dimensional space form of sectional curvature
\kappa
,
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
|M|
denotes the volume of
M
. It follows from the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison that
\theta\leq 1
.
Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded domain with nonempty smooth boundary,
f(x)
be a nonnegative smooth function not identically zero on
\Omega
,
and
\beta(x)
be a positive smooth function on
\partial\Omega
. We consider the following Poisson equation with mixed boundary condition

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u(x)=f(x),&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta(x)u=0,&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Delta
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator and
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
. Since
\beta(x)>0
and
f\geq 0
, it then follows easily from maximum principle that the solution to (
1.3
) is positive on
\Omega
, see Proposition
2.1
. Let
\Omega^{\sharp}
be a round geodesic ball in
M_{\kappa}
satisfying
\theta|\Omega^{\sharp}|=|\Omega|
,

f^{\sharp}
defined on
\Omega^{\sharp}
be the Schwarz decreasing rearrangement of
f
, and
v(x)
be the solution to the Schwarz decreasing rearrangement equation of (
1.3
), namely

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta v(x)=f^{\sharp}(x),&\quad x\in\Omega^{\sharp},\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}+\bar{\beta}v=0,&\quad x\in\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

where
\bar{\beta}
is a positive constant defined by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}=\frac{\theta|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|}{\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA}.

Throughout the paper,
dA
denotes the induced measure on boundary
\partial\Omega
or
\partial\Omega^{\sharp}
.
Since the boundary value problem (
1.4
) is radially symmetric, then the solution
v(x)
is radially symmetric as well. Moreover
v(x)
is monotone decreasing along the radial direction, see Proposition
2.2
below.

Our first result is concerning a
L^{1}
comparison between
u(x)
and
v(x)
. Precisely we prove

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold, which is either compact with
\operatorname{Ric}\geq(n-1)\kappa
for
\kappa>0
, or noncompact with nonnegative Ricci curvature and positive asymptotic volume ratio.
Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded domain with nonempty smooth boundary,
f(x)
be a nonnegative smooth function not identically zero on
\Omega
,
and
\beta(x)
be a positive smooth function on
\partial\Omega
. Let
u(x)
and
v(x)
be the solutions to equations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) respectively. If
n\geq 3
, we assume further that for all measurable
E\subset\Omega
, it holds

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{E}f(x)\,dx\leq\frac{|E|^{\frac{n-2}{n}}}{|\Omega|^{\frac{n-2}{n}}}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx.

Then

(1.7)

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\theta||v||_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}.

Remark 1.2
.

In this paper, we mainly focus on
L^{1}
estimate (
1.7
) on manifolds and we then set up Theorem
1.1
in smooth case. In fact, Theorem
1.1
remains valid under the assumptions that
\Omega
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary,
f(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\beta(x)
is a positive measurable function defined on
\partial\Omega
, see
[
2
]
.

When
n=2
and
f
is a constant, we prove a Talenti-type comparison, similarly as in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemann surface, which is either compact with
\operatorname{Ric}\geq\kappa>0
, or noncompact with
\operatorname{Ric}\geq 0
and positive asymptotic volume ratio. Let
u(x)
and
v(x)
be the solutions to equations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) respectively. Suppose
f(x)\equiv 1
and
\beta(x)>0
. Then

(1.8)

\displaystyle u^{\sharp}(x)\leq v(x),\quad x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

Moreover, the equality case of (
1.8
) occurs if and only if
M
and
\Omega
are isometric to
M_{\kappa}
and
\Omega^{\sharp}
respectively, and
\beta(x)
is a constant on
\partial\Omega
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the Schwarz decreasing rearrangement and isoperimetric inequalities on manifolds, and give some properties on solutions to Poisson equation with Robin boundary condition. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Schwarz decreasing rearrangements.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
\operatorname{Ric}\geq(n-1)\kappa
, which is either noncompact with
\kappa=0
and positive asymptotic volume ratio, or compact with
\kappa>0
. Let
M_{\kappa}
be the
n
-dimensional space form of constant sectional curvature
\kappa
.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
M
and
\Omega^{\sharp}
be a round geodesic ball in
M_{\kappa}
with volume

|\Omega|/\theta
, where
\theta\in(0,1]
is a constant defined by (
1.2
).

We recall the definitions and properties of the Schwarz decreasing rearrangement of non-negative functions on manifolds, see also
[
8
, Section 2]
and
[
11
, Section 2]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
h(x)
be a nonnegative measurable function on
\Omega
. Denote by

\Omega_{h,t}=\{x\in\Omega:h(x)>t\}

and

\mu_{h}(t)=|\Omega_{h,t}|
,
the decreasing rearrangement
h^{*}
of
h
is defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle h^{*}(s)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{ess}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\,h(x),&\quad s=0,\\
\inf\{t\geq 0:\mu_{h}(t)<s\},&\quad s>0,\end{cases}

for
s\in[0,|\Omega|]
. The Schwarz decreasing rearrangement of
h(x)
is defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle h^{\sharp}(x)=h^{*}\big(\theta\omega_{n}r(x)^{n}\big),\quad x\in\Omega^{\sharp},

where
r(x)
is the distance function from the center of
\Omega^{\sharp}
in
M_{\kappa}
, and
\omega_{n}
is the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu_{h}(t)=\theta\mu_{h^{\sharp}}(t)

for
t\geq 0
. The Fubini’s theorem gives that for
h\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
p\geq 1
it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h^{p}(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}(h^{*})^{p}(s)\,ds=\theta\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}(h^{\sharp})^{p}(x)\,dx.

For any nonnegative functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
, the Hardy-Littlewood inequality gives

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x)g(x)\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}f^{*}(s)g^{*}(s)\,ds,

and taking
g(x)
as the characteristic function of
\Omega_{h,t}
in above inequality yields

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{h,t}}f(x)\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mu_{h}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds.

2.2.
Isoperimetric inequalities on manifolds.

To prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
, we require the following isoperimetric inequality on manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
M
,
\theta
,
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\sharp}
be the same as in Theorem
1.1
. It holds

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\partial\Omega|\geq\theta|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|,

where
|\partial\Omega|
denotes the
(n-1)
-dimensional area of
\partial\Omega
. Moreover the equality case of (
2.6
) occurs if and only if
\Omega
is isometric to
\Omega^{\sharp}
.

When
\kappa>0
, inequality (
2.6
) was known as Lévy-Gromov isoperimetric inequality
[
13
]
,
see also
[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
. When
M
is noncompact and
\kappa=0
, inequality (
2.6
) was proved by Agostiniani, Fogagnolo and Mazzieri
[
1
]
when
n=3
, and by Brendle
[
7
]
for all dimensions. Brendle proved in
[
7
]
that the isoperimetric inequality (
2.6
) also holds true when
\Omega
is an
n
-dimensional compact minimal submanifold of
M
of dimension
n+2
with nonnegative sectional curvature as well.

2.3.
Poisson equation with Robin boundary

In this subsection, we collect some known
facts about solutions to Poisson equation with Robin boundary condition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u(x)
be the solution to (
1.3
). Assume
f(x)
is nonnegative and not identically zero on
\Omega
, and
\beta(x)>0
on
\partial\Omega
. Then we have

\displaystyle u(x)>0

for all
x\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Letting
\displaystyle\bar{u}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(u(x)-|u(x)|)
and using integration by parts, we estimate that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\bar{u},\nabla u\rangle\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,dA-\int_{\Omega}\bar{u}\Delta u\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta u\bar{u}\,dA+\int_{\Omega}\bar{u}f\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,

where we used equation (
1.3
) in the second equality and nonnegativity of
f
in the inequality. Observing that

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\bar{u},\nabla u\rangle\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\bar{u}|^{2}\,dx,

then we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\bar{u}|^{2}\,dx=0.

So
\bar{u}(x)
is a constant on
\Omega
and inequality (
2.7
) holds as an equality, hence
\bar{u}(x)=0
, equivalently
u(x)\geq 0
. Since
u(x)
is a supharmonic function by equation (
1.3
) and
f
is not identically zero, then we conclude from strong maximum principle that
u(x)>0
on
\Omega
.

\square

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
v(x)
be the solution to (
1.4
), rewritten as
\varphi(r(x))
. If
f(x)
is non-negative and not identically zero on
\Omega
, and
\bar{\beta}>0
, then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(r)<0

for
r\in(0,R_{0})
. Where
\Omega^{\sharp}=B(R_{0})
, and
R_{0}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\kappa}}
if
\kappa>0
in view of the Myers theorem.

Proof.

We rewrite
f^{\sharp}(x)
as
h(r(x))
and denote by

\operatorname{sn}_{\kappa}(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{\sin\sqrt{\kappa}r}{\sqrt{\kappa}},&\kappa>0,\\
r,&\kappa=0,\end{cases}

then equation (
1.4
) is equivalent to

\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+(n-1)\frac{\operatorname{sn}_{\kappa}^{\prime}(r)}{\operatorname{sn}_{\kappa}(r)}\varphi^{\prime}(r)=-h(r).

Since
h(r)\geq 0
, we then have

(\operatorname{sn}_{\kappa}^{n-1}(r)\varphi^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=-\operatorname{sn}_{\kappa}^{n-1}(r)h(r)\leq 0

for
r\in(0,R_{0})
.
Since
h(r)
is monotone nonincreasing in
(0,R_{0})
and not identically zero, then there exists a
\varepsilon_{0}<R_{0}
such that

h(r)>0
for
r<\varepsilon_{0}
. Hence
\varphi^{\prime}(r)<0
for all
r\in(0,R_{0})
.

\square

3.
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3

In this section, we will prove the main theorems.
For simplicity, we rewrite
\Omega_{u,t}
as
\Omega_{t}
, and
\Omega^{\sharp}_{v,t}
as
\Omega^{\sharp}_{t}
for short. For
s\in(0,|\Omega^{\sharp}|)
, we denote

\displaystyle a(s)=\frac{s^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}{|\partial B_{s}|},

where
B_{s}
is a round geodesic ball in
M_{\kappa}
with volume
s
. It is easily checked that
a(s)=n^{-1}\omega_{n}^{-1/n}
if
\kappa=0
, and
a(s)
is monotone increasing in
s
if
\kappa>0
. Using isoperimetric inequality (
2.6
), we prove the following lemma, which will be used later.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\theta^{2}(\frac{\mu_{u}(t)}{\theta})^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}\leq a^{2}\big(\frac{\mu_{u}(t)}{\theta}\big)\Big(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\Big)\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds,

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{v}^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(t)=a^{2}\big(\mu_{v}(t)\big)\Big(-\mu_{v}^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}_{t}\cap\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}v(x)}\,dA\Big)\int_{0}^{\mu_{v}(t)}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds

for a.e.
t>0
.

Proof.

By the Morse-Sard theorem, we have

\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{t}=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)=t\}\cup\{x\in\partial\Omega:u(x)\geq t\}

for a.e.
t>0
. Let

\displaystyle g(x)=\begin{cases}|\nabla u|,&x\in\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\Omega,\\
-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu},&x\in\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Applying the divergence theorem and equation (
1.3
), we derive that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}g(x)\,dA=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,dA=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}\Delta u\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{t}}f(x)\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds,

where we have used (
2.5
) in the inequality.
Using the Hölder inequality, we estimate

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\partial\Omega_{t}|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}g(x)\,dA\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\frac{1}{g(x)}\,dA

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\Big(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\Omega}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\,dA+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\Big(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\Big),

where we have used inequality (
3.3
) in the second inequality and the coarea formula in the equality.

On the other hand, the isoperimetric inequality (
2.6
) yields

(3.5)

\displaystyle|\partial\Omega_{t}|\geq\theta|\partial(\Omega_{t}^{\sharp})|=\frac{\theta}{a(\mu_{u}(t)/\theta)}(\frac{\mu_{u}(t)}{\theta})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Assembling inequalities (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{2}}{a^{2}(\mu_{u}(t)/\theta)}\mu_{u}^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(t)\leq\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\Big(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\Big),

which proves inequality (
3.1
).

If
v(x)
is the solution to equation (
1.4
),
v(x)
is a radial function on
\Omega^{\sharp}
and decreasing along the radial direction by inequality (
2.8
), and
\Omega^{\sharp}_{t}
is a round ball. Therefore all previous inequalities hold as equalities if we replace
u(x)
by
v(x)
, hence

\displaystyle\mu_{v}^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(t)=

\displaystyle a^{2}(\mu_{v}(t))\Big(-\mu_{v}^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}_{t}\cap\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}v(x)}\,dA\Big)\int_{0}^{\mu_{v}(t)}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds

for all
t>0
. Thus we complete the proof of the lemma.

\square

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
u
and
v
are solutions to (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). Then both
u
and
v
attain their minima on
\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega^{\sharp}
. Moreover if we denote by
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
the minima of
u
and
v
respectively, then

(3.6)

\displaystyle u_{0}\leq v_{0}.

Proof.

Recall that
f
is nonnegative, then
-\Delta u\geq 0
and
-\Delta v\geq 0
. Therefore
u
and
v
attain their minima on
\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega^{\sharp}
. Moreover
u(x)>u_{0}
for
x\in\Omega
,
v(x)>v_{0}
for
x\in\Omega^{\sharp}
unless
f
is identically zero.

Noting that
v(x)=v_{0}
on
\partial\Omega^{\sharp}
and using integration by parts we compute that

\displaystyle v_{0}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}}\,dA\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\bar{\beta}v(x)\,dA

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}}\,dA\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}-\Delta v\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA\int_{\Omega}-\Delta u(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x)u(x)\,dA,

where we have used equations (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and equality (
1.5
). By the Hölder inequality, it holds

(\int_{\partial\Omega}\sqrt{u(x)}\,dA)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x)u(x)\,dA,

then we have

\displaystyle v_{0}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}(\int_{\partial\Omega}\sqrt{u(x)}\,dA)^{2}\geq\frac{u_{0}}{\theta^{2}}|\partial\Omega|^{2}\geq u_{0}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|^{2},

where we used isoperimetric inequality (
2.6
) in the last inequality. Thus we have
u_{0}\leq v_{0}
, proving (
3.6
).

\square

Now we turn to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Set

\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)=\frac{\mu_{u}(t)}{\theta}
.
Dividing inequality (
3.1
) by
\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)^{\frac{2}{n}-1}
and integrating over
[0,\tau]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)\,dt\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(a^{2}(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t))\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)\left(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t)\right)\right)\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}a^{2}(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t))\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\frac{1}{u(x)}\,dA\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(a^{2}(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t))\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds\right)(-d\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t))

\displaystyle+\theta a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|)|\Omega^{\sharp}|^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\sharp}|}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\frac{1}{u(x)}dA\right)\,dt,

where we have used

\mu_{u}(t)^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx=|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}f^{*}(s)\,ds,

which follows from the assumption (
1.6
) and the fact
a(s)
is monotone increasing in
s
. Applying Fubini’s theorem, we estimate

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\,dt=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA,

then we conclude

(3.8)

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)\,dt\leq

\displaystyle-\theta^{2}F(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(\tau))+\theta^{2}F(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(0))

\displaystyle+\theta a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|)|\Omega^{\sharp}|^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\sharp}|}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA,

where
F(s)
is defined by

F(s):=\int_{0}^{s}a^{2}(\sigma)\sigma^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{\sigma}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(r)\,drd\sigma.

Substituting (
1.5
) into inequality (
3.8
) yields

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)\,dt\leq-F(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(\tau))+F(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(0))+a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|)|\Omega^{\sharp}|^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\sharp}|}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}}\,dA.

For
\tau>v_{0}
, we have
\Omega^{\sharp}_{\tau}\subset\subset\Omega^{\sharp}
, and then equality (
3.2
) becomes to

\displaystyle\mu_{v}^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(t)=a^{2}\big(\mu_{v}(t)\big)\Big(-\mu_{v}^{\prime}(t)\Big)\int_{0}^{\mu_{v}(t)}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds,

implying

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu_{v}(t)\,dt+F(\mu_{v}(\tau))=\int_{0}^{v_{0}}\mu_{v}(t)\,dt+F(\mu_{v}(v_{0})).

Dividing equation (
3.2
) by
\mu_{v}(t)^{\frac{2-n}{n}}
and integrating over
[0,v_{0}]
yields

\int_{0}^{v_{0}}\mu_{v}(t)\,dt+F(\mu_{v}(v_{0}))=F(\mu_{v}(0))+a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|)|\Omega^{\sharp}|^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\sharp}|}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}}\,dA,

and substituting above equality to (
3.10
), we obtain for all
\tau>v_{0}
that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu_{v}(t)\,dt+F(\mu_{v}(\tau))=

\displaystyle F(\mu_{v}(0))+a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|)|\Omega^{\sharp}|^{\frac{2}{n}-1}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\sharp}|}(f^{\sharp})^{*}(s)\,ds\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}}\,dA.

Combining inequality (
3.9
) and equality (
3.11
), we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu_{v}(t)\,dt\leq

\displaystyle-F(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t))+F(\mu_{v}(t))+F(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(0))-F(\mu_{v}(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-F(\tilde{\mu}_{u}(t))+F(\mu_{v}(t)),

where in the equality we used that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{u}(0)=\frac{|\Omega|}{\theta}=|\Omega^{\sharp}|=\mu_{v}(0).

Letting
\tau\to\infty
in (
3.12
), we get

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{u}(t)\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta\mu_{v}(t)\,dt,

where we have used
\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mu_{u}(\tau)=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mu_{v}(\tau)=0
. Hence (
1.7
) holds true.

\square

Now we deal with the case
n=2
and
f(x)\equiv 1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

When
n=2
and
f(x)\equiv 1
, inequality (
3.1
) gives

(3.13)

\displaystyle\theta\leq

\displaystyle\Big(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\Big)a^{2}(\mu_{u}(t)/\theta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\Big)a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|),

where we have used the fact that
a(s)
is monotone increasing in
s
in the last inequality.
Integrating inequality (
3.13
) over
[0,\tau]
yields

(3.14)

\displaystyle\theta\tau\leq

\displaystyle\Big(-\mu_{u}(\tau)+|\Omega|+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)u(x)}\,dA\,dt\Big)a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|)

\displaystyle\Big(-\mu_{u}(\tau)+|\Omega|+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{\beta(x)}\,dA\Big)a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|),

where we used (
3.7
) in the last inequality.

Analogously, by using equality (
3.2
) we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\tau=\Big(-\mu_{v}(\tau)+|\Omega^{\sharp}|+\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\bar{\beta}}\,dA\Big)a^{2}(|\Omega^{\sharp}|)

for
\tau\geq v_{0}
, then
putting (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) together we get

\mu_{u}(\tau)-\theta\mu_{v}(\tau)\leq 0.

For

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(\tau)\leq|\Omega|=\theta|\Omega^{\sharp}|=\theta\mu_{v}(\tau),

thus

\mu_{u}(\tau)-\theta\mu_{v}(\tau)\leq 0

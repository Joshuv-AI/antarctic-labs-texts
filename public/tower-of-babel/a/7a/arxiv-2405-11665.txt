Revisiting Baer elements
Amartya Goswami, Themba Dube

Abstract
. The objective of this paper is to extend certain properties observed in d d -ideals of rings and d d -elements of frames to Baer elements in multiplicative lattices introduced in D. D. Anderson, C. Jayaram, and P. A. Phiri, Baer lattices, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) , 59 (1994), 61–74. Additionally, we present results concerning these elements that have not been addressed in the study of d d -ideals of rings. Furthermore, we introduce Baer closures and explore Baer maximal, prime, semiprime, and meet-irreducible elements.

1.
Introduction

Baer-ideals were first introduced in
[
Spe97
]
for commutative Baer rings, where they were defined as the kernels of Baer ring homomorphisms. Subsequently, in
[
Jay84
]
, the study of Baer ideals was extended to semiprime commutative rings. In
[
Con88
]
, the concept of such ideals was generalized to any commutative ring and termed “
B
-ideals”. The term “
d
-ideal” in rings, not necessarily commutative, was introduced in
[
Mas89
]
, with the terminology adapted from the series of papers
[
HP80
,
HP80’
,
Pag81
]
focusing on
d
-ideals of Riesz spaces. Furthermore, in
[
AKA99
]
, a study was conducted on some properties of
d
-ideals in rings
C(X)
, referred to as
z^{0}
-ideals. Also,
d
-ideals have been examined for reduced rings under the same name in
[
AKA00
]
.

The initial effort to extend
d
-ideals to “
d
-elements” in lattices was undertaken in
[
MZ03
]
for frames, once again borrowing terminology from
[
HP80
]
. Subsequent research on
d
-elements can be found in
[
DS19
]
and
[
Bha19
]
, where the second focused on maximal
d
-elements. It was in
[
AJP94
]
where
d
-elements were further extended to encompass more general types of multiplicative lattices, not necessarily frames, and labelled Baer elements.

Since the introduction of Baer elements in
[
AJP94
]
for multiplicative lattices, several studies have been conducted on
d
-ideals in rings and
d
-elements in frames, as can be seen above. All of these studies may warrant scrutiny for further extensions to multiplicative lattices in the sense of
[
AJP94
]
. Furthermore, there are several properties of
d
-ideals of rings studied in earlier literature that have not been fully captured in their extended form in
[
AJP94
]
. Our modest aim in this paper is to extend some of those results to Baer elements. Moreover, we shall include some aspects of Baer elements that, to the best of our knowledge, have not even been studied for
d
-ideals in rings per se.

Let us now briefly describe the content of this paper.
In Section
2
, we gather the multiplicative lattice theoretic machinery needed throughout this paper. All of the material presented is standard, with references including
[
Dil36
,
War37
,
WD39
,
Dil62
,
And74
,
And76
]
.

In Section
3
, we present various equivalent characterizations and basic properties of Baer elements. We provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the closedness of Baer elements under finite products (Theorem
3.6
), characterize lattices where every Baer element is a
z
-element (Theorem
3.13
), characterize domains (Theorem
3.16
), and discuss several relations between prime, maximal, and Baer elements (Proposition
3.17
). We also give a sufficient condition for the contraction of Baer elements under a multiplicative lattice homomorphism (Theorem
3.11
).

In Section
4
, we introduce Baer closures and discuss some properties of them (Proposition
4.2
). We provide some equivalent criteria on closedness of Baer elements under joins (Proposition
4.4
). We view Baer closures as nuclei of various types, and apply them in studying some distinguished classes of Baer elements, namely maximal, prime, semiprime, and meet-irreducible.

According to

In the next proposition, we gather some elementary results on Baer elements.

Proposition 3.1

In a multiplicative lattice

(1)

An element

(3)

(4)

Proof.

(1)–(4) See

(5) For any elements

(6) Let us suppose

We shall now inquire when Baer elements are closed under finite products, and the following theorem provides us with a necessary and sufficient condition for that.

The product of two Baer elements in a multiplicative lattice

c^{\perp\perp}c^{\perp\perp}=c^{\perp\perp}

We next wish to show that certain types of multiplicative lattice homomorphisms “contract” Baer elements to Baer elements. Since we are going to impose certain conditions on the homomorphisms, we fist show by a simple example that the conditions we will impose are not so stringent as to make them isomorphisms.

Example 3.9
.

Let
\mathbf{2}
be the two-element chain
\{0,1\}
and
\mathbf{3}=\{0,m,1\}
be the three-element chain; both viewed as multiplicative lattices with product given by

\phi\colon\mathbf{2}\to\mathbf{3}
be the unique homomorphism. Then
\phi
has the following properties:

\bullet

\phi
maps compact elements to compact element.

\bullet

\phi
is injective, but not an isomorphism.

\bullet

For any compact
c

\phi(c^{\perp})=\phi(c)^{\perp}
.

Definition 3.10
.

We call a multiplicative lattice homomorphism
\phi\colon L\to M

strong
if it is injective, it maps compact elements to compact elements, and it commutes with annihilation of compact elements in the sense that for any compact

\phi(c^{\perp})=\phi(c)^{\perp}

.

If
\phi\colon L\to M

\phi^{-1}[\mathfrak{B}(M)]\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(L)

Proof.

Let

and

L

and

. We aim to show that

is a Baer element. Since

\phi(x)\in\mathfrak{B}(M)
. It follows, therefore, that
\phi(d)\leqslant\phi(x)
, whence

, as desired.
∎

In the next theorem, we shall characterize those multiplicative lattices in which every Baer element is a

Theorem 3.13

In a multiplicative lattice

Proof.

Let

Theorem 3.16

Our next proposition is on establishing some relations between Baer, prime, and maximal elements in multiplicative lattices, and first three of them extend

Proposition 3.17
.

In a multiplicative lattice

(1)

is a Baer element or the maximal

(1) Let us consider the case:

, and we are done. So, let

. Then there exists an

. Suppose

(

, and hence

(2) Let us suppose that

(3) Since

(4) Let us define a set

4.
Baer closures and their applications

We shall now introduce a closure on a multiplicative lattice that will assign a unique Baer element to each element of the lattice and will give another alternative definition of a Baer element. Furthermore, using these closures, we shall study some distinguished classes of Baer elements in due course.

Definition 4.1
.

The
Baer closure
on a multiplicative lattice
L
is the map
\cz\colon L\to L
defined as

\cz(a):=\bigwedge\left\{x\in\mathfrak{B}(L)\mid a\leqslant x\right\}.

In the next proposition, we shall establish some essential properties of Baer closures.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
a
and
b
are elements in a multiplicative lattice
L
. Then the following hold.

(1)

\cz(a)
is the smallest Baer element such that
a\leqslant\cz(a)
.

(2)

\cz(a)=a
if and only if
a
is a Baer element.

(3)

\cz(a)=1
if and only if
a=1
.

(4)

\cz(0)=0
.

(5)

If
a\leqslant b
, then
\cz(a)\leqslant\cz(b).

(6)

\cz(\cz(a))=\cz(a).

(7)

\sqrt{a}\leqslant\cz(a)
.

(8)

\cz(a)=\cz(\sqrt{a})
.

(9)

\cz(a^{n})=\cz(a)
, for any
n\in\mathds{N}^{+}
.

(10)

\cz(a)\vee\cz(b)\leqslant\cz(a\vee b)=\cz(\cz(a)\vee\cz(b)).

(11)

\cz(ab)=\cz(a\wedge b)=\cz(a)\wedge\cz(b)
.

(12)

If
L
is a
B
-multiplicative lattice, then
\cz(ab)=\cz(a)\cz(b)
.

Proof.

(1) From Definition
4.1
it follows that
a\leqslant\cz(a)
. By Proposition
3.1
(
6
) we know that
\cz(a)
is a Baer element in
L
. Now suppose
y
is a Baer element with the property
a\leqslant y
. Then from Definition
4.1
, it implies that
\cz(a)\leqslant y
.

(2) If
a
is a Baer element, then by Definition
4.1
, we have
\cz(a)=a
. Conversely, if the identity holds, then
a
is a Baer element by (
1
).

(3) Since
1
is a Baer element by Proposition
3.1
(
5
), the claim
\cz(1)=1
follows by (
2
). The converse is obvious.

(4) Follows from Proposition
3.1
(
5
) and (
2
).

(5) Let us suppose that
x\in\mathfrak{B}(L)
with
b\leqslant x
. Then
a\leqslant x
and hence
\cz(a)\leqslant x
. Since
x
was an arbitrary Baer element with the property
b\leqslant x
, we must have
\cz(a)\leqslant\cz(b).

(6) By (
1
), we have
\cz(a)\leqslant\cz(\cz(a)).
Now suppose that
x\in\mathfrak{B}(L)
with
\cz(a)\leqslant x
. Since by definition,
\cz(\cz(a))
is the infimum of all such
x\in\mathfrak{B}(L)
, we must have
\cz(\cz(a))\leqslant\cz(a).

(7) Let us suppose that
\mathrm{Spec}_{B}(L)
denotes the set of all prime Baer elements of
L
. Now by applying (
1
), (

†

‣
2
), and Proposition
3.1
(
4
), we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{a}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\{p\in\mathfrak{P}(L)\mid a\leqslant p\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigwedge\{p\in\mathrm{Spec}_{B}(L)\mid a\leqslant p\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigwedge\{p\in\mathrm{Spec}_{B}(L)\mid\cz(a)\leqslant p\}=\cz(a).

(8) Applying (
5
), (
7
), and (
6
), we have
\cz(\sqrt{a})\leqslant\cz(a)
. On the other hand, applying (
5
) on
a\leqslant\sqrt{a},
we get
\cz(a)\leqslant\cz(\sqrt{a}).

(9) Since
a^{n}\leqslant a
, by (
5
), we have
\cz(a^{n})\leqslant\cz(a).
Let us now suppose
k\in\mathfrak{C}(L)
and
k\leqslant\cz(a).
This implies that
k\leqslant x
for all
x\in\mathfrak{B}(L)
with
a\leqslant x
. Since
x\in\mathfrak{B}(L)
and
a^{\perp}\leqslant(a^{n})^{\perp}
, we have
a^{n}\leqslant x
. Therefore
k\leqslant\cz(a^{n}),
proving that
\cz(a)\leqslant\cz(a^{n}).

(10) Applying (
1
) and (

), we obtain
\cz(a\vee b)\leqslant\cz(\cz(a)\vee\cz(b)),
whereas applying (

), we have
\cz(a)\vee\cz(b)\leqslant\cz(a\vee b)
, and applying (
6
) on it gives

\cz(\cz(a)\vee\cz(b))\leqslant\cz(\cz(a\vee b))=\cz(a\vee b).

(11) Since by Lemma
2.1
(

), we have

and

(

), Proposition

(

), and (

\cz(ab)\leqslant\cz(a\wedge b)\leqslant\cz(a)\wedge\cz(b)\in\mathfrak{B}(L).

Therefore, by (

\cz(a)\wedge\cz(b)
is the smallest Baer element that is above

. We shall show that

By

Now if

(12) The proof is similar to (

).

Remark 4.3
.

In the context of frames, another closure operation (see
[
MZ03
, Definition and Remarks 5.1.]
) is well-known for
d
-elements (that is, Baer elements in our context).
For an element
x
in a frame
L
, the
closure

d

of

x
is defined as

d(x):=\bigvee\{c^{\perp\perp}\mid c\leqslant x,c\in\mathfrak{C}(L)\}.

In this paper, we have not considered the above closure operator because in general, joins of
d
-elements are not
d
-elements, and we want to have our closure
\cz(\cdot)
be such that
\cz(x)
is the smallest
d
-element that is above
x
(see Proposition

(

)).

In Section
3
, we have studied closedness properties of Baer elements under meets and products. The following proposition gives some equivalent criteria on closedness of Baer elements under joins. We are not aware of an explicit characterization of multiplicative lattices in which Baer elements are closed under joins.

Proposition 4.4
.

In a multiplicative lattice
L
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

If
a
and
b
are Baer elements in
L
, then so is
a\vee b
.

(2)

Whenever
a
,
b\in L
, we have
\cz(a\vee b)=\cz(a)\vee\cz(b).

(3)

If
\{a_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(L)
, then
\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{B}(L).

(4)

If
\{a_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq L
, then
\cz\left(\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\right)=\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\cz(a_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

We shall only show (1)
\Rightarrow
(3). The rest of the implications are straightforward. Suppose
u
,
v\in\mathfrak{C}(L)
,
u^{\perp}\leqslant v^{\perp}
, and
u\leqslant\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}
. Since
L
is compactly generated, there exists a finite subset
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
of
\Lambda
such that
u\leqslant\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}a_{\lambda_{i}}
. Since by hypothesis
\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}a_{\lambda_{i}}
is a Baer element, we have

v\leqslant\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}a_{\lambda_{i}}\leqslant\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda},

implying that
\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{B}(L).

∎

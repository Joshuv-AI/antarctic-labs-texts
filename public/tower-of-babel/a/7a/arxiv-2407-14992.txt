On the strength of Burer’s lifted convex relaxation to quadratic programming with ball constraints
Fatma Kılınç-Karzan, Shengding Sun

Abstract
We study quadratic programs with m m ball constraints, and the strength of a lifted convex relaxation for it recently proposed by Burer (2024). Burer shows this relaxation is exact when m = 2 m=2 . For general m m , Burer (2024) provides numerical evidence that this lifted relaxation is tighter than the Kronecker product based Reformulation Linearization Technique (RLT) inequalities introduced by Anstreicher (2017), and conjectures that this must be theoretically true as well. In this note, we provide an affirmative answer to this question and formally prove that this lifted relaxation indeed implies the Kronecker inequalities. Our proof is based on a decomposition of non-rank-one extreme rays of the lifted relaxation for each pair of ball constraints. Burer (2024) also numerically observes that for this lifted relaxation, an RLT-based inequality proposed by Zhen et al. (2021) is redundant, and conjectures this to be theoretically true as well. We also provide a formal proof that Zhen et al. (2021) inequalities are redundant for this lifted relaxation. In addition, we establish that Burer’s lifted relaxation is a particular case of the moment-sum-of-squares hierarchy.

Introduction

In this note, we study a specific class of quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs) with a general quadratic objective and
m\geq 2
ball constraints:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left\{q(x):~x\in S\right\},\quad\text{where }~S:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:~\|x-c^{i}\|_{2}\leq r_{i},~\forall i\in[m]\right\}.

(1)

Here,
[m]:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and
c^{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
i\in[m]
, and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
stands for the Euclidean norm. We assume
S\neq\varnothing
.

QCQP has a long history in the optimization community, dating back to 1990s. For fixed
m
, QCQPs with domain
S
given by the intersection of
m
ball constraints can be solved within desired accuracy in polynomial time
[
4
]
. However, no exact convex relaxation is known for all
m
.

A closely related direction is to study the conical hull of lifted quadratic set of
S
given by

{\cal D}:=\cone\{(xx^{\top},x):~x\in S\}\subseteq{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(2)

where
\cone\{{\cal X}\}
denotes the conical hull of the set
{\cal X}
(i.e., set of all nonnegative combinations of the vectors from
{\cal X}
),
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
is the space of
n\times n
symmetric matrices and
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
is the cone of positive semidefinite matrices in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.

Clearly any (possibly non-convex) quadratic objective over
S
can be equivalently written as a linear objective over
{\cal D}
. Thus, whenever
{\cal D}
admits an explicit and concise description, problem (
1
) can be efficiently solved.

The set
{\cal D}
immediately brings to our attention the well known Shor semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxation
[
14
]
, which is computationally efficient but in general only a relaxation.
Shor SDP relaxation has been studied a lot in the literature and several efforts have been made to tighten it (see
[
3
,
6
,
5
]
and other references therein). One of the latest such effort is the Kronecker product based
Reformulation Linearization Technique
(RLT) inequalities proposed by
Anstreicher [1]
, which are applicable when
S
is defined by second-order cone (SOC) constraints. When
S
is defined by SOC constraints,
Zhen et al. [17]
propose another RLT-based inequality that aims to strengthen the Shor relaxation.

There has been much work
[
15
,
8
,
16
]
on studying exact or tight description of
{\cal D}
for certain classes of
S
. Recently, when
S
is given by the intersection of two ball constraints,
Kelly et al. [10]
gave an exact disjunctive SDP reformulation. Inspired by this result, for arbitrary number of ball constraints,
Burer [7]
proposed a lifted convex relaxation which involves only one more additional variable for lifting, and showed that although it is not an exact relaxation for general
m>2
, its admits a very good numerical performance in terms of both relaxation quality and efficiency of computation.
In
[
7
]
, it was conjectured that this lifted relaxation is provably tighter than the Kronecker RLT inequalities and Zhen et al.’s RLT inequalities are redundant for this lifted relaxation. We answer these two conjectures affirmatively. In particular, in Theorem
1
, by proving a decomposition theorem (see Theorem
4
) for the non-rank-one extreme rays of the lifted relaxation proposed in
[
7
]
and studying the properties of both rank-one and non-rank-one extreme rays, we show that Kronecker RLT inequalities are redundant for the projection of this lifted relaxation. In Theorem
6
we show that Zhen et al. RLT inequalities are implied by this lifted relaxation as well. Finally, we close by giving a new interpretation of Burer’s lifted relaxation using the techniques from moment-sum-of-squares (moment-SOS) hierarchy.

-1\\

Then Burer’s lifted relaxation

3
Domination of Kronecker RLT inequalities

In order to project
{\cal C}_{m}
back to the space of
{\cal D}
, we define the following linear maps of
Z\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n+2}

\pi_{X}(Z):=\begin{bmatrix}Z_{11}&\ldots&Z_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Z_{n1}&\ldots&Z_{nn}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n},\quad\pi_{x}(Z):=\begin{bmatrix}Z_{1,n+2}\\
\vdots\\
Z_{n,n+2}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

and denote
\pi(Z):=(\pi_{X}(Z),\pi_{x}(Z))\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Although
{\cal D}\subsetneq\pi({\cal C}_{m})
for any
m\geq 2
,
Burer [7]
shows that for the case
m=2
the relaxation (
3
) can be slightly modified to become exact, i.e., setting
\left\langle Q,Z\right\rangle\geq 0
and
(d^{1})^{\top}Zd^{2}=0
and keeping everything else the same.
Indeed, when
m=2
this modification is justified and leads to a valid convex relaxation that is shown to be exact in
[
7
]
.
However, this modification does not generalize to
m\geq 3
.

For
m\geq 3
,
Burer [7]
numerically compares the strength of (
3
) against a lifted SDP formulation strengthened by the Kronecker RLT inequalities proposed in
[
1
]
. The idea for the Kronecker inequalities is as follows: each ball constraint
\|x-c^{i}\|_{2}\leq r_{i}
is equivalent to

{\cal A}(x-c^{i},r_{i}):=\begin{bmatrix}r_{i}&&&x_{1}-c^{i}_{1}\\
&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&r_{i}&x_{n}-c^{i}_{n}\\
x_{1}-c^{i}_{1}&\ldots&x_{n}-c^{i}_{n}&r_{i}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.

Recall also that for any two positive semidefinite matrices
A^{i}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n_{1}}
and
A^{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n_{2}}
, we always have their Kronecker product
A^{i}\otimes A^{j}
is positive semidefinite as well.
Thus, if a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfies two ball constraints
\|x-c^{i}\|_{2}\leq r_{i}
and
\|x-c^{j}\|_{2}\leq r_{j}
, then the Kronecker product
{\cal A}(x-c^{i},r_{i})\otimes{\cal A}(x-c^{j},r_{j})
is positive semidefinite as well.
Note that the entries in
{\cal A}(x-c^{i},r_{i})\otimes{\cal A}(x-c^{j},r_{j})
are quadratic functions of
x
and as such they can be expressed as linear functions of the rank-one matrix
\begin{pmatrix}x\\
1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x^{\top}&1\end{pmatrix}
. In order to obtain the Kronecker inequalities, we simply replace the quadratic terms
x_{k}x_{l}
in
{\cal A}(x-c^{i},r_{i})\otimes{\cal A}(x-c^{j},r_{j})
by
X_{kl}
and
x_{k}^{2}
by
X_{kk}
. Let
{\cal K}_{ij}
denote the resulting linear map from
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{S}}^{(n+1)^{2}}
of the matrix variable
X
and the vector
x
(see
[
1
]
for formal entry-wise definition of
{\cal K}_{ij}
). Hence, we arrive at the conclusion that any
(X,x)\in{\cal D}
must satisfy

{\cal K}_{ij}(X,x)\succeq 0,\quad\forall i,j\in[m].

(4)

The numerical study in
[
7
]
indicates that the lifted relaxation (
3
) is stronger than the Kronecker inequalities (
4
), and as a result
[
7
]
conjectures that this must be true theoretically as well. In this note we resolve this conjecture and show that this is indeed the case.

Theorem 1
.

For any
m\geq 2
and for all
Z\in{\cal C}_{m}
, we have
{\cal K}_{ij}(\pi_{X}(Z),\pi_{x}(Z))\succeq 0
for all
i,j\in[m]
.

Indeed, we prove a slightly stronger form of Theorem
1
.
Note that for all
i,j\in[m]
where
i\neq j
, by defining

{\cal C}_{\{i,j\}}:=\left\{Z\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n+2}_{+}:\,\left\langle Q,Z\right\rangle=0,\,PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2},~PZd^{j}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2},~(d^{i})^{\top}Zd^{j}\geq 0\right\},

we observe that
{\cal C}_{m}=\bigcap\limits_{i\in[m]}\bigcap\limits_{j<i}{\cal C}_{\{i,j\}}
. In the view of this, in fact, we will prove the following stronger claim:

Theorem 2
.

For any
m\geq 2
and
i,j\in[m]
where
i\neq j
we have the relation

\displaystyle Z\in{\cal C}_{\{i,j\}}\implies{\cal K}_{ij}(\pi_{X}(Z),\pi_{x}(Z))\succeq 0.

Clearly, Theorem
1
is a corollary of Theorem
2
. Thus, from now on we focus on the case of
m=2
,
(i,j)=(1,2)
, and consider the set

{\cal C}_{2}=\left\{Z\succeq 0:~\left\langle Q,Z\right\rangle=0,~PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2},\,\forall i\in[2],~(d^{1})^{\top}Zd^{2}\geq 0\right\}.

(5)

We start with some preliminaries that relate to properties of extreme rays of
{\cal C}_{2}
that are spanned by rank-one matrices in Section
3.1
.
The key ingredient of proving Theorem
2
is a decomposition of non-rank-one extreme rays of
{\cal C}_{2}
, which we present in Section
3.2
. We believe this result is of separate interest in order to further understand the geometry of relaxation (
3
).
Finally, in Section
3.3
we give the proof of Theorem
2
.

3.1
Preliminaries

We start with the following simple fact on the relationship between matrices
P,Q
and the second-order cone. Given a set
{\cal X}
, we let
\bd({\cal X})
denote its boundary.

Fact 1
.

For any
w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}
, the relation
w^{\top}Qw\geq 0
holds if and only if
Pw\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}\cup(-{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})
. Moreover,
w^{\top}Qw=0
holds if and only if
Pw\in\left(\bd({\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})\cup\bd(-{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})\right)
.

Proof.

Write
w^{\top}=(x^{\top},t,x_{0})
. Then,
w^{\top}Qw\geq 0
if and only if
tx_{0}\geq\|x\|_{2}^{2}
which is equivalent to
4\|x\|_{2}^{2}+(t-x_{0}^{2})\leq(t+x_{0})^{2}
.
Thus,
w^{\top}Qw\geq 0
if and only if

Pw=\begin{bmatrix}2x\\
t-x_{0}\\
t+x_{0}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}\cup(-{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}).

Similarly,
w^{\top}Qw=0
holds if and only if
4\|x\|_{2}^{2}+(t-x_{0}^{2})=(t+x_{0})^{2}
, which holds if and only if
Pw\in\left(\bd({\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})\cup\bd(-{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})\right)
.
∎

We characterize rank-one extreme rays of
{\cal C}_{m}
(recall from (
3
)) for any
m\geq 2
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
w^{\top}:=(x^{\top},t,x_{0})
be such that

spans an extreme ray of
{\cal C}_{m}
. Then,
\|x\|_{2}^{2}=tx_{0}
and

. Moreover, if
x_{0}>0
, then
w^{\top}d^{i}\geq 0,\forall i\in[m]
.

Proof.

As
Z=ww^{\top}
spans an extreme ray of
{\cal C}_{m}
,
w\neq 0
.

\left\langle Q,ww^{\top}\right\rangle=0
implies
\|x\|_{2}^{2}=tx_{0}
. Assume for contradiction that
x_{0}=0
. Then,
x=0
and
w^{\top}=\alpha(0^{\top},1,0)
for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
d^{i}_{n+1}=-1
for all
i\in[m]
, we have
(Pww^{\top}d^{i})^{\top}=-\alpha^{2}(0^{\top},1,1)
. Thus, the constraint
Pww^{\top}d^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
implies
\alpha=0
, and so

which is a contradiction. Hence, we conclude

.

Since
\left\langle Q,ww^{\top}\right\rangle=0

we deduce

. After choosing

we have
Pw\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
. Since

is invertible and

we have

. Hence, the constraint
Pw(w^{\top}d^{i})=PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}

for all

.

In particular, Proposition

states that if

(i.e.,

.

Lemma 1

Let

Although

is not ROG, we have the following characterization of its non-rank-one extreme rays.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\bar{Z}
span an extreme ray of

. If

\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\

where
\|x^{1}-c^{1}\|_{2}<r_{1},~\|x^{1}-c^{2}\|_{2}<r_{2}
,
\|x^{i}-c^{1}\|_{2}=r_{1}

for all
2\leq i\leq r
, and
P\bar{Z}d^{2}\in\bd({\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})
.

Proof.

Based on Theorem
3
and Lemma

, the set

\left\{Z\succeq 0:~\begin{array}[]{l}\left\langle Q,Z\right\rangle=0,\\
PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2},\,\forall i\in[2],\\
(d^{1})^{\top}Zd^{2}=0\end{array}\right\}

is ROG. Since
\bar{Z}
has rank

we must have
(d^{1})^{\top}\bar{Z}d^{2}>0
.
In addition, as
\left\langle Q,\bar{Z}\right\rangle=0
, by Lemma

we also have

for
i\in[2]
.

Note that by Proposition
2
for each
i\in[2]
the sets

\left\{Z\succeq 0:~\left\langle Q,Z\right\rangle=0,~PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}\right\}

are ROG.
Thus, we can write
\bar{Z}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}w^{i}(w^{i})^{\top}
, where for each
i\in[r]
,
\alpha_{i}>0
and

satisfies

,
\left\langle Q,w^{i}(w^{i})^{\top}\right\rangle=0,~P(w^{i}(w^{i})^{\top})d^{1}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
.
For any

, let
(w^{i})^{\top}=((x^{i})^{\top},t_{i},y_{i})

if necessary, we may assume

. We argue that

, since otherwise we have

,

1\\
1\end{bmatrix}
which violates

. Thus,

. Upon rescaling

and

we may assume

and hence

. Thus, we deduce that each

the vector

is of the form

w^{i}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\
1\end{bmatrix},~\text{ and }~Pw^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}.

As
Pw^{i}((w^{i})^{\top}d^{1})\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
and

is a pointed convex cone, we also get

.

Now note that
P\bar{Z}d^{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\left\langle d^{1},w^{i}\right\rangle Pw^{i}
, while we also know
P\bar{Z}d^{1}\in\bd({\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})
and

and
\left\langle d^{1},w^{i}\right\rangle\geq 0
for all

. Then, since

is a strictly convex cone (i.e., the boundary does not contain any polyhedron of dimension at least two) and

, we deduce that exactly one of the terms

and

Hence,

\|x^{1}-c^{2}\|_{2}<r_{2}
.

.

Let us now define
{\cal K}
more formally for our proof. For convenience, we denote the arrow matrices as
{\cal A}_{i}(x):={\cal A}(x-c^{i},r_{i}),~i\in[2]
. Let
X_{j}
denote the
j
-th column of
X
, and let
H_{j}(X,x)
be the matrix obtained from
(x_{j}-c^{2}_{j}){\cal A}_{1}(x)
by replacing
x_{j}x
terms by
X_{j}
, i.e.,

H_{j}(X,x):=\begin{bmatrix}(x_{j}-c^{2}_{j})r_{1}I&X_{j}-x_{j}c^{1}-c^{2}_{j}x+c^{2}_{j}c^{1}\\
(X_{j}-x_{j}c^{1}-c^{2}_{j}x+c^{2}_{j}c^{1})^{\top}&(x_{j}-c^{2}_{j})r_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus, the Kronecker RLT constraint derived from the two ball constraints
\|x-c^{i}\|_{2}\leq r_{i},i\in[2]
can be written as

{\cal K}(X,x)=\begin{bmatrix}r_{2}{\cal A}_{1}(x)&&&H_{1}(X,x)\\
&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&r_{2}{\cal A}_{1}(x)&H_{n}(X,x)\\
H_{1}(X,x)&\ldots&H_{n}(X,x)&r_{2}{\cal A}_{1}(x)\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.

Note that both
{\cal A}_{i}(x)
and
H_{j}(X,x)
are affine maps of
(X,x)
, and thus
{\cal K}(X,x)
is an affine map of
(X,x)
as well and so we arrive at the following result.

Lemma 2
.

For any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

{\cal K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}x_{i}x_{i}^{\top},\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\left({\cal A}_{2}(x)\otimes{\cal A}_{1}(x)\right).

Based on the characterization of rank-one and non-rank-one extreme rays of
{\cal C}_{2}
given in Proposition
1
and Theorem
4
, we conclude that to finish the proof of Theorem
2
, it suffices to prove the following result.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\bar{Z}
span an extreme ray of
{\cal C}_{2}
and suppose
r:=\rank(\bar{Z})>1
. Define
\bar{X}:=\pi_{X}(\bar{Z}),\bar{x}:=\pi_{x}(\bar{Z})
. Then,
{\cal K}(\bar{X},\bar{x})\succeq 0
.

The proof of Theorem
5
is mostly algebraic. We will use the following lemma to simplify our computations.

Lemma 3
.

For any
{\cal V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(n+1)^{2}}
we use the notation

{\cal V}^{\top}:=\left((v^{1})^{\top},b_{1},\ldots,(v^{n+1})^{\top},b_{n+1}\right),

where
v^{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},~b_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
j\in[n+1]
.
For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that either
\|x-c^{1}\|_{2}=r_{1}
or
\|x-c^{1}\|_{2}<r_{1}
and
\|x-c^{2}\|_{2}<r_{2}
, we have

{\cal V}^{\top}\left({\cal A}_{2}(x)\otimes{\cal A}_{1}(x)\right){\cal V}\geq\frac{1}{r_{1}r_{2}}\left(r_{2}^{2}-\|x-c^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\left\|r_{1}v^{n+1}+b_{n+1}(x-c^{1})\right\|_{2}^{2}.

We defer the proof of this lemma to appendix.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Based on Theorem
4
, we can write

\bar{Z}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\begin{bmatrix}x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\
1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\
1\end{bmatrix}^{\top},

where
\alpha_{i}>0
for all
i\in[r]
,
\|x^{1}-c^{1}\|_{2}<r_{1},~\|x^{1}-c^{2}\|_{2}<r_{2}
,
\|x^{i}-c^{1}\|_{2}=r_{1}
for all
2\leq i\leq r
, and
P\bar{Z}d^{2}\in\bd({\mathbb{L}}^{n+2})
.

In order to complete the proof, we will show that
{\cal V}^{\top}{\cal K}(\bar{X},\bar{x}){\cal V}\geq 0
holds for any
{\cal V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(n+1)^{2}}
. Let us write
{\cal V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(n+1)^{2}}
as
{\cal V}^{\top}=\left((v^{1})^{\top},b_{1},\ldots,(v^{n+1})^{\top},b_{n+1}\right)
, where
v^{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},b_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
j\in[n+1]
.
Then, by Lemmas
2
and
3
, it suffices to prove that for any
i\in[r]
we have

\frac{1}{r_{1}r_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\left(r_{2}^{2}-\|x^{i}-c^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\left\|r_{1}v^{n+1}+b_{n+1}(x^{i}-c^{1})\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0.

Let us define
\eta_{i}:=r_{2}^{2}-\|x^{i}-c^{2}\|_{2}^{2}
for all
i\in[r]
. Thus, we need to show

\frac{1}{r_{1}r_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\left\|r_{1}v^{n+1}+b_{n+1}(x^{i}-c^{1})\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0.

(7)

Note that for all
i\in[r]
we have

\displaystyle P\begin{bmatrix}x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\
1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\
1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}d^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}2I&0&0\\
0&1&-1\\
0&1&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\
1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\\
1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}2c^{2}\\
-1\\
r_{2}^{2}-\|c^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}2x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}-1\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+1\end{bmatrix}\left(2(c^{2})^{\top}x^{i}-\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-\|c^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(r_{2}^{2}-\|x^{i}-c^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\begin{bmatrix}2x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}-1\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+1\end{bmatrix}=\eta_{i}\begin{bmatrix}2x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}-1\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+1\end{bmatrix}.

Hence,

\displaystyle P\bar{Z}d^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\begin{bmatrix}2x^{i}\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}-1\\
\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}x^{i}\\
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\right)\\
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\right)\end{bmatrix}.

Thus, using
P\bar{Z}d^{2}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\left(\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+1\right)\right)\geq 0,

(8)

and

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\geq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}x^{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(9)

Therefore, we must have
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0

since by (
8
) the sum of these terms is nonnegative and by (
9
) their product is nonnegative.

We define and examine the following
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix

M:=\begin{bmatrix}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\right)I&\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}x^{i}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}(x^{i})^{\top}&\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

We claim that
M\succeq 0
as a result of (
8
) and (
9
). First, consider the case when at least one of the terms
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}
is equal to zero. Then, by (
9
), we deduce that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}x^{i}=0
and from (
8
) we conclude that the other term must be nonnegative and so
M\succeq 0
holds in this case.
Now, consider the case when both
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}
are nonzero. Since both terms are always nonnegative, in this case they both must be positive. Then, both of the diagonal block matrices in
M
are positive definite, and by Schur Complement Lemma
M\succeq 0
if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\right)^{-1}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}x^{i}\right)^{\top}I\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}x^{i}\right)}_{=\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}x^{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\geq 0.

By recalling that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}>0
and rearranging the above inequality, we observe that it is precisely the same as (
9
). Thus, we conclude that
M\succeq 0
holds in this case as well.

Now, as
M\succeq 0
, for any
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\begin{bmatrix}v\\
1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}v\\
1\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\|v+x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}.

(10)

Finally, note that whenever
b_{n+1}\neq 0
we have
\|r_{1}v^{n+1}+b_{n+1}(x^{i}-c^{1})\|_{2}^{2}=b_{n+1}^{2}\|\frac{r_{1}v^{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}-c^{1}+x^{i}\|_{2}^{2}
, hence (
10
) implies the desired relation of (
7
). The case when
b_{n+1}=0
follows from continuity letting
b_{n+1}\to 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.4
Domination of Zhen et al RLT inequalities

In this section we examine an inequality proposed by Zhen et al. in
[
17
]
to strengthen the Shor SDP relaxation of the sets of form
S
. Once again, in the numerical study of
[
7
]
, it was identified that this inequality is redundant for
{\cal C}_{m}
, and it was conjectured in
[
7
]
that this must be theoretically true as well.

The main idea of Zhen et al.
[
17
]
’s inequality is as follows.
Recall that given two vectors
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell}
, we have
\|uv^{\top}\|_{m2}=\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{m2}
denotes the matrix 2-norm.
Then, given any two SOC constraints
\|x-c^{i}\|_{2}\leq r_{i}
and
\|x-c^{j}\|_{2}\leq r_{j}
with
i\neq j
, we have

\displaystyle\|xx^{\top}-x(c^{j})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{j})^{\top}\|_{m2}

\displaystyle=\|(x-c^{i})(x-c^{j})^{\top}\|_{m2}

\displaystyle=\|x-c^{i}\|_{2}\|x-c^{j}\|_{2}\leq r_{i}r_{j}.

Now, by defining a matrix variable
X
to capture
xx^{\top}
, this inequality can be linearized in the lifted space as

\displaystyle\|X-x(c^{j})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{j})^{\top}\|_{m2}\leq r_{i}r_{j}

(11)

\displaystyle\iff\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}r_{i}^{2}I_{n}&X-x(c^{j})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{j})^{\top}\\
X-c^{j}x^{\top}-x(c^{i})^{\top}+c^{j}(c^{i})^{\top}&r_{j}^{2}I_{n}\end{bmatrix}}_{:={\cal Z}_{i,j}(X,x)}\succeq 0.

Note that this inequality (
11
), i.e.,
{\cal Z}_{i,j}(X,x)\succeq 0
, is linear in terms of the vector variable
x
and the matrix variable
X
. Thus, Zhen et al. suggest to add the inequality
{\cal Z}_{i,j}(X,x)\succeq 0
for all
i,j\in[m]
to strengthen the Shor SDP relaxation of the set
S
.

We next show that the inequality (
11
) follows from adding linear- and SOC-RLT inequalities to the standard Shor relaxation. Thus, the inequalities (
11
) are redundant for the lifted relaxation
{\cal C}_{m}
.

Theorem 6
.

For any
m\geq 2
and
i,j\in[m]
where
i\neq j
we have the relation

\displaystyle\left\{Z\in{\cal C}_{\{i,j\}},~Z_{n+2,n+2}=1\right\}\implies{\cal Z}_{ij}(\pi_{X}(Z),\pi_{x}(Z))\succeq 0.

Proof.

Consider any
Z\in{\cal C}_{\{i,j\}}
satisfying
Z_{n+2,n+2}=1
.
To simplify our notation, let us set
X:=\pi_{X}(Z)
and
x:=\pi_{x}(Z)
.
Thus, by the definition of
Z
, observe that

Z=\begin{bmatrix}X&Z_{t,x}&x\\
Z_{t,x}^{\top}&Z_{t,t}&t\\
x^{\top}&t&1\end{bmatrix}

Now since
Z\succeq 0
, we deduce
\begin{bmatrix}X&x\\
x^{\top}&1\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0
, which by Schur Complement Lemma implies that
X-xx^{\top}\succeq 0
.

Using the definitions of
Q
and
Z
, we observe that

QZ=\begin{bmatrix}-2I&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}X&Z_{t,x}&x\\
Z_{t,x}^{\top}&Z_{t,t}&t\\
x^{\top}&t&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-2X&-2Z_{t,x}&-2x\\
x^{\top}&t&1\\
Z_{t,x}^{\top}&Z_{t,t}&t\end{bmatrix}.

Then, as
\left\langle Q,Z\right\rangle=\tr(QZ)=0
, we deduce that
0=-2\tr(X)+t+t
, i.e.,
t=\tr(X)
.

Moreover, by definition of
P
and
d^{i}
, we have

\displaystyle PZd^{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}2I&0&0\\
0&1&-1\\
0&1&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}X&Z_{t,x}&x\\
Z_{t,x}^{\top}&Z_{t,t}&t\\
x^{\top}&t&1\end{bmatrix}d^{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}2X&2Z_{t,x}&2x\\
Z_{t,x}^{\top}-x^{\top}&Z_{t,t}-t&t-1\\
Z_{t,x}^{\top}+x^{\top}&Z_{t,t}+t&t+1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2c^{i}\\
-1\\
r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}4Xc^{i}-2Z_{t,x}+2(r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2})x\\
2(Z_{t,x}-x)^{\top}c^{i}-(Z_{t,t}-t)+(t-1)(r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2})\\
2(Z_{t,x}+x)^{\top}c^{i}-(Z_{t,t}+t)+(t+1)(r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}4Xc^{i}-4xx^{\top}c^{i}-2Z_{t,x}+2tx+2\delta_{i}x\\
2Z_{t,x}^{\top}c^{i}-Z_{t,t}+t(r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2})-\delta_{i}\\
2Z_{t,x}^{\top}c^{i}-Z_{t,t}+t(r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2})+\delta_{i}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\xi^{i}+2\delta_{i}x\\
\beta_{i}-\delta_{i}\\
\beta_{i}+\delta_{i}\end{bmatrix}

where in the last two equations we used the definitions
\delta_{i}:=2x^{\top}c^{i}-t+r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2}
,
\beta_{i}:=2Z_{t,x}^{\top}c^{i}-Z_{t,t}+t(r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2})
and
\xi^{i}:=4Xc^{i}-4xx^{\top}c^{i}-2Z_{t,x}+2tx
.
Since
PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
, we must thus have
\beta_{i}+\delta_{i}\geq 0
and

\|\xi^{i}+2\delta_{i}x\|_{2}^{2}+(\beta_{i}-\delta_{i})^{2}\leq(\beta_{i}+\delta_{i})^{2}\quad\iff\quad\|\xi^{i}+2\delta_{i}x\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\beta_{i}\delta_{i}.

Because we have both
\beta_{i}+\delta_{i}\geq 0
and
\beta_{i}\delta_{i}\geq 0
, we conclude that both
\beta_{i}
and
\delta_{i}
must be nonnegative.

Note that
\delta_{i}\geq 0
is equivalent to

0\leq\delta_{i}=-t+2x^{\top}c^{i}+r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2}=-\tr(X)+2x^{\top}c^{i}+r_{i}^{2}-\|c^{i}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus, we arrive at

\tr(X-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{i})^{\top})\leq r_{i}^{2}.

(12)

In fact, (
12
) can be obtained in much simpler way by noting that squaring both sides of
\|x-c_{i}\|_{2}\leq r_{i}
results in
(x-c^{i})^{\top}(x-c^{i})=x^{\top}x-(c^{i})^{\top}x-x^{\top}c^{i}+(c^{i})^{\top}c^{i}\leq r_{i}^{2}
and then writing the nonlinear term
x^{\top}x
as
\tr(X)
in the lifted space.
Typically (
12
) is added to the Shor SDP relaxation as the most basic constraint to capture the underlying quadratic. We just showed that it is implied by SOC-RLT constraint and
X\succeq xx^{\top}
requirement.

Moreover, from
0\preceq(X-xx^{\top})+(x-c^{i})(x-c^{i})^{\top}=X-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{i})^{\top}
and (
12
) we deduce that

X-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{i})^{\top}\preceq r_{i}^{2}I_{n}.

(13)

In addition, we always have

\displaystyle 0\preceq

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}x-c^{i}\\
x-c^{j}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x-c^{i}\\
x-c^{j}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}xx^{\top}-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{i})^{\top}&xx^{\top}-x(c^{j})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{j})^{\top}\\
xx^{\top}-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{j}x^{\top}+c^{j}(c^{i})^{\top}&xx^{\top}-x(c^{j})^{\top}-c^{j}x^{\top}+c^{j}(c^{j})^{\top}\end{bmatrix}.

As
xx^{\top}\preceq X
and
0\preceq\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}
and Kronecker product of two PSD matrices is PSD as well, we also have

0\preceq\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\otimes(X-xx^{\top})=\begin{bmatrix}X-xx^{\top}&X-xx^{\top}\\
X-xx^{\top}&X-xx^{\top}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus, summing up these two inequalities, we conclude that

\displaystyle 0\preceq\begin{bmatrix}X-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{i})^{\top}&X-x(c^{j})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{j})^{\top}\\
X-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{j}x^{\top}+c^{j}(c^{i})^{\top}&X-x(c^{j})^{\top}-c^{j}x^{\top}+c^{j}(c^{j})^{\top}\end{bmatrix}.

(14)

Note that the diagonal blocks in the matrix in (
14
) are of form
X-x(c^{i})^{\top}-c^{i}x^{\top}+c^{i}(c^{i})^{\top}
, and by (
13
) these block matrices are dominated in the PSD sense by
r_{i}^{2}I_{n}
. Therefore, we conclude that inequality (
11
) is satisfied whenever
Z\in{\cal C}_{\{i,j\}}
and
Z_{n+2,n+2}=1
.
∎

4
The moment-SOS viewpoint

Here we describe another way to derive
{\cal C}_{m}
as a particular case of moment-SOS hierarchy.

The moment-SOS hierarchy, introduced by Lasserre
[
11
]
and Parrilo
[
13
]
, is a well known and studied systematic way of obtaining semidefinite relaxations of increasing tightness and size. See also
[
12
]
for a comprehensive introduction.

Here we briefly describe the idea for the moment hierarchy. For a ground set
\widehat{S}:=\{x:~g_{i}(x)\geq 0,\,i\in[k]\}
defined by
k
polynomial inequalities
g_{i}(x)\geq 0
,
i\in[k]
, the variable in the moment hierarchy is a linear functional on some finite dimensional subspace of polynomials, usually polynomials up to a fixed degree
2d
. Computationally, this linear functional is encoded by the so-called
pseudomoments
, which are the values of this linear functional on a basis of the subspace of polynomials, usually chosen to be the monomial basis. These pseudomoments on
\widehat{S}
are required to share certain properties of true moments on
\widehat{S}
, which send
f
to
\int f\,d\mu
for some nonnegative measure
\mu
on
\widehat{S}
. Namely, the pseudoments of provably nonnegative polynomials should be nonnegative, which include global sum-of-squares, product of constraint
g_{i}
with sum-of-squares (Putinar type), and product of the constraints
g_{i}g_{j}
with possibly other constraints or sum-of-squares (Schmüdgen type). The pseudomoments can be conveniently arranged into the form of a pseudomoment matrix, whose rows and columns are indexed by polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
, and the
(f_{i},f_{j})
entry of the matrix is the pseudomoment of
f_{i}f_{j}
.

Going back to our setting, the constraints
g_{i}(x)\geq 0
are the ball constraints
r_{i}^{2}-\|x-c^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0
. The moment matrix will have size
(n+2)\times(n+2)
, indexed by monomials of degree at most 1, plus one more
term
\|x\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}
. Same as the usual moment hierarchy, the
(f_{i},f_{j})
entry of the pseudomoment matrix models the pseudoment
{\cal M}(f_{i}f_{j})
. Specifically, we write our matrix variable
Z
as:

Z=\begin{bmatrix}{\cal M}(x_{1}^{2})&\ldots&{\cal M}(x_{1}x_{n})&{\cal M}(x_{1}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})&{\cal M}(x_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
{\cal M}(x_{1}x_{n})&\ldots&{\cal M}(x_{n}^{2})&{\cal M}(x_{n}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})&{\cal M}(x_{n})\\
{\cal M}(x_{1}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})&\ldots&{\cal M}(x_{n}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})&{\cal M}((\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})^{2})&{\cal M}(\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})\\
{\cal M}(x_{1})&\ldots&{\cal M}(x_{n})&{\cal M}(\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})&{\cal M}(1)\end{bmatrix}

Now we derive constraints on
Z
as a pseudomoment matrix as follows:

1.

Z\succeq 0
, as
{\cal M}(\sigma^{2})\geq 0
for all
\sigma
that is a linear combination of
1,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}
;

2.

\left\langle Q,Z\right\rangle=0
, as by linearity
\sum_{i}{\cal M}(x_{i}^{2})={\cal M}(\sum x_{i}^{2})
;

3.

(d^{i})^{\top}Zd^{j}\geq 0
for all
i,j\in[m]
, as
{\cal M}(g_{i}(x)g_{j}(x))\geq 0
;

4.

PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
follows from that
{\cal M}(g_{i}(x)\sigma^{2})\geq 0
for
\sigma
of the form
ax_{j}+b
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R},j\in[m]
. This is equivalent to
\begin{bmatrix}{\cal M}(x_{j}^{2}g_{i}(x))&{\cal M}(x_{j}g_{i}(x))\\
{\cal M}(x_{j}g_{i}(x))&{\cal M}(g_{i}(x))\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0
for all
j\in[m]
, which then implies

P\begin{bmatrix}{\cal M}(x_{1}g_{i}(x))\\
\vdots\\
{\cal M}(x_{n}g_{i}(x))\\
{\cal M}(g_{i}(x)\sum_{j}x_{j}^{2})\\
{\cal M}(g_{i}(x))\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2},

and this is exactly
PZd^{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{n+2}
.

Thus we have arrived at Burer’s lifted formulation in
[
7
]
using moment hierarchy. This also shows that the lifted formulation is implied by the full second-level moment hierarchy.

5
Conclusion and further remarks

In this paper, we show that Burer’s lifted convex relaxation for
S
is provably tighter than the Kronecker RLT inequalities as well as Zhen et al.’s RLT inequalities.

From a more theoretical perspective, we show that Burer’s lifted relaxation can be interpreted as a particular case of moment-SOS hierarchy (to be more precise, a relaxation of the second-level of moment-SOS hierarchy). The first-level of the moment-SOS hierarchy is known to be the same as the Shor relaxation. While the usual setup of moment hierarchy is known to be computationally inefficient even for the second level, Burer’s lifted convex relaxation increases the matrix size by only one compared to the first level hierarchy, and yet seemingly retains most of the strength of the second-level hierarchy, as suggested by results of this paper and
[
7
]
. In general, it will be interesting to study the relationship between various RLT type inequalities and the moment-SOS hierarchy.

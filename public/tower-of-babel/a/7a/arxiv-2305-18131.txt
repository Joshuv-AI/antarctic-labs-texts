Abstract
. We present a uniform theory of constructible sheaves on arbitrary schemes with coefficients in topological or even condensed rings. This is accomplished by defining lisse sheaves to be the dualizable objects in the derived ∞ \infty -category of proétale sheaves, while constructible sheaves are those that are lisse on a stratification. We show that constructible sheaves satisfy proétale descent. We also establish a t-structure on constructible sheaves in a wide range of cases. We finally provide a toolset to manipulate categories of constructible sheaves with respect to the choices of coefficient rings, and use this to prove that our notions reproduce and extend the various approaches to, say, constructible ℓ \ell -adic sheaves in the literature.

Introduction

Initially, étale cohomology of a scheme

In order to have a cohomology theory producing vector spaces over characteristic zero fields, one fixes a prime number

For many purposes, it is useful to impose a finiteness condition on sheaves known as constructibility.
This is accomplished in

Here we introduce a notion of lisse and constructible sheaves on arbitrary schemes

\mathrm{Perf}_{\Lambda_{i}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Perf}_{\Lambda_{1}},M\mapsto M\otimes_{\Lambda_{i}}\Lambda_{1}
is conservative for any
i\geq 1
.
So let
M\in\mathrm{Perf}_{\Lambda_{i}}
such that
M\otimes_{\Lambda_{i}}\Lambda_{1}\simeq 0
.
We choose a bounded complex
(M^{a}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow M^{b})
of finitely generated projective
\Lambda_{i}
-modules representing
M
.
We need to show that the complex is exact.
The derived tensor product
M\otimes_{\Lambda_{i}}\Lambda_{1}
is represented by
(M^{a}\otimes_{\Lambda_{i}}\Lambda_{1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow M^{b}\otimes_{\Lambda_{i}}\Lambda_{1})
.
As the complex is exact by assumption, the map
M^{b-1}\otimes_{\Lambda_{i}}\Lambda_{1}\rightarrow M^{b}\otimes_{\Lambda_{i}}\Lambda_{1}
is surjective.
Nakayama’s lemma shows that
M^{b-1}\rightarrow M^{b}
is surjective as well, using that
\ker(\Lambda_{i}\rightarrow\Lambda_{1})
is generated by nilpotent elements.
By projectivity, this map splits so that
M^{b-1}\simeq M^{b}\oplus N^{b-1}
.
We are reduced to show that the resulting complex
(M^{a}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow M^{b-2}\rightarrow N^{b-1})
is exact.
Continuing by induction on the length
b-a
implies our claim.
∎

Write
\mathrm{Cat}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
for the full subcategory spanned by
n
-categories
[
Lur09
, Section 2.3.4]
, and similarly
\mathrm{Cat}_{n}(\mathrm{Idem})\subset\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}(\mathrm{Idem})
for the full subcategory consisting of idempotent complete

-categories.
Recall that
\mathrm{Cat}_{n},\mathrm{Cat}_{n}(\mathrm{Idem})
admit limits and filtered colimits and that the above inclusions preserve these.
Let us record the following lemma from
[
BM21
, Lemma 3.7]
for later use:

Lemma 2.3
.

In
\mathrm{Cat}_{n}
and
\mathrm{Cat}_{n}(\mathrm{Idem})
, filtered colimits commute with totalizations (=
\Delta
-indexed limits).

Proof.

By
[
BM21
, Lemma 3.7, Example 3.6]
, this holds true for

.
Now use that
\mathrm{Cat}_{n}(\mathrm{Idem})\subset\mathrm{Cat}_{n}
preserves limits and filtered colimits.

∎

3.
Definitions and basic facts

In this section, we start out setting up the basics of a theory of lisse (French for smooth) and constructible sheaves on schemes, with coefficients in a condensed (unital, commutative) ring
\Lambda
.

Throughout the paper, following
[
BS15
]
, we fix a strong limit cardinal
\kappa
. All schemes
X
appearing in this paper are required to have
|X|<\kappa
. (Alternatively, one may avoid such a choice using the set-theoretic conventions in
[
BH19
, §1.2]
.)

For a scheme

, we denote by

its proétale site introduced in
[
BS15
, §4]
.
Thus,
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
is the category of weakly étale schemes over

with covers given by families
\{U_{i}\rightarrow U\}
of maps in

that are fpqc-coverings, that is, any open affine in
U
is mapped onto by an open affine in
\sqcup_{i}U_{i}
.
Being a major difference with étale site

, any scheme
X
admits a proétale cover by w-contractible affine schemes, that is, by affine schemes
U
such that any weakly étale surjection
V\rightarrow U
splits.

Also, for any profinite set
S
, we denote by

the category of profinite sets over
S
with covers given by finite families of jointly surjective maps.
The
w-contractible objects in

are the extremally disconnected profinite sets.
We use this in the special case where
S=*
is the singleton: For any scheme
X
, there is the map of sites

p_{X}\colon X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}\longrightarrow*_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}

given by the limit-preserving functor (in the opposite direction)
S=\lim S_{i}\mapsto\lim X^{S_{i}}=:X\times S

Throughout this section,
\Lambda
is a condensed (unital, commutative) ring
\Lambda
, that is, a sheaf of rings on

, see
[
CS
]
.
We write

.
The condensed rings considered in this paper arise as follows:

Example 3.1
.

Every T1-topological ring
\Lambda
induces sheaf of rings, also denoted by
\Lambda
, on
*_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
:

S\longmapsto\mathop{\rm Maps}\nolimits_{\mathrm{cont}}(S,\Lambda),

that is, continuous maps from the profinite set
S
to
\Lambda
.
In fact, we have a functor from the category of T1-topological rings into the category of condensed rings.
This functor is fully faithful when restricted to those

whose underlying topological space is compactly generated.

In fact, for any scheme or profinite set
X
, the sheaf
\Lambda_{X}
is nothing but the sheaf of rings on
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
given by

U\longmapsto\mathop{\rm Maps}\nolimits_{\mathrm{cont}}(U,\Lambda),

that is, continuous maps from the underlying topological space of
U
into
\Lambda
: any continuous map
U\to\Lambda
from a w-contractible affine

factors uniquely through

because

is T1.
In
[

, Lemma 4.2.12]

.

Favorable examples of T1-topological rings

include all discrete rings, adic rings,
algebraic field extensions

equipped with the colimit topology, their open subrings of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
, but also the ring of adeles
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{T}
prime to some finite set of places

in some number field

For any scheme
X
, we denote by
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
the derived
\infty
-category of the abelian category of sheaves of
\Lambda_{X}
-modules on
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
.

\infty
-category which is symmetric monoidal and closed.
The monoidal structure is denoted by
\operatorname{-}\otimes_{\Lambda_{X}}\operatorname{-}
and the inner homomorphisms by

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda_{X}}(\operatorname{-},\operatorname{-})=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)}(\operatorname{-},\operatorname{-})={\rm R}\Gamma\big(X,\underline{\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits}_{\Lambda_{X}}(\operatorname{-},\operatorname{-})\big)

For any

, the truncations

,
\tau^{\leq n}
in the standard t-structure on
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
induce (in cohomological notation) adjunctions

\tau^{\geq n}:\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{D}^{\geq n}(X,\Lambda):\operatorname{incl},\;\;\;\;\operatorname{incl}:\mathrm{D}^{\leq n}(X,\Lambda)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda):\tau^{\leq n},

where

is the inclusion of the respective subcategories.
The subcategory
\mathrm{D}^{\leq 0}(X,\Lambda)
is preserved under
\operatorname{-}\otimes_{\Lambda_{X}}\operatorname{-}

As

is locally weakly contractible

BS15
, Proposition 4.2.8]

\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
is left-complete (equivalently, Postnikov towers converge in the associated hypercompleted
\infty
-topos) and the
\infty
-category

is compactly generated, see
[
BS15
, Proposition 3.2.3]
.
A family of compact generators is given by the objects
\Lambda_{X}[U]\in\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
, for

w-contractible affine, corepresenting the functor
{\rm R}\Gamma(U,\operatorname{-})
.
We remark that
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of
\Lambda_{X}
-modules on the hypercompleted
\infty
-topos associated with

.
The above also applies to any profinite set
S
and the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{D}(S,\Lambda)
in place of
X
and
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
(in fact, this is just a special case).

If
f\colon Y\rightarrow X
is any morphism of schemes, then
\Lambda_{Y}=f^{-1}\Lambda_{X}
.
It is formal to check that the ordinary pullback, respectively pushforward of sheaves induces an adjunction

f^{*}=f^{-1}:\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{D}(Y,\Lambda):f_{*},

where

is exact, t-exact and symmetric monoidal.
Similarly, if
\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda^{\prime}
is a morphism of condensed rings, then the forgetful functor
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
admits a symmetric monoidal left adjoint

\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda^{\prime}),\;\;M\mapsto M\otimes_{\Lambda_{X}}\Lambda_{X}^{\prime}.

Let us denote by
\Gamma(X,\Lambda)
the (underived) global sections of
\Lambda_{X}
viewed as a ring.
Then there is the functor

(3.1)

\mathrm{Mod}_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda),\;\;M\mapsto M_{X},

characterized in

as the colimit-preserving extension of
\Gamma(X,\Lambda)\mapsto\Lambda_{X}
.
Explicitly,

is the hypersheaf associated with the presheaf on

given by

.
This functor is symmetric monoidal and makes
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
a commutative algebra object in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{St}}_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}
.
The sections of

on w-contractible qcqs

{\rm R}\Gamma(U,M_{X})\cong M\otimes_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}\Gamma(U,\Lambda).

In particular, if

.

Remark 3.2
.

Here is an equivalent way of defining the functor
M\mapsto M_{X}
in (

):
For any scheme or profinite set
X
, there is a natural map
\underline{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}\rightarrow\Lambda_{X}
of sheaf of rings on

\underline{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}

Then the functor (

where

denotes the constant sheaf.
As an example, let

,

(see ?THM?
) and

a

More precisely, writing

.

Recall that a subset of a qcqs topological space is called

Also, recall the notion of dualizable objects in symmetric monoidal categories
[
Lur17

Definition 3.3
.

Let

be a scheme or a profinite set, and
\Lambda
a condensed ring.

(1)

A sheaf

is called

if it is dualizable.

(2)

A sheaf

is called

if, for any open affine

into constructible locally closed subschemes

is lisse.

If

is qcqs (=quasi-separated and quasi-compact) and

into constructible locally closed subschemes

such that

is lisse.
The argument is purely topological and the same as in
[
Sta17
, Tag
095E
]
.

The full subcategories of
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
of lisse, respectively constructible
\Lambda
-sheaves are denoted by

\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(X,\Lambda)\;\subset\;\mathrm{D}_{\operatorname{cons}}(X,\Lambda).

Both
\infty
-categories are naturally commutative algebra objects in

, that is, idempotent complete stable
\Gamma(X,\Lambda)
-linear symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories.

If

For lisse sheaves, this follows from the monoidality of

.
For constructible sheaves, one additionally reduces to the case of affine schemes so that

induces a spectral map on the underlying topological spaces, and thus is continuous in the constructible topology, see
[

]

If

is a map of condensed rings, then the functor

preserves lisse, respectively constructible sheaves and hence induces functors

\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(X,\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(X,\Lambda^{\prime}),\;\;\;\;\mathrm{D}_{\operatorname{cons}}(X,\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\operatorname{cons}}(X,\Lambda^{\prime}).

For any constructible closed immersion

with open complement

, we have adjunctions

(3.3)

j_{!}:\mathrm{D}(U,\Lambda)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda):j^{*},\;\;\;i_{*}:\mathrm{D}(Z,\Lambda)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda):i^{!},

fitting in fiber sequences
j_{!}j^{*}\rightarrow{\rm id}\rightarrow i_{*}i^{*}

Lemma 3.4
.

In the above situation, the functors

are t-exact and preserve the full subcategories of constructible sheaves.

Proof.

By
[
BS15
, Lemma 6.2.1 (1) + (2)]
, the functors

induce equivalences onto the full subcategory of

spanned by objects supported on

, respectively

Their inverses are given by

which are clearly

-exact,
hence so are the functors
i_{*},j_{!}
.
Using the formulas (

∎

We use the following terminology throughout:

Definition 3.5
.

A sheaf

for some (perfect) complex of

The converse holds for discrete coefficient rings

(?THM?

.
It also holds for schemes having locally finitely many irreducible components, see ?THM?
.
The following example of a lisse sheaf, which is based on

Example 3.6

Let

Take

viewed as a profinite ring.
Then the endomorphisms of

By contrast, if

4.
Descent properties

Lisse and constructible sheaves interact nicely with proétale descent.
We first study lisse sheaves on w-contractible schemes, which may be thought of as a basis for the proétale topology of objects having cohomological dimension zero.
We then establish hyperdescent for
\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}
and
\mathrm{D}_{\operatorname{cons}}
and draw some consequences.

4.1.
Lisse sheaves on w-contractible schemes

Recall the functor
\mathrm{Mod}_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda),M\mapsto M_{X}
from (
3.1
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a w-contractible qcqs scheme, or an extremally disconnected profinite set.

(1)

The functor (
3.1
) induces an adjunction

(\operatorname{-})_{X}:\mathrm{Mod}_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda):{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-}).

Both adjoints are colimit-preserving and symmetric monoidal.
In addition,
(\operatorname{-})_{X}
is fully faithful and
{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})
is t-exact.

(2)

The adjunction induces an equivalence on dualizable objects:

(\operatorname{-})_{X}:\mathrm{Perf}_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}\cong\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(X,\Lambda):{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})

Proof.

For (
1
), we note that the spectra-valued functor
{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})\colon\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}
is right adjoint to the unique colimit-preserving functor
\mathrm{Sp}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
mapping the sphere spectrum to
\Lambda_{X}
.
Since
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}
is the category of modules over the monad associated to the adjunction, this induces an adjunction

\mathrm{Mod}_{{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda).

Since
X
is w-contractible qcqs, respectively extremally disconneced, it is w-contractible coherent in the topos-theoretic sense.
Thus
{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})
is limit-preserving, t-exact and preserves colimits of uniformly bounded below diagrams, hence all colimits using the left-completeness of
\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
.
In particular,
{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\Lambda)=\Gamma(X,\Lambda)
.
The unit of the adjunction
{\rm id}\rightarrow{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})\circ(\operatorname{-})_{X}
is an equivalence by (
3.2
) so that
(\operatorname{-})_{X}
is fully faithful.
Clearly,
(\operatorname{-})_{X}
is also colimit-preserving and symmetric monoidal.
For
M,N\in\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)
, their tensor product
M\otimes_{\Lambda_{X}}N
is the
\infty
-sheafification of the presheaf
U\mapsto{\rm R}\Gamma(U,M)\otimes_{{\rm R}\Gamma(U,\Lambda)}{\rm R}\Gamma(U,N)
.
Again, since
X
is w-contractible coherent, the sheafification is unnecessary, so its global sections are equivalent to
{\rm R}\Gamma(X,M)\otimes_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}{\rm R}\Gamma(X,N)
.

For (
2
), we note that the adjunction restricts to an adjunction on dualizable objects by monoidality of both functors.
The counit of this adjunction
(\operatorname{-})_{X}\circ{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})\rightarrow{\rm id}
is an equivalence if and only if the functor

{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})\colon\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(X,\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{Perf}_{\Gamma(X,\Lambda)}

is fully faithful, see
[
Lur09
, Proposition 5.2.7.4]
.
For
M,N\in\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(X,\Lambda)
, this follows from
\underline{\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits}_{\Lambda_{X}}(M,N)\cong N\otimes_{\Lambda_{X}}M^{\vee}
upon applying the symmetric monoidal functor
{\rm R}\Gamma(X,\operatorname{-})
.
∎

The following example, communicated to us by Peter Scholze, shows that the functor
(\operatorname{-})_{X}
is not t-exact in general.

Example 4.2
.

Let
X=\beta{{\mathbb{N}}}
be the Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers viewed as an extremally disconnected profinite set.
Let
\Lambda={\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
viewed as a condensed ring, see ?THM?
.
The map
{{\mathbb{N}}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

uniquely extends to a continuous map

by the universal property of

, that is,
f\in\mathop{\rm Maps}\nolimits_{\mathrm{cont}}(\beta{{\mathbb{N}}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})=\Gamma(X,\Lambda)
.
One checks that the complex

is exact.
However, the induced complex on the level of sheaves is not exact because

, where

denotes the boundary.

If
X
is a qcqs scheme, then its underlying topological space is spectral
[

, Tag

]
.
Thus, the set of connected components
\pi_{0}X
endowed with the quotient topology is a profinite space.
Any map

of profinite sets can be written as profinite
\pi_{0}X
-sets

such that each

is the base change of a map of finite sets, see

.
If we equip the topological space

.
The induced transition maps
X\times_{\pi_{0}X}S_{j}\rightarrow X\times_{\pi_{0}X}S_{i}
,

are affine so that the limit

X\times_{\pi_{0}X}S\hskip 2.84544pt\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\hskip 2.84544pt\lim X\times_{\pi_{0}X}S_{i}

exists in the category of

-schemes.
The functor

from profinite

(4.1)

\pi_{X}\colon X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}\longrightarrow(\pi_{0}X)_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},

factorizing

Proposition 4.3

Let

(1)

The functor

(2)

A

lies in the essential image of

if and only if for all maps

between w-contractible affine schemes inducing isomorphisms

(3)

The functor
\pi_{X}^{*}
induces an equivalence

\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(\pi_{0}X,\Lambda)\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\mathrm{D}_{\rm lis}(X,\Lambda).

Proof.

We adjust the argument given in

Singular Degenerations of Lie Supergroups of Type 𝑫 ⁡ ( 𝟐 , 𝟏 , 𝒂 ) \boldsymbol{D(2,1;a)}
Kenji IOHARA † and Fabio GAVARINI ‡

Abstract
The complex Lie superalgebras 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} of type D ⁡ ( 2 , 1 , a ) D(2,1;a) – also denoted by 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 4 , 2 , a ) \mathfrak{osp}(4,2;a) – are usually considered for “non-singular” values of the parameter a a , for which they are simple. In this paper we introduce five suitable integral forms of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , that are well-defined at singular values too, giving rise to “singular specializations” that are no longer simple: this extends the family of simple objects of type D ⁡ ( 2 , 1 , a ) D(2,1;a) in five different ways. The resulting five families coincide for general values of a a , but are different at “singular” ones: here they provide non-simple Lie superalgebras, whose structure we describe explicitly. We also perform the parallel construction for complex Lie supergroups and describe their singular specializations (or “degenerations”) at singular values of a a . Although one may work with a single complex parameter a a , in order to stress the overall 𝔖 3 \mathfrak{S}_{3} -symmetry of the whole situation, we shall work (following Kaplansky) with a two-dimensional parameter σ = ( σ 1 , σ 2 , σ 3 ) \mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}) ranging in the complex affine plane σ 1 + σ 2 + σ 3 = 0 \sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}=0 .

1
Introduction

In the classification of simple finite-dimensional complex Lie superalgebras – due to Kac (cf.
[
12
]
) – a special one-parameter family occurs, whose elements
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
depend on a parameter
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}
. These are “generically non-isomorphic”, and all isomorphisms between them are encoded in a free action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
on the family
\{\mathfrak{g}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}}
. It was pointed out in
[
12
]
that the Cartan matrix
A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&-1&0\\
-1&0&a\\
0&-1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)
used to define this Lie superalgebra had already appeared in
[
18
]
, as a Cartan matrix of a one-parameter family of
16
-dimensional simple Lie algebras over a field
k
of characteristic
2
with
a\in k\setminus\{0,1\}
.

For any
a\in\{1,-2,-1/2\}
one has
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\cong\mathfrak{osp}(4,2)
, which is of type
D(2,1)
: thus Kac called each
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
to be “
of type
D(2,1;a)
” – while
D(m,n)
is the type of the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(2m,2n)
. For the same reason, some authors, for example
[
3
]
– cf. also
[
2
]
– use instead notation
\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;a)
.
By general theory, one can complete each of the (simple) Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
and form a so-called super Harish-Chandra pair: and then one associates to the latter a corresponding complex Lie supergroup, say
\mathbf{G}_{a}
, whose tangent Lie superalgebra is
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
– as prescribed in Kac’ classification of simple algebraic supergroups, cf.
[
11
]
. All these
\mathbf{G}_{a}
’s form a family
\{\mathbf{G}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}}
, which bears a free
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
-action that induces the
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
-action on
\{\mathfrak{g}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}}
. The starting point of the present paper is the following question: can we “take the limit” (in some sense) of
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
for
a
approaching to the “singular values”
a=0
and
a=-1
? And if yes, what is the structure of the resulting “limit” Lie superalgebra? Similarly, we raise the same questions for the family of the supergroups
\mathbf{G}_{a}
.

In this article, we show that there are several ways to answer, in the positive, these questions. In fact, we present
five
possible ways to complete the family of simple Lie superalgebras
D(2,1;a)
with additional Lie superalgebras for the “singular values”
a\in\{0,-1\}
. Each one of these new, extra objects can be thought of as a “limit” of the older ones; however, the existence of different options show that such “limits” have no intrinsic meaning, but strongly depend on some choice – roughly, on “how you approach the singular point”. For each of these choices, the corresponding new objects that are “limits” of the (original) simple Lie superalgebras
D(2,1;a)
happen to be non-simple, and we describe explicitly their structure, which is different for the different choices. Therefore, we extend the old family
\{\mathfrak{g}_{a}=\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;a)\}_{a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}}
of simple Lie superalgebras to five larger families, indexed by the points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\cup\{\ast\}
, whose elements at “non-singular values”
a\in\{0,-1,\infty,\ast\}
are
non-simple
– which is why we call them “degenerations” – and (when comparing one family with a different one) non-isomorphic.

By the way, our analysis is by no means exhaustive: one can still provide further ways to complete the family of the simple
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
’s (for non-singular values of
a
) by adding some extra objects at singular values of
a
, right in the same spirit but with different outcomes. Our goal here is only to explain the
existence
and
non-uniqueness
of such constructions.
A few words about our construction. First, instead of working with Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
indexed by a single parameter
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}
– later extended to
a\in\mathbb{C}
– we rather deal with a multiparameter
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V:=\big\{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\,\big|\,\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}=0\big\}
. The starting point is a construction – due to Kaplansky, cf.
[
13
]
; see also
[
15
]
– that for each
\sigma\in V
provides a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
: this yields a full family of Lie superalgebras
{\big\{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\big\}}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
, forming a bundle over
V
, naturally endowed with an action of the group
\mathcal{G}:=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathfrak{S}_{3}
via Lie superalgebra isomorphisms. For each
\mathbf{\sigma}
in the “general locus”
V^{\times}:=V\setminus\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}\{\sigma_{i}=0\}\big)
we have
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{a}
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}
so the original family
\{\mathfrak{g}_{a}=\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;a)\}_{a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}}
of simple Lie superalgebras
is taken into account; in addition, the
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
’s are well-defined also at singular values
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V\cap\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}\{\sigma_{i}=0\}\big)
, but there they are non-simple instead.

Thus Kaplansky’s family of Lie superalgebras provides a first solution to our problem. In addition, we re-visit this construction and devise five recipes to construct similar families, as follows. For
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
, we fix in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
a particular
\mathbb{C}
-basis, call it
B
, in such a way that the structure constants are polynomials in
\mathbf{\sigma}
. When we replace
\mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
with a formal parameter
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, the previous multiplication table defines a Lie superalgebra structure on the free
{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}
-module with basis
B
, denoted by
\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{B}}({\boldsymbol{x}})
. Then for each
\mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in V
the quotient
\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{B}}(\mathbf{\sigma}):=\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{B}}({\boldsymbol{x}})/{(x_{i}-\sigma_{i})}_{i=1,2,3}\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{B}}({\boldsymbol{x}})
is a well-defined complex Lie superalgebra, such that
\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{B}}(\mathbf{\sigma})\cong\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
for
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
; thus we get a whole family
\{\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{B}}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
as requested, that actually depends on the choice of the basis
B
. We present five explicit examples that give rise to five different outcomes – one being Kaplansky’s family. Indeed, at each point of the “singular locus”
V\cap\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}\{\sigma_{i}=0\}\big)
these families present different (non-isomorphic) non-simple Lie superalgebras, that we describe in detail. As a second contribution, we perform a parallel construction at the level of Lie supergroups: namely, for each
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V
we “complete” each Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{B}}(\mathbf{\sigma})
to form a super Harish-Chandra pair, and then take the corresponding (complex holomorphic) Lie supergroup. This yields a family
\{\mathbf{G}_{{}_{B}}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
of Lie supergroups, with
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
isomorphic to
\mathbf{G}_{a}
for a suitable
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}
for non-singular values of
\mathbf{\sigma}
, while
\mathbf{G}_{{}_{B}}(\mathbf{\sigma})
is not simple for singular values instead; moreover, the group
\mathcal{G}:=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathfrak{S}_{3}
freely acts on this family via Lie supergroup isomorphisms. In other words, we complete the “old” family of the simple Lie supergroups
\mathbf{G}_{a}
’s (isomorphic to suitable
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
’s) by suitably adding new, non-simple Lie supergroups at singular values of
\mathbf{\sigma}
. The construction depends on
B
, and with our five, previously fixed choices we find five different families: for each of them, we describe explicitly the non-simple supergroups
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
at singular values of
\mathbf{\sigma}
– which are referred to as “degenerations” of the (previously known, simple)
\mathbf{G}_{a}
’s.

This analysis might be reformulated in the language of
deformation theory
of supermanifold – e.g., as treated in
[
17
]
. However, this goes beyond the scope of the present article.
This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly recall the basic algebraic background necessary for this work, in particular, some language about
supermathematics
. In Section
3
, we introduce our Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}=\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})
. Several
integral forms
of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
are introduced in Section
4
. In particular, as an application, the structure of their
singular degenerations
is studied in detail (Theorems
4.1
,
4.2
,
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.5
). Section
5
is the last highlight of this paper: we introduce and analyze the Lie supergroups whose Lie superalgebras are studied in Section
4
, and we describe the (non-simple) structure of their degenerations – i.e., the member of the families at singular values of
\mathbf{\sigma}
(Theorems
5.1
,
5.2
,
5.3
,
5.4
and
5.5
).

As the main objects treated in this article have many special features, most of the above descriptions are given in a down-to-earth manner, so that even the readers who are not familiar with the subject could follow easily our exposition.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the notions and language of Lie superalgebras and Lie supergroups. Our purpose is to fix the terminology, but everything indeed is standard matter.

2.1
Basic superobjects

All throughout the paper, we work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers (nevertheless, immediate generalizations are possible), unless otherwise stated. By
\mathbb{C}
-supermodule
, or
\mathbb{C}
-super vector space
, any
\mathbb{C}
-module
V
endowed with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{{\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}\}
is the group with two elements. Then
V_{\bar{0}}
and its elements are called
even
, while
V_{\bar{1}}
and its elements
odd
. By
|x|(\in\mathbb{Z}_{2})
we denote the
parity
of any non-zero homogeneous element, defined by the condition
x\in V_{|x|}
.

We call
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebra
any associative, unital
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A
which is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded: so
A
has a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading
A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}
, and
A_{\mathbf{a}}A_{\mathbf{b}}\subseteq A_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}
. Any such
A
is said to be
commutative
if
xy=(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
for all homogeneous
x,y\in A
; so, in particular,
z^{2}=0
for all
z\in A_{\bar{1}}
. All
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebras form a category, whose morphisms are those of unital
\mathbb{C}
-algebras preserving the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading; inside it, commutative
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebras form a subcategory, that we denote by
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{salg}}}
)
. We denote by
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{alg}}}
)
the category of (associative, unital) commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebras, and by
{(\text{\bf mod})}
that of
\mathbb{C}
-modules. Note also that there is an obvious functor
{(\ )}_{\bar{0}}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{salg}}}$)}\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{alg}}}$)}
given on objects by
A\mapsto A_{\bar{0}}
. We call
Weil superalgebra
any finite-dimensional commutative
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebra
A
such that
A=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathfrak{N}(A)
where
\mathbb{C}
is even and
\mathfrak{N}(A)={\mathfrak{N}(A)}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{N}(A)}_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded nilpotent ideal (the
nilradical
of
A
). Every Weil superalgebra
A
is endowed with a canonical epimorphisms
p_{A}\colon A\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and an embedding
u_{A}\colon\mathbb{C}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow A
, such that
p_{A}\circ u_{A}={\rm id}
. Weil superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
form a full subcategory of
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{salg}}}
)
, denoted by
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}
)
. Finally, let
\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Walg}}}$)}:=\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\cap\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{alg}}}$)}
be the category of
Weil algebras
(
over
\mathbb{C})
, i.e., the full subcategory of all totally even objects in
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}
)
– namely, those whose odd part is trivial. Then the functor
{(\ )}_{\bar{0}}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{salg}}}$)}\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{alg}}}$)}
obviously restricts to a similar functor
{(\ )}_{\bar{0}}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Walg}}}$)}
given again by
A\mapsto A_{\bar{0}}
.

2.2
Lie superalgebras

By definition, a
Lie superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{C}
-supermodule
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
with a
(
Lie super
)
bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
,
(x,y)\mapsto[x,y]
, which is
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear, preserving the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading and satisfies the following (for all homogenenous
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
):

(a)

[x,y]+{(-1)}^{|x||y|}[y,x]=0

(
anti-symmetry
)
;

(b)

{(-1)}^{|x||z|}[x,[y,z]]+{(-1)}^{|y||x|}[y,[z,x]]+{(-1)}^{|z||y|}[z,[x,y]]=0

(
Jacobi identity
)
.

In this situation, we write
Y^{\langle 2\rangle}:=2^{-1}[Y,Y]

(\!\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})
for all
Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
. All Lie
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebras form a category, denoted by
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sLie}}}
)
, whose morphisms are
\mathbb{C}
-linear, preserving the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading and the bracket. Note that if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebra, then its even part
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
is automatically a Lie
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

Lie superalgebras can also be described in functorial language. Indeed, let
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lie}}}
)
be the category of Lie
\mathbb{C}
-algebras. Then every Lie
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sLie}}}$)}
defines a functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\ \text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lie}}}$)},\qquad A\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{g}}(A):=(A\otimes\mathfrak{g})_{\bar{0}}=(A_{\bar{0}}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})\oplus(A_{\bar{1}}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}).

Indeed,
A\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie superalgebra (in a suitable, more general sense, over
A
) on its own, with Lie bracket

[a\otimes X,a^{\prime}\otimes X^{\prime}]:={(-1)}^{|X||a^{\prime}|}aa^{\prime}\otimes[X,X^{\prime}]
; now
\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
is the even part of
A\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, hence it is a Lie algebra on its own.

2.3
Lie supergroups

We shall now recall, in steps, the notion of complex holomorphic “Lie supergroups”, as a special kind of “supermanifold”. The following is a very concise summary of a long, detailed theory: further details are, for instance, in
[
1
,
4
,
17
]
.

2.3.1
.

Supermanifolds.
By
superspace
we mean a pair
S=\big(|S|,\mathcal{O}_{S}\big)
of a topological space
|S|
and a sheaf of commutative superalgebras
\mathcal{O}_{S}
on it such that the stalk
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}
of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
at each point
x\in|S|
is a local superalgebra. A
morphism

\phi\colon S\longrightarrow T
between superspaces
S
and
T
is a pair
(|\phi|,\phi^{*})
where
|\phi|\colon|S|\longrightarrow|T|
is a continuous map of topological spaces and the induced morphism
\phi^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}_{T}\longrightarrow|\phi|_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
of sheaves on
|T|
is such that
\phi_{x}^{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{|\phi|(x)})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{x}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{|\phi|(x)}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
denote the maximal ideals in the stalks
\mathcal{O}_{T,|\phi|(x)}
and
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}
, respectively.

As basic model, the
linear supervariety

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{p|q}
(in Leites’ terminology) is, by definition, the topological space
\mathbb{C}^{p}
endowed with the following sheaf of commutative superalgebras:
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{p|q}}(U):=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}^{p}}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{q})
for any open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{p}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}^{p}}
is the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{q})
is the complex Grassmann algebra on
q
variables
\xi_{1}
,
\dots
,
\xi_{q}
of
odd
parity.
A
(
complex holomorphic
)
supermanifold
of (super)dimension
p|q
is a superspace
M=(|M|,\mathcal{O}_{M})
such that
|M|
is Hausdorff and second-countable and
M
is locally isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{p|q}
, i.e., for each
x\in|M|
there is an open set
V_{x}\subseteq|M|
with
x\in V_{x}
and
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{p}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{M}{|}_{V_{x}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{p|q}}{|}_{U}
(in particular, it is locally isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{p|q}
). A
morphism
between holomorphic supermanifolds is just a morphism (between them) as superspaces.

We denote the category of (complex holomorphic) supermanifolds by (
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hsmfd}}}
).

Let now
M
be a holomorphic supermanifold and
U
an open subset in
|M|
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{M}(U)
be the (nilpotent) ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{M}(U)
generated by the odd part of the latter: then
\mathcal{O}_{M}\big/\mathcal{I}_{M}
defines a sheaf of purely even superalgebras over
|M|
, locally isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}^{p}}
. Then
M_{\rm rd}:=(|M|,\mathcal{O}_{M}/\mathcal{I}_{M})
is a
classical
holomorphic manifold, called the
underlying holomorphic
(
sub
)
manifold
of
M
; the standard projection
s\mapsto\tilde{s}:=s+\mathcal{I}_{M}(U)
(for all
s\in\mathcal{O}_{M}(U)
) at the sheaf level yields an embedding
M_{\rm rd}\longrightarrow M
, so
M_{\rm rd}
can be seen as an embedded sub(super)manifold of
M
. The whole construction is clearly functorial in
M
.

Finally, each “classical” manifold can be seen as a “supermanifold”, just regarding its structure sheaf as one of superalgebras that are actually
totally even
, i.e., with trivial odd part. Conversely, any supermanifold enjoying the latter property is actually a manifold, nothing more. In other words, every manifold identify with a supermanifold
M
that actually coincides with its underlying (sub)manifold
M_{\rm rd}
.

2.3.2
.

Lie supergroups and the functorial approach.
A
group object
in the category (
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hsmfd}}}
) is called
(
complex holomorphic
)
Lie supergroup
. These objects, together with the obvious morphisms, form a subcategory among supermanifolds, denoted (
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lsgrp}}}
).

Lie supergroups – as well as supermanifolds – can also be conveniently studied via a functorial approach that we now briefly recall (cf.
[
1
]
or
[
9
]
for details).
Let
M
be a supermanifold. For every
x\in|M|
and every
A\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
we set
M_{A,x}={\mathrm{Hom}}_{\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{salg}}}$)}}(\mathcal{O}_{M,x},A)
and
M_{A}=\bigsqcup_{x\in|M|}M_{A,x}
; then we define
\mathcal{W}_{M}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{set}}}$)}
to be the functor given by
A\mapsto M_{A}
and
\rho\mapsto\rho^{(M)}
with
\rho^{(M)}\colon M_{A}\longrightarrow M_{B}
,
x_{A}\mapsto\rho\circ x_{A}
. Overall, this provides a functor
\mathcal{B}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hsmfd}}}$)}\longrightarrow[\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)},\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{set}}}$)}]
given on objects by
M\mapsto\mathcal{W}_{M}
; we can now refine still more.

Given a finite dimensional commutative algebra
A_{\bar{0}}
over
\mathbb{C}
, a (complex holomorphic)
A_{\bar{0}}
-manifold
is any manifold that is locally modelled on some open subset of some finite dimensional
A_{\bar{0}}
-module, so that the differential of every change of charts is an
A_{\bar{0}}
-module isomorphism. An
A_{\bar{0}}
-morphism
between two
A_{\bar{0}}
-manifolds is any smooth morphism whose differential is everywhere
A_{\bar{0}}
-linear (we then say that “it is
A_{\bar{0}}
-smooth”). Gathering all
A_{\bar{0}}
-manifolds (for all possible
A
), and suitably defining morphisms among them, one defines the category
(
\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\text{-}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hmfd}}}
)
of all “
\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}
-manifolds”.

A key point now is that
each
\mathcal{W}_{M}
turns out to be a functor from
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}
)
into
(
\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\text{-}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hmfd}}}
)
.
Furthermore, let
[[\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)},\text{\rm($\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\text{-}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hmfd}}}$)}]]
be the subcategory of
\big[\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)},\text{\rm($\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\text{-}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hmfd}}}$)}\big]
with the same objects but whose morphisms are all natural transformations
\phi\colon\mathcal{G}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that for every
A\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
the induced
\phi_{A}\colon\mathcal{G}(A)\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}(A)
is
A_{\bar{0}}
-smooth. Then
the second key point is that if
\phi\colon M\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow N
is a morphism of supermanifolds, then
\phi_{A}
is a morphism in
[[\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)},\text{\rm($\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\text{-}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hmfd}}}$)}]]
. The final outcome is that we have a functor
\mathcal{S}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hsmfd}}}$)}\longrightarrow[[\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
,
\text{\rm($\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\text{-}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hmfd}}}$)}]]
,
given on objects by
M\mapsto\mathcal{W}_{M}
; the key result is that
this embedding is full and faithful
, so that for any two supermanifolds
M
and
N
one has
M\cong N
if and only if
\mathcal{S}(M)\cong\mathcal{S}(N)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{W}_{M}\cong\mathcal{W}_{N}
.

Still relevant to us, is that the embedding
\mathcal{S}
preserves products, hence also group objects. Therefore,
a supermanifold
M
is a Lie supergroup if and only if
\mathcal{S}(M):=\mathcal{W}_{M}
takes values in the subcategory
(
among
\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}
-manifolds
)
of group objects
– thus each
\mathcal{W}_{M}(A)
is a group.

Finally, in the functorial approach the “classical” manifolds (i.e., totally even supermanifolds) can be recovered as follows: in the previous construction one simply has to replace the words “Weil superalgebras” with “Weil algebras” everywhere. It then follows, in particular, that the functor of points
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{M}}
of any holomorphic, manifold
\mathcal{M}
is actually a functor from
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Walg}}}
)
to
(
\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\text{-}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{hmfd}}}
)
; one can still see it as (the functor of points of) a
super
manifold – that is totally even, though – by composing it with the natural functor
{(\ )}_{\bar{0}}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Walg}}}$)}
. On the other hand, given any supermanifold
M
, say holomorphic, the functor of points of its underlying submanifold
M_{\rm rd}
is given by
\mathcal{W}_{M_{\rm rd}}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{W}_{M}(\mathcal{A})
for each
\mathcal{A}\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Walg}}}$)}
, or in short
\mathcal{W}_{M_{\rm rd}}=\mathcal{W}_{M}{\big|}_{\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Walg}}}$)}}
.

Finally, it is worth stressing that the functorial point of view on supermanifolds was originally developed – by Leites, Berezin, Deligne, Molotkov, Voronov and many others – in a slightly different way. Namely, they considered functors defined, rather than on Weil superalgebras, on
Grassmann
(
super
)
algebras
. Actually, the two approaches are equivalent: see
[
1
]
for a detailed, critical analysis of the matter.

There are some advantages in restricting the focus onto Grassmann algebras. For instance, they are the superalgebras of global sections onto the superdomains of dimension
0|q
– i.e., “super-points”. Therefore, if
M
is a supermanifold considered as a super-ringed space, its description via a functor defined on Grassmann algebras (only) can be really seen as the true restriction of the functor of points of
M
, considered as a super-ringed space.

On the other hand, the use of Weil superalgebras has the advantage that one can use it to perform differential calculus on functors
\mathcal{W}_{M}
, much in the spirit of Weil’s approach to differential calculus in algebraic geometry. Note also that some peculiar properties for Grassmann algebras are still available for every Weil superalgebra
A
: e.g., the existence of the maps
p_{A}\colon A\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
u_{A}\colon\mathbb{C}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow A
, key tools in the theory (for instance, for any Lie supergroup
G
this implies the existence of a semidirect product splitting of the group
G(A)
of
A
-points of
G
). See
[
1
]
for further details.

2.4
Super Harish-Chandra pairs and Lie supergroups

A different way to deal with Lie supergroups (or algebraic supergroups) is via the notion of “super Harish-Chandra pair”, that gathers together the infinitesimal counterpart – that of Lie superalgebra – and the classical (i.e., “non-super”) counterpart – that of Lie group – of the notion of Lie supergroup. We recall it shortly, referring to
[
9
]
(and
[
8
]
) for further details.

2.4.1
.

Super Harish-Chandra pairs.
We call
super Harish-Chandra pair
– or just “
sHCp
” in short – any pair
(G,\mathfrak{g})
such that
G
is a (complex holomorphic) Lie group,
\mathfrak{g}
a complex Lie superalgebra such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
, and there is a (holomorphic)
G
-action on
\mathfrak{g}
by Lie superalgebra automorphisms, denoted by
\operatorname{Ad}\colon G\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
, such that its restriction to
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
is the adjoint action of
G
on
\operatorname{Lie}(G)=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
and the differential of this action is the restriction to
\operatorname{Lie}(G)\times\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\times\mathfrak{g}
of the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself. Then a
morphism

(\Omega,\omega)\colon(G^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow(G^{\prime\prime},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime\prime})
between sHCp’s is given by a morphism of Lie groups
\Omega\colon G^{\prime}\longrightarrow G^{\prime\prime}
and a morphism of Lie superalgebras
\omega\colon\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime\prime}
such that
\omega{|}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}=d\Omega
and
\omega\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{g}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Omega_{+}(g)}\circ\omega
for all
g\in G
.

We denote the category of all super Harish-Chandra pairs by
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sHCp}}}
)
.

2.4.2
.

From Lie supergroups to sHCp’s.
For any
A\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
, let
A[\varepsilon]:=A[x]\big/\big(x^{2}\big)
, with
\varepsilon:=x\mod\big(x^{2}\big)
being
even
. Then
A[\varepsilon]=A\oplus A\varepsilon\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
, and there exists a natural morphism
p_{{}_{A}}\colon A[\varepsilon]\longrightarrow A
given by
(a+a^{\prime}\varepsilon){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mapsto}\limits^{{p_{{}_{A}}}}}}a
. For a Lie supergroup
\mathbf{G}
, thought of as a functor
\mathbf{G}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{groups}}}$)}
– i.e., identifying
\mathbf{G}\cong\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{G}}
– let
\mathbf{G}(p_{A})\colon\mathbf{G}(A[\varepsilon])\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}(A)
be the morphism associated with
p_{A}\colon A[\varepsilon]\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow A
. Then there exists a unique functor
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G})\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{groups}}}$)}
given on objects by
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G})(A):=\mathrm{Ker}\big(\mathbf{G}(p)_{A}\big)
. The key fact now is that
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G})
is actually valued in the category
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lie}}}
)
of Lie algebras, i.e., it is a functor
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G})\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lie}}}$)}
. Furthermore, there exists a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
– identified with the
tangent superspace to
\mathbf{G}
at the unit point
– such that
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G})=\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{g}}
(cf. Section
2.2
). Moreover, for any
A\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
one has
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G})(A)=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G}(A))
, the latter being the Lie algebra of the Lie group
\mathbf{G}(A)
.

Finally, the construction
\mathbf{G}\mapsto\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G})
for Lie supergroups is actually natural, i.e., provides a functor
\operatorname{Lie}\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lsgrp}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sLie}}}$)}
from Lie supergroups to Lie superalgebras.

On the other hand, each Lie supergroup
\mathbf{G}
is a group object in the category of (holomorphic) supermanifolds: therefore, its underlying submanifold
\mathbf{G}_{\rm rd}
is in turn a group object in the category of (holomorphic) manifolds, i.e., it is a Lie group. Indeed, the naturality of the construction
\mathbf{G}\mapsto\mathbf{G}_{\rm rd}
provides a functor from Lie supergroups to (complex) Lie groups.

On top of this analysis, if
\mathbf{G}
is any Lie supergroup, then
(\mathbf{G}_{\rm rd},\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G}))
is a super Harish-Chandra pair; more precisely, we have a functor
\Phi\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lsgrp}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sHCp}}}$)}
given on objects by
\mathbf{G}\mapsto(\mathbf{G}_{\rm rd},\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{G}))
and on morphisms by
\phi\mapsto(\phi_{\rm rd},\operatorname{Lie}(\phi))
.

2.4.3
.

From sHCp’s to Lie supergroups.
The functor
\Phi\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lsgrp}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sHCp}}}$)}
has a quasi-inverse
\Psi\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sHCp}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lsgrp}}}$)}
that we can describe explicitly (see
[
8
,
9
]
).

Indeed, let
\mathcal{P}:=\big(G,\mathfrak{g}\big)
be a super Harish-Chandra pair, and let
B:=\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
. For any
A\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
, we define
\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(A)
as being the group with generators the elements of the set
\Gamma^{B}_{A}:=G(A){\textstyle\bigcup}\{(1+\eta_{i}Y_{i})\}_{(i,\eta_{i})\in I\times A_{\bar{1}}}
and relations

\displaystyle 1_{{}_{G}}=1,\qquad g^{\prime}\cdot g^{\prime\prime}=g^{\prime}\cdot_{{}_{G}}g^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle(1+\eta_{i}Y_{i})\cdot g=g\cdot(1+c_{j_{1}}\eta_{i}Y_{j_{1}})\cdots(1+c_{j_{k}}\eta_{i}Y_{j_{k}})

\displaystyle\hphantom{( 1 + \eta_i Y_i ) \cdot g =}{}\ \text{with}\quad\operatorname{Ad}\big(g^{-1}\big)(Y_{i})=c_{j_{1}}Y_{j_{1}}+\cdots+c_{j_{k}}Y_{j_{k}},

\displaystyle(1+\eta^{\prime}_{i}Y_{i})\cdot(1+\eta^{\prime\prime}_{i}Y_{i})=\big(1_{{}_{G}}+\eta^{\prime\prime}_{i}\eta^{\prime}_{i}Y_{i}^{\langle 2\rangle}\big)_{{}_{G}}\cdot(1+(\eta^{\prime}_{i}+\eta^{\prime\prime}_{i})Y_{i}),

\displaystyle(1+\eta_{i}Y_{i})\cdot(1+\eta_{j}Y_{j})=(1_{{}_{G}}+\eta_{j}\eta_{i}[Y_{i},Y_{j}])_{{}_{G}}\cdot(1+\eta_{j}Y_{j})\cdot(1+\eta_{i}Y_{i})

for
g,g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in G(A)
,
\eta_{i},\eta^{\prime}_{i},\eta^{\prime\prime}_{i},\eta_{j}\in A_{\bar{1}}
,
i,j\in I
. This defines the functor
\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}
on objects, and one then defines it on morphisms as follows: for any
\varphi\colon A^{\prime}\longrightarrow A^{\prime\prime}
in
(
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}
)
we let
\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(\varphi)\colon\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(A^{\prime})

\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}\big(A^{\prime\prime}\big)
be the group morphism uniquely defined on generators by
\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(\varphi)(g^{\prime}):=G(\varphi)(g^{\prime})
,
\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(\varphi)(1+\eta^{\prime}Y_{i}):=(1+\varphi(\eta^{\prime})Y_{i})
.

One proves (see
[
8
,
9
]
) that every such
\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}
is in fact a Lie supergroup – thought of as a special functor, i.e., identified with its associated Weil–Berezin functor. In addition,
the construction
\mathcal{P}\mapsto\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}
is natural in
\mathcal{P}
, i.e., it yields a functor
\Psi\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sHCp}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lsgrp}}}$)}
; moreover,
the latter is a quasi-inverse to
\Phi\colon\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Lsgrp}}}$)}\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{sHCp}}}$)}
.

3
Lie superalgebras of type
\boldsymbol{D(2,1;\mathbf{\sigma})}

In this section, we present the complex Lie superalgebras of type
D(2,1;a)
. On the one hand, one can construct them directly, through Kaplansky’s representation (cf.
[
14
]
; a widely accessible account of it is also in Scheunert’s book
[
15
, Chapter I, Section 1.5]
), which depend on parameters. On the other hand, for non-singular values of the parameters one can realize them via Kac’ method, choosing a suitable Cartan matrix, which still depends on parameters.

3.1
Construction of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
(after Kaplansky)

To fix notation, we set
V:=\big\{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\,\big|\,\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}=0\big\}
– a plane in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
– and
V^{\times}:=V\cap(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{3}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{\times}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
; also,
\boldsymbol{0}:=(0,0,0)\in V\setminus V^{\times}
. The Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
we deal with will depend on a parameter
\mathbf{\sigma}:=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in V
.

3.1.1
.

Kaplansky’s realization.
We recall hereafter the construction of Lie superalgebras introduced by Kaplansky (cf.
[
14
]
) who denoted them by
\varGamma(A,B,C)
, with
A,B,C\in\mathbb{C}
. With a suitable normalization, and different terminology, they form the family nowadays called “of type
D(2,1;a)
”, with
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
a\not\in\{0,-1\}
to ensure simplicity; we shall stick to Kaplansky’s point of view, but using the parameter
\mathbf{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
(and later in
V
) and adopting the convention of denoting by
\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})
the Lie superalgebra of type
D(2,1;\mathbf{\sigma})
. A detailed account of Kaplansky’s realization of these Lie algebras can be found in Scheunert’s book (cf.
[
15
, Chapter I, Section 1, Example 5]
). Recall that having a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
amounts to having:

(a)
a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
;
(b)
a
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
-module
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
;
(c)
a
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
-valued symmetric product on
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
, such that the
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
-action is by derivations of the (symmetric) product.

Indeed, in the family of Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}:=\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})
parts
(a)
and
(b)
above will stand the same for any
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V
, while the dependence (of the Lie superalgebra structure) on
\mathbf{\sigma}
will actually occur only for part
(c)
.

Step
(a)
:
Let
\mathfrak{sl}_{i}(2):=\mathbb{C}e_{i}\oplus\mathbb{C}h_{i}\oplus\mathbb{C}f_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
be three isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, in its standard realization. Then we consider their direct sum
\mathfrak{sl}_{1}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(2)
with its natural structure of Lie algebra: this will be the even part
{\big(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\big)}_{\bar{0}}
of our
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
.

Step
(b)
:
Let
\Box:=\mathbb{C}|+\rangle\oplus\mathbb{C}|-\rangle
be the (natural) tautological
2
-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module, and
\Box_{i}
(for all
i=1,2,3
) its
i
-th copy for
\mathfrak{sl}_{i}(2)
. The odd part
{\big(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\big)}_{\bar{1}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
is (isomorphic to)
\Box_{1}\boxtimes\Box_{2}\boxtimes\Box_{3}
, with its natural structure of
\big(\mathfrak{sl}_{1}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(2)\big)
-module. We describe a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of it with the following shorthand notation: for every
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}\in\{+,-\}
, we set
v_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}}:=|\epsilon_{1}\rangle\otimes|\epsilon_{2}\rangle\otimes|\epsilon_{3}\rangle\in\Box_{1}\boxtimes\Box_{2}\boxtimes\Box_{3}
.

Step
(c)
:
We define a projection
\psi:\Box^{\otimes 2}\cong S^{2}\Box\oplus\wedge^{2}\Box\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\wedge^{2}\Box\cong\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle|\pm\rangle\otimes|\pm\rangle\longmapsto 0,\qquad|\pm\rangle\otimes|\mp\rangle\longmapsto\pm 2^{-1}.

Then for
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the linear map
p\colon\Box^{\otimes 2}\cong S^{2}\Box\oplus\wedge^{2}\Box\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow S^{2}\Box\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
by

\displaystyle p(u,v).w:=\sigma^{\prime}\big(\psi(v,w).u-\psi(w,u).v\big)\qquad\forall\,u,v,w\in\Box

that more explicitly reads

\displaystyle|+\rangle\otimes|+\rangle\longmapsto\sigma^{\prime}e,\qquad|\pm\rangle\otimes|\mp\rangle\longmapsto-2^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}h,\qquad|-\rangle\otimes|-\rangle\longmapsto-\sigma^{\prime}f

with
\{e,h,f\}
being the standard
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, i.e.,
[e,f]=h
,
[h,e]=2e
,
[h,f]=-2f
.

Now, for each triple
\mathbf{\sigma}:=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
and each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, let
p_{i}\colon\Box_{i}^{\otimes 2}\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_{i}(2)
be the above map with scalar factor
\sigma^{\prime}:=-2\sigma_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. The Lie superbracket
[\,\ ]
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\big[{\otimes}_{i=1}^{3}u_{i},\otimes_{i=1}^{3}v_{i}\big]=\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}}\psi(u_{\tau(1)},v_{\tau(1)})\psi(u_{\tau(2)},v_{\tau(2)})p_{\tau(3)}(u_{\tau(3)},v_{\tau(3)}).

Tidying everything up, we can define a bracket on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}={\big(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\big)}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\big(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\big)}_{\bar{1}}
by the formula

\displaystyle[x+v,y+w]:=[x,y]+x.w-y.v+[v,w]\qquad\forall\,x,y\in(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}})_{\bar{0}},\quad v,w\in(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}})_{\bar{1}}.

The following proposition resumes the outcome of this construction (see also Theorem
4.1
later on for what happens for singular values of
\mathbf{\sigma}
):

Proposition 3.1

(cf.
[
15
, Chapter II, Section 4.5, p. 135]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

(a)

The bracket given above defines a structure of Lie superalgebra on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
if and only if the given
\mathbf{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
satisfies the condition
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}=0
, that is,
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V:=\mathbb{C}^{3}\bigcap\big\{\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}=0\big\}
.

(b)

Let
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V
. Then the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
is simple if and only if
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
.

(c)

Let
\mathbf{\sigma}^{\prime},\mathbf{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}\in V
. Then the Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}}
are isomorphic if and only if there exists
\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}
such that
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
and
\tau.\sigma^{\prime}
are proportional.

Thus, the isomorphism classes of our
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
’s are in bijection with the orbits of the
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
-action onto
{\mathbb{P}}(V)\bigcup\{\boldsymbol{0}:=(0,0,0)\}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigcup\{*\}
, a complex projective line plus an extra point.

3.1.2
.

The multiplicative table of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
.

For later use, we record hereafter the
complete multiplication table
of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
– for every
\mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in V
– with respect to the
\mathbb{C}
-basis

\{h_{i},e_{j},f_{j},v_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}}\,|\,i,j\in\{1,2,3\},\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}\in\{+,-\}\}
given from scratch. In addition, in the formulas below we also take into account the following
coroots
:

\displaystyle h_{\beta_{1}}:=+2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}-2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}-2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3},

\displaystyle h_{\beta_{2}}:=-2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}+2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}-2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3},

\displaystyle h_{\beta_{3}}:=-2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}-2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}+2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3},

\displaystyle h_{\theta}:=+2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}+2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}+2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3}.

In short, the table is the following (
\forall\,i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
,
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}\in\{+,-\}
):

\displaystyle[h_{i},h_{j}]=0,\qquad[e_{i},e_{j}]=0,\qquad[f_{i},f_{j}]=0,

\displaystyle[h_{i},e_{j}]=2\delta_{ij}e_{j},\qquad[h_{i},f_{j}]=-2\delta_{ij}f_{j},\qquad[e_{i},f_{j}]=\delta_{ij}h_{j},

\displaystyle[e_{i},v_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}}]=\delta_{\epsilon_{i},-}v_{{(-1)}^{\delta_{1,i}}\epsilon_{1},{(-1)}^{\delta_{2,i}}\epsilon_{2},{(-1)}^{\delta_{3,i}}\epsilon_{3}},

\displaystyle[h_{i},v_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}}]=\epsilon_{i}v_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}},

\displaystyle[f_{i},v_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}}]=\delta_{\epsilon_{i},+}v_{{(-1)}^{\delta_{1,i}}\epsilon_{1},{(-1)}^{\delta_{2,i}}\epsilon_{2},{(-1)}^{\delta_{3,i}}\epsilon_{3}},

\displaystyle[v_{+,+,+},v_{+,-,-}]=-\sigma_{1}e_{1},\qquad[v_{+,+,+},v_{-,+,-}]=-\sigma_{2}e_{2},\qquad

\displaystyle[v_{+,+,+},v_{-,-,+}]=-\sigma_{3}e_{3},\qquad[v_{+,+,-},v_{+,-,+}]=+\sigma_{1}e_{1},

\displaystyle[v_{+,+,-},v_{-,+,+}]=+\sigma_{2}e_{2},\qquad[v_{+,-,+},v_{-,+,+}]=+\sigma_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle[v_{+,+,+},v_{-,-,-}]=+2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}+2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}+2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3}=h_{\theta},

\displaystyle[v_{+,+,-},v_{-,-,+}]=-2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}-2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}+2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3}=h_{\beta_{3}},

\displaystyle[v_{+,-,+},v_{-,+,-}]=-2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}+2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}-2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3}=h_{\beta_{2}},

\displaystyle[v_{-,+,+},v_{+,-,-}]=+2^{-1}\sigma_{1}h_{1}-2^{-1}\sigma_{2}h_{2}-2^{-1}\sigma_{3}h_{3}=h_{\beta_{1}},

\displaystyle[v_{-,+,+},v_{-,-,-}]=+\sigma_{1}f_{1},\qquad[v_{+,-,+},v_{-,-,-}]=+\sigma_{2}f_{2},\qquad

\displaystyle[v_{+,+,-},v_{-,-,-}]=+\sigma_{3}f_{3},\qquad[v_{-,+,-},v_{-,-,+}]=-\sigma_{1}f_{1},

\displaystyle[v_{+,-,-},v_{-,-,+}]=-\sigma_{2}f_{2},\qquad[v_{+,-,-},v_{-,+,-}]=-\sigma_{3}f_{3}.

3.2
Kac’ realization

We show now how the Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
of Section
3.1
, for
non-singular values

\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
, can be also realized as contragredient Lie superalgebras (via Kac’ method).

3.2.1
.

First construction.
For any
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V
, we consider the Cartan matrix

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{\sigma}}=(a_{i,j})_{i=1,2,3}^{j=1,2,3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\sigma_{3}&-\sigma_{2}\\
-\sigma_{3}&0&-\sigma_{1}\\
-\sigma_{2}&-\sigma_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}

(see
[
3
]
for a far-reaching analysis of what else is associated with such a Cartan matrix). We associate to it a Dynkin diagram for which the nodes are defined as in
[
12
, Section 2.5.5]
, and we join any two vertices
i
and
j
with an edge labeled by
a_{ij}
: the resulting diagram then is

Following Kac, we consider a realization
\big(\mathfrak{h},\Pi^{\vee}={\{H_{\beta_{i}}\}}_{i=1,2,3}
,
\Pi={\{\beta_{i}\}}_{i=1,2,3}\big)
of
A_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
, that is

1)

\mathfrak{h}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space,

2)

\Pi^{\vee}
is the set of
simple coroots
, a basis of
\mathfrak{h}
,

3)

\Pi
is the set of
simple roots
, a basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
,

4)

\beta_{j}(H_{\beta_{i}})=a_{i,j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 3
.

The (contragredient) Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
is, by definition, the simple Lie superalgebra defined as follows. First, let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
be the Lie superalgebra with the nine generators
H_{\beta_{i}}
,
X_{\pm\beta_{i}}
(
i=1,2,3
), and relations (for
1\leq i,j\leq 3
)

\displaystyle[H_{\beta_{i}},H_{\beta_{j}}]=0,\qquad[H_{\beta_{i}},X_{\pm\beta_{j}}]=\pm\beta_{j}(H_{\beta_{i}})X_{\pm\beta_{j}},

\displaystyle[X_{\beta_{i}},X_{-\beta_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}H_{\beta_{i}},\qquad[X_{\pm\beta_{i}},X_{\pm\beta_{i}}]=0

with parity
|H_{\beta_{i}}|=\bar{0}
and
|X_{\pm\beta_{i}}|=\bar{1}
for all
i
. Then one considers the maximal homogeneous ideal
I_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
which meets trivially the
\mathbb{C}
-span of the generators, and finally defines
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}}):=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})/I_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
.

A straightforward (and easy) analysis shows that
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
is finite-dimensional. Then, by general results (cf.
[
12
, Section 2.5.1]
), there exists an epimorphism
\Phi_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
of Lie superalgebras uniquely determined by

\displaystyle h_{\beta_{1}}\mapsto H_{\beta_{1}},\qquad

\displaystyle h_{\beta_{2}}\mapsto H_{\beta_{2}},\qquad

\displaystyle h_{\beta_{3}}\mapsto H_{\beta_{3}},

\displaystyle v_{-,+,+}\mapsto X_{+\beta_{1}},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{+,-,+}\mapsto X_{+\beta_{2}},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{+,+,-}\mapsto X_{+\beta_{3}},

\displaystyle v_{+,-,-}\mapsto X_{-\beta_{1}},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{-,+,-}\mapsto X_{-\beta_{2}},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{-,-,+}\mapsto X_{-\beta_{3}}.

When
\mathbf{\sigma}
is non-singular, that is
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
, this epimorphism
\Phi_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
is actually an isomorphism, so that
\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})=:\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\cong\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
. One can see it in two ways: first, since
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
is simple for
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
, the kernel of
\Phi_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
is then necessarily trivial; second, direct inspection shows that for
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
the ideal
I_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
has the “correct” codimension in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
so that
\Phi_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
be injective.

We assume now, for the rest of the present subsection, that
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}

(
non-singular case
)
.

Then the set
\Delta^{+}
of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
has the following description:

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}=\big\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{1}+\beta_{2},\beta_{2}+\beta_{3},\beta_{3}+\beta_{1},\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}\big\}.

This can be seen as a consequence of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\cong\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
, or more directly by inspection (namely, describing
I_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
explicitly). The set of roots then is
\Delta=\Delta^{+}\cup\Delta^{-}
, with
\Delta^{-}:=-\Delta^{+}
.

The dual
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
of the Cartan subalgebra has the following description. Let
{\{\varepsilon_{i}\}}_{i=1,2,3}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
be an orthogonal basis normalized by the conditions
(\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{i})=-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
); then one can verify that
(\beta_{i},\beta_{j})=-\sigma_{k}
with
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, where the simple roots are

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=-\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3},\qquad\beta_{2}=\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3},\qquad\beta_{3}=\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{3}.

Now let
\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}
and
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
be the set of even (resp. odd) positive roots. One has

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}=\big\{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2},\beta_{2}+\beta_{3},\beta_{3}+\beta_{1}\big\}=\big\{2\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,1\leq i\leq 3\big\},\qquad\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}=\big\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\theta\big\},

where
\theta:=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}=\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3}
is the highest root.

We introduce now further root vectors and coroots, defined by

\displaystyle X_{2\varepsilon_{1}}:=[X_{\beta_{2}},X_{\beta_{3}}],\qquad

\displaystyle X_{2\varepsilon_{2}}:=[X_{\beta_{3}},X_{\beta_{1}}],\qquad

\displaystyle X_{2\varepsilon_{3}}:=[X_{\beta_{1}},X_{\beta_{2}}],

\displaystyle X_{-2\varepsilon_{1}}:=-[X_{-\beta_{2}},X_{-\beta_{3}}],\qquad

\displaystyle X_{-2\varepsilon_{2}}:=-[X_{-\beta_{3}},X_{-\beta_{1}}],\qquad

\displaystyle X_{-2\varepsilon_{3}}:=-[X_{-\beta_{1}},X_{-\beta_{2}}],

\displaystyle H_{2\varepsilon_{1}}:=-(H_{\beta_{2}}+H_{\beta_{3}}),\qquad

\displaystyle H_{2\varepsilon_{2}}:=-(H_{\beta_{3}}+H_{\beta_{1}}),\qquad

\displaystyle H_{2\varepsilon_{3}}:=-(H_{\beta_{1}}+H_{\beta_{2}}).

It can be checked that, for
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
, one has

\displaystyle[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-2\varepsilon_{j}}]=\sigma_{i}\delta_{i,j}H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad[H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{j}}]=\pm 2\sigma_{i}\delta_{i,j}X_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{j}},

(3.1)

which implies that each
\mathfrak{a}_{i}:=\mathbb{C}X_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{C}H_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{C}X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}
(for
1\leq i\leq 3
) is a Lie sub-(super)algebra, with
[\mathfrak{a}_{j},\mathfrak{a}_{k}]=0
for
j\neq k
, and
\mathfrak{a}_{i}

is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
since
\sigma_{i}\neq 0
. In particular, the even part of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
is nothing but
{\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})}_{\bar{0}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\mathfrak{a}_{i}
.

For
i=1,2,3
we set
X_{\theta}^{i}=\big[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\beta_{i}}\big]\in{\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})}_{\theta}
and
X_{-\theta}^{i}=\big[X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-\beta_{i}}\big]\in{\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})}_{-\theta}
; then the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}X_{\pm\theta}^{i}=0,\qquad\big[X_{\theta}^{j},X_{-\theta}^{k}\big]=-\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{3}H_{\beta_{i}}.

(3.2)

These formulas imply that there exists
X_{\theta}\in{\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})}_{\theta}
and
X_{-\theta}\in{\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})}_{-\theta}
such that

\displaystyle X_{\theta}^{i}=\sigma_{i}X_{\theta},\qquad X_{-\theta}^{i}=\sigma_{i}X_{-\theta},

(3.3)

for any
1\leq i\leq 3
. Hence, setting
H_{\theta}=-(H_{\beta_{1}}+H_{\beta_{2}}+H_{\beta_{3}})
, one has also
[X_{\theta},X_{-\theta}]=H_{\theta}
. Moreover, it also follows that
H_{2\varepsilon_{1}}+H_{2\varepsilon_{2}}+H_{2\varepsilon_{3}}=2H_{\theta}
. Eventually, from all this we see that the odd part
{\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})}_{\bar{1}}
of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
is the
\mathbb{C}
-span of
\{X_{\pm\beta_{i}}\}_{i=1,2,3}\cup\{X_{\pm\theta}\}
.

Finally, from the previous description we see that the isomorphism
\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})\cong\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
can be described on basis elements by

\displaystyle h_{i}\mapsto\sigma_{i}^{-1}H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad

\displaystyle e_{i}\mapsto\sigma_{i}^{-1}X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad

\displaystyle f_{i}\mapsto\sigma_{i}^{-1}X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad

\displaystyle i=1,2,3,

\displaystyle v_{-,+,+}\mapsto X_{\beta_{1}}\phantom{k},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{+,-,+}\mapsto X_{\beta_{2}}\phantom{k},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{+,+,-}\mapsto X_{\beta_{3}}\phantom{k},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{+,+,+}\mapsto X_{\theta},

\displaystyle v_{+,-,-}\mapsto X_{-\beta_{1}},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{-,+,-}\mapsto X_{-\beta_{2}},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{-,-,+}\mapsto X_{-\beta_{3}},\qquad

\displaystyle v_{-,-,-}\mapsto X_{-\theta}.

(3.4)

Note
that in first line of (
3.4
) the non-singularity of
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
plays a key role!

3.2.2
.

Second construction.
For the reader’s convenience, we present now a second construction based upon a different, more familiar Cartan matrix (and associated Dynkin diagram). The link with the previous construction of Section
3.2.1
is through the application of the odd reflection with respect to the root
\beta_{2}
, following V. Serganova (see
[
16
]
).

To begin with, set
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}
,
\alpha_{2}=-\beta_{2}
,
\alpha_{3}=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}
. Then
\Pi^{\prime}:={\{\alpha_{i}\}}_{i=1,2,3}
is another set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
, which is not Weyl-group conjugate to
\Pi
; the corresponding set of coroots
(\Pi^{\prime})^{\vee}={\big\{h_{\alpha_{i}}\big\}}_{i=1,2,3}
should be taken as
h_{\alpha_{1}}=H_{2\varepsilon_{1}}
,
h_{\alpha_{2}}=H_{\beta_{2}}
,
h_{\alpha_{3}}=H_{2\varepsilon_{3}}
. With such a choice, the associated Cartan matrix
A^{\prime}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}:=(a^{\prime}_{i,j}:=\alpha_{j}(h_{\alpha_{i}}))_{i=1,2,3}^{j=1,2,3}
is given by

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}=\begin{pmatrix}2\sigma_{1}&-\sigma_{1}&0\\
-\sigma_{1}&0&-\sigma_{3}\\
0&-\sigma_{3}&2\sigma_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\sigma_{1}\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\
-1&0&-\frac{\sigma_{3}}{\sigma_{1}}\\
0&-\frac{\sigma_{3}}{\sigma_{1}}&2\frac{\sigma_{3}}{\sigma_{1}}\end{pmatrix},

where the second equality is available only if
\sigma_{1}\neq 0
. In particular,
for
\sigma_{1}\neq 0
and
\sigma_{3}\neq 0
, our original
\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
can be also defined via the Cartan matrix

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{a}=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\
1&0&a\\
0&-1&2\end{pmatrix}

with
a:=\frac{\sigma_{3}}{\sigma_{1}}
. When
a\not=-1
, this in turn corresponds – following Kac’ conventions, up to renumbering the vertices (cf.
[
12
, Section 2.5]
) – to the simple Lie superalgebra attached to the following
distinguished
(i.e., with just one odd vertex) Dynkin diagram of type
D(2,1;a)

(instead,
a=-1
corresponds to
\sigma_{2}=0
, that is a
singular
case). Kac’ results tell us that,
for all
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1,0\}
, the set of positive roots with respect to
\Pi^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime,+}=\big\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\big\},

while the coroots can be expressed as

\displaystyle h_{\alpha_{1}}=H_{\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}}=-(H_{\beta_{2}}+H_{\beta_{3}}),\qquad h_{\alpha_{2}}=H_{-\beta_{2}}=H_{\beta_{2}},

\displaystyle h_{\alpha_{3}}=H_{\beta_{2}+\beta_{1}}=-\big(H_{\beta_{2}}+H_{\beta_{1}}\big),

\displaystyle h_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}=-H_{\beta_{3}}=h_{\alpha_{1}}+h_{\alpha_{2}},\qquad h_{\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{2}}=-H_{\beta_{1}}=h_{\alpha_{3}}+h_{\alpha_{2}},

\displaystyle h_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}=H_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}}=-H_{\beta_{1}}-H_{\beta_{2}}-H_{\beta_{3}}=h_{\alpha_{1}}+h_{\alpha_{2}}+h_{\alpha_{3}},

\displaystyle h_{\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}=H_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{3}}=-H_{\beta_{1}}-H_{\beta_{3}}=h_{\alpha_{1}}+2h_{\alpha_{2}}+h_{\alpha_{3}}.

3.2.3
.

The singular case.
We saw in Section
3.2.1
that for every non-singular parameter
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
, we have a Lie superalgebra isomorphism
\Phi_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\colon\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow}\limits^{\cong}}}\mathfrak{g}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
. For singular values
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V\setminus V^{\times}
, instead, the epimorphism
\Phi_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
is no longer an isomorphism: indeed, in this case, the Lie ideal
I_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}(A_{\mathbf{\sigma}})
is bigger than in the non-singular case, for instance (as a straightforward calculation shows), we have

\displaystyle\big[X_{\beta_{i}},X_{\beta_{j}}\big]\in I_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\ \iff\ \sigma_{k}=0\qquad\forall\,\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.

Similarly, the contragredient Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(A^{\prime}_{a})
of Section
3.2.2
can be defined also for the “singular value”
a=-1
(corresponding to
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{3}=0
, which is equivalent to
\sigma_{2}=0
). However, in this case the corresponding Lie ideal
I^{\prime}_{a}
in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}(A^{\prime}_{a})
is bigger, as we have, for instance,

\displaystyle[[[X_{\alpha_{1}},X_{\alpha_{2}}],X_{\alpha_{3}}],X_{\alpha_{2}}]\in I^{\prime}_{a}\quad\iff\quad a=-1,

therefore
\mathfrak{g}\big(A^{\prime}_{a=-1}\big)
has strictly smaller dimension than
\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})
for
\mathbf{\sigma}:=(1,0,-1)
, whereas
\mathfrak{g}(A^{\prime}_{a})\cong\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}:=\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})
via
\Phi_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
for all
\mathbf{\sigma}=(1,a+1,a)\not=(1,0,-1)
.

This shows that, in a sense, describing our objects
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}=\mathfrak{osp}(4,2;\mathbf{\sigma})
of Section
3.1
as contragredient Lie superalgebras is problematic, so to say, at singular values of
\mathbf{\sigma}
, in that the contragredient construction yields not the outcome we are looking for.

3.3
Bases of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}}

In this subsection, for any given
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
we sort out three special bases of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
. Later on (in Section
4
) we use them to construct three different families, indexed by
V
, of Lie superalgebras: by construction these families will coincide on all non-singular parameters
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
but will actually differ instead on singular values
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V\setminus V^{\times}
.

3.3.1
.

First basis.
Let
B:=\{H_{2\varepsilon_{1}},H_{2\varepsilon_{2}},H_{2\varepsilon_{3}}\}\bigcup\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}
and
B_{+}:=B\cup\{H_{\theta}\}
be the subsets of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
whose elements are defined by

\displaystyle H_{2\varepsilon_{i}}:=\sigma_{i}h_{i},\qquad X_{\phantom{+}2\varepsilon_{i}}:=\sigma_{i}e_{i},\qquad X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}:=\sigma_{i}f_{i},\qquad i=1,2,3,\qquad H_{\theta}:=2^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sigma_{i}h_{i},

\displaystyle X_{\beta_{1}}\phantom{k}:=v_{-,+,+},\qquad X_{\beta_{2}}\phantom{k}:=v_{+,-,+},\qquad X_{\beta_{3}}\phantom{k}:=v_{+,+,-},\qquad\phantom{k}X_{\theta}:=v_{+,+,+},

\displaystyle X_{-\beta_{1}}:=v_{+,-,-},\qquad X_{-\beta_{2}}:=v_{-,+,-},\qquad X_{-\beta_{3}}:=v_{-,-,+},\qquad X_{-\theta}:=v_{-,-,-},

(3.5)

(cf. Section
3.1.1
); the analysis in Section
3.1.1
tells us that, for every
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
, the set
B
is a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
, hence
B_{+}
is a spanning set. Again from Section
3.1
– in particular Section
3.1.2
– for the Lie brackets among the elements of
B_{+}
we find the following multiplication table

\displaystyle[H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},H_{2\varepsilon_{j}}]=0,\qquad[H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{j}}]=\pm 2\delta_{i,j}\sigma_{i}X_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{j}},

\displaystyle[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{2\varepsilon_{j}}]=0,\qquad[X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-2\varepsilon_{j}}]=0,\qquad[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-2\varepsilon_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}\sigma_{i}H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},

\displaystyle[H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\pm\beta_{j}}]=\pm{(-1)}^{\delta_{i,j}}\sigma_{i}X_{\pm\beta_{j}},\qquad[H_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\pm\theta}]=\pm\sigma_{i}X_{\pm\theta},

\displaystyle[H_{\theta},X_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{i}}]=\pm\sigma_{i}X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad[H_{\theta},X_{\pm\beta_{i}}]=\mp\sigma_{i}X_{\pm\beta_{i}},\qquad[H_{\theta},X_{\pm\theta}]=0,

\displaystyle[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\beta_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}\sigma_{i}X_{\theta},\qquad[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-\beta_{j}}]=(1-\delta_{i,j})\sigma_{i}X_{\beta_{k}},

\displaystyle[X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\beta_{j}}]=(1-\delta_{i,j})\sigma_{i}X_{-\beta_{k}},\qquad[X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-\beta_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}\sigma_{i}X_{-\theta},

\displaystyle[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\theta}]=0,\quad[X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-\theta}]=\sigma_{i}X_{-\beta_{i}},\qquad[X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{\theta}]=\sigma_{i}X_{\beta_{i}},\qquad[X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X_{-\theta}]=0,

\displaystyle[X_{\beta_{i}},X_{\beta_{j}}]=(1-\delta_{i,j})X_{2\varepsilon_{k}},\qquad[X_{-\beta_{i}},X_{-\beta_{j}}]=-(1-\delta_{i,j})X_{-2\varepsilon_{k}},

\displaystyle[X_{\beta_{i}},X_{-\beta_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}(H_{2\varepsilon_{i}}-H_{\theta}),

\displaystyle[X_{\beta_{i}},X_{\theta}]=0,\qquad[X_{\beta_{i}},X_{-\theta}]=X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad[X_{-\beta_{i}},X_{\theta}]=-X_{2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad[X_{-\beta_{i}},X_{-\theta}]=0,

\displaystyle[X_{\theta},X_{\theta}]=0,\qquad[X_{\theta},X_{-\theta}]=H_{\theta},\qquad[X_{-\theta},X_{-\theta}]=0

for all
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
, with
k\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i,j\}
.

3.3.2
.

Second basis.
Let now
B^{\prime}:=\{H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{1}},H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{2}},H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{3}}\}\bigcup\{X^{\prime}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}
and
B^{\prime}_{+}:=B^{\prime}\cup\{H^{\prime}_{\theta}\}
be the subsets of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
with elements

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}}:=h_{i},\qquad X^{\prime}_{+2\varepsilon_{i}}:=e_{i},\qquad X^{\prime}_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}:=f_{i},\qquad i=1,2,3,\qquad H^{\prime}_{\theta}:=2^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sigma_{i}h_{i},

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{\beta_{1}}:=v_{-,+,+},\qquad X^{\prime}_{\beta_{2}}:=v_{+,-,+},\qquad X^{\prime}_{\beta_{3}}:=v_{+,+,-},\qquad X^{\prime}_{\theta}:=v_{+,+,+},

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{-\beta_{1}}:=v_{+,-,-},\qquad X^{\prime}_{-\beta_{2}}:=v_{-,+,-},\qquad X^{\prime}_{-\beta_{3}}:=v_{-,-,+},\qquad X^{\prime}_{-\theta}:=v_{-,-,-},

(3.6)

(cf. Section
3.1.1
). Again from Section
3.1.1
we see that, for every
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V
(including also the singular locus),
B^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
, so
B^{\prime}_{+}
is a spanning set: indeed,
B^{\prime}
is nothing but a different notation for the natural, built-in
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
in Kaplansky’s realization (cf. Section
3.1.1
). Then from Section
3.1.2
we get the following multiplication table for Lie brackets among elements of
B^{\prime}_{+}

\displaystyle[H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{j}}]=0,\qquad[H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{j}}]=\pm 2\delta_{i,j}X^{\prime}_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{j}},

\displaystyle[X^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{j}}]=0,\qquad[X^{\prime}_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{-2\varepsilon_{j}}]=0,\qquad[X^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{-2\varepsilon_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},

\displaystyle[H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{\pm\beta_{j}}]=\pm{(-1)}^{\delta_{i,j}}X^{\prime}_{\pm\beta_{j}},\qquad[H^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{\pm\theta}]=\pm X^{\prime}_{\pm\theta},

\displaystyle[H^{\prime}_{\theta},X^{\prime}_{\pm 2\varepsilon_{i}}]=\pm\sigma_{i}X^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},\qquad[H^{\prime}_{\theta},X^{\prime}_{\pm\beta_{i}}]=\mp\sigma_{i}X^{\prime}_{\pm\beta_{i}},\qquad[H^{\prime}_{\theta},X^{\prime}_{\pm\theta}]=0,

\displaystyle[X^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{\beta_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}X^{\prime}_{\theta},\qquad[X^{\prime}_{2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{-\beta_{j}}]=(1-\delta_{i,j})X^{\prime}_{\beta_{k}},

\displaystyle[X^{\prime}_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{\beta_{j}}]=(1-\delta_{i,j})X^{\prime}_{-\beta_{k}},\qquad[X^{\prime}_{-2\varepsilon_{i}},X^{\prime}_{-\beta_{j}}]=\delta_{i,j}X^{\prime}_{-\theta},

4.1

.
In order to formalize the description of the family

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V
as above, and set
\mathfrak{a}_{i}:=\mathbb{C}X_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{C}H_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{C}X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}

,

as defined in Section

, for all

(1)

If
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}
, then the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
is simple.

(2)

If
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V\setminus V^{\times}
, with
\sigma_{i}=0
and
\sigma_{j}\not=0\not=\sigma_{k}
for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is a central Lie ideal of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{3|0}
, and
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
is the universal central extension of
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
by
\mathfrak{a}_{i}

(
cf.
[
10
, Theorem 4.7]
)
; in other words, there exists a short exact sequence of Lie superalgebras

\displaystyle 0\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{3|0}\cong\mathfrak{a}_{i}\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow 0.

A parallel result also holds true when working with
\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathbf{\sigma})
over the ground ring
{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{\sigma}]}
.

(3)

If
\mathbf{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{0}

(\!\in V\setminus V^{\times})
, i.e.,
\sigma_{h}=0
for all

, then
{\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})}_{\bar{0}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{9|0}
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})
, and the quotient
\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})\Big/{\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})}_{\bar{0}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{0|8}
is Abelian; in particular,
\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})
is a non-trivial, non-Abelian central extension of
\mathbb{C}^{0|8}
by
\mathbb{C}^{9|0}
, i.e., there exists a short exact sequence of Lie superalgebras, with non-Abelian middle term,

\displaystyle 0\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{9|0}\cong{\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})}_{\bar{0}}\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{0|8}\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow 0.

A parallel result holds true when working with
\mathfrak{g}_{\raisebox{-1.70717pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{Z}$}}}(\boldsymbol{0})
over the ground ring
\mathbb{Z}[\boldsymbol{0}]=\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Part (1) is a direct consequence of (
4.1
) and Proposition
3.1
.
Claim (2) instead follows at once by direct inspection of the formulas in Section
3.3.1
. For instance, reading the lines in the first and second line in the table of formulas for Lie brackets therein, we see that
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is Abelian when
\sigma_{i}=0
, so that
\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cong\mathbb{C}^{3}
as claimed. Moreover, the formulas from the third to the seventh in the same table tell us also that all brackets of the generators of
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
with all other generators turn to zero when
\sigma_{i}=0
: thus
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is central in
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
– hence, in particular, it is a Lie ideal – as claimed. Similarly, a direct verification (setting
\sigma_{i}=0
in those formulas) shows that the quotient
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})/\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
.

Claim (3) follows again by straightforward analysis of the table of formulas in Section
3.3.1
. Indeed, the first two lines in the table describes the structure of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})}_{\bar{0}}
: when
\mathbf{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{0}
they simply tell that this structure is trivial, that is
\mathfrak{g}(\boldsymbol{0})
is Abelian, hence isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{9}

\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})_{\bar{0}}
onto
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})_{\bar{1}}

\mathbf{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{0}

\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})_{\bar{1}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\tau,I}=\mathcal{F}_{\tau,I}\ltimes\mathcal{C}_{\tau,I},

Remark 4.6
.

We considered five families of Lie superalgebras, denoted by
\{\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\sigma\in V}
,
\{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
,
\{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
,
\{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
and
\{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
, all being indexed by the points of the complex plane
V
. Now, our analysis shows that these five families share most of their elements, namely all those indexed by “general points”
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}:=V\cap(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{3}
. On the other hand, the five families are drastically different at all points in the “singular locus”
\mathcal{S}:=V\setminus V^{\times}=V\cap\big(\bigcup_{i=1,2,3}\{\sigma_{i}=0\}\big)
. In other words, the five sheaves
\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime\prime}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
of Lie superalgebras over
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]})\cong V\cup\{\star\}\big({\cong}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\cup\{\star\}\big)
share the same stalks on all “general” points (i.e., those outside
\mathcal{S}
), and have different stalks instead on “singular” points (i.e., those in
\mathcal{S}
). Likewise, the five fibre bundles

\mathbb{L}_{\mathfrak{g}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
,
\mathbb{L}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
,
\mathbb{L}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime\prime}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
,
\mathbb{L}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{{}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}}
over
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]})
share the same fibres on all general points and have different fibres on singular points.
Let us also stress that the second family
\{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(\mathbf{\sigma})\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
is just Kaplansky’s one, as
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(\mathbf{\sigma})\cong\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
– cf. Section
4.2
.
The outcome of the previous discussion is, loosely speaking, that
our construction provides five different “completions” of the family
\{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\sigma\in V\setminus\mathcal{S}}
of simple Lie superalgebras, by adding – in five different ways – some new non-simple extra elements
on top of each point of the “singular locus”
\mathcal{S}
. In particular, this shows that
it makes no sense to speak of “taking the limit for
\mathbf{\sigma}
going to
\mathcal{S}
” of the simple Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}

(
for
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V)
, unless one states exactly what is the
total
family – namely, indexed over
all of
V
– of Lie superalgebras one has chosen to complete the family
\{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\sigma\in V\setminus\mathcal{S}}
. Indeed, as our results show, depending on such a choice one finds very different, non-isomorphic “limits”.

5
Lie supergroups of type
\boldsymbol{D(2,1;\mathbf{\sigma})}
:

presentations and degenerations

In this section, we introduce (complex) Lie supergroups of type
D\big(2,1;\mathbf{\sigma})
, basing on the five families of Lie superalgebras introduced in Section
4
and following the approach of Section
2.4.3
. For simplicity, we formulate everything over
\mathbb{C}
, but the reader may see some subtleties to discuss about the Chevalley groups over a

-algebra. The latter had been discussed in
[
6
]

[
7
]
for some basis, i.e., for one particular choice of

5.1
First family: the Lie supergroups
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}}

Given
\mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in V
, let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
be the complex Lie superalgebra associated with
\mathbf{\sigma}
as in Section
4.1
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
its even part. We recall that
\mathfrak{g}
is spanned over

by
\{H_{2\varepsilon_{1}},H_{2\varepsilon_{2}},H_{2\varepsilon_{3}},H_{\theta}\}\cup{\{X_{\alpha}\}}_{\Delta}
. Like in Section
4.1
, we set
\mathfrak{a}_{i}:=\mathbb{C}X_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{C}H_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{C}X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}
for each
i
– all these being Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
, with
{\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})}_{\bar{0}}=\oplus_{i=1}^{3}\mathfrak{a}_{i}
. When
\sigma_{i}\not=0
, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
: an explicit isomorphism is realized by mapping
X_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\mapsto\sigma_{i}e
,
H_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\mapsto\sigma_{i}h
and
X_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}\mapsto\sigma_{i}f
, where

is the standard basis
\mathfrak{sl}(2)

\sigma_{i}=0

\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\oplus 3}
becomes the 3-dimensional Abelian Lie algebra.

Let us now set
A_{i}:=\text{SL}_{2}
if
\mathbf{\sigma}_{i}\not=0
and
A_{i}:=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}
if
\mathbf{\sigma}_{i}=0
, and define
G:=\times_{i=1}^{3}A_{i}
– a complex Lie group such that

{\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})}_{\bar{0}}
onto
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
integrates to a Lie group action of
G
onto
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
again, so that the pair
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}:=\big(G,\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})\big)
– endowed with that action – is a super Harish-Chandra pair (cf. Section
2.4.1
); note that its dependence on
\mathbf{\sigma}
lies within all its constituents: the structure of
G
, the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{\sigma})
, and the action of the former onto the latter.

Finally, we let

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}:=\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}_{{}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}}

be the complex Lie supergroup associated with the super Harish-Chandra pair
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
trough the category equivalence given in Section
2.4.3
.

5.1.1
.

A presentation of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
.
We shall now provide an explicit presentation by generators and relations for the supergroups
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
, i.e., for the abstract groups
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}(A)
,
A\in\text{\rm(${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Wsalg}}}$)}
.

To begin with, inside each subgroup
A_{i}
we consider the elements

\displaystyle x_{2\varepsilon_{i}}(c):=\exp(cX_{2\varepsilon_{i}}),\qquad h_{2\varepsilon_{i}}(c):=\exp(cH_{2\varepsilon_{i}}),\qquad x_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}(c):=\exp(cX_{-2\varepsilon_{i}})

.

We define also elements
h_{\theta}(c):=\exp\big(cH_{\theta}\big)
for all

: then the commutation relations
[H_{2\varepsilon_{r}},H_{2\varepsilon_{s}}]=0

The complex Lie group
G
is clearly generated by

\displaystyle\varGamma_{\bar{0}}:=\{x_{2\varepsilon_{i}}(c),h_{2\varepsilon_{i}}(c),h_{\theta}(c),x_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}(c)\}^{i\in\{1,2,3\}}_{c\in\mathbb{C}}

In addition, when we consider

the abstract group
G(A)
of its

\displaystyle\varGamma_{\bar{0}}(A):=\{x_{2\varepsilon_{i}}(a),h_{2\varepsilon_{i}}(a),h_{\theta}(a),x_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}(a)\}^{i\in\{1,2,3\}}_{a\in A_{\bar{0}}}.

(5.1)

Note that here the generators do make sense – as operators in

, but also formally – since

(cf. Section

), so each

for some

and a nilpotent

Following the recipe in Section
2.4.3
, in order to generate the group
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}(A):=\mathbf{G}_{{}_{\mathcal{P}_{{}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}}(A)
, beside the subgroup

we need also all the elements of the form

with

– cf. Section
2.4.3
– where now the
\mathbb{C}
-basis

is

5.5.4
.

A geometrical interpretation.
In the previous discussion we considered five families of Lie supergroups indexed by the points of
V
, namely
\{\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\sigma\in V}
,
\{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\sigma\in V}
,
\{\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\sigma\in V}
,
\{\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\sigma\in V}
and
\{\widehat{\mathbf{G}}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\mathbf{\sigma}\in V}
. Our analysis shows that these families have in common all the elements indexed by “general” points, i.e., elements
\mathbf{\sigma}\in V^{\times}:=V\setminus\mathcal{S}
with
\mathcal{S}:=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{3}\{\sigma_{i}=0\}
. On the other hand, these families are entirely different at all points in the “singular locus”
\mathcal{S}
.

In geometrical terms, each family forms a fibre space, say
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}
,
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}
,
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}
,
\mathbb{L}_{\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}
, and
\mathbb{L}_{\widehat{\mathbf{G}}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}}
, respectively, over the base space
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]})\cong V\cup\{\star\}

\big({\cong}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\cup\{\star\}\big)
, whose fibres are Lie supergroups. Our results show that the fibres in any two of these fibre spaces do coincide at general points – where they are
simple
Lie supergroups – and do differ instead at singular points – where they are non-simple indeed.

As an outcome, loosely speaking we can say that
our construction provides five different “completions” of the family
\{\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{\sigma}}\}_{\sigma\in V\setminus\mathcal{S}}
of simple Lie supergroups, by adding – in five different ways – some new non-simple extra elements
. Note also that, a priori, many other such “completions”, more or less similar, could be devised: we just presented these ones as significant, interesting examples, with no claims whatsoever of being exhaustive.

Implicit Tracking-Based Distributed Constraint-Coupled Optimization
Jingwang Li, Housheng Su

Abstract
A class of distributed optimization problem with a globally coupled equality constraint and local constrained sets is studied in this paper. For its special case where local constrained sets are absent, an augmented primal-dual gradient dynamics is proposed and analyzed, but it cannot be implemented distributedly since the violation of the coupled constraint needs to be used. Benefiting from the brand-new comprehending of a classical distributed unconstrained optimization algorithm, the novel implicit tracking approach is proposed to track the violation distributedly, which leads to the birth of the i mplicit tracking-based d istribut e d a ugmented primal-dual gradient dynamics (IDEA). A projected variant of IDEA, i.e., Proj-IDEA, is further designed to deal with the general case where local constrained sets exist. With the aid of the Lyapunov stability theory, the convergences of IDEA and Proj-IDEA over undigraphs and digraphs are analyzed respectively. As far as we know, Proj-IDEA is the first constant step-size distributed algorithm which can solve the studied problem without the need of the strict convexity of local cost functions. Besides, if local cost functions are strongly convex and smooth, IDEA can achieve exponential convergence with a weaker condition about the coupled constraint. Finally, numerical experiments are taken to corroborate our theoretical results.

can be regarded as the standard Lagrangian of

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|A\mathbf{x}-b\|^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle A\mathbf{x}=b,

which is equivalent to
Eq.

P4
. The dual function of
Eq.

4
is

\displaystyle g(\lambda)=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x},\lambda),

define
\mathbf{x}^{*}(\lambda)=\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x},\lambda)
,
\mathbf{x}^{*}(\lambda)
is unique
[
32
]
if
\alpha
is small enough, then
g
is differentiable and

\displaystyle\nabla g(\lambda)=A\mathbf{x}^{*}(\lambda)-b.

Therefore,
A\mathbf{x}-b
of APGD can be seen as the inexact dual gradient.

III-B

Implicit Tracking Approach

Though APGD can solve
Eq.

P4
, it cannot be implemented in a distributed manner since its primal and dual updates both need the global information
A\mathbf{x}-b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}x_{i}-b
. One solution that obtain distributed versions of APGD is employing DAC algorithms to track
A\mathbf{x}-b
distributedly, an example is EDEA:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(\nabla f(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\lambda})-\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{r},

(5a)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

(5b)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{r}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma(\mathbf{r}-(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}))-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{r},

(5c)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{r},

(5d)

where
\mathbf{L}=L\otimes I_{p}
,
\mathbf{A}=\text{diag}\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{n}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{np\times d}
,
\mathbf{b}=[b_{1}^{\top},\cdots,b_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}
(where
b_{i}
satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}=b
),
\bm{\lambda}=[\lambda_{1}^{\top},\cdots,\lambda_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}
,
\mathbf{r}=[r_{1}^{\top},\cdots,r_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}
, and
\mathbf{z}=[z_{1}^{\top},\cdots,z_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}
. The key role in EDEA is
Eqs.

5c
and
5d
, which is the continuous-time DAC algorithm proposed in
[
39
]
, theoretically
r_{i}
will track to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}x_{i}-b
if
\gamma
and
\beta
are chosen appropriately, i.e.,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}r_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}x_{i}(t)-b,\ i\in\mathcal{V}.

Therefore, EDEA will act like APGD as time goes on. However, there are two state variables that need to be exchanged in EDEA. Is is possible design another distributed version of APGD, with fewer state variables that need to be exchanged? To achieve that goal, we can resort to the novel implicit tracking approach.

To illustrate the implicit tracking approach, consider the unconstrained set-up
Eq.

P2
and assume that it has at least an optimal solution
x^{*}
. As mentioned before,
Eq.

P2
is equivalent to
Eq.

P3
.
If each agent takes the following dynamics:

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}(t)=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla h_{j}(x_{j}(t))-\left(x_{i}(t)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}(t)\right),

(6)

it is easy to verify that
x_{i}(t)
will eventually converge to
x^{*}
, but
Eq.

6
is obviously not distributed. Similarly, we can employ the DAC algorithm to track the global gradient
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla h_{j}(x_{j}(t))
, which leads to the following distributed algorithm:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

(7)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{r}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma(\mathbf{r}-\nabla h(\mathbf{x}))-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{r},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{r},

where
\mathbf{x}=[x_{1}^{\top},\cdots,x_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
,
\mathbf{r}=[r_{1}^{\top},\cdots,r_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
, and
\mathbf{z}=[z_{1}^{\top},\cdots,z_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
. It is worth mentioning that
Eq.

7
is not a new algorithm essentially, since its discrete-time counterpart has been studied in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, where the discrete-time DAC algorithm
[
20
]
is used.

Except
Eq.

7
, there exists another distributed version of
Eq.

6
. Notice that
Eq.

6
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}(t)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{j}(t)))-x_{i}(t),

it is reasonable to track
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}(t)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{j}(t)))
directly, which leads to the following distributed algorithm:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma(\mathbf{x}-(\mathbf{x}-\nabla h(\mathbf{x})))-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\nabla h(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

(8)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

which is originally proposed in
[
8
]
, but with a different design idea. The dynamics of agent
i
is given as

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=-\gamma\nabla h_{i}(x_{i}(t))-z_{i}(t)-\beta\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)),

(9)

\displaystyle\dot{z}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)).

Along
Eq.

9
, theoretically we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}x_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}(t)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{j}(t))),

if
\gamma
and
\beta
are chosen appropriately. Therefore,
Eq.

9
will act like
Eq.

6
as time goes on, which implies that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\gamma\nabla h_{i}(x_{i}(t))+z_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla h_{j}(x_{j}(t)).

That is,
\gamma\nabla h_{i}(x_{i}(t))+z_{i}(t)
plays a role in tracking the global gradient
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla h_{j}(x_{j}(t))
and
z_{i}(t)
can be seen as agent
i
’s estimation of the gap between its local gradient and the global gradient. Different from
Eq.

7
, there is no explicit state variable to track the global gradient in
Eq.

8
, instead, the global gradient is tracked in an implicit way. Therefore, we call the tracking approach used in
Eq.

8
the implicit tracking approach (mechanism). In the following, we will show how to use the implicit tracking approach to design a more efficient distributed version of APGD.

Remark 4
.

The implicit tracking mechanism dose not simply exist in
Eq.

8
, but many existing distributed unconstrained optimization algorithms. Inspired by the implicit tracking mechanism, we develop a unified algorithmic framework–unified gradient tracking
[
27
]
, which can unify most existing first-order distributed unconstrained optimization algorithms.

Remark 5
.

Though
Eq.

8
is not a new algorithm, the above derivation of
Eq.

8
and the resulting implicit tracking approach are novel. In
[
8
]
, the design of
Eq.

8
is based on the feedback control idea. Specifically speaking, it is observed that

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}=-\gamma\nabla h(\mathbf{x})-\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x}

cannot converge to the optimal solution since local gradients are generally different. Therefore, the integral feedback term
\mathbf{z}
is constructed to correct the error among agents caused by local gradients. In fact, we can offer the third explanation of
Eq.

8
when
\mathcal{G}
is undirected and connected (which implies that
L\succeq 0
). Let
L=\sqrt{L}\sqrt{L}
, where
\sqrt{L}=PD^{\frac{1}{2}}P^{\top}
,
P
is an orthogonal matrix, and
D
is the diagonal matrix consisting of the eigenvalues of
L
. Like
L
,
\sqrt{L}
is also symmetric. Besides,
L
and
\sqrt{L}
possess the following two properties: (i)
\mathbf{1}^{\top}L=\mathbf{0}^{\top}
is equivalent to
\mathbf{1}^{\top}\sqrt{L}=\mathbf{0}^{\top}
; (ii) for any
z=[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\sqrt{L}z=\mathbf{0}
is equivalent to
Lz=\mathbf{0}
, which implies that
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{n}
.
Define
\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}=\sqrt{L}\otimes I_{m}
, then
Eq.

P2
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}}

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{x})

(10)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0},

whose augmented Lagrangian can be defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=h(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{y}^{\top}\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{x}+\frac{\beta}{2\gamma}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x}.

Then we can employ APGD (let
A=\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}
and
b=\mathbf{0}
):

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma(\nabla h(\mathbf{x})+\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{y})-\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

(11a)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle=\beta\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{x},

(11b)

to solve
Eq.

10
, letting
\mathbf{z}=\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{y}
gives that

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma(\nabla h(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{z})-\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\beta\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x},

which is equivalent to
Eq.

8
. Therefore,
Eq.

8
can be seen as a special case of APGD, when
\mathcal{G}
is undirected and connected. It is worth noting that this perspective can help us study the convergence of
Eq.

8
. Concretely speaking, due to the equivalence of
Eq.

8
and
Eq.

11
,
Propositions

1
and
2
are also applicable for
Eq.

8
. In
[
8
]
, it is proved that
Eq.

8
can converge if
h_{i}
is convex and
h
is strictly convex, but according to
Proposition

1
, we know that the strict convexity of
h
is not necessary.

III-C

Implicit Tracking-based Distributed Augmented Primal-dual Gradient Dynamics

The dual function of
Eq.

P4
is given as

\displaystyle G(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}f(\mathbf{x})+\lambda^{\top}(A\mathbf{x}-b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\inf_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}f_{i}(x_{i})+\lambda^{\top}(A_{i}x_{i}-b_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(\lambda),

where
b_{i}
satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}=b
, then the dual problem of of
Eq.

P4
can be written as

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(\lambda),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}}

\displaystyle g(\bm{\lambda})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(\lambda_{i}),

(12)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j},\ i,j\in\mathcal{V},

where
\bm{\lambda}=[\lambda_{1}^{\top},\cdots,\lambda_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}
.
If
f_{i}
is strictly convex,
x_{i}^{*}(\lambda_{i})
is unique, then
g_{i}
is differentiable and

\displaystyle\nabla g_{i}(\lambda_{i})=A_{i}x_{i}^{*}(\lambda_{i})-b_{i},

where

x_{i}^{*}(\lambda_{i})=\arg\min_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\Big\{f_{i}(x_{i})+\lambda_{i}^{\top}(A_{i}x_{i}-b_{i})\Big\}.

Employing
Eq.

8
to solve
Eq.

12
gives that

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{*}(\bm{\lambda})-\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

(13)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

where
\mathbf{x}^{*}(\bm{\lambda})=[x_{1}^{*}(\lambda_{1})^{\top},\cdots,x_{n}^{*}(\lambda_{n})^{\top}]^{\top}
. Ignoring local constrained sets for the moment and replacing the exact dual gradient with the inexact one, we can further obtain the following inexact counterpart of
Eq.

13
:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(\nabla f(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}),

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda}.

Recall the implicit tracking mechanism discussed before, then
\gamma(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{z}
can serve as a distributed approximation of the inexact global dual gradient
A\mathbf{x}-b
, which leads to another distributed version of APGD:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(\nabla f(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\lambda})-\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\gamma(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{z}),

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda}.

Based on the ideology that using as few parameters as possible on the premise of guaranteeing the convergence, we further derive IDEA:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(\nabla f(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\lambda})-\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z}),

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda}.

To deal with the general case of
Eq.

P4
, we further develop a projected variant of IDEA, which is called Proj-IDEA:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{x}+\nabla f(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\lambda})-\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z}),

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\text{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbf{w}).

For agent
i
, Proj-IDEA is implemented as

\displaystyle\dot{w}_{i}(t)=

\displaystyle-\alpha\left(w_{i}(t)-x_{i}(t)+\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}(t))+A_{i}^{\top}\lambda_{i}(t)\right)

\displaystyle-A_{i}^{\top}m_{i}(t),

\displaystyle\dot{\lambda}_{i}(t)=

\displaystyle m_{i}(t)-\beta\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(\lambda_{i}(t)-\lambda_{j}(t)),

\displaystyle\dot{z}_{i}(t)=

\displaystyle\alpha\beta\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(\lambda_{i}(t)-\lambda_{j}(t)),

\displaystyle x_{i}(t)=

\displaystyle\text{P}_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}(w_{i}(t)),

\displaystyle m_{i}(t)=

\displaystyle A_{i}x_{i}(t)-b_{i}-z_{i}(t).

Remark 6
.

As two distributed versions of APGD,
\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z})
of IDEA and
\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{r}
of EDEA correspond to
A^{\top}(A\mathbf{x}-b)
of APGD, which are the keys that allow IDEA and EDEA to converge when
f_{i}
is not strictly convex. Though IDEA has only one state variable that needs to be exchanged, it usually shows a better convergence rate in numerical experiments, which means that the total communication cost of IDEA is usually no more than half that of EDEA for a given accuracy, this is exactly the reason we choose IDEA instead of EDEA. Without
\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z})
, IDEA will become

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(\nabla f(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}),

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z}-\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda}.

we call it Unaugmented IDEA. It is worth mentioning that a special case of Unaugmented IDEA has been studied in
[
14
]
(
A_{i}=I
,
\alpha=1
), and before that, a similar algorithm has been proposed in
[
13
]
:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\nabla f(\mathbf{x})-\bm{\lambda},

(14)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda},

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{L}\bm{\lambda}.

The major limitation of Unaugmented IDEA and
Eq.

14
is that they cannot converge for non-strictly convex cases. More than that, for strongly convex cases, the convergence rate of IDEA is usually slightly faster than Unaugmented IDEA in numerical experiments.

IV
Convergence Analysis

In this section, the convergences of IDEA and Proj-IDEA over undigraphs and digraphs are analyzed respectively.
For the sake of readability, all proofs are placed in the appendix.

IV-A

Convergence of IDEA

We first analyze the convergence of IDEA over undigraphs, then extend the results to digraphs.
To begin the convergence analysis, it is necessary to figure out the relation between the equilibrium point of IDEA and the saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
, which is depicted by the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
1
holds,
\mathcal{G}
is a strongly connected and weight-balanced digraph, and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(0)=\mathbf{0}
, then
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\lambda^{*})
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
iff there exists
\mathbf{z}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}
such that
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\mathbf{z}^{*})
is an equilibrium point of IDEA, where
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\mathbf{1}_{n}\otimes\lambda^{*}
.

Assumption 2
.

\mathcal{G}
is undirected and connected.

As mentioned before, the major advantage of IDEA is that its convergence can be guaranteed when
f_{i}
is only convex, which benefits from the distributed augmented term
\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z})
.
As a distributed version of APGD, this property of IDEA is in line with
Proposition

1
, which is presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
1
and
2
hold and
\mathcal{X}_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
,
\forall i\in\mathcal{V}
. Then, given
\alpha,\ \beta>0
, for any
(\mathbf{x}(0),\bm{\lambda}(0),\mathbf{z}(0))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{np}\times\mathbb{R}^{np}
satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(0)=\mathbf{0}
,
(\mathbf{x}(t),\bm{\lambda}(t),\mathbf{z}(t))
driven by IDEA converges to
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\mathbf{z}^{*})
, where
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is an optimal solution of
Eq.

P4
.

The following theorem shows that IDEA can achieve exponential convergence when
f_{i}
is strongly convex and smooth and
A
has full row rank.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
1
and
2
hold,
A
has full row rank,
f_{i}
is
\mu_{i}
-strongly convex and
l_{i}
-smooth,
\mathcal{X}_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
,
\forall i\in\mathcal{V}
. Define
\mu=\min_{i\in\mathcal{V}}{\mu_{i}}
,
l=\max_{i\in\mathcal{V}}{l_{i}}
, and
\varphi>0
, let
\beta>0
,
\varphi
and
\alpha

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle>\max\left\{\frac{l}{2}-1,2\overline{\sigma}^{2}(\mathbf{A})\right\},

(15)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle>\max\left\{1,\frac{\frac{\varphi\overline{\sigma}^{2}(\mathbf{A})}{\mu}+\frac{l}{2}}{\varphi+1-\frac{l}{2}}\right\}.

(\mathbf{x}(t),\bm{\lambda}(t),\mathbf{z}(t))

(\mathbf{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\mathbf{z}^{*})
, where
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is the unique optimal solution of
Eq.

P4
.

Remark 7
.

An important property used in the proof of
Proposition

2
is that
z^{\top}AA^{\top}z\geq\underline{\sigma}^{2}(A)\|z\|^{2}
for
z\in\mathbf{C}(A)
. For APGD, if we let
\lambda(0)=\mathbf{0}
, then
\lambda(t)
will always stay in
\mathbf{C}(A)
. As a consequence, the exponential convergence of APGD can be guaranteed even if

does not have full row rank. However, for IDEA, we cannot make
\bm{\lambda}(t)
always stay in
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{A})
or
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{L})
, hence the above property cannot be applied, then some extra conditions have to be imposed to
\mathbf{A}
(or
A
). As shown in
Theorem

2
, the extra condition is that

has full row rank, which is weaker than existing results.

Remark 8
.

Recently we noticed the independent work
[
40
]
, where the dual consensus proximal algorithm (DCPA) is proposed, which can achieve exponential convergence under the same condition with IDEA. However, there are several significant differences between IDEA and DCPA: (i) DCPA is essentially a distributed version of the unaugmented primal-dual gradient algorithm, hence its convergence relies on the strong convexity of
f_{i}
, but IDEA, as a distributed augmented primal-dual gradient dynamics, does not suffer from that issue; (ii) As a continuous-time algorithm, the convergence analysis of IDEA is based on the Lyapunov stability theory, which is of independent interest.

Remark 9
.

Note that
\overline{\sigma}(\mathbf{A})=\max_{i\in\mathcal{V}}\overline{\sigma}(A_{i})
, it is not difficult to estimate the lower bound of

and the upper bounds of

\overline{\sigma}(\mathbf{A})
distributedly if

,

, and

, and

Since the Lyapunov function defined in the proof of

Corollary 1

Assumption 3

\mathcal{G}

For undigraphs, IDEA can converge even if

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
1
and
3

is
\mu_{i}
-strongly convex,
\mathcal{X}_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
,
\forall i\in\mathcal{V}

\varphi>0

and
\beta

VI
Conclusion

This paper studys a class of distributed constraint-coupled optimization problem, i.e.,
Eq.

P1
. APGD is first proposed to solve the special case of
Eq.

P1
where local constrained sets are absent, but it cannot be implemented distributedly. Benefiting from the brand-new comprehending of a classical distributed unconstrained optimization algorithm, we propose the novel implicit tracking approach and design a distributed version of APGD, i.e., IDEA. To deal with the general case of
Eq.

P1
, a projected variant of IDEA, i.e., Proj-IDEA, is further designed. With the help of the Lyapunov stability theory, the convergences of IDEA and Proj-IDEA over undigraphs and digraphs are analyzed respectively. Finally, numerical experiments are taken to corroborate our theoretical results. Compared with the results obtained under undigraphs, the conditions to guarantee the convergences of IDEA and Proj-IDEA over digrpahs is stronger, hence future work will focus on obtaining weaker convergence conditions under digraphs.

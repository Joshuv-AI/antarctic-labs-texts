Adding Circumscription to Decidable Fragments of First-Order Logic: A Complexity Rollercoaster
Carsten Lutz, Quentin Manière

Abstract
We study extensions of expressive decidable fragments of first-order logic with circumscription, in particular the two-variable fragment FO 2 , its extension C 2 with counting quantifiers, and the guarded fragment GF. We prove that if only unary predicates are minimized (or fixed) during circumscription, then decidability of logical consequence is preserved. For FO 2 the complexity increases from coNExp to coNExp NP \textnormal{\sc coNExp}^{\textnormal{\sc NP}} -complete, for GF it (remarkably!) increases from 2 Exp to Tower -complete, and for C 2 the complexity remains open. We also consider querying circumscribed knowledge bases whose ontology is a GF sentence, showing that the problem is decidable for unions of conjunctive queries, Tower -complete in combined complexity, and elementary in data complexity. Already for atomic queries and ontologies that are sets of guarded existential rules, however, for every k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 there is an ontology and query that are k k - Exp -hard in data complexity.



1
Introduction

There are various approaches to defining non-monotonic logics such as
default rules, autoepistemic operators, and circumscription. Most of
these are mainly used with propositional logic rather than with
first-order logic (FO), for two reasons. First, many of the
approaches such as default rules can yield non-intuitive results
when used with first-order logics, interacting in unexpected ways with
existential quantification; see for example

[
\citeauthoryearBaader and
Hollunder1995
]
for a discussion of this
issue. And second, the undecidability of first-order logic of course
carries over to its non-monotonic variants.

Description logics (DLs) are decidable fragments of FO for which
non-monotonic variations have been studied extensively, see e.g.
[
\citeauthoryearBaader and
Hollunder1995
,
\citeauthoryearDonini, Nardi, and
Rosati2002
,
\citeauthoryearBonatti, Lutz, and Wolter2009
,
\citeauthoryearGiordano et al.2013
,
\citeauthoryearBonatti et al.2015b
]
. It
turned out that circumscription provides one of the most well-behaved
of such variations: it does not interact in dramatic ways with
existential quantification, has a simple and appealing semantics that
boils down to minimizing the interpretation of certain predicates, and
comes with a clean way to preserve the decidability of the base
logic. The latter is in fact achieved by permitting only unary
predicates to be minimized or fixed during minimization while binary
predicates must be allowed to vary
[
\citeauthoryearBonatti, Lutz, and Wolter2009
]
. This still covers the main application of circumscription which is
reasoning about typical properties of objects that belong to a certain
class. To model the statement that KR papers are typically interesting, for example,
one may write

{\sf KRPaper}(x)\wedge\neg{\sf abKRpaper}(x)\rightarrow{\sf Interesting}(x)

and then minimize the unary ‘abnormality
predicate’
{\sf abKRpaper}
. In this way, one may conclude that any
concrete KR paper is interesting unless there is concrete evidence
against that. For more information on DLs with circumscription, we
refer to

[
\citeauthoryearBonatti et al.2015a
,
\citeauthoryearStefano, Ortiz, and
Simkus2023
,
\citeauthoryearLutz, Manière, and
Nolte2023
]

It is well-known that DLs are generalized by various decidable and
more expressive FO fragments, of which the two-variable fragment
FO
2
, the guarded fragment GF, and the extension C
2
of FO
2

with counting quantifiers are the most important ones. In this paper,
we ask the following questions:
Do expressive decidable
fragments of FO remain decidable when extended with circumscription
(when only unary predicates are minimized or fixed)?
And if so, what is the impact on computational complexity?
The
answers are, in our opinion, somewhat surprising.

We study the reasoning problems of
circumscribed consequence

and
circumscribed querying
. In the former, two sentences
\phi

and
\psi
are given along with a ‘circumscription pattern’
{\sf CP}
that
specifies which predicates are minimized, fixed, and varying. We are
then interested in deciding whether
\psi
holds in every model that
is minimal in the sense specified by
{\sf CP}
, written

\phi\models_{\sf CP}\psi
. Circumscribed querying is defined in the same
way, but now
\phi
is a knowledge base that consists of a sentence
from the FO fragment under consideration (specifying an ontology) and
a database, and
\psi
is a query. As query languages, we consider
single-atom queries (AQs), conjunctive queries (CQs), and
and unions thereof (UCQs).

We start with studying FO
2
. Similarly to the case of description
logic
[
\citeauthoryearBonatti, Lutz, and Wolter2009
]
, a crucial step for proving decidability is
to show that circumscribed FO
2
has the finite model property (FMP)
in the sense that if
\phi\not\models_{\sf CP}\psi
, then there is a

{\sf CP}
-minimal model
\mathfrak{A}
of
\phi
with
\mathfrak{A}\not\models\psi
that is
of bounded size. To prove this, we build
on a well-known construction from
[
\citeauthoryearGrädel, Kolaitis, and Vardi1997
]
, used there to establish
the FMP of non-circumscribed FO
2
, which converts a potentially
infinite model
\mathfrak{A}
of an FO
2
sentence
\phi
into a model
\mathfrak{B}
of
single exponential size. To apply this construction in the
circumscribed case, however, we need an additional condition to be
satisfied:

(\heartsuit)

\mathfrak{B}
must not realize any 1-type more often
than
\mathfrak{A}
(for a suitable notion of 1-type).

The construction of
[
\citeauthoryearGrädel, Kolaitis, and Vardi1997
]
does not satisfy this condition. We
remark that this is in contrast to filtration, the (much simpler)
finite model construction used for description logics such as
\mathcal{ALC}
.

We thus rework the construction of
[
\citeauthoryearGrädel, Kolaitis, and Vardi1997
]
in a suitable way,
obtaining a version that satisfies Condition
(\heartsuit)
. This
yields the FMP for circumscribed FO
2
and decidability as well as a

\textnormal{\sc coNExp}^{\textnormal{\sc NP}}
upper complexity bound for circumscribed
consequence. A matching lower bound is obtained from
\mathcal{ALC}
and thus
circumscribed consequence in FO
2
is of the same complexity as in
(the much less expressive)
\mathcal{ALC}
. We obtain the same result for the
combined complexity of circumscribed AQ-querying and also show

\Pi^{p}_{2}
-completeness for data complexity, again the same as in

\mathcal{ALC}
. Querying with UCQs is undecidable already for non-circumscribed
FO
2
, so we do not study it.

For GF, we follow the same general approach, with a remarkably
different outcome. There are two constructions that show the finite
model property of GF, both of them rather intricate. The historically
first one was proposed by Grädel, based on a combinatorial
construction due to Herwig
[
\citeauthoryearGrädel1999
]
.
Later, Rosati introduced a different finite model construction while
studying certain integrity constraints for databases

[
\citeauthoryearRosati2006
]
, and this construction, now known as
the
Rosati cover
, has been adapted to GF in
[
\citeauthoryearBárány, Gottlob, and
Otto2014
]
. Both
constructions fail to yield Property
(\heartsuit)
and modifying them to
achieve this property turns out to be much more difficult than in the
case of FO
2
. We give a modified version of the Rosati cover
that yields finite models of non-elementary size, compared to single
exponential size as for the original Rosati cover. This yields the
FMP for circumscribed GF. We then show that the non-elementary size of
finite models is unavoidable: circumscribed consequence in GF is

Tower
-complete! To us, this huge difference to the FO
2
case came
as a big surprise. We also show that circumscribed querying in GF is
decidable, generalizing recent work on DLs

[
\citeauthoryearLutz, Manière, and
Nolte2023
]
. In combined complexity, it is

Tower
-complete with the lower bound applying to AQs and the upper
bound to UCQs. Regarding data complexity, it is elementary in the
sense that for each GF ontology
\mathcal{O}
, circumscription pattern
{\sf CP}
, and
UCQ
q
, querying is in
k
-
Exp
for some
k
. We also show that there
is no uniform bound on
k
: for each
k\geq 1
we identify an
ontology
\mathcal{O}
, circumscription pattern
{\sf CP}
, and AQ
q
for which
querying is
k
-
Exp
-hard. In fact,
\mathcal{O}
is a set of existential
rules, a ‘positive’ fragment of GF that is important for querying. We
also show that with a single minimized predicate and all other
predicates varying, the data complexity of AQ-querying is
Exp
-hard in
GF. Note that since CQs are sandwiched beween AQs and UCQs, this
also completely clarifies the (combined and data) complexity for
this query language.

In addition, we provide first results on circumscribed consequence and
AQ-querying in C
2
. Using a reduction to Boolean
algebra with Presburger arithmetic, we show that these problems
are decidable. The complexity remains open.

2
Preliminaries

When speaking of first-order logic (FO), we generally mean the version
with equality and constants (unless otherwise noted) and without
function symbols. FO
2
is the two-variable fragment of FO, obtained
by fixing two variables
x
and
y
and disallowing the use of any
other variables
[
\citeauthoryearScott1962
,
\citeauthoryearMortimer1975
,
\citeauthoryearGrädel, Kolaitis, and Vardi1997
]
. C
2
is the extension of FO
2

with counting quantifiers of the form
\exists_{\leq n}
,

\exists_{\geq n}
, and
\exists_{=n}
for every

n\geq 0

[
\citeauthoryearGrädel, Otto, and
Rosen1997
,
\citeauthoryearPacholski, Szwast, and
Tendera1997
,
\citeauthoryearPratt-Hartmann2005
]
. In FO
2
and C
2
, we generally only
admit predicates of arity at most two. In the guarded fragment of FO,
denoted GF, quantification is restricted to the pattern

\forall\bar{y}(\alpha(\bar{x},\bar{y})\rightarrow\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}))\qquad\exists\bar{y}(\alpha(\bar{x},\bar{y})\wedge\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}))

where
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a GF formula with free variables
among
\bar{x},\bar{y}
and
\alpha(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a
relational atom
R(\bar{x},\bar{y})
or an equality atom
x=y
that in either
case contains all variables in
\bar{x},\bar{y}

[
\citeauthoryearAndréka, Németi, and van
Benthem1998
,
\citeauthoryearGrädel1999
]
. The
formula
\alpha
is called the
guard
of the quantified formula.

We use the standard notation of first-order logic, denoting
structures with
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
, their universes with
A
and

B
, and the interpretation of predicates
R
with
R^{\mathfrak{A}}
and

R^{\mathfrak{B}}
. We reserve a countably infinite set of predicates of each
arity. We use
|\phi|
to denote the
length
of the
formula
\phi
, that is, the length of
\phi
when encoded as a word
over a suitable alphabet.

Circumscription.

A
circumscription pattern
is a tuple

{\sf CP}=(\prec,\mathsf{M},\mathsf{F},\mathsf{V})
, where
\mathsf{M}
,

\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{V}
partition the unary predicates into
minimized
,

fixed
and
varying
predicates, and
\prec
is a strict partial
order on
\mathsf{M}
called the
preference relation
.
The order
\prec
also induces a preference relation

<_{\sf CP}
on structures by setting
\mathfrak{B}<_{\sf CP}\mathfrak{A}
if the
following conditions hold:

1.

B=A
and
c^{\mathfrak{A}}=c^{\mathfrak{B}}

for all constants
c
,

2.

for all
P\in\mathsf{F}
,
P^{\mathfrak{B}}=P^{\mathfrak{A}}
,

3.

for all
P\in\mathsf{M}
with
P^{\mathfrak{B}}\not\subseteq P^{\mathfrak{A}}
, there
is a
Q\in\mathsf{M}
,

Q\prec P
, such that
Q^{\mathfrak{B}}\subsetneq Q^{\mathfrak{A}}
,

4.

there exists a
P\in\mathsf{M}
such that
P^{\mathfrak{B}}\subsetneq P^{\mathfrak{A}}

and for all
Q\in\mathsf{M}
,
Q\prec P
implies
Q^{\mathfrak{B}}=Q^{\mathfrak{A}}
.

A
{\sf CP}
-minimal model
of an FO sentence
\phi
is a model
\mathfrak{A}
of

\phi
such that there is no
\mathfrak{B}<_{\sf CP}\mathfrak{A}
that
is a model of
\phi
. Note that predicates of arity larger than
one always vary to avoid undecidability
[
\citeauthoryearBonatti, Lutz, and Wolter2009
]
. We also
assume
that nullary predicates always vary, which is w.l.o.g. as they can
be simulated by unary predicates.

For FO sentences
\phi
and
\psi
, we write

\phi\models_{\sf CP}\psi
if every
{\sf CP}
-minimal model
\mathfrak{A}
of
\phi

satisfies
\mathfrak{A}\models\psi
. Take any fragment
F
of FO such as
FO
2
. With
circumscribed consequence
in
F
we mean the
problem to decide, given sentences
\phi
and
\psi
from
F
and a
circumscription pattern
{\sf CP}
, whether
\phi\models_{\sf CP}\psi
.

Ontology-mediated querying.

Ontology-mediated querying with
circumscribed knowledge bases, as recently studied in
[
\citeauthoryearLutz, Manière, and
Nolte2023
]
, can
be seen as a version of circumscribed consequence where
\phi
encodes
an ontology and a database and
\psi
is a query. We next make this
precise.

A
database
is a finite set of ground atoms, in this context
called
facts
. We use
{\sf adom}(D)
to denote the set of
constants that occur in
D
. A structure
\mathfrak{A}

satisfies
a
database
D
if (1) it satisfies all facts in it and (2) interprets
all constant symbols
c
in
{\sf adom}(D)
as
c^{\mathfrak{A}}=c

(and thus no two such
c
denote the same element of
A
).
We then also say that
\mathfrak{A}
is a
model
of
D
and write

\mathfrak{A}\models D
. Note that Point (2) is the
standard names
assumption
, as usually made in the context of databases.
A

knowledge base (KB)

\mathcal{K}
takes the form

\bigwedge\mathcal{O}\wedge D
with
\mathcal{O}
a finite set of FO sentences,
called the
ontology
, and
D
a database. We usually denote
\mathcal{K}
as a pair
(\mathcal{O},D)
. We call
\mathcal{K}
a GF-KB if all sentences in
\mathcal{O}
fall into GF, and likewise for other FO fragments.

A
conjunctive query (CQ)
is an FO formula
of the form

q=\exists\bar{x}\,\varphi(\bar{x})
where
\varphi

is a
conjunction of relational atoms, possibly involving constants.
An
atomic query (AQ)
is a CQ of the simple form
R(\bar{c})
with
\bar{c}
a tuple of constants. A
union of conjunctive queries (UCQ)

q(\bar{x})
is a disjunction of CQs. Let
\mathcal{K}
be a KB and
q
a UCQ. We write
\mathcal{K}\models_{\sf CP}q
if
\mathfrak{A}\models q
for every
{\sf CP}
-minimal model
\mathfrak{A}
of

\mathcal{K}
. The notion
\mathcal{K}\models q
is defined analogously, except that all models of
\mathcal{K}
are
considered, not only
{\sf CP}
-minimal ones. Take a fragment

F
of FO such as GF and a query language

Q
such as UCQs. With
circumscribed
Q
-querying in

F
, we mean the problem to decide, given a knowledge base

\mathcal{K}=(\mathcal{O},D)
with
\mathcal{O}
a set of sentences from
F
and a query

q
from
Q
, whether
\mathcal{K}\models_{\sf CP}q
. When studying the combined complexity of this problem, all of

\mathcal{K}
,
{\sf CP}
, and

q
are treated as inputs. For data complexity, we assume

\mathcal{O}
,
{\sf CP}
, and

q
to be fixed and thus of constant size. We remark that our queries
are Boolean, that is, they do not have answer variables. This is
without loss of generality since constants are admitted in queries.

We shall
also consider ontologies
\mathcal{O}
that are sets of guarded existential
rules. An
existential rule
is an FO sentence of the form

\forall\bar{x}\forall\bar{y}\,(\phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\rightarrow\exists\bar{z}\,\psi(\bar{x},\bar{z}))

where
\phi
and

\psi
are conjunctions of relational atoms. We call

\phi
the
body
of the rule and

\psi
the
head
. The rule is
guarded
if the body
contains an atom that contains all variables in it. When writing
existential rules, we usually omit the universal quantifiers. For every ontology
\mathcal{O}
that is a set of guarded existential rules,
there is a GF ontology
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
such that for all
databases
D
and UCQs
q
, we have
(\mathcal{O},D)\models q
iff
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},D)\models q

[
\citeauthoryearCalì, Gottlob, and
Lukasiewicz2009
]
. To construct

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, one simply adds a fresh predicate to the head of each rule in

\mathcal{O}
that contains all variables in the head, and then translates the
resulting set of rules into an equivalent GF sentence in a
straightforward way. This proof also applies to circumscribed
querying, letting the fresh predicates vary.

Example 1
.

Consider the database

\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}c@{\;}l}D&=&\{W(w_{1}),W(w_{2}),E(w_{1}),E(w_{2}),{\sf offers}(s,p)\}.\end{array}

where
{\sf offers}(s,p)
means that supplier
s
offers product
p
,

W
stands for warehouse, and
E
for express. Assuming that we
have complete knowledge of all existing warehouses (e.g. in our
company), we use a circumscription pattern
{\sf CP}
that minimizes predicate

W
and lets all other predicates vary. Let
the ontology
\mathcal{O}
contain the guarded existential rules

\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}c@{\;}l}{\sf offers}(x,y)&\rightarrow&\exists z\,{\sf supplies}(x,y,z)\\[2.84526pt]
{\sf supplies}(x,y,z)&\rightarrow&W(z)\end{array}

where
{\sf supplies}(x,y,z)
expresses that supplier
x
supplies
product
y
to warehouse
z
. Note that since
W
is minimized,
the existentially quantified variable
z
in the upper rule can only
bind to
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
. We thus obtain

(\mathcal{O},D)\models_{\sf CP}\exists z\,({\sf supplies}(s,p,z)\wedge E(z)).

We now illustrate a basic trick that underlies the hardness proofs
in Section
5
. Extend the database with

{\sf mirror}(w_{1},w_{2})
and
{\sf mirror}(w_{2},w_{1})

expressing that
w_{1}

and
w_{2}
are supplied with the same products by the same
suppliers. We wish to extend
\mathcal{O}
with

{\sf supplies}(x,y,z_{1})\wedge{\sf mirror}(z_{1},z_{2})\rightarrow{\sf supplies}(x,y,z_{2})

which yields

(\mathcal{O},D)\models_{\sf CP}{\sf supplies}(s,p,w_{i})\text{ for all }i\in\{1,2\}.

(
*
)

However, the above rule is not
guarded. We may work around this by using the guarded rules

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}{\sf supplies}(x,y,z_{1})\rightarrow\exists z_{2}\,({\sf mirror}(z_{1},z_{2})\wedge{\sf supplies}(x,y,z_{2}))\\[2.84526pt]
{\sf mirror}(x,y)\wedge\overline{{\sf mirror}}(x,y)\rightarrow{\sf false}\end{array}

and extend the data with
\overline{{\sf mirror}}(w_{i},w_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then if
z_{1}
binds to
w_{1}
, the
existentially quantified variable
z_{2}
can only bind to
w_{2}
and
vice versa, and we again obtain
(*)
.

Substitutions, Signatures, Types.

For a tuple
\bar{a}
, we generally use
a_{i}
to denote the
i
-th
element of
\bar{a}
, for
1\leq i\leq|\bar{a}|
.
A
substitution

\sigma
is a function that maps variables to
variables. We typically write
\sigma x
in place of
\sigma(x)
. For
a tuple
\bar{u}
of variables and constants, we write
\sigma\bar{u}

to denote the tuple obtained by applying
\sigma
componentwise,
treating it as the identity on constants.

A
signature
is a set of constants and relation symbols.
For an
FO sentence
\phi
, we use
{\sf sig}(\phi)
to denote the set of such
symbols in
\phi
,
{\sf const}(\phi)
to denote the set of constants in

\phi
, and
{\sf const}_{=}(\phi)
to denote the set of constants used in
\phi
in an equality
atom.

Fix a signature
\Sigma
.
A
term
is a variable or a constant
from
\Sigma
. An
atom
is of the form
R(\bar{u})
or
v_{1}=v_{2}

with
R
a relation symbol from
\Sigma
,
\bar{u}
a tuple of terms and

v_{1},v_{2}
terms. A
literal
is an atom or a negated atom. For
every
n\geq 1
, fix a sequence of variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. An

n
-type
on
\Sigma
is a maximal satisfiable
set of literals that uses
exactly the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a structure. If
\bar{a}\in A^{n}
, then
the
n
-type on
\Sigma

realized at

\bar{a}
in
\mathfrak{A}
, denoted

{\sf tp}^{n}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(\bar{a})
, is the unique
n
-type
t
on
\Sigma
with

\mathfrak{A}\models t(\bar{a})
.
We may drop superscript
n
as
n

is always identical to the length of
\bar{a}
.
For a set
S\subseteq A
, we use

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(S)
to denote the set of
1
-types

\{{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(a)\mid a\in S\}
. As an
abbreviation,
we may write
{\sf tp}^{1}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A})
in place of
{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(A)
.

3
The Two-Variables Fragment FO
2

We show that circumscribed consequence is
\textnormal{\sc coNExp}^{\textnormal{\sc NP}}
-complete in
FO
2
and so is circumscribed AQ-querying, in combined
complexity. Note that querying with CQs or UCQs is undecidable already
for non-circumscribed FO
2
. We remark that this section showcases the
general approach that we also use, in a more intricate form, for GF later
on.

An FO
2
sentence is in
Scott normal form
if it
has the form

\phi=\forall x\forall y\,\varphi\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1..n_{\exists}}\forall x\exists y\,\psi_{i}

(
*
)

with
\varphi
and
\psi_{i}
quantifier-free. It has been shown in

[
\citeauthoryearScott1962
,
\citeauthoryearGrädel, Kolaitis, and Vardi1997
]
that every FO
2
sentence
\phi_{0}
can be
converted in polynomial time into an FO
2
sentence
\phi
in Scott
normal form that is a conservative extension of
\phi_{0}
: every model of
\phi
is a model of
\phi_{0}
and, conversely,
every model of
\phi_{0}
can be extended to a model of
\phi
by
interpreting the fresh predicates in
\phi
.

We now establish an improved finite model property for
non-circumscribed FO
2
that satisfies
Property
(\heartsuit)
from the introduction.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\phi
be an FO
2
sentence of the form
(*)
,
\Sigma={\sf sig}(\phi)
,
\mathfrak{A}
a
model of
\phi
, and
k=|\Sigma\setminus{\sf const}(\phi)|
.
Then there exists a model
\mathfrak{B}
of
\phi
such that

1.

|B|\leq|\phi|^{n_{\exists}+1}\cdot 2^{n_{\exists}4(k+6)}
;

2.

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A})={\sf tp}^{1}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{B})
;

3.

|\{a\in B\mid{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{B},\Sigma}^{1}(a)=t\}|\leq|\{a\in A\mid{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}^{1}(a)=t\}|
for every 1-type
t

on
\Sigma
;

4.

c^{\mathfrak{A}}=c^{\mathfrak{B}}
for all constants
c
in
\phi
.

We remark that the construction from
[
\citeauthoryearGrädel, Kolaitis, and Vardi1997
]
only yields
Proposition
1
without Point 3, that is, it
may
increase
the number of instances of some of the
1-types realized in the original model.
We next use Proposition
1
to establish
the following.

Proposition 2
.

Circumscribed
FO
2
has the finite model property: if
\phi,\psi
are
FO
2
-sentences with
\phi\not\models_{\sf CP}\psi
,
then there is a
{\sf CP}
-minimal model

Proof.

Assume that

of

-minimal model of

Assume to the contrary that there is a model

of
\phi

It is easy to verify that

<_{\sf CP}
,
and the construction of the initial

It can be verified that
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}<_{\sf CP}\mathfrak{A}

∎

It is now easy to derive the main result of this section.

Theorem 1
.

Circumscribed consequence in FO

\textnormal{\sc coNExp}^{\textnormal{\sc NP}}
-complete.

Proof.

The lower bound is inherited from the description logic
\mathcal{ALC}

[
\citeauthoryearBonatti, Lutz, and Wolter2009
]
.
The upper bound is based on Proposition
2
,
as follows.

We show that circumscribed consequence in GF is in

and so is

Elementary
in data complexity, that is,
for every GF ontology
\mathcal{O}
, circumscription pattern

, and AQ

A(\bar{x})

k\geq 1
such that given a database

, it is in
k
-

to decide whether

.

We remind the reader of the relevant complexity

\textsc{Elementary}=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\text{$k$-{\sc Exp} }

and

\textsc{Tower}=\bigcup_{f\in\textsc{FElem}}\textsc{Space}(\textrm{tower}(f(n))

4.1
Circumscribed Consequence

We consider GF sentences
\phi
in Scott normal form. Such a sentence
takes the shape

\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n_{\forall}}\forall\bar{x}\,(\alpha_{i}\rightarrow\varphi_{i})\wedge\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n_{\exists}}\forall\bar{x}\,(\beta_{i}\rightarrow\exists\bar{y}\,(\gamma_{i}\wedge\psi_{i}))

where the
\alpha_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
and
\gamma_{i}
are atoms and the

\varphi_{i}
and
\psi_{i}
are quantifier-free. It has been shown in

[
\citeauthoryearGrädel1999
]
that every GF sentence
\phi
can be converted in
polynomial time into a GF sentence in Scott normal form that is a
conservative extension of
\phi
.

We now state the improved finite model property for GF that satisfies
Property
(\heartsuit)
from the introduction.
Let
{\sf tower}(0,n):=n
and, for every
k\geq 1
, define

{\sf tower}(k,n):=2^{{\sf tower}(k-1,n)}
, so that
{\sf tower}(k,n)

refers to an exponentiation tower that consists of
k
twos followed
by an
n
. A signature is
unary
if it only contains constants
symbols and unary predicates.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\phi
be a GF sentence,
\Sigma
a unary signature that contains
{\sf const}_{=}(\phi)
, and
\mathfrak{A}
a model of

\phi
.
Then there exists a model
\mathfrak{B}
of
\phi

that satisfies
the following properties:

1.

|B|\leq{\sf tower}(4^{{\left|{\Sigma}\right|}+4},{\left|{\phi}\right|})
;

2.

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A})={\sf tp}^{1}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{B})
;

3.

|\{a\in B\mid{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{B},\Sigma}(a)=t\}|\leq|\{a\in A\mid{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(a)=t\}|
for every 1-type
t
on
\Sigma
;

4.

c^{\mathfrak{A}}=c^{\mathfrak{B}}
for all constants
c
in
\phi
.

The reader should think of
\Sigma
as the signature that contains,
apart from
{\sf const}_{=}(\phi)
, all unary predicates that are minimized
and fixed in a circumscription pattern.

To establish Proposition
3
, we use a modified
version of the Rosati cover that leaves untouched a selected part

\Delta
from the original model. In addition, if
\Delta
contains
all the instances of some unary type in the original model, then so
does
\Delta
in the modified Rosati cover. The precise formulation follows.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\phi
be a GF sentence and
\Sigma
a unary signature that contains
{\sf const}_{=}(\phi)
.
For all
models
\mathfrak{A}
of
\phi
and all
\Delta\subseteq A
that contain
c^{\mathfrak{A}}
for every constant
c
in
\phi
,
there exists a model
\mathfrak{B}
of
\phi
that satisfies
the following properties:

1.

{\left|{B}\right|}\leq 2^{({\left|{\Delta}\right|}+{\left|{\phi}\right|})^{{\left|{\phi}\right|}+11}}
;

2.

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(a)={\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{B},\Sigma}(a)

for all

;

3.

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(A\setminus\Delta)={\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{B},\Sigma}(B\setminus\Delta)
;

4.

\Delta\subseteq B
and
c^{\mathfrak{A}}=c^{\mathfrak{B}}
for all constants
c
in
\phi
.

We now prove Proposition
3
by using
Lemma
1
and choosing an appropriate
\Delta
.
For any
\Delta\subseteq A
that contains
c^{\mathfrak{A}}
for all constants

c
in
\phi
, we use
\mathsf{rc}(\mathfrak{A},\Delta)
to denote the finite model
of
\phi
produced by Lemma
1
(where ‘rc’ stands
for ‘Rosati cover’).

Let
\phi
,
\Sigma
, and
\mathfrak{A}
be as in
Proposition
3
. For every 1-type

on
\Sigma
,
set

The challenge is to choose
\Delta
so that Point 3 of

is satisfied. Call a 1-type

if

and call

. To

\Delta
.

We use a set
\Delta
that contains

of 1-types realized only a certain number of times: for

\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}c@{\;}l}\Delta_{m}&:=&\{a\in A\mid\#_{\mathfrak{A}}({\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(a))\leq m\}\;\cup\\[2.84526pt]
&&\{c^{\mathfrak{A}}\mid c\in{\sf const}(\phi)\}.\end{array}

Now consider
\Delta_{m}
, for some

. For those 1-types

at most

So can we find a value for

is trivially the case for

Lemma 2
.

There exists a stabilizing set

such that

Proof.

We start with

(starting with

\Sigma
that is not stable

. Using the bound from Point 1 of

, we set

and for each

\Sigma

is a strict superset of the set of 1-types on

\Sigma

\Delta_{m_{i}}

many 1-types to consider. Therefore, after at

is stabilizing. Let us argue that we have achieved

∎

To conclude the proof of Proposition

be as in Lemma

and set

.
Since

For Point 1, we may use Point 1 of

To apply Proposition

4.2
Circumscribed Querying

We prove that UCQ-querying (and thus also AQ-querying) in
GF is decidable.

Theorem 5
.

Circumscribed UCQ-querying in GF is in
Tower
w.r.t. combined complexity and in
Elementary
w.r.t. data complexity.

Without circumscription, decidability of UCQ-querying in GF is almost
immediate as one can replace the UCQ
q
with the disjunction
q^{\prime}
of
all acyclic CQs that imply a CQ in
q
(up to a certain size) and then
express
q^{\prime}
as a GF sentence, obtaining a reduction to
unsatisfiability
[
\citeauthoryearBárány, Gottlob, and
Otto2014
]
. This does not work with circumscription.

Example 2
.

Take the ontology
\mathcal{O}
that consists of the sentence

\begin{array}[]{r}\forall x\,\big(A(x)\rightarrow\exists y(R(x,y)\wedge\exists z(R(y,z)\;\wedge\\[2.84526pt]
\exists u\,(R(z,u)\wedge A(u))))\big),\end{array}

the database
D=\{A(a)\}
, and let
{\sf CP}
minimize
A
and vary all
other predicates. Then
(\mathcal{O},D)\models_{\sf CP}q
where:

q:=\exists x\ \exists y\ \exists z\,\ R(x,y)\wedge R(y,z)\wedge R(z,x),

but
there is no acyclic CQ
q^{\prime}
that implies
q
and satisfies

(\mathcal{O},D)\models_{\sf CP}q^{\prime}
.

We thus use a somewhat different, mosaic-based approach which exploits
the fact that if
(\mathcal{O},D)\not\models_{\sf CP}q
, then this is witnessed by a
model
\mathfrak{B}
that, in a certain loose sense, has the shape of a forest.
More precisely,
\mathfrak{B}
can be obtained from any model
\mathfrak{A}
that
witnesses
(\mathcal{O},D)\not\models_{\sf CP}q
by a version of guarded unraveling
(see e.g.
[
\citeauthoryearGrädel and
Otto2014
]
) that leaves untouched
a ‘core’ of
\mathfrak{A}
defined as

{\sf core}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A}):=\{a\in A\mid\#_{\mathfrak{A}}({\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(a))\leq{\sf tower}(4^{{\left|{\Sigma}\right|}+4},{\left|{\phi}\right|})\}

for a suitable signature
\Sigma
.
With ‘leaving untouched’, we mean that elements from this core are not
duplicated during unraveling, but ‘reused’ whenever needed. This is
required to guarantee minimality w.r.t. the circumscription pattern.
It is not important to make this unraveling explicit for the
subsequent development, but it may still guide the reader’s intuition.

To prepare for the subsequent development, we give a central lemma
that establishes a sufficient condition for a model
\mathfrak{B}
to be

{\sf CP}
-minimal, based on comparing it to a
{\sf CP}
-minimal ‘reference model’

\mathfrak{A}
. This is a version of the ‘core lemma’ of

[
\citeauthoryearLutz, Manière, and
Nolte2023
]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\phi
be a GF sentence,
{\sf CP}=(\prec,\mathsf{M},\mathsf{F},\mathsf{V})
, and
\Sigma={\sf const}_{=}(\phi)\cup\mathsf{M}\cup\mathsf{F}
.
Further let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
{\sf CP}
-minimal model of
\phi
and let

\mathfrak{B}
be a model of
\phi
such that

1.

\mathsf{core}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A})\subseteq B
and
c^{\mathfrak{B}}=c^{\mathfrak{A}}
for all
c\in{\sf const}(\phi)
;

2.

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(a)={\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{B},\Sigma}(a)

for all
a\in\mathsf{core}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A})
;

3.

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{B},\Sigma}(B\setminus\mathsf{core}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A}))={\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(A\setminus\mathsf{core}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A}))
,

Then
\mathfrak{B}
is a
{\sf CP}
-minimal model of
\phi
.

Intuitively, Lemma
3
says that the exact multiplicity
of types realized in
\mathfrak{A}
outside of
\mathsf{core}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A})
is irrelevant for

{\sf CP}
-minimality.

Assume that we are given as an input a GF knowledge base
(\mathcal{O},D)
,
a circumscription pattern
{\sf CP}
, and a Boolean UCQ
q
. We want to decide whether
there is a countermodel
\mathcal{I}
against

(\mathcal{O},D)\models_{\sf CP}q(\bar{a})
. This may be rephrased as

\phi\models_{\sf CP}q(\bar{a})
for

\phi=\bigwedge\mathcal{O}\wedge\bigwedge D\wedge\!\!\!\bigwedge_{c\in{\sf adom}(D)}\!\!\!\big(P_{c}(c)\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\bigwedge_{c^{\prime}\in{\sf adom}(D)\setminus\{c\}}\!\!\!\!\!\neg P_{c^{\prime}}(c)\big).

We shall use the latter formulation. We may assume that
\phi
is in
Scott normal form, that is,
\phi
is

\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n_{\forall}}\forall\bar{x}\,(\alpha_{i}\rightarrow\varphi_{i})\wedge\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n_{\exists}}\forall\bar{x}\,(\beta_{i}\rightarrow\exists\bar{y}\,(\gamma_{i}\wedge\psi_{i})).

Let
\Sigma={\sf const}_{=}(\phi)\cup\mathsf{M}\cup\mathsf{F}
.
Set

M={\left|{{\sf const}(\phi)}\right|}+2^{\left|{\Sigma}\right|}\cdot{\sf tower}(4^{{\left|{\Sigma}\right|}+4},{\left|{\phi}\right|})+2^{\left|{\Sigma}\right|}
, and
fix a set
U
of size
M
. In an outer loop, our algorithm iterates
over all pairs
(\mathfrak{A}_{0},T_{\mathsf{\overline{core}}})
with
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
a finite
structure that interprets all constants from
\phi
and

T_{\mathsf{\overline{core}}}
a set of 1-types such that the following conditions
are satisfied:

•

A_{0}\subseteq U
;

•

T_{\mathsf{\overline{core}}}\subseteq{\sf tp}^{1}_{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{A}_{0})
.

We define
\Delta:=\{a\in A_{0}\mid{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{0},\Sigma}(a)\notin T_{\mathsf{\overline{core}}}\}
.

For each pair
(\mathfrak{A}_{0},T_{\mathsf{\overline{core}}})
, we then check
whether the following additional conditions are satisfied:

(I)

\mathfrak{A}_{0}
can be extended to a model
\mathfrak{A}
of
\phi

such that

(a)

\mathfrak{A}|_{A_{0}}=\mathfrak{A}_{0}
,

(b)

{\sf tp}^{1}_{\mathfrak{A},\Sigma}(A\setminus\Delta)=T_{\mathsf{\overline{core}}}
,

(c)

\mathfrak{A}\not\models q
;

(II)

there exists a
{\sf CP}
-minimal model
\mathfrak{B}
of

\phi
such that

&\forall\overline{B}^{\prime}\,\big(\overline{B}<_{\sf CP}\overline{B}^{\prime}\rightarrow\neg\vartheta^{\prime}\big)\big).\end{array}

It can be verified that

[
\citeauthoryearFeferman and Vaught1959

\citeauthoryearKuncak, Nguyen, and

2

carries over to circumscribed AQ-querying in the same straightforward
way as for FO
2
.

Theorem 9
.

In C
2
, circumscribed consequence and circumscribed AQ-querying are
decidable.

Since BAPA is also decidable over finite models, we also obtain the
version of Theorem
9
where circumscribed
consequence and querying are defined w.r.t. finite models.

7
Conclusion

We have studied the impact on computational complexity of adding
circumscription to decidable fragments of first-order logic, which
turns out to be remarkably varied: while FO
2
is very tame and does
not have higher complexity than
\mathcal{ALC}
in its circumscribed version, GF
suffers from a dramatic complexity explosion. We remark that there is
a close connection between circumscription and querying with closed
predicates as studied in
[
\citeauthoryearNgo, Ortiz, and Simkus2016
,
\citeauthoryearLutz, Seylan, and
Wolter2019
]
,
see also Example
1
. More details are in

[
\citeauthoryearLutz, Manière, and
Nolte2023
]
. As an example,
Theorem
6
also applies to AQ-querying of
guarded existential rules with a single unary closed predicate. This,
in turn, is related to results in
[
\citeauthoryearBenedikt et al.2016
]
.

Several interesting questions remain open. What is the exact
complexity of circumscribed consequence in C
2
? We speculate that by making careful
use of the techniques in
[
\citeauthoryearPratt-Hartmann2005
]
,
one can bring it down to
\textnormal{\sc coNExp}^{\textnormal{\sc NP}}
. What is the complexity of
circumscribed consequence in GF with only a single minimized predicate
or with multiple such predicates but no preference order? Is
circumscribed UCQ-querying in GF finitely controllable? What is the
complexity of circumscribed querying with less expressive classes of
existential rules such as inclusion dependencies? Is circumscribed
consequence decidable in the unary / guarded negation
fragments of FO?
Note that satisfiability in the latter fragment is known to be reducible to UCQ-querying in GF
[
\citeauthoryearBárány, ten Cate, and
Segoufin2015
]
, but that this reduction relies on arguments based on treeifications of some subformulas of interest, a technique that cannot be applied in presence of circumscription as discussed with Example
2
.

This property crucially relies on the condition
j\notin J(\bar{b}|_{\textrm{dom}(\rho)})
dictating the permitted function symbol at the root of each term.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
for some
m\geq 0
and
t\in{\sf gtp}(\mathfrak{A})
.
The tuple
\bar{a}/_{0}
, and a fortiori tuples
\bar{a}/_{1}
and
\bar{a}
, do not contain repeated elements, that is
a_{i}/_{0}=a_{k}/_{0}
iff
i=k
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
r
.
For
r=0
, this is immediate by definition of
\mathcal{K}^{0}(t)
.
For the induction step, assume the claim holds for all integers smaller than some
r>0
.
Let
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{r}(t)
for some
t
.
Since
r>0
, there is some type
s

and
\bar{b}\in\mathcal{K}^{r-1}(s)

from which
\bar{a}
has been obtained
in the construction of
\mathcal{K}^{r}(t)
, that is, there exist
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in{\sf mgt}(\mathfrak{A})
with
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{u})=s
,
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{v})=t
and for all
i=1,\dots,{\left|{\bar{a}}\right|}
:

a_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\;}l}b_{\ell}&\textrm{ if }(x_{\ell},x_{i})\in\rho\\
f^{j}_{s,\rho,t,i}(\bar{b}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)}/_{1})&\textrm{ if }x_{i}\notin\textrm{img}(\rho)\end{array}\right.

where
\rho=\rho(u,v)
and
j\notin J(\bar{b}|_{\textrm{dom}(\rho)})
.
By induction hypothesis, it is clear that if
x_{i},x_{k}\in{\sf img}(\rho)
, then
a_{i}/_{0}=a_{k}/_{0}
iff
i=k
.
In the case
x_{i},x_{k}\notin{\sf img}(\rho)
, then we have
a_{i}/_{0}=a_{k}/_{0}
iff
f^{j}_{s,\rho,t,i}(\bar{b}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)}/_{1})/_{0}=f^{j}_{s,\rho,t,k}(\bar{b}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)}/_{1})/_{0}
iff
c^{j}_{t,i}=c^{j}_{t,k}
iff
i=k
.
It remains to treat the case of
x_{i}\in{\sf img}(\rho)
and
x_{k}\not\in{\sf img}(\rho)
, for which we clearly have
i\neq k
and thus aim to prove
a_{i}/_{0}\neq a_{k}/_{0}
.
Note that
a_{k}/_{0}=c^{j}_{t,k}
while
a_{i}/_{0}
has shape
c^{j^{\prime}}_{t^{\prime},i^{\prime}}
for some
j^{\prime}\in J(b_{\ell})
with
\ell
such that
\rho(x_{\ell})=x_{i}
.
From
b_{\ell}\in\bar{b}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)}
and
j\notin J(\bar{b}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)})
, we obtain
a_{i}/_{0}\neq a_{k}/_{0}
.
∎

We
now define the
structure
\mathfrak{B}
. The universe
B
consists of all elements
in
\Delta
and all elements that appear in a tuple in one of the sets

\mathcal{K}^{r}(t)
. Set

\begin{array}[]{r@{}c@{}l}R^{\mathfrak{B}}&=&\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\geq 0}\bigcup_{t\in{\sf gtp}(\mathfrak{A})}\{[\bar{a}/\bar{e}]\mid R(\bar{e})\in t\text{ and }\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{r}(t)\}.\end{array}

for each relation symbol
R
where
[\bar{a}/\bar{e}]
denotes the
tuple obtained from
\bar{e}
by replacing every variable
x_{i}

with the
i
-th element
a_{i}
of
\bar{a}
.
Note that
\bar{e}
may involve elements from
\Delta
which are left unchanged.
For each constant
c
,
set
c^{\mathfrak{B}}=c^{\mathfrak{A}}
.

We remark that the above model construction is exactly the one from

[
\citeauthoryearBárány, Gottlob, and
Otto2014
]
except that we have defined types and the set

{\sf gtp}(\mathfrak{A})
in a different way, giving a special role to the
elements of
\Delta
. Moreover, we truncate terms at height 1
while
[
\citeauthoryearBárány, Gottlob, and
Otto2014
]
, which additionally deals with conjunctive queries,
truncates at larger heights. Without truncation, the above
construction falls back to being a standard unraveling
construction. Such a construction, however, does not deliver finite
models and truncation is used as a way to reuse elements so as to
achieve finiteneness. One has to be careful, though, because reusing
elements in a too agressive way will result in different guarded types
to overlap, which effectively generates guarded types that are not
present in the original structure
\mathfrak{A}
and may result in the
constructed structure
\mathfrak{B}
to not be a model of
\phi
. An extreme
case of such aggressive reuse is the filtration technique from modal logic
which is well-known to fail for the guarded fragment. The indexes

\cdot^{j}
result in several copies of each element to be introduced
which makes it possible to avoid too aggressive reuse when truncating.

We have to show that
\mathfrak{B}
is a model of
\phi
and satisfies
Conditions 1-4 from Lemma
1
.

The following lemma establishes a central ‘compatibility property’ for
the sets
\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
. If we find tuples
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
and

\bar{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}(t^{\prime})
that overlap, then
t
and
t^{\prime}
agree
regarding the positions in which
\bar{a}
and
\bar{a}^{\prime}
overlap. If
that property would fail, then our construction of
\mathfrak{B}
could generate types
that did not exist in the original model
\mathfrak{A}
, and this would lead to

\mathfrak{B}
not necessarily being a model of
\phi
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
,
\bar{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}(t^{\prime})
and let
\sigma=\rho(\bar{a},\bar{a}^{\prime})
.
If
R(\bar{e})\in t
and all variables in
\bar{e}
are in
{\sf dom}(\sigma)
, then
R(\sigma\bar{e})\in t^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The proof is by a nested induction on
m
and
n
.

For the outer
induction start, assume that
m=0
.

For the inner induction start, assume that
n=0
.
Let
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{0}(t)
and
\bar{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{0}(t^{\prime})
for some
t
and
t^{\prime}
, and let
\sigma=\rho(\bar{a},\bar{a}^{\prime})
.
By definition of
\mathcal{K}^{0}(t)
and

\mathcal{K}^{0}(t^{\prime})
, there are
j
and
j^{\prime}

such that
\bar{a}=\bar{c}^{j}_{t}
and

\bar{a}^{\prime}=\bar{c}^{j^{\prime}}_{t^{\prime}}
. Let
R(\bar{e})\in t
such that all variables in
\bar{e}
are in
{\sf dom}(\sigma)
.

If
\bar{e}
indeed contains a variable

x_{i}
, say with
\sigma x_{i}=x_{i^{\prime}}
, then from
a_{i}=a^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}
we obtain
c^{j}_{t,i}=c^{j^{\prime}}_{t^{\prime},i^{\prime}}
.
Therefore,
t=t^{\prime}
,
j=j^{\prime}
,
i=i^{\prime}
, thus
\bar{a}=\bar{a}^{\prime}
and
\sigma
is the identity.
It follows trivially that
R(\sigma\bar{e})\in t^{\prime}
.

Otherwise there is no variable in

\bar{e}
, and thus
\bar{e}
only
involves elements from
\Delta
. Since
t
is a
\Delta
-extended
type from
{\sf gtp}(\mathfrak{A})
,
by definition of such types we have

\mathfrak{A}\models R(\bar{e})
, which in turn yields
R(\bar{e})\in t^{\prime}
.

Now for the (inner) induction step.
Assume that the claim holds for
m=0

and all integers smaller than some
n>0
.
Let
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{0}(t)
and
\bar{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}(t^{\prime})
for some
t
and
t^{\prime}
and let
\sigma=\rho(\bar{a},\bar{a}^{\prime})
.
As
m=0
, there is again a
j
such that
\bar{a}=\bar{c}^{j}_{t}
.
Since
n>0
, there is some type
s^{\prime}

and
\bar{b}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{n-1}(s^{\prime})

from which
\bar{a}^{\prime}
has been obtained
in the construction of
\mathcal{K}^{n}(t^{\prime})
, that is, there exist
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in{\sf mgt}(\mathfrak{A})
with
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{u})=s^{\prime}
,
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{v})=t^{\prime}
and for all
i=1,\dots,{\left|{\bar{a}^{\prime}}\right|}
:

a^{\prime}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\;}l}b^{\prime}_{\ell}&\textrm{ if }(x_{\ell},x_{i})\in\rho\\
f^{j^{\prime}}_{s^{\prime},\rho,t^{\prime},i}(\bar{b}^{\prime}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)}/_{1})&\textrm{ if }x_{i}\notin\textrm{img}(\rho)\end{array}\right.

where
\rho=\rho(u,v)
and
j^{\prime}\notin J(\bar{b}^{\prime}|_{\textrm{dom}(\rho)})
.
Since
\bar{a}
does not contain any function symbol, it must be that every variable in the image of
\sigma
is also in the image of
\rho
, that is
{\sf img}(\sigma)\subseteq{\sf img}(\rho)
.
Since
\rho
is injective, we can consider
\rho^{-1}\circ\sigma
, which is the restriction to
{\sf dom}(\sigma)
of
\sigma^{\prime}:=\rho(\bar{a},\bar{b}^{\prime})
.
Let
R(\bar{e})\in t
such that all variables in
\bar{e}
are in
{\sf dom}(\sigma)
and, a fortiori, in
{\sf dom}(\sigma^{\prime})
.
By induction hypothesis, we obtain
R(\sigma^{\prime}\bar{e})\in s^{\prime}
, thus
R(\rho^{-1}\sigma\bar{e})\in s^{\prime}
.
Now, from
\mathsf{tp}_{\mathfrak{A}}({\bar{u}})=s^{\prime}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{A}\models R(\rho^{-1}\sigma\bar{e})[\bar{u}]
.
Recalling
\rho=\rho(\bar{u},\bar{v})
, this
yields
\mathfrak{A}\models R(\sigma\bar{e})[\bar{v}]
.
Recalling that
\mathsf{tp}_{\mathfrak{A}}({\bar{v}})=t^{\prime}
now guarantees
R(\sigma\bar{e})\in t^{\prime}
.

Now for the outer induction step.
Assume that the claim holds for all integers smaller than some
m>0
.

For the inner induction start, where
n=0
, the arguments are similar to those used in the case
m=0
and
n>0
.
For the inner induction step, assume that the claim holds for all integers smaller than some
n>0
.
Let
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
and
\bar{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}(t^{\prime})
for some
t
and
t^{\prime}
and
\sigma=\rho(\bar{a},\bar{a}^{\prime})
.
Let
\bar{b}\in\mathcal{K}^{m-1}(s)
and
\bar{b}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{n-1}(s^{\prime})
be tuples from which
\bar{a}
and

\bar{a}^{\prime}
have been obtained in the construction of

\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
and
\mathcal{K}^{n}(t^{\prime})
,
meaning there exist
\bar{u},\bar{v},\bar{u}^{\prime},\bar{v}^{\prime}\in{\sf mgt}(\mathfrak{A})
with
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{u})=s
,
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{v})=t
,
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{u}^{\prime})=s^{\prime}
,
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{A},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{v}^{\prime})=t^{\prime}
such that for all
i=1,\dots,{\left|{\bar{a}}\right|}
:

a_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\;}l}b_{\ell}&\textrm{ if }(x_{\ell},x_{i})\in\rho\\
f^{j}_{s,\rho,t,i}(\bar{b}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)}/_{1})&\textrm{ if }x_{i}\notin\textrm{img}(\rho)\end{array}\right.

where
\rho=\rho(u,v)
and
j\notin J(\bar{b}|_{\textrm{dom}(\rho)})
,
and for all
i=1,\dots,{\left|{\bar{a}^{\prime}}\right|}
:

a^{\prime}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\;}l}b^{\prime}_{\ell}&\textrm{ if }(x_{\ell},x_{i})\in\rho^{\prime}\\
f^{j^{\prime}}_{s^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},t^{\prime},i}(\bar{b}^{\prime}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho^{\prime})}/_{1})&\textrm{ if }x_{i}\notin\textrm{img}(\rho^{\prime})\end{array}\right.

where
\rho^{\prime}=\rho(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
and
j^{\prime}\notin J(\bar{b}^{\prime}|_{\textrm{dom}(\rho^{\prime})})
.
Let
R(\bar{e})\in t
such that all variables in
\bar{e}
are in
{\sf dom}(\sigma)
.

If
{\sf dom}(\sigma)\subseteq{\sf img}(\rho)
,
then we can form
\sigma\circ\rho
, which restricts
\sigma^{\prime}=\rho(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b})
to
{\sf dom}(\rho)
.
We may then conclude by
applying the induction hypothesis on
m
, with

\bar{a}:=\bar{b}
,
t=s
,
m=m-1
, and
R(\bar{e})=R(\rho^{-1}\bar{e})
.

Similarly, if
{\sf img}(\sigma)\subseteq{\sf img}(\rho^{\prime})
, then we can form

\rho^{-1}\circ\sigma
which restricts
\sigma^{\prime}=\rho(\bar{a},\bar{b}^{\prime})
to
{\sf dom}(\sigma)
.
In that case, the induction hypothesis
on
n
lets us conclude.

In the remaining case, that is

{\sf dom}(\sigma)\not\subseteq{\sf img}(\rho)
and

{\sf img}(\sigma)\not\subseteq{\sf img}(\rho^{\prime})
,
there are

x_{\iota}\in{\sf dom}(\sigma)\setminus{\sf img}(\rho)

and

x_{\kappa^{\prime}}\in{\sf img}(\sigma)\setminus{\sf img}(\rho^{\prime})
.
De facto,

a_{\iota}=f^{j}_{s,\rho,t,\iota}(\bar{b}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho)}/_{1})
and

a^{\prime}_{\kappa^{\prime}}=f^{j^{\prime}}_{s^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},t^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}(\bar{b}^{\prime}|_{{\sf dom}(\rho^{\prime})}/_{1})
.
We let
\iota^{\prime}
and
\kappa
be the indexes such that
\sigma(x_{\iota})=x_{\iota^{\prime}}
and
\sigma(x_{\kappa})=x_{\kappa^{\prime}}

We now prove
\sigma={\sf Id}
and

, which will directly conclude the proof.
Let
x_{i}\in{\sf dom}(\sigma)
and let
x_{i^{\prime}}:=\sigma x_{i}
.
We want to prove

.

Consider first the case of
x_{i^{\prime}}\not\in{\sf img}(\rho^{\prime})

.

.

a^{\prime}_{\iota^{\prime}}\not\in{\sf img}(\rho^{\prime})

x_{i}\not\in{\sf img}(\rho)

Notice the above case covers

,
\rho=\rho^{\prime}
,

,

.
This enforces

.
Indeed, if

, then

, this would contradict

.
Therefore, we know
x_{i}\in{\sf img}(\rho)

.
Thus, there is
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
such that

and

.

.

.

Lemma 8
.

If
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
,
then
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{B},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{a})=t
.

Proof.

Let
t\in{\sf gtp}(\mathfrak{A})
be an
n
-type on
\widehat{\Sigma}
and
\bar{a}\in\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
for some
m
. We have to show that for any atom

\alpha
using as terms only variables among
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and constants
from
\Delta
, we have
\alpha\in t
iff

\mathfrak{B}\models\alpha[\bar{a}]
.
From the maximality of
t
and
{\sf tp}_{\mathfrak{B},\widehat{\Sigma}}(\bar{a})
, it will
follow that the same equivalence holds also for literals, concluding the proof.
Now for an atom
\alpha
, we distinguish the following cases:

•

\alpha
has the form
x_{i}=c
, with
1\leq i\leq n
and
c\in\Delta
.

Assume that
x_{i}=c\in t
.
Since
t\in{\sf gtp}(\mathfrak{A})
, there exists a
\bar{b}\in{\sf mgt}(\mathfrak{A})
with type
t
.
However, by definition,
\bar{b}
does not contain any element from
\Delta
, hence a contradiction.
Conversely, notice that, by definition of
\mathcal{K}^{m}(t)
, none of its tuples contains an element from
\Delta
.
Therefore, assuming
\mathfrak{B}\models(x_{i}=c)[\bar{a}]
already yields a contradiction.

•

\alpha
has the form
x_{i}=x_{j}
, with
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Recall that, from Lemma
6
,
\bar{a}
is without repetition.
Now, if
x_{i}=x_{j}\in t
, recalling that
t
is the type of a maximally guarded tuple in
\mathfrak{A}
, hence of a repetition-free tuple, it must be that
i=j
.
Naturally,
\mathfrak{B}\models a_{i}=a_{i}
.
For the other direction, assume
\mathfrak{B}\models a_{i}=a_{j}
.
Since
\bar{a}
is without repetition, it must be that
i=j
.
Every type from
\mathfrak{A}
trivially contains
x_{i}=x_{i}
.

•

\alpha
has the form
c_{1}=c_{2}
, with
c_{1},c_{2}\in\Delta
.

Since elements of
\Delta
are treated as constants in
\mathfrak{B}
and we defined
c^{\mathfrak{B}}=c^{\mathfrak{A}}
for each constant
c
, we immediately have
\mathfrak{B}\models c_{1}=c_{2}
iff
\mathfrak{A}\models c_{1}=c_{2}
iff
c_{1}=c_{2}\in t
(by definition of
{\sf gtp}(\mathfrak{A})
).

•

\alpha
has the form
R(\bar{e})
.

It is immediate by construction of
\mathfrak{B}
that
R(\bar{e})\in t

implies
\mathfrak{B}\models R(\bar{e})[\bar{a}]
. Conversely, assume that

\mathfrak{B}\models R(\bar{e})[\bar{a}]
. Then by construction of
\mathfrak{B}
there is
some
n^{\prime}
-type
t^{\prime}
,
m^{\prime}\geq 0
,
\bar{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{m^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})
, and
atom
R(\bar{e}^{\prime})\in t^{\prime}
such that
R(\bar{e}^{\prime})[\bar{a}^{\prime}]=R(\bar{e})[\bar{a}]
, that is:

e^{\prime}_{\ell}=x_{i}
and
e_{\ell}=x_{j}
implies
a^{\prime}_{i}=a_{j}
.
Letting
\sigma=\rho(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{a})
, the above yields
\sigma\bar{e}^{\prime}=\bar{e}
and,
in particular, all variables occurring in
\bar{e}^{\prime}
are contained in
{\sf dom}(\sigma)
.
Lemma
7
thus delivers

R(\sigma\bar{e}^{\prime})\in t
, that is
R(\bar{e})\in t
as desired.

∎

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a multiplicative function and let

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{p:|f(p)|\neq 1}\frac{1}{p}=\infty,

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{p:f(p)=0}\frac{1}{p}<\infty.

Then for

\{n\in\mathbb{N}:f(n)=uf(n+h)\neq 0\}

both have logarithmic density 0, i.e., for both

(4)

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
f(n)f(n+h)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1_{f(n)=uf(n+h)}}{n}=0.

The condition (

Let

1.1.2.
Proof of Theorem

The proof of Theorem

is the main goal of the paper.
We will assume for the sake of contradiction the existence of a parameter
\delta\in(0,1)
and an infinite increasing sequence
(x_{j})_{j}
such that

(7)

\frac{1}{\log x_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x_{j}\\

for

In order to analyse the condition (

[
13
]
(see Theorem

\mathbb{U}
, the closed unit disc. This result enables one to gain structural information on the prime values

whenever the correlation sums

\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{g(an+b)\overline{g}(cn+d)}{n}

\mathbb{U}

We observe, crucially, that as

. Moreover, by taking norms of both sides we have the immediate implication

, so that

. Thus, upon replacing

by

, which is still multiplicative, and

by
U,V
respectively, the condition (
7
) still holds, and we can reduce immediately to the case that

and

\mathbb{Z}
(hence why we assume (
2

For ease of exposition, for the remainder of this section we will suppose that the affine linear forms

for all

The archimedean argument.

Under the assumption that

, the additive function

is well-defined; moreover, we have

f(n)=f(n+h)\text{ if and only if }g(n)=g(n+h),

5

5

Here we use the standard notation

f_{t}(n)=e(tg(n)/2\pi)

can be used as Fourier characters to study the distribution of

, in intervals of the real line. This allows us to probe the

passing from an equality to an inequality, in tandem with the Fourier pairing

to translate the lower bound in (

\frac{1}{\log x_{j}}\left|\sum_{n\leq x_{j}}\frac{e(y(g(n+h)-g(n)))}{n}\right|=\frac{1}{\log x_{j}}\left|\sum_{n\leq x_{j}}\frac{f_{2\pi iy}(n+h)\overline{f}_{2\pi iy}(n)}{n}\right|

More precisely, Tao’s theorem allows us to show (after some manipulations) that for each

\sum_{p\leq x_{j}}\frac{1-\text{Re}(f(p)^{2\pi iy}p^{-2\pi it_{y}})}{p}=\sum_{p\leq x_{j}}\frac{1-\text{Re}((f(p)^{y}p^{-t_{y}})^{2\pi i})}{p}=O_{\delta}(1).

Using an idea going back to Halász

Having utilised the compositions

Seeking a contradiction to the original premise (

for all but a sparse set of

Remark 1.8
.

In ongoing joint work with O. Klurman, we consider the related problem of bounding the sets of solutions

satisfying

for
f
an integer-valued multiplicative function and
h\geq 1
, with a view towards understanding the gaps between consecutive values of such functions (among other applications). Unlike in the
homogeneous
(i.e.,

) problem considered in this paper, examples do exist where the number of such solutions has positive density (e.g., take

, then

is such an example). We thus seek to classify those functions for which this rigid behaviour occurs.

While some parts of the arguments given here are still applicable in the context of that problem, the non-archimedean part is not directly so, due to the fact that the map

is
not
multiplicative whenever the conductor of

does not divide

2.
Background Results about Multiplicative Functions

In this section we collect some background notions and results about multiplicative functions and pretentious number theory to be used in the sequel.

2.1.
Properties of the pretentious distance

A key rôle is played by the pretentious distances (in the sense of Granville and Soundararajan). Precisely, for arithmetic functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
and
x\geq 2
, define

\mathbb{D}(f,g;x):=\left(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1-\text{Re}(f(p)\overline{g}(p))}{p}\right)^{1/2}.

We also write
\mathbb{D}(f,g;\infty):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{D}(f,g;x)
.

Clearly,
0\leq\mathbb{D}(f,g;x)^{2}\leq 2\log\log x
by Mertens’ theorem. When
\mathbb{D}(f,g;\infty)<\infty
we say that
f

pretends to be

g
(symmetrically,
g

pretends to be

f
), or that
f
is
g
-pretentious
.

The pretentious distance functions satisfy the
pretentious triangle inequality
, which we will use in the following two forms
[
3
, Lem. 3.1]
. For functions
f,g,h,f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f,h;x)\leq\mathbb{D}(f,g;x)+\mathbb{D}(g,h;x),

(12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f_{1}f_{2},g_{1}g_{2};x)\leq\mathbb{D}(f_{1},g_{1};x)+\mathbb{D}(f_{2},g_{2};x).

Applying (
12
) inductively, we can show that for
f,g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
and
m\geq 1
,

(13)

\mathbb{D}(f^{m},g^{m};x)\leq m\mathbb{D}(f,g;x).

This result implies, for instance, that if
f
pretends to be
g
then
f^{m}
pretends to be
g^{m}
. The latter is especially helpful when, say,
g
takes its non-zero values in roots of unity of some order (as is the case for instance for Dirichlet characters), in which case
g^{m}\equiv|g|
, and thus
f^{m}
pretends to be a function taking values
0
or
1
(and thus is, in a precise sense, close to being non-negative).

Note that when
f(p),g(p)\in S^{1}
we may rewrite the numerators of the summands in the definition of
\mathbb{D}(f,g;x)
using the relation

|f(p)-g(p)|^{2}=2(1-\text{Re}(f(p)\overline{g}(p))).

In particular, it follows that if
f
is
g
-pretentious and
\eta>0
then the set

\mathcal{G}_{\eta}:=\{p:|f(p)-g(p)|>\eta\}

satisfies

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\in\mathcal{G}_{\eta}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\leq\eta^{-2}\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{|f(p)-g(p)|^{2}}{p}=\eta^{-2}\mathbb{D}(f,g;x)^{2}\ll_{\eta}1.

We will make conclusions of this kind throughout the paper.
For convenience, therefore, in the sequel we will refer to a subset of primes
S
as being
sparse
(respectively
C
-sparse
) if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\in S\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\ll 1,

(respectively,
\ll_{C}1
) as
x\rightarrow\infty
.

The archimedean characters
n\mapsto n^{it}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, form an important class of examples of multiplicative functions with non-zero mean values and correlations (see Theorem
2.2
below, for example). In many results in the sequel we will need control of the distance between
n^{it}
and
1
. This is provided by the following standard result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x\geq 2
and let
|t|\leq x^{2}
.

If
|t|\geq 10
then as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\mathbb{D}(n^{it},1;x)^{2}\geq(1/3-o(1))\log\log x.

On the other hand, if
|t|\leq 10
then

\mathbb{D}(n^{it},1;x)^{2}=\log(1+|t|\log x)+O(1).

Proof.

Set
\sigma:=1+1/\log x
. Since

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}(p^{it})}{p}=\log|\zeta(\sigma+it)|+O(1),

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
the result follows from Mertens’ theorem and the elementary estimate

\zeta(\sigma+it)=\frac{\log x}{1+it\log x}+O(1+|t|)\text{ for $|t|\leq 10$,}

as well as the estimates

|\zeta(\sigma+it)|,|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^{-1}\ll\log^{2/3}(2+|t|)\log\log^{1/3}(2+|t|)\text{ for $|t|\geq 10$,}

which are consequences of the Vinogradov-Korobov exponential sum bounds
[
5
, Cor. 8.28, Thm. 8.29]
.
∎

2.2.
Tao’s theorem on logarithmically-averaged correlations

As described in the previous section, a key tool in our analysis of the distribution of pairs
(f(an+b),f(cn+d))
is the following theorem of Tao
[
13
]
, which prescribes necessary conditions for a multiplicative function

to have large, logarithmically-averaged binary correlations.

Theorem 2.2

(Tao’s theorem on binary correlations)
.

Let
\eta>0
, and let
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
with
ad-bc\neq 0
. Let

be a 1-bounded multiplicative function with the property that for some

\frac{1}{\log x}\left|\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{g(an+b)\overline{g}(cn+d)}{n}\right|\geq\eta.

Then there is a primitive Dirichlet character
\psi=\psi_{x}
of conductor
O_{\eta}(1)
and a real number

with

such that

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(g(p)\overline{\psi}(p)p^{-it})}{p}=O_{\eta}(1).

the sequence of Galois conjugates
(\sigma(a_{\phi}(n)))_{n}
is also the sequence of Fourier coefficients for a cusp form
\phi^{\sigma}
of the same weight and level as
\phi
. By the Sato-Tate theorem

[
1
]
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we then have

(43)

|\{p\leq y:|\sigma(a_{\phi}(p))/p^{(k-1)/2}|\leq\varepsilon\}|=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\sqrt{4-u^{2}}du+o(1)\right)\int_{2}^{y}\frac{dt}{\log t}<2\varepsilon\int_{2}^{y}\frac{dt}{\log t}

whenever
y\geq y_{0}(\varepsilon,\phi)
.

Observing the obvious implication

|\sigma(a_{\phi}(p))|>\varepsilon p^{(k-1)/2}\text{ for all }\sigma\in G_{k}\Rightarrow|N_{K}(a_{\phi}(p))|=\prod_{\sigma\in G_{K}}|\sigma(a_{\phi}(p))|\neq 1

whenever
p\geq p_{0}(\varepsilon)
, we deduce from (
43
) and the union bound that

\displaystyle|\{p\leq y:|N_{K}(a_{\phi}(p))|\neq 1\}|

\displaystyle\geq|\{p\leq y:|\sigma(a_{\phi}(p))|>\varepsilon p^{(k-1)/2}\text{ for all }\sigma\in G_{K}\}|

\displaystyle\geq(1-5\varepsilon|G_{K}|)\int_{2}^{y}\frac{dt}{\log t}

for all
y\geq y_{0}(\varepsilon,\phi)
.
By partial summation, we therefore obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
|N_{K}(a_{\phi}(p))|\neq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq(1-10\varepsilon|G_{K}|)\log\log x

for
x\geq x_{0}(\varepsilon,\phi)
. Choosing
\varepsilon:=1/(20|G_{K}|)
and picking
x
large enough, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
|N_{K}(a_{\phi}(p))|=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\log x,

which verifies (
2
) in the general case of Corollary
1.7
, and completes the proof.

8.
Generalisations to complex-valued functions

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a complex-valued multiplicative function. It is natural to try to extend the scope of the main theorems in this paper to handle the function
f
even when it might not only take (algebraic) integer values. While heuristically such functions should also fall into the purview of these theorems, there are some technical obstacles to achieving such an extension, particularly as it pertains to the non-archimedean arguments in Section
4
.

Naturally, in order for the composition
\chi(f)
of a Dirichlet character
\chi
with
f
to be well-defined we require at the very least that
f
take rational values. One way of avoiding the assumption that
f
itself takes rational values, and perhaps to reduce matters to the work on integer-valued functions described already, is to use a lower bound for the number of solutions in
n\leq x_{j}
to

uf(an+b)=vf(cn+d)\neq 0

with
u,v\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
, to obtain one for the number of solutions in
n\leq x_{j}
to

\tilde{u}\tilde{f}(an+b)=\tilde{v}\tilde{f}(cn+d)\neq 0,

where
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is an associated integer-valued multiplicative function, and
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
. In order to fulfill the hypotheses of Theorem
1.4
,
\tilde{f}
must also inherit the properties

\sum_{p:\tilde{f}(p)=0}\frac{1}{p}<\infty,\quad\sum_{p:|\tilde{f}(p)|\neq 1}\frac{1}{p}=\infty

from
f
. Inspired by an idea of Halász
[
4
, p. 144]
, we present here a construction in this direction, which depends crucially on the scales
(x_{j})_{j}
, and yields a sequence of functions
\tilde{f}_{j}
, which we call
rationalisations
of
f
, for which either
\tilde{f}_{j}(n)
or
1/\tilde{f}_{j}(n)
is a rational integer.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a complex-valued multiplicative function satisfying

(44)

\displaystyle\sum_{p:f(p)=0}\frac{1}{p}<\infty,\quad\sum_{p:|f(p)|\neq 1}\frac{1}{p}=\infty.

Assume that there are
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
,
u,v\in\mathbb{C}
and
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
uv(ad-bc)\neq 0
, and a
\delta>0
such that

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
f(an+b)f(cn+d)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1_{uf(an+b)=vf(cn+d)}}{n}\geq\delta,

as
x\rightarrow\infty
along an infinite increasing sequence
(x_{j})_{j\geq 1}
.

Then we can find a sequence
(\tilde{f}_{j})_{j}
of rational-valued multiplicative functions
\tilde{f}_{j}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{+}\cup\{0\}
, and positive integers
u_{j},v_{j}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that (
44
) holds with
\tilde{f}_{j}
in place of
f
, and

\frac{1}{\log x_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x_{j}\\
\tilde{f}_{j}(an+b)\tilde{f}_{j}(cn+d)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1_{u_{j}\tilde{f}_{j}(an+b)=\tilde{f}_{j}(cn+d)}}{n}\geq\delta-o_{j\rightarrow\infty}(1).

Proof.

Note first of all that

uf(an+b)=vf(cn+d)\Rightarrow|u||f(an+b)|=|v||f(cn+d)|.

Moreover, the conditions (
44
) are left unchanged when
f
is replaced by
|f|
. Thus, in making this substitution we may assume in the foregoing that
u,v
and
f
are real-valued and non-negative, and
f
satisfies both (
44
) and (
45
).

Next, for each prime power
p^{k}
define

g(p^{k}):=\begin{cases}\frac{\log f(p^{k})}{\log 2}&\text{ if }f(p^{k})>0\\
0&\text{ if }f(p^{k})=0.\end{cases}

We extend
g
to an additive function, so that either
f(n)=0
or else
f(n)=2^{g(n)}
whenever
f(n)>0
. Now, for each
j\geq 1
let
d_{j}\geq 1
denote the dimension of

V_{j}:=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\{g(p^{k}):p^{k}\leq x_{j}\}\cup\{(\log u)/\log 2,(\log v)/\log 2\}),

and let
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d_{j}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a basis for
V_{j}
. Thus, for each
p^{k}\leq x_{j}
there exist coefficients
(\lambda_{i}(p^{k}))_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\subset\mathbb{Q}
such that

g(p^{k})=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})\theta_{i},

and also

\frac{\log u}{\log 2}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\mu_{i}\theta_{i},\quad\frac{\log v}{\log 2}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\nu_{i}\theta_{i}.

Note that whenever
f(p^{k})=1
we have
\lambda_{i}(p^{k})=0
for all
i
since the
\theta_{i}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. We define, for each
i
,

\lambda_{i}(n):=\sum_{p^{k}||n}\lambda_{i}(p^{k}),

so that
g(n)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\lambda_{i}(n)\theta_{i}
for all
n
. We observe then, by linear independence, that whenever
an+b,cn+d\leq x_{j}
,

(46)

uf(an+b)=vf(cn+d)\,\Leftrightarrow\,\lambda_{i}(an+b)+\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}(cn+d)+\nu_{i}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq d_{j}.

Next, let
\boldsymbol{R}:=(R_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a tuple of integers to be chosen momentarily, and define a rational-valued additive function
\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}
at prime powers via

\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(p^{k}):=\begin{cases}\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})R_{i}&\text{ if }f(p^{k})\neq 0,\,p^{k}\leq x_{j}\\
g(p^{k})&\text{ if }f(p^{k})\neq 0,\,p^{k}>x_{j}\\
0&\text{ if }f(p^{k})=0.\end{cases}

In addition, define
\tilde{u}_{\boldsymbol{R}},\tilde{v}_{\boldsymbol{R}}
implicitly via

\frac{\log\tilde{u}_{\boldsymbol{R}}}{\log 2}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\mu_{i}R_{i},\quad\frac{\log\tilde{v}_{\boldsymbol{R}}}{\log 2}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\nu_{i}R_{i}.

Finally, define the multiplicative function
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
via

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(n):=\begin{cases}2^{\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(n)}&\text{ if }f(n)\neq 0\\
0&\text{ if }f(n)=0.\end{cases}

Note that
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(n)\neq 0
iff
f(n)\neq 0
. We now claim that there is a choice of
\boldsymbol{R}
such that the following holds:

(i)

if
p\leq x_{j}
and
|f(p)|\neq 1
then
|\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(p)|\neq 1
,

(ii)

\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(n)\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
n\leq x_{j}
, and

(iii)

if
an+b,cn+d\leq x_{j}
with
uf(an+b)=vf(cn+d)\neq 0
then

\tilde{u}_{\boldsymbol{R}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(an+b)=\tilde{v}_{\boldsymbol{R}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(cn+d)\neq 0.

Under these conditions, putting
\tilde{f}_{j}:=\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}
and
u_{j}:=\tilde{u}_{\boldsymbol{R}}
and
v_{j}:=\tilde{v}_{\boldsymbol{R}}
, we would see that (
44
) holds with
\tilde{f}_{j}
in place of
p
, and moreover

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log x_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x_{j}\\
\tilde{f}_{j}(an+b)\tilde{f}_{j}(cn+d)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1_{u_{j}\tilde{f}_{j}(an+b)=v_{j}\tilde{f}_{j}(cn+d)}}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\log x_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x_{j}\\
f(an+b)f(cn+d)\neq 0\\
an+b,cn+d\leq x_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1_{uf(an+b)=vf(cn+d)}}{n}-\frac{\log(2(a+c))}{\log x_{j}}

\displaystyle\geq\delta-\frac{2\log(2(a+c))}{\log x_{j}},

which suffices to prove the claim as
j\rightarrow\infty
(since
a,c
are fixed).

Note first of all that both
f
and
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}
are non-negative for all
n
. Also, by our previous observation, if
f(p)=0
then
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(p)=0
.
Now, consider
\mathcal{P}_{j}:=\{p\leq x_{j}:f(p)\neq 0,1\}
. Then by the definition of
g(p)
,

\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\lambda_{i}(p)\theta_{i}\neq 0\text{ for all }p\in\mathcal{P}_{j}.

For each
p\in\mathcal{P}_{j}
put
\Lambda_{j}(p):=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}|\lambda_{i}(p)|>0
, and let

\eta:=\min_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{j}}\|g(p)\|/\max\{1,\Lambda_{j}(p)\}\in(0,1/2).

By Dirichlet’s theorem we may pick a sequence of rationals
\rho_{i}=r_{i}/s_{i}
such that

\max_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}|\rho_{i}-\theta_{i}|<\eta/2

(and such that the denominators
s_{i}\leq 2/\sqrt{\eta}
). Putting
S_{j}:=\text{lcm}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d_{j}})
, we see that
S_{j}\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
1\leq i\leq d_{j}
, and

\min_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{j}}|\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\lambda_{i}(p)S_{j}\rho_{i}|>\eta S_{j}/2.

If we select
D_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
so that
D_{j}\mu_{i},D_{j}\nu_{i}
and
D_{j}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})
are integers for all
1\leq i\leq d_{j}
and
p^{k}\leq x_{j}
with
f(p^{k})\neq 0
, and then define
R_{i}:=D_{j}S_{j}\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i
then we see that

\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(p)\neq 0\text{ for all }p\in\mathcal{P}_{i},

so (i) holds. Moreover, as

\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(p^{k})=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})R_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }p^{k}\leq x_{j},

we have
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(p^{k})=2^{\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(p^{k})}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
p^{k}\leq x_{j}
, and thus also for all
n\leq x_{j}
. for which
f(n)\neq 0
. Hence, (ii) holds as well.

Finally, we verify (iii). Suppose
uf(an+b)=vf(cn+d)\neq 0
, with
an+b,cn+d\leq x_{j}
. Then
uv\neq 0
and
f(p^{k})\neq 0
for all
p^{k}||(an+b)
and
p^{k}||(cn+d)
. By (
46
) we thus have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}||an+b\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})+\mu_{i}=\sum_{p^{k}||cn+d}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})+\nu_{i}

for all
1\leq i\leq d_{j}
. Scaling this equation by
R_{i}
and then summing over
i
, we thus also have

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(an+b)+\frac{\log\tilde{u}_{\boldsymbol{R}}}{\log 2}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}(\sum_{p^{k}||an+b}R_{i}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})+R_{i}\mu_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}(\sum_{p^{k}||cn+d}R_{i}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})+R_{i}\nu_{i})

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(cn+d)+\frac{\log\tilde{v}_{\boldsymbol{R}}}{\log 2},

and so exponentiating in powers of
2
we obtain

\tilde{u}_{\boldsymbol{R}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(an+b)=\tilde{v}_{\boldsymbol{R}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(cn+d)\neq 0,

as claimed.
∎

The effect of having
scale-dependent
rationalisations
\tilde{f}_{j}
as constructed above is that, unlike in the current circumstances where
f(p)=O_{f,\delta}(1)
at
small primes

p=O_{\delta}(1)
, the size of
\max_{p=O_{\delta}(1)}\tilde{f}_{j}(p)
is now dependent on
x_{j}
. In particular, we would no longer be able to show that the exponent
r
arising in the conclusion to Proposition
3.1
is independent of
x_{j}
. This makes it difficult to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.4
(as is done in Section
5
), where the fact that
r
could be solely bounded in terms of
\delta
was crucial in making a choice of small prime
p=O_{\delta}(1)
for which
f(p)\approx p^{r}
is small compared to a chosen modulus
q
.

This defect, currently present in the construction of rationalisations
\tilde{f}_{j}
just given, only arises in choosing the scaling parameters
R_{j}
needed to produce the
integer
-valued additive function
\tilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{R}}
. Following the proof of Lemma
8.1
,
suppose instead that we worked with the rational-valued additive function
g_{\mathbb{Q}}
defined at prime powers
p^{k}
via

g_{\mathbb{Q}}(p^{k}):=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{j}}\lambda_{i}(p^{k})\rho_{i}.

Define also the
algebraic
-valued multiplicative function

f_{\mathbb{Q}}(n):=\begin{cases}2^{g_{\mathbb{Q}}(n)}&\text{ if }f(n)\neq 0\\
0&\text{ if }f(n)=0.\end{cases}

Then, as

|g(p)-g_{\mathbb{Q}}(p)|<\eta/2<1/4\text{ for }p\in\mathcal{P}_{j},

it would transpire (assuming once again that
f\geq 0
) that
f(p)/f_{\mathbb{Q}}(p)\in[1/2,2]
for all
p
with
f(p)\neq 0,1
, and thus the algebraic-valued
f_{\mathbb{Q}}
would still satisfy
\max_{p=O_{\delta}(1)}f_{\mathbb{Q}}(p)=O_{f,\delta}(1)
.

From the above observations, therefore, it would be desirable to either find an alternative construction of rationalisations of
f
that are
scale-independent
, or to find a suitable treatment of algebraic-valued multiplicative functions (even when their values do not belong to any fixed number field).

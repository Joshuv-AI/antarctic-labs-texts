Simultaneous recovery of a locally rough interface and the embedded obstacle with its surrounding medium
Jiaqing Yang, Jianliang Li, Bo Zhang

Abstract
Consider the scattering of time-harmonic point sources by an infinite locally rough interface with bounded obstacles embedded in the lower half-space. The model problem is first reduced to an equivalent integral equation formulation defined in a bounded domain, where the well-posedness is obtained in L p L^{p} by the classical Fredholm theory. Then a global uniqueness theorem is proved for the inverse problem of recovering the locally rough interface, the embedded obstacles and the wave number in the lower-half space by means of near-field measurements above the interface. Keywords : inverse acoustic scattering, Lippmann-Schwinger equation, uniqueness, rough interface, embedded obstacle.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the two-dimensional inverse scattering of time-harmonic acoustic point sources by a locally rough interface with obstacles embedded in the lower half-space. This type of problems can find applications in diverse scientific areas such as radar, underwater exploration and non-destructive testing, where the shape, location and boundary conditions of both the interface and embedded obstacles need to be simultaneously
reconstructed by the measurements of the scattered fields taken on some certain subdomain of the upper-half space.

The physical configuration of the scattering problem.

Let the scattering interface be denoted by a smooth curve
\Gamma:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}=f(x_{1})\}
, where
f
is assumed to be a Lipschitz continuous function with compact support.
This means that
\Gamma
is just a local perturbation of the planar interface
\Gamma_{0}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}=0\}

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\Gamma

\Omega_{1}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}>f(x_{1})\}
and the lower half-space
\Omega_{2}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}<f(x_{1})\}
, where a bounded obstacle
D
is embedded into

\Omega_{2}
which is assumed to be of
C^{2}
-class. We refer the reader to Figure
1

Consider the incident field to be generated by a point source

\displaystyle u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s})=\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s}):=\frac{\rm i}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(\kappa_{1}|x-x_{s}|),\qquad x_{s}\in\Omega_{1}

\mathcal{B}u=0&\textrm{on}\;\;\partial{D},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{r}\left(\frac{\partial u^{s}}{\partial r}-{\rm i}\kappa u^{s}\right)=0&{\rm for}\;\;r=|x|\end{array}\right.

If

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta u+\kappa^{2}n(x)u=0&\textrm{in}\;\;{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{x_{s}\},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{r}\left(\frac{\partial u^{s}}{\partial r}-{\rm i}\kappa u^{s}\right)=0&{\rm for}\;\;r=|x|,\end{array}\right.

where the total field

in

and

in

Preliminaries

2.1
Some useful function spaces

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with a Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
denote the usual Sobolev space with index
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, equipped with
the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{m,p}:=\left(\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/p}.

For
p=2
, we also write
W^{m,2}(\Omega)
by the notation
H^{m}(\Omega)
which is a Hilbert space under the inner product

\displaystyle(u,v)_{m}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}(\partial^{\alpha}u,\partial^{\alpha}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For
m=0
,
W^{0,p}(\Omega)
is reduced to the usual
L^{p}(\Omega)
space consisting of all
L^{p}
-integrable functions on
\Omega
. Moreover, we also introduce the following function space

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega):=\{u\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\Omega)|u\in H^{1}(\Omega),\;\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

which is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product

\displaystyle(u,v)_{H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega)}=(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\Delta u,\Delta v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad{\rm for\;}u,v\in H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega),

where
\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\Omega)
denotes the set consisting of all distribution functions defined on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.

2.2
The background Green’s function

In this subsection, we introduce the two-dimensional background Green’s function associated with the Helmholtz equation in a two-layered medium separated by the planar surface
\Gamma_{0}
. Suppose the incident wave
u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s})
is induced by a point source
\Phi_{\kappa}(x,x_{s})
for
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
or
\kappa_{2}
, which means
u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s})=\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
for

by the planar surface

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{x}{\mathbb{G}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})+\kappa_{0}^{2}(x){\mathbb{G}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})=-\delta(x-x_{s})&\textrm{in}\;{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{r}\left(\frac{\partial{\mathbb{G}}^{s}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})}{\partial r}-{\rm i}\kappa_{0}{\mathbb{G}}^{s}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})\right)=0&{\rm for}\;r=|x|\end{array}\right.

where
\kappa_{0}>0
is the wave number satisfying
\kappa_{0}(x):=\kappa_{1}>0
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
and
\kappa_{0}(x):=\kappa_{2}>0
for

, and

denotes the scattered field when the observation point

and the source point

belong to the same half-space, denotes the transmitted field when the observation point
x
and the source point
x_{s}
belong to the different half-space. For the relation between
{\mathbb{G}}
and

, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{G}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\mathbb{G}}^{s}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})+u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s})\quad{\rm for}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}\;\;{\rm or}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-},\\[5.69054pt]
{\mathbb{G}}^{s}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;{\rm for}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}\;\;{\rm or}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-}.\end{array}\right.

Now we are in position to deduce the formulation of

. It follows from the Fourier transform
[
22
]
that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{G}}^{s}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\rm i}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}\frac{\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}e^{{\rm i}\beta_{1}(x_{2}+x_{s2})}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x_{1}-x_{s1})}d\xi\quad\;\;{\rm for}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\rm i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}e^{{\rm i}(\beta_{1}x_{s2}-\beta_{2}x_{2})}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x_{1}-x_{s1})}d\xi\quad\;\;\;{\rm for}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\rm i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}e^{{\rm i}(\beta_{1}x_{2}-\beta_{2}x_{s2})}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x_{1}-x_{s1})}d\xi\quad\;\;\;{\rm for}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-},\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\rm i}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{2}}\frac{\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}e^{-{\rm i}\beta_{2}(x_{2}+x_{s2})}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x_{1}-x_{s1})}d\xi\quad{\rm for}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{-},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
are defined by

\displaystyle\beta_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sqrt{\kappa_{j}^{2}-\xi^{2}}\quad\;{\rm for}\quad|\kappa_{j}|>|\xi|,\\
{\rm i}\sqrt{\xi^{2}-\kappa_{j}^{2}}\quad{\rm for}\quad|\kappa_{j}|<|\xi|,\end{array}\right.

for

.

To establish the uniqueness results for inverse problems, we need to consider the scattering of a hyper-singular point source

and for

\displaystyle{\mathbb{U}}^{s}(x,x_{s};\Gamma_{0})=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}e^{{\rm i}\beta_{1}(x_{2}+x_{s2})}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x_{1}-x_{s1})}d\xi\qquad\;\;\;{\rm for}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\;\;x_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},\\[5.69054pt]

) and (
1

To show the existence, a special case of

Theorem 3.1
.

The direct scattering problem (

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}+|u|^{2}\right)ds=0,

3.1
The special case
D=\emptyset
or
n(x)\equiv 1

In this subsection, we study the scattering of point sources only by a locally rough interface
\Gamma
, which means that no obstacles are embedded into the two-layered medium. Different from all existing works, e.g.,
[
8
,
34
,
25
,
31
,
37
]
, we will propose a novel technique to prove the existence of a unique solution by transforming the reduced model into an equivalent Lippmann-Schwinger type integral equation defined in a bounded domain. Then the well-posedness in
L^{p}
space will directly follows from the classical Fredholm theory.

We consider two classes of incident waves

\displaystyle u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})\qquad\;&{\rm for}\;\;x_{s}\in\Omega_{1}\\
&\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})\;\;\;&{\rm for}\;\;x_{s}\in\Omega_{1}\end{aligned}\right.

where
\ell=1,2
. Compared with the incident wave
\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
, it is easily seen that
\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
is a hyper-singular point source at
x_{s}
.
To indicate the dependence of the total field on
u^{\rm inc}
, we use
{\mathbb{G}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)
and
{\mathbb{U}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)
to denote the total field corresponding to the scattering of
\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
and
\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
, respectively, by the locally rough surface
\Gamma
. In the following, for simplicity, we only provide a detailed proof for the existence of
{\mathbb{U}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)
. The existence of
{\mathbb{G}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)

\varphi_{3}(x):=\kappa^{2}(1-n(x)){\mathbb{U}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)
. Thus, the well-posedness of Problem (
1
) or (
1
) can be now reduced to the case of Problem (
3.2
) or (
3.2
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Problem (
1
) admits a unique solution.

Proof.

The uniqueness of (
3.2
) follows immediately from Theorem
3.1
. For the existence of the solution, we only consider the case of a sound-soft embedded obstacle
D
.
Other cases can be dealt with in a similar manner with a slight modification. In this case, one tries to seek a solution in the form of a combined acoustic double- and single-layer potential

\displaystyle w(x,x_{s})=\int_{\partial D}\left(\frac{\partial{\mathbb{G}}(x,y;\Gamma)}{\partial\nu(y)}-{\rm i}{\mathbb{G}}(x,y;\Gamma)\right)\psi(y)ds(y)\quad\rm{for}\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}

(3.38)

with some unknown density
\psi\in L^{p}(\partial{D})
for
p>1
.
Then from the boundary condition imposed on
\partial D
, we see that the potential (
3.38
) solve Problem (
3.2
), provided the density
\psi
is a solution of a second-kind integral equation

\displaystyle(I+K-{\rm i}S)\psi=-2{\mathbb{U}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)\quad{\rm on}\;\partial D,

where
S
and
K
are the single- and double-layer operators given by

\displaystyle(S\psi)(x):=2\int_{\partial D}{\mathbb{G}}(x,y;\Gamma)\psi(y)ds(y)\qquad\quad x\in\partial D

\displaystyle(K\psi)(x):=2\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial{\mathbb{G}}(x,y;\Gamma)}{\partial\nu(y)}\psi(y)ds(y)\qquad x\in\partial D,

respectively. Since
\partial D\in C^{2}
, we deduce by Lemma 1 in
[
32
]
that both
S
and
K
are bounded and compact operators in
L^{p}(\partial D)
. It is thus
concluded that
I+K-{\rm i}S:L^{p}(\partial{D})\rightarrow L^{p}(\partial{D})
is a Fredholm operator with index
0
. It then follows from the arguments for Theorem 3.11 in
[
11
]
, the existence of a density
\psi
can be established with the aid of the Riesz-Fredholm theory. Furthermore, we also have the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\leq C\|{\mathbb{U}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)\|_{L^{p}(\partial D)}.

(3.39)

The proof is thus complete.
∎

For Problem (
1
), we can also establish the following unique solvability.

Theorem 3.7
.

Problem (
1
) admits a unique solution.

Proof.

With the aid of Theorem
3.1
, we only need to show the existence of a solution of Problem (
1
). Note that
w(x,x_{s}):=u(x,x_{s})-{\mathbb{U}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma)
satisfies Problem (
3.2
).
By an application of Green’s theorems, it is derived that (
3.2
) is equivalent to the following Lippmann-Schwinger equation

\displaystyle w(x,x_{s})=-\kappa^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\mathbb{G}}(x,y;\Gamma)m(y)(w(y,x_{s})+{\mathbb{U}}(x,y;\Gamma))dy

where
m:=1-n
.

Recalling
w(x,x_{s})=u(x,x_{s})-{\mathbb{U}}(x,y;\Gamma)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
gives

\displaystyle u(x,x_{s})+\kappa^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\mathbb{G}}(x,y;\Gamma)m(y)u(y,x_{s})dy={\mathbb{U}}(x,x_{s};\Gamma).

(3.40)

By the similar arguments with Theorem
3.3
, we obtain that (
3.40
) is uniquely solvable and

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,x_{s})\|_{L^{p}(D)}\leq C\|{\mathbb{U}}(\cdot,x_{s};\Gamma)\|_{L^{p}(D)}.

∎

4
Uniqueness of the inverse problem

Based on the well-posedness of Problem (
1
) and (
1
) in Section 3, we investigate in this section the inverse problem of recovering both the locally rough surface
\Gamma
and the embedded obstacle
D
(or the refractive index
n
) with its surrounding homogeneous medium
\kappa_{2}
from the knowledge of scattered near-field data, associated with incident point sources
u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s})=\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
, taken only on a line segment denoted by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{b,a}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:\;|x_{1}|\leq a,\;x_{2}=b,\;b>\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\right\}.

Motivated by
[
36
]
, we establish two global uniqueness theorems for determining all unknowns in the inverse problems. Our method is based on two ingredients, one is constructing an interior transmission problem in a small domain, which is uniquely solvable when the domain is chosen to be small enough; the other is a priori estimate of solutions to the transmission problem with data in
L^{p}(1<p<2)
, where the priori estimate depends on the well-posedness of the direct scattering problem in the previous section.

For convenience, let
u_{j}^{s}(x,x_{s})
,
j=1,2
, denote the scattered field in
\Omega_{1}
and
u_{j}(x,x_{s})
denote the transmitted field in
\Omega_{2}
associated with Problem (
1
) (or (
1
)) with the complex scatterer

(\Gamma_{j},\kappa_{2,j},D_{j},\mathcal{B}_{j})
(or
(\Gamma_{j},\kappa_{2,j},n_{j})
).

4.1
The case of an impenetrable obstacle
D

In this subsection,
D
is considered to be an impenetrable obstacle with the physical property
\mathcal{B}
which is embedded into the lower-half space
\Omega_{2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
u_{1}^{s}(x,x_{s})=u_{2}^{s}(x,x_{s})
for all
x,x_{s}\in\Gamma_{b,a}
, then
(\Gamma_{1},\kappa_{2,1},D_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1})=(\Gamma_{2},\kappa_{2,2},D_{2},\mathcal{B}_{2})
.

Proof.

Our first goal is to establish a generalized reciprocity relation for the solutions of Problem (
1
) corresponding to two classes of different incident point sources. More precisely,
let
u(\cdot,x_{s}^{1})
and
u(\cdot,x_{s}^{2})
be the solutions to Problem (
1
) with the incident fields
u^{\rm inc}(\cdot,x_{s}^{1})
and
u^{\rm inc}(\cdot,x_{s}^{2})
, respectively, for
x_{s}^{1},x_{s}^{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(\overline{D}\cup\Gamma)
.
Define two functions
E_{\varepsilon}(z_{1})
and
F_{\varepsilon}(z_{2})
by

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{1}):=\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{1})}\left[\frac{\partial u^{\rm inc}(y,x_{s}^{1})}{\partial\nu(y)}u(y,x_{s}^{2})-\frac{\partial u(y,x_{s}^{2})}{\partial\nu(y)}u^{\rm inc}(y,x_{s}^{1})\right]{\rm d}s(y)

(4.1)

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{2}):=\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{2})}\left[\frac{\partial u^{\rm inc}(y,x_{s}^{2})}{\partial\nu(y)}u(y,x_{s}^{1})-\frac{\partial u(y,x_{s}^{1})}{\partial\nu(y)}u^{\rm inc}(y,x_{s}^{2})\right]{\rm d}s(y),

(4.2)

where
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{j})
,
j=1,2,
are two balls centered at
x_{s}^{j}
with radius
\varepsilon>0
which is small enough such that
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{1})\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{2})=\emptyset
.
A direct application of the Green’s theorem shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{1})=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}F_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{2})\qquad{\rm for}\;\;x_{s}^{1},x_{s}^{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(\overline{D}\cup\Gamma)

(4.3)

with
x_{s}^{1}\not=x_{s}^{2}
, if
u(\cdot,x_{s}^{j})\in L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{j}))
,
p\geq 1
.

Throughout, we consider two classes of incident wave
\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
and
\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
for
\ell=1,2
. To distinguish these two incident waves, from now on, we will always denote them by
u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s}):=\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
and
\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s},\ell):=\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s})
. Moreover, to distinguish corresponding total waves and scattered waves, we will always use
u(x,x_{s})
,
u^{s}(x,x_{s})
and
\widetilde{u}(x,x_{s},\ell)
,
\widetilde{u}^{s}(x,x_{s},\ell)
to denote the total fields and the scattered fields associated with (
1
) corresponding to the incident wave
u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s})
and
\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s},\ell)
, respectively.

In (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we choose the incident field
u^{\rm inc}
in the following form

\displaystyle u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s}^{1}):=\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s}^{1}),\qquad u^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s}^{2})=\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(x,x_{s}^{2},\ell):=\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,x_{s}^{2}).

Notice
\widetilde{u}(\cdot,x_{s}^{2},\ell)
is smooth in
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{1})
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
due to
x_{s}^{1}\not=x_{s}^{2}
. It is well-known by a standard argument that

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{1})=\widetilde{u}(x_{s}^{1},x_{s}^{2},\ell)
.
One then has by (
4.3
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(x_{s}^{1},x_{s}^{2},\ell)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x_{s}^{2})}\left[\frac{\partial\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(y,x_{s}^{2},\ell)}{\partial\nu(y)}u(y,x_{s}^{1})-\frac{\partial u(y,x_{s}^{1})}{\partial\nu(y)}\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(y,x_{s}^{2},\ell)\right]{\rm d}s(y),

(4.4)

which will play an important role in the proof of the uniqueness of the inverse problem.

Our second goal is to prove uniqueness of
\Gamma
.
Assuming on the contrary
\Gamma_{1}\neq\Gamma_{2}
, without loss of generality, there exists
z^{*}\in\Gamma_{1}\setminus\Gamma_{2}
. Furthermore, we can choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle z_{n}:=z^{*}+\frac{\delta_{0}}{n}\nu(z^{*})\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(z^{*})\qquad{\rm for}\;\;n=1,2,\cdots

(4.5)

and
B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(z^{*})\cap\Gamma_{2}=\emptyset
.

Let
\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)
,
\widetilde{u}_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)
denote the solutions to Problem (
1
) with
(\Gamma,\kappa_{2},D,\mathcal{B})
replaced by
(\Gamma_{1},k_{2,1},D_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
,
(\Gamma_{2},k_{2,2},D_{2},\mathcal{B}_{2})
, respectively.
Then we aim to show that

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{1}(y,z_{n},\ell)=\widetilde{u}_{2}(y,z_{n},\ell)\quad{\rm for}\quad y\in\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{1})\cap\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{2})\setminus\{z_{n}\}

(4.6)

holds for
\ell=1,2
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{j})
and
\Omega_{2}(\Gamma_{j})
for
j=1,2
denote the upper and lower half-spaces separated by
\Gamma_{j}
, respectively. In view of (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{m}(y,z_{n},\ell)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(z_{n})}\left[\frac{\partial\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(x,z_{n},\ell)}{\partial\nu(x)}u_{m}(x,y)-\frac{\partial u_{m}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)}\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(x,z_{n},\ell)\right]ds(x),

(4.7)

for
m=1,2
. For
y\in\Gamma_{b,a}
, it is apparent from

on
\Gamma_{b,a}
and the analyticity of the scattered field that

on
\Gamma_{b}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}=b\}
, which implies
u_{1}^{s}(\cdot,y)=u_{2}^{s}(\cdot,y)
on
\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{1})\cap\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{2})
due to the uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem and the analytic continuation principle. Hence,
u_{1}(\cdot,y)=u_{2}(\cdot,y)
on
\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{1})\cap\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{2})\setminus\{y\}
. Thus, for any
z_{n}\in\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{1})\cap\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{2})
, we can choose sufficient small
\varepsilon>0
such that
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(z_{n})\subset\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{1})\cap\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{2})
, so we have
u_{1}(\cdot,y)=u_{2}(\cdot,y)
on
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(z_{n})
which yields (
4.6
) holds for
y\in\Gamma_{b,a}
from (
4.7
). The analyticity, the uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem and the analytic continuation principle give that (
4.6
) holds for
y\in\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{1})\cap\Omega_{1}(\Gamma_{2})\setminus\{z_{n}\}.

Define
v_{1}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell):=\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)|_{D_{z^{*}}}
,
v_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell):=\widetilde{u}_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)|_{D_{z^{*}}}
where

which can be chosen as a Lipschitz domain with a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
According to the definitions, it is clear that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
solve the following interior transmission problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta v_{1}+\kappa_{2,1}^{2}v_{1}=0\qquad\textrm{in}\quad D_{z^{*}},\\
\Delta v_{2}+\kappa_{1}^{2}v_{2}=0\qquad\;\;\textrm{in}\quad D_{z^{*}},\\
v_{2}-v_{1}=f^{\ell}_{1}\qquad\qquad\textrm{on}\quad\partial D_{z^{*}},\\
\partial_{\nu}(v_{2}-v_{1})=f^{\ell}_{2}\qquad\;\textrm{on}\quad\partial D_{z^{*}},\end{array}\right.

with
f^{\ell}_{1}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D_{z^{*}})
and
f^{\ell}_{2}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D_{z^{*}})
.
Furthermore, it follows from (
4.6
) that
f^{\ell}_{1}=0
and
f^{\ell}_{2}=0
on
B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(z^{*})\cap\Gamma_{1}
.
With the aid of this, we conclude from the well-posedness (cf.
[
36
]
) of Problem (
4.1
) for sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}
with the a priori estimates (
3.24
),(
3.30
), (
3.39
) in

for Problem (
1
) that the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})v_{1}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)\|_{L^{2}(D_{z^{*}})}+\|\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})v_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)\|_{L^{2}(D_{z^{*}})}\leq C

(4.13)

holds for uniformly
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\nu(z^{*}):=(\nu_{1}(z^{*}),\nu_{2}(z^{*}))
denotes the upward unit normal at

. By linearity, it is clear that
\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})v_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)
is the solution to Problem (
1
) with
\Gamma=\Gamma_{2}
associated with the incident wave
\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(x,z_{n},\ell)
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})v_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)\|_{L^{2}(D_{z^{*}})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)\|_{L^{2}(D_{z^{*}})}

\displaystyle-\|\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})\left[v_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)-\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)\right]\|_{L^{2}(D_{z^{*}})},

by the triangle inequality. A direct calculation leads to
\|\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D_{z^{*}})}=O(n)
for sufficiently large
n
. Since there is a positive distance between
z^{*}
and
\Gamma_{2}
, it can be obtained by the well-posedness of Problem (
1
) with
\Gamma=\Gamma_{2}
that the scattered field
v_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)-\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)
is uniformly bounded for all

. Hence

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\ell=1,2}\nu_{\ell}(z^{*})v_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)\|_{L^{2}(D_{z^{*}})}\rightarrow+\infty\quad{\rm as}\;\;n\to\infty

which contradicts with (
4.13
). So,
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}
.

Now we are in position to show
\kappa_{2,1}=\kappa_{2,2}
. Let
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}
and assume
\kappa_{2,1}\neq\kappa_{2,2}
. We choose
z^{*}\in\Gamma
and define
z_{n}
as (
4.5
). Recalling that
\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)
,
\widetilde{u}_{2}(\cdot,z_{n},\ell)
denote the solutions of the scattering problems (
1
) with
(\Gamma,\kappa_{2},D,\mathcal{B})
replaced by
(\Gamma,\kappa_{2,1},D_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
,
(\Gamma,\kappa_{2,2},D_{2},\mathcal{B}_{2})
, respectively, with the point sources
\widetilde{u}^{\rm inc}(x,z_{n},\ell)=\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\kappa_{1}}(x,z_{n})
. By the same analysis as (
4.6
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,z_{n},\ell)=\widetilde{u}_{2}(x,z_{n},\ell)\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega_{1}.

(4.14)

For
z^{*}\in\Gamma
, we choose a Lipschitz domain
D^{\prime}_{z^{*}}:=B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(z^{*})\cap\Omega_{2}
and define
\widetilde{v}_{1}(x,z_{n},\ell):=\widetilde{u}_{1}(x,z_{n},\ell)|_{D^{\prime}_{z^{*}}}
and
\widetilde{v}_{2}(x,z_{n},\ell):=\widetilde{u}_{2}(x,z_{n},\ell)|_{D^{\prime}_{z^{*}}}
. Then
\widetilde{v}_{1}
and
\widetilde{v}_{2}
satisfy the following interior transmission problem in a different domain

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta\widetilde{v}_{1}+\kappa_{2,1}^{2}\widetilde{v}_{1}=0\qquad\textrm{in}\quad D^{\prime}_{z^{*}},\\

\partial_{\nu}(\widetilde{v}_{2}-\widetilde{v}_{1})=g^{\ell}_{2}\qquad\;\textrm{on}\quad\partial D^{\prime}_{z^{*}},\end{array}\right.

with

and

. From (
4.14
), we deduce that
g^{\ell}_{1}=0
and
g^{\ell}_{2}=0
on
B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(z^{*})\cap\Gamma
. The remaining part of this proof is completely analogous to the proof of uniqueness for

, so we omit it here.

Due to
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}
and

, the inverse problem is reduced to the case of determining the embedded obstacle from measurements in a known two-layered medium. It follows from the standard discussions (cf.

and

. The proof is completed.
∎

In this subsection, we investigate the case where

Theorem 4.2
.

It follows from the same arguments as Theorem

that

and

.
Thus, the inverse problem is reduced to recover the inhomogeneous medium into a known two-layered medium and the uniqueness

.

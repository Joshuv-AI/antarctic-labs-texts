Frobenius pull-back of parabolic bundles and dormant opers
Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. We study parabolic bundles on an algebraic curve in positive characteristic. Our motivation is to properly formulate Frobenius pull-backs of parabolic bundles in a way that extends various previous facts and arguments for the usual non-parabolic Frobenius pull-backs. After defining that operation, we generalize a classical result by Cartier concerning Frobenius descent, that is, we establish a bijective correspondence (including the version using higher-level 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules) between parabolic flat bundles with vanishing p p -curvature on a pointed curve and parabolic bundles on its Frobenius twist. This correspondence gives a description of maximally Frobenius-destabilized parabolic bundles in terms of dormant opers admitting logarithmic poles. As an application of that description together with a previous result in the enumerative geometry of dormant opers, we obtain an explicit formula for computing the number of such parabolic bundles of rank 2 2 under certain assumptions.

.

3.2.
Monodromy operators

We shall set

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{S}:=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{O}_{S}\cdot\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle j\rangle},

(3.4)

where
\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle i\rangle}
’s are abstract symbols (cf.
[
Wak4
, § 4.2]
).
Here, for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let

denote a unique nonnegative integer satisfying

and

.
Then, we equip
\mathcal{B}_{S}
with a structure of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra given by

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}\cdot\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle j^{\prime\prime}\rangle}:=\sum_{j=\mathrm{max}\{j^{\prime},j^{\prime\prime}\}}^{j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}}\frac{j!}{(j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}-j)!\cdot(j-j^{\prime})!\cdot(j-j^{\prime\prime})!}\cdot\frac{q_{j^{\prime}}!\cdot q_{j^{\prime\prime}}!}{q_{j}!}\cdot\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle j\rangle}.

(3.5)

The
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{S}
is commutative and generated by the global sections
\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle 1\rangle},\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle p\rangle},\cdots,\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle p^{N-1}\rangle}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module and
\mu
a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras

\mathcal{B}_{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\mathcal{G})
.
For each

,
we set

, which is an element of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\mathcal{G})
.
The morphism
\mu
is uniquely determined by
the
N
-tuple

\displaystyle\mu^{\langle\bullet\rangle}:=(\mu^{\langle 1\rangle},\mu^{\langle p\rangle}\cdots,\mu^{\langle p^{N-1}\rangle})\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\mathcal{G})^{\oplus N}.

(3.6)

Next, let
(\mathcal{F},\nabla)
be a
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module, and choose
i\in\{1,\cdots,r\}
.
Note that there exists a canonical isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras

\sigma_{i}^{*}({{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{B}_{S}
such that
the local basis

\{\partial^{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
of
{{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
associated to
any logarithmic coordinate around a point in
\mathrm{Im}(\sigma_{i})

is mapped to
\{\partial_{\mathcal{B}}^{\langle j\rangle}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
(cf.
[
Wak4
, Lemma 2.2.2 and § 6.1]
).
The
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure
\nabla
induces an
\sigma_{i}^{*}({{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)})
-action
\sigma_{i}^{*}\nabla
on
\sigma_{i}^{*}\mathcal{F}
; it gives
the composite

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(\nabla):\mathcal{B}_{S}\xrightarrow{\sim}\sigma_{i}^{*}({{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)})\xrightarrow{\sigma_{i}^{*}\nabla}\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\sigma_{i}^{*}\mathcal{F}),

(3.7)

and the pair

specifies a
\mathcal{B}_{S}
-module.
In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(\nabla)^{\langle\bullet\rangle}:=(\mu_{i}(\nabla)^{\langle 1\rangle},\mu_{i}(\nabla)^{\langle p\rangle}\cdots,\mu_{i}(\nabla)^{\langle p^{N-1}\rangle})\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\sigma_{i}^{*}\mathcal{F})^{\oplus N}.

(3.8)

Since
\mathcal{B}_{S}
is commutative,
the elements

We refer to
\mu_{i}(\nabla)
and

of

at
\sigma_{i}
.
In the case where

4.4.

Let

In the non-parabolic case, it is immediate that the operation of takings Frobenius pull-backs is transitive, which means

For each integer

an
r

5.3.
Harder-Narasimhan polygons

We shall consider a characterization of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}^{(N)}
-opers by using their Harder-Narasimhan polygons; our result is a higher-level and parabolic generalization of
[
JoPa
, Theorem 1.1.2]
.
In this subsection,
we suppose that
S=\mathrm{Spec}(k)
, i.e.,
\mathscr{X}
is an
r
-pointed smooth proper curve of genus
g
over
k
.

Recall that
each vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
X
admits the Harder-Narasimhan filtration

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{F}^{m}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{m-1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{1}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{F}.

(5.20)

That is to say,
\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1}
are all semistable, and the slopes (in the usual sense) of these vector bundles satisfy

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{max}}(\mathcal{F}):=\mu(\mathcal{F}^{m-1}/\mathcal{F}^{m})>\mu(\mathcal{F}^{m-2}/\mathcal{F}^{m-1})>\cdots>\mu(\mathcal{F}^{0}/\mathcal{F}^{1})=:\mu_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathcal{F}).

(5.21)

This filtration associates a polygon in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose vertices
are given by the points
(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F}^{j}),\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j}))
(
0\leq j\leq m
).
We denote this polygon by

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{F}}.

(5.22)

The segment of
P_{\mathcal{F}}
joining
(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F}^{j}),\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j}))
and
(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F}^{j-1}),\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j-1}))
has slope
\mu(\mathcal{F}^{j-1}/\mathcal{F}^{j})
.

Let us consider
the set
B
of convex polygons in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
starting at
(0,0)
and ending at

(n,na-\frac{n(n-1)(2g-2+r)}{2})
; this set
is partially ordered:
if
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
are two such polygons, we say that
P_{1}\succcurlyeq P_{2}

if
P_{1}
lies on or above
P_{2}
.
Also, this set contains a specific polygon
with
vertices
q_{j}:=\left(j,ja-\frac{j(j-1)(2g-2+r)}{2}\right)
for
0\leq j\leq n
; we denote it by

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{oper}}^{a}.

(5.23)

As asserted in the proposition below, this convex polygon characterizes
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-modules admitting a structure of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}^{(N)}
-oper.

Proposition 5.6

(cf.
[
JoPa
]
, Theorem 1.1.2)
.

Let
(m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
be an
r
-tuple of positive integers and

\vec{a}:=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r})
an element of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Xi_{m_{i},N}^{\leq}

such that
s_{1}^{1}(\vec{a})\in\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Xi_{m_{i},1}^{\leq}
and
s_{2}^{1}(\vec{a})\in\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Xi_{m_{i},N-1}^{\leq}
(cf. (
4.25
)).
Also, let
n
be a positive integer and
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\vec{\mathfrak{e}},\vec{a}/p^{N})
a rank-
n
parabolic bundle on
\mathscr{X}^{(N)}
of parabolic weights
\vec{a}/p^{N}
whose parabolic Frobenius pull-back by
F^{(N-1)}_{X/S}
is semistable.
Denote by
\mathscr{E}^{F}:=(\mathcal{E}^{F},\nabla^{F},\vec{\mathfrak{e}}^{F},\vec{a})
the parabolic Frobenius pull-back of
\mathscr{E}
by
F^{(N)}_{X/S}
and by

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{F}^{m}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{m-1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{F}

(5.24)

the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{E}^{F}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
has degree
na-\frac{n(n-1)(2g-2+r)}{2}
for some integer

.
Then, the following assertions hold.

(i)

We have
P_{\mathrm{oper}}^{a}\succcurlyeq P_{\mathcal{F}}
.

(ii)

The equality
P_{\mathrm{oper}}^{a}=P_{\mathcal{F}}
holds if and only if the triple
(\mathcal{F},\nabla^{F},\{\mathcal{F}^{j}\}_{j})
forms a (dormant)
\mathrm{GL}_{n}^{(N)}
-oper.
(If these conditions are fulfilled, then
n=m
and the equality
\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{n-1})=a

Proof.

First, we shall prove assertion (i).
Suppose that there exists a vertex of
P_{\mathcal{F}}
that lies above
P_{\mathrm{oper}}^{a}
.
The segment
S_{j}
joining the vertices
q_{j-1}
and
q_{j}
has slope
s_{j}:=a-(j-1)(2g-2+r)
.
Since
s_{j}-s_{j+1}=2g-2+r
,
it follows from an elementary argument about slopes of convex polygons that
there exists
j\in\{1,\cdots,m-1\}
satisfying
\mu(\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1})-\mu(\mathcal{F}^{j-1}/\mathcal{F}^{j})>2g-2+r
.
This contradicts the result of Lemma
5.7
proved below.
Thus, we have
P_{\mathrm{oper}}^{a}\succcurlyeq P_{\mathcal{F}}
, as desired.

Next, we shall prove assertion (ii).
Since the “if” part of the required equivalence is clear, we only consider
the “only if” part.
Suppose that
P_{\mathrm{oper}}^{a}=P_{\mathcal{F}}
(hence
m=n
).
As an intermediate step,
we shall prove, by descending induction on
j
, the claim that
\nabla^{F}_{0}(\mathcal{F}^{j})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{j-1}
(
j=1,\cdots,n
), where
\nabla^{F}_{0}
denotes the log connection on
\mathcal{F}
induced from
\nabla^{F}
.

The base step, i.e., the case of
j=n
, is clear.
To consider the induction step, we suppose that the claim with
j
replaced by
j+1
has been proved.
The composite

\displaystyle\alpha:\mathcal{F}^{j}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inclusion}}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\nabla^{F}_{0}}\Omega\otimes\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{quotient}}\Omega\otimes(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{j})

(5.25)

is verified to be
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear.
By the induction assumption,
\alpha
induces, via the quotient
\mathcal{F}^{j}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1}
, an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism

\overline{\alpha}:\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1}\rightarrow\Omega\otimes(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{j})
.
Since
\alpha
(hence also
\overline{\alpha}
) is nonzero by Lemma
5.7
,

\mathrm{Im}(\overline{\alpha})
turns out to be a rank one vector bundle.
Thus,
there exists at least one integer
s<j
such that the image of
\mathrm{Im}(\overline{\alpha})
via the natural quotient
\Omega\otimes(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{j})\twoheadrightarrow\Omega\otimes(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{s+1})

let
s_{\mathrm{min}}
be the minimum element among such
s
’s.
By definition,
\overline{\alpha}
induces an injection
\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1}\hookrightarrow\Omega\otimes(\mathcal{F}^{s_{\mathrm{min}}}/\mathcal{F}^{s_{\mathrm{min}}+1})
.
But,
the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \mathrm{deg}(\Omega\otimes(\mathcal{F}^{s_{\mathrm{min}}}/\mathcal{F}^{s_{\mathrm{min}}+1}))-\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1})

(5.26)

\displaystyle=\left((2g-2+r)+a-(n-s_{\mathrm{min}}-1)(2g-2+r)\right)-\left(a-(n-j-1)(2g-2+r)\right)

\displaystyle=(2g-2+r)(s_{\mathrm{min}}-j+1).

(5.27)

It follows that the equality

must be fulfilled, which means

\nabla^{F}_{0}(\mathcal{F}^{j})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{j-1}
.
This completes the proof of the induction step, and hence completes the proof of the claim.

By comparing the respective degrees, we see that
the injection
\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1}\xrightarrow{}\Omega\otimes(\mathcal{F}^{j-1}/\mathcal{F}^{j})
induced by
\nabla^{F}_{0}
is bijective.
Therefore, the morphism (

) for

turns out to be an isomorphism.
That is to say, it forms a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}^{(N)}
-oper.
This completes the proof of the “only if” part.

is as in the proof of the above proposition,

is verified to be
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}^{j}
is closed under
\nabla^{F}_{0}
, i.e., (

) becomes the zero map.
It follows from Proposition
4.6
that
the parabolic
p
-flat subbundle

determined by

corresponds to a parabolic subbundle
\mathscr{G}
of
\mathscr{E}
.
By (
4.22
) and the definition of parabolic slope, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{E})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\cdot\left(\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{E}^{F})\right)

(5.31)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\cdot\left(\mu(\mathcal{F})-\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m_{i}}a_{i}^{[j^{\prime}]}\ell_{i}^{[j^{\prime}]}\right)\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{p}\cdot\left(\mu(\mathcal{F}^{j})-\frac{1}{n}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m_{i}}a_{i}^{[j^{\prime}]}\ell_{i,\mathcal{F}^{j}}^{[j^{\prime}]}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\cdot\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{E}^{F,j})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{G})

where the “

is the Harder-Narasimhan filtration (which implies

Moreover, this fact means that (

(5.32)

We suppose that

\mathscr{X}
is an
r

6.1.
Maximally

-destabilized bundles

Let

\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\vec{\mathfrak{e}},\vec{a}/p^{N})
(where
\vec{\mathfrak{e}}:=(\mathfrak{e}_{1},\cdots\mathfrak{e}_{r})

\mathfrak{e}_{i}
at

coincides with

For convenience, we write

Definition 6.1
.

We shall say that

is

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{F}^{n}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{n-1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{1}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\left(\mu(\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1})-\mu(\mathcal{F}^{j-1}/\mathcal{F}^{j})=\right)\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1})-\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j-1}/\mathcal{F}^{j})=2g-2+r

We shall refer to such a filtration as a

of

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be as above,
and let us consider
the following three conditions:

(a)

\mathscr{E}
is maximally
F^{(N)}
-destabilized;

(b)

s_{1}^{1}(\vec{a})\in(\Xi_{n,1}^{\leq})^{\times r}
and
s_{2}^{1}(\vec{a})\in(\Xi_{n,N-1}^{\leq})^{\times r}

(c)

The inequalities

hold.

Then, the following assertions hold.

(i)

If (a) is satisfied, then
a destabilizing filtration of
\mathscr{E}
is uniquely determined.
(Thus, it makes sense to speak of “the” destabilizing filtration of
\mathscr{E}
.)

(ii)

If
n\mid\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{E})
and all the conditions (a)-(c) are satisfied, then

\mathscr{E}
is stable.

Proof.

Assertion (i) can be proved by a routine argument using
2g-2+r>0
(so we omit the detail of the proof).

In the following, we prove assertion (ii).
Suppose that there exists a proper parabolic subbundle
\mathscr{G}:=(\mathcal{G},\vec{\mathfrak{g}},\vec{a}/p^{N})
of
\mathscr{E}
satisfying
\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{G})\geq\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{E})
.
By Proposition
4.6
,
the parabolic Frobenius pull-back

specifies a parabolic
p^{N}
-flat subbundle of
\mathscr{E}^{F}

Denote by
m\left(<n\right)
the rank of
\mathcal{G}^{F}
, and
define a decreasing filtration
\{\mathcal{G}^{F,j}\}_{j=0}^{n}
on
\mathcal{G}^{F}
by putting
\mathcal{G}^{F,j}:=\iota^{-1}(\mathcal{F}^{j})
, where

\{\mathcal{F}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{n}
denotes the destabilized filtration of
\mathscr{E}
and

\mathcal{G}^{F}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}\left(=\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{E}^{F}\right)

of

By Lemma
6.3

an argument similar to the proof of Proposition
5.6
(by using the assumption (b)),
we see
that the log connection on

induced from

It follows that

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{G}^{F})\leq\mu(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{m}).

(6.3)

On the other hand, if we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{E})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{N}\cdot n}\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F})

(6.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{N}\cdot n}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{N}\cdot n}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(c-\frac{(n-j-1)(2g-2+r)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{N}}\cdot\left(c-\frac{(n-1)(2g-2+r)}{2}\right),

-destabilized.

\displaystyle\mathrm{par}\text{-}\mu(\mathscr{G})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{N}}\cdot\mu(\mathcal{G}^{F})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{N}\cdot m}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}^{j}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{N}\cdot m}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(c-\frac{(n-j-1)(2g-2+r)}{2}\right)

Model-independent measurement of the Hubble Constant and the absolute magnitude of Type Ia Supernovae
and Tong-Jie Zhang

Abstract
In this work, we propose a cosmological model-independent and non-local method to constrain the Hubble Constant H 0 H_{0} . Inspired by the quasi cosmological model-independent and H 0 H_{0} -free properties of the ‘shifted’ Hubble diagram of HII galaxies (HIIGx) defined by Wei et al. (2016), we joint analyze it with the parametric type Ia supernova (SN Ia) Hubble diagram (e.g. the joint-lightcurves-analysis sample, JLA) and get a Bayesian Inference of Hubble constant, H 0 = 71 ± 20 ​ km ​ s − 1 ​ Mpc − 1 H_{0}=71\pm 20\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}} . Although with large uncertainty, we find that H 0 H_{0} is only strongly degenerate with the B-band absolute magnitude ( M B M_{B} ) of SN Ia but almost independent on other nuisance parameters. Therefore the accuracy can be simultaneously improved by a tight constraint of M B M_{B} through a cosmological and H 0 H_{0} independent way. This method can be extended further to get more-literally non-local results of H 0 H_{0} by using other Hubble diagrams at higher redshifts.



1
Introduction

The value of the Hubble constant
H_{0}
, which is defined as the current expansion rate of the universe, has not yet reached consensus in this era of the precision cosmology. The two major measurement methods are cosmic distance ladders (the direct measurements) in the local universe (over which the effects of cosmic evolution are small) and the cosmic microwave background (CMB) inference in the epoch of the recombination. The former provides the cosmological model-independent and local measurements of the
H_{0}
, while the latter is in the opposite. The principle of the distance ladders measurements are based on the Hubble’s law
v=H_{0}D
, which tells us that the receding speeds
v
of galaxies are proportional to their distances
D
from us, and the coefficient
H_{0}
is constant. To measure the
H_{0}
more accurately, we need to improve the precisions of both receding speeds and distances. Different from this approach, the CMB inference method measures the imprints of the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) on the anisotropic power spectrum of CMB and combines with the assumption that our universe evolves as a Flat-
\Lambda
CDM model to constrain the
H_{0}
.

Recently, collaborators in the SH0ES team used 70 long-period Cepheids in the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) as the standard candle and combined Type Ia Supernovae (SN Ia) as secondary distance indicator to finally make the most precise local result as
H_{0}=74.03\pm 1.42{\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}}

[
1
]
. But the latest observational CMB result from the Plank collaboration (
H_{0}=67.36\pm 0.54{\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}}

[
2
]
) is in
4.4\sigma
difference with the latest SH0ES result. Such difference between the early and late universe measurements of
H_{0}
is an urgent crisis named the Hubble-Tension in modern cosmology.

To deal with this tension, we need to both estimate all systematic effects which might be included in these measurements and try some new physics that beyond the
\Lambda
CDM framework. Besides, new independent meaurements of the
H_{0}
are particularly important to verify this tension. Collaborators in the H0LiCOW (
H_{0}
Lenses in COSMOGRAIL’s Wellspring) team apply a strong-lensing cosmography and get their latest result as
H_{0}=73.3^{+1.7}_{-1.8}{\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}}

[
3
]
. This method is fully independent of all rungs of the distance ladders, and the result is in agreement with the latest SH0ES result, but is in
3.1\sigma
tension with the latest Planck result. In combination of the latest SH0ES result, this tension is intensified up to
5.3\sigma
. However, a cosmological model-independent non-local measurement of the
H_{0}
is even more important in determining whether we need to go beyond the standard
\Lambda
CDM framework or not.

In this paper, we propose such a Bayesian approach which conjointly analyzes the Hubble-free quasi cosmology-independent shifted HIIGx Hubble diagram with the JLA
[
4
]
SN Ia Hubble diagram to constrain
H_{0}
. In section
2
, we illuminate the methodology which makes our intents workable. In section
3
, we describe the data used in this paper and present our results. In section
4
, we draw our conclusions and make some discussions.

2
Methodology

The Hubble diagrams, which show the correlation between distances (luminosity distances
D_{L}
or angular diameter distances
D_{A}
) and redshifts, are used to depict the evolutionary history of the universe. Mathematically, the
D_{L}
as a function of redshift can be expanded into Taylor series as

D_{L}(z)=\frac{cz}{H_{0}}\left[\mathcal{D}_{L}^{0}+\mathcal{D}_{L}^{1}z+\mathcal{D}_{L}^{2}z^{2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{3})\right]\;,

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L}^{0}=1

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L}^{1}=-\frac{1}{2}(1-q_{0})

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L}^{2}=-\frac{1}{6}(1-q_{0}-3q_{0}^{2}+j_{0})\;.

The
q_{0}
and
j_{0}
in above equation are the deceleration parameter the jerk parameter respectively, which are the dimensionless second and third derivative of the scale factor
a(t)
with respect to cosmic time
t_{0}
. The first-order approximation at low redshifts of the formula (
2.1
) is the famous Hubble’s law. Theoretically, the
D_{L}(z)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{D_{L}(z)}{1+z}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{c}{H_{0}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\Omega_{k}}}}\sinh\left[\sqrt{{\Omega_{k}}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})}\right]\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{for}\;{\Omega_{k}}>0\\
\frac{c}{H_{0}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})}\ \hskip 9.24994pt&\text{for}\;{\Omega_{k}}=0\\
\displaystyle\frac{c}{H_{0}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|{\Omega_{k}}|}}\sin\left[\sqrt{|{\Omega_{k}}|}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})}\right]\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{for}\;{\Omega_{k}}<0\\
\end{cases}\;,

(2.3)

where the dimensionless Hubble parameter
E(z)
is model-dependent, i.e. in
\Lambda
CDM model

\displaystyle E(z)=\sqrt{\Omega_{\Lambda}+\Omega_{k}(1+z)^{2}+\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}}\;.

(2.4)

The direct measurements of
H_{0}
by Hubble’s Law are intrinsically local, while all the constraints of
H_{0}
by Hubble diagrams applying Equation (
2.3
) are inevitably model-dependent. Currently, we could only conclude that the Hubble tension is existed between the local direct and non-local model-dependent measurements. To develop a cosmological model-independent non-local approach which can be applied to measure
H_{0}
is quite promising. Fortunately, the ‘shifted’ Hubble-free HIIGx Hubble diagram makes achieving this aspiration becoming pragmatical.

2.1
SN Ia Hubble diagram

SN Ia are widely used as secondary standard candles to measure luminosity distances
D_{L}
because the peak luminosities of light curves of all SN Ia are nearly identical. Finding a SN Ia which shares the same host galaxy with a Cepheid variable, one can measure the
D_{L}
of the host galaxy through this Cepheid variable. Principally, combining peak magnitude
m_{\max}
of the with the formula for distance modulus,

\displaystyle\mu=5\lg\frac{D_{L}}{\mathrm{Mpc}}+25=m_{\max}-M_{\max},

(2.5)

the peak absolute magnitude
M_{\max}
of all SN Ia can be obtained. Therefore, the
D_{L}
of an arbitrary SN Ia can be easily obtained by measuring its
m_{max}
.

However, the
M_{\max}
of SN Ia are not exactly the same but also related to the shapes can colors of the light curves. Considering this, the formula for SN Ia distance modulus should be modified by adding perturbations of shapes
x
and color
c
as
[
5
]

\displaystyle\mu_{B}(\alpha,\beta,M_{B})=m_{B}^{\rm max}-M_{B}+\alpha x-\beta c,

(2.6)

where the subscript
B
stands for B band, while
\alpha
and
\beta
are nuisance parameters for modification. It is well known that
M_{B}
is degenerate with
H_{0}
, and inevitably, its value and also the SN Ia Hubble diagram are commonly based on an assumed value of
H_{0}=70\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}}
. This makes it illogical to constrain
H_{0}
using the SN Ia Hubble diagram which consists of higher redshifts (non-local) observations. But if there exists a method which can be used to calibrate the SN Ia Hubble diagram in a cosmological model-independent way, the two interested parameters will be constrained simultaneously.

2.2
‘shifted’ Hubble-free HIIGx Hubble diagram

The luminosity of Balmer
\mathrm{H}\beta
lines that emitted in HIIGx is strongly correlated with the ionized gas velocity dispersion
\sigma_{v}

[
6
]
, because both the intensity of ionizing radiation and
\sigma_{v}
increase with the starbust mass
[
7
]
. This correlation can be approximated as
[
8
]

\displaystyle\lg\left[\frac{L(\mathrm{H}\beta)}{\mathrm{erg}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}}\right]=\alpha\,\lg\left[\frac{\sigma_{v}(\mathrm{H}\beta)}{\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}}\right]+\kappa\ ,

(2.7)

where
\alpha
and
\kappa
are constants. The relatively small scatter in the relationship between
L(\mathrm{H}\beta)
and
\sigma_{v}
allows these galaxies and local HII regions to be used as standard candles
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

With the selecting criteria that guarantee the selected HIIGx are comprised of systems in which the luminosity is dominated by single and very young starbursts (less than
5\,\mathrm{Myr}
in age)
[
9
]
, the bolometric flux of the HIIGx may thereby be regarded as constituting principally the
\mathrm{H}\beta
line. Therefore the luminosity distance of an HIIGx can be aprroximated with the luminosity
L(\mathrm{H}\beta)
and
flux
F(\mathrm{H}\beta)
pertaining to the
\mathrm{H}\beta
line,

D_{L}=\left[\frac{L(\mathrm{H}\beta)}{4\pi F(\mathrm{H}\beta)}\right]^{1/2}\;,

(2.8)

where
F(\mathrm{H}\beta)
is the reddening corrected
\mathrm{H}\beta
flux.

Wei et al.
[
10
]
defined the ‘shifted’ distance modulus as

\displaystyle\eta\equiv 5\lg\left[\frac{H_{0}D_{L}}{\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}}\right]\;,

(2.9)

which is shifted by a constant difference from the conventional distance modulus as the relation

\displaystyle\mu=\eta-5\lg\left[\frac{H_{0}}{\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}}\right]+25\ .

(2.10)

Combining with the “
H_{0}
-free” logarithmic luminosity defined as

\displaystyle\delta\equiv-2.5\kappa-5\lg\left[\frac{H_{0}}{\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}}\right]+125.2\;,

(2.11)

thus the
\eta
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\eta=-\delta+2.5\left[\alpha_{0}\lg\sigma(\mathrm{H}\beta)-\lg F(\mathrm{H}\beta)\right]\ .

(2.12)

Authors in Ref.
[
10
,
13
,
14
]
adopt the maximum likelihood estimation to constrain the two ‘nuisance’ parameters
\alpha_{0}
and
\delta
within three cosmological models (
Rh=ct
,
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
and
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
). They have found that the two parameters are very insensitive to these cosmological models (see results in the Table 2 of Ref.
[
10
]
). This critical property allows us to use the HIIGx Hubble diagram in a quasi cosmological model-independent way (maybe some exotic models are excluded
[
15
]
). Here we adopt the average value of these parameters as a reasonable representation, i.e.,
\alpha_{0}=4.87^{+0.11}_{-0.08}
and

\delta=32.42^{+0.42}_{-0.33}
. Then given the flux and gas velocity dispersion (along with their uncertainties) of HIIGx and GEHR, we can get the observed ‘shifted’ distance modulus
\eta_{\mathrm{obs}}
using Equation (
2.12
).

2.3
Juxtapositon of Hubble diagrams

Known that the shifted amount of HIIGx Hubble diagram is
H_{0}
-only related, an optimized value of
H_{0}
can be extracted in a cosmological model-independent fashion by jointly fitting the HIIGx and SN Ia Hubble diagrams with these nuisance parameters (
M_{B}
,
\alpha
and
\beta
) and
H_{0}
. We name this analysis procedure as juxtaposition of Hubble diagrams. In this article, we perform the Bayesian statistical methods and the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) technique to calculate their joint posterior probability density function (PDF).

We use a
\chi^{2}
minimization to constrain
H_{0}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\chi^{2}(H_{0},M_{B},\alpha,\beta)\\
&=\sum\frac{[\mu_{\mathrm{HII}}(H_{0};z_{i})-\mu_{B}(\alpha,\beta,M_{B};z_{i})]^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\mu_{\rm HII}}(z_{i})+\sigma^{2}_{\mu_{B}}(z_{i})},\end{aligned}

(2.13)

where
\mu_{\mathrm{HII}}(H_{0};z_{i})
of HIIGx comes from Equation (
2.10
) and (
2.12
),
\mu_{B}(\alpha,\beta,M_{B};z_{i})
of the SN Ia sample is given by Equation (
2.6
), and the total uncertainty comes from the uncertainty of
\mu_{\rm HII}
and
\mu_{B}
. The error propagation of
\sigma_{\mu_{\mathrm{HII}}}(z_{i})
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sigma^{2}_{\mu_{\mathrm{HII}}}(z_{i})=\sigma_{\delta}^{2}+2.5^{2}[\alpha_{0}^{2}\sigma^{2}_{\lg\sigma}+\sigma_{\alpha_{0}}^{2}\lg^{2}\sigma+\sigma^{2}_{\lg F}],\end{aligned}

(2.14)

where
\sigma_{\alpha_{0}}
,
\sigma_{\delta}
,
\sigma_{\lg\sigma}
and
\sigma_{\lg F}
are the errors of
\alpha_{0}
,
\delta
,
\lg\sigma(\rm H\beta)
and
\lg F(\rm H\beta)
respectively. Meanwhile, the error propagation of
\sigma_{\mu_{B}}(z_{i})
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sigma^{2}_{\mu_{B}^{\max}}(z_{i})&=\sigma_{m}^{2}(z_{i})+\alpha^{2}\sigma_{x}^{2}(z_{i})+\beta^{2}\sigma_{c}^{2}(z_{i})+2\alpha\beta\Cov^{2}(x,c)\\
&+2\alpha\Cov^{2}(m_{B}^{\max},x)+2\beta\Cov^{2}(m_{B}^{\max},c),\end{aligned}

(2.15)

where
\sigma_{m}
,
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{c}
are the errors of
m_{B}^{\max}
,
x
and
c
respectively, while
\Cov(m_{B}^{\max},x)
,
\Cov(m_{B}^{\max},c)

\Cov(x,c)
are the covariances of them.

In principle, the
\chi^{2}
can only be calculated for those
\mu_{\rm HII}
and
\mu_{B}
that share the same redshift. However, this condition usually cannot be satisfied for the two different targeted observations. So we need to reconstruct one of the Hubble diagrams in a model-independent way. In this article, we choose to reconstruct the "shifted" HIIGx Hubble diagram by using the Gaussian Process (GP), which is a machine learning algorithm without any cosmological nor astrophysical assumptions, therefore are widely used in recent researches. Once we get the GP reconstructed data points
\{\eta,\sigma_{\eta}\}
from the observed HIIGx data, we can combine the observational SN Ia light curve data
\{m_{B}^{\max},x,c,\sigma_{m},\sigma_{x},\sigma_{c},\Cov(m_{B}^{\max},x),\Cov(m_{B}^{\max},c),\Cov(x,c)\}
to calculate the
\chi^{2}
that is defined in Eq. (
2.13
). Then, the joint PDF of these parameters can be obtained,
P(H_{0},M_{B},\alpha,\beta)=A\exp(-\chi^{2}/2)
, where
A
is a nomarlized coefficient, which makes the total probability
\iiiint P\mathrm{d}H_{0}\mathrm{d}M_{B}\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta=1
. Finally, by integrating over (
M_{B}
,
\alpha
and
\beta
) the PDF of
H_{0}
is obtained, i.e.,
P(H_{0})=\iiint P\mathrm{d}M_{B}\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta
.

4
Conclusion and Discussion

With the joint analysis of HIIGx Hubble diagram and JLA SN Ia Hubble diagram, we prove that our juxtaposition of the Hubble diagrams method to constrain
H_{0}
is feasible. In our proof-of-principle example, the value of
H_{0}
is strongly degenerate with the B-band absolute magnitude of SN Ia
M_{B}
but almost independent with the other nuisance parameters. Although our result gives a large uncertainty in
H_{0}
constraint, the accuracy can be simultaneously improved by a tight constraint of
M_{B}
through a cosmological and
H_{0}
independent way. The self-calibrated distance of GW sources can be treated as a standard siren to independently construct the Hubble diagram, therefore, to circumvent the
H_{0}-M_{B}
degeneracy and measure the
H_{0}
directly. The third generation GW detection can also model-independently measure
M_{B}
with uncertainty more than one order of magnitude smaller than that from the present cepheid calibration
[
23
]
.

This method can also be applied to the juxtaposition of HIIGx Hubble diagram with other Hubble diagrams, which might be a larger sample or consist of higher redshift objects such as quasars and FRBs. With higher redshift samples, we expect to obtain more-literally cosmological model-independent non-local constraint on
H_{0}
, which will be constructive to fill in the gaps in its measurement from the long evolutionary history between the early- and late- Universe, thereby adjudicate whether there is an an early time transition or late time transition in the expansion rate that drives us to go beyond the
\Lambda
CDM model.

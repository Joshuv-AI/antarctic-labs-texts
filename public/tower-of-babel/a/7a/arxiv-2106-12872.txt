Boundedness criterion for sublinear operators and commutators on generalized mixed Morrey spaces
Mingquan Wei ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, the author studies the boundedness for a large class of sublinear operators T α , α ∈ [ 0 , n ) T_{\alpha},\alpha\in[0,n) generated by Calderón-Zygmund operators ( α = 0 \alpha=0 ) and generated by fractional integral operator ( α > 0 \alpha>0 ) on generalized mixed Morrey spaces M q → φ ​ ( ℝ n ) M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) . Moreover, the boundeness for commutators of T α , α ∈ [ 0 , n ) T_{\alpha},\alpha\in[0,n) on generalized mixed Morrey spaces M q → φ ​ ( ℝ n ) M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) is also studied. As applications, we obtain the boundedness for Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators, fractional integral operator, fractional maximal operator and their commutators on generalzied mixed Morrey spaces.

1.
Introduction

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
r>0
, let
B(x,r)
be the open ball centered at
x
with the radius
r
, and
B^{c}(x,r)
be its complement. The well-known fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
and fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
are defined by

M_{\alpha}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy,~~0\leq\alpha<n,

and

I_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy,~~0<\alpha<n

respectively, where
f
is locally integrable and
|B(x,r)|
is the Lebesgue measure of
B(x,r)
. If
\alpha=0
, then
M\equiv M_{0}
is the classical Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.

Other than fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
and fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
, Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators (see [
8
]) are also basic integral operators in harmonic analyis.
A Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator is a linear operator bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which takes all infinitely continuously differentiable functions
f
with
compact support to the functions
f\in L_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and can be represented by

Kf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k(x,y)f(y)dy,~~\mathrm{a.e.~off~supp}f.

Here
k(x,y)
is a continuous function away from the diagonal which satisfies
the standard estimates: there exist some
0<\epsilon\leq 1
, such that

|k(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},x\neq y
and

|k(x,y)-k(x^{\prime},y)|+|k(y,x)-k(y,x^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\epsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+\epsilon}}

whenever
2|x-x^{\prime}|<|x-y|
.

In order to study the above operators and some related operators in harmonic analysis uniformly, many researchers introduced the following sublinear operators satisfying some size conditions.

The first one is
T\equiv T_{0}
, which is a sublinear opertor, and satisfies that for any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
x\notin\mathrm{supp}f
,

|Tf(x)|\lesssim\int\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}dy.

(1)

Another one is
T_{\alpha}
(
0<\alpha<n
), the fractional version of
T_{0}
, which is also a sublinear opertor, and satisfies that for any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
x\notin\mathrm{supp}f
,

|T_{\alpha}f(x)|\lesssim\int\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy.

(2)

We point out that condition (
1
) was first introduced by Soria and
Weiss [
26
] and condition (
2
) was introduced by Guliyev et al. [
12
]. Conditions (
1
) and (
2
) are satisfied by many interesting operators in harmonic analysis, such as the Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators, the Carleson’s maximal operators, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators, the Fefferman’s singular multipliers, the Fefferman’s singular integrals, the Ricci-Stein’s oscillatory singular integrals, the Bochner-Riesz means and so on (see
[
16
,
26
] for more details).

As is well known, commutators are also important operators and play a key role in harmonic analysis. Recall that for a locally integrable function
b
and an integral operator
T
, the commutator formed by
b
and
T
is defined by
[b,T]=bT-Tb
. Commutators of fractional maximal operator, fractional integral operator and Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators have been intensively studied, see [
8
] for more details. It is worthy pointing out that there are two different commutators of the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
. In this paper, the commutator of fractional maximal operator
[b,M_{\alpha}]
under consideration is of the form

[b,M_{\alpha}]f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{B(x,r)}|b(x)-b(y)|~|f(y)|dy,~~0<\alpha\leq n,

for all locally integrable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

To study a class of commutators uniformly, one can also introduce some sublinear operators with additional size conditions as before.
For a function
b
, suppose that the operator
T_{b}\equiv T_{b,0}

represents a linear or a sublinear operator, which satisfies that for any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
x\notin\mathrm{supp}f
,

|T_{b}f(x)|\lesssim\int\frac{|b(x)-b(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}|f(y)|dy.

(3)

Similarly, we assume that the operator
T_{b,\alpha},\alpha\in(0,n)
represents a linear or a sublinear operator, which
satisfies that for any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
x\notin\mathrm{supp}f
,

|T_{b,\alpha}f(x)|\lesssim\int\frac{|b(x)-b(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}|f(y)|dy.

(4)

The operator
T_{b,\alpha},\alpha\in[0,n)
has been studied in [
12
,
16
].

As we know, the classical Morrey space is an important generalization of Lebesgue spaces. The classical Morrey space was introduced by Morrey in [
18
] to study the regularity of elliptic partial differential equations.
Nowadays, the Morrey space has become one of the most important function spaces in the theory of function spaces.
By a slight modification, Guliyev et al. [
3
,
12
] introduced generalized Morrey spaces and studied the boundedness of many important operators in harmonic analysis on generalized Morrey spaces. In particular, the boundedness of sublinear operators
T
,
T_{\alpha}
,
T_{b}
and
T_{b,\alpha}
was considered in [
12
]. Another interesting extension of the classical Morrey space is the mixed Morrey space, which was introduced by Nogayama et al. [
20
,
21
,
22
]. The boundedness of many integral operators and their commutators on mixed Morrey spaces was studied in [
20
,
21
].

Highly inspired by the work of Guliyev [
3
,
12
,
13
] and Nogayama et al. [
20
,
21
,
22
], we are going to study the boundedness of the operators
T
,
T_{\alpha}
,
T_{b}
and
T_{b,\alpha}
under some size conditions on generalized mixed Morrey spaces in this paper. Here, generalized mixed Morrey spaces are the combination of generalized Morrey spaces and mixed Morrey spaces (see Definition
2.1
in the following section), and theorefore are much more general.
Our main results extend the boundedness of many operators on Morrey spaces [
1
,
24
,
25
,
28
], mixed Lebesgue spaces [
2
,
4
], generalized Morrey spaces [
3
,
12
,
13
], and mixed Morrey spaces [
20
,
21
]. Moreover, one can obtain the boundedness of many integral operators in harmonic analysis on generalized mixed Morrey spaces from our main theorems.

This paper is organized as follows. The definitions and some preliminaries are presented in Sect. 2. The boundedness of
T
and
T_{\alpha}
on generalized mixed Morrey spaces is studied in Sect. 3. The boundedness of
T_{b}
and
T_{b,\alpha}
on generalized mixed Morrey spaces is obtained in Sect. 4. Some applications are given in Sect. 5 to show the power of our main theorems.

2.
Definitions and preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we use the following notations.

For any
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
B(x,r)=\{y:|y-x|<r\}
be the ball centered at
x
with radius
r
. Let
\mathbb{B}=\{B(x,r):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0\}
be the set of all such balls. We also use
\chi_{E}
and
|E|
to denote the characteristic function and the Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
E
.

The letter
\vec{p}
denotes
n
-tuples of the numbers in
(0,\infty]
, (
n\geq 1
),
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})
. By definition, the inequality, for example,
0<\vec{p}<\infty
means
0<p_{i}<\infty
for all
i
. For
1\leq\vec{p}\leq\infty
, we denote
\vec{p}^{\prime}=(p^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,p^{\prime}_{n})
, where
p^{\prime}_{i}
satisfies

\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}=1
. By
A\lesssim B
, we
mean that
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
, and
A\sim B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the class of Lebesgue measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For
0<\vec{p}<\infty
, a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
belongs to
L^{\vec{p}}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
f\chi_{E}\in L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any compact subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We also use
\mathbb{C}
to represent all the complex numbers, and
\mathbb{N}
to represent the collection of all non-negative integers.

The classical Morrey space
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a natural generalization of Lebesgue spaces, which consist of all
functions
f\in L^{p}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with finite norm

\|f\|_{M^{p}_{q}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{p}-{\frac{1}{q}}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(B(x,r))},

where
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
.
Note that
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
p=q
, and
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
p=\infty
. If
q>p
, then
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Theta
, where
\Theta
is the set of all functions equivalent to 0 on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The classical Morrey space is a proper substitution when we consider the boundedness of integral operators in harmonic analysis. For example,
Chiarenza and Frasca [
5
] studied the boundedness of the maximal operator
M
in
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The well known Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality was also extented to Morrey spaces by Spanne (but published by Peetre [
23
]) and Adams [
1
]. Since
M_{\alpha}f(x)\lesssim I_{\alpha}|f|(x),0<\alpha<n
, the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is also bounded on
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In recent years, classical Morrey spaces
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
were extended to generalized Morrey spaces by Guliyev et al. [
3
,
10
,
12
,
13
].

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
\varphi(x,r):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a Lebesgue measurable function. A function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the generalized Morrey spaces, if it satisfies

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{\varphi}_{q}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}|B(x,r)|^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(B(x,r))}<\infty.

(5)

Roughly speaking, generalized Morrey space
M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be obtained by substituting
|B(x,r)|^{1/p-1/q}
with a more general function in the definition of
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. From the definition, we recover the classical Morrey space
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by taking
\varphi(x,r)=|B(x,r)|^{-1/p}
.
The boundedness of the sublinear operators
T
,
T_{\alpha}
,
T_{b}
and
T_{b,\alpha}
on spaces
M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under some size conditions was obtained by Guliyev et al. [
12
].

In 2019, Nogayama [
21
] considered a new Morrey type space, with the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm replaced by the mixed Lebesgue norm
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is call the mixed Morrey space.

We first recall the definition of mixed Lebesgue spaces defined in [
2
].

Let
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})\in(0,\infty]^{n}
. Then the mixed Lebesgue norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})|^{p_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}dx_{2}\right)^{\frac{p_{3}}{p_{2}}}\cdots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a measurable function. If
p_{j}=\infty
for some
j=1,\cdots,n
, then we have to make appropriate modifications. We define the mixed Lebesgue space
L^{{\vec{p}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

to be the set of all
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}<\infty
.

Let
1\leq\vec{q}<\infty,1\leq p<\infty
and
n/p\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}1/{q_{i}}
. A function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to the mixed Morrey spaces
M^{p}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\|f\|_{M^{p}_{\vec{q}}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{p}-{\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\right)}}\|f\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}<\infty.

Obviously, we recover the classical Morrey space
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\vec{q}=q
. We point out that in [
20
,
21
], the author used the cubes to define the mixed Morrey spaces. It is not hard to verify that the two definitions are equivalent.

In [
21
], the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
, the fractional interal operator
I_{\alpha}
and the singular integral operators
K
were proved to be bounded in
M^{p}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The boundedness of the commutator of
I_{\alpha}
on
M^{p}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
was also obtained in [
20
].

Noting that generalized Morrey spaces and mixed Morrey spaces are different extensions of the classical Morrey spaces, it is natural for us to unify the two spaces.

Now we are in a position to give the difinition of generalized mixed Morrey spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq\vec{q}<\infty
, and
\varphi(x,r):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a Lebesgue measurable function. A function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to the mixed Morrey space
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\|f\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}\|\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{-1}\|f\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}<\infty.

Generalized mixed Morrey spaces contain generalized Morrey spaces and mixed Morrey spaces as special cases. In fact,
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\vec{q}=q
, and
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=M^{p}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\varphi(x,r)=|B(x,r)|^{-1/p}
.

For a non-negative locally integrable function
w
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the weighted Hardy operators
H_{w}
and
H^{*}_{w}
are defined by defined

H_{w}g(t):=\int_{t}^{\infty}g(s)w(s)ds,~~~0<t<\infty

and

H^{*}_{w}g(t):=\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{s}{t}\right)g(s)w(s)ds,~~~0<t<\infty,

respectively, where
g\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We have the following boundedness results for the weighted Hardy operators
H_{w}
and
H^{*}_{w}
proved in [
9
,
11
].

Lemma 2.1
.

The inequality

\mathrm{ess}\sup_{t>0}v_{2}(t)H_{w}g(t)\leq C~\mathrm{ess}\sup_{t>0}v_{1}(t)g(t)

holds for all non-negative and non-increasing
g
on
(0,\infty)
if and only if

A=\sup_{t>0}v_{2}(t)\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{w(s)ds}{\mathrm{ess}\sup_{s<\tau<\infty}v_{1}(\tau)}<\infty,

and
C\sim A
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The inequality

\mathrm{ess}\sup_{t>0}v_{2}(t)H^{*}_{w}g(t)\leq C~\mathrm{ess}\sup_{t>0}v_{1}(t)g(t)

holds for all non-negative and non-increasing
g
on
(0,\infty)
if and only if

A=\sup_{t>0}v_{2}(t)\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{s}{t}\right)\frac{w(s)ds}{\mathrm{ess}\sup_{s<\tau<\infty}v_{1}(\tau)}<\infty,

and
C\sim A
.

3.
Sublinear operators
T
and
T_{\alpha}
in spaces
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

In this section, we investigate the boundedness of
T
and
T_{\alpha}
satisfying the size conditions (
1
) and (
2
) respectively, on generalized mixed Morrey spaces
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We first prove two lemmas, which give us the explicit estimats for the
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of
T
and
T_{\alpha}
on a given ball
B(x_{0},r)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
,
T
be a sublinear operator satisfying condition
(
1
)
, and bounded on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Then for
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, the inequality

\|Tf\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}t^{-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}dt

holds for any ball
B(x_{0},r)
and all
f\in L^{\vec{q}}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

For any ball
B=B(x_{0},r)
, let
2B=B(x_{0},2r)
be the ball centered at
x_{0}
, with the radius
2r
. we represent
f
as
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where

f_{1}(y)=f\chi_{2B}(y),~~f_{2}(y)=f\chi_{(2B)^{c}}(y),~~r>0.

Since
T
is a sublinear operator, we have

\|Tf\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B)}\leq\|Tf_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B)}+\|Tf_{2}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B)}.

Noting that
f_{1}\in L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
T
is bounded in
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|Tf_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B)}\leq\|Tf_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}.

It is clear that
x\in B
,
y\in(2B)^{c}
imply
\frac{1}{2}|x_{0}-y|\leq|x-y|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x_{0}-y|
, which further yields

|Tf_{2}(x)|\lesssim\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{(2B)^{c}}|f(y)|\int_{|x_{0}-y|}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{n+1}}dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{2r}^{\infty}\int_{2r\leq|x_{0}-y|<t}|f(y)|dy\frac{dt}{t^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{2r}^{\infty}\int_{B(x_{0},t)}|f(y)|dy\frac{dt}{t^{n+1}}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality on mixed Lebesgue spaces (see [
2
]), we obtain

\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy\lesssim\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}.

Moreover, for all
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, we have

\|Tf_{2}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}.

Therefore, one gets

\|Tf\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}+r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}.

Thus

\|Tf\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}.

∎

The second lemma is about the estimates of
\|T_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B(x_{0},r))}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
,
0<\alpha<n/\vec{q}
,
\frac{1}{\vec{p}}=\frac{1}{\vec{q}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
,
T_{\alpha}
be a sublinear operator satisfying condition
(
2
)
, and bounded from
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Then for
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, the inequality

\|T_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}t^{-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}dt

holds for any ball
B(x_{0},r)
and all
f\in L^{\vec{q}}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

For any ball
B=B(x_{0},r)
, we also let
2B=B(x_{0},2r)
as before. Write
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, with

f_{1}=f\chi_{2B}
and
f_{2}=f\chi_{(2B)^{c}}
.

By the fact that
T_{\alpha}
is a sublinear operator, we have

\|T_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B)}\leq\|T_{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B)}+\|T_{\alpha}f_{2}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B)}.

Noting that
f_{1}\in L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
T_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|T_{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B)}\leq\|T_{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}.

Since
x\in B
,
y\in(2B)^{c}
imply
\frac{1}{2}|x_{0}-y|\leq|x-y|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x_{0}-y|
, it yields that

|T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)|\lesssim\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{(2B)^{c}}|f(y)|\int_{|x_{0}-y|}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{n+1-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{2r}^{\infty}\int_{2r\leq|x_{0}-y|<t}|f(y)|dy\frac{dt}{t^{n+1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{2r}^{\infty}\int_{B(x_{0},t)}|f(y)|dy\frac{dt}{t^{n+1-\alpha}}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality on mixed Lebesgue spaces, we obtain

\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy\lesssim\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}.

(6)

Moreover, for all
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, we have

\|T_{\alpha}f_{2}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}.

Therefore,

\|T_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}+r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(2B)}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}

(7)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}.

Thus

\|T_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(B(x_{0},r))}\lesssim r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}.

∎

4.
Sublinear operators
T_{b}
and
T_{b,\alpha}
in spaces

In this section, we investigate the boundedness of
T_{\alpha}
and
T_{b,\alpha}
satisfying the size conditions (

) and (
4
) respectively, on generalized mixed Morrey space
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

First, we review the definition of
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the bounded mean oscillation space.
A function
f\in L_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f_{B(x,r)}|dy<\infty.

(8)

If one regards two functions whose difference is a constant as one, then the
space
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space with respect to norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm BMO}}
.
The John-Nirenberg ineuqalitiy for
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
yields that for any
1<q<\infty
and
f\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of
f
is equivalent to

\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}^{q}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\left(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f_{B(x,r)}|^{q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Recall that for any
\vec{q}=(q_{1},\cdots,q_{n})\in(1,\infty)^{n}
, the John-Nirenberg inequality for mixed norm spaces [
15
] shows that the
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of all
f\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is also equivalent to

\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}^{\vec{q}}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\frac{\|(f-f_{B(x,r)})\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}}{\|\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}}.

(9)

The following property for
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
functions is valid.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for all
0<2r<t
, we have

|f_{B(x,r)}-f_{B(x,t)}|\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}}\ln\frac{t}{r}.

(10)

We first prove two lemmas, which give us the explicit estimats for the
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of
T_{b}
and
T_{b,\alpha}
on a given ball
B(x_{0},r)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
,

,

be a sublinear operator satisfying condition
(
3
)

For any ball

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|b(x)-b(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|b(x)-b(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}|f(y)|dy.

\displaystyle\|T_{b}f_{2}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|b(\cdot)-b(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}|f(y)|dy\right\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B(x_{0},r))}

9
), (
10

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

.

\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{t}{r}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{ess}\inf}_{t<s<\infty}\varphi_{1}(x,s)s^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}dt\lesssim\varphi_{2}(x,r).

Suppose
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
T_{b}
is a sublinear operator satisfying condition
(
3
)
, and bounded on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then for
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, the operator
T_{b}
is bounded form
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|T_{b}f\|_{M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\|f\|_{M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}}.

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
follows by Lemma
4.2
and Lemma

2.2
in the same manner as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

From Theorem
4.1
, on can recover the result of Guliyev et al. [
12
, Theorem 6.6] by taking
\vec{q}=q
.

Similarly, for the sublinear operator
T_{b,\alpha}
with size condition (
4
), we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
,
0<\alpha<n/\vec{q}
,
\frac{1}{\vec{p}}=\frac{1}{\vec{q}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
, and
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
satisfy the condition

\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{t}{r}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{ess}\inf}_{t<s<\infty}\varphi_{1}(x,s)s^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}dt\lesssim\varphi_{2}(x,r).

Suppose
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
T_{b,\alpha}
is a sublinear operator satisfying condition
(
4
)
, and bounded from
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then for

, the operator
T_{b,\alpha}
is bounded from
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|T_{b,\alpha}f\|_{M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{p}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\|f\|_{M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}}.

Proof.

The statement of Theorem
4.2
follows by Lemma
4.3
and Lemma

2.2
in the same manner as in the proof of Theorem

.
∎

By taking
\vec{q}=q
in Theorem

5.
Some applications

This section gives some applications of our main theorems. We will show that many important integral operators and commutators appearing in harmonic analysis satisfy the assumptions mentioned above. Therefore, we can obtain the boundedness of various operators on generalized mixed Morrey spaces by using our main results.

As stated in the introduction, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
and the Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator
K

are all sublinear operators satisfying the condition (
1
). One can also see that
[b,M]
and
[b,K]
are also subilinear operators and satisfiy the condition (
3
).
Note that the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
is a linear operator satisfying the condition (
2
), and the commutator
[b,I_{\alpha}]
is also a linear operator satisfying (
4
).
So in order to apply our main theorems to the mentioned operators, we need to show the boundedness of
M,K,[b,M],[b,K]
on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the boundedness of
I_{\alpha},[b,I_{\alpha}]
from
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

As we know, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
is bounded on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1<\vec{q}<\infty
(see [
21
]), but there is no complete boundedness results for some other operators on mixed Lebesgue spaces. To prove the boundedness of some important operators on mixed Lebesgue spaces in a uniform way, we will give the extrapolation theorems on mixed Lebesgue spaces, which have their own interest.

The extrapolaton theory on mixed Lebesgue spaces relies on the classical
A_{p}
weight (see [
8
]).

Definition 5.1
.

For
1<p<\infty
, a non-negative function
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be an
A_{p}
weight if

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p}}=\sup_{B\in\mathbb{B}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

A non-negative local integrable function
w
is said to be an
A_{1}
weight if

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(y)dy\leq Cw(x),~~~a.e.x\in B

for some constant
C>0
. The infimum of all such
C
is denoted by
[w]_{A_{1}}
. We denote
A_{\infty}
by the union of all
A_{p}~(1\leq p<\infty)
functions.

We also need the boundedness of
M
on mixed norm spaces
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see [
21
].

Lemma 5.1
.

For
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, there holds

\|Mf\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}.

(11)

By
\mathfrak{F}
, we mean a family of pair
(f,g)
of non-negative measurable functions that are not identical to zero. For such a family
\mathfrak{F}
,
p>0
and a weight
w\in A_{q}
, the expression

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{p}w(x)dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{p}w(x)dx,~~~(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}

means that this inequality holds for all pair
(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}
if the left hand side is finite, and the implicated constant depends only on
p
and
[w]_{A_{q}}
.

Now we give the extrapolation theorems on mixed Lebesgue spaces. The first one is the diagonal extrapolation theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
0<q_{0}<\infty
and
\vec{q}=(q_{1},\cdots,q_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
. Let
f,g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose for every
w\in A_{1}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{q_{0}}w(x)dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{q_{0}}w(x)dx,~~~(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}.

(12)

Then if
\vec{q}>q_{0}
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\vec{q}}},~~~(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}.

(13)

Our second main result in this section is the off-diagonal extrapolation on mixed Lebesgue spaces.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{M}
. Suppose that for some
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
with
0<q_{0}<p_{0}<\infty
and every
w\in A_{1}
,

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{p_{0}}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{q_{0}}w(x)^{\frac{q_{0}}{p_{0}}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},~~(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}.

(14)

Then for all
q_{0}<\vec{q}<\frac{p_{0}q_{0}}{p_{0}-q_{0}}
, and
\vec{p}
satisfies
1/\vec{q}-1/\vec{p}=1/q_{0}-1/p_{0}
,
we have

\|f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\vec{q}}},~~~(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}.

(15)

Proof.

By the similarity, we only prove Theorem
5.2
.

We use the Rubio de Francia iteration algorithm presented in [
7
].

Let
\bar{\vec{q}}=\vec{q}/q_{0}
and
\bar{\vec{p}}=\vec{p}/p_{0}
. By the assumptions and Lemma
5.1
, the
maximal operator is bounded on
L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so there exists a positive constant
B
such
that

\|Mf\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}\leq B\|f\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}.

For any non-negative function
h
, define a new operator
\mathfrak{R}h
by

\mathfrak{R}h(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{M^{k}h(x)}{2^{k}B^{k}},

where for
k\geq 1
,
M^{k}
denotes
k
iterations of the maximal operator,
and
M^{0}
is the identity operator.

The operator
\mathfrak{R}
satisfies

h(x)\leq\mathfrak{R}h(x),

(16)

\|\mathfrak{R}h\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}\leq 2\|h\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}},

(17)

\|\mathfrak{R}h\|_{A_{1}}\leq 2B.

(18)

The inequality (
16
) is straight-forward.

Since

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{R}h)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{M^{k+1}h}{2^{k}B^{k}}\leq 2B\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{M^{k}h}{2^{k}B^{k}}\leq 2B\mathfrak{R}h,

the properties (
17
) and (
18
) are consequences of Lemma
5.1
and the definition of
A_{1}
.

Since the dual of
L^{\bar{\vec{p}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f^{p_{0}}\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}}}

(19)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p_{0}}h(x)dx:~\|h\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}\leq 1,h\geq 0\right\}.

By using Hölder’s inequality on mixed Lebesgue spaces and (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{p_{0}}h(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{p_{0}}\mathfrak{R}h(x)dx

(20)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f^{p_{0}}\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}}}\|h\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}<\infty.

In view of (
16
) and
\mathfrak{R}h\in A_{1}
, we use (
14
) with
w=\mathfrak{R}h(x)
to obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{p_{0}}h(x)dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{p_{0}}\mathfrak{R}h(x)dx\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{q_{0}}[\mathfrak{R}h(x)]^{q_{0}/p_{0}}dx\right)^{p_{0}/q_{0}}.

Combining (
17
) with (
20
) and using Hölder’s inequality on mixed Lebesgue spaces again, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{p_{0}}h(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g^{q_{0}}\|^{p_{0}/q_{0}}_{L^{\bar{\vec{q}}}}\|(\mathfrak{R}h)^{q_{0}/p_{0}}\|^{p_{0}/q_{0}}_{L^{\bar{\vec{q}}^{\prime}}}

(21)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\|g\|^{p_{0}}_{L^{\vec{q}}}\|(\mathfrak{R}h)^{q_{0}/p_{0}}\|^{p_{0}/q_{0}}_{L^{\bar{\vec{q}}^{\prime}}}.

A direct calculation yields
q_{0}\bar{\vec{q}}^{\prime}=p_{0}\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}
. Therefore

\|(\mathfrak{R}h)^{q_{0}/p_{0}}\|^{p_{0}/q_{0}}_{L^{\bar{\vec{q}}^{\prime}}}=\|\mathfrak{R}h\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}.

(22)

By taking the supremum over all
h\in L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|h\|_{L^{\bar{\vec{p}}^{\prime}}}\leq 1
and
h\geq 0
, (
19
), (
21
) and (
22
) give us the desired conclusion (
15
). ∎

We point out that when
n=2
, there are other versions of the diagonal extrapolation theorem [
14
] and the off-diagonal extrapolation theorem [
27
] on mixed Lebesgue spaces, which are different from Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
.

By the density of smooth functions with compact support
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the mixed Lebesgue space
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1<\vec{q}<\infty
(see [
2
]), one can apply Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
to the mapping property of some sublinear operators.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose
0<q_{0}<\vec{q}<\infty
and

T
is a sublinear operator such that for every
w\in A_{1}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Tf(z)|^{q_{0}}w(z)dz\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(z)|^{q_{0}}w(z)dz,~~f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then
T
can be extended to a bounded operator on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
, for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|Tf\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}.

Since
T
is a sublinear operator, we have
|T(f)-T(g)|\leq|T(f-g)|
, and hence, for any
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|T(f)-T(g)\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\leq\|T(f-g)\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}.

Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the above inequalities guarantee that
T
can be extended
to be a bounded operator on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

The following corollary is a consequence of Theorem
5.3
and the weighted boundedness of the corresponding operators.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
,
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
M,K,[b,M],[b,K]
are all bounded on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

It is well known that
M,K,[b,M],[b,K]
are all sublinear operators, and bounded on
L^{q_{0}}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for arbitrary
1<q_{0}<\infty
and
w\in A_{q_{0}}
(see [
8
] for example). Since
A_{1}\subset A_{q_{0}}
, Theorem
5.3
implies that
M,K
,
[b,M],[b,K]
are all bounded on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q_{0}<\vec{q}<\infty
.
In view of the arbitrariness of
1<q_{0}<\infty
,
M
,
K,[b,M],[b,K]
are also bounded on
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<\vec{q}<\infty
.
∎

Similarly, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
0<q_{0}<p_{0}<\infty
, and

T
is a sublinear operator such that every
w\in A_{1}
,

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Tf(x)|^{p_{0}}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{q_{0}}w(x)^{\frac{q_{0}}{p_{0}}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},~~f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(23)

Then for all
q_{0}<\vec{q}<\frac{p_{0}q_{0}}{p_{0}-q_{0}}
, and
\vec{p}
satisfies
1/\vec{q}-1/\vec{p}=1/q_{0}-1/p_{0}
,

T
can be extended to a bounded operator from
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Since the proof of Theorem
5.4
is similar to that of Theorem
5.3
, we leave it to readers.

To apply Theorem
5.4
to fractional integral operator and its commutator, we need a different
class of weights: Suppose
0<\alpha<n
,
1<q<n/\alpha
and
1/q-1/p=\alpha/n
, we say
w\in A_{q,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if for any ball
B\in\mathbb{B}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-q^{\prime}/p}dx\right)^{p/q^{\prime}}<\infty.

Note that this is equivalent to
w\in A_{r}
, where
r=1+p/q^{\prime}
, so, in particular if
w\in A_{1}
, then
w\in A_{q,p}
. The boundedness of fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
and its commutator
[b,I_{\alpha}]
on weighted Lebesgue spaces was obtained in [
17
,
19
].

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
and
1<q<p<\infty
with
1/q-1/p=\alpha/n
. If

w\in A_{q,p}
and
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|I_{\alpha}f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{q}w(x)^{q/p}dx\right)^{1/q},

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|[b,I_{\alpha}]f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{q}w(x)^{q/p}dx\right)^{1/q}.

These results are usually stated with the class
A_{q,p}
defined slightly differently, with

w
replaced by
w^{p}
(see [
6
,
17
,
19
] for example). Our formulation, though non-standard, is better for our purposes.

By Theorem
5.4
and Lemma
5.2
, we can get the following result.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
,
1<\vec{q}<n/\alpha
,
\frac{1}{\vec{q}}-\frac{1}{\vec{p}}=\frac{\alpha}{n}
, and
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then both
I_{\alpha}
and
[b,I_{\alpha}]
are bounded from
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

The proof is just a repetition of the proof of Corollary
5.1
, so we omit the details.
∎

We point out that the boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
on mixed Lebesgue spaces has already proved in [
2
] in a more general setting. However, our proof, relying on the extrapolation theory on mixed norm spaces, has its own interest.

From the statement at the beginning of this section, we can obtain the boundedness of
M,K
on generalized mixed Morrey spaces, whose proof is just a combination of Theorem
3.1

and Corollary
5.1
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, and
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
satisfy the condition

\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{ess}\inf}_{t<s<\infty}\varphi_{1}(x,s)s^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}dt\lesssim\varphi_{2}(x,r).

Then the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
and the Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator
K
are both bounded from
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|Mf\|_{M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}},

\|Kf\|_{M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}}.

Similarly, we have

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
, and
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
satisfy the condition

\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{t}{r}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{ess}\inf}_{t<s<\infty}\varphi_{1}(x,s)s^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}dt\lesssim\varphi_{2}(x,r).

If
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the commutator of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
[b,M]
and the commutator of the Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator
[b,K]
are both bounded from
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|[b,M]f\|_{M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}},

\|[b,K]f\|_{M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}}.

For the boundedness of
I_{\alpha},[b,I_{\alpha}]
on generalized mixed Morrey spaces, we have the following theorems.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
,
0<\alpha<n/\vec{q}
,
\frac{1}{\vec{p}}=\frac{1}{\vec{q}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
, and
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
satisfy the condition

\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{ess}\inf}_{t<s<\infty}\varphi_{1}(x,s)s^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}dt\lesssim\varphi_{2}(x,r).

Then the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{M^{\varphi_{2}}_{\vec{p}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q}}}.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
,
0<\alpha<n/\vec{q}
,
\frac{1}{\vec{p}}=\frac{1}{\vec{q}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
, and
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
satisfy the condition

\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{t}{r}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{ess}\inf}_{t<s<\infty}\varphi_{1}(x,s)s^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}{t^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}}dt\lesssim\varphi_{2}(x,r).

If
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the commutator of the fractional integral operator

is bounded from

to

and Theorem

remain true for fractional maximal operator and its commutator, sicnce

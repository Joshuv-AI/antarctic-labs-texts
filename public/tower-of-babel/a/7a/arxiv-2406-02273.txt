A KL-based Analysis Framework with Applications to Non-Descent Optimization Methods
Junwen Qiu, Bohao Ma, Xiao Li, Andre Milzarek

Abstract
We propose a novel analysis framework for non-descent-type optimization methodologies in nonconvex scenarios based on the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property. Our framework allows covering a broad class of algorithms, including those commonly employed in stochastic and distributed optimization. Specifically, it enables the analysis of first-order methods that lack a sufficient descent property and do not require access to full (deterministic) gradient information. We leverage this framework to establish, for the first time, iterate convergence and the corresponding rates for the decentralized gradient method and federated averaging under mild assumptions. Furthermore, based on the new analysis techniques, we show the convergence of the random reshuffling and stochastic gradient descent method without necessitating typical a priori bounded iterates assumptions.

Introduction

The analysis of algorithms under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) inequality
[

,

. Notably, in

,

, Absil, Attouch and Bolte et al. have made significant contributions in developing a comprehensive KL analysis framework for optimization algorithms. This framework serves as a valuable blueprint, simplifying the study and verification of the asymptotic behavior and iterate convergence of descent-type methods for nonsmooth nonconvex minimization problems. Successful instances of applying the analysis framework to obtain convergence encompass splitting methods

, Douglas-Rachford splitting

, proximal gradient method

The large-scale nature of modern machine learning problems has made stochastic and distributed optimization techniques crucial tools in manifold tasks, including the training of neural networks. Nonetheless, the asymptotic convergence analysis of these algorithms in the nonconvex setting is relatively limited compared to deterministic methods. This limitation is primarily due to the challenges posed by the lack of a sufficient descent property. Additionally, stochastic and distributed algorithms do not have access to the full gradient information, necessitating the use of diminishing step sizes to mitigate the stochastic errors. This significantly differs from the common step size conditions in the standard frameworks
[

,

,

, where the step sizes are typically bounded away from zero.

Given these observations in stochastic and distributed optimization, we introduce a novel KL-based analysis framework outlined (in an informal way) below.

1.1
Related Work

Attouch, Bolte and Svaiter
[
4
]
have developed a comprehensive KL inequality-based analysis framework (outlined below) that unifies and simplifies the convergence analysis of descent-type methods applied to nonconvex problems.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the KL property at
\mathrm{crit}(f)
. If
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
has at least one accumulation point and satisfies
(H.1)
–
(H.2)
below, then
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
converges to some
x^{*}\in\mathrm{crit}(f)
.

(H.1)

Sufficient descent.
There exists a constant
a>0
, such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

f({x}^{k+1})+a\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\leq f({x}^{k}).

(1)

(H.2)

Relative error.
There exist constants
b>0
, such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial f(x^{k+1}))\leq b\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|.\vskip-4.30554pt

(2)

Here,
\partial f(x)
denotes the limiting subdifferential
[
2
, Definition 1]
of
f
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathrm{dist}(0,S):=\inf_{s\in S}\|s\|
.
(H.1)
is intended to model a descent property as it involves a measure of the quality of the descent.
(H.2)
reflects relative inexact optimality conditions.

As mentioned before, the framework
[
4
]
has been successfully utilized to establish iterate convergence for a vast variety of algorithms.
Nonetheless,
(H.1)
typically does not hold when analyzing inertial methods due to additional momentum terms. To fit inertial methods, Ochs et al. have studied adjustments of the conditions
(H.1)
and
(H.2)
in
[
31
, Section 3.2]
and iterate convergence of inertial proximal methods was shown
[
31
, Theorem 4.9]
.
Frankel, Garrigos and Peypouquet
[
11
]
noted that the condition
(H.2)
is restrictive when algorithmic updates contain small computational errors. To address this, a relaxation of condition
(H.2)
was proposed in
[
11
]
that allows incorporating additive errors:

(H.2
′
)

Relative error.
There exist constants
b>0
, such that for all
k\geq 1
,

\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial f(x^{k+1}))\leq b\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|+\varepsilon_{k},\quad\text{where}\quad{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}}\;\varepsilon_{k}<\infty.

(3)

This modification is non-trivial because it helps to cover the inexact proximal gradient method and the generalized Newton’s method, cf.
[
11
, Theorem 6 and Proposition 4]
. Moreover, explicit convergence rates in terms of the parameters (usually the KL exponent) are also provided in
[
11
, Theorem 4 and 5]
.

Still, the necessity for sufficient descent,
(H.1)
, in the existing frameworks
[
4
,
31
,
11
]
has hindered the convergence analyses of algorithms without such property. Typical examples for such algorithms include methods with nonmonotone line-search schemes
[
12
,
5
,
13
,
33
]
, to name a few. Taking the specific line search conditions (cf.
[
12
, Section 3]
) into account, Qian and Pan
[
33
]
developed a new condition
(H.1′)
(shown below) and established the iterates convergence.

(H.1
′
)

Nonmonotone descent.
There exists a constant
a>0
, such that for all
k\geq 1
,

f({x}^{k+1})+a\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\leq{\max}_{j=[k-m]_{+},\ldots,k}\;f({x}^{j}).

(4)

The integer
m
is a preset parameter in the line-search scheme and
[k-m]_{+}:=\max\{0,k-m\}
. The adjusted framework enables the convergence analysis of nonmonotone line-search methods, cf.
[
33
, Proposition 2.5 and Theorem 2.9]
.

Incomplete gradient information during iterations is another factor leading to the lack of a sufficient descent property. Common approaches with this feature include stochastic methods such as stochastic gradient descent (
\mathsf{SGD}
), random reshuffling (
\mathsf{RR}
)
[
22
]
, and distributed algorithms such as decentralized gradient descent (
\mathsf{DGD}
)
[
29
]
and federated averaging (
\mathsf{FedAvg}
)
[
26
]
. Unfortunately, the existing frameworks fail to provide convergence guarantees for these methods. The reasons behind such limitations are twofold: (i) the absence of sufficient descent properties and (ii) the complex dynamics arising from stochastic (inexact) gradients and diminishing step sizes. It is important to note that the step sizes in the existing frameworks
[
4
,
31
,
11
,
33
]
are required to be bounded away from zero. This excludes the use of diminishing step sizes, which can help mitigate the errors from stochastic (inexact) gradients. To our knowledge, existing KL-based frameworks are not applicable to approaches that only possess approximate descent guarantees and utilize diminishing step sizes.

Remark 5

(Optimal choice of
\gamma
)
.

The results in
Theorem

4
provide guidance for selecting the optimal step size parameter
\gamma
, when information about the local geometry of the function is available (i.e., when the KL-exponent is known).
In particular, if the exact KL-exponent
\theta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
is known, then, based on
Theorem

4
, the optimal step size parameter
\gamma^{*}
to maximize the convergence rate is given by

\gamma^{*}=\frac{2\theta}{(2\theta-1)p+1}\quad\implies\quad\psi(\theta,\gamma^{*})=\frac{p-1}{(2\theta-1)p+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(\theta,\gamma^{*})=\frac{(p-1)(1-\theta)}{(2\theta-1)p+1}.

Noting
\gamma^{*}>\frac{2}{p+1}
, this choice of
\gamma
guarantees convergence of
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
to some
x^{*}\in\mathrm{crit}(f)
.

3
Application Area I : Stochastic Approximation Methods

In areas of stochastic approximation and online learning, the objective function
f
corresponds to a data-driven predictive learning task in the form of

f(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\Xi}[F(x,\xi)]=\int_{\Xi}F(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)

(8)

where the underlying probability space
(\Xi,{\cal H},\mu)
is usually unknown.

In this section, we establish convergence results for
\mathsf{SGD}
applying the framework presented in
Section

2
.
\mathsf{SGD}
generates a stochastic process
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
via the update rule

\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\alpha_{k}\bm{g}^{k},\quad\forall\,k\geq 1,

(9)

where
\bm{x}^{1}\equiv x^{1}
is a deterministic initial point. Throughout this section, we will use bold letters to describe random variables
{\bm{x}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, while lowercase letters are typically reserved for realizations of a random variable,
x={\bm{x}}(\omega)
, or deterministic parameters. In addition, we assume that there is a sufficiently rich probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
that allows modeling and studying the iterates generated by
\mathsf{SGD}
in a unified way. Hence, each stochastic approximation of
\nabla f(x^{k})
is understood as a realization of a random vector
\bm{g}^{k}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We further consider the natural filtration
{\cal F}_{k}:=\sigma(\bm{x}^{1},\bm{x}^{2},\ldots,\bm{x}^{k})
; each iterate
\bm{x}^{k}
is then
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable for
k\geq 1
. Next, let us define the stochastic error term
\bm{e}^{k}:=\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k})-\bm{g}^{k}
and let us introduce our main assumptions on
\{\bm{e}^{k}\}_{k}
.

Assumption 3
.

Given the probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},\{{\cal F}_{k}\}_{k},\mathbb{P})
, the stochastic errors
\{\bm{e}^{k}\}_{k}
are assumed to satisfy:

(a)

Mean zero:

\mathbb{E}[\bm{e}^{k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
almost surely for all
k\geq 1
.

(b)

Bounded variance:

\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{e}^{k}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq\sigma^{2}
almost surely for all
k\geq 1
.

This assumption is fairly standard in the analysis of stochastic methods
[
8
,
37
,
27
]
.
We note that the convergence results presented in this subsection can be covered by our recent preprint
[
34
]
, which proves the global convergence, iterate convergence, and convergence rates of
\mathsf{SGD}
with momentum. However, the analysis therein requires some nontrivial adaptation to the framework, including the use of auxiliary iterates and a merit function. For simplicity, we only present the convergence results tailored to
\mathsf{SGD}
, which does not require additional adaptations to the framework.

Setting
{\sf T}:=\frac{1}{50{\sf L}}
, we define the sequence of indices
\{I_{k}\}_{k}
inductively via

I_{1}=1\quad\text{and}\quad I_{k+1}=\max\Big\{I_{k}+1,\;\sup\big\{n\geq k:{\sum}_{i=k}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}\leq{\sf T}\big\}\Big\},\quad k\geq 1.

We also consider the aggregated stochastic error terms
\{\bm{s}_{k}\}_{k}
and the associated event
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
:

\bm{s}_{k}:=\max_{I_{k}<t\leq I_{k+1}}\Big\|{\sum}_{i=I_{k}}^{t-1}\alpha_{i}\bm{e}^{i}\Big\|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{\mu}=\Big\{\omega\in\Omega:{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big({\sum}_{i=1}^{k}\bm{s}_{i}^{2}(\omega)\Big)^{\mu}<\infty\Big\}.

Before presenting the main convergence results, we first provide the approximate descent and gradient-bounded update properties for
\{x^{I_{k}}\}_{k}
. For their proofs, we simply refer to the relevant parts in
[
34
]
.

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
Assumption

1
and
3
hold and let
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{SGD}
with step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying

\alpha_{k}>0,\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}\Big({\sum}_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\Big)^{r}<\infty,\quad\text{for some}\;r>1.

(10)

Then, we have
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S}_{\mu})=1
for all
\mu>1/r
. In addition, for any
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
, we consider the realizations
x^{k}\equiv\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)
and
s_{k}\equiv\bm{s}_{k}(\omega)
. There then exists

such that for all

, it holds that:

(a)

f(x^{I_{k+1}})+\frac{{\sf T}}{100}\|\nabla f(x^{I_{k}})\|^{2}\leq f(x^{I_{k}})+\frac{6}{{\sf T}}s_{k}^{2}
.

(b)

\max_{I_{k}<i\leq I_{k+1}}\|x^{i}-x^{I_{k}}\|\leq\frac{20}{19}({\sf T}\|\nabla f(x^{I_{k}})\|+s_{k})
.

Proof.

The proof of
Proposition

6
mainly relies on the work
[
34
]
where the stochastic gradient method with momentum is considered. First,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S}_{\mu})=1
follows from
[
34
, Eq. (4.10)]
. The bound in (a) restates
[
34

and (b) follows from
[
34
, Eq. (A.5)]
.
∎

Remark 7
.

The step size condition
{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}({\sum}_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i})^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
is not very common; it is satisfied by polynomial step sizes
\alpha_{k}\sim k^{-\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(\tfrac{2}{3},1]
. Indeed, using
\alpha_{k}=k^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma\in(\tfrac{2}{3},1)
, and the integral test, we have

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}\Big({\sum}_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\Big)^{r}={\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-2\gamma}\Big({\sum}_{i=1}^{k}i^{-\gamma}\Big)^{r}\leq{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-2\gamma}\left(1+\tfrac{k^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\right)^{r}=\mathcal{O}\Big({\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-2\gamma+r(1-\gamma)}\Big).

The right hand side is summable when
1<r<\frac{2\gamma-1}{1-\gamma}
. The case
\gamma=1
is trivial.

Based on the key properties presented above, the global and iterate convergence of
\mathsf{SGD}
follows quite easily from
Theorem

1
.

.

Remark 9
.

4
Application Area II : Finite-sum Optimization

This section demonstrates the versatility of our framework by establishing iterate convergence of several widely-used algorithms for finite-sum optimization problems. Let us consider the optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}~f(x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x),

(11)

where
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable but not necessarily convex for all
i\in[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
. We make the following assumption on the objective function.

Assumption 4
.

Every component function
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i\in[n]
, is
{\sf L}
-smooth and bounded from below by
\bar{f}
.

This assumption can be guaranteed if every component function
f_{i}
is
{\sf L}_{i}
-smooth and bounded from below by
\bar{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. In this case, we set
{\sf L}=\max_{i\in[n]}{\sf L}_{i}
and
\bar{f}:=\min_{i\in[n]}\bar{f}_{i}
.

4.1
Decentralized Gradient Descent

While prior research has examined the convergence properties of decentralized gradient descent (
\mathsf{DGD}
), primarily in convex settings
[
29
,
38
,
40
]
, a comprehensive understanding of its behavior in nonconvex scenarios remains elusive.
Zeng and Yin
[
40
]
and Daneshmand et al.
[
9
]
have analyzed
\mathsf{DGD}
with constant step sizes under the KL property. However, as highlighted in
[
40
, Proposition 2]
, their results do not guarantee the consensus of limit points generated by the agents’ iterates. Furthermore,
[
9
, Theorem 3.9]
establishes convergence to a neighborhood of the set of critical points
\mathrm{crit}(f)
, rather than convergence to some stationary point.

In this subsection, we address these limitations by employing our proposed KL-based analysis framework. This approach enables us to establish the convergence of
\mathsf{DGD}
iterates to a stationary point consensually with convergence rate guarantees.

Suppose that there are
n
agents and the goal is to solve problem (
11
) collaboratively. Let
x_{i}^{1}=x^{1}
,
i\in[n]
be the initial points. The update step of
\mathsf{DGD}

[
29
]
is given as

x^{k+1}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}x^{k}_{j}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k}),\quad\forall\,i\in[n]\;\text{and}\;k\geq 1

(12)

where
w_{ij}\geq 0
is the weight that neighbor
j
assigns to the agent
i
and
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
are step sizes satisfying

\alpha_{k}\in\Big(0,{\frac{1}{{\sf L}n}}\Big),\quad\alpha_{k+1}\leq\alpha_{k},\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty,\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{3}<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_{k+1}}{\alpha_{k}}=1.

(13)

We make the following assumption on the weights
\{w_{ij}\}_{i,j}
,
i,j\in[n]
.

Assumption 5
.

The matrix
W=[w_{ij}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is symmetric and has the following properties.

(a)

W
is doubly stochastic, i.e.,
\mathrm{null}(I-W)=\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})
.

(b)

W
has
1
-bounded eigenvalues, i.e.,
-I\prec W\preceq I.

Here,
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the vector of all ones. Condition (a) can be equivalently written as
W\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{1}^{\top}W=\mathbf{1}^{\top}
. Condition (b) means that the second largest magnitude eigenvalue of
W
lies in the interval
(0,1)
. This assumption is standard in the study of distributed optimization, see, e.g.,
[
35
,
29
,
38
,
15
]
. To simplify the notations, we denote the average iterates of the agents and the corresponding accumulation points set as

\bar{x}^{k}:=\frac{1}{n}\,{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}(\{\bar{x}^{k}\}_{k}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:{\liminf}_{k\to\infty}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x\|=0\}.

Before presenting the main convergence results, we first provide an upper bound for the consensus error and the descent-type property for
\{\bar{x}^{k}\}_{k}
. The proofs are postponed to
Section

C.1
and
Section

C.2
, respectively.

Proposition 10
.

Let
Assumption

4
and
5
hold and let
\{x_{i}^{k}\}_{k\geq 1,i\in[n]}
be generated by
\mathsf{DGD}
with step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying (
13
). Then there exists
{\sf G}>0
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\leq{\sf G}\alpha_{k}^{2}
.

Proposition 11
.

Let
Assumption

4
and
5
hold and let
\{x_{i}^{k}\}_{k\geq 1,i\in[n]}
be generated by
\mathsf{DGD}
. Further assume that the step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfy (
13
). Then, there is
{\sf G}>0
such that for all
k\geq 1
, it holds that

(a)

f(\bar{x}^{k+1})\leq f(\bar{x}^{k})-\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{{\sf L}{\sf G}}{2n}\alpha_{k}^{3}
.

(b)

\|\bar{x}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k+1}\|\leq\alpha_{k}\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{k})\|+\frac{{\sf L}\sqrt{{\sf G}}}{\sqrt{n}}\alpha_{k}^{2}
.

Based on the properties presented above, we are able to apply
Theorem

1
to establish the convergence for the averaged iterates
\{\bar{x}^{k}\}_{k}
. Then, by using
Proposition

10
, we can show that each agent converges consensually to the stationary point of
f
. Importantly, this convergence result holds without requiring a unique or isolated stationary point.

Theorem 12

(Convergence of
\mathsf{DGD}
)
.

Let
Assumption

4
and
5
hold and let
\{x_{i}^{k}\}_{k\geq 1,i\in[n]}
be generated by
\mathsf{DGD}
with the step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying
(
13
) and

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\Big({\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3}\Big)^{\mu}<\infty\quad\text{for some }\mu\in(0,1).

(14)

Then,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x^{k}_{i})\|=0
and
\{f(x_{i}^{k})\}_{k}
converges to some
f^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
i\in[n]
. Moreover, if
Assumption

2
holds, then
\{x_{i}^{k}\}_{k}
either converges to some stationary point of
f
consensually or we have
\|x_{i}^{k}\|\to\infty
for all
i\in[n]
.

Proof.

Clearly, all conditions in
Proposition

10
and
11
are satisfied.
Using
Proposition

11
, we verify
(A.1)
–
(A.2)
with

I_{k}=k,\quad\beta_{k}=\alpha_{k},\quad p_{k}={\textstyle\frac{{\sf L}{\sf G}}{2n}}\cdot\alpha_{k}^{3},\quad q_{k}={\textstyle\frac{{\sf L}\sqrt{{\sf G}}}{\sqrt{n}}}\cdot\alpha_{k}^{2},\quad a_{1}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad a_{2}=1.

Moreover, the step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfy
(A.3)
–
(A.4)
. It follows from

1
(a) that
\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{k})\|\to 0
and

\|\nabla f(x^{k}_{i})\|\leq\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{k})\|+{\sf L}\|x^{k}_{i}-\bar{x}^{k}\|

\displaystyle|f(\bar{x}^{k})-f(x_{i}^{k})|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2{\sf L}}\max\{\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2},\|\nabla f(x_{i}^{k})\|^{2}\}+{\sf L}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2},

where the last line is due to
\|a\|\cdot\|b\|\leq\frac{1}{2{\sf L}}\|a\|^{2}+\frac{{\sf L}}{2}\|b\|^{2}

for all

.

2

Theorem

1
(b) implies that
\{\bar{x}^{k}\}_{k}

of

4.3

The concept of federated learning was first proposed by McMahan et al.

5
Conclusion

We propose a novel KL-based analysis framework that is applicable to algorithms that do not necessarily possess a sufficient descent property. This framework allows us to study a broader class of optimization methods, such as stochastic and distributed algorithms. Leveraging the framework, we provide new convergence results for decentralized gradient (
\mathsf{DGD}
) and federated averaging (
\mathsf{FedAvg}
) methods and recover existing results for
\mathsf{SGD}
and random reshuffling (
\mathsf{RR}
) without requiring an a priori boundedness condition on the iterates.

As an additional by-product, we also provide a stream-lined way to quantify the convergence rates of an algorithm if it and the utilized step sizes fit in a specialized form of our proposed framework. This allows providing new rates for the function values and gradient norms for
\mathsf{RR}
. In addition, this specialized framework facilitates the derivation of convergence rates for the iterates and gradient norms for
\mathsf{DGD}
and
\mathsf{FedAvg}
. These results appear to be new – even if we would work with a significantly more restrictive, global Polyak-Łojasiewicz assumption.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\{y_{k}\}_{k}
be a non-negative sequence and let
\varsigma\geq 0
,
d,p,q>0
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
t>s
be given constants.

(a)

Suppose that the sequence
\{y_{k}\}_{k}
satisfies

y_{k+1}\leq\Big(1-\frac{q}{k+\varsigma}\Big)y_{k}+\frac{d}{(k+\varsigma)^{p+1}},\quad\forall\ k\geq 1.

Then, if

, it holds that
y_{k}\leq\frac{d}{q-p}\cdot(k+\varsigma)^{-p}+o((k+\varsigma)^{-p})
(as
k\to\infty
).

(b)

Suppose that
\{y_{k}\}_{k}
satisfies the recursion

y_{k+1}\leq\Big(1-\frac{q}{(k+\varsigma)^{s}}\Big)y_{k}+\frac{d}{(k+\varsigma)^{t}},\quad\forall~k\geq 1.

Then, it follows
y_{k}\leq\frac{d}{q}\cdot(k+\varsigma)^{s-t}+o((k+\varsigma)^{s-t})
(as
k\to\infty
).

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\theta\in[0,1)
be given and let
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
be defined via

\alpha_{k}=\frac{\alpha}{(k+\varsigma)^{\gamma}},\quad\alpha>0,\quad\varsigma\geq 0,\quad\gamma\in(1/p,1].

(a)

For all
k\geq 1
, we have

{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{p}\leq\frac{a_{\gamma}}{(k+\varsigma)^{p\gamma-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{k}\left[{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{p}\right]^{2\theta}\leq\frac{a_{\gamma}}{(k+\varsigma)^{(1+2p\theta)\gamma-2\theta}},

(36)

where
a_{\gamma}>0
is a numerical constant depending on
\alpha
and
\gamma
.

(b)

Moreover, if
\gamma>\frac{1+\theta}{1+p\theta}
, then it holds that

{\sum}_{t=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{t}\left[{\sum}_{j=t}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{p}\right]^{\theta}\leq\frac{{a}_{\theta}}{(k+\varsigma)^{(1+p\theta)\gamma-(1+\theta)}}\quad\forall~k\geq 1,

(37)

where
{a}_{\theta}>0
is a numerical constant (depending on
\alpha,\gamma,\theta
).

Proof.

Using the integral test and noting that
p\gamma>1
, we obtain

{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{p}={\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\;\frac{\alpha^{p}}{(j+\varsigma)^{p\gamma}}\leq\frac{\alpha^{p}}{(k+\varsigma)^{p\gamma}}+\int_{k}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{p}}{(x+\varsigma)^{p\gamma}}\;\textup{{d}}x\leq\frac{p\gamma\alpha^{p}}{p\gamma-1}\cdot\frac{1}{(k+\varsigma)^{p\gamma-1}}.

In addition, it follows
\alpha_{k}[{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{p}]^{2\theta}\leq\alpha^{1+2p\theta}(\frac{p\gamma}{p\gamma-1})^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(k+\varsigma)^{2\theta(p\gamma-1)+\gamma}}
. Note that
\alpha^{1+2p\theta}\leq(1+\alpha)^{1+2p}
, which completes the proof of the statement (a). We refer to
[
22
, Lemma 3.7]
for the proof of statement (b).
∎

Lemma B.3

We introduce Gronwall’s inequality (cf.

Lemma B.4

(Gronwall’s Inequality)
.

Let
\{a_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
be given sequences. Suppose that we have
y_{t+1}\leq s+r\sum_{j=0}^{t}a_{j}y_{j}
for all
t
and some
s,r\geq 0
.
Then, it holds that
y_{t+1}\leq s\cdot\exp({r\sum_{j=0}^{t}a_{j}})
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof of
Lemma

B.3
.
When
i=0
, the bound in
Lemma

B.3
holds trivially. It holds for any integer
i\in[1,t]
that

\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|\leq{\sf L}\|x^{k+i}-x^{k}\|.

(38)

According to the condition
(R.2)
and
{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\;\alpha_{k+j}\leq 1/(3c{\sf L})
, we may bound the term
\|x^{k+i}-x^{k}\|
as:

\displaystyle\|x^{k+i}-x^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq c{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}\|\nabla f(x^{k+j})\|+c{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq(3{\sf L})^{-1}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+c\;{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}\|\nabla f(x^{k+j})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|+c\;{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q}.

Combining estimates (
38
) and (
39
), then

\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|\leq c{\sf L}\,{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\,\alpha_{k+j}\|\nabla f(x^{k+j})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|+\tfrac{1}{3}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+c{\sf L}\,{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q}.

Invoking Gronwall’s inequality (
Lemma

B.4
) upon setting

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{1}{3}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+c{\sf L}\,{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q},\quad r:=c{\sf L},\quad a_{j}:=\alpha_{k+j},\quad y_{j}:=\|\nabla f(x^{k+j})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|

and utilizing

, we obtain

\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|\leq\exp(1/3)\cdot\big(\tfrac{1}{3}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+c{\sf L}\,{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q}\big).

Noticing that

, we then invoke the triangle inequality and the above estimate to yield

\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|\leq\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|+\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f(x^{k})\|+\frac{3c{\sf L}}{2}{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q}+\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})\|.

Rearranging the above estimate gives
\|f(x^{k})\|\leq 3c{\sf L}{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q}+2\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})\|
. Taking square of both sides and using
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
, we have
\|f(x^{k})\|^{2}\leq 18c^{2}{\sf L}^{2}({\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q})^{2}+8\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})\|^{2}
. Since
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
is non-increasing, by using
{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\;\alpha_{k+j}\leq 1/(3c{\sf L})

\Big({\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q}\Big)^{2}\leq\Big(\alpha_{k}^{q-1}{\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}\Big)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{9c^{2}{\sf L}^{2}}\cdot\alpha_{k}^{2q-2}.

Merging this estimate into
\|f(x^{k})\|^{2}\leq 18c^{2}{\sf L}^{2}({\sum}_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{k+j}^{q})^{2}+8\|\nabla f(x^{k+i})\|^{2}
completes the proof. ∎

that can maximize

is to set
\vartheta=\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2(p\gamma-1)}
if
\theta\leq\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2(p\gamma-1)}
, and
\vartheta=\theta
, otherwise. In conclusion, we have
\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(k^{-\varphi(\theta,\gamma)})
, where
\varphi(\theta,\gamma):=\max_{\theta\leq\vartheta<1}\phi(\vartheta,\gamma)
, that is,

\displaystyle\varphi(\theta,\gamma):=\begin{cases}\tfrac{(p+1)\gamma}{2}-1&\text{if}\;\theta\in\big[\frac{1}{2},\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2(p\gamma-1)}\big]\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{(1-\gamma)(1-\theta)}{2\theta-1}&\text{if}\;\theta\in\big(\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2(p\gamma-1)},1\big)\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\in(\tfrac{2}{p+2},1),

and
\varphi(\theta,\gamma):=\frac{p-1}{2}
when
\gamma=1,\theta=\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha>\frac{2(p-1)}{b{\sf C}^{2}}
. Alternatively, when
\gamma\in(\tfrac{2}{p+2},1)
, we can express the rates function as
\varphi(\theta,\gamma)=\min\{\tfrac{(p+1)\gamma}{2}-1,\frac{(1-\gamma)(1-\theta)}{2\theta-1}\}
.

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{n(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\alpha_{k}}-Emn\cdot\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{E}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{\pi^{i,j}_{t}}(y_{t,k}^{i,j})-\nabla h^{t}_{\pi^{i,j}_{t}}(x^{k})\|

(62)

\displaystyle\leq{\sf L}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{E}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|y_{t,k}^{i,j}-x^{k}\|\leq E{{\sf L}}m\sum_{t=1}^{n}\max_{i\in[E],j\in[m]}\|y_{t,k}^{i,j}-x^{k}\|=:V_{k},

where the last line is due to Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla h^{t}_{j}(\cdot)
. Based on the update of
\mathsf{FedAvg}
, we unfold
\|y_{t,k}^{i,j}-x^{k}\|
and invoke the triangle inequality again, then the following holds for all client
t\in[n]
,

\|y_{t,k}^{i,j}-x^{k}\|\leq\alpha_{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{E}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{\pi_{t}^{\ell,r}}(y_{t,k}^{\ell,r})\|,\quad\forall\,i\in[E]\;\text{and}\;\forall\,j\in[m].

Based on the definition of
V_{k}
in (
62
) and the above estimate, we can establish

\displaystyle V_{k}

\displaystyle\leq E{\sf L}m\alpha_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{E}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{\pi_{t}^{\ell,r}}(y_{t,k}^{\ell,r})\|

(63)

\displaystyle\leq E{\sf L}m\alpha_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{E}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\big(\|\nabla h^{t}_{\pi_{t}^{\ell,r}}(y_{t,k}^{\ell,r})-\nabla h^{t}_{\pi_{t}^{\ell,r}}(x^{k})\|+\|\nabla h^{t}_{\pi_{t}^{\ell,r}}(x^{k})\|\big)

\displaystyle\leq E{\sf L}m\alpha_{k}V_{k}+E{\sf L}m\alpha_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{E}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{\pi_{t}^{\ell,r}}(x^{k})\|=E{\sf L}m\alpha_{k}V_{k}+E^{2}{\sf L}m\alpha_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{r}(x^{k})\|,

where the second line is due to triangle inequality and the last equation holds because
\{\pi_{t}^{\ell,1},\ldots,\pi_{t}^{\ell,m}\}
is a permutation of
[m]
, indicating
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{\pi_{t}^{\ell,r}}(x^{k})\|
=
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{r}(x^{k})\|
.
Note that
h^{t}_{r}(\cdot)
is
{\sf L}
-Lipschitz smooth and lower bounded by
\bar{f}
for all
t\in[n]
and
r\in[m]
, utilizing (
18
), we have

\|\nabla h^{t}_{r}(x^{k})\|^{2}\leq 2{\sf L}(h^{t}_{r}(x^{k})-\bar{f})\leq 2mn{\sf L}(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\|\nabla h^{t}_{r}(x^{k})\|\leq\sqrt{2mn{\sf L}(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})}.

Since
\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2mE{\sf L}}
, we rearrange (
63
) and apply the bound shown above, then the quantity
V_{k}
is upper bounded by

V_{k}\leq 2E^{2}{\sf L}m\alpha_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|\nabla h^{t}_{r}(x^{k})\|\leq 2E^{2}{\sf L}m^{2}n\alpha_{k}\sqrt{2mn{\sf L}(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})}.

(64)

Using (
62
) and inserting this upper bound of
V_{k}
into (
62
), we obtain

\left\|\frac{n(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\alpha_{k}}-Emn\cdot\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|^{2}\leq V_{k}^{2}\leq 8E^{4}{\sf L}^{3}m^{5}n^{3}(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})\alpha_{k}^{2}.

(65)

It then follows from estimates (
58
) and (
65
) and the condition
\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2mE{\sf L}}
that

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})-\bar{f}

\displaystyle\leq(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})-\frac{Em\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2Emn^{2}}\left\|\frac{n(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\alpha_{k}}-Emn\cdot\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|^{2}

(66)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\underbracket{4E^{3}{\sf L}^{3}m^{4}n}_{=:{\sf H}}\alpha_{k}^{3})(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})-\frac{Em\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{2}.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{3}<\infty
and it holds for all
k\geq 1
that

f(x^{k+1})\leq(1+{\sf H}\alpha_{k}^{3})(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})\leq(f(x^{1})-\bar{f})\cdot{\prod}_{i=1}^{k}(1+{\sf H}\alpha_{i}^{3})\leq(f(x^{1})-\bar{f})\cdot\exp\Big({\sf H}{\sum}_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}^{3}\Big),

we conclude that the sequence
f(x^{k})
is bounded above for all
k\geq 1
. Thus, there exists a constant
{\sf G}>0
such that
{\sf H}(f(x^{k})-\bar{f})\leq{\sf G}
for all
k\geq 1
. It follows from (
64
) and (
66
) that

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{Em\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{2}+{\sf G}\alpha_{k}^{3},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|\leq Em\alpha_{k}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+\frac{\alpha_{k}}{n}\left\|\frac{n(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\alpha_{k}}-Emn\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\hskip 51.6665pt\leq Em\alpha_{k}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+\sqrt{2mE{\sf G}}\cdot\alpha_{k}^{2},

as desired.
∎

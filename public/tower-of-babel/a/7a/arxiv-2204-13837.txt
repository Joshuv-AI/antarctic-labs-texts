Linear stability of black holes with static scalar hair in full Horndeski theories: generic instabilities and surviving models Preprint: YITP-22-41, WUAP-22-03
Masato Minamitsuji, 1 Kazufumi Takahashi, 2 and Shinji Tsujikawa 3

Abstract
In full Horndeski theories, we show that the static and spherically symmetric black hole (BH) solutions with a static scalar field ϕ \phi whose kinetic term X X is nonvanishing on the BH horizon are generically prone to ghost/Laplacian instabilities. We then search for asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions with a vanishing X X on the horizon free from ghost/Laplacian instabilities. We show that models with regular coupling functions of ϕ \phi and X X result in no-hair Schwarzschild BHs in general. On the other hand, the presence of a coupling between the scalar field and the Gauss-Bonnet (GB) term R GB 2 R_{\rm GB}^{2} , even with the coexistence of other regular coupling functions, leads to the realization of asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions without ghost/Laplacian instabilities. Finally, we find that hairy BH solutions in power-law F ⁡ ( R GB 2 ) F(R_{\rm GB}^{2}) gravity are plagued by ghost instabilities. These results imply that the GB coupling of the form ξ ⁡ ( ϕ ) ​ R GB 2 \xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2} plays a prominent role for the existence of asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions free from ghost/Laplacian instabilities.

Introduction

General Relativity (GR) has been tested by numerous experiments
in the Solar System. While gravity can be well described by GR
on the weak gravitational background in our local
Universe

gravitational-wave (GW) astronomy

On the other hand, we also know that the Universe

is the simplest candidate for the source of this acceleration,
i.e., dark energy, the theoretical value of vacuum energy

A simple and robust alternative to GR is provided by

The most general class of scalar-tensor theories with second-order

Assuming that the deviation from GR with a canonical scalar field is controlled by a single coupling constant, we perform consistent expansions of the metric and scalar field in terms of the coupling constant.

\displaystyle\frac{2\phi^{\prime}[4r^{2}(fh)^{3/2}{\cal H}c_{4}(2\phi^{\prime}a_{1}+r\sqrt{fh}\,{\cal H})-2a_{1}^{2}f^{3/2}\sqrt{h}\phi^{\prime}{\cal G}+(a_{1}f^{\prime}+2c_{2}f)r^{2}fh{\cal H}^{2}]}{f^{5/2}h^{3/2}(2{\cal P}_{1}-{\cal F})\mu^{2}}>0\,,

(20)

where
c_{2}
and
c_{4}
are presented
in Appendix
B
.
Since
c_{r1,{\rm even}}^{2}
is the same as

c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}
, only the second propagation
speed squared
c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}

provides an additional stability condition.

For the monopole mode (
\ell=0
), there is no propagation
for the gravitational perturbation
\psi
, while the scalar-field perturbation
\delta\phi

propagates with the same radial
velocity as Eq. (
20
).
For the dipole mode (
\ell=1
), there is
a gauge degree
of freedom for fixing
\delta\phi=0
,
under which the perturbation
\psi
propagates
with the same radial speed squared as Eq. (
20
).

In the limit of large multipoles
\ell
,
the conditions associated with
the squared angular propagation speeds of even-parity
perturbations are
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022

c_{\Omega\pm}^{2}=-B_{1}\pm\sqrt{B_{1}^{2}-B_{2}}>0\,,

(21)

where we present the explicit form of

B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in Appendix
B
.
These conditions are satisfied if

B_{1}^{2}\geq B_{2}>0\quad{\rm and}\quad B_{1}<0\,.

(22)

The conditions (
13
), (
14
), (
15
),
(
17
), (
20
), and (
22
)
ensure the existence of consistent
hyperbolic evolution of perturbations, which are essential for
formulating the well-posed initial value problems.
For the complete proof of the stability of BHs,
as in the case of GR and conventional scalar-tensor theories,
on top of these conditions, we further need to clarify
the absence of mode instabilities and the stability
at the nonlinear level,
which will not be addressed in this paper.

III
Generic instabilities of black holes
with nonvanishing scalar kinetic term on the horizon

In this section, we will show the presence of
ghost or Laplacian instabilities of BHs
for a nonvanishing kinetic term on the BH horizon,

X_{s}\equiv X(r_{s})\neq 0\,,

(23)

where
r_{s}
denotes the radius of the BH horizon.
We assume that
X
is an analytic function of
r

around
r=r_{s}
.
Here, we will focus on the asymptotically Minkowski BHs
and assume that the metric solution contains only
a single horizon, namely the BH event horizon.
Since the metric component
h
vanishes at
r=r_{s}
,

X_{s}
can be finite only when
the derivative of the scalar field
\phi^{\prime}
diverges on the horizon.
In shift-symmetric Horndeski theories
like
G_{4}\supset X

or
G_{4}\supset(-X)^{1/2}

with
G_{2}=\eta X-\Lambda
, where
\eta
and

\Lambda
are constants,
there are some exact non-asymptotically Minkowski BHs
with
X_{s}\neq 0

Rinaldi 2012
;
Anabalon et al. 2014
;
Minamitsuji 2014a
;
Cisterna and Erices 2014
;
Babichev et al. 2017
.

Since the sign of
the scalar-field kinetic term is
X<0
for
r>r_{s}

and
X>0
for
r<r_{s}
, the scalar field is spacelike
outside the horizon and timelike inside the horizon,
respectively. This means that
X
undergoes a sudden
change of the sign across the horizon, so the BH horizon
corresponds to a singular hypersurface.
Then, we can define a BH solution only outside the horizon
in which the scalar field is spacelike.
In Ref.
Minamitsuji et al. 2022
, it was shown that
BH solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0
generically suffer from
either ghost or Laplacian instabilities in the domain
where the solution can exist, and hence
such solutions could not be realistic.
We will show that the same instability problem
persists in full Horndeski theories with analytic
coupling functions when
X_{s}\neq 0
.

III.1
Generic instabilities

We expand the background metric components
around
r=r_{s}
as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(r-r_{s})+f_{2}(r-r_{s})^{2}+\cdots\,,

(24)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{1}(r-r_{s})+h_{2}(r-r_{s})^{2}+\cdots\,,

(25)

where
f_{j}
and
h_{j}
(
j=1,2,3,\cdots
) are constants.
Here and in the following, we focus on the standard case in which
h
and
f

simultaneously approach 0 as
r\to r_{s}
.
Note that
there are some spherically symmetric solutions where
f

does not vanish as
h\to 0
in
specific Lorentz violating scalar-tensor theories
Chagoya and Tasinato 2019
,
but we will not consider such cases in the context of Horndeski theories.
Since both
f
and
h
are positive outside the horizon,
we have
f_{1}>0
and
h_{1}>0
.
We are assuming that
X
is an analytic
function of
r
around the horizon,
so we can expand
X
and
\phi
in the forms

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{s}+X_{1}(r-r_{s})+X_{2}(r-r_{s})^{2}+\cdots\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{s}+\phi_{1}(r-r_{s})^{1/2}+\phi_{2}(r-r_{s})^{3/2}+\cdots\,,

(27)

where
X_{j}
,
\hat{\phi}_{j}
, and
\phi_{s}

are constants, with

X_{s}=-\frac{1}{8}h_{1}\phi_{1}^{2}\,.

(28)

The expansion of the scalar field (
27
) is valid
only outside the horizon (
r>r_{s}
), in which regime

X_{s}<0
for
\phi_{1}\neq 0
.
As we already mentioned, the BH solution can be defined
only in the domain outside the horizon.
Hence, it is enough to show instabilities only outside
the horizon in order to exclude these BH solutions.
Since the expansion of the scalar field (
27
)
is valid for
X_{s}\neq 0
, in this section we focus on
the solution with
X_{s}<0
.

In the vicinity of
r=r_{s}
, the left-hand sides of the background equations (
5
)–(
7
)
reduce, respectively, to

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{tt}

\displaystyle=G_{2}-2X_{s}G_{3,\phi}+\frac{2(1-h_{1}r_{s})}{r_{s}^{2}}G_{4}+\frac{4h_{1}X_{s}}{r_{s}}G_{4,X}+4X_{s}G_{4,\phi\phi}-\frac{2(1+h_{1}r_{s})X_{s}}{r_{s}^{2}}G_{5,\phi}+\mathcal{O}((r-r_{s})^{1/2})\,,

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{rr}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-2h_{1}X_{s}}\left(-X_{s}G_{3,X}+G_{4,\phi}+2X_{s}G_{4,\phi X}-\frac{X_{s}}{r_{s}^{2}}G_{5,X}\right)(r-r_{s})^{-1/2}+\mathcal{O}((r-r_{s})^{0})\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{-h_{1}X_{s}}{2}}\left(2G_{4,\phi}-4X_{s}G_{4,\phi X}+\frac{h_{1}X_{s}}{r_{s}}G_{5,X}+2X_{s}G_{5,\phi\phi}\right)(r-r_{s})^{-1/2}+\mathcal{O}((r-r_{s})^{0})\,,

(31)

where the coupling functions
G_{2,3,4,5}
and their derivatives
should be evaluated on the horizon.
Then, the leading-order terms obey
the following relations:

\displaystyle G_{2}-2X_{s}G_{3,\phi}+\frac{2(1-h_{1}r_{s})}{r_{s}^{2}}G_{4}+\frac{4h_{1}X_{s}}{r_{s}}G_{4,X}+4X_{s}G_{4,\phi\phi}-\frac{2(1+h_{1}r_{s})X_{s}}{r_{s}^{2}}G_{5,\phi}=0\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\sqrt{-h_{1}X_{s}}\left(-X_{s}G_{3,X}+G_{4,\phi}+2X_{s}G_{4,\phi X}-\frac{X_{s}}{r_{s}^{2}}G_{5,X}\right)=0\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\sqrt{-h_{1}X_{s}}\left(2G_{4,\phi}-4X_{s}G_{4,\phi X}+\frac{h_{1}X_{s}}{r_{s}}G_{5,X}+2X_{s}G_{5,\phi\phi}\right)=0\,.

(34)

For given functions
G_{2,3,4,5}
, the values
of
h_{1}
,
\phi_{s}
, and
X_{s}
are
fixed by
solving Eqs. (
32
)–(
34
) in general.
Since we are now interested in
solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0
, we will focus on the
case in which the terms inside the parentheses of
Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
) vanish.
Depending on the coupling functions
G_{3,4,5}
, there are specific
cases in which the left-hand sides of Eqs. (
33
) or
(
34
) vanish identically.
In such cases, we need to compute their next-to-leading-order
terms. For example, the next-order contributions to

\mathcal{E}_{rr}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{rr}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r_{s}^{2}}\{G_{2}r_{s}^{2}+2G_{4}(1-h_{1}r_{s})+2X_{s}[G_{5,\phi}-3G_{5,\phi}h_{1}r_{s}-(G_{2,X}-G_{3,\phi}-2G_{4,\phi\phi})r_{s}^{2}-2G_{4,X}(1-2h_{1}r_{s})]

(35)

\displaystyle-4X_{s}^{2}[G_{5,\phi X}-(2G_{4,XX}-G_{5,\phi X})h_{1}r_{s}+(G_{3,\phi X}-2G_{4,\phi\phi X})r_{s}^{2}]\}\,.

Later, we will consider specific theories in which
the equation
\mathcal{E}_{rr}^{(2)}=0
needs to be used.

Around the BH horizon, the quantities in Eqs. (
13
)–(
15
) are expanded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=2(G_{4}-X_{s}G_{5,\phi})+\mathcal{O}((r-r_{s})^{1/2})\,,

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2h_{1}}(-X_{s})^{3/2}G_{5,X}(r-r_{s})^{-1/2}+2(G_{4}-2X_{s}G_{4,X}+X_{s}G_{5,\phi}-2X_{s}^{2}G_{5,\phi X})+\mathcal{O}((r-r_{s})^{1/2})\,,

(37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=2(G_{4}-2X_{s}G_{4,X}+X_{s}G_{5,\phi})+\mathcal{O}((r-r_{s})^{1/2})\,.

(38)

Provided that
G_{5,X}(\phi_{s},X_{s})\neq 0
, we have
c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}=c_{r1,{\rm even}}^{2}={\cal G}/{\cal F}\to\infty

in the limit of
r\to r_{s}
.
Since this signals the strong coupling,
we require the
condition

G_{5,X}(\phi_{s},X_{s})=0\,,

(39)

to realize finite values of

c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}
and
c_{r1,{\rm even}}^{2}
.
We exploit the condition (
39
)
in the following discussion.
We note that the unusual divergence of
\mathcal{G}
for

G_{5,X}(\phi_{s},X_{s})\neq 0
arises from
the assumption of

X_{s}\neq 0

with the scalar field expansion (
27
).

For the computation of
c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}
,
we resort to the expansions (
24
)–(
27
) around
r=r_{s}

as well as the leading-order background
Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
) with
X_{s}\neq 0

to eliminate the terms
G_{4,\phi X}(\phi_{s},X_{s})

and
G_{5,\phi\phi}(\phi_{s},X_{s})
.
Then, the radial propagation speed squared
of the scalar field perturbation
\delta\phi
reads

c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}=\frac{2h_{1}X_{s}\kappa_{r}}{\zeta_{r}(r-r_{s})}+{\cal O}((r-r_{s})^{0})\,,

(40)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle X_{s}r_{s}^{2}(2X_{s}G_{3,XX}-G_{3,X})+r_{s}^{2}(3G_{4,\phi}-4X_{s}^{2}G_{4,\phi XX})+2X_{s}^{2}G_{5,XX}\,,

(41)

\displaystyle\zeta_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle X_{s}^{3}[8r_{s}^{2}(2G_{4,\phi\phi\phi X}-G_{3,\phi\phi X})+16h_{1}r_{s}(2G_{4,\phi XX}-G_{5,\phi\phi X})-4h_{1}^{2}G_{5,XX}-8G_{5,\phi\phi X}]

(42)

\displaystyle+X_{s}^{2}\{[2h_{1}X_{1}(G_{3,XX}-2G_{4,\phi XX})+8G_{4,\phi\phi\phi}]r_{s}^{2}-8h_{1}r_{s}G_{3,X}+2X_{1}h_{1}G_{5,XX}\}

\displaystyle-X_{s}h_{1}r_{s}(G_{3,X}X_{1}r_{s}-24G_{4,\phi})+3h_{1}r_{s}^{2}X_{1}G_{4,\phi}\,.

The product
{\cal K}c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}
is
expanded as

{\cal K}c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{-2X_{s}}h_{1}^{3/2}r_{s}^{2}(G_{4}-2X_{s}G_{4,X}+G_{5,\phi}X_{s})^{2}\kappa_{r}}{2\zeta^{2}(r-r_{s})^{3/2}}+{\cal O}((r-r_{s})^{-1/2})\,,

(43)

where

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2G_{3,\phi X}X_{s}^{2}r_{s}^{2}+r_{s}(G_{4}h_{1}-2G_{4,\phi\phi}X_{s}r_{s}-4G_{4,X}X_{s}h_{1}-4G_{4,XX}X_{s}^{2}h_{1}-4G_{4,\phi\phi X}X_{s}^{2}r_{s})

(44)

\displaystyle+X_{s}[3G_{5,\phi}h_{1}r_{s}+2G_{5,\phi X}X_{s}(1+h_{1}r_{s})]\,.

The necessary condition for avoiding ghost
or Laplacian instabilities of
even-parity perturbations is that
the leading-order contribution to

{\cal K}c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}
is positive.
Since the term
G_{4}-2X_{s}G_{4,X}+G_{5,\phi}X_{s}

in Eq. (
43
) corresponds to the leading-order term
of
{\cal H}/2
on the horizon, we require the condition
G_{4}-2X_{s}G_{4,X}+G_{5,\phi}X_{s}>0
.
Then, the positivity of
{\cal K}c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}

amounts to the inequality
\kappa_{r}>0
,
under which there is the divergence of

c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}
on the horizon,
which signals the strong coupling problem.
For
\kappa_{r}<0
, there is either ghost or
Laplacian instability along the radial direction.
Then, so long as

\kappa_{r}\neq 0

on the horizon, we encounter either strong coupling or
ghost/Laplacian instability of even-parity perturbations.
If
\kappa_{r}=0
, then the leading-order contribution to

c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}
in Eq. (
40
) vanishes,
in which case it may be possible to avoid the
strong coupling problem.
Even in this case, however, we further require that the
next-to-leading-order terms of
Eqs. (
40
) and (
43
)
are both positive for the absence of ghost
and Laplacian instabilities.

Around
r=r_{s}
, the product
{\cal F}{\cal K}B_{2}

can be expanded as

{\cal F}{\cal K}B_{2}=-\frac{4h_{1}^{2}X_{s}^{4}r_{s}^{4}\kappa^{2}}{\zeta^{2}(r-r_{s})^{2}}+{\cal O}((r-r_{s})^{-1})\,,

(45)

where

\kappa\equiv G_{4}G_{4,XX}+G_{4,X}^{2}-G_{5,\phi X}(G_{4}-X_{s}G_{4,X})-G_{5,\phi}(2G_{4,X}+X_{s}G_{4,XX}-G_{5,\phi})\,.

(46)

As long as

\kappa\neq 0

on the horizon, the leading-order term of
Eq. (
45
) is negative, i.e.,

{\cal F}{\cal K}B_{2}<0\quad{\rm as}\quad r\to r_{s}\,.

(47)

Since one of the quantities
{\cal F}
,
{\cal K}
,
and
B_{2}
must be negative, we cannot avoid either
ghost or Laplacian instabilities.
The angular propagation of even-parity perturbations
plays a crucial role for reaching the conclusion of
generic instabilities of BH solutions with

X_{s}\neq 0
and
\kappa\neq 0
.
For
\kappa=0
, there is a possibility for avoiding the
above instability,
in which case the next-to-leading-order
term of the product
{\cal F}{\cal K}B_{2}
must be positive.

In summary, as long as
\kappa_{r}\neq 0
or
\kappa\neq 0
,
the BH solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0
in Horndeski theories
having analytic coupling functions
are subject to either
intrinsic ghost/Laplacian instabilities
or strong coupling problems.

III.2
Concrete models having BH solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0

Let us proceed to the discussion of concrete models
giving rise to BH solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0
.
In such cases, the BH solutions
with
X_{s}\neq 0
are excluded by the
instability around the horizon.

We first discuss the following example in the
framework of shift-symmetric Horndeski theories:

G_{2}=\eta X\,,\qquad G_{3}=\alpha_{3}X\,,\qquad G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+\alpha_{4}X\,,\qquad G_{5}=\alpha_{5}X\,,

(48)

where
\eta
and
\alpha_{3,4,5}
are nonvanishing
constants and
M_{\rm Pl}
is the reduced Planck mass.
If all
\alpha_{3}
,
\alpha_{4}
, and
\alpha_{5}
are nonzero,
there is no solution with
X_{s}\neq 0
for Eqs. (
32
)–(
34
).
The same conclusion persists if either

\alpha_{3}
or
\alpha_{5}
is vanishing.
On the other hand, if both
\alpha_{3}
and
\alpha_{5}

are zero in Eq. (
48
), the left-hand sides of
Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
) vanish
identically, so we exploit the next-to-leading-order
equation
\mathcal{E}_{rr}^{(2)}=0
together with
Eq. (
32
).
Then, there is the following solution:

X_{s}=\frac{\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r_{s}^{2}}{4\alpha_{4}(\eta r_{s}^{2}+2\alpha_{4})}\,,\qquad h_{1}=\frac{\eta r_{s}^{2}+2\alpha_{4}}{2\alpha_{4}r_{s}}\,,

(49)

which corresponds to non-asymptotically Minkowski hairy
BHs studied in Refs.
Rinaldi 2012
;
Anabalon et al. 2014
;
Minamitsuji 2014a
;
Cisterna and Erices 2014
.
Since the coupling functions
G_{2}
and
G_{4}

are both analytic functions of
X
, we can resort to the
analytic expansion of
X
used in Eq. (
26
)
around the horizon.
The quantities
\kappa_{r}
and
\kappa
are given,
respectively, by

\kappa_{r}=0\,,\qquad\kappa=\alpha_{4}^{2}\,.

(50)

As long as
\eta\neq 0
and
\alpha_{4}\neq 0
,
we have
X_{s}\neq 0
and
\kappa\neq 0
.
Then, this solution inevitably suffers from the
ghost or Laplacian instability around the horizon.
This conclusion agrees with what was found for
shift- and reflection-symmetric Horndeski theories containing
the functional dependence
G_{2}(X)

and
G_{4}(X)
with
G_{3}=G_{5}=0

Minamitsuji et al. 2022
.

As our second example, let us consider
the following model:

G_{2}=\eta X\,,\qquad G_{3}=\alpha_{3}X\,,\qquad G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+\alpha_{4}\phi^{2}+\beta_{4}\phi^{2}X\,,\qquad G_{5}=\alpha_{5}\phi^{2}\,,

(51)

where
\eta
,
\alpha_{3,4,5}
, and
\beta_{4}

are nonzero constants. We note that all the coupling functions
in Eq. (
51
) are analytic functions of

\phi
and
X
.
Solving the leading-order background equations for

X_{s}
,
\phi_{s}
, and
h_{1}
,
we find that there is the solution

X_{s}=\frac{\alpha_{4}(\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{5})}{\beta_{4}(\alpha_{3}-6\alpha_{5})}\,,\qquad\phi_{s}=\frac{\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{5}}{8\beta_{4}}\,,

(52)

which exists for
\beta_{4}\neq 0
and

\alpha_{3}-6\alpha_{5}\neq 0
.
Here, the explicit form of
h_{1}
is
not shown due to its complexity.
For this solution, the quantities
\kappa_{r}

and
\kappa
reduce, respectively, to

\kappa_{r}=\frac{\alpha_{4}(\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{5})r_{s}^{2}}{2\beta_{4}}\,,\qquad\kappa=\frac{(\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{5})^{2}(\alpha_{3}-14\alpha_{5})^{2}}{4096\beta_{4}^{2}}\,.

(53)

The existence of the
solution with
X_{s}\neq 0
requires
that
\alpha_{4}(\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{5})\neq 0
, under
which
\kappa_{r}\neq 0
.
Also, we have
\kappa\neq 0
unless
\alpha_{3}=14\alpha_{5}
.
We note that, even when
\alpha_{5}=0
,
there are solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0
plagued by
the instability problem.

IV
Theories with no-hair Schwarzschild black holes

Given the existence of instabilities for the BH solutions
with
X_{s}\neq 0
, we are now interested in
solutions with
X_{s}=0
.
For example, the models (
48
) and (
51
)
give rise to the branch
X_{s}=0
besides the branch
X_{s}\neq 0

discussed in Sec.
III
.
For the solution with
X_{s}=0
, there are in general
two possibilities.
One is a trivial field profile with

\phi(r)=\phi_{s}={\rm constant}
at any radius.
The other is a hairy solution where
the scalar field varies
as a function of
r
. For this hairy solution
with
X_{s}=0
, the scalar field regular around the horizon
can be expanded as

\phi(r)=\phi_{s}+\phi_{1}(r-r_{s})+\phi_{2}(r-r_{s})^{2}+\cdots\,.

(54)

Since this is different from Eq. (
27
),
we need to handle this case separately for the discussion of
ghost/Laplacian instabilities.
Furthermore, the expansion (
54
) is insufficient
to ensure the BH stability
throughout the horizon exterior.
Thus, in this and subsequent sections,
we will derive perturbative
BH solutions with respect to a small parameter
arising from the coupling functions
G_{2,3,4,5}

and explore the issue of ghost/Laplacian instabilities
of BHs in the region outside the horizon.

In the horizon limit
r\to r_{s}
, the hairy BH solutions
with
X_{s}=0
should satisfy the following boundary conditions:

f(r)\to 0\,,\quad h(r)\to 0\quad\text{with}\quad\frac{h(r)}{f(r)}\to{\rm finite}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad|\phi^{\prime}(r)|\to|\phi_{1}|<\infty\,.

(55)

On the other hand, at spatial infinity
r\to\infty
,
we impose that the metric is asymptotically Minkowski and the scalar-field derivative vanishes, i.e.,

\displaystyle f(r)\to{\rm constant}\,,\quad h(r)\to 1\,,\quad\phi^{\prime}(r)\to 0\,.

(56)

This means that the scalar field
\phi(r)
approaches a constant value. Here, we do not impose that the asymptotic constant value of the scalar field is a specific value, e.g., zero,
but we allow an arbitrary constant value
as long as it does not conflict with the asymptotically Minkowski metric.
We will construct hairy BH solutions under the boundary
conditions (
55
) and (
56
).

Now, we search for the possibility of
BH solutions with
X_{s}=0
which are not
prone to the generic problem of ghost/Laplacian instabilities found in the previous section.
We recall that the scalar-field equation of motion is given by Eq. (
9
).
Outside the horizon, the solution to Eq. (
9
) can be expressed in an integrated form

J^{r}=\frac{Q}{r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\int_{r_{s}}^{r}r^{2}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}P_{\phi}\,{\rm d}r\,,

(57)

where
Q
is an integration constant and
J^{r}
is the radial current component defined in Eq. (
10
), which we repeat here for the reader’s convenience:

\displaystyle J^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\phi^{\prime}\biggl[G_{2,X}-\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\right)h\phi^{\prime}G_{3,X}+2\left(\frac{1-h}{r^{2}}-\frac{hf^{\prime}}{rf}\right)G_{4,X}+2h\phi^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{h}{r^{2}}+\frac{hf^{\prime}}{rf}\right)G_{4,XX}

(58)

\displaystyle-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2r^{2}f}(1-3h)h\phi^{\prime}G_{5,X}-\frac{f^{\prime}h^{3}\phi^{\prime 3}}{2r^{2}f}G_{5,XX}\biggr]\,.

In shift-symmetric Horndeski theories, we have
P_{\phi}=0
,
and hence the second term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
57
) vanishes. In non-shift-symmetric Horndeski theories containing the
\phi
-dependence in
G_{2,3,4,5}
, the integral containing
P_{\phi}
contributes to
J^{r}
.
Throughout the discussion below, we include the kinetic term
\eta X
in
G_{2}
and the Einstein-Hilbert term
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}/2
in
G_{4}
, i.e.,

G_{2}\supset\eta X\,,\qquad G_{4}\supset\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}\,,

(59)

with
\eta
being a constant.

IV.1
Shift-symmetric theories

In shift-symmetric theories, the radial current component
is given by

J^{r}=\frac{Q}{r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\,.

(60)

Let us first consider
the simplest case of GR with a linear kinetic term of the scalar field, i.e.,

G_{2}=\eta X\,,\qquad G_{3}=0\,,\qquad G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}\,,\qquad G_{5}=0\,.

(61)

Since
J^{r}=\eta h\phi^{\prime}
in this case,
the scalar-field derivative is given by

\phi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{Q}{\eta r^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{fh}}\,.

(62)

As
r
approaches the horizon radius
r_{s}
,
there is the divergence of
\phi^{\prime}
.
To avoid this behavior, we require that
Q=0
and hence

\phi^{\prime}(r)=0\,,

(63)

which corresponds to a no-hair solution.

We can generalize the above argument to more general theories
where the coupling functions
G_{2,3,4,5}
are analytic functions of
X
, i.e.,

G_{I}(X)=\sum_{p\geq 0}(\alpha_{I})_{p}(-X)^{p}\qquad(I=2,3,4,5)\,,

(64)

where
(\alpha_{I})_{p}
are constants and
the sum is taken over all integers
p\geq 0
.
Taking into account the terms in Eq. (
59
),
we consider the following coupling functions:

\begin{split}&G_{2}=\eta X+\sum_{p_{2}\geq 2}(\alpha_{2})_{p_{2}}(-X)^{p_{2}}\,,\qquad G_{3}=\sum_{p_{3}\geq 1}(\alpha_{3})_{p_{3}}(-X)^{p_{3}}\,,\\
&G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{p_{4}\geq 1}(\alpha_{4})_{p_{4}}(-X)^{p_{4}}\,,\qquad G_{5}=\sum_{p_{5}\geq 1}(\alpha_{5})_{p_{5}}(-X)^{p_{5}}\,.\end{split}

(65)

We drop constant terms in each
G_{I}
apart from
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}/2

in
G_{4}
, as they are irrelevant to the existence of asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions.
Using the expansions (
24
) and (
25
)
around the horizon, it follows that the metric functions
f
,
h
, and their derivatives do not cause
divergences for the terms appearing in the
square brackets in Eq. (
58
).
This is also the case at spatial infinity
where
f
and
h
approach
constants with
\phi^{\prime}(r)\to 0
for asymptotically Minkowski solutions.
The radial current component (
58
) can be written in the form
J^{r}=h\phi^{\prime}[\eta+F(\phi^{\prime})]
, where
F(\phi^{\prime})
is a regular power-law function of
\phi^{\prime}
containing only positive powers.
Note that the constant terms arising from
the other parts of the coupling
functions [i.e.,
\sum(\alpha_{I})_{p_{I}}(-X)^{p_{I}}
]
have been absorbed into
\eta
, and hence
F(0)=0
.
We can rewrite
J^{r}=(Q/r^{2})\sqrt{h/f}
in the form

h=\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}[\eta+F(\phi^{\prime})]}\frac{Q}{r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\,.

(66)

To realize
h=0
on the horizon for a finite or vanishing
value of
\phi^{\prime}
, we require that
Q=0
. Then, we obtain

J^{r}=h\phi^{\prime}[\eta+F(\phi^{\prime})]=0\,.

(67)

Under the boundary condition
\phi^{\prime}(\infty)=0
,
the function
F(\phi^{\prime})
approaches zero
at spatial infinity.
Then, so long as
\eta\neq 0
, we have to choose
the branch
\phi^{\prime}(r)=0
.
This means that, for theories with the coupling functions of the form (
65
), we end up with no-hair BH solutions.
This fact was already recognized in Ref.
Hui and Nicolis 2013
.

In the above discussion, the main reason for reaching
the no-hair conclusion is that the dominant contribution to

J^{r}
in the limit
\phi^{\prime}\to 0
is the linear term in
\phi^{\prime}
.
This behavior can be avoided by considering the following
nonanalytic coupling functions
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014a
;
Babichev et al. 2017
:

G_{2}=\eta X+\alpha_{2}\sqrt{-X}\,,\qquad G_{3}=\alpha_{3}\ln|X|\,,\qquad G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+\alpha_{4}\sqrt{-X}\,,\qquad G_{5}=\alpha_{5}\ln|X|\,,

(68)

with
\alpha_{2,3,4,5}
being constants.
For
\phi^{\prime}>0
, we have

J^{r}=\eta h\phi^{\prime}-\sqrt{\frac{h}{2}}\alpha_{2}+\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}+\frac{4}{r}\right)h\alpha_{3}-\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{r^{2}}\alpha_{4}-\frac{f^{\prime}h(h-1)}{fr^{2}}\alpha_{5}\,.

(69)

Apart from the first term, there are
no
\phi^{\prime}
-dependent terms.
For any coupling functions with stronger divergence as
X\to 0
than the choice (
68
),
J^{r}
diverges as
\phi^{\prime}\to 0
.
In such theories, we cannot take the proper Minkowski limit at large
distances
Creminelli et al. 2020
.
In this sense, the choice of the coupling functions (
68
)
is unique for the realization of hairy BH solutions
in shift-symmetric Horndeski theories.
From Eq. (
60
), the scalar-field derivative is now expressed as

\phi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\eta h}\left[\sqrt{\frac{h}{2}}\alpha_{2}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}+\frac{4}{r}\right)h\alpha_{3}+\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{r^{2}}\alpha_{4}+\frac{f^{\prime}h(h-1)}{fr^{2}}\alpha_{5}+\frac{Q}{r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\right]\,.

(70)

The terms associated with
\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{4}
are
dominated over the term
(Q/r^{2})\sqrt{h/f}
around the horizon.

In the case of
\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{5}=0
,
to avoid the divergence of
\phi^{\prime}

induced by the term
(Q/r^{2})\sqrt{h/f}

for
\alpha_{2}\neq 0
or
\alpha_{4}\neq 0
,
we need to set
Q=0
.
However, even under
Q=0
,
\phi^{\prime}(r)
still diverges on the horizon.
Indeed, the scalar-field kinetic term
has the following dependence:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{4\eta^{2}}\qquad(\alpha_{2}\neq 0,~\alpha_{4}=0)\,,

(71)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{4}^{2}}{\eta^{2}r^{4}}\qquad(\alpha_{4}\neq 0,~\alpha_{2}=0)\,.

(72)

In both cases,

X
is an analytic function of
r

which is nonvanishing at
r=r_{s}
. Hence, these BH solutions
are excluded by the intrinsic instability problem discussed in Sec.
III
.

In the case of
\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{4}=0
,
the terms associated with

\alpha_{3}
and
\alpha_{5}
as well as the term
(Q/r^{2})\sqrt{h/f}
in the square brackets in Eq. (
70
)
approach constants as
r\to r_{s}
.
In such cases, we can choose

Q
such that
\phi^{\prime}
becomes
regular on the horizon.
Using the expansions (
24
) and (
25
),
the values of
Q
and
\phi^{\prime}
at
r=r_{s}

are given by

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}\sqrt{f_{1}h_{1}}r_{s}^{2}\,,\qquad\phi^{\prime}(r_{s})=-\frac{\alpha_{3}(f_{1}h_{2}r_{s}+3f_{2}h_{1}r_{s}+12f_{1}h_{1})}{2\eta f_{1}h_{1}r_{s}}\qquad(\alpha_{3}\neq 0,~\alpha_{5}=0)\,,

(73)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{5}\sqrt{f_{1}h_{1}}\,,\qquad\phi^{\prime}(r_{s})=\frac{\alpha_{5}(2f_{1}h_{1}^{2}-f_{1}h_{2}-3f_{2}h_{1})}{2\eta f_{1}h_{1}r_{s}^{2}}\qquad(\alpha_{5}\neq 0,~\alpha_{3}=0)\,.

(74)

In both cases, the scalar-field derivative is finite on the horizon
and hence
X_{s}=0
.
As we will show in Sec.
IV.3
,
the BHs present for the cubic coupling
case (
73
)
are not asymptotically Minkowski.
On the other hand, the quintic coupling
case (
74
) realizes
asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions.
Indeed, this is equivalent to the
scalar field linearly coupled to the Gauss-Bonnet term studied in
Refs.
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014a
;
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014b
.

IV.2
Non-shift-symmetric theories

Let us investigate the possibility for realizing hairy BH
solutions in non-shift-symmetric theories containing the
dependence of
\phi
as well as
X
in
G_{2,3,4,5}
.
For the
X
-dependent part of the couplings, we take one
of the powers
(-X)^{p_{I}}
in Eq. (
65
)
for each
I=2,3,4,5
for simplicity.
Multiplying such terms with
\phi
-dependent analytic
functions
\alpha_{I}(\phi)
,
we can consider the following couplings:

G_{2}=\eta X+\alpha_{2}(\phi)(-X)^{p_{2}}\,,\qquad G_{3}=\alpha_{3}(\phi)(-X)^{p_{3}}\,,\qquad G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+\alpha_{4}(\phi)(-X)^{p_{4}}\,,\qquad G_{5}=\alpha_{5}(\phi)(-X)^{p_{5}}\,,

(75)

where
p_{2,3,4,5}\geq 0
are integers.
Since
\alpha_{2,3,4,5}(\phi)
are analytic functions
of
\phi
, they can be expanded around
some
\phi_{0}
as

\alpha_{I}(\phi)=\sum_{q_{I}\geq 0}(\alpha_{I})_{q_{I}}\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right)^{q_{I}}\qquad(I=2,3,4,5)\,,

(76)

where
(\alpha_{I})_{q_{I}}
are constants.
For
p_{2,3,4,5}=0
, the purely
\phi
-dependent couplings in

G_{2,3,4,5}
can be accommodated.

Analogous to the discussion in shift-symmetric theories
Minamitsuji et al. 2022
,
the radial current component for the couplings (
75
) can be expressed in the form
J^{r}=h\phi^{\prime}[\eta+\tilde{F}(\phi^{\prime},\phi)]
,
where
\tilde{F}(\phi^{\prime},\phi)
is
an analytic function containing the positive power-law
dependence of
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi
.
Assuming that
P_{\phi}
is finite in the limit of the horizon, which will be confirmed in the perturbative approach later,
the last integral in Eq. (
57
) vanishes as
r\to r_{s}
.
Then, on the horizon, there is the relation

h=\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}_{s}[\eta+\tilde{F}(\phi^{\prime}_{s},\phi_{s})]}\frac{Q}{r_{s}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\,,

(77)

with
\phi^{\prime}_{s}\equiv\phi^{\prime}(r_{s})
.
Since
h
is vanishing at
r=r_{s}
with finite
values of
\phi^{\prime}_{s}
and
\phi_{s}
, we require that
Q=0
.
Then, from Eq. (
57
), we obtain

h\phi^{\prime}[\eta+\tilde{F}(\phi^{\prime},\phi)]=-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\int_{r_{s}}^{r}r^{2}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}P_{\phi}\,{\rm d}r\,.

(78)

Regarding the finiteness of
P_{\phi}
in the horizon limit
r\to r_{s}
, we note that

P_{\phi}
contains the second scalar-field derivative
\phi^{\prime\prime}
as well as the contributions from
f
,
h
, and their derivatives [see Eq. (
11
) with Eq. (
169
)].
This means that we need to integrate Eq. (
9
) together with the other background equations (
5
) and (
6
) to determine the value of
P_{\phi}
.
We will explicitly see this by using
perturbative solutions in the small
coupling regime.

For the purpose of deriving perturbative BH solutions, we write
the coupling functions
\alpha_{I}
(
I=2,3,4,5
)
in Eq. (
75
) as

\alpha_{I}(\phi)=\alpha\tilde{\alpha}_{I}(\phi)\,,

(79)

where
\alpha
is a dimensionless coupling constant
and
\tilde{\alpha}_{2,3,4,5}(\phi)
are analytic
functions of
\phi
.
This ansatz allows us to control the deviation from GR with
a canonical scalar field by a single parameter
\alpha
.
Since we are considering BH solutions with the vanishing kinetic term on the event horizon,

X_{s}=0
,
the scalar-field derivative
\phi^{\prime}(r)
(and the scalar field itself) is finite and regular on the horizon.
We perform the perturbative expansions of
f
,
h
, and
\phi
with respect to
the small coupling constant
\alpha
around the Schwarzschild
background given by the metric components

f=h=1-2m/r
, where
m
is a constant.
Namely, we consider the metric and scalar field given by

\displaystyle f(r)=\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\left[1+\sum_{j\geq 1}\hat{f}_{j}(r)\alpha^{j}\right]^{2}\,,\qquad h(r)=\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\left[1+\sum_{j\geq 1}\hat{h}_{j}(r)\alpha^{j}\right]^{-2}\,,

(80)

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\phi_{0}(r)+\sum_{j\geq 1}\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)\alpha^{j}\,,

(81)

where the coefficients
\hat{f}_{j}
,
\hat{h}_{j}
,
\phi_{0}
,
and
\hat{\phi}_{j}
are functions of
r
.
We note that, for the perturbative
ansatze (
80
) and (
81
),
the horizon distance
r_{s}
is given by
r_{s}=2m
.
We require the validity of the perturbative expansion (
80
) and (
81
) with respect to a
small coupling constant
|\alpha|\ll 1

and impose that the coefficients
\hat{f}_{j}(r)
,
\hat{h}_{j}(r)
, and
\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)
are regular
r=2m
.
Moreover, since
\phi
is a scalar quantity,
its value does not depend on the choice of the coordinates.
If
\phi
is divergent at
r=2m
in the original coordinates,
it would also diverge at the corresponding position in the new coordinates.
On the other hand,
a divergence of the metric at
r=2m
might be
removed by an appropriate coordinate transformation.
However, since the metric and scalar field are coupled in our system,
a choice of the integration constants different from the below would result in the
divergence of the scalar field.
Thus, the constructed perturbative solution (
80
) and (
81
)
indeed exists, irrespective of the choice of the coordinates.
The coupling functions
\tilde{\alpha}_{I}(\phi)
(
I=2,3,4,5
)
can be expanded as

\tilde{\alpha}_{I}(\phi)=\tilde{\alpha}_{I}(\phi_{0})+\sum_{n\geq 1}\tilde{\alpha}^{(n)}_{I}(\phi_{0})\frac{(\phi-\phi_{0})^{n}}{n!}\,,

(82)

where
\tilde{\alpha}^{(n)}_{I}(\phi_{0})\equiv{\rm d}^{n}\tilde{\alpha}_{I}/{\rm d}\phi^{n}|_{\phi=\phi_{0}}
.
We derive the BH solutions perturbatively by using the dimensionless
parameter
\alpha
arising from each coupling
\alpha_{2,3,4,5}
.
By construction, the no-hair argument given below is valid
in the regime of the small coupling constant,
|\alpha|\ll 1
.

In Eqs. (
58
) and (
11
), the zeroth-order terms
in
\alpha
are
J^{r}=\eta h\phi^{\prime}=(1-2m/r)\eta\phi^{\prime}
and
P_{\phi}=0
.
From Eq. (
9
), the zeroth-order scalar field
obeys the differential equation

\left[r^{2}\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\phi_{0}^{\prime}(r)\right]^{\prime}=0\,,

(83)

whose solution is given by

\phi_{0}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{0}+\frac{D_{0}}{2m}\ln\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\,,

(84)

where
\tilde{\phi}_{0}
and
D_{0}
are integration constants.
To avoid the divergent behavior of
\phi_{0}(r)
on the horizon at
r=2m
, we
require that
D_{0}=0
and hence

\phi_{0}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{0}={\rm constant}
.
In the following, we discuss two different cases:
1)
p_{2}\neq 0
and
2)
p_{2}=0
, in turn,
where
p_{2}
is the power
appearing in the coupling function
G_{2}
in Eq. (
75
).

IV.2.1
p_{2}\neq 0

For
p_{2}\neq 0
, the first-order expanded solutions in
\alpha
are given by

\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{1}+\frac{D_{1}}{2m}\ln\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\,,\qquad\hat{h}_{1}(r)=\frac{C_{1}}{r-2m}\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{1}(r)=-\frac{C_{1}}{r-2m}+C_{2}\,,\qquad

(85)

where
D_{1},C_{1},C_{2}
are integration constants.
For the regularity at
r=2m
, we require that

D_{1}=0
and
C_{1}=0
, and we also set
C_{2}=0

by a suitable time reparametrization.
Then, the first-order solution is given by
\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{1}
,

\hat{h}_{1}(r)=0
, and
\hat{f}_{1}(r)=0
.
One can show that the
j
th-order perturbative
solutions (
j\geq 2
) are of the same form
as Eq. (
85
).
Then, the solutions regular on the horizon are

\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{j}\,,\qquad\hat{h}_{j}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{j}(r)=0\,,

(86)

where
\tilde{\phi}_{j}
are constants.
Since these relations hold for all integers
j
, this
corresponds to the no-hair BH solution [i.e.,
\phi(r)={\rm constant}
] with the Schwarzschild metric components
f(r)=h(r)=1-2m/r
.

IV.2.2
p_{2}=0

Let us consider the theories with

p_{2}=0
, i.e., the coupling
\alpha_{2}(\phi)=\alpha\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(\phi)
in
G_{2}
.
The first-order solutions of
h
and
f
regular at
r=2m

are given by

\hat{h}_{1}(r)=-\frac{4m^{2}+2mr+r^{2}}{6M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(\phi_{0})\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{1}(r)=\frac{(2m+r)r}{6M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(\phi_{0})\,,

(87)

where we have set the integration constant
in
\hat{f}_{1}(r)
to be zero.
To realize the asymptotically Minkowski metric, we require that

\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(\phi_{0})=0
.
The first-order solution to the scalar field is given by

\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{1}+\frac{D_{1}}{2m}\ln\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)-\frac{[r^{2}+4mr+8m^{2}\ln(r/2m)]}{6\eta}\tilde{\alpha}_{2}^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\,,

(88)

with
D_{1}
being an integration constant.
We impose
\tilde{\alpha}_{2}^{(1)}(\phi_{0})=0
, as otherwise
the last term leads to the divergence of
\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)

at spatial infinity and the
condition (
56
)
[i.e.,
\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(\infty)=0
]
is not respected.
Then, the regularity of
\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(r)
at
r=2m
requires that

D_{1}=0
, and hence
\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{1}={\rm constant}
.
To avoid the divergences of
\hat{\phi}_{j}^{\prime}(r)
,
\hat{h}_{j}(r)
, and
\hat{f}_{j}(r)
at spatial infinity for higher-order
solutions (
j\geq 2
), we require
that
\tilde{\alpha}_{2}^{(n)}(\phi_{0})=0
for all
n~(\geq 1)
.
Hence, we end up with the no hair solution characterized by
\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{j}={\rm constant}

and
\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(\phi)=\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(\phi_{0})=0
.

The above discussion shows that, in theories with
the coupling functions (
75
), the asymptotically Minkowski BH solutions
respecting the regularity on the horizon
are restricted to be no-hair solutions
with
\phi(r)={\rm constant}
.
Substituting
\phi^{\prime}(r)=0
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)=0
into
the expression of
P_{\phi}
given in Eq. (
11
)
and using the property that the
\phi
- and
X
-derivatives
of the couplings
G_{2,3,4,5}

do not contain negative powers of
\phi^{\prime}
,
it follows that
P_{\phi}=G_{2,\phi}+\lambda_{4}G_{4,\phi}
, where
\lambda_{4}

is defined in Eq. (
169
).
Since the background geometry is the Schwarzschild metric,
the quantity
\lambda_{4}
vanishes.
The coupling
\alpha_{2}(\phi)(-X)^{p_{2}}
with

p_{2}=0
gives rise to
\phi
-dependent contributions
in
G_{2,\phi}
, but they vanish due to the property
\tilde{\alpha}_{2}^{(n)}(\phi_{0})=0

derived above.
Then, we have
P_{\phi}=0
, so the right-hand side of
Eq. (
78
) vanishes. Since
\tilde{F}(\phi^{\prime}_{s},\phi_{s})

does not contain negative powers of
\phi^{\prime}
,
the no-hair solution with
\phi^{\prime}(r)=0
is consistent
with Eq. (
78
) everywhere outside the horizon.

The above results show that, for the theories
characterized by the coupling functions (
75
)
with (
76
), there are no asymptotically Minkowski
BHs with scalar hair.
Such theories include couplings of the forms
G_{I}\supset\phi^{q_{I}}(-X)^{p_{I}}

(
I=2,3,4,5
) ,
with integers
q_{I}\geq 0
and
p_{I}\geq 0
.
The no-hair property persists for the product of
two analytic functions
\alpha_{I}(\phi)
and

F_{I}(X)
, i.e.,
G_{I}\supset\alpha_{I}(\phi)F_{I}(X)
.

As in the case of shift-symmetric theories, the possibility
for evading the no-hair property of BHs is
to choose couplings with specific
non-analytic functions of
X
.
In shift-symmetric theories,
for the coupling functions (
69
),
the
X
-dependences in
G_{2,3,4,5}
are uniquely fixed in
such a way that they give rise to terms without containing
the
\phi^{\prime}
-dependence in
J^{r}
, except for
\eta h\phi^{\prime}

Sotiriou and Zhou 2014a
;
Babichev et al. 2017
.
This can be straightforwardly extended to non-shift-symmetric
theories by multiplying analytic functions
of
\phi
to each non-analytic functions of
X
as

G_{2}=\eta X+\alpha_{2}(\phi)\sqrt{-X}\,,\qquad G_{3}=\alpha_{3}(\phi)\ln|X|\,,\qquad G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+\alpha_{4}(\phi)\sqrt{-X}\,,\qquad G_{5}=\alpha_{5}(\phi)\ln|X|\,,

(89)

where
\alpha_{I}(\phi)
’s (
I=2,3,4,5
) are
analytic functions of
\phi
.
From Eq. (
57
), we obtain

\phi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\eta h}\left[\sqrt{\frac{h}{2}}\alpha_{2}(\phi)-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}+\frac{4}{r}\right)h\alpha_{3}(\phi)+\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{r^{2}}\alpha_{4}(\phi)+\frac{f^{\prime}h(h-1)}{fr^{2}}\alpha_{5}(\phi)+\frac{Q+Q_{\phi}}{r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\right]\,,

(90)

where

Q_{\phi}=-\int_{r_{s}}^{r}r^{2}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}P_{\phi}\,{\rm d}r\,.

(91)

Around the horizon, we can use the expansions (
24
)
and (
25
) of the metric
components and the expansion (
54
)
of the scalar field which is valid for
X_{s}=0
.
Then, the leading-order terms of
P_{\phi}

for the couplings
G_{2}
,
G_{3}
,
G_{4}
,
G_{5}

in Eq. (
89
) are
proportional to
\sqrt{r-r_{s}}
,
\ln(r-r_{s})
,

1/\sqrt{r-r_{s}}
, and
\ln(r-r_{s})
in the vicinity of
r=r_{s}
,
respectively.
Even in those cases, however, the integral (
91
)
vanishes for
r\to r_{s}
, i.e.,
Q_{\phi}=0
.
This means that the discussion
performed in shift-symmetric theories
can be applied to the present
\phi
-dependent couplings
as well.

For the quadratic and quartic couplings, we need to choose
Q=0

as in shift-symmetric theories, but the scalar-field kinetic term on the horizon reduces to

IV.3
Cubic logarithmic couplings

We study the possibility for realizing asymptotically Minkowski BH
solutions in theories containing cubic logarithmic couplings
given by

\alpha_{3}(\phi)

\phi

In what follows, we write
\alpha_{3}(\phi)=\alpha\gamma(\phi)

\alpha

Performing the expansions (
80

81

with respect to the small parameter
\alpha

\phi_{0}(r)=\phi_{0}={\rm constant}

The first-order solutions in
\alpha

The regularity of
\hat{h}_{1}(r)

\hat{f}_{1}(r)

\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)

\alpha

(95)

In the following, we will discuss shift-symmetric and
non-shift-symmetric theories separately.

IV.3.1
Shift-symmetric theories

We first consider the case

\gamma(\phi)=\gamma_{0}={\rm constant}\,.

(96)

Then, the metric components (
95

have the asymptotic behavior
\hat{h}_{2}(r)\to 0
and

\hat{f}_{2}(r)\to-[4\gamma_{0}^{2}/(\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{2})]\ln[r/(2m)]

at spatial infinity.
The logarithmic divergence of
\hat{f}_{2}(r)

can be eliminated by imposing
\gamma_{0}=0
,
but in this case we end up with
the no-hair Schwarzschild solution.

IV.3.2
Non-shift-symmetric theories

Let us next proceed to the case in which
\gamma(\phi)
is a nontrivial analytic function of
\phi
.
Analogous to Eq. (
82
), we can
expand
\gamma(\phi)
around
\phi=\phi_{0}
as

\gamma(\phi)=\gamma(\phi_{0})+\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma^{(n)}(\phi_{0})\frac{(\phi-\phi_{0})^{n}}{n!}\,,

(97)

where
\gamma^{(n)}(\phi_{0})\equiv{\rm d}^{n}\gamma/{\rm d}\phi^{n}|_{\phi=\phi_{0}}
.
From Eq. (
95
), the metric can be asymptotically Minkowski only if

\gamma(\phi_{0})=0\,.

(98)

Deriving the higher-order solutions in
\alpha
,
we find that the regularity of
\hat{\phi}_{j}
(
j\geq 2
)
on the horizon requires the conditions

\gamma^{(n)}(\phi_{0})=0\qquad(n\geq 1)\,.

(99)

Namely, the
G_{3}
term in the action must be absent.
In this case, we obtain the following regular solutions

\hat{h}_{j}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{j}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{j}={\rm constant}\,,

(100)

for all
j

Then, we obtain
the no-hair Schwarzschild solution with
\phi={\rm constant}

due to the absence of the
G_{3}
term in the action.

We thus showed that, for both the shift-symmetric and non-shift-symmetric
forms of the cubic logarithmic couplings (
92
),
asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions cannot be realized.

IV.4
Quintic logarithmic couplings

Let us finally discuss the model with the quintic
logarithmic coupling only,

G_{2}=\eta X\,,\qquad G_{3}=0\,,\qquad G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}\,,\qquad G_{5}=\alpha_{5}(\phi)\ln|X|\,,

(101)

where
\alpha_{5}(\phi)
is a regular function of
\phi
.
Except for shift-symmetric theories with
\alpha_{5}={\rm constant}
,
this model generally gives rise to terms of the
form
(-X)^{p}\ln|X|
(
p\geq 1
)
in the background equations of motion. Although they themselves
vanish in the limit
X\to 0
, higher-order
X
-derivatives
of them diverge for
X\to 0
.
In this case, we may have the problem of instability
at the level of higher-order perturbations.
In the process of deriving perturbative solutions with
the expansions (
80
) and (
81
),
we also encounter terms like
\alpha^{p}\ln|\alpha|
,
so the power-law expansions with respect to
\alpha

lose their validity.
The quintic couplings like

G_{5}=[1+\sum_{p=1}C_{p}(-X)^{p}]\alpha_{5}(\phi)\ln|X|
, which reduce to
G_{5}\to\alpha_{5}(\phi)\ln|X|

in the limit
X\to 0
,
again generate terms
of the form
(-X)^{p}\ln|X|
in the background equations.

As we will see in Sec.
V
,
the scalar-GB coupling
\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
, which amounts to the coupling functions of the form (
103
),
corresponds to the only exceptional case in which

\ln|X|
-dependent terms completely
disappear from the background equations.
In this case, since we do not face the aforementioned
problem, we can resort to the expansions (
80
) and (
81
) to derive BH solutions perturbatively.
As long as all the coupling functions in Eq. (
103
) are present,
we can add other regular functions like
(-X)^{p}
and
\phi^{q}

in the coupling functions
to see how the structure of hairy BH solutions
is modified.
We will also address this issue in Sec.
V.2
.

scalarized BHs can be obtained only nonperturbatively
for
|\alpha|={\cal O}(1)
.

V.2
GB couplings with other interactions

We also study how the hairy BH solutions discussed in
Sec.
V.1
are subject to modifications by taking
into account power-law coupling functions
(-X)^{p}
or
\phi^{q}
to the GB theory
given by Eq. (
104
).
For simplicity, we consider lowest-order power-law functions in most cases, but we will also study theories containing the couplings
G_{3}\supset\gamma_{3}\ln(-X)
and
G_{4}\supset\gamma_{4}\sqrt{-X}
.
In scalar-GB theories
with the quadratic potential
V(\phi)=\mu_{2}\phi^{2}

in
G_{2}
, the presence of hairy BH solutions was
numerically confirmed in Ref.
Doneva et al. 2019
.
Hence, we do not analyze the same model here.
In the presence of the term
\alpha\tilde{\alpha}_{2}X^{2}
in
G_{2}
,
using the expansions (
80
) and (
81
) with respect to the small coupling constant
\alpha

shows that there are no corrections to
\hat{h}_{j}
,
\hat{f}_{j}
, and
\hat{\phi}_{j}
derived in GB theories (
104
) up to the order
j=3
. Similarly, in the presence of

G_{2}\supset\alpha_{2}X^{n}
(
n\geq 3
),
the nontrivial corrections do not appear up to the order
j=n+1
.

V.2.1
Cubic and GB couplings

The
\phi
-dependent cubic coupling
G_{3}(\phi)
is equivalent
to the term
-2XG_{3,\phi}
in
G_{2}

Kobayashi et al. 2011
, so adding the linear coupling
\alpha\mu_{3}\phi
to
G_{3}

does not modify the structure of the theory (
104
).
We then consider

G_{3}(\phi)\supset\alpha\mu_{3}\phi^{2}\,,

(121)

with
\mu_{3}
being a nonvanishing constant, which is equivalent
to
-4\alpha\mu_{3}\phi X
in
G_{2}
.
We perform the expansions (
80
) and (
81
)
in terms of the small coupling constant
\alpha
.
The first-order solutions regular on the horizon are
equivalent to those in Eq. (
105
), while
the second- and third-order solutions are

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{2}(r)=\hat{h}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{2}(r)=\hat{f}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{2}(r)=\hat{\phi}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)+\frac{8(3\hat{r}^{2}+3\hat{r}+4)\phi_{0}\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\mu_{3}}{3\eta^{2}m^{2}\hat{r}^{3}}\,,

(122)

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{3}(r)=\hat{h}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)+\frac{(147\hat{r}^{5}+174\hat{r}^{4}+228\hat{r}^{3}-1624\hat{r}^{2}-3488\hat{r}-7360)\phi_{0}\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})^{2}\mu_{3}}{30\eta^{2}m^{4}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\hat{r}^{6}}\,,

(123)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{3}(r)=\hat{f}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)-\frac{(147\hat{r}^{5}+294\hat{r}^{4}+548\hat{r}^{3}+56\hat{r}^{2}-416\hat{r}-1600)\phi_{0}\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})^{2}\mu_{3}}{30\eta^{2}m^{4}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\hat{r}^{6}}\,,

(124)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{3}(r)=\hat{\phi}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)+\frac{\mu_{3}}{\eta^{3}m^{4}\hat{r}^{6}}\varphi_{3}(\hat{r})\,,

(125)

where
\varphi_{3}(\hat{r})
is the fifth degree polynomial of
\hat{r}
.
The cubic coupling (
121
) gives rise to modifications in
\hat{\phi}_{2}(r)
,
\hat{h}_{3}(r)
,
\hat{f}_{3}(r)
, and
\hat{\phi}_{3}(r)

in comparison to those derived for the GB couplings.
At large distances, these new terms have
the same radial dependence as
their leading-order terms.

Using the expanded solutions with
|\phi_{0}|
at most of order
M_{\rm pl}
, it follows that the conditions
for the absence of ghost/Laplacian instabilities
against odd- and even-parity perturbations
are also satisfied for

|M_{\rm pl}\mu_{3}|\lesssim 1
,
|\alpha|\ll 1
,
and
\eta>0
.
Then, the cubic coupling
G_{3}(\phi)\supset\alpha\mu_{3}\phi^{2}

besides the GB coupling
\alpha\xi(\phi)G

leads to the existence of hairy BH solutions free from ghost or Laplacian instabilities.
Similarly, for more general
\phi
-dependent
cubic coupling
G_{3}\supset\alpha\mu_{3}(\phi)
,
nontrivial corrections to
the metric functions and those to the scalar field
show up at the orders
j=3
and
j=2
, respectively.

Second, we discuss the case in which
the cubic Galileon coupling

G_{3}(X)\supset\alpha\alpha_{3}X\,,

(126)

with
\alpha_{3}
being a nonvanishing constant,
is present besides GB coupling
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.
Then, the corrections to
the BH solutions in GB theories,
up to the order
j=3
, appear only in
\hat{\phi}_{3}(r)
as

\hat{\phi}_{3}(r)=\hat{\phi}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)-\frac{2(21\hat{r}^{5}+42\hat{r}^{4}+84\hat{r}^{3}-24\hat{r}^{2}-84\hat{r}-224)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})^{2}\alpha_{3}}{21\eta^{3}m^{6}\hat{r}^{9}}\,.

(127)

At large distances, the correction to
\hat{\phi}_{3}^{\prime}(r)
arising from
the cubic Galileon is proportional to
\hat{r}^{-5}
, which
decays faster than
\hat{\phi}_{3{\rm GB}}^{\prime}(r)\propto\hat{r}^{-2}
.
Since the metric components are not modified
up to the order
j=3
, the cubic Galileon does not
induce strong modifications to hairy GB BHs in comparison
to the coupling
G_{3}(\phi)\supset\alpha\mu_{3}\phi^{2}
.
The absence of ghost/Laplacian instabilities
of BHs is also ensured for

|M_{\rm pl}\alpha_{3}/m^{2}|\lesssim 1
,

|\alpha|\ll 1
, and
\eta>0
.
Similarly, in the presence of
G_{3}\supset\alpha\alpha_{3}X^{n}
(
n\geq 2
), nontrivial corrections
to the scalar field
show up at the order
j=n+2
.

The next example is the cubic logarithmic
interaction given by

G_{3}\supset\alpha\gamma_{3}\ln(-X)\,,

(128)

with
\gamma_{3}
being a nonvanishing constant,
which belongs to
the couplings in Eq. (
68
).
The first-order solutions in
\alpha
regular on the horizon (
\hat{r}=2
) are

\hat{h}_{1}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{1}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}^{\prime}_{1}(r)=-\frac{2}{\eta m^{3}\hat{r}^{4}}\left[(\hat{r}^{2}+2\hat{r}+4)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})+2m^{2}\hat{r}^{3}\gamma_{3}\right]\,.

(129)

At large distances, the leading-order contributions to

\hat{\phi}^{\prime}_{1}(r)
arise from the cubic logarithmic coupling.
The second-order solutions regular
on the horizon are given by

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)+\frac{\gamma_{3}}{2\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\left[\frac{(3\hat{r}^{2}+10\hat{r}-40)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})}{m^{2}\hat{r}^{3}}+\frac{8\gamma_{3}}{\hat{r}-2}\ln\left(\frac{\hat{r}}{2}\right)\right]\,,

(130)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)+\frac{\gamma_{3}}{6\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}-\left[\frac{(9\hat{r}^{2}+18\hat{r}-88)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})}{m^{2}\hat{r}^{3}}+24\gamma_{3}\frac{\hat{r}-1}{\hat{r}-2}\ln\left(\frac{\hat{r}}{2}\right)\right]\,,

(131)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{2}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)-\frac{64\xi^{(2)}(\phi_{0})\gamma_{3}}{\eta^{2}m^{3}\hat{r}^{4}(\hat{r}-2)}\ln\left(\frac{\hat{r}}{2}\right)\,.

(132)

At spatial infinity, the metric component
\hat{f}_{2}(r)
exhibits the logarithmic divergence

\hat{f}_{2}(r)\to-\frac{4\gamma_{3}^{2}}{\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\ln\hat{r}\quad{\rm as}\quad\hat{r}\to\infty\,.

(133)

This means that, even in the presence of the GB couplings, the cubic
logarithmic coupling prevents the realization of asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions.

V.2.2
Quartic and GB couplings

We proceed to the model of a linear nonminimal coupling

G_{4}\supset\alpha\mu_{4}\phi\,,

(134)

with
\mu_{4}
being a nonvanishing constant,
besides the GB coupling
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.
Then, we find that the first-order solutions in
\alpha

regular on the horizon
are the same as those derived in Eq. (
105
).
The second-order solutions are given by

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)-\frac{(\hat{r}+4)(\hat{r}+10)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\mu_{4}}{6\eta m^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\hat{r}^{3}}\,,

(135)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)-\frac{(23\hat{r}^{2}+22\hat{r}+24)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\mu_{4}}{6\eta m^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\hat{r}^{3}}\,,

(136)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{2{\rm GB}}(r)\,.

(137)

The linear nonminimal coupling affects
\hat{h}_{2}(r)

and
\hat{f}_{2}(r)
,
while its effect does not appear in
\hat{\phi}_{2}(r)
.
In comparison to the cubic-order interactions discussed in Sec.
V.2.1
, the modifications to the background
geometry arising from the quartic coupling
\alpha\mu_{4}\phi

in
G_{4}
already appear at the order of
j=2
.
Higher-order solutions of
\hat{h}_{j}(r)
,
\hat{f}_{j}(r)
, and

\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)
(
j\geq 3
) also receive corrections from
the linear nonminimal coupling.
Since all

\hat{f}_{j}(r)
,
\hat{h}_{j}(r)
, and
\hat{\phi}_{j}^{\prime}(r)
(
j\geq 1
)
vanish at spatial infinity, the resulting hairy BH solutions are asymptotically Minkowski.
Similarly, for the
\phi
-dependent coupling function
G_{4}\supset\alpha\mu_{4}(\phi)
,
nontrivial corrections to the metric components
show up at the order
j=2
.

Using the expanded solutions, the quantities associated with
odd-parity perturbations are given by

{\cal F}=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}+2\phi_{0}\mu_{4}\alpha+{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{2}\right)\,,\quad{\cal G}=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}+2\phi_{0}\mu_{4}\alpha+{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{2}\right)\,,\quad{\cal H}=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}+2\phi_{0}\mu_{4}\alpha+{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{2}\right)\,,

(138)

so that the linear nonminimal coupling
gives rise to corrections of order
\alpha
.
We can avoid the ghost and Laplacian instabilities
under the condition

M_{\rm Pl}^{2}+2\phi_{0}\mu_{4}\alpha>0\,.

(139)

As long as
|\alpha|\ll 1
and
|\mu_{4}/M_{\rm pl}|\lesssim 1
,
this condition is satisfied for
|\phi_{0}|\lesssim M_{\rm Pl}
.
We note that the radial and angular propagation speed
squares for the odd modes,
c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}={\cal G}/{\cal F}
and

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}={\cal G}/{\cal H}
, are
both
1+{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
.

Up to the order of
\alpha^{2}
, the quantity
{\cal K}

is the same as that given in Eq. (
117
).
The propagation speed squared of the scalar field
perturbation
\delta\phi
in the even-parity sector is estimated as

c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}=1+{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
for theories
with
\xi^{(2)}(\phi)\neq 0
.
For the linear GB coupling
\xi(\phi)\propto\phi
, we have

c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}=1+{\cal O}(\alpha^{3})
, where
the terms of order
\alpha^{3}
arise from

\alpha\mu_{4}\phi
in
G_{4}
.
Up to the linear order in
\alpha
, the squared angular
propagation speeds
c_{\Omega,\pm}^{2}
in the even-parity sector are
identical to those in Eq. (
120
).
These discussions show that, provided
|\alpha|\ll 1
,

|\mu_{4}/M_{\rm Pl}|\lesssim 1
,
\eta>0
, and
|\phi_{0}|\lesssim M_{\rm Pl}
,
there are neither ghost nor Laplacian instabilities
for hairy BHs discussed above.

Instead of the linear nonminimal coupling of
the form
\beta_{4}\phi R
, we can also consider
nonminimal couplings with higher-order powers, i.e.,

\alpha\mu_{4}\phi^{p}R
with
p\geq 2
, besides the GB
couplings
Antoniou et al. 2021
.
As in the case of
p=1
, the contributions
to
f
and
h
from
\mu_{4}
appear at the order
of
j=2
, while the scalar-field derivative starts to
receive corrections from the third order.
In such models, we can also realize asymptotically Minkowski hairy BHs
satisfying all the conditions for the absence of ghost/Laplacian instabilities.

Let us next study the model of quartic derivative couplings
of the form

G_{4}\supset\alpha\alpha_{4}X\,,

(140)

with
\alpha_{4}
being a nonvanishing constant,
besides the GB coupling.
Performing the expansions (
80
) and (
81
),
we obtain the same first- and second-order
regular solutions as those given in
Eqs. (
105
) and (
106
)–(
108
).
The third-order solutions are

\hat{h}_{3}(r)=\hat{h}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)+\frac{4(\hat{r}^{2}+2\hat{r}+4)^{2}(\hat{r}-2)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})^{2}\alpha_{4}}{\eta^{2}m^{6}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\hat{r}^{9}}\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{3}(r)=\hat{f}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{3}(r)=\hat{\phi}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)\,.

(141)

At this order, the effect of the quartic
derivative coupling appears only in the expression
of
\hat{h}_{3}(r)
.
This correction vanishes on the horizon,
with the asymptotic
behavior
\hat{h}_{3}(r)-\hat{h}_{3{\rm GB}}(r)\propto\hat{r}^{-4}
at spatial infinity.

We recall that the BH solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0

realized by quartic derivative interactions
without GB couplings are prone to
the instability problem around
the horizon
Minamitsuji et al. 2022
.
On the other hand, the GB coupling besides
the term
\alpha\alpha_{4}X

in
G_{4}
gives rise to asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions.
The leading-order
corrections due to
\alpha
to

{\cal F}
,
{\cal G}
,
{\cal H}
,
{\cal K}
,

c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}
,
and
c_{\Omega,\pm}^{2}

are the same as those given in
Eqs. (
113
), (
114
), (
115
),
(
117
), (
119
), and (
120
), respectively, so
these hairy solutions can satisfy
all the conditions for the absence of
ghost/Laplacian instabilities.

For quartic derivative interactions with higher-order powers,
i.e.,
G_{4}\supset\alpha\alpha_{4}X^{n}
(
n\geq 2
),
nontrivial corrections to the metric components
or the scalar field show up at the order
j=n+2
.
In such models there are BH solutions consistent with
conditions for the absence of ghost/Laplacian instabilities
and strong coupling problems, but it is difficult to distinguish
them from those realized by GB couplings alone.

We also study the model given by

G_{4}\supset\alpha\gamma_{4}\sqrt{-X}\,,

(142)

with
\gamma_{4}
being a nonvanishing constant,
which belongs to couplings in Eq. (
68
).
In the absence of the GB coupling, this model gives rise to
an exact BH solution
Babichev et al. 2017
, but
it is unstable due to the property
X_{s}\neq 0
on
the horizon
Minamitsuji et al. 2022
.
The first-order solutions with respect to the GB
coupling constant
\alpha
are

\hat{h}_{1}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{1}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{C\eta m\hat{r}^{3}+m\hat{r}^{5/2}\sqrt{2(\hat{r}-2)}\gamma_{4}+16\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})}{\eta m^{3}\hat{r}^{4}(\hat{r}-2)}\,,

(143)

where
C
is an integration constant.
The numerator of
\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(r)
needs to vanish
for its regularity at
r=2m
, which gives

C=-2\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})/(\eta m)
.
Then, we obtain the following solution

\hat{\phi}^{\prime}_{1}(r)=-\frac{2(\hat{r}^{2}+2\hat{r}+4)\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})}{\eta m^{3}\hat{r}^{4}}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\gamma_{4}}{\eta m^{2}\hat{r}^{3/2}\sqrt{\hat{r}-2}}\,.

(144)

For
\gamma_{4}\neq 0
, there is still the divergence
of
\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(r)
at
\hat{r}=2
.
The leading-order term of
X
on the horizon is
a nonvanishing constant given by

X_{s}=-\gamma_{4}^{2}/(16\eta^{2}m^{4})
.
Hence, even in the presence of the GB term, the quartic coupling
\alpha\gamma_{4}\sqrt{-X}
in
G_{4}
violates
the conditions for the absence of ghost/Laplacian
instabilities of hairy BH solutions.

The linear coupling
\mu_{5}\phi
in

is equivalent
to the quartic derivative coupling

in

Kobayashi et al. 2011

Finally, we consider the quintic derivative

VI
Black holes in
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity

In this section, we explore the BH solutions in gravitational theories where the Lagrangian contains an arbitrary function
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})

of the GB curvature invariant
R_{\rm GB}^{2}
, besides the Einstein-Hilbert term.
As was pointed out in
Kobayashi et al. 2011
,
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity can be embedded in Horndeski theories.
We then generalize
this
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
-equivalent Horndeski theory
by adding a canonical kinetic term of the scalar
field.

VI.1
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity

Let us consider theories given by the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})\,,

(149)

which can be equivalently expressed as

\mathcal{L}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+F_{,\varphi}R_{\rm GB}^{2}-V(\varphi)\,,

(150)

where
\varphi
is a new scalar degree of freedom associated
with the GB term and we have defined

V(\varphi)\equiv F_{,\varphi}\varphi-F(\varphi)\,.

(151)

Indeed, varying the Lagrangian (
150
)
with respect to
\varphi
leads to

(\varphi-R_{\rm GB}^{2})F_{,\varphi\varphi}=0\,.

(152)

Therefore, provided that
F_{,\varphi\varphi}\neq 0
, we have
\varphi=R_{\rm GB}^{2}
, and hence
the Lagrangian (
150
)
reduces to the original one in Eq. (
149
).
From Eq. (
150
), we find that
a scalar field
\varphi
with the potential
V(\varphi)

couples to the GB term of the form
F_{,\varphi}R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.

We introduce the following quantities:

\phi\equiv M_{\rm Pl}m^{4}\varphi\,,\qquad\xi(\phi)\equiv F_{,\varphi}\,,

(153)

where
2m
corresponds to the
horizon radius of a BH solution (if it exists).
The quantity
\phi
has mass dimension one, which we identify as the scalar field in Horndeski theories.
Then, the Lagrangian for
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity is
equivalent to

\mathcal{L}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}-V(\phi)\,,\qquad{\rm where}\qquad V(\phi)=\xi\varphi-F\,.

(154)

For a given function
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
, the GB
coupling
\xi(\phi)
and the scalar potential
V(\phi)
are
fixed by using the correspondence (
153
) with
\varphi=R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.
In the language of Horndeski theories,
the theory (
154
) corresponds to the
following choice of the coupling functions
Kobayashi et al. 2011
;
Langlois et al. 2022
:

\displaystyle G_{2}=-V(\phi)+8\xi^{(4)}(\phi)X^{2}(3-\ln|X|)\,,\qquad G_{3}=4\xi^{(3)}(\phi)X(7-3\ln|X|)\,,

\displaystyle G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+4\xi^{(2)}(\phi)X(2-\ln|X|)\,,\qquad G_{5}=-4\xi^{(1)}(\phi)\ln|X|\,.

(155)

In the following, for concreteness, we consider the power-law
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
models given by

F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})=\beta(R_{\rm GB}^{2})^{n}\,,

(156)

where
\beta
and
n
are constants.
Introducing the dimensionless coupling
\alpha=2m^{2-4n}M_{\rm Pl}^{-2}\beta
and
performing the expansions (
80
) of
metric components with respect to the small parameter
|\alpha|\ll 1
, the scalar potential is given by

V(\phi)=\alpha(n-1)\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2m^{2}}\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{\rm Pl}}\right)^{n}\,.

(157)

Apart from the specific powers
n=0
and
n=1
,
the scalar field has a nonvanishing effective mass squared
M_{\phi}^{2}\equiv V_{,\phi\phi}
.
For
n=0
, we have

F=\beta={\rm constant}

and hence the resulting solution is the
Schwarzschild–(anti-)de Sitter spacetime.
When
n=1
, we have
\xi=\beta={\rm constant}

and
V=0
, so we end up with the no-hair
Schwarzschild solution.
Thus, we will focus on integer powers
with
n\geq 2
.

The scalar-field equation at first order
in

\phi_{0}(r)=\frac{48m^{6}M_{\rm Pl}}{r^{6}}\,,

(159)

which corresponds to the GB term in
the Schwarzschild spacetime.
In this case, we obtain

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5\hat{r}(\hat{r}+2)(\hat{r}^{2}+4)(\hat{r}^{4}+16)-17152}{8\hat{r}^{9}}\,,

\displaystyle{\hat{f}}_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{5\hat{r}(\hat{r}+2)(\hat{r}^{2}+4)(\hat{r}^{4}+16)-2816}{8\hat{r}^{9}}\,,

\hat{r}=r/m

the dominant terms in
{\cal F}

{\cal G}

are
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}

{\cal K}=-\frac{15925248M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{\hat{r}^{18}}\alpha^{2}+{\cal O}(\alpha^{3})\,.

Since the leading-order term in

there is the ghost instability for even-parity perturbations.
While
c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}=1+{\cal O}(\alpha)

associated with the angular propagation speeds in the even-parity sector are

For
n\geq 3

in the even-parity sector, with
a similar behavior of
B_{1}
and

n=2

n=3

(162)

whose leading-order term is negative, and hence
the hairy BH solutions with
\phi_{0}(r)=48m^{6}M_{\rm Pl}/r^{6}

When
n\geq 3

\phi_{0}(r)=0

(

\hat{h}_{j}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{j}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)=0\,,

(163)

which correspond to no-hair BHs.

We have thus shown that the power-law
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
models with (
156

all the conditions for the absence of
ghost/Laplacian instabilities.
We also studied the logarithmic model with

F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})=\beta\ln|R_{\rm GB}^{2}|
and
reached the same conclusion.

VI.2
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
-equivalent Horndeski theories with a scalar-field kinetic term

We also study BHs in theories where
the kinetic term
\eta X
is added to the
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
-equivalent
Horndeski theories (
154
), i.e.,

\mathcal{L}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+\eta X+\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}-V(\phi)\,,

(164)

where
\xi(\phi)
and
V(\phi)
are given by
Eqs. (
153
) and (
154
), respectively.

Considering the power-law coupling functions of the form
F(\varphi)=\beta\varphi^{n}
analogous to Eq. (
156
)
and introducing the dimensionless parameter
\alpha=2m^{2-4n}M_{\rm Pl}^{-2}\beta
,
the scalar potential is given by the same form as that in Eq. (
157
).
We first investigate the case of
n=2
,
which corresponds to the quadratic potential
V(\phi)=\alpha\phi^{2}/(2m^{2})
with the linear
GB coupling
\xi(\phi)=\alpha m^{2}M_{\rm Pl}\phi
.
Although
V(\phi)
and
\xi(\phi)

are similar to those in the model studied
in Ref.
Doneva et al. 2019
,
both
V(\phi)
and
\xi(\phi)
are proportional
to
\alpha
in our case.
This fact affects the resulting BH solutions
derived by using the expansions (
80
)
and (
81
) with respect to
\alpha
.

As discussed in Sec.
IV.2
,
the zeroth-order equation for the scalar field reduces to
the differential equation (
83
).
The solution to
\phi_{0}(r)
regular on the horizon is

\phi_{0}(r)=\phi_{0}={\rm constant}
.
The first-order regular solutions are given by

\hat{h}_{1}(r)=\frac{(\hat{r}^{2}+2\hat{r}+4)\phi_{0}^{2}}{12M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{1}(r)=-\frac{\hat{r}(\hat{r}+2)\phi_{0}^{2}}{12M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{(\phi_{0}\hat{r}^{3}-6M_{\rm Pl})(\hat{r}^{2}+2\hat{r}+4)}{3\eta m\hat{r}^{4}}\,.

The asymptotically Minkowski metric can be realized
only if
\phi_{0}=0
, under which both

\hat{h}_{1}(r)
and
\hat{f}_{1}(r)
vanish with
the dependence
\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(r)\propto\hat{r}^{-2}

at large distances.

At second order, the metric components
\hat{h}_{2}(r)
and

\hat{f}_{2}(r)
are equivalent to those derived by
taking the limit of
\xi^{(1)}(\phi_{0})^{2}/(m^{4}M_{\rm Pl}^{2})\to 1
in
Eqs. (
106
) and (
107
), respectively.
The scalar-field derivative consistent
with the regularity on the horizon
(
\hat{r}=2
) yields

\hat{\phi}_{2}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}}{3\eta^{2}m\hat{r}(\hat{r}-2)}\left[3(\hat{r}+4)(\hat{r}-2)+8\ln\left(\frac{\hat{r}}{2}\right)\right]\,,

(166)

which has the dependence
\hat{\phi}_{2}^{\prime}(r)\to M_{\rm Pl}/(\eta^{2}m)
as

\hat{r}\to\infty
.
Then, the scalar field does not satisfy the boundary
condition
\phi^{\prime}(r)\to 0
at spatial infinity [see Eq. (
56
)].

For
n\geq 3
, again the asymptotically Minkowski metric is realized only if
\phi_{0}=0
, under which
\hat{h}_{1}(r)=0
and
\hat{f}_{1}(r)=0
.
The first-order solution to the scalar-field derivative
regular on the horizon is
\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(r)=0
,
so that
\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{1}={\rm constant}
for arbitrary
r
.
When
n=3
, the second-order regular solutions are given by

\hat{h}_{2}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{2}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{2}(r)=\tilde{\phi}_{2}+\frac{2(3\hat{r}^{2}+3\hat{r}+4)}{\eta\hat{r}^{3}}\tilde{\phi}_{1}\,,

(167)

where
\tilde{\phi}_{2}
is a constant.
Similarly, the metric components of
third-order solutions are
\hat{h}_{3}(r)=0
and
\hat{f}_{3}(r)=0
,
while the leading-order contribution to
\hat{\phi}^{\prime}_{3}(r)
at spatial
infinity is
\tilde{\phi}_{1}^{2}\hat{r}/(\eta mM_{\rm Pl})
. To satisfy the
condition
\hat{\phi}_{3}^{\prime}(r)\to 0
as

, we require that

\tilde{\phi}_{1}=0
.
We also find that the solutions compatible with the boundary conditions (
56
),
\hat{\phi}_{j}^{\prime}(r)\to 0
at spatial
infinity, are given by

\hat{h}_{j}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{f}_{j}(r)=0\,,\qquad\hat{\phi}_{j}(r)=0\qquad(j\geq 1)\,.

(168)

The same conclusion holds also for
n\geq 4
.
Thus, for
n\geq 3
, we only have the
Schwarzschild BH solutions without scalar hair.
These results show the absence of asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions,
at least as long as the perturbative ansatze (
80
)
and (
81
) are valid in the small coupling limit.
This does not exclude the possibility for the existence of asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions beyond
the perturbative regime.

For a massive scalar field with the potential
V(\phi)=M_{\phi}^{2}\phi^{2}/2
, the property of
BHs was studied in Ref.
Doneva et al. 2019

for the linear GB coupling
\xi(\phi)=\alpha\phi
.
Since in this case the mass
M_{\phi}
is not related
to the GB coupling
\alpha
, the resulting BH
solution is different from that discussed above for
n=2
.
Indeed, the second-order differential equation for

\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)
contains
a mass term
-M_{\phi}^{2}\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)
. In such a case, we do not have an analytic solution for
\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)
,
so it requires numerical integration as performed in Ref.
Doneva et al. 2019
.
At spatial infinity, the scalar field solution is approximately given by the form
\hat{\phi}_{1}(r)\simeq C_{1}e^{M_{\phi}r}/r+C_{2}e^{-M_{\phi}r}/r
, with
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
being constants.
In order to satisfy the boundary
condition (
56
), namely

\hat{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(\infty)=0
,
we need to choose the coefficient
C_{1}
to be
zero.
The existence of hairy BH solutions was numerically confirmed
for the case of quadratic potential
V(\phi)=M_{\phi}^{2}\phi^{2}/2
with several different choices
of GB couplings including the linear coupling
\xi(\phi)=\alpha\phi
.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we scrutinized the existence and
linear stability of static and spherically symmetric
BH solutions with a static scalar field
in full Horndeski theories
without imposing the shift symmetry from the outset.
For this purpose, we employed a perturbative method of deriving
BH solutions, which is valid in the regime of small coupling
constant(s).
We then exploited the conditions for the absence of ghost/Laplacian
instabilities against odd- and even-parity perturbations derived in
Refs.
Kobayashi et al. 2012
;
Kobayashi et al. 2014
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
,
which are summarized in Sec.
II
.
In particular, the angular propagation speed of
even-parity perturbations plays an important role for
ruling out some of the BH solutions by
the Laplacian instability around the BH horizon.
In shift-symmetric Horndeski theories, it was shown in
Ref.
Minamitsuji et al. 2022
that hairy BH solutions present
for theories with the k-essence Lagrangian
G_{2}(X)
and
a nonminimal derivative coupling
G_{4}(X)R

Rinaldi 2012
;
Anabalon et al. 2014
;
Minamitsuji 2014a
;
Cisterna and Erices 2014

are subject to this generic instability around the horizon.

In Sec.
III
, we extended the linear stability analysis for BHs in shift-symmetric theories performed in
Minamitsuji et al. 2022
to full Horndeski theories.
For hairy BHs where the scalar-field kinetic term
X

is an analytic function of
r
with a nonvanishing value
on the horizon (
X_{s}\neq 0
), the product
{\cal F}{\cal K}B_{2}

was shown to be negative
for nonzero values of
\kappa
defined by Eq. (
46
).
This implies that the linear stability conditions summarized in Sec.
II
cannot be satisfied simultaneously, and hence
BHs with
X_{s}\neq 0
are generally subject to either ghost or Laplacian instability. We also found that, as long as
X_{s}\neq 0
with
\kappa_{r}\neq 0
, there is the divergence of the radial propagation speed of scalar-field perturbations.
In Sec.
III.2
, we presented examples of theories
that give rise to the branch of unstable hairy BH
solutions with
X_{s}\neq 0
.
Our results show that, even in full Horndeski theories,
BH solutions free from instabilities should not
have a nonvanishing
X_{s}
in general.
Under this condition, there is also a jump of
X
across
the horizon, so the BH solutions are physically unacceptable.

Given the generic instability for BHs with
X_{s}\neq 0
, for the search of hairy BHs which are free from ghost or Laplacian instabilities, we focused on theories leading to the solutions with
X_{s}=0
, i.e., a finite scalar-field derivative
\phi^{\prime}(r_{s})
on the horizon.
Using the scalar-field equation of motion for theories containing
the scalar-field kinetic term
\eta X
and the Einstein-Hilbert term
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}/2

in the action, we discussed the possibility
for realizing such hairy BHs in both shift-symmetric and non-shift-symmetric Horndeski theories in Sec.
IV
.
For the couplings of the form
G_{I}\supset\alpha_{I}(\phi)F_{I}(X)

(
I=2,3,4,5
) with
\alpha_{I}(\phi)
and
F_{I}(X)

being arbitrary regular functions,
the asymptotically Minkowski solutions respecting the regularity on the horizon are restricted to be no-hair solutions with
\phi^{\prime}(r)=0
.
There are possibilities for evading this no-hair feature of BHs
in theories given by the coupling functions (
89
),
which are not analytic at
X=0
.
However, the couplings
G_{2}\supset\alpha_{2}(\phi)\sqrt{-X}
and

G_{4}\supset\alpha_{4}(\phi)\sqrt{-X}
result in BH solutions
with
X_{s}\neq 0
, so they are excluded in terms of the gradient or Laplacian instabilities around the horizon.
For the cubic logarithmic coupling
G_{3}\supset\alpha_{3}(\phi)\ln|X|
, where
\alpha_{3}(\phi)
is an analytic function of
\phi
,
we showed the absence of asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions.

The remaining theories allowing for the existence of hairy BH solutions should possess a quintic coupling of the form
G_{5}=\alpha_{5}(\phi)\ln|X|
.
With this coupling only, the background equations contain
terms like
X^{p}\ln|X|
, which causes the breakdown of
our perturbative analysis
by using the expansions (
80
) and (
81
)
with respect to a small coupling parameter.
The scalar field coupled to the GB curvature invariant,

\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
, which is equivalent to the Horndeski functions (
103
), is the only exceptional case in which
\ln|X|
-dependent terms disappear from
the background equations due to the presence of the other specific
couplings
G_{2,3,4}
besides
G_{5}=\alpha_{5}(\phi)\ln|X|
.

In Sec.
V.1
, we derived the solutions to
the metric components and the scalar field for the GB term
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
with an analytic
function
\xi(\phi)
by resorting to the expansions with
respect to the small GB coupling
\alpha
.
We also showed that ghost and Laplacian instabilities
of hairy BHs against odd- and even-parity
perturbations are absent for the small GB coupling with
\eta>0
.
In Sec.
V.2
, we implemented positive
power-law functions of
\phi
or
X

in
G_{2,3,4,5}
besides the GB coupling
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
and obtained new classes of hairy BH solutions free from
ghost or Laplacian instabilities.
Since all such hairy BH solutions disappear in the absence of
the term
\alpha_{5}(\phi)\ln|X|
in
G_{5}
,
the existence of this form of quintic couplings is
crucial for realizing asymptotically Minkowski hairy BH solutions. We also found that, even
in the presence of the GB term
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
,
the couplings
G_{3}\supset\gamma_{3}\ln(-X)
or

G_{4}\supset\gamma_{4}\sqrt{-X}

prevent the existence of asymptotically Minkowski solutions with scalar hair which do not suffer from ghost or Laplacian instabilities.

In Sec.
VI
, we studied whether BH solutions
free from ghost or Laplacian instabilities are present
in
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity where
F
is a regular function
of the GB term
R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.
For power-law couplings
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})=\beta(R_{\rm GB}^{2})^{n}

with
n\geq 2
, we found a new class of asymptotically Minkowski BH solutions where the GB term plays a role of the new scalar degree of freedom. However, they are prone to the ghost instability of even-parity perturbations.
Although we also studied
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
-equivalent
Horndeski theories with a scalar-field kinetic
term
\eta X
, there are no hairy BH solutions
with asymptotically Minkowski metric.
These results show that the presence of
the GB coupling
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
besides the kinetic
term
\eta X
play a prominent role for realizing
asymptotically Minkowski hairy BHs free from ghost or Laplacian instabilities in full Horndeski theories.

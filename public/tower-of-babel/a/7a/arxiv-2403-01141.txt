Singular dynamics for discrete weak K.A.M. solutions of exact twist maps Thanks: The datasets generated during and/or analyzed during the current study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.
Jianxing Du †, Xifeng Su †

Abstract
. For any exact twist map f f and any cohomology class c ∈ ℝ c\in\mathbb{R} , let u c u_{c} be any associated discrete weak K.A.M. solution, and we introduce an inherent Lipschitz dynamics Σ + \Sigma_{+} given by the discrete forward Lax-Oleinik semigroup. We investigate several properties of Σ + \Sigma_{+} and show that the non-differentiable points of u c u_{c} are globally propagated and forward invariant by Σ + \Sigma_{+} . In particular, such propagating dynamics possesses the same rotation number α ′ ​ ( c ) \alpha^{\prime}(c) as the associated Aubry-Mather set at cohomology class c c . As applications, we provide via Σ + \Sigma_{+} a discrete analogue of Bernard’s regularization theorem [ Ber07 ] and a detailed exposition of Arnaud’s observation [ Arn11 ] . Furthermore, we construct and analyze the corresponding dynamics on the full pseudo-graphs of discrete weak K.A.M. solutions.

Introduction

In the 1890 volume of Acta Mathematica, H. Poincaré introduce some of the basic ideas about twist maps of the annulus while he researched on the stability of orbits of the three body problem.

We focus on some recent developments on the twist maps. The celebrated Aubry-Mather theory developed independently by S. Aubry
[
Aub83
,
ALD83
]
and J. Mather
[
Mat82
]
provides a novel approach to the study of the dynamics of twist diffeomorphisms of the annulus (a weak analogue for twist homeomorphisms can be found in
[
KO97
]
). Especially, the existence of action minimizing sets (referred as Aubry-Mather sets) –which could be invariant circles or invariant Cantor sets etc–is established.
Moreover, one may have

•

every Aubry-Mather set is a subset of a homotopically nontrivial circle that is a graph over the zero section,

•

every Aubry-Mather set is associated a rotation number and

•

all the Aubry-Mather sets are vertically ordered in the annulus according to the rotation numbers. See e.g.
[
Gol01
]
.

We concentrate on those Aubry-Mather sets with gaps and intend to study how these gaps evolve. To this aim, we will introduce a singular dynamics such that
the gaps are forward invariant by such dynamics.

We now state our first result below:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a twist map of the annulus and
c\in\mathbb{R}
be some cohomology class of
\mathbb{T}
. Let
u_{c}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a discrete weak K.A.M. solution for
f
at cohomology class
c
. Then the optimal forward map

\Sigma_{+}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ x\mapsto\mathop{\arg\max}_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{u_{c}(y)-S_{c}(x_{n},y)\}

is single-valued.
Moreover, the map
\Sigma_{+}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\Sigma_{+}
is a lift of a circle map with degree
1
, i.e.,
\Sigma_{+}(x+1)=\Sigma_{+}(x)+1
;

(ii)

\Sigma_{+}
is non-decreasing and Lipschitz continuous;

(iii)

\Sigma_{+}
propagates singularities of
u_{c}
, i.e., if x is not a differentiable point of
u_{c}
, neither is
\Sigma_{+}(x)
; for simplicity, we denote by
\operatorname{Sing}(u_{c})
the set of all the non-differentiable points of
u_{c}
;

(iv)

\Sigma_{+}
admits a rotation number for positive iterations, that is,

\displaystyle\rho(\Sigma_{+}):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(\Sigma_{+}^{n}(x)-x\right)

exists for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and is independent of
x
. In fact,
\rho(\Sigma_{+})=\alpha^{\prime}(c)
.

As an application of the above properties of the singular dynamics, we immediately obtain
that the singularities of any discrete weak K.A.M. solution can be globally propagated with a rotation number, which answers a question of Wei Cheng
[
Che21
]
.

2.1.
A notion of important invariant set

As standard, we introduce the following invariant sets and investigate several properties of them. In Section
2.1
, we operate under the assumption that the function
S
satisfies Hypothesis
1
.

We recall the
discrete backward Lax-Oleinik operator
and
discrete forward Lax-Oleinik operator
defined in the introduction:

Definition 2.1
.

The
discrete backward Lax-Oleinik operator

T^{-}
and
discrete forward Lax-Oleinik operator

T^{+}
are defined by

\displaystyle\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad T^{-}[u](y):=

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big\{u(x)+S(x,y)\big\},

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad T^{+}[u](x):=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big\{u(y)-S(x,y)\big\},

for every bounded
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic function (not necessarily continuous)
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(namely
u\in B(\mathbb{T}^{d})
).

Remark 2.2
.

(a)

The image
T^{-}(B(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
of
T^{-}
is equicontinuous and has uniformly bounded oscillation, as shown in
[
GT11
, Lemma 4.2]
.

(b)

We can derive directly from the definition that

\displaystyle\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad T^{-n}[u](y)=

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y_{-n},\dots,y_{-1})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\\
y_{0}=y\end{subarray}}\left\{v(y_{-n})+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})\right\},

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad T^{+n}[u](x)=

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\\
x_{0}=x\end{subarray}}\left\{u(x_{n})-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S(x_{i},x_{i+1})\right\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

(1)

The
optimal backward map
is the closed multi-valued map

\displaystyle\Sigma_{-}[u]:\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathop{\arg\min}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{u(x)+S(x,y)\};

(2)

the
optimal forward map
is the closed multi-valued map

\displaystyle\Sigma_{+}[u]:\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathop{\arg\max}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{u(y)-S(x,y)\};

(3)

The

n
-step optimal backward map
is the multi-valued map

\displaystyle\Sigma_{-}^{n}[u]:\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi_{1}\left(\mathop{\arg\min}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y_{-n},\dots,y_{-1})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\\
y_{0}=y\end{subarray}}\left\{u(y_{-n})+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})\right\}\right);

(4)

the

n
-step optimal forward map
is the multi-valued map

\displaystyle\Sigma_{+}^{n}[u]:\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi_{n}\left(\mathop{\arg\max}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\\
x_{0}=x\end{subarray}}\left\{u(x_{n})-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S(x_{i},x_{i+1})\right\}\right).

Remark 2.4
.

(a)

Given
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, as
S
is continuous and coercive (Hypothesis
1
), for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can find
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that attains the infimum in the definition of
T^{-}[u](y)
. Similarly, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can find
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that attains the supremum in the definition of
T^{+}[u](x)
. In other words,
\Sigma_{-}[u](y)\neq\emptyset,\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Sigma_{+}[u](x)\neq\emptyset,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(b)

We can derive directly from the definition and Remark
2.2
(b) that for any
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u]\circ\Sigma_{-}^{n}[T^{-m}[u]](y)=\Sigma_{-}^{m+n}[u](y).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
u=T^{-n}[v]
,
v\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The following two statements are equivalent:

(i)

x\in\Sigma_{-}^{n}[v](y)
;

(ii)

y\in\Sigma_{+}^{n}[u](x)
and
T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[v](x)=v(x).

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Suppose that
x\in\Sigma_{-}^{n}[v](y)
. Using the definition of
T^{-}
, by
u=T^{-n}[v]
, we have

(1)

u(y)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y_{-n},\dots,y_{-1})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\\
y_{0}=y\end{subarray}}\left\{v(y_{-n})+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})\right\}=v(x)+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})

where
y_{-n}=x,y_{0}=y
and for any
(z_{-n+1},\dots,z_{0}=z)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
,

u(z)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z_{-n},\dots,z_{-1})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\\
z_{0}=z\end{subarray}}\left\{v(z_{-n})+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(z_{i},z_{i+1})\right\}\leq v(x)+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(z_{i},z_{i+1}),

where
z_{-n}=x,z_{0}=z
.
By combining these equation and inequality and eliminating
v(x)
, we obtain

u(y)-\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})\geq u(z)-\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(z_{i},z_{i+1}),\quad\forall(z_{-n+1},\dots,z_{0}=z)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n},

which implies
T^{+n}[u](x)=u(y)-\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})
and
y\in\Sigma_{+}^{n}[u](x)
. Notice that the equation (
1
) says
u(y)-\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})=v(x)
. Hence
T^{+n}[u](x)=v(x)
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Suppose that
y\in\Sigma_{+}^{n}[u](x)
and
T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[v](x)=v(x).
Using the definition of
T^{-}
and
T^{+}
, by
u=T^{-n}[v]
, we have

u(y)\leq v(y_{-n})+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})

for any
(y_{-n},\dots,y_{-1})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
,
y_{0}=y
and

T^{+n}[u](x)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\\
x_{0}=x\end{subarray}}\left\{u(x_{n})-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S(x_{i},x_{i+1})\right\}=u(y)-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S(x_{i},x_{i+1})

where
x_{0}=x,x_{n}=y
.
By combining these equation and inequality and eliminating
u(y)
, we obtain

T^{+n}[u](x)+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq v(y_{-n})+\sum_{i={-n}}^{-1}S(y_{i},y_{i+1})

for any
(y_{-n},\dots,y_{-1})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
,
y_{0}=x_{n}=y
,
x_{0}=x
.
Since
T^{+n}[u](x)=T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[v](x)=v(x)
, we conclude that
x\in\Sigma_{-}^{n}[v](y)
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

If
u\in T^{-}(C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, then
\Sigma_{+}[u]
is surjective. In other words,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Sigma_{+}[u](x)=\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Suppose that
u=T^{-}[v]
for some
v\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. By Remark
2.4
(a), for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
x\in\Sigma_{-}[v](y)
. It follows from Lemma
2.5
that
y\in\Sigma_{+}[u](x)
.
∎

For any
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

T^{+}\circ T^{-}[u](x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(z)+S(z,y)-S(x,y)\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(x)=u(x).

Similarly, for any
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

(2)

T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\leq\cdots\leq T^{+}\circ T^{-}[u]\leq u\leq T^{-}\circ T^{+}[u]\leq\cdots\leq T^{-n}\circ T^{+n}[u].

Let
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]
the closed set

\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u](x)=u(x)\}.

It follows from the equation (
2
) that
\mathcal{O}^{n+1}[u]\subset\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. It follows from Remark
2.4
(a) and Lemma
2.5
that

\Sigma_{-}^{n}[u](\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathcal{O}^{n}[u],\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

So
\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]
is nonempty as the image of the multi-valued map
\Sigma_{-}^{n}[u]
. Hence

\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]

is a nonempty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The following proposition reveals that
\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]
can, to some extent, be regarded as a closed invariant set (see Proposition
2.24
).

Proposition 2.7
.

For any
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u](\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[T^{-m}[u]])=\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u].

Proof.

By Remark
2.4
(b),

\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u]\circ\Sigma_{-}^{n}[T^{-m}[u]](\mathbb{R}^{d})=\Sigma_{-}^{m+n}[u](\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

This implies that

\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u](\mathcal{O}^{n}[T^{-m}[u]])=\mathcal{O}^{m+n}[u],\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Taking the limit as
n\to+\infty
, we conclude that

\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u](\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[T^{-m}[u]])\subset\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u].

Conversely, for any
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]
, by definition,
x\in\mathcal{O}^{m+n}[u]
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Using Remark
2.4
(b) again, we have
x\in\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u](\mathcal{O}^{n}[T^{-m}[u]])
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Therefore, there exists a sequence
y_{n}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}[T^{-m}[u]]
such that
x\in\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u](y_{n}).
By Lemma
2.5
,
y_{n}\in\Sigma_{+}^{m}[T^{-m}[u]](x)
, which is a compact subset (depending on
x
). Hence, there exists a convergent subsequence
y_{n_{k}}\rightarrow y\in\Sigma_{+}^{m}[T^{-m}[u]](x)
. Since
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]
, by Lemma
2.5
again, we have
x\in\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u](y)
. Furthermore, since for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathcal{O}^{n+1}[T^{-m}[u]]\subset\mathcal{O}^{n}[T^{-m}[u]]
and
\mathcal{O}^{n}[T^{-m}[u]]
is closed, we conclude
y\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[T^{-m}[u]]
. Thus,

\Sigma_{-}^{m}[u](\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[T^{-m}[u]])\supset\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u].\qed

The following theorem is well-known:

Theorem 2.8

(Discrete Weak K.A.M. Theorem)
.

There exists a unique constant
\overline{S}
such that the equation
v=T^{-}[v]-\overline{S}
admits at least one solution
v\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and the equation
u=T^{+}[u]+\overline{S}
admits at least one solution
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Such functions
v:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are called
negative discrete weak K.A.M solutions
and such functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are called
positive discrete weak K.A.M solutions
. The constant
\overline{S}
is called the
effective interaction
.

Definition 2.9
.

A function
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is called a
sub-action
with respect to
S
if

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \quad u(y)-u(x)\leq S(x,y)-\overline{S},

or equivalently, if
u\leq T^{-}[u]-\overline{S}
, or equivalently, if
u\geq T^{+}[u]+\overline{S}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
u
be a sub-action. We say that
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
u
-calibrated
if
u(y)-u(x)=S(x,y)-\overline{S}
. A sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called
u
-calibrated
if
(x_{n},x_{n+1})
is
u
-calibrated for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
non-strict set

\mathcal{NS}[u]
of
u
collects all
u
-calibrated pairs:

\displaystyle\mathcal{NS}[u]:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid u(y)-u(x)=S(x,y)-\overline{S}\}.

Remark 2.11
.

For any sub-action
u
, the functions
T^{\mp}[u]
are also sub-actions
[
Zav23
, Proposition 1.2.6]
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle u\leq T^{-}[u]-\overline{S}\leq T^{-2}[u]-2\overline{S}\leq T^{-3}[u]-3\overline{S}\leq\cdots

This implies that the sequence
\{T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is non-decreasing. Moreover, we can show that the sequence
\{T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is uniformly bounded
[
Zav23
, Proposition 1.2.3]
. Note that (a) of Remark
2.2
states that the sequence
\{T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is equicontinuous. Therefore,
\{T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
converges uniformly to a function
u_{-}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, which is a negative discrete weak K.A.M. solution.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
u
be a sub-action. Set

u_{-}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S},\quad u_{+}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{+n}[u]+n\overline{S}

and

u_{-+}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{+n}[u_{-}]+n\overline{S},\quad u_{+-}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{-n}[u_{+}]-n\overline{S}

Then
u_{+}=u_{-+}
and
u_{-}=u_{+-}
.

Proof.

We show the first equality.
By Remark
2.11
, we have
u\leq u_{-}
and thus
u_{+}\leq u_{-+}
. Letting
\varepsilon(n):=\sup(u_{-}-T^{-n}[u]+n\overline{S})
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, then

u_{-}-\varepsilon(n)\leq T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}.

Then using the definition of
T^{+}
, we have

T^{+n}[u_{-}]-\varepsilon(n)\leq T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}\leq u-n\overline{S}.

Then for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

T^{+(n+m)}[u_{-}]+(n+m)\overline{S}-\varepsilon(n)\leq T^{+m}[u]+m\overline{S}.

Letting
m\to+\infty
, we have
u_{-+}-\varepsilon(n)\leq u_{+}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Letting
n\to+\infty
, we have
u_{-+}\leq u_{+}
.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}

converges uniformly to a function
\widetilde{u}_{+}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\{T^{-n}\circ T^{+n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}

converges uniformly to a function
\widetilde{u}_{-}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Furthermore, if
u
is a sub-action, then

\widetilde{u}_{+}=u_{+}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{+n}[u]+n\overline{S}

is a positive discrete weak K.A.M. solution and

\widetilde{u}_{-}=u_{-}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{-n}[u]-n\bar{S}

is a negative discrete weak K.A.M. solution. We call
(u_{-},u_{+})
a conjugate pair.

Proof of Proposition
2.13
.

Let us provide the proof for the positive discrete weak K.A.M. solution and the the rest can be obtain similarly.

Let
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. It follows from the equation (
2
) that
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is non-increasing. It follows from item (a) of Remark
2.2
that
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is equicontinuous. It follows from item (a) of Remark
2.2
that
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
has uniformly bounded oscillation. Combining this with the fact that
\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]\neq\emptyset
, we obtain
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is uniformly bounded. Therefore,
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
converges uniformly to a function
\widetilde{u}_{+}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Let
u
be a sub-action. We show that
\widetilde{u}_{+}=u_{-+}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{+n}[u_{-}]+n\overline{S}
. Then by Lemma
2.12
we get
\widetilde{u}_{+}=u_{+}
. Letting
\varepsilon(n):=\sup(u_{-}-T^{-n}[u]+n\overline{S})
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, then

u_{-}-\varepsilon(n)\leq T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}\leq u_{-}+\varepsilon(n).

Applying the operator
T^{+n}
to both sides of the inequality, we have

T^{+n}[u_{-}]-\varepsilon(n)\leq T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]-n\overline{S}\leq T^{+n}[u_{-}]+\varepsilon(n).

Then

T^{+n}[u_{-}]+n\overline{S}-\varepsilon(n)\leq T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\leq T^{+n}[u_{-}]+n\overline{S}+\varepsilon(n).

Letting
n\to+\infty
, we obtain
\widetilde{u}_{+}=u_{-+}
.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
u
be a sub-action. Then

\{x\mid u_{-}(x)=u_{+}(x)\}=\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u_{-}]\subset O^{\infty}[u].

Proof.

Since
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u_{-}]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is non-increasing and converges to
u_{+}
, we obtain
\{x\mid u_{-}(x)=u_{+}(x)\}=\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u_{-}]
by the definition of
\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u_{-}]
. Since
u_{-}\geq u
and
\{T^{+n}\circ T^{-n}[u]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is also non-increasing and also converges to
u_{+}
, we obtain
\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u_{-}]\subset O^{\infty}[u]
.
∎

We can directly deduce from the above lemma:

Proposition 2.15
.

\bigcap_{(u_{-},u_{+})\text{ conjugate pair}}\{x\mid u_{-}(x)=u_{+}(x)\}=\bigcap_{u\text{ weak K.A.M.}}\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]=\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u].

Moreover, we have

Proposition 2.16
.

\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]=\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{NS}[u]).

Proof.

Let
u
be a sub-action. Then we have

u(x)\geq T^{+}[u](x)+\overline{S}\geq u_{+}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

If
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]
, by Proposition
2.13
and the definition of
\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]
, it follows that
u_{+}(x)=\widetilde{u}_{+}(x)=u(x)
.
Thus

u(x)=T^{+}[u](x)+\overline{S},

which implies that
x\in\pi_{1}(NS[u])
.

Let
u
be a sub-action. If
x\in\pi_{1}(\mathcal{NS}[u])
for any sub-action
u
, then
x\in\pi_{1}(\mathcal{NS}[T^{+n}[u]])
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Consequently,

u(x)=T^{+n}[u](x)+n\overline{S}

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. This implies
u(x)=u_{+}(x)
. Therefore, by Proposition
2.13
,
u(x)=\widetilde{u}_{+}(x)
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. That is,
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]
.
∎

The following proposition gives equivalent definitions of the projected Aubry set.

Proposition 2.17
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the projected Aubry set. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{(u_{-},u_{+})\text{ conjugate pair}}\{x\mid u_{-}(x)=u_{+}(x)\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{u\text{ weak K.A.M.}}\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{NS}[u])

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}\left(\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\mathcal{NS}[u]\right).

Proof.

It follows from
[
GT11
, Lemma 10.3]
and
[
GT11
, Proposition 10.5]
that

\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{NS}[u])=\pi_{1}\left(\bigcap_{u\text{ sub-action}}\mathcal{NS}[u]\right)=\mathcal{A}.\qed

We list below, without proof, some fundamental properties of the projected Aubry set
\mathcal{A}
(see for instance
[
Ber08
,
GT11
,
Zav23
]
).

Proposition 2.18
.

Assume that
S
satisfies Hypothesis
1
and Hypothesis
2
.

(i)

\mathcal{A}
is closed, non-empty and

\mathcal{A}=\pi_{2}\left(\bigcap_{u\text{ is a sub-action}}\mathcal{NS}(u)\right);

(ii)

\mathcal{A}+m=\mathcal{A},\ \forall m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
;

(iii)

Any sub-action
u
is differentiable on
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, the differential
\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}
is independent of
u
;

(iv)

For any
x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
, there exists a unique sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
which is calibrated by any sub-action
u
;

(v)

Denote the
Aubry set

\mathcal{A}^{*}=\{(x,p)\mid p=\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x),x\in\mathcal{A}\}
. Then the projection
\pi_{1}:\mathcal{A}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism.

2.2.
Regularity results in discrete weak K.A.M. theory

In Section
2.2
, we operate under the assumption that the function
S
satisfies both Hypothesis
1
and Hypothesis
2
.

The image
T^{-}(B(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
(resp.
T^{+}(B(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
) constitutes a family of semiconcave (resp. semiconvex) functions with linear modulus and the same constant, as demonstrated in
[
Zav23
, Proposition 2.4.10]
.

For any

(3)

Define

\displaystyle x_{1}\leq x_{1}^{\prime}\leq x_{2}^{\prime}\leq x_{2},\quad\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{1}^{\prime})=\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{1})\quad\text{ and }\quad\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{2}^{\prime})=\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{2}).

Then,

|\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{1})-\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{2})|=|\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{1}^{\prime})-\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{2}^{\prime})|.

Since
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{-}[v](\mathbb{R})
and
\Sigma_{+}[u]
is Lipschitz continuous on
\Sigma_{-}[v](\mathbb{R})
with constant
K^{\prime\prime}
, we have

|\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{1}^{\prime})-\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{2}^{\prime})|\leq K^{\prime\prime}|x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime}|.

From the relationship
x_{1}\leq x_{1}^{\prime}\leq x_{2}^{\prime}\leq x_{2}
, it follows that

|x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime}|\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{1})-\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{2})|\leq K^{\prime\prime}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

This proves that
\Sigma_{+}[u]
is Lipschitz continuous on the whole
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

3.4.
\Sigma_{+}[u]
admits a rotation number

The following lemma is well-known and we provide its proof for selfcontainedness.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous map such that

(i)

\Phi(x+1)=\Phi(x)+1
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

if
x_{1}<x_{2}
, then
\Phi(x_{1})\leq\Phi(x_{2})
.

Then the limit

\displaystyle\rho(\Phi):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(\Phi^{n}(x)-x\right)

exists for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and is independent of
x
.

Proof.

From property (i), by induction, we have:

\displaystyle\Phi^{m}(x+1)=\Phi^{m}(x)+1,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Thus,
\Phi^{m}-\operatorname{id}
is
1
-periodic. Define

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}:=\inf_{\mathbb{R}}\{\Phi^{m}-\operatorname{id}\},\quad\quad\beta_{m}:=\sup_{\mathbb{R}}\{\Phi^{m}-\operatorname{id}\}.

From property (ii), for any
x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq x_{1}+1
, we have:

\Phi^{m}(x_{1})\leq\Phi^{m}(x_{2})\leq\Phi^{m}(x_{1})+1.

This implies

|(\Phi^{m}(x_{1})-x_{1})-(\Phi^{m}(x_{2})-x_{2})|\leq 1,

and hence
\beta_{m}-\alpha_{m}\leq 1
.

Let
n=qm+r
, where
0\leq r<m
. Then, using
\alpha_{m}\leq\Phi^{m}(x)-x\leq\beta_{m},
we obtain:

\displaystyle q\alpha_{m}\leq\Phi^{qm}(x)-x\leq q\beta_{m}.

Additionally,

r\alpha_{1}\leq\Phi^{r}(x)-x\leq r\beta_{1}.

Combining these, we have:

\displaystyle q\alpha_{m}+r\alpha_{1}\leq\Phi^{n}(x)-x\leq q\beta_{m}+r\beta_{1}.

Dividing through by
n
, we get:

\displaystyle\frac{q\alpha_{m}+r\alpha_{1}}{n}\leq\frac{\Phi^{n}(x)-x}{n}\leq\frac{q\beta_{m}+r\beta_{1}}{n}.

As
n\to+\infty
,
q/n\to 1/m
, and the terms involving
r/n
vanish. Moreover, since
\beta_{m}-\alpha_{m}\leq 1
, the difference between the bounds
(\beta_{m}-\alpha_{m})/m
vanishes as
m\to+\infty
.
Thus
\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\;$}}\frac{1}{n}\left(\Phi^{n}(x)-x\right)
exists and is independent of
x
.
∎

By combining Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
3.9
, we proved that if
u\in T^{-}(SC(\mathbb{T}))
, then
\Sigma_{+}[u]
admits a rotation number
\rho(\Sigma_{+}[u])
for positive iterations for any
u\in T^{-}[SC(\mathbb{T})]
.

Proposition 3.10
.

If
u\in T^{-}(SC(\mathbb{T}))
and
u
is a sub-action, then
\rho(\Sigma_{+}[u])
is the rotation number of the Aubry set.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
u
-calibrated sequence contained in the projected Aubry set. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have:

u(x_{n+1})-S(x_{n},x_{n+1})=u(x_{n})-\overline{S}.

Since
u
is a sub-action, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle u(x_{n})-\overline{S}\geq u(y)-S(x_{n},y).

Thus:

u(x_{n+1})-S(x_{n},x_{n+1})\geq u(y)-S(x_{n},y),

which implies
x_{n+1}\in\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{n})
. By Lemma
3.2
,
x_{n+1}=\Sigma_{+}[u](x_{n})
. The rotation number is given by:

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}(\Sigma_{+}^{n}[u](x_{0})-x_{0})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}(x_{n}-x_{0}).

This is the rotation number of the Aubry set. ∎

Let

be a discrete weak K.A.M. solution. Then
\operatorname{Sing}(u)
is a forward invariant set under
\Sigma_{+}[u]
, with the same rotation number as the Aubry set.

4.2.

To prove Theorem

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

with

and assume

Proof.

We consider two cases based on the differentiability of
u
at
y
.

Case 1:
u
is differentiable at
y
. By Proposition
3.6
,
(\Sigma_{+}[u])^{-1}(y)=\{x\}
, a singleton. From Lemma
2.19
(b),
\partial_{2}S(x,y)=\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(y)
. Using the definition of the discrete standard map (see Remark

), we conclude:

Case 2:
u
is non-differentiable at
y
. By Proposition
3.6
,
(\Sigma_{+}[u])^{-1}(y)
is a non-degenerate closed interval
[x_{-},x_{+}]
. From the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we can construct an increasing sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
such that
x_{n}\rightarrow x_{-}
and

is differentiable at

\displaystyle x_{n}=\pi_{1}\circ F^{-1}(y_{n},\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(y_{n})).

Taking limits, we obtain

\displaystyle x_{-}\in\pi_{1}\circ F^{-1}(y,\nabla^{*}u(y)).

Similarly, we derive

. Since

is semiconcave, by Proposition

(iv):

\nabla^{*}u(y)\subset\partial\nabla^{+}u(y).

Hence:

\displaystyle x_{-},x_{+}\in\pi_{1}\circ F^{-1}(y,\partial\nabla^{+}u(y)).

Since

is a closed convex subset of

\displaystyle\{x_{-},x_{+}\}=\pi_{1}\circ F^{-1}(y,\partial\nabla^{+}u(y)).

Observing that

and using the fact that

:

y_{-n+1}\in\Sigma_{+}[u](x),\quad y_{-n+2}\in\Sigma_{+}[u](y_{-n+1}),\quad\cdots\quad y\in\Sigma_{+}[u](y_{-1}),

leading to:

(x,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x))\in\Lambda^{-n}[u](y,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(y)).

Step 2.
Inclusion of
\{\Lambda^{i}[u](x,p)\mid i\leq-n,(x,p)\in\operatorname{Graph}(\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u)\}\subset\{(x,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x))\mid x\in\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]\}:

By Proposition
3.7
,
u
remains differentiable along the backward iterates of
\lambda[u]
.
If
u
is differentiable at
y
,
u
is differentiable on

\bigcup_{i\leq 0}\Lambda^{i}[u](y,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(y)).

So if

(x,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x))\in\bigcup_{i\leq-n}\Lambda^{i}[u](y,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(y)),

the corresponding chain of points
y_{-n+1},\dots,y_{-1}
satisfy

y_{-n+1}\in\Sigma_{+}[u](x),\quad y_{-n+2}\in\Sigma_{+}[u](y_{-n+1}),\quad\cdots\quad y\in\Sigma_{+}[u](y_{-1}),

and
u
is differentiable at
y_{-n+1},\dots,y_{-1}
. By Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
3.6
, we have

x\in\Sigma_{-}[u](y_{-n+1}),\quad y_{-n+1}\in\Sigma_{-}[u](y_{-n+2}),\quad\cdots\quad y_{-1}\in\Sigma_{-}[u](y).

This implies
x\in\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]
.

Step 3.
Equivalence of
\alpha(\operatorname{Graph}(\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u))
and
\{(x,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x))\mid x\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]\}
:

Using the inclusions from Steps 1 and 2, we deduce:

\displaystyle\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\{(x,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x))\mid x\in\mathcal{O}^{n}[u]\}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\overline{\{\Lambda^{i}[u](x,p)\mid i\leq-n,(x,p)\in\operatorname{Graph}(\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u)\}}.

Thus,

\alpha(\operatorname{Graph}(\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u))=\{(x,\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x))\mid x\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u]\},

showing by Proposition
2.24
it is closed, non-empty and invariant. By Proposition
2.17
,

\bigcap_{u\text{ weak K.A.M.}}\alpha(\operatorname{Graph}(\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u))=\bigcap_{u\text{ weak K.A.M.}}\left\{(x,p)\mid x\in\mathcal{O}^{\infty}[u],p=\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x)\right\}=\mathcal{A}^{*}.

This completes the proof of both parts of the proposition.
∎

Appendix A
Semiconcave functions

Let us recall some fundamental definitions and properties on semiconcave functions (see
[
CS04
,
Ber07
]
for instance).

Definition A.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function.

•

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the sets

\displaystyle\nabla^{-}u(x)=\left\{p\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}:\liminf_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{u(y)-u(x)-p(y-x)}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\nabla^{+}u(x)=\left\{p\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}:\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{u(y)-u(x)-p(y-x)}{\|y-x\|}\leq 0\right\}

are called, respectively, the
superdifferential
and
subdifferential
of
u
at
x
. From the definition it follows that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
-\nabla^{-}u(x)=\nabla^{+}(-u)(x).

•

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex open set. We say that
u
is
semiconcave
(with a linear modulus) on
\Omega
if there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\lambda u(x)+(1-\lambda)u(y)-u(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leqslant\frac{C}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|^{2}

for any
x,y\in\Omega
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Then
C
is called the
semiconcavity constant
of
u
. A function
v
is called
semiconvex
on
\Omega
if
-v
is semiconcave. We say that
u
is
locally semiconcave
(with a linear modulus) if for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a convex open neighborhood
U_{x}
such that
u
is semiconcave on
U_{x}
.

•

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a covector
p\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}
is called a reachable differential of
u
at
x
if there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{x\}
such that
u
is differentiable at
x_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}x_{k}=x,\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u\left(x_{k}\right)=p.

The set of all reachable gradients of
u
at
x
is denoted by
\nabla^{*}u(x)
.

One can then have the following well-known facts.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex open set and
x,x_{0}\in\Omega
.

(i)

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a semiconcave function on
\Omega
with a linear modulus and some semiconcave constant
C
. Then

\displaystyle u(x)-u(x_{0})\leq p_{0}(x-x_{0})+\frac{C}{2}\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}

for any
p_{0}\in\nabla^{+}u(x_{0})
. Conversely,
if there exists
C\geq 0
and
p_{0}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}
such that

\displaystyle u(x)-u(x_{0})\leq p_{0}(x-x_{0})+\frac{C}{2}\|x-x_{0}\|^{2},\ \quad\forall x\in\Omega

then
u
is semiconcave with semiconcavity constant
C
and
p_{0}\in\nabla^{+}u(x_{0})
.

(ii)

Let
v:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be another continuous function. If
u\leq v
on
\Omega
and
u(x)=v(x)
, then
\nabla^{+}u(x)\supset\nabla^{+}v(x)
and
\nabla^{-}u(x)\subset\nabla^{-}v(x)
.

(iii)

u
is differentiable at
x
if and only if
\nabla^{+}u(x)
and
\nabla^{-}u(x)
are both nonempty; in
this case, we have that

\nabla^{+}u(x)=\nabla^{-}u(x)=\{\mathop{}\hphantom{\mskip-3.0mu}\mathrm{d}u(x)\}.

(iv)

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a semiconcave function on
\Omega
. Then
\nabla^{*}u(x)\subset\partial\nabla^{+}u(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\partial
is the boundary taken with respect to the standard topology of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(v)

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a semiconcave function on a compact set
\Omega
. Then the gradient of
u
, defined almost everywhere in
\Omega
, has bounded variation. Hence the gradient of
u
is bounded in
\Omega
.

Appendix B
Twist maps of the
2
-dimensional annulus

We begin by introducing the notion of twist maps.

Definition B.1
.

A
twist map
of the
2
-dimensional annulus is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
f:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}\righttoleftarrow
such that

(1)

f
is isotopic to identity ;

(2)

f
is exact symplectic, i.e. if
f(\theta,p)=(\theta^{\prime},p^{\prime})
, then the
1
-form
p^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime}-pd\theta
is exact;

(3)

f
twists verticals to the right: if
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}\righttoleftarrow
, denoted by
F(x,p)=(y,p^{\prime})
, is a lift of
f
, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle 0<\frac{\partial y}{\partial p}(x,p)<\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\quad\forall(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}.

Definition B.2
.

A
generating function
of the lift
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}\righttoleftarrow
is a
C^{2}
function
S:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

S
is diagonal-periodic:

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad S(x+1,y+1)=S(x,y);

(2)

there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial x\partial y}(x,y)<-\varepsilon,\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

(3)

for all
(x,p),(y,p^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have the following equivalence

\displaystyle F(x,p)=(y,p^{\prime})\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}p=-\partial_{1}S(x,y)\\
p^{\prime}=\partial_{2}S(x,y)\end{cases}.

Remark B.3
.

For any lift
F
of a twist map
f
, there exists a unique generating function
S
, up to addition by a constant, that satisfies Definition
B.2
(see
[
MMS89
, Appendix 1]
).

Proposition B.4
.

Let
f
be a twist map and
S
be a generating function. For any
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the function

S_{c}(x,y):=S(x,y)+c(x-y)

satisfies Hypothesis
1
, Hypothesis
2
and Hypothesis
3
.

Proof.

The diagonal-periodicity and ferromagnetic property of
S_{c}
follows directly from items (1) and (3) of Definition
B.2
, respectively. Since
S
is of class
C^{2}
, it follows that
S_{c}
is of class
C^{1}
and locally semiconcave (see
[
CS04
, Proposition 2.1.2]
). The remaining task is to demonstrate that
S_{c}
is coercive. This property follows from the superlinearity of the generating function
S
, expressed as

\displaystyle\lim_{|x-y|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{S(x,y)}{|x-y|}=+\infty.

This conclusion is an immediate consequence of a lemma from the work of MacKay, Meiss and Stark
[
MMS89
, Lemma A1.6]
. Furthermore, the Non-crossing Lemma
[
ALD83
]
can be obtained by integrating
\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial x\partial y}
over a quadrangle; we omit its detailed proof here.
∎

Remark B.5
.

(a)

The function
S_{c}
is referred to as the generating function at the cohomology class
c
of
\mathbb{T}
. As proved in Proposition
B.4
, all theories in this paper are applicable to
S_{c}
. For instance, consider any
c
in the first cohomology group
H^{1}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
, the Discrete Weak K.A.M. Theorem
2.8
defines the effective interaction
\overline{S_{c}}
. The function
\alpha(c)=-\overline{S_{c}}
is called Mather’s
\alpha
-function, which is
C^{1}
. As a second example, the Aubry set
\mathcal{A}_{c}^{*}
at cohomology class
c
is
F_{c}
-invariant, where

\displaystyle F_{c}(x,p)=F(x,p+c)-(0,c).

This corresponds to the discrete standard map associated with
S_{c}
.
Consequently, the set
(0,c)+\mathcal{A}_{c}^{*}
is
F
-invariant, where
F
is the lift of the twist map. Thus, by examining the discrete weak K.A.M. theory of
S_{c}
, we can investigate the dynamics of
F
at the cohomology class
c
.

(b)

Since the dimension
d=1
, the the Aubry set
\mathcal{A}_{c}^{*}
at cohomology class
c
possesses additional properties beyond Proposition
2.18
(see
[
GT11
, Theorem 11.10]
):

•

(0,c)+\mathcal{A}^{*}_{c}
is an
F
-ordered set, i.e.,
\forall z,z^{\prime}\in(0,c)+\mathcal{A}^{*}_{c}
,

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(z)<\pi_{1}(z^{\prime})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\pi_{1}(F(z))<\pi_{1}(F(z^{\prime})).

•

The rotation number
\rho(c)
of
(0,c)+\mathcal{A}_{c}^{*}
exists, i.e., the limit
\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}\;$}}\frac{\pi_{1}(F^{n}(x,p))-x}{n}
exists and is independent of
(x,p)\in(0,c)+\mathcal{A}^{*}_{c}
. In fact,
\rho(c)=\alpha^{\prime}(c)
.

Appendix C
Proofs of Lemmas
2.19
and
2.22

For selfcontainedness, we give proofs for Lemmas
2.19
and
2.22
.

Proof of Lemma
2.19
.

(a). Suppose that
T^{-}[u](y)=u(x)+S(x,y)
for some
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

To establish the first inclusion,
-\partial_{1}S(x,y)\in\nabla^{-}u(x)
, we begin with the definition of the discrete backward Lax-Oleinik operator :
T^{-}[u](y)=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(z)+S(z,y)
. Then we have

\displaystyle u(x)+S(x,y)\leq u(z)+S(z,y),\ \quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex open neighborhood of
x
. Since
S
is locally semiconcave, we have
S(\cdot,y)
is semiconcave on
\Omega
.
By utilizing item (i) of Proposition
A.2
, there exists
C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle S(z,y)-S(x,y)\leq\partial_{1}S(x,y)(z-x)+\frac{C}{2}\|z-x\|^{2}

for any
z\in\Omega
. Combining these two inequalities, we get

\displaystyle u(x)-u(z)\leq\partial_{1}S(x,y)(z-x)+\frac{C}{2}\|z-x\|^{2},\ \quad\forall z\in\Omega.

Hence,
\partial_{1}S(x,y)\in\nabla^{+}(-u)(x)
follows from item (i) of Proposition
A.2
. Since
\nabla^{+}(-u)(x)=-\nabla^{-}u(x)
, we conclude that
-\partial_{1}S(x,y)\in\nabla^{-}u(x)
.

Now, we move on to the second inclusion,
\partial_{2}S(x,y)\in\nabla^{+}T^{-}[u](y)
.
According to the definition of the discrete backward Lax-Oleinik operator
T^{-}
, we have
T^{-}[u](\cdot)\leq u(x)+S(x,\cdot)
. Hence it follows from items (ii) and (iii) of Proposition
A.2
that

\displaystyle\{\partial_{2}S(x,y)\}=\nabla^{+}(u(x)+S(x,\cdot))(y)\subset\nabla^{+}T^{-}[u](y).

(b). The proof for item (b) follows from a similar argument as that of (a).
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.22
.

(a). Suppose there exists
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
such that
T^{-}[u](y)=u(x_{1})+S(x_{1},y)
and
T^{-}[u](y)=u(x_{2})+S(x_{2},y)
.
Using (a) of Lemma
2.19
, we get:

\displaystyle\{\partial_{2}S(x_{1},y),\partial_{2}S(x_{2},y)\}\subset\nabla^{+}T^{-}[u](y).

The ferromagnetic condition of
S
asserts that if
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, then
\partial_{2}S(x_{1},y)\neq\partial_{2}S(x_{2},y)
. Hence
\nabla^{+}T^{-}[u](y)
is not a singleton set. It follows from item (iii) of Proposition
A.2
that
T^{-}[u]
is not differentiable at
y
.

Thanks to item (a) of Remark
2.4
, we only need to demonstrate that if
x
is the unique point such that
T^{-}[u](y)=u(x)+S(x,y)
, then
T^{-}[u]
is differentiable at
y
.

For any converging sequence
y_{k}\rightarrow y
, let us pick
x_{k}
such that
T^{-}[u](y_{k})=u(x_{k})+S(x_{k},y_{k})
. Since
x
is the unique point such that
T^{-}[u](y)=u(x)+S(x,y)
, we have
x_{k}\rightarrow x
due to the continuity of functions
T^{-}[u]
,
u
and
S
. Then

\displaystyle T^{-}[u](y_{k})

\displaystyle=u(x_{k})+S(x_{k},y)+\left(S(x_{k},y_{k})-S(x_{k},y)\right)

\displaystyle\geq T^{-}[u](y)+\left(S(x_{k},y_{k})-S(x_{k},y)\right)

\displaystyle=T^{-}[u](y)+\partial_{2}S(x_{k},y)(y_{k}-y)+o(\|y_{k}-y\|)

Hence
\partial_{2}S(x,y)\in\nabla^{-}T^{-}[u](y)
by the definition of superdifferential. Using item (a) of Lemma
2.19
and item (iii) of Proposition
A.2
, we have that
T^{-}[u]
is differentiable at
y
.

(b). The proof for item (b) follows from a similar argument as that of (a).
∎

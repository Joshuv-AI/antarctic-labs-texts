Sausage Volume of the Random String and Survival in a medium of Poisson Traps
Siva Athreya and Mathew Joseph and Carl Mueller

Abstract
. We provide asymptotic bounds on the survival probability of a moving polymer in an environment of Poisson traps. Our model for the polymer is the vector-valued solution of a stochastic heat equation driven by additive spacetime white noise; solutions take values in ℝ d , d ≥ 1 \mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 1 . We give upper and lower bounds for the survival probability in the cases of hard and soft obstacles. Our bounds decay exponentially with rate proportional to T d / ( d + 2 ) T^{d/(d+2)} , the same exponent that occurs in the case of Brownian motion. The exponents also depend on the length J J of the polymer, but here our upper and lower bounds involve different powers of J J . Secondly, our main theorems imply upper and lower bounds for the growth of the Wiener sausage around our string. The Wiener sausage is the union of balls of a given radius centered at points of our random string, with time less than or equal to a given value.

1.
Introduction

The model of particles performing random diffusive motion in a region containing randomly located traps is known as the trapping problem (see
[
dHW94
]
for review). Particle motion is typically Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or a random walk in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The traps are placed in a Poissonian manner and the particle gets annihilated on encountering a trap. The main question of interest in such models is the “Survival Probability” of the particle. We refer the reader to
[
Szn98
]
and references there in for a review of the problem of Brownian motion among Poissonian obstacles, to
[
Kön16
]
and references there in for a review of the problem of a random walk in a random potential and to
[
ADS19
]
for a review of Random walks among mobile and immobile traps.

There is an extensive literature about such trapping problems, see the references in the preceding paragraph. These results often depend on refined estimates for the eigenvalues of the Laplacian or potential theory. On the other hand, the process we consider takes values in function space, and we found it impossible to analyze the situation using existing techniques. Indeed, carrying over finite-dimensional potential theoretic arguments to the infinite dimensional case is often difficult or impossible. We will consider a Gaussian process, but we did not find any Gaussian tools which were relevant to trapping problems.

In this article we will study the annealed survival probability of a random string in a Poissonian trap environment. Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}_{t},\mathbb{P}_{0})
be a filtered probability space on which
\dot{\mathbf{W}}=\dot{\mathbf{W}}(t,x)
is a
d
-dimensional random vector whose components are i.i.d. two-parameter white noises adapted to
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
. We consider a
random string

\mathbf{u}(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is the solution to the following stochastic heat equation (SHE)

(1.1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}{\mathbf{u}}(t,x)&=\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{x}^{2}{\mathbf{u}}(t,x)+\,\dot{\mathbf{W}}(t,x)\\
\mathbf{u}(0,x)&=\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)\end{split}

on the circle
x\in[0,J]
, having endpoints identified, and
t\in[0,T].
The initial profile
\mathbf{u}_{0}
is assumed to be continuous. Note that we will use boldface letters to denote vector-valued quantities.

We will be interested in the evolution of the random string in a field of obstacles centered at points coming from an independent Poisson point process. More precisely, let
(\Omega_{1},\mathcal{G},\mathbb{P}_{1})
be a second
probability space on which is defined a Poisson point process
\bm{\eta}
with intensity
\nu
given by

\bm{\eta}(\omega_{1})=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{\bm{\xi}_{i}(\omega_{1})},\quad\omega_{1}\in\Omega_{1},

with points
\{\bm{\xi}_{i}(\omega_{1})\}_{i\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The obstacles will be formed via a potential
\text{V}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{1}\rightarrow[0,\infty]

\text{V}(\mathbf{z},\bm{\eta})=\sum_{i\geq 1}\text{H}(\mathbf{z}-\bm{\xi_{i}}),

where
\text{H}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is a non-negative, measurable function whose support of
H
is contained in the
closed
ball
B(\mathbf{0},a)
of radius
0<a\leq 1
centered at
\mathbf{0}
.

We will work in the product space
(\Omega\times\Omega_{1},\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{G},\mathbb{P}_{0}\times\mathbb{P}_{1})
along with the filtration

(\mathcal{F}_{t}\times\mathcal{G})_{t\geq 0}
.
We will write
\mathbb{E}
for the expectation with respect to

\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{0}\times\mathbb{P}_{1}
,
and
\mathbb{E}_{i}
for the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{i}
for
i=0,1
. Our main quantity of interest is the quenched and the annealed survival probabilities given by

\begin{split}S_{T,\eta}(\omega_{1})&=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{J}\text{V}\Big(\mathbf{u}(s,x),\bm{\eta}(\omega_{1})\Big)dxds\right)\right],\text{ and }\\
S_{T}&=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{J}\text{V}\left(\mathbf{u}(s,x),\bm{\eta}\right)dxds\right)\right]\end{split}

respectively.
Sometimes we will write
S_{T}^{\text{H},J,\nu}
and
S_{T,\bm{\eta}}^{\text{H},J,\nu}
to emphasize the dependence on
\text{H},J,\nu
.

1.1.
Main Result

Our first result on
hard
obstacles, i.e. the string is killed immediately on contact and the only way it can survive is to avoid them.

Theorem 1.1

(Hard obstacles)
.

Consider the solution to (
1.1
) with
d\geq 2
and
J\geq 1
, and let

\nu
and
a
be as above. Then the following hold in the case
\text{H}(\cdot)\equiv\infty\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B(\mathbf{0},a)}

(1)

(Lower bound)
There exist positive constants
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}
independent of
T,J
such that for
T\geq C_{0}J^{2+\frac{d}{2}}

(1.2)

S^{\textnormal{H},J,\nu}_{T}\geq C_{1}\exp\left(-C_{2}\left(\frac{T}{J}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\right).

(2)

(Upper bound)
There exist positive constants
C_{3},C_{4}
independent of

such that for all
T>0,J\geq 1

(1.3)

S^{\textnormal{H},J,\nu}_{T}\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}}{1+|\log J|}\left(\frac{T}{J^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\right).

In the case of hard obstacles we immediately see that the survival of the string is only possible if the string avoids the traps. Thus the“sausage of radius

(1.4)

S_{T}^{\text{H},J,\nu}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left(-\nu\left|\mathscr{S}^{J}_{T}(a)\right|\right),

where

(1.5)

\mathscr{S}^{J}_{T}(a)=\mathop{\bigcup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq T,\\
0\leq y\leq J\end{subarray}}\left\{\mathbf{u}(s,y)+B(\mathbf{0},a)\right\},

is the sausage of radius
a
around
\mathbf{u}
. Thus Theorem
1.1
also provides bounds on the exponential moments of the volume of the sausage of radius
a
around the string up to time
T
.

We next turn our attention to the case of
soft
obstacles, i.e.
H
does not take the value
\infty
. We make the following specific assumptions on

.

Assumption 1.1
.

There is a

\text{H}(\mathbf{x})\geq\mathscr{C}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B(\mathbf{0},\frac{a}{2})}(\mathbf{x})
.

We note that under this assumption there is a positive probability of survival even if the string interacts with the obstacle environment. We are now ready to state our result in this setting.

Theorem 1.2

(Soft obstacles)
.

Consider the solution to (
1.1
) with
d\geq 2
and
J\geq 1
, let

\nu>0
be as above and let
H
be a soft obstacle satisfying Assumption

. Then

(1)

(Lower bound)
There exist positive constants
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}
independent of
T,J
such that for
T\geq C_{0}J^{2+\frac{d}{2}}

(1.6)

S^{\textnormal{H},J,\nu}_{T}\geq C_{1}\exp\left(-C_{2}\left(\frac{T}{J}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\right).

(2)

(Upper bound)
Fix
\beta>0
. There exist positive constants
C_{3},C_{4}
independent of
T,J
such that for all
T>0,J\geq 1

(1.7)

S^{\textnormal{H},J,\nu}_{T}\leq C_{4}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{5}}{J^{3+\beta}(1+|\log J|)}\left(\frac{T}{J^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\right).

1.2.
Overview of Proof

We will say that
{\mathbf{u}}
is a solution to (
1.1
) if it satisfies,

(1.8)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{J}G^{(J)}(t,x-y)\mathbf{u}_{0}(y)dy+\int_{[0,t]\times[0,J]}G^{(J)}(t-s,x-y)\mathbf{W}(dsdy),

where
[0,J]
is the circle with endpoints identified and
G^{(J)}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,J]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the fundamental solution of
the heat equation

\begin{split}\partial_{t}G^{(J)}(t,x)&=\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{x}^{2}G^{(J)}(t,x),\\
G^{(J)}(0,x)&=\delta(x).\end{split}

Furthermore, the final integral in (
1.8
) can be regarded as
either a Wiener integral or a white
noise integral in the sense of Walsh
[
Wal86
]
.

The first reduction in the proof is to reduce to the case
J=1.
We will do this by deriving a scaling relation for
S_{T}^{\text{H},J,\nu}
. Consider

\mathbf{v}(t,x):=J^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{u}(J^{2}t,Jx),

defined for
x\in[0,1]
with endpoints identified, and
t\in[0,TJ^{-2}]
. The initial profile is
\mathbf{v}(0,x)=\mathbf{v}_{0}(x)=J^{-1/2}\mathbf{u}_{0}(Jx)
. It was proved in Lemma 2.2 of
[
AJM21
]
that
\mathbf{v}
satisfies

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}&=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\mathbf{v}+\dot{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}},\quad t\in[0,TJ^{-2}]\\
\mathbf{v}(0,x)&=\mathbf{v}_{0}(x),\quad x\in[0,1]\end{split}

for some other white noise
\dot{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}}
. Now it is easily checked

\displaystyle S_{T}^{\text{H},J,\nu}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{J}\sum_{i\geq 1}\text{H}\left(\mathbf{u}(s,x)-\bm{\xi}_{i}\right)dxds\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{J^{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i\geq 1}J^{3}\text{H}\left(J^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{v}(\tilde{s},\tilde{x})-\frac{\bm{\xi}_{i}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)d\tilde{x}d\tilde{s}\right)\right]

Define

(1.9)

\widetilde{\text{H}}(\cdot):=J^{3}\text{H}(J^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot),\qquad\tilde{\bm{\xi}_{i}}:=\frac{\bm{\xi}_{i}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}},\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\widetilde{\nu}:=\nu J^{\frac{d}{2}}.

It is easily seen that
\widetilde{\text{H}}
is supported in the ball
B(\mathbf{0},aJ^{-\frac{1}{2}})
. The points
\tilde{\bm{\xi}}
form a Poisson point process of intensity
\widetilde{\nu}
.
Setting
\widetilde{T}=TJ^{-2}
we obtain

(1.10)

S_{T}^{\text{H},J,\nu}=S_{\widetilde{T}}^{\widetilde{\text{H}},1,\widetilde{\nu}}.

(
Important).

For the rest of article will focus on
J=1
, so (

) becomes

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}G(t,x-y)\mathbf{u}_{0}(y)\,dy+\mathbf{N}(t,x),

\mathbf{N}(t,x)=\int_{[0,t]\times[0,1]}G(t-s,x-y)\mathbf{W}(dsdy)

The strategy for proving the lower bound for survival probability in Theorem

Theorem

is the same as that of Random walks or Brownian motions moving in a field of Poisson Traps. This is obtained by identifying an optimal configuration for the traps

We show that
\mathbf{X}_{t}
and
\mathbf{R}_{t}
are independent.
Then separately we compute the probabilities of

Unlike the lower bound, the proof of upper bound differs from the classical setting of random walks or that of Brownian motion.
Following Remark
1.2
we first obtain an upper bound for
S_{T}^{\text{H},1,\nu}
. Recall

(1.12)

S_{T}^{\text{H},1,\nu}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left(-\nu\left|\mathscr{S}^{1}_{T}(a)\right|\right),

\mathscr{S}^{1}_{T}(a)=\mathop{\bigcup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq T,\\

We will explain the strategy for the proof in the case of hard obstacles, the argument for the soft obstacles not being very different. Due to (
1.12
), an upper bound on the partition function
S_{T}^{\text{H},1,\nu}
essentially boils down to obtaining a lower bound on the volume of the sausage
\mathscr{S}_{T}^{1}(a)
around
\mathbf{u}
. For this, we consider the sausage around
\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{u}(t,\cdot)
, that is

(1.13)

\mathscr{S}^{1}(a;t)=\mathop{\bigcup}_{0\leq y\leq 1}\left\{\mathbf{u}(t,y)+B(\mathbf{0},a)\right\},

so that

\mathscr{S}^{1}_{T}(a)=\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{S}^{1}(a;t).

We will identify times at which
\mathscr{S}^{1}(a;t)
do not intersect, so the sum of the volumes of these fixed time sausages will provide the desired lower bound.

We will consider a set of stopping times
\tau_{i}
(see (

)) such that the center of mass at these time points,
{\mathbf{X}}_{\tau_{i}}
are separated by at least
4\Lambda
from each other (where
\Lambda
is suitably chosen see Lemma

Using known results on the volume of the Wiener sausage, we show that the number of

before time

(see Lemma

). Now, let

\mathbf{N}(s,t;x):=\int_{[s,t]\times[0,1]}G(t-r,x-y)\mathbf{W}(drdy),

which represents the noise term from time
s
to
t
. Then
\mathbf{N}(s,t)
will represent the function from
x\in[0,1]
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
s<t
, we use the Markov property for
\mathbf{u}
to write

\mathbf{u}(t)=G_{t-s}*\mathbf{u}(s)+\mathbf{N}(s,t).

If
s\ll t
then the first term is almost a constant because of the smoothening effect of the Laplacian. The volume of the sausage around
G_{t-s}*\mathbf{u}(s)
will then be approximately

.
We show in Lemma

, using the independence of
\mathbf{X}_{t}
and
\mathbf{R}_{t}
, that with probability

the range (see (

) for precise definition) of

is at most

and the volume of the sausage of radius

around
\mathbf{N}(s,t)
is of order at least

. Consequently there is a subset
\{T_{i}\}
of

of positive density, such that the sausages around
\mathbf{u}(T_{i})
are disjoint and have volume

each. This gives a lower bound of

on the volume

.

As mentioned earlier, our strategy for the lower bound of the annealed survival
probability is the same as for a random walk or Brownian motion surviving in a
field of random traps. The upper bound is different, since the usual strategies
depend on potential theory and eigenvalues of the Laplacian, and both of these
are much harder to study for infinite dimensional processes such as the random
string. We are forced to go back to first principles, which perhaps explains the
fact that our upper and lower bounds do not completely match. It is also important to note that while the upper and lower bounds match for the case
J=1
, the scaling relations in (
1.9
) imply that they don’t carry over to the general case via space-time scaling.

The quenched survival probability is of keen interest and will be the focus of future work. Here the geometry of the string and its topology will come into play. We did not explore large deviations for the volume of the sausage as there was no immediate ergodicity to establish a limiting value of a Lyapunov exponent.

2.
Proof of the lower bound in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

As indicated above we will use the same strategy for the lower bound for the survival probability for both the
hard
and
soft
obstacle case. We need a couple of technical results before we begin the proof.

The following lemma is crucial.

Lemma 2.1
.

With
\mathbf{X}_{t}
and
\mathbf{R}_{t}
as in (
1.11
), we have

(a)

\mathbf{X}_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion starting at
\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)dx
.

(b)

\mathbf{X}_{t}
and
\mathbf{R}_{t}
are independent.

Proof.

It is easily checked that

\mathbf{X}_{t}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)dx+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{W}(dyds)

is a standard Brownian motion, and

\mathbf{u}(t,x)-\mathbf{X}_{t}=\int_{0}^{1}\left[G(t,x-y)-1\right]\mathbf{u}_{0}(y)dy+\int_{[0,t]\times[0,1]}\left[G(t-s,x-y)-1\right]\mathbf{W}(dsdy),

where
G=G^{(1)}
is the heat kernel on the unit circle. Both these processes are Gaussian. The components of
\mathbf{X}_{t}
and
\mathbf{u}(t,x)-\mathbf{X}_{t}
are uncorrelated since

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\left[G(t-s,x-y)-1\right]\,dyds=0.

The second part of the lemma immediately follows.
∎

We will also need

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
\sup_{x}|\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}
. Then there are constants
0<C_{0}<1
and
K_{0}>0
such that for all
\alpha\geq K_{0}

(2.1)

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\leq\alpha^{2}}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}\left|\mathbf{u}(s,x)\right|\leq\alpha,\;\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\mathbf{u}(\alpha,x)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\geq C_{0}.

Proof.

Let us consider first the case
\mathbf{u}_{0}\equiv{\mathbf{0}}
. From the previous lemma

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\leq\alpha^{2}}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}\left|\mathbf{u}(s,x)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq\alpha^{2}}|\mathbf{R}_{s}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq\alpha^{2}}|\mathbf{X}_{s}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}\right).

Since the last term is a positive constant independent of
T
, it is enough to show that there is a
K_{0}>0
such that

\sup_{\alpha\geq K_{0}}\;\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq\alpha^{2}}|\mathbf{R}_{s}|>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)<1.

Now

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq\alpha^{2}}|\mathbf{R}_{s}|>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\leq\alpha^{2}}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}|\mathbf{u}(s,x)-\mathbf{X}_{s}|>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right).

By splitting the time interval into subintervals of length
1
we have the bound

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\leq\alpha^{2}}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}|\mathbf{u}(s,x)-\mathbf{X}_{s}|>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha^{2}]+1}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\in[k,k+1]}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}|\mathbf{u}(s,x)-\mathbf{X}_{s}|>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha^{2}]+1}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|\mathbf{u}(k,0)-\mathbf{X}_{k}|>\frac{\alpha}{8}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha^{2}]+1}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\in[i,i+1]}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}|\left[\mathbf{u}(k,0)-\mathbf{X}_{k}\right]-\left[\mathbf{u}(s,x)-\mathbf{X}_{s}\right]|>\frac{\alpha}{8}\right)\end{split}

Using the standard Fourier decomposition of
G(t,x)
(see Section 3 of
[
AJM21
]
) one obtains that each coordinate of
\mathbf{u}(i,0)-\mathbf{X}_{i}
has variance

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k}\int_{0}^{1}\left[G(s,y)-1\right]^{2}\,dyds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{k}\int_{0}^{1}G^{2}(s,y)\,dyds-k

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{k}\sum_{l\geq 1}\exp\left(-(2\pi l)^{2}s\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C.

See Lemma 3.1 of
[
AJM21
]
for details.
Therefore for large
\alpha

\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha^{2}]+1}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|\mathbf{u}(k,0)-\mathbf{X}_{k}|>\frac{\alpha}{8}\right)\leq\exp\left(-C\alpha^{2}\right).

Now we turn to the last term in (
2.3
). Consider the process

\mathbf{M}(s,x)=\left[\mathbf{u}(k,0)-\mathbf{X}_{k}\right]-\left[\mathbf{u}(s,x)-\mathbf{X}_{s}\right],\quad s\in[k,k+1],\;x\in[0,1].

Note that

. A quick calculation gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}(s,x)-\mathbf{M}(s,\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,s]\times[0,1]}\left[G(s-r,\tilde{x}-y)-G(s-r,x-y)\right]\mathbf{W}(drdy)

whose components have variance less than
C|x-y|
(see Lemma 3.1 of
[
AJM21
]
for details). Similarly for

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}(s,x)-\mathbf{M}(\tilde{s},x)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,s]\times[0,1]}\left[G(\tilde{s}-r,x-y)-G(s-r,x-y)\right]\mathbf{W}(drdy)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{[s,\tilde{s}]\times[0,1]}G(\tilde{s}-r,x-y)\mathbf{W}(drdy)+\left[\mathbf{X}_{s}-\mathbf{X}_{\tilde{s}}\right].

Following Lemma 3.1 of
[
AJM21
]
we obtain that the components have variance less than

. Note that

so that the variance of the components of

are also bounded by

.

Therefore the conditions of Lemma 3.4 of
[

]
are satisfied, and we obtain for large

\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha^{2}]+1}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\in[k,k+1]}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}\left|\left[\mathbf{u}(k,0)-\mathbf{X}_{k}\right]-\left[\mathbf{u}(s,x)-\mathbf{X}_{s}\right]\right|>\frac{\alpha}{8}\right)\leq\exp\left(-C\alpha^{2}\right).

Returning to (

) we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq\alpha^{2}}|\mathbf{R}_{s}|>\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\leq\exp\left(-C\alpha^{2}\right)<1,

uniformly in

for some

. This completes the proof of the proposition in the case that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\equiv{\mathbf{0}}
.

In the general case

, we apply a Girsanov change of measure argument. Consider the measure
\mathbb{Q}_{0}
given by

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}_{0}}{d\mathbb{P}_{0}}=\exp\left(-\int_{[0,\alpha^{2}]\times[0,1]}\frac{(G_{s}*\mathbf{u}_{0})(y)\cdot\mathbf{W}(dsdy)}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\alpha^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|G_{s}*\mathbf{u}_{0})(y)\right|^{2}}{\alpha^{4}}\right),

where

is the convolution of

with
\mathbf{u}_{0}
. Under the measure
\mathbb{Q}_{0}
,

is a white noise (see
[

]
). Moreover

(2.4)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\left(1-\frac{t}{\alpha^{2}}\right)(G_{t}*\mathbf{u}_{0})(x)+\int_{[0,t]\times[0,1]}G(t-s,x-y)\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(dsdy),\quad 0\leq t\leq\alpha^{2},

and the first term is
0
at time

. The case of
\mathbf{u}_{0}\equiv{\mathbf{0}}
shows

\mathbb{Q}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\leq\alpha^{2}}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}\left|\int_{[0,t]\times[0,1]}G(t-s,x-y)\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(dsdy)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\geq\tilde{C}_{0},

for some

. An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\leq\alpha^{2}}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}\left|\int_{[0,t]\times[0,1]}G(t-s,x-y)\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(dsdy)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{Q}_{0}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\leq\alpha^{2}}}{{x\in[0,1]}}}\left|\int_{[0,t]\times[0,1]}G(t-s,x-y)\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(dsdy)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}_{0}}{d\mathbb{P}_{0}}\right)^{2}\right]^{-1/2}

The first term in (
2.4
) is at most

, and so the above lower bound is also a lower bound for the probability in (

). This completes the proof of the proposition.

3.2.

For a

We will need the following lemma

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{f}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(G_{t}*\mathbf{f})\leq 4de^{-4\pi^{2}t}\left\|\mathbf{f}-\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{f}(x)dx\right\|_{2}\leq 4de^{-4\pi^{2}t}\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{f})

Proof.

We expand each component

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y}\Big|(G_{t}*f_{j})(x)-(G_{t}*f_{j})(y)\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x,y}\left|\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{-4\pi^{2}k^{2}t}a_{k}^{(j)}\left[e^{i2\pi kx}-e^{i2\pi ky}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4e^{-4\pi^{2}t}\left(\sum_{k\neq 0}\left[a_{k}^{(j)}\right]^{2}\right)^{1/2}

Now observe that

is the zeroth Fourier coefficient of

.

There exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for any
\lambda>0

(3.14)

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{x,y\in[0,1]}\left|\mathbf{N}^{(2)}(t;x,y)\right|>\lambda\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{e^{t}\lambda^{2}}{C_{1}}\right).

Proof.

We first observe

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left|\mathbf{N}^{(2)}(t;x,y)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{1}\left[G(t-s,x,z)-G(t-s,y,z)\right]^{2}dzds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}ds\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-k^{2}(t-s)}\left|1-e^{i\cdot 2\pi k(x-y)}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{e^{-k^{2}t}}{k^{2}}\left|1\wedge(k|x-y|)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-t}|x-y|.

An application of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality then gives us

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left|\mathbf{N}^{(2)}(t;x,y)\right|^{2k}\right]\leq C^{k}(2\sqrt{2})^{2k}k^{k}e^{-kt}|x-y|^{k}

for all positive integers
k\geq 1
.
From the argument leading up to inequality (6.9) in
[
CJK13
]
one then obtains

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\sup_{x,y\in[0,1]}\left|\mathbf{N}^{(2)}(t;x,y)\right|^{2k}\right]\leq C^{k}e^{-kt}k^{k},\quad k\geq 2.

Therefore there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\sup_{x,y\in[0,1]}\exp\left(\frac{\exp(t)\cdot|\mathbf{N}^{(2)}(t;x,y)|^{2}}{C_{1}}\right)\right]\leq 2.

The inequality (
3.14
) follows immediately from this.
∎

The following lemma indicates that the process
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)
behaves locally like Brownian motion. It will be used in Lemma
3.7
below.

Lemma 3.6
.

There are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for all
x,y\in[0,1]
and all
t\geq 1
one has

C_{1}d(x,y)\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\left[G(s,x,z)-G(s,y,z)\right]^{2}dzds\leq C_{2}d(x,y),

where
d(x,y)
is the distance between
x
and
y
on the torus
\mathbb{T}=[0,1)
.

Proof.

Using the Fourier decomposition of
G(s,x)
we obtain

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}dz\left[G(s,x,z)-G(s,y,z)\right]^{2}\\
&=C\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{k\geq 1}\exp\left(-(2\pi k)^{2}s\right)\big|1-\exp\left[i(2\pi k)d(x,y)\right]\big|^{2}\\
&=C\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\left[1-\exp(-(2\pi k)^{2}t)\right]}{k^{2}}\big|1-\exp\left[i(2\pi k)d(x,y)\right]\big|^{2}.\end{split}

Now use
|1-e^{iz}|\leq 1\wedge|z|
and
|1-e^{-4\pi^{2}k^{2}t}|\leq 1
to obtain that the above is less than

C\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\big|1\wedge[kd(x,y)]\big|\leq Cd(x,y).

The final inequality is obtained by splitting the sum according to whether
k\leq d(x,y)^{-1}
or
k>d(x,y)^{-1}
.

Next we turn to the lower bound. For this we only consider the sum in (
3.15
) for
1\leq k\leq\frac{1}{2d(x,y)}
. Now
|1-e^{iz}|\geq C|z|
for all
z\in[0,\pi]
and some
C>0
. Therefore since
t\geq 1

\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}dz\left[G(s,x,z)-G(s,y,z)\right]^{2}\geq C\sum_{k=1}^{[2d(x,y)]^{-1}}\frac{k^{2}d(x,y)^{2}}{k^{2}}\geq Cd(x,y).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Before proceeding we recall

Definition 3.2
.

The lower Minkowski dimension of a set
A
is

\underline{\textnormal{dim}}_{M}(A):=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\log N_{\epsilon}(A)}{\log(\epsilon^{-1})},

where
N_{\epsilon}(A)
is the minimum number of balls of radius
\epsilon
needed to cover
A
.

We will need

Lemma 3.7
.

For
t\geq 1

\underline{\textnormal{dim}}_{M}\left[\textnormal{Range}\big(\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)\big)\right]\geq 2\;\;\text{a.s.}

Proof.

We first recall that for any set
A
we have

\underline{\textnormal{dim}}_{M}(A)\geq{\textnormal{dim}}_{H}(A),

where
{\textnormal{dim}}_{H}
is the Hausdorff dimension (see page 115 of
[
MP10
]
). We use the
energy method
(Theorem 4.27 in
[
MP10
]
) to get a lower bound on the Hausdorff dimension of the range. Let
\mu_{t}
be the occupation measure of
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})d\mu_{t}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{0}^{1}f\left(\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;x,0)\right)dx.

We just need to show that for any
0<\alpha<2

\mathbb{E}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{d\mu_{t}(\mathbf{x})d\mu_{t}(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{\alpha}}=\mathbb{E}_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxdy}{|\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;x,0)-\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;y,0)|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

Now
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t,x,0)-\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t,y,0)
is a Gaussian random variable with mean
0
and variance

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left[G(s,x,z)-G(s,y,z)\right]^{2}dzds.

From Lemma
3.6
this expression is bounded above and below by a constant multiple of
d(x,y)
. Therefore it follows that

\mathbb{E}_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxdy}{|\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;x,0)-\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;x,0)|^{\alpha}}\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxdy}{d(x,y)^{\alpha/2}}<\infty,

as required. Consequently

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{d\mu_{t}(\mathbf{x})d\mu_{t}(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{\alpha}}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

It thus follows from Theorem 4.27 in
[
MP10
]
that
\underline{\textnormal{dim}}_{M}\left[\textnormal{Range}\big(\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)\big)\right]\geq 2\;\text{a.s.}
as required.
∎

The lower bound in the lower Minkowski dimension gives a lower bound on the number of balls of radius
a
which intersect the range of
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)
, and consequently a lower bound on the volume of the sausage of radius
a
around
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Fix an arbitrary
0<\gamma<1
. There exists a
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\gamma}>0
such that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and any
0<a\leq 1

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|\mathscr{S}\left(a;\;\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)\right)|\geq\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\gamma}a^{d-2+\gamma}\right)\geq\frac{4}{5}.

Proof.

First note that the process
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)
is stationary in
t
. Partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into cubes of side length
a
. Let
\tilde{N}_{a}(\mathbf{N}^{(1)},t)
be the number of cubes through which
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t;\cdot,0)
passes. By Lemma
3.7
we obtain almost surely

\tilde{N}_{a}(\mathbf{N}^{(1)},t)\geq\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)^{2-\gamma}\text{ for all $a$ small enough}.

Therefore there exists a positive random variable
A(\omega)
which is finite almost surely such that

\tilde{N}_{a}(\mathbf{N}^{(1)},t)\geq A(\omega)\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)^{2-\gamma}\text{ for all }0<a\leq 1.

We now choose the largest possible subcollection of these
\tilde{N}_{a}(\mathbf{N}^{(1)},t)
cubes such that no two cubes are adjacent (that is, share a common edge). Let
N_{a}^{*}(\mathbf{N}^{(1)},t)
be the number of cubes in this subcollection. Clearly there exists a constant
C_{d}
such that almost surely

N_{a}^{*}(\mathbf{N}^{(1)},t)\geq A(\omega)C_{d}\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)^{2-\gamma}\text{ for all }0<a\leq 1.

From each of these
N_{a}^{*}(\mathbf{N}^{(1)},t)
cubes choose any point in the range of
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t,\cdot,0)
. The union of the balls of radius
a
around these points is contained in the sausage of radius
a
around
\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t,\cdot,0)
, so that

|\mathscr{S}\left(a;\;\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(t,\cdot,0)\right)|\geq A(\omega)\tilde{C}_{d}a^{d-2+\gamma}\quad\text{ for all }0<a\leq 1,

for some other constant
\tilde{C}_{d}>0
.
The constant
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}>0
is chosen so that

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(A(\omega)\tilde{C}_{d}\geq\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\gamma}\right)\geq\frac{4}{5}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We now use Lemma

on the smallness of
\mathbf{N}^{(2)}(t;\cdot,0)
to control the volume of the sausage around
\mathbf{N}(0,t)

If we choose

large enough the right hand side above is at most

As a consequence of this we obtain by a similar argument as in Lemma

Lemma 3.12
.

There exists

Proof.

Recall from (

S_{i+1}=\inf\left\{t\geq T_{i}+L:\mathcal{R}\Big(G_{t-T_{i}}*\mathbf{N}(T_{i-1},T_{i})\Big)\leq\delta\right\}.

The event

implies that
\mathcal{R}\Big(G_{t}*\mathbf{N}(T_{i-1},T_{i})\Big)>\delta
, and in light of Lemma

\left\|\mathbf{N}(T_{i-1},T_{i})-\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{N}(T_{i-1},T_{i};\,x)dx\right\|_{2}>\frac{\delta}{4d}e^{4\pi^{2}t}.

Using the subscript

to denote the components of

it follows that there exists a

We can formally write each component

of the white noise as
\mathbf{W}_{j}(dydr)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\imath(2\pi ky)}dB_{k}(r)dy
, where the

and so there exist positive constants

and

The second part of the lemma follows from

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\exp\left(\eta(S_{i+1}-T_{i})\right)\;\bigg|\;\mathcal{H}_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{\eta L}-\int_{L}^{\infty}e^{\eta t}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left[S_{i+1}-T_{i}>t\;\Big|\;\mathcal{H}_{i-1}\right]\,dt,

and the above tail bound.

\sqrt{s}\leq|x-y|

C\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{1}{2\pi|x-y|}}\frac{k^{2}s}{k^{2}}k^{2}|x-y|^{2}+C\sum_{k=\frac{1}{2\pi|x-y|}+1}^{\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{s}}}s+C\sum_{k=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{s}}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq C\sqrt{s}.

In the case that
|x-y|\leq\sqrt{s}
we obtain a bound

C\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{s}}}\frac{k^{2}s}{k^{2}}k^{2}|x-y|^{2}+C\sum_{k=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{s}}+1}^{\frac{1}{2\pi|x-y|}}\frac{k^{2}|x-y|^{2}}{k^{2}}+C\sum_{k=\frac{1}{2\pi|x-y|}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq C|x-y|.

Let us next consider the term (
3.22
). This is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dr\sum_{k\geq 1}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(2\pi k)^{2}(t+s-r)}{2}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(2\pi k)^{2}(t-r)}{2}\right)\right]^{2}\left[1\wedge\left|2\pi k(x-y)\right|\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}dr\sum_{k\geq 1}\exp\left(-\pi^{2}k^{2}r\right)\left|1-\exp\left(-2\pi^{2}k^{2}s\right)\right|^{2}\left[1\wedge\left|2\pi k(x-y)\right|\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}dr\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\left[1\wedge 2\pi^{2}k^{2}s\right]^{2}}{k^{2}}\left[1\wedge\left|2\pi k(x-y)\right|\right]^{2}

Therefore a similar bound as that for (
3.23

3.22
). The conclusion of our arguments is that

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{2}(t,s;x,y)\right]\leq C\left[\sqrt{s}\wedge|x-y|\right]

Since
\mathbf{Z}(t,s;x,y)
is Gaussian we obtain the lemma by standard arguments.
∎

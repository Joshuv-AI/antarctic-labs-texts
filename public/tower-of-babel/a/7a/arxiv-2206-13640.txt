Introduction

Let
N_{g,s}^{n}
be a smooth, non–orientable, compact surface of genus
g
with
s
boundary components and
n
punctures. If
s
and/or
n
is zero, then we omit it from the notation. If we do not want to emphasise the numbers

for a surface

. Recall that
N_{g}
is a connected sum of
g

is obtained from

by removing

\Sigma=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
of
n

N

Let
{\textrm{Diff}}(N)
be the group of all diffeomorphisms
h\colon N\to N
such that

is the identity
on each boundary component. By
{\mathcal{M}}(N)
we denote the quotient group of
{\textrm{Diff}}(N)
by
the subgroup consisting of maps isotopic to the identity, where we assume that isotopies are
the identity on each boundary component.
{\mathcal{M}}(N)
is called the
mapping class group
of
N
.

Let
{\mathcal{PM}}^{+}(N)
be the subgroup of
{\mathcal{M}}(N)
consisting of elements which fix
\Sigma
pointwise and preserve a local orientation around the punctures
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\}
.

1.1.
Main results

We showed in
[
2
]
that if
N_{g,s}
is a non–orientable surface of genus
g\geq 3
with
s\leq 1
boundary components, then

H_{1}({\mathcal{M}}(N_{g,s});H_{1}(N_{g,s};\mathbb{Z}))\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\text{if $g\in\{3,4,5,6\},$}\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\text{if $g\geq 7$.}\end{cases}

It is quite natural to extend this result to surfaces with more than one boundary component. However, this is quite challenging, because we do not have a simple presentation for the mapping class group in this case. Nevertheless,
in the forthcoming paper
[
1
]
we managed (among other homological results) to compute the first homology group
H_{1}({\mathcal{M}}(N_{g,s});H_{1}(N_{g,s};\mathbb{Z}))
for any

by bounding it both from above and below. However, it turned out that the proof of nontriviality of two important families of homology generators (denoted by
a_{1,3+j}
and
u_{1,3+j}
in
[
1
]
) is beyond the methods used in

[
1
]
. By a simple reduction, the required argument in
[
1
]
boils down to the exceptional case of a non-orientable surface
N_{3}^{2}
of genus 3 with two punctures. The main purpose of this paper is to fill this gap, and to prove the following two theorems

Theorem 1.1
.

If
N_{3,2}
is a non–orientable surface of genus
g=3
with 2 boundary components, then

H_{1}({\mathcal{M}}(N_{3,2});H_{1}(N_{3,2};\mathbb{Z}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{6}.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
N_{3}^{2}
is a non–orientable surface of genus
g=3
with 2 punctures, then

H_{1}({\mathcal{PM}^{+}}(N_{3}^{2});H_{1}(N_{3}^{2};\mathbb{Z}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{5}.

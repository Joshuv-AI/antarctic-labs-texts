Relative mixed multiplicities and mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities
Yairon Cid-Ruiz

Abstract
. We define and study the natural multigraded extension of the relative multiplicities introduced by Simis, Ulrich and Vasconcelos. We call these new invariants relative mixed multiplicities . We show that they have a stable value equal to the mixed Buchsbaum–Rim multiplicity of Kleiman and Thorup. Furthermore, we prove that integral dependence and birationality can be detected via the vanishing of relative mixed multiplicities.

1.
Introduction

Let
A\subseteq B
be an inclusion of standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded algebras over a Noetherian local ring
R=[A]_{(0,\ldots,0)}=[B]_{(0,\ldots,0)}
.
Assume that
{\rm length}_{R}\left([B]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}/[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\right)<\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.
Our main goal is to define and study a set of new invariants that we call
relative mixed multiplicities
and that provide the natural multigraded extension of the
relative multiplicities
introduced in seminal work of Simis, Ulrich and Vasconcelos
[
23
]
.
In the
\mathbb{N}
-graded setting (i.e., when
p=1
), they introduced a series of relative multiplicities
e_{t}(A,B)
, one for each
t\geq 1
, associated to the numerical functions
{\rm length}_{R}\left([B]_{n}\big/[A]_{n-t+1}[B]_{t-1}\right)
.
They also showed that the relative multiplicities
e_{t}(A,B)
have a stable value
e_{\infty}(A,B)
, and that the vanishing of
e_{\infty}(A,B)
detects integrality and the vanishing of
e_{1}(A,B)
detects both integrality and birationality.
The relative multiplicity
e_{1}(A,B)
was considered before by Kirby and Rees
[
16
]
(including a multigraded setting), whereas
e_{\infty}(A,B)
was introduced by Kleiman and Thorup
[
17
]
under the name of
generalized Buchsbaum–Rim multiplicity
.

In this paper, we extend the above invariants and results to a multigraded setting.
Our first main result is the following theorem that serves as the stepping stone to define the notion of relative mixed multiplicities.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 5.2
)
.

Fix a tuple
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
of positive integers.
Then the following function

\lambda_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{\left[B\right]_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})}}{\left[A\right]_{(n_{1}-t_{1}+1,\ldots,n_{p}-t_{p}+1)}\left[B\right]_{(t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{p}-1)}}\right)

coincides with a polynomial
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
for all
n_{i}\gg 0
.
Moreover,
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}
has total degree at most
\dim\left(\text{MultiProj}(B)\right)
and its normalized leading coefficients are nonnegative integers.

Let
X=\text{MultiProj}(B)
and
r=\dim(X)
.
The polynomial
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}
from
Theorem A
can be written as

P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;=\;\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,|\beta|=r}e_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)\,\frac{n_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots n_{p}^{\beta_{p}}}{\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{p}!}\;+\;\text{\rm(lower degree terms)}.

We say that the nonnegative integers
e_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)\geq 0
are the
relative mixed multiplicities of
B
over
A
.
We show that these new invariants are nonincreasing on the parameter
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and that they have a stable value.
Indeed, from
Section 5
, we obtain the inequality
e_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}(\beta;A,B)\leq e_{\mathbf{t}}(\beta;A,B)
for any
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}=(t_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,t_{p}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
with
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\geq\mathbf{t}
and the existence of the following limit

e_{\infty}(\beta;A,B)\;=\;\lim_{t_{1}\to\infty,\ldots,t_{p}\to\infty}e_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})}\left(\beta;A,B\right).

As it turns out, this stable value coincides with the
mixed Buchsbaum–Rim multiplicity
of Kleiman and Thorup
[
18
]
:

e_{\infty}(\beta;A,B)\;=\;\br_{\beta}\left([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};B\right)\;=\;\int_{P}c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{1})\right)^{\beta_{1}}\cdots c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{p})\right)^{\beta_{p}}\,\cap\,\left[P\right]_{r};

here
P=P_{X}^{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{p}}
denotes the Kleiman–Thorup transform of
X=\text{MultiProj}(B)
with respect to
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{p}
, where
Z_{i}=V_{+}\left([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}B\right)\subset X
is the closed subscheme determined by
[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}
(see
Section 2
and
Section 2
for further details).

Our proof of
Theorem A
is based upon using two main ingredients.
Firstly, we consider the stable value
e_{\infty}(\beta;A,B)=\br_{\beta}([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};B)
, which is geometrical by nature.
For that, we extend the joint blow-up construction of Kleiman and Thorup to an arbitrary multigraded setting in
Section 2
, and this allows us to define the mixed Buchsbaum–Rim multiplicity
\br_{\beta}([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};B)
.
Secondly, in
Section 4
, we identify and introduce new joint blow-up constructions that measure the difference between each relative mixed multiplicity
e_{\mathbf{t}}(\beta;A,B)
and the stable value
e_{\infty}(\beta;A,B)
.
Therefore, our approach is to define
e_{\mathbf{t}}(\beta;A,B)
as a sum of intersection numbers (multidegrees) in terms of the joint blow-up construction of Kleiman and Thorup and our new joint blow-up construction.
More precisely, in
Section 5
, we show the equality

e_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)\;=\;\br_{\beta}\left([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};B\right)\;+\;j_{\beta}^{\#}\big([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}\big),

where
j_{\beta}^{\#}([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}})
is a mixed multiplicity of one of our new joint blow-up constructions considered over the ideal
\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}=\big([B]_{(t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{p}-1)}\big)\subset B
(see
Section 4
for further details).

Similarly to the
\mathbb{N}
-graded case, we obtain that the distinguished relative mixed multiplicities
e(\beta;A,B)=e_{(1,\ldots,1)}(\beta;A,B)
and
e_{\infty}(\beta;A,B)
can detect integral dependence and birationality.
Our second main result is the following theorem.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 6.2
)
.

Consider the associated morphism

f\;:\;U\;\subseteq\;X=\text{MultiProj}(B)\;\longrightarrow\;Y=\text{MultiProj}(A)

where
U=X\setminus V_{+}(A_{++}B).

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If we obtain a finite morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, then
e_{\infty}\left(\beta;A,B\right)=0
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|=r
.
The converse holds if
B
is equidimensional and catenary.

(ii)

If we obtain a finite birational morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, then
e\left(\beta;A,B\right)=0
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|=r
.
The converse holds if
B
is equidimensional and catenary.

To the best of our knowledge, even in the
\mathbb{N}
-graded case, the result of
Theorem B
seems to be new (see
Section 6
and
Section 6
): for the converse statements of
Theorem B
, it is typically assumed that
B
is equidimensional and
universally
catenary (see
[
23
,
17
,
15
]
).

.

Let
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}

\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}

\mathbf{0}:=(0,\ldots,0)

\mathbf{1}:=(1,\ldots,1)

and
1
, respectively.

{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}

.
We say that an

2.
An extension of a joint blow-up construction of Kleiman and Thorup

Here, we provide a multigraded extension of a joint blow-up construction of Kleiman and Thorup
[
18
]
, and we closely follow their approach.
As a consequence, we can introduce the notion of mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities in an arbitrary multigraded setting.
Throughout this section the following setup is fixed.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
B
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded algebra over a Noetherian local ring
R=\left[B\right]_{(0,\ldots,0)}
.
Denote by
{\mathfrak{m}}\in R
the maximal ideal of
R
.
Let
X=\text{MultiProj}(B)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{m_{1}}\times_{R}\cdots\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{m_{p}}
be the multiprojective scheme determined by
B
, embedded in a product of projective spaces over
R
.
Let
q\geq 1
be a positive integer.
For each
1\leq i\leq q
, let
\mathbf{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
be a tuple of nonnegative integers and
H_{i}\subseteq[B]_{\mathbf{d}_{i}}
be a nonzero
R
-submodule.
Let
Z_{i}\subset X
be the closed subscheme determined by
H_{i}
and
\mathcal{J}_{i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
be the corresponding ideal sheaf.
Let
Z\subset X
be the closed subscheme defined by the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{1}\cdots\mathcal{J}_{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Let
\widehat{X}:=\fSpec_{X}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}[t]\right)=X\times_{\text{Spec}(R)}\mathbb{A}_{R}^{1}
be the relative affine line over
X
.
We view
X
as a closed subscheme embedded in
\widehat{X}
as the principal divisor
\{t=0\}
.
Let
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}:={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{q}}\big(\widehat{X}\big)
be the joint blow-up of
\widehat{X}
along the closed subschemes
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{q}
.
This scheme can be obtained by successively blowing-up
\widehat{X}
along
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{q}
.
It arises from the
\mathbb{N}^{q}
-graded quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}
-algebra

\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}\big((\mathcal{J}_{1},t),\ldots,(\mathcal{J}_{q},t)\big)\;:=\;\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{q})\in\mathbb{N}^{q}}(\mathcal{J}_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(\mathcal{J}_{q},t)^{n_{q}};

i.e., the multi-Rees algebra of the ideal sheaves
(\mathcal{J}_{1},t),\ldots,(\mathcal{J}_{q},t)\subset\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}
.
As we wish to embed
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
in a product of projective spaces, we consider the “twisted”
\mathbb{N}^{q}
-graded algebra

\mathscr{T}\;:=\;\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{q})\in\mathbb{N}^{q}}(\mathcal{J}_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(\mathcal{J}_{q},t)^{n_{q}}\,\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}}\,\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}\left(n_{1}\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+n_{q}\mathbf{d}_{q}\right).

The algebras
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{T}
yield the same scheme
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
(see
[
11

\mathbb{N}
-graded case).
Since the sheaf
(\mathcal{J}_{i},t)(\mathbf{d}_{i})

\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}^{r_{i}+1}\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal{J}_{i},t)(\mathbf{d}_{i})
for some
r_{i}\geq 0
.
In turn, this naturally gives the surjection

\text{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}^{r_{1}+1}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}}\text{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}^{r_{q}+1}\right)\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\mathscr{T}.

Consequently, we obtain a closed immersion
\iota:\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{X}}^{r_{1}}\times_{\widehat{X}}\cdots\times_{\widehat{X}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{X}}^{r_{q}}\cong\widehat{X}\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{r_{1}}\times_{R}\cdots\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{r_{q}}
.
Then we set

\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathbf{e}}_{i})\,:=\,\iota^{*}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{X}}^{r_{1}}\times_{\widehat{X}}\cdots\times_{\widehat{X}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\widehat{X}}^{r_{q}}}({\mathbf{e}}_{i})

for any
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
with
1\leq i\leq q
.
Thus our choice of
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathbf{e}}_{i})
equals the corresponding tautological line bundle determined by
\mathscr{R}
but twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}(\mathbf{d}_{i})
.

By an abuse of notation, we shall write
\mathcal{O}_{X}({\mathbf{e}}_{i})
for some
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
1\leq i\leq p
and
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathbf{e}}_{j})
for some
{\mathbf{e}}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
with
1\leq j\leq q
.
Thus, depending on the context,
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
could denote an elementary basis vector either in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
or in
\mathbb{N}^{q}
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
B
-module and
\mathscr{M}:=\widetilde{M}
be the corresponding coherent sheaf on
X
.
Let
\widehat{\mathscr{M}}:=\mathscr{M}[t]
be the

Denote by

The main objects of interest in this section are introduced in the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
P=P_{X}^{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{q}}:=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\times_{\widehat{X}}Z

Lemma 2.3
.

We have the inequality
\dim\left(\text{Supp}(P(\mathscr{M}))\right)\leq\dim\left(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M})\right)

Proof.

Let
U=\text{Spec}(A)\subset X
be an affine open subscheme and
N
be a finitely generated

\mathscr{M}{\mid_{U}}=\widetilde{N}
.
Let
J_{i}\subset A
be the ideal corresponding to

, and set
K_{i}=(J_{i},t)A[t]
.
Consider the module
L=\mathscr{R}_{N[t]}\left(K_{1},\ldots,K_{q}\right):=(N[t])\left[K_{1}T_{1},\ldots,K_{q}T_{q}\right]

over the multi-Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}_{A[t]}\left(K_{1},\ldots,K_{q}\right):=(A[t])\left[K_{1}T_{1},\ldots,K_{q}T_{q}\right]

Since

(see, e.g.,

[
5

Remark 2.4
.

As in the work of Kleiman and Thorup
[
17
,
18
]
, the intersection numbers stated below are well-defined by utilizing the rudiments of intersection theory developed by Thorup in
[

]

.
This treatment of Thorup extends the necessary results from Fulton’s book
[

]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume
Section 2
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
B
-module and
\mathscr{M}=\widetilde{M}
be the corresponding coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Set
r=\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
, and suppose that
Z\cap\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M})
is contained in the closed fiber
X\times_{\text{Spec}(R)}\text{Spec}(R/{\mathfrak{m}})
of
X
.
Then the following function

\lambda_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p},n_{1},\ldots,n_{q})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{[M]_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})+n_{1}\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+n_{q}\mathbf{d}_{q}}}{H_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots H_{q}^{n_{q}}\,[M]_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})}}\right)

coincides with a polynomial
P_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p},n_{1},\ldots,n_{q})
for all
v_{i}\gg 0
and
n_{i}\gg 0
.
Moreover,
P_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}
has total degree at most
r
and we can write

P_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p},n_{1},\ldots,n_{q})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{q}\\
|\alpha|+|\beta|=r\end{subarray}}e(\alpha,\beta)\,\frac{v_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots v_{p}^{\alpha_{p}}n_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots n_{q}^{\beta_{q}}}{\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{p}!\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{q}!}\;+\;\text{\rm(lower degree terms)}

where the normalized coefficient
e(\alpha,\beta)
equals the intersection number

\int_{P}c_{1}\left(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}({\mathbf{e}}_{1}))\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots c_{1}\left(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}({\mathbf{e}}_{p}))\right)^{\alpha_{p}}\,c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{1})\right)^{\beta_{1}}\cdots c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{q})\right)^{\beta_{q}}\,\cap\,\left[P(\mathscr{M})\right]_{r}.

Proof.

From our conventions above, we can identify
P
as a closed subscheme of a product of projective spaces over
R
via the following composition of natural closed immersions

\displaystyle f\;:\;P\;\hookrightarrow\;\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\times_{\text{Spec}(R[t])}\text{Spec}\left(R[t]/(t)\right)\;\hookrightarrow\;X\times_{R}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{r_{1}}\times_{R}\cdots\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{r_{q}}

\displaystyle\;\hookrightarrow\;{\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{m_{1}}\times_{R}\cdots\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{m_{p}}\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{r_{1}}\times_{R}\cdots\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{r_{q}}.

For any
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{q})\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
, the pullback of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
to
P
is given by

f^{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\big)\;\cong\;\pi^{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathbf{v})\big)\,\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}\,\mathcal{O}_{P}(\mathbf{n}).

To simplify notation, let
\mathcal{F}:=P(\mathscr{M})
,
\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{n}}):=P(\mathscr{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{n}})
and

\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\;:=\;P(\mathscr{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}f^{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\big)\;\cong\;P(\mathscr{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}\pi^{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathbf{v})\big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}\mathcal{O}_{P}(\mathbf{n})

for any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
.
From
[
17
, Lemma 4.3]
, it follows that the function

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi\big(f_{*}(\mathcal{F})(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\big)&=\sum_{i}{(-1)}^{i}{\rm length}_{R}\big(\text{H}^{i}\left({\mathbb{P}},f_{*}(\mathcal{F})(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\right)\big)\\
&=\sum_{i}{(-1)}^{i}{\rm length}_{R}\big(\text{H}^{i}\left(P,\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\right)\big)\end{split}

equals a polynomial
P_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}})
of total degree equal to
\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathcal{F}))\leq r=\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
(see
Section 2
).
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|=r
, the coefficient of
\mathbf{v}^{\alpha}\mathbf{n}^{\beta}
in
P_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
is equal to
\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{p}!\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{q}!}
times the intersection number

\int_{\mathbb{P}}\,c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbf{e}}_{1},\mathbf{0})\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbf{e}}_{p},\mathbf{0})\right)^{\alpha_{p}}\,c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbf{0},{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\right)^{\beta_{1}}\cdots c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbf{0},{\mathbf{e}}_{q})\right)^{\beta_{q}}\,\cap\,\left[f_{*}(\mathcal{F})\right]_{r};

where, depending on the context, we have either
\mathbf{0}=(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
or
\mathbf{0}=(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
.
By the projection formula, this latter intersection number coincides with
e(\alpha,\beta)
.

We need two observations regarding the natural projection
\pi:P\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{X}^{r_{1}}\times_{X}\cdots\times_{X}{\mathbb{P}}_{X}^{r_{q}}\rightarrow X
.
We set
\overline{T}(\mathscr{M}):=T(\mathscr{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
.
For any affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, by utilizing
[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
, we obtain that
\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{n}})\right){\mid_{U}}\cong\left[\overline{T}(\mathscr{M})\mid_{U}\right]_{\mathbf{n}}
and
R^{i}\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{n}})\right){\mid_{U}}=0
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}
and
i\geq 1
.
Hence we globally get

\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{n}})\right)\;\cong\;\left[\overline{T}(\mathscr{M})\right]_{\mathbf{n}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad R^{i}\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{n}})\right)=0

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}
and
i\geq 1
.
So, the Leray spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=\text{H}^{i}\left(X,R^{j}\pi_{*}(\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}))\right)\;\Rightarrow\;\text{H}^{i+j}\left(P,\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\right)

degenerates and yields the isomorphism

(2)

\text{H}^{i}\big(P,\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}})\big)\;\cong\;\text{H}^{i}\big(X,\left[\overline{T}(\mathscr{M})\right]_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{v})\big)

for all
i\geq 0
,
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
.

We now study the algebraic counterpart of
\overline{T}(\mathscr{M})
.
Given a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
B
-module
N
, we define the
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
B
-module

\overline{T}(N)\;:=\;\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{q})\in\mathbb{N}^{q}}\frac{(H_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(H_{q},t)^{n_{q}}N[t]}{(H_{1}\cdots H_{q},t)(H_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(H_{q},t)^{n_{q}}N[t]}\;\otimes_{B}\,B\left(n_{1}\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+n_{q}\mathbf{d}_{q}\right),

where
\deg(t)=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
We consider the Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded algebra
S:=\overline{T}(B)
.
Notice that
S
is a quotient of the multi-Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}_{B[t]}\left((H_{1},t),\ldots,(H_{q},t)\right):=(B[t])\big[(H_{1},t)T_{1},\ldots,(H_{q},t)T_{q}\big]
with
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-grading induced by the grading of
B
and by setting
\deg(T_{i})=-\mathbf{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and
\deg(t)=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Then
\overline{T}(M)
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
S
-module.
Notice that
B_{++}S=S_{++}:=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{v}\geq\mathbf{1}}[S]_{\mathbf{v}}
, where
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Therefore, we get the vanishing
\big[\text{H}_{B_{++}}^{i}\big(\overline{T}(M)\big)\big]_{\mathbf{v}}\cong\big[\text{H}_{S_{++}}^{i}\big(\overline{T}(M)\big)\big]_{\mathbf{v}}=0
for all
i\geq 0
and
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
(this follows verbatim to the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded result from
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
).
By utilizing
[
14
, Corollary 1.5]
, we obtain

(3)

\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\overline{T}(\mathscr{M})(\mathbf{v})\big)\;\cong\left[\overline{T}(M)\right]_{\mathbf{v}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{H}^{i}\big(X,\overline{T}(\mathscr{M})(\mathbf{v})\big)=0

for all
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
and
i\geq 1
.

By combining
(1)
,
(2)
and
(3)
, we get tuples of integers
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
, such that

(4)

P_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(E_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}}\right)

for all
\mathbf{v}\geq\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{n}^{\prime}
, where

E_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}}\;:=\;\left[\frac{(H_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(H_{q},t)^{n_{q}}M[t]}{(H_{1}\cdots H_{q},t)(H_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(H_{q},t)^{n_{q}}M[t]}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+n_{1}\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+n_{q}\mathbf{d}_{q}}.

We write

(H_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(H_{q},t)^{n_{q}}M[t]\;=\;\sum_{0\leq j_{i}\leq n_{i}}H_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots H_{q}^{j_{q}}\,t^{|{\mathbf{n}}|-(j_{1}+\cdots+j_{q})}\,M[t]\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{|{\mathbf{n}}|}L_{k}t^{|{\mathbf{n}}|-k}M[t],

where
L_{k}
is the
R
-module
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j_{i}\leq n_{i}\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{q}=k\end{subarray}}H_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots H_{q}^{j_{q}}
; by standard convention, we have
H_{i}^{0}=R=[B]_{\mathbf{0}}
.
Since
H_{1}\cdots H_{q}L_{k}M\subset L_{k+1}M
for
0\leq k<|{\mathbf{n}}|
, we get the equality

(H_{1}\cdots H_{q},t)(H_{1},t)^{n_{1}}\cdots(H_{q},t)^{n_{q}}M[t]\;=\;\Big(H_{1}\cdots H_{q}L_{|{\mathbf{n}}|}+\sum_{k=1}^{|{\mathbf{n}}|}L_{k}t^{|{\mathbf{n}}|+1-k}+t^{|{\mathbf{n}}|+1}\Big)M[t].

Therefore, we obtain the isomorphism of
R
-modules

E_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}}\;\cong\;\left[\frac{L_{|{\mathbf{n}}|}M}{H_{1}\cdots H_{q}L_{|{\mathbf{n}}|}M}\,\;\;\bigoplus\;\;\,\bigoplus_{k=0}^{|{\mathbf{n}}|-1}\frac{L_{k}M}{L_{k+1}M}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+n_{1}\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+n_{q}\mathbf{d}_{q}}

and consequently the equality

(5)

{\rm length}_{R}\left(E_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}}\right)={\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{[M]_{\mathbf{v}+n_{1}\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+n_{q}\mathbf{d}_{q}}}{H_{1}^{n_{1}+1}\cdots H_{q}^{n_{q}+1}\,[M]_{\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{d}_{1}-\cdots-\mathbf{d}_{q}}}\right)=\lambda_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{d}_{1}-\cdots-\mathbf{d}_{q},\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{1}).

Finally,
(4)
and
(5)
yield the equality
\lambda_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})=P_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{d}_{q},\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1})
for all
\mathbf{v}\geq\mathbf{v}^{\prime}-\mathbf{d}_{1}-\cdots-\mathbf{d}_{q}
and
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{n}^{\prime}+\mathbf{1}
.
The polynomials
P_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{d}_{q},\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1})
and
P_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
have the same top degree part.
Therefore, by setting
P_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}):=P_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{d}_{q},\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1})
, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Finally, after having the result of
Theorem 2.5
, we can make the following definition.

Definition 2.6
.

Under the notation and assumptions of
Theorem 2.5
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|\geq r
, we say that the
mixed Buchsbaum–Rim multiplicity of
M
of type
(\alpha,\beta)
with respect to
H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}
is the following nonnegative integer

\br_{\alpha,\beta}\left(H_{1},\ldots,H_{q};M\right)\;:=\;\begin{cases}e(\alpha,\beta)&\;\;\text{ if }|\alpha|+|\beta|=r\\
0&\;\;\text{ if }|\alpha|+|\beta|>r.\end{cases}

Notice that
\br_{\alpha,\beta}\left(H_{1},\ldots,H_{q};M\right)
can be defined either in geometrical terms as an intersection number associated to the Kleiman–Thorup transform
P(\mathscr{M})
or in algebraic fashion by utilizing the Hilbert-like function
\lambda_{M}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}
.
For any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|\geq r
, we also define

\br_{\beta}(H_{1},\ldots,H_{q};M)\;:=\;\br_{\mathbf{0},\beta}\left(H_{1},\ldots,H_{q};M\right)\;=\;\int_{P}c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{1})\right)^{\beta_{1}}\cdots c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{q})\right)^{\beta_{q}}\,\cap\,\left[P(\mathscr{M})\right]_{r}.

3.
Mixed
j
-multiplicities

In this short section, we quickly develop some basic results regarding a multigraded version of
j
-multiplicities
(see
[
1
]
,
[
8
, §6.1]
).
These results will be needed in the next section.
The setup below is used throughout this section.

Setup 3.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Noetherian local ring and
B
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
R
-algebra.
Set
X=\text{MultiProj}(B)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
B
-module and
\mathscr{M}:=\widetilde{M}
be the corresponding coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Recall that
\dim(\mathscr{M})=\dim\big(M/\text{H}_{B_{++}}^{0}(M)\big)-p
(see, e.g.,
[
14
, §1]
,
[
5
, §3.1]
).
Notice that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M)
is finitely generated over
B/{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}B
for
k\gg 0
, and this latter ring is a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded algebra over the Artinian local ring
R/{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}
.
Then we may define the mixed
j
-multiplicities of
M
as the mixed multiplicities of
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M)
.
For details on the notion of
mixed multiplicities
, the reader is referred to
[
6
,
12
]
.
Fix
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|\geq\dim\big(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M})\big)
.

Definition 3.2
.

The
mixed
j
-multiplicity of
M
of type
\beta
is given by

j_{\beta}(M):=e\big(\beta;\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M)\big).

We have the following expected results (cf.,
[
8
, §6.1]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0\rightarrow M^{\prime}\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
B
-modules.
Then we have the equality

j_{\beta}(M)\;=\;j_{\beta}(M^{\prime})+j_{\beta}(M^{\prime\prime}).

Proof.

The proof follows similarly to
[
8
, Proposition 6.1.2]
.
We have an exact sequence
0\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M^{\prime\prime})
.
Let
Q:=\text{Coker}\left(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M^{\prime\prime})\right)
.
For any minimal prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
M
that belongs to the closed fiber
X\times_{\text{Spec}(R)}\text{Spec}(R/{\mathfrak{m}})
, we obtain
M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}=\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})
,
M_{\mathfrak{p}}=\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime\prime}=\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime\prime})
.
Hence
\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{Q}))<\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
, and the result follows from the additivity of mixed multiplicities.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

We have that
j_{\beta}(M)=j_{\beta}\big(M/\text{H}_{B_{++}}^{0}(M)\big)
.

Proof.

Since
j_{\beta}\big(\text{H}_{B_{++}}^{0}(M)\big)=0
, the result follows from
Section 3
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

We have the equality

j_{\beta}(M)\;=\;\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}}{\rm length}_{B_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\cdot e(\beta;B/{\mathfrak{p}})

where
{\mathfrak{p}}
runs over the minimal primes of
M
that belong to the closed fiber
X\times_{\text{Spec}(R)}\text{Spec}(R/{\mathfrak{m}})
and satisfy
\dim(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=|\beta|+p
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
8
, Proposition 6.1.3]
.
However we now use the additivity result of
Section 3
.
∎

4.
New joint blow-up constructions

This section introduces new joint blow-up constructions that will be our second main ingredient to define relative mixed multiplicities.
The setup below is used throughout this section.

Setup 4.1
.

Let
B
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded algebra over a Noetherian local ring
R=\left[B\right]_{(0,\ldots,0)}
with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset R
.
For each
1\leq i\leq p
, let
H_{i}\subseteq[B]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}
be a nonzero
R
-submodule, and
I_{i}=\left(H_{i}\right)\subset B
be the ideal generated by
H_{i}
.
Let
I=I_{1}+\cdots+I_{p}\subset B
be the ideal given as the sum of the ideals
I_{i}
.
Let
X=\text{MultiProj}(B)
.

We consider the multi-Rees algebra

\mathscr{R}\;=\;\mathscr{R}\left(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}\right)\;:=\;\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\;T_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots T_{p}^{n_{p}}\;\subset\;B[T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}],

where
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
are new variables.
We endow
\mathscr{R}
with a standard
(\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-grading.
For any homogeneous element
b\in B
, we set

\deg_{\mathscr{R}}(b)=\deg_{B}(b)\oplus\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p},

and for any nonzero element
h\in H_{i}
, we set

\deg_{\mathscr{R}}(hT_{i})=\mathbf{0}\oplus{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p}.

This implies that
\mathscr{R}
is generated by elements of total degree one, and thus it is standard
(\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-graded as an
R
-algebra.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
B
-module and
\mathscr{M}:=\widetilde{M}
be the corresponding coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Then we obtain the following finitely generated
(\mathbb{Z}^{p} \oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module

\mathscr{R}_{M}\;=\;\mathscr{R}\left(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p};M\right)\;:=\;\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}M\;T_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots T_{p}^{n_{p}}\;\subset\;M[T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}].

For any
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, the corresponding
(\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}})
-graded part of
\mathscr{R}_{M}
is given by

\left[\mathscr{R}_{M}\right]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}\;=\;\left[I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}M\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}.

We now introduce our new joint blow-up constructions.
We take the Rees algebra of the ideal
I\mathscr{R}
over the previously defined multi-Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}
, that is:

\mathfrak{C}\;=\;\mathfrak{C}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\;:=\;\mathscr{R}\left(I\mathscr{R}\right)\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\,I^{k}\mathscr{R}\,T^{k}\;\subset\;\mathscr{R}[T].

We put a standard
(\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-grading on
\mathfrak{C}
by naturally extending the one of
\mathscr{R}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{C}
has an “internal grading” induced by
\mathscr{R}
.
For any homogeneous element
w\in\mathscr{R}
, we set
\deg_{\mathfrak{C}}(w)=\deg_{\mathscr{R}}(w)
, and for any nonzero element
h\in H_{i}\subset I
, we set

\deg_{\mathfrak{C}}(hT)=\deg_{\mathscr{R}}(h).

Hence
\mathfrak{C}
is generated by elements of total degree one, and so it is also standard
(\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-graded as an
R
-algebra.
The corresponding associated graded ring

\mathfrak{G}\;=\;\mathfrak{G}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\;:=\;\gr_{I\mathscr{R}}(\mathscr{R})\;=\;\mathfrak{C}/I\mathfrak{C}\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{I^{k}\mathscr{R}}{I^{k+1}\mathscr{R}}

also obtains a standard
(\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-grading as an
R
-algebra.
We have the following finitely generated
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-graded modules

\mathfrak{C}_{M}\;=\;\mathfrak{C}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p};M)\;:=\;\mathscr{R}\left(I\mathscr{R};\mathscr{R}_{M}\right)\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\,I^{k}\mathscr{R}_{M}T^{k}\;\subset\;\mathscr{R}_{M}[T]

and

\mathfrak{G}_{M}\;=\;\mathfrak{G}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p};M)\;:=\;\mathfrak{C}_{M}/I\mathfrak{C}_{M}\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{I^{k}\mathscr{R}_{M}}{I^{k+1}\mathscr{R}_{M}}

over
\mathfrak{C}
and
\mathfrak{G}
, respectively.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=\text{MultiProj}(\mathfrak{G})
be the multiprojective scheme determined by
\mathfrak{G}
(which can naturally be embedded in a product of the form
{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{m_{1}}\times_{R}\cdots\times_{R}{\mathbb{P}}_{R}^{m_{2p}}
), and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}_{M}}
be the corresponding coherent
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-module.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have the inequality
\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}_{M}}))\leq\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{M}=M/\text{H}_{B_{++}}^{0}(M)
.
Notice that the
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
-graded part of
\mathfrak{G}_{M}
is given by

\displaystyle\big[\mathfrak{G}_{M}\big]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{I^{k}\mathscr{R}_{M}}{I^{k+1}\mathscr{R}_{M}}\right]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{I^{k}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}M}{I^{k+1}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}M}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{|\mathbf{v}|-{\rm indeg}(M)}\left[\frac{I^{k}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}M}{I^{k+1}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}M}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}

where
{\rm indeg}(M):=\inf\big\{v\in\mathbb{Z}\mid[M]_{\mathbf{v}}\neq 0\text{ for some $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}$ with $|\mathbf{v}|=v$}\big\}
.
Since
[M]_{\mathbf{v}}\cong\left[\overline{M}\right]_{\mathbf{v}}
for all
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
, it follows that
\big[\mathfrak{G}_{M}\big]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}\cong\big[\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{M}}\big]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}
for all
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Therefore
\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}_{M}}\cong\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{M}}}
.
We have
\dim(\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{M}})\leq\dim(\overline{M})+p
(see, e.g,
[
13
, Chapter 5]
).
Hence we obtain

\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}_{M}}))\;\leq\;\dim(\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{M}})-2p\;\leq\;\dim(\overline{M})-p\;=\;\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M})),

as required.
∎

As a consequence of the above dimension computation, we can introduce the following invariants.
Similar invariants were introduced in the
\mathbb{N}
-graded case by Ulrich and Validashti
[
25
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|\geq\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
, we consider the following mixed
j
-multiplicities

j_{\alpha,\beta}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big)\;:=\;j_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{G}_{M})\quad\text{ and }\quad j_{\alpha,\beta}^{\#}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big)\;:=\;j_{\alpha,\beta}(B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{M});

see
Section 3
.

We also set
j_{\beta}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big):=j_{\mathbf{0},\beta}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big)
and
j_{\beta}^{\#}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big):=j_{\mathbf{0},\beta}^{\#}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big)
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|\geq\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
.

The next remark tells us that under the conditions needed to define relative mixed multiplicities, we have that the mixed
j
-multiplicities of
B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{M}
are just mixed multiplicities.

Remark 4.4
.

Assume
{\rm length}_{R}\left([B]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}/H_{i}\right)<\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.
Then
\overline{\mathfrak{G}}:=\mathfrak{G}/(0:_{\mathfrak{G}}B_{+}\mathfrak{G})
is a standard
(\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-graded algebra over the Artinian local ring
\Big[\overline{\mathfrak{G}}\Big]_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0}}=R/\mathfrak{a}
with
\mathfrak{a}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\text{Ann}_{R}\left([B]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}/H_{i}\right)
.
Notice that
B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{M}
is always a finitely generated
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-graded module over
\overline{\mathfrak{G}}
.
Therefore, we obtain that
j_{\alpha,\beta}^{\#}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big)=e(\alpha,\beta;B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{M})
.

The following theorem shows the existence of a polynomial that will be essential to our approach to defining relative mixed multiplicities.
This existence result is a byproduct of considering the above joint blow-up constructions.
The leading coefficients of this polynomial will coincide with the mixed multiplicities of
B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}}
for a certain ideal
\mathscr{H}\subset B
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume
Section 4
and that
{\rm length}_{R}\left([B]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}/H_{i}\right)<\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.
Set
r=\dim(X)
.
Fix a tuple
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
of nonnegative integers, and let
\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}\subset B
be the ideal generated by the elements in the graded part
[B]_{\mathbf{t}}
.
Then the following function

\lambda_{\mathbf{t}}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p},n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})={\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{{H_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{H_{p}}^{n_{p}}\,[B]_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})}}{H_{1}^{v_{1}+n_{1}-t_{1}}\cdots H_{p}^{v_{p}+n_{p}-t_{p}}\,[B]_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})}}\right)

coincides with a polynomial
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p},n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
for all
v_{i}\gg 0
and
n_{i}\gg 0
.
Moreover,
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}}
has total degree at most
r
and we can write

P_{\mathbf{t}}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p},n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\alpha|+|\beta|=r\end{subarray}}e\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\,\frac{v_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots v_{p}^{\alpha_{p}}n_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots n_{p}^{\beta_{p}}}{\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{p}!\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{p}!}\;+\;\text{\rm(lower degree terms)}

where
e(\alpha,\beta):=j_{\alpha,\beta}^{\#}\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{p};M\big)=e\left(\alpha,\beta;B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}\right)
is the mixed multiplicity of
B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}
of type
(\alpha,\beta)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathbf{v}>\mathbf{t}
.
Let
c=|\mathbf{v}|-|\mathbf{t}|
.
We study the finitely generated
(\mathbb{N}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{p})
-graded
\mathfrak{G}
-module
B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}
. From the above conventions, the
(\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}})
-graded part is given by

\displaystyle\big[B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}\big]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[B_{+}\cdot\frac{I^{k}\mathscr{R}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}}{I^{k+1}\mathscr{R}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}}\right]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[B_{+}\cdot\frac{I^{k}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}{I^{k+1}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{c-1}\left[B_{+}\cdot\frac{I^{k}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}{I^{k+1}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{c-1}\left[\frac{I^{k}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}{I^{k+1}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}.

Thus, we obtain the equality

{\rm length}_{R}\left(\big[B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}\big]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}\right)\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{\left[I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}}{\left[I^{c}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}}\right).

It is clear that

\left[I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}\;=\;H_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots H_{p}^{n_{p}}[B]_{\mathbf{v}}.

On the other hand, since

I^{c}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\;=\;\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{p}=c}I_{1}^{n_{1}+j_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}+j_{p}}

and
\big[I_{1}^{n_{1}+j_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}+j_{1}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}\big]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}=0
when any
j_{i}>v_{i}-t_{i}
, by the pigeonhole principle it follows that

\left[I^{c}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}+{\mathbf{n}}}\;=\;H_{1}^{v_{1}+n_{1}-t_{1}}\cdots H_{p}^{v_{p}+n_{p}-t_{p}}[B]_{\mathbf{t}}.

Then the result follows from the existence of (multigraded) Hilbert polynomials (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Lemma 4.3]
,
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
6
, Theorem 3.4]
).
Indeed, there is a polynomial
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}):=P_{B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
of total degree equal to
\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}}))
such that

P_{\mathbf{t}}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\;=\;P_{B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\big(\big[B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}\big]_{\mathbf{v},{\mathbf{n}}}\big)\;=\;\lambda_{\mathbf{t}}^{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})

for all
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Finally, we have the upper bound
\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{B_{+}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}}}}))\leq r=\dim(X)
(see
Section 4
).
∎

5.
Relative mixed multiplicities

In this section, we introduce the notion of relative mixed multiplicities.
Our existence proof follows by combining
Theorem 2.5
and
Theorem 4.5
.
The setup below is used during this section.

Setup 5.1
.

Let
A\subseteq B
be an inclusion of standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded algebras over a Noetherian local ring
R=[A]_{(0,\ldots 0)}=[B]_{(0,\ldots 0)}
.
Assume that
{\rm length}_{R}\left([B]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}/[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\right)<\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.
Let
X=\text{MultiProj}(B)
and
r=\dim(X)
.

The theorem below is the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Fix a tuple
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
of positive integers.
Then the following function

\lambda_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{\left[B\right]_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})}}{\left[A\right]_{(n_{1}-t_{1}+1,\ldots,n_{p}-t_{p}+1)}\left[B\right]_{(t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{p}-1)}}\right)

coincides with a polynomial
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
for all
n_{i}\gg 0
.
Moreover,
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}
has total degree at most
r
and its normalized leading coefficients are nonnegative integers.

Proof.

The proof follows by using
Theorem 2.5
,
Theorem 4.5
and the following short exact sequence

0\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{[A]_{\mathbf{n}}[B]_{\mathbf{v}}}{[A]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{1}}[B]_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{[B]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}}}{[A]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{1}}[B]_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{[B]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}}}{[A]_{\mathbf{n}}[B]_{\mathbf{v}}}\;\longrightarrow\;0.

From
Theorem 4.5
, we obtain a polynomial
P_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
of total degree at most
r
that satisfies the equality

P_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{[A]_{\mathbf{n}}[B]_{\mathbf{v}}}{[A]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{1}}[B]_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}}\right)

for all
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Similarly,
Theorem 2.5
gives a polynomial
P_{B}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
of total degree at most
r
such that

P_{B}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{[B]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}}}{[A]_{\mathbf{n}}[B]_{\mathbf{v}}}\right)

for all
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Consider the polynomial
P(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}):=P_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})+P_{B}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})
.
Then there exist vectors
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that

P(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{[B]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}}}{[A]_{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{1}}[B]_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}}\right)

for all
\mathbf{v}\geq\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{n}^{\prime}
.
By taking
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}({\mathbf{n}}):=P(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
, the result of the theorem follows.
∎

With
Theorem 5.2
in hand we are ready to introduce our new invariants:
relative mixed multiplicities
.

Definition 5.3
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Fix a tuple
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
of positive integers, and let
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}
as in
Theorem 5.2
.
Write

P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;=\;\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,|\beta|=r}e_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)\,\frac{n_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots n_{p}^{\beta_{p}}}{\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{p}!}\;+\;\text{\rm(lower degree terms)}.

We say that the nonnegative integer
e_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)\geq 0
is the
relative mixed multiplicity of
B
over
A
of type
(\mathbf{t},\beta)
.
For any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|>r
, we set
e_{\mathbf{t}}(\beta;A,B):=0
.

The next lemma shows that relative mixed multiplicities are nonincreasing on the parameter
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and that they eventually stabilize with value equal to a mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicity.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|\geq r
.
The following statements hold:

(i)

e_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)=\br_{\beta}\left([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};B\right)+j_{\beta}^{\#}\big([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}\big)
for all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
.

(ii)

(Nonincreasing):
For any
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
with
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\geq\mathbf{t}
, we have
e_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)\leq e_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\beta;A,B\right)
.

(iii)

(Stabilization):

\lim_{t_{1}\to\infty,\ldots,t_{p}\to\infty}e_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})}\left(\beta;A,B\right)\;=\;\br_{\beta}\left([A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}};B\right).

Proof.

(i) This part is clear from the proof of
Theorem 5.2
.

(ii) The result follows from part (i) and
Section 4
.

(iii) By
Theorem 2.5
and
Theorem 5.2
, there is some
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that, for all
\mathbf{t}>\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
and
{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}
, we have the equalities

P_{B}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(\frac{[B]_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}}{[A]_{\mathbf{n}}[B]_{\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}}}\right)=P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}({\mathbf{n}}+\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1}).

So, for all
\mathbf{t}\geq\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
, by comparing the leading coefficients of the polynomials
P_{B}^{[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}}(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1},{\mathbf{n}})
and
P_{\mathbf{t}}^{A,B}({\mathbf{n}}+\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{1})
in
{\mathbf{n}}
, the claim follows.
∎

In our applications in the next section, the relative mixed multiplicities
e_{(1,\ldots,1)}(\beta;A,B)
and the stable values will play an important role.
Hence we introduce the following notation.

Notation 5.5
.

For all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|\geq r
, set

e(\beta;A,B):=e_{(1,\ldots,1)}(\beta;A,B)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad e_{\infty}\left(\beta;A,B\right):=\lim_{t_{1}\to\infty,\ldots,t_{p}\to\infty}e_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})}\left(\beta;A,B\right).

.
Assume that
{\rm length}_{R}\left([B]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}/[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\right)<\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.

Our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume
Section 6
.
Consider the associated morphism

f\;:\;U\;\subseteq\;X=\text{MultiProj}(B)\;\longrightarrow\;Y=\text{MultiProj}(A)

where
U=X\setminus V_{+}(A_{++}B).

Set
r=\dim(X)
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If we obtain a finite morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, then
e_{\infty}\left(\beta;A,B\right)=0
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|=r
.
The converse holds if
B
is equidimensional and catenary.

(ii)

If we obtain a finite birational morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, then
e\left(\beta;A,B\right)=0
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\beta|=r
.
The converse holds if
B
is equidimensional and catenary.

Remark 6.3
.

We compare
Theorem 6.2
to known results in the
\mathbb{N}
-graded case.
To prove the converse statements of
Theorem 6.2
, one typically assumes that
B
is equidimensional and
universally
catenary.
For instance, the approach in
[
23
]
uses the associated graded ring
G=\gr_{A_{+}B}(B)
as the main tool, which yields an elegant and unified treatment.
However, if one considers
G
, one needs
G
to be equidimensional and catenary.
Then, the standard assumption for
G
to be equidimensional and catenary is that
B
is equidimensional and universally catenary (see
[
20
, Theorem 3.8]
,
[
23
, Lemma 2.2]
).

To circumvent any assumption on
G
, we work directly over the algebra
B
, and our primary tool is the technical result of
Section 6
.

The next corollary slightly improves some criteria from
[
23
]
by weakening an assumption of universally catenary to just catenary.
In the
\mathbb{N}
-graded case (i.e.,
p=1
), to recover the original definition of Simis, Ulrich and Vasconcelos
[
23
]
, we set
e_{t}(A,B):=e_{t}\left(\dim(B)-1;A,B\right)
(notice that one can have the strict inequality
\dim(X)<\dim(B)-1
).

Corollary 6.4

(cf.,
[
23
, Corollary 2.7, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Adopt the notation of
Theorem 6.2
in an
\mathbb{N}
-graded setting
(
i.e.,
p=1
)
.
Let
G=\gr_{A_{+}B}(B)
and
t\geq 1
.
Consider the following conditions:

(a)

B
is integral over
A
and
B_{\mathfrak{p}}=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}[B]_{i}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every minimal prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
A
.

(b)

\dim\left([B]_{t}G\right)<\dim(G)=\dim(B)
.

(c)

e_{t}(A,B)=0
.

In general, the implications
(a)

\Rightarrow

(b)

\Rightarrow

(c)
hold.
If
B
is equidimensional and catenary, the three conditions are equivalent.

Proof.

The implications (a)
\Rightarrow
(b)
\Rightarrow
(c) follow from
[
23
, Corollary 2.4, Proposition 3.2]
.
Thus we only need to show (c)
\Rightarrow
(a) under the assumption that
B
is equidimensional and catenary.
Let
\overline{B}=B/(0:_{B}B_{+}^{\infty})
and
\overline{A}=A/A\cap(0:_{B}B_{+}^{\infty})
.
Notice that
\overline{B}
is equidimensional and catenary, and that
A\hookrightarrow B
is integral if and only if
\overline{A}\hookrightarrow\overline{B}
is.
As
B
is equidimensional, we have either
\dim(\overline{B})=\dim(B)
or
\overline{B}=0
.
Therefore, we may assume that
r=\dim(X)=\dim(\overline{B})-1=\dim(B)-1
.
Since
e_{\infty}(r;A,B)=e_{\infty}(A,B)\leq e_{t}(A,B)=0
,
Theorem 6.2
(i) implies that
A\hookrightarrow B
is integral.

Next, we consider the finitely generated graded
A
-modules
M=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}[B]_{i}A\subseteq B
and
B
.
The associativity formula of
Section 3
yields the equalities

j_{r}(M)=\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}}{\rm length}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(M_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)e(A/{\mathfrak{p}})\quad\text{ and }\quad j_{r}(B)=\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}}{\rm length}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(B_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)e(A/{\mathfrak{p}}),

where, in both summations,
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
runs through the minimal primes of
A
such that
\dim(A/{\mathfrak{p}})=r+1
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R={\mathfrak{m}}
.
Notice that
A
is also equidimensional.
By additivity, we have
j_{r}(B)-j_{r}(M)=j_{r}(B/M)=e_{t}(A,B)=0
(see
Section 3
).
Since
{\rm length}_{R}([B]_{1}/[A]_{1})<\infty
, we obtain
M_{\mathfrak{p}}=B_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
with
{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R\subsetneq{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Therefore, it follows that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}=B_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every minimal prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
A
.
∎

Before proving the above theorem, we need a couple of technical lemmas.
The first one is inspired by Rees’ original proof of his celebrated integral dependence criterion
[
21
,
22
]
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
T
be a standard graded
R
-algebra, and
\pi:X=\text{Proj}(T)\rightarrow\text{Spec}(R)
be the natural projective morphism.
Let
x\in X
be a closed point and
P\subset T
be the corresponding relevant homogeneous prime ideal.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\pi
maps
x
to the closed point of
\text{Spec}(R)
, and thus
P\supset{\mathfrak{m}}T
.

(ii)

{\rm length}_{R}\big(\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\,\widetilde{P}\big)\big)=[\kappa(x):\kappa]
, where
\kappa(x)=\mathcal{O}_{x}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{x}
is the residue field at
x
.

(iii)

\lambda_{x}(n):={\rm length}_{R}\big(\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\,\widetilde{P^{n}}\big)\big)
eventually coincides with a polynomial
P_{x}(n)
of degree
d_{x}:=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{x})
and whose leading coefficient is
[\kappa(x):\kappa]\cdot e(\mathcal{O}_{x})/d_{x}!
.

(iv)

Let
H=[P]_{d}
such that
(H)=[P]_{\geq d}=\bigoplus_{v\geq d}[P]_{v}

(
i.e.,
d
is larger than or equal to the degrees of a set of homogeneous generators of
P
)
.
Then the function
L_{d}(v,n):={\rm length}_{R}\left([T]_{v+nd}\big/H^{n}[T]_{v}\right)
eventually becomes a polynomial
P_{d}(v,n)
.
Furthermore, we actually have
P_{d}(v,n)=P_{x}(n)
.

Proof.

(i) The morphism
\pi
is closed, hence it should send the closed point
x\in X
to the unique closed point in
\text{Spec}(R)
.
It clearly follows that
P\supset{\mathfrak{m}}T
.

(ii) Since
\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\,\widetilde{P}\big)\cong\kappa(x)
, the equality is clear.

(iii) For all
n\geq 1
, we have
\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\,\widetilde{P^{n}}\big)\cong\mathcal{O}_{x}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{x}^{n}
, and so we obtain

\lambda_{x}(n)\;=\;[\kappa(x):\kappa]\cdot{\rm length}_{\mathcal{O}_{x}}(\mathcal{O}_{x}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{x}^{n}).

This settles the claim, since
{\rm length}_{\mathcal{O}_{x}}(\mathcal{O}_{x}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{x}^{n})
is eventually the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of the local ring
(\mathcal{O}_{x},{\mathfrak{m}}_{x})
.

(iv) The fact that
L_{d}(v,n)
eventually coincides with a polynomial
P_{d}(v,n)
follows from the existence of mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities (see
Theorem 2.5
or
[
18
]
).
We fix
n_{0}
big enough so that
L_{d}(v,n_{0})
eventually becomes the polynomial
P_{d}(v,n_{0})
in
v
.
However, for
v\gg 0
, we have

L_{d}(v,n_{0})={\rm length}_{R}\left({\left[T/P^{n_{0}}\right]}_{v+n_{0}d}\right)={\rm length}_{R}\left(\text{H}^{0}\left(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\,\widetilde{P^{n_{0}}}\,(v+n_{0}d)\right)\right)=\lambda_{x}(n_{0}),

and the last equality follows because
x\in X
is a closed point.
This shows that
P_{d}(v,n)=P_{x}(n)
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

With the same notation of
Theorem 6.2
, assume that
B
is equidimensional and catenary.
Then, for any closed point
x\in X
, we have
d_{x}=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{x})=r
.

Proof.

We may assume
(0:_{B}B_{++})=0
, and so
\dim(B)=r+p
.
Notice that
B
is naturally a *local ring with *maximal ideal given by
{\mathfrak{M}}={\mathfrak{m}}+B_{+}
and that
r+p=\dim(B)=\HT({\mathfrak{M}})
; see
[
2
, §1.5]
.
Since
B
is equidimensional and catenary, any maximal chain of homogeneous prime ideals has length
r+p
. Let
x\in X
be a closed point with associated prime
P\subset B
.
As
x\in X
is a closed point,
\dim(B/P)=\dim\big(\overline{\{x\}}\big)+p=p
.
Therefore, we obtain
d_{x}=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{x})=\dim(B_{P})=\dim(B)-\dim(B/P)=(r+p)-p=r
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Proof of
Theorem 6.2
.

We may substitute
A
and
B
by
\overline{A}=A/A\cap(0:_{B}B_{++}^{\infty})
and
\overline{B}=B/(0:_{B}B_{++}^{\infty})
, respectively.
By utilizing the Segre embedding, we obtain the isomorphisms
X\cong\text{Proj}(T)
and
Y\cong\text{Proj}(S)
, where
S:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}[A]_{(n,\ldots,n)}
and
T:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}[B]_{(n,\ldots,n)}
.
Then we see
f:U\subseteq X=\text{Proj}(T)\rightarrow Y=\text{Proj}(S)
as the morphism associated to the inclusion
S\subseteq T
of standard graded
R
-algebras.

By considering the substituted polynomial
P_{\mathbf{1}}^{A,B}(n,\ldots,n)
(see
Theorem 5.2
), we can compute the equality

e(S,T)\;=\;\bigoplus_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,|\beta|=r}\frac{r!}{\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{p}!}\,e(\beta;A,B).

Similarly, as a consequence of
Section 5
(iii), by choosing
t>0
large enough and considering the substituted polynomial
P_{(t,\ldots,t)}^{A,B}(n,\ldots,n)
, we can compute the equality

e_{\infty}(S,T)\;=\;\bigoplus_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,|\beta|=r}\frac{r!}{\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{p}!}\,e_{\infty}(\beta;A,B).

From these reductions, we may instead prove the theorem in terms of the vanishing of
e(S,T)
and
e_{\infty}(S,T)
.

(i)
Suppose that
S\subseteq T
is an integral extension.
Then we may choose
t>0
large enough such that
[T]_{n}=[S]_{n-t+1}[T]_{t-1}
for all
n\geq 0
.
This shows that
e_{\infty}(S,T)=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
S\subseteq T
is not an integral extension and that
B
is equidimensional and catenary.
Then
S_{+}T
is a relevant ideal of
T
(i.e.,
\sqrt{S_{+}T}\not\supseteq T_{+}
).
Let
x\in X
be a closed point with associated prime
P\subset T
such that
P\supseteq S_{+}T
.
From
Section 6
, we get the equality
d_{x}=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{x})=r
.
By
Section 6
, for large enough integers
d>0
and
v>0
, we obtain

{\rm length}_{R}\big([T]_{v+nd}/[S]_{nd}[T]_{v}\big)\;\geq\;{\rm length}_{R}\big([T]_{v+nd}/H^{n}[T]_{v}\big)\;=\;P_{x}(n)\quad\text{ for }\quad n\gg 0,

where
H=[P]_{d}
and
P_{x}(n)
is a polynomial of degree
r
with leading coefficient
[\kappa(x):\kappa]\cdot e(\mathcal{O}_{x})/r!
.
It then follows that

e_{\infty}(S,T)\;\;=\;\;\frac{r!}{d^{r}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\rm length}_{R}\big([T]_{v+nd}/[S]_{nd}[T]_{v}\big)}{n^{r}}\;\;\geq\;\;\frac{r!}{d^{r}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{P_{x}(n)}{n^{r}}\;\;=\;\;\frac{[\kappa(x):\kappa]\cdot e(\mathcal{O}_{x})}{d^{r}}\;\;>\;\;0.

Therefore,
e_{\infty}(S,T)\geq 1
is a positive integer, as required.

(ii) After proving part (i), the result of
Section 6
is already valid.
In particular, the statement about birationality follows.
∎

It should be mentioned that in a multigraded setting the inclusion
A\subseteq B
may not be integral and yet we can still obtain a finite morphism:

Example 6.7
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and consider the following inclusion of standard
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebras

A=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]\;\;\hookrightarrow\;\;B=\frac{\mathbb{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]}{\big(x_{3}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}),\,y_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\big)}

with
\deg(x_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and
\deg(y_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
.
Then
f:{\text{BiProj}}(B)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\rightarrow{\text{BiProj}}(A)={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}
is a finite morphism (in fact, it is an isomorphism since
B/(0:_{B}B_{++})\cong B/(x_{3},y_{3})
).
However, the extension
A\hookrightarrow B
is not integral.

Finally, we briefly discuss the particular case of graphs of rational maps.

Remark 6.8
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
\mathfrak{d}_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{d_{2}}\subseteq\text{H}^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}(d)\big)
be two linear systems.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s_{1}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s_{2}}
be the respective rational maps.
Let
\Gamma_{i}
and
E_{i}
be the graph (blow-up of the base locus) and exceptional divisor of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, respectively.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

The induced rational map
f:\Gamma_{2}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s_{2}}\dashrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s_{1}}
between graphs is a finite birational morphism.

(b)

The projective degrees of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
coincide (see
[
10
, Example 19.4]
,
[
7
, §7.1.3]
).

(c)

The multidegrees of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
coincide.

The equivalence between (a) and (b) follows from
Theorem 6.2
.
Since
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{i}}(-E_{i})\cong\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{i}}(-d,1)
, we have the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{i}}(-d,1)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{i}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{E_{i}}\rightarrow 0
, and so we can compute the following equality on multidegrees (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
):

\deg^{(n_{1},n_{2})}(E_{i})\;=\;d\cdot\deg^{(n_{1}+1,n_{2})}(\Gamma_{i})-\deg^{(n_{1},n_{2}+1)}(\Gamma_{i})\quad\text{ for all }\quad n_{1}+n_{2}=r-1.

Therefore, since
\deg^{(r,0)}(\Gamma_{i})=1
, it follows that parts (b) and (c) are equivalent.

Alternatively, the equivalence between parts (a) and (c) could be deduced as a very particular case of the main result of
[
19
]
.
Indeed, important recent work of Polini, Trung, Ulrich and Validashti
[
19
]
shows that, over an equidimensional and universally catenary Noetherian local ring, two ideals
I\subset J
have the same integral closure if and only if their multiplicity sequences coincide.

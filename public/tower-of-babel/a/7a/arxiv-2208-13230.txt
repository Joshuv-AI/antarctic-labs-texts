Arithmetic Demailly Approximation Theorem
Binggang Qu, Hang Yin

Abstract
. We generalize the Demailly approximation theorem from complex geometry to Arakelov geometry. As an application, let X / ℚ X/\mathbb{Q} be an integral projective variety and N ¯ \overline{N} be an adelic line bundle on X X , we prove that ess ⁡ ( N ¯ ) ≥ 0 \operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0 ⟹ \Longrightarrow N ¯ \overline{N} pseudo-effective. This was proved in [ Bal21 ] , assuming N ¯ \overline{N} relatively semipositive. We show in the appendix that the above assertion is also true for adelic line bundles on quasi-projective varieties, under the framework of [ YZ22 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety and
\overline{L}
be an adelic line bundle.
\overline{L}
induces an height function
h_{\overline{L}}
on
X(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, which is of central importance in the study of Diophantine geometry.

It is natural to believe that the positivity of the height function
h_{\overline{L}}
is correlated with the positivity of the line bundle
\overline{L}
. In fact, in
[
BGPS15
]
Burgos Gil, Philippon and Sombra confirmed that
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0

\Longleftrightarrow

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective in toric cases. Later, Ballay showed for general (non-toric) varieties that

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Bal21
]
, Theorem 1.1)
.

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety and
\overline{N}
be an adelic line bundle. Assume
\overline{N}
is relatively semipositive and
N
is big, then
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0

\Longleftrightarrow

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective.

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, with the condition “
N
is big” Ballay was able to prove
\overline{N}
pseudo-effective
\Longrightarrow

\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0
and with the condition “
\overline{N}
relatively semipositive” he was able to prove
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0

\Longrightarrow

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective.

In a private conversation, Yuan made the following conjecture that both conditions are unnecessary.

Conjecture 1.3

(Yuan)
.

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety and
\overline{N}
be an adelic line bundle. Then
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0

\Longleftrightarrow

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective.

In this article, we will develop a new arithmetic Bertini type theorem and use it to prove (half of) Yuan’s conjecture that
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0

\Longrightarrow

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective.

1.2.
Function field analogue

Let us first consider the function field analogue, which follows after BDPP theorem and Bertini theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow S
be an integral projective variety, fibered over a curve
S
, over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
on
\mathcal{X}
be a line bundle. Then
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0\Longrightarrow

\mathcal{N}
pseudo-effective.

Proof.

Say
\dim\mathcal{X}=n+1
. Suppose conversely that
\mathcal{N}
is not pseudo-effective. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
s.t.
\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)
is also not pseudo-effective. Here
\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon\,\pi^{*}D
where
D
is any divisor on
S
of degree
1
. Then by BDPP theorem, and because essential minimum is birationally invariant, we may assume there exists an ample line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
(rather than on some
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
birational to
\mathcal{X}
) such that
\mathcal{L}^{n}\cdot\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)<0
.

Let
X\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{C}(S))
be the generic fiber of
\pi:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow S
. For any closed subset
Z\subseteq X
, by Bertini theorem, there exists a section
s
of
\mathcal{L}
such that
\operatorname{div}(s)
is irreducible (hence horizontal),
\operatorname{div}(s)_{\mathbb{C}(S)}
is smooth and
\operatorname{div}(s)_{\mathbb{C}(S)}
does not meet
Z
. Repeat this procedure
n
times, we produce a horizontal curve
C
that does not meet
Z
and
C\cdot\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)<0
. Since
Z
is arbitrary, we see that
\operatorname{ess}(\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon))=\operatorname{ess}(\mathcal{N})+\varepsilon\leq 0
. Then
\operatorname{ess}(\mathcal{N})\leq-\varepsilon
, contradiction.
∎

1.3.
Motivation

Now back to number fields, the idea is to mimic the above proof in function field cases.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
is an arithmetic surface and
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
is a Hermitianl line bundle. Again, to show
\mathrm{ess}(\overline{\mathcal{N}})\geq 0
implies
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
pseudo-effective, by arithmetic BDPP theorem of Ikoma (
[
Iko15b
]
, Theorem 6.4), essentially we need to show that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{N}}\geq 0
for any ample Hermitian line bundle
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
.

Now if we have a sequence of good sections
s_{n}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n\mathcal{L})
(which will be produced by our arithmetic Bertini theorem), such that

•

\tfrac{1}{n}\log\|s_{n}\|\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-sense.

•

{\operatorname{div}}(s_{n})
is irreducible (then
{\operatorname{div}}(s_{n})
is horizontal, say
{\operatorname{div}}(s_{n})=\big\{x_{n}\big\}
where
x_{n}
is a closed point of the generic fiber
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
).

Then we use these
s_{n}
to compute the arithmetic intersection number and see

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad n\overline{\mathcal{L}}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\deg}\left(\overline{\mathcal{N}}|_{\operatorname{div}(s_{n})}\right)-\int_{\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})}\log\|s_{n}\|\;c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{N}}).

Note that
x_{n}
is of degree
n\deg(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
h_{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}(x_{n})=\widehat{\deg}\left(\overline{\mathcal{N}}|_{\operatorname{div}(s_{n})}\right)/\deg(x_{n})
, so divide by
n\deg(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})
on both sides we see

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{N}}}{\deg(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})}=h_{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}(x_{n})-\frac{1}{n\deg(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})}\int_{\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})}\log\|s_{n}\|\;c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{N}}).

By assumption
\tfrac{1}{n}\log\|s_{n}\|\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-sense so when
n\longrightarrow\infty
, the second term in the right hand side tends to zero and we have

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{N}}}{\deg(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})}=\liminf_{n\longrightarrow\infty}h_{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}(x_{n})\geq\operatorname{ess}(\overline{\mathcal{N}})\geq 0.

The latter inequality is because
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a curve so the sequence
\big\{x_{n}\big\}
is automatically generic (meaning that it does not have a subsequence contained in a proper closed subvariety). We win.

Main theorems

Theorem 1.5

(arithmetic Demailly approximation, Theorem
4.10
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
be an arithmetic variety, normal and generically smooth. Let
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
be an ample Hermitian line bundle. Let
Y
be a closed subset of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Then there exists
s_{n_{i}}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n_{i}\mathcal{L})
such that

•

\|s_{n_{i}}\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 1
and
\frac{1}{n_{i}}\log\|s_{n_{i}}\|\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-topology.

•

\operatorname{div}(s_{n_{i}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
is smooth and does not contain any irreducible components of
Y
.

•

\operatorname{div}(s_{n_{i}})
has no vertical components.

As an application, we prove

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
be an arithmetic variety of relative dimension
d
and
\mathcal{\overline{N}}
be a Hermitian line bundle. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{ess}(\mathcal{\overline{N}})\leq\inf\frac{\mathcal{\overline{L}}^{d}\cdot\pi^{*}\mathcal{\overline{N}}}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}},

where the infimum is taken over all

•

\pi:\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a birational morphism with
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
normal and generically smooth.

•

\mathcal{\overline{L}}
is an ample Hermitian line bundle on
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.

Theorem
1.6
plus the arithmetic BDPP theorem of Ikoma (
[
Iko15b
]
, Theorem 6.4) gives

Theorem 1.7

(Corollary
5.4
)
.

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety and
\overline{N}
be an adelic line bundle. Then
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0

\Longrightarrow

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective.

In appendix we prove that this is also true for adelic line bundles on quasi-projective varieties.

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
A.1
)
.

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral quasi-projective variety and
\overline{N}
be an adelic line bundle. Then
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})\geq 0

\Longrightarrow

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective.

1.5.
Comparison with other arithmetic Bertini type theorems

In
[
Mor95
]
, Moriwaki proved

Theorem 1.9

(Moriwaki)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
be an arithmetic variety that is generically smooth, and let
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
be an ample Hermitian line bundle. Then for (not necessarily closed) points
x_{1},\dots,x_{q}
on
\mathcal{X}
, there exists a large
m
and a section
s\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},m\mathcal{L})
s.t.

(1)

\|s\|_{\infty}<1
.

(2)

{\operatorname{div}}(s)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is smooth over
\mathbb{Q}
;

(3)

s(x_{i})\neq 0
for each
i
;

Moriwaki’s theorem was later generalized by Ikoma
[
Iko15a
]
to general arithmetic linear series (rather than the full
\bigoplus_{n}H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n\mathcal{L})
).

A vast generalization is made in
[
Cha21
]
, where Charles proved

Theorem 1.10

(Charles)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
be an arithmetic variety that is generically smooth, and let
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
be an ample Hermitian line bundle. Let
Y\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be a closed subset. Then the set

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad E_{n}=\big\{

\displaystyle s\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{Ar}}(\mathcal{X},n\overline{\mathcal{L}}):{\operatorname{div}}(s)\text{ is irreducible},

\displaystyle{\operatorname{div}}(s)_{\mathbb{Q}}\text{ is smooth and does not contain any irreducible component of $Y$}\big\}

has density tends to
1
(i.e.
\#E_{n}/\#H^{0}_{\mathrm{Ar}}(\mathcal{X},n\overline{\mathcal{L}})\longrightarrow 1
). Here under Charles’ notation,

\displaystyle H^{0}_{\mathrm{Ar}}(\mathcal{X},\overline{\mathcal{L}})=\big\{s\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}):\|s\|_{\infty}\leq 1\big\}.

One sees that all existing results are imposing at infinite places the condition “
\|s\|_{\infty}\leq 1
”. This gives control of
\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})}\log\|s\|\;c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{N}})
only when the other line bundle is semipositive at infinity (i.e.
c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{N}})\geq 0
).

The novelty of our result is that, we at infinite places use a new condition “
\tfrac{1}{n}\log\|s_{n}\|\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-sense”. This controls arithmetic intersection number by making the
\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})}\log\|s\|\;c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{N}})
term vanish and works for arbitrary
\mathcal{\overline{N}}
. This is the reason why we could drop the relatively semipositive condition in Ballay’s theorem (
[
Bal21
]
, Theorem 1.1).

2.
Conventions and terminology

Definition 2.1
.

An
arithmetic variety
is a projective flat morphism
\pi:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
with
\mathcal{X}
integral and a
Hermitian line bundle
on
\mathcal{X}
is the datum
\overline{\mathcal{L}}=(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|)
where

•

\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
;

•

\|\cdot\|
is a smooth Hermitian metric on the line bundle
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})
that is smooth and invariant under complex conjugation.

Definition 2.2
.

A Hermitian line bundle induces an Arakelov height function

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}}:X(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{\widehat{\deg}\left(\overline{\mathcal{L}}|_{\overline{\{x\}}}\right)}{\deg(x)}.

Definition 2.3
.

A Hermitian line bundle
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
is called
ample
if

•

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is ample on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

•

\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is vertically positive, i.e.

–

c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}})
is a positive
(1,1)
-form,

–

for any vertical curve
C
,
\deg{\mathcal{L}}|_{C}>0
.

•

\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is horizontally positive, i.e. for any horizontal closed subvariety
\mathcal{Y}
,
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\dim\mathcal{Y}}\cdot\mathcal{Y}>0
.

Remark 2.4
.

This is equivalent to the definition used by Ikoma
[
Iko15b
]
and is stronger than Zhang’s original definition, which required only vertically semipositive. We want to be consistent with Ikoma, so that no confusion is made when we invoke his arithmetic BDPP theorem.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety. An
adelic line bundle
is the datum
\overline{L}=\big(L,\big\{\|\cdot\|_{p}\big\}_{p\leq\infty}\big)
where

•

L
is a line bundle on
X
;

•

\|\cdot\|_{p}
is a continuous metric on
L_{p}^{\text{an}}
on
X_{p}^{\text{an}}
,

and satisfying the
coherence
condition that there exists an open set
U\subseteq{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{Z})
and a model
(\mathcal{X}_{U},\mathcal{L}_{U})
of
(X,L)
over
U
that induces
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for all
p\in U
.

Definition 2.6
.

An adelic line bundle induces an adelic height function

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad h_{\overline{L}}:X(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto-\frac{1}{\deg(x)}\sum_{p\leq\infty}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in O(x)}\log\|s(x^{\prime})\|_{p}

where
s
is any rational section of
L
non-vanishing at
x
.

Theorem 2.7
.

An adelic line bundle is a uniform limit of Hermitian line bundles. To be precise, let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety and
\overline{L}=\big(L,\big\{\|\cdot\|_{p}\big\}_{p\leq\infty}\big)
be an adelic line bundle, then there exists an open subset
U\subseteq{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{Z})
and a sequence of Hermitian models
(\mathcal{X}_{n},\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n})
of
(X,L)
(here
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
are allowed to be
\mathbb{Q}
-Hermitian line bundles) such that

•

for
p\in U
,
\|\cdot\|_{n,p}=\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

•

for
p\notin U
and finite,
\|\cdot\|_{n,p}/\|\cdot\|_{p}\longrightarrow 1
uniformly. Here
\|\cdot\|_{n,p}
is the metric induced by the model
(\mathcal{X}_{n},{\mathcal{L}}_{n})
at
p
.

•

for
p=\infty
,
\|\cdot\|_{n,\infty}/\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 1
uniformly.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\overline{L}
be an adelic line bundle, the set of
small sections
is defined to be

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\overline{L})=\big\{s\in H^{0}(X,L):\sup\|s\|_{p}\leq 1\text{ for all $p\leq\infty$}\big\}

and we write
h^{0}(X,L)=\log\#H^{0}(X,\overline{L})
.
Say
\dim X=d
, the
arithmetic volume
is defined to be

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\overline{L})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,n\overline{L})}{n^{d+1}/(d+1)!}.

Definition 2.9
.

An adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
is called
big
if
\operatorname{vol}(\overline{L})>0
.

Definition 2.10
.

An adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
is called
pseudo-effective
if for any big adelic line bundle
\overline{B}
and any

,
\overline{L}+\frac{1}{n}\overline{B}
is still big.

Theorem 2.11

(arithmetic BDPP theorem,
[
Iko15b
]

.

Let
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
be an arithmetic variety and
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
be a Hermitian line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
. Then
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
is pseudo-effective if and only if for any birational morphism
\pi:\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
normal and generically smooth, and for any ample Hermitian line bundle
\overline{\mathcal{L}}

3.
Arithmetic Demailly approximation I: infinite places

3.1.
Demailly approximation theorem in complex geometry

The following is a baby case of the Demailly approximation theorem (see
[
Dem10
]
, Corollary 13.23 or
[
GZ17
]
, Theorem 8.9), but with extra sup-norm estimation. It is not written directly in the literature, so for the readers’ convenience we shall give a short proof here. There is nothing original.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a projective complex manifold and
\overline{L}=(L,\|\cdot\|)
be an ample line bundle with smooth positive Hermitian metric
\|\cdot\|
. Then there exists
s_{n_{i}}\in H^{0}(X,n_{i}L)
s.t.
\|s_{n_{i}}\|_{\infty}=1
and
\frac{1}{n_{i}}\log\|s_{n_{i}}\|\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-sense.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,r_{n}
be orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(X,nL)
. Consider the Bergman kernel
b_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\|\sigma_{i}(x)\|^{2}
. By Bouche-Tian,

\displaystyle b_{n}(x)\sim r_{n}=\dim H^{0}(X,nL)

uniformly on
X
as
n\longrightarrow\infty
. In particular,
\frac{1}{n}\log b_{n}(x)\longrightarrow 0
. Let
u_{n}(x)=\max_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\|\sigma_{i}(x)\|^{2}
. Then
1\leq b_{n}/u_{n}\leq r_{n}
and hence
\frac{1}{n}\log u_{n}\longrightarrow 0
.

Fix
n
, consider
s_{n,\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\sigma_{i}^{\ell}\in H^{0}(X,n\ell L)
. We have
\frac{1}{\ell}\log\|s_{n,\ell}\|\longrightarrow\log u_{n}
as
\ell\longrightarrow\infty
.

By Gromov’s inequality, there exists
C
s.t. for any
n
and any
s\in H^{0}(X,nL)
we have

\displaystyle\|s\|_{L^{2}}<\|s\|_{\infty}\leq C\sqrt{r_{n}}\cdot\|s\|_{L^{2}}.

So
\|s_{n,\ell}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\|\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}^{\ell}\leq r_{n}\max_{i}\|\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}^{\ell}
and thus
\|s_{n,\ell}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\leq r_{n}^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\max_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\|\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq Cr_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\ell}}
.

Take
n_{i}
be s.t.
\Big\|\frac{1}{n_{i}}\log u_{n_{i}}\Big\|_{L^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2i}
. For each
n_{i}
, take
\ell_{i}
be s.t.
\Big\|\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\log\|s_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\|-\frac{1}{n_{i}}\log u_{n_{i}}\Big\|_{L^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2i}
. Then
s_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\in H^{0}(X,n_{i}\ell_{i}L)
satisfies
\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\log\|s_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\|\longrightarrow 0
and
\lim_{i}\|s_{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}}\leq 1
.

Note that if
\|s_{n}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n}}\longrightarrow c<1
then
\frac{1}{n}\log\|s_{n}\|\leq\log c<0
can not tends to
0
. This forces
\lim_{i}\|s_{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}}\geq 1
and thus
\lim_{i}\|s_{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}}=1
.

Then consider
s^{\prime}_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}=\frac{s_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}}{\|s_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\|_{\infty}}\in H^{0}(X,n_{i}\ell_{i}L)
. We have
\|s^{\prime}_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\|_{\infty}=1
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\log\|s^{\prime}_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\|=\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\log\|s_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\|-\frac{1}{n_{i}\ell_{i}}\log\|s_{n_{i},\ell_{i}}\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0.

∎

3.2.
Real sections

Let
\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{Z}
be an arithmetic variety, normal and generically smooth. Let
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
be an ample Hermitian line bundle.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is normal, the Stein factorization of
\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
is of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}O_{K}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}

where
K
is a number field and
O_{K}
is the ring of integers of
K
.

We know

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})=\coprod_{\sigma:K\rightarrow\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),

each
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C})
is a connected projective complex manifold and the complex conjugation
F
acts on
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})
via the following rule:

•

if
\sigma
is a real embedding, then
F
acts on
X_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C})
via
Fx_{\sigma}=\overline{x}_{\sigma}
.

•

if
(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})
is a pair of complex embeddings, then
F
acts on
X_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C})\coprod X_{\overline{\sigma}}(\mathbb{C})
via
F(x,y)=(\overline{y},\overline{x})
.

We know

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n\mathcal{L})_{\mathbb{C}}=H^{0}(\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}),n\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}))=\bigoplus_{\sigma:K\rightarrow\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),n\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}))

and the complex conjugation
F
acts on
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}),n\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}))
via the following rule:

•

if
\sigma
is a real embedding, then
F
acts on
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),n\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}))
via
Fs_{\sigma}=\overline{s}_{\sigma}
.

•

if
(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})
is a pair of complex embeddings,

then
F
acts on
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),n\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}))\oplus H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\mathbb{C}),n\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\mathbb{C}))
via
F(s_{1},s_{2})=(\overline{s}_{2},\overline{s}_{1})
.

Real sections
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n\mathcal{L})_{\mathbb{R}}
are elements of
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n\mathcal{L})_{\mathbb{C}}
fixed by complex conjugation. In

\bigoplus_{\sigma:K\rightarrow\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),n\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}))
, they are elements of the following form:

•

if
\sigma
is a real embedding,
\overline{s}_{\sigma}=s_{\sigma}
is a real section.

•

if
(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})
is a pair of complex embeddings, sections over
\sigma
and
\overline{\sigma}
are
(s_{\sigma},\overline{s}_{\overline{\sigma}})
, i.e. a conjugate pair.

H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n\mathcal{L})_{\mathbb{R}}
inherits sup-norm from
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},n\mathcal{L})_{\mathbb{C}}
and makes itself a normed
\mathbb{R}
-vector space.

3.3.
Plurisubharmonic functions

We briefly recall basic properties of plurisubharmonic (psh) functions that will be used in this article. The reference is
[

]
, Chapter VIII.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. A function
f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is called
\omega
-psh
if

•

locally in complex topology,
f
is sum of a psh function and a smooth function.

•

dd^{c}f+\omega
is a positive
(1,1)
current.

The set of all
\omega
-psh functions on

is denoted by
\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega)
and is endowed with the
L^{1}
-topology.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
GZ17
]
Proposition 8.5)
.

\operatorname{PSH}_{0}(X,\omega)=\big\{f\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega):\sup_{X}f=0\big\}
is compact.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
(f_{n})
be a sequence of
\omega
-psh functions. Then
f_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-topology if and only if
f_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in measure.

Remark 3.8
.

Recall on a probability space
(X,\mu)
, a sequence of measurable functions
(f_{n})
is said to converge to
f
in measure if for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\mu\{|f_{n}-f|>\varepsilon\}=0.

It is known that if
f_{n}\longrightarrow f
in
L^{1}
, then
f_{n}\longrightarrow f
in measure. The coverse is in general false.

Proof.

From discussion above, we only prove sufficiency. For any subsequence
(f_{n_{i}})
of
(f_{n})
, by compactness (Theorem
3.6
), there exists a subsubsequence
(f_{n_{i_{j}}})
and constant numbers
(c_{n_{i_{j}}})
such that
f_{n_{i_{j}}}+c_{n_{i_{j}}}\longrightarrow f
in
L^{1}
-topology. In particular,
f_{n_{i_{j}}}+c_{n_{i_{j}}}\longrightarrow f
in measure.

By assumption
f_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in measure, so
c_{n_{i_{j}}}\longrightarrow f
in measure. Since
c_{n_{i——j}}
are constants,
f=c
is constant almost everywhere. Therefore,
c_{n}\longrightarrow c
and
f_{n_{i_{j}}}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-topology.

We have proved that for any subsequence
f_{n_{i}}
, there exists a subsubsequence
f_{n_{i_{j}}}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-topology. This forces the whole sequence
f_{n}\longrightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
-topology.
∎

.
In other words, if

must contain
\mathcal{C}
. Use Theorem

0

Step II
. Take a positive integer

By Charles

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety and
\overline{N}=\big(N,\big\{\|\cdot\|_{p}\big\}_{p\leq\infty}\big)
be an adelic line bundle that is not pseudo-effective. Then there exists a Hermitian model
(\mathcal{X},\overline{\mathcal{N}})
of
(X,N)
such that

•

\overline{\mathcal{N}}
is not pseudo-effective.

•

\operatorname{ess}(\overline{\mathcal{N}})>\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})
.

Proof.

There exists a sequence
(\mathcal{X}_{n},\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{n})
such that
\|\cdot\|_{n,p}/\|\cdot\|_{p}=1
for all but finitely many
p
and
\|\cdot\|_{n,p}/\|\cdot\|_{p}\longrightarrow 1
uniformly for the other
p
.

Since
\overline{N}
is not pseudo-effective (and that being not pseudo-effective is an open condition), there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that when
n
is large,
\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\varepsilon)
is not pseudo-effective.

For this
\varepsilon
, when
n
is large we will have
\Big|h_{\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{n}}(x)-h_{\overline{N}}(x)\Big|<\varepsilon/2
for every
x\in X(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, so
h_{\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\varepsilon)}(x)=h_{\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{n}}(x)+\varepsilon>h_{\overline{N}}(x)+\varepsilon/2
. In particular,
\operatorname{ess}(\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\varepsilon))>\operatorname{ess}(\overline{N})
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be an integral projective variety and
\overline{N}

\overline{N}
pseudo-effective.

If not, then by the above lemma we could produce a Hermitian line bundle which is not pseudo-effective but with positive essential minimum, contradicting Corollary

On deformation cohomology of compatible Hom-associative algebras
Taoufik Chtioui and Ripan Saha 1. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia2. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj University Raiganj 733134, West Bengal, India

Abstract
In this paper, we consider compatible Hom-associative algebras as a twisted version of compatible associative algebras. Compatible Hom-associative algebras are characterized as Maurer-Cartan elements in a suitable bidifferential graded Lie algebra. We also define a cohomology theory for compatible Hom-associative algebras generalizing the classical case. As applications of cohomology, we study abelian extensions and deformations of compatible Hom-associative algebras.



Key words
: Compatible Hom-associative algebras, Cohomology, Extensions, Formal deformations.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2020
: 16E40, 16S80.

Introduction

Two (linear) product operations of a certain kind defined on the same vector space are said to be compatible if their linear combinations are still of the same kind. In recent years a lot of studies have been done on various types of compatible algebraic structures, for example, compatible associative algebras
[
20
]
, compatible Lie algebras
[

[

showed a close connection between compatible Lie algebras and compatible Poisson structures via dualization. Compatible algebras showed up in the study of loop algebras over Lie algebras
[
9
]
, elliptical theta functions
[
19
]
, classical Yang-Baxter equation
[
21
]
, and principal chiral fields
[
8
]
. Compatible algebraic structures also appeared in many other interesting works of mathematical physics, see
[
23
,
16
,
13
,
24
,
22
]
. In
[
3
]
, the authors studied the cohomology and deformation of compatible associative algebras. In
[
14
]
, the authors characterized compatible Lie algebras as Maurer-Cartan elements and studied cohomology and deformation of compatible Lie algebras. In
[
4
]
, the authors studied compatible Hom-Lie algebras generalizing the work of
[
14
]
.

In this paper, we consider the twisted analog of compatible associative algebras, that is, compatible Hom-associative algebras. We study the cohomology of compatible Hom-associative algebras, generalizing the cohomology of compatible associative algebras. We also show some relationship between the cohomology of compatible Hom-associative algebras and cohomology of compatible Hom-Lie algebras defined in
[
4
]
. We define and study a one-parameter formal deformation theory of compatible Hom-associative algebras. We show that the infinitesimal is a
2
-cocycle and our cohomology is the ‘deformation cohomology’, that is, our cohomology controls the formal deformation. We also study the abelian extension of the compatible Hom-associative algebras and show that equivalence classes of the extensions are one-to-one correspondence with the elements of the second cohomology group.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we recall the basics, cohomology of Hom-associative algebras, and the Gerstenhaber bracket. In Section
2

3
, we introduce the cohomology of a compatible Hom-associative algebra with coefficients in a compatible bimodule. In Section
4
, we study one-parameter formal deformations of compatible Hom-associative algebras from cohomological points of view. Finally, in Section
5
we study abelian extensions of compatible Hom-associative algebras and their relationship with the second cohomology group.

1.1
Cohomology of Hom-associative algebras

Definition 1.1
.

A Hom-associative algebra is a triple
(A,\mu,\alpha)
in which
A
is a vector space endowed with a bilinear map
\mu:A\otimes A\rightarrow A,(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot b
and a linear map
\alpha:A\to A
, satisfying the following condition

\displaystyle\alpha(a\cdot b)=\alpha(a)\cdot\alpha(b),

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\cdot\alpha(c)=\alpha(a)\cdot(b\cdot c),~\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

We may also denote a Hom-associative algebra simply by
(A,\alpha)
when the multiplication map is clear from the context.

Definition 1.2
.

A bimodule (or a representation) over a Hom-associative algebra
A
consists of a vector space
M
together with bilinear maps (called left and right
A
-actions)
\ell:A\otimes M\rightarrow M,(a,m)\mapsto a\lhd m
and
r:M\otimes A\rightarrow M,(m,a)\mapsto m\rhd a
and a linear map
\beta:M\to M
satisfying the following compatibilities

\displaystyle\beta(a\lhd m)=\alpha(a)\lhd\beta(m),\quad\beta(m\rhd a)=\beta(m)\rhd\alpha(a),

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\lhd\beta(m)=\alpha(a)(b\lhd m),

\displaystyle(a\lhd m)\rhd\alpha(b)=\alpha(a)\lhd(m\rhd b),

\displaystyle(m\rhd a)\rhd\alpha(b)=\beta(m)\rhd(a\cdot b),

for
a,b\in A
and
m\in M
. Such a bimodule is denoted by
(M,\ell,r,\beta)
.

It follows that
A
is an
A
-bimodule with both left and right
A
-actions are given by the algebra multiplication map.

Let
(M,\ell,r,\beta)
be a representation of a Hom-associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)

and

\displaystyle r^{\star}(a)(\xi):=r^{*}(\alpha(a))\big((\beta^{-2})^{*}(\xi)\big),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\ell^{\star}(a)(\xi):=\ell^{*}(\alpha(a))\big((\beta^{-2})^{*}(\xi)\big).

1.2
Bidifferential graded Lie algebras

Now, we recall bidifferential graded Lie algebras
[
14
]
. Before that,
let us first give the definition of a differential graded Lie algebra.

Definition 1.3
.

A differential graded Lie algebra is a triple
(L=\oplus L^{i},[~,~],d)
consisting of a graded Lie algebra together with a degree
+1
differential
d:L\rightarrow L
which is a derivation for the bracket
[~,~]
.

An element
\theta\in L^{1}
is said to be a Maurer-Cartan element in the differential graded Lie algebra
(L,[~,~],d)
if
\theta
satisfies

\displaystyle d\theta+\frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta]=0.

Definition 1.4
.

A bidifferential graded Lie algebra is a quadruple
(L=\oplus L^{i},[~,~],d_{1},d_{2})
in which the triples
(L,[~,~],d_{1})
and
(L,[~,~],d_{2})
are differential graded Lie algebras additionally satisfying

d_{1}\circ d_{2}+d_{2}\circ d_{1}=0.

Remark 1.1
.

Any graded Lie algebra can be considered as a bidifferential graded Lie algebra with both the differentials
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
to be trivial.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
(L,[~,~],d_{1},d_{2})
be a bidifferential graded Lie algebra. A pair of elements
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in L^{1}\oplus L^{1}
is said to be a Maurer-Cartan element if

(i)

\theta_{1}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the differential graded Lie algebra
(L,[~,~],d_{1})
;

(ii)

\theta_{2}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the differential graded Lie algebra
(L,[~,~],d_{2})
;

(iii)

the following compatibility condition holds

\displaystyle d_{1}\theta_{2}+d_{2}\theta_{1}+[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]=0.

Like a differential graded Lie algebra can be twisted by a Maurer-Cartan element, the same result holds for bidifferential graded Lie algebras.

Proposition 1.3
.

[
14
]
Let
(L,[~,~],d_{1},d_{2})
be a bidifferential graded Lie algebra and let
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
be a Maurer-Cartan element. Then the quadruple
(L,[~,~],d_{1}^{\theta_{1}},d_{2}^{\theta_{2}})
is a bidifferential graded Lie algebra, where

\displaystyle d_{1}^{\theta_{1}}=d_{1}+[\theta_{1},-]~~~\text{ and }~~~d_{2}^{\theta_{2}}=d_{2}+[\theta_{2},-].

For any
\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}\in L^{1}
, the pair
(\theta_{1}+\vartheta_{1},\theta_{2}+\vartheta_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(L,[~,~],d_{1},d_{2})
if and only if
(\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(L,[~,~],d_{1}^{\theta_{1}},d_{2}^{\theta_{2}}).

2
Compatible Hom-associative algebras and their characterization

In this section, we introduce compatible Hom-associative algebras and then define compatible bimodules over them. We also construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible Hom-associative structures.

2.1
Compatible Hom-associative algebras

In the following, we introduce compatible Hom-associative algebras and compatible bimodules over them.

Definition 2.1
.

A compatible Hom-associative algebra is a triple
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
in which
(A,\mu_{1},\alpha)
and
(A,\mu_{2},\alpha)
are both Hom-associative algebras satisfying the following compatibility

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{1}b)\cdot_{2}\alpha(c)+(a\cdot_{2}b)\cdot_{1}\alpha(c)=\alpha(a)\cdot_{1}(b\cdot_{2}c)+\alpha(a)\cdot_{2}(b\cdot_{1}c),~\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

(2.1)

Here
\cdot_{1}
and
\cdot_{2}
are used for the multiplications
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, respectively.

In this case, we say that
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is a compatible Hom-associative algebra structure on
A
when the twisting map is clear from the context.

Remark 2.1
.

It follows from the above definition that the sum
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
also defines a Hom-associative product on
A
. In other words,
(A,\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},\alpha)
is a Hom-associative algebra. In fact, one can show that
(A,\lambda_{1}\mu_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mu_{2},\alpha)
is a Hom-associative algebra, for any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{K}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A=(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
and
A^{\prime}=(A^{\prime},\mu_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime},\alpha)
be two compatible Hom-associative algebras. A morphism of compatible Hom-associative algebras from
A
to
A^{\prime}
is a linear map
\phi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
which is a Hom-associative algebra morphism from
(A,\mu_{1},\alpha)
to
(A^{\prime},\mu_{1}^{\prime},\alpha)
and a Hom-associative algebra morphism from
(A,\mu_{2},\alpha)
to
(A^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime},\alpha)
.

In what follow, we give some examples of compatible Hom-associative algebras.

Example 2.1
.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
be compatible associative algebra and
\alpha:A\to A
be a compatible associative algebra morphism, i.e.
\alpha
is and associative algebra morphism for both
(A,\mu_{1})
and
(A,\mu_{2})
. Then
(A,\alpha\circ\mu_{1},\alpha\circ\mu_{2},\alpha)
is a compatible Hom-associative algebra.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra . Then for each
n\geq 0
, the tuple
(A,\alpha^{n}\circ\mu_{1},\alpha^{n}\circ\mu_{2},\alpha^{n+1})
is a compatible Hom-associative algebra. It is called the
n-th
derived compatible Hom-associative algebra of
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra. A Nijenhuis operator on
A
is a linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
satisfying

\displaystyle N\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ N,

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot N(b)=N\big(N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b)\big),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Nijenhuis operators are used to study linear deformations of Hom-associative algebras.
A Nijenhuis operator
N
induces a new Hom-associative structure on
(A,\alpha)
, denoted by
\mu_{N}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A,(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot_{N}b
and it is defined by

\displaystyle a\cdot_{N}b:=N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b),~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Then it is easy to see that
(A,\mu,\mu_{N},\alpha)
is a compatible Hom-associative algebra.

Example 2.4
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra and
(M,\ell,r,\beta)
be an
A
-bimodule. If
f\in C^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
is a Hochschild
2
-cocycle on
A
with coefficients in the
A
-bimodule
M
, then
A\oplus M
can be equipped with the
f
-twisted semidirect product Hom-associative algebra given by

\displaystyle(a,m)\cdot_{f}(b,n)=(a\cdot b,a\lhd n+m\rhd b+f(a,b)),~\text{ for }(a,m),(b,n)\in A\oplus M.

With this notation, it can be easily checked that
(A\oplus M,\cdot_{0},\cdot_{f},\alpha+\beta)
is a compatible Hom-associative algebra.

The next class of examples come from compatible Rota-Baxter operators on Hom-associative algebras. See
[
11
]
for more on Rota-Baxter operators.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra. A Rota-Baxter operator on
A
is a linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
satisfying
R\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ R
and

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot R(b)=R\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big),~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

A Rota-Baxter operator
R
induces a new Hom-associative product
\mu_{R}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A,~(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot_{R}b
on the underlying vector space
A
with the same twisting map
\alpha
and it is given by

\displaystyle a\cdot_{R}b=R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b),~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Definition 2.4
.

Two Rota-Baxter operators
R,S:A\rightarrow A
on a Hom-associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
are said to be compatible if for any
k,l\in\mathbb{K}
, the sum
kR+lS:A\rightarrow A
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(A,\mu,\alpha)
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot S(b)+S(a)\cdot R(b)=R\big(S(a)\cdot b+a\cdot S(b)\big)+S\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big),~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

It is straightforward to check the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
R,S:A\rightarrow A
be two compatible Rota-Baxter operators on a Hom-associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
. Then
(A,\cdot_{R},\cdot_{S},\alpha)
is a compatible Hom-associative algebra.

A

Any compatible Hom-associative algebra

Let

The following result generalizes the semidirect product for associative algebras

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
A=(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra and
(M,\ell_{1},r_{1},\ell_{2},r_{2},\beta)
be a compatible

-bimodule.

\displaystyle(a,m)\bullet_{1}(b,n)=

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{1}b,~a\lhd_{1}n+m\rhd_{1}b),

\displaystyle(a,m)\bullet_{2}(b,n)=

Given a compatible Hom-associative algebra and a bimodule, we can construct the dual bimodule as follows.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra and
(M,\ell_{1},r_{1},\ell_{2},r_{2},\beta)
a bimodule. Then
(M^{*},r_{1}^{\star}-\ell_{1}^{\star}+r_{2}^{\star}-\ell_{2}^{\star},-\ell_{1}^{\star}-\ell_{2}^{\star},(\beta^{-1})^{\ast})
is a bimodule of
A
called the dual bimodule of
(M,\ell_{1},r_{1},\ell_{2},r_{2},\beta)
.

3
Cohomology of compatible Hom-associative algebras

In this section, we introduce the cohomology of a compatible Hom-associative algebra with coefficients in a compatible bimodule.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra and
(M,\ell_{1},r_{1},\ell_{2},r_{2},\beta)
be a compatible
A
-bimodule. Let

\displaystyle{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}:C^{n}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M),~n\geq 1,

be the coboundary operator for the Hochschild cohomology of
(A,\mu_{1},\alpha)
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,\ell_{1},r_{1},\beta)
, and

\displaystyle{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}:C^{n}(_{\alpha,\beta}A,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M),~n\geq 1,

be the coboundary operator for the Hochschild cohomology of
(A,\mu_{2},\alpha)
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,\ell_{2},r_{2},\beta)
. Then, we obviously have

\displaystyle({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta})^{2}=0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad({}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta})^{2}=0.

Since the two Hom-associative structures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
on
A
, and the corresponding bimodule structures on
M
are compatible, it is natural that there is some compatibility
between the coboundaries
{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
and
{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Before we state the compatibility, we observe the followings.
Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be compatible Hom-associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. We consider the compatible Hom-associative algebra structure on the semidirect product

A\oplus M
given in Proposition
2.9
.
We denote by

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in C^{2}_{\alpha+\beta}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)
the elements corresponding to the Hom-associative products
\bullet_{1}
and
\bullet_{2}
on
A\oplus M
, respectively .
Let

\displaystyle\delta^{1}_{\alpha+\beta}:C^{n}_{\alpha+\beta}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)\to C^{n+1}_{\alpha+\beta}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M),n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\delta^{2}_{\alpha+\beta}:C^{n}_{\alpha+\beta}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)\to C^{n+1}_{\alpha+\beta}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M),n\geq 1,

denote respectively the coboundary operator for the Hochschild cohomology of the Hom-associative algebra
(A\oplus M,\bullet_{1},\alpha+\beta)
and
(A\oplus M,\bullet_{2},\alpha+\beta)
with coefficients in themselves.

Note that any map
f\in C^{n}(A,M)
can be lifted to a map
\widetilde{f}\in C^{n}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((a_{1},m_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},m_{n})\big)=\big(0,f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\big),

for
(a_{i},m_{i})\in A\oplus M
and
i=1,\ldots,n
. Moreover, we have
f=0
if and only if
\widetilde{f}=0
. With all these notations, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f)}=\delta^{1}_{\alpha+\beta}(\widetilde{f})=(-1)^{n-1}~[\pi_{1},\widetilde{f}]_{\alpha}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\widetilde{({}^{2}\delta f)}=\delta^{2}_{\alpha+\beta}(\widetilde{f})=(-1)^{n-1}~[\pi_{2},\widetilde{f}]_{\alpha},

for
f\in C^{n}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
. We are now ready to prove the compatibility condition satisfied by
{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
and
{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
. More precisely, we have the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

The coboundary operators
{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
and
{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
are related by the following compatibility identity:

\displaystyle{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\circ{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}+{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\circ{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}=0.

Proof.

For any
f\in C^{n}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\circ{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}+{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\circ{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta})(f)}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}({}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{f})}~+~\widetilde{{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{f})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}~[\pi_{1},\widetilde{{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{f}}]_{\alpha}~+~(-1)^{n}~[\pi_{2},\widetilde{{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{f}}]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-[\pi_{1},[\pi_{2},\widetilde{f}]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}-[\pi_{2},[\pi_{1},\widetilde{f}]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-[[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]_{\alpha},\widetilde{f}]_{\alpha}~+~[\pi_{2},[\pi_{1},\widetilde{f}]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}-[\pi_{2},[\pi_{1},\widetilde{f}]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=0~~~~\qquad(\because~[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]_{\alpha}=0).

Therefore, it follows that
({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\circ{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}+{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\circ{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta})(f)=0
. Hence the result follows.
∎

We are now, in a position to define the cohomology of a compatible Hom-associative algebra
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
with coefficients in a bimodule
(M,\ell_{1},r_{1},\ell_{2},r_{2},\beta)
.

We define the
n
-th cochain group
C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
, for each
n\geq 1
, by

\displaystyle C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M):=~\underbrace{C^{n}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{n}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)}_{n\text{ copies}},~\text{ for }n\geq 1.

Define a map
\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}:C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
, for
n\geq 1
, by

\displaystyle\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):=~

\displaystyle({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{1},\ldots,\underbrace{{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i-1}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i}}_{i-\text{th place}},\ldots,{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{n}),

(3.1)

for
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
and
2\leq i\leq n
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The map
\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}
is a coboundary operator, i.e.,
\delta^{n+1,c}_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
.

Proof.

For any
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
,
n\geq 1
and according to Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{n+1,c}_{\alpha,\beta}\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})

\displaystyle=\delta^{n+1,c}_{\alpha,\beta}({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{1},\ldots,{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i-1}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i},\ldots,{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{n})

\displaystyle=\Big({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{1}~,{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{1}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{1}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{2}~,\ldots,

\displaystyle\underbrace{{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i-2}+{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i-1}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i-1}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i}}_{3\leq i\leq n-1}~,\ldots,

\displaystyle{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{n-1}+{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{n}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{n}~,~{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=(0,0,\ldots,0)

Then we have
\delta^{n+1,c}_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
.
∎

Thus, we obtain a cochain complex
\big(C^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M),\delta^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}\big)
. Let
Z^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
denote the space of
n
-cocycles and
B^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
the space of
n
-coboundaries. Then we have
B^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\subset Z^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
, for
n\geq 1
. The corresponding quotient groups

\displaystyle H^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M):=\frac{Z^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)}{B^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)},\text{ for }n\geq 1

are called the cohomology groups of the compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
with coefficients in the compatible
A
-bimodule
M
.

Remark 3.1
.

When we consider compatible Hom-associative algebra as a bimodule over itself, we denote
C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
as
C^{n,c}_{\alpha}(A,A)
and
\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha}:C^{n,c}_{\alpha}(A,A)\to C^{n+1,c}_{\alpha}(A,A)
. We denote the
n
th cohomology group as
H^{n,c}_{\alpha}(A,A)
.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra and
(M,\ell_{1},r_{1},\ell_{2},r_{2},\beta)
be a compatible
A
-bimodule.
Then we know that
A^{+}=(A,\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},\alpha)
is a Hom-associative algebra and
M^{+}=(M,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2},r_{1}+r_{2},\beta)
is an
A^{+}
-bimodule.
Consider the cochain complex
\big(C^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M),\delta^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}\big)
of the compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
with coefficients in
M
and the cochain complex
\big(C^{\ast}_{\alpha,\beta}(A^{+},M^{+}),\delta^{\ast}_{\alpha,\beta}\big)
of the Hom-associative algebra
A^{+}
with coefficients in
M^{+}
.
For each
n\geq 1
, define the map

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n}:C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\to C^{n}_{\alpha,\beta}(A^{+},M^{+}),\ \mathrm{by}\ \Upsilon_{n}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=f_{1}+f_{2}+\ldots+f_{n}.

Observe that for any
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n})\in C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
, we have

\displaystyle(\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\Upsilon_{n})((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}))=

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{n})

\displaystyle=

{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{n})+^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n+1}({}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{1},\ldots,\underbrace{{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i-1}+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i}}_{i-\text{th place}},\ldots,{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}f_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n+1}\circ\delta^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}).

Therefore, we get the following

Theorem 3.3
.

The collection
\{\Upsilon_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
defines a morphism of cochain complexes from
\big(C^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M),\delta^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}\big)
to
\big(C^{\ast}_{\alpha,\beta}(A^{+},M^{+}),\delta^{\ast}_{\alpha,\beta}\big)
. Hence, it induces a morphism
H^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\to H^{\ast}_{\alpha,\beta}(A^{+},M^{+})

between corresponding cohomologies.

3.1
Relation with the cohomology of compatible Hom-Lie algebras

Recently, A. Das in
[
4
]
introduced a cohomology theory for compatible Hom-Lie algebras. In this paragraph, we show that the cohomology of compatible Hom-associative algebras is related to the cohomology of compatible Hom-Lie algebras. Let us first recall some results from the above-mentioned reference.

Definition 3.1
.

A compatible Hom-Lie algebra is a tuple
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\alpha)
consists of a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
and a linear map
\alpha:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\alpha)
and
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\alpha)
are both Hom-Lie algebras and the following compatibility condition holds:

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{1},\alpha(z)]_{2}+[[y,z]_{1},\alpha(x)]_{2}+[[z,x]_{1},\alpha(y)]_{2}+[[x,y]_{2},\alpha(z)]_{1}+[[y,z]_{2},\alpha(x)]_{1}+[[z,x]_{2},\alpha(y)]_{1}=0,

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-Lie algebra. A compatible
\mathfrak{g}
-representation is a tuple
(V,\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\beta)
, where
(V,\rho_{1},\beta)
is a representation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\alpha)
and
(V,\rho_{2},\beta)
is a representation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\beta)
satisfying additionally

\displaystyle\rho_{2}([x,y]_{1})\beta(v)+\rho_{1}([x,y]_{2})\beta(v)=\rho_{1}(\alpha(x))\rho_{2}(y)v-\rho_{1}(\alpha(y))\rho_{2}(x)v+\rho_{2}(\alpha(x))\rho_{1}(y)v-\rho_{2}(\alpha(y))\rho_{1}(x),

for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},v\in V
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-Lie algebra and
(V,\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\beta)
be a representation. There is a cochain complex
\{C^{\ast,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V),d^{\ast,cL}_{\alpha,\beta})
defined as follows:

\displaystyle C^{n,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V):=~

\displaystyle\underbrace{C^{n,L}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V)\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{n,L}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V)}_{n\text{ times}},\text{ for }n\geq 1,

where
C^{n,L}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\{f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)|f\circ\alpha^{\otimes n}=\beta\circ f\}.
The coboundary operator
d^{n,cL}:C^{n,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow C^{n+1,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is given by

\displaystyle d^{n,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):=(^{1}d^{n,L}_{\alpha,\beta}f_{1},\ldots,\underbrace{{}^{1}d^{n,L}_{\alpha,\beta}f_{i}+^{2}d^{n,L}f_{i-1}}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,^{2}d^{n,L}f_{n}),

for
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in C^{n,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V)
. Here
{}^{1}d^{n,L}_{\alpha,\beta}
(resp.
{}^{2}d^{n,L}_{\alpha,\beta}
) is the coboundary operator for the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\alpha)
with coefficients in
(V,\rho_{1},\beta)
(resp. of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\alpha)
with coefficients in
(V,\rho_{2},\beta)
). The cohomology of the cochain complex
\{C^{\ast,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V),d^{\ast,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}\}
is called the cohomology of the compatible Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]_{1},[~,~]_{2},\alpha)
with coefficients in the compatible
\mathfrak{g}
-representation
(V,\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\beta)
, and they are denoted by
H^{\ast,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{g},V).

On the other hand, it is a well-known fact that the standard skew-symmetrizatio gives rise to a map from the Hochschild cochain complex of a Hom-associative algebra to the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology complex of the corresponding sub-adjacent Hom-Lie algebra (obtained by commutator). This can be generalized to compatible Hom-algebras as well.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra. Then it can be easily checked that the triple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2})

is a compatible Hom-Lie algebra, where

\displaystyle[a,b]_{1}:=a\cdot_{1}b-b\cdot_{1}a~~~~\text{ and }~~~~[a,b]_{2}:=a\cdot_{2}b-b\cdot_{2}a,\text{ for }a,b\in A.

We denote this compatible Hom-Lie algebra by
A_{c}
. Moreover, if
M
is a compatible Hom-associative
A
-bimodule, then
M
can be regarded as a compatible
A_{c}
-representation by

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(a)(m):=a\lhd_{1}m-m\rhd_{1}a~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\rho_{2}(a)(m):=a\lhd_{2}m-m\rhd_{2}a,~\text{ for }a\in A_{s},m\in M.

This compatible
A_{c}
-representation is denoted by
M_{c}
. With these notations, we have the following.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra and
M
be a compatible
A
-bimodule. Then the standard skew-symmetrization

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}:C^{n,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(A_{c},M_{c}),~(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\mapsto(\overline{f_{1}},\ldots,\overline{f_{n}}),~\text{ for }n\geq 1,

where

\displaystyle\overline{f_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}}(-1)^{\sigma}~f_{i}(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(n)}),~~~i=1,\ldots,n,

gives rise a morphism of cochain complexes. Hence it induces a map
\Theta^{\ast}:H^{\ast,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)\rightarrow H^{\ast,cL}_{\alpha,\beta}(A_{c},M_{c})
.

4
One-parameter formal deformation of compatible Hom-associative algebras

In this section, we study formal deformation theory of compatible Hom-associative algebras following the series of works by Gerstenhaber
[
7
,
6
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra. A one-parameter formal deformation of
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
is a quadruple
(A[[t]],\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
, where

\displaystyle\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t}:A[[t]]\times A[[t]]\to A[[t]]

are
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-bilinear maps, and

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}:A[[t]]\to A[[t]]

is a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-linear map such that

(i)

\mu_{1,t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu_{1,i}t^{i},~\mu_{2,t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu_{2,i}t^{i},~\text{and}~\alpha_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha_{i}t^{i}
,where
\mu_{1,i},\mu_{2,i}:A\times A\to A
are
\mathbb{K}
-bilinear maps,
\alpha_{i}:A\to A
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map for all
i\geq 0
.

(ii)

\mu_{1,0}=\mu_{1},\mu_{2,0}=\mu_{2}
, and
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
are the original multiplications and twisting maps respectively.

(iii)

(A[[t]],\mu_{1,t},\alpha_{t})
and
(A[[t]],\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
are both Hom-associative algebras, that is, for all
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1,t}(\mu_{1,t}(a,b),\alpha_{t}(c))=\mu_{1,t}(\alpha_{t}(a),\mu_{1,t}(b,c)),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{2,t}(\mu_{2,t}(a,b),\alpha_{t}(c))=\mu_{2,t}(\alpha_{t}(a),\mu_{2,t}(b,c)).

(4.2)

(iv)

(A[[t]],\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
satisfies the following compatibility conditions:

\displaystyle\mu_{1,t}(\mu_{2,t}(a,b),\alpha_{t}(c))+\mu_{2,t}(\mu_{1,t}(a,b),\alpha_{t}(c))=\mu_{1,t}(\alpha_{t}(a),\mu_{2,t}(b,c))+\mu_{2,t}(\alpha_{t}(a),\mu_{1,t}(b,c)),

(4.3)

for all
a,b,c\in A
.

The condition (4.1) and (
4.2
) is equivalent to the following equations. For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\big(\mu_{1,i}(\mu_{1,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))-\mu_{1,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{1,k}(b,c))\big)=0

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\big(\mu_{2,i}(\mu_{2,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))-\mu_{2,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{2,k}(b,c))\big)=0.

(4.5)

Equivalently, we can write Equations
4.4
and
4.5
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}[\mu_{1,i},\mu_{1,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}[\mu_{2,i},\mu_{2,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0.

(4.7)

The condition
4.3
is equivalent to the following equations. For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\big(\mu_{2,i}(\mu_{1,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))+\mu_{1,i}(\mu_{2,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))\big)

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j+k=n}\big(\mu_{1,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{2,k}(b,c))+\mu_{2,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{1,k}(b,c))\big)

For all
n\geq 0
, we can re-write the Equation
4.8
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}[\mu_{1,i},\mu_{2,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0.

(4.9)

Therefore, using Equations
4.6
,
4.7
, and
4.9
, we can say that
(A[[t]],\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
is a one-parameter formal deformation of the compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
if for all
n\geq 0
, and
a,b,c\in A
, it satisfies the following equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}[\mu_{1,i},\mu_{1,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}[\mu_{2,i},\mu_{2,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}[\mu_{1,i},\mu_{2,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0.

For
n=0
, we have

[\mu_{1,0},\mu_{1,0}]_{\alpha_{0}}=0,~[\mu_{2,0},\mu_{2,0}]_{\alpha_{0}}=0,~[\mu_{1,0},\mu_{2,0}]_{\alpha_{0}}=0.

This is same as

[\mu_{1},\mu_{1}]_{\alpha}=0,~[\mu_{2},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha}=0,~[\mu_{1},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha}=0.

Note that the above relations are nothing but original Hom-associative and Hom-compatibilty relations.

For
n=1
, we have

\displaystyle[\mu_{1,1},\mu_{1,0}]_{\alpha_{0}}+[\mu_{1,0},\mu_{1,1}]_{\alpha_{0}}+[\mu_{1,0},\mu_{1,0}]_{\alpha_{1}}=0,

\displaystyle[\mu_{2,1},\mu_{2,0}]_{\alpha_{0}}+[\mu_{2,0},\mu_{2,1}]_{\alpha_{0}}+[\mu_{2,0},\mu_{2,0}]_{\alpha_{1}}=0,

\displaystyle[\mu_{1,1},\mu_{2,0}]_{\alpha_{0}}+[\mu_{1,0},\mu_{2,1}]_{\alpha_{0}}+[\mu_{1,0},\mu_{2,0}]_{\alpha_{1}}=0.

Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle[\mu_{1,1},\mu_{1}]_{\alpha}+[\mu_{1},\mu_{1,1}]_{\alpha}+[\mu_{1},\mu_{1}]_{\alpha_{1}}=0,

\displaystyle[\mu_{2,1},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha}+[\mu_{2},\mu_{2,1}]_{\alpha}+[\mu_{2},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha_{1}}=0,

\displaystyle[\mu_{1,1},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha}+[\mu_{1},\mu_{2,1}]_{\alpha}+[\mu_{1},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha_{1}}=0.

As
[\mu_{1},\mu_{1}]_{\alpha_{1}}=0
and
[\mu_{2},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha_{2}}=0
, we have

\displaystyle[\mu_{1,1},\mu_{1}]_{\alpha}=0,

\displaystyle[\mu_{2,1},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha}=0,

\displaystyle[\mu_{1,1},\mu_{2}]_{\alpha}+[\mu_{1},\mu_{2,1}]_{\alpha}=0.

Therefore,

\delta^{2,c}_{\alpha}(\mu_{1,1},\mu_{2,1})=0.

Thus,
(\mu_{1,1},\mu_{2,1})
is a
2
-cocyle in the cohomology of the compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
with coeffients in itself. This pair
(\mu_{1,1},\mu_{2,1})
is called the infinitesimal of the deformation. This means the infinitesimal of the deformation is a
2
-cocycle. More generally, we have the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

If
(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
is the first non-zero term after
(\mu_{1,0},\mu_{2,0})=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
of the formal deformation
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t})
, then we say that
(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
is the
n
-infinitesimal of the deformation.

Similar to the proof of showing that the infinitesimal is a
2
-cocycle, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

The
n
-infinitesimal is a
2
-cocycle.

4.1
Equivalent deformation and cohomology

Suppose
A_{t}=(A,\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
and
A^{\prime}_{t}=(A,\mu^{\prime}_{1,t},\mu^{\prime}_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
be two one-parameter compatible Hom-associative algebra deformations of
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
, where
\mu_{1,t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu_{1,i}t^{i},~\mu_{2,t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu_{2,i}t^{i},~\text{and}~\alpha_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha_{i}t^{i}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{1,t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu^{\prime}_{1,i}t^{i},~\mu^{\prime}_{2,t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu^{\prime}_{2,i}t^{i}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Two deformations
A_{t}
and
A^{\prime}_{t}
are said to be equivalent if there exists a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-linear isomorphism
\Psi_{t}:A[[t]]\to A[[t]]
of the form
\Psi_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\psi_{i}t^{i}
, where
\psi_{0}=id
and
\psi_{i}:A\to A
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear maps such that the following relations holds:

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}\circ\mu_{1,t}^{\prime}=\mu_{1,t}\circ(\Psi_{t}\otimes\Psi_{t}),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}\circ\mu_{2,t}^{\prime}=\mu_{2,t}\circ(\Psi_{t}\otimes\Psi_{t}),

(4.11)

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}\circ\Psi_{t}=\Psi_{t}\circ\alpha_{t}.

(4.12)

Definition 4.4
.

A deformation
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
of a compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
is called trivial if
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
is equivalent to the deformation
\mu_{1,0},\mu_{2,0},\alpha_{0})
, which is same as the undeformed one. A compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
is called rigid if it has only trivial deformation upto equivalence.

Equations
4.10
-
4.12
may be written as

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}(\mu^{\prime}_{1,t}(a,b))=\mu_{1,t}(\Psi_{t}(a),\Psi_{t}(b)),

(4.13)

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}(\mu^{\prime}_{2,t}(a,b))=\mu_{2,t}(\Psi_{t}(a),\Psi_{t}(b)),

(4.14)

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}(\Psi_{t}(a))=\Psi_{t}(\alpha_{t}(a)),\,\,\,\text{for all}~a,b\in A.

(4.15)

Note that the above equations are equivalent to the following equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\psi_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\mu^{\prime}_{1,j}(a,b)t^{j}\bigg)t^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu_{1,i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\psi_{j}(a)t^{j},\sum_{k\geq 0}\psi_{k}(b)t^{k}\bigg)t^{i},

(4.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\psi_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\mu^{\prime}_{2,j}(a,b)t^{j}\bigg)t^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu_{2,i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\psi_{j}(a)t^{j},\sum_{k\geq 0}\psi_{k}(b)t^{k}\bigg)t^{i},

(4.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\psi_{j}(a)t^{j}\bigg)t^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\psi_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha_{j}(a)t^{j}\bigg)t^{i}.

(4.18)

This is same as the following equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\psi_{i}(\mu^{\prime}_{1,j}(a,b))t^{i+j}=\sum_{i,j,k\geq 0}\mu_{1,i}(\psi_{j}(a),\psi_{k}(b))t^{i+j+k},

(4.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\psi_{i}(\mu^{\prime}_{2,j}(a,b))t^{i+j}=\sum_{i,j,k\geq 0}\mu_{2,i}(\psi_{j}(a),\psi_{k}(b))t^{i+j+k},

(4.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\alpha_{i}(\psi_{j}(a))t^{i+j}=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\psi_{i}(\alpha_{j}(a))t^{i+j}.

(4.21)

Using
\psi_{0}=Id
and comparing constant terms on both sides of the above equations, we have

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{1,0}(a,b)=\mu_{1}(a,b),

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{2,0}(a,b)=\mu_{2}(a,b),

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(a)=\alpha(x).

Now comparing coefficients of
t
, we have

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{1,1}(a,b)+\psi_{1}(\mu^{\prime}_{1,0}(a,b))=\mu_{1,1}(a,b)+\mu_{1,0}(\psi_{1}(a),b)+\mu_{1,0}(a,\psi_{1}(b)),

(4.22)

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{2,1}(a,b)+\psi_{1}(\mu^{\prime}_{2,0}(a,b))=\mu_{2,1}(a,b)+\mu_{2,0}(\psi_{1}(a),b)+\mu_{2,0}(a,\psi_{1}(b)),

(4.23)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(a)+\alpha_{0}(\psi_{1}(a))=\alpha_{1}(a)+\psi_{1}(\alpha_{0}(a)).

(4.24)

The Equations (
4.22
)-(
4.24
) are same as

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{1,1}(a,b)-\mu_{1,1}(a,b)=\mu_{1}(\psi_{1}(a),b)+\mu_{1}(a,\psi_{1}(b))-\psi_{1}(\mu_{1}(a,b))={}^{1}\delta_{\alpha}\psi_{1}(a,b).

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{2,1}(a,b)-\mu_{2,1}(a,b)=\mu_{2}(\psi_{1}(a),b)+\mu_{2}(a,\psi_{1}(b))-\psi_{1}(\mu_{2}(a,b))={}^{2}\delta_{\alpha}\psi_{1}(a,b).

\displaystyle\alpha(\psi_{1}(a))-\psi_{1}(\alpha(a))=0.

Thus, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Two equivalent deformations have cohomologous infinitesimals.

Proof.

Suppose
A_{t}=(A,\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
and
A^{\prime}_{t}=(A,\mu^{\prime}_{1,t},\mu^{\prime}_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
be two equivalent one-parameter formal deformations of compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
. Suppose
(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
and
(\mu^{\prime}_{1,n},\mu^{\prime}_{2,n})
be two
n
-infinitesimals of the deformations
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
and
(\mu^{\prime}_{1,t},\mu^{\prime}_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
respectively. Using Equation (
4.19
) we get,

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{1,n}(a,b)+\psi_{n}(\mu^{\prime}_{1,0}(a,b))=\mu_{1,n}(a,b)+\mu_{1,0}(\psi_{n}(a),b)+\mu_{1,0}(a,\psi_{n}(b)),

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{1,n}(a,b)-\mu_{1,n}(a,b)=\mu_{1}(\psi_{n}(a),b)+\mu_{1}(a,\psi_{n}(b))-\psi_{n}(m^{\prime}_{1}(a,b))={}^{1}\delta_{\alpha}\psi_{n}(a,b),

and

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{2,n}(a,b)+\psi_{n}(\mu^{\prime}_{2,0}(a,b))=\mu_{2,n}(a,b)+\mu_{2,0}(\psi_{n}(a),b)+\mu_{2,0}(a,\psi_{n}(b)),

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{2,n}(a,b)-\mu_{2,n}(a,b)=\mu_{2}(\psi_{n}(a),b)+\mu_{2}(a,\psi_{n}(b))-\psi_{n}(m^{\prime}_{2}(a,b))={}^{2}\delta_{\alpha}\psi_{n}(a,b).

Using equation (
4.21
) we get,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(\psi_{n}(a))-\psi_{0}(\alpha_{n}(a))+\alpha_{n}(\psi_{0}(a))-\psi_{n}(\alpha_{0}(a))=0,

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(\alpha(a))-\alpha(\psi_{n}(a))=0.

Thus, infinitesimals of two deformations determines same cohomology class.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

A non-trivial deformation of a compatible Hom-associative algebra is equivalent to a deformation whose infinitesimal is not a coboundary.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
be a deformation of the compatible Hom-associative algebra
A
and
(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
be the
n
-infinitesimal of the deformation for some
n\geq 1
. Then by Theorem (
4.1
),
(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
is a
2
-cocycle, that is,
\delta^{2,c}_{\alpha}(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})=0
. Suppose
(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})=-\delta^{1,c}_{\alpha}\phi_{n}
for some
\phi_{n}\in C_{\alpha}^{1,c}(A,A)
, that is,
(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
is a coboundary. We define a formal isomorphism
\Psi_{t}
of
A[[t]]
as follows:

\Psi_{t}(a)=a+\phi_{n}(a)t^{n}.

We set

\displaystyle\bar{\mu_{1,t}}=\Psi^{-1}_{t}\circ\mu_{1,t}\circ(\Psi_{t}\otimes\Psi_{t}),

\displaystyle\bar{\mu_{2,t}}=\Psi^{-1}_{t}\circ\mu_{2,t}\circ(\Psi_{t}\otimes\Psi_{t}).

Thus, we have a new deformation
(\bar{\mu_{1,t}},\bar{\mu_{2,t}},\alpha_{t})
which is isomorphic to
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
. By expanding the above equations and comparing coefficients of
t^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\bar{\mu_{1,n}}-\mu_{1,n}={}^{1}\delta_{\alpha}\phi_{n},

\displaystyle\bar{\mu_{2,n}}-\mu_{2,n}={}^{2}\delta_{\alpha}\phi_{n}.

Hence,
\bar{\mu_{1,n}}=0,~\bar{\mu_{2,n}}=0
. By repeating this argument, we can kill off any infinitesimal which is a coboundary. Thus, the process must stop if the deformation is non-trivial.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(A,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\alpha)
be a compatible Hom-associative algebra. If
H^{2,c}_{\alpha}(A,A)=0
then
A
is rigid.

4.2
Obstruction and deformation cohomology

Next, we define a deformation of order
n
and discuss associated obstructions in extending the given deformation of a compatible Hom-associative algebra to a deformation of order
(n+1)
.

Definition 4.5
.

A deformation of order
n
of a compatible Hom-associative algebra

consist of a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-bilinear maps
\mu_{1,t}:A[[t]]\times A[[t]]\to A[[t]]

such that

satisfy all the conditions of a one-parameter formal deformation in the Definition
4.1

(mod~t^{n+1})

A deformation of order
1
is called an infinitesimal deformation. We say a deformation
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
of order
n
of a compatible Hom-associative algebra is extendable to a deformation of order

\mu_{1,n+1},\mu_{2,n+1}\in C_{\alpha}^{2,c}(A,A)
and
\alpha_{n+1}\in C_{\alpha}^{1,c}(A,A)

\displaystyle\bar{\mu_{1,t}}=\mu_{1,t}+\mu_{1,n+1}t^{n+1},

\displaystyle\bar{\mu_{2,t}}=\mu_{2,t}+\mu_{2,n+1}t^{n+1},

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha_{t}}=\alpha_{t}+\alpha_{n+1}t^{n+1},

and
(\bar{\mu_{1,t}},\bar{\mu_{2,t}},\bar{\alpha_{t}})
satisfies all the conditions of Definition
4.1

(mod~t^{n+2})
.

The deformation
(\bar{\mu_{1,t}},\bar{\mu_{2,t}},\bar{\alpha_{t}})
of order
(n+1)
gives us the following equations.

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1}\big(\mu_{1,i}(\mu_{1,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))-\mu_{1,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{1,k}(b,c))\big)=0

(4.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1}\big(\mu_{2,i}(\mu_{2,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))-\mu_{2,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{2,k}(b,c))\big)=0.

(4.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1}\big(\mu_{2,i}(\mu_{1,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))+\mu_{1,i}(\mu_{2,j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))\big)

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j+k=n+1}\big(\mu_{1,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{2,k}(b,c))+\mu_{2,i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\mu_{1,k}(b,c))\big)

This is same as the following equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1}[\mu_{1,i},\mu_{1,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0,

(4.28)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1}[\mu_{2,i},\mu_{2,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0,

(4.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1}[\mu_{1,i},\mu_{2,j}]_{\alpha_{k}}=0.

(4.30)

Equivalently, we can rewrite the above equations as follows:

\displaystyle{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha}(\mu_{1,n+1})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
i,j,k>0\end{subarray}}[\mu_{1,i},\mu_{1,j}]_{\alpha_{k}},

(4.31)

\displaystyle{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha}(\mu_{2,n+1})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\

(4.32)

\displaystyle{}^{2}\delta_{\alpha}(\mu_{1,n+1})+{}^{1}\delta_{\alpha}(\mu_{2,n+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\

(4.33)

We define the

\text{Obs}^{n}=(\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{2}})

\displaystyle\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\

(4.34)

\displaystyle\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{2}}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
i,j,k>0\end{subarray}}[\mu_{2,i},\mu_{2,j}]_{\alpha_{k}},

(4.35)

\displaystyle\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\

(4.36)

Thus,
(\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{2}})\in C^{3,c}_{\alpha}(A,A)
and
(\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{2}})=\delta^{2,c}_{\alpha}(\mu_{1,n+1},\mu_{2,n+1})
.

Theorem 4.5
.

A deformation of order
n
extends to a deformation of order
n+1
if and only if the cohomology class of
\text{Obs}^{n}
vanishes.

Proof.

Suppose a deformation
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
of order
n
extends to a deformation of order
n+1
. From the obstruction equations, we have

\text{Obs}^{n}=(\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}},\text{Obs}^{n}_{\mu_{2}})=\delta^{2,c}_{\alpha}(\mu_{1,n+1},\mu_{2,n+1}).

As
\delta\circ\delta=0
, we get the cohomology class of
\text{Obs}^{n}
vanishes.

Conversely, suppose the cohomology class of
\text{Obs}^{n}
vanishes, that is,

\text{Obs}^{n}=\delta^{2,c}_{\alpha}(\mu_{1,n+1},\mu_{2,n+1}),

for some
2
-cochains
(\mu_{1,n+1},\mu_{2,n+1})
. We define
(\mu^{\prime}_{1,t},\mu^{\prime}_{2,t}\alpha^{\prime}_{t})
extending the deformation
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
of order
n
as follows-

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{1,t}=\mu_{1,t}+\mu_{1,n+1}t^{n+1},

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{2,t}=\mu_{2,t}+\mu_{2,n+1}t^{n+1},

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{t}=\alpha_{t}+\alpha_{n+1}t^{n+1}.

It is a routine work to check that
(\mu^{\prime}_{1,t},\mu^{\prime}_{2,t},\alpha^{\prime}_{t})
defines a formal deformation of order
n+1
.
Thus,
(\mu^{\prime}_{1,t},\mu^{\prime}_{2,t},\alpha^{\prime}_{t})
is a deformation of order
n+1
which extends the deformation
(\mu_{1,t},\mu_{2,t},\alpha_{t})
of order
n
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

If

\mu^{E}_{2}((0,a),(0,b))=(f(a,b),\mu_{2}(a,b))
, for some
f\in C^{2,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M).
The Hom-associativity of
\mu^{E}_{1},\mu^{E}_{2}
then implies that
f
is a
2
-cocycle. Similarly, one can observe that any two equivalent extensions are related by a map
E=M\oplus A\xrightarrow{\phi}M\oplus A=E^{\prime}
,
(m,a)\mapsto(m+g(a),a)
for some
g\in C^{1,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
. Since
\phi

\displaystyle\phi\circ\mu^{E}_{1}((0,a),(0,b))=\mu^{\prime E}_{1}(\phi(0,a),\phi(0,b))

\displaystyle\phi\circ\mu^{E}_{2}((0,a),(0,b))=\mu^{\prime E}_{2}(\phi(0,a),\phi(0,b))

which implies that
f^{\prime}(a,b)=f(a,b)-(\delta_{\alpha,\beta}~g)(a,b)
. Here
f^{\prime}
is the
2
-cocycle induced from the extension
E^{\prime}
. This shows that the map
\mathcal{E}xt_{c}(A,M)\rightarrow H^{2,c}_{\alpha,\beta}(A,M)
is well defined. Moreover, these two maps are inverses to each other.

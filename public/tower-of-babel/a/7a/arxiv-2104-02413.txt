Bias Correction in Deterministic Policy Gradient Using Robust MPC
Arash Bahari Kordabad, Hossein Nejatbakhsh Esfahani, Sebastien Gros

Abstract
In this paper, we discuss the deterministic policy gradient using the Actor-Critic methods based on the linear compatible advantage function approximator, where the input spaces are continuous. When the policy is restricted by hard constraints, the exploration may not be Centred or Isotropic (non-CI). As a result, the policy gradient estimation can be biased. We focus on constrained policies based on Model Predictive Control (MPC) schemes and to address the bias issue, we propose an approximate Robust MPC approach accounting for the exploration. The RMPC-based policy ensures that a Centered and Isotropic (CI) exploration is approximately feasible. A posterior projection is used to ensure its exact feasibility, we formally prove that this approach does not bias the gradient estimation.



I
INTRODUCTION

Reinforcement learning (RL) provides powerful tools for tackling Markov Decision Processes (MPDs) without depending on the probability distribution underlying the state transition
[
1
,
2
]
. RL methods attempt to enhance the closed-loop performance of a control policy deployed on the MDP, using observed realisation of the state transitions and of the corresponding stage cost. RL methods are usually either direct, based on an approximation of the optimal policy (e.g., deterministic and stochastic policy gradient methods
[
3
]
) or indirect, based on an approximation of the action-value function (e.g., Q-learning). Unstructured function approximation techniques (e.g., Deep Neural Networks) are often used to carry these approximations. Unfortunately, the closed-loop behavior of such approximators can be challenging to analyze formally. In contrast, structured function approximations such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) schemes provide a formal framework to analyse the stability and feasibility of the closed-loop system
[
4
]
. Recent research have focused on MPC-based policy approximation for RL
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

For computational reasons, simple models are usually preferred in the MPC scheme. Hence, the MPC model often does not have the structure required to correctly capture the real system dynamics and stochasticity. As a result, while MPC can deliver a reasonable approximation of the optimal policy, it is usually suboptimal
[
11
]
. Choosing the MPC model parameters that maximise the closed-loop performance of the MPC scheme is a difficult problem, and the parameters that best fit the MPC model to the real system are not guaranteed to yield the best MPC policy
[
6
]
. In
[
9
,
6
]
, it is shown that adjusting not only the MPC model, but also the cost and constraints can be beneficial to achieve the best closed-loop performances, and RL is proposed as a possible approach to perform that adjustment in practice. In the presence of uncertainties and stochasticity, if constraints satisfaction is critical, Robust Model Predictive Control (RMPC) provides tools to ensure that the constraints are satisfied, and can be used in the RL context
[
12
]
.

Actor-Critic (AC) techniques combine the strong points of actor-only (policy search methods) and critic-only (e.g., Q-learning) methods
[
13
]
. AC approaches are based on genuine optimality conditions of the closed-loop policy and typically deliver less noisy policy gradients than direct policy search. The deterministic policy gradient is built based on an approximation of the advantage function associated with the policy. To this end, a linear compatible advantage function approximator is a convenient choice, because it provides a correct policy gradient estimation with a given structure and a low number of parameters
[
3
]
. For deterministic policies, exploration is required in order to estimate the corresponding policy gradient. In the presence of hard constraints, this exploration can be restricted. As a result, the exploration may become non-CI. In
[
14
]
it is shown that a linear compatible advantage function approximator can deliver an incorrect policy gradient estimation for a non-CI exploration.

In this paper, we propose to use a RMPC scheme that is robust with respect to a bounded disturbance of its first control input to enable the feasibility of a CI exploration. Because RMPC is computationally expensive, we use an inexpensive approximate RMPC instead, feasible to a first-order approximation. To ensure the feasibility of the exploration, a posterior projection technique is used. As a main result of this paper, we formally prove that the exploration resulting from RMPC scheme delivers an unbiased policy gradient estimation.

The paper is structured as follows. Section
II
provides background material on RL and details the bias problem. Section
III
presents the RMPC-based approach that tackles the problem. For the sake of simplicity, we will consider a formulation robust with respect to the exploration only, while in practice the formulation can also be robust against model uncertainties and the stochasticity of the real system, as in
[
12
]
. Section
IV
presents the projection approach required for nonlinear problems. Section
V
describes the main theorem in the gradient bias correction using RMPC-based policy and proves that the resulting approach asymptotically yields a correct policy gradient. Section
VI
provides numerical examples of the method. Section
VII
delivers a conclusion.

Background

For a given MDP with continuous state-input space, a deterministic policy parametrized by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
delivers an input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
as a function of state
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as,

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
If delivered by an MPC scheme, this policy is obtained as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}^{\star}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}\right),

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}^{\star}
is the first element of the solution
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}^{\star}
given by:

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}

\displaystyle\quad V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{N})+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{k}\ell_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}),

(2a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}\right),\,\,\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},

(2b)

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}\right)\leq 0,\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{N}\right)\leq 0,

(2c)

where
V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
and
\ell_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
are the MPC terminal and stage costs, respectively. Function
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
is the model dynamics and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}
are the stage and terminal inequality constraints, respectively. Vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{0,\ldots,N}\}
is the predicted state trajectory and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0,\ldots,N-1}\}
is the input profile. State
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}
is the current state of the system,
N
is the horizon length and
\gamma\in[0,1]
is the discount factor. For the following theoretical developments, it will be useful to consider a single-shooting formulation of MPC (
2
) resulting in a parametric Nonlinear Program (NLP):

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}

\displaystyle\quad\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}),

(3a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)\leq 0,

(3b)

delivering the input profile of (
2
) for all
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}
for some cost
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
and inequality constraints
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
. We seek the policy parameters
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
that minimize the overall closed-loop cost
J
of the policy
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})=\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left[\left.\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\gamma^{k}L(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k})\,\right|\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k}\right)\,\right],

(4)

where
L(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})\in\mathbb{R}
is the baseline stage cost evaluating the policy performance. It is shown in
[
6
]
that using an MPC stage cost
\ell_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
different from the baseline stage cost
L
can be beneficial when the MPC model is not exact. The expectation
\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}
is taken over the distribution of the Markov chain in closed-loop with the policy
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
. The policy gradient for the deterministic policy
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
is obtained as follows
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\,J({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\left[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}))\right],

(5)

where
A_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})=Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})-V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})
is the advantage function associated to
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
, and where
Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
and
V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
are the action-value and value functions for the policy
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
, respectively. In a non-episodic context, the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}
is taken over the steady-state distribution of the Markov chain. In an RL context, the advantage function
A_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
must be approximated and evaluated from data. In the following, we label the advantage function approximation as
A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
with parameter vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}
. The corresponding estimation of the policy gradient in (
5
) reads as:

\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\,J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}))\right].

(6)

The following theorem provides the condition allowing one to replace the exact advantage
A_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
in (
5
) by an approximation
A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
, without affecting the policy gradient.

Theorem 1
.

[
3
]

If
A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
satisfies

i.

\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}
,

ii.

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}
minimizes the following mean-squared error:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}=\mathrm{arg}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}\,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\left[\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right\|^{2}\right],

(7)

where the gradients are evaluated at
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
, then we have:

\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\,J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})}={\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\,J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})}.

(8)

Proof.

See
[
3
]
.
∎

An advantage function approximator that achieves (
8
) is labelled
compatible
. A linear compatible advantage function approximator
A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
, parametrized by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}
can read as
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}^{\top}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\right).

(9)

It is well known that estimating
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
directly is very difficult
[
3
]
. As a surrogate to (
7
), the least-squares problem:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}=\mathrm{arg}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}\,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left[\left(Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-\hat{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right)^{2}\right],

(10)

is used, where the value function estimation
\hat{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\approx V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
is a baseline supporting the evaluation of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}
.
In order to obtain
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}
from (
10
), the input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}
applied to the real system must be different from the actual policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
, i.e. the input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}
applied to the real system should include some exploration in order to depart from the given policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
. One common choice of exploration is to add a random disturbance
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}
to the policy as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}.

(11)

For the sake of clarity, we define hereafter a CI exploration.

Definition 1
.

An exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}
is Centred and Isotropic (CI) if
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}[\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}]=0
, and there exists a scalar
p
such that,
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}[\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}]=pI.

Otherwise it is non-CI.

Since the policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
is subject to the hard constraints (
3b
), an arbitrary input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}
resulting from a random exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}
may not be feasible. Hence the exploration ought to be restricted such that it respects the constraints. A possible solution for this problem is, e.g., to use a projection of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}
on the feasible set of NLP (
3
). In the following we provide a definition for the projection operator.

Definition 2
.

For an arbitrary input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}
, the projection operator
P(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle P(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}^{\perp}_{0},

(12a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{arg}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}\quad\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}\|^{2},

(12b)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)\leq 0,

(12c)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}^{\perp}_{0}
is the first element of the input profile
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}^{\perp}_{0,\ldots,N-1}
solution of (
12b
)-(
12c
).

In particular, at a given state
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}
, the input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{\perp}
resulting from projecting the exploration is given by:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{\perp}=P\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\right)+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}\right).

(13)

Then the projected exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}_{\perp}
is given by:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}_{\perp}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}.

(14)

Unfortunately, even if the selected exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}
is CI, the projected exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}_{\perp}
may not be
[
14
]
. It is shown in
[
14
]
that the linear compatible function approximator (
9
) using the fitting problem (
10
) delivers a correct estimated policy gradient (
6
) only for a CI exploration.

In this paper, we modify (
3
) to find a policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\theta}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\right)
for which a CI exploration is feasible. This policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\theta}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\right)
is based on creating a small distance from the boundaries of the constraints so that a small CI exploration is feasible. To perform this modification, in the next section we will introduce an approximate RMPC scheme having a computational complexity similar to a standard MPC scheme. This RMPC scheme delivers a policy that can be disturbed with an additive perturbation in a given ball while keeping feasibility to a first-order approximation.

III
RMPC-based deterministic policy

In this section, we propose a modified policy
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\theta}
based on an RMPC-scheme such that any input
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}}
resulting from:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\theta}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\right)+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\in B(0,\eta),

(15)

is feasible for the MPC (
2
), where
B(0,\eta)
is a ball of radius
\eta
. For the sake of brevity, we consider in the following that the exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}
is uniformly distributed in the ball
B(0,\eta)
. In that specific case, the exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}
is CI with
p=\frac{1}{3}\eta^{2}
. To generate
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\theta}
we tighten the inequality constraint (
3b
) of NLP (
3
) as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}

\displaystyle\quad\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}),

(16a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)\leq 0,

(16b)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)\geq 0
is a back-off term added to ensure that the NLP (
3
) is feasible for any additive perturbation
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\in B\left(0,\eta\right)
of the input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}
obtained from (
16
). In general, evaluating
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)
is difficult. To address this issue, we propose to compute
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)
using a first-order approximation of the constraint (
3b
). More specifically, we will impose the approximated constraint:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{u}}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)+\left.\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\,\leq 0,

(17)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{u}}}
is the input profile resulting from perturbing
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}
with the exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\in B\left(0,\eta\right)
in the first input
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}
. The following Lemma provides an explicit form for (
17
).

Lemma 1
.

Inequality (
17
) holds tightly for all
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\in B\left(0,\eta\right)
if

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\right)+\left\|\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}\right\|\eta\leq 0

(18)

holds, where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
is the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
element of the vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
.

Proof.

The following inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\leq\left\|\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}\right\|\eta\,,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\in B(0,\eta),

(19)

holds and is tight, where
\|.\|
indicates an Euclidean norm.
∎

The principles detailed above readily apply to MPC scheme (
2
). More specifically, an input disturbance
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}
yields:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}

\displaystyle\approx\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}\right)+\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}+\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}},

(20)

where the left hand side is evaluated of the perturbed trajectory and
\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}
is obtained from the following linear dynamics:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k-1}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k-1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}+\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\right)\Big|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k-1},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k-1}},

(21)

with the initial condition
\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{0}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}=0
.

Imposing an arbitrary exploration radius
\eta
may be infeasible for some state
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}
. To avoid this issue, we consider the radius as a decision variable
\nu\in[0,\bar{\eta}]
whose optimal solution is
\eta
. We label
\bar{\eta}
the maximum desired radius for the exploration. The RMPC-based policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
is then obtained as the first element of the input sequence given by:

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}},\nu}

\displaystyle\quad-w\nu+V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{N})+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{k}\ell_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}),

(22a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}\right),\,\,\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},

(22b)

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{0},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}\right)_{i}+\left\|\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\right)_{i}\right\|\nu\leq 0,

(22c)

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}\right)_{i}+\left\|\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\right)_{i}\right\|\nu\leq 0,\,\,k>0,

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{N}\right)_{i}+\left\|\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{N}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{N}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\right)_{i}\right\|\nu\leq 0

(22d)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}},\quad k=2,\ldots,N-1,

(22e)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}},\quad 0\leq\nu\leq\bar{\eta},

(22f)

where
w
is a positive constant weight, chosen large enough such that
\eta=\bar{\eta}
when feasible. Index
i
indicates the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
element of the vectors.

One can observe that this RMPC scheme is feasible if the original MPC scheme (
2
) is feasible. Indeed, the choice
\nu=0
makes the RMPC and MPC schemes equivalent. It follows that the RMPC scheme (
22
) inherits the recursive feasibility of (
2
). We ought to stress again here that the recursive feasibility of (
2
) may require the robust formulation to be extended to take the stochastic disturbances and model errors into account, as e.g. in
[
12
]
. We have omitted this aspect here for the sake of brevity and simplicity. The theory presented hereafter is applicable to that extension. Additionally, one ought to note that RMPC (
22
) is accounting for a disturbance on the initial input only. However, exploration is meant to be applied on all times. This could be reflected in the RMPC by accounting for a disturbance of the entire input profile, with minor modifications of the formulation. These modifications would, however, unnecessarily reduce the feasible domain of (
22
). The proposed formulation arguably avoids that issue, and ensures feasibility via introducing the exploration radius as a decision variable in the NLP. Finally, a stabilizing feedback ought to be considered when forming the sensitivities (
21
), especially when the dynamics (
22e
) are unstable. This additional feedback is a classic tool to reduce the conservatism of the RMPC schemes. It is not presented here for the sake of brevity.

Since a first-order approximation of the constraints is used when forming (
22
), its solution may not ensure the feasibility of all exploration
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\in B\left(0,\eta\right)
. In the next section, we will address this problem with a posterior projection technique. We will show that this projection does not bias the policy gradient estimation.

IV
Ensuring feasibility

Because we considered a first-order approximation of the constraints when forming the RMPC (
22
), a posterior projection ought to be used to ensure the feasibility of the exploration. Using (
12
), we apply the projection of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}_{\perp}=P\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\right).

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}_{\perp}:=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}_{\perp}}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\epsilon}}.

Assumption 1

\displaystyle\forall i\,\,,\,\,\left\|\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}}\Big|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right\|\neq 0.

Lemma 2

For the projection error
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\epsilon}}\|\leq\alpha\eta^{2}.

Proof.

Let us define
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}):=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}),

(28)

where

\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
as the

\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{v}}
, i.e.
\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{v}}\|=1

\zeta\leq\eta
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}=\zeta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{v}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{i}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\right)\leq\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{i}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\right)+(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}+R\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\right),

(29)

where

. The inequality (
29

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\in B\left(0,\eta\right)
for some continuous function

\displaystyle\left|R\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\right)\right|\leq c\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\|^{2}.

(30)

Additionally:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{i}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\right)\leq-\eta\left\|(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\right\|,

(31)

because

is feasible for the RMPC scheme (
22

Consider any sequence
\eta_{k}>0
converging uniformly to
0

for some positive constant
\alpha

such that for all

as the exploration in the right side of (

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}^{i}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\right)+(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\left(1-\alpha\eta_{k}\right)+R\left(\left(1-\alpha\eta_{k}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\right)\leq

\displaystyle-\eta_{k}\left\|(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\right\|+(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\zeta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{v}}\left(1-\alpha\eta_{k}\right)+c\left(1-\alpha\eta_{k}\right)^{2}\zeta^{2}\leq

\displaystyle-\eta_{k}\left\|(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\right\|+\left\|(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\right\|\eta_{k}\left(1-\alpha\eta_{k}\right)+c\left(1-\alpha\eta_{k}\right)^{2}\zeta^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left\|(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\right\|\alpha\eta_{k}^{2}+c\left(1-\alpha\eta_{k}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\leq 0,

(32)

where the first inequality uses (
30
) and (
31
). The second inequality is obtained from selecting
\zeta=\eta_{k}
and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Using Assumption
1
, the last inequality holds for
c\left\|(\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})^{\prime}\right\|^{-1}\leq\alpha
. Therefore,
t_{k}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}
is a feasible exploration for (
3
) and has a larger (or equal) error than the projection error. Then we have:

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\epsilon}}\|=\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}\|=\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\|\leq\|t_{k}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\|=

\displaystyle\|(1-t_{k})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\|=\|\alpha\eta_{k}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}}}\|\leq\alpha\eta^{2}.\quad\quad\quad\qed

(33)

V
Corrected Policy Gradient

In this section, we will show that the robust policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
delivers the true gradient as
\bar{\eta}\rightarrow 0
. Indeed, the deterministic policy gradient method uses “small” exploration and all results are valid in the sense of
\bar{\eta}\rightarrow 0
. We propose the compatible advantage function:

\displaystyle A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}_{\perp}\right)=\frac{\bar{\eta}^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}^{\top}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\right),

(43)

where the factor
\frac{\bar{\eta}^{2}}{\eta^{2}}
is required to account for the varying exploration radius
\eta
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}
is obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}=\mathrm{arg}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}\,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}}\left[\frac{1}{\bar{\eta}^{2}}\left(Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-\hat{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right)^{2}\right]

(44)

where
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}}=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}[\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}}[.|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}]]
and
\bar{\eta}^{-2}
is introduced such that (
44
) remains well-posed for
\bar{\eta}\rightarrow 0
.

Assumption 2
.

Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
is analytic and at least twice differentiable for almost every feasible
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}
and
\nabla^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
is bounded.

Assumption
2
is usually satisfied in practice, as
Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
tends to be at least piecewise smooth for the many problems based on continuous state-input spaces. This assumption can be relaxed, but it requires more technical developments.

Theorem 3
.

The RMPC-based policy gradient estimation using the compatible advantage function in (
43

given by (

\displaystyle\lim_{\bar{\eta}\rightarrow 0}\widehat{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\,J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})}={\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\,J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})}.

(45)

Proof.

The solution of (
44
) is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}}\left[\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}\left(Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-\hat{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right)\right]=0.

(46)

Using Assumption
2
, the Taylor expansions of

and

are valid almost everywhere. They read as:

\displaystyle Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}_{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle=V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\right)+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})\right)^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\xi}},

\displaystyle A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{a}}}_{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})\right)^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}},

(47)

where

By substitution of (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}}\left[\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{e}_{\perp}}}^{\top}}\left(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right)\right]+

VII
CONCLUSION

This paper presented the AC approach using a linear compatible advantage function approximation for the MPC-based deterministic policies. When the policy is restricted by hard constraints, the exploration may be non-CI and delivers a bias in the policy gradient. We proposed RMPC using constraint tightening to provide an approximated feasible and CI exploration. A posterior projection is used to ensure feasibility and formally we showed that the RMPC-based policy gradient converges to the true policy gradient for a small enough radius of exploration.

Left θ \theta -derivations on weighted convolution algebras
M. Eisaei, M. J. Mehdipour and Gh. R. Moghimi

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. In this paper, we study left
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. We show that every left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is always a
\theta
-derivation, and if
\theta
is isomorphism, then
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
has no non-zero left
\theta
-derivation. We also investigate automatic continuity, Singer-Wermer’s conjecture and Posner’s first theorem for left
\theta-
derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

1
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
\omega
is a weight function on
{{R}}^{+}=[{0,\infty})
. Let us recall that a
weight function
on
{{R}}^{+}
is a continuous function

\omega:{{R}}^{+}\rightarrow[{1,+\infty})
such that
\omega\left(0\right)=1
and for every
x,y\in{{R}}^{+}

\omega\left({x+y}\right)\mathchar 13366\relax\omega\left(x\right)\omega\left(y\right).

Let
L^{1}({{R}}^{+},\omega)
be the Banach space of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
{{R}}^{+}
with
\omega f\in{L^{1}}({{R}}^{+})
, the Banach algebra of all Lebesgue integrable functions on
{{R}}^{+}
.
Let also
M({{R}}^{+},\omega)
be the Banach algebra of all complex regular Borel measures
\mu
on
{{R}}^{+}
such that
\omega\mu\in M({{R}}^{+})
, the Banach algebra of all complex regular Borel measures on
{{R}}^{+}
. It is well-known that
M({{R}}^{+},\omega)

is a commutative Banach algebra with the convolution product and the norm

\|\mu\|_{\omega}=\|\omega\mu\|\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt(\mu\in M({{R}}^{+},\omega)).

Also,
L^{1}({{R}}^{+},\omega)
can be regarded as a closed ideal in
M({{R}}^{+},\omega)
; for a study of these spaces see [7, 14].

Let
{L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)}
be the Banach space of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
{{R}}^{+}
such that

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\hbox{ess sup}\{\frac{f(y)\chi_{(x,\infty)}(y)}{\omega(y)}:y\in{{R}}^{+}\}=0.

It is proved that the dual of
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)
is a Banach algebra with the first Arens product
defined by

\langle{s\cdot t,f}\rangle=\langle{s,tf}\rangle,

where
\langle{tf,g}\rangle=\langle{t,fg}\rangle
and

fg(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{f\left({x+y}\right)g\left(y\right)dy}

for all
s,t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
,
f\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
g\in L^{1}({{R}}^{+},\omega)
; for more details see [9, 10, 11]. We denote by
\Lambda\left(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\right)
the set of all right identities of
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
with norm one. Let us remark that if
\nu\in\Lambda\left(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\right)
, then
\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
M\left({{R}}^{+},\omega\right)
are isometric isomorphic. Since
M({{R}}^{+},\omega)
is a commutative Banach algebra,
\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is commutative and hence

k\cdot s\cdot t=k\cdot\nu\cdot s\cdot\nu\cdot t=k\cdot\nu\cdot t\cdot\nu\cdot s=k\cdot t\cdot s

for all
k,s,t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. By Titchmarsh’s convolution theorem [3],
M({{R}}^{+},\omega)
is an integral domain. So
\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is an integral domain; see also Proposition 5.1 of [1]. We denote the right annihilator
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
by
\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
and defind by

\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})=\{r\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}:s\cdot r=0\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{for all}\hskip 10.00002pts\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\}.

It is easy to see that

L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}=\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\oplus\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}).

Let
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. A linear operator
\delta:L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\rightarrow L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is called a
left
\theta-
derivation
if

\delta(s\cdot t)=\theta(s)\cdot\delta(t)+\theta(t)\cdot\delta(s)

for all
s,t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Also,
\delta
is a
\theta-
derivation
if

\delta(s\cdot t)=\theta(s)\cdot\delta(t)+\delta(s)\cdot\theta(t)

for all
s,t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

Ashraf [3] studied Jordan left
\theta
-derivations and left
\theta
-derivations of a prime ring
R
with characteristic different from 2. He proved that if
d
is a Jordan left
\theta
-derivation of
R
, then
d=0
or
R
is commutative; see [5, 8] for Jordan left derivations. He also showed that every Jordan left
\theta
-derivation is a left
\theta
-derivation; see [4] for generalized left
\theta
-derivations. Here, we investigate the results for Banach algebra
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Note that it is easy to see that

L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is not a prime ring.

In this paper, we investigate left
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and show that every left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is a
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. In the case where
\theta
is isomorphism, the zero map is the only left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. We also study continuity of left
\theta
-derivations and prove that if
\|\theta(\nu)\|<1
, then the only continuous left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is zero, where
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then we give an analog of Posner’s first theorem. Finally, we investigate Singer-Wermer’s conjecture and establish that a left
\theta-
derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
maps
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
into its radical if and only if it is zero.

2
Left
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}

First, we investigate homomorphisms on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

Proposition 2.1

Let
\theta
be a non-zero homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)
If
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
, then

\theta(\nu)=\nu+r_{0}
for some
{r_{0}}\in\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
.

(ii)

\theta(\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}))\subseteq\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}))
.

(iii)
If
\theta
is monomorphism, then
\theta(\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}))=\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})\cap\emph{Im}(\theta)
.

(iv)
If
\theta
is isometric, then

\theta(\nu)=\nu
for all
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then there exist
{s_{0}}\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
r_{0}\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
such that
\theta(\nu)=\nu\cdot{s_{0}}+{r_{0}}
. So we have

\displaystyle({\nu\cdot{s_{0}}+{r_{0}}})\cdot({\nu\cdot{s_{0}}+{r_{0}}})=\theta(\nu)\cdot\theta(\nu)=\theta(\nu)=\nu\cdot{s_{0}}+{r_{0}}.

It follows that

({\nu\cdot{s_{0}}})\cdot({\nu\cdot{s_{0}}})-\nu\cdot{s_{0}}={r_{0}}-{r_{0}}\cdot{s_{0}}.

From this and the fact that

\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\cap\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})=\{0\},

we conclude that

({\nu\cdot{s_{0}}})\cdot({\nu\cdot{s_{0}}})-\nu\cdot{s_{0}}=0.

Since
\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is an integral domain,
\nu\cdot{s_{0}}=0
or
\nu\cdot{s_{0}}=\nu
.
If
\nu\cdot{s_{0}}=0
, then
\theta(\nu)={r_{0}}
and thus

\theta(\nu)=\theta(\nu)\cdot\theta(\nu)={r_{0}}\cdot{r_{0}}=0.

Hence for any
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, we have

\theta(s)=\theta({s\cdot\nu})=\theta(s)\cdot\theta(\nu)=0.

That is,
\theta=0
, a contradiction. So
\nu\cdot s_{0}=\nu
and therefore,

\theta(\nu)=\nu+{r_{0}}
.

(ii) Let
r\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
and
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then

0=\theta({\nu\cdot r})=\theta(\nu)\cdot\theta(r)=({\nu+{r_{0}}})\cdot\theta(r)

for some
{r_{0}}\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. This shows that

s\cdot\theta(r)=s\cdot({\nu+{r_{0}}})\cdot\theta(r)=0

for all
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. That is,
\theta(r)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
.

(iii) Let
{r}\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})\cap\hbox{Im}(\theta)
. Then
\theta(s)={r}
for some

s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. For every
t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, we have

\theta(t\cdot s)=\theta(t)\cdot\theta(s)=\theta(t)\cdot{r}=0.

Hence
\theta(t\cdot s)=0
. By injectivity of
\theta
,

t\cdot s=0

for all
t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. That is,
s\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Therefore,

\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})\cap\hbox{Im}(\theta)
is a subset of
\theta({\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})})
.

(iv) Let
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then
\theta(\nu)=\nu+{r_{0}}
for some

{r_{0}}\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. For every
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
we have
s\cdot\theta(\nu)=s
. Thus
\theta(\nu)
is a right identity for
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Since
\theta
is isometry,

\|{\theta(\nu)}\|=\|\nu\|=1
. So
\theta(\nu)=\nu_{1}\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
and consequently,
\nu-\nu_{1}=r_{0}
. Hence,
\nu=\nu_{1}
.

For a homomorphism
\theta
on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, let us recall that a left
\theta
-derivations
\delta
on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
is called
\theta
-
commuting
if for every
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}

\delta(s)\cdot\theta(s)=\theta(s)\cdot\delta(s).

In the case where
\theta=id
,
\theta

is called
commuting
, where
id
is the identity map on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Now, we are ready to prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.2

Let
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. If
\delta
is a left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, then the following statements hold.

(i)

\delta(\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}))=\delta({\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})})=\{0\}
.

(ii)

\delta(s)=\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(s)
for all
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

(iii)

\delta(s)\cdot\theta(t)=\theta(t)\cdot\delta(s)
for all
s,t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

(iv)

\delta
is a
\theta
-commating
\theta
-derivation.

Proof.
(i) Let
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then

\delta(\nu)=\delta({\nu\cdot\nu})=\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(\nu)+\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(\nu)=2\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(\nu).

(1)

It follows that

\delta(\nu)=4\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(\nu).

Therefore,
\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(\nu)=0
. According to (1),
\delta(\nu)=0
.

Assume now that
r\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then by (1) we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(\nu\cdot r)

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(r)+\theta(r)\cdot\delta(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(r)+\theta(r)\cdot({2\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(\nu)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(r)+2\theta(r)\cdot\delta(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\cdot\delta(r)+r_{0}\cdot\delta(r)+2\theta(r)\cdot\delta(\nu),

where
\theta(\nu)=\nu+r_{0}
for some
r_{0}\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. This together with Proposition 2.1(ii) and the fact that

\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\cap\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})=\{0\}

shows that
\nu\cdot\delta(r)=\{0\}
and hence
\delta(r)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Thus
\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(r)=0
. From this and (2) we see that

\theta(r)\cdot\delta(\nu)=0
. So

\delta(r)=\delta({r\cdot\nu})=\theta(r)\cdot\delta(\nu)+\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(r)=0.

That is,
\delta
is zero on
{\hbox{ran}({L_{0}^{\infty}{{({{R}}^{+},\omega)}^{*}}})}
.

(ii) Let
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then
s-\nu\cdot s\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. By (i), we have

\delta(s-\nu\cdot s)=0
. Since
\delta(\nu)=0
, we have

\delta(s)=\delta(\nu\cdot s)=\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(s)+\theta(s)\cdot\delta(\nu)=\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(s).

(iii) Let
\delta
be a left
\theta
-derivation,
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
,
s,t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
r\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then

0=\delta({s\cdot r})=\theta(s)\cdot\delta(r)+\theta(r)\cdot\delta(s)=\theta(r)\cdot\delta(s)

and

\delta(s)\cdot\theta({\nu\cdot t})=\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(s)\cdot\theta({\nu\cdot t})=\theta({\nu\cdot t})\cdot\delta(s).

Choose
{r_{s}},{r_{t}}\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
such that

s=\nu\cdot s+{r_{s}}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptt=\nu\cdot t+{r_{t}}.

Since
\delta({\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})})=\{0\}
, it follows that
\theta({\nu\cdot s})\cdot\delta({{r_{t}}})=0
and so

\theta({{r_{t}}})\cdot\delta({\nu\cdot s})=\delta({({{\nu\cdot s}})\cdot{r_{t}}})=0.

We also have

\displaystyle\delta({\nu\cdot s})\cdot\theta({\nu\cdot t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(s)\cdot\theta({\nu\cdot t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta({\nu\cdot t})\cdot\delta(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta({\nu\cdot t})\cdot\delta({\nu\cdot s}).

These imply that
\theta(t)\cdot\delta(s)=\delta(s)\cdot\theta(t)
.

(iv) This follows at once from (iii).

We now give some consequence of Theorem 2.2.

Corollary 2.3

Let
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. If
\delta
is a left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, then
\delta=0
.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then
\delta(\nu)=0
and so

\delta(s)=\delta({s\cdot\nu})=\delta(s)\cdot\delta(\nu)=0

for all
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

\hfill\mathchar 1027\relax

Corollary 2.4

Let
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
,
\delta
be a non-zero left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then
\delta
is continuous if and only if
\delta|_{\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}}
is continuous. In this case,
\|\theta(\nu)\|\geq 1
.

Proof.
Let
\delta|_{\nu\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}}
be continuous.
If
\{s_{i}\}_{i}
is a sequence in
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
with

s_{i}\rightarrow s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*},

then
\nu\cdot s_{i}\rightarrow\nu\cdot s.
It follows that

\delta(s_{i})=\delta(\nu\cdot s_{i})\rightarrow\delta(\nu\cdot s)=\delta(s).

Hence
\delta
is continuous. The converse is clear.

To complete the proof, let
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
such that
\delta(s)\neq 0
. Then

\|\delta(s)\|=\|\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(s)\|\leq\|\theta(\nu)\|\|\delta(s)\|.

Hence
\|\theta(\nu)\|\geq 1
.

As an immediate consequence of Corollary 2.4, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.5

Let
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
and
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
with
\|\theta(\nu)\|<1
. Then the zero map is the only continuous left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

Note that if
\theta
is a monomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, then there exists a map
\bar{\theta}
on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
such that
\bar{\theta}\theta=id
.

Theorem 2.6

Let
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. If
\delta
is a left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, then the following statements hold.

(i)

\emph{Im}(\delta)\subseteq\cap\{u\cdot L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}:u\;\emph{isarightidentityof}\;L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}\}
.

(ii)
If
\theta
is monomorphism and
\bar{\theta}
is homomorphism, then
\emph{Im}(\delta)\subseteq\emph{ker}(\bar{\theta})
and
\hbox{Im}(\delta)\cap\hbox{Im}(\theta)=\{0\}
. In this case,
\delta
is not surjective.

(iii)
If
\theta
is isomorphism, then
\delta=0
on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

Proof.
(i) Let
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. Then
\theta(\nu)
is a right identity of
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
by Proposition 2.1(i). Now, apply Theorem 2.2(ii).

(ii) Let
\theta
be a monomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Set
\eta:=\bar{\theta}\delta
. Then
\eta
is a left derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and so by Theorem 4.1 of [1],

\bar{\theta}\delta=\eta=0.

This implies that

\hbox{Im}(\delta)\subseteq\hbox{ker}(\bar{\theta}).

Let
s,t\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
\delta(s)=\theta(t)
. Then

0=\bar{\theta}(\delta)(s)=\bar{\theta}\theta(t)=t.

This shows that
\hbox{Im}(\delta)\cap\hbox{Im}(\theta)=\{0\}
. That is, (i) holds. Finally, suppose that
\delta
is surjective. Then

L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}=\hbox{Im}(\delta)\subseteq\hbox{ker}(\bar{\theta}).

Therefore,
\bar{\theta}=0
, a contradiction.

(iii) Let
\theta
be isomorphism. Then
\hbox{ker}(\bar{\theta})=0
. So by (ii),
\delta
is zero on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

Singer-Wermer’s conjecture states that the range of a derivation of a Banach algebra
A
is into
\hbox{rad}(A)
, the radical of
A
. Brešar and Vukmann [5] studied the conjecture for left derivations and proved that the range of a continuous left derivation of a Banach algebra is into its radical. Now, we investigate the conjecture for left
\theta
-derivations of
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

Theorem 2.7

Let
\theta
be a monomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
\delta
be a left
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Then the range of
\delta
is contained into
\emph{rad}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
if and only if
\delta=0
.

Proof.
Let
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
and
\delta(s)\in\hbox{rad}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. By Theorem 2.1 of [12], we have

\hbox{rad}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})=\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}).

Hence
\delta(s)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. In view of Theorem 2.2(ii), we have

\delta(s)=\theta(\nu)\cdot\delta(s)=0,

where
\nu\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
.
Therefore,
\delta=0.

Posenr [13] proved that if the product of two derivations of a prime ring of characteristic not 2 is a derivation, then the one of derivations is zero; see Creedon [6] for product of derivations of Banach algebras. This result is known as Posner’s first theorem.

Theorem 2.8

Let
\theta
be an idempotent monomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, and let
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
and
\delta_{1}\delta_{2}
be left
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

\delta_{1}|_{\emph{Im}(\theta)}=0
or
\delta_{2}=0
.

(ii)
If
\theta\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}\theta
, then
\delta_{1}=0
or
\delta_{2}=0
.

Proof.
Since
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
and
\delta_{1}\delta_{2}
are left
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, it follows that

\theta\delta_{2}(s)\delta_{1}\theta(s)=0

for all
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. In view of Proposition 5.1 of [1], we have

\theta\delta_{2}(s)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{or}\hskip 10.00002pt\delta_{1}\theta(s)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}).

If
\theta\delta_{2}(s)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
, then Proposition 2.1 (iii) implies that
\delta_{2}(s)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
. By Theorem 2.7,
\delta_{2}=0
. If
\delta_{1}\theta(s)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*})
, then another application of Theorem 2.7 shows that
\delta_{1}\theta(s)=0
for all
s\in L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. Therefore,
\delta_{1}|_{\hbox{Im}(\theta)}=0
. That is, (i) holds. For (ii), we only recall that if
\theta\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}\theta
, then by the proof of (i)

\theta\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}\theta=0.

This implies that
\delta_{1}=0
on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
.

\hfill\mathchar 1027\relax

Corollary 2.9

Let
\theta
be an idempotent monomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
, and let
\delta
and
\delta^{2}
be left
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}({{R}}^{+},\omega)^{*}
. If
\delta\theta=\theta\delta
, then
\delta
is zero.

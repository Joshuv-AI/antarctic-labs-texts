Abstract
. We consider the limit of solutions of scaled linear kinetic equations with a reflection-transmission-absorption condition at the interface. Both the coefficient describing the probability of absorption and the scattering kernel degenerate. We prove that the long-time, large-space limit is the unique solution of a version of the fractional in space heat equation that corresponds to the Kolmogorov equation for a symmetric stable process, which is reflected, or transmitted while crossing the interface and is killed upon the first hitting of the interface. The results of the paper are related to the work in [ 19 ] , where the case of a non-degenerate probability of absorption has been considered.



1.
Introduction

We study the asymptotic behaviour of a linear kinetic equation on
the real line
{\mathbb{R}}
:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}W(t,y,k)+\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)\partial_{y}W(t,y,k)\,=\,\gamma L_{k}W(t,y,k),&\mbox{ on }(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}\times{\mathbb{T}};\\
W(0,y,k)\,=\,W_{0}(y,k),&\mbox{ on }{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}},\end{cases}

with the following boundary condition at
x=0
:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}W(t,0^{+},k)\,=\,p_{+}(k)W(t,0^{-},k)+p_{-}(k)W(t,0^{+},-k)+p_{0}(k)T_{o},&\mbox{ on }{\mathbb{T}}_{+};\\
W(t,0^{-},k)\,=\,p_{+}(k)W(t,0^{+},k)+p_{-}(k)W(t,0^{-},-k)+p_{0}(k)T_{o},&\mbox{ on }{\mathbb{T}}_{-}.\end{cases}

Here,
{\mathbb{T}}
denotes the unit one-dimensional
torus
, understood as the
interval
[-1/2,1/2]
with identified endpoints,

{\mathbb{T}}_{\pm}:=[k\in{\mathbb{T}}:\pm k>0]
and

{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}:={\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. We call the origin
o:=[y=0]

interface
. The parameters
\gamma>0
,
T_{o}\geq 0

are given
and the functions

\bar{\omega},p_{\pm},p_{0}
, defined on
{\mathbb{T}}
, are assumed to be
continuous and non-negative. The

scattering operator

L_{k}
, acting only on the variable
k
in
{\mathbb{T}}
, is given by

(1.3)

L_{k}u(k)\,:=\,\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}R(k,k^{\prime})\left[u(k^{\prime})-u(k)\right]\,dk^{\prime}.

Here
R:{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\to[0,+\infty)
is
C^{2}
smooth and
u
belongs to
L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
.

This equation
arises in the kinetic limit of the evolution of the energy density
in a
stochastically perturbed
harmonic chain interacting with a point Langevin thermostat located at
y=0
,
see
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
The energy density
W(t,y,k)
at time
t
is resolved in both the

spatial
variable
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
and the
frequency
variable
k\in{\mathbb{T}}
. The function

\bar{\omega}
is the
dispersion relation
of the harmonic chain and

\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)
is the
group velocity
of

phonons
of mode
k
– theoretical particles that carry the energy due
to the chain vibrations of frequency
k
.
The presence of
the Langevin thermostat results in the boundary (interface) condition (
1.2
).
The number
T_{o}

corresponds to the temperature of the thermostat, while

p_{+}(k)
,
p_{-}(k)
and
p_{0}(k)
are the respective probabilities of
transmission, reflection and killing of a mode
k
phonon at
the interface; see
[
20
]
. They are continuous even functions that satisfy

(1.4)

p_{+}(k)+p_{-}(k)+p_{0}(k)\,=\,1,\quad k\in{\mathbb{T}}.

With a small risk of ambiguity, in what follows we also write

(1.5)

p_{+}:=p_{+}(0),\quad p_{-}:=p_{-}(0),\quad p_{0}:=p_{0}(0).

Our main goal is to study the asymptotic behaviour of the solutions
of (
1.1
) on macroscopic space-time scales. More precisely, we are
interested in the limits of solutions, when
\lambda\to+\infty
, for the family of
rescaled
equations

(1.6)

\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\partial_{t}W_{\lambda}(t,y,k)+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{1/\alpha}}\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)\partial_{y}W_{\lambda}(t,y,k)\,=\,\gamma L_{k}W_{\lambda}(t,y,k),&(t,y,k)\in(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}\times{\mathbb{T}};\\
W_{\lambda}(0,y,k)\,=\,W_{0}(y,k),&(y,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}},\end{cases}

subject to the boundary condition (
1.2
),
where

1<\alpha<2
is a suitably chosen exponent.

It turns out, see
[
4
,
19
]
, that under appropriate assumptions on the total scattering kernel
R(k):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}R(k,k^{\prime})dk^{\prime}
, group velocity
\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)
and a suitable choice of
\alpha
, the limit
\bar{W}(t,y):=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}W_{\lambda}(t,y,k)
exists in a weak
(distribution) sense. In the case where
\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{T}}}[\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)]^{2}/R(k)<+\infty
and
\alpha=2
(diffusive
space-time
scaling),
\bar{W}(t,y)
satisfies the heat equation with the Dirichlet boundary
condition
\bar{W}(t,0)=T_{o}
and
\bar{W}(0,y)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}W(0,y,k)dk
, see
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
. On the
other hand, when
|\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)|/R(k)\sim|k|^{-\beta}
, for
|k|\ll 1
,
with
\beta>1
and
p_{0}=\lim_{k\to 0}p_{0}(k)>0
, then letting

\alpha=1+1/\beta
one can show, see
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
, that

\bar{W}(t,y)
satisfies a fractional diffusion equation with a
boundary condition at
y=0
. In informal terms, the equation states
that

(1.7)

\partial_{t}\bar{W}(t,y)={\cal L}\Big(\bar{W}(t,y)-T_{o}\Big),

where

{\cal L}
is the generator of a modified symmetric

(1+1/\beta)
-stable process. The process behaves like a “regular” symmetric

(1+1/\beta)
-stable process outside the interface
y=0
, but
transmits, reflects, or dies at the interface with the
respective probabilities
p_{+}
,
p_{-}
and
p_{0}
of (
1.5
).

In the present paper, we address the situations where
p_{0}=0
, in fact, we assume that the
absorption probability

p_{0}(k)
satisfies, for some

\kappa>0
,

(1.8)

p_{*}:=\liminf_{k\to 0}\left(\log|k|^{-1}\right)^{\kappa}p_{0}(k)\in(0,+\infty).

Furthermore, we suppose that the
transmission probability
does not vanish, that is,

(1.9)

\inf_{k\in{\mathbb{T}}}p_{+}(k)>0.

The dispersion relation
\bar{\omega}:{\mathbb{T}}\to[0,+\infty)
is assumed to be even and

unimodal
, i.e., it possesses exactly one local maximum, at
1/2
, and
one local minimum, at
0
. In addition it is of
the
C^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{*})
class of regularity, where
{\mathbb{T}}_{*}:={\mathbb{T}}\setminus\{0\}
, and there exist one-sided limits of its
both derivatives at

k=0
. A typical dispersion relation we have in mind is

\bar{\omega}(k)=|\sin(\pi k)|
, which corresponds to the
harmonic chain with the nearest-neighbour interaction.

Concerning the scattering kernel, we assume that it is of the
multiplicative form

(1.10)

R(k,k^{\prime})\,=\,R_{1}(k)R_{2}(k^{\prime}),

where
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
are two non-negative, even functions belonging
to
C^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
.
Without loss of generality, we also suppose that

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}R_{j}(k)\,dk\,=\,1,\quad j=1,2.

The functions
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
may possibly vanish at some points in

{\mathbb{T}}
, but we suppose that there exist exponents
\beta_{j}>0
,
j=1,2,3
,
such that

(1.11)

\displaystyle R^{\ast}_{j}\,

\displaystyle:=\,\lim_{k\to 0}\frac{R_{j}(k)}{|k|^{\beta_{j}}}>0,\quad j=1,2,

(1.12)

\displaystyle S_{\ast}\,

\displaystyle:=\,\lim_{k\to 0}\frac{|k|^{\beta_{3}}|\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)|}{\gamma R_{1}(k)}>0,

(1.13)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\,

\displaystyle<\,1+\beta_{2},

(1.14)

\displaystyle\alpha\,

\displaystyle:=\,\frac{1+\beta_{2}}{\beta_{3}}\,\in\,(1,2).

Consistent with the condition (
1.13
), we further require that

(1.15)

{\cal R}:=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{R_{2}(k)}{R_{1}(k)}dk\in(0,+\infty).

We define the probability measure
\pi(dk):=R_{2}(k)/\big({\cal R}R_{1}(k)\big)dk
and we can
informally
state the main result of the present work; see
Theorem
2.3
for a rigorous formulation.

Main Theorem.

Let
W_{0}(y,k)
be a sufficiently regular function satisfying the interface conditions in (
1.2
) and such that the averaged function

(1.16)

\bar{W}_{0}(y)\,:=\,\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}W_{0}(y,k)\,\pi(dk)

satisfies
\bar{W}_{0}(0)=T_{o}
. Let
W_{\lambda}(t,y,k)
be the solution to
(
1.6
) with interface conditions
(
1.2
). Then, under the hypotheses
(
1.8
)–(
1.14
) made above, the limit

\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}W_{\lambda}(t,y,k)\,=\,\bar{W}(t,y)
exists
in a distributional sense on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
for any
t>0
. Moreover,
\bar{W}(t,y)

is a unique weak solution, in the sense of Definition
2.2

below, of the equation:

(1.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{W}(t,y)\,=\,\bar{\gamma}\,\,{\rm p.v.}\int_{yy^{\prime}>0}q_{\alpha}(y^{\prime}-y)[\bar{W}(t,y^{\prime})-\bar{W}(t,y)]\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\bar{\gamma}p_{+}\,\int_{yy^{\prime}<0}q_{\alpha}(y^{\prime}-y)[\bar{W}(t,y^{\prime})-\bar{W}(t,y)]\,dy^{\prime}+\bar{\gamma}p_{-}\,\int_{yy^{\prime}<0}q_{\alpha}(y^{\prime}-y)[\bar{W}(t,-y^{\prime})-\bar{W}(t,y)]\,dy^{\prime},

with the initial and boundary conditions

\bar{W}(0,y)=\bar{W}_{0}(y)
and

\bar{W}(t,0)\,=\,T_{o}
, respectively.
Here,
\bar{\gamma}
is a positive constant depending on the
parameters of the model (see (
2.9
) below),

(1.18)

q_{\alpha}(y)\,:=\,\frac{c_{\alpha}}{|y|^{1+\alpha}},\qquad c_{\alpha}\,:=\,\frac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma((1+\alpha)/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha/2-1)},\quad y\not=0,

and
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Euler gamma function.

Remark 1.1
.

The above result should be compared with the result of
[
19
]
,
where the case
p_{0}=\lim_{k\to 0}p_{0}(k)>0

has been considered. As we have already mentioned, the informal
formulation of the limiting fractional dynamics involves the generator

{\cal L}
of the process that is symmetric stable and can
be transmitted, reflected or killed when crossing the interface at

y=0
. In the situation considered in the present paper we can also
view the evolution of
\bar{W}(t,y)
as described by equation
(
1.7
), with
{\cal L}
corresponding to a symmetric

\alpha
-stable process that is transmitted or reflected when
crossing the interface at
y=0
, with the respective probabilities

p_{+}
and
p_{-}
. Furthermore, the process is killed upon the first
hitting of the interface.

The fractional diffusion limit of a kinetic equation
has been the subject of intense investigation in recent years. We refer the interested reader to a review of the existing literature contained in

[
23
]
. However, there seem to be only few results dealing with a
fractional diffusion limit for kinetic equations with a boundary
condition. In this context, we mention the papers
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
19
]
.
The case that is somewhat related to ours is
considered in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
. In the first paper, the
convergence of scaled solutions to kinetic
equations in spatial dimension one, with diffusive reflection
condition on the boundary, is investigated.
However, this condition is different from ours. Furthermore, the results of

[
8
]

do not establish
the uniqueness of the limit,
stating only that it satisfies a certain fractional diffusive equation
with a boundary condition and leaving the question of the uniqueness of solutions for the limiting equation open, see the
remark after Theorem 1.2 in
[
8
]
.
In the paper
[
9
]
, the authors complete the results of

[
8
]
and
establish an anomalous
diffusive limit for a family of solutions of scaled
linear kinetic equations in a one-dimensional bounded domain with
diffusive boundary conditions. The scattering kernel, defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, is also of
multiplicative form, as in (
1.10
), and decays according to an appropriate
power law.

Concerning our methods of proof, we rely on the probabilistic interpretation of solutions to kinetic equations. As shown in
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
, the solution can be expressed, with the help of an underlying two-dimensional stochastic process
\{Y(t),K(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, see Proposition
3.1
below. The component
K
is the frequency mode of a phonon. If outside the interface
[y=0]
, it is described by a
pure jump,
{\mathbb{T}}
-valued Markov process corresponding to the operator
L_{k}
in
(
1.3
). The component
Y
of the process is the position of
the phonon. If the phonon does not cross the
interface, it performs a uniform motion between the consecutive scattering events, with velocity
-\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(K(t))
.
On the other hand, if
the phonon tries to cross the interface at time
t
, it will be transmitted,
reflected, or killed with the respective probabilities
p_{+}(K(t))
,

p_{-}(K(t))
and
p_{0}(K(t))
. In the case of reflection, the frequency
mode of the phonon changes to
-K(t)
. Using this probabilistic
interpretation, the asymptotic behaviour of the solutions
of (
1.6
) can be reformulated into the problem of finding
the limit of appropriately scaled processes

\{Y_{\lambda}(t),K_{\lambda}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, with the parameter
\lambda>0

corresponding to the ratio between the macro- and microscopic time units. This
approach has been successfully implemented in the case of solutions of
scaled kinetic equations without interface in
[
3
,
17
,
18
]
. In the
case when the killing probability
p_{0}
is strictly positive, as assumed in

[
19
]
, one can expect that typically the phonon crosses the interface
only finitely many times before being killed. Its trajectory can then be obtained by a path transformation (consisting in performing suitable reflections)
of the trajectory when no interface is present. This transformation
turns out to be continuous in the Skorokhod
J_{1}
topology on the path
space, thus the convergence of the scaled processes in the presence of
the interface is a consequence of the result for the process in the free
space, done, e.g., in
[
18
]
. This approach cannot be applied in our present situation, since
with small
p_{0}
it is not so obvious how to effectively control the number of interface crossings performed by
the phonon before it is killed. For this reason, we use a different method to deal with the problem. We define a Lévy-type process

\big\{Z(t)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
whose scaled limit
\big\{Z_{\lambda}(t)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
, as

\lambda\to+\infty
, is related to the respective limit of the position of the phonon by a deterministic time change. To find the limit of
\big\{Z_{\lambda}(t)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
, we prove that the associated Dirichlet forms converge, in
the sense of the
\Gamma
-convergence of forms (see Definition

6.1
below, or
[
24
]
), to the
Dirichlet form corresponding to the modified
\alpha
-stable process,
described in Remark
1.1
. Apart from the limit theory of kinetic equations, we present here some new technical results, which may be of independent interest, for the construction of skew-stable processes, for the characterisation of the fractional Sobolev spaces and for the definition and uniqueness of solutions to non-local parabolic equations.

The paper is organised as follows: after presenting some preliminaries in Section
2
, we reformulate the problem of finding the limit of solutions of (
1.6
), with the
boundary condition (
1.2
) into the problem of
finding the limit of the respective stochastic processes. This is done
in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we show how to use the
result concerning the limit of stochastic processes to conclude our
main result, formulated rigorously inTheorem
2.3
. Section
5
is devoted
to the proof of the convergence of the scaled Lévy
type processes, as stated in Theorem
3.4
, assuming the

\Gamma
-convergence of their respective Dirichlet forms. The latter is established
in Section
6
. Some auxiliary facts are proved in
Appendix
A
and
B
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Precise assumptions on the model.

Given an arbitrary set
A
and two functions
f,g:A\to[0,+\infty]
,
we write
f\preceq g
on
A
if there is a constant
C>0
such that

f(a)\,\leq\,Cg(a),\quad a\in A.

We also write
f\approx g
on
A
when
f\preceq g
and
g\preceq f
. In what follows, we always assume the following conventions:
\sum_{A}f(a)=0
and
\prod_{A}f(a)=1
, if
A=\emptyset
, and
\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(0):=0
, even though as a rule
\omega
is not differentiable at
k=0
.

Let us now denote for any
k
in
{\mathbb{T}}_{\ast}
,

(2.1)

\bar{t}(k)\,:=\,(\gamma R_{1}(k))^{-1},

which can be interpreted as
expected waiting time
for the scattering of a phonon at frequency
k
. The interplay between scattering and
drift
will be captured by the function

(2.2)

S(k)\,:=\,\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)\bar{t}(k),\quad k\in[-1/2,1/2].

Clearly, we can interpret
S(k)
as the expected distance travelled by the phonon before scattering.
According to our assumptions,
S
is an odd function,

C^{1}
smooth on
[-1/2,1/2]\setminus\{0\}
.
To simplify some
considerations below, we also assume that
S
is a bijection and hence decreases in
(0,1/2]
. By (
1.12
),

\lim_{k\to 0}|k|^{\beta_{3}}|S(k)|=S_{\ast}
and
we impose some further restrictions on the model coefficients:

(2.3)

\begin{split}&R_{j}(k)\,\approx\,|k|^{\beta_{j}},\\
&|S(k)|\,\approx\,\frac{\cos(\pi k)}{|k|^{\beta_{3}}},\quad|S^{\prime}(k)|\,\approx\,\frac{1}{|k|^{1+\beta_{3}}}\,\,\text{ on }{\mathbb{T}}_{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\\
&S^{\prime}_{\ast}\,:=\,\lim_{k\to 0}|k|^{1+\beta_{3}}|S^{\prime}(k)|>0.\end{split}

From this point on, we shall assume that all the above hypotheses are in force.

2.2.
Solution of the kinetic equation.

Let us write

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=(0,+\infty)
,
\bar{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}:=[0,+\infty)
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{-}:=(-\infty,0)
and
\bar{{\mathbb{R}}}_{-}:=(-\infty,0]
. Given
T\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{T}
the class of functions
\phi
in
C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}_{*}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*})
that can be continuously extended to
\bar{{\mathbb{R}}}_{\iota}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*}
, for
\iota\in\{+,-\}
, and satisfy the following interface conditions:

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\phi(0^{+},k)\,=\,p_{+}(k)\phi(0^{-},k)+p_{-}(k)\phi(0^{+},-k)+p_{0}(k)T,&\mbox{ on }{\mathbb{T}}_{+};\\
\phi(0^{-},k)\,=\,p_{+}(k)\phi(0^{+},k)+p_{-}(k)\phi(0^{-},-k)+p_{0}(k)T,&\mbox{ on }{\mathbb{T}}_{-}.\end{cases}

Clearly, the constant function
T_{o}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{T_{o}}
. Furthermore,

F\in\mathcal{C}_{T_{o}}
if and only if
F-T_{o}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
.
This allows us to reduce the proofs of some results below to the case
T_{o}=0
. Following
[
19
]
, we now recall the definition of solution to equation (
1.1
) with the interface conditions (
1.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

A bounded, continuous function
W\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{*}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called a solution to equation (
1.1
) with the interface conditions (
1.2
) if all the following conditions are satisfied:

•

for any
\iota,\iota^{\prime}
in
\{-,+\}
, the restriction of
W
to
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\iota}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}
can be extended to a bounded, continuous function on

\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{\iota}\times\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}
;

•

for any
(t,y,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{*}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*}
fixed, the function
s\mapsto W(t+s,y+\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)s,k)
is continuously differentiable in a neighbourhood of
s=0
, the directional derivative

(2.5)

D_{t}W(t,y,k):=\frac{d}{ds}_{|s=0}W(t+s,y+\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)s,k)

is bounded in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{*}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*}
, and

D_{t}W(t,y,k)\,=\,\gamma L_{k}W(t,y,k),\quad(t,y,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{*}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*};

•

the interface conditions in (
1.2
) hold, together with the initial condition

\lim_{t\to 0+}W(t,y,k)\,=\,W_{0}(y,k),\quad(y,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{*}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*}.

We highlight that, at least formally, the directional derivative satisfies

D_{t}W(t,y,k)\,=\,\left[\partial_{t}+\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(k)\partial_{y}\right]W(t,y,k),

which justifies the notation in (
2.5
). It has been shown in
[
19
]
that there exists a unique (classical) solution, in the sense of Definition
2.1
, to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), if the initial distribution
W_{0}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{T_{o}}
. On this regard, see as well Proposition
3.1
below.

2.3.
Weak solution of the limit equation

Recalling the definitions of
p_{\pm}
in (
1.9
), we introduce the
bilinear form

(2.6)

\hat{\cal E}[u,v]\,:=\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u(y^{\prime})-u(y))(v(y^{\prime})-v(y))q_{\alpha}(y^{\prime}-y)\left(\mathds{1}_{yy^{\prime}>0}+p_{+}\mathds{1}_{yy^{\prime}<0}\right)dydy^{\prime}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u(-y^{\prime})-u(y))(v(-y^{\prime})-v(y))q_{\alpha}(y^{\prime}-y)p_{-}\mathds{1}_{yy^{\prime}<0}\,dydy^{\prime},

and the associated
quadratic form

\hat{\cal E}[u]:=\hat{\cal E}[u,u]
.
We can now define the semi-norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{o}}\,:=\,\hat{\cal E}^{1/2}[u]
for any Borel function
u\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that the expression is finite and then denote by
\mathcal{H}_{o}
the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
under
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{o}}
.
We proceed to the definition of a weak solution to (
1.17
) with the interface conditions (
1.2
).

Definition 2.2
.

A bounded function

\bar{W}\colon\bar{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a weak
solution to equation (
1.17
) with interface conditions (
1.2
)
if

i)

\bar{W}(\cdot)-T_{o}\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}_{o})
and
\bar{W}(\cdot)-T_{\infty}\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
for some
T_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

ii)

for any
F\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{*})
and any
t\geq 0
, it holds that

(2.7)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F(0,y)[\bar{W}_{0}(y)-T_{o}]\,dy=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F(t,y)[\bar{W}(t,y)-T_{o}]\,dy\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\partial_{s}F(s,y)[\bar{W}(s,y)-T_{o}]\,dyds+\bar{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{\cal E}[F(s,\cdot),\bar{W}(s,\cdot)-T_{o}]\,ds.

By Proposition
3.7
below, our definition
guarantees the uniqueness of solutions.

3.
Probabilistic representation of solutions

3.1.
Construction of the position/momentum processes

As usual,
{\mathbb{N}}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space carrying the following random objects. We
consider
a Markov chain
K_{n}(k)

reporting the consecutive frequencies of the phonon,
and the renewal process
{\mathfrak{T}}_{n}(k)
of the
scattering times of the phonon frequency (this is Fraktur font
T
).
More precisely,
\{K_{n}(k)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is such that
K_{0}(k)=k
and
\{K_{n}(k)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are i.i.d. random variables on
{\mathbb{T}}
, distributed
according to the following probability measure:

(3.1)

\mu(dk)\,:=\,R_{2}(k)dk,

and

(3.2)

{\mathfrak{T}}_{n}(k)\,:=\,\sum_{0\leq j<n}\bar{t}(K_{j}(k))\tau_{j},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

where
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is an independent sequence of i.i.d. exponentially distributed random variables with intensity
1
and, we recall,
\bar{t}(k)
was defined in (
2.1
).
We introduce the process
\{{\mathfrak{T}}(t,k)\}_{t\geq 0}
as the linear interpolation between the values of
{\mathfrak{T}}_{n}(k)
:

{\mathfrak{T}}(t,k)={\mathfrak{T}}_{n}(k)+(t-n)({\mathfrak{T}}_{n+1}(k)-{\mathfrak{T}}_{n}(k))\quad\mbox{if}\quad t\in[n,n+1),\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

We then define the continuous-time frequency (momentum) process as

K(t,k)\,:=\,K_{[{\mathfrak{T}}^{-1}(t,k)]}(k)=K_{n}(k)\quad\mbox{if}\quad t\in[{\mathfrak{T}}_{n}(k),{\mathfrak{T}}_{n+1}(k)),\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Here
{\mathfrak{T}}^{-1}
is the inverse function of
t\mapsto{\mathfrak{T}}(t,k)
and
[\cdot]
denotes the integer part.

According to (
2.2
), the phonon position at the time of the
n
-th scattering of its frequency is

Z_{n}(y,k)\,:=\,y-\sum_{0\leq j<n}S(K_{j})\tau_{j},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

In particular,
the law of
Z_{n}(y,k)
, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is absolutely continuous with respect to the
Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
We then consider an auxiliary Poisson process

\{N(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of intensity
1
that is independent of both
\{K_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. We define

\{\tilde{N}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
as the linear
interpolation between the nodal points of
N
. Namely, if
n:=N(t)
then

\tilde{N}(t):=n+(t-l)/(r-l),

where
l:=\inf\{s:N(s)=n\}
and
r:=\inf\{s:N(s)=n+1\}
.
Our next process,
\{\tilde{Z}(t,y,k)\}_{t\geq 0}
, is obtained by linearly interpolating between the nodal points of
Z_{N(t)}(y,k)
: if
n=N(t)
, then

\tilde{Z}(t,y,k):=Z_{n}(y,k)+(Z_{n+1}(y,k)-Z_{n}(y,k))(t-l)/(r-l).

We next define the transmission/reflection/absorption mechanism at
the interface
o=[y=0]
. To this end, we fix
(y,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{*}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{*}
and consider the times

\left\{n_{m}\left(\Big\{Z_{n}(y,k)\Big\}_{n\geq 0}\right)\right\}_{m\geq 0}

when
\Big\{Z_{n}(y,k)\Big\}_{n\geq 0}
crosses the interface. Namely, we let
n_{0}:=0
and then define recursively

(3.3)

n_{m+1}\,:=\,\inf\left\{n>n_{m}\colon(-1)^{m}yZ_{n}(y,k)<0\right\},\quad m=0,1,\ldots.

For the sake of brevity, when there is no danger of confusion, we skip the sequence
\Big\{Z_{n}(y,k)\Big\}_{n\geq 0}

from the notation.
Similarly, we define the sequence
\Big\{\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m}\Big(\{\tilde{Z}(t,y,k)\}_{t\geq 0}\Big)\Big\}_{m\geq 0}
of consecutive times when the process
\tilde{Z}(t,y,k)
crosses the interface
o
. Namely, we let
\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}=0
and

(3.4)

\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m+1}\,:=\,\inf\left\{t>\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m}\colon(-1)^{m}y\tilde{Z}(t,y,k)<0\right\},\quad m=0,1,\ldots.

Again, we simplify the notation by omitting the path of the process when
there is no danger of confusion. Sometimes, to highlight the
dependence of the crossing times on the starting point (which will be
relevant in the argument), we may write

\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m,y,k}
, or
\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m,y}
.

We define
{\cal S}(t,k):=\tilde{N}^{-1}\left({\mathfrak{T}}^{-1}(t,k)\right)
and
{\mathfrak{s}}_{m}:=\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m},k)
. Then,
n_{m}=N(\mathfrak{s}_{m})
and

\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m}\,<\,\mathfrak{s}_{m}\,<\,\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m+1},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

We now consider a sequence
\{\sigma_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
of

\{-1,0,1\}
-valued random variables, that are independent when
conditioned on
\{K_{n}(k)\}_{n\geq 0}
, such that
\sigma_{0}:=1
and

(3.5)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sigma_{m}=\iota|\{K_{n}(k)\}_{n\geq 0}\right)\,=\,p_{\iota}(K_{n_{m}-1}(k)),\quad\iota\in\{-1,0,1\},\,m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Here and below,
p_{\iota}
means
p_{\pm}
if
\iota=\pm 1
, respectively.
Of course,
\{\sigma_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
can be
defined
by applying the quantile functions for (
3.5
) to independent i.i.d. uniform random variables.
We can finally add the random interface mechanism to the processes considered. Namely,

(3.6)

\tilde{Z}^{o}(t,y,k)\,:=\,\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}\right)\tilde{Z}(t,y,k),\quad K^{o}(t,k)\,:=\,\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}\right)K(t,k),\quad t\in[\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m},\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{m+1}),

for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
The “true” interface position process is then given by

(3.7)

Y^{o}(t,y,k)\,:=\,\tilde{Z}^{o}({\cal S}(t,k),y,k)\,=\,y-\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\omega}^{\prime}(K^{o}(s,k))\,ds.

We now denote by
{\mathfrak{f}}:=\min\left\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\sigma_{m}=0\right\}
the interface crossing at which the particle gets absorbed and let
\mathfrak{s}_{{\mathfrak{f}}}:={\cal S}(\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{{\mathfrak{f}}},k)
. The following probabilistic representation of
the solution to (
1.1
), with interface conditions
(
1.2
), holds.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
W_{0}
be in
\mathcal{C}_{T_{o}}
such that
W_{0}(0,k)=T_{o}
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{\ast}
. Then, the function

(3.8)

W(t,y,k)\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[W_{0}\left(Y^{o}(t,y,k),K^{o}(t,k)\right)\right],\qquad(t,y,k)\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}[0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{\ast},

is the unique classical solution, in the sense of Definition
2.1
, to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with initial distribution
W_{0}
.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of a classic solution and the following representation

(3.9)

W(t,y,k)\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[W_{0}\left(Y^{o}(t,y,k),K^{o}(t,k)\right),\,t<\mathfrak{s}_{y,k,{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]+T_{o}\mathbb{P}\left[t\geq\mathfrak{s}_{y,k,{\mathfrak{f}}}\right],

have already been shown in

[
19
]
, Section
A
, and Proposition
3.2
. Recalling that
Y^{o}(t,y,k)=0
if
t\geq{\mathfrak{s}}_{y,k,{\mathfrak{f}}}
, we can use that
W_{0}(0,k)=T_{o}
to get that

T_{o}\mathbb{P}\left[t\geq\mathfrak{s}_{y,k,{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[W_{0}\left(0,K^{o}(t,k)\right),\,t\geq\mathfrak{s}_{y,k,{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[W_{0}\left(Y^{o}(t,y,k),K^{o}(t,k)\right),\,t\geq\mathfrak{s}_{y,k,{\mathfrak{f}}}\right].

Equation (
3.8
) then follows immediately from (
3.9
).
∎

Scaling of the process

Equations (

\displaystyle Z_{\lambda}(t,y,k)\,

\displaystyle:=\,Z_{N_{\lambda}(t)}^{\lambda}(y,k),

\{{\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}}_{m}^{\lambda}\}_{m\geq 0}

from the notation, when unambiguous. Since the law of

To set the notation for the processes subject to the random mechanism at the interface, let

The jump process

Z_{\lambda}^{o}(t,y,k)\,:=\,\left(\prod_{j=0}^{m}\sigma_{j}^{\lambda}\right)Z_{\lambda}(t,y,k),\quad t\in[\bar{\mathfrak{s}}^{\lambda}_{m},\bar{\mathfrak{s}}^{\lambda}_{m+1}).

Here

Similarly, the continuous trajectory process

In what follows, we show that it is unlikely that the scaled position

A^{\lambda}(y,k):=\left[yZ_{1}^{\lambda}(y,k)<0\right]

.

For all

,

The next

5.1.
Construction of the associated Markov process

Let us denote by

(see (

Recalling that the function

\mu_{S}(A)\,:=\,\int_{A}r(y)\,dy\,=\,\int_{S(k)\in A}R_{2}(k)\,dk,\quad A\in{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}}).

Since
S
is odd, its law

is even, and thus

is even.
Let us now define, for simplicity,

\tilde{p}_{\iota,\lambda}(z)\,:=\,p_{\iota}\left(S^{-1}(\lambda^{1/\alpha}z)\right),\quad\iota\in\{-1,0,1\}

for any
\lambda>0
and any

in
{\mathbb{R}}
, with the classical notation

\tilde{p}_{\iota}(z):=\tilde{p}_{\iota,1}(z)
. Clearly, all the above
functions are even. Thanks to (

) we may assume, with no
loss of generality, that

(5.2)

\tilde{p}_{0}\left(y\right)\succeq\frac{1}{(1+\log|y|)^{\kappa}},\quad|y|\geq 1.

From the general assumptions of the model (cf.
(
1.8
), (
1.11
), (
1.12
) and (

)), it is not difficult to verify, by direct calculations, the following properties:

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

(5.3)

r\left(y\right)\,\approx\,\frac{1}{(1+|y|)^{1+\alpha}},\qquad y\in{\mathbb{R}}

and

(5.4)

\lim_{y\to+\infty}r\left(y\right)y^{1+\alpha}\,=\,r_{*}\,:=\,\frac{R^{\ast}_{2}S_{\ast}^{1+\alpha}}{S^{\prime}_{\ast}}.

In addition,

(5.5)

\liminf_{y\to+\infty}\tilde{p}_{0}\left(y\right)\log^{\kappa}y=:\tilde{p}_{*}>0.

where

and

Consider, for any
y
in
{\mathbb{R}}
,

(5.6)

. Lemma

then implies that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\bar{r}(y)\,

\displaystyle\approx\,\frac{1}{(1+|y|)^{1+\alpha}}\quad\text{ on }\,\,{\mathbb{R}};

(5.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to+\infty}\bar{r}(y)y^{1+\alpha}\,

\displaystyle=\,\bar{r}_{*}\,=\,r_{*}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tau^{\alpha}e^{-\tau}\,d\tau;

(5.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\bar{r}_{\lambda}(y)\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\bar{r}_{*}}{|y|^{1+\alpha}},\quad\mbox{if }y\neq 0.

We now consider the Markov process
\left\{\hat{Z}_{\lambda}^{o}(t,y)\right\}_{t\geq 0}

where the jump and killing kernels are given by

\begin{split}\hat{r}_{\lambda}(y,y^{\prime})\,&:=\,\mathds{1}_{yy^{\prime}>0}\bar{r}_{\lambda}(y^{\prime}-y)+\mathds{1}_{yy^{\prime}<0}\left[\tilde{p}_{+,\lambda}(y^{\prime}-y)\bar{r}_{\lambda}(y^{\prime}-y)+\tilde{p}_{-,\lambda}(y^{\prime}+y)\bar{r}_{\lambda}(y^{\prime}+y)\right];\\

5.2.
Properties of the Markov semigroup corresponding to

In order to show Theorem

P_{t}^{o,\lambda}u(y)\,:=\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[u\left(\hat{Z}_{\lambda}^{o}(t,y)\right),\,t<\hat{\mathfrak{s}}_{y,{\mathfrak{f}}}^{\lambda}\right],\qquad u\in B_{b}({\mathbb{R}}).

\hat{P}_{t}^{o,\lambda}u(y)\,:=\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[u\left(\hat{Z}_{\lambda}^{o}(t,y)\right)\right]\,=\,P_{t}^{o,\lambda}u(y)+u(0){\mathbb{P}}\left(t\geq\hat{\mathfrak{s}}_{y,{\mathfrak{f}}}^{\lambda}\right).

Its Markov semigroup satisfies the following.

Proposition 5.3
.

For any

{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}
, the process

6.
Proof of Theorem
5.5

We are going to show here the strong
L^{2}
-convergence of the semigroups
P_{t}^{o,\lambda}
, defined in (
5.11
) and associated with the Markov processes
\hat{Z^{o}}_{\lambda}(t,y)
, to the semigroup

\{P_{t}^{o}\}_{t\geq 0}
, given in (
5.13
) and corresponding to
the process
\zeta^{o}(t,y)
. The main tool used in the proof is
the notion of the
\Gamma
-convergence of the Dirichlet forms
corresponding to the semigroups. Before going into the actual proof, we recall some basic facts on the subject.

6.1.
Basic notions on Sobolev spaces and Dirichlet forms

Given
\beta
in
(0,2)
, we define the following form:

(6.1)

{\cal E}[u]\,:=\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}[u(y^{\prime})-u(y)]^{2}q_{\beta}(y^{\prime}-y)\,dydy^{\prime}

for any Borel function
u\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. We admit the possibility
that
the right-hand side of (
6.1
) equals infinity.
Then,

H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\colon{\cal E}[u]<\infty\right\}
is a Hilbert space when endowed with the following norm

(6.2)

\|u\|_{H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}})}\,:=\,\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}+{\cal E}[u]\right)^{1/2}.

We have
H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})\subset C_{b}({\mathbb{R}})
when, as in our case,

\alpha>1
(see, e.g.,
[
14
]
, equation
(1.4.33)
) and thus
u(0)
is well defined. Moreover,
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
is dense in

H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})
and it is not difficult to check that the form

{\cal E}
is
regular
on
H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})
in the sense of
[
14
, Example 1.4.1]
.

Recalling that
{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}:={\mathbb{R}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, we also consider the
family
H_{0}^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
of functions given by the completion of

C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}})}
. When
\alpha>1
, the space

H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
can be equivalently characterised as the set of
functions
u
in
H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
u(0)=0
. For a proof of
this fact, see Corollary
6.4
below. It easily follows
that the form
{\cal E}
is regular on
H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
. By (
1.2
), we also notice that the form
\hat{\cal E}
, given in (
2.6
), is comparable with
{\cal E}
on the space
H^{\alpha/2}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
. In particular, it is regular and thus, it is a Dirichlet form on
H^{\alpha/2}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
.

To prove Proposition
5.5
, we will show that the corresponding Dirichlet forms, defined below,
converge in a suitable sense. More precisely (cf.
[
11
, Definition
4.1]
, or
[
24
, Section 1]
),

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
be a family of Dirichlet forms on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
endowed with their natural domains:

{\cal D}\left({\cal E}_{\lambda}\right)\,:=\,\left\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\colon{\cal E}_{\lambda}[u]<+\infty\right\}.

Then,
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
is called
\Gamma
-convergent to a Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
, as
\lambda\to+\infty
, if for any
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})

the following conditions are satisfied:

i)

for any family
\{u_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
weakly convergent to
u
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
,
it holds that

\liminf_{\lambda\to+\infty}{\cal E}_{\lambda}[u_{\lambda}]\,\geq\,{\cal E}_{\infty}[u]

ii)

there exists a family
\{v_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
strongly convergent to
u
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\limsup_{\lambda\to+\infty}{\cal E}_{\lambda}[v_{\lambda}]\,\leq\,{\cal E}_{\infty}[u].

The notion of
\Gamma
-convergence is particularly useful for our purposes since it naturally implies (cf.
[
24
, Corollary
2.6.1
]
) the strong convergence of the corresponding semigroups on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

We conclude this subsection presenting some properties of the Sobolev
spaces we have just introduced. The proofs of the results formulated
below can be found in Appendix
A
. Let us start with
the following variant of the Hardy-type inequality of Dyda
[
13
]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

For
\beta\not=1
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\beta}:=C(\beta)
such that

(6.3)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy\,\leq\,C_{\beta}\|u\|_{H^{\beta/2}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{\ast})}^{2},\quad\mbox{for all
}u\in H_{0}^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*}).

Moreover, if
1<\beta_{0}<\beta_{1}<2
, then
\sup_{\beta\in[\beta_{0},\beta_{1}]}C_{\beta}\,<\,+\infty
.

We then obtain a characterisation of
H_{0}^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
as a subspace of
H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Proposition 6.3
.

For
\beta\not=1
, we have

(6.4)

H_{0}^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})\,=\,\left\{u\in H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}})\colon\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|y|^{\beta}}dy<+\infty\right\}.

Finally, two other characterisations
of the space
H^{\alpha/2}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{\ast})
are proposed for
indices
\alpha>1
.

Corollary 6.4
.

For every
\alpha
in
(1,2)
, we have

(6.5)

\displaystyle H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})\,=\,H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap\bigcap_{1<\beta<\alpha}H_{0}^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*});

(6.6)

\displaystyle H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})\,=\,\left\{u\in H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})\colon u(0)=0\right\}.

6.2.
Properties of the associated Dirichlet forms

Let us consider the Dirichlet form corresponding to
P_{t}^{o,\lambda}
:

(6.7)

\hat{\cal E}_{\lambda}[u]\,:=\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\hat{r}_{\lambda}(y,y^{\prime})[u(y^{\prime})-u(y)]^{2}\,dy^{\prime}dy+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}k_{\lambda}(y)u^{2}(y)\,dy,

where the two kernels
\hat{r}_{\lambda}

were defined in (
5.10

that the process
\{\zeta^{0}(t,y)\}_{t\geq 0}
admits a Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}^{o}

(6.8)

\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]\,:=\,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left[u(y)-P_{t}^{o}u(y)\right]u(y)\,dy,

for any function
u
belonging to its natural domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{o}):=\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\colon\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]<\infty\}
. The main result of the present section is the following:

Theorem 6.5
.

The Dirichlet forms
\{\hat{\cal E}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
are
\Gamma
-convergent, as

{\cal E}^{o}

Before proving Theorem
6.5

\mathcal{E}^{o}
actually coincides with the Dirichlet form

, defined by (

with
\beta=\alpha

Proposition 6.6
.

For any
u\in H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{\ast})
, we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]\,

\displaystyle=\,\bar{r}_{\ast}\hat{\mathcal{E}}[u];

(6.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]\,

\displaystyle\approx\,\mathcal{E}[u].

Proof.

For notational simplicity, we start by denoting

(6.11)

\mathcal{E}^{o}_{t}[u]\,:=\,\frac{1}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}[u(y)-P_{t}^{o}u(y)]u(y)\,dy,

so that, in particular,
\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}^{o}_{t}[u]
. Using the symmetry of the semigroup

P^{o}_{t}
, we write

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{E}}[(u(y)-u(\zeta^{o}(t,y)))^{2},\,t<\mathfrak{t}_{y,\mathfrak{f}}]\,dy\,&=\,\frac{1}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}P_{t}^{o}u^{2}(y)\,dy-\frac{1}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u(y)P_{t}^{o}u(y)\,dy\\
&=\mathcal{E}^{o}_{t}[u]-\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u^{2}(y)\big(1-P_{t}^{o}1(y)\big)\,dy\right).\end{split}

We stress here that even though the constant function
1
is not in

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, it is still possible to use the symmetry of the operator

P_{t}^{o}
, arguing by approximation. Therefore,

(6.12)

\mathcal{E}^{o}_{t}[u]\,=\,\frac{1}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u^{2}(y)\mathbb{P}(t\geq\mathfrak{t}_{y,\mathfrak{f}})\,dy+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{E}}[(u(y)-u(\zeta^{o}(t,y)))^{2},\,t<\mathfrak{t}_{y,\mathfrak{f}}]\,dy.

It is then easy to verify that, for any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
,

(6.13)

\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]\,=\,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}^{o}_{t}[u]\,=\,\bar{r}_{\ast}\hat{\mathcal{E}}[u].

Fixed
y
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}
, let
\{\tilde{\zeta}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be the
symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy process starting at
y
but killed at
hitting
0
. Clearly, its transition semigroup
\{\tilde{P}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, given as in (
5.13
), is made of symmetric Markov contractions
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. In particular, its corresponding Dirichlet form equals:

\tilde{\cal E}[u]\,:=\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}[u(y^{\prime})-u(y)]^{2}q_{\alpha}(y^{\prime}-y)\,dydy^{\prime}\,=\,\mathcal{E}[u],

for any function
u
belonging to its natural domain

\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}})=H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
. For a proof
see, e.g.,
[
10
, Section 3.3.3]
.
We consider as well

\tilde{\cal E}_{t}[u]
defined as in (
6.11
) with
respect to
\tilde{P}_{t}
. The same calculations that lead to
(
6.12
) can be performed again to show that

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{t}[u]\,=\,\frac{1}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u^{2}(y)\mathbb{P}(t\geq\mathfrak{t}_{y,\mathfrak{f}})\,dy+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{E}}[(u(y)-u(\tilde{\zeta}(t,y)))^{2},\,t<\mathfrak{t}_{y,\mathfrak{f}}]\,dy.

Recalling the construction of the process
\zeta^{o}(t,y)
in (
3.18
), we now write that

(6.14)

\displaystyle t\mathcal{E}^{o}_{t}[u]\,=\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u^{2}(y)\mathbb{P}(t\geq\mathfrak{t}_{y,\mathfrak{f}})\,dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}\in\{\pm 1\}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}p_{\varepsilon_{j}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(u(y)-u\left(\zeta(t,y)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\right)\right)^{2},\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{y,m}\leq t<{\mathfrak{t}}_{y,m+1}\right]\,dy.

We then denote by
{\cal P}_{m}
the family of sets

\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}\}\subseteq\{-1,1\}^{m}
such that
\prod_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}=1

and by
{\cal P}_{m}^{c}
its complement. Let

s_{m}\,:=\,\sum_{\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}\}\in{\cal P}_{m}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}p_{\varepsilon_{j}}.

In particular,
s+1=p_{+}
.
By a direct calculation, it is possible to show the following:

Lemma 6.7
.

The sequence
\{s_{m}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
tends to
1/2
. Moreover, if
p_{+}>1/2
, then
\{s_{m}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is strictly decreasing, if
p_{+}=1/2
, then it holds that
s_{m}=1/2
for
any
m\geq 1
and if
p_{+}<1/2
, then
\{s_{2m-1}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
increases while
\{s_{2m}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
decreases.

From (
6.14
), we now have that

\displaystyle t\mathcal{E}^{o}_{t}[u]\,

\displaystyle\geq\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u^{2}(y)\mathbb{P}(t\geq\mathfrak{t}_{y,\mathfrak{f}})\,dy+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\geq 0}s_{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(u(y)-u(\zeta(t,y)\right)^{2},\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{y,m}\leq t<{\mathfrak{t}}_{y,m+1}\right]\,dy

(6.15)

\displaystyle\geq\,\left(p_{+}\wedge\frac{1}{2}\right)t\tilde{\cal E}_{t}(f).

If we suppose now that
u
belongs to
{\cal D}(\mathcal{E}^{o})
, the
closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{\ast})
with respect to the form

\mathcal{E}^{o}
, then (
6.15
) implies that
u
belongs to

\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}})=H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
, as well. On the other hand, if
u
is in
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
then we can use the Hardy inequality (
6.3
) in (
6.13
) to show that
\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]\preceq\tilde{\mathcal{E}}[u]
. Such an estimate then extends to
H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
and implies in
particular that
H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{o})
. We have thus proven that
H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{o})
and there exists a positive constant
C
such that

C^{-1}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}[u]\,\leq\,\mathcal{E}^{o}[u]\,\leq\,C\tilde{\mathcal{E}}[u],\quad u\in H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{\ast}),

which ends the proof of the proposition. ∎

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
6.5

In the present section, we are going to
show that the Dirichlet forms

\{\hat{\cal E}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
, defined in
(
6.7
), are
\Gamma
-convergent, when

\lambda
tends to
+\infty
, to the
Dirichlet form
\bar{r}_{\ast}\hat{\cal E}
given in
(
2.6
)-(
5.8
). Thanks to Proposition
6.6
, Theorem
6.5
will then follow immediately.

Recalling the meaning of the
\Gamma
-convergence in Definition
6.1
, condition ii) easily follows choosing the trivial family
\{v_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
of functions given by
v_{\lambda}=u
and using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, together with (
1.9
) and (
5.9
). We now focus on showing condition i). Let
\{u_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
be a family of functions weakly convergent to
u
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. We start by noticing that if
\liminf_{\lambda\to+\infty}\hat{\cal E}_{\lambda}[u_{\lambda}]=+\infty
,
then condition i) clearly holds. We can then suppose without loss of generality that

(6.16)

\liminf_{\lambda\to+\infty}\hat{\cal E}_{\lambda}[u_{\lambda}]\,<\,+\infty.

Let
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
(0,+\infty)
such that
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
and

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\hat{\cal E}_{n}[u_{n}]\,=\,\liminf_{\lambda\to+\infty}\hat{\cal E}_{\lambda}[u_{\lambda}],

where we denoted
u_{n}:=u_{\lambda_{n}}
,
\hat{\cal E}_{n}:=\hat{\cal E}_{\lambda_{n}}
. We will use the following lemma, whose proof is
presented in Section
6.4
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{n}[u_{n}]<\infty
. Then, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
that is a.s. convergent to
u
.

We claim that
u
is in
H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
. Indeed, by Lemma

6.8
above, there exists a sub-sequence of

\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, that for simplicity we denote again by the same symbol, that is a.s. convergent in
{\mathbb{R}}
.
From (
5.10
) and (
1.9
), we now notice that

\hat{r}_{\lambda_{n}}(y,y^{\prime})\,\geq\,\mathds{1}_{yy^{\prime}>0}\bar{r}_{\lambda_{n}}(y^{\prime}-y)+\mathds{1}_{yy^{\prime}<0}\tilde{p}_{+,\lambda_{n}}(y^{\prime}-y)\bar{r}_{\lambda_{n}}(y^{\prime}-y)\,\,\succeq\,\bar{r}_{\lambda_{n}}(y^{\prime}-y).

Fatou’s lemma and (
5.9
) then imply that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\hat{\cal E}_{n}[u_{n}]\,\geq\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\left[\hat{r}_{\lambda_{n}}(y,y^{\prime})\left(u_{n}(y^{\prime})-u_{n}(y)\right)^{2}\right]\,dy^{\prime}dy\,\succeq\,\mathcal{E}[u].

From (
6.16
), we now have that
u\in H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Using again (
6.16
), we notice that

(6.17)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u_{n}^{2}(y)k_{\lambda_{n}}(y)\,dy\,\preceq\,1,\quad n\geq N,

for some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assuming that
\lambda^{1/\alpha}_{n}|y|\geq 1
, we can use (
5.2
)-(
5.7
) to show that

(6.18)

k_{\lambda_{n}}(y)\,\succeq\,\lambda_{n}\int_{\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}|y|}^{+\infty}\frac{\left[1+\log(1+z)\right]^{-\kappa}}{\left(1+z\right)^{1+\alpha}}\,dz\,\succeq\,\frac{\left[1+\log(1+\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}|y|)\right]^{-\kappa}}{\left(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+|y|\right)^{\alpha}}.

The second case, where
\lambda^{1/\alpha}_{n}|y|<1
, is trivial, since then

k_{\lambda_{n}}(y)\,\succeq\,\lambda_{n}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\left[1+\log(1+z)\right]^{-\kappa}}{\left(1+z\right)^{1+\alpha}}\,dz\,\succeq\lambda_{n}.

Thus,
k_{\lambda_{n}}(y)
can also be controlled from below by the term
appearing on the utmost right hand side of (
6.18
). From
(
6.17
) and (
6.18
), it follows that

(6.19)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{\left(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+|y|\right)^{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{dy}{\left[1+\log(1+\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}|y|)\right]^{\kappa}}\,\,\preceq\,1,

for any
n\geq N
.
We shall use the following.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{n}[u_{n}]<\infty
and the inequality (
6.19
) holds. Then, there exists
\rho^{\prime}
in
(0,1)
such that

(6.20)

\sup_{n\geq 1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)dy}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+|y|)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,\,<\,+\infty,\qquad\rho\in(0,\rho^{\prime}).

The lemma is shown in Section
6.5
. We proceed with its
application to the proof of Theorem
6.5
.
Using Fatou’s lemma in (
6.20
), we conclude that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|y|^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,dy\,<\,+\infty,

for any
\rho\in(0,1)
. Thanks to Proposition
6.3
and Corollary
6.4
, we then infer that
u
is in

H^{\alpha/2}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
. Since
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a.s. convergent
to a function
u
in
H^{\alpha/2}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
, we can use Fatou’s lemma and (
5.9
) to write that:

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\hat{\cal E}_{n}[u_{n}]\,\geq\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\lim_{n\to+\infty}(u_{n}(y^{\prime})-u_{n}(y))^{2}\hat{r}_{\lambda_{n}}(y,y^{\prime})\,dydy^{\prime}\,=\,\bar{r}_{\ast}\hat{\cal E}[u],

and we have proven part i) of Definition
6.1
, which ends the
proof of Theorem
6.5
.

What remains to be done is to prove the auxiliary results presented above.
Before doing so, we however need the following result:

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{n}[u_{n}]<\infty
. Then,

\lim_{K\to+\infty}\int_{|\xi|\geq K}|\hat{u}_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,=\,0,

uniformly in
n
. Here we have denoted by
\hat{f}(\cdot)
the Fourier
transform of a function
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

Thanks to (
1.9
), we know that
p_{\ast}:=\inf_{k\in{\mathbb{T}}}p_{+}(k)\,>\,0
.
Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{n}[u_{n}]<\infty
, we then
conclude from (
6.7
) that

1\,\succeq\,\hat{\cal E}_{n}[u_{n}]\,\geq\,\frac{p_{*}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left[u_{n}(y^{\prime})-u_{n}(y)\right]^{2}\bar{r}_{\lambda_{n}}(y^{\prime}-y)\,dydy^{\prime},

where
\bar{r}_{\lambda}
is given by (
5.6
). The right-hand side is then of the same order of magnitude as

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\hat{u}_{n}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\sin^{2}\left(\pi\xi y\right)\bar{r}_{\lambda_{n}}\left(y\right)\,dy\Big)\,\preceq\,1.

Fixing
K>0
, it now follows that

(6.21)

\int_{|\xi|\geq K}\Psi_{n}(\xi)|\hat{u}_{\lambda_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,\preceq\,1,

where
\Psi_{n}:=\Psi_{\lambda_{n}}
was defined in (
5.24
). Thus,
for any
n
so large that
K\leq\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}
, the estimates (
6.21
), (
5.25
) and (
5.27
) imply that

K^{\alpha}\int_{\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}\geq|\xi|\geq K}|\hat{u}_{\lambda_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,\preceq\,K^{\alpha}\int_{\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}\geq|\xi|\geq K}|\hat{u}_{\lambda_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\lambda_{n}\theta_{*}\int_{\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}\leq|\xi|}|\hat{u}_{\lambda_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,\preceq\,1,

and the conclusion of Lemma
6.10
follows. ∎

6.4.
Proof of Lemma
6.8
.

Fix
\delta>0
and let us consider the function
\phi_{\delta}
given in (
5.36
).
We claim that the sequence
\{u_{n}\phi_{\delta}\}_{n\geq 1}
is strongly
compact in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Using Pego Criterion, see
[
26
, Theorem 3]
, it is enough to show that

(6.22)

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to+\infty}\int_{|y|>K}|u_{n}(y)\phi_{\delta}(y)|^{2}\,dy\,=\,0;

(6.23)

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to+\infty}\int_{|\xi|>K}|\widehat{u_{n}\phi_{\delta}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,=\,0,

uniformly in
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Clearly, (
6.22
) holds since the sequence is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. To show (
6.23
), we notice that

(6.24)

\int_{|\xi|>K}|\widehat{u_{n}\phi_{\delta}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,=\,\int_{|\xi|>K}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\widehat{u}_{n}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\phi}_{\delta}(\eta)\,d\eta\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,\leq\,I_{1}(K,L)+I_{2}(K,L),

where for any fixed
L>0
, we have

\begin{split}I_{1}(K,L)\,&:=\,2\int_{|\xi|>K}\left|\int_{|\eta|\leq L}\widehat{u}_{n}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\phi}_{\delta}(\eta)\,d\eta\right|^{2}\,d\xi,\\
I_{2}(K,L)\,&:=\,2\int_{|\xi|>K}\left|\int_{|\eta|>L}\widehat{u}_{n}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\phi}_{\delta}(\eta)\,d\eta\right|^{2}\,d\xi.\end{split}

The Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Lemma
6.10
then imply that for any fixed
L>0
,

(6.25)

\lim_{K\to+\infty}\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}I_{1}(K,L)\,\leq\,4L\|\phi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\lim_{K\to+\infty}\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\int_{|\xi|>K-L}\left|\widehat{u}_{n}(\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,=\,0.

On the other hand, recalling that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is uniformly
bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\hat{\phi}_{\delta}
belongs to the Schwartz
class, we conclude that for any
\varepsilon>0
it is possible to choose
L:=L(\varepsilon)
, sufficiently large, such that

(6.26)

I_{2}(K,L)\,\preceq\,2\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\left(\int_{|\eta|>L}|\widehat{\phi}_{\delta}(\eta)|d\eta\right)^{2}\,\preceq\,\varepsilon,

uniformly in
n
. From (
6.24
)-(
6.26
), we can
then conclude that (
6.23
) follows. From the compactness of
\left\{u_{n}\phi_{\delta}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
, we can now choose a subsequence that is a.e. convergent on
[-\delta,\delta]
. Using the Cantor diagonal argument, we then
find a subsequence of
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
that converges a.e. on

{\mathbb{R}}
. ∎

6.5.
Proof of Lemma
6.9
.

First observe that since the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, it is enough to show that for any
\rho
small enough, we have:

(6.27)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{\left(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+|y|\right)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,dy\,<+\infty.

We will actually prove an analogue of (
6.27
) with the
integral over
[0,1]
, as the argument in the case of
[-1,0]
is similar.
The proof is divided into three separate steps.

Step 1.
We start by claiming that

(6.28)

\int_{0}^{c_{n}(\gamma)}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)\,dy}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,\preceq\,c_{n}(\gamma/2),

for any
\lambda_{n}\geq e
, any
\rho
in
(0,1)
and any
\gamma>1
, where

(6.29)

c_{n}(\gamma)\,:=\,\exp\left\{-\log^{\gamma}_{2}\lambda_{n}\right\}

and
\log_{2}x:=\log\log x
,
x>1
.
Indeed, let us denote

I_{m}\,:=\,\left\{y\in[0,1]\colon e^{m-1}\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}\leq y<e^{m}\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}\right\},

for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
I_{0}:=\left\{y\in[0,1]\colon y<\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}\right\}
. Estimate (
6.19
) now implies that

(6.30)

\sum_{m=0}^{N_{1}}a_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{\left(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y\right)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,\preceq\,\sum_{m=0}^{N_{1}}\frac{1}{(1+m)^{\kappa}}\int_{I_{m}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{\left(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y\right)^{\alpha}}\,dy\,\preceq\,1,

where a positive integer
N_{1}
is such that

e^{N_{1}-1}\lambda^{-1/\alpha}_{n}\leq 1<e^{N_{1}}\lambda^{-1/\alpha}_{n}
and

a_{m}\,:=\,\frac{e^{\rho\alpha(N_{1}-m)}}{(1+m)^{\kappa}}.

It is not difficult to check now that the sequence

\{a_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}

is decreasing and thus, its minimum in
1\leq m\leq m_{*}(\gamma):=N_{1}-[\log^{\gamma}N_{1}]
equals
a_{m_{*}(\gamma)}
. Hence,
(
6.30
) implies that

a_{m_{*}(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{e^{m_{*}(\gamma)-N_{1}}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{\left(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y\right)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,dy\,\preceq\,1,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}

since

e^{m_{\ast}(\gamma)}\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}=e^{m_{\ast}(\gamma)-N_{1}}
. Estimate
(
6.28
) follows from an observation that
e^{m_{*}(\gamma)-N_{1}}\succeq c_{n}(\gamma)
and

\frac{1}{a_{m_{*}(\gamma)}}\,\preceq\,\frac{N_{1}^{k}}{e^{\rho\alpha[\log^{\gamma}N_{1}]}}\,\preceq\,\exp\left\{\kappa\log N_{1}-\rho\alpha\log^{\gamma}N_{1}\right\}\,\preceq\,c_{n}(\gamma/2).

Step 2.
We then show that for any
\lambda_{n}>1
, the function
u_{n}
can be decomposed as:

(6.31)

u_{n}(y)\,=\,u^{(1)}_{n}(y)+u_{n}^{(2)}(y),

for two functions
u^{(1)}_{n}
,
u^{(2)}_{n}
such that

(6.32)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}}|u^{(1)}_{n}(y+h)-u^{(1)}_{n}(y)|\,

\displaystyle\preceq\,h^{(\alpha-1)/2},\quad h>0,

(6.33)

\displaystyle\|u^{(2)}_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\,

\displaystyle\preceq\,1/\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}.

We start by defining

u^{(1)}_{n}(y)\,:=\,\int_{|\xi|\leq\lambda^{1/\alpha}}\exp\left\{2\pi i\xi y\right\}\hat{u}_{n}(\xi)\,d\xi,\qquad u^{(2)}_{n}(y)\,:=\,\int_{|\xi|>\lambda^{1/\alpha}}\exp\left\{2\pi i\xi y\right\}\hat{u}_{n}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Clearly, (
6.31
) then holds.
We now use again the function
\Psi_{n}
defined in (
5.24
). Fixed

h>0
, we apply (
5.27
) to write that

|u^{(1)}_{n}(y+h)-u^{(1)}_{n}(y)|\,\preceq\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{\ast}}}\int_{|\xi|\leq\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}}\Psi_{n}^{1/2}(\xi)\left|\hat{u}_{\lambda}(\xi)\right|\frac{\left|1-\exp\left\{2\pi i\xi h\right\}\right|}{|\xi|^{\alpha/2}}\,d\xi.

Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and then (
6.21
), we
conclude that estimate (
6.32
) holds:

|u^{(1)}_{n}(y+h)-u^{(1)}_{n}(y)|\,\preceq\,\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\left|1-\exp\left\{2\pi i\xi\right\}\right|^{2}}{|\xi|^{\alpha}}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}h^{(\alpha-1)/2}\,\preceq\,h^{(\alpha-1)/2}.

On the other hand, estimate (
6.33
) follows from
(
5.25
) and (
6.21
):

\|u^{(2)}_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{\theta_{\ast}\lambda_{n}}\int_{|\xi|>\lambda_{n}^{1/\alpha}}\Psi_{n}(\xi)\left|\hat{u}_{\lambda}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,\preceq\,\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}.

Step 3.
Fix
n
large enough so that
\lambda_{n}\geq e
and
\gamma>1
, we consider

\tilde{I}_{m}\,:=\,\{y\in[0,1]\colon mc_{n}(\gamma)\leq y<(m+1)c_{n}(\gamma)\},

for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. We can then write

(6.34)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,dy\,\leq\,\sum_{m=0}^{N_{2}}\int_{\tilde{I}_{m}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,dy\,\preceq\,I_{n}+I\!I_{n}

where
N_{2}
is the (unique) positive integer such that
N_{2}c_{n}(\gamma)\leq 1<(N_{2}+1)c_{n}(\gamma)
and

\begin{split}I_{n}\,:=\,\sum_{m=0}^{N_{2}}\int_{0}^{c_{n}(\gamma)}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(y)}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+mc_{n}(\gamma)+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,dy;\\
I\!I_{n}\,:=\,\sum_{m=1}^{N_{2}}\int_{0}^{c_{n}(\gamma)}\frac{[u_{n}(y+mc_{n}(\gamma))-u_{n}(y)]^{2}}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+mc_{n}(\gamma)+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,dy.\end{split}

To control the first term
I_{n}
, we start by noticing that if
|y|\leq c_{n}(\gamma)
, then
\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y\leq 2c_{n}(\gamma)
for sufficiently large
n
. We can then write:

\begin{split}\sum_{m=0}^{N_{2}}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+mc_{n}(\gamma)+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,\preceq\,\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}+\frac{1}{c_{n}^{\alpha(1-\rho)}(\gamma)}\,\preceq\,\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{-1/\alpha}+y)^{\alpha(1-\rho)}},\end{split}

provided that
\rho
is so small that
\alpha(1-\rho)>1
.
From (
6.28
) we thus have that

I_{n}\preceq c_{n}(\gamma/2)\preceq 1
, uniformly in
n
. Concerning the second term
I\!I_{n}
, we use (
6.31
) to decompose it as
I\!I^{(1)}_{n}+I\!I^{(2)}_{n}
where for any
j=1,2
,
I\!I^{(j)}_{n}
is obtained from
I\!I_{n}
by replacing there
u_{n}
with
u^{(j)}_{n}
. Noticing that
N_{2}=\left[c^{-1}_{n}(\gamma)\right]
,
Estimate (
6.32
) now implies that

(6.35)

\displaystyle I\!I^{(1)}_{n}\,\preceq\,\sum_{m=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{c_{n}(\gamma)(mc_{n}(\gamma))^{\alpha-1}}{(mc_{n}(\gamma))^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,\preceq\,c_{n}^{\alpha\rho}(\gamma)\sum_{m=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{1}{m^{1-\rho\alpha}}\,\preceq\,(c_{n}(\gamma)N_{2})^{\alpha\rho}\,\preceq\,1,

To control
I\!I^{(2)}_{n}
, we recall that

\lambda_{n}
dominates

c_{n}^{-\alpha(1-\rho)}(\gamma)=\exp\left\{\alpha(1-\rho)\log_{2}^{\gamma}\lambda_{n}\right\}
(cf. (
6.29
))
for
n
sufficiently large,
and then,
thanks to (
6.33
), we write

\begin{split}I\!I^{(2)}_{n}\,&\preceq\,\|u^{(2)}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{1}{(mc_{n}(\gamma))^{\alpha(1-\rho)}}\,\preceq\,\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}c_{n}^{\alpha(1-\rho)}(\gamma)}\,\preceq\,1.\end{split}

This ends the proof of (
6.27
). ∎

A.2.
Proof of Proposition
6.3

For simplicity, let us denote the set on the right hand side of
(
6.4
) by
{\cal H}
. We start by noting that Hardy inequality (
6.3
) immediately implies that
H_{0}^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})\subset{\cal H}
. In order to show the other
inclusion, let us take
u\in\mathcal{H}
. For any
\delta>0
, let us
consider again the function
\phi_{\delta}
, as considered in
(
5.36
), and define

\psi_{\delta}=1-\phi_{\delta}
. Without any loss of generality, we can also assume that
\|\psi_{\delta}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 2/\delta
. Let
u_{\delta}:=u\psi_{\delta}
. It is easy to check that
u_{\delta}
is in
\mathcal{H}
and that it can be approximated by elements of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
in
H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}})
-norm.
To conclude, it is then enough to show that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|v_{\delta}\|_{H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}})}=0
where we denoted
v_{\delta}:=u_{\delta}-u
. Clearly,
\|v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
as
\delta
goes to
0
, by the dominated convergence theorem. On the other hand,
\mathcal{E}[v_{\delta}]
can be decomposed as

c_{\beta}\iint_{yy^{\prime}<0}\frac{[v_{\delta}(y^{\prime})-v_{\delta}(y)]^{2}}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}+c_{\beta}\iint_{yy^{\prime}>0}\frac{[v_{\delta}(y^{\prime})-v_{\delta}(y)]^{2}}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}\,=:\,I_{\delta}+I\!I_{\delta}.

To control the first term
I_{\delta}
, we notice that for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta:=\delta(\epsilon)>0
sufficiently small such that

(A.1)

I_{\delta}\,\preceq\,\iint_{yy^{\prime}<0}\frac{[v^{2}_{\delta}(y^{\prime})+v^{2}_{\delta}(y)]}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}dydy^{\prime}\,\preceq\,\iint_{yy^{\prime}<0}\frac{v^{2}_{\delta}(y)}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}\,\preceq\,\int_{-2\delta}^{2\delta}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy\,<\,\varepsilon.

To deal with the second term
I\!I_{\delta}
, we start by splitting it as

\iint_{yy^{\prime}>0,\,|y-y^{\prime}|\geq 4\delta}\frac{[v_{\delta}(y^{\prime})-v_{\delta}(y)]^{2}}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}+\iint_{yy^{\prime}>0,\,|y-y^{\prime}|<4\delta}\frac{[v_{\delta}(y^{\prime})-v_{\delta}(y)]^{2}}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}\,=:\,I\!I_{\delta}^{1}+I\!I_{\delta}^{2}.

Recalling that
v_{\delta}(y)=0
if
|y|\geq 2\delta
, we denote
A=\{yy^{\prime}>0,|y-y^{\prime}|\geq 4\delta,|y|\leq 2\delta\}
so that

(A.2)

I\!I_{\delta}^{1}\,\preceq\,\iint_{A}\frac{v^{2}_{\delta}(y)}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}\,\preceq\,\int_{|y|\leq 2\delta}\frac{v^{2}_{\delta}(y)}{(4\delta)^{\beta}}\,dy\,\preceq\,\int_{|y|\leq 2\delta}\frac{v^{2}_{\delta}(y)}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy\,<\,\varepsilon,

provided that
\delta:=\delta(\epsilon)>0
is sufficiently small. Similarly, we also have that

I\!I_{\delta}^{2}\,\leq\,\iint_{yy^{\prime}>0,\,|y|\vee|y^{\prime}|<10\delta}\frac{[v_{\delta}(y^{\prime})-v_{\delta}(y)]^{2}}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy\,\preceq\,I\!I_{\delta}^{2,1}+I\!I_{\delta}^{2,2}+I\!I_{\delta}^{2,3},

where we have denoted

\begin{split}I\!I_{\delta}^{2,1}\,&:=\,\iint_{|y|\vee|y^{\prime}|<6\delta}\frac{[\psi_{\delta}(y^{\prime})-\psi_{\delta}(y)]^{2}u^{2}(y^{\prime})}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}\\
I\!I_{\delta}^{2,2}\,&:=\,\iint_{|y|\vee|y^{\prime}|<6\delta}\frac{[u(y^{\prime})-u(y)]^{2}\psi_{\delta}^{2}(y)}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime},\\
I\!I_{\delta}^{2,3}\,&:=\,\iint_{|y|\vee|y^{\prime}|<6\delta}\frac{[u(y^{\prime})-u(y)]^{2}}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}.\end{split}

Recalling that
u\in\mathcal{H}
, it follows that for any sufficiently small
\delta>0
,

(A.3)

I\!I_{\delta}^{2,2}+I\!I_{\delta}^{2,3}\,<\,\varepsilon.

Since we have chosen
\psi_{\delta}
such that
\|\psi_{\delta}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 2/\delta
, we have that

(A.4)

I\!I_{\delta}^{2,1}\,\preceq\,\delta^{-\beta}\int_{|y^{\prime}|<6\delta}u^{2}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}\,\preceq\,\int_{|y^{\prime}|<6\delta}\frac{u^{2}(y^{\prime})}{|y^{\prime}|^{\beta}}\,dy^{\prime}\,<\,\varepsilon

for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Summarising, estimates
(
A.1
) –
(
A.4
) imply that
\mathcal{E}[v_{\delta}]<\epsilon
for any
\delta
small enough, and the conclusion of the proof then follows.
∎

A.3.
Proof of Corollary
6.4

First, we show (
6.5
). For simplicity, let us denote the
set appearing on its right-hand side by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Then, the
inclusion
H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})\subset\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
trivially follows
once we show that

(A.5)

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{\beta/2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\left(1+\frac{8}{\beta}\right)\|u\|^{2}_{H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})},

for any
\beta<\alpha
in
(0,2)
and
u
in
H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})
. If

u\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
, then the above inequality immediately follows from

\iint_{|y-y^{\prime}|\geq 1}\frac{[u(y^{\prime})-u(y)]^{2}}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}\,\leq\,2\iint_{|y-y^{\prime}|\geq 1}\frac{u^{2}(y^{\prime})+u^{2}(y)}{|y^{\prime}-y|^{1+\beta}}\,dydy^{\prime}\,\leq\,\frac{8}{\beta}\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}.

The general conclusion can be then obtained by an approximation
argument. To show the other inclusion, let us take now a function
u

in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Proposition
6.2
and estimate
(
A.5
) then imply that for any
(1+\alpha)/2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy\,\leq\,\left(\sup_{(1+\alpha)/2\leq\beta\leq\alpha}C_{\beta}\right)\left(1+\frac{8}{\beta}\right)\|u\|_{H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}.

Thanks to Fatou’s lemma and Proposition
6.3
, it
follows that
u\in H_{0}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{*})
. We have thus showed (
6.5
).
To prove (
6.6
), let us suppose that
u\in H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfies
u(0)=0
. From (
6.5
) and Proposition
6.3
, it suffices to show that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy\,<\,+\infty,

for any
\beta\in(1,\alpha)
. Since
\alpha>1
, we recall from

[
25
, Theorem 1, p. 394]
that
u
possesses an Hölder continuous representative
and that in particular,

\sup_{y\neq y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{|u(y)-u(y^{\prime})|}{|y-y^{\prime}|^{(\alpha-1)/2}}\,\leq\,C\|u\|_{H^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

It then follows that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy\,=\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{(u(y)-u(0))^{2}}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy\,\preceq\,\int_{|y|\leq 1}\frac{|y|^{\alpha-1}}{|y|^{\beta}}\,dy+\int_{|y|\geq 1}u^{2}(y)\,dy\,<\,+\infty,

and the proof of the corollary is concluded.
∎

Appendix B
Proof of Proposition

B.1.
Representation of a weak solution.

We will assume without loss of generality that
T_{\infty}=0
. We also
suppose for the moment that
T_{o}=0
. Then Proposition

3.7
can be obtained form the observation that

\bar{W}(t,y)\,=\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bar{W}_{0}(\eta^{o}(t,y))\right]\,=\,P^{o}_{\bar{\theta}t}\bar{W}_{0}(y),

is the semigroup solution to Cauchy problem (
1.17
), where

P^{o}_{t}
and
\bar{\theta}
have been defined in (
5.13
) and (
3.11
), respectively.

If
T_{o}\neq 0
, let us consider again the smooth function
\phi_{M}
given in (
5.36
). Since
\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}\phi_{M}
is in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap\mathcal{H}_{o}
and
\big(\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}\phi_{M}\big)(0)=0
, we already know that

\bar{W}_{M}(t,y)\,:=\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}\phi_{M}\right)(\eta^{o}(t,y))\right]\,=\,P^{o}_{\bar{\theta}t}\left(\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}\phi_{M}\right)(y),

is a weak solution of (
1.17
) for
T_{o}=0
. Thus,
\bar{W}_{M}\in C\left([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\right)\cap L^{2}_{\rm loc}\left([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}_{o}\right)
and

(B.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F(0,y)[\bar{W}_{0}(y)-T_{o}\phi_{M}(y)]\,dy\,=\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F(t,y)[\bar{W}_{M}(t,y)]\,dy\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\partial_{s}F(s,y)[\bar{W}_{M}(s,y)]\,dyds+\bar{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{\cal E}[F(s,\cdot),\bar{W}_{M}(s,\cdot)]\,ds.

It is not difficult to check now that
\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}\phi_{M}\to\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}
, as
M\to+\infty
, both in
\mathcal{H}_{o}
and pointwise. Recalling that
P^{o}_{t}
is the Markov semigroup associated with the form
\hat{\mathcal{E}}
, we have that

\hat{\mathcal{E}}\left[\bar{W}_{M}(t)\right]\,=\,\hat{\mathcal{E}}\left[P^{o}_{\bar{\theta}t}\left(\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}\phi_{M}\right)\right]\,\leq\,\hat{\mathcal{E}}\left[\bar{W}_{0}-T_{o}\phi_{M}\right].

It then follows that
\bar{W}_{M}\to\bar{W}-T_{o}
in
\mathcal{H}_{o}
and
\bar{W}-T_{o}
is in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(0,+\infty,\mathcal{H}_{o})
. Finally, we can follow the same arguments in the proof of Lemma
5.6
to show that

\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(y)\bar{W}_{M}(t,y)\,dy\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(y)\left[\bar{W}(t,y)-T_{o}\right]\,dy,

for any
F
in
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
. Hence, we can pass to the limit in
(
B.1
) and we conclude that
\bar{W}(t,y)
given by
(
3.21
) is a weak solution in the sense of
Definition
2.2
.

B.2.
Uniqueness of a weak solution.

For notational simplicity, let us denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{o}}
the scalar product on
\mathcal{H}_{o}
. Let
\bar{W}_{1}
,
\bar{W}_{2}
be two weak solutions (in the sense of Definition
2.2
) of Cauchy problem (
1.17
). By definition, we know that for each
j=1,2
, there exists
T^{j}_{\infty}
such that
\bar{W}_{j}-T^{j}_{\infty}\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
and
\bar{W}_{1}(0)=\bar{W}_{2}(0)=\bar{W}_{0}
. It then follows immediately that
T^{1}_{\infty}=T^{2}_{\infty}
. This in turn implies that

\bar{W}\,:=\,\bar{W}_{1}-\bar{W}_{2}\,\in\,C\left([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\right)\cap L^{2}_{\rm loc}\left([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}_{o}\right).

Assuming for the moment that
\bar{W}
belongs to
C^{1}\left([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}_{o}\right)
, it is easy to check that

(B.2)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{W}(s)\,ds\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\,=\,2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bar{W}(s),\int_{0}^{s}\bar{W}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{o}}\,ds,\quad t\geq 0.

In the more general case where
\bar{W}
is in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}\left([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}_{o}\right)

B.2
) follows by a mollification argument in time. Recalling that
\bar{W}
satisfies (
2.7

\bar{W}_{0}=T_{o}=0

(B.3)

\int_{0}^{t}\langle F,\bar{W}(s)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{o}}\,ds\,=\,-\langle F,\bar{W}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

In particular, from (
B.2
) and (
B.3
) with

, we finally get that

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{W}(s)\,ds\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{o}}\,=\,-2\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{W}(s)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\,ds,

which implies that

.
∎

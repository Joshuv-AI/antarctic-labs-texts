Arithmetic level raising on triple product of Shimura curves and Gross-Schoen Diagonal cycles I: Ramified case
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this article we study the Gross-Schoen diagonal cycle on a triple product of Shimura curves at a place of bad reduction. We relate the image of the diagonal cycle under the Abel-Jacobi map to certain period integral that governs the central critical value of the Garrett-Rankin type triple product L L -function via level raising congruences. As an application we prove certain rank 0 0 case of the Bloch-Kato conjecture for the symmetric cube motive of a weight 2 2 modular form.

1.
Introduction

Let
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
be a triple of normalized newforms in
S^{\mathrm{new}}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))^{3}
of level
N\geq 5
. We assume that
N=N^{+}N^{-}
is a factorization of
N
such that
(N^{+},N^{-})=1
and
N^{-}
is square-free with odd number of prime factors. We fix a prime

which will be used as the residual characteristic of the coefficient rings throughout this article. For each
i=1,2,3
, let
\mathrm{V}_{i}=\mathrm{V}_{f_{i},\lambda_{i}}
be the
\lambda_{i}
-adic Galois representation attached
f_{i}
given by Eichler-Shimura construction where
\lambda_{i}
is a place over
l
in the Hecke field
\mathbf{Q}(f_{i})
of
f_{i}
. Then we can attach a
Garret-Rankin type triple product
L
-function

L(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3},s)

to the triple product Galois representation
\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}
. The parity of the order of vanishing of
L(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3},s)
at the central critical point
s=2
is controlled by the
the
global root number

\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}
which can be factored into
\epsilon=\prod_{v\mid N\infty}\epsilon_{v}
of local root numbers
\epsilon_{v}\in\{\pm 1\}
. In our setting,
\epsilon_{\infty}=-1
since the weights
(2,2,2)
of
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
are
balanced
. In this article, we will assume the following assumption.

(
\epsilon=1
)

The product of local root numbers
\prod_{v\mid N}\epsilon_{v}=-1
and therefore
\epsilon=1
.

One can also attach a more arithmetic object to the triple tensor product Galois representation
\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}
called the Bloch-Kato Selmer group. We remind its definition below. Denote by
\mathrm{B_{cris}}
the crystalline period ring with respect to
\mathbf{Q}_{l}
. The
triple product Bloch-Kato Selmer group

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}(-1))

is the subset of classes
s\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}(-1))
such that

{\rm{loc}}_{l}(s)\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q}_{l},\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}(-1))

where
\mathrm{loc}_{l}
is the localization map to the local Galois cohomology group at
l
and

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q}_{l},\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}(-1))

is given by the Bloch-Kato local condition

\ker[\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{l},\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}(-1))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q}_{l},(\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3})\otimes\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}(-1))].

The
Bloch-Kato conjecture
predicts a relationship between the order of the vanishing of the triple product
L
-function
L(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3},s)
at
s=2
and the rank of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}(-1))
. The present article will be concerned with the
rank 0 case
of the Bloch-Kato conjecture for
\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}
. More precisely, we are concerned with the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

Suppose that the central critical value
L(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3},2)
is non-zero, then we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}(-1))=0.

One of the most successful approaches to prove such conjectures is via the
Euler-Kolyvagin system
arguments. In practice, one need to impose various assumptions on the triple
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
to apply the Euler system argument. We will formulate a more precise conjecture with a few additional assumptions in the last section of this article, see Conjecture
5.4
. This article makes the first step towards the construction of such an Euler-Kolyvagin system for the triple product representation. More precisely, the main result of this article concerns an explicit reciprocity formula á la Bertonilli-Darmon for the representation
\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}
. Such formula, loosely speaking, relates cycles classes on Shimura varieties to period integrals that govern the central critical values of automorphic
L
-functions via level raising congruences among automorphic forms. We refer the readers to the recent article
[
LTXZZ
]
for the most general results along this line. In this article, we relate the
Gross-Schoen diagonal cycle class
on the triple product of Shimura curves at a place of bad reduction to a period integral that represents the algebraic part of the triple product
L
-function. Note that our results are not subsumed by
[
LTXZZ
]
. In the companion article
[
Wang
]
, we will study the Gross-Schoen diagonal cycle class on the triple product of Shimura curves at a place of good reduction and so-called second reciprocity law is proved there. In the case when
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f,f,f)
for a single modular form
f
of weight two, our constructions here and in
[
Wang
]
produce the desired Euler-Kolyvagin system for the symmetric cube representation of
f
and we can give some evidences to the Bloch-Kato conjecture for the symmetric cube representation of
f
in the rank
0
and rank
1
case.

Moreover these three classes should satisfy a similar reciprocity law as the one we obtained for

1.2.
The symmetric cube motive

It is natural to consider the degenerate case when
f_{1}=f_{2}=f_{3}=f
for a single modular form

\mathrm{V}_{f}

Correspondingly, we have a factorization of the

We introduce in this case the notion of

(n,1)
-admissible primes
for

(n,1)
-admissible prime for
f

(1)

p\nmid Nl
;

(2)

;

(3)

\varpi^{n}\mid p+1-a_{p}(f)
.

Comparing to the definition of an
n
-admissible prime for a triple
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
, we require here that
\epsilon_{p,i}=1
for all
i=1,2,3
. We let
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f,f,f)
. Let
\mathrm{M}^{\diamond}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1)
be the symmetric cube component of
\mathrm{M}^{[p]}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1)
. Then we find that the singular quotient
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{M}^{\diamond}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))
at
p
is in fact of rank
1
. Therefore the Euler system argument can be applied here. However, the factorization of the
L
-function and the Galois representation suggest that the results concerning the rank
0
case of the Bloch-Kato conjecture here will be conditional on the non-vanishing of
L(f,1)
. Therefore we have the following result.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that the modular form
f
satisfies the following assumptions:

(1)

the maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{f}
are all residually irreducible;

(2)

the
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}
-module
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}
is free of rank
1
;

(3)

the residual Galois representation
\bar{\rho}_{f}
is surjective;

(4)

the value
L(f,1)
is non-vanishing.

Assume that value
L(\mathrm{Sym}^{3}f,2)
is non-zero. Then the Bloch-Kato Selmer group for
\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\mathrm{V}_{f}(-1)

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\mathrm{V}_{f}(-1))=0.

This theorem is proved in the last section of this article and is based on a familiar Euler system argument, see
[
Gro
]
,
[
BD3
]
,
[
Liu1
]
and
[
Liu2
]
for a few examples. A similar result in the rank
1
case for the Bloch-Kato Selmer group of
\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\mathrm{V}_{f}(-1)
will be proved in
[
Wang
]
.

1.3.
Bipartite Euler system and other applications

We close this introduction by discussing some applications and questions the present work points to.
In the companion work
[
Wang
]
, we prove the
second reciprocity law
in our setting which should have applications to the rank
1
case of the Bloch-Kato conjecture for the triple product motive of modular forms. It also implies that the diagonal cycle classes
\Theta^{[p]}_{n}
and the period integrals
I(f^{B}_{1},f^{B}_{2},f^{B}_{3})
form an analogue of the Bipartite Euler system in the sense of Howard
[
How
]
. Note that the definition there requires the only Frobenius eigenvalues on the local Galois representation at an
n
-admissible prime
p
are
p
and
1
and therefore the singular quotient is of rank
1
. This is obviously different from our setting. Therefore what we have for the triple product motive is only an analogue of his Bipartite Euler system. On the other hand, the classes
\Theta^{\diamond[p]}_{n}
we produced for the symmetric cube motive of modular forms do form a Bipartite Euler system in his sense.

Secondly, by replacing the
l
-adic étale cohomology and
l
-adic Abel-Jacobi map in the present work with crystalline cohomology and the
p
-adic Abel-Jacobi map one should be able to prove certain type of
p
-adic Gross-Zagier formulas for certain triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions. See
[
Hsi
]
for the relevant construction of
p
-adic
L
-functions and see
[
BD2
]
for the type of
p
-adic Gross-Zagier formula for Heegner points on Shimura curves.

1.4.
Notations and conventions

We will use common notations and conventions in algebraic number theory and algebraic geometry. The cohomologies of schemes appear in this article will be understood as computed over the étale sites.

For a field
K
, we denote by
K^{\mathrm{ac}}
a separable closure of
K
and put
G_{K}:=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{ac}/K)
the Galois group of
K
. We let
\mathbf{A}
be the ring of adèles over
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\infty}
be the subring of finite adèles. For a prime
p
,
\mathbf{A}^{\infty,(p)}
is the prime-to-
p
part of
\mathbf{A}^{\infty}
.

Let
K
be a local field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and residue field
k
. We let
I_{K}
be the inertia subgroup of
G_{K}
. Suppose
\mathrm{M}
is a
G_{K}
-module. Then the finite part
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K,\mathrm{M})
of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\mathrm{M})
is defined to be
H^{1}(k,\mathrm{M}^{I_{K}})
and the singular part
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(K,\mathrm{M})
of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\mathrm{M})
is defined to be the quotient of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\mathrm{M})
by the image of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K,\mathrm{M})
.

Two quaternion algebras will be used in this article: the definite quaternion algebra
B
with discriminant
N^{-}
and the indefinite quaternion algebra
B^{\prime}
with discriminant
pN^{-}
.

2.2.
Monodromy filtration and spectral sequence

Suppose
A
is an object in an abelian category and
N
is a nilpotent endomorphism on
A
. We define two filtrations on
A
.

•

We define the kernel filtration
F_{\bullet}
by putting
F_{p}A=\ker(N^{p+1}:A\rightarrow A)
for
p\geq 0
.

•

We define the image filtration
G^{\bullet}
by putting
G^{q}A=\mathrm{im}(N^{q}:A\rightarrow A)
for
q>0
.

Using these two filtrations,

•

we define the convolution filtration
M_{\bullet}A
by
M_{r}A=\oplus_{p-q=r}F_{p}A\cap G^{q}A
.

To calculate the graded piece of the convolution filtration, we define the induced
G^{\bullet}
-filtration on the graded piece of
F_{\bullet}
by
G^{q}Gr^{F}_{p}A=\mathrm{im}(G^{q}A\cap F_{p}A\rightarrow Gr^{F}_{p}A)
. Then we see that the
r
-th graded piece of the convolution filtration
M_{\bullet}A
is given by

Gr^{M}_{r}A\cong\bigoplus_{p-q=r}Gr^{q}_{G}Gr^{F}_{p}A.

The convolution filtration in this case is known as the
Monodromy filtration
and it is characterized by

(1)

M_{n}A=0
and
M_{-n-1}A=0
.

(2)

N:A\rightarrow A
sends
M_{r}A
into
M_{r-2}A
for
r\in\mathbf{Z}
.

(3)

N^{r}:Gr^{M}_{r}A\rightarrow Gr^{M}_{-r}A
is an isomoprhism.

Let
A=R\Psi(\Lambda)
. Let
T
be an element in
I_{K}
such that
t_{l}(T)
is a generator of
\Lambda(1)
then
T
induces a nilpotent operator
T-1
on
R\Psi(\Lambda)
. Let
N=(T-1)\otimes\breve{T}
where
\breve{T}\in\Lambda(-1)
be the dual of
t_{l}(T)
. Then with respect to
N
, we have the following characterization of the Monodromy filtration on
R\Psi(\Lambda)

(1)

M_{n}R\Psi(\Lambda)=0
and
M_{-n-1}R\Psi(\Lambda)=0
.

(2)

N:R\Psi(\Lambda)(1)\rightarrow R\Psi(\Lambda)
sends
M_{r}R\Psi(\Lambda)(1)
into
M_{r-2}R\Psi(\Lambda)
for
r\in\mathbf{Z}
.

(3)

N^{r}:Gr^{M}_{r}R\Psi(\Lambda)(r)\rightarrow Gr^{M}_{-r}R\Psi(\Lambda)
is an isomoprhism.

We can use Proposition
2.1
to calculate the Monodromy filtration on
R\Psi(\Lambda)
. In addition to Proposition
2.1
, we need the following results in
[
Sai
, Lemma 2.5, Corollary 2.6]
.

(1)

The kernel filtration
F_{p}R\Psi(\Lambda)
is given by the canonical truncated filtration
\tau_{\leq p}R\Psi(\Lambda)
and therefore

(2.4)

Gr^{F}_{p}R\Psi(\Lambda)\cong R^{p}\Psi(\Lambda)[-p]\cong[a_{p*}\Lambda(-p)\xrightarrow{\delta\wedge}\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta\wedge}a_{n*}\Lambda(-p)\rightarrow 0].

(2)

The image filtration
G^{q}Gr^{F}_{p}R\Psi(\Lambda)
on
Gr^{F}_{p}R\Psi(\Lambda)
is given by the truncation in (
2.4
) to
p+q
position

G^{q}Gr^{F}_{p}R\Psi(\Lambda)=[a_{p+q*}\Lambda(-p)\xrightarrow{\delta\wedge}\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta\wedge}a_{n*}\Lambda(-p)\rightarrow 0].

(3)

Combining the above two results, we have

Gr^{q}_{G}Gr^{F}_{p}R\Psi(\Lambda)=a_{p+q*}\Lambda[-p-q](-p)

and therefore we arrive at the following equation

Gr^{M}_{r}R\Psi(\Lambda)=\bigoplus_{p-q=r}a_{p+q*}\Lambda[-p-q](-p).

The Monodromy filtration induces the
weight spectral sequence

(2.5)

\mathrm{E}^{p,q}_{1}=\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(X_{k^{\mathrm{ac}}},Gr^{M}_{-p}R\Psi(\Lambda))\Rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(X_{k^{\mathrm{ac}}},R\Psi(\Lambda))=\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(X_{K^{\mathrm{ac}}},\Lambda).

The
\mathrm{E}_{1}
-term of this spectral sequence can be made explicit by

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(X_{k^{\mathrm{ac}}},Gr^{M}_{-p}R\Psi(\Lambda))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i-j=-p,i\geq 0,j\geq 0}\mathrm{H}^{p+q-(i+j)}(X^{(i+j)}_{k^{\mathrm{ac}}},\Lambda(-i))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i\geq\mathrm{max}(0,-p)}\mathrm{H}^{q-2i}(X^{(p+2i)}_{k^{\mathrm{ac}}},\Lambda(-i)).

This spectral sequence is first found by Rapoport-Zink in
[
RZ
]
and thus is also known as the Rapoport-Zink spectral sequence.

\mathbf{P}(L_{0}/pL_{0})
given by
VM_{1}/pM_{0}\subset^{1}M_{0}/pM_{0}=L_{0}/pL_{0}\otimes k^{\mathrm{ac}}
. Notice in this case the vertex lattices
L_{0},L_{1}
satisfies the following inclusions

pL_{0}\subset L_{1}\subset L_{0}.

We summarize the above discussion in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have the following statements.

(1)

The irreducible components of the special fiber of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
can be partitioned into two types according to whether
0
or
1
is a critical index. Each irreducible component corresponds to a vertex lattice and is isomorphic to a projective line.

(2)

Two irreducible components corresponding two vertex lattices of the same type will not intersect. Let
L_{0}
be a vertex lattice of type
0
and
L_{1}
be a vertex lattice of type
1
, then the corresponding irreducible components intersect if an only if
pL_{0}\subset L_{1}\subset L_{0}
.

(3)

The irreducible components are parametrized by
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})/\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})\times\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
. The intersection points of the irreducible components are parametrized by
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})/\mathrm{Iw}_{p}
where
\mathrm{Iw}_{p}
is the Iwahori subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
.

Proof.

The first two points follow from the previous discussions. For the third point, note that the stabilizer of a vertex lattice in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
is obviously
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
. The condition
pL_{0}\subset L_{1}\subset L_{0}
defines a standard alcove in the Bruhat-Tits building of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
and therefore the stabilizer of the pair
(L_{0},L_{1})
is the Iwahori subgroup
\mathrm{Iw}_{p}
.
∎

3.3.
Cerednick-Drinfeld uniformization

From here on, we will set
k=\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}
,
K=\mathbf{Q}_{p^{2}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbf{Z}_{p^{2}}
. Recall we have the integral model
\mathfrak{X}
of the Shimura curve
X
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and we denote by
\mathfrak{X}^{\wedge}
its completion along the ideal defined by
p
. The Cerednick-Drinfeld uniformization theorem asserts that the
{\mathfrak{X}}^{\wedge}
can be uniformized by the formal scheme

:

(3.6)

{\mathfrak{X}}^{\wedge}\xrightarrow{\sim}G(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathcal{M}\times G(\mathbf{A}^{\infty,(p)})/K^{p}.

It follows from the descriptions in Proposition
3.1
and the above uniformization theorem that the irreducible components of the special fiber

are projective lines. It also follows that
\mathfrak{X}
has strict semistable reduction. More precisely, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

We have the following descriptions of the scheme
X_{k}
.

(1)

The scheme
X_{k}
is a union
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-bundles over Shimura sets

X_{k}=\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{+})\cup\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{-}).

where both
X^{B}_{+}
and
X^{B}_{-}
are isomorphic to the Shimura set
X^{B}
as in (
3.1
).

(2)

The intersection points of the two
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-bundles
\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{+})
and
\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{-})
are given by

\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{+})\cap\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{-})=X^{B}_{0}(p).

This also can be identified with the set of singular points on
X_{k}
.

Proof.

For
(1)
, recall that there are two types of irreducible components of the special fiber of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
corresponding to vertex lattices of type
0
and vertex lattices of type
1
. By the Cerednick–Drinfeld uniformization (
3.6
), we only need to notice that the irreducible components of corresponding to vertex lattices of type
0
or vertex lattice of type
1
are uniformized by

G(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})/\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})\times G(\mathbf{A}^{\infty,(p)})/K^{p}.

This is just another way of writing
X^{B}
. We will define
X^{B}_{+}
to be the copy of
X^{B}
corresponding to the vertex lattice of type
0
and define
X^{B}_{-}
to be the copy of
X^{B}
corresponding to the vertex lattice of type
1
. For
(2)
, the Cerednick–Drinfeld uniformization (

) shows that the set of intersection points are given by

G(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})/\mathrm{Iw}_{p}\times G(\mathbf{A}^{\infty,(p)})/K^{p}.

Again this is another way of writing
X^{B}_{0}(p)
. The rest of the claims are clear.
∎

We denote by
\pi_{+}:X^{B}_{0}(p)\rightarrow X^{B}_{+}
and
\pi_{-}:X^{B}_{0}(p)\rightarrow X^{B}_{-}
the natural specialization maps. These two maps give the Hecke correspondence of

. This can be seen as follows: the map

is induced by sending the pair of vertex lattices

to

and the map
\pi_{-}
is induced by sending
(L_{1}\subset L_{0})
to

. The two lattices are related by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&p\\

\end{pmatrix}

that is if one chooses a suitable basis

for

then
(e_{2},pe_{1})
will be a basis for

.

The nontrivial element in
\mathrm{Gal}(k/\mathbf{F}_{p})
acts on
X_{k}
and it permutes the two
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-bundles
\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{+})
and
\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{-})
. On the set

it acts by the classical

. For this, see
[
BD1
, §1.7]
for example.

3.4.
Ramified level raising on Shimura curves

Recall
N=N^{+}N^{-}
with
N^{+}
and
N^{-}
defined previously. Let
f\in\mathrm{S}^{\mathrm{new}}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a newform of weight
2
. Let
E=\mathbf{Q}(f)
be the Hecke field of
f
. Let
\lambda
be a place of
E
above
l
and
E_{\lambda}
be the completion of
E
at
\lambda
. Let
\varpi
be a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{E_{\lambda}}
and we write
\mathcal{O}_{n}=\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}
for

. We let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}_{N^{+},N^{-}}
, respectively
\mathbb{T}^{[p]}=\mathbb{T}_{N^{+},pN^{-}}
, be the
l
-adic Hecke algebra corresponding to the cusp forms of level
N=N^{+}N^{-}
, respectively of level
Np=N^{+}N^{-}p
, which is new at primes dividing
N^{-}
, respectively at primes dividing
pN^{-}
. Since

is an eigenform, we have a morphism

\phi_{f}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}

corresponding to the system of eigenvalues of
f
. More precisely, we have
\phi_{f}(T_{v})=a_{v}(f)
for
v\nmid N
and
\phi_{f}(U_{v})=a_{v}(f)
for
v\mid N
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. We say that a prime
p
is
n
-admissible
for
f
if

(1)

p\nmid Nl
;

(2)

l\nmid p^{2}-1
;

(3)

\varpi^{n}\mid p+1-\epsilon_{p}(f)a_{p}(f)
for some
\epsilon_{p}(f)\in\{-1,1\}
.

Let
\phi_{f,n}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}
be reduction of the map
\phi_{f}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
modulo
\varpi^{n}
. We denote by
I_{f,n}
the kernel of this map and
\mathfrak{m}_{f}
the unique maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}
containing
I_{f,n}
. We will say
\mathfrak{m}_{f}
is residually irreducible if the residue Galois representation
\bar{\rho}_{f}
attached to
f
is irreducible. The following result is known as the
ramified arithmetic level raising for Shimura curves
and was first proved in
[
BD3
, Theorem 5.15, Corollary 5.18]
. The proof of the theorem below is inspired by two lectures given by Liang Xiao at the Morningside center
[
Xiao
]
on the subject.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
f
. We assume that

(i)

the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{f}
is residually irreducible;

(ii)

The module
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}
is free of rank
1
over
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}
.

Then we have the following.

(1)

There exists a surjective homomorphism
\phi^{[p]}_{f,n}:\mathbb{T}^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}
such that
\phi^{[p]}_{f,n}
agrees with
\phi_{f,n}
at all Hecke operators away from
p
and sends
U_{p}
to
\epsilon_{p}(f)
. We will denote by
I^{[p]}_{f,n}
the kernel of
\phi^{[p]}_{f,n}
.

(2)

We have an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
-modules of rank
1

S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}){/I_{f,n}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(K,\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{K^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}(1)){/I^{[p]}_{f,n}}).

Proof.

In this proof we will set
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{k^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}(1))
and
\mathrm{M}_{n}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{k^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}(1)){/I_{f,n}}
. Then we use the following formula as proved in (
2.12
) and (
2.13
)

(3.7)

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(K,\mathrm{M})\cong\mathrm{coker}[\mathrm{H}^{0}(a_{0*}\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathrm{H}^{0}(a_{1*}\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathrm{H}^{2}(a_{0*}\mathcal{O}(1))]^{G_{k}}.

Here

\mathrm{H}^{0}(a_{0*}\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{+}),\mathcal{O})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{-}),\mathcal{O})

and we can identify it with the space

S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}
. Similarly under the Poincare duality, we can also identify

\mathrm{H}^{2}(a_{0*}\mathcal{O}(1))=\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{+}),\mathcal{O}(1))\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{-}),\mathcal{O}(1))

with
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}
. The space
\mathrm{H}^{0}(a_{1*}\mathcal{O})
can be identified with
S^{B}_{2}(pN^{+},\mathcal{O})
. Under these identifications, the composition appearing in (
3.7
)

\mathrm{H}^{0}(a_{0*}\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathrm{H}^{0}(a_{1*}\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathrm{H}^{2}(a_{0*}\mathcal{O}(1))

is given by the
intersection matrix

\begin{pmatrix}-(p+1)&T_{p}\\
T_{p}&-(p+1)\\
\end{pmatrix}

which we will also denote by
\nabla
. Since
p
is
n
-admissible for
f
, the singular quotient of
\mathrm{M}_{n}

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(K,\mathrm{M}_{n})\cong\mathrm{coker}[S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n}}\xrightarrow{\nabla}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n})}]

is of rank one over
\mathcal{O}_{n}
and is isomorphic to
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})_{/I_{f,n}}
. Note here the isomorphism between

\mathrm{coker}[S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n}}\xrightarrow{\nabla}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n})}]

and
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n}}
is induced by the map

S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n}}\rightarrow S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})_{/I_{f,n}},\hphantom{aa}(x,y)\mapsto\frac{1}{2}(x+\epsilon_{p}(f)y)

This proves the second part of the theorem.

By
[
BD3
, Theorem 5.8]
and
[
CH2
, §3.5]
, the natural
U_{p}
-action on

\mathrm{H}^{2}(a_{0*}\mathcal{O}(1))\cong S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}

is given by
(x,y)\mapsto(-py,x+T_{p}y)
.
We consider the automorphism

\delta:S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}\rightarrow S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}

given by

(x,y)\mapsto(x+T_{p}y,y)
.
Then a quick calculation gives us that
\nabla\circ\delta=U^{2}_{p}-1
. This means that the quotient

\frac{S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}}{(I_{f,n},U^{2}_{p}-1)}\cong\mathrm{coker}[S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n}}\xrightarrow{\nabla}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n})}]

is of rank
1
. Since
p
is
n
-admissible for
f
, we see immediately
U_{p}+\epsilon_{f}
is invertible on
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}_{/I_{f,n}}
. Therefore we have

\frac{S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}}{(I_{f,n},U^{2}_{p}-1)}\cong\frac{S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})^{\oplus 2}}{(I_{f,n},U_{p}-\epsilon_{p}(f))}

and the latter quotient is of rank
1
over
\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Then the action of
\mathbb{T}^{[p]}
on this rank
1
quotient gives the desired morphism

\phi^{[p]}_{f,n}:\mathbb{T}^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}
.

∎

We will now compare the ingredients in the above proof with those in
[
BD3
]
. Let

be the dual reduction graph of

defined in an obvious manner. Let

be the Néron model of the Jacobian

.

We have the following statements

(1)

where

Now we consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathfrak{X}^{3}\otimes\mathbf{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{M}^{[p]}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{p}}

where

is the composite of the right part of the diagram. Then we define the

to be image of
\theta_{*}[\mathfrak{X}\otimes\mathbf{Q}]
under the map
\mathrm{AJ}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}
and we denote this element by

Similarly we denote the image of
\theta_{*}[\mathfrak{X}\otimes\mathbf{Q}]

p

in the following. By definition, this is the image of the cycle
\theta_{*}[\mathfrak{X}\otimes\mathbf{Q}]
under the map
\partial_{p}\mathrm{AJ}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}
which we will denote by

\partial_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{M}^{[p]}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1)).

By Corollary
4.11
, we can view
\partial_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n}
as an element of

\oplus^{3}_{j=1}(\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i}){/I_{i,n}}).

For
j=1,2,3
, we denote by

\partial^{(j)}_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n}\in\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i}){/I_{i,n}}

its projection to the
j

\oplus^{3}_{j=1}(\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i}){/I_{i,n}}).

We define a pairing

(4.7)

(\hphantom{a},\hphantom{a}):\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i})\times\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}

by the following formula. Let

\zeta_{1}\otimes\zeta_{2}\otimes\zeta_{3},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{3}\in\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i})

then we define

(\zeta_{1}\otimes\zeta_{2}\otimes\zeta_{3},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{3})=\sum_{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}}\zeta_{1}\phi_{1}(z_{1})\otimes\zeta_{2}\phi_{2}(z_{2})\otimes\zeta_{3}\phi_{3}(z_{3})

where
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
runs through all the elements in the finite set
(X^{B})^{3}
. Note that when
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{2}=\mathcal{O}_{3}
, the pairing which apriori valued in
\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}
can be taken to be valued in
\mathcal{O}
by using the natural multiplication map. This pairing subsequently induces a pairing

\displaystyle(\hphantom{a},\hphantom{a}):\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i})[I_{i,n}]\times\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i}){/I_{i,n}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}.

The following theorem is the analogue of the
first reciprocity law
for Heegner points on Shimura curves
[
BD3
, Theorem 4.1]
, the
congruence formulae
in
[
Liu1
, Theorem 4.11]
and
[
Liu2
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Theorem 4.12

(First reciprocity law)
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. We assume the assumptions in Theorem
4.7
are satisfied. Let
\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{3}\in\otimes^{3}_{i=1}S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i})[I_{i,n}]
. Then for
j=1,2,3
, the following formula holds

(\partial^{(j)}_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{3})=(p+1)^{3}\sum_{z\in X^{B}}\phi_{1}(z)\otimes\phi_{2}(z)\otimes\phi_{3}(z).

Proof.

We only prove the formula for
j=1
, the other cases are proved exactly the same way. Consider the diagonal embedding of

\theta:\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{3}

of the model of
X
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
into its threefold fiber product. Since
\mathfrak{X}
is regular, the map
\theta
extends to a map
\tilde{\theta}:\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
such that
\pi\circ\tilde{\theta}=\theta
by the universal property of the blow-up. We use the same notation
\tilde{\theta}:X_{k}\rightarrow Y_{k}
to denote the map induced on the special fiber. We apply Proposition
2.4
to calculate
\partial^{(1)}_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n}
. Thus we need to find the image of

Y^{(0)}_{k}\times_{Y_{k}}\tilde{\theta}_{*}X_{k}

under the cycle class map in
A^{2}(Y_{k},\Lambda)^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}}
. By Lemma
4.9
and the proof of Theorem
4.7
, the class
\partial^{(1)}_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n}
is represented by the image of the characteristic function
\mathbf{1}_{0}(p)
on
X^{B}_{0}(p)
under the map

(4.8)

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X^{B}_{0}(p),\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{\pm}),\Lambda(1))\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(X^{B}_{0}(p),\Lambda)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(X^{B}_{0}(p),\Lambda).

which is induced by the Gysin map on the first factor of the tensor product and by the identity maps on the rest of the two factors of the tensor product. Recall that we have two natural transition maps of Shimura sets

\pi_{+}:X^{B}_{0}(p)\rightarrow X^{B}\text{\hphantom{a}and\hphantom{b}}\pi_{-}:X^{B}_{0}(p)\rightarrow X^{B}.

By making explicit the maps

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbf{P}^{1}(X^{B}_{\pm}),\Lambda(1))\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(X^{B}_{0}(p),\Lambda)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(X^{B}_{0}(p),\Lambda)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Phi_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}/I^{[p]}_{1,n}\otimes\mathcal{X}^{\vee}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}/I^{[p]}_{2,n}\otimes\mathcal{X}^{\vee}_{\mathcal{O}_{3}}/I^{[p]}_{3,n}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Phi_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}/I^{[p]}_{1,n}\otimes\Phi_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}/I^{[p]}_{2,n}\otimes\Phi_{\mathcal{O}_{3}}/I^{[p]}_{3,n}

\displaystyle\cong S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{1}){/I_{1,n}}\otimes S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{2}){/I_{2,n}}\otimes S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{3}){/I_{3,n}}.

The element
\partial^{(1)}_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n}\in S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{1}){/I_{1,n}}\otimes S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{2}){/I_{2,n}}\otimes S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{3}){/I_{3,n}}
is given by

\frac{\theta_{*}(\pi_{+,*}+\epsilon_{p,1}\pi_{-,*})}{2}(\mathbf{1}_{0}(p))\otimes\frac{\theta_{*}(\pi_{+,*}+\epsilon_{p,2}\pi_{-,*})}{2}(\mathbf{1}_{0}(p))\otimes\frac{\theta_{*}(\pi_{+,*}+\epsilon_{p,3}\pi_{-,*})}{2}(\mathbf{1}_{0}(p))

where we abuse the notation and denote by
\theta:X^{B}\rightarrow(X^{B})^{3}
the diagonal embedding of the Shimura set
X^{B}
.
Since
\pi_{+,*}\mathbf{1}_{0}(p)=\epsilon_{p,i}\pi_{-,*}\mathbf{1}_{0}(p)
is the constant function on
X^{B}
with value
p+1
, we have

\displaystyle(\partial^{(1)}_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{3})

\displaystyle=(\frac{\theta_{*}(\pi_{+,*}+\epsilon_{p,1}\pi_{-,*})}{2}(\mathbf{1}_{0}(p))\otimes\frac{\theta_{*}(\pi_{+,*}+\epsilon_{p,2}\pi_{-,*})}{2}(\mathbf{1}_{0}(p))\otimes\frac{\theta_{*}(\pi_{+,*}+\pi_{-,*})}{2}(\mathbf{1}_{0}(p)),\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{3})

\displaystyle=(p+1)^{3}\sum_{z\in X^{B}}\phi_{1}(z)\otimes\phi_{2}(z)\otimes\phi_{3}(z).

The formula is proved.
∎

5.
The Bloch-Kato conjecture for the triple product motive

5.1.
Selmer groups of triple product motive

Let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(f)q^{n}\in S^{\mathrm{new}}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a normalized newform of weight
2
. We assume that
N
admits a factorization

such that

and

, we can attach a Galois representation

over

with determinant the
l
-adic cyclotomic character and satisfying

Let
\mathrm{T}_{f}

\mathcal{O}
-lattice in
\mathrm{V}_{f}

Let
\bar{\rho}_{f}

. Let
\mathrm{A}_{f}=\mathrm{V}_{f}/\mathrm{T}_{f}

\mathrm{A}_{f,n}=\ker[\mathrm{A}_{f}\xrightarrow{\varpi^{n}}\mathrm{A}_{f}]
. We denote by
\mathbf{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{f})
the field extension of
\mathbf{Q}
in
{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathrm{ac}}
cut out by
\bar{\rho}_{f}

f

\phi_{f}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}

\phi_{f,n}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}

f
. Recall we denote by

the kernel of the map
\phi_{f,n}
. Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
f
, by Theorem
3.4
, we have a morphism
\phi^{[p]}_{f,n}:\mathbb{T}^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}
with kernel

. We have
\mathfrak{m}_{f}=I_{f,1}
the maximal ideal in
\mathbb{T}
containing
I_{f,n}

\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}_{f}=I_{f,1}
the maximal ideal in
\mathbb{T}^{[p]}
containing
I^{[p]}_{f,n}

Now we consider a triple
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})

. We will denote the representation
\mathrm{V}_{f_{i}}
simply by
\mathrm{V}_{i}
for

. They are defined over the
l

where
\lambda_{i}

\mathbf{Q}(f_{i})
over
l
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{i}

\varpi_{i}
and we put
\mathcal{O}_{i,n}=\mathcal{O}_{i}/\varpi^{n}_{i}

\mathcal{O}_{i}
-lattice
\mathrm{T}_{i}
and the
\mathcal{O}_{i,n}
-module
\mathrm{T}_{i,n}
. Let
I_{i,n}=I_{f_{i},n}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{i}=\mathfrak{m}_{f_{i}}
. We have the fields
\mathbf{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{i}):=\mathbf{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{f_{i}})
defined by the residual Galois representations
\bar{\rho}_{f_{i}}

\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})=\mathrm{V}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{2}\otimes\mathrm{V}_{3}

We have the following result about the Galois cohomology of the representation

which says the representation

Lemma 5.1
.

For all places

\mathbf{Q}

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{v},\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))=0.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is the same as that of

by replacing the the elliptic curve by the Jacobians of suitable Shimura curves.
∎

Definition 5.2
.

The
Bloch-Kato Selmer group

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))
is the subspace of classes
s\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))
such that

{\rm{loc}}_{l}(s)\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q}_{l},\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1)):=\ker[\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q}_{l},\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q}_{l},\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})\otimes\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}(-1))].

Let
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
be the triple of automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A})
associated to the triple
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
. Then one can attach the
triple product L-function

L(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3},s)=L(s-\frac{3}{2},\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{3},r)

where
L(s-\frac{3}{2},\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{3},r)
is the Langlands
L
-function attached to
r
which is the natural eight-dimensional representation of the
L
-group of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. We will be concerned with case when
global root number

\epsilon(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{3},r)=1

that is the order of vanishing of the triple product
L
-function

is even. The following proposition relates

to certain explicit period integral appeared in the statement of Theorem

.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(f^{B}_{1},f^{B}_{2},f^{B}_{3})\in S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})
be the Jacquet-Langlands transfer of the triple
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
. If the value

(5.1)

I(f^{B}_{1},f^{B}_{2},f^{B}_{3})=\sum_{z\in X^{B}}f^{B}_{1}(z)\otimes f^{B}_{2}(z)\otimes f^{B}_{3}(z)

This follows from the main result of
[
KH
]
which resolves a conjecture of Jacquet. See also
[
GK
]
and
[
Ich
]

.
∎

We make the following conjecture for the motive attached to the triple
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
that we hope to address in a future work.

Conjecture 5.4
.

Suppose that the triple
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
satisfies the following assumptions: for each
i=1,2,3

(1)

the maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
are all residually irreducible;

(2)

the
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
-module
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O}_{i})_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
is free of rank
1
;

(3)

the residual Galois representations
\bar{\rho}_{i}
are surjective and the fields
\mathbf{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{i})
are linearly disjoint.

If the central critical value
L(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3},2)
is non-zero, then the Bloch-Kato Selmer group vanishes

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{V}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))=0.

Note that the assumptions made in the conjecture guarantees us that there are abundance of
n
-admissible primes for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
5.4
, there are infinitely many
n
-admissible primes
p
for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{i,n}:G_{\mathbf{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i,n})
be the representation on
\mathrm{T}_{i,n}
defined by reducing
\mathrm{T}_{i}
modulo
\varpi_{i}^{n}
. Consider the direct sum representation

\rho_{1,n}\oplus\rho_{2,n}\oplus\rho_{3,n}:G_{\mathbf{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{1,n})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2,n})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{3,n}).

Since
\bar{\rho}_{i}
is surjective and the fields
\mathbf{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{i})
are linearly disjoint, we can find infinitely many primes
p
such that

\rho_{1,n}\oplus\rho_{2,n}\oplus\rho_{3,n}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{1}p&0\\
0&\epsilon_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{2}p&0\\
0&\epsilon_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{3}p&0\\
0&\epsilon_{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{1,n})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2,n})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{3,n})

with
l\nmid p^{2}-1
and
\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}=1
by the Chebotarev density theorem. By definition one can check that
p
is an
n
-admissible prime for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
.
∎

For
i=1,2,3
, recall that
\mathrm{T}^{[p]}_{i}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{i}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}_{i}}

and
\mathrm{T}^{[p]}_{i,n}=\mathrm{T}^{[p]}_{i}/I^{[p]}_{i,n}
. We have the following lemma which we have already implicitly used in Lemma
4.6
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. There is an isomorphism of

-representations

\mathrm{T}^{[p]}_{i,n}\cong\mathrm{T}_{i,n}.

Proof.

This follows from the same proof given for
[

.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

This Lemma implies that we have an isomorphism
\mathrm{M}^{[p]}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})\cong\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})
of
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
-modules. Under the assumption of Conjecture
5.4
, in particular
L(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3},s)
is non-vanishing at
s=2
, the period integral
I(f^{B}_{1},f^{B}_{2},f^{B}_{3})
is non-vanishing by Proposition
5.3
. Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
, the cohomology class
\Theta^{[p]}_{n}
has the property that
\partial_{p}\Theta^{[p]}_{n}
is non-zero in
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{M}^{[p]}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))
for some

by the first reciprocity law in Theorem

. Therefore the class

could be viewed as an annihilator of the Selmer group. However as we have seen in Lemma

, the singular quotient at

is of rank

and therefore the class
\Theta^{[p]}_{n}
alone can not fill up the whole singular quotient and therefore is not enough to bound the Selmer group. However we conjecture here that there exist three global cohomology classes in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}})(-1))
which are intimately related to

and satisfy a similar reciprocity law as in Theorem

. Using these three classes, one can indeed prove Conjecture

.

5.2.
The symmetric cube motive

We specialize the discussions in this article to the case when

\mathrm{V}_{f}^{\otimes 3}(-1)=\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\mathrm{V}_{f}(-1)\oplus\mathrm{V}_{f}\oplus\mathrm{V}_{f}

where we will refer to

We can define the Bloch-Kato Selmer group

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\mathrm{V}_{f}(-1))

exactly the same way as we did for the triple product representation in Definition

\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\mathrm{V}_{f}(-1)

Theorem 5.8
.

Suppose that the modular form
f
satisfies the following assumptions:

(1)

the maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{f}
are all residually irreducible;

(2)

the
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}
-module
S^{B}_{2}(N^{+},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}_{f}}
is free of rank
1
;

(3)

\bar{\rho}_{f}
is surjective;

(4)

the value
L(f,1)
is non-vanishing.

If the central critical value
L(\mathrm{Sym}^{3}f,2)
is non-zero, then the Bloch-Kato Selmer group

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\mathrm{V}_{f}(-1))=0.

Remark 5.9
.

Let
f^{B}
be the Jacquet-Langlands transfer of

(4)
in the assumptions of the above theorem implies that the period integral

is non-vanishing.

Partially Coherent Direct Sum Channels
Stefano Chessa, Vittorio Giovannetti

Abstract
We introduce Partially Coherent Direct Sum (PCDS) quantum channels, as a generalization of the already known Direct Sum quantum channels. We derive necessary and sufficient conditions to identify the subset of those maps which are degradable, and provide a simplified expression for their quantum capacities. Interestingly, the special structure of PCDS allows us to extend the computation of the quantum capacity formula also for quantum channels which are explicitly not degradable (nor antidegradable). We show instances of applications of the results to dephasing channels, amplitude damping channels, and combinations of the two.

Introduction

Since the seminal work of Shannon
[
1
]
the performance analysis of means of communication has been rephrased into the search of the maximum information transmission rates achievable by noisy channels. In the last few decades the possibility of exploiting quantum-mechanical effects to perform communication has been brought to the light. The approach to the evaluation of channel capacities was then directed also towards noisy quantum channels, opening the road to the field of quantum communication
[

,

.

The features of quantum information can bring advantages w.r.t. classical settings, e.g., among others, quantum key distribution

, quantum teleportation

, superactivation of the quantum capacity of a quantum channel

. The qualitatively different phenomena that typically are involved when dealing with quantum systems on the other hand might constitute challenging obstacles, see as an example the often intractable regularizations needed for the definitions of channel capacities
[

. Even for few uses of the channel considered, in absence of useful properties or further symmetries, maximizations over Hilbert spaces (to find capacities) can reveal themselves computationally hard, especially in higher dimensions. This makes the study, in terms of information capacities, of a wide realm of channels unattractive and unexplored, despite quantum information in higher dimensions being a well established field of research. For this family of systems and channels potential advantages are showed either from the quantum computation (see e.g.
[

. All this considered, methods to overcome these kind of obstacles are still researched and this paper aims to contribute to this corpus of literature. Specifically, we present compact expressions for the quantum capacity and entanglement assisted quantum capacity of a new class of channels that we called
Partially Coherent Direct Sum
(PCDS) channels, a generalization of the direct sum (DS) channels described in
[
30
]
. This formalism appears in a variety of contexts
[

,

,

,

,

]
, among which recently the “gluing” procedure in
[

derived as a generalization of the construction in
[

. DS channels were initially introduced in
[

in the context of the additivity conjecture for the classical capacity, later proven wrong
[
10
]
. There, the direct-sum structure was proposed in order to simplify the expressions of those functionals, e.g. output entropy and Holevo information, whose additivity was conjectured. Among these results also an expression for the coherent information for DS channels was found. In the subsequent literature the direct-sum structure has been mainly exploited to show superadditivity features of quantum channels.

We draw attention to PCDS because, other than encompassing the already known DS channels, they provide a framework for the efficient description of wide classes of physical noise models and their capacities. An instance is given by damping processes in multi-level systems, denominated multi-level amplitude damping (MAD) channels
[
40

In this sense, the knowledge already acquired about low dimensional quantum channels can be exploited to compose new PCDS channels. At the same time the introduction of the PCDS can push the study of all accessible zoology of low dimensional channels. In addition to that, through the techniques here developed, in some cases we are able to evaluate exactly the quantum capacity even if the channel can be proven not to be degradable
[

associated with the direct sum structure of the Hilbert space and the introduction of coherences.

2
The model

Let us start fixing some notation:
given
{\cal H}_{\text{X}}
and
{\cal H}_{\text{Y}}
two Hilbert spaces associated with two
(possibly unrelated) quantum systems X and Y, we shall use the symbol

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}:=\{\hat{\Theta}_{\text{YX}}:{\cal H}_{\text{X}}\longrightarrow{\cal H}_{\text{Y}}\}\;,

(1)

to represent the set of linear operators
\hat{\Theta}_{\text{YX}}

mapping the input vectors of X into the output vectors of Y.
The symbol
\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{X}}}=\mathfrak{S}({\cal H}_{{\text{X}}})
will describe the special subset of
\cal{L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}
formed by the density operators
\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}}
of the system X.
We also define

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}:=\{{\Phi}_{\text{YX}}:\cal{L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}\longrightarrow\cal{L}_{{\text{Y}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}\}\;,

(2)

to be the set of super-operators
\Phi_{\text{YX}}
which
transform operators
\hat{\Theta}_{\text{XX}}\in\cal{L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}
into elements of
\cal{L}_{{\text{Y}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}
. We’ll indicate with
{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}

the special subset formed by the quantum channels of
{\cal M}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}
, i.e. by the super-operators
{\Phi}_{\text{YX}}
which are Completely Positive and Trace preserving (CPT)
[
42
]
.
Finally, for X
\neq
Y we shall use the special symbol

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{X}\rightarrow\text{Y}}:=\{{\Phi}^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{YX}}:\cal{L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}\longrightarrow\cal{L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}\}\;,

(3)

to describe linear mappings
{\Phi}^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{YX}}
which connect operators
\cal{L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}

into themselves.

Consider next C, a quantum system described by a Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{\text{C}}
admitting the following direct sum decomposition

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\text{C}}={\cal H}_{\text{A}}\oplus{\cal H}_{\text{B}}\;,

(4)

with
{\cal H}_{\text{A}}
and
{\cal H}_{\text{B}}
two nontrivial subspaces of dimensions

d_{\text{A}}
,
d_{\text{B}}=d_{\text{C}}-d_{\text{A}}
. We’ll associate with them the projectors
{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}
and
{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}

that fulfill the
orthonormalization conditions

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}={\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}=0\;,\quad{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}+{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}=\hat{I}_{\text{CC}}\;,

(5)

\hat{I}_{\text{CC}}
being the identity operator on
{\cal H}_{\text{C}}
.
Accordingly, any operator

{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}\in{\cal{L}}_{{\text{C}}\rightarrow{\text{C}}}
mapping the space of C into itself can then be written
as a sum of diagonal and off-diagonal block terms, i.e.

\displaystyle{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}=\bigoplus_{\text{X,Y}=\text{A,B}}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{YX}}\equiv\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}&{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AB}}\\
\hline\cr{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BA}}&{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}\end{array}\right],

with
{\text{X,Y}}={\text{A,B}}
and
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{XY}}
is an element of
{\cal{L}}_{{\text{Y}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}
defined by the identity

\displaystyle{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{XY}}\equiv\hat{P}_{\text{XX}}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}\hat{P}_{\text{YY}}\;.

(9)

Let now
\Phi_{\text{CC}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{C}}\rightarrow{\text{C}}}
be a CPT channel mapping C into itself.
We say that it is a
Partially Coherent Direct Sum
(PCDS) map if it preserves the block structure in Eq. (
2
). Or equivalently if we can identify super-operators

\Phi_{\text{AA}}\in{\cal M}_{{\text{A}}\rightarrow{\text{A}}}
,
\Phi_{\text{BB}}\in{\cal M}_{{\text{B}}\rightarrow{\text{B}}}
,
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(off)}}_{{\text{B}}\rightarrow{\text{A}}}
, and
\Phi_{\text{BA}}^{\text{(off)}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(off)}}_{{\text{A}}\rightarrow{\text{B}}}

such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{CC}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}&{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AB}}\\
\hline\cr{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BA}}&{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Phi_{\text{AA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]&\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AB}}]\\
\hline\cr\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BA}}]&\Phi_{\text{BB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]\end{array}\right]\;,

for all input
\hat{\Theta}_{\text{CC}}\in{\cal{L}}_{{\text{C}}\rightarrow{\text{C}}}
. In brief, simplifying the notation

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{CC}}=\Phi_{\text{AA}}+\Phi_{\text{BB}}+\Phi_{\text{AB}}^{\text{(off)}}+\Phi_{\text{BA}}^{\text{(off)}}\;,

(16)

where each channel
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
implicitly assumes the projection on the suitable subspace as in Eq. (
9
).
Quantum channels that can be cast in form of Eq. (
16
) arise whenever the quantum system
C is affected by a (possibly noisy) evolution that preserves the relative populations associated with
the
subsystems
{\cal H}_{\text{A}}
and
{\cal H}_{\text{B}}
, but
(possibly) deteriorates the quantum coherence among them.
In Appendix
A
it is shown a necessary and sufficient condition for this to happen. This condition
is that, given
\{{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}\}_{j}
a Kraus set
[
43
]
for

\Phi_{\text{CC}}
, its
elements must only involve diagonal terms when cast into the block form as in Eq. (
2
), i.e.

Theorem 2.1
.

A quantum channel
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
described by a Kraus set
\{{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}\}_{j}

admits the PCDS structure (
16
) if and only if

\displaystyle{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}={\hat{M}}_{\text{AA}}^{(j)}+{\hat{M}}_{\text{BB}}^{(j)}\;,

(17)

or equivalently that
{\hat{M}}_{\text{AB}}^{(j)}={\hat{M}}_{\text{BA}}^{(j)}=0
, for all
j
.

The explicit proof of this result is given in Appendix
A
. There we also show that the maps on the right-hand-side of Eq. (
16
) can
be expressed in terms of the operators

{\hat{M}}_{\text{AA}}^{(j)}
and
{\hat{M}}_{\text{BB}}^{(j)}

of Eq. (
17
) as

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{XX}}[\cdots]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j)}\cdots{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j){\dagger}}\;,\quad\

(18)

for all X
=
A,B
and

\displaystyle\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{XY}}[\cdots]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j)}\cdots{\hat{M}}_{\text{YY}}^{(j){\dagger}}\;,

(19)

for all X
\neq
Y
=
A,B. Notice that in particular Eq. (
18
) implies that
the diagonal terms
define proper CPT channels on A and B respectively, i.e.
\Phi_{\text{AA}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{A}}\rightarrow{\text{A}}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{B}}\rightarrow{\text{B}}}
, with Kraus sets provided by

\{{\hat{M}}_{\text{AA}}^{(j)}\}_{j}
and
\{{\hat{M}}_{\text{BB}}^{(j)}\}_{j}
.

One can easily check that given
\Phi^{\prime}_{\text{CC}},\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\text{CC}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{C}}\rightarrow{\text{C}}}

fulfilling the constraint of Eq. (
16
), then the same holds true for both the channel
p\Phi^{\prime}_{\text{CC}}+(1-p)\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\text{CC}}
with
p\in[0,1]

and for the channel
\Phi^{\prime}_{\text{CC}}\circ\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\text{CC}}
, with
``\circ"
representing super-operator composition.
The first property implies that the set of PCDS quantum evolutions is closed under convex combination. The second property instead,
together with the observation that the identity channel
\text{Id}_{\text{CC}}
is also trivially PCDS, tells us that the set forms a semi-group under channel concatenation.
Observe also that a special instance of PCDS transformations is provided by the
purely dephasing channels
\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}
, which induce the mapping

\displaystyle\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}&{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AB}}\\
\hline\cr{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BA}}&{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}&\kappa{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AB}}\\
\hline\cr\kappa^{*}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BA}}&{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}\end{array}\right]\;,

with
\kappa
being a complex parameter of norm
|\kappa|\leq 1
.
The semi-group property mentioned above also allows us to state the following. Starting from any PCDS channel
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
, described as in Eq. (
16
) for some
proper choice of the maps
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
,
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
,
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}
, and
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}
, we can
construct an entire family of new PCDS elements

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{CC}}^{(\kappa)}\equiv\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}\circ\Phi_{\text{CC}}=\Phi_{\text{CC}}\circ\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}\;,

(26)

whose off-diagonal components are rescaled versions of

\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}
, and
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\Phi^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}=\Phi_{\text{AA}}+\Phi_{\text{BB}}+\kappa\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}+\kappa^{*}\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}\;.

(27)

(Here the commutativity property exhibited in Eq. (
26
) follows from the linearity of the super-operators
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}

and
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}
).
In particular by setting
\kappa=0
, Eq. (
27
) describes the

Direct Sum
(DS) channels discussed in
Ref.
[
30
]
which
completely suppress coherence among
{\cal H}_{\text{A}}
and
{\cal H}_{\text{B}}
.
This special condition is met whenever the Kraus elements in Eq. (
17
) of a PCDS map are given by
operators that have support exclusively either on
{\cal H}_{\text{A}}
or on
{\cal H}_{\text{B}}
. We can summarize this constraint in terms of the following simple relation

\displaystyle{\hat{M}}_{\text{AA}}^{(j)}\neq 0\Longrightarrow M_{\text{BB}}^{(j)}=0\;,\qquad\forall j\;.

(28)

It is worth stressing that the properties discussed above, as well as the results we are going to present in the following sections,
admit a simple generalization in case of multi-block decompositions of the map PCDS – see Appendix
C
.

3
Complementary channels and degradability conditions for PCDS maps

We remind that, via the Stinespring dilation theorem
[
44
]
,
given
\Phi_{\text{XX}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}
a generic CPT transformation on an arbitrary system X, its complementary channel can be identified with a
CPT map
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{E}}}

coupling X with the (sufficiently large) auxiliary quantum system E that plays the role of the system environment.
Let
\{M_{\text{XX}}^{(j)}\}_{j}
be a Kraus set for
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
and
\{|j_{\text{E}}\rangle\}_{j}
be a fixed set of orthonormal vectors on the Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{\text{E}}
of E. Then the
action of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}
on a generic operator
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{XX}}\in{\cal L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{XX}}]=\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}|j_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle j^{\prime}_{\text{E}}|\;\mbox{Tr}\big[M_{XX}^{(j^{\prime}){\dagger}}M_{XX}^{(j)}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{XX}}\big]\;,

(notice that due to the arbitrariness of the choice of
\{|j_{\text{E}}\rangle\}_{j}
,
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}
can always be
redefined up to a unitary rotation on E).
We also remind that the map
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
is said to be degradable
[
41
]
if
we can identify a degrading CPT quantum channel
\Lambda_{\text{EX}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{E}}}
which allows us to reconstruct the action of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}

by acting on the corresponding output of
{\Phi}_{\text{XX}}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}=\Lambda_{\text{EX}}\circ\Phi_{\text{XX}}\;.

(30)

Similarly we say that
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
is antidegradable
[
45
]
if exists

\Lambda_{\text{XE}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{E}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{XX}}=\Lambda_{\text{XE}}\circ\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}\;.

(31)

In the case of DS channel
{\Phi}_{\text{CC}}
, using Eq. (
28
) and the orthogonality
between
{\hat{M}}_{\text{AA}}^{(j)}
and
{\hat{M}}_{\text{BB}}^{(j)}
, from Eq. (
3
)
one can then easily verify that for all
input operators
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}
the following identity holds

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EC}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]+\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]\;.

(32)

Here
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}
and
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}
are, respectively, the complementary channels associated with the diagonal components
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
entering in the decomposition of Eq. (
16
),
while
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}
and
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}
are the diagonal terms of Eq. (
2
).
We refer the reader to Appendix
B
for a physical insight on this identity.
As we’ll see next, Eq. (
32
) holds also for PCDS. Notice though that, while for generic PCDS channels
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
the operators
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]

and
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]
may have nontrivial commutation relations,
in the special case of the DS channels
\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{CC}}

[
30
]
they have always zero overlap, i.e.

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}^{(0)}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]\tilde{\Phi}^{(0)}_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]=\tilde{\Phi}^{(0)}_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]\tilde{\Phi}^{(0)}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]=0\;.

In this scenario this implies that the sum appearing in Eq. (
32
) is indeed a direct sum.

We can now prove a necessary and sufficient condition for the degradability of a generic PCDS channel
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
. This condition establishes that such property only depends upon the diagonal blocks entering in the decomposition of Eq. (
16
):

Theorem 3.1
.

A PCDS quantum channel
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
is degradable if and only if all of its diagonal block terms

\Phi_{\text{AA}}
,
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
are degradable too.

Proof:

First of all let us show that the degradability of
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}

implies the degradability of
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
.
Indeed for
\text{X}=\text{A,B}
, let
\Lambda_{\text{XE}}
be the CPT degrading map from X to E, which
allows us to express
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{XX}}
in terms of
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
as in Eq. (
30
).
Consider then the super-operator
\Lambda_{\text{EC}}

from C to E defined as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\text{EC}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}]\equiv\Lambda_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]+\Lambda_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]\;,

(34)

which is CPT thanks to the fact that both
\Lambda_{\text{EA}}
and
\Lambda_{\text{EB}}
fulfill the same constraint by hypothesis – see Appendix
B.1
for details. Furthermore for all
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\text{EC}}\circ\Phi_{\text{CC}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}]=\Lambda_{\text{EC}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Phi_{\text{AA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]&\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AB}}]\\
\hline\cr\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BA}}]&\Phi_{\text{BB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\Lambda_{\text{EA}}\circ\Phi_{\text{AA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]+\Lambda_{\text{EB}}\circ\Phi_{\text{BB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]+\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]=\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EC}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]\;,

that proves that
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
is degradable with degrading channel as in Eq. (
34
).

Let’s now show next that if
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
is degradable then also
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
must be degradable.
For this purpose, given
\Lambda_{\text{EC}}
the CPT transformation from C to E which allows us to reconstruct

\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EC}}
from
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
, from Eqs. (
16
) and (
32
) we get

\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]+\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]=\sum_{\text{X,Y}=\text{A,B}}(\Lambda_{\text{EC}}\circ\Phi_{\text{YX}})[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{YX}}]\;,

(38)

which must hold true for all
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{YX}}\in{\cal L}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{Y}}}
.
In the particular case
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}={\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AB}}={\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BA}}=0
, this implies
that for all
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}\in{\cal L}_{{\text{A}}\rightarrow{\text{A}}}
we have

\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]=(\Lambda_{\text{EC}}\circ\Phi_{\text{AA}})[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]=(\Lambda_{\text{EA}}\circ\Phi_{\text{AA}})[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]\;,

(39)

where in the last identity we introduced

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\text{EA}}[\cdots]\equiv\Lambda_{\text{EC}}[\hat{P}_{\text{AA}}\cdots\hat{P}_{\text{AA}}]\;,

(40)

by exploiting the fact that
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
maps operators of A into A, i.e. that
\hat{P}_{\text{BB}}\Phi_{\text{AA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]=\Phi_{\text{AA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]\hat{P}_{\text{BB}}=0
.
Since the map in Eq. (
40
) is CPT – see Appendix
B.1
, we can finally conclude that
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
is degradable.
The degradability of
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
can be proved in the same way.

\square

4
Computing the quantum capacity of PCDS channels

As firstly shown in
[
46
,
47
,
48
]
, the quantum capacity
Q(\Phi_{\text{XX}})
of a channel
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
is expressed as:

Q(\Phi_{\text{XX}})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{\hat{\rho}^{(n)}_{\text{XX}}\in\mathfrak{S}({\cal H}_{\text{X}}^{\otimes n})}I_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{XX}}^{\otimes n};\hat{\rho}^{(n)}_{\text{XX}}),

(41)

where
I_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{XX}};\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}})
is the
coherent information
and is defined as

I_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{XX}};\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}})\equiv S(\Phi_{\text{XX}}(\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}}))-S(\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}(\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}}))\;,

(42)

being
S(\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}})\equiv-\text{Tr}_{\text{X}}\left[\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}}\log_{2}\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}}\right]
the von Neumann entropy and
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EX}}
the complementary channel of
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
as defined in Eq. (
3
). As already mentioned in the introduction, the challenging aspect of the computation of the quantum capacity is given by the regularization over the number
n
of channel uses. This since the behavior for many uses doesn’t scale linearly w.r.t. the single shot formula, due to the well known property of non additivity of quantum channels. The issue can be bypassed when the channel is degradable (see Sec.
4.1
) for which the single letter formula is sufficient
[
41
]
, or antidegradable (the complementary channel is degradable) for which, due to no-cloning argument, we have
Q(\Phi_{\text{XX}})=0
.
Since we’ll make use of this feature, it is finally worth noticing that from the
invariance of the von Neumann entropy under unitary transformations it follows that the capacity formula
reported above does not depend on the specific form of the complementary channel. Indeed as already mentioned, the complementary channel can be chosen freely up to a unitary rotation acting on the environment E – see more about this in App.
B
.

Moving now towards DS and PCDS channels, in Ref.
[
30
]
it was shown that the quantum capacity of DS channels is given by the maximum of the quantum capacity of their diagonal contributions. Expressed in our notation

\displaystyle Q(\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{CC}})=\max\{Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}}),Q(\Phi_{\text{BB}})\}\;,

(43)

with
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
being the diagonal block terms.
The presence of non-zero off-diagonal contributions in Eq. (
16
) is clearly bound to challenge the above result.
To begin with, invoking the channel
data-processing inequality
(DPI)
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
49
,
50
,
5
]
from Eq. (
26
), it follows that the right-hand-side of Eq. (
43
)
is an explicit lower bound for the quantum capacity of an arbitrary PCDS channel

\Phi_{\text{CC}}
having the same diagonal block terms of
\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{CC}}
, i.e.

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})\geq Q(\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{CC}})=\max\{Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}}),Q(\Phi_{\text{BB}})\}\;.

This fact alone paves the way to higher communication performances. The easiest way to see this is by comparing the
case of the identity map
{\text{Id}}_{\text{CC}}
, which has capacity

\displaystyle Q({\text{Id}}_{\text{CC}})=\log_{2}d_{\text{C}}=\log_{2}(d_{\text{A}}+d_{\text{B}})\;,

(45)

with the case of the completely dephasing channel
\Delta^{(\kappa=0)}_{\text{CC}}
of Eq. (
2
).
\Delta^{(\kappa=0)}_{\text{CC}}
shares the same diagonal terms of
{\text{Id}}_{\text{CC}}
(i.e.
\Phi_{\text{AA}}={\text{Id}}_{\text{AA}}

and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}={\text{Id}}_{\text{BB}}
) but, according to Eq. (
43
),
has instead quantum capacity equal to

\displaystyle Q(\Delta^{(\kappa=0)}_{\text{CC}})=\max\{\log_{2}d_{\text{A}},\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}\}\;.

(46)

Exploiting the results of the previous section we are going to set this observation on a broader context, computing the explicit
value of the quantum capacity of large class of PCDS channels. Interestingly enough this will allow us to determine the quantum capacity of channels which are not degradable.

4.1
The quantum capacity of degradable PCDS channels

Consider the case of a PCDS channel
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
which is degradable. According to
[
41
]
we can
hence express it in terms of the following single-letter expression

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})=\max_{\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{C}}}}I_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{CC}};\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}})\;,

(47)

with
I_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{CC}};\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}})
the single-use coherent information functional introduced in Eq. (
42
).
Observe next that from Eq. (
2
) and the monotonicity of
S
under block diagonalization, it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip-11.0ptS(\Phi_{\text{CC}}(\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}}))=S\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}p\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]&\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}[\hat{\rho}_{\text{AB}}]\\
\hline\cr\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}[\hat{\rho}_{\text{BA}}]&(1-p)\Phi_{\text{BB}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}]\end{array}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq S\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}p\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]&0\\
\hline\cr 0&(1-p)\Phi_{\text{BB}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}]\end{array}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\quad=S\left(p\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]\right)+S\left((1-p)\Phi_{\text{BB}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}]\right)

\displaystyle\quad=pS\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]\right)+(1-p)S\left(\Phi_{\text{BB}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}]\right)+H_{2}(p)\;.

Here we fixed
p\equiv\mbox{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{\text{AA}}]
,
we introduced the density matrices of A and B defined as
\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}=\hat{\rho}_{\text{AA}}/p
and
\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}=\hat{\rho}_{\text{BB}}/(1-p)
, and
called
H_{2}(p)\equiv-p\log_{2}p-(1-p)\log_{2}(1-p)
the binary entropy function.
Considering then that Eq. (
4.1
) can be saturated by focusing on density matrices

\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}}
with zero off-diagonal blocks (i.e.
\hat{\rho}_{\text{AB}}=\hat{\rho}_{\text{BA}}=0
),
and using the fact that according to Eq. (
32
)

\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EC}}(\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}})
does not depend upon such terms,
Eq. (
47
) reduces to

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})=\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)

(54)

\displaystyle\qquad+\max_{\tiny{\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{A}}}}}\max_{\tiny{\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{B}}}}}J_{p}(\Phi_{\text{AA}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}},\Phi_{\text{BB}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}})\Big\}\;.

We can see that this expression involves an optimization only on the diagonal components of
\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}}
.
The functional
J_{p}
appearing in the above expression can be expressed as a rescaled convex combination of the
coherent information terms of the channels
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
. Explicitly

\displaystyle J_{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle pI_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{AA}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}})+(1-p)I_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{BB}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}})

(55)

\displaystyle-\Delta S_{p}(\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}],\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}])\;,

where for generic density matrices
\hat{\rho}^{\prime}_{\text{EE}}
and
\hat{\rho}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{EE}}
of E,
we introduced

\displaystyle\Delta S_{p}(\hat{\rho}^{\prime}_{\text{EE}},\hat{\rho}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{EE}})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle S\Big(p\hat{\rho}^{\prime}_{\text{EE}}+(1-p)\hat{\rho}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{EE}}\Big)

\displaystyle-pS\left(\hat{\rho}^{\prime}_{\text{EE}}\right)-(1-p)S\left(\hat{\rho}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{EE}}\right)\;,

which is non-negative due to the concavity of the von Neumann entropy.
Notice that by simply specifying the above expression for the extreme cases
p=1
and
p=0
one can
easily verify that Eq. (
54
) correctly complies with the bound in Eq. (
4
).
On the contrary, an upper bound for
Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})
can be obtained
by dropping
\Delta S_{p}(\hat{\rho}^{\prime}_{\text{EE}},\hat{\rho}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{EE}})
in the right-hand-side
of Eq. (
55
), leading to the following inequality

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+pQ(\Phi_{\text{AA}})+(1-p)Q(\Phi_{\text{BB}})\Big\}

(57)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{2}(2^{Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}})}+2^{Q(\Phi_{\text{BB}})})\;,

where we introduced
Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}})
and
Q(\Phi_{\text{BB}})
using the optimization over
\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}

and
\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}
, and where
in the second line we carried out the maximization over
p
. This bound makes physical sense as
it implies that the dimension of the optimal noiseless subspace of
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
cannot be larger than
the direct sum of the noise-free subspace associated with the channels
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}

when used independently. Notice also that the inequality (
57
) is saturated by taking
\Phi_{\text{CC}}

to be the identity channel.

4.2
Entanglement-assisted quantum capacity formula for PCDS channels

For the sake of completeness we report here the value of the entanglement assisted quantum capacity

Q_{E}(\Phi_{\text{XX}})

[
51
,
52
,
53
]
for the case of arbitrary (non-necessarily degradable) PCDS channels.
We remind that if we allow shared entanglement between sender and receiver the reliable transferring of quantum messages through the map
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
can be improved via teleportation. The associated improvement is captured by the following expression

\displaystyle Q_{E}(\Phi_{\text{XX}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\max_{\rho_{\text{XX}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\text{X}}}I(\Phi_{\text{XX}};\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}})\;,

(58)

where now

\displaystyle I(\Phi_{\text{XX}};\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle S(\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}})+I_{coh}(\Phi_{\text{XX}};\hat{\rho}_{\text{XX}})\;,

(59)

is the
quantum mutual information
, which being sub-additive needs no regularization even if the map
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
is not degradable.

In this case, besides Eq. (
4.1
) we also invoke the inequality

\displaystyle S(\hat{\rho}_{\text{CC}})\leq pS\left(\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\right)+(1-p)S\left(\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}\right)+H_{2}(p)\;,

that can be derived along the same line of reasoning. Replacing all this into Eq. (
58
)
we get

\displaystyle Q_{E}(\Phi_{\text{CC}})=\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\max_{\tiny{\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{A}}}}}\max_{\tiny{\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{B}}}}}I_{p}(\Phi_{\text{AA}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}},\Phi_{\text{BB}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}})\Big\}\;,

where now

\displaystyle I_{p}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle pI\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\right)+(1-p)I\left(\Phi_{\text{BB}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}\right)

(62)

\displaystyle-\Delta S_{p}(\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}],\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}])\;,

with
I\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\right)
and
I\left(\Phi_{\text{BB}};\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}\right)

the quantum mutual information functional of Eq. (
59
) of
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
respectively.
As in the case of the formula in Eq. (
54
) we can get a lower bound for it by taking
p=0,1
and an upper bound by
dropping the term
\Delta S_{p}(\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}],\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}])

in Eq. (
62
). This leads to the inequality

Q_{E}(\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{CC}})\leq Q_{E}(\Phi_{\text{CC}})\leq\log_{2}(2^{Q_{E}(\Phi_{\text{AA}})}+2^{Q_{E}(\Phi_{\text{BB}})})\;,

(63)

with
Q_{E}(\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{CC}})=\min\{Q_{E}(\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{AA}}),Q_{E}(\Phi^{(0)}_{\text{BB}})\}
as in Ref.
[
30
]
.

4.3
The special case of
\Phi_{\text{BB}}=\text{Id}_{\text{BB}}

We now focus on the special case where the diagonal block
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
of the PCDS channel
\Phi_{\text{CC}}

defined in Eq. (
16
)
corresponds to the identity map
\text{Id}_{\text{BB}}
. Under this condition
{\cal H}_{\text{B}}
is a decoherence-free subspace for the communication model. This implies that the value of

Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})
can always be lower bounded by
\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}
, a condition that is automatically
granted by the inequality (
4
), noticing that in this case
Q(\Phi_{\text{BB}})=\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}
.
Deeper insight on the model arises by observing that
from Eq. (
3
) we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]=|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|\;\mbox{Tr}_{\text{B}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{BB}}]\;,

(64)

with
|0_{\text{E}}\rangle
being an element of the orthonormal set
\{|j_{\text{E}}\rangle\}_{j}
of

{\cal H}_{\text{E}}
. Accordingly from Eq. (
55
)
we have

\displaystyle J_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle pS\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]\right)+(1-p)S\left(\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}\right)

(65)

\displaystyle-S\Big(p\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}(\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}})+(1-p)|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle pS\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]\right)+(1-p)\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}

\displaystyle-S\Big(p\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}(\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}})+(1-p)|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|\Big)\;,

the upper bound being achieved by taking as input
\hat{\tau}_{\text{BB}}
for B the completely mixed state
\hat{P}_{\text{BB}}/d_{\text{B}}
.
Hence the capacity formulas in Eqs. (
54
) and (
4.2
) now write respectively

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+(1-p)\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}

\displaystyle+\max_{\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{A}}}}\Big\{pS\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]\right)

\displaystyle-S(p\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]+(1-p)|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|)\Big\}\Big\}\;,

which holds true for all choices of CPT maps
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
that are degradable, and

\displaystyle Q_{E}(\Phi_{\text{CC}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+{(1-p)}\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\max_{\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{\text{A}}}}\Big\{pS\left(\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}\right)+pS\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]\right)

\displaystyle-S(p\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]+(1-p)|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|)\Big\}\Big\}\;,

that instead applies also for non degradable CPT maps
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
– both expressions
now involving only an optimization with respect to
\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}
and
p
.

Notice that the relatively simple expression reported in Eq. (
4.3
)
paves the way to refine a little the
lower bound discussed in Sec.
4
for general PCDS channels.
In particular, assume that there exists a density matrix
\hat{\rho}^{*}_{\text{AA}}
of A such that
the complementary channel
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}
of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{AA}}

fulfills the following identity

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[\hat{\rho}^{*}_{\text{AA}}]=|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|\;,

(68)

with
|0_{E}\rangle
being the pure vector that via Eq. (
64
) defines the action of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}
. Interestingly enough, in Appendix
B.2
we show that this special requirement can always be met if the channel

{\Phi}_{\text{AA}}
admits a fixed point state that is pure (examples of those maps are provided by the cases
studied in Sec.
5.2
and
Sec.
5.3
).
Under the hypothesis in Eq. (
68
), setting
\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}=\hat{\rho}^{*}_{\text{AA}}
in the right-end-side of
Eqs. (
4.3
) and dropping a positive term we can then arrive to the inequality

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+(1-p)\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}\Big\}

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{2}(d_{\text{B}}+1)\;.

For
\log_{2}(d_{\text{B}}+1)>Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}})
represents an improvement with respect to the general lower bound given in Eq. (
4
).
At the physical level Eq. (
69
) implies that under the condition in Eq. (
68
) the model admits the presence of a decoherence-free subspace. The dimension of this subspace is
d_{\text{B}}+1
and is slightly larger than the value
d_{\text{B}}

that is granted for free by having the block B preserved during the evolution.
An interesting consequence of Eq. (
69
) can finally be
drawn by comparing it with Eq. (
57
). Indeed in the present case, due to the fact that
Q(\Phi_{\text{BB}}=\text{Id}_{\text{BB}})=\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}
, such an upper bound reduces to

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})\leq\log_{2}(2^{Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}})}+d_{\text{B}})\;,

(70)

whose right-hand-side term exactly matches that of the lower bound of Eq. (
69
) whenever
Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}})=0
.
Putting all this together we can then arrive to the following observation

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
be a PCDS quantum channel (
16
)
with
\Phi_{\text{BB}}=\text{Id}_{\text{BB}}
. If
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
is a zero-capacity (i.e.
Q(\Phi_{\text{AA}})=0
),
degradable map admitting a pure fixed point state then we have

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{\text{CC}})=\log_{2}(d_{\text{B}}+1)\;.

(71)

Explicit examples of
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
obeying the
structural constraints imposed by the Lemma will be presented in Secs.
5.2
and
5.3
,
together with a rather important consequence of the identity in Eq. (
71
).

5
Applications

Here we report few applications of the identity in Eq. (
4.3
)
that allows us to
fully characterize the quantum capacity of a large class of nontrivial PCDS quantum channels, including some
specific examples of CPT maps which are not degradable.

5.1
Purely Dephasing channels

As a first example of PCDS channels
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
described in Sec.
4.3

we focus on the purely dephasing maps
[
54
,
55
]

\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}
of Eq. (
2
).
Accordingly in this case both

\Phi_{\text{BB}}
and
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
are the identity transformation and
we can assign the Kraus set of the model by taking the following operators

\displaystyle{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(0)}=\kappa{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}+{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}\;,\quad{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(1)}=\sqrt{1-|\kappa|^{2}}{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}\;.

Via Eq. (
3
) this leads us to Eq. (
64
) for the complementary channel

\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EB}}

and to

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\text{EA}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]=|v^{(\kappa)}_{\text{E}}\rangle\langle v^{(\kappa)}_{\text{E}}|\;\mbox{Tr}_{\text{A}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{AA}}]\;,

(73)

where now
|v_{\text{E}}\rangle
is the pure state vector

\displaystyle|v^{(\kappa)}_{\text{E}}\rangle\equiv\kappa|0_{\text{E}}\rangle+\sqrt{1-|\kappa|^{2}}|1_{\text{E}}\rangle\;.

(74)

Since in the present case
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
is the identity channel, hence degradable, we can compute the quantum capacity of

\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}
via the single letter formula in Eq. (
4.3
) which,
by trivially upper-bounding
S\left(\Phi_{\text{AA}}[\hat{\tau}_{\text{AA}}]\right)
with
\log_{2}d_{\text{A}}
,
rewrites as

\displaystyle Q(\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+p\log_{2}d_{\text{A}}+(1-p)\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}

(75)

\displaystyle-S(p|v^{(\kappa)}_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle v^{(\kappa)}_{\text{E}}|+(1-p)|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|)\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}+\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+p\log_{2}(d_{\text{A}}/d_{\text{B}})

\displaystyle-H_{2}\left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt{1-4p(1-p)(1-|\kappa|^{2})}}{2}\right)\Big\}\;.

In the limiting cases
|\kappa|=1
(no noise) and
\kappa=0
(full dephasing)
the maximization can be explicitly performed leading to the expected results of Eqs. (
45
) and (
46
),
respectively. For all the other choices of
\kappa
we resort to numerical evaluation and report the obtained results in
Fig.
1
a). Partial analytical information can however be recovered by noticing that the function we have to optimize with respect to
p
depends, apart from the noise coefficient
|\kappa|
,
only upon the ratio
d_{\text{A}}/d_{\text{B}}
. From this fact, by simple analytical considerations it follows that functions
Q(\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}})
associated with models with same value of ratio

d_{\text{A}}/d_{\text{B}}
will only differ by an additive constant.
Furthermore, in the special case where

d_{\text{A}}/d_{\text{B}}=1

the maximization
can be again carried out analytically, e.g. by noticing that the associated functional is symmetric for exchange of
p
and
1-p
:
accordingly we can conclude that in this case the optimal value for
p
is
1/2
, implying

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0ptQ(\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-H_{2}((1-|\kappa|^{2})/2)+\log_{2}(d_{\text{A}})\;,

(76)

For
d_{\text{A}}=1
this expression correctly reproduces the capacity formula of Ref.
[
41
]

for the qubit (
d_{\text{C}}=2)
dephasing channel. It’s worth noticing from Fig.
1
a) that depending on the combination of
(d_{\text{A}},d_{\text{B}})
a structure among the channels emerges. The noiseless subspace associated with
d_{\text{B}}
defines a “multiplet” of curves that converge to
\log_{2}(d_{\text{B}})
at
\kappa\sim 0
and spread with increasing
\kappa
toward the values
\log_{2}(d_{\text{A}}+d_{\text{B}})
, never intersecting each other. Intersections can take place between elements of different multiplets, as happens e.g. for the curves (3,3) and (1,4). In this case we can see that when
\kappa\gtrsim 0.75
, having 3 decohering levels and 3 noiseless performs better than having only 1 decohering level and 4 noiseless.

Similar conclusions can be drawn for the entanglement assisted capacity of
\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}
, which
from Eq. (
4.3
) we express as

\displaystyle Q_{E}(\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+{p}\log_{2}d_{\text{A}}+{(1-p)}\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}

(77)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}S(p|v^{(\kappa)}_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle v^{(\kappa)}_{\text{E}}|+(1-p)|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|)\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{2}d_{\text{B}}+\max_{p\in[0,1]}\Big\{H_{2}(p)+p\log_{2}(d_{\text{A}}/d_{\text{B}})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}H_{2}\left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt{1-4p(1-p)(1-|\kappa|^{2})}}{2}\right)\Big\}\;,

whose values are plotted in Fig.
1
b) (notice again that
for
d_{\text{A}}=d_{\text{B}}
the optimization can be performed analytically resulting in

Q_{E}(\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}})=1-\frac{1}{2}H_{2}((1-|\kappa|^{2})/2)+\log_{2}(d_{\text{A}})
).

Figure 1:
a)
Quantum capacity
Q
of the purely dephasing channel
\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}}

of Eq. (

) for some values of the couple

w.r.t. the dephasing parameter

.

.
It is worth observing that the curves associated with the same value of the ratio
d_{\text{A}}/d_{\text{B}}

differs only by an additive constant as predicted in the main text, and that the presence of the entanglement resource removes the degeneracy of
the
Q(\Delta^{(\kappa)}_{\text{CC}})
capacity for

.

5.2
Multi-level Amplitude Damping channels

As a second example we now focus on a multi-level version of the qubit Amplitude Damping channel
[
56
]
, hereafter indicated as MAD channels in brief, which describes the probability for levels of a
d_{\text{C}}
-dimensional system to decay into each other
[
40
]
.
In their most general form, given
\{|i_{\text{C}}\rangle\}_{i=0,\cdots,d_{\text{C}}-1}
an orthornormal basis for

{\cal H}_{\text{C}}
,
these maps can be assigned by introducing the set of
Kraus operators
\{\hat{M}_{\text{CC}}^{(0)}\}\bigcup\{\hat{M}_{\text{CC}}^{(ij)}\}_{i<j}
formed by the
d_{\text{C}}(d_{\text{C}}-1)/2
matrices

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\text{CC}}^{(ij)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma_{ji}}\ket{i_{\text{C}}}\!\!\bra{j_{\text{C}}},\qquad\forall i<j\;,

(78)

with
\gamma_{ji}
real quantities on the interval
[0,1]
describing the decay rate from the

6
Conclusions

We firstly introduced the new class of
Partially Coherent Direct Sum
channels. We showed that an explicit and compact formula for the quantum capacity and entanglement assisted quantum capacity is attainable, given suitable degradability conditions of the sub-blocks channels. Since for degradable channels quantum capacity
Q
and private classical capacity
C_{p}
are equivalent
[
58
]
, the degradability provides us also the latter. Since the expression of
Q_{E}
differs from the entanglement assisted classical capacity
C_{E}
just by a factor
1/2

[
51
,
52
]
, given the former we have immediately the latter. We are also able to exhibit upper and lower bounds which, in some occasions, also allow us to state exactly the quantum capacity of non degradable channels. We applied the results to instances of the purely dephasing channels, qubit ADC and combinations of the two. The choice of a qubit ADC made here though was just adopted for the sake of simplicity. The same approach can be straightforwardly applied to higher dimensions ADC when known to be degradable
[
40
]
or in general to “extend” any other finite dimensional degradable channel. The new approach is immediately generalizable to PCDS composed by
n>2
block channels. Moreover, since the maximization is reduced to sub-blocks, the overall problem complexity is considerably lower, making a large class of higher dimensional noisy channels capacities accessible.

Appendix A
Necessary and sufficient conditions for PCDS quantum channels

Here we discuss necessary and sufficient conditions for a quantum channel
\Phi_{\text{CC}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(cpt)}}_{{\text{C}}\rightarrow{\text{C}}}
to admit the PCDS block-structure defined in Eq. (
2
).
We start by observing that when introducing this special decomposition
we did not explicitly require the diagonal blocks
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
and
\Phi_{\text{BB}}
to be CPT (indeed we merely asked them to be elements of the
super-operators sets
{\cal M}_{{\text{A}}\rightarrow{\text{A}}}
and
{\cal M}_{{\text{B}}\rightarrow{\text{B}}}
). This
property however is automatically imposed by the CPT constraint on
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
– see the derivation that follows.

We now give an explicit proof of Theorem
2.1
.
First of all, assume that the element
{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}
of the Kraus set

\{{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}\}_{j}
of
\Phi_{\text{CC}}
fulfills the identity in Eq. (
17
). Accordingly for all
{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}\in{\cal{L}}_{{\text{C}}\rightarrow{\text{C}}}
we can write

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{CC}}[{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j){\dagger}}

(96)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{\text{Y}=\text{A,B}}{\hat{M}}_{\text{YY}}^{(j)}\right){\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{CC}}\left(\sum_{\text{X}=\text{A,B}}{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j){\dagger}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{\text{X,Y}=\text{A,B}}{\hat{M}}_{\text{YY}}^{(j)}{\hat{\Theta}}_{\text{YX}}{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j){\dagger}}\;,

which can be cast into the form of Eq. (
16
) with the super-operators

\Phi_{\text{AA}}
,
\Phi_{\text{AA}}
,
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}
,
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{BA}}
defined as in Eqs. (
18
) and
(
19
).
Notice in particular that with this choice, for
{\text{X}=\text{A,B}}

the diagonal component
writes

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{XX}}[\cdots]=\sum_{j}{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j)}\cdots{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j){\dagger}}\;,

(97)

with the operators
{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j)}
fulfilling the constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{j}{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j){\dagger}}{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}_{\text{XX}}\sum_{j}{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j){\dagger}}{\hat{M}}_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}{\hat{P}}_{\text{XX}}

(98)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}_{\text{XX}}\hat{I}_{\text{CC}}{\hat{P}}_{\text{XX}}={\hat{P}}_{\text{XX}}\;.

This implies that

\{{\hat{M}}_{\text{XX}}^{(j)}\}_{j}
is a proper Kraus set for a map action on X, i.e. that
\Phi_{\text{XX}}
is indeed a CPT element of
{\cal M}_{{\text{X}}\rightarrow{\text{X}}}
, as anticipated in the introduction of the
present section.

Consider now the reverse property, i.e. assume that exist

\Phi_{\text{AA}}\in{\cal M}_{{\text{A}}\rightarrow{\text{A}}}
,
\Phi_{\text{BB}}\in{\cal M}_{{\text{B}}\rightarrow{\text{B}}}
,
\Phi^{\text{(off)}}_{\text{AB}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(off)}}_{{\text{B}}\rightarrow{\text{A}}}
, and
\Phi_{\text{BA}}^{\text{(off)}}\in{\cal M}^{\text{(off)}}_{{\text{A}}\rightarrow{\text{B}}}

such that
Eq. (
2
) holds true for all possible choices of
\hat{\Theta}_{\text{CC}}\in{\cal{L}}_{{\text{C}}\rightarrow{\text{C}}}
.
Observe then that this in particular implies

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}\Phi_{\text{CC}}[{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}]{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}=\sum_{j}{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}M_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}M_{\text{CC}}^{(j){\dagger}}{\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\sum_{j}\left({\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}M_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}\right)\left({\hat{P}}_{\text{BB}}M_{\text{CC}}^{(j)}{\hat{P}}_{\text{AA}}\right)^{{\dagger}}=\sum_{j}|M_{BA}^{(j)}|^{2}\;.

Which is verified if and only if

\displaystyle{\hat{M}}_{\text{BA}}^{(j)}={\hat{M}}_{\text{AB}}^{(j)}=0\qquad\forall j\;,

(99)

or equivalently if and only if Eq. (
17
) holds true.

Appendix B
Complementary maps via Stinespring dilation

Given

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{\text{XX}}[\cdots]=\mbox{Tr}_{\text{E}}[\hat{U}_{\text{XEXE}}(\cdots\otimes|0_{\text{E}}\rangle\!\langle 0_{\text{E}}|)\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\text{XEXE}}]\;,

\mbox{Tr}_{\text{E}}[\cdots]
is the partial trace over E,
|0_{\text{E}}\rangle
a pure state vector of the Hilbert space

{\cal H}_{\text{E}}
of E, and finally

\hat{U}_{\text{XEXE}}
is
a unitary transformation on
{\cal H}_{X}\otimes{\cal H}_{\text{E}}
. For future purposes it is worth stressing that, by taking the dimensionality of
{\cal H}_{\text{E}}
to be sufficiently large, we can make sure that the dependence of the representation in Eq. (
B
) upon the specific choice of

{\Phi}_{\text{XX}}
is completely carried on by just
\hat{U}_{\text{XEXE}}

is given by the set

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}}\hat{M}_{\text{EA}}^{(j_{1}){\dagger}}\hat{M}_{\text{EA}}^{(j_{1})}+\sum_{j_{2}}\hat{M}_{\text{EB}}^{(j_{2}){\dagger}}\hat{M}_{\text{EB}}^{(j_{2})}

Generalization to the multi-block decomposition

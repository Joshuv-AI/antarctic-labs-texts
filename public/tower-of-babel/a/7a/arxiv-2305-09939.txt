Electroweak penguins and radiative B decays at Belle II
C. Praz, on behalf of the Belle II collaboration

Abstract
Decays of B B mesons involving the transition of a b b quark into an s s quark are good probes of physics beyond the standard model. Such decays are studied at the Belle II experiment, a detector located along the SuperKEKB electron-positron collider, and with data corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 189 ​ fb − 1 189\,\mathrm{fb^{-1}} collected at the energy of the Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) \Upsilon(4S) resonance. The radiative decay of a B B meson into inclusive final states involving a strange hadron and a photon ( B → X s ​ γ B\to X_{s}\gamma ) is studied by fully reconstructing the partner B B meson in a hadronic decay. The photon-energy spectrum and the branching fraction of the inclusive B → X s ​ γ B\to X_{s}\gamma decay are reported. In addition, the branching fraction of the decay of a B B meson into an excited K K meson and a pair of charged leptons ( B → K ∗ ​ ( 892 ) ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − B\to K^{\ast}(892)\ell^{+}\ell^{-} , with ℓ + ​ ℓ − \ell^{+}\ell^{-} either an electron-positron or a muon-antimuon pair) is reported. A control channel for the study of the charmless B → K ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − B\to K\ell^{+}\ell^{-} decay is the decay B → J / ψ ⁡ ( ℓ + ​ ℓ − ) ​ K B\to J/\psi(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})K , for which a lepton-flavour universality ratio is reported. All the reported measurements are in agreement with the world averages and the standard model predictions.

Introduction

The transition of a
b
quark into an
s
quark, which involves a flavour-changing neutral current, is an excellent probe of physics beyond the standard model (SM).
In the SM, flavour-changing neutral currents are forbidden at the tree level, meaning that the lowest-order Feynman diagrams contributing to a
b\to s
transition are of the loop type or the box type, as illustrated in
Figure

1
.
These loop or box diagrams have a low probability of occurrence, implying that the decays of
B
mesons based on a
b\to s
transition are rare, with branching fractions typically ranging from
\mathcal{O}(10^{-7})
to
\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})\,

[
1
]
.
In these decays, new particles or interactions may contribute to the Feynman diagrams at the same level as the SM particles and interactions, and affect the measured branching fractions and angular distributions of the final-state particles.

Decays of
B
mesons are studied by the Belle II experiment
[
2
]
, which is located along the SuperKEKB collider
[
3
]
in Tsukuba, Japan.
SuperKEKB is colliding electrons and positrons at the energy of the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance to produce pairs of
B
mesons, and Belle II is designed to detect the products of the
B
-meson decays.
Since the beginning of the data collection in March 2019, Belle II has recorded an integrated luminosity of
362\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
at the
\Upsilon(4S)
energy, which corresponds to
387\times 10^{6}
pairs of
B
mesons, with an uncertainty of the order of 1%.
The studies presented below are based on a partial data sample of
189\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
collected between 2019 and 2021.

In these studies, two important variables are derived for a given
B
-meson candidate: the beam-constrained mass (
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
) and the energy difference (
\Delta E
).
They are defined as

M_{\mathrm{bc}}\equiv\sqrt{\left(\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2}\right)^{2}-p^{\ast 2}_{B}},\hskip 28.45274pt\Delta E\equiv E^{\ast}_{B}-\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2},

(1)

where
\sqrt{s}
is the available energy in the center-of-mass system (CMS), and
E^{\ast}_{B}
and
p^{\ast}_{B}
are the energy and momentum of the
B
-meson candidate in the CMS, respectively.

2
Measurement of the photon-energy spectrum in inclusive
B\to X_{s}\gamma
decays

This section reports a study of the inclusive
B\to X_{s}\gamma
decay, where
X_{s}
denotes any final state with a net strangeness.
In addition to its sensitivity to new physics for the reasons mentioned in introduction, this decay is also sensitive to the motion of the
b
quark inside the
B
meson
[
7
]
.

The main experimental challenge is to suppress and subtract large background contributions coming from
e^{+}e^{-}\to B\overline{B}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\overline{q}
events, where
q=u,d,c,s
quarks.
The strategy for background suppression and signal selection relies on a technique called
B
-tagging, in which the partner
B
meson (
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
) in an
e^{+}e^{-}\to B\overline{B}
event is reconstructed to infer information about the signal decay.

The first step of the event selection is to reconstruct
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
candidates in hadronic decays.
An algorithm called full event interpretation (FEI
[
8
]
) uses tracks and energy deposits in the Belle II calorimeter to reconstruct final-state particle candidates and combine them to form intermediate-state particle candidates.
The FEI combines final-state and intermediate-state particle candidates to build
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
candidates in more than 30 hadronic modes.

The signal-photon candidate is reconstructed from an energy deposit in the Belle II calorimeter.
The energy of the signal-photon candidate in the signal
B
-meson frame, noted
E_{\gamma}^{B}
, is inferred from the kinematic properties of the
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
candidate and the beam energy.
Only signal-photon candidates with
E_{\gamma}^{B}>1.4\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
are considered, and the highest-energy candidate is chosen in case of presence of multiple candidates in a single event.

Background arising from
\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma
and
\eta\to\gamma\gamma
decays is suppressed with a multivariate statistical-learning classifier that combines information from the signal-photon candidate and other photon candidates in the event.
Background due to
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\overline{q}
events is suppressed with another multivariate statistical-learning classifier trained in particular with variables that are functions of the distribution of the momenta in the event.

The yield of
e^{+}e^{-}\to B\overline{B}
events with a correctly reconstructed
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
candidate is determined from a fit to the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
distribution of the
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
candidate.
The fit is done simultaneously in multiple
E_{\gamma}^{B}
intervals, one of which is shown in
Figure

2
.

Measurement of a lepton-flavour universality ratio in
B\to J/\psi K
decays

The
B\to J/\psi(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})K
decay (with
\ell=e,\mu
) serves as a control channel for the study of the charmless
B\to K\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
decay, since both decays share the same final-state particles.
The former decay is allowed at the tree-level in the SM, and has therefore a larger branching fraction than the latter.
This section reports a measurement of the lepton-flavour universality ratio
R_{K}{\left(J/\psi\right)}
, which is defined and measured as

R_{K}{\left(J/\psi\right)}\equiv\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(B\to J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})K\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(B\to J/\psi(e^{+}e^{-})K\right)}=\frac{n_{\rm sig}^{J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})K}}{n_{\rm sig}^{J/\psi(e^{+}e^{-})K}}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon^{J/\psi(e^{+}e^{-})K}}{\varepsilon^{J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})K}},

(2)

where
n_{\rm sig}^{J/\psi(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})K}
and
\varepsilon^{J/\psi(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})K}
are the number of signal candidates and the signal selection efficiency of the
B\to J/\psi(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})K
mode, respectively.

Charged pion, kaon and lepton candidates are reconstructed from the information collected by the tracking subdetectors of Belle II.
A particle identification system (PID) combining the information of multiple subdetectors allows to suppress the background from mis-identified particle candidates.
Pairs of oppositely-charged lepton candidates are used to define
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
candidates, and pair of oppositely-charged pion candidates to define
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
candidates.
Requirements on the invariant mass of the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
and
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
candidates suppress the background coming from wrong combinations.

B
-meson candidates are built by combining a
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
and a kaon candidate.

shows the yields of signal and background candidates as determined from a combined fit to

and

, defined in
Equation

1
, for the
B^{+}\to J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})K^{+}
mode.
After selection, the sample is largely dominated by signal candidates,
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
is peaking at the mass of the
B
meson, and
\Delta E
is peaking at zero.
For this mode, one important remaining background is coming from the
B^{+}\to J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})\pi^{+}
decay, where the pion is mis-identified as a kaon.

Measurement of the branching fraction of
B\to K^{\ast}(892)\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
decays

This section reports a measurement of the branching fractions of the
B\to K^{\ast}(892)\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
decays, with
\ell=e,\mu
.
These branching fractions are very small, of the order of
10^{-6}
, making their determination challenging due to the relatively large level of background.

As in
Section

3
, charged pion, kaon and lepton candidates are reconstructed from the information collected by the tracking subdetectors of Belle II and selected using their PID information.
Neutral pion candidates are reconstructed from pairs of photon candidates, and
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
candidates from pairs of oppositely-charged pion candidates.
Excited kaon candidates are reconstructed in the
K^{*0}(892)\to K^{+}\pi^{-}
,
K^{*+}(892)\to K^{+}\pi^{0}
and
K^{*+}(892)\to K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}\pi^{+}
modes, and
B
-meson candidates are built by combining a
K^{*}(892)
candidate and a pair of oppositely-charged lepton candidates.

Background coming from
B\to{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{*}(892)
,
B\to\psi(2S)K^{*}(892)
and
B\to\gamma K^{*}(892)
decays is suppressed by vetoing regions of the di-lepton invariant mass.
In addition, a multivariate statistical-learning classifier is trained on simulated events to suppress the background coming from non-resonant
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\overline{q}
events, where
q=u,d,c,s
quarks.

Similarly to what is done in
Section

3
, the signal and background yields are determined from a combined fit to
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
and
\Delta E
, defined in
Equation

1
.

Figure

shows the result of the fit in
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
for the electron and muon modes.
The remaining background mainly comes from mis-reconstructed
e^{+}e^{-}\to B\overline{B}
events.

Summary

The result presented in
Section

2
and detailed in Ref.
[
4
]
is the first measurement of the inclusive
b\to s\gamma
transition with the hadronic-tagging method by the Belle II experiment.
This measurement is competitive with the only other hadronic-tagging measurement from Ref.
[
9
]
by the BaBar experiment.
The results of the studies of
B\to J/\psi(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})K
and
B\to K^{\ast}(892)\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
decays, presented in
Sections

3
and
4
, and detailed in Refs.
[
5
,
6
]
, are in agreement with the world averages; these results show a good and similar identification of electrons and muons by the Belle II experiment and prepare the ground for precision measurements of rare decays when more data will be collected.

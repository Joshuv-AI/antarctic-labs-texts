Models, metrics, and their formulas for typical electric power system resilience events
Ian Dobson, Iowa State University

Abstract
Poisson process models are defined in terms of their rates for outage and restore processes in power system resilience events. These outage and restore processes easily yield the performance curves that track the evolution of resilience events, and the area, nadir, and duration of the performance curves are standard resilience metrics. This letter analyzes typical resilience events by analyzing the area, nadir, and duration of mean performance curves. Explicit and intuitive formulas for these metrics are derived in terms of the Poisson process model parameters, and these parameters can be estimated from utility data. This clarifies the calculation of metrics of typical resilience events, and shows what they depend on. The metric formulas are derived with lognormal, exponential, or constant rates of restoration. The method is illustrated with a typical North American transmission event. Similarly nice formulas are obtained for the area metric for empirical power system data.

II
Area and nadir metrics

This section starts by giving general formulas for the area of the mean performance curve.
This area (regarded as a positive area by including the minus sign) is

\displaystyle\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muA\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muO\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)-\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)]dt

(3)

Moreover,
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muA\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu
is also the mean of the area
A
of the performance curve
P(t)
, since

\displaystyle\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muA\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu=-\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}P(t)dt=-{\rm E}\int_{0}^{\infty}P(t)dt={\rm E}A

(4)

Integrating (
3
) by parts, and using
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(0)=\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(\infty)=0
and
\lambda_{O}(t)=\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muO^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
,
\lambda_{R}(t)=\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muR^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
gives the very nice formulas

\displaystyle\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muA\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu

\displaystyle=\!-\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\hskip-8.53581ptt{\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu^{\prime}\!}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\hskip-8.53581pt[t\lambda_{R}(t)-t\lambda_{O}(t)]dt={\rm E}[\lambda_{R}]-{\rm E}[\lambda_{O}]

\displaystyle=n_{c}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hskip-8.53581pt[tf_{r}(t)-tf_{o}(t)]dt=n_{c}(\overline{r}-\overline{o})

(5)

According to (
3
),
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muA\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu
is also the area between the mean outage and mean restore processes (see Fig.
2
), so that (
5
) can be understood as the height
n_{c}
of this area times its average width.

This section now defines the nadir and observes where it occurs.
The nadir
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muN\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu
of
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
is the maximum mean number of elements simultaneously outaged during the event, or the negative of the minimum value of
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
:

\displaystyle\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muN\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu=-\min\{\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t),\ t\geq 0\}

(6)

Simulations and frameworks of resilience
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

often make the idealization that outages end before the restores start so that
r_{a}\geq o_{b}
.
Then
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
is decreasing for

, constant in
[o_{b},r_{a}]
, and increasing for
t>r_{a}
.
Therefore the nadir
occurs at all the times in
[o_{b},r_{a}]
and is simply
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muN\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu=n_{c}
.
For example, when
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
is a trapezoid, the nadir occurs along the bottom of the trapezoid.
However, in real data
[
6
,
5
,
4
,
7
]
the restores usually start before the end of the outages.
Accordingly, the next paragraph and section
III
assume that
r_{a}<o_{b}
.

Since
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)=0
and
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muO\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
is increasing for
t<r_{a}
, and

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muO\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)=n_{c}
and
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
is increasing for

, the nadir
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muN\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu
of
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)=\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)-\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muO\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
must occur inside the time interval
[r_{a},o_{b}]
or at its endpoints.

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)
is a smooth function in
[r_{a},o_{b}]
.

III
Metric formulas for different cases

III-A

Constant rate outage and restore processes

The simplest model has outage and restore processes
with constant rates

\lambda_{O}
and

\lambda_{R}
respectively.
Then

\displaystyle\!\!\lambda_{O}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{c}}{o_{b}}\mbox{ and }\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muO\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)=\lambda_{O}t,\ 0\leq t<o_{b}

(7)

\displaystyle\!\!\lambda_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{c}}{r_{b}-r_{a}}\mbox{ and }\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)=\lambda_{R}(t-r_{a}),\ r_{a}\leq t\leq r_{b}

(8)

The mean area
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muA\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu
is obtained using (
5
) with the mean restore time
\overline{r}=\tfrac{1}{2}(r_{a}+r_{b})
and mean outage time
\overline{o}=\tfrac{1}{2}o_{b}
:

\displaystyle\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muA\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu

\displaystyle=n_{c}\,(\tfrac{1}{2}(r_{a}+r_{b})-\tfrac{1}{2}o_{b})

(9)

To compute the nadir
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muN\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu
, which always occurs in
[r_{a},o_{b}]
, note that

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)=\lambda_{R}-\lambda_{O}
in
[r_{a},o_{b}]
.
Therefore, if
\lambda_{R}>\lambda_{O}
then
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)>0
and
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muN\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu=\lambda_{O}r_{a}=n_{c}r_{a}/o_{b}
occurs at time
r_{a}
. Or if

\lambda_{R}<\lambda_{O}
then
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)<0
and
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muN\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu=n_{c}-\lambda_{R}(o_{b}-r_{a})=n_{c}(r_{b}-o_{b})/(r_{b}-r_{a})
occurs at time
o_{b}
.

The restore duration can be measured by

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muD\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{n}=r_{b}-r_{a}
.

III-B

Lognormal rate restore process

Consider the case of constant rate outage process (

)

\displaystyle=((t-r_{a})\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\exp[-(\ln(t-r_{a})-\mu)^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})]

\displaystyle=n_{c}\,(r_{a}+e^{\mu+\sigma^{2}/2}-o_{b}/2)

\displaystyle\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t_{\rm inflect})

\displaystyle=n_{c}(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\exp[-\mu+\sigma^{2}/2]-n_{c}/o_{b}

Write

for a local minimum of

. Then

,
and some algebra

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptt_{*}

\displaystyle=r_{a}+\exp\!\big[\mu-\sigma^{2}-\sigma\sqrt{\sigma^{2}-2\mu-2\ln[\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}/o_{b}]}\,\big]\hskip-4.2679pt

\displaystyle=r_{a}+\exp\!\big[\mu-\sigma^{2}-\sigma\sqrt{2\ln[1\!+\!(o_{b}/n_{c})\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t_{\rm inflect})]}\,\big]\hskip-4.2679pt

The negative sign for the square root in (

) is chosen to obtain the solution in the convex part of
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)

A real solution for

exists if
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t_{\rm inflect})\geq 0
.

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(r_{a})=-\lambda_{O}<0

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime\prime}\!\!\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu\,\,(t)\geq 0

due to convexity.

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t_{\rm inflect})\leq 0
, then, since
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime\prime}\!\!\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu\,\,(t)\leq 0
in
[t_{\rm inflect},o_{b}]
,
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)\leq 0
in

[r_{a},o_{b}]

.
If
\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP^{\prime}\!\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t_{\rm inflect})>0

\mkern 3.0mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu(t)

\displaystyle=n_{c}\,(r_{a}+\tau-o_{b}/2)

IV
Area
A
for empirical data

Similar methods apply to analyzing the data recorded from an actual event, except that
O(t)
,
R(t)
,
P(t)

are now actual instead of their means. This section analyzes the area
A
of
P(t)
.
Suppose the outages occur at times
o_{1}\leq...\leq o_{n}
, with quantities
c_{1},...,c_{n}
. Restores
occur at
r_{1}\leq...\leq r_{n}
, with quantities
c_{\pi(1)},...,c_{\pi(n)}
, where

1.
Introduction

We assume throughout the paper that
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the variable exponents p and q are continuous functions defined on
\Omega
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, satisfying

(p1)

1\leq p^{-}:=inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\leq sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)=:p^{+}<\infty

(q1)

-\infty<q^{-}:=inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}q(x)\leq sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}q(x)=:q^{+}<\infty
.

The following two conditions on
p
and
q
will also be used which, in literature, are known as the log-Hölder continuous and the log-log-Hölder continuous respectively:

(p2)

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C}{\log(e+1/|x-y|)}
whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(q2)

|q(x)-q(y)|\leq\frac{C}{\log(e+\log(e+1/|x-y|))}
whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For the variable exponent Sobolev space
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega),
the Sobolev-type (continuous) embedding
W^{1,p(\cdot)}\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
was established in
[
Die04
]
for bounded domains with locally Lipschitz boundary, with the condition
(p2)
on the exponent
p.

For Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, Sobolev-type embedding have been studied in
[
AC24
,
Fan12
,
HMOS10
,
MOS18
]
. In this paper, we concentrate on the class of functions
\Phi(x,t):=t^{p(x)}{(\log(e+t))}^{q(x)}
. For this class of functions
\Phi(x,t)
the following embedding was established in
[
HMOS10
]
for the space
W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
HMOS10
]
)
.

Let p satisfy (p1), (p2) and q satisfy (q1), (q2). If
p^{+}<n
then for every
u\in W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega),

||u||_{L^{\Psi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}\leq C||u||_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)},

where
\Phi(x,t):=t^{p(x)}{(\log(e+t))}^{q(x)}
and
\Psi(x,t):=t^{p^{*}(x)}{(\log(e+t))}^{q(x)p^{*}(x)/p(x)}.
Here
p^{*}(x)
denotes the Sobolev conjugate of
p(x),
that is
1/p^{*}(x)=1/p(x)-1/n
.

Here we establish the embedding for
W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)
for bounded domains
\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open and bounded set with Lipschitz boundary so that
\diam(\Omega)>0
. Suppose that the exponent
p:\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty)
satisfies both log-Hölder continuous and Nekvinda’s decay condition with
1\leq p^{-}\leq p^{+}<n
and the exponent
q:\Omega\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty)
is log-log-Hölder continuous with
p^{+}+q^{+}\geq 1
. Consider
\Phi(x,t):=t^{p(x)}(\log(e+t))^{q(x)}
and
\Psi(x,t):=t^{p^{*}(x)}(\log(e+t))^{q(x)p^{*}(x)/p(x)},

where
1/p^{*}(x)=1/p(x)-1/n
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that whenever
u\in W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega),

(1.1)

||u||_{L^{\Psi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}\leq C||u||_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

For the necessary part, it was shown in
[
GKP21
,
GKP23
]
that
\Omega
must satisfy the measure density condition to have the embedding
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*}}(\Omega),
if
p
satisfies the log-Hölder condition. Note that
\Omega
satisfies measure density condition if there exists a constant
c>0
such that for every
x
in
\bar{\Omega}
and each
R
in
]0,1/2],
one has
|B_{R}(x)\cap\Omega|\geq cR^{n}.
This condition was first appeared as a necessary condition for Sobolev embedding in
[
HaKT08
]
and later appeared as the same for other Sobolev-type embeddings as well,
[
AYY22
,
AYY24
,
Kar19
,
Kar20
]
. Recently, a weaker version of the measure density condition, namely log-measure density condition, has appeared in
[
HK22
]
as a necessary condition of certain Orlicz-Sobolev embedding and also in
[
GKP23
]
as a necessary condition of Sobolev-type embedding of
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
if
p
is log-log-Hölder continuous on
\Omega.

Definition 1.3
.

A subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to satisfy the log
s
-measure density condition if there exist two positive constants
c
and
\alpha
such that
for every
x
in
\bar{\Omega}
and each
R
in
]0,1/2]
one has

cR^{s}(\log(\frac{1}{R}))^{-\alpha}\leq|B_{R}(x)\cap\Omega|.

If
s=n
, one says that
\Omega
satisfies the log-measure density condition.

Here we prove that if the embedding holds, then
\Omega
satisfies log-measure density condition.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n},

\Phi(x,t):=t^{p(x)}{(\log(e+t))}^{q(x)}
and
\Psi(x,t):=t^{p^{*}(x)}{(\log(e+t))}^{q(x)p^{*}(x)/p(x)}
with
{p^{-}+q^{-}}\geq 1,

where
p^{*}(x)
denotes the Sobolev conjugate of
p(x),
that is,
1/p^{*}(x)=1/p(x)-1/n
. Suppose that

1.

The exponent
p(\cdot)
is log-Hölder continuous with
p^{+}<n
,

2.

W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\Psi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega).

Then
\Omega
satisfies the log-measure density condition.

Note that, we do not require the log-log-Hölder continuity of
q
here, unlike Theorem
1.2
. On the other hand, the condition
{p^{-}+q^{-}}\geq 1
implies the condition
{p^{+}+q^{+}}\geq 1
of Theorem
1.2
, both of which are trivial if
q^{-}\geq 0.

2.
Notations and Preliminary Results

Definition 2.1
.

A function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is almost increasing if there exists a constant
a\geq 1
such that
f(s)\leq af(t)
for all
0<s<t.
Similarly, a function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is almost decreasing if there exists a constant
b\geq 1
such that
f(s)\geq bf(t)
for all
0<s<t.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
p,q>0
.We say that f satisfies

(i)

(Inc)_{p}
if
f(t)/t^{p}
is increasing;

(ii)

(aInc)_{p}
if
f(t)/t^{p}
is almost increasing;

(iii)

(Dec)_{q}
if
f(t)/t^{q}
is decreasing;

(iv)

(aDec)_{q}
if
f(t)/t^{q}
is almost decreasing.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that the exponent
p(\cdot)
satisfies the log-Hölder decay condition if there exist
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
and a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\log(e+|x|)}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that the exponent
p(\cdot)
satisfies Nekvinda’s decay condition, if there exists
c_{1}\in(0,1)
and
p_{\infty}\in[1,\infty]
such that

\int_{(p(x)\not=p_{\infty})}c_{1}^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}dx<\infty
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite, complete measure space. A function
\Phi:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is said to be a (generalized)
\Phi
-prefunction on
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
if
x\rightarrow\Phi(x,|f(x)|)
is measurable for every
f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mu)
and
\Phi(x,.)
is a
\Phi
-prefunction for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\Omega
. We say that the
\Phi
-prefunction
\Phi
is a (generalized weak)
\Phi
-function if it satisfies
(aInc)_{1}
.
The sets of generalized weak
\Phi
-function is denoted by
\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
satisfies
(A0)
if there exits a constant
\beta\in(0,1]
such that

\beta\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,1)\leq{1/\beta}
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\Omega
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
. We say that
\Phi
satisfies (A1) if there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

\beta\Phi^{-1}(x,t)\leq\Phi^{-1}(y,t)

for every
t\in[1,\frac{1}{|B|}]
, almost every
x,y\in B\cap\Omega
and every ball
B
with
|B|\leq 1
.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
satisfies
(A2)
if for every
s>0
there exists
\beta\in(0,1]
and
h\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\beta\Phi^{-1}(x,t)\leq\Phi^{-1}(y,t)

for almost every
x,y\in\Omega
and every
t\in[h(x)+h(y),s]
.

Here we prove an elementary result regarding a class of functions satisfying the above conditions.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
p(\cdot)
satisfies
(p1),(p2)
and Nekvinda’s decay condition and
q(\cdot)
satisfies
(q1),(q2)

then the function
\Phi(x,t):=t^{p(x)}{(\log(e+t))}^{q(x)}
satisfies
(A0),(A1),(A2)
and
(Dec)_{p^{+}+q^{+}}
.

Proof.

Case-1 (
q(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\Omega
):
It is easy to verify the existence of constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

\frac{c_{2}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}}{(\log(e+t))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,t)\leq\frac{c_{1}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}}{(\log(e+t))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}

and hence

\frac{c_{2}}{2^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}}\leq\frac{c_{2}}{(\log(e+1))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,1)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{(\log(e+1))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq c_{1}.

Now we can choose
c_{1}\geq 1
so that
c_{1}c_{2}\geq 2^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}
. Then (A0) follows by choosing
\beta=\frac{1}{c_{1}}.

Case-2 (
q(x)=-r(x)
with
r(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\Omega
): It is easy to verify the existence of constants
c_{3}
and
c_{4}
such that

\frac{c_{4}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}}{(\log(e+t))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,t)\leq\frac{c_{3}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}}{(\log(e+t))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}

and hence

c_{4}\leq\frac{c_{4}}{(\log(e+1))^{\frac{-r(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,1)\leq\frac{c_{3}}{(\log(e+1))^{\frac{-r(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq c_{3}2^{\frac{r^{+}}{p^{-}}}=\frac{c_{3}}{2^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{-}}}}.

Now we can choose
c_{4}\leq 1
so that
c_{3}c_{4}\leq 2^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{-}}}
. Then (A0) follows by choosing
\beta={c_{4}}.

Case-3 (
q(x)<0
for some
x\in\Omega
and
q(x)\geq 0
for some
x\in\Omega
): It is easy to verify the existence of constants
c_{5}
and
c_{6}
such that

\frac{c_{6}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}}{(\log(e+t))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,t)\leq\frac{c_{5}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}}{(\log(e+t))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}

and hence

\frac{c_{6}}{2^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}}\leq\frac{c_{6}}{(\log(e+1))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,1)\leq\frac{c_{5}}{(\log(e+1))^{\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}}\leq\frac{c_{5}}{\log^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}(e+1)}.

Now we can choose
c_{6}\leq 1
so that
c_{5}c_{6}\leq 2^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}\log^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}(e+1)
. Then (A0) follows by choosing
\beta=\frac{c_{6}}{2^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}}.

So all the cases of (A0) are done.

To show condition (A1), by symmetry, we may assume that
p(x)<p(y)
. If
t\in[1,\frac{1}{|B|}]
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi^{-1}(x,t)}{\Phi^{-1}(y,t)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p(y)}}(\log(e+t))^{\frac{q(y)}{p(y)}-\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}}\leq c_{1}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p(y)}}(\log(e+t))^{|\frac{q(y)}{p(y)}-\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}t^{\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p(y)}}(\log(e+t))^{|q(y)-q(x)|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}|B|^{\frac{-c}{\log(e+\frac{1}{|x-y|})}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|B|}))^{\frac{C}{\log(e+\log(e+\frac{1}{|x-y|}))}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}e^{\frac{cn\log{\frac{1}{|x-y|}}}{\log(e+\frac{1}{|x-y|})}}{e^{\frac{c_{0}\log(\log(e+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}))}{\log(e+\log(e+\frac{1}{|x-y|}))}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}e^{cn}e^{c_{0}+\frac{c_{0}\ln n}{\ln(\ln(e+1))}}.

This yields that
\beta\Phi^{-1}(x,t)\leq\Phi^{-1}(y,t)
where
\frac{1}{\beta}=c_{1}e^{cn}e^{c_{0}+\frac{c_{0}\ln n}{\ln(\ln(e+1))}}
and hence (A1) follows.

To show condition (A2), first note that since the function
p(\cdot)
satisfies Nekvinda’s decay condition, so there exists
c_{1}\in(0,1)
and
p_{\infty}\in[1,\infty]
such that

\int_{(p(x)\not=p_{\infty})}c_{1}^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}dx<\infty
.

Case-1 (
q(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\Omega
): For this case, take
s=1,

\phi_{\infty}(t)=t^{p_{\infty}}
and
\beta\leq 1.
Note that
\phi_{\infty}(t)\leq 1
implies
t\leq 1
. We will consider two cases. In the points where
p(x)<p_{\infty},
by Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\Phi(x,\beta t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log^{q^{+}}(e+1)\beta^{p(x)}t^{p(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{p(x)}{p_{\infty}}t^{p_{\infty}}+\frac{p_{\infty}-p(x)}{p_{\infty}}(\log^{q^{+}}(e+1))^{\frac{p_{\infty}}{p_{\infty}-p(x)}}\beta^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi_{\infty}(t)+(\beta\log^{q^{+}}(e+1))^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}.

Let us take

\beta<c_{1}[\log^{q^{+}}(e+1)]^{-1},

h(x)=(\beta[\log^{q^{+}}(e+1)])^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}
,

where
c_{1}\in(0,1)
is the constant of Nekvinda’s decay condition of

Then we have that

. In the points where
p(x)\geq p_{\infty},
by taking same choice of
\beta

\Phi(x,\beta t)\leq\log^{q^{+}}(e+1)\beta t^{p_{\infty}}\leq\phi_{\infty}(t)\leq\phi_{\infty}(t)+h(x).

We do the other inequality similarly. In the points where
p(x)\leq p_{\infty},
as

\phi_{\infty}(\beta t)\leq t^{p(x)}\leq\Phi(x,t)\leq\Phi(x,t)+h(x),

and in the points where

\phi_{\infty}(\beta t)\leq\frac{p_{\infty}}{p(x)}t^{p(x)}+\frac{{p(x)}-p_{\infty}}{{p(x)}}\beta^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}\leq\Phi(x,t)+h(x),

which proves that
\Phi(x,t)
satisfies
(A2)^{\prime}.

Case-2 (
q(x)=-r(x)
with
r(x)\geq 0
for all

\phi_{\infty}(t)=t^{p_{\infty}}

Note that

\displaystyle\beta^{p(x)}t^{p(x)}\leq\frac{p(x)}{p_{\infty}}t^{p_{\infty}}+\frac{{p_{\infty}}-p(x)}{{p_{\infty}}}\beta^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}\leq\phi_{\infty}(t)+h(x)

where

\Phi(x,\beta t)=\log^{q(x)}(e+\beta t)\beta^{p(x)}t^{p(x)}\leq t^{p(x)}\leq\phi_{\infty}(t)\leq\phi_{\infty}(t)+h(x).

Case-3 (

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
and let
\rho_{\Phi}
be given by

\rho_{\Phi}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|f(x)|)d\mu(x)

for all
f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mu)
. The function
\rho_{\Phi}
is called a modular. The set

L^{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega,\mu)
:=
\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mu):\rho_{\Phi}(\lambda f)<\infty
for some
\lambda>0\}

is called a generalized Orlicz space or Musielak-Orlicz (M-O) space.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
. The function
u\in L^{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}\cap L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
belongs to Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)
if its weak partial derivatives
\delta_{\alpha}u
exist and belong to
L^{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)
for all
|\alpha|\leq 1
. We define a semimodular on
W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)
by

\rho_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}(u):=\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq 1}\rho_{\Phi}(\delta_{\alpha}u)
.

It induces a (quasi-) norm

||u||_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\}
.

Here we prove three lemmas to estimate the norm of the characteristic function of a measurable set, considering three different sets of values of
q.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\Phi:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be given by
\Phi(x,t):=t^{p(x)}(\log(e+t))^{q(x)}
with
q(x)\geq 0
for all
x
and
A\subset\Omega
is a measurable set. Then

(2.1)

\min\{|A|^{\frac{1}{p_{A}^{+}}},|A|^{\frac{1}{p_{A}^{-}}}\}\leq\|1_{A}\|_{L^{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}\leq\max\{|A|^{\frac{1}{p_{A}^{+}}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A|}))^{q_{A}^{+}},|A|^{\frac{1}{p_{A}^{-}}}(\log(1+e))^{q_{A}^{+}}\},

Proof.

We start with the proof of the second inequality of (
2.1
). Let
u>|A|
and assume first that
u\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle A$}}$}}\nolimits\Phi\bigg(x,\frac{1}{u^{\frac{1}{p_{A}^{+}}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{u}))^{q_{A}^{+}}}\bigg)dx

\displaystyle=

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded set with Lipschitz boundary. Let

for all

\rho
is concave for
t\geq 0
and
\rho(t)\rightarrow 0
for
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Then there exists an extension
p_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
p
and a constant

, such that

|p_{1}(x)-p_{1}(y)|\leq\rho(C|x-y|)
for all
x,y\in\Omega

Moreover, there holds
p_{1}^{-}=p^{-}
and
p_{1}^{+}=p^{+}
.

Proof.

Let
{V_{j}},j=1,...,k,
be the covering of the boundary
\partial\Omega
which corresponds to the local description of
\partial\Omega
. More precisely, for each
j=1,...,k,
there is a local coordinate system
(x^{\prime},x_{n})
such that

V_{j}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n}):|x_{i}|<\delta,i=1,...,n-1,{a_{j}}(x^{\prime})-\beta<x_{n}<{a_{j}}(x^{\prime})+\beta\},

Then the

are bi-Lipschitz mappings.
To these flattened domains
{{T_{j}}^{-1}}(V_{j})
, define the reflection operator

f(x^{\prime},x_{n})\quad for\quad x_{n}<0\end{cases}

and the functions

preserving their upper and lower bounds. Note that this extension is possible due to McShane
[

and the fact that

. Define

To continue the proof of Theorem
1.4
, construct the sequence

by setting

inductively for

.

Case-1 (
q(x)\geq 0
for all

): Using Lemma
3.2
one obtains

\displaystyle R_{i}-R_{i+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}|A_{R_{i}}|^{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p_{A_{R_{i}}}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{A_{R_{i}}}^{-}}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R_{i}}|}))^{q_{A_{R_{i}}}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}|A_{R_{i}}|^{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{-}}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R_{i}}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}\frac{|A_{R}|^{\eta_{R}}}{2^{i\eta_{R}}}(\log(e+\frac{2^{i}}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}},

where
\eta_{R}:=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{-}}.
Since we have
(\log(e+\frac{2^{i}}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}\leq i^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}(\log(e+\frac{2}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}\leq 2^{q^{+}}i^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}
for
i\geq 1,
we obtain

\displaystyle R_{i}-R_{i+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}\frac{i^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}|A_{R}|^{\eta_{R}}}{2^{i\eta_{R}}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}},

where
c_{3}={C_{1}}2^{q^{+}}.

Note that
\eta_{R}\geq\eta:=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}-\frac{1}{p^{-}}>0
and by integral test
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{i^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}}{2^{-i\eta_{R}}}\leq\frac{(q_{A_{R}}^{+})!}{(\eta_{R}\ln 2)^{(q_{A_{R}}^{+}+1)}}
and hence

\displaystyle R=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(R_{i}-R_{i+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}|A_{R}|^{\eta_{R}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{i^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}}{2^{-i\eta_{R}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}|A_{R}|^{\eta_{R}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}\Big(\frac{(q_{A_{R}}^{+})!}{(\eta_{R}\ln 2)^{(q_{A_{R}}^{+}+1)}}+1\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}|A_{R}|^{\eta_{R}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}\Big(\frac{(q_{A_{R}}^{+})!}{(\eta\ln 2)^{(q_{A_{R}}^{+}+1)}}+1\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}|A_{R}|^{\eta_{R}}(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R}|}))^{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}\Big(\frac{(q^{+})!}{({\min(1,\eta)}\ln 2)^{(q^{+}+1)}}+1\Big)

Moreover, since
c_{4}:=1/\max\Big\{1,\Big(\frac{c_{3}(q^{+})!}{({\min(1,\eta)}\ln 2)^{(q^{+}+1)}}+c_{3}\Big)\Big\}\leq 1
one has

(3.4)

\displaystyle|A_{R}|(\log(e+\frac{1}{|A_{R}|}))^{\frac{q_{A_{R}}^{+}}{\eta_{R}}}\geq c_{4}^{1/\eta_{R}}R^{1/\eta_{R}}\geq c_{4}^{1/\eta}R^{1/\eta_{R}}=c_{4}^{1/\eta}R^{n}R^{\beta_{R}/\eta_{R}},

where
\beta_{R}:=1-n\eta_{R}
.

Now, we would like to find a constant
\tilde{\eta}>0
, independent of
x
and
R
, such that
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{-}}=:\eta_{R}\geq\tilde{\eta}>0
for all
R\leq r_{0}.
Towards this end, the log-Hölder continuity of
p
gives, for any

and

in
A_{R},

|\frac{1}{p(z)}-\frac{1}{p(y)}|\leq\frac{C_{\text{log}}}{\log(e+1/|z-y|)},

and taking the supremum over all pairs of points in
A_{R}
one gets

(3.5)

\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{A_{R}}^{+}}\leq\frac{C_{\text{log}}}{\log(1/(2R))}.

Suppose now that for some

we have that
\eta_{R}\leq 0
. Then (
3.5
) gives

\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{C_{\text{log}}}{\log\left(\frac{1}{2R}\right)}
,
which further implies

R\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{-sC_{\text{log}}}
.

Hence we have the following conclusion:

•

If
\frac{1}{2}e^{-sC_{\text{log}}}>\frac{1}{4}
, then there is no
R\leq\frac{1}{4}
for which
\eta_{R}\leq 0
.

•

If
\frac{1}{2}e^{-sC_{\text{log}}}\leq\frac{1}{4}
, then
\eta_{R}\leq 0
implies
R\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{-sC_{\text{log}}}.

Therefore if we choose

(3.6)

\frac{1}{s}>\frac{C_{\text{log}}}{\log(1/(2r_{0}))}.

for all

. This is our desired

Therefore, from equation (

) one sees that if a positive lower bound for

1.4

is finished. To achieve such a lower bound, we see that from the log-Hölder continuity of

|p(z)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C_{\text{log}}}{\log(e+1/|z-y|)};

taking the supremum over pairs of points in
A_{R}
one gets

therefore

(3.8)

. So,
\Omega
satisfies the log-measure density condition.

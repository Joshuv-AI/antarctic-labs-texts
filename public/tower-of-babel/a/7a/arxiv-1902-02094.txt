Diophantine approximation on curves
Mumtaz Hussain, Johannes Schleischitz, David Simmons

Abstract
. Let g g be a dimension function. The Generalised Baker-Schmidt Problem (1970) concerns the g g -dimensional Hausdorff measure ( ℋ g \mathcal{H}^{g} -measure) of the set of Ψ \Psi -approximable points on nondegenerate manifolds. The problem relates the ‘size’ of the set of Ψ \Psi -approximable points with the convergence or divergence of a certain series. In the dual approximation setting, the divergence case has been established by Beresnevich-Dickinson-Velani (2006) for any nondegenerate manifold. The convergence case, however, represents a major challenging open problem and progress thus far has been effectuated in limited cases only. In this paper, we discuss and prove several results on ℋ g \mathcal{H}^{g} -measure on Veronese curves in any dimension n n . As a consequence of one of our results, we generalize recent results of Pezzoni [Acta Arith. 193 (2020), no. 3, 269–281] regarding n = 3 n=3 . This improvement evolves from a deeper investigation on general irreducibility considerations applicable in arbitrary dimension. We further investigate the ℋ g \mathcal{H}^{g} -measure for convergence on planar curves. We show that the monotonicity assumption on a multivariable approximating function cannot be removed for planar curves.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Psi:\mathbb{N}\to{[0,\infty)}
be a function such that
\Psi(q)\to 0
as
q\to\infty
. Khintchine’s theorem (1924) is a fundamental result which states that, if
\Psi
is decreasing, the Lebesgue measure of the set of
\Psi
-approximable numbers

W(\Psi):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x-p/q|<\Psi(q)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ (p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}\}

is either zero or full if the sum
\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q\Psi(q)
converges or diverges, respectively, see
[
8
]
for a statement of the modern version of this theorem. The monotonicity of
\Psi
is only needed
for the divergence part. In fact, Duffin–Schaeffer (1941) constructed a counterexample to show that without the monotonicity assumption Khintchine’s theorem is false. However, they conjectured a refined statement that holds
for non-monotonic
\Psi
as well, which has been recently affirmatively proved by Koukoulopoulos–Maynard
[
29
]
.

The higher dimensional theory of Diophantine approximation splits into two different types: the simultaneous and the dual.
Simultaneous Diophantine approximation comprises the component-wise approximation of points
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
n
-tuples of rational points
\{\mathbf{p}/q:(\mathbf{p},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}\}
, whereas dual Diophantine approximation consists of the approximation of points
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by ‘rational’ hyperplanes of the form
q_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}x_{n}=p
, where
(p,\mathbf{q})=(p,q_{1},\ldots q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
.
The metrical theory for these problems date back to
Khintchine, Jarník, Groshev, and remains to this day a prestigious, well-studied topic in Diophantine approximation.
In this paper we are concerned with a problem in dual approximation.

Notation.

Throughout, unless otherwise specified, let
\Psi:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)
be a decreasing function such that

\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)\rightarrow~0\text{ as }\|\mathbf{q}\|:=\max(|q_{1}|,\ldots,|q_{n}|)\rightarrow~\infty,

referred to as an
approximating function
. By a dimension function
g
we mean an increasing continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
g(0)=0
. By
\mathcal{H}^{g}
-measure, we mean the
g
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and when
g(r)=r^{s}
we
denote
\mathcal{H}^{g}
by
\mathcal{H}^{s}
.
If
s
is an integer
then
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is proportional to the standard
s
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
We refer to Section
4
below for a brief introduction to Hausdorff measure. For real quantities
A,B
and a parameter
t
, we write
A\ll_{t}B
if
A\leq c(t)B
for a constant
c(t)>0
that depends on
t
only (while
A
and
B
may depend on other parameters). We write
A\asymp_{t}B
if
A\ll_{t}B\ll_{t}A
.
If the constant
c>0
is absolute, we simply write
A\ll B
and
A\asymp B
.

1.1.
Dual Diophantine approximation.

We consider the dual approximation
problem with respect to approximating function
\Psi
. Concretely, we are concerned with the set

\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi):=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\begin{array}[]{l}|q_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}x_{n}+p|<\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)\\[4.30554pt]
\text{for i.m.}\ (p,q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\end{array}\right\},

where ‘i.m.’ stands for ‘infinitely many’. A vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be called
\Psi
-approximable
if it lies in the set
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)
. The following statement is the most modern result which relates the ‘size’ of the set
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)
in terms of
\mathcal{H}^{g}
-measure to the convergence or divergence of a certain series. This result encompasses contributions from many authors but most importantly works of Jarník
[
27
]
, Schmidt
[
33
]
, Dickinson–Velani
[
18
]
, and Beresnevich–Velani
[
10
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Psi
be an approximating function. Let
g
be a dimension function such that
r^{-n}g(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0.
Assume that
r\mapsto r^{-n}g(r)
is decreasing and
r\mapsto r^{1-n}g(r)
is increasing. Then

\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&{\rm if}\quad\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\|\mathbf{q}\|^{n}\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)^{1-n}g\left(\frac{\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)}{\|\mathbf{q}\|}\right)<\infty.\\[12.91663pt]
\infty&{\rm if}\quad\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\|\mathbf{q}\|^{n}\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)^{1-n}g\left(\frac{\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)}{\|\mathbf{q}\|}\right)=\infty\text{ and $\Psi$ is decreasing or $n\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

The conditions on the approximating function and dimension function comes into play only for the divergence case. In particular, the assumption that
\Psi
is decreasing for
n=1
is necessary due to work of Duffin–Schaeffer, as indicated above. For
n\geq 2
, the monotonicity assumption on the approximating function
\Psi
is not required
[
10
]
.

1.2.
Dual Diophantine approximation on manifolds

Diophantine approximation on manifolds (or Diophantine approximation of dependent quantities) concerns the study of approximation properties of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which are functionally related or in other words restricted to a sub-manifold
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To estimate the size of sets of points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
which lie on a
k
-dimensional, nondegenerate
1
1

1

In this context ‘nondegenerate’ means suitably curved, see
[
6
,
28
]
for precise formulations. Actually, within the theory of metric Diophantine approximation on manifolds, most of the results requires the manifolds to be nondegenerate. Removing this assumption presents significant challenges and the progress thus far has been achieved for limited situations only, see for example
[
9
]
for the latest results in this direction.
, analytic submanifold
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an intricate and challenging problem. The fundamental aim is to estimate the size of the set
\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)
in terms of Lebesgue measure, Hausdorff measure and Hausdorff dimension. When asking such questions it is natural to phrase them in terms of a suitable measure supported on the manifold, since when
k<n
the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)
is zero irrespective of the approximating functions. For this reason, results in the dependent Lebesgue measure theory (for example, Khintchine–Groshev type theorems for manifolds) are posed in terms of the
k
-dimensional
Lebesgue measure (=Hausdorff measure) on
\mathcal{M}
.

In full generality, a complete Hausdorff measure treatment akin to Theorem
1.1
for manifolds
\mathcal{M}
represents a deep open problem in the theory of Diophantine approximation. The problem is referred to as the Generalised Baker-Schmidt Problem (GBSP) inspired by the pioneering work of Baker-Schmidt
[
3
]
. Ideally one would want to solve the following problem in full generality.

Problem 1.2

(Generalised Baker-Schmidt Problem for Hausdorff Measure)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a nondegenerate submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim\mathcal{M}=k
and
n\geq 2
. Let
\Psi
be an approximating function. Let
g
be a dimension function such that
r^{-k}g(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0.
Assume that
r\mapsto r^{-k}g(r)
is decreasing and
r\mapsto r^{1-k}g(r)
is increasing. Prove that

\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{M})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0,&{\rm if}\quad\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\|\mathbf{q}\|^{k}\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)^{1-k}g\left(\frac{\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)}{\|\mathbf{q}\|}\right)<\infty.\\[12.91663pt]
\infty,&{\rm if}\quad\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\|\mathbf{q}\|^{k}\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)^{1-k}g\left(\frac{\Psi(\|\mathbf{q}\|)}{\|\mathbf{q}\|}\right)=\infty.\end{array}\right.

In fact, this problem is stated in an idealistic format and solving it in this form is extremely challenging. The main difficulties lie
in the convergence case and therein constructing a suitable nice cover for the set
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{M}
. We list the contributions to date to highlight the significance of this problem.

Diophantine approximation on manifolds dates back to the profound conjecture of K. Mahler
[
30
]
in 1932, which can be rephrased as the statement that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{V}_{n})=0
for
\Psi(q)=q^{-\tau}
with
\tau>n
. Here and throughout
\mathcal{V}_{n}=\{(x,x^{2},\ldots,x^{n}):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
denotes the
Veronese
curve. Mahler’s conjecture was eventually proven in 1965 by Sprindžuk
[
35
]
, who then conjectured that the same holds when
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is replaced by any nondegenerate analytic manifold, and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is replaced by
\mathcal{H}^{k}
where
k
is the dimension of this manifold. Although particular cases of Sprindžuk’s conjecture were known, it was not until 1998 that Kleinbock and
Margulis
[
28
]
established Sprindžuk’s conjecture in full generality by using dynamical tools based on diagonal flows on homogeneous spaces. This breakthrough result acted as a catalyst for the subsequent progress in this area of research. The convergence Lebesgue measure result for
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{M}
was established first in
[
6
]
and then in a generalised form in
[
13
]
. The divergence case was established for the Veronese curve in
[
4
]
and for any nondegenerate manifolds in
[
5
]
.
The first inhomogeneous Lebesgue measure result for the divergence case of
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{M}
was established very recently in
[
1
]
.

With regards to the Hausdorff measure or dimension theory on manifolds,
the progress has proven to be difficult. The first major result in this direction relating to the approximation of points on the Veronese curve appeared in the landmark paper of Baker and Schmidt
[
3
]
in which they proved upper and lower bounds for Hausdorff dimension on the Veronese curve. Further, they conjectured that their lower bound is actually sharp,
which was later proven to be true in
[
12
]
.
In 2000, Dickinson and Dodson
[
19
]
proved a lower bound for Hausdorff dimension on extremal manifolds for approximating functions
of the type
\Psi(q)=q^{-\tau}
for any
\tau>n
.
In 2006, the divergence case for the
\mathcal{H}^{g}
-measure of
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{M}
was established in
[
8
]
as a consequence of their ubiquity framework.

Proving the genuine Hausdorff measure result for convergence for any manifold remained out of reach until recently when the first-named author proved it in
[
24
]
for
\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{V}_{2}
i.e. for
\mathcal{H}^{g}
-measure on the parabola under some mild assumptions on the dimension function
g
. Soon after that, J.-J. Huang
[
22
]
proved that
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{C})=0
for all nondegenerate planar curves
\mathcal{C}
if a certain sum converges.

Inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation is the generalisation of the homogeneous Diophantine approximation discussed above and results for the latter settings can be derived from the former settings by considering the inhomogeneous parameter to be zero. We refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
25
]
for accounts of inhomogeneous Baker-Schmidt problem. In particular, authors of this paper proved the GBSP for hypersurfaces
for
\mathcal{H}^{g}
-measure upon a mild assumption on
g
, for both homogeneous and inhomogeneous settings with non-monotonic multivariable approximating functions
[
25
]
. However, the results of
[
25
]
are not applicable to the one dimensional curves, for example, planar curves or Veronese curves.

The aim of this paper is to contribute in making some advances on the GBSP on nondegenerate curves in a reasonable generality. We emphasize on the case of Veronese curves, especially the parabola.
In Section
3
we discuss planar curves.

2.
The generalised Hausdorff measure on the Veronese curve

We start with the simple case of a parabola to familiarise with some intricacies of reducible and irreducible polynomials.

2.1.
The parabola

Throughout, denote by

\mathcal{V}_{2}=\{(x,x^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\in[0,1]\}

the standard parabola.
In this case, we change the notation slightly, instead of
\mathbf{q}\cdot(x,x^{2})-p
we use
a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}
, i.e.
(\mathbf{q},p)\to(a_{2},a_{1},a_{0})
and
\|\mathbf{a}\|=\max\{|a_{1}|,|a_{2}|\}
. So we consider the set

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)=\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Psi)\cap\mathcal{V}_{2}=\left\{x\in[0,1]:\begin{array}[]{l}|a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}|<\Psi(\|\mathbf{a}\|)\\
\text{for\ i.m.}\ (a_{0},a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\end{array}\right\}.

As stated earlier, the first result regarding the convergence case of the
\mathcal{H}^{g}
-measure on the parabola was proven by Hussain
[
24
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Hussain, 2015)
.

Let
\Psi
be an approximating function and let
g
be a dimension function such that
r^{-1}g(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0

and
g(r)r^{-1}
is decreasing.
Assume that there exist positive constants
s_{1}
and
s_{2}\leq 1
such
that
2s_{1}<3s_{2}
and

(2.1)

r^{s_{1}}<g(r)<r^{s_{2}}\qquad\text{for all sufficiently small $r>0$}\,.

Then

(2.2)

\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi))=0\ \ {\rm if}\quad\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}g\left(\frac{\Psi(q)}{q}\right)q^{2}<\infty.

Clearly one can see that Theorem
2.1
is not as general as the convergence part of the GBSP for Hausdorff measure.
It is desirable to drop the condition (
2.1
).
As an intermediate step, we introduce alternative conditions,
on the approximating function
\Psi
and the dimension function

g
, which allow the conclusion in (
2.2
).
Our first result deals with a special class of approximating
functions of fast decay.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Psi
be an approximating function such that for
q\geq q_{0}
, we have

(2.3)

\Psi(q^{2})\leq\Psi(q)^{2}.

Then for any dimension function
g
with the property that
r^{-1}g(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0
, and
r\mapsto r^{-1}g(r)
decreasing, we have the conclusion
(
2.2
) of Theorem
2.1
.

Observe that there is an identity in (
2.3
) for any
function
\Psi:q\mapsto q^{-\tau}
. Moreover
the condition is easy to check for functions of exponential decay.
We want to explicitly highlight our result for these natural classes of approximating functions.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\tau>0
. Let
\Psi
be of the
form
\Psi:q\mapsto q^{-\tau}
or of the form

\Psi:q\mapsto e^{-q\tau}
. Let
g
be any dimension function such that
r^{-1}g(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0
and
r\mapsto r^{-1}g(r)
is decreasing. Then the conclusion (
2.2
) holds.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
splits into two parts: the polynomial
P(x)=a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}
having repeated roots or distinct roots. To be precise, identifying
P
with its coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}
,
the limsup set
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)
can be covered by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)=\mathcal{D}_{(1)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)\cup\mathcal{D}_{(2)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)

where the index brackets are used to distinguish them from the sets

D_{n}(\Psi)
above and we have put

\mathcal{D}_{(1)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)=\left\{\bigcap_{N\geq 1}\bigcup_{k\geq N}^{\infty}{}\bigcup_{2^{k}\leq\|\mathbf{a}\|<2^{k+1}}\Delta(P):P{\rm\ has\ repeated\ roots}\right\},

\mathcal{D}_{(2)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)=\left\{\bigcap_{N\geq 1}\bigcup_{k\geq N}^{\infty}{}\bigcup_{2^{k}\leq\|\mathbf{a}\|<2^{k+1}}\Delta(P):P{\rm\ has\ distinct\ roots}\right\},

and

\Delta(P)=\{x\in[0,1):|P(x)|=|a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}|<\Psi(\|\mathbf{a}\|)\}.

Hence, the desired statement that
\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi))=0
follows by establishing separately,
\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}_{(1)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi))=0
and
\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}_{(2)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi))=0
. In the paper,
[
24
, pp.55–57]
, the proof of the distinct root case has been provided without assuming the assumption (
2.1
) on the dimension functions
g
. Condition (
2.1
) is only needed for the repeated roots case (see the proof on
[
24
, p.54]
). In the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we provide a modified treatment (to the corresponding proof for the repeated root case
[
24
, p.54]
) and as a consequence we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}_{(1)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi))=0\quad{\rm if}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{2k}g\left(\frac{\sqrt{\Psi(2^{2k})}}{2^{k}}\right)<\infty.

On the other hand, the series in Theorem
2.1
, arising from covering the set
\mathcal{D}_{(2)}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi)
, is

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{2}g\left(\frac{\Psi(q)}{q}\right)\asymp\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{3k}g\left(\frac{\Psi(2^{k})}{2^{k}}\right).

Hereby we use our assumption
that
\Psi
is decreasing to see that the parameter change from
q
to
k

is justified, upon a suitable fixed factor.
This leads us to study the
convergence/divergence relations
between the two series

(2.4)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{3k}g\left(\frac{\Psi(2^{k})}{2^{k}}\right)

and

(2.5)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{2k}g\left(\frac{\sqrt{\Psi(2^{2k})}}{2^{k}}\right).

We show a result somehow dual to Theorem
2.1
, where
we allow arbitrary dimension function
g
at the cost
of a condition on
\Psi
similar to (
2.1
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
s_{1},s_{2}
be positive real numbers and suppose that

q^{-s_{2}}\leq\Psi(q)\leq q^{-s_{1}}

where
2s_{2}\leq 3s_{1}+1
. Then, for any dimension function
g
, if (
2.4
) converges, the series (
2.5
) converges as well.

On the other hand
we have the following dichotomy.

Theorem 2.5
.

There exist pairs of a (decreasing) approximating function
\Psi

and a dimension function
g
such that
r^{-1}g(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0
, and
r\mapsto r^{-1}g(r)
is decreasing, and so that
(
2.4
) converges but (
2.5
)
diverges.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
g
and
\Psi
be as
in Theorem
2.5
. Then, we have

\mathcal{H}^{g}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\Psi))=\infty.

2.2.
General Veronese curves and irreducibility

As explained above, in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the restriction (
2.1
) was only required for the
class of reducible polynomials. The proof of Theorem
2.2
relies on the condition (
2.3
) on
\Psi
which can be regarded as a replacement for the condition (
2.1
) on
g
in Theorem
2.1
.

We now consider the Veronese curve in
arbitrary dimension
n
.
In view of the above observation, one may expect
that, in general, the reducibility of polynomials
might cause problems. However,
generalizing the ideas of Theorem
2.2
, our new results
(Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
2.17
) below suggest that reducible
polynomials should not play an important role
upon some
strengthened version of (
2.3
) on the approximating
function
\Psi
.

We denote by

H_{P}=\max_{0\leq i\leq n}|a_{i}|
the height of the polynomial

P(t)=a_{n}t^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}t+a_{0}
. The careful reader may observe that, strictly speaking, this
treats a slightly altered problem than introduced
in Section
1
, where the approximating function only
depends on
n
instead of
n+1
elements (not on
a_{0}
).
However, for fixed
t=\xi
, by triangle inequality
|a_{0}|\ll_{n,\xi}\max\{|a_{1}|,\ldots,|a_{n}|\}
if
|P(\xi)|
is small, and the results of this section can be verified similarly in the classical setting as well.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer.
Let
\Psi
be an approximating function with the property that for
large
q>q_{0}

the derived function

(2.6)

q\longmapsto\frac{\log\Psi(q)}{\log q}

is monotonically nonincreasing.
Let
x
be a real number not algebraic of degree at most
n
.
Suppose that the set of polynomials

Z_{\Psi}^{\prime}(x):=\{P(t)=a_{n}t^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[t]:\;|P(x)|\leq\Psi(H_{P})\}

is infinite.
Assume that for every
c_{1}>1
there
exists
c_{2}>0
such that

(2.7)

\Psi(q/c_{1})\leq c_{2}\Psi(q),\qquad\qquad q\geq q_{0}(c_{1}).

Then there exists an explicitly computable constant
C=C(n,x)>0
and
infinitely many irreducible integer polynomials
Q

of degree at most
n
with the property

(2.8)

|Q(x)|\leq C\Psi(H_{Q}).

These polynomials
Q
can be chosen among divisors of
polynomials in
Z_{\Psi}^{\prime}(x)
.

Remark 2.8
.

In the arXiv version (first and second version) of this paper
[
26
]
, we additionally
show the following claim that admits a similar but
slightly more technical proof.
Assume there are infinitely many
polynomials
P\in Z_{\Psi}^{\prime}(x)

with the property that when factorized into irreducible polynomials

P(t)=Q_{1}(t)Q_{2}(t)\cdots Q_{k}(t),\qquad\qquad k=k(P)\leq n,

then there is no index
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
so that

(2.9)

H_{Q_{i}}>H_{P}.

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist infinitely many irreducible integer polynomials
Q
(among
the
Q_{i}
above) such that the estimate

(2.10)

|Q(x)|\leq\Psi(H_{Q})^{1-\varepsilon}

holds. Moreover, if an index
i
satisfies (
2.9
) then
for the codivisor polynomial we have

(2.11)

H_{P/Q_{i}}\leq\binom{n}{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}\sqrt{n+1}.

Hence (
2.10
) is in particular true if
the following property
(\ast)
holds:

Property (
\ast
).
Only finitely many
P\in Z_{\Psi}^{\prime}(x)

have a fixed, non-constant divisor polynomial.

This property further implies
that
C=C(n)
can be chosen independently from
x
in
Theorem
2.7
.

Remark 2.9
.

The assumption on
x
is necessary. If
x
is algebraic of degree at most

n
and we take
\Psi(q)=q^{-n}
(or
any other
\Psi(q)=o(q^{-n+1})
), a variant of
Liouville’s inequality
[
14
, Theorem A1]
implies that

Z_{\Psi}^{\prime}(x)
consists essentially only
of the infinite set
of polynomial multiples of

4.
Hausdorff measure and dimension

For completeness we give below a very brief introduction to Hausdorff measures and dimension. For further details see
[
20
]
.

Let

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then for any
0<\rho\leq\infty
, any finite or countable collection
\{B_{i}\}
of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\Omega\subseteq\bigcup_{i}B_{i}
and
\mathrm{diam}(B_{i})\leq\rho
is called a
\rho
-cover
of
\Omega
.
Let

\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{g}(\Omega)=\inf\sum_{i}g\left(\diam(B_{i})\right),

where the infimum is taken over all possible
\rho
-covers

\{B_{i}\}
of
\Omega
. The
g
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\Omega
is defined to be

\mathcal{H}^{g}(\Omega)=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{g}(\Omega).

The map
\mathcal{H}^{g}:\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to[0,\infty]
defines an outer measure
on all sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which becomes a proper
measure when restricted to the subset of
\mathcal{H}^{g}
-measurable sets,
i.e. sets
A
that satisfy
\mathcal{H}^{g}(B)=\mathcal{H}^{g}(A\cap B)+\mathcal{H}^{g}(B\setminus A)
for
any
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In the case that
g(r)=r^{s}\;\;(s\geq 0)
, the measure
\mathcal{H}^{g}
is denoted
\mathcal{H}^{s}
and is called
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
. For any set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
one can easily verify that there exists a unique critical value of
s
at which the function
s\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
“jumps” from infinity to zero. The value taken by
s
at this discontinuity is referred to as the
Hausdorff dimension
of
\Omega
is denoted by
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Omega
; i.e.

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Omega:=\inf\{s\geq 0\;:\;\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)=0\}.

The countable collection
\{B_{i}\}
is called a
fine cover
of
\Omega
if for every
\rho>0
it contains a subcollection that is a
\rho
-cover of
\Omega
.

We state the Hausdorff measure analogue of the famous Borel–Cantelli lemma (see
[
11
, Lemma 3.10]
) which will allow us to estimate the Hausdorff measure of certain sets via calculating the Hausdorff
f
-sum of a fine cover.

Lemma 4.1

(Hausdorff–Cantelli lemma)
.

Let
\{B_{i}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a fine cover of a set
\Omega
and let
g
be a dimension function such that

(4.1)

\sum_{i}g\left(\diam(B_{i})\right)\,<\,\infty.

Then

\mathcal{H}^{g}(\Omega)=0.

c_{8}>0
we get

\max\{|R_{l}^{n}(\xi_{w})|,|Q(\xi_{w})|\}\geq c_{8}H_{R_{l}^{n}}^{-(2n-1)}=c_{8}q_{l}^{-n(2n-1)}.

Now since
w>n(2n-1)>2n-1
, for large
l
from (
5.20
) we infer

|R_{l}^{n}(\xi_{w})|<c_{4}^{n}q_{l}^{-wn}<c_{8}q_{l}^{-n(2n-1)},\qquad\qquad l\geq l_{0}.

Therefore for large
l
we must have

|Q(\xi_{w})|\geq c_{8}q_{l}^{-n(2n-1)}=c_{9}\Psi(H_{Q})^{\frac{2n-1}{w}},

where we used (
5.22
) and let
c_{9}=c_{8}c_{4}^{-n(2n-1)/w}>0
. Since we may assume
\Psi(H_{Q})<1
,
our choice
w>n(2n-1)
guarantees that the right hand side exceeds
c_{9}\Psi(H_{Q})^{1/n}
again, so we may take
\Delta(n)=c_{9}
. Clearly we can just decrease
\Delta(n)
, if needed,
to deal with small
l
. This proves the second claim.

Remark 5.3
.

Our choice of locally constant, discontinuous
\Psi

is just for simplicity. It is easy to alter
\Psi
to derive an arbitrarily
smooth strictly decaying function that still satisfies the claim of the theorem.

The proof can be extended to show the claims for
any
x=[a_{0};a_{1},\ldots]
with convergent sequence
[a_{0};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}]=p_{i}/q_{i}

with the property
\liminf_{i\to\infty}\log a_{i+1}/\log q_{i}>n(2n-1)-1
,
or equivalently
\liminf_{i\to\infty}\log q_{i+1}/\log q_{i}>n(2n-1)
.
The set of such numbers has Hausdorff dimension
(2n^{2}-n+1)^{-1}

due to Tan–Zhou
[
36
]
.
In the example constructed in the proof above, the function

\Psi
depends on
x=\xi_{w}
.
With some more effort, the proof can be refined to provide
uncountably many
x

with the properties of Theorem
2.15

for a fixed approximating function
\Psi
. Possibly the same can be obtained
for a set of Hausdorff dimension
(2n^{2}-n+1)^{-1}
as above.

Proof of Theorem

Let

5.10.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Call
\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
a good pair if
\mathbf{q}=(-2ab,b^{2})
for some
a,b
coprime with
|a|<b
.
Let
\Psi
be a single-variable approximating function, and derive
the multi-variable function
\psi
by letting

\psi(\mathbf{q})=b^{2}\Psi^{2}(b)
for good pairs
\mathbf{q}
, and
\psi(\mathbf{q})=0
otherwise. For good pairs,
we calculate

|\mathbf{q}\cdot(x,x^{2})+a^{2}|=|q_{1}x+q_{2}x^{2}+a^{2}|=q_{2}(x-a/b)^{2}.

Therefore a point
(x,x^{2})
with
x\notin\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
|x|<1
is
\psi
-approximable if
there exist infinitely many
a,b
coprime such that
|a|<b
and

q_{2}(x-a/b)^{2}<b^{2}\Psi^{2}(b).

Rearranging and plugging in
q_{2}=b^{2}
gives

|x-a/b|<\Psi(b).
We apply the divergence case for
n=1
of
Theorem
1.1
,
and notice that obviously coprimality of
a,b
is not a restriction.
It yields that for
s\in(0,1)
, any decreasing
\Psi
and the dimension function
g(r)=r^{s}
, the latter set has full
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure as soon as the series

(5.25)

\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}b\Psi^{s}(b)

diverges. On the other hand, in view of
\|\mathbf{q}\|=\max\{|q_{1}|,|q_{2}|\}=b^{2}

for good pairs,
the corresponding series for the generalized Baker-Schmidt problem for the multivariable approximating function
\psi
becomes

(5.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{{{\mathbf{0}}}\}}\|\mathbf{q}\|^{1-s}\psi(\mathbf{q})^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}{\sum_{|a|<b,(a,b)=1}}b^{2(1-s)}b^{2s}\Psi^{2s}(b)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{b}{\sum_{|a|<b}b^{2(1-s)}b^{2s}\Psi^{2s}(b)\ll\sum_{b}b^{3}\Psi^{2s}(b)}.

To complete the proof we choose an (ultimately) decreasing approximating function
\Psi
such that (
5.26
) converges but (
5.25
) diverges, for example

\Psi(q)=q^{-2/s}\log^{-1/s}(q),\;\;(q\geq 2).

Difference in peripherality of the inclusive ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) and ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reactions and its implications for phenomenological reaction model Preprint: APS/123-QED
Hibiki Nakada, Shinsuke Nakayama, Kazuki Yoshida, Yukinobu Watanabe, Kazuyuki Ogata

Abstract
Background : Previous studies have revealed the importance of introducing surface correction into a phenomenological model for inclusive ( n , n ′ ​ x ) (n,n^{\prime}x) and ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) reactions. These findings have contributed significantly to the improvement of nuclear data evaluation. However, the necessity for the surface correction in an inclusive ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reaction has hardly been investigated. Purpose : The purpose of this study is to investigate the difference in the peripherality of the ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) and ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reactions by a theoretical analysis using a quantum mechanical model, and to obtain a theoretical basis on the surface correction in the ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reaction. Methods : The energy spectra and their radial distributions for the ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) and ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reactions are calculated by the one-step semiclassical distorted wave model. Results : The radial distribution of the energy spectra for the ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reaction is shifted toward the outer region of the nucleus compared to the ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) reaction. Based on this finding, we consider a larger surface correction into a phenomenological model for the ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reaction than that for the ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) reaction, and calculated values reproduce the experimental ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) spectra well. Conclusion : The peripherality of the ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reaction is more prominent than that of the ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) reaction. The stronger surface correction thus should be introduced for the ( d , d ′ ​ x ) (d,d^{\prime}x) reaction than for the ( p , p ′ ​ x ) (p,p^{\prime}x) reaction.



I
INTRODUCTION

Phenomenological models have played an important role in the nuclear data evaluation for nucleon-induced reactions
Iwamoto et al. 2023
;
Brown et al. 2018
;
Plompen et al. 2020
;
Koning et al. 2019
;
Chadwick et al. 1999
.
For the calculation of pre-equilibrium processes, which are prominent at incident energies above about 10 MeV, the two-component exciton model
Kalbach 1986
has been widely used.
This phenomenological model has achieved great success in combination with global parameterization based on analyses of a large number of experimental data for
(N,N^{\prime}x)
reactions
Kalbach 1985
;
Kalbach 2000
;
Koning and Duijvestijn 2004
.
It has been argued in these analyses that the correction related to surface localization is important in reproducing the shape of experimental energy spectrum
Kalbach 1985
;
Kalbach 2000
.
Note that we will refer to the above correction as “surface correction” in this paper even though the correction has been referred to as “surface effects” in previous literature including Refs.
Kalbach 1985
;
Kalbach 2000
;
Koning and Duijvestijn 2004
.
This is because the “surface effects” is actually interpreted as a correction within the phenomenological model, as explained below.

The surface correction is a simple means to effectively incorporate the peripherality of the
(N,N^{\prime}x)
reaction into the exciton model by restricting a hole degree of freedom with respect to the excitation energy.
The original exciton model
Griffin 1966
assumes that the hole states could extend to infinite depth.
This model was later modified by considering a finite well depth in the density of states
Běták and Dobeš 1976
.
The modification restricts the region where the hole states are generated to a more realistic range.
In the surface correction, the finite well depth is made shallower only in the one-hole state, i.e., the state where the number of collision is small as discussed in Ref.
Kalbach 1985
.
This is an idea based on the following two matters in the exciton model that does not have a radial dependence:
(i) reactions with fewer collisions would be more likely to occur around the nuclear surface,
(ii) nuclear potential is shallower around the nuclear surface than the interior.
It is worth mentioning that in Refs.
Avrigeanu et al. 1996
;
Avrigeanu et al. 1997
, it is indicated that the first
NN
collision in
(N,N^{\prime}x)
reactions is localized on the nuclear surface using a semiclassical approach. Furthermore, the peripherality of the
(p,p^{\prime}x)
reaction is clarified by the theoretical analysis
Watanabe et al. 1999
using the semiclassical distorted wave (SCDW) model, which has no free adjustable parameter
Luo and Kawai 1991
;
Kawai and Weidenmüller 1992
;
Ogata et al. 1999
;
Weili et al. 1999
;
Ogata et al. 2002
.

On another front, evaluation activities of deuteron nuclear data have gradually begun
Iwamoto et al. 2023
;
Nakayama et al. 2021
;
Koning et al. 2019
,
mainly motivated by the development of deuteron accelerator-based intensive neutron sources
Moeslang et al. 2006
;
Ledoux et al. 2014
.
In the evaluation of deuteron nuclear data, phenomenological models
Nakayama et al. 2022
;
Kalbach 2005
using state densities similar to those in the exciton model are often employed to calculate the components of direct inelastic scattering to continuum states.
These components correspond to inelastic scattering processes involving excitation of a nucleon particle-hole pair in the target nucleus.
The evaluation of the inelastic scattering is important since it affects the transport of deuterons in a material.
Therefore, as is the case with the exciton model, whether the surface correction should be taken into account in the state densities used in the above inelastic scattering model, and if so, to what extent, is an important factor in the deuteron nuclear data evaluation.
However, since experimental data for the
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions are very limited, there is concern that making this judgment based on a comparison with specific experimental data may give unphysical results under other conditions.
A theoretical basis for the surface correction of the
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reaction founded on a deep and comprehensive understanding of the peripherality of the reaction is required.

Under these circumstances, in Ref.
Nakada et al. 2023
, the one-step SCDW model
Luo and Kawai 1991
has been successfully applied to describe the inclusive
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions. This SCDW model has two noteworthy features. First, the model possesses the property of representing the inclusive cross section as an incoherent integral of contributions at individual collision points
Cowley et al. 2000
;
Watanabe et al. 1999
. This property allows us to analyze the peripherality of the inclusive reaction, i.e., to investigate where and to what extent the reaction occurs in the nucleus. The second is the adoption of local Fermi gas (LFG) to the single-particle states. Although LFG would be unrealistic for describing a specific nuclear state, it can reasonably describe the overall response of a nucleus, involving many initial and final states. Note that, in Ref
Weili et al. 1999
, the SCDW model adopts the Wigner transform of one-body density matrices calculated with a single-particle state model for nuclei instead of LFG. On the other hand, we use LFG for reducing numerical tasks in this work.

The purpose of this study is to investigate the peripherality in the inclusive
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reaction and the cause of the difference from that in the inclusive
(p,p^{\prime}x)
reaction.
The analyses are performed utilizing the above-mentioned property of the SCDW model.
Exploring the difference in the peripherality of the two reactions is of interest from the viewpoint not only of nuclear data evaluation but also of fundamental nuclear physics.
Furthermore, we apply the findings from the SCDW analysis to the implementation of the surface correction into the phenomenological deuteron inelastic scattering model.

The construction of this paper is as follows.
In Sec.
II
we briefly describe the one-step SCDW model for the inclusive
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions.
We also explain the phenomenological inelastic scattering model.
In Sec.
III
we compare the calculated energy spectrum of the inclusive
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions with experimental data and clarify the difference of the peripherality between the two reactions.
Improvements of the phenomenological model are also suggested.
Finally, a summary is given in Sec.
IV
.

II
Methods

II.1
SCDW model

We briefly describe the inclusive
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions with the one-step SCDW model. The double differential cross section for the energy
E_{f}
and the solid angle
\Omega_{f}
of the emitted particle
c~(=~p~\mathrm{or}~d)
is expressed with
Nakada et al. 2023

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{c}}{\partial E_{f}\partial\Omega_{f}}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{A_{c}A}{A_{c}+A}\right]^{2}\frac{k_{f}}{k_{i}}\int d\bm{R}

\displaystyle\times|\chi^{(-)}_{f}(\bm{R})|^{2}|\chi^{(+)}_{i}(\bm{R})|^{2}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{c}}{\partial E_{f}\partial\Omega_{f}}\right]_{\bm{R}}\rho(\bm{R}),

(1)

where
A_{c}
and
A
are the mass numbers of the particle
c
and the target nucleus, respectively.
k_{i}~(k_{f})
is the asymptotic momentum of the incident (emitted) particle,
\bm{R}
is the coordinate of the collision point with respect to the center of the target nucleus. The distorted waves for
c
in the initial and final states are denoted by
\chi_{i}
and
\chi_{f}
, respectively.
\rho(\bm{R})
is the nuclear density at
\bm{R}
. The averaged double differential cross section of the elementary process at
\bm{R}
is given by

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{c}}{\partial E_{f}\partial\Omega_{f}}\right]_{\bm{R}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi/3)k^{3}_{F}(\bm{R})}\left[\frac{A_{c}+1}{A_{c}}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\times\int_{k_{\alpha}\leq k_{F}(\bm{R})}d\bm{k}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{d\sigma_{cN}}{d\Omega}\right)_{\theta_{cN}(\bm{R}),E_{cN}(\bm{R})}

\displaystyle\times\delta(E_{i}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}-E_{f}-\varepsilon_{\beta}),

(2)

where
\bm{k}_{\alpha}
is the momentum of the nucleon of the target in the initial state.
k_{F}(\bm{R})
represents the local Fermi momenta of nucleon.
d\sigma_{cN}/d\Omega
is the free scattering cross section determined by the local scattering angle
\theta_{cN}(\bm{R})
and the local scattering energy
E_{cN}(\bm{R})
between
c
and the nucleon of the target. The incident energy of
c
is represented by
E_{i}
and
\varepsilon_{\alpha}~(\varepsilon_{\beta})
is the kinetic energy of the nucleon of the target nucleus in the initial (final) state.
Equations (
1
) and (
2
) are the same as Eqs. (19) and (20) of Ref.
Nakada et al. 2023
, respectively, and the derivation of the equations is discussed in detail in Ref.
Nakada et al. 2023
.

From Eq. (
1
), one can see that the inclusive cross section is described by an incoherent integral of
\bm{R}
.
This description is made possible by the short-ranged property
of the kernel, Eq. (2.8) of Ref.
Luo and Kawai 1991
, which is realized when
a large number of s.p. states are involved; see Fig. 11 of
Ref.
Luo and Kawai 1991
. In other words, the interference between the transition through different interacting points disappears in the situation. Thus, the projectile-nucleus cross section is given by integrating a product of the probability of the projectile reaching the point
{\bm{R}}
, that of the ejectile being emitted from
{\bm{R}}
, and the averaged cross section of the elementary process at
{\bm{R}}
. For more details, readers are referred to Ref.
Luo and Kawai 1991
.

By applying the local Fermi gas model (LFG) to the initial and final nuclear single-particle states,
k_{F}(\bm{R})
is given by
k_{F}(\bm{R})=[3\pi^{2}\rho(\bm{R})/2]^{1/3}

Luo and Kawai 1991
. Therefore, LFG can takes into account the spread of nucleon density on the nuclear surface. To clearly show the contribution of the nuclear surface to the inclusive cross section, we also consider the Fermi gas model (FG), which gives a uniform Fermi momentum distribution. When FG is used instead of LFG,
k_{F}(\bm{R})
becomes step functions with respect to
R
in Eq. (
2
). On the other hand, the Fermi momentum in LFG decreases smoothly as
R
increases, as shown later in Fig.
1
.

The energy spectra of the
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions are obtained by integrating Eq. (
1
) over
\Omega_{f}
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{c}}{dE_{f}}=\int d\Omega_{f}\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{c}}{\partial E_{f}\partial\Omega_{f}}.

(3)

We use the proton- and deuteron-nucleus global optical potential by Koning-Delaroche
Koning and Delaroche 2003
and An-Cai
An and Cai 2006
for describing projectile-nucleus scattering, respectively.
The nuclear density
\rho(R)
is assumed to be the Woods-Saxon form, where the radial parameter is defined as
R_{\rho}=r_{\rho}A^{1/3}
, with
r_{\rho}
= 1.15 fm,
and the diffuseness parameter is set to
a_{\rho}=0.5
fm as in Ref.
Nakada et al. 2023
.
For the differential cross sections of
d
-
N
scattering used in the
(d,d^{\prime}x)
calculation, we utilize the numerical table from Ref.
Chazono et al. 2022
, which was made by fitting experimental data of
p
-
d
elastic scattering.
In the calculations of the
(p,p^{\prime}x)
process, the free
p
-
N
scattering cross sections are calculated by using the nucleon-nucleon
t
matrix provided by Franey and Love
Love and Franey 1981
;
Franey and Love 1985
.

II.2
Phenomenological model by Kalbach

As a phenomenological model for continuum deuteron inelastic scattering, we adopt the model proposed by Kalbach
Kalbach 1977
.
Although this model was originally developed for
\alpha
-particle-induced reactions, the model itself is considered to be applicable to deuteron-induced ones
Nakayama et al. 2022
.
Note that what is calculated in this model is the direct process component, which is not included in the exciton model.

According to Ref.
Kalbach 1977
, the energy spectrum of the direct deuteron inelastic scattering with a nucleon particle-hole pair creation is calculated by:

\begin{split}\frac{d\sigma_{d}}{dE_{f}}=C\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{rea}}(E_{i})}{E^{3}_{i}}(2s+1)E_{f}&\sigma_{\mathrm{rea}}(E_{f})\frac{D_{F}(U)}{A^{2}}\\
&\times 0.12\ \left(\frac{\mathrm{MeV}^{2}}{\mathrm{mb}}\right),\end{split}

(4)

where
C
is a normalization constant determined from fitting experimental energy spectrum,
\sigma_{\mathrm{rea}}(E)
is the deuteron total reaction cross section at scattering energy
E
, and
E_{i}
and
E_{f}
are the incident and emitted deuteron energies, respectively.
According to Ref.
Kalbach 1977
, the overall normalization constant
C
can vary in the range of a factor of 3 depending on the target.
As shown later in Sec.
III.3
, in this study,
C
is adjusted so that the peak of the spectrum matches available experimental data.

\sigma_{\mathrm{rea}}(E)
is obtained from the optical model calculation with the global potential by An-Cai
An and Cai 2006
.

s
is the spin of the emitted deuteron.

A
denotes the mass number of the target, and
U
represents the effective excitation energy of residual nucleus considering the paring effect
Kalbach 1995
.
The final state density
D_{F}
is expressed as follows:

D_{F}(U)=(g_{n}^{2}U+g_{p}^{2}U)f(V,U),

(5)

where
g_{n}=N/(13~\mathrm{MeV})
,
g_{p}=Z/(13~\mathrm{MeV})
, and
N
and
Z
are the numbers of neutrons and protons in the target.
The setting of values for
g_{n}
and
g_{p}
follows the original paper by Kalbach
Kalbach 1977
.
The squaring of
g_{n}
and
g_{p}
corresponds to considering the
1p
-
1h
states of nucleons.
The finite well function
f(V,U)
with the well depth
V
is defined for the
1p
-
1h
states as follows
Běták and Dobeš 1976
:

f(V,U)=1-\left[\frac{U-V}{U}\right]\Theta(U-V),

(6)

where
\Theta
is the unit step function.
Note that the finite well function is not taken into account in the original Kalbach model
Kalbach 1977
and the first application of the model to the deuteron-induced reaction
Nakayama et al. 2022
.
The introduction of the finite well function enables us to consider the surface correction as shown below.

As mentioned in Sec.
I
, a simple method to include the peripherality of the nuclear reaction in the exciton model was proposed in Ref.
Kalbach 1985
, and has been successfully applied to the analysis of
(N,N^{\prime}x)
reactions
Kalbach 2000
;
Koning and Duijvestijn 2004
.
In the method, the peripherality is effectively taken into account by fixing the small value of
V
for the
h
=1 state only.
In this work we apply this method to Eq. (
4
).
In other words, we set
V
in Eq. (
6
) smaller than the typical value of 38 MeV associated to the Fermi energy.
By introducing
F(U,V),D_{F}(U)
is replaced by smaller density of states
D_{F}(V)
when
U>V
.
Therefore, giving a smaller V corresponds to making the reaction more likely occur in the peripheral region of the nucleus, based on the fact that the nuclear potential is shallower around the nuclear surface than in the interior.
The details of fixing the value of
V
are described later in Sec.
III.3
.

Following the idea in Kalbach’s original paper
Kalbach 1977
, the values calculated with the inelastic scattering model are treated as the direct component in this study.
Therefore, we do not consider in the model the reduction of the composite nucleus production cross section caused by other direct processes, such as the breakup reactions.
The use of the total reaction cross section obtained from the optical model as
\sigma_{\mathrm{rea}}
in Eq. (
4
) reflects the above idea.
The interference with other direct processes is not considered.

IV
SUMMARY

We have clarified the difference in the peripherality of the inclusive
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions and the cause of the difference.
The energy spectra calculated by SCDW with LFG and FG were compared to the experimental data of the
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions on several combinations of targets and incident energies.
By comparing the energy spectra with LFG and FG, we have found that the consideration of the nuclear surface is necessary to reproduce the experimental data for both
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions.

We have investigated the difference in the peripherality of the
(p,p^{\prime}x)
and
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reactions by comparing the radial distributions of the energy spectra.
The radial peak positions for each energy transfer
\omega
shifts to the outer region of the nucleus as
\omega
decrease and the tendencies were almost the same for the two reactions.
However, the opposite trend was observed between the two reactions in terms of the peak height.
As a result, it has been found that the peripherality get stronger for the
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reaction than the
(p,p^{\prime}x)
reaction.
Moreover, we compared the radial distributions ignoring the absorption by the nucleus.
The results show almost identical radial distributions and it has been found that the cause of the stronger peripherality of the
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reaction is the stronger absorption of deuteron than proton.

We have applied the above findings on the peripherality of
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reaction to the improvement of the phenomenological model by Kalbach to calculate continuum deuteron inelastic scattering.
For the finite well depth
V
, which is the adjustable parameter associated with the surface correction, the optimized value for the proton-induced reactions was insufficient to reproduce the experimental data of the
(d,d^{\prime}x)
reaction.
When the value of
V
was set much smaller, as suggested by the analysis with SCDW model, the calculated values reproduced the experimental data well over a wide emission energy range.
This result has indicated that parameter optimization of the phenomenological reaction model has been justified by the SCDW model.

As one of the future works, it will be necessary to extend the present one-step SCDW model to describe the multi-step processes.
With this extension, the SCDW model could guide the improvement of phenomenological models not only with respect to surface corrections, which are prominent in the small
\omega
region, but also with respect to wider energy spectra and angular distributions of outgoing deuterons.
On another front, it is of interest to perform a similar analysis for other particles.
Ref.
Watanabe et al. 1999
was the first example to analyze the peripherality of the
(p,p^{\prime}x)
reaction in detail using the SCDW model. In this study, we presented that a similar analysis is also valid for reactions induced by deuteron. Future similar analyses for various composite particles will improve our understanding of the peripherality of nuclear reactions.

\alpha
-particle is a good candidate since it is well known to undergo strong absorption and is of large importance in the application fields.

1
Introduction

For an open
\mathfrak{D}_{M}\subset TM
containing
M
, the adapted complex structure is the unique complex structure in
\mathfrak{D}_{M}

turning each leaf
\gamma^{\mathbb{C}}\cap\mathfrak{D}_{M}

into a holomorphic curve. It is equivalent to the solving of the Monge-Ampère equation
({\partial}\bar{\partial}\sqrt{\rho})^{n}=0
on
\mathfrak{D}_{M}-M
subjected to the initial condition that the restriction of the Kähler metric
2i{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho
to
M
has coincided with the original Riemannian metric
g
on
M
. Near
M
, the asymptotic expansion

\rho(x+iy)=\sum_{ij}g_{ij}(x)y_{i}y_{j}+O(y^{4})

is well-known for quite a long while.
Any attempt to reach the 4th order terms will encounter tedious computations on complicated curvature tensors. As far as we know, there is no further development on this yet.
In Theorem
3.5
, we complete the expansion up to 4th order terms

\rho(x+iy)=\sum_{i}y_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{pqij}R_{ipjq}(0)x_{p}x_{q}y_{i}y_{j}+O(5).

Despite the vanishing of the holomorphic sectional curvature along any Riemann foliation
\gamma^{\mathbb{C}},
no other sectional curvature is known.

With these 4th order terms at hand, we are able to compute any sectional curvature of the
Kähler metric
2i{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho
at
M
. It turns out that if

(M,g)
is positively curved some sectional curvature of
2i{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho
at
M
must be negative.

If a non-flat
(M,g)
has non-negative sectional curvature, an immediate application of the above result is that
the adapted complex structure cannot be globally defined on
the whole tangent bundle of the Kähler manifold
(\mathfrak{D}_{M},2i{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho).

A hyperkähler structure in a differentiable
manifold
M
consists of two anti-commuting complex structures
I,J
and a Riemannian metric
\kappa
, which is Kählerian with respect to all
I,J
and
IJ
.

It is natural to expect the tangent bundle of a Kähler manifold might be able to produce an extra complex structure anti-commuting with the original one to produce such a hyperkähler structure. A local existence was proved in
[
Fe
]
and in
[
Kal
]
.
In other words,
near the zero section of the tangent bundle,
the adapted complex structure and hyperkähler structures have existed simultaneously provided the underlying Kähler manifold is either compact or homogeneous.
For a compact Hermitian symmetric
M
,
both the hyperkähler and the adapted complex structures have existed on the whole
TM
. It is thus interesting to see examples where one of the structure has existed but not the other one.

We show that the hyperkähler structure on
TG^{\mathbb{C}}/H^{\mathbb{C}}
constructed in
[
Da-Sw
]
can be viewed as a hyperkähler structure on the tangent bundle of
(T(G/H,g_{n}),2i{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho),
the Kähler manifold obtained from doing the adapted complexification on the tangent bundle of the Riemannian manifold
(G/H,g_{n})
. However, the adapted complex structure cannot exist on the whole tangent bundle of
(T(G/H,g_{n}),2i{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho)
.

We briefly review the adapted complexification and prove the existence of it on
T(G/H,g_{n})

in the coming section. In §3, the 4th order terms of the Monge-Ampère equation
({\partial}{\bar{\partial}}\sqrt{\rho})^{n}=0
are computed and are used to decide the sign of sectional curvatures at the zero section in §4. The last section is devoted to those hyperkähler metrics on
TG^{\mathbb{C}}/H^{\mathbb{C}}
obtained from solving Nahm’s equations. It turns out the adapted complexification on
T(G/H,g_{n})
is reduced to the solving of the baby Nahm’s equation
\dot{T}_{1}+[T_{0},T_{1}]=0.

2
Adapted complex structure
TG/H

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
be a geodesic in a complete real-analytic Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
parametrized by the arclength. We denote its complexification as
the immersion

\gamma^{\mathbb{C}}:\mathbb{C}\to TM;\ \ \gamma^{\mathbb{C}}(t+is)=(\gamma(t),s\dot{\gamma}(t))\in T_{\gamma(t)}M.

(2.1)

The adapted complex structure, first established in [Gu-Sten] and in [Le-Sz],
is the unique complex structure
J
in a domain
\mathfrak{D}_{M}\subset TM
containing
M
such that, for any geodesic
\gamma
,
the immersion
\gamma^{\mathbb{C}}:\ \mathbb{C}\cap{\gamma^{\mathbb{C}}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{D}_{M})\to(\mathfrak{D}_{M},J)

is holomorphic. In other words, it is the unique complex structure in
\mathfrak{D}_{M}

turning each leaf of the foliation
\cup_{\gamma}(\gamma^{\mathbb{C}}\cap\mathfrak{D}_{M})
into a holomorphic curve.

For generic
(M,g)
, the domain
\mathfrak{D}_{M}
is properly contained in
TM
. One necessary condition for the adapted complex structure to exist on the whole tangent bundle
is that
(M,g)
is
non-negatively curved. Since any bi-invariant metric of a compact Lie group
G
has positive sectional curvature, it is natural to invest the definition on the tangent bundle of a compact Lie group.
The following construction has borrowed
ideas from [Sz1,2].

The tangent bundle
TG
of a Lie group is identified with
G\times\mathfrak{g}
through the mapping
(a,v)\overset{\phi}{\longrightarrow}(a,(L_{a^{-1}})_{*}v)
where

is the left translation by
b
. For compact

, any bi-invariant metric
\langle,\rangle
can be picked up to make the following
\varphi

\varphi:G\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow G^{\mathbb{C}};\ (g,\eta)\to g\exp i\eta

(2.2)

a diffeomorphism. That is, the tangent bundle
TG
can be identified with
G^{\mathbb{C}}
through
\varphi\cdot\phi
.

A geodesic in
(G,\langle,\rangle)
is of the form
\gamma(t)=a\exp tX
for some

and
\phi(\gamma(t),s\cdot\gamma(t))=(\gamma(t),sX)\in G\times\mathfrak{g}
.

The mapping

\mathbb{C}\overset{\eta}{\longrightarrow}TG\overset{\phi}{\longrightarrow}G\times\mathfrak{g}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}G^{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi\cdot\phi\cdot\eta(t+is)

\displaystyle=\varphi\cdot\phi(\gamma(t),s\dot{\gamma}(t))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\gamma(t),sX)

\displaystyle=a\exp tX\exp isX

\displaystyle=a\exp(t+is)X

Thus,

The adapted complex structure is defined on the whole

in a compact Lie group
G

and the resulting complex manifold is
G^{\mathbb{C}}
.

The next step is to extend the above from compact Lie groups to compact homogeneous spaces.

[

]
.

We compute

\rho

The adapted complexification
(\mathfrak{D}_{M},J)
is a Bruhat-Whitney type of complexification of
M
in the sense that
M
can be embedded as a totally real submanifold of
(\mathfrak{D}_{M},J)
. Such kind of complexifications is unique up to biholomorphisms near
M
. That is, for two complexifications
\Omega_{j}
of
M
and embeddings

, there exist neighborhoods
\Theta_{j}
of
M
in
\Omega_{j}
and a biholomorphism
\phi:\Theta_{1}\to\Theta_{2}
such that
i_{2}=\phi\cdot i_{1}
.

In
[

]
, the authors have treated this problem by solving
({\partial}\bar{\partial}\sqrt{\rho})^{n}=0
. In
\mathfrak{D}_{M}
, the Monge-Ampère equation

({\partial}\bar{\partial}\sqrt{\rho})^{n}=0
has a solution unique up to a scalar. The preferred initial condition is that the restriction of the Kähler metric
h
, induced from the Kähler form
2\sqrt{-1}{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho
, to
M
has coincided with the original Riemannian metric
g
of
M
. In other words, for any vector fields
X
and

in
M

h(X,Y)=2\sqrt{-1}{\partial}\bar{\partial}\rho(X,JY)=g(X,Y).

(3.1)

This metric is called the
canonical Kähler metric
of the adapted complexification over
(M,g).

The solution
\rho
is strictly plurisubharmonic and

\displaystyle\rho(p,v)=\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}

(3.2)

for any tangent vector
(p,v)\in\mathfrak{D}_{M},v\in T_{p}M
,
cf.
p.693
[
Le-Sz
]
.

Near the zero section

, the solution is rather concrete in terms of local coordinates. Let
p\in M
and let

\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
be a geodesic normal coordinate for a neighborhood
U
of

in
M
such that
TU\simeq U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is trivial.
Taking

small enough so that

for an open subset

\mathbb{R}_{\epsilon}^{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a Bruhat-Whitney complexification
which is biholomorphic to the adapted complexification
(T^{\epsilon}U,J)
.

A holomorphic coordinates
\{z_{j}=x_{j}+\sqrt{-1}y_{j}:j=1,\cdots,n\}
can thus be formed for the complex manifold
(T^{\epsilon}U,J)
where
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
is a geodesic normal coordinates for

and

Solving the defining Monge-Ampère equation in

\rho(x+\sqrt{-1}y)=\sum_{ij}g_{ij}(x)y_{i}y_{j}+O(y^{4})

(3.3)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=g(R(X,Y)W,Z).

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}(p):=R(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x_{i}},\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x_{j}},\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x_{k}},\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x_{l}})(p).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

Proof.

The determinant

\displaystyle\triangle=\det A=1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{jj}(y)+O(y^{4}),

(3.9)

then

\displaystyle\triangle^{-1}=1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{jj}(y)+O(y^{4}).

(3.10)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k\neq j}a_{kk}(y)+O(y^{4});

\displaystyle b^{jj}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\triangle}(-1)^{j+j}\det A^{jj}=1-a_{jj}(y)+O(y^{4}).

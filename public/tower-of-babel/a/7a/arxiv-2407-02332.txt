Li-Yau Conformal Volume and Colding-Minicozzi Entropy of Self-Shrinkers Thanks: The author was partially supported by the NSF Grant DMS-2203132. The author would like to thank Jeffrey Case for a stimulating discussion that lead to this work and Letian Chen for helpful comments on an earlier draft.
Jacob Bernstein

Abstract
. We show the (normalized) Li-Yau conformal volume of a self-shrinker of mean curvature flow in Euclidean space bounds its Colding-Minicozzi entropy from below. This bound is independent of codimension and sharp on planes. As an application we verify a conjecture of Colding-Minicozzi about the entropy of closed self-shrinkers of arbitrary codimension for self-shrinkers that are topologically two-dimensional real projective planes. As part of the proof we introduce two auxiliary functionals which we call stable conformal volume and virtual entropy which should be of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

In
[
32
]
, Li-Yau introduced a quantity associated to branched conformal immersions into
\mathbb{S}^{N}
they called the (
N
-)
conformal volume
– this quantity is also called
visual volume
by Gromov
[
28
]
. Among other applications, it has proven to be of significant importance both in the study of the Willmore energy and spectral properties of surfaces. There is a vast literature on this quantity – we refer in particular to
[
14
]
for helpful background discussion. In this paper, we relate the conformal volume to another important geometric quantity introduced in
[
22
]
called
Colding-Minicozzi entropy
or
entropy
for short, which has proven important in the study of mean curvature flow.

Consider the inverse stereographic projection map

(1.1)

\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{S}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1},\mathbf{x}\mapsto\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{1+\frac{1}{4}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}},\frac{1-\frac{1}{4}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{1+\frac{1}{4}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}\right).

For an
n
-dimensional submanifold,
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and embedding
j:M^{n}\to\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
define

\lambda_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma]=V_{c}(N,\mathcal{S}\circ j)

where
V_{c}(N,\mathcal{S}\circ j)
is the
N
-conformal volume of the embedding
\mathcal{S}\circ j:M\to\mathcal{S}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbb{S}^{N}
introduced in
[
32
]
. A straightforward computation yields,

\lambda_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma]=\sup_{\rho>0,\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\Sigma}W^{n}_{\rho}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0})d\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathbf{x}).

where the weights
W^{n}_{\rho}
are given explicitly by

W^{n}_{\rho}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\rho^{n}}{(1+\frac{1}{4}\rho^{2}|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{n}}.

These are the volume element on
\Sigma
induced by homothetic scalings of
\mathcal{S}^{*}g_{\mathbb{S}}
, i.e., the pull-back of the round metric on the sphere. Denote the
normalized
conformal volume by

\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma]=|\mathbb{S}^{n}|^{-1}\lambda_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma].

When
\Sigma
is an affine
n
-plane,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or round
n
-sphere,
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma]=1
.

Following
[
22
]
, for a
n
-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the entropy of
\Sigma
is the value

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]=\sup_{\rho>0,\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}(4\pi\rho)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2}}{4\rho}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

This quantity is monotone non-increasing along mean curvature flows and has found many applications both in the study of mean curvature flow and as a measure of complexity, especially of closed hypersurfaces, e.g.,
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
19
,
40
]
. For affine
n
-planes,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the value is one, but is strictly larger for all other proper submanifolds – see
[
17
,
6
]
.
While there has been extensive study of the entropy of hypersurfaces, comparatively little is known about it in higher codimension – see however recent work of Colding-Minicozzi
[
24
,
23
]
.

One readily observes – see Proposition
4.4
– that

(4\pi\rho)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4\rho}}\leq\left(\frac{n}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}W^{n}_{(n\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}(\mathbf{x})

which leads to the non-sharp bound

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\left(\frac{n}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma].

Note that asymptotically the constant behaves like

\left(\frac{n}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}|\mathbb{S}^{n}|=\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}+l.o.t,n\to\infty.

which grows rapidly as
n
grows.

We introduce two auxiliary measures of complexity of submanifolds and then relate the new quantities with the conformal volume and entropy. These relationships are particularly striking when the submanifold,
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, is a
self-shrinker
. That is, satisfies

\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma}+\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}=\mathbf{0}

where
\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma}
is the mean curvature vector and
\mathbf{x}^{\perp}
is the normal component of the position vector. Such submanifolds are important in the study of mean curvature flow, i.e., in the geometric evolution equation

t\in I\mapsto\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}.

Here
I
is an interval of time and
t\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
is a smooth evolution of
n
-dimensional submanifolds.
This is because for a self-shrinker
\Sigma
,

t\in(-\infty,0)\mapsto\sqrt{-t}\Sigma

is an evolution by mean curvature flow that provides a model of singularity formation of the flow. As
\lambda_{CM}^{n}
is invariant under rescaling, it is constant along this flow.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a
n
-dimensional properly embedded submanifold that is, up to translation and dilation, a self-shrinker. One has

1\leq\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma].

The inequality of Theorem
1.1
is sharp in the sense that equality holds throughout on affine
n
-planes and by
[
17
]
this double equality characterizes these planes. When
n\geq 2
planes and round spheres are characterized by equality in the first inequality by
[
14
]
, though for round spheres the second inequality is strict. While they are not self-shrinkers, certain double logarithmic spirals provide examples with equality in the second inequality but not the first – see Appendix
A
.

In
[
24
, pg. 119]
, Colding-Minicozzi made the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2
.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a closed
n
-dimensional self-shrinker, then

\sqrt{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{2n}{4\pi e}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}|\mathbb{S}^{n}|<\left(\frac{2n}{4\pi e}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}|\mathbb{S}^{n}|=\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\mathbb{S}^{n}]\leq\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma].

When
n=N+1
, i.e.,
\Sigma
is a hypersurface, this was shown in
[
21
]
and when
n=1
it follows from the classification of self-shrinking curves
[
24
, Lemma 6.16]
.
It was shown in
[
10
]
that the only non-flat proper self-shrinking surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with entropy below that of the shrinking cylinder, i.e., less than
\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}]=\lambda_{CM}^{1}[\mathbb{S}^{1}]\approx 1.52
, are the flat plane which has entropy 1, the shrinking sphere which has entropy
\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\mathbb{S}^{2}]\approx 1.47
and the shrinking cylinder itself. A strengthening of Conjecture (
1.2
) is that this phenomena continues to hold for surfaces of any codimension
[
24
, Conjecture 8.1]
.

In
[
32
]
, Li-Yau observed that two-dimensional real projective planes have conformal volume at least
6\pi
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
gives is a partial confirmation of Conjecture
1.2
:

Corollary 1.3
.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a self-shrinker that is topologically
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
, then

1.47\approx\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\mathbb{S}^{2}]<\frac{3}{2}\leq\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\Sigma].

There is a minimal
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
embedded into
\mathbb{S}^{4}\subset\mathbb{R}^{5}
, which we denote by
\Sigma_{\mathbb{RP}^{2}}
and is sometimes called the Veronese surface, which has conformal volume
6\pi
. One may check that
2\Sigma_{\mathbb{RP}^{2}}
is a self-shrinker and compute that

\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{2}[\Sigma_{\mathbb{RP}^{2}}]=\frac{3}{2}<2.207\approx\frac{6}{e}=\frac{3}{2}\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\mathbb{S}^{2}]=\lambda^{2}_{CM}[\Sigma_{\mathbb{RP}^{2}}].

In the case of tori, the conformal area has been estimated in terms of the complex structure by Montiel and Ros
[
36
]
. Moreover, they gave exact values for a large class of tori; this was refined by Bryant in
[
15
]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a self-shrinking torus conformally parametrized by the lattice
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{\omega}
where
\omega=x+iy
and
0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\leq y
.

One has

\frac{\pi y}{x^{2}+y^{2}-x+1}\leq\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{2}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\Sigma].

Remark 1.5
.

This bound comes from the bound established in
[
36
]
on (unnormalized) conformal volume of the lattice

\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{x^{2}+y^{2}-x+1}\leq V_{c}(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{\tau},[g_{\mathbb{R}}]).

This inequality becomes strict for large
y
. However, as conjectured and partially shown in
[
36
]
and fully established in
[
15
]
, it is an equality in the restricted domain satisfying

(x-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+y^{2}\leq\frac{9}{4}.

In this region, the minimum is achieved by the rectangular torus with
\omega=i\sqrt{2}
and the maximum is achieved by the hexagonal torus with
\omega=\frac{1}{2}+i\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
and the values satisfy

\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\mathbb{S}^{2}]\approx 1.47<1.48\approx\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{3}\leq\frac{\pi y}{x^{2}+y^{2}-x+1}\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}\pi}{3}\approx 1.814.

1.1.
Virtual entropy

In order to establish the link between the Li-Yau conformal volume and the Colding-Minicozzi entropy, it will be helpful to introduce an alternative notion of entropy. This is inspired by work of Magni-Mantegazza
[
34
]
, who, prior to Colding-Minicozzi’s paper on the subject, introduce a quantity based off of Hamilton’s matrix Harnack inequality
[
29
]
and its application to a generalized notion of Huisken monotonicity
[
30
]
. We revisit the approach of
[
34
]
. Using a simple observation about the matrix Harnack inequality in Euclidean space, we are led to a more flexible concept which should have other applications beyond the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us first briefly recall some observations and ideas drawn from
[
30
,
34
]
. For
t>0
and
u_{0}\in C^{0}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, consider the convolution by the heat kernel,

(1.2)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{t}u_{0})(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(\mathbf{y})\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})d\mathcal{L}^{N}(\mathbf{y});

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{2}}{4t}}.

For such
u_{0}
,
U(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}_{t}u_{0}
is a solution of the heat equation continuous on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For
t>0
, consider the collection time
t
evolutions of positive mass one initial data,

\mathcal{H}^{+}_{1}(t)=\left\{\mathcal{H}_{t}u_{0},u_{0}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),u_{0}>0,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}d\mathcal{L}^{N}=1\right\}\subset C^{\infty}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

For any
\delta>0
, the Gaussian profiles satisfy

\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)=\left\{\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2}}{4t}}:\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\}\subset\mathcal{H}^{+}_{1}(t+\delta).

For
t>0
, any
U\in\mathcal{H}_{1}^{+}(t)\cup\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
, satisfies Hamilton’s matrix Harnack inequality:

(1.3)

\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log U+\frac{1}{2t}g_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 0.

When traced this is the Li-Yau Harnack inequality. Both inequalities hold in more general settings – see
[
29
,
33
]
.
For the convenience of the reader, an elementary proof of this fact in Euclidean space and for positive solutions given by convolving with the heat kernel as in (
1.2
) is provided in Lemma
2.7
.

Hamilton observed in
[
30
]
, that (
1.3
) could allowed one to generalize the Huisken monotonicity formula
[
31
]
– see also
[
27
]
. It was this observation that was used in
[
34
]
and which we will also exploit. Following Magni-Mantegazza, for
t>0
and
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
an
n
-dimensional submanifold set

\sigma_{MM}[\Sigma,t]=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{H}^{+}_{1}(t)}(4\pi t)^{\frac{N-n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}Ud\mathcal{H}^{n}.

It is shown in
[
34
]
that it suffices to take the sup over
U\in\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
and so

\sigma_{MM}[\Sigma,t]=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)}(4\pi t)^{\frac{N-n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}Ud\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]=\sup_{t>0}\sigma_{MM}[\Sigma,t].

That is, the Magni-Mantegezza functionals lead to the Colding-Minicozzi entropy.

We modify the approach of Magni-Mantegazza by focusing on the condition (
1.3
). To that end, for a positive function
U\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, set

\tau(U)=\sup\left\{\tau\geq 0:2\tau\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log U+g_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 0\right\}

which we call the
virtual time
of
U
. Note that
\tau(U)\geq 0
, but can be equal to
0
. When
U\in\mathcal{H}^{+}_{1}(t)\cup\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
,
t>0
, (
1.3
) holds and so

\tau(U)\geq t>0

and one has equality for
U\in\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
. That is, for positive solutions of the heat equation the virtual time estimates the real time from above. When
u_{0}\in C^{2}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
has
\tau(u_{0})=\tau_{0}>0
and
U(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}_{t}u_{0}
is heat flow with initial data
u_{0}
the proof of matrix Harnack inequality from
[
29
]
suggests that

(1.4)

\tau(U(t,\cdot))\geq t+\tau_{0}.

That is, the virtual time of a positive solution of the heat equation grows at least linearly in the evolution time.
This is slightly subtle due to the non-compactness of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and is proved in Proposition
2.7
using ideas from
[
16
]
.

For
T>0
, set

\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)=\left\{u\in C^{2}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u>0,\tau(u)\geq T,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}ud\mathcal{L}^{N}=1\right\}.

Clearly, for
0<T_{1}<T_{2}
,
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T_{2})\subset\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T_{1})
. As observed above

(1.5)

\mathcal{H}_{1}^{+}(T)\cup\mathcal{G}_{1}(T)\subset\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T).

The inclusion is strict as the elements of
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{+}(T)
must also have strong regularity properties not required of elements in
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(t)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\subset\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
for
t\geq T
and
\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\cap\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)=\emptyset
for
0<t<T

Standard facts about heat flow and (
1.4
) also imply,

\mathcal{H}_{t}(\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T))\subset\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T+t).

Given an
n
-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
we define its
virtual entropy
to be

\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma]=\sup_{T>0}\sup_{U\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)}\left((4\pi T)^{\frac{N-n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}Ud\mathcal{H}^{n}\right).

As in
[
30
,
34
]
, this quantity is monotone along reasonable mean curvature flows.

Theorem 1.6
.

If
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a mean curvature flow of
n
-dimensional proper submanifolds with
\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma_{0}]<\infty
, then

t\mapsto\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma_{t}]

is monotone non-increasing in
t
.

It follows immediately from (
1.5
) that

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma].

The long-time behavior of suitable solutions of the heat equation is towards the Gaussian – see, e.g.,
[
37
]
. Using Theorem
1.6
, this suggests that when
\Sigma
is a self-shrinker,

(1.6)

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]=\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma].

This is proved in Theorem
3.6
. It is unclear to the author how general this equality is.

1.2.
Stable conformal volume

Finally, we introduce a functional obtained by “stabilizing” the (normalized) Li-Yau conformal volume in a manner reminiscent of the way stable homotopy groups are introduced. This is distinct from the stabilization procedure of
[
32
]
which involved conformally embedding in higher and higher dimensional spheres. This quantity is related to both the Colding-Minicozzi and virtual entropies.

Given a
n
-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, its
stable conformal volume
is defined as

\bar{\lambda}^{\infty}_{LY}[\Sigma]=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n+2m}[\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}^{2m}].

Crucially, this sequence is monotonically increasing in the stabilization parameter:

Proposition 1.7
.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a
n
-dimensional proper submanifold, then

\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n+2m}[\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}^{2m}]\leq\bar{\lambda}^{\infty}_{LY}[\Sigma].

In particular,
m\mapsto\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n+2m}[\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}^{2m}]
is increasing in
m
and so

\bar{\lambda}^{\infty}_{LY}[\Sigma]=\lim_{m\to\infty}\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n+2m}[\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}^{2m}].

In contrast with process in
[
32
]
, this method of stabilization breaks the full conformal invariance – though the functionals remain invariant under Euclidean translations and dilations. For instance, it follows from
[
14
]
that

\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n+2}[\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}^{2}]=1

if and only if
\Sigma
is an affine
n
-plane. In particular, even though
\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\mathbb{R}^{n}]=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\mathbb{S}^{n}]=1
,

1=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\mathbb{S}^{n}]<\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n+2}[\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}].

Rather remarkably, the stable conformal volume lies between the Colding-Minicozzi entropy and the virtual entropy.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
n
-dimensional proper submanifold,
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma].

That is, the Colding-Minicozzi entropy of
\Sigma
provides a lower bound for its stable conformal volume while the virtual entropy provides an upper bound.

These inequalities are sharp as, when
\Sigma
is a self-shrinker, appealing to (
1.6
) yields

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[\Sigma]=\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma].

Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from this, Proposition
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
.

2.
Properties of elements of
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
and their heat flows

In
[
29
]
, Hamilton used a (tensor) maximum principle to prove the matrix Harnack inequality (
1.3
) for positive solutions of the heat equation. We use the same idea to establish a linear in (real) time growth of the measure of convexity, i.e., the
t
in (
1.3
). In
[
29
]
the underlying manifold is closed and so, as we work in Euclidean space, we instead adapt an argument of
[
16
]
. While
[
29
]
treats quite general positive solutions of the heat equation, in order to simplify exposition we focus on the subclass of solutions given by (
1.2
) with initial data in
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
,
T>0
. As such, we establish some basic properties of elements of
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
and of their evolution by (
1.2
).

We first give an elementary proof that (
1.3
) holds for elements of
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{+}(t)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
u_{0}\in C^{0}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
u_{0}\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}d\mathcal{L}^{N}=1
, the function

U(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}_{t}u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{+}_{1}(t)
given by (
1.2
)
satisfies, for
t>0
,

\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\log U(t,\mathbf{x})\geq-\frac{1}{2t}g_{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

The hypotheses on
u_{0}
ensure that, for
t>0
,

U>0\mbox{ and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U(t,\mathbf{x})d\mathcal{L}^{N}=1.

As
u_{0}
is integrable and, for
t>0
,
|\nabla^{i}_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\leq C_{N,i}t^{-\frac{N+2i}{2}}
, one may differentiate under the integral sign and obtain for any parallel unit vector field
\mathbf{v}
that,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle U(t,\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(\mathbf{y})\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})d\mathcal{L}^{N}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(\mathbf{y})\left(\frac{(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}))^{2}}{\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{2}}-\frac{|\mathbf{v}|^{2}}{2t}\right)\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})d\mathcal{L}^{N}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=-\frac{U(t,\mathbf{x})}{2t}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(u_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{y})\frac{\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}{\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}\right)^{2}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})d\mathcal{L}^{N}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{U(t,\mathbf{x})}{2t}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}+U^{-1}(t,\mathbf{x})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(\mathbf{y})\frac{\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}{\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})d\mathcal{L}^{N}(\mathbf{y})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{U(t,\mathbf{x})}{2t}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}+U^{-1}(t,\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x}))^{2}.

That is, for
t>0
, we get the claimed inequality

\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log U(t,\mathbf{x})\geq-\frac{1}{2t}g_{\mathbb{R}},

∎

We next establish a useful pointwise bound on elements of
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There is a
C_{0}=C_{0}(N)>0
so that, for
f\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
and
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{B_{\sqrt{T}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})}fd\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{N}{2}}.

Proof.

The fact that
f\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
implies

-\frac{N}{2T}\leq\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\log f.

Hence, for the given
\mathbf{x}_{0}
, the function

F_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}(\mathbf{x})=\log f(\mathbf{x})+\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2}}{4T}

is sub harmonic and so, by the mean value inequality, for any
R>0
,

\log f(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\log F_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{N}R^{N}}\int_{B_{R}(\mathbf{x}_{0})}\log f+\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2}}{4T}d\mathcal{L}^{N},

where
\omega_{N}=|B_{1}|
, the volume of unit
N
-ball.
Taking
R=\sqrt{T}
, yields

\log f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{N}T^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{B_{\sqrt{T}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})}\log fd\mathcal{L}^{n}+\frac{1}{4(N+2)}.

It follows from Jensen’s inequality that

f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{B_{\sqrt{T}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})}fd\mathcal{L}^{N},\mbox{ where }C_{0}=C_{0}(N)=\omega_{N}^{-1}\exp(\frac{1}{4(N+2)}).

To complete the proof, observe that as
f\geq 0
,

f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\leq\int_{B_{\sqrt{T}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})}fd\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}fd\mathcal{L}^{N}=C_{0}T^{-\frac{N}{2}}.

∎

We now establish some useful estimates for elements of
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
u\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
,
T>0
. The following hold:

(1)

\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}u(\mathbf{x})=0
and there is a point
\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{z}_{0}(u)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
so that

0<u(\mathbf{z}_{0})e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}_{0}|^{2}}{4T}}\leq u(\mathbf{x})\leq u(\mathbf{z}_{0})\leq(4\pi T)^{-\frac{N}{2}};

(2)

\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u(\mathbf{x})|=0
and

|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\leq\frac{4\pi}{e}(4\pi T)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}u(\mathbf{x})\leq\frac{4\pi}{e}(4\pi T)^{-N-1};

(3)

|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and there is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(N)>0
so

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u|d\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq C_{1}T^{-1}.

Remark 2.4
.

In particular, for
u\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
,
T>0
,
\tau(u)<\frac{1}{4\pi}u(\mathbf{z}_{0})^{-\frac{2}{N}}<\infty
.

Proof.

As
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u>0
, for every
\epsilon>0
, there is
R_{\epsilon}
so that

0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{\epsilon}}}ud\mathcal{L}^{N}<\epsilon.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that, for any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\not\in B_{R_{\epsilon}+\sqrt{T}}
,

u(\mathbf{x}_{0})\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{\epsilon}}}ud\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{N}{2}}\epsilon.

In other words,

\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}u(\mathbf{x})=0.

As
u
is also continuous, there is a
\mathbf{z}_{0}
so that

\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(\mathbf{x})=u(\mathbf{z}_{0}).

Hence,
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u(\mathbf{z}_{0})=\mathbf{0}
.
Observe that as
U\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
,
u
satisfies the convexity property given by (
1.3
) with
t=T
. This implies that

-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}_{0}|^{2}}{4T}+\log u(\mathbf{z}_{0})\leq\log u(\mathbf{x}).

This yields the claimed lower bound
u
. The upper bound on
u(\mathbf{z}_{0})
, and hence on
u
, follows by integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and using that
u
has mass one. We have established Item (1).

For the gradient estimate pick
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Set
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}\log u(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathbf{v}
and

Q(\mathbf{x})=-\frac{1}{4T}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}-2T\mathbf{v}|^{2}+\log u(\mathbf{x}_{0})+T|\mathbf{v}|^{2}.

Thus,
Q(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\log u(\mathbf{x}_{0}),

\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}Q(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathbf{v}=\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}\log u(\mathbf{x}_{0})
and
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}Q=-\frac{g_{\mathbb{R}}}{2T}
.
The convexity of
\log u
, i.e., (
1.3
), ensures that

Q(\mathbf{x})\leq\log u(\mathbf{x}).

Hence, integrating
\exp(Q)
and using the mass of
u
is one yields,

(4\pi T)^{\frac{N}{2}}u(\mathbf{x}_{0})e^{T|\mathbf{v}|^{2}}\leq 1.

Using the elementary identity

-x\log x\leq e^{-1}

and the upper bound on
u
yields the claimed bounds

\displaystyle|\nabla u(\mathbf{x}_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-T^{-1}u^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\log\left((4\pi T)^{\frac{N}{2}}u(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\pi}{e}(4\pi T)^{-\frac{N}{2}-1}u(\mathbf{x}_{0})\leq\frac{4\pi}{e}(4\pi T)^{-N-1}.

Moreover, as
u(\mathbf{x})
tends to zero as
|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty
this implies same is true of
\nabla u(\mathbf{x})
. This completes proof of Item (2).
While we don’t use it, we note the lower bound on
u
yields

|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u(\mathbf{x}_{0})|^{2}\leq\frac{|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{z}_{0}|^{2}}{4T^{2}}u^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{0}).

For Item (3), we first observe that, in the sense of symmetric matrices,

-\frac{1}{2T}g_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log u=\frac{\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u}{u}-\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u\otimes\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u}{u^{2}}\leq\frac{\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u}{u}.

By Item (1),
0<u<(4\pi T)^{-\frac{N}{2}}
so

(2.1)

-2\pi(4\pi T)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}g_{\mathbb{R}}\leq-\frac{u}{2T}g_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u\mbox{ and }-\frac{N}{2T}u\leq\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u.

An immediate consequence of this is that

|\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u|\leq\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u+NT^{-1}u.

For
R>0
, let
\phi_{R}=(1-(4R)^{-2}|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{2}
this is a smooth function which vanishes to first order on
\partial B_{2R}
and satisfies
\phi_{R}\leq 1
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{R}\leq C(N)R^{-2}
on
B_{2R}
where
C(N)>0
is a dimensional constant.
The above inequalities and an integration by parts implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u|d\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq\int_{B_{2R}}\phi_{R}|\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u|d\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq\int_{B_{2R}}\phi_{R}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u+NT^{-1}u\right)d\mathcal{L}^{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2R}}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{R}+NT^{-1}\phi_{R}\right)ud\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq\left(C(N)R^{-2}+NT^{-1}\right)\int_{B_{2R}}ud\mathcal{L}^{N}.

As
u
has mass one, sending
R\to\infty
shows that
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u|d\mathcal{L}^{N}\leq 4NT^{-1}.

Finally, we observe that the lower bounds on the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u
imply that

|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u|^{2}\leq N\left(|\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u|+\frac{(N-1)u}{2T}\right)^{2}\mbox{ and so }|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u|\leq\sqrt{N}|\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}u|+\frac{N^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2T}u.

The final claim follows after making an appropriate choice of
C_{1}=C_{1}(N)>0
.
∎

Next we establish some properties of the evolution by (
1.2
) of elements in
\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(\tau_{0})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
\tau_{0}>0
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(\tau_{0})
, let
U(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}_{t}u_{0}

be the solution of the heat equation on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
given by (
1.2
). This solution has the following properties:

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U(t,\mathbf{x})=1
, for all
t\geq 0
;

(2)

U(t,\mathbf{x})\geq u_{0}(\mathbf{z}_{0})(4\pi(t+\tau_{0}))^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}_{0}|^{2}}{4(t+\tau_{0})}}>0.

(3)

U(t,\mathbf{x})+\tau_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x})|\leq C_{2}\tau_{0}^{-\frac{N}{2}}
, for all
t\geq 0
;

(4)

|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x})|\leq C_{2}\tau_{0}^{-1}t^{-\frac{N}{2}}
for all
t>0
;

Here
C_{2}=C_{2}(N)>0
and
\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{z}_{0}(u_{0})
is given by Lemma
2.3
.
Moreover,

(5)

\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x})\geq-\frac{U(t,\mathbf{x})}{2\tau_{0}}g_{\mathbb{R}}
for all
t\geq 0
;

(6)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}U(t,\cdot)=u_{0}
, in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(7)

U,\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U,\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}U\in C^{0}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Remark 2.6
.

In fact,
U
is the unique continuous and non-negative solution of the heat equation on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
with initial data
u_{0}
– see
[
25
,
26
,
33
]
.

Proof.

The first item is a consequence of the fact that
u_{0}
has mass one and Fubini’s theorem. The second follows from Item (1) of Lemma
2.3
and an explicit integration of (
1.2
).

For the remaining items we observe that as
u_{0}
is integrable and for
t>0
and
i\geq 0
,

|\nabla_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}^{i}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\leq C_{N,i}t^{-\frac{N+2i}{2}}

we may differentiate under the integral sign and obtain

\nabla^{i}_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(\mathbf{y})\nabla^{i}_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})d\mathcal{L}^{N}.

As the symmetries of the heat kernel ensure

\nabla_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{x}}^{i}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=(-1)^{i}\nabla_{\mathbb{R},\mathbf{y}}^{i}\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

The uniform boundedness of
u_{0}
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}
and fact that
|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
allows one to integrate by parts and obtain, for a fixed parallel unit vector field
\mathbf{v}
and
t>0
,

\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(\mathbf{y})\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})d\mathcal{L}^{N}(\mathbf{y});

\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})\mathcal{H}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})d\mathcal{L}^{N}(\mathbf{y}).

Items (3) is then immediate consequences of the uniform bounds of Item (1) and (2) of Lemma
2.3
. Likewise, Item (4) follows directly from the integral bound of Item (3) of Lemma
2.3
. It is also clear Item (5) follows from (
2.1
) and representation formula (
1.2
).

As both
u_{0}
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}
are continuous and tend to zero at infinity, they are both uniformly continuous and bounded. Standard results about approximations to the identity give

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\|U(t,\cdot)-u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\cdot)-\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{\infty}=0.

It is also an elementary fact that because
|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{0}
is
C^{2}
, for any
\mathbf{x}_{0}

\lim_{t\to 0^{+},\mathbf{x}\to\mathbf{x}_{0}}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}U(t,\mathbf{x})=\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{0}).

Moreover, this convergence can be taken to be uniform on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Together this gives Item (6). Item (7) is an immediate consequence.
∎

We can now establish the linear increase in virtual time under heat flow.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose
u_{0}\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(\tau_{0})
for
\tau_{0}>0
and let
U(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}_{t}u_{0}

be the solution of the heat equation on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
given by (
1.2
).
For all
t\geq 0
,

U(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(\tau_{0}+t).

Moreover, if
2(\tau_{0}+t_{0})\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log U(t_{0},\mathbf{x}_{0})+g_{\mathbb{R}}=0
for some
t_{0}>0
and
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then
U(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}(\tau_{0}+t,\cdot,\mathbf{x}_{0}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{G}_{1}(\tau_{0}+t)
for some
\mathbf{x}_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

The positivity and unit mass condition are preserved by Lemma
2.5
. Hence, it suffices to establish that, for all
t>0
,

(\tau_{0}+t)\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log U(t,\mathbf{x})+g_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 0.

To that end, fix a parallel unit length vector field
\mathbf{v}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By the rotational symmetry of the problem, is enough to take
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
. Set

\displaystyle H(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=U(t,\mathbf{x})\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log U(t,\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})=U(t,\mathbf{x})\partial^{2}_{1}\log U(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\partial^{2}_{1}U(t,\mathbf{x})-\frac{(\partial_{1}U(t,\mathbf{x}))^{2}}{U(t,\mathbf{x})}.

The hypotheses ensure that

2\tau_{0}H(0,\mathbf{x})+u_{0}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0.

We compute that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H=\partial_{1}^{2}\partial_{t}U-\frac{2\partial_{1}U\partial_{1}\partial_{t}U}{U}+\frac{(\partial_{1}U)^{2}\partial_{t}U}{U^{2}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}^{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}U-\frac{2\partial_{1}U\partial_{1}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}U}{U}+\frac{(\partial_{1}U)^{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}U}{U^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}^{2}U-\frac{\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{1}U)^{2}-2|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}U|^{2}}{U}-(\partial_{1}U)^{2}(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}U^{-1})+2\frac{(\partial_{1}U|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U|)^{2}}{U^{3}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}^{2}U-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(\partial_{1}U)^{2}}{U}\right)+\frac{2|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}U|^{2}}{U}-\frac{4\partial_{1}U\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}U}{U^{2}}+2\frac{(\partial_{1}U|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U|)^{2}}{U^{3}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}H+2U^{-1}|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}U-U^{-1}\partial_{1}U\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}U|^{2}\geq\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}H+2U^{-1}\left|\partial_{1}^{2}U-U^{-1}(\partial_{1}U)^{2}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}H+2U^{-1}H^{2}.

Now set

N(t,\mathbf{x})=2(t+\tau_{0})H(t,\mathbf{x})+U(t,\mathbf{x})

we see that
N(0,\mathbf{x})\geq 0
and

(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}})N\geq 2H+4(t+\tau_{0})U^{-1}H^{2}=2U^{-1}HN

As
U(0,\mathbf{x})=u_{0}\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(\tau_{0})
, Lemmas
2.3
and
2.5
together imply that

\displaystyle H(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2\tau_{0}}u_{0}(\mathbf{x})-C(t+\tau_{0})^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}_{0}|^{2}}{4(t+\tau_{0})}}

\displaystyle\geq-C-C(t+\tau_{0})^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}_{0}|^{2}}{4(t+\tau_{0})}}

where
C=C(\tau_{0},N,u_{0})>0
is a constant and
\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{z}_{0}(u_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a fixed point.

Now let

\Psi_{T}(t,\mathbf{x})=e^{t}(T-t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}_{0}|^{2}}{4(T-t)}}

One verifies that
\Psi_{T}
satisfies

\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\right)\Psi_{T}=\Psi_{T}

on
(-\infty,T]
.
Moreover,
\Psi_{T}
has very rapid growth as
\mathbf{x}\to\infty
.

Set

N_{\epsilon}(t,\mathbf{x})=N(t,\mathbf{x})+\epsilon\Psi_{\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})

which is smooth for
t\in[0,\frac{\tau_{0}}{2})

One readily checks that

N_{\epsilon}(0,\mathbf{x})\geq\epsilon\Psi_{\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}}(0,\mathbf{x})\geq T^{-\frac{N}{2}}\epsilon>0

where
C=C(\tau_{0},N)
.
Likewise, for
t\in(0,\frac{\tau_{0}}{2})
and outside of a very large ball,

N_{\epsilon}(t,\mathbf{x})\geq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\Psi_{\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})>0.

Now suppose that
N_{\epsilon}(t_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{0})<0
for some
t_{0}\in(0,\frac{\tau_{0}}{2})
. It follows that there is a value
t_{1}\in(0,t_{0}]
and
\mathbf{x}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

0>N_{\epsilon}(t_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{0})\geq N_{\epsilon}(t_{1},\mathbf{x}_{1})=\min_{[0,t_{1}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}N_{\epsilon}.

Hence, at
(t_{1},\mathbf{x}_{1})
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}\right)N_{\epsilon}=2HU^{-1}N=2U^{-1}HN+\epsilon\Psi_{\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}}>2H(1+2(t_{1}+\tau_{0})U^{-1}H),

which is incompatible with
H(t_{1},\mathbf{x}_{1})\geq 0
and so at
(t_{1},\mathbf{x}_{1})

0>H\mbox{ and }U^{-1}N=1+2(t_{1}+\tau_{0})U^{-1}H>0.

That is,

N_{\epsilon}(t_{1},\mathbf{x}_{1})>N(t_{1},\mathbf{x}_{1})>0

which is a contradiction. Hence,
N_{\epsilon}>0
on
[0,\frac{\tau_{0}}{2})
. By taking
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
we see that

N(t,\cdot)\geq 0

for
t\in(0,\frac{\tau_{0}}{2})
. In particular,

U\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{4},\cdot\right)\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}\left(\tau_{0}+\frac{\tau_{0}}{4}\right).

As
\tau_{0}+\frac{\tau_{0}}{4}\geq\tau_{0}
, one may translate in time and apply the same argument to obtain

N(t,\cdot)\geq 0

on
[0,\frac{3}{4}\tau_{0})
.
Iterating this completes the proof that
U(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(\tau_{0}+t)
.

Finally, if for some
t_{0}>0
,
\mathbf{x}_{0}
,
2(\tau_{0}+t_{0})\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\log U(t_{0},\mathbf{x}_{0})+g_{\mathbb{R}}=0
, then for any choice of
\mathbf{v}
we have that
N\geq 0
and
N(t_{0},\mathbf{x}_{0})=0
. By the strict maximum principle this means
N
identically vanishes and the claim follows.

∎

for a proof and further discussion.

Proposition 2.8
.

3.
Properties of Virtual Entropy

In this section we establish some properties of of the virtual entropy. First, we recall that finite Colding-Minicozzi entropy is equivalent to uniform bounds on area ratios.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
an
n
-dimensional proper submanifold

|\Sigma\cap B_{R}(p)|\leq e^{\pi}\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]R^{n}.

Conversely, suppose there is a
\Theta\geq 1
so, for all
p
and
R>0
,

|\Sigma\cap B_{R}(p)|\leq\Theta\omega_{n}R^{n},

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\Theta.

Proof.

Let
T=(4\pi)^{-1}R^{2}
, one has

R^{-n}e^{-\pi}1_{B_{R}(p)}\leq(4\pi T)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}(p)|^{2}}{4T}}

Hence,

|\Sigma\cap B_{R}(p)|=\int_{B_{R}(p)\cap\Sigma}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq e^{\pi}\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]R^{n}.

This establishes the first claim.

For the second we observe that arbitrary scalings and translations of
\Sigma
satisfy the same area ratio bounds. Hence, it is enough to check using the co-area formula that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{4}}\frac{d}{dR}|\Sigma\cap B_{R}|dR=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{R}{2}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{4}}|\Sigma\cap B_{R}|dR

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Theta\omega_{n}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}R^{n+1}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{4}}dR=\Theta(4\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Where the final equality can be verified by computing on the flat
n
-plane. It follows that

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\Theta.

∎

We next establish that the virtual and Colding-Minicozzi entropies are comparable.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a non-empty open subset and
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
an
n
-dimensional submanifold. There is a constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(N)\geq 1
so that if
f\in\mathcal{VT}_{1}^{+}(T)
,
T>0
, then

(4\pi T)^{\frac{N-n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma\cap\Omega}fd\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq C_{3}\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]\int_{\mathcal{T}_{2\sqrt{T}}(\Omega)}fd\mathcal{L}^{N}.

where
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Omega)=\bigcup_{p\in\Omega}B_{r}(p)
.
In particular,

\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma]\leq C_{3}\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma].

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma\cap B_{\sqrt{T}}(q)}fd\mathcal{H}^{n}

When

n

Likewise, when
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
a general

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that

\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n+2m}[\Sigma]=\hat{C}_{n,m}\sup_{\rho>0,\mathbf{y}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\Sigma}\hat{W}_{n+m,\rho}^{n}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}_{0})d\mathcal{H}^{n},

Loxodromes

For
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
consider the rectifiable set:

S_{\alpha}^{0}=\left\{(e^{t}\cos\alpha t,e^{t}\sin\alpha t):t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

This is a smooth curve away from
\mathbf{0}
.
When
\alpha>0
this is a logarithmic spiral (i.e., a special case of a loxodrome or rhumb line) and for
\alpha=0
it is the non-negative part of the
x
-axis. If
R_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is rotation counter clockwise by
\theta
, then we see that for
\alpha>0
,
S^{\theta}_{\alpha}=R_{\theta}(S_{\alpha}^{0})=e^{-\alpha^{-1}\theta}\cdot S_{\alpha}^{0}
, i.e., scaling
S_{\alpha}^{0}
about the origin is the same as rotating it. Consider the doubled curve

S_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha}^{0}\cup S_{\alpha}^{\pi}

which is symmetric with respect to reflection through the origin.

Proposition A.1
.

For any
\alpha\geq 0
one has,

\lambda_{CM}^{1}[S_{\alpha}]=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[S_{\alpha}]=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{1}[S_{\alpha}]=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}.

Remark A.2
.

This shows the existence of multiplicity one curves that have entropy any value larger than one. In particular, it suggests some of the results of
[
17
]
are optimal.

Proof.

When
\alpha=0
this is elementary as
S_{0}
is a line. In what follows we suppose
\alpha>0
.
In general, the parameterization of
S_{\alpha}^{0}
and a change of variables gives

\int_{S_{\alpha}}f(|\mathbf{x}|)d\mathcal{H}^{1}=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(e^{r})\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}e^{r}dr=2\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u)du.

As scaling
S_{\alpha}
is the same as rotating it around the origin, it follows from the change of variables formula and rotational symmetry of the weights that

\lambda_{CM}^{1}[S_{\alpha}]=\sup_{\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}(4\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{S_{\alpha}}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

Hence,

\lambda_{CM}^{1}[S_{\alpha}]\geq(4\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{S_{\alpha}}e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{1}=2(4\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{4}}du=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}.

For the same reason,

\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[S_{\alpha}]\geq\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{1}[S_{\alpha}]=(2\pi)^{-1}\sup_{\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{S_{\alpha}}W^{1}_{1}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0})d\mathcal{H}^{1}\geq\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}.

In order to establish the claim, let us suppose
\mathbf{x}_{0}=(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a fixed unit vector.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\geq 0
set

L_{m}^{\alpha,\mathbf{x}_{0}}(t)=\int_{S_{\alpha}}W^{m+1}_{1}(\mathbf{x}-t\mathbf{x}_{0})d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

Up to a constant factor, these are the integrals that compute
\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{1+2m}[S_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{R}^{2m}]
.

Using the parameterization of
S_{\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle L_{m-1}^{\alpha,\mathbf{x}_{0}}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{4}|\mathbf{x}-t\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2})^{m}}d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}e^{r}}{(1+\frac{1}{4}((e^{r}\cos(\alpha r)-tx_{0})^{2}+(e^{r}\sin(\alpha r)-ty_{0})^{2}))^{m}}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}e^{r}}{(1+\frac{1}{4}((e^{r}\cos(\alpha r)+tx_{0})^{2}+(e^{r}\sin(\alpha r)+ty_{0})^{2}))^{m}}dr

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{r}}{(I_{1}(r)+I_{2}(r))^{m}}+\frac{e^{r}}{(I_{1}(r)-I_{2}(r))^{m}}dr

where here

I_{1}(r)=1+\frac{1}{4}(e^{2r}+t^{2})\geq 1\mbox{ and }I_{2}(r)=-\frac{1}{2}tx_{0}e^{r}\cos(\alpha r)-\frac{1}{2}ty_{0}e^{r}\sin(\alpha r).

By the Cauchy-Schwarz and absorbing inequality,

I_{2}(r)^{2}\leq J_{2}(r)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}t^{2}e^{2r}\leq\frac{1}{16}(t^{2}+e^{2r})^{2}\leq I_{1}(r)^{2}.

The following inequality holds: for

,
x\geq 0
and an integer

(x-a)^{m}+(x+a)^{m}\leq(x-b)^{m}+(x+b)^{m}.

Using this we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{L_{m-1}^{\alpha,\mathbf{x}_{0}}(t)}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(I_{1}(r)-I_{2}(r))^{m}+(I_{1}(r)+I_{2}(r))^{m}}{(I_{1}(r)^{2}-I_{2}(r)^{2})^{m}}e^{r}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(I_{1}(r)-J_{2}(r))^{m}+(I_{1}(r)+J_{2}(r))^{m}}{(I_{1}(r)^{2}-J_{2}(r)^{2})^{m}}e^{r}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{r}}{(I_{1}(r)+J_{2}(r))^{m}}+\frac{e^{r}}{(I_{1}(r)-J_{2}(r))^{m}}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{m-1}^{0,\mathbf{e}_{1}}(t)}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}=\frac{L_{m-1}^{0,\mathbf{e}_{1}}(0)}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}

Where here the final equality follows from the observation that last line is exactly what would be obtained from evaluating on
S_{0}
, i.e., the
x
-axis, and with
\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{e}_{1}=(1,0)
. In particular, the value is independent of
t
. We conclude that for all
m\geq 0
,

\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{1+2m}[S_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{R}^{2m}]\leq\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{1+2m}[S_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{2m}]=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}.

Combined with the lower bound already established this verifies that

\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[S_{\alpha}]=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{1}[S_{\alpha}].

Finally, by Proposition
4.4
and the previously established lower bound

\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\leq\lambda_{CM}^{1}[S_{\alpha}]\leq\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[S_{\alpha}]=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
Further Questions and Observations

First, we ask whether the inequalities between Colding-Minicozzi entropy, stable conformal volume and virtual entropy are ever strict when
\Sigma
is not a self-shrinker.

Question B.1
.

Is there a
n
-dimensional submanifold, possibly singular,
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]<\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma]
? More generally, can one ever have
\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]<\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[\Sigma]<\lambda_{V}^{n}[\Sigma]
? If
\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]<\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{\infty}[\Sigma]
can occur, may one also have
\lambda_{CM}^{n}[\Sigma]<\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{n}[\Sigma]
?

It would be particularly interesting to compute
\lambda_{V}^{1}[S_{\alpha}]
for the
S_{\alpha}
from Appendix
A
.

Question B.2
.

What is the lowest entropy self-shrinking
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
?

When
N=3
, any immersed
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
must have a triple point
[
3
]
, and so entropy at least three. There are no candidate self-shrinkers in this setting – see however
[
38
]
where the existence of a shrinking Möbius band bounded by a straight line in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is shown. For
N\geq 5
, it follows from
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
that
\Sigma_{\mathbb{RP}^{2}}
is (Colding-Minicozzi) entropy unstable, suggesting there should be a lower entropy unoriented shrinker. Likewise, as
\Sigma_{\mathbb{RP}^{2}}
is unstable as a minimal surface in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
, there is a non-constant flow out of it that remains in the sphere. As observed in
[
39
]
such a flow has decreasing Colding-Minicozzi entropy when viewed in the ambient
\mathbb{R}^{5}
. As the flow is constrained to lie in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
and must become singular, this suggests the existence of an unoriented self-shrinker of lower entropy in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. However, the fact that
\lambda^{2}_{CM}[\Sigma_{\mathbb{RP}^{2}}]>2
introduces significant technical issues.

In their proof of the Willmore conjecture
[
35
]
, Marques-Neves showed that any positive genus surface in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
sits inside a five parameter family and at least one member of the family has area at least that of the Clifford torus. Together with Theorem
1.1
, this suggests:

Conjecture B.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a closed self-shrinker of positive genus, then

1.57\approx\frac{\pi}{2}=\bar{\lambda}_{LY}^{2}[\Sigma_{C}]\leq\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\Sigma]

where here
\Sigma_{C}\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}
is the Clifford torus.

It was shown in
[
10
]
that such a
\Sigma
satisfies the bound

1.52\approx\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}]\leq\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\Sigma].

In
[
4
]
, numerical methods established that there a rotationally symmetric genus one self-shrinker, i.e., an Angenent torus
[
2
]
,
\Sigma_{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, whose entropy satisfies

\lambda_{CM}^{2}[\Sigma_{A}]\approx 1.852.

Recently, Chu-Sun
[
20
]
have shown that no Angenent torus can be the lowest entropy positive genus shrinker. Their work suggests that the lowest entropy is achieved by a non-compact shrinker of genus one.

Finally, by using the heat kernel on hyperbolic space, a notion of Colding-Minicozzi entropy has been extended to some spaces of non-positive curvature
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
39
]
.

Question B.4
.

Is there a useful notion of virtual entropy in other ambient Riemannian manifolds – in particular those of non-positive curvature?

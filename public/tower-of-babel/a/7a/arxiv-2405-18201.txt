Automorphisms of unstable ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1} -bundles
János Kollár

Abstract
. Let P → X P\to X be a ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1} -bundle over a variety X X . The aim of this note is to understand all connected, algebraic groups Aut ∘ ⁡ ( P ) ⊂ G ⊂ Bir ⁡ ( X × ℙ 1 ) . \operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(P)\subset G\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}). We get a quite complete answer if Aut ∘ ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X) is a maximal, connected, algebraic subgroup of Bir ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{Bir}(X) , and P P is sufficiently unstable. This gives examples of connected, algebraic subgroups of Bir ⁡ ( ℙ 4 ) \operatorname{Bir}({\mathbb{P}}^{4}) that are not contained in any maximal one.

Corollary 4

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}\bigl({\mathbb{P}}_{X}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}{\oplus}L)\bigr)

1.
Groups acting on ruled varieties

The idea behind the proof could be summarized as follows.

Principle 9
.

Let
G\subset\operatorname{Bir}(Y)
be an algebraic subgroup.
If a normal, unipotent subgroup
U\subset G
acts on
Y
with small orbits, then
every
G\subset\bar{G}\subset\operatorname{Bir}(Y)
is likely to have
a normal, unipotent subgroup
\bar{U}\subset\bar{G}
that acts on
Y
with the same orbits.

Theorem
12
illustrates this when the orbits are 1 dimensional.

Definition 10
.

Let

\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1})

denote the subgroup of those birational maps
\phi:X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\dasharrow X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
that commute with projection to
X
and map
X\times\{\infty\}
birationally to
X\times\{\infty\}
. Thus there is a birational map

\phi_{X}:X\dasharrow X
and a commutative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\dasharrow}}&X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\\
\pi\downarrow&&\hphantom{\pi}\downarrow\pi\\
X&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{X}}}{{\dasharrow}}&X,\end{array}

where
\pi
is the first coordinate projection.

Note that
\phi\mapsto\phi_{X}
is a group homomorphism; its kernel is denoted by
\operatorname{Bir}_{X}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\bigr)
. Thus we have a group extension

1\to\operatorname{Bir}_{X}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\bigr)\to\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)\to\operatorname{Bir}(X)\to 1.

Definition 11
.

Given any connected, algebraic, subgroup

G\subset\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)
,
let
G^{v}:=G\cap\operatorname{Bir}_{X}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\bigr)
denote its

vertical subgroup,
and
G^{h}:=G/G^{v}\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X)

its
horizontal quotient.

Thus we have a group extension

1\to G^{v}\to G\to G^{h}\to 1.

Note that
G^{v}
is a solvable, normal subgroup of
G
.
Let
U^{v}_{G}
denote its unique maximal, connected, unipotent subgroup. It is a normal subgroup of
G
.
Let
U_{G}\subset G
denote a maximal, connected, unipotent subgroup; it is unique up to conjugation.
Note that
U^{v}_{G}\subset U_{G}
, and
U^{h}_{G}:=U_{G}/U^{v}_{G}\subset G^{h}
is a
maximal, connected, unipotent subgroup.
(Connectedness is automatic in characteristic 0.)

The following is the main result about subgroups of

\operatorname{Bir}(X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
.

Theorem 12
.

Let
X
be a normal, projective variety and

G\subset\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)

a connected, algebraic subgroup. Assume that

(12.1)

G^{h}\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X)
is a maximal, connected, algebraic subgroup,

(12.2)

\dim U^{v}_{G}\geq 2
, and

(12.3)

for every connected, algebraic subgroup
\{1\}\neq W\subset U^{h}_{G}
, the fixed point set of the
W
-action by conjugation on
U^{v}_{G}
has
codimension
\geq 2
in
U^{v}_{G}
.

Then every connected, algebraic subgroup

G\subset\bar{G}\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1})

is contained in
\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)
.
Furthermore

(12.4)

\bar{G}^{h}=G^{h}
, so
U^{h}_{\bar{G}}=U^{h}_{G}
,

(12.5)

G^{v}\subset\bar{G}^{v}
, and

(12.6)

U^{v}_{G}\subset U^{v}_{\bar{G}}
.

In particular,
\bar{G}
also satisfies (
12
.1–3).

Note.
(
12
.3) implies (
12
.2) whenever

\{1\}\neq U^{h}_{G}
.

Proof. We may assume that
G\neq\bar{G}
.

First we show that there is an intermediate group

G\subsetneq G_{1}\subset\bar{G},

such that

G_{1}\subset\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)
.

To see this, we apply the purely group theoretic Lemma
16

with
R:=U^{v}_{G}
.
Assumption (
12
.3) says
that the alternative
(
16
.2) does not happen.

Thus there is a subgroup

\{1\}\neq R^{\prime}\subset U^{v}_{G}
whose (connected) normalizer

N_{\bar{G}}(R^{\prime})^{\circ}
is not contained in
G
.

A key point is that generically
R^{\prime}
and
U^{v}_{G}
have the same orbits. In particular,
the closures of the 1-dimensional
R^{\prime}
-orbits are still the
fibers of the projection
\pi:X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to X
. Thus

N_{\bar{G}}(R^{\prime})^{\circ}
birationally preserves
\pi:X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to X
.
The section
X\times\{\infty\}
is the fixed point set of the
R^{\prime}
-action, so this is also preserved. Thus

N_{\bar{G}}(R^{\prime})^{\circ}\subset\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr).

We can then take

G_{1}:=\langle G,N_{\bar{G}}(R^{\prime})^{\circ}\rangle\subset\bar{G}
.

Now
G_{1}
clearly satisfies
G^{v}\subset G_{1}^{v}
,

U^{v}_{G}\subset U^{v}_{G_{1}}
, and

G^{h}\subset G_{1}^{h}\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X)
. Since

G^{h}
is a maximal, connected, algebraic subgroup, we must have
G^{h}=G_{1}^{h}
.
Therefore
G_{1}
also satisfies (
12
.1–3), and
induction on
\dim\bar{G}-\dim G
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 13
.

If we do not assume that
G^{h}
is maximal, the above proof still gives a
G\subsetneq G_{1}\subset\bar{G}
that is a subgroup of

\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)
.
However, if
U_{G}\neq U_{G_{1}}
then
(
12
.3) may not hold for
G_{1}
, and the induction may break down.

2.
Lemmas on algebraic groups

Notation 14
.

For an algebraic group
G
, we let
G^{\circ}
denote the maximal, connected subgroup.

B_{G}\subset G
denotes a Borel subgroup,

U_{G}\subset B_{G}
a maximal, connected, unipotent subgroup,
and
R_{G}\subset U_{G}
the unipotent radical of
G
.

Z(\ )
denotes the center, and
N_{G}(\ )
the normalizer in
G
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
G\subsetneq G^{\prime}
be connected, algebraic groups.
Then there is a subgroup
V\subset U_{G}
of codimension
\leq 1
such that

N_{G^{\prime}}\bigl(Z(V)\bigr)^{\circ}\not\subset G
.

Proof. Let
U_{G}\subset U_{G^{\prime}}
be maximal unipotent subgroups.

If
U_{G}\neq U_{G^{\prime}}
then let
U_{G}\subset N\subset U_{G^{\prime}}
be the normalizer of
U_{G}
in
U_{G^{\prime}}
. Note that
U_{G}\neq N
since
U_{G^{\prime}}
is nilpotent, and

N\not\subset G
since
U_{G}
is a maximal unipotent subgroup of
G
.
Then
N\subset N_{G^{\prime}}(Z(U_{G}))^{\circ}
, and we can take
V:=U_{G}
.

Otherwise
U_{G}=U_{G^{\prime}}
.
For every simple root of
G^{\prime}
there is a minimal parabolic subgroup

B_{G^{\prime}}\subsetneq P_{i}\subset G^{\prime}
; let

V_{i}\subset U_{G}
be its unipotent radical.
Then
P_{i}/V_{i}
is either
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
.
Therefore
V_{i}\subset U_{G^{\prime}}=U_{G}
has codimension 1 and

P_{i}\subset N_{G^{\prime}}(V_{i})
.

The
P_{i}
generate
G^{\prime}
, so
P_{i}\not\subset G
for some
i_{0}
.
Then
V:=V_{i_{0}}
works. ∎

Lemma 16
.

Let
G\subsetneq G^{\prime}
be connected, algebraic groups,

U_{G}\subset G
a maximal, connected, unipotent subgroup
and
\{1\}\neq R\subset U_{G}
a connected, normal subgroup.
Then one of the following holds.

(16.1)

There is a connected subgroup
\{1\}\neq R^{\prime}\subset R
such that

N_{G^{\prime}}(R^{\prime})^{\circ}\not\subset G
.

(16.2)

There is a connected subgroup
\{1\}\neq W\subset U_{G}/R
such that
the
W
-action by conjugation on
R
is
trivial on a subgroup
V_{R}\subset R
of codimension
\leq 1
in
R
.

Proof. By (
15
) there is a subgroup
V\subset U_{G}
of codimension
\leq 1
such that

N_{G^{\prime}}\bigl(Z(V)\bigr)^{\circ}\not\subset G
. We are done if

Z(V)\subset R
.
Otherwise

W:=\langle Z(V),R\rangle/R\cong Z(V)/\bigl(Z(V)\cap R\bigr)

acts trivially on
V_{R}:=V\cap R
, which has codimension
\leq 1
. ∎

3.
Regularizing group actions

First recall some results on automorphisms of vector bundles; see for example
[
Mar71
]
or
[
FFZ23
, Sec.3]
.

17
Automorphisms of vector bundles.

Let
C
be a smooth, projective curve and
E
a rank 2 vector bundle over
C
. Up to twisting with a line bundle, we have one of the following cases.

(17.1)

E
is stable, and then
\operatorname{Aut}_{C}(E)\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

(17.2)

E\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{C}{\oplus}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
, and then
\operatorname{Aut}_{C}(E)\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.

(17.3)

There is an extension
0\to L\to E\to{\mathcal{O}}_{C}\to 0
,
where
\deg L\geq 0
and the extension is non-split if
L\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
.
Then the unipotent radical of
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
is naturally isomorphic to
H^{0}(C,L)
, and

(17.3.a)

\operatorname{Aut}_{C}(E)/H^{0}(C,L)\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
if the extension is non-split, and

(17.3.b)

\operatorname{Aut}_{C}(E)/H^{0}(C,L)\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2}
if the extension is split.

Let now
\bar{X}
be a normal, projective variety and

X\subset\bar{X}
an open subset such that
\bar{X}\setminus X
has codimension
\geq 2
. Let
E
be a rank 2 vector bundle over
X
such that
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
contains a unipotent subgroup of dimension
\geq 2
.
Working on general curve sections and using (
17
.1–3) we get that, after twisting with a line bundle and possibly shrinking
X
, there is a unique extension

[formula]

0\to L\to E\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to 0

such that
h^{0}(X,L)\geq 2
.
Furthermore

(17.5)

the extension (
17
.4) is
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(E)
invariant,

(17.6)

the unipotent radical of
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(E)
is naturally isomorphic to
H^{0}(X,L)
,

(17.7)

\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(E)/H^{0}(X,L)\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
if (
17
.4) is non-split, and

(17.8)

\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(E)/H^{0}(X,L)\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2}
if (
17
.4) is split.

18
Automorphisms of unstable
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundles.

Continuing with the notation of (
17
),
let
\pi:P:={\mathbb{P}}_{X}(E)\to X
be the corresponding
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle.
Then
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(P)
is the quotient if
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(E)
by the scalars.
Thus we get that, if

\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(P)
contains a unipotent subgroup of dimension
\geq 2
, then

(18.1)

there is a unique
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(P)
invariant section
D\subset P
,

(18.2)

the unipotent radical of
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(P)
is naturally isomorphic to
H^{0}(X,L)
,

(18.3)

\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(P)=H^{0}(X,L)
if (
17
.4) is non-split,

(18.4)

\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(P)/H^{0}(X,L)\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
if (
17
.4) is split, and

(18.5)

the splittings of
E\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
correspond to the
splittings of
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(P)\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

Note that the natural way to get a vector bundle on
X
is
to take
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{P}(D)
. This sits in the exact sequence

[formula]

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}=\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{P}\to\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{P}(D)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{P}(D)|_{D}\to 0.

Thus
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{P}(D)\cong E\otimes L^{-1}
.

Next we show a converse to these observations.

Proposition 19
.

Let
\bar{X}
be a normal, projective variety with smooth locus
X\subset\bar{X}
, and
G\subset\operatorname{Bir}\bigl(X\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty)\to X\bigr)
an algebraic subgroup. Assume that
G^{h}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{X})=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. Then
there is a
G^{h}
-invariant open subset

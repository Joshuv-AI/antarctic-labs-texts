Sharp Phase Transition for the Random-Cluster Model with Summable External Magnetic Field
R. Vila

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove sharpness of the phase transition for the random-cluster model in summable positive external fields, with cluster weight q ∈ { 2 , 3 , … } q\in\{2,3,\ldots\} , on the hypercubic lattice ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , d ⩾ 2 d\geqslant 2 . That is, there exists some critical parameter 0 < β c < ∞ 0<\beta_{c}<\infty that depends on the cluster weight and the external field, below which the model exhibits exponential decay and above which there exists almost surely an infinite cluster.

1.
Introduction

The random-cluster model is defined as a correlated bond percolation model for phase transitions in lattice systems. This model was introduced by Fortuin
and Kasteleyn around 1970
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
satisfying specific series and parallel laws. The random-cluster model is
a graphical representation of a range of important models of statistical mechanics, among them the independent bond percolation
[
13
]
, the Potts model
[
38
]
and the uniform spanning trees
[
32
]
.
Graphical representations are commonly used to study the existence of
phase transitions in Ising/Potts-type models
relating them to the random-cluster model
through the Edwards-Sokal measure
[
20
]
.
Recently, a variety of results concerning the phase transition of the
planar random-cluster model have emerged; see
[
5
,
15
,
17
,
19
]
.
When non-translation-invariant external fields
are considered, phase transition in the random-cluster model
does not always correspond to a phase transition in the corresponding spin system. In this case, as noticed by
[
6
]
,
the FKG property is harder to obtain because this property does not even hold for general boundary conditions. Moreover, other complications appear, for example, in the analysis of Dobrushin-like states

[
25
]
and the effect of weak boundary conditions in the Potts model
[
10
]
.
Graphical representations
[
13
]
for the Potts model in external fields
have been the object of revived interest after rigorous results were proved; see
[
6
,
11
,
12
]
.

This paper is motivated by some recent works on ferromagnetics spin systems in non-uniform external fields; see

[
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
30
,
36
,
37
]
.
The objective of this paper is to prove that
the phase transition for the random-cluster model over the hypercubic lattice, allowing a summable positive external field, is sharp.
Roughly speaking, by using
the strong FKG property and comparison inequalities,
we showed that
there is a certain non-trivial critical point,
\beta_{c}
above which there is almost surely a path of connected points of infinite length through the network. Below it, the model exhibits exponential decay of the infinite-volume connectivity function
for two points.
Sufficient conditions for the (uniform) exponential decay of finite-volume connectivities in the planar random-cluster model, with external field, was established in
[
3
]
.
In the absence of an external magnetic field, sharp phase transitions for the random-cluster model, Potts model and related percolation models, on several different types of graphs, have been studied by
[
1
,
2
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
33
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present
the random-cluster model with free and max-wired boundary conditions, and some definitions related to this model.
We close this section by stating the main result of paper.
In Section
3
, we present some preliminary monotonicities of random-cluster measures.
Finally, in Section
4
, we present the proof of the main result in details.

2.
The model and the main result

In this section, we introduce the random-cluster model and state
the main result of the paper. For general results and historical background of the random-cluster model, we refer the reader to
[
6
,
11
,
13
,
26
]
and the references therein.

The model is defined as follows.
Consider a finite subgraph
G=(V,E)
of the
infinite countable connected graph

\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}^{d})
, where
\mathbb{E}^{d}

is the set of the nearest-neighbor edges
xy
in the
d
-dimensional hypercubic lattice

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, with
d\geqslant 2
.
The boundaries of
V
and
E
are given by

\partial V=\{x\notin V:xy\in\mathbb{E}^{d}\ \text{for some}\ y\in V\}

and

\partial E=\{xy\in\mathbb{E}^{d}:xy\cap V\neq\emptyset\ \text{and}\ xy\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset\}
, respectively.
The configuration space of the random-cluster model is the product space
\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}
.
A generic element of this space, denoted by
\omega=(\omega_{xy})_{{xy}\in E\cup\partial E}
, is often called edge configuration.
We say that an edge
xy
is open in a configuration
\omega
if
\omega_{xy}=1
, and closed otherwise.
Given
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}
,
we set
x\longleftrightarrow y
in
\omega
if
x
and
y
are in the same connected component of
\omega
. The random sets

\eta(\omega)=\{xy\in E\cup\partial E:\omega_{xy}=1\}
and

C_{x}(\omega)=\{y\in V\cup\partial V:x\longleftrightarrow y\ \text{in}\ \omega\}\cup\{x\}

denote the set of open edges and the open cluster
of a vertex
x\in V
in
\omega
, respectively.
We write
|C_{x}|
to denote the number of vertices in
C_{x}
.

We fix two families
\bm{J}=(J_{xy})_{xy\in\mathbb{E}^{d}}\in[0,\infty)^{\mathbb{E}^{d}}

and
\bm{\widehat{h}}=(h_{x,m})_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},m\in\{1,\ldots,q\}}\in(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})^{q}

called coupling constant and external field, respectively, where
q\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
.
Throughout this paper, we define

h_{x,\mathrm{max}}=\max_{m\in\{1,\ldots,q\}}h_{x,m},

for each vertex
x\in V
, denote

(1)

\displaystyle{Q}_{x,\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\widehat{h}})=\big\{m\in\{1,\ldots,q\}:h_{x,m}=h_{x,\mathrm{max}}\big\},

and assume that

(2)

\displaystyle\left|{\cap}_{x\in{V}}{Q}_{x,\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\widehat{h}})\right|\geqslant 1.

Fix
\beta>0
and
{\rm m}\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
. For
\#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm m}\}
, following the references
[
6
,
11
]
,
let

\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
be the measure
satisfying, for any
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}
,

(3)

\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(\omega)={1\over Z^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}}\,\prod_{xy\in E}\big[\exp({q\beta J_{xy}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}\,\prod_{C(\omega)}\Theta^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big],

where
Z^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
is a normalizing constant,
the second product is over all open clusters of sites, and the weights of the clusters are given by

\Theta^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]=\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,m}\Bigg),

for all open clusters
C(\omega)

of the graph
\big(V,\eta(\omega)\big)
with
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}
(since
\omega_{xy}=0
for all
xy\in\partial E
); and

\displaystyle\Theta^{\rm m}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]=\begin{cases}\Theta^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big],&\text{if}\ C(\omega)\cap\partial V=\emptyset,\\[11.38092pt]
\displaystyle\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,{\rm m}}\Bigg),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for all open clusters
C(\omega)
of the graph

\big(V\cup\partial V,\eta(\omega)\big)
with
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}
, where

\omega_{xy}=1
for all
xy\in\partial E
.
If
{\rm m},\widetilde{{\rm m}}\in{\cap}_{x\in{V}}{Q}_{x,\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\widehat{h}}),
then

\displaystyle\Theta^{\rm m}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]=\Theta^{\widetilde{{\rm m}}}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]\quad\text{for all}\ \omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E},

and therefore

\phi^{\rm m}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}=\phi^{\widetilde{{\rm m}}}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}.

This measure is denoted by

\phi^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
.
The measures
\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
and
\phi^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
are called the random-cluster measures on
G
with free and max-wired boundary conditions, respectively.
By taking the weak limit of measures defined in finite volume, the measures
\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
, with
\#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\}
, can be extended to
\mathbb{G}
; see Theorem
3.3
.
The expected value of a real-valued function
f:\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}\to\mathbb{R}

in the random-cluster model is denoted by

\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)
.
When
f=\mathds{1}_{A}
, for some cylindrical event
A
, we write
\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(A)
instead
\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(\mathds{1}_{A}).

The functions of principal interest are the following:

(4)

\displaystyle\theta(\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}})\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\phi^{\rm f}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big(|C_{x}|=\infty\big);\quad\chi(\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}})\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\phi^{\rm w}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big(|C_{x}|\big).

These functions are known as percolation-probability and susceptibility function, respectively.

We write
\bm{\widehat{h}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
when
\bm{\widehat{h}}
is summable, that is,
\lVert\bm{\widehat{h}}\rVert_{1}=\sum_{m=1}^{q}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|h_{x,m}|<\infty
.
The following theorem is the main result of paper.
This result discusses
the existence of phase transition
and the exponential decay of the infinite-volume connectivity function
for two points.

Theorem 1
.

Fix
q\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
and
d\geqslant 2
. Consider
the random-cluster model on
\mathbb{G}
, with uniform coupling constant
\bm{J}
, i.e.
J_{xy}=J\geqslant 0
is constant for all
xy\in\mathbb{E}^{d}
, and external field
\bm{\widehat{h}}=(h_{x,m})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},m\in\{1,\ldots,q\}}\in(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})^{q}
satisfying the condition (
2
).
Then, there exists a critical point
0<\beta_{c}=\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{h}})<\infty
so that

1)

\theta(\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}})=0
if
\beta<\beta_{c}
, and
\theta(\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}})>0
if
\beta>\beta_{c}
.

2)

\chi(\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}})<\infty
if
\beta<\beta_{0},

for some

0<\beta_{0}<\beta_{c}
; and

\chi(\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}})=\infty
if
\beta>\beta_{c}
.

3)

In the case that
\bm{\widehat{h}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
has positive terms,
for all
\beta<\beta_{c}

there exist constants
C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}}),\gamma(\beta,q)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\phi^{\rm w}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\leqslant C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}})\,{\exp}\big[{-\|x-y\|\gamma(\beta,q)}\big]\quad\text{for all}\ x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm.
This implies finite susceptibility.

This theorem extends to a large class of amenable infinite
transitive graphs.

As a consequence of Theorem
1
we have the sharpness of the phase transition for the random-cluster model in summable positive external fields, on the hypercubic lattice. Remains to investigate the following. For which classes of non-summable external fields, the phase transition of the random-cluster on the graph
\mathbb{G}
is sharp?
Sharp phase transitions for spin systems and related percolation models, in the absence of an external magnetic field, on different types of graphs have emerged in references
[
1
,
2
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
33
]
.

The rest of this paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
. To this end, some comparison inequalities are essential.

3.
Monotonic measures

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite subgraph of
\mathbb{G}
and
q\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
.
We consider the usual partial order on
\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}
where

\omega\preceq\tilde{\omega}
iff
\omega_{xy}\leqslant\tilde{\omega}_{xy}
for all
xy\in E\cup\partial E
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\preceq)
be a partially ordered space.
A measure
\mu
over
\Omega
said to have the

\mathrm{FKG}
property if

\mu(fg)\geqslant\mu(f)\mu(g),

for any increasing (with respect to
\preceq
)
measurable functions
f,g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, if
\Omega
is a cartesian product

\Omega=\prod_{xy\in B}\Omega_{xy}
, with
|\Omega_{xy}|<\infty
,
then
\mu
is said to have
the
strong FKG property
, if

\mu(\cdot|A)
has the
\mathrm{FKG}
property for each
cylinder event

A=\{\omega\in\Omega:\omega_{xy}=\alpha_{xy}\ \text{for all}\ xy\in\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}\in\widetilde{B}\}
,
where
\widetilde{B}\subset{B}
is finite and

\alpha_{xy}\in\Omega_{xy}
for all
xy\in\widetilde{B}
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
11
]
)
.

The finite-volume measures

\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}

and

\phi^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}

have the strong
\mathrm{FKG}
property.

By using the strong FKG property for the random-cluster model, one can prove the following two theorems.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
11
]
)
.

For each increasing quasilocal function f (see
[
24
]
),

(a)

The following limits exist

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm f}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)=\lim_{G\uparrow\mathbb{G}}\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f);\quad\phi^{\rm w}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)=\lim_{G\uparrow\mathbb{G}}\phi^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f).

(b)

The finite-volume measures
\phi^{\rm f}_{{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
and
\phi^{\rm w}_{{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
are the extremal ones,
in the sense that, if

\phi_{{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
is a finite-volume random-cluster measure, then

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm f}_{{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)\leqslant\phi_{{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)\leqslant\phi^{\rm w}_{{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f).

Consequently,
\phi^{\rm f}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
and
\phi^{\rm w}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
are also extremal measures.

Hereafter
N_{\infty}
denotes the random variable that counts the number of infinite open clusters in the sample space
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{E}^{d}}
.
The following theorem states that the probability that a unique infinite cluster exists is either zero or one.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
11
]
)
.

The measures

\phi^{\#}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
, with
\#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\}
, have the almost-sure uniqueness of the infinite open cluster property. That is,

\displaystyle\phi^{\#}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(N_{\infty}\leqslant 1)=1\quad\text{for}\ \#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\}.

The next monotonicity result is valid for a large class of probability measures with
the restriction that these measures are positively associated.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mu_{i})
,
i=1,2,
two probability spaces with probability measures

\mu_{i}(\omega)=\mathcal{K}(\omega)\mathcal{W}_{i}(\omega)/Z_{i}
,
\omega\in\Omega
,
i=1,2,
satisfying the FKG property, where

Z_{i}
is the respective partition function and
\mathcal{K}
is a non-negative function.
If
\mathcal{W}_{1}/\mathcal{W}_{2}
is an increasing non-negative function,
then
\mu_{1}(f)\geqslant\mu_{2}(f)
for each cylindrical increasing function
f
.

Proof.

Let

g(\omega)={\mathcal{W}_{1}(\omega)/\mathcal{W}_{2}(\omega)}
,
\omega\in\Omega
. Note that
g
is an increasing function.
A simple computation shows that, for all cylindrical valued-real function
f
,

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(f)={Z_{2}\over Z_{1}}\,\mu_{2}(fg).

Taking
f\equiv 1
we obtain that

\mu_{2}(g)={Z_{1}/Z_{2}}.
Then, by using the above identity, from FKG inequality we have, for each cylindrical increasing function
f
,

\mu_{1}(f)={\mu_{2}(fg)\over\mu_{2}(g)}\geqslant\mu_{2}(f).

Thus the proof is complete.
∎

The next lemma shows that the part of the measures
\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
in (
3
), with
\#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\}
, due to the external magnetic field are decreasing functions.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under the condition (
2
), the following hold:

(a)

The function
{g}^{\rm f}
defined as, for any
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}
,

{g}^{\rm f}(\omega)\coloneqq\prod_{C(\omega)}\Theta^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]=\prod_{C(\omega)}\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,m}\Bigg),

is monotone decreasing in the FKG sense, where the above product runs over all the open clusters
C(\omega)

of the graph
\big(V,\eta(\omega)\big)
.

(b)

The function
{g}^{\rm w}
defined as, for any
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}
,

\displaystyle{g}^{\rm w}(\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{C(\omega)}\Theta^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]

\displaystyle=\prod_{C(\omega):C(\omega)\cap\partial V=\emptyset}\,\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,m}\Bigg)\prod_{C(\omega):C(\omega)\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg),

is monotone decreasing in the FKG sense.

Proof.

Let
xy
be a nearest-neighbor edge such that

\omega_{xy}=0
and let
\omega^{xy}
be the configuration obtained by flipping
\omega_{xy}
to
1
. It suffices to study the single-bond flips. That is,
it is sufficient to prove the following

(5)

\displaystyle{g}^{\#}(\omega^{xy})\leqslant{g}^{\#}(\omega)\quad\text{with}\ \#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\}.

If the vertices
x
and
y
are connected in the configuration
\omega
,
{g}^{\#}(\omega^{xy})={g}^{\#}(\omega)
.
If the vertices
x
e
y
are not connected in
\omega
,
then there are two open clusters

A=C_{x}(\omega)
and
B=C_{y}(\omega)
containing the vertices

x
and
y
, respectively.
If
xy
is an open edge at
\omega
, then the clusters
A
and
B
are connected,
creating a new open cluster denoted by

C=A\cup B
. Then,
|C|=|A|+|B|,
and so to prove
(
5
), it is enough to verify that

(6)

\displaystyle\Theta^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C\big]\leqslant\Theta^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[A\big]\Theta^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[B\big]\quad\text{with}\ \#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\}.

(a) When
\#={\rm f}
, the inequality in (
6
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C}h_{x,m}\Bigg)\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_{x,m}\Bigg)\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in B}h_{x,m}\Bigg).

But the above inequality is true since

\sum_{k=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_{x,k}\Bigg)\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in B}h_{x,m}\Bigg)\\[5.69046pt]
\geqslant\sum_{k=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_{x,k}\Bigg)\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in B}h_{x,\mathrm{max}}\Bigg)\left|{\cap}_{x\in{V}}{Q}_{x,\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\widehat{h}})\right|\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\geqslant}}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C}h_{x,j}\Bigg),

where in the last line we have used the condition (
2
).

(b) When
\#={\rm w}
, we analyze the following possible cases.
(i)
A\cap\partial V=\emptyset
and
B\cap\partial V=\emptyset
;
(ii)
A\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset
and
B\cap\partial V=\emptyset
;
(iii)
A\cap\partial V=\emptyset
and
B\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset
; and
(iv)
A\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset
and
B\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset
.

In the case (i), the inequality in (
6
) follows directly from Item (a). In the case (ii), (
6
) is equivalent to the following inequality

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)\leqslant\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in B}h_{x,m}\Bigg).

But this is true since

\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in B}h_{x,m}\Bigg)\\[2.84544pt]
\geqslant\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in B}h_{x,\mathrm{max}}\Bigg)\left|{\cap}_{x\in{V}}{Q}_{x,\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\widehat{h}})\right|\\
\geqslant\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg),

where, again, in the last line we have used the condition (
2
).
In the case (iii), the inequality in (
6
) is proven analogously to Item (ii). Finally, in the case (iv), (
6
) is equivalent to the following identity

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)=\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in B}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg).

Hence the proof follows.
∎

It is known that stochastic dominance allows to compare different probability measures on the same sample space.
One of the common uses of this type of comparisons is to prove that certain
characteristics are inherited from one random model to another, which is generally not trivial.
The following lemma states that, at finite volume, the Bernoulli measure dominates
the random-cluster measure with external field.
Hence, for example, we can use this lemma to prove that,
at the thermodynamic limit, the almost-sure uniqueness (under amenabilitity condition in the infinite graph) of the infinite open cluster would be inherited
for the infinite-volume random-cluster measure; see Theorem
3.4
and Theorem 10 in
[
11
]
for a rigorous proof of this fact.

Lemma 3.7
.

For each cylindrical increasing function
f
we have

\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)\leqslant\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,1,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)=\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,1,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(f)\eqqcolon\mathbb{P}_{G;\beta}(f)\quad\text{with}\ \#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{G;\beta}
becomes
the product probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{E}
defined by the Bernoulli factor

\prod_{xy\in E}p_{xy}^{\omega_{xy}}(1-p_{xy})^{1-\omega_{xy}}
,
with
p_{xy}=1-\exp({-q\beta J_{xy}})
.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
be two probability measures defined by
\mathbb{P}_{G;\beta}
and

\phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
,
respectively. The respective weights of these measures are given by

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\omega)=\exp\big({-q\beta\sum_{xy\in E}J_{xy}}\big)
,

\mathcal{W}_{2}(\omega)={g}^{\#}(\omega)
and
\mathcal{K}(\omega)=\prod_{xy\in E}\big[\exp({q\beta J_{xy}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}
,

\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}
,
where

On the other hand, the identity

In what follows we consider as usual the partial order on

\bm{J}\curlyeqprec\bm{J}^{\prime}

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that
\bm{J}\curlyeqprec\bm{J}^{\prime}
are two coupling constants. The function

defined as, for any

\widetilde{g}(\omega)\coloneqq{\prod_{xy\in E}\big[\exp({q\beta J_{xy}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}\over\prod_{xy\in E}\big[\exp({q\beta J_{xy}^{\prime}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}},

Proof.

Let

be a nearest-neighbor edge such that

\omega_{x_{0}y_{0}}=0

\omega^{x_{0}y_{0}}
be the configuration obtained by flipping

To prove the monotonicity of

But this is true because

\displaystyle{\widetilde{g}(\omega^{x_{0}y_{0}})\over\widetilde{g}(\omega)}

\displaystyle={\prod_{xy\in E}\big[{\exp}({q\beta J_{xy}})-1\big]^{\omega^{x_{0}y_{0}}_{xy}}\over\prod_{xy\in E}\big[{\exp}({q\beta J_{xy}^{\prime}})-1\big]^{\omega^{x_{0}y_{0}}_{xy}}}\,{\prod_{xy\in E}\big[{\exp}({q\beta J_{xy}^{\prime}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}\over\prod_{xy\in E}\big[{\exp}({q\beta J_{xy}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}}

\displaystyle={{\exp}({q\beta J_{x_{0}y_{0}}})-1\over{\exp}({q\beta J_{x_{0}y_{0}}^{\prime}})-1}\leqslant 1,

whenever
\bm{J}\curlyeqprec\bm{J}^{\prime}

∎

Our next result is the monotonicity, in the FKG

Proposition 3.9

\displaystyle\phi^{{\#},\bm{J}}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)\leqslant\phi^{{\#},\bm{J}^{\prime}}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f).

(b)

(ii)

\mu_{1}

\mu_{2}

\phi^{{\rm w},\bm{J}}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}
,
respectively. The respective weights of these measures are given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}(\omega)=\prod_{xy\in E}\big[\exp({q\beta J_{xy}^{\prime}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}\,{g}^{\rm w}(\omega);

The next result compares the random-cluster measure in summable positive external fields with the random-cluster measure
of their counterparts under the zero external field.

Lemma 3.12
.

If

\bm{\widehat{h}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})

\displaystyle\ \phi^{\#}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(f)\leqslant C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}})\,\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(f)\quad\text{with}\ \#\in\{{\rm f},{\rm w}\}.

Proof.

We only present the argument for
\#={\rm w}
, since for the free
boundary condition case the proof works similarly. Indeed,
for all open cluster
C(\omega)
such that

C(\omega)\cap\partial V=\emptyset
,
since
h_{x,{\rm max}}>h_{x,m}
for all
x
and

, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,m}\Bigg)\leqslant q\bm{\cdot}\exp\Bigg(\beta\!\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg).

This implies that, for any open cluster
C(\omega)
of the graph

\big(V\cup\partial V,\eta(\omega)\big)
with
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\cup\partial E}

\displaystyle\prod_{C(\omega)}\Theta^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]

\displaystyle=\prod_{C(\omega):C(\omega)\cap\partial V=\emptyset}\,\sum_{m=1}^{q}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,m}\Bigg)\prod_{C(\omega):C(\omega)\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset}\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in C(\omega)}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)

(8)

\displaystyle\leqslant q^{\big|\big\{C(\omega):\,C(\omega)\cap\partial V=\emptyset\big\}\big|}\,\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in V\cup\partial V}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg).

Since
\bm{\widehat{h}}
has positive terms,

Z^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\geqslant Z^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}\,\exp(\beta\sum_{x\in\partial V}h_{x,{\rm max}})
. Hence, by combining this inequality with the one in (
8

{1\over Z^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}}\,\sum_{\omega}f(\omega)\prod_{xy\in E}\big[\exp({q\beta J_{xy}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}\,\prod_{C(\omega)}\Theta^{\rm w}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}\big[C(\omega)\big]\\[4.26773pt]
\leqslant{\exp\big(\beta\sum_{x\in V}h_{x,{\rm max}}\big)\over Z^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}}\,\sum_{\omega}f(\omega)\prod_{xy\in E}\big[\exp({q\beta J_{xy}})-1\big]^{\omega_{xy}}\,q^{\big|\big\{C(\omega):\,C(\omega)\cap\partial V=\emptyset\big\}\big|}\\[4.26773pt]

because

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in V}h_{x,{\rm max}}\Bigg)\leqslant\exp\Bigg(\beta\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\max_{m\in\{1,\ldots,q\}}|h_{x,m}|\Bigg)\leqslant{\exp}\big({\beta\lVert\bm{\widehat{h}}\rVert_{1}}\big).

.
Hence, the validity of lemma, with

by taking

.

Proof of Item 3

From Lemma
3.12
with
f=\mathds{1}_{\{x\longleftrightarrow y\}}
, in the thermodynamic limit, we have

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm w}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\leqslant C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}})\,\phi^{\rm f}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\quad\text{for all}\ x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

whenever
\bm{\widehat{h}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
has positive terms.
It is known that, for
\beta<\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{0}})
and

,
there exists
\gamma(\beta,q)>0
such that (see main theorem in
[
18
]
),

\phi^{\rm f}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\leqslant{\exp}\big[{-\|x-y\|\gamma(\beta,q)}\big].

And thereby, for
\beta<\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{0}})
,

(18)

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm w}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\leqslant C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}})\,{\exp}\big[{-\|x-y\|\gamma(\beta,q)}\big]\quad\text{for all}\ x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

In what follows we claim that

\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{0}})=\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{h}})
. Indeed,
by Item (
12
),

\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{0}})\geqslant\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{h}}).
Now, suppose that
\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{0}})>\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{h}})
. From Theorem
1
-Item 2),

(19)

\displaystyle\chi(\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}})=\infty\quad\text{when}\ \beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{h}})<\beta<\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{0}}).

From (

), for
\beta<\beta_{c}(q,\bm{\widehat{0}})
and
for all

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\phi^{\rm w}_{\mathbb{G};\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\leqslant C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}})\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\exp}\big[{-n\gamma(\beta,q)}\big]\Bigg(\sum_{y:\|x-y\|=n}1\Bigg)<\infty.

Remark 5
.

Let
x,z\in V
be distinct vertices. A subset
W
of
V
is said to separate
x
and
z
if
x,z\notin W
and every path from
x
to
z
contains some vertex of
W
.
Let
q=2
and let
\bm{\widehat{h}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be a summable positive external field.
Assume that
x,z\in V
are distinct vertices,
and that
W
separate
x
and
z
.
For
\beta\geqslant 0
there exists
C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}})>0
such that

(20)

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow z)\leqslant C(\beta,\bm{\widehat{h}})\sum_{y\in W}\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\,\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(y\longleftrightarrow z).

The above correlation inequality is known as the (modified) Simon-Lieb inequality.
Indeed, since the event
\{x\longleftrightarrow y\}
is monotone increasing,
Corollary
3.11
gives

(21)

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\leqslant\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\quad\text{for all}\ x,y\in V.

The Simon-Lieb inequality
[
28
,
29
,
31
,
39
]

for the measure
\phi_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}
, with
q=2
, gives

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(x\longleftrightarrow z)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in W}\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\,\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{0}}}(y\longleftrightarrow z)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq-fund-1}}}{{\leqslant}}\sum_{y\in W}\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(x\longleftrightarrow y)\,\phi^{\rm f}_{G;\beta,q,\bm{\widehat{h}}}(y\longleftrightarrow z).

By combining this with Lemma
3.12
, the validity of the inequality in (
20
) follows.

Remark 6

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following semi-linear structurally damped
\sigma
-evolution models:

(1.1)

\partial_{tt}u(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\left|u(t,x)\right|^{p},

where the Cauchy data are from the space
H^{\sigma,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with different additional
L^{m}
regularity:

(1.2)

u(0,x)\in H^{\sigma,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{m_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \ \partial_{t}u(0,x)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

with the parameters:

t\in[0,\infty),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ n\geq 1,\ \sigma\in[1,\infty),\ q\in(1,\infty),\ m_{1},m_{2}\in[1,q),\ p\in(1,\infty).

At first, the presence of the Laplacian operators with fractional orders in the above models make them describe many practical applications in the real world, this is due to the fact that the integral representation of the fractional Laplacian operator that contains a singular kernel can represent the memory or hereditary process, we recall the following example when
\sigma=1
in (
1.1
), the obtained model without non-linearity is frequently used in the determination of lifespan for primary or rechargeable batteries, for more details one can see
[
4
]
and references therein.

In recent years, after the pioneering work
[
1
]
of Hiroshi Fujita in 1966, many mathematicians focus their research works on finding or improving the so-called critical exponent
p_{c}
of some nonlinear Cauchy problems. In general, this critical exponent
p_{c}
is exactly a threshold that divides the range of
p\in(1,\infty)
into two parts such that when:

•

1<p<p_{c}
there exist arbitrarily small Cauchy data, such that there exists no global (in time) weak solution, only local existence can be proved.

•

p_{c}<p<\infty
then there exist global (in time) small data Sobolev or energy solutions.

Sometimes, the critical case
p=p_{c}
belongs to the sets of global existence or blow-up. Moreover, the well-known power nonlinearity
|u|^{p}
is one of some types of nonlinearities such that when
p\in(p_{c},\infty)
, the decay estimates for the unique solution are exactly coincide with those for solutions to the linear problem.

Here, let us recall the derived critical exponent for the problem (
1.1
) with Cauchy data in the energy space
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the
same
additional
L^{m}
regularity, that is (
1.2
) with
q=2
and
m_{1}=m_{2}=m\in[1,2)
, the authors in
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
9
]
found the following expression:

(1.3)

p_{c}(n,m,\sigma)=1+\frac{2m\sigma}{n-m\sigma},\ m\in[1,2).

We note that the idea of choosing the Cauchy data from the energy space with the same additional
L^{m}
regularity has its origin in the work
[
7
]
, where the authors studied the Cauchy problem for semi-linear dissipative wave equations:

w_{tt}-\Delta w+w_{t}=|w|^{p-1}w,

and Cauchy data as in (
1.2
) with
\sigma=1
,
q=2
,
m_{1}=m_{2}=m\in[1,2]
, they first showed the direct influence of the parameter
m
on the Fujita exponent
p_{F}(n)=1+2/n
, more precisely, they found the following modified Fujita exponent
p_{F}(n/m)=1+2m/n
.

From the above results, one can easily see that when the Cauchy data have a unified additional regularity, then one can expect at least one critical exponent, for more results about the equation (
1.1
) see
[
8
,
10
,
11
]
. So, it is interesting to study the models (
1.1
) with a generalized class of Cauchy data in (
1.2
).

Our essential goal is to show a new influence of different additional
L^{m}
regularity on the critical exponents (
1.3
), to do this, we would like to prove the global (in time) existence of small data solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). By standard tools such as the Banach fixed point theorem, Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and some mixed
(L^{m}\cap L^{q})-L^{q}
linear estimates, we find the following form of
two different values
:

p_{1}(m_{2},n,\sigma)=1+\frac{2m_{2}\sigma}{n-m_{2}\sigma},\ \ \ \ \ p_{2}(m_{1},m_{2},n,\sigma)=\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}+\frac{m_{1}\sigma}{n},

under some restrictions on
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
,
q
,
\sigma
and
n
provided in (
2.6
)-(
2.7
).

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
we introduce the tools that will be used, especially, the linear estimates which are a powerful tool. Section
3
contains our main results of global existence and their proofs. We end this paper with a conclusion and an open question.

u=\mathcal{O}u
.

We clarify that the condition
\left\|(u(0,x),\partial_{t}u(0,x))\right\|_{\mathcal{D}}<\varepsilon
makes the operator
\mathcal{O}
maps balls of
B(T)
into balls of
B(T)
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
we choose the following suitable norm

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B(T)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big((1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)-1}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}+(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u,\partial_{t}u)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\Big),

while to prove Theorem
3.2
we choose the following suitable norm

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B(T)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big((1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}+(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)+1}\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u,\partial_{t}u)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\Big).

Let us start with the proof of the first theorem. We divide the proof into three steps.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

In the first step, we verify that
u^{L}\in B(T)
.

Step 1:
Using Corollary
1
, we deduce:

\displaystyle\|u^{L}\|_{B(T)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big((1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)-1}\|u^{L}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}+(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u^{L},\partial_{t}u^{L})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lesssim\left\|(u(0,x),\partial_{t}u(0,x))\right\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

Step 2:
To conclude inequality (
3.7
), we need to prove:

(3.11)

\|u^{N}\|_{B(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}.

To do this, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\|u^{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-s)^{1-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{L^{qp}\cap L^{m_{2}p}}ds

(3.12)

\displaystyle+\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-s)\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{L^{qp}}ds,

\displaystyle\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u^{N},\partial_{t}u^{N})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{L^{qp}\cap L^{m_{2}p}}ds

(3.13)

\displaystyle+\int_{t/2}^{t}\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{L^{qp}}ds.

Before applying the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality from Lemma
2.3
to estimate the norms

\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{L^{rp}},r=m_{2},q,

we should know from (
3.9
) that:

(1+s)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)-1+\frac{a}{\sigma}}\|(-\Delta)^{a/2}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)},\ \ \ a=0,\sigma.

So, we can estimate the above norms as follows:

\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{rp}}^{p}\lesssim(1+s)^{-\frac{p(n-m_{2}\sigma)}{m_{2}\sigma}+\frac{n}{r\sigma}}\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p},\ \ r=m_{2},q,

where the conditions in (
3.2
) are satisfied. Now, (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) become:

\displaystyle\|u^{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+s)^{-\frac{p(n-m_{2}\sigma)}{m_{2}\sigma}+\frac{n}{m_{2}\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{p(n-m_{2}\sigma)}{m_{2}\sigma}+\frac{n}{q\sigma}}\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-s)ds,

\displaystyle\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u^{N},\partial_{t}u^{N})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+s)^{-\frac{p(n-m_{2}\sigma)}{m_{2}\sigma}+\frac{n}{m_{2}\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{p(n-m_{2}\sigma)}{m_{2}\sigma}+\frac{n}{q\sigma}}\int_{t/2}^{t}ds,

where we used the equivalences

(1+t-s)\approx(1+t)\ if\ s\in[0,t/2],\ \ (1+s)\approx(1+t)\ if\ s\in[t/2,t].

Thanks to Lemma
2.4
together with (
3.3
), all the integrals can be estimated immediately

(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)-1}\|u^{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p},

(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u^{N},\partial_{t}u^{N})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}.

In this way we proved

\|\mathcal{O}u\|_{B(T)}=\|u^{L}+u^{N}\|_{B(T)}\lesssim\left\|(u(0,x),\partial_{t}u(0,x))\right\|_{\mathcal{D}}+\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}.

Step 3:
Let us briefly show the proof of (
3.8
). We choose two elements
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
belong to
B(T)
, and we write

\mathcal{O}u_{1}-\mathcal{O}u_{2}:=u_{1}^{N}-u_{2}^{N}=\int_{0}^{t}L_{2}(t-s,x)\ast(|u_{1}(s,x)|^{p}-|u_{2}(s,x)|^{p})ds.

Here, we do as in step 2, we have again:

\|(u_{1}^{N}-u_{2}^{N})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-s)^{1-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left\||u_{1}(s,\cdot)|^{p}-|u_{2}(s,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{m_{2}}\cap L^{q}}ds

+\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-s)\left\||u_{1}(s,\cdot)|^{p}-|u_{2}(s,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{q}}ds,

and

\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}(u_{1}^{N}-u_{2}^{N}),\partial_{t}(u_{1}^{N}-u_{2}^{N}))(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left\||u_{1}(s,\cdot)|^{p}-|u_{2}(s,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{m_{2}}\cap L^{q}}ds

+\int_{t/2}^{t}\left\||u_{1}(s,\cdot)|^{p}-|u_{2}(s,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{q}}ds.

To calculate the above integrals, we use the Hölder’s inequality which gives

\||u_{1}(s,\cdot)|^{p}-|u_{2}(s,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|(u_{1}-u_{2})(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{rp}}\left(\|u_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{rp}}^{p-1}+\|u_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{rp}}^{p-1}\right),

as well as use the definition of the norms

\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{B(T)},\ \ \|u_{1}\|_{B(T)},\ \ \|u_{2}\|_{B(T)}.

and the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality we get (
3.8
). The proof of Theorem
3.1
is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Now, let us briefly mention the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Step 1:
Using Corollary
2
and (
3.10
) we deduce again:

\displaystyle\|u^{L}\|_{B(T)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big((1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u^{L}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}+(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)+1}\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u^{L},\partial_{t}u^{L})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lesssim\left\|(u(0,x),\partial_{t}u(0,x))\right\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

Step 2:
To prove (
3.11
), we come to estimate

\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{L^{rp}},\ \ r=m_{2},q.

So, recalling again from (
3.10
)

(1+s)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)+\frac{a}{\sigma}}\|(-\Delta)^{a/2}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)},\ \ \ a=0,\sigma.

Hence, we have the following estimates

\left\|u(s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{rp}}^{p}\lesssim(1+s)^{-\frac{np}{m_{1}\sigma}+\frac{n}{r\sigma}}\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p},\ \ r=m_{2},q,

where the conditions in (
3.2
) are satisfied.
Now, (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) become

\displaystyle\|u^{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+s)^{-\frac{np}{m_{1}\sigma}+\frac{n}{m_{2}\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle+\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{np}{m_{1}\sigma}+\frac{n}{q\sigma}}\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-s)ds,

\displaystyle\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u^{N},\partial_{t}u^{N})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+s)^{-\frac{np}{m_{1}\sigma}+\frac{n}{m_{2}\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle+\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{np}{m_{1}\sigma}+\frac{n}{q\sigma}}\int_{t/2}^{t}ds.

The condition (
3.5
) ensures that the integral over
[0,t/2]
is bounded. Thanks to (
3.4

(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u^{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p},

(1+t)^{\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)+1}\|((-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u^{N},\partial_{t}u^{N})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B(T)}^{p}.

In this way we proved inequality (
3.7
). One can prove (
3.8

∎

Conclusion

In this paper, we proved how the critical exponent (
1.3
) is really affected by the different additional
L^{m}
regularity of the Cauchy data. In particular, our results coincide with previous results when
m_{1}=m_{2}
. We recall that the two exponents (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) are imposed only in order to have the global (in time) existence, they coincide in case

m_{2}<m_{1},\ \ n=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}\sigma}{m_{1}-m_{2}}.

So, we can ask the following question:

What exactly is the critical exponent of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) ?

Spherical cones: classification and a volume minimization principle
Tran-Trung Nghiem

Abstract
. Using a variational approach, we establish the equivalence between a weighted volume minimization principle and the existence of a conical Calabi-Yau structure on horospherical cones with mild singularities. This allows us to do explicit computations on the examples arising from rank-two symmetric spaces, showing the existence of many irregular horospherical cones.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background, motivation and main results

Since the resolution of the Calabi conjecture by Yau and Aubin, the problem of finding canonical metrics has been essential in Kähler geometry. In the last three decades, various authors formulated and showed that the existence of Kähler-Einstein metrics on Fano manifolds is equivalent to a purely algebro-geometric condition called K-stability
[
Tia97
,
Don02
,
CDS15a
,
CDS15b
,
CDS15c
]
. Even if the corresponding picture of non-compact varieties is still largely unexplored, partial results have been made in the case of normal affine varieties.

A normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein affine variety with klt singularities and a good action of a complex torus
T
is often called a
Fano cone
(or a Fano cone singularity as in
[
LWX21
]
). Here, by a
good action
, we mean that the action of the torus is effective with a unique fixed point that is contained in the closure of any
T
-orbit. Basically, a Fano cone is the affine cone over a log Fano variety. The log Fano base is smooth if and only if the cone is smooth outside the fixed point.

Consider a Fano cone
X
with a unique singularity at the fixed point
\left\{0_{X}\right\}
. Let
\xi
be a vector in the Lie algebra of the maximal compact torus
T_{c}\subset T
such that
-J\xi
is a homothetic-scaling vector field on
X
. The element
\xi
is called a
Reeb vector
. A
conical Calabi-Yau metric
on
(X,\xi)
is a Kähler metric
\omega
on
X\backslash\left\{0_{X}\right\}
which is Ricci-flat and
2
-homogeneous under
-J\xi
, i.e.

\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}\omega=2\omega

When
X
is toric, a result of Martelli-Sparks-Yau relates the existence of a conical Calabi-Yau metric to a
volume minimizing
condition
[
MSY08
]
. This was generalized to any Fano cone with an isolated singularity by Collins and Székelyhidi
[
CS19
]
, broadening the volume minimization principle (as
\xi
varies) to a suitable notion of K-stability for the pair
(X,\xi)
. The Fano toric case with non-isolated singularities was settled recently by Berman
[
Ber20
]
, recovering and extending the main result of
[
MSY08
]
. Finally, a generalized notion of volume minimization was obtained by Li for
any
Fano cone and shown to be in fact equivalent to K-semistability
[
Li17
]
. Interestingly, a conical Calabi-Yau structure on a Fano cone can be translated to a particular
g
-soliton structure on quasi-regular quotient orbifolds
[
LH20
]
. In algebro-geometric words, the K-stability of the cone is equivalent to the
g
-weighted K-stability of any quotient orbifold. Both conditions can be verified over special test configurations only
[
Li21
, Theorem 2.9]
.

Even then, the K-stability condition still remains hard to be checked in practice, since there is generally an infinite number of special test configurations to consider. However, on varieties with low-complexity group actions (e.g. toric varieties), the condition becomes more or less simplified. For instance, the central fiber of any test configuration of a toric Fano variety is always isomorphic to the variety itself, hence the vanishing of the Futaki invariant (which is equivalent to the barycenter of a polytope being zero) is necessary and sufficient for K-stability. The main objective of this article is to conduct a case study of conical Calabi-Yau metrics on a class of spherical cones (with non-isolated singularities in general), called
horospherical cones
, and find an equivalent combinatorial K-stability notion for these cones.

To do this, we first provide a classification of conical embeddings using ingredients from the Luna-Vust theory. Before stating the classification theorem, let us give some preliminaries. Let
G
be a complex connected linear reductive group.

Definition 1.1
.

A
spherical space
is a homogeneous space
G/H
containing a Zariski-open orbit of a Borel subgroup
B
.
A
G/H
-spherical embedding
is a pair
(X,x)
where
X
is a normal
G
-variety such that
G.x
is an open
G
-orbit in
X
and
H
is the stabilizer of
x
.

Such a variety is called
spherical
. A
spherical cone
is a spherical affine variety with a fixed point under the action by automorphisms of a non-trivial torus that commutes with
G
. If a spherical embedding
G/H\subset X
is moreover a spherical cone, then
X
is said to be a
conical embedding
of
G/H
. Spherical cones form a large class of affine cones which notably contains the toric cones. The description of the latter is very simple: a toric cone can be completely characterized by a polyhedral convex cone of maximal dimension.

Analogously, the spherical embeddings of
G/H
are classified by combinatorial objects in a vector space, called
colored fans
. A colored fan is a collection of strongly convex and polyhedral colored cones satisfying certain compatibility conditions (see Defn.
2.12
, Defn.
2.15
for the precise definitions). A
color
in
G/H
is an irreducible
B
-stable divisor in
G/H
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Y
be a conical embedding of a spherical space
G/H
. Let
\mathcal{V}(G/H)
be the valuation cone of
G/H
, i.e. the set of
G
-invariant valuations on the rational functions of
G/H
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

Y
is a spherical cone.

(ii)

Y
has a unique fixed point under the action of
G
.

(iii)

The valuation cone
\mathcal{V}(G/H)
contains a line, and the colored cone of
Y
is
(\mathcal{C}_{Y},\mathcal{D})
where
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a strictly convex polyhedral cone of maximal dimension, and
\mathcal{D}
is the set of all colors of
G/H
.

A horospherical variety is a spherical variety whose open
G
-orbit is an equivariant torus bundle over a flag variety. The class of horospherical cones, which has a rather simple description, contains strictly the class of toric cones. Moreover, horospherical cones can be shown to satisfy a Yau-Tian-Donaldson correspondence.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Y
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein
G
-horospherical cone and
T_{H}
the connected component of the group of automorphisms commuting with
G
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

Y
admits a
\xi
-conical Calabi-Yau metric.

(ii)

(Y,T_{H},\xi)
is K-stable.

(iii)

\xi
minimizes the normalized Duistermaat-Heckman volume of
Y
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.3
, which is based on
[
Ber20
]
, uses the variational approach by Berman-Berndtsson
[
BB13
]
to solve a weighted real Monge-Ampère equation with exponential right-hand side and a relatively gentle condition on weight. The combinatorial condition is then equivalent to the existence of a locally bounded solution on the open dense orbit
G/H
. This solution is actually smooth if the weight is smooth (see Thm.
3.19
), and extends to a locally bounded conical Calabi-Yau potential over all
Y
(thanks to a
C^{0}
-estimate). We then use a regularity result, shown in a companion paper
[
Ngh22
]
, to conclude.

The volume minimization principle implies that there is a unique choice of a Reeb vector giving rise to a conical Calabi-Yau structure on the horospherical cone. This allows us to verify K-stability by explicit computations, thus producing many new examples of irregular Calabi-Yau cones. In the context of string theory, an infinite family of irregular toric cones (with an isolated singularity) was constructed by Gauntlett-Martelli-Sparks-Waldram
[
GMSW04
]
. These are the first examples of irregular Sasaki-Einstein manifolds, contradicting a conjecture of Cheeger and Tian that such manifolds do not exist. In our article, we construct a possibly infinite family of irregular
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein horospherical cones, arising more or less unexpectedly from symmetric spaces.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exist irregular Calabi-Yau horospherical cones arising from irreducible rank-two symmetric spaces of type
BC_{2}
.

In fact, another motivation of our result comes from the problem of finding asymptotic cones for Ricci-flat symmetric spaces. It turns out that we can build horospherical cones from combinatorial data associated to rank-two symmetric spaces. Such conical embeddings, endowed with their conical Calabi-Yau metrics, are expected to be asymptotic cones of suitable Ricci-flat metrics on the symmetric spaces considered in this article. This will be studied in a future paper.

1.2.
Organization

•

We start in Section
2
with generalities on the theory of equivariant embeddings of spherical spaces, as well as a brief description of the canonical divisor and
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein singularities. We then give a combinatorial necessary and sufficient condition for a spherical embedding to be a cone, and show that a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein spherical cone is a Fano cone. Several examples of homogeneous spaces (not) admitting conical embeddings are gathered at the end of the section.

•

Section
3
aims to study conical Calabi-Yau metrics on
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein horospherical cones. We derive an explicit expression of the canonical volume form. The conical Calabi-Yau problem is then translated into a weighted real Monge-Ampère equation with exponential right-hand side, which can be solved using the variational approach of
[
BB13
]
.

•

In Section
4
, we provide examples of regular and irregular cones arising from irreducible symmetric spaces of rank
2
.

2.3.

If

2.3.1.

Definition 2.32

A

-variety

.

[

For any spherical cone

Notation

Theorem 2.34
.

Let

be a spherical embedding of

with colored cone

i)

is a spherical cone.

ii)

\mathcal{V}
contains a line, the cone

Remark

Since

Proof.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
.

a) The valuation cone contains a line.

If
Y
is a cone, then it admits a unique fixed point

under the action of

b)

The unique fixed point

of

are
\mathcal{D}_{Y}=\mathcal{D}

Since

Recall that a

), hence does not contain any line. It follows that sober spaces never admit any conical embedding. In particular, semisimple symmetric spaces do not embed equivariantly into any symmetric cone, since their valuation cone is the restricted negative Weyl chamber, which is strictly convex.

3.
Conical Calabi-Yau metrics on horospherical cones

3.1.
Preliminaries

3.1.1.
Structure of horospherical cones

Let us recall some basic properties of horospherical varieties. For more information, the reader might consult Pasquier’s thesis
[
Pas08
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A horospherical cone is a conical embedding of a horospherical space.

Consider a conical embedding
G/H\subset Y
of a horospherical space of rank
r>0
with colored cone
(\mathcal{C}_{Y},\mathcal{D}_{Y})
.
Let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
and
T
be a maximal torus of
G
such that
T\cap K
is the maximal compact subtorus of
T
.

Denote by
(.,.)
the Weyl-invariant scalar product on
\mathfrak{t}
, whose restriction to the semisimple part
\mathfrak{t}\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
coincides with the Killing form. For all
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we denote by
t_{m_{2}}
the unique element in
\mathfrak{t}
such that

(m_{1},m_{2})=\left<m_{1},t_{m_{2}}\right>

Recall that the connected automorphism group compatible with
G
is isomorphic to the torus
T_{H}:=(N_{G}(H)/H)^{0}
. In the horospherical case,
N_{G}(H)=P
(see Prop.
2.23
), so
T_{H}=P/H
. In particular,

P
is the right-stabilizer of the open Borel-orbit. After choosing an adapted Lévi subgroup of
Q
, (cf.
[
Bri97
, 2.4, Proposition 2]
), we have
Q/H=T/T\cap H=T_{H}
. We can thus identify
T_{H}
with
T/T\cap H
as a group, and the weight lattice
\mathcal{M}(G/H)
with the weight lattice
\mathcal{M}(T_{H})
of the torus
T_{H}
. Moreover,
r=\text{rank}(G/H)=\dim T_{H}
.

Remark
.

We emphasize here the fact that
T_{H}
acts on
G/H
in two different ways: on the left and on the right. In the rest of this article, unless stated otherwise, by an action of
T_{H}
, we mean its action on the right, i.e.

p.(gH)=gp^{-1}H,\;\forall g\in G,p\in T_{H}

By a
G\times T_{H}
action, we mean the combined action of
G
on the left and
T_{H}
on the right.

Let
\mathfrak{t}_{H}
be the Lie algebra of
T_{H}
. The compact and non-compact part of
\mathfrak{t}_{H}
are denoted by
\mathfrak{t}_{H,c}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}
. They are respectively the Lie algebras of the compact real torus
T_{H,c}\simeq(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{r}
and
T_{H,nc}\simeq(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{r}
in the decomposition
T_{H}=T_{H,c}\times T_{H,nc}
. Moreover, we can choose the maximal compact subgroup
K
such that
T_{H,c}=T_{H}\cap K
.

Recall that there exists a natural isomorphism between

\mathfrak{t}_{H,c}
and
\mathcal{N}(T_{H})_{\mathbb{R}}
.
We identify an element
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}_{H,c}
in the Reeb cone with the vector field it generates (called the Reeb vector field). Let
J
be the complex structure on
Y_{\text{reg}}
. The vector field generated by
-J\xi\in\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}
defines a radial right-action of
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
on
Y
.

Remark
.

The reader should be aware that for a horospherical cone, the Reeb cone is exactly the opposite of the cone
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
. Indeed, if we look at the decomposition of
\mathbb{C}[Y]
into
T_{H}
-modules with respect to the left-action of
T_{H}
, then the cone generated by the weights is exactly
\mathcal{C}_{Y}^{\vee}
. However, with respect to the right-action, it is
-\mathcal{C}^{\vee}_{Y}
. Below is the Reeb cone for the conical embedding in Ex.
2.41
.

\rho(D_{1})
\rho(D_{2})
Reeb cone

-conical embedding.

Denote by
T_{\xi}
the compact torus generated by
\xi
, which is the closure of
\exp(\mathbb{R}\xi)
in
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(Y_{\text{reg}})^{0}
. In particular,
T_{\xi}
is a compact subtorus of
T_{H,c}
. If
\dim T_{\xi}=1
, or equivalently
\xi\in\mathcal{N}(T_{H})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we say that

is
quasi-regular
. If
\dim T_{\xi}>1

.

Let
\Phi
be the root system corresponding to
(G,T)
. Let us denote by
\Phi^{+}
the set of positive roots (with respect to a choice of the Borel subgroup) and
S
the positive simple roots. Let
P^{u}
(resp.
Q^{u}
) be the nilpotent radical of
P
(resp.
Q
). The choice of the parabolic group
P=N_{G}(H)
is equivalent to the choice of a subset
I\subset S
(cf. Prop.
2.23
). Moreover, let
\Phi_{I}
be the root system generated by

, the set of
P^{u}
-roots is then
\Phi_{P^{u}}:=\Phi^{+}\backslash\Phi_{I}
. In particular,
\Phi_{P^{u}}=-\Phi_{Q^{u}}
.
The Lie algebra of
G
can be decomposed as:

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{q}^{u}\oplus\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{u}

where
\mathfrak{q}^{u}
,
\mathfrak{p}^{u}
and
\mathfrak{l}
are the Lie algebras of
Q^{u}
,
P^{u}
, and the adapted Lévi subgroup
L=P\cap Q
.
Since
G/H
is a bundle with fiber
P/H\simeq T_{H}
over
G/P
, we have:

n=r+\left|\Phi_{P^{u}}\right|

We will denote by
S_{P^{u}}:=\Phi_{P^{u}}\cap S=S\backslash I
the set of simple roots of
P^{u}
and (two times) the sum of roots in
Q^{u}
(resp.
P^{u}
) by:

\varpi_{Q}=2\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{Q^{u}}}\alpha=-\varpi_{P}

The following proposition describes the colors in a horospherical space in terms of coroots.

.

[

The images of the colors

in a horospherical space are exactly the restriction to
\mathcal{M}
of the coroots of
S_{P^{u}}

\rho(\mathcal{D})=\left\{\alpha^{\vee}|_{\mathcal{M}},\alpha\in S_{P^{u}}\right\}

Since we only work with quasi-affine spherical spaces,
\rho(\mathcal{D})
does not contain
0
, i.e. for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{P^{u}}
,
\alpha^{\vee}|_{\mathcal{M}}\neq 0
.

The following proposition follows straightforwardly from our criterion in Thm.

.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

be a horospherical affine variety with open

-orbit

and colored cone
(\mathcal{C}_{Y},\mathcal{D}_{Y})
. The following assertions are equivalent :

(i)

Y
is a cone.

(ii)

\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is full dimensional and
\mathcal{D}_{Y}=\mathcal{D}
.

(iii)

G
acts on

with a fixed point.

(iv)

T_{H}
acts on

with a fixed point.

3.1.2.
Conical Calabi-Yau metrics on horospherical cones

Let
Y
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein horospherical cone of rank
r
and of dimension
n
, which is
G\times T_{H}
-equivariantly embedded in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Let us first state a useful remark.

Remark
.

By the Iwasawa decomposition of
G
, cf.
[
Del20a
, Prop. 2.7]
,
\exp(\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc})H=T_{H}
defines a fundamental domain for the
K
-action on
G/H
, i.e.
G/H
can be written as a disjoint union of
K
-translated copies of
T_{H}
. It follows that every
K
-invariant function
u
on
G/H
restricts on
T_{H}
to a function left-invariant by
T_{H,c}
. In particular, in the coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
of
T_{H}
,

u(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})=u(\log\left|z_{1}\right|^{2},\dots,\log\left|z_{r}\right|^{2})

As a consequence,
u
is also right-invariant by
T_{H,c}
. Therefore every
K
-invariant function on
G/H
is automatically
(K\times T_{H,c})
-invariant. The same goes for
K
-invariant forms on
G/H
.

Definition 3.4
.

A
K
-invariant plurisubharmonic (psh) function
on
Y
is a function
f
such that for every local
K
-equivariant embedding of
Y
,
f
is the restriction of a global
U_{N}
-invariant psh function on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a polarized horospherical cone. A
K
-invariant
\xi
-radial function
(or
\xi
-conical potential
) on
Y
is a positive
K
-invariant strictly psh function
\rho^{2}:Y\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\rho^{2}|_{G/H}
is smooth and

\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}\rho^{2}=2\rho^{2}

on
Y_{\text{reg}}
. If moreover
\rho^{2}
is locally bounded on
Y
, the
K
-invariant Kähler (1,1)-current defined as

\omega:=dd^{c}\rho^{2}

is then called a
Kähler cone current
.

Note that
\omega
is well-defined by the local theoy of Bedford-Taylor
[
BT76
]
. Since
T_{\xi}\subset T_{H,c}
, a
K
-invariant
\xi
-radial function is automatically
\xi
-invariant. Before stating the definition of conical Calabi-Yau metrics on
Y
, let us first make some digressions on linearized line bundles.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group and
X
be any
G
-variety. A
G
-linearized line bundle
on
X
is a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
X
endowed with an action of
G
such that (i) the projection
\pi:\mathcal{L}\to X
is
G
-equivariant and that (ii)
G
acts linearly on the fibers of
\mathcal{L}
.

For all section
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
and
x\in X
,
G
induces an action on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
by:

(g.s)(x)=g.s(g^{-1}x)

The group
G
then acts on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
and the latter is a rational
G
-module. For every
reductive
group
G
and every line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over a
normal

G
-variery, there exists a positive integer
m>0
such that
m\mathcal{L}
is
G
-linearized. If in addition
\mathbb{C}[G]
is factorial, then one can choose
m=1
. Every
G
-linearized line bundle over
G
is trivial. We refer the reader to
[
KKLV89
]
for the proofs of these assertions.

Example 3.7
.

Let
H
be any closed subgroup of
G
. Every
G
-linearized line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
G/H
is determined by a character
\chi_{H}
of
H
. Indeed, given
\chi_{H}
, the quotient
\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{H}}
of
G\times\mathbb{C}
by the action:

h(g,z)=(gh^{-1},\chi_{H}(h)z)

is a
G
-linearized line bundle over
G/H
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{L}
is
G
-linearized, then
H
acts on the fiber
\mathcal{L}_{eH}
by a character
\chi_{H}
. We can then directly prove that

G\times\mathcal{L}_{eH}\to\mathcal{L}
,
(g,z)\to(gH,g.z)
induces an isomorphism between
\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{H}}
and
\mathcal{L}
.

Let us come back to the setting of our cone
Y
.
Since
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, the canonical bundle
m\mathcal{K}_{Y}
is a well-defined Cartier divisor and naturally
(G\times T_{H})
-linearized for some integer
m>0
.

Note that the Picard group of
Y
is trivial (since
Y=Y_{\left\{0_{Y}\right\},B}
by Thm.
2.10
, and
\text{Pic}(Y_{\left\{0_{Y}\right\},B})=0
by
[

). It follows that there exists a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
(G\times T_{H})
-invariant section
s
of
m\mathcal{K}_{Y}
, and a
K
-invariant volume form
dV_{Y}
defined by

dV_{Y}=(\sqrt{-1}^{mn^{2}}s\wedge\overline{s})^{1/m}

Remark
.

To simplify the notation, we will sometimes abuse the language and say that
s
is a “multivalued” holomorphic section of
\mathcal{K}_{Y}
and simply write
dV_{Y}=i^{n^{2}}s\wedge\overline{s}
.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein singularities of
Y
implies the existence of a linear function
l:(-\mathcal{C}_{Y})\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(which is
-\beta
in Prop.
2.31
) such that for all
D_{\nu}\in\mathcal{V}_{Y}
and
D\in\mathcal{D}
:

\left<l,\rho(D_{\nu})\right>=-1,\quad\left<l,\rho(D)\right>=-a_{D}

where
a_{D}
is the coefficient of
D
in the expression of
\mathcal{K}_{Y}
.

Definition 3.8
.

A
T_{H,c}
-invariant nowhere-vanishing section
s_{Y}\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{K}_{Y})
is said to be a
canonical section
if

\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}s_{Y}=\left<l,\xi\right>s_{Y}

on
Y_{\text{reg}}
for all
\xi\in\text{Int}(-\mathcal{C}_{Y})
, where
-\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is the Reeb cone of
Y
and
\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}
is the Lie derivative for the right-action of
-J\xi
.

A volume form
dV_{Y}
on
Y
is called
canonical
if on
Y_{\text{reg}}
,

\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}dV_{Y}=2\left<l,\xi\right>dV_{Y}

Remark
.

Since every
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein spherical variety has klt singularities
[
Pas17
]
, it follows from
[
MSY08
]
(see also
[
CS19
, Lemma 6.2]
) that a canonical volume form always exists. In what follows, we will give an explicit expression of the canonical volume form on
Y
.

Remark
.

A canonical section
s_{Y}
determines a canonical volume form by setting
dV_{Y}:=i^{n^{2}}s_{Y}\wedge\overline{s_{Y}}
. Conversely, let
dV_{Y}
be a canonical volume form determined by a multivalued holomorphic section
s
. For each
\xi
, there exists a real function
f_{\xi}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}s=f_{\xi}s

It follows that
\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}dV_{Y}=2f_{\xi}i^{n^{2}}s\wedge\overline{s}
, hence
f_{\xi}=\left<l,\xi\right>
for all
\xi\in-\mathcal{C}_{Y}
.

Remark
.

A canonical section is unique up to a constant. Indeed, for any two canonical sections, there exists
f\in\mathbb{C}[Y]^{*}=\mathbb{C}
such that
s_{1}=fs_{2}
. In particular, a canonical volume form is unique up to a constant.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein polarized horospherical cone of dimension
n
and
dV_{Y}
its canonical volume form. A
K
-invariant conical Calabi-Yau metric
on
Y
is a
K
-invariant Kähler cone current
\omega
on
Y
satisfying

\omega^{n}=(dd^{c}\rho^{2})^{n}=dV_{Y}

such that the
\xi
-radial function
\rho^{2}
is smooth on the regular locus
Y_{\text{reg}}
. The function
\rho^{2}
is then said to be a
conical Calabi-Yau potential
.

In particular, a Calabi-Yau metric is a singular Kähler-Einstein metric in the sense of
[
EGZ09
]
. As one will see, the above equation takes the form of a real Monge-Ampère equation due to symmetry by
K
.

3.2.1.

The expression of a

Recall that
\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r}

\mathfrak{p}^{u}
be the Lie algebra of the unipotent radical
P^{u}

J
the complex structure of
G/H
, and
\sqrt{-1}
the complex structure coming from the complexification of the real vector space
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}

\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C}\simeq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{q}^{u}}\mathbb{C}u_{\alpha}\bigoplus\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}\bigoplus\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}

Here

is the eigenvector with weight
\alpha
of

.

Let us come back to our conical embedding
G/H\hookrightarrow Y
. Consider a
(G\times T_{H})
-linearized line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
G/H
with a
K
-invariant hermitian metric
h
. Let
i^{*}\mathcal{L}
be the line bundle over
T_{H}
induced by the natural inclusion
i:T_{H}\hookrightarrow G/H
, endowed with the
T_{H,c}
-invariant metric
i^{*}h
.

After replacing
i^{*}\mathcal{L}
by a multiple large enough, we can suppose that
i^{*}\mathcal{L}
is
T_{H}
-linearized and trivial over
T_{H}

i^{*}h
is the curvature form of a

u

By pullback using the map
\exp:\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}\to T_{H}

as a function

.

[

The metric

of the metric
i^{*}h

.

By the Iwasawa decomposition in

K
-invariant curvature form of

T_{H}
.

Let
(l_{j})_{1\leq j\leq r}
be a real basis of
\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}
,
(Jl_{j})_{1\leq j\leq r}
a basis of
J\mathfrak{t}_{H,nc}

(u_{-\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Phi_{P^{u}}}

.

Consider the associated holomorphic vector

\zeta_{j}:=\frac{1}{2}(l_{j}-\sqrt{-1}Jl_{j}),\quad w_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{-\alpha}-\sqrt{-1}Ju_{-\alpha})

and associate to them the
T_{H}
-invariant holomorphic vector fields of the tangent bundle of
G/H
by translation under the action of
T_{H}
, i.e. for all

\mathfrak{z}_{j}:p\to(R_{p^{-1}})_{*}\zeta_{j},\quad\mathfrak{w}_{\alpha}:p\to(R_{p^{-1}})_{*}w_{\alpha}

Let
\left\{\mathfrak{z}^{*}_{j}\right\}_{1\leq j\leq r}\cup\left\{\mathfrak{w}^{*}_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in\Phi_{P^{u}}}
be the dual

.

[

For all

3.2.2.
Canonical volume form

We provide in this part an expression of the canonical volume form of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein horospherical cone.

Proposition 3.12
.

The canonical volume form on
Y
is
K
-invariant on
G/H
and writes in
T_{H}
as (up to a constant):

(dV_{Y})_{\exp(x)H}=e^{\left<\beta-\varpi_{Q},x\right>}\bigwedge_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{i}{2}\mathfrak{z}^{*}_{j}\wedge\overline{\mathfrak{z}_{j}^{*}}\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\Phi_{P^{u}}}\frac{i}{2}\mathfrak{w}_{\alpha}^{*}\wedge\overline{\mathfrak{w}^{*}_{\alpha}}

for all

Proof.

Let
dV_{Y}
be the canonical section of
Y
(which always exists, cf. the remark that follows Defn.
3.8
). Consider the following
K
-invariant volume form of
G/H
as constructed in
[

, Prop 2.6]

dV_{\exp(x)H}:=e^{\left<-\varpi_{Q},x\right>}\bigwedge_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{i}{2}\left(\mathfrak{z}^{*}_{j}\wedge\overline{\mathfrak{z}^{*}_{j}}\right)_{\exp(x)H}\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\Phi_{P^{u}}}\frac{i}{2}\left(\mathfrak{w}^{*}_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{\mathfrak{w}^{*}_{\alpha}}\right)_{\exp(x)H}

Now for all
(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})\in T_{H}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}
, let
z^{\alpha}:=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots z_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
and
x_{j}=\log\left|z_{j}\right|^{2}
. Consider the following
K
-invariant volume form:

(dV^{\prime}_{Y})_{\exp(x)H}:=\left|z\right|^{2\beta}dV_{\exp(x)H}

Let us prove that
dV_{Y}
restricts to

on

. Indeed, since

is

\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}dV=0
, hence:

\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}dV^{\prime}_{Y}=(\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}\left|z\right|^{2\beta})dV

By embedding equivariantly
T_{H}
into
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}
with coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
, one can identify
-J\xi
with

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\xi_{j}\Re(z_{j}\partial_{z_{j}}),\;\xi_{j}>0

where
\xi_{j}
is the weight of
v_{\xi}=(-J\xi-\sqrt{-1}\xi)/2
on

. By integration, one finds that the one-parameter right-action generated by
-J\xi
is

\varphi_{t}^{-J\xi}(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})=(e^{-t\xi_{1}}z_{1},\dots,e^{-t\xi_{r}}z_{r})

A computation on
T_{H}
then yields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}\left|z\right|^{2\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\lvert_{t=0}e^{-2t\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\xi_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left|z_{j}\right|^{2\beta_{j}}

\displaystyle=2\left<-\beta,\xi\right>\left|z\right|^{2\beta}

It follows that
\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}dV^{\prime}_{Y}=2\left<l,\xi\right>dV^{\prime}_{Y}

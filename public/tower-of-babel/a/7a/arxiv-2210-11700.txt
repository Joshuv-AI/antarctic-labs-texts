\overrightarrow{K}_{n,n}
the complete bipartite digraph of order

with vertex set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\cup\{1^{\prime},2^{\prime},\ldots,n^{\prime}\}
and arc set
\{(i,j^{\prime})\ |\ 1\leq i,j\leq n\}
. For a digraph
\Gamma
and two subsets

and

of

, denote by
[U,W]_{\Gamma}
the sub-digraph of

\Gamma
induced by
U
and
W
, namely the vertex set is
U\cup W
and the arc set is
\{(u,w)\in{\rm Arc}(\Gamma)\ |\ u\in U,w\in W\}
. Let
G
be a group and

a subgroup of
G
. A
right transversal
of
H
in

is a set of right coset representations of
H
in
G
, that is, a set

with
G=\cup_{\ell\in L}H\ell
and
H\ell_{1}\not=H\ell_{2}
for all

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
H\unlhd G
and
H\leq M\leq G
. Assume that
C\subseteq G\backslash H
is a union of some cosets of
H
in
G
, and that
S_{M}
and
T_{M}
are subsets of
M\backslash\{1\}
such that
S_{M}^{\alpha}=T_{M}
for some
\alpha\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(M)
and
\alpha
fixes every coset of
H
in
M
. Then
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,S)\cong\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,T)
, where
S=C\cup S_{M}
and
T=C\cup T_{M}
.

Proof.
Let
\Gamma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,S)
and
\Sigma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,T)
. Then
V(\Gamma)=V(\Sigma)=G
. Let
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\}
be a right transversal of
M
in
G
. Define a map from
G
to
G
as follows:

\varphi:mg_{k}\mapsto m^{\alpha}g_{k},\ \mbox{ for every }1\leq k\leq\ell\ \mbox{ and }m\in M.

It is easy to see that
\varphi
is a permutation on
G
. Clearly,
g_{k}^{\varphi}=g_{k}
, and since
\alpha
fixes every coset of
H
in
M
,
\varphi
fixes every coset of
H
in
G
. To finish the proof, it suffices to show that
\varphi
is an isomorphism from
\Gamma
to
\Sigma
.

Let
(v,w)
be an arc of
\Gamma
. Then
v=mg_{k}\in Mg_{k}
for some
1\leq k\leq\ell
and some
m\in M
. Since
S=C\cup S_{M}
, we have
w=gv
, where
g\in S_{M}
or
C
.

Assume
g\in S_{M}
. Then
g\in M
. Furthermore,
v=mg_{k}
and
w=gmg_{k}
. Thus,
v^{\varphi}=m^{\alpha}g_{k}
and
w^{\varphi}=(gm)^{\alpha}g_{k}=g^{\alpha}m^{\alpha}g_{k}
because
\alpha\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(M)
. Since
g^{\alpha}\in S_{M}^{\alpha}=T_{M}\subseteq T
,
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is an arc of
\Sigma
.

Assume
g\in C
. By hypothesis,
g\in Hx\subseteq C
for some
x\in G\backslash H
. Then
g=wv^{-1}\in Hx
and
Hg=Hx
. For any
y\in Hv
and
z\in Hw
,
zy^{-1}\in Hwv^{-1}H=Hwv^{-1}=Hx\subseteq C
because
H\unlhd G
.
So the induced sub-digraph
[Hv,Hw]_{\Gamma}
is isomorphic the complete bipartite digraph
\overrightarrow{K}_{|H|,|H|}
. Since
\varphi
fixes every coset of
H
in
G
, we have
(Hv)^{\varphi}=Hv
and
(Hw)^{\varphi}=Hw
, which implies
v^{\varphi}\in Hv
and
w^{\varphi}\in Hw
. Since
g\in Hx\subseteq C\subseteq T
, similarly we have
[Hv,Hw]_{\Sigma}\cong\overrightarrow{K}_{|H|,|H|}
. It follows that
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is an arc of
\Sigma
.

We have proved that
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is always an arc of
\Sigma
, and hence
\varphi
is an isomorphism from
\Gamma
to
\Sigma
. This completes the proof.
\Box

Next we give a result on isomorphic Cayley digraphs of
\mathrm{D}_{2n}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 4
be even and let
\mathrm{D}_{2n}=\langle a,b\ |\ a^{n}=b^{2}=1,a^{b}=a^{-1}\rangle
. Let
H\subseteq\langle a^{2}\rangle

such that
H^{-1}=H
and

\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},S)\cong\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},T)
, where
S=bH\cup K
and
T=bH\cup bK
. In particular, if
\mathrm{D}_{2n}
has the
|S|
-DCI property then

.

Proof.
Since
n
is even,
\langle a^{2}\rangle
is a subgroup of
\langle a\rangle
with index
2
. Then
\mathrm{D}_{2n}=\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle
. Define
\varphi
by

\varphi:x\mapsto x\mbox{ for }x\in\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle\mbox{ and }x\mapsto bx\mbox{ for }x\in a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle,

that is,
\varphi
fixes every element in
\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle
and interchanges
x
and
bx
for every
x\in a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle
. Then
\varphi
is a permutation on
\mathrm{D}_{2n}
. Set
\Gamma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},S)
and
\Sigma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},T)
. We claim that
\varphi
is an isomorphism from
\Gamma
to
\Sigma
.

Let
(v,w)
be an arc of
\Gamma
. Then
w=sv
with
s\in bH\cup K
, that is,
s\in K
or
bH
.

Case 1:

s\in K\subseteq a\langle a^{2}\rangle
.

Since
\mathrm{D}_{2n}=\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle
, we have
v\in\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle
or
a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle
. If
v\in\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle
, then
w=sv\in a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle
as
s\in a\langle a^{2}\rangle
. By the definition of
\varphi
,
v^{\varphi}=v
and
w^{\varphi}=bw=bsv
, which implies that
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is an arc of
\Sigma
because
bs\in bK\subseteq T
. If
v\in a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle
, then
w=sv\in\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle
. It follows that
v^{\varphi}=bv
and
w^{\varphi}=w=sv=bs^{-1}bv
as
s\in a\langle a^{2}\rangle
. Since
K=K^{-1}
, we have
bs^{-1}\in bK\subseteq T
and hence
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is an arc of
\Sigma
.

Case 2:

s\in bH\subseteq b\langle a^{2}\rangle
.

If
v\in\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle
, then
w=sv\in\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup b\langle a^{2}\rangle
as
s\in b\langle a^{2}\rangle
. Thus,
v^{\varphi}=v
and
w^{\varphi}=w=sv
, which implies that
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is an arc of
\Sigma
because
s\in bH\subseteq T
. If
v\in a\langle a^{2}\rangle\cup ba\langle a^{2}\rangle
, then

v^{\varphi}=bv
and
w^{\varphi}=bw=bsb(bv)
. Since
H=H^{-1}\subseteq\langle a^{2}\rangle
, we have
bsb\in b(bH)b=Hb=bH^{-1}=bH\subseteq T
, and hence
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is an arc of
\Sigma
.

By Cases
1
and
2
,
(v^{\varphi},w^{\varphi})
is always an arc of
\Sigma
, and so
\varphi
is an isomorphism from
\Gamma
to
\Sigma
, as claimed. Thus,
\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},S)\cong\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},T)
.

Assume that
\mathrm{D}_{2n}
has the

-DCI property. Since
\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},S)\cong\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},T)
,
\mathrm{D}_{2n}
has an automorphism

. Since

\langle a\rangle

\langle a\rangle^{\alpha}=\langle a\rangle

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. The following lemma shows that a dihedral group has “similar” properties on CI-subset with its unique cyclic subgroup.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathrm{D}_{2n}=\langle a,b\ |\ a^{n}=b^{2}=1,a^{b}=a^{-1}\rangle
with
n\geq 3
, and let
\Gamma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},S)
be a Cayley digraph of
\mathrm{D}_{2n}
with
A=\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. Assume that
A
has a regular subgroup
R
isomorphic
\mathrm{D}_{2n}
. Then
R
and
R(\mathrm{D}_{2n})
are conjugate in
A
if and only if the unique cyclic subgroup of order
n
in
R
is conjugate to
\langle R(a)\rangle
in
A
.

Proof.
The necessity is clear because a dihedral group of order
2n
(
n\geq 3
) has a unique cyclic subgroup of order
n
. To prove the sufficiency, we may write
R=\langle x\rangle\rtimes\langle y\rangle
with
o(x)=n
,
o(y)=2
and
x^{y}=x^{-1}
, and assume that
A
has an element, say
\alpha
, such that
\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}=\langle R(a)\rangle
. To finish the proof, it suffices to show that

R^{\alpha}=R(\mathrm{D}_{2n})
.

Clearly,
R^{\alpha}=\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}\rtimes\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}=\langle R(a)\rangle\rtimes\langle y^{\alpha}\rangle\cong\mathrm{D}_{2n}
is regular on
V(\Gamma)
and
R(a)^{y^{\alpha}}=R(a)^{-1}
. Since
R(a)^{R(b)}=R(a)^{-1}
, we have
R(a)^{y^{\alpha}R(b)}=R(a)
. Note that
\langle R(a)\rangle
has exactly two orbits on
V(\Gamma)
, that is,
\langle a\rangle
and
b\langle a\rangle
. Since both
R^{\alpha}
and
R(\mathrm{D}_{2n})
are regular on
V(\Gamma)
and contain the normal subgroup
\langle R(a)\rangle
, both
R(b)
and
y^{\alpha}
interchanges
\langle a\rangle
and
b\langle a\rangle
, respectively. In particular,
R(b)
interchanges
1
and
b
, and
y^{\alpha}
interchanges
1
and
ba^{i}
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Let
z=y^{\alpha}R(a)^{-i}\in R^{\alpha}
. Then
1^{z}=b
, and
z
interchanges
\langle a\rangle
and
b\langle a\rangle
. It follows that
R(b)z
fixes
\langle a\rangle
and
b\langle a\rangle
setwise, respectively.

Since
\langle R(a)\rangle\rtimes\langle{y^{\alpha}}\rangle\cong\mathrm{D}_{2n}
,
z=y^{\alpha}R(a)^{-i}
is an involution and hence
b^{z}=1
as
1^{z}=b
. Thus,
R(b)z
fixes
1
and
b
, and so
\langle R(a),R(b)z\rangle
has two orbits on
V(\Gamma)
, that is,
\langle a\rangle
and
b\langle a\rangle
. Since
R(a)^{z}=R(a)^{y^{\alpha}R(a)^{-i}}=R(a)^{-1}
, we have
R(a)^{R(b)z}=R(a)
and so
\langle R(a),R(b)z\rangle
is abelian. This implies that
R(b)z
fixes
\langle a\rangle
and
b\langle a\rangle
pointwise. It follows that
R(b)z=1
, that is
R(b)=z
, and so
R^{\alpha}=\langle R(a)\rangle\rtimes\langle y^{\alpha}\rangle=\langle R(a)\rangle\rtimes\langle y^{\alpha}R(a)^{-i}\rangle=\langle R(a)\rangle\rtimes\langle z\rangle=\langle R(a)\rangle\rtimes\langle R(b)\rangle=R(\mathrm{D}_{2n})
. This completes the proof.
\Box

Next we give a sufficiency condition for CI-digraphs on dihedral groups.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(\mathrm{D}_{2n},S)
be a digraph with
n
odd and let
A=\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. If
(|A_{1}|,n)=1
then
\Gamma
is a CI-digraph, and if
\Gamma
is connected and
|S|<p
for the least prime divisor
p
of
n
, then
\Gamma
is a CI-digraph.

Proof.
Let
\mathrm{D}_{2n}=\langle a,b\ |\ a^{n}=b^{2}=1,a^{b}=a^{-1}\rangle
and let
\pi=\{q\ |\ q\mbox{ is a prime divisor of }n\}
. Assume that
(|A_{1}|,n)=1
. Since
A=R(G)A_{1}
and
n
is odd,
\langle R(a)\rangle
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
A
. By
[
34
, Theorem 9.1.10]
, all nilpotent Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
A
are conjugate, and then Lemma
4.1
implies that all regular subgroups of
A
isomorphic to
R(G)
are conjugate. By Proposition
2.1
,
\Gamma
is a CI-digraph.

Now assume that
\Gamma
is connected and
|S|<p
, where
p
is the least prime divisor of
n
. Then
\Gamma
has out-valency less than
p
, and the connectivity of
\Gamma
implies that each prime divisor of
|A_{1}|
is less than
p
. It follows that
(|A_{1}|,n)=1
and then the above paragraph means that
\Gamma
is a CI-digraph.
\Box

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:
Let
G=\mathrm{D}_{2n}=\langle a,b\ |\ a^{n}=b^{2}=1,a^{b}=a^{-1}\rangle
be the dihedral group of order
2n
with
n\geq 3
.

To prove part (1), assume that
G
has the
m
-DCI property for some
1\leq m\leq n-1
, and that
p
is a prime divisor of
2n
. Then
n
is odd by Lemma
3.3
. If further
p+1\leq m\leq n-1
. then
G_{p}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
by Lemma
3.4
. This completes the proof of part (1).

To prove part (2), assume
n=q^{r}
with
q
a prime and
r
a positive integer, and assume
1\leq m\leq n-1
.

To prove the necessity of part (2), let
G
be of the
m
-DCI property. By part (1),
n
is odd, and hence
q
is odd. Now assume that
r\geq 2
. If
q+1\leq m\leq n-1
, then
G_{q}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{q}
by part (1), contradicting
r\geq 2
. Thus
1\leq m\leq q
, completing the proof of the necessity.

To prove the sufficiency of part (2), assume that
q
is odd, and that either
n=q
or
1\leq m\leq q
. For
n=q
,
\mathrm{D}_{2q}
is a DCI-group by Proposition
2.5
, and hence has the
m
-DCI property. Thus, we may assume
1\leq m\leq q
. Let
\Gamma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,S)
with
|S|=m
and let
\Sigma=\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,T)
such that
\Gamma\cong\Sigma
. To finish the proof, we only need to show that
G
has an automorphism
\alpha
such that
S^{\alpha}=T
.

Let
K=\langle S\rangle
and
H=\langle T\rangle
. Then
K\leq G
and
H\leq G
. Note that
\Gamma=|G:K|\,\hbox{\rm Cay}(K,S)
, the
|G:K|
distinct copies of the connected digraph
\hbox{\rm Cay}(K,S)
, and similarly,
\Sigma=|G:H|\,\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,T)
, the
|G:H|
distinct copies of the connected digraph
\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,T)
. Since
\Sigma\cong\Gamma
, we have
|K|=|H|
, and since
G
is the dihedral group of order
2q^{r}
with
q
odd, both
K
and
H
are either a cyclic subgroup of
\langle a\rangle
or a dihedral subgroup of
G
, with the same order, which implies
K\cong H
. By Proposition
2.7
, there is
\beta\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(G)
such that
K^{\beta}=H
, and then
\hbox{\rm Cay}(K,S)\cong\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,S^{\beta})
. It follows that
\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,S^{\beta})\cong\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,T)
. Let
\Delta=\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,S^{\beta})
and
B=\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Delta)
.

Now we claim that
\Delta
is a CI-digraph. Recall that
H
is cyclic subgroup of
\langle a\rangle
or a dihedral subgroup of
G
. If
H\leq\langle a\rangle
then Proposition
2.6
implies that
H
is a
q
-DCI-group and hence has the
m
-DCI property for each
1\leq m\leq q
. So
\Delta
is a CI-digraph. Now we may assume that
H
is dihedral. If
q\nmid|B_{1}|
(in this case
(|B_{1}|,q^{r})=1
) or
|S^{\beta}|<q
, Lemma
4.2
implies that
\Delta
is a CI-digraph. Thus, we may further assume that
q\mid|B_{1}|
and
|S^{\beta}|=q
, which implies that
\Delta
is arc-transitive. Since
H
is dihedral and
\Delta
is connected,
S^{\beta}
contains an involution, and then the arc-transitivity of
\Delta
implies that
\Delta
is a graph, that is,
S^{\beta}=(S^{\beta})^{-1}
. The classification of connected arc-transitive Cayley graphs of a dihedral group of order
2
times
q
-power, given by Kovács
[
17
]
, implies that
\Delta\cong K_{q,q}
. It follows that
H\cong\mathrm{D}_{2q}
, and since
\mathrm{D}_{2q}
is a DCI-group,
\Delta
is a CI-digraph as claimed.

Since
\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,S^{\beta})\cong\hbox{\rm Cay}(H,T)
and
\Delta
is a CI-digraph,
H
has an automorphism
\gamma^{\prime}
such that
(S^{\beta})^{\gamma^{\prime}}=T
. By Proposition
2.7
,
G
has an automorphism
\gamma
such that
\gamma|_{H}=\gamma^{\prime}
, that is, the restriction of
\gamma
on
H
is
\gamma^{\prime}
. It follows that
S^{\beta\gamma}=T
. Take
\alpha=\beta\gamma
. Then
\alpha\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(G)
and
S^{\alpha}=T
. This completes the proof.
\Box

A categorical proof of the Carathéodory extension theorem
Ruben Van Belle

Abstract
The Carathéodory extension theorem is a fundamental result in measure theory. Often we do not know what a general measurable subset looks like. The Carathéodory extension theorem states that to define a measure we only need to assign values to subsets in a generating Boolean algebra. To prove this result categorically, we represent (pre)measures and outer measures by certain (co)lax and strict transformations. The Carathéodory extension then corresponds to a Kan extension of strict transformations. We develop a general framework for extensions of transformations between poset-valued functors and give several results on the existence and construction of extensions of these transformations. We proceed by showing that transformations and functors corresponding to measures satisfy these results, which proves the Carathéodory extension theorem.

Introduction

The Carathéodory extension theorem is an important result in measure theory. It guarantees the existence of the Lebesgue measure (or more generally the Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure) and of product measures. But it also is a key result in the proof of the Kolmogorov extensions theorem, which guarantees the existence of Brownian motion and which is closely related to martingale convergence results.

If we want to define a measure on a measurable space
(X,\Sigma)
, we need to assign to every subset
A\in\Sigma
an element of
[0,\infty]
. However, we often work with
\sigma
-algebras of the form
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
, where
\mathcal{B}
is an algebra of subsets of
X
, i.e. closed under finite unions and complements. In this case it can be very difficult to know what a general measurable subset looks like and to assign real numbers to them in a
\sigma
-additive way. This problem is solved by the Carathéodory extension theorem. It states that a
\sigma
-additive map
\mathcal{B}\to[0,\infty]
can be extended to a
\sigma
-additive map
\sigma(\mathcal{B})\to[0,\infty]
. Here,
\sigma
-additive should be interpreted as ‘
\sigma
-additive whenever unions of countable pairwise disjoint collections exist’. Moreover, this extension is very often, but not always, unique.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[0,\infty]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sigma(\mathcal{B})}}

The classical proof for this result is relatively long and technical (see for example Theorem 1.41 in
[
3
]
). It consists of different steps of extending and restricting back and requires several smart ‘tricks’ and constructions. In this paper we will give a categorical proof for the Carathéodory extension theorem using results on extensions of lax,colax and strict transformations between functors. Several parts of this proof look similar to steps in the classical proof. However, in our proof all constructions follow from the Kan extension formulas. Moreover, viewing the Carathéodory extension theorem in this categorical framework, allows us to compare it to extension results in other areas of mathematics. Furthermore, this technique allows us to easily generalize Carathéodory’s result to measures taking values in other posets or spaces.

To do this we start by studying categories of certain transformations between functors and extensions of transformations along transformations. In section
2
and
3
we do this on a fairly abstract level. We discuss (co)laxification (co)monads and are in particular interested in the case that their (co)algebras are strict transformations. We give several abstract conditions for extensions of certain transformations to exist.

In section
4
and
5
, we give concrete constructions for the operations and extensions discussed in
2
and
3
. In this part (co)lax coends and (co)lax morphism classifiers play an important role.

In the last two sections of the paper we apply the previous sections to measure theory. We start by representing inner and outer premeasures by certain transformations between certain functors. By applying the extension results to these functors, we obtain our categorical proof for Carathédory’s extension result. We furthermore, make a distinction between
left
and
right
Carathédory extensions. The right one always exists and is the same as the extension in the classical proof, the left one however does not always exist. This is also related to the fact that inner measures and outer measures can behave surprisingly different from each other. Moreover, we immediately obtain a way to characterize these extensions by a universal property, namely as a maximal or minimal extension. This is interesting when uniqueness is not guaranteed.

6
Premeasures as transformations

In this section we will represent certain premeasures by certain transformations between functors. Roughly speaking, colax and lax transformation correspond to inner and outer premeasures and strict transformations correspond to measures. Using these representations, we can apply the results from Section

We will study two approaches. In the first approach we will look at functors whose domain is

Proposition 6.4

strict

Let
(X_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
and
(X_{2},\mathcal{B}_{2})

if

for all

for all

\mathbf{PartPreMble}
.

A partial map

of countable sets is called

if
f^{-1}(B^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}_{f}(A)
for all

\mathbf{Part}_{c}^{f}
for the poset-enriched category of countable sets and partial finite maps, which are ordered. The collection of injective partial finite maps is denoted by
\Sigma

again yields an enriched functor

as

For a countable set

is the set of

Let

be the poset

such that

\mathcal{B}
. It is called an

if

for pairwise disjoint subsets

\mathcal{B}

7
The Carathéodory extension theorem

In this last section, we will apply the results of extensions of transformations from sections
3

5
to the transformations that represent measures and premeasures, as described in Section

Let

be a premeasurable space. Let
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{B}
. Let

and

. For a countable set

\iota_{A}:F_{\mathcal{B}}A\to F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})}A
. These define a strict transformation
\iota:F_{\mathcal{B}}\to F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})}

along
\iota
and that these extensions are proper.

We will show that right extensions of strict transformations always exist, which will imply the Carathéodory extension theorem. We will refer to this extension as the
right Carathéodory extension
. Moreover, this characterizes the right Carathéodory extension by a universal property. We will also briefly discuss left extensions. However, we will show that the
left Carathéodory extension
does not exist in general.

We first look at a useful result for right extensions of

-natural general transformations. The following result shows a partial objectwiseness and will later be used to reduce extensions of transformations to the usual Kan extension of order-preserving maps between posets.

We will use the notations

and

to emphasize that we are looking at the right Kan extensions of
\Sigma
-natural general transformations or

-natural lax transformation respectively.

[\mathrm{Ran}^{\mathrm{lax}}_{\iota}\lambda]_{A}(x)_{a}=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(B_{n})\mid X_{a}\subseteq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n},\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\in\mathcal{B}\right\}

for all countable sets
A
and
x:=(X_{a})_{a\in A}\in F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})}A
.

Proof.

The existence follows from Proposition
3.4
. The left adjoints in the following diagram commute

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})},G]^{\Sigma}_{l}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\mathcal{B}},G]^{\Sigma}_{l}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})},G]^{\Sigma}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\mathcal{B}},G]^{\Sigma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{l}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{l}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\circ\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\circ\iota}

Therefore their right adjoints also commute, i.e.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})},G]^{\Sigma}_{l}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\mathcal{B}},G]^{\Sigma}_{l}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})},G]^{\Sigma}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[F_{\mathcal{B}},G]^{\Sigma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{l}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{l}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Ran}^{\text{lax}}_{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Ran}^{\text{gen}}_{\iota}}

Therefore we have,

\mathrm{Ran}^{\mathrm{lax}}_{\iota}\lambda=R_{l}(\mathrm{Ran}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{\iota}\lambda).

Let
A
be a countable set and let
x:=(X_{a})_{a\in A}\in F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})}A
. By Proposition
6.13
and Lemma
7.1
together with the fact that
\mathrm{Ran}_{\iota_{\textbf{1}}}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}\circ\iota_{\textbf{1}}=\lambda_{\textbf{1}}
, since
\iota_{\textbf{1}}
is full and faithful, we find that

\displaystyle[\mathrm{Ran}^{\mathrm{lax}}_{\iota}\lambda]_{A}(x)_{a}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathrm{Ran}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{\iota}\lambda)_{\textbf{1}}(A_{n})\mid X_{a}\subseteq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n},A_{k}\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ran}_{\iota_{\textbf{1}}}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(A_{n})\mid X_{a}\subseteq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n},A_{k}\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ran}_{\iota_{\textbf{1}}}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(B_{n})\mid X_{a}\subseteq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n},\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }B_{k}\in\mathcal{B}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(B_{n})\mid X_{a}\subseteq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n},\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }B_{k}\in\mathcal{B}\right\}.

We will now show that the inequality in the third line is in fact an equality.

First, it is easy to see that for
E\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})
we have that

\mathrm{Ran}_{\iota_{\textbf{1}}}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(E)=\inf\{\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(B)\mid E\subseteq B,B\in\mathcal{B}\},

as this is a usual right Kan extension of order-preserving maps between posets. Let
(A_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be pairwise disjoint in
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
such that
X_{a}\subseteq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}
. Let
\epsilon>0
. For every
n\geq 1
, there exists
A_{n}\subseteq B_{n}
such that

\mathrm{Ran}_{\iota_{\textbf{1}}}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(A_{n})+\frac{\epsilon}{2^{n}}\geq\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(B_{n})\geq\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(\tilde{B}_{n}),

where
\tilde{B}_{1}:=B_{1}
and
\tilde{B}_{n}:=B_{n}\setminus\tilde{B}_{n-1}
for

. Summing over

gives

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ran}_{\iota_{\textbf{1}}}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(A_{n})+\epsilon\geq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(\tilde{B}_{n})\geq\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\textbf{1}}(B_{n})\mid X_{a}\subseteq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n},\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\in\mathcal{B}\right\}.

Taking

and taking the infimum over all such

, yields the claimed equality.

Using Proposition
6.13
, it follows that

\mathrm{Ran}^{\mathrm{lax}}_{\iota}\lambda\circ\iota=R_{l}(\lambda)=\lambda.

This shows that the extension is proper.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Right extensions along
\iota
of strict transformations exist and are proper.

Proof.

Corollary
B.3
states that
\overline{\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}}=\overline{\mu}\mid_{\mathcal{B}}
for all outer measures
\mu
.
This shows that
\overline{\lambda}\circ\iota=\overline{\lambda\circ\iota}
for all
\lambda\in[F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})},G]_{l}^{\Sigma}
. Because
(F_{\mathcal{B}},G,\Sigma)
and
(F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})},G,\Sigma)
satisfy the strictness condition, the result now follows from Corollary
3.8
and Proposition
7.2
.
∎

Theorem 7.4

(Carathédory)
.

Let
\rho
be a premeasure on a premeasurable space
(X,\mathcal{B})
. There exists a measure
\mu
on
(X,\sigma(\mathcal{B}))
, such that
\mu(B)=\rho(B)
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be the measure corresponding to the right extension along
\iota
of the strict transformation
\tau^{\rho}:F_{\mathcal{B}}\to G
. Since the extension is proper, it follows that
\mu(B)=\rho(B)
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
∎

7.2
The left Carathéodory extension

We start by showing that extensions of
\Sigma
-natural colax transformations along
\iota
are objectwise and proper. This is an application of Theorem
5.2
.

Proposition 7.5
.

Left extensions along
\iota
of
\Sigma
-natural colax transformations exist and are objectwise and proper.

Proof.

We want to apply Theorem
5.2
. Therefore we will show that the three conditions of this result are satisfied.

Let
f:A\to B
be a finite partial map. We know from Proposition
6.10
that
Gf
preserves directed joins. This shows that
(G1)
from Theorem
5.2
is satisfied.

Consider a countable set
A
and
x:=(X_{a})_{a\in A}\in F_{\sigma(\mathcal{B})}A
and
b^{1}:=(B^{1}_{a})_{a\in A}
and
b^{2}:=(B^{2}_{a})_{a\in A}
in
F_{\mathcal{B}}A
such that
b^{1}\leq x
and
b^{2}\leq x
. Then
(B^{1}_{a}\cup B^{2}_{a})_{a\in A}
are pairwise disjoint premeasurable subsets and clearly
B^{1}_{a}\cup B^{2}_{a}\subseteq X_{a}
for all
a\in A
. Therefore
\iota_{A}\downarrow x
is directed. This means that condition
(\iota 1)
of Theorem
5.2
holds for
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
.

By Remark
6.8
, we can restrict
\Sigma
to the collection of partial maps of the form
s_{a_{0}}:A\to\textbf{1}
, where
A
is a countable set,
a_{0}
is an element of
A
and the domain of
s_{a_{0}}
is
\{a_{0}\}
.

For such a partial map
s_{a_{0}}:A\to\textbf{1}
, we have that
F_{\mathcal{B}}s_{a}:F_{\mathcal{B}}A\to F_{\mathcal{B}}\textbf{1}
is defined by the assignment

(B_{a})_{a\in A}\mapsto B_{a_{0}}.

Now define
(F_{\mathcal{B}}s_{a_{0}})_{*}:F_{\mathcal{B}}\textbf{1}\to F_{\mathcal{B}}A
by

(F_{\mathcal{B}}s_{a_{0}})_{*}(B)_{a}:=\begin{cases}B\text{ if }a=a_{0}\\
\emptyset\text{ otherwise}\end{cases},

for all
B\in F_{\mathcal{B}}\textbf{1}
and
a\in A
.

It is straightforward to verify that
(F_{\mathcal{B}}s_{a_{0}})_{*}
is left adjoint to
F_{\mathcal{B}}s_{a_{0}}
and these left adjoints satisfy condition
(\iota_{2})
in Theorem
5.2
.

It follows now from Theorem
5.2
that left extensions along
\iota
exist and are objectwise. Since
\iota_{A}
is full and faithful for all countable sets
A
, we know by Lemma
3.1
that the extension is proper.
∎

However, left extensions along
\iota
of
strict
transformations might not exist in general. In other words, the left Carathéodory extension of a premeasure does not always exist, as can be seen from the following counterexample, based on Example 4.20 in
[
8
]
.

Example 7.6
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the algebra of subsets of
\mathbb{Q}
, generated by
\{(a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{Q}\}
. Let
\rho:\mathcal{B}\to[0,\infty]
be the premeasure that takes the value
\infty
for all non-empty subsets in
\mathcal{B}
. Suppose that this premeasure has a left Carathéodory extension
\mu
. Note that since right Caratheodory extensions exist and are proper, we know by Proposition
3.9
that the left Carathédoroy extension has to be proper. For
r>0
, there is a measure
\mu_{r}
on
\mathbb{Q}
that is defined by
\mu_{r}(\{q\})=r
for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
. We clearly have that
\mu_{r}(B)=\rho(B)
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
. By the universal property of left extensions it follows that
\mu\leq\mu_{r}
, i.e. for all
r>0
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\mu(\{q\})\leq r.

This implies that
\mu=0
, which is clearly a contradiction.

Remark 7.7
.

It follows immediately from the
\pi
-
\lambda
theorem (Theorem 1.19 in
[
3
]
), that a
finite
measure is determined by its values on a generating algebra of subsets.
This means that if there exist a proper extension, it has to be unique. By the Carathéodory extension theorem (Theorem
7.4
), we know that such an extension exist.

It follows now that for
finite
premeasures, the right and the left Carathédory extensions exist and are equal.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B})
be a premeasubrable space and let
\mu
be an outer premeasure on
(X,\sigma(\mathcal{B}))
. Then
\mu
is finite if and only if
\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}
is finite. In this case for every
\epsilon>0
and
A\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})
, there exists a
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\mu(A\Delta B)<\epsilon.
Also in this case,

\overline{\mu}(X)=\overline{\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}}(X).

Proof.

If
\mu
is finite then,
\overline{\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}}(X)\leq\overline{\mu}(X)<\infty
and therefore
\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}
is finite.

Suppose now that
\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}
is finite. Define the set

S:=\left\{A\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})\mid\forall\epsilon>0\ \exists B\in\mathcal{B}:\mu(A\Delta B)<\epsilon\right\}

If
A\in S
and
\epsilon>0
, then there exists
B
such that
\mu(A\Delta B)<\epsilon
. Therefore
\mu(A^{c}\Delta B)=\mu(A\Delta B^{c})<\epsilon
. We conclude that
A^{c}\in S
and therefore
S
is closed under complements.

Let
\epsilon>0
. For
A_{1},A_{2}\in S
, there exists
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\mu(A_{1}\Delta B_{1})<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
and
\mu(A_{2}\Delta B_{2})<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

(A_{1}\cup A_{2})\Delta(B_{1}\cup B_{2})\subseteq(A_{1}\Delta B_{1})\cup(A_{2}\Delta B_{2})

\mu((A_{1}\cup A_{2})\Delta(B_{1}\cup B_{2}))\leq\epsilon
. Therefore
S
is closed under finite unions.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
(A_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
pairwise disjoint in
S
. For
N\geq 1

(B_{n})_{n=1}^{N}

\mu(A_{n}\Delta B_{n})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2N}
for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. We can choose
(B_{n})_{n=1}^{N}

Corollary B.2
.

Let
\mu
be an outer measure on
(X,\sigma(\mathcal{B}))
, then
\overline{\mu}(X)=\overline{\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}}(X)
.

Proof.

If
\mu
is finite, then this follows from Theorem
B.1
. If
\mu
is not finite then,
\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}
is also not finite by Theorem
B.1
and the result also follows.
∎

Corollary B.3
.

Let
\mu
be an outer measure on
(X,\sigma(\mathcal{B}))
, then
\overline{\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}}(B)=\overline{\mu}(B)
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Let
B\in\mathcal{B}
. Consider
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}:=\{C\in\mathcal{B}\mid C\subseteq B\}
. Note that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is an algebra on the subset
B
, i.e.
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(B)
is a subalgebra. It can be checked that
\sigma(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\sigma(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}
.
10
10

10

Clearly
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}
and since
\sigma(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}
is a
\sigma
-algebra, we have
\sigma(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}
. For the other inclusion, consider

S:=\{A\subseteq X\mid A\cap B\in\sigma(\mathcal{B^{\prime}})\}.

It is easy to see that
S
is a
\sigma
-algebra containing
\mathcal{B}
. Therefore
\sigma(\mathcal{B})\subseteq S
. Thus for
A\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}
, we have that
A\in S
, which means that
A=A\cap B\in\sigma(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
.
Let
\mu^{\prime}
be the outer measure on
(B,\sigma(\mathcal{B}^{\prime}))
defined by
\mu^{\prime}(C):=\mu(C)
for all
C\in\sigma(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
. Using the formula from Proposition
6.11
, it is now easy to see that
\overline{\mu^{\prime}}(B)=\overline{\mu}(B)
and
\overline{\mu^{\prime}\mid_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}}(B)=\overline{\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}}(B)
. Using Corollary
B.2
, we conclude that
\overline{\mu\mid_{\mathcal{B}}}(B)=\overline{\mu}(B)
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
∎

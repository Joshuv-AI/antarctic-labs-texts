Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of bicommutative algebras Thanks: ♯ Corresponding author
Yuxiu Bai, Yuqun Chen, Zerui Zhang ♯

Abstract
. We first offer a fast method for calculating the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely presented commutative algebra by investigating certain finite set. Then we establish a Gröbner–Shirshov bases theory for bicommutative algebras, and show that every finitely generated bicommutative algebra has a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis. As an application, we show that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated bicommutative algebra is a nonnegative integer.

1.
Introduction

The Gelfand-Kirillov dimension measures the asymptotic rate of growth of algebras. And it has become an important tool in the study of algebras. There are many well-known results on the Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions of algebras. For instance, the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}
over a field is the classical Krull dimension of
\mathcal{A}

[
4
, Theorem 4.5]
, in particular, it is a nonnegative integer.

There exist a lot of important results concerning the Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions of algebras. Bergman’s gap Theorem
[
5
]
shows that there is no algebra with Gelfand-Kirillov dimension strictly between one and two.
Martinez and Zelmanov
[
18
]
studied finitely generated linear Jordan algebras of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension one and showed that the gap theorem holds for Jordan algebras.
Petrogradsky and Shestakov
[
19
]
showed that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a Jordan superalgebra can be an arbitrary number in
\{0\}\cup[1,+\infty]
.
The Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a Leavitt path algebra was investigated by Alahmadi et al. in
[
2
]
.
Qi et al.
[
21
]
proved that no finitely generated locally finite nonsymmetric operad has Gelfand-Kirillov dimension strictly between 1 and 2.
And Bao et al.
[
3
]
classified
k
-linear symmetric operads of low Gelfand-Kirillov dimension. Petrogradsky, Shestakov, Zelmanov
[
20
]
and
Shestakov, Zelmanov
[
22
]
investigated the Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions of a family of infinite-dimensional nil Lie algebras. Drensky and Papistas
[
11
]
showed that, under some natural restrictions, the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of the group of tame automorphisms of relatively free algebras
F_{n}(V)
is equal to the
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of the algebra
F_{n}(V)
.
Centrone
[
9
]
proved a strict relation between the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of the relatively free (graded) algebra of a PI-algebra and its (graded) exponent.
Zhao and Zhang
[
26
]
offered an algorithm for computing the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of certain type of differential difference modules.

Recall that
an algebra
\mathcal{B}
over a field
k
is called
bicommutative
if it satisfies the identities

\displaystyle x(yz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(xz)\ \ \ \ \mbox{(left commutativity)}

\displaystyle(xy)z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xz)y\ \ \ \ \mbox{(right commutativity)}

for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{B}
. We denote by
\mathsf{BC}(X)
the free bicommutative algebra
generated by
X
. In
[
14
]
, Dzhumadil’daev, Ismailov and Tulenbaev constructed a linear basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)
when
X
is finite, which can be easily generalized to the case when
X
is an arbitrary linearly ordered set, see also
[
12
]
.
Drensky and Zhakhayev
[
12
]
proved that every finitely generated bicommutative algebra is weakly noetherian and offered a positive solution to the finite basis problem for varieties of bicommutative algebras over an arbitrary field of any characteristic. Classifications on low dimensional bicommutative algebras also attracts peoples’ attraction, and it is proved that there are only three pairwise nonisomorphic two-dimensional bicommutative algebras over an algebraically closed field by Drensky
[
10
]
.
Kaygorodov, Páez-Guillán and Voronin
[
16
]
offered the algebraic and geometric classification of 4-dimensional nilpotent
bicommutative algebras.
Dzhumadil’daev and Ismailov
[
13
]
established criterions for elements of a free bicommutative algebra to be Lie or Jordan.

We continue to study bicommutative algebras and show that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of an arbitrary finitely generated bicommutative algebra is a nonnegative integer.
Moreover, we show that, in order to calculate the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated bicommutative algebra, it suffices to investigate a finite specific set instead of constructing a possibly infinite linear basis for the bicommutative algebra under consideration. The method we applied for the proof consists of using Gröbner bases and Gröbner–Shirshov bases, as introduced by Buchberger
[
7
,
8
]
and Shirshov
[
24
,
25
]
.
For more history on Gröbner bases and Gröbner–Shirshov bases theory, we refer to
[
6
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the Gröbner bases theory for commutative algebras and offer a fast method for calculating the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated commutative algebra. In Section
3
, we establish a Gröbner–Shirshov bases theory for bicommutative algebras and show that every finitely generated bicommutative algebra has a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis. In Section
4
, we apply the method of Gröbner–Shirshov bases to prove that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated bicommutative algebra is a nonnegative integer. Moreover, in Theorem
4.2
we offer a fast method for calculating the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely presented bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{B}
by investigating certain finite set in
\mathcal{B}
.

2.
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of commutative algebras

Our aim in this section is to offer a new proof for the known fact that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated commutative algebra is a nonnegative integer by using the Gröbner bases theory for commutative algebras.

Let
(X,\prec)
be a fixed well-ordered set and let
\mathbb{N}
(
resp.

\mathbb{N^{+}}
) be the set of all nonnegative integers (
resp.
all positive integers). Furthermore, we denote by
[X]
(
resp.

[X]^{+}
) the free commutative monoid (
resp.
free commutative semigroup) generated by
X
, that is

[X]=\{x_{i_{1}}^{n_{i_{1}}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{n_{i_{t}}}\mid x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{t}}\in X,\ x_{i_{1}}\prec\cdots\prec x_{i_{t}},t,n_{i_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,n_{i_{t}}\in\mathbb{N}\}

and
[X]^{+}=[X]\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
, where
x^{0}=\varepsilon
for every
{x\in X}
and
\varepsilon
is the unit of the monoid
[X]
.
When we write a monomial
u=x_{i_{1}}^{n_{i_{1}}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{n_{i_{t}}}
, we always assume that all the letters
x_{i_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,x_{i_{t}}
are pairwise different unless otherwise specified.
Finally, we denote by
k[X]
the free commutative algebra generated by
X
, and for an arbitrary set
Y
, we denote by
\#Y
the cardinality of
Y
.

Now we recall the Gröbner bases theory for commutative algebras, for more details, see
[
7
,
1
]
. For every monomial
u=x_{i_{1}}^{n_{i_{1}}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{n_{i_{t}}}\in[X]
, we define the
length
of
u
, denoted by
\ell(u)
, to be
\sum_{j=1}^{t}n_{i_{j}}
.
Let
<
be a monomial order on
[X]
, that is,
<
is a well order and for all monomials
u,v,w\in[X]
, we have
u<v\Rightarrow wu<wv
.
For every nonzero polynomial
f=\sum\alpha_{i}u_{i}
in
k[X]
, where each
0\neq\alpha_{i}
lies in
k
, each
u_{i}
lies in
[X]
and
u_{1}>u_{2}>\cdots
,
we call
u_{1}
the
leading monomial
of
f
and denote it by
\bar{f}
.
We call
f
a
monic polynomial
if
\alpha_{1}=1
. And a set
S\subseteq k[X]
is called a
monic set
if every polynomial in
S
is monic. By convention, the empty set
S
is a monic set but
\{0\}
is not. We denote by
\mathsf{Id}(S)
the ideal of
k[X]
generated by
S
and define
\overline{S}=\{\overline{f}\mid f\in S\}
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
k[X]
. A monic set
S
is called a
Gröbner basis
(in
k[X]
) for the quotient algebra
k[X]/I
if
\mathsf{Id}(S)=I
and for every nonzero polynomial
f\in I
,
we have
{\overline{f}=\overline{sv}}
for some
s\in S
,
v\in[X]
. By the Gröbner bases theory for commuative algebras
[
7
,
1
]
, if
S
is a Gröbner basis for the algebra
k[X|S]:=k[X]/\mathsf{Id}(S)
, then the set

\mathsf{Irr}(S)=[X]\setminus\{w\in[X]\mid w=\overline{sv}\ \mbox{for some}\ s\in S,v\in[X]\}

forms a linear basis for the algebra
k[X|S]
. In particular, if
S
is empty, then
S
is a Gröbner basis for
k[X|S]=k[X]
and we have
\mathsf{Irr}(S)=[X]
.

Now we recall the definition of the Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions for associative algebras. Let
V
be a finite dimensional subspace of an associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
with a linear basis
\{a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{r}\}
. Define
V^{1}=V
, and for
n\geq 1
, denote by
V^{n}
the subspace of
\mathcal{A}
spanned by all monomials of the form
a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}
, where
a_{i_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{i_{n}}\in\{a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{r}\}
. Finally, define
V^{\leq n}=V^{1}+\cdots+V^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
17
]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an associative algebra over a field
k
. Then the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of
\mathcal{A}
is defined to be

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathcal{A})=\sup\limits_{V}\overline{\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}}\log_{n}\mathsf{dim}(V^{\leq n}),

where the supremum is taken over all finite dimensional subspaces of
\mathcal{A}
.

It is well-known that if
\mathcal{A}
is an associative algebra generated by a finite set
X
, then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathcal{A})=\overline{{\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}}}\log_{n}\mathsf{dim}((kX)^{\leq n}).

In particular, for a commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}=k[X|S]
, where
X
is a finite set and
S
is a Gröbner basis for
\mathcal{A}
, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathcal{A})=\overline{{\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}}}\log_{n}\#\{u\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\mid\ell(u)\leq n\}.

Obviously, for all commutative algebras, their Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions have very close relations with their Gröbner bases. Now we have a close look at the case of finitely generated commutative algebras. It is well-known that every finitely generated commutative algebra has a finite Gröbner basis by using Hilbert’s Theorem, see also
[
1
, Sect. 1.6]
.

We recall that for all monomials
u,v\in[X]
, the monomial
u
is called a
multiple
of
v
, denoted by
v|u
, if
u=vw
in
[X]
for some monomial
w\in[X]
.
Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set and let
S
be a finite Gröbner basis for
k[X|S]
.
Define

\displaystyle C_{S}=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if }S=\emptyset\\
\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\bar{s}|x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{r}^{n},s\in S\},&\mbox{if }S\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

In particular, if
S=\{\varepsilon\}
, then we have
C_{S}=0
. For every
u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in[X]
, define

\mathsf{Sdeg}(u)=\#\{i\mid n_{i}>C_{S},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}.

It follows immediately that we have

\mathsf{Sdeg}(\varepsilon)=0.

Finally, for an arbitrary nonempty subset
B\subseteq\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, we define

\mathsf{Sdeg}(B)=\max\{\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)\mid\mu\in B\}

and define

\mathsf{Sdeg}(\emptyset)=0.

We shall soon see that
\mathsf{Sdeg}(B)
is always finite if
X
is a finite set.

Now we offer a characterization for the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated commutative algebra. In particular, as far as the authors know, it offers a new alternative proof for the fact that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated commutative algebra is a nonnegative integer.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a nonempty finite set and let
S
be a finite Gröbner basis for
k[X|S]
. Then we have
\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S))
.

Proof.

Assume that
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
. Define
m=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S))
.
If
m=0
, then we have
\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq\{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\mid n_{i}\leq C_{S},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}
. It follows that
\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=0
.

Now we suppose
m\geq 1
. Since
m=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S))
, for every
u\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq[X]
, we obtain that
\mathsf{Sdeg}(u)\leq m
. Therefore, we have

\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq\{u\in[X]\mid\mathsf{Sdeg}(u)\leq m\}.

Moreover, there exists
u\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
such that
\mathsf{Sdeg}(u)=m
. Without loss of generality, we assume
u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}
, where each
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}>C_{S}
.
For every
w\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
and for every monomial
v\in[X]
such that
v|w
, it is clear that
v
also lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
. So we deduce that

x_{1}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots x_{m}^{C_{S}+1}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S).

Now we show that

\mathsf{Irr}(S)\supseteq\{x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{p_{m}}\mid p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\in\mathbb{N}\}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}.

It suffices to show that
x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{m}^{n}
belongs to
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
for every
n>C_{S}
. Suppose for the contrary that

{x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{m}^{n}\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S)}
for some integer
n>C_{S}+1
.
Then we define

n_{0}=\mathsf{min}\{n\mid x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{m}^{n}\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S)\}.

Clearly, we have

n_{0}>C_{S}+1

and
x_{1}^{n_{0}-1}\cdots x_{m}^{n_{0}-1}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
Therefore, there exists some element
s\in S
such
that we have
\bar{s}|x_{1}^{n_{0}}\cdots x_{m}^{n_{0}}
while
\bar{s}\nmid x_{1}^{n_{0}-1}\cdots x_{m}^{n_{0}-1}
.
By the definition of
C_{S}
, we deduce
C_{S}\geq n_{0}
, which contradicts with the fact that
n_{0}>C_{S}+1
.

Therefore we have

\{x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{p_{m}}\mid p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\in\mathbb{N}\}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}\subseteq\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq\{u\in[X]\mid\mathsf{Sdeg}(u)\leq m\}.

By straightforward calculations, we obtain
\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=m
immediately.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set and
S
a finite Gröbner basis for
k[X|S]
. Then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\{u\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S),n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}).

Proof.

Define

m^{\prime}=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\{u\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S),n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\})

and

m=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S)).

Clearly we have
m^{\prime}\leq m
. By Lemma
2.2
, it suffices to show
m\leq m^{\prime}
.

If
m^{\prime}=0
, then
\{u\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus\{\varepsilon\},n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},1\leq i\leq r\}
is an empty set. It follows that we have
\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq\{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\mid n_{i}\leq C_{S},1\leq i\leq r\}
. Thus we have
m=0=m^{\prime}
.

Suppose
0<m^{\prime}
. Then we have
m\geq m^{\prime}>0
. So there exists a monomial
u\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
with
\mathsf{Sdeg}(u)=m>0
. Thus we may assume

u=y_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{t_{m}}u^{\prime},

where
u^{\prime}\in[X]
,
\mathsf{Sdeg}(u^{\prime})=0
,
t_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,t_{m}\geq C_{S}+1
,
y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{m}\in X
and
y_{1}<\cdots<y_{m}
.
Then
y_{1}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots y_{m}^{C_{S}+1}
also lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, in other words, we have
m^{\prime}\geq\mathsf{Sdeg}(u)=m
.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=m=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\{u\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S),n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}).

The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem
2.3
offers a fast method for calculating the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely presented commutative algebra by investigating the finite set

\{u\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}},n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}

rather than
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
As a special case, if
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
and
S=\emptyset
, then we have
C_{S}=0
,
x_{1}^{1}\cdots x_{r}^{1}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, and thus
\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X])=r
.

Remark 2.4
.

Theorem
2.3
offers an algorithm for computing the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension (or equivalently, Krull dimension
[
4
, Proposition 5.1]
) of a finitely presented commutative algebra. More precisely, let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite
set and let
T
be a finite subset of
k[X]
. Then by applying Buchberger’s algorithm
[
1
, Algotithm 1.7.1]
, we can find a finite Gröbner basis
S
for the algebra
\mathcal{A}:=k[X|T]
. Then we obtain the nonnegative integer
C_{S}

and the set
B:=\{u\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S),n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}
.
Since
B
and
S
are finite sets, there exists an algorithm for calculating
\mathsf{Sdeg}(B)
, which is exactly
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathcal{A})
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,x_{6}\}
and
S=\{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{3},x_{3}x_{4}\}
.
Then
C_{S}=3
.
For every subset
\{y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{5}\}\subseteq X
, we have
y_{1}^{4}\cdots y_{5}^{4}\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S)
. Since
x_{2}^{4}x_{3}^{4}x_{5}^{4}x_{6}^{4}
lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
Thus we obtain
\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=4
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
S
be a nonempty subset of
[X]^{+}
and
u\in[X]^{+}
. Suppose that
\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=p\geq 1
. If there exists a subset
\{y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{p}\}\subseteq X
such that some monomial in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
of the form
y_{1}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots y_{p}^{C_{S}+1}
lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{u\})
,
where
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
for all distinct
i,j\leq p
,
then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S\cup\{u\}])=\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S]);

Otherwise, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S\cup\{u\}])=\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])-1.

Proof.

Obviously, every nonempty subset of
[X]^{+}
is a Gröbner basis in
k[X]
, and thus
S
is a Gröbner basis for
k[X|S]
.
By assumption, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\mid n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},1\leq i\leq r\})=p,\mbox{ where }p\geq 1.

If there exists a subset
\{y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{p}\}\subseteq X
, such that some monomial in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
of the form
y_{1}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots y_{p}^{C_{S}+1}
lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{u\})
,
where
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
for all distinct
i,j\leq p
,
then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S\cup\{u\}])=p=\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S]).

Now we assume that for every subset
\{y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{p}\}\subseteq X
, every monomial in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
of the form
y_{1}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots y_{p}^{C_{S}+1}
does not belong to the set
\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{u\})
,
then we have
u|y_{1}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots y_{p}^{C_{S}+1}
.
If
p=1
, then
\{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{u\})\mid n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}=\{\varepsilon\}
, and thus

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S\cup\{u\}])=0=p-1=\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])-1.

If
p>1
, then without loss of generality, we may assume
x_{i_{1}}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots x_{i_{p}}^{C_{S}+1}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)

and
x_{i_{1}}|u
. It follows that
x_{i_{2}}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots x_{i_{p}}^{C_{S}+1}

lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{u\})
. Therefore we obtain that

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S\cup\{u\}])=p-1=\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])-1.

The proof is completed.
∎

The following proposition offers a rough estimation of the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely presented commutative algebra.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set and let
S=\{f_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,f_{t}\}
be a finite Gröbner basis for
k[X|S]
.
Then the following hold:

(i)
If
1\leq t\leq r
and
\varepsilon\notin S
, then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])\in\{r-t,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r-1\};

(ii)
If
t>r
, then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])\in\{0,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r-1\}.

Moreover, if
r>1
and
t<r
, then
\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=0\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon\in S
.

Proof.

(i)
We use induction on
t
.
If
t=1
, then we may assume that
\bar{f}_{1}=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}
, where
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n_{1}>0
.
Thus we have
C_{S}=\max\{n_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,n_{r}\}
. Clearly we have

x_{2}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{C_{S}+1}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\mbox{ and }x_{1}^{C_{S}+1}x_{2}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{C_{S}+1}\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S).

Therefore by Theorem
2.3
, we
obtain

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\{u\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S),n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\})=r-1.

Now we assume
t\geq 2
.
Obviously,
\{\overline{f_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,\overline{f_{t-1}}\}
is a Gröbner basis in
k[X]
and
\mathsf{Irr}(S)=\mathsf{Irr}(\overline{S})
.
By induction hypothesis, we have
r-t+1\geq 1
and

p:=\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|\{\overline{f_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,\overline{f_{t-1}}\}])\in\{r-t+1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r-1\}.

By Corollary
2.6
, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|\overline{S}])=\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])\in\{p,p-1\}\subseteq\{r-t,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r-1\}.

(ii)

Since
x_{1}^{C_{S}+1}x_{2}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{C_{S}+1}\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, by Theorem
2.3
, the result follows.
∎

Example 2.8
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set.
Given a positive integer
t
, for every integer
p
such that
1\leq p\leq\min\{t,r\}
, we define
m=t-p
and define

S=\{x_{i}^{4m+1}\mid 1\leq i\leq p\}\cup\{x_{1}^{j}x_{r}^{3m-j}\mid m\leq j\leq 2m-1\}.

Note that if
t=p
, then we have
S=\{x_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq t\}
.
Clearly
S
is a Gröbner basis for
k[X|S]
with
\#S=t
and we have
C_{S}=4m+1
. Let

B:=\{u\in[X]\mid u=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}},n_{i}\in\{0,C_{S}+1\},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}.

If
p<r
, then we have

x_{p+1}^{C_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{C_{S}+1}\in B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S),

and for every monomial
u\in B
with
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)=r-p+1
,
u
does not lie in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
Thus we obtain

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=r-p\in\{r-t,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r-1\}.

If
p=r
, then we have

B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S)=\{\varepsilon\}.

Thus

\mathsf{GKdim}(k[X|S])=r-p=0.

It follows that if
S
is a Gröbner basis consisting of
t\geq 1
elements and
\varepsilon\notin S
, then the possible Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of the algebra
k[X|S]
can be an arbitrary integer between
\max\{r-t,0\}
and
r-1
.

Remark 2.9
.

In Proposition
2.7
, a Gröbner basis
S
with smaller cardinality might improve the estimation. So in Proposition
2.7
, we may assume
S
to be a minimal Gröbner basis. Recall that a Gröbner basis
S
is called
minimal
if for all
f,g\in S
,
\bar{f}
is not a multiple of
\bar{g}
. Moreover, when the order is fixed, the cardinality of a minimal Gröbner basis is also minimum among all the possible cardinalities of Gröbner bases for

.
If we use another monomial order
\prec
on
[X]
, then we can find a minimal Gröbner basis
T
for

with respect to the order
\prec
and probably we have
\#T\neq\#S
.

3.
Gröbner–Shirshov bases theory for bicommutative algebras

Our aim in this section is to
establish a Gröbner–Shirshov bases theory for bicommutative algebras and show that every finitely generated bicommutative algebra has a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis.

3.1.
Composition-Diamond lemma for bicommutative algebras

We first recall that
terms
over
X
are “words with brackets”; they are
defined by the following induction:

(i)
Every element of
X
is a term over
X
;

(ii)
If
\mu,\nu
are terms over
X
, then so is
(\mu\nu)
.

We denote by
X^{(*)}
the set of all terms over
X
.

Let
kX^{(*)}
be the free nonassociative algebra generated by
X
. Then clearly
X^{(*)}
forms a linear basis for
kX^{(*)}
.
For every
\mu
in
X^{(*)}
, we define the
content

\mathsf{cont}(\mu)
of
\mu
to be the set of all letters that appear in
\mu
and define the
length

\ell(\mu)
of
\mu
to be the number of letters (with repetition) that appear in
\mu
. For instance, for
\mu=((x_{1}x_{1})x_{2})\in X^{(\ast)}
, we have
\mathsf{cont}(\mu)=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
and
\ell(\mu)=3
.

For all
a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{n}
(
n\geq 1
) in a bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{B}
, we define

[a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{n}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}=(\cdots(((a_{1}a_{2})a_{3})\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})\ \ \mbox{(left-normed bracketing),}

and define

[a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{n}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}}=(a_{1}(a_{2}\cdots(a_{n-2}(a_{n-1}a_{n}))\cdots))\ \ \mbox{(right-normed bracketing).}

In particular, we have
[a_{1}]_{\mathsf{{}_{L}}}=[a_{1}]_{\mathsf{{}_{R}}}=a_{1}
and
[a_{1},a_{2}]_{\mathsf{{}_{L}}}=[a_{1},a_{2}]_{\mathsf{{}_{R}}}=(a_{1}a_{2})
.
For all elements
a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{p},b_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,b_{q}\in\mathcal{B}
, where
p,q\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we define

[a_{1}\cdots a_{p};b_{1}\cdots b_{q}]=[a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{p-1},[a_{p},b_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,b_{q}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}}.

Now we recall some known identities in bicommutative algebras established in
[
15
,
14
]
, see also
[
23
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
15
,
14
]

For all
a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{p},b_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,b_{q},c_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,c_{m},d_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,d_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, for all permutations
\sigma\in S_{p},\delta\in S_{q}
, where
p,q,m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and
S_{p},S_{q}
are symmetric groups of orders
p,q
respectively, we have

(i)

((a_{1}(a_{2}a_{3}))a_{4})=(a_{1}((a_{2}a_{3})a_{4}))
,

(ii)

[a_{1}\cdots a_{p};b_{1}\cdots b_{q}]=[a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{p-1},[a_{p},b_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,b_{q}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}}=[{[a_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,a_{p},b_{1}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}},b_{2},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,b_{q}}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}
. In particular, we have
[a_{1}\cdots a_{p};b_{1}\cdots b_{q}]=[a_{\sigma(1)}\cdots a_{\sigma(p)};b_{\delta(1)}\cdots b_{\delta(q)}]
.

(iii)

([a_{1}\cdots a_{p};b_{1}\cdots b_{q}][c_{1}\cdots c_{m};d_{1}\cdots d_{n}])=[a_{1}\cdots a_{p}c_{1}\cdots c_{m};b_{1}\cdots b_{q}d_{1}\cdots d_{n}]
.

Let
\mathsf{BC}(X)
be the free bicommutative algebra
generated by a well-ordered set
X
.
Note that by Lemma
3.1
, the ordering of
a_{i}
and that of
b_{j}
do not make difference in the monomial
[a_{1}\cdots a_{p};b_{1}\cdots b_{q}]
, and what is important is whether they are on the “left hand side” or on the “right hand side”.
In particular, for all
p,q\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, for all letters
z_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,z_{p},y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{q}\in X
, when we write an element of the form
[z_{1}\cdots z_{p};y_{1}\cdots y_{q}]\in\mathsf{BC}(X)
, it is not necessary to assume
z_{1}\leq\cdots\leq z_{p}
or
y_{1}\leq\cdots\leq y_{q}
.

Moreover, for all
u=z_{1}\cdots z_{p},v=y_{1}\cdots y_{q}\in[X]^{+}
, we define

[u;v]=[z_{1}\cdots z_{p};y_{1}\cdots y_{q}],

and

\mathsf{NF}(X)=\{[u;v]\mid u,v\in[X]^{+}\}\cup X.

Then by
[
14
]
,
\mathsf{NF}(X)
forms a linear basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)
.
For the convenience of the readers, we list the multiplication table below. For all
z,y\in X
and
[u;v],[u^{\prime};v^{\prime}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X
, we have

(3.1)

(zy)=[z;y];\ (z[u;v])=[zu;v];\ ([u;v]z)=[u;vz];\ ([u;v][u^{\prime};v^{\prime}])=[uu^{\prime};vv^{\prime}].

In light of the multiplication table (
3.1
) above, we can generalize the notion of multiple in
\mathsf{NF}(X)
. More precisely, for all
\mu,\nu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
, we call
\nu
a
multiple
of
\mu
, denoted by
\mu|\nu
, if one of the followings holds:

(i)

\mu=x\in X
and
x\in\mathsf{cont}(\nu)
;

(ii)

\mu=[u;v]
,
\nu=[u^{\prime};v^{\prime}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
for some
u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in[X]^{+}
, and we have
u|u^{\prime}
,
v|v^{\prime}
in
[X]^{+}
.

Let
<
be a well order on
\mathsf{NF}(X)
.
For every nonzero polynomial
f=\sum\alpha_{i}\mu_{i}
in
\mathsf{BC}(X)
, where each
0\neq\alpha_{i}
lies in
k
, each
\mu_{i}
lies in
\mathsf{NF}(X)
and
\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\cdots
,
we call
\mu_{1}
the
leading monomial
of
f
, denoted by
\bar{f}
; and call
\alpha_{1}
the
leading coefficient
of
f
, denoted by
\mathsf{lc}(f)
.
We call
f
a
monic polynomial
if
\mathsf{lc}(f)=1
. Finally, a set
S\subseteq\mathsf{BC}(X)
is called a
monic set
if every polynomial in
S
is monic. By convention, the empty set
S
is a monic set but
\{0\}
is not.

A well order
<
on
\mathsf{NF}(X)
is called a
monomial order
if for all monomials
\mu,\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
, we have
\mu<\nu\Rightarrow\overline{(\mu\nu^{\prime})}<\overline{(\nu\nu^{\prime})}\mbox{ and }\overline{(\nu^{\prime}\mu)}<\overline{(\nu^{\prime}\nu)}
.
Here we offer two specific examples of monomial order on
\mathsf{NF}(X)
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\prec
be a monomial order on
[X]^{+}
.
For every
\mu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
, we define

\mathsf{wt}(\mu)=\begin{cases}(1,1,x),&\text{if}\ \mu=x\in X,\\
(\ell(u)+\ell(v),\ell(u),u,v)&\text{if}\ \mu=[u;v]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X.\end{cases}

For all
\mu,\nu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
, we define

\mu<\nu\text{ if and only if }\mathsf{wt}(\mu)<\mathsf{wt}(\nu)\text{ lexicographically},

where the elements in
[X]^{+}
are compared by
\prec
. Then clearly
<
is a monomial order on
\mathsf{NF}(X)
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\prec
be a monomial order on
[X]^{+}
. For every
\mu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
, we define

\mathsf{wt}(\mu)=\begin{cases}(x,x,x),&\text{if}\ \mu=x\in X,\\
(uv,u,v)&\text{if}\ \mu=[u;v]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X,\end{cases}

where
uv
is a commutative word in
[X]^{+}
.
For all
\mu,\nu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
, we define

\mu<\nu\text{ if and only if }\mathsf{wt}(\mu)<\mathsf{wt}(\nu)\text{ lexicographically},

where the elements in
[X]^{+}
are compared by
\prec
. Then clearly
<
is a monomial order on
\mathsf{NF}(X)
.

From now on, let
<
be a fixed monomial order on
\mathsf{NF}(X)
and let
S
be a monic subset of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
. Denote by
\mathsf{Id}(S)
the ideal of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
generated by
S
. For every
s\in S
, we call
s
an
S
-polynomial
, and for every
S
-polynomial
h
, for every
\mu
in
\mathsf{NF}(X)
, both
(\mu h)
and
(h\mu)
are called
S
-polynomials
.
Obviously every element in
\mathsf{Id}(S)
can be written as a linear combination of
S
-polynomials.
Among
S
-polynomials, we introduce some special polynomials in
\mathsf{Id}(S)
, which will form a linear generating set of
\mathsf{Id}(S)
under some conditions.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
S
be a monic set in
\mathsf{BC}(X)
. For every
s\in S
,

(i)
the polynomial
s
is called a
normal
S
-polynomial
;

(ii)
if
\ell(\bar{s})\geq 2
, then for all
z_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,z_{p},y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{q}\in X
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, we call
[z_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,z_{p},[s,y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{q}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}}
a
normal
S
-polynomial
.

The following lemma shows that the set:

\mathsf{Irr}(S):=\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus\{\mu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\mid\mu=\overline{h},h\mbox{ is a normal }S\mbox{-polynomial}\}

is a linear generating set of the bicommutative algebra
\mathsf{BC}(X|S):=\mathsf{BC}(X)/\mathsf{Id}(S)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For every
f\in\mathsf{BC}(X)
, we have

f=\sum_{\mu_{i}\leq\bar{f}}\alpha_{i}\mu_{i}+\sum_{\overline{h_{j}}\leq\bar{f}}\beta_{j}h_{j},

where each
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\in k
,
\mu_{i}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
and
h_{j}
is
a normal
S
-polynomial. In particular, it follows that
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
is a set of linear generators of the
algebra
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
.

Proof.

The result follows by induction on
\bar{f}
.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
. A monic set
S
is called a
Gröbner-Shirshov basis
(in
\mathsf{BC}(X)
) for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/I
, if
\mathsf{Id}(S)=I
and for every nonzero polynomial

, we have
\overline{f}=\overline{h}
for some normal
S
-polynomial

.

We shall see that, if

is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
, then
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
forms a linear basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
. In particular, if
S
is an empty set, then
S
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
and we have
\mathsf{Irr}(S)=\mathsf{NF}(X)
. But the conditions in Definition
3.7
is not tractable in practice. Therefore, we shall introduce certain special polynomials (hereafter called compositions) in

, such that if these polynomials satisfy some easily managed conditions, then

forms a Gröbner–Shirshov basis.

Before going there, we introduce the notion of least common multiple in
\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X
.
For all

, we denote by
\mathsf{lcm}(u,v)
the least common multiple of

and

in
[X]
.
For all

of
\mu
and

.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
S
be a monic subset of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
. For all

of

(i)

;

Lemma 3.8

Let

\mathsf{BC}(X)
such that all the left multiplication compositions, right multiplication compositions and multiplication compositions of

(i)
Every
S

can be written as a linear combination of normal

-polynomials whose leading monomials are
\leq\overline{h}
.

(ii)

If

, where

,
\ell(\overline{s})\geq 2
,

, then we have
h-[z_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,z_{p-1},[z_{p},s,y_{1},y_{2},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{q}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}}\equiv 0\ \mod(S,\overline{h})

(i)
Since every element in

(resp.

Assume that

S
-polynomial.
We use induction on

.
Suppose that
\ell(\mu)=1
and
\mu=x\in X

If
\ell(\bar{h})=1

\ell(\bar{s})=1

and
(h\mu)=(sx)
. Since all left multiplication compositions and right multiplication compositions of

\ell(\bar{h})>1

h=[z_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,z_{p},[s,y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{q}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}},

where
z_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,z_{p},y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{q}\in X
and
p,q\in\mathbb{N}

Now we are ready to prove the Composition-Diamond lemma for bicommutative algebras.

Theorem 3.10
.

(

)

Let
S
be a monic subset of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
. Let
\mathsf{Id}(S)
be the ideal of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
generated by
S
. Then the followings are equivalent.

(i)
All the compositions of
S
are trivial.

(ii)
The set
S
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/\mathsf{Id}(S)
, that is, for every nonzero polynomial
f\in\mathsf{Id}(S)
, we have
\overline{f}=\overline{h}
for some normal
S
-polynomial
h
.

(iii)
The set
\mathsf{Irr}(S)=\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus\{\mu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\mid\mu=\overline{h},h\mbox{ is a normal }S\mbox{-polynomial}\}
forms a linear basis of the algebra
\mathsf{BC}(X|S):=\mathsf{BC}(X)/\mathsf{Id}(S)
.

Proof.

(i)

\Rightarrow

(ii)
For every nonzero element
f
in
\mathsf{Id}(S)
, by Lemma
3.8
(i)
, we may assume that
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}h_{i}
,
where each

\alpha_{i}\in k,\alpha_{i}\neq 0
and each

is a normal
S
-polynomial.
Define
\mu_{i}=\overline{h_{i}}
and assume that
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\cdots=\mu_{{}_{l}}>\mu_{l+1}\geq\cdots.

.
If

, then there is nothing to prove.

, then we have

and

where each
\overline{h_{j}^{\prime}}<\mu_{1}
by Lemma
3.9
.
Claim
(ii)
follows by induction hypothesis.

(ii)

\Rightarrow

(iii)
By Lemma
3.5
,

\mathsf{Irr}(S)
generates

\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
as a vector space. Suppose that
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\mu_{i}=0
in
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
,
where each
\alpha_{i}\in k\setminus\{0\}
and each

\overline{\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\mu_{i}}=\mu_{1}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
,
which contradicts with

(iii)

\Rightarrow

(i)
Every composition of
S

is an element in
\mathsf{Id}(S)
. By Lemma
3.5
and
(iii)

we obtain

.
∎

3.2.
Finite Gröbner-Shirshov bases

Our aim in this subsection is to show that every finitely generated bicommutative algebra has a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
S
be a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
. Then we call
S
a
minimal Gröbner–Shirshov basis
, if for every
s\in S
, we have
\overline{s}\in\mathsf{Irr}(S\setminus\{s\})
.

The following lemma is a general fact for various kind of algebras, but we still offer a proof for the convenience of the readers.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
and
S
a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/I
. For every
T\subseteq S
,
if
\mathsf{Irr}(T)=\mathsf{Irr}(S)
then
T
is also a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/I
.

Proof.

For every
f\in I
, since
\mathsf{Irr}(T)=\mathsf{Irr}(S)
and
S
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/I
, we have
\overline{f}=\overline{h}=\overline{h^{\prime}}
for some normal
S
-polynomial
h
and for some normal
T
-polynomial
h^{\prime}
. So we obtain
f_{1}=f-\mathsf{lc}(f)h^{\prime}\in I
and
\overline{f_{1}}<\overline{f}
. By induction on
\overline{f}
, we deduce that
f
can be written as a linear combination of normal
T
-polynomials, in particular, we have
f\in\mathsf{Id}(T)
. This shows that
I=\mathsf{Id}(T)
. So the result follows from Theorem
3.10
.
∎

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathsf{BC}(X)
. Then the set
\{f\in I\mid\mathsf{lc}(f)=1\}
forms a Gröbner-Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/I
, so Gröbner-Shirshov bases always exist. Now we show that minimal Gröbner–Shirshov bases always exist.

.

S
be a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/\mathsf{Id}(S)

such that

is a minimal Gröbner–Shirshov basis for the algebra
\mathsf{BC}(X)/\mathsf{Id}(S)

Without loss of generality, assume that

is a nonempty set.
For each
\mu\in\overline{S}:=\{\overline{s}\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\mid s\in S\}
, we fix an arbitrary polynomial

in
S

\overline{f_{\mu}}=\mu
. Define

Then it is clear that

and
\mathsf{Irr}(S^{\prime})=\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
By Lemma
3.12
,
S^{\prime}
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X)/\mathsf{Id}(S)
.
Define

Then we have

Let

Lemma 3.14
.

Let

∎

Theorem 3.15
.

and

to be normal

-polynomials. And in our way, there is also some disadvantage. For example, let

and

. Then one naturally expects

into

in its appearance in

[z_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,z_{m-1},[z_{m},f,y_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,y_{n}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{L}}}}]{{}_{{}_{\mathsf{R}}}}

4.
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of bicommutative algebras

Our aim in this section is to prove the main result of this article, namely, the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of an arbitrary finitely generated bicommutative algebra is a nonnegative integer. The method we applied here is the Gröbner-Shirshov bases theory for bicommutative algebras, which is established in Section
3.1
.
Moreover, we show that, in order to calculate the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated bicommutative algebra, it suffices to investigate some property of a finite set instead of constructing a possibly infinite linear basis for the bicommutative algebra under consideration.

We begin with some notation. Let
V,V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be vector subspaces of a bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{B}
. We define

(V_{1}V_{2})=\mathsf{span}_{k}\{(xy)\mid x\in V_{1},y\in V_{2}\}.

Then we define
V^{1}=V
and
V^{n}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n-1}(V^{i}V^{n-i})
for every
n\geq 2
. Finally, we define

V^{\leq n}=V^{1}+V^{2}+\mathrel{\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt{\cdot}{\cdot}{\cdot}\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt}+V^{n}.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a bicommutative algebra. Then the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of
\mathcal{B}
is defined to be

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathcal{B})=\sup\limits_{V}\overline{\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}}\log_{n}\mathsf{dim}(V^{\leq n}),

where the supremum is taken over all finite dimensional subspaces of
\mathcal{B}
.
It follows immediately that, for a bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{B}=\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
, where
X
is a finite set and
S
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathcal{B})=\overline{{\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}}}\log_{n}\#\{\mu\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\mid\ell(\mu)\leq n\}.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set and let
S
be a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
.
Define

\displaystyle N_{S}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }S=\emptyset\\
\mathsf{min}\{n\mid\bar{s}|[x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{r}^{n};x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{r}^{n}],s\in S\},&\mbox{if }S\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

For every
x\in X
, we define

\mathsf{Sdeg}(x)=0,

and for every monomial
\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X
,
we define

\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)=\#\{i\mid n_{i}>N_{S},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}+\#\{i\mid m_{i}>N_{S},i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}.

Finally, for an arbitrary nonempty set
B\subseteq\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, we define

\mathsf{Sdeg}(B)=\max\{\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)\mid\mu\in B\}

and define

\mathsf{Sdeg}(\emptyset)=0.

We shall soon see that
\mathsf{Sdeg}(B)
is always finite if
X
is a finite set.
Now we investigate the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated bicommutative algebra.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a nonempty finite set and let
S
be a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
. Then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S))
.

Proof.

Assume
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
.
Define
M=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S))
.
If
M=0
, then we have
\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq X\cup\{[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid n_{i},m_{j}\leq N_{S},1\leq i,j\leq r\}
. It follows that
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=0=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X)
.

Now we suppose
M\geq 1
. Since
M=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S))
, for every
\mu\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X\subseteq\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X
, we have
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)\leq M
.
Therefore, we have

\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq\{\mu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\mid\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)\leq M\}.

It follows immediately that we have

(4.1)

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))\leq M.

Moreover, since
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X)=M
, there exists some monomial
\mu\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X
such that
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)=M
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]
, where
n_{i_{1}},\dots,n_{i_{p}},m_{j_{1}},\dots,m_{j_{q}}>N_{S}
,

p+q=M
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{l},m_{l^{\prime}}\in\{0,1\}
for all
l\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\setminus\{i_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,i_{p}\}
and
l^{\prime}\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\setminus\{j_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,j_{q}\}
.
There are three cases to consider.

If
q=0
, then we have
p=M
. Without loss of generality, we may assume

\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}],

where
{n_{1},\dots,n_{{}_{M}}>N_{S}}
, and there exists some integer
t\in\{1,...,r\}
such that
m_{t}\geq 1
.
For every monomial
\nu\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
and for every monomial
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathsf{NF}(X)
with
\nu^{\prime}|\nu
, it is clear that
\nu^{\prime}
also lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
. So we deduce

[x_{1}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{N_{S}+1};x_{t}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S).

Now we show that

\mathsf{Irr}(S)\supseteq\{[x_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{l_{{}_{M}}};x_{t}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid l_{1},\dots,l_{{}_{M}}\geq 0\}.

It suffices to show that
[x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{n};x_{t}]
belongs to
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
for every
n\geq N_{S}+1
.
Suppose for the contrary that
[x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{n};x_{t}]\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S)
for some
n>N_{S}+1
.
Then we define

n_{0}=\mathsf{min}\{n\mid[x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{n};x_{t}]\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S)\}.

Clearly, we have

n_{0}>N_{S}+1

and
[x_{1}^{n_{0}-1}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{n_{0}-1};x_{t}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
Therefore, there exists some element
s\in S
such that we have
\bar{s}|[x_{1}^{n_{0}}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{n_{0}};x_{t}]
and
\bar{s}\nmid[x_{1}^{n_{0}-1}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{n_{0}-1};x_{t}]
.
By the definition of
N_{S}
, we deduce
N_{S}\geq n_{0}
,
which contradicts the fact that
n_{0}>N_{S}+1
.
So we have

\{[x_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots x_{{}_{M}}^{l_{{}_{M}}};x_{t}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid l_{1},\dots,l_{{}_{M}}\geq 0\}\subseteq\mathsf{Irr}(S).

Therefore, if
q=0
, then there exist
M
distinct letters
x_{i_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,x_{i_{M}}\in X
, and
x_{t}\in X
such that

(4.2)

\{[x_{i_{1}}^{l_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{M}}^{l_{{}_{M}}};x_{t}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid l_{1},\dots,l_{{}_{M}}\geq 0\}\subseteq\mathsf{Irr}(S).

If
p=0
, then similarly there exist
M
distinct letters
x_{i_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,x_{i_{M}}\in X
, and
x_{t}\in X
such that

(4.3)

\{[x_{t};x_{i_{1}}^{l_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{M}}^{l_{{}_{M}}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid l_{1},\dots,l_{{}_{M}}\geq 0\}\subseteq\mathsf{Irr}(S).

If
p,q\geq 1
, then by a similar proof as for the case when
q=0
, we can show that

(4.4)

\{[x_{i_{1}}^{n_{i_{1}}}\cdots x_{i_{p}}^{n_{i_{p}}};x_{j_{1}}^{m_{j_{1}}}\cdots x_{j_{q}}^{m_{j_{q}}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid n_{i_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,m_{j_{q}}\geq 0\}\subseteq\mathsf{Irr}(S).

Combining (
4.2
),(
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we have
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))\geq M
. Finally, by (
4.1
), we obtain
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=M
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set and let
\mathcal{B}=\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
, where
S
is a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
. Then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathcal{B})=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\{\mu\mid\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X,n_{i},m_{j}\in\{0,1,N_{S}+1\},i,j\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}).

In particular, the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated bicommutative algebra is a nonnegative integer.

Proof.

Define

M^{\prime}=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\{\mu\mid\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X,n_{i},m_{j}\in\{0,1,N_{S}+1\},1\leq i,j\leq r\})

and

M=\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mathsf{Irr}(S)).

Clearly we have
M^{\prime}\leq M
. By Lemma
4.1
, it suffices to show
M\leq M^{\prime}
.

If
M^{\prime}=0
, then we have

\mathsf{Irr}(S)\subseteq X\cup\{[x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{p_{r}};x_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{q_{r}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid p_{i},q_{j}\leq N_{S},1\leq i,j\leq r\}.

Then it follows immediately that we have
M=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=0=M^{\prime}
.

If
M^{\prime}>0
, then we have
M\geq M^{\prime}>0
, and thus there exists some monomial
\nu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X
satisfying
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\nu)=M>0
. By the construction of
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, every monomial
\nu^{\prime}
satisfying
\nu^{\prime}|\nu
lies in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
For every
i\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}
, we define

\displaystyle n^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}N_{S}+1,&\mbox{if }n_{i}>N_{S},\\
1,&\mbox{if }n_{i}\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,N_{S}\},\\
0,&\mbox{if }n_{i}=0;\end{cases}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ m^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}N_{S}+1,&\mbox{if }m_{i}>N_{S},\\
1,&\mbox{if }m_{i}\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,N_{S}\},\\
0,&\mbox{if }m_{i}=0.\end{cases}

Then it follows that we have

\mathsf{Sdeg}(\nu^{\prime})=M

and

\nu^{\prime}=[x_{1}^{n_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}^{\prime}};x_{1}^{m_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}^{\prime}}]\in B,

where

B:=\{\mu\mid\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X,n_{i},m_{j}\in\{0,1,N_{S}+1\},i,j\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}.

Therefore we have

M^{\prime}\geq\mathsf{Sdeg}(\nu)=M
.
The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem
4.2
offers a fast algorithm for calculating the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely presented bicommutative algebra by investigating the finite set

\{\mu\mid\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X,n_{i},m_{j}\in\{0,1,N_{S}+1\},i,j\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}

rather than
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.

Example 4.3
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set. Then the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of the free bicommutative algebra
\mathsf{BC}(X)
generated by
X
is
2r
.

Proof.

As a special case of Theorem
4.2
, say
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
and
S=\emptyset
, then we
have
N_{S}=1
and
[x_{1}^{2}\cdots x_{r}^{2};x_{1}^{2}\cdots x_{r}^{2}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, and thus
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X))=2r
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set.
Let
S
be a nonempty finite subset of
\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X
and
\mu\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X
. Suppose that
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=n\geq 3
.
Let

B=\{\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu^{\prime}=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X,n_{i},m_{j}\in\{0,1,N_{S}+1\},i,j\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}.

If there exists some monomial
\nu\in B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{\mu\})
with
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\nu)=n
, then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{\mu\}))=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S));

Otherwise, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{\mu\}))=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))-1.

Proof.

Obviously, every nonempty subset of
\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis in
\mathsf{BC}(X)
and thus
S
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
.
Then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=\mathsf{Sdeg}(B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S))=n

for some positive integer
n\geq 3
.

If there exists some monomial
\nu\in B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{\mu\})
with
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\nu)=n
, then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{\mu\}))=n=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S)).

If for every monomial
\nu^{\prime}\in B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S)
with
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\nu^{\prime})=n
,
\nu^{\prime}
does not lie in
\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{\mu\})
,
then we have
\mu|\nu^{\prime}
.
Suppose
\nu_{0}\in B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S)
and
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\nu_{0})=n
.
Then we may assume

\nu_{0}=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\setminus X,

where
n_{i_{1}}=\cdots=n_{i_{p}}=m_{j_{1}}=\cdots=m_{j_{q}}=N_{S}+1
,

p+q=n
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{l},m_{l^{\prime}}\in\{0,1\}
for all
l\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\setminus\{i_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,i_{p}\}
and
l^{\prime}\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\setminus\{j_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,j_{q}\}
.
Since
n\geq 3
, we have
p>1
or
q>1
. Without loss of generality, we assume
p>1
and suppose
i_{1}=1
,
i_{2}=2
and
[x_{1};x_{t}]|\mu
for some
x_{t}\in X
. Then
[x_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{\mu\})\setminus X
. Therefore, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{\mu\}))=n-1=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))-1.

The proof is completed.
∎

If we have
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=n>0
, then in general we cannot have a good estimation on
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{x\}))
, where
x
lies in
X
. For instance, let
X=\{x_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,x_{r}\}\ (r>1)
and let
S=\{[x;y]\mid x,y\in X,x\neq x_{1}\}
. Then we have
N_{S}=1
and
[x_{1}^{2};x_{1}^{2}...x_{r}^{2}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
. Clearly, for every
\mu\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, we have
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)\leq r+1
, so
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=r+1
. Moreover, we have
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{x_{1}\}))=0
and
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{x_{2}\}))=r
.

The following example shows that if
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=2
, then we cannot obtain a similar result as in Corollary
4.4
.

Example 4.5
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
and

S=\{[x_{1};x_{1}],[x_{1};x_{2}],[x_{2};x_{1}]\}
.
Then we obtain that
N_{S}=1
.
Let
B:=\{\mu\mid\mu=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}x_{2}^{n_{2}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X,n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2}\in\{0,1,2\}\}
.
Clearly
S
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
and we have
B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S)=\{[x_{2}^{2};x_{2}^{2}]\}
. Thus
\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=2
.
Let
\nu=[x_{2};x_{2}]
.
Then we have
B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S\cup\{\nu\})=\emptyset
. Therefore

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S\cup\{\nu\}))=0=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))-2.

The following proposition offers a rough estimation of the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a finitely presented bicommutative algebra.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a nonempty finite set and
S=\{f_{1},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,f_{t}\}
a finite Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
, where
r>1
.
Then the following hold:

(i)
If
1\leq t\leq r
and
\overline{S}\cap X=\emptyset
, then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))\in\{2r-t,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,2r-1\};

(ii)
If
t>r
, then we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))\in\{0,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,2r-1\}.

Proof.

(i)

We use induction on
t
.
If
t=1
, then we may assume
f_{1}=[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]
, where
n_{1}>0
and
m_{i}>0
for some integer
i\leq r
. Thus we obtain that

[x_{2}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1};x_{1}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1}]\in\mathsf{Irr}(S)\mbox{ and }[x_{1}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1};x_{1}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1}]\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S).

Therefore by Theorem
4.2
, we
have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=2r-1.

Now we assume
t>1
.
Obviously,
\{\overline{f_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,\overline{f_{t-1}}\}
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis in
\mathsf{BC}(X)
and
\mathsf{Irr}(S)=\mathsf{Irr}(\overline{S})
.
By induction hypothesis, we have

p:=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|\{\overline{f_{1}},...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,\overline{f_{t-1}}\}))\in\{2r-t+1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,2r-1\}.

Since
2\leq t\leq r
, we have
2r-t+1\geq 3
.
So by Corollary
4.4
, we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|\overline{S}))\in\{p-1,p\}\subseteq\{2r-t,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,2r-1\}.

(ii)

Since
[x_{1}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1};x_{1}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1}]\notin\mathsf{Irr}(S)
, by Theorem
4.2
, the result follows immediately.
∎

Example 4.7
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
,
r>1
and let
t
be a positive integer.
For every positive integer
p\leq\min\{r,t\}
,
we define
m=t-p
and define

S=\{[x_{i};x_{i}]\mid 1\leq i\leq p\}\cup\{[x_{r}^{i};x_{1}^{3m-i}]\mid m\leq i\leq 2m-1\}.

Let
B:=\{[x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}};x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{m_{r}}]\in\mathsf{NF}(X)\setminus X\mid n_{i},m_{j}\in\{0,1,N_{S}+1\},i,j\in\{1,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,r\}\}
.
Clearly
S
is a Gröbner–Shirshov basis for
\mathsf{BC}(X|S)
with
\#S=t
and we have
N_{S}=\max\{2m,1\}
. Then we obtain

[x_{1}^{N_{S}+1}x_{p+1}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1};x_{2}^{N_{S}+1}\cdots x_{r}^{N_{S}+1}]\in B\cap\mathsf{Irr}(S),

and for every monomial
\mu\in B
with
\mathsf{Sdeg}(\mu)=2r-p+1
,
\mu
does not lie in
\mathsf{Irr}(S)
.
Thus we have

\mathsf{GKdim}(\mathsf{BC}(X|S))=2r-p.

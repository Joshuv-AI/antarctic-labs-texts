On Conditional least squares estimation for the 𝐴𝐷 ⁡ ( 1 , n ) \mathit{AD}(1,n) model
Mohamed Ben Alaya, Houssem Dahbi, Hamdi Fathallah

Abstract
This paper deals with the problem of global parameter estimation of 𝐴𝐷 ⁡ ( 1 , n ) \mathit{AD}(1,n) where n n is a positive integer which is a subclass of affine diffusions introduced by Duffie, Filipovic, and Schachermayer in [ 16 ] . In general affine models are applied to the pricing of bond and stock options, which is illustrated for the Vasicek, Cox-Ingersoll-Ross and Heston models. Our main results are about the conditional least squares estimation of 𝐴𝐷 ⁡ ( 1 , n ) \mathit{AD}(1,n) drift parameters based on two types of observations : continuous time observations and discrete time observations with high frequency and infinite horizon. Then, for each case, we study the asymptotic properties according to ergodic and non-ergodic cases. This paper introduces as well some moment results relative to the 𝐴𝐷 ⁡ ( 1 , n ) \mathit{AD}(1,n) model.

The set of affine processes contains a large class of

\Gamma^{(1)}_{T,N}=\begin{bmatrix}\lfloor NT\rfloor&-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{(1)}_{T,N}=\begin{bmatrix}Y_{\frac{\lfloor NT\rfloor}{N}}-Y_{0}\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}\left(Y_{\frac{i}{N}}-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\right)Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\end{bmatrix}.

Then, povided that
\lfloor NT\rfloor\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{2}-\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\right)^{2}>0
, we deduce that

\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{a}_{T,N}\\
\tilde{b}_{T,N}\end{bmatrix}=\left(\Gamma^{(1)}_{T,N}\right)^{-1}\phi^{(1)}_{T,N}.

Next, in order to solve the second extremum problem
(
8
)
, it is enough to solve the system

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}X_{\frac{i}{N}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}-\left(\tilde{m}-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\tilde{\kappa}-\tilde{\theta}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\right)=\mathbf{0}_{n}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\left(X_{\frac{i}{N}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}-\left(\tilde{m}-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\tilde{\kappa}-\tilde{\theta}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\right)\right)=\mathbf{0}_{n}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}\left(X_{\frac{i}{N}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}-\left(\tilde{m}-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\tilde{\kappa}-\tilde{\theta}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\right)\right)X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}=\mathbf{0}_{n\times n}.\end{cases}

(10)

The above system can be written in the following matrix form

\Gamma^{(2)}_{T,N}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{m}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
\tilde{\kappa}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
\tilde{\theta}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}=\phi^{(2)}_{T,N},

with

\Gamma^{(2)}_{T,N}=\begin{bmatrix}\lfloor NT\rfloor&-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}&-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{2}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{(2)}_{T,N}=\begin{bmatrix}X_{\frac{\lfloor NT\rfloor}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}-X_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\left(X_{\frac{i}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\left(X_{\frac{i}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\end{bmatrix}.

Then, provided the invertibility of
\Gamma^{(2)}_{T,N}
, we deduce that

\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{m}_{T,N}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
\tilde{\kappa}_{T,N}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
\tilde{\theta}_{T,N}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}=\left(\Gamma^{(2)}_{T,N}\right)^{-1}\phi^{(2)}_{T,N}.

Consequently, it remains to prove the invertibility of the matrices
\Gamma^{(1)}_{T,N}
and
\Gamma^{(2)}_{T,N}
. In our case, it is enough to show that they are almost surely positive definite. Let

\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\Gamma^{(1)}_{T,N}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}(x_{1}-x_{2}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}})^{2}\geq 0.

(11)

From the other side,

\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\Gamma^{(1)}_{T,N}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ x_{1}-x_{2}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}=0,
for all

which is impossible since

Y_{0}
is absolutely continuous.
Likewise,

y^{\mathsf{T}}\Gamma^{(2)}_{T,N}y=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}y^{\mathsf{T}}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\\
-X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}\\
-X_{\frac{i-1}{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}y=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor NT\rfloor}(y_{1}-y_{2}Y_{\frac{i-1}{N}}-\tilde{y}^{\mathsf{T}}X_{\frac{i-1}{N}})^{2}\geq 0,

(12)

where
\tilde{y}=(y_{3},\ldots,y_{n+2})^{\mathsf{T}}
.
In addition,

and, for each

is absolutely continuous (See Theorem
B.1
and relation (31) in

]

(13)

-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}X_{s}\mathrm{d}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}:=f_{T}^{(2)},

(14)

as

\tilde{\theta}_{T,N}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\end{bmatrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\begin{bmatrix}(G_{T}^{(1)})^{-1}f_{T}^{(1)}\\

\hat{b}_{T}\\

\hat{\kappa}_{T}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
\hat{\theta}_{T}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\end{bmatrix}=:\hat{\tau}_{T},\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let us consider the affine model
(
1
)
with
a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\theta\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Then for each
T\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, we have
\hat{\tau}_{T,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{\tau}_{T}
, as
N\to\infty
.

Proof.

Thanks to relation (
15
), it is easy to check that

(\tilde{a}_{T,N},\tilde{b}_{T,N},\tilde{m}_{T,N},\tilde{\kappa}_{T,N},\tilde{\theta}_{T,N})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\left(0,0,\mathbf{0}_{n},\mathbf{0}_{n},\mathbf{0}_{n,n}\right),\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

(16)

Hence, since the function
g_{N}
admits an inverse function on the neighbourhood of the zero point, we deduce that

g_{N}^{-1}(\tilde{a}_{T,N},\tilde{b}_{T,N},\tilde{m}_{T,N},\tilde{\kappa}_{T,N},\tilde{\theta}_{T,N})=\hat{\tau}_{T,N},

with probability tending to one as
N\to\infty
. Namely, we get

\hat{b}_{T,N}=-N\log(1-\tilde{b}_{T,N}),\quad\hat{a}_{T,N}=\dfrac{\tilde{a}_{T,N}}{\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}u}\mathrm{d}u},\quad\hat{\theta}_{T,N}=-N\log(I_{n}-\tilde{\theta}_{T,N}),

\hat{\kappa}_{T,N}=\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{\hat{\theta}_{T,N}(u-{\frac{1}{N}})}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}u}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{-1}\tilde{\kappa}_{T,N}

and

\hat{m}_{T,N}=\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{-\hat{\theta}_{T,N}u}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{-1}\left(\tilde{m}_{T,N}+\hat{a}_{T,N}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{(u-{\frac{1}{N}})\hat{\theta}_{T,N}}\hat{\kappa}_{T,N}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}(u-v)}\mathrm{d}v\right)\mathrm{d}u\right).

The following discussion is similar in spirit to the one used in the proof of 3.3 Lemma in
[
11
]
. Using the following first order Taylor approximation at zero point
\ln(1-x)=-x+o(x)
, relations (
15
) and (
16
) implies

\hat{b}_{T,N}=-N\log(1-\tilde{b}_{T,N})=N\tilde{b}_{T,N}+o(\tilde{b}_{T,N})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{b}_{T},\quad\text{as}\ N\to\infty.

Moreover, by relations (
15
) and (
16
), thank to the following first order Taylor approximation at zero point
\dfrac{1-e^{-x}}{x}=1+o(x)
, we get

\hat{a}_{T,N}=\dfrac{\tilde{a}_{T,N}}{\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}u}\mathrm{d}u}=N\tilde{a}_{T,N}\dfrac{\frac{1}{N}\hat{b}_{T,N}}{1-e^{-\frac{1}{N}\hat{b}_{T,N}}}=\dfrac{N\tilde{a}_{T,N}}{1+o(\frac{1}{N}\hat{b}_{T,N})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{a}_{T}

likewise, thanks to the following matrix version of the first order Taylor approximation at zero point
\ln(\mathbf{I}_{n}-A)=-A+o(\left\|A\right\|_{2})
, we obtain

\hat{\theta}_{T,N}=-N\ln(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\tilde{\theta}_{T,N})=N\tilde{\theta}_{T,N}+o\left(\left\|\tilde{\theta}\right\|_{2}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{\theta}_{T},\quad\text{as}\ N\to\infty.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\kappa}_{T,N}=\left(N\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{(\hat{\theta}_{T,N}(u-\frac{1}{N})}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}u}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{-1}N\tilde{\kappa}_{T,N}.

On the one hand, by the convergence
(
15
)
, we have
N\tilde{\kappa}_{T,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{\kappa}_{T}
as
N\to\infty
. On the other hand, by the mean value theorem, for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, there exists
0\leq u^{\star}\leq\dfrac{1}{N}
, such that

\left(N\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{\hat{\theta}_{T,N}(u-\frac{1}{N})}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}u}\mathrm{d}u\right)_{i,j}=\left(e^{\hat{\theta}_{T,N}(u^{\star}-\frac{1}{N})}\right)_{i,j}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}u^{\star}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\delta_{ij},\quad\text{as}\ N\to\infty.

Consequently, we obtain
\hat{\kappa}_{T,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{\kappa}_{T}
as
N\to\infty
.
Finally, since we have

\hat{m}_{T,N}=\left(N\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{-\hat{\theta}_{T,N}u}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{-1}\left(N\tilde{m}_{T,N}+N\hat{a}_{T,N}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}e^{\hat{\theta}_{T,N}(u-{\frac{1}{N}})}\hat{\kappa}_{T,N}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}e^{-\hat{b}_{T,N}(u-v)}\mathrm{d}v\right)\mathrm{d}u\right),

then it is easy to check using the same arguments that
\hat{m}_{T,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{m}_{T}
which completes the proof.
∎

Therefore, by combining these results with the second property in (

\hat{\tau}_{T}-\tau=\begin{bmatrix}(G_{T}^{(1)})^{-1}h_{T}^{(1)}\\

(17)

3.1.1
Consistency of the CLSE

Theorem 3.1
.

Let us consider the model
(
1
)
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
,
m,\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\theta\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
a positive definite diagonalizable matrix with random initial value
(Y_{0},X_{0})
satisfying
\mathbb{P}(Y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{++})=1
. Then the
CLSE
of
\tau
is strongly consistent, i.e.,
\hat{\tau}_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tau
, as
T\to\infty
.

Proof.

By relation
(
17
)
, we have

\hat{\tau}_{T}-\tau=(T^{-1}G_{T})^{-1}(T^{-1}h_{T})
. Furthermore, using Theorem
48
, we get

T^{-1}G_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty}),\quad\text{as }T\to\infty,

where

G_{\infty}:=\begin{bmatrix}G_{\infty}^{(1)}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&I_{n}\otimes G_{\infty}^{(2)}\end{bmatrix}

(18)

with

G_{\infty}^{(1)}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&-Y_{\infty}\\
-Y_{\infty}&Y_{\infty}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{\infty}^{(2)}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&-Y_{\infty}&-X_{\infty}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-Y_{\infty}&-Y_{\infty}^{2}&-Y_{\infty}X_{\infty}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-X_{\infty}&Y_{\infty}X_{\infty}&X_{\infty}X_{\infty}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix},

where
Y_{\infty}
and
X_{\infty}
are defined by the Fourier-Laplace transform given in Theorem
B.1
. It is worth to note that
\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty})
is finite by Remark
A.2
. In addition,
\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty})
is an invertible matrix since it is positive definite matrix. In fact, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}\setminus\{0\}
,

\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty}^{(1)})\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\mathbb{E}((y_{1}-y_{2}Y_{\infty})^{2})>0

and

\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\\
\tilde{y}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty}^{(2)})\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\\
\tilde{y}\end{bmatrix}=\mathbb{E}((y_{1}-y_{2}Y_{\infty}-\tilde{y}^{\mathsf{T}}X_{\infty})^{2})>0,

since
(Y_{\infty},X_{\infty})
is absolutely continuous, hence we obtain
y_{1}-y_{2}Y_{\infty}\neq 0
and
y_{1}-y_{2}Y_{\infty}-\tilde{y}^{\mathsf{T}}X_{\infty}\neq 0
with probability
1
, where
\tilde{y}=(y_{3},\ldots,y_{n+2})^{\mathsf{T}}
. Hence, we deduce that

(T^{-1}G_{T})^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\begin{bmatrix}(\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty}^{(1)}))^{-1}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&I_{n}\otimes(\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty}^{(2)}))^{-1}\end{bmatrix}=(\mathbb{E}(G_{\infty}))^{-1},\quad\text{as }T\to\infty.

In order to finish the proof, it is enough to check that

T^{-1}h_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}{0},
as
T\to\infty
.
Firstly, for all
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let
(h_{T,1}^{(2)},h_{T,2}^{(2)},\ldots,h_{T,n}^{(2)}):=h_{T}^{(2)}
, which means that
h_{T,i}^{(2)}
would be the
i^{\text{th}}
column of
h_{T}^{(2)}
of size
n+2
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Consequently, for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
\langle h^{(1)}_{T},\left.h^{(1)}_{T}\right.^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle
,
\langle h_{T}^{(1)},\left.h^{(2)}_{T,i}\right.^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle
and
\langle h^{(2)}_{T,i},\left.h^{(2)}_{T,j}\right.^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle
are given, respectively, by

\mathrm{H}_{T}^{(1)}=\sigma_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{s}&-Y_{s}^{2}\\
-Y_{s}^{2}&Y_{s}^{3}\end{bmatrix}\mathrm{d}s
,
\mathrm{H}_{T}^{(2)}=\sigma_{1}\tilde{\rho}_{i1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{s}&-Y_{s}^{2}&-Y_{s}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-Y_{s}^{2}&Y_{s}^{3}&Y_{s}^{2}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\mathrm{d}s
and

\mathrm{H}_{T}^{(3)}=\left(\tilde{\rho}\tilde{\rho}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)_{ij}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{s}&-Y_{s}^{2}&-Y_{s}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-Y_{s}^{2}&Y_{s}^{3}&Y_{s}^{2}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-Y_{s}X_{s}&Y_{s}^{2}X_{s}&Y_{s}X_{s}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\mathrm{d}s.
Secondly, by Remark
A.2
and Theorem
48
, the quadratic variation
\mathrm{H}_{T}
of
h_{T}
satisfies

\dfrac{1}{T}\mathrm{H}_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{H}_{\infty}),\quad\text{as }T\to\infty,

where

\mathrm{H}_{\infty}=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{1}^{2}\mathrm{H}_{\infty}^{(1)}&\sigma_{1}(\rho_{J1}\otimes\mathrm{H}_{\infty}^{(2)})\\
\sigma_{1}(\rho_{J1}\otimes\mathrm{H}_{\infty}^{(2)})^{\mathsf{T}}&\tilde{\rho}\tilde{\rho}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\mathrm{H}_{\infty}^{(3)}\end{bmatrix},

(19)

with
\mathrm{H}_{\infty}^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix}Y_{\infty}&-Y_{\infty}^{2}\\
-Y_{\infty}^{2}&Y_{\infty}^{3}\end{bmatrix}
,
\mathrm{H}_{\infty}^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix}Y_{\infty}&-Y_{\infty}^{2}&-Y_{\infty}X_{\infty}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-Y_{\infty}^{2}&Y_{\infty}^{3}&Y_{\infty}^{2}X_{\infty}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}
and

-Y_{\infty}^{2}&Y_{\infty}^{3}&Y_{\infty}^{2}X_{\infty}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
-Y_{\infty}X_{\infty}&Y_{\infty}^{2}X_{\infty}&Y_{\infty}X_{\infty}X_{\infty}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}.

Finally, the strong law of large numbers for continuous local martingales given by Theorem

applied on

Theorem 3.2

V((Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},T)-V((\mathcal{Y}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},(\mathcal{X}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},T)
. In the following, we treat, for example, the proof related to the last term. By a simple decomposition then by applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\dfrac{1}{T^{2}}\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}X_{s}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{X}_{s}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{T^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s})\mathrm{\penalty\ d}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)}+\dfrac{1}{T^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{X}_{s}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}(X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s})^{\mathsf{T}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)}.

On the one hand, by equation (6.11) in
[
11
]
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{4}\right)=O(T^{3})
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s})\mathrm{\penalty\ d}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|m\right\|_{1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s})\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)+2\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{Y_{s}}(X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s})\mathrm{\penalty\ d}B_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|m\right\|_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}\left\|X_{u}-\mathcal{X}_{u}\right\|_{1}\right)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{\penalty\ d}u

\displaystyle\quad+2\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}\left\|X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|m\right\|_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X_{u}-\mathcal{X}_{u}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s\mathrm{\penalty\ d}u

\displaystyle\quad+2\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{4}\right)}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{T^{2}}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s\mathrm{\penalty\ d}u+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{T^{5}}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s\right)\leq CT^{\frac{7}{2}}.

On the other hand, using equation (6.12) in
[
11
]
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{4}\right)=O(T^{4})
, then we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{X}_{s}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}(X_{s}-\mathcal{X}_{s})^{\mathsf{T}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\sqrt{Y_{s}}-\sqrt{\mathcal{Y}_{s}})^{2}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{2}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s\right)\leq\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left((Y_{s}-\mathcal{Y}_{s})\left\|\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left((Y_{s}-\mathcal{Y}_{s})^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{1}^{4}\right)}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s\leq C\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{T^{5}}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s=CT^{\frac{7}{2}}.

Hence, by combining the above two results, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\dfrac{1}{T^{2}}\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}X_{s}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}X_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{X}_{s}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\|_{1}\right)\leq C\dfrac{T^{\frac{7}{4}}}{T^{2}}\to 0,\quad\text{as }T\to\infty,

which completes the proof.
∎

CLSE based on discrete time observations

We consider
(Y_{t},X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
the
\mathit{AD}(1,n)

\displaystyle\times\left(X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}-\left(\check{m}-Y_{t_{k-1}}\check{\kappa}-\check{\theta}X_{t_{k-1}}\right)\right).

Hence, in order solve the extremum problem, we proceed with similar calculation as in the previous section (see relations (
9
) and (
10

\left(\check{a}_{t_{N}}^{\Delta_{N}},\check{b}_{t_{N}}^{\Delta_{N}}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}=\left(\Delta_{N}\check{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{t_{N},\Delta_{N}}\right)^{-1}\check{\phi}^{(1)}_{t_{N},\Delta_{N}}.

and

\left(\check{m}_{t_{N}}^{\Delta_{N}},\check{\kappa}_{t_{N}}^{\Delta_{N}},\check{\theta}_{t_{N}}^{\Delta_{N}}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}=\left(\Delta_{N}\check{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{t_{N},\Delta_{N}}\right)^{-1}\check{\phi}^{(2)}_{t_{N},\Delta_{N}},

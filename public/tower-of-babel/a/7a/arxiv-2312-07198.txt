B ¯ d , s → K ∗ 0 ​ K ¯ ∗ 0 {\rm\overline{B}}_{d,s}\to\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0} decays, a serious problem for the Standard Model
R. Aleksan, L. Oliver

Abstract




ABSTRACT

We underline the theoretical and experimental interest of the vector-vector penguin decays
{\rm\overline{B}}_{d,s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
for which the data show a strong U-spin violation. Indeed, with the latest LHCb data one has for these two modes very different longitudinal polarization fractions,
f_{L}^{exp}({\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})=0.24\pm 0.04
and
f_{L}^{exp}({\rm\overline{B}}_{d}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})=0.74\pm 0.05
. This feature is very striking because both modes are related by the exchange
s\leftrightarrow d
, i.e. they are related by U-spin symmetry as pointed out in earlier work by other authors. We illustrate this phenomenon by computing different observables for these modes within the QCD Factorization scheme, and we find, as expected, rather close values for the longitudinal fractions, with central values
f_{L}^{th}({\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})\simeq 0.42
and
f_{L}^{th}({\rm\overline{B}}_{d}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})\simeq 0.49
and rather small errors. Furthermore, due to the
V-A
nature of the weak currents and the heavy quark limit, one expects
(f_{\parallel}/f_{\perp})_{th}\simeq 1
, which in the
\rm B_{s}
case is in contradiction with the data
(f_{\parallel}/f_{\perp})_{\rm exp}\simeq 0.44\pm 0.06
.

1
Introduction

In the absence of clear indications of physics beyond the Standard Model (SM), one may study in detail the rare decays of particles to search for deviations from the SM predictions. In this regard, B-meson decays to two vector particles are a very rich source of observables, which can exibit signs of New Physics (NP). It has been shown that the decay
{\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to\phi\phi
is a good candidate in order to search for evidence of NP
[
1
]
, in particular at FCC-ee
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
where high sensitivities can be obtained. This can be achieved by searching for CP violating effects in time-dependent studies while almost no effect is expected in the Standard Model. Similar studies can be carried out with the decays
{\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
, with which sensitivities to NP can be improved further. In the present paper, we study this latter decay together with the decay
{\rm\overline{B}}_{d}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
, which is related to the former through U-spin symetry, and we show that it behaves in a way which is theoretically unexpected using QCD Factorization, thus requiring particular attention.

3
Expectations using Naive Factorization

A detailed theoretical study of
\overline{B}\to V_{1}V_{2}
has been carried out
[
7
]
using QCD Factorization. In the following, we summarize the theoretical expectations using the values of the parameters shown in the Tables
2
,
3
and
4
.

In
\rm\overline{B}\to V_{1}V_{2}
decays, one is dealing with 3 helicity amplitudes.

\begin{array}[]{lclcccc}{\cal\overline{A}}_{0}&=&A[\overline{B}\to V_{1}(0)V_{2}(0)]&,&{\cal\overline{A}}_{\pm}&=&A[\overline{B}\to V_{1}(\pm)V_{2}(\pm)]\\
\end{array}

(6)

Moving from the helicity representation to the transversity one, one gets :

\begin{array}[]{lclcccc}{\cal\overline{A}}_{L}&=&{\cal\overline{A}}_{0}\\
\\
{\cal\overline{A}}_{\parallel}&=&\frac{{\cal\overline{A}}_{+}+{\cal\overline{A}}_{-}}{\sqrt{2}}&,&{\cal\overline{A}}_{\perp}&=&\frac{{\cal\overline{A}}_{+}-{\cal\overline{A}}_{-}}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\end{array}

(7)

with the corresponding transversity rate fractions
f_{L}
,
f_{\parallel}
and
f_{\perp}
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{lclcccc}f_{L}+f_{\parallel}+f_{\perp}=1\\
\end{array}

(8)

From Beneke et al.
[
12
]
, one has :

\begin{array}[]{cccc}{\cal\overline{A}}_{0}^{V_{1}V_{2}}&=&i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}m_{B}^{2}f_{V_{2}}A_{0}^{B\to V_{1}}(0)\\
\\
{\cal\overline{A}}_{\pm}^{V_{1}V_{2}}&=&i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}m_{B}m_{V_{2}}f_{V_{2}}F_{\pm}^{B\to V_{1}}(0)\\
\end{array}

(9)

with

\begin{array}[]{cccc}F_{\pm}^{B\to V_{1}}(q^{2})&\equiv&\left(1+\frac{m_{1}}{{m_{B}}}\right)A_{1}^{B\to V_{1}}(q^{2})\mp\left(1-\frac{m_{1}}{{m_{B}}}\right)V^{B\to V_{1}}(q^{2})\\
\end{array}

(10)

Since the final quark is dominantly left-handed because of the
V-A
structure of the Standard Model, heavy quark symmetry implies the hierarchy

\begin{array}[]{cccc}{\cal\overline{A}}_{L}:{\cal\overline{A}}_{-}:{\cal\overline{A}}_{+}&=&1:\frac{\Lambda_{QCD}}{m_{b}}:\left(\frac{\Lambda_{QCD}}{m_{b}}\right)^{2}\\
\end{array}

(11)

The transverse amplitude
{\cal\overline{A}_{-}}
is suppressed by factor
m_{V_{2}}/m_{b}
relative to
{\cal\overline{A}}_{L}
, and the axial and vector contributions to
{\cal\overline{A}}_{+}
cancel out in the heavy quark limit and large recoil energy for the light mesons. Indeed, in this latter limit, the
A_{1}
and
V
form factors are related and one has
[
8
]

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{m_{B}}{m_{B}+m_{V}}V(q^{2})&=&\frac{m_{B}+m_{V}}{2E}A_{1}(q^{2})\\
\\
\mathrm{with}\ E&=&(m_{B}^{2}+m_{V}^{2}-q^{2})/2m_{B}\end{array}

(12)

At
q^{2}=0
, one gets
2Em_{B}=m_{B}^{2}+m_{V}^{2}
and therefore in the limit
m_{V}<<m_{B}
,

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{V(0)}{A_{1}(0)}&\simeq&1\\
\end{array}

(13)

giving in the heavy quark limit and large recoil energy for the light meson,

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}{\cal\overline{A}}_{+}\simeq 0\end{array}

(14)

and one then has

\begin{array}[]{cccc}{\cal\overline{A}}_{\parallel}\simeq-{\cal\overline{A}}_{\perp}&\simeq&\frac{{\cal\overline{A}}_{-}}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\\
|{\cal\overline{A}}_{\parallel}|^{2}+|{\cal\overline{A}}_{\perp}|^{2}&\simeq&|{\cal\overline{A}}_{-}|^{2}\equiv|{\cal\overline{A}}_{T}|^{2}\\
\end{array}

(15)

With the hierachy above and the limit
{\cal\overline{A}}_{+}\simeq 0
, one gets

\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{\parallel}&\simeq&f_{\perp}\end{array}

(16)

Accordingly, we are left with only one transverse form factor
{F_{T}^{B_{d,s}\to x}(0)}\equiv{F_{-}^{B_{d,s}\to x}(0)}
.
As can be seen in Table
1
, the experiment is in agreement with equations (
15
) within errors, with the noticeable exception of
{\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
if one uses the LHCb measurements of formula (
2
). This latter mode is discussed further in this note.

In most Penguin dominated decays such as
{\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to\phi\phi
, as we have exposed in detail in
[
1
]
, although the equality
f_{\parallel}\simeq f_{\perp}
is approximately satisfied by the data, the longitudinal fraction
f_{L}
is experimentally much smaller, of the order of
f_{\parallel,\perp}
, a feature that is qualitatively described within the QCD Factorization (QCDF) scheme
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, in papers devoted to the
B
decays to two vector mesons
\rm\overline{B}_{d,s}\to V_{1}V_{2}

[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

{\rm\overline{B}}_{d,s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
. The important point that we want to underline is that these two decays are related by

In the limit of Naive Factorization, the amplitudes for the modes
{\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
and
{\rm\overline{B}}_{d}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
are respectively governed by the products of CKM factors

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}A({\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})\sim\lambda^{\prime}_{t}=V_{tb}V^{*}_{ts}=-\lambda^{\prime}_{u}-\lambda^{\prime}_{c}\\
A({\rm\overline{B}}_{d}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})\sim\lambda_{t}=V_{tb}V^{*}_{td}=-\lambda_{u}-\lambda_{c}\end{array}

(17)

and the expected ratio of rates is of the order of magnitude

{{\rm Br}({\rm\overline{B}}_{d}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})\over{\rm Br}({\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})}\simeq 0.046

(18)

i.e. consistent within errors with the branching ratios of Table 1,

\left[{{\rm Br}({\rm\overline{B}}_{d}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})\over{\rm Br}({\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}})}\right]_{\rm exp}=0.075\pm 0.028

(19)

We conclude that, for the time being, it does not seem to be a contradiction between theory and experiment for the rates.

U

U

6
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have made evident in a quantitative way that there is a problem of U-spin violation, very much larger than could be expected, in the decays
{\rm\overline{B}}_{d,s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
, a trend that had been suggested by previous authors. This clear feature asks for new measurements, mainly on the decay
{\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
. For example, should the error on
f_{L,\mathrm{Exp}}
for
{\rm\overline{B}}_{s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
be reduced by a factor 2 in the near future, the significance would exceed 5 standard deviations. On a longer term, FCC-ee would enable to measure the polarizations with oustanding precisions for both
{\rm\overline{B}}_{d,s}
, allowing one to reveal whether new physics appears in
{\rm\overline{B}}_{d,s}\to{\rm K^{*0}\overline{K}^{*0}}
. More generally,
{\rm\overline{B}}_{u,d,s}\to V_{1}V_{2}
is a very rich area for testing in depth the standard model further.

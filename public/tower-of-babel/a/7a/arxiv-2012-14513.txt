Abstract
. The variety generated by the Brandt semigroup 𝐁 2 {\bf B}_{2} can be defined within the variety generated by the semigroup 𝐀 2 {\bf A}_{2} by the single identity x 2 ​ y 2 ≈ y 2 ​ x 2 x^{2}y^{2}\approx y^{2}x^{2} . Preliminaries: hypergraphs

A
hypergraph

\mathcal{H}
is a pair
(V,E)
, where
V
is a set and
E
is a set of nonempty subsets of
V
. For

\mathcal{H}
is
k
-uniform
if the elements of
E
all have exactly
k

The class of
2
-uniform hypergraphs coincides with the class of simple graphs, and many concepts from simple graphs extend to higher values of
k
in obvious ways. In particular, we say that a point

is
isolated
if it does not lie in any hyperedge, and that a
n

v_{0},e_{0},v_{1},e_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},e_{n-1}
alternating between vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1}
and hyperedges
e_{0},\dots,e_{n-1}
, with no repeats and such that
v_{i+1}\in e_{i}\cap e_{i+1}
(with addition in the subscript taken modulo
n
).

The
girth
of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, denoted
\operatorname{girth}(\mathcal{H})
is the length of the shortest cycle in
\mathcal{H}
.
We consider
3
-uniform hypergraphs with no isolated points and of girth at least
4
. The following easy lemma recasts these girth assumptions in an equivalent form aimed at the needs of our construction.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a
3
-uniform hypergraph. Condition
(I)
below is equivalent to
\operatorname{girth}(\mathcal{H})\geq 3
, while conditions
(I)
and
(II)
together are equivalent to
\operatorname{girth}(\mathcal{H})\geq 4
.

(I)

every two-element subset
\{u,v\}\subseteq V
is a subset of at most one hyperedge.

(II)

if all two-element subsets of a triple
\{u,v,w\}\subseteq V
extend to hyperedges in
E
, then
\{u,v,w\}
is a hyperedge.

Moreover, conditions
(I)
and
(II)
imply the following condition
:

(III)

Every
4
-set
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
of vertices contains a two-element subset not extending to a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that a 2-cycle corresponds to a pair of hyperedges
\{u,v,w\}
and
\{u,v,w^{\prime}\}
in
E
with
w\neq w^{\prime}
, which is identical to what it means to fail (I). Thus (I) is equivalent to having no
2
-cycles.

Now assume
(V,E)
has no
3
-cycles and assume that all two-element subsets of
\{u,v,w\}\subseteq V
extend to hyperedges. So, there are
u^{\prime},v^{\prime},w^{\prime}
such that

\{u,v,w^{\prime}\},\{u,v^{\prime},w\},\{u^{\prime},v,w\}\in E.

As
(V,E)
avoids
3
-cycles, and
u,v,w
avoids repeats, the sequence

u,\{u,v,w^{\prime}\},v,\{u^{\prime},v,w\},w,\{u,v^{\prime},w\}

must repeat in its hyperedges. This then gives one of
u=u^{\prime}
,
v=v^{\prime}
or
w=w^{\prime}
and then that
\{u,v,w\}\in E
, so that (II) holds.

The converse needs (I) (which we already know forces there to be no 2-cycles). Assume that (I) and (II) hold and consider, for contradiction, a
3
-cycle
u,e_{1},v,e_{2},w,e_{3}
. Now,
\{u,v\}\subseteq e_{1}
,
\{v,w\}\subset e_{2}
and
\{u,w\}\subseteq e_{3}
, so that
\{u,v,w\}
is a hyperedge by (II). But then condition (I) forces
e_{1}=e_{2}=e_{3}=\{u,v,w\}
, contradicting the fact that
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
are pairwise distinct.

Finally, let us assume that (I) and (II) hold and consider a
4
-element subset
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
of
V
. By (I) we cannot have both
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
and
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{4}\}
hyperedges. But then the contrapositive of (II) implies that there is a
2
-element subset of
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
or
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{4}\}
(and in particular, of
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
) that does not extend to a hyperedge. Thus (III) holds.
∎

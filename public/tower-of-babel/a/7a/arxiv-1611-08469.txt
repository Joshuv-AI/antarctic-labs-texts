Biwarped product submanifolds of a Kähler manifold
Hakan Mete Taştan

Abstract
. We study biwarped product submanifolds which are special cases of multiply warped product submanifolds in Kähler manifolds. We observe the non-existence of such submanifolds under some circumstances. We show that there exists a non-trivial biwarped product submanifold of a certain type by giving an illustrate example. We also give a necessary and sufficient condition for such submanifolds to be locally trivial. Moreover, we establish an inequality for the squared norm of the second fundamental form in terms of the warping functions for such submanifolds. The equality case is also discussed.

Introduction

Bishop and O’ Neill
[

construct a large class of complete manifolds of negative curvature.
This concept is also a generalization of the usual product of Riemannian manifolds. Nölker

Curvature properties of such products were investigated by Dobarro and Ünal

The concept of warped products or multiply warped products play very important roles in physics as well as in differential geometry,
especially in the theory of relativity. Actually, the standard spacetime models such as Robertson-Walker, Schwarschild, static and Kruscal are
warped products. Also, the simplest models of neighborhoods of stars and black holes are warped products

In differential geometry, especially in almost complex geometry, one of the most intensively research areas

The theory of warped product submanifolds has been becoming a popular research area since Chen

In this paper, we consider and study biwarped product submanifolds in Kähler manifolds.
Here, a biwarped product means that a multiply warped product which has only two fibers.
We observe the non-existence of biwarped product submanifolds under some circumstances.
After giving an illustrate example, we study such submanifolds in case of the base factor is
holomorphic and one of the two fibers is totally real
and the other one is pointwise slant submanifold.
We also give characterization for this kind of submanifolds.
Moreover, we investigate the behavior of the second fundamental form of such submanifolds and as a result,
we give a necessary and sufficient condition for such manifolds to be locally trivial.
Furthermore, an inequality for the squared norm of the second fundamental form in terms
of the warping functions for such submanifolds is obtained. The equality case is also
considered.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the fundamental definitions and notions needed further study. Actually, in subsection 2.1,
we will recall the definition of the multiply warped product manifolds. In subsection 2.2, we will give the basic background for submanifolds of Riemannian manifolds.
The definition of a Kähler manifold and the some classes of submanifolds of Kähler manifolds are placed in subsection 2.3.

2.1.
Multiply product manifolds

Let
(M_{i},g_{i})
be Riemannian manifolds for any
i\in\{0,1,...,k\}
and let
f_{j}:M_{0}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be smooth functions for any

j\in\{1,2,...,k\}.
Then the
multiply product manifold

[
21
]

\bar{M}=M_{0}\times_{f_{1}}M_{1}\times...\times_{f_{k}}M_{k}

is the product manifold
\tilde{M}=M_{0}\times M_{1}\times...\times M_{k}
endowed with the metric

g=\pi^{*}_{0}(g_{0})\oplus(f_{1}\circ\pi_{0})^{2}\pi^{*}_{1}(g_{1})\oplus...\oplus(f_{k}\circ\pi_{0})^{2}\pi^{*}_{k}(g_{k}).

More precisely, for any vector fields
X
and
Y
of
\bar{M}
, we have

g(X,Y)=g_{0}(\pi_{0*}X,\pi_{0*}Y)+\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}(f_{i}\circ\pi_{0})^{2}g_{i}(\pi_{i*}X,\pi_{i*}Y),

where
\pi_{i}:\tilde{M}\rightarrow M_{i}
is the canonical projection of
\tilde{M}
onto
M_{i}
,

\pi^{*}_{i}(g_{i})
is the pullback of
g_{i}
by
\pi_{i}
and the subscript
*

denotes the derivative map of
\pi_{i}
for each
i.
Each function

is called a
warping function
and each manifold
(M_{j},g_{j})
,
j\in\{1,2,...,k\}
is called a
fiber
of the multiply warped product
\bar{M}.
The manifold
(M_{0},g_{0})
is called a
base manifold
of
\bar{M}.
As well known, the base manifold of
\bar{M}
is totally geodesic and the fibers of
\bar{M}
are totally umbilic in
\bar{M}
.

Let
\bar{M}=M_{0}\times_{f_{1}}M_{1}\times...\times_{f_{k}}M_{k}
be multiply product manifold,
if
k=1
, then we get a (singly) warped product
[
4
]
. We call the multiply product manifolds as
biwarped product manifolds
for
k=2.

In other words, a biwarped product manifold has the form
M_{0}\times_{f_{1}}M_{1}\times_{f_{2}}M_{2}.
We say that
a biwarped product manifold is

, if the warping functions
f_{1}
and

are constants. Note that
biwarped product manifolds were also studied under the name of
twice warped products

[
1
]
.

Let
\bar{M}=M_{0}\times_{f_{1}}M_{1}\times_{f_{2}}M_{2}
be a biwarped product manifold with the Levi-Civita connection
\bar{\nabla}

and
{}^{i}\nabla
denote the Levi-Civita connection of
M_{i}
for
i\in\{0,1,2\}.
By usual convenience, we denote the set of lifts of vector fields on

by

and use the same notation for a vector field and for its lift. On the other hand, since the map
\pi_{0}
is an isometry and
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are (positive) homotheties, they preserve the Levi-Civita connections. Thus, there is no confusion using the same notation for a connection on
M_{i}
and for its pullback via
\pi_{i}.
Then, the covariant derivative formulas for a biwarped product manifold are given by the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

(

]

be a biwarped product manifold. Then, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{U}V=\,^{0}\nabla_{U}V,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{V}X=\bar{\nabla}_{X}V=V(\ln f_{i})X,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{X}Z=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }i\neq j,\\
{}^{i}\nabla_{X}Z-g(X,Z)\emph{grad}\,(\ln f_{i})&\mbox{if }i=j,\end{cases}\qquad\qquad\qquad

where

,
X\in\mathcal{L}(M_{i})
and

We note that grad

for

]

2.2.
Submanifolds of Riemannian manifolds

Let
M
be an isometrically immersed submanifold in a Riemannian manifold
(\bar{M},g)
.
Let
\bar{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\bar{M}
with respect to the metric

and let
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\bot}
be the induced, and induced normal connection on
M
, respectively. Then, for all
U,V\in TM
and

\xi\in T^{\bot}M
, the Gauss and Weingarten formulas are given respectively by

(2.4)

\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\nabla}_{U}V=\nabla_{U}V+h(U,V)\end{array}

and

(2.5)

\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\nabla}_{U}\xi=-A_{\xi}U+\nabla_{U}^{\bot}\xi\end{array},

where
TM
is the tangent bundle and
T^{\bot}M
is the normal bundle of
M
in
\bar{M}
.
Additionally,

is the

of

and

is the Weingarten endomorphism associated with
\xi.
The second
fundamental form

and the
shape operator

A
related by

(2.6)

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h(U,V),\xi)=g(A_{\xi}U,V)\end{array}.

The
mean curvature vector field

H
of
M
is given by

H=\frac{1}{m}(trace\,h),
where
dim(M)=m.
We say that the submanifold
M
is

totally geodesic
in
\bar{M}
if
h=0
, and

if
H=0.
The submanifold
M
is called
totally umbilical
if
h(U,V)=g(U,V)H
for all
U,V\in TM.

If the manifold
M
is totally umbilical and its mean curvature vector field

is

, i.e.

for all

and
\xi\in T^{\bot}M

.

Let

. Then we say that

is

-

(\mathcal{D}^{1},\mathcal{D}^{2})
-

if

for

and
X\in\mathcal{D}^{2}.

If for all

and
X\in\mathcal{D}^{2}
,
\nabla_{X}V\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
, then
\mathcal{D}^{1}
is called
\mathcal{D}^{2}
-
parallel
.
We say that
\mathcal{D}^{1}
is

if
\mathcal{D}^{1}
is
\mathcal{D}^{1}
-parallel. If a distribution on

2.3.
Some classes of submanifolds of Kähler manifolds

Let
\bar{M}
be an almost complex manifold with almost complex structure
J.
If there is a Riemannian metric
g
on
\bar{M}
satisfying

(2.7)

\begin{array}[]{c}g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y)\end{array}

for any
X,Y\in T\bar{M},
then we say that
(\bar{M},J,g)
is an almost Hermitian manifold.
Let
\bar{\nabla}
be the Levi-Civita connection of the almost Hermitian manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
with respect to

Then
(\bar{M},J,g)
is called a
Kähler manifold

[
40
]
if
J
is parallel with respect to
\bar{\nabla},

i.e.,

(2.8)

\begin{array}[]{c}(\bar{\nabla}_{X}J)Y=0\end{array}

for all
X,Y\in T\bar{M}.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold isometrically immersed submanifold in a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
. Then the submanifold

M
is called a
pointwise slant submanifold

[
10
,
15
]
if for every point
p
of
M,
the Wirtinger angle
\theta(V)
between
JV

and the tangent space
T_{p}M
at
p
is independent of the choice of the nonzero vector
V\in T_{p}M.
In this case, the angle
\theta

can be viewed as a function on
M
and it is called the
slant function
of
M
. We say that the pointwise slant submanifold
M
is

proper
neither
\cos\theta(p)=0
nor
\sin\theta(p)=0
at each point
p\in M
. (This condition is different from Chen’s definition, see
[
10
]
).

Now, let
M
be a submanifold of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
. For any
V\in TM
, we put

(2.9)

\begin{array}[]{c}JV=TV+FV\end{array}.

Here
TV
is the tangential part of
JV,
and
FV
is the normal
part of
JV.
Then
M
is a pointwise slant submanifold of
\bar{M}
if and only if, for any
V\in TM
, we have

(2.10)

\begin{array}[]{c}T^{2}V=-\cos^{2}\!\theta V\end{array}

for some function
\theta
defined on
M

[
10
]
. For a pointwise slant submanifold of
\bar{M}
,
using (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and the Kähler structure, it is not difficult to prove the following two facts.

(2.11)

\begin{array}[]{c}g(TU,TV)=\cos^{2}\!\theta g(U,V)\end{array},

(2.12)

\begin{array}[]{c}g(FU,FV)=\sin^{2}\!\theta g(U,V)\end{array}

for
U,V\in TM
.

Let
M
be a pointwise slant submanifold with slant function
\theta
of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g).

If the function
\theta
is a constant, i.e., it is also independent of the choice of the point
p\in M
, then we say that

M
is a
slant submanifold

[
7
]
.

If
\theta\equiv 0,
then
M
is called a
holomorphic
or
complex submanifold

[
40
]
. In that case, the tangent space

T_{p}M
is invariant with respect to the almost complex structure
J
at each point
p\in M
, i.e.,
J(T_{p}M)\subseteq T_{p}M.

If
\theta\equiv\frac{\pi}{2},
then
M
is called a
totally real submanifold
of
[
40
]
. In which case, the tangent space

T_{p}M
is anti-invariant with respect to the almost complex structure
J
for every point
p
of
M
, i.e.,
J(T_{p}M)\subseteq T^{\bot}_{p}M.

In a special case, we have the following definition.

4.
Biwarped product submanifolds of Kähler manifolds

4.1.
Existence problems

Chen proved that there do not exist (non-trivial) warped product submanifolds in the form

M_{\bot}\times_{f}M_{T}
in a Kähler manifold
\bar{M}
such that
M_{\bot}
is a totally real and

5.
Biwarped product generalized
J
-induced submanifolds of order 1

in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
of a Kähler manifold

In this section, we give a characterization for biwarped product generalized
J
-induced submanifolds of order 1
in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
, where
M_{T}
is a holomorphic,
M_{\bot}
is a totally real and
M_{\theta}
is
a pointwise slant submanifold of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
. After that,
we investigate the behavior of the second fundamental form of such submanifolds and as a result,
we give a necessary and sufficient condition for such manifolds to be locally trivial.
Now, we give one of the main theorems of this paper. We first recall the following fact given in
[
13
]
to prove our theorem.

Remark 5.1
.

(Remark 2.1
[
13
]
) Suppose that the tangent bundle of a Riemannian manifold
M
splints into an orthogonal sum

TM=\mathcal{D}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus...\oplus\mathcal{D}_{k}
of non-trivial distributions such that each
\mathcal{D}_{j}
is spherical
and its complement in
TM
is autoparallel for
j\in\{1,2,...,k\}.
Then the manifold
M
is locally isometric to a
multiply warped product
M_{0}\times_{f_{1}}M_{1}\times...\times_{f_{k}}M_{k}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
M
be a proper generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1 of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g).

Then
M
is a locally biwarped product submanifold of the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
if and only if

(5.1)

\displaystyle A_{JX}V=-JV(\lambda)X,

(5.2)

\displaystyle A_{FTZ}V-A_{FZ}JV=-\sin^{2}\!\theta V(\mu)Z,

for some functions
\lambda
and
\mu
satisfying
X(\lambda)=Z(\lambda)=0
and
X(\mu)=Z(\mu)=0,
and

(5.3)

\displaystyle g(A_{JY}TZ,X)=g(A_{FTZ}Y,X),

(5.4)

\displaystyle g(A_{JY}TW,Z)=g(A_{FTZ}Y,W).

for
V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
and
Z,W\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a biwarped product proper generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1 of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)

in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
. Then for any

,
X\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
and

2.4
)
\sim
(

\displaystyle g(A_{JX}V,U)=-g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}JX,V)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}X,JV)=g(\nabla_{U}X,JV).

from (

\begin{array}[]{c}g(A_{JX}V,U)=U(\ln f)g(X,JV)=0\end{array},

\begin{array}[]{c}g(A_{JX}V,Z)=0\end{array},

5.5
)
\sim
(

5.1
). Now,

,

be the

,
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}

5.2
), we get

\begin{array}[]{c}g(\nabla_{U}V,Z)=g(\nabla_{X}U,Z)=0\end{array}.

Using (
3.10
) and (
5.3
), we find

\begin{array}[]{c}g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=0\end{array}.

So,
\mathcal{D}^{T}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
is autoparallel.
Thus, by Remark
5.1
,
M
is locally biwarped product submanifold of the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}.

∎

Let
M=M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
be a non-trivial biwarped product generalized

J
-induced submanifold of order 1 of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g).
Next, we investigate the behavior of the second fundamental form
h
of such submanifolds.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M
be a biwarped product generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1 in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
. Then for the second fundamental form
h
of
M
in
\bar{M}
, we have

(5.19)

\displaystyle g(h(U,V),JX)=0\,,\qquad\qquad

(5.20)

\displaystyle g(h(V,X),JY)=-JV(\ln f)g(X,Y)\,,

(5.21)

\displaystyle g(h(V,Z),JX)=0\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

where
U,V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
and
Z\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
.

Proof.

Using (
2.4
),(
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h(U,V),JX)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}V,JX)=-g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}JX,V)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}X,JV)\end{array}

for
U,V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
X\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
. Again, using (
2.4
),
we obtain
g(h(U,V),JX)=g(\nabla_{U}X,JV)
. Here, we know
\nabla_{U}X=U(\ln f)X
from (
2.2
). Thus, we obtain

g(h(U,V),JX)=U(\ln f)g(X,V)=0
, since
g(X,V)=0.
So, (
5.19
) follows. Now, let

V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
. Then using (
2.4
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h(V,X),JY)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}V,JY)=-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}JV,Y)=-g(\nabla_{X}JV,Y)\end{array}.

Here, we know
\nabla_{X}JV=JV(\ln f)X
from (
2.2
). Thus, we get (
5.20
). The last assertion can be obtained in a similar method.
∎

The previous lemma shows partially us the behavior of the second fundamental form
h
of the biwarped product generalized

J
-induced submanifolds of order 1 has the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
in the normal subbundle
J(\mathcal{D}^{T}).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
M
be a biwarped product generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1 in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
. Then for the second fundamental form
h
of
M
in
\bar{M}
, we have

(5.22)

\displaystyle g(h(U,V),FZ)=0\,,

(5.23)

\displaystyle g(h(V,X),FZ)=0\,,

(5.24)

\displaystyle g(h(V,Z),FW)=-JV(\ln\sigma)g(Z,W)-V(\ln\sigma)g(Z,TW)\,,\qquad\qquad

where
U,V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
,
X\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
and
Z,W\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
.

Proof.

Using (
2.4
),(
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h(U,V),FZ)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}V,FZ)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}V,JZ)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}V,TZ)\end{array}

for
U,V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
Z\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
. After some calculation, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h(U,V),FZ)=g(\nabla_{U}Z,JV)+g(\nabla_{U}TZ,V)\end{array}.

Here, we know
\nabla_{U}Z=U(\ln\sigma)Z
and
\nabla_{U}Z=U(\ln\sigma)TZ
from (
2.2
). Thus, we get

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h(U,V),FZ)=U(\ln\sigma)g(Z,JV)+U(\ln\sigma)g(TZ,V)=0\end{array},

since
g(Z,JV)=g(TZ,V)=0.
So, (
5.22
) follows.
Now, we prove (
5.24
). Let
V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
Z,W\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
. Then using (
2.4
),(
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h(V,Z),FW)=-g(\nabla_{Z}JV,W)-g(\nabla_{Z}V,TW)\end{array}.

Again, by (
2.2
), we easily get (
5.24
).
Similarly, we can obtain (
5.23
).
∎

The last lemma shows partially us the behavior of the second fundamental form
h
of the biwarped product generalized

J
-induced submanifolds of order 1 of type
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
in the normal subbundle
F(\mathcal{D}^{\theta}).

Remark 5.5
.

Equation (
5.19
) of Lemma
5.3
and equation (
5.22
) of Lemma
5.4
also hold
for skew CR-warped product submanifolds of Kähler manifolds, see Lemma 4 of
[
30
]
.
Moreover, equations (
5.22
) and (
5.24
) of Lemma
5.4
are also valid for warped product pointwise semi-slant submanifolds
in Kähler manifolds, see Lemma 5.3 of
[
31
]
.

By (
5.19
) and (
5.22
), we immediately get the following result.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
M
be a biwarped product generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1
in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)

such that the invariant normal subbundle
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{T}=\{0\}.
Then
M
is
\mathcal{D}^{T}
-geodesic.

Lastly, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for such submanifolds to be locally trivial.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
M
be a biwarped product proper generalized
J
-induced submanifold in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}

of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
such that the invariant normal subbundle
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{T}=\{0\}.

Then
M
is locally trivial if and only if
M
is both
(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
-mixed geodesic.

Proof.

Let
M
be a biwarped product proper generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1 in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}

of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
such that the invariant normal subbundle
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{T}=\{0\}.

If
M
is locally trivial, then the warping functions
f
and
\sigma
are constants.
By (
5.20
), we have
g(h(V,X),JY)=0
for
V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
, since
JV(\ln f)=0.

Taking into account the equation (
3.2
) and (
5.23
), we get
h(V,X)=0
.
It means that
M
is
(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
-mixed geodesic.

On the other hand, for any
V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
Z,W\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta},
we have
g(h(V,Z),FW)=0
from (
5.24
),
since
JV(\ln\sigma)=0
and
V(\ln\sigma)=0.
Taking into account the equation (
3.2
) and (
5.21
), we obtain

h(V,Z)=0
. Which says us
M
is
(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
-mixed geodesic.

Conversely, let
M
be both
(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
-mixed geodesic.
Then, for any
V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
, from (
5.20
) we conclude that
JV(\ln f)=0
, since
M
is

(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
-mixed geodesic. Hence, it follows that
f
is a constant. Since

M
is also
(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
-mixed geodesic, for
V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
Z,W\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta},
we have

(5.25)

\begin{array}[]{c}JV(\ln\sigma)g(Z,W)+V(\ln\sigma)g(Z,TW)=0\end{array}

from (
5.24
). If we put
V=JV
in (
5.25
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{c}-V(\ln\sigma)g(Z,W)+JV(\ln\sigma)g(Z,TW)=0\end{array}.

If we take
W=TW
in the last equation and use (
2.10
), the last equation becomes

(5.26)

\begin{array}[]{c}-V(\ln\sigma)g(Z,TW)-\cos^{2}\!\theta JV(\ln\sigma)g(Z,W)=0\end{array}.

From (
5.25
) and (
5.26
), we get

(5.27)

\begin{array}[]{c}\sin^{2}\!\theta JV(\ln\sigma)g(Z,W)=0\end{array}.

Since
M
is proper,
\sin\!\theta\neq 0.
So, we deduce that
JV(\ln\sigma)=0
from (
5.27
).
Hence, it follows that
\sigma
is a constant. Thus,
M
must be locally trivial, since we found the warping functions

f
and
\sigma
as constants.
∎

6.
An inequality for non-trivial biwarped product generalized

J
-induced submanifolds of order 1 in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}

In this section, by using the results given the preceding section, we shall establish an inequality for the squared
norm of the second fundamental form in terms of the warping functions for biwarped product generalized
J
-induced submanifolds of order 1
in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
,
where
M_{T}
is a holomorphic,
M_{\bot}
is a totally real and
M_{\theta}
is
a pointwise slant submanifold of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
.

Let
M
be a
(k+n+m)
-dimensional biwarped product generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1 of type

M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}
of a Kähler manifold
\bar{M}
and let

\{e_{1},...,e_{k},\tilde{e}_{1},...,\tilde{e}_{n},\bar{e}_{1},...,\bar{e}_{m},e_{1}^{*},...,e_{k}^{*},J\tilde{e}_{1},...,J\tilde{e}_{n},\hat{e_{1}},...,\hat{e_{l}}\}

be a canonical orthonormal basis of
\bar{M}
such that
\{e_{1},...,e_{k}\}

is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{D}^{T}
,
\{\tilde{e}_{1},...,\tilde{e}_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of

\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
,
\{\bar{e}_{1},...,\bar{e}_{k}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
,
\{J\tilde{e}_{1},...,J\tilde{e}_{n}\}
is
an orthonormal basis of
J\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
,
\{e_{1}^{*},...,e_{m}^{*}\}
is an orthonormal basis of

F\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
and
\{\hat{e_{1}},...,\hat{e_{l}}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{T}.

Here,
k=dim(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
n=dim(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
,
m=dim(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
and
l=dim(\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{T}).

Remark 6.1
.

In view of (
2.7
), we can observe that
\{Je_{1},...,Je_{k}\}
is
also an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{D}^{T}
. On the other hand,
with the help of (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we can
see that
\{\sec\!\theta T\bar{e}_{1},...,\sec\!\theta T\bar{e}_{m}\}
is also an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}

and
\{\csc\!\theta F\bar{e}_{1},...,\csc\!\theta F\bar{e}_{m}\}
is
also an orthonormal basis of
F\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
, where
\theta
is the slant function of
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
M
be a biwarped product proper generalized
J
-induced submanifold of order 1 in the form
M_{T}\times_{f}M_{\bot}\times_{\sigma}M_{\theta}

of a Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},J,g)
. Then the squared norm of the second fundamental form
h
of
M
satisfies

(6.1)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}\geq 2\{n\|\nabla(\ln f)\|^{2}+m(\csc^{2}\!\theta+\cot^{2}\!\theta)\|\nabla(\ln\sigma)\|^{2}\}

where
n=dim(M_{\bot})
and
m=dim(M_{\theta}).
The equality case of (
6.1
) holds identically if and only if the following assertions are true.

a)

M_{T}
is a totally geodesic submanifold in
\bar{M}.

b)

M_{\bot}
and
M_{\theta}
are totally umbilic submanifolds in
\bar{M}

with their mean curvature vector fields
-\nabla(\ln f)
and
-\nabla(\ln\sigma)
, respectively.

c)

M
is minimal in
\bar{M}.

d)

M
is
(\mathcal{D}^{\bot},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
-mixed geodesic.

Proof.

By the decomposition (
3.1
), the squared norm of the
second fundamental form
h
can be written as

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\,\|h(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{T})\|^{2}+\|h(\mathcal{D}^{\bot},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})\|^{2}+\|h(\mathcal{D}^{\theta},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\bigg\{\|h(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})\|^{2}+\|h(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})\|^{2}+\|h(\mathcal{D}^{\bot},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})\|^{2}\bigg\}.

In view of decomposition (
3.2
) and by (
5.19
)
\sim
(
5.24
), which can be explicitly written as follows:

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a,b,c=1}\!\!\!g(h(\tilde{e}_{a},\tilde{e}_{b}),J\tilde{e}_{c})^{2}+\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a,b=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r=1}g(h(\tilde{e}_{a},\tilde{e}_{b}),e_{r}^{*})^{2}

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a=1}g(h(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s}),J\tilde{e}_{a})^{2}+\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s,q=1}g(h(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s}),e_{q}^{*})^{2}

(6.2)

\displaystyle+2\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a,b=1}g(h(e_{i},\tilde{e}_{a}),J\tilde{e}_{b})^{2}+2\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}g(h(e_{i},\bar{e}_{r}),e_{s}^{*})^{2}

\displaystyle+2\!\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a,b,c=1}g(h(\tilde{e}_{a},\tilde{e}_{b}),J\tilde{e}_{c})^{2}+2\!\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a,b=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r=1}g(h(\tilde{e}_{a},\tilde{e}_{b}),e_{r}^{*})^{2}

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum^{k+n+m}_{A,B=1}\displaystyle\sum^{l}_{t=1}g(h(e_{A},e_{B}),\hat{e}_{t})^{2}.

Where the set
\{e_{A}\}_{1\leq A\leq(k+n+m)}
is an orthonormal basis of
M.
Hence, we get

(6.3)

\begin{array}[]{c}\|h\|^{2}\geq 2\bigg\{\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a,b=1}g(h(e_{i},\tilde{e}_{a}),J\tilde{e}_{b})^{2}+\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}g(h(e_{i},\bar{e}_{r}),e_{s}^{*})^{2}\bigg\}\end{array}.

Using (
5.20
) and Remark (
6.1
), we arrive

\begin{array}[]{c}\|h\|^{2}\geq 2\bigg\{\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{a,b=1}(Je_{i}(\ln f)g(\tilde{e}_{a},\tilde{e}_{b}))^{2}+\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}g(h(e_{i},\bar{e}_{r}),\csc\!\theta F\bar{e}_{s}))^{2}\bigg\}\end{array}.

from the inequality (
6.3
). Using (
5.24
) and after some calculation we find

(6.4)

\begin{array}[]{c}\|h\|^{2}\geq 2n\|\nabla(\ln\!f)\|^{2}+2\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}(\csc^{2}\!\theta)\bigg\{(Je_{i}(\ln\sigma)g(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s}))^{2}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad+2Je_{i}(\ln\sigma)g(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s})e_{i}(\ln\sigma)g(\bar{e}_{r},T\bar{e}_{s})+(e_{i}(\ln\sigma)g(\bar{e}_{r},T\bar{e}_{s}))^{2}\bigg\}.\end{array}

from the last inequality. Here,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}\bigg\{(Je_{i}(\ln\sigma)g(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s}))^{2}Je_{i}(\ln\sigma)g(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s})e_{i}(\ln\sigma)g(\bar{e}_{r},T\bar{e}_{s})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}g(\nabla\ln\sigma,Je_{i})g(\nabla\ln\sigma,e_{i})g(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s})g(\bar{e}_{r},T\bar{e}_{s})

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}\bigg\{\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}g(g(\nabla(\ln\sigma),e_{i})e_{i},J\nabla(\ln\sigma))\bigg\}g(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s})g(\bar{e}_{r},T\bar{e}_{s})

\displaystyle=-g(\nabla(\ln\sigma),J\nabla(\ln\sigma))\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{r,s=1}g(\bar{e}_{r},\bar{e}_{s})g(\bar{e}_{r},T\bar{e}_{s})=0.

Thus, by Remark (
6.1
), the equation (
2.11
) and the last yield, we deduce the inequality (
6.1
) from the inequality (
6.4
).

Next, from (
6.2
) we see that the equality case of (
6.1
) holds identically if and only if the following conditions hold.

(6.5)

\begin{array}[]{c}h(\mathcal{D}^{T},\mathcal{D}^{T})=\{0\},\qquad h(\mathcal{D}^{\bot},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})=\{0\},\qquad h(\mathcal{D}^{\theta},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})=\{0\}\end{array}

and

(6.6)

\begin{array}[]{c}h(\mathcal{D}^{\bot},\mathcal{D}^{\theta})=\{0\}.\end{array}

Since
M_{T}
is totally geodesic in
M
, from the first condition in (
6.5
) it
follows that
M_{T}
is also totally geodesic in
\bar{M}.
So, assertion
a)
follows.
Now, let
h^{\bot}
denote the second fundamental of
M_{\bot}
in
M
. We know that
h^{\bot}(\mathcal{D}^{\bot},\mathcal{D}^{\bot})\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{T}

from
[
21
]
. Then for
V\in\mathcal{D}^{T}
and
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\bot},
we have
g(h^{\bot}(X,Y),V)=g(\nabla_{X}Y,V).

Here, we know
\nabla_{X}Y=^{\bot}\!\nabla_{X}Y-g(X,Y)\nabla(\ln f)
from (
2.3
),
where
{}^{\bot}\nabla
is an induced connection on
M_{\bot}.
Hence, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{c}g(h^{\bot}(X,Y),V)=-V(\ln f)g(X,Y)=-g(g(X,Y)\nabla(\ln f),V)\end{array}.

It follows that

(6.7)

\begin{array}[]{c}h^{\bot}(X,Y)=-g(X,Y)\nabla(\ln f)\end{array}

from the last equation. Thus, combining the second condition in (
6.5
) and (
6.7
), we can deduce that

M_{\bot}
is a totally umbilic submanifold in
\bar{M}

with its mean curvature vector field
-\nabla(\ln f).
By a similar argument, we can find

M_{\theta}
as a totally umbilic submanifold in
\bar{M}
with its mean curvature vector field
-\nabla(\ln\sigma).

So, assertion
b)
is obtained. Assertions
c)
and
d)

immediately follow from (
6.5
) and (
6.6
), respectively.
∎

.

\mathcal{D}^{\theta}=\{0\}
, Theorem
6.2
coincides with Theorem 5.1 of

8
]
.
In other words, Theorem
6.2
is a generalization of Theorem 4.2 of
[
8
]
.
Moreover, Theorem
6.2
coincides with Theorem 5.2 of
[

]
if

Thus, Theorem

is also a generalization of Theorem 5.2 of

]
.

On the orbits of plane automorphisms and their stabilizers


Abstract
. Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be a perfect field with algebraic closure 𝕜 ¯ \overline{\Bbbk} . If H H is a subgroup of plane automorphisms over 𝕜 \Bbbk and p ∈ 𝕜 ¯ 2 p\in\overline{\Bbbk}^{2} is a point, we describe the subgroup consisting of plane automorphisms which stabilize the orbit of p p under H H , when this orbit has irreducible closure in 𝕜 ¯ 2 \overline{\Bbbk}^{2} . As an application, we treat the case where H H is cyclic and the closure of the orbit of p p is an arbitrary (non-necessarily irreducible) curve.

1.
Introduction

In
[
BlSt2015
]
the authors study the group of automorphisms
of the affine plane preserving some given curve over an arbitrary
field
\Bbbk
. As an application of their main results, they propose a
classification of the group of automorphisms stabilizing an arbitrary
set
\Delta\subset\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}^{2}
— here
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
denotes the algebraic
closure of
\Bbbk
. However, this classification (see

[
BlSt2015
, Prop. 3.11]
) is
incomplete, since the authors assume implicitly that

\Delta
is such that the Zariski closure of

\Delta
in
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}^{2}
coincides with the zero
locus of
I_{\mathbb{k}}(\Delta)
, the ideal of the
{\Bbbk}
–polynomials
vanishing at
\Delta
.

Inspired by the aim of completing the classification
mentioned above, we have been led to consider the following problem,
which is interesting in its own right and whose study is the objective
of this
paper:

If
H
is a subgroup of plane
automorphisms over a perfect field
{\mathbb{k}}
and
p\in\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}^{2}
, then
describe the stabilizer of its orbit, i.e. the set of plane
automorphisms which leave invariant
{\rm O}_{H}(p)=\bigl\{h(p):h\in H\bigr\}
.

In particular, we show that when
\Delta
is
not closed in
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}^{2}
an interesting phenomenon occurs: in many cases the group
of automorphisms stabilizing
\Delta
is infinite countable and
therefore not algebraic.

In Section
2
we present the basic definitions and
preliminary results concerning the automorphisms group of a
subset of the affine plane over
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
.

In Section
3
we present our main result, namely, we describe the stabilizer of
{\rm O}_{H}(p)
when it has irreducible closure in
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}^{2}
, see Theorem
3.4
. As an application, in Section
4
we consider the case where
H
is a cyclic group and the closure of
{\rm O}_{H}(p)
is a (non-necessarily irreducible) curve, see Theorem
4.8
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce the definition of the automorphisms group
of a subset of the affine plane
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
and recall the description of
the automorphisms group of a curve in
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.

Notation 2.1
.

The following notations will be kept through the paper.

(1)

In what follows
\Bbbk
is an arbitrary perfect field and
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
is an
algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{k}}
.

(2)

We let
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl({\mathbb{k}}[x,y]\bigr)
and denote
the set of
{\mathbb{k}}
-points and
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
-points in
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
by
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}({\mathbb{k}})

and
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
respectively.
Recall that
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
identifies with the
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
-points of

{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}=\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.

(3)

If
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, we denote the
vanishing
ideals
of
\Delta
by
\mathcal{I}(\Delta)\subset{\mathbb{k}}[x,y]
and
\mathcal{I}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(\Delta)\subset\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}[x,y]
. If

J\subset{{\Bbbk}[x,y]}
is an ideal, we denote
\mathcal{V}(J)=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl({\mathbb{k}}[x,y]/J\bigr)
viewed as a closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
, and
\mathcal{V}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(J)=\mathcal{V}(J)(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
the

zero set of
J
.

(4)

If
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, we set

\widehat{\Delta}=\mathcal{V}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}\bigl(I(\Delta)\bigr)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
; the Zariski
closure of
\Delta
in
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
is denoted by
\overline{\Delta}

— hence,
\overline{\Delta}\subset\widehat{\Delta}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
.

(5)

The
automorphisms
group
of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
is denoted by
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
— recall that
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
is the (abstract) group of isomorphisms of
{\mathbb{k}}
-schemes
f:{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.

The following well known result, that gives further insight on the
relationship between
\overline{\Delta}
and
\widehat{\Delta}
, follows from
Galois descent (see for example
[
Mi2017
, Appendix A.j]
); we include
its proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

Consider the canonical
action of

G={\rm Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. If
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
,
then
\widehat{\Delta}=G\cdot\overline{\Delta}
. In
particular,
every irreducible component of
\widehat{\Delta}
is the image of an
irreducible component of
\overline{\Delta}
under an element in

G
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\widehat{\Delta}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
is a

G
–stable closet subset
containing
\overline{\Delta}
. Therefore, by Galois descent,
there exists an unique closed subscheme
X\subset\operatorname{Spec}\bigl({\mathbb{k}}[x,y]\bigr)
such
that
\widehat{\Delta}
identifies with the set of
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
-points of

\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\times_{\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{k}})}X
. Under the identification
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}({\mathbb{k}})\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, we have that
X({\mathbb{k}})
is the set of
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
-points
of
\widehat{\Delta}
fixed by the Galois action and

\widehat{\Delta}=G\cdot\bigr(X\cap{\mathbb{A}}^{2}({\mathbb{k}})\bigr)
. It follows that
X({\mathbb{k}})=\overline{\Delta}\cap{\mathbb{A}}^{2}({\mathbb{k}})
and
G\cdot\overline{\Delta}=\widehat{\Delta}
. The last assertion is an easy
consequence of Galois descent.
∎

We introduce now the main object of study of this work, namely the
group of automorphisms that stabilize an arbitrary subset of

\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
-points of the
affine plane.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
a:{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\times{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
,
a(f,p)=f(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})(p)
, be the
canonical action of
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. If
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, we denote the
stabilizer
of
\Delta

under
a
by
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)
.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

Recall that an automorphism
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
is given by a
pair
(f,g)\in{\mathbb{k}}[x,y]\times{\mathbb{k}}[x,y]
, such that the
corresponding endomorphism of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}
is an automorphism,
with inverse
(f,g)^{-1}=(h,j)\in{\mathbb{k}}[x,y]\times{\mathbb{k}}[x,y]
.
Under this identification,
a\bigl((f,g),p\bigr)=\bigl(f(p),g(p)\bigr)
.

(2)

Let
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)
. If
\varphi^{*}:\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}[x,y]\to\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}[x,y]
denotes the induced automorphism (which
is defined over
{\mathbb{k}}
), then
\varphi^{*}
stabilizes the ideals

\mathcal{I}(\Delta)
and
\mathcal{I}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(\Delta)
.
If follows that
\varphi
stabilizes
\overline{\Delta}
and

\widehat{\Delta}
; therefore
\varphi^{*}
also stabilizes the ideals

\mathcal{I}(\widehat{\Delta})
and and
\mathcal{I}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(\widehat{\Delta})
.

In other words:

{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\overline{\Delta}\bigr)\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\widehat{\Delta}\bigr).

(1)

Notation 2.5
.

If
H\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
is a subgroup
and
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, we denote the
H
-orbit
of
p
as
{\rm O}_{H}(p)
.

Example 2.6
.

It is well known that the inclusions (
1
) may be
strict. For example if
{\mathbb{k}}={\mathbb{Q}}

and
\Delta=\bigl\{(\sqrt{2},0)\bigr\}
, then
\widehat{\Delta}=\bigl\{(\sqrt{2},0),(-\sqrt{2},0)\bigr\}

and

\varphi=(-x,y+x^{2}-2)\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\widehat{\Delta})\setminus{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)
.

The fact that the first inclusion may be strict is a key
point in our study of the stabilizers of orbits.

In
[
BlSt2015
]
the authors study the stabilizer of a closed subset
of the affine plane. In particular, they
give an explicit description of the geometrically irreducible curves
\mathcal{C}

such that
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)
is an algebraic
group. We briefly recall now these results — according to Remark
2.4
, we present an
element of
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
as a pair of polynomials
(f,g)\in{\mathbb{k}}[x,y]
.

Remark 2.7
.

If
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}({\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}})
is finite, then

{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)
is not an algebraic group (see

[
BlSt2015
, Proposition 3.11]
) — notice that
if
\Delta=\bigl\{(a_{i},b_{i})\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}});i=1,\ldots,\ell\bigr\}

and
m\gg 0
, then there exists a polynomial
P_{m}\in{\mathbb{k}}[x]
of degree
m
such that

P(a_{i})=0
for any
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. Therefore,
{\rm Aut}_{\mathbb{k}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)

contains the automorphisms
\bigl(x,y+P_{m}(x)\bigr)\in{{\Bbbk}[x,y]}
for all

m\gg 0
, and it follows from
[
Ka1979
, Theorem 1.3]
that
{\rm Aut}_{\mathbb{k}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)

is not algebraic.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
be a geometrically
irreducible and reduced curve. Then, up to applying a plane automorphism,

\mathcal{C}
is one of the curves of the following list:

(1)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(x^{b}-\lambda y^{a}\bigr)
, where

a,b>1
are coprime integers and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
. If this is
the case, then

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)=\bigl\{(t^{a}x,t^{b}y)\mathrel{:}t\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\bigr\}\cong{\mathbb{k}}^{*}.

(2)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(x^{b}y^{a}-\lambda\bigr)
, where

a,b\geq 1
are coprime integers, with
ab\neq 1
, and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
. If this is
the case, then

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)=\bigl\{(t^{a}x,t^{-b}y)\mathrel{:}t\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\bigr\}\cong{\mathbb{k}}^{*}.

(3)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(xy-\lambda\bigr)
, where

\lambda\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
. If this is
the case, then

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)=\bigl\{(tx,t^{-1}y)\mathrel{:}t\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\bigr\}\rtimes\{\operatorname{id},\sigma\}\cong{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},

where
\sigma(x,y)=(y,x)
is the permutation of coordinates.

(4)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(\lambda x^{2}+\nu y^{2}-1\bigr)
, where
{\rm char}({\mathbb{k}})\neq 2
, and
\lambda,\nu\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
are such that

-\lambda\nu
is not a square in
{\mathbb{k}}
. If this is
the case, then
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)

is the subgroup of

\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{k}})
preserving the form
\lambda x^{2}+\nu y^{2}
:

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)=\bigl\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&-\nu b\\
\lambda b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mathrel{:}(a,b)\in{\mathbb{k}}\times{\mathbb{k}}\,,\ a^{2}+\lambda\nu b^{2}=1\bigr\}\rtimes\left\{\operatorname{id},\tau\right\}=T_{\lambda,\nu}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},

where
\tau(x,y)=(x,-y)
. Notice that
T_{\lambda,\nu}
is a non-
{\mathbb{k}}
-split torus.

(5)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(x^{2}+\mu xy+y^{2}-1\bigr)
, where
{\rm char}({\mathbb{k}})=2
and
\mu\in\Bbbk^{*}

is such that
x^{2}+\mu x+1
has no root in
{\mathbb{k}}
. If this is
the case, then
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)

is the subgroup of

\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{k}})
preserving the form
x^{2}+\mu xy+y^{2}
:

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
b&a+\mu b\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mathrel{:}a,b\in{\mathbb{k}}\,,a^{2}+\mu ab+b^{2}=1\right\}\rtimes\left\{\operatorname{id},\sigma_{\mu}\right\}=T_{\mu}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},

where
\sigma_{\mu}(x,y)=(x+\mu y,y)
.
Notice that
T_{\mu}
is a non-
{\mathbb{k}}
-split torus.

(6)

\mathcal{C}
is the line of equation
x=0
, in which case

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)=\Bigl\{\bigl(ax,by+P(x)\bigr)\mathrel{:}a,b\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\,,\ P\in{\mathbb{k}}[x]\Bigr\}.

In particular, if
R:{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)\to{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{1})=\bigl\{y\mapsto by+c\mathrel{:}b\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\,,c\in{\mathbb{k}}\bigr\}
denotes the restriction map, then

\operatorname{Ker}(R)=\Bigl\{(x,y)\mapsto\bigl(ax,y+P(x)\bigr)\mathrel{:}a\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\,,\ P\in{\mathbb{k}}[x]\,,\ P(0)=0\Bigr\}

and

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)=\operatorname{Ker}(R)\rtimes{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}).

(7)

In any other case, the curve
\mathcal{C}
is such that
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)
is finite.

Proof.

See
[
BlSt2015
, Theorem 2]
.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

We will abuse notations and say that

D\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
is a
curve of type
(1) – (7), if
D=\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, where
\mathcal{C}
verifies the respective condition
in Theorem

. The

of a curve
of type (1) – (6) is the curve of equations given in the Theorem

for the corresponding type.

Following

]
, we say that a curve

is of

if up to applying an automorphism of

,

\mathcal{C}
has equation

, with

.

Corollary 2.10
.

Assume that
\Delta
is a proper closed subset of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. Then one of the following assertions holds:

(a)

\Delta
is contained in a curve of fence type, in which case
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)
is not an algebraic group.

(b)
The group
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\Delta)
is conjugate to an algebraic subgroup of either
{\rm Aff}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
or either
J_{n}
for some
n\geq 2
(notations as in
[
BlSt2015
]
).

Proof.

With the added hypothesis of the closedness of
\Delta
, the
proof of
[
BlSt2015
, Proposition 3.11]
now applies without obstructions.
∎

3.
The orbit of a group of automorphisms of the plane

Let
H\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
be a subgroup and
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. We are
interested now in the calculation of

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{H}(p)\bigr)
. Notice that we have that

H\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{H}(p)\bigr)\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}\bigr).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
be a subgroup and
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. Then

\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
is either:

(1)

a finite set;

(2)

a curve
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{C}_{s}
,
with
\mathcal{C}_{i}\cong\mathcal{C}_{j}
for all
i,j
, where

\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a curve of type (1) – (6);

(3)

{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
.

Proof.

Assume that

\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
is neither finite nor all the
plane; then
\dim\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}=1
and therefore
H
is
necessarily infinite. Let

\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the
decomposition in irreducible components, with
\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}=1
. By continuity and the
irreducibility of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, it follows that there exists
h_{i}\in H
such that
h_{i}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Hence, it remains to prove
that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a curve of type (1) – (6).

Let
H_{i}=\{h\in H\mathrel{:}h\cdot\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{i}\}\subset H
,
i=1,\dots s
. Since
H
is infinite, there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
\#H_{i}=\infty
. Let
h_{i}\in H_{i}
be a fixed element. Then

h_{i}^{-1}H_{i}\subset H_{1}\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})
, and it follows
that
\#{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})=\infty
. We deduce from Theorem
2.8

that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a curve of type (1) – (6).
∎

We collect now some technical remarks that we need in order
to calculate the stabilizers of an orbit.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a curve of type (1) – (6) given by its canonical
form and
p=(x_{p},y_{p})\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. If we keep the notations
of Theorem
2.8
, then the isotropy group
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C})_{p}
is trivial unless

(i)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(xy-\lambda\bigr)
, in which case

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)_{p}=\bigl\{{\rm Id},\frac{x_{p}}{y_{p}}\sigma\bigr\}
,
where
\sigma=(y,x)
is the permutation of coordinates.

(ii)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(\lambda x^{2}+\nu y^{2}-1\bigr)
, in which case

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)_{p}=\bigl\{{\rm Id},t_{\lambda x_{p}^{2}-\nu y_{p}^{2},2x_{p}y_{p}}\tau\bigr\}=\bigl\{{\rm Id},t_{2\lambda x_{p}^{2}-1,2x_{p}y_{p}}\tau\bigr\},

where
t_{a,b}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&-\nu b\\
\lambda b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in T_{\lambda,\nu}
and
\tau=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

(iii)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(x^{2}+\mu xy+y^{2}-1\bigr)
, in which case

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)_{p}=\bigl\{{\rm Id},t_{\mu x_{p}y_{p}+1,\mu y_{p}^{2}}\sigma_{\mu}\bigr\}=\bigl\{{\rm Id},t_{x_{p}^{2}+y_{p}^{2},\mu y_{p}^{2}}\sigma_{\mu}\bigr\},

where
t_{a,b}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
b&a+\mu b\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in T_{\mu}
and
\sigma_{\mu}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

(iv)

\mathcal{C}
is the line of equation
x=0
, in which case

\begin{split}{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)_{p}=&\ \Bigl\{\bigl(ax,by+P(x)\bigr)\mathrel{:}a,b\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\,,\ P\in{\mathbb{k}}[x],P(0)=-by_{p}\Bigr\}\cong\\
&\ \operatorname{Ker}(R)\rtimes\bigl\{(0,y)\mapsto(0,by+(1-b)y_{p})\mathrel{:}\ b\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\bigr\}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
G={\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)
. If
\mathcal{C}
is of
type (1) or (2), it is clear that
G_{p}=\{{\rm Id}\}
for any
p\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
.

(i)

Easy calculations show that if
p=(x_{p},y_{p})
is a
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
-point of
\mathcal{V}\bigl(xy-\lambda\bigr)
, then
G_{p}=\bigl\{{\rm Id},\frac{x_{p}}{y_{p}}\sigma\bigr\}
.

(ii)

Consider now
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(\lambda x^{2}+\nu y^{2}-1\bigr)
and let
p=(x_{p},y_{p})\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. Then

(T_{\lambda,\nu})_{p}=\{{\rm Id}\}
and an involution
t_{a,b}\tau=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&\nu b\\
\lambda b&-a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
fixes
p
if and only if
there exists
s\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-s\nu b&=&x_{p}\\
s(a-1)&=&y_{p}\\
\end{array}\right.

(2)

Indeed,
(-\nu b,a-1)
is a basis of the space of eigenvectors of eigenvalue

1
of
t_{a,b}\tau
.

In order to solve Equation (
2
), we first recall that we
also have the compatibility conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}a^{2}+\lambda\nu b^{2}&=&1\\
\lambda x_{p}^{2}+\nu y_{p}^{2}&=&1\\
\end{array}\right.

(3)

If
(a,b)\neq(1,0)
then we can eliminate
s
in Equation
(
2
). If we substitute
x_{p}
in Equation (
3
), we
obtain that
(a,b)=(\lambda x_{p}^{2}-\nu y_{p}^{2},2x_{p}y_{p})=(2\lambda x_{p}^{2}-1,2x_{p}y_{p})
.
Notice that since
a\neq 1
, we deduce that
p\neq\bigl(\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}},0\bigr)
. An easy calculations show
that
G_{{}_{(\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}},0)}}=\{{\rm Id},\tau\}
.

(iii)

If
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}\bigl(\lambda x^{2}+\nu y^{2}-1\bigr)
and
p=(x_{p},y_{p})\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, we proceed as in the previous case: we have that

(T_{\mu})_{p}=\{{\rm Id}\}
and

t_{a,b}\sigma_{\mu}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&\mu a+b\\
b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
fixes
p
if and only if
there exists
s\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}s(a-1)&=&x_{p}\\
sb&=&y_{p}\\
\end{array}\right.

with
compatibility conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}a^{2}+\mu ab+b^{2}&=&1\\
x_{p}^{2}+\mu x_{p}y_{p}+y_{p}^{2}&=&1\\
\end{array}\right.

If
(a,b)\neq(1,0)
then we can eliminate
s
and obtain that

(a,b)=(\mu x_{p}y_{p}+1,\mu y_{p}^{2})=(x_{p}^{2}+y_{p}^{2},\mu y_{p}^{2})
.
Notice that
t_{1,\mu}\sigma_{\mu}
has not
1
as eigenvalue, and that since
a\neq 1
, we deduce that
p\neq\bigl(1,0\bigr)
. An easy calculation show
that
G_{(1,0)}=\{{\rm Id},\sigma_{\mu}\}
.

(iv)

For the case
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}(x)
, just recall that

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)
is the semi-direct product of

\operatorname{Ker}(R)
and
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{1})
.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

(1) Let
\mathcal{C}
be a curve of type (3) – (5) and consider

G={\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C})
. Let
p\in\mathcal{C}
be such that

\overline{{\rm O}_{G}(p)}=\mathcal{C}
— it is clear that such a
p
exists. Then, it follows from Theorem

2.8
and Lemma
3.2
that

G=T\rtimes G_{p}=T\rtimes\{{\rm Id},\tau_{p}\}=T\cup T\tau_{p},

where
T
is a possibly non-split torus and
\tau_{p}
is the involution given in Lemma

3.2
.

Notice that
t\tau_{p}=t^{-1}\tau_{p}
for all
t\in T
.
In particular, any element of
T\tau_{p}
is also an involution and

{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C})=T\rtimes\{{\rm Id},t\tau_{p}\}
for all
t\in T
.

(2)
It follows that if
H\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C})
is a subgroup, then either
H\subset T
or

H=H_{0}\cup H_{0}\gamma=H_{0}\rtimes\{{\rm Id},\gamma\},

where
H_{0}=H\cap T
and
\gamma\in H
is a non trivial
involution. Indeed, if
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in H\cap(T\gamma)
, then

\gamma^{\prime}=h\gamma
, with
h\in H_{0}
.

(3) In particular, if a subgroup
H\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})

and
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
are such that
\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
is a curve
of type (3) – (5), it follows that either
H
is a subgroup of the
corresponding torus
T\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(0)}\bigr)

or

H=H_{0}\rtimes\{{\rm Id},t_{0}\tau_{p}\}=H_{0}\cup H_{0}t_{0}\tau_{p},

where
H_{0}=T\cap H_{0}
and
t_{0}\in T
.

Analogously, we have that
A={\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{H}(p)\bigr)
is either
a subgroup of
T
or

A=A_{0}\rtimes\{{\rm Id},t_{1}\tau_{p}\}=A_{0}\cup A_{0}t_{1}\tau_{p},

where
A_{0}=T\cap A_{0}
and
t_{1}\in T
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
H\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
be a subgroup and let
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})

such that
\mathcal{C}=\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
is an
irreducible curve. In the notations of Remark

3.3
, we have that:

(a)

If
\mathcal{C}
is a curve of type (1) or (2) then
A=H
.

(b)

If
\mathcal{C}
is a curve of type (3), (4) or
(5), then
G_{p}=\{{\rm Id},\tau_{p}\}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and:

(i)

If
H=H_{0}\subset T
then
A=H_{0}\cup H_{0}\tau_{p}=H_{0}\rtimes G_{p}
.

(ii)

If
H=H_{0}\rtimes G_{p}=H_{0}\cup H_{0}\tau_{p}
, then

A=H
.

(iii)

If
H=H_{0}\rtimes\{{\rm Id},t_{0}\tau_{p}\}=H_{0}\cup H_{0}t_{0}\tau_{p}
, with
t_{0}\notin H_{0}
, then

A=H
unless
t_{0}^{2}\in H_{0}
, in which case

A=\bigl\langle H_{0}\cup\{t_{0}\}\bigr\rangle\cup\bigr\langle H_{0}\cup\{t_{0}\}\bigr\rangle t_{0}\tau_{p}=\bigl\langle H_{0}\cup\{t_{0}\}\bigr\rangle\rtimes\{{\rm Id},t_{0}\tau_{p}\};

in particular,
A_{0}=\bigl\langle H_{0}\cup\{t_{0}\}\bigr\rangle
and

A_{0}/H_{0}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

(c)

If
\mathcal{C}
is a curve of type (6),
then

A=H(G_{p}\cap A).

In particular,
H\operatorname{Ker(R)}\subset A
.

Proof.

In what follows, we
keep the notations of
Theorem
2.8
and assume (without loss of generality) that
\mathcal{C}
is given by
its canonical form. It is
clear that
H\subset A\subset G
— the last inclusion follows by
continuity of the action; then

\mathcal{C}=\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}=\overline{{\rm O}_{A}(p)}=\overline{{\rm O}_{G}(p)}
.

(a)

If
\mathcal{C}=\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
is of type (1) or (2), it
easily follows that
G_{p}=\{{\rm Id}\}
. Thus, if
\varphi\in A
then

\varphi(p)\in{\rm O}_{H}(p)
, and therefore there exists
h\in H
such
that
h\varphi\in G_{p}
. It follows that
A=H
.

(b)

Let

G=T\rtimes\{Id,\tau_{p}\}
; since
H\subset A\subset G
, it follows
from Remark
3.3
that both
H

and
A
are determined by their intersections with
T
and the
involutions
t_{0}\tau_{p}
, with
t_{0}\in T
,
belonging to the group. Hence, we may distinguish three cases:

(i)

H=H_{0}
. If
s\in A_{0}
then
sp\in{\rm O}_{H}(p)
and therefore

sp=hp
for some

h\in H
, so
s=h\in H_{0}
. On the other hand
\tau_{p}(hp)=h^{-1}\tau_{p}p=h^{-1}p\in{\rm O}_{H}(p)
for any
h\in H
. It follows that
A=H\rtimes\{{\rm Id},\tau_{p}\}
.

(ii)

H=H_{0}\cup H_{0}\tau_{p}=H_{0}\rtimes G_{p}
. If

s\in A_{0}
then
sp=hp
for some
h\in H
. If
h\in H_{0}
we
deduce that
s=h\in H_{0}
. If
h=h_{0}\tau_{p}
,
then
hp=h_{0}p
and it follows that
s\in H_{0}
. Hence

A=H
.

(iii)

H=H_{0}\cup H_{0}t_{0}\tau_{p}=H_{0}\rtimes\{{\rm Id},t_{0}\tau_{p}\}
, where

t_{0}\notin H_{0}
.

If
s\in A_{0}
, then
sp=hp
for some
h\in H
. If
h\in H_{0}
we
deduce that
s=h\in H_{0}
. If
h=h_{0}t_{0}\tau_{p}
,
then
hp=h_{0}t_{0}p
and it follows that
s=h_{0}t_{0}
.
Hence,
s\in A_{0}
if and only if

t_{0}\in A_{0}
(because
h_{0}\in H_{0}
), and it remains to determine
under which conditions we have that

t_{0}\in A_{0}
.

Consider
h_{1}\in H_{0}
; then

t_{0}\cdot(h_{1}p)=h_{1}t_{0}p=h_{1}t_{0}\tau_{p}(p)\in{\rm O}_{H}(p)
(since
t_{0}\tau_{p}\in H
) and

t_{0}\cdot\bigl(h_{1}t_{0}\tau_{p}(p)\bigr)=h_{1}t_{0}^{2}\tau_{p}(p)=h_{1}t_{0}^{2}p
. Notice
that

h_{1}t_{0}^{2}p\in{\rm O}_{H}(p)
if and only if either

h_{1}t_{0}^{2}p\in{\rm O}_{H_{0}}(p)
, or there exists
h_{2}\in H_{0}
such that

h_{1}t_{0}^{2}p=h_{2}t_{0}\tau_{p}(p)=h_{2}t_{0}p
. In the first case
it follows that
t_{0}^{2}\in H_{0}
, and in the last case it follows that

h_{1}t_{0}^{2}=h_{2}t_{0}
, and therefore
t_{0}\in H_{0}
. Since

t_{0}h_{1}t_{0}\tau_{p}(p)=h_{1}t_{0}^{2}p
, we conclude that
t_{0}\in A_{0}
if
and only if
t_{0}^{2}\in H_{0}
.

(c)

If
\mathcal{C}
is the line of equation
x=0
and
\varphi=\bigl(ax,by+P(x)\bigr)\in A
, then
\bigr(0,by_{p}+P(0)\bigr)=h\cdot p
for some
h\in H
. It
follows that
h^{-1}\varphi\in G_{p}
and therefore

\varphi\in HG_{p}
; the result
follows.

∎

If
\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
is an irreducible curve of type (6), the
inclusion
H\operatorname{Ker}(R)\subset A
may be strict, as the
following example shows.

Example 3.5
.

Assume that
\operatorname{char}({\Bbbk})=0
and let

\varphi=(x,y+c)
. If
H={\langle\varphi\rangle}=\{\varphi^{n}\mathrel{:}n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
, then

\psi=(ax,ny)
belongs to
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{H}(0,0)\bigr)
for
all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
– indeed
\varphi^{n}(0,0)=(0,nc)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
H\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
be an infinite countable subgroup of plane
automorphisms and
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
a point such that
\mathcal{C}=\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
is an irreducible curve. Then the stabilizer
of
{\rm O}_{H}(p)
is not an algebraic group.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.4
that if
\mathcal{C}
is of
type (1)–(5) then
A
is infinite countable and therefore is not
algebraic. If
\mathcal{C}
is of type (6) then
A
contains

\operatorname{Ker}(R)
and therefore
A
contains automorphisms of
arbitrary degree; it follows from
[
Ka1979
, Theorem 1.3]
that
A

is not algebraic.
∎

4.
The orbit of an automorphism of the plane

Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
.
In this section, as an application of
Theorem
3.4
, we
study the geometry of the
orbit of
p
by
\varphi
. More precisely, we
consider
H=\langle\varphi\rangle
and describe

\mathcal{C}=\overline{{\rm O}_{H}(p)}
,
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C})
and

{\rm Aut}\bigr({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{H}(p)\bigr)
.
Along this section, we keep the previous notations and set
{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)={\rm O}_{H}(p)
.

As Lemma
3.1
indicates, the closure of the orbit

{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)
is either finite, a curve or the whole plane.
We begin by showing that under mild conditions, there exist pairs

p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
such that

{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)
is infinite and
\mathcal{C}=\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}
is
of type (1)–(6) — in particular,

{\rm Aut}\bigr({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{H}(p)\bigr)
is not algebraic (see Corollary
3.6
).

Example 4.1
.

(a)

Assume that
{\mathbb{k}}
is such that it contains an element

t_{0}\in{\mathbb{k}}

that is not a root of unity.
Then:

(i)

If
p=(x_{p},y_{p})
,
x_{p}y_{p}\neq 0
and

\varphi(x,y)=(t_{0}^{a}x,t_{0}^{b}y)
, with
a,b>1
coprimes,
then
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}(x^{b}-\frac{x_{p}^{b}}{y_{p}^{a}}y^{a})
.

(ii)

If
p=(x_{p},y_{p})
,
x_{p}y_{p}\neq 0
and

\varphi(x,y)=(t_{0}^{a}x,t_{0}^{-b}y)
, with
a,b\geq 1
coprimes,
then
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}(x^{b}y^{a}-x_{p}^{b}y_{p}^{a})
. Notice that
if
ab\neq 1
then
\mathcal{C}
is of type (2), whereas
if
a=b=1
, then
\mathcal{C}
is of type (3).

(iii)

If
p=(x_{p},y_{p})\neq(0,0)
, and

\varphi(x,y)=t_{0}\cdot(x,y)=(t_{0}x,t_{0}y)
, then
\mathcal{C}
is a line.

(b)

Assume now that
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable.

(i)

If
{\rm char}({\mathbb{k}})\neq 2
and there exist

\lambda,\nu\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
are such that

-\lambda\nu
is not a square in
{\mathbb{k}}
, consider

T_{\lambda,\nu}=\bigl\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&-\nu b\\
\lambda b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mathrel{:}(a,b)\in{\mathbb{k}}\times{\mathbb{k}}\,,\ a^{2}+\lambda\nu b^{2}=1\bigr\}
. If
\varphi=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&-\nu b\\
\lambda b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in T_{\lambda,\nu}
, then

\varphi
has eigenvalues
a\pm\sqrt{-\lambda\mu}b
. Since
{\Bbbk}

is uncountable, there exists
(a,b)\in{\Bbbk}^{2}
such that
a\pm\sqrt{-\lambda\mu}b
is not a root of unity, and therefore

(0,0)
is the unique periodic point of
\varphi
. It follows that
if
p=(x_{p},y_{p})\in\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}(\lambda x^{2}+\nu y^{2}-1)
, then
{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)
is infinite, with

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\mathcal{C}
;
in particular,
\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}
is of type (4).

(ii)

Analogously, if
{\rm char}({\mathbb{k}})=2
and
\mu\in\Bbbk^{*}

is such that
x^{2}+\mu x+1
has no root in
{\mathbb{k}}
, one can prove that there
exists
\varphi\in\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
b&a+\mu b\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mathrel{:}a,b\in{\mathbb{k}}\,,a^{2}+\mu ab+b^{2}=1\right\}
such that
(0,0)
is
the unique periodic point of
\varphi
, and therefore if
p\in\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}(x^{2}+\mu xy+y^{2}-1)
then
{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)
is infinite, with

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\mathcal{C}
;
in particular,
\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}
is of type (5).

If
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
, let
\mathcal{S}
be
the family of
\varphi
-stable curves and
\mathcal{P}
the set of
periodic points of
\varphi
. It is clear
that there exists

p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
such that
\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}={\mathbb{A}}^{2}

if and only if
\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{P}\neq{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
. This simple remark allows us to show that there exist
automorphisms of the plane
with orbits which closure is all the plane as follows.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
; for
n\geq 1
, we denote by
F_{n}
the (closed)
subset of fixed points of
\varphi^{n}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{P}=\bigcup F_{n}
.

Recall that the
(first)
dynamical degree
of
\varphi

is defined as

\lambda_{1}(\varphi)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sqrt[m]{\deg\varphi^{m}}
— notice that
\lambda_{1}(\varphi)>1
implies
\lambda_{1}(\varphi^{n})>1

for any
n\geq 1
; for more details see
[
DiFa2001
]
, where the complex case is treated. It follows from
[
Ca2010
, Thm. B]
and

[
Xi2015
, Thm. 6.5]
that if
\lambda_{1}(\varphi)>1
, then
\mathcal{S}
is a curve and
F_{n}
is finite. Therefore

\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{P}
is contained in a countable union of curves.
Hence, if
\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
is uncountable and
\lambda_{1}(\varphi)>1
, there
exists
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
such that
\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.

One particular example of this situation concerns the so-called Hénon
maps, that is morphisms of the form
\varphi=\bigl(y,-\delta x+P(y)\bigr)
, where
\delta\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
and
P\in{\Bbbk}[y]
is a polynomial of
degree greater or equal that
2
, since in this case
\lambda_{1}(\varphi)=\deg P
.

A priori
, the closure of an orbit
{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)
is not
necessarily an irreducible curve. In what follows, we exploit Lemma
2.2
in order to describe

\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}
.
If
{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)
is
finite then
\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})\cdot{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)
.
On the other hand, if
\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}={\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
, then

\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}
is the whole plane.
Next proposition deal with the case when

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}
is a curve.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}
be such that

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\mathcal{C}
is a curve, and let

\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{i}
and

\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the decomposition in
irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
and

\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}
respectively (see Lemma

2.2
). Then
s=k\ell
and, up to reordering the indexes, we
have that:

(1)

The morphism
\varphi
restricts to isomorphisms

\varphi|_{{}_{\mathcal{C}_{i+j\ell}}}:\mathcal{C}_{i+j\ell}\to\mathcal{C}_{i+j\ell+1}
and

\varphi|_{{}_{\mathcal{C}_{j\ell+\ell}}}:\mathcal{C}_{j\ell+\ell}\to\mathcal{C}_{j\ell+1}
, for
i=1,\ldots,\ell-1
,
j=0,\dots,k-1
.

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{i+j\ell}=\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}\bigl(\varphi^{i+j\ell-1}(p)\bigr)}

for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
,
j=0,\dots,k-1
.

(3)

The curves
\mathcal{C}_{i}
are isomorphic — in
particular, the curves have the same type.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we
assume that
p\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
. We reorder
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
in such a way that
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\varphi^{i-1}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
(see
Lemma
3.1
); then
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\varphi(\mathcal{C}_{\ell})
. It follows from Galois descent that

\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})\cdot\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{i},

where if
i\neq j
, then
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{i}\cap\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{j}
does not contain a curve. Since
\varphi
is fixed
by the
Galois action, it follows from Galois descent that if
i=1,\dots,\ell-1
, then

\varphi|_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}:\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{i+1}

induces an
{\mathbb{k}}
-isomorphism
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
. Assertion (1) follows; assertion (2) is clear.

In order to prove assertion (3), we first observe that
\mathcal{C}_{j\ell+1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{j\ell+\ell}
being isomorphic for

j=0,\dots k-1
, they have the same type. Up to reordering, we can
assume that there exists
g_{j}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}/{\mathbb{k}})
,
j=1,\dots,k-1
, such that
\mathcal{C}_{i+j\ell}=g_{j}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{i}
.
Since
\psi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},g_{j}\mathcal{C}_{1})
if only if
\psi=g^{-1}\psi g\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})
, the result follows.
∎

Example 4.4
.

Suppose that
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
is such that
\widehat{\Delta}
is a fence of
equation
F(x)=0
, with
F\in{\mathbb{k}}[x]
— then it follows from

[
BlSt2015
, Theorem 1]
that

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\widehat{\Delta}\bigr)=\bigl\{\bigl(\alpha x+\beta,\gamma y+P(x)\bigr)\mathrel{:}\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{k}}\,,\ F(\alpha x+\beta)/F(x)\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}\,,\ P\in{\mathbb{k}}[x]\bigr\}.

(a)

If
F=x-1
, then
\varphi=\bigl(\alpha x+\beta,\gamma y+P(x)\bigr)\in{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\widehat{\Delta})
if and only if
\alpha=1
and

\beta=0
. Choose
\gamma\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
which is not a root
of the unity and consider
p=(1,1)\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})
. Let

P\in{\mathbb{k}}[x]
be such that
P(1)=1
. Then
\varphi^{n}(p)\neq p

for all
n
and
{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)=\bigl\{(1,\sum_{i=0}^{n}\gamma^{i});n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr\}
. It follows that

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}=\widehat{\Delta}
. Thus, in this
case
\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}
is an irreducible curve, stable
by
\varphi
— in the notations of Proposition
4.3
,

\ell=s=1
. Recall that in this case the group

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)
is not
algebraic.

(b)

If
F=x^{2}-1
, then
\varphi=\bigl(\alpha x+\beta,\gamma y+P(x)\bigr)\in{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\widehat{\Delta}\bigr)
if and only if

\alpha=\pm 1
and

\beta=0
. Choose
\gamma\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
which is not a root
of the unity,
d\geq 1
, and consider the automorphism

\varphi_{d}=\bigl(-x,\gamma y+(x^{2}-1)^{d}\bigr)
. If
p=(1,y_{0})
, with
y_{0}\neq 0
, then

{\rm O}_{\varphi_{d}}(p)=\bigl\{\bigl((-1)^{n},\gamma^{n}y_{0}\bigr);n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr\}
is dense
in the fence
F(x)=0
. It follows that

\widehat{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}=\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\widehat{\Delta}
, and therefore it is a curve with two
irreducible components. Moreover, in the notations of Proposition

4.3
,
\ell=s=2
and once again

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)
is not algebraic because the orbit does not
depend on

.

The following example, very similar to the ones given above shows
that is not sufficient for

\overline{\Delta}
and
\widehat{\Delta}
to be equals.

Example 4.5
.

Assume that
{\Bbbk}\neq\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}
and let

\xi\in\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}\setminus{\Bbbk}
. Denote by
P\in{\Bbbk}[x]
the minimal polynomial
of
\xi
over
{\Bbbk}
and consider
\varphi=\bigl(x,\gamma y+P(x)\bigr)\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
, where
\gamma\in{\mathbb{k}}^{*}
is not a root of

\widehat{\Delta}=\mathcal{V}_{\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}}(P)

\Delta\subsetneq\overline{\Delta}\subsetneq\widehat{\Delta}

Notice that the automorphisms

Remark 4.6
.

Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and

be such that

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

is a curve. Then, by Proposition
4.3
, up to
reordering we can assume that

\mathcal{C}_{i}=\varphi^{i-1}\mathcal{C}_{1}

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}(p)}.

Moreover,
each curve

assume that

2.8
.

Since any automorphism

induces a

, we have
the following exact sequence of abstract groups:

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{i})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}\bigr)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}}

where
\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}
is group of permutations of
\ell

elements. Since
\varphi^{n\ell}\in{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}(\varphi^{i}(p))}\bigr)
, if follows that

\bigcap{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{i})
is an infinite subgroup of

{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})

Moreover,
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{i})=\varphi^{i-1}{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})\varphi^{1-i}
and if
\psi\in{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}\bigr)
, then

such that
\varphi^{i}\psi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})

\psi\in\varphi^{-i}{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})
.

Notation 4.7
.

If
G
is a group and
A,B\subset G
arbitrary subsets, we denote

AB=\{ab\mathrel{:}a\in A\,,\ b\in B\}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let

\varphi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and
p=(x_{p},y_{p})\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\bigl(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}}\bigr)
be such that

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

is a curve. Then up to applying an automorphism of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}

one of the following assertions holds:

(a)

\mathcal{C}_{1}
is of type (1) or (2) and
p\neq(0,0)
. In this case,

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)=\langle\varphi\rangle

(b)

\mathcal{C}_{1}

{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})_{p}=\{{\rm Id},\tau_{p}\}

3.2

(i)

\tau_{p}\varphi=\varphi^{i}\tau_{p}

(ii)

In other case,
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)=\langle\varphi\rangle
.

(c)

\mathcal{C}_{1}
is of type (6). In this case

\begin{split}{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)=&\ \bigcap_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\langle\varphi\rangle\Bigl(\varphi^{i}\Bigl(G_{p}\cap{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}(p)\bigr)\Bigr)\varphi^{-i}\Bigr)\\

=&\ \bigcap_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\langle\varphi\rangle\Bigl({\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}(p)\bigr)\varphi^{-i}\Bigr),\end{split}

where
G={\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)
.

Moreover, if
{\mathbb{k}}
is uncountable, there exist infinitely many

where
R:{\rm Aut}\bigr({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)\to{\rm Aut}\bigl(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\overline{{\mathbb{k}}})\bigr)
is the restriction morphism (see
2.8
).

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, we have that

\{\varphi^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is infinite and
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is of type (1) – (6).
Hence, it remains to
describe
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)
in each case.
We assume that
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\varphi^{i-1}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
, and

\varphi^{\ell}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})
(see Remark
4.6
).

(a)

If
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{V}(x^{b}-y^{a})

then, since
\varphi^{\ell}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})
, we have
that
\varphi^{\ell}(0,0)=(0,0)
and therefore
p\neq(0,0)
.
If
\psi\in{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)
, then by Remark

4.6

\psi=\varphi^{j}t
for some
t\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})
and some

n\in{\mathbb{Z}}

and

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi}(p)\bigr)=\langle\varphi\rangle

If
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is of type (2) an analogous argument applies.

(b)

Since

\overline{{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}(p)}=\mathcal{C}_{1}
and

\varphi^{\ell}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})

\varphi^{\ell}=t_{0}

If there exists

If

(c)

Assume now that

\varphi^{\ell}

.
Moreover, we deduce from Theorem

2.8

\varphi^{\ell}=\bigl(ax,by+P(x)\bigr)
, where
a,b\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
and

P\in{\Bbbk}[x]
— notice that

is not a root of unity different

\varphi^{n\ell}(p)=\bigr(0,b^{n}y_{p}+(1+\dots+b^{n-1})P(0)\bigr)

Analogously, since

\psi\in\bigcap_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\langle\varphi\rangle\Bigl(\varphi^{i}\Bigl(G_{p}\cap{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}(p)\bigr)\Bigr)\varphi^{-i}\Bigr)=\bigcap_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\langle\varphi\rangle\Bigl(\Bigl(G_{p}\cap{\rm Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{A}}^{2},{\rm O}_{\varphi^{\ell}}(p)\bigr)\Bigr)\varphi^{-i}\Bigr)

\psi=\bigl(a^{\prime}x,y+Q(x)\bigr)\in\operatorname{Ker}(R)

Remark 4.9

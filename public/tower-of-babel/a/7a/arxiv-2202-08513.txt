On the energy of Schwarzschild spacetime with the post-Newtonian approximation
I-Ching Yang

Abstract




ABSTRACT

With the post-Newtonian approxination, the energy of Schwarzschild spacetime in the Weinberg prescription
is obtained. The energy for the first post-Newtonian approximation
E^{(1)}=m
gives the Newtonian
treatment of Schwarzschild spacetime. However, for the second post-Newtonian approximation, the erergy
is shown that
E^{(1)}
adds extra terms
E^{(2)}
which consist of the energy stored in the configuration

E_{\rm config}
, in the gravitational field
E_{\rm field}
. and a term of surface integral. These extra terms
gives post-Newtonian corrections to the Newtonian treatment.

Keywords: post-Newtonian approximation of the Schwarzschild metric, the
Weinberg energy-momentum complex.

In special relativity, the physical quantity “mass” has two meanings. The “invariant mass”
m_{0}
(also called rest
mass) is, in effect, the resistance that a body of matter offers to change in its velocity upon the application of a
force, and the “relativistic mass”
m_{\rm ref}
depends on the observer’s inertial frame of reference for an object
moving at a velocity
\vec{v}
relative to the observer. Although, general relativity does not offer a single
definition of the term mass, but offers several different definitions that are applicable under different circumstances,
like ADM
[
1
]
and Bondi masses
[
2
]
in asymptotically flat spacetimes, and Komar mass
[
3
]

in stationary spacetimes, etc. Because of mass-energy equivalence, the concepts of enerrgy and mass are indistinguishable.
So, similar tricky situation also would come about the definition of energy in general relativity.

There are two famous ideas on the definition of the localized energy-momentum associated with the gravitational field,
which are energy-mometum complex and quasilocal energy.
One idea, called
energy-momentum complex
, is introduced

\Theta^{\mu}_{\nu}=\sqrt{-g}\left(T^{\mu}_{\nu}+t^{\mu}_{\nu}\right)

(1)

which satisfies the differential conservation form
\partial_{\nu}\Theta^{\mu}_{\nu}=0
.
Here
T^{\mu}_{\nu}
is the energy-momentum tensor of matter and
t^{\mu}_{\nu}
is the energy-momentum
pseudotensor from the gravitational field.
There have been many proposals of energy-momentum complex including Einstein

Another ideaa,
quasilocal energy

H({\bf N},\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}N^{\mu}{\cal H}_{\mu}+\oint_{\partial\Sigma}{\cal B}({\bf N}),

(2)

which generates the spacetime displacement of a finite spacelike hypersurface
\Sigma
along a vector field
N^{\mu}

Im my recent study, according to the first post-Newtonian approximation of Schwarzschild metric,
the energy components of the Einstein and Møller energy-momentum complex were obtained,
which involve the rest-mass energy
m
, the energy stored in the configuration and that in the gravitational field.
Thus, in this article, I will estimate the energy component of the Weinberg energy-momentum complex
with the first and second post-Newtonian approxination of Schwarzschild metric.

In oder to suppose a coordinate system that is quasi-Minkowskian, the metric was given as

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},

(3)

where
\eta_{\mu\nu}
is the Minkowski metric.
Weinberg adopt the energy-momentum pseudotensor of the gravitational field

t_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\left[R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R^{\lambda}_{\lambda}-{R^{(1)}}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}{R^{(1)}}^{\lambda}_{\lambda}\right],

(4)

and the part of the Ricci tensor linear in
h_{\mu\nu}

{R^{(1)}}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}h^{\lambda}_{\lambda}}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial x^{\nu}}-\frac{\partial^{2}h^{\lambda}_{\mu}}{\partial x^{\lambda}\partial x^{\nu}}-\frac{\partial^{2}h^{\lambda}_{\nu}}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial x^{\lambda}}+\frac{\partial^{2}h^{\mu}_{\nu}}{\partial x^{\lambda}\partial x^{\lambda}}\right).

(5)

Hence the Winberg energy-momentum complex
[
9
]
can be written as

\Theta^{\mu\nu}={R^{(1)}}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\mu\nu}{R^{(1)}}^{\lambda}_{\lambda}=\partial_{\rho}Q^{\rho\mu\nu}

(6)

with Winberg’s superpotential is

Q^{\rho\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\partial h^{\lambda}_{\lambda}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\eta^{\rho\nu}-\frac{\partial h^{\lambda}_{\lambda}}{\partial x^{\rho}}\eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial h^{\lambda}_{\mu}}{\partial x^{\lambda}}\eta^{\rho\nu}+\frac{\partial h^{\lambda}_{\rho}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\eta^{\mu\nu}+\frac{\partial h^{\mu}_{\nu}}{\partial x^{\rho}}-\frac{\partial h^{\rho}_{\nu}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\right]

(7)

Thus, the energy component of the Weinberg energy-momentum complex within the region
\Sigma
is shown

\begin{split}E&=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\Theta^{00}d^{3}x=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}Q^{i00}d^{3}x,\\
&=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{ik}}{\partial x^{k}}-\frac{\partial h_{kk}}{\partial x^{i}}\right)d^{3}x.\end{split}

(8)

For a weak-field case, the first post-Newtonian approximation of the Schwarzschild metric
[
9
]
is studied

\begin{split}g_{00}&=1-2\phi+{\cal O}(\phi^{2})\\
g_{0i}&=0\\
g_{ij}&=(-1-2\phi)\delta_{ij}+{\cal O}(\phi^{2}),\end{split}

(9)

and the qualities
h_{\mu\nu}
are given as

\begin{split}h_{00}&=-2\phi+{\cal O}(\phi^{2})\\
h_{ij}&=-2\phi\delta_{ij}+{\cal O}(\phi^{2}),\end{split}

(10)

where
\phi
is the Newton’s gravitational potential and normalized such that
\phi(\infty)=0
.
Then Winberg’s superpotential is obtained

Q^{i00}=2\nabla\phi,

(11)

and the energy component of the Weinberg energy-momentum complex is described to

{}_{W}\Theta^{0}_{0}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\nabla^{2}\phi.

(12)

Therefore, in the Winberg prescription, the energy within the region
\Sigma
that includes mass distribution is given by

E=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla^{2}\phi d^{3}x.

(13)

Since the gravitational potential satisfes the field equation with a matter density
\rho

\nabla^{2}\phi=4\pi\rho

(14)

and the total mass is given by

\int_{\Sigma}\rho d^{3}x=m.

(15)

Such, in first post-Newtonian approximation, the energy in the Winberg prescription can be rewritten

E=m,

(16)

and the energy is contributed by the “rest-mass energy” as the invariant mass is the rest energy in special relativity.

To study the contribution other than the rest-mass energy, the second post-Newtonian approximation of the Schwarzschild
metric
[
9
]
is considered

\begin{split}g_{00}&=1-2\phi+2\phi^{2}+{\cal O}(\phi^{3}),\\
g_{0i}&=0,\\
g_{ij}&=(-1-2\phi-\phi^{2})\delta_{ij}-\phi^{2}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{2}}+{\cal O}(\phi^{3}),\end{split}

(17)

and the qualities
h_{\mu\nu}
are obtained as

\begin{split}h_{00}&=-2\phi+2\phi^{2}+{\cal O}(\phi^{3}),\\
h_{ij}&=(-2\phi-\phi^{2})\delta_{ij}-\phi^{2}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{2}}+{\cal O}(\phi^{3}).\end{split}.

(18)

Here the Winberg’s superpotential is computed

Q^{i00}=2\nabla\phi+2\phi\nabla\phi-\frac{2\phi^{2}}{r}\hat{r}..

(19)

The energy component of the Møller energy-momentum complex is obtained

{}_{W}\Theta^{0}_{0}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\nabla^{2}\phi+\frac{1}{4\pi}\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla\phi)-\frac{1}{4\pi}\nabla\cdot\frac{\phi^{2}}{r}\hat{r}

(20)

and the energy within the region
\Sigma
is followed

E=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla^{2}\phi d^{3}x-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla\phi)d^{3}x.+\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\phi^{2}}{r}\right)d^{3}x.

(21)

Although, the second term of the right-hand side in Eq. (19) can be rewriiten as

\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla\phi)d^{3}x=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\phi\nabla^{2}\phi d^{3}x+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}(\nabla\phi)^{2}d^{3}x.

(22)

and the first term of right-hand side in Eq.(20) is the energy stored in the configuration

E_{\rm config}\equiv\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\phi\nabla^{2}\phi d^{3},

(23)

and the second term is the energy stored in the gravitational field

E_{\rm field}\equiv\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}(\nabla\phi)^{2}d^{3}x.

(24)

The third term of the right-hand side in Eq.(19) can be expressed in terms of surface integral

\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\phi^{2}}{r}\right)d^{3}x=\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\frac{\phi^{2}}{r}\hat{r}\cdot d\hat{a}.

(25)

Consequently, in second post-Newtonian approximation, the energy in the Winberg prescription within the
region
\Sigma
is shown as

E=m+2E_{\rm config}+2E_{\rm field}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\frac{\phi^{2}}{r}\hat{r}\cdot d\hat{a},

(26)

and resembles the energy in the Einstein prescription

E_{\rm E}=m+E_{\rm config}+E_{\rm field}+\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\frac{\phi^{2}}{r}\hat{r}\cdot d\hat{a}.

(27)

In summary, the energy of the Schwarzschild spacetime for the first post-Newtionian approximation by using the
Weinberg energy-momentum complex is obtained

E^{(1)}=m,

(28)

and gives the Newtonian treatmenet of Schwarzschild spacetime
[
22
]
. The energy for the second post-Newtionian
approximation is that
E^{(1)}
adds extra terms

E^{(2)}=2E_{\rm config}+2E_{\rm field}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\frac{\phi^{2}}{r}\hat{r}\cdot d\hat{a},

(29)

and
E^{(2)}
gives post-Newtonian corrections to the Newtonian treatemnt
[
22
]
.
Referrung to quantum electrodynamics,
E^{(1)}
and
E^{(1)}+E^{(2)}
behave like bare mass and experimentally observable
masss, and
E^{(2)}
is the increase in mass owing to the interaction of the matter with the field.
Particularly, the term of surface integral in Eq.(29) could be
suggested as the flux of
(\phi^{2}/r)\hat{r}
through a surface
\partial\Sigma
. Thus, for energy-momentum complexes, to
study the energy in higher-order post-Newtonian approximation will be useful to clarify their physical meanings.

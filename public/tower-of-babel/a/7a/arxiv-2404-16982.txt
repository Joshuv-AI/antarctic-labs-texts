Abstract
. In this article we generalize the q q -difference operator due to Carlitz in order to derive explicit sum formulae for several extensions of Stirling numbers of the second kind, including complete homogeneous symmetric functions, complementary symmetric functions, r r -Whitney numbers and elliptic analogues of rook, Stirling and Lah numbers. Furthermore, we generalize Carlitz’ q q -Eulerian numbers to a Lagrange polynomial extension. We define them by generalizing Worpitzky’s identity appropriately, and derive a recursion and an explicit sum formulae. Special cases include r r -Whitney Eulerian numbers and elliptic Eulerian numbers.

1.
Introduction

The classical
Stirling numbers of the second kind
,
S(n,k)
, count set partitions of an
n
-elementary set into
k
non-empty sets. They fulfil the recurrence relation

\displaystyle S(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

(1.1a)

\displaystyle S(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(1.1b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle S(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=S(n,k-1)+kS(n,k).

(1.1c)

Given a nonnegative integer
n
and a variable
z
, we define the corresponding
falling factorial
as

z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}z(z-1)\cdots(z-n+1)

for
n>0
and
z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{0}=1
. Then,
S(n,k)
are the connection coefficients in

\displaystyle z^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}.

(1.2)

Let
E_{z}
be the shift operator that shifts a polynomial
p(z)
by
E_{z}p(z)=p(z+1)
and
\Delta
be the difference operator
\Delta=E_{z}-I
. Then it is a classical result that we can derive an explicit formula for
S(n,k)
from (
1.2
) by applying the
k
-th power of the difference operator
\Delta
to both sides of the equation to obtain

\displaystyle S(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}(k-j)^{n}.

(1.3)

We recommend
[
10
,
25
]
for further reading on the theory of difference operators.

Following Carlitz
[
5
,
6
]
, we can define
q
-Stirling numbers of the second kind
,
S_{q}(n,k)
, by

[z]_{q}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{\binom{k}{2}}S_{q}(n,k)[z]_{q}[z-1]_{q}\dots[z-k+1]_{q},

(1.4)

where

[z]_{q}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\frac{1-q^{z}}{1-q}

(1.5)

is the
q
-number of
z
.
We define
q
-falling factorials
by

z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{q}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}z(z-[1]_{q})\cdots(z-[n-1]_{q}),\text{ for $n>0$,}\quad z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{0}^{q}=1,

(1.6)

and use the identity

[m]_{q}-[n]_{q}=[m-n]_{q}q^{n}

to reformulate equation (
1.4
) as

[z]_{q}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{q}(n,k)[z]_{q}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{q}.

(1.7)

One can derive from this relation that
S_{q}(n,k)
satisfies

\displaystyle S_{q}(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

(1.8a)

\displaystyle S_{q}(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(1.8b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle S_{q}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=S_{q}(n,k-1)+[k]_{q}S_{q}(n,k).

(1.8c)

In order to derive an explicit sum formula for these numbers, Carlitz generalizes the difference operator
\Delta
by the recursive definition

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}p(z)

\displaystyle=p(z+1)-p(z),

(1.9a)

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}^{n+1}p(z)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{q}^{n}p(z+1)-q^{n}\Delta_{q}^{n}p(z)

(1.9b)

for
n>0
, or explicitly by

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(E_{z}-q^{n-i}).

(1.9c)

Applying
\Delta_{q}^{k}
to both sides of
(
1.4
)
, Carlitz derives (
[
6
, Equation (3.3)]
)

\displaystyle S_{q}(n,k)=\frac{q^{-\binom{k}{2}}}{[k]_{q}!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{\binom{j}{2}}{\begin{bmatrix}k\\
j\end{bmatrix}}_{q}[k-j]_{q}^{n},

(1.10)

where the
q
-factorials
are defined by

[n]_{q}!\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[i]_{q}\quad\text{for $n>0$ and}\quad[0]_{q}!=1,

(1.11)

and the
q
-binomial coefficient is defined as

{\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}}_{q}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!}.

(1.12)

In
[
15
]
, Kereskényiné Balogh and Schlosser study an elliptic analogue of Carlitz’
q
-Stirling numbers of the second kind. We introduce
elliptic falling factorials
by the definition

z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{a,b;q,p}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}z(z-[1]_{a,b;q,p})\cdots(z-[n-1]_{a,b;q,p}),

and
z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{0}^{a,b;q,p}=1
, where
[z]_{a,b;q,p}
is the elliptic number of
z
, which will be defined in the next section.
Then, the
elliptic Stirling numbers of the second kind
,
S_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)
, can be defined by the relation

[z]_{a,b;q,p}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)[z]_{a,b;q,p}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{a,b;q,p}.

(1.13)

In
[
32
]
, Schlosser and Yoo study elliptic Stirling numbers of the second kind in the context of rook theory. They ask for a generalization of the explicit sum formula (
1.10
) to
S_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)
and work out special cases for
0\leq k\leq 3
. Furthermore, the authors suggest using an appropriate extension of the
q
-difference operator
\Delta_{q}
to achieve this goal.

In this article, we deduce an explicit formula for
S_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)
analogous to (
1.10
) by using the fact that elliptic Stirling numbers of the second kind are a specialization of the complete homogeneous symmetric functions
h_{k}
. An explicit sum-formula for
h_{k}
is well-known, and we obtain a formula for
S_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)
as a special case. Furthermore, we will generalize Carlitz’
q
-difference operator appropriately to deduce a new operator proof of the formula for
h_{k}
. Special cases also contain Stirling numbers of type
B
and
(q,r)
-Whitney numbers of the second kind.

We show that this operator proof can be generalized to prove explicit sum formulae for more general polynomials, including elliptic rook numbers and Lah numbers.

Another very interesting number sequence linked to Stirling numbers is the Eulerian number sequence. They were also generalized to the
q
-case by Carlitz
[
7
]
but an elliptic extension of these numbers has not been defined so far. In Section
6
we study a generalization of Eulerian numbers similarly to the generalization of Stirling numbers of the second kind and derive explicit summation formulae. As special cases, we obtain elliptic Eulerian numbers and a
q
-analogue of
r
-Whitney Eulerian numbers.

2.
Elliptic Stirling numbers

In the past 15 years, several authors have studied elliptic function analogues of
q
-analogues of combinatorial objects and special combinatorial numbers
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
17
,
27
,

,

. This has led to interesting extensions of weight functions assigned to several combinatorial models like lattice paths, lozenge tilings or rook placements. This study is closely connected to the theory of elliptic hypergeometric series, which naturally extends the theory of basic hypergeometric series. An introduction to elliptic hypergeometric series can be found in
[
13
, Chapter 11]
,
[
16
]
and
[
24
]
. In
[
15
]
and
[
32
]
, elliptic analogues of Stirling numbers, Lah numbers, and rook numbers were introduced. A main goal of this article is to find explicit summation formulae of these analogues. In this section, we will recall the most important notations and identities for the theory of elliptic functions.

We begin by defining the
modified Jacobi theta function
as

\theta(x;p)\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\prod_{j\geq 0}\left((1-p^{j}x)(1-\frac{p^{j+1}}{x})\right),\quad\theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell};p)=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}\theta(x_{k};p),

where
x,x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\neq 0
and
|p|<1
.
The modified Jacobi theta function satisfies some fundamental
relations
[
34
, cf. p. 451, Example 5]
, like the inversion formula

\displaystyle\theta(x;p)=-x\theta(1/x;p),

(2.1a)

the quasi-periodicity relation

\displaystyle\theta(px;p)=-\frac{1}{x}\theta(x;p),

(2.1b)

and the three-term identity

\displaystyle\theta(xy,x/y,uz,u/z;p)=\theta(uy,u/y,xz,x/z;p)+\frac{x}{z}\theta(zy,z/y,ux,u/x;p).

(2.1c)

An
elliptic function
is a function of a complex variable that is
meromorphic and doubly periodic, and it is well known that elliptic functions can
be written as quotients of modified Jacobi theta functions
[
24
,
34
]
.

An example, which we will use throughout this article, is the
elliptic number
of a complex number
z
,

\displaystyle[z]_{a,b;q,p}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\frac{\theta(q^{z},aq^{z},bq,aq/b;p)}{\theta(q,aq,bq^{z},aq^{z}/b;p)}.

(2.2)

Here,
a
and
b
are two additional parameters.

1

and corresponds to the definition in
[

]

The elliptic number is a generalization of the
q

, one recovers the

The function

We also define the elliptic weight

W_{a,b;q,p}(k)\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\frac{\theta(aq^{2k+1},b,bq,a/b,aq/b;p)}{\theta(aq,bq^{k},bq^{k+1},aq^{k}/b,aq^{k+1}/b;p)}q^{k},

As an application of the three-term identity (

\displaystyle[y+z]_{a,b;q,p}=[y]_{a,b;q,p}+W_{a,b;q,p}(y)[z]_{aq^{2y},bq^{y};q,p}.

(2.4)

In this article we will also use the relations (

, Equation (3.3b)]
)

W_{a,b;q,p}(k+j)=W_{a,b;q,p}(j)W_{aq^{2j},bq^{j};q,p}(k)

(2.5)

and (

[-k]_{a,b;q,p}=-W_{a,b;q,p}(-1)[k]_{1/a,b/a;q,p}.

(2.6)

Now we are ready to define elliptic Stirling numbers of the second kind. Recall the definition of
elliptic falling factorials

z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{a,b;q,p}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}z(z-[1]_{a,b;q,p})\cdots(z-[n-1]_{a,b;q,p})\quad\text{and}\quad z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{0}^{a,b;q,p}=1.

and the definition of
elliptic Stirling numbers of the second kind
,

[z]_{a,b;q,p}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)[z]_{a,b;q,p}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{a,b;q,p}.

(2.7)

Note that each factor
[z]_{a,b;q,p}-[j]_{a,b;q,p}
in the elliptic falling factorial of

factorizes to

\displaystyle[z]_{a,b;q,p}-[j]_{a,b;q,p}=W_{a,b;q,p}(j)[z-j]_{aq^{2j},bq^{j};q,p}

by (

).

From (

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

\displaystyle S_{a,b;q,p}(n+1,k)

(2.8c)

3.
Complete homogeneous symmetric functions

Complete homogeneous symmetric functions,
h_{k}(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})
, are defined by

h_{k}(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})=\sum_{0\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\dots\leq j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\dots a_{j_{k}}.

We introduce a generalization of falling factorials and factorials for a series of variables
\textbf{a}=a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots
(following
[
26
]
) as

z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{0}^{\textbf{a}}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}1,\quad z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{\textbf{a}}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(z-a_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad{a}_{n}!\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}a_{n}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{\textbf{a}}.

(3.1)

It is a well-known fact that
h_{k}
satisfies the relation

\displaystyle z^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{n-k}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}.

(3.2)

This equation appears, for example, in
[
26
, Theorem 2.2]
and
[
12
, p. 208]
.
By setting
a_{i}=[i]_{q}
for all
i\geq 0
and
z=[z]_{q}
in this equation, we obtain the generating function (
1.7
) of Carlitz’
q
-Stirling numbers of the second kind. This leads to the specialization

S_{q}(n,k)=h_{n-k}([0]_{q},[1]_{q},\dots,[k]_{q}),

which is also well known and appeared, for example, recently in
[
26
]
.

By setting
a_{i}=[i]_{a,b;q,p}
for all
i
and
z=[z]_{a,b;q,p}
in (
3.2
), we obtain the generating function for elliptic Stirling numbers of the second kind (
1.13
), therefore

\displaystyle S_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)=h_{n-k}([0]_{a,b;q,p},[1]_{a,b;q,p},\dots,[k]_{a,b;q,p}).

(3.3)

By multiplying both sides of (
3.2
) with
z
it follows that

\displaystyle z^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{n-k}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}(z-a_{k}+a_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{n-k}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k+1}^{\textbf{a}}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}h_{n-k}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\big(h_{n-k+1}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1})+a_{k}h_{n-k}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\big)z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}

and we can derive the recursion

\displaystyle h_{n-k}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

(3.4a)

\displaystyle h_{0}(a_{0})

\displaystyle=1

(3.4b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle h_{n-k+1}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})

\displaystyle=h_{n-k+1}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1})+a_{k}h_{n-k}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})

(3.4c)

analogous to (
1.1
), (
1.8
) and (
2.8
).

By setting

4.
A generalized difference operator

We consider functions
f(z;\textbf{x})
in the variables
z
and
\textbf{x}=x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\dots
and recall the generalized notation for the falling factorial and the factorial according to
z
and
x
as follows:

z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{0}^{\textbf{x}}=1,\quad z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{\textbf{x}}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(z-x_{i})\quad\text{for $n>0$ and}\quad{x}_{n}!=x_{n}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{\textbf{x}}.

(4.1)

We define the
shift operator

E_{z,\textbf{x}}
as
E_{z,\textbf{x}}f(z;x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\dots)=f(z+1;x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\dots)
. For example,

E_{z,\textbf{x}}\frac{z-x_{1}+a_{2}}{x_{k}-x_{2}}=\frac{z+1-x_{2}+a_{2}}{x_{k+1}-x_{3}}.

The
difference operator

\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{n}
is recursively defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textbf{x}}f(z,\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=E_{z,\textbf{x}}f(z,\textbf{x})-f(z,\textbf{x}),

(4.2a)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{n+1}f(z,\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=E_{z,\textbf{x}}\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{n}f(z,\textbf{x})-\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{x_{n+1}-x_{j+1}}{x_{n}-x_{j}}\Big)\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{n}f(z,\textbf{x})

(4.2b)

for
n>0
, or explicitly by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big(E_{z,\textbf{x}}-\prod_{j=0}^{n-i-2}\frac{x_{n-i}-x_{j+1}}{x_{n-i-1}-x_{j}}\Big).

(4.2c)

It follows by induction on
n
(and (
3.8
)) that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{x}_{n}!\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}(x_{k}-x_{i})^{-1}\Big)E_{z,\textbf{x}}^{k}.

(4.3)

We are now ready to prove the explicit formula for
h_{n}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
C_{k}
be the connection coefficients in

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}C_{k}a_{z}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}},

(4.4)

where
f(z)
is a polynomial in
a_{z}
of degree at most
n
and independent of the variables
x
. Then the
C_{k}
are independent of
z
and
x
.
We apply
\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{j}
to both sides of (
4.4
) and set
z=0
and
x_{i}=a_{i}
for
i\geq 0
afterwards to obtain

\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{j}f(z)\big|_{z=0,x_{i}=a_{i}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}C_{k}\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{j}a_{z}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}\big|_{z=0,x_{i}=a_{i}}

since
C_{k}
is independent of
z
and
x_{i}
. The above notation means that we set
z=0
and
x_{i}=a_{i}
for
i\geq 0
after applying the operators to both sides. Evaluating the operator on the right hand side gives

\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{j}a_{z}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}=\sum_{s=0}^{j}{x}_{j}!\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq s\end{subarray}}^{j}(x_{s}-x_{i})^{-1}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(a_{z+s}-a_{i}).

In this expression we can set
z=0
and
x_{i}=a_{i}
for
i\geq 0
to obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{j}a_{z}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}\big|_{z=0,x_{i}=a_{i}}=\sum_{s=0}^{j}{a}_{j}!\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(a_{s}-a_{i})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq s\end{subarray}}^{j}(a_{s}-a_{i})}.

We want to show that this sum simplifies to
\delta_{j,k}{a}_{j}!
, where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta.

Suppose
0\leq j<k
. Then we have a factor
(a_{s}-a_{s})=0
in the numerator of each summand, and the sum vanishes.

For
k=j
the sum reduces to
a_{k}!
by the same argument.

Suppose
k<j\leq n
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{j}{a}_{j}!\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(a_{s}-a_{i})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq s\end{subarray}}^{j}(a_{s}-a_{i})}=\sum_{s=k}^{j}{a}_{j}!\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(a_{s}-a_{i})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq s\end{subarray}}^{j}(a_{s}-a_{i})}=\sum_{s=k}^{j}{a}_{j}!\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=k\\
i\neq s\end{subarray}}^{j}(a_{s}-a_{i})^{-1}.

In order to prove

\sum_{s=k}^{j}{a}_{j}!\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=k\\
i\neq s\end{subarray}}^{j}(a_{s}-a_{i})^{-1}=0

for
k<j
, we assume
a_{j}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}\neq 0
(otherwise the equation would be true as well) and divide both sides by
a_{j}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}}
. By a simple computation we arrive at the equivalent equation

\sum_{s=k}^{j-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=k\\
i\neq s\end{subarray}}^{j-1}\frac{(a_{j}-a_{i})}{(a_{s}-a_{i})}=1.

The sum on the left side can be seen as a polynomial in
a_{j}
of degree
j-k-1
which is equal to
1
in the
j-k
values
a_{k},a_{k+1},\dots,a_{j-1}
inserted for
a_{j}
. In fact, it is the Lagrange interpolation polynomial for the function
f(a_{j})=1
. Therefore, the equality holds and we have proven the case
k<j
.

In summary, we have shown that

\frac{\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{j}f(z)}{{a}_{j}!}\big|_{z=0,x_{i}=a_{i}}=C_{j}.

To prove the theorem, we take
f(z)=a_{z}^{n}
to obtain

C_{k}=\frac{\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{k}a_{z}^{n}}{{a}_{k}!}\big|_{z=0,x_{i}=a_{i}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{k}(a_{j}-a_{i})^{-1}\Big)a_{j}^{n}.

(4.5)

By comparing (
4.4
) with (
3.2
) (where
z
is replaced by
a_{z}
) we finally arrive at

h_{n}(a_{0},\dots,a_{k})=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{k}(a_{j}-a_{i})^{-1}\Big)a_{j}^{n+k}.\qed

Remark 4.1

(Specializations)
.

Besides the elliptic and
q
-Stirling numbers, there are several interesting specializations of
h_{n}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
. For example, we obtain
(q,r)
-Whitney numbers of the second kind,
W_{m,r}[n,k]_{q}
(see for example
[
11
]
or
[

]
for

W_{m,r}[n,k]_{q}q^{-kr-m\binom{k}{2}}=W_{m,r}^{\ast}[n,k]_{q}=h_{n-k}([r]_{q},[m+r]_{q},[2m+r]_{q},\dots,[km+r]_{q}).

Then, Theorem
3.1
reduces to

.

Remmel and Wachs defined a
(p,q)

S_{n,k}^{r,m}(p,q)

(p,q)

reduces to

An elliptic analogue of

5.
More general cases

The proof of Theorem
3.1
can be adapted to any polynomial
f
in
a_{z}
of degree at most
n
which is independent of the variables
x
. Let
C
and
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n}
be complex numbers. If we take
f(z)=c_{0}(a_{z}-c_{1})(a_{z}-c_{2})\cdots(a_{z}-c_{n})
, then (
4.4
) becomes

c_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{z}-c_{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}C_{n,k}a_{z}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{\textbf{a}},

(5.1)

and, following the proof of Theorem
3.1
, the coefficients have the explicit form

C_{n,k}=\frac{\Delta_{\textbf{x}}^{k}f(z)}{{a}_{k}!}\big|_{z=0,x_{i}=a_{i}}=c_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{k}(a_{j}-a_{i})^{-1}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{j}-c_{i}).

(5.2)

If we see
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
as complex numbers, then
C_{n,k}
is the change of basis coefficient between the two series of polynomials over
\mathbb{C}

\Big(c_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{z}-c_{i})\Big)_{n\geq 0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Big(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(a_{z}-a_{i})\Big)_{n\geq 0}.

From (
5.1
) we can derive the recurrence relations

\displaystyle C_{n,k}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$ },

(5.3a)

\displaystyle C_{0,0}

\displaystyle=c_{0},

(5.3b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle C_{n+1,k}

\displaystyle=C_{n,k-1}+(a_{k}-c_{n+1})C_{n,k}.

(5.3c)

Remark 5.1
.

Note that the results discussed in this section are well-studied in other contexts.
For
c_{0}=1
, the coefficient
C_{n,k}
is equal to the complementary symmetric function
\comp_{k}(\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n}|a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\})
defined over hybrid sets in
[
12
]
. This can be seen by comparing (
5.1
) with
[
12
, Corollary 2]
.

We can also see the results from the perspective of Newton polynomials and divided differences. The falling factorials
z\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{n}^{\textbf{x}}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(z-x_{i})
are also known as Newton polynomials, and (
4.5
) is the explicit formula for the divided differences of
z^{n}
(see for example
[
20
, p. 7]
). By linearity, this formula extends to arbitrary polynomials in
z
, yielding (
5.2
).

5.1.
Rook numbers

In
[
32
]
, Schlosser and Yoo define the
k
-th elliptic rook number,
r_{k}(a,b;q,p;B)
, for a Ferrers board

B=B(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n})=\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq b_{i}\}

as the sum of specific elliptic weights for rook placements of
B
. We neglect the details here and only focus on the following algebraic expression. The authors obtain the following generating function for the elliptic rook numbers
[
32
, Theorem 12]
:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}[z-i+1+b_{i}]_{aq^{2(i-1-b_{i})},bq^{i-1-b_{i}};q,p}\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{n}r_{n-k}(a,b;q,p;B)\prod_{j=1}^{k}[z-j+1]_{aq^{2(j-1)},bq^{j-1};q,p}.

(5.4)

We can reformulate this equation by using (
2.4
) as

\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{a,b;q,p}(i-1-b_{i})^{-1}([z]_{a,b;q,p}-[i-1-b_{i}]_{a,b;q,p})\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{n}r_{n-k}(a,b;q,p;B)\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}W_{a,b;q,p}(j-1)^{-1}\Big)[z]_{a,b;q,p}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{a,b;q,p}.

(5.5)

Comparing this expression with (
5.1
) yields

r_{n-k}(a,b;q,p;B)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{W_{a,b;q,p}(j)}{W_{a,b;q,p}(k)}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}[j-i+b_{i}+1]_{aq^{2(i-1-b_{i})},bq^{i-1-b_{i}};q,p}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{k}[j-i]_{aq^{2i};bq^{i};q,p}}

(5.6)

as a special case of (
5.2
) with
c_{0}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{a,b;q,p}(i-1-b_{i})
,
a_{i}=[i]_{a,b;q,p}
,
c_{i}=[i-1-b_{i}]_{a,b;q,p}
and

C_{n,k}=r_{n-k}(a,b;q,p;B)\prod_{j=1}^{k}W_{a,b;q,p}(j-1)^{-1}.

These elliptic rook numbers generalize elliptic Stirling numbers of the second kind, which can be recovered by considering the staircase Ferrers board
B(1,2,3,\dots,n-1)
(with the original definition of elliptic Stirling numbers).

In the case of
p\to 0
,
b\to 0
and
B=[n+r-1]\times[n-r]
the identity (
5.6
) can be evaluated in closed form by virtue of the
q
-Pfaff–Saalschütz summation, thus yielding a closed form of the respective
r
-restricted
a;q
-Lah numbers (compare to
[
31
, Subsection 3.1.1]
).

5.2.
Lah numbers

Another interesting board considered in
[
32
]
is
B(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n})=B(n-1,n-1,\dots,n-1)
. In this case, the elliptic rook numbers are equal to elliptic Lah numbers, defined in
[
15
]
. We will again use a slightly different definition (up to a power of elliptic weights). We introduce
elliptic rising factorials
by the definition

z\hskip-2.0pt\uparrow_{n}^{a,b;q,p}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}z(z-[-1]_{a,b;q,p})\cdots(z-[-n+1]_{a,b;q,p}),

for
n>0
and
z\hskip-2.0pt\uparrow_{0}^{a,b;q,p}=1
, and define
elliptic Lah numbers
,
L_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)
, by

[z]_{a,b;q,p}\hskip-2.0pt\uparrow_{n}^{a,b;q,p}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)[z]_{a,b;q,p}\hskip-2.0pt\downarrow_{k}^{a,b;q,p}.

(5.7)

They fulfil the recurrence relation

\displaystyle L_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$ },

(5.8a)

\displaystyle L_{a,b;q,p}(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(5.8b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle L_{a,b;q,p}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=L_{a,b;q,p}(n,k-1)+W_{a,b;q,p}(-n)[n+k]_{aq^{-2n},bq^{-n};q,p}L_{a,b;q,p}(n,k).

(5.8c)

We derive the explicit expression

L_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}([j]_{a,b;q,p}-[-n+i]_{a,b;q,p})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{k}([j]_{a,b;q,p}-[i]_{a,b;q,p})}

(5.9)

by comparing (
5.7
) with (
5.4
) or directly with (
5.1
).

6.
Eulerian numbers

The
Eulerian numbers
,
A(n,k)
, play an important role in combinatorics. We follow the notation of Carlitz
[
7
]
and define Eulerian numbers by the recurrence relation

\displaystyle A(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

(6.1a)

\displaystyle A(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(6.1b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle A(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=(n-k+2)A(n,k-1)+kA(n,k).

(6.1c)

The Eulerian numbers can also be defined as the connection coefficients in the identity

\displaystyle z^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A(n,k)\binom{z+k-1}{n},

(6.2)

which is known as Worpitzky’s identity. Besides several other properties, Eulerian numbers can be expressed by the explicit sum formula

\displaystyle A(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{n+1}{j}(k-j)^{n}.

(6.3)

We recommend
[
21
]
for an introduction to Eulerian numbers.

Carlitz introduced a
q
-analogue of Eulerian numbers
[
7
]
, the
q
-Eulerian numbers
,
A_{q}(n,k)
, and defined them as the connection coefficients in

\displaystyle[z]_{q}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{q}(n,k){\begin{bmatrix}z+k-1\\
n\end{bmatrix}}_{q}.

(6.4)

He derives from this relation that
A_{q}(n,k)
satisfies

\displaystyle A_{q}(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$ },

(6.5a)

\displaystyle A_{q}(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(6.5b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle A_{q}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=[n-k+2]_{q}A_{q}(n,k-1)+q^{n-k+1}[k]_{q}A_{q}(n,k)

(6.5c)

and the explicit formula (see
[
7
, Equation (5.5), corrected]
)

\displaystyle A_{q}(n,k)=q^{\binom{n-k+1}{2}-\binom{k}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{\binom{j}{2}}{\begin{bmatrix}n+1\\
j\end{bmatrix}}_{q}[k-j]_{q}^{n}.

(6.6)

6.1.
Generalized Eulerian numbers

It turns out that it is possible to generalize Carlitz’
q
-Eulerian numbers similarly to the generalizations in the previous sections to elliptic Eulerian numbers and a Laurent polynomial analogue. We begin by extending
a
to variables with negative index by
\textbf{a}\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\dots,a_{-2},a_{-1},a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots
and define generalized Eulerian numbers,
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
, as the connection coefficients in

\displaystyle z^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}.

(6.7)

This definition was chosen due to the fact that these coefficients can be characterized by a nice recursion, and we will derive a simple explicit sum formula for
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
.

Note that
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)=0
for
k<0
or
k>n
and
A_{\textbf{a}}(0,0)=1
.
We can derive a recursion for
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
by using the identity

z=\frac{a_{n-k+1}(z-a_{-k})-a_{-k}(z-a_{n-k+1})}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{-k}}

(6.8)

and we introduce the notation

P(n,k)\mbox{\,\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\scriptsize$\mathrm{:}$}$=$\,}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{a_{n-k+2}-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-1-k}}.

(6.9)

By multiplying
z
to both sides of (
6.7
) using (
6.8
) we obtain

\displaystyle z^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}\Big)\frac{a_{n-k+1}(z-a_{-k})-a_{-k}(z-a_{n-k+1})}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{-k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)a_{n-k+1}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{z-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)a_{-k}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{z-a_{i-k+1}}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k-1)a_{n-k+2}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{z-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+2}-a_{i-k}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)a_{-k}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{a_{n-k+2}-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-1-k}}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{z-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+2}-a_{i-k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big(a_{n-k+2}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k-1)-a_{-k}P(n,k)A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{z-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+2}-a_{i-k}}.

By comparing the last sum with the definition of
A_{\textbf{a}}(n+1,k)
in (
6.7
) we obtain the recursive description for the generalized Eulerian numbers

\displaystyle A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

(6.10a)

\displaystyle A_{\textbf{a}}(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(6.10b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle A_{\textbf{a}}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=a_{n-k+2}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k-1)-a_{-k}P(n,k)A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k).

(6.10c)

Note that in comparison to the generalized Stirling, Lah and rook numbers,
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
is not a polynomial in the variables
a
due to the factor
P(n,k)
in the recursion.
However,
P(n,k)
factorizes to
1
or a power of
q
in the following specializations.

Remark 6.1

(Specializations)
.

By setting
a_{i}=i
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain
P(n,k)=1
and
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
reduces to the ordinary Eulerian numbers
A(n,k)
.

By setting
a_{i}=[i]_{q}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain
P(n,k)=q^{n+1}
,
a_{-k}=[-k]_{q}=-q^{-k}[k]_{q}
, thus we can see by comparing the recursion formulae (
6.10
) and (
6.5
) (or by comparing the definitions) that
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
reduces to Carlitz’
q
-Eulerian numbers
A_{q}(n,k)
.

We study more specializations in Section
6.3
.

6.2.
Explicit formula for
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)

In this subsection, we derive an explicit formula for the generalized Eulerian numbers.

Theorem 6.2
.

For
n\geq 0
and
0\leq k\leq n
there holds

A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)a_{-j}^{n}.

(6.11)

Proof.

We begin by letting
f(z)
be a polynomial in
a_{z}
of degree at most
n
and let
C_{n,k}
be the connection coefficients in

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}C_{n,k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{z}-a_{i-k}}{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}

(6.12)

and we claim that

C_{n,k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)f(-j).

(6.13)

We prove this claim by inserting (
6.12
) into the right side of (
6.13
) to derive

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)f(-j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{n}C_{n,l}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{-j}-a_{i-l}}{a_{n-l+1}-a_{i-l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}C_{n,l}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{-j}-a_{i-l}}{a_{n-l+1}-a_{i-l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}C_{n,l}\sum_{j=l}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{-j}-a_{i-l}}{a_{n-l+1}-a_{i-l}}

We prove that the last expression is equal to
C_{n,k}
by showing that

\sum_{j=l}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{-j}-a_{i-l}}{a_{n-l+1}-a_{i-l}}=\delta_{k,l}

(6.14)

for
0\leq l\leq k\leq n
. First, we factor out

\frac{\prod_{i=k-l+1}^{n}(a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{n-l+1}-a_{i-l})}\sum_{j=l}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{k-l}\frac{a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k}}{a_{-j}-a_{i-k}}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{k-l}(a_{-j}-a_{i+n-k})

(6.15)

to obtain that the sum in this expression is the Lagrange interpolation polynomial of the polynomial

g(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{k-l}(z-a_{i+n-k}),

for the
k-l+1
values
a_{-l},a_{-l-1},\dots,a_{-k}
inserted for
z
and
z
is replaced by
a_{n-k+1}
. Since
g(z)
is a polynomial in the variable
z
of degree
k-l
, the Lagrange polynomial and
g(z)
are equal, such that (
6.15
) is equal to

\frac{\prod_{i=k-l+1}^{n}(a_{n-k+1}-a_{i-k})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{n-l+1}-a_{i-l})}\prod_{i=1}^{k-l}(a_{n-k+1}-a_{i+n-k})=\delta_{k,l}.

(6.16)

We therefore proved (
6.14
) and (
6.13
). By comparing (
6.12
) with (
6.7
) and setting
f(z)=a_{z}^{n}
, we complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

6.3.
Specializations

In the literature, several generalizations of Eulerian numbers and
q
-Eulerian numbers were studied. It appears that some generalizations are special cases of
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
.

For example, the
r
-Whitney Eulerian numbers

[
18
,
22
]
satisfy (see
[
22
, Equation (6)]
)

\displaystyle A_{m,r}(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

(6.17a)

\displaystyle A_{m,r}(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(6.17b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle A_{m,r}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=(m(n-k+2)-r)A_{m,r}(n,k-1)+(mk+r)A_{m,r}(n,k).

(6.17c)

The
r
-Whitney Eulerian numbers reduce to classical Eulerian numbers for
(m,r)=(1,0)
and to the
q
-Eulerian numbers due to Brenti
[
4
]
for
(m,r)=(q+1,1)
. By comparing (
6.17
) with (
6.10
) one can see that setting
a_{i}=mi-r
in

yields
A_{m,r}(n,k)
, since the polynomials

reduce to
1
in this case. Furthermore, for
z=mx-r
the Worpitzky identity (
6.7
) reduces to

(mx-r)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{m,r}(n,k)\binom{x+k-1}{n}

which is in fact different to the Worpitzky identity given in
[
22
, Theorem 5]
. The explicit formula (
6.11
) yields
[
22
, Theorem 6]
.

A
q
-analogue of the
r
-Whitney Eulerian numbers,
A_{q}^{m,r}(n,k)
, can be obtained by setting
a_{i}=[mi-r]_{q}
. From (
6.10
) we obtain the recursion

\displaystyle A_{q}^{m,r}(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$ },

(6.18a)

\displaystyle A_{q}^{m,r}(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(6.18b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle A_{q}^{m,r}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=[m(n-k+2)-r]_{q}A_{q}^{m,r}(n,k-1)

\displaystyle+q^{m(n+1)-mk-r}[mk+r]_{q}A_{q}^{m,r}(n,k),

(6.18c)

For
z=[mx-r]_{q}
the Worpitzky identity (
6.7
) reduces to

[mx-r]_{q}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{q}^{m,r}(n,k){\begin{bmatrix}x+k-1\\
n\end{bmatrix}}_{q^{m}}

(6.19)

and from (

) we obtain the explicit formula

A_{q}^{m,r}(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{m\binom{n-j+1}{2}-n(m(k-j)+r)}{\begin{bmatrix}n+1\\
j\end{bmatrix}}_{q^{m}}[m(k-j)+r]_{q}^{n}.

(6.20)

For
(m,r)=(1,0)
this reduces to Carlitz’
q
-Eulerian numbers (
6.4
). The case
(m,r)=(m,1)
was studied in
[
9
]
as a

-Eulerian number analogue for the wreath product

(m,r)=(2,1)
we get the

-Eulerian numbers of type
B

[

]

6.4.
Elliptic Eulerian numbers

One of the main motivations of this article was to introduce an elliptic analogue of Carlitz’
q
-Eulerian numbers. We will obtain such an analogue by specializing
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
in the obvious way.
We define
elliptic Eulerian numbers

A_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)
by specializing
a_{i}=[i]_{a,b;q,p}
in
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
to obtain the recurrence relation

\displaystyle A_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for $k<0$ or $k>n$},

(6.21a)

\displaystyle A_{a,b;q,p}(0,0)

\displaystyle=1,

(6.21b)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle A_{a,b;q,p}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=[n-k+2]_{a,b;q,p}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k-1)

\displaystyle\quad+W_{a,b;q,p}(-1)[k]_{1/a,b/a;q,p}P_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k),

(6.21c)

where

P_{a,b;q,p}(n,k)=W_{aq^{-2k},bq^{-k};q,p}(n+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{[n-i+2]_{aq^{2(i-k)},bq^{i-k};q,p}}{[n-i+2]_{aq^{2(i-k-1),bq^{i-k-1};q,p}}},

where we used (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).
For the elliptic Eulerian numbers the generalized Worpitzky identity

[z]_{a,b;q,p}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{[z-i+k]_{aq^{2(i-k)},bq^{i-k};q,p}}{[n-i+1]_{aq^{2(i-k)},bq^{i-k};q,p}}

(6.22)

holds and from Theorem
6.2
we obtain the explicit sum-formula

\displaystyle A_{a,b;q,p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq k-j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{[n-i+1]_{aq^{2(i-k)},bq^{i-k};q,p}}{[k-j-i]_{aq^{2(i-k)},bq^{i-k};q,p}}\Big)[-j]_{a,b;q,p}^{n}

(6.23)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}W_{a,b;q,p}(-1)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{[n-i+1]_{aq^{2(i-k)},bq^{i-k};q,p}}{[j-i]_{aq^{2(i-k)},bq^{i-k};q,p}}\Big)[k-j]_{a,b;q,p}^{n}.

(6.24)

An
elliptic analogue of the
r
-Whitney Eulerian numbers
,
A_{a,b;q,p}^{m,r}(n,k)
, can be obtained by specializing
a_{i}=[mi-r]_{a,b;q,p}
in
A_{\textbf{a}}(n,k)
. We omit the details here.

Analytic continuation of ℓ \ell -generalized Fibonacci zeta function Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 11B39, Secondary 11M41, 30D30. Keywords: Generalized Fibonacci sequence, Analytic continuation, Fibonacci zeta function, Residues
D. K. Sahoo, N.K. Meher

Abstract
. In this paper, for any positive integer ℓ ≥ 2 , \ell\geq 2, we define ℓ \ell -generalized Fibonacci zeta function. We then study its analytic continuation to the whole complex plane ℂ . \mathbb{C}. Further, we compute a possible list of singularities and residues of the function at these simple poles. Moreover, we deduce that the special values of ℓ \ell -generalized Fibonacci zeta function at negative integer arguments are rational.



1.
Introduction

Let
\ell\geq 2
be an integer. The
n^{th}
generalized Fibonacci sequence
\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)_{n\geq 2-\ell}
defined as

F_{n}^{(\ell)}=F_{n-1}^{(\ell)}+F_{n-2}^{(\ell)}+\cdots+F_{n-\ell}^{(\ell)}

with the initial conditions

\displaystyle F_{-(\ell-2)}^{(\ell)}=F_{-(\ell-3)}^{(\ell)}=\cdots=F_{0}^{(\ell)}=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad F_{1}^{(\ell)}=1.

F_{n}^{(\ell)}
is also called as
n^{th}

\ell
-generalized Fibonacci number.
From
[
5
]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(\ell)}=2^{n-2}\quad\hbox{for all}\quad 2\leq n\leq\ell+1,\quad\hbox{and }\quad F_{n}^{(\ell)}<2^{n-2}\quad\hbox{for all}\quad n\geq\ell+2.

The characteristics polynomial of the
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci number is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{\ell}(x)=x^{\ell}-x^{\ell-1}-\cdots-x-1.

(1.1)

It is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}[x]
and has one root outside the unit circle. Let
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
be that single root which lies in between
2\left(1-2^{-\ell}\right)
and
2
(see
[
16
]
) and it is the dominant root of
\phi_{\ell}(x).
The other roots of the polynomial (
1.1
) are
\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}.
When
\ell
is an even integer, then
\phi_{\ell}(x)
has one negative real root which lies in the interval
(-1,0).
In 2014, G. P. B. Dresden and Z. Du
[
6
]
gave the “Binet like formula for the terms
F_{n}^{(\ell)}
” which is given by

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(\ell)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}.

(1.2)

From
[
5
]
, it is also known that

\displaystyle\left|F_{n}^{(\ell)}-\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right|<\frac{1}{2}\quad\hbox{ for all}\quad n\geq 2-\ell.

In 2013, J. J. Bravo and F. Luca
[
3
]
obtained that

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-2}\leq F_{n}^{(\ell)}\leq\alpha^{n-1}\quad\hbox{holds for all}\quad n\geq 1\quad\hbox{and}\quad\ell\geq 2.

(1.3)

When
\ell=2
,
F_{n}^{(\ell)}
is same as Fibonacci number
F_{n}
and when
\ell=3,
it coincides with Tribonacci numbers
T_{n}
.

The Fibonacci zeta function is defined by the series

\zeta_{F}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{F_{n}^{s}},\quad\mathrm{Re}(s)>0.

Analytic continuation of Fibonacci zeta function was studied by L. Navas
[
13
]
in 2001. In 2013, K. Kamano
[
9
]
studied the analytic continuation of Lucas zeta function. Further, analytic continuation of the multiple Fibonacci and Lucas zeta functions were studied by the second author and S. S. Rout in (
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
). The arithmetic nature of special values of Fibonacci zeta function and the special value of Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
behaves similar in nature. The irrationality of
\zeta_{F}(1)
was proved by R. Andr
\acute{e}
-Jeannin
[
1
]
in 1989, while the the transcendence of
\zeta_{F}(2m),\ \hbox{for}\ m\in\mathbb{N}
was given by D. Duverney et.al.
[
7
]
in 1997. Furthermore, in 2007, C. Elsner et.al.
[
8
]
showed that
\zeta_{F}(2),\zeta_{F}(4),\zeta_{F}(6)
are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Likewise, M. Ram Murty
[
12
]
deduced that
\zeta_{F}(2m),\ \hbox{for}\ m\in\mathbb{N}
is transcendental by using the theory of modular form and a result of Yu. V. Nesterenko
[
14
]
.

In this paper, we introduce the
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function which is defined as

\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{s}}.

When
\ell=2,
it is same as Fibonacci zeta function
\zeta_{F}(s).
In this paper, we study the analytic continuation of the
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function with a possible list of poles and their corresponding residues. Moreover, we discuss the arithmetic nature of
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function at negative integer arguments. The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2,
we prove that
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}
is absolute convergent in
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0.
In Section
3,
we obtain the analytic continuation of
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}
, compute a possible list of poles and residues at these poles. In Section
4,
we deduce that the special values of
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function at negative integer arguments are rational.

2.
Preliminaries

Proposition 1
.

The infinite series
\sum_{n>0}\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-s}
converges absolutely in the right half plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0.

Proof.

From (
1.3
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-s}\right|=\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-\sigma}\leq\left(\alpha^{n-2}\right)^{-\sigma}=\alpha^{2\sigma}\left(\alpha^{-n\sigma}\right).

(2.1)

Since
\sigma=\mathrm{Re}s>0,
from (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-s}\right|\leq\alpha^{2\sigma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\alpha^{-n\sigma}\right)=\frac{\alpha^{2\sigma}}{\alpha^{\sigma}-1}<\infty.

∎

The next lemma describes that the integer closest to first term of Binet-like formula is the
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci number.

Lemma 2.1

(G. P. B. Dresden and Z. Du
[
6
]
)
.

Let
F_{n}^{(\ell)}
be the
n^{th}

\ell
-generalized Fibonacci number. Then

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(\ell)}=\mathrm{rnd}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)\quad\hbox{for all}\quad n\geq 2-\ell,

where
\alpha
is the unique positive dominant root and
\mathrm{rnd}(x)
denotes the values of
x
rounded to the nearest integer.

3.
Analytic continuation of
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function

Theorem 1
.

The
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
can be meromorphically continued to whole complex plane
\mathbb{C}
with possible simple poles at

s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}=-k+\frac{2ni\pi+k_{2}\log\alpha_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}\log\alpha_{\ell}}{\log\alpha}\,,\,n\in\,\mathbb{Z},k,k_{2},k_{3},...,k_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.1)

k=k_{2}+k_{3}+\cdots+k_{\ell},

where
\mathbb{N}_{0}
denotes the set of non-negative integers and
\log(\alpha_{i})\ (i=1,2,\ldots,\ell)
are the principal values whose imaginary part lies in the interval
(-\pi,\pi].

Moreover, the residue of
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
at
s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}
is

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}}\binom{-s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}}{k}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle\frac{k!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\log\alpha}\,.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle 0<

\displaystyle\left|F_{n}^{(\ell)}-\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)\right|<1

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left|\frac{F_{n}^{(\ell)}}{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)}-1\right|<1

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}\right)}{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)}\right|<1.

Now we consider the following.

\displaystyle\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-s}=

\displaystyle\left(\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}\right)\right)^{-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)^{-s}\left(1+\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}\right)}{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)}\right)^{-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)^{-s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}\right)}{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)}\right)^{k}.

Using Proposition
1
, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)^{-s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}\right)}{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)}\right)^{k}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-s}\right|<\infty.

On interchanging the order of summation and using multinomial expansion formula, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(F_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)^{-s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}\right)}{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)^{-s}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}\right)}{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{n-1}\right)}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\bigg\{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{2}+k_{3}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=k}\frac{k!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{{\ell}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots\,\alpha_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}}{\alpha^{s+k}}\right)^{n}\bigg\}.

Note that
\left|\frac{\alpha_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}}{\alpha^{s+k}}\right|\leq\frac{\alpha^{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}}}{\alpha^{\sigma+k}}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\sigma}}<1
for
\sigma>0.

Therefore, the above series becomes

\displaystyle\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\bigg\{\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-k}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{2}+k_{3}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=k}\frac{k!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{s+k}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\,\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1}\bigg\}\,.

From (
1.2
), it is clear that for any integer
i
with
1\leq i\leq\ell,

\left(2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)\right)\neq 0.
Note that the function
h_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha^{s+k}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\,\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1}
has poles at

s=-k+\frac{2ni\pi+k_{2}\log\alpha_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}\log\alpha_{\ell}}{\log\alpha}\,,\,n\in\,\mathbb{Z},

k,k_{2},k_{3},...,k_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
k=k_{2}+k_{3}+\cdots+k_{\ell}
.

Consider the function
g_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}(s)=\alpha^{s+k}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\,\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1.
Then

g_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}^{\prime}(s)=\alpha^{s+k}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\,\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}\log\alpha.

Thus,
g_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}^{\prime}(s)
at
s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}=-k+\frac{2ni\pi+k_{2}\log\alpha_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}\log\alpha_{\ell}}{\log\alpha}
is
\log\alpha
which is non-zero. Therefore, the poles of
h_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}(s)
are simple.

The series (
3.2
) determines the holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
except for the poles derived from the function
h_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}(s).
Hence, the function
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
can be meromorphically continued to the whole
s
-plane and it has possible simple poles st
s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}=-k+\frac{2ni\pi+k_{2}\log\alpha_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}\log\alpha_{\ell}}{\log\alpha}.

The residue of
h_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha^{s+k}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\,\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1}
at
s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}=-k+\frac{2ni\pi+k_{2}\log\alpha_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}\log\alpha_{\ell}}{\log\alpha}
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}}h_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}}(s)=\frac{1}{\frac{d}{ds}\left(\alpha^{s+k}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\,\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1\right)}\Bigg|_{s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}}=\frac{1}{\log\alpha}.

Now the residue of
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
at
s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{s=s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}}\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}}\binom{-s_{k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell},n}}{k}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle\frac{k!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\log\alpha}\,.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

When
\ell=2,
we have
\alpha\alpha_{2}=-1\implies\alpha_{2}=\frac{-1}{\alpha}.
Since
\alpha
is a positive real number,
\arg\alpha_{2}=\pi.
From (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle s=s_{k,k_{2},n}=

\displaystyle-k+\frac{2ni\pi+k\log\alpha_{2}}{\log\alpha}=-k+\frac{2ni\pi+k\log|\alpha_{2}|+ik\arg\alpha_{2}}{\log\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k+\frac{2ni\pi-k\log|\alpha|+ik\arg\alpha_{2}}{\log\alpha}=-2k+i\frac{2n\pi+k\arg\alpha_{2}}{\log\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2k+\frac{i\pi(2n+k)}{\log\alpha}.

These are the same list of poles as obtained by L. Navas
[
13
]
.

Next, we discuss the special cases of singularities for our better understanding.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z},\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
(\ell-1)|k
. Then the possible poles of
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
are given by

s_{k,\frac{k}{\ell-1},\dots,\frac{k}{\ell-1},n}=\begin{cases}-(k+\frac{k}{\ell-1})+i\frac{\pi(2n+\frac{k}{\ell-1})}{\log\alpha}\quad\,\hbox{if}\,\,\ell\,\,\hbox{is even}\,,\\
-(k+\frac{k}{\ell-1})+i\frac{2n\pi}{\log\alpha}\quad\quad\quad\hbox{if}\,\,\ell\,\,\hbox{is odd}\,.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Proof.

Note that
\alpha\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell+1}.

Therefore, we have

|\alpha\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{\ell}|=1\implies\log\alpha+\log|\alpha_{2}|+\cdots+\log|\alpha_{\ell}|=0.

(3.4)

Observe that
\alpha>1
and for
1\leq j\leq\ell
, we can write
\log\alpha_{j}=\log|\alpha_{j}|+i\arg\alpha_{j}
, where
i=\sqrt{(-1)}
and
\arg\alpha_{j}\in(-\pi,\pi].

Since
\log\alpha_{j}
’s are principal values of complex logarithms, if
\ell
is odd,

\arg\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\arg\alpha_{\ell}=0.

(3.5)

If
\ell
is even,

\arg\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\arg\alpha_{\ell}=\pi.

(3.6)

Now using (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) in (
3.1
) with
k_{2}=k_{3}=\cdots=k_{\ell}=\frac{k}{\ell-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we get (
3.3
).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

From Corollary
3.2
, we have seen that some of the possible poles lie on the lines
\mathrm{Re}(s)=-(k+\frac{k}{\ell-1})
spaced at intervals of
\frac{2\pi i}{\log\alpha}.
In particular, when
\ell
is an odd integer,
s=-(k+\frac{k}{\ell-1}),k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are possible simple poles and when
\ell
is an even integer,
s=-(k+\frac{k}{\ell-1})+i\frac{k\pi}{(\ell-1)\log\alpha},k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are possible simple poles. Furthermore, when
\ell
is an even integer and
k=-2n(\ell-1),
then from (
3.3
), we obtain
s=2n\ell,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
are possible simple negative integer poles.

4.
Special values at negative integer arguments

Furthermore, we will discuss the values of
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
at negative integers.

Theorem 2
.

Let
m
be positive integer such that
-m
is not a pole of
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
. Then
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(-m)\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
-m\,,\,m\in\mathbb{N}
is not a pole of
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(s)
.
From (
3.2
), we have

\begin{split}\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}&(-m)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose\,k}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{m-k}\\
&\left(\sum_{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=k}\frac{k!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-(m-k)}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1}\right).\end{split}

(4.1)

Note that since
0\leq k\leq m,
we have
0\leq m-k\leq m.
Let
b
be a fixed integer with
0\leq b\leq m.
If we choose
m-k=b,
then
k=m-b
and
-m+k=-b.
Then the coefficient of
\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{b}
in expression (
4.1
) is

\displaystyle{m\choose\,m-b}\sum_{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=m-b}\frac{(m-b)!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-b}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1}.

Let
\sigma\,:\,\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{\ell})\,\to\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{\ell})
be a non-trivial field automorphism.
We know that any field automorphism permutes the roots of an irreducible polynomial. Without loss generality, let us assume that
\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha_{r}
and
\sigma(\alpha_{j})=\alpha
for some
1\leq j,r\leq\ell.

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle(\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(-m))=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose\,k}\left(\frac{\alpha_{r}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{r}-2)}\alpha_{r}^{-1}\right)^{m-k}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=k}\frac{k!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\sigma(\alpha_{i})-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\sigma(\alpha_{i})-2)}\sigma(\alpha_{i})^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}^{-(m-k)}\sigma(\alpha_{2})^{-k_{2}}\cdots\sigma(\alpha_{\ell})^{-k_{\ell}}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose\,k}\left(\frac{\alpha_{r}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{r}-2)}\alpha_{r}^{-1}\right)^{m-k}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{j}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=k}\frac{k!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2,i\neq j}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\sigma(\alpha_{i})-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\sigma(\alpha_{i})-2)}\sigma(\alpha_{i})^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{k_{j}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}^{-(m-k)}\sigma(\alpha_{2})^{-k_{2}}\cdots\sigma(\alpha_{\ell})^{-k_{\ell}}-1}\Bigg).

In (
4.2
), the coefficient of
\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{b}
is

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=b}^{m}{m\choose\,k}\left(\frac{\alpha_{r}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{r}-2)}\alpha_{r}^{-1}\right)^{m-k}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{j-1}+k_{j+1}\cdots+k_{\ell}=k-b}\frac{k!}{\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq\,j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}k_{i}!\right)b!}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq\,j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-b}\alpha_{r}^{-(m-k)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq\,j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}^{-k_{i}}-1}\right)

\displaystyle={m\choose\,m-b}\sum_{k=b}^{m}\left(\frac{\alpha_{r}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{r}-2)}\alpha_{r}^{-1}\right)^{m-k}\frac{1}{(m-k)!}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{j-1}+k_{j+1}\cdots+k_{\ell}=k-b}\frac{(m-b)!}{\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq\,j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}k_{i}!\right)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq\,j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-b}\alpha_{r}^{-(m-k)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq\,j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}^{-k_{i}}-1}\right).

Note that
(m-k)+(k-b)=m-b.
Since
k
varies from
b
to
m,
we have
0\leq m-k\leq m-b,
and
0\leq k_{i}\leq m-b
Thus the above sum will be

\displaystyle{m\choose\,m-b}\sum_{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=m-b}\frac{(m-b)!}{k_{2}!\cdots\,k_{\ell}!}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha_{i}-2)}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-b}\alpha_{2}^{-k_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{\ell}^{-k_{\ell}}-1}.

Note that for any integer
b
with
0\leq b\leq m,
and for any field automorphism
\sigma
we have proved that the coefficient of
\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2+(\ell+1)(\alpha-2)}\alpha^{-1}\right)^{b}
in
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(-m)
is same as
\sigma(\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(-m))
. Therefore,
for any field automorphism
\sigma,
we have
\sigma(\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(-m))=\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(-m)
. Hence
\zeta_{F^{(\ell)}}(-m)\in\mathbb{Q}.

∎

5.
Concluding Remark

Analogous to Fibonacci zeta function, it is interesting to find the zeros of
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function. The study of arithmetic nature of special values of
\ell
-generalized Fibonacci zeta function at positive even integer arguments is another important object for future discussion.

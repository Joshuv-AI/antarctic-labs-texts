Sorting Probability for Large Young Diagrams
Greta Panova

Abstract
For a finite poset P = ( X , ≺ ) P=(X,\prec) , let ℒ P \mathcal{L}_{P} denote the set of linear extensions of P P . The sorting probability δ ⁡ ( P ) \delta(P) is defined as δ ( P ) := min x , y ∈ X | P [ L ( x ) ≤ L ( y ) ] − P [ L ( y ) ≤ L ( x ) ] | , \delta(P)\hskip 1.70709pt:=\hskip 1.70709pt\min_{x,y\in X}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl|{\text{\bf P}}\hskip 0.85355pt[L(x)\leq L(y)]\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355pt{\text{\bf P}}\hskip 0.85355pt[L(y)\leq L(x)]\bigr|\hskip 1.70709pt, where L ∈ ℒ P L\in\mathcal{L}_{P} is a uniform linear extension of P P . We give asymptotic upper bounds on sorting probabilities for posets associated with large Young diagrams and large skew Young diagrams, with bounded number of rows.

Introduction

Random linear extensions
of finite posets occupy an unusual
place in combinatorial probability by being remarkably interesting
with numerous applications, and at the same time by being unwieldy
and lacking general structure.
One reason for this lies in the broad nature of posets, when some
special cases are highly structured, extremely elegant and well
studied, while there is no universal notion of “large poset” or
“random poset” in the opposite extreme.
As a consequence, the results in the area tend to range widely
across the
generality spectrum
:
from weaker results for large classes of posets to stronger results
for smaller classes of posets.

In this framework, the famous
\frac{1}{3}
–
\frac{2}{3}
Conjecture

1.1

is very surprising in both the scope and precision, as it bounds the

sorting probability

\delta(P)\leq\frac{1}{3}
for
all
finite posets
P
.
There are numerous partial results on the conjecture, as well as the
Kahn–Saks general upper bound
\delta(P)\leq\frac{5}{11}
.
At the same time, the asymptotic analysis of
\delta(P)

remains out of reach even for the most classical examples.
In this paper we obtain sharp asymptotic upper bounds on
\delta(P)

for large Young diagrams and large skew Young diagrams. These are the
first asymptotic results of this type, as we are moving down the
generality spectrum.

1.1
Sorting probability

Let
P=(X,\prec)
be a finite poset with
n=|X|
elements.
A
linear extension

L
of
P
is an order preserving bijection
L:X\to[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, so that
x\prec y
implies
L(x)<L(y)
for all
x,y\in X
.
The set of linear extensions is denoted
\mathcal{L}(P)
, and
e(P)=|\mathcal{L}(P)|
is the
number
of linear extensions
of
P
.

The
sorting probability of two elements

x,y\in X
,

x\neq y

\delta(P;\hskip 0.85355ptx,y)\,:=\,\hskip 1.70709pt\Bigl|{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[L(x)<L(y)\bigr]\hskip 1.70709pt-\hskip 1.70709pt{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[L(y)<L(y)\bigr]\Bigr|\hskip 1.70709pt,

(1.1)

where the probability is over uniform linear extensions
L\in\mathcal{L}(P)
.
This is a measure of how independent random linear extensions on
elements
x
and
y
are. The
sorting probability

Main results

The linear extensions

We state our results, roughly, from less general to more general.
Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

,

. Note that

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix

. For every Thoma sequence

\lambda\simeq\alpha\hskip 0.85355ptn

\alpha

We say that a partition
\lambda\vdash n

\varepsilon

the smallest part
\lambda_{d}\geq\varepsilon\hskip 0.85355ptn
, where

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix

. For every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a universal constant

,
such that for every
\varepsilon
-thick partition
\lambda\vdash n

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})

. We write
\mu\subset\lambda
,
if
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
,
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\alpha_{d}

.

Fix

When

We say that a partition
\lambda
is
\varepsilon
-smooth

\lambda
is
\varepsilon
-thick,
and
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\geq\varepsilon\hskip 0.85355ptn

\varepsilon
-smooth
.

.

Theorem 1.5

(Main theorem)
.

Fix
d\geq 2
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a universal constant
C_{d,\varepsilon}
,
such that for every
\varepsilon
-smooth skew partition
\lambda/\mu\vdash n
,

\ell(\lambda)=d
, we have:

\delta\bigl(P_{\lambda/\mu}\bigr)\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{C_{d,\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{n}}\,.

In the TVK case, when

, we obtain

. However, when the inequalities are non-strict,

for

, and

The results are proved by using random walks estimates and the

]
–
[

]
on the

in order to estimate the sorting probabilities

, we need very careful bounds
on the number of standard Young tableaux

for the typical

from

.
The NHLF gives a useful technical tool, which combined with various

of our results until after a brief literature review.

1.3
Prior work on sorting probability

The
\frac{1}{3}
–
\frac{2}{3}
Conjecture
1.1
was proposed independently by
Kislitsyn
[
Kis68
]
and Fredman
[
Fre75
]
in the context of sorting
under partial information. The name is motivated by the following
attractive equivalent formulation. In notation of (
1.2
),
for every
P=(X,\prec)
that is not a chain, there exist elements

x,y\in X
, such that

\frac{1}{3}\,\leq\,{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[L(x)<L(y)\bigr]\,\leq\,\frac{2}{3}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(1.3)

A major breakthrough was made by Kahn and Saks
[
KS84
]
, who
proved (
1.3
) with slightly weaker constants
\frac{3}{11}-\frac{8}{11}
. In our notation, they showed that

\delta(P)\leq\frac{5}{11}\approx 0.4545
for all finite
P
. A much
simplified geometric proof (with a slightly weaker bound) was given
later in
[
KL91
]
. By utilizing technical combinatorial
tools, the Kahn–Saks bound was slightly improved in
[
BFT95
]
to
\delta(P)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\approx 0.4472
, where it currently stands.
For more on the history
and various related results, we refer the reader to a dated
but very useful survey
[
Bri99
]
.

While the conjecture does not ask for an efficient algorithm for
finding the desired elements
x,y\in X
, a nearly optimal sorting algorithm
using
O\bigl(\log e(P)\bigr)
comparisons was found in
[
KK95
]
.
See also
[
C+13
]
for a simpler version.

Note that the bound
\delta(P)\leq\frac{1}{3}
in the conjecture
is tight for a 3-element poset that is a union of a
2
-chain and
a single element. The effort to establish the conjecture and
improve the constants remains very active. First, Linial
[
Lin84
]

proved that
\delta(P)\leq\frac{1}{3}
for posets of width
2
,
where
\mathrm{width}(P)
is the size of the maximal antichain in
P
.
In this class, Aigner showed that the tight bound
\delta(P)=\frac{1}{3}
can come only from decomposable posets,
and Sah
[
Sah21
]
recently improved the bound to a slightly lower
bound
\delta(P)<0.3225
in the indecomposable case (see also
[
Chen18
]
).

Conjecture
1.1
was further established for several other classes
of posets, including semiorders
[
Bri89
]
,
N
-free posets
[
Zag12
]
,
height 2 posets
[
TGF92
]
, and posets whose cover graph is a
forest
[
Zag19
]
. For posets with a nontrivial automorphism
the conjecture was proved by Pouzet, see
[
GHP87
]
, and
a stronger bound
\delta(P)<1-\frac{2}{e}\approx 0.2642
was shown by
Saks
[
Saks85
]
. Closer to the subject of this paper, Olson
and Sagan
[
OS18
]
recently applied Linial’s approach to establish
Conjecture
1.1
for all Young diagrams and skew Young
diagrams.

There are very few results proving that
\delta(P_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for a sequence
\{P_{n}\}
of posets on

n
elements. Some of them are motivated by the following interesting
conjecture of Kahn and Sacks
[
KS84
]
.

Conjecture 1.6

(Kahn–Saks)
.

Let
\eta(d)
denotes the supremum of
\delta(P)
over all finite posets
P

of width
d
. Then
\eta(d)\to 0
as
d\to\infty
.

The most notable result in this direction is due to Komlós
[
Kom90
]
,
who proved it for height
2
posets, as well as posets with
n/f(n)

minimal elements, for some undetermined, but possibly very
slowly growing function
f(n)=\omega(1)
. Similarly, Korshunov
[
Kor94
]
proved
that Conjecture
1.6
holds for
random posets
, which are
known to have height
3
w.h.p.
[
KR75
]
. Note that these are the
opposite extremes to our setting, as we consider posets
P_{\lambda/\mu}
with
width
d=O(1)
and height
\Theta(n)
, see
also
\lx@sectionsign
13.1
.

Before we conclude, let us note that for general posets,
counting the number
e(P)
of linear extensions, as well as
computing the sorting probability
\delta(P)
, is
#P
-complete
[
BW91
]
.
Thus, there is little hope of getting good asymptotic bounds
on
\delta(P)
, except possibly for one of several notions of
“random poset”
[
Bri93
]
and “large poset”
[
Jan11
]
.
In fact, the same complexity results hold for counting linear extensions
of general
2
-dimensional posets, as well as for posets of
height
2
; both results are recently proved in
[
DP18
]
.
This makes (skew) Young diagrams refreshingly accessible
in comparison.

1.4
Prior work on asymptotics for standard Young tableaux

The combinatorics of standard Young tableaux is a classical subject, but
until relatively recently, much of the work was on exact counting rather
than on asymptotics and probabilistic aspects.

The
hook-length formula
(HLF) gives an explicit product formula
for
e(P_{\lambda})=|\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda)|
, see e.g.
[
Sta99
]
. In the

stable limit shape
, the Young diagram
\lambda
scaled by
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
in both directions
\to\pi
, a curve of area 1.
Then the HLF gives a tight asymptotic bound for
e(P_{\lambda})
via
hook integral

[
VK81
]

(see also
[
MPP4
]
).
Feit’s determinant formula

is an exact formula for
f(\lambda/\mu)
, which can also be
derived from the
Jacobi–Trudi identity
for skew shapes,
see e.g.
[
Sta99
]
. Unfortunately, its determinantal nature
makes finding exact asymptotics exceedingly difficult, see e.g.
[
BR10
,
MPP4
]
.

For large skew shapes, Okounkov–Olshanski
[
OO98
]
and
Stanley
[
Sta03
]
computed the asymptotics of
f(\lambda/\mu)

for fixed
\mu
, as
|\lambda|\to\infty
. Both papers rely on
the
factorial Schur functions
introduced by Macdonald
in
[
Mac92
,
\lx@sectionsign
6]
. The
Naruse hook-length formula
(NHLF)
was introduced by Hiroshi Naruse in a talk in 2014, and given multiple
proofs and generalizations in
[
MPP1
,
MPP2
]
. While the
formula itself is algebro-geometric in nature, coming from
the equivariant cohomology of the Grassmannian,
some of the proofs are direct and combinatorial, using
factorial Schur functions and explicit bijections
[
Kon
,
MPP1
,
MPP2
]

(see also
[
Pak21
]
for an overview).

In
[
MPP4
]
, Morales–Pak–Panova used the NHLF and the hook integral
approach to prove an exact asymptotic formula for
f(\lambda/\mu)
when

\lambda/\mu
have a TVK
(\alpha,\beta)
-shape. In
[
MPT18
]
, based on
a bijection with lozenge tilings given in
[
MPP3
]
and the variational
principle in
[
CKP01
]
, Morales–Pak–Tassy proved an asymptotic
formula for
f(\lambda/\mu)
when both
\lambda
and
\mu
have a stable limit shape.
In a parallel investigation, Pittel–Romik
[
PR07
]
found limit curves for
the shape of random Young tableaux of a rectangle. Most recently,
Sun
[
Sun18
]
established existence of such limit curves for general
skew stable limit shapes.

1.5
Some examples

The main difficulty in estimating the sorting probability
is finding the “right”
sorting elements

x,y\in X
, such that,
even when suboptimal, still give a good bound for
\delta(P;\hskip 0.85355ptx,y)
.
To better understand this issue, let us illustrate the
sorting probability in some simple examples.

First, take

\lambda=(n,1)
and
\mu=(1)
. Then poset
P_{\lambda/\mu}
consists
of two chains, of length
1
and
(n-1)
. There is an easy optimal
pair of elements
x=(1,\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor)
and
y=(2,1)
.
Then
\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu})=0
for even
n
, and
\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu})=\frac{1}{n}
for odd
n
. Similarly, let
\lambda=(n,2)
and
\mu=(2)
. The poset
P_{\lambda/\mu}
again
consists of two chains, of length
2
and
(n-2)
. In this case, the

x
as above give suboptimal
\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu};\hskip 1.70709ptx,y)\sim\frac{1}{2}
.
Perhaps counterintuitively, the optimal sorting elements are
y=(2,1)
and
x=(1,m)
, where
m=n(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})+O(1)
.
We have
\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu};\hskip 1.70709ptx,y)=\Theta(\frac{1}{n})
bound
in this case. We generalize this example in
\lx@sectionsign
3.2
.

Now let
\lambda=(m,m)
,
\mu=\varnothing
,
n=2m
. We have

f(\lambda/\mu)=|\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(m,m)|=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}
,
the
Catalan number
. One can check in this case that

\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu};\hskip 1.70709ptx,y)=\Omega(1)
for
y=(2,1)

and every
x=(1,i)
. In fact, the bounds that work in this
case are given by
x=\bigl(1,\frac{m}{2}+k\bigr)
and
y=\bigl(2,\frac{m}{2}-k\bigr)
, for some
k=\Theta(\sqrt{m})
. We prove in
[
CPP21
]
that
\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu})=O\bigl(n^{-5/4}\bigr)
by a direct asymptotic
argument.
A weaker
O\bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)
bound
can be proved via standard bijection from standard
Young tableaux
A\in\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(m,m)
and Dyck paths
(0,0)\to(m,m)
,
which in the limit
m\to\infty
converge to the Brownian excursion
(see e.g.
[
Pit06
]
). This is the motivational example for this paper.

1.6
Our work in context

The differences between various approaches can now be explained
in the way the authors look for the sorting elements. In
[
Lin84
]
,
Linial takes
P
of width two, breaks it into two chains, takes
x

to be the minimal element in one of them and looks for
y
in
another chain. As the previous examples show, this approach can
never give
\delta(P)=o(1)
for general Young tableaux even
with two rows. This approach has been influential, and was later
refined and applied in a more general settings, see e.g.
[
Bri89
,
Zag12
]
.

In
[
KS84
]
and followup papers
[
BFT95
,
KL91
,
Kom90
,
Zag12
]
, a more
complicated pigeonhole principle is used, at the end of which
there is no clear picture of what sorting elements are chosen.
In fact, the geometric approach in
[
KS84
,
KL91
]
can never
give
\delta(P)=o(1)
, as they also point out, cf.
[
Saks85
]
.
The paper most relevant to our paper is
[
OS18
]
, where the authors look
for elements
x,y
on the boundary
\partial\lambda
, and apply
the pigeonhole principle, Linial-style. Already in the Catalan
example this approach cannot be used to prove that

\delta(P;\hskip 0.85355ptx,y)=o(1)
.

Now, following
[
PR07
,
Sun18
]
, let
\lambda\vdash n
be the stable
limit shape. It is natural to take
x
and
y
from the same limit curve
C_{\lambda}(\alpha):=\partial\{(i,j)\in\lambda,\hskip 1.70709ptA(i,j)\leq\alpha\hskip 0.85355ptn\}
,
where
0<\alpha<1
, and
A\in\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda)
is a
uniform standard Young tableau of shape
\lambda
.
An example of these limit curves is given in Figure
1.3
.
Since the curves
C_{\lambda}(\alpha)
have
\Theta(\sqrt{n})
elements,
and all
(i,j)\in C_{\lambda}(\alpha)
can be permuted nearly independently,
this could in principle give a small sorting probability.
Making this precise would be both interesting and challenging, but this
approach fails in our case, since we have
d=O(1)
rows.
It does have a few heuristic implications.

Figure 1.3:
The limit curves in a
d\times 2d
rectangle (created by Dan Romik, April 2020).

On the one hand, there are likely to be many good sorting pairs of
elements
x=(i,j)
,
y=(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
, for all
i<j
.
On the other hand, in general, the limit curves do not have
a closed-form formula of any kind, and arise as the solution of
a variational problem
[
Sun18
]
. The same holds for the
asymptotics of
f(\lambda/\mu)

[
MPT18
]
. As a consequence,
we are essentially forced to make an indirect argument,
which proves the result without explicitly
specifying the exact location of
x,y
in
\lambda
.

Our approach is based on a combination of tools and ideas
from algebraic combinatorics and discrete probability.
The general philosophy is somewhat similar to the pigeonhole
principle of Linial
[
Lin84
]
, in the sense that we find a
sorting pair
x=(1,a)
and
y=(2,b)
by searching
over suitable choices of
a,b
. As in the Catalan case,
we start with extreme cases
a=\lambda_{1}
,
b=\mu_{2}+1
, and
decrease
(a-b)
until the sorting probabilities
of
x
and
y
becomes small. The main difficulty,
of course, is estimating these sorting probabilities.

In fact, by analogy with the Catalan example, one can interpret
random standard Young tableaux as random walks from
(0,\ldots,0)
to
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
, which are confined
to a certain simplex region in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
defined by combinatorial
constraints.
The sorting probability
\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu};\hskip 1.70709ptx,y)
can then
be interpreted as the probability the walk passes below versus
above of certain codimension-2 subspace. These probabilities
are then bounded by comparing the simplex-confined lattice walk with
the usual (unconstrained) lattice walk. This comparison is based
on delicate estimates which largely rely on the Schur functions
technology combined with the NHLF. This technical part occupies
much of the paper.

2.3
Schur polynomials

A

s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\,:=\,\det\bigl(x_{j}^{m_{i}}\bigr)_{i,j=1}^{d}\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq d}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{-1}\hskip 1.70709pt,

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\,:=\,\sum_{A\in\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda,d)}\,\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\hskip 1.70709ptx_{A(i,j)}\,=\,\sum_{A\in\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda,d)}\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt(x_{i})^{t_{i}(A)}\hskip 0.85355pt,

(2.3)

t_{i}(A)\,:=\,\bigl|\bigl\{(j,k)\in\lambda/\mu\,\mid\,A(j,k)=i\bigr\}\bigr|\hskip 1.70709pt,\qquad 1\leq i\leq d\hskip 0.85355pt.

(2.4)

The product formula below is classical and follows from (
2.2
)

s_{\mu}(1,\ldots,1)\,=\,\bigl|\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda,d)\bigr|\,=\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\frac{m_{i}-m_{j}}{j-i}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(2.5)

Theorem 3.1

\displaystyle q_{k+1}

\displaystyle=_{\eqref{eq:qk-formula}}\qquad\frac{(n-k-2)!}{n!}\,\sum_{D^{\prime}\in\ED(\lambda/\nu)}\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{(i,j)\in D^{\prime}}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,j)

\displaystyle\leq_{\eqref{eq:ED-sum-formula}\,\eqref{eq:hook-sum-formula}}\ \frac{(n-k-2)!}{n!}\,\sum_{D\in\ED(\lambda/\mu)}\,\hskip 1.70709pt\hskip 0.85355pt\prod_{(i,j)\in D}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,j)\hskip 1.70709pt(n-k-1)\ =_{\eqref{eq:qk+1-formula}}\ q_{k}\,,

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that

p_{1}\,:=\,\Pbl\bigl[A(1,2)\hskip 1.70709pt<\hskip 1.70709ptA(2,1)\bigr]\,\leq\,\frac{1}{2}\hskip 1.70709pt,

since we can conjugate diagram
\lambda
, otherwise. If
p_{1}\geq\frac{1}{3}
,
this implies
\delta(P_{\lambda};\hskip 0.85355ptx,y)\leq\frac{1}{3}
for
x=(1,2)
and

,
and proves the theorem.

Suppose now that
p\leq\frac{1}{3}
. By the lemma, we have:

\frac{1}{3}\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\hskip 1.70709ptp_{1}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptq_{1}\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\hskip 1.70709ptq_{2}\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\hskip 1.70709pt\ldots\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\hskip 1.70709ptq_{\ell}\hskip 0.85355pt.

Observe that

p_{k}\,:=\,\Pbl\bigl[A(1,2)\hskip 1.70709pt<\hskip 1.70709ptA(k+1,1)\bigr]\,=\,q_{1}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\ldots\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709ptq_{k}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Since
q_{k}\leq\frac{1}{3}
and
q_{1}+\ldots+q_{\ell}=1

this implies that at least one of these probabilities

Therefore, the sorting probability
\delta(P_{\lambda};\hskip 0.85355ptx,y)\leq\frac{1}{3}
for
x=(1,2)
and

∎

3.2
General upper bounds

For a partition
\lambda\vdash n
define the
imbalance

\textrm{q}(\lambda)
as follows:

\textrm{q}(\lambda)\hskip 1.70709pt:=\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\hskip 1.70709pt\sum_{i\leq j}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,i)\hskip 0.85355pth_{\lambda}(j,j+1)\hskip 0.85355pt.

(3.6)

Note that

\sum_{i\leq j}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,i)\hskip 0.85355pth_{\lambda}(j,j+1)\,\leq\,\sum_{i}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,i)\,\sum_{j}h_{\lambda}(j,j+1)\,\leq\,n\hskip 0.85355pt(n-1)\hskip 0.85355pt,

(3.7)

so
0\leq\textrm{q}(\lambda)\leq 1
. The following result is a generalization of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
\lambda\vdash n
, we have:

\delta(P_{\lambda})\,\leq\,\min\big\{\hskip 1.70709pt\textrm{q}(\lambda),\hskip 1.70709pt1-\textrm{q}(\lambda),\hskip 1.70709pt|1-2\hskip 0.85355pt\hskip 0.85355pt\textrm{q}(\lambda)|\hskip 1.70709pt\big\}.

Proof.

In the notation of the proof above, let
k=1
,
\mu=(2)
, and observe that
excited diagrams
D\in\ED(\lambda/\mu)
consist of two squares:
(i,i)

and
(j,j+1)\in\lambda
, s.t.
1\leq i\leq j
. Therefore,

p_{1}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptq_{1}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{f(\lambda/\mu)}{f(\lambda)}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\hskip 1.70709pt\sum_{D\in\ED(\lambda/\mu)}\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{(i,j)\in D}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,j)\,=_{\eqref{eq:imbalance}}\,\textrm{q}(\lambda)\hskip 0.85355pt.

There are three possibilities. First, if
q_{1}\leq\frac{1}{3}
, then
the sorting probability
\delta(P_{\lambda})\leq q_{k}\leq q_{1}
. Similarly, if

q_{1}\geq\frac{2}{3}

\textrm{q}(\lambda^{\prime})=1-q_{1}
,
we have
\delta(P_{\lambda})\leq 1-\textrm{q}(\lambda)

\frac{1}{3}\leq q_{1}\leq\frac{2}{3}
, we have
\delta(P_{\lambda})\leq|1-2\hskip 0.85355ptq_{1}|
by definition of
p_{1}=q_{1}
.
This implies the result. ∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
, and
m=n-\lambda_{1}
. Then:

\delta(P_{\lambda})\,\leq\,\frac{m\hskip 0.85355ptn\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt(m-1)(m-2)}{n(n-1)}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
3.3
to the conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
.
We have

, and

\sum_{j\geq 1}\hskip 0.85355pth_{\lambda^{\prime}}(j,j+1)\,=\,\sum_{j\geq 1}\hskip 0.85355pth_{\lambda}(j+1,j)\,=\,m.

Thus, the first term
i=1
of the summation (

)
for the imbalance
\textrm{q}(\lambda^{\prime})
, is at most
m\hskip 0.85355ptn
. The remaining

are equal to
\textrm{q}(\tau^{\prime})
,
where
\tau=(\lambda_{2}-1,\lambda_{3}-1,\ldots)
of size

. We conclude:

\textrm{q}(\lambda^{\prime})\,\leq\,\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl(m\hskip 0.85355ptn\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\textrm{q}(\tau^{\prime})\bigr)\,\leq_{\eqref{eq:imbalance-max}}\,\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl(m\hskip 0.85355ptn\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt(m-1)(m-2)\bigr),

as desired. ∎

4.1
The balance function

Define

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda/\mu)\,:=\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\hskip 1.70709pt\min\left\{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}+j-i,\frac{\lambda_{i}+d-i}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+j-i}\right\}.

(4.1)

We refer to

as the

(not to be confused
with the
balance constant
). We will need the following simple estimate:

Proposition 4.1
.

For all

1\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt\Phi(\lambda/\mu)\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt(d\hskip 0.85355ptN)^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}+d-i\,\geq\,\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+j-i,\ \ \text{and}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}+j-i\,\geq\,1.

(4.3)

for all
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d

The second inequality follows from:

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}+d-i\,\leq\,d\hskip 0.85355ptN,\ \ \text{and}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}+j-i\,\leq\,d\hskip 0.85355ptN,

(4.5)

for all
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d
.
∎

4.2

\varepsilon

(4.6)

Denote by

\varepsilon
-admissible pairs of

Let

\varepsilon
-admissible pair,

\varepsilon\leq 1/d

We have:

\varepsilon\,\leq\,\frac{1}{d}\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}{|\lambda|}\,=\,\frac{|\lambda|-|\mu|}{d\hskip 0.85355pt|\lambda|}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{d}\,,

4.3

\varepsilon

Fix

4.8
A tale of two styles

The underlying logic of the paper is rather convoluted and somewhat
buried in the avalanche of technical estimates, so let us clarify it a bit.
There are really two things going on at the same time. On a higher
level, we develop various probabilistic tools to obtain
the desired estimates. While largely elementary from a technical point
of view, these tools seem to be necessary. They are also unavoidably
tedious largely because we are starting from scratch in the absence
of such approach in the existing literature on the subject.

On a lower level, our probabilistic calculations employ a variety
of highly technical estimates on a host of Young tableau parameters.
Some of the tools involved, such as NHLF (
2.8
),
are relatively recent and come from a long series of works in
Algebraic Combinatorics, including some by the last two authors.
While we make our presentation largely self-contained and clarify the
NHLF in the Warmup Section
3
, this technology remains
difficult and yet to be fully understood on a conceptual level.

To make a musical comparison, we have a guitar duo with a new
accessible melody played on a lead guitar, paired with a famously
difficult theme on a rhythm guitar. The result may appear
cacophonous at first, but we hope the reader can persevere,
become oblivious to the noise, and learn to appreciate the tune.

5
Standard Young tableaux as lattice paths

We interpret the standard Young tableaux
A\in\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu)
as lattice paths
within a simplex in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. We compare them to unconstrained lattice
paths to estimate the sorting probabilities.

5.1
Setup

Let
\lambda/\mu\vdash n
, and let
L\in\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu)
be a uniform random standard Young tableau. Denote by
\textbf{{Z}}=(Z_{0},Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})

the sequence of
Z_{0}=\mu
,
Z_{n}=\lambda
, and
Z_{t}=\{(i,j)\hskip 1.70709pt|\hskip 1.70709ptL(i,j)\leq t\}
is a partition.
Denote by
{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)
the set of all such lattice paths
\textbf{{Z}}:\mu\to\lambda
.
Note that
{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)
is in bijection with
\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu)
.

We write
Z
as a sequence of vectors
\bigl(Z_{t}(1),\ldots,Z_{t}(d)\bigr)_{0\leq t\leq n}\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
.
From this point on, we think of
Z_{t}\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
as a random vector,
and the sequence
(Z_{0},Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
as a random lattice path
\textbf{{Z}}:\mu\to\lambda
in
\mathbb{P}_{d}
. We refer to
Z
as

tableau random walk
. Recall that
\Pblm
denotes the probability over uniform standard
Young tableaux
A\in\operatorname{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu)
. By a mild abuse of notation,
we refer to tableau random walks

as being
sampled from
\Pblm
.

Below we give an upper bound for the sorting probability
\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu})
in terms of the probability of the lattice path

(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
visiting a particular codimension 2 hyperplane
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

5.2
Sorting probability via tableau random walks

Let
(a,b)
be two integers, such that
\mu_{1}<a\leq\lambda_{1}
and
\mu_{2}<b\leq\lambda_{2}
.
Consider the event

\Ac(a,b)\,:=\,\bigl\{\hskip 0.85355pt(Z_{t})_{0\leq t\leq n}\,\mid\,Z_{t}(1)=a,\hskip 1.70709ptZ_{t}(2)=b,\,\text{ for some }\,t\geq 0\hskip 0.85355pt\bigr\}.

In other words,
\Ac(a,b)
is the event that the tableau random walk
\textbf{{Z}}=(Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{n})
intersects the hyperplane in
\Rb^{d}
given by
\bigl\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\hskip 1.70709pt\mid\,x_{1}=a,\hskip 1.70709ptx_{2}=b\bigr\}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
,
\lambda/\mu\vdash n
, and let
a\in\mathbb{N}
, s.t.
\mu_{1}\leq a\leq\lambda_{1}
.
Define

\varphi(a)\hskip 1.70709pt:=\hskip 1.70709pt\max_{\mu_{2}<k\leq\lambda_{2}}\Pblm\big[\Ac(a,k)\big].

(5.1)

Then there exists
b\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\mu_{2}\leq b\leq a
,

\Bigl|\Pblm\bigl[L(1,a)<L(2,b)\bigr]\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\Bigr|\,\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\,\varphi(a).

In particular, we have

\delta(P_{\lambda/\mu})\,\leq\,2\hskip 0.85355pt\varphi(a).

Proof.

Observe that
L(1,a)<L(2,b)
in the language of paths means
Z_{t}(1)=a
and
Z_{t}(2)<b
, for some
0\leq t\leq n
.
By taking the probabilities of both events, we then have

\Pblm\bigl[L(1,a)<L(2,b)\bigr]\,=\,\Pblm\bigl[Z_{t}(1)=a,\ Z_{t}(2)<b\ \text{ for some }t\geq 0\bigr]\,=\,\sum_{k=\mu_{2}}^{b-1}\Pblm\bigl[\Ac(a,k)\bigr].

Denote by
W(a,b)
the sum on the right. It then suffices to show that
W(a,b)\in\left[\frac{1}{2}-\varphi,\frac{1}{2}+\varphi\right]

for some
b\in[\mu_{2},\lambda_{2}]
and
\varphi>0
.

Note that, when
b=\mu_{2}
, the sum has zero summands, so
W(a,b)=0
.
On the other hand, when
b=a
, we have
W(a,b)=1
. As the sum is
nondecreasing, there exists an integer
b^{\prime}\in[\mu_{2},a)
,
such that
W(a,b^{\prime})<\frac{1}{2}
, while
W(a,b^{\prime}+1)\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
This completes our proof.
∎

5.3
Conditioned lattice random walks are tableau random walks

Fix
\lambda/\mu\vdash n
, where
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
as above.
Recall the notation in
\lx@sectionsign
2.6
. Denote by
E=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Define the
lattice random walk

\textbf{{X}}=(X_{0},\ldots,X_{n})
on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, as follows:

X_{0}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pt\mu,\ \ X_{t+1}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptX_{t}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pte_{i}\hskip 1.70709pt,\quad\text{where \ $i\in[d]$ \ is chosen with probability}\ \ q_{i}:=\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{n}\hskip 0.85355pt\bigl(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}\bigr).

(5.2)

Denote by

\Cc\hskip 1.70709pt:=\bigl\{X_{n}=\lambda,\,\textbf{{X}}\in\mathbb{P}_{d}\bigr\}

(5.3)

the event that
\textbf{{X}}\in{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[\hskip 0.85355pt\textbf{{X}}\mid\hskip 0.85355pt\Cc\hskip 1.70709pt\bigr]\,=\,\frac{1}{f(\lambda/\mu)}\hskip 1.70709pt.

The proposition is saying that the lattice random walk
X
conditioned
to
\Cc
coincides with the tableau random walk
Z
defined above.

Proof.

Suppose
(X_{0},\ldots,X_{n})\in{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)
.
Then
X
takes
(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})
steps
e_{i}
.
Therefore,

{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[\hskip 0.85355pt\textbf{{X}}\mid\hskip 0.85355pt\Cc\hskip 1.70709pt\bigr]\,=\,{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[\hskip 0.85355pt\textbf{{X}}\,\bigl|\bigr.\,X_{n}=\lambda,\,\textbf{{X}}\in\mathbb{P}_{d}\bigr]\,\propto\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt(q_{i})^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}\,=\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}{n}\right)^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}.

In other words, conditioned on
\Cc
, the random walk
X
is uniform
in
{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)
. Since
f(\lambda/\mu)=\big|{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)\big|
by definition, we obtain the result.∎

The reason for the non-uniform choice of distribution
Q
given above
will become clear in the next subsection. For now, let us mention that
this distribution is chosen so that
\Eb[X_{n}]=\lambda
.
This is to ensure that the probability
{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[\Cc]
decays polynomially rather than exponentially, i.e., so that the paths
in
{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)
are living in the typical regime and
not the large deviation regime.

5.4
Polynomial decay

Let
\textbf{{X}}=(X_{t})_{1\leq t\leq n}
be the random walk on
\Zb^{d}
defined above.
It follows from Proposition
5.2
that
{\text{\bf P}}[\textbf{{X}}\mid\Cc]
is uniform
in
{\text{\rm Path}}(\lambda/\mu)
. The following lemma gives a lower bound on
{\text{\bf P}}[\Cc]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix
d\geq 2
. There exists an absolute constant
C_{d}>0
such that the
following holds. Let
\lambda/\mu\vdash n
, and
\mu_{i}<\lambda_{i}
,
for all
1\leq i\leq d
. Then

{\text{\bf P}}[\Cc]\,\geq\,C_{d}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{-\frac{d^{2}-1}{2}}.

Proof.

It follows from the proof of Proposition
5.2
that

{\text{\bf P}}\big[\Cc\big]\,=\,\frac{{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[\hskip 0.85355pt\textbf{{X}}\hskip 0.85355pt\bigr]}{{\text{\bf P}}\bigl[\hskip 0.85355pt\textbf{{X}}\mid\hskip 0.85355pt\Cc\hskip 1.70709pt\bigr]}\,=\,f(\lambda/\mu)\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}{n}\right)^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}.

Recall the definition of
F(\lambda/\mu)
in (
2.9
).
Theorem
2.2
and definition (
2.6
) give:

\displaystyle f(\lambda/\mu)\ \geq\ F(\lambda/\mu)\,=\,n!\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda\setminus\mu}\frac{1}{h_{\lambda}(i,j)}\,\geq\,n!\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+d-i)!}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Combining the two equations above, we then get that
{\text{\bf P}}\big[\Cc\big]
is bounded
from below by

\displaystyle{\text{\bf P}}\big[\Cc\big]\,

\displaystyle\geq\,\frac{n!}{n^{n}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+d-i)!}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2\hskip 0.85355pt\pi\hskip 0.85355ptn}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-n}\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+d-i)^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+d-i+1/2}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-\lambda_{i}+\mu_{i}-d+i+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2\hskip 0.85355pt\pi\hskip 0.85355ptn}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-d+i+1}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+d-i)^{d-i+1/2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2\pi}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-d(d-2)}n^{-d^{2}/2+1/2}\hskip 0.85355pt.

Here we used Stirling’s formula (
2.12
) to bound the factorials and

\left(1+\frac{d-i}{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}\right)^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}\,\leq\,e^{d-i}\hskip 0.85355pt.

The assumption
\mu_{i}<\lambda_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, is used to conclude that
(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+d-i)\leq n
. Taking
C_{d}=\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-d(d-2)}
implies the result.
∎

5.5
Most triplets are
\varepsilon
-admissible

We can now prove the main result of this section, that the probability
of
(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)
not being
\varepsilon
-admissible is exponentially small.

Lemma 5.4
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda(n,d,\varepsilon)
be an
\varepsilon
-admissible pair. Suppose
t\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies

\varepsilon^{2}\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\,\frac{t}{n}\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt1-\varepsilon^{2}.

(5.4)

Then there exists a constant
C_{d,\varepsilon}>0
, such that

\Pblm\bigl[(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\notin\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\bigr]\,\leq\,C_{d,\varepsilon}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{\frac{d^{2}-1}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\sqrt{n}}\hskip 0.85355pt.

Proof.

Let

\xi_{t}\,:=\,\frac{t}{n}\hskip 0.85355pt(\lambda-\mu)\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\mu\hskip 0.85355pt.

Note that
\xi_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
is not necessarily in
\mathbb{P}_{d}
. Suppose that
Z_{t}
satisfies

\left\|Z_{t}-\xi_{t}\right\|\,\leq\,n^{3/4}.

(5.5)

This assumption implies:

\displaystyle\left|Z_{t}(i)-\mu_{i}\right|\

\displaystyle\geq\ |\xi_{t}(i)-\mu_{i}|\hskip 1.70709pt-\hskip 1.70709ptn^{3/4}\,=\,\frac{t}{n}(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})\,-\,n^{3/4}

\displaystyle\geq_{\eqref{eq:assumption on t}}\ \varepsilon^{2}(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})\,-\,n^{3/4}\,\geq_{\eqref{eq:def-admiss-pair}}\ \varepsilon^{3}|\lambda|\,-\,n^{3/4}\,\geq\,\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{2}\hskip 1.70709pt|\lambda|\hskip 1.70709pt,

for all
1\leq i\leq d
, and
n
large enough. By the same reasoning, the assumption (
5.5
) implies:

\left|\lambda_{i}-Z_{t}(i)\right|\,\geq\,\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{2}\hskip 1.70709pt|\lambda|\hskip 1.70709pt,

for all
1\leq i\leq d
, and
n
large enough. By the definitions (
4.7
)
and (
4.8
) of
\varepsilon
-admissible triplets, the
assumption (
5.5
) implies that
(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\in\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)

for
n
large enough. We conclude:

\Pblm\bigl[(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\notin\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\bigr]\,\leq\,{\text{\bf P}}\big[\left\|Z_{t}-\xi_{t}\right\|\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\,n^{3/4}\big]\hskip 0.85355pt,

for
n
large enough.

By Proposition
5.2
, the lattice random walk
X
conditioned to
\Cc

coincides with
Z
. Observe that
\xi_{t}=\Eb[X_{t}]
. Since
t\leq n
, we have:

\displaystyle{\text{\bf P}}\big[\left\|Z_{t}-\xi_{t}\right\|\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\,n^{3/4}\big]\,

\displaystyle\leq\,{\text{\bf P}}\big[\left\|X_{t}-\xi_{t}\right\|\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\,n^{3/4}\ \mid\ \Cc\big]\,\leq\,\frac{1}{{\text{\bf P}}[\Cc]}\hskip 1.70709pt\cdot\hskip 1.70709pt{\text{\bf P}}\big[\left\|X_{t}-\xi_{t}\right\|\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\,n^{3/4}\big]

\displaystyle\leq_{(\text{Thm}~\ref{t:Hoeffding's inequality})}\ \frac{1}{{\text{\bf P}}[\Cc]}\hskip 1.70709pt\cdot\hskip 1.70709pt2\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\hskip 0.85355ptn^{3/2}/t}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{{\text{\bf P}}[\Cc]}\hskip 1.70709pt\cdot\hskip 1.70709pt2\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\sqrt{n}}

\displaystyle\leq_{(\text{Lem}~\ref{l:lower bound for Cc})}\ 2\hskip 0.85355ptC_{d}\,n^{\frac{d^{2}-1}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pt\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\sqrt{n}}\hskip 1.70709pt,

for
n
large enough, and where
C_{d}>0
is the constant from Lemma
5.3
.
This implies the result.
∎

6.1
Asymptotics for hook-lengths

In this subsection, we prove an asymptotic estimate for

defined in (

\varepsilon
-admissible pairs

.
First, we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda(n,d,\varepsilon)
be an
\varepsilon
-admissible pair.
Then:

\frac{\lambda_{i}!}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})!}\,\leq\,\prod_{j=1}^{\mu_{i}}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,j)\,\leq\,\varepsilon^{-(d-i)}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\lambda_{i}!}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})!}\hskip 1.70709pt,

for all

Proof.

The lower bound is clear since
h_{\lambda}(i,j)\geq\lambda_{i}-j+1
. For the upper bound,
let

be the largest nonnegative integer such that
\lambda_{J+i}\geq\mu_{i}

It follows from the definition of hook-lengths that

\prod_{j=1}^{\mu_{i}}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,j)\,=\,\frac{\lambda_{i}!}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})!}\,\cdot\,\prod_{k=1}^{d-i}\hskip 1.70709pt(\lambda_{i}+k)\,\prod_{k=1}^{J}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+k}\,\prod_{k=J+1}^{d-i}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+k}+k}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(6.1)

First, note that for all
k>J
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+k}\ \geq

\displaystyle\ \lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}\ \geq\ \varepsilon|\lambda|,

where the first inequality follows from the maximality of
J
,
and the second inequality follows from (
4.6
).
This implies

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{d-i}(\lambda_{i}+k)\,\prod_{k=1}^{J}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}+k}\,\prod_{k=J+1}^{d-i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+k}+k}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\,\prod_{k=1}^{d-i}(|\lambda|+k)\,\prod_{k=1}^{J}\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\lambda|+k}\,\prod_{k=J+1}^{d-i}\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\lambda|+k}\,\leq\,\prod_{k=1}^{d-i}\frac{|\lambda|+k}{\varepsilon|\lambda|+k}\,\leq\,\varepsilon^{-(d-i)},

where the last inequality follows since
\varepsilon^{-1}\geq d\geq 1
, by Proposition
4.2
.
Together with (
6.1
), this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0

G(\lambda/\mu)\,\leq\,F(\lambda/\mu)\,\leq\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\hskip 1.70709ptG(\lambda/\mu)\hskip 1.70709pt,

G(\lambda/\mu)\,:=\,\frac{n!}{(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})!\hskip 1.70709pt\cdots\hskip 0.85355pt(\lambda_{d}-\mu_{d})!}\ \prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+j-i}{\lambda_{i}+j-i}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(6.2)

Proof.

By definition (

The result now follows by substituting the upper and lower bounds
in Lemma
6.1
to the

∎

(6.3)

6.3

(6.4)

(6.5)

Lemma 6.4

Let

,
\varepsilon>0

\left(\frac{g_{i}-g_{j}}{\gamma_{i}+j-i}\right)\,\frac{\min\bigl\{m_{i}-m_{j},\frac{g_{i}}{g_{i}-g_{j}}\bigr\}\,\min\bigl\{g_{i}-g_{j},\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}}\bigr\}}{\min\bigl\{m_{i}-m_{j},\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}}\bigr\}}\ \leq\ \frac{32}{d^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt\varepsilon^{12}}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl((y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}+1\bigr),

Sublemma 6.5

\min\{1,c\}\cdot\min\{x,y\}\,\leq\,\min\{x,cy\}\,\leq\,\max\{1,c\}\cdot\min\{x,y\}.

Sublemma 6.6

For all

, we have:

y\hskip 1.70709pt\cdot\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\min\{x,\frac{1}{y}\}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdot\hskip 0.85355pt\min\{y,\frac{1}{z}\}}{\min\{x,\frac{1}{z}\}}\,\leq\,4\left(y-\frac{x+z}{2}\right)^{2}+4.

Proof of Lemma
6.4
.

We start with estimating

,
\ell_{i}
, and
\gamma_{i}
.
Since

is
\varepsilon
-admissible, we have
the following upper bound for

and

g_{i}\,\leq\,\ell_{i}\,=\,\lambda_{i}+d-i\,\leq\,|\lambda|+d-i\,\leq\,d\hskip 0.85355pt|\lambda|\,\leq_{\eqref{eq:def-admiss-pair}}\ \sum_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\varepsilon}\,=\,\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(6.7)

Similarly, we have the following lower bounds:

\ell_{i}\,=\,\lambda_{i}+d-i\,\geq\,\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}\,\geq_{\eqref{eq:def-admiss-pair}}\ \varepsilon\hskip 0.85355pt|\lambda|\,\geq\,\varepsilon\hskip 0.85355ptn\hskip 0.85355pt,

\gamma_{i}+j-i\,\geq\,\gamma_{i}-\mu_{i}\,\geq_{\eqref{eq:separated-def}}\ \frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{2}\hskip 1.70709pt|\lambda|\,\geq\,\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{2}\hskip 1.70709ptn\hskip 0.85355pt.

(6.9)

Finally, we have the following lower and upper bounds for

p\,=\,1-\frac{|\lambda|-|\gamma|}{n}\,\leq_{\eqref{eq:separated-def}}\ 1\hskip 1.70709pt-\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{d\hskip 0.85355pt\varepsilon^{3}\hskip 0.85355pt|\lambda|}{2\hskip 0.85355ptn}\,\leq\,1-\frac{d\hskip 0.85355pt\varepsilon^{3}}{2}\,,

Now note that

\begin{split}\frac{g_{i}-g_{j}}{\gamma_{i}+j-1}\,\leq_{\eqref{eq:gamma lower bound}}\ \frac{2\hskip 0.85355pt(g_{i}-g_{j})}{\varepsilon^{3}\hskip 0.85355ptn}.\end{split}

(6.12)

6.5

4.2

\min\left\{m_{i}-m_{j},\frac{g_{i}}{g_{i}-g_{j}}\right\}\,\leq_{\eqref{eq:p upper bound},\hskip 1.70709pt\eqref{eq:g l upper bound}}\ \frac{1}{d\varepsilon^{4}}\hskip 1.70709pt\min\left\{{2\hskip 0.85355pt(1-p)\hskip 0.85355pt(m_{i}-m_{j})},\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{n}{g_{i}-g_{j}}\right\},

(6.15)

, and combining these upper bounds with (

\text{LHS \hskip 0.85355ptin~\eqref{eq:min-bound-mgl}}\ \leq\,\frac{8}{d^{2}\varepsilon^{12}}\hskip 0.85355pt\left(\frac{g_{i}-g_{j}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\min\bigl\{\frac{2(1-p)(m_{i}-m_{j})}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{\sqrt{n}}{g_{i}-g_{j}}\bigr\}\,\cdot\,\min\bigl\{\frac{g_{i}-g_{j}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}}\bigr\}}{\min\bigl\{\frac{m_{i}-m_{j}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}}\bigr\}}\hskip 1.70709pt.

∎

6.4
Upper bounds for solid triplets

Recall the definition of solid triplets in
\lx@sectionsign
4.4
.
We can now give an upper bound for the probability that a tableau random walk
\textbf{{Z}}:\mu\to\lambda
goes through

.

Lemma 6.7
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
(\lambda,\gamma,\mu)\in\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)
be an
\varepsilon
-admissible solid triplet, with the solid constant
C
defined
in (
4.10

Let

. Then

\Pblm\big[Z_{k}=\gamma\big]\,\leq\,C^{3}\hskip 0.85355ptC_{d,\varepsilon}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{\frac{1-d}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl((y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}+1\bigr)\hskip 1.70709pt\cdot\hskip 1.70709pt\exp\left[-2\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{2}\right],

(6.16)

for an absolute constant

Proof.

\Pblm\big[Z_{k}\,=\,\gamma\big]\,=\,\frac{f(\gamma/\mu)\hskip 1.70709ptf(\lambda/\gamma)}{f(\lambda/\mu)}\,\leq_{\eqref{eq:if conjecture is true}}\ C^{3}\hskip 1.70709pt\left[\frac{F(\gamma/\mu)\hskip 1.70709ptF(\lambda/\gamma)}{F(\lambda/\mu)}\right]\hskip 1.70709pt\left[\frac{\Phi(\gamma/\mu)\,\Phi(\lambda/\gamma)}{\Phi(\lambda/\mu)}\right].

\displaystyle\frac{F(\gamma/\mu)\hskip 1.70709ptF(\lambda/\gamma)}{F(\lambda/\mu)}\ \leq_{\text{Lem}~\ref{l:F -- asymptotic estimate}}\ \varepsilon^{-d(d-1)}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{G(\gamma/\mu)\hskip 1.70709ptG(\lambda/\gamma)}{G(\lambda/\mu)}

) and (

), we have

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{g_{i}-g_{j}}{\gamma_{i}+j-i}\hskip 1.70709pt\left[\frac{\Phi(\gamma/\mu)\,\Phi(\lambda/\gamma)}{\Phi(\lambda/\mu)}\right]

(6.19)

\displaystyle\leq_{\eqref{eq:G-def}}\ \prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\hskip 1.70709pt\left(\frac{g_{i}-g_{j}}{\gamma_{i}+j-i}\right)\frac{\min\bigl\{m_{i}-m_{j},\frac{g_{i}}{g_{i}-g_{j}}\bigr\}\,\min\bigl\{g_{i}-g_{j},\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}}\bigr\}}{\min\bigl\{m_{i}-m_{j},\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}}\bigr\}}

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{Lem~\ref{l:minimum bound with m,g,l}}}\ \left(\frac{32}{d^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt\varepsilon^{12}}\right)^{d(d-1)/2}\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl((y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}+1\bigr).

The lemma now follows by combining (
6.17
), (
6.18
) and (
6.19
).
∎

Sorting probability via lattice paths

7.1
Sorting probability of
\varepsilon
-admissible pairs

The following technical lemma is central to our proof.

Lemma 7.1
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda(n,d,\varepsilon)
be an
\varepsilon
-admissible pair. Let
a
be an integer that satisfies

\varepsilon\ \leq\ \frac{a-\mu_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}\ \leq\ 1-\varepsilon.

(7.1)

Suppose there exists a constant
C=C_{\lambda,\mu}>0
, such that
for every
\gamma
for which
(\lambda,\gamma,\mu)\in\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)
,
this triplet is solid with solid constant
C
.
Then, there exists an absolute constant
C_{d,\varepsilon}>0
such that

\varphi(a)\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709ptC_{d,\varepsilon}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{C^{3}+1}{\sqrt{n}}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Proof.

Let
b
be an arbitrary integer in
[\mu_{2},\lambda_{2}]
.
It follows from the definition of
\varphi(a)
in (
5.1
) that it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\Pblm\big[\Ac(a,b)\big]\,\leq\,C_{d,\varepsilon}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{C^{3}+1}{\sqrt{n}}\,,\quad\text{for all}\ \ \,\mu_{2}\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709ptb\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt\lambda_{2}\hskip 1.70709pt.

We start with

\begin{split}&\Pblm\big[\Ac(a,b)\big]\ =\ \Pblm\big[Z_{t}(1)=a,\,Z_{t}(2)=b\ \text{ for some }t\geq 0\big]\\
&\quad=\ \Pblm\big[Z_{t}(1)=a,\,Z_{t}(2)=b,\,(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\notin\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\,\text{ for some $t\geq 0$}\big]\\
&\qquad\ +\,\Pblm\big[Z_{t}(1)=a,\,Z_{t}(2)=b,\,(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\in\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\text{ for some $t\geq 0$}\big].\end{split}

(7.2)

We will bound each term in the RHS separately.

Since
Z_{t}(1)=a
, by definition of
Z_{t}
we have:

a-\mu_{1}\ \leq\ t\ \leq\ n-(\lambda_{1}-a).

From (
7.1
) and the assumption that
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda(n,d,\varepsilon)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{t}{n}\ \geq\ \frac{a-\mu_{1}}{n}\ \geq\ \varepsilon\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}{n}\ \geq\ \varepsilon^{2}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{|\lambda|}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\ \varepsilon^{2},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle 1-\frac{t}{n}\ \geq\ \frac{a-\lambda_{1}}{n}\ \geq\ \varepsilon\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\ \varepsilon^{2}.

This implies that condition (
5.4
) holds. By Lemma
5.4
, we then get:

\Pblm\bigl[Z_{t}(1)=a,\,\hskip 1.70709pt(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\notin\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\bigr]\,\leq\,C_{d,\varepsilon}\,n^{\frac{d^{2}-1}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\sqrt{n}},

for all
0\leq t\leq n
, and for some absolute constant
C_{d,\varepsilon}>0
.
Thus, for the first term in the RHS of (
7.2
), we have:

\begin{split}&\Pblm[Z_{t}(1)=a,Z_{t}(a)=b,\ (\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\notin\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\text{ for some $t\geq 0$}]\\
&\leq\ \sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\hskip 1.70709ptC_{d,\varepsilon}\,n^{\frac{d^{2}-1}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\sqrt{n}}\,=\,C_{d,\varepsilon}\,n^{\frac{d^{2}+1}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\sqrt{n}}.\end{split}

(7.3)

For the second term in the RHS of (
7.2
), denote by
\Gc(a,b)
the set of partitions given by

\Gc(a,b)\,:=\,\bigl\{\gamma\ \mid\ \gamma_{1}=a,\ \gamma_{2}=b,\ \text{ and }\Gc(a,b)\in\Lambda(n,d,\varepsilon)\bigr\}.

Then

\begin{split}&\ \Pblm\big[Z_{t}(1)=a,\,Z_{t}(2)=b,\,(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\in\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\text{ for some $t\geq 0$}\big]\ \leq\hskip 1.70709pt\sum_{\gamma\in\Gc(a,b)}\Pblm\big[Z_{t}=\gamma\big]\\
&\qquad\leq_{\text{Lem~\ref{l:pmf of Zt upper bound}}}\ \sum_{\gamma\in\Gc(a,b)}\hskip 1.70709ptC^{3}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{-\frac{(d-1)}{2}}\ \exp\left[-2\hskip 0.85355pt\sum_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 0.85355pty_{i}^{2}\right]\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\bigl((y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}+1\bigr).\end{split}

(7.4)

Using
(x-z)^{2}+1\leq(x^{2}+1)(z^{2}+1)
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\exp\left[-2\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{2}\right]\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\bigl((y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}+1\bigr)\,\leq\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl(y_{i}^{2}+1\bigr)^{d-1}\hskip 0.85355pte^{-2y_{i}^{2}}.

Plugging this upper bound into (
7.4
), we obtain:

\text{RHS of~\eqref{eq:pdf 3}}\,\leq\,C^{3}\hskip 0.85355ptn^{-\frac{(d-1)}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pt\sum_{\gamma\in\Gc(a,b)}\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt(y_{i}^{2}+1)^{d-1}\hskip 0.85355pte^{-2y_{i}^{2}}.

(7.5)

Note that for all
\gamma\in\Gc(a,b)
,
the value
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
is fixed by the assumption that
\gamma_{1}=a
and
\gamma_{2}=b
.
For
i\in\{3,\ldots,d\}
, it follows from (
6.3
) that as
\gamma

varies between
\mu
and
\lambda
, an increment of
\gamma_{i}
to
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}=\gamma_{i}+1
would
lead to an increment in the

\bigl|y_{i}^{\prime}-y_{i}\bigr|\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptn^{-1/2}(1-\frac{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}}{n})\hskip 1.70709pt\geq\hskip 1.70709ptn^{-1/2}(d-1)\varepsilon
(by

-admissibility).
Thus, we can bound each term for

, as

\sum_{z\in n^{-1/2}(d-1)\varepsilon\,\mathbb{Z}}\hskip 1.70709pt(z^{2}+1)^{d-1}\hskip 0.85355pte^{-2z^{2}}\,\leq\,\frac{\sqrt{n}}{(d-1)\varepsilon}\hskip 1.70709pt\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(z^{2}+1)^{d-1}\hskip 0.85355pte^{-2z^{2}}dz\,\leq\,\sqrt{n}\hskip 1.70709ptC^{\prime}_{d,\varepsilon},

since the integral converges.
This allows us to bound (

) as

\begin{split}\ &\sum_{\gamma\in\Gc(a,b)}\ \prod_{i=1}^{d}(y_{i}^{2}+1)^{d-1}e^{-2y_{i}^{2}}\leq\ \left[\prod_{i=1,2}\hskip 1.70709pt(y_{i}^{2}+1)^{d-1}e^{-2y_{i}^{2}}\right]\,n^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl(C^{\prime}_{d,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{d-2}\,\leq\,C^{\prime\prime}_{d,\varepsilon}\,n^{\frac{d-2}{2}},\end{split}

where

.
Thus we get the following upper bound for the second term in the RHS of (

):

\begin{split}\ \Pblm\big[Z_{t}(1)=a,\,Z_{t}(2)=b,\,\hskip 1.70709pt(\lambda,Z_{t},\mu)\in\Omega(n,d,\varepsilon)\,\text{ for some $t\geq 0$}\big]\leq\ C_{d,\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\,C^{3}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.\end{split}

(7.6)

Using the upper bounds from (

) and (

) in (

\displaystyle\Pblm\big[\Ac(a,b)\big]\,\leq\,C_{d,\varepsilon}\,n^{\frac{d^{2}+1}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-2\sqrt{n}}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709ptC_{d,\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\,C^{3}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Since the second term dominates for sufficiently large

\displaystyle\Pblm\big[\Ac(a,b)\big]\,\leq\,C_{d,\varepsilon}\,\frac{C^{3}+1}{\sqrt{n}}\hskip 1.70709pt,

as desired.
∎

8
Upper bounds for the number of standard Young tableaux

8.1
Upper bound via Schur polynomials

In this subsection we give an upper bound to
f(\lambda/\mu)
in terms of
F(\lambda/\mu)
(see (
2.9
)), and evaluations of Schur polynomial
(see (
2.3
)). For
\lambda/\mu\vdash n
, recall the definition
of shifted values
\ell_{i}
and

(see (
6.4

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition.
Then, for every
(i,j)\in\lambda
, and every
k\geq 0
, we have:

\frac{h_{\lambda}(i+k,j+k)}{h_{\lambda}(i,j)}\,\leq\,\frac{\ell_{i+k}}{\ell_{i}}\,.

Proof.

We have:

h_{\lambda}(i+k,j+k)\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,j)-2k+\lambda_{i+k}-\lambda_{i}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pth_{\lambda}(i,j)-k+\ell_{i+k}-\ell_{i}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Note that
h_{\lambda}(i,j)=\lambda_{i}-i+\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-j+1\leq\lambda_{i}+d-i
. Hence:

\frac{h_{\lambda}(i+k,j+k)}{h_{\lambda}(i,j)}\,\leq\,1\hskip 1.70709pt-\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\ell_{i}-\ell_{i+k}+k}{h_{\lambda}(i,j)}\,\leq\,1\hskip 1.70709pt-\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\ell_{i}-\ell_{i+k}+k}{\ell_{i}}\,\leq\,\frac{\ell_{i+k}}{\ell_{i}}\,,

as desired.
∎

We now apply Lemma
8.1

to derive an upper bound for the product of hooks of a flagged tableau.
Let
T\in\FT(\lambda/\mu)
. Recall the notation (
2.4
),
for the number

of
i

T
.
Lemma
8.1

immediately gives:

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and let

be a flagged tableau of
\lambda/\mu
.
Then:

\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{h_{\lambda}\bigl(T(i,j),\hskip 0.85355ptj+T(i,j)-i\bigr)}{h_{\lambda}(i,j)}\ \leq\ \prod_{i=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{(\ell_{i})^{t_{i}(T)}}{(\ell_{i})^{\mu_{i}}}\,.

We now arrive to the main result of this subsection.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and let
\lambda,\mu
be partitions such that
\mu\subseteq\lambda
.
Then

1\ \leq\ \frac{f(\lambda/\mu)}{F(\lambda/\mu)}\ \leq\ \frac{s_{\mu}(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d})}{\ell_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots\ell_{d}^{\mu_{d}}}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Proof.

The lower bound is given in Theorem
2.2
. For the upper bound, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{f(\lambda/\mu)}{F(\lambda/\mu)}\

\displaystyle=_{\text{Thm~\ref{t:NHLF}}}\ \sum_{T\in\Ec(\lambda/\mu)}\,\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}\frac{h_{\lambda}\bigl(T(i,j),j+T(i,j)-i\bigr)}{h_{\lambda}(i,j)}\ \leq_{\text{Cor~\ref{c:asymptotic upper bound for an excited diagram}}}\ \sum_{T\in\Ec(\lambda/\mu)}\,\prod_{i=1}^{d}\,\frac{\ell_{i}^{t_{i}(T)}}{\ell_{i}^{\mu_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{\eqref{eq:definition flagged tableau}}}\ \frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots\ell_{d}^{\mu_{d}}}\,\sum_{T\in\text{SSYT}(\mu)}\ell_{1}^{t_{1}(T)}\ldots\ell_{d}^{t_{d}(T)}\ \leq_{\text{\eqref{eq:Schur polynomial}}}\ \frac{s_{\mu}(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d})}{\ell_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots\ell_{d}^{\mu_{d}}}\,,

as desired.
∎

In this subsection we give a refinement to the upper bound in

An interval decomposition of

is defined
as the following collection of subsets:

(8.1)

for some

For all

when

and

are

, and

Let

.

. Let

, such that

, and let

be an interval decomposition of

.
Then:

\frac{s_{\mu}(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d})}{\ell_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots\ell_{d}^{\mu_{d}}}\ \leq\ C_{d}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\

i\eBnsim j\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}}\,,

for some absolute constant

and Theorem

immediately imply:

(

.

\frac{f(\lambda/\mu)}{F(\lambda/\mu)}\ \leq\ \ C_{d}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\

8.3
Expanding the determinant

We now build toward the proof of Theorem
8.4
.
Our strategy is to break down the Schur function evaluated at the sequence
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d}

into evaluations of separate parts, and use either (
2.2
)
when the values of
\ell_{i}
are sufficiently distinct,
or (
2.5
) when they are close.
Denote

{\text{\rm M}}\,:=\,\Big(x_{j}^{m_{i}}\Big)_{i,j=1}^{d}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(8.4)

Lemma 8.6
.

Fix
d\geq 2
. Let
\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
, and let
x_{1}\geq\ldots\geq x_{d}>0
. Then:

0\,\leq\,\det{\text{\rm M}}\ \leq\ {x_{1}^{m_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{m_{d}}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\frac{(m_{i}-m_{j})(x_{i}-x_{j})}{(j-i)\hskip 0.85355ptx_{i}}\,.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from (
2.2
):

\det{\text{\rm M}}\,\hskip 1.70709pt=\,s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}(x_{i}-x_{j})\,=\,\sum_{A\in\text{SSYT}(\mu)}{x_{1}^{t_{1}(A)}\ldots x_{d}^{t_{d}(A)}}\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}(x_{i}-x_{j})\,\geq\,0.

(8.5)

For the second inequality, since
x_{1}\geq\ldots\geq x_{d}
, and
\mu_{1}\geq t_{1}(A)
,
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\geq t_{1}(A)+t_{2}(A)
,
…, we have:

x_{1}^{t_{1}(A)}\ldots x_{d}^{t_{d}(A)}\,\leq\,x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{\mu_{d}}.

(8.6)

We conclude:

\displaystyle\det{\text{\rm M}}

\displaystyle\leq_{\eqref{eq:det-Schur-lemma},\hskip 1.70709pt\eqref{eq:majorization-Schur}}\ \sum_{A\in\text{SSYT}(\mu)}{x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{\mu_{d}}}\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle\leq_{\eqref{eq:HCF}}\ {x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{\mu_{d}}}\ \prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\,\frac{(m_{i}-m_{j})(x_{i}-x_{j})}{(j-i)}\,,

which implies the result by the definition (
6.4

∎

To simplify presentation, we use notation
\text{{\rm DET}}(A):=|\det(A)|
.

Lemma 8.7
.

Fix
d\geq 2
. Let
\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
,
x_{1}\geq\ldots\geq x_{d}>0
, and let
\mathcal{B}
be an interval decomposition of
[d]
.
Then:

\displaystyle{s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})}\ \leq\ \ \sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\,\frac{x_{1}^{m_{\sigma(1)}}\ldots x_{d}^{m_{\sigma(d)}}}{x_{1}^{d-1}\ldots x_{d}^{d-d}}\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\
i\eBsim j\end{subarray}}\hskip 0.85355pt\frac{|m_{\sigma(i)}-m_{\sigma(j)}|}{j-i}\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\
i\eBnsim j\end{subarray}}\,\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}.

(8.7)

Proof.

Apply the Laplace expansion of

along the interval decomposition
\mathcal{B}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{r})
, defined as in (
8.1
). We get:

\displaystyle\det{\text{\rm M}}\ =\,\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}/{\text{\rm Stab}}(\mathcal{B})}\hskip 1.70709pt\text{sign}(\sigma)\,\prod_{k=1}^{r}\hskip 1.70709pt\det\big[x_{j}^{m_{\sigma(i)}}\big]_{i,j\in B_{k}}\,,

where
{\text{\rm Stab}}(\mathcal{B})\subset S_{d}
is the stabilizer subgroup of
\mathcal{B}
, so
{\text{\rm Stab}}(\mathcal{B})\simeq S_{b_{1}}\times S_{b_{2}-b_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{d-b_{r-1}}

\det{\text{\rm M}}\ \,\leq\ \sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\,\prod_{k=1}^{r}\,\text{{\rm DET}}\hskip 1.70709pt\big[\hskip 0.85355ptx_{j}^{m_{\sigma(i)}}\big]_{i,j\in B_{k}}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(8.8)

We now analyze each term in the right side of (
8.8
) separately.
We have for every
\sigma\in S_{d}
that

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{r}\,\text{{\rm DET}}\hskip 1.70709pt\big[\hskip 0.85355ptx_{j}^{m_{\sigma(i)}}\big]_{i,j\in B_{k}}\

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{Lem~\ref{l:determinant estimate}}}\ \prod_{k=1}^{r}\ \prod_{i\in B_{k}}\,{x_{i}^{m_{\sigma(i)}}}\ \prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in B_{k}\\
j>i\end{subarray}}\frac{|m_{\sigma(i)}-m_{\sigma(j)}|\cdot(x_{i}-x_{j})}{x_{i}\hskip 0.85355pt(j-i)}

\displaystyle\leq\ x_{1}^{m_{\sigma(1)}}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdots\,x_{d}^{m_{\sigma(d)}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\
i\eBsim j\end{subarray}}\frac{|m_{\sigma(i)}-m_{\sigma(j)}|\cdot(x_{i}-x_{j})}{x_{i}\hskip 0.85355pt(j-i)}\,.

Using the inequality above for the RHS of (

), we obtain:

\det{\text{\rm M}}\ \leq\ \sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\,x_{1}^{m_{\sigma(1)}}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdots\,x_{d}^{m_{\sigma(d)}}\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\
i\eBsim j\end{subarray}}\frac{|m_{\sigma(i)}-m_{\sigma(j)}|\cdot(x_{i}-x_{j})}{x_{i}\hskip 0.85355pt(j-i)}

We conclude:

\displaystyle s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\

\displaystyle=_{\eqref{eq:Schur-def}}\ \det{\text{\rm M}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\,\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\ \sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\hskip 1.70709ptx_{1}^{m_{\sigma(1)}}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdots\,x_{d}^{m_{\sigma(d)}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\
i\eBsim j\end{subarray}}\frac{|m_{\sigma(i)}-m_{\sigma(j)}|\cdot(x_{i}-x_{j})}{x_{i}\hskip 0.85355pt(j-i)}

\displaystyle\leq\ \prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\frac{x_{1}^{m_{\sigma(1)}}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdots\,x_{d}^{m_{\sigma(d)}}}{x_{1}^{d-1}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdots\,x_{d}^{d-d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\
i\eBsim j\end{subarray}}\frac{|m_{\sigma(i)}-m_{\sigma(j)}|\cdot(x_{i}-x_{j})}{x_{i}\hskip 0.85355pt(j-i)}

\displaystyle\leq\ \ \sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\frac{x_{1}^{m_{\sigma(1)}}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdots\,x_{d}^{m_{\sigma(d)}}}{x_{1}^{d-1}\hskip 0.85355pt\cdots\,x_{d}^{d-d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d\\

i\eBnsim j\end{subarray}}\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\,,

12
Conjectures and open problems

We believe our results can be further strengthened in several directions,
and would like to mention a few possibilities.

12.1
Sorting probability

The bound
\delta\bigl(P_{\lambda/\mu}\bigr)=O\bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)
that we obtain in
Theorems
1.2
–
1.5
is likely not tight. In fact,
\Omega\bigl(\frac{1}{n}\bigr)
is the only lower bound that we know in some cases
(see
\lx@sectionsign
1.5
). The results in Corollary
3.5

and
[
CPP21
]
also seem to suggest that
O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
is
perhaps the best one can aim for in full generality. We believe the TVK shapes are likely
the easiest case to make progress as they are most similar to the
Catalan poset case:

Conjecture 12.1
.

There is a universal constant
C>0
,
such that for all
d\geq 2
, and for every Thoma sequence
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{>0}
, we have:

\delta\bigl(P_{\lambda}\bigr)\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{C}{n^{5/4}}\,,

where
\lambda\simeq\alpha\hskip 0.85355ptn
is a TVK
\alpha
-shape.

We believe the same bound holds for more general cases. To understand
our reasoning, note that we take
a=\lfloor\lambda_{1}/2\rfloor
in this
case to minimize the sorting probability.
Even if the bound we obtain is tight, by varying
a
one is
likely to obtain lower global minimum in the definition of
the sorting probability. In fact, we believe the following general
claim with a weaker bound:

Conjecture 12.2
.

There is a universal constant
C>0
, such that for every

\lambda\vdash n
,
\lambda\neq(n),(1^{n})
, we have:

\delta\bigl(P_{\lambda}\bigr)\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}\hskip 1.70709pt.

This conjecture is suggesting that the constants
C_{d,\varepsilon}
in Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5

can be made independent of parameters
d
and
\varepsilon
, even though
the proofs give dependence that is relatively wild.
See, e.g. the last line of the proof of Theorem
8.4
.
At the moment, we cannot even prove that
\delta\bigl(P_{\lambda}\bigr)\to 0
for general partitions
\lambda
, with
n=|\lambda|\to\infty
.

In a different direction, suppose
\lambda
is a
3
-dimensional diagram
defined as lower ideals in
\mathbb{N}^{3}
. The tools of this paper are
heavily based on the HLF (
2.7
), NHLF (
2.8
),
asymptotics of Schur functions and other Algebraic Combinatorics
results. None of these are available for
3
-dimensional diagrams,
even for the boxes (products of three chains). Finding new tools
to establish such bounds would be a major breakthrough.

Conjecture 12.3
.

Fix
d,r\geq 2
. Denote by
P_{d,r,m}
the
3
-dimensional poset
given by a
[d\times r\times m]\subset\mathbb{N}^{3}
box (product of chains
on size
d
, and
m
, respectively). Then:

\delta\bigl(P_{d,r,m}\bigr)\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptO\Bigl(\frac{1}{m}\Bigr)\,,\ \ \text{as}\ \hskip 1.70709ptm\to\infty.

A more general problem would be to find conditions on the poset
P=(X,\prec)
of bounded width, which would guarantee
that the sorting probability
\delta(P)\to 0
as the
size
|X|\to\infty
.

[

, Thm 7.21.2]
.
On the other hand, the upper bound (
12.2
) is easy to check in this case.

13
Final remarks

13.1

Although much of the paper is motivated by the work surrounding the

\frac{1}{3}
–
\frac{1}{3}
Conjecture
1.1
, we do not
resolve the conjecture in any new cases. As mentioned in the introduction,
for all skew Young diagrams the conjecture was already established in
[
OS18
]
.
In fact, when compared with the Kahn–Saks Conjecture
1.6
, our
results are counterintuitive since we obtain the conclusion of the conjecture
in a strong form, while the width of our posets remains bounded. Clearly,
much of the subject remains misunderstood and open to further exploration.

13.2

The technical assumption in Theorem
1.3
,
that
\lambda
is
\varepsilon
-thick is likely unnecessary,
but at the moment we do not know how to avoid it.
The same applies for the
\varepsilon
-smooth assumption, and
the Main Theorem
1.5
most likely holds
under much weaker assumptions. Let us remark though,
that in some formal sense these two assumptions are
equivalent.
Indeed, let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
and

\mu=(\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i+2},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
.
The skew shape
\nu:=\lambda/\mu=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})
is then the straight shape, so the
\varepsilon
-smooth condition

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\geq\varepsilon\hskip 0.85355ptn
becomes the

\varepsilon
-thick condition for
\nu
.

13.3

For a fixed number of rows
d=\ell(\lambda)
,
Corollary
3.5
shows that
\delta(P_{\lambda})=O\big(\frac{1}{n}\big)
for all
\lambda\vdash n
, such that
\lambda_{2}=O(1)
.
This is the opposite extreme of
\varepsilon
-thick diagrams
\lambda
,
suggesting that the
\varepsilon
-thick assumption in
Theorem
1.3
might be unnecessary indeed.

13.4

The upper bound in (
11.2
) and (
11.3
) can be
replaced with an arbitrary constant
K
at the cost of changing the positive
constant
C_{d,\varepsilon}
in our results into the positive constant
C_{d,\varepsilon,K}
,
which now also depends on
K
. The rest of the proof follows verbatim and
gives a slight extension of Theorem
1.4
under weaker conditions
\bigl|\lambda_{i}-\alpha_{i}\hskip 0.85355ptn\bigr|\leq K
, and the same for the
\mu
.
We omit the details.

13.5

The Naruse’s hook-length formula (
2.8
) works well
when
|\lambda/\mu|
is relatively small compared to
|\lambda|
.
On the other hand, when
|\mu|

is very small, there is another positive formula due to Okounkov
and Olshanski
[
OO98
]
, which was observed in
[
OO98
,
Sta99
]
to give
sharp estimates in that regime. In
[
MPP1
,
\lx@sectionsign
9.4]
, the authors
suggested that this rule is equivalent to the Knutson–Tao
“equivariant puzzles” rule.
This was proved in
[
MZ+
]
, which reworked the Okounkov–Olshanski
formula in the NHLF-style. It would be interesting to see if this
formula can be used in place of NHLF to obtain sharper bounds on the
sorting probability of skew Young diagrams, at least in some cases.

13.6

When
\lambda=(m^{d})
is a rectangle, one can estimate
\delta(P_{\lambda})
without the NHLF, since
f(\lambda/\mu)
can be computed by the
hook-length formula (
2.7
). This greatly simplifies the
calculations, and is an approach take in
[
CPP21
]
for the
Catalan numbers example
\lambda=\bigl(\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}\bigr)
, see
\lx@sectionsign
1.5
.

13.7

As we mentioned in the previous section, there are several places
where our bounds are likely not sharp. First, the argument
in
\lx@sectionsign
5.2
, is a quantitative version of
Linial’s pigeonhole principle argument, which we also employ
in
\lx@sectionsign
3.1
. But the real obstacle to improving
the
O\bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)
bound is not apparent until
Section
8
, where the interval decompositions
are introduced and a different pigeonhole argument is used.

13.8

Most recently, Conjecture
1.1
was generalized to all Coxeter
groups
[
GG20
]
. It would be interesting to see if our results extend
to this setting.

Rank-expanding satellite operators on the topological knot concordance group
Charles Livingston

Abstract
. Given a fixed knot P ⊂ S 1 × B 2 P\subset S^{1}\times B^{2} and any knot K ⊂ S 3 K\subset S^{3} , one can form the satellite of K K with pattern P P . This operation induces a self-map of the concordance group of knots in S 3 S^{3} . It has been proved by Dai, Hedden, Mallick, and Stoffregen that in the smooth category there exist P P for which this function is rank-expanding; that is, for some K K , the set { P ⁡ ( n ​ K ) } n ∈ ℤ \{P(nK)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}} generates an infinite rank subgroup. Here we demonstrate that similar examples exist in the case of the topological locally flat concordance group. Such examples cannot exist in the algebraic concordance group.

Main theorem

Let

Let

{\mathbb{Z}}

In contrast to the situation in the smooth category, in the algebraic setting

In this paper we will work in the topological locally flat category and use Casson-Gordon theory to prove that

To state the main result, we need to set up some notation. For a knot

Theorem 1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
P
be an even winding number knot in
S^{1}\times B^{2}
satisfying
H_{1}(M_{2}(P(U)))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
for some
n>1
and assume that
V_{1}(P)
generates
H_{1}(M_{2}(P(U))
. Let
\mathcal{J}=\{J_{i}\}
be an infinite set of knots satisfying
\max_{1\leq j<n/2}\{\sigma_{j/n}(J_{i})\}<\min_{1\leq j<n/2}\{\sigma_{j/n}(J_{i+1})\}

\{P(J_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
is linearly independent in the concordance group.

Example.
Let
P(a,b)
denote the
a
–full twisted Whitehead pattern with the clasp having

–full twists. Thus, the standard untwisted Whitehead double is

[

According to
[

. If the meridians of the Hopf link are denoted

|4ab-1|

The curves

satisfies the conditions of the theorem as long as

the Alexander polynomial is trivial and

To apply Theorem

Comment 1.
The condition that

Comment 2.

2.
Preliminary lemma

Lemma 2
.

With
P
as in the main theorem, for every knot
K
,
H_{1}(M_{2}(P(K)))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. There is a canonical isomorphism
H_{1}(M_{2}(P(U))\cong H_{1}(M_{2}(P(K))
.

Proof.

The knot
P(K)
is formed from
P(U)
by removing
\nu(V)
and replacing it with a copy of the complement of
K
. Thus, the space
M_{2}(P(K))
is built from
M_{2}(P(U))
by removing
\nu(V_{1}(P))
and
\nu(V_{2}(P))
and replacing them with copies of the complement of

,
S^{3}\setminus\nu(K)
.

Recall that
H_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu(K))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
. A Mayer-Vietoris argument shows that
H_{1}(M_{2}(P(U)))
and

are both formed as the quotient of

by the subgroup generated by the meridians of

∎

3.
Casson-Gordon invariant results

For any knot
K
and prime
p
we let
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)
denote the
p
–primary summand of
H^{1}(M_{2}(K),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
. Elements of
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)
can be thought of as homomorphisms of
H_{1}(M_{2}(K))
to
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
of order a power of
p
. Using duality (formally, the linking form on
H_{1}(M_{2}(K))
), the group
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)
can be identified with
H_{1}(M_{2}(K))_{(p)}
, the
p
–primary part of the first homology.

Let
\chi\in\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)
. We set
\overline{\tau}_{K}(\chi)=\tau_{K}(\chi)-\tau_{K}(0)\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
\tau_{K}
is the Casson-Gordon invariant defined in
[
2
]
. It is evident that
\overline{\tau}_{K}(0)=0
. Two basic properties are that
\overline{\tau}_{K}(-\chi)=\overline{\tau}_{K}(\chi)
and that
\overline{\tau}_{K}
is additive under connected sum
[
9
]
.

Here is a result of Gilmer
[
9
]
and Litherland
[
16
]
. In the statement, the value of the subscript
\chi(V_{1}(P))
in
\sigma_{\chi(V_{1})}
is viewed as an element in
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
using the natural embedding
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
P\subset S^{1}\times B^{2}
be as above and let
\chi\in\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)
. For all knots
K

\overline{\tau}_{P(K)}(\chi)=\overline{\tau}_{P(U)}(\chi)+2\sigma_{\chi(V_{1}(P))}(K),

where
V_{1}(P)
is defined above.

The main Casson-Gordon theorem concerning slice knots implies the following when restricted to the case of interest here.

Theorem 4
.

If
K
is slice and
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)\cong({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}})^{n}
, then
kn
is even and there is an order
p^{kn/2}
subgroup
\mathcal{M}_{(p)}\subset\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)
satisfying
\overline{\tau}_{K}(\chi)=0
for all
\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
.

Applying this to the case of linear combinations of the knots we are considering yields the following.

Corollary 5
.

Let
P\subset S^{1}\times B^{2}
be as above and let
\{K_{i}\}
be a sequence of knots for which
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(K)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}
. If the connected sum
{\bf K}=\mathbin{\#}_{i}^{n}P(K_{i})
is slice, then there is an order
p^{nk/2}
subgroup
\mathcal{M}_{(p)}\subset\mathcal{H}_{(p)}({\bf K})
with the property that if
(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n})\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
, then

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}(\overline{\tau}_{P(U)}(\chi_{\alpha})+2\sigma_{\chi_{\alpha}(V_{1}(P))}(K_{\alpha}))=0,

where the parameter set
\mathcal{A}
selects the knots

for which the corresponding
\chi_{i}\neq 0
.

4.
Linaer independence

Here we prove the main theorem.

We start out by replacing
\mathcal{J}
with an infinite subset so that the following holds:

0<\overline{\tau}(P(U),\chi)+2\sigma_{\chi(V_{1}(P))}(J_{i})<\overline{\tau}(P(U),\chi^{\prime})+2\sigma_{\chi^{\prime}(V_{1}(P))}(J_{i+1})

for all
\chi\neq 0
,
\chi^{\prime}\neq 0
, and
i\geq 1
. This is possible because there is only a finite set of values of
\overline{\tau}(P(U),\chi)
and the signature functions of the
J_{i}
are assumed to be going to infinity as described in the statement of the theorem.

If the set of knots
\{P(J_{i})\}_{J_{i}\in\mathcal{J}}
is linearly dependent, we can find knots
K_{i}\in\mathcal{J}
and
L_{i}\in\mathcal{J}
such that following concordance relation holds.

{\bf K}=\big(\mathbin{\#}_{i=1}^{m}P(K_{i})\big)\mathbin{\#}-\big(\mathbin{\#}_{j=1}^{n}P(L_{i})\big)=0.

The
K_{i}
need not be distinct and the
L_{i}
need not be distinct, but we can assume that
\{K_{i}\}\cap\{L_{i}\}
is empty.
We can also assume that the indices for the
K_{i}
and
L_{i}
are such that they form nondecreasing sequences with respect to the ordering on
\mathcal{J}
.

The group
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}({\bf K})
splits as
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{2}
where
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}=\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(\mathbin{\#}_{i=1}^{m}P(K_{i}))
and
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{2}=\mathcal{H}_{(p)}(-\mathbin{\#}_{i=1}^{n}P(L_{i}))
. The orders of these summands are
p^{km}
and
p^{kn}
, respectively.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}
and
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}
denote the image of the projections of
\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
to
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{2}
, respectively.

Lemma 6
.

For each
\chi_{1}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}
, there is a unique
\chi_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}
such that
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
.

Proof.

Clearly, for each
\chi_{1}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}
there exists an
\chi_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}
such that
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
. Suppose that
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
and
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
for some
\chi_{2}\neq\chi_{2}^{\prime}
. Then
(0,\chi_{2}-\chi_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}
. This would imply the vanishing of a Casson-Gordon invariant
\overline{\tau}(\mathbin{\#}L_{i},\chi_{2}-\chi_{2}^{\prime})
. By additivity, this is the the sum of nonnegative numbers, at least one of which is positive, and so cannot be 0.
∎

Lemma 7
.

There is an equality of orders
\big|\mathcal{M}_{(p)}\big|=\big|\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}\big|=\big|\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}\big|=p^{nk/2}
.

Proof.

Lemma
6
provides an injection
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}\to\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}
. Similarly there is an injection
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}\to\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}
, implying that
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}
and
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}
have the same order. The projection map
\mathcal{M}_{(p)}\to\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}
is by definition surjective and Lemma
6
implies it is injective.
∎

Lemma 8
.

The are equalities

\big|\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}\big|=\big|\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}\big|=p^{nk/2}=\big|\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{2}\big|=\big|\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(p)}\big|.

Proof.

The product of the orders
\big|\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}\big|
and
\big|\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{2}\big|
is
p^{kn}
. Thus, one of them, say
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}
, is of order at most
p^{nk/2}
. But
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}
contains the subgroup
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{(p)}
of order
p^{nk/2}
. If follows that
\big|\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{1}\big|=p^{nk/2}
. It now follows that we also have that
\big|\mathcal{H}_{(p)}^{2}\big|=p^{nk/2}
.
∎

Conclusion of Proof of Main Theorem.

Choose a prime
p
that divides
n
; this implies that
\mathcal{H}_{p}(P(U))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}
for some
k\geq 1
. Choose a knot
K
among the
K_{i}
or
L_{i}
and a
\chi\neq 0
of order a power of
p
for which
\overline{\tau}(P(U),\chi)+2\sigma_{\chi(V_{1}(P))}(K)
has the minimum value among all such
\chi
. This will be either
K_{1}
or
L_{1}
. Assume it is
K_{1}
. The minimum might be achieved for different values of
\chi
, but it is not attained by any
L_{i}
. We denote one such minimizing
\chi
by
\chi_{1}

We have that
\mathcal{H}_{(p)}\subset({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}})^{n}\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}})^{n}
. There exists an element

(\chi,0,\ldots 0)\oplus(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,\chi_{n/2}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{(p)}

for some set of
\chi_{i}^{\prime}
. If all the
\chi_{i}^{\prime}
are 0, then the Casson-Gordon invariant associated to the element in the left summand is positive and the invariant for the element in the right summand is 0, so they cannot be equal. On the other hand, if any
\chi_{i}^{\prime}
is nonzero, then the Casson-Gordon invariant associated the element in the right summand is greater than that for the left summand.
∎

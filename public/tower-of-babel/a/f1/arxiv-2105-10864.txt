Drag of Two Cylindrical Intruders in a Two-Dimensional Granular Environment
Takumi Kubota 1, Haruto Ishikawa 1, and Satoshi Takada 1,2

Abstract
The drag of two cylindrical intruders in a two-dimensional granular environment is numerically studied by the discrete element method. We find the yield force, below which the intruders cannot move because of interactions with the surrounding particles. Above the yield force, on the other hand, the intruders can move at a constant speed. We investigate the relationship between the drag force and the steady speed of the intruders, where the speed becomes higher as the distance between the intruders decreases. We confirm that the origin of the yield is the Coulombic friction between the particles and the bottom plate by changing the value of the friction coefficient. We also find that the yield force is almost proportional to the friction coefficient, which means that the number of particles determining the yield force is almost constant. On the other hand, the two-dimensional elasticity is applicable to determine the stress fields around the intruders. We confirm that fields asymmetric with respect to the drag direction are reproduced by using the information of the stresses on the surfaces of the intruders by introducing bipolar coordinates.

Although there are few studies on the force below the threshold, it is important to understand this regime because there are fracture and other phenomena.
Once this regime is understood, we can expect to be able to predict the minimum drag force required to move an object, which will yield a reduction of energy loss.
In addition, knowledge of this phenomena may be applied to the application of fracture mechanics or plastic mechanics to granular systems.

4
Discussion

Let us discuss our results.
First, we consider the magnitude of
M_{\rm Y}
.
As shown in Fig
4
,
F_{\rm Y}
is almost constant (
F_{\rm Y}=9.2\times 10^{2}\mu_{\rm b}mg
).
Now, let us estimate this yield force on the bases of a simple argument.
From Fig.
3
, it might be a good estimation that the intruders push the surrounding particles inside a square region whose size is
\Delta+D=30d
.
In this case, the number of particles
N_{\rm c}
present inside this region is approximately given by

N_{\rm c}\simeq\frac{(\Delta+D)^{2}}{\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{8}(1+1.4^{2})d^{2}}\varphi,

(14)

where we assume that the numbers of particles of diameters
d
and
1.4d
are the same.
Under this assumption, the frictional force from the surrounding particles becomes

F_{\rm Y}\simeq\frac{N_{\rm c}}{2}\mu_{\rm b}(1+1.4^{2})mg\simeq\frac{4}{\pi}(\Delta+D)^{2}\varphi\mu_{\rm b}mg.

(15)

For our choice of parameters, this approximately becomes
F_{\rm Y}\simeq 9\times 10^{2}\mu_{\rm b}mg
, which gives a good estimation of the simulation result in Fig.
4
.

Next, let us discuss the stress fields.
Figure
6
shows that two coefficients
E
and
B_{1}
are dominant over other coefficients.
Then, let us assume that

E=-\frac{F_{\rm drag}}{\pi d},\ B_{1}=\frac{F_{\rm drag}}{\pi d},

(16)

as suggested in the previous section.
In this case, the Airy function becomes

\chi=\frac{F_{\rm drag}}{\pi}J(1-\vartheta)\cos\vartheta.

(17)

Let us consider this meaning.
The angle
\vartheta
is related to the angle
\theta
, which is defined as the angle between the vector
(x,y-\Delta/2)
and the
x
-axis.
The relationship is approximately given by
\theta\sim 1-\vartheta\sim\pi/2-\vartheta
.
We also introduce the distance
r
from the center of the upper (bottom) intruder to the point
(x,y)
.
With the aid of Eq. (
29
), this is approximately given by

r=\sqrt{x^{2}+\left(y\mp\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)^{2}}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-D^{2}}}{2\sinh\varrho}.

(18)

Substituting Eq. (
18
) into Eq. (
10
), we get

J=\frac{r\sinh\varrho}{\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta}.

(19)

From Eq. (
19
), Eq. (
11
) can be approximately estimated as

\chi\sim\frac{F_{\rm drag}}{\pi}r\theta\sin\theta\frac{\sinh\varrho}{\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta}.

(20)

In the vicinity of the upper intruder,
\varrho\to\infty
is satisfied.
This means that
\sinh\varrho/(\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta)\to 1
, and then, we get

\chi\to\frac{F_{\rm drag}}{\pi}r\theta\sin\theta,

(21)

which is consistent with the case when the concentration force is applied to a semi-infinite plane
Timoshenko
.

5
Summary

We investigated the drag of two cylindrical objects in a two-dimensional granular environment by the discrete element method.
A yield force was found to exist, and this yield force was insensitive to the distance between the intruders.
When the drag force was above the yield force, we found that the steady speed of the intruders increased as the distance between intruders decreased, because particles between them were influenced by both of them.
On the other hand, the stress fields around the intruders were investigated below the yield force, where the Airy stress function in the bipolar coordinates well reproduced the stress fields obtained from the simulations.

Appendix A
Determination of System Size

In this Appendix, we explain how the system size (
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
) is determined in the simulations.
Because we put the smaller particles at
y=\pm L_{y}/2
, the system size in the
x
-direction must be an integer multiple of the diameter
d
.
We also note that the aspect ratio of the system should be close to a certain value
\nu
.
Under these conditions, let us determine
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
for a given packing fraction
\varphi
.

The areas occupied by the intruders, the bulk particles, and the wall particles are, respectively, given by

\displaystyle S_{\rm intruder}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{4}D^{2}\cdot N_{\rm intruder},

(22a)

\displaystyle S_{\rm bulk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\pi}{4}(N-N_{\rm wall})(1+1.4^{2})d^{2},

(22b)

\displaystyle S_{\rm wall}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\pi}{4}d^{2}N_{\rm wall},

(22c)

where
N_{\rm intruder}
and
N_{\rm wall}
are the numbers of intruders and wall particles, respectively.
Here, the factor
1/2
in Eq. (
22c
) comes from the fact that half of the particles are located inside the system.
Summing up Eqs. (
22a
)–(
22c
), the total area occupied by the intruders, the bulk particles, and the wall particles is given by

S=\frac{\pi}{4}N_{\rm intruder}D^{2}+\frac{\pi}{8}(1+1.4^{2})Nd^{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}1.4^{2}N_{\rm wall}d^{2}.

(23)

If the system size is given by
L\times\nu L
, the size
L
is determined by Eq. (
23
) as

\varphi\nu L^{2}=\frac{\pi}{4}N_{\rm intruder}D^{2}+\frac{\pi}{8}(1+1.4^{2})Nd^{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}1.4^{2}N_{\rm wall}d^{2}.

(24)

Because the wall is made of the smaller particles,
N_{\rm wall}
should be given by

N_{\rm wall}=2\left\lfloor\frac{L}{d}\right\rfloor,

(25)

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the floor function that returns an integer
n
for
x-1<n\leq x
.
In this case, the system area is unchanged when we choose

L_{x}=\lfloor L\rfloor,\quad L_{y}=\frac{\nu L^{2}}{\lfloor L\rfloor}.

(26)

Rigorously, the aspect ratio is larger than
\nu
, but it is very close to
\nu
.
Substituting Eq. (
25
) into Eq. (
24
), we get

L^{2}+\frac{\pi}{4\varphi\nu}1.4^{2}d^{2}\left\lfloor\frac{L}{d}\right\rfloor-\frac{\pi}{4\varphi\nu}N_{\rm intruder}D^{2}-\frac{\pi}{8\varphi\nu}(1+1.4^{2})d^{2}N=0.

(27)

We can numerically determine
L
from this equation.
For our choice of parameters (
N_{\rm intruder}=2
,
N=4000
,
\varphi=0.80
, and
\nu=3/2
), we obtain
L/d\simeq 62.65\cdots
, and then
L_{x}=62d
and
L_{y}=94.96d
.

Appendix B
Case of three intruders

In this Appendix, we investigate how the drag changes when there exist three intruders in the system.

First, we discuss the drag law when the drag force is larger than the yield force.
Figure
10
shows the drag for two and three intruders.
This suggests that the yield force is almost unchanged because the areas of interactions with the intruders are limited to near them.
On the other hand, the difference becomes larger as the steady speed of the intruders increases.
This can be understood as follows.
As the speed increases, the number of particles that interact with the intruders also increases.
Then, there exist areas where particles interact with two adjacent intruders.
This area becomes more movable than other areas.
Therefore, the resistance from the surrounding particles decreases.
Here, a more systematic study is needed but is beyond the scope of this paper.

Appendix C
Derivation of Stress Fields in terms of Bipolar Coordinates

In this Appendix, we revisit the general solutions of the stress fields around intruders when we adopt bipolar coordinates
Jeffery21
.
The bipolar coordinates
(\varrho,\vartheta)
are transformed from the
(x,y)
–plane via the following conformal mapping:

x+iy=ia\coth\left(\frac{\varrho+i\vartheta}{2}\right),

(28)

with an imaginary number
i
and a constant
a=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-D^{2}}/2
.
Here, the explicit relationship between
(\varrho,\vartheta)
and
(x,y)
is given by

\begin{cases}\displaystyle x=\frac{a\sin\vartheta}{\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta}\\
\displaystyle y=\frac{a\sinh\varrho}{\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta}\end{cases}.

(29)

We note that
\varrho={\rm const.}
and
\vartheta={\rm const.}
correspond to the following circles in the
(x,y)
–plane, respectively:

\displaystyle x^{2}+\left(y-a\coth\varrho\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{2}}{\sinh^{2}\varrho},

(30a)

\displaystyle\left(x-a\cot\vartheta\right)^{2}+y^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{2}}{\sin^{2}\vartheta}.

(30b)

For later convenience, we introduce a quantity
J
as

J=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial\varrho}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial\vartheta}\right)^{2}}=\frac{a}{\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta},

(31)

which corresponds to the growth rate of the conformal mapping (
28
).

Now, let us consider the Airy stress function
\chi
.
In the
(x,y)
–plane, the stress function should satisfy

\Delta\Delta\chi(x,y)=0

(32)

with the Laplacian
\Delta\equiv(\partial^{2}/\partial x^{2}+\partial^{2}/\partial y^{2})
.
By using the relationship (
29
), Eq. (
32
) in the
(\varrho,\vartheta)
–plane becomes

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{4}}{\partial\varrho^{4}}+2\frac{\partial^{4}}{\partial\varrho^{2}\partial\vartheta^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{4}}{\partial\vartheta^{4}}-2\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varrho^{2}}+2\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\vartheta^{2}}+1\right)\left(\frac{\chi}{J}\right)=0.

(33)

The general solution of this equation is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\chi}{J}

\displaystyle=(E\cos\vartheta+F\sin\vartheta+G\cosh\varrho+H\sinh\varrho)\vartheta

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+(B_{0}\cosh\varrho+D_{0}\sinh\varrho)\varrho

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+[A_{1}\cosh(2\varrho)+B_{1}+C_{1}\sinh(2\varrho)+D_{1}\varrho]\cos\vartheta

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+[A_{1}^{\prime}\cosh(2\varrho)+C_{1}^{\prime}\sinh(2\varrho)+D_{1}^{\prime}\varrho]\sin\vartheta

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left\{A_{n}\cosh[(n+1)\varrho]+B_{n}\cosh[(n-1)\varrho]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\left.+C_{n}\sinh[(n+1)\varrho]+D_{n}\sinh[(n-1)\varrho]\right\}\cos(n\vartheta)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left\{A_{n}^{\prime}\cosh[(n+1)\varrho]+B_{n}^{\prime}\cosh[(n-1)\varrho]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\left.+C_{n}^{\prime}\sinh[(n+1)\varrho]+D_{n}^{\prime}\sinh[(n-1)\varrho]\right\}\sin(n\vartheta),

(34)

with constants
E
,
F
,
G
,
H
,
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
D_{n}
,
A_{n}^{\prime}
,
B_{n}^{\prime}
,
C_{n}^{\prime}
, and
D_{n}^{\prime}
(
n\geq 0
).
Using Eq. (
34
), the stress can be calculated as
Jeffery21

\displaystyle a\sigma_{\varrho\varrho}

\displaystyle=\left[(\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\vartheta^{2}}-\sinh\varrho\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\left.-\sin\vartheta\frac{\partial}{\partial\vartheta}+\cosh\varrho\right]\left(\frac{\chi}{J}\right)

(35a)

\displaystyle a\sigma_{\vartheta\vartheta}

\displaystyle=\left[(\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varrho^{2}}-\sinh\varrho\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\left.-\sin\vartheta\frac{\partial}{\partial\vartheta}+\cos\vartheta\right]\left(\frac{\chi}{J}\right)

(35b)

\displaystyle a\sigma_{\varrho\vartheta}

\displaystyle=-(\cosh\varrho-\cos\vartheta)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varrho\partial\vartheta}\left(\frac{\chi}{J}\right).

(35c)

These are the general solutions of the stress fields.
Once we get the stresses
\sigma_{\varrho\varrho}
,
\sigma_{\varrho\vartheta}
, and
\sigma_{\vartheta\vartheta}
, the stresses on Cartesian coordinates
\sigma_{xx}
,
\sigma_{xy}
, and
\sigma_{yy}
can be calculated by

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\varrho\varrho}\cos^{2}\gamma+\sigma_{\vartheta\vartheta}\sin^{2}\gamma-2\sigma_{\varrho\vartheta}\sin\gamma\cos\gamma,

(36a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{xy}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{\varrho\varrho}-\sigma_{\vartheta\vartheta})\sin\gamma\cos\gamma+\sigma_{\varrho\vartheta}(\cos^{2}\gamma-\sin^{2}\gamma),

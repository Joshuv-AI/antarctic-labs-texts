Tower multitype and global regularity of the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -Neumann operator
Dmitri Zaitsev

Abstract
. A new approach is given to property ( P q ) (P_{q}) defined by Catlin for q = 1 q=1 in a global and by Sibony in a local context, subsequently extended by Fu-Straube for q > 1 q>1 . This property is known to imply compactness and global regularity in the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -Neumann problem as well as condition R R by Bell-Ligocka. In particular, we provide a self-contained proof of property ( P q ) (P_{q}) for pseudoconvex hypersurfaces of finite D’Angelo q q -type, the case originally studied by Catlin. Moreover, our proof covers more general classes of hypersurfaces inspired by a recent work of Huang-Yin. Proofs are broken down into isolated steps, some of which do not require pseudoconvexity. Our tools include: a new multitype invariant based on distinguished nested sequences of ( 1 , 0 ) (1,0) subbundles, defined in terms of derivatives of the Levi form; real and complex formal orbits; k k -jets of functions relative to pairs of formal submanifolds; relative contact orders generalizing the usual contact orders; a new notion of supertangent vector fields having higher than expected relative contact orders; and a formal variant of a result by Diederich-Fornæss arising as a key step in their proof of Kohn’s ideal termination in the real-analytic case.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Origin of the paper

A fundamental question
for
a boundary value problem for
a system of Partial Differential Equations
is finding conditions for

global regularity
,
i.e. for
(weak) solutions being smooth whenever
the data of the problem are smooth.
Global regularity of the

\bar{\partial}
-Neumann
problem
,
due to its implication
via Kohn’s formula

[
K84
]

of

condition
R

[
BeL80
,
Be81
]
,
is
of further importance
for the study of smooth boundary extension
of proper holomorphic maps,
generalizing the famous Fefferman’s theorem
[
Fe74
]
,
and other geometric applications,
see e.g.
[
Hu93
]
.
For background material on the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem,
that was formulated in the fifties by Spencer as a means
to generalize Hodge theory to non-compact
complex manifolds,
the reader is referred
to
[
FK72
,
D93
,
BS99
,
CS01
,
FS01
,
Za08
,
St10
,
Has14
]
,
in particular,
to
the excellent monograph by Straube
[
St10
]

for specific aspects of the theory used here.

In his influential work,
Catlin

[
C84a
,
C84b
]

developed highly elaborate
geometric and potential-theoretic
techniques
to tackle the problem of
global regularity
of the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem
for

pseudoconvex bounded
domains in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
of finite D’Angelo type

[
D82
]
.
More specifically,
Catlin’s property
(P)

inspired vast
applications
(see §
1.6
)
and
continuing research
on

compactness
in the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem,
that is known to imply global regularity
by the celebrated work of Kohn-Nirenberg
[
KN65
]
.

In a sharp contrast,
there seems to be a lack
in understanding
the orginal work
[
C84b
]
,
which
still, after 40 years, remains
the only available source establishing
property
(P)

(and hence
compactness, global regularity of the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann operator
and condition
R
)
for general
smoothly
bounded

pseudoconvex domains of finite type in any dimension
.
Despite its significance
and numerous requests by experts in the field
(apart from some few more special situations

[
Z17
,
BNZ18
,
A24
]
),
there had not been
any alternative exposition or simplification
of the techniques
covering the general case.

1.2.
Some new techniques introduced here

One of the present paper’s goals
is to fill this gap and provide
a new approach
using a
different multitype invariant
,
called the
tower multitype
,
and a different set of new techniques.
In comparison with approach in
[
C84b
]
,
our tower multitype is
based on
minimization of numbers of vector fields
defining certain

distinguished nested sequences of
(1,0)
subbundles
,
called
towers
,
rather than maximization
of rational weights
over choices of local holomorphic coordinates.
We don’t use weighted
truncations
of geometric objects and their
coordinate representations
(cf.
[
C84b
, §4-§10]
).
Instead,
further new techniques
include:

(1)

formal (Nagano type) orbits

O

and their
complexifications

V

for certain

special subbundles

defined in terms of derivatives
of the Levi form,
that arise as obstruction
to the finiteness of the tower multitype;

(2)

formal CR property

for orbits
O
as above, that
we call the
formal Huang-Yin property

in analogy with its convergent variant
crucial in their work
[
HuY21
]
;
a key consequence is the
regularity of the complexifications
V
of
O
;

(3)

k
-jets relative to a pair
(O,V)
,
or briefly
(k,O,V)
-jets
,
where
O,V
are as above
or, more generally, any pair of formal submanifolds;

(4)

relative contact orders

of real hypersurfaces with pairs
(O,V)

of formal submanifolds,
generalizing usual

contact orders of with complex submanifolds
;

(5)

supertangent vector fields

having higher than expected relative contact orders,
and

complex-supertangent vector fields

L
, such that both
L
and
JL

are supertangent,
for which we establish an important

Lie algebra property

(here
J
is the complex structure);

(6)

infiniteness of relative contact orders
with
(O,V)

when
O
is
complex-tangential
,
(i.e. tangent to the complex distribution of the hypersurface);
this may be regarded as
a formal variant of
the important result by
Diederich-Fornæss

[
DF78
, Proposition 3]

—
a difficult key step in their proof
of Kohn’s ideal termination
in the real-analytic
case, also
Siu

[
S10
, p 1170 and Part IV]
.

1.3.
New finiteness conditions

Another goal is this paper
is to provide a range of

new finiteness
conditions

implying global regularity.
Our inspiration here
comes from the recent
breakthrough work by Huang-Yin
[
HuY21
]

on the Bloom conjecture
comparing 3 different approaches
to (regular) types
going back to Kohn’s fundamental paper
[
K72
]

— respectively based on:

(1)

contact orders with complex submanifolds;

(2)

iterated commutators of sections in subbundles;

(3)

orders of nonzero derivatives of the Levi form along subbundles.

See the survey
[
HuY23
]
for a thorough
discussion and comparison of
respective regular types
defined via these approaches
and for the recent progress on the
Bloom and D’Angelo conjectures.

Using each of the
3
approaches,
we formulate
3
finiteness conditions
on a real hypersurface and show
that each condition leads to global regularity.
Simpler special cases of each of these conditions
are formulated in Theorem
1.2
,
while their refined more general versions in
Theorem
1.8
.
The difference is that
in Theorem
1.2
,

orders of contact, commutators and derivatives

are calculated for arbitrary
submanifolds or subbundles,
while in Theorem
1.8
,

only special submanifolds and subbundles

are used, that are
defined in terms of the geometry of the Levi form.

Both Theorems
1.2
and
1.8
will be reduced
to Theorem
1.11

asserting the same conclusion
under the assumption
of the
q
-finite tower multitype

(Definition
2.7
).
This reduction is obtained via Theorem
1.12

asserting that assumptions in
either of Theorems
1.2
or
1.8

imply
q
-finite tower multitype.
We also provide
Example
1.13
,
where the tower multitype is finite,
while other finiteness conditions fail to hold.

On the other hand, by the celebrated irregularity result
of Christ
[
Ch96
]
using the work of Barrett
[
Ba92
]
,
global regularity does not hold
for general pseudoconvex
smoothly bounded domains,
hence some conditions are needed.

More broadly,
providing conditions implying
global regularity in the

\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem
is the subject
of an active
and vast research area,
for which we refer
to the sources
mentioned in §
1.1

and references therein.

A key step towards global regularity in

It has been extended to arbitrary compact sets

Definition 1.1

1.7.
Three finiteness conditions

We now give precise definitions
of the finiteness conditions
mentioned briefly before in §
1.3

in their simplest form,
while their more general and refined
form appear in Theorem
1.8
below.

As a matter of notation,
when saying “smooth” we always mean the
C^{\infty}
category.
Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be a smooth real hypersurface
with
a
local defining function

r
at a point
p\in S
,
i.e. a smooth real function
in a neighborhood
U
of
p

with
S\cap U=\{r=0\}

and
dr\neq 0
at every point of
U
.
It is an easy consequence of the implicit function theorem
that any two local defining functions
differ by a nonzero smooth function factor.
We write
{\mathbb{C}}TS
for the complexified tangent bundle of
S
,

HS\subset TS
and
{\mathbb{C}}HS\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS

for the complex tangent bundle and its complexification,
and

H^{10}S,H^{01}S=\overline{H^{10}S}\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS
for the smooth
complex subbundles
of the tangent
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
vectors respectively,
so that

{\mathbb{C}}HS=H^{10}S\oplus\overline{H^{10}S}
.

We begin
with special cases of our
finiteness conditions
implying property
(P_{q})

that are the easiest to formulate
and will
provide their more refined versions
in Theorem
1.8

below.

Theorem 1.2

(Special case of Theorem
1.8
)
.

Let
D\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
,

, be a pseudoconvex bounded domain
with smooth

(C^{\infty})

,
and

,

(1)

\gamma\colon({\mathbb{C}}^{q},0)\to({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},p)

S

(2)

(3)

L^{m}\cdots L^{3}\partial r([L^{2},L^{1}])(p)=0

,

Then

.

It is easy to see that conditions in the theorem do not depend

.

Definition 1.5
.

Let

In analogy with Catlin’s argument in

CR submanifold

M\subset S
,
which is any real submanifold of
S

such that the dimension of

H_{x}^{10}M:={\mathbb{C}}T_{x}M\cap H_{x}^{10}S,\quad x\in S,

is constant on
M
.
By
H^{10}M\subset{\mathbb{C}}HM
denote the
(1,0)
bundle
by
HM={\sf Re}H^{10}M\subset TM
its real part,
hence
{\mathbb{C}}HM=H^{10}M\oplus\overline{H^{10}M}
.
We shall omit the manifold
M
whenever it is unambiguous,
i.e. write
H^{10}=H^{10}M
,
{\mathbb{C}}T={\mathbb{C}}TM
etc.
As a slight abuse of notation,
we shall write
L\in E
(instead of
L\in\Gamma(E)
)
for a complex vector field
L

whenever
L
is a section of a subbundle
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}TM
,
and write
L\in E_{1}\cup E_{2}

when
L
is a section of either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
.

As before, by a
complex contact form

\theta
on
S

we mean any nonzero
1
-form on
S

(i.e. nonzero at each point of
S
),
which vanishes
on
{\mathbb{C}}HS=H^{10}S+\overline{H^{10}S}
.
When
M\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
is a real hypersurface
with defining function
r
, one can choose
\theta

to be any nonzero complex multiple of
\partial r|_{M}
.
We shall frequently abbreviate
H^{10}S
to
H^{10}
for brevity.

2.1.
Towers

Our main new tool is a certain

nested sequence of complex
subbundles

E_{s}\subset H^{10}
,
s=0,1,\ldots
, with

{\rm rank\,}E_{s}={\rm rank\,}H^{10}-s,

that we call a
tower
.
For convenience, we include
the definition of
\theta
-dual forms (Definition
1.7
(1))
in part (1) below.

Definition 2.1

(Tower)
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
be a smooth real hypersurface with
a complex contact form
\theta
.

(1)

The
\theta
-dual form
of
an (ordered) collection of complex vector fields

L^{t},\ldots,L^{1}\in H^{10}\cup\overline{H^{10}},\quad t\geq 1,

is
the complex
1
-form

\omega_{L^{t},\ldots,L^{1};\theta}

on
H^{10}

defined
for
L\in H^{10}
,
p\in S
,
by

\begin{cases}\omega_{L^{1};\theta}(L_{p}):=\theta([L,L^{1}])(p)&t=1\\
\omega_{L^{t},\ldots,L^{1};\theta}(L_{p}):=Lf_{L^{t},\ldots,L^{1};\theta}(p),\;\;f_{L^{t},\ldots,L^{1};\theta}:={\sf Re}(L^{t}\cdots L^{3}\theta([L^{2},L^{1}]))&t\geq 2\end{cases},

where for
t\geq 2
, we call
f_{L^{t},\ldots,L^{1};\theta}

the
\theta
-dual function
of the collection
L^{t},\ldots,L^{1}
.

(2)

A
tower on
S
of the multi-order

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in({\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\})^{n}
,
is a nested sequence of
complex subbundles

H^{10}=E_{0}\supset\ldots\supset E_{m},\quad 0\leq m\leq n,

such that
t_{m+1}=\ldots=t_{n}=\infty
, and
for each
k=1,\ldots,m
, one has
t_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}
and
there exists a collection of
t_{k}-1
vector fields

L_{k}^{t_{k}-1},\ldots,L_{k}^{1}\in E_{k-1}\cup\overline{E}_{k-1},

for which the form

\omega_{k}:=\omega_{L_{k}^{t_{k}-1},\ldots,L_{k}^{1};\theta}

does not vanish on
(fibers of)
E_{k-1}
,
and such that
E_{k}
satisfies

E_{k}=E_{k-1}\cap\{\omega_{k}=0\}.

(3)

For any choice of the vector fields
(L_{k}^{s})
as above,
collect all
\theta
-dual functions as defined in (1)
for all
k
with
t_{k}\geq 2
into the set

\{f_{L_{k}^{t_{k}-1},\ldots,L_{k}^{1};\theta}:t_{k}\geq 2\}

that we call an
associated set of functions
of the given tower.

Remarks 2.2
.

(1)

It is clear from part (2) of the definition that
the multi-order of a tower

H^{10}=E_{0}\supset\ldots\supset E_{m}

always satisfies

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in({\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2})^{m}\times(\{\infty\})^{n-m}.

(2)

Note that for

k=1,\ldots,l
,
the
1
-form

\omega_{k}=\theta([\cdot,L_{k}])

is a contraction of the
(restriction of)

Levi tensor

\lambda_{\theta}\colon{\mathbb{C}}HS\times{\mathbb{C}}HS\to{\mathbb{C}},

defined by

\lambda_{\theta}(L^{2}_{p},L^{1}_{p})=\theta([L^{2},L^{1}]_{p}),\quad L^{2},L^{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}HS,\quad p\in S.

The standard hermitian
Levi form

is obtained by choosing a purely imaginary contact form
\theta

for
L^{2}\in H^{10}S
,
L^{1}\in H^{01}S=\overline{H^{10}S}
,
while
\lambda_{\theta}
vanishes on
H^{10}S\times H^{10}S
and

H^{01}S\times H^{01}S

as consequence of the integrability of the induced CR structure.
When
r
is
a defining function of
S
and

\theta=c\partial r
, where
c\neq 0
is a real constant, we have

\lambda_{\theta}=-d\theta=c\partial\bar{\partial}r
by the Cartan formula for the exterior derivative.

(3)

Even though
the
\theta
-dual forms
\omega_{L_{k}^{t_{k}-1},\ldots,L_{k}^{1};\theta}
depend on
the choice of the contact form
\theta
,
the notion of a tower only depends on the (principal)
ideal generated by
\theta
,
which is uniquely determined by the CR structure for hypersurfaces.
Indeed, let

\widetilde{\theta}=h\theta
,
where
h
is a nonzero smooth complex function.
Then

\omega_{L_{k}^{t_{k}-1},\ldots,L_{k}^{1};h\theta}=\omega_{L_{k}^{t_{k}-1},\ldots,hL_{k}^{1};\theta}
.
Hence, with
\widetilde{\theta}
instead of
\theta
,
we can modify the vector fields
(L_{k}^{s})
to obtain the same
forms
\omega_{k}
defining the same tower as claimed.
Since
any two complex contact forms differ by a multiplication with a
complex nonzero smooth function, the tower is defined invariantly independently of the choice of the complex contact form
\theta
.

2.2.
First structure property of towers

The first main property of towers
is the existence of certain CR submanifolds of
S

with controlled restiction of the Levi kernels.

Proposition 2.3

(First structure property)
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
be a smooth real hypersurface
and

H^{10}S=E_{0}\supset\ldots\supset E_{m},

a tower of the multi-order
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
on
S
with
an associated set of functions

\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\}
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

the
restrictions to
H^{10}S
of the differentials

df_{1},\ldots,df_{l}
are linearly independent,
in particular, the zero set

M:=\{f_{1}=\ldots=f_{l}=0\}\subset S

is a smooth CR submanifold;

(2)

the kernel distribution (of varying rank)

K^{10}\subset H^{10}S
of the Levi form of
S

satisfies

H^{10}M\cap K^{10}\subset E_{m}.

Proof.

It follows from Definition
2.1
(2)
that the forms

\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}

defined there
are linearly independent
when restricted to
H^{10}S
.
If the set
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\}
is empty, (1) is void.
Otherwise,
the equality of the sets of the forms

\{\omega_{k}:t_{k}\geq 2\}=\{df_{j}|_{H^{10}S}:1\leq j\leq l\}

proves (1).

To show (2), let
\xi\in K^{10}
.
Since
\omega_{k}=\omega_{L_{k};\theta}=\lambda_{\theta}(\cdot,L_{k})

when
t_{k}=1

in the notation of Definition
2.1
(1),
it follows that
\omega_{k}(\xi)=0
.
On the other hand, when
t_{k}\geq 2
,

\xi\in H^{10}M

implies

\omega_{k}(\xi)=df_{l_{k}}(\xi)=0
for some
l_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
.
Hence

\xi\in K^{10}\cap H^{10}M\implies\xi\in H^{10}S\cap\{\omega_{1}=\ldots=\omega_{m}=0\}=E_{m}

as desired.
∎

2.3.
Tower multitype

Using the notion of the tower (Definition
2.1
),
we define
a CR invariant at each point as follows:

Definition 2.4

(tower multitype)
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
be a smooth real hypersurface.
The
tower multitype
of a point
p\in S

{\mathcal{T}}(p)\in({\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\})^{n},

is the
lexicographically minimum multi-order

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})

of a tower
on a neighborhood of
p
in
S
.

In view of Remark
2.2
(3),
the tower multitype
{\mathcal{T}}(p)
is independent
of the contact form
\theta
and hence is a CR invariant of
a germ of
S
at
p
.
Since the multi-orders
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
in Definition
2.4

consist of integers
and
\infty
, their lexicographic minimum
always
exists and is realized for certains towers.
Our second main property is for those towers
whose multi-orders realize
their lexicographic minimum —
the tower multitype:

Proposition 2.5

(Second structure property)
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
be a smooth real hypersurface,

p\in S
a point,
U\subset S

an open neighborhood of
p
, and

H^{10}U=E_{0}\supset\ldots\supset E_{m}

a tower
on
U
,
whose multi-order equals the multitype
{\mathcal{T}}(p)
.
Choose
any associated set of functions

\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\}

of the given tower
(see Definition
2.1
(3)).
Then the following hold:

(1)

{\mathcal{T}}(p^{\prime})\leq{\mathcal{T}}(p)
for any
p^{\prime}\in U
;

(2)

the tower multitype level set satisfies

\{p^{\prime}\in U:{\mathcal{T}}(p^{\prime})={\mathcal{T}}(p)\}\subset\{f_{1}=\ldots=f_{l}=0\}.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{T}}(p)=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})

is the multi-order of the given tower on
U

and
{\mathcal{T}}(p^{\prime})
is the minimum multi-order for a tower on
a neighborhood of
p^{\prime}
,
(1) is immediate.

To show (2), let
p^{\prime}\in U
be such that

f_{j}(p^{\prime})\neq 0
for some
j=1,\ldots,l
,
which
in view of Definition
2.1
, is of the form

(2.1)

f_{j}(p^{\prime})={\sf Re}(L_{k}^{t_{k}-1}\cdots L_{k}^{3}\theta([L_{k}^{2},L_{k}^{1}]))(p^{\prime})\neq 0,\quad L_{k}^{t_{k}-1},\cdots,L_{k}^{1}\in E_{k-1}\cup\overline{E}_{k-1}

for some
k
that we choose to be minimal with this property
for any
j
.
If
L_{k}^{t_{k}-1}\in\overline{E}_{k-1}
, taking conjugates of all vector fields
and of
\theta
and replacing
\theta

with
f\theta
, where
f
is a nonzero function,
we may assume that
L_{k}^{t_{k}-1}\in E_{k-1}
. In particular,
we obtain

\omega^{\prime}_{k}|_{(E_{k-1})_{p^{\prime}}}\neq 0,

where
for
x\in S
,
L\in\Gamma(H^{10}S)
,

\omega^{\prime}_{k}(L_{x}):=\theta([L,L_{k}^{1})(x)\text{ for }t_{k}=3,

or

\omega^{\prime}_{k}(L_{x}):=LL_{k}^{t_{k}-2}\cdots L_{k}^{3}\theta([L_{k}^{2},L_{k}^{1}]))(x)\text{ for }t_{k}>3.

In the case
t_{k}=3
, we have
\omega^{\prime}_{k}=\omega_{L_{k}^{1};\theta}

in the notation of Definition
2.1
.
In the case
t_{k}>3
,
splitting into real and imaginary parts, we obtain

\omega^{\prime}_{k}(L_{p^{\prime}})=L({\sf Re}f+i{\sf Im}f)(p^{\prime}),\quad f:=L_{k}^{t_{k}-2}\cdots L_{k}^{3}\theta([L_{k}^{2},L_{k}^{1}])).

Taking the term that does not identically vanish for
L_{p^{\prime}}\in(E_{k-1})_{p^{\prime}}

and multiplying
L_{k}^{t_{k}-2}
by
i
if necessary,
we may assume

\omega^{\prime}_{k}(L_{x})=L{\sf Re}f(x)
, hence

\omega^{\prime}_{k}=\omega_{L_{k}^{t_{k}-2},\ldots,L_{k}^{1};\theta}
.
In both cases, we obtain a new tower

H^{10}U^{\prime}=E_{0}\supset\ldots\supset E_{k-1}\supset E^{\prime}_{k}

in a neighborhood
U^{\prime}\subset U
of
p^{\prime}
of
the lexicographically smaller multi-order

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k-1},t_{k}-1,\infty,\ldots,\infty)<(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k-1},t_{k},\ldots,t_{n}),

by setting

E^{\prime}_{k}:=E_{k-1}\cap\{\omega^{\prime}_{k}=0\}.

In view of Definition
2.4
, it follows that

{\mathcal{T}}(p^{\prime})<{\mathcal{T}}(p)
, hence
p^{\prime}
is not in the level set of
p
,
completing the proof of (2).
∎

Part (1) of Proposition
2.5
immediately yields:

Corollary 2.6
.

For a smooth real hypersurface
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
,
the following hold:

(1)

the tower multitype
{\mathcal{T}}(p)
is upper-semicontinuous as function of
p\in S
;

(2)

any tower multitype level set
\{q\in S:{\mathcal{T}}(q)={\rm const}\}

is locally closed, i.e. closed in its open neighborhood.

2.4.
Hypersurfaces of
q
-finite tower multitype

We now define a finiteness condition for the multitype
depending on the integer
q

that will guarantee that the
decomposition into level sets of the tower multitype
defines
a stratification as in Definition
1.14
.

Definition 2.7
.

An
n
-tuple
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in({\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\})^{n}

is called

q
-finite
, where
1\leq q\leq n
is an integer,
if the number of the entries
t_{k}
equal to
\infty
is less than
q
.
(In particular, the
n
-tuple is
1
-finite if all entries
are finite.)
If the tuple is not
q
-finite, we call it

q
-infinite
.

We can now establish

countable
q
-regularity

defined in
Definition
1.14

as a consequence of the
q
-finiteness of the tower multitype:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
be a
(not necessarily pseudoconvex)
smooth hypersurface
whose
tower multitype is
q
-finite at every point.
Then
S
is countably
q
-regular
in the sense of Definition
1.14

where the “strata”
S_{k}

can be chosen to be the level sets
of the tower multitype function
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

Since the tower multitype of
S
is

q
-finite at every point,

S
splits into the countable disjoint union of the
{\mathcal{T}}
-level sets

(2.2)

S=\bigcup_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in({\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\})^{n}}\{p:{\mathcal{T}}(p)=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\}.

By
Corollary
2.6
,
each level set of
{\mathcal{T}}

is locally closed,
and by the Second structure property (Proposition
2.5
), it
is locally contained in the zero set

M=\{f_{1}=\ldots=f_{l}=0\},

where
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\}
is an associated
set of functions
of a tower

H^{10}S=E_{0}\supset\ldots\supset E_{m}

on an open subset of
S
.

In view of the
q
-finiteness assumption
and Remark
2.2
(1),
we must have

{\rm rank\,}E_{m}={\rm rank\,}H^{10}S-m=n-m,

which implies
{\rm rank\,}E_{m}<q
.
On the other hand, by the First structure property (Proposition
2.3
),

M
is a CR submanifold of
S

satisfying

H^{10}M\cap K^{10}\subset E_{m},

where
K^{10}
is the distribution of the kernels of the Levi form of
S
, hence

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(H^{10}_{x}\cap K^{10}_{x})\leq{\rm rank\,}E_{m}<q

for all
x\in M
.
Thus, the decomposition (
2.2
)
satisfies all conditions of Definition
1.14

and the proof is complete.
∎

3.1.
Subbundles of inifinite Levi type

We thus turn to study points where the multitype is

q
-infinite

and begin by establishing the existence
of

subbundles of infinite Levi type

along which the Levi form of the hypersurface
vanishes
together with its arbitrarily high derivatives
at the given point.
As before, we write
L\in E
instead of
L\in\Gamma(E)
for brevity.
For the reader’s convenience,
we restate the definition of the
Levi type of a subbundle

(Definition

(2)):

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
be a smooth real hypersurface
with a complex contact form
\theta

in a neighborhood of a point
p\in S
.

(1)

The
Levi type of a complex subbundle

E\subset H^{10}S
at
p
is
defined by

c(E,p):=\min\{t\geq 2:\exists L^{t},\ldots,L^{1}\in E\cup\overline{E},L^{t}\cdots L^{3}\theta([L^{2},L^{1}])(p)\neq 0\}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\}.

(2)

If the set inside the minimum above is empty,
i.e.
c(E,p)=\infty
,
the subbundle

is said to be

of infinite Levi type at

and any smooth vector fields

one has

(3.1)

L^{t}\cdots L^{3}\theta([L^{2},L^{1}])(p)=0.

Since
\theta
is unique up to a multiple with a nonzero function,
this definition is independent of
\theta
.

Remark 3.2
.

The invariant
c(E,p)
in Definition
3.1
slightly
differs from
the original definition of Bloom
in that we
differentiate the Levi form
evaluated along vector fields
instead of differentiating its trace as
it is done in

[
Bl81

.
However, in their recent work
Huang-Yin
[

]

proved that both versions — with or without the trace —
yield the same values of

for

pseudoconvex hypersurfaces
.

Subbundles of infinite Levi type arise naturally
at points of
q
-infinite tower multitype
(see Definition

):

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}

be a smooth real hypersurface a complex contact form
\theta
.
Assume that a point

has

q
-infinite tower multitype

,

U\subset S
is
an open neighborhood of

, and

(3.2)

satisfies

and

.

Proof.

In view of Definition

,
the multi-order of the given tower has the form

with

Since

is

-infinite,

implies

Next assume by contradiction that

L^{t}\cdots L^{3}\theta([L^{2},L^{1}])(p)\neq 0

for some

and some choice of vector fields

Then, by repeating the arguments of the proof of Proposition

∎

4.
Formal orbits

4.1.
Real formal orbit of a subbundle

When working with
vector fields given by convergent power series,
identities (
3.1
) for a fixed point
p

provide some information along
the so-called Nagano leaf, see e.g.
[
BER99
]
.
In the smooth case considered here, we instead have to consider
the so-called
formal orbit
.
We use some basic geometric theory of formal power series
that is reviewed in the Appendix §
11
.

For every complex vector field
L\in{\mathbb{C}}T{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

and
p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, consider its

\infty
-jet represented by the formal Taylor series

j_{p}^{\infty}L\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x-p]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m},\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),

where
{\mathbb{C}}[[x-p]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

consists of complex vector fields

\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}=\sum_{j,\alpha}a_{j,\alpha}(x-p)^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}},\quad a_{j,\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}},

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

with formal power series coefficients,
where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 0}^{m}
are multi-indices.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a smooth submanifold
and

E\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS

a smooth complex subbundle.
To construct the
real formal orbit of
E
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
we have to work with formal vector fields in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Consider the space
j^{\infty}_{p}E
of
\infty
-jets
at
p

of all complex vector fields in

{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

whose restrictions to
S
are in
E
:

j_{p}^{\infty}E:=\{j_{p}^{\infty}L:L\in{\mathbb{C}}T{\mathbb{R}}^{m},L|_{S}\in E\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x-p]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m}.

To obtain
real
formal vector fields, consider the real part

{\sf Re}j_{p}^{\infty}E:=\{{\sf Re}L:L\in j_{p}^{\infty}E\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x-p]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m}.

Note that since
E
is a complex subbundle,
we have the equality
{\sf Re}j_{p}^{\infty}E={\sf Im}j_{p}^{\infty}E
.
Finally, consider the generated Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p):={\sf Lie}({\sf Re}j^{\infty}_{p}E)\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x-p]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m}.

Now we can use Proposition
11.15

and the following definition
of the orbit of a Lie algebra of formal vector fields
from the Appendix in the following definition:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smooth submanifold
and
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS
a smooth complex subbundle.
The
real formal orbit

{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
of
E
at
p\in S

is defined to be the orbit of the Lie subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
of the Lie algebra
of all real formal vector fields in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

Recall from Proposition
11.15

that
O={\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is a
real formal submanifold
,
i.e. defined by an ideal
I(O)

in the ring of formal power series
{\mathbb{R}}[[x-p]]
,
with
\dim O=\dim\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
,
such that any formal vector field
L\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)

is tangent to
O
.
The tangency of
L
to
O
is defined as
preservation of the ideal
I(O)
, i.e.
L(I(O))\subset I(O)
.
By Remark
11.16
,
the tangent space to
O
at
p
satisfies

T_{p}O=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p),

where
the tangent space at
p
is defined
by

T_{p}O=\{df_{p}=0:f\in I(O)\},

see Definition
11.5
in the Appendix.
Note that the tangency to

O
for all vector fields in
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)

is equivalent to that for all vector fields in

{\sf Re}j^{\infty}_{p}E={\sf Im}j^{\infty}_{p}E
,
hence also to the tangency for all vector fields in
j^{\infty}_{p}E
.

The orbit
{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
can be compared
with the
\infty
-jet
j^{\infty}_{p}S
of
S
, which is
the formal submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
defined by
the ideal
I(j^{\infty}_{p}S)\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x-p]]
consisting of all Taylor series at
p

of smooth functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
vanishing on
S
.
We have the following inclusion relation between
these two formal submanifolds:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smooth submanifold
and
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS
a smooth complex subbundle.
Then the formal orbit
{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
at
p\in S
is
contained in the
\infty
-jet
j^{\infty}_{p}S

in the sense that
I({\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p))\supset I(j^{\infty}_{p}S)
.

Proof.

Since formal vector fields
L\in j^{\infty}_{p}E

are tangent to the real formal submanifold
O^{\prime}:=j^{\infty}_{p}S
,
i.e.
L(I(O^{\prime}))\subset I(O^{\prime})
,
so are vector fields from the real part
{\sf Re}j^{\infty}_{p}E

and hence from the entire Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
.
Then the last statement in Proposition
11.15

implies that
O^{\prime}
contains the orbit
{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)

of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
as desired.
∎

4.2.
Orbits of subbundles of infinite Levi type

We shall use the following basic property of formal orbits:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a smooth submanifold through
0

and
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS
a smooth complex subbundle.
Let
f
be a complex smooth function in
a neighborhood of
0
in
S
such that

(4.1)

L^{t}\ldots L^{1}f(0)=0

for all
t\geq 0
and all vector fields

L^{t},\ldots,L^{1}\in E
.
Then the Taylor series
j^{\infty}_{0}f
of
f
at
0
vanishes
along the formal orbit
O={\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(0)
of
E
at
0
,
i.e.
j^{\infty}_{0}f\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{q},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m},0)

be a formal parametrization of
O
,
see Definition
11.2
in the Appendix.
It follows from the definition of the formal orbit
that there exists formal vector fields

X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
that span
T_{0}{\mathbb{R}}^{q}

and such that each push-forward
\varphi_{*}X_{j}

is the Taylor series at
0
of a vector field in the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
.
Therefore (
4.1
) implies

(4.2)

X_{j_{1}}\ldots X_{j_{k}}(\varphi^{*}f)(0)=0

for all
k\geq 0
and all indices
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
.
Replacing
X_{j}
by their linear combinations over the ring of formal power series, we may assume
X_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}
.
But then (
4.2
) implies that the Taylor series

j^{\infty}_{0}(\varphi^{*}f)
vanishes.
Since
\varphi
is a parametrization of
O
,
this is equivalent to
j^{\infty}_{0}f\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
as claimed.
∎

Let
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a smooth real hypersurface
with a complex contact form
\theta
.
As customary, we identify
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}

by means of the correspondence
x+iy\mapsto(x,y)

for
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
As immediate corollary of Lemma
4.3
, we obtain:

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
a smooth complex subbundle
E\subset H^{10}S
is
of infinite Levi type at
p

(in the sense of Definition
3.1
).
Then for all
complex vector fields
L^{2},L^{1}
in a neighborhood of
p
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

with
L^{j}|_{S}\in E\cup\overline{E}
,
j=1,2
,
the Taylor series
j^{\infty}_{p}\theta([L^{2},L^{1}])
vanishes
along the formal orbit
{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
, i.e.

j^{\infty}_{p}\theta([L^{2},L^{1}])\in{\mathbb{C}}I({\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)).

Proof.

Indeed, the statement follows by applying
Lemma
4.3
to
f=\theta([L^{2},L^{1}])
.
∎

4.3.
Consequences of pseudoconvexity

Recall that a real hypersurface
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
is

pseudoconvex

if for every
p\in S
, a purely imaginary
contact form
\theta
can be chosen
in a neighborhood of
p
in
S
such that

\theta([L,\overline{L}])\geq 0
holds
for all vector fields
L\in H^{10}S

in a neighborhood of
p
in
S
.
In the sequel we assume
\theta

to be smoothly extended to a neighborhood of
p
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

whenever necessary.

We make use of the following well-known lemma:

Lemma 4.5

(positivity of the lowest weight component)
.

Let
f\geq 0
be a nonnegative smooth function
in a neighborhood of
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Fix a collection of positive weights
\mu_{j}>0
,
j=1,\ldots,m
,
and number
k\geq 0
,
and consider a decomposition

f=f_{k}+f_{>k},

where
f_{k}
is weighted homogeneous of degree
k
,
while the Taylor expansion of
f_{>k}
at
0
consists of terms
of weight
>k
.
Then
f_{k}\geq 0
.

Proof.

The conclusion is obtained by taking the limit of

t^{-k}f(t^{\mu_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,t^{\mu_{m}}x_{m})\geq 0

as
t\to 0
for each fixed
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
∎

A well-known result from Linear Algebra
for a positive semi-definite hermitian form
h(\cdot,\cdot)

asserts that
h(L,L)=0
for some
L
implies

h(L,L^{\prime})=0
for any
L^{\prime}
.
We need the following formal refinement
of this result for vector fields along a formal submanifold:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a pseudoconvex smooth real hypersurface
through
0

with a contact form
\theta

(extended to a neighborhood of
0
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
)
and
O\subset j^{\infty}_{0}S

a real formal submanifold.
Let
L,L^{\prime}
be smooth complex vector fields
in a neighborhood of
0
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
with

L|_{S},L^{\prime}|_{S}\in H^{10}S
.
Then

(4.3)

j^{\infty}_{0}\theta([L,\overline{L}])\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)\implies j^{\infty}_{0}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}])\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O).

Proof.

Since
S
is pseudoconvex and the conclusion
is independent of the choice of the contact form
\theta
,
we can choose
\theta
such that

\theta([L^{\prime\prime},\overline{L^{\prime\prime}}])\geq 0
for all
L^{\prime\prime}\in H^{10}S

in a neighborhood of
0
.
Then
for all
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

(4.4)

\theta([L+cL^{\prime},\overline{L+cL^{\prime}}])=\theta([L,\overline{L}])+2{\sf Re}(\overline{c}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}]))+c\overline{c}\theta([{L^{\prime}},\overline{L^{\prime}}])\geq 0

holds on
S
in a neighborhood of
0
.
Since
O\subset j^{\infty}_{p}S
,
there exists a smooth map germ

\gamma\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{q},0)\to(S,0)
such that

j^{\infty}_{0}\gamma
is a formal parametrization of
O
,
see §
11.2
.
Taking pullbacks of (
4.4
) under
\gamma

we obtain that

(4.5)

\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L}])+2{\sf Re}(\overline{c}\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}]))+c\overline{c}\gamma^{*}\theta([{L^{\prime}},\overline{L^{\prime}}])\geq 0

holds in a neighborhood of
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
.

The
right-hand side
of the implication
(
4.3
)
is equivalent
to
\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}])
vanishing of infininte order at
0
.
Assume by contradiction that this order is finite and equal
to some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 0}
.
Assign the weight
1
to
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{q})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
and
the weight
\mu=s+1
to
({\sf Re}c,{\sf Im}c)
.
Then

2{\sf Re}(\overline{c}\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}]))

has a nonzero lowest weight term

(4.6)

2{\sf Re}(\overline{c}\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}]))_{s+\mu}\not\equiv 0

of weight
s+\mu
,
while all terms of the expansion of

c\overline{c}\gamma^{*}\theta([{L^{\prime}},\overline{L^{\prime}}])
at
0
have weight
\geq 2\mu>s+\mu
.
On the other hand, since
\gamma
is a parametrization of
O
,
the first term
\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L}])

vanishes of infinite order when

j^{\infty}_{p}\theta([L,\overline{L}])\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
.
Therefore, the lowest weight term in (
4.5
)
is
2{\sf Re}(\overline{c}\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}]))_{s+\mu}
,
which satisfies

2{\sf Re}(\overline{c}\gamma^{*}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}]))_{s+\mu}\geq 0

in view of Lemma
4.5
.
Since
c
is arbitrary, the left-hand side must vanish,
which contradicts (
4.6
), completing the proof.
∎

4.4.
Identities modulo formal ideals

As before we work in the ring of
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]

of formal power series
with complex coefficients
in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
For an ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]

adopt the standard notation

f=f^{\prime}\mod I\iff f-f^{\prime}\in I,\quad f,f^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]].

A similar notation will be used
for formal vector fields

L=L^{\prime}\mod I\iff L-L^{\prime}\in I\cdot{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{m},\quad L,L\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{m}.

Here

I\cdot{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

consists of complex formal vector fields
with coefficients in
I
,
while
the notation modulo
I

is shorter.
Similar notation will be adopted for ideals
in the ring of smooth functions of their germs.

We next study implications of the conclusion of
Proposition
4.6
.
When
L
is a smooth vector field tangent to
S

with
\theta(L)_{p}=0
at a point
p\in S
, then
L_{p}\in{\mathbb{C}}H_{p}S
.
We need a formal version of this conclusion
where
\theta(L)
vanishes along a formal submanifold:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a smooth submanifold
through
0
with a nonzero complex
1
-form
\theta

(smoothly extended to a neighborhood of
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
),
so that
\theta=0
defines a corank
1
complex subbundle
H\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS
.
Let
O\subset j^{\infty}_{0}S

be a real formal submanifold and
L
a
smooth real vector field
in a neighborhood of
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
that is tangent to
S
.
Then

j^{\infty}_{0}\theta(L)=0\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O)\implies j^{\infty}_{0}L\in j^{\infty}_{0}H\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O).

Proof.

Choose a smooth frame
e_{1},\ldots,e_{s}
of
H

and
complete it to a smooth frame
e_{1},\ldots,e_{s+1}
of
{\mathbb{C}}TS

with
\theta(e_{s+1})=1
,
that we may assume to be
extended smoothly to a neighborhood of
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Since
L
is tangent to
S
, we have

L=\sum_{j=1}^{s+1}a_{j}e_{j}\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(S)

in a fixed neighborhood of
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
where
{\mathbb{C}}I(S)
is the ideal of complex smooth functions
in that neighborhood vanishing on
S
.
Since
H\subset{\mathbb{C}}TS
is defined by
\theta=0
,
one has

\theta(L)=a_{s+1}\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(S).

By definition, the inclusion
O\subset j^{\infty}_{0}S

is equivalent to

j^{\infty}_{0}I(S)\subset I(O)
, and then

j^{\infty}_{0}\theta(L)=0\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O)\implies j^{\infty}_{0}a_{s+1}=0\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O).

The right-hand side implies

j^{\infty}_{0}L=j^{\infty}_{0}L^{\prime}\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O),\quad L^{\prime}:=\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}e_{j},

which proves the desired conclusion
since
L^{\prime}|_{S}\in H
.
∎

As per Definition
11.6
in the Appendix,
for a formal submanifold
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
we write
D_{O}\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

for the submodule of real formal vector fields
L

satisfying
L(I(O))\subset I(O)
, so that

{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}=\{f+ig:f,g\in D_{O}\}=\{L\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:L({\mathbb{C}}I(O))\subset{\mathbb{C}}I(O))\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

holds
for the complexification of
D_{O}
.
We summarize the main properties
of subbundles of infinite Levi type
(Definition
3.1
)
inside

pseudoconvex
hypersurfaces
in the following statement:

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a pseudoconvex smooth real hypersurface
with a smooth complex subbundle
E\subset H^{10}S

that is of infinite Levi type at
p\in S
.
Denote by
O=O^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
the real formal orbit.
Then the following holds:

(1)

commutators with Taylor series at
p
of sections in
E\cup\overline{E}

leave invariant
the space of Taylor series at
p
of sections in
{\mathbb{C}}HS

modulo the ideal of the orbit, i.e.

[j^{\infty}_{p}(E\oplus\overline{E}),j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS]\subset j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O);

(2)

commutators with
vector fields in
D_{O}
tangent to
O
leave invariant
the space of Taylor series at
p
of sections in
{\mathbb{C}}HS

modulo the ideal of the orbit, i.e.

[{\mathbb{C}}D_{O},j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS]\subset j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O);

(3)

the orbit
O
is complex-tangential to

j^{\infty}_{p}S
in the sense that

{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}\subset j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O).

Proof.

Choose a complex contact form
\theta
on
S
as before,
smoothly extended to a neighborhood of
p
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.
To show (1),
consider
formal vector fields in

{j^{\infty}_{p}E}

and

{j^{\infty}_{p}H^{10}S}

which are
Taylor series at
p
of smooth vector fields
L,L^{\prime}

whose restrictions to
S
are in
E
and
H^{10}S
respectively.
Then, since
E
is of infinite Levi type, by Corollary
4.4

j^{\infty}_{p}\theta([L,\overline{L}])\in{\mathbb{C}}I({\mathcal{O}}),

and, in view of the pseudoconvexity, Proposition
4.6

implies

(4.7)

j^{\infty}_{p}\theta([L,\overline{L^{\prime}}])\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O).

On the other hand,
(
4.7
) holds also for
L^{\prime}
with
L^{\prime}|_{S}\in H^{01}S
,
since
H^{10}S
is an involutive distribution in
{\mathbb{C}}TS

by the integrability of the CR structure.
Then by Lemma
4.7
,
we obtain

[j^{\infty}_{p}E,j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS]\subset j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O),

from which (1) follows,
since the right-hand side is invariant under conjugation.

To show (2), observe that
(1) along with Jacobi identity implies that

[L,j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS]\subset j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS\mod{\mathbb{C}}I(O)

holds for any

which is an iterated Lie bracket
of formal vector fields in

Since

Finally, by Lemma

in the Appendix,

,
proving (2).

Now, since

,
by
repeatedly using (1),
we conclude that
any iterated Lie bracket of vector fields
in

is contained in
j^{\infty}_{p}{\mathbb{C}}HS
modulo
{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
.
As before, using the relation

D_{O}=\mathfrak{g}\mod I(O)

completes the proof of (3).
∎

5.
Special subbundles and a formal Huang-Yin condition

In their detailed study of different notions of types
for subbundles
E\subset H^{10}S
,
Huang and Yin
[
HuY21
]

formulated an important condition
on the Nagano leaf
of a weighted truncation of
E

to be CR.
We shall need an analogous condition
on the real formal orbit
that we call the
formal Huang-Yin condition
.

Recall from §
11.3
in the Appendix
that a real formal submanifold
O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

given by formal power series
f(z,\overline{z})

can be identified with its complexification
given by the complexified series
f(z,\zeta)
.
I.e.
O
is identified with
a complex formal submanifold of
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

given by the complex manifold ideal
{\mathbb{C}}I(O)

that we identify with its complexification

\{f(z,\zeta):f(z,\bar{z})\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)\}

in the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]
,
z,\zeta\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
,
that we still denote by
{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
in a slight abuse of notation.
Then
the
(1,0)
tangent space
H^{10}_{0}O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

is defined by

H^{10}_{0}=\Big\{\xi\ :\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(0)\xi=0\text{ for all }f(z,\zeta)\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)\Big\},

and
the space
D^{10}_{O}
of formal vector fields
by

D^{10}_{O}=\{L=\sum_{j}a_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{z_{j}}:L({\mathbb{C}}I(O))\subset{\mathbb{C}}I(O)\},

and following
Della Salla-Juhlin-Lamel
[
DJL12
]
,

O
is
said to be CR

if

H^{10}_{0}O\subset D^{10}_{O}(0)

holds,
where
D^{10}_{O}(0)=\{L_{0}:L\in D^{10}_{O}\}

is the space of evaluations at
0
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a smooth real hypersurface.
We say that a smooth complex subbundle
E\subset H^{10}S

satisfies the
formal Huang-Yin condition
at
p\in S

if the real formal orbit
O={\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
is CR,
i.e.
H^{10}_{p}O\subset D^{10}_{O}(p)
.

It follows from
Lemma
11.10

in the Appendix
(a consequence of a result from
[
DJL12
]
))
that if
O={\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
is CR,
the ideal
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]\cap{\mathbb{C}}I(O)\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]

is a complex manifold ideal
defining the

intrinsic complexification
of
O

as a complex formal submanifold of
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.
In other words, the intrinsic complexification of
O

is the complex formal submanifold
defined by the ideal
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]\cap{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
.

We shall prove that in our situation
the formal Huang-Yin condition always holds.
For this, recall that our subbundle
E

is a part of the tower

H^{10}S=E_{0}\supset\ldots\ldots E_{m}=E,

where all subbundles
E_{k}\subset H^{10}

are
special

in the sense of Defintion
1.7
,
i.e. defined by linearly independent
\theta
-dual forms.

In fact, we establish
the desired formal Huang-Yin property
under somewhat more general assumptions
that may be of independent interest.
The difference is that we assume
that
E\subset H^{10}S
is defined by
any collection of linearly independent

1
-forms
that are either contractions of the Levi form
or differential of arbitrary smooth functions
— an assumption that always
holds for special subbundles.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a pseudoconvex smooth real hypersurface
with a complex contact form
\theta
and
a smooth complex subbundle
E\subset H^{10}S

such that the following holds:

(1)

E
is of infinite Levi type at
p\in S

in the sense of Definition
3.1
;

(2)

E
is defined by

(5.1)

E=\{\omega_{L_{1};\theta}=\ldots=\omega_{L_{l};\theta}=\partial f_{l+1}=\ldots=\partial f_{m}=0\},

where

L_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}HS
are smooth vector fields
and
f_{k}
are smooth real functions
satisfying

\omega_{L_{1};\theta}\wedge\ldots\wedge\omega_{L_{l};\theta}\wedge\partial f_{l+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial f_{m}\neq 0,\quad\omega_{L_{j};\theta}(L_{x})=\theta([L,L_{j}])(x),\quad x\in S.

Then the real formal orbit of
E
at
p
is CR,
i.e.
E
satisfies the formal Huang-Yin condition at
p
.

Proof.

As before we write
O={\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(p)
for the real formal orbit of
E
at
p
. Since
T_{p}O=D_{O}(p)
, Corollary
4.8
implies that
T_{p}O\subset H_{p}S
and the Levi form of
S
at
p

vanishes on
T_{p}O
. In particular, all
1
-forms
\omega_{L_{j};\theta}

vanish on
T_{p}O
.

On the other hand,
it follows from (
5.1
)
that
the subbundle
E\oplus\overline{E}
is tangent to the CR submanifold

M=\{x:f_{s}(x)=f_{s}(p),\;s=l+1,\ldots,m\}\subset S.

Then, also any iterated Lie bracket of vector fields in
E\oplus\overline{E}

is tangent to
M
, hence
the formal orbit
O
is tangent to
j^{\infty}_{p}M
,
implying
T_{p}O\subset T_{p}M
, and therefore

H^{10}_{p}O\subset\{\partial f_{l+1}=\ldots=\partial f_{m}=0\}.

Together with vanishing
\omega_{L_{j};\theta}
obtained above,
this implies

H^{10}_{p}O\subset\{\omega_{L_{1};\theta}=\ldots=\omega_{L_{l};\theta}=\partial f_{l+1}=\ldots=\partial f_{m}=0\},

hence

H^{10}_{p}O\subset E_{p}
in view of (
5.1
).

On the other hand,
since formal vector fields in

are tangent to
O

and
E\subset H^{10}S
,

\infty
-jets of
(1,0)
vector fields

with

are contained in
the module
D^{10}_{O}
of formal
(1,0)
vector fields
tangent to

(recall (
11.10
) in the Appendix for the definition).
Since any vector in
E_{p}
can be extended to such vector field,
it follows that
E_{p}\subset D^{10}_{O}(p)
.
Combining with above inclusion
H^{10}_{p}O\subset E_{p}
, we obtain

H^{10}_{p}O\subset D^{10}_{O}(p)
as claimed
in view of Definition
5.1
.
∎

As observed above, assumption (2)
in Theorem
5.2

is always satisfied
for special subbundles.
Hence we obtain as a special case of Theorem

:

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a pseudoconvex smooth real hypersurface
with a complex contact form
\theta
.
Then any special subbundle
E\subset H^{10}S

of infinite Levi type at
p

is CR, i.e. satisfies the formal Huang-Yin condition at
p
.

We conclude by providing
an example,
that is communicated to the author
by Lamel and Lebl,
demonstrating
that the formal Huang-Yin condition
is crucial to guarantee
that the

formal complex orbit
,
see Definition
1.5
(1),
is regular, i.e. a formal submanifold:

Example 5.4

(Lamel-Lebl)
.

Consider the following
real non-CR submanifold
of codimension
2
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
:

M:=\{(z,w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}:w_{1}=|z|^{4},\;w_{2}=|z|^{6}\}.

It is clear that the complex tangent space
has complex dimension
2

at points with
z=0

and
1
otherwise,
hence
M
is not CR.
Taking any collection
{\mathcal{L}}
of real-analytic vector fields

that everywhere span the tangent space,
we can realize

as the (formal) real orbit of
{\mathcal{L}}
.
It is also clear that

,

containing

,

,

.

6.
A formal variant of a result of Diederich-Fornaess

In
[
DF78
]

Diederich and Fornaess proved a fundamental result about
the equivalence of
finite type and zero holomorphic dimension
for

real-analytic
hypersurfaces,
which together
with Kohn’s celebrated
work
[
K79
]

yields subelliptic estimates under these conditions.
The most difficult part of their proof
is Proposition 3.1 in
[
DF78
]

roughly stating
that intrinsic complexifications
of smooth CR submanifolds of pseudoconvex hypersurfaces
S
,
along which
the Levi form vanishes, are tangent to
S
of infinite order
at generic points.
This result has been generalized by Bedford-Fornaess
[
BF81
]

and more recently by Pinton-Zampieri
[
PZ15
]
.

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
,
we shall need a

formal variant
of this important result,
that may be of independent interest.
Instead of a CR submanifold in
S
,
in our setup
we have a formal CR submanifold —
the formal orbit of a special subbundle
of infinite Levi type at a given point,
where we use Corollary
5.3

to ensure the CR condition,
which in turn,
ensures the existence of
the intrinsic complexification
as a formal complex submanifold,
see Corollary
11.11

and
Definition
11.12

in the Appendix.
We shall denote by

J
the standard complex structure of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
S\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a
pseudoconvex
smooth
real hypersuface through
0
,
and let

O,V\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be respectively
a real and a complex formal submanifold with
O\subset V
.
Assume:

(1)

O
is generic in
V
in the sense that

T_{0}O+JT_{0}O=T_{0}V
;

(2)

O
is contained in
S
in the formal sense, i.e.

O\subset j_{0}^{\infty}S
;

(3)

O
is complex-tangential to
j_{0}^{\infty}S
in the sense
that
D_{O}\subset j_{0}^{\infty}HS\mod I(O)
;

(4)

O
is of finite commiutator type, i.e.
{\sf Lie}(D^{10}_{O}\oplus\overline{D^{10}_{O}})(0)={\mathbb{C}}T_{0}O.

Then
V

is tangent to
S
of infinite order
or, equivalently,
formally contained in
S
, i.e.
V\subset j^{\infty}_{0}S
.
In particular,
S
is of infinite regular type at
0
.

We shall apply Theorem
6.1

at points of

q
-infinite tower multitype

(Definition
2.7
),
where
O
is a real formal orbit of
the corresponding special
subbundle
E\subset H^{10}S

of infinite Levi type
with
{\rm rank\,}E\geq q

given by Proposition
3.3
,
and
V
is the intrinsic complexification of
O
.

The original proof in
[
DF78
]
(and in
[
PZ15
]
)
is based on a careful analysis of the tangent vector fields
at
generic points
and cannot be transferred to the
present formal setup, where all power series are defined
at a fixed point
p

and cannot be evaluated generically.
The approach in
[
BF81
]
uses attached analytic disks
to the smooth CR submanifold in
S
,
a tool that is not available for formal CR submanifolds.

Thus we need to use a different approach
based on another set of new tools
such as

jets along formal submanifolds

in §
7

and

supertangent vector fields

in §
9
.
The proof
of Theorem
6.1

will be completed
at the end of §
9
below.

6.1.
Assumptions of Theorem
6.1
hold
with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V\geq q

at points of
q
-infinite tower multitype

Before embarking on our proof of Theorem
6.1
,
we show how its assumptions arise in our context
for a point of
q
-infinite tower multitype
in the sense of Definition
2.7
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
S
be a smooth pseudoconvex hypersurface through
0
.
Assume that the tower multitype of
S
at
0
is
q
-infinite.
Then there exists a smooth complex subbundle
E\subset H^{10}S

in a neighborhood of
0
with
{\rm rank\,}E\geq q
,
which is special (see Definition
1.7
),
whose formal orbit

is CR and
such that

and its intrinsic complexification

satisfy the assumptions (1)–(4) of Theorem
6.1
,
and one has
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V\geq q
.

Proof.

We begin by applying Proposition
3.3

establishing the existence of the tower (
3.2
)
in a neighborhood of

whose last subbundle

is of rank at least
q
and
of infinite Levi type at
p
(in the sense of Definition
3.1
).
Since
E_{m}
is a subbundle from a tower,
it is
special
in the sense of Definition
1.7

as a direct consequence of the definitions.

Let
O
denote the (real) formal orbit
O
of
E
,
see Definition
4.1
.
Since the subbundle
E
is
special

in the sense of Definition
1.7
,
and hence
O
is CR
by Corollary
5.3
.
In particular, the
intrinsic complexification

V
of
O

is defined as a complex formal submanifold of
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
,
see Definition
11.12
in the Appendix.

We now establish assumptions (1)–(4) of Theorem
6.1
.
Assumption (1) is a general property
of the intrinsic complexification, see
Corollary
11.13
.
Assumption (2)
follows from Lemma
4.2
.
Assumption (3) follows from
Corollary
4.8
, part (3),
by taking real parts.
Finally, to show assumption (4),
recal from the definition of the orbit, Definition
4.1
,
that, in particular,

(6.1)

T_{0}O=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{E}(0)={\sf Lie}({\sf Re}j^{\infty}_{0}E),

where

(6.2)

{\sf Re}j^{\infty}_{0}E=\{j^{\infty}_{0}{\sf Re}L:L\in{\mathbb{C}}T{\mathbb{C}}^{n},L|_{S}\in E\}.

Let
L
be as in (
6.2
) and
split it into
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts:

L=L^{10}+L^{01},\quad L^{10}\in H^{10}{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},\quad L^{01}\in H^{01}{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}.

Since

Then

is a
(1,0)

,

.
Finally, taking arbitrary

Finally, since

∎

6.2.
Approximation by maps into hypersurfaces

For the proof of Theorem
6.1

we shall need to compare
the Levi form of the hypersurface
S

with the complex hessian of its defining function
r

along the complex formal submanifold
V
.
Our approach is to “approximate” a formal parametrization of
V
,
or more generally, a formal map
A
into
V

with a smooth map into
S
.
We obtain a fine control of this approximation
modulo the ideal of the pull-back
(j^{\infty}_{0}r)\circ A

as follows:

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a formal hypersurface
defined by a principal ideal
I(Y)
.
Let

R\in{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
,
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
,
be a generator of
I(Y)
with

dR(0)\neq 0
,
and
A\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m},0)

any formal power series map.
Then there is an “approximating” formal power series map

\widetilde{A}\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m},0)
satisfying

(1)

R\circ\widetilde{A}=0
;

(2)

\widetilde{A}-A=0\mod(R\circ A)
.

In case
Y=j^{\infty}_{0}S
for a smooth real hypersurface
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
through
0
, the approximating map can be chosen of the form

\widetilde{A}=j^{\infty}_{0}a
, where

a\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to(S,0)
is a (germ of a) smooth map.

Proof.

By the implicit function theorem for formal power series,
we may assume the given generator
R
of the principal ideal
I(Y)

to be of the form

R(x^{\prime},x_{m})=x_{m}-\Phi(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1}),\quad\Phi\in{\mathbb{R}}[[x^{\prime}]],\quad(x^{\prime},x_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1}\times{\mathbb{R}},

cf. (
11.2
).
Splitting
A
into components

A=(A^{\prime},A_{m}),\quad A^{\prime}\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m-1},0),\quad A_{m}\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{R}},0),

we obtain (1) by setting

\widetilde{A}:=(A^{\prime},\Phi\circ A^{\prime})
.
Then

A-\widetilde{A}=(0,A_{m}-\Phi\circ A^{\prime})=(0,R\circ A)

proving (2).

When
Y=j^{\infty}_{0}S
for a smooth real hypersurface
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
through
0
,
a local defining function for
S
near
0
can be chosen of the form
r(x^{\prime},x_{m})=x_{m}-\varphi(x^{\prime})
with
j^{\infty}_{0}\varphi=\Phi
.
Then
R:=j^{\infty}_{0}r
yields a generator of
I(j^{\infty}_{0}S)
.
By Borel’s theorem, there exists a germ of a smooth map

a^{\prime}\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m-1},0)
with
j^{\infty}_{0}a^{\prime}=A^{\prime}
.
Then the smooth germ
a:=(a^{\prime},\varphi\circ a^{\prime})
satisfies the desired properties.
∎

6.3.
Approximation by tangent vector fields

Similar to the approximation of maps
in §
6.2
,
we approximate a formal
(1,0)
vector field
L

with a smooth
(1,0)
vector field tangent to
the given real hypersurface.
We control the approximation
by the ideal obtained from applying
L

to the Taylor series
j^{\infty}_{0}r
of the defining function
r
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
R\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,{\bar{z}}]]
,
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
,
be a real formal power series with
dR(0)\neq 0

and

L=\sum a_{j}(z,{\bar{z}})\partial_{z_{j}}

a
(1,0)
formal vector field
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.
Then there exists an (approximating)
(1,0)
formal vector field

\widetilde{L}
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
satisfying:

(1)

\widetilde{L}R=0
;

(2)

\widetilde{L}=L\mod(LR)
,
where
(LR)

is the principal ideal generated by
LR
.

In case
R=j^{\infty}_{0}r

for a smooth defining function
r

of a real hypersurface in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
,
the approximating vector field
\widetilde{L}

can be chosen of the form

\widetilde{L}=j^{\infty}_{0}\widetilde{l}
, where

\widetilde{l}
is a germ at
0
of a smooth
(1,0)
vector field in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

with
\widetilde{l}r=0
.

Proof.

Since

R
is real and

dR(0)\neq 0
, after possible reordering coordinates, we may assume

R_{z_{n}}(0)\neq 0
.
Consider a
(1,0)
formal vector field
\widetilde{L}=L+f\partial_{z_{n}}
,
where
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,{\bar{z}}]]
is a formal power series. Then

\widetilde{L}R=LR+fr_{z_{n}}=0\iff f=-\frac{LR}{R_{z_{n}}}.

Since
R_{z_{n}}(0)\neq 0
,
\frac{1}{R_{z_{n}}}
is a formal power series.
Hence taking
f=-\frac{LR}{R_{z_{n}}}
, we obtain
the formal vector field
\widetilde{L}=L+f\partial_{z_{n}}

satisyfing both conditions (1) and (2) as desired.

When
R=j^{\infty}_{0}r

for a smooth defining function
r

of a real hypersurface in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
,
by Borel’s theorem, there exists
a germ
l
of a smooth vector field in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
at
0

with
L=j^{\infty}_{0}l
.
Then setting

\widetilde{l}:=l-\frac{lr}{r_{z_{n}}}\partial_{z_{n}}

yields the desired properties.
∎

7.
Jets along formal submanifolds

7.1.
(k,X,Y)
-equivalence and jets

Our next tool needed for proving Theorem
6.1

provided by a formal variant
of jets of functions restricted to submanifolds
along smaller submanifolds.
When
X\subset Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
are smooth submanifolds through

p\in X
,
we can identify germs at
p
of smooth complex functions
f
on
Y
with
germs of smooth complex functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
modulo
the complexified ideal
{\mathbb{C}}I_{p}(Y)

of smooth germs at
p

vanishing on
Y
,
and their
k
-jets
j^{k}_{x}f

restricted to
X
as germs at
p
of functions of
x\in X

with their equivalence classes modulo the ideal

{\mathbb{C}}I_{p}(X)^{k+1}+{\mathbb{C}}I_{p}(Y)

of smooth function germs at
p
.
This motivates the following formal variant of this concept:

Definition 7.1
.

Let
X\subset Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be
real formal submanifolds given respectively by
their manifold ideals
I(Y)\subset I(X)\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
.
We say that formal power series

F,G\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]],\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),

are

(k,X,Y)
-equivalent

if

F-G\in{\mathbb{C}}I(X)^{k+1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(Y)).

A
formal
(k,X,Y)
-jet
is a
(k,X,Y)
-equivalence class of
formal power series in
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
.

As a matter of notation, we shall write

J^{k}_{X}{\mathbb{C}}[[Y]]={\mathbb{C}}[[x]]/({\mathbb{C}}I(X)^{k+1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(Y))

for the ring of all
(k,X,Y)
-jets of power series in
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]

and

j^{k}_{X,Y}F\in J^{k}_{X}{\mathbb{C}}[[Y]],\quad F\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]],

for
(k,X,Y)
-jets of individual formal power series.

A convenient way of representing
(k,X,Y)
-jets
is via joint parametrizations of
X,Y

as provided by Lemma
11.4
in the Appendix,
i.e. formal maps

(7.1)

A\colon({\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime\prime}}^{s^{\prime\prime}},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m},0)

such that
A
is a parametrization of
Y

and
A|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}}}
is a parametrization of
X
.
Then the pullback of
F\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
takes the form

F\circ A=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}f_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(t^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}(t^{\prime\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}},\quad(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime\prime}}^{s^{\prime\prime}},

where
\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}
are multi-indices,
the pullback of the ideal
{\mathbb{C}}I(X)^{k+1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(Y)
is

A^{*}({\mathbb{C}}I(X)^{k+1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(Y))={\mathbb{C}}I({\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k+1},

and the pullback
(j^{k}_{X,Y}F)\circ A
of the
(k,X,Y)
-jet of
F

can be represented
by polynomials of degree
\leq k
in
t^{\prime\prime}

with formal power series coefficients in
t^{\prime}
:

(j^{k}_{X,Y}F)\circ A=j^{k}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}},{\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}+s^{\prime\prime}}}(F\circ A)=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},|\alpha^{\prime\prime}|\leq k}f_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(t^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}(t^{\prime\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}.

7.2.
Testing vanishing jets by their derivatives

In proving Theorem
6.1

we shall need a conclusion about
a
(k,X,Y)
-jet being zero
when suitable lower jets are
zero for all derivatives
along formal vector fields tangent to
Y
:

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
X\subset Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be
real formal submanifolds given respectively by
their manifold ideals
I(Y)\subset I(X)\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
.
Fix integers
0<k^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime\prime}

and a formal power series

F\in I(X)^{k^{\prime\prime}}+I(Y).

Assume that

(7.2)

j^{k^{\prime\prime}-k^{\prime}}_{X,Y}(L^{k^{\prime}}\cdots L^{1}F)=0

for all real formal vector fields
L^{k^{\prime}},\ldots,L^{1}\in D_{Y}

(i.e. tangent to
Y
).
Then
j^{k^{\prime\prime}}_{X,Y}F=0
, i.e.
F\in I(X)^{k^{\prime\prime}+1}+I(Y)
.

Proof.

We shall use a joint formal parametrization
A

as in (
7.1
) such that

A|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}}}
is a parametrization of
X
.
Then
F\circ A\in I({\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k^{\prime\prime}}

and hence

F\circ A=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},|\alpha^{\prime\prime}|\geq k^{\prime\prime}}f_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(t^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}(t^{\prime\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}.

Assume by contradiction
F\notin I(X)^{k^{\prime\prime}+1}+I(Y)
,
which is equivalent to
F\circ A\notin I({\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k^{\prime\prime}+1}
.
Then there exists a multi-index
(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
with

(7.3)

f_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\neq 0,\quad|\alpha^{\prime\prime}|=k^{\prime\prime}.

Choosing a basis of
D_{Y}
as in (
11.3
) in the Appendix,
we can rewrite (
7.2
) in the equivalent form

(7.4)

\partial_{t_{j_{k}}}\cdots\partial_{t_{j_{1}}}(F\circ A)\in I({\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k^{\prime\prime}-k^{\prime}+1},\quad j_{k},\ldots,j_{1}\in\{1,\ldots,s^{\prime}+s^{\prime\prime}\}.

Now let
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
be as in (
7.3
)
and choose indices
j_{k},\ldots,j_{1}
among the components
of the multi-index
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
, so that

g:=\partial_{t_{j_{k^{\prime}}}}\ldots\partial_{t_{j_{1}}}(f_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(t^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}(t^{\prime\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}})\neq 0.

Then
g
is a nontrivial monomial of order
k^{\prime\prime}-k^{\prime}
in
t^{\prime\prime}

in the expansion of the left-hand side of (
7.4
),
which hence cannot belong to
I({\mathbb{R}}_{t^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k^{\prime\prime}-k^{\prime}+1}
,
a contradiction with (
7.4
)
proving the desired conclusion.
∎

7.3.
Taylor series of nonnegative functions

In our proof of Theorem
6.1
,

pseudoconvexity
of the given hypersurface
yields nonegative functions
by means of evaluating the Levi form
along vector fields.
The lemma below captures
the way we use nonnegativity of those functions.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let

f(x,y)\geq 0,\quad(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{y},\quad m,n\geq 1,

be a real smooth nonnegative function
in a neighborhood of
0
,
whose Taylor series
j_{0}^{\infty}f
at
0
satisfies

(7.5)

j_{0}^{\infty}f\in I({\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{x})^{k},\quad k\geq 0.

Assume that
k
is odd.
Then
j_{0}^{\infty}f\in I({\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{x})^{k+1}
.

Proof.

We shall write
(x)
and
(y)
for the ideals
generated by all component coordinates of
x
and
y
respectively,
in particular,
(y)=I({\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{x})
.
Denote by
P
the (formal) sum
of all terms of
j_{0}^{\infty}f
of degree
k
in
y
.
Then

(7.6)

j^{\infty}_{0}f(x,y)=P(x,y)\mod(y)^{k+1},\quad P(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[[x]][y],\quad P\in(y)^{k},

where
P

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
in
y

with formal power series coefficients in
x
.
It suffices to prove that
P=0
.

Assume by contradiction that
P\neq 0
.
Then we have the integer

b:=\max\{a:P(x,y)\in(x)^{a}(y)^{k}\}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 0},

which also equals the minimum order in
x
of a nonzero coefficient of
P
,
and
P
can be written as

(7.7)

P(x,y)=P_{0}(x,y)\mod(x)^{b+1}(y)^{k},

where

(7.8)

P_{0}\neq 0\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y],

is a bi-homogeneous polynomial in
(x,y)

of the bi-degree
(b,k)
.

We restrict
f
and
P
to the real curves given by

(x,y)=(At,Bt^{b+1}),\quad(A,B)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{y},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Then the bi-homogeneity of
P_{0}

along with (
7.7
) imply

P(At,Bt^{b+1})=P_{0}(A,B)t^{b+k(b+1)}\mod(t)^{b+k(b+1)+1},

which, together with (
7.5
), implies

(j_{0}^{\infty}f)(At,Bt^{b+1})=P_{0}(A,B)t^{b+k(b+1)}\mod(t)^{(k+1)(b+1)}.

Since

(j_{0}^{\infty}f)(At,Bt^{b+1})

is the Taylor series at
0
of

f(At,Bt^{b+1})
and
f\geq 0

in a neighborhood of
0
, we must have

(7.9)

P_{0}(A,B)\geq 0,\quad(A,B)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{y}.

Now we use our assumption that
k
is odd.
Recall that
P_{0}(x,y)
is homogeneous of degree
k
in
y
.
Then (
7.9
) is only possible when
P_{0}=0
,
contradicting (
7.8
).
Hence our assumption
P\neq 0
is false and the proof is complete.
∎

Let

8.1
),

6.3

8.3.

9.
Part II of Proof of Theorem
6.1
: Supertangent vector fields

9.1.
Definition and examples of complex-supertangent vector fields

Our next new tool in the proof of Theorem
6.1

is the notion that we call
supertangent

and
complex-supertangent
vector fields to a (formal) hypersurface,
which applied to a defining function of the hypersurface
do not reduce the relative contact order.
As before, we write
R=j^{\infty}_{o}r
,
where
r
is a smooth local
defining function of the given real hypersurface
S

near the origin.

Definition 9.1
.

In the setup of Theorem
6.1
,
let
k
be the relative contact order
defined by (
8.2
), in particular,
R\in I(O)^{k}+I(V)
.
A formal vector field
L\in{\mathbb{C}}D_{V}
is
said to be

(1)

supertangent
to
S
(or
j^{\infty}_{0}S
) if

LR\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V)
;

(2)

complex-supertangent
to
S
(or
j^{\infty}_{0}S
)
if both
L
and
JL
are supertangent.

As before,
J
stands for the standard complex structure
of
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.
Note that applying a general
L\in{\mathbb{C}}D_{V}

to
R
may potentially reduce the relative contact order,
i.e. we may only be able to conclude that

LR\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k-1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V)
.
The meaning of the condition (1) in the above definition
is that supertangent vector fields
provide a better partial order of contact in comparison.

An immediate source of supertangent vector fields
is provided by the following simple lemma:

Lemma 9.2
.

Any vector field
L\in(D^{10}_{O}\oplus\overline{D^{10}_{O}})\cap{\mathbb{C}}D_{V}

is complex-supertangent.

Proof.

Given
L
as in the lemma, it follows that

L,JL\in{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}\cap{\mathbb{C}}D_{V},

i.e.,
L
and
JL
leave both ideals

{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
,
{\mathbb{C}}I(V)
invariant.
Since
R\in I(O)^{k}+I(V)
, it follows that

LR,(JL)R\in I(O)^{k}+I(V)

proving the desired conclusion.
∎

9.2.
Vanishing jets of complex hessians along supertangent vector fields

In Lemma
8.2

we showed the vanishing of
(k-2,O,V)
-jets
of complex hessians of
R
when
k
is odd.
Now that we established in Corollary
8.5

that
k
must be in fact even,
we show a similar vanishing property of

(k-1,O,V)
-jets along

supertangent vector fields
:

Lemma 9.3
.

Assume
k
is even. Then

j^{k-1}_{O,V}\partial\overline{\partial}R(L,\overline{L})=0,

or, equivalently,

\partial\overline{\partial}R(L,\overline{L})\in I(O)^{k}+I(V)

hold
for any supertangent vector field
L\in D^{10}_{V}
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of Lemma
8.2
.
As in that proof,
let
A
be a joint parametrization given by (
8.1
),
we have
R\circ A\in I({\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k}
.
Then by Lemma
6.3
,
there exists an approximation by a smooth map germ

a\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime\prime}},0)\to(S,0)

with

j^{\infty}_{0}a-A=0\mod I({\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime\prime}})^{k}.

Since
L
is assumed to be
supertangent
,
applying Lemma
6.4
to
L\in D^{10}_{V}
,
we obtain an approximation by a smooth
(1,0)
vector field germ

l
at
0
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
with

l|_{S}\in HS,\quad j^{\infty}_{0}l-L\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V).

Using both approximations, we conclude

(9.1)

j^{\infty}_{0}(\partial\bar{\partial}r(l,\overline{l})\circ a)-\partial\bar{\partial}R(L,\overline{L})\circ A\in I({\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k}.

By the Cartan formula for the exterior derivative,

(9.2)

\partial\overline{\partial}R(L,\overline{L})=L\overline{\partial}R(\overline{L})-\overline{L}\;\overline{\partial}R(L)-\overline{\partial}R([L,\overline{L}]).

Since
L

is supertangent and
R
is real,

\overline{\partial}R(\overline{L})=\overline{LR}\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V),

hence the first term in (

belongs to
{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k-1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V)
.
The second term vanishes since

,
while the last term again belongs to
{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k-1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V)
,
since
[L,\overline{L}],J[L,\overline{L}]\in D_{V}
. Hence

(9.3)

\partial\overline{\partial}R(L,\overline{L})\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k-1}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V).

Since
S
is
pseudoconvex
, we may assume
r
chosen such that

\partial\bar{\partial}r(l,\overline{l})|_{S}\geq 0
,
where
l
is the approximation of

chosen above.
Then (
9.1
) and (
9.3
) yield

j^{\infty}_{0}(\partial\bar{\partial}r(l,\overline{l})\circ a)\in I({\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k-1}.

Now, since
k
is even,
k-1
is odd and Lemma

implies

j^{\infty}_{0}(\partial\bar{\partial}r(l,\overline{l})\circ a)\in I({\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k}
.
Together with (

), this yields

\partial\bar{\partial}R(L,\overline{L})\circ A\in I({\mathbb{R}}^{s^{\prime}})^{k}

that is equivalent to the desired conclusion.
∎

9.3.
Lie algebra property of complex-supertangent vector fields

We now derive the key

Lie algebra property
of complex-supertangent vector fields

among those tangent to both
O
and
V
:

Corollary 9.4
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
9.3
,
let
L^{2},L^{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}\cap{\mathbb{C}}D_{V}

be complex-supertangent vector fields, i.e.

(9.4)

L^{j}R,(JL^{j})R\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V),\quad j=1,2.

Then their commutator
[L^{2},L^{1}]
is also complex-supertangent, i.e.

[L^{2},L^{1}]R,(J[L^{2},L^{1}])R\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V).

Thus the complex-supertangent vector fields in

{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}\cap{\mathbb{C}}D_{V}
form a Lie algebra.

Proof.

By the Cartan formula for the exterior derivative applied to the
1
-forms
\overline{\partial}R
,
\partial R
,

(9.5)

\begin{split}\partial\overline{\partial}R(L^{2},L^{1})=L^{2}\overline{\partial}R(L^{1})-L^{1}\overline{\partial}R(L^{2})-\overline{\partial}R([L^{2},L^{1}]),\\
\overline{\partial}\partial R(L^{2},L^{1})=L^{2}\partial R(L^{1})-L^{1}\partial R(L^{2})-\partial R([L^{2},L^{1}]).\end{split}

Every
L^{j}
,
j=1,2
, can be represented in the standard way
as the sum of its
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
components:

(9.6)

L^{j}=L^{j}_{10}+L^{j}_{01},\quad L^{j}_{10}=\frac{1}{2}(L^{j}+iJL^{j})\in D^{10}_{V},\quad L^{j}_{01}=\frac{1}{2}(L^{j}-iJL^{j})\in D^{01}_{V}=\overline{D^{10}_{V}}

and since
R
is real,
our assumption (
9.4
) implies that
both components
L^{j}_{10},L^{j}_{01}

are supertangent and

(9.7)

\partial R(L^{j})=\partial R(L^{j}_{10})\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V),\quad\overline{\partial}R(L^{j})=\partial R(L^{j}_{01})\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V).

Applying Lemma
9.3
along with polarization,
we compute

\partial\overline{\partial}R(L^{2},L^{1})=-\overline{\partial}\partial R(L^{2},L^{1})=\partial\overline{\partial}R(L^{2}_{10},L^{1}_{01})-\partial\overline{\partial}R(L^{2}_{01},L^{1}_{10})\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V),

i.e. left-hand side terms in both equations (
9.5
) belong to

{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V)
.
Applying
L^{j}
to the identities in (
9.7
)
and using our assumption
L^{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}\cap{\mathbb{C}}D_{V}
for
j=1,2
,
we compute

L^{2}\overline{\partial}R(L^{1}),L^{1}\overline{\partial}R(L^{2}),L^{2}\partial R(L^{1}),L^{1}\partial R(L^{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V).

Hence (
9.5
) imply

\overline{\partial}R([L^{2},L^{1}]),\partial R([L^{2},L^{1}])\in{\mathbb{C}}I(O)^{k}+{\mathbb{C}}I(V).

Splitting
[L^{2},L^{1}]
into
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
components
similar to (
9.6
), this proves the desired conclusion.
∎

9.4.
Lie algebras generated by complex-supertangent vector fields

The assumption (4) in Theorem

11.
Appendix: formal submanifolds

Here we summarize basic results on formal submanifolds
used throughout the paper.

11.1.
Preliminaries

We consider a field
{\mathbb{K}}
that is either
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
,
and
the ring

{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]={\mathbb{K}}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]],\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in{\mathbb{K}}^{m},

of

formal power series
over
{\mathbb{K}}

in
m
indeterminates.
To every smooth complex function germ
f(x)
at
p\in{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
is associated
its formal Taylor series at
p
, given by

Tf(x)=T_{p}f(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\alpha}}(p)(x-p)^{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x-p]],

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
are multiindices and
{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
.
It follows directly from the Leibnitz rule that

T_{p}
is a ring homomorphism
from the ring of germs of smooth functions
at
p
into the ring of formal power series in
x-p
.
We shall also use the jet notation and identify
T_{p}f
with
the infinite order jet

j^{\infty}_{p}f=T_{p}f.

Similarly,
by taking Taylor series of the coefficients,
to every germ at
p
of a smooth
{\mathbb{K}}
-valued
q
-form

\omega=\sum_{J}dx_{J},\quad J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}),\quad dx_{J}=dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q}

is associated
its Taylor series

T\omega=j^{\infty}_{p}\omega=\sum_{J}(T\omega_{J})dx_{J}\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x-p]]\otimes\Lambda^{q}({\mathbb{K}}^{m})^{*}.

Analogously, by taking Taylor series of the coefficients,
to every germ at
p
of a smooth
{\mathbb{K}}
-valued vector field

L=\sum_{j}a_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}
is associated
its Taylor series

TL=j^{\infty}_{p}L=\sum_{j}(Ta_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x-p]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m}.

For every smooth real submanifold
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of codimension
k
and
p\in O
,
the set
I_{p}(O)
of germs at
p
of smooth real functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
vanishing on
O

forms an ideal in the ring of all germs at
p
of smooth real functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
The ideal
I_{p}(O)
is
generated by a
k
-tuple
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
whose differentials at
p

(11.1)

df_{1}(p),\ldots,df_{k}(p),

are linearly independent.
Then the set of all Taylor series

TI_{p}(O)=j^{\infty}_{p}I(O)=\{Tf(x):f\in I_{p}(O)\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x-p]]

forms an ideal generated by the
k
-tuple

(Tf_{1},\ldots,Tf_{k})
of the Taylor series of the generators
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})

and the formal differentials

Tdf_{1},\ldots,Tdf_{k},

when evaluated at
p
, coincide with (
11.1
),
and hence are also linearly independent.
Note that a formal power series in
(x-p)
can only be evaluated at
x=p
.

Vice versa, for any ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x-p]]

generated by
k
elements
F_{1}\ldots,F_{k}\in I

with linearly independent differentials

dF_{1}(p),\ldots,dF_{k}(p),

there is a smooth real submanifold
O
through
p
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

of codimension
k
, for which
I=TI_{p}(O)
.
Indeed, it suffices to choose a smooth function germ
f_{j}
at
p

with
Tf_{j}=F_{j}
for each
j=1,\ldots,k
,
and take
O
defined near
0
by
f_{1}=\ldots=f_{k}=0
.
Here we use the classical

Borel theorem

on surjectivity of the Taylor series map
T

for smooth functions.

11.2.
Manifold ideals and formal submanifolds

The above correspondence between submanifolds
O

and their formal power series ideals
TI_{p}(O)=j^{\infty}_{p}I_{p}(O)

motivates the following abstract definition:

Definition 11.1
.

An ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
, where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
, is a
manifold ideal
if
it is generated by
k
elements
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\in I

with
F_{1}(0)=\ldots=F_{k}(0)
and
linearly independent differentials

dF_{1}(0),\ldots,dF_{k}(0).

A manifold ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
is said to define
a
formal submanifold

O
of
{\mathbb{K}}^{n}

with its
formal ring
given by

{\mathbb{K}}[[O]]:={\mathbb{K}}[[x]]/I.

One major source of formal submanifolds over
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}}

is provided by
\infty
-jets of real submanifolds
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
If
I_{p}(S)
is the ideal of germs at
p\in S
of smooth functions
in a neighborhood of
p
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

vanishing on
S
, then

j^{\infty}_{p}I(O)=\{j^{\infty}_{p}f:f\in I(O)\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x-p]]

defines a manifold ideal as can be seen
by taking
\infty
-jets of a local
system of smooth defining equations of
S

with independent differentials.
We denote by
j^{\infty}_{p}S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

the corresponding formal submanifold given by
the ideal
j^{\infty}_{p}I(O)
.

By the implicit function theorem for formal power series,
after possible reordering coordinates, a manifold ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]

with
k
generators also
admits generators of the form

(11.2)

x_{j+m-k}-\varphi_{j}(x^{\prime}),\quad\varphi_{j}\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x^{\prime}]],\quad x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-k}),\quad j=1,\ldots,k,

and hence the formal power series map

A(x^{\prime}):=(x^{\prime},\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime}),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(x^{\prime}))\colon({\mathbb{K}}^{m-k},0)\to({\mathbb{K}}^{m},0)

satisfies

{\rm rank\,}dA(0)=k,\quad F\circ A=0\text{ for all }F\in I.

In particular, for any
F\in I
, its differential
dF(0)
vanishes on the image
dA({\mathbb{K}}^{m-k})\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
, and hence the number of any generators of
I
with linearly independent differentials at
0
is always
k
.
We say that the formal submanifold
O
defined by
I

has
codimension
k
and
dimension
m-k
.

Definition 11.2
.

Given a formal submanifold
O\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
of codimension
k
,
any formal power series map

H\colon({\mathbb{K}}^{m-k},0)\to({\mathbb{K}}^{m},0),\quad H\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x^{\prime}]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m},

satisfying (
11.2
)
is called a
(formal) parametrization of
O
.

Again, as a consequence of the implicit function theorem for formal power series,
any two formal parametrizations
A_{1},A_{2}
of
O
satisfy

A_{2}=A_{1}\circ\psi,\quad\psi\in({\mathbb{K}}^{k},0)\to({\mathbb{K}}^{k},0),\quad{\rm rank\,}d\psi(0)=k,

for a suitable formal power series map
\psi
.

Remark 11.3
.

If
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a smooth real submanifold
with
p\in S
, and
a\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m},p)

a germ of a local smooth parametrization of
S
,
then its Taylor series
A=j^{\infty}_{p}a
is a formal
parametrization of
j^{\infty}_{p}S
.
Vice versa,
given any formal parametrization

A
of
j^{\infty}_{p}S
, there exists
a germ
a\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{R}}^{m},p)

of a local smooth parametrization of
S

with
A=j^{\infty}_{0}a
.
Indeed,
writing
S
locally as graph
x^{\prime\prime}=\varphi(x^{\prime})

in coordinates
(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})

and splitting
A
as
(A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime})
in these coordinates,
we can construct
a
as
(a^{\prime},\varphi\circ a^{\prime})

where
a^{\prime}
is any smooth germ at
0
with
A^{\prime}=j^{\infty}_{0}a^{\prime}
.

Since we cannot talk about “points” in a formal submanifold
O
,
we only consider
O
at the level of formal ideals,
i.e.
O
is

identified with its manifold ideal
.
We write

I=I(O)

for the ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
defining
O
.

Using the language of ideals, we can transfer
ideas and concepts from Algebraic Geometry
to formal submanifolds.
For example, given two formal submanifolds
X,Y\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
,
their inclusion
X\subset Y
is defined as
I(Y)\supset I(X)
.
In that situation, it will be useful to have

joint formal parametrizations
:

Lemma 11.4

(joint formal parametrization)
.

Let
X\subset Y\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
be formal submanifolds
and
s=\dim Y
,
s^{\prime}=\dim X
be their dimensions with

s^{\prime\prime}:=s-s^{\prime}\geq 0
.
Then there exists a formal parametrization

A(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\colon({\mathbb{K}}^{s^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{K}}^{s^{\prime\prime}},0)\to({\mathbb{K}}^{m},0)

of
Y
such that the restriction

A(t^{\prime},0)=A|_{{\mathbb{K}}^{s^{\prime}}}\colon({\mathbb{K}}^{s^{\prime}},0)\to({\mathbb{K}}^{m},0)

is a formal parametrization of
X
.

Proof.

Let
B\colon({\mathbb{K}}^{s},0)\to({\mathbb{K}}^{m},0)

be a formal parametrization of
Y
.
Then it is easy to see that the pullback

B^{*}(I(X))\subset{\mathbb{K}}[[t]]
is a manifold ideal,
hence admitting generators of the form (
11.2
).
After a formal change of coordinates
t=(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\in{\mathbb{K}}^{s^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{K}}^{s^{\prime\prime}}
,
these generators become coordinate functions in
the variable
t^{\prime\prime}
,
leading to the desired property.
∎

Note that, unlike manifold ideals in
{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
arising as Taylor series map images of ideals defined by smooth real submanifolds, a manifold ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
corresponds to a complex submanifold
through
0
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
if and only if
I
has convergent power series as generators.

When
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}}
, for an ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
, we also consider its complexification

{\mathbb{C}}I:=\left\{f+ig:f,g\in I\right\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x]],

which is the ideal in
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
generated by
I
,
that is invariant under conjugation.
If
I
is a manifold ideal in
{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
with generators
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
,
then
{\mathbb{C}}I
is a manifold ideal in
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
with the same generators.
Vice versa, if
\widetilde{I}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
is a manifold ideal invariant under the conjugation, it equals the complexification
{\mathbb{C}}I

of a real manifold ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
.

11.3.
Formal tangent spaces and vector fields

Since we can evaluate formal power series at the origin,
we can define the tangent space
T_{0}O
for every formal submanifold
as follows:

Definition 11.5

(tangent space of a formal submanifold)
.

For a formal submanifold
O\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
, define its

tangent space at
0

as

T_{0}O:=\{\xi\in T_{0}{\mathbb{K}}^{n}:df(\xi)=0\text{ for all }f\in I(O)\}\subset T_{0}{\mathbb{K}}^{n}\cong{\mathbb{K}}^{n}.

Note that
0
is the only point where we can evaluate
formal power series, hence only the tangent space at
0

is defined for a formal submanifold.
Another standard notion is that of formal

tangent vector fields

to a formal submanifold:

Definition 11.6
.

If
O\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
is a formal submanifold,
a formal vector field
L\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{n}

is said to be
tangent to
O
whenever

L(I(O))\subset I(O).

We denote by
D_{O}
the
{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
-module of

all formal vector fields
tangent to
O
, which is obviously a Lie algebra
with respect to the Lie bracket.

With generators of
I
chosen as in (
11.2
),
the space
D_{O}
is spanned, modulo
I(O)
, by

(11.3)

D_{O}=\text{\rm span}_{{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]}\big\{\partial_{x_{s}}+\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{s}}\partial_{x_{j+m-k}}:s=1,\ldots,m-k\big\}+I(O)\cdot{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m},

where as before
{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
is identified
with the space of all formal vector fields in
{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
.
In particular, evaluating at
0
, it is easy to see that

(11.4)

D_{O}(0):=\{L_{0}:L\in D_{O}\}=T_{0}O.

More generally, to span
D_{O}
over the ring
{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]

by a collection of formal vector fields,
it suffices to span the tangent space at
0
:

Lemma 11.7
.

Let
O\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
be a formal submanifold and

L^{1},\ldots,L^{l}\in D_{O}
be such that

T_{0}O={\sf span}_{\mathbb{K}}\{L^{1}_{0},\ldots,L^{l}_{0}\}.

Then

D_{O}={\sf span}_{{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]}\{L^{1},\ldots,L^{l}\}+I(O)\cdot{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m}.

Proof.

Reordering coordinates if necessary, we may assume that

I(O)
has generators
as in (
11.2
), so that (
11.3
) holds, i.e.

(11.5)

D_{O}={\sf span}_{{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]}\{X^{s}:s=1,\ldots,m-k\}+I(O)\cdot{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m},\quad X^{s}=\partial_{x_{s}}+\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{s}}\partial_{x_{j+m-k}}.

In particular, we can write

L^{j}=\sum_{s}A^{j}_{s}X^{s}\mod I(O),

where
A^{j}_{s}\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
.
Since
L^{j}_{0}
span
T_{0}O
,
the matrix
(A^{j}_{s}(0))
evaluated at
0
has rank
m-k
.
Reordering
L^{j}
if necessary, we may assume the first

(m-k)\times(m-k)
minor of
(A^{j}_{s}(0))
is nonzero.
Then the same minor of
(A^{j}_{s})
is invertible in
{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
.
Taking the inverse, we conclude that each
X^{j}
is contained in the span of
L^{j}
modulo
I(O)
, which proves the desired conclusion
in view of (
11.5
).
∎

For a pair of formal submanifolds
O\subset V
,
we can approximate vector fields in
D_{O}

by those in
D_{V}
in the following sense:

Lemma 11.8
.

Let
O\subset V\subset{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
be formal submanifolds.
Then for every
L\in D_{O}
there exists
\widetilde{L}\in D_{V}

with

(11.6)

\widetilde{L}=L\mod I(O).

In particular, it follows that
\widetilde{L}\in D_{O}\cap D_{V}
.

Proof.

Reordering coordinates
x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in{\mathbb{K}}^{m-k}\times{\mathbb{K}}^{k}

if necessary, we may assume
that
I(V)
has generators
as in (
11.2
), so that (
11.3
) holds for
D_{V}
, i.e.

D_{V}={\sf span}_{{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]}\{X^{s}:s=1,\ldots,m-k\}+I(V)\cdot{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m},\quad X^{s}=\partial_{x_{s}}+\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{s}}\partial_{x_{j+m-k}}.

Writing

L=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}
,
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
,
we set

\widetilde{L}:=\sum_{s=1}^{m-k}a_{s}X^{s}\in D_{V}
.
Since
O\subset V
, hence
I(V)\subset I(O)
,
we have
L(I(V))\subset I(O)
.
Writing this condition for the generators

x_{j+m-k}-\varphi_{j}(x^{\prime})
of
(V)
, we obtain

L(x_{j+m-k}-\varphi_{j}(x^{\prime}))=a_{j+m-k}-\sum_{s}a_{s}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{s}}\in I(O)

and hence

L-\widetilde{L}=\sum_{s=1}^{m-k}a_{s}\partial_{x_{s}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j+m-k}\partial_{x_{j+m-k}}-\sum_{s=1}^{m-k}a_{s}(\partial_{x_{s}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{s}}\partial_{x_{j+m-k}})\in I(O)\cdot{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{K}}^{m}

proving (
11.6
).
The inclusion
\widetilde{L}\in D_{O}

follows immediately from (
11.6
)
and the definition of
D_{O}
(Definition
11.6
).
∎

It is customary to write a real formal power series

{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}

:

(11.7)

f(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{\alpha\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}z^{\alpha}\bar{z}^{\beta}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\bar{z}]],\quad c_{\alpha\beta}=\overline{c_{\beta\alpha}},\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}),

which we identify with the complexified series

(11.8)

\sum_{\alpha\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}z^{\alpha}\zeta^{\beta}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]],\quad c_{\alpha\beta}=\overline{c_{\beta\alpha}},\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}),\;\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}).

With that identification,
the complex conjugation
\sigma
for series (
11.7
)
is
given by

(11.9)

\sigma\left({\sum_{\alpha\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}z^{\alpha}\zeta^{\beta}}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\beta}\overline{c_{\alpha\beta}}\zeta^{\alpha}z^{\beta}.

Then a real
formal submanifold
O\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

has its ideal

I=I(O)

in the ring

\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]:\sigma f=f\}\cong{\mathbb{R}}[[x,y]],\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

whose complexification

{\mathbb{C}}I=\{f+ig:f,g\in I\}

is a
\sigma
-invariant ideal in
{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]
.
Conversely, a
\sigma
-invariant ideal in
{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]

is a complexification of a real ideal in
the ring
\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]:\sigma f=f\}
,
and a
\sigma
-invariant manifold ideal in
{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]

is a complexification of a real manifold ideal in
\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]:\sigma f=f\}
.
We call

{\mathbb{C}}I={\mathbb{C}}I(O)\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]

the
complexified ideal
of
O
.

For a complex formal submanifold
O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

given by a manifold ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]
,
define the spaces of
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
formal tangent vector fields by

(11.10)

{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}=\left\{L=\sum a_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{z_{j}}:L(I)\subset I\right\},\quad{\mathcal{D}}^{01}_{O}=\left\{L=\sum b_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{\zeta_{j}}:L(I)\subset I\right\},

where
a_{j}(z,\zeta),b_{j}(z,\zeta)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]
,
and the space of complex formal tangent vector fields by

{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}=\left\{L=\sum(a_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{z_{j}}+b_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{\zeta_{j}}):L(I)\subset I\right\},

while the space
D_{O}
of real formal tangent vector fields is

D_{O}=\{L\in{\mathbb{C}}D_{O}:\sigma L=L\}.

If
O
is a
real formal submanifold
, i.e. defined
by a real submanifold ideal
I(O)\subset\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]:\sigma f=f\}
,
the corresponding complexification
{\mathbb{C}}I(O)
in
{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]

is generated by the
\sigma
-invariant formal power series (
11.8
) and one has

(11.11)

{\mathcal{D}}^{01}_{O}=\sigma{{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}}=\left\{\sum\bar{a}_{j}(\zeta,z)\partial_{\zeta_{j}}:\sum a_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{z_{j}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}\right\},

where we keep the notation
\sigma

for complex formal vector fields
given by

\sigma\left(\sum(a_{j}\partial_{z_{j}}+b_{j}\partial_{\zeta_{j}})\right)=\sum(\sigma a_{j}\partial_{\zeta_{j}}+\sigma b_{j}\partial_{z_{j}}),

or, equivalently, by

\sigma(L)f=\sigma(L(\sigma f)),\quad f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]].

It is easy to see that the evaluations

{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}(0)=\left\{\sum a_{j}(0)\partial_{z_{j}}:\sum a_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{z_{j}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}\right\},

{\mathcal{D}}^{01}_{O}(0)=\left\{\sum a_{j}(0)\partial_{z_{j}}:\sum a_{j}(z,\zeta)\partial_{\zeta_{j}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{01}_{O}\right\},

are contained in the corresponding

(1,0)
and
(0,1)
tangent spaces at
0
:

H^{10}_{0}O=H^{10}_{0}{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\cap{\mathbb{C}}T_{0}O,\quad H^{01}_{0}O=H^{01}_{0}{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\cap{\mathbb{C}}T_{0}O,

i.e.

{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}(0)\subset H^{10}_{0}O,\quad{\mathcal{D}}^{01}_{O}(0)\subset H^{01}_{0}O.

The opposite inclusions do not hold in general
and, in the case
O
is real-analytic,
are equivalent to
O
being CR in a neighborhood of
0
.
This motivates the following definition (cf.
[
DJL12
, p. 105]
:

Definition 11.9
.

A real formal submanifold
O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

is said to be CR if

H^{10}_{0}O\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}(0),

or, equivalently, if
every vector in
H^{10}_{0}O

extends to a formal vector field in
{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}
.

In view of (
11.11
),
Definition
11.9
can be restated for
(0,1)
vector fields,
i.e. a real formal submanifold
O\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is CR
if and only if

{\mathcal{D}}^{10}_{O}(0)=H^{10}_{0}O
or, equivalently,

{\mathcal{D}}^{01}_{O}(0)=H^{01}_{0}O
.
We quote the following structure result for formal CR submanifolds
from Della Salla-Juhlin-Lamel
[
DJL12
, Lemma 1]
:

Lemma 11.10
.

Let
O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a formal CR submanifold of codimension
d

with its complexified ideal
I={\mathbb{C}}I(O)\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]
.
Then there exists integers
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\geq 0
with

n=d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}
,

d=2d_{1}+d_{3},

such that after renumbering coordinates
z
and
\zeta

if necessary, we can write

z=(z^{1},z^{2},z^{3}),\;\zeta=(\zeta^{1},\zeta^{2},\zeta^{3})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{3}},

and choose a set of generators of
I
given by
the components of

z^{1}-\varphi(z^{2},z^{3}),\quad\zeta^{1}-\sigma\varphi(\zeta^{2},\zeta^{3}),\quad\zeta^{2}-R(z^{2},z^{3},\zeta^{3})

for some vector-valued formal power series

\varphi\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z^{2},z^{3}]]^{d_{1}}
,

R\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z^{2},z^{3},\zeta^{3}]]
.

It is well-known that real-analytic CR submanifolds
O

are generic in their local

intrinsic complexifications

defined as minimal complex submanifolds (locally) containing
O
,
see e.g.
[
BER99
, Chapter I]
.
We shall need the following formal analogue:

Corollary 11.11
.

Let

O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
be a formal CR submanifold.
Then the maximal formal holomorphic ideal
I^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]

contained in the complexification

{\mathbb{C}}I(O)=I(O)+iI(O)\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]
, i.e.

I^{\prime}:={\mathbb{C}}I(O)\cap{\mathbb{C}}[[z]],

is a complex manifold ideal,
(where
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
is identified with the subring of all power series
in
{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]

independent of
\zeta
).

Proof.

Using the notation and generators of
I(O)

in Lemma
11.10
,
it follows that
I^{\prime}
is generated over
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
by the components
of
z^{1}-\varphi(z^{2},z^{3})
, hence is a manifold ideal as claimed.
∎

Definition 11.12
.

The
intrinsic complexification
of
a formal CR submanifold
O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

is the complex
formal submanifold
V\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

defined by the ideal

I^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}I(O)\cap{\mathbb{C}}[[z]].

To any formal complex submanifold
V\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

given by an ideal
I(V)\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
,
there is also the associated real formal submanifold

V^{\mathbb{R}}\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

given by the real formal ideal

I(V^{\mathbb{R}})\subset\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]:f=\sigma f\}

whose complexification
{\mathbb{C}}I(V^{\mathbb{R}})
is
generated by

I(V)\oplus\sigma I(V)=\{f+\sigma g:f,g\in I(V)\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]],

and using these generators, it follows that

{\mathbb{C}}D_{V^{\mathbb{R}}}=D_{V^{\mathbb{R}}}^{10}\oplus D_{V^{\mathbb{R}}}^{01}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[z,\zeta]]\otimes{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}.

In the sequel, we slightly abuse the notation
writing
V
for both complex formal submanifold
and its associated real formal submanifold
V^{\mathbb{R}}
.
We have the following consequence of Lemma
11.10
:

Corollary 11.13
.

If
V
is the intrinsic complexification of
a formal CR submanifold
O\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
,
it follows that
O
is generic in
V
in the sense that

(11.12)

T_{0}O+JT_{0}O=T_{0}V,

where
J
is the standard complex structure acting on
the standard basis vectors by

J\partial_{z_{j}}=i\partial_{z_{j}},\quad J\partial_{\zeta_{j}}=-i\partial_{\zeta_{j}},\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

As an application,
we obtain a convenient
expression of tangent vector fields to
the complexification
V
in terms
of vector fields tangent to both
O
and
V
:

Corollary 11.14
.

If
O\subset V\subset{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
are formal submanifolds
satisfying (
11.12
),
it follows that

(11.13)

D_{V}=(D_{O}\cap D_{V})+J(D_{O}\cap D_{V})\mod I(V).

In particular, (
11.13
)
holds when
O
is a formal CR submanifold and

V
is the intrinsic complexification of
O
.

Proof.

In view of (
11.4
),
there exists a collection of vector fields

L^{1},\ldots,L^{s}\in D_{O}
such that

T_{0}O=\text{\rm span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L^{1}_{0},\ldots,L^{s}_{0}\}.

By Lemma
11.6
, there exists approximations

\widetilde{L}^{1},\ldots,\widetilde{L}^{s}\in D_{O}\cap D_{V}

with
\widetilde{L}^{j}=L^{j}\mod I(O)
.
In particular, the values
\widetilde{L}^{1}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{L}^{s}_{0}

also span
T_{0}O
.
Then by Corollary
11.13
,

T_{0}V=\text{\rm span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L^{1}_{0},\ldots,L^{s}_{0},JL^{1}_{0},\ldots,JL^{s}_{0}\}.

Finally, since
V
is a complex formal submanifold,
each
JL^{j}
belongs to
D_{V}
,
and the desired conclusion follow from
Lemma
11.7
applied
to
V
.
∎

11.5.
Formal orbits

We make use of
a result of Baouendi-Ebenfelt-Rothschild
[
BER03
, Proposition 5.1]

(an adaptation to the formal case
of an unpublished proof of Nagano’s theorem by the author)
to obtain a formal variant of Nagano’s theorem:

Proposition 11.15

(existence of a formal orbit)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be Lie algrebra of formal vector fields over
{\mathbb{K}}
in

n
indeterminates
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
Then there exists an unique
formal submanifold
O\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{n}

with
\dim O=\dim\mathfrak{g}(0)

such that any vector field
L\in\mathfrak{g}
is tangent to
O
,
i.e.
L(I(O))\subset I(O)
.
Moreover if
O^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
is
any other formal manifold such that
all vector fields in
\mathfrak{g}
are tangent to
O^{\prime}
,
then
O\subset O^{\prime}
.

Here
\mathfrak{g}(0)\subset T_{0}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
denotes the subspace
consisting of all values
L_{0}
at
0
of
L\in\mathfrak{g}
.
We call the formal submanifold
O
in Proposition
11.15

the
orbit
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 11.16
.

Since every value
L_{0}
is contained in
T_{0}O
,
the equality
\dim O=\dim\mathfrak{g}(0)
holds
if and only if
T_{0}O=\mathfrak{g}(0)
.

Proof of Proposition
11.15
.

In case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
, the
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
-submodule
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

generated by
\mathfrak{g}

is a Lie algebra satisfying the assumptions of
[
BER03
, Proposition 5.1]
,
which yields the existence and uniqueness of a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-orbit
O
.
Then the same
O
is also a
\mathfrak{g}
-orbit, proving the statement for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
.
The uniqueness follows since any
\mathfrak{g}
-orbit is also a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-orbit.
Finally if
O^{\prime}
is as in the proposition, then all vector fields
in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
are tangent to
O^{\prime}
and
O\subset O^{\prime}

by the second statement of
[
BER03
, Proposition 5.1]
.

In case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}}
, consider
the complexification

\{X+iY:X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n},

which is a Lie subalgebra over
{\mathbb{C}}

that is invariant under conjugations.
By the statement for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
,
there exists a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-orbit
\widetilde{O}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
which is a formal submanifold over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Since
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is invariant under conjugations
and
L\overline{f}=\overline{\overline{L}f}
for
L\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
,
f\in I(\widetilde{O})
,
it follows that
\overline{I(\widetilde{O})}
is also a submanifold ideal
defining a formal submanifold of the same dimension as
\widetilde{O}
,
to which
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is also tangent.
Hence by the uniqueness of the
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-orbit,

\overline{I(\widetilde{O})}=I(\widetilde{O})
, which implies that
I(\widetilde{O})

is the complexification of a real manifold ideal
I=I(O)\subset{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]

defining a formal manifold
O\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Then any
L\in\mathfrak{g}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}

satisfies
L(I(\widetilde{O}))\subset I(\widetilde{O})

and hence
L(I(O))\subset I(O)

since
L
is real and
I(O)=I(\widetilde{O})\cap{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
.
If
O^{\prime}
is any other
\mathfrak{g}
-orbit,
its complexification
\widetilde{O}^{\prime}
is a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-orbit,
hence
\widetilde{O}^{\prime}=\widetilde{O}
by the uniqueness for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
,
and then intersecting with
{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
yields
O^{\prime}=O
.
Finally, if
O^{\prime}
is as in the proposition,
all vector fields in
\widetilde{g}
are tangent to
its complexification
\widetilde{O}^{\prime}
,
hence by the case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
of the proposition,

\widetilde{O}\subset\widetilde{O}^{\prime}
, i.e.
I(\widetilde{O})\supset I(\widetilde{O}^{\prime})
.
Intersecting with
{\mathbb{R}}[[x]]
, we obtain

I(O)\supset I(O^{\prime})
, i.e.
O\subset O^{\prime}
as claimed.
∎

Taking generators of
O
of the form (
11.2
)
and corresponding basis of
D_{O}
given by (
11.3
),
and using the assumption that
\mathfrak{g}
is a
{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
-submodule,
one can prove the following property of the orbit:

Lemma 11.17
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be as in Proposition
11.15

that is also a
{\mathbb{K}}[[x]]
-module.
Then every formal vector field
that is tangent to the orbit

O
of
\mathfrak{g}
is necessarily contained in
\mathfrak{g}
modulo
O
,
in fact

D_{O}=\mathfrak{g}\mod I(O).

This ends the Appendix section.

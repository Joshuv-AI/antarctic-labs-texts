Filtration of cohomology via symmetric semisimplicial spaces
Oishee Banerjee

Abstract
In the simplicial theory of hypercoverings we replace the indexing category  \Delta by the symmetric simplicial category  ​ S \Delta S and study (a class of)  inj ​ S \Delta_{\mathrm{inj}}S -hypercoverings, which we call spaces admitting symmetric (semi)simplicial filtration - this special class happens to have a structure of a module over a graded commutative monoid of the form Sym ​ M \mathrm{Sym\,}M for some space M M . For  ​ S \Delta S -hypercoverings we construct a spectral sequence, somewhat like the Čech-to-derived category spectral sequence. The advantage of working with  ​ S \Delta S over  \Delta is that various combinatorial complexities that come with working on  \Delta are bypassed, giving simpler, unified proof of results like the computation of (in some cases, stable) singular cohomology (with ℚ \mathbb{Q} coefficients) and étale cohomology (with ℚ ℓ \mathbb{Q}_{\ell} coefficients) of the moduli space of degree n n maps C → ℙ r C\to\mathbb{P}^{r} with C C a smooth projective curve of genus g g , of unordered configuration spaces, of the moduli space of smooth sections of a fixed 𝔤 d r \mathfrak{g}^{r}_{d} that is m m -very ample for some m m etc. In the special case when a  inj ​ S \Delta_{\mathrm{inj}}S -object X X admits a symmetric semisimplicial filtration by M M , we relate these moduli spaces to a certain derived tensor.



1  Introduction

The theory of simplicial spaces forms the core of Verdier’s theory of hypercoverings and the subsequent vast generalisations in Deligne’s theory of cohomological descent. We build a theory of hypercoverings where the traditional indexing category
\Delta
(commonly known as the simplicial category) is replaced by
\Delta S
(which we call the
symmetric simplicial category
(see Definition
2.1
), and which contains
\Delta
as a subcategory). Intuitively speaking, whereas
\Delta S
enjoys all the properties that makes
\Delta
a fundamental part of homotopy theory, its objects also have nontrivial automorphism groups, isomorphic to the symmetric groups.

1

1

This might give the reader the impression that
\Delta S
is the equivalent to the category of finite sets, but it’s not, as one can gather immediately from the axioms of Definition
2.1
. And this is the main advantage of working with
\Delta S
. The category
\Delta S
was first introduced as a part of the concept of
crossed simplicial groups
independently by Fiedorowicz-Loday (
[
FL91
]
) and Krasauskas (
[
Kra87
]
).
In particular, if one’s goal is, for example, to compute (stable) (co)homology of moduli spaces (which often come in families indexed by a parameter, say
n
) that are naturally quotients of spaces equipped with permutation actions by
\{S_{n}\}
, then
\Delta S
, by encoding the permutation action as automorphisms in the category itself, gives us a precise tool to entirely bypass all the combinatorial complexities that form a part of
\Delta
.

Amongst
\Delta S
spaces, we define, and give special attention to,
spaces admitting symmetric semisimplicial filtration
(see Definition
2.10
) because of their frequent manifestations in topology and geometry. Roughly speaking, given a family of spaces
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we say
X:=\sqcup X_{n}
(or equivalently
\{X_{n}\}
)admits symmetric semisimplicial filtration by a space
M
with filter gap
e
, if
X
forms a module over the graded commutative topological monoid
\mathrm{Sym\,}M
, where
M
has grading
e
, and satisfies two minor additional conditions (see Definition
2.10
).
We call

U_{n}:=X_{n}-f_{0}(M\times X_{n-e})

the space of
M
-indecomposables of
X_{n}
; we use the same term for

U:=\sqcup U_{n}

as well. We also write

U=X-(M\times X)

which has the unambiguous meaning of
X-f_{0}(M\times X)
under the module structure.

Results.

To state our first two theorems we need some notations and conventions.
For a graded vector space
V
, let
V^{(r)}
denote its
r^{th}
graded component, and let
V^{\textrm{odd}}:=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{(2j+1)}
and
V^{\textrm{even}}:=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{(2j)}
denote the odd and even graded subspaces of
V
, respectively. Throughout this paper, by a
space
we mean a locally-compact Hausdorff topological space or a quasi-projective algebraic variety over some field. By a
morphism
we mean a continuous map of topological spaces or a morphism of algebraic varieties. For a
\mathbb{Z}
-scheme
X
we continue to denote its base change to any algebraically closed field
K
by
X
; in turn we mean
H^{q}(X;\mathbf{Q})
(respectively,
H^{q}_{c}(X;\mathbf{Q})
) to stand for both the singular cohomology
H^{q}(X(\mathbb{C});\mathbb{Q})
(respectively,
H^{q}_{c}(X(\mathbb{C});\mathbb{Q})
, singular cohomology with compact support) as well as the étale cohomology
H_{\acute{e}t}^{q}(X(K);\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
(respectively,
H_{\acute{e}t,c}^{q}(X(K);\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, étale cohomology with compact support),
\ell
coprime to
\mathrm{char}\,\,K
.

Theorem 1

(
Cohomology of indecomposables vs. indecomposables in cohomology
)
.

Let
M
and
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be locally compact connected Hausdorff topological spaces and let
X=\sqcup X_{n}
. Suppose that
X
admits a semisimplicial filtration by
M
, with face maps given by

f_{i}:M^{p}\times X_{n-ep}\to M^{p-1}\times X_{n-e(p-1)}.

Let
e>0
be the filter gap and
U=\sqcup U_{n}
the space of
M
-indecomposables. Then there exists a spectral sequence

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,q}=\bigoplus_{l+m=q}\,\,\bigoplus_{i+j=p}\big(\mathrm{Sym\,}^{i}H_{c}^{\textrm{odd}}(M;\mathbb{Q})\otimes\Lambda^{j}H_{c}^{\textrm{even}}(M;\mathbb{Q})\big)^{(l)}\otimes H^{m}_{c}(X_{n-ep};\mathbb{Q})\implies H^{p+q}_{c}(U_{n};\mathbb{Q})

(1.1)

where the differentials are given by alternating sum of the pullbacks on cohomology induced by the face maps:

\displaystyle d_{1}^{p,q}:E_{1}^{p,q}\to E_{1}^{p+1,q}

\displaystyle d_{1}^{p,q}:=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}f_{i}^{*}.

If
\{X_{n}\}
and
M
are quasi-projective algebraic varieties over a field
K
, then there is a spectral sequence of
Gal(\overline{K}/K)
-representations

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,q}=\bigoplus_{l+m=q}\,\,\bigoplus_{i+j=p}\big(\mathrm{Sym\,}^{i}H_{\acute{e}t,c}^{\textrm{odd}}(M;\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\otimes\Lambda^{j}H_{\acute{e}t,c}^{\textrm{even}}(M;\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\big)^{(l)}\otimes H^{m}_{\acute{e}t,c}(X_{n-ep};\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\implies H^{p+q}_{\acute{e}t,c}(U_{n};\mathbb{Q_{\ell}})

where
\ell
is coprime to
\mathrm{char}\,K
, and the differentials are exactly the same as above.

\hfill\square

In the special case when all spaces are smooth projective varieties or compact oriented manifolds without boundaries, one obtains close cousin (essentially the Verdier dual) of Theorem

as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
M
and
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be compact oriented manifolds without boundaries. Suppose that
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a semisimplicial filtration by
M
, with face maps given by

f_{i}:M^{p}\times X_{n-ep}\to M^{p-1}\times X_{n-e(p-1)}.

Let
e>0
be the filter gap and
\{U_{n}\}
the space of
M

Then there exists a second quadrant spectral sequence which converges to

as an algebra. The

page of that spectral sequence reads as:

\displaystyle E_{1}^{-p,q}=\bigoplus_{l+m=q-c(n,p)}\,\,\bigoplus_{i+j=p}\big(\mathrm{Sym\,}^{i}H^{\textrm{odd}}(M;\mathbb{Q})\otimes\Lambda^{j}H^{\textrm{even}}(M;\mathbb{Q})\big)^{(l)}\otimes H^{m}(X_{n-ep};\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\implies H^{q+p}(U_{n};\mathbb{Q})

(1.2)

with the differentials given by the alternating sum of the Gysin pushforwards induced by the face maps i.e.

\displaystyle d_{1}^{-p,q}:E_{1}^{-p,q}\to E_{1}^{-(p-1),q}

\displaystyle d_{1}^{p,q}:=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}{f_{i}}_{*}.

If
\{X_{n}\}
and
M
are smooth projective algebraic varieties over a field
K
, and if we define

c(n,p):=\dim_{\overline{K}}(X_{n})-\dim_{\overline{K}}(M^{p}\times X_{n-ep}),

then we have a second quadrant spectral sequence of

-representations whose
E_{1}

\displaystyle E_{1}^{-p,q}=\bigoplus_{l+m=q-2c(n,p)}\,\,\bigoplus_{i+j=p}\big(\mathrm{Sym\,}^{i}H_{\acute{e}t}^{\textrm{odd}}(M;\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\otimes\Lambda^{j}H_{\acute{e}t}^{\textrm{even}}(M;\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\big)^{(l)}\otimes H^{m}_{\acute{e}t}(X_{n-ep};\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})(-c(n,p))

\displaystyle\implies H^{q+p}_{\acute{e}t}(U_{n};\mathbb{Q_{\ell}})

where
\ell
is coprime to
\mathrm{char}\,K
, the differentials exactly the same as above, and the spectral sequence converges to
H^{*}(U_{n};\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
as an algebra.

\hfill\square

Remark 1.1
.

To the reader familiar with the concept of derived indecomposables (see, for example,
[
GKRW21
, Defnition 8.5]
) note that the dual (in the sense of Verdier duality) of the spectral sequence above is

\mathrm{Tor}^{H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(\mathrm{Sym\,}M)}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q})\otimes H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(X)\cong\mathrm{Sym\,}(H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(\Sigma M))\otimes H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(X)

where
\Sigma M
denotes the suspension of
M
; which is the associated graded of

\mathrm{Tor}^{H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(\mathrm{Sym\,}M)}(H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(X),\mathbf{Q}),

the derived indecomposables of
H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(X)
as a
H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(\mathrm{Sym\,}M)
-module. It is not true in general that
\mathrm{Tor}^{H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(\mathrm{Sym\,}M)}(H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(X),\mathbf{Q})\cong H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{*}(U,\mathbf{Q})
- a similar isomorphism does, however, hold if we replace homology by the corresponding chain complexes:

\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathrm{Sym\,}(\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{c}(M,\omega_{M}))}(\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{c}(X,\omega_{X}),\mathbf{Q})\cong\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{c}(U,\omega_{U})

where, for any space
B
,
\omega_{B}
denotes its dualizing sheaf. This isomorphism follows from Lemma
2.11
, and is proved in a more general setting in
[
Ban23
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that the ground field is assumed to be
\mathbb{C}
in the theorem above. The proof of the theorem works for all fields except the part where we use some results from Brill-Noether theory (see
[

Remark 1.3

Strictly speaking Corollary

One should note here that spaces admitting symmetric semisimplicial filtration arise as a special type of

More context and one more example: The moduli space of smooth sections of a
\mathfrak{g}^{r}_{d}

A

\dim\mathcal{V}(-\xi)=r+1-(m+1),

\mathcal{V}(-\xi):=H^{0}(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}(-\xi))\cap\mathcal{V}

Though the following result cannot be obtained as a corollary to Theorem
1

\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p-1}H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})(-(p-1))\oplus\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p}H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})(-(p+1))&i=2p+1.\end{cases}

Remark 1.4

Note that the requirement

Our method of proof shares certain similarities with Petersen’s work in

It has been brought to our attention that Tommasi (
[

Finally, to the best of my knowledge, the fact that all of these examples can be studied under the same framework joined by a common thread- the property of being a

Apart from the applications of Theorems

and the author, in

For

Elements of

; in particular,

\displaystyle z\mapsto[s_{0}(z):\ldots:s_{r}(z)].

Conversely, any map

n
morphisms
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
Let

be the

Note that

is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
; indeed, it’s cut out by polynomials in

defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. In turn

is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
.

We apply Theorem

to compute

by plugging in

and showing it admits a semisimplicial filtration by

, with the space of topological

- indecomposables being

and the filter gap

.
To begin, note that for each

we have face maps given by

:

\displaystyle f_{i}:(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{p+1}\times X_{n-(p+1)}\to(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{p}\times X_{n-p}

\displaystyle\big([a_{0}:b_{0}],\ldots,[a_{p}:b_{p}]\big),\,\,[s_{0}:\ldots:s_{r}]\mapsto

\displaystyle\big([a_{0},b_{0}],\ldots,\widehat{[a_{i}:b_{i}]},\ldots[a_{p}:b_{p}]\big),\,\,[(b_{i}x-a_{i}y)s_{0}:\ldots:(b_{i}x-a_{i}y)s_{r}]

(3.6)

In other words, the hypercover under consideration is the following:

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{3}\times\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-3}(r)\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\textstyle\rightarrow\\[-2.15277pt]
\textstyle\rightarrow\\[-2.15277pt]
\textstyle\rightarrow\end{subarray}}(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{2}\times\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-2}(r)\rightrightarrows\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r)\to\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r)

It is almost immediate that the face maps satisfy all the conditions from Definition

with

and

. Plugging them in Theorem

, we obtain a second quadrant spectral sequence which reads as

Computing

.

For simplicity we denote the differential by
d_{1}^{1}
. Let

\iota:\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r)

denote the inclusion given by adding a basepoint. Choose generators

and

, and let

denote the hyperplane class in
\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r)
. Then we claim that:

\displaystyle d_{1}^{1}={f_{0}}_{*}:H^{*-2r}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r))\to H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r))

\displaystyle\mathbb{1}\otimes\iota^{*}\alpha+e\otimes\iota^{*}\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto\alpha h^{r}+\alpha^{\prime}h^{r+1}

is a map of

-modules, where

. To see this, first note that

\iota^{*}:H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r))\to H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r))

is a surjection; next, the image of the fundamental class

is the locus of elements in
\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r)
that has a basepoint i.e.

, which is rationally equivalent, and thus cohomologous, to (a multiple of)

; and finally, for a fixed point

\{[s_{0}:\ldots:s_{r}]\in\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r):s_{i}([a:b])=0\}

is rationally equivalent, and in turn cohomologous, to (a multiple of)

. For the sake of simplicity we won’t bother ourselves with the scalar multiples, which is fine because we’re working over
\mathbf{Q}
.

The Gysin pushforward

surjects onto the ideal generated by
h^{r}
in
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r))
. Indeed, the preimage of

is given by

\displaystyle d_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{1}\otimes\iota^{*}h^{i})=h^{r+i}=d_{1}^{1}(e\otimes\iota^{*}h^{i-1})\text{ for }i\geq 1

\displaystyle d_{1}^{1}([\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r)])=h^{r},

which shows that the image of

is the ideal generated by
h^{r}
in
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r))
. The kernel of

is given by elements of the form

for all
\alpha\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r))
. Again, recalling that
\iota^{*}:H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n}(r))\to H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r))
is a surjection, we conclude that

is generated by elements of the form

(h-e)\otimes\beta,\,\,\,\,\,\,\beta\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r)).

The upshot is that on the
E_{2}
page, for

we have:

\displaystyle E_{2}^{0,q}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{Q}(0)&q=2j,\,\,0\leq j\leq 2(r-1)\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.7)

•

Computing
d_{1}^{2,q}:E_{1}^{-2,q}\to E_{1}^{-1,q}

For simplicity, we denote the differential by

\displaystyle{f_{0}}_{*}:H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\otimes H^{*-2r-2}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-2}(r))\to H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Gamma_{n-1}(r))

\displaystyle{f_{1}}_{*}:H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\otimes H^{*-2r-2}(\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-2}(r))\to H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}\Gamma_{n-1}(r))

\displaystyle H^{i}_{\acute{e}t}(U_{n}(r);\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-j)&i=2j,0\leq j\leq r-1\\

3.4
Moduli space of degree
n
morphisms

,

.

When

, we have

and we discussed it above. Now, let

, and fix a positive integer

A degree
n

is equivalent to the following data:

•

a line bundle
L
of degree
n
on
C
,

•

an
(r+1)
-tuple
(s_{0},\ldots,s_{r})
where
s_{i}\in H^{0}(C,L)

•

the sections
s_{0},\ldots,s_{r}
satisfy the condition that they have no common zeroes (also known as
\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{r}\}

).

Then,
\mathrm{Mor}_{n}(C,\mathbb{P}^{r})
is a Zariski open dense subset of the smooth projective variety

defined by

\displaystyle X_{n}:=\{L,[s_{0}:\ldots:s_{r}]:L\in\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C),s_{i}\in H^{0}(C,L)\text{ for all }i\}.

(3.8)

When
n\geq 2g
(for
g\geq 2
, even

works for our purposes), by the Riemann-Roch theorem
\dim H^{0}(C,L)=n-g+1
for all

, and

, in turn, is isomorphic to the projectivization of a vector bundle

on
\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C)
whose fibres are isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{(n-g+1)(r+1)}
. To elaborate, let
P(n)
be a

C
of degree
n
(see
[
ACGH07
, Chapter IV, Section 2]
for the definition of a Poincaré line bundle and its properties) and let

\nu_{n}:C\times\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C)\to\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C)

we have a vector bundle

E_{n}={\nu_{n}}_{*}P(n)\to\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C),

with the fibre over a point
[L]\in\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C)
being

\big(E_{n}\big)_{L}=H^{0}(C,L)\cong\mathbb{A}^{n-g+1},

and then

can be equivalently described as:

\rho_{n}:X_{n}=\mathbb{P}E_{n}^{r+1}\to\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C)

where
E_{n}^{r+1}
is the
(r+1)
-fold fibre product of
E_{n}
over
\mathit{Pic}^{n}(C)
. We show that
X_{n}
admits symmetric semisimplicial filtration by
C
with filter gap

, and use Theorem
2
to compute

for

.

3

\xi\in\mathrm{Sym\,}^{m}X
is given by

(E_{L})_{\xi}=H^{0}(X,L/L(-\xi)).

Note that the degeneracy loci being empty corresponds to
\mathcal{V}
being
m
-very ample. In theory one can deduce inequalities involving
g,r,d
and
m
for which the virtual fundamental class is empty, as Farkas does in
[
Far08
]
, for most purposes, the formula is extremely complicated and unyielding.

The vector space
\mathcal{V}\subset H^{0}(X,L)
contains, as a Zariski open dense subvariety, the locus of smooth sections
\mathcal{V}^{\circ}
, i.e.

\mathcal{V}^{\circ}:=\{s\in\mathcal{V}:\nexists x\in X\text{ such that }v_{x}(s)\geq 2\}

where
v_{x}(s)
denotes the order of vanishing of
s
at
x
.

Geometrically, when
\mathcal{V}
is
m
-very ample with
m\geq 1
, the image of
X
under the induced embedding
\phi_{\mathcal{V}}:X\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{*})
is a smooth projective curve of degree
d
and up to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
an element of
\mathcal{V}^{\circ}
determines, and is determined by, a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{*})
that intersects
\phi_{\mathcal{V}}(X)
smoothly i.e. at exactly
d
distinct points.

Our goal, now, is to compute the (stable) cohomology
H^{*}(\mathcal{V}^{\circ};\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

We fix
\mathcal{V}
, an
m
-very ample
\mathfrak{g}^{r}_{d}
for the rest of this section.

Step 1.

Construction of a
\mathbb{S}
-object
T_{\bullet}
.

First we construct a
\Delta S
object in the category of schemes augmented on the ‘discriminant locus’
Z:=\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}^{\circ}
.
Define

T_{0}:=\{(s,x):s\in\mathcal{V},v_{x}(s)\geq 2\}

i.e.
T_{0}
is the normalisation of the discriminant locus
Z
; indeed
T_{0}
is smooth,

\displaystyle\pi_{0}:T_{0}\to Z

\displaystyle(s,x)\mapsto s

is a finite surjective morphism, and an isomorphism over a Zariski open dense subset of
T_{0}
given by the locus of sections which are singular at exactly one point in
X
. Now for
p\geq 0
define

T_{p}:=\overline{T_{0}^{\times_{Z}{(p+1)}}-\{\text{all diagonals}\}}

where
T_{0}^{\times_{Z}(p+1)}
is the
(p+1)
-fold fibre product over
Z
, and for convenience that will be clear later, we set

T_{-1}:=\mathcal{V},

(deviating from the standard texts that define
T_{-1}
to be
Z
.) Equivalently, for
p\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle T_{p}=\Big\{(s,(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p})):\mathit{div}(s)\geq 2{\textstyle\sum}x_{i}\Big\}

where
\mathit{div}(s)
denotes the divisor of
s\in\mathcal{V}
.

Henceforth, unless otherwise mentioned, we use
T_{\bullet}
to mean the semisimplicial space
T_{p\geq 0}
. Clearly
T_{\bullet}\to Z
is a symmetric semisimplicial object augmented over
Z
, with face maps corresponding to forgetting one of the factors of
X
:

\displaystyle f^{i}:T_{p}\to T_{p-1}

\displaystyle s,(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p})\,\,\,\mapsto\,\,\,s,(x_{0},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{p})

which are all finite morphisms, and for all permutations
\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{p}
of
0,\ldots,p
under the action of
{S}_{p}
, we define

\displaystyle\pi_{p}:=f^{\sigma_{0}}\circ\ldots\circ f^{\sigma_{p}}:T_{p}\to Z

\displaystyle s,(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p})\mapsto s.

For simplicity we abuse notation and denote, for all
p\geq 0
, the composition

T_{p}\xrightarrow{\pi_{p}}Z\xhookrightarrow{\iota}\mathcal{V}

by
\pi_{p}
as well, instead of
\iota_{\circ}\pi_{p}
. On the other hand, for all
p\geq 0
we have the other projection map:

\displaystyle{}_{p}:T_{p}\to X^{p+1}

\displaystyle s,(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p})\mapsto(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p});

this will be particularly useful in the next step.

Step 2.

Geometry of
T_{p}
.

For each
p\geq 0
, define a vector bundle
E_{p}\to X^{p+1}
as follows (a similar construction is followed in
[
ACGH07
, Chapter IV, Section 2]
over
\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p+1}X
). Let
D(p+1)\subset X\times X^{p+1}
be defined by

D(p+1):=\{x,(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p}):x=x_{i}\text{ for some }0\leq i\leq p\},

and let
pr_{j}
, for
j=1,2
denote, respectively, the projection to the first factor
X
and the second factor
X^{p+1}
. Then

{E}_{p}:={pr_{2}}_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{2{D(p+1)}}\otimes pr_{1}^{*}L).

is a locally free sheaf (because,
pr_{1}^{*}L
is locally free and
2D(p+1)
being flat over
X^{p+1}
imply
\mathcal{O}_{2D(p+1)}\otimes pr_{1}^{*}L
is also flat) and equivalently a vector bundle, with stalks given by

\big({E_{p}}\big)_{\overline{x}}=H^{0}(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{2\xi(\overline{x})})

where for all
\overline{x}\in X^{p+1}
we define
\xi(\overline{x})
to be the corresponding unordered
(p+1)
-tuple. We will often abuse notation and denote an divisor by
\xi
when there is no scope of confusion.

The natural map of sheaves given by restriction

pr_{1}^{*}L\to\mathcal{O}_{2D(p+1)}\otimes pr_{1}^{*}L

induces a map on pushforwards called the evaluation map

ev:{pr_{2}}_{*}pr_{1}^{*}L=H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{p+1}}\to E_{p},

where
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{p+1}}
is the trivial bundle on
X^{p+1}
with fibres
H^{0}(X,L)
. Restricting this to
\mathcal{V}\subset H^{0}(X,L)
gives us the map

ev:\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{p+1}}\to E_{p+1}.

At the level of stalks, one obtains the map

\mathcal{V}\to H^{0}(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{2\xi(\overline{x})}),

the kernel of which is precisely
\mathcal{V}(-2\xi(\overline{x}))
. Indeed, for any divisor
\xi
on
X
, we have a short exact sequence of locally free sheaves on
X
:

0\to L(-2\xi)\to L\to L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{2\xi}\to 0

that induces a long exact sequence of cohomology

0\to H^{0}(X,L(-2\xi))\to H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{2\xi})\to\ldots

and taking intersection with
\mathcal{V}
gives us that at the level of stalks

\mathrm{Kernel}\Big((ev_{\overline{x}}):\mathcal{V}\to H^{0}(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{2\xi(\overline{x})})\Big)=\mathcal{V}(-2\xi(\overline{x})).

And now note that we have the following diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}\times X^{p+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{p+1}}
p
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ev}

where, by definition,
{}_{p}:T_{p}\to X^{p+1}
is the kernel of the evaluation map. And now observe that by the very definition of
m
-very ampleness (see Definition
4.1
), we obtain the following:

Lemma 4.3

(Stable bound for cohomology)
.

For each
0\leq p\leq{m+1\over 2}
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{p}:T_{p}\to X^{p+1}

is a vector bundle with the fibre over a point
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p})\in X^{p+1}
given by

{}^{-1}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p})=\mathcal{V}(-2\xi(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p}))\cong\mathbb{C}^{r+1-2(p+1)}.

Step 3.

Constructing a spectral sequence for the semisimplicial object
T_{\bullet}
.

Let
j:\mathcal{V}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}
denote the inclusion. Then by Lemma
2.11
we have an acylic complex of sheaves of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces on
\mathcal{V}
given by:

\displaystyle j_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{V}^{\circ}}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{V}}\to{\pi_{0}}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{T_{0}}\to\Big({\pi_{1}}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{T_{1}}\otimes\mathit{sgn}_{2}\Big)^{{S}_{2}}\to\ldots\to\big({\pi_{p}}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{T_{p}}\otimes\mathit{sgn}_{p+1}\big)^{{S}_{p+1}}\to\ldots

Let
C^{\bullet}
denote the complex

\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{V}}\to{\pi_{0}}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{T_{0}}\to\Big({\pi_{1}}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{T_{1}}\otimes\mathit{sgn}_{2}\Big)^{{S}_{2}}\to\ldots.

Let us define
T_{-1}=\mathcal{V}
, and
\pi_{-1}:=id_{T_{-1}}
, the identity map on
T_{-1}
.
Taking
\mathbf{R}\Gamma_{c}
of the complex
C^{\bullet}
one obtains a spectral sequence that reads as

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,q}=R^{q}\Gamma_{c}\Big(\mathcal{V},\big({\pi_{p-1}}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{T_{p-1}}\big)^{{S}_{p}}\Big)\implies R^{p+q}\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{V},j_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{V}^{\circ}})

(4.1)

On the right hand side, we have

R^{p+q}\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{V},j_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{V}^{\circ}})=H_{c}^{p+q}(\mathcal{V}_{\circ};\mathbb{Q}).

To simplify the
E_{1}^{p,q}
terms we go through the following steps:

\displaystyle R^{q}\Gamma_{c}\Big(\mathcal{V},\big({\pi_{p-1}}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{T_{p-1}}\big)^{{S}_{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(H_{c}^{q}(T_{p-1})\otimes\mathit{sgn}_{p}\Big)^{{S}_{p}}(-p)

\displaystyle(\pi_{p}\text{ finite})

\displaystyle\cong\Big(H_{c}^{q}(X^{p}\times\mathbb{C}^{r+1-2p})\otimes\mathit{sgn}_{p}\Big)^{{S}_{p}}(-p)

\displaystyle(\text{for all }p\leq{m\over 2,}\text{ by Lemma \ref{vbness}})

\cong\begin{cases}H^{2}_{c}(X)\otimes\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p-1}H^{1}_{c}(X)(-p),&q=2(r+1)-3p+1\\
H^{0}_{c}(X)\otimes H^{2}_{c}(X)\otimes\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p-2}H^{1}_{c}(X)(-p)\bigoplus\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p}H^{1}_{c}(X)(-p),&q=2(r+1)-3p\\
H^{0}_{c}(X)\otimes\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p-1}H^{1}_{c}(X)(-p),&q=2(r+1)-3p-1\\
0,&q\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

where the last step comes from the Macdonald’s result on the permutation action of the symmetric group
S_{p}
(twisted by the sign representation) on the cohomology
H^{*}(X)^{\otimes p}
(see
[
Mac62
]
).

Now observe the following: from Lemma
2.11
we have an equality
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{V},C^{\bullet})=\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{V},j_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\circ})
in the derived category of constructible sheaves over
\mathcal{V}
; on the other hand, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\mathrm{R}^{i}\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{V},C^{\bullet})\cong\mathrm{R}^{i}\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{V},C^{\bullet}/\tau_{\geq N}C^{\bullet})

for all
i\geq 2(r+1)-2N
, where
\tau_{\geq N}C^{\bullet}
denotes the truncated complex up to the
(N-1)
term and this is because
\tau_{\geq N}C^{\bullet}
is supported on complex codimension
N
in
\mathcal{V}
.

This observation, paired with Poincaré duality
H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{V}^{\circ};\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{2(r+1)-i}(\mathcal{V}^{\circ};\mathbb{Q})
gives us that for all
i\leq{m-1\over 2}
:

\displaystyle H^{i}(\mathcal{V}^{\circ};\mathbb{Q})\cong\begin{cases}\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p-2}H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})(-(p-1))\oplus\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p}H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})(-p)&i=2p\\
\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p-1}H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})(-(p-1))\oplus\mathrm{Sym\,}^{p}H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})(-(p+1))&i=2p+1\end{cases}

(4.2)

which completes the proof of Theorem
5
.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

Note that we could have defined the
\Delta S
-space
\tilde{T}_{\bullet}
by defining
\tilde{T}_{p}:=T_{0}^{\times_{Z}(p+1)}
and directly used Lemma
2.8
. The rest of the computation, and in particular the analysis of the spectral sequence would have remained exactly the same though, because the permutation action of the symmetric groups twisted by sign kills all degeneracies.

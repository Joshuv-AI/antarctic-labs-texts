Abstract
. Let Ω ⊂ ℝ n + 1 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} be a bounded chord-arc domain, let ℒ = − div A ∇ \mathcal{L}=-{\rm div}A\nabla be an elliptic operator in Ω \Omega associated with a matrix A A having Dini mean oscillation coefficients, and let 1 < p ≤ 2 1<p\leq 2 . In this paper we show that if the regularity problem for ℒ \mathcal{L} is solvable in L q L^{q} for some q > p q>p in Ω \Omega , ∂ Ω {\partial\Omega} supports a weak p p -Poincaré inequality, and Ω \Omega has very big pieces of superdomains for which the Neumann problem for ℒ \mathcal{L} is solvable uniformly in L q L^{q} , then the Neumann problem for ℒ \mathcal{L} is solvable in L p L^{p} in Ω \Omega .

Introduction

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}
be a matrix with real measurable coefficients in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. We say that
A
is
uniformly elliptic
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with constant
\Lambda\geq 1
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle,\quad\mbox{ for all $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}$ and a.e. $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}$.}

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|a_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}\leq\Lambda,\quad\mbox{ for all $i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,n+1\}$.}

Notice that the matrix
A
is
possibly non-symmetric
.

For a ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we denote
m_{B}(A)=\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B}A(y)\,dy
and we consider the mean oscillation function
\omega_{A}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
defined by

\omega_{A}(r)=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|A(y)-m_{B(x,r)}(A)|\,dy.

We say that
A
has Dini mean oscillation if

\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{A}(r)\,\frac{dr}{r}<\infty.

If

\mathcal{L}=-{\rm div}(A(\cdot)\nabla)

is an elliptic operator of divergence form associated with a uniformly elliptic matrix
A
,
we write
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
. If, moreover
A
has
Dini mean oscillation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we write
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm DMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
. We denote its formal adjoint operator by
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}=-{\rm div}(A^{T}(\cdot)\nabla)
, where
A^{T}
is the transpose matrix of
A
. If the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is only assumed to be defined in an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we write either
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
or
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm DMO}(\Omega)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a bounded chord-arc domain. For
\xi\in{\partial\Omega}
, let
\nu_{A}(\xi)=A^{T}(\xi)\,\nu(\xi)
, where
\nu(\xi)
is the outer unit normal to
{\partial\Omega}
.
Given
g\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}({\partial\Omega})
with
\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,d\sigma=0
, we consider the Neumann problem

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{L}}u=0&\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\partial_{\nu_{A}}u=g&\quad\mbox{in ${\partial\Omega}$},\\
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega).\end{array}\right.

The identity
\partial_{\nu_{A}}u=g
in
{\partial\Omega}
should be understood in the following weak sense:

(1.4)

\int_{\Omega}A\nabla u\,\nabla{\varphi}\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,{\varphi}\,d\sigma\quad\mbox{ for all ${\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})$,}

where
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega}
is the “surface measure” on
{\partial\Omega}
.

For a function
v:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the
non-tangential maximal function
of
v
by

(1.5)

{\mathcal{N}}_{\Omega}v(\xi)=\sup_{x\in\gamma_{\Omega}(\xi)}|v(x)|,\quad\mbox{ for $\xi\in{\partial\Omega}$,}

where, for
\xi\in{\partial\Omega}
and a fixed

\alpha>0
,
\gamma_{\Omega}(\xi)\equiv\gamma_{\Omega,\alpha}(\xi)
is the non-tangential “cone” in
\Omega
with vertex in
\xi

defined by

(1.6)

\gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)=\{x\in\Omega:|\xi-x|<(1+\alpha){\rm dist}(x,{\partial\Omega})\}.

We also define the
modified non-tangential maximal function
of
v
by

(1.7)

{\widetilde{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{\Omega}v(\xi)=\sup_{x\in\gamma_{\Omega}(\xi)}\bigg(\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B(x,\delta_{\Omega}(x)/2)}|v|^{2}\,dm\bigg)^{1/2},\quad\mbox{ for $\xi\in{\partial\Omega}$,}

where
\delta_{\Omega}(x)={\rm dist}(x,{\partial\Omega})
.

For
1<p<\infty
, we say that the

(for
{\mathcal{L}}
) is solvable in

For the sake of brevity, we will write that

(or

We say that

As in

MT
]

.

In the last decade, there has been significant activity in the area of boundary value problems for elliptic PDE’s in rough domains and related free boundary problems. The primary goal of this research program was to find necessary and sufficient geometric conditions for a domain
\Omega
with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary that guarantee the solvability of the Dirichlet problem with
L^{p}
boundary data for the Laplace operator or more general operators
{\mathcal{L}}
in the domain, denoted as
(D_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
being solvable in
\Omega
. This question was settled for the Laplace operator by Azzam, Hofmann, Martell, and the authors of the present manuscript in
[
AHMMT
]
.

The method to achieve solvability of
(D_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
in domains more general than Lipschitz is via the so-called Big Pieces functor. Specifically, chord-arc domains have Big Pieces of (interior or exterior) starlike Lipschitz subdomains (see
[
DJ
]
and
[
Se
]
). Using either the work of Dahlberg
[
Dah
]
for the Laplace operator or the work of Kenig and Pipher
[
KP01
]
for the so-called Dahlberg-Kenig-Pipher or DKP operators (i.e., operators where
\nabla A
satisfies certain
L^{2}
type Carleson measure conditions), combined with the maximum principle, it can be shown that harmonic/elliptic measures belong to the
A_{\infty}
class of Muckenhoupt weights. This condition implies that there exists
p>1
such that
(D_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable in the chord-arc domain. Similarly, one can use the aforementioned result to obtain
(D_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
solvability in domains that have Big Pieces of interior chord-arc domains. This is shown in
[
AHMMT
]
to be the optimal class of corkscrew domains with Ahlfors regular boundaries in which
(D_{p})_{\Delta}
is solvable.

Despite the fact that solvability of
(D_{p})_{\Delta}
in chord-arc domains was settled as early as 1990 by David and Jerison
[
DJ
]
, and independently by Semmes
[
Se
]
, solvability of the regularity problem in
L^{q}
for
\Delta
had only been proved in Lipschitz domains up to recently (see
[
JK81a
]
for
q=2
,
[
V
]
for
1<q\leq 2
, and
[
DaKe
]
for the optimal range of exponents). The existence of
q
such that
(R_{q})_{\Delta}
is solvable in chord-arc domains, a question posed by Kenig in 1991
[
Ke
, Problem 3.2.2]
, was finally proved in 2021 by the authors (see
[
MT
]
). In fact, in this work a more general result was shown, namely, that in corkscrew domains with Ahlfors regular boundaries,
(D_{p})_{\Delta}\Rightarrow(R_{p^{\prime}})_{\Delta}
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Moreover, the same paper demonstrated that
(R_{p^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{L}}\Rightarrow(D_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
for any
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
.

For elliptic operators in divergence form with non-constant coefficients less is known.
For example,
for the so-called DKP operators, solvability of
(R_{q})_{\mathcal{L}}
for some
q>1
was not known even in the ball until quite recently. The only known result was by Dindoš, Pipher, and Rule
[
DiPR
]
, where the authors showed in 2017 that
(R_{q})_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable in Lipschitz domains with sufficiently small Lipschitz constants and for DKP operators whose coefficients have sufficiently small oscillations. In
[
MPT
]
, inspired by ideas in
[
MT
]
, the authors, in collaboration with Poggi, proved that if a corkscrew domain has a uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary, then
(D_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}\Rightarrow(R_{p^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}
for any operator
\mathcal{L}
satisfying the DKP condition (without any smallness assumption on the oscilation of the coefficients). To do so, they introduced two new Poisson problems with data in certain scale-invariant
L^{p}
-Carleson-type spaces. They called them Poisson Dirichlet and Poisson regularity problems, denoted by
(PD_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
and
(PR_{p^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}
, and proved that in corkscrew domains with Ahlfors regular boundaries, for any
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
, it holds that

(D_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}\Longleftrightarrow(PD_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}\Longleftrightarrow(PR_{p^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}.

Simultaneously and independently, Dindoš, Hofmann, and Pipher
[
DiHP
]
(see also
[
Fe
]
) showed that
(R_{q})_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable for DKP operators in Lipschitz graph domains. Their proof is significantly shorter although it uses the existence of a preferred direction and thus it can’t be generalized to rougher domains. We would also like to mention the work by Gallegos and us
[
GMT
]
, where extrapolation of solvability of the regularity and the Poisson regularity problems is obtained in corkscrew domains with Ahlfors regular boundaries for any
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
, as well as the work by one of us and Zacharopoulos
[
MZ
]
where Varopoulos’ extensions are constructed and used to obtain similar duality results for elliptic systems with complex coefficients.

While recent advances have led to a pretty good understanding of the Dirichlet and regularity problems in rough domains, this is not the case for the Neumann problem with data in
L^{p}
. Indeed, for a Lipschitz domain with connected boundary, in 1987 Dahlberg and Kenig proved in
[
DaKe
]
that
(N_{p})_{\Delta}
is solvable for
p\in(1,2+\varepsilon)
. This range is optimal since for any
p>2
, there exists a Lipschitz domain such that
(N_{p})_{\Delta}
is not solvable. Their proof uses the solvability of
(N_{2})_{\Delta}
, achieved previously by Jerison and Kenig in
[
JK81b
]
via the so-called two-sided Rellich inequality in
L^{2}
, i.e.,
\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{2}\approx\|\nabla_{t}u\|_{2}
.

Moreover, in
[
DiPR
]
it was shown that in a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a sufficiently small Lipschitz constant and for DKP operators with coefficients with sufficiently small oscillation,
(N_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable for any
p\in(1,\infty)
. More recently, it was proved in
[
DiHP
]
that if
\Omega
is a Lipschitz graph domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathcal{L}
satisfying the DKP condition, then there exists
q\in(1,\infty)
such that
(N_{q})_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable. This result follows from a reduction to the solvability of a relevant regularity problem
(R_{q})_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=-\text{div}(\widetilde{A}(\cdot)\nabla)
and
\widetilde{A}=A/\det A
(an idea originated from work of Kenig and Rule
[
KR
]
). On the other hand, for any
p>2
there are examples of Lipschitz domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, with
n\geq 2
, such that
(R_{p})_{\Delta}\nRightarrow(N_{p})_{\Delta}
(see
[
KP95
, Lemma 3.1]
). Nevertheless, it is not clear if one should expect that
(R_{p})_{\Delta}\Rightarrow(N_{p})_{\Delta}
for
p\in(1,2]
.

An open problem posed by Kenig in 1991
[
Ke

and reintroduced by Toro at the ICM in 2010

is the following:

Problem
.

In a bounded chord-arc domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,

p>1
such that the Neumann problem for the Laplacian with boundary data in

is solvable?

If
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a bounded chord-arc domain, Jerison and Kenig
[
JK82b
]
showed that
(N_{p})_{\Delta}\Leftrightarrow(R_{p})_{\Delta}\Leftrightarrow(D_{p^{\prime}})_{\Delta}
. For a result that applies to rougher but also flatter domains than Lipschitz, we refer to the work of Hofmann, Mitrea, and Taylor
[
HMT
, Section 7]
, where they prove that for every

, there exists an

such that for every
\varepsilon

[
HMT
, Definition 4.8]
for the definition),

is solvable.
By Semmes’ decomposition (see
[

. Note that for general chord-arc domains,
the lack of flatness does not allow one to prove invertibility of layer potentials as in
[
HMT
]
, thus necessitating different
methods.

In a recent interesting work, Feneuil and Li
[
FL
]
proved the Neumann counterpart of
[
MPT
, Theorem 1.22]
, exploring the connections between the solvability of Poisson Neumann problems with interior data in appropriate Carleson-type spaces and the solvability of
(N_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
. They also demonstrated the extrapolation of the solvability of the Poisson Neumann problem, which, in turn, led to the extrapolation of the solvability of
(N_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
under the assumption of the solvability of
(D_{p^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}
. This result enhances
[
KP93
, Theorem 6.3]
, even in the context of the ball, where the extrapolation of
(N_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
was proved assuming the solvability of
(R_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
(which, by
[
MT
, Theorem A.2]
, in corkscrew domains with Ahlfors regular boundaries, implies the solvability of
(D_{p^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}
). It is noteworthy that, simultaneously and independently,
[
KP93
, Theorem 6.3]
was extended to chord-arc domains by Hofmann and Sparrius
[
HS
]
adapting the method in
[
KP93
]
, which itself was an interesting achievement.

The Neumann problem with
L^{p}
data is a notoriously difficult problem. Unlike the Dirichlet problem, one cannot use the maximum principle to transfer solvability from the subdomains to the original domain. Note that the solution of the Dirichlet problem has a representation via the “Poisson kernel,” which is a positive function, allowing one to split the data into its positive and negative parts and work with positive solutions. One of the major challenges in the Neumann problem is to find a way to achieve the transference of solvability between domains and subdomains (or superdomains).

The appropriate dual version of the Neumann problem is a “rough” Neumann problem with data
f
in a “negative” Sobolev space. Loosely speaking, for a function
f:{\partial\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\int_{\partial\Omega}f\,d\sigma=0
,
there exists a vector field
\vec{g}\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N_{n}})
1
1

1

If
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
and
\partial\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this is exactly the space we are interested in, with
N_{n}=n
.
such that
f=-\text{div}_{t}\vec{g}
in a certain sense, where
\text{div}_{t}
denotes the tangential divergence along the boundary. Then
the solution
u
of the variational Neumann with boundary data
f
problem can be expressed via the Neumann function as (see
Theorem
2.9
):

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}N(x,\xi)f(\xi)\,d\sigma(\xi)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla_{t}N(x,\xi)\cdot\vec{g}(\xi)\,d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle=c\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\tilde{\partial_{j}}N(x,\xi)g_{j}(\xi)\,d\sigma(\xi),

where

represents an appropriate version of the tangential partial derivatives. Now, it is clear that

plays the role that the Poisson kernel plays in the Dirichlet problem, which, in nice domains, coincides with
\partial_{\nu_{A}}G(x,\xi)
(here

stands for the Green function). Due to the lack of information about the sign of the partial derivatives of the Neumann function on the boundary, we cannot hope to be able to introduce some kind of positive Neumann harmonic measure and apply the maximum principle.
We remark that in Section

2.1.
Measures, rectifiability, and dyadic lattices

All measures in this paper are assumed to be Borel measures.
We denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and we assume
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
to be normalized so that it coincides with
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure in

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Sometimes we will denote the
(n+1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measures in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
by
m
, and integration with respect to
dx
or
dy
also means integration with respect to Lebesgue measure.
For a measure
\mu
, a Borel set
A
, and a function
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
, we use the notation

m_{\mu,A}(f)=\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{A}f\,d\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}f\,d\mu.

In case
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure, we just write
m_{A}(f)
.

A measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is called

C_{0}
-
n
-Ahlfors regular
(or just
n
-Ahlfors regular or Ahlfors regular
or Ahlfors-David regular) if there exists some
constant
C_{0}>0
such that

C_{0}^{-1}r^{n}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{0}\,r^{n}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and $0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)$.}

The measure
\mu
is
uniformly
n
-rectifiable
if it is

n
-Ahlfors regular and
there exist constants
\theta,M>0
such that for all
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and all
0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)

there is a Lipschitz mapping
g
from the ball
B_{n}(0,r)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with
\text{Lip}(g)\leq M
such that

\mu(B(x,r)\cap g(B_{n}(0,r)))\geq\theta r^{n}.

A set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is called
n
-
rectifiable
if there are Lipschitz maps

f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, such that

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\Bigl(E\setminus\bigcup_{i}f_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\Bigr)=0.

The set
E
is
C_{0}
-
n
-Ahlfors regular if
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}
is
C_{0}
-
n
-Ahlfors regular.
Also,

E
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable if
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable.
The notion of uniform rectifiability is a quantitative version of rectifiability which was introduced by David and Semmes in the pioneering works
[
DS1
]
and
[
DS2
]
.

Given an
n
-Ahlfors measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we consider
the dyadic lattice of “cubes” built by David and Semmes in
[
DS2
, Chapter 3 of Part I]
. The properties satisfied by
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
are the following.
Assume first, for simplicity, that
{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)=\infty
). Then for each
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists a family
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
of Borel subsets of
\operatorname{supp}\mu
(the dyadic cubes of the
j
-th generation) such that:

(a)

each
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
is a partition of
\operatorname{supp}\mu
, i.e.
\operatorname{supp}\mu=\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}}Q
and
Q\cap Q^{\prime}=\varnothing
whenever
Q,Q^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
and

Q\neq Q^{\prime}
;

(b)

if
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
and
Q^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,k}
with
k\leq j
, then either
Q\subset Q^{\prime}
or
Q\cap Q^{\prime}=\varnothing
;

(c)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
, we have
2^{-j}\lesssim{\rm diam}(Q)\leq 2^{-j}
and
\mu(Q)\approx 2^{-jn}
;

(d)

there exists
C>0
such that, for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
, and
0<\tau<1
,

(2.1)

\begin{split}\mu\big(\{x\in Q:\,&{\rm dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}\mu\setminus Q)\leq\tau 2^{-j}\}\big)\\
&+\mu\big(\{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\setminus Q:\,{\rm dist}(x,Q)\leq\tau 2^{-j}\}\big)\leq C\tau^{1/C}2^{-jn}.\end{split}

This property is usually called the
small boundaries condition
.
From (
2.1
), it follows that there is a point
x_{Q}\in Q
(the center of
Q
) such that
{\rm dist}(x_{Q},\operatorname{supp}\mu\setminus Q)\gtrsim 2^{-j}
(see
[
DS2
, Lemma 3.5 of Part I]
).

We set
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}:=\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
.

In case that
{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)<\infty
, the families
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
are only defined for
j\geq j_{0}
, with

2^{-j_{0}}\approx{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)
, and the same properties above hold for
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}:=\bigcup_{j\geq j_{0}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
.

Given a cube
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,j}
, we say that its side length is
2^{-j}
, and we denote it by
\ell(Q)
. Notice that
{\rm diam}(Q)\leq\ell(Q)
.
We also denote

(2.2)

B(Q):=B(x_{Q},c_{1}\ell(Q)),\qquad B_{Q}:=B(x_{Q},\ell(Q)),

where
c_{1}>0
is some fixed constant such that
B(Q)\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset Q
for all
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
. Clearly, we have
Q\subset B_{Q}
.

For
\lambda>1
, we write

\lambda Q=\bigl\{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu:\,{\rm dist}(x,Q)\leq(\lambda-1)\,\ell(Q)\bigr\}.

The side length of a “true cube”
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is also denoted by
\ell(P)
. On the other hand, given a ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, its radius is denoted by
r(B)
. For
\lambda>0
, the ball
\lambda B
is the ball concentric with
B
with radius
\lambda\,r(B)
.

2.2.
The Whitney decomposition of
\Omega

For any open set
\Omega\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, there is a family
{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega)
(the Whitney cubes of
\Omega
) of dyadic cubes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with disjoint interiors contained in
\Omega
such that

\bigcup_{P\in{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega)}P=\Omega,

and moreover there are
some constants
\Lambda>20
and
D_{0}\geq 1
such that the following holds for every
P\in{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega)
:

(i)

10P\subset\Omega
;

(ii)

\Lambda P\cap\partial\Omega\neq\varnothing
;

(iii)

there are at most
D_{0}
cubes
P^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega)

such that
10P\cap 10P^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. Further, for such cubes
P^{\prime}
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\ell(P^{\prime})\leq\ell(P)\leq 2\ell(P^{\prime})
.

From the properties (i) and (ii) it is clear that
{\rm dist}(P,\partial\Omega)\approx\ell(P)
. We assume that
the Whitney cubes are small enough so that

(2.3)

{\rm diam}(P)<\frac{1}{20}\,{\rm dist}(P,\partial\Omega).

The arguments to construct a Whitney decomposition satisfying the properties above are
standard.

Suppose that
{\partial\Omega}
is
n
-Ahlfors regular and consider the dyadic lattice
{\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma}
defined above, for
\sigma={\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega}
.
Then, for each Whitney cube
P\in{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega)
there is some cube
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma}
such that
\ell(Q)=\ell(P)

and
{\rm dist}(P,Q)\approx\ell(Q)
, with the implicit constant depending on the parameters of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma}
and on the Whitney decomposition. We denote this by
Q=b(P)
(“b” stands for “boundary”). Conversely,
given
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma}
, we let

(2.4)

w(Q)=\bigcup_{P\in{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega):Q=b(P)}P.

It is immediate to check that
w(Q)
is made up at most of a uniformly bounded number of cubes
P
, but it may happen
that
w(Q)=\varnothing
.

2.3.
Chord-arc domains

A domain is a connected open set.
In the whole paper,
\Omega
will be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, with
n\geq 1
.
Often we will denote the
n
-Hausdorff measure on
{\partial\Omega}
by
\sigma
.

The open set
\Omega
satisfies the
C_{1}
-corkscrew condition if
there exists some
C_{1}>0
such that for all

x\in{\partial\Omega}
and all
r\in(0,2{\rm diam}(\Omega))
there exists a ball
B\subset B(x,r)\cap\Omega
such that

r(B)\geq C_{1}^{-1}\,r
. We also say that
\Omega
is a
C_{1}
-corkscrew domain, or just a corkscrew domain if we do not want to mention the constant
C_{1}
.

Given two points
x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega
, and a pair of numbers
M,N\geq 1
,
an
(M,N)
-
Harnack chain connecting
x
to
x^{\prime}
, is a chain of
open balls

B_{1},\dots,B_{N}\subset\Omega
,
with
x\in B_{1},\,x^{\prime}\in B_{N},

B_{k}\cap B_{k+1}\neq\varnothing

and
M^{-1}{\rm diam}(B_{k})\leq{\rm dist}(B_{k},\partial\Omega)\leq M{\rm diam}(B_{k}).

For
C_{2}\geq 1
, we say that
\Omega
satisfies the
C_{2}
-Harnack chain condition
if
for any two points
x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega
,
there is an
(M,N)
-Harnack chain connecting them, with
M\leq C_{2}
and
N
such that

N\leq C_{2}\,\bigg(1+\log^{+}\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\min(\delta_{\Omega}(x),\delta_{\Omega}(x^{\prime}))}\bigg).

Recall that
\delta_{\Omega}(x)={\rm dist}(x,{\partial\Omega})
.

We say that a domain
\Omega
is
C_{3}
-uniform, if it satisfies the

C_{3}
-Harnack chain condition and the
C_{3}
-corkscrew condition.
Following
[
JK82a
]
, we say that a
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
C_{3}
-NTA (
non-tangentially accessible
) if it is uniform
2
2

2

In
[
JK82a
]
the Harnack chain condition is formulated in a different but equivalent form.
and
\Omega_{\textup{ext}}:={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
satisfies the
C_{3}
-corkscrew condition. We also say that a connected open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}

is a
C_{3}
-CAD (
chord-arc domain
), if it is
C_{3}
-NTA and
{\partial\Omega}
is
C_{3}
-
n
-Ahlfors regular. In this case, we say that
C_{3}
is the CAD constant of
\Omega
. Additionally, if a domain
\Omega
and its exterior
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
are
C
-CAD, then we say that
\Omega
is a two-sided
C
-CAD. To simplify notation, we may write NTA or CAD in place of
C
-NTA or
C
-CAD.

It was shown independently by David and Jerison
[
DJ
]
and
[
Se
]
that if
\Omega
is a CAD, then its boundary
{\partial\Omega}
is uniformly

n
-rectifiable.

2.4.
Finite perimeter sets and reduced boundary,

An open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
has
finite perimeter
if the distributional gradient
\nabla\chi_{\Omega}
of
\chi_{\Omega}
is a locally finite
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
-valued measure. From results of De Giorgi and Moser it follows that
\nabla\chi_{\Omega}=-\nu_{\Omega}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{{\partial\Omega}^{*}}
, where
\partial^{*}\Omega\subset{\partial\Omega}
is the reduced boundary of
\Omega
and

|\nu_{\Omega}(x)|=1

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
-a.e. in
\partial^{*}\Omega
. By
[
EG
, Theorem 5.15]
,
\partial^{*}\Omega
can be written, up to a set of
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
-measure zero, as a countable union of compact sets
\{K_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
where
K_{j}\subset S_{j}
for a
C^{1}
hypersurface
S_{j}
and
\nu_{\Omega}|_{S_{j}}
is normal to
S_{j}
. Moreover, the following Green’s formula is satisfied: for every
{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1};{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
,

(2.5)

\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\rm div}\varphi(x)\,dx=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}\nu_{\Omega}(\xi)\cdot{\varphi}(\xi)\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\xi).

More generally, given a unit vector
\nu_{\Omega}
and
x\in{\partial\Omega}
, we define the (closed) half-spaces

H^{\pm}_{\nu_{\Omega}}(x)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:\pm\nu_{\Omega}\cdot(y-x)\geq 0\}.

Then, for
x\in\partial^{*}\Omega
, it holds

(2.6)

\lim_{r\to 0}r^{-(n+1)}m\left(B(x,r)\cap\Omega^{\pm}\cap H^{\pm}_{\nu_{\Omega}}\right)>0,

where
\Omega^{+}=\Omega
and
\Omega^{-}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\Omega
(see for instance
[
EG
, p. 230]
). A unit vector
\nu_{\Omega}
satisfying (
2.6
)
is called the
measure theoretic outer unit normal
to
\Omega
at
x
and we denote by
\partial_{0}\Omega
all the points of
{\partial\Omega}
for which (
2.6
) holds. It is clear that
\partial^{*}\Omega\subset\partial_{0}\Omega
.

The
measure theoretic boundary

\partial_{*}\Omega
consists of the points
x\in{\partial\Omega}
such that

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{m(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)}{r^{n+1}}>0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{m(B(x,r)\setminus\overline{\Omega})}{r^{n+1}}>0.

When
\Omega
has finite perimeter, it holds that
\partial_{*}\Omega\subset\partial_{0}\Omega\subset\partial^{*}\Omega\subset{\partial\Omega}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega\setminus\partial_{*}\Omega)=0
. A good reference for those results is either the book of Evans and Gariepy
[
EG
]
or the book of Maggi
[
Mag
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a bounded open set with Ahlfors regular boundary, then it has finite perimeter. If it also satisfies the two-sided corkscrew condition, then it holds that
{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{*}\Omega
, (see
[
HMT
, (3.1.25), p. 52]
). Therefore, for such domains,
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega)=0
.

2.5.
The Hajłasz Sobolev space and the HMT Sobolev space

Let
\Sigma
be a metric space equipped with a doubling
measure
\sigma
on
\Sigma
, which means that there is a uniform constant
C_{\sigma}\geq 1
such that
\sigma(B(x,2r))\leq C_{\sigma}\,\sigma(B(x,r))
, for all
x\in\Sigma
and
r>0
. We will now define the
Hajłasz’s Sobolev spaces

\dot{M}^{1,p}(\Sigma)
and
{M}^{1,p}(\Sigma)
, which were introduced in
[
Ha
]
. For more information on those spaces and, in general, Sobolev spaces in metric measure spaces, the reader may consult
[
He
]
.

For a Borel function
f:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that a non-negative Borel function
g:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
Hajłasz upper gradient of
f
if

(2.7)

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq|x-y|\,(g(x)+g(y))\quad\mbox{ for $\sigma$-a.e. $x,y\in\Sigma$.}

We denote the collection of all the Hajłasz upper gradients of
f
by
D(f)
.

For
p\geq 1
, we denote by
\dot{M}^{1,p}(\Sigma)
the space of Borel functions
f
which have
a Hajłasz upper gradient in
L^{p}(\sigma)
, and we let
M^{1,p}(\Sigma)
be the space of functions
f\in L^{p}(\sigma)
which have a Hajłasz upper gradient in
L^{p}(\sigma)
, i.e.,
M^{1,p}(\Sigma)=\dot{M}^{1,p}(\Sigma)\cap L^{p}(\sigma)
.
We define the semi-norm (as it annihilates constants)

(2.8)

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1.p}(\Sigma)}=\inf_{g\in D(f)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}

and the scale-invariant norm

(2.9)

\|f\|_{M^{1,p}(\Sigma)}={\rm diam}(\Sigma)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}+\inf_{g\in D(f)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}.

Remark that,
for any a metric space
\Sigma
, in the case
p\in(1,\infty)
, from the uniform convexity of
L^{p}(\sigma)
, one easily deduces
that the infimum in the definition of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{M^{1,p}(\Sigma)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\Sigma)}
in (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) is attained and is unique. We denote by
\nabla_{H,p}f
the function
g
which attains the infimum.

In
[
HMT
]
, Hofmann, Mitrea, and Taylor have introduced some tangential derivatives and another tangential gradient which
are well suited for arguments involving integration by parts in chord-arc and other more general domains.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a set of finite perimeter. The HMT-derivatives we will introduce below are defined on
\partial^{*}\Omega
. To this end, consider a
C_{c}^{1}
function
{\varphi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
1\leq j,k\leq n+1
. Then, one defines the tangential derivatives of
{\varphi}
on
{\partial^{*}\Omega}
by

(2.10)

\partial_{t,j,k}{\varphi}:=\nu_{j}\,(\partial_{k}{\varphi})|_{\partial^{*}\Omega}-\nu_{k}\,(\partial_{j}{\varphi})|_{\partial\Omega},

where
\nu_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n+1
are the components of the outer unit normal
\nu
. Remark that, by integration by parts, if and
{\varphi},\psi
are
C^{1}
in a neighborhood of
{\partial\Omega}
, the arguments in
[
HMT
, p. 2676]
show that

(2.11)

\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}\partial_{t,j,k}\psi\,{\varphi}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}\psi\,\partial_{t,k,j}{\varphi}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}.

Let
\sigma_{*}:={\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial^{*}\Omega}
. For
1<p<\infty
, one defines the Sobolev type space
W^{1,p}(\sigma_{*})\equiv W^{1,p}(\partial^{*}\Omega)
(see
[
HMT
, display (3.6.3)]
) as the subspace of functions in
L^{p}(\sigma_{*})

for which there exists some constant
C(f)
such that

(2.12)

\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n+1}\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}f\,\partial_{t,k,j}{\varphi}\,d\sigma_{*}\right|\leq C(f)\,\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\sigma_{*})}

for all
{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
. By the Riesz representation theorem, for
each
f\in W^{1,p}(\partial^{*}\Omega)
and each
j,k=1,\ldots,n+1
, there exists some function
h_{j,k}\in L^{p}(\sigma_{*})
such that

\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}h_{j,k}\,{\varphi}\,d\sigma_{*}=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}f\,\partial_{t,k,j}{\varphi}\,d\sigma_{*}

and we set

\partial_{t,j,k}f:=h_{j,k}
, so that this is coherent with (
2.11
). It is easy to check that Lipschitz functions with compact support are contained in
W^{1,p}(\partial^{*}\Omega)
.

If
f:{\partial\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is Lipschitz on
{\partial\Omega}
, it is shown in
[
MT
, Lemma 6.4]
that

\partial_{t,j,k}f(x)=-\nu_{j}\,(\nabla_{t}f)_{k}(x)+\nu_{k}\,(\nabla_{t}f)_{j}(x)\quad\mbox{ for ${\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial^{*}\Omega}$-a.e.\ $x\in\partial^{*}\Omega$,}

where
(\nabla_{t}f)_{k}
denotes the
k
component of the tangential gradient
\nabla_{t}f
at
x
.

The proof of the following lemma is given in Subsection
2.7
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a bounded open set with an Ahlfors regular boundary. Then, it holds that
M^{1,p}({\partial\Omega})\subset W^{1,p}(\partial^{*}\Omega)
and, for any
f\in M^{1,p}({\partial\Omega})
and
j,k\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
,

(2.13)

\|\partial_{t,j,k}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial^{*}\Omega)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})},

where the implicit constant depends on
n
and the Ahlfors regularity constants.

For
\Omega
such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}({\partial\Omega})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)
, we say that
{\partial\Omega}
supports a weak
p
-Poincaré inequality if there are constants
C
,
C^{\prime}
such that for any Lipschitz function
f:{\partial\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and any ball
B
centered in
{\partial\Omega}
, the following holds:

\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B\cap{\partial\Omega}}|f-m_{\sigma,B}f|\,d\sigma\leq C\,r(B)\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{C^{\prime}B\cap{\partial\Omega}}|\nabla_{t}f|^{p}\,d\sigma.

If this weak
p
-Poincaré inequality holds, then the space
W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)
of Hofmann, Mitrea, Taylor
coincides with the Hajłasz-Sobolev space
M^{1,p}({\partial\Omega})
(in particular, this happens when
\Omega
is a two-sided CAD). The Hajłasz-Sobolev space is specially useful when studying the regularity problem in rather general domains, as shown in
[
MT
]
.

2.6.
Lorentz spaces on Ahlfors regular sets

Here we recall some basic facts about Lorentz spaces, which will play an important role in this paper. One can find proofs
of the results described in this subsection in
[
BS
, Chapter 4]
.

Let
\mu
be an
n
-Ahlfors regular finite measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
(for the purposes of this paper, we may think that
\mu={\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega}
, where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a bounded CAD).
For
X=\operatorname{supp}\mu
and a
\mu
-measurable function
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote by
d_{f}
its distribution function, given by

d_{f}(\lambda)=\mu\big(\big\{x\in X:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big),\quad\mbox{ for $\lambda\geq 0$.}

The decreasing rearrangement of
f
is the function
f^{*}:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{\lambda\geq 0:d_{f}(\lambda)\leq t\},\quad\mbox{ for $t\geq 0$.}

For
0<p,q\leq\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\mu)
consists of the
\mu
-measurable functions
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that the quantity

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t))^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{q}&\quad\mbox{for $1\leq q<\infty$,}\\
&\\
\sup_{t>0}(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t))&\quad\mbox{for $q=\infty$,}\end{array}\right.

is finite.

Remark the space
L^{p,p}(\mu)
coincides with the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mu)
, while
L^{p,\infty}(\mu)
coincides with the usual space weak-
L^{p}(\mu)
.
From the definition above, it easily follows that for
0<p\leq\infty
and
0<q\leq r\leq\infty
,

(2.14)

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mu)}\lesssim_{p,q,r}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}.

The spaces
L^{p,q}(\mu)
are quasi-Banach spaces (assuming the functions in the spaces to be defined modulo sets of zero measure, as usual), and

\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}
is a quasinorm. Further, for
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, the space
L^{p,q}(\mu)
is normable. That is,
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}
is comparable to a norm.
Additionally, for
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
, the dual of
L^{p,q}(\mu)
can identified with
L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mu)
, with equivalence of norms.
More generally, for
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

(2.15)

\|f\|_{L^{p.q}(\mu)}\approx_{p,q}\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mu)}\leq 1}\int|f\,g|\,d\mu.

In this paper we will make use of the spaces
L^{p,1}(\mu)
,
L^{p,p}(\mu)=L^{p}(\mu)
, and
L^{p,\infty}(\mu)
, with
1<p<\infty
.
Clearly, from (
2.14
) it follows that

L^{p,1}(\mu)\subset L^{p}(\mu)\subset L^{p,\infty}(\mu).

Recall that, by Kolmogorov’s inequality, if
1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty
,

\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mu)}\lesssim_{p_{1},p_{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{2},\infty}(\mu)}\,\mu(X)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}}}.

Then, from (
2.15
), it follows easily that, for
1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}
,

(2.16)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{1},1}(\mu)}\lesssim_{p_{1},p_{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mu)}\,\mu(X)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}}}.

We also recall that (a special case of) the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem asserts that, for

1\leq p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
,
if
T
is a quasilinear operator bounded from
L^{p_{i},1}(\mu)
to
L^{p_{i},\infty}(\mu)
for
i=0,1
,
then
T
is also bounded in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
for all
p_{0}<p<p_{1}
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

We denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(X)
the space of Lipschitz functions on
X
. We also define

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}}_{0}(X):=\{f\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(X):\int_{X}f\,d\mu=0\}\qquad L^{p,q}_{0}(X):=\{f\in L^{p,q}(X):\int_{X}f\,d\mu=0\}

Note that the space
(X,\mu)
is a strongly resonant space; see
[
BS
, p.45, Definition 2.3]
and
[
BS
, p.49, Theorem 2.6]
. Therefore, by
[
BS
, p. 23, Corollary 4.3]
and
[
BS
, p. 221, Corollary 4.8]
, we have that
L^{p,q}(\mu)
has absolutely continuous norm.
In fact, for every
f\in L^{p,q}(\mu)
, with
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in[1,\infty)
, and every

{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(f,{\varepsilon})>0
such that

(2.17)

\textup{if}\,\,E\subset X\,\,\textup{with}\,\,\mu(E)<\delta,\,\,\textup{then}\,\,\|f\,\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}<{\varepsilon}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mu
be an Ahlfors
n
-Ahlfors regular measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
X=\operatorname{supp}\mu
.
For
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q<\infty
,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(X)
is dense in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
. Moreover,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}_{0}(X)
is dense in
L_{0}^{p,q}(\mu)
for
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,p]
.

We remark that this lemma holds for more general measures
\mu
. However, we only need this for the
n
-Ahlfors regular ones.

Proof.

We first record that, by
[
CC
, Theorem 2.20]
, the set of simple functions is dense in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
. We will then show that for every Borel set
E\subset X
with
\mu(E)<\infty
, the function
\chi_{E}
can be approximated by continuous functions in the
L^{p,q}(\mu)
norm, which would imply the same result for any simple function. To this end, since
\mu
is a Radon measure, for fixed
\delta>0
to be chosen momentarily, there exist a compact set
K
and an open set
U
such that
K\subset E\subset U
and
\mu(U\setminus K)<\delta
. By Urysohn’s lemma, there exists
f\in C(X)
such that
\chi_{K}\leq f\leq\chi_{U}
. Therefore, for fixed
{\varepsilon}>0
, if
\delta=\delta({\varepsilon})>0
is the one for which (
2.17
) holds, we have that

\|f-\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}\leq\|\chi_{U}-\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}=\|\chi_{U\setminus E}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}\leq\|\chi_{U\setminus K}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}<{\varepsilon}.

We have proved that any
L^{p,q}(\mu)
function can be approximated by continuous functions. Since any continuous function on a compact set
X
can be approximated by Lipschitz functions on
X
in the uniform norm, we readily infer that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(X)
is dense in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
.

Let now
f\in L_{0}^{p,q}(\mu)
. By the first part of the lemma, there exists
f_{k}\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(X)
is such that
f_{k}\to f
in the
L^{p,q}(\mu)
norm. Since
\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}
, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,p]
, we get that
\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}\to f
in
L^{1}(\mu)
and so
\fint_{X}f_{k}\,d\mu\to\fint_{X}f\,d\mu=0
as
k\to\infty
. Thus, if we set
g_{k}:=f_{k}-\fint_{X}f_{k}\,d\mu
, it is easy to see that
g_{k}\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}_{0}(X)
and
g_{k}\to f
in the
L^{p,q}(\mu)
-norm.
∎

2.7.
Approximations of the identity on Ahlfors regular sets

Let
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth radial function such that
\chi_{B(0,1/2)}\leq\phi\leq\chi_{B(0,1)}
and, for
\rho>0
, denote

\phi_{\rho}(x)=\phi(\rho^{-1}x)
.

Let
\sigma
be an
n
-dimensional Ahlfors regular measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
\Sigma:=\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)
. For a function
g\in L^{1}_{loc}(\sigma)
,
\rho\in(0,{\rm diam}\Sigma)
, and
x\in\ \Sigma
, we denote

{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}g(x)=\frac{\phi_{\rho}*(g\sigma)(x)}{\phi_{\rho}*\sigma(x)}.

We denote by
{\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(x,y)
the kernel of
{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}
with respect to
\sigma
. That is,

{\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\phi_{\rho}*\sigma(x)}\,\phi_{\rho}(x-y)\quad\mbox{ for $x,y\in\Sigma$},

so that
{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}g(x)=\int{\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(x,y)\,g(y)\,d\sigma(y)
. It is easy to prove that,
{\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(\cdot,y)\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(\Sigma)
with
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}({\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(\cdot,y))\lesssim\rho^{-n-1}\|\sigma\|
, uniformly in
y\in\Sigma
.

Let
{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}^{*}
the dual operator of
{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}
. That is,

{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}^{*}g(x)=\int{\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(y,x)\,g(y)\,d\sigma(y).

Notice that
{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}^{*}1\approx 1
, but
{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}^{*}1\not\equiv 1
, in general.
To solve this drawback, let
W_{\rho}
be the operator of multiplication by
1/{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}^{*}1
. Then we consider the operator

S_{\rho}={\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}\,W_{\rho}\,{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}^{*},

Notice that
S_{\rho}
is self-adjoint. Moreover

S_{\rho}1=S_{\rho}^{*}1\equiv 1
.
On the other hand, the kernel of
S_{\rho}
is the following:

s_{\rho}(x,y)=\int{\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(x,z)\,\frac{1}{{\widetilde{S}}_{\rho}^{*}1(z)}\,{\widetilde{s}}_{\rho}(y,z)\,d\sigma(z).

We remark that the above construction of the operators
S_{\rho}
appeared first in the proof of David, Journé, and Semmes
of the
Tb
theorem in homogeneous spaces
[
DJS
]
. In this work the authors attribute the construction of these operators to Coifman.

The proof of the following lemma is standard and we will omit it.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\sigma
be an
n
-dimensional Ahlfors regular measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
\Sigma:=\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)
. For
\rho\in(0,{\rm diam}(\Sigma))
, let
S_{\rho}
be defined as above.
The following holds:

(a)

For every
x\in\Sigma
, the kernel
s_{\rho}(x,\cdot)
is supported in
\bar{B}(x,2\rho)
and it holds

s_{\rho}(x,\cdot)\gtrsim\chi_{B(x,\frac{1}{4}\rho)}
.

(b)

For every
x\in\Sigma
, the kernels
s_{\rho}(x,\cdot)
and
s_{\rho}(\cdot,x)
are Lipschitz on
\Sigma
, with Lipschitz constants
{\rm Lip}(s_{\rho}(x,\cdot))+{\rm Lip}(s_{\rho}(\cdot,x))\lesssim\rho^{-n-1}.

(c)

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
S_{\rho}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\sigma)
with norm at most
1
.

(d)

For
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in L^{p}(\sigma)
, then
S_{\rho}(f)
converges to
f
in
L^{p}(\sigma)
as
\rho\to 0
.

(e)

If
f\in L^{1}(\sigma)
, then
S_{\rho}(f)\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(\Sigma)
and
{\rm Lip}(S_{\rho}g)\lesssim\rho^{-n-1}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\sigma
be an
n
-dimensional Ahlfors regular measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
\Sigma:=\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)
. For
\rho\in(0,{\rm diam}(\Sigma))
, let
S_{\rho}
be defined as above. The following hold:

(a)

If
g\in\dot{M}^{1,p}(\sigma)
, then
S_{\rho}g\in\dot{M}^{1,p}(\sigma)
satisfying

\|\nabla_{H}S_{\rho}g\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}g\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}.

(b)

If
g\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(\Sigma)
, then
S_{\rho}g\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(\Sigma)
satisfying

{\rm Lip}(S_{\rho}g)\lesssim{\rm Lip}(g).

The implicit constants depend only on
n
and the Ahlfors regularity constants of
\sigma
.

Proof.

Fix
\rho\in(0,{\rm diam}(\Sigma))
.
Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\Sigma
and suppose first that
|x-x^{\prime}|\geq\rho
.
Then we write

|S_{\rho}g(x)-S_{\rho}g(x^{\prime})|\leq|S_{\rho}g(x)-g(x)|+|g(x)-g(x^{\prime})|+|g(x^{\prime})-S_{\rho}g(x^{\prime})|.

Since
S_{\rho}1=1
and
\operatorname{supp}(s_{\rho}(x,\cdot))\subset\bar{B}(x,2\rho)
,
we have

\displaystyle|S_{\rho}g(x)-g(x)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\partial\Omega}s_{\rho}(x,y)(g(y)-g(x)))\,d\sigma(y)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho^{-n}\int_{B(x,2\rho)}|x-y|\,\left(\nabla_{H}g(y)+\nabla_{H}g(x)\right)\,d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho\,\left({\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)(x)+\nabla_{H}g(x)\right),

where
{\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function with respect to
\sigma
. The same estimate holds with
x^{\prime}
in place of
x
. Thus,

\displaystyle|S_{\rho}g(x)

\displaystyle-S_{\rho}g(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left({\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)(x)+\nabla_{H}g(x)+{\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)(x^{\prime})+\nabla_{H}g(x^{\prime})\right)\,\rho+|g(x)-g(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left({\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)(x)+\nabla_{H}g(x)+{\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)(x^{\prime})+\nabla_{H}g(x^{\prime})\right)\,|x-x^{\prime}|.

In case that
|x-x^{\prime}|<\rho
, we write

\displaystyle S_{\rho}g(x)-S_{\rho}g(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}(s_{\rho}(x,y)-s_{\rho}(x^{\prime},y))\,g(y)\,d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}(s_{\rho}(x,y)-s_{\rho}(x^{\prime},y))\,(g(y)-g(x))\,d\sigma(y).

Notice that the support of the integrand is contained in

\operatorname{supp}(s_{\rho}(x,\cdot))\cup\operatorname{supp}(s_{\rho}(x^{\prime},\cdot))\subset\bar{B}(x,2\rho)\cup\bar{B}(x^{\prime},2\rho)\subset\bar{B}(x,3\rho).

Using also that
{\mathrm{L}ip}(s_{\rho}(\cdot,y))\lesssim\rho^{-n-1}
, we deduce

\displaystyle|S_{\rho}g(x)-S_{\rho}g(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\rho\,\int_{{\partial\Omega}\cap\bar{B}(x,3\rho)}|s_{\rho}(x,y)-s_{\rho}(x^{\prime},y)|\,\,\left(\nabla_{H}g(y)+\nabla_{H}g(x)\right)\,d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho\,\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\rho^{n+1}}\int_{{\partial\Omega}\cap\bar{B}(x,3\rho)}\left(\nabla_{H}g(y)+\nabla_{H}g(x)\right)\,\,d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x^{\prime}|\,\left({\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)(x)+\nabla_{H}g(x)\right).

Hence,
C({\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)+\nabla_{H}g)
is an upper gradient of
g
with
L^{p}(\sigma)
norm bounded above by
C^{\prime}\|\nabla_{H}g\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}
.
This completes the proof of (a).

To prove (b), one readily checks that in the case that
g\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(\Sigma)
, the above estimates
hold replacing
\nabla_{H}g
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(\nabla_{H}g)
by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}(g)
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
2.2
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

If
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
, then Lemma
2.2
follows from
[
MT
, eq. (4.15)]
and
[
MT
, Lemma 6.3]
, where the implicit constants are independent of the Lipschitz constant of
f
. Let us now assume that
f\in M^{1,p}({\partial\Omega})
and let
{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
. By Lemma
2.4
(d) and (e), Hölder’s inequality, (
2.13
) for Lipschitz functions, and Lemma
2.5
(a), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}f\,\partial_{t,k,j}{\varphi}\,d\sigma_{*}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}S_{\rho}f\,\partial_{t,k,j}{\varphi}\,d\sigma_{*}=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}\partial_{t,j,k}S_{\rho}f\,{\varphi}\,d\sigma_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\rho\to 0}\|\partial_{t,j,k}S_{\rho}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial^{*}\Omega)}\,\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial^{*}\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\rho\to 0}\|\nabla_{H}S_{\rho}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\,\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial^{*}\Omega)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\,\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial^{*}\Omega)},

which, by the definition of
W^{1,p}(\partial^{*}\Omega)
, concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.8.
The variational Neumann problem in bounded uniform domains with Ahlfors regular boundaries

Let us first introduce the function spaces in the interior of the domain and the boundary which are necessary in order to solve the variational Neumann problem.
We consider the Sobolev space

W^{1,p}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\},\quad p\in[1,\infty],

with norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.

We say that a domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a
p
-Sobolev extension domain
if there exists a linear and bounded (
extension
) operator
\textup{Ext}_{\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
and a constant
{\widetilde{C}}_{E}>0
such that

(2.18)

\|\textup{Ext}_{\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}(u)\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}\leq{\widetilde{C}}_{E}\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

By the work of Jones
[
Jo
]
, we know that any uniform domain is a
p
-Sobolev extension domain for every
p\in[1,\infty]
.

If
\mu
is an Ahlfors
d
-regular measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with
F:=\operatorname{supp}\mu
, then, for
s\in(0,1)
, we define the fractional Sobolev space

H^{s}(F):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(F):\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(F)}:=\int_{F}\,\,\int_{F}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)<\infty.\right\}

equipped with norm
\|f\|_{H^{s}(F)}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}(F)}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(F)}
, which is a Banach space. Thus, we define via duality

H^{-s}(F)=(H^{s}(F))^{*}.

We also define by
H_{0}^{s}(F)
the subspace of
H^{s}(F)
of those functions satisfying
\int_{F}f\,d\mu=0
.

Let
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a
d
-Ahlfors regular closed set, with
s\in(0,n+1]
. Then, by
[
JW
, Theorem 1, p.182]
, it holds that there exists a linear and bounded (
trace
) operator

\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to F}:W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\to H^{\beta}(F)

such that, for every
u\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
,

\|\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to F}(u)\|_{H^{\beta}(F)}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}.

where
\beta=1-\frac{n+1-d}{2}>0
.

Notice that if
{\partial\Omega}
which is
n
-Ahlfors regular, then we may take
F={\partial\Omega}
and
\mu:={\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega}
, and get that
\beta=1/2
and
\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\partial\Omega}}:W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\to H^{1/2}({\partial\Omega})
. If
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a corkscrew domain, then we may take
F=\overline{\Omega}
and equip it with the Lebesgue measure
m|_{\Omega}
, which is
(n+1)
-Ahlfors regular (by the corkscrew condition). In that case, we have
d=n+1
and so
\beta=1
. Therefore, there exists a linear and bounded trace operator
\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to\overline{\Omega}}:W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\to H^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. Note that since
m|_{\Omega}(\partial\Omega)=0
, we have that
H^{1}(\overline{\Omega})=H^{1}(\Omega)\equiv W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Consequently, if
\Omega
is a uniform domain with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary, we can define
\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to\Omega}:W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\to W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

Assuming still
{\partial\Omega}
to be
n
-Ahlfors regular, by
[
JW
, Theorem 3, p.155]
, there exists a linear and bounded (
extension
) operator
\textup{Ext}_{{\partial\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}:H^{s}({\partial\Omega})\to W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
such that, for every
f\in H^{1/2}({\partial\Omega})
,

\|\textup{Ext}_{{\partial\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}(f)\|_{W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\Omega})}.

Combining all the results above one can show the following:

Theorem 2.6
.

If
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a uniform domain with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary, there exists a bounded linear trace operator
\textup{Tr}_{\Omega\to{\partial\Omega}}:W^{1,2}(\Omega)\to H^{1/2}({\partial\Omega})
and a bounded linear extension operator
\textup{Ext}_{{\partial\Omega}\to\Omega}:H^{1/2}({\partial\Omega})\to W^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|\textup{Tr}_{\Omega\to{\partial\Omega}}(u)\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\,\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}

(2.20)

\displaystyle\|\textup{Ext}_{{\partial\Omega}\to\Omega}(f)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{E}\,\|f\|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\Omega})}.

Moreover,
\textup{Ext}_{{\partial\Omega}\to\Omega}\circ\textup{Tr}_{\Omega\to{\partial\Omega}}=\textup{Id}
, the identity on
H^{1/2}({\partial\Omega})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\rho\in(0,{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega}))
, and let
\Omega
be a bounded chord-arc domain.
Suppose that the Dirichlet problem for
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}
is solvable in
L^{p^{\prime}}
and in
L^{p^{\prime},\infty}
.
Then
(N_{p}(\rho))_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable if and only if
(N^{R}_{p^{\prime}}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}}
is solvable. Also,

(N_{L^{p,1},L^{p}}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}}
is solvable if and only if
(N^{R}_{L^{p^{\prime}},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}}
is solvable.
Further,

C_{\mathcal{L}}(N_{p}(\rho))\approx C_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}}(N^{R}_{p^{\prime}}(\rho))\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{\mathcal{L}}(N_{L^{p,1},L^{p}}(\rho))\approx C_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}}(N^{R}_{L^{p^{\prime}},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}(\rho)).

Proof.

The arguments are very similar to the ones for Proposition
3.1
. However, we show the details for completeness.
We will only prove the second statement since the first one is similar.

Suppose that
(N_{L^{p,1},L^{p}}(\rho))_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable with constant
C_{4}
. We will check that
(N^{R}_{L^{p^{\prime}},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}}
is solvable with constant
\lesssim C_{4}
as well.
Let
j,k,u,g,\rho
be as in (
3.8
) with
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}
and
A^{T}
in place of
{\mathcal{L}}
and
A
and consider an arbitrary function
{\varphi}\in L^{p,1}({\partial\Omega})\cap\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
.
Let
v:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the solution of the Neumann problem for
{\mathcal{L}}
with boundary data
{\varphi}-m_{\sigma,{\partial\Omega}}{\varphi}
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,{\varphi}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,({\varphi}-m_{\sigma,{\partial\Omega}}{\varphi})\,d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,\partial_{\nu_{A}}v\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu_{A^{T}}}u\,v\,d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{t,j,k}S_{\rho}(g)\,v\,d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,S_{\rho}(\partial_{t,k,j}v)\,d\sigma.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,{\varphi}\,d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\partial\Omega})}\,\|S_{\rho}(\partial_{t,k,j}v)\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\partial\Omega})}\,\|\partial_{\nu_{A}}v\|_{L^{p,1}({\partial\Omega})}=C_{4}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\partial\Omega})}\,\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{p,1}({\partial\Omega})},

where
C_{4}
is the constant of
(N_{L^{p,1},L^{p}})
. By density and duality, we deduce that

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim C_{4}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\partial\Omega})}.

Using that the Dirichlet problem is solvable from
L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\partial\Omega})
to
L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\partial\Omega})
, we infer that

\|{\mathcal{N}}_{\Omega}(u)\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim C_{4}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\partial\Omega})}.

That is,
(N^{R}_{L^{p^{\prime}},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}}
is solvable with constant
\lesssim C_{4}
.

Suppose now that
(N^{R}_{L^{p^{\prime}},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}}
is solvable with constant
C_{*}
and let us prove that then
(N_{L^{p,1},L^{p}}(\rho))_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable with constant
\lesssim C_{*}
.
Let
u
be solution of
(N_{L^{p,1},L^{p}}(\rho))_{\mathcal{L}}
with boundary data
\partial_{\nu_{A}}u=f
, for some
f\in L^{p,1}({\partial\Omega})\cap\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}_{0}({\partial\Omega})
. We aim to show that for all
1\leq j,k\leq n+1
,

(3.13)

\|S_{\rho}(\partial_{t,j,k}u)\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim C_{*}\,\|f\|_{L^{p,1}({\partial\Omega})}.

To this end, consider an arbitrary Lipschitz function
\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}({\partial\Omega})
and let
w:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the solution of the
rough Neumann problem for
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}
with
\partial_{\nu_{A^{T}}}w=\partial_{t,k,j}S_{\rho}(\psi)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{\rho}(\partial_{t,j,k}u)\,\psi\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,\partial_{t,k,j}S_{\rho}(\psi)\,d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,\partial_{\nu_{A^{T}}}w\,d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu_{A}}u\,w\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{\nu_{A}}u\|_{L^{p,1}(\Omega)}\,\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\leq C_{*}\,\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(\Omega)}\,\|\psi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\partial\Omega})},

which gives (
3.13
) by duality.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a bounded chord-arc domain and let
\rho\in(0,{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})]
. Then
(N_{p}^{R}(\rho))_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable for any
1<p<\infty
with constant

C_{\mathcal{L}}(N^{R}_{p}(\rho))\lesssim\frac{{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})}{\rho}.

Proof.

Let
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the variational solution of (
3.8
). Then

u(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}N(x,y)\,\partial_{t,j,k}(S_{\rho}g)(y)\,d\sigma(y).

For brevity, we write

u=N_{\sigma}(\partial_{t,j,k}(S_{\rho}g)).

From the properties of the kernel of
S_{\rho}
in Lemma
2.4
and Schur’s lemma, it follows that
\partial_{t,j,k}S_{\rho}
is an operator bounded in
L^{p}(\sigma)
with norm
\lesssim\rho^{-1}
, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. On the other hand, since

\int_{{\partial\Omega}}N(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)\lesssim\int_{{\partial\Omega}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\,d\sigma(y)\lesssim{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})

and the same estimate holds for
N(y,x)
, by Schur’s criterion again, we deduce that the operator
N_{\sigma}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\sigma)
with norm

\lesssim{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})
.
Therefore,

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}=\|N_{\sigma}(\partial_{t,j,k}(S_{\rho}g))\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\lesssim{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})\,\|\partial_{t,j,k}(S_{\rho}g))\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\lesssim\frac{{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})}{\rho}\,\|g\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a bounded chord-arc domain and suppose that
(D_{p})_{{\mathcal{L}}}
is solvable for some
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then
(N^{R}_{p})_{\mathcal{L}}
is solvable if and only if

is solvable uniformly on

Proof.

Suppose that

.
For

, let

be the variational solution of (
3.8
).
Then, by the solvability of
(N^{R}_{p})
and the

with norm

,

\|{\mathcal{N}}_{\Omega}(u_{\rho})\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|S_{\rho}g\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}.

So
(N^{R}_{p}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}}
is solvable uniformly on
0<\rho\leq{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})
.

Conversely, suppose that
(N^{R}_{p}(\rho))_{{\mathcal{L}}}
is solvable uniformly on
0<\rho\leq{\rm diam}({\partial\Omega})
.
For
g\in L^{p}({\partial\Omega})\cap\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
, let
u
and
u_{\rho}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the respective variational solutions of
(
3.5
) and (
3.8
).
We claim that
u
and
u_{\rho}
are continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
and

u_{\rho}
converges to
u
in
L^{p}(\sigma)
as
\rho\to 0
. Together with the

solvability of the Dirichlet problem, this implies that

\|{\mathcal{N}}_{\Omega}(u)\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\|u_{\rho}\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\leq C\,\|g\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)},

which completes the proof of the lemma, modulo our claim.

To prove the claim, observe first that, from the fact that
g
is Lipschitz, by Lemma
2.5
, it follows that

S_{\rho}g
is also Lipschitz uniformly on
\rho
. Hence,
\partial_{t,j,k}g
and
\partial_{t,j,k}S_{\rho}g

L^{\infty}(\sigma)
uniformly in
\rho

3.3
, it follows that

N_{\sigma}
is bounded in

(with norm depending on

(vi) and the local integrability implied by (v) in the same theorem, using standard arguments, it follows that

u

u_{\rho}

and

{\partial\Omega}

First we check the weak convergence of

Theorem 4.1

(Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a uniform domain and let
B:=B(x_{0},R)
be a ball of radius
R>0
centered at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. There exists a constant
C>2
, depending only on the uniformity constants of
\Omega

1<p<n+1

(4.1)

\bigg(\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.71397pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.05457pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.4484pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.27722pt}}\!\int_{B\cap\Omega}|u-u_{B}|^{p+{\varepsilon}_{p}}\,dm\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p+{\varepsilon}_{p}}}\\
\lesssim R\left(\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.71397pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.05457pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.4484pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.27722pt}}\!\int_{CB\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dm\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
u_{B}:=\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.71397pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.05457pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.4484pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.27722pt}}\!\int_{B\cap\Omega}u\,dm
and
{\varepsilon}_{p}
is some positive constant depending only on

and
n
.

Proof.

Since (
4.1
) is scale invariant, we may assume that
R=1
. If
T_{\Delta}\subset\Omega
is the Carleson box associated with the surface ball
\Delta:=B\cap\partial\Omega
(see
[
HM
, Eq. 3.59]
for its definition), then by
[
HM
, Eq. (3.60)]
, we have
\frac{5}{4}B\cap\Omega\subset T_{\Delta}\subset CB\cap\Omega
for some large constant
C>2
depending only on the uniformity constants of
\Omega
. Furthermore, according to
[

T_{\Delta}
is a uniform domain, where the uniformity constants depend only on those of
\Omega

B
. It is evident that

.

By
[

, any uniform domain is a Sobolev extension domain for the homogeneous Sobolev space

. Therefore, since

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{B\cap\Omega}|u-u_{B}|^{q}\,dm\bigg)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\bigg(\int_{CB}|{\widetilde{u}}-{\widetilde{u}}_{CB}|^{q}\,dm\bigg)^{1/q}\lesssim\bigg(\int_{CB}|\nabla{\widetilde{u}}|^{p}\,dm\bigg)^{1/p}

It is trivial to see that (

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\chi_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}\setminus\Omega}\nabla{\widetilde{{\varphi}}}_{\delta,{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}))\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}+\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\chi_{\varepsilon}\,\nabla h_{F,\delta})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\chi_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}\setminus\Omega}\nabla v_{F,\delta})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}

\displaystyle=:T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}.

We have already shown that
T_{3}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
in (
A.10
). To estimate
T_{1}
, we write, for
\xi\in{\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}))(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}\cap B(\xi,r)}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta})|\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}\,r^{n}}\int_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}\cap B(\xi,r)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon})}|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|\,dm.

Denote by
{\mathcal{W}}^{({\varepsilon})}({\widetilde{\Omega}})
the family
family of cubes from
{\mathcal{W}}({\widetilde{\Omega}})
with side length comparable to
{\varepsilon}
such that

\operatorname{supp}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon})
is contained in the union of cubes from this family.
Also, for a function
u:{\widetilde{\Omega}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
\zeta\in{\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}}
, and
t>0
, let

{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},t}u(\zeta)=\sup_{x\in\gamma_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\zeta)\cap B(\zeta,t)}\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B(x,\delta_{\Omega}(x)/2)}|u|\,dm,

for a cone
\gamma_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\xi)
with big enough aperture.
Then, for
0<r\leq{\rm diam}({\widetilde{\Omega}})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}\,r^{n}}\int_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}\cap B(\xi,r)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon})}|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|\,dm

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}\,r^{n}}\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{W}}^{({\varepsilon})}({\widetilde{\Omega}})}\int_{Q\cap B(\xi,r)}|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,Q,{\varepsilon}}}\inf_{\zeta\in P}{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|)(\zeta)\,\ell(P)^{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{B(\xi,Cr)\cap{\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}}}{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|)\,d{\widetilde{\sigma}},

where we denoted by
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,Q,{\varepsilon}}
the family of cubes of the form
P=b(Q)
for some
Q\in{\mathcal{W}}^{({\varepsilon})}({\widetilde{\Omega}})
with
Q\cap B(\xi,r)\neq\varnothing
(notice that the latter condition implies that
r\gtrsim{\varepsilon}
). So we get

{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}))(\xi)\lesssim{\mathcal{M}}_{{\widetilde{\sigma}}}({\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|))(\xi)\quad\mbox{ for all $\xi\in{\partial\Omega}$},

and therefore

\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}))\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}\lesssim\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}.

Notice now that
\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}<\infty
, because

h_{F,\delta}
,
v_{F,\delta}
, and thus
{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|)
, are uniformly bounded.
Also, for any fixed
\delta>0
we have

{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|)(\xi)\leq{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-{\varphi}_{F,\delta}(\xi)|)(\xi)+{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|v_{F,\delta}-{\varphi}_{F,\delta}(\xi)|)(\xi)\to 0

as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
for
{\widetilde{\sigma}}
-a.e.
\xi\in{\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}}
, because of (
A.9
) (which also holds with

h_{F,\delta}
in place of
v_{F,\delta}
).
Thus, by dominated convergence,
\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}},C{\varepsilon}}(|h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}|)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}\to 0
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, and so we can pick
{\varepsilon}
small enough (depending on
\delta
) such that

\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{F,\delta}-v_{F,\delta}))\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}\leq\delta.

It only remains to show that the term
T_{2}=\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\chi_{\varepsilon}\nabla h_{F,\delta})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}
in (
A.11
) goes to
0
as
\delta\to 0
. Since
h_{F,\delta}
is Lipschitz (with constant depending on
\delta
), for any
\xi\in{\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}}
and any
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\chi_{\varepsilon}\nabla h_{F,\delta})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}\cap B(\xi,r)}|\chi_{\varepsilon}\,\nabla h_{F,\delta}|\,dm

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)\,\sup_{r>0}\frac{m(U_{C{\varepsilon}}({\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}})\cap B(\xi,r))}{r^{n}}\lesssim C(\delta)\,{\varepsilon}.

Therefore,
T_{2}\leq C(\delta)\,{\varepsilon}\,{\widetilde{\sigma}}({\partial{\widetilde{\Omega}}})^{1/q^{\prime}}
and thus we can choose
{\varepsilon}
small enough so that
T_{2}\leq\delta
.
Altogether, we deduce that

\|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}}(\chi_{{\widetilde{\Omega}}\setminus\Omega}\nabla{\widetilde{{\varphi}}}_{\delta,{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\widetilde{\sigma}})}\leq T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}\to 0

as
\delta\to 0
, which completes the proof of Claim
A.2
.
∎

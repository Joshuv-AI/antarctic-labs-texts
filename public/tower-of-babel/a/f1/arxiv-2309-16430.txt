Adiabatic connection interaction strength interpolation method made accurate for the uniform electron gas
Lucian A. Constantin, Subrata Jana, Szymon Śmiga, Fabio Della Sala

Abstract
The adiabatic connection interaction strength interpolation (ISI)-like method provides a high-level expression for the correlation energy, being in principle exact in the weak-interaction limit, where it recovers the second-order Görling-Levy perturbation term, but also in the strong-interaction limit that is described by the strictly correlated electron approach. In this work, we construct the genISI functional made accurate for the uniform electron gas, a solid-state physics paradigm that is a very difficult test for ISI-like correlation functionals. We assess the genISI functional for various jellium spheres with the number of electrons Z ≤ \leq 912 and for the non-relativistic noble atoms with Z ≤ \leq 290. For the jellium clusters, the genISI is remarkably accurate, while for the noble atoms, it shows a good performance, similar to other ISI-like methods. Then, the genISI functional can open the path using the ISI-like method in solid-state calculations.

Introduction

The Density Functional Theory (DFT)
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
, is exact in
principle, but in practice, the
exchange-correlation (XC) energy
E_{xc}[n(\mathbf{r})]
as a functional of the electronic density
n(\mathbf{r})

must be approximated. The XC functional should contain all the many-body quantum effects raised
by the electron-electron interactions beyond
the Hartree method. Nowadays, there are known important exact properties of

E_{xc}[n]
that have been used in the construction of many XC functional approximations, that are
classified on the so-called Jacob’s ladder
Perdew and Schmidt 2001
;
Perdew et al. 2005
.
The first rung of the ladder is the Local Density Approximation (LDA)

Kohn and Sham 1965
;
Dirac 1930
;
Perdew and Wang 1992
which has been constructed from the uniform
electron gas (UEG) model system
Loos and Gill 2016
. The UEG is one of the most important model systems for the XC
functional development, being a solid-state paradigm. In fact, LDA is still often used in solid-state
calculations because of its remarkable accuracy for various properties,
such as surface energy of transition metals
Patra et al. 2020
and work function

Patra et al. 2017
;
Singh-Miller and Marzari 2009
.

One of the most rigorous paths for constructing new XC functionals is via the
adiabatic connection (AC) method
Langreth and Perdew 1975
;
Gunnarsson and Lundqvist 1976
;
Savin et al. 2003
;
Cohen et al. 2007
;
Ernzerhof 1996
;
Burke et al. 1997
;
Colonna and Savin 1999
, that
gives the exact XC energy as the following coupling-constant integral

E_{xc}[n]=\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\;W_{xc,\alpha}[n],

(1)

where

W_{xc,\alpha}[n]=\langle\Psi_{n}^{min,\alpha}|\hat{V}_{ee}|\Psi_{n}^{min,\alpha}]\rangle-U[n].

(2)

Here
U[n]=(1/2)\int d\mathbf{r}\int d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\;n(\mathbf{r})n(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})/|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|
is the Hartree energy,
\hat{V}_{ee}
is the
Coulomb repulsion operator, and

\Psi_{n}^{min,\alpha}
is the antisymmetric wave function that yields the density
n(\mathbf{r})
and
minimizes the expectation value
\langle\hat{T}+\alpha\hat{V}_{ee}\rangle
,
with
\hat{T}
being the kinetic energy operator, and
\alpha\geq 0
the coupling constant (known also
as interaction strength). We note that
\Psi_{n}^{min,\alpha=1}=\Psi_{n}^{min}
is the
interacting, exact ground-state wavefunction for density
n(\mathbf{r})
and
\Psi_{n}^{min,\alpha=0}=\Phi_{n}^{min}

is the non-interacting KS wavefunction for density
n(\mathbf{r})
given in the form of Slater determinant.
Considering that the exchange energy functional is, by definition,

E_{x}[n(\mathbf{r})]=\langle\Phi_{n}^{min}(\mathbf{r})|\hat{V}_{ee}|\Phi_{n}^{min}(\mathbf{r})\rangle-U[n(\mathbf{r})],

then the adiabatic connection integrand
W_{xc,\alpha}[n]
can be also written as

\displaystyle W_{xc,\alpha}[n]=E_{x}[n]+W_{c,\alpha}[n],

\displaystyle W_{c,\alpha}[n]=\langle\Psi_{n}^{min,\alpha}|\hat{V}_{ee}|\Psi_{n}^{min,\alpha}\rangle-\langle\Phi_{n}^{min}|\hat{V}_{ee}|\Phi_{n}^{min}\rangle.

(3)

The AC was often used in the construction of accurate hybrid
functionals

;
Kohn et al. 1996
;
Becke 1993
;
Adamo and Barone 1998
;
Śmiga and Constantin 2020a
, and
especially
the most sophisticated, fifth-rung functionals, including the

;
Görling and Levy 1993
;
Görling and Levy 1995

;

;

In the weak-interaction limit, the GL perturbation theory becomes exact, and

W_{xc,\alpha}[n]
is
Seidl et al. 2000a
;
Görling and Levy 1993
;
Jana et al. 2020

W_{xc,\alpha\rightarrow 0}[n]=W_{0}[n]+W^{\prime}_{0}[n]\alpha+...+W^{(m)}_{0}[n]\alpha^{m}+....,

(4)

where
W_{0}[n]=E_{x}[n]
is the exact DFT exchange functional,

W^{\prime}_{0}[n]=2E_{c}^{GL2}[n]
, and
W^{(m)}_{0}[n]=(m+1)E_{c}^{GL_{m+1}}[n]
. In this work, as in most of the
ISI-like methods, we consider only the first two terms of the perturbation expansion.

On the other hand, in the strong-interaction limit,
W_{xc,\alpha}[n]
behaves as

Liu and Burke 2009a

W_{xc,\alpha\rightarrow\infty}[n]=W_{\infty}[n]+W^{\prime}_{\infty}[n]\alpha^{-1/2}+W^{(2)}_{\infty}[n]\alpha^{-3/2}+...,

(5)

where
W_{\infty}[n]
,
W^{\prime}_{\infty}[n]
can be in principle exactly calculated using the strictly
correlated electron (SCE) approach
Gori-Giorgi et al. 2009b
;
Malet and Gori-Giorgi 2012
;
Friesecke et al. 2022
.
In practice, generalized gradient approximation (GGA) models
have been developed for
W_{\infty}[n]
and

W^{\prime}_{\infty}[n]
Seidl et al. 2000b
;
Constantin 2019
;
Śmiga et al. 2022
.

Eq. (
5
) shows that the
\alpha^{-1}
-term in the expansion should be zero. However,
the original ISI method
Seidl et al. 2000a
has a spurious
\alpha^{-1}
-term, that
was removed in the revISI
Gori-Giorgi et al. 2009a
and LB
Liu and Burke 2009a
methods.

The total XC energy of ISI methods can be expressed as

E_{xc}[n]={\cal F}(E_{x},E^{GL2}_{c},W_{\infty},W^{\prime}_{\infty})

(6)

where
{\cal F}
is a non-linear function of the ingredients

E_{x}
and
E^{GL2}_{c}
and of the density functionals
W_{\infty}
and

W^{\prime}_{\infty}
.
Eq.
6
thus resembles (also from a computational cost point-of-view) the one of double-hybrid (DH) functionals
Goerigk and Grimme 2014
: however, the former employs a non-linear dependence from the GL2 (or MP2) term and do not diverges for systems with vanishing gaps
Fabiano et al. 2016
;
Śmiga and Constantin 2020b
;
Franzke et al. 0
, which is a clear superiority with respect to DH approaches.

However, the ISI methods have difficulties to recover the logarithmic singularity
of the UEG correlation energy per particle in the high-density limit (
\epsilon_{c}\propto\ln(r_{s})

when
r_{s}\rightarrow 0
, where
r_{s}
is the bulk parameter), but all of them are accurate in the UEG
low-density limit (at
r_{s}\rightarrow\infty
)
Seidl et al. 2000b
;
Harding et al. 2022
. We recall that the high-density limit of the UEG correlation energy is exactly described by the RPA method
Perdew and Wang 1992
.

In spite of this limitation,
we construct an ISI-like method, named genISI, that is very accurate for the UEG correlation energy
when
r_{s}\geq 1
. Note that most materials are characterized by
1\leq r_{s}\leq 10
. Thus, one could expect increased accuracy of the proposed method.

II
the genISI XC functional

We start the construction of genISI XC functional considering the UEG limit (denoted by UEG-ISI), where
W^{\prime}_{0}\rightarrow-\infty
and
W^{\prime}_{0}/W_{0}\rightarrow\infty
. In fact, in the UEG, the energy-gap vanishes and thus the GL2 correlation diverges.

II.1
The UEG-ISI XC functional

Let us consider the revISI expression
Gori-Giorgi et al. 2009a
for the UEG-ISI XC functional

W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]=W_{\infty}[n]+\frac{b(2+c\alpha+2d\sqrt{1+c\alpha})}{2\sqrt{1+c\alpha}(d+\sqrt{1+c\alpha})^{2}},

(7)

so that the UEG-ISI XC energy is

\displaystyle E^{UEG-ISI}_{xc}[n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}d\alpha\;W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{\infty}[n]+\frac{b[n]}{d+\sqrt{1+c[n]}}

In Ref.
Gori-Giorgi et al. 2009a
the parameters b, c, d were functions of
W^{\prime}_{0}
, which tends to
-\infty
for the UEG.
Here, instead, we define:

\displaystyle b[n]=(W_{0}-W_{\infty})(1+d),

\displaystyle c[n]=b[n]^{2}/[4W^{\prime 2}_{\infty}].

(9)

The functionals
b[n]
and
c[n]
have been found such that

W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]
has the
following properties:

\displaystyle W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha\rightarrow 0}[n]\longrightarrow W_{0}-\frac{(W_{0}-W_{\infty})^{3}(1+d)}{4W^{\prime 2}_{\infty}}\alpha+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}),

\displaystyle W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha\rightarrow\infty}[n]\longrightarrow W_{\infty}+W^{\prime}_{\infty}\alpha^{-1/2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{-3/2}).

(10)

Then,
W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]
recovers the strong interaction limit of Eq. (
5
), and
the first leading term of the weak interaction limit expansion of Eq. (
4
). Of course, the
first perturbation term
W^{\prime}_{0}[n]\alpha
can not be fulfilled by
W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]
,
because in the UEG limit
W^{\prime}_{0}=-\infty
, such that
W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]
should be a
function only of
W_{0}
,
W_{\infty}
, and
W^{\prime}_{\infty}
.

We also mention that, under the uniform scaling of the density
n_{\lambda}(\mathbf{r})=\lambda^{3}n(\lambda\mathbf{r})
,
with
\lambda\geq 0
(
\lambda=\alpha^{-1}
), the functionals have the following scaling behavior

Seidl et al. 2000b
;
Görling and Levy 1993
:

\displaystyle W_{\infty}[n_{\lambda}]=\lambda W_{\infty}[n],\;\;\;W_{0}[n_{\lambda}]=\lambda W_{0}[n],

\displaystyle W^{\prime}_{\infty}[n_{\lambda}]=\lambda^{3/2}W^{\prime}_{\infty}[n],\;\;\;W^{\prime}_{0}[n_{\lambda}]=W^{\prime}_{0}[n],

(11)

such that

W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}]=\alpha W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n].

(12)

Note that all ISI-like functionals in the limit of
W^{\prime}_{0}\rightarrow-\infty
, can be written as

\displaystyle E_{xc}[n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{\infty}+W^{\prime}_{\infty}F(q),

(13)

\displaystyle W_{\alpha}[n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{\infty}+(E_{x}-W_{\infty})f(x),

(14)

with

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{x}-W_{\infty}}{W_{\infty}^{\prime}}>0,

(15)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sqrt{\alpha},

(16)

and
F(q)=\int_{0}^{1}qf(\sqrt{\alpha}q)d\alpha
.
The functions
F(q)
and
f(x)
for common ISI-like functionals are reported in the Appendix A.
For the UEG-ISI we have

\displaystyle F^{UEG-ISI}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2q(d+1)}{H(q)+2d},

(17)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d+1)\frac{H(x)^{2}+4dH(x)+4}{H(2d+H(x))^{2}},

(18)

\displaystyle H(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{4+(d+1)^{2}x^{2}}.

(19)

For small
q
we have

F^{UEG-ISI}(q)\rightarrow q-\frac{q^{3}}{8}(d+1)+\mathcal{O}(q^{5}),

(20)

and thus

E_{xc}^{UEG-ISI}(q)\rightarrow E_{x}+W^{\prime}_{\infty}\left(-\frac{q^{3}}{8}(d+1)+\mathcal{O}(q^{5})\right).

(21)

For one electron systems, if
W_{\infty}
is computed exactly (which is the case for
W_{\infty}^{TPSS}
and for the point-charge-plus-continuum (PC) model
Seidl et al. 2000b
for the hydrogen atom), we have that
q=0
and thus UEG-ISI is correctly one-electron self-correlation free.
Actually for one electron systems, it should also be that
W^{\prime}_{\infty}=0
, which can only be the case for a meta-GGA functional like TPSS: in this case
q
is undefined, but still
W^{\prime}_{\infty}q^{n}
vanishes for any n, and thus the UEG-ISI correlation is zero.
For other GGA models of
W_{\infty}
and
W^{\prime}_{\infty}
see e.g. Refs.
Śmiga et al. 2022
;
Constantin 2019
the genISI correlation of one electron systems is small but not exactly zero.

The only parameter of the UEG-ISI functional is
d

and it is fixed as described in section
IV.1

II.2
The genISI XC functional

To restore the
\alpha
-term of the weak interaction limit expansion of Eq. (
4
) and
to preserve the UEG limit constructed above,
we consider the following expression for the genISI functional

\displaystyle W^{genISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W^{UEG-ISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]+

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{a[n]~p[n]~\alpha}{(1+r[n]~p[n]~\alpha)^{3}}

where

\displaystyle a[n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{0}\Big[1+q^{3}\frac{W^{\prime}_{\infty}}{4W^{\prime}_{0}}(1+d)\Big],

\displaystyle p[n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W^{\prime}_{0}/W_{0},

\displaystyle r[n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\Big(\frac{W_{0}}{W_{\infty}}\Big)^{3},

(23)

and
m=18.0
was fixed by fitting to the
correlation energy of the Hooke’s atom with force constant
k=1/4

Taut 1994
;
Constantin 2019
.
Considering the density scaling relations, we observe that

W^{genISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n_{\alpha}(\mathbf{r})]=\alpha W^{genISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n(\mathbf{r})].

(24)

The genISI XC functional satisfies all the exact
conditions also recovered by revISI
Gori-Giorgi et al. 2009a
and LB
Liu and Burke 2009a

functionals, and additionally has an improved UEG limit.

The genISI XC energy is

\displaystyle E^{genISI}_{xc}[n]=\int^{1}_{0}d\alpha\;W^{genISI}_{xc,\alpha}[n]

\displaystyle=E^{UEG-ISI}_{xc}[n]+E^{add}_{xc}[n]

(25)

\displaystyle{\rm with\;}E^{add}_{xc}[n]=\frac{a[n]p[n]}{2(r[n]p[n]+1)^{2}}.

(26)

For small
q
and small
E^{GL2}_{c}
we have

\displaystyle E_{xc}^{add}\rightarrow W^{\prime}_{\infty}(\frac{q^{3}}{8}(d+1)+\mathcal{O}(q^{5}))+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{m(d+1)W^{\prime}_{\infty}(q^{3}+\mathcal{O}(q^{4}))}{2W_{\infty}}\right)E^{GL2}_{c}+\mathcal{O}((E^{GL2}_{c})^{2}),

which cancels the second term in Eq. (
21
),
so that
E_{x}
is recovered for a larger range of
q
.
Finally the total genISI XC energy behaves as

\displaystyle E_{xc}^{genISI}\rightarrow E_{x}+W^{\prime}_{\infty}\mathcal{O}(q^{5})+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{m(d+1)W^{\prime}_{\infty}(q^{3}+\mathcal{O}(q^{4}))}{2W_{\infty}}\right)E^{GL2}_{c}+\mathcal{O}((E^{GL2}_{c})^{2}).

For one electron systems
E^{GL2}_{c}=0
and thus genISI is one-electron self-correlation free, as discussed in section
II.2
.

Finally, we recall that the genISI total energy is not size-consistent (as all other interpolation formula), but a simple size-consistent correction can be added to it

Vuckovic et al. 2018
.

The XC energy

V
Conclusions

We have constructed the UEG-ISI XC functional, which depends on

W_{0}[n]
,
W_{\infty}[n]
and
W^{\prime}_{\infty}[n]
, being remarkably accurate for the UEG model system.
The UEG-ISI XC functional behaves correctly in the strong-interaction limit, while in the
weak-interaction limit it recovers only the leading term
W_{0}[n]
. We have also developed the
genISI XC functional, by adding a GL2 correction to the UEG-ISI, such that the genISI fulfills
all the known exact requirements, and additionally it is accurate for the UEG, especially in the
region
1\leq r_{s}\leq 10
, that is the most relevant in solid-state applications.

The genISI and UEG-ISI have been tested for the correlation energies of 24 neutral jellium
spheres with number of electrons
8\leq Z\leq 912
.
The
W_{xc,\alpha}^{genISI}
and

W_{xc,\alpha}^{UEG-ISI}
are similar, with the exception of
\alpha\rightarrow 0
region, where

W_{xc,\alpha}^{genISI}

recovers the first two terms of the GL perturbation expansion of Eq. (
4
).
Consequently, both genISI and UEG-ISI give very good and almost similar
performances for jellium clusters, as reported in Table
1
and Fig.
3
.

We have also tested the genISI and UEG-ISI functionals for noble atoms with
2\leq Z\leq 290
,
where the ratio
p[n]
is small (
p[n]\leq 0.1
), such that genISI and UEG-ISI behave
differently. Thus while the genISI is quite accurate with a relative error below 7% (see Fig.

5
(lower panel)), the
UEG-ISI fails badly, with errors larger than 40%, as shown in Fig.
6
(lower panel). This
fact shows that the genISI correction to the UEG-ISI plays a vital role in this
functional construction.

Finally, we have also presented the results from few small systems where the ingredients
W_{0}
,
W^{\prime}_{0}
,
W^{\mathrm{SCE}}_{\infty}
, and

W^{\prime\mathrm{SCE}}_{\infty}
are known with high accuracy. The genISI shows a reasonable performance, in line with the other ISI-like methods.

Application of genISI to molecular systems is straightforward, with the methods and limitations previously discussed in Refs.
Fabiano et al. 2016
;
Vuckovic et al. 2018
;
Śmiga and Constantin 2020b
;
Śmiga et al. 2022
;
Franzke et al. 0
.
We expect that the method of genISI development can be further used for the construction of
more accurate ISI-like functionals, towards their use in solid-state and material science
calculations.

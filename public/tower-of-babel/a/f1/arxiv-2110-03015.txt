Fast and flexible preconditioners for solving multilinear systems
Eisa Khosravi Dehdezi, Saeed Karimi

Abstract
This paper investigates a type of fast and flexible preconditioners to solve multilinear system 𝒜 ​ x m − 1 = b \mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{m-1}=\textbf{b} with ℳ \mathcal{M} -tensor 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and obtains some important convergent theorems about preconditioned Jacobi, Gauss-Seidel and SOR type iterative methods. The main results theoretically prove that the preconditioners can accelerate the convergence of iterations. Numerical examples are presented to reverify the efficiency of the proposed preconditioned methods.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some definitions, notations, and related properties which will be used in the following.

Let
0
,
O
and
\mathcal{O}
denote for null vector, null matrix and null tensor, respectively. Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be a tensor (vector or matrix) with the same sign. The order
\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}(>\mathcal{B})
means that each element of
\mathcal{A}
is no less than (larger than) corresponding one of
\mathcal{B}
.

A tensor
\mathcal{A}
consists of
n_{1}\times...\times n_{m}
elements in the complex field
\mathbb{C}
:

\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}…i_{m}}),~~a_{i_{1}i_{2}…i_{m}}\in\mathbb{C},~~1\leq i_{j}\leq n_{j},j=1,...,m.

When
m=2
,
\mathcal{A}
is an
n_{1}\times n_{2}
matrix. If
n_{1}=...=n_{m}=n
,
\mathcal{A}
is called an
m
order
n
-dimensional tensor. By
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times...\times n_{m}}
we denote all
m
order tensors consist of
n_{1}\times...\times n_{m}
entries and by
\mathbb{C}^{[m,n]}
we denote the set of all
m
order
n
-dimensional tensors. When
m=1
,
\mathbb{C}^{[1,n]}
is simplified as
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which is the set of all
n
-dimension complex vectors. Similarly, the above notions can be used to the real number field
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
. If each entry of
\mathcal{A}
is nonnegative, then
\mathcal{A}
is called a nonnegative tensor. The set of all
m
order
n
-dimensional nonnegative tensors is denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{[m,n]}
. The
m
order
n
-dimensional identity tensor, denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{m}=(\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}})\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
, is the tensor with entries:

\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}1,~~i_{1}=i_{2}=...=i_{m}\\
0,~~otherwise.\end{array}\right.

When
m=2
, the identity tensor reduces to identity matrix of size
n\times n
, denoted by
I
.

Definition 1

[
19
]

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{[m,n]}
is called a reducible tensor if there exists a nonempty proper index subset
\mathbb{I}\subseteq\{1,2,...,n\}
such that

a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}}=0,~\forall i_{1}\in\mathbb{I},~\forall i_{2}...i_{m}\notin\mathbb{I},

else, we say that
\mathcal{A}
is irreducible.

Definition 2

[
31
]

A tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
is called a
\mathcal{Z}
-tensor if its off-diagonal entries are non-positive.
\mathcal{A}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-tensor if there
exists a nonnegative tensor
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{[m,n]}
and a positive real number
\eta\geq\rho(\mathcal{B})
such that
\mathcal{A}=\eta\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{B}
. If
\eta>\rho(\mathcal{B})
, then
\mathcal{A}
is called a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor.

Definition 3

[
20
]

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{[2,n]}
(
A
is an
n
-dimensional square matrix) and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
. Then a product
\mathcal{C}=A\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
is defined by

c_{ji_{2}...i_{m}}=\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{n}a_{jj_{2}}b_{j_{2}i_{2}...i_{m}},

(3)

which can be written as follows

\mathcal{C}_{(1)}=(A\mathcal{B})_{(1)}=A\mathcal{B}_{(1)},

where
\mathcal{C}_{(1)}
and
\mathcal{B}_{(1)}
are the matrices obtained from
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{B}
flattened along the first index, respectively.

Definition 4

[
25
]

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
. The majorization matrix of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted by
M(\mathcal{A})
, is defined as a square matrix of size
n\times n
with its entries

M(\mathcal{A})_{ij}=a_{ij...j},~~i,j=1,2,...,n.

If

is a nonsingular matrix and

\mathcal{A}=M(\mathcal{A})\mathcal{I}_{m}
, then

is the order 2 left-inverse of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
M(\mathcal{A})^{-1}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}_{m}
, and then we call
\mathcal{A}
a left-invertible tensor or left-nonsingular tensor.

Definition 5

[
26
]

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
. A pair
(\lambda,\textbf{x})\in\mathbb{C}\times(\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
is called an eigenvalue-eigenvector(or simply eigenpair) of
\mathcal{A}
if they satisfy the equation

\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{m-1}=\lambda\textbf{x}^{[m-1]},

(4)

where
\textbf{x}^{[m-1]}=(x_{1}^{m-1},...,x_{n}^{m-1})^{T}
. We call

an H-eigenpair if both

and

Let

be the spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}
, where
\sigma(\mathcal{A})
is the set of all eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
.

If

Lemma 2

A general tensor splitting iterative method for solving (

\textbf{x}_{j+1}=[M(\mathcal{E})^{-1}\mathcal{F}\textbf{x}_{j}^{m-1}+M(\mathcal{E})^{-1}\textbf{b}]^{[\tfrac{1}{m-1}]},~j=0,1,....

3.1

\hskip 48.36958pt=\{I+(I-(D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+D_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}))^{-1}H+[(I-(D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+D_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})^{-1}H]^{2}+...+\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+[(I-(D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+D_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}))^{-1}H]^{n-1}\}(I-(D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+D_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}))^{-1}\\
\hskip 48.36958pt\geq O.\end{array}

Since

(by the same discussion in proofing how
H\geq O
),

is a weak regular splitting and using Lemma
3
, is convergent. For cases
k<s
and
k>s
, similar discussion can be used for obtain desired results.

When

and

, proof is similar to the proof of the case
\mathcal{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(s,k)=\mathcal{M}_{2}-\mathcal{N}_{2}
.

3.3
The preconditioned SOR type method

In
[
20
]
, the SOR type method for solving Eq. (
1
) is given by taking
\mathcal{E}=\tfrac{1}{\displaystyle\omega}(\mathcal{I}-\displaystyle\omega\mathcal{L})
and

\textbf{x}_{j+1}=(M(\mathcal{I}-\displaystyle\omega\mathcal{L})^{-1}((1-\displaystyle\omega)\mathcal{I}+\displaystyle\omega\mathcal{F})\textbf{x}_{j}^{m-1}+\displaystyle\omega M(\mathcal{I}-\omega\mathcal{L})^{-1}\textbf{b})^{[\tfrac{1}{m-1}]}.

In this paper, we consider the following preconditioned SOR type method:

\begin{array}[]{l}~\hskip 28.45274pt\textbf{x}_{j+1}=(\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\displaystyle\omega)\textbf{x}_{j}^{m-1}+\textbf{h}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}({\displaystyle\omega}))^{[\tfrac{1}{m-1}]},\end{array}

where

\begin{array}[]{l}~\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\displaystyle\omega)=M(\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\displaystyle\omega))^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\displaystyle\omega),~\textbf{h}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\displaystyle\omega)=M(\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\displaystyle\omega))^{-1}\textbf{b}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}(s,k),\\
~\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}({\displaystyle\omega})=\tfrac{1}{\displaystyle\omega}(\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\displaystyle\omega\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}),~\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}({\displaystyle\omega})=\tfrac{1}{\displaystyle\omega}((1-\displaystyle\omega)\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}+\displaystyle\omega\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}),\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{l}~\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}-\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\\
~\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\mathcal{L}-K_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{k}\mathcal{I}+K_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{k}\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\\
~\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\mathcal{F}-S_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{s}\mathcal{I}+S_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{s}\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}.\end{array}

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we proposed new types of flexible and fast preconditioners tensor splitting methods for solving multilinear system
\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{m-1}=\textbf{b}
, when
\mathcal{A}
is a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor. Some properties of convergent theorems about preconditioned Jacobi, Gauss-Seidel and SOR type iterative methods are obtained. Numerical examples are given to show the efficiency and superiority of the proposed methods.

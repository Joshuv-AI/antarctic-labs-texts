Kernel bounds for parabolic operators having first-order degeneracy at the boundary
L. Negro, C. Spina

Abstract
We study kernel estimates for parabolic problems governed by singular elliptic operators ∑ i , j = 1 N + 1 q i ​ j ​ D i ​ j + c ​ D y y , c + 1 > 0 , \sum_{i,j=1}^{N+1}q_{ij}D_{ij}+c\frac{D_{y}}{y},\qquad c+1>0, in the half-space ℝ + N + 1 = { ( x , y ) : x ∈ ℝ N , y > 0 } \mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}=\{(x,y):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},y>0\} under Neumann boundary conditions at y = 0 y=0 . Mathematics subject classification (2020): 35K08, 35K67, 47D07, 35J70, 35J75. Keywords: degenerate elliptic operators, boundary degeneracy, kernel estimates.



1
Introduction

In this paper we study upper estimates for the parabolic heat kernel of the degenerate operator

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N+1}q_{ij}D_{ij}+c\frac{D_{y}}{y}

(1)

in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}=\{(x,y):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},y>0\}
, under Neumann boundary conditions at
y=0
. Here
Q=\left(q_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1,\dots,N+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1,N+1}
is a constant and positive definite matrix and
c\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\frac{c}{\gamma}+1>0
where
\gamma=q_{N+1,N+1}
.

In the special case

\mathcal{L}=\Delta_{x}+B_{y},\qquad B_{y}=D_{yy}+\frac{c}{y}D_{y},

that is when the mixed second order derivatives do not appear in the operator, the one-dimensional Bessel operator
B_{y}
and the Laplacian
\Delta_{x}
commute and sharp kernel estimates can be proved after an explicit description of the kernel of the Bessel operator (see
[
10
]
). These operators play a major role in the investigation of the fractional powers
(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
and
(D_{t}-\Delta_{x})^{s}
,
s=(1-c)/2
, through the “extension procedure” of Caffarelli and Silvestre, see
[
1
]
. The strategy above based on the commutativity of
\Delta_{x}
and
B_{y}
clearly fails for the general operator (
1
) due to the presence of the mixed derivatives
D_{xy}
. On the other hand, adding this term is crucial for treating degenerate operators in domains, through a localization procedure.

For this reason we investigate, in this work, the validity of upper kernel estimate for the general operator
\mathcal{L}
defined in (
1
) by revisiting the classical method based upon the equivalence between Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities and ultracontractivity estimates.

Before describing the strategy here employed, we remark that elliptic and parabolic solvability of the associated problems in weighted
L^{p}
spaces have been already investigated in

[
14
,
12
,
10
,
13
]
where we proved that, under suitable assumptions on
m
and
p
and for
c>-1
,
\mathcal{L}
, endowed with Neumann boundary conditions, generates an analytic semigroup in
L^{p}_{m}

=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+};y^{m}dxdy)
. In addition we characterized its domain as a weighted Sobolev space and showed that it has the maximal regularity. In addition we also refer to
[
5
,
7
,
4
,
6
]
where the authors studied operators of this form, even for variable coefficients, using tools form linear PDE and Muckhenoupt weights. In
[
17
]
, among other properties, kernel estimates for some second order degenerate operators in divergence form are considered.
In our knowledge, kernel estimates for the operators in the general form (
1
) are new.

Our main results are Theorems
4.18
and
5.3
where, if
\frac{c}{\gamma}+1>0
, we prove that
\mathcal{L}
generates a contractive analytic semigroup in
L^{2}_{\frac{c}{\gamma}}
and its heat kernel
p_{{\mathcal{L}}}
, written with respect the measure
y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}dz
, satisfies the upper Gaussian estimate

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{{\mathcal{L}}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})\leq Ct^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}y_{1}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{1}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}y_{2}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\,\exp\left(-\dfrac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{kt}\right),

where
t>0
,
z_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1}),\ z_{2}=(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
; we also extend the estimate to complex times.
As consequence of our kernel estimates, we deduce, after a linear transformation, upper kernel estimates also for the operator

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N+1}q_{ij}D_{ij}+\frac{b\cdot\nabla_{x}}{y}+\frac{cD_{y}}{y},\qquad b\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad c\neq 0,

(2)

under an oblique boundary conditions at
y=0
.

We now describe the strategy of proof employed. A suitable linear change of variables, allows to deal with the equivalent degenerate operator

\mathcal{L}:=\Delta_{x}u+2a\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}u+B_{y}u,\qquad a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ |a|<1.

Note that the condition
|a|<1
is equivalent to the ellipticity of the top order coefficients.

We start by constructing, in Section
2
, using form methods, the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}
in a weighted
L^{2}
-space, where the weighted measure
y^{c}\,dx\,dy
takes into account the degeneracy of the operator. We prove continuity, accretivity and sectoriality for the form associated with
\mathcal{L}
and its adjoint.

Then, we try to use the standard machinery, relying on Gagliardo-Nirenberg
type inequalities, to deduce the ultracontractivity of the semigroup. To this aim, we investigate in Section
3
the validity of some anisotropic weighted Sobolev inequalities. Some difficulties appear since the semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
is not ultracontractive when
c<0
: this is due to the fact that the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities are valid only locally due to the singularity of the operator at the boundary
y=0
.

To overcome this obstruction, we introduce, in Section
4
, a further weight
\phi

which allow us to take into account the degeneracy of the operator at the boundary. This introduction is essential for
c<0
since allows to prove Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities in the whole half-space for any
c>-1
. When
c\geq 0
, this approach, although not mandatory, is needed in order to deduce precise heat kernel estimates (see Remark
4.16
). Another obstruction to the application of the standard machinery is the non-symmetry of the operator
\mathcal{L}
: this force to study at the same time also the adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
, proving
L^{2}-L^{\infty}
estimate for
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
and
e^{t\mathcal{L}^{\ast}}
. Then we implement Davies’ trick to add the Gaussian factor. Moreover we extend the estimates to complex times. We do not prove lower bounds; however, in the case
\Delta_{x}+B_{y}
, our upper bounds coincide with those of
[
10
]
which are optimal.

Finally in Section
5
we deduce upper kernel estimates for the operator in (
2
) having oblique boundary condition.

The operator
\mathcal{L}=\Delta_{x}+2a\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}+B_{y}u
in

Let

,

,

\mathcal{L}:=\Delta_{x}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}D_{x_{i}y}+D_{yy}+\frac{c}{y}D_{y}=\Delta_{x}u+2a\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}u+B_{y}u

(3)

in

equipped with Neumann boundary condition. The requirement

is equivalent to the ellipticity of the top order coefficients.

We use the Sobolev space
H^{1}_{c}:=\{u\in L^{2}_{c}:\nabla u\in L^{2}_{c}\}
equipped with the inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{c}}:=\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{c}}+\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{c}}.

The condition
c+1>0
assures, by
[
11
, Theorem 4.9]
, that the set

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty)\right),\ D_{y}u(x,y)=0\ {\rm for}\ y\leq\delta\ {\rm and\ some\ }\delta>0\right\},

(4)

is dense in
H^{1}_{c}
. Moreover, by
[
11
, Remark 4.14]
, if a function

has support in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,b]
, then there exists a sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{C}

\mbox{supp }u_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,b]
and
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{1}_{c}
.

We consider the form in
L^{2}_{c}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\overline{v}\rangle\,y^{c}dx\,dy+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}D_{y}u\,a\cdot\nabla_{x}\overline{v}\,y^{c}dx\,dy,\quad D(\mathfrak{a})=H^{1}_{c}

and its adjoint
\mathfrak{a}^{*}(u,v)=\overline{\mathfrak{a}(v,u)}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a^{*}}(u,v)=\overline{\mathfrak{a}(v,u)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\overline{v}\rangle\,y^{c}dx\,dy+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\,D_{y}\overline{v}\,y^{c}dx\,dy.

Proposition 2.1
.

The forms
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{a^{*}}
are continuous, accretive and sectorial.

Proof.
We consider only the form
\mathfrak{a}
since the adjoint form can be handled similarly. If
u\in H^{1}_{c}

\displaystyle\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{a}(u,u)\geq\|\nabla_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c}}+\|D_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c}}-2|a|\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}\|D_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}\geq(1-|a|)(\|\nabla_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c}}+\|D_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c}}).

By the ellipticity assumption
|a|<1
, the accretivity follows.
Moreover

\displaystyle|\textrm{\emph{Im}\,}\mathfrak{a}(u,u)|\leq 2|a|\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}\|D_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}\leq|a|(\|\nabla_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c}}+\|D_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c}})\leq\frac{|a|}{(1-|a|)}\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{a}(u,u).

This proves the sectoriality and then the continuity of the form.

We define the operators
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{*}
associated respectively to the forms
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
by

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle=\{u\in H^{1}_{c}:\exists f\in L^{2}_{c}\ {\rm such\ that}\ \mathfrak{a}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}f\overline{v}y^{c}\,dz\ {\rm for\ every}\ v\in H^{1}_{c}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u

\displaystyle=-f;

(5)

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{L}^{*})

\displaystyle=\{u\in H^{1}_{c}:\exists f\in L^{2}_{c}\ {\rm such\ that}\ \mathfrak{a}^{*}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}f\overline{v}y^{c}\,dz\ {\rm for\ every}\ v\in H^{1}_{c}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{*}u

\displaystyle=-f.

(6)

If
u,v
are smooth function with compact support in the closure of

(so that they do not need to vanish on the boundary), it is easy to see integrating by parts that

and therefore

is the operator

,

,

3
Weighted Sobolev inequalities

In this section we investigate the validity of anisotropic weighted Sobolev inequalities for the space
H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. An essential role is played by the sign of
c
. Indeed assuming that for some
p>2
one has

\displaystyle H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right),\qquad\|u\|_{L^{p}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)}.

then a simple scaling argument implies that the right expression of the Sobolev exponent
p
is

\displaystyle 2^{\ast}_{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}2\frac{N+1+c}{N+c-1},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\frac{1}{2^{*}_{c}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{N+1+c}.

Then a necessary condition for the validity of the latter inequalities is
c\geq 0
: this assumption guarantees that
2^{\ast}_{c}
is not greater than the Sobolev exponent
2^{\ast}=2\frac{N+1}{N-1}
for the Lebesgue measure since otherwise this would imply better Sobolev embedding on sets far from the singularity
y=0
, which is impossible.

For this reason we distinguish between the cases
c\geq 0
and
c<0
. When
c\geq 0
we prove that the embedding
H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L_{c}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
holds for any
2\leq q<{2^{*}_{c}}
. On the other hand, when
c<0
, we prove that the Sobolev immersion holds only locally and with the same Sobolev exponent
2^{\ast}
as for the Lebesgue measure.

We start by the case
c\geq 0
. We need some preparation. We treat first the case
c=0
where the latter embedding is just the standard Sobolev inequality which we state below in the form we use.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
for every
q\in(2,\infty)
satisfying
\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{N+1}
,
there exists
\widetilde{C}_{q}>0
such that
for every
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{q}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{(N+1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-(N+1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}.

We state now the following lemmas which can be found in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\geq 1
satisfies

\displaystyle 1\leq q\leq(N+1)^{\prime}=\frac{N+1}{N},\qquad\beta=\alpha-1+\frac{q-1}{q}(N+1)>-\frac{1}{q}

(in particular
\alpha+1>0
) then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|y^{\beta}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\|y^{\alpha}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\quad\forall u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right).

(9)

Proof.
See
[
8
, Corollary 1, Section 2.1.7, page 138]

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q,\ \beta,\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfying the following assumptions:

(i)

2\leq q\leq 2^{\ast}=2\frac{N+1}{N-1}
(
2\leq q
when
N=1
);

(ii)

\beta+\frac{1}{q}>0
;

(iii)

\beta=\alpha-1+(N+1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)
i.e.
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta-\alpha+1}{N+1}
.

Then one has for some positive constant
C

\displaystyle\|y^{\beta}u\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)}\leq C\|y^{\alpha}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)},\qquad\text{for all }\quad u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right).

Proof.

The proof follows by applying Lemma
3.2
with
q
,
\alpha
,
\beta
replaced respectively by
\frac{2q}{q+2}
,
\alpha+\frac{q\beta}{2}
,
\left(\frac{q}{2}+1\right)\beta
to the function
|u|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}
which yields

\displaystyle\|y^{\beta}u\|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\frac{q+2}{2}\|y^{\alpha}|\nabla u|y^{\frac{q\beta}{2}}|u|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}.

Applying then the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the right hand side yields

\displaystyle\|y^{\beta}u\|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\frac{q+2}{2}\|y^{\alpha}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\|y^{\beta}u\|^{\frac{q}{2}}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

which is the required claim.

We can now prove the Sobolev immersion

H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{\ast}_{c}}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
assuming

\displaystyle 2^{\ast}_{c}=2\frac{N+1+c}{N+c-1},\qquad c\geq 0.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
c>0
if
N=1
,
c\geq 0
otherwise. Then for some positive constant
C=C(c,N)
one has

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}_{c}}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)},\qquad\text{for all }\quad u\in H^{1}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right).

Proof.
Since the set
\mathcal{C}
defined in (
4
) is dense in
H^{1}_{c}
, we can assume without any loss of generality
u\in\mathcal{C}
. The proof then follows using Lemma
3.3
with
\alpha=\frac{c}{2}
,
\beta=\frac{c}{q}
.

The following Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities easily follow.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
c\geq 0
and let us fix
q\in(2,\infty)
satisfying satisfying
\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{N+1+c}
i.e.

(i)

2<q\leq 2^{\ast}_{c}
if
N>1
and
c\geq 0
, or
N=1
and
c>0

(ii)

q>2

N=1
and
c=0
.

Then there exists a positive constant
C
, which may depends on
c,N
(and also on
q

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{(N+1+c)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-(N+1+c)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)},\qquad\text{for all }\quad u\in H^{1}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right).

Proof.
Case (ii) is just Lemma
3.1
. To prove (i) let us fix
u\in H^{1}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
. Then, by Holder’s inequality and by Proposition
3.4

C(c,N)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{2_{c}^{\ast}}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{(N+1+c)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-(N+1+c)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\Big]^{(N+1+c)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-(N+1+c)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}

which is the required estimates.

When
c<0
, as explained before, the immersion
H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{\ast}_{c}}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
fails since otherwise would imply better Sobolev embedding on sets far from the singularity
y=0
.
In the next lemma we show that, in this case, the Sobolev immersion still holds locally (note that in this case the Sobolev exponent for
|y|^{c}\,dz
is the same as for the Lebesgue measure).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
c+1>0
. The following properties hold.

(i)

If
N>1
then one has for some positive constant
C

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\,\|y^{-\frac{c}{N+1}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\text{for all }\quad u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right).

(ii)

If
N=1
then for every
q\geq 2
there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\,\|y^{\frac{c+2}{q}-\frac{c}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\text{for all }\quad u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right).

Proof.
The proof follows by using Lemma
3.3
with
\beta=\frac{c}{q}
and, for claim (i), by imposing also
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2^{\ast}}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
-1<c<0
and
r>0
. The following properties hold.

(i)

If
N>1
then one has for some positive constant
C=C(r)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\text{for all }\quad u\in H^{1}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right),\quad\mbox{supp}\,u\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,r].

(ii)

If
N=1
then for every
q\geq 2
there exists a positive constant
C=C(r)
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\text{for all }\quad u\in H^{1}_{c}\left({\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right),\quad\mbox{supp}\,u\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,r].

Proof.
For simplicity we can assume
r=1
. Using the density of
\mathcal{C}
, we can assume without any loss of generality
u\in\mathcal{C}
. The proof then follows by (i) and (ii) of Lemma
3.6
after noticing that if
y\leq 1
then in (i) one has
y^{-\frac{c}{N+1}}\leq 1
and in (ii),
y^{\frac{c+2}{q}-\frac{c}{2}}\leq 1
.

We can prove some local Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
-1<c<0
,
r>0
and
q\in(2,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{N+1}
. Then for any
u\in H^{1}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
such that
\mbox{supp}\,u\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,r]
one has

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{(N+1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-(N+1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}.

Proof.
For simplicity we can assume
r=1
. The claim for
N>1
follows from (i) of Proposition
3.7
using Holder’s inequality as in Proposition
3.5
.

Let us prove the claim for
N=1
. Using Lemma
3.2
with
q=2
,
\beta=\alpha=\frac{c}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\|y^{-\frac{c}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\quad\forall u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right).

In particular since
c<0
, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\quad\forall u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right),\quad\mbox{supp}\,u\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1].

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
; applying the latter inequality to
|u|^{m-1}u
instead of
u
and using Holder’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|^{m}_{L^{2m}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq Cm\|\nabla u|u|^{m-1}\|_{L^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq Cm\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\|u\|^{m-1}_{L^{2m-2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

that is

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2m}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq(Cm)^{\frac{1}{m}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{\frac{1}{m}}\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{m}}_{L^{2(m-1)}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

(10)

which is valid for any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right)
with
\mbox{supp}\,u\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1]
. The inequality above and an easy inductive argument then proves that, for some positive constant
C=C(m)>0
, one has

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2m}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-\frac{1}{m}}\|u\|^{\frac{1}{m}}_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\quad\forall u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right),\quad\mbox{supp}\,u\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1]

(11)

which is the required claim for
q=2m
. Indeed for
m=1
the latter inequality is simply
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}
which is obviously true. Let us suppose (
11
) true for
m=k
; then applying (
10
) we get for any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\right)
with
\mbox{supp}\,u\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1]
and for some positive constant
C
depending on
k

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2(k+1)}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}_{L^{2k}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}\left(C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\|u\|^{\frac{1}{k}}_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}

\displaystyle=C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}\|u\|^{\frac{1}{k+1}}_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

which is the claim for
m=k+1
.

The claim for a general
q>2
then follows by interpolating
L^{q}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
between
L^{2m}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
and
L^{2m+2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
for a suitable
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

4
Kernel estimates

In this section we deduce kernel estimates for the degenerate operator

\mathcal{L}=\Delta_{x}u+2a\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}u+B_{y}u

of Section
2
. We use the standard machinery of
[
15
, Chapter 6]
, relying on Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities for deducing the ultracontractivity of the semigroup, which gives bounds on the diagonal, and on Davies’ method to add the gaussian term. Some difficulties appear since the semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
is not ultracontractive when
c<0
: this is due to the fact that the Sobolev immersions of Proposition
3.5
fail to hold, in the whole space, for
c<0
due to the singularity of the operator at the boundary
y=0
. To overcome this obstruction, we introduce then a further weight
\phi
and an equivalent operator
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
which allow us to take into account the degeneracy of the operator at the boundary. This introduction is essential for
c<0
since allows to prove the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities of Proposition
4.11
which hold in the whole half-space and for any
c>-1
; when
c\geq 0
, this approach, although not mandatory, is needed in order to deduce better heat kernel estimates (see Remark
4.16
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}[0,\infty)
satisfying
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta=1
on
[0,1/2]

and
\eta=0
on
[2,\infty)
and let us
define the function
\phi\in C^{\infty}[0,+\infty)
by

\phi(y):=\eta(y)+(1-\eta(y))y^{-\frac{c}{2}}.

(12)

By elementary computations,
\phi
satisfies for some positive constant
C_{0}>0
, see also
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
:

\displaystyle\phi(y)\simeq y^{-\frac{c}{2}}(1\wedge y)^{\frac{c}{2}},\qquad\left|D_{y}\phi\right|\leq C_{0}y^{-1}\chi_{\{y\geq 1\}}\,\phi,\qquad|B\phi|\leq C_{0}y^{-2}\chi_{\{y\geq 1\}}\,\phi.

(13)

In particular
\left|D_{y}\phi\right|\leq C_{0}\,\phi
,
|B\phi|\leq C_{0}\,\phi
.

Definition 4.2
.

We introduce the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\nu}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},d\nu)
,
and consider the isometry

\displaystyle\Phi:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},d\nu)\to L^{2}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right),\qquad f\mapsto\phi\,f

(14)

where by (
13
)

\displaystyle d\nu=\phi^{2}\,y^{c}dx\ dy\simeq(y\wedge 1)^{c}\,dxdy.

(15)

The map
\Phi
defines a similar operator
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}=\Phi^{-1}\mathcal{L}\Phi
which acts on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},d\nu)
.

In what follows we prove that the semigroup generated by
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
is ultracontractive. We need some preparation. Let us consider the symmetric form on
L^{2}_{\nu}
induced by
\Phi
and defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(u,v):=\mathfrak{a}(\phi u,\phi v)\qquad\quad D(\mathfrak{b})=\Phi^{-1}\left(D(\mathfrak{a})\right)=\frac{1}{\phi}H^{1}_{c}.

\mathfrak{b}
inherits, by construction, all the properties of
\mathfrak{a}
stated in Propositions
2.1
,
2.2
. In particular, since
\mathcal{C}
is preserved by
\Phi
,
\mathcal{C}
is dense in
D(\mathfrak{b})
. Moreover
\mathfrak{b}
is formally associated to
-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
. By (
13
), one can recognize that
D(\mathfrak{b})
coincides with the Hilbert space

H^{1}_{\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}_{\nu}}},\qquad\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{\nu}}:=\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

where the closure is taken with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\nu}}:=\sqrt{\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}}.

We clarify the latter result in the following propositions.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
u,v\in D(\mathfrak{b})
one has

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle D_{y}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-\left\langle\frac{B\phi}{\phi}\,u,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle\frac{D_{y}\phi}{\phi}\,u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

(16)

Proof.

By density we can assume
u,v\in\mathcal{C}
; then by the definition of
\mathfrak{a}
we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(\phi u,\phi v)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\langle\nabla(\phi u),\nabla(\phi\overline{v})\rangle\,y^{c}dx\,dy+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}D_{y}(\phi u)\,a\cdot\nabla_{x}\overline{v}\phi\,y^{c}dx\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\overline{v}\rangle\,\phi^{2}y^{c}dx\,dy+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}D_{y}u\,a\cdot\nabla_{x}\overline{v}\phi^{2}\,y^{c}dx\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\Big[D_{y}\phi D_{y}u\overline{v}\phi+D_{y}\phi D_{y}\overline{v}u\phi+D_{y}\phi D_{y}\phi u\overline{v}\Big]\,y^{c}dx\,dy

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}D_{y}\phi u\,a\cdot\nabla_{x}\overline{v}\phi\,y^{c}dx\,dy

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(\phi u,\phi v)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle D_{y}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}D_{y}\phi D_{y}\left(u\overline{v}\phi\right)\,y^{c}dx\,dy+2\left\langle\frac{D_{y}\phi}{\phi}\,u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}.

The claim then follows after observing that using (
13
) and integrating by parts we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}D_{y}\phi D_{y}\left(u\overline{v}\phi\right)\,y^{c}dx\,dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}(B\phi)u\overline{v}\,\phi\,y^{c}dx\,dy+\Big[D_{y}\phi\,\phi\,u\overline{v}\,y^{c}\Big]_{0}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}(B\phi)u\overline{v}\,\phi\,y^{c}dx\,dy.

Proposition 4.4
.

The following two norms are equivalent on
\mathcal{C}
:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathfrak{b}}=\|\phi u\|_{\mathfrak{a}}=\sqrt{\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{a}(\phi u,\phi u)+\|\phi u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}},\qquad\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\nu}}=\sqrt{\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}}

In particular
D(\mathfrak{b})=H^{1}_{\nu}
.

Proof.

We start by proving that for some positive constant
C
one has
\|u\|_{\mathfrak{b}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\nu}}
for any
u\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
\|\phi u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}=\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}
, it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{b}(u,u)=\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{a}(\phi u,\phi u)\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\nu}}^{2},\qquad u\in\mathcal{C}.

Let
C_{0}
be the constant in (
13
). Then using Lemma
4.3
, the property of
\phi
in (
13
) and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{b}(u,u)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle D_{y}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-\left\langle\frac{B\phi}{\phi}\,u,u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle\frac{D_{y}\phi}{\phi}\,u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+|a|\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+C_{0}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+C_{0}|a|\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+\|\nabla_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+|a|(1+C_{0})\right)\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+C_{0}(1+|a|)\,\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

which is the required claim.

On the other hand, with
C_{0}
as in (
13
) and using Lemma
4.3
, the property of
\phi
in (
13
) and Young’s inequality again we have also, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{b}(u,u)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle D_{y}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-\left\langle\frac{B\phi}{\phi}\,u,u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle\frac{D_{y}\phi}{\phi}\,u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\geq(1-|a|)\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-C_{0}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-\frac{C_{0}}{\varepsilon}|a|\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-C_{0}|a|\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}.

Choosing
\varepsilon
small enough such that
(1-|a|)-C_{0}|a|\epsilon>0
, it follows that

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{{L^{2}_{\nu}}}\leq C\left(\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{b}(u,u)+\|u\|^{2}_{{L^{2}_{\nu}}}\right)

for some positive constant
C
.

By similarity and by Proposition
2.2
,
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
generates a contractive analytic semigroup of angle
\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\frac{|a|}{1-|a|}
in
L^{2}_{\nu}
. However, in order to deduce pointwise kernel estimates by Davies’ method, we need to introduce perturbed forms as follows.

Definition 4.5
.

Let

\Psi:=\Big\{\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\;;|\nabla\psi|\leq 1;\quad|D^{2}\psi|\leq 1\Big\}.

(17)

We introduce the quadratic forms
\{\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\psi\in\Psi}

in
L^{2}_{\nu}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}(u,v)=\mathfrak{a}(e^{\alpha\psi}\phi u,e^{-\alpha\psi}\phi v),\qquad D(\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi})=D(\mathfrak{b}).

Remark 4.6
.

By standard computation one can recognize that

\displaystyle d(z_{1},z_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\sup_{\psi\in\Psi}\{\psi(z_{1})-\psi(z_{2})\},\qquad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}

defines a distance in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
which is actually equivalent to the euclidean one i.e.

\displaystyle|z_{1}-z_{2}|\simeq\sup_{\psi\in\Psi}\{\psi(z_{1})-\psi(z_{2})\},\qquad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}.

We refer the reader to
[
16
, pages 200-203]
or to
[
3
, Corollary 3.2.8]
for the proof of this classic result.

Since
\Phi
is an isometry from
L^{2}_{\nu}
onto
L^{2}_{c}
which maps
D(\mathfrak{b})
onto
D(\mathfrak{a})
and since

v\in L^{2}_{\nu}\to e^{\pm\alpha\psi}v\in L^{2}_{\nu}
are bounded operators
preserving
D(\mathfrak{b})
, the following result easily follows.

Proposition 4.7
.

For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\psi\in\Psi
the operator

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\psi}u=\phi^{-1}e^{-\alpha\psi}\mathcal{L}(\phi e^{\alpha\psi}u)

with domain
D(\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\psi})=\phi^{-1}e^{-\alpha\psi}(D(\mathcal{L}))
is associated
to the form
\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}
.
Moreover
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\psi}
generates a bounded analytic semigroups
e^{z\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\psi}},\;z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\frac{|a|}{1-|a|}}
in
L^{2}_{\nu}
which satisfies

\displaystyle e^{z\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\psi}}f=\phi^{-1}e^{-\alpha\psi}e^{z\mathcal{L}}\left(\phi e^{\alpha\psi}f\right),\quad f\in L^{2}_{\nu}.

(18)

Proposition 4.8
.

For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\psi\in\Psi
,
\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}

is quasi-accretive in
L^{2}_{\nu}
and for any
0<\epsilon<1
there exists some positive constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
dependent only on
\epsilon,a,c
such that

\displaystyle{\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}}(u,u)\geq

\displaystyle\,\epsilon(1-|a|)\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-C_{\epsilon}\left(\alpha^{2}+1\right)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}.

Moreover,
e^{t\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\psi}}
is positive and one has

\displaystyle\|e^{t\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\psi}}f\|_{\infty}\leq e^{C_{\epsilon}(1+\alpha^{2})t}\|f\|_{\infty},\quad f\in L^{2}_{\nu}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}).

Proof.
Since the form is real (i.e.
\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}
if
u,\ v\in\mathbb{R}
), one has
\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}(u,u)=\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}(\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}u,\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}u)+\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}(\textrm{\emph{Im}\,}u,\textrm{\emph{Im}\,}u)
so we can restrict the analysis, without any loss of generality, to real
u\in D(\mathfrak{b})
.
Using Lemma
4.3
and a straightforward calculation we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}(u,u)=

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla(e^{\alpha\psi}u),\nabla(e^{-\alpha\psi}u)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle D_{y}(e^{\alpha\psi}u),a\cdot\nabla_{x}(e^{-\alpha\psi}u)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle-\left\langle\frac{B\phi}{\phi}\,e^{\alpha\psi}u,e^{-\alpha\psi}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle\frac{D_{y}\phi}{\phi}\,e^{\alpha\psi}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}(e^{-\alpha\psi}u)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-\alpha^{2}\left\langle\nabla\psi u,\nabla\psi u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-2\alpha^{2}\left\langle D_{y}\psi u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle D_{y}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle-2\alpha\left\langle D_{y}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\alpha\left\langle D_{y}\psi u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle-\left\langle\frac{B\phi}{\phi}u,u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-2\alpha\left\langle\frac{D_{y}\phi}{\phi}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}+2\left\langle\frac{D_{y}\phi}{\phi}u,a\cdot\nabla_{x}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

Using the properties of
\phi
in (
13
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha\psi}(u,u)\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-\alpha^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-2\alpha^{2}|a|\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-2|a|\|D_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle-2|\alpha||a|\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}\|D_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-2|\alpha||a|\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle-C_{0}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-2|\alpha||a|C_{0}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}}-2C_{0}|a|\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{\nu}}.

(19)

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get for any
\epsilon_{1}>0

z_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1}),\ z_{2}=(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}

\displaystyle e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}f(z_{1})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}p_{{\mathcal{L}}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})f(z_{2})\,y_{2}^{c}\,dz_{2},\quad f\in L^{2}_{c}.

Its heat kernel
p_{{\mathcal{L}}}
, written with respect the measure
y^{c}dz
, satisfies for some
C,k>0
,

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{{\mathcal{L}}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})\leq Ct^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}y_{1}^{-\frac{c}{2}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{1}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2}}y_{2}^{-\frac{c}{2}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2}}\,\exp\left(-\dfrac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{kt}\right).

The last estimate can be written equivalently as

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{{\mathcal{L}}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})\leq Ct^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}y_{2}^{-c}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{c}\,\exp\left(-\dfrac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{kt}\right).

Proof.
The existence of the heat kernel follows from Lemma
4.13
and (
26
). To prove the required estimate we use (
22
) for
t=1
and (
13
) and (
26
) to get

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{L}}(1,z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\leq Cy_{1}^{-\frac{c}{2}}(1\wedge y_{1})^{\frac{c}{2}}y_{2}^{-\frac{c}{2}}(1\wedge y_{2})^{\frac{c}{2}}y_{2}^{c}\,\exp\left(\frac{-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{k}\right)

for any
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}.

On the other hand using the scaling property (
8
) of Proposition
2.3
we get

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{L}}(s^{2}t,z_{1},z_{2})=s^{-(N+1+c)}p_{\mathcal{L}}(t,s^{-1}z_{1},s^{-1}{z_{2}}),\qquad s,t>0,\quad{z_{1}},{z_{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}.

In particular

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{L}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})=t^{-\frac{N+1+c}{2}}p_{\mathcal{L}}\left(1,\frac{z_{1}}{\sqrt{t}},\frac{z_{2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)

which combined with the above inequality for
t=1
gives the first required estimate.

The second equivalent estimate, up to a possibly different constants
C,k
, follows by applying
[
10
, Lemma 10.2]
.

5
General operators and oblique derivative

Kernel estimates for more general operators and boundary conditions follow by a linear change of variables, as we explain below. Let us first remove the assumption on the special form of
\mathcal{L}=\Delta_{x}+2a\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}+B_{y}u
by considering the general operator in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}

\mathcal{L}=\mbox{Tr }\left(QD^{2}u\right)+\frac{c}{y}D_{y}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}q_{ij}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}q_{i}D_{x_{i}y}+\gamma D_{yy}+\frac{c}{y}D_{y}

with Neumann boundary condition
\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}D_{y}u=0
.
If
Q_{1}
is the
N\times N
matrix
(q_{ij})
,
\gamma=q_{N+1,N+1}
and
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{N})
, we assume that the quadratic form
Q(\xi,\eta)=Q_{1}(\xi,\xi)+\gamma\eta^{2}+2q\cdot\xi\,\eta
is positive definite.
Through a linear change of variables in the
x
variables the term
\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}q_{ij}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}
is transformed into
\gamma\Delta_{x}
and all the results of the previous sections remain true. In particular if
\frac{c}{\gamma}+1>0
then
\mathcal{L}
generates a contractive analytic semigroups
e^{z\mathcal{L}}

in
L^{2}_{\frac{c}{\gamma}}
and the kernel estimates of Theorems
4.14
and
4.18
remain true.

A further change of variables allows to deal with the operator

\mathcal{L}=\mbox{Tr }\left(QD^{2}u\right)+\frac{v\cdot\nabla}{y}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N+1}q_{ij}D_{ij}+\frac{b\cdot\nabla_{x}}{y}+\frac{cD_{y}}{y},\qquad c\neq 0,

where
v=(b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and is
Q
positive definite. We impose, in this case, an oblique derivative boundary condition

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}\,v\cdot\nabla u=0.

We transform
\mathcal{L}
into a similar operator with
b=0
and Neumann boundary conditions by defining the following isometry of
L^{2}_{\frac{c}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle T\,u(x,y)

\displaystyle:=u\left(x-\frac{b}{c}y,y\right),\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}.

(24)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
v=(b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
c\neq 0
. Then for
u\in W^{2,1}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\right)

(i)

\displaystyle T^{-1}\,\left(v\cdot\nabla\right)T\,u=cD_{y}u
;

(ii)

\displaystyle T^{-1}\,\left(\mbox{Tr }\left(QD^{2}\right)+\frac{v\cdot\nabla}{y}\right)T\,u=\mbox{Tr }\left(\tilde{Q}D^{2}u\right)+\frac{c}{y}D_{y}u
.

Here
\tilde{Q}
is a uniformly elliptic symmetric matrix defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}Q_{N}-\frac{2}{c}b\otimes q{+\frac{\gamma}{c^{2}}b\otimes b}&q^{t}-\frac{\gamma}{c}b^{t}\\[4.30554pt]
\hline\cr q-\frac{\gamma}{c}b&\gamma\end{array}\right)

and
\gamma=q_{N+1,N+1}
.

Proof.
The proof follows by a straightforward computation.

The latter lemma shows that the operator
\mathcal{L}
, endowed with the oblique boundary condition
\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}\,v\cdot\nabla u=0
is unitarily equivalent to the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{L}}:=T^{-1}\mathcal{L}T\,=\mbox{Tr }\left(\tilde{Q}D^{2}\right)+\frac{c}{y}D_{y}

(25)

with the Neumann boundary condition
\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}\,D_{y}u=0
. Then, by similarity and the previous results, if
\frac{c}{\gamma}+1>0
,
\mathcal{L}
generates a contractive analytic semigroups
e^{z\mathcal{L}}
. In order to deduce kernel estimates we observe that, if
p_{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}}
is the heat kernel of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
with respect to the measure
y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}dz
, then one has for
z_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1}),z_{2}=(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}}f(z_{1})

\displaystyle=T\big[e^{t\tilde{\mathcal{L}}}(T^{-1}f)\Big](z_{1})=\big[e^{t\tilde{\mathcal{L}}}(T^{-1}f)\Big](x_{1}-\frac{b}{c}y_{1},y_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}p_{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}}\left(t,\left(x_{1}-\frac{b}{c}y_{1},y_{1}\right),z_{2}\right)f\left(x_{2}+\frac{b}{c}y_{2},y_{2}\right)\,y_{2}^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}dx_{2}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}p_{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}}\left(t,\left(x_{1}-\frac{b}{c}y_{1},y_{1}\right),\left(x_{2}-\frac{b}{c}y_{2},y_{2}\right)\right)f(z_{2})\,y_{2}^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}dz_{2}.

This shows that the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}
is given by

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{L}}(t,z_{1},z_{2}):=p_{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}}\left(t,\left(x_{1}-\frac{b}{c}y_{1},y_{1}\right),\left(x_{2}-\frac{b}{c}y_{2},y_{2}\right)\right).

(26)

By Theorem
4.14
, we then obtain kernel estimates for
\mathcal{L}
. We state them for real
t
only for simplicity. We need a preliminary lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
one has, for some positive constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle|x-ky|^{2}+y^{2}\geq C\left(|x|^{2}+y^{2}\right),\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.
Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get for
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle|x-ky|^{2}+y^{2}

\displaystyle=|x|^{2}+(1+|k|^{2})y^{2}-2x\cdot ky\geq|x|^{2}+(1+|k|^{2})y^{2}-2|x||k||y|

\displaystyle\geq|x|^{2}+(1+|k|^{2})y^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}|x|^{2}-\epsilon{|k|^{2}}y^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon})|x|^{2}+(1+(1-\epsilon)|k|^{2})y^{2}.

The required claim then follows by choosing
1<\epsilon<1+\frac{1}{|k|^{2}}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
v=(b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
with
b=0
if
c=0
,
\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}Q&{q}^{t}\\[4.30554pt]
\hline\cr q&\gamma\end{array}\right)

an elliptic matrix.
If
\frac{c}{\gamma}+1>0
then the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mbox{Tr }\left(QD^{2}u\right)+\frac{v\cdot\nabla}{y}

endowed with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}\,v\cdot\nabla u=0,\qquad\text{(if $c\neq 0$)},\qquad\qquad\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}\,D_{y}u=0,\qquad\hskip 8.61108pt\text{(if $c=0$)},

generates a contractive analytic semigroup in
L^{2}_{\frac{c}{\gamma}}
. The semigroup
(e^{t{\mathcal{L}}})_{t>0}
consists of integral operators and its heat kernel
p_{{\mathcal{L}}}
, written with respect the measure
y^{\frac{c}{\gamma}}dz
, satisfies for some
C,k>0
,

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{{\mathcal{L}}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})\leq Ct^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}y_{1}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{1}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}y_{2}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\,\exp\left(-\dfrac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{kt}\right).

Proof.
The generation property follows by similarity using the equality (
25
). To prove the kernel estimates we use (
26
) and Theorem
4.14
getting

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{{\mathcal{L}}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})\leq Ct^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}y_{1}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{1}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}y_{2}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\dfrac{|x_{1}-x_{2}-\frac{b}{c}(y_{1}-y_{2})|^{2}+|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}}{kt}\right).

The required claim then follows using Lemma
5.2
.

Having at disposal good kernel estimates for
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
implies various consequences about the regularity of the semigroup and its spectral properties; as an application one can study, for example, generation results in
L^{p}
space.
We refer the reader to
[
14
,
12
]
where it has been proved that the above semigroup extrapolates to the weighted spaces
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{m}dxdy\right)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, when
0<\frac{m+1}{p}<\frac{c}{\gamma}+1
; furthermore a characterization of the domain of the generators is given in terms of suitable anisotropic weighted Sobolev spaces.

q q -bic forms
Raymond Cheng

Abstract
. A q q -bic form is a pairing V × V → 𝐤 V\times V\to\mathbf{k} that is linear in the second variable and q q -power Frobenius linear in the first; here, V V is a vector space over a field 𝐤 \mathbf{k} containing the finite field 𝐅 q 2 \mathbf{F}_{q^{2}} . This article develops a geometric theory of q q -bic forms in the spirit of that of bilinear forms. I find two filtrations intrinsically attached to a q q -bic form, with which I define a series of numerical invariants. These are used to classify, study automorphism group schemes of, and describe specialization relations in the parameter space of q q -bic forms.



Introduction

Eschewing the semi-linear description of the abstract, a

q
-bic form
is a bilinear pairing
\beta
between a
finite-dimensional vector space
V
over a field
\mathbf{k}
and its

Frobenuis twist

V^{[1]}\coloneqq\mathbf{k}\otimes_{\Fr,\mathbf{k}}V
, where the
scalar action is twisted by the
q
-power Frobenius map

\Fr\colon\mathbf{k}\to\mathbf{k}
. Equivalently, this is a linear map

\beta\colon V^{[1]}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}V\to\mathbf{k}.

This article develops an intrinsic theory of
q
-bic forms, with an eye
towards the geometry of their hypersurfaces of isotropic vectors, the

q
-bic hypersurfaces
described in more detail below; briefly, these
are a large class of special hypersurfaces which are important to study as they
exemplify many poorly understood positive characteristic phenomena, such as
supersingularity
[
Tat65
,
SK79
]
,
unirationality despite being of general type
[
Shi74
,
Shi77
]
,
Fano varieties that do not carry minimal free rational curves
[
Con06
,
She12
,
Che24a
]
, and
inseparable Gauss maps
[
Wal56
,
Nom95
,
KP91
]
.
They are extremal in many respects, such as in relation to
F
-singularity
measures
[
KKP+22
]
, and arise in relation to with Deligne–Lusztig
theory
[
Lus76
,
DL76
,
Li23
]
, unitary Shimura varieties

[
Vol10
,
LZ22
,
LTX+22
]
, and finite Hermitian geometries

[
BC66
,
Seg65
,
Hir79
]
. The theory
developed here opens up new deformation- and moduli-theoretic techniques in the

explored in the companion papers
[

,
Che24b
]
.

To begin to appreciate the main aspects of the theory of
q
-bic forms,
assume from now on that the base field
\mathbf{k}
contains the finite field

\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
, so that the
q
-power Frobenius is a nontrivial
endomorphism of
\mathbf{k}
. A
q
-bic form
\beta
then pairs two distinct

\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces which are nonetheless related by the canonical

q
-linear map
V\to V^{[1]}
given by
v\mapsto v^{[1]}\coloneqq 1\otimes v
. To emphasize: this map is not
\mathbf{k}
-linear and is not a
bijection if
\mathbf{k}
is imperfect. In any case, a basis
V=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle
induces a basis
V^{[1]}=\langle e_{1}^{[1]},\ldots,e_{n}^{[1]}\rangle
and the
q
-bic form
\beta
may be
explicitly understood through its
Gram matrix
:

\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})\coloneqq\Big(\beta(e_{i}^{[1]},e_{j})\Big)_{i,j=1}^{n}.

Moreover, if
(e_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,e_{n}^{\prime})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})\cdot A^{\vee}
is another
basis of
V
, related to the original via an invertible matrix
A
, then
the two Gram matrices are related by
q
-twisted conjugation
:

\Gram(\beta;e_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,e_{n}^{\prime})=A^{[1],\vee}\cdot\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})\cdot A

where
A^{[1],\vee}
is obtained from the transpose of
A
by raising
each matrix entry to the
q
-th power. In concrete terms, this article
studies the invariants, orbits, and stabilizers of
q
-twisted conjugation.

Returning to the intrinsic situation, contemplate: what are invariants
of
\beta
? The key observation is that the Frobenius map makes it
possible to iteratively take left and right orthogonals, thereby canonically
attaching two sequences of vector spaces to
\beta
. Left orthogonals give
rise to a sequence of subspaces of various Frobenius twists of
V
, collectively
referred to as the
\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration
;
for instance, the first space is the left kernel

\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}V^{[1]}=V^{\perp}\coloneqq\ker(\beta^{\vee}\colon V^{[1]}\to V^{\vee})
.
Right orthogonals give rise to a finite filtration on
V
,
the
\perp
-filtration
, which has the form

\mathrm{P}_{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}V\colon\{0\}\eqqcolon\mathrm{P}_{-1}V\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{1}V\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{3}V\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{-}V\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{+}V\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{4}V\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{2}V\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{0}V\eqqcolon V

where the odd- and even-indexed pieces form increasing and decreasing filtrations,
limiting to subspaces
\mathrm{P}_{-}V
and
\mathrm{P}_{+}V
, respectively;
this time, the first piece is

\mathrm{P}_{1}V=V^{[1],\perp}\coloneqq\ker(\beta\colon V\to V^{[1],\vee})
.
Numerical invariants of
\beta
are then obtained by taking dimensions of
graded pieces of this filtration:
for each
m\geq 1
, set
\epsilon\coloneqq(-1)^{m}
, and

a\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{+}V/\mathrm{P}_{-}V,\qquad a_{m}\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{m-\epsilon-1}V/\mathrm{P}_{m+\epsilon-1}V,\qquad b_{m}\coloneqq a_{m}-a_{m+1}.

The first result of this article classify
\beta
in terms of its

type

(a;b_{m})_{m\geq 1}
and properties of its

\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}

Applications and interrelations

—

Isotropic lines for a nonzero

-bic form

There have been a few precedents to this circle of ideas in the literature:

[

,
Seg65
]
. This perspective is also adopted by
[

]

in a spirit closer to that of the present article. The classification of

-conjugacy classes of matrices has been considered in varying degrees of
generality: for nonsingular matrices, this follows from Lang’s Lemma, see

[
Lan56
, Theorem 1]
, and can be found in
[
Hef85
,
Bea90
]
; for corank
1
matrices, this is
[

, Proposition
1]
; and for general matrices, this is
[

.

1.
Basic notions

This Section begins with the basic definitions, constructions, and properties
of
q
-bic forms. For the schematic constructions later, this material
is developed in the setting of finite projective modules over an

\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
-algebra
R
. To set notation: Write

\Fr\colon R\to R
for the
q
-power Frobenius morphism.
Given an
R
-module
M
, write
M^{\vee}\coloneqq\Hom_{R}(M,R)
for its
dual; the double dual is tacitly identified with
M

itself. For each integer
i\geq 0
, write

M^{[i]}\coloneqq R\otimes_{\Fr^{i},R}M
for the
i
-th Frobenius
twist
of
M
: the (left)
R
-module on which the action of
R
is
twisted by the
q^{i}
-power Frobenius. The canonical map
M\to M^{[i]}

given by
m\mapsto m^{[i]}\coloneqq 1\otimes m
is the universal

\Fr^{i}
-linear map out of
M
. Given a submodule
N^{\prime}\subseteq M^{[i]}
, a submodule
N\subseteq M
such that
N^{[i]}=N^{\prime}
is called a

Frobenius descent
of
N^{\prime}
to
M
.

1.1.
Definitions

—

A
q
-bic form
over
R
is a pair
(M,\beta)
consisting of an

R
-module
M
and an
R
-linear map

\beta\colon M^{[1]}\otimes_{R}M\to R
. A
morphism

\varphi\colon(M_{1},\beta_{1})\to(M_{2},\beta_{2})

between two
q
-bic forms is a morphism
\varphi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
of

R
-modules such that, for every
m\in M_{1}
and
m^{\prime}\in M_{1}^{[1]}
,

\beta_{1}(m^{\prime},m)=\beta_{2}(\varphi^{[1]}(m^{\prime}),\varphi(m))

where
\varphi^{[1]}\colon M_{1}^{[1]}\to M_{2}^{[1]}
is the Frobenius twist
of
\varphi
. The morphism
\varphi
is an
isomorphism
if the underlying
module map is an isomorphism.

Adjunction induces two mutually dual
R
-linear maps which, by an abuse of
notation, are denoted

\beta\colon M\to M^{[1],\vee}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta^{\vee}\colon M^{[1]}\to M^{\vee}.

The form
(M,\beta)
is said to be
nondegenerate
if
the map
\beta\colon M\to M^{[1]}
is injective, and

nonsingular
if this map is an isomorphism.

1.2.
Gram matrices

—

Suppose that
M
is, moreover, a finite free
R
-module. The

Gram matrix
of a
q
-bic form
\beta
with respect to a basis

\varphi\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R\cdot e_{i}\xrightarrow{\sim}M
is the

n
-by-
n
matrix

\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})\coloneqq\big(\beta(\varphi(e_{i})^{[1]},\varphi(e_{j}))\big)_{i,j=1}^{n}.

Equivalently, this is the matrix of the map
\beta\colon M\to M^{[1],\vee}

with respect to the bases

\varphi\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R\cdot e_{i}\xrightarrow{\sim}M
and

\varphi^{[1]}\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R\cdot e_{i}^{[1]}\xrightarrow{\sim}M^{[1]}
.

Given another basis

\varphi^{\prime}\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R\cdot e_{i}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\sim}M
,
view the change of basis isomorphism

A\coloneqq\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}
as an invertible
n
-by-
n

matrix over
R
. Its Frobenius twist
A^{[1]}
is the matrix obtained from

A
by taking
q
-powers entrywise, and the two Gram matrices are related
by

\Gram(\beta;e_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,e_{n}^{\prime})=A^{[1],\vee}\cdot\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})\cdot A.

It is now clear that a
q
-bic form
\beta
on a free module
M
is
nonsingular if and only if its Gram matrix in some—equivalently, any—basis
is invertible.

1.3.
Standard forms

—

Gram matrices provide a convenient way to encode
q
-bic forms.
Given an
n
-by-
n
matrix
B
over
R
, let
(R^{\oplus n},B)

denote the unique
q
-bic form with Gram matrix
B
in the given basis.
Particularly simple, and important, are the following:
For each integer
m\geq 1
, let

\mathbf{N}_{m}\coloneqq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&&\\
&0&\cdots&&\\
&&\cdots&&\\
&&\cdots&0&1\\
&&\cdots&&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)

denote the
m
-by-
m
Jordan block with
0
on the diagonal, and write

\mathbf{1}
for the
1
-by-
1
identity matrix.
Given two matrices
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, write
B_{1}\oplus B_{2}
for their
block diagonal sum. Let
\mathbf{b}\coloneqq(a;b_{1},b_{2},\ldots)
be a
sequence of nonnegative integers such that
n=a+\sum_{m\geq 1}mb_{m}
.
The
q
-bic form

\Big(R^{\oplus n},\mathbf{1}^{\oplus a}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus\nolimits_{m\geq 1}\mathbf{N}_{m}^{\oplus b_{m}}\Big)\Big)

is the
standard
q
-bic form of type
\mathbf{b}
.

1.4.
Orthogonals

—

To discuss orthogonals, consider generally a map

\beta\colon M_{2}\otimes_{R}M_{1}\to R
of
R
-modules, viewed
as a pairing between
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. Given submodules

N_{i}\subseteq M_{i}
for
i=1,2
, write

N_{1}^{\perp}\coloneqq\ker(M_{2}\xrightarrow{\beta^{\vee}}M_{1}^{\vee}\to N_{1}^{\vee})\quad\text{and}\quad N_{2}^{\perp}\coloneqq\ker(M_{1}\xrightarrow{\beta}M_{2}^{\vee}\to N_{2}^{\vee}).

These are the
orthogonals
, with respect to
\beta
, of
N_{1}
and

N_{2}
, respectively. The orthogonals
M_{2}^{\perp}\subseteq M_{1}
and

M_{1}^{\perp}\subseteq M_{2}
are called the
kernels
of

\beta
(and
\beta^{\vee}
). It is formal to see that taking orthogonals
is an inclusion-reversing operation. Precisely: for submodules
N_{1}
and

N_{1}^{\prime}
of
M_{1}
:

(i)

if
N_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq N_{1}
, then
N_{1}^{\prime\perp}\supseteq N_{1}^{\perp}
;

(ii)

(N_{1}+N_{1}^{\prime})^{\perp}=N_{1}^{\perp}\cap N_{1}^{\prime\perp}
; and

(iii)

(N_{1}\cap N_{1}^{\prime})^{\perp}\supseteq N_{1}^{\perp}+N_{1}^{\prime\perp}
.

With further assumptions, orthogonals behave as expected:

1.5 Lemma
. —

Suppose that the image of the map
\beta\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}^{\vee}

is a local direct summand, and that
N_{1}^{\prime},N_{1}\subseteq M_{1}
are local
direct summands. Then there are exact sequences:

(i)

0\to M_{2}^{\perp}\to M_{1}\to M_{2}^{\vee}\to M_{1}^{\perp,\vee}\to 0
,

(ii)

0\to N_{1}\cap M_{2}^{\perp}\to N_{1}\to M_{2}^{\vee}\to N_{1}^{\perp,\vee}\to 0
, and

(iii)

0\to N_{1}\cap M_{2}^{\perp}/N_{1}^{\prime}\cap M_{2}^{\perp}\to N_{1}/N_{1}^{\prime}\to(N_{1}^{\prime\perp}/N_{1}^{\perp})^{\vee}\to 0
if additionally
N_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq N_{1}
.

The operation of taking orthogonals further satisfy:

(iv)

N_{1}^{\perp,\perp}=M_{2}^{\perp}+N_{1}
, that is, reflexivity, and

(v)

1.6 Lemma
. —

Let

The inclusion “

1.7.
Frobenius twists and orthogonals

—

Return to the situation of a
q
-bic form
(M,\beta)
over
R
.
Twisting by Frobenius yields a sequence of associated
q
-bic forms

(M^{[i]},\beta^{[i]})
for each integer
i\geq 0
; the
i
-th Frobenius
twisted form is characterized by the formula

\beta^{[i]}(m^{\prime[i]},m^{[i]})=\beta(m^{\prime},m)^{q^{i}}\quad\text{for every}\;m\in M\;\text{and}\;m^{\prime}\in M^{[1]}.

Observe that the module
M^{[i]}
is involved with the
two Frobenius twists of
\beta
, namely
\beta^{[i-1]}
and

\beta^{[i]}
. Accordingly, a submodule
N\subseteq M^{[i]}
induces two
orthogonals:

\displaystyle N^{\perp^{[i-1]}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\ker\big(\beta^{[i-1]}\colon M^{[i-1]}\to M^{[i],\vee}\to N^{\vee}\big),\;\text{and}

\displaystyle N^{\perp^{[i]}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\ker\big(\beta^{[i],\vee}\colon M^{[i+1]}\to M^{[i],\vee}\to N^{\vee}\big).

These are the
(i-1)
-th and
i
-th
Frobenius-twisted
orthogonals of
N
.

In this setting, suppose further that taking Frobenius twists commutes with
taking kernels: for instance, suppose that
R
is regular so that the
Frobenius morphism is flat by
[
Kun69
]
; or suppose that the image in

N^{\vee}
of
\beta^{[i-1]}
or
\beta^{[i],\vee}
has vanishing

\Tor_{1}^{R}
. Then the Frobenius-twisted orthogonal commutes with taking
Frobenius twists in the following sense: for any integer
j\geq 0
,

N^{\perp^{[i-1]},[j]}=N^{[j],\perp^{[i+j-1]}}\quad\text{and}\quad N^{\perp^{[i]},[j]}=N^{[j],\perp^{[i+j]}}

as submodules of
M^{[i+j-1]}
and
M^{[i+j+1]}
, respectively.

1.8.
Total orthogonals and complements

—

The
total orthogonal
of a submodule
N\subseteq M
is

N^{\perp}\cap N^{[2],\perp^{[1]}}=\Set{m^{\prime}\in M^{[1]}}{\beta(m^{\prime},n)=\beta^{[1]}(n^{[2]},m^{\prime})=0\;\text{for all}\;n\in N}.

This is a submodule of
M^{[1]}
and, in general, it need not have a
Frobenius descent to
M
. The
radical
of
(M,\beta)
is
the total orthogonal of
M
itself, and may be written as

\operatorname{rad}(\beta)=\Set{m^{\prime}\in M^{[1]}}{\beta(m^{\prime},m)=\beta^{[1]}(m^{[2]},m^{\prime})=0\;\text{for all}\;m\in M}.

In particular,
\beta^{[1]}
passes to the quotient and induces a
q
-bic
form on
M^{[1]}/\operatorname{rad}(\beta)
.

An
orthogonal complement
to a submodule
M^{\prime}
is a submodule

M^{\prime\prime}
such that
M=M^{\prime}\oplus M^{\prime\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime[1]}
lies in the
total orthogonal of
M^{\prime}
. This situation is signified by

(M,\beta)=(M^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\perp(M^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime})

where
\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}
denote the restriction of
\beta
to

M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
, respectively; when the underlying modules are understood,
also write
\beta=\beta^{\prime}\oplus\beta^{\prime\prime}
. Orthogonal complements need
not exist, and when they do, need not be unique: one exists if and only if
the total orthogonal contains a module complement that descends to
M
; it is
unique if and only if the total orthogonal is the complement.

2.
Hermitian forms

A
q
-bic form
(M,\beta)
over
R
linearizes a biadditive map

M\times M\to R
which is
R
-linear in the second
variable, but only
q
-linear in the first. In the case that

R=\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
, such forms are sesquilinear with respect the

q
-power Frobenius, and the Hermitian property gives a notion of symmetry.
In general, the Hermitian condition does not make sense. This Section identifies
a distinguished subset of any
q
-bic form consisting of those elements
that satisfy the Hermitian equation. This construction provides an invariant of

q
-bic forms, sensitive to the arithmetic of its ring of definition.

2.1.
Hermitian elements

—

An element
m\in M
is said to be
Hermitian
if

\beta(-,m)=\beta^{[1]}(m^{[2]},-)\;\text{as elements of $M^{[1],\vee}$}

or equivalently, if
\beta(n^{[1]},m)=\beta(m^{[1]},n)^{q}
for all
n\in M
,
see
1.7
.
It is straightforward to check that the set
M_{\mathrm{Herm}}
of Hermitian
elements is a vector space over
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
and that

\beta(m_{1}^{[1]},m_{2})\in\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
for every

m_{1},m_{2}\in M_{\mathrm{Herm}}
. Therefore the restriction of
\beta

to the space of Hermitian elements gives

\beta_{\mathrm{Herm}}\colon M^{[1]}_{\mathrm{Herm}}\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}}M_{\mathrm{Herm}}\to\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}

a Hermitian form for the quadratic extension

\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbf{F}_{q}
. This partially justifies the nomenclature:
if
M
is a free module with basis
\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle

consisting of Hermitian elements, then the associated Gram matrix is a
Hermitian matrix over
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
in that it satisfies

\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})^{\vee}=\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})^{[1]}.

The space
M_{\mathrm{Herm}}
may not be of finite dimension over

\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
. For instance, it contains the preimage in
M
of the
radical of
\beta
. It is therefore helpful to note that taking Hermitian
vectors is compatible with orthogonal decompositions:

2.2 Lemma
. —

An orthogonal decomposition

(M,\beta)=(M^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\perp(M^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime})

induces an orthogonal decomposition of Hermitian spaces

(M_{\mathrm{Herm}},\beta_{\mathrm{Herm}})=(M_{\mathrm{Herm}}^{\prime},\beta_{\mathrm{Herm}}^{\prime})\perp(M_{\mathrm{Herm}}^{\prime\prime},\beta_{\mathrm{Herm}}^{\prime\prime}).

Proof.

Let
m\in M_{\mathrm{Herm}}
, and let
m=m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}
be its
decomposition with
m^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
and
m^{\prime\prime}\in M^{\prime\prime}
. Consider
m^{\prime}
.
Since
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
are orthogonal, for every
n\in M^{\prime}
,

\beta^{\prime}(n^{[1]},m^{\prime})=\beta(n^{[1]},m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime})=\beta((m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime})^{[1]},n)^{q}=\beta^{\prime}(m^{\prime[1]},n)^{q}.

Therefore
m^{\prime}\in M_{\mathrm{Herm}}^{\prime}
. An analogous
argument shows
m^{\prime\prime}\in M_{\mathrm{Herm}}^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

In the remainder of this Section, let

(V,\beta)
be a
q
-bic form over a field
\mathbf{k}
containing
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
.

2.3.
Scheme of Hermitan vectors

—

The subgroup
V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
of Hermitian
vectors of
V
may be endowed with the structure of a closed subgroup scheme
of the affine space
\mathbf{A}V\coloneqq\Spec\Sym V^{\vee}
on
V
.
Namely, the functor taking a
\mathbf{k}
-algebra
R
to the group of Hermitian
elements of the
q
-bic form
(V\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R,\beta\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R)
over

R
obtained by extension of scalars is represented by the closed subscheme

\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}=\mathrm{V}\big(\beta(\phantom{-},-)-\beta^{[1]}(-^{[2]},\phantom{-})\big)

cut out by the difference of two morphisms

\mathbf{A}V\to\mathbf{A}V^{[1],\vee}
which are induced by the linear maps

v\mapsto\beta(\phantom{-},v)\in\Hom_{\mathbf{k}}(V^{[1]},V^{\vee})\quad\text{and}\quad v\mapsto\beta^{[1]}(v^{[2]},\phantom{-})\in\Hom_{\mathbf{k}}(V^{[1]},V^{\vee,[2]}),

where
V^{\vee,[2]}
is identified as the subspace of

q^{2}
-powers in
\Sym^{q^{2}}(V^{\vee})\subset\Gamma(\mathbf{A}V,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}V})
.
Concretely, choose a basis
V=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle
,
let
B\coloneqq\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
be the associated Gram matrix,
and let
\mathbf{x}^{\vee}\coloneqq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the associated
coordinates for
\mathbf{A}V\cong\mathbf{A}^{n}
.
Then
\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
is the closed subscheme given by
the system of equations

B\mathbf{x}-B^{[1],\vee}\mathbf{x}^{[2]}=0.

Observe furthermore that, by
2.1
,
\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
is a scheme of vector spaces over
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
, and
is equipped with a Hermitian bilinear form

\mathbf{A}\beta_{\mathrm{Herm}}\colon\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}^{[1]}\times_{\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}\to\mathbf{F}_{q^{2},\mathbf{k}}

with values in the constant group scheme associated with
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
.

2.4.
Examples

—

Schemes of Hermitian vectors for the standard forms of
1.3

are determined with the help of
2.2

by the following two computations:

First, let
(V,\beta)=(\mathbf{k}^{\oplus n},\mathbf{N}_{n})
be
the standard form with just one nilpotent block. Then

\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}=\Spec\mathbf{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{2},x_{3}-x_{1}^{q^{2}},\ldots,x_{n}-x_{n-2}^{q^{2}},x_{n-1}^{q^{2}}).

The structure of this scheme depends on the parity of
n
:

\frac{\mathbf{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]}{(x_{2},x_{3}-x_{1}^{q^{2}},\ldots,x_{n}-x_{n-2}^{q^{2}},x_{n-1}^{q^{2}})}\cong\begin{cases}\mathbf{k}[x_{1}]&if $n$ is odd, and\\
\mathbf{k}[x_{1}]/(x_{1}^{q^{n}})&if $n$ is even.\end{cases}

Indeed, the first
n-1
equations imply that the even-indexed
variables vanish, and that
x_{2k+1}
is the
q^{2k}
-power
of
x_{1}
. As for the final equation: when
n
is odd, this is implied by
the vanishing of the even-indexed variables, whereas when
n
is even, this
shows that
x_{n-1}^{q^{2}}=x_{1}^{q^{n}}=0
.

Second, let
V=\langle e\rangle
be a
1
-dimensional vector space
and let
\beta(e^{[1]},e)=\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{k}
. Then

\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}=\Spec\mathbf{k}[x]/(\lambda x-\lambda^{q}x^{q^{2}}).

This is a form of the constant group scheme on
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
that
splits with a
(q+1)
-th root of
\lambda
. By

2.2
, this determines the structure the
scheme of Hermitian vectors for all diagonal
q
-bic forms. This also suggests
that the scheme is quite simple for all nonsingular
q
-bic forms. Indeed:

2.5 Proposition
. —

If
(V,\beta)
is a nonsingular
q
-bic form, then
\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
is an
étale group scheme of degree
q^{2}n
over
\mathbf{k}
, geometrically isomorphic
to the constant group scheme associated with
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}^{\oplus n}
.

Proof.

Since
\beta
is nonsingular, the equations for

\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
in
2.3
may be
written as

\mathbf{x}^{[2]}=B^{[1],\vee,-1}B\mathbf{x}.

This is a system of
n
equations in
n
variables, with Jacobian equations
the linear system given by
B^{[1],\vee,-1}B
. Since
\beta
is
nonsingular, this is of full rank, and so
\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
is
étale of degree
q^{2}n
over
\mathbf{k}
.
Since the group of points
\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}(\mathbf{k}^{\mathrm{sep}})

is a vector space over
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
by
2.1
,

\mathbf{A}V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
must be a form of the constant group scheme
associated with
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}^{\oplus n}
, see
[
Mil17
, 2.16]
.
∎

This implies that a nonsingular
q
-bic form is spanned by its Hermitian
vectors after a finite separable field extension. This is not true in general:

2.4
shows that a form of type

\mathbf{N}_{2k}
has no nonzero Hermitian vectors.

2.6 Proposition
. —

If
(V,\beta)
is a nonsingular
q
-bic form over a separably closed field

\mathbf{k}
, then the natural map

V_{\mathrm{Herm}}\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}}\mathbf{k}\to V
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The two
\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces have the same dimension by

2.5
, so it suffices to show that the map
is injective. If not, there is a linear relation in
V
of the form

v_{m+1}=a_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}v_{m}\quad\text{with}\;m\geq 1,\;v_{i}\in V_{\mathrm{Herm}},\;\text{and}\;a_{i}\in\mathbf{k}.

Choose such a relation with
m
minimal. Minimality implies that

v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
are linearly independent in
V
. Since
\beta
is
nonsingular, there exists
w\in V
such that
\beta(w^{[1]},v_{i})=0
for

1\leq i\leq m-1
, and
\beta(w^{[1]},v_{m})\neq 0
; up to scaling

v_{m}
, this last value may be taken to be
1
. Since the
v_{i}

are Hermitian,

a_{m}=\beta(w^{[1]},v_{m+1})=\beta(v_{m+1}^{[1]},w)^{q}\\
=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{q^{2}}\beta(v_{i}^{[1]},w)^{q}=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{q^{2}}\beta(w^{[1]},v_{i})=a_{m}^{q^{2}}

so
a_{m}\in\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
, whence

v_{m}^{\prime}\coloneqq v_{m+1}-a_{m}v_{m}
lies in
V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
by

2.1
. The relation

v_{m}^{\prime}=a_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+a_{m-1}v_{m-1}
then
contradicts the minimality of
m
, as required.
∎

In particular, since Hermitian forms over
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
always have
an orthonormal basis, this implies that all nonsingular
q
-bic forms over
a separably closed field have a Gram matrix given by the identity:

2.7 Corollary
. —

If
(V,\beta)
is a nonsingular
q
-bic form over a separably closed field,
then there exists a basis
V=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle
such that

\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})=\mathbf{1}^{\oplus n}
.
∎

When
\mathbf{k}
is separably closed,
V
is spanned by its Hermitian vectors

V_{\mathrm{Herm}}
by
2.6
, and so
the restriction map
\Aut(V,\beta)\to\Aut(V_{\mathrm{Herm}},\beta_{\mathrm{Herm}})
from the automorphism group of
a nonsingular
q
-bic form to that of its associated Hermitian form is an
isomorphism. Combined with
2.7
, Galois descent
implies that isomorphism classes of nonsingular
q
-bic forms of dimension

n
over a general field
\mathbf{k}
are in bijection with forms of the standard
Hermitian form on
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}^{\oplus n}
over
\mathbf{k}
. This implies:

2.8 Corollary
. —

Two nonsingular
q
-bic forms are isomorphic over
\mathbf{k}
if and only
if their group schemes of Hermitian vectors equipped with their
Hermitian forms are isomorphic over
\mathbf{k}
.
∎

3.
Canonical filtrations

Although a
q
-bic form
\beta
pairs distinct modules
M
and
M^{[1]}
,
the canonical
q
-linear map
M\to M^{[1]}
makes it possible to iterate
left and right orthogonals. This gives rise to two intrinsic filtrations whose
interactions ultimately encode the structure of
\beta
. After their
definition in
3.1
and

3.2
, a symmetry relation is established
in
3.6
, and a series of numerical invariants
of
\beta

and

—

The map

M^{[i]}
of the form:

\{0\}=\mathrm{P}_{-1}^{\prime}M^{[i]}\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}M^{[i]}\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{3}^{\prime}M^{[i]}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{i}^{\prime}M^{[i]}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{2}^{\prime}M^{[i]}\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{0}^{\prime}M^{[i]}=M^{[i]}.

Since the two filtrations are inductively defined via kernels of
\beta

and
\beta^{\vee}
, it is straightforward to check that their formation is
compatible with orthogonal decompositions:

3.3 Lemma
. —

If
(M,\beta)=(M^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\perp(M^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime})
is an orthogonal decomposition,
then for every
i
,

\mathrm{P}_{i}M=\mathrm{P}_{i}M^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{P}_{i}M^{\prime\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{P}_{i}^{\prime}M^{[i]}=\mathrm{P}_{i}^{\prime}M^{\prime[i]}\oplus\mathrm{P}_{i}^{\prime}M^{\prime\prime[i]}.\qed

3.4.
Example

—

Consider the
\perp
- and
\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtrations for the
standard forms defined in
1.3
. Note that the filtrations
are trivial when
(M,\beta)
is nondegenerate, since they are constructed by
taking iterated kernels of
\beta
and
\beta^{\vee}
. So by

3.3
, it remains to describe
the filtrations when
\beta
has a Gram matrix given by
\mathbf{N}_{n}

for some basis
\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle
of
M
. A direct
computation shows that the
\perp
-filtration has
n
steps, given for

0\leq i\leq n-1
by

\mathrm{P}_{i}M=\begin{cases}\phantom{\Big(}\bigoplus\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k}R\cdot e_{2\ell-1}&if $i=2k-1$, and\\
\Big(\bigoplus\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k}R\cdot e_{2\ell-1}\Big)\oplus\Big(\bigoplus\nolimits_{j=2k+1}^{n}R\cdot e_{j}\Big)&if $i=2k$.\end{cases}

In particular,
\mathrm{P}_{-}M=\mathrm{P}_{+}M
is the span of the odd-indexed
basis elements.

The
\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration similarly has
n
steps. Moreover,
in this special case, each step of the filtration descends to
M
, and is
given for
0\leq i\leq n-1
by

\mathrm{P}_{i}^{\prime}M=\begin{cases}\phantom{\Big(}\bigoplus\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k}R\cdot e_{n-2\ell+2}&if $i=2k-1$, and\\
\Big(\bigoplus\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k}R\cdot e_{n-2\ell+2}\Big)\oplus\Big(\bigoplus\nolimits_{j=1}^{n-2k}R\cdot e_{j}\Big)&if $i=2k$.\end{cases}

For the remainder of the Section, specialize to the situation of a

q
-bic form
(V,\beta)
on a finite-dimensional vector space over a
field
\mathbf{k}
.

3.5.
Symmetry

—

Dimensions of various pieces of the
\perp
- and

\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration provide a series of numerical
invariants for
(V,\beta)
; taking just the first pieces gives the familiar

\displaystyle\rank(V,\beta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\rank(\beta\colon V\to V^{[1],\vee})=\rank(\beta^{\vee}\colon V^{[1]}\to V^{\vee}),\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\corank(V,\beta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{k}}V-\rank(V,\beta)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{1}V=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{1}V^{[1]}.

In particular, the first pieces of the two filtrations have the same
dimension. As is also suggested by the examples in

3.4
, this dimensional symmetry persists
amongst higher pieces. To prove this, first observe that
restricting the
j
-th Frobenius twisted pairing

\beta^{[j]}\colon V^{[j+1]}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}V^{[j]}\to\mathbf{k}

to
\mathrm{P}_{i-1}V^{[j+1]}
and
\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}
and using

1.5
(ii)

twice gives an exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{P}_{i}V^{[j]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}\to\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}\xrightarrow{\beta^{[j]}}\mathrm{P}_{i-1}V^{[j+1],\vee}\to(\mathrm{P}_{i-1}V^{[j+1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j+1}^{\prime}V^{[j+1]})^{\vee}\to 0.

The symmetry statement is the following:

3.6 Proposition
. —

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{i}V^{[j]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V^{[i]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{i}^{\prime}V^{[i]}

for all integers
i,j\geq 0
.

Proof.

By symmetry, it suffices to consider
i\geq j\geq 0
. Proceed by
induction on
i+j
. The base case is when
i=j=i+j=0
so that
both sides are just
V
and there is nothing to prove. Now fix the quantity

i+j\geq 1
and further induct on the difference

\delta\coloneqq i-j\geq 0
. There are
two base cases: If
\delta=0
, then there is nothing to prove. If

\delta=1
, considering the sequence in

3.5
with
i=j+1
gives

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j+1}V^{[j]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}-\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V^{[j+1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j+1}^{\prime}V^{[j+1]}-\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V^{[j+1]}.

Since
j<i+j=2j+1
, induction applies to the negative terms to show

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{0}V^{[j]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V\cap\mathrm{P}_{0}^{\prime}V=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V

whence

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j+1}V^{[j]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V^{[j+1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j+1}^{\prime}V^{[j+1]}
.

Assume
\delta\geq 2
. Taking dimensions in the sequence of

3.5
gives the first equation in:

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{i}V^{[j]}\cap\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{j}V^{[j]}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{i-1}V^{[j+1]}\cap\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{j+1}V^{[j+1]}-\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{i-1}V^{[j+1]}+\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j+1}V^{[i-1]}\cap\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{i-1}V^{[i-1]}-\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{i-1}^{\prime}V^{[i-1]}+\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V^{[i]}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{j}V^{[i]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{i}^{\prime}V^{[i]}.

Since
i-1-(j+1)=\delta-2
and
\max\{i-1,j\}<i+j
, induction gives
the equality in the middle. The final equality follows from the sequence in

3.5
upon substituting
i\mapsto j+1

and
j\mapsto i-1
.
∎

3.7.
Numerical invariants

—

A sequence of numerical invariants of
(V,\beta)
is now obtained by taking
dimensions of graded pieces for the filtrations: For each integer
m\geq 0
,
set
\epsilon\coloneqq(-1)^{m}
, set

a_{m}(V,\beta)\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{m-\epsilon-1}V/\mathrm{P}_{m+\epsilon-1}V=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{m-\epsilon-1}^{\prime}V^{[i]}/\mathrm{P}_{m+\epsilon-1}^{\prime}V^{[m]},

and set
a(V,\beta)\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{+}V/\mathrm{P}_{-}V
. Since

V
is finite-dimensional,
a_{m}(V,\beta)
vanishes for large
m
.
Slightly more convenient are the successive differences of these dimensions:
for each
m\geq 0
, set

b_{m}(V,\beta)\coloneqq a_{m}(V,\beta)-a_{m+1}(V,\beta)\quad\text{so that}\quad a_{m}(V,\beta)=\sum\nolimits_{i\geq m}b_{i}(V,\beta).

In fact, these differences are also dimensions:

3.8 Lemma
. —

\displaystyle b_{m}(V,\beta)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\Bigg(\frac{\mathrm{P}_{m-\epsilon-1}V^{[1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}V^{[1]}}{\mathrm{P}_{m+\epsilon-1}V^{[1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}V^{[1]}}\Bigg)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\Bigg(\frac{\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[m]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{m-\epsilon-1}^{\prime}V^{[m]}}{\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[m]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{m+\epsilon-1}^{\prime}V^{[m]}}\Bigg)
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the definitions in
3.1

and
3.2
of the filtrations, and taking
dimensions of the exact sequence

1.5
(iii)

applied to the nested sequences of subspaces

\mathrm{P}_{m+\epsilon-1}V^{[1]}\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{m-\epsilon-1}V^{[1]}\subseteq V^{[1]}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{P}_{m+\epsilon-1}^{\prime}V^{[m]}\subseteq\mathrm{P}_{m-\epsilon-1}^{\prime}V^{[m]}\subseteq V^{[m]}

for the maps
\beta^{\vee}
and
\beta^{[m]}
, respectively.
∎

3.9.
Type

—

The sequence
(a(V,\beta);b_{m}(V,\beta))_{m\geq 1}
is the
type
of

(V,\beta)
and is the fundamental invariant: all other intrinsic numerical
invariants may be expressed in terms of the type. For instance,

\displaystyle\corank(V,\beta)

\displaystyle=a_{1}(V,\beta)=\sum\nolimits_{m\geq 1}b_{m}(V,\beta),\;\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbf{k}}V

\displaystyle=a(V,\beta)+\sum\nolimits_{m\geq 1}a_{m}(V,\beta)=a(V,\beta)+\sum\nolimits_{m\geq 1}mb_{m}(V,\beta).

Dimensions of each intersection

\mathrm{P}_{i}V^{[j]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{j}^{\prime}V^{[j]}
may be expressed
similarly. Particularly useful is the following direct consequence of

3.8
: for every integer
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{2k-1}V^{[1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}V^{[1]}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k}b_{2\ell-1}(V,\beta),\;\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{P}_{2k}V^{[1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}V^{[1]}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k}b_{2\ell-1}(V,\beta)+\sum\nolimits_{m\geq 2k+1}b_{m}(V,\beta).

Examining the description of the filtrations for standard forms given in

3.4
shows that this notion of type
matches that given for standard forms in
1.3
.
In particular,
a(V,\beta)
is the dimension of the nonsingular summand
of a standard form. This is true more generally in the following sense:

3.10 Lemma
. —

The restriction of
\beta
to
\mathrm{P}_{+}V
has

\mathrm{P}_{-}V
as its radical, and the induced
q
-bic form on

\mathrm{P}_{+}V/\mathrm{P}_{-}V
is nonsingular.

Proof.

It follows from definitions that

\mathrm{P}_{\pm}V=\mathrm{P}_{\mp}V^{[1],\perp}
, so the
kernels of
\beta
restricted to
\mathrm{P}_{+}V
are

\displaystyle\ker(\beta\colon\mathrm{P}_{+}V\to\mathrm{P}_{+}V^{[1],\vee})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P}_{+}V\cap\mathrm{P}_{+}V^{[1],\perp}=\mathrm{P}_{-}V,\;\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\ker(\beta^{\vee}\colon\mathrm{P}_{+}V^{[1]}\to\mathrm{P}_{+}V^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P}_{+}V^{[1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{+}V^{\perp}=\mathrm{P}_{+}V^{[1]}\cap(\mathrm{P}_{-}V^{[1]}+\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}V^{[1]}),

where the final equality follows from

1.5
(iv)
.
Since the two kernels have the same dimension, the latter must simply be

\mathrm{P}_{-}V^{[1]}
. Thus
\beta
restricted to
\mathrm{P}_{+}V
has

\mathrm{P}_{-}V
as its radical, and since this is the entire kernel,
the form induced on the quotient is nonsingular.
∎

3.11.
Descending the
\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration

—

Since
V
is finite-dimensional, the filtrations are finite, and
all pieces of the
\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration are defined on all
sufficiently large Frobenius twists of
V
. Thus

\nu(V,\beta)\coloneqq\min\Set{i\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}}{\text{$\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}$-filtration descends to}\;(V^{[i]},\beta^{[i]})\;\text{over $\mathbf{k}$}}

is well-defined. This depends on
\mathbf{k}
: for instance,

\nu(V,\beta)=0
for any form over a perfect field. The following
gives an
a priori
upper bound
\nu(V,\beta)\leq\nu_{0}
depending only
on the type of
\beta
. The statement is optimal, as can be shown by considering
infinitesimals in automorphism groups as in
5.14

and comparing with the examples
5.7
,

5.8
, and

\AutSch_{(\mathbf{k}^{\oplus 5},\mathbf{N}_{2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{3})}
.

3.12 Lemma
. —

Assume that
\beta
is degenerate and let

\mu\coloneqq\max\set{m\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 1}}{b_{m}(V,\beta)\neq 0}
. Then the

\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration canonically descends to
V^{[\nu_{0}]}

for

\nu_{0}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mu-2&if $\mu>1$ and $b_{m}(V,\beta)=0$ for all even $m$,\\
\mu-1&if $\mu$ is odd, or $\mu$ is even and $a(V,\beta)=0$, and\\
\mu&otherwise.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\mu
is the length of the
\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration, the
last case is clear. When
\mu
is even and
a(V,\beta)=0
, then

\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}=\mathrm{P}_{\mu-1}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}
, so the last step of the
filtration canonically descends to
V^{[\mu-1]}
. It remains to consider the
situation when
\mu
is odd. Since
b_{m}(V,\beta)=0
for all
m>\mu
the
formulae of
3.7
imply that

a_{\mu}(V,\beta)=b_{\mu}(V,\beta)
, so that by comparing dimensions via

3.8
, the natural injection

\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\mu]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}/\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\mu]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{\mu-2}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}/\mathrm{P}_{\mu-2}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}

is an isomorphism. This implies that
\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}
is spanned by

\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\mu]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}
and

\mathrm{P}_{\mu-2}^{\prime}V^{[\mu-2]}
. By the formulae in

3.9
, the intersection coincides with

\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\mu]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{\mu-1}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}
. Therefore

\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu-1]}=\mathrm{P}_{\mu-2}^{\prime}V^{[\mu-1]}+\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\mu-1]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{\mu-1}^{\prime}V^{[\mu-1]}

is a canonical descent of
\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu]}
to
V^{[\mu-1]}
.
Furthermore, when
\mu>1
and
b_{m}(V,\beta)=0
for all even
m
, the
intersection is all of
\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\mu-1]}
and so the sum
descends further to
V^{[\mu-2]}
. By

1.5
(iv)
,

\mathrm{P}_{\mu-1}^{\prime}V^{[\mu-3]}\coloneqq\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu-2],\perp^{[\mu-3]}}\coloneqq\ker\big(\beta^{[\mu-3]}\colon V^{[\mu-3]}\to\mathrm{P}_{\mu}^{\prime}V^{[\mu-2],\vee}\big)

and so the entire
\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration admits a descent
to
V^{[\mu-2]}
in this case.
∎

4.
Classification

The object of this Section is to prove the following Classification Theorem,
which says that, after passing to a suitable Frobenius twist upon which its

\perp^{[\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}]}
-filtration is defined as in
3.11
,
a
q
-bic form over a field is essentially classified by its type, as defined
in
3.9
:

4.1 Theorem
. —

Let
(V,\beta)
be a
q
-bic form over a field
\mathbf{k}
of
type
(a;b_{m})_{m\geq 1}
, and let
\nu\coloneqq\nu(V,\beta)
.
Then there exists an orthogonal decomposition

\beta^{[\nu]}=\beta_{0}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus\nolimits_{m\geq 1}\beta_{m}\Big)

such that
\beta_{0}
is nonsingular of dimension
a
, and
\beta_{m}

has a Gram matrix given by
\mathbf{N}_{m}^{\oplus b_{m}}
for each
m\geq 1
.

Combined with the classification of nonsingular forms in

2.7
and the remarks on
\nu
from

3.11
, this gives a normal form for
q
-bic forms over an
algebraically closed field:

4.2 Corollary
. —

If
\mathbf{k}
is algebraically closed, then there exists a basis

V=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle
such that

\Gram(\beta;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})=\mathbf{1}^{\oplus a}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus\nolimits_{m\geq 1}\mathbf{N}_{m}^{\oplus b_{m}}\Big).\qed

Throughout this Section, let
(V,\beta)
denote a
q
-bic form over a field

\mathbf{k}
. The idea of proof is as follows: Let
m\geq 1
be an integer, and
choose a subspace of dimension
b\coloneqq b_{m}(V,\beta)
in

\begin{cases}\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\nu]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{2k-1}^{\prime}V^{[\nu]}&if $m=2k-1$, or\\
\mathrm{P}_{1}V^{[\nu]}\cap\mathrm{P}_{2k-2}^{\prime}V^{[\nu]}&if $m=2k$,\end{cases}

lifting the quotient from
3.8
.
The seesaw relation between the two filtrations, as in

4.6
, extends this subspace to an

mb
-dimensional subspace of
V
on which
\beta
has a Gram matrix given
by
\mathbf{N}_{m}^{\oplus b}
, and which admits an orthogonal complement.
Since the filtrations are compatible with orthogonal decompositions,
inductively continuing this method on the complement yields the Theorem.

4.3.
Recognizing a standard form

—

An intrinsic formulation of the property that a
q
-bic form
(V,\beta)

has a Gram matrix given by
\mathbf{N}_{m}^{\oplus b}
is: there exists a
vector space decomposition
V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}
such that, for
integers
1\leq i,j,\leq m
, the map

\beta_{i,j}\colon V_{j}\subseteq V\xrightarrow{\beta}V^{[1],\vee}\twoheadrightarrow V_{i}^{[1],\vee}\;\text{is}\;\begin{cases}\text{an isomorphism}&if $j=i+1$, and\\
\text{zero}&otherwise.\end{cases}

Indeed, if
V
has a basis with Gram matrix
\mathbf{N}_{m}^{\oplus b}
,
set
V_{i}
to be the span of the vectors corresponding to the
i
-th column
of each
\mathbf{N}_{m}
block. Conversely, given the vector space decomposition,
begin with a basis of
V_{1}
and use the maps
\beta_{i,i+1}
to construct a
basis of
V
with the desired Gram matrix.

The following is a more flexible characterization of such forms:

4.4 Lemma
. —

Let
V
be an
mb
-dimensional vector space with decomposition

V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}
such that

(i)

V_{1}=\mathrm{P}_{1}V
and

V_{m}^{[1]}=\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\prime}V^{[1]}
,

(ii)

\beta_{1,2}\colon V_{2}\subseteq V\to V^{[1],\vee}\twoheadrightarrow V_{1}^{[1],\vee}

is an isomorphism, and

(iii)

\image(\beta\colon V_{i+1}\to V^{[1],\vee})=\image(\beta^{[1],\vee}\colon V_{i-1}^{[2]}\to V^{[1],\vee})

for each
1<i<m
.

Then
\beta
has a Gram matrix given by
\mathbf{N}_{m}^{\oplus b}
with

b\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{k}}V_{1}
.

Proof.

The task is to adjust the given decomposition of
V
so that the maps

\beta_{i,j}
from
4.3
are isomorphisms
when
j=i+1
and zero otherwise. Post-composing the maps appearing in

(iii)

with the restriction
V^{[1],\vee}\to V_{j}^{[1],\vee}
shows that:
for all
1<i<m
and
1\leq j\leq m
,

\rank(\beta_{j,i+1}\colon V_{i+1}\to V_{j}^{[1],\vee})=\rank(\beta_{i-1,j}\colon V_{j}\to V_{i-1}^{[1],\vee}).

The maps
\beta\colon V_{i+1}\to V^{[1],\vee}
and

\beta^{\vee}\colon V_{i-1}^{[1]}\to V^{\vee}
are injections for each

1<i<m
by assumption
(i)
, so the rank
equations combined with
(ii)
implies that each
subspace
V_{i}
is
b
-dimensional and that the maps
\beta_{i,i+1}
are
isomorphisms for each
1\leq i<m
. Since
\beta_{j,1}
and

\beta_{m,j}
are the zero maps for all
1\leq j\leq m
by

(i)
, the rank equations furthermore imply that

\beta_{i,j}=0\;\text{whenever $j\neq i+1$ and}\;\begin{cases}\phantom{\max\{i,j\}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\min\{i,j\}$}\equiv\hbox to0.0pt{$1$\hss}\phantom{m}\pmod{2},\\
\max\{i,j\}\equiv m\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

Adjust now the even-indexed subspaces via a Gram–Schmidt-like procedure,
in a way depending on the parity of
m
, to arrange for the remaining

\beta_{i,j}
to vanish.

Case
m
is odd.

It remains to arrange for
\beta_{i,j}=0

when both
i
and
j
are even. Let
1\leq k<m/2
and inductively
assume that
\beta_{i,j}=0
whenever
\min\{i,j\}<2k
. The task is to
modify the subspaces
V_{i}
with
i
even and
2k\leq i\leq m
so that
vanishing furthermore holds when
\min\{i,j\}=2k
.

Consider the subspace
V^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=2k}^{m}V_{i}
and let

\beta^{\prime}
be the restriction of
\beta
thereon. For

2k\leq i\leq m
, set
V_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq V_{i}
for odd
i
, and

5.7.

this Section is to characterize this partial ordering. As a first step,
rephrase this in terms of specialization relations for
q
-bic forms:

\mathbf{b}\geq\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
if and only if for some—equivalently, for
any—pair of
q
-bic forms
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
on
V
of types

\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
, respectively, there exists a discrete
valuation ring
R
over
\mathbf{k}
, a
q
-bic form
(M,\gamma)
over
R
,
and isomorphisms

(V_{K},\beta_{K})\cong(M_{K},{}_{K})\quad\text{and}\quad(V_{\kappa},\beta_{\kappa}^{\prime})\cong(M_{\kappa},{}_{\kappa})

as
q
-bic forms over the fraction field
K
and residue field
\kappa

of
R
, respectively. Denote this situation by

\beta\rightsquigarrow\beta^{\prime}
and say that
\beta
specializes to

\beta^{\prime}
.

The remainder of this Section will be phrased in terms of specialization
relations amongst
q
-bic forms on
V
, and the goal is to determine
necessary and sufficient conditions for a specialization

\beta\rightsquigarrow\beta^{\prime}
to exist. A sequence of necessary
conditions is obtained by combining the summation formula

5.16
with the fact that boundary strata in the
closure have smaller dimension:

6.2 Proposition
. —

If there exists a specialization
\beta\rightsquigarrow\beta^{\prime}
, then

\Phi_{m}(\beta)\leq\Phi_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
for all
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

Specializing subforms induces

\beta\oplus\gamma\rightsquigarrow\beta^{\prime}\oplus\gamma
for
all
q
-bic forms
(W,\gamma)
. Thus
6.1

gives

\dim\AutSch_{(V\oplus W,\beta\oplus\gamma)}\leq\dim\AutSch_{(V\oplus W,\beta^{\prime}\oplus\gamma)}.

According to
5.16
, the
dimensions of the automorphism groups grow linearly in the invariants of

\gamma
, so comparing coefficients of
d_{m}
gives the result.
∎

The following constructs a collection of basic specializations amongst
standard
q
-bic forms:

6.3 Lemma
. —

Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{2s+1}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-1},\qquad\mathbf{N}_{2s}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-1},\qquad\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-2}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2s},

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{2s-2t}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s+2}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2s-2t+2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s},\quad\quad\mathbf{N}_{2s+1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s+2t-1}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2s-1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s+2t+1}.

Proof.

Let

be a discrete valuation ring over
\mathbf{k}
with uniformizing

\pi

\mathbf{k}
, and fraction field

\scalebox{0.75}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\mathbf{N}_{2s-1}&{\begin{matrix}0&0\\[-6.00006pt]

Then the reduction of

-bic form of type

, it
suffices to compute the

-filtration of

on

\mathbf{N}_{2s+1}

The third Gram matrix satisfies

\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-2}
.

Completely analogous computations show that for the fourth and fifth
Gram matrices,

\mathbf{N}_{2s-2t}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s+2}
and

\mathbf{N}_{2s+1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s+2t-1}
, respectively.

6.5 Proposition
. —

Let
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
be
q
-bic forms of type
(a;b_{m})_{m\geq 1}

and
(a^{\prime};b_{m}^{\prime})_{m\geq 1}
, respectively. If

\Phi_{m}(\beta)\leq\Phi_{m}(\beta^{\prime})

and
\Theta_{m}(\beta)\leq\Theta_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
for all
m\geq 1
, then
there exists a specialization
\beta\rightsquigarrow\beta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For the arguments below, it is convenient to replace
\Phi_{m}
from

5.16
by

{}_{m}(\beta)\coloneqq\begin{cases}b_{2k-1}+2\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(k-\ell)b_{2\ell-1}&if $m=2k-1$, and\\
\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\ell b_{2\ell}+k\Big(\sum\nolimits_{\ell\geq 1}b_{2\ell-1}+\sum\nolimits_{\ell\geq k}b_{2\ell}\Big)&if $m=2k$,\end{cases}

in which redundant constants are removed, so that

\Phi_{m}(\beta)\leq\Phi_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
if and only if

{}_{m}(\beta)\leq{}_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
.

The argument proceeds in steps. Each time,

6.3
is applied to a subform of
\beta
to
produce an intermediate specialization
\beta^{\prime\prime}
. The new form will satisfy

{}_{m}(\beta^{\prime\prime})\leq{}_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
for all
m
, together with
successively stronger conditions that, in particular, imply

\Theta_{m}(\beta^{\prime\prime})\leq\Theta_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
for all
m
. Replace

\beta
by
\beta^{\prime\prime}
and continue in this manner until

{}_{m}(\beta)={}_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
for all
m
. This implies that

\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
are of the same type, at which point the
Proposition follows from the Classification Theorem

4.1
.

In what follows, abbreviate

{}_{m}(\beta)
,
{}_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
, and
{}_{m}(\beta^{\prime\prime})
to

m
,
{}_{m}^{\prime}
, and
{}^{\prime\prime}_{m}
;
similarly for
\Theta_{m}
, etc.

Step 1.

After a sequence of specializations of the form

\mathbf{N}_{2s+1}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-1},

may assume that
b_{2k-1}\leq b_{2k-1}^{\prime}
for every
k\geq 1
.

The inequalities
{}_{2k-1}\leq{}_{2k-1}^{\prime}
imply
that
b_{2k-1}
must be smaller than
b_{2k-1}^{\prime}
the first time they
differ. This implies: if
s
is minimal such that

b_{2s+1}>b_{2s+1}^{\prime}
, then
{}_{2s-1}<{}_{2s-1}^{\prime}
and

\Theta_{s}<\Theta_{s}^{\prime}
. The former inductively implies that

{}_{2k-1}<{}_{2k-1}^{\prime}
for every
k\geq s
since

{}_{2k+1}-{}_{2k-1}=\Theta_{k+1}+\Theta_{k-1}\leq\Theta_{k+1}^{\prime}+\Theta_{k-1}^{\prime}={}_{2k+1}^{\prime}-{}_{2k-1}^{\prime}

upon using the inequalities
\Theta_{m}\leq\Theta_{m}^{\prime}
for
m=k-1
and

m=k+1
. The latter shows that this inequality is strict for
k=s+1
,
implying that, furthermore,
{}_{2k+1}+1<{}_{2k+1}^{\prime}
for all

k\geq s+1
. So if
\beta^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
via a
specialization of the form

\mathbf{N}_{2s+1}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-1}
,
then

{}_{m}^{\prime\prime}=\begin{cases}{}_{2k-1}+1&if $m=2s-1$ or $m=2s+1$,\\
{}_{2k-1}+2&if $m=2k-1>2s+1$,\\
{}_{m}&otherwise,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta_{m}^{\prime\prime}=\begin{cases}\Theta_{s}+1&if $m=s$,\\
\Theta_{m}&otherwise,\end{cases}

and so with the inequalities above, this implies

{}_{m}^{\prime\prime}\leq{}_{m}^{\prime}
and
\Theta_{m}^{\prime\prime}\leq\Theta_{m}^{\prime}
for all
m
.

Step 2.

After a sequence of specializations of the form

\mathbf{N}_{2s}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2s-2t+1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2t-1}\quad\text{or}\quad\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2t-2s-1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2t-1},

may assume that
b_{2k-1}=b_{2k-1}^{\prime}
for every
k\geq 1
.

From now on, assume that
b_{2k-1}\leq b_{2k-1}^{\prime}
for all
k\geq 1
,
superseding the inequalities
\Theta_{m}\leq\Theta_{m}^{\prime}
. Note this also
implies
{}_{2k-1}\leq{}_{2k-1}^{\prime}
for all
k
, so it will suffice to
verify the inequalities
{}_{2k}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
.

To begin this Step, suppose first that the inequalities

(6.6)

\sum\nolimits_{k\geq m}b_{2k}\leq\sum\nolimits_{k\geq m}b_{2k}^{\prime}

are satisfied for every
m\geq 1
.
Comparing the formulae from
3.9
for

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}V
in terms of the invariants of

\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
, and successively applying the inequalities
(
6.6
) for increasing
m
gives
the inequality

(6.7)

a=a^{\prime}+\sum\nolimits_{i\geq 1}i(b_{i}^{\prime}-b_{i})\geq a^{\prime}+\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}(2k-1)(b_{2k-1}^{\prime}-b_{2k-1}).

Let
t
be any index such that
b_{2t-1}<b_{2t-1}^{\prime}
. Then
(
6.7
) implies

a\geq 2t-1
, meaning
\beta
contains a subform of type

\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2t-1}
. Let
\beta^{\prime\prime}
be obtained via a specialization

\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2t-1}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2t-1}
. The choice of

t
ensures that
b_{2k-1}^{\prime\prime}\leq b_{2k-1}^{\prime}
for all
k
. Combined with
the
m=1
case of (
6.6
), this
implies that

{}_{2}^{\prime\prime}=\sum\nolimits_{i\geq 1}b_{i}^{\prime\prime}\leq\sum\nolimits_{i\geq 1}b_{i}^{\prime}={}_{2}^{\prime}.

Then, for each
k\geq 1
, the
m=k+1
case of
(
6.6
) gives inequalities

(6.8)

{}_{2k+2}^{\prime\prime}-{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}=\sum\nolimits_{\ell\geq 1}b_{2\ell-1}^{\prime\prime}+\sum\nolimits_{\ell\geq k+1}b_{2\ell}^{\prime\prime}\leq\sum\nolimits_{\ell\geq 1}b_{2\ell-1}^{\prime}+\sum\nolimits_{\ell\geq k+1}b_{2\ell}^{\prime}={}_{2k+2}^{\prime}-{}_{2k}^{\prime}.

Starting from
{}_{2}^{\prime\prime}\leq{}_{2}^{\prime}
, this inductively implies that

{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
for all
k\geq 1
.

It remains to consider the situation when at least one of the inequalities in
(
6.6
) fails. Since

b_{2k}=b_{2k}^{\prime}=0
for large
k
, there is a maximal
s
such that

\sum\nolimits_{k\geq s}b_{2k}>\sum\nolimits_{k\geq s}b_{2k}^{\prime}.

Comparing this with (
6.6
)
where
m=s+1
gives
b_{2s}>b_{2s}^{\prime}
. Now consider two cases:

Case 2A.

There exists
t\leq s
such that
b_{2t-1}<b_{2t-1}^{\prime}
.

Let
\beta^{\prime\prime}
be any specialization of
\beta
obtained via

\mathbf{N}_{2s}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2s-2t+1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2t-1}
.
Then
{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}={}_{2k}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
for all
k\leq s
.
Step 1 together with maximality of
s
implies that
(
6.8
) holds for all
k\geq s
, which
then inductively implies that

{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
also holds for
k>s
.

Case 2B.

Every
k
for which
b_{2k-1}<b_{2k-1}^{\prime}
satisfies
k>s
.

Applying the inequality
{}_{2s}^{\prime}-{}_{2s}\geq 0
twice to the middle
term of (
6.7
) gives the inequality

a\geq a^{\prime}+\sum\nolimits_{k\geq s+1}(2k-2s)(b_{2k}^{\prime}-b_{2k})+\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}(2k-2s-1)(b_{2k-1}^{\prime}-b_{2k-1}).

The first sum on the right is nonnegative by
(
6.6
) and maximality of
s
. The
assumption in this case implies that the second sum is nonnegative, and that if

t
is any index such that
b_{2t-1}<b_{2t-1}^{\prime}
, then in fact

a\geq 2t-2s-1>0
. In other words,
\beta
contains

\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2t-2s-1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s}
as a subform;
let
\beta^{\prime\prime}
be obtained by specializing such a subform to
\mathbf{N}_{2t-1}
.
Arguing as above shows that
{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
for all
k
.

Step 3.

After a sequence of specializations of the form

\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-2}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2s}

may assume that

\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}kb_{2k}=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}kb_{2k}^{\prime}
.

After Step 2, by passing to subforms, it may be assumed that

b_{2k-1}=b_{2k-1}^{\prime}=0
for all
k
.

Let
s\geq 1
be maximal such that
{}_{2s-2}={}_{2s-2}^{\prime}
,
setting
{}_{0}={}_{0}^{\prime}=0
so that the maximum always exists. If

s=\max\set{k}{b_{2k-2}\neq 0\;\text{or}\;b_{2k-2}^{\prime}\neq 0}
,
then this is equivalent to

\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}kb_{2k}=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}kb_{2k}^{\prime}
,
as required. Otherwise,

\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}kb_{2k}<\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}kb_{2k}^{\prime}
,
so combined with the first equation in (
6.7
),

a=a^{\prime}+2\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}k(b_{2k}^{\prime}-b_{2k})\geq 2.

Since
{}_{2k}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
for all
k
with strict inequality for

k\geq s
, it follows that

b_{2s-2}=2{}_{2s-2}-{}_{2s-4}-{}_{2s}>2{}_{2s-2}^{\prime}-{}_{2s-4}^{\prime}-{}_{2s}^{\prime}=b_{2s-2}^{\prime}.

Thus, in this case,

\beta
contains a subform of type
\mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-2}
;
let
\beta^{\prime\prime}
be obtained by specializing it to a form of
type
\mathbf{N}_{2s}
. Then
{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}={}_{2k}
for

1\leq k\leq s-1
and
{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}={}_{2k}+1
for
k\geq s
, so
maximality of
s
, implies
{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
for all
k
.

Step 4.

After a sequence of specializations of the form

\mathbf{N}_{2s}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-2}

may assume that
\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}b_{2k}=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}b_{2k}^{\prime}
.

Let
s\geq 1
be minimal such that
{}_{2s}={}_{2s}^{\prime}
. If
s=1
, this
is equivalent to the desired equality. Otherwise, as in Step 3,

b_{2s}=2{}_{2s}-{}_{2s-2}-{}_{2s+2}>2{}_{2s}^{\prime}-{}_{2s-2}^{\prime}-{}_{2s+2}^{\prime}=b_{2s}^{\prime}.

Therefore
\beta
contains a subform of type
\mathbf{N}_{2s}
; specialize
it to
\mathbf{N}_{2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-2}
to obtain
\beta^{\prime\prime}
. Then

{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}={}_{2k}+1
for
1\leq k<s
and

{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}={}_{2k}
for
k\geq s
, so the choice
of
s
implies
{}_{2k}^{\prime\prime}\leq{}_{2k}^{\prime}
for all
k
.

Step 5.

After a sequence of specializations of the form

\mathbf{N}_{2t}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2t+2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s-2}

where
s>t
, may assume that

{}_{m}={}_{m}^{\prime}

6.9.
Remarks

—

First, the hypotheses of
6.5

may be relaxed: it is enough that there is some specialization

\beta\rightsquigarrow\beta^{\prime\prime}
such that

\Theta_{m}(\beta^{\prime\prime})\leq\Theta_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
for all
m
. For instance, the
specialization

\mathbf{N}_{3}^{\oplus 2}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{5}

found at the right end of Figure
2
does not
satisfy the inequality
\Theta_{2}
,
but nonetheless exists by
6.3
; more generally,
any immediate specialization via

\mathbf{N}_{2s+1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s+2t-1}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{N}_{2s-1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{2s+2t+1},

the fifth basic specialization of
6.3
, will
violate some of the inequalities
\Theta_{m}
. Note further that the proof of

6.5
did not incorporate these
specializations at all.

Second, there are examples of
q
-bic forms
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}

that satisfy the inequalities
\Phi_{m}(\beta)\leq\Phi_{m}(\beta^{\prime})
, but
cannot be specialized to one another via a sequence of basic specializations
from
6.3
. The first examples appear in
dimension
15
and corank
3
; for instance, consider

\beta\coloneqq\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{3}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{8}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathbf{N}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{7}^{\oplus 2}.

I do not know whether or not
\beta
specializes to
\beta^{\prime}
.

To go further, it would be interesting to study
q
-bic forms over
discrete valuation rings and the fine geometry of the closure of

q\operatorname{\bf\!-bics}_{V,\mathbf{b}}
. For the former, it may be helpful to reformulate
the canonical filtrations in terms of quotients so as to deal with jumps
in types upon specialization. For the latter, it would be interesting to
know the degrees and singularities of these varieties.

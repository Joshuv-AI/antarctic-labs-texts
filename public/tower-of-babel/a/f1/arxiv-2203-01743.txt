New power-law tailed distributions emerging in κ \kappa -statistics
G. Kaniadakis

Abstract
Over the last two decades, it has been argued that the Lorentz transformation mechanism, which imposes the generalization of Newton’s classical mechanics into Einstein’s special relativity, implies a generalization, or deformation, of the ordinary statistical mechanics. The exponential function, which defines the Boltzmann’s factor, emerges properly deformed within this formalism. Starting from this, so-called κ \kappa -deformed exponential function, we introduce new classes of statistical distributions emerging as the κ \kappa -deformed version of already known distribution as the Generalized Gamma, Weibull, Logistic which can be adopted in the analysis of statistical data that exhibit power-law tails.

I
Introduction

There is a vast phenomenology related to experimental statistical distributions with a bulk described by well known theoretical exponential models, in the following called classical models, like the Generalized Gamma distribution, the Weibull distribution, the Logistic distribution etc, while it seems that their tails are gradually substituted by fat, not exponential tails following Pareto’s law
p(x)=A\,x^{-b}
,
PowerLawKaniadakis
(and references therein). This anomaly regards not only physical systems but also other natural or artificial systems.

Let us focus on a specific classical model as for instance the Generalized Gamma distribution though discussion can be extended also to Weibull and Logistic models. It is important to emphasize that the Gamma model and the Pareto model refer to two distinct spectral regions, namely the lower and the upper region of the spectrum respectively. This dichotomy gives rise to the question about whether the two distributions are actually two approximations, in the low and in the high spectral regions respectively, of a unique theoretical distribution, which hold over in the whole spectrum. A further question that arises spontaneously is whether there exists a simple and transparent underlying mechanism, based on first principle, that generates this unique distribution.

With reference to physical systems, the metaphor of relativistic physics is of great help in the search for the mechanism that generates a statistical distribution holding at both low and high energies.
It should be recalled that the laws of relativistic physics and the involved physical quantities emerge as generalizations of the corresponding ones of classical physics. For instance, the dimensionless relativistic kinetic energy is given by
{\cal E}_{\kappa}=\left(\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}q^{2}}-1\right)/{\kappa^{2}}
, where
q

\kappa

\kappa\rightarrow 0

{\cal E}_{0}\approx q^{2}/2

q\rightarrow+\infty

{\cal E}_{\kappa}\approx q/\kappa

When one moves from classical to relativistic physics, not only the expressions of the various physical quantities (momentum, velocity, total energy, force etc) but also all the mathematical tools of the theory (relativistic additivity law of velocities, momenta additivity law, Lorentz invariant integral, relativistic derivative etc) emerge properly generalized or deformed. This
\kappa

\displaystyle G_{\kappa}=1-\frac{\alpha+\kappa}{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\kappa}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)}\ \ .

(3.50)

The mode of the pdf is located at

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!x_{mode}\,=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\beta^{-1/\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}+2\kappa^{2}(\alpha-1)}{2\kappa^{2}(\alpha^{2}-\kappa^{2})}\right)^{\!1/2\alpha}

(3.51)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\times\!\left(\!\sqrt{1+\frac{4\kappa^{2}(\alpha^{2}-\kappa^{2})(\alpha-1)^{2}}{[\alpha^{2}+2\kappa^{2}(\alpha-1)]^{2}}}-1\!\right)^{\!1/2\alpha}\!\!\!\!,

if
\alpha>1
; for
\alpha=1
the pdf is a monotonically decreasing function
p_{\kappa}(0)=\beta
; otherwise, the distribution is zero-modal with a pole at the origin.

The quantile function, after inversion of the cdf given by Eq. (
3.42
), is available in the following closed form

\displaystyle x_{\kappa}(P)=\beta^{-1/\alpha}\left(\ln_{\kappa}\frac{1}{1-P}\right)^{1/\alpha}\ \ ,

(3.52)

with
0\leq P\leq 1
and
\ln_{\kappa}t=(t^{\kappa}-t^{-\kappa})/2\kappa
the
\kappa
-logarithm function. Starting from the expression of the quantile function and after posing
P=1/2
, it obtains the median of the distribution as
x_{med}=\beta^{1/\alpha}\left(\ln_{\kappa}2\right)^{1/\alpha}
.

The asymptotic behavior of
p_{\,\kappa}(x)
, is defined by

\displaystyle p_{\,\kappa}(x)\,\,{\atop\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle\sim}}{{\scriptstyle x\rightarrow+\infty}}}\,\,\,\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}\,(2\,\kappa\,\beta)^{-1/\kappa}\,\,x^{-1-\alpha/\kappa}\ \ ,

(3.53)

\displaystyle p_{\,\kappa}(x)\,\,{\atop\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle\sim}}{{\scriptstyle x\rightarrow 0^{+}}}}\,\,\,\alpha\,\beta\,\,\,x^{\alpha-1}\ \ .

(3.54)

III.1
Distribution of Type
{\rm II_{1}}

The cdf and the pdf given by Eqs. (
3.42
) and (
3.43
) in the special case
\alpha=1
represent a
\kappa
-deformation of the ordinary exponential distribution. In this case the moment of order
m<1/\kappa
simplifies to

\displaystyle<x^{m}>\,=\frac{\beta^{-m}\,m!}{\prod_{n=0}^{m}[1-(2n-m)\,\kappa]}\,\,\ \ .

(3.55)

In particular the mean value and the variance of
p_{\kappa}(x)
assumes a very simple form and are given by

\displaystyle<x>=\frac{1}{\beta}\,\frac{1}{1-\kappa^{2}}\ \ ;\ \ \sigma^{2}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\,\frac{1+2\kappa^{4}}{(1-4\kappa^{2})(1-\kappa^{2})^{2}}\ \ .

(3.56)

The function

{\cal L}_{\kappa}(P)=\int_{\,0}^{x_{\kappa}(P)}\,t\,p(t)\,dt\,/\int_{\,0}^{\infty}\,t\,p(t)\,dt
defines the Lorenz curve which assumes the explicite form

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\kappa}(P)=1+\frac{1-\kappa}{2\kappa}(1-P)^{1+\kappa}-\frac{1+\kappa}{2\kappa}(1-P)^{1-\kappa}\ \ ,

(3.57)

while the Gini coefficient simplifies to

\displaystyle G_{\kappa}=\frac{2+\kappa^{2}}{4-\kappa^{2}}\ \ .

(3.58)

III.2
Distribution of Type
{\rm II_{2}}

Corresponds to
\alpha=2
and defines the
\kappa
-deformed version of Rayleigh distribution.

IV
Distribution of Type
{\rm III}

The cdf of the model of Type III is defined for
x\geq 0
, according to

P_{\kappa}(x)\!=\!\frac{\int_{0}^{x}\!h(t)\exp_{\kappa}[-f(t)]\,dt}{\int_{0}^{x}\!h(t)\exp_{\kappa}[-f(t)]\,dt\!+\!\lambda\int_{x}^{\infty}\!h(t)\exp_{\kappa}[-f(t)]\,dt}\ ,

(4.59)

where
\lambda>0
, while the functions
f(x)
and
h(x)
are the same of the model of Type II and are given by Eq.(
2.3
) and Eq. (
3.41
) respectively. It results that the model of Type II, emerges to be a special case of the model of Type III, corresponding to
\lambda=1
called also model of Type III
1
.

After performing the integrals in Eq. (
4.59
), the obtaining cdf and the corresponding pdf assume the forms

\displaystyle P_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{1-\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})}{1+(\lambda-1)\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})}\ \ ,

(4.60)

\displaystyle p_{\kappa}(x)=\lambda\,h_{\kappa}(x)\,\frac{\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})}{\big[1+(\lambda-1)\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})\big]^{2}}\ \ ,\ \ \ \

(4.61)

while the survival function
S_{\kappa}(x)\!=\!1\!-\!P_{\kappa}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle S_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{\lambda}{\exp_{\kappa}(\beta x^{\alpha})+\lambda-1}\ \ .

(4.62)

By direct comparison of Eq. (
4.61
) and Eq. (
4.62
)
it follows the relation linking
p_{\kappa}(x)
and
S_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle p_{\kappa}(x)=h_{\kappa}(x)\,S_{\kappa}(x)\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}S_{\kappa}(x)\right)\ \ ,

(4.63)

and finally after recalling that
p_{\kappa}(x)=-d\,S_{\kappa}(x)/dx
the evolution equation for the
S_{\kappa}(x)
assume the form

\displaystyle\frac{d\,S_{\kappa}(x)}{dx}=-h_{\kappa}(x)\,S_{\kappa}(x)\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}S_{\kappa}(x)\right)\ \ ,

(4.64)

with
S_{\kappa}(0)=1
. The solution of this equation produces just the expression of
S_{\kappa}(x)
as given by Eq. (
4.62
).

The cumulative hazard function
H_{\kappa}(x)
is the same of the model of Type II and is given by Eq. (
3.48
). After performing a variable change the rate equation (
4.64
) semplifies to
\frac{dS_{\kappa}}{dH_{\kappa}}=-S_{\kappa}\left(1\!-\!\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}S_{\kappa}\right)
with solution given by
S_{\kappa}=\lambda/\left(e^{H_{\kappa}}+\lambda-1\right)
. From this rate equation it follows that also the
\kappa
-deformed model of Type III continuous to describe a population kinetics with a bosonic
(0<\lambda<1)
or fermionic
(\lambda>1)
character like in the case of the ordinary undeformed model.

IV.1
Distribution of Type
{\rm III_{2}}

In the special case where
\lambda=2
and
\alpha=1
, the cdf defined for
x\geq 0
reads

\displaystyle P_{\,\kappa}\,(x)=\frac{1-\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x)}{1+\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x)}\ \ ,

(4.65)

and can be viewed as the
\kappa
-deformed version of the ordinary half-Logistic model which recover in the

), is equal to two times the

V
Distributions of Type
{\rm IV}

After recalling the relationship
\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})\approx(2\kappa\beta x^{\alpha})^{-1/\kappa}
, with
\alpha>0
, holding for
x\rightarrow\infty
, the following cdf of Type IV

\displaystyle P_{\kappa}(x)=(2\kappa\beta)^{1/\kappa}\,\,x^{\alpha/\kappa}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})\ \ ,

(5.67)

can be introduced. The related pdf assumes the form

\displaystyle p_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}\,\,(2\beta\kappa)^{1/\kappa}\!\left(1-\frac{\kappa\,\beta\,x^{{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2{\alpha}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\,x^{-1+\alpha/\kappa}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta\,x^{\alpha})\ \ .

(5.68)

It is noteworthy that the above distributions do not admit a classic counterpart because in the
\kappa\rightarrow 0
limit, both
P_{\kappa}(x)
and
p_{\kappa}(x)
reduces to zero.

The asymptotic behavior of
P_{\kappa}(x)
is described by

\displaystyle P_{\,\kappa}(x)\,\,{\atop\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle\sim}}{{\scriptstyle x\rightarrow 0^{+}}}}\,\,\,(2\,\kappa\,\beta)^{1/\kappa}\,x^{\alpha/\kappa}\ \ ,

(5.69)

\displaystyle P_{\,\kappa}(x)\,\,{\atop\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle\sim}}{{\scriptstyle x\rightarrow+\infty}}}\,\,\,1-\frac{x^{-2\alpha}}{4\kappa^{3}\beta^{2}}\,\,\ \ .

(5.70)

The moment of order
m<2\alpha
, of
p_{\kappa}(x)

SupplementaryMaterial
, is given by

\displaystyle<x^{m}>\,=\frac{(2\kappa\beta)^{-m/\alpha}}{1+\kappa m/2\alpha}\,\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)\,\Gamma\left(1-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{m}{2\alpha}\right)}\ \ .

(5.71)

VI
Distributions of Type V

Let us consider the derivative
p^{(n)}(x)
, of order
n
, of
the function
p\,(x)=p^{(0)}(x)
, in following called generatrix function, and suppose that for
0\leq n\leq N
has the properties i)

p^{(n)}(0)
is finite ii) the function
p^{(n)}(x)/p^{(n)}(0)
is
positive and monotonically decreasing in
x>0
, iii)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}p^{(n)}(x)\,dx<+\infty
.

We postulate the following distributions of order
n\geq 1

\displaystyle P(n,x)=1-\frac{p^{(n-1)}(x)}{p^{(n-1)}(0)}\ \ ;\ \ p\,(n,x)=-\frac{p^{(n)}(x)}{p^{(n-1)}(0)}\ \ ,

(6.72)

where
P(n,x)
is the cdf and
p\,(n,x)=\frac{d\,P(n,x)}{dx}
the pdf.

The relationship

\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!x^{m}\,p\,(n,x)\,dx=-m\frac{p^{(n-2)}(0)}{p^{(n-1)}(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!x^{m-1}\,p\,(n-1,x)\,dx\ \ ,

(6.73)

if iterated
m
times permits to obtain the moment of order
m
of
p\,(n,x)
, with
0\leq m\leq n-1
, as follows

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{m}\,p\,(n,x)\,dx=(-1)^{m}\,m!\,\frac{p^{\,(n-1-m)}(0)}{p^{\,(n-1)}(0)}\ \ .

(6.74)

The procedure described here can be applied to any distribution and
can produce in some case new distributions. In the simplest case of the ordinary exponential distribution this procedure does not produce new distributions.

Here after, by applying this procedure, we introduce the family of distributions of Type V, having as generatrix function the pdf of Type
I_{1}
. For
x\rightarrow\infty
the pdf
p_{\,\kappa}(n,x)
of Type V behave as
p_{\,\kappa}(n,x)\propto x^{-n-\frac{1}{\kappa}}
. In the following we consider the fist three elements of the family. The first element i.e. the distribution of order
n=1
, is just the already discussed distribution of Type
II_{1}
.

The distributions of order
n=2
and
n=3
, are new and the related cumulative and probability density functions are given by:

\displaystyle P_{\,\kappa}(2,x)=1-\frac{\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta\,x)}{\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2}}}\ \ ,

(6.75)

\displaystyle p_{\,\kappa}(2,x)=\left[\frac{1}{1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta x}{(1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2})^{3/2}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\,\beta\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta\,x)\ \ ,

(6.76)

\displaystyle P_{\,\kappa}(3,x)=1-\left[\frac{1}{1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta x}{(1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2})^{3/2}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta\,x)\ \ ,

(6.77)

\displaystyle p_{\,\kappa}(3,x)=\bigg[\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{(1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{3\kappa^{2}\beta x}{(1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{3\kappa^{4}\beta^{2}x^{2}}{(1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2})^{5/2}}\bigg]\beta\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta\,x)\ \ .

(6.78)

The mean value and the variance of the pdf of
p_{\,\kappa}(2,x)
are respectively
<x>\,=\frac{1}{\beta}
and
\sigma^{2}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\,\frac{1+\kappa^{2}}{1-\kappa^{2}}
.
It is remarkable that the mean value and the variance of
p_{\,\kappa}(3,x)
do not depend on the parameter
\kappa
and are the same of the ordinary exponential function i.e.
<x>\,=\frac{1}{\beta}
,
\sigma^{2}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}
.

In the
\kappa\rightarrow 0
limit it obtains
P_{0}(n,x)=1-\exp(-\beta x)
and
p_{0}(n,x)=\beta\exp(-\beta x)
holding for
n\in N
.

VIII.1
Mellin transform of
\exp_{\kappa}(-x)

Here it is obtained the Mellin transform of the function
\exp_{\kappa}(-x)=(\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}x^{2}}-\kappa x)^{1/\kappa}
i.e. its moment of order
r-1

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\kappa}(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r-1}\exp_{\kappa}(-x)\,dx\ \ .

(8.79)

After performing a variable change through the transformation
w=\left(\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}x^{2}}-\kappa x\right)^{2}
from which it follows
x=\frac{1}{2\kappa}w^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-w)
,
dx=-\frac{1}{4\kappa}w^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+w)\,dw
and
\exp_{\kappa}(-x)=w^{\frac{1}{2\kappa}}
.

The integration can be performed as follows

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\kappa}(r)\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\kappa}\,(2\kappa)^{1-r}\!\int_{0}^{1}{w}^{\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}-1}\left(1-w\right)^{r-1}\left(1+w\right)\,dw

(8.80)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,(2\kappa)^{-r}\!\int_{0}^{1}{w}^{\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}-1}\left(1-w\right)^{r-1}\,dw

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{2}\,(2\kappa)^{-r}\!\int_{0}^{1}{w}^{\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}}\left(1-w\right)^{r-1}\,dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,(2\kappa)^{-r}\,B\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}\,\,,\,\,r\right)

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{2}\,(2\kappa)^{-r}\,B\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}+1\,\,,\,\,r\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,(2\kappa)^{-r}\,\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}\right)\,\Gamma\left(r\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{r}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{2}\,(2\kappa)^{-r}\,\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}+1\right)\,\Gamma\left(r\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{r}{2}+1\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,(2\kappa)^{-r}\,\left(1+\frac{\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}}{\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{r}{2}}\right)\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{r}{2}\right)}\,\Gamma\left(r\right),

obtaining in this way the Mellin transform of
\exp_{\kappa}(-x)
in the form

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\kappa}(r)=\frac{(2\kappa)^{-r}}{1+\kappa r}\,\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{r}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{r}{2}\right)}\,\,\Gamma\left(r\right)\ \ .

(8.81)

holding for
r<1/\kappa
. In the
\kappa\rightarrow 0
limit, after taking into account
the relationship
\Gamma\left(z-c\right)/\,\Gamma\left(z+c\right)\approx z^{-2c}

holding in the Stirling approximation for
z\rightarrow\infty
, it obtains
{\cal M}_{0}(r)=\Gamma(r)
.

VIII.2
Normalization constant of the the distribution of Type I

The normalization constant
N_{\kappa}
appearing in the expression of the pdf

\displaystyle p_{\,\kappa}\,(x)=N_{\kappa}\,\,x^{\alpha\nu-1}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})\ \ ,

(8.82)

follows from the condition
\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{\kappa}(x)=1
according to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\kappa}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha\nu-1}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})\,dx\ \ .

(8.83)

After introducing the variable change through
w=\beta x^{\alpha}
from which follows that
x=\beta^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}w^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
and
dx=\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}w^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\,dw
, the integral defining the normalization constant becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!=\frac{1}{\alpha\beta^{\,\nu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\nu-1}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-w)\,dw\noindent

(8.84)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!=\frac{1}{\alpha\beta^{\,\nu}}{\cal M}_{\kappa}(\nu)\ \ .

(8.85)

After takining into account the expression of
{\cal M}_{\kappa}(\nu)
it follows

\displaystyle N_{\kappa}=(1+\kappa\,\nu)\,(2\,\kappa)^{\nu}\,\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)}\,\,\frac{\alpha\,\beta^{\,\nu}}{\Gamma\!\left(\nu\right)}\ \ ,

(8.86)

with
\nu<1/\kappa
.

VIII.3
Moment of the distribution of Type I

The moment of order
m
,
<x^{m}>\,=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{m}\,p_{\kappa}(x)\,dx

for the pdf of Type I

\displaystyle p_{\,\kappa}\,(x)=N_{\kappa}\,\,x^{\alpha\nu-1}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})\,dx\ \ ,

(8.87)

is defined according to

\displaystyle<x^{m}>\,=N_{\kappa}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{m+\alpha\nu-1}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta x^{\alpha})\ \ .

(8.88)

After introducing the variable change
w=\beta x^{\alpha}
, the moment assume form

\displaystyle<x^{m}>\,\,=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!N_{\kappa}\,\,\frac{1}{\alpha\beta^{\,\nu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\frac{m}{\alpha}+\nu-1}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-w)\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!N_{\kappa}\,\,\frac{1}{\alpha\beta^{\,\nu}}\,\,{\cal M}_{\kappa}\left(\nu+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)\ \ .

Finally by taking into account the expressions of the normalization constant

and the Mellin transform
{\cal M}_{\kappa}\left(\nu+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)
, obtained previously, the moment of order
m
of

assumes the form

\displaystyle<x^{m}>

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=(2\kappa\beta)^{-\frac{m}{\alpha}}\,\frac{1+\kappa\nu}{1+\kappa\left(\nu+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)}

(8.90)

m

VIII.5
Moment of the distribution of Type IV

Let us consider the moment of order
m
, defined through the relationship
<x^{m}>\,=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{m}\,p_{\kappa}(x)\,dx
, of the pdf

\displaystyle p_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}\,\,(2\beta\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\!\left(1-\frac{\kappa\,\beta\,x^{{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}x^{2{\alpha}}}}\right)x^{\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}-1}

\displaystyle\times\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta\,x^{\alpha})\ \ .

(8.97)

After substitution of the expression of
p_{\kappa}(x)
in the integral defining the moment and after performing the variable change
t=\beta x^{\alpha}
it obtains

\displaystyle<x^{m}>\,=2^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\,{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}-1}\,{\beta}^{-\frac{m}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}{t}^{\frac{m}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\kappa}-1}\left(1-\frac{\kappa\,t}{\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}t^{2}}}\right)

(8.98)

\displaystyle\times\exp_{\kappa}(-t)\,dt\ \ .

A farther variable change is introduced at this point through
w=\left(\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}t^{2}}-\kappa t\right)^{2}
. From this transformation it obtains
t=\frac{1}{2\kappa}w^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-w)
,
dt=-\frac{1}{4\kappa}w^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+w)\,dw
,
\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}t^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}w^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+w)
and
\exp_{\kappa}(-t)=w^{\frac{1}{2\kappa}}
.

Therefore the moment assumes the form

\displaystyle<x^{m}>\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa}\,(2\kappa\beta)^{-\frac{m}{\alpha}}\!\int_{0}^{1}\!{w}^{\left(1-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\right)-1}\left(1-w\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)-1}\,dw

(8.99)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa}\,(2\kappa\beta)^{-\frac{m}{\alpha}}\,B\left(1-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\,,\,\,\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa}\,(2\kappa\beta)^{-\frac{m}{\alpha}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)\,\Gamma\left(1-\frac{m}{2\alpha}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{m}{2\alpha}\right)}\ \ .

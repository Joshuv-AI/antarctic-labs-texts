Mixed Hodge Structure on Theta divisor
Mohammad Reza Rahmati

Abstract
. We explain the mixed Hodge structure (MHS) on the complement of the generalized theta divisor of a curve in its generalized jacobians. Our approach considers the generalized jacobian via degeneration of smooth jacobians as a determinantal varieties. We make the question if the MHS can be explained by graphs or their degenerations.

Introduction

The geometry of the theta divisor of curves is related to many parts of mathematics and has been studied from many different view points. The concept has also been studied in Hodge theory and the degenerations of mixed Hodge structure (MHS). As singular curves can be considered as degeneration of smooth curves in a family, the generalized jacobian can be a degeneration of smooth jacobians. In this text we describe the MHS on the cohomologies of the complement of generalized
\Theta
-divisors
on (compactification of) the generalized Jacobians of curves. We define singular curves useing modulus on projective curves. Using the Leray spectral sequence argument for the universal Poincare line bundle of curves one can show that the theta divisor can be defined by a determinant section of the jacobian.

The complement of hypersurfaces in the projective space or varieties carries a mixed Hodge structure. The associated MHS is usually studied on a compactification of quasiprojective variety by normal crossing divisors. We explain this as the degeneration of the spectral sequence of the double complex associated to the de Rham cohomogy of the natural stratification along the normal crossing divisors. For this reason we we compactify the generalized theta divisor and consider the complement of its determinant section.

On the other hand There exists combinatorial way to define mixed Hodge structures for complements of the hypersurface sections of projective varieties. In fact any graphs defines a hypersurface by a homogeneous equation that can be described through its homology. The MHS associated to the graph is the one of the complement of the zero locus of this polynomial in some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Then there raises the question if the theta divisor may also be discussed as a degeneration of MHS associated to the graphs.

1.
In case of singular curves the canonical map

Theorem 2.1

The generalized

Proof.

By applying the Leray cohomological fiber functor

0\to pr_{2*}\mathcal{L}\to pr_{2*}\mathcal{L}(D)\to pr_{2*}(\mathcal{L}(D)/\mathcal{L})\to R^{1}pr_{2*}\mathcal{L}\to R^{1}pr_{2*}\mathcal{L}(D)

We have

(5)

0\to pr_{2*}\mathcal{L}(D)\to pr_{2*}(\mathcal{L}(D)/\mathcal{L})\to R^{1}pr_{2*}\mathcal{L}\to 0

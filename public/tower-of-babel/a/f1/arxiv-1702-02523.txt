The Nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) is a universal model that describes the propagation of nonlinear waves in
dispersive media. It may e.g. appear as a so-called modulation equation, describing the complex enveloppe of a highly
oscillating field in nonlinear optics, and in particular in fiber optics (see
[
2
,
22
]
).
It may also be derived from the water wave problem, thanks to scaling and perturbation arguments,
to describe the propagation of surface waves of finite amplitude in deep fluids (see
[
16
,
27
]
).
The propagation of nonlinear dispersive waves in nonhomogeneous or
random media (or taking account of temperature effects) can be modelled by the nonlinear equation
with a random force, or a random potential (see e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
18
]
).

When the stochastic perturbation is a Wiener process, the equation is well treated and existence and uniqueness of the solution is known,
under reasonable assumptions on the noise correlation and on the nonlinearity.
For more information see
[
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. The case where the nonlinear Schrödinger equation is
perturbed by a Lévy process
is much less treated in the literature. In
[
25
,
26
]
, the authors consider the NLS equation with randomly distributed,
but isolated jumps. In the context of fiber optics, the model would describe random amplification of the signal at random
(but isolated) locations along the fiber (see
[
21
]
). In that situation the existence and uniqueness of solutions is easily deduced
from the classical results known in the deterministic case, and the motivations in
[
25
,
26
]
were to obtain the evolution law
of some physical observables of the solution.

Here, we consider the more general case where the noise is an infinite dimensional Lévy process, with possibly non isolated jumps, and
we investigate the existence of martingale solutions. Before stating the precise result, let us introduce some notations.

\left|\mathfrak{T}u\right|_{L^{q_{0}}(0,T;H^{s}_{p_{0}})}\leq C\left|u\right|_{L^{q_{1}^{\prime}}(0,T;H^{s}_{p_{1}^{\prime}})}.

In order to treat the nonlinearity,
let
F:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be given by
F(u)=|u|^{\alpha-1}u
and
let
\mathfrak{F}
be the convolution operator given by

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}u(t):=\int_{0}^{t}{{\mathcal{T}}}(t-r)F(u(r))\,dr.

Remark 3.3
.

We would like to mention that the Nemitskii operator associated to
F
, defined by

{{\mathcal{F}}}(u)(x):=F(u(x)),\quad u:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to\mathbb{C},

is a continuous operator from
L^{\alpha+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{\alpha+1\over\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and hence
from
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{\alpha+1\over\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
1<\alpha<1+4/d
.
Let
p=\alpha+1
and
q=4(\alpha+1)/d(\alpha-1)
.
Then we have for all
T>0
and any admissible pairs
(m,l)
,

\left|\mathfrak{F}u\right|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{m})}\leq{C}(T)\,\left|u\right|^{\alpha}_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{p})},

with
C(T)\to 0
as
T\to 0
.

The proposition can be extended to
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume

\begin{cases}1<\alpha<{d+2\over d-2},&\;\mathrm{if}\;d>2;\\
1<\alpha<\infty,&\;\mathrm{if}\;d=1,2.\end{cases}

Let

p^{\sharp}={d(\alpha+1)\over d+\alpha-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad q^{\sharp}={4(\alpha+1)\over(d-2)(\alpha-1)}.

Then we have for all
T>0
,

\left|\mathfrak{F}u\right|_{L^{q^{\sharp}}(0,T;H^{1}_{p^{\sharp}})}\leq C\,\left|u\right|^{\alpha}_{L^{q^{\sharp}}(0,T;H^{1}_{p^{\sharp}})}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition
3.4
. One only has to take into account the following
estimate, which follows from Hölder’s inequality :

||u|^{\alpha-1}\nabla u|_{L^{{p}^{\sharp^{\prime}}}}\leq c\,||u|^{\alpha-1}|_{L^{l}}|\nabla u|_{L^{p^{\sharp}}}\leq c\,|u|_{L^{({\alpha-1})l}}^{\alpha-1}|\nabla u|_{L^{p^{\sharp}}}\leq c|\nabla u|_{L^{p^{\sharp}}}^{\alpha}.

Here
\frac{1}{l}=1-\frac{2}{p^{\sharp}}
and
\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)l}=\frac{1}{p^{\sharp}}-\frac{1}{d}
.
Therefore,
(\alpha+1)/p^{\sharp}=(d+\alpha-1)/d
.
∎

R_{m}\uparrow\infty
, such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(R_{m})}|u_{n}(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4}\,2^{-m}|u_{n}|^{p}_{L^{p}},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,\,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Due to the first condition on
{{\mathcal{A}}}
such a sequence exists. In particular, it follows by an application of the Chebyscheff inequality and the fact that
|u_{n_{k}}|^{p}_{L^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{2}a^{\ast}
.
Since
{{\mathcal{A}}}
is bounded in
H^{s}_{p}(B(R_{1}))
and

H^{s}_{p}(B(R_{1}))\hookrightarrow L^{p}(B(R_{1}))
compactly,
there exists a subsequence
\{n^{1}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\{n_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

\{u_{n^{1}_{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(B(R_{1}))
.

Again, since
{{\mathcal{A}}}
is bounded in
H^{\gamma}_{p}(B(R_{2}))
and

H^{\gamma}_{p}(B(R_{2}))\hookrightarrow L^{p}(B(R_{2}))
compactly,
there exists a subsequence
\{n^{2}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\{n^{1}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

\{u_{n^{2}_{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(B(R_{2}))
.

Proceeding in this way we obtain subsequences
\{n^{m}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
such that

•

\{n_{k}^{1}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}\supset\{n_{k}^{2}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}\supset\{n_{k}^{3}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}\supset\ldots
;

•

for each fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{u_{n^{m}_{l}}:l\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(B(R_{m}))
;

•

for each fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(R_{m})}|u_{n}(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq 2^{-m}|u_{n}|^{p}_{L^{p}},\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
;

Let
\{\tilde{n}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the diagonal sequence defined for
k\in\mathbb{N}
by
\tilde{n}_{k}:=n_{k}^{k}
.
Now, we claim that
\{u_{\tilde{n}_{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
In order to show it, fix
\varepsilon>0
. The task is now to find an index
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx\leq\varepsilon,\quad\forall\,l,k\geq k_{1}.

Let

m\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest integer such that
m\geq\ln_{2}(a^{\ast})-\ln_{2}(\frac{\varepsilon}{6})
.
Since
\{u_{n^{m}_{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(B(R_{m}))
and

\{\tilde{u}_{n_{k}}:k\geq m\}\subset\{u_{n^{m}_{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
, it follows that
\{\tilde{u}_{n_{k}}:k\geq m\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(B(R_{m}))
. Therefore, there exists a
k_{1}\geq m
such that

\int_{B(R_{m})}|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad k,l\geq k_{1}.

Then we have for any
k,l\geq k_{1}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{B(R_{m})}|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(R_{m})}|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx.

By the choice of
m
we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(R_{m})}|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(R_{m})}|u_{n_{k}}|^{p}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(R_{m})}|u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-m}|u_{n_{k}}|^{p}+2^{-m}|u_{n_{l}}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-m+1}\frac{3}{2}a^{\ast}\leq\varepsilon/2.

Collecting altogether, we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon,

and the assertion follows.

∎

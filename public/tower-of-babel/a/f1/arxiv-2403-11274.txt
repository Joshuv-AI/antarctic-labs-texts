Comment on “Spatiotemporal torquing of light”
Miguel A. Porras

Abstract
The recent paper Phys. Rev. X. 14, 011031 (https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.01019) includes an appendix that casts doubts on the validity the theory of the transverse orbital angular momentum of spatiotemporal optical vortices (STOVs) in Prog. Electromagn. Res. 177, 95 (https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.09105). The argumentation in that appendix mixes a mechanical approach where STOVs are seen in space evolving in time with an optical approach where STOVs are seen in space-time evolving with propagation distance, which leads to wrong conclusions. We consistently carry out the analysis from the mechanical approach, and the results confirm the theory in Prog. Electromagn. Res. 177, 95 consistently performed within the optical approach.



In Ref.
MILCHBERG_PRX
, page 11, a scheme similar to that in Fig.
1
is used to infer that the intrinsic transverse orbital angular momentum (OAM) about a
y
axis (perpendicular to the screen) passing through the center of the energy
{\bf r}_{\rm CE}
of a symmetric STOV propagating along the
z

y
axis passing though the origin O. Indeed, the extrinsic transverse OAM,
{\bf r}_{\rm CE}\times{\bf P}
, seems to vanish. This is in contradiction with Ref.
PORRAS_PIERS
demonstrating that in this same problem the transverse OAM about O vanishes, and the extrinsic and intrinsic transverse OAM do not, but are opposite. Expressed per unit energy
W

PORRAS_PIERS
demonstrates that

\frac{J_{y}}{W}=0\,,\quad\frac{J_{y}^{(e)}}{W}=-\frac{l}{2\omega_{0}}\gamma\,,\quad\frac{J_{y}^{(i)}}{W}=\frac{l}{2\omega_{0}}\gamma\,,

(1)

where
\omega_{0}
is the carrier frequency,
l
is the topological charge of the STOV at the transversal plane where it is elliptical with ellipticity
\gamma
.

The fact that the transverse OAM is different about the moving
y
through CE and about the fixed
y
axis through O is not evident. Angular momentum issues are often very subtle. Understanding why they are different will inform us of the actual nature of the OAM of STOVs —a hot topic in the structured-light community already moving towards applications
HUANG
, and incidentally will reveal details of the spatial structure of STOVs that have gone unnoticed.

The error in the analysis in Ref.
MILCHBERG_PRX
and in Fig.
1
comes from confusing 1) the structure of the STOV in space
(x,y,z)
at each instant of time, as usually viewed with moving objects in mechanics, i.e., a snapshot, with 2) the structure of the STOV in space-time
(x,y,t^{\prime})
, where
t^{\prime}=t-z/c
is the local time, at each transversal plane, as usually observed in optics (in Refs.
MILCHBERG_PRX
;
MILCHBERG_PRL
,
\xi=ct-z=ct^{\prime}
proportional to
t^{\prime}
is used to endow the local time with dimensions of length). This confusion leads to the “collage” of Fig.
1
, or of Fig. 7(b) in Ref.
MILCHBERG_PRX
, where the structure in
(x,y,z)
at an instant of time is mixed with the structure in
(x,y,t^{\prime})
at a transversal section
z
. In Ref.
PORRAS_PIERS
the analysis is consistently performed in space-time
(x,y,t^{\prime})
at each
z
.

Specifically, the intrinsic OAM, or OAM about the center of the energy, is written in
MILCHBERG_PRX
as

\displaystyle{\bf J}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf r}({\bf r}-{\bf r}_{\rm CE})\times{\bf p}

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf r}\,{\bf r}\times{\bf p}-{\bf r}_{\rm CE}\times\int d^{3}{\bf r}\,{\bf p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf r}\,{\bf r}\times{\bf p}-{\bf r}_{\rm CE}\times{\bf P}

where
d^{3}{\bf r}=dxdydz
,
{\bf r}_{\rm CE}
is the center of the energy of the STOV,
{\bf p}={\bf S}/c^{2}
is the momentum density, and
{\bf S}
is the Poynting vector
{\bf S}=\mu^{-1}_{0}{\bf E}\times{\bf B}
. The first term in Eq. (
2
) is the angular momentum about the origin O, and therefore the second term is the extrinsic angular momentum of the STOV about O. Then, inspection of Fig.
1
“evidences” that
{\bf r}_{\rm CE}=c(t-t_{0})\hat{\bf z}
, where
\hat{\bf z}
is the unit vector along the
z
direction, and the total momentum
{\bf P}
are parallel, concluding that the extrinsic OAM
{\bf r}_{\rm CE}\times{\bf P}
vanishes.

(x,y,t^{\prime})

(x,y,x)

{\bf r}_{\rm CE}
has an
x

(x,y,t^{\prime})

z_{0}

{\bf r}_{\rm CE}\times{\bf P}
of the STOV about the

MILCHBERG_PRX

is positive, yielding a negative extrinsic transverse OAM with respect to O. For negative
l

PORRAS_PIERS

Supplementary material

A quantitative evaluation of the energy density pattern, the surface in Fig.
2
(b), the center of the energy and the transverse OAM in a consistent way with the mechanical picture requires the knowledge of the full
(x,y,z)
structure of the STOV. We start with a STOV of scalar field
\psi(x,y,t^{\prime})e^{-i\omega_{0}t^{\prime}}
,

\psi(x,y,t^{\prime})=e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{w_{0}^{2}}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{w_{0}^{2}}}e^{-\frac{t^{\prime 2}}{t_{0}^{2}}}\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{t_{0}}\mp i\mbox{sign}(l)\frac{x}{w_{0}}\right)^{|l|}\,,

(3)

at a plane
z_{0}
, whose energy density is elliptical in
(x,y,t^{\prime})
with ellipticity
\gamma\equiv ct_{0}/w_{0}
, as in Fig.
2
(a). Its forward and backward propagated field
\psi(x,y,t^{\prime},z)e^{-i\omega_{0}t^{\prime}}
has been evaluated in
PORRAS_OL
(and in
MILCHBERG_PRL
for
|l|=1
). Once
t^{\prime}
is replaced with
t-z/c
, the STOV field reads as
\psi(x,y,z,t)e^{-i[\omega_{0}(t-z/c)]}
, where

\begin{split}&\psi(x,y,z,t)=\frac{-iz_{R}}{q(z)}e^{\frac{ik_{0}y^{2}}{2q(z)}}e^{\frac{ik_{0}x^{2}}{2q(z)}}e^{-\frac{(t-z/c)^{2}}{t_{0}^{2}}}\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{q(z)}\right)^{\frac{|l|}{2}}\\
&\times\frac{1}{2^{|l|}}H_{|l|}\!\left\{\!\left(\frac{q(z)}{z-z_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\left[\frac{t-z/c}{t_{0}}\mp\mbox{sign}(l)\frac{x}{w_{0}}\frac{z_{R}}{q(z)}\right]\!\right\}\end{split}

(4)

k_{0}=\omega_{0}/c
,
q(z)=(z-z_{0})-iz_{R}
,
z_{R}=k_{0}w_{0}^{2}/2
, and
H_{|l|}(\cdot)
is the Hermite polynomial of order
|l|
. From Eq. (
4
), one can construct paraxial and quasi-monochromatic electromagnetic fields following Lax’s
et al.
perturbation theory
LAX
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{y}=&{\rm Re}\left\{\psi e^{-i\omega_{0}\left(t-\frac{z}{c}\right)}\right\},\\
E_{z}=&{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{i}{k_{0}}\partial_{y}\psi e^{-i\omega_{0}\left(t-\frac{z}{c}\right)}\right\},\\
B_{x}=&-{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{1}{c}\psi e^{-i\omega_{0}\left(t-\frac{z}{c}\right)}\right\},\\
B_{z}=&-{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{i}{k_{0}c}\partial_{x}\psi e^{-i\omega_{0}\left(t-\frac{z}{c}\right)}\right\}.\end{split}

(5)

Linear polarization along the
y
direction is chosen, but the results of the following calculations are the same for
x
polarization.

The electromagnetic energy density
w=(1/2)\left(\varepsilon_{0}|{\bf E}|^{2}+\mu_{0}^{-1}|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)
of the fields in Eqs. (
5
) averaged over the optical cycles yields
\langle w\rangle=\varepsilon_{0}|\psi|^{2}/2
.
Note that
\langle w\rangle
and the intensity
\langle S_{z}\rangle=\epsilon_{0}c|\psi|^{2}/2
are proportional in the paraxial approximation so the choice of one or another, or simply
|\psi|^{2}
, is irrelevant to determine their distributions in space. The center of energy is

{\bf r}_{\rm CE}(t)=\frac{1}{W}\int d^{3}{\bf r}\,{\bf r}\langle w\rangle=\frac{\int d^{3}{\bf r}\,{\bf r}|\psi|^{2}}{\int d^{3}{\bf r}|\psi|^{2}}\,,

(6)

where

W=\int d^{3}{\bf r}\,\langle w\rangle=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\int d^{3}{\bf r}\,|\psi|^{2}

(7)

is the energy carried by the STOV.

Let us continue with the
y
component of the OAM. Its density
j_{y}=(zp_{x}-xp_{z})=(zS_{x}-xS_{z})/c^{2}=\varepsilon_{0}(zE_{y}B_{z}+xE_{y}B_{x})
time-averaged over the optical cycles and integrated in space yields the transverse OAM about O as

J_{y}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2\omega_{0}}\int d^{3}{\bf r}\left(z{\rm Im}\left\{\psi^{\star}\partial_{x}\psi\right\}-k_{0}x|\psi|^{2}\right).

(8)

Similarly, the extrinsic transverse OAM
J_{y}^{(e)}=[{\bf r}_{\rm CE}(t)\times{\bf P}]_{y}=z_{\rm CE}(t)P_{x}-x_{\rm CE}(t)P_{z}
yields

J_{y}^{(e)}=z_{\rm CE}(t)\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2\omega_{0}}\int d^{3}{\bf r}{\rm Im}\left\{\psi^{\star}\partial_{x}\psi\right\}-x_{\rm CE}(t)\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2c}\int d^{3}{\bf r}|\psi|^{2},

(9)

and the intrinsic transverse OAM is calculated as
J_{y}^{(i)}=J_{y}-J_{y}^{(e)}
.

None of these integrals in
x,y,z
can be performed analytically, given the complexity of the expression of the STOV in full space in Eq. (
4
), but the numerical results corroborate Eqs. (
1
), as pointed out above for the STOV of Fig.
2
.
